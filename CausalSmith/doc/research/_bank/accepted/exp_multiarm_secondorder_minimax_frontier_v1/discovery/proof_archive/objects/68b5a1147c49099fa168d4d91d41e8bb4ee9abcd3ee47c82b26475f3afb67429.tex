\textbf{Established partial result.}
Put \(x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c)\), \(\alpha_c=C_0(c)\kappa_c\), and \(\beta_c=C_0(c)C_A\).  The universal second-order rate theorem gives
\[
 0<\alpha_c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)\leq\beta_c<\infty.
\]
Consequently, the subsequential-limit set
\[
 \mathcal L(c)=\bigcap_{N\geq1}
 \overline{\{x_n(c):n\geq N\}}
\]
is a nonempty compact subset of \([\alpha_c,\beta_c]\), and every one of its elements is finite and strictly positive.  Indeed, the displayed liminf and limsup bounds put the tail of \((x_n(c))\) in a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\), after which Bolzano--Weierstrass gives nonemptiness and the intersection-of-tail-closures representation gives closedness.

There is also an exact characterization of what a slowly varying correction could accomplish.  Every regularly varying normalizer for which \(a_nd_n(c)\to C\in(0,\infty)\) has index \(4/3\) by the universal second-order rate theorem, and hence can be written as
\[
 a_n=n^{4/3}\ell_n,
\]
where \(\ell_n\) is slowly varying.  The preceding two-sided bounds force \(\ell_n\) to be eventually bounded above and bounded away from zero.  More precisely, there exist a slowly varying positive sequence \(\ell_n\) and a constant \(C\in(0,\infty)\) such that
\[
 \ell_nx_n(c)\longrightarrow C
\]
if and only if \((x_n(c))\) is itself slowly varying, meaning that
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}\longrightarrow1
 \qquad\text{for every }s>0.
\]
For the forward implication, write
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}
      {\ell_nx_n(c)},
\]
and take limits.  Conversely, eventual positivity of \(x_n(c)\) and its slow variation allow the choice \(\ell_n=C/x_n(c)\), which is slowly varying and makes the product identically \(C\) on the eventual tail.  Thus, if \(x_n(c)\) fails to converge, a slowly varying correction can rescue it precisely when its nonconvergence occurs within the slowly varying class; in that case any rescuing correction is necessarily nonconvergent as well.

For \(c=c^\dagger\), choose integers \(M_n\) such that \(n^{4/3}/M_n^2\to0\), and define the exactly computable certificate improvements
\[
 J_n^{\Lambda}=n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-\Lambda_{n,M_n}^\dagger\},
 \qquad
 L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}=n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-U_{n,M_n}\},
 \qquad
 U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}=n^{4/3}
 \{n^{-1}-B_n(\nu_{n,M_n})\}.
\]
The grid-certificate sandwich gives
\[
 J_n^{\Lambda}\leq L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}
 \leq x_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}},
 \qquad
 0\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}-J_n^{\Lambda}
 \leq\frac{n^{4/3}}{4M_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
It follows that every one of the three certificate sequences differs from \(x_n(c^\dagger)\) by \(o(1)\).  Therefore all four sequences have exactly the same subsequential-limit set and converge if and only if any one of them converges, in which case their limits agree.  Since \(x_n(c^\dagger)\) is eventually bounded away from zero, the same \(o(1)\) comparison also shows that one of these four sequences is slowly varying if and only if all four are.  Hence the exact rational LP values \(\Lambda_{n,M_n}^\dagger\) and their primal--dual certificates diagnose not only a proposed sharp constant but also the exact slowly varying correction criterion, to vanishing error; they do not themselves prove which behavior occurs.
