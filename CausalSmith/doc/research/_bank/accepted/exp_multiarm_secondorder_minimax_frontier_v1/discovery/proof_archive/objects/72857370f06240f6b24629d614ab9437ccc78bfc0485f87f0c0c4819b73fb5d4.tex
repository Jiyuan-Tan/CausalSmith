\textbf{Proof.}
Suppose first that \(|S_c|=2\). Its active coefficients are \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\), where
\[
 \alpha=\frac{L_c}{2}=\sqrt{C_0(c)}>0.
\]
The exact response-type game theorem, specialized to \(K=2\), supplies an attained minimax procedure \(p_{n,2}^\star\) and a least-favorable prior \(\mu_{n,2}^\star\) on two-arm response-count orbits forming a saddle at value \(\rho_{n,2}\). Distribute the mass of each orbit uniformly over its labeled schedules; the resulting invariant complete-schedule prior has the same Bayes risks because both target and symmetrized procedure risk are orbit-constant. Lift \(p_{n,2}^\star\) by mapping its two assignment labels to the two active arms, assigning no unit to inactive arms, and multiplying its estimator by \(\alpha\). Every \(K\)-arm schedule induces a binary schedule on the active coordinates, so the reverse-reduction calculation in the embedded theorem gives maximum risk at most \(\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\).

Push \(\mu_{n,2}^\star\) forward through the sign-group embedding \(E_c\). For an arbitrary \(K\)-arm procedure, the conditional-Jensen construction in the embedded theorem produces a legitimate two-arm procedure and proves schedule by schedule
\[
 R_{n,2}(p_2;y)\leq\alpha^{-2}R_n(p_K;E_c(y)).
\]
Integrating under \(\mu_{n,2}^\star\) shows that the Bayes risk of every \(K\)-arm procedure under the pushed-forward prior is at least \(\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\). Thus the lifted procedure and pushed-forward prior form an exact finite-\(n\) saddle, and
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
This construction uses only internal finite-game attainment; it names no particular published optimizer or prior.

For every support size, the universal theorem constructs the independent \(q^\star\) design and observable clipped-shrinkage estimator and proves, for all \(n\geq N_c\),
\[
 \max_zR_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\}.
\]
The same theorem combines this procedure with the internal embedded converse to give
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_n n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_n n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq43C_0(c).
\]
This also proves the corresponding support-two display.

For the certificate assertion, choose rational zero-sum approximants and define \(\eta_n,B_n,U_n,R_n^-\), and \(R_n^+\) as in the statement. The real-contrast certificate-transfer theorem supplies an observed-data procedure with worst-case risk at most \(R_n^+\) and a response-count prior whose Bayes risk against the unrestricted decision class is at least \(R_n^-\). Therefore
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
 \qquad
 \underline d_n\leq d_n(c)\leq\overline d_n.
\]
That theorem also proves
\[
 R_n^+-R_n^-
 \leq\frac{5C_0(c^{(n)})}{4M_n^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c^{(n)})}\,\eta_nn^{-1/2}+3\eta_n^2.
\]
It constructs approximants with \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\). With \(M_n=n\), the three terms are respectively \(O(n^{-2})\), \(o(n^{-4/3})\), and \(o(n^{-5/3})\), so the improvement bracket has width \(o(n^{-4/3})\). For rational \(c\), take \(c^{(n)}=c\); then \(\eta_n=0\), and the direct rational sandwich gives the sharper gap \(C_0(c)/(4n^2)\).

Finally, for \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\),
\[
 L_{c^\dagger}=2,
 \qquad C_0(c^\dagger)=1.
\]
The three-arm certificate theorem specializes the rational sandwich to
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
Its calculations through \(n=5\) are exact-rational same-population diagnostics only. None supplies a relation between population sizes, a sharp normalized limit, convergence of designs or priors, or an analytic limiting equation. This proves exactly the stated attainment and certificate conclusions and leaves the listed frontier questions open. \(\square\)
