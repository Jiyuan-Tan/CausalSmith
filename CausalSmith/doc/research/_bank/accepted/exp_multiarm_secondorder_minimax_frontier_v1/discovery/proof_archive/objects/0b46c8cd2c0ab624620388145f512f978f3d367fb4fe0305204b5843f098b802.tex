Let \(\widetilde\tau=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_i\), where
\[
 Z_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a^\star}
 \bigl(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1/2\bigr).
\]
The only divisions are over \(a\in S_c\), where \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/L_c>0\).  By independent assignment and \(\sum_ac_a=0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}Z_i
 =\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a(t_{i,a}-1/2)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde\tau\) is unbiased for \(\tau_c(z)\).  Since every potential outcome is binary,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}Z_i^2
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a^\star}
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}L_c|c_a|
 =\frac{L_c^2}{4}=C_0(c).
\]
The assignment variables are independent across units, so
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\{\widetilde\tau-\tau_c(z)\}^2
 =\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \left[C_0(c)-\left\{\sum_ac_at_{i,a}\right\}^2\right]
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
Because the total positive coefficient mass and total absolute negative coefficient mass both equal \(L_c/2\),
\[
 -L_c/2\leq\sum_ac_at_{i,a}\leq L_c/2
\]
for every binary response type, including the all-zero and all-one boundary types.  Hence \(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), and metric projection onto this interval cannot increase squared distance from the target.  This proves the claimed finite-sample upper bound for \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star\).

For the converse, the embedded-two-arm theorem gives the sharper finite-sample inequality
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
The cited two-arm expansion yields
\[
 C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}
 =\frac{C_0(c)}n-C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
 =\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
\]
The procedure-level construction in that theorem applies to each member of any sequence of arbitrary dependent assignment laws and possibly biased estimators.  Therefore each such sequence has worst-case risk at least the last display.  Together with the finite-sample upper bound,
\[
 C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\leq\rho_n(c)\leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
Multiplying by \(n\) and using \(n\rho_{n,2}\to1\) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n\rho_n(c)=C_0(c)=\frac14\left(\sum_a|c_a|\right)^2.
\]
Finally, for any allocation vector \(q\) positive on \(S_c\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives the matching allocation certificate
\[
 \frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}
 \geq\frac14\frac{\left(\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|\right)^2}{\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a}
 \geq\frac{L_c^2}{4}.
\]
Equality holds precisely when no probability is assigned outside \(S_c\) and \(q_a=|c_a|/L_c\) on \(S_c\).  This certifies the displayed contrast-weighted allocation among all independent fixed-probability allocations.
