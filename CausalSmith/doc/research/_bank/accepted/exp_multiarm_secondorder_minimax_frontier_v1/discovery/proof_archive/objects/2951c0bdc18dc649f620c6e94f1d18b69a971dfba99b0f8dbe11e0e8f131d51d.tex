Take \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)\).  Directly from the orbit target,
\[
 \tau_c(m)=\frac1n\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^2}m_t(t_1-t_2)
 =\frac{m_{(1,0)}-m_{(0,1)}}n
 =\frac{p_+-p_-}{n}.
\]
The two zero-effect types contribute zero to this contrast, and
\(r_0=m_{(0,0)}+m_{(1,1)}\).  Nevertheless, in \(P_m(x\mid r)\) the factors \(m_t!\) and the row constraints \(\sum_a h_{t,a}=m_t\) are indexed by each of the four response types separately.  Thus the orbit likelihood retains \(m_{(0,0)}\) and \(m_{(1,1)}\) as separate coordinates; the displayed algebra for the target does not identify the likelihood through \(r_0\).

The cited published two-arm theorem states that this unrestricted binary finite-population minimax value equals the scalar-experiment value based on
\[
 X_2=p_++\operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)
\]
for estimating \((p_+-p_-)/n\), and that
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))=\frac1n-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Composing that cited equality with \(\rho_n((1,-1))=G_n((1,-1))\), proved by the exact response-type game theorem, gives the claimed value-level regression check without identifying the four-count orbit likelihood with the scalar kernel.  Here \(L_c=2\), so \(C_0(c)=L_c^2/4=1\).  Hence
\[
 d_n(c)=\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)
 =C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
which verifies both the sign and normalization of the Airy clause.

For every fixed \(K\ge3\), the exact response-type game theorem applies without changing the assignment or estimator classes and yields
\[
 \rho_n(c)=G_n(c)
\]
for every nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), with nature ranging over all \(2^K\) response types.  The cited published theorem is restricted to the two-arm experiment, so these \(K\ge3\) equalities strictly extend its treatment-domain scope.  No step asserts that the resulting multi-arm value lacks some other scalar representation.
