Put \(x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c)\). The universal second-order rate theorem proves
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A.
\]
Hence the subsequential-limit set
\[
 \mathcal L(c)=\bigcap_{N\geq1}\overline{\{x_n(c):n\geq N\}}
\]
is a nonempty compact subset of \([C_0(c)\kappa_c,C_0(c)C_A]\); every subsequential limit is finite and strictly positive. Every regularly varying normalizer producing a finite positive limit has the form \(a_n=n^{4/3}\ell_n\) with \(\ell_n\) slowly varying. If \(a_nd_n(c)\to C\in(0,\infty)\), the displayed two-sided bounds force \(\ell_n\) to be eventually bounded above and bounded away from zero, but they do not force \(\ell_n\) to converge. For \(c=c^\dagger\), choose integers \(M_n\) with \(n^{4/3}/M_n^2\to0\), and define
\[
 L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}=n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-U_{n,M_n}\},\qquad
 U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}=n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-B_n(\nu_{n,M_n})\}.
\]
The grid-certificate theorem gives
\[
 L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}\leq x_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}},\qquad
 0\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}-L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}\leq\frac{n^{4/3}}{4M_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore \(L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}\), \(x_n(c^\dagger)\), and \(U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}\) have exactly the same subsequential-limit set. This makes every eventual convergence, nonconvergence, or sharp-constant claim for the diagnostic contrast finitely certifiable to vanishing error, while leaving the analytic selection of that behavior open.
