\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(n\geq8\) and set \(a=n^{-1/3}\), so \(0<a\leq1/2\). Define
\[
 w_0(u)=\frac{15}{8}(1-4u^2)^2\mathbf 1\{|u|\leq1/2\},
 \qquad
 w_a(\theta)=a^{-1}w_0(\theta/a).
\]
The extension of \(w_0\) by zero is continuously differentiable, is positive on \((-1/2,1/2)\), and vanishes together with its derivative at the endpoints. Direct integration gives
\[
 \int_{-1/2}^{1/2}(1-4u^2)^2\,du=\frac8{15},
 \qquad
 J(w_0)=\int\frac{\{w_0'(u)\}^2}{w_0(u)}\,du=40.
\]
Hence \(w_a\) is a smooth compactly supported probability density on \((-a/2,a/2)\) with
\[
 J(w_a)=\frac{40}{a^2}.
\]

Conditional on \(\theta\sim w_a\), draw the response types of the \(n\) labeled units independently with probabilities
\[
 \Pr_\theta\{(1,0)\}=\frac{a+\theta}{2},\qquad
 \Pr_\theta\{(0,1)\}=\frac{a-\theta}{2},\qquad
 \Pr_\theta\{(0,0)\}=\Pr_\theta\{(1,1)\}=\frac{1-a}{2}.
\]
All four probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one because \(|\theta|<a/2\) and \(a\leq1/2\). This is a probability distribution on complete fixed labeled schedules. Conditional on its three effect-class counts, it is exactly the ordered-partition schedule kernel of the preceding lemma, with the two zero-effect types generated by independent fair bits. It is therefore a lawful Bayes prior for the unrestricted design game, including all boundary schedules in its finite support.

Fix an arbitrary assignment law \(\mathcal D\), chosen independently of the schedule, and any estimator \(T\). Define \(S_i=Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) on arm \(1\), \(S_i=1-Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) on arm \(2\), and \(R_i=2S_i-1\). Given \(\theta\), the preceding probabilities imply, under either assigned arm,
\[
 \Pr_\theta(S_i=1)=\frac{a+\theta}{2}+\frac{1-a}{2}
 =\frac{1+\theta}{2}.
\]
Conditional on any realized assignment vector, the response types are independent, so \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\) are independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \((1+\theta)/2\). Their conditional law does not depend on the assignment vector. Thus \(A\) is ancillary even when its coordinates are dependent, and the Fisher information in the full observed data is
\[
 I_n(\theta)=\frac{n}{1-\theta^2}.
\]
The success probabilities stay in \([3/8,5/8]\), so the likelihood is differentiable with common finite support and satisfies all score and differentiation conditions of the Bayesian information lemma.

Let \(U_i=Y_i(1)-Y_i(2)\in\{-1,0,1\}\), so the finite-population target is \(\tau=n^{-1}\sum_iU_i\). Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta(U_i\mid S_i=1)=\frac{a+\theta}{1+\theta},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\theta(U_i\mid S_i=0)=-\frac{a-\theta}{1-\theta}.
\]
With
\[
 b(\theta)=\frac{a-\theta^2}{1-\theta^2},
 \qquad
 \overline R=n^{-1}\sum_iR_i,
\]
both cases are summarized by
\[
 g(\theta,A,S):=\mathbb E_\theta(\tau\mid A,S)
 =\theta+b(\theta)(\overline R-\theta).
\]
Because \(|\theta|<a/2\) and \(0<a<1\),
\[
 0\leq b(\theta)\leq a.
\]
Moreover, \(\mathbb E_\theta(\overline R)=\theta\), and differentiation at fixed observed \((A,S)\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\{\partial_\theta g(\theta,A,S)\}
 =1-b(\theta).
\]
The target and estimator are bounded, the parameter support is compactly inside the regular Bernoulli region, and the prior and its derivative vanish at the endpoints. Thus the boundary, integrability, and absolute-continuity conditions of the Bayesian information lemma hold.

Conditional square completion gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta[(T-\tau)^2\mid A,S]
 =(T-g(\theta,A,S))^2+\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\tau\mid A,S)
 \geq(T-g(\theta,A,S))^2.
\]
Applying the Bayesian information lemma to the data-dependent target \(g\), then using \(b\leq a\), \(|\theta|\leq a/2\), and the information calculations above, gives the Bayes-risk lower bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(T-\tau)^2
 &\geq
 \frac{\left[\mathbb E\{1-b(\theta)\}\right]^2}
 {40/a^2+\mathbb E\{n/(1-\theta^2)\}}\\
 &\geq\frac{(1-a)^2}{40/a^2+n/(1-a^2/4)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Maximum risk over fixed schedules dominates Bayes risk under every schedule prior. Since \((\mathcal D,T)\) was arbitrary, infimizing proves the first displayed minimax lower bound.

For the coarse closed form, divide its denominator by \(n\) and use \(na^2=n^{1/3}=a^{-1}\):
\[
 \frac{(1-a)^2}{40/a^2+n/(1-a^2/4)}
 =\frac1n\frac{(1-a)^2}{40a+(1-a^2/4)^{-1}}.
\]
For \(0<a\leq1/2\), one has \((1-a^2/4)^{-1}\leq1+a\) and \((1-a)^2\geq1-2a\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{(1-a)^2}{40a+(1-a^2/4)^{-1}}
 \geq\frac{1-2a}{1+41a}
 =1-\frac{43a}{1+41a}
 \geq1-43a.
\]
Substitution of \(a=n^{-1/3}\) proves \(\rho_n((1,-1))\geq n^{-1}-43n^{-4/3}\). No property of a published optimizer or asymptotic expansion was used. \(\square\)
