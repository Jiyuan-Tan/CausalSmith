For every integer \(n\geq1\), every \(K\geq2\), and every nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), write \(\rho_{n,2}=\rho_n((1,-1))\) for the unrestricted two-arm minimax risk with the same population size. Then
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
If \(|S_c|=2\), this inequality is an equality. The contrast-weighted first-order procedure gives \(\rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c)/n\), and the internal Bayesian-information construction gives, for every \(n\geq8\),
\[
 \rho_{n,2}\geq n^{-1}-43n^{-4/3}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(n\geq8\),
\[
 0\leq d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}
 \leq43C_0(c)n^{-4/3}.
\]
When \(|S_c|=2\), the middle inequality is an equality, so \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}\). The finite-sample sign-group embedding permits arbitrary dependent assignment designs and arbitrary possibly biased estimators; no published two-arm optimizer, least-favorable prior, or Airy expansion is used.
