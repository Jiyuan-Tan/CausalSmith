A rigorous partial answer is available.  Fix \(K\geq2\) and a nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\).

First, if \(|S_c|=2\), then contrast-weighted independent assignment, with probability \(1/2\) on each active arm, and the coefficient-rescaled published two-arm posterior-mean rule form an exact finite-\(n\) minimax saddle.  The correspondingly embedded published least-favorable prior is an exact Bayes-risk converse.  In particular, if \(\rho_{n,2}=\rho_n((1,-1))\), then
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2},
 \qquad
 d_n(c)=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]

Second, for arbitrary support, the independent \(q^\star\) design and the explicit clipped-shrinkage estimator in the universal second-order rate theorem satisfy, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \max_z R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_c n^{-4/3}\},
 \qquad \kappa_c>0,
\]
where \(\kappa_c\) is the explicit constant in that theorem.  Together with the embedded two-arm converse, this gives
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)C_A.
\]

Third, choose rational nonzero zero-sum contrasts \(c^{(n)}\in\mathbb Q^K\) with \(c^{(n)}\to c\), put
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c^{(n)}}}{2},
\]
and choose integers \(M_n\geq1\).  Let
\[
 B_n=B_{n,c^{(n)}}(\nu_{n,M_n,c^{(n)}}),
 \qquad
 U_n=U_{n,M_n}(c^{(n)})
\]
be the exact rational prior and procedure certificates, and define
\[
 R_n^-=\left\{\max(0,\sqrt{B_n}-\eta_n)\right\}^2,
 \qquad
 R_n^+=(\sqrt{U_n}+\eta_n)^2.
\]
Then
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+.
\]
The projected rational-certificate procedure has worst-case risk at most \(R_n^+\), while the transferred rational prior certifies the lower bound \(R_n^-\).  If
\[
 \underline d_n=\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+,
 \qquad
 \overline d_n=\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-,
\]
then
\[
 \underline d_n\leq d_n(c)\leq\overline d_n
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\leq\overline d_n-\underline d_n
 &=R_n^+-R_n^-\\
 &\leq\frac{5C_0(c^{(n)})}{4M_n^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c^{(n)})}\,\eta_n n^{-1/2}
 +3\eta_n^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
One may choose the approximants so that \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\); with \(M_n=n\), the displayed improvement bracket then has width \(o(n^{-4/3})\).  If \(c\) is rational, one may take \(c^{(n)}=c\), and the direct exact-rational specialization with \(M_n=n\) has risk-certificate gap at most \(C_0(c)/(4n^2)\).

For \(c=c^\dagger\), one has \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\), and the direct three-arm certificates satisfy, for every \(n,M\geq1\),
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
The available exact rational calculations through \(n=5\) are the computed three-arm showcase for this formula; they are not used to infer a cross-\(n\) limit.

These conclusions establish exact analytic attainment for two active arms, explicit order attainment for every fixed number of active arms, and scale-matched computable procedure and converse certificates for every real zero-sum contrast, directly exact-rational when the contrast is rational.  They do not determine, when \(|S_c|\geq3\), convergence or the sharp constant of \(n^{4/3}d_n(c)\), sharp second-order optimality of \(q^\star\), convergence of the finite-program optimizers or priors, an analytic limiting feedback rule, or a limiting Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman, spectral, separable, or other operator.
