\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(n\geq 1\).  First take \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)\).  Directly from the orbit target,
\[
 \tau_c(m)
 =\frac1n\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^2}m_t(t_1-t_2)
 =\frac{m_{(1,0)}-m_{(0,1)}}n
 =\frac{p_+-p_-}{n},
\]
and \(r_0=m_{(0,0)}+m_{(1,1)}\), so \(p_++p_-+r_0=n\).  This aggregation is valid for the target only.  In the orbit likelihood, the factors \(m_t!\), the row constraints \(\sum_a h_{t,a}=m_t\), and the success constraints \(\sum_{t:t_a=1}h_{t,a}=x_a\) remain indexed by the four separate response types.  Hence neither the formula for \(P_m(x\mid r)\) nor the exact-game theorem asserts that the likelihood is determined by \((p_+,p_-,r_0)\).

The exact response-type game theorem gives
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))=G_n((1,-1)).
\]
For this contrast, \(S_c=\{1,2\}\), \(L_c=2\), and therefore
\[
 C_0(c)=\frac{L_c^2}{4}=1.
\]
The support-two equality and published two-arm conclusion recorded in the embedded two-arm converse identify this minimax value with the published scalar value based on
\[
 X_2=p_++\operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)
\]
for estimating \((p_+-p_-)/n\), and yield
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))
 =n^{-1}-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Using the definition \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)/n-\rho_n(c)\) and \(C_0(c)=1\) gives
\[
 d_n(c)=C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Thus the scalar reduction is used only at the minimax-value level and does not collapse the four-count orbit likelihood.

Now let \(K\geq3\) be fixed and let \(c\) be any nonzero zero-sum contrast.  By the exact response-type game theorem, unit-label symmetrization is lossless over the full classes of assignment laws and clipped estimators, and
\[
 \rho_n(c)=G_n(c),
\]
where nature ranges over \(\mathcal M_{n,K}\), the count simplex on all \(|\mathcal T_K|=2^K\) response types, and the likelihood is the displayed contingency-table kernel \(P_m(x\mid r)\).  The design class in the labeled game contains every probability law on \(\mathcal A_K^n\), including dependent assignments, and the estimator class contains arbitrary measurable, possibly biased rules with values in \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\).  These \(K\geq3\) decision problems are outside the domain of the cited two-arm scalar theorem, so the exact-game equality is a strict extension in treatment-domain scope.  This domain comparison makes no claim that a different scalar representation for the multi-arm value is impossible.

No exposure-positivity, overlap, rank, or inverse condition is used: allocation counts may lie on faces or corners of \(\mathcal R_{n,K}\), and zero-probability observations do not affect risk.  The only support conditions are \(n\geq1\), fixed finite \(K\geq2\), binary response types \(t\in\mathcal T_K\), and a nonzero zero-sum contrast.  Since all factorial arguments in the likelihood are nonnegative integers and \(n!>0\), the kernel is well defined on every feasible fiber.  In particular, the all-zero and all-one response types remain admissible boundary schedules.  \(\square\)
