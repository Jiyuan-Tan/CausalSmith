\textbf{Proof.}
First take \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)\). Directly from the orbit target,
\[
 \tau_c(m)=\frac1n\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^2}m_t(t_1-t_2)
 =\frac{m_{(1,0)}-m_{(0,1)}}n
 =\frac{p_+-p_-}{n},
\]
and \(r_0=m_{(0,0)}+m_{(1,1)}\). This aggregation is valid for the target only. In the orbit likelihood, the factors \(m_t!\) and the row constraints \(\sum_a h_{t,a}=m_t\) remain indexed separately by all four response types. Thus neither the likelihood formula nor the exact-game theorem identifies \(P_m(x\mid r)\) through \((p_+,p_-,r_0)\) alone.

The exact response-type game theorem gives \(\rho_n((1,-1))=G_n((1,-1))\). The embedded two-arm converse theorem applies with \(|S_c|=2\) and \(C_0(c)=1\); its equality clause confirms that this is exactly the unrestricted binary risk used by the cited two-arm theorem. That cited result, reached through the helper theorem, equates the minimax value with the scalar experiment
\[
 X_2=p_++\operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)
\]
for estimating \((p_+-p_-)/n\), and gives
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))=n^{-1}-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Since \(L_c=2\), one has \(C_0(c)=1\), and consequently
\[
 d_n(c)=C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
This is a minimax-value composition; it does not collapse the four-count orbit likelihood.

For every fixed \(K\geq3\) and every nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), the exact response-type game theorem applies to the unrestricted assignment and estimator classes and proves
\[
 \rho_n(c)=G_n(c),
\]
with nature ranging over all \(2^K\) response types. The published cited theorem concerns only the two-arm scalar experiment, whereas this equality covers every such multi-arm labeled-schedule game, including dependent designs and possibly biased estimators. This is the asserted strict extension in treatment-domain scope.
