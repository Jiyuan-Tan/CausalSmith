\paragraph{Proof.}
We first prove the finite-sample upper bound.  Put
\[
 \widetilde\tau=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i,
 \qquad
 Z_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}
 \frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a^\star}
 \left(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-\frac12\right).
\]
Because \(c\neq0\), one has \(L_c>0\).  For every \(a\in S_c\), the definition
\(q_a^\star=|c_a|/L_c\) gives \(q_a^\star>0\), so every displayed division is valid.  No division is made for \(a\notin S_c\), and the design may assign those zero-coefficient arms probability zero.

Fix a response schedule \(z\).  Under the independent design in \(\mathrm{def:first\text{-}order\text{-}procedure}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Z_i\mid z)
 &=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a(t_{i,a}-1/2)\\
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}=:\mu_i.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses \(c_a=0\) off \(S_c\) and \(\sum_a c_a=0\).  Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(\widetilde\tau\mid z)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mu_i=\tau_c(z).
\]
Since each \(t_{i,a}\) is binary, \((t_{i,a}-1/2)^2=1/4\), and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Z_i^2\mid z)
 &=\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a^\star}
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}L_c|c_a|\\
 &=\frac{L_c^2}{4}=C_0(c).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The assignments, and therefore the \(Z_i\), are independent across units conditional on the fixed schedule.  Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_n(\mathcal D,\widetilde\tau;z)
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D}(Z_i\mid z)\\
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\{C_0(c)-\mu_i^2\}
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
 \end{aligned}
\]

To verify the estimator's range, let \(P_c=\{a:c_a>0\}\) and \(N_c=\{a:c_a<0\}\).  The zero-sum identity implies
\[
 \sum_{a\in P_c}c_a=-\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a=\frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
For every binary response type \(t_i\), therefore,
\[
 -\frac{L_c}{2}\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}\leq\frac{L_c}{2},
\]
and averaging over units gives \(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\).  Metric projection onto a closed interval cannot increase squared distance from any point of that interval.  It follows that the projected estimator \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star\) belongs to the stated action space and obeys
\[
 \max_{z\in\mathcal T_K^n}
 R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star;z)
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
In particular, \(\rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c)/n\).

We next prove a lower bound uniform over all assignment laws, including dependent laws, and all possibly biased estimators.  Since \(K\) is fixed, \(s:=|S_c|\) is fixed.  Let
\[
 \epsilon_n=\frac14n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 w(u)=\frac{15}{16}(1-u^2)^2\mathbf 1\{|u|\leq1\},
 \qquad
 J=\int_{-1}^1\frac{\{w'(u)\}^2}{w(u)}\,du<\infty.
\]
For every \(a\in S_c\), independently draw \(P_a\) with density
\[
 p\longmapsto\epsilon_n^{-1}w\!\left(\frac{p-1/2}{\epsilon_n}\right),
\]
and set \(P_a=1/2\) for \(a\notin S_c\).  Independently draw \(U_1,\ldots,U_n\) identically and uniformly on \([0,1]\), and define the fixed binary schedule
\[
 Y_i(a)=\mathbf 1\{U_i\leq P_a\}.
\]
After \((P,U)\) is drawn this is an element of \(\mathcal T_K^n\), so the construction induces a legitimate prior on the finite schedule space.  It is drawn independently of the design, as required because the assignment law is chosen without knowledge of the response schedule.

Define
\[
 \vartheta(P)=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_aP_a,
 \qquad
 g_P(u)=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a\mathbf 1\{u\leq P_a\}.
\]
Conditional on \(P\),
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^ng_P(U_i),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{\tau_c(z)\mid P\}=\vartheta(P).
\]
Because \(\sum_a c_a=0\), the function \(g_P\) vanishes outside
\([\min_{a\in S_c}P_a,\max_{a\in S_c}P_a]\), whose length is at most \(2\epsilon_n\).  Also \(|g_P(u)|\leq L_c/2\).  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e_n^2
 &:=\mathbb E\{\tau_c(z)-\vartheta(P)\}^2\\
 &=\frac1n\mathbb E\{\operatorname{Var}(g_P(U_1)\mid P)\}
 \leq\frac{L_c^2\epsilon_n}{2n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(e_n=O(n^{-5/8})=o(n^{-1/2})\).

Fix an arbitrary assignment law \(\mathcal D\), and let
\[
 r_a=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\},
 \qquad s_a=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}r_a.
\]
Conditional on \((P,A)\), the observed outcomes are independent and satisfy
\(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_{A_i})\).  Symmetry and compact support of \(w\) give
\[
 \kappa_n:=\mathbb E\!\left\{\frac1{P_a(1-P_a)}\right\}
 =4+O(\epsilon_n^2).
\]
For \(a\in S_c\), let \(S_a\) be the score, with respect to \(P_a\), of the joint prior--likelihood density of \((P,A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The conditional mean of each Bernoulli likelihood score is zero.  Independence of the arm priors and conditional independence of the observations therefore make the score covariance matrix diagonal, with
\[
 I_a:=\mathbb E(S_a^2)
 =\kappa_ns_a+\frac{J}{\epsilon_n^2}>0.
\]
The prior-information term makes the denominator strictly positive even if \(s_a=0\); the converse therefore needs no exposure-positivity condition.

Let \(T=T(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) be any admissible, possibly biased estimator.  The density \(w\) and its derivative vanish at both endpoints.  Integration by parts in \(P_a\), with no boundary term, gives
\[
 \mathbb E(TS_a)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{\vartheta(P)S_a\}=-c_a,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\{T-\vartheta(P)\}S_a]=c_a.
\]
For every vector \(v=(v_a)_{a\in S_c}\), Cauchy--Schwarz and score diagonality yield
\[
 \mathbb E\{T-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 \geq\frac{(v^\top c)^2}{\sum_{a\in S_c}v_a^2I_a}.
\]
Taking \(v_a=c_a/I_a\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\((|c_a|/\sqrt{I_a})_a\) and \((\sqrt{I_a})_a\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{T-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 &\geq\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{I_a}
 \geq\frac{L_c^2}{\sum_{a\in S_c}I_a}\\
 &\geq\frac{L_c^2}{\kappa_n n+sJ/\epsilon_n^2}
 =\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sum_{a\in S_c}s_a\leq n\), \(\kappa_n=4+O(n^{-1/2})\), and \(sJ/\epsilon_n^2=O(n^{1/2})\).  All remainder bounds depend only on the fixed contrast and fixed \(K\), not on \(\mathcal D\) or \(T\).

The reverse triangle inequality in \(L^2\) now gives, uniformly over \(\mathcal D\) and \(T\),
\[
 \left[\mathbb E\{T-\tau_c(z)\}^2\right]^{1/2}
 \geq
 \frac{L_c}{\sqrt{\kappa_n n+sJ/\epsilon_n^2}}-e_n.
\]
The first term is asymptotic to \(L_c/(2\sqrt n)\), while \(e_n=o(n^{-1/2})\).  Squaring the positive part proves
\[
 \mathbb E\{T-\tau_c(z)\}^2
 \geq\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}),
\]
uniformly over all allowed procedures.  The maximum over fixed schedules dominates the average under the constructed schedule prior.  Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal D\) and \(T\) proves
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
\]
Together with the finite-sample upper bound,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n\rho_n(c)
 =C_0(c)=\frac{L_c^2}{4}
 =\frac14\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a|\right)^2.
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}response\text{-}type\text{-}game}\), the same value and procedure certificate apply to the explicit orbit game \(G_n(c)\).

For completeness, the allocation has a direct convex certificate.  For every allocation vector \(q\) with \(q_a>0\) on \(S_c\),
\[
 \frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}
 \geq\frac14\frac{\left(\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|\right)^2}
 {\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a}
 \geq\frac{L_c^2}{4}.
\]
Equality holds exactly when no probability is assigned outside \(S_c\) and \(q_a\) is proportional to \(|c_a|\) on \(S_c\), namely \(q=q^\star\).  This is the first-order optimality certificate for the displayed independent centered Horvitz--Thompson family.

As a boundary check, at \(n=1\) the exact identity is
\[
 R_1(\mathcal D,\widetilde\tau;z)=C_0(c)-\mu_1^2\leq C_0(c),
\]
and projection can only reduce it.  Zero-probability inactive arms cause no division, while every active arm has strictly positive \(q_a^\star\).  No positivity, overlap, rank, or support condition is imposed on the arbitrary designs covered by the converse.

The comparison with Kallus is one of decision class and adversary, not an equality of results.  Kallus minimizes worst-case variance of a fixed design-unbiased two-arm sample-average-treatment-effect estimator over a covariate-structured conditional-mean class.  The present theorem instead minimizes worst-case squared error jointly over every fixed-\(K\) assignment law and every possibly biased estimator against fixed binary response schedules; conversely, it neither adopts Kallus's covariate-structured mean class nor imposes his inference-uniformity restriction.
