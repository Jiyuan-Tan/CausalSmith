The attainment problem is already solved when \(|S_c|=2\). After relabeling the two active arms and multiplying the published two-arm rule by \(L_c/2\), the embedded two-arm equality and the published construction give an exact finite-\(n\) saddle: independent assignment supported on \(S_c\) with probability \(1/2\) on each active arm, the coefficient-rescaled nonlinear posterior-mean estimator, and the correspondingly lifted least-favorable prior. This assignment is \(q^\star\), its minimax risk is \(\rho_n(c)\), and its coefficient-scaled Airy converse is \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\). Consequently, the unresolved construction and attainment question is restricted to nonzero zero-sum contrasts with \(|S_c|\geq3\). The universal second-order rate theorem fixes the normalization at \(n^{4/3}\): independent assignment with \(q^\star\) and the explicit clipped-shrinkage rule stated in that theorem achieve worst-case risk at most \(C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_c n^{-4/3}\}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(\kappa_c>0\) is explicit. Thus a contrast-weighted rule already gives a uniform positive \(n^{-4/3}\) improvement, but this does not prove that \(q^\star\) is second-order optimal or determine the sharp constant. At the established \(n^{4/3}\) scale, construct a finite-\(n\) multi-arm procedure and a least-favorable-prior sequence whose upper and lower risk corrections match along the full sequence if the frontier converges, or along every characterized sharp subsequence otherwise. Decide whether independent assignment with \(q^\star\) remains second-order optimal; if it does, derive the analytic multivariate nonlinear shrinkage rule, and if it does not, characterize the necessary finite-\(n\) design correction and certify its improvement in \(G_n(c)\). For \(c=c^\dagger\), the exact rational grid certificates already provide, for every \(n,M\geq1\), a computable multivariate orbit rule and a rational-prior lower certificate separated by at most \(1/(4M^2)\). Hence every integer sequence \((M_n)\) satisfying \(n^{4/3}/M_n^2\to0\), for example \(M_n=n\), makes the certificate gap \(o(n^{-4/3})\). These certificates do not decide sharp second-order optimality of \(q^\star\), the sharp constant, an analytic limiting feedback rule, or convergence of a least-favorable-prior sequence; computations for \(n\leq5\) are evidence only.
