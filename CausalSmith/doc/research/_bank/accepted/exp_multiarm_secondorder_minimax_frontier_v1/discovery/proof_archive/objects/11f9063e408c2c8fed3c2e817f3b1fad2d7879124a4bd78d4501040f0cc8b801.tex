\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(c\) as in the statement. The support condition \(|S_c|\geq3\) is part of the scope of the open question, but the cited universal theorem in fact applies to every nonzero zero-sum contrast. Let \(\kappa_c>0\) be its explicit shrinkage constant. That theorem gives
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)C_A<\infty.
\]
The first and last quantities are finite and positive because \(c\neq0\) implies \(L_c>0\), hence \(C_0(c)=L_c^2/4>0\), while \(\kappa_c>0\) and \(C_A>0\). This proves the displayed bracket and \(d_n(c)=\Theta(n^{-4/3})\).

Put \(x_n=x_n(c)\), \(\alpha_c=C_0(c)\kappa_c\), and \(\beta_c=C_0(c)C_A\). There is an integer \(N\) such that
\[
 \frac{\alpha_c}{2}\leq x_n\leq2\beta_c
 \qquad(n\geq N).
\]
Define
\[
 \mathcal L(c)=\bigcap_{N'\geq N}
 \overline{\{x_n:n\geq N'\}}.
\]
Each set in this intersection is a nonempty compact subset of \([\alpha_c/2,2\beta_c]\), and the sets are nested. The nested-compact-set property therefore makes \(\mathcal L(c)\) nonempty and compact. By the usual tail-closure characterization, it is exactly the set of subsequential limits. The liminf--limsup bracket further gives
\[
 \mathcal L(c)\subseteq[\alpha_c,\beta_c]\subset(0,\infty).
\]

We next characterize possible normalizers. Suppose \((a_n)\) is positive and regularly varying with index \(\gamma\), and
\[
 a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C\in(0,\infty).
\]
The universal second-order theorem gives \(\gamma=4/3\). Hence
\[
 \ell_n=a_nn^{-4/3}
\]
is slowly varying and \(\ell_nx_n\to C\). For every \(s>0\), eventual positivity and the definition of slow variation yield
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}{x_n}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}
 {\ell_nx_n}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus \((x_n)\) is slowly varying. Conversely, if \((x_n)\) is slowly varying, its eventual positive lower bound permits us, after assigning arbitrary positive values at finitely many initial indices, to set
\[
 \ell_n=\frac{C}{x_n},
 \qquad a_n=n^{4/3}\ell_n.
\]
Then \((\ell_n)\) is slowly varying, \((a_n)\) is regularly varying with index \(4/3\), and \(a_nd_n(c)=C\) eventually. This proves the equivalence. If \((x_n)\) is nonconvergent, then its reciprocal \((\ell_n)\) is nonconvergent because \((x_n)\) is bounded above and away from zero. This proves the assertion about the genuinely nonconvergent correction.

Now suppose that \(c\) is rational and specialize the rational certificate theorem to \(M=n\). Abbreviate
\[
 B_n=B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,n,c}),\qquad
 U_n=U_{n,n}(c),\qquad
 \Lambda_n=\Lambda_{n,n}(c).
\]
Its finite-sample sandwich is
\[
 B_n\leq\rho_n(c)\leq U_n\leq\Lambda_n
 \leq B_n+\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]
Define the four normalized improvements
\[
 J_n^{\mathrm{grid}}=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-\Lambda_n\right\},
 \quad
 J_n^{\mathrm{proc}}=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-U_n\right\},
\]
\[
 J_n^{\mathrm{risk}}=x_n,
 \quad
 J_n^{\mathrm{prior}}=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-B_n\right\}.
\]
Subtracting the risk sandwich from \(C_0(c)/n\) gives
\[
 J_n^{\mathrm{grid}}\leq J_n^{\mathrm{proc}}
 \leq J_n^{\mathrm{risk}}\leq J_n^{\mathrm{prior}},
\]
and the diameter of these four numbers is bounded by
\[
 0\leq J_n^{\mathrm{prior}}-J_n^{\mathrm{grid}}
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}4n^{-2/3}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Their pairwise distances are therefore \(o(1)\). Two real sequences at distance \(o(1)\) have the same finite subsequential limits: convergence of one along a subsequence implies convergence of the other to the same value, and the converse follows by symmetry. They also converge simultaneously and to the same limit. This proves the rational assertion.

For a real contrast, the contrast-continuity and real-contrast transfer theorems provide nonzero rational zero-sum approximants \(c^{(n)}\) satisfying
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c^{(n)}}}{2}=o(n^{-5/6}),
\]
and, with \(M=n\), computable endpoints \(R_n^-\) and \(R_n^+\) such that
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
 \qquad R_n^+-R_n^-=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Define
\[
 J_n^-=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+\right\},
 \qquad
 J_n^+=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
 J_n^-\leq x_n\leq J_n^+,
 \qquad J_n^+-J_n^-=o(1).
\]
Hence both endpoint sequences are at distance \(o(1)\) from \((x_n)\), proving equality of their subsequential-limit sets and simultaneous convergence behavior.

The exact-rational certificate theorem keeps every \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}\) as a risk constraint, including schedules composed of all-zero or all-one response types, and its design action ranges over arbitrary mixtures of allocation orbits; its construction assumes no positive allocation coordinate, overlap, or rank condition. The real-contrast transfer uses projection and a uniform target perturbation, so it introduces none of those restrictions. For \(K=3\) and \(c=c^\dagger\), the three-arm certificate theorem identifies the general rational objects with the exact three-arm program. The scalar-score proposition separately gives a strict finite-sample witness at \(n=3\) showing that the sign-score compression is not minimax-preserving.

All bounds above compare a procedure and a prior certificate at the same population size \(n\). They furnish no cross-population relation between \(x_n(c)\) and \(x_{n+1}(c)\), no compactness or convergence statement for the optimizing designs or priors, and no derived limiting generator with compatible face and corner boundary conditions. Accordingly, the proved conclusions stop at the rate, compact subsequential-set statement, slow-variation equivalence, and scale-matched certificates asserted in the theorem; the listed sharp-limit and limiting-operator questions remain unresolved. \(\square\)
