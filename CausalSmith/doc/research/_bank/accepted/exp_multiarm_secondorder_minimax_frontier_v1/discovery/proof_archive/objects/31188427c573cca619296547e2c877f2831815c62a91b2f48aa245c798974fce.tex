Let
\[
 \widetilde\tau=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i,
 \qquad
 Z_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a^\star}
 \bigl(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1/2\bigr)
\]
be the unprojected rule in the contrast-weighted procedure.  Since \(c\ne0\), one has \(L_c>0\).  For every \(a\in S_c\),
\[
 q_a^\star=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}>0,
\]
so every displayed inverse is well defined.  The assignments are independent across units under the stated procedure.  Conditional on the fixed schedule \(z\), the assignment identity \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=t_{i,A_i}\) and the contrast identity \(\sum_a c_a=0\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Z_i\mid z)
 &=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a(t_{i,a}-1/2)\\
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}
 =:\mu_i,
 \end{aligned}
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(\widetilde\tau\mid z)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mu_i=\tau_c(z).
\]
Because every potential outcome is binary, \((t_{i,a}-1/2)^2=1/4\).  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Z_i^2\mid z)
 &=\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a^\star}
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}L_c|c_a|\\
 &=\frac{L_c^2}{4}=C_0(c).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Independence of the assignments makes \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) independent conditional on \(z\), so the variance identity yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\!\left[\{\widetilde\tau-\tau_c(z)\}^2\mid z\right]
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D}(Z_i\mid z)\\
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\{C_0(c)-\mu_i^2\}
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
 \end{aligned}
\]

We next verify the boundary condition used by projection.  Since \(c\) is zero-sum,
\[
 \sum_{a:c_a>0}c_a=\sum_{a:c_a<0}|c_a|=\frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
For any binary vector \((t_{i,a})_a\), selecting any subset of its positive and negative coefficients therefore gives
\[
 -\frac{L_c}{2}\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}\leq\frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
Averaging over \(i\) proves \(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\) for every schedule.  Metric projection onto this closed interval cannot increase squared distance from a point in the interval.  Consequently,
\[
 \max_{z\in\mathcal T_K^n}
 R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star;z)
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n,
\]
and the projected estimator is an admissible member of the frozen action space.  In particular,
\[
 \rho_n(c)\leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]

For the converse, invoke Theorem \(\text{thm:embedded-two-arm-converse}\), which applies for every \(n\), every nonzero zero-sum contrast, every assignment law, and every possibly biased estimator.  It gives the finite-sample inequality
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
The cited two-arm theorem \(\text{lem:published-two-arm-minimax-airy}\) gives
\[
 \rho_{n,2}=\frac1n-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
and in particular \(n\rho_{n,2}\to1\).  Combining the finite-sample lower and upper bounds gives
\[
 C_0(c)n\rho_{n,2}\leq n\rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c).
\]
The squeeze theorem now proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n\rho_n(c)=C_0(c)
 =\frac{L_c^2}{4}
 =\frac14\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a|\right)^2.
\]
Moreover, for any sequence of designs and estimators, its worst-case risk is at least \(\rho_n(c)\), and the preceding two-arm expansion implies
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq\frac{C_0(c)}n-C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
 =\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
\]
This is the stated unrestricted asymptotic converse.

Finally, the allocation rule also has a direct first-order optimality certificate within the centered independent-assignment family.  If \(q_a>0\) on \(S_c\) and \(\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a\leq1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \left(\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|\right)^2
 \leq\left(\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}\right)
 \left(\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a\right)
 \leq\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}.
\]
Thus the worst-case second-moment bound is at least \(L_c^2/4\), with equality precisely when all allocation mass is on \(S_c\) and \(q_a=|c_a|/L_c\).  No positivity is required for inactive arms, and no rank, overlap, or matrix-inverse condition is used.
