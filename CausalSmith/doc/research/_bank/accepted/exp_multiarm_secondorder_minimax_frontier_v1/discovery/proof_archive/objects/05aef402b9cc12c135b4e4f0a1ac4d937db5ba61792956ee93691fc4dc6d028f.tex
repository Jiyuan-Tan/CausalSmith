Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2026, Theorems 2.1--2.2 and 3.1, with their upper and lower constructions) prove the unrestricted binary two-arm scalar minimax value, independent half-Bernoulli attainment, nonlinear shrinkage, a least-favorable converse, and the Airy expansion.  The present sign-group embedding turns that result into a finite-\(n\) lower bound for every multi-arm contrast and an exact result whenever \(|S_c|=2\); this is stronger than using the binary theorem only as a \(K=2\) regression check.  It does not identify the full \(2^K\)-type likelihood with the scalar kernel.  Hull (2026) supplies a distinct two-arm coordinate-swap construction and does not resolve the \(|S_c|\geq3\) orbit game.  Bai (2023) motivates minimax allocation under permutation invariance in a different, linear framework.  Levit (1986) supplies spectral language but no stratified simplex convergence theorem.  Rosenman and Hunter (2026) motivate multi-arm shrinkage in a Gaussian model without a fixed-population unrestricted converse.  Mukerjee, Dasgupta, and Rubin (2018) provide the multiple-treatment finite-population contrast framework.  Hammer et al. (1996) supply ACTG 175 as the four-arm consumer.
