\textbf{Proof.}
Write
\[
 \alpha=\frac{L_c}{2}=\sqrt{C_0(c)}.
\]
Because \(c\) is nonzero and has zero sum, \(L_c>0\), so \(\alpha>0\) and every scaling by \(\alpha\) below is well defined.

Suppose first that \(|S_c|=2\).  The zero-sum condition forces the two active coefficients, after relabeling, to be \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\).  Hence
\[
 q_a^\star=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}=\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha}=\frac12
\]
on each active arm.  By the published two-arm attainment result, balanced independent assignment, the published posterior mean, and its least-favorable prior form an exact finite-\(n\) saddle for the contrast \((1,-1)\).  Multiplying that estimator by \(\alpha\) multiplies squared loss by \(\alpha^2=C_0(c)\).  The reverse reduction in the embedded two-arm theorem therefore supplies an admissible \(K\)-arm procedure with worst-case risk \(C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\), while the forward reduction supplies the matching lower bound
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
Thus equality holds.

For completeness, the prior also lifts as a genuine unrestricted-design converse rather than merely as a value identity.  Embed every schedule in the support of the published two-arm least-favorable prior by copying its positive-arm outcome to the positive active arm, copying its negative-arm outcome to the negative active arm, and fixing every inactive-arm outcome at zero.  The forward reduction in the embedded two-arm theorem turns any \(K\)-arm design and estimator into a two-arm procedure and, by conditional Jensen's inequality, makes the latter's squared loss no larger than the former's loss divided by \(\alpha^2\), schedule by schedule.  Averaging over the published least-favorable prior shows that every \(K\)-arm procedure has Bayes risk at least \(\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\) under the embedded prior.  The lifted balanced procedure attains that bound, proving the asserted exact saddle.  Finally,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_n(c)
 &=\frac{C_0(c)}n-C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\\
 &=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}\\
 &=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is the cited two-arm Airy expansion.  This proves the first assertion, including exact positivity on the two active arms and the lawfulness of zero assignment probability on inactive arms.

Now allow arbitrary \(S_c\).  The universal second-order rate theorem applies under precisely the frozen fixed-\(K\), binary-potential-outcome model.  Its explicit independent \(q^\star\) assignment has \(q_a^\star>0\) for every \(a\in S_c\), and its estimator is a measurable map of the realized assignments and observed outcomes into the target interval.  That theorem proves, rather than assumes,
\[
 \max_z R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_c n^{-4/3}\}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Taking the infimum over all designs and estimators gives
\[
 d_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\kappa_c n^{-4/3}.
\]
The embedded two-arm theorem, which permits dependent designs and biased estimators, gives the opposite order bound
\[
 d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Multiplication by \(n^{4/3}\) and passage to lower and upper limits proves the second assertion.  No assignment positivity is required off \(S_c\), because those coefficients are zero; all boundary schedules, including the all-zero and all-one response types, remain in the maximum over \(z\).

It remains to verify the computable three-arm matching statement.  For \(c=c^\dagger\), one has \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\), and the grid-certificate theorem gives, for every \(n,M\geq1\),
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac{1}{4M^2}.
\]
Subtracting these inequalities from \(n^{-1}\), with the inequality directions reversed, yields
\[
 n^{-1}-U_{n,M}
 \leq d_n(c^\dagger)
 \leq n^{-1}-B_n(\nu_{n,M}).
\]
The difference between the two endpoints is \(U_{n,M}-B_n(\nu_{n,M})\), which is nonnegative and at most \(1/(4M^2)\).  Substituting \(M=M_n\) and multiplying by \(n^{4/3}\) proves the displayed vanishing-gap bound.

If \((n_j)\) is any subsequence such that
\[
 n_j^{4/3}d_{n_j}(c^\dagger)\longrightarrow \ell,
\]
then the two scaled certificate corrections bracket this sequence and differ by a quantity converging to zero.  The squeeze theorem gives
\[
 n_j^{4/3}\underline d_{n_j}\longrightarrow\ell,
 \qquad
 n_j^{4/3}\overline d_{n_j}\longrightarrow\ell.
\]
This covers the full sequence by taking \(n_j=j\) when the frontier converges.  For \(M_n=n\), the gap is
\[
 \frac{n^{4/3}}{4n^2}=\frac{1}{4n^{2/3}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The finite procedure and prior are explicit outputs of a rational linear program, and the grid-certificate theorem proves that the lower certificate is valid against unrestricted designs and estimators.  Thus the matching is a genuine design--estimator upper certificate and prior Bayes-risk converse, not an assumed asymptotic limit law.

None of these inequalities equates the risk of the independent \(q^\star\) procedure with \(\rho_n(c)\) at second order when \(|S_c|\geq3\), and the certificate sandwich does not by itself imply convergence of either the optimal designs or the dual priors.  The final sentence of the theorem therefore records the exact boundary of this partial answer rather than asserting an unproved sharp analytic attainment result. \(\square\)
