\paragraph{Proof.}
By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}signed\text{-}contrast\text{-}stabilizer}\), for every \(\sigma\in\mathfrak G_c\) there is a sign \(s_\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) such that
\[
c_{\sigma(a)}=s_\sigma c_a
\qquad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A_K.
\]
Consequently, if \(a\in S_c\), then
\[
|c_{\sigma(a)}|=|s_\sigma c_a|=|c_a|>0,
\]
so \(\sigma(a)\in S_c\). Thus the action preserves the active support.

Suppose now that this action is transitive on \(S_c\). For any \(a,b\in S_c\), transitivity supplies \(\sigma\in\mathfrak G_c\) with \(\sigma(a)=b\). The preceding identity gives
\[
|c_b|=|c_{\sigma(a)}|=|c_a|.
\]
Hence there is a number \(d>0\) such that \(|c_a|=d\) for every \(a\in S_c\). Define
\[
N_+=\#\{a\in S_c:c_a>0\},
\qquad
N_-=\#\{a\in S_c:c_a<0\}.
\]
Because \(c\) is a contrast, \(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a=0\), while coefficients outside \(S_c\) vanish. Therefore
\[
0=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a
 =\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a
 =dN_+-dN_-
 =d(N_+-N_-).
\]
Division by \(d>0\) yields \(N_+=N_-\).

For the diagnostic contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), let \(\sigma\in\mathfrak G_{c^\dagger}\) and let \(s\) be its associated sign. If \(s=-1\), then the defining relation at \(a=1\) would require
\[
c^\dagger_{\sigma(1)}=-c^\dagger_1=-1,
\]
but none of the three coordinates of \(c^\dagger\) equals \(-1\). Hence \(s=1\). The value \(1\) occurs at the unique arm \(1\), so
\[
c^\dagger_{\sigma(1)}=c^\dagger_1=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma(1)=1.
\]
The permutation \(\sigma\) may therefore only fix or exchange arms \(2\) and \(3\). Conversely, both the identity and the transposition \((2\ 3)\) preserve \(c^\dagger\). It follows that
\[
\mathfrak G_{c^\dagger}=\{\operatorname{id},(2\ 3)\},
\]
whose arm orbits are exactly \(\{1\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\). A quotient whose equivalence relation is generated solely by this group can identify only elements in the same group orbit, and hence cannot merge arm \(1\) with arm \(2\) or arm \(3\).

\paragraph{Scope and sanity checks.}
This argument uses only that \(K\) is finite and fixed and that \(c\) is a nonzero, zero-sum contrast; there are no probabilistic support, positivity, overlap, rank, asymptotic, or boundary assumptions. For \(c^\dagger\), the unequal active magnitudes and the unequal sign counts also independently rule out transitivity by the first part. The conclusion concerns only quotients generated by signed contrast-preserving arm relabelings, so it makes no assertion about coordinatewise swap constructions or other possible scalar representations.
