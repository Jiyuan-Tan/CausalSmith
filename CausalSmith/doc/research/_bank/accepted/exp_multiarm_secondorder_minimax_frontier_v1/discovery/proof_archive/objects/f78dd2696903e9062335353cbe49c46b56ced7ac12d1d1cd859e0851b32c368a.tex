\textbf{Proof.}

\paragraph{The two-arm binary regression clause.}
Set \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)\).  The orbit form of the target is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau_c(m)
 &=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^2}m_t(t_1-t_2)\\
 &=\frac{m_{(1,0)}-m_{(0,1)}}{n}
 =\frac{p_+-p_-}{n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Also \(r_0=m_{(0,0)}+m_{(1,1)}\), and therefore
\[
 p_++p_-+r_0=n.
\]
This aggregation is an identity for the target, not an identity for the likelihood.  Indeed, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}orbit\text{-}likelihood}\) gives
\[
 P_m(x\mid r)
 =\frac{\prod_{a\in\mathcal A_2}r_a!}{n!}
 \sum_{h\in\mathcal H(m,r,x)}
 \prod_{t\in\mathcal T_2}
 \frac{m_t!}{\prod_{a\in\mathcal A_2}h_{t,a}!}.
\]
Both the factors \(m_t!\) and the row restrictions
\[
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_2}h_{t,a}=m_t
\]
remain separately indexed by \(t=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\).  Thus neither the likelihood definition nor the exact response-type game theorem claims that \(P_m(x\mid r)\) is determined by \((p_+,p_-,r_0)\).

The exact response-type game theorem yields
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))=G_n((1,-1)).
\]
The equality clause of the embedded two-arm converse applies because \(|S_c|=2\).  Here
\[
 L_c=|1|+|-1|=2,
 \qquad
 C_0(c)=\frac{L_c^2}{4}=1,
\]
so the orbit-game value equals the unrestricted binary two-arm value \(\rho_{n,2}\).  The published result incorporated in the embedded converse identifies that minimax value, at the value level, with the scalar experiment
\[
 X_2=p_++\operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)
\]
for estimating \((p_+-p_-)/n\), and gives
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))
 =\frac1n-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Using \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)/n-\rho_n(c)\) therefore gives
\[
 d_n((1,-1))=C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
This composition equates minimax values only and does not collapse the four-count likelihood.

\paragraph{The fixed multi-arm binary extension.}
Now fix \(K\geq3\) and a nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\).  The exact response-type game theorem permits every assignment law \(\mathcal D\in\Delta(\mathcal A_K^n)\), including dependent assignments, and every measurable clipped estimator, including biased estimators.  It proves
\[
 \rho_n(c)=G_n(c),
\]
where nature ranges over \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}\), the count simplex formed from all
\[
 |\mathcal T_K|=2^K
\]
binary response types, and the payoff is evaluated with the explicit joint kernel \(P_m(x\mid r)\).  Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen's cited scalar theorem is a two-arm binary theorem, so this equality for every fixed \(K\geq3\) is new on the number-of-arms axis.

The cited Hull scope result draws the second necessary boundary: Hull optimizes over unrestricted assignment mechanisms and measurable estimators for two bounded-outcome arms.  The present theorem also permits unrestricted designs and estimators, but its \(K\geq3\) extension assumes binary response schedules.  Consequently, ``strict treatment-domain extension'' means extension beyond two arms within the binary model; it does not mean that the present model contains Hull's two-arm bounded-outcome model, and it does not prove any bounded-outcome result for \(K\geq3\).  Nor does the orbit-game equality rule out a different scalar representation of the multi-arm minimax value.

\paragraph{Support and boundary conditions.}
No exposure-positivity, overlap, rank, or inverse condition is imposed.  Allocation cells with \(r_a=0\) are legal in \(\mathcal R_{n,K}\), and the all-zero and all-one response types remain in \(\mathcal T_K\).  The only boundary restrictions are the frozen ones: \(K\) and \(n\) are finite, \(c\) is nonzero and zero-sum, potential outcomes are binary, the schedule is fixed before assignment, and the estimator is clipped to the attainable target interval.  These facts complete the stated scope comparison and the regression check. \(\square\)+
