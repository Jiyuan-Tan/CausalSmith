For a finite population of \(N\) fixed values \(y_j\in[\ell_j,u_j]\), put \(r_j=(u_j-\ell_j)/2\). For every sampling design having inclusion probabilities \(\pi_j>0\) and every design-unbiased estimator of the finite-population total, the worst-case squared error is at least
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^N r_j^2\frac{1-\pi_j}{\pi_j}.
\]
The midpoint-differenced Horvitz--Thompson estimator attains equality exactly when the inclusion indicators are pairwise independent for coordinates with \(r_j>0\). Jointly over sampling designs with a stated expected-sample-size budget \(b\) and design-unbiased estimators, the value is the minimum of the displayed expression; it is attained by independent sampling with \(\pi_j^\star=\min\{1,\gamma r_j\}\), where \(\gamma\) is chosen so that \(\sum_j\pi_j^\star=b\). This result concerns unit-inclusion sampling and design-unbiased estimation of a bounded total, not mutually exclusive multi-arm assignment or a decision class containing possibly biased estimators.
