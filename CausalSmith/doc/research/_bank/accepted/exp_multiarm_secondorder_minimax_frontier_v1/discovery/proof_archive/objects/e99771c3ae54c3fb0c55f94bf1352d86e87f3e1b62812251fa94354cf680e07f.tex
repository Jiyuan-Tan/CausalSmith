\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(x_n=x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c)\).  The universal second-order theorem gives a constant \(\kappa_c>0\), a finite \(N_c\), and the bounds
\[
 C_0(c)\kappa_c\leq x_n
 \qquad(n\geq N_c),
\]
and
\[
 x_n\leq C_0(c)C_A+o(1).
\]
Therefore
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n
 \leq C_0(c)C_A<\infty.
\]
This is equivalent to \(d_n(c)=\Theta(n^{-4/3})\).  The sequence \((x_n)\) is eventually contained in a compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\).  Its subsequential-limit set is nonempty by Bolzano--Weierstrass and closed because a limit of subsequential limits can be diagonalized into a subsequence of \((x_n)\).  It is therefore a nonempty compact subset of such an interval.

The universal theorem already proves that if a positive regularly varying sequence \((a_n)\) satisfies
\[
 a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C\in(0,\infty),
\]
then its regular-variation index is \(4/3\).  Define
\[
 \ell_n=a_nn^{-4/3}.
\]
Then \((\ell_n)\) is slowly varying, and
\[
 \ell_nx_n=a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C.
\]
For every fixed \(y>0\), the convergence of the product and slow variation of \(\ell_n\) imply
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}}{x_n}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}}{\ell_nx_n}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus \((x_n)\) is slowly varying.  Conversely, if \((x_n)\) is slowly varying, the positive lower bound makes it eventually positive.  Define
\[
 a_n=\frac{n^{4/3}}{x_n}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), and assign arbitrary positive values at the finitely many earlier indices.  Finite modification does not affect regular variation, so \((a_n)\) is regularly varying with index \(4/3\), and \(a_nd_n(c)=1\) eventually.  This proves the stated equivalence.  If \((x_n)\) does not converge, then its reciprocal is also nonconvergent and slowly varying exactly when \((x_n)\) is; hence the corresponding normalizer is \(n^{4/3}\) times a nonconvergent slowly varying correction.  Conversely, if such a correction yields a finite positive normalized limit while \(x_n\) is nonconvergent, the preceding argument forces \(x_n\) to be slowly varying.

We next compare the computable certificates.  Suppose first that \(c\) is rational, set \(M=n\), and abbreviate
\[
 \Lambda_n=\Lambda_{n,n}(c),
 \qquad U_n=U_{n,n}(c),
 \qquad B_n=B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,n,c}).
\]
Define the four normalized improvements
\[
 X_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)\right\}=x_n,
\]
\[
 G_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-\Lambda_n\right\},
 \quad
 P_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-U_n\right\},
 \quad
 Q_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-B_n\right\}.
\]
The exact rational certificate theorem gives
\[
 B_n\leq\rho_n(c)\leq U_n\leq\Lambda_n
 \leq B_n+\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]
Every pair among \(X_n,G_n,P_n,Q_n\) consequently differs by at most
\[
 n^{4/3}\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}
 =\frac{C_0(c)}4n^{-2/3}=o(1).
\]
Sequences at pointwise distance \(o(1)\) have the same subsequential-limit set; moreover, one converges if and only if all do, and then their limits agree.  Exact rational primal and dual certificates exist at every \(n\), so these are computable statements rather than asymptotic representations involving unspecified optimizers.

For a real contrast, the transfer theorem constructs rational zero-sum \(c^{(n)}\) satisfying
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c^{(n)}}}{2}=o(n^{-5/6})
\]
and, with \(M=n\), risk bounds \(R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+\) for which
\[
 R_n^+-R_n^-=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Thus, for the normalized improvement endpoints
\[
 \underline X_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+\right\},
 \qquad
 \overline X_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-\right\},
\]
one has
\[
 \underline X_n\leq x_n\leq\overline X_n,
 \qquad
 \overline X_n-\underline X_n=o(1).
\]
Each endpoint is therefore at distance \(o(1)\) from \(x_n\), proving equality of subsequential-limit sets and convergence behavior.  The rational finite program includes every \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}\); its allocation mixture may put zero mass on any orbit, posterior terms with zero denominator are simply omitted, and no inverse exposure probability appears.  Hence no exposure-positivity, allocation-support, rank, or overlap condition is imposed on the unrestricted minimax design.

When \(K=3\) and \(c=c^\dagger\), the equality \(\Gamma_{M,c^\dagger}=\Gamma_M\) and \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\) identifies the general rational certificates with the exact three-arm certificates proved above.  Finally, \(\mathrm{prop:k3\text{-}scalar\text{-}score\text{-}not\text{-}minimax\text{-}preserving}\) supplies an explicit \(n=3\) full-data rule whose maximum risk is strictly below a rational lower bound for the compressed sign-score experiment.  Thus that scalar compression cannot replace the full three-arm certificate game.  None of the same-\(n\) bounds supplies a relation between optimizers or priors at successive population sizes, so the convergence, sharp-constant, second-order-design, and limiting-operator questions listed in the theorem remain unresolved. \(\square\)
