\textbf{Established partial theorem.}
Fix a real nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), and put
\[
 x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c),\qquad
 \alpha_c=C_0(c)\kappa_c,\qquad
 \beta_c=C_0(c)C_A,
\]
where \(\kappa_c>0\) is the explicit constant in the universal second-order rate theorem.  Define the subsequential-limit set
\[
 \mathcal L(c)=\bigcap_{N\geq1}
 \overline{\{x_n(c):n\geq N\}}.
\]
Then \(\mathcal L(c)\) is a nonempty compact subset of
\([\alpha_c,\beta_c]\).  In particular, every subsequential limit is finite and strictly positive.

There is an exact answer to the normalizer part of the question.  A regularly varying positive normalizer \(a_n\) can satisfy
\[
 a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C\in(0,\infty)
\]
only if \(a_n=n^{4/3}\ell_n\) for a slowly varying positive sequence \(\ell_n\).  Such an \(\ell_n\) and \(C\) exist if and only if \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying, namely
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}\longrightarrow1
 \qquad\text{for every }s>0.
\]
Consequently, if \(x_n(c)\) does not converge, a nonconstant slowly varying correction produces a finite positive limit exactly when the nonconvergent sequence \(x_n(c)\) is itself slowly varying.

The subsequential characterization is computably certified for every real contrast, not only for rational contrasts.  Choose rational nonzero zero-sum contrasts \(c_n\in\mathbb Q^K\) such that
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c_n}}2=o(n^{-5/6}),
\]
and use the rational program with \(M_n=n\).  Such a sequence exists for every fixed real \(c\).  Abbreviate
\[
 b_n=B_{n,c_n}(\nu_{n,n,c_n}),
 \qquad
 u_n=U_{n,n}(c_n),
\]
and define the fully computable real-contrast risk brackets
\[
 R_n^-=\left(\max\{0,\sqrt{b_n}-\eta_n\}\right)^2,
 \qquad
 R_n^+=\left(\sqrt{u_n}+\eta_n\right)^2.
\]
Then
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
 \qquad
 R_n^+-R_n^-=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Equivalently, the computable improvement brackets
\[
 \underline x_n(c)=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+\right\},
 \qquad
 \overline x_n(c)=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-\right\}
\]
satisfy
\[
 \underline x_n(c)\leq x_n(c)\leq\overline x_n(c),
 \qquad
 \overline x_n(c)-\underline x_n(c)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the two certificate sequences and \(x_n(c)\) have exactly the same subsequential-limit set, converge simultaneously to the same value, and are slowly varying simultaneously.  For \(c=c^\dagger\), one may take \(c_n=c^\dagger\) and \(\eta_n=0\); the brackets then specialize to the exact three-arm rational certificates, and
\[
 n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-\Lambda_{n,n}^\dagger\}
\]
has the same subsequential-limit set and convergence behavior as \(x_n(c^\dagger)\).

\textbf{Proof.}
The universal second-order rate theorem gives
\[
 0<\alpha_c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)\leq\beta_c<\infty.
\]
Hence \(x_n(c)\) is eventually bounded above and bounded away from zero.  Its tail closures are nonempty compact subsets of a common compact interval and are nested.  Their intersection is therefore nonempty and compact, and the standard subsequence characterization of limit points identifies that intersection with \(\mathcal L(c)\).

Suppose that \(a_n\) is regularly varying and that \(a_nd_n(c)\to C\in(0,\infty)\).  The index conclusion of the universal theorem forces the index of \(a_n\) to be \(4/3\).  Therefore \(\ell_n=a_n/n^{4/3}\) is slowly varying and \(\ell_nx_n(c)\to C\).  For every \(s>0\),
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}
 {\ell_nx_n(c)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Conversely, if \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying, its eventual strict positivity permits the definition \(\ell_n=C/x_n(c)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), with arbitrary positive values at the finitely many remaining indices.  Then \(\ell_n\) is slowly varying and \(n^{4/3}\ell_nd_n(c)=C\) eventually.  The two-sided rate bounds show in either direction that \(\ell_n\) is eventually bounded above and bounded away from zero.  If \(x_n(c)\) is nonconvergent, the reciprocal correction \(C/x_n(c)\) is necessarily nonconstant.

We next construct the rational approximants.  For each \(n\), approximate the first \(K-1\) coordinates of \(c\) by rationals \(c_{n,a}\) with total absolute error at most \(n^{-2}/2\), and set
\[
 c_{n,K}=-\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}c_{n,a}.
\]
Then \(c_n\) is rational and has zero sum, while the triangle inequality and \(c_K=-\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}c_a\) give
\[
 L_{c-c_n}\leq
 2\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}|c_a-c_{n,a}|\leq n^{-2}.
\]
Thus \(\eta_n\leq n^{-2}/2=o(n^{-5/6})\).  Since \(c\neq0\) and \(L_{c-c_n}\to0\), the approximants are nonzero for all sufficiently large \(n\); changing finitely many initial approximants makes every \(c_n\) nonzero.  Also
\[
 |L_{c_n}-L_c|\leq L_{c-c_n}=2\eta_n,
\]
so \(C_0(c_n)\to C_0(c)>0\).

The all-real-contrast finite-program certificate theorem, applied with these \(c_n\) and \(M_n=n\), gives
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
\]
and the explicit width estimate
\[
 0\leq R_n^+-R_n^-
 =O\!\left(
 \frac{C_0(c_n)}{n^2}+\eta_nn^{-1/2}+\eta_n^2
 \right).
\]
Here every square root is applied to a nonnegative risk certificate, and the maximum with zero handles the lower boundary.  Since \(C_0(c_n)\) is bounded and \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\), the three terms obey
\[
 n^{-2}=o(n^{-4/3}),\qquad
 \eta_nn^{-1/2}=o(n^{-4/3}),\qquad
 \eta_n^2=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
This proves the asserted \(o(n^{-4/3})\) risk-bracket width.  The invoked certificate theorem also proves that the upper endpoint comes from a projected observed-data rule and that the lower endpoint is valid against every unrestricted design and estimator; thus no new overlap, positivity, support, or rank condition is being assumed.

Subtracting the risk bracket from \(C_0(c)/n\), reversing the inequalities, and multiplying by \(n^{4/3}\) proves the displayed improvement bracket and its vanishing diameter.  Since \(x_n(c)\) is eventually bounded away from zero, both endpoints are eventually positive.  Sequences at additive distance \(o(1)\) from such a sequence have the same subsequential limits and convergence behavior, and their pairwise ratios tend to one.  For any fixed \(s>0\), applying these ratio relations at \(n\) and \(\lfloor sn\rfloor\) proves that slow variation of any one of the three sequences is equivalent to slow variation of the other two.

Finally, for \(c=c^\dagger\), the identities \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\), \(c_n=c^\dagger\), and \(\eta_n=0\) reduce \(R_n^-\) and \(R_n^+\) to \(B_n(\nu_{n,n})\) and \(U_{n,n}\).  The exact three-arm sandwich places \(\Lambda_{n,n}^\dagger\) between these risks with gap at most \(1/(4n^2)\).  Its normalized improvement is therefore at additive distance at most \(1/(4n^{2/3})\) from \(x_n(c^\dagger)\), proving the final claim. \(\square\)
