\emph{Sign-group embedding and the lower bound.}
Define
\[
P_c:=\{a\in\mathcal A_K:c_a>0\},\qquad
N_c:=\{a\in\mathcal A_K:c_a<0\},\qquad
I_c:=\mathcal A_K\setminus(P_c\cup N_c).
\]
Because \(c\ne0\) and \(\sum_a c_a=0\), both \(P_c\) and \(N_c\) are nonempty and
\[
\sum_{a\in P_c}c_a=-\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a=\frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
For a two-arm binary schedule \(y=((Y_i(1),Y_i(0)))_{i=1}^n\), form the \(K\)-arm schedule \(E_cy\) by
\[
t_{i,a}=\begin{cases}
Y_i(1),&a\in P_c,\\
Y_i(0),&a\in N_c,\\
0,&a\in I_c.
\end{cases}
\]
This schedule is admissible because every displayed coordinate is binary. Its causal contrast is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_c(E_cy)
&=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\left\{Y_i(1)\sum_{a\in P_c}c_a
+Y_i(0)\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a\right\}\\
&=\frac{L_c}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n\{Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\}
=\frac{L_c}{2}\tau_2(y),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\tau_2(y):=n^{-1}\sum_i\{Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\}\).

Fix an arbitrary \(K\)-arm procedure \((\mathcal D,f)\). Let \(A\sim\mathcal D\), independently of the fixed schedule, and define the two-arm assignment \(B=\phi(A)\) coordinatewise by
\[
\phi(a)=\begin{cases}
1,&a\in P_c,\\
0,&a\in N_c\cup I_c.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the law of \(B\) is a legitimate design chosen without knowledge of \(y\). Conditional on observing \((B,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) in the two-arm experiment, draw no new data. For every \(A\) in the known conditional law \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal D}(A\mid B)\), construct the pseudo-outcome vector
\[
\widetilde Y_i(A)=
\begin{cases}
Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}},&A_i\in P_c\cup N_c,\\
0,&A_i\in I_c.
\end{cases}
\]
The inactive-arm observation is discarded. It is not used to manufacture \(\widetilde Y_i(A)\). Define the deterministic two-arm estimator
\[
g(B,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})
:=\frac{2}{L_c}\,
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\!\left[
f(A,\widetilde Y(A))\mid B,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}
\right].
\]
The conditional distribution of \(A\) given \(B\) is completely specified by \(\mathcal D\) and is schedule-independent. In addition, \(\widetilde Y(A)\) is a deterministic function of \((A,B,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\). Hence \(g\) is a lawful function of the observed two-arm data. Since \(f\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), one has \(g\in[-1,1]\), the range of \(\tau_2\).

Under the embedded schedule \(E_cy\), the joint law of \((A,\widetilde Y(A))\) is exactly the joint law of the assignment and observed outcomes supplied to the original \(K\)-arm procedure: an assignment to a positive arm reveals \(Y_i(1)\), an assignment to a negative arm reveals \(Y_i(0)\), and an assignment to an inactive arm reveals the fixed value zero. Conditional Jensen's inequality and the preceding target identity therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{n,2}(g;y)
&:=\mathbb E\left[\{g(B,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})-\tau_2(y)\}^2\right]\\
&\le \frac{4}{L_c^2}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\left[
\{f(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})-\tau_c(E_cy)\}^2
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_{z\in\mathcal T_K^n}R_n(\mathcal D,f;z)
\ge \max_y R_n(\mathcal D,f;E_cy)
\ge \frac{L_c^2}{4}\max_y R_{n,2}(g;y)
\ge C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
Taking the infimum over all \((\mathcal D,f)\) proves
\[
\rho_n(c)\ge C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
No positivity or overlap condition is used: assignments to inactive arms are permitted and simply yield discarded observations in the reduction.

\emph{Reverse inequality for support two.}
Suppose \(S_c=\{a_+,a_-\}\), labeled so that \(c_{a_+}=\lambda>0\) and \(c_{a_-}=-\lambda\). Then \(L_c=2\lambda\) and \(C_0(c)=\lambda^2\). Take any two-arm procedure and relabel its treated and control assignments as \(a_+\) and \(a_-\), assigning no unit to an inactive arm. Multiply its estimate by \(\lambda\). For every \(K\)-arm schedule, only the two active potential outcomes enter either the data or the target, and
\[
\tau_c(z)=\lambda\,\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\{Y_i(a_+)-Y_i(a_-)\}.
\]
The lifted estimator belongs to \([-\lambda,\lambda]=[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), and its risk is exactly \(\lambda^2\) times the risk of the two-arm procedure. Taking infima gives
\[
\rho_n(c)\le \lambda^2\rho_{n,2}=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
Together with the lower bound, this proves equality whenever \(|S_c|=2\). Notice that the lifted design lawfully ignores all inactive arms; their potential outcomes can neither affect the target nor enter the data.

\emph{A universal first-order upper bound.}
It remains to prove the nonnegativity of \(d_n(c)\), rather than assume it. Assign units independently with
\[
q_a=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}\quad(a\in S_c),
\qquad q_a=0\quad(a\notin S_c),
\]
and define the unprojected centered Horvitz--Thompson estimate
\[
T=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n W_i,
\qquad
W_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}
\frac{c_a\mathbf1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a}
\{Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1/2\}.
\]
Every denominator is positive because the sum is restricted to \(S_c\). The zero-sum identity gives
\[
\mathbb E[W_i]
=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a\{Y_i(a)-1/2\}
=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_aY_i(a).
\]
For every realized active arm,
\[
|W_i|=
\left|\frac{c_{A_i}}{|c_{A_i}|/L_c}
\{Y_i(A_i)-1/2\}\right|=\frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
The \(W_i\) are independent across units under this product design. Thus
\[
\mathbb E[T]=\tau_c(z),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(T)
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(W_i)
\le\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb E[W_i^2]
=\frac{L_c^2}{4n}=\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
Since \(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), projection of \(T\) onto this interval cannot increase its squared distance from \(\tau_c(z)\). Therefore
\[
\rho_n(c)\le\frac{C_0(c)}n,
\qquad d_n(c)=\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)\ge0.
\]
Combining this upper bound with the embedded converse yields the finite-sample sandwich
\[
0\le d_n(c)
\le C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}.
\]
The cited two-arm theorem gives
\[
n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}=C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Because \(c\) is fixed, multiplication by \(C_0(c)\) preserves the remainder order. If \(|S_c|=2\), the exact equality \(\rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\) makes the upper inequality for \(d_n(c)\) an equality. This proves every assertion.
