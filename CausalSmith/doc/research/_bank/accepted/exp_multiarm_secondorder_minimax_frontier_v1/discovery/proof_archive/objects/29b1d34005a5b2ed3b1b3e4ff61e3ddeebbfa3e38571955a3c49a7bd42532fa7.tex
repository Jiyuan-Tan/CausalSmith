Fix \(K\geq2\) and a nonzero real zero-sum contrast \(c\in\mathbb R^K\).  Let \(c^{(n)}\in\mathbb Q^K\setminus\{0\}\) be zero-sum rational contrasts such that \(c^{(n)}\to c\), let \(M_n\geq1\) be integers, and put
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c^{(n)}}}{2}.
\]
For each \(n\), let
\[
 B_n=B_{n,c^{(n)}}(\nu_{n,M_n,c^{(n)}}),
 \qquad
 U_n=U_{n,M_n}(c^{(n)})
\]
be the exact rational prior and procedure certificates from the rational-contrast grid theorem, and define
\[
 R_n^-=left\{\max(0,\sqrt{B_n}-\eta_n)\right\}^2,
 \qquad
 R_n^+=(\sqrt{U_n}+\eta_n)^2.
\]
Then
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+.
\]
The upper endpoint is attained as a valid worst-case-risk certificate by using the rational finite-program design and projecting its barycenter rule onto \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\).  The lower endpoint is a valid Bayes-risk certificate against every design and estimator, obtained from the same rational response-count prior.  Moreover,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\leq R_n^+-R_n^-
 &\leq \frac{5C_0(c^{(n)})}{4M_n^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c^{(n)})}\,\eta_n n^{-1/2}
 +3\eta_n^2\\
 &=O\!\left(\frac{C_0(c^{(n)})}{M_n^2}
 +\eta_n n^{-1/2}+\eta_n^2\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For every fixed real \(c\), the rational zero-sum approximants may be chosen with \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\).  With that choice and \(M_n=n\),
\[
 R_n^+-R_n^-=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
 \underline d_n=\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+,
 \qquad
 \overline d_n=\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-,
\]
then
\[
 \underline d_n\leq d_n(c)\leq\overline d_n,
 \qquad
 \overline d_n-\underline d_n=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
For rational \(c\), one may take \(c^{(n)}=c\); the certificates are then direct exact-rational finite programs and their gap for \(M_n=n\) is at most \(C_0(c)/(4n^2)\).  Thus scale-matched finite-program procedure and prior certificates exist directly for every rational contrast and, through the displayed approximation brackets, for every real contrast.  This conclusion does not assert convergence of \(n^{4/3}d_n(c)\), convergence of the finite-program optimizers or priors, sharp second-order optimality of \(q^\star\), a sharp constant, an analytic limiting rule, or a limiting operator.
