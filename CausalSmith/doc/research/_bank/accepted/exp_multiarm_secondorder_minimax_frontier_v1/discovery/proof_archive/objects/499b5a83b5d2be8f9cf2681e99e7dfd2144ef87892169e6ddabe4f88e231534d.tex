Restrict attention to genuinely multivariate contrasts with \(|S_c|\geq3\).  The embedded two-arm converse and the contrast-weighted upper rule already imply the universal ceiling
\[
 0\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A.
\]
Determine whether there exists a regularly varying normalizer \(a_n\to\infty\) for which \(a_nd_n(c)\) converges to a finite positive limit.  In that existence branch, determine \(C_{\mathrm{so}}(c)\); in the alternative branch, prove that no regularly varying normalization has a finite positive limit and identify the correct asymptotic behavior.  Derive, rather than posit, the limiting value problem and its unique admissible solution, and decide whether the active simplex faces and corners yield a genuinely multidimensional free-boundary Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman problem, an elliptic cone spectral problem, or a separable collection of scalar layers.  For \(c=c^\dagger\), the conclusion must agree with certified rational LP scaling; the finite \(n\leq5\) certificates and the scalar-score obstruction are evidence only and do not establish \(n^{4/3}\) convergence, positivity of a new constant, separability, or a multidimensional operator.  When \(|S_c|=2\), no question remains: \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\), and the cited two-arm Bernoulli/shrinkage construction lifts to the active arms.
