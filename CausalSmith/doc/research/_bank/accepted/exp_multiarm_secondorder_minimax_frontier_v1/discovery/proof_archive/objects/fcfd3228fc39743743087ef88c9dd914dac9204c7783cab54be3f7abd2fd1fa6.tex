\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(n\) and abbreviate \(c_n=c^{(n)}\), \(M=M_n\), \(\eta=\eta_n\), \(B=B_n\), and \(U=U_n\).  The rational-contrast certificate theorem gives
\[
 0\leq B\leq\rho_n(c_n)\leq U
 \leq B+\Delta_n,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=\frac{C_0(c_n)}{4M^2}.
\]
The contrast-risk continuity theorem therefore immediately gives
\[
 \sqrt{\rho_n(c)}
 \leq\sqrt{\rho_n(c_n)}+\eta
 \leq\sqrt U+\eta
\]
and
\[
 \sqrt{\rho_n(c)}
 \geq\max\{0,\sqrt{\rho_n(c_n)}-\eta\}
 \geq\max\{0,\sqrt B-\eta\}.
\]
Squaring proves \(R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+\).

We record why these are procedure and prior certificates, not merely inequalities between abstract minimax values.  Let \((\pi_n,\delta_n)\) be the rational finite-program procedure whose worst-case risk for \(c_n\) is \(U\), and put
\[
 \delta_n^c(r,x)=\operatorname{proj}_{[-L_c/2,L_c/2]}\{\delta_n(r,x)\}.
\]
The pointwise projection and Minkowski calculation from the continuity theorem gives, for every schedule \(z\),
\[
 \sqrt{R_n(\pi_n,\delta_n^c;z;c)}
 \leq\sqrt{R_n(\pi_n,\delta_n;z;c_n)}+\eta
 \leq\sqrt U+\eta.
\]
Thus the projected observed-data rule has worst-case risk at most \(R_n^+\).  No inverse probability or new overlap condition is introduced.

For the lower endpoint, use the rational response-count prior \(\nu_{n,M,c_n}\).  Fix any procedure for contrast \(c\), and project its action onto \([-L_{c_n}/2,L_{c_n}/2]\) to obtain a legitimate procedure for contrast \(c_n\).  Apply Minkowski in the joint law of the response-count prior and the design assignment.  The target perturbation is uniformly at most \(\eta\), so
\[
 \sqrt{\operatorname{BR}_{c_n}}
 \leq\sqrt{\operatorname{BR}_{c}}+\eta.
\]
The posterior-mean calculation in the rational certificate theorem says that every procedure for \(c_n\) has Bayes risk at least \(B\) under this prior, including procedures induced from arbitrary dependent designs.  Hence
\[
 \sqrt{\operatorname{BR}_{c}}
 \geq\max(0,\sqrt B-\eta),
\]
and the same prior certifies the lower endpoint \(R_n^-\) against the unrestricted real-contrast game.

It remains to bound the width.  Set \(b=\sqrt B\) and \(u=\sqrt U\).  For every \(b,\eta\geq0\),
\[
 B-(b-\eta)_+^2\leq2\eta b+\eta^2.
\]
Consequently, using \(b\leq u\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_n^+-R_n^-
 &=U-B+2\eta u+\eta^2+\bigl\{B-(b-\eta)_+^2\bigr\}\\
 &\leq\Delta_n+4\eta u+2\eta^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The embedded two-arm theorem includes \(\rho_n(c_n)\leq C_0(c_n)/n\).  Since \(U\leq B+\Delta_n\leq\rho_n(c_n)+\Delta_n\),
\[
 u\leq\sqrt{C_0(c_n)}\,n^{-1/2}
 +\frac{\sqrt{C_0(c_n)}}{2M}.
\]
Substitution and \(2xy\leq x^2+y^2\), with \(x=\sqrt{C_0(c_n)}/M\) and \(y=\eta\), yield
\[
 R_n^+-R_n^-
 \leq\frac{5C_0(c_n)}{4M^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c_n)}\,\eta n^{-1/2}
 +3\eta^2.
\]
Because \(c_n\to c\neq0\), \(C_0(c_n)\to C_0(c)>0\); hence the exact display implies the asserted big-\(O\) form with a constant depending only on a fixed neighborhood of \(c\).

We finally construct approximants at the required rate.  Choose any positive sequence \(e_n=o(n^{-5/6})\).  By density of \(\mathbb Q\), for \(a=1,\ldots,K-1\) choose \(q_{n,a}\in\mathbb Q\) so that
\[
 |q_{n,a}-c_a|<\frac{e_n}{2(K-1)},
\]
and set \(q_{n,K}=-\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}q_{n,a}\).  Then \(c_n=(q_{n,1},\ldots,q_{n,K})\) is rational and zero-sum, and
\[
 L_{c-c_n}\leq2\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}|c_a-q_{n,a}|<e_n.
\]
Since \(c\neq0\), the approximants are nonzero for all sufficiently large \(n\); altering finitely many initial approximants makes every \(c_n\) nonzero without affecting the rate.  Thus \(\eta_n<e_n/2=o(n^{-5/6})\).

With \(M_n=n\), the three terms in the exact width bound are respectively \(O(n^{-2})\), \(o(n^{-4/3})\), and \(o(n^{-5/3})\).  Their sum is \(o(n^{-4/3})\).  Subtracting the risk bracket from \(C_0(c)/n\) proves the improvement bracket.  If \(c\) is rational, taking \(c_n=c\) makes \(\eta_n=0\), and the sharper rational sandwich gives gap at most \(C_0(c)/(4n^2)\). \(\square\)+
