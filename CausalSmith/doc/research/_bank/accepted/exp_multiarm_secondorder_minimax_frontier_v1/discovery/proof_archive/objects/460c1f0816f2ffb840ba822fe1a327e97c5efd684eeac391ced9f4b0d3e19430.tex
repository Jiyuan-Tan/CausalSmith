\textbf{Proof.}
The contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\) is rational, \(L_{c^\dagger}=2\), \(h_{c^\dagger}=1\), and \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\).  Therefore
\[
 \Gamma_{M,c^\dagger}=\{-1+j/M:j=0,\ldots,2M\}=\Gamma_M.
\]
After substituting \(K=3\) and \(c=c^\dagger\), the constraints in \(\mathrm{def:rational\text{-}contrast\text{-}grid\text{-}lp}\) are term-for-term the constraints in \(\mathrm{def:k3\text{-}rational\text{-}lp}\).  Hence
\[
 \Lambda_{n,M}(c^\dagger)=\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger.
\]
The exact rational primal and dual certificates whose existence is also established in \(\mathrm{prop:k3\text{-}lp\text{-}certificate}\) may consequently be chosen as the specialization of the certificates in \(\mathrm{thm:rational\text{-}contrast\text{-}grid\text{-}certificate\text{-}sandwich}\).  Under the same substitution, its quantities \(\kappa_{r,x}^{n,M,c}\), \(\delta_{n,M,c}\), \(U_{n,M}(c)\), \(D_{r,x,c}\), \(N_{r,x,c}\), and \(B_{n,c}\) become exactly the quantities displayed in the present statement.  Its finite-sample sandwich therefore becomes
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n^\dagger\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
All support qualifications also specialize unchanged: divisions by \(\pi_r^{n,M}\) occur only for positive coordinates, zero posterior denominators are omitted, and every \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}\), including the all-zero and all-one response types, remains a risk constraint.

For any \(M_n\) with \(a_n/M_n^2\to0\), the specialized certificate gap is at most \(a_n/(4M_n^2)\), proving the displayed convergence.  Moreover,
\[
 a_n\{n^{-1}-U_{n,M_n}\}
 \leq a_nd_n(c^\dagger)
 \leq a_n\{n^{-1}-B_n(\nu_{n,M_n})\},
\]
and the extreme terms differ by a quantity tending to zero.  If the middle sequence converges, squeezing proves that both certificate improvements have the same limit.  The proof uses only same-\(n\) finite programs, so it entails none of the optimizer-convergence, sharp-constant, second-order design-optimality, or analytic-feedback conclusions expressly excluded by the theorem. \(\square\)
