\textbf{Proof.}
Fix \(c\) and a response schedule \(z\). Since \(c\neq0\), one has \(L_c>0\) and hence \(h_c>0\). For every \(a\in S_c\),
\[
 q_a^\star=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}>0,
\]
so every inverse probability in the construction is defined. On the event \(A_i=a\), binary outcomes and the identity \(c_a/q_a^\star=L_c\operatorname{sign}(c_a)\) give
\[
 W_i=L_c\operatorname{sign}(c_a)(t_{i,a}-1/2)\in\{-h_c,h_c\}.
\]
Put
\[
 \eta_i=h_c^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a},
 \qquad
 \theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_i
 =\frac{\tau_c(z)}{h_c}.
\]
The design is independent across units, and direct inverse-probability algebra gives
\[
 V_i\in\{-1,1\},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}(V_i\mid z)=\eta_i,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}(X_n\mid z)=\theta_n.
\]
The equality for the mean uses \(c_a=0\) off \(S_c\) and \(\sum_a c_a=0\). The positive and negative coefficients of a zero-sum contrast each have total absolute mass \(L_c/2=h_c\); therefore \(|\sum_ac_at_{i,a}|\leq h_c\), so \(|\eta_i|\leq1\).

The set of possible \(\eta_i\)'s is finite. It contains a nonzero element because a response type supported on any single arm \(a\in S_c\) has normalized score \(c_a/h_c\neq0\). Thus the minimum defining \(\lambda_c\) is over a nonempty finite set and is strictly positive; the preceding range bound gives \(\lambda_c\leq1\). If
\[
 Q_n=\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_i^2,
\]
then every nonzero score obeys \(\eta_i^2\geq\lambda_c|\eta_i|\), while the same inequality is trivial at zero. Hence
\[
 Q_n\geq\lambda_c\sum_{i=1}^n|\eta_i|
 \geq\lambda_c\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_i\right|
 =\lambda_cn|\theta_n|.
\]
Independence and \(V_i^2=1\) also yield the exact conditional variance identity
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D^\star}(X_n\mid z)
 =\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n(1-\eta_i^2)
 =\frac1n-\frac{Q_n}{n^2}.
\]

We next check admissibility without imposing any additional projection or overlap condition. For \(|x|\leq b_n\),
\[
 x-\varepsilon_ng_n(x)=(1-\varepsilon_n)x,
\]
whereas for \(|x|>b_n\),
\[
 x-\varepsilon_ng_n(x)=x-\varepsilon_nb_n\operatorname{sign}(x).
\]
Because \(0<\varepsilon_n\leq1/4\), both formulas move \(x\in[-1,1]\) toward zero without crossing zero. Thus \(|X_n-\varepsilon_ng_n(X_n)|\leq1\), and multiplication by \(h_c=L_c/2\) proves that \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}}\) lies in the stipulated estimator action space. It is observable because \(W_i\) uses only \(A_i\), \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and the known contrast and design probabilities.

Let
\[
 C_n=\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal D^\star}\{X_n,g_n(X_n)\mid z\}.
\]
Since \(g_n\) is nondecreasing, an independent conditional copy \(X_n'\) gives
\[
 2C_n=
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}\left[
 (X_n-X_n')\{g_n(X_n)-g_n(X_n')\}\mid z
 \right]\geq0.
\]
We also need a local lower bound. The requisite Hoeffding inequality follows here from the binary scores rather than being assumed. For \(s\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
 \psi_i(s)=\log\mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}
 \left[\exp\{s(V_i-\eta_i)\}\mid z\right].
\]
Direct differentiation of the two-point moment generating function gives \(\psi_i(0)=\psi_i'(0)=0\) and
\[
 \psi_i''(s)=\operatorname{Var}_{i,s}(V_i)\leq1,
\]
where \(\operatorname{Var}_{i,s}\) is variance under the exponentially tilted two-point law. Taylor's formula with integral remainder therefore gives \(\psi_i(s)\leq s^2/2\). Independence, exponential Markov inequality, and minimization over \(s>0\) imply, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \Pr_{\mathcal D^\star}(X_n-\theta_n\geq u\mid z)
 \leq\inf_{s>0}\exp\{-snu+ns^2/2\}
 =\exp(-nu^2/2).
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-V_i\) proves
\[
 \Pr_{\mathcal D^\star}(|X_n-\theta_n|\geq u\mid z)
 \leq2\exp(-nu^2/2).
\]

Suppose first that \(|\theta_n|\leq b_n/2\). On \(|X_n|\leq b_n\), one has \(g_n(X_n)-X_n=0\); moreover, \(|X_n|>b_n\) implies \(|X_n-\theta_n|\geq b_n/2\). Since \(|X_n-\theta_n|\leq2\) and \(|g_n(X_n)-X_n|\leq1\), the preceding tail bound at \(u=b_n/2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_n
 &=\operatorname{Var}(X_n\mid z)
 +\mathbb E[(X_n-\theta_n)\{g_n(X_n)-X_n\}\mid z]\\
 &\geq\operatorname{Var}(X_n\mid z)-\Delta_n,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=4\exp(-n^{1/3}/8).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Define the normalized improvement over \(n^{-1}\) by
\[
 I_n(z)=\frac1n-
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}
 \left[\{X_n-\varepsilon_ng_n(X_n)-\theta_n\}^2\mid z\right].
\]
Expanding the square, using \(\mathbb E(X_n\mid z)=\theta_n\), and using \(g_n(X_n)^2\leq b_n^2\) yields
\[
 I_n(z)\geq\frac{Q_n}{n^2}+2\varepsilon_nC_n-
 \varepsilon_n^2b_n^2.
\]
In the local region \(|\theta_n|\leq b_n/2\), the covariance and variance identities give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I_n(z)
 &\geq \frac{2\varepsilon_n}{n}
 +(1-2\varepsilon_n)\frac{Q_n}{n^2}
 -2\varepsilon_n\Delta_n-\varepsilon_n^2b_n^2\\
 &\geq A_cn^{-4/3}
 -2\sqrt{\lambda_c}\,n^{-1/3}\exp(-n^{1/3}/8).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For every \(n\geq N_c\), the definition of \(N_c\) bounds the last term by \((A_c/2)n^{-4/3}\), so
\[
 I_n(z)\geq\frac{A_c}{2}n^{-4/3}
 \geq\kappa_cn^{-4/3}.
\]

In the outer region \(|\theta_n|>b_n/2\), monotonicity gives \(C_n\geq0\), and the score inequality gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I_n(z)
 &\geq\frac{Q_n}{n^2}-\varepsilon_n^2b_n^2\\
 &\geq\lambda_c\frac{|\theta_n|}{n}-\frac{\lambda_c}{16}n^{-4/3}\\
 &\geq\frac{7\lambda_c}{16}n^{-4/3}
 =B_cn^{-4/3}
 \geq\kappa_cn^{-4/3}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The local case includes the boundary \(|\theta_n|=b_n/2\), so the two cases are exhaustive. Also \(A_c>0\) because \(0<\lambda_c\leq1\), and \(m\exp(-m^{1/3}/8)\to0\), so a finite \(N_c\) with the stated property exists.

Multiplying the normalized risk inequality by \(h_c^2=C_0(c)\) proves, uniformly over every fixed schedule,
\[
 R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\}.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(z\) and then using this procedure in the minimax infimum gives
\[
 d_n(c)=\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)
 \geq C_0(c)\kappa_cn^{-4/3}
 \qquad(n\geq N_c).
\]
The unrestricted embedded two-arm converse supplies the matching order ceiling
\[
 d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Together these inequalities prove the displayed liminf--limsup bracket and \(d_n(c)=\Theta(n^{-4/3})\). The converse permits arbitrary dependent designs and arbitrary possibly biased estimators, so the lower certificate for minimax risk is matched in order by one explicit design--estimator pair rather than by an assumed limit law.

Finally, suppose \(a_n\) is regularly varying with index \(\beta\) and \(a_nd_n(c)\to C\in(0,\infty)\). The two-sided rate bounds imply that
\[
 b_n:=a_nn^{-4/3}
\]
is eventually bounded above and bounded away from zero. It is regularly varying with index \(\gamma=\beta-4/3\). If \(\gamma>0\), then \(b_{2n}/b_n\to2^\gamma>1\); iterating an eventual ratio strictly larger than one along \(n,2n,4n,\ldots\) contradicts the upper bound on \(b_n\). If \(\gamma<0\), the same iteration gives convergence to zero along that subsequence, contradicting the positive lower bound. Hence \(\gamma=0\) and \(\beta=4/3\).

No boundary response type was removed. In particular, the all-zero and all-one types have normalized score zero because \(\sum_ac_a=0\), fall in the local case, and obey the same covariance bound. At \(n=1\) the rule remains an admissible observed-data map, although the uniform improvement certificate is asserted only from the explicit threshold \(N_c\) onward. \(\square\)
