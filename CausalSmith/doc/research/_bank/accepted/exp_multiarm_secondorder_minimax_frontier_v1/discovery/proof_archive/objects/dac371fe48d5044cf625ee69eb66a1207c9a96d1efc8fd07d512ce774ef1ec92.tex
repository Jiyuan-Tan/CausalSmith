\textbf{Proof.}
Write
\[
 \alpha=\frac{L_c}{2}=\sqrt{C_0(c)}.
\]
Because \(c\) is nonzero and has zero sum, \(L_c>0\), so \(\alpha>0\).

Suppose first that \(|S_c|=2\).  The zero-sum condition forces the two active coefficients, after relabeling, to be \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\).  Hence
\[
 q_a^\star=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}=\frac12
\]
on each active arm.  The published two-arm attainment result supplies balanced independent assignment, the published posterior-mean estimator, and a least-favorable prior forming an exact finite-\(n\) saddle for contrast \((1,-1)\).  Multiplying the estimator and target by \(\alpha\) multiplies squared loss by \(\alpha^2=C_0(c)\).  The reverse reduction in the embedded two-arm theorem supplies the admissible lifted \(K\)-arm procedure, while its forward reduction supplies the matching lower bound.  Therefore
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]

The least-favorable prior lifts as a genuine unrestricted-design converse.  Embed every binary schedule in its support by copying the positive-arm outcome to the positive active arm, copying the negative-arm outcome to the negative active arm, and fixing inactive-arm outcomes at zero.  The forward reduction maps any \(K\)-arm procedure to a binary procedure and, by conditional Jensen's inequality, makes the latter's squared loss no larger than the former's loss divided by \(\alpha^2\), schedule by schedule.  Averaging over the published prior proves Bayes risk at least \(\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\) for every \(K\)-arm procedure, and the lifted balanced rule attains it.  The cited Airy expansion then gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_n(c)
 &=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}\\
 &=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Zero probability on inactive arms is lawful because their coefficients vanish and the lifted target and procedure use only the two active coordinates.

Now allow arbitrary support.  The universal second-order rate theorem constructs the independent \(q^\star\) design and the displayed clipped-shrinkage estimator as an observed-data map into the target interval.  It proves, uniformly over every fixed response schedule and for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \max_z R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\},
 \qquad \kappa_c>0.
\]
Taking the minimax infimum gives
\[
 d_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\kappa_cn^{-4/3}.
\]
The embedded two-arm converse, valid for arbitrary dependent designs and possibly biased estimators, gives
\[
 d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Multiplying by \(n^{4/3}\) and taking lower and upper limits proves the second displayed bracket.  Every active arm has \(q_a^\star>0\); no positivity is required on zero-coefficient arms.  The maximum continues to include the all-zero, all-one, and every other boundary response schedule.

For the three-arm specialization, apply the real-contrast certificate-transfer theorem with the constant rational sequence \(c^{(n)}=c^\dagger\).  Then \(\eta_n=0\) and \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\).  The three-arm specialization theorem identifies the general rational certificates with the displayed \(U_{n,M}\) and \(B_n(\nu_{n,M})\), so
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
Subtracting from \(n^{-1}\) reverses the inequalities and yields
\[
 n^{-1}-U_{n,M}
 \leq d_n(c^\dagger)
 \leq n^{-1}-B_n(\nu_{n,M}).
\]
Putting \(M=M_n\), multiplying the endpoint gap by \(n^{4/3}\), and using \(n^{4/3}/M_n^2\to0\) proves the stated scale-matched bracket.  If \((n_j)\) is any subsequence on which \(n_j^{4/3}d_{n_j}(c^\dagger)\) converges, the two certificate corrections bracket it and differ by a term tending to zero; the squeeze theorem gives the same subsequential limit for both.  The full-sequence assertion is the special case \(n_j=j\).  For \(M_n=n\), the scaled gap is at most
\[
 \frac{n^{4/3}}{4n^2}=\frac1{4n^{2/3}}.
\]

The transfer theorem proves the stronger companion fact required by the theorem spine: the same scale-matched certification is direct for every rational contrast and is available for every real contrast through rational zero-sum approximants with an \(o(n^{-4/3})\) risk bracket.  The contrast \(c^\dagger\) is therefore the exact computed \(n\leq5\) showcase, not the boundary of the certification theorem.  None of these same-\(n\) brackets relates optimizers or priors across population sizes, identifies the sharp constant, proves sharp second-order optimality of \(q^\star\), or derives an analytic limiting feedback rule or limiting operator. \(\square\)
