\textbf{Proof.}
We prove the three assertions in order.

Suppose first that \(|S_c|=2\).  Since \(c\) is nonzero and zero-sum, its two active coefficients can be written as
\[
 c_{a_+}=\alpha,
 \qquad c_{a_-}=-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \alpha=\frac{L_c}{2}>0,
 \qquad
 C_0(c)=\alpha^2.
\]
It follows directly that \(q_{a_+}^\star=q_{a_-}^\star=1/2\).  Let \((\mathcal D_2,f_{n,2}^\star)\) be the balanced Bernoulli design and posterior-mean estimator supplied by the published two-arm attainment lemma.  Map its two assignments to \(a_+\) and \(a_-\), assign no unit to an inactive arm, and report \(\alpha f_{n,2}^\star\).  This is an admissible design because each active arm has probability \(1/2>0\), while the target contains no inactive coefficient and therefore requires no positivity on inactive arms.  For any fixed \(K\)-arm binary response schedule \(z\), define the induced binary schedule by \(y_i^+=t_{i,a_+}\) and \(y_i^-=t_{i,a_-}\).  Then
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\alpha\tau_2(y),
 \qquad
 R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\alpha f_{n,2}^\star;z)
 =\alpha^2R_{n,2}(\mathcal D_2,f_{n,2}^\star;y).
\]
Thus this lifted procedure has worst-case risk \(\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\).

For the converse certificate, embed every binary schedule by copying its positive-arm potential outcome to all positive-coefficient arms, copying its negative-arm potential outcome to all negative-coefficient arms, and fixing inactive-arm outcomes at zero, exactly as in the embedded two-arm converse theorem.  Average the resulting embedded schedules under the published two-arm least-favorable prior.  The conditional-Jensen reduction in that theorem maps every \(K\)-arm design and estimator to a two-arm procedure and gives
\[
 \operatorname{BR}_{K,c}
 \geq \alpha^2\operatorname{BR}_{2}
 \geq \alpha^2\rho_{n,2}.
\]
The first inequality is valid for arbitrary dependent \(K\)-arm assignment laws and arbitrary possibly biased estimators; the second is the least-favorable-prior property of the cited two-arm saddle.  Hence the lifted procedure and embedded prior form an exact finite-\(n\) minimax saddle, and
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
Using the cited two-arm expansion gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_n(c)
 &=\frac{C_0(c)}n-C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\\
 &=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}\\
 &=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
 \end{aligned}
\]

For arbitrary support, the universal second-order rate theorem constructs the displayed independent \(q^\star\) design and the observable clipped-shrinkage estimator, proves that the rule lies in \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), and supplies a finite \(N_c\) such that, for every \(n\geq N_c\),
\[
 \max_zR_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\},
 \qquad \kappa_c>0.
\]
The same theorem combines this upper procedure with the unrestricted embedded two-arm converse to prove
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)C_A.
\]
No convergence, sharp constant, or second-order optimality of \(q^\star\) is used in this conclusion.

For the certificate assertion, choose rational nonzero zero-sum approximants \(c^{(n)}\), and define \(\eta_n\), \(B_n\), \(U_n\), \(R_n^-\), and \(R_n^+\) as in the statement.  The procedure and prior constructions in the real-contrast grid-certificate transfer theorem give, respectively,
\[
 \rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
 \qquad
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c).
\]
The upper inequality is certified by the projected rational finite-program rule, and the lower inequality is certified against all designs and estimators by the transferred rational response-count prior.  Subtracting these inequalities from \(C_0(c)/n\) gives
\[
 \underline d_n\leq d_n(c)\leq\overline d_n.
\]
That theorem also proves the explicit finite-sample width bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\leq\overline d_n-\underline d_n
 &=R_n^+-R_n^-\\
 &\leq\frac{5C_0(c^{(n)})}{4M_n^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c^{(n)})}\,\eta_n n^{-1/2}
 +3\eta_n^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
It constructs the approximants with \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\).  Choosing \(M_n=n\) makes the three terms respectively \(O(n^{-2})\), \(o(n^{-4/3})\), and \(o(n^{-5/3})\).  Hence the improvement bracket has width \(o(n^{-4/3})\).  If \(c\) is rational, taking \(c^{(n)}=c\) makes \(\eta_n=0\), and the direct rational certificate theorem gives the sharper gap \(C_0(c)/(4n^2)\).

Finally, \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\) is rational and satisfies
\[
 L_{c^\dagger}=2,
 \qquad C_0(c^\dagger)=1.
\]
The three-arm grid-certificate sandwich theorem therefore yields, for every \(n,M\geq1\),
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
Its displayed exact-rational enclosures are finite same-population diagnostics through \(n=5\); none is used to infer convergence across \(n\).  The cited support-two saddle, the explicit multi-arm rate-attaining rule, and the finite-program certificate theorems establish precisely the three asserted levels of attainment.  The convergence, sharp-constant, optimizer, prior, feedback-rule, and limiting-operator questions listed in the statement are not consequences of these finite-sample sandwiches. \(\square\)
