\textbf{Proof.}
Write
\[
 \psi(\vartheta)=\frac{p_+-p_-}{n},
 \qquad
 N_\vartheta=\binom{n}{p_+,p_-,r_0}
 =\frac{n!}{p_+!p_-!r_0!}.
\]
All three counts are nonnegative integers with sum \(n\), so \(N_\vartheta\) is finite and positive, including at every boundary where one of the counts is zero. The design requirement in the labeled-schedule game says that \(A\sim\mathcal D\) is selected without knowledge of the response schedule. Hence, under the constructed prior, \(A\) is independent of \(\vartheta\), the ordered partition, and the fair bits \((H_i)_{i\in R}\). Dependence among the coordinates of \(A\) is unrestricted.

Fix \(\vartheta\) and a realized assignment vector \(A=a\). If \(i\in P\), then the response type is \((1,0)\), and the definition of \(S_i\) gives \(S_i=1\) under either assigned arm. If \(i\in N\), its type is \((0,1)\), and similarly \(S_i=0\) under either arm. If \(i\in R\), then its observed outcome is \(H_i\); the transformation either leaves \(H_i\) unchanged or replaces it by \(1-H_i\). Thus, conditional on \((\vartheta,P,N,R,A=a)\), the variables \((S_i)_{i\in R}\) are independent fair bits. In particular,
\[
 X:=\sum_{i=1}^nS_i
 =p_++\operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)
\]
conditional on \(\vartheta\), and this distribution is independent of \(a\).

The stronger conditional-kernel assertion follows by counting. Fix \(s\in\{0,1\}^n\), put \(x=\sum_i s_i\), and suppose \(p_+\leq x\leq p_++r_0\), equivalently \(p_-\leq n-x\). A partition can generate \(S=s\) only if \(P\) is a \(p_+\)-element subset of \(\{i:s_i=1\}\) and \(N\) is a \(p_-\)-element subset of \(\{i:s_i=0\}\); after these choices, \(R\) is forced. There are therefore
\[
 \binom{x}{p_+}\binom{n-x}{p_-}
\]
compatible ordered partitions. For each compatible partition and fixed \(a\), exactly one realization of the \(r_0\) fair bits produces \(s\), because the necessary bit is \(H_i=s_i\) on the positive arm and \(H_i=1-s_i\) on the negative arm. Consequently,
\[
 \Pr(S=s\mid\vartheta,A=a)
 =\frac{\binom{x}{p_+}\binom{n-x}{p_-}}
 {N_\vartheta 2^{r_0}}.
\]
The right-hand side depends on \(s\) only through \(x\), and it does not depend on \(a\). Summing it over the \(\binom nx\) vectors of sum \(x\), or using the factorial identity
\[
 \binom nx\binom{x}{p_+}\binom{n-x}{p_-}
 =N_\vartheta\binom{r_0}{x-p_+},
\]
gives
\[
 \Pr(X=x\mid\vartheta,A=a)
 =2^{-r_0}\binom{r_0}{x-p_+}.
\]
Hence, whenever this probability is positive,
\[
 \Pr(S=s\mid X=x,\vartheta,A=a)
 =\binom nx^{-1}\mathbf 1\!\left\{\sum_i s_i=x\right\}.
\]
This is the uniform kernel claimed in the statement. It is parameter-free and assignment-free. On values of \(x\) outside the support the conditional distribution may be defined arbitrarily, because those values have probability zero. The factorization
\[
 \Pr(A=a,S=s\mid\vartheta)
 =\mathcal D(a)\Pr(S=s\mid\vartheta)
\]
also proves that \(A\) is ancillary and independent of \((S,X,\vartheta)\) under every mixture over \(\vartheta\).

We now compare decision rules. Given \(A\), the transformations between \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(S\) are inverse to one another: on the positive arm \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=S_i\), and on the negative arm \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=1-S_i\). Thus an arbitrary full-data estimator can be written as a function \(T(A,S)\in[-1,1]\). For an assignment value outside the support of \(\mathcal D\), define all subsequent conditional expectations arbitrarily. Set
\[
 T_1(s)=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\{T(A,s)\},
 \qquad
 T_2(x)=\binom nx^{-1}
 \sum_{s:\,\sum_i s_i=x}T_1(s).
\]
Both averages remain in \([-1,1]\). Independence of \(A\) from \((S,\vartheta)\), followed by conditional Jensen's inequality, yields
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\{T_1(S)-\psi(\vartheta)\}^2
 \mid S,\vartheta\right]
 \leq
 \mathbb E\!\left[\{T(A,S)-\psi(\vartheta)\}^2
 \mid S,\vartheta\right].
\]
The parameter-free uniform law of \(S\) given \((X,\vartheta)\), followed by the same inequality, yields
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\{T_2(X)-\psi(\vartheta)\}^2
 \mid X,\vartheta\right]
 \leq
 \mathbb E\!\left[\{T_1(S)-\psi(\vartheta)\}^2
 \mid X,\vartheta\right].
\]
Integrating first over the schedule kernel and then over \(\pi_n^\star\) shows that every full-data procedure has Bayes risk at least the risk of an \(X\)-only rule in the published scalar experiment. The cited scalar minimax theorem identifies the infimum of the latter risks as the Bayes risk of \(\pi_n^\star\), equal to the scalar minimax value.

Conversely, balanced Bernoulli assignment together with the cited posterior-mean rule \(f_n^\star(X)\) is a legitimate full-data procedure, and under the lifted prior its joint law of \((X,\vartheta)\) is exactly the scalar experiment just displayed. Its Bayes risk is therefore the scalar value. The lower and upper inequalities coincide, so the Bayes risk of \(\widetilde\pi_n^\star\) in the full schedule game equals that scalar value. The cited reduction theorem, together with the exact labeled-schedule game, identifies the unrestricted two-arm minimax value with the same number. Hence \(\widetilde\pi_n^\star\) is an exact full-game least-favorable prior. No division by an assignment probability was used, and the empty-set and zero-count conventions above cover \(r_0=0\), \(p_+=0\), and \(p_-=0\). \(\square\)
