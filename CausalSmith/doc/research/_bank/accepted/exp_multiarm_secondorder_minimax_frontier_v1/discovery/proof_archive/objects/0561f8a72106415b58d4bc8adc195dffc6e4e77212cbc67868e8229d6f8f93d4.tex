The paper proves an exact finite \(2^K\)-response-type game and a universal embedded two-arm converse.  For every fixed nonzero zero-sum contrast, \(\rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\), while the contrast-weighted centered Horvitz--Thompson rule gives \(\rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c)/n\), with \(C_0(c)=(\sum_a|c_a|)^2/4\).  When exactly two coefficients are active, the bounds collapse to \(\rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\), so the published Bernoulli/nonlinear-shrinkage procedure and Airy correction lift exactly.  For every support size, the unresolved improvement obeys \(0\leq d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\).  The genuinely open frontier is therefore only \(|S_c|\geq3\): exact \(c^\dagger\) rational certificates and a finite-sample scalar-score obstruction provide diagnostics, but they do not prove \(n^{4/3}\) convergence, a positive new constant, separability, or a multidimensional Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman problem.  ACTG 175 inherits the exact finite game, first-order allocation benchmark, and universal second-order ceiling; its multivariate second-order prescription remains open.
