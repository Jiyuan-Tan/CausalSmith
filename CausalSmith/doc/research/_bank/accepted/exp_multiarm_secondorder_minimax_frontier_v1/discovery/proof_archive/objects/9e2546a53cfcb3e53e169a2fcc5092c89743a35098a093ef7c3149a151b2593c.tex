For every fixed nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\) with \(|S_c|\geq3\), the universal second-order rate theorem proves
\[
 0<\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)C_A,
\]
and hence \(d_n(c)=\Theta(n^{-4/3})\); every regularly varying normalizer that yields a finite positive limit has index \(4/3\). The residual question is whether \(n^{4/3}d_n(c)\) converges and, if it does, what its sharp constant is; if it does not, characterize its subsequential limits and decide whether a nonconstant slowly varying correction can produce a finite positive limit. Derive, rather than posit, any limiting value problem and prove existence, admissibility, and uniqueness of its value. In particular, decide whether active simplex faces and corners yield a multidimensional free-boundary Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman problem, an elliptic cone spectral problem, a separable collection of scalar layers, or no stable limit of these forms. For \(c=c^\dagger\), every proposed constant or subsequential characterization must agree with the exact rational certificates \(\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\). The finite certificates and the scalar-score nonpreservation witness establish neither convergence nor a limiting operator.
