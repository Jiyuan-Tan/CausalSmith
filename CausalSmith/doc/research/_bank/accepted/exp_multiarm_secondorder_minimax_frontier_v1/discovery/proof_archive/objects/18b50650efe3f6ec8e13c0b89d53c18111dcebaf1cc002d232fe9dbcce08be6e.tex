\textbf{Proof.}
We prove the three assertions in order.

Suppose first that \(|S_c|=2\). Since \(c\) is nonzero and zero-sum, its two active coefficients can be written as
\[
 c_{a_+}=\alpha,
 \qquad c_{a_-}=-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \alpha=\frac{L_c}{2}>0,
 \qquad
 C_0(c)=\alpha^2.
\]
It follows directly that \(q_{a_+}^\star=q_{a_-}^\star=1/2\). Let \((\mathcal D_2,f_{n,2}^\star)\) be the balanced Bernoulli design and posterior-mean estimator supplied by the narrowed published two-arm attainment result. Map its two assignments to \(a_+\) and \(a_-\), assign no unit to an inactive arm, and report \(\alpha f_{n,2}^\star\). This is an admissible design because each active arm has probability \(1/2>0\); no positivity is required on inactive arms because their coefficients vanish. For any fixed \(K\)-arm binary response schedule \(z\), define the induced binary schedule by \(y_i^+=t_{i,a_+}\) and \(y_i^-=t_{i,a_-}\). Then
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\alpha\tau_2(y),
 \qquad
 R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\alpha f_{n,2}^\star;z)
 =\alpha^2R_{n,2}(\mathcal D_2,f_{n,2}^\star;y).
\]
The cited reduction theorem says that the right-hand procedure has worst-case two-arm risk \(\rho_{n,2}\). Hence the lifted procedure has worst-case risk \(\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\).

The converse prior is supplied by the internal lift rather than by an unsupported reading of the citation. Let \(\widetilde\pi_n^\star\) be the full two-arm schedule prior constructed in the two-arm scalar-prior lift lemma. That lemma proves
\[
 \inf_{\mathcal D_2,f_2}
 \operatorname{BR}_{\widetilde\pi_n^\star}(\mathcal D_2,f_2)
 =\rho_{n,2}.
\]
Embed every schedule in the support of \(\widetilde\pi_n^\star\) into the \(K\)-arm game by placing its positive-arm potential outcome on \(a_+\), its negative-arm potential outcome on \(a_-\), and zero on every inactive arm. Call the pushforward prior \(\widetildePi_{n,c}^\star\). Fix an arbitrary, possibly dependent, \(K\)-arm assignment law and an arbitrary, possibly biased, estimator. The conditional-Jensen construction in the embedded two-arm converse theorem maps this procedure to a legitimate two-arm procedure \((\mathcal D_2,g)\) and proves, schedule by schedule,
\[
 R_{n,2}(\mathcal D_2,g;y)
 \leq \alpha^{-2}R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau;E_c(y)).
\]
Integrating this inequality under \(\widetilde\pi_n^\star\) and using its full-game least-favorable property gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{BR}_{\widetildePi_{n,c}^\star}(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau)
 &\geq \alpha^2
 \operatorname{BR}_{\widetilde\pi_n^\star}(\mathcal D_2,g)\\
 &\geq \alpha^2\rho_{n,2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\widetildePi_{n,c}^\star\) is a Bayes-risk converse against the entire unrestricted \(K\)-arm decision class. The preceding procedure attains the same value, so the two form an exact finite-\(n\) saddle and
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
The cited two-arm Airy expansion now gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_n(c)
 &=\frac{C_0(c)}n-C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\\
 &=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}\\
 &=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
 \end{aligned}
\]

For arbitrary support, the universal second-order rate theorem constructs the independent \(q^\star\) design and the observable clipped-shrinkage estimator, proves that the rule lies in \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), and supplies a finite \(N_c\) such that, for every \(n\geq N_c\),
\[
 \max_zR_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\},
 \qquad \kappa_c>0.
\]
The same theorem combines this upper procedure with the unrestricted embedded two-arm converse to prove
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)C_A.
\]
No convergence, sharp constant, or second-order optimality of \(q^\star\) is used in this conclusion.

For the certificate assertion, choose rational nonzero zero-sum approximants \(c^{(n)}\), and define \(\eta_n\), \(B_n\), \(U_n\), \(R_n^-\), and \(R_n^+\) as in the statement. The procedure and prior constructions in the real-contrast grid-certificate transfer theorem give, respectively,
\[
 \rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
 \qquad
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c).
\]
The upper inequality is certified by the projected rational finite-program rule, and the lower inequality is certified against all designs and estimators by the transferred rational response-count prior. Subtracting these inequalities from \(C_0(c)/n\) gives
\[
 \underline d_n\leq d_n(c)\leq\overline d_n.
\]
That theorem also proves the explicit finite-sample width bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\leq\overline d_n-\underline d_n
 &=R_n^+-R_n^-\\
 &\leq\frac{5C_0(c^{(n)})}{4M_n^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c^{(n)})}\,\eta_n n^{-1/2}
 +3\eta_n^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
It constructs the approximants with \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\). Choosing \(M_n=n\) makes the three terms respectively \(O(n^{-2})\), \(o(n^{-4/3})\), and \(o(n^{-5/3})\). Hence the improvement bracket has width \(o(n^{-4/3})\). If \(c\) is rational, taking \(c^{(n)}=c\) makes \(\eta_n=0\), and the direct rational certificate theorem gives the sharper gap \(C_0(c)/(4n^2)\).

Finally, \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\) is rational and satisfies
\[
 L_{c^\dagger}=2,
 \qquad C_0(c^\dagger)=1.
\]
The three-arm grid-certificate sandwich theorem therefore yields, for every \(n,M\geq1\),
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
Its displayed exact-rational enclosures are finite same-population diagnostics through \(n=5\); none is used to infer convergence across \(n\). The internally lifted support-two saddle, the explicit multi-arm rate-attaining rule, and the finite-program certificate theorems establish precisely the three asserted levels of attainment. The convergence, sharp-constant, optimizer, prior, feedback-rule, and limiting-operator questions listed in the statement are not consequences of these finite-sample sandwiches. \(\square\)
