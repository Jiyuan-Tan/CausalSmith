Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2026) prove the published two-arm scalar minimax equality and Airy correction; their theorem remains a value-level regression check, not a likelihood-kernel identity for the present multi-arm response-type game.  The natural three-arm score analyzed here has a scalar local wedge and therefore reproduces their Airy scaling only as a conditional relaxation.  The exact \(n=3\) risk witness shows that retaining arm labels is minimax-relevant at finite sample.  Hull (2026) uses a different coordinate-swap and assignment-bit action, so the scalar-score obstruction is not a claim about Hull's construction.
