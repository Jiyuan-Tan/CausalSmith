\textbf{Proof.}
Put \(x_n=x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}rate}\), there are a constant \(\kappa_c>0\) and a finite integer \(N_c\) such that
\[
 C_0(c)\kappa_c\leq x_n
 \qquad(n\geq N_c),
\]
while the same theorem, using \(\mathrm{thm:embedded\text{-}two\text{-}arm\text{-}converse}\), gives
\[
 x_n\leq C_0(c)C_A+o(1).
\]
Therefore
\[
 0<C_0(c)\kappa_c
 \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n
 \leq C_0(c)C_A<\infty.
\]
Equivalently, \(d_n(c)=\Theta(n^{-4/3})\).  Choose constants \(0<\alpha< C_0(c)\kappa_c\) and \(\beta>C_0(c)C_A\).  The preceding bounds imply \(x_n\in[\alpha,\beta]\) for every sufficiently large \(n\).  Bolzano--Weierstrass therefore makes the set of subsequential limits nonempty.  That set is closed: if \(y_j\) is a convergent sequence of subsequential limits with \(y_j\to y\), choose recursively indices \(n_j>n_{j-1}\) such that \(|x_{n_j}-y_j|<1/j\); then \(x_{n_j}\to y\).  Hence the subsequential-limit set is a nonempty closed subset of the compact interval \([\alpha,\beta]\subset(0,\infty)\), and is itself compact.

We next settle the assertion about normalizers.  The universal second-order theorem proves that if a positive regularly varying sequence \((a_n)\) satisfies
\[
 a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C\in(0,\infty),
\]
then its regular-variation index is \(4/3\).  Define
\[
 \ell_n=a_nn^{-4/3}.
\]
It follows that \((\ell_n)\) is slowly varying, and
\[
 \ell_nx_n=a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C.
\]
For every fixed \(y>0\), positivity and the two preceding limits give
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}}{x_n}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor yn\rfloor}}{\ell_nx_n}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus \((x_n)\) is slowly varying.  Conversely, suppose \((x_n)\) is slowly varying.  Its positive lower limit makes it strictly positive for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Set
\[
 a_n=\frac{n^{4/3}}{x_n}
\]
at those indices and assign arbitrary positive values at the finitely many remaining indices.  A finite modification does not alter regular variation, the reciprocal of a positive slowly varying sequence is slowly varying, and hence \((a_n)\) is regularly varying with index \(4/3\).  Moreover, \(a_nd_n(c)=1\) eventually.  This proves the if-and-only-if statement.  In the branch where \((x_n)\) does not converge, its reciprocal is likewise nonconvergent.  Therefore the preceding construction is exactly a normalizer \(n^{4/3}\ell_n\) with the nonconvergent slowly varying correction \(\ell_n=1/x_n\).  Conversely, if a nonconvergent slowly varying correction \(\ell_n\) makes \(n^{4/3}\ell_nd_n(c)=\ell_nx_n\) converge to a finite positive limit, the displayed ratio argument forces \((x_n)\) to be slowly varying.  This proves the stated equivalence on the nonconvergent branch.

We now compare the computable certificates.  First suppose that \(c\) is rational, set \(M=n\), and abbreviate
\[
 \Lambda_n=\Lambda_{n,n}(c),
 \qquad U_n=U_{n,n}(c),
 \qquad B_n=B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,n,c}).
\]
Define the four normalized improvements
\[
 X_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)\right\}=x_n,
\]
\[
 G_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-\Lambda_n\right\},
 \qquad
 P_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-U_n\right\},
 \qquad
 Q_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-B_n\right\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:rational\text{-}contrast\text{-}grid\text{-}certificate\text{-}sandwich}\),
\[
 B_n\leq\rho_n(c)\leq U_n\leq\Lambda_n
 \leq B_n+\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]
Thus \(Q_n\geq X_n\geq P_n\geq G_n\), and the difference between the largest and smallest of these four numbers is at most
\[
 n^{4/3}\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}
 =\frac{C_0(c)}4n^{-2/3}=o(1).
\]
They consequently have pairwise distance \(o(1)\).  Sequences at pointwise distance \(o(1)\) have the same subsequential-limit set; furthermore, one converges if and only if all four converge, and then their limits agree.  The cited certificate theorem supplies exact rational primal and dual certificates at every \(n\), so each displayed quantity is computable by the stated finite program and finite rational post-processing.

For a real contrast, \(\mathrm{thm:real\text{-}contrast\text{-}grid\text{-}certificate\text{-}transfer}\) constructs rational zero-sum contrasts \(c^{(n)}\) satisfying
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c^{(n)}}}{2}=o(n^{-5/6})
\]
and, with \(M=n\), bounds \(R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+\) such that
\[
 R_n^+-R_n^-=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
For the corresponding normalized improvement endpoints, write
\[
 \underline X_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+\right\},
 \qquad
 \overline X_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \underline X_n\leq x_n\leq\overline X_n,
 \qquad
 \overline X_n-\underline X_n=o(1).
\]
It follows directly that each endpoint is at distance \(o(1)\) from \(x_n\).  Hence the endpoints and \((x_n)\) have the same subsequential-limit set and the same convergence behavior.  The rational finite program includes a risk constraint for every \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}\); its allocation mixture is permitted to assign zero probability to any allocation orbit; observations with zero posterior denominator are omitted from the posterior-mean sum; and no inverse exposure probability occurs in either certificate.  Thus the certificates impose no exposure-positivity, allocation-support, rank, or overlap restriction on the unrestricted minimax design.  These statements also cover response-count vectors supported on the all-zero or all-one response type.

Finally, let \(K=3\) and \(c=c^\dagger\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:k3\text{-}grid\text{-}certificate\text{-}sandwich}\), one has \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\), \(\Gamma_{M,c^\dagger}=\Gamma_M\), and the general rational certificates coincide with the exact three-arm certificates.  The result \(\mathrm{prop:k3\text{-}scalar\text{-}score\text{-}not\text{-}minimax\text{-}preserving}\) gives, at \(n=3\), an explicit full-data rule whose maximum risk is strictly smaller than a valid lower bound for the compressed sign-score experiment.  Therefore that scalar compression cannot replace the full three-arm orbit game.

All comparisons above are same-\(n\) risk or certificate comparisons.  None supplies a relation between optimizers or least-favorable priors at successive population sizes, nor an analytic limit equation for the multivariate boundary layer.  Accordingly, they do not determine convergence of \(x_n(c)\), its sharp limit or exact subsequential-limit set, slow variation in the nonconvergent branch, convergence of optimizers or priors, second-order optimality of \(q^\star\), or a limiting face--corner Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman, cone-spectral, separable, or other operator.  These are exactly the unresolved conclusions stated in the theorem. \(\square\)+
