For a fixed \(K\)-arm binary finite population and a prespecified zero-sum contrast, the paper proves that the unrestricted game over complete labeled schedules is exactly the explicit finite orbit game on all \(2^K\) response types. The contrast-weighted allocation \(q_a^\star\propto|c_a|\) gives the first-order constant \(C_0(c)=(\sum_a|c_a|)^2/4\). An explicit clipped-shrinkage rule improves its risk by a positive multiple of \(n^{-4/3}\). A paper-local smooth-prior Bayesian information inequality, applied to a complete-schedule two-arm submodel and then lifted by the sign-group embedding, proves the matching coarse converse \(d_n(c)\leq43C_0(c)n^{-4/3}\); hence the universal second-order exponent is \(4/3\) without using the published Airy theorem. Exact-rational finite programs give matched procedure and prior certificates for rational contrasts, continuity transfers them to every real contrast, and the \(K=3\), \(c=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), \(n\leq5\) calculations remain a finite diagnostic. The sharp normalized constant, convergence, second-order optimality of \(q^\star\), optimizer and prior convergence, and any limiting face--corner operator remain open, including for an ACTG 175 regimen-versus-average consumer.
