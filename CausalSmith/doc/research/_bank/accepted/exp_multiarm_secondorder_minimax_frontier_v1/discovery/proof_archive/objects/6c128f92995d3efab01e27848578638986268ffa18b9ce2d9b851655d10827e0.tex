For every \(n\geq1\), every fixed \(K\geq2\), and every nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), put \(P_c=\{a\in\mathcal A_K:c_a>0\}\) and \(N_c=\{a\in\mathcal A_K:c_a<0\}\).  Write \(\rho_{n,2}\) for the unrestricted two-arm minimax risk with binary potential outcomes and contrast \((1,-1)\).  For every two-arm response schedule, the group-homogeneous embedding defined by \(t_{i,a}=Y_i(1)\) on \(P_c\), \(t_{i,a}=Y_i(0)\) on \(N_c\), and \(t_{i,a}=0\) off \(S_c\) satisfies
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\frac{L_c}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n\{Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\}.
\]
From every randomized \(K\)-arm design and every possibly biased estimator one can construct, without using the response schedule, a legitimate two-arm design and deterministic estimator whose maximum risk is at most \(C_0(c)^{-1}\) times the maximum risk of the original procedure.  Consequently, for every \(n\),
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
If \(|S_c|=2\), the reverse inequality follows by assigning only the two active arms and lifting an optimal two-arm procedure, and hence
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
The contrast-weighted centered Horvitz--Thompson procedure also gives \(\rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c)/n\).  Therefore
\[
 0\leq d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}
 =C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
and the upper inequality is an equality whenever \(|S_c|=2\).
