\textbf{Established partial theorem.}
Fix a nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), and put
\[
 x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c),\qquad
 \alpha_c=C_0(c)\kappa_c,\qquad
 \beta_c=C_0(c)C_A,
\]
where \(\kappa_c>0\) is the explicit constant in the universal second-order rate theorem.  Then the set of subsequential limits
\[
 \mathcal L(c)=\bigcap_{N\geq1}
 \overline{\{x_n(c):n\geq N\}}
\]
is a nonempty compact subset of \([\alpha_c,\beta_c]\).  In particular, every subsequential limit is finite and strictly positive.

There is also an exact answer to the slowly varying part of the question.  A regularly varying normalizer \(a_n\) can satisfy
\[
 a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C\in(0,\infty)
\]
only if \(a_n=n^{4/3}\ell_n\) for a slowly varying sequence \(\ell_n\).  Such a sequence \(\ell_n\) and constant \(C\) exist if and only if \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying, that is,
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}\longrightarrow1
 \qquad\text{for every }s>0.
\]
Thus a nonconvergent \(x_n(c)\) admits a nonconstant slowly varying correction precisely when its nonconvergence is itself slowly varying.

For a rational contrast \(c\), take any integers \(M_n\) such that \(n^{4/3}/M_n^2\to0\), for example \(M_n=n\), and define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_n^{\Lambda}(c)
 &=n^{4/3}\{C_0(c)n^{-1}-\Lambda_{n,M_n}(c)\},\\
 L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(c)
 &=n^{4/3}\{C_0(c)n^{-1}-U_{n,M_n}(c)\},\\
 U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(c)
 &=n^{4/3}\{C_0(c)n^{-1}-B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M_n,c})\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Then
\[
 J_n^{\Lambda}(c)\leq L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(c)
 \leq x_n(c)\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(c),
\]
and
\[
 0\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(c)-J_n^{\Lambda}(c)
 \leq \frac{C_0(c)n^{4/3}}{4M_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently, these three certificate sequences and \(x_n(c)\) have exactly the same subsequential-limit set; they converge simultaneously to the same value, and slow variation of any one is equivalent to slow variation of all the others.  For \(c=c^\dagger\), the general grid and certificate quantities specialize to \(\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\), \(U_{n,M}\), and \(B_n(\nu_{n,M})\), so this characterization agrees exactly with every rational three-arm certificate.

\textbf{Proof.}
The universal second-order rate theorem gives
\[
 0<\alpha_c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)\leq\beta_c<\infty.
\]
Hence \(x_n(c)\) is eventually bounded above and away from zero.  Its tail closures are nonempty compact subsets of a common compact interval and are nested.  Their intersection is therefore nonempty and compact, and the standard subsequence characterization of limit points identifies that intersection with \(\mathcal L(c)\).

If \(a_n\) is regularly varying and \(a_nd_n(c)\to C\in(0,\infty)\), the index conclusion of the universal theorem forces its index to be \(4/3\).  Thus \(\ell_n=a_n/n^{4/3}\) is slowly varying and \(\ell_nx_n(c)\to C\).  For every \(s>0\),
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}
 {\ell_nx_n(c)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Conversely, if \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying, its eventual strict positivity permits the definition \(\ell_n=C/x_n(c)\).  This \(\ell_n\) is slowly varying and satisfies \(n^{4/3}\ell_nd_n(c)=C\) eventually.  The rate bounds also show in either direction that \(\ell_n\) is eventually bounded above and away from zero.

Finally, the rational-contrast certificate sandwich gives
\[
 B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M_n,c})\leq\rho_n(c)\leq U_{n,M_n}(c)
 \leq\Lambda_{n,M_n}(c)
 \leq B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M_n,c})+\frac{C_0(c)}{4M_n^2}.
\]
Subtracting every term from \(C_0(c)/n\), multiplying by \(n^{4/3}\), and using \(n^{4/3}/M_n^2\to0\) yields the displayed certificate inequalities and a common \(o(1)\) diameter.  Sequences at pairwise distance \(o(1)\) have the same subsequential limits and the same convergence behavior.  Since they are eventually bounded above and away from zero, an \(o(1)\) perturbation also has ratio tending to one, which proves the asserted equivalence of slow variation.  The identities \(L_{c^\dagger}=2\), \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\), and \(\Gamma_{M,c^\dagger}=\Gamma_M\) give the exact three-arm specialization. \(\square\)
