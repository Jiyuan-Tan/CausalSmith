For every nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), the established first-order upper rule and embedded two-arm converse give \[0\leq d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),\qquad 0\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A.\] If \(|S_c|=2\), the embedded two-arm theorem together with the published two-arm Airy theorem solves the second-order problem exactly: \(a_n=n^{4/3}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{so}}(c)=C_0(c)C_A\). The unresolved question is restricted to nonzero contrasts with \(|S_c|\geq3\). Determine whether there exists a regularly varying normalizer \(a_n\to\infty\) for which \(a_nd_n(c)\) converges to a finite positive limit; in that branch determine \(C_{\mathrm{so}}(c)\), and otherwise prove nonexistence of such a normalization and identify the correct asymptotic behavior. Derive, rather than posit, any limiting value problem and its unique admissible solution, and decide whether the active simplex faces and corners yield a multidimensional free-boundary Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman problem, an elliptic cone spectral problem, a separable collection of scalar layers, or no stable limit of these forms. For \(c=c^\dagger\), the conclusion must agree with certified rational scaling. The exact-rational certificates for \(n\leq5\) and the finite-sample scalar-score nonpreservation result are evidence only: they do not establish \(n^{4/3}\) convergence, a positive multi-arm constant, separability, or a multidimensional limiting operator.
