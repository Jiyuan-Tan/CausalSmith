For \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), \(L_{c^\dagger}=2\), so both \(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\) and every admissible continuous estimate lie in \([-1,1]\).  Every indexing set in the program is finite.  The factorial formula makes \(P_m(x\mid r)\) rational, while \(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\) and every \(g\in\Gamma_M\) are rational.  Thus every objective and constraint coefficient is rational.  The action \(g=0\) is in \(\Gamma_M\) and gives a feasible rule with \(u\le1\); for minimization one may therefore restrict \(u\) to \([0,1]\).  All other variables lie in finite products of simplexes, so the relevant feasible set is a compact rational polytope.

For completeness, rational primal and dual certificates follow by an elementary elimination argument.  Put the finite program in equality-and-inequality form and eliminate its variables one at a time by Fourier--Motzkin elimination, retaining the objective-coordinate variable.  At each step the new inequalities are nonnegative rational linear combinations of preceding inequalities.  The final feasible objective set is a one-dimensional rational polyhedron, whose finite attained lower endpoint is rational.  Back-substitution at rational endpoints produces a rational primal optimizer.  Recording the nonnegative rational combinations that produce the endpoint inequality gives feasible rational dual multipliers with that same objective value; weak duality then certifies optimality of both.  Hence the program admits exact-rational primal and dual certificates.

We next compare it with the continuous orbit game.  Let \((\pi,w,u)\) be feasible.  If \(\pi_r>0\), define
\[
 \kappa_{r,x}(g)=\frac{w_{r,x,g}}{\pi_r},
 \qquad
 \bar\delta(r,x)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma_M}\kappa_{r,x}(g)g;
\]
when \(\pi_r=0\), choose \(\kappa_{r,x}\) arbitrarily.  The constraints
\(\sum_gw_{r,x,g}=\pi_r\) make \(\kappa_{r,x}\) a probability kernel selected after observing \((r,x)\).  For every \(m,r,x\), the conditional variance identity gives
\[
 \sum_g\kappa_{r,x}(g)\{g-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}^2
 =\{\bar\delta(r,x)-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}^2
 +\sum_g\kappa_{r,x}(g)\{g-\bar\delta(r,x)\}^2.
\]
Because \(\bar\delta(r,x)\in[-1,1]\), it is an admissible deterministic continuous action.  Dropping the nonnegative variance term and using the exact-game equality gives
\[
 \rho_n^\dagger=G_n(c^\dagger)\le u.
\]
Taking the infimum over feasible grid programs proves
\(\rho_n^\dagger\le\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\).

Conversely, let \((\pi,\delta)\) be a minimax continuous orbit procedure, whose existence was proved in the exact response-type game theorem.  Let \(Q_M(v)\) be a nearest point of \(\Gamma_M\) to \(v\in[-1,1]\), with any fixed tie rule.  The mesh is \(1/M\), so
\[
 e(r,x)=Q_M(\delta(r,x))-\delta(r,x),
 \qquad |e(r,x)|\le\frac1{2M}.
\]
Set
\(w_{r,x,g}=\pi_r\mathbf 1\{g=Q_M(\delta(r,x))\}\).  This satisfies every normalization constraint.  For every \(m,r,x\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \{Q_M(\delta)-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}^2
 -\{\delta-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}^2
 &=2e\{\delta-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}+e^2\\
 &\le2|e|\,|\delta-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)|+e^2\\
 &\le\frac2M+\frac1{4M^2},
 \end{aligned}
\]
because \(|\delta-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)|\le2\).  Averaging against \(\pi_rP_m(x\mid r)\), using \(\sum_r\pi_r=1\) and \(\sum_xP_m(x\mid r)=1\), and maximizing over \(m\) gives
\[
 \Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger
 \le\rho_n^\dagger+\frac2M+\frac1{4M^2}
 \le\rho_n^\dagger+\frac2M+\frac1{M^2}.
\]
For \(M=n^2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &n^{4/3}\left|\{n^{-1}-\Lambda_{n,n^2}^\dagger\}
 -\{n^{-1}-\rho_n^\dagger\}\right|\\
 &\qquad=n^{4/3}\{\Lambda_{n,n^2}^\dagger-\rho_n^\dagger\}
 \le2n^{-2/3}+n^{-8/3}\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the certified sequence has the same \(n^{4/3}\)-scale behavior as the unrestricted improvement without presupposing that \(4/3\) is the correct exponent.  Finally, the LP constraints are indexed by every \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}\), and the likelihood uses every \(t\in\mathcal T_3\).  In particular, schedules concentrated on the all-zero or all-one response type remain present.+
