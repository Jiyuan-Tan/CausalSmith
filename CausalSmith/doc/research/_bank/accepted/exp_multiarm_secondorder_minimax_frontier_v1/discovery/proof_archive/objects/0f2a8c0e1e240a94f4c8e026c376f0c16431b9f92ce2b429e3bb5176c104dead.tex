Let
\[
 \widetilde\tau=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i,
 \qquad
 Z_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a^\star}
 \bigl(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1/2\bigr).
\]
On \(S_c\), \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/L_c>0\), so every division is valid.  Since \(\sum_a c_a=0\), independent assignment gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}Z_i
 =\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a(t_{i,a}-1/2)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}=:\mu_i,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\widetilde\tau=\tau_c(z).
\]
Binary outcomes imply \((t_{i,a}-1/2)^2=1/4\), whence
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}Z_i^2
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a^\star}
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}L_c|c_a|
 =\frac{L_c^2}{4}=C_0(c).
\]
The \(Z_i\) are independent, and therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\{\widetilde\tau-\tau_c(z)\}^2
 =\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\{C_0(c)-\mu_i^2\}
 \le\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
For every binary response vector, the positive and negative coefficients of the zero-sum contrast each have total absolute mass \(L_c/2\), so
\[
 -L_c/2\le\sum_a c_at_{i,a}\le L_c/2.
\]
Thus \(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\).  Metric projection onto this closed interval cannot increase squared distance from \(\tau_c(z)\), proving the finite-sample bound for \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star\).

We next prove the unrestricted converse from a legitimate prior on fixed schedules.  Put \(s=|S_c|\), choose \(\epsilon_n=n^{-1/4}/4\), and define
\[
 w(u)=\frac{15}{16}(1-u^2)^2\mathbf 1\{|u|\le1\},
 \qquad
 J=\int_{-1}^1\frac{\{w'(u)\}^2}{w(u)}\,du<\infty.
\]
For each \(a\in S_c\), independently draw \(P_a\) with density
\[
 p\longmapsto\epsilon_n^{-1}w\!\left(\frac{p-1/2}{\epsilon_n}\right),
\]
and put \(P_a=1/2\) for \(a\notin S_c\).  Independently draw \(U_1,\ldots,U_n\) identically and uniformly on \([0,1]\), and set
\[
 Y_i(a)=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le P_a\}.
\]
After \((P,U)\) is drawn, this is an ordinary fixed binary schedule.  The hierarchy therefore induces a prior on the finite state space \(\mathcal T_K^n\), independent of every admissible assignment law.

Define
\[
 \vartheta(P)=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_aP_a,
 \qquad
 g_P(u)=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a\mathbf 1\{u\le P_a\}.
\]
Conditional on \(P\),
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^ng_P(U_i),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{\tau_c(z)\mid P\}=\vartheta(P).
\]
The function \(g_P\) vanishes outside \([\min_aP_a,\max_aP_a]\), because \(\sum_a c_a=0\).  That interval has length at most \(2\epsilon_n\), and \(|g_P|\le L_c/2\).  It follows that
\[
 e_n^2:=\mathbb E\{\tau_c(z)-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 =\frac1n\mathbb E\{\operatorname{Var}(g_P(U_1)\mid P)\}
 \le\frac{L_c^2\epsilon_n}{2n}=o(n^{-1}).
\]
In fact, \(e_n=O(n^{-5/8})=o(n^{-1/2})\).

Fix an arbitrary design \(\mathcal D\), including designs with dependent assignments, and write \(s_a=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}r_a\).  Conditional on \((P,A)\), the observed outcomes are independent with
\(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_{A_i})\).  By symmetry of \(w\),
\[
 \kappa_n=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{P_a(1-P_a)}\right]
 =4+O(\epsilon_n^2).
\]
For \(a\in S_c\), let \(S_a\) be the joint prior--likelihood score with respect to \(P_a\).  Its covariance matrix is diagonal with entries
\[
 I_a=\mathbb E(S_a^2)=\kappa_ns_a+\frac{J}{\epsilon_n^2}.
\]
Indeed, conditional Bernoulli information is \(r_a/[P_a(1-P_a)]\), prior information is \(J/\epsilon_n^2\), cross-arm likelihood scores have zero conditional covariance even when \(A\) is dependent, and likelihood and prior scores are orthogonal.  Every \(I_a\) is positive, so no exposure-positivity condition is needed.

Let \(T=T(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) be any, possibly biased, estimator.  Since \(w\) and \(w'\) vanish at both endpoints, integration by parts in \(P_a\) has no boundary term and gives
\[
 \mathbb E(TS_a)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{\vartheta(P)S_a\}=-c_a,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\{T-\vartheta(P)\}S_a]=c_a.
\]
For any vector \(v=(v_a)_{a\in S_c}\), Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 \mathbb E\{T-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 \ge\frac{(v^\top c)^2}{\sum_{a\in S_c}v_a^2I_a}.
\]
Taking \(v_a=c_a/I_a\), and then applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
\((|c_a|/\sqrt{I_a})_a\) and \((\sqrt{I_a})_a\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{T-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 \ge\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{I_a}
 \ge\frac{L_c^2}{\sum_{a\in S_c}I_a}
 \ge\frac{L_c^2}{\kappa_n n+sJ/\epsilon_n^2}
 =\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
\]
The first denominator is strictly positive even if some arm is never assigned, and
\(\sum_{a\in S_c}s_a\le n\) justifies the final inequality.

The reverse triangle inequality in \(L^2\) now implies, uniformly over all \(\mathcal D\) and \(T\),
\[
 \left\{\mathbb E(T-\tau_c(z))^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \ge\frac{L_c}{\sqrt{\kappa_n n+sJ/\epsilon_n^2}}-e_n.
\]
The first term is asymptotic to \(L_c/(2\sqrt n)\), whereas \(e_n=o(n^{-1/2})\).  Squaring the positive part therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E(T-\tau_c(z))^2\ge\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
\]
The maximum risk over deterministic schedules dominates its average under the constructed prior.  Hence this lower bound holds for the worst-case risk of every sequence of arbitrary designs and possibly biased estimators.  Taking the infimum, combining with the finite-sample upper bound, and multiplying by \(n\) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n\rho_n(c)=C_0(c)=\frac{L_c^2}{4}.
\]
Finally, for every allocation \(q\) positive on active arms,
\[
 \frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}
 \ge\frac14\left(\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|\right)^2
\]
by Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a\le1\), with equality exactly when \(q_a=|c_a|/L_c\) on \(S_c\) and no mass is assigned to inactive arms.  This is the allocation optimality certificate.
