For every fixed nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), the explicit independent-\(q^\star\) clipped-shrinkage rule in \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}rate}\) has worst-case risk at most \(C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(\kappa_c>0\) is explicit. Together with the embedded two-arm converse, this proves \(0<C_0(c)\kappa_c\leq\liminf n^{4/3}d_n(c)\leq\limsup n^{4/3}d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A\), so \(d_n(c)=\Theta(n^{-4/3})\), and every regularly varying normalizer producing a finite positive limit has index \(4/3\). Convergence, the sharp multi-arm constant, and the limiting face--corner problem remain open for \(|S_c|\geq3\).
