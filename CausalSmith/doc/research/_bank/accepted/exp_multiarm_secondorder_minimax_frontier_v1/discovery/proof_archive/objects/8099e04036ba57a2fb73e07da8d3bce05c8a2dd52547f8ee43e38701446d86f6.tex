\textbf{Proof.}
Write
\[
 \alpha=\frac{L_c}{2}=\sqrt{C_0(c)}.
\]
Because \(c\) is nonzero and has zero sum, \(L_c>0\), so \(\alpha>0\).

Suppose first that \(|S_c|=2\).  The zero-sum condition forces the two active coefficients, after relabeling, to be \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\).  Hence the frozen contrast-weighted allocation obeys
\[
 q_a^\star=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}=1/2
\]
on each active arm.  The published two-arm attainment lemma supplies balanced independent assignment, the posterior-mean estimator, and a least-favorable prior forming an exact finite-\(n\) saddle for contrast \((1,-1)\).  Multiplying the target and estimator by \(\alpha\) multiplies squared loss by \(\alpha^2=C_0(c)\).  The reverse reduction in the embedded two-arm converse supplies the admissible lifted \(K\)-arm procedure, and its forward reduction supplies the matching lower bound.  Therefore
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
The same forward reduction maps every \(K\)-arm procedure to a binary procedure whose squared loss is no larger than the original loss divided by \(\alpha^2\), schedule by schedule.  Averaging this inequality under the embedded published least-favorable prior gives Bayes risk at least \(C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\), so that prior is an exact unrestricted-design converse.  The cited Airy expansion gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_n(c)
 &=C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}\\
 &=C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Inactive arms require no positivity: their coefficients vanish, and neither the lifted target nor the lifted design uses them.

Now allow arbitrary support.  The universal second-order rate theorem constructs the independent \(q^\star\) design and its explicit clipped-shrinkage estimator as an observed-data map into the target interval.  It proves, uniformly over all complete fixed response schedules and for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \max_zR_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_cn^{-4/3}\},
 \qquad \kappa_c>0.
\]
Using this procedure in the minimax infimum yields
\[
 d_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\kappa_cn^{-4/3}.
\]
The embedded two-arm converse, which permits arbitrary dependent assignments and arbitrary possibly biased estimators, gives
\[
 d_n(c)\leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Multiplication by \(n^{4/3}\) and passage to the lower and upper limits proves the displayed second-order bracket.  For the explicit procedure, \(q_a^\star>0\) on every active arm, so each inverse probability is defined; zero-coefficient arms may have zero probability.  The maximum over schedules still includes the all-zero type, the all-one type, and every face and corner response type.

We next prove the all-real certificate branch.  Let \(c^{(n)}\), \(M_n\), \(\eta_n\), \(B_n\), \(U_n\), \(R_n^-\), and \(R_n^+\) be as in the statement.  The real-contrast grid-certificate transfer theorem applies because each \(c^{(n)}\) is rational, nonzero, and zero-sum, and because \(c^{(n)}\to c\).  It gives directly
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+.
\]
Its upper construction uses the rational finite-program design and projects the barycenter rule onto \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\); therefore it certifies worst-case risk at most \(R_n^+\), without asserting equality.  Its lower construction transfers the same rational response-count prior and gives Bayes risk at least \(R_n^-\) against every design and estimator.

Subtracting the risk bracket from \(C_0(c)/n\) reverses its inequalities.  With the endpoint definitions in the statement,
\[
 \underline d_n=\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-=\overline d_n.
\]
The endpoint width is exactly
\[
 \overline d_n-\underline d_n=R_n^+-R_n^-.
\]
The quantitative part of the same transfer theorem now yields
\[
 0\leq R_n^+-R_n^-
 \leq\frac{5C_0(c^{(n)})}{4M_n^2}
 +4\sqrt{C_0(c^{(n)})}\,\eta_n n^{-1/2}
 +3\eta_n^2,
\]
which is the claimed displayed bound.

The transfer theorem also constructs rational zero-sum approximants satisfying \(\eta_n=o(n^{-5/6})\).  Since \(c^{(n)}\to c\), one has \(C_0(c^{(n)})\to C_0(c)\), so these constants are bounded.  Taking \(M_n=n\), the three terms in the last display obey
\[
 O(n^{-2})=o(n^{-4/3}),
 \qquad
 o(n^{-5/6})n^{-1/2}=o(n^{-4/3}),
 \qquad
 o(n^{-5/3})=o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
respectively.  Thus the improvement bracket has width \(o(n^{-4/3})\).  If \(c\) is rational, take \(c^{(n)}=c\), so \(\eta_n=0\).  The rational certificate theorem then gives, with \(M_n=n\),
\[
 0\leq U_{n,n}(c)-B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,n,c})
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]
This is a direct exact-rational risk-certificate gap rather than an approximation in the contrast.

Finally, set \(c=c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\).  Then \(c^\dagger\) is rational, \(L_{c^\dagger}=2\), and \(C_0(c^\dagger)=1\).  The three-arm certificate specialization identifies the general rational certificates with \(U_{n,M}\) and \(B_n(\nu_{n,M})\), and hence, for every \(n,M\geq1\),
\[
 B_n(\nu_{n,M})\leq\rho_n(c^\dagger)\leq U_{n,M}
 \leq B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac1{4M^2}.
\]
Thus the existing exact calculations through \(n=5\) instantiate the general theorem but do not enter any passage between population sizes.  Every certificate assertion above is a same-\(n\) risk bracket.  It therefore proves neither convergence of normalized values nor convergence of designs, estimator rules, or priors, and it supplies no analytic limiting operator.  This proves exactly the stated conclusions and exclusions. \(\square\)
