\textit{Definitions and sign balance.}
Because \(c\neq0\) and \(\sum_a c_a=0\), both \(P_c\) and \(N_c\) are nonempty. Moreover,
\[
 s:=\sum_{a\in P_c}c_a
   =-\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a
   =\frac12\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a|
   =\frac{L_c}{2}>0.
\]
For a binary schedule \(y=((y_i(1),y_i(0)))_{i=1}^n\), form the \(K\)-arm schedule \(z^y\) by
\[
 t_{i,a}^y=
 \begin{cases}
 y_i(1),&a\in P_c,\\
 y_i(0),&a\in N_c,\\
 0,&c_a=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
This schedule is admissible because every displayed coordinate is binary. Direct substitution and the sign-balance identity give
\[
 \tau_c(z^y)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
   \left\{s y_i(1)-s y_i(0)\right\}
 =\frac{L_c}{2}\tau_{(1,-1)}(y).
 \tag{1}
\]

\textit{Reduction of an arbitrary \(K\)-arm procedure.}
Fix any admissible \(K\)-arm procedure \((\mathcal D,F)\). We construct a legitimate two-arm procedure without revealing any latent arm label to its estimator. Internally draw \(\widetilde A\sim\mathcal D\), independently of the fixed schedule, and apply the coordinatewise map
\[
 B_i=\phi(\widetilde A_i),
 \qquad
 \phi(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&a\in P_c,\\
 0,&a\in N_c\text{ or }c_a=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The law of \(B\) is an arbitrary but valid two-arm assignment design and is independent of \(y\). Let \(O_i=y_i(B_i)\) be the resulting observed binary outcome. From the internal label and the binary observation define the synthetic \(K\)-arm outcome
\[
 W_i=
 \begin{cases}
 O_i,&\widetilde A_i\in P_c\cup N_c,\\
 0,&c_{\widetilde A_i}=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The randomized action
\[
 H=\frac{2}{L_c}F((\widetilde A_i,W_i)_{i=1}^n)
\]
belongs to \([-1,1]\). It is not yet an admissible deterministic estimator because it uses the internal label \(\widetilde A\). To remove that label, for every binary data realization \((b,o)\) with positive probability define
\[
 g(b,o)=\mathbb E\!\left[H\mid B=b,O=o\right],
 \tag{2}
\]
where the conditional distribution of \(\widetilde A\) on the fiber \(\{\widetilde a:\phi(\widetilde a)=b\}\) is induced solely by the known law \(\mathcal D\). Conditional on \(B=b\), this distribution is independent of the fixed schedule and of \(O\); thus (2) is a single known function of the observed two-arm data, not a schedule-dependent rule. Define \(g\) arbitrarily in \([-1,1]\) on zero-probability data. Convexity of squared loss and conditional Jensen's inequality yield, for every binary schedule \(y\),
\[
 \mathbb E_y\!\left[\{g(B,O)-\tau_{(1,-1)}(y)\}^2\right]
 \leq
 \mathbb E_y\!\left[\{H-\tau_{(1,-1)}(y)\}^2\right].
 \tag{3}
\]
Under \(y\), the pair \((\widetilde A,W)\) has exactly the distribution of the observed data from \((\mathcal D,F)\) under \(z^y\): positive arms reveal \(y_i(1)\), negative arms reveal \(y_i(0)\), and inactive arms have the fixed potential outcome zero. Combining this observation with (1)--(3) gives
\[
 R_{n,2}(g;y)
 \leq \frac{4}{L_c^2}R_n(\mathcal D,F;z^y)
 =\frac{1}{C_0(c)}R_n(\mathcal D,F;z^y).
 \tag{4}
\]
Here \(R_{n,2}\) denotes the same frozen design-based squared-error risk with \(K=2\) and contrast \((1,-1)\); it is only shorthand inside this proof. Taking maxima and then using the defining infimum of the two-arm minimax risk,
\[
 \max_{z\in\mathcal T_K^n}R_n(\mathcal D,F;z)
 \geq C_0(c)\max_y R_{n,2}(g;y)
 \geq C_0(c)\rho_n((1,-1)).
\]
Since \((\mathcal D,F)\) was arbitrary, infimizing proves
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq C_0(c)\rho_n((1,-1)).
 \tag{5}
\]
This construction also explains why assignments to inactive arms grant no extra information: their binary observations are discarded, and the latent \(K\)-arm labels are removed by the schedule-independent conditional expectation (2).

\textit{Reverse reduction when \(|S_c|=2\).}
Suppose \(S_c=\{a_+,a_-\}\), labeled so that \(c_{a_+}>0>c_{a_-}\). The zero-sum condition forces
\[
 c_{a_+}=\lambda,qquad c_{a_-}=-\lambda,qquad
 \lambda=\frac{L_c}{2},qquad C_0(c)=\lambda^2.
\]
Given any two-arm procedure, send binary arm \(1\) to \(a_+\), binary arm \(0\) to \(a_-\), never allocate an inactive arm, and multiply its estimator by \(\lambda\). For every \(K\)-arm schedule, this lifted procedure has risk
\[
 R_n^{K}=\lambda^2R_{n,2}
 =C_0(c)R_{n,2}
\]
on the restricted pair of potential-outcome coordinates. Conversely, every binary schedule occurs as such a restriction of a \(K\)-arm schedule, while inactive coordinates affect neither the data nor the contrast. Therefore maxima over the two schedule spaces agree after this scaling. Infimizing over two-arm procedures gives
\[
 \rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c)\rho_n((1,-1)).
\]
Together with (5), this proves equality for \(|S_c|=2\).

\textit{Universal first-order upper bound.}
For completeness, we derive the upper bound from a primitive design. Independently across units, assign arm \(a\in S_c\) with probability
\[
 q_a=\frac{|c_a|}{L_c}.
\]
These probabilities are strictly positive on \(S_c\) and sum to one; no positivity is required on inactive arms. Define
\[
 V_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}
 \frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a}
 \left(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-\frac12\right),
 \qquad
 \widetilde\tau=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n V_i.
\]
The zero-sum identity gives
\[
 \mathbb E[V_i]
 =\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a\left(Y_i(a)-\frac12\right)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_aY_i(a),
\]
so \(\widetilde\tau\) is unbiased for \(\tau_c(z)\). Binary outcomes imply \((Y_i(a)-1/2)^2=1/4\), whence
\[
 \mathbb E[V_i^2]
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}L_c|c_a|
 =\frac{L_c^2}{4}
 =C_0(c).
\]
Independence across units and \(\operatorname{Var}(V_i)\leq\mathbb E[V_i^2]\) show
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_c(z))^2\right]
 =\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(V_i)
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}{n}.
\]
Projection of \(\widetilde\tau\) onto \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\) cannot increase its distance from \(\tau_c(z)\), which lies in that interval. Hence
\[
 \rho_n(c)\leq\frac{C_0(c)}n.
 \tag{6}
\]

Finally, (5), (6), and the definition of \(d_n(c)\) imply
\[
 0\leq d_n(c)
 =\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_n((1,-1))\}.
\]
When \(|S_c|=2\), the exact risk equality makes the last inequality an equality. The cited two-arm theorem supplies
\[
 n^{-1}-\rho_n((1,-1))
 =C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
and substitution proves the asserted universal ceiling and the support-two expansion. Every argument is finite-population and design-based: the potential outcomes are fixed, assignment is the sole source of randomness, all-zero and all-one schedules are included, no rank or inverse is used, and the only divisions are by \(L_c>0\) and by \(q_a>0\) on \(S_c\).
