\textbf{Established partial result and proof.}
Fix a nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\), and put
\[
 x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c),\qquad
 \alpha_c=C_0(c)\kappa_c,\qquad
 \beta_c=C_0(c)C_A,
\]
where \(\kappa_c>0\) is the explicit constant in the universal second-order rate theorem. That theorem gives
\[
 0<\alpha_c\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
 \leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
 \leq\beta_c<\infty.
\]
Thus \(x_n(c)\) is eventually bounded above and bounded away from zero. Its subsequential-limit set can be written as
\[
 \mathcal L(c)=\bigcap_{N\geq1}
 \overline{\{x_n(c):n\geq N\}}.
\]
The tail closures are nonempty compact subsets of a common compact interval and are nested. Their intersection is therefore nonempty and compact, and the subsequence characterization of limit points shows
\[
 \varnothing\neq\mathcal L(c)\subseteq[\alpha_c,\beta_c].
\]

Suppose next that \(a_n\) is positive and regularly varying and that
\[
 a_nd_n(c)\longrightarrow C\in(0,\infty).
\]
The universal rate theorem forces the regular-variation index of \(a_n\) to be \(4/3\). Hence
\[
 \ell_n=a_nn^{-4/3}
\]
is slowly varying and \(\ell_nx_n(c)\to C\). For every \(s>0\), eventual positivity gives
\[
 \frac{x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}
 =\frac{\ell_n}{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}}
 \frac{\ell_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}x_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}(c)}
 {\ell_nx_n(c)}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying. Conversely, if \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying, choose arbitrary positive values for finitely many initial indices and set
\[
 \ell_n=\frac{C}{x_n(c)}
\]
thereafter. Then \(\ell_n\) is slowly varying, \(a_n=n^{4/3}\ell_n\) is regularly varying with index \(4/3\), and \(a_nd_n(c)=C\) eventually. Consequently a finite positive regularly varying normalization exists if and only if \(x_n(c)\) is slowly varying. If \(x_n(c)\) is nonconvergent, the correcting \(\ell_n\) is itself nonconvergent; this is the precise genuinely nonconstant branch.

Now suppose \(c\) is rational and choose \(M=n\). Denote the exact-rational prior lower certificate by
\[
 B_n=B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,n,c}),
\]
the barycenter-procedure upper certificate by \(U_n=U_{n,n}(c)\), and the grid value by \(\Lambda_n=\Lambda_{n,n}(c)\). The rational-contrast certificate theorem gives, at every \(n\geq1\),
\[
 B_n\leq\rho_n(c)\leq U_n\leq\Lambda_n
 \leq B_n+\frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]
Define the normalized certificate improvements
\[
 J_n^\Lambda=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-\Lambda_n\right\},
 \qquad
 L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-U_n\right\},
\]
and
\[
 U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-B_n\right\}.
\]
Subtracting the finite-sample risk sandwich from \(C_0(c)/n\) and multiplying by \(n^{4/3}\) yields
\[
 J_n^\Lambda\leq L_n^{\mathrm{cert}}
 \leq x_n(c)\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}},
\]
while
\[
 0\leq U_n^{\mathrm{cert}}-J_n^\Lambda
 \leq\frac{C_0(c)}4n^{-2/3}\longrightarrow0.
\]
All four sequences therefore have pairwise distance \(o(1)\). Such perturbations preserve the subsequential-limit set and preserve convergence together with its limit. This proves the rational-contrast certificate assertion without assuming a cross-\(n\) limit.

Finally, let \(c\) be real. The contrast-continuity and real-contrast transfer theorems permit zero-sum rational approximants \(c^{(n)}\to c\) satisfying
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{L_{c-c^{(n)}}}{2}=o(n^{-5/6}),
\]
with grid resolution \(M=n\). Their explicit transferred endpoints obey
\[
 R_n^-\leq\rho_n(c)\leq R_n^+,
 \qquad
 R_n^+-R_n^-=o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 \underline x_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^+\right\},
 \qquad
 \overline x_n=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}n-R_n^-\right\},
\]
then
\[
 \underline x_n\leq x_n(c)\leq\overline x_n,
 \qquad
 \overline x_n-\underline x_n=o(1).
\]
The two rational-approximation endpoints and \(x_n(c)\) thus have the same subsequential-limit set and convergence behavior. No overlap condition is used in this transfer: the minimax design remains unrestricted. The underlying rational programs retain every \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}\), including the all-zero and all-one response-type schedules. For \(c=c^\dagger\), the general rational objects specialize to the three-arm exact-rational certificates; the available computations through \(n\leq5\) are finite-sample checks only. None of these same-\(n\) sandwiches compares different population sizes, so they do not prove convergence or a limiting operator. \(\square\)
