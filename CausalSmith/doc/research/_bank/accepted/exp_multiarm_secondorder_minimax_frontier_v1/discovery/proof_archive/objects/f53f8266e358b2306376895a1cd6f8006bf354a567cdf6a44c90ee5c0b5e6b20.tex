The sign-group subexperiment is a robustness floor.  Whatever assignment dependence or bias a multi-arm procedure uses, Nature may choose schedules on which all positive-coefficient arms share one potential outcome and all negative-coefficient arms share the other.  The procedure then cannot beat the two-arm minimax risk after the scale factor \(C_0(c)\).  This explains both the first-order constant and the universal second-order ceiling.  With two active arms the floor is attainable exactly; with three or more active arms, retaining arm labels can improve finite-sample performance, but the asymptotic size and geometry of that improvement are not yet determined.
