\paragraph{Proof.}
We first establish the finite-sample upper bound.  Write
\[
 \widetilde\tau=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i,
 \qquad
 Z_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}
 \frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a^\star}
 \left(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-\frac12\right).
\]
The contrast is nonzero, so \(L_c>0\).  For every \(a\in S_c\),
\(q_a^\star=|c_a|/L_c>0\); thus every displayed division is valid.  No
division is made for \(a\notin S_c\), and the constructed design assigns
such inactive arms probability zero.

Fix a labeled response schedule \(z\).  Under the independent assignment
in \(\mathrm{def:first\text{-}order\text{-}procedure}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Z_i\mid z)
 =\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a(t_{i,a}-1/2)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}
 =:\mu_i.
\]
The second equality uses \(c_a=0\) off \(S_c\) and
\(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a=0\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(\widetilde\tau\mid z)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mu_i=\tau_c(z).
\]
Because every \(t_{i,a}\) is binary,
\((t_{i,a}-1/2)^2=1/4\), and therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Z_i^2\mid z)
 &=\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a^\star}
 =\frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}L_c|c_a|\\
 &=\frac{L_c^2}{4}=C_0(c).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The assignments, and hence the variables \(Z_i\), are independent across
units conditional on the fixed schedule.  It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_n(\mathcal D,\widetilde\tau;z)
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
   \operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D}(Z_i\mid z)\\
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\{C_0(c)-\mu_i^2\}
 \le \frac{C_0(c)}n.
 \end{aligned}
\]

We next verify the relevant range and boundary condition.  Put
\(P_c=\{a:c_a>0\}\) and \(N_c=\{a:c_a<0\}\).  The zero-sum identity gives
\[
 \sum_{a\in P_c}c_a=-\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a=\frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
For every binary response type \(t_i\),
\[
 -\frac{L_c}{2}
 \le \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_at_{i,a}
 \le \frac{L_c}{2}.
\]
Averaging over units yields
\(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\).  Metric projection onto this closed
interval cannot increase squared distance from any point in the interval.
Consequently the projected estimator \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star\)
is an admissible action and satisfies
\[
 \max_{z\in\mathcal T_K^n}
 R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{cHT}}^\star;z)
 \le \frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
In particular, \(\rho_n(c)\le C_0(c)/n\).

For the converse, we derive the required information inequality directly.
This argument covers every assignment law, including laws with dependent
assignments and zero exposure probabilities, and every admissible possibly
biased estimator.  Since \(K\) is fixed, \(s:=|S_c|\) is fixed.  Define
\[
 \epsilon_n=\frac14n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 w(u)=\frac{15}{16}(1-u^2)^2\mathbf 1\{|u|\le1\},
 \qquad
 J=\int_{-1}^1\frac{\{w'(u)\}^2}{w(u)}\,du.
\]
The density \(w\) integrates to one.  Both \(w\) and its one-sided
derivative vanish at \(-1\) and \(1\), and direct cancellation gives
\(\{w'(u)\}^2/w(u)=15u^2\) for \(|u|<1\); hence \(J=10<\infty\).
For every \(a\in S_c\), independently draw \(P_a\) with density
\[
 p\longmapsto \epsilon_n^{-1}
 w\!\left(\frac{p-1/2}{\epsilon_n}\right),
\]
and set \(P_a=1/2\) for \(a\notin S_c\).  Because
\(0<\epsilon_n\le1/4\), every active \(P_a\) lies in \([1/4,3/4]\);
all Bernoulli likelihood denominators below are therefore uniformly
positive.  Independently draw \(U_1,\ldots,U_n\) identically and uniformly
on \([0,1]\), and define
\[
 Y_i(a)=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le P_a\}.
\]
After \((P,U)\) is drawn, this is a fixed schedule in
\(\mathcal T_K^n\).  The construction therefore induces a legitimate
prior on the finite schedule space, independently of the design.

Set
\[
 \vartheta(P)=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_aP_a,
 \qquad
 g_P(u)=\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a\mathbf 1\{u\le P_a\}.
\]
Conditional on \(P\),
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^ng_P(U_i),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{\tau_c(z)\mid P\}=\vartheta(P).
\]
The identity \(\sum_a c_a=0\) implies that \(g_P\) vanishes outside
\([\min_{a\in S_c}P_a,\max_{a\in S_c}P_a]\), an interval of length at
most \(2\epsilon_n\).  Moreover \(|g_P(u)|\le L_c/2\).  Independence of
the \(U_i\)'s consequently gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e_n^2
 &:=\mathbb E\{\tau_c(z)-\vartheta(P)\}^2\\
 &=\frac1n\mathbb E\{\operatorname{Var}(g_P(U_1)\mid P)\}
 \le\frac{L_c^2\epsilon_n}{2n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(e_n=O(n^{-5/8})=o(n^{-1/2})\).

Now fix an arbitrary assignment law \(\mathcal D\), chosen without
knowledge of the schedule, and write
\[
 r_a=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\},
 \qquad
 s_a=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}r_a.
\]
Conditional on \((P,A)\), the observed outcomes are independent and
\(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) has the Bernoulli distribution with parameter
\(P_{A_i}\).  By symmetry of \(w\) and the support bound,
\[
 \kappa_n
 :=\mathbb E\!\left\{\frac1{P_a(1-P_a)}\right\}
 =4+O(\epsilon_n^2)=4+O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
where the bound is uniform in \(a\in S_c\).  Indeed, after writing
\(P_a=1/2+\epsilon_n V_a\), the integrand equals
\(4/(1-4\epsilon_n^2V_a^2)\), and \(|V_a|\le1\).

For \(a\in S_c\), let \(S_a\) be the score in coordinate \(P_a\) of the
joint prior--likelihood density of \((P,A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Conditional
means of the Bernoulli likelihood scores are zero.  Independence of the arm
priors, conditional independence of the observed outcomes, and independence
of \(A\) from \(P\) show that the score covariance matrix is diagonal, with
\[
 I_a:=\mathbb E(S_a^2)
 =\kappa_ns_a+\frac{J}{\epsilon_n^2}>0.
\]
The prior-information term makes \(I_a\) strictly positive even when
\(s_a=0\); hence no exposure-positivity assumption is used.

Let \(T=T(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) be any admissible estimator.  Its bounded
range makes every following expectation finite.  Integration by parts in
\(P_a\) has no boundary term because the scaled prior density vanishes at
both endpoints.  Since \(T\) is independent of the integration variable
once the observed data are fixed, while
\(\partial\vartheta(P)/\partial P_a=c_a\), this gives
\[
 \mathbb E(TS_a)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{\vartheta(P)S_a\}=-c_a,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\{T-\vartheta(P)\}S_a]=c_a.
\]
For every real vector \(v=(v_a)_{a\in S_c}\), Cauchy--Schwarz and score
diagonality imply
\[
 \mathbb E\{T-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 \ge
 \frac{(\sum_{a\in S_c}v_ac_a)^2}
 {\sum_{a\in S_c}v_a^2I_a}.
\]
Choosing \(v_a=c_a/I_a\) and then applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
\((|c_a|/\sqrt{I_a})_a\) and \((\sqrt{I_a})_a\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{T-\vartheta(P)\}^2
 &\ge\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{I_a}
 \ge\frac{L_c^2}{\sum_{a\in S_c}I_a}\\
 &\ge\frac{L_c^2}{\kappa_n n+sJ/\epsilon_n^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(\sum_{a\in S_c}s_a\le n\).  This is the
specialized Bayesian information inequality needed here, obtained only
from integration by parts and Cauchy--Schwarz.

The reverse triangle inequality in \(L^2\), followed by the preceding
schedule-approximation bound, now gives uniformly over \(\mathcal D\) and
\(T\),
\[
 \left[\mathbb E\{T-\tau_c(z)\}^2\right]^{1/2}
 \ge
 \frac{L_c}{\sqrt{\kappa_n n+sJ/\epsilon_n^2}}-e_n.
\]
The first term equals \(L_c/(2\sqrt n)+o(n^{-1/2})\), whereas
\(e_n=o(n^{-1/2})\).  Taking the positive part and squaring therefore
proves
\[
 \mathbb E\{T-\tau_c(z)\}^2
 \ge \frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}),
\]
with a remainder uniform over all allowed procedures.  The maximum risk
over fixed schedules dominates the average risk under the constructed
schedule prior.  Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal D\) and \(T\) gives
\[
 \rho_n(c)\ge\frac{C_0(c)}n+o(n^{-1}).
\]
Together with the finite-sample upper bound,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n\rho_n(c)
 =C_0(c)=\frac{L_c^2}{4}
 =\frac14\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a|\right)^2.
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}response\text{-}type\text{-}game}\), the same
value certificate applies to the explicit orbit game \(G_n(c)\).

Finally, the allocation itself has an elementary optimality certificate
within the displayed independent centered Horvitz--Thompson family.  For
every allocation vector \(q\) satisfying \(q_a>0\) on \(S_c\),
\[
 \frac14\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}
 \ge
 \frac14\frac{(\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|)^2}
 {\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a}
 \ge\frac{L_c^2}{4}.
\]
Equality holds precisely when no probability is assigned outside \(S_c\)
and \(q_a\) is proportional to \(|c_a|\) on \(S_c\), namely
\(q=q^\star\).  At the finite boundary \(n=1\), the exact identity
\[
 R_1(\mathcal D,\widetilde\tau;z)=C_0(c)-\mu_1^2\le C_0(c)
\]
checks the upper bound directly.  Thus active-arm positivity is explicit
for the attaining design, inactive arms create no division, and the
converse imposes no positivity, overlap, rank, or invertibility condition
on the arbitrary competing designs.  This completes the proof.
