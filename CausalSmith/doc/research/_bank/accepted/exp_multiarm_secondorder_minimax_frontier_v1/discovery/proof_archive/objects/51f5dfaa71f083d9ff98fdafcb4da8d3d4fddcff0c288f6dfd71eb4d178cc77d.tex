\textbf{Proof.}
Put
\[
 P_c=\{a:c_a>0\},\qquad N_c=\{a:c_a<0\},\qquad I_c=\{a:c_a=0\}.
\]
Because \(c\) is nonzero and \(\sum_a c_a=0\), both \(P_c\) and \(N_c\) are nonempty and
\[
 \alpha:=\sum_{a\in P_c}c_a=-\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a
 =\frac12\sum_a|c_a|=\frac{L_c}{2}>0.
\]
In particular, division by \(\alpha\) below is lawful and \(\alpha^2=C_0(c)\).

\textbf{The embedded two-arm experiment.}
Let \(y=((y_i^+,y_i^-))_{i=1}^n\in\{0,1\}^{2n}\) be an arbitrary binary two-arm response schedule, where \(+\) and \(-\) denote the two arm labels. Embed it into the \(K\)-arm schedule \(E_c(y)\) by
\[
 t_{i,a}=y_i^+\quad(a\in P_c),\qquad
 t_{i,a}=y_i^-\quad(a\in N_c),\qquad
 t_{i,a}=0\quad(a\in I_c).
\]
Writing \(\tau_2(y)=n^{-1}\sum_i(y_i^+-y_i^-)\), the zero-sum identity gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau_c(E_c(y))
 &=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \left(\sum_{a\in P_c}c_a y_i^+
       +\sum_{a\in N_c}c_a y_i^-\right)\\
 &=\alpha\,\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i^+-y_i^-)
 =\alpha\tau_2(y).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Fix any legitimate \(K\)-arm procedure \((\mathcal D,f)\). To construct a legitimate two-arm procedure, first draw a latent \(A\sim\mathcal D\), independently of \(y\). Set the binary assignment \(B_i=+\) when \(A_i\in P_c\), set \(B_i=-\) when \(A_i\in N_c\), and, when \(A_i\in I_c\), draw \(B_i\) by a fair coin independent of everything else. This induces a probability law on \(B\in\{+,-\}^n\) that is fixed before the response schedule is revealed, so it is an admissible two-arm design. Let \(v_i=y_i^{B_i}\) be the observed binary outcome and define the pseudo-observation supplied to \(f\) by
\[
 \widetilde v_i(A,B,v)=
 \begin{cases}
 v_i,&A_i\in P_c\cup N_c,\\
 0,&A_i\in I_c.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the embedded schedule, \((A_i,\widetilde v_i)_{i=1}^n\) is exactly the data seen by the original \(K\)-arm procedure: active-arm outcomes agree because \(B_i\) is the sign group of \(A_i\), and inactive-arm outcomes were fixed at zero.

The latent scaled action \(H=\alpha^{-1}f((A_i,\widetilde v_i)_{i=1}^n)\) need not be a function of the observed binary data, because it retains \(A\). Remove this hidden information by defining, for every binary observation \((b,v)\) having positive design probability,
\[
 g(b,v)=\mathbb E\!\left[H\mid B=b,\,(y_i^{B_i})_{i=1}^n=v\right],
\]
and define \(g\) arbitrarily in \([-1,1]\) on zero-probability observations. This is a schedule-independent function of \((b,v)\): conditional on \(B=b\), the latent law of \(A\) is determined entirely by \(\mathcal D\) and the inactive-arm coins, while \(v\) is a deterministic function of \((b,y)\) and supplies no further restriction on \(A\). Equivalently, \(g(b,v)\) is obtained by averaging \(\alpha^{-1}f(A,\widetilde v(A,b,v))\) under the known conditional kernel \(\Pr(A\in\cdot\mid B=b)\). Since \(f\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]=[-\alpha,\alpha]\), one has \(g\in[-1,1]\), as required for the two-arm estimator action space.

Conditional Jensen's inequality and the embedded-target identity now yield, for every binary schedule \(y\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_y\{g(B,v)-\tau_2(y)\}^2
 &\leq \mathbb E_y\{H-\tau_2(y)\}^2\\
 &=\alpha^{-2}
 R_n(\mathcal D,f;E_c(y)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(y\), then using that \(E_c(y)\) ranges over a subset of the \(K\)-arm schedules, gives
\[
 \rho_{n,2}
 \leq\alpha^{-2}\max_z R_n(\mathcal D,f;z).
\]
The original \((\mathcal D,f)\) was arbitrary. Taking its infimum proves
\[
 \rho_n(c)\geq\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
This argument explicitly handles assignments to inactive arms: their binary outcomes are lawfully generated under the induced design and then discarded, while the original procedure receives only the fixed zero specified by the embedded schedule. No outcome unavailable under the two-arm assignment is used.

\textbf{Reverse reduction for two active coefficients.}
Suppose \(|S_c|=2\). The zero-sum condition implies that the two active coefficients are \(c_{a_+}=\alpha\) and \(c_{a_-}=-\alpha\). Given any two-arm procedure \((\mathcal D_2,f_2)\), define a \(K\)-arm design by mapping every binary assignment \(+\) to \(a_+\) and every binary assignment \(-\) to \(a_-\); inactive arms receive probability zero. Report \(\alpha f_2\). For every \(K\)-arm schedule \(z\), the induced binary schedule is \(y_i^+=t_{i,a_+}\), \(y_i^-=t_{i,a_-}\), and therefore
\[
 \tau_c(z)=\alpha\tau_2(y),
 \qquad
 R_n(\mathcal D_K,\alpha f_2;z)
 =\alpha^2R_{n,2}(\mathcal D_2,f_2;y).
\]
Taking worst cases and then the infimum over two-arm procedures gives
\(\rho_n(c)\leq\alpha^2\rho_{n,2}\). Together with the embedded lower bound,
\[
 \rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}\qquad\text{when }|S_c|=2.
\]
No positivity condition is needed for inactive arms because neither the target nor the lifted design uses them.

\textbf{Universal first-order upper bound and the second-order ceiling.}
Under the frozen contrast-weighted independent design, \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/L_c>0\) for every \(a\in S_c\). Before projection, write the estimator as \(n^{-1}\sum_i W_i\), where
\[
 W_i=\sum_{a\in S_c}
 \frac{c_a\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}}{q_a^\star}
 \left(t_{i,a}-\frac12\right).
\]
The zero-sum condition implies
\[
 \mathbb E(W_i\mid z)
 =\sum_{a\in S_c}c_a\left(t_{i,a}-\frac12\right)
 =\sum_a c_at_{i,a}.
\]
Moreover, \(c_a/q_a^\star=L_c\operatorname{sign}(c_a)\) on \(S_c\), and binary outcomes give \((t_{i,a}-1/2)^2=1/4\). Hence
\[
 \mathbb E(W_i^2\mid z)=\frac{L_c^2}{4},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(W_i\mid z)\leq\frac{L_c^2}{4}.
\]
The assignments are independent across units, so the unprojected rule is unbiased for \(\tau_c(z)\) and has risk at most
\[
 \frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(W_i\mid z)
 \leq\frac{L_c^2}{4n}=\frac{C_0(c)}n.
\]
Since every \(\tau_c(z)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), projection onto that interval cannot increase squared loss. Therefore \(\rho_n(c)\leq C_0(c)/n\). Combining this upper bound with the embedded converse gives
\[
 0\leq d_n(c)=\frac{C_0(c)}n-\rho_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}\}.
\]
The cited two-arm theorem gives
\(n^{-1}-\rho_{n,2}=C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\), so, for fixed \(c\),
\[
 0\leq d_n(c)
 \leq C_0(c)C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
When \(|S_c|=2\), the exact equality for \(\rho_n(c)\) makes the upper bound an equality. This also checks the boundary schedules with all embedded outcomes zero or all one: they remain legal schedules, the target identity still holds, and the simulation reveals no unavailable potential outcome. \(\square\)
