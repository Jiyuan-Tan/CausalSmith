Take first \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)\). The orbit form of the causal target is
\[
 \tau_c(m)
 =\frac1n\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^2}m_t(t_1-t_2)
 =\frac{m_{(1,0)}-m_{(0,1)}}n
 =\frac{p_+-p_-}{n}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Thus the two zero-effect response types contribute zero to the target and satisfy \(r_0=m_{(0,0)}+m_{(1,1)}\). This target identity does not aggregate the likelihood. Indeed, the frozen likelihood contains the factors
\[
 \prod_{t\in\{0,1\}^2}m_t!
\]
and the separate row constraints \(\sum_a h_{t,a}=m_t\) for every \(t\in\{0,1\}^2\). Consequently, \(m_{(0,0)}\) and \(m_{(1,1)}\) remain distinct coordinates of \(P_m(x\mid r)\), even though only their sum enters the published scalar parameterization. No likelihood identification from (7) is being asserted.

By the exact response-type-game theorem,
\[
 G_n((1,-1))=\rho_n((1,-1)).
 \tag{8}
\]
The cited published two-arm theorem identifies the right-hand side of (8), at the minimax-value level, with its scalar experiment
\[
 X_2=p_++\operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)
\]
for estimating \((p_+-p_-)/n\), and supplies
\[
 \rho_n((1,-1))=n^{-1}-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
 \tag{9}
\]
Here \(L_c=|1|+|-1|=2\), so \(C_0(c)=L_c^2/4=1\). Substitution of (9) into the definition of \(d_n(c)\) yields
\[
 d_n(c)=C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
This also agrees with the support-two equality in the embedded-two-arm converse lemma, which establishes the finite-sample scaling before the cited asymptotic expansion is applied.

Now fix \(K\geq3\) and any nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\). The exact response-type-game theorem applies to the unrestricted assignment class \(\Delta(\mathcal A_K^n)\), to every clipped measurable estimator, and to nature's complete schedule space \(\mathcal T_K^n\). It therefore proves the exact equality
\[
 \rho_n(c)=G_n(c),
\]
where the orbit state \(m\) has one coordinate for each of the \(2^K\) binary response types and the likelihood is the joint contingency-table kernel \(P_m(x\mid r)\). The cited published theorem concerns only \(K=2\); hence these equalities for every fixed \(K\geq3\) are a strict extension in treatment-domain scope. Since the conclusion is only an exact finite-game representation, it neither asserts nor requires that no alternative scalar representation exists. Zero allocation probabilities, arbitrary dependence among assignments, boundary response types, and biased estimators are all retained exactly as allowed by the labeled game; no overlap, rank, or inverse condition is introduced.
