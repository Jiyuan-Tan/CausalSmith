\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}crl\_\allowbreak{}coverratio\_\allowbreak{}mmd\_\allowbreak{}genericity}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} One unknown-target perfect intervention per latent node generically identifies an arbitrary finite nonlinear causal representation in the note's positive smooth compact-support, causal-minimal regime under shared diffeomorphic mixing. The observable ratio graph uses Gaussian-kernel MMD to compare the law of each observational-to-interventional likelihood ratio across environments; positive discrepancies on ancestral covers recover the transitive closure. Normalized affine interpolation toward a certified sparse edge witness makes this cover condition open dense in every nonempty causal-minimal derivative-sign stratum. Conditional ranks then recover componentwise coordinates and align intervention targets, while strict positivity and causal minimality make the true parent set the unique minimal ordered Markov boundary. On the overlapping smooth compact-support faithful regime, the result improves the arbitrary-dimensional intervention count of von Kugelgen et al. (2023); faithfulness is retained as a distinct stronger premise for that immediate corollary. The result does not settle their full \(\mathbb R^n\)/\(C^1\) class. A sample-split MMD theorem supplies simultaneous confidence edges conditional on its ratio-error event, while the generated-ratio conditional-CDF coordinate rate remains explicitly open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The closest invertible-mixing nonparametric result proves one unknown-target perfect intervention per node only for two latents and uses two interventions per node in arbitrary dimension. On the overlapping smooth compact-support faithful regime, reducing that arbitrary-dimensional count to one is unresolved. The anchor also treats a broader \(\mathbb R^n\)/\(C^1\) class, which the present compact \(C^2\)-mixing theorem does not settle. Jiang and Aragam recover a latent measurement-model graph under different graphical assumptions; Ahuja et al. use point-mass interventions and polynomial support geometry; Buchholz et al. combine unknown targets and nonlinear mixing with a linear Gaussian latent SCM.

\paragraph{Niche.} A mixing-invariant law of observed likelihood ratios can encode ancestry directly on environment labels, but its noncancellation must be derived inside normalized causal-minimal derivative-sign strata rather than assumed. Faithfulness remains a distinct stronger condition used only to state the immediate overlapping-regime corollary to the anchor.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves ratio-specific open density by analytic interpolation on the causal-minimal stratum, decodes the full causal representation and exact DAG because causal minimality prunes parents under strict positivity, gives conditional simultaneous MMD confidence edges, and isolates generated-ratio conditional-CDF theory as the remaining statistical question. Its intervention-count comparison is confined to the overlapping smooth compact-support faithful regime and makes no claim for the anchor's full \(\mathbb R^n\)/\(C^1\) class.

\section{Related work}

Von Kugelgen et al. (2023, Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 and Section 7) are the direct invertible-mixing comparator: targets are unknown, neither the graph nor an order is supplied, one perfect stochastic intervention per node identifies the graph and representation for two latents under a ratio-law witness, and the arbitrary-dimensional theorem uses two distinct paired interventions per node. On the overlapping smooth compact-support faithful regime, Theorem 2 improves that arbitrary-dimensional intervention count from two to one. Separately, the present theorem works in its own positive compact-support causal-minimal regime; this is a structural extension within the note's assumptions, not a claim to settle the comparator's full \(\mathbb R^n\)/\(C^1\) class. Faithfulness is retained as a distinct stronger premise for the immediate overlapping-regime corollary. Wendong et al. (2023, Theorem 4.2) use one target-aligned perfect stochastic intervention per node and arbitrary \(C^1\) diffeomorphic mixing with nonparametric mechanisms, but take the DAG and targets as inputs; on the relevant overlap, Theorem 2 recovers those supplied structural inputs. Yao et al. (2025, Assumption D.1 and Corollary D.1) similarly supply a topological target order. Jiang and Aragam (2023, Theorem 3.4) recover a maximal latent measurement-model graph, up to isolated latent edges, under marginal-independence, imaginary-subset, and isolated-edge conditions; they do not recover coordinates under shared invertible mixing, and neither model subsumes the other. Ahuja et al. (2023, Theorem 5.3) use one point-mass perfect intervention per latent and polynomial support geometry to recover intervened coordinates up to affine ambiguity, not the DAG; their assumptions are orthogonal to positive common-support stochastic interventions with arbitrary shared diffeomorphic mixing. Buchholz et al. (2023, Theorem 1) share unknown single-node targets and general nonlinear mixing but require a linear Gaussian latent SCM, again an incomparable regime. Var\i{}c\i{} et al. (2025, Theorem 29) use two uncoupled hard interventions per node under general transformations, while Ng et al. (2025, Theorems 1--2) use derivative-rich general environments with additive-Gaussian or heteroscedastic latent restrictions. Boeken, Forr\'e, and Mooij (2026, Definition 4 and Lemmas 3--4) provide normalized conditional-kernel interpolation and faithfulness topology for a fixed DAG, but not the surgical density-ratio analytic contrast or mixed target assignment. Gretton et al. (2012, Theorems 5, 7, and 13) provide characteristicness and directly observed two-sample MMD concentration, not plug-in ratio graphs. Xie (2023, Theorems 1--3) and Mammen, Rothe, and Schienle (2012, Theorem 1) provide complementary observed-argument and generated-covariate ingredients for the still-open generated-rank question. Lee, Jin, and Aragam (2026, Theorems 4.6--4.7) study finite-sample recovery under high-dimensional linear factor mixing and unknown multi-node interventions, a different statistical regime.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms]\label{ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms}
For every \(i\in[n]\), the mechanism \(p_i\) and intervention density \(q_i\) are strictly positive normalized \(C^3\) densities on their closed cubes.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (smooth full-support density condition on a compact product support, \cite{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:faithfulness]\label{ass:faithfulness}
The observational density \(p^0\) is faithful to the DAG \(G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (causal faithfulness, \cite{SpirtesGlymourScheines2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing]\label{ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing}
The same map \(f\) is a \(C^2\) diffeomorphism of \([0,1]^n\) onto \(\mathcal X\) in every environment.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (shared diffeomorphic mixing, \cite{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node]\label{ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node}
Besides the known observational law \(P^0\), the laws \(P^1,\ldots,P^n\) arise from distinct unknown targets and replace exactly one \(p_i\) by the corresponding parent-independent density \(q_i\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (unknown-target stochastic perfect intervention coverage, \cite{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign]\label{ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign}
For every \(i\in[n]\), \(s_i\,\partial_{v_i}\log\{q_i(v_i)/p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\}>0\) throughout the closed cube.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed-sign compact-support variant of the everywhere-change condition, \cite{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-environment-sampling]\label{ass:independent-environment-sampling}
Within each environment \(e\), the \(N_e\) observations are independent draws from \(P^e\), and the training and evaluation folds are independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent multi-sample sampling with sample splitting, \cite{GrettonEtAl2012KernelTwoSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-holder-radius]\label{ass:bounded-holder-radius}
Every \(p_i\), \(q_i\), and latent-coordinate log-ratio \(L_i\circ f\) has Holder norm of order \(\beta\) at most \(M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded Holder ball, \cite{Xie2023UniformConditionalCDF}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-lower-envelope]\label{ass:density-lower-envelope}
Every \(p_i\) and \(q_i\) is bounded below by \(c\) on its closed cube.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform full-support lower envelope, \cite{Xie2023UniformConditionalCDF}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:own-derivative-margin]\label{ass:own-derivative-margin}
For every \(i\in[n]\), \(s_i\,\partial_{v_i}L_i(f(v))\ge c\) throughout the closed cube.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This author-imposed strengthening is credible for compact-support exponential tilts and monotone likelihood-ratio mechanisms whose own-coordinate score stays uniformly away from zero. It is confined to the bounded generated-rank subclass and supplies quantitative stability of the inverse rank coordinate on the interior domain.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-interior-domain]\label{ass:common-interior-domain}
The evaluation domain satisfies \(\operatorname{dist}(f^{-1}(K),\partial[0,1]^n)\ge\rho\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (common interior compact evaluation domain, \cite{Xie2023UniformConditionalCDF}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-shared-mixing-geometry]\label{ass:bounded-shared-mixing-geometry}
The shared mixing map obeys \(\max\{\|f\|_{C^2([0,1]^n)},\|f^{-1}\|_{C^2(\mathcal X)},\sup_{v\in[0,1]^n}|\det Df(v)|,\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}|\det Df^{-1}(x)|\}\le M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This author-imposed strengthening is credible for compact-domain bi-Lipschitz \(C^2\) normalizing flows with bounded weights and uniformly conditioned Jacobians. It is confined to the bounded generated-rank subclass and supplies quantitative control of transformed coordinate neighborhoods and their densities.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-log-ratio-regularity]\label{ass:observed-log-ratio-regularity}
The observed log-ratios obey \(\max_{i\in[n]}\|L_i\|_{C^2(K)}\le M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded smooth generated regressors on an interior compactum, \cite{MammenRotheSchienle2012GeneratedCovariates}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-design-envelope]\label{ass:conditional-design-envelope}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:sparse-witness-certificate]\label{prop:sparse-witness-certificate}
The constructions \(\theta^{\mathrm{sp}}\) and \(\theta^{\mathrm{can}}\) are positive, normalized, \(C^\infty\), faithful to \(1\to2\) plus isolated node \(3\), and satisfy the prescribed derivative signs after reflection. The sparse witness obeys \(\mathbb E_{P^0}[R_2^2]-\mathbb E_{P^1}[R_2^2]>3\times10^{-4}\) and \(D_{12}>5\times10^{-8}\), whereas the entire direct-edge ratio law under \(\theta^{\mathrm{can}}\) is identical in environments \(0\) and \(1\), so \(D_{12}=0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(c=4/(\exp(4)-1)\), \(h(z)=2z-1\), and \(q(z)=c\exp(4z)\).  All integrals below are with respect to Lebesgue measure on the displayed unit cubes.  Direct calculation gives
\[
 \int_0^1q(z)\,dz=\frac{c}{4}(\exp(4)-1)=1,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1h(z)\,dz=0.
\]
Consequently both intervention densities and the two displayed child mechanisms are normalized.  All of them are \(C^\infty\).  For the sparse construction write \(K(x)=h(x)\), and for the cancellation construction write
\[
 K(x)=H(x):=\frac{\exp(4x)-1}{\exp(4)-1}-x.
\]
Convexity of \(x\mapsto\exp(4x)\) on \([0,1]\) gives
\[
 1\leq \exp(4x)\leq 1+x(\exp(4)-1),
\]
so \(-1\leq H(x)\leq0\).  Thus in either construction \(|K(x)|\leq1\), and hence
\[
 \frac9{10}\leq p_2(y\mid x)=1+\frac1{10}K(x)h(y)\leq\frac{11}{10}.
\]
This proves strict positivity on the closed support, including its boundary.  The sparse \(K=h\) is nonconstant.  The cancellation \(K=H\) is also nonconstant, since
\[
 H'(x)=q(x)-1
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 H'(0)=c-1<0;
\]
here \(c<1\) follows from \(\exp(4)>1+4=5\).  Hence \(p_2(\cdot\mid x)\) genuinely depends on \(x\) in both constructions.  The joint density factorizes over \(1\to2\) with node \(3\) independent, so it has every conditional independence implied by that DAG.  Its only pairs that are d-connected given a set disjoint from the pair are \(1,2\), with the optional conditioning set contained in \(\{3\}\).  Since \(V_3\) is independent and \(p_2(\cdot\mid x)\) varies with \(x\), neither \(V_1\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}V_2\) nor \(V_1\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}V_2\mid V_3\) holds.  Thus both observational laws are faithful to \(1\to2\) plus isolated node \(3\).

For the unreflected constructions, the own-coordinate derivative of the child log ratio is
\[
 \partial_y\log\frac{q(y)}{p_2(y\mid x)}
 =4-\frac{(1/5)K(x)}{1+(1/10)K(x)h(y)}
 \geq 4-\frac{2}{9}=\frac{34}{9}>0,
\]
because \(K(x)/p_2(y\mid x)\leq10/9\).  At nodes \(1\) and \(3\) the derivative equals \(4\).  For a prescribed sign vector define \(T_\ell(z)=z\) if \(s_\ell=1\) and \(T_\ell(z)=1-z\) if \(s_\ell=-1\), and apply \(T_\ell\) to every occurrence of coordinate \(\ell\) in all observational and interventional densities.  This unit-Jacobian coordinate bijection preserves normalization, positivity, smoothness, faithfulness, ratio laws, and MMD values.  Its derivative is \(s_\ell\), so the displayed positive own derivative becomes a derivative of sign \(s_\ell\).  This verifies the sign assertion for every \(s\).

It remains to certify the two quantitative assertions.  Reflections do not change the required expectations, so take the positive-sign sparse construction and put
\[
 r_2(x,y)=\frac{q(y)}{1+(1/10)h(x)h(y)},
 \qquad
 A(x)=\int_0^1\frac{q(y)^2}{1+(1/10)h(x)h(y)}\,dy.
\]
Cancellation of one factor of \(p_2\) in \(p^0r_2^2\), followed by the analogous calculation under the intervention on node \(1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^0}[R_2^2]=\int_0^1A(x)\,dx,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{P^1}[R_2^2]=\int_0^1q(x)A(x)\,dx.
\]
For \(a=h(x)/10\), define
\[
 w_a(y)=\frac{q(y)^2}{(1+a h(y))^2}.
\]
Differentiation under the integral is justified by smoothness and the uniform lower bound \(p_2\geq9/10\), and yields
\[
 -A'(x)=\frac15\int_0^1h(y)w_a(y)\,dy.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{d}{dy}\log w_a(y)=8-\frac{4a}{1+a h(y)}\geq\frac{68}{9},
 \qquad
 w_a(y)\geq\frac{c^2}{(11/10)^2}.
\]
Thus \(w_a'(y)\geq\lambda\), where
\[
 \lambda:=\frac{68}{9}\frac{c^2}{(11/10)^2}.
\]
The function \(g(y)=w_a(y)-\lambda y\) is nondecreasing.  Since \(h\) is nondecreasing and has integral zero,
\[
 \int_0^1h(y)g(y)\,dy
 =\frac12\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
       (h(y)-h(z))(g(y)-g(z))\,dy\,dz\geq0.
\]
Because \(\int_0^1h(y)y\,dy=1/6\), it follows that
\[
 \int_0^1h(y)w_a(y)\,dy\geq\frac{\lambda}{6},
 \qquad
 -A'(x)\geq\frac{\lambda}{30}.
\]
The distribution function of \(q\) is
\[
 F_q(x)=\frac{\exp(4x)-1}{\exp(4)-1}\leq x,
\]
where the inequality is the convexity inequality already used above.  If \(U\) is uniform and \(X=F_q^{-1}(U)\), then \(X\geq U\), \(X\) has density \(q\), and the derivative bound on \(A\) gives pointwise
\[
 A(U)-A(X)\geq\frac{\lambda}{30}(X-U).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^0}[R_2^2]-\mathbb E_{P^1}[R_2^2]
 \geq\frac{\lambda}{30}\left(\mathbb E_q[X]-\frac12\right).
\]
Integration by parts gives
\[
 \mathbb E_q[X]-\frac12
 =\frac{1}{1-\exp(-4)}-\frac34.
\]
The exponential series supplies entirely rational bounds.  Indeed,
\[
 \exp(4)>1+4+\frac{4^2}{2}=13,
\]
while, because successive terms from the term of order eight onward have ratio at most \(4/9\),
\[
 \exp(4)
 <\sum_{m=0}^{7}\frac{4^m}{m!}
   +\frac{4^8/8!}{1-4/9}
 =\frac{86203}{1575}<55.
\]
Hence \(c>2/27\) and
\[
 \mathbb E_q[X]-\frac12>\frac{29}{108}.
\]
Combining these inequalities gives the strict certificate
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^0}[R_2^2]-\mathbb E_{P^1}[R_2^2]
 >\frac1{30}\frac{68}{9}\frac{4}{729}\frac{100}{121}\frac{29}{108}
 =\frac{19720}{64304361}>\frac3{10000};
\]
the last comparison is the positive integer difference
\(19720\cdot10000-3\cdot64304361=4286917\).

We next turn this gap into the claimed quantitative MMD bound without invoking an unrecorded characteristic-kernel theorem.  Since \(\exp(4)>13\),
\[
 q(y)\leq\frac{4\exp(4)}{\exp(4)-1}<\frac{13}{3},
 \qquad
 0<r_2(x,y)<\frac{10}{9}\frac{13}{3}<5.
\]
Let \(\nu=\operatorname{Law}_{P^0}(R_2)-\operatorname{Law}_{P^1}(R_2)\).  The Gaussian kernel has the explicit \(\ell^2\) feature expansion
\[
 \exp(-(a-b)^2)
 =\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{2^m}{m!}}\exp(-a^2)a^m\right)
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{2^m}{m!}}\exp(-b^2)b^m\right).
\]
Because the squared norm of this feature vector is \(k(a,a)=1\), its Bochner mean exists, and the definition of MMD gives, coordinate by coordinate,
\[
 \left|\int \exp(-r^2)r^m\,d\nu(r)\right|
 \leq\sqrt{\frac{m!}{2^m}}\,D_{12}.
\]
On \([0,5]\), expand \(r^2=\exp(-r^2)r^2\sum_{\ell\geq0}r^{2\ell}/\ell!\).  For \(L=100\), the preceding coordinate inequalities and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \left|\int r^2\,d\nu(r)\right|
 \leq D_{12}\sum_{\ell=0}^{100}
       \frac{\sqrt{(2\ell+2)!}}{2^{\ell+1}\ell!}
   +50\sum_{\ell>100}\frac{25^\ell}{\ell!}.
\]
Writing \(m=\ell+1\), the central-binomial inequality
\(\binom{2m}{m}\leq4^m\) shows that the \(\ell\)-th coefficient is at most \(\ell+1\).  Thus the finite sum is at most
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{100}(\ell+1)=5151.
\]
For the tail, successive terms have ratio at most \(25/102\), whence the exact rational comparison
\[
 50\sum_{\ell>100}\frac{25^\ell}{\ell!}
 \leq 50\frac{25^{101}}{101!}\frac{102}{77}<10^{-15}.
\]
Using the already proved moment gap therefore yields
\[
 D_{12}>\frac{3\times10^{-4}-10^{-15}}{5151}>5\times10^{-8},
\]
where both final inequalities reduce, after multiplication by their positive integer denominators, to integer comparisons.

Finally consider the cancellation mechanism.  Its endpoint identities and derivative are
\[
 H(0)=H(1)=0,
 \qquad
 H'(x)=q(x)-1.
\]
For a bounded continuous test function \(\psi\) and fixed \(y\), set
\[
 B_y(u)=\left(1+\frac{u h(y)}{10}\right)
 \psi\!\left(\frac{q(y)}{1+u h(y)/10}\right).
\]
This is continuous for \(u\in[-1,0]\), and so has an antiderivative \(\mathcal B_y\).  The integrands are bounded on the compact support, permitting Fubini's theorem.  Direct substitution of the observational and parent-interventional densities gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_{P^1}[\psi(R_2)]-\mathbb E_{P^0}[\psi(R_2)]\\
 &\quad=\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
       (q(x)-1)B_y(H(x))\,dx\,dy\\
 &\quad=\int_0^1
       \bigl\{\mathcal B_y(H(1))-\mathcal B_y(H(0))\bigr\}\,dy=0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equality for every bounded continuous \(\psi\) implies equality of the two ratio laws.  Consequently their Gaussian mean embeddings coincide and \(D_{12}=0\).  The preceding reflection argument preserves this equality.  All assertions of the proposition follow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The separating endpoint makes the analytic perturbation nontrivial, while the exact cancellation example shows that cover separation is genuinely generic rather than automatic. \textit{Gap.} Von Kugelgen et al. (2023, equation (3.2)) motivate fine tuning abstractly but do not give this compact-support certified separator and full-law cancellation pair. \textit{Consumer.} The endpoint and cancellation pair are the bounded early kill test for the open-density theorem.

\begin{lemma}[lem:analytic-edge-perturbation]\label{lem:analytic-edge-perturbation}
After the harmless target relabeling \(\pi=\operatorname{id}\), fix \(\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}\) and an edge \(j\to i\) of \(G\). Along the path \(\theta^t\), \[F_{ji}(\theta^t)=\int_{[0,1]^n}\frac{(q_i^t)^2(q_j^t-p_j^t)\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}p_\ell^t}{p_i^t}\,dv\] is real analytic on an open interval containing \([0,1]\), is nonzero at \(t=1\), and has isolated zeros. For every relative \(C^2\) neighborhood of \(\theta\), some sufficiently small \(t>0\) remains in \(\Theta_{G,s}\) and satisfies \(F_{ji}(\theta^t)\ne0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Relabeling the interventional environments by the bijection \(\pi\) changes neither the probability laws nor the relative product topology, so it suffices to prove the claim for \(\pi=\operatorname{id}\). Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}\) and an edge \(j\to i\). Let \(\theta^*\) denote the reflected, edge-specific sparse mechanism used by \(\texttt{def:affine-path}\): its child conditional at \(i\) depends only on the parent \(j\), and all unused parent arguments and all other observational edges are ignored. The endpoint need not be causally minimal for the full graph; only a neighborhood of the starting point will be used. By \(\texttt{def:affine-path}\),
\[
 p_\ell^t=(1-t)p_\ell+t p_\ell^*,
 \qquad
 q_\ell^t=(1-t)q_\ell+t q_\ell^*.
\]

We first derive the displayed contrast identity. By \(\texttt{ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node}\), the observational density, the density after intervention on \(j\), and the ratio for intervention on \(i\) are, with every conditional evaluated at its own node and parent arguments,
\[
 p^{0,t}=\prod_{\ell=1}^n p_\ell^t,
 \qquad
 p^{j,t}=q_j^t\prod_{\ell\ne j}p_\ell^t,
 \qquad
 R_i^t=\frac{q_i^t}{p_i^t}.
\]
Strict positivity and compactness make all ratios bounded, so the following integrals are finite. Since \(i\ne j\), cancellation of one factor \(p_i^t\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_{ji}(\theta^t)
 &=\int_{[0,1]^n}(R_i^t)^2\bigl(p^{j,t}-p^{0,t}\bigr)\,dv\\
 &=\int_{[0,1]^n}
   \frac{(q_i^t)^2(q_j^t-p_j^t)
   \prod_{\ell\ne i,j}p_\ell^t}{p_i^t}\,dv.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the asserted identity follows from the intervention factorization rather than from the definition of the path.

Each factor in the numerator is affine in \(t\). By \(\texttt{ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms}\) and \(\texttt{prop:sparse-witness-certificate}\), both endpoint conditionals \(p_i\) and \(p_i^*\) are continuous and strictly positive on their compact domains. Consequently \(p_i^t\) has a positive minimum jointly over \([0,1]\) and the relevant latent cube. Because \(p_i^t\) is affine in \(t\), the same denominator remains bounded away from zero on \(I\times[0,1]^{1+|\operatorname{pa}_G(i)|}\) for some connected open interval \(I\supset[0,1]\). For any \(t_0\in I\), after shrinking a neighborhood of \(t_0\) if necessary,
\[
 \frac{1}{p_i^t}
 =\frac{1}{p_i^{t_0}}
  \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
  \left[-(t-t_0)\frac{p_i^*-p_i}{p_i^{t_0}}\right]^m,
\]
and the series converges uniformly on the compact latent cube. Multiplication by the polynomial numerator and termwise integration are therefore valid. Hence \(t\mapsto F_{ji}(\theta^t)\) is real analytic on \(I\).

At \(t=1\), every mechanism other than the two-node sparse edge is uniform, so normalization of every coordinate other than \(v_j,v_i\) reduces the integral to the reflected two-node calculation. Coordinate reflections preserve both ratio laws and their moments. Therefore \(\texttt{prop:sparse-witness-certificate}\) gives
\[
 F_{ji}(\theta^1)
 =\mathbb E_{P^j}[R_i^2]-\mathbb E_{P^0}[R_i^2]
 <-3\times10^{-4}.
\]
In particular, the analytic function is not identically zero.

For completeness, we derive the required isolation property. If the zeros accumulated at \(t_0\in I\), the Taylor series at \(t_0\) could have no least nonzero coefficient, because a least nonzero coefficient of order \(m\) would factor the function as \((t-t_0)^m\) times a nonvanishing analytic function near \(t_0\). Thus all Taylor coefficients would vanish and the function would be zero on a neighborhood of \(t_0\). Let \(S\subseteq I\) be the set of points having a neighborhood on which the function vanishes. The set \(S\) is open. It is also closed in \(I\): if \(t_r\in S\) and \(t_r\to t\in I\), then every derivative vanishes at every \(t_r\); continuity of all derivatives makes every derivative vanish at \(t\), and the Taylor expansion makes the function vanish near \(t\). Since \(I\) is connected, a nonempty \(S\) would equal \(I\), contradicting the value at \(1\). Hence every zero is isolated.

It remains to show that useful arbitrarily small values of \(t\) remain in the stratum. Normalization is preserved as an affine equality: for every fixed parent value,
\[
 \int_0^1p_\ell^t(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})\,dv_\ell
 =(1-t)+t=1,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1q_\ell^t(v_\ell)\,dv_\ell=1.
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), convexity preserves strict positivity, and the path mechanisms remain \(C^3\). Moreover,
\[
 \|p_\ell^t-p_\ell\|_{C^2}=t\|p_\ell^*-p_\ell\|_{C^2},
 \qquad
 \|q_\ell^t-q_\ell\|_{C^2}=t\|q_\ell^*-q_\ell\|_{C^2},
\]
so \(\theta^t\to\theta\) in the topology of \(\texttt{def:model-stratum}\).

For the sign constraint, define on the corresponding closed cube
\[
 g_\ell(\vartheta)
 =s_\ell\,\partial_{v_\ell}
   \log\!\left(\frac{q_{\ell,\vartheta}}{p_{\ell,\vartheta}}\right).
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign}\), continuity and compactness give \(\min g_\ell(\theta)>0\) for every \(\ell\). On a neighborhood where \(p_{\ell,\vartheta}\) and \(q_{\ell,\vartheta}\) retain positive lower bounds, the map \(\vartheta\mapsto g_\ell(\vartheta)\) is continuous from product \(C^2\) to \(C^0\), because it uses only first derivatives and division by functions bounded away from zero. Thus all finitely many sign inequalities persist for small \(t\). Derivatives at the boundary are the one-sided restrictions of the continuous derivatives on the closed cubes.

Causal minimality is also locally open. For every edge \(a\to b\), put \(S_{ab}=\operatorname{pa}_G(b)\setminus\{a\}\). For a path mechanism \(\vartheta\), let \(g_{A,\vartheta}\) be the marginal density of its observational joint density on coordinates \(A\), with \(g_{\varnothing,\vartheta}=1\), and define
\[
 J_{ab}(\vartheta)
 =\int
 \left(g_{\{a,b\}\cup S_{ab},\vartheta}g_{S_{ab},\vartheta}
       -g_{\{a\}\cup S_{ab},\vartheta}g_{\{b\}\cup S_{ab},\vartheta}\right)^2
 \,d(v_a,v_b,v_{S_{ab}}).
\]
Because the joint density is strictly positive, conditional independence of \(V_a\) and \(V_b\) given \(V_{S_{ab}}\) is equivalent to
\[
 g_{\{a,b\}\cup S_{ab},\vartheta}g_{S_{ab},\vartheta}
 =g_{\{a\}\cup S_{ab},\vartheta}g_{\{b\}\cup S_{ab},\vartheta}
\]
almost everywhere: this is obtained by writing the conditional joint and marginal densities and multiplying by the positive square of \(g_{S_{ab},\vartheta}\). Continuity promotes an almost-everywhere identity to an everywhere identity. It follows that \(J_{ab}(\vartheta)=0\) is equivalent to this conditional independence. Hence \(\texttt{ass:causal-minimality}\) gives \(J_{ab}(\theta)>0\) for every edge. Products of finitely many mechanisms, marginal integration over fixed compact cubes, and the squared integral are continuous under product \(C^0\), hence product \(C^2\), convergence. Since \(G\) has finitely many edges, all inequalities \(J_{ab}>0\) persist on one neighborhood of \(\theta\). This is exactly preservation of the edgewise causal-minimality condition; the DAG factorization itself is preserved because every \(p_\ell^t\) remains a conditional indexed by the same parent set.

Combining normalization, positivity, smoothness, sign preservation, and causal minimality, there is \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(\theta^t\in\Theta_{G,s}\) for \(0\le t<\varepsilon\). If \(F_{ji}(\theta)\ne0\), continuity makes the contrast nonzero for every sufficiently small \(t\). If \(F_{ji}(\theta)=0\), isolation of the zero at \(t=0\) gives the same conclusion for every sufficiently small positive \(t\). Choosing such a \(t\) below \(\varepsilon\) and below the radius imposed by the given relative \(C^2\) neighborhood proves the last assertion.

Finally, the requested cancellation-to-sparse check is an explicit special case. On \(1\to2\) plus the isolated node, the root mechanisms and intervention densities agree at the endpoints, while
\[
 p_2^t(y\mid x)=1+\frac{1}{10}K_t(x)h(y),
 \qquad
 K_t(x)=(1-t)H(x)+t h(x).
\]
Since both the distribution function \((\exp(4x)-1)/(\exp(4)-1)\) and \(x\) lie in \([0,1]\), one has \(H\in[-1,1]\); also \(h\in[-1,1]\). Thus \(p_2^t\) is normalized because \(\int_0^1h(y)\,dy=0\), and it lies in \([9/10,11/10]\) for every \(t\in[0,1]\). Its positive-sign child margin satisfies
\[
 \partial_y\log\!\left(\frac{q_2(y)}{p_2^t(y\mid x)}\right)
 =4-\frac{(1/5)K_t(x)}{1+(1/10)K_t(x)h(y)}
 \ge \frac{34}{9},
\]
and coordinate reflections give the corresponding prescribed signed margins. Moreover \(K_t\) is nonconstant. Indeed, for \(t<1\), constancy would imply
\[
 (1-t)\{q_1(x)-1\}+2t=0
\]
for every \(x\), which is impossible because \(q_1\) is nonconstant; at \(t=1\), one has \(K_1'=2\). Therefore the conditional law of node \(2\) varies with node \(1\) for every \(t\). Since the root density is positive, the two nodes are dependent, so the edge is causally minimal. The third node remains jointly independent of them. These are exactly the nontrivial separation facts for this three-node DAG, so the law is faithful throughout the segment.

Direct substitution in the already-derived identity gives
\[
 F_{12}(\theta^t)=
 \int_0^1\!\int_0^1
 \frac{q_2(y)^2(q_1(x)-1)}{1+(1/10)K_t(x)h(y)}\,dx\,dy.
\]
The denominator bound proves analyticity on an open interval containing \([0,1]\). By \(\texttt{prop:sparse-witness-certificate}\), the full ratio laws coincide at \(t=0\), so this expression is zero there, while it is less than \(-3\times10^{-4}\) at \(t=1\). This independently verifies the analytic moment identity and the positive normalization, common support, faithfulness, causal minimality, and derivative-sign margins at, and indeed along the whole segment from, the cancellation endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:generic-cover-separation]\label{thm:generic-cover-separation}
For every finite labeled DAG \(G\), sign vector \(s\), and target permutation \(\pi\) with \(\Theta_{G,s}\ne\varnothing\), the set \(\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\) is open dense in \(\Theta_{G,s}\). Therefore \(\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\) is open dense and residual, and its complement is closed nowhere dense. For the empty graph, both sets equal \(\Theta_{G,s}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that \(G\) has at least one edge. For each edge \(a\to b\) of \(G\), let \(j=\pi^{-1}(a)\) and \(i=\pi^{-1}(b)\), and define
\[
 \mathcal O_{ab}
 =\{\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}:F_{ji}(\theta)\ne0\}.
\]
This set is relatively open. Indeed, in a sufficiently small product \(C^2\) neighborhood of any \(\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}\), compactness and the positivity included in \(\texttt{def:model-stratum}\) give common positive lower bounds on every denominator \(p_\ell\). The exact integral formula proved in \(\texttt{lem:analytic-edge-perturbation}\) then has an integrand that varies uniformly under product \(C^2\), and therefore product \(C^0\), perturbations. Integration over the fixed compact cube shows that \(F_{ji}\) is continuous locally. Hence \(\mathcal O_{ab}\) is relatively open.

The same set is dense. Given \(\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}\) and a relative neighborhood \(W\) of \(\theta\), relabel the intervention environments so their labels agree with their latent targets, apply \(\texttt{lem:analytic-edge-perturbation}\) to \(a\to b\), and undo the relabeling. The lemma supplies \(\theta'\in W\) for which \(F_{\pi^{-1}(a),\pi^{-1}(b)}(\theta')\ne0\). Thus every \(\mathcal O_{ab}\) is open dense.

The edge set is finite, and \(\texttt{def:edge-separated-set}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}
 =\bigcap_{a\to b\ \mathrm{in}\ G}\mathcal O_{ab}.
\]
A finite intersection of open dense subsets of a topological space is open dense. Explicitly, if \(W\) is nonempty and relatively open, then \(W\cap\mathcal O_{a_1b_1}\) is again nonempty and relatively open; iterating through the finite edge list leaves a nonempty intersection. Hence \(\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\) is open dense in \(\Theta_{G,s}\). Because the perturbation lemma starts from an arbitrary point, no connectedness of the causal-minimal sign stratum is required.

We next prove \(\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\subseteq\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\). Fix \(\theta\in\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\) and suppose \(\pi(j)\lessdot_G\pi(i)\). An ancestral cover in a DAG is a directed edge: a directed path of length at least two would contain an internal vertex strictly between its endpoints in the ancestral order. Therefore \(\texttt{def:edge-separated-set}\) gives \(F_{ji}\ne0\), so the second moments of \(R_i\) under \(P^j\) and \(P^0\) differ.

Here is a direct verification that this forces positive Gaussian-kernel MMD. Strict positivity and continuity on compact cubes imply that \(R_i=q_{\pi(i)}/p_{\pi(i)}\) is bounded, say \(0<R_i\le B<\infty\). Define
\[
 \Phi(r)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{2^m}{m!}}e^{-r^2}r^m\right)_{m\ge0}\in\ell^2.
\]
The exponential series gives
\[
 \langle\Phi(a),\Phi(b)\rangle_{\ell^2}=e^{-(a-b)^2}=k(a,b),
 \qquad
 \|\Phi(r)\|_{\ell^2}=1.
\]
Thus, by the definition of MMD as the distance between kernel mean embeddings, \(D_{ji}=0\) would make the two mean feature vectors equal and would imply, for every \(m\ge0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^j}[e^{-R_i^2}R_i^m]
 =\mathbb E_{P^0}[e^{-R_i^2}R_i^m].
\]
The identity
\[
 r^2=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-r^2}r^{2\ell+2}}{\ell!}
\]
converges uniformly for \(0\le r\le B\). Termwise expectation would therefore make the two second moments equal, contradicting \(F_{ji}\ne0\). Hence \(D_{ji}>0\), and \(\texttt{def:cover-separated-set}\) proves the inclusion.

It remains to prove the topology asserted for \(\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\). For fixed \(j,i\), write \(\mu_e^i=\operatorname{Law}_{P^e}(R_i)\). The squared discrepancy is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{ji}^2
 &=\iint k(r,r')\,d\mu_0^i(r)d\mu_0^i(r')
   +\iint k(r,r')\,d\mu_j^i(r)d\mu_j^i(r')\\
 &\quad-2\iint k(r,r')\,d\mu_0^i(r)d\mu_j^i(r').
\end{aligned}
\]
Each term can be written as an integral over one or two copies of the fixed latent cube, with integrand formed from the environment densities, the ratios, and the bounded continuous kernel \(k\). On a local neighborhood with common positive denominator bounds, both the environment densities and ratios converge uniformly whenever the mechanisms converge in product \(C^2\). Their ranges lie in a common compact interval, on which \(k\) is uniformly continuous. The three displayed integrands therefore converge uniformly. Consequently \(D_{ji}^2\), and then \(D_{ji}\) by continuity of the square root on \([0,\infty)\), is continuous.

There are finitely many ancestral covers, so
\[
 \mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}
 =\bigcap_{\pi(j)\lessdot_G\pi(i)}
   \{\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}:D_{ji}(\theta)>0\}
\]
is relatively open. It contains the dense set \(\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\), so it is dense. Its complement is closed and has empty interior; being equal to its closure, the complement is nowhere dense. Equivalently, the complement is meagre and \(\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\) is residual.

If \(G\) is empty, both the edge condition in \(\texttt{def:edge-separated-set}\) and the cover condition in \(\texttt{def:cover-separated-set}\) are intersections over empty index sets. Both sets therefore equal \(\Theta_{G,s}\), which proves all conclusions in the empty-graph case.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem derives generic ratio separation from normalized mechanisms; causal minimality prunes parents in the downstream decoder. \textit{Gap.} Von Kugelgen et al. (2023, Theorem 3.2 and equation (3.2)) assume bivariate ratio-law separation; no cited result proves open density of cover separation for every finite DAG and fixed derivative-sign stratum. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem certifies generic population decoding in causal-minimal strata, including disconnected strata containing roots, isolates, forks, colliders, redundant ancestral edges, and opposite parent effects.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-ratio-decoder]\label{thm:exact-ratio-decoder}
For every mechanism in \(\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\), the observed laws \((P^0,\ldots,P^n)\) identify the latent representation and structure via \(\mathscr D\): \(\operatorname{TC}(H_D)=\operatorname{TC}(G^\pi)\); each \(U_i\) equals \(Q_{\pi(i)}(V_{\pi(i)})\) when \(s_{\pi(i)}=+1\) and \(1-Q_{\pi(i)}(V_{\pi(i)})\) when \(s_{\pi(i)}=-1\); and environment \(i\) is thereby aligned with its recovered coordinate. Every topological ordering of \(H_D\) places the environment-label parent set \(\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i)))\) inside \(B_i\). Under the observational law \(P^0\), for every \(i\in[n]\) and every subset \(A\subseteq B_i\), \[U_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{P^0}U_{B_i\setminus A}\mid U_A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi^{-1}(\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i)))\subseteq A.\] Consequently causal minimality makes \(\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i)))\) the unique inclusion-minimal admissible conditioning set, so parent pruning returns exactly \(G^\pi\), independently of the selected topological ordering. Any compatible causal-minimal representation satisfying the same positive normalized scalar perfect-intervention, shared \(C^2\)-mixing, and own-derivative conditions differs only by simultaneous relabeling and componentwise \(C^2\) diffeomorphisms. On the overlapping positive \(C^3\) compact-cube, \(C^2\)-mixing faithful regime, this improves the arbitrary-dimensional intervention count of von Kugelgen et al. (2023, Theorem 3.4) from two perfect interventions per node to one. Within the present regime, the decoding and uniqueness conclusions extend structurally from faithful to causal-minimal mechanisms; they do not settle the anchor's full \(\mathbb R^n\), \(C^1\) class. At \(n=2\), Gaussian-MMD positivity supplies the continuous law-separation witness used in their Theorem 3.2. Relative to Wendong et al. (2023, Theorem 4.2) and Yao et al. (2025, Corollary D.1), the decoder recovers rather than takes as given the graph or topological order and intervention alignment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We distinguish the structural target \((f^{-1},G,\pi)\), modulo simultaneous relabeling and componentwise diffeomorphisms, from the observable functional \(\mathscr D(P^0,\ldots,P^n)\).  The proof below shows that the latter recovers the former.

Write \(P_i=\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\).  The structural nonredundancy condition used below is precisely \texttt{ass:causal-minimality}:
\[
 b\in P_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 V_i\mathbin{\not\!\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{P^0}V_b
 \mid V_{P_i\setminus\{b\}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Full observational faithfulness, when additionally imposed, implies (1): the length-one path \(b\to i\) has no internal vertex and is not blocked by conditioning on \(P_i\setminus\{b\}\).  Thus \texttt{ass:faithfulness} is used only for the faithful-overlap corollary at the end, whereas the primary decoder and uniqueness argument use (1).

First relabel the latent coordinates by the intervention environments: set \(\bar V_i=V_{\pi(i)}\), pull \(G\), the mechanisms, and the signs back by \(\pi\), and prove the result with \(\pi=\operatorname{id}\).  This is an allowed simultaneous relabeling.  Strict positivity in \texttt{ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms} makes every ratio below finite and continuous on the closed cube.  By \texttt{ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing}, change of variables under the common map \(f\) cancels its Jacobian.  The one-target replacement in \texttt{ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node} therefore gives, pointwise on \([0,1]^n\),
\[
 R_i(f(v))=\frac{p^i(v)}{p^0(v)}
 =\frac{q_i(v_i)}{p_i(v_i\mid v_{P_i})}
 =:r_i(v),
 \qquad
 L_i(f(v))=\lambda_i(v):=\log r_i(v).
\tag{2}
\]

We next identify the ancestral order.  Put \(A_i=\operatorname{an}_G(i)\cup\{i\}\).  This is an ancestral set containing \(P_i\cup\{i\}\).  If \(j\ne i\) and \(j\notin\operatorname{an}_G(i)\), integrate either the observational factorization or the factorization after intervention \(j\) over \([0,1]^{[n]\setminus A_i}\) in reverse topological order.  At every eliminated vertex the relevant factor is a normalized conditional density, except at \(j\), where it is the normalized density \(q_j\); no eliminated vertex is a parent of a retained vertex because \(A_i\) is ancestral.  Hence
\[
 p^j_{A_i}(v_{A_i})=p^0_{A_i}(v_{A_i})
 =\prod_{a\in A_i}p_a(v_a\mid v_{P_a}).
\tag{3}
\]
The function \(r_i\) is measurable with respect to \(V_{P_i\cup\{i\}}\).  Equation (3) therefore implies
\[
 \operatorname{Law}_{P^j}(R_i)=\operatorname{Law}_{P^0}(R_i),
 \qquad j\notin\operatorname{an}_G(i),\quad j\ne i,
\tag{4}
\]
and hence \(D_{ji}=0\).  Consequently every arrow of \(H_D\) is an ancestral relation of \(G\).  Conversely, \texttt{def:cover-separated-set} puts every cover of the ancestral order into \(H_D\).  Covers generate the whole finite ancestral order: starting with any comparable pair that is not a cover, insert a strictly intermediate vertex; repetition terminates because \([n]\) is finite and produces a saturated chain of covers.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{TC}(H_D)\subseteq\operatorname{TC}(G),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TC}(G)
 =\operatorname{TC}\bigl(\{j\to i:j\lessdot_G i\}\bigr)
 \subseteq\operatorname{TC}(H_D),
\tag{5}
\]
so \(\operatorname{TC}(H_D)=\operatorname{TC}(G)\).  In particular, \(H_D\) is acyclic.

Fix any topological ordering of \(H_D\), and let \(B_i\) be the set of labels before \(i\).  Equality (5) says that this ordering respects every ancestral relation of \(G\); hence
\[
 P_i\subseteq B_i.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, \(B_i\) is ancestral: if \(b\in B_i\) and \(a\) is an ancestor of \(b\), then (5) gives a directed \(H_D\)-path from \(a\) to \(b\), so \(a\) precedes \(b\).

For \(b\in B_i\), the function \(\lambda_b\) in (2) depends only on \(v_b\) and \(v_{P_b}\), all of whose parent coordinates precede \(b\).  Thus
\[
 \Lambda_{B_i}:v_{B_i}\longmapsto
 \bigl(\lambda_b(v_b,v_{P_b})\bigr)_{b\in B_i}
\tag{7}
\]
is triangular in the selected order.  By \texttt{ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign},
\[
 s_b\,\partial_{v_b}\lambda_b>0
\tag{8}
\]
throughout the closed cube.  Continuity and compactness make the absolute value in (8) bounded away from zero.  Given the output of (7), one recovers the coordinates successively: with the earlier parents fixed, the \(b\)-th output is a strictly monotone function of \(v_b\).  Hence \(\Lambda_{B_i}\) is injective.  It is continuous, and its inverse on its image is continuous: if image points converge, compactness supplies a convergent subsequence of their preimages, and continuity and injectivity force every such subsequential limit to be the unique preimage of the limiting point.  Consequently
\[
 \sigma(L_{B_i})=\sigma(V_{B_i}).
\tag{9}
\]

The set \(B_i\cup\{i\}\) is ancestral.  Marginalizing the intervention-\(i\) factorization outside that set as in (3) yields
\[
 p^i_{B_i\cup\{i\}}(v_{B_i},v_i)
 =q_i(v_i)\prod_{b\in B_i}p_b(v_b\mid v_{P_b}).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus, under \(P^i\), \(V_i\) has density \(q_i\) and is independent of \(V_{B_i}\).  If \(\ell_{B_i}=\Lambda_{B_i}(v_{B_i})\), equations (2), (9), and (10) supply the following conditional-distribution version on the model support:
\[
 C_i(t\mid\ell_{B_i})
 =\int_0^1
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{\lambda_i(w,v_{P_i})\le t\right\}q_i(w)\,dw.
\tag{11}
\]
The continuous inverse following (7), the strictly monotone inverse furnished by (8), and continuity of \(q_i\) show that (11) is continuous on its model support, including its relative boundary.  It is therefore the continuous version required in the definition of \(C_i\), rather than only an almost-everywhere version.

Evaluate (11) at \(t=L_i(f(v))=\lambda_i(v_i,v_{P_i})\).  Strict monotonicity, the absence of atoms under the density \(q_i\), and normalization give
\[
 U_i(f(v))=
 \begin{cases}
 Q_i(v_i),&s_i=+1,\\[3pt]
 1-Q_i(v_i),&s_i=-1.
 \end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(q_i\) is positive and \(C^3\) on the compact interval, both alternatives in (12) are componentwise \(C^2\) diffeomorphisms of the closed interval, with one-sided derivatives at its boundary.  Because (12) constructs \(U_i\) using environment \(i\), it also aligns that environment with the recovered coordinate it targets.

We now prove exact parent pruning using only (1).  Componentwise bimeasurable bijections preserve conditional independence, so (12) implies, for every \(A\subseteq B_i\),
\[
 U_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{P^0}U_{B_i\setminus A}\mid U_A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 V_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{P^0}V_{B_i\setminus A}\mid V_A.
\tag{13}
\]
Marginalization of the observational factorization outside the ancestral set \(B_i\cup\{i\}\), followed by division by its strictly positive \(B_i\)-marginal, gives
\[
 p^0(v_i\mid v_{B_i})=p_i(v_i\mid v_{P_i}).
\tag{14}
\]
If \(P_i\subseteq A\), the right side of (14) is measurable with respect to \((v_i,v_A)\) and does not depend on \(v_{B_i\setminus A}\); this is the conditional-density factorization for the right side of (13).

Conversely, suppose that the right side of (13) holds and choose an omitted parent \(b\in P_i\setminus A\).  Decompose \(B_i\setminus A\) into \(\{b\}\) and its remaining variables.  The weak-union consequence of the assumed conditional independence is
\[
 V_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}V_b
 \mid V_{B_i\setminus\{b\}}.
\tag{15}
\]
Put \(Z=P_i\setminus\{b\}\) and \(W=B_i\setminus P_i\).  Equation (15) is \(V_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}V_b\mid(V_Z,V_W)\).  Equation (14), on the other hand, gives the ordered local-Markov relation
\[
 V_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}V_W\mid(V_Z,V_b).
\tag{16}
\]
Strict positivity proves the required intersection step directly.  Indeed, conditional independence first gives the following identities almost everywhere; positivity and continuity of the marginal and conditional densities extend them to every point of the product support:
\[
 p^0(v_i\mid v_b,v_W,v_Z)=p^0(v_i\mid v_W,v_Z),
 \qquad
 p^0(v_i\mid v_b,v_W,v_Z)=p^0(v_i\mid v_b,v_Z).
\tag{17}
\]
For each fixed \((v_i,v_Z)\), the left member of the resulting equality \(p^0(v_i\mid v_W,v_Z)=p^0(v_i\mid v_b,v_Z)\) depends only on \(v_W\), while the right member depends only on \(v_b\).  Full positive product support permits arbitrary \((v_b,v_W)\), so both sides are constant in those arguments.  Hence
\[
 V_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}V_b\mid V_Z,
\tag{18}
\]
contradicting causal minimality (1).  Therefore no parent can be omitted.  Together with the sufficient direction this proves
\[
 U_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{P^0}U_{B_i\setminus A}\mid U_A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_i\subseteq A.
\tag{19}
\]
The admissible subsets are exactly the supersets of \(P_i\); consequently, \(P_i\) is their unique inclusion-minimal member.  This proves that \texttt{def:population-decoder} returns \(G\), and the argument applies to every topological ordering of \(H_D\).

It remains to prove uniqueness among compatible representations; cover separation is not imposed on the competitor.  Relabel both representations by their target bijections, write the competing DAG as \(\widetilde G\), its coordinates as \(z\), its mixing map as \(\widetilde f\), and its ratios and intervention distribution functions with tildes.  The implication (4) holds for the competitor because it used only factorization, normalization, positivity, and a perfect intervention.  Therefore every observable arrow in \(H_D\) is also an ancestral relation in \(\widetilde G\), and (5) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TC}(G)=\operatorname{TC}(H_D)
 \subseteq\operatorname{TC}(\widetilde G).
\tag{20}
\]
Choose a topological order of \(\widetilde G\).  By (20), it is also a topological order of \(G\) and of \(H_D\); use this common order below, writing \(j>i\) when \(j\) is later than \(i\).

The transition map
\[
 T=f^{-1}\circ\widetilde f:[0,1]^n\longrightarrow[0,1]^n
\tag{21}
\]
is a \(C^2\) diffeomorphism.  Equality of the observed laws makes their likelihood ratios equal almost everywhere.  Their latent versions are continuous, and the positive observational density assigns positive probability to every nonempty relatively open set, so the equality extends everywhere:
\[
 r_i(T(z))=\widetilde r_i(z),\qquad i\in[n].
\tag{22}
\]
We prove inductively in the common order that \(T_i\) depends only on \(z_1,\ldots,z_i\).  If \(j>i\), the right side of (22) has zero \(z_j\)-derivative because it depends only on \(z_i\) and the earlier \(\widetilde G\)-parents of \(i\).  The chain rule and the induction hypothesis for every \(a\in P_i\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0=\partial_{z_j}\widetilde r_i(z)
 &=\partial_{v_i}r_i(T(z))\,\partial_{z_j}T_i(z)
 +\sum_{a\in P_i}\partial_{v_a}r_i(T(z))\,\partial_{z_j}T_a(z)\\
 &=\partial_{v_i}r_i(T(z))\,\partial_{z_j}T_i(z).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
By (8), \(\partial_{v_i}r_i=r_i\partial_{v_i}\lambda_i\ne0\).  Hence \(\partial_{z_j}T_i=0\), proving that \(T\) is lower triangular.  Since \(DT\) is nonsingular and triangular, every diagonal derivative \(\partial_{z_i}T_i\) is nonzero; its sign is constant on the connected cube.

Every prefix map \(T_{\le i}\) is a diffeomorphism of the corresponding prefix cube.  Indeed, strict monotonicity in each diagonal coordinate gives injectivity successively.  For surjectivity, fix a desired output prefix, extend it arbitrarily to a point of \([0,1]^n\), use surjectivity of \(T\), and then use triangularity to see that the preimage's input prefix maps to the desired prefix.  Compare prefix densities in environment \(i\).  Normalizing all later factors in the common topological order gives
\[
 p^i_{\le i}(v_{\le i})=p^0_{<i}(v_{<i})q_i(v_i),
 \qquad
 \widetilde p^i_{\le i}(z_{\le i})
 =\widetilde p^0_{<i}(z_{<i})\widetilde q_i(z_i).
\tag{24}
\]
Triangularity, prefix bijectivity, and equality of the observational prefix laws give
\[
 \widetilde p^0_{<i}(z_{<i})
 =p^0_{<i}(T_{<i}(z_{<i}))\,|\det DT_{<i}(z_{<i})|.
\tag{25}
\]
Apply change of variables under \(T_{\le i}\) to the two environment-\(i\) identities in (24), substitute (25), and cancel the strictly positive common factor.  The result is the scalar fiber identity
\[
 q_i(T_i(z_{<i},z_i))
 \left|\partial_{z_i}T_i(z_{<i},z_i)\right|
 =\widetilde q_i(z_i).
\tag{26}
\]

Every fiber \(z_i\mapsto T_i(z_{<i},z_i)\) maps \([0,1]\) onto \([0,1]\).  Indeed, fix the input prefix \(z_{<i}\) and a desired output \(v_i\); extend \((T_{<i}(z_{<i}),v_i)\) to an output vector and invoke surjectivity of \(T\).  Prefix injectivity forces the resulting preimage to have prefix \(z_{<i}\).  Thus an increasing fiber has endpoints \(0,1\), and a decreasing fiber has endpoints \(1,0\).  Integrating (26) from \(0\) to \(z_i\) now yields
\[
 Q_i(T_i(z_{<i},z_i))=
 \begin{cases}
 \widetilde Q_i(z_i),&\partial_{z_i}T_i>0,\\[3pt]
 1-\widetilde Q_i(z_i),&\partial_{z_i}T_i<0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(Q_i\) is strictly increasing, (27) makes \(T_i\) a function of \(z_i\) alone.  Positivity and smoothness of the two intervention densities make this scalar map a \(C^2\) diffeomorphism.  Induction proves that \(T\) is componentwise.

It remains only to compare the DAGs.  Apply the already-proved equivalence (19) to \(G\) and to \(\widetilde G\) in the common order.  Componentwise bimeasurable transformations preserve all conditional independences used by the pruning functional.  Thus the family of admissible subsets \(A\subseteq B_i\) is the same for both representations.  Causal minimality makes its unique inclusion-minimal member equal to \(P_i\) in the first representation and to \(\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde G}(i)\) in the second.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_G(i)=\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde G}(i)
 \quad\text{for every }i,
\tag{28}
\]
so \(G=\widetilde G\).  Undoing the two target relabelings proves uniqueness up to simultaneous relabeling and componentwise \(C^2\) diffeomorphisms.  Restoring the original \(\pi\) in (5), (6), (12), and (19) gives every displayed identification conclusion in the statement and identifies the target assignment through the environment-to-coordinate alignment.

We finally verify the comparison clauses.  The established scope records \texttt{lem:von-kugelgen-comparator-scope}, \texttt{lem:cauca-comparator-scope}, and \texttt{lem:yao-comparator-scope} supply the published intervention-count and supplied-structure facts.  The present theorem assumes positive \(C^3\) mechanisms on a compact cube and a shared \(C^2\) mixing map, so its conclusion does not extend by this proof to the anchor's full \(\mathbb R^n\), \(C^1\) class.  On the overlapping faithful regime, faithfulness implies (1), and the theorem uses one intervention per node rather than the paired design in the arbitrary-dimensional comparator.  Within the present regime, the proof of (19) and (28) uses only causal minimality, which establishes the stated structural extension without describing the causal-minimal class as a subclass or specialization of the anchor's faithful class.

For the bivariate witness clause, let \(\nu=\operatorname{Law}_{P^0}(R_i)-\operatorname{Law}_{P^j}(R_i)\) and define
\[
 \varphi(r)=\int_{\mathbb R}k(r,t)\,d\nu(t).
\tag{29}
\]
Boundedness and continuity of the Gaussian kernel make \(\varphi\) bounded and continuous by dominated convergence.  The defining double-integral formula for MMD and Fubini's theorem, applicable because \(|k|\le1\), give
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^0}\varphi(R_i)-\mathbb E_{P^j}\varphi(R_i)
 =\int_{\mathbb R}\!\int_{\mathbb R}k(r,t)\,d\nu(t)\,d\nu(r)
 =D_{ji}^{2}>0.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus Gaussian-MMD positivity supplies the continuous law-separation witness used in the bivariate comparator.  Together with the established scope records, this proves every comparison clause.  Finally, the implication from full observational faithfulness to (1), proved at the start, makes the faithful version an immediate corollary of the causal-minimal decoder.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-confidence-edges]\label{thm:simultaneous-confidence-edges}
For arbitrary training-fold estimates \(\widehat R_i\), suppose the simultaneous first-stage event \(\{\max_{i,e}\mathbb E_{P^e}|\widehat R_i(X)-R_i(X)|\le a_N\}\) has probability at least \(1-\eta\). Conditional on the training folds and this event, the independent evaluation folds satisfy \[\Pr_{\mathrm{eval}}\!\left\{\max_{j\ne i}|\widehat D_{ji}-D_{ji}|\le\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)\right\}\ge1-\alpha.\] Consequently the same simultaneous bound has unconditional probability at least \(1-\alpha-\eta\). Every edge of \(\widehat H\) is a true ancestral relation in \(G^\pi\); if \(\min_{\pi(j)\lessdot_G\pi(i)}D_{ji}>2\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)\), then \(\operatorname{TC}(\widehat H)=\operatorname{TC}(G^\pi)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All statements below are conditional on the training folds until the last probability calculation.  Let \(\mathcal H_k\) and \(\phi(a)=k(a,\cdot)\) be the reproducing-kernel Hilbert space and canonical feature map in \texttt{lem:bounded-rkhs-empirical-mean}, and define
\[
 \mu_{ie}=\mathbb E_{P^e}\phi(R_i(X)),\qquad
 \widetilde\mu_{ie}=\mathbb E_{P^e}\phi(\widehat R_i(X)),\qquad
 \widehat\mu_{ie}=\frac1{N_e}\sum_{r=1}^{N_e}
 \phi(\widehat R_i(X_{er})).
\tag{31}
\]
These Bochner expectations are well-defined because \(\|\phi(a)\|_{\mathcal H_k}^2=k(a,a)=1\).  The Gaussian feature map satisfies, for all real \(a,b\),
\[
 \|\phi(a)-\phi(b)\|_{\mathcal H_k}^2
 =k(a,a)+k(b,b)-2k(a,b)
 =2\{1-e^{-(a-b)^2}\}
 \le2(a-b)^2.
\tag{32}
\]
On the stated simultaneous first-stage event, Jensen's inequality and (32) give, for every \((i,e)\),
\[
 \|\widetilde\mu_{ie}-\mu_{ie}\|_{\mathcal H_k}
 \le\mathbb E_{P^e}\|\phi(\widehat R_i(X))-\phi(R_i(X))\|_{\mathcal H_k}
 \le\sqrt2\,a_N.
\tag{33}
\]
Put
\[
 \delta_N(\alpha)=
 \frac{1+\sqrt{2\log\{n(n+1)/\alpha\}}}
 {\sqrt{\min_{0\le e\le n}N_e}}.
\tag{34}
\]
Because the declared finite-valued radius \(\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)\in[0,\infty)\) contains \(\log\{n(n+1)/\alpha\}\) and \(1/\sqrt{\min_eN_e}\), its well-typed sampling domain has \(n\ge1\) and \(\min_eN_e\ge1\).  On this domain, \texttt{ass:independent-environment-sampling} and the proved simultaneous bound \texttt{lem:bounded-rkhs-empirical-mean} imply that, with conditional evaluation-fold probability at least \(1-\alpha\),
\[
 \max_{i,e}\|\widehat\mu_{ie}-\widetilde\mu_{ie}\|_{\mathcal H_k}
 \le\delta_N(\alpha).
\tag{35}
\]
The population and empirical discrepancies are respectively
\(D_{ji}=\|\mu_{ij}-\mu_{i0}\|_{\mathcal H_k}\) and
\(\widehat D_{ji}=\|\widehat\mu_{ij}-\widehat\mu_{i0}\|_{\mathcal H_k}\).  By the reverse triangle inequality for a norm, followed by (33)--(35), every \(j\ne i\) obeys on the event in (35)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat D_{ji}-D_{ji}|
 &\le
 \|\widehat\mu_{ij}-\mu_{ij}\|_{\mathcal H_k}
 +\|\widehat\mu_{i0}-\mu_{i0}\|_{\mathcal H_k}\\
 &\le2\sqrt2\,a_N+2\delta_N(\alpha)
 =\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
This proves the asserted conditional simultaneous bound.

Let \(E_N\) denote the training-fold first-stage event and let \(A_N\) denote the event in (36).  The conditional conclusion just proved holds for every training realization in \(E_N\).  The tower property therefore gives, without any additional independence assumption,
\[
 \Pr(A_N)
 \ge \mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf 1_{E_N}\Pr(A_N\mid\text{training folds})\right]
 \ge(1-\alpha)\Pr(E_N)
 \ge(1-\alpha)(1-\eta)
 \ge1-\alpha-\eta.
\tag{37}
\]

On \(A_N\), an edge \(j\to i\) selected by \texttt{def:sample-split-confidence-graph} satisfies
\[
 D_{ji}\ge\widehat D_{ji}-\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)>0.
\tag{38}
\]
The non-ancestor calculation (2)--(4) in \texttt{thm:exact-ratio-decoder} therefore shows that \(j\) is an ancestor of \(i\) in \(G^\pi\).  If every ancestral cover obeys \(D_{ji}>2\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)\), then (36) gives
\[
 \widehat D_{ji}-\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)
 \ge D_{ji}-2\varepsilon_N(\alpha,\eta)>0,
\tag{39}
\]
so every ancestral cover is selected.  The selected graph contains all covers and, by (38), contains only ancestral pairs.  Since every comparable pair in a finite partially ordered set is joined by a saturated chain of covers, taking transitive closures yields
\[
 \operatorname{TC}(\widehat H)=\operatorname{TC}(G^\pi).
\tag{40}
\]
Equations (36)--(40) prove all finite-sample clauses at the stated confidence level.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result separates the verifiable MMD concentration layer from the unresolved construction of a ratio learner with a data-driven radius. \textit{Gap.} Gretton et al. (2012) study observed MMD inputs, while Acarturk et al. (2024) analyze linear score contrasts rather than shared plug-in likelihood-ratio evaluations across a graph. \textit{Consumer.} The lower confidence edges give familywise-valid ancestral discoveries and exact order recovery whenever the finite-sample cover margin clears the explicit radius.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:generated-rank-frontier]\label{oeq:generated-rank-frontier}
Assume the first-stage contract \(\inf_{\theta\in\Theta^{\beta}_{G,s}(c,M,K,\rho)}\Pr_\theta\{\mathcal E_N^{\infty}(b_N)\}\ge1-\eta_N\), and let \(h_N\to0\) satisfy \(b_N=o(h_N)\) and \(\underline N h_N^{d_\star}/\log(\underline N)\to\infty\). Using the handle \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{rank}}\), can one construct cross-fitted estimators \(\widehat U_i\) such that \(\max_{i\in[n]}\|\widehat U_i-U_i\|_{\infty,K}=O_{\Pr,\Theta^{\beta}_{G,s}(c,M,K,\rho)}(r_N)\), where \(r_N=h_N^2+\sqrt{\log(\underline N)/(\underline N h_N^{d_\star})}+b_N/h_N\)? When the first-stage log-ratio estimators additionally admit a uniform asymptotically linear representation on \(K\), derive the corresponding expansion for \(\widehat U_i-U_i\) that separates threshold crossings, generated-conditioning perturbations, oracle conditional-CDF variance, and smoothing bias, and characterize the weakest sufficient first-stage remainder bound.
\end{openendedquestion}
Let \(\widetilde U_i\) denote the oracle local-linear conditional-CDF estimator formed with the true responses \(L_i(X)\), true conditioning vectors \(L_{B_i}(X)\), and true query arguments.  On a first-stage event that controls \(\widehat L_\ell-L_\ell\) in \(C^1\) on the whole evaluation support, the kernel-neighborhood perturbation and threshold-crossing decomposition gives
\[
 \max_i\|\widehat U_i-\widetilde U_i\|_{\infty,K}
 =O_{\Pr}\!\left(
 \frac{b_N}{h_N}+b_N+\sqrt{\frac{b_N\log(\underline N)}{\underline N h_N^{d_\star}}}
 +\frac{\log(\underline N)}{\underline N h_N^{d_\star}}
 \right).
\]
Indeed, Lipschitz perturbation of a kernel argument is \(O(b_N/h_N)\); monotonicity with derivative margin \(c\) bounds the conditional density of \(L_i\), so response-indicator changes are confined to a band of conditional probability \(O(b_N)\); and Bernstein's inequality for the localized band supplies the last two displayed terms.  Under \(b_N=o(h_N)\) and \(\underline N h_N^{d_\star}/\log(\underline N)\to\infty\), this is
\[
 O_{\Pr}\!\left(\frac{b_N}{h_N}
 +\sqrt{\frac{\log(\underline N)}{\underline N h_N^{d_\star}}}\right).
\]
Thus an oracle bound of order \(h_N^2+\sqrt{\log(\underline N)/(\underline N h_N^{d_\star})}\) would imply the advertised \(r_N\) rate after the proposed support correction.  If a uniform first-stage linearization has remainder \(\varrho_N\) in the same \(C^1\) norm, its generated-conditioning contribution is \(O_{\Pr}(\varrho_N/h_N)\); consequently the transparent sufficient negligibility condition relative to the oracle scale \(s_N=h_N^2+\sqrt{\log(\underline N)/(\underline N h_N^{d_\star})}\) is \(\varrho_N=o(h_Ns_N)\).  A claim that this condition is weakest requires a precisely stated influence representation and second-stage weight convention, which the frozen handle does not yet provide.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit \(C^1(K)\) first-stage contract makes the effects of a generated threshold and generated conditioning neighborhoods quantitatively separable from oracle smoothing error. \textit{Gap.} Xie (2023, Theorems 1--3) assumes observed thresholds and covariates; Mammen, Rothe, and Schienle (2012, Theorem 1) linearize generated covariates for smooth regression responses, and neither result covers their joint generated-ratio combination. \textit{Consumer.} A solved expansion supplies coordinate error bars for the one-intervention nonlinear CRL decoder and is the missing statistical ingredient for a compliant ROPES implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bounded-rkhs-empirical-mean]\label{lem:bounded-rkhs-empirical-mean}
Conditional on the training folds, let \(X_{e1},\ldots,X_{eN_e}\) be the independent evaluation sample from \(P^e\), let \(\mathcal H_k\) be the reproducing-kernel Hilbert space of \(k\), and put \(\phi(a)=k(a,\cdot)\).  For \(n\ge1\), with conditional probability at least \(1-\alpha\), simultaneously for every \(i\in[n]\) and \(e\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\),
\[
 \left\|\frac1{N_e}\sum_{r=1}^{N_e}\phi(\widehat R_i(X_{er}))
 -\mathbb E_{P^e}\phi(\widehat R_i(X))\right\|_{\mathcal H_k}
 \le
 \frac{1+\sqrt{2\log\{n(n+1)/\alpha\}}}{\sqrt{\min_{0\le a\le n}N_a}}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((i,e)\) and condition on the training folds, so \(\widehat R_i\) is fixed and the evaluation observations are independent by \texttt{ass:independent-environment-sampling}.  Write \(m=N_e\), \(Y_r=\phi(\widehat R_i(X_{er}))\), \(\mu=\mathbb E Y_r\), and \(Z=\|m^{-1}\sum_rY_r-\mu\|_{\mathcal H_k}\).  Since \(k(a,a)=1\), \(\|Y_r\|_{\mathcal H_k}=1\).  Independence and centering make all cross inner products vanish in expectation, whence
\[
 \mathbb E Z
 \le (\mathbb E Z^2)^{1/2}
 =\left\{\frac1{m^2}\sum_{r=1}^m
 \mathbb E\|Y_r-\mu\|_{\mathcal H_k}^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \le m^{-1/2}.
\tag{23}
\]
Replacing one observation changes \(Z\) by at most \(2/m\).  Here is a self-contained bounded-differences calculation.  Reveal the observations successively and let the Doob differences be \(M_r\).  Conditional on the past, \(M_r\) has mean zero and lies in an interval of length at most \(2/m\).  Convexity of the exponential on that interval, followed by maximizing the resulting two-point bound, gives the conditional Hoeffding bound
\[
 \mathbb E\{e^{\lambda M_r}\mid X_{e1},\ldots,X_{e,r-1}\}
 \le \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{2m^2}\right).
\tag{24}
\]
Iterating (24), applying Markov's inequality, and optimizing at \(\lambda=mt\) gives
\[
 \Pr\{Z-\mathbb EZ>t\}
 \le\inf_{\lambda>0}\exp\!\left(-\lambda t+\frac{\lambda^2}{2m}\right)
 =\exp(-mt^2/2).
\tag{25}
\]
Take \(t=\sqrt{2\log\{n(n+1)/\alpha\}/m}\), combine (23)--(25), and union bound over the \(n(n+1)\) pairs \((i,e)\).  Replacing each \(m=N_e\) by \(\min_aN_a\) proves the displayed simultaneous inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The genericity statement is topological: within every nonempty smooth compact-support causal-minimal fixed-sign stratum, the failure of cover-ratio separation is closed and nowhere dense. It is not a probability-one claim under an unspecified prior and supplies no uniform separation margin. Causal minimality is exactly sufficient for local path admissibility and parent pruning; full faithfulness gives an immediate stronger-class corollary but is not part of the primary identifying class.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact cancellation mechanism with \(H'(x)=q_1(x)-1\) is faithful, hence also causal-minimal, and sign-valid, yet has \(D_{12}=0\). Thus even the stronger faithfulness condition plus monotone interventions does not imply ratio separation; the genericity theorem supplies no uniform positive MMD margin. Cover margins also do not control exact-parent uncertainty: an arbitrarily weak shortcut edge can be added to a chain while cover discrepancies remain separated.

\section{Honest scope}

The population open-density and exact-decoder claims are primary results for the note's positive smooth compact-support causal-minimal regime. Strict positivity supplies the intersection property used in parent pruning, and causal minimality makes the true parent set the unique minimal ordered Markov boundary. Faithfulness is preserved byte-for-byte as a distinct stronger assumption and yields an immediate corollary on the overlapping smooth compact-support faithful regime; only on that overlap does the note claim an arbitrary-dimensional intervention-count improvement relative to von Kugelgen et al. (2023). The note does not settle that anchor's full \(\mathbb R^n\)/\(C^1\) class. The all-DAG existence argument may use a terminating certified search rather than a closed-form family. The fixed-bandwidth MMD result remains conditional on its simultaneous first-stage \(L^1\) ratio-error event and does not provide a data-driven radius. The uniform own-derivative margin and bounded shared-mixing geometry are confined to the bounded Holder subclass and the generated-rank open question; they do not enter population genericity or exact decoding. The generated-rank question remains open: neither attainment nor sharpness of its proposed rate, nor the first-stage remainder required for a uniform asymptotically linear expansion, is claimed. Exact-parent inference under a separate conditional-dependence margin, noncompact supports, vector-valued nodes, imperfect interventions, and noninvertible observations remain outside the settled core. ROPES is only a protocol consumer after adopting a positive common-support intervention generator and a ratio-based estimator; its existing bounded additive-noise benchmark is not asserted to satisfy this model.
