We prove sufficiency, the equivalence with the affine-minor margin, and necessity in turn.

Assume first that \(\delta_H>c\), and fix an arbitrary assignment of positive-definite matrices to the \(c\) slots in \(E\setminus H\).  By \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\), \(\text{ass:invariant-noise-interior}\), and \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\), the choice \(K=H\), \(\Psi=\Omega\), and \(t_e=s_e\) in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) shows that \(D\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).  Conversely, take any \(A\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).  By \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there are \(K\subseteq E\), \(|K|\ge h\), \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and \(t_e\in[0,\infty)^p\) such that \(A\) fits the BACKSHIFT covariance equation on \(K\).  The overlap \(J=H\cap K\) satisfies
\[
 |J|\ge |H|+|K|-m\ge2h-m=h-c. \tag{6}
\]
By \(\text{def:environment-distance}\) and \(\text{def:max-line-occupancy}\), \(\delta_H>c\) is equivalent to
\[
 M_{k\ell}<h-c\qquad\text{for every }k<\ell. \tag{7}
\]
If the honest \(k,\ell\) cloud restricted to \(J\) lay in an affine line, then (6) would imply \(M_{k\ell}\ge |J|\ge h-c\), contradicting (7).  Thus every pairwise cloud on \(J\) is noncollinear.  Applying \(\text{lem:overlap-uniqueness}\) to the explanations \((D,H,\Omega,s)\) and \((A,K,\Psi,t)\) gives \(A=D\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_c(\Sigma)=\{D\}. \tag{8}
\]
Nothing in this argument used the values assigned outside \(H\), so (8) holds for every allowed replacement assignment.

We next verify the asserted equivalence with \(\gamma_H\).  Put \(q=h-c\).  Any set of \(h\) points in \(\mathbb R^2\) has at least \(\min(2,h)\) points on an affine line, and hence
\[
 M_{k\ell}\ge\min(2,h). \tag{9}
\]
Consequently (7) is impossible when \(q<3\), exactly the regime in which \(\text{def:affine-separation}\) sets \(\gamma_H=0\).  Suppose now that \(q\ge3\).  For a fixed \(S\subseteq H\), \(|S|=q\), and fixed \(k<\ell\), all determinants
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 1&s_{ek}&s_{e\ell}\\
 1&s_{fk}&s_{f\ell}\\
 1&s_{gk}&s_{g\ell}
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad e<f<g,\quad e,f,g\in S, \tag{10}
\]
vanish if and only if the matrix with rows \((1,s_{ek},s_{e\ell})\), \(e\in S\), has rank at most two, which is equivalent to the \(k,\ell\) points indexed by \(S\) lying in one affine line.  Thus the inner maximum in \(\text{def:affine-separation}\) is positive exactly when \(S\) is not contained in an affine line.  There are finitely many pairs and subsets, so their minimum is positive exactly when no affine line contains \(q\) honest points for any pair.  Therefore
\[
 \gamma_H>0
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ M_{k\ell}<q\ \text{for all }k<\ell
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \delta_H>c. \tag{11}
\]

For necessity, suppose \(\delta_H\le c\).  We construct one legal replacement assignment having a second compatible structural matrix.  Again write \(q=h-c=m-2c\).

If \(q\le0\), then \(c\ge h\), so \(E\setminus H\) contains a subset \(H'\) of size \(h\).  Fix \(k\ne\ell\), and let \(E_{k\ell}\) be the matrix with a one in position \(k,\ell\) and zeros elsewhere.  For all sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\),
\[
 D'=D+\varepsilon E_{k\ell}
\]
has unit diagonal, is invertible, and satisfies \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D')<1\): invertibility and the strict cycle-product inequality persist by continuity from \(\varepsilon=0\).  Hence \(D'\in\mathcal D\) and \(D'\ne D\).  Set \(\Omega'=I_p\), and assign
\[
 \Sigma_e=(D')^{-1}\Omega'(D')^{-\mathsf T},\qquad e\in E\setminus H.
\]
These matrices are positive definite.  The original \(D\) fits the \(h\) slots in \(H\), while \(D'\) fits the \(h\) slots in \(H'\) with zero shifts.  Thus both matrices belong to \(\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).

It remains to treat \(q>0\).  By \(\delta_H\le c\), there are \(k<\ell\), an affine line \(L\), and a subset \(S\subseteq H\) of size \(q\) whose \(k,\ell\) shift points lie on \(L\).  Choose a nonzero direction \(v=(v_k,v_\ell)\) for \(L\), fix \(e_0\in S\), and write
\[
 (s_{ek}-s_{e_0k},s_{e\ell}-s_{e_0\ell})=\tau_e(v_k,v_\ell),\qquad e\in S. \tag{12}
\]
For a scalar \(\varepsilon\), define
\[
 \eta_k(\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon v_kD_{\ell k},\qquad
 \eta_\ell(\varepsilon)=1-\varepsilon v_\ell D_{k\ell},
\]
and let \(Q_\varepsilon\) be the identity outside rows and columns \(k,\ell\), with \(k,\ell\) block
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \eta_k(\varepsilon)^{-1}&\varepsilon v_k\eta_k(\varepsilon)^{-1}\\
 -\varepsilon v_\ell\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)^{-1}&\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{13}
\]
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), both denominators and \(Q_\varepsilon\) are invertible.  Put \(D'_\varepsilon=Q_\varepsilon D\).  The two changed diagonal entries are
\[
 (D'_\varepsilon)_{kk}
 =\frac{D_{kk}+\varepsilon v_kD_{\ell k}}{\eta_k(\varepsilon)}=1,\qquad
 (D'_\varepsilon)_{\ell\ell}
 =\frac{D_{\ell\ell}-\varepsilon v_\ell D_{k\ell}}{\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)}=1, \tag{14}
\]
and the other diagonal entries remain one.  Since \(Q_0=I_p\), the strict inequality \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D)<1\) and invertibility persist by continuity.  Because \(v\ne0\), every sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\) also has \(Q_\varepsilon\ne I_p\).  Hence we may choose a nonzero \(\varepsilon\) such that
\[
 D'=D'_\varepsilon\in\mathcal D,\qquad D'\ne D. \tag{15}
\]

For \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\), equations (12)--(13) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [Q_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(x_e)Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}]_{k\ell}
 &=\frac{-\varepsilon v_\ell x_{ek}+\varepsilon v_kx_{e\ell}}
 {\eta_k(\varepsilon)\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)}\\
 &=\frac{-\varepsilon v_\ell\tau_ev_k+\varepsilon v_k\tau_ev_\ell}
 {\eta_k(\varepsilon)\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)}=0.
 \end{aligned} \tag{16}
\]
All other off-diagonal entries in (16) vanish because \(Q_\varepsilon\) differs from the identity only in the \(k,\ell\) block.  Define \(\operatorname{off}(G)=G-\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{diag}(G))\).  Equation (16) shows that
\[
 C_\varepsilon
 =\operatorname{off}\!\left(Q_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(s_e)Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}\right)
\]
does not depend on \(e\in S\).  Set
\[
 \Omega'_\varepsilon=Q_\varepsilon\Omega Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}+C_\varepsilon. \tag{17}
\]
At \(\varepsilon=0\), \(C_0=0\) and \(\Omega'_0=\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\) by \(\text{ass:invariant-noise-interior}\).  Positive definiteness is open, so the nonzero \(\varepsilon\) in (15) may be chosen still smaller so that
\[
 \Omega'=\Omega'_\varepsilon\in\mathbb S_{++}^p. \tag{18}
\]
For \(e\in S\), define
\[
 t_{ei}=[Q_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(s_e)Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}]_{ii}
 =\sum_{j=1}^p(Q_\varepsilon)_{ij}^2s_{ej}\ge0, \tag{19}
\]
where the inequality uses \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\).  Equations (17)--(19) imply, for every \(e\in S\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D'\Sigma_eD'^{\mathsf T}
 &=Q_\varepsilon(\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e))Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=\Omega'+\operatorname{diag}(t_e).
 \end{aligned} \tag{20}
\]
Now set \(H'=S\cup(E\setminus H)\), which has size \(q+c=h\), and assign every replaced slot the positive-definite covariance
\[
 \Sigma_e=(D')^{-1}\Omega'(D')^{-\mathsf T},\qquad e\in E\setminus H. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (20)--(21) show that \(D'\) fits all of \(H'\), using shifts \(t_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts outside \(H\).  The original \(D\) continues to fit \(H\).  Thus \(D,D'\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\), while \(D'\ne D\), and (18), (19), and (21) give precisely the required positive-definite nuisance, nonnegative shifts, and positive-definite replacements.

As an exact check of the smallest nontrivial ambiguity, take \(p=2\), \(m=3\), \(c=1\), \(D=I_2\), \(\Omega=I_2\), \(H=\{1,2\}\), \(s_1=(0,0)\), and \(s_2=(1,1)\).  Put \(\Sigma_1=I_2\), \(\Sigma_2=2I_2\), and replace the third slot by \(\Sigma_3=I_2\).  For \(0<|\varepsilon|<1\),
\[
 D'=\begin{pmatrix}1&\varepsilon\\-\varepsilon&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Omega'=(1+\varepsilon^2)I_2
\]
satisfy \(\operatorname{CP}(I_2-D')=\varepsilon^2<1\).  Moreover, \(D'\Sigma_eD'^{\mathsf T}=\Omega'\) for \(e=1,3\), and
\[
 D'\Sigma_2D'^{\mathsf T}
 =\Omega'+\operatorname{diag}(1+\varepsilon^2,1+\varepsilon^2).
\]
Thus the ambiguity is literal, not a boundary limit.

Finally, if \(c=0\), then \(h=m\) and
\[
 \delta_H>0
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ M_{k\ell}<h\ \text{for every }k<\ell,
\]
which says exactly that no full pairwise shift cloud is contained in an affine line.  This is the stated BACKSHIFT pairwise noncollinearity specialization.
