Put \(q=m-2c=h-c\). By \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), the case \(q<3\) would give \(\gamma_H=0\), contrary to \(\gamma_H\geq\gamma_0>0\). Therefore
\[
q\geq3.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Uniform structural bounds.}
Let \(G\) denote either the true structural matrix or a feasible candidate structural matrix satisfying the stated normalization-slack and condition-number bounds. Its diagonal is the all-ones vector by \(\text{ass:backshift-normalization}\) on the true side and by \(G\in\mathcal D\), via \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\), on the candidate side. In the Leibniz expansion of \(\det G\), the identity-permutation term has absolute value one. Every other permutation decomposes into fixed points and pairwise disjoint directed simple cycles of length at least two. Fixed points contribute one, and the absolute product from each nontrivial cycle is a cycle product in \(I_p-G\), hence is at most \(1-\zeta_0\leq1\). Thus every Leibniz term has absolute value at most one, and the triangle inequality gives
\[
|\det G|\leq p!.
\tag{2}
\]
Write the singular values in decreasing order as \(\sigma_1(G),\ldots,\sigma_p(G)\). The condition-number bound implies
\[
\sigma_j(G)\geq\sigma_p(G)
\geq\frac{\sigma_1(G)}{\overline\kappa}
\qquad(2\leq j\leq p).
\]
Combining this inequality with (2) yields
\[
\frac{\sigma_1(G)^p}{\overline\kappa^{p-1}}
\leq\prod_{j=1}^p\sigma_j(G)
=|\det G|
\leq p!,
\]
and therefore
\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\bigl(p!\,\overline\kappa^{p-1}\bigr)^{1/p}
=L_0.
\tag{3}
\]
Because a unit diagonal entry has absolute value one, \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq1\). Hence the condition-number bound also gives
\[
\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Compact overlap family and positive far-family gap.}
Define
\[
\rho_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2K_0\overline M}\right\}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
For every \(H_1\subseteq E\) with \(|H_1|=h\) and every \(S\subseteq H_1\) with \(|S|=q\), consider tuples
\[
\tau=\bigl(D,U,\Omega,(s_e,\Sigma_e)_{e\in H_1},
A,V,\Psi,(t_e,\Gamma_e)_{e\in S}\bigr)
\tag{6}
\]
satisfying the following conditions. First,
\[
DU=UD=I_p,
\qquad
AV=VA=I_p.
\tag{7}
\]
Second, \(D\) and \(A\) have unit diagonal and satisfy
\[
1-\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D)\geq\zeta_0,
\qquad
1-\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)\geq\zeta_0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa,
\qquad
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{9}
\]
Third,
\[
\Omega,\Sigma_e\in\mathbb S_+^p,\quad
s_e\in[0,\infty)^p\quad(e\in H_1),
\]
\[
\Psi,\Gamma_e\in\mathbb S_+^p,\quad
t_e\in[0,\infty)^p\quad(e\in S),
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)
\quad(e\in H_1),
\qquad
A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)
\quad(e\in S).
\tag{11}
\]
Fourth, the true-side affine-separation functional over \(H_1\) is at least \(\gamma_0\), and
\[
1+\|\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline M.
\tag{12}
\]
Finally, for every \(e\in S\),
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1.
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the finite disjoint union of these families, retaining \((H_1,S)\) as a discrete part. Equations (3)--(4) bound \(D,U,A,V\). From (12),
\[
\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline M-1
\quad(e\in H_1),
\]
so (13) gives
\[
\|\Gamma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline M
\quad(e\in S).
\tag{14}
\]
The candidate equation in (11), (3), and (14) give
\[
\|\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0^2\overline M.
\tag{15}
\]
Both summands on the left of (15) are positive semidefinite. For positive-semidefinite matrices \(P,Q\), the Rayleigh-quotient inequalities \(0\leq v^{\mathsf T}Pv\leq v^{\mathsf T}(P+Q)v\) for every unit vector \(v\) imply \(\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|P+Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), and similarly for \(Q\). Consequently,
\[
\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0^2\overline M,
\qquad
\|\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0^2\overline M
\quad(e\in S).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every variable in (6) is bounded; notably, (14)--(16) derive the candidate-side bounds from the true scale bound and the local unit perturbation restriction rather than assuming a candidate scale bound.

Each fixed-index family is closed. Indeed, (7) and (11) are polynomial equalities; the positive-semidefinite cones and nonnegative orthants in (10) are closed; all displayed norm inequalities are closed conditions; \(\operatorname{CP}\) is the maximum of finitely many continuous absolute cycle-product functions; and the true affine-separation functional is a finite minimum of finite maxima of continuous absolute determinants. Hence each fixed-index family is closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional real Euclidean space, so \(\text{lem:euclidean-heine-borel}\) makes it compact. A finite disjoint union of compact spaces is compact, because finite subcovers may be chosen component by component. Therefore \(\mathfrak T\) is compact. This construction uses neither candidate affine separation nor a positive lower eigenvalue for any covariance matrix.

Define the continuous residual
\[
\mathfrak r(\tau)=
\max_{e\in S}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
\tag{17}
\]
and the closed subfamily
\[
\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}
=\{\tau\in\mathfrak T:\|AU-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\geq\rho_0\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Continuity follows from continuity of matrix multiplication, subtraction, the Frobenius norm, and a finite maximum. Hence \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\) is compact as a closed subset of \(\mathfrak T\). If \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}=\varnothing\), set \(\mu_0=1\). Otherwise, \(\text{lem:compact-extreme-value}\) gives an attaining minimum
\[
\mu_0=\min_{\tau\in\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}}\mathfrak r(\tau).
\tag{19}
\]
We prove \(\mu_0>0\). In the nonempty case, suppose instead that \(\mu_0=0\). An attaining tuple then satisfies, for every \(e\in S\),
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e),
\qquad
A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e).
\tag{20}
\]
The true-side affine-separation condition, applied to the particular \(q\)-element set \(S\), gives for every \(k<\ell\) a triple in \(S\) whose affine determinant in coordinates \((k,\ell)\) has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0>0\). Hence \(\{(s_{ek},s_{e\ell}):e\in S\}\) is not contained in an affine line.

By (1), \(|E\setminus S|=m-q=2c\). Choose a disjoint partition \(E\setminus S=C_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_2\) with \(|C_1|=|C_2|=c\), and define
\[
\widetilde\Sigma_e=
\begin{cases}
\Sigma_e,&e\in S,\\
D^{-1}\Omega D^{-\mathsf T},&e\in C_1,\\
A^{-1}\Psi A^{-\mathsf T},&e\in C_2.
\end{cases}
\tag{21}
\]
Every matrix in (21) is positive semidefinite by (10) and preservation of positive semidefiniteness under congruence. The first explanation fits \(S\cup C_1\) with shifts \(s_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts on \(C_1\); the second fits \(S\cup C_2\) with shifts \(t_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts on \(C_2\). Both fitted sets have cardinality \(q+c=h\), their overlap is \(S\), and their invariant nuisances and shifts satisfy the required sign conditions. Equations (7)--(9) and \(\zeta_0>0\) also give \(D,A\in\mathcal D\) by \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\). Thus \(\text{lem:overlap-uniqueness}\), applied to \(\widetilde\Sigma\), yields \(A=D\). Equation (7) then yields \(AU=I_p\), contradicting (18). Therefore
\[
\mu_0>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Set, non-effectively,
\[
r_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{\mu_0}{2\sqrt p\,L_0^2}\right\}>0.
\tag{23}
\]
The finite family \(\mathfrak T\), the threshold \(\rho_0\), and the minimum \(\mu_0\) are defined solely from \(p,m,c,\gamma_0,\zeta_0,\overline\kappa,\overline M\). Thus \(r_0\) has exactly the dependence asserted in the theorem. The compactness argument supplies no method for evaluating \(\mu_0\) or \(r_0\).

\textbf{Entry into the local neighborhood.}
Fix an outcome in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\), write \(r=r_{n,\alpha,H}\), and assume \(r\leq r_0\). The result \(\text{thm:confidence-union-coverage}\) gives \(D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), so this union is nonempty. Take arbitrary \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\). By \(\text{def:confidence-union}\), there exists a simultaneous selection \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\), \(e\in E\), for which \(A\in\mathcal R_c(\Gamma)\). By \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there are a fitted set of cardinality at least \(h\), a matrix \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and nonnegative shifts satisfying the candidate covariance equations. Restrict that fitted set and its witnesses to a set \(K\subseteq E\) with \(|K|=h\). The declarative candidate premise then gives
\[
1-\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)\geq\zeta_0,
\qquad
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|A^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{24}
\]
Inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
|H\cap K|
\geq|H|+|K|-|E|
=2h-m=q.
\tag{25}
\]
Choose \(S\subseteq H\cap K\) with \(|S|=q\), and set \(U=D^{-1}\), \(V=A^{-1}\). For \(e\in S\subseteq H\), \(\text{def:honest-region-radius}\) gives
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq r\leq r_0\leq1.
\tag{26}
\]
The true assumptions and four bounds, the candidate equations and (24), and (26) place the resulting overlap tuple in \(\mathfrak T\). Using the candidate equation, (3), (23), (26), submultiplicativity, and \(\|X\|_{\mathrm F}\leq\sqrt p\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), for every \(e\in S\) we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
&=\left\|A(\Sigma_e-\Gamma_e)A^{\mathsf T}\right\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\leq\sqrt p\,L_0^2r
\leq\mu_0/2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
If \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\) is nonempty, (19) and (27) show that the tuple cannot belong to it; if it is empty, nonmembership is immediate. Consequently, with
\[
Q=AD^{-1}=I_p+R,
\qquad
u=\|R\|_{\mathrm F},
\]
we have
\[
u<\rho_0\leq1,
\qquad
K_0\overline M u<\frac12.
\tag{28}
\]

\textbf{Local affine-minor inversion.}
Fix \(e_0\in S\) and put \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\). For every \(i<j\), true-side affine separation on \(S\) supplies distinct \(a,b,d\in S\) such that the absolute affine determinant in coordinates \((i,j)\) is at least \(\gamma_0\). Translation of all three points by the point indexed by \(e_0\) leaves that determinant unchanged, and direct expansion gives
\[
\det(\widetilde x_a-\widetilde x_b,
\widetilde x_d-\widetilde x_b)
=\det(\widetilde x_a,\widetilde x_d)
+\det(\widetilde x_d,\widetilde x_b)
+\det(\widetilde x_b,\widetilde x_a),
\tag{29}
\]
where \(\widetilde x_e=(x_{ei},x_{ej})\). The triangle inequality applied to (29) supplies two labels \(e,f\in S\) satisfying
\[
|x_{ei}x_{fj}-x_{ej}x_{fi}|\geq\gamma_0/3.
\tag{30}
\]
Let \(E_e=\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\). Subtract the true covariance equation at \(e_0\) from that at \(e\), do the same for the candidate equation, use \(A=QD\), and take the \((i,j)\) off-diagonal entry. The invariant nuisances cancel and give the exact identity
\[
R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}
=-\sum_{k=1}^pR_{ik}R_{jk}x_{ek}
-[A(E_e-E_{e_0})A^{\mathsf T}]_{ij}.
\tag{31}
\]
The true scale bound and nonnegativity in \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\) imply \(|x_{ek}|\leq\overline M\). Equation (26) gives \(\|E_e-E_{e_0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2r\), and (3) gives \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_0\). Since the Euclidean norm of every row of \(R\) is at most \(u\), Cauchy--Schwarz in (31) yields
\[
|R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}|
\leq\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r.
\tag{32}
\]
Apply (32) at the two labels in (30), and denote the two right-hand sides of (31) by \(b_e,b_f\). Their absolute values are at most \(\beta=\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\). The determinant \(\Delta=x_{ej}x_{fi}-x_{fj}x_{ei}\) of the two-by-two coefficient matrix for \((R_{ij},R_{ji})\) satisfies \(|\Delta|\geq\gamma_0/3\), while every coefficient has absolute value at most \(\overline M\). Direct elimination gives
\[
R_{ij}=\frac{b_ex_{fi}-b_fx_{ei}}{\Delta},
\qquad
R_{ji}=\frac{x_{ej}b_f-x_{fj}b_e}{\Delta}.
\]
The triangle inequality therefore gives, for every ordered pair \(i\neq j\),
\[
|R_{ij}|
\leq\frac{6\overline M}{\gamma_0}
\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\right).
\tag{33}
\]
The unit diagonals of \(A\) and \(D\) imply
\[
0=(A-D)_{ii}=(RD)_{ii}
=R_{ii}+\sum_{k\neq i}R_{ik}D_{ki}.
\tag{34}
\]
For each \(i\), the squared Euclidean norm of the \(i\)-th column of \(D\) is at most \(L_0^2\) by (3). Squaring (33), summing over the \(p(p-1)\) ordered off-diagonal entries, applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (34), and taking square roots yields
\[
u\leq K_0\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\right).
\tag{35}
\]
If \(u=0\), the next bound is immediate. If \(u>0\), (28) makes the quadratic term on the right of (35) strictly less than \(u/2\); rearranging (35) then gives the same bound. Thus in both cases
\[
u\leq4K_0L_0^2r.
\tag{36}
\]
Using (3), (36), and \(C_0=16K_0L_0^3\), we obtain
\[
\|A-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|RD\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq uL_0
\leq4K_0L_0^3r
\leq C_0r.
\tag{37}
\]
Because \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) was arbitrary, (37) and \(\text{def:operator-outer-radius}\) prove
\[
\operatorname{outer}_{\mathrm{op}}(\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha,D)
\leq C_0r.
\]
For any \(A_1,A_2\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), the triangle inequality and (37) give
\[
\|A_1-A_2\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|A_1-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|A_2-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2C_0r.
\]
Taking the supremum and invoking \(\text{def:operator-diameter}\) proves the diameter bound. Finally, along any sequence in the theorem, eventually \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\leq r_0\); the two bounds and \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\to0\) give both asserted limits. This proves the theorem without a candidate affine-separation assumption, a candidate scale assumption, or covariance invertibility.
