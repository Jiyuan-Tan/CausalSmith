Fix \(k<\ell\), and write
\[
 P_e=(s_{ek},s_{e\ell})=(Z_{ek}a_{ek},Z_{e\ell}a_{e\ell}),\qquad e\in H.
\]
The four support types \(00,10,01,11\) place \(P_e\), respectively, at the origin, on the positive horizontal axis, on the positive vertical axis, and in the open positive quadrant.

We first identify all incidences forced by the support pattern.  The horizontal axis contains exactly \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell}\) indexed points, the vertical axis contains exactly \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{01}^{k\ell}\), and, if \(n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\), the line through the origin and any one \(11\) point contains \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+1\) indexed points.  Finally, an affine line contains any prescribed two points, counting distinct environment indices even when the two points coincide.  Hence, deterministically,
\[
 M_{k\ell}\ge
 \max\!\left\{
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{01}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+\mathbf 1\{n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\},
 \min(2,h)
 \right\}. \tag{22}
\]

We now show that no other incidence occurs outside a Lebesgue-null set of active amplitudes.  For two nonzero-support points \(P_e=(x_e,y_e)\) and \(P_f=(x_f,y_f)\), collinearity with the origin is the polynomial equation
\[
 x_ey_f-x_fy_e=0. \tag{23}
\]
Unless both support types are \(10\), or both are \(01\), the polynomial in (23) is nonzero: for the mixed types \(10,01\), \(10,11\), and \(01,11\), it contains a nonzero product of positive active coordinates, while for two \(11\) points it is \(x_ey_f-x_fy_e\) with two distinct monomials.  Likewise, for three nonzero-support points, collinearity is the polynomial equation
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 1&x_e&y_e\\1&x_f&y_f\\1&x_g&y_g
 \end{pmatrix}=0. \tag{24}
\]
Except when all three types are \(10\), or all three are \(01\), the polynomial in (24) is not identically zero.  This can be checked within each support type without leaving the positive orthant: two horizontal points and a vertical or \(11\) point can be chosen noncollinear; the analogous assertion holds with the axes exchanged; one point of each relevant mixed type can be chosen off the line through the other two; and three \(11\) points may be chosen as \((1,1),(1,2),(2,1)\).  We also exclude equality of the active coordinates of two distinct \(10\) points and, analogously, of two distinct \(01\) points; each is the zero set of a nonzero linear polynomial.

For completeness, the zero set of a nonzero polynomial in finitely many real variables has Lebesgue measure zero.  This follows by induction on the number of variables: in one variable there are finitely many roots; in the induction step, write the polynomial as a polynomial in the last variable, choose one nonzero coefficient polynomial, use the induction hypothesis on the zero set of that coefficient, and observe that every remaining one-dimensional section has finitely many roots.  Integrating these null sections gives a null set.  There are only finitely many equality, pair, triple, and coordinate-pair events above.  The joint absolute continuity in \(\text{ass:generic-active-amplitudes}\) consequently implies that, simultaneously for every \(k<\ell\), none of these accidental equations occurs almost surely.

On this probability-one event, let \(L\) be an affine line.  If \(L\) passes through the origin and contains a \(10\) point, then \(L\) is the horizontal axis and has occupancy \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell}\).  The corresponding conclusion for a \(01\) point gives the vertical-axis term.  If it contains neither axis type, (23) permits at most one \(11\) point, giving occupancy at most \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+\mathbf 1\{n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\}\).  If \(L\) does not pass through the origin, it contains no \(00\) point.  Were it to contain three indexed points, (24) would force all three to be \(10\) or all three to be \(01\); but their line would then be an axis and would pass through the origin, a contradiction.  Such a line therefore has occupancy at most \(\min(2,h)\).  Combining these upper bounds with (22) proves
\[
 M_{k\ell}
 =\max\!\left\{
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{01}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+\mathbf 1\{n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\},
 \min(2,h)
 \right\}
 =M_{k\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{25}
\]
The exceptional set was chosen as a finite union over all \(k<\ell\), so (25) holds simultaneously for every pair.

By \(\text{thm:sharp-replacement-radius}\) and \(\text{def:environment-distance}\), robust identification is equivalent to
\[
 \delta_H>c
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ h-M_{k\ell}>c\ \text{for every }k<\ell
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ M_{k\ell}<h-c\ \text{for every }k<\ell. \tag{26}
\]
Substitution of (25) into (26) gives exactly the four-term support criterion in the statement.

Because the fourth term in (25) is \(\min(2,h)\), the strict inequality in (26) requires \(h-c\ge3\).  Since \(h=m-c\), this is
\[
 m\ge2c+3. \tag{27}
\]
The bound is attained.  For any \(c\ge0\), take \(m=2c+3\), so \(h=c+3\), and choose the all-active pattern \(Z_{ek}=1\) for every \(e,k\).  Then for every \(k<\ell\),
\[
 n_{11}^{k\ell}=h,\qquad
 n_{00}^{k\ell}=n_{10}^{k\ell}=n_{01}^{k\ell}=0,\qquad
 M_{k\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}=2<h-c=3.
\]
Thus this pattern has the smallest possible forced affine occupancy and robustly identifies almost surely at equality in (27), completing the proof.
