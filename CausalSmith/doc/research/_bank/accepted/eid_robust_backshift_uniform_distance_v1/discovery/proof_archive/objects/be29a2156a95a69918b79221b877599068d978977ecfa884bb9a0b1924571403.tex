Put \(q=m-2c=h-c\). By \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), \(q<3\) would imply \(\gamma_H=0\), contrary to \(\gamma_H\geq\gamma_0>0\). Therefore
\[
q\geq3.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Uniform structural bounds.}
Let \(G\) be either the true structural matrix or a feasible candidate structural matrix satisfying the asserted normalization-slack and condition-number bounds. In the Leibniz expansion of \(\det G\), the identity-permutation term has absolute value one because \(\operatorname{diag}(G)=\mathbf 1\). Every other permutation is a product of fixed points and pairwise disjoint directed simple cycles of length at least two. For each such nontrivial cycle, the absolute edge product equals the corresponding cycle product in \(I_p-G\), and hence it is at most
\[
\operatorname{CP}(I_p-G)\leq1-\zeta_0\leq1.
\]
Thus every one of the \(p!\) Leibniz terms has absolute value at most one, so
\[
|\det G|\leq p!.
\tag{2}
\]
Write the singular values in decreasing order as \(\sigma_1(G),\ldots,\sigma_p(G)\). Since
\[
\sigma_1(G)=\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad
\frac{\sigma_1(G)}{\sigma_p(G)}
=\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa,
\]
one has \(\sigma_j(G)\geq\sigma_1(G)/\overline\kappa\) for every \(j\geq2\). Consequently,
\[
\frac{\sigma_1(G)^p}{\overline\kappa^{p-1}}
\leq\prod_{j=1}^p\sigma_j(G)
=|\det G|
\leq p!,
\]
and therefore
\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq
\bigl(p!\,\overline\kappa^{p-1}\bigr)^{1/p}=L_0.
\tag{3}
\]
A unit diagonal entry implies \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq1\), whence
\[
\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\frac{\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
{\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{A compact positive-semidefinite comparison family.}
Set
\[
\rho_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2K_0\overline M}\right\}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
For each choice of \(H_1,H_2\subseteq E\) with \(|H_1|=|H_2|=h\) and \(S\subseteq H_1\cap H_2\) with \(|S|=q\), consider all tuples
\[
\tau=\bigl(D,U,\Omega,(s_e,\Sigma_e)_{e\in H_1},
A,V,\Psi,(t_e,\Gamma_e)_{e\in H_2}\bigr)
\]
with the following properties. First,
\[
DU=UD=I_p,
\qquad
AV=VA=I_p.
\tag{6}
\]
Second, \(D\) and \(A\) have unit diagonal, normalization slack at least \(\zeta_0\), and condition number at most \(\overline\kappa\), where the inverse matrices in the condition numbers are \(U\) and \(V\), respectively. Third,
\[
\Omega,\Psi,\Sigma_e,\Gamma_e\in\mathbb S_+^p,
\qquad
s_e,t_e\in[0,\infty)^p,
\tag{7}
\]
and the two covariance systems obey
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)
\quad(e\in H_1),
\qquad
A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)
\quad(e\in H_2).
\tag{8}
\]
Fourth, the affine-separation functional, computed over every \(q\)-element subset of the corresponding size-\(h\) fitted set, is at least \(\gamma_0\) on both sides. Finally, both sides obey the scale bound \(\overline M\), namely
\[
1+\|\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline M
\tag{9}
\]
and the analogous inequality with \((\Omega,H_1,\Sigma,s)\) replaced by \((\Psi,H_2,\Gamma,t)\).

Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the union of these tuple families over the finitely many choices of \(H_1,H_2,S\). Equations (3)--(4) bound \(D,U,A,V\). The two inequalities in (9) bound the positive-semidefinite nuisances, covariance matrices, and every nonnegative shift coordinate. All defining conditions are closed: (6) and (8) are polynomial equalities; the positive-semidefinite cones and nonnegative orthants in (7) are closed; the operator-norm inequalities are closed; the normalization-slack condition is a weak inequality for the maximum of finitely many continuous cycle products; and the affine-separation functional is a finite minimum of finite maxima of continuous absolute determinants. Thus each fixed-index tuple family is closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, hence compact, and the finite union \(\mathfrak T\) is compact. Notice that no lower eigenvalue bound was used: allowing \(\Sigma_e\) and \(\Gamma_e\) to reach the positive-semidefinite boundary is exactly what makes this comparison family closed.

Define the continuous residual
\[
\mathfrak r(\tau)=
\max_{e\in S}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
\tag{10}
\]
and the closed far family
\[
\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}
=\{\tau\in\mathfrak T:\|AU-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\geq\rho_0\}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}=\varnothing\), set \(\mu_0=1\). Otherwise compactness and continuity give an attained minimum
\[
\mu_0=\min_{\tau\in\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}}\mathfrak r(\tau).
\tag{12}
\]
This minimum is strictly positive. Indeed, suppose that a far tuple satisfies \(\mathfrak r(\tau)=0\). Equations (8) and (10) then give, for every \(e\in S\),
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e),
\qquad
A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e).
\tag{13}
\]
For every \(k<\ell\), first-side affine separation on the particular \(q\)-element set \(S\) supplies three labels whose affine minor has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0\). Hence the first-side cloud
\[
\{(s_{ek},s_{e\ell}):e\in S\}
\]
is not contained in an affine line.

Since \(|E\setminus S|=m-q=2c\), choose a partition \(E\setminus S=C_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_2\) with \(|C_1|=|C_2|=c\). Construct one positive-semidefinite covariance family \(\widetilde\Sigma\) by setting
\[
\widetilde\Sigma_e=
\begin{cases}
\Sigma_e,&e\in S,\\
D^{-1}\Omega D^{-\mathsf T},&e\in C_1,\\
A^{-1}\Psi A^{-\mathsf T},&e\in C_2.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
The first explanation fits \(S\cup C_1\) using shifts \(s_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts on \(C_1\); the second fits \(S\cup C_2\) using shifts \(t_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts on \(C_2\). The two fitted sets have size \(q+c=h\), their overlap is \(S\), all shifts are nonnegative by (7), and both invariant nuisances and every covariance in (14) are positive semidefinite by congruence. The two structural matrices satisfy the BACKSHIFT normalization by construction. Therefore all hypotheses of \(\text{lem:overlap-uniqueness}\) hold, and that lemma gives \(A=D\). By (6), \(U=D^{-1}\), so \(AU=I_p\), contradicting (11). It follows that
\[
\mu_0>0,
\tag{15}
\]
including under the convention \(\mu_0=1\) when the far family is empty. Define, non-effectively,
\[
r_0=\frac{\mu_0}{2\sqrt p\,L_0^2}>0.
\tag{16}
\]
The family \(\mathfrak T\) and hence \(\mu_0\) were defined solely from \(p,m,c,\gamma_0,\zeta_0,\overline\kappa,\overline M\). Thus \(r_0\) has precisely the dependence asserted in the theorem. Equation (16) is an existence definition and supplies no method for computing \(r_0\).

\textbf{Entry into the identity neighborhood.}
Fix an outcome in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\), put \(r=r_{n,\alpha,H}\), assume \(r\leq r_0\), and take any \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\). By \(\text{def:confidence-union}\), there exists a simultaneous selection \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\), \(e\in E\), for which \(A\in\mathcal R_c(\Gamma)\). By \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there exist a fitted set of cardinality at least \(h\), one nuisance \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and nonnegative shifts satisfying the candidate covariance equations. Restrict that fitted set and its witnesses to a subset \(K\subseteq E\) with \(|K|=h\). The declarative candidate premise gives all four candidate-side bounds for this restricted tuple.

Inclusion--exclusion yields
\[
|H\cap K|\geq|H|+|K|-|E|=2h-m=q.
\tag{17}
\]
Choose \(S\subseteq H\cap K\) with \(|S|=q\), and put \(U=D^{-1}\) and \(V=A^{-1}\). The true assumptions and four true-side bounds, together with the candidate equations and four candidate-side bounds, place the resulting objects in \(\mathfrak T\). For \(e\in S\subseteq H\), \(\text{def:honest-region-radius}\) gives
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq r.
\tag{18}
\]
The candidate covariance equation, (3), (16), (18), submultiplicativity, and \(\|X\|_{\mathrm F}\leq\sqrt p\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
&=\left\|A(\Sigma_e-\Gamma_e)A^{\mathsf T}\right\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\leq\sqrt p\,L_0^2r
\leq\mu_0/2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
If this tuple belonged to \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\), then (12) would make the maximum residual at least \(\mu_0\) when the far family is nonempty, contradicting (19); membership is impossible by definition when the far family is empty. Consequently, with
\[
Q=AD^{-1}=I_p+R,
\qquad
u=\|R\|_{\mathrm F},
\]
one has
\[
u<\rho_0\leq1,
\qquad
K_0\overline M u\leq\frac12.
\tag{20}
\]

\textbf{Local affine-minor inversion.}
Fix \(e_0\in S\) and write \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\). For every \(i<j\), the true-side affine-separation bound on \(S\) supplies a triple \(a,b,d\in S\) whose \((i,j)\) affine determinant has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0\). With \(\widetilde x_e=(x_{ei},x_{ej})\), translation invariance of the affine determinant gives
\[
\det(\widetilde x_a-\widetilde x_b,
\widetilde x_d-\widetilde x_b)
=\det(\widetilde x_a,\widetilde x_d)
+\det(\widetilde x_d,\widetilde x_b)
+\det(\widetilde x_b,\widetilde x_a).
\tag{21}
\]
By the triangle inequality, two labels \(e,f\in S\) therefore satisfy
\[
|x_{ei}x_{fj}-x_{ej}x_{fi}|\geq\gamma_0/3.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(E_e=\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\). Subtract the candidate equation at \(e_0\) from that at \(e\), subtract the true covariance equation at \(e_0\) from that at \(e\), and use \(A=QD\). The common nuisances cancel, and the \((i,j)\) off-diagonal entry is
\[
R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}
=-\sum_{k=1}^pR_{ik}R_{jk}x_{ek}
-[A(E_e-E_{e_0})A^{\mathsf T}]_{ij}.
\tag{23}
\]
The true scale bound and nonnegativity give \(|x_{ek}|\leq\overline M\). Equation (18) gives \(\|E_e-E_{e_0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2r\), while (3) and (20) give
\[
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|QD\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq(1+u)L_0.
\tag{24}
\]
Using Cauchy--Schwarz, \(\sum_k|R_{ik}R_{jk}|\leq u^2\), and (23)--(24), one obtains
\[
|R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}|
\leq\overline M u^2+2rL_0^2(1+u)^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Apply (25) at the two labels from (22). The coefficient determinant of the resulting two-by-two linear system has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0/3\), while every coefficient has absolute value at most \(\overline M\). Cramer's rule therefore gives, for every ordered pair \(i\neq j\),
\[
|R_{ij}|
\leq\frac{6\overline M}{\gamma_0}
\left\{\overline M u^2+2rL_0^2(1+u)^2\right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The unit diagonals of \(A\) and \(D\) imply
\[
0=(A-D)_{ii}=(RD)_{ii}
=R_{ii}+\sum_{k\neq i}R_{ik}D_{ki}.
\tag{27}
\]
For each \(i\), the squared norm of the \(i\)-th column of \(D\) is at most \(L_0^2\) by (3). Squaring (26), summing over the \(p(p-1)\) ordered off-diagonal entries, applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (27), and taking square roots yields
\[
u\leq K_0
\left\{\overline M u^2+2rL_0^2(1+u)^2\right\}.
\tag{28}
\]
By (20), the quadratic term on the right of (28) is at most \(u/2\), and \((1+u)^2\leq4\). Hence
\[
u\leq16K_0L_0^2r.
\tag{29}
\]
It follows from (3) that
\[
\|A-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|RD\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq uL_0
\leq16K_0L_0^3r
=C_0r.
\tag{30}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) proves the outer-radius inequality. On \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\), \(\text{thm:confidence-union-coverage}\) gives \(D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), so the confidence union is nonempty. For any \(A_1,A_2\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), the triangle inequality and (30) give
\[
\|A_1-A_2\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|A_1-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|A_2-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2C_0r.
\]
Taking the supremum proves the diameter inequality. The constants \(r_0\) and \(C_0\) are common to the fixed four-margin class, so along covered outcomes \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\to0\) implies both asserted limits.
