The causal substrate is the cyclic linear equilibrium model whose population covariance equation is given in \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\).  Its structural target is \(D\), and hence \(B=I_p-D\); the observed-law recovery map is the compatibility set \(\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\) in the corrected \(\text{def:compatible-set}\).  We prove their relationship rather than defining the causal target to equal an observed-law functional.

First suppose \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_+^p\) and \(\delta_H>c\), and fix an arbitrary assignment of positive-semidefinite matrices to the \(c\) slots in \(E\setminus H\).  By \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\), \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\), the local premise \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and \(D\in\mathcal D\) from \(\text{ass:backshift-normalization}\), the choices
\[
 K=H,\qquad \Psi=\Omega,\qquad t_e=s_e
\]
satisfy every constraint in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\).  Thus \(D\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).  This argument does not require an honest or replaced covariance to be invertible.

Take any \(A\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).  By \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there are \(K\subseteq E\), \(|K|\ge h\), \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and \(t_e\in[0,\infty)^p\) such that
\[
 \Sigma_e=A^{-1}\{\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\}A^{-\mathsf T},
 \qquad e\in K. \tag{1}
\]
For \(J=H\cap K\), inclusion--exclusion and \(|E|=m\), \(|H|=h=m-c\), give
\[
 |J|=|H|+|K|-|H\cup K|
 \ge h+h-m=h-c. \tag{2}
\]
By \(\text{def:environment-distance}\) and \(\text{def:max-line-occupancy}\),
\[
 \delta_H>c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M_{k\ell}<h-c\quad\text{for every }1\le k<\ell\le p. \tag{3}
\]
If the indexed points \(\{(s_{ek},s_{e\ell}):e\in J\}\) lay on an affine line for some \(k<\ell\), that line would contain at least \(|J|\ge h-c\) honest indices, contradicting (3).  Thus every pairwise cloud on \(J\) is noncollinear.  Equation (1), \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\), the positive-semidefinite nuisances \(\Psi,\Omega\), the nonnegative shifts \(t_e,s_e\), and \(A,D\in\mathcal D\) verify all premises of \(\text{lem:overlap-uniqueness}\), which yields \(A=D\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_c(\Sigma)=\{D\}. \tag{4}
\]
No value of a slot outside \(H\) entered this argument, so (4) holds for every positive-semidefinite replacement assignment, including singular assignments.

We next establish the equivalence with the affine-minor margin.  Put \(q=h-c\).  Any family of \(h\) indexed points has at least \(\min(2,h)\) indices on one affine line, whence
\[
 M_{k\ell}\ge\min(2,h). \tag{5}
\]
Thus (3) is impossible when \(q<3\), exactly the case in which \(\text{def:affine-separation}\) sets \(\gamma_H=0\).  Suppose \(q\ge3\).  For fixed \(S\subseteq H\), \(|S|=q\), the points \((s_{ek},s_{e\ell})\), \(e\in S\), lie on an affine line if and only if the matrix with rows \((1,s_{ek},s_{e\ell})\) has rank at most two.  By the elementary rank criterion, this is equivalent to the vanishing of every minor
\[
 \det\!\begin{pmatrix}
 1&s_{ek}&s_{e\ell}\\
 1&s_{fk}&s_{f\ell}\\
 1&s_{gk}&s_{g\ell}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad e<f<g,\quad e,f,g\in S. \tag{6}
\]
All index collections are finite.  Hence the minimum in \(\text{def:affine-separation}\) is positive exactly when every \(q\)-element subset contains a noncollinear triple for every coordinate pair, or equivalently when no affine line contains \(q\) honest indices.  Therefore
\[
 \gamma_H>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M_{k\ell}<q\ \text{for every }k<\ell
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \delta_H>c. \tag{7}
\]

It remains to prove the pointwise converse under the stated local interior condition \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  Assume \(\delta_H\le c\) and retain \(q=h-c=m-2c\).

If \(q\le0\), then \(|E\setminus H|=c\ge h\).  Choose \(H'\subseteq E\setminus H\) with \(|H'|=h\), fix distinct coordinates \(k,\ell\), and let \(E_{k\ell}\) have its only nonzero entry, equal to one, in position \((k,\ell)\).  For a sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\), set
\[
 D'=D+\varepsilon E_{k\ell}. \tag{8}
\]
Its diagonal is one.  Invertibility persists because determinant is continuous and \(D\) is invertible.  Moreover, \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)\) is the maximum of finitely many absolute cycle-product polynomials in \(A\), hence is continuous; the strict inequality \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D)<1\) therefore persists.  Thus \(D'\in\mathcal D\), and \(D'\ne D\).  Set \(\Omega'=I_p\) and assign, for every \(e\in E\setminus H\),
\[
 \Sigma_e=(D')^{-1}\Omega'(D')^{-\mathsf T}. \tag{9}
\]
These matrices are positive definite by invertible congruence.  The original explanation fits \(H\), while \((D',\Omega')\) with zero shifts fits \(H'\).  Hence \(D,D'\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).

Suppose now \(q>0\).  From \(\delta_H\le c\), \(\text{def:environment-distance}\), and \(\text{def:max-line-occupancy}\), there are \(k<\ell\), an affine line \(L\), and \(S\subseteq H\) with \(|S|=q\) such that all \((s_{ek},s_{e\ell})\), \(e\in S\), lie on \(L\).  Choose a nonzero direction \(v=(v_k,v_\ell)\) of \(L\), fix \(e_0\in S\), and define \(\tau_e\in\mathbb R\) by
\[
 (s_{ek}-s_{e_0k},s_{e\ell}-s_{e_0\ell})
 =\tau_e(v_k,v_\ell),\qquad e\in S. \tag{10}
\]
For real \(\varepsilon\), put
\[
 \eta_k(\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon v_kD_{\ell k},
 \qquad
 \eta_\ell(\varepsilon)=1-\varepsilon v_\ell D_{k\ell}, \tag{11}
\]
and let \(Q_\varepsilon\) equal the identity outside its \(k,\ell\) principal block, where
\[
 (Q_\varepsilon)_{\{k,\ell\},\{k,\ell\}}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 \eta_k(\varepsilon)^{-1}&\varepsilon v_k\eta_k(\varepsilon)^{-1}\\
 -\varepsilon v_\ell\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)^{-1}&\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{12}
\]
At \(\varepsilon=0\), the two denominators and \(\det(Q_\varepsilon)\) equal one, so all remain nonzero near zero.  Define \(D'_\varepsilon=Q_\varepsilon D\).  Since \(D_{kk}=D_{\ell\ell}=1\),
\[
 (D'_\varepsilon)_{kk}
 =\frac{1+\varepsilon v_kD_{\ell k}}{\eta_k(\varepsilon)}=1,
 \qquad
 (D'_\varepsilon)_{\ell\ell}
 =\frac{1-\varepsilon v_\ell D_{k\ell}}{\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)}=1, \tag{13}
\]
and all other diagonal entries remain one.  The determinant and cycle-product continuity argument used after (8) shows that \(D'_\varepsilon\in\mathcal D\) for all sufficiently small \(|\varepsilon|\).  Since \(v\ne0\), one off-diagonal entry of (12) is nonzero for sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\); thus \(Q_\varepsilon\ne I_p\) and \(D'_\varepsilon\ne D\).

For \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\), direct multiplication of the block in (12), followed by (10), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \relax[Q_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(x_e)Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}]_{k\ell}
 &=\frac{-\varepsilon v_\ell x_{ek}+\varepsilon v_kx_{e\ell}}
 {\eta_k(\varepsilon)\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)}\\
 &=\frac{-\varepsilon v_\ell\tau_ev_k+\varepsilon v_k\tau_ev_\ell}
 {\eta_k(\varepsilon)\eta_\ell(\varepsilon)}=0.
 \end{aligned} \tag{14}
\]
Every other off-diagonal entry vanishes because \(Q_\varepsilon\) is block diagonal with an identity complement.  Consequently
\[
 C_\varepsilon
 =\operatorname{off}\!\left(Q_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(s_e)Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}\right),
 \qquad \operatorname{off}(G)=G-\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{diag}(G)), \tag{15}
\]
is independent of \(e\in S\).  Define
\[
 \Omega'_\varepsilon=Q_\varepsilon\Omega Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}+C_\varepsilon. \tag{16}
\]
This symmetric matrix depends continuously on \(\varepsilon\), and at zero equals \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  Openness of the positive-definite cone therefore permits a sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\) for which, simultaneously,
\[
 D'=D'_\varepsilon\in\mathcal D,\qquad D'\ne D,\qquad
 \Omega'=\Omega'_\varepsilon\in\mathbb S_{++}^p. \tag{17}
\]
For \(e\in S\) and \(1\le i\le p\), set
\[
 t_{ei}
 =[Q_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(s_e)Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}]_{ii}
 =\sum_{j=1}^p(Q_\varepsilon)_{ij}^2s_{ej}\ge0, \tag{18}
\]
where the last inequality is \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\).  Equations (15)--(18) and \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D'\Sigma_eD'^{\mathsf T}
 &=Q_\varepsilon\{\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\}Q_\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=\Omega'+\operatorname{diag}(t_e),
 \qquad e\in S.
 \end{aligned} \tag{19}
\]
Let \(H'=S\cup(E\setminus H)\).  The union is disjoint and
\[
 |H'|=q+c=h. \tag{20}
\]
For every \(e\in E\setminus H\), assign
\[
 \Sigma_e=(D')^{-1}\Omega'(D')^{-\mathsf T}. \tag{21}
\]
These replacements are positive definite by (17) and invertible congruence.  Equations (18)--(21) show that \(D'\) fits every slot of \(H'\), with zero shifts on \(E\setminus H\), while \(D\) fits \(H\).  Hence \(D,D'\in\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\), proving the fixed-interior-instance converse.

The published class permits \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_+^p\) and every positive-semidefinite covariance family, and contains the strict subclass with \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\) and all covariances positive definite.  For any design with \(\delta_H\le c\), the preceding construction can be run, for example, at \(D=I_p\) and \(\Omega=I_p\).  Thus a uniform guarantee over the published class fails below the threshold, proving minimax sharpness without asserting ambiguity for a preassigned singular \(\Omega\).  Finally, when \(c=0\), \(h=m\) and
\[
 \delta_H>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M_{k\ell}<h\ \text{for every }k<\ell,
\]
which says exactly that no complete pairwise shift cloud is contained in an affine line, the stated BACKSHIFT noncollinearity criterion.
