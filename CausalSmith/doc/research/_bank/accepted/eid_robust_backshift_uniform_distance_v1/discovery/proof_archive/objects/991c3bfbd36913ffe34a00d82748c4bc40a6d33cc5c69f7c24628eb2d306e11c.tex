\textbf{Coverage.}
Fix \(n\geq 1\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and a sample outcome in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\).  For \(e\in H\), set \(\Gamma_e=\Sigma_e\).  For each \(e\in E\setminus H\), choose \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\); this finite choice is possible because every region is nonempty by the declared type of \(\mathcal C^{(n)}_\alpha\).  Every \(\Gamma_e\) is positive definite because every region is a subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  In the compatibility definition take
\[
 A=D,\qquad K=H,\qquad \Psi=\Omega,\qquad t_e=s_e\quad(e\in H).
\]
The cardinality condition \(|K|=h\) follows from the definition of \(H\).  The BACKSHIFT-normalization assumption gives \(D\in\mathcal D\); the invariant-noise-interior assumption gives \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\subseteq\mathbb S_+^p\); and the nonnegative-shifts assumption gives \(s_e\in[0,\infty)^p\).  Finally, for every \(e\in K=H\), the honest-covariance-model assumption and the choice of \(\Gamma_e\) give
\[
 \Gamma_e=\Sigma_e
 =D^{-1}\bigl(\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\bigr)D^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus all conjuncts in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) hold, so \(D\in\mathcal R_c(\Gamma)\).  Since this \(\Gamma\) is one of the families in the union in \(\text{def:confidence-union}\),
\[
 G_{n,\alpha,H}\subseteq\{D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\}.
\]
Monotonicity of probability therefore yields
\[
 \Pr\{D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\}
 \geq \Pr(G_{n,\alpha,H})\geq 1-\alpha.
\]

\textbf{Exact rational semialgebraic representation.}
Suppose every entry of every \(\Sigma_e\) is rational.  It is enough to enumerate sets \(K\subseteq E\) of cardinality exactly \(h\): a witness on a set of size at least \(h\) restricts to any \(h\)-element subset, and a witness on an \(h\)-element set already satisfies the definition.  This is precisely the finite subset reduction named in \(\text{def:subset-consensus}\).

Fix such a set \(K\).  Introduce real variables \(A\in\mathbb R^{p\times p}\), a symmetric real matrix \(\Psi\), and \(t_e\in\mathbb R^p\) for \(e\in K\).  The following is a first-order formula over the ordered real field:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_K(A,\Psi,t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 &A_{ii}=1 &&(1\leq i\leq p),\\
 &(\det A)^2>0,\\
 &1-\pi_C(A)>0\ \text{ and }\ 1+\pi_C(A)>0
   &&(C\text{ a directed simple cycle}),\\
 &\Psi=\Psi^{\mathsf T},\qquad
   v^{\mathsf T}\Psi v\geq0 &&(v\in\mathbb R^p),\\
 &t_{ej}\geq0 &&(e\in K,\ 1\leq j\leq p),\\
 &A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)
   &&(e\in K).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\pi_C(A)\) is the product of the entries of \(I_p-A\) along \(C\).  There are only finitely many directed simple cycles.  By the definition of \(\operatorname{CP}\), their two strict inequalities are equivalent to \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)<1\).  The determinant inequality is equivalent to invertibility.  The universally quantified quadratic-form inequalities are exactly the definition of \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\).  All displayed atoms are polynomial sign conditions with rational coefficients, because \(\Sigma_e\) is rational.

If \(F_K(A,\Psi,t)\) holds, invertibility permits multiplication of its last equality by \(A^{-1}\) and \(A^{-\mathsf T}\), giving
\[
 \Sigma_e=A^{-1}\bigl(\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\bigr)A^{-\mathsf T}
 \quad(e\in K).
\]
Thus \(A\) is compatible.  Conversely, multiplying each covariance equality in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) by \(A\) and \(A^{\mathsf T}\) gives \(F_K\).  Consequently the exact recovery set is
\[
 \mathcal R_c(\Sigma)
 =\bigcup_{\substack{K\subseteq E\\ |K|=h}}
   \left\{A:\exists\Psi\,\exists(t_e:e\in K)\ F_K(A,\Psi,t)\right\}.
\]
The union is finite.  Applying \(\text{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) to the displayed formula produces an effectively computable quantifier-free Boolean combination \(\varphi_\Sigma(A)\) of rational-polynomial sign conditions whose solution set is exactly \(\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).

For completeness, put
\[
 d(A,A')=\sum_{i=1}^p\sum_{j=1}^p(A_{ij}-A'_{ij})^2.
\]
The three mutually relevant output assertions are expressed exactly by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Empty}&:\quad \neg\exists A\,\varphi_\Sigma(A),\\
 \mathsf{Singleton}&:\quad
   \exists A\left[\varphi_\Sigma(A)\wedge
   \forall A'\bigl(\varphi_\Sigma(A')\Rightarrow d(A,A')=0\bigr)\right],\\
 \mathsf{Multiple}&:\quad
   \exists A\,\exists A'\left[
   \varphi_\Sigma(A)\wedge\varphi_\Sigma(A')\wedge d(A,A')>0\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Each is again a first-order ordered-field formula with rational-polynomial atoms.  A second application of \(\text{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) gives a finite rational-polynomial sign certificate deciding each assertion.  The singleton formula includes existence, so it does not confuse an empty set with a set having at most one point.

For every \(e<f<g\) in \(K\) and \(k<\ell\), define the fitted-shift minor
\[
 \Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)=
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 1&t_{ek}&t_{e\ell}\\
 1&t_{fk}&t_{f\ell}\\
 1&t_{gk}&t_{g\ell}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is a polynomial, so all atoms \(\Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)=0\), \(\Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)>0\), and \(-\Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)>0\) may be added to the finite sign family before elimination; no ratio is introduced.  For a set of at least three fitted profiles, some such determinant is nonzero exactly when those profiles are not contained in one affine line: a nonzero determinant gives three affinely independent points, whereas if all determinants vanish then the affine span has dimension at most one.  Hence these atoms certify affine separation.  They do not certify feasibility by themselves.  For example, the profiles \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\) have determinant one regardless of whether a proposed matrix has unit diagonal or whether a proposed invariant matrix is positive semidefinite.  The covariance equations, quadratic-form inequalities for \(\Psi\), nonnegativity of \(t\), determinant inequality, unit-diagonal equations, and all cycle-product inequalities in \(F_K\) remain separate necessary conjuncts.

The same representation also proves the chart assertion.  Let \(C\) be a raw diagonalizer in a normalization chart, set \(q_i=C_{ii}\), and suppose \(q_i\neq0\) for all \(i\).  Introduce the normalized matrix \(A\) by the polynomial relations
\[
 q_iA_{ij}=C_{ij},\qquad q_i=C_{ii},\qquad q_i^2>0.
\]
Then \(A_{ii}=1\) and \(A=\operatorname{diag}(q_1,\ldots,q_p)^{-1}C\).  Invertibility is encoded by \((\det C)^2>0\).  If a cycle product becomes \(N_C(C)/Q_C(q)\), where \(Q_C(q)\neq0\), then
\[
 \left|\frac{N_C(C)}{Q_C(q)}\right|<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_C(q)^2-N_C(C)^2>0.
\]
Covariance equalities are cleared by multiplying by the relevant nonzero pivot products, while inequalities are cleared with positive squared denominators.  Thus pivot nonvanishing together with the polynomial normalization, invertibility, covariance, and cycle conditions is equivalent to the normalized chart conditions.  Omitting any of them would enlarge the projected set and would not be a sound chart certificate.

\textbf{Finite-membership-query obstruction.}
It remains to show that arbitrary confidence regions do not supply enough information for exact computation.  Consider
\[
 p=2,\qquad m=h=3,\qquad c=0,\qquad H=E=\{1,2,3\},
\]
and take
\[
 D=I_2,\qquad \Omega=2I_2,\qquad
 s_1=(0,0),\quad s_2=(1,0),\quad s_3=(0,1).
\]
These choices satisfy the normalization, strict invariant-noise positivity, and nonnegative-shift assumptions.  The honest-covariance equation gives the rational positive-definite matrices
\[
 \Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
There is one coordinate pair and one three-element subset in \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), and
\[
 \gamma_H=left|
 \det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}
 \right|=1.
\]

We first compute the baseline compatibility set.  Let \(A\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma)\).  Since \(h=m=3\), the compatible subset must be all of \(E\).  Unit diagonal writes
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\y&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtracting the transformed covariance equation for environment one from those for environments two and three cancels the common \(\Psi\) and gives diagonal matrices.  Their off-diagonal entries are therefore zero.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 A(\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
 =A\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}A^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\y&y^2\end{pmatrix},
\]
so \(y=0\), and
\[
 A(\Sigma_3-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
 =A\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}A^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}x^2&x\\x&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so \(x=0\).  Hence every compatible \(A\) is \(I_2\).  Conversely, \(I_2\) is compatible with \(\Psi=2I_2\) and the displayed nonnegative shifts.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_0(\Sigma)=\{I_2\}.
\]

For any nonzero rational number \(q\), define
\[
 A_q=\begin{pmatrix}1&q\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Psi_q=A_q\Sigma_1A_q^{\mathsf T}
 =2\begin{pmatrix}1+q^2&q\\q&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
 \Gamma_{3,q}
 =A_q^{-1}\bigl(\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\bigr)A_q^{-\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}2+q^2&-q\\-q&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix \(A_q\) is invertible, has unit diagonal, and \(I_2-A_q\) has zero product around its only directed two-cycle; hence \(A_q\in\mathcal D\).  The matrix \(\Psi_q\) is positive definite because it is the congruence of \(2I_2\) by the invertible matrix \(A_q\).  Moreover, \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) is positive definite since its leading diagonal entry is positive and
\[
 \det(\Gamma_{3,q})=3(2+q^2)-q^2=6+2q^2>0.
\]
The first column of \(A_q\) is \((1,0)^{\mathsf T}\), so
\[
 A_q\Sigma_1A_q^{\mathsf T}=\Psi_q,
 \qquad
 A_q\Sigma_2A_q^{\mathsf T}=\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(1,0),
 \qquad
 A_q\Gamma_{3,q}A_q^{\mathsf T}=\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
\]
Thus \(A_q\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,q})\), with an invariant nuisance that is positive definite and shifts that are nonnegative.  All entries are rational, and \(A_q\neq I_2\).

Now suppose, for contradiction, that a deterministic procedure always returns the exact confidence union after finitely many membership queries to each region.  Run it on the nonempty singleton regions
\[
 \mathcal C^0_1=\{\Sigma_1\},\qquad
 \mathcal C^0_2=\{\Sigma_2\},\qquad
 \mathcal C^0_3=\{\Sigma_3\}.
\]
Its exact union is \(\mathcal U^0=\mathcal R_0(\Sigma)=\{I_2\}\).  The run asks only finitely many questions of the third membership oracle.  Because the matrices \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) are distinct as \(q\) ranges over the infinitely many nonzero rationals, choose a rational \(q\neq0\) for which \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) was not queried.  Define a second region family by
\[
 \mathcal C^1_1=\mathcal C^0_1,\qquad
 \mathcal C^1_2=\mathcal C^0_2,\qquad
 \mathcal C^1_3=\{\Sigma_3,\Gamma_{3,q}\}.
\]
Every region remains a nonempty subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^2\), and every listed matrix is rational.  Every membership answer along the first transcript is identical for the two families: the first two regions are unchanged, and the only new member of the third region was not queried.  Induction over the deterministic query sequence therefore gives the same complete transcript and the same returned set on both inputs.  However,
\[
 A_q\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,q})
 \subseteq\mathcal U^1,
 \qquad
 A_q\notin\mathcal U^0=\{I_2\}.
\]
The exact unions differ, a contradiction.  Hence no deterministic finite-membership-query procedure can compute the exact union for the frozen arbitrary-set region class, even at the strictly separated rational design \(\gamma_H=1\).  In particular, a rule required to return the full exact union away from \(\gamma_H=0\) and to abstain only at the boundary already fails on this example.  An effective representation of the regions is therefore necessary for the requested computable full-output-or-abstain construction.

Finally, the structural causal target was fixed independently as \(B=I_p-D\).  The coverage inclusion is a confidence statement about that target, and \(\varphi_\Sigma\) is an observed-law compatibility functional.  The preceding arguments prove their stated relationships; neither argument defines the causal target to equal an observed functional.
