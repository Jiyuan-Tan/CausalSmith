Put \(q=m-2c=h-c\). By \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), the case \(q<3\) would give \(\gamma_H=0\), contrary to \(\gamma_H\geq\gamma_0>0\). Hence
\[
q\geq3.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Uniform structural bounds.}
Let \(G\) denote either the true structural matrix or any feasible candidate structural matrix satisfying the asserted normalization-slack and condition-number bounds. Its diagonal is the all-ones vector by \(\text{ass:backshift-normalization}\) on the true side and by \(G\in\mathcal D\), via \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\), on the candidate side. In the Leibniz expansion of \(\det G\), the identity-permutation term has absolute value one. Every other permutation is a product of fixed points and pairwise disjoint directed simple cycles of length at least two. Fixed points contribute one, and the absolute product contributed by each nontrivial cycle equals the corresponding cycle product in \(I_p-G\). Therefore the normalization-slack bound and \(0<\zeta_0\leq1\) show that every Leibniz term has absolute value at most one. The triangle inequality gives
\[
|\det G|\leq p!.
\tag{2}
\]
Write the singular values in decreasing order as \(\sigma_1(G),\ldots,\sigma_p(G)\). Since
\[
\sigma_1(G)=\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad
\frac{\sigma_1(G)}{\sigma_p(G)}
=\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa,
\]
we have \(\sigma_j(G)\geq\sigma_1(G)/\overline\kappa\) for \(2\leq j\leq p\). Thus (2) implies
\[
\frac{\sigma_1(G)^p}{\overline\kappa^{p-1}}
\leq\prod_{j=1}^p\sigma_j(G)
=|\det G|
\leq p!,
\]
and hence
\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\bigl(p!\,\overline\kappa^{p-1}\bigr)^{1/p}
=L_0.
\tag{3}
\]
Because a unit diagonal entry has absolute value one, \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq1\). Consequently the condition-number bound also yields
\[
\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{A compact comparison family and a positive far-family gap.}
Define
\[
\rho_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2K_0\overline M}\right\}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
For every \(H_1,H_2\subseteq E\) with \(|H_1|=|H_2|=h\) and every \(S\subseteq H_1\cap H_2\) with \(|S|=q\), consider tuples
\[
\tau=\bigl(D,U,\Omega,(s_e,\Sigma_e)_{e\in H_1},
A,V,\Psi,(t_e,\Gamma_e)_{e\in H_2}\bigr)
\]
satisfying all of the following conditions. First,
\[
DU=UD=I_p,
\qquad
AV=VA=I_p.
\tag{6}
\]
Second, \(D\) and \(A\) have unit diagonal, satisfy
\[
1-\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D)\geq\zeta_0,
\qquad
1-\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)\geq\zeta_0,
\]
and satisfy \(\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline\kappa\) and \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|V\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline\kappa\). Third,
\[
\Omega,\Psi,\Sigma_e,\Gamma_e\in\mathbb S_+^p,
\qquad
s_e,t_e\in[0,\infty)^p,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)
\quad(e\in H_1),
\qquad
A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)
\quad(e\in H_2).
\tag{8}
\]
Fourth, the affine-separation functional appearing in the theorem, computed over all \(q\)-element subsets of \(H_1\) for \(s\) and of \(H_2\) for \(t\), is at least \(\gamma_0\) on both sides. Finally,
\[
1+\|\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline M,
\tag{9}
\]
and the analogous inequality holds with \((\Omega,H_1,\Sigma,s)\) replaced by \((\Psi,H_2,\Gamma,t)\).

Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the finite disjoint union of these families, retaining \((H_1,H_2,S)\) as a discrete part of each tuple. Equations (3)--(4) bound \(D,U,A,V\). The two scale inequalities bound every positive-semidefinite nuisance and covariance matrix and, since the shifts are nonnegative, every shift coordinate. Each fixed-index family is closed: (6) and (8) are polynomial equalities; the positive-semidefinite cones and nonnegative orthants in (7) are closed; operator norms are continuous; \(\operatorname{CP}\) is the maximum of finitely many continuous absolute cycle-product functions; and each affine-separation functional is a finite minimum of finite maxima of continuous absolute determinants. Thus every fixed-index family is closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional real Euclidean space. By \(\text{lem:euclidean-heine-borel}\), it is compact. Given any open cover of the finite disjoint union, restrict it to each component, choose a finite subcover there, and take the union of the finitely many resulting finite subcovers. Hence \(\mathfrak T\) is compact. No positive lower eigenvalue is used; allowing the covariance matrices to lie on the positive-semidefinite boundary makes this comparison family closed.

Define the continuous residual
\[
\mathfrak r(\tau)=
\max_{e\in S}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
\tag{10}
\]
and the closed subfamily
\[
\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}
=\{\tau\in\mathfrak T:\|AU-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\geq\rho_0\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The map \(\mathfrak r\) is continuous on each discrete component because matrix multiplication, subtraction, the Frobenius norm, and a finite maximum are continuous. The family \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\) is closed in \(\mathfrak T\). Indeed, it is the inverse image of the closed interval \([\rho_0,+\infty)\) under the continuous map \(\tau\mapsto\|AU-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\). A closed subset of a compact space is compact: add its open complement to any open cover, take a finite subcover of the ambient compact space, and then discard the complement. If \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}=\varnothing\), set \(\mu_0=1\). Otherwise it is therefore a nonempty compact set, and \(\text{lem:compact-extreme-value}\) gives
\[
\mu_0=\min_{\tau\in\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}}\mathfrak r(\tau).
\tag{12}
\]
We show that this minimum is positive. If instead \(\mu_0=0\), an attaining tuple would satisfy, for every \(e\in S\),
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e),
\qquad
A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e).
\tag{13}
\]
For every \(k<\ell\), first-side affine separation on the particular \(q\)-element set \(S\) supplies a triple with nonzero affine determinant. Hence
\[
\{(s_{ek},s_{e\ell}):e\in S\}
\]
is not contained in an affine line.

By (1), \(|E\setminus S|=m-q=2c\), so choose a disjoint partition \(E\setminus S=C_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_2\) with \(|C_1|=|C_2|=c\). Define a positive-semidefinite covariance family by
\[
\widetilde\Sigma_e=
\begin{cases}
\Sigma_e,&e\in S,\\
D^{-1}\Omega D^{-\mathsf T},&e\in C_1,\\
A^{-1}\Psi A^{-\mathsf T},&e\in C_2.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
Positive semidefiniteness follows from (7) and congruence. The first explanation fits \(S\cup C_1\) with shifts \(s_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts on \(C_1\); the second fits \(S\cup C_2\) with shifts \(t_e\) on \(S\) and zero shifts on \(C_2\). Both fitted sets have cardinality \(q+c=h\), their overlap is \(S\), and all invariant nuisances and shifts have the required signs. Moreover, the positive normalization slack implies \(D,A\in\mathcal D\) by \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\). Therefore \(\text{lem:overlap-uniqueness}\) applied to \(\widetilde\Sigma\) gives \(A=D\). Equation (6) then gives \(AU=I_p\), contradicting (11). Thus
\[
\mu_0>0.
\tag{15}
\]
Set, non-effectively,
\[
r_0=\frac{\mu_0}{2\sqrt p\,L_0^2}>0.
\tag{16}
\]
The family \(\mathfrak T\), the threshold \(\rho_0\), and the minimum \(\mu_0\) use only \(p,m,c,\gamma_0,\zeta_0,\overline\kappa,\overline M\). Hence \(r_0\) has exactly the dependence asserted in the statement. This compactness definition supplies no method for evaluating \(\mu_0\) or \(r_0\).

\textbf{Entry into the local neighborhood.}
Fix an outcome in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\), write \(r=r_{n,\alpha,H}\), and assume \(r\leq r_0\). By \(\text{thm:confidence-union-coverage}\), \(D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), so the confidence union is nonempty. Take arbitrary \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\). By \(\text{def:confidence-union}\), there is a simultaneous selection \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\), \(e\in E\), such that \(A\in\mathcal R_c(\Gamma)\). By \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there are a fitted set of cardinality at least \(h\), a matrix \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and nonnegative shifts satisfying the candidate covariance equations. Restrict the fitted set and its witnesses to some \(K\subseteq E\) with \(|K|=h\). The declarative candidate premise supplies all four candidate-side bounds for this restricted tuple.

Inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
|H\cap K|\geq|H|+|K|-|E|=2h-m=q.
\tag{17}
\]
Choose \(S\subseteq H\cap K\) with \(|S|=q\), and set \(U=D^{-1}\), \(V=A^{-1}\). The true assumptions and bounds and the candidate equations and bounds put the resulting tuple in \(\mathfrak T\). For \(e\in S\subseteq H\), \(\text{def:honest-region-radius}\) gives
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq r.
\tag{18}
\]
Using the candidate equation, (3), (16), (18), submultiplicativity, and \(\|X\|_{\mathrm F}\leq\sqrt p\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
&=\left\|A(\Sigma_e-\Gamma_e)A^{\mathsf T}\right\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\leq\sqrt p\,L_0^2r
\leq\mu_0/2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
If the tuple belonged to \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\), then (12) would make the maximum in (10) at least \(\mu_0\) when that family is nonempty, contradicting (19); membership is impossible by definition when it is empty. Consequently, with
\[
Q=AD^{-1}=I_p+R,
\qquad
u=\|R\|_{\mathrm F},
\]
we have
\[
u<\rho_0\leq1,
\qquad
K_0\overline M u\leq\frac12.
\tag{20}
\]

\textbf{Local affine-minor inversion.}
Fix \(e_0\in S\) and put \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\). For every \(i<j\), true-side affine separation on \(S\) supplies \(a,b,d\in S\) such that the absolute affine determinant in coordinates \((i,j)\) is at least \(\gamma_0\). Translation by the point indexed by \(e_0\) leaves that determinant unchanged, and direct expansion gives
\[
\det(\widetilde x_a-\widetilde x_b,
\widetilde x_d-\widetilde x_b)
=\det(\widetilde x_a,\widetilde x_d)
+\det(\widetilde x_d,\widetilde x_b)
+\det(\widetilde x_b,\widetilde x_a),
\tag{21}
\]
where \(\widetilde x_e=(x_{ei},x_{ej})\). The triangle inequality therefore supplies two labels \(e,f\in S\) with
\[
|x_{ei}x_{fj}-x_{ej}x_{fi}|\geq\gamma_0/3.
\tag{22}
\]

Let \(E_e=\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\). Subtract the true covariance equation at \(e_0\) from that at \(e\), do the same for the candidate equation, use \(A=QD\), and take the \((i,j)\) off-diagonal entry. The invariant nuisances cancel and yield the exact identity
\[
R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}
=-\sum_{k=1}^pR_{ik}R_{jk}x_{ek}
-[A(E_e-E_{e_0})A^{\mathsf T}]_{ij}.
\tag{23}
\]
The true scale bound and \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\) imply \(|x_{ek}|\leq\overline M\). Equation (18) implies \(\|E_e-E_{e_0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2r\), and the candidate version of (3) gives \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_0\). Since the Euclidean norms of every row of \(R\) are at most \(u\), Cauchy--Schwarz in (23) yields
\[
|R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}|
\leq\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply (24) at the two labels from (22). The determinant of the two-by-two coefficient matrix for \((R_{ij},R_{ji})\) has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0/3\), every coefficient has absolute value at most \(\overline M\), and the explicit two-by-two solution formula therefore gives, for every ordered pair \(i\neq j\),
\[
|R_{ij}|
\leq\frac{6\overline M}{\gamma_0}
\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\right).
\tag{25}
\]
The unit diagonals of \(A\) and \(D\) imply
\[
0=(A-D)_{ii}=(RD)_{ii}
=R_{ii}+\sum_{k\neq i}R_{ik}D_{ki}.
\tag{26}
\]
For each \(i\), the squared norm of the \(i\)-th column of \(D\) is at most \(L_0^2\) by (3). Squaring (25), summing over the \(p(p-1)\) ordered off-diagonal entries, applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (26), and taking square roots gives
\[
u\leq K_0\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\right).
\tag{27}
\]
By (20), the quadratic term on the right of (27) is at most \(u/2\). It follows that
\[
u\leq4K_0L_0^2r.
\tag{28}
\]
Consequently, using (3) and the definition of \(C_0\),
\[
\|A-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|RD\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq uL_0
\leq4K_0L_0^3r
\leq C_0r.
\tag{29}
\]
Because \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) was arbitrary, (29) and \(\text{def:operator-outer-radius}\) prove
\[
\operatorname{outer}_{\mathrm{op}}(\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha,D)
\leq C_0r.
\]
For any \(A_1,A_2\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), the triangle inequality and (29) give
\[
\|A_1-A_2\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|A_1-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|A_2-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2C_0r.
\]
Taking the supremum and invoking \(\text{def:operator-diameter}\) proves the diameter inequality. Finally, along any sequence in the statement, eventually \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\leq r_0\); the two inequalities and \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\to0\) then give both asserted limits. This completes the proof.
