Write \(q=m-2c=h-c\). Since \(\gamma_H\geq\gamma_0>0\), \(\text{def:affine-separation}\) implies
\[
q\geq3.
\tag{52}
\]
Moreover, \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D)\geq0\) and \(\zeta_D\geq\zeta_0>0\) imply \(0<\zeta_0\leq1\).

\textbf{Uniform matrix bounds.}
Let \(G\) denote either a true structural matrix or a candidate structural matrix satisfying the unit-diagonal, cycle-slack, and condition-number bounds in the theorem. In the Leibniz expansion of \(\det G\), the identity permutation contributes one. Every nonidentity permutation decomposes into disjoint directed cycles and fixed points. The absolute value of a nontrivial cycle product equals the absolute value of the corresponding cycle product in \(I_p-G\), and is at most \(1-\zeta_0<1\); fixed-point factors equal one. Therefore every one of the \(p!\) Leibniz terms has absolute value at most one, and
\[
|\det G|\leq p!.
\tag{53}
\]
Let \(\sigma_1(G)=\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) be the largest singular value. Since
\[
\frac{\sigma_1(G)}{\sigma_p(G)}
=\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa,
\]
each other singular value is at least \(\sigma_1(G)/\overline\kappa\). Hence (53) gives
\[
\frac{\sigma_1(G)^p}{\overline\kappa^{p-1}}
\leq\prod_{j=1}^p\sigma_j(G)
=|\det G|
\leq p!,
\]
and therefore
\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_0.
\tag{54}
\]
Because \(G_{ii}=1\), \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq1\), so the condition-number bound also yields
\[
\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\frac{\overline\kappa}{\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{55}
\]

\textbf{A compact global residual gap.}
Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the finite union, over \(H_1,H_2\subseteq E\) with \(|H_1|=|H_2|=h\) and \(S\subseteq H_1\cap H_2\) with \(|S|=q\), of all tuples
\[
\tau=\bigl(H_1,H_2,S,D,U,\Omega,(s_e,\Sigma_e)_{e\in H_1},
A,V,\Psi,(t_e,\Gamma_e)_{e\in H_2}\bigr)
\tag{56}
\]
satisfying the following conditions. The inverse equations are \(DU=UD=I_p\) and \(AV=VA=I_p\). Both \(D\) and \(A\) have unit diagonal, cycle slack at least \(\zeta_0\), and condition number at most \(\overline\kappa\). The matrices \(\Omega,\Psi\) are positive semidefinite, the covariances obey \(\Sigma_e,\Gamma_e\succeq\lambda_0I_p\), and the shifts are nonnegative. The two covariance equations are
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\quad(e\in H_1),
\qquad
A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\quad(e\in H_2).
\tag{57}
\]
For each side, every coordinate pair and every \(q\)-element subset of its fitted set contains a triple whose affine determinant has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0\). Finally, each side satisfies the corresponding scale bound \(\overline M\) in (44) or (49).

Every constraint just listed is closed: inverse and covariance relations are polynomial equalities; cycle slack, positive semidefiniteness, covariance interiority, nonnegativity, condition number with the displayed inverse variables, affine-minor separation over finitely many subsets, and the scale bounds are closed inequalities of continuous functions. Equations (54)--(55) bound \(D,A,U,V\). The scale bounds control the operator norms of both positive-semidefinite nuisances, all covariances, and all diagonal shifts; nonnegativity then bounds every shift coordinate. Thus each fixed \((H_1,H_2,S)\)-piece is closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. By finite-dimensional Heine--Borel compactness it is compact, and the finite union \(\mathfrak T\) is compact.

Define the continuous residual
\[
\mathfrak r(\tau)=
\max_{e\in S}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}.
\tag{58}
\]
With \(K_0\) as in (45), put
\[
\rho_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2K_0\overline M}\right\}>0,
\qquad
\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}
=\{\tau\in\mathfrak T:\|AU-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\geq\rho_0\}.
\tag{59}
\]
The far family is a closed subset of a compact set. If it is empty, set \(\mu_0=1\). Otherwise, the extreme-value theorem supplies a minimizer of \(\mathfrak r\) on \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\); let \(\mu_0\) be that minimum.

We claim that \(\mu_0>0\). This is immediate in the empty case. Otherwise, suppose a minimizing tuple had \(\mathfrak r=0\). On \(S\), equations (57)--(58) then give
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e),
\qquad
A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e),
\qquad e\in S.
\tag{60}
\]
For every \(k<\ell\), the first design's separation constraint supplies a triple in \(S\) with nonzero affine minor, so its \((k,\ell)\)-cloud on \(S\) is not contained in an affine line.

By (52), \(|E\setminus S|=m-q=2c\). Partition \(E\setminus S\) into disjoint sets \(C_1,C_2\) of cardinality \(c\). Define one common covariance family by retaining \(\Sigma_e\) on \(S\), putting \(D^{-1}\Omega D^{-\mathsf T}\) on \(C_1\), and putting \(A^{-1}\Psi A^{-\mathsf T}\) on \(C_2\). These added covariance matrices are positive semidefinite because \(\Omega,\Psi\succeq0\) and the congruence matrices are invertible. The first explanation uses fitted set \(S\cup C_1\), its shifts \(s_e\) on \(S\), and zero shifts on \(C_1\); the second uses \(S\cup C_2\), its shifts \(t_e\) on \(S\), and zero shifts on \(C_2\). Both fitted sets have size \(q+c=h\), their overlap is exactly \(S\), and all shifts are nonnegative. Equation (60), the unit-diagonal and cycle-slack conditions, and the preceding noncollinearity verify every premise of \(\text{lem:overlap-uniqueness}\). Consequently \(A=D\). Since \(U=D^{-1}\), this gives \(AU=I_p\), contradicting (59). Therefore
\[
\mu_0>0.
\tag{61}
\]
The family \(\mathfrak T\), and hence \(\mu_0\), is defined solely from \(p,m,c,\gamma_0,\zeta_0,\lambda_0,\overline\kappa,\overline M\). Choose, non-effectively,
\[
r_0=\frac{\mu_0}{2\sqrt p\,L_0^2}>0.
\tag{62}
\]

\textbf{Entry into the identity neighborhood.}
Fix a covered outcome satisfying the declarative candidate bounds, and take \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\). By \(\text{def:confidence-union}\) and \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there are a full region selection, a set \(K'\subseteq E\) with \(|K'|\geq h\), a nuisance \(\Psi\succeq0\), and nonnegative shifts such that the candidate covariance equations hold on \(K'\). Choose an \(h\)-element subset \(K\subseteq K'\) and restrict the witnesses to \(K\). The declarative hypothesis gives (47)--(49) for this tuple. Inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
|H\cap K|\geq|H|+|K|-m=2h-m=q;
\]
choose \(S\subseteq H\cap K\) with \(|S|=q\). On the coverage event, \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\) and the definition of \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\) imply
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq r_{n,\alpha,H},
\qquad e\in S.
\tag{63}
\]
The true equations and bounds (44), together with the candidate equations and bounds, place these objects in \(\mathfrak T\). Using (54), (57), (63), submultiplicativity, and \(\|X\|_{\mathrm F}\leq\sqrt p\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), for every \(e\in S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
&=\left\|A(\Sigma_e-\Gamma_e)A^{\mathsf T}\right\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\leq\sqrt p\,L_0^2r_{n,\alpha,H}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{64}
\]
If \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}=\varnothing\), the inequality \(\|AD^{-1}-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\geq\rho_0\) is impossible for this tuple. If \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\neq\varnothing\) and that inequality held, then (58) and the definition of \(\mu_0\) would make the maximum of the left side of (64) at least \(\mu_0\), whereas (62) and \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\leq r_0\) bound it by \(\mu_0/2\), a contradiction. Hence, with
\[
Q=AD^{-1}=I_p+R,
\qquad u=\|R\|_{\mathrm F},
\]
one has
\[
u<\rho_0\leq1,
\qquad K_0\overline M u<\frac12.
\tag{65}
\]

\textbf{Local affine-minor inversion.}
Fix \(e_0\in S\) and put \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\). For each \(i<j\), the true separation bound supplies a triple in \(S\) whose affine determinant has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0\). Expanding this determinant into the three pairwise cross-products based at \(e_0\) and applying the triangle inequality gives \(e,f\in S\) such that
\[
|x_{ei}x_{fj}-x_{ej}x_{fi}|\geq\gamma_0/3.
\tag{66}
\]
Let \(E_e=\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\). Subtract the candidate covariance equation at \(e_0\) from the equation at \(e\), and subtract the corresponding true equations. The invariant nuisances cancel. Taking the \((i,j)\) off-diagonal entry after substituting \(Q=I_p+R\) yields
\[
R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}
=-\sum_{k=1}^pR_{ik}R_{jk}x_{ek}
-[A(E_e-E_{e_0})A^{\mathsf T}]_{ij}.
\tag{67}
\]
The true scale bound and shift nonnegativity give \(|x_{ek}|\leq\overline M\). Equations (54) and (63) give
\[
\|E_e-E_{e_0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2r_{n,\alpha,H},
\qquad \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_0.
\]
Thus Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\sum_k|R_{ik}R_{jk}|\leq u^2\) imply
\[
|R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}|
\leq\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r_{n,\alpha,H}.
\tag{68}
\]
Apply (68) at the two environments in (66) and solve the resulting two-by-two linear system for \(R_{ij}\) and \(R_{ji}\). Every coefficient has absolute value at most \(\overline M\), and the determinant has absolute value at least \(\gamma_0/3\), so, for every ordered pair \(i\neq j\),
\[
|R_{ij}|
\leq\frac{6\overline M}{\gamma_0}
\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r_{n,\alpha,H}\right).
\tag{69}
\]
The unit diagonals of \(A\) and \(D\) give
\[
0=(A-D)_{ii}=(RD)_{ii}
=R_{ii}+\sum_{k\neq i}R_{ik}D_{ki}.
\tag{70}
\]
The squared norm of every column of \(D\) is at most \(L_0^2\). Summing the squared off-diagonal bounds in (69), applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (70), and taking square roots gives, by the definition of \(K_0\),
\[
u\leq K_0\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r_{n,\alpha,H}\right).
\tag{71}
\]
If \(u=0\), the next bound is immediate. If \(u>0\), (65) makes the quadratic term on the right strictly smaller than \(u/2\). In either case, (71) implies
\[
u\leq4K_0L_0^2r_{n,\alpha,H}.
\tag{72}
\]
It follows that
\[
\|A-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|RD\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq uL_0
\leq4K_0L_0^3r_{n,\alpha,H}
\leq C_0r_{n,\alpha,H}.
\tag{73}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) proves the outer-radius inequality in (51). On the coverage event, \(D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) by \(\text{thm:confidence-union-coverage}\), so the union is nonempty. For \(A_1,A_2\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), the triangle inequality and (73) give
\[
\|A_1-A_2\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|A_1-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|A_2-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2C_0r_{n,\alpha,H},
\]
which proves the diameter inequality. The constants and the compact family used to choose \(r_0\) depend only on the displayed fixed dimensions and margins. If \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\to0\) along covered outcomes satisfying the same declarative bounds, then eventually \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\leq r_0\), and both radii converge to zero by (51).
