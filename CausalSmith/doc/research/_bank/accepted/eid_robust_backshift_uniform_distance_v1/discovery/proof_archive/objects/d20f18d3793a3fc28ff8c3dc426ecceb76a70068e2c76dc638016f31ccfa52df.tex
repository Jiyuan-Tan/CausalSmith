Take
\[
\Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All three matrices are rational and positive definite because their diagonal entries are positive. They arise with \(D=I_2\), \(\Omega=2I_2\), and shifts \(s_1=(0,0)\), \(s_2=(1,0)\), \(s_3=(0,1)\). Direct expansion gives
\[
\gamma_H=
\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\right|=1.
\]

Let \(A\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_3)\). Since \(h=m=3\), \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) forces the compatible subset to be all three environments. Unit diagonal gives
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\y&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtracting the transformed covariance equation at environment one from those at environments two and three cancels the common invariant matrix. The differences must be diagonal, while direct multiplication gives
\[
A(\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\y&y^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A(\Sigma_3-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
=\begin{pmatrix}x^2&x\\x&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their off-diagonal entries imply \(y=0\) and \(x=0\). Thus \(A=I_2\). Conversely, \(I_2\) is compatible with invariant matrix \(2I_2\) and the displayed nonnegative shifts, so
\[
\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_3)=\{I_2\}.
\]

For each nonzero \(q\in\mathbb Q\), set
\[
A_q=\begin{pmatrix}1&q\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Psi_q=2\begin{pmatrix}1+q^2&q\\q&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Gamma_{3,q}=\begin{pmatrix}2+q^2&-q\\-q&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix \(A_q\) is invertible and has unit diagonal. The two-cycle product of \(I_2-A_q\) is zero, so \(A_q\in\mathcal D\). For every nonzero vector \(v\),
\[
v^{\mathsf T}\Psi_qv=2\|A_q^{\mathsf T}v\|_2^2>0,
\]
because \(A_q\) is invertible. If \(v=(u,w)^{\mathsf T}\neq0\), then
\[
v^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_{3,q}v
=2u^2+(qu-w)^2+2w^2>0.
\]
Thus both \(\Psi_q\) and \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) are positive definite. Direct multiplication yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_q\Sigma_1A_q^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_q,\\
A_q\Sigma_2A_q^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\\
A_q\Gamma_{3,q}A_q^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore \(A_q\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,q})\). Since \(q\neq0\), \(A_q\neq I_2\). The matrices \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) are pairwise distinct because their off-diagonal entry is \(-q\).

Suppose, for contradiction, that a deterministic finite-membership-query procedure computes the exact union for every arbitrary nonempty region family. Run it on the singleton regions
\[
\mathcal C^0_1=\{\Sigma_1\},\qquad
\mathcal C^0_2=\{\Sigma_2\},\qquad
\mathcal C^0_3=\{\Sigma_3\}.
\]
Its required output is \(\{I_2\}\). Only finitely many matrices are queried from the third oracle. Since the family \((\Gamma_{3,q}:q\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\})\) is infinite and pairwise distinct, choose \(q\neq0\) for which \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) was not queried. Replace only the third region by
\[
\mathcal C^1_3=\{\Sigma_3,\Gamma_{3,q}\}.
\]
Induction over the query number shows that the two runs have identical transcripts. Initially their internal states agree. If their transcripts agree before a query, determinism selects the same region and matrix. Answers from the first two oracles agree because those regions are unchanged. Answers from the third agree along the original transcript because membership differs only at the unqueried point \(\Gamma_{3,q}\). Hence the next internal states also agree. The original run terminates after finitely many queries, so the modified run terminates with the same output \(\{I_2\}\).

But the modified exact union contains \(A_q\), because
\[
A_q\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,q}),
\]
whereas \(A_q\notin\{I_2\}\). This contradiction proves the finite-membership-query obstruction. The witness consists entirely of rational positive-definite matrices and has \(\gamma_H=1\), so the obstruction is about input representation rather than vanishing affine separation.
