Fix \(k<\ell\), and write
\[
 P_e=(s_{ek},s_{e\ell})=(Z_{ek}a_{ek},Z_{e\ell}a_{e\ell}),
 \qquad e\in H, \tag{22}
\]
using \(\text{ass:support-factorization}\).  The support types \(00,10,01,11\) put \(P_e\), respectively, at the origin, on the positive horizontal axis, on the positive vertical axis, and in the open positive quadrant.

The horizontal and vertical axes have respective indexed occupancies
\[
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{01}^{k\ell}. \tag{23}
\]
If \(n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\), the line through the origin and any one \(11\) point has occupancy at least \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+1\).  Any two indexed points lie on an affine line, including when the two coordinates coincide.  Thus
\[
 M_{k\ell}\ge
 \max\!\left\{
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{01}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+\mathbf 1\{n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\},
 \min(2,h)
 \right\}. \tag{24}
\]

We prove the reverse inequality outside a null set.  Write \(P_r=(x_r,y_r)\).  For two nonzero-support points, collinearity with the origin is the polynomial equation
\[
 x_ey_f-x_fy_e=0. \tag{25}
\]
Except when both points have type \(10\), or both have type \(01\), the polynomial in (25) is not identically zero on the active coordinates: mixed axis types give a nonzero product, an axis type paired with type \(11\) gives a nonzero product, and two type \(11\) points give two distinct monomials.  For three nonzero-support points, collinearity is
\[
 \det\!\begin{pmatrix}
 1&x_e&y_e\\
 1&x_f&y_f\\
 1&x_g&y_g
 \end{pmatrix}=0. \tag{26}
\]
Unless all three support types are \(10\), or all three are \(01\), this determinant polynomial is not identically zero.  Indeed, two horizontal points and a vertical or interior point can be chosen noncollinear, as can the coordinate-swapped configuration; the remaining mixed cases admit one point off the line through the other two; and three interior points may be chosen as \((1,1),(1,2),(2,1)\).  Equality of two active coordinates belonging to distinct type \(10\) points, or to distinct type \(01\) points, is likewise the zero set of a nonzero linear polynomial.

For completeness, the zero set of a nonzero real polynomial has Lebesgue measure zero.  This follows by induction on its number of variables: the one-variable assertion follows from finiteness of roots; in the induction step, expand in the last variable, discard the null set where every coefficient vanishes by the induction hypothesis, and use finitely many roots on each remaining section.  Integrating these null sections gives a null set.  There are finitely many coordinate pairs and indexed pairs and triples.  Joint absolute continuity in \(\text{ass:generic-active-amplitudes}\) therefore implies that, simultaneously for every \(k<\ell\), none of the accidental equations above holds almost surely.

On this probability-one event, a line through the origin that contains a type \(10\) point is the horizontal axis and has the first occupancy in (23); the analogous statement for type \(01\) gives the second.  A line through the origin containing neither axis type has at most one type \(11\) point by (25), so its occupancy is at most \(n_{00}^{k\ell}+\mathbf 1\{n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\}\).  A line not through the origin contains no type \(00\) point.  If it contained three indices, (26) would force all three to have type \(10\) or all three to have type \(01\), but their line would then be an axis through the origin.  Hence its occupancy is at most \(\min(2,h)\).  Together with (24), this proves
\[
 M_{k\ell}
 =\max\!\left\{
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{10}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+n_{01}^{k\ell},
 n_{00}^{k\ell}+\mathbf 1\{n_{11}^{k\ell}>0\},
 \min(2,h)
 \right\}
 =M_{k\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}} \tag{27}
\]
simultaneously for all \(k<\ell\), almost surely.

By \(\text{def:environment-distance}\),
\[
 \delta_H>c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M_{k\ell}<h-c\quad\text{for every }k<\ell. \tag{28}
\]
Substituting (27) into (28) gives the four-term criterion.  If it holds, the positive-semidefinite sufficiency part of \(\text{thm:sharp-replacement-radius}\) proves uniqueness for every admissible published-class instance and every replacement.  If it fails, then \(\delta_H\le c\); choosing an invariant covariance in \(\mathbb S_{++}^p\), the pointwise converse in that theorem supplies a legal ambiguity.  Thus the criterion is necessary and sufficient for a uniform guarantee over the published positive-semidefinite class, without asserting failure for each singular boundary instance.

The term \(\min(2,h)\) makes the strict inequality in (28) possible only if \(h-c\ge3\).  Since \(h=m-c\),
\[
 m\ge2c+3. \tag{29}
\]
This lower bound is attained.  If \(m=2c+3\), take the all-active pattern \(Z_{ek}=1\).  Then, for every \(k<\ell\),
\[
 n_{11}^{k\ell}=h,\qquad
 n_{00}^{k\ell}=n_{10}^{k\ell}=n_{01}^{k\ell}=0,\qquad
 M_{k\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}=2<h-c=3. \tag{30}
\]
Hence the all-active pattern gives uniform robust identification almost surely at the smallest possible environment count.
