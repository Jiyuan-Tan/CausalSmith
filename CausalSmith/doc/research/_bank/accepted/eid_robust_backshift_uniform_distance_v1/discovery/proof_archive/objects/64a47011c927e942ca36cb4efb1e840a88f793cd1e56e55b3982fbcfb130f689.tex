\textbf{Coverage.}
Fix \(n\geq 1\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and a sample outcome in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\).  For \(e\in H\), set \(\Gamma_e=\Sigma_e\).  For each \(e\in E\setminus H\), choose \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\); this finite choice is possible because every region is nonempty by the declared type of \(\mathcal C^{(n)}_\alpha\).  Every chosen \(\Gamma_e\) is positive definite because every region is a subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  In \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), take
\[
 A=D,\qquad K=H,\qquad \Psi=\Omega,\qquad t_e=s_e\quad(e\in H).
\]
The equality \(|K|=h\) follows from the definition of \(H\).  Assumption \(\text{ass:backshift-normalization}\) gives \(D\in\mathcal D\).  Assumption \(\text{ass:invariant-noise-interior}\) gives \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\subseteq\mathbb S_+^p\), and assumption \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\) gives \(s_e\in[0,\infty)^p\).  Finally, for every \(e\in K=H\), membership in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\) and assumption \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\) give
\[
 \Gamma_e=\Sigma_e
 =D^{-1}\bigl(\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\bigr)D^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus every conjunct in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) holds, so \(D\in\mathcal R_c(\Gamma)\).  Since this \(\Gamma\) is one of the families over which \(\text{def:confidence-union}\) takes its union,
\[
 G_{n,\alpha,H}\subseteq\{D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\}.
\]
Monotonicity of probability now yields
\[
 \Pr\{D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\}
 \geq \Pr(G_{n,\alpha,H})\geq 1-\alpha.
\]

\textbf{Exact rational semialgebraic representation.}
Suppose that every entry of every \(\Sigma_e\) is rational.  It suffices to enumerate sets \(K\subseteq E\) of cardinality exactly \(h\): a witness on a set of size at least \(h\) restricts to any \(h\)-element subset, and a witness on an \(h\)-element set already satisfies \(\text{def:compatible-set}\).  This is the finite subset reduction named in \(\text{def:subset-consensus}\).

Fix one such \(K\).  Introduce real variables \(A\in\mathbb R^{p\times p}\), a symmetric real matrix \(\Psi\), and \(t_e\in\mathbb R^p\) for \(e\in K\).  For each directed simple cycle \(C\) of length at least two, let \(\pi_C(A)\) be the product of the corresponding entries of \(I_p-A\).  Define the ordered-field formula
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_K(A,\Psi,t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 &A_{ii}=1 &&(1\leq i\leq p),\\
 &(\det A)^2>0,\\
 &1-\pi_C(A)>0\ \text{ and }\ 1+\pi_C(A)>0
   &&(C\text{ a directed simple cycle}),\\
 &\Psi=\Psi^{\mathsf T},\qquad
   v^{\mathsf T}\Psi v\geq0 &&(v\in\mathbb R^p),\\
 &t_{ej}\geq0 &&(e\in K,\ 1\leq j\leq p),\\
 &A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)
   &&(e\in K).
\end{aligned}
\]
The line quantified by \(v\) means \(\forall v\in\mathbb R^p\), so it is itself a first-order clause.  There are finitely many directed simple cycles.  By the definition of \(\operatorname{CP}\), the two strict inequalities for every \(C\) are equivalent to \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)<1\).  The determinant inequality is equivalent to invertibility.  The universally quantified quadratic-form inequalities are equivalent to \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\).  All displayed atomic predicates are polynomial sign conditions with rational coefficients because the entries of the fixed matrices \(\Sigma_e\) are rational.

If \(F_K(A,\Psi,t)\) holds, invertibility permits multiplication of its last equality by \(A^{-1}\) and \(A^{-\mathsf T}\), giving
\[
 \Sigma_e=A^{-1}\bigl(\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\bigr)A^{-\mathsf T}
 \quad(e\in K).
\]
Thus \(A\) is compatible.  Conversely, multiplying each covariance equality in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) by \(A\) and \(A^{\mathsf T}\) gives \(F_K\).  Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal R_c(\Sigma)
 =\bigcup_{\substack{K\subseteq E\\ |K|=h}}
   \left\{A:\exists\Psi\,\exists(t_e:e\in K)\ F_K(A,\Psi,t)\right\}.
\]
The union is finite.  Applying \(\text{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) to this formula produces an effectively computable quantifier-free Boolean combination \(\varphi_\Sigma(A)\) of rational-polynomial sign conditions whose solution set is exactly \(\mathcal R_c(\Sigma)\).

For completeness, define the polynomial
\[
 d(A,A')=\sum_{i=1}^p\sum_{j=1}^p(A_{ij}-A'_{ij})^2.
\]
The three output assertions in the theorem are represented exactly by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Empty}&:\quad \neg\exists A\,\varphi_\Sigma(A),\\
 \mathsf{Singleton}&:\quad
   \exists A\left[\varphi_\Sigma(A)\wedge
   \forall A'\bigl(\varphi_\Sigma(A')\Rightarrow d(A,A')=0\bigr)\right],\\
 \mathsf{Multiple}&:\quad
   \exists A\,\exists A'\left[
   \varphi_\Sigma(A)\wedge\varphi_\Sigma(A')\wedge d(A,A')>0\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Each is again a first-order ordered-field formula with rational-polynomial atoms.  A further application of \(\text{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) yields a finite quantifier-free rational-polynomial sign certificate deciding each assertion.  The singleton formula includes existence and therefore does not confuse the empty set with a set having at most one point.

For every \(e<f<g\) in \(K\) and \(k<\ell\), define the fitted-shift minor
\[
 \Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)=
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 1&t_{ek}&t_{e\ell}\\
 1&t_{fk}&t_{f\ell}\\
 1&t_{gk}&t_{g\ell}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is a polynomial.  Hence the atoms \(\Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)=0\), \(\Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)>0\), and \(-\Delta_{efg}^{k\ell}(t)>0\) may be included in the finite sign family before elimination; no division is used.  For a set of at least three fitted profiles, some such determinant is nonzero exactly when the profiles are not contained in a single affine line: a nonzero determinant exhibits three affinely independent points, whereas if every such determinant vanishes then the affine span has dimension at most one.  Thus these atoms certify affine separation.

They cannot certify feasibility alone.  For example, the profiles \((0,0)\), \((1,0)\), and \((0,1)\) have determinant one independently of whether a proposed structural matrix has unit diagonal or a proposed invariant matrix is positive semidefinite.  The covariance equations, the quadratic-form inequalities for \(\Psi\), nonnegativity of \(t\), the determinant inequality, the unit-diagonal equations, and every cycle-product inequality in \(F_K\) remain separate necessary conjuncts.

The representation also proves the normalization-chart assertion.  Let \(C\) be a raw diagonalizer in a row-normalization chart, put \(q_i=C_{ii}\), and impose \(q_i^2>0\) for every \(i\).  Introduce the normalized matrix \(A\) by the polynomial relations
\[
 q_iA_{ij}=C_{ij},\qquad q_i=C_{ii},\qquad q_i^2>0.
\]
Then \(A_{ii}=1\) and
\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(q_1,\ldots,q_p)^{-1}C.
\]
Invertibility is encoded by \((\det C)^2>0\).  If a cycle product in this chart is \(N_C(C)/Q_C(q)\), where pivot nonvanishing gives \(Q_C(q)\neq0\), then
\[
 \left|\frac{N_C(C)}{Q_C(q)}\right|<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_C(q)^2-N_C(C)^2>0.
\]
Covariance equalities are cleared by multiplication by the relevant nonzero pivot products, and inequalities are cleared with positive squared denominators.  Therefore pivot nonvanishing together with the polynomial normalization, invertibility, covariance, positivity, nonnegativity, and cycle-product conditions is equivalent to the normalized-chart instance of \(F_K\).  Omitting any of these conjuncts would enlarge the projected set, so a certificate obtained after such an omission would not be a sound chart certificate.

\textbf{Finite-membership-query obstruction.}
Consider
\[
 p=2,\qquad m=h=3,\qquad c=0,\qquad H=E=\{1,2,3\},
\]
and take
\[
 D=I_2,\qquad \Omega=2I_2,\qquad
 s_1=(0,0),\quad s_2=(1,0),\quad s_3=(0,1).
\]
The matrix \(D\) satisfies \(\text{ass:backshift-normalization}\), the matrix \(\Omega\) satisfies \(\text{ass:invariant-noise-interior}\), and the shifts satisfy \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\).  Assumption \(\text{ass:honest-covariance-model}\) therefore gives the rational positive-definite matrices
\[
 \Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
There is one coordinate pair and one three-element subset in \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), and direct expansion gives
\[
 \gamma_H=
 \left|\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\right|=1.
\]

We first compute the baseline compatibility set.  Let \(A\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma)\).  Since \(h=m=3\), the compatible subset must be all of \(E\).  Unit diagonal writes
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\y&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtracting the transformed covariance equation for environment one from those for environments two and three cancels the common \(\Psi\), and the resulting matrices must be diagonal.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 A(\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
 =A\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}A^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\y&y^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its off-diagonal entry is zero, so \(y=0\).  Similarly,
\[
 A(\Sigma_3-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
 =A\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}A^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}x^2&x\\x&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so \(x=0\).  Hence every compatible \(A\) is \(I_2\).  Conversely, \(I_2\) is compatible with \(\Psi=2I_2\) and the displayed nonnegative shifts.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_3)=\{I_2\}.
\]

For any nonzero rational number \(q\), define
\[
 A_q=\begin{pmatrix}1&q\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Psi_q=A_q\Sigma_1A_q^{\mathsf T}
 =2\begin{pmatrix}1+q^2&q\\q&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
 \Gamma_{3,q}
 =A_q^{-1}\bigl(\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\bigr)A_q^{-\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}2+q^2&-q\\-q&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix \(A_q\) is invertible and has unit diagonal.  Moreover, \(I_2-A_q\) has zero product around its only directed two-cycle, so \(A_q\in\mathcal D\).  The matrix \(\Psi_q\) is positive definite because it is the congruence of \(2I_2\) by the invertible matrix \(A_q\).  The matrix \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) is positive definite by Sylvester's criterion, since its leading diagonal entry is positive and
\[
 \det(\Gamma_{3,q})=3(2+q^2)-q^2=6+2q^2>0.
\]
All entries of these matrices are rational.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_q\Sigma_1A_q^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_q,\\
 A_q\Sigma_2A_q^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\\
 A_q\Gamma_{3,q}A_q^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_q+\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(A_q\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,q})\), with a positive-definite invariant nuisance and nonnegative diagonal shifts, while \(A_q\neq I_2\).

Suppose for contradiction that a deterministic procedure returns the exact confidence union for every admissible region family after making only finitely many membership queries to the regions.  Run it with the membership oracles for
\[
 \mathcal C^0_1=\{\Sigma_1\},\qquad
 \mathcal C^0_2=\{\Sigma_2\},\qquad
 \mathcal C^0_3=\{\Sigma_3\}.
\]
By the baseline computation, its required output is
\[
 \mathcal U^0=\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_3)=\{I_2\}.
\]
Only finitely many matrices are queried of the third oracle during this run.  The matrices \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) are pairwise distinct as \(q\) ranges over the infinitely many nonzero rational numbers because their off-diagonal entry is \(-q\).  Hence one may choose a nonzero rational \(q\) such that \(\Gamma_{3,q}\) was not queried.  Define a second region family by
\[
 \mathcal C^1_1=\mathcal C^0_1,\qquad
 \mathcal C^1_2=\mathcal C^0_2,\qquad
 \mathcal C^1_3=\{\Sigma_3,\Gamma_{3,q}\}.
\]
Every region is a nonempty subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^2\).  We prove by induction on the query number that the procedure has the same transcript on \(\mathcal C^0\) and \(\mathcal C^1\).  Before the first query the internal states agree.  If the transcripts agree through a given query, determinism makes the next queried region and matrix the same.  Queries to the first two regions receive the same answer because those regions are unchanged.  A query to the third region also receives the same answer: along the baseline transcript the only point at which membership differs is \(\Gamma_{3,q}\), and that point was not queried.  The internal states therefore remain equal.  Since the baseline run terminates after finitely many queries, the second run follows the same finite transcript, terminates at the same time, and returns the same set.

The exact unions are nevertheless different, because
\[
 A_q\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,q})
 \subseteq\mathcal U^1,
 \qquad
 A_q\notin\mathcal U^0=\{I_2\}.
\]
This contradiction proves that no deterministic finite-membership-query procedure can compute the exact confidence union for the frozen arbitrary-set region class.  The counterexample uses rational positive-definite matrices and has \(\gamma_H=1\).  Thus even a rule required to return the full exact union away from \(\gamma_H=0\) and to abstain only at that boundary fails on this separated instance.  An additional effective representation of the regions is necessary for an exact computable union or such a full-output-or-abstain rule.

Finally, the structural causal target was fixed independently as \(B=I_p-D\).  The first part proves coverage for that target through compatibility of \(D\), while \(\varphi_\Sigma\) is an observed-law compatibility functional.  Neither object is defined to equal the other; the preceding arguments prove precisely the coverage and recovery-set relations asserted in the theorem.
