Put \(q=m-2c=h-c\). By \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), if \(q<3\), then \(\gamma_H=0\), contradicting \(\gamma_H\geq\gamma_0>0\). Hence
\[
q\geq3.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Uniform structural bounds.}
Let \(G\) be a real invertible matrix with unit diagonal, \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-G)\leq1\), and
\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{2}
\]
In the Leibniz expansion of \(\det G\), the identity-permutation term has absolute value one. Every other permutation is a disjoint union of fixed points and directed simple cycles of length at least two. A fixed point contributes \(G_{ii}=1\); up to sign, the product contributed by each nontrivial cycle is a cycle product in \(I_p-G\), and therefore has absolute value at most one. Thus every Leibniz term has absolute value at most one, so
\[
|\det G|\leq p!.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing the singular values in decreasing order as \(\sigma_1(G),\ldots,\sigma_p(G)\), (2) gives
\[
\sigma_j(G)\geq\sigma_p(G)
\geq\frac{\sigma_1(G)}{\overline\kappa}
\qquad(2\leq j\leq p).
\]
Together with (3), this yields
\[
\frac{\sigma_1(G)^p}{\overline\kappa^{p-1}}
\leq\prod_{j=1}^p\sigma_j(G)
=|\det G|
\leq p!,
\]
and consequently
\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\bigl(p!\,\overline\kappa^{p-1}\bigr)^{1/p}
=L_0.
\tag{4}
\]
Since a unit diagonal entry has absolute value one, \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq1\); (2) therefore also gives
\[
\|G^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{5}
\]
The true matrix \(D\) satisfies the premises above because \(\zeta_D\geq\zeta_0>0\), while every actual feasible candidate \(A\in\mathcal D\) satisfies \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)<1\) by \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\). The same bounds will also apply to candidate limit points for which only \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)\leq1\) is retained.

\textbf{Compact one-sided normalization closure.}
Define
\[
\rho_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2K_0\overline M}\right\}>0.
\tag{6}
\]
For every \(H_1\subseteq E\) with \(|H_1|=h\) and every \(S\subseteq H_1\) with \(|S|=q\), consider tuples
\[
\tau=\bigl(D,U,\Omega,(s_e,\Sigma_e)_{e\in H_1},
A,V,\Psi,(t_e,\Gamma_e)_{e\in S}\bigr)
\tag{7}
\]
satisfying the following conditions:
\[
DU=UD=I_p,
\qquad
AV=VA=I_p;
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\operatorname{diag}(D)=\operatorname{diag}(A)=\mathbf 1,
\qquad
\operatorname{CP}(I_p-D)\leq1-\zeta_0,
\qquad
\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)\leq1;
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa,
\qquad
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa;
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\Omega,\Sigma_e\in\mathbb S_+^p,\quad
s_e\in[0,\infty)^p\quad(e\in H_1),
\]
\[
\Psi,\Gamma_e\in\mathbb S_+^p,\quad
t_e\in[0,\infty)^p\quad(e\in S);
\tag{11}
\]
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e)
\quad(e\in H_1),
\qquad
A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)
\quad(e\in S);
\tag{12}
\]
the true-side affine-separation quantity from \(\text{def:affine-separation}\), with \(H_1\) in place of \(H\), is at least \(\gamma_0\), and
\[
1+\|\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\max_{e\in H_1}\|\operatorname{diag}(s_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline M;
\tag{13}
\]
finally,
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1
\qquad(e\in S).
\tag{14}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the finite disjoint union of these families, retaining \((H_1,S)\) as a discrete index.

Equations (4)--(5), applied using (9)--(10), bound \(D,U,A,V\). Equation (13) gives \(\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline M-1\), so (14) implies
\[
\|\Gamma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline M
\qquad(e\in S).
\tag{15}
\]
Using (4), (12), and (15),
\[
\|\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|A\Gamma_eA^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0^2\overline M.
\tag{16}
\]
The two summands on the left of (16) are positive semidefinite. If \(P,Q\succeq0\), then for each unit vector \(v\),
\[
0\leq v^{\mathsf T}Pv\leq v^{\mathsf T}(P+Q)v,
\]
so the Rayleigh-quotient characterization of the operator norm gives \(\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|P+Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), and similarly for \(Q\). Hence
\[
\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0^2\overline M,
\qquad
\|\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0^2\overline M
\qquad(e\in S).
\tag{17}
\]
Thus every variable in (7) is bounded. These candidate covariance, nuisance, and shift bounds were derived from the true scale bound, the local radius-one restriction, and candidate conditioning; they were not assumed.

For fixed \((H_1,S)\), the family is closed: (8) and (12) are polynomial equalities; the positive-semidefinite cones and nonnegative orthants in (11) are closed; every norm inequality is closed; \(\operatorname{CP}\) is the maximum of finitely many continuous absolute cycle-product functions; and the affine-separation quantity is a finite minimum of finite maxima of continuous absolute determinants. By \(\text{lem:euclidean-heine-borel}\), each fixed-index family is compact. The finite disjoint union \(\mathfrak T\) is therefore compact.

Define the continuous residual
\[
\mathfrak r(\tau)=
\max_{e\in S}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
\tag{18}
\]
and the closed subfamily
\[
\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}
=\{\tau\in\mathfrak T:\|AU-I_p\|_{\mathrm F}\geq\rho_0\}.
\tag{19}
\]
It is compact. If it is empty, set \(\mu_0=1\). Otherwise, \(\text{lem:compact-extreme-value}\) gives an attaining minimum
\[
\mu_0=\min_{\tau\in\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}}\mathfrak r(\tau).
\tag{20}
\]

We next prove \(\mu_0>0\). Suppose, to the contrary in the nonempty case, that an attaining tuple has \(\mathfrak r(\tau)=0\). Then for every \(e\in S\),
\[
D\Sigma_eD^{\mathsf T}=\Omega+\operatorname{diag}(s_e),
\qquad
A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}=\Psi+\operatorname{diag}(t_e).
\tag{21}
\]
Fix \(e_0\in S\), which is possible by (1), and set
\[
Q=AD^{-1}=AU,
\qquad
x_e=s_e-s_{e_0},
\qquad
y_e=t_e-t_{e_0}.
\]
Subtracting (21) at \(e_0\) from (21) at \(e\) cancels both invariant nuisances and gives
\[
Q\operatorname{diag}(x_e)Q^{\mathsf T}
=\operatorname{diag}(y_e)
\qquad(e\in S).
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(W=\operatorname{span}\{x_e:e\in S\}\). The affine-separation condition in (13), applied to this particular \(q\)-element set \(S\), says that for each \(k<\ell\) some triple has a nonzero affine determinant in coordinates \((k,\ell)\). Therefore the projection of \(W\) onto coordinates \((k,\ell)\) is all of \(\mathbb R^2\): otherwise a nonzero linear functional annihilating that projection would place the entire \((k,\ell)\)-cloud on an affine line through the point indexed by \(e_0\), making every such determinant zero.

Choose a basis \(w^1,\ldots,w^d\) of \(W\), and define the coordinate profiles
\[
u_k=(w^1_k,\ldots,w^d_k)^{\mathsf T}
\qquad(1\leq k\leq p).
\]
The preceding projection property says that \(u_k,u_\ell\) are linearly independent whenever \(k\ne\ell\); in particular, every \(u_k\ne0\). Choose \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\) outside the finitely many proper hyperplanes \(\{\beta:\beta^{\mathsf T}u_k=0\}\). Then choose \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^d\) outside the finitely many proper hyperplanes
\[
\left\{\alpha:
\alpha^{\mathsf T}\bigl((\beta^{\mathsf T}u_\ell)u_k
-(\beta^{\mathsf T}u_k)u_\ell\bigr)=0\right\},
\qquad k<\ell.
\tag{23}
\]
Each displayed normal is nonzero, since otherwise the nonzero scalars \(\beta^{\mathsf T}u_k\) and \(\beta^{\mathsf T}u_\ell\) would make \(u_k,u_\ell\) proportional. A finite union of proper hyperplanes cannot cover \(\mathbb R^d\): the product of their nonzero defining linear forms is a nonzero polynomial, and induction on \(d\), using the one-variable root bound, shows that a nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish everywhere. Thus both choices exist.

Set
\[
b=\sum_{j=1}^d\beta_jw^j,
\qquad
a=\sum_{j=1}^d\alpha_jw^j,
\qquad
\rho_k=\frac{a_k}{b_k}.
\]
The choice of \(\beta\) gives \(b_k\ne0\), and (23) gives pairwise distinct \(\rho_k\). By linearity of (22), both
\[
G=Q\operatorname{diag}(a)Q^{\mathsf T}
\quad\text{and}\quad
K=Q\operatorname{diag}(b)Q^{\mathsf T}
\]
are diagonal. Moreover, \(K\) is invertible. Hence
\[
GK^{-1}
=Q\operatorname{diag}(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_p)Q^{-1}
\tag{24}
\]
is diagonal. Its eigenvalues are pairwise distinct, so its eigenspaces are the coordinate axes. The columns of \(Q\), which are the corresponding eigenvectors on the right of (24), are therefore supported on coordinate axes. Since \(Q\) is invertible, it is monomial: there are a permutation \(\pi\) and nonzero numbers \(q_i\) such that
\[
Q_{i,\pi(i)}=q_i,
\qquad
Q_{ij}=0\quad(j\ne\pi(i)).
\tag{25}
\]

It remains to eliminate the permutation using the one-sided normalization closure. From \(A=QD\), (25), and the unit diagonals of \(A,D\),
\[
1=A_{ii}=q_iD_{\pi(i),i}.
\tag{26}
\]
If \(\pi\) had a nontrivial cycle \(i_1,\ldots,i_r\), with \(r\geq2\) and \(\pi(i_t)=i_{t+1}\) cyclically, then (26) would imply
\[
\prod_{t=1}^r|D_{i_{t+1},i_t}|
=\prod_{t=1}^r|q_{i_t}|^{-1}.
\tag{27}
\]
The left side of (27) is the absolute product on a directed simple cycle of \(I_p-D\), so (9) and \(\zeta_0>0\) give
\[
\prod_{t=1}^r|q_{i_t}|^{-1}<1.
\tag{28}
\]
Also,
\[
A_{i_t,i_{t+1}}
=q_{i_t}D_{i_{t+1},i_{t+1}}
=q_{i_t},
\]
so these entries form the reverse directed simple cycle of \(I_p-A\), up to signs. The weak candidate bound in (9) gives
\[
\prod_{t=1}^r|q_{i_t}|\leq1.
\tag{29}
\]
Multiplying (28) and (29) gives a strict upper bound below one, whereas the two reciprocal products multiply to one, a contradiction. Thus \(\pi\) is the identity. Equation (26) and \(D_{ii}=1\) then give \(q_i=1\) for every \(i\), whence \(Q=I_p\) and \(A=D\). This contradicts (19). Therefore
\[
\mu_0>0.
\tag{30}
\]
Set, non-effectively,
\[
r_0=\min\left\{1,\frac{\mu_0}{2\sqrt p\,L_0^2}\right\}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
All continuous constraints defining \(\mathfrak T\), as well as \(\rho_0\), use only \(p,m,c,\gamma_0,\zeta_0,\overline\kappa,\overline M\). Thus \(r_0\) has exactly the asserted dependence. The compactness argument supplies no method for evaluating \(\mu_0\) or \(r_0\).

\textbf{Entry into the local neighborhood.}
Fix an outcome in \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\), write \(r=r_{n,\alpha,H}\), and assume \(r\leq r_0\). By \(\text{thm:confidence-union-coverage}\), \(D\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), so the union is nonempty. Let \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) be arbitrary. By \(\text{def:confidence-union}\), there is a simultaneous selection \(\Gamma_e\in\mathcal C^{(n)}_{e,\alpha}\), \(e\in E\), for which \(A\in\mathcal R_c(\Gamma)\). By \(\text{def:compatible-set}\), there are a fitted set of cardinality at least \(h\), a matrix \(\Psi\in\mathbb S_+^p\), and nonnegative shifts satisfying the candidate covariance equations. Restricting the fitted set and its witnesses, choose \(K\subseteq E\) with \(|K|=h\). The sole declarative candidate premise gives
\[
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|A^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\overline\kappa.
\tag{32}
\]
Because \(A\in\mathcal D\), \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\) gives its unit diagonal and \(\operatorname{CP}(I_p-A)<1\). Inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
|H\cap K|
\geq|H|+|K|-|E|
=2h-m=q.
\tag{33}
\]
Choose \(S\subseteq H\cap K\) with \(|S|=q\), and set \(U=D^{-1}\), \(V=A^{-1}\). For \(e\in S\subseteq H\), \(\text{def:honest-region-radius}\) and the event \(G_{n,\alpha,H}\) give
\[
\|\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq r\leq r_0\leq1.
\tag{34}
\]
The true assumptions and four true-side bounds, the candidate equations and (32), membership \(A\in\mathcal D\), and (34) put this tuple in \(\mathfrak T\). By the candidate equation, (4), (31), (34), submultiplicativity, and \(\|X\|_{\mathrm F}\leq\sqrt p\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), for every \(e\in S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|A\Sigma_eA^{\mathsf T}
-\Psi-\operatorname{diag}(t_e)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
&=\left\|A(\Sigma_e-\Gamma_e)A^{\mathsf T}\right\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\leq\sqrt p\,L_0^2r
\leq\mu_0/2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Thus the tuple is not in \(\mathfrak T_{\mathrm{far}}\), whether that family is nonempty or empty. With
\[
Q=AD^{-1}=I_p+R,
\qquad
u=\|R\|_{\mathrm F},
\]
we consequently have
\[
u<\rho_0\leq1,
\qquad
K_0\overline M u<\frac12.
\tag{36}
\]

\textbf{Local affine-minor inversion.}
Fix \(e_0\in S\) and put \(x_e=s_e-s_{e_0}\). For each \(i<j\), true-side affine separation on \(S\) supplies distinct \(a,b,d\in S\) such that the absolute affine determinant in coordinates \((i,j)\) is at least \(\gamma_0\). Translating the three points by the point indexed by \(e_0\) preserves that determinant, and direct expansion gives
\[
\det(\widetilde x_a-\widetilde x_b,
\widetilde x_d-\widetilde x_b)
=\det(\widetilde x_a,\widetilde x_d)
+\det(\widetilde x_d,\widetilde x_b)
+\det(\widetilde x_b,\widetilde x_a),
\tag{37}
\]
where \(\widetilde x_e=(x_{ei},x_{ej})\). By the triangle inequality, some \(e,f\in S\) satisfy
\[
|x_{ei}x_{fj}-x_{ej}x_{fi}|\geq\gamma_0/3.
\tag{38}
\]
Let \(E_e=\Gamma_e-\Sigma_e\). Subtract the true covariance equation at \(e_0\) from the equation at \(e\), do the same for the candidate equations, use \(A=QD\), and take the \((i,j)\) off-diagonal entry. The invariant nuisances cancel, giving the exact identity
\[
R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}
=-\sum_{k=1}^pR_{ik}R_{jk}x_{ek}
-[A(E_e-E_{e_0})A^{\mathsf T}]_{ij}.
\tag{39}
\]
The true scale bound in (13) and nonnegativity from \(\text{ass:nonnegative-shifts}\) imply \(|x_{ek}|\leq\overline M\). Equation (34) gives \(\|E_e-E_{e_0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2r\), while (4) gives \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_0\). Since each row of \(R\) has Euclidean norm at most \(u\), Cauchy--Schwarz in (39) yields
\[
|R_{ij}x_{ej}+R_{ji}x_{ei}|
\leq\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r.
\tag{40}
\]
Apply (40) at the two labels supplied by (38), and call the two right sides of (39) \(b_e,b_f\). Both have absolute value at most \(\beta=\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\). The determinant \(\Delta=x_{ej}x_{fi}-x_{fj}x_{ei}\) of the coefficient matrix for \((R_{ij},R_{ji})\) satisfies \(|\Delta|\geq\gamma_0/3\), and each coefficient has absolute value at most \(\overline M\). Direct elimination gives
\[
R_{ij}=\frac{b_ex_{fi}-b_fx_{ei}}{\Delta},
\qquad
R_{ji}=\frac{x_{ej}b_f-x_{fj}b_e}{\Delta}.
\]
Therefore, for every ordered pair \(i\ne j\),
\[
|R_{ij}|
\leq\frac{6\overline M}{\gamma_0}
\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\right).
\tag{41}
\]
The unit diagonals of \(A\) and \(D\) imply
\[
0=(A-D)_{ii}=(RD)_{ii}
=R_{ii}+\sum_{k\ne i}R_{ik}D_{ki}.
\tag{42}
\]
For each \(i\), the squared Euclidean norm of the \(i\)-th column of \(D\) is at most \(L_0^2\) by (4). Squaring (41), summing over the \(p(p-1)\) ordered off-diagonal entries, applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (42), and taking square roots gives
\[
u\leq K_0\left(\overline M u^2+2L_0^2r\right).
\tag{43}
\]
If \(u=0\), the next bound is immediate. If \(u>0\), (36) makes the quadratic term on the right of (43) strictly less than \(u/2\). Rearrangement gives, in either case,
\[
u\leq4K_0L_0^2r.
\tag{44}
\]
Using (4), (44), and \(C_0=16K_0L_0^3\),
\[
\|A-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|RD\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq uL_0
\leq4K_0L_0^3r
\leq C_0r.
\tag{45}
\]
Since \(A\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\) was arbitrary, (45) and \(\text{def:operator-outer-radius}\) give
\[
\operatorname{outer}_{\mathrm{op}}(\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha,D)
\leq C_0r.
\]
For \(A_1,A_2\in\mathcal U^{(n)}_\alpha\), the triangle inequality and (45) give
\[
\|A_1-A_2\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\|A_1-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|A_2-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2C_0r.
\]
Taking the supremum and using \(\text{def:operator-diameter}\) proves the diameter bound. Finally, along any sequence covered by the theorem with \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\to0\), eventually \(r_{n,\alpha,H}\leq r_0\); the two displayed bounds then force both asserted radii to converge to zero. No candidate affine-separation, normalization-slack, scale, or covariance-interiority condition was used.
