Take \(E=H=\{1,2,3\}\), \(D=I_2\), \(\Omega=2I_2\), and
\[
 s_1=(0,0),\qquad s_2=(1,0),\qquad s_3=(0,1).
\]
The associated rational positive-definite covariances are
\[
 \Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]
The unique affine minor is
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}=1,
\]
so \(\text{def:affine-separation}\) gives \(\gamma_H=1\).

We first calculate the compatibility set in (1).  Because \(c=0\), \(h=m=3\), and hence every witness in \(\text{def:compatible-set}\) uses all three slots after restriction to cardinality \(h\).  Unit diagonal gives
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\y&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtracting the transformed covariance equation at slot one from those at slots two and three cancels the common invariant matrix.  The two differences must be diagonal, whereas direct multiplication gives
\[
 A(\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\y&y^2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 A(\Sigma_3-\Sigma_1)A^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}x^2&x\\x&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their off-diagonal entries imply \(y=0\) and \(x=0\).  Thus every compatible matrix is \(I_2\).  Conversely, \(I_2\) is compatible with invariant matrix \(2I_2\) and the displayed nonnegative shifts.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Sigma_3)=\{I_2\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For a nonzero rational number \(u\), define
\[
 A_u=\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Psi_u=2\begin{pmatrix}1+u^2&u\\u&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{3,u}=\begin{pmatrix}2+u^2&-u\\-u&3\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
The matrix \(A_u\) has unit diagonal and is invertible.  The only directed two-cycle product of \(I_2-A_u\) is zero, so \(A_u\in\mathcal D\) by \(\text{def:admissible-matrices}\).  The equality
\[
 \Psi_u=A_u(2I_2)A_u^{\mathsf T}
\]
shows that \(\Psi_u\) is positive definite by invertible congruence.  Moreover, the first leading principal minor of \(\Gamma_{3,u}\) is \(2+u^2>0\), and
\[
 \det(\Gamma_{3,u})=6+2u^2>0.
\]
Completing the square, or equivalently applying the two-dimensional quadratic-form criterion, therefore shows that \(\Gamma_{3,u}\in\mathbb S_{++}^2\).  Direct multiplication yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_u\Sigma_1A_u^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_u,\\
 A_u\Sigma_2A_u^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_u+\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\\
 A_u\Gamma_{3,u}A_u^{\mathsf T}&=\Psi_u+\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(A_u\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,u})\), and \(A_u\ne I_2\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a deterministic finite-membership-query procedure computes the exact union for every arbitrary nonempty region family.  Run it on
\[
 \mathcal C^0_1=\{\Sigma_1\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C^0_2=\{\Sigma_2\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C^0_3=\{\Sigma_3\}.
\]
By (2) and \(\text{def:confidence-union}\), its required answer is \(\mathcal U^0=\{I_2\}\).  Its terminating run makes only finitely many queries to the third region.  The matrices \(\Gamma_{3,u}\) in (3) are pairwise distinct over nonzero rational \(u\), because their off-diagonal entry is \(-u\).  Choose a nonzero rational \(u\) for which \(\Gamma_{3,u}\) was not queried, and replace only the third region by
\[
 \mathcal C^1_3=\{\Sigma_3,\Gamma_{3,u}\}.
\]
The other two regions remain unchanged.

Induction over the query number proves that the two runs have identical transcripts.  Initially their internal states agree.  If the previous transcripts agree, determinism selects the same next region and matrix.  The answer is unchanged for regions one and two.  Along the baseline transcript it is also unchanged for region three, because the only added point is \(\Gamma_{3,u}\), which was not queried.  Hence the states remain equal through the finite baseline run, and the procedure returns the same output under \(\mathcal C^0\) and \(\mathcal C^1\).  But (4) and \(\text{def:confidence-union}\) give
\[
 A_u\in\mathcal R_0(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2,\Gamma_{3,u})
 \subseteq\mathcal U^1,
 \qquad
 A_u\notin\mathcal U^0.
\]
The exact unions differ, a contradiction.  This proves the finite-membership-query obstruction with the claimed rational, positive-definite, and separated witness.
