%%% FIELD proof-chebyshev-factorial-certificate
Let \(T_j\) be the ordinary Chebyshev polynomial, characterized by
\(T_j(\cos \vartheta)=\cos(j\vartheta)\), and put
\(Q_j(z)=T_j(1-2z)\).  Since \(Q_L(0)=T_L(1)=1\), the polynomial
\(1-Q_L(z)\) is divisible by \(z\).  Moreover,
\[
 T_L'(1)=\lim_{\vartheta\to0}
 \frac{1-\cos(L\vartheta)}{1-\cos\vartheta}=L^2.
\]
It follows that \(H_L'(0)=1\), so the polynomial continuation of
\(E_L=H_L/z\) satisfies \(E_L(0)=1\).  Therefore \(1-E_L\) is also
divisible by \(z\).  Degree counting now gives
\(\deg(E_L)\le L-1\) and \(\deg(G_L)\le L-2\), proving all the asserted
continuation and degree claims.

For \(z\in[0,1]\), choose \(\vartheta\in[0,\pi]\) such that
\(1-2z=\cos\vartheta\).  The definitions give, with the value at zero
understood by continuity,
\[
 E_L(z)=\frac{1-\cos(L\vartheta)}{L^2(1-\cos\vartheta)}
 =\left\{\frac{\sin(L\vartheta/2)}{L\sin(\vartheta/2)}\right\}^{\!2}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The elementary inequality \(|\sin(Lv)|\le L|\sin v|\), obtained by
induction from the sine addition formula, proves \(0\le E_L(z)\le1\).
Using \(1-\cos(L\vartheta)\le2\) in the first expression in (1) also
gives \(E_L(z)\le (L^2z)^{-1}\) for \(z>0\).  Finally,
\(zG_L(z)+E_L(z)=1\) is the defining identity for \(G_L\).

It remains to prove the factorial-moment assertion.  For a polynomial
let \(\|\cdot\|_1\) denote the sum of the absolute values of its
coefficients.  The recurrence
\[
 Q_{j+1}(z)=2(1-2z)Q_j(z)-Q_{j-1}(z)
\]
and \(\|1-2z\|_1=3\) imply
\(\|Q_{j+1}\|_1\le6\|Q_j\|_1+\|Q_{j-1}\|_1\).  Since
\(\|Q_0\|_1=1\) and \(\|Q_1\|_1=3\), induction yields
\(\|Q_j\|_1\le7^j\).  Division by \(z\) of a polynomial having zero
constant term merely shifts its coefficients.  Hence there is a
universal \(C_0>1\) such that the coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm of the
dimensionless bivariate polynomial
\[
 (z_0+z_1)G_L(z_a)
\]
is at most \(C_0^L\).  Write its coefficients as \(b_{a,r_0,r_1}\).
The factorial lift in the statement can equivalently be written as
\[
 \widehat W_{a,L}=
 \sum_{r_0,r_1}b_{a,r_0,r_1}
 \frac{(K_0)_{r_0}(K_1)_{r_1}}{(tB)^{r_0+r_1}},
 \qquad K_b\sim\operatorname{Poi}(ts_b),
 \tag{2}
\]
where the two counts are independent and \(z_b=s_b/B\).

For \(K\sim\operatorname{Poi}(\lambda)\), counting two ordered samples
of sizes \(r\) and \(v\) according to the number \(h\) of common
indices gives the exact identity
\[
 E[(K)_r(K)_v]
 =\sum_{h=0}^{\min(r,v)}\binom rh\binom vh h!\lambda^{r+v-h}.
 \tag{3}
\]
After division by \((tB)^{r+v}\), the \(h\)-th summand is bounded by
\[
 \frac{(rv)^h}{h!(tB)^h}(1+z)^{r+v},
 \qquad z=\frac{\lambda}{tB},
\]
because \(z^{r+v-h}\le(1+z)^{r+v}\).  All degrees in (2) are at most
\(L\); thus \(rv\le L^2\), and \(tB\ge L\) makes the sum in (3) at
most \(e^L(1+z)^{r+v}\).  Applying this bound independently to
\(K_0,K_1\), expanding the square in (2), and taking absolute values
gives
\[
 E\widehat W_{a,L}^{,2}
 \le C_0^{2L}e^{2L}
 \left(1+\frac{s_0+s_1}{B}\right)^{2L}.
\]
Taking a universal \(A\ge C_0^2e^2\) proves the first claimed bound,
since \(p=s_0+s_1\).  If \(p\le B\), the last factor is at most
\(2^{2L}\), which is absorbed by replacing \(A\) with \(4A\).  This
proves the lemma.

%%% FIELD proof-poisson-hybrid-risk
Write \(\ell=\log(en)\), and let all unspecified constants in this
proof depend only on \(\epsilon\) and on the fixed numerical comparison
constants in \(u\asymp n\) and \(t_p\asymp t\asymp N\).  These
comparison constants are fixed by the construction and hence introduce
no dependence on \(d\), \(N/n\), or \(m\).  For a cell \(x\), define
\[
 \theta_x=p_x(\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x}),\qquad
 I_x=\mathbf 1\{J_x\le \lfloor t_pB/4\rfloor\},
\]
and denote its polynomial and inverse-count statistics by \(P_x\) and
\(H_x\), respectively.  By the independent Poisson construction, the
pilot variable \(I_x\) is independent of \((P_x,H_x)\), and the triples
belonging to different cells are independent.

We first calculate the two deterministic branch means.  Put
\(z_a=s_{ax}/B\).  Unbiasedness of Poisson factorial moments and the
identity \(zG_L(z)=1-E_L(z)\) from
\(\mathrm{lem:chebyshev\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}certificate}\) give
\[
 EP_x=\theta_x-p_x\{\mu_{1x}E_L(z_1)-\mu_{0x}E_L(z_0)\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(p_x\le B\), then \(0\le z_a\le1\), while
\(s_{ax}\ge\epsilon p_x\) by \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) whenever
\(p_x>0\).  The bound on \(E_L\) therefore yields
\[
 |EP_x-\theta_x|\le \frac{2B}{\epsilon L^2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The assertion is also valid at \(p_x=0\), where both sides contributed
by the cell are zero.

For the inverse-count branch, independence of the two arm counts and
the shifted Poisson series give, for \(s_{ax}>0\),
\[
 E\frac{K_{0x}+K_{1x}+1}{K_{ax}+1}
 =1+\frac{s_{1-a,x}}{s_{ax}}(1-e^{-ts_{ax}}).
\]
Multiplication by \(q_{ax}=s_{ax}\mu_{ax}\), with the null-arm case
obtained by continuity, shows that the error of the arm-
\(a\) mean relative to \(p_x\mu_{ax}\) is
\(-s_{1-a,x}\mu_{ax}e^{-ts_{ax}}\).  Thus overlap implies
\[
 |EH_x-\theta_x|\le2p_xe^{-\epsilon tp_x}.
 \tag{6}
\]

We next establish the wrong-branch bounds, including the requested
early-kill check.  Enlarge \(H_\epsilon\), if necessary, so that
\(t_pB\ge8\), and set \(k_0=\lfloor t_pB/4\rfloor\).  Then
\(k_0\ge t_pB/8\).  If \(p_x>B\), the exponential Markov bound for a
Poisson variable of mean \(t_pp_x\) gives
\[
 \pi_x:=P(I_x=1)\le e^{-c_0t_pp_x}
 \le e^{-c_0H_\epsilon Ly_x},
 \qquad y_x=p_x/B>1,
 \tag{7}
\]
for a numerical \(c_0>0\).  The coefficient recurrence used in the
proof of the factorial certificate, applied directly to the
deterministic polynomial in (4), gives
\[
 |EP_x|\le p_x C_0^L(1+y_x)^L.                 \tag{8}
\]
This is a bound on the deterministic expectation itself; it does not
apply Cauchy--Schwarz to the outcome-bearing statistic.  On the other
hand, independence of the outcome and factorial blocks, the factorial
second-moment certificate, \(q_{ax}\le p_x\), and
\(\operatorname{Var}(U-V)\le2\operatorname{Var}(U)+2\operatorname{Var}(V)\)
give
\[
 EP_x^2\le C(p_x/u+p_x^2)A^L(1+y_x)^{2L}.      \tag{9}
\]
Since \(\sup_{y\ge1}\log(1+y)/y<\infty\), one may choose
\(H_\epsilon\) so large that the negative exponent in (7) absorbs the
polynomial factors in both (8) and (9).  Consequently, for some
\(c_1>0\),
\[
 \sum_{p_x>B}\pi_x|EP_x|
 \le\sum_{p_x>B}p_xe^{-c_1H_\epsilon Ly_x},
 \qquad
 \sum_{p_x>B}\pi_xEP_x^2
 \le C\sum_{p_x>B}p_xe^{-c_1H_\epsilon Ly_x}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Both sums are mass-weighted, since \(\sum_xp_x=1\); no factor \(d\)
has been introduced.

For the other wrong branch let
\(\eta_x=P(I_x=0)\).  Put \(\lambda=t_pp_x\) and
\(r=\epsilon t/t_p\), whose positive lower bound follows from
\(t\asymp t_p\).  If \(\lambda<k_0\), the Poisson exponential-series
bound gives
\[
 \eta_x e^{-\epsilon tp_x}
 \le \exp\{-k_0\log(k_0/\lambda)+k_0-(1+r)\lambda\}
 \le (1+r)^{-k_0};                              \tag{11}
\]
the last inequality follows by maximizing the displayed exponent at
\(\lambda=k_0/(1+r)\).  If \(\lambda\ge k_0\), the trivial bound
\(\eta_x\le1\) and \(k_0\ge t_pB/8\) give the same conclusion with a
possibly smaller exponential constant.  Hence, uniformly for
\(p_x\le B\),
\[
 \eta_xe^{-\epsilon tp_x}\le e^{-c_2H_\epsilon L}.                 \tag{12}
\]
Choose \(c_\epsilon^\circ>0\) sufficiently small for the moment
absorption below, and then choose \(H_\epsilon\) sufficiently large.
After increasing \(n_\epsilon^\circ\),
\(L\ge c_\epsilon^\circ\ell/2\), and the right sides of (10)--(12) are
at most \(n^{-12}\).  In particular, at \(\epsilon=1/4\), (10)
controls the side \(p_x>B\), (12) controls the side \(p_x\le B\), and
both bounds remain uniform at the threshold because
\(t_pB\asymp tB\asymp H_\epsilon L\).  This proves the requested
early-kill check without either \(d e^{-cL}\) or an \(N/n\) factor.

Combining (5), (6), (10), and (12), separately on the two regions and
using \(\sum_xp_x=1\), gives the bias bound for the unclipped sum
\(Z=\sum_x\{I_xP_x+(1-I_x)H_x\}\):
\[
 |EZ-\tau(P)|\le C_\epsilon\frac{dB}{L^2}+Cn^{-10}.                \tag{13}
\]
For example, on \(p_x\le B\) the wrong heavy-branch error is bounded
by \(2p_x\eta_xe^{-\epsilon tp_x}\), and on \(p_x>B\) the ordinary
heavy-branch error is bounded by \(2p_xe^{-\epsilon tB}\).  These are
the mass weights used in (13).

We now control variance separately.  Conditional on the pilot branch,
independence gives the exact decomposition
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{I_xP_x+(1-I_x)H_x\}
 =\pi_x\operatorname{Var}(P_x)+(1-\pi_x)\operatorname{Var}(H_x)
  +\pi_x(1-\pi_x)(EP_x-EH_x)^2.                 \tag{14}
\]
If \(p_x\le B\), the product-variance identity and independence of the
outcome and factorial blocks yield, arm by arm,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{(S_{ax}/u)\widehat W_{a,L,x}\}
 =\frac{q_{ax}}uE\widehat W_{a,L,x}^{\,2}
  +q_{ax}^{,2}\operatorname{Var}(\widehat W_{a,L,x}).             \tag{15}
\]
The factorial certificate, \(q_{ax}\le p_x\le B\), and
\(\sum_xp_x=1\) imply
\[
 \sum_{p_x\le B}\operatorname{Var}(P_x)
 \le C A^L\left\{\frac{\min(1,dB)}u+dB^2\right\}.                \tag{16}
\]
No independence between the two arm statistics is used here; their
covariance is absorbed by the variance inequality used in (9).
For \(p_x>B\), equations (7), (9), and (10) show
\[
 \sum_{p_x>B}\pi_x\operatorname{Var}(P_x)\le Cn^{-10}.           \tag{17}
\]

The cells of the all-heavy inverse-count statistic are independent.
Therefore its variance is the sum of its cell variances, and
\(\mathrm{lem:poisson\mbox{-}inverse\mbox{-}count\mbox{-}risk}\),
whose premises are supplied by \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) and
\(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\), gives
\[
 \sum_x(1-\pi_x)\operatorname{Var}(H_x)
 \le\sum_x\operatorname{Var}(H_x)
 \le C_\epsilon(u^{-1}+t^{-1}).                 \tag{18}
\]
For the branch-mean term in (14), equations (5)--(6) give
\[
 \sum_{p_x\le B}(EP_x-EH_x)^2
 \le C_\epsilon\left\{\frac{dB^2}{L^4}+\frac1t\right\},         \tag{19}
\]
where \(p^2e^{-2\epsilon tp}\le C_\epsilon p/t\) and
\(\sum_xp_x=1\) were used.  On \(p_x>B\), (7)--(10) and
\(|EH_x|\le3p_x\), which follows from (6) and \(|\theta_x|\le p_x\),
give
\[
 \sum_{p_x>B}\pi_x(1-\pi_x)(EP_x-EH_x)^2\le Cn^{-10}.            \tag{20}
\]
Summing (14), using independence across cells, and then adding the
squared bias (13), proves
\[
 E[(Z-\tau(P))^2]\le C_\epsilon\left\{
 \frac1u+\frac1t+\frac{d^2B^2}{L^4}
 +\frac{A^L\min(1,dB)}u+A^LdB^2
 +\frac{dB^2}{L^4}+n^{-9}\right\}.              \tag{21}
\]

It remains to verify that no displayed remainder leaks into the rate.
Because \(t_p\asymp t\asymp N\),
\(B\asymp_\epsilon L/N\).  Put
\(\delta=d/(NL)\).  Choose \(c_\epsilon^\circ\) small enough and
\(n_\epsilon^\circ\) large enough that
\[
 L^4A^{2L}\le n,\qquad L^3A^L\le\sqrt n                         \tag{22}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_\epsilon^\circ\); this is possible because
\(L=O(c_\epsilon^\circ\log(en))\).  If \(dB\le1\), the inequality
\(2vw\le v^2+w^2\) and the first part of (22) give
\[
 \frac{A^LdB}{n}\le C(\delta^2+n^{-1}).
\]
If \(dB>1\), then \(\delta\ge c_\epsilon/L^2\), while (22) gives
\(A^L/n\le L^{-4}\); the same conclusion follows.  The second part
of (22), \(N\ge n\), and the product inequality give
\[
 A^LdB^2\le C\frac{\delta L^3A^L}{N}
 \le C(\delta^2+n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \frac{dB^2}{L^4}\le C\frac{\delta}{NL}
 \le C(\delta^2+n^{-1}).                         \tag{23}
\]
Also \(u^{-1}+t^{-1}\le C/n\) and
\(d^2B^2/L^4\le C_\epsilon\delta^2\).  Substitution in (21) yields
\[
 E[(Z-\tau(P))^2]\le C_\epsilon
 \left\{\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{N^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.             \tag{24}
\]
Because \(\tau(P)\in[-1,1]\) by \(\mathrm{def:ate\mbox{-}functional}\),
projection of \(Z\) onto \([-1,1]\) cannot increase its loss and has
loss at most four.  Combining this fact with (24) gives the asserted
capped rate.  For the finitely many \(n<n_\epsilon^\circ\), the zero
estimator has risk at most one, which is bounded by a constant times
the asserted rate because that rate is at least \(1/n\).  The proof is
uniform in \(P,d,N/n\), and \(m\), as required.

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-sample-hybrid-transfer
Assume first that \(n\) exceeds a fixed numerical constant; the
remaining finitely many values are handled at the end.  Partition the
labeled indices deterministically into three disjoint blocks whose
sizes lie between \(n/4\) and \(n/2\).  Use the first block for outcome
marks.  Split the auxiliary indices into two blocks differing in size
by at most one, and append these to the \((X,A)\)-projections of the
second and third labeled blocks.  By \(\mathrm{ass:labeled\mbox{-}iid}\),
\(\mathrm{ass:auxiliary\mbox{-}iid}\), and
\(\mathrm{ass:sample\mbox{-}independence}\), the resulting three
pools are independent; the first consists of iid observations from
\(P\), and the other two consist of iid observations from \(P_{XA}\).
Their deterministic sizes \(M_0,M_p,M_f\) satisfy
\[
 c n\le M_0\le Cn,
 \qquad cN\le M_p,M_f\le CN                         \tag{25}
\]
for fixed numerical \(0<c<C<\infty\).

Independently draw prefix lengths
\(D_0\sim\operatorname{Poi}(M_0/8)\),
\(D_p\sim\operatorname{Poi}(M_p/8)\), and
\(D_f\sim\operatorname{Poi}(M_f/8)\).  If every prefix fits in its
pool, apply the clipped hybrid statistic to those prefixes; otherwise
output zero.  After averaging over an untruncated Poisson prefix
length, the category counts are independent Poisson variables: this
follows directly by substituting the multinomial
probability-generating function into the Poisson
probability-generating function.  Thus the
untruncated statistic has exactly the experiment in
\(\mathrm{lem:poisson\mbox{-}hybrid\mbox{-}risk}\), with
\[
 u=M_0/8\asymp n,\qquad t_p=M_p/8\asymp N,\qquad t=M_f/8\asymp N.  \tag{26}
\]

For \(D\sim\operatorname{Poi}(M/8)\), exponential Markov's inequality
applied to \(e^D\) gives \(P(D>M)\le e^{-c_0M}\) for a numerical
\(c_0>0\).  Hence (25) and a union bound show that the overflow event
\(F\) satisfies
\[
 P(F)\le Ce^{-cn}.                                  \tag{27}
\]
Couple each finite pool to an infinite iid sequence having the same
law.  Off \(F\), the truncated and untruncated clipped statistics are
identical.  Both lie in \([-1,1]\), and
\(\tau(P)\in[-1,1]\) by \(\mathrm{def:ate\mbox{-}functional}\).
Consequently
\[
 E[(Z_{\mathrm{trunc}}-\tau(P))^2]
 \le E[(Z_{\mathrm{Pois}}-\tau(P))^2]+4P(F)
 \le 2E[(Z_{\mathrm{Pois}}-\tau(P))^2]+C/n.         \tag{28}
\]
The last inequality uses (27), \(e^{-cn}\le C/n\), and nonnegativity
of risk.

Finally define the reported statistic by
\[
 \widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}
 =E[Z_{\mathrm{trunc}}\mid\mathcal L_n,\mathcal U_m].             \tag{29}
\]
There are only finitely many nonoverflow triples of prefix lengths, so
(29) is a finite sum of explicitly known Poisson weights and observed
sample functions.  It is therefore a total deterministic computable
measurable map.  Conditional Jensen's inequality makes its squared
risk no larger than the left side of (28).  For the finitely many
small values of \(n\), use the stipulated zero branch; its risk is at
most one and hence at most \(C/n\) after enlarging the numerical
constant.  This proves the fixed-sample transfer uniformly over
\(\mathrm{def:annotation\mbox{-}experiment}\).

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-mixed-upper
The deterministic partition and prefix construction in
\(\mathrm{def:mixed\mbox{-}estimator\mbox{-}handle}\) is legitimate
under \(\mathrm{ass:labeled\mbox{-}iid}\),
\(\mathrm{ass:auxiliary\mbox{-}iid}\), and
\(\mathrm{ass:sample\mbox{-}independence}\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem:fixed\mbox{-}sample\mbox{-}hybrid\mbox{-}transfer}\),
its risk is bounded by twice the independent-Poisson risk plus
\(C/n\).  The premises of
\(\mathrm{lem:poisson\mbox{-}hybrid\mbox{-}risk}\) hold because the
three prefix intensities satisfy \(u\asymp n\) and
\(t_p\asymp t\asymp N=n+m\).  That lemma and
\(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate\mbox{-}handle}\) therefore give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}}
 E[\{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}-\tau(P)\}^2]
 \le C_\epsilon r_\epsilon(n,m,d)+\frac Cn.
\]
For every \(n\ge1\), the definition of the capped rate implies
\(r_\epsilon(n,m,d)\ge1/n\), so the last term is absorbed into the
first.  The small-sample zero branch was covered in the transfer
lemma.  Every split, threshold, polynomial coefficient, clipping map,
and Poisson prefix weight is determined by \((n,m,d,\epsilon)\), and
the conditional expectation over prefix lengths is a finite sum.
Thus the displayed estimator is total, computable, measurable, and
does not optimize over an unknown law.  This proves the theorem
uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\).

%%% FIELD proof-sharp-annotation-frontier
Because \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}\) is a member of
\(\mathcal T_{n,m,d}\), the definition
\(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}risk}\) and
\(\mathrm{thm:uniform\mbox{-}mixed\mbox{-}upper}\) give the upper
inequality.  The lower inequality is
\(\mathrm{thm:common\mbox{-}marginal\mbox{-}converse}\).  Both use
the same class \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\) and the same observation
experiment, so their constants combine to give
\[
 R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\asymp_\epsilon
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{(n+m)^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.     \tag{30}
\]
Putting \(m=0\) in (30) gives the displayed supervised formula by
algebra.  The external record
\(\texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap/oeq:sharp-minimax}\)
is used only for the attribution stated in the theorem, not as a
logical premise here.  The fixed-parameter limit is exactly
\(\mathrm{thm:known\mbox{-}marginal\mbox{-}limit}\), and
\(\mathrm{thm:known\mbox{-}marginal\mbox{-}boundary}\) shows that its
order is \(n^{-1}\).

It remains to prove the three sequence characterizations.  Put
\[
 N_n=n+m_n,\qquad \ell_n=\log(en),\qquad
 a_n=\frac{d_n}{n\ell_n},\qquad
 b_n=\frac{d_n}{N_n\ell_n}.
\]
The numerical semi-supervised and supervised rates in (30) are
\[
 r_{mn}=\min\{1,n^{-1}+b_n^2\},
 \qquad r_{0n}=\min\{1,n^{-1}+a_n^2\}.             \tag{31}
\]
Since \(n^{-1}\to0\), (31) gives
\(r_{mn}\to0\) if and only if \(b_n\to0\).  The two-sided constants
in (30) transfer this equivalence to \(R_\epsilon(n,m_n,d_n)\), proving
the first characterization.

If \(r_{mn}=O(n^{-1})\), then eventually the cap in (31) is inactive,
and \(b_n^2=O(n^{-1})\).  Conversely this last relation plainly makes
\(r_{mn}=O(n^{-1})\).  Thus, again using (30),
\[
 R_\epsilon(n,m_n,d_n)=O(n^{-1})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d_n=O\!\left(\frac{N_n\ell_n}{\sqrt n}\right).
\]

For strict improvement, first observe from (31) that
\(r_{mn}\ge n^{-1}\).  Hence \(r_{mn}=o(r_{0n})\) requires
\(n^{-1}=o(r_{0n})\).  Directly from (31), this is equivalent to
\[
 a_n\sqrt n=\frac{d_n}{\sqrt n\,\ell_n}\longrightarrow\infty.    \tag{32}
\]
Under (32), the \(n^{-1}\) term is negligible relative to the
supervised rate and
\[
 r_{0n}\asymp\min\{1,a_n^2\}.                    \tag{33}
\]
Because \(0\le b_n\le a_n\), equations (31)--(33) show that
\(r_{mn}=o(r_{0n})\) is then equivalent to
\[
 \frac{b_n}{\min(1,a_n)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which, after substituting the definitions of \(a_n,b_n\), is precisely
the second limit displayed in the theorem.  Conversely, that limit
together with (32) makes both \(n^{-1}/r_{0n}\) and
\(b_n^2/r_{0n}\) tend to zero, so (31) gives
\(r_{mn}/r_{0n}\to0\).  Finally, applying the upper and lower constants
in (30) at \(m=m_n\) and at \(m=0\) transfers this if-and-only-if from
the numerical rates to the ratio of minimax risks.  This completes all
claims.
