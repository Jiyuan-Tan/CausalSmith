%%% FIELD known-marginal-boundary-proof
Fix \(n\geq 1\), \(d\geq 2\), and \(P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\).  In the experiment of \(\texttt{def:known-marginal-experiment}\), the supplied table \(P_{XA}\) determines
\[
p_x=s_{0x}+s_{1x},\qquad
e_x=\frac{s_{1x}}{p_x}\quad\text{when }p_x>0.
\]
The division is legitimate because \(p_x>0\), and \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\) then gives \(e_x\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\).  Values of \(e_x\) on cells with \(p_x=0\) are immaterial; fix them to \(1/2\).  Define the following explicit measurable function of the labeled sample and the supplied table:
\[
\widehat\tau_{K}
:=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_i,\quad
Z_i:=\frac{A_iY_i}{e_{X_i}}-
       \frac{(1-A_i)Y_i}{1-e_{X_i}}.
\]
The sample space is finite, the displayed denominators are positive on every event of positive probability by \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\), and hence \(\widehat\tau_K\in\mathcal T^{\mathrm{known}}_{n,d}\).  By the definitions of \(e_x\) and \(\mu_{ax}\), followed by \(\texttt{def:ate-functional}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[Z_i]
&=\sum_{x:p_x>0}p_x\left{
e_x\frac{\mu_{1x}}{e_x}
-(1-e_x)\frac{\mu_{0x}}{1-e_x}
\right}\\
&=\sum_{x=1}^{d}p_x(\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x})
=\tau(P).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the estimator targets the identified functional rather than defining it.  Since \(A_i(1-A_i)=0\), \(Y_i^2=Y_i\), and \(0\leq\mu_{ax}\leq1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[Z_i^2]
&=\sum_{x:p_x>0}p_x\left{
\frac{\mu_{1x}}{e_x}+
\frac{\mu_{0x}}{1-e_x}
\right}\\
&\leq \frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{x=1}^{d}p_x
=\frac{2}{\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:labeled-iid}\) makes the \(Z_i\)'s independent and identically distributed.  Therefore
\[
E_P[(\widehat\tau_K-\tau(P))^2]
=\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\tau_K)
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(Z_1)}{n}
\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\) and then using the infimum in \(\texttt{def:known-marginal-risk}\) proves
\[
R_\epsilon^{\mathrm{known}}(n,d)\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon n}.
\]

For the converse, set \(\delta=(16\sqrt n)^{-1}\).  For each sign \(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\), define \(P_\sigma\) by
\[
p_1=1,\quad p_x=0\ (x\geq2),\quad e_1=\frac12,
\quad \mu_{01}=\frac12,
\quad \mu_{11}=\frac12+\sigma\delta,
\]
and choose arbitrary admissible values on null cells.  Because \(n\geq1\), \(0<\delta\leq1/16\), so both outcome means lie in \([0,1]\).  Because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \(e_1=1/2\) satisfies \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\); hence \(P_-\) and \(P_+\) belong to the same class \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\) defined by \(\texttt{def:model-class}\).  They also have exactly the same supplied table \(P_{XA}\).  Their targets, computed from \(\texttt{def:ate-functional}\), are
\[
\tau(P_\sigma)=\sigma\delta.
\]

Write \(Q_\sigma=P_\sigma\) for the law of one labeled record.  The two laws agree on the two atoms with \(A=0\).  On the two atoms \((A,Y)=(1,1)\) and \((1,0)\), their probabilities under \(Q_-\) are respectively \(1/4-\delta/2\) and \(1/4+\delta/2\), and the changes from \(Q_-\) to \(Q_+\) are respectively \(\delta\) and \(-\delta\).  Consequently, by the definition of chi-squared divergence,
\[
\chi^2(Q_+\Vert Q_-)
=\frac{\delta^2}{1/4-\delta/2}
 +\frac{\delta^2}{1/4+\delta/2}
=\frac{8\delta^2}{1-4\delta^2}
\leq\frac{2}{63n}.
\]
Indeed, \(8\delta^2=1/(32n)\) and \(1-4\delta^2\geq63/64\).  If \(L=dQ_+/dQ_-\), independence from \(\texttt{ass:labeled-iid}\) gives
\[
1+\chi^2(Q_+^{\otimes n}\Vert Q_-^{\otimes n})
=E_{Q_-^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}L_i^2\right]
=\{E_{Q_-}[L^2]\}^{n}
=\{1+\chi^2(Q_+\Vert Q_-)\}^{n}.
\]
Using \(1+u\leq e^u\), and using the power series together with \(k!\geq1\) to obtain \(e^v\leq(1-v)^{-1}\) for \(0\leq v<1\), yields
\[
\chi^2(Q_+^{\otimes n}\Vert Q_-^{\otimes n})
\leq e^{2/63}-1
\leq\frac{2}{61}<1.
\]
The total-variation bound needed below follows directly, rather than being invoked as an undeclared theorem.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{TV}(Q_-^{\otimes n},Q_+^{\otimes n})
&=\frac12E_{Q_-^{\otimes n}}\left|
\frac{dQ_+^{\otimes n}}{dQ_-^{\otimes n}}-1
\right|\\
&\leq\frac12
\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_+^{\otimes n}\Vert Q_-^{\otimes n})}
<\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to turn indistinguishability into squared-error risk.  Let \(T\in\mathcal T^{\mathrm{known}}_{n,d}\) be arbitrary.  The deterministic side information is identical under \(P_-\) and \(P_+\), so take densities \(f_-\) and \(f_+\) only for the labeled samples with respect to a common dominating measure.  Pointwise, for every real \(t\),
\[
(t+\delta)^2+(t-\delta)^2=2t^2+2\delta^2\geq2\delta^2.
\]
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_{P_-^{\otimes n}}[(T-\tau(P_-))^2]
 +E_{P_+^{\otimes n}}[(T-\tau(P_+))^2]\\
&\quad\geq
\int \min(f_-,f_+)
\bigl\{(T+\delta)^2+(T-\delta)^2\bigr\}\\
&\quad\geq2\delta^2\int\min(f_-,f_+)
=2\delta^2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_-^{\otimes n},Q_+^{\otimes n})\}
>\delta^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence the larger of the two risks is at least \(\delta^2/2=1/(512n)\).  Both laws are members of \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\), so taking the supremum over the model class and then the infimum over \(T\), exactly as in \(\texttt{def:known-marginal-risk}\), gives
\[
R_\epsilon^{\mathrm{known}}(n,d)\geq\frac{1}{512n}.
\]
Thus the claimed uniform inequalities hold with \(c_\epsilon=1/512\) and \(C_\epsilon=2/\epsilon\), which are positive and finite for the fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).

%%% FIELD uniform-mixed-upper-partial
The endpoint in which the exact table \(P_{XA}\) is supplied is completely resolved: the explicit inverse-propensity estimator \(\widehat\tau_K\) has uniform mean squared error at most \(2/(\epsilon n)\).  This does not control the finite-\(m\) pilot error or the false-light polynomial branch required by the question.

%%% FIELD common-marginal-converse-partial
The two-point family \(P_-\), \(P_+\) used in \(\texttt{thm:known-marginal-boundary}\) has identical \(P_{XA}\), target separation \(1/(8\sqrt n)\), and labeled-sample total variation below \(1/2\).  Therefore it also yields the dimension-free lower bound \(R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\geq1/(512n)\) for every finite \(m\), because the auxiliary laws agree exactly.  It does not yield the dimension-dependent part or the capped constant-risk regime of the proposed frontier.

%%% FIELD sharp-annotation-frontier-partial
The limiting benchmark scale is proved uniformly: \(R_\epsilon^{\mathrm{known}}(n,d)\asymp_\epsilon n^{-1}\).  The same common-marginal two-point construction gives \(R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\geq1/(512n)\) for all \(m\).  These endpoint facts alone do not determine the finite-\(m\), dimension-dependent rate or the requested necessary-and-sufficient growth conditions.
