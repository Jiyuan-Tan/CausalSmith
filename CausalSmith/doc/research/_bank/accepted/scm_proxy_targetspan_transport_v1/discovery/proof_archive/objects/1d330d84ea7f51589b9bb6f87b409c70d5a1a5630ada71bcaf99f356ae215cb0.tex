Fix an arbitrary baseline \(\mathcal M=(\pi,S,M,a,f,q)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal D)\), an arbitrary outcome value \(y\in\mathcal Y\), and an arbitrary \(h\in\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\). By Definition~\ref{def:failure-separator},
\[
h^{\top}B_x=0,
\qquad
h^{\top}b\ne0.
\tag{1}
\]
Put \(v=M^{\top}h\), as in Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation}. Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} and (1) imply
\[
v^{\top}R_x=h^{\top}MR_x=h^{\top}B_x=0,
\qquad
v^{\top}q=h^{\top}Mq=h^{\top}b\ne0.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(v\ne0\).

Fix any \(w_{\star}\in\mathcal W\) and any \(y^{\circ}\in\mathcal Y\setminus\{y\}\). The first choice is possible because \(m\ge r\ge2\), and the second because \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\ge2\). Assumption~\ref{ass:strict-primitive-positivity} gives
\[
M_{w_{\star}u}>0,
\qquad
f_{x,y}(u,w_{\star})>0,
\qquad
f_{x,y^{\circ}}(u,w_{\star})>0
\]
for every \(u\in\mathcal U\). Since \(\mathcal U\) is finite and \(v\ne0\),
\[
\varepsilon
=\frac12\min_{u:v_u\ne0}
\frac{M_{w_{\star}u}\min\{f_{x,y}(u,w_{\star}),
f_{x,y^{\circ}}(u,w_{\star})\}}{|v_u|}
\tag{3}
\]
is well defined and strictly positive. If \(|t|<\varepsilon\), each nonzero change in Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation} has magnitude strictly smaller than half of each of the two outcome probabilities it changes. Hence those probabilities remain strictly positive. The two changes have opposite signs, so their sum is zero; all other outcome probabilities are unchanged. Thus \(f^{(t)}\) is a strictly positive stochastic kernel. The other primitive kernels and \(M\) are unchanged, including \(\operatorname{rank}(M)=r\). Therefore \(\mathcal M_t\in\mathfrak M_{+}\).

We next verify compatibility with the entire supplied source law, rather than only with its balancing moments. By Assumption~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization} and Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation}, the only possibly changed source cell with outcome \(y\) obeys, for each \(e\in\mathcal E\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_{O,t}(e,w_{\star},x,y)-P_O(e,w_{\star},x,y)\\
&\quad=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\pi_eS_{ue}M_{w_{\star}u}a_x(u)
\left(t\frac{v_u}{M_{w_{\star}u}}\right)\\
&\quad=t\pi_e\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}S_{ue}a_x(u)v_u\\
&\quad=t\pi_e\rho_x(e)\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}R_x(u,e)v_u
=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The division by \(M_{w_{\star}u}\) is valid by strict positivity, the identity in the penultimate line is the definition of \(R_x\), and the last equality is the first identity in (2). The change for outcome \(y^{\circ}\) is the negative of (4), and hence also vanishes. A cell with another outcome, proxy value, or treatment value is unchanged by construction. Consequently every cell of \(P_O\) is preserved. The target proxy law is also preserved because \(M\) and \(q\) are fixed and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization},
\[
b_t=Mq=b.
\tag{5}
\]
Definition~\ref{def:compatible-fiber} now gives
\[
\mathcal M_t\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal D)
\qquad\text{for every }|t|<\varepsilon.
\tag{6}
\]

For the causal query, Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation} changes the \(u\)-th coordinate of \(g_{x,y}\) by
\[
M_{w_{\star}u}\left(t\frac{v_u}{M_{w_{\star}u}}\right)=tv_u.
\tag{7}
\]
The interventional identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization}, followed by (2), therefore yields
\[
\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M_t)-\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)
=tq^{\top}v
=t h^{\top}Mq
=t h^{\top}b.
\tag{8}
\]
Its slope is nonzero by (1).

For completeness, the off-span premise guarantees that a separator exists. Let \(C=\operatorname{col}(B_x)\), choose a matrix \(D\) whose columns form a basis of \(C\), and put
\[
p=D(D^{\top}D)^{-1}D^{\top}b,
\qquad
\widetilde h=b-p.
\tag{9}
\]
There is at least one column of \(B_x\), and every such column is a probability vector, so \(C\ne\{0\}\). The basis matrix \(D\) consequently has positive column dimension and full column rank. Thus \(D^{\top}D\) is positive definite and the inverse in (9) exists. Direct multiplication gives \(D^{\top}\widetilde h=0\). Since each column of \(B_x\) belongs to \(\operatorname{col}(D)\), it follows that \(\widetilde h^{\top}B_x=0\). Moreover, \(p\in C\) and \(\widetilde h\perp C\), while \(b\notin C\) implies \(\widetilde h\ne0\). Hence
\[
\widetilde h^{\top}b
=\widetilde h^{\top}(p+\widetilde h)
=\|\widetilde h\|_2^2
>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\widetilde h\in\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\). Applying (6)--(8) to this separator shows that the compatible values contain
\[
\left(
\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)-\varepsilon|\widetilde h^{\top}b|,
\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)+\varepsilon|\widetilde h^{\top}b|
\right),
\tag{11}
\]
which is a nondegenerate open interval around the baseline value. This proves the arbitrary-baseline assertion.

Here is the promised exact finite certificate. Take
\[
\mathcal E=\{e_0,e_1\},
\qquad
\mathcal U=\mathcal W=\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\{0,1\},
\]
and set
\[
\pi=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
S_{ue}=\frac12,
\qquad
a_0(u)=a_1(u)=\frac12,
\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&1/5\\1/5&4/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
q=\begin{pmatrix}3/5\\2/5\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{12}
\]
Let the baseline outcome kernel satisfy \(f_{x,y}(u,w)=1/2\) in every cell. Define an alternative kernel \(\widetilde f\) by changing only the cells with \((X,W)=(1,0)\):
\[
\widetilde f_{1,1}(0,0)=\frac9{16},
\qquad
\widetilde f_{1,0}(0,0)=\frac7{16},
\qquad
\widetilde f_{1,1}(1,0)=\frac14,
\qquad
\widetilde f_{1,0}(1,0)=\frac34,
\tag{13}
\]
with \(\widetilde f=f\) elsewhere. Both kernels are strictly positive and stochastic. All entries in (12) are strictly positive, and
\[
\det(M)=\frac35\ne0,
\tag{14}
\]
so both SCMs belong to \(\mathfrak M_{+}\).

Because both \(S_{ue}\) and \(a_1(u)\) are constant in \(u\), the posterior distribution of \(U\) given \((E=e,X=1)\) is \((1/2,1/2)^{\top}\) for either environment. Therefore
\[
B_1
=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b=Mq
=\begin{pmatrix}14/25\\11/25\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{15}
\]
The coordinates of every vector in \(\operatorname{col}(B_1)\) are equal, whereas the two coordinates of \(b\) are unequal; hence \(b\notin\operatorname{col}(B_1)\).

It remains to check the complete supplied laws. Every source cell other than those with \((W,X)=(0,1)\) is unchanged immediately. For each \(e\in\mathcal E\), Assumption~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization} gives the two possibly changed cells as
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_O(e,0,1,1)
&=\frac18\sum_{u=0}^1M_{0u}f_{1,1}(u,0),\\
P_O(e,0,1,0)
&=\frac18\sum_{u=0}^1M_{0u}f_{1,0}(u,0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Under the baseline, each sum on the right of (16) equals \(1/2\). Under the alternative, the corresponding sums are
\[
\frac45\frac9{16}+\frac15\frac14=\frac12,
\qquad
\frac45\frac7{16}+\frac15\frac34=\frac12.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus every one of the \(2^4=16\) observed source cells is identical under the two SCMs. Their supplied target proxy laws are also identical because both retain the same \(M\) and \(q\), and hence the same \(b\) in (15).

Finally, Assumption~\ref{ass:target-mechanism-invariance} gives the baseline intervention probability \(\theta_{1,1}=1/2\). The alternative differs only through the two cells in (13), so its change is
\[
\frac35\frac45\left(\frac9{16}-\frac12\right)
+\frac25\frac15\left(\frac14-\frac12\right)
=\frac1{100}.
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently its intervention probability is \(51/100\). Equations (12)--(18) therefore give two strictly positive, full-column-rank SCMs that agree on the entire supplied source law and target proxy law but disagree on the causal intervention law, concretely certifying the off-span obstruction.
