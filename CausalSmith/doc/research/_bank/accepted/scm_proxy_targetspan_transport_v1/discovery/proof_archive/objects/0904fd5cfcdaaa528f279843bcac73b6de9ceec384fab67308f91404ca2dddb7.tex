The following two parts are proved, but they do not complete the requested studentized construction.

First, define
\[
r_{s,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2L/\alpha)}{2n_s(n)}},
\qquad
r_{t,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2L/\alpha)}{2n_t(n)}}
\]
and let \(\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}_{x,y,n,1-\alpha}\) be the row-indexed version of Definition~\ref{def:concentration-projection-set}. Fix \(n\ge2\) and \(\mathbb M\in\mathcal A_p\). Assumption~\ref{ass:array-target-span} gives \(b_n\in\operatorname{col}(B_{x,n})\). Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample-projection-coverage} to the row model \(\mathcal M_n\), with sample sizes \(n_s(n)\) and \(n_t(n)\), gives
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M_n}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\left\{\theta_{x,y,n}\in
\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}_{x,y,n,1-\alpha}\right\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{1}
\]
The lower bound is independent of the row model, its rank, its nonzero singular values, and the norm of a balancing solution. Consequently,
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb M\in\mathcal A_p}
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M_n}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\left\{\theta_{x,y,n}\in
\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}_{x,y,n,1-\alpha}\right\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
This is uniform finite-sample coverage for the concentration projection, not a studentized calibration or a shrinking-set claim.

Second, the regular Wald comparator is centered at the causal target without a separate outcome-row assumption. Fix \(\mathcal M\in\mathcal R_{r_0,c,\eta}\), put \(p_x(e)=P_O(E=e,X=x)>0\), and let \(D_x=\operatorname{diag}(p_x(e):e\in\mathcal E)\). Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} gives
\[
H_x=MR_xD_x,
\qquad
z_{x,y}=D_xR_x^{\top}g_{x,y}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(M\) has full column rank, \(M^{\top}:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^r\) is surjective. Choose \(h\in\mathbb R^m\) with \(M^{\top}h=g_{x,y}\). Then (3) implies
\[
H_x^{\top}h=D_xR_x^{\top}M^{\top}h
=D_xR_x^{\top}g_{x,y}=z_{x,y},
\tag{4}
\]
so \(z_{x,y}\in\operatorname{row}(H_x)\) automatically. The defining target-span condition for \(\mathcal R_{r_0,c,\eta}\) gives \(b\in\operatorname{col}(H_x)\). Since \(D_x\) is invertible and \(H_x=B_xD_x\), this is equivalent to \(b\in\operatorname{col}(B_x)\). Also \(\operatorname{rank}(H_x)=r_0\), so the rank-\(r_0\) truncation obeys \((H_x)_{r_0}=H_x\). The Moore--Penrose solution \(\kappa_0=H_x^{\dagger}b\) satisfies \(H_x\kappa_0=b\), and \(\lambda_0=D_x\kappa_0\) satisfies \(B_x\lambda_0=b\). Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} and Theorem~\ref{thm:target-span-iff} therefore yield
\[
\phi_{r_0}(H_x,z_{x,y},b)
=z_{x,y}^{\top}H_x^{\dagger}b
=z_{x,y}^{\top}\kappa_0
=\theta_{x,y}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus automatic row membership together with \(b\in\operatorname{col}(H_x)\) centers the unchanged selector-based Wald comparator at the causal target.

Finally, \(\operatorname{rank}(H_x)=r_0\) and \(\sigma_{r_0}(H_x)\ge c\) give \(\sigma_{r_0+1}(H_x)=0\) and a strict population cutoff gap. Put \(\delta_n=\lVert\widehat H_{x,n}-H_x\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\). On the fixed finite cell space, row-wise i.i.d. sampling and the allocation limit imply \(\delta_n\to_P0\). The variational characterization of singular values gives, on \(\{\delta_n<c/2\}\),
\[
\sigma_{r_0}(\widehat H_{x,n})\ge c-\delta_n>c/2,
\qquad
\sigma_{r_0+1}(\widehat H_{x,n})\le\delta_n<c/2,
\tag{6}
\]
where the second inequality is replaced by the convention \(\sigma_{r_0+1}=0\) when \(r_0=\min(m,\lvert\mathcal E\rvert)\). Hence
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\left\{\sigma_{r_0}(\widehat H_{x,n})>
\sigma_{r_0+1}(\widehat H_{x,n})\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{7}
\]
The fixed selector and zero-variance fallback are therefore asymptotically inactive on each fixed regular branch. Equations (2), (5), and (7) do not construct a single studentized inversion satisfying both the array-uniform coverage requirement and the two regular-branch equivalences.
