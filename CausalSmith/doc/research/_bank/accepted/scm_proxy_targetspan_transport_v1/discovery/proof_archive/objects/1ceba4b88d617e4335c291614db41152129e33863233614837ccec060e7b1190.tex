Fix a baseline \(\mathcal M=(\pi,S,M,a,f,q)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal D)\), an outcome value \(y\in\mathcal Y\), and \(h\in\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\). By Definition~\ref{def:failure-separator},
\[
h^{\top}B_x=0,
\qquad
h^{\top}b\ne0.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(v=M^{\top}h\), as in Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation}. Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} and (1) give
\[
v^{\top}R_x=h^{\top}MR_x=h^{\top}B_x=0,
\qquad
v^{\top}q=h^{\top}Mq=h^{\top}b\ne0.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(v\ne0\).

Fix any \(w_{\star}\in\mathcal W\) and any \(y^{\circ}\in\mathcal Y\setminus\{y\}\). These choices exist because \(\mathcal W\) is nonempty and \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\ge2\). Assumption~\ref{ass:strict-primitive-positivity} gives \(M_{w_{\star}u}>0\), \(f_{x,y}(u,w_{\star})>0\), and \(f_{x,y^{\circ}}(u,w_{\star})>0\) for every \(u\in\mathcal U\). Since \(\mathcal U\) is finite and \(v\ne0\), the number
\[
\varepsilon
=\frac12\min_{u:v_u\ne0}
\frac{M_{w_{\star}u}\min\{f_{x,y}(u,w_{\star}),
f_{x,y^{\circ}}(u,w_{\star})\}}{|v_u|}
\tag{3}
\]
is well defined and strictly positive. For \(|t|<\varepsilon\), Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation} changes the two displayed outcome probabilities by opposite amounts whose absolute values are strictly smaller than half of the corresponding original probabilities. Thus the changed probabilities remain strictly positive. Every other outcome probability is unchanged, and the opposite changes preserve the sum over \(\mathcal Y\). Hence \(f^{(t)}\) is a strictly positive stochastic kernel. All other primitive kernels and the full-column rank of \(M\) are unchanged, so Definition~\ref{def:positive-latent-shift-class} implies \(\mathcal M_t\in\mathfrak M_{+}\).

It remains to verify compatibility with the entire supplied law, rather than only with its balancing moments. For the only possibly changed source cell with outcome \(y\), Assumption~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization} and Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation} give, for each \(e\in\mathcal E\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_{O,t}(e,w_{\star},x,y)-P_O(e,w_{\star},x,y)\\
&\quad=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\pi_eS_{ue}M_{w_{\star}u}a_x(u)
\left(t\frac{v_u}{M_{w_{\star}u}}\right)\\
&\quad=t\pi_e\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}S_{ue}a_x(u)v_u\\
&\quad=t\pi_e\rho_x(e)\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}R_x(u,e)v_u
=0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
where division by \(M_{w_{\star}u}\) is valid by Assumption~\ref{ass:strict-primitive-positivity}. The same assumption and the definition of \(\rho_x\) imply \(\rho_x(e)=\sum_u a_x(u)S_{ue}>0\), so the definition of \(R_x\) justifies the penultimate equality; the last equality is (2). The change for outcome \(y^{\circ}\) is the negative of (4), hence is also zero. Cells with another outcome, another proxy value, or another treatment value are unchanged by Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation}. Therefore every cell of \(P_O\) is preserved. Moreover, \(M\) and \(q\) are fixed, so Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} gives \(b_t=Mq=b\). Definition~\ref{def:compatible-fiber} now yields
\[
\mathcal M_t\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal D)
\qquad (|t|<\varepsilon).
\tag{5}
\]

For the causal query, the perturbation changes \(g_{x,y}(u)\) by
\[
M_{w_{\star}u}\left(t\frac{v_u}{M_{w_{\star}u}}\right)=tv_u.
\tag{6}
\]
The interventional identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization}, followed by (2), therefore yields
\[
\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M_t)-\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)
=tq^{\top}v
=t h^{\top}Mq
=t h^{\top}b.
\tag{7}
\]
The slope in (7) is nonzero by (1).

For completeness, the off-span hypothesis also guarantees that separators exist. Every column of \(B_x\) is orthogonal to \(\ker(B_x^{\top})\), so \(\operatorname{col}(B_x)\subseteq(\ker(B_x^{\top}))^{\perp}\). Rank--nullity gives
\[
\dim (\ker(B_x^{\top}))^{\perp}
=m-\dim\ker(B_x^{\top})
=\operatorname{rank}(B_x^{\top})
=\operatorname{rank}(B_x)
=\dim\operatorname{col}(B_x),
\]
and hence the inclusion is equality. If every \(h\in\ker(B_x^{\top})\) obeyed \(h^{\top}b=0\), then \(b\) would lie in \((\ker(B_x^{\top}))^{\perp}=\operatorname{col}(B_x)\), contrary to the hypothesis. Thus \(\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\ne\varnothing\). For every specified \(h\in\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\), equations (5)--(7) show that the compatible values contain
\[
\bigl(\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)-\varepsilon|h^{\top}b|,
      \theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)+\varepsilon|h^{\top}b|\bigr),
\tag{8}
\]
which is a nondegenerate open interval around the baseline value.

Here is an exact finite certificate of the obstruction. Take
\[
\mathcal E=\{e_0,e_1\},
\qquad
\mathcal U=\mathcal W=\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\{0,1\},
\]
and set
\[
\pi=(1/2,1/2),
\qquad
S_{ue}=1/2,
\qquad
a_0(u)=a_1(u)=1/2,
\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&1/5\\[2pt]1/5&4/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
q=\begin{pmatrix}3/5\\[2pt]2/5\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let the baseline outcome kernel satisfy \(f_{x,0}(u,w)=f_{x,1}(u,w)=1/2\) in every cell. Define an alternative kernel \(\widetilde f\) to equal \(f\) except when \((x,w)=(1,0)\), where
\[
\widetilde f_{1,1}(0,0)=\frac9{16},
\qquad
\widetilde f_{1,0}(0,0)=\frac7{16},
\qquad
\widetilde f_{1,1}(1,0)=\frac14,
\qquad
\widetilde f_{1,0}(1,0)=\frac34.
\tag{10}
\]
Define the source and target laws of the baseline and alternative SCMs by Assumptions~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization} and~\ref{ass:target-mechanism-invariance}, respectively. All primitive probabilities in (9)--(10) are strictly positive, every displayed kernel is stochastic, and
\[
\det(M)=\frac{16}{25}-\frac1{25}=\frac35\ne0.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus both constructions belong to \(\mathfrak M_{+}\) by the two factorization assumptions, Assumption~\ref{ass:strict-primitive-positivity}, Assumption~\ref{ass:proxy-channel-injectivity}, and Definition~\ref{def:positive-latent-shift-class}.

Because \(S_{ue}\) and \(a_1(u)\) do not depend on \(u\), the latent posterior at treatment value one is uniform. Hence, for both \(e_0\) and \(e_1\),
\[
B_1(\,cdot\,,e)
=M\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\[2pt]1/2\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\[2pt]1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{12}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
b=Mq
=\begin{pmatrix}
(4/5)(3/5)+(1/5)(2/5)\\[2pt]
(1/5)(3/5)+(4/5)(2/5)
\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}14/25\\[2pt]11/25\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{13}
\]
The column space in (12) consists of vectors with equal coordinates, whereas the coordinates in (13) differ. Therefore \(b\notin\operatorname{col}(B_1)\).

We next check the complete observed source law. By Assumption~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization}, under either kernel,
\[
P_O(e,w,x,y)=\frac18\sum_{u=0}^1 M_{wu}f_{x,y}(u,w),
\tag{14}
\]
with \(f\) replaced by \(\widetilde f\) for the alternative model. Every cell not listed in (10) agrees immediately. For the only potentially changed cells, direct calculation gives
\[
\frac45\frac9{16}+\frac15\frac14=\frac12,
\qquad
\frac45\frac7{16}+\frac15\frac34=\frac12.
\tag{15}
\]
The corresponding baseline sums are also \((4/5)(1/2)+(1/5)(1/2)=1/2\). Thus (14)--(15) show equality for both environments and both outcome values at \((w,x)=(0,1)\); all remaining cells were unchanged. Since each row of the matrix in (9) sums to one, (14) also shows that every one of the sixteen source observed cells equals \(1/16\) in each model. Equation (13) shows separately that their supplied target proxy vectors agree. The two SCMs therefore induce exactly the same pair \(\mathcal D=(P_O,b)\).

Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} gives baseline target effect \(\theta_{1,1}=1/2\), whereas the alternative has
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\theta_{1,1}
&=\frac35\left(\frac45\frac9{16}+\frac15\frac12\right)
 +\frac25\left(\frac15\frac14+\frac45\frac12\right)\\
&=\frac35\frac{11}{20}+\frac25\frac9{20}
=\frac{51}{100}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Thus this strictly positive, full-column-rank pair agrees on the entire supplied source law and the supplied target proxy law but differs on the intervention law, exactly as asserted.
