Fix a baseline \(\mathcal M=(\pi,S,M,a,f,q)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal D)\), an outcome value \(y\in\mathcal Y\), and \(h\in\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\).  Definition~\ref{def:failure-separator} gives
\[
h^{\top}B_x=0,
\qquad
h^{\top}b\ne0.
\tag{1}
\]
Put \(v=M^{\top}h\), as in Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} and (1) imply
\[
v^{\top}R_x=h^{\top}MR_x=h^{\top}B_x=0,
\qquad
v^{\top}q=h^{\top}Mq=h^{\top}b\ne0.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(v\ne0\).

Choose any \(w_{\star}\in\mathcal W\) and any \(y^{\circ}\in\mathcal Y\setminus\{y\}\).  These choices exist because \(\mathcal W\) is nonempty and \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\ge2\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:strict-primitive-positivity} gives \(M_{w_{\star}u}>0\), \(f_{x,y}(u,w_{\star})>0\), and \(f_{x,y^{\circ}}(u,w_{\star})>0\) for every \(u\).  Since \(\mathcal U\) is finite and \(v\ne0\),
\[
\varepsilon
=\frac12\min_{u:v_u\ne0}
\frac{M_{w_{\star}u}\min\{f_{x,y}(u,w_{\star}),
f_{x,y^{\circ}}(u,w_{\star})\}}{|v_u|}
\tag{3}
\]
is well defined and strictly positive.  If \(|t|<\varepsilon\), Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation} changes the two displayed outcome probabilities by opposite amounts whose magnitudes are less than half the corresponding original probabilities.  Both remain strictly positive, all other outcome probabilities remain unchanged, and normalization is preserved.  Hence \(f^{(t)}\) is a strictly positive stochastic kernel.  The other primitive kernels and the full-column rank of \(M\) are unchanged, so \(\mathcal M_t\in\mathfrak M_{+}\).

We verify compatibility with the full observed law, rather than merely with its balancing moments.  For a possibly changed source cell with outcome \(y\), the latent-shift factorization and Definition~\ref{def:outcome-null-perturbation} give, for every \(e\in\mathcal E\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_{O,t}(e,w_{\star},x,y)-P_O(e,w_{\star},x,y)\\
&\quad=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\pi_eS_{ue}M_{w_{\star}u}a_x(u)
\left(t\frac{v_u}{M_{w_{\star}u}}\right)\\
&\quad=t\pi_e\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}S_{ue}a_x(u)v_u\\
&\quad=t\pi_e\rho_x(e)\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}R_x(u,e)v_u
=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Division is valid by strict positivity; also \(\rho_x(e)>0\), so the definition of \(R_x\) justifies the penultimate equality, and (2) gives the last.  The change for outcome \(y^{\circ}\) is the negative of (4), hence also zero.  Cells with another outcome, proxy value, or treatment value are unchanged.  Thus every cell of \(P_O\) is preserved.  Moreover, \(M\) and \(q\) are fixed, so Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} gives \(b_t=Mq=b\).  Definition~\ref{def:compatible-fiber} now yields
\[
\mathcal M_t\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal D)
\qquad(|t|<\varepsilon).
\tag{5}
\]

The perturbation changes \(g_{x,y}(u)\) by
\[
M_{w_{\star}u}\left(t\frac{v_u}{M_{w_{\star}u}}\right)=tv_u.
\tag{6}
\]
The interventional identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} and (2) therefore imply
\[
\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M_t)-\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)
=tq^{\top}v
=t h^{\top}Mq
=t h^{\top}b.
\tag{7}
\]
The slope is nonzero by (1).

It remains only to show that the off-span hypothesis produces a separator.  Finite-dimensional rank--nullity gives
\[
\operatorname{col}(B_x)=\bigl(\ker(B_x^{\top})\bigr)^{\perp}.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, the inclusion from left to right is immediate, while both sides have dimension \(\operatorname{rank}(B_x)\).  If every \(h\in\ker(B_x^{\top})\) obeyed \(h^{\top}b=0\), then (8) would imply \(b\in\operatorname{col}(B_x)\), contrary to hypothesis.  Hence \(\mathcal H_x(\mathcal D)\ne\varnothing\).  For each specified separator, (5)--(7) show that the compatible values contain
\[
\bigl(\theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)-\varepsilon|h^{\top}b|,
      \theta_{x,y}(\mathcal M)+\varepsilon|h^{\top}b|\bigr),
\tag{9}
\]
a nondegenerate open interval.

For an exact smallest-instance stress test, take
\[
\mathcal E=\{e_0,e_1\},
\qquad
\mathcal U=\mathcal W=\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\{0,1\},
\]
and
\[
\pi=(1/2,1/2),\quad S_{ue}=1/2,\quad
a_0(u)=a_1(u)=1/2,\quad
M=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&1/5\\1/5&4/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
q=\begin{pmatrix}3/5\\2/5\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
Let the baseline kernel have \(f_{x,0}(u,w)=f_{x,1}(u,w)=1/2\) everywhere.  Let \(\widetilde f=f\) except at \((x,w)=(1,0)\), where
\[
\widetilde f_{1,1}(0,0)=\frac9{16},\quad
\widetilde f_{1,0}(0,0)=\frac7{16},\quad
\widetilde f_{1,1}(1,0)=\frac14,\quad
\widetilde f_{1,0}(1,0)=\frac34.
\tag{11}
\]
All primitives are strictly positive and stochastic, and \(\det(M)=3/5\ne0\).  The latent posterior for treatment one is uniform, so every column of \(B_1\) equals \((1/2,1/2)^{\top}\), whereas
\[
b=Mq=(14/25,11/25)^{\top}\notin\operatorname{col}(B_1).
\tag{12}
\]
Under either kernel,
\[
P_O(e,w,x,y)=\frac18\sum_{u=0}^1M_{wu}f_{x,y}(u,w).
\tag{13}
\]
The only nonautomatic alternative sums are
\[
\frac45\frac9{16}+\frac15\frac14=\frac12,
\qquad
\frac45\frac7{16}+\frac15\frac34=\frac12,
\tag{14}
\]
which equal the baseline sums.  Thus all sixteen observed source cells equal \(1/16\) in both SCMs, and (12) shows that their target proxy laws also agree.  Nevertheless, the baseline effect is \(1/2\), while
\[
\widetilde\theta_{1,1}
=\frac35\left(\frac45\frac9{16}+\frac15\frac12\right)
 +\frac25\left(\frac15\frac14+\frac45\frac12\right)
=\frac{51}{100}.
\tag{15}
\]
This verifies directly that full-law agreement need not determine the intervention law off the span.
