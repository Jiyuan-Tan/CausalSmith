Take \(\mathcal E=\{e_0,e_1\}\) and \(\mathcal U=\mathcal W=\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\{0,1\}\). Put
\[
\pi=(1/2,1/2),\qquad
S_{ue}=1/2,\qquad
a_0(u)=a_1(u)=1/2,
\]
for every \(u,e\), and let
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&1/5\\1/5&4/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
q=(3/5,2/5)^{\top}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\mathcal M_0\) have \(f^{(0)}_{x,y}(u,w)=1/2\) in every cell. Let \(\mathcal M_1\) share all other primitives and all other outcome cells, but set
\[
f^{(1)}_{1,1}(0,0)=\frac9{16},\quad
f^{(1)}_{1,1}(1,0)=\frac14,\quad
f^{(1)}_{1,0}(0,0)=\frac7{16},\quad
f^{(1)}_{1,0}(1,0)=\frac34.
\tag{2}
\]
The two entries in (2) for each fixed latent state sum to one, and all outcome probabilities in both models belong to \((0,1)\). Every entry of \(\pi,S,a,M,q\) is strictly positive and each displayed primitive is stochastic. Moreover,
\[
\det(M)=\frac{16}{25}-\frac1{25}=\frac35\ne0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(M\) has full column rank \(r=2\). Defining the source and target laws by the factorizations in Assumptions~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization} and~\ref{ass:target-mechanism-invariance}, respectively, proves \(\mathcal M_0,\mathcal M_1\in\mathfrak M_{+}\).

For treatment one, \(\rho_1(e)=1/2\), so
\[
R_1(0,e)=R_1(1,e)=\frac12
\qquad(e\in\mathcal E).
\tag{4}
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization} and (1)--(4) therefore give, in both models,
\[
B_1=MR_1=
\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b=Mq=(14/25,11/25)^{\top}.
\tag{5}
\]
The column space in (5) consists of scalar multiples of \((1,1)^{\top}\), whereas the two coordinates of \(b\) differ. Hence \(b\notin\operatorname{col}(B_1)\).

We next check the entire source observed law. Only cells with \((W,X)=(0,1)\) can change. For either \(e\in\mathcal E\), Assumption~\ref{ass:latent-shift-factorization} gives for the \(Y=1\) cell
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_{O,1}(e,0,1,1)-P_{O,0}(e,0,1,1)\\
&\quad=\sum_{u=0}^1 \pi_eS_{ue}M_{0u}a_1(u)
 \bigl(f^{(1)}_{1,1}(u,0)-f^{(0)}_{1,1}(u,0)\bigr)\\
&\quad=\frac18\left\{\frac45\left(\frac1{16}\right)
 +\frac15\left(-\frac14\right)\right\}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The change in the corresponding \(Y=0\) cell is the negative of (6), hence is also zero. Every cell with another proxy value or treatment value is unchanged by construction. Therefore \(P_{O,0}=P_{O,1}\) cell by cell. The proxy channel and target latent law are common, so (5) also shows that the target proxy law is common. Thus the two SCMs induce the same \(\mathcal D=(P_O,b)\).

Finally, the definition of \(g_{x,y}\) gives
\[
g^{(0)}_{1,1}=(1/2,1/2)^{\top},
\qquad
g^{(1)}_{1,1}=(11/20,9/20)^{\top}.
\tag{7}
\]
Using the target intervention identity \(\theta_{x,y}=g_{x,y}^{\top}q\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-factorization},
\[
\theta_{1,1}(\mathcal M_0)=\frac12,
\qquad
\theta_{1,1}(\mathcal M_1)
=\frac35\frac{11}{20}+\frac25\frac9{20}
=\frac{51}{100}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (5)--(8) give the required strictly positive full-observed-law countermodel pair.
