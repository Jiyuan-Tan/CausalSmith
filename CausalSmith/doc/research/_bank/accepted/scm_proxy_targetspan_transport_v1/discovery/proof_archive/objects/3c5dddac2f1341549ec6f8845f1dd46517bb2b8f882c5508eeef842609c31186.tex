The uniformly valid nonstudentized part is complete. Define
\[
L=m\lvert\mathcal E\rvert+\lvert\mathcal E\rvert+m,
\qquad
r_{s,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2L/\alpha)}{2n_s(n)}},
\qquad
r_{t,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2L/\alpha)}{2n_t(n)}}
\]
and
\[
\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}_{x,y,n,1-\alpha}
=\left\{\vartheta\in[0,1]:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there exists }\kappa\in\mathbb R^{\lvert\mathcal E\rvert}\text{ such that}\\[2pt]
\lVert\widehat H_{x,n}\kappa-\widehat b_n\rVert_{\infty}
\le r_{s,n}\lVert\kappa\rVert_1+r_{t,n},\\[2pt]
\lvert\widehat z_{x,y,n}^{\top}\kappa-\vartheta\rvert
\le r_{s,n}\lVert\kappa\rVert_1
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Fix \(n\ge2\) and \(\mathbb M\in\mathcal A_p\). Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}array\text{-}target\text{-}span}\) gives \(b_n\in\operatorname{col}(B_{x,n})\), and the row model belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{+}\) by the definition of \(\mathbb M\). Apply Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}sample\text{-}projection\text{-}coverage}\) to \(\mathcal M_n\), with sample sizes \(n_s(n)\) and \(n_t(n)\). Its confidence set is exactly the display above after adding the row index. Therefore
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M_n}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\left\{\theta_{x,y,n}\in
\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}_{x,y,n,1-\alpha}\right\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The right-hand side is independent of \(\mathbb M\), of the rank and nonzero singular values of \(B_{x,n}\), and of the norm of a balancing solution. Hence, for every \(n\),
\[
\inf_{\mathbb M\in\mathcal A_p}
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M_n}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\left\{\theta_{x,y,n}\in
\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}_{x,y,n,1-\alpha}\right\}\ge1-\alpha,
\]
and taking \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\) proves the requested uniform coverage inequality for \(\widehat C^{\mathrm{conc}}\).

The repaired regular benchmark is determinate. Let \(\bar q=\min(m,\lvert\mathcal E\rvert)\) and adopt \(\sigma_{\bar q+1}(H)=0\). For a fixed \(\mathcal M\in\mathcal R_{r_0,c,\eta}\), Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass{:}regular\text{-}fixed\text{-}rank}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}regular\text{-}singular\text{-}gap}\) give
\[
\operatorname{rank}(H_x)=r_0,
\qquad
\sigma_{r_0}(H_x)\ge c,
\qquad
\sigma_{r_0+1}(H_x)=0.
\]
Thus \(\sigma_{r_0}(H_x)>\sigma_{r_0+1}(H_x)\), the retained subspace is unique, and the population value of \(\phi_{r_0}\) is independent of the fixed selector. To see directly that the empirical fallback is asymptotically inactive, put \(\delta_n=\lVert\widehat H_{x,n}-H_x\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\). Row-wise i.i.d. sampling on the fixed finite cell space gives entrywise convergence in probability, hence \(\delta_n\to_P0\). On \(\{\delta_n<c/2\}\), the variational definition of ordered singular values gives
\[
\sigma_{r_0}(\widehat H_{x,n})
\ge \sigma_{r_0}(H_x)-\delta_n
\ge c-\delta_n>c/2.
\]
If \(r_0<\bar q\), choosing the null space of \(H_x\) in the complementary variational formula gives
\[
\sigma_{r_0+1}(\widehat H_{x,n})\le\delta_n<c/2;
\]
if \(r_0=\bar q\), the same conclusion is \(\sigma_{r_0+1}(\widehat H_{x,n})=0\) by convention. Consequently
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\left\{\sigma_{r_0}(\widehat H_{x,n})>
\sigma_{r_0+1}(\widehat H_{x,n})\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
The tied-cutoff selector and the zero fallback therefore do not alter the first-order fixed-regular benchmark. What remains unproved is the existence of one computable studentized inversion that has uniform coverage over all of \(\mathcal A_p\) and is simultaneously root-\(n\) shrinking and first-order Hausdorff-equivalent to the Wald interval on every fixed \(\mathcal R_{r_0,c,\eta}\) branch.
