We give an explicit two-point-sequence obstruction and verify every support, rank, span, and limiting condition used below.

Let
\[
\mathcal E=\{-1,+1\},\qquad
\mathcal U=\mathcal W=\mathcal Y=\{0,1\},\qquad
\mathcal X=\{x\}.
\]
Vectors over a binary state space use the order \((1,0)\); columns indexed by the environment use the order \((+1,-1)\), and columns of \(M\) use the latent order \((1,0)\). Define a baseline model \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\) by
\[
\pi=(1/2,1/2),\qquad
S^{(0)}=
\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M^{(0)}=
\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
a_x(u)=1,\qquad q^{(0)}=(1/2,1/2)^{\top},\qquad
f^{(0)}_{x,1}(u,w)=f^{(0)}_{x,0}(u,w)=1/2.
\]
Every primitive entry is strictly positive, every displayed kernel is stochastic, and \(\det M^{(0)}=1/2\ne0\). Thus the latent-shift factorization, target-mechanism invariance, strict primitive positivity, and proxy-channel injectivity assumptions imply \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\in\mathfrak M_{+}\). Directly summing the factorization over \(U\) gives, for every \(e,w,y\),
\[
P_{O,0}(e,w,x,y)=\frac18,\qquad
b_0=(1/2,1/2)^{\top},\qquad
\theta_{x,1,0}=\frac12.
\]
Consequently,
\[
H_{x,0}=
\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\1/4&1/4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
z_{x,1,0}=(1/4,1/4)^{\top}.
\]
The matrix \(H_{x,0}\) has rank one and singular values \(1/2\) and \(0\), while \(b_0=H_{x,0}(1,1)^{\top}\). Hence the fixed-rank, singular-gap, and target-span conditions hold with \(r_0=1\) and \(c=1/4\). All source observed cells equal \(1/8\), and all target proxy cells equal \(1/2\), so the cell-floor condition holds with \(\eta=1/16\).

The positive-variance condition also holds with this \(\eta\). At the baseline point,
\[
H_{x,0}^{\dagger}=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_z\phi_1(H_{x,0},z_{x,1,0},b_0)
=\bigl(H_{x,0}^{\dagger}b_0\bigr)^{\top}=(1,1).
\]
Therefore the source contribution to the scalar delta-method influence function has the form \(\mathbf 1\{Y=1\}+\psi(E,W)\), up to an irrelevant centering constant, for a function \(\psi\) contributed by the \(H\)-coordinates. Under \(P_{O,0}\), conditional on \((E,W)\), the variable \(Y\) is fair and independent of \((E,W)\), because the baseline outcome kernel is identically \(1/2\). Expanding the variance by finite conditional sums gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{P_{O,0}}\!\bigl(\mathbf 1\{Y=1\}+\psi(E,W)\bigr)
=\mathbb E_{P_{O,0}}\!\left[
\operatorname{Var}_{P_{O,0}}\!\bigl(\mathbf 1\{Y=1\}\mid E,W\bigr)
\right]
+\operatorname{Var}_{P_{O,0}}\!\left(
\mathbb E_{P_{O,0}}[\mathbf 1\{Y=1\}+\psi(E,W)\mid E,W]
\right)
\ge\frac14.
\]
The source allocation multiplies this term by \(p^{-1}\), the independent target-sample contribution is nonnegative, and hence
\[
V_{1,x,1}(\mathcal M^{(0)};p)\ge(4p)^{-1}>1/16.
\]
Thus every defining condition has been checked and
\[
\mathcal M^{(0)}\in\mathcal R_{1,1/4,1/16}.
\]

We next construct an admissible weak-rank array. Put \(\delta_n=\epsilon_n=n^{-2}\) and define \(\mathcal M^{(1)}_n\) using the same \(\pi\) and \(a_x\), but with
\[
S^{(1)}_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2+\epsilon_n&1/2-\epsilon_n\\
1/2-\epsilon_n&1/2+\epsilon_n
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M^{(1)}_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2+\delta_n&1/2-\delta_n\\
1/2-\delta_n&1/2+\delta_n
\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
q^{(1)}=(3/4,1/4)^{\top},\qquad
f^{(1)}_{x,1}(1,w)=3/4,\qquad
f^{(1)}_{x,1}(0,w)=1/4,
\qquad f^{(1)}_{x,0}=1-f^{(1)}_{x,1}.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), all entries of these kernels belong to \([1/4,3/4]\), and
\[
\det M^{(1)}_n=2\delta_n>0,
\qquad
\det S^{(1)}_n=2\epsilon_n>0.
\]
Thus every row lies in \(\mathfrak M_{+}\). Since treatment is the singleton \(x\), the conditional posterior matrix is \(R_{x,n}=S^{(1)}_n\). The observable factorization therefore gives
\[
B^{(1)}_{x,n}=M^{(1)}_nS^{(1)}_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2+2\delta_n\epsilon_n&1/2-2\delta_n\epsilon_n\\
1/2-2\delta_n\epsilon_n&1/2+2\delta_n\epsilon_n
\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
H^{(1)}_{x,n}=\tfrac12B^{(1)}_{x,n},\qquad
\det B^{(1)}_{x,n}=4\delta_n\epsilon_n>0,
\qquad
b^{(1)}_n=M^{(1)}_nq^{(1)}=
\begin{pmatrix}1/2+\delta_n/2\\1/2-\delta_n/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Because \(B^{(1)}_{x,n}\) is invertible, \(b^{(1)}_n\in\operatorname{col}(B^{(1)}_{x,n})\) for every \(n\). Together with the fixed allocation and the two row-wise independent i.i.d. sampling assumptions, this proves \(\mathbb M^{(1)}=(\mathcal M^{(1)}_n)_{n\ge2}\in\mathcal A_p\). Indeed, the unique conditional balancing vector satisfies
\[
\lambda_{1,n}+\lambda_{2,n}=1,
\qquad
\lambda_{1,n}-\lambda_{2,n}=\frac{1}{4\epsilon_n},
\]
so its norm, and the norm of the corresponding unconditional weight, diverges. This array thus uses the unrestricted weak-rank portion of \(\mathcal A_p\) without violating target span.

The target causal probability in every row is separated from the baseline by a constant. Target-mechanism invariance and intervention at the singleton treatment give
\[
\theta_{x,1,n}
=\sum_{u,w}q^{(1)}_uM^{(1)}_n(w,u)f^{(1)}_{x,1}(u,w)
=\frac34\frac34+\frac14\frac14
=\frac58.
\]

We now prove that the two samples cannot distinguish this separation. For probability vectors \(P\) and \(Q\) on the same finite set, write
\[
\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\frac12\sum_a|P(a)-Q(a)|.
\]
As an auxiliary comparison law, retain \(f^{(1)}\), but replace both \(S^{(1)}_n\) and \(M^{(1)}_n\) by the all-\(1/2\) binary kernels \(S^{\star}\) and \(M^{\star}\). Its observed \((E,W,X,Y)\) marginal is exactly \(P_{O,0}\): \(E\) and \(W\) are independent and fair, and averaging the response probabilities \((3/4,1/4)\) against the fair latent variable makes \(Y\) fair. At the full-data level, adding and subtracting the density using \(S^{\star}\) and \(M^{(1)}_n\) yields
\[
\begin{split}
&\pi_eS^{(1)}_n(u,e)M^{(1)}_n(w,u)f^{(1)}_{x,y}(u,w)
-\pi_eS^{\star}(u,e)M^{\star}(w,u)f^{(1)}_{x,y}(u,w)\\
&=\pi_e\bigl(S^{(1)}_n(u,e)-S^{\star}(u,e)\bigr)
M^{(1)}_n(w,u)f^{(1)}_{x,y}(u,w)\\
&\quad+\pi_eS^{\star}(u,e)
\bigl(M^{(1)}_n(w,u)-M^{\star}(w,u)\bigr)
f^{(1)}_{x,y}(u,w).
\end{split}
\]
Summing absolute values, using stochasticity of the unchanged kernels, and dividing by two shows that the full-data total variation is at most \(\epsilon_n+\delta_n\), the sum of the two binary-kernel total variations. Marginalization only groups summands and hence cannot increase the absolute-sum bound. Therefore
\[
\lVert P^{(1)}_{O,n}-P_{O,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\epsilon_n+\delta_n=2n^{-2}.
\]
Directly from the displayed target proxy vectors,
\[
\lVert b^{(1)}_n-b_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\delta_n/2.
\]
For arbitrary finite probability vectors, inserting one tensor factor at a time gives
\[
\bigotimes_{i=1}^N P_i-\bigotimes_{i=1}^N Q_i
=\sum_{j=1}^N
\left(\bigotimes_{i<j}P_i\right)\otimes(P_j-Q_j)\otimes
\left(\bigotimes_{i>j}Q_i\right).
\]
The triangle inequality and the fact that every probability vector has \(\ell_1\)-norm one prove that product total variation is at most the sum of the coordinatewise total variations. Hence, using \(n_s(n)+n_t(n)=n\),
\[
\begin{split}
&\left\lVert
(P^{(1)}_{O,n})^{\otimes n_s(n)}\otimes(b^{(1)}_n)^{\otimes n_t(n)}
-P_{O,0}^{\otimes n_s(n)}\otimes b_0^{\otimes n_t(n)}
\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
&\qquad\le n_s(n)(\epsilon_n+\delta_n)+n_t(n)\delta_n/2
\le2n^{-1}\longrightarrow0.
\end{split}
\]

Suppose, for contradiction, that the confidence sets in the theorem satisfy both requested properties. The uniform-coverage property applied to the particular array \(\mathbb M^{(1)}\in\mathcal A_p\) gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M^{(1)}_n}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\{5/8\in\widehat C^{\mathrm{SAR}}_{x,1,n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Indeed, at each \(n\) the displayed probability is bounded below by the infimum over \(\mathcal A_p\). Total-variation convergence of the two sample laws then implies
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M^{(0)}}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\{5/8\in\widehat C^{\mathrm{SAR}}_{x,1,n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge1-\alpha>0. \tag{1}
\]

We next localize the Wald interval under the fixed baseline model. Every coordinate of \(\widehat H_{x,n}\), \(\widehat z_{x,1,n}\), and \(\widehat b_n\) is an average of binary variables. If \(\widehat\mu\) is such an average based on \(N\) observations, independence gives \(\mathbb E[(\widehat\mu-\mu)^2]\le(4N)^{-1}\), and
\[
\Pr\{|\widehat\mu-\mu|>t\}
\le t^{-2}\mathbb E[(\widehat\mu-\mu)^2]
\le(4Nt^2)^{-1}.
\]
There are finitely many coordinates, and the allocation assumption makes both sample sizes proportional to \(n\). A finite union bound therefore gives joint \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) convergence to \((H_{x,0},z_{x,1,0},b_0)\).

At \(H_{x,0}\), the retained squared singular value is \(1/4\) and the discarded one is zero. For a real \(2\times2\) matrix \(H\) near \(H_{x,0}\), the two eigenvalues of \(HH^{\top}\) are the two roots of its quadratic characteristic polynomial; the positive eigenvalue gap at \(H_{x,0}\) makes those roots, and the leading projector
\[
P_1(H)=\frac{HH^{\top}-\lambda_2(H)I}{\lambda_1(H)-\lambda_2(H)},
\]
continuously differentiable with bounded derivatives on a sufficiently small neighborhood. The rank-one truncation \(P_1(H)H\), its pseudoinverse, \(\phi_1\), and the derivative of \(\phi_1\) are consequently continuous with bounded derivatives there. The plug-in multinomial covariance is a polynomial function of empirical cell probabilities and is bounded on the finite probability simplex. The definitions of the Wald estimator and its totalized variance therefore yield
\[
\widehat\theta^{\,W}_{x,1,n}=\frac12+O_P(n^{-1/2}),
\qquad
\widehat V_n=O_P(1).
\]
Thus, after intersection with \([0,1]\),
\[
\Delta_n:=\sup_{\zeta\in I^W_{n,1-\alpha}}|\zeta-1/2|
=O_P(n^{-1/2}). \tag{2}
\]
In particular the Wald interval is nonempty with probability tending to one.

The asserted regular-branch equivalence applies because \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\in\mathcal R_{1,1/4,1/16}\), so
\[
d_H\!\left(
\widehat C^{\mathrm{SAR}}_{x,1,n,1-\alpha},I^W_{n,1-\alpha}
\right)=o_P(1). \tag{3}
\]
On the event \(5/8\in\widehat C^{\mathrm{SAR}}_{x,1,n,1-\alpha}\), whenever the Wald interval is nonempty, the definition of Hausdorff distance and the triangle inequality give
\[
\frac18
\le d_H\!\left(
\widehat C^{\mathrm{SAR}}_{x,1,n,1-\alpha},I^W_{n,1-\alpha}
\right)+\Delta_n.
\]
Equations \((2)\) and \((3)\) make the right-hand side converge to zero in probability. Hence
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathcal M^{(0)}}^{\,n_s(n),n_t(n)}
\{5/8\in\widehat C^{\mathrm{SAR}}_{x,1,n,1-\alpha}\}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
contradicting \((1)\). No confidence-set construction, studentized or otherwise, can therefore satisfy the two requirements on \(\mathcal A_p\). A root-\(n\) diameter condition cannot rescue the construction, because the contradiction already follows from the demanded Hausdorff localization.

Finally, the positive uniform-coverage assertion requires no studentized calibration. For each row of each \(\mathbb M\in\mathcal A_p\), the array target-span assumption gives \(b_n\in\operatorname{col}(B_{x,n})\). Applying the already-established finite-sample projection coverage theorem to \(\mathcal M_n\), \(n_s(n)\), and \(n_t(n)\) gives rowwise coverage at least \(1-\alpha\). This inequality holds for every array at every row, so taking the infimum over \(\mathcal A_p\) and then the lower limit preserves the bound. Thus the row-indexed concentration projection set attains uniform coverage even along changing-rank arrays. The counterexample proves why it must be allowed to remain wide near the displayed weak sequence unless separation, bounded weights, or comparable regularity is imposed.
