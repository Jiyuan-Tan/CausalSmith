Let \(K=K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}=2\lceil4L_d\rceil\).  Then \(K\) is even and
\[
 8L_d\leq K<8L_d+2.                                                    \tag{19}
\]
With \(\lambda=\lambda_{n,d}=2n/d\), the definition gives
\[
 a_{n,d}^2=\frac K{64\lambda}=\frac{Kd}{128n}<\frac K{128d}.
\]
Since \(K=O(\log d)\), a universal \(D_0\) makes this at most \(1/4\) whenever \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n>d^2\).

For \(|\theta_x|\leq a_{n,d}\leq1/2\), the Bernoulli means in \(\mathrm{def:dense-moment-matching-construction}\) lie in \([1/4,3/4]\).  The displayed completion satisfies consistency and conditional exchangeability; \(p_x=1/d\), \(\pi_x=1/2\), and fixed overlap holds because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Hence every such law belongs to \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).  Identification gives
\[
 V^\star(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})
 =\frac1d\sum_x\max\left\{\frac{1+\theta_x}{2},\frac{1-\theta_x}{2}\right\}
 =\frac12+\frac1{2d}\sum_x|\theta_x|.                               \tag{20}
\]

The existence, moment equality, absolute-moment gap, and \(E_K\asymp K^{-1}\) are exactly \(\mathrm{lem:cai-low-absolute-moment-priors}\).  Scaling those measures by \(a=a_{n,d}\) and using (20) gives
\[
 \Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}
 =\frac1{2d}\,d\,(2aE_K)=aE_K\asymp\frac aK.                        \tag{21}
\]

In the \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) experiment, Poisson splitting makes the sign counts in one cell independent with means \(\lambda(1+\theta_x)/2\) and \(\lambda(1-\theta_x)/2\).  Relative to \(\theta_x=0\), their likelihood ratio is
\[
 L_{\theta_x}=(1+\theta_x)^{N_+}(1-\theta_x)^{N_-}.
\]
The Poisson generating function therefore gives, without approximation,
\[
 E_0(L_{\theta_x}L_{\theta_x'})
 =\exp(\lambda\theta_x\theta_x').                                   \tag{22}
\]
If \(h_0,h_1\) are the one-cell mixtures under the two scaled priors and \(h_\circ\) is the null density, expansion of (22) yields
\[
 \int\frac{(h_1-h_0)^2}{h_\circ}
 =\sum_{r=0}^\infty\frac{\lambda^r}{r!}
   \{E_1\theta_x^r-E_0\theta_x^r\}^2
 \leq4\sum_{r=K+1}^\infty\frac{(\lambda a^2)^r}{r!},               \tag{23}
\]
because the moments through \(K\) cancel and each remaining moment difference is at most \(2a^r\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives one-cell total variation at most the square root of the tail in (23).  Product-mixture independence over cells and the telescoping inequality for product measures then give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\mathbb M_{0,n,d},\mathbb M_{1,n,d})
 \leq d\left\{\sum_{r=K+1}^\infty\frac{(\lambda a^2)^r}{r!}\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{24}
\]
Here \(\lambda a^2=K/64\), and \(r!\geq(r/e)^r\) implies, for \(r\geq K+1\),
\[
 \frac{(\lambda a^2)^r}{r!}\leq(e/64)^r.
\]
Thus (24) is bounded by
\[
 d\left\{\frac{(e/64)^{K+1}}{1-e/64}\right\}^{1/2}\leq\frac1{16}  \tag{25}
\]
after increasing the universal \(D_0\), using \(K\geq8\log(ed)\).

Under either product prior, independence and (20) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{V^\star(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}
 =\frac1{4d^2}\sum_x\operatorname{Var}(|\theta_x|)
 \leq\frac{a^2}{4d}.                                                   \tag{26}
\]
By (21), Chebyshev's inequality, derived from Markov's inequality applied to the squared centered target, bounds each prior probability outside a radius \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}/4\) by
\[
 \frac{16(a^2/4d)}{a^2E_K^2}=\frac4{dE_K^2}\leq C\frac{K^2}{d}\leq\frac18 \tag{27}
\]
for the same sufficiently large \(D_0\).

Let \(r\) be the larger integrated squared risk of an arbitrary estimator under the two priors, and test the prior index by thresholding the estimator at the midpoint of the prior target means.  On the event in (27), estimation error smaller than \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}/4\) forces a correct test.  Hence the sum of the two testing errors is at most
\[
 \frac14+\frac{32r}{(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}})^2}.
\]
For every test that sum is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb M_0,\mathbb M_1)\geq15/16\); this follows directly by integrating the two error indicators against the common dominating measure.  Therefore
\[
 r\geq c(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}})^2
 \geq c\frac{a^2}{K^2}=c\frac1{\lambda K}=c\frac d{nK}.             \tag{28}
\]
The priors are supported on \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\), so (28) lower-bounds \(\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{2n,d,\epsilon}\).

Finally take any fixed-\(n\) estimator, clipped to \([0,1]\) without increasing risk, and apply it to the first \(n\) observations of a \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) sample when at least \(n\) observations are present, outputting zero otherwise.  Its Poisson risk is at most its fixed-sample risk plus \(\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\).  The exponential Markov bound gives the latter probability at most \(e^{-n/4}\).  Taking infima and using (19),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c\frac d{nL_d}-e^{-n/4}
 \geq c'\frac d{nL_d},                                                \tag{29}
\]
where the last inequality holds uniformly over \(d\geq D_0,n>d^2\) after one further increase of \(D_0\).  This proves every assertion of the lemma.
