By \(\mathrm{thm:jackson-factorial-upper}\), the displayed Jackson--factorial estimator has worst-case risk at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\).  Since \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is the infimum of worst-case risks over all measurable estimators by \(\mathrm{def:minimax-risk}\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_P[(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-V^\star(P))^2].
\]
The reverse comparison with \(c_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\) is \(\mathrm{thm:all-estimator-lower}\).  Concatenating these three inequalities proves the theorem for every \(n\geq1,d\geq2\).
