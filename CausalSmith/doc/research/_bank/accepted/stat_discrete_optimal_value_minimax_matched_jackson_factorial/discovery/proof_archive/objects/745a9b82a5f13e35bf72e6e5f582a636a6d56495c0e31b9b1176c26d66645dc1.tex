Choose the universal cutoff \(D_0\) sufficiently large.  For every \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n>d^2\), the construction in \(\mathrm{def:dense-moment-matching-construction}\) has even degree \(8L_d\leq K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}<8L_d+2\), amplitude \(a_{n,d}^2=K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}d/(128n)\leq1/4\), and, for every \(\theta\in[-a_{n,d},a_{n,d}]^d\), an observed margin \(\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) satisfying \(p_x=1/d\), \(\pi_x=1/2\), \(\mu_{1x}=(1+\theta_x)/2\), \(\mu_{0x}=(1-\theta_x)/2\), and \(\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}=1/2+(2d)^{-1}\sum_{x=1}^d\lvert\theta_x\rvert\).  The symmetric measures \(\nu_{0,K}\) and \(\nu_{1,K}\) exist for every even \(K\), match moments through degree \(K\), and have absolute-moment gap \(2E_K\) with \(E_K\asymp K^{-1}\).  Consequently \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}=a_{n,d}E_{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}}\asymp a_{n,d}/K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}\).  Writing \(\lambda_{n,d}=2n/d\), the one-cell likelihood identity under \(\theta_x=0\) is \(E_0[L_{\theta_x}L_{\theta_x'}]=\exp(\lambda_{n,d}\theta_x\theta_x')\); hence moment matching and tensorization give
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb M_{0,n,d},\mathbb M_{1,n,d})
\leq d\left\{\sum_{r=K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{n,d}a_{n,d}^2)^r}{r!}\right\}^{1/2}
\leq d\left\{\frac{(e/64)^{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}+1}}{1-e/64}\right\}^{1/2}\leq\frac1{16}.
\]
Under either product prior, \(\operatorname{Var}[\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}]\leq a_{n,d}^2/(4d)\), and \(D_0\) can be chosen so that each prior assigns probability at most \(1/8\) to a deviation of the target from its prior mean exceeding \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}/4\).  The two fuzzy hypotheses therefore imply \(\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{2n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_\epsilon(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}})^2\geq c_\epsilon d/[nK_d^{\mathrm{lb}}]\).  Finally, applying any fixed-sample estimator to the first \(n\) observations whenever an independent \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) total count is at least \(n\) yields \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{2n,d,\epsilon}-P\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\geq c_\epsilon d/[n\log(ed)]\), after decreasing \(c_\epsilon\).
