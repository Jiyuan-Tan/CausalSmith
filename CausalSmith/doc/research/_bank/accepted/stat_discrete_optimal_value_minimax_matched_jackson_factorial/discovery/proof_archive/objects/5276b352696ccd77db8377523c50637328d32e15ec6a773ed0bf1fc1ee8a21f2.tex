Fix a law in the stated class and write \(q_{\jmath,x}=P(X=x,(A,Y)=\jmath)\), so that \(p_x=\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}q_{\jmath,x}\).  We first remove the event \(\{M>n\}\) and adjoin an infinite independent continuation of the observed sample.  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), Poisson thinning applied to \(M\sim\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)\) and the independent fair marks \(B_i\) gives, with \(m=n/8\), mutually independent random variables
\[
 N'_{\jmath,x}\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m q_{\jmath,x}),
 \qquad N_{\jmath,x}\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m q_{\jmath,x}),
 \tag{1}
\]
over all \((\jmath,x)\), and the entire pilot array is independent of the evaluation array.  This is precisely the uncapped comparison experiment in the statement.

We first prove the two factorial identities.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(mq)\), then the definition of \(U_h\) and the binomial theorem give the formal exponential generating function
\[
 \sum_{h=0}^{\infty}U_h(N;z)\frac{w^h}{h!}
 =e^{-zw}(1+w/m)^N.
 \tag{2}
\]
The Poisson probability generating function, obtained directly by summing its mass function, is \(E[t^N]=\exp\{mq(t-1)\}\).  Applying it to (2) yields
\[
 E\!\left[e^{-zw}(1+w/m)^N\right]=e^{(q-z)w}.
\]
Equating coefficients of \(w^h/h!\) proves \(E[U_h(N;z)]=(q-z)^h\).  Applying the same calculation to the product of (2) at \(w\) and \(v\) gives
\[
 E\!\left[e^{-z(w+v)}(1+w/m)^N(1+v/m)^N\right]
 =\exp\{(q-z)(w+v)+(q/m)wv\}.
\]
Equating the coefficient of \(w^hv^t/(h!t!)\) proves
\[
 E[U_h(N;z)U_t(N;z)]
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{h\wedge t}\binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!
 (q/m)^\ell(q-z)^{h+t-2\ell}.
 \tag{3}
\]

We next control the random rectangles, including their failure event.  Put \(L=L_d\), \(\tau=L/m\), \(c_{\jmath}=N'_{\jmath,x}/m\), and abbreviate the displayed pilot half-width by
\[
 h_{\jmath}=H_0\{\sqrt{c_{\jmath}\tau}+\tau\}.
\]
Define
\[
 G_x:=\bigcap_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}
 \{|c_{\jmath}-q_{\jmath,x}|\leq h_{\jmath}/4\},
 \qquad v_x:=\sqrt{p_x\tau}+\tau.
 \tag{4}
\]
Since \(\ell_{\jmath,x}=\max(0,c_{\jmath}-h_{\jmath})\) and \(u_{\jmath,x}=c_{\jmath}+h_{\jmath}\), its radius obeys
\[
 h_{\jmath}/2\leq r_{\jmath,x}\leq h_{\jmath}.
 \tag{5}
\]
On \(G_x\), the defining inequalities imply \(q_x\in Q_x\).  Solving the quadratic inequalities in \(\sqrt{c_{\jmath}}\) gives
\[
 c_{\jmath}\leq C(q_{\jmath,x}+\tau),
 \qquad q_{\jmath,x}\leq C(c_{\jmath}+\tau).
 \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\sum_{\jmath}q_{\jmath,x}=p_x\) therefore give
\[
 \sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x}\leq C v_x,
 \qquad
 \frac{q_{\jmath,x}}{m r_{\jmath,x}^2}
 \leq \frac{C(q_{\jmath,x}+\tau)}{m(c_{\jmath}\tau+\tau^2)}
 \leq\frac C L.
 \tag{7}
\]
If \(q_{\jmath,x}=0\), the corresponding count is zero almost surely, and these conclusions hold without division by that mass.

For completeness, we derive a local, rather than dimension-only, bound on \(G_x^c\).  For \(W\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)\), exponential Markov applied to
\[
 E e^{t(W-\lambda)}=\exp\{\lambda(e^t-1-t)\}
\]
on the upper tail and to \(-t(W-\lambda)\) on the lower tail gives, for \(z\geq1\),
\[
 \Pr\{|W-\lambda|>\sqrt{2\lambda z}+2z\}\leq2e^{-z}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Choose the single numerical constant \(H_0\) large enough that the event complementary to (8) with \(z=100L\) implies
\[
 |W/m-q|\leq\frac{H_0}{4}
 \{\sqrt{(W/m)L/m}+L/m\},\qquad \lambda=mq.
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, if \(\lambda\) exceeds a sufficiently large multiple of \(L\), (8) implies \(W\geq\lambda/2\), and the square-root term in (9) dominates \(\sqrt{\lambda L}/m\); if \(\lambda\) is below that multiple, the additive term dominates both \(W/m\) and \(q\) on the same event.  Integrating (8) above \(100L\), using
\[
 E[Z^t\mathbf1\{Z>z_0\}]
 =z_0^t\Pr(Z>z_0)+\int_{z_0}^{\infty}t z^{t-1}\Pr(Z>z)\,dz
\]
for nonnegative \(Z\), gives, for \(t\in\{1,2,4\}\),
\[
 E\!\left[
 \{|W/m-q|+\sqrt{(W/m)\tau}+\tau\}^t\mathbf1_{\mathrm{bad}}
 \right]
 \leq C e^{-40L}(\sqrt{q\tau}+\tau)^t.
 \tag{10}
\]
The same expression without the indicator has moment at most \(C(\sqrt{q\tau}+\tau)^t\).  A union bound over the four coordinates, independence for the nonfailing coordinates, and Cauchy--Schwarz with the fourth moment in (10) now imply
\[
 E\!\left[
 \{\lVert b_x-q_x\rVert_1+\sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x}\}^t
 \mathbf1_{G_x^c}\right]
 \leq C e^{-20L}v_x^t,qquad t=1,2.
 \tag{11}
\]
Equations (5)--(7) and the corresponding unconditional second moments also give
\[
 E\!\left[(\sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x})^2\mathbf1_{G_x}\right]
 \leq C v_x^2.
 \tag{12}
\]

We now apply \(\mathrm{lem:simultaneous\text{-}jackson\text{-}certificate}\) to the same polynomial used by the estimator.  On \(G_x\), consider one coordinate.  If \(\ell_{\jmath,x}=0\), then \(c_{\jmath}\leq h_{\jmath}\); solving this inequality gives \(c_{\jmath}+h_{\jmath}\leq C\tau\), and hence
\[
 \sqrt{(q_{\jmath,x}-\ell_{\jmath,x})
 (u_{\jmath,x}-q_{\jmath,x})}
 \leq C\sqrt{q_{\jmath,x}\tau},qquad
 r_{\jmath,x}\leq C\tau.
\]
If \(\ell_{\jmath,x}>0\), then \(c_{\jmath}>h_{\jmath}\), so (4) gives \(q_{\jmath,x}\geq3c_{\jmath}/4\), while the definition of \(h_{\jmath}\) gives \(r_{\jmath,x}\leq C\sqrt{q_{\jmath,x}\tau}\).  Since \(\sum_{\jmath}\sqrt{q_{\jmath,x}}\leq2\sqrt{p_x}\), the vertex-adaptive approximation part of that certificate yields
\[
 |P_{x,K_d}(q_x)-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{
 \frac{\sqrt{p_x\tau}}{K_d}+\frac{\tau}{K_d^2}\right\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
This derivation includes null coordinates and rectangles touching coordinate faces.

Conditional on the pilot array, (3) and evaluation-count independence imply
\[
 E[Z_x\mid N']=P_{x,K_d}(q_x).
 \tag{14}
\]
Moreover, if \(|q-z|\leq r\) and \(q/(mr^2)\leq C/L\), (3) with \(h=t\), divided by \(r^{2h}\), gives
\[
 \frac{E\{U_h(N;z)^2\}}{r^{2h}}
 \leq\sum_{\ell=0}^h\binom h\ell^2\ell!(C/L)^\ell
 \leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(Ch^2/L)^\ell}{\ell!}
 =e^{Ch^2/L}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Use (15) in each coordinate, the independence in (1), Minkowski's inequality, and the centered-monomial coefficient envelope in \(\mathrm{lem:simultaneous\text{-}jackson\text{-}certificate}\).  Since every coordinate degree is at most \(2(K_d-1)\), this gives, with \(S_x=(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x}\),
\[
 E\{(Z_x-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x))^2\mid N'\}
 \leq S_x^2\exp\{C_1K_d+C_2K_d^2/L\}.
 \tag{16}
\]
There is no rectangle-aspect-ratio factor in (16): the radii were divided out in the centered basis before (15) was applied.  Choose \(\kappa>0\) sufficiently small and then \(D_0\) sufficiently large.  From \(K_d=\max\{2,\lfloor\kappa L\rfloor\}\),
\[
 \exp\{C_1K_d+C_2K_d^2/L\}\leq C d^{1/16};
 \tag{17}
\]
enlarging \(C\) covers the finitely many smaller values of \(d\).

It remains to account for clipping.  Put \(W_x=Z_x-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)\) and \(t_x=d^{1/4}S_x>0\).  Directly from the three cases \(w<-t\), \(|w|\leq t\), and \(w>t\),
\[
 |\Pi_{[-t,t]}(w)-w|\leq w^2/t,
 \qquad |\Pi_{[-t,t]}(w)|\leq|w|.
 \tag{18}
\]
On \(G_x\), (13)--(18), together with \(d^{-3/16}\leq C/L^2\), prove
\[
 |E(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\mid N')-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\sqrt{\frac{p_x}{mL}}+\frac1{mL}\right\},
 \tag{19}
\]
because the clipping-bias term is at most \(S_xd^{-3/16}\), and
\[
 E\{(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x))^2\mid N'\}
 \leq C_\epsilon S_x^2d^{1/16}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Here the Lipschitz conclusion in \(\mathrm{lem:simultaneous\text{-}jackson\text{-}certificate}\), through its dependency on the identification-and-extension proposition, gives
\(|\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|\leq S_x\) on \(G_x\).

On \(G_x^c\), the clipping interval and the same global Lipschitz bound give the deterministic inequality
\[
 |T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq(1+\epsilon^{-1})\lVert b_x-q_x\rVert_1+d^{1/4}S_x.
 \tag{21}
\]
Its first two moments are controlled by (11).  The elementary bounds
\(d^{1/4}e^{-20L}L^2\leq C\) and
\(d^{1/2}e^{-20L}\leq Cd^{1/16}\) show that these moments fit, respectively, the right side of (19) and the expectation of (20).  Integrating (19)--(21), using (12) and
\[
 v_x^2=(\sqrt{p_xL/m}+L/m)^2
 \leq2\{p_xL/m+L^2/m^2\},
\]
proves
\[
 |E[T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}]-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\sqrt{\frac{p_x}{mL_d}}+\frac1{mL_d}\right\},
\]
and, since variance is bounded by mean-squared error about any constant,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}})
 \leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
 \left\{\frac{p_xL_d}{m}+\frac{L_d^2}{m^2}\right\}.
\]
This proves the lemma at arbitrary masses, null coordinates, overlap and outcome boundaries, and exact tie surfaces.
