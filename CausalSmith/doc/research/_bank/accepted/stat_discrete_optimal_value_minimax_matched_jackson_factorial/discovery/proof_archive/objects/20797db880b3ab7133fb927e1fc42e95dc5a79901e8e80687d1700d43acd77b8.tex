Every \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\) mapped by \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\) yields a law in \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) with \(p_x=(P_x+Q_x)/2\), \(\pi_x=1/2\), and \(V^\star(P)=1/2+\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1/4\). From \(n\) draws from each of \(\mathbf P\) and \(\mathbf Q\), an explicit Markov kernel produces exactly \(n\) independent causal observations, so the causal minimax risk is at least one sixteenth of the corresponding normalized two-sample \(L_1\) risk.
