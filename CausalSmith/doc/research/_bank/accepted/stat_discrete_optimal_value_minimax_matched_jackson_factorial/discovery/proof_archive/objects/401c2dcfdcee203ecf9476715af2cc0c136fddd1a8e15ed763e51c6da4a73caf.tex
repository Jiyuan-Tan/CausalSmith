Fix \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification-and-extension}\) gives \(0\leq\Psi(\mathbb P)\leq\sum_xs(q_x)=1\) and supplies a completion \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) with observed margin \(\mathbb P\).  Since every statistic below is a function only of the observed sample and the displayed auxiliary variables, its distribution under this completion is its distribution under \(\mathbb P\).  Therefore the uniform cell bounds of the already established \(\mathrm{lem:centered-factorial-pilot-control}\) apply to every \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), with \(\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\) as the cell target.

First consider the Jackson branch \(d\geq D_0\), \(n\geq d/L_d\), and put \(m=n/8\).  In the uncapped Poisson comparison experiment, counts belonging to different \(x\)'s are independent by Poisson splitting; thus the clipped statistics \(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\), including their pilots, are independent.  The cell bias bound, \(\sum_xp_x=1\), and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \left|\sum_xE_{\mathbb P}T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right|^2
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\frac d{mL_d}+\frac{d^2}{m^2L_d^2}\right\}, \tag{7}
\]
because \(\sum_x\sqrt{p_x}\leq\sqrt d\).  Independence and the cell variance bound give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P}\left(\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\right)
 \leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
 \left\{\frac{L_d}{m}+\frac{dL_d^2}{m^2}\right\}.                 \tag{8}
\]
Since \(m\geq d/(8L_d)\), the second term of (7), divided by \(d/(mL_d)\), is at most eight.  The two terms of (8), divided by \(d/(mL_d)\), are bounded respectively by
\[
 d^{-15/16}L_d^2,\qquad 8d^{-15/16}L_d^4,
\]
and both are uniformly bounded for integers \(d\geq2\).  Hence
\[
 E_{\mathbb P}\left[\left\{\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right\}^2\right]
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d{mL_d}.                                    \tag{9}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase squared distance from \(\Psi(\mathbb P)\).

Adjoin an infinite independent continuation of the fixed sample.  The ideal Poisson statistic and the capped randomized statistic in \(\mathrm{def:jackson-factorial-estimator}\) agree on \(\{M\leq n\}\).  Both projected losses are at most one, while the exponential Markov bound for a Poisson variable gives
\[
 \Pr\{M>n\}=\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)>n\}\leq e^{-c n}.       \tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) bound the randomized fixed-sample risk by \(C_\epsilon d/(nL_d)+e^{-cn}\).  The exponential term is at most a universal multiple of \(d/(nL_d)\) for \(d\geq2\).  Conditional Jensen for squared loss then shows that the Rao--Blackwellized, all-data estimator \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=E(\widetilde V\mid O_1,\ldots,O_n)\) has no larger risk.

If \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<d/L_d\), the estimator equals \(1/2\), its risk is at most \(1/4\), and the minimum in the claim equals one.  It remains to consider \(2\leq d<D_0\).  Conditional on \(\overline N_x=k>0\), the arm count \(\overline N_{ax}\) is binomial with success probability \(\rho_{ax}\geq\epsilon\).  For \(H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k,\rho)\),
\[
 E\frac{\mathbf1\{H>0\}}H\leq\frac2{k\rho},\qquad
 \Pr(H=0)=(1-\rho)^k\leq\frac1{k\rho}.                            \tag{11}
\]
The first inequality follows from \(H^{-1}\mathbf1\{H>0\}\leq2/(H+1)\) and
\(E(H+1)^{-1}=[1-(1-\rho)^{k+1}]/[(k+1)\rho]\); the second follows from \((1-\rho)^k\leq(1+k\rho)^{-1}\).  Conditional binomial variance and the \(0/0=0\) convention imply
\[
 E\{(\widehat\mu_{ax}-\mu_{ax})^2\mid\overline N_x=k\}
 \leq\frac3{k\epsilon}.                                            \tag{12}
\]
The maximum is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm.  Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, followed by (12), yields
\[
 E\left[\left\{\sum_x\widehat p_x
 (\max_a\widehat\mu_{ax}-\max_a\mu_{ax})\right\}^2\right]
 \leq\frac{6d}{n\epsilon}.                                         \tag{13}
\]
Also \(E\lVert\widehat p-p\rVert_1^2\leq d\sum_x\operatorname{Var}(\widehat p_x)\leq d/n\).  Decomposing the empirical-ratio error into the regression term in (13) and the mass-estimation term therefore gives risk at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/n\}\).  Because \(L_d\) is bounded above and below on \(2\leq d<D_0\), this is at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/(nL_d)\}\).  Every step was uniform over \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), proving the claim at null cells, overlap boundaries, outcome boundaries, unequal masses, and exact ties.
