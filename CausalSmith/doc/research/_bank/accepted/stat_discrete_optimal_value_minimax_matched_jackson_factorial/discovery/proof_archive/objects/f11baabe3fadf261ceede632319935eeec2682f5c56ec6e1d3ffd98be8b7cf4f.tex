Let \(d=d_n\geq2\) and \(n\to\infty\).  The matched inequalities in \(\mathrm{thm:matched\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\), with constants depending only on the fixed \(\epsilon\), show that uniform consistency is possible if and only if
\[
 \frac d{n\log(ed)}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{36}
\]
Indeed, sufficiency is attained by the displayed Jackson--factorial estimator, while necessity follows from the all-estimator minimax lower inequality.

We prove that (36) is equivalent to \(d=o(n\log(en))\).  Put \(t=d/n\) and \(L_n=\log(en)\).  First suppose \(t/L_n\to0\).  On indices with \(d\geq n\), \(\log(ed)\geq L_n\), so
\[
 \frac d{n\log(ed)}\leq\frac t{L_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
On indices with \(d<n\) and \(d\geq\sqrt n\), one has \(\log(ed)\geq L_n/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), whence the ratio in (36) is at most \(2/L_n\).  On indices with \(d<\sqrt n\), it is at most \(n^{-1/2}\), because \(\log(ed)\geq1\).  Thus (36) follows.

Conversely, suppose (36).  On indices with \(t<1\), \(t/L_n\leq1/L_n\to0\).  On indices with \(t\geq1\), define
\[
 \eta_n:=\frac{t}{L_n+\log t}=\frac d{n\log(ed)}.
\]
Then \(\eta_n\to0\).  Since \(\log t\leq t\), eventually \(\eta_n\log t\leq t/2\).  The identity
\[
 t=\eta_nL_n+\eta_n\log t
\]
therefore gives \(t/L_n\leq2\eta_n\to0\).  This proves the consistency equivalence.

Multiplying the matched frontier by \(n\) shows that \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}=\Theta_\epsilon(n^{-1})\) along a sequence exactly when
\[
 n\min\left\{1,\frac d{n\log(ed)}\right\}
 =\min\left\{n,\frac d{\log(ed)}\right\}
 \tag{37}
\]
is bounded above and bounded away from zero.  If \(d=O(1)\), then \(2\leq d\leq D\) for some finite \(D\), so \(d/\log(ed)\) is bounded above and away from zero and (37) has the required property.  If \(d\) is unbounded, there is a subsequence on which \(d\to\infty\).  Along that subsequence, either the first branch of (37) is selected and (37) equals \(n\to\infty\), or the second branch is selected and (37) equals \(d/\log(ed)\to\infty\).  Thus (37) cannot be bounded above.  Parametric mean-squared risk therefore holds if and only if \(d=O(1)\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop:identification\text{-}and\text{-}extension}\) gives surjectivity of \(P\mapsto\operatorname{Obs}(P)\) from \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) onto \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and \(\mathrm{prop:causal\text{-}optimal\text{-}value\text{-}corollary}\) gives \(V^\star(P)=\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P)\}\).  Since the sampling experiment observes only \((X,A,Y)\), the worst-case risks over the observed class and over its causal completions are identical.  The two phase equivalences consequently transfer verbatim to estimating \(V^\star(P)\) over \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).
