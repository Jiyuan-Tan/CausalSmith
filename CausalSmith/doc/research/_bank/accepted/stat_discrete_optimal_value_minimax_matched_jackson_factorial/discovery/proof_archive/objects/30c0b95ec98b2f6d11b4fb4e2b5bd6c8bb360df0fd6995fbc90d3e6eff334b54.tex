Put
\[
 r_{n,d}:=\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\},
 \qquad a:=d/[n\log(ed)].
\]
Since \(\log(ed)\geq1\), \(r_{n,d}\leq d/n\).  Also
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d/n}{d/[n\log(ed)]}=\frac{\log(ed)}{\sqrt d}
\]
is bounded uniformly over integers \(d\geq2\).  If \(a>1\), then
\[
 \min\{1,\sqrt d/n+d/[n\log(en)]\}\leq1=r_{n,d}.
\]
Suppose instead that \(a\leq1\).  If \(n\geq d\), then \(\log(en)\geq\log(ed)\), and hence \(d/[n\log(en)]\leq a\).  If \(n<d\), put \(t=d/n>1\).  The identity
\[
 \log(ed)=\log(en)+\log t
\]
and \(a\leq1\) imply \(t\leq\log(en)+\log t\).  For \(t\geq2\), \(\log t\leq t/2\), so \(t\leq2\log(en)\); for \(1<t<2\), the same conclusion holds after enlarging the numerical constant.  Consequently \(\log(ed)\leq C\log(en)\) and again
\[
 \frac d{n\log(en)}\leq C a.
\]
Combining the cases gives the deterministic comparison
\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \leq C r_{n,d}.
 \tag{35}
\]
Applying the upper and lower inequalities of \(\mathrm{thm:matched\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\) to these two deterministic bounds proves the two claimed comparisons with the banked bracket on the identical model class.

For \(2\leq d<D_0\), the first branch of \(\mathrm{def:jackson\text{-}factorial\text{-}estimator}\) is exactly
\(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}\).  Finally, along a sequence with \(2\leq d\leq D<\infty\), the quantity \(d/\log(ed)\) is bounded above and bounded away from zero.  The matched frontier thus gives
\[
 c_{\epsilon,D}n^{-1}\leq\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq C_{\epsilon,D}n^{-1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), which is the asserted bounded-alphabet parametric conclusion.
