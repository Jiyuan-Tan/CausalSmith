Fix \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  By \(\mathrm{prop:identification\text{-}and\text{-}extension}\),
\[
 \Psi(\mathbb P)=\sum_{x=1}^d\bar f_\epsilon(q_x),
 \qquad 0\leq\Psi(\mathbb P)\leq\sum_{x=1}^ds(q_x)=1.
 \tag{22}
\]
That proposition also supplies a causal completion having observed margin \(\mathbb P\).  Every statistic under consideration depends only on the observed sample and the auxiliary variables displayed in \(\mathrm{def:jackson\text{-}factorial\text{-}estimator}\); hence its distribution under the completion is exactly its distribution under \(\mathbb P\).  The uniform bounds in \(\mathrm{lem:centered\text{-}factorial\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) therefore apply to every member of the full observed-law class.

First suppose \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n\geq d/L_d\), and put \(m=n/8\).  In the uncapped Poisson comparison experiment, all pilot and evaluation counts belonging to distinct cells \(x\) are independent by (1).  Consequently the clipped statistics \(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\) are independent across \(x\).  The cell bias bound, \(\sum_xp_x=1\), and \(\sum_x\sqrt{p_x}\leq\sqrt d\) yield
\[
 \left|\sum_xE T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right|^2
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\frac d{mL_d}+\frac{d^2}{m^2L_d^2}\right\}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Independence and the cell variance bound yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\right)
 \leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
 \left\{\frac{L_d}{m}+\frac{dL_d^2}{m^2}\right\}.
 \tag{24}
\]
Because \(m\geq d/(8L_d)\), the second term of (23) is at most eight times \(d/(mL_d)\).  Dividing the two terms in (24) by \(d/(mL_d)\) gives, respectively,
\[
 d^{-15/16}L_d^2,
 \qquad 8d^{-15/16}L_d^4,
\]
which are uniformly bounded for integers \(d\geq2\).  Thus
\[
 E_{\mathbb P}\left[
 \left\{\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right\}^2
 \right]
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d{mL_d}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase squared distance from the target in (22), since a metric projection onto a closed interval is distance reducing relative to every point of the interval.

Adjoin an infinite independent continuation of the fixed sample.  The ideal statistic just analyzed and the capped randomized statistic \(\widetilde V\) in \(\mathrm{def:jackson\text{-}factorial\text{-}estimator}\) agree on \(\{M\leq n\}\).  Both projected losses are at most one.  Exponential Markov, with \(t=\log4\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{M>n\}
 \leq e^{-tn}E(e^{tM})
 =\exp\{-n\log4+(n/4)(4-1)\}
 \leq e^{-c n}
 \tag{26}
\]
for a universal \(c>0\).  Equations (25)--(26), and \(m=n/8\), imply
\[
 E_{\mathbb P}[(\widetilde V-\Psi(\mathbb P))^2]
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d{nL_d}+e^{-cn}
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d{nL_d},
 \tag{27}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\sup_{n\geq1}ne^{-cn}<\infty\) and \(d/L_d\geq2/\log(2e)>0\).  Conditional Jensen, applied to squared loss, shows that the all-data Rao--Blackwellization
\(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=E(\widetilde V\mid O_1,\ldots,O_n)\) has risk no larger than (27).
This is a measurable sample map: on \(\{M\leq n\}\) all counts and markings range over finite sets, each \(P_{x,K_d}\) is a fixed finite-degree polynomial obtained from the continuous compact-torus integral in \(\mathrm{def:jackson\text{-}kernel}\), projection is continuous, and the displayed conditional expectation is a finite sum over \(M=0,\ldots,n\) and the finitely many markings.  Thus it is an admissible estimator in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risk}\).

If \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<d/L_d\), the estimator is \(1/2\) by its definition.  Equation (22) gives risk at most \(1/4\), while \(d/(nL_d)>1\), so the required truncated bound follows.

It remains to treat \(2\leq d<D_0\), where the definition selects \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}\).  Put \(m_x=\max_a\mu_{ax}\) and \(\widehat m_x=\max_a\widehat\mu_{ax}\).  Conditional on \(\overline N_x=k\geq1\), the arm count \(H=\overline N_{ax}\) is \(\operatorname{Bin}(k,\rho_{ax})\), where \(\rho_{1x}=\pi_x\), \(\rho_{0x}=1-\pi_x\), and \(\rho_{ax}\geq\epsilon\) by \(\mathrm{def:observed\text{-}model\text{-}class}\).  Direct summation of the binomial mass function gives
\[
 E\frac1{H+1}=\frac{1-(1-\rho)^{k+1}}{(k+1)\rho}.
\]
Since \(H^{-1}\mathbf1\{H>0\}\leq2/(H+1)\) and
\((1-\rho)^k\leq e^{-k\rho}\leq1/(k\rho)\),
\[
 E\frac{\mathbf1\{H>0\}}H\leq\frac2{k\rho},
 \qquad \Pr(H=0)\leq\frac1{k\rho}.
 \tag{28}
\]
Conditional on \(H>0\), \(\overline S_{ax}\) is binomial with parameters \((H,\mu_{ax})\); on \(H=0\), the convention is \(\widehat\mu_{ax}=0\).  Its conditional variance and (28) therefore imply
\[
 E\{(\widehat\mu_{ax}-\mu_{ax})^2\mid\overline N_x=k\}
 \leq\frac3{k\epsilon}.
 \tag{29}
\]
The maximum map is one-Lipschitz for the sup norm, so weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and (29) give
\[
 E\left[
 \left\{\sum_x\widehat p_x(\widehat m_x-m_x)\right\}^2
 \right]
 \leq E\sum_x\widehat p_x(\widehat m_x-m_x)^2
 \leq\frac{6d}{n\epsilon}.
 \tag{30}
\]
For the mass term, pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz and multinomial variances give
\[
 E\lVert\widehat p-p\rVert_1^2
 \leq d\sum_xE(\widehat p_x-p_x)^2
 =\frac d n\sum_xp_x(1-p_x)\leq\frac d n.
 \tag{31}
\]
The exact decomposition
\[
 \widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)
 =\sum_x\widehat p_x(\widehat m_x-m_x)
 +\sum_x(\widehat p_x-p_x)m_x
\]
and \(0\leq m_x\leq1\) now imply risk at most \(C_\epsilon d/n\); the loss is also at most one because both quantities lie in \([0,1]\).  Since \(L_d\) is bounded above and away from zero on the finite range \(2\leq d<D_0\), this is
\[
 C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/(nL_d)\}.
\]
All bounds used only the defining restrictions of \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  They therefore hold for unknown unequal propensities, arbitrary and null cell masses, boundary outcome means, and exact ties, as claimed.
