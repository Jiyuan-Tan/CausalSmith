Fix \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\) and apply \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\).  Direct summation of its four observed masses gives
\[
 p_x=\sum_{a,y}q_{ay,x}=\frac{P_x+Q_x}{2},qquad
 s_1(q_x)=s_0(q_x)=\frac{P_x+Q_x}{4}.
\]
Thus every occupied cell has \(\pi_x=1/2\), which satisfies fixed overlap for every \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  The potential-outcome completion specified in the definition satisfies conditional exchangeability and consistency, so it is in \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).  On a cell with \(P_x+Q_x>0\),
\[
 \mu_{1x}=\frac{P_x}{P_x+Q_x},\qquad
 \mu_{0x}=\frac{Q_x}{P_x+Q_x}.
\]
The null-cell contribution is zero.  By \(\mathrm{prop:identification-and-extension}\),
\[
 V^\star(P)=\frac12\sum_x\max\{P_x,Q_x\}
 =\frac12+\frac14\|\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\|_1,                         \tag{18}
\]
because \(\max(r,s)=(r+s+|r-s|)/2\) and both probability vectors sum to one.

Now suppose \(X_i^P\sim\mathbf P\) and \(X_i^Q\sim\mathbf Q\), independently for \(i=1,\ldots,n\).  For each \(i\), toss an independent fair source coin.  If it selects \(P\), use \(X_i^P\) and choose \((A,Y)=(1,1)\) or \((0,0)\) with equal probabilities; if it selects \(Q\), use \(X_i^Q\) and choose \((A,Y)=(1,0)\) or \((0,1)\) with equal probabilities.  The resulting observations are independent and have exactly the four masses in \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\).

For any causal estimator \(\widehat V\), this Markov kernel and (18) define the randomized distance estimator \(\widehat L=4(\widehat V-1/2)\).  Conditional expectation over the auxiliary coins is measurable in the original two samples and, by Jensen,
\[
 E_{\mathbf P,\mathbf Q}\left[(E(\widehat L\mid X^P,X^Q)-\|\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\|_1)^2\right]
 \leq16E_P[(\widehat V-V^\star(P))^2].
\]
Taking the supremum over \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\), then the infimum over all causal estimators, proves that the causal minimax risk is at least one sixteenth of the normalized two-sample \(L_1\) minimax risk.  The construction uses exactly \(n\) causal observations and introduces no asymptotic approximation.
