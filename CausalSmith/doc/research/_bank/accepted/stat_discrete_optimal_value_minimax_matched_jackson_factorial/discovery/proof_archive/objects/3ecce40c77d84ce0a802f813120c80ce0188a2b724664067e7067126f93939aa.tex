The target belongs to \([0,1]\) by \(\mathrm{prop:identification-and-extension}\).  First take the Jackson branch \(d\geq D_0\), \(n\geq d/L_d\), and put \(m=n/8\).  In the uncapped Poisson comparison experiment the cell statistics are independent, since each is a measurable function only of its own independent pilot and evaluation Poisson counts.  From \(\mathrm{lem:centered-factorial-pilot-control}\), \(\sum_xp_x=1\), and \(\sum_x\sqrt{p_x}\leq\sqrt d\),
\[
 \left|\sum_xE T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-V^\star(P)\right|^2
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\frac d{mL_d}+\frac{d^2}{m^2L_d^2}\right\}, \tag{15}
\]
while independence gives
\[
 \sum_x\operatorname{Var}(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}})
 \leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}\left\{\frac{L_d}{m}+\frac{dL_d^2}{m^2}\right\}. \tag{16}
\]
Because \(m\geq d/(8L_d)\), the second term in (15) divided by \(d/(mL_d)\) is at most eight.  The two terms in (16), divided by the same quantity, are bounded by
\[
 d^{-15/16}L_d^2,qquad 8d^{-15/16}L_d^4,
\]
respectively; both are uniformly bounded for \(d\geq2\).  Thus
\[
 E_P\left[\left\{\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-V^\star(P)\right\}^2\right]
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d{mL_d}.                                      \tag{17}
\]
Projection of the sum onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase its squared distance from \(V^\star(P)\).

Couple this ideal experiment to the construction in \(\mathrm{def:jackson-factorial-estimator}\) by adjoining an infinite iid continuation of the fixed sample.  The ideal and capped randomized statistics agree on \(\{M\leq n\}\).  Since both projected losses are at most one and
\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)>n\}\leq e^{-c n},
\]
equation (17) implies risk at most \(C_\epsilon d/(nL_d)+e^{-cn}\).  The exponential term is at most a constant multiple of \(d/(nL_d)\) for \(d\geq2\).  Finally, conditional Jensen applied to the square loss shows that Rao--Blackwellization over \(M\) and the marks cannot increase risk.  This proves the advertised rate in the Jackson branch.

If \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<d/L_d\), the estimator is \(1/2\); its risk is at most \(1/4\), and the minimum in the theorem equals one.  It remains to treat \(2\leq d<D_0\).  Conditional on \(\overline N_x=k>0\), \(\overline N_{ax}\) is binomial with success probability at least \(\epsilon\).  For \(H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k,\rho)\),
\[
 E\frac{\mathbf1_{\{H>0\}}}{H}\leq\frac2{k\rho},qquad
 \Pr(H=0)=(1-\rho)^k\leq\frac1{k\rho},
\]
where the first inequality follows from \(H^{-1}\mathbf1_{H>0}\leq2/(H+1)\) and
\(E(H+1)^{-1}=[1-(1-\rho)^{k+1}]/[(k+1)\rho]\).  Conditional binomial variance therefore gives
\[
 E\{(\widehat\mu_{ax}-\mu_{ax})^2\mid\overline N_x=k\}\leq\frac3{k\epsilon}.
\]
Using the Lipschitz property of the maximum and Cauchy--Schwarz with weights \(\widehat p_x\),
\[
 E\left[\left\{\sum_x\widehat p_x(\max_a\widehat\mu_{ax}-\max_a\mu_{ax})\right\}^2\right]
 \leq\frac{6d}{n\epsilon}.
\]
Also \(E\|\widehat p-p\|_1^2\leq d\sum_x\operatorname{Var}(\widehat p_x)\leq d/n\).  Hence the empirical-ratio branch has risk at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/n\}\).  Since \(L_d\) is bounded above and below on \(2\leq d<D_0\), this is at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/(nL_d)\}\).  All bounds are uniform over the model class, so they include unknown unequal propensities, arbitrary masses, null cells, boundary means, and exact ties.
