The last inequality in the display is \(\mathrm{thm:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}upper}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}risk}\), \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is the infimum of worst-case risks over all measurable estimators.  Since \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}\) is one such measurable estimator,
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}
 E_{\mathbb P}\left[
 (\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P))^2\right].
\]
The first inequality is \(\mathrm{thm:all\mbox{-}estimator\mbox{-}lower}\).  Concatenating these three inequalities proves the displayed frontier for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\).

For the causal conclusion, \(\mathrm{prop:identification\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}extension}\) proves that the observed margins of \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) fill all of \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and \(\mathrm{prop:causal\mbox{-}optimal\mbox{-}value\mbox{-}corollary}\) proves
\[
 V^\star(P)=\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P)\}.
\]
Thus the observation experiments and targets coincide under the surjective completion map.  The causal statement is consequently a corollary of the statistical frontier, rather than an equality imposed by definition.
