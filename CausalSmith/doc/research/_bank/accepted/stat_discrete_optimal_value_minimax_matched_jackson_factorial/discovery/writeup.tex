\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}discrete\_\allowbreak{}optimal\_\allowbreak{}value\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}matched}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} jackson\_\allowbreak{}factorial\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Relative to the archived downgraded predecessor \(\texttt{stat\_discrete\_optimal\_value\_minimax\_diagonal\_adaptive\_ratio}/\texttt{tv}\), this estimation upgrade replaces the certified \(d/n\) empirical-ratio upper bound by an explicit all-data Jackson--factorial estimator and proves the matched finite-sample risk \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\asymp_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\). A globally Lipschitz extension of the cell oracle permits one pilot-local tensor Jackson polynomial to handle null cells, off-cone pilots, unequal propensities, boundary means, and exact ties; centered factorial moments, local clipping, Poisson coupling, and Rao--Blackwellization yield the upper bound, while a dense moment-matching experiment, the exact normalized \(L_1\) slice, and padding yield the all-regime converse. The result settles uniform consistency exactly at \(d=o(n\log(en))\) and retains parametric mean-squared error exactly for bounded \(d\).

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The closest accepted in-repository comparator, \(\texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match}/\texttt{thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}\), treats the signed aggregate \(\tau=\sum_xp_x(\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x})\) on the same four-cell overlap cone and obtains mean-squared error \(n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2\log^2 n]\) for \(d\leq\rho_\epsilon n\log n\) using a heavy/light ratio-polynomial hybrid. It does not treat the cellwise maximum, whose decomposition contains an absolute tie contrast. The archived downgraded predecessor for the present target certifies only \(d/n\) achievability and therefore leaves a logarithmic gap and the region between \(d=o(n)\) and \(d=o(n\log n)\) unresolved.

\paragraph{Niche.} The oracle maximum is a degree-one homogeneous functional of four observed cell masses. Its decomposition into a smooth average and a cellwise absolute treatment-effect contrast distinguishes it from the signed aggregate comparator. Its extension to the whole orthant is globally Lipschitz, so a positive tensor Jackson convolution can adapt pointwise at the cone vertex while retaining an exponential coefficient envelope in a centered local basis.

\paragraph{Fill.} The explicit all-data Jackson--factorial estimator has uniform mean-squared error at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\), and the dense moment-matching, exact normalized \(L_1\), padding, and regular two-point constructions give the matching lower bound against every measurable estimator. Thus the note proves an all-regime optimal-value frontier rather than transferring the signed-aggregate rate. The archived downgraded predecessor is retained solely to document lineage on the identical assumptions and target.

\section{Related work}

The accepted in-repository result \(\texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match}/\texttt{thm:sharp-minimax-fixed-interior}\) studies the same four-cell overlap cone but the signed aggregate \(\tau=\sum_xp_x(\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x})\). It proves mean-squared error \(n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2\log^2 n]\) on \(d\leq\rho_\epsilon n\log n\) with a heavy/light ratio-polynomial hybrid. The present functional is \(\sum_xp_x\max_a\mu_{ax}=2^{-1}\sum_xp_x(\mu_{0x}+\mu_{1x})+2^{-1}\sum_xp_x\lvert\mu_{1x}-\mu_{0x}\rvert\), so its nonsmooth term is cellwise absolute rather than a signed aggregate; neither result subsumes the other and no rate is transferred between them. Zeng, Balakrishnan, Han, and Kennedy, \cite{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete} [reused from the archived predecessor], Introduction, Theorems 1--2, and Discussion, formulate the same structure-free categorical adjustment problem and prove a logarithmically separated minimax analysis for linear treatment-specific means; their target has no maximum or tie surface. Jiao, Han, and Weissman, \cite{JiaoHanWeissman2018L1} [reused from the archived predecessor], Introduction, Theorem 3, Construction 2, Theorem 6, and Section VI, show that approximation must follow the equality diagonal for \(L_1\) distance and supply the normalized-distance lower theorem used on the exact equal-propensity slice; their unknown--unknown upper estimator does not apply to four observational masses with unknown arm denominators. Cai and Low, \cite{CaiLow2011L1} [reused from the archived predecessor], Lemma 1 and Sections 3.1--4, provide the approximation--moment dual for the absolute-value cusp and the fuzzy-hypothesis template adapted in the dense lower experiment. Luedtke and van der Laan, \cite{LuedtkeVanderLaan2016OptimalValue} [reused from the archived predecessor], Theorem 1 and Sections 4--7, characterize local nonregularity and online inference for the same scalar value, but do not analyze a growing unrestricted alphabet. Whitehouse, Chen, Austern, and Syrgkanis, \cite{WhitehouseChenAusternSyrgkanis2025} [reused from the archived predecessor], the static-treatment theorem and controlled-density lemma, regularize the maximum by softmax; their condition permits an atom at an exact tie but restricts near ties and nuisance rates, so it does not provide a uniform result for the present class. Han, Jiao, and Weissman, \cite{HanJiaoWeissman2018Local}, Introduction and Sections 2--3, establish cell localization and moment reconstruction for symmetric functionals, but their quotient forgets the four-coordinate grouping needed by causal ratios. Jiao, Venkat, Han, and Weissman, \cite{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015Functionals}, Sections III--IV, provide the generic large-alphabet architecture of polynomial approximation, sample splitting, and unbiased factorial-moment lifting for additive discrete-distribution functionals; they do not provide the coupled four-coordinate overlap-ratio maximum or the present all-regime frontier.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
For each \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and have common law \(\mathbb P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (consistency, \cite{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-exchangeability]\label{ass:conditional-exchangeability}
For each \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(Y(a)\perp A\mid X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (armwise conditional exchangeability, \cite{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-overlap]\label{ass:fixed-overlap}
For every \(\mathbb P\) under consideration and every \(x\in[d]\) with \(p_x=\mathbb P(X=x)>0\), the conditional probability \(\pi_x=\mathbb P(A=1\mid X=x)\) satisfies \(\epsilon\leq\pi_x\leq1-\epsilon\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed strong overlap, \cite{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:model-class]\label{def:model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{causal completion class for the observed discrete experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}:=\{P:P\text{ is a law of }(X,A,Y,Y(0),Y(1))\text{ with }X\in[d],\ A,Y,Y(0),Y(1)\in\{0,1\},\ P\text{ satisfies }\mathrm{ass:consistency}\text{ and }\mathrm{ass:conditional-exchangeability},\text{ and }\operatorname{Obs}(P)\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\}\) for every \(d\geq2\) and \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}exchangeability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}overlap}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:overlap-cone]\label{def:overlap-cone}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{four-\allowbreak{}cell overlap cone}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal C_\epsilon:=\{u\in\mathbb R_+^4:\epsilon s(u)\leq s_1(u)\leq(1-\epsilon)s(u)\}\), where \(s_a(u)=u_{a0}+u_{a1}\) and \(s(u)=s_0(u)+s_1(u)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\epsilon\); \(u\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:global-extension]\label{def:global-extension}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{globally Lipschitz observational cell-\allowbreak{}value extension}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(u\in\mathbb R_+^4\) with \(s(u)>0\), define \(g_{a,\epsilon}(u):=s(u)u_{a1}/\max\{s_a(u),\epsilon s(u)\}\) and \(\bar f_\epsilon(u):=\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}g_{a,\epsilon}(u)\); set \(g_{a,\epsilon}(0)=\bar f_\epsilon(0)=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(u\); \(\epsilon\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:oracle-value]\label{def:oracle-value}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{causal unrestricted oracle value}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(V^\star(P):=E_P[\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E_P\{Y(a)\mid X\}]\).  This is a full-data causal functional; its equality to an observed-law functional is asserted only by \(\mathrm{prop:causal-optimal-value-corollary}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:empirical-ratio-estimator]\label{def:empirical-ratio-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}alphabet empirical-\allowbreak{}ratio fallback}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\overline N_x=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}\), \(\overline N_{ax}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x,A_i=a\}\), and \(\overline S_{ax}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x,A_i=a,Y_i=1\}\). Put \(\widehat p_x=\overline N_x/n\), \(\widehat\mu_{ax}=\overline S_{ax}/\overline N_{ax}\) with \(0/0=0\), and \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}=\sum_{x=1}^d\widehat p_x\max_a\widehat\mu_{ax}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(O_i\); \(n\); \(d\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:jackson-kernel]\label{def:jackson-kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{positive order-\allowbreak{}four tensor Jackson approximation}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For integer \(K_d\geq2\), let \(J_{K_d}(t)=c_{K_d}[\sin(K_dt/2)/\sin(t/2)]^4\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\), with removable values filled continuously and \(c_{K_d}\) chosen so that \(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}J_{K_d}(t)\,dt=1\). For a rectangle \(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\) with center \(b_{\jmath,x}=(\ell_{\jmath,x}+u_{\jmath,x})/2\) and radius \(r_{\jmath,x}=(u_{\jmath,x}-\ell_{\jmath,x})/2\), define \(P_{x,K_d}\) by the even product convolution \(P_{x,K_d}(b_x+r_x\odot\cos\theta)=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^4}\bar f_\epsilon(b_x+r_x\odot\cos(\theta+t))\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}J_{K_d}(t_{\jmath})\,dt\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(K_d\); \(Q_x\); \(\bar f_\epsilon\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:jackson-factorial-estimator]\label{def:jackson-factorial-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{explicit all-\allowbreak{}data pilot-\allowbreak{}local Jackson-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}factorial estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(L_d=\log(ed)\), \(m=n/8\), and \(K_d=\max\{2,\lfloor\kappa L_d\rfloor\}\). If \(d<D_0\), return \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}\); if \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<d/L_d\), return \(1/2\). Otherwise draw \(M\sim\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)\), and on \(M\leq n\) mark the first \(M\) observations independently by \(B_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), producing independent pilot and evaluation counts \(N'_{\jmath,x}\) and \(N_{\jmath,x}\); on \(M>n\), set the randomized output to zero. For \(M\leq n\), let \(c_{\jmath,x}=N'_{\jmath,x}/m\), \(h_{\jmath,x}=H_0\{\sqrt{c_{\jmath,x}L_d/m}+L_d/m\}\), \(\ell_{\jmath,x}=\max\{0,c_{\jmath,x}-h_{\jmath,x}\}\), \(u_{\jmath,x}=c_{\jmath,x}+h_{\jmath,x}\), and \(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\), with center \(b_x\) and coordinate radii \(r_x\). Expand \(P_{x,K_d}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)=\sum_\alpha b_{x,\alpha}((v-b_x)/r_x)^\alpha\). Define \(U_h(N;z)=\sum_{t=0}^h\binom ht(-z)^{h-t}(N)_t/m^t\), \(S_x=(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x}\), \(Z_x=\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)+\sum_\alpha b_{x,\alpha}\prod_{\jmath}U_{\alpha_{\jmath}}(N_{\jmath,x};b_{\jmath,x})/r_{\jmath,x}^{\alpha_{\jmath}}\), and \(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}=\Pi_{[\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)-d^{1/4}S_x,\,\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)+d^{1/4}S_x]}(Z_x)\). The randomized statistic is \(\widetilde V=\Pi_{[0,1]}(\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}})\). In the Jackson branch define the all-data estimator \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}:=E[\widetilde V\mid O_1,\ldots,O_n]\), where the conditional expectation integrates only the displayed auxiliary Poisson count and marks.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(O_i\); \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}global-\allowbreak{}extension}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}empirical-\allowbreak{}ratio-\allowbreak{}estimator}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}jackson-\allowbreak{}kernel}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:identification-and-extension]\label{prop:identification-and-extension}
For every \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), one has \(q_x\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\) and \(\Psi(\mathbb P)=\sum_{x=1}^d\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\).  Moreover, \(0\leq\bar f_\epsilon(u)\leq s(u)\) and \(\lvert\bar f_\epsilon(u)-\bar f_\epsilon(v)\rvert\leq(1+\epsilon^{-1})\lVert u-v\rVert_1\) for all \(u,v\in\mathbb R_+^4\).  Every \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) has a completion \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) with \(\operatorname{Obs}(P)=\mathbb P\), obtained by drawing conditionally independent Bernoulli potential outcomes with means \(\mu_{0x},\mu_{1x}\), then drawing \(A\) independently of their joint vector given \(X\), and setting \(Y=Y(A)\); conversely, \(\operatorname{Obs}(P)\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:observed-model-class}\), choose parameters \(p_x,\pi_x,\mu_{ax}\) in the displayed ranges.  Summing the two outcome probabilities in arm \(a\) gives
\[
 s_a(q_x)=p_x\pi_x^a(1-\pi_x)^{1-a},\qquad s(q_x)=p_x,
 \qquad q_{a1,x}=s_a(q_x)\mu_{ax}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(p_x>0\), the bound \(\epsilon\leq\pi_x\leq1-\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{def:observed-model-class}\) implies
\(\epsilon s(q_x)\leq s_1(q_x)\leq(1-\epsilon)s(q_x)\); if \(p_x=0\), then \(q_x=0\) and the same inequalities hold.  Hence \(q_x\in\mathcal C_\epsilon\) by \(\mathrm{def:overlap-cone}\).  On this cone, \(s_a(q_x)\geq\epsilon s(q_x)\), so (1) and \(\mathrm{def:global-extension}\) yield
\[
 g_{a,\epsilon}(q_x)=\frac{s(q_x)q_{a1,x}}{s_a(q_x)}=p_x\mu_{ax},
 \qquad \bar f_\epsilon(q_x)=p_x\max_a\mu_{ax}                    \tag{2}
\]
on occupied cells.  Both sides of (2) are zero on null cells.  Summing (2) and invoking \(\mathrm{def:observed-optimal-value}\) proves \(\Psi(\mathbb P)=\sum_x\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\).

For the global bounds, put \(D_a(u)=\max\{s_a(u),\epsilon s(u)\}\).  Because \(0\leq u_{a1}\leq s_a(u)\leq D_a(u)\),
\[
 0\leq g_{a,\epsilon}(u)=\frac{s(u)u_{a1}}{D_a(u)}\leq s(u),        \tag{3}
\]
and (3) passes to the maximum over \(a\).  On \(s_a(u)\leq\epsilon s(u)\), one has \(g_{a,\epsilon}(u)=u_{a1}/\epsilon\), whose coordinate derivatives have absolute value at most \(\epsilon^{-1}\).  On \(s_a(u)\geq\epsilon s(u)\), write \(S=s(u)\), \(t=s_a(u)\), and \(z=u_{a1}\).  The derivatives of \(Sz/t\), respectively in a coordinate outside arm \(a\), the success coordinate in arm \(a\), and the failure coordinate in arm \(a\), are
\[
 \frac zt,\qquad \frac zt+\frac St\left(1-\frac zt\right),
 \qquad \frac zt-\frac St\frac zt.                                \tag{4}
\]
Here \(0\leq z/t\leq1\) and \(S/t\leq\epsilon^{-1}\), so every derivative in (4) has absolute value at most \(1+\epsilon^{-1}\).  The two formulas agree on \(t=\epsilon S\).  A line segment in \(\mathbb R_+^4\setminus\{0\}\) is divided by this hyperplane into at most two nontrivial pieces, and integration of the coordinate-gradient bound along those pieces gives
\[
 |g_{a,\epsilon}(u)-g_{a,\epsilon}(v)|
 \leq(1+\epsilon^{-1})\lVert u-v\rVert_1.                           \tag{5}
\]
At the origin, (5) follows by continuity and (3), since \(s(u)=\lVert u\rVert_1\).  Finally,
\[
 |\max_a g_{a,\epsilon}(u)-\max_a g_{a,\epsilon}(v)|
 \leq\max_a|g_{a,\epsilon}(u)-g_{a,\epsilon}(v)|,
\]
which proves the asserted Lipschitz bound, including coordinate faces and tie surfaces.

It remains to prove equivalence of the experiments.  For the chosen parameters of \(\mathbb P\), draw \(X\) with masses \(p_x\); conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(Y(0)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\mu_{0x})\) and \(Y(1)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\mu_{1x})\) independently, draw \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\pi_x)\) independently of their joint vector, and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  This construction satisfies consistency, satisfies the armwise conditional independence in \(\mathrm{ass:conditional-exchangeability}\), and has its observed margin equal to \(\mathbb P\) by the two formulas in \(\mathrm{def:observed-model-class}\).  Its observed margin also has the required overlap, so \(\mathrm{def:model-class}\) places the completion in \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).  Conversely, if \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\), then \(\mathrm{def:model-class}\) states that its observed margin belongs to \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  This proves both directions, including arbitrary null cells and boundary means.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition proves the observed cell representation, global analytic control, and surjectivity of armwise-exchangeable causal completions without defining away identification. \textit{Gap.} The primary discrete-covariate paper separates statistical and causal targets but does not supply this optimal-value extension and completion equivalence. \textit{Consumer.} Both the Jackson estimator and causal interpretation use this bridge.

\begin{lemma}[lem:simultaneous-jackson-certificate]\label{lem:simultaneous-jackson-certificate}
Let \(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\) have center \(b_x\) and positive radii \(r_{\jmath,x}\). The single polynomial \(P_{x,K_d}\) in \(\mathrm{def:jackson-kernel}\) has coordinate degree \(2(K_d-1)\) and satisfies, for every \(v\in Q_x\), \(\lvert P_{x,K_d}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(v)\rvert\leq C_\epsilon\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\{\sqrt{(v_{\jmath}-\ell_{\jmath,x})(u_{\jmath,x}-v_{\jmath})}/K_d+r_{\jmath,x}/K_d^2\}\). If \(P_{x,K_d}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)=\sum_\alpha b_{x,\alpha}((v-b_x)/r_x)^\alpha\), then \(\sum_\alpha\lvert b_{x,\alpha}\rvert\leq A^{K_d}(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x}\) for a universal \(A<\infty\). Both bounds hold for the same polynomial when \(Q_x\) touches the origin, a coordinate face, or a treatment-effect tie surface.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(B=1+\epsilon^{-1}\), which is a Lipschitz constant for \(\bar f_\epsilon\) by \(\mathrm{prop:identification-and-extension}\).  Suppress the cell subscript and write \(Q=\prod_{j=1}^4[\ell_j,u_j]\), \(b_j=(\ell_j+u_j)/2\), and \(r_j=(u_j-\ell_j)/2>0\).  For \(K=K_d\), define
\[
 H(\vartheta)=\bar f_\epsilon(b+r\odot\cos\vartheta),
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_KH(\vartheta)=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^4}H(\vartheta+t)\prod_{j=1}^4J_K(t_j)\,dt.
\]
This is exactly the construction in \(\mathrm{def:jackson-kernel}\).

The elementary inequalities
\[
 \left|\frac{\sin(Kt/2)}{\sin(t/2)}\right|
 \leq C\min\{K,|t|^{-1}\},
 \qquad |t|\leq\pi,
\]
and the reverse bound on \(|t|\leq K^{-1}\) show that the unnormalized fourth power has integral between constant multiples of \(K^3\).  Hence its normalized version obeys
\[
 0\leq J_K(t)\leq\frac{CK}{(1+K|t|)^4},\qquad
 \int|t|J_K(t)\,dt\leq\frac CK,\qquad
 \int t^2J_K(t)\,dt\leq\frac C{K^2}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The fourth power is an even trigonometric polynomial of degree \(2(K-1)\).  Convolution therefore makes \(\mathcal J_KH\) an even trigonometric polynomial, separately in each coordinate, of coordinate degree at most \(2(K-1)\).  Since an even trigonometric polynomial of degree \(D\) is a polynomial of degree \(D\) in \(\cos\vartheta\), this defines the polynomial \(P_{x,K_d}\) in the statement with the asserted coordinate degree.

If \(v=b+r\odot\cos\vartheta\), Taylor's formula for cosine with second derivative bounded by one gives
\[
 r_j|\cos(\vartheta_j+t_j)-\cos\vartheta_j|
 \leq r_j|\sin\vartheta_j|\,|t_j|+\frac12r_jt_j^2.
\]
Moreover
\[
 r_j|\sin\vartheta_j|=\sqrt{(v_j-\ell_j)(u_j-v_j)}.
\]
Apply the \(B\)-Lipschitz inequality to the integrand, integrate with (1), and sum over the four coordinates.  This proves
\[
 |P_{x,K_d}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(v)|
 \leq C_\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^4\left\{
 \frac{\sqrt{(v_j-\ell_j)(u_j-v_j)}}{K_d}+\frac{r_j}{K_d^2}
 \right\}.                                                               \tag{2}
\]

For the coefficient assertion, positivity and normalization of the product kernel imply
\[
 \sup_{v\in Q}|P_{x,K_d}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(b)|
 \leq B\sum_{j=1}^4r_j=:S.                                               \tag{3}
\]
Expand the left side of (3) in the tensor Chebyshev basis on \([-1,1]^4\).  The cosine integral formula bounds each tensor Chebyshev coefficient by \(16S\).  If \(c_h\) denotes the sum of the absolute monomial coefficients of the Chebyshev polynomial \(T_h\), then \(T_{h+1}=2zT_h-T_{h-1}\) gives
\[
 c_{h+1}\leq2c_h+c_{h-1},\qquad c_0=c_1=1,\qquad
 c_h\leq(1+\sqrt2)^h.
\]
Writing \(D=2(K_d-1)\), multiplying the four one-dimensional bounds and summing over at most \((D+1)^4\) tensor coefficients gives
\[
 \sum_\alpha|b_{x,\alpha}|
 \leq16S(D+1)^4(1+\sqrt2)^{4D}
 \leq A^{K_d}(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}r_{\jmath,x}
\]
for a universal finite \(A\).  Equations (2) and (3) concern the same convolution polynomial.  No part of the argument requires an interior rectangle or differentiability of \(\bar f_\epsilon\), so it applies when \(Q_x\) touches the origin, a coordinate face, or a tie surface.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact certificate the parent could not supply: pointwise vertex adaptation and the centered coefficient envelope are properties of one explicit polynomial. \textit{Gap.} JiaoHanWeissman2018L1 localizes a two-dimensional absolute difference and CaiLow2011L1 controls scalar absolute-value approximation; neither proves the joint four-dimensional observational-ratio certificate. \textit{Consumer.} The centered factorial lift uses the coefficient envelope while the global bias calculation uses the pointwise bound, so this lemma connects both halves of the estimator proof.

\begin{lemma}[lem:centered-factorial-pilot-control]\label{lem:centered-factorial-pilot-control}
In the uncapped Poisson comparison experiment induced by \(M\) and \(B_i\), conditional on the pilot counts the statistics \(U_h(N;z)\) satisfy \(E[U_h(N;z)]=(q-z)^h\) and \(E[U_h(N;z)U_t(N;z)]=\sum_{\ell=0}^{h\wedge t}\binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!(q/m)^\ell(q-z)^{h+t-2\ell}\). With the pilot rectangles and clipping in \(\mathrm{def:jackson-factorial-estimator}\), uniformly over \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(\lvert E[T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}]-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\rvert\leq C_\epsilon\{\sqrt{p_x/(mL_d)}+1/(mL_d)\}\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}})\leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}\{p_xL_d/m+L_d^2/m^2\}\). The contribution of pilot failure obeys these same local \(p_x\)-dependent scales.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), write \(q_{\jmath,x}=\mathbb P\{(A,Y)=\jmath,X=x\}\), put \(L=L_d\), \(m=n/8\), and set \(\tau=L/m\).  We work in the uncapped comparison experiment, namely with an infinite continuation of the independent observations allowed in \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\).  Since \(M\sim\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)=\operatorname{Pois}(2m)\), the joint probability generating function of all pilot and evaluation counts is
\[
 \begin{split}
 &E_{\mathbb P}\!\left[
   \prod_{x,\jmath}z_{\jmath,x}^{N'_{\jmath,x}}
                      w_{\jmath,x}^{N_{\jmath,x}}
 \right]\\
 &\quad=\exp\!\left[
 2m\left\{\sum_{x,\jmath}q_{\jmath,x}
       \frac{z_{\jmath,x}+w_{\jmath,x}}2-1\right\}
 \right]\\
 &\quad=\prod_{x,\jmath}
   \exp\{mq_{\jmath,x}(z_{\jmath,x}-1)\}
   \exp\{mq_{\jmath,x}(w_{\jmath,x}-1)\}.
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Thus the displayed counts are mutually independent, \(N'_{\jmath,x}\sim\operatorname{Pois}(mq_{\jmath,x})\), \(N_{\jmath,x}\sim\operatorname{Pois}(mq_{\jmath,x})\), and the entire pilot array is independent of the evaluation array.  This derives the required Poisson splitting directly from the sampling assumption and the construction in \(\mathrm{def:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}estimator}\).

For a scalar \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(mq)\), the definition of \(U_h\) and the binomial theorem give the identity of formal power series
\[
 \sum_{h=0}^{\infty}U_h(N;z)\frac{w^h}{h!}
 =e^{-zw}(1+w/m)^N.                                                \tag{2}
\]
Because every coefficient in (2) is a finite polynomial in \(N\), coefficient extraction after expectation is legitimate.  The Poisson generating function yields
\[
 E[e^{-zw}(1+w/m)^N]=e^{(q-z)w}                                   \tag{3}
\]
and, with two formal variables \(w,v\),
\[
 E\!\left[e^{-z(w+v)}(1+w/m)^N(1+v/m)^N\right]
 =\exp\{(q-z)(w+v)+(q/m)wv\}.                                    \tag{4}
\]
The coefficient of \(w^h/h!\) in (3) is \((q-z)^h\).  Expanding the last exponential in (4), the coefficient of \(w^hv^t/(h!t!)\) is
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{h\wedge t}
 \binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!(q/m)^\ell
 (q-z)^{h+t-2\ell}.                                               \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5), together with the pilot--evaluation independence in (1), prove both centered-factorial identities conditional on the pilot counts.

We next prove the local pilot bounds used by the Jackson branch of the estimator.  Fix \(x\), abbreviate \(q_\jmath=q_{\jmath,x}\), \(c_\jmath=N'_{\jmath,x}/m\), and let \(h_\jmath,\ell_\jmath,u_\jmath,b_\jmath,r_\jmath\) be the quantities in \(\mathrm{def:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}estimator}\).  Define
\[
 G_x:=\bigcap_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}
 \{|c_\jmath-q_\jmath|\leq h_\jmath/4\},
 \qquad
 v_x:=\sqrt{p_x\tau}+\tau.                                       \tag{6}
\]
Since \(h_\jmath=H_0\{\sqrt{c_\jmath\tau}+\tau\}>0\), every radius is positive.  Directly from the truncation at zero,
\[
 h_\jmath/2\leq r_\jmath\leq h_\jmath,
 \qquad |b_\jmath-c_\jmath|\leq h_\jmath/2.                     \tag{7}
\]
On \(G_x\), if \(c_\jmath\geq h_\jmath\), then \(b_\jmath=c_\jmath\) and \(|q_\jmath-b_\jmath|\leq r_\jmath\).  If \(c_\jmath<h_\jmath\), then \(\ell_\jmath=0\), while nonnegativity and \(q_\jmath\leq c_\jmath+h_\jmath/4<u_\jmath\) again give \(q_\jmath\in[\ell_\jmath,u_\jmath]\).  Hence
\[
 q_x\in Q_x\quad\hbox{on }G_x.                                  \tag{8}
\]

The inequality \(c\leq q+(H_0/4)(\sqrt{c\tau}+\tau)\) on \(G_x\), viewed as a quadratic inequality in \(\sqrt c\), implies \(c\leq C(q+\tau)\).  Substitution back into the reverse inequality gives \(q\leq C(c+\tau)\).  Therefore (7), \(\sum_\jmath q_\jmath=p_x\), and Cauchy--Schwarz in four coordinates imply
\[
 \sum_\jmath r_\jmath
 \leq C\left\{\sqrt{\tau}\sum_\jmath\sqrt{q_\jmath}+\tau\right\}
 \leq C v_x.                                                       \tag{9}
\]
Moreover, \(r_\jmath^2\geq(H_0^2/4)(c_\jmath\tau+\tau^2)\), so
\[
 \frac{q_\jmath}{m r_\jmath^2}
 \leq \frac{C(c_\jmath+\tau)}{m\tau(c_\jmath+\tau)}
 =\frac C L.                                                       \tag{10}
\]
No division by a cell probability was used.  In particular, if \(q_\jmath=0\), then its pilot and evaluation counts are zero almost surely, whereas its radius remains positive because \(\tau>0\).

We now control \(G_x^c\) at the same local scale.  If \(W\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)\), exponential Markov applied to
\[
 E e^{t(W-\lambda)}=\exp\{\lambda(e^t-1-t)\}
\]
and the corresponding expression for \(-t\) gives, for \(z\geq1\),
\[
 \Pr\{|W-\lambda|>\sqrt{2\lambda z}+2z\}\leq2e^{-z}.             \tag{11}
\]
Indeed, optimizing the Chernoff exponent and using \(e^t-1-t\leq t^2/[2(1-t/3)]\) for \(0<t<3\) gives the upper tail, and \(e^{-t}-1+t\leq t^2/2\) gives the lower tail.  Choose the fixed constant \(H_0\) sufficiently large.  Splitting into \(\lambda\geq C L\) and \(\lambda<C L\) shows that
\[
 |W-\lambda|\leq\sqrt{200\lambda L}+200L
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |W/m-\lambda/m|\leq\frac{H_0}{4}
 \{\sqrt{(W/m)\tau}+\tau\}.                                    \tag{12}
\]
For the first case, (12)'s premise gives \(W\geq\lambda/2\) after enlarging \(C\), so the square-root term controls \(\sqrt{\lambda L}/m\); for the second case the additive \(L/m\) term controls both \(\lambda/m\) and \(W/m\).  Thus the coordinate pilot-bad event is contained in the tail in (11) with \(z=100L\).

For completeness, integrating (11) over \(z\geq100L\), after substituting the threshold \(\sqrt{2\lambda z}+2z\), gives for \(s\in\{1,2,4\}\)
\[
 E\!\left[
 \left\{|W/m-\lambda/m|+\sqrt{(W/m)\tau}+\tau\right\}^{s}
 \mathbf 1_{\mathrm{bad}}
 \right]
 \leq C e^{-40L}\left\{\sqrt{(\lambda/m)\tau}+\tau\right\}^{s}. \tag{13}
\]
To verify the scale in (13), use \(\sqrt W\leq\sqrt\lambda+\sqrt{|W-\lambda|}\) and the layer-cake identity
\[
 E[R^s\mathbf 1\{R>r_0\}]
 =r_0^s\Pr(R>r_0)+\int_{r_0}^{\infty}s r^{s-1}\Pr(R>r)\,dr.
\]
For \(z\geq100L\), each fixed power of the ratio of \(\sqrt{\lambda z}+z\) to \(\sqrt{\lambda L}+L\) is at most a fixed polynomial in \(z/L\), whose integral against \(e^{-z}\) is at most \(C e^{-40L}\).  The identical calculation beginning at \(z=1\), together with the bounded central region, gives the unconditional version of (13) without the indicator and without the exponential factor.

The deterministic inequalities in (7) show that
\[
 \|b_x-q_x\|_1+\sum_\jmath r_\jmath
 \leq C\sum_\jmath
 \{|c_\jmath-q_\jmath|+\sqrt{c_\jmath\tau}+\tau\}.              \tag{14}
\]
The four pilot coordinates are independent by (1).  Apply a union bound to \(G_x^c\), use (13) on a bad coordinate, and use the unconditional moment bound following (13) on the other coordinates.  Cauchy--Schwarz with the fourth moments when \(s=2\) loses at most half the exponential exponent.  Since \(\sum_\jmath\sqrt{q_\jmath}\leq2\sqrt{p_x}\), this proves
\[
 E\!\left[
 \left\{\|b_x-q_x\|_1+\sum_\jmath r_\jmath\right\}^{s}
 \mathbf1_{G_x^c}\right]
 \leq C e^{-20L}v_x^s,
 \qquad s=1,2.                                                      \tag{15}
\]
Equation (9) also gives
\[
 E\!\left[\left(\sum_\jmath r_\jmath\right)^2
 \mathbf1_{G_x}\right]\leq C v_x^2.                              \tag{16}
\]
Thus exceptional pilots retain the local \(p_x\)-dependent scale.

We turn to approximation.  On \(G_x\), apply \(\mathrm{lem:simultaneous\mbox{-}jackson\mbox{-}certificate}\) to \(q_x\in Q_x\).  If \(\ell_\jmath=0\), then \(c_\jmath\leq h_\jmath\), and solving this inequality gives \(c_\jmath+h_\jmath\leq C\tau\).  Hence
\[
 \sqrt{(q_\jmath-\ell_\jmath)(u_\jmath-q_\jmath)}
 \leq C\sqrt{q_\jmath\tau},
 \qquad r_\jmath\leq C\tau.                                    \tag{17}
\]
If \(\ell_\jmath>0\), then \(c_\jmath>h_\jmath\); on \(G_x\), \(q_\jmath\geq3c_\jmath/4\) and \(r_\jmath=h_\jmath\leq C\sqrt{q_\jmath\tau}\).  Combining the two cases, the pointwise Jackson inequality and \(\sum_\jmath\sqrt{q_\jmath}\leq2\sqrt{p_x}\) give
\[
 |P_{x,K_d}(q_x)-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{
 \frac{\sqrt{p_x\tau}}{K_d}+\frac{\tau}{K_d^2}\right\}.         \tag{18}
\]
The same polynomial supplies the coefficient envelope required below; no second approximant is introduced.

Conditional on the pilot and on \(G_x\), apply (5) with \(t=h\), \(z=b_\jmath\), (8), and (10).  For every nonnegative integer \(h\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac{E\{U_h(N_{\jmath,x};b_\jmath)^2\}}{r_\jmath^{2h}}
 &\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^h\binom h\ell^2\ell!(C/L)^\ell\\
 &\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(Ch^2/L)^\ell}{\ell!}
 =\exp(Ch^2/L),
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{19}
\]
where \(\binom h\ell\leq h^\ell/\ell!\).  Factorial unbiasedness also gives
\[
 E(Z_x\mid N')=P_{x,K_d}(q_x).                                    \tag{20}
\]
Write
\[
 P_{x,K_d}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)
 =\sum_\alpha b_{x,\alpha}\{(v-b_x)/r_x\}^\alpha,
 \qquad
 S_x=(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_\jmath r_\jmath.
\]
On \(G_x\), evaluation counts are independent across coordinates by (1).  Minkowski's inequality in \(L_2\), (19), and the coefficient envelope in \(\mathrm{lem:simultaneous\mbox{-}jackson\mbox{-}certificate}\) therefore imply
\[
 E\!\left[\{Z_x-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)\}^2\mid N'\right]
 \leq S_x^2\exp(C_1K_d+C_2K_d^2/L)
 \quad\hbox{on }G_x.                                               \tag{21}
\]
Indeed each coordinate degree is at most \(2(K_d-1)\), so \(\sum_\jmath\alpha_\jmath^2\leq16K_d^2\); (19) controls the \(L_2\)-norm of each tensor factorial monomial, and summing those norms costs exactly the declared coefficient \(\ell_1\)-envelope.

Choose the fixed \(\kappa>0\) sufficiently small and then the fixed cutoff \(D_0\) sufficiently large, as permitted by their declarations.  Since \(K_d=\max\{2,\lfloor\kappa L\rfloor\}\), throughout the Jackson branch
\[
 \exp(C_1K_d+C_2K_d^2/L)\leq C d^{1/16}.                           \tag{22}
\]
Increasing \(C\) handles the finitely many values absorbed into the cutoff.

It remains to verify clipping.  Put \(W_x=Z_x-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)\) and \(t_x=d^{1/4}S_x>0\).  For every \(w\in\mathbb R\) and \(t>0\), direct consideration of \(|w|\leq t\) and \(|w|>t\) gives
\[
 |\Pi_{[-t,t]}(w)-w|\leq w^2/t,
 \qquad |\Pi_{[-t,t]}(w)|\leq|w|.                                \tag{23}
\]
On \(G_x\), equations (18), (20)--(23) imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 |E(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\mid N')-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 &\leq C_\epsilon\left\{
 \frac{\sqrt{p_x\tau}}{K_d}+\frac{\tau}{K_d^2}\right\}
   +CS_xd^{-3/16}.
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{24}
\]
For \(d\geq D_0\), \(K_d\geq cL\) and \(d^{-3/16}\leq C/L^2\).  Using (9) and \(v_x/L=\sqrt{p_x/(mL)}+1/(mL)\), (24) becomes
\[
 |E(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\mid N')-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\sqrt{\frac{p_x}{mL}}+\frac1{mL}\right\}
 \quad\hbox{on }G_x.                                              \tag{25}
\]

The global Lipschitz conclusion of \(\mathrm{prop:identification\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}extension}\), (8), and the definition of \(S_x\) give \(|\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|\leq S_x\) on \(G_x\).  Hence (21)--(23) also give
\[
 E\!\left[\{T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\}^2
 \mid N'\right]\mathbf1_{G_x}
 \leq C S_x^2d^{1/16}\mathbf1_{G_x}.                              \tag{26}
\]
On \(G_x^c\), clipping and the same global Lipschitz bound yield the deterministic inequality
\[
 |T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)|
 \leq(1+\epsilon^{-1})\|b_x-q_x\|_1+d^{1/4}S_x.                  \tag{27}
\]
By (15), the first moment of the right side on \(G_x^c\) is at most \(C_\epsilon d^{1/4}e^{-20L}v_x\), and its second moment is at most \(C_\epsilon d^{1/2}e^{-20L}v_x^2\).  Since
\[
 d^{1/4}e^{-20L}\leq C/L,
 \qquad d^{1/2}e^{-20L}\leq C d^{1/16},                            \tag{28}
\]
these are precisely bounded by the right side of (25) and by \(C_\epsilon d^{1/16}v_x^2\), respectively.  Thus (15) explicitly proves that pilot failure has the asserted local scale.

Finally integrate (25)--(27) over the pilot.  Equation (16) and
\[
 v_x^2=(\sqrt{p_xL/m}+L/m)^2
 \leq2\{p_xL/m+L^2/m^2\}                                        \tag{29}
\]
give
\[
 \left|E_{\mathbb P}T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\right|
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{\sqrt{\frac{p_x}{mL_d}}+\frac1{mL_d}\right\} \tag{30}
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P}(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}})
 \leq E_{\mathbb P}\{T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\}^2
 \leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
 \left\{\frac{p_xL_d}{m}+\frac{L_d^2}{m^2}\right\}.             \tag{31}
\]
All constants in (30)--(31) are uniform in \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and in the cell \(x\); their only nonuniversal dependence is on the fixed overlap constant \(\epsilon\).  The argument used neither a causal completion nor positivity of \(p_x\), an outcome mean, or a treatment-effect contrast, so it includes null cells, outcome-boundary means, unequal propensities, and exact ties.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact centered factorial identity prevents the polynomial center from reappearing in the variance, while local pilot tails retain the cell scale uniformly over the full observed-law class. \textit{Gap.} JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015Functionals and JiaoHanWeissman2018L1 use factorial moments after localization, but not with random four-coordinate ratio boxes, local clipping, and arbitrary cell masses. \textit{Consumer.} Summing these observed-law cell bounds yields the logarithmic upper rate without treating uncentered cell mean-squared errors as independent contributions.

\begin{theorem}[thm:jackson-factorial-upper]\label{thm:jackson-factorial-upper}
For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there is \(C_\epsilon<\infty\) such that the explicit all-data estimator \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}\) satisfies \(\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}E_{\mathbb P}[(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P))^2]\leq C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\) for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\).  The bound permits unknown unequal propensities, arbitrary occupied-cell masses, null cells, outcome means at zero or one, and exact treatment-effect ties.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}extension}\) gives
\[
 0\leq\Psi(\mathbb P)=\sum_x\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)
 \leq\sum_xs(q_x)=1
\]
and supplies a completion \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) whose observed margin is \(\mathbb P\).  Every statistic below depends only on the observed sample and the displayed auxiliary variables, so its law under the completion is its law under \(\mathbb P\).  Hence \(\mathrm{lem:centered\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}pilot\mbox{-}control}\) applies uniformly over the full observed model.

First suppose \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n\geq d/L_d\), and put \(m=n/8\).  In the uncapped Poisson comparison experiment, counts belonging to distinct \(x\)'s are independent by Poisson splitting; therefore the clipped statistics \(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\), including their pilot components, are independent.  The cell bias bound, \(\sum_xp_x=1\), and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \left|\sum_xE_{\mathbb P}T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right|^2
 \leq C_\epsilon\left\{
 \frac d{mL_d}+\frac{d^2}{m^2L_d^2}\right\}.                     \tag{15}
\]
Independence and the cell variance bound give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P}\left(\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\right)
 \leq C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
 \left\{\frac{L_d}{m}+\frac{dL_d^2}{m^2}\right\}.               \tag{16}
\]
Because \(m\geq d/(8L_d)\), the second term of (15), divided by \(d/(mL_d)\), is at most eight.  The two terms of (16), divided by \(d/(mL_d)\), are bounded respectively by
\[
 d^{-15/16}L_d^2,
 \qquad 8d^{-15/16}L_d^4,
\]
which are uniformly bounded for integer \(d\geq2\).  Thus
\[
 E_{\mathbb P}\left[
 \left\{\sum_xT_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right\}^2\right]
 \leq C_\epsilon\frac d{mL_d}.                                    \tag{17}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase squared distance from \(\Psi(\mathbb P)\in[0,1]\).

Adjoin an infinite independent continuation of the fixed sample.  The ideal uncapped Poisson statistic and the capped randomized statistic in \(\mathrm{def:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}estimator}\) agree on \(\{M\leq n\}\).  Both projected losses are at most one, whereas exponential Markov gives
\[
 \Pr\{M>n\}=\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)>n\}\leq e^{-cn}.       \tag{18}
\]
Equations (17)--(18) bound the randomized fixed-sample risk by
\[
 C_\epsilon\frac d{nL_d}+e^{-cn}.
\]
For \(d\geq2\), the exponential term is at most a universal multiple of \(d/(nL_d)\).  Conditional Jensen for squared loss then shows that the Rao--Blackwellized estimator
\[
 \widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=E(\widetilde V\mid O_1,\ldots,O_n)
\]
has no larger risk.

If \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<d/L_d\), the estimator is \(1/2\), its risk is at most \(1/4\), and \(d/(nL_d)>1\), so the minimum in the theorem equals one.

It remains to treat \(2\leq d<D_0\).  Conditional on \(\overline N_x=k>0\), the arm count \(\overline N_{ax}\) is binomial with success probability \(\rho_{ax}\geq\epsilon\).  For \(H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k,\rho)\),
\[
 E\frac{\mathbf1\{H>0\}}H\leq\frac2{k\rho},
 \qquad
 \Pr(H=0)=(1-\rho)^k\leq\frac1{k\rho}.                            \tag{19}
\]
The first inequality follows from \(H^{-1}\mathbf1\{H>0\}\leq2/(H+1)\) and
\[
 E(H+1)^{-1}=\frac{1-(1-\rho)^{k+1}}{(k+1)\rho};
\]
the second follows from \((1-\rho)^k\leq(1+k\rho)^{-1}\).  Conditional binomial variance and the \(0/0=0\) convention imply
\[
 E\{(\widehat\mu_{ax}-\mu_{ax})^2\mid\overline N_x=k\}
 \leq\frac3{k\epsilon}.                                            \tag{20}
\]
The maximum is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm.  Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz followed by (20) yields
\[
 E\left[
 \left\{\sum_x\widehat p_x
 (\max_a\widehat\mu_{ax}-\max_a\mu_{ax})\right\}^2\right]
 \leq\frac{6d}{n\epsilon}.                                         \tag{21}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 E\lVert\widehat p-p\rVert_1^2
 \leq d\sum_x\operatorname{Var}(\widehat p_x)\leq\frac dn.       \tag{22}
\]
Decomposing the empirical-ratio error into the regression term in (21) and the mass-estimation term in (22), and also using that both the estimator and target lie in \([0,1]\), gives risk at most \(C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/n\}\).  Since \(L_d\) is bounded above and below for \(2\leq d<D_0\), this is at most
\[
 C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/(nL_d)\}.
\]
All bounds are uniform over \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), so they permit unequal unknown propensities, arbitrary masses, null cells, overlap boundaries, outcome boundaries, and exact ties.  This proves the theorem in every regime.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit estimator attains the logarithmically improved uniform rate on the full observed model. \textit{Gap.} Neither the parent empirical-ratio proof nor normalized-distance estimators cover unknown observational arm denominators. \textit{Consumer.} High-dimensional categorical adjustment obtains a worst-case point-risk guarantee robust to null cells and ties.

\begin{proposition}[prop:equal-propensity-l1-reduction]\label{prop:equal-propensity-l1-reduction}
Every \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\) mapped by \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\) has an observed margin in \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) with \(p_x=(P_x+Q_x)/2\), \(\pi_x=1/2\), and \(\Psi=1/2+\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1/4\).  From \(n\) draws from each of \(\mathbf P\) and \(\mathbf Q\), an explicit Markov kernel produces exactly \(n\) independent observations from that margin, so \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is at least one sixteenth of the corresponding normalized two-sample \(L_1\) minimax mean-squared error.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\) and apply \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\).  Summing its four masses gives
\[
 p_x=\sum_{a,y}q_{ay,x}=\frac{P_x+Q_x}{2},\qquad
 s_1(q_x)=s_0(q_x)=\frac{P_x+Q_x}{4}.                               \tag{14}
\]
Thus every occupied cell has \(\pi_x=1/2\), which lies in \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\) because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  On a cell with \(P_x+Q_x>0\),
\[
 \mu_{1x}=\frac{P_x}{P_x+Q_x},\qquad
 \mu_{0x}=\frac{Q_x}{P_x+Q_x}.                                     \tag{15}
\]
Equations (14)--(15) and \(0\leq\mu_{ax}\leq1\) verify every condition of \(\mathrm{def:observed-model-class}\); null cells contribute zero.  By \(\mathrm{def:observed-optimal-value}\),
\[
 \Psi=\frac12\sum_x\max\{P_x,Q_x\}
 =\frac12+\frac14\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1,              \tag{16}
\]
using \(\max(r,s)=(r+s+|r-s|)/2\) and the two simplex constraints.

Let \(X_i^P\sim\mathbf P\) and \(X_i^Q\sim\mathbf Q\), independently for \(i=1,\ldots,n\).  For each \(i\), toss an independent fair source coin.  If it selects \(\mathbf P\), use \(X_i^P\) and choose \((A,Y)=(1,1)\) or \((0,0)\) with equal probabilities; if it selects \(\mathbf Q\), use \(X_i^Q\) and choose \((A,Y)=(1,0)\) or \((0,1)\) with equal probabilities.  The resulting observations are independent and have exactly the masses in \(\mathrm{def:l1-embedding}\).

For any observed-law estimator \(\widehat V\), define the randomized distance estimator \(\widehat L=4(\widehat V-1/2)\), and condition it on the two original samples.  Equation (16) and conditional Jensen give
\[
 E_{\mathbf P,\mathbf Q}\left[
 \left\{E(\widehat L\mid X^P,X^Q)-\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1\right\}^2\right]
 \leq16E_{\mathbb P}\left[(\widehat V-\Psi(\mathbb P))^2\right]. \tag{17}
\]
Taking the supremum over \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\) and then the infimum over all \(\widehat V\) proves the factor-one-sixteenth reduction.  The kernel uses exactly \(n\) observations and introduces no limiting or support approximation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition transfers a published normalized-distance obstruction through an exact observed-data Markov kernel. \textit{Gap.} The source paper does not state the four-cell embedding or the \(\Psi\) identity. \textit{Consumer.} The converse uses this slice in the middle and saturated regimes.

\begin{lemma}[lem:dense-moment-matching-lower]\label{lem:dense-moment-matching-lower}
Choose the universal cutoff \(D_0\) sufficiently large.  For every \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n>d^2\), the construction in \(\mathrm{def:dense-moment-matching-construction}\) has even degree \(8L_d\leq K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}<8L_d+2\), amplitude \(a_{n,d}^2=K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}d/(128n)\leq1/4\), and, for every \(\theta\in[-a_{n,d},a_{n,d}]^d\), an observed margin \(\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) satisfying \(p_x=1/d\), \(\pi_x=1/2\), \(\mu_{1x}=(1+\theta_x)/2\), \(\mu_{0x}=(1-\theta_x)/2\), and \(\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}=1/2+(2d)^{-1}\sum_{x=1}^d\lvert\theta_x\rvert\).  The symmetric measures \(\nu_{0,K}\) and \(\nu_{1,K}\) exist for every even \(K\), match moments through degree \(K\), and have absolute-moment gap \(2E_K\) with \(E_K\asymp K^{-1}\).  Consequently \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}=a_{n,d}E_{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}}\asymp a_{n,d}/K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}\).  Writing \(\lambda_{n,d}=2n/d\), the one-cell likelihood identity under \(\theta_x=0\) is \(E_0[L_{\theta_x}L_{\theta_x'}]=\exp(\lambda_{n,d}\theta_x\theta_x')\); hence moment matching and tensorization give
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb M_{0,n,d},\mathbb M_{1,n,d})
\leq d\left\{\sum_{r=K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{n,d}a_{n,d}^2)^r}{r!}\right\}^{1/2}
\leq d\left\{\frac{(e/64)^{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}+1}}{1-e/64}\right\}^{1/2}\leq\frac1{16}.
\]
Under either product prior, \(\operatorname{Var}[\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}]\leq a_{n,d}^2/(4d)\), and \(D_0\) can be chosen so that each prior assigns probability at most \(1/8\) to a deviation of the target from its prior mean exceeding \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}/4\).  The two fuzzy hypotheses therefore imply \(\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{2n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_\epsilon(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}})^2\geq c_\epsilon d/[nK_d^{\mathrm{lb}}]\).  Finally, applying any fixed-sample estimator to the first \(n\) observations whenever an independent \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) total count is at least \(n\) yields \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{2n,d,\epsilon}-P\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\geq c_\epsilon d/[n\log(ed)]\), after decreasing \(c_\epsilon\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(K=K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}=2\lceil4L_d\rceil\).  Then \(K\) is even and
\[
 8L_d\leq K<8L_d+2.                                                  \tag{18}
\]
With \(\lambda=\lambda_{n,d}=2n/d\), the construction gives
\[
 a_{n,d}^2=\frac K{64\lambda}=\frac{Kd}{128n}<\frac K{128d}.
\]
Because \(K=O(\log d)\), a universal \(D_0\) makes this at most \(1/4\) for every \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n>d^2\).

For \(|\theta_x|\leq a_{n,d}\leq1/2\), the two Bernoulli means in \(\mathrm{def:dense-moment-matching-construction}\) lie in \([1/4,3/4]\); its observed margin has \(p_x=1/d\), \(\pi_x=1/2\), and binary conditional means in \([0,1]\).  Thus \(\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), since \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  The definition of \(\Psi\) gives
\[
 \Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}
 =\frac1d\sum_x\max\left\{\frac{1+\theta_x}{2},\frac{1-\theta_x}{2}\right\}
 =\frac12+\frac1{2d}\sum_x|\theta_x|.                              \tag{19}
\]

The cited leaf \(\mathrm{lem:cai-low-absolute-moment-priors}\) supplies, for this even \(K\), the symmetric measures with matching moments through \(K\), absolute-moment gap \(2E_K\), and constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) for which \(c/K\leq E_K\leq C/K\).  Scaling by \(a=a_{n,d}\) and using (19) yields
\[
 \Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}=\frac1{2d}\,d\,(2aE_K)=aE_K
 \asymp\frac aK.                                                     \tag{20}
\]

In the \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) experiment, Poisson splitting makes the two sign counts in one cell independent with means \(\lambda(1+\theta_x)/2\) and \(\lambda(1-\theta_x)/2\).  Relative to \(\theta_x=0\), their likelihood ratio is
\[
 L_{\theta_x}=(1+\theta_x)^{N_+}(1-\theta_x)^{N_-}.
\]
The Poisson generating function gives the exact identity
\[
 E_0(L_{\theta_x}L_{\theta_x'})
 =\exp(\lambda\theta_x\theta_x').                                  \tag{21}
\]
Let \(h_0,h_1\) be the one-cell mixtures under the two scaled priors and \(h_\circ\) the density at \(\theta_x=0\).  Expanding (21) into its nonnegative power series and using cancellation of moments through \(K\) gives
\[
 \int\frac{(h_1-h_0)^2}{h_\circ}
 =\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^r}{r!}
   \{E_1\theta_x^r-E_0\theta_x^r\}^2
 \leq4\sum_{r=K+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda a^2)^r}{r!}.           \tag{22}
\]
Here each remaining moment difference has absolute value at most \(2a^r\).  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds one-cell total variation by the square root of the tail in (22), because its usual factor \(1/2\) cancels the factor \(2\).  Independence across cells under the product priors and telescoping of product measures therefore yield
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\mathbb M_{0,n,d},\mathbb M_{1,n,d})
 \leq d\left\{\sum_{r=K+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda a^2)^r}{r!}\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{23}
\]
Since \(\lambda a^2=K/64\) and \(r!\geq(r/e)^r\), for \(r\geq K+1\),
\[
 \frac{(\lambda a^2)^r}{r!}\leq(e/64)^r.
\]
Using \(K\geq8\log(ed)\), (23) is at most
\[
 d\left\{\frac{(e/64)^{K+1}}{1-e/64}\right\}^{1/2}\leq\frac1{16} \tag{24}
\]
after increasing \(D_0\).

Under either product prior, coordinate independence and (19) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}[\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P_\theta^{\mathrm{dense}})\}]
 =\frac1{4d^2}\sum_x\operatorname{Var}(|\theta_x|)
 \leq\frac{a^2}{4d}.                                                 \tag{25}
\]
Chebyshev's inequality, obtained by applying Markov's inequality to the squared centered target, and (20) bound either prior probability outside radius \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}/4\) by
\[
 \frac{16(a^2/4d)}{a^2E_K^2}=\frac4{dE_K^2}\leq C\frac{K^2}{d}\leq\frac18 \tag{26}
\]
for the same sufficiently large \(D_0\).

Let \(r_\star\) be the larger integrated squared risk of an arbitrary estimator under the two priors, and test the prior index by thresholding the estimator at the midpoint of the two target means.  On the concentration event in (26), estimation error smaller than \(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}}/4\) forces a correct test.  Markov's inequality therefore bounds the sum of the two testing errors by
\[
 \frac14+\frac{32r_\star}{(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}})^2}.
\]
For every test this sum is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb M_0,\mathbb M_1)\geq15/16\), by integration against a common dominating measure.  Hence
\[
 r_\star\geq c(\Delta_{n,d}^{\mathrm{dense}})^2
 \geq c\frac{a^2}{K^2}=c\frac1{\lambda K}=c\frac d{nK}.           \tag{27}
\]
All prior observation laws lie in \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), so (27) lower-bounds \(\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{2n,d,\epsilon}\).

Finally clip any fixed-\(n\) estimator to \([0,1]\), which cannot increase its loss.  Apply it to the first \(n\) observations of a \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) sample when at least \(n\) observations are present, and output zero otherwise.  Its Poisson risk is at most its fixed-sample risk plus \(P\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\).  The Poisson lower-tail Chernoff bound is \(e^{-n/4}\).  Taking infima and using (18) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c\frac d{nL_d}-e^{-n/4}
 \geq c'\frac d{nL_d},                                               \tag{28}
\]
where the last inequality is uniform for \(d\geq D_0,n>d^2\) after one further increase of \(D_0\).  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma supplies the missing lower range \(n>d^2\) through an explicit observed subclass and exact Poisson mixture calculation. \textit{Gap.} The cited moment-prior result does not contain the observational sign experiment or its target concentration. \textit{Consumer.} The all-regime lower theorem invokes it in the superquadratic-sample regime.

\begin{theorem}[thm:all-estimator-lower]\label{thm:all-estimator-lower}
For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there is \(c_\epsilon>0\) such that the observed-law minimax risk satisfies \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\) for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\).  This covers all measurable estimators over \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) by splicing the dense Poisson moment-matching subclass when \(n>d^2\), the exact normalized \(L_1\) subclass when \(d/\log(ed)\leq n\leq d^2\), and null-cell padding plus a regular two-point subclass in the saturated and bounded-alphabet regimes.
\end{theorem}
Write \(L_d=\log(ed)\).  We first give a regular observed-law submodel used below.  Put all mass on \(X=1\), take \(A\) fair, fix \(\mu_{01}=0\), and compare \(\mu_{11}=1/2\) with \(\mu_{11}=1/2+\delta\), where \(\delta=1/(8\sqrt n)\).  Both binary laws belong to \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), including after padding with null cells.  Their \(\Psi\)-values differ by \(\delta\).  Their one-observation chi-square divergence, computed from the two affected probabilities \(1/4\pm\delta/2\), is
\[
 \chi^2(\mathbb P_1,\mathbb P_0)
 =\frac{(\delta/2)^2}{1/4}+\frac{(\delta/2)^2}{1/4}=2\delta^2.
\]
Product expansion and \(1+t\leq e^t\) give
\[
 \chi^2(\mathbb P_1^n,\mathbb P_0^n)
 =(1+2\delta^2)^n-1\leq e^{1/32}-1.                                \tag{29}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz against \(\mathbb P_0^n\) implies \(\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb P_1^n,\mathbb P_0^n)\leq\tfrac12\sqrt{e^{1/32}-1}<1/4\).  Thresholding an arbitrary estimator at the target midpoint, and applying Markov's inequality to squared error under the two laws, therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c/n.                               \tag{30}
\]
Indeed the sum of testing errors is at least \(1-\operatorname{TV}>3/4\), whereas it is at most eight times the larger squared risk divided by \(\delta^2\).

We now cover four exhaustive regimes.  If \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n>d^2\), \(\mathrm{lem:dense-moment-matching-lower}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c\frac d{nL_d};                    \tag{31}
\]
the ratio is below one in this regime.

Let \(c_0\geq2\) be a universal constant large enough that the sample-size hypothesis of the cited \(\mathrm{lem:jhw\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}l1\mbox{-}lower}\) is \(n\geq c_0d/L_d\).  Suppose \(d\geq D_0\), \(c_0d/L_d\leq n\leq d^2\).  Applied with alphabet \(d\) and Poisson means \(2n\), that lemma has both of its hypotheses: the first is the displayed lower bound on \(n\), and \(\log(en)\leq\log(ed^2)\leq2L_d\).  It gives the Poissonized two-sample lower bound \(c d/[n\log(en)]\geq c_1d/(nL_d)\).  Given a fixed-\(n\) two-sample estimator clipped to \([0,2]\), apply it to the first \(n\) observations from each of two independent \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\) samples when both totals exceed \(n\), and output zero otherwise.  The two Poisson lower-tail probabilities are at most \(2e^{-n/4}\); bounded loss therefore gives

\[
 \mathfrak R^{L_1}_{n,d}\geq c\frac d{nL_d}-Ce^{-n/4}
 \geq c_1\frac d{nL_d},                                              
\tag{32}
\]

after increasing \(D_0\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:equal\mbox{-}propensity\mbox{-}l1\mbox{-}reduction}\) transfers (32), with factor \(1/16\), to \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\).  The embedded margins are a subclass of \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), so this restriction lower-bounds the supremum over the full observed class.

It remains to cover \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<c_0d/L_d\); throughout this range the desired minimum is bounded below by \(1/c_0\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), put \(L_n=\log(en)\) and \(s_n=\lfloor nL_n/(2c_0)\rfloor\).  Increase the fixed numerical cutoff \(n_0\) so that \(L_n\geq2c_0\) for \(n\geq n_0\).  In this range the displayed inequality forces \(d\geq n\): otherwise it implies \(L_d<c_0\), hence both \(d\) and \(n<c_0d/L_d\) belong to a finite set, which is excluded by increasing \(n_0\).  Consequently \(L_d\geq L_n\) and

\[
 d>nL_d/c_0\geq nL_n/c_0\geq s_n\geq n,
 \qquad c_0\frac{s_n}{\log(es_n)}\leq\frac n2.                    
\tag{33}
\]

The second inequality uses \(\log(es_n)\geq L_n\).  Thus the cited lemma applies at alphabet \(s_n\), with its stated logarithm condition as well, and gives a positive universal lower bound because \(s_n/[n\log(en)]\geq1/(4c_0)\) after increasing \(n_0\).  The same fixed/Poisson transfer and exact reduction give \(\mathfrak R_{n,s_n,\epsilon}\geq c\).  Padding the remaining \(d-s_n\) cells by zeros preserves both the observation law and \(\Psi\), hence

\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c.                                 
\tag{34}
\]

For the finitely many \(n<n_0\) in this remaining range, use the preceding two-point pair with the fixed separation \(1/(8\sqrt{n_0})\).  Equation (29) remains uniformly bounded, while the target gap is a fixed positive constant; decreasing \(c\) proves (34) there as well.If \(d\geq D_0\) and \(n<d/L_d\), the desired scale equals one.  For every sufficiently large \(n\), put \(s_n=\lfloor n\log(en)\rfloor\).  The regime implies \(d>nL_d>n\log(en)\), since it first implies \(d>n\) and hence \(L_d>\log(en)\).  Thus \(2\leq s_n\leq d\).  Also \(s_n\asymp n\log(en)\), so
\[
 n\geq c\frac{s_n}{\log(es_n)},\qquad \log(en)\leq C\log(es_n)   \tag{33}
\]
with universal constants.  The cited \(L_1\) lower lemma at alphabet \(s_n\), followed by the same fixed/Poisson transfer and the exact reduction, gives a positive constant.  Padding the remaining \(d-s_n\) cells by zeros preserves both the observation law and \(\Psi\), hence
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c.                                 \tag{34}
\]
For the finitely many smaller \(n\), use the preceding two-point pair with the fixed separation \(1/(8\sqrt{n_0})\), where \(n_0\) is the finite cutoff.  Equation (29) then remains uniformly bounded for \(n\leq n_0\), while the target gap is a fixed positive constant; decreasing \(c\) proves (34) there as well.

Finally, if \(2\leq d<D_0\), (30) applies.  Since \(d/L_d\) is bounded above and away from zero on this finite set, there is a universal constant depending only on the fixed cutoff such that
\[
 \frac cn\geq c'\min\left\{1,\frac d{nL_d}\right\}.              \tag{35}
\]
Equations (31)--(35) cover every \(n\geq1,d\geq2\).  Every hard margin has propensity \(1/2\), so it lies in the full observed class for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  Decreasing a single constant over the finitely many cutoffs proves the theorem for all measurable estimators.

\begin{theorem}[thm:matched-minimax-frontier]\label{thm:matched-minimax-frontier}
For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there are constants \(0<c_\epsilon\leq C_\epsilon<\infty\) such that, for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\),
\[
c_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\leq\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\leq\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}E_{\mathbb P}[(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P))^2]
\leq C_\epsilon\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}.
\]
Through \(\mathrm{prop:causal-optimal-value-corollary}\), the same risk frontier applies to the causal optimal-treatment value over all causal completions whose observed margins range over \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\end{theorem}
Let \(c_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm L}>0\) be the constant supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:all\mbox{-}estimator\mbox{-}lower}\), and let \(C_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm U}<\infty\) be the constant supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}upper}\).  These two established results apply for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\) under the common observed-law class \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \geq c_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm L}
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\},                                      \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}
 E_{\mathbb P}\!\left[
 \left\{\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right\}^{2}
 \right]
 \leq C_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm U}
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}.                                      \tag{2}
\]

It remains to justify the middle inequality rather than identify it with either bound.  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}risk}\), \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) is the infimum, over all measurable sample maps, of the displayed worst-case squared risk.  The explicit all-data estimator whose admissibility and risk are asserted by \(\mathrm{thm:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}upper}\) is one such map.  Evaluating an infimum at this particular admissible estimator therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq
 \sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}
 E_{\mathbb P}\!\left[
 \left\{\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right\}^{2}
 \right].                                                                            \tag{3}
\]
Set \(c_\epsilon=c_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm L}\) and \(C_\epsilon=\max\{C_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm U},c_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm L}\}\).  Then \(0<c_\epsilon\leq C_\epsilon<\infty\), and concatenating \((1)\), \((3)\), and \((2)\) proves the three inequalities in the theorem.

Finally, the causal assertion is a transfer, not a definition of the causal target.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}extension}\) proves both inclusions needed for the experiment: every \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) has \(\operatorname{Obs}(P)\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and every \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) has at least one completion \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) with \(\operatorname{Obs}(P)=\mathbb P\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:causal\mbox{-}optimal\mbox{-}value\mbox{-}corollary}\), using consistency and armwise conditional exchangeability, proves for every such completion that
\[
 V^\star(P)=\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P)\}.
\]
Therefore, for every observed-data estimator \(\widehat V\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_{\operatorname{Obs}(P)}\!\left[
 \left\{\widehat V-V^\star(P)\right\}^{2}
 \right]
 =
 \sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}
 E_{\mathbb P}\!\left[
 \left\{\widehat V-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right\}^{2}
 \right].                                                                             \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, the first inclusion gives the inequality from left to right in \((4)\), while the completion of each \(\mathbb P\) and the target equality give the reverse inequality.  Taking the infimum over the same measurable observed-data maps on both sides of \((4)\) transfers the already proved statistical frontier to \(V^\star(P)\), which establishes the final sentence.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem combines the explicit Jackson--factorial upper bound, the all-measurable-estimator converse, and the causal identification bridge into the exact all-regime sample-size law. \textit{Gap.} The closest accepted in-repository theorem treats a signed aggregate on the same overlap cone with a different rate and construction; no cited result combines the observational ratio maximum, a full-class logarithmic estimator, and an all-regime converse. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations of individualized optimal-treatment value estimation with high-dimensional categorical adjustment can use the frontier as a direct MSE and sample-size law for data-adaptive treatment maximization.

\begin{proposition}[prop:parent-reduction]\label{prop:parent-reduction}
The matched frontier recovers the archived downgraded predecessor as a corollary on the identical class: uniformly over \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\), the new upper rate is at most a constant multiple of \(d/n\), while its lower rate is at least a constant multiple of \(\min\{1,\sqrt d/n+d/[n\log(en)]\}\). For every \(2\leq d<D_0\), \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}\). Separately, along each fixed bounded sequence of alphabets, the risk has two-sided \(n^{-1}\) bounds whose comparison constants may depend on \(\epsilon\) and on the sequence and its finite alphabet bound.
\end{proposition}
Put
\[
 r_{n,d}=\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\},
 \qquad a=d/[n\log(ed)].
\]
Since \(\log(ed)\geq1\), a two-case check according as \(d\geq n\) or \(d<n\) gives \(r_{n,d}\leq d/n\).

Next,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt d/n}{d/[n\log(ed)]}=\frac{\log(ed)}{\sqrt d}
\]
is bounded uniformly over integers \(d\geq2\).  If \(a>1\), the parent minimum is at most one and hence at most \(r_{n,d}=1\).  Suppose \(a\leq1\).  If \(n\geq d\), then \(\log(en)\geq\log(ed)\), so
\[
 \frac d{n\log(en)}\leq a.
\]
If \(n<d\), put \(t=d/n>1\).  The inequality \(a\leq1\) says
\[
 t\leq\log(ed)=\log(en)+\log t.
\]
For \(t\geq2\), \(\log t\leq t/2\), hence \(t\leq2\log(en)\); the case \(1<t<2\) obeys the same conclusion after changing the universal constant.  Therefore \(\log(ed)\leq C\log(en)\), and \(d/[n\log(en)]\leq Ca\).  We have proved
\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac d{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \leq C r_{n,d}.                                                     \tag{23}
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{thm:matched\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}frontier}\) to these deterministic comparisons proves that the new upper rate is bounded by a constant multiple of \(d/n\) and that the new lower rate dominates the stated parent lower expression on the identical model class.

The equality \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}\) for \(2\leq d<D_0\) is the first branch of \(\mathrm{def:jackson\mbox{-}factorial\mbox{-}estimator}\).  Finally, along a sequence for which \(2\leq d\leq D<\infty\), the quantity \(d/\log(ed)\) is bounded above and bounded away from zero.  The matched frontier therefore gives
\[
 c_{\epsilon,D}n^{-1}\leq\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq C_{\epsilon,D}n^{-1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), which is the asserted bounded-alphabet parametric conclusion.  These constants are chosen after fixing the sequence and may depend on \(\epsilon\) and its finite bound \(D\).
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This explicit corner check establishes that the run is an estimation refinement of the archived downgraded predecessor rather than a different model or estimand. \textit{Gap.} The archived downgraded predecessor leaves a nonmatching bracket and an unresolved consistency region; this proposition records exactly how every certified predecessor conclusion is retained. \textit{Consumer.} Downstream users of the predecessor bracket can substitute the matched rate without changing their causal assumptions or scalar target.

\begin{theorem}[thm:consistency-and-parametric-boundaries]\label{thm:consistency-and-parametric-boundaries}
Along arbitrary sequences \(n\to\infty\) with \(d=d_n\geq2\), uniform consistency for estimating \(\Psi(\mathbb P)\) over \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) is possible if and only if \(d=o(n\log(en))\).  Moreover, along each fixed alphabet sequence, two-sided parametric mean-squared-error bounds hold if and only if \(d=O(1)\); for a bounded sequence the comparison constants may depend on \(\epsilon\) and on that sequence and its finite alphabet bound, and are not uniform over all bounded sequences.  By \(\mathrm{prop:causal-optimal-value-corollary}\) and observed-margin surjectivity, the same two equivalences hold for estimating \(V^\star(P)\) over \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).
\end{theorem}
By \(\mathrm{thm:matched\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}frontier}\), uniform consistency is equivalent to
\[
 \frac d{n\log(ed)}\longrightarrow0.                               \tag{24}
\]
We prove that (24) is equivalent to \(d=o(n\log(en))\).  Put \(t=d/n\) and \(L_n=\log(en)\).  If \(t/L_n\to0\), then on any subsequence with \(d\geq n\),
\[
 \log(ed)\geq L_n,
 \qquad
 \frac d{n\log(ed)}\leq\frac t{L_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
On a subsequence with \(d<n\), if \(d\geq\sqrt n\), then \(\log(ed)\geq L_n/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), so
\[
 \frac d{n\log(ed)}\leq\frac2{L_n}\longrightarrow0;
\]
if \(d<\sqrt n\), that ratio is at most \(n^{-1/2}\).  Thus (24) follows.

Conversely, assume (24).  If \(t<1\), then \(t/L_n\leq1/L_n\to0\).  If \(t\geq1\), define
\[
 \eta_n=\frac{t}{L_n+\log t}.
\]
Because \(L_n+\log t=\log(ed)\), equation (24) says \(\eta_n\to0\).  Since \(\log t\leq t\), eventually \(\eta_n\log t\leq t/2\).  The identity
\[
 t=\eta_nL_n+\eta_n\log t
\]
then gives \(t/L_n\leq2\eta_n\to0\).  This proves the consistency equivalence.

Multiplying the matched frontier by \(n\) shows that parametric mean-squared risk is equivalent, up to constants, to boundedness above and away from zero of
\[
 n\min\left\{1,\frac d{n\log(ed)}\right\}.                         \tag{25}
\]
If \(d=O(1)\) and \(d\geq2\), then \(d/\log(ed)\) is bounded above and away from zero, so (25) is bounded above and away from zero for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Conversely, if \(d\to\infty\) along a subsequence, then either the minimum in (25) equals one, in which case (25) equals \(n\to\infty\), or it equals \(d/[n\log(ed)]\), in which case (25) equals
\[
 \frac d{\log(ed)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus, for each fixed sequence, parametric mean-squared risk holds exactly for bounded alphabets; the comparison constants may depend on \(\epsilon\) and the finite alphabet bound, as in \(c_{\epsilon,D},C_{\epsilon,D}\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop:identification\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}extension}\) gives surjectivity of \(P\mapsto\operatorname{Obs}(P)\) from \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) onto \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and \(\mathrm{prop:causal\mbox{-}optimal\mbox{-}value\mbox{-}corollary}\) gives equality of the causal and observed targets.  Hence the causal and observed minimax experiments have identical attainable worst-case risks, and both equivalences transfer to estimation of \(V^\star(P)\) over \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The phase theorem converts the matched rate into exact operational consistency and parametric criteria. \textit{Gap.} The parent leaves the region between \(d=o(n)\) and \(d=o(n\log n)\) unresolved. \textit{Consumer.} Workflows can determine when expanding a categorical adjustment table destroys uniform reliability.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:sharp-overlap-constant]\label{oeq:sharp-overlap-constant}
Along each nonsaturated sequence with \(d\to\infty\) and \(d/[n\log(ed)]\to0\), does the normalized risk \((n\log(ed)/d)\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) converge to a finite positive limit, and can an estimator attaining that limit be derived through \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{const}}\)?
\end{openendedquestion}
The matched frontier proves only
\[
 0<c_\epsilon\leq\liminf\frac{n\log(ed)}d\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq\limsup\frac{n\log(ed)}d\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\leq C_\epsilon<\infty
\]
along every nonsaturated sequence in the question.  It neither proves equality of the liminf and limsup nor identifies a cone-dependent optimal leading constant or an attaining estimator.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The matched rate makes a correctly normalized constant question well posed and asks whether the estimation upgrade can be sharpened from comparison constants to exact efficiency. \textit{Gap.} CaiLow2011L1 obtains a Bernstein-constant characterization in a Gaussian absolute-value experiment, while JiaoHanWeissman2018L1 gives rate equivalence for multinomial distance; neither identifies the overlap-cone constant. \textit{Consumer.} An exact constant would calibrate polynomial degree and quantify the efficiency price of unknown observational treatment imbalance.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cai-low-absolute-moment-priors]\label{lem:cai-low-absolute-moment-priors}
For every even integer \(K\), let \(E_K=\inf_{p:\deg(p)\leq K}\sup_{t\in[-1,1]}\lvert\lvert t\rvert-p(t)\rvert\).  There exist symmetric probability measures \(\nu_{0,K}\) and \(\nu_{1,K}\) on \([-1,1]\) whose moments agree through degree \(K\) and such that \(\int\lvert t\rvert\,d\nu_{1,K}-\int\lvert t\rvert\,d\nu_{0,K}=2E_K\).  Moreover, there are universal constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that \(c/K\leq E_K\leq C/K\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:jhw-poisson-l1-lower]\label{lem:jhw-poisson-l1-lower}
For every fixed pair of positive constants \(c_0,C_0\), there is \(c_1(c_0,C_0)>0\) such that, whenever \(d\geq2\), \(n\geq c_0d/\log(ed)\), and \(\log(en)\leq C_0\log(ed)\), the minimax mean-squared error for estimating \(\lVert\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\rVert_1\) from two independent samples of respective sizes \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n)\), one from each of \(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q\in\Delta_d\), is at least \(c_1(c_0,C_0)\min\{1,d/[n\log(en)]\}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[prop:causal-optimal-value-corollary]\label{prop:causal-optimal-value-corollary}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\), consistency and armwise conditional exchangeability imply
\[
 V^\star(P)=\Psi\{\operatorname{Obs}(P)\}
 =\sum_{x=1}^d p_x\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\mu_{ax}.
\]
Thus the observed-law target is the unrestricted optimal-treatment value for every causal completion in \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\), while the minimax theorems themselves require only the observed law to lie in \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) and put \(\mathbb P=\operatorname{Obs}(P)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:model-class}\), \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  For an occupied cell \(x\), its overlap probabilities are positive, and \(\mathrm{ass:conditional-exchangeability}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) give, separately for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 E_P\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}
 =E_P\{Y(a)\mid A=a,X=x\}
 =E_P(Y\mid A=a,X=x)=\mu_{ax}.                                     \tag{6}
\]
The first equality in (6) is armwise conditional independence; the second is consistency on \(\{A=a\}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:oracle-value}\), finite-support conditioning and (6) imply
\[
 V^\star(P)=\sum_{x:p_x>0}p_x\max_a\mu_{ax}.
\]
Null-cell summands vanish, so \(\mathrm{def:observed-optimal-value}\) identifies the last display with \(\Psi(\mathbb P)\).  This proves the causal equality rather than placing it in either definition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This corollary keeps the causal estimand distinct from the observed-law functional and proves their equality under the stated causal assumptions. \textit{Gap.} ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete explicitly separates its observed regression functional from its causal interpretation; this proposition supplies the corresponding completion and equality for the optimal-regression maximum. \textit{Consumer.} Causal applications may invoke the minimax frontier only after checking consistency and armwise exchangeability.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The estimator is explicit as a measurable Rao--Blackwellized finite-sample rule, but the conditional integration over auxiliary markings and the tensor coefficient calculation are not claimed to run in near-linear time or to be numerically stable at machine precision. The theorem gives constants depending only on fixed overlap, not a sharp leading minimax constant.

\section{Honest scope}

The formal target is the scalar value of the unrestricted oracle policy, not regret of a learned policy and not uniform confidence intervals. Fixed overlap is maintained; vanishing overlap, growing treatment count, and sharp leading constants remain outside the theorem. The accepted in-repository signed-aggregate theorem and the present cellwise-maximum theorem concern different functionals, so the comparison claims neither subsumption nor rate transfer. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:equal\mbox{-}propensity\mbox{-}l1\mbox{-}reduction}\) is only a lower-bound sanity slice. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:parent\mbox{-}reduction}\) records lineage to the archived downgraded predecessor: the model and estimand are identical, \(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}\) only for \(2\leq d<D_0\), the matched rate implies the predecessor's bracket, and, along each fixed bounded alphabet sequence, minimax risk has two-sided \(n^{-1}\) bounds with constants allowed to depend on \(\epsilon\) and the finite bound \(D\). The exact constant is exposed separately in \(\mathrm{oeq:sharp\mbox{-}overlap\mbox{-}constant}\).
