Fix \(M\ge2\), \(p\in(0,1)\), \(\rho\in(0,1]\), \(B\ge1\), and design sequences satisfying the three fraction and growth assumptions. Put
\[
d_{\mathrm{same}}=\frac1{Mp(1-p)},\qquad
d_{\mathrm{ind}}=\frac1{Mp(1-p)}-\frac{2\rho}{M},
\]
and suppose \(0<c_\sigma<d_{\mathrm{ind}}\). There are high-prior-probability sets of deterministic Rademacher schedule rows whose Cartesian diagonal selections all belong to \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\); after conditioning \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) and \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\) on these sets, their induced one-realization mixture laws have total-variation distance tending to zero, while \(G_n\sigma_n^2\) converges uniformly on the respective supports to \(d_{\mathrm{same}}\) and \(d_{\mathrm{ind}}\). Consequently there exists \(\varepsilon>0\) such that every measurable \(\widehat S_n(\mathcal O_n)\) obeys
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{Y\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}}
\Pr_Y\left(\left|\frac{\widehat S_n}{\sigma_n^2}-1\right|>\varepsilon\right)
\ge\frac12.
\]
Thus no estimator from one realization is uniformly ratio-consistent for the exact variance over the full dense class. The deterministic support arrays, rather than a generally unavailable pair of deterministic arrays with close laws, are the valid diagonal certificate.
%%% FIELD qv-proof
Let \(j\in\{\mathrm{same},\mathrm{ind}\}\), and write \(d_j\) for the corresponding constant in the theorem. Proposition~\ref{prop:rademacher-mixture-separation} gives
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2\xrightarrow{p}d_j
\tag{1}
\]
under the schedule prior \(\mathbb H_n^j\). For an integer \(k\ge1\), define the schedule event
\[
E_{n,k}^j=
\left\{Y:\left|G_n\sigma_n^2(Y)-d_j\right|\le k^{-1}\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Equation~(1) implies \(\mathbb H_n^j(E_{n,k}^j)\to1\) for every fixed \(k\) and each \(j\).

We first turn these rowwise prior statements into deterministic array supports. Choose strictly increasing integers \(m_k\) so that, simultaneously for \(j=\mathrm{same}\) and \(j=\mathrm{ind}\),
\[
n\ge m_k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbb H_n^j(E_{n,k}^j)\ge1-k^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(n\ge m_1\), put \(k_n=\max\{k:m_k\le n\}\), and put \(k_n=1\) for \(n<m_1\). Then \(k_n\to\infty\). Define
\[
\Gamma_n^j=
\begin{cases}
E_{n,k_n}^j,&n\ge m_1,\\
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb H_n^j),&n<m_1.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
By (3)--(4),
\[
\mathbb H_n^j(\Gamma_n^j)\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\sup_{Y\in\Gamma_n^j}
\left|G_n\sigma_n^2(Y)-d_j\right|le k_n^{-1}longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

Every row in \(\Gamma_n^j\) is additive and has \(|Y_{i,n}(z,A)|=1\le B\). The deterministic design sequences obey Assumptions~\ref{ass:group-count-growth}, \ref{ass:stable-treatment-fraction}, and \ref{ass:sampling-fraction}. The theorem assumes \(0<c_\sigma<d_{\mathrm{ind}}\), and \(d_{\mathrm{same}}>d_{\mathrm{ind}}\); hence (5) gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}G_n\sigma_n^2(Y_n)=d_j>c_\sigma
\tag{6}
\]
for every Cartesian selection \((Y_n)_{n\ge1}\in\prod_n\Gamma_n^j\). Definition~\ref{def:dense-schedule-class} and (6) therefore show that every such selection belongs to \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\). This verifies support, boundedness, fraction, and scaled-variance conditions without imposing any filtration-side premise.

Let \(P_n^{j,0}\) be the law of \(\mathcal O_n\) after drawing the schedule from \(\mathbb H_n^j\) and then applying the two-stage design. Let \(\widetilde{\mathbb H}_n^j=\mathbb H_n^j(\mathord\cdot\mid\Gamma_n^j)\), and let \(P_n^j\) be the corresponding conditioned observation law. For any event \(A\) in the schedule space, direct decomposition over \(\Gamma_n^j\) and its complement gives
\[
\left|\widetilde{\mathbb H}_n^j(A)-\mathbb H_n^j(A)\right|
\le \mathbb H_n^j((\Gamma_n^j)^c).
\tag{7}
\]
Applying the observation kernel cannot increase this bound. Indeed, for any observed-data event \(C\), let \(K_C(Y)\in[0,1]\) be its conditional probability given the schedule. The layer-cake identity
\[
K_C(Y)=\int_0^1\mathbf 1\{K_C(Y)>t\}\,\mathrm dt
\]
and (7), applied at each \(t\), give
\[
\left|\int K_C\,\mathrm d\widetilde{\mathbb H}_n^j
-\int K_C\,\mathrm d\mathbb H_n^j\right|
\le\mathbb H_n^j((\Gamma_n^j)^c).
\]
Taking the supremum over \(C\), with total variation defined as the supremum over measurable events, proves
\[
\delta_{n,j}:=\lVert P_n^j-P_n^{j,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le \mathbb H_n^j((\Gamma_n^j)^c)=o(1).
\tag{8}
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:rademacher-mixture-separation} states that \(P_n^{\mathrm{same},0}=P_n^{\mathrm{ind},0}\) exactly. Calling this common law \(P_n^*\), the triangle inequality and (8) give
\[
\lVert P_n^{\mathrm{same}}-P_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\delta_{n,\mathrm{same}}+\delta_{n,\mathrm{ind}}=o(1).
\tag{9}
\]

We next verify that the indistinguishability in (9) is not generated by a degenerate observable law. Write \(D_n=(\mathbf A_n,\mathbf Z_n)\), let \(i_n=\min A_{n1}\), and define the first observed sign
\[
W_n=Y_{i_n,n}(Z_{n1},A_{n1})\in\{-1,+1\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Under either unconditioned prior, the schedule signs are independent of \(D_n\). Under \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\), \(W_n=U_{i_n}\); under \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\), it is \(U_{i_n}\) if \(Z_{n1}=1\) and \(V_{i_n}\) if \(Z_{n1}=0\). The signs involved are Rademacher in every case. Consequently, if \(\mu_n\) denotes the common design law of \(D_n\), then
\[
P_n^*(W_n=+1\mid D_n)=P_n^*(W_n=-1\mid D_n)=\frac12,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{P_n^*}(W_n\mid D_n)=1
\tag{11}
\]
at every design point in the support of \(\mu_n\).

Because \(\Gamma_n^j\) is a schedule event and schedules are drawn independently of the design, conditioning on \(\Gamma_n^j\) leaves the marginal law of \(D_n\) equal to \(\mu_n\). On the finite design space define
\[
q_{n,j}(d)=P_n^j(W_n=+1\mid D_n=d).
\tag{12}
\]
Projection from \(\mathcal O_n\) to \((D_n,W_n)\) cannot increase total variation, for the same event-supremum reason used in (8). Since the two compared laws have the same \(D_n\)-marginal, direct summation over \(d\) and \(W_n\in\{-1,+1\}\) gives
\[
\int\left|q_{n,j}(d)-\frac12\right|\,\mu_n(\mathrm d d)
=\left\lVert
\mathcal L_{P_n^j}(D_n,W_n)-\mu_n\otimes\operatorname{Rad}(1/2)
\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\delta_{n,j}.
\tag{13}
\]
Set
\[
\mathcal R_n=
\bigcap_{j\in\{\mathrm{same},\mathrm{ind}\}}
\left\{d:\left|q_{n,j}(d)-\frac12\right|\le\frac14\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The pointwise inequality
\(\mathbf 1\{|q-1/2|>1/4\}\le4|q-1/2|\), followed by (13), yields
\[
\mu_n(\mathcal R_n^c)
\le4(\delta_{n,\mathrm{same}}+\delta_{n,\mathrm{ind}})=o(1).
\tag{15}
\]
For every \(d\in\mathcal R_n\) and either \(j\), both conditional support probabilities are bounded below:
\[
P_n^j(W_n=+1\mid D_n=d)\ge\frac14,
\qquad
P_n^j(W_n=-1\mid D_n=d)\ge\frac14.
\tag{16}
\]
Moreover, because \(W_n^2=1\), equations (12) and (16) imply
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{P_n^j}(W_n\mid D_n=d)
=4q_{n,j}(d)(1-q_{n,j}(d))
\ge\frac34.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the design region has probability \(1-o(1)\), both observable support points retain probabilities uniformly bounded away from zero there, and their conditional variance is uniformly nondegenerate. Meanwhile (5) preserves the failure-relevant separation
\[
\Delta:=d_{\mathrm{same}}-d_{\mathrm{ind}}=\frac{2\rho}{M}>0.
\tag{18}
\]

It remains to convert (5), (9), and (18) into the all-statistic lower bound. Put \(a=d_{\mathrm{same}}\), \(b=d_{\mathrm{ind}}\), and choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<\frac{a-b}{4a}.
\tag{19}
\]
Since the uniform error in (5) vanishes, for all sufficiently large \(n\) the event
\[
\left|\frac{\widehat S_n}{\sigma_n^2}-1\right|\le\varepsilon
\tag{20}
\]
implies
\[
G_n\widehat S_n>\frac{a+b}{2}
\quad\text{on }\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{same}},
\qquad
G_n\widehat S_n<\frac{a+b}{2}
\quad\text{on }\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{ind}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, writing the error in (5) as \(o(1)\), the respective margins are bounded below by
\[
(1-\varepsilon)(a-o(1))-\frac{a+b}{2}
\ge\frac{a-b}{2}-\varepsilon a-o(1)>0
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
\frac{a+b}{2}-(1+\varepsilon)(b+o(1))
\ge\frac{a-b}{2}-\varepsilon a-o(1)>0.
\tag{23}
\]

Let \(T_n=\{G_n\widehat S_n>(a+b)/2\}\), interpreted as the decision ``same.'' By (20)--(21), a wrong decision under either conditioned prior entails relative estimation error greater than \(\varepsilon\). Furthermore, directly from the event-supremum definition of total variation,
\[
P_n^{\mathrm{same}}(T_n^c)+P_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(T_n)
=1-P_n^{\mathrm{same}}(T_n)+P_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(T_n)
\ge1-\lVert P_n^{\mathrm{same}}-P_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (9), (21), and (24) shows
\[
\max_{j\in\{\mathrm{same},\mathrm{ind}\}}
\int
\mathbb P_{\mathbf A_n,\mathbf Z_n}\!\left(
\left.\left|\frac{\widehat S_n}{\sigma_n^2}-1\right|>\varepsilon
\,\right|Y\right)
\,\mathrm d\widetilde{\mathbb H}_n^j(Y)
\ge\frac12-o(1).
\tag{25}
\]

Finally, every row in \(\Gamma_n^j\) can be extended to an infinite array in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\): retain that row at index \(n\), select an arbitrary member of each nonempty \(\Gamma_m^j\) at every sufficiently large \(m\ne n\), and use arbitrary bounded rows at the finitely many earlier indices. Nonemptiness eventually follows from (5), and membership follows from (6). Therefore each mixture risk in (25) is bounded above by
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}}
\Pr_Y\left(\left|\frac{\widehat S_n}{\sigma_n^2}-1\right|>\varepsilon\right).
\tag{26}
\]
Taking lower limits in (25)--(26) proves the asserted bound \(1/2\). The schedules used above are additive and have no composition interference, so they form a subclass of the arbitrary bounded composition-interference class. Failure on this subclass transfers to the full class. The conclusion is a positive-density nonextension result: it does not contradict a birthday-sparse theorem or an independent-cluster working-model guarantee on its own stated domain.
