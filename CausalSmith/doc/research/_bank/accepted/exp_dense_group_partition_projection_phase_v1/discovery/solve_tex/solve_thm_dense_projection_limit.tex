Put \(d_n=h_{1,n}-h_{0,n}\) and
\[
e_{k,n}=\|\Pi_{n,k}d_n\|_n^2,
\qquad k=1,\ldots,M.
\]
The complete potential-outcome schedule is fixed throughout; every probability, covariance, and variance without the subscript \(n\) is taken only over the two-stage assignment in Definition~\ref{def:random-partition-design}.
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} and the definition of \(h_{z,n}\) imply
\[
|h_{z,n}(A)|
\leq \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i\in A}|Y_{i,n}(z,A)|
\leq B.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
V_{z,n}
=\mathbb E_n\!\left[(h_{z,n}-\mathbb E_nh_{z,n})^2\right]
\leq \mathbb E_n[h_{z,n}^2]
\leq B^2.                                                   \tag{2}
\]
By the orthogonal decomposition in Definition~\ref{def:johnson-decomposition},
\[
\sum_{k=1}^M e_{k,n}
=\operatorname{Var}_n\{d_n(A)\}
\leq \mathbb E_n[d_n(A)^2]
\leq 4B^2.                                                  \tag{3}
\]
Since \(N_n=MG_n\leq n\), fixed \(M\), and \(G_n\to\infty\) by Assumption~\ref{ass:group-count-growth}, we have \(n\to\infty\).  Thus it suffices to consider \(n\geq4M\).

Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kneser-identity} gives, for \(1\leq k\leq M\),
\[
|\lambda_{n,k}|
=\frac{(M)_k}{(n-M)_k}
=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{M-j}{n-M-j}.
\]
When \(n\geq4M\), each numerator is at most \(M\), and each denominator is at least \(n-2M+1\geq n/2\).  Hence
\[
|\lambda_{n,k}|\leq\left(\frac{2M}{n}\right)^k.            \tag{4}
\]
For the degrees \(k\geq2\), equations (3)--(4) and \(G_n=N_n/M\leq n/M\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|G_n\sum_{k=2}^M\lambda_{n,k}e_{k,n}\right|
&\leq G_n\left(\frac{2M}{n}\right)^2
       \sum_{k=2}^M e_{k,n}\\
&\leq \frac{n}{M}\frac{4M^2}{n^2}\,4B^2
=\frac{16MB^2}{n}.                                         \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves, uniformly over all schedules obeying Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes}, that the combined contribution of all Johnson degrees at least two is \(O(n^{-1})\) after multiplication by \(G_n\).

For degree one, Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kneser-identity} gives \(\lambda_{n,1}=-M/(n-M)\).  Combining this identity with (5) and \(e_{1,n}=E_{\tau,1,n}\) gives the sharper deterministic expansion
\[
G_nC_{\tau\tau,n}
=-\frac{MG_n}{n-M}e_{1,n}+O(n^{-1})
=-\frac{N_n}{n-M}E_{\tau,1,n}+O(n^{-1}).                   \tag{6}
\]

The same argument controls the arm-specific disjoint covariances.  Indeed, orthogonality and (2) give
\[
C_{zz,n}
=\sum_{k=1}^M\lambda_{n,k}
  \|\Pi_{n,k}h_{z,n}\|_n^2,
\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^M\|\Pi_{n,k}h_{z,n}\|_n^2=V_{z,n}\leq B^2.
\]
Because \(2M/n\leq1/2\), equation (4) implies \(|\lambda_{n,k}|\leq2M/n\) for every \(k\geq1\).  Therefore
\[
|C_{zz,n}|\leq\frac{2MB^2}{n}.                              \tag{7}
\]
Assumption~\ref{ass:stable-treatment-fraction} supplies constants \(\delta>0\) and \(n_0\) such that \(p_n\geq\delta\) and \(1-p_n\geq\delta\) for \(n\geq n_0\).  Thus (7) implies
\[
\left|\frac{C_{11,n}}{p_n}+
       \frac{C_{00,n}}{1-p_n}\right|
\leq\frac{4MB^2}{\delta n}=O(n^{-1}).                      \tag{8}
\]

Multiplying the exact identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-pame-variance} by \(G_n\), and using \(G_{1n}=p_nG_n\) and \(G_{0n}=(1-p_n)G_n\), gives
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2
=R_n+G_nC_{\tau\tau,n}
 -\frac{C_{11,n}}{p_n}-\frac{C_{00,n}}{1-p_n}.
\]
Substitution of (6) and (8) proves the schedule-uniform finite-density expansion
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2
=R_n-\frac{N_n}{n-M}E_{\tau,1,n}+O(n^{-1}).                \tag{9}
\]
No scaled-variance lower bound has been used.

Assumption~\ref{ass:sampling-fraction} and fixed \(M\) imply
\[
\frac{N_n}{n-M}
=\frac{N_n/n}{1-M/n}\longrightarrow\rho.                  \tag{10}
\]
Moreover, (3) gives \(0\leq E_{\tau,1,n}\leq4B^2\).  It follows from (6) and (10) that
\[
G_nC_{\tau\tau,n}+\rho E_{\tau,1,n}
=O(n^{-1})+
 \left(\rho-\frac{N_n}{n-M}\right)E_{\tau,1,n}
\longrightarrow0.                                         \tag{11}
\]
Equation (8) proves the second asserted vanishing relation, while (9)--(10) prove
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2=R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}+o(1).
\]
Together with (5), these equations prove every assertion of the theorem using only group-count growth, stable treatment and sampling fractions, fixed \(M\), and bounded potential outcomes.

For the unconditional CR2 comparison used downstream, fix \(z\in\{0,1\}\), put \(m=G_{zn}\), and let \(f\) be either \(h_{z,n}\) or \(h_{z,n}^2\).  Under Definition~\ref{def:random-partition-design}, the groups selected into arm \(z\) are an exchangeable ordered disjoint \(m\)-tuple.  If \(f^\circ=f-\mathbb E_nf\), direct expansion of the variance of their average gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left\{\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m f(A_j)\right\}
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_n(f)}m
\frac{m-1}{m}\langle f^\circ,\mathsf K_nf^\circ\rangle_n. \tag{12}
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kneser-identity}, orthogonality, and (4),
\[
\left|\langle f^\circ,\mathsf K_nf^\circ\rangle_n\right|
\leq\frac{2M}{n}\operatorname{Var}_n(f).                  \tag{13}
\]
Stable treatment fractions give \(m\asymp G_n\), so \(m\to\infty\) and \(m\geq2\) eventually; also \(G_n\leq n/M\).  Since (1) bounds both \(f=h_{z,n}\) and \(f=h_{z,n}^2\), equations (12)--(13) give a constant \(K<\infty\), independent of the schedule, such that
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left\{m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m f(A_j)\right\}
\leq\frac{K}{G_n}.                                         \tag{14}
\]
For every \(L>0\), the elementary inequality
\[
\mathbb E[X^2]\geq \frac{L^2}{G_n}
 \Pr\!\left\{|X|>\frac{L}{\sqrt{G_n}}\right\}
\]
applied to the centered empirical average and (14) proves an \(O_{\mathbb P}(G_n^{-1/2})\) error for both its first and second moments.  Since \(m/(m-1)=1+O(G_n^{-1})\), the sample-variance identity in Definition~\ref{def:cr2-statistic} therefore gives
\[
s_{z,n}^2-V_{z,n}=O_{\mathbb P}(G_n^{-1/2}).                \tag{15}
\]
Consequently,
\[
G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-R_n
=O_{\mathbb P}(G_n^{-1/2}),                                \tag{16}
\]
and combining (9) with (16) yields the entirely unconditional comparison
\[
G_n\bigl(\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-\sigma_n^2\bigr)
=\frac{N_n}{n-M}E_{\tau,1,n}
 +O_{\mathbb P}(G_n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}).                        \tag{17}
\]
Neither predictable quadratic-variation stabilization nor scaled-variance nondegeneracy is used in (12)--(17).  When \(N_n=o(n)\) and \(E_{\tau,1,n}\to e>0\), the deterministic term in (17) has order \(N_n/n\), whereas the proved stochastic remainder is bounded by order \(G_n^{-1/2}\asymp N_n^{-1/2}\).  These rates agree at \(N_n\asymp n^{2/3}\).  Because (15) is only an upper bound, this identifies a benchmark crossover and does not assert a matching stochastic lower bound.
