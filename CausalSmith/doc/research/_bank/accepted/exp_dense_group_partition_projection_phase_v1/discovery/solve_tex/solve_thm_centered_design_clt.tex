For every array in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\),
\[
 G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-R_n\xrightarrow{p}0
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 G_n\bigl(\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-\sigma_n^2\bigr)
   -\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\xrightarrow{p}0.
\]
Consequently, CR2 is asymptotically conservative in the sense that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
   \widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}/\sigma_n^2<1-\varepsilon
 \right\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and it is ratio-consistent if and only if
\[
 \frac{\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}{R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
These conclusions are unconditional over \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) and use no predictable quadratic-variation premise.

We first prove the two stochastic expansions.  Put \(\pi_{1n}=p_n\) and \(\pi_{0n}=1-p_n\).  Fix \(z\in\{0,1\}\), and set \(m_n=G_{zn}=\pi_{zn}G_n\).  By Definition~\ref{def:dense-schedule-class}, group-count growth and stable treatment fractions hold, so \(m_n\to\infty\) and \(m_n\asymp G_n\).  Since \(N_n=MG_n\le n\), group-count growth also implies that \(n\ge2M\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For a deterministic function \(f:\Omega_{n,M}\to\mathbb R\), define
\[
 \mu_n(f)=\mathbb E_nf,
 \qquad
 W_n(f)=\mathbb E_n\!\left[(f-\mu_n(f))^2\right],
 \qquad
 C_n(f)=\operatorname{Cov}(f(A),f(C)),
 \tag{1}
\]
where \((A,C)\) is uniform over ordered disjoint pairs.  Under Definition~\ref{def:random-partition-design}, conditional on any set of \(m_n\) arm-\(z\) positions, the groups in those positions are uniform over ordered pairwise-disjoint \(m_n\)-tuples.  Independence and uniformity of the complete label assignment therefore give the same marginal statement without conditioning: each selected group is uniform on \(\Omega_{n,M}\), and every two distinct selected groups form a uniform ordered disjoint pair.  Expanding the variance of their mean yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}f(A_j)\right)
 =\frac{W_n(f)}{m_n}+\frac{m_n-1}{m_n}C_n(f).
 \tag{2}
\]

By Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kneser-identity} and the orthogonal decomposition in Definition~\ref{def:johnson-decomposition},
\[
 C_n(f)=\sum_{k=1}^M\lambda_{n,k}\|\Pi_{n,k}f\|_n^2,
 \qquad
 W_n(f)=\sum_{k=1}^M\|\Pi_{n,k}f\|_n^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
For \(1\le k\le M\), \(0\le j<k\), and \(n\ge2M\), every denominator below is positive and
\[
 0\le\frac{M-j}{n-M-j}\le\frac{M}{n-M}\le1.
\]
The exact Kneser eigenvalue formula consequently gives
\[
 |\lambda_{n,k}|
 =\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{M-j}{n-M-j}
 \le\frac{M}{n-M}.
\]
Using (3) and then \(MG_n\le n\), we obtain
\[
 |C_n(f)|\le\frac{M}{n-M}W_n(f)
 \le\frac{W_n(f)}{G_n-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}f(A_j)\right)
 \le W_n(f)\left(\frac1{m_n}+\frac1{G_n-1}\right).
 \tag{5}
\]

Bounded potential outcomes, inherited from membership in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\), imply \(|h_{z,n}(A)|\le B\).  Thus \(W_n(h_{z,n})\le B^2\) and \(W_n(h_{z,n}^2)\le B^4\).  Since \(m_n\asymp G_n\to\infty\), (5) and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 \frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}h_{z,n}(A_j)
   =\mathbb E_nh_{z,n}+o_p(1),
 \qquad
 \frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}h_{z,n}(A_j)^2
   =\mathbb E_nh_{z,n}^2+o_p(1).
 \tag{6}
\]
The means in (6) are uniformly bounded by \(B\), so squaring the first convergence preserves its \(o_p(1)\) remainder.  The exact sample-variance identity and \(m_n\to\infty\) therefore yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 s_{z,n}^2
 &=\frac{m_n}{m_n-1}\left\{
   \frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}h_{z,n}(A_j)^2
   -\left(\frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}h_{z,n}(A_j)\right)^2
   \right\}\\
 &=\mathbb E_nh_{z,n}^2-(\mathbb E_nh_{z,n})^2+o_p(1)
 =V_{z,n}+o_p(1).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
Stable treatment fractions make both \(p_n\) and \(1-p_n\) eventually bounded away from zero.  Definition~\ref{def:cr2-statistic}, (7), and Definition~\ref{def:dense-correction} now give
\[
 G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}
 =\frac{s_{1,n}^2}{p_n}+\frac{s_{0,n}^2}{1-p_n}
 =R_n+o_p(1).
 \tag{8}
\]

Set \(\Delta_n=R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\).  The unconditional expansion in Theorem~\ref{thm:dense-projection-limit} gives
\[
 G_n\sigma_n^2=\Delta_n+o(1).
 \tag{9}
\]
Subtracting (9) from (8) proves
\[
 G_n\bigl(\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-\sigma_n^2\bigr)
   -\rho E_{\tau,1,n}=o_p(1),
 \tag{10}
\]
which is the second asserted expansion.

It remains to derive, rather than assume, the two ratio conclusions.  Scaled-variance nondegeneracy in Definition~\ref{def:dense-schedule-class} and (9) imply that \(\Delta_n\ge c_\sigma/2>0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Also \(V_{z,n}\le B^2\), so stable treatment fractions give \(R_n\le C\) eventually for some finite constant \(C\).  Since \(E_{\tau,1,n}\ge0\), eventual positivity of \(\Delta_n=R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\) also gives \(0\le\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\le R_n\le C\).  Write
\[
 u_n=G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-R_n=o_p(1),
 \qquad
 v_n=G_n\sigma_n^2-\Delta_n=o(1).
\]
The denominator \(\Delta_n+v_n=G_n\sigma_n^2\) is eventually bounded below by a positive constant.  Direct algebra, followed by the preceding deterministic bounds, gives
\[
 \frac{\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{\sigma_n^2}-1
 =\frac{\rho E_{\tau,1,n}+u_n-v_n}{\Delta_n+v_n}
 =\frac{\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}{\Delta_n}+o_p(1).
 \tag{11}
\]
The deterministic leading term in (11) is nonnegative.  Hence, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
   \widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}/\sigma_n^2<1-\varepsilon
 \right\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is the stated asymptotic conservativeness.  If \(\rho E_{\tau,1,n}/\Delta_n\to0\), (11) gives ratio consistency.  Conversely, if this deterministic nonnegative sequence does not converge to zero, some subsequence is bounded below by a positive constant; (11) then rules out convergence of the variance ratio to one on that subsequence.  This proves the ``if and only if'' assertion.  No predictable quadratic variation, martingale limit, Gaussian approximation, studentization, or coverage claim is used anywhere in the proof.
%%% FIELD proof-sparse
Assume \(\rho=0\).  By Assumption~\ref{ass:sampling-fraction}, this is equivalent to \(N_n/n\to0\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:dense-projection-limit} gives the unconditional variance expansion
\[
 G_n\sigma_n^2=R_n+o(1),
 \tag{1}
\]
because the degree-one correction is multiplied by \(\rho\).  Independently, Theorem~\ref{thm:cr2-phase-frontier} gives
\[
 G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}=R_n+o_p(1).
 \tag{2}
\]
Scaled-variance nondegeneracy, which is one of the conditions in Definition~\ref{def:dense-schedule-class}, states
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}G_n\sigma_n^2\ge c_\sigma>0.
\]
Thus the deterministic denominator \(G_n\sigma_n^2\) is bounded away from zero for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Subtracting (1) from (2) and dividing by that denominator yields
\[
 \frac{\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{\sigma_n^2}
 =\frac{G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{G_n\sigma_n^2}
 \xrightarrow{p}1.
 \tag{3}
\]
This proof used only the vanishing sampling fraction \(N_n/n\to0\), not the stronger birthday-scale condition \(N_n^2/n\to0\).  To verify that the distinction is nonempty, take
\[
 G_n=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{3/4}}M\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 N_n=MG_n,
\]
and choose integers \(G_{1n}\) with \(G_{1n}/G_n\to p\in(0,1)\).  Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the design constraints \(G_n\to\infty\), \(N_n=MG_n\le n\), and \(1\le G_{1n}\le G_n-1\) hold, while
\[
 \frac{N_n}{n}=n^{-1/4}(1+o(1))\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{N_n^2}{n}=n^{1/2}(1+o(1))\to\infty.
\]
Therefore every schedule array with these counts that satisfies the remaining conditions of \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) is covered by (3), even though whole-tuple birthday sparsity fails.  No Gaussian or Wald conclusion is asserted.
