It suffices to prove the upper-contact assertion at one threshold, because coverage simultaneous in \(y\) implies coverage at that threshold.  Use outcomes \(\{0,1\}\) and \(y_0=0\).  Under the boundary observational law, let \(\Pr(Y=0\mid A=a)=1\); under the \(n\)-th alternative let \(\Pr(Y=1\mid A=a)=\delta_n\).  Keep \(\Pr(A=a)=e\) and the conditional law in the other treatment arm identical.  These laws are realizable within the bow-SCM setup, for example by the latent construction in \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\).

Couple one observation under the two laws by using the same treatment and changing the arm-\(a\) outcome only on an independent event of conditional probability \(\delta_n\).  The observations differ with probability \(e\delta_n\).  Coupling \(n\) independent observations and applying the union bound shows that the total-variation distance between the two sample laws is at most
\[
 ne\delta_n=o(1). \tag{17}
\]
The lawful lower endpoint at \(y_0\) equals \(L_e(1)=1\) under the boundary law, whereas under the alternative it equals
\[
 L_e(1-\delta_n)=e(1-\delta_n). \tag{18}
\]

Let \(C_n^L(y_0)\) be the lower-endpoint projection of any candidate rectangular band.  Uniform asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) gives, under the boundary law,
\[
 \Pr\{1\in C_n^L(y_0)\}\ge1-\alpha-o(1).
\]
It gives probability at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) of containing \(e(1-\delta_n)\) under the alternative.  By (17), the latter event has the same probability up to \(o(1)\) under the boundary law.  A union bound therefore gives probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)\) that \(C_n^L(y_0)\) contains both values in (18).  On that event its diameter is at least
\[
 1-e(1-\delta_n)=1-e+e\delta_n=1-e-o(1).
\]
Thus no uniformly honest band on this local class can shrink to the pointwise endpoint, regardless of how a directional derivative is estimated.

For the lower-contact assertion, interchange zero and one.  Compare \(F_P(y_0)=0\) with \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=\delta_n\).  Their sample total-variation distance has the same bound, while the lawful upper endpoints are \(U_e(0)=0\) and \(U_e(\delta_n)=1-e+e\delta_n\), again separated by \(1-e+o(1)\).  The identical coverage argument proves the upper-endpoint claim.  The finite-sample construction remains honest by \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\), so retaining it outside separated regular strata is the valid fallback.
