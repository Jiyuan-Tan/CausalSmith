Write the rectangular band at \(y_0\) as \(I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\), where the factors are intervals for the lower and upper endpoints. A statistical band is understood to be a measurable set-valued report: in particular, for fixed \(n,y_0\), and endpoint value \(z\), the event that \(z\) belongs to the relevant projection is measurable. This is the regularity needed for every coverage probability and total-variation comparison below.

Uniform asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) means that there is a deterministic sequence \(\rho_n\downarrow0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), every law in the local class has simultaneous coverage probability at least \(1-\alpha-\rho_n\). Indeed, this is an equivalent sequential form of the lower-limit bound on the infimum of the coverage probabilities over the class.

First consider a boundary observed-data law \(\nu_0\) with \(F_P(y_0)=1\) and alternative laws \(\nu_n\). By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), the corresponding \(n\)-sample laws are \(\nu_0^{\otimes n}\) and \(\nu_n^{\otimes n}\). By hypothesis, on some tail,
\[
 F_{P,n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n,
 \qquad
 d_n:=\lVert\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=o(1).
 \tag{1}
\]
Because \(n\delta_n\to0\) and \(\delta_n>0\), there is a second tail on which \(0<\delta_n<1\): choose the tail so that \(n\delta_n<1\) and \(n\ge1\), whence \(0<\delta_n\le n\delta_n<1\). Intersect this tail with the one in (1) and the coverage tail. On this common tail, \(1-\delta_n\in(0,1)\), and \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) gives the two lower endpoints
\[
 L_e(1)=1,
 \qquad
 L_e(1-\delta_n)=e(1-\delta_n).
 \tag{2}
\]
Define the measurable data events
\[
 C_{0n}=\{1\in I_n^L(y_0)\},
 \qquad
 C_{1n}=\{e(1-\delta_n)\in I_n^L(y_0)\}.
\]
Simultaneous coverage implies coordinatewise coverage. Hence, on the common tail,
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n,
 \qquad
 \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n.
 \tag{3}
\]
By the definition of total variation applied to the measurable event \(C_{1n}\),
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})
 \ge \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})-d_n
 \ge1-\alpha-\rho_n-d_n.
 \tag{4}
\]
The elementary union bound under \(\nu_0^{\otimes n}\) now yields
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n}\cap C_{1n})
 \ge1-2\alpha-2\rho_n-d_n
 =1-2\alpha-o(1).
 \tag{5}
\]
On \(C_{0n}\cap C_{1n}\), the interval \(I_n^L(y_0)\) contains both values in (2), so
\[
 \operatorname{diam} I_n^L(y_0)
 \ge 1-e(1-\delta_n)
 =1-e+e\delta_n
 \ge1-e.
 \tag{6}
\]
This proves the first probability assertion.

For the other contact, let the boundary law satisfy \(F_P(y_0)=0\), and suppose that on a tail the alternatives satisfy \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=\delta_n\). On the same type of common tail, \(0<\delta_n<1\), and \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) gives
\[
 U_e(0)=0,
 \qquad
 U_e(\delta_n)=e\delta_n+1-e.
 \tag{7}
\]
Apply (3)--(5) to the measurable events that \(I_n^U(y_0)\) contains, respectively, the two values in (7). Under the boundary law, with probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{diam} I_n^U(y_0)
 \ge e\delta_n+1-e
 \ge1-e.
 \tag{8}
\]

We next construct the asserted bow-SCM alternatives and verify every support and locality condition. Fix real numbers \(z_-\le y_0<z_+\). Let \((\delta_n)\) be any positive sequence with \(n\delta_n\to0\). As above, choose \(N\) such that \(0<\delta_n<1\) for every \(n\ge N\). For \(n\ge N\), define the upper-contact arm laws
\[
 P_0^-:=\delta_{z_-},
 \qquad
 P_n^-:=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_-}+\delta_n\delta_{z_+},
 \tag{9}
\]
and the lower-contact arm laws
\[
 P_0^+:=\delta_{z_+},
 \qquad
 P_n^+:=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_+}+\delta_n\delta_{z_-}.
 \tag{10}
\]
All coefficients in (9)--(10) are nonnegative and sum to one, so these are probability laws. Moreover,
\[
 F_{P_0^-}(y_0)=1,
 \quad F_{P_n^-}(y_0)=1-\delta_n,
 \qquad
 F_{P_0^+}(y_0)=0,
 \quad F_{P_n^+}(y_0)=\delta_n.
 \tag{11}
\]
At the finitely many indices \(n<N\), choose any fixed valid bow-SCM observed-data law; no CDF identity is required there by the eventual statement.

For each sign and each tail index, choose the residual law \(R_j^\pm=P_j^\pm\), where \(j\) is either \(0\) or \(n\). Then \(R_j^\pm\ll P_j^\pm\) and
\[
 eP_j^\pm+(1-e)R_j^\pm=P_j^\pm.
 \tag{12}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), (12) is realized by a bow-ADMG SCM satisfying consistency, strict positivity, and mutual absolute continuity, with latent variable \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\). To keep the full observed laws identical away from arm \(a\), take \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), take \(Y(a)\) independent of \(A\) with law \(P_j^\pm\), choose the same fixed value of \(Y(1-a)\) in every model, and set \(Y=Y(A)\). Consistency is then immediate, \(\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)=P_j^\pm\), and \(\mathcal L(Y(a))=P_j^\pm\sim P_j^\pm\). This is a special case of the latent realization certified by the completeness theorem.

For \(n\ge N\), couple one observation from the boundary and alternative models by using the same treatment \(A\), the same outside-arm outcome, and, on \(\{A=a\}\), the same outcome except on the probability-\(\delta_n\) mass shifted between \(z_-\) and \(z_+\). The coupled observations differ with probability \(e\delta_n\). Couple the \(n\) observations independently in this way. The probability that at least one coordinate differs is at most \(ne\delta_n\) by the union bound. For any measurable sample event \(H\), the indicators of \(H\) agree whenever the coupled sample vectors agree, and consequently
\[
 \bigl|\Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(H)-\Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(H)\bigr|
 \le \Pr\{\text{the coupled sample vectors differ}\}
 \le ne\delta_n.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(H\) proves
\[
 \lVert\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le ne\delta_n=o(1).
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus both eventual alternative sequences exist inside the maintained bow-SCM class. The arbitrary finite prefix does not affect (11) eventually or the limit in (13).

Finally, \(0<e<1\) by \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), so \(1-e>0\). If a uniformly honest rectangular band had projections shrinking uniformly at either contact, then under the corresponding boundary law the probability of diameter at least \(1-e\) would tend to zero. Equations (6) and (8) instead give lower limit at least \(1-2\alpha>0\), a contradiction. Hence an implementation of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{dir}}\) cannot be both uniformly shrinking and uniformly honest over a total-variation-local class containing either contact; derivative-aware use must be restricted to strata separated from the contacts. The finite-sample construction \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}\) remains an honest fallback by \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\). Every separation step used \(0<e<1\); the values at \(e=0\) enter only through the algebraic extension in the clipped finite-sample construction.
