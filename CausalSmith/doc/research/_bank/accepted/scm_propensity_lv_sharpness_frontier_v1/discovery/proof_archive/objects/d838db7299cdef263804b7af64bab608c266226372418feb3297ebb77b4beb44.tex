Fix \(y\in\mathbb R\), let \(B=(-\infty,y]\), and put \(p=P(B)=F_P(y)\).  In the unrestricted bow class, \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) gives
\[
 Q=eP+(1-e)R
\]
with arbitrary \(R\).  Thus, for \(s=R(B)\),
\[
 F_Q(y)=ep+(1-e)s. \tag{38}
\]
Both \(B\) and \(B^c\) are nonempty.  A point mass in \(B\) gives \(s=1\), a point mass in \(B^c\) gives \(s=0\), and their mixtures attain every \(s\in[0,1]\).  Hence
\[
 \{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\}
 =[ep,ep+1-e]=[L^{\to}_e(p),U^{\to}_e(p)]. \tag{39}
\]
The unrestricted completeness construction supplies an attaining bow SCM for every value.  When \(p=0\) or \(p=1\), an endpoint requiring mass on a \(P\)-null side necessarily uses a singular residual law and hence falls outside the mutual-support regime.

In the mutual regime, \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) gives the same formula (38) with \(R\ll P\).  If \(0<p<1\), the laws
\[
 R_1(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B)}p,
 \qquad
 R_0(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B^c)}{1-p}
\]
are well defined, absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), and satisfy \(R_1(B)=1\) and \(R_0(B)=0\).  Their convex mixtures attain every \(s\in[0,1]\).  If \(p=0\), absolute continuity forces \(R(B)=0\), so the unique value is zero.  If \(p=1\), it forces \(R(B)=1\), so the unique value is one.  The choice \(R=P\) attains either boundary value.  These cases are exactly
\[
 \{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\}=[L_e(p),U_e(p)]. \tag{40}
\]
Every residual used for (40) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), so the mutual completeness theorem supplies an attaining SCM satisfying all maintained assumptions.  Nothing in the eventwise argument requires atomlessness, proving the claim also when \(P\) has an atom at \(y\).
