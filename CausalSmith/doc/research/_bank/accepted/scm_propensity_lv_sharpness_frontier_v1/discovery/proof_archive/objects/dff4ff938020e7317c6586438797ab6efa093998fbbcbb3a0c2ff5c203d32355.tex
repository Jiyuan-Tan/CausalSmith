Fix a supporting slope \(b\) of \(f\) at \(1\), and put \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\).  As in the proof of \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}generator\text{-}iff\text{-}frontier}\),
\[
 g\ge0,\qquad g(1)=0,\qquad D_g=D_f,\qquad B_g(e)=B_f(e). \tag{15}
\]
We separate two exhaustive cases.

First suppose \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\).  Then \(g\) is not identically zero on that interval.  If \(g(0)=g(c)=0\), convexity would give \(g\le0\) on \([0,c]\), contrary to nonnegativity unless \(g\) vanished there.  Hence at least one endpoint is positive and
\[
 B_g(e)=\frac1c g(c)+\left(1-\frac1c\right)g(0)>0. \tag{16}
\]
Choose any \(t>c\).  For \(x\in(0,1)\), define
\[
 \lambda(t,x)=\frac{1-x}{t-x},\qquad
 q(t,x)=\lambda(t,x),\qquad p(t,x)=t\lambda(t,x).
\]
The Bernoulli laws \(Q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q(t,x))\) and \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p(t,x))\) have full support and likelihood ratios \(t\) and \(x\).  They violate the cap because \(t>c\), whereas
\[
 D_g(P\|Q)=\lambda(t,x)g(t)+(1-\lambda(t,x))g(x)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }x\uparrow1. \tag{17}
\]
By (16)--(17), for \(x\) sufficiently close to one the pair is strictly ball feasible.  On \((0,1)^2\), the function
\[
 (p,q)\longmapsto qf(p/q)+(1-q)f((1-p)/(1-q))
\]
and both likelihood ratios are continuous.  The strict divergence inequality and the strict cap violation therefore persist on a nonempty Euclidean-open neighborhood.  Every pair in a sufficiently small such neighborhood has full support, so it belongs simultaneously to
\[
 \mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\setminus\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\setminus\mathfrak L_e(P). \tag{18}
\]

Second suppose \(f\) is affine on \([0,c]\).  Choose its slope there for \(b\).  Then \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\); this affine segment is supporting, so convexity gives \(g\ge0\) everywhere.  Since \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), strict positivity above \(c\) fails: there is \(d>c\) with \(g(d)=0\).  Convexity and nonnegativity force
\[
 g=0\quad\text{on }[0,d],\qquad B_g(e)=0. \tag{19}
\]
Choose \(t\in(c,d)\) and \(x\in(0,1)\), and form the same binary pair.  At that pair both likelihood ratios lie strictly inside \((0,d)\), while the first is strictly above \(c\).  These strict conditions persist on a small open neighborhood.  Equation (19) then gives throughout the neighborhood
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)=D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e)=B_f(e),
\]
while the cap is violated.  Full support again yields both differences in (18).  The two cases use only finiteness, continuity, and convexity, so they include nondifferentiable and piecewise-linear generators.  Their respective neighborhoods give the required set \(\mathcal O_f\).
