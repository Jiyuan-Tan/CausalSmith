Fix the setup in the theorem.  First let \(\boldsymbol Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\), witnessed by one global SCM.  Because \(\mathcal Y\) is standard Borel, its Borel sigma-field has a countable generating algebra \(\mathcal C\).  For every \(C\in\mathcal C\), consistency and the conditional versions in the definition give, for each arm and almost every \(x\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_a(x,C)
 &=\Pr\{Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}\\
 &=\Pr\{A=a,Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}
   +\Pr\{A\ne a,Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}\\
 &=e_a(x)P_a(x,C)
   +\Pr\{A\ne a,Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}\\
 &\ge e_a(x)P_a(x,C).
 \end{aligned} \tag{54}
\]
There are two arms and countably many \(C\), so deleting the union of all exceptional sets leaves one measurable \(\mu_X\)-conull set \(S\) on which (54) holds simultaneously.

For fixed \(x\in S\) and \(a\), put \(\lambda_{a,x}=Q_a(x,\cdot)+e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)\).  Every Borel set can be approximated in \(\lambda_{a,x}\)-symmetric-difference measure by members of \(\mathcal C\): the approximable sets contain \(\mathcal C\), and finite-measure continuity shows directly that they are closed under increasing unions and decreasing intersections, hence under the monotone closure of the generating algebra.  Passing (54) through such approximations yields
\[
 Q_a(x,B)\ge e_a(x)P_a(x,B) \tag{55}
\]
for every Borel \(B\), both arms, and every \(x\in S\).

Fix any probability law \(H\) on \(\mathcal Y\), and define
\[
 R_a(x,B)=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{Q_a(x,B)-e_a(x)P_a(x,B)}{1-e_a(x)},&x\in S,\\[6pt]
 H(B),&x\notin S.
 \end{cases} \tag{56}
\]
Overlap gives \(1-e_a(x)\ge\kappa>0\).  By (55), the numerator in (56) is a nonnegative countably additive measure of total mass \(1-e_a(x)\); therefore each section is a probability law.  For every Borel \(B\), the right side is measurable in \(x\), so \(R_a\) is a Markov kernel.  Equation (56) proves the required mixture identity on \(S\), and hence
\[
 \mathfrak M^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
 \subseteq\mathfrak L^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P). \tag{57}
\]

Conversely, suppose residual Markov kernels \(R_0,R_1\) give the mixture identities on one measurable conull set \(S\).  Paste the common fallback law \(H\) into each of \(P_a,Q_a,R_a\) on \(S^c\).  These are versions of the original kernels, remain measurable, and satisfy the mixture identities for every \(x\).  Define a Markov kernel from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Y^2\) by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_x(\{0\}\times dy_0\times dy_1)
 &=e_0(x)P_0(x,dy_0)R_1(x,dy_1),\\
 K_x(\{1\}\times dy_0\times dy_1)
 &=e_1(x)R_0(x,dy_0)P_1(x,dy_1).
 \end{aligned} \tag{58}
\]
Products and finite sums of Markov kernels on standard Borel spaces are Markov kernels; explicitly, measurability first holds on measurable rectangles by iterated kernel integration and extends to the generated sigma-field by closure under bounded monotone limits.  The total mass in (58) is \(e_0(x)+e_1(x)=1\).  Integrating \(K_x\) against \(\mu_X(dx)\) gives one joint law of \((X,A,Y(0),Y(1))\).

Take \(U=(X,A,Y(0),Y(1))\).  Let the equations for \(X\) and \(A\) return their corresponding coordinates, with the stipulated observed parents available, and let the outcome equation return \(Y(A)\).  Coordinate projections and the binary selector are measurable.  The global conditional architecture permits this common latent parent together with the arrows \(X\to A\), \(X\to Y\), and \(A\to Y\); the construction therefore defines one compatible SCM and satisfies consistency.  From (58),
\[
 \Pr(A=a,Y\in B\mid X=x)=e_a(x)P_a(x,B).
\]
Overlap permits division by \(e_a(x)\ge\kappa\), yielding the observed arm kernel \(P_a\).  Also,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid X=x)
 &=e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)+e_{1-a}(x)R_a(x,\cdot)\\
 &=e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)+(1-e_a(x))R_a(x,\cdot)
 =Q_a(x,\cdot).
 \end{aligned} \tag{59}
\]
Thus one SCM realizes both arms simultaneously, giving the reverse inclusion in (57).

Finally, at \(x\in S\), (55) implies \(P_a(x,\cdot)\ll Q_a(x,\cdot)\).  If \(r_{a,x}=dP_a(x,\cdot)/dQ_a(x,\cdot)\), then
\[
 Q_a-e_aP_a\ge0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 1-e_a(x)r_{a,x}\ge0\quad Q_a(x,\cdot)\text{-almost surely},
\]
which is the displayed cap.  Reversing the density calculation proves the converse.  All measure inequalities and kernel identities hold on the one common measurable conull set \(S\); no assertion is made at a prescribed null-stratum version.
