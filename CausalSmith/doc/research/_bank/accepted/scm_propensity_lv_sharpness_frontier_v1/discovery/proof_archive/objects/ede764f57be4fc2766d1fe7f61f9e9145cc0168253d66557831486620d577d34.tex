Fix the objects in the theorem.  For any compatible SCM, consistency gives \(\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=P\), and total probability gives \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), where \(R=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A\ne a)\).  The mutual-absolute-continuity assumption gives \(Q\ll P\).  Since \((1-e)R\le Q\) and \(1-e>0\), it follows that \(R\ll P\).  Thus \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak L_e(P)\).

Conversely, let \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\).  Use the latent construction from the preceding paragraph directly: draw \(A\) with propensity \(e\), draw \(Y(a)\) from \(P\) when \(A=a\) and from \(R\) otherwise, draw the other potential outcome from \(P\), take \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  This is a bow-ADMG SCM satisfying consistency.  The mixture gives \(P\ll Q\) because \(Q\ge eP\), while \(R\ll P\) gives \(Q\ll P\).  Hence the same witness satisfies mutual absolute continuity and realizes \(Q\).  Therefore \(\mathfrak M_e(P)=\mathfrak L_e(P)\).

Finally, the measure calculation in the one-sided argument is repeated verbatim: \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) implies \(P\ll Q\) and \(dP/dQ\le1/e\); if also \(R\ll P\), then \(Q\ll P\).  Conversely, if \(Q\sim P\) and \(dP/dQ\le1/e\), then \(R=(Q-eP)/(1-e)\) is a probability law, and \(R\ll P\) because \(Q\ll P\).  This proves the equivalent likelihood-ratio characterization and the realization claim.
