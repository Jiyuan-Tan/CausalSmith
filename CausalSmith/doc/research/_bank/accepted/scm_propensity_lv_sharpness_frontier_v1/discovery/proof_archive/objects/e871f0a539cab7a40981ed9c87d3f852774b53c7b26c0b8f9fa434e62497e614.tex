Write the rectangular band at \(y_0\) as \(I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\), where the two factors are intervals for the lower and upper endpoint, respectively.  Uniform asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) means that there is a deterministic sequence \(\rho_n\downarrow0\) such that, for every sufficiently large \(n\), every law in the stated local class has simultaneous coverage probability at least \(1-\alpha-\rho_n\).

First consider a boundary law \(\nu_0\) with \(F_P(y_0)=1\) and an alternative \(\nu_n\) with \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n\), and let
\[
 d_n=\|\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=o(1).
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\), the lower endpoints under these two laws are
\[
 L_e(1)=1,
 \qquad
 L_e(1-\delta_n)=e(1-\delta_n), \tag{1}
\]
where the second identity uses \(\delta_n>0\), and eventually \(\delta_n<1\) because \(n\delta_n\to0\).  Define the data events
\[
 C_{0n}=\{1\in I_n^L(y_0)\},
 \qquad
 C_{1n}=\{e(1-\delta_n)\in I_n^L(y_0)\}.
\]
Simultaneous coverage implies the corresponding coordinatewise coverage, so
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n,
 \qquad
 \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n. \tag{2}
\]
The definition of total variation gives, for the same measurable data event \(C_{1n}\),
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})
 \ge \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})-d_n
 \ge1-\alpha-\rho_n-d_n. \tag{3}
\]
The union bound applied under \(\nu_0^{\otimes n}\) to (2)--(3) yields
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n}\cap C_{1n})
 \ge1-2\alpha-2\rho_n-d_n
 =1-2\alpha-o(1). \tag{4}
\]
On this intersection, the interval \(I_n^L(y_0)\) contains both numbers in (1), and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{diam} I_n^L(y_0)
 \ge 1-e(1-\delta_n)
 =1-e+e\delta_n
 \ge1-e. \tag{5}
\]
This proves the first assertion.

For the lower-support boundary, \(F_P(y_0)=0\), and alternatives with \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=\delta_n>0\), \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) gives
\[
 U_e(0)=0,
 \qquad
 U_e(\delta_n)=e\delta_n+1-e. \tag{6}
\]
Repeating (2)--(4) with the events that the upper projection \(I_n^U(y_0)\) contains the two values in (6) shows, under the boundary law, with probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)\), that
\[
 \operatorname{diam} I_n^U(y_0)
 \ge e\delta_n+1-e
 \ge1-e. \tag{7}
\]

It remains to verify that the required indistinguishable alternatives occur inside the bow-SCM class.  Choose real numbers \(z_-\le y_0<z_+\).  For the upper-support boundary in (1), take observational arm laws
\[
 P_0^- =\delta_{z_-},
 \qquad
 P_n^-=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_-}+\delta_n\delta_{z_+}. \tag{8}
\]
Then \(F_{P_0^-}(y_0)=1\) and \(F_{P_n^-}(y_0)=1-\delta_n\).  For the lower-support boundary in (6), take
\[
 P_0^+=\delta_{z_+},
 \qquad
 P_n^+=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_+}+\delta_n\delta_{z_-}, \tag{9}
\]
which have CDF values zero and \(\delta_n\) at \(y_0\).

For each law \(P_j^\pm\), choose residual law \(R_j^\pm=P_j^\pm\).  Then \(R_j^\pm\ll P_j^\pm\), and
\[
 eP_j^\pm+(1-e)R_j^\pm=P_j^\pm. \tag{10}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), (10) is realized by a bow-ADMG SCM with latent variable \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), consistency, propensity \(e\), and mutual absolute continuity.  Concretely, one may take \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), take \(Y(a)\) independent of \(A\) with law \(P_j^\pm\), fix \(Y(1-a)\) at one common value for all \(j\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  This is the latent bow construction in the completeness theorem and also makes the observed conditional law outside arm \(a\) identical across \(j\).

Couple one observation from the boundary and alternative models using the same \(A\), the same outside-arm outcome, and, on \(\{A=a\}\), the same outcome except for the probability-\(\delta_n\) mass moved between \(z_-\) and \(z_+\) in (8) or (9).  The coupled observations differ with probability \(e\delta_n\).  Taking independent copies of this coupling and using the union bound gives a coupling of the \(n\)-sample laws whose vectors differ with probability at most \(ne\delta_n\).  For any event \(H\), the absolute difference of its probabilities under two coupled laws is at most the probability that the coupled vectors differ; hence
\[
 \|\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le ne\delta_n=o(1). \tag{11}
\]
Thus the alternatives required in both parts exist in the maintained bow-SCM class.

Because \(0<e<1\) by \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), the fixed lower bounds \(1-e\) in (5) and (7) are strictly positive.  Any implementation of the directional handle \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{dir}}\) that produced uniformly shrinking rectangular projections while retaining the stipulated uniform coverage would contradict (5) or (7).  It therefore cannot provide a nonconservative shrinking band on a local class containing either contact; such use must exclude these contacts.  The finite-sample construction \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}\) remains an honest fallback by \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\).  Every separation argument above assumes \(0<e<1\); the values at \(e=0\) enter only through the algebraic extension used by the clipped finite-sample construction.
