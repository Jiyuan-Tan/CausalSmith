Put \(B=({-}\infty,y]\), so \(B\) is Borel measurable and \(p=P(B)=F_P(y)\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\) gives \(0<e<1\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\),
\[
 Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q=eP+(1-e)R
 \text{ for some probability law }R\ll P. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, if \(r=R(B)\), then \(0\le r\le1\) and
\[
 F_Q(y)=Q(B)=eP(B)+(1-e)R(B)=ep+(1-e)r. \tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that \(0<p<1\).  For each \(t\in[0,1]\), define a probability law \(R_t\) by
\[
 R_t(C)
 =t\frac{P(C\cap B)}{p}
 +(1-t)\frac{P(C\cap B^{\mathsf c})}{1-p},
 \qquad C\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R). \tag{3}
\]
Both denominators in (3) are strictly positive in this case.  The two normalized restrictions in (3) are probability laws, so their convex combination \(R_t\) is a probability law.  If \(P(C)=0\), then \(P(C\cap B)=P(C\cap B^{\mathsf c})=0\), whence (3) gives \(R_t(C)=0\); thus \(R_t\ll P\).  Moreover,
\[
 R_t(B)
 =t\frac{P(B)}{p}
 +(1-t)\frac{P(B\cap B^{\mathsf c})}{1-p}
 =t. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (2), and (4) show that every number \(ep+(1-e)t\), \(t\in[0,1]\), is attained by some \(Q_t\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\), while (2) shows that no other number is possible.  Since \(1-e>0\),
\[
 \{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\}
 =[ep,ep+1-e]
 =[L_e(p),U_e(p)], \qquad 0<p<1, \tag{5}
\]
where the last equality is \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\).  The choice \(t=1\) is the normalized restriction of \(P\) to \(B\), hence is supported on \(({-}\infty,y]\), and attains \(U_e(p)\).  The choice \(t=0\) is the normalized restriction of \(P\) to \(B^{\mathsf c}\), hence is supported on the complement, and attains \(L_e(p)\).

If \(p=0\), absolute continuity in (1) gives \(R(B)=0\) for every admissible \(R\).  Equation (2) therefore gives \(F_Q(y)=0\) for every \(Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\),
\[
 L_e(0)=0=U_e(0). \tag{6}
\]
If \(p=1\), then \(P(B^{\mathsf c})=0\), so \(R\ll P\) implies \(R(B^{\mathsf c})=0\) and hence \(R(B)=1\).  Thus (2) gives \(F_Q(y)=1\), while \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) gives
\[
 L_e(1)=1=U_e(1). \tag{7}
\]
In either boundary case the admissible choice \(R=P\) yields \(Q=P\), so the unique value in (6) or (7) is attained.

For every residual law just constructed, \(R\ll P\) and \(e>0\) also imply mutual support of \(P\) and \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\): indeed, \(P(C)=0\) implies \(Q(C)=0\), whereas \(Q(C)=0\) and \(eP(C)\le Q(C)\) imply \(P(C)=0\).  The realization clause of \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) therefore supplies a bow-ADMG SCM, with latent variable \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), satisfying the structural, consistency, positivity, and mutual-absolute-continuity member conditions for each witness.  Nothing in (3)--(7) assumes \(P(\{y\})=0\): the event \(B\) includes any atom at \(y\).  Hence the argument covers atoms and both support boundaries.  This eventwise calculation is also exactly the mutual-absolute-continuity specialization asserted by \(\mathrm{thm:support\text{-}regime\text{-}cdf\text{-}endpoints\text{-}sharp}\), completing the claimed exact interval and attainment statement.
