Fix \(0<e<1\) and write \(c=1/e\).  For \(P\ll Q\), let \(r=dP/dQ\).  Then \(\int r\,dQ=1\), so addition of \(b(t-1)\) to a generator changes neither \(D_f(P\|Q)\) nor \(B_f(e)\).  Convexity gives
\[
 f(t)\le\left(1-\frac tc\right)f(0)+\frac tc f(c),\qquad 0\le t\le c. \tag{15}
\]
For \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with arbitrary probability law \(R\), the domination \(Q\ge eP\) implies \(P\ll Q\) and \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  Integrating (15) gives \(D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P) \tag{16}
\]
for every admissible generator.

If \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\), choose the affine representative \(g\) which is zero on \([0,c]\) and strictly positive above \(c\).  Then \(B_g(e)=0\).  If \(Q\in\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\), nonnegativity of \(g\) and affine invariance imply
\[
 0\le\int g(r)\,dQ=D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)=0.
\]
Thus \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  The measure \((Q-eP)/(1-e)\) is a probability law because it has nonnegative \(Q\)-density \((1-er)/(1-e)\).  Hence \(Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).  Together with (16), this proves universal exactness.

For necessity, universal exactness implies exactness for every full-support binary \(P\).  On a full-support binary alphabet the support distinction disappears: arbitrary residual binary laws are automatically absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\).  We nevertheless give the boundary argument explicitly.  For \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\), \(p\in(0,1)\), the law \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(ep)\) is in \(\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).  Every \(Q_q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\) with \(0<q<ep\) violates \(p/q\le c\), so it is outside \(\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).  Exactness and continuity in \(q\) therefore force
\[
 D_f(P\|Q_0)=B_f(e). \tag{17}
\]
At the boundary the ratios are \(c\) and \(t_p=(1-p)/(1-ep)\).  Letting \(p\downarrow0\) in (17) gives \(B_f(e)=0\).

Set \(g(t)=f(t)+f(0)(t-1)\).  The radius identity gives \(g(0)=g(c)=0\), and \(g(1)=0\).  Equation (17) then gives \(g(t_p)=0\) for every \(p\in(0,1)\), hence \(g=0\) on \([0,1]\).  Given \(y\in(1,c)\) and \(x\in(0,1)\), set \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\).  Construct full-support binary \(P,Q\) with likelihood ratios \(y,x\) and \(Q\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\).  The ratios have mean one and are at most \(c\), so \(Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).  Exactness, convexity at the mean, and \(B_g(e)=0\) yield
\[
 0=g(1)\le \lambda g(y)+(1-\lambda)g(x)=D_g(P\|Q)\le0,
\]
whence \(g(y)=0\).  Thus \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\).  Convexity implies \(g(t)\ge0\) for \(t>c\).  If equality held at some \(t>c\), the same two-ratio construction would give \(D_g=0\) but would violate the cap, contradicting exactness.  Hence \(g(t)>0\) above \(c\), so \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).

This proves the three-way equivalence.  The one-sided bow-mixture completeness theorem identifies \(\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\) with \(\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\), giving the stated graphical interpretation.
