The divergence identity follows immediately from the definitions and \(Q\sim P\iff(P\ll Q\text{ and }Q\ll P)\):
\[
 \mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)=\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}. \tag{1}
\]
If \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), then \(Q\ll P\) if and only if \(R\ll P\).  Indeed, the reverse implication follows by addition of absolutely continuous measures; for the forward implication, \((1-e)R\le Q\) and \(1-e>0\), so every \(P\)-null set is \(R\)-null.  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak L_e(P)=\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}. \tag{2}
\]

If a mutual-support SCM witnesses \(Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\), dropping its mutual-support property gives a one-sided witness and mutual support gives \(Q\ll P\).  Conversely, let a one-sided SCM witness \(Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\) and suppose \(Q\ll P\).  By the one-sided completeness theorem, \(P\ll Q\).  Consistency identifies \(P=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)\), so the same witness satisfies the mutual-absolute-continuity property.  This proves
\[
 \mathfrak M_e(P)=\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}. \tag{3}
\]
On a two-point alphabet, full support of \(P\) makes every probability law \(Q\) satisfy \(Q\ll P\).  The intersections in (1)--(3) are then vacuous, proving the last assertion.
