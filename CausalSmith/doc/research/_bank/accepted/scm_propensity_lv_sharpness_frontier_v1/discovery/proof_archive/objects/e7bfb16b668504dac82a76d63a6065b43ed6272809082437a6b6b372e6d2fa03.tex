Fix \(0<e<1\), put \(c=1/e\), and define the chord
\[
 \ell(t)=\left(1-\frac tc\right)f(0)+\frac tc f(c).
\]
Then convexity gives \(f\le\ell\) on \([0,c]\), and
\[
 \ell(1)=(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e). \tag{10}
\]
Adding an affine function neither changes divergence or radius nor changes whether the generator is affine on an interval.  Thus the first case in the statement is precisely that \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\).

Assume first that \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\).  Then
\[
 f(x)<\ell(x),\qquad 0<x<c. \tag{11}
\]
To verify strictness, suppose equality held at an interior \(x_0\).  If \(z<x_0\), express \(x_0\) as a convex combination of \(z\) and \(c\); convexity and \(f\le\ell\) squeeze \(f(z)\) to \(\ell(z)\).  If \(z>x_0\), express \(x_0\) as a convex combination of \(0\) and \(z\) and obtain the same conclusion.  Thus one interior equality would force \(f=\ell\) throughout \([0,c]\), contrary to the case assumption.

Choose \(x\in(0,1)\) and set
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1-x}{c-x},\qquad q_0=\lambda,\qquad p_0=c\lambda.
\]
Since \(c>1\) and \(0<x<1\), \(p_0,q_0\in(0,1)\).  For \(P_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0)\) and \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_0)\), the two likelihood ratios are \(c\) and \(x\), with respective \(Q_0\)-weights \(\lambda\) and \(1-\lambda\).  Also \(q_0=ep_0\).  Equations (10)--(11) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_f(P_0\|Q_0)
 &=\lambda f(c)+(1-\lambda)f(x)\\
 &<\lambda\ell(c)+(1-\lambda)\ell(x)
 =\ell\bigl(\lambda c+(1-\lambda)x\bigr)
 =\ell(1)=B_f(e).
 \end{aligned} \tag{12}
\]
Continuity of the displayed binary divergence permits a choice \(q_1<q_0=ep_0\), sufficiently close to \(q_0\), for which the strict divergence inequality persists at \((p_0,q_1)\).  Both inequalities
\[
 D_f(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\|\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q))<B_f(e),
 \qquad q<ep,
\]
are open conditions on \((0,1)^2\).  They therefore hold on a nonempty Euclidean-open neighborhood \(\mathcal O_f\) of \((p_0,q_1)\).  Every pair in this neighborhood has mutual support and belongs to \(\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\), whereas \(p/q>c\) violates the likelihood-ratio cap and excludes it from \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\).

Now assume that \(f\) is affine on \([0,c]\).  Since \(f(1)=0\), there is \(b\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)=0,\qquad 0\le t\le c. \tag{13}
\]
Convexity and (13) imply \(g(t)\ge0\) for \(t>c\): express \(c\) as a convex combination of \(1\) and \(t\).  Since \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), strict positivity above \(c\) fails, so some \(d>c\) satisfies \(g(d)=0\).  Convexity between \(c\) and \(d\), together with nonnegativity, yields
\[
 g(t)=0,\qquad 0\le t\le d. \tag{14}
\]
Choose \(x\in(0,1)\), \(y\in(c,d)\), and \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\).  Define full-support binary laws by assigning \(Q\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\) to cells with likelihood ratios \(y,x\).  In Bernoulli coordinates, \(q=\lambda\) and \(p=\lambda y\), and both lie in \((0,1)\) because the two positive weighted ratios have mean one.  By (14) and affine invariance,
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)=D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e)=B_f(e),
\]
whereas \(p/q=y>c\), so the cap is violated.  The strict conditions
\[
 c<p/q<d,\qquad 0<(1-p)/(1-q)<d
\]
persist on a sufficiently small nonempty open neighborhood.  On that neighborhood both likelihood ratios remain in \((0,d)\), so (14) keeps the divergence identically zero, while the first ratio remains above the cap.  This supplies the asserted open illegal region inside the zero-divergence face.  No differentiability was used in either case, so the proof includes nondifferentiable and piecewise-linear generators.
