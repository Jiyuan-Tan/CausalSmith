By \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), \(0<e<1\).  Hence the definition
\(c=1/e\) gives \(c>1\).  For the generator \(f_c(t)=(t-c)_+\),
\[
f_c(c)=0,
\qquad
f_c(0)=0.
\]
Therefore, by the definition of the propensity radius,
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{f_c}(e)
&=e f_c(1/e)+(1-e)f_c(0)\\
&=e f_c(c)+(1-e)f_c(0)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now take \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) and
\(Q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\), where \(p,q\in(0,1)\), on the
two-point space \(\{0,1\}\).  Both laws put positive mass on both points,
so \(P\sim Q\); in particular, the Radon--Nikodym derivative required in
the definition of \(D_f(P\|Q)\) exists and satisfies
\[
\frac{dP}{dQ}(1)=\frac{p}{q},
\qquad
\frac{dP}{dQ}(0)=\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
\]
The defining integral for the divergence is consequently the two-point
sum
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{f_c}(P\|Q)
&=q\left(\frac{p}{q}-c\right)_+
 +(1-q)\left(\frac{1-p}{1-q}-c\right)_+\\
&=(p-cq)_+ +\bigl((1-p)-c(1-q)\bigr)_+.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses the elementary identity
\(s(x/s-c)_+=(x-cs)_+\), valid for \(s>0\); its two applications are
legitimate because \(q>0\) and \(1-q>0\).

Each summand in the last display is nonnegative.  Since \(x_+=0\) if and
only if \(x\leq 0\), and since \(1/c=e\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{f_c}(P\|Q)=0
&\Longleftrightarrow p-cq\leq0
 \ \text{and}\ \ (1-p)-c(1-q)\leq0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow q\geq \frac{p}{c}
 \ \text{and}\ \ q\leq 1-\frac{1-p}{c}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow ep\leq q\leq 1-e+ep.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to compare this zero set with the binary fiber in
\(\mathrm{def:mixture\text{-}class}\).  Every probability law \(R\) on
\(\{0,1\}\) is \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(r)\) for a unique
\(r\in[0,1]\).  Because \(p\in(0,1)\), the law \(P\) has full support;
hence every such \(R\) satisfies \(R\ll P\).  If
\(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), evaluation on \(\{1\}\) gives
\[
q=ep+(1-e)r,
\]
and therefore \(ep\leq q\leq ep+1-e\).  Conversely, suppose that
\(ep\leq q\leq ep+1-e\) and define
\[
r=\frac{q-ep}{1-e}.
\]
The denominator is positive by \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), and the two
assumed inequalities imply \(0\leq r\leq1\).  Thus
\(R=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(r)\) is a probability law, \(R\ll P\), and
the equality of the masses at \(1\) gives
\(Q=eP+(1-e)R\).  This proves that the zero-divergence interval is exactly
the binary propensity-mixture fiber \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  Notice also
that full support makes this certificate valid under either the one-sided
condition \(P\ll Q\) or the mutual condition \(P\sim Q\).

Finally, let \(\lambda>0\), let \(b\in\mathbb R\), and set
\[
\widetilde f(t)=\lambda f_c(t)+b(t-1).
\]
Since \(c>1\), \(f_c(1)=0\).  Positive scaling and addition of an affine
function preserve finiteness, continuity, and convexity, while
\(\widetilde f(1)=0\).  Moreover, using \(ec=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{\widetilde f}(e)
&=e\{\lambda f_c(c)+b(c-1)\}
 +(1-e)\{\lambda f_c(0)-b\}\\
&=b\{e(c-1)-(1-e)\}=b(ec-1)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
For any probability laws with \(P\ll Q\), and hence in particular for
the present full-support Bernoulli laws, the defining divergence integral
and \(\int(dP/dQ)\,dQ=P(\mathcal Y)=1\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\widetilde f}(P\|Q)
&=\lambda D_{f_c}(P\|Q)
 +b\int_{\mathcal Y}\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}-1\right)dQ\\
&=\lambda D_{f_c}(P\|Q)
 +b\{P(\mathcal Y)-Q(\mathcal Y)\}\\
&=\lambda D_{f_c}(P\|Q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\lambda>0\), this quantity is zero if and only if
\(D_{f_c}(P\|Q)=0\).  Thus positive scaling of the hinge and addition of
any term \(b(t-1)\) preserve both the zero radius and the exact binary
legality fiber, as claimed.
