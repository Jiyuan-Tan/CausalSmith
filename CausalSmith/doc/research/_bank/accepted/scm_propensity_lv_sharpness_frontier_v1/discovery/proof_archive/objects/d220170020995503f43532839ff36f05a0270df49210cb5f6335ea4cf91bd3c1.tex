Fix \(0<e<1\), an arm \(a\), and a bow-SCM belonging to \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\).  By consistency,
\[
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)=P.
\]
Put \(R=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A\ne a)\).  The law of total probability, with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), gives
\[
 Q=\mathcal L(Y(a))=eP+(1-e)R. \tag{1}
\]
The mutual-absolute-continuity assumption says \(P\sim Q\).  If \(N\) is measurable and \(P(N)=0\), then \(Q(N)=0\), and (1), nonnegativity, and \(1-e>0\) imply \(R(N)=0\).  Thus \(R\ll P\), proving \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak L_e(P)\).

Conversely, suppose that \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) for a probability law \(R\ll P\).  On a standard Borel probability space construct \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\).  Conditionally on \(A=a\), draw \(Y(a)\) with law \(P\); conditionally on \(A\ne a\), draw \(Y(a)\) with law \(R\).  Draw the other potential outcome \(Y(1-a)\) conditionally independently with any fixed law, for example \(P\).  Take the single latent variable
\[
 U=(A,Y(0),Y(1)),
\]
set the treatment structural equation to the first coordinate of \(U\), and set the outcome structural equation to \(Y=Y(A)\).  This is a latent-variable representation of the bow ADMG: the common latent parent \(U\) supplies \(A\leftrightarrow Y\), while the outcome equation has the direct input \(A\), supplying \(A\to Y\).  It obeys consistency by construction, has the specified propensity and observed arm law, and (1) gives \(\mathcal L(Y(a))=Q\).  Moreover, \(Q\ll P\) because \(R\ll P\), and \(P\ll Q\) because \(Q(B)\ge eP(B)\) for every measurable \(B\).  Hence \(P\sim Q\), so the constructed SCM belongs to \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\).  This proves the reverse inclusion and the asserted realization.

It remains to verify the likelihood-ratio formulation.  If \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\), then \(Q\sim P\), and \(Q\ge eP\) implies
\[
 \frac{dP}{dQ}\le \frac1e\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, if \(Q\sim P\) and (2) holds, define
\[
 R(B)=\frac{Q(B)-eP(B)}{1-e}.
\]
Writing \(r=dP/dQ\), the numerator has density \(1-er\ge0\) with respect to \(Q\); it therefore defines a nonnegative countably additive measure.  Its total mass is \((1-e)/(1-e)=1\), and \(R\ll P\) follows from \(Q\ll P\).  Thus \(R\) is an admissible residual law and \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), completing all equivalences.
