Fix \(0<e<1\), put \(c=1/e\), and define the chord of \(f\) between \(0\) and \(c\) by
\[
 \ell(t)=\left(1-\frac tc\right)f(0)+\frac tc f(c).
\]
Convexity gives \(f(t)\le\ell(t)\) on \([0,c]\), and
\[
 \ell(1)=(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e). \tag{6}
\]
Subtracting an affine function does not change whether a function is affine on an interval.  Thus the first case in the statement is precisely the case in which \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\).

Assume first that \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\).  Then
\[
 f(x)<\ell(x)\qquad\text{for every }x\in(0,c). \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, \(h=\ell-f\) is concave and nonnegative on \([0,c]\), with \(h(0)=h(c)=0\).  If \(h(x_0)=0\) at one interior point, concavity applied first with endpoints \(x<x_0<c\) and then with endpoints \(0<x_0<x\) forces \(h(x)=0\) on both sides of \(x_0\), contradicting non-affinity.

Choose \(x\in(0,1)\), let
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1-x}{c-x},\qquad q_0=\lambda,\qquad p_0=c\lambda,
\]
and put \(P_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0)\), \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_0)\).  Since \(c>1\) and \(0<x<1\), both laws have full support.  Their likelihood ratios are \(c\) and \(x\), and \(q_0=ep_0\), so \(Q_0\in\mathfrak L_e(P_0)\).  Because affine functions commute with expectation and the two ratios have \(Q_0\)-mean one, (6)--(7) give
\[
 D_f(P_0\|Q_0)
 =\lambda f(c)+(1-\lambda)f(x)
 <\lambda\ell(c)+(1-\lambda)\ell(x)
 =\ell(1)=B_f(e). \tag{8}
\]
The binary divergence is continuous on \((0,1)^2\).  Hence one may choose \(q_1<q_0=ep_0\), sufficiently close to \(q_0\), such that
\[
 D_f\!\left(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0)\middle\|\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_1)\right)<B_f(e).
\]
Both strict inequalities \(D_f<B_f(e)\) and \(q<ep\) persist on some nonempty Euclidean-open neighborhood \(\mathcal O_f\) of \((p_0,q_1)\) contained in \((0,1)^2\).  Throughout this neighborhood, \(P\sim Q\), the strict divergence inequality places \(Q\) in \(\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\), while \(p/q>c\) excludes \(Q\) from \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  This proves the first alternative.

Assume next that \(f\) is affine on \([0,c]\).  Since \(f(1)=0\), there is \(b\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)=0,\qquad 0\le t\le c. \tag{9}
\]
Convexity and (9) imply \(g(t)\ge0\) for \(t>c\).  Because \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), strict positivity fails: there is \(d>c\) with \(g(d)=0\).  Convexity between \(c\) and \(d\), together with nonnegativity, shows that in fact
\[
 g(t)=0,\qquad 0\le t\le d. \tag{10}
\]
Choose \(x\in(0,1)\) and \(y\in(c,d)\), let \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\), and form full-support binary laws for which \(dP/dQ\) equals \(y\) on a cell of \(Q\)-mass \(\lambda\) and \(x\) on its complement.  In Bernoulli coordinates this means \(q=\lambda\) and \(p=\lambda y\).  Then \(p/q=y>c\), so the pair is SCM-illegal, whereas affine invariance and (10) give
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)=D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e)=B_f(e).
\]
The inequalities \(p/q>c\), \((1-p)/(1-q)\in(0,d)\), and \(p/q<d\) persist on a sufficiently small open neighborhood.  On that neighborhood both likelihood ratios lie in \((0,d)\), so (10) keeps the divergence identically zero, while the cap remains violated.  This is the asserted nonempty open illegal set in the zero-divergence face.  The argument uses only finiteness, continuity, and convexity, and therefore includes kinked and piecewise-linear generators.
