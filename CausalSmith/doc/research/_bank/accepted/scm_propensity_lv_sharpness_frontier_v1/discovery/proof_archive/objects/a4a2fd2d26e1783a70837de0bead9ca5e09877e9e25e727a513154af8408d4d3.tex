Fix \(0<e<1\) and put \(c=1/e\).  If \(P\ll Q\), write \(r=dP/dQ\), so \(\int r\,dQ=1\).  Replacing \(f\) by \(f_b(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\) changes neither divergence nor radius because
\[
 \int(r-1)\,dQ=0,
 \qquad
 e(c-1)+(1-e)(0-1)=0. \tag{41}
\]
Convexity gives
\[
 f(t)\le\left(1-\frac tc\right)f(0)+\frac tc f(c),
 \qquad 0\le t\le c. \tag{42}
\]
If \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), then \(Q\ge eP\), hence \(P\ll Q\) and \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  Integrating (42) yields
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)\le(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e). \tag{43}
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\).

If \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\), choose an affine representative \(g\) that is zero on \([0,c]\) and strictly positive above \(c\).  Then \(g\ge0\) and \(B_g(e)=0\).  For \(Q\in\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\), affine invariance gives
\[
 0\le\int g(r)\,dQ=D_g(P\|Q)\le B_g(e)=0.
\]
Thus \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  The measure \(R=(Q-eP)/(1-e)\) has nonnegative \(Q\)-density \((1-er)/(1-e)\) and total mass one, so \(Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).  Together with (43), this proves universal exactness.

Conversely, assume exactness for every full-support binary \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\).  For \(Q_q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\), the boundary \(q=ep\) belongs to the mixture class, while every \(0<q<ep\) violates the cap \(p/q\le c\).  Exactness and continuity in \(q\) therefore give
\[
 epf(c)+(1-ep)f\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-ep}\right)=B_f(e). \tag{44}
\]
Letting \(p\downarrow0\) and using \(f(1)=0\) gives \(B_f(e)=0\).  Normalize
\[
 g(t)=f(t)+f(0)(t-1).
\]
Affine invariance gives \(B_g(e)=0\), while \(g(0)=g(1)=0\) and \(B_g(e)=eg(c)\) give \(g(c)=0\).  Equation (44) applied to \(g\) shows that \(g((1-p)/(1-ep))=0\).  This ratio ranges over \((0,1)\), so \(g=0\) on \([0,1]\).

For \(y\in(1,c)\), choose \(x\in(0,1)\) and \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\).  Construct full-support binary \(P,Q\) whose likelihood ratios are \(y,x\) with \(Q\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\).  Their mean ratio is one, and both ratios obey the cap, so exactness and convexity imply
\[
 0=g(1)\le\lambda g(y)+(1-\lambda)g(x)\le B_g(e)=0.
\]
Thus \(g(y)=0\), and \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\).  Convexity then gives \(g(t)\ge0\) for \(t>c\).  If \(g(t)=0\) for some \(t>c\), the two-ratio construction with ratios \(t\) and any \(x\in(0,1)\) produces a zero-divergence ball member that violates the cap, contradicting exactness.  Hence \(g(t)>0\) above \(c\), so \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).  Universal exactness implies binary exactness, and \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) identifies the exact set with the causal class.
