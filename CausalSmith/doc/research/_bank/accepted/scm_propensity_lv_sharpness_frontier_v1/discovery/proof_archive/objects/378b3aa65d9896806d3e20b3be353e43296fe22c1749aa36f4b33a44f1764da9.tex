Let \(c_i=1/e_i\).  If one generator were universally exact at both propensities, the one-sided generator frontier would imply \(f\in\mathfrak H_{c_1}\cap\mathfrak H_{c_2}\).  Suppose without loss of generality that \(c_1<c_2\).  Membership supplies numbers \(b_i\) such that \(f(t)=b_i(t-1)\) on \([0,c_i]\).  These identities agree on \([0,c_1]\), which contains a point different from \(1\), so \(b_1=b_2=:b\).  Therefore \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\) vanishes on \([0,c_2]\).  Membership in \(\mathfrak H_{c_1}\), however, requires \(g(t)>0\) for every \(t>c_1\), a contradiction on \((c_1,c_2]\).  Thus the intersection is empty and no fixed generator is exact at both propensities.

For any \(e\in(0,1)\), the adaptive generator \(f_e(t)=(t-1/e)_+\) is finite, continuous, convex, satisfies \(f_e(1)=0\), vanishes on \([0,1/e]\), and is strictly positive above \(1/e\).  Hence \(f_e\in\mathfrak H_{1/e}\).  The two fixed-stratum frontier theorems give exactness at that stratum in the one-sided and mutual-support regimes.
