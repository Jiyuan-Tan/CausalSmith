By \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), an unrestricted compatible law has the form \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), and every such law is realized by the stated latent construction.  That representation implies \(P\ll Q\).  The mutual-support member property therefore adds precisely \(Q\ll P\).  Moreover,
\[
 Q\ll P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R\ll P. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if \(Q\ll P\), then \((1-e)R\le Q\) and \(1-e>0\), so \(R\ll P\); conversely, \(R\ll P\) implies that both summands in \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\).  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak M_e(P)=\{eP+(1-e)R:R\ll P\}=\mathfrak L_e(P).
\]
Intersecting the one-sided cap characterization with \(Q\ll P\) yields the equivalent condition \(Q\sim P\) and \(dP/dQ\le1/e\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  Finally, the same construction with \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\) satisfies mutual absolute continuity exactly when (1) holds, so every admissible residual law is realized.
