Write the rectangular band at \(y_0\) as \(I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\).  Uniform asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) supplies a deterministic sequence \(\rho_n\downarrow0\) such that every law in the stated local class has simultaneous coverage probability at least \(1-\alpha-\rho_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

First let \(\nu_0\) be a boundary observed-data law with \(F_P(y_0)=1\), and let \(\nu_n\) be an alternative with the same \(e\) and \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n\).  Put
\[
 d_n=\|\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=o(1).
\]
The endpoint definition gives
\[
 L_e(1)=1,
 \qquad
 L_e(1-\delta_n)=e(1-\delta_n), \tag{25}
\]
where eventually \(0<\delta_n<1\) because \(n\delta_n\to0\).  Define
\[
 C_{0n}=\{1\in I_n^L(y_0)\},
 \qquad
 C_{1n}=\{e(1-\delta_n)\in I_n^L(y_0)\}.
\]
Simultaneous coverage implies coordinatewise coverage, so
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n,
 \qquad
 \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n. \tag{26}
\]
By the definition of total variation, the same data event satisfies
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})
 \ge\Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})-d_n
 \ge1-\alpha-\rho_n-d_n. \tag{27}
\]
The union bound under \(\nu_0^{\otimes n}\) gives
\[
 \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n}\cap C_{1n})
 \ge1-2\alpha-2\rho_n-d_n
 =1-2\alpha-o(1). \tag{28}
\]
On this intersection, the interval \(I_n^L(y_0)\) contains both values in (25), and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{diam}I_n^L(y_0)
 \ge1-e(1-\delta_n)=1-e+e\delta_n\ge1-e. \tag{29}
\]

For a boundary law with \(F_P(y_0)=0\) and alternatives satisfying \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=\delta_n\), the upper endpoints are
\[
 U_e(0)=0,
 \qquad
 U_e(\delta_n)=e\delta_n+1-e. \tag{30}
\]
Repeating (26)--(28) for the events that \(I_n^U(y_0)\) contains the two values in (30) yields, under the boundary law, probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)\) that
\[
 \operatorname{diam}I_n^U(y_0)\ge e\delta_n+1-e\ge1-e. \tag{31}
\]

It remains to realize the stipulated alternatives inside the maintained bow-SCM class.  Choose \(z_-\le y_0<z_+\).  For the first boundary take
\[
 P_0^-=\delta_{z_-},
 \qquad
 P_n^-=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_-}+\delta_n\delta_{z_+}; \tag{32}
\]
then their CDF values at \(y_0\) are \(1\) and \(1-\delta_n\).  For the second boundary take
\[
 P_0^+=\delta_{z_+},
 \qquad
 P_n^+=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_+}+\delta_n\delta_{z_-}; \tag{33}
\]
their CDF values are \(0\) and \(\delta_n\).  For each \(P_j^\pm\), choose \(R_j^\pm=P_j^\pm\).  Then \(R_j^\pm\ll P_j^\pm\) and
\[
 eP_j^\pm+(1-e)R_j^\pm=P_j^\pm. \tag{34}
\]
The bow-mixture completeness theorem realizes (34) by a bow SCM with propensity \(e\), consistency, and mutual support.  The observed conditional law outside arm \(a\) may be held fixed across \(j\).

Couple one observation from the boundary and alternative laws using the same treatment, the same outside-arm outcome, and, on \(\{A=a\}\), the same outcome except on the probability-\(\delta_n\) mass shifted in (32) or (33).  The two observations differ with probability \(e\delta_n\).  Independent copies and the union bound give a coupling of the sample vectors for which
\[
 \Pr\{\text{the two sample vectors differ}\}\le ne\delta_n.
\]
For any data event, the probability difference under a coupling is bounded by the mismatch probability; hence
\[
 \|\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le ne\delta_n=o(1). \tag{35}
\]
Thus the required alternatives exist in the bow-SCM class.

Positivity makes \(1-e>0\), so (29) and (31) preclude uniformly shrinking rectangular endpoint projections on any local class containing these contacts.  Any implementation of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{dir}}\) with both the asserted uniform coverage and shrinking projections would contradict those inequalities; derivative-aware inference must therefore be restricted to separated regular strata.  The finite-sample band remains honest by \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\).  Every separation argument here uses \(0<e<1\); the value at \(e=0\) appears only in the algebraically extended map used by the clipped finite-sample construction.
