Fix the objects in the statement.  All conditional identities below are asserted on a single \(\mu_X\)-conull measurable set.  Such a set may be chosen because the spaces are standard Borel and hence have countable generating classes; intersect the conull sets for the two arms and the countably many generator events.  On its complement all conditional versions will be set equal to one fixed probability law \(H\) on \(\mathcal Y\).

Let a global SCM witness \(\boldsymbol Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\).  For each arm define the regular conditional kernel
\[
 R_a(x,\cdot)=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=1-a,X=x).
\]
The denominator of this conditioning is \(e_{1-a}(x)=1-e_a(x)\ge\kappa>0\).  Consistency gives
\[
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a,X=x)=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a,X=x)=P_a(x,\cdot).
\]
Therefore conditional total probability yields, for every measurable \(B\subseteq\mathcal Y\),
\[
 Q_a(x,B)=e_a(x)P_a(x,B)+(1-e_a(x))R_a(x,B). \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\boldsymbol Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\).

Conversely, suppose Markov kernels \(R_0,R_1\) satisfy (1).  Define a kernel from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Y^2\) by
\[
 K_x(\{a\},dy_0,dy_1)
 =e_a(x)P_a(x,dy_a)R_{1-a}(x,dy_{1-a}),\qquad a\in\{0,1\}. \tag{2}
\]
For rectangles, measurability in \(x\) follows from measurability of the component kernels.  The class of product-Borel sets on which \(x\mapsto K_x(C)\) is measurable is a monotone class containing the rectangles; hence (2) defines a Markov kernel.  Its total mass is \(e_0(x)+e_1(x)=1\).  Combine it with \(\mu_X\), take \(U=(X,A,Y(0),Y(1))\) to have the resulting joint law, let the structural equations for \(X\) and \(A\) return their coordinates, and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  The response type shared by \(A\) and \(Y\) realizes \(A\leftrightarrow Y\), and the arguments \(X,A\) in the treatment and outcome equations realize the directed edges of the covariate-augmented bow graph.

From (2), for every measurable \(B\),
\[
 \Pr(A=a,Y\in B\mid X=x)=e_a(x)P_a(x,B),
\]
so strict overlap permits division by \(e_a(x)\) and gives \(\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a,X=x)=P_a(x,\cdot)\).  Also,
\[
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid X=x)
 =e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)+e_{1-a}(x)R_a(x,\cdot)
 =Q_a(x,\cdot). \tag{3}
\]
This is one simultaneous construction for both arms, proving equality of the graphical and mixture-kernel classes.

It remains to prove the two analytic characterizations.  Equation (1) implies the measure inequality \(Q_a(x,\cdot)\ge e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)\).  Conversely, under that inequality define directly
\[
 R_a(x,B)=\frac{Q_a(x,B)-e_a(x)P_a(x,B)}{1-e_a(x)}. \tag{4}
\]
On the common conull set this is a probability measure; its total mass is one, and for every fixed \(B\) the right side is measurable in \(x\).  On the exceptional set put \(R_a(x,cdot)=H\).  Countable generation and the monotone-class argument above show that \(R_a\) is a Markov kernel.  Thus (4) proves equivalence with mixture membership without a measurable-selection theorem.

Finally, the measure inequality implies \(P_a(x,\cdot)\ll Q_a(x,\cdot)\).  If \(r_{a,x}=dP_a(x,\cdot)/dQ_a(x,\cdot)\), then it is equivalent to \(e_a(x)r_{a,x}\le1\), \(Q_a(x,\cdot)\)-almost surely.  Conversely that density inequality makes the numerator in (4) nonnegative.  This proves the cap characterization.
