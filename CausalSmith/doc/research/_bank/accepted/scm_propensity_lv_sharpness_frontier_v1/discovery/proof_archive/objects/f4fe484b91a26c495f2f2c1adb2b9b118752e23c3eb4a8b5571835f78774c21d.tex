Let \(Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), \(P\ll Q\) and \(r=dP/dQ\le c=1/e\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  Convexity gives the chord inequality
\[
 f(r)\le(1-r/c)f(0)+(r/c)f(c).
\]
Since \(E_Qr=1\), integration yields \(D_f(P\|Q)\le(1-e)f(0)+ef(1/e)=B_f(e)\).  Hence \(\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\), and therefore
\[
 \mathfrak B^{\mathrm{law},\to}_{f,e}(P)
 =\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\cap\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)
 =\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)=\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P).
\]
On a finite alphabet, write \(p_j=P(\{j\})\) and \(q_j=Q(\{j\})\).  The inequalities \(q_j\ge ep_j\) are equivalent to the probability vector \(r_j=(q_j-ep_j)/(1-e)\), and hence to \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\).  If cells with \(p_j=0\) remain in the ambient alphabet, this is exactly the unrestricted lawful class, including possible new support.  Set inclusion gives weak tightening of every linear optimization; compactness of the simplex and uniqueness of a relaxed cap-violating optimizer give strict tightening by the second-optimizer contradiction.

If \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), \(\mathrm{thm:support\text{-}regime\text{-}open\text{-}illegal\text{-}dichotomy}\) supplies a full-support binary open set of ball-feasible cap violations.  On a Bernoulli alphabet, a cap violation is \(q<ep\) or \(q>ep+1-e\).  One alternative holds on a nonempty open subset; the success indicator then makes the relaxed lower endpoint strictly smaller than \(ep\), or the relaxed upper endpoint strictly larger than \(ep+1-e\).  Projection onto the \(p\)-axis is open and nonempty, proving the one-sided query-level claim.
