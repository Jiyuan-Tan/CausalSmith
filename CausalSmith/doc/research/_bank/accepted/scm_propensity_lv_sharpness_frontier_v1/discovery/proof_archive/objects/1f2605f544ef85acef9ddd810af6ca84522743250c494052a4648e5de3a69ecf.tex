Let \(c_i=1/e_i\).  If one generator were universally exact at both propensities, the one-sided frontier theorem would imply \(f\in\mathfrak H_{c_1}\cap\mathfrak H_{c_2}\).  Assume \(c_1<c_2\).  Membership supplies slopes \(b_i\) for which
\[
 f(t)=b_i(t-1),\qquad 0\le t\le c_i.
\]
Agreement on \([0,c_1]\), which contains a point other than \(1\), forces \(b_1=b_2=b\).  Then \(f(t)-b(t-1)\) vanishes on \([0,c_2]\), contradicting the strict positivity above \(c_1\) required by \(f\in\mathfrak H_{c_1}\).  Hence the intersection is empty.

For any \(e\in(0,1)\), the function \(f_e(t)=(t-1/e)_+\) is finite, continuous, and convex, satisfies \(f_e(1)=0\), vanishes on \([0,1/e]\), and is strictly positive above \(1/e\).  Thus \(f_e\in\mathfrak H_{1/e}\) with affine slope \(b=0\).  The one-sided and mutual frontier theorems then give exactness at that fixed stratum.
