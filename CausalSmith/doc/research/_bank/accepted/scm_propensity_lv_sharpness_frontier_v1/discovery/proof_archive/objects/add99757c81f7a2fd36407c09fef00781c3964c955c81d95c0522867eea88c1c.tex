Fix \(y\in\mathbb R\), let \(B=(-\infty,y]\), and put \(p=P(B)=F_P(y)\).  In the unrestricted bow class, \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) gives \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with arbitrary \(R\).  Thus, writing \(s=R(B)\),
\[
 F_Q(y)=Q(B)=ep+(1-e)s. \tag{20}
\]
Both \(B\) and \(B^c\) are nonempty.  A point mass at any point of \(B\) has \(s=1\), a point mass at any point of \(B^c\) has \(s=0\), and mixtures of these two laws attain every \(s\in[0,1]\).  Hence, for every \(p\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\}
 =[ep,ep+1-e]=[L^{\to}_e(p),U^{\to}_e(p)]. \tag{21}
\]
The unrestricted construction in the completeness theorem supplies an attaining bow SCM for each value.  When \(p=0\) or \(p=1\), an endpoint requiring mass on a \(P\)-null side is attained by a singular residual law and therefore does not satisfy mutual absolute continuity.

Now impose the mutual-AC condition.  The mutual corollary \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) gives exactly the representations (20) with \(R\ll P\).  If \(0<p<1\), define
\[
 R_1(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B)}p,
 \qquad
 R_0(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B^c)}{1-p}.
\]
Both laws are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), and they satisfy \(R_1(B)=1\) and \(R_0(B)=0\).  Their convex mixtures attain all \(s\in[0,1]\), so the mutual interval is \([ep,ep+1-e]\).  If \(p=0\), absolute continuity forces \(R(B)=0\), and (20) is uniquely zero.  If \(p=1\), it forces \(R(B^c)=0\), and (20) is uniquely one.  At either boundary \(R=P\) is an admissible attaining residual law.  These are precisely the piecewise values in \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\):
\[
 \{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\}=[L_e(p),U_e(p)]. \tag{22}
\]
Every residual law used for (22) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), so the mutual construction in the completeness theorem gives an attaining SCM satisfying all four maintained assumptions.  The argument is eventwise and does not assume that \(P\) is atomless; in particular an atom at \(y\) causes no change.
