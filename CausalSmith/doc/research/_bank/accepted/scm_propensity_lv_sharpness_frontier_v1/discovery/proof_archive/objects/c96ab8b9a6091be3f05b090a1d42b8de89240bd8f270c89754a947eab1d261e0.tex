Let \(c_i=1/e_i\) for \(i=1,2\).  If one generator were universally exact at both propensities, \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}generator\text{-}iff\text{-}frontier}\) would imply
\[
 f\in\mathfrak H_{c_1}\cap\mathfrak H_{c_2}. \tag{49}
\]
Assume without loss of generality that \(c_1<c_2\).  Membership in \(\mathfrak H_{c_i}\) supplies \(b_i\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 f(t)=b_i(t-1),\qquad 0\le t\le c_i.
\]
The two identities agree on \([0,c_1]\); evaluation at \(t=0\ne1\) gives \(b_1=b_2=:b\).  Hence \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\) vanishes on \([0,c_2]\).  Membership in \(\mathfrak H_{c_1}\), however, requires \(g(t)>0\) for every \(t>c_1\), contradicting its vanishing on \((c_1,c_2]\).  Thus the intersection in (49) is empty, and no fixed generator is universally exact at both distinct propensities.

For any \(e\in(0,1)\),
\[
 f_e(t)=(t-1/e)_+
\]
is finite, continuous, and convex, satisfies \(f_e(1)=0\), vanishes on \([0,1/e]\), and is strictly positive above \(1/e\).  Taking the affine coefficient \(b=0\) shows \(f_e\in\mathfrak H_{1/e}\).  The one-sided and mutual generator frontier theorems therefore give exactness at that stratum in their respective support regimes.
