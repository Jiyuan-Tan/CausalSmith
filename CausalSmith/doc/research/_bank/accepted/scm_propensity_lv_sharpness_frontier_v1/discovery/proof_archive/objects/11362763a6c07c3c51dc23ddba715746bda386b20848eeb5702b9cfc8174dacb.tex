Fix \(0<e<1\), a probability law \(P\) on the standard Borel outcome space \(\mathcal Y\), and an admissible generator \(f\).  All absolute-continuity statements below concern probability measures on the same measurable space \(\mathcal Y\).

First consider the divergence classes.  By \(\mathrm{def:jk\text{-}ball}\), mutual absolute continuity means the conjunction of the two directed relations, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)
&=\left\{Q:Q\sim P,\ D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)\right\}\\
&=\left\{Q:P\ll Q,\ D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)\right\}
  \cap\left\{Q:Q\ll P\right\}\\
&=\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\cap\left\{Q:Q\ll P\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The second equality uses only the definition \(Q\sim P\iff(P\ll Q\text{ and }Q\ll P)\), and the last equality is \(\mathrm{def:jk\text{-}ball\text{-}one\text{-}sided}\).  Finiteness, continuity, and convexity of \(f\) ensure that the displayed divergence class is the admissible one specified in the core; no additional property of \(f\) is needed for this set identity.

Next consider the mixture classes.  Let
\[
Q=eP+(1-e)R
\tag{2}
\]
for a probability law \(R\).  We claim
\[
Q\ll P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R\ll P.
\tag{3}
\]
For the forward implication, let \(E\) be measurable with \(P(E)=0\).  If \(Q\ll P\), then \(Q(E)=0\); equation (2) gives
\[
0=Q(E)=eP(E)+(1-e)R(E)=(1-e)R(E).
\]
Because \(1-e>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), division gives \(R(E)=0\), proving \(R\ll P\).  Conversely, if \(R\ll P\) and \(P(E)=0\), then \(R(E)=0\), and (2) yields \(Q(E)=0\); hence \(Q\ll P\).  Therefore (3), together with \(\mathrm{def:mixture\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:mixture\text{-}class\text{-}one\text{-}sided}\), gives
\[
\mathfrak L_e(P)
=\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\cap\left\{Q:Q\ll P\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

It remains to prove the graphical-class identity.  Suppose first that \(Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:bow\text{-}compatible}\), a witnessing SCM obeys \(\mathrm{ass:bow\text{-}structure}\), \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:mutual\text{-}ac}\).  Dropping only the last member property leaves a witness for \(Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\) under \(\mathrm{def:bow\text{-}compatible\text{-}one\text{-}sided}\).  Moreover, because \(e=\Pr(A=a)>0\), conditioning on \(A=a\) is well defined, and consistency gives
\[
\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)=P.
\tag{5}
\]
The mutual-absolute-continuity member property consequently reads \(P\sim Q\), where \(Q=\mathcal L(Y(a))\); in particular \(Q\ll P\).  Thus
\[
\mathfrak M_e(P)
\subseteq \mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\cap\left\{Q:Q\ll P\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Conversely, let \(Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\) and suppose \(Q\ll P\).  Choose the one-sided bow-ADMG SCM witness furnished by \(\mathrm{def:bow\text{-}compatible\text{-}one\text{-}sided}\).  Equation (5) holds in this witness by consistency and positivity.  The already established \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) states, in particular, that every member of \(\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\) satisfies \(P\ll Q\).  Combining that conclusion with the assumed \(Q\ll P\) gives
\[
\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=P\sim Q=\mathcal L(Y(a)).
\tag{7}
\]
Hence the same SCM witness also satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:mutual\text{-}ac}\), and therefore belongs to the class in \(\mathrm{def:bow\text{-}compatible}\).  This proves the reverse inclusion in (6), and so
\[
\mathfrak M_e(P)
=\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\cap\left\{Q:Q\ll P\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Finally, suppose that \(\mathcal Y\) is a two-point alphabet and that \(P\) has full support.  If \(E\subseteq\mathcal Y\) satisfies \(P(E)=0\), full support forces \(E=\varnothing\).  Thus every probability law \(Q\) on \(\mathcal Y\) satisfies \(Q\ll P\).  The intersections on the right sides of (1), (4), and (8) therefore impose no restriction.  Consequently each one-sided class equals its corresponding mutual-absolute-continuity class on every full-support binary alphabet, as claimed.
