Write an arbitrary rectangular band at \(y\) as
\[
 \mathcal C_n(y)=I_n^L(y)\times I_n^U(y),
\]
where the two factors are random intervals.  The assumed uniform asymptotic coverage means, in particular, that along the boundary law and along every displayed alternative sequence the relevant simultaneous-coverage probability is at least \(1-\alpha-r_n\) for some \(r_n=o(1)\).  Marginal coverage at \(y_0\) follows because the simultaneous-coverage event is a subset of the corresponding marginal event.

First suppose \(F_{P,0}(y_0)=1\), and let \(\mathbb P_{0,n}\) and \(\mathbb P_{1,n}\) denote the boundary and alternative \(n\)-sample observed-data laws.  By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\), the boundary and alternative lower endpoints are respectively
\[
 \ell_0=L_e(1)=1,
 \qquad
 \ell_n=L_e(1-\delta_n)=e(1-\delta_n), \tag{6}
\]
where the second equality uses \(0<1-\delta_n<1\), which holds for all sufficiently large \(n\) because \(n\delta_n\to0\) and \(\delta_n>0\).  Let
\[
 C_{0,n}=\{\ell_0\in I_n^L(y_0)\},
 \qquad
 C_{1,n}=\{\ell_n\in I_n^L(y_0)\}.
\]
Uniform coverage gives
\[
 \mathbb P_{0,n}(C_{0,n})\ge1-\alpha-o(1),
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{1,n}(C_{1,n})\ge1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{7}
\]
For any event \(C\), the definition of total-variation distance gives
\[
 |\mathbb P_{0,n}(C)-\mathbb P_{1,n}(C)|
 \le \operatorname{TV}(\mathbb P_{0,n},\mathbb P_{1,n}).
\]
The assumed \(o(1)\) total-variation distance and (7) therefore imply \(\mathbb P_{0,n}(C_{1,n})\ge1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Bonferroni's inequality now gives
\[
 \mathbb P_{0,n}(C_{0,n}\cap C_{1,n})
 \ge1-2\alpha-o(1). \tag{8}
\]
On this intersection the interval \(I_n^L(y_0)\) contains both values in (6), and hence
\[
 \operatorname{diam} I_n^L(y_0)
 \ge |\ell_0-\ell_n|
 =1-e+e\delta_n
 \ge1-e. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)--(9) prove the lower-endpoint assertion.

If instead \(F_{P,0}(y_0)=0\) and \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=\delta_n\), then \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) gives
\[
 u_0=U_e(0)=0,
 \qquad
 u_n=U_e(\delta_n)=1-e+e\delta_n. \tag{10}
\]
Repeating (7)--(8) with the events \(\{u_0\in I_n^U(y_0)\}\) and \(\{u_n\in I_n^U(y_0)\}\) yields, under the boundary law, probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)\) that
\[
 \operatorname{diam}I_n^U(y_0)
 \ge |u_n-u_0|
 =1-e+e\delta_n
 \ge1-e, \tag{11}
\]
which is the upper-endpoint assertion.  Strict positivity \(0<e<1\) from \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\) makes the fixed lower bound \(1-e\) positive and ensures both treatment conditionals used below are defined.

It remains to verify that the assumed close alternatives are genuinely available in the graphical model.  Write \(\Delta_z\) for the unit point mass at \(z\).  For the first boundary, choose \(z_+>y_0\) and, for all sufficiently large \(n\), define
\[
 \widetilde P_n=(1-\delta_n)P_0+\delta_n\Delta_{z_+}.
\]
Because \(P_0(({-}\infty,y_0])=1\), one has \(\widetilde P_n(({-}\infty,y_0])=1-\delta_n\) and \(\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde P_n,P_0)=\delta_n\).  Keep the propensity \(e\) and the off-arm observed law \(S=\mathcal L(Y\mid A\ne a)\) fixed.  The resulting one-observation laws are
\[
 \nu_0=e(\Delta_a\otimes P_0)+(1-e)(\Delta_{1-a}\otimes S),
 \qquad
 \nu_n=e(\Delta_a\otimes\widetilde P_n)+(1-e)(\Delta_{1-a}\otimes S),
\]
so \(\operatorname{TV}(\nu_n,\nu_0)=e\delta_n\).  The telescoping identity
\[
 \nu_n^{\otimes n}-\nu_0^{\otimes n}
 =\sum_{k=1}^n
 \nu_n^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes(\nu_n-\nu_0)
 \otimes\nu_0^{\otimes(n-k)}
\]
and the triangle inequality for the total-variation norm give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\nu_n^{\otimes n},\nu_0^{\otimes n})
 \le n\operatorname{TV}(\nu_n,\nu_0)
 =ne\delta_n=o(1). \tag{12}
\]
For each \(n\), realize \(\nu_n\) by drawing \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), drawing \(Y(a)\sim\widetilde P_n\) and \(Y(1-a)\sim S\) independently of \(A\), and setting \(Y=Y(A)\).  With latent variable \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), this is a nonparametric latent-variable SCM Markov to the bow ADMG and it satisfies consistency.  Moreover,
\[
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=\widetilde P_n
 =\mathcal L(Y(a)),
\]
so the mutual-absolute-continuity condition holds.  Equivalently, \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) certifies the interventional law \(Q_n=\widetilde P_n\) because it has the admissible representation \(Q_n=e\widetilde P_n+(1-e)\widetilde P_n\).  Thus the alternatives are members of the bow-SCM class.  For the second boundary, choose \(z_-\le y_0\) and use \(\widetilde P_n=(1-\delta_n)P_0+\delta_n\Delta_{z_-}\); since \(P_0(({-}\infty,y_0])=0\), the same argument gives \(F_{\widetilde P_n}(y_0)=\delta_n\) and (12).

Because \(1-2\alpha>0\), (9) or (11) contradicts convergence of the relevant projection diameter to zero in boundary-law probability.  The conclusion applies to every rectangular band, hence also to any band produced through the handle \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{dir}}\): such a band cannot be both uniformly covering and shrinking on a class containing these support contacts.  The theorem therefore rules derivative-based shortening out on tie-inclusive local classes, while \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\) supplies the stated honest fallback.  Throughout this argument \(0<e<1\); the algebraic value of the endpoint maps at \(e=0\), used only when clipping the finite-sample propensity interval, is not invoked.
