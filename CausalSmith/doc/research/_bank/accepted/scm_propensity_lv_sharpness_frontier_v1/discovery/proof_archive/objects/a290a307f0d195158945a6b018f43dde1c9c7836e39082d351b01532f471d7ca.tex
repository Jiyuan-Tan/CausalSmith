Let \(\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P)\) be the class in the statement.  For the adaptive divergence classes, the identity is definitional: fiberwise equivalence \(P_a\sim Q_a\) is the conjunction of the one-sided support condition \(P_a\ll Q_a\) and reverse support \(Q_a\ll P_a\).  Intersecting the finitely many conull carriers produces one common measurable conull set, so
\[
 \mathfrak B^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
 =\mathfrak B^{\mathrm{ad},\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
  \cap\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P). \tag{60}
\]

For a mixture identity \(Q_a=e_aP_a+(1-e_a)R_a\), overlap gives \(e_a\ge\kappa>0\), so \(Q_a\ge e_aP_a\) and therefore \(P_a\ll Q_a\).  Moreover,
\[
 Q_a(x,\cdot)\ll P_a(x,\cdot)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R_a(x,\cdot)\ll P_a(x,\cdot). \tag{61}
\]
The reverse implication follows by addition.  For the forward implication, \((1-e_a(x))R_a(x,\cdot)\le Q_a(x,\cdot)\) and \(1-e_a(x)\ge\kappa>0\).  Applying (61) for two arms and intersecting their conull sets gives
\[
 \mathfrak L_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
 =\mathfrak L^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
  \cap\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P). \tag{62}
\]

Dropping reverse support from a mutual SCM witness gives a one-sided witness.  Conversely, take a one-sided witness whose \(\boldsymbol Q\) lies in \(\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P)\).  Conditional one-sided completeness gives \(P_a\ll Q_a\) for both arms on one common conull set; intersecting it with the reverse-support carrier gives \(P_a\sim Q_a\) for both arms.  The same SCM witness therefore satisfies the conditional reverse-support member property.  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak M_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
 =\mathfrak M^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
  \cap\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P). \tag{63}
\]
Equations (60)--(63) also prove the asserted automatic forward support.
