Write \(c=1/e\).  We first record directly from convexity that the likelihood-ratio class is always inside the divergence ball.  If \(Q\sim P\), \(r=dP/dQ\le c\), and \(E_Qr=1\), then the chord inequality on \([0,c]\) gives
\[
 f(r)\le \left(1-\frac r c\right)f(0)+\frac r c f(c).
\]
Integration yields
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)\le \left(1-\frac1c\right)f(0)+\frac1c f(c)
 =(1-e)f(0)+ef(1/e)=B_f(e). \tag{3}
\]
By the likelihood-ratio equivalence in \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), (3) proves \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\).

Suppose first that \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).  Choose \(b\) from the definition and put \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\).  Since \(E_Q[dP/dQ]=1\),
\[
 D_g(P\|Q)=D_f(P\|Q),
\]
and, because \(ec=1\),
\[
 B_g(e)=B_f(e)-b\{e(c-1)+(1-e)(0-1)\}=B_f(e). \tag{4}
\]
For this representative, \(B_g(e)=0\), \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\), and \(g>0\) on \((c,\infty)\).  Therefore
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \int g(dP/dQ)\,dQ=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{dP}{dQ}\le c\quad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{5}
\]
The last equivalence uses the pointwise nonnegativity of \(g\) and its strict positivity above \(c\).  Combining (5) with the likelihood-ratio characterization proves \(\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)=\mathfrak L_e(P)\) on every standard Borel space.

For necessity, assume equality of the two classes for every full-support binary \(P\).  Because \(f\) is finite and convex and \(1\) is an interior point of its domain, the left and right secant slopes at \(1\) bound a finite supporting slope \(b\): convexity gives a number \(b\) satisfying
\[
 f(t)\ge f(1)+b(t-1)=b(t-1),\qquad t\ge0.
\]
Set \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\).  Then \(g\ge0\), \(g(1)=0\), and (4) shows that both the divergence and radius are unchanged.

For each \(x\in(0,1)\), let
\[
 \lambda_x=\frac{1-x}{c-x},\qquad
 Q_x=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\lambda_x),qquad
 P_x=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(c\lambda_x).
\]
Both binary laws have full support, and the two values of \(dP_x/dQ_x\) are \(c\) and \(x\).  Hence \(Q_x\in\mathfrak L_e(P_x)\).  We claim
\[
 D_g(P_x\|Q_x)=B_g(e). \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, (3) gives the weak inequality.  If it were strict, keep \(x\) fixed, replace \(c\) by \(t>c\), and set \(\lambda(t)=(1-x)/(t-x)\), \(Q_t=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\lambda(t))\), and \(P_t=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(t\lambda(t))\).  The divergence
\[
 \lambda(t)g(t)+(1-\lambda(t))g(x)
\]
is continuous at \(t=c\).  It would remain below \(B_g(e)\) for all sufficiently small \(t-c>0\), although \(dP_t/dQ_t=t>c\) on the success atom.  This would put \(Q_t\) in the ball but outside \(\mathfrak L_e(P_t)\), contradicting binary set equality.  Thus (6) holds.

Letting \(x\uparrow1\) in (6), continuity of \(g\), \(g(1)=0\), and \(\lambda_x\to0\) give \(B_g(e)=0\).  Since
\[
 B_g(e)=\frac1c g(c)+\left(1-\frac1c\right)g(0)
\]
and both summands are nonnegative with positive coefficients, \(g(0)=g(c)=0\).  Convexity gives \(g(t)\le0\) between these endpoints, while the supporting-line construction gives \(g(t)\ge0\); hence \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\).  Finally, if \(g(t_0)=0\) for some \(t_0>c\), choose any \(x\in(0,1)\) and form the preceding binary pair with likelihood ratios \(t_0,x\).  It is illegal, but its divergence is zero and its radius is zero, another contradiction.  Therefore \(g(t)>0\) for every \(t>c\), so \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).

Universal equality plainly implies equality for every full-support binary law; the preceding paragraph proves that binary equality implies membership in \(\mathfrak H_c\), and (5) proves that membership implies universal equality.  This closes the three-way equivalence.
