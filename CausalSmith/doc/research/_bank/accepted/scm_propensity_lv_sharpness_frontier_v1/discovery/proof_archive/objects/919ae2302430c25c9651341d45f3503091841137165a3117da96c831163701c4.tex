The one-sided theorem and the support-intersection proposition give the mutual result without another boundary argument.  If \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\), then for every \(P\),
\[
 \mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)
 =\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}
 =\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}
 =\mathfrak L_e(P).
\]
Conversely, if mutual equality holds for every full-support binary \(P\), then every binary probability law \(Q\) satisfies \(Q\ll P\).  The one-sided and mutual divergence balls, and likewise the two mixture classes, therefore coincide on that alphabet.  Mutual binary equality is thus one-sided binary equality, and \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}generator\text{-}iff\text{-}frontier}\) yields \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).  The implication from universal equality to binary equality is immediate.  Finally, \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) identifies the mutual mixture class with the compatible bow-SCM class.
