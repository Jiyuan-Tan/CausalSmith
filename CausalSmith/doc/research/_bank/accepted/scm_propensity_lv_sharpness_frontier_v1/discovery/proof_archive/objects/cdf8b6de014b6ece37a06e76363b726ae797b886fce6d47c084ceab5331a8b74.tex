Fix a threshold \(y\), put \(B=(-\infty,y]\), and write \(p=P(B)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), every compatible law and only such a law has the form \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\).  Therefore, with \(r_B=R(B)\),
\[
 F_Q(y)=Q(B)=ep+(1-e)r_B. \tag{9}
\]
If \(0<p<1\), absolute continuity permits every \(r_B\in[0,1]\): the two endpoint residual laws are
\[
 R_1(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B)}{p},\qquad
 R_0(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B^c)}{1-p},
\]
and their convex mixtures attain every intermediate value.  Equation (9) therefore ranges over \([ep,ep+1-e]=[L_e(p),U_e(p)]\).  The completeness theorem turns each such residual law into an attaining bow-ADMG SCM satisfying consistency, positivity, and mutual absolute continuity.

If \(p=0\), then \(R\ll P\) forces \(R(B)=0\), so (9) is uniquely zero, equal to \(L_e(0)=U_e(0)\).  If \(p=1\), then \(P(B^c)=0\) forces \(R(B^c)=0\), so (9) is uniquely one, equal to \(L_e(1)=U_e(1)\).  These arguments depend only on the event and its complement and hence remain valid when \(P\) has an atom at \(y\).  They also provide the claimed attaining SCMs in every case.
