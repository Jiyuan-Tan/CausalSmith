Fix \(y\in\mathbb R\), set \(B=(-\infty,y]\), and write \(p=P(B)=F_P(y)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), \(0<e<1\), so in particular \(1-e>0\).

First consider the unrestricted support regime.  By \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), a law \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\) if and only if there is an arbitrary probability law \(R\) on \(\mathbb R\) such that
\[
Q=eP+(1-e)R.
\tag{1}
\]
Evaluating (1) on \(B\) and putting \(s=R(B)\in[0,1]\) gives
\[
F_Q(y)=Q(B)=eP(B)+(1-e)R(B)=ep+(1-e)s.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every attainable value lies in \([ep,ep+1-e]\).  Conversely, for each \(s\in[0,1]\), define
\[
R_s=s\delta_y+(1-s)\delta_{y+1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(y\in B\) and \(y+1\in B^c\), (3) is a probability law satisfying \(R_s(B)=s\).  The realization clause of \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) supplies a bow-ADMG SCM whose interventional law is \(Q_s=eP+(1-e)R_s\).  Equation (2) therefore shows that every point of \([ep,ep+1-e]\), and in particular both endpoints, is attained.  By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps\text{-}one\text{-}sided}\),
\[
\{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\}
=[ep,ep+1-e]
=[L^{\to}_e(p),U^{\to}_e(p)].
\tag{4}
\]

Now consider the mutual-absolute-continuity regime.  By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), a law \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\) if and only if it has representation (1) with \(R\ll P\).  Equation (2) remains valid.

Suppose first that \(0<p<1\).  For every Borel set \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\), define
\[
R_1(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B)}{p},
\qquad
R_0(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B^c)}{1-p}.
\tag{5}
\]
The denominators in (5) are positive by the present case assumption.  Both \(R_1\) and \(R_0\) are probability laws, and each is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\).  Moreover,
\[
R_1(B)=1,
\qquad
R_0(B)=0.
\tag{6}
\]
For any \(s\in[0,1]\), the probability law
\[
R_s=sR_1+(1-s)R_0
\tag{7}
\]
satisfies \(R_s\ll P\) and \(R_s(B)=s\).  Hence (2), (7), and the realization clause of \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) show that the set of attainable values is exactly \([ep,ep+1-e]\), with its lower and upper endpoints attained respectively by \(R_0\) and \(R_1\).

If \(p=0\), then \(P(B)=0\), and \(R\ll P\) forces \(R(B)=0\).  Equation (2) consequently gives the unique attainable value
\[
F_Q(y)=0.
\tag{8}
\]
This value is attained, for example, by taking \(R=P\).  If \(p=1\), then \(P(B^c)=0\), so \(R\ll P\) forces \(R(B^c)=0\), equivalently \(R(B)=1\).  Equation (2) then gives the unique attainable value
\[
F_Q(y)=ep+(1-e)=1,
\tag{9}
\]
again attained by \(R=P\).  Combining the interior calculation with (8)--(9) and using \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) yields
\[
\{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\}
=[L_e(p),U_e(p)].
\tag{10}
\]
All residual laws used to attain (10) are dominated by \(P\), so \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) realizes them by bow-ADMG SCMs satisfying the maintained mutual-support regime.

It remains to verify the claimed support obstruction at the boundary.  When \(p=0\), attainment of the unrestricted upper endpoint \(1-e\) forces \(s=1\) in (2), because \(1-e>0\).  Thus \(R(B)=1\) while \(P(B)=0\), which implies \(R\perp P\).  When \(p=1\), attainment of the unrestricted lower endpoint \(e\) forces \(s=0\), hence \(R(B^c)=1\) while \(P(B^c)=0\), and again \(R\perp P\).  These are exactly the unrestricted boundary endpoints lying outside the corresponding singleton mutual-AC intervals.

Finally, no step assumed that \(P\) is atomless.  In particular, if \(P\) has an atom at \(y\), that atom is simply included in \(B=(-\infty,y]\); whenever \(0<p<1\), the conditional residual laws in (5) remain well defined and attain the two endpoints.  Therefore all endpoint and attainment conclusions hold in the presence of atoms as stated.
