Fix \(0<e<1\), put \(c=1/e\), and let \(f\) be any finite continuous convex generator with \(f(1)=0\).  If \(Q\in\mathfrak L_e(P)\), then \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) for some \(R\ll P\).  Thus \(Q\sim P\), and for \(r=dP/dQ\) one has \(0<r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely, and \(\int r\,dQ=1\).  The convex chord inequality on \([0,c]\) yields
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)
 \le\int\left\{\left(1-\frac rc\right)f(0)+\frac rc f(c)\right\}dQ
 =(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e). \tag{11}
\]
Hence \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\).  By the definition of the lawful correction,
\[
 \mathfrak B^{\mathrm{law}}_{f,e}(P)
 =\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\cap\mathfrak L_e(P)
 =\mathfrak L_e(P).
\]
The frozen bow-mixture completeness theorem then gives \(\mathfrak L_e(P)=\mathfrak M_e(P)\).

Let \(h\in L^\infty(P)\), and write
\[
 m=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_P h,\qquad M=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_P h.
\]
For \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\),
\[
 E_Q[h]=eE_P[h]+(1-e)E_R[h],\qquad m\le E_R[h]\le M. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the claimed expressions bound the infimum and supremum.  Conversely, for every \(\eta>0\), the defining properties of essential infimum and supremum give sets of positive \(P\)-probability
\[
 C^-_\eta=\{h<m+\eta\},\qquad C^+_\eta=\{h>M-\eta\}.
\]
Taking \(R^-_\eta=P(\,\cdot\mid C^-_\eta)\) and \(R^+_\eta=P(\,\cdot\mid C^+_\eta)\), both of which are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), and then letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) in (12), proves
\[
 \underline\theta_h=eE_P[h]+(1-e)m,\qquad
 \overline\theta_h=eE_P[h]+(1-e)M. \tag{13}
\]

Now suppose the alphabet is finite and restricted to \(\operatorname{supp}(P)\), so every retained cell has \(p_j>0\).  A probability vector \(q=(q_j)_j\) belongs to \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\) if and only if
\[
 q_j\ge ep_j\quad\text{for every }j. \tag{14}
\]
The forward implication follows from \(q_j=ep_j+(1-e)r_j\).  Conversely, (14) makes
\[
 r_j=\frac{q_j-ep_j}{1-e}
\]
nonnegative with \(\sum_jr_j=1\), and the support restriction gives \(R\ll P\).  By (11), the divergence constraint is redundant on (14).  Therefore adjoining (14) to the divergence program gives exactly the mutual-absolute-continuity compatible class.  Its feasible set is a closed subset of the finite simplex and hence compact.  Restricting a feasible set can only weakly lower a linear maximum and weakly raise a linear minimum.  If a relaxed optimizer is unique and violates (14), equality of the corresponding optimal values would make an optimizer over the compact capped set a second relaxed optimizer, a contradiction.  The tightening is then strict.  The same coordinate argument is consistent with the unrestricted-alphabet formulation in \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}query\text{-}legality\text{-}correction}\).

Finally suppose \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\).  The construction in \(\mathrm{thm:open\text{-}illegal\text{-}dichotomy}\) yields an open binary set with \(q<ep\) and \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\).  It contains a rectangle \(I\times J\) about one of its points.  For every \(p\) in a sufficiently small nonempty open interval \(I\), some \(q\in J\) remains divergence-feasible and satisfies \(q<ep\).  For the bounded success indicator \(h(z)=\mathbf 1\{z=1\}\), the lawful lower endpoint is \(ep\) by (13), whereas the relaxed lower value is at most \(q<ep\).  This is the claimed strict legality gap on an open region of observational laws.
