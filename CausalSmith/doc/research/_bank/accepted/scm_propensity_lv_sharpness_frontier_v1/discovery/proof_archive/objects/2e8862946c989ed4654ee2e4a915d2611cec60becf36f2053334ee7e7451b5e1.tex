For the divergence classes, the definitions and the identity
\[
 Q\sim P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(P\ll Q\ \text{and}\ Q\ll P)
\]
give directly
\[
 \mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)
 =\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f,e}(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}. \tag{50}
\]

If \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), then
\[
 Q\ll P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R\ll P. \tag{51}
\]
Indeed, the reverse implication follows by addition of absolutely continuous measures.  For the forward implication, if \(P(B)=0\), then \(Q(B)=0\), while \((1-e)R(B)\le Q(B)\) and \(1-e>0\), so \(R(B)=0\).  Consequently,
\[
 \mathfrak L_e(P)
 =\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}. \tag{52}
\]

If a mutual-support SCM witnesses \(Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\), dropping the mutual-support member property gives a one-sided witness and that property itself gives \(Q\ll P\).  Conversely, suppose a one-sided SCM witnesses \(Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\) and \(Q\ll P\).  One-sided bow-mixture completeness gives \(P\ll Q\).  Consistency identifies \(P=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)\), so the same witness satisfies \(\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)\sim\mathcal L(Y(a))\).  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak M_e(P)
 =\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\cap\{Q:Q\ll P\}. \tag{53}
\]
On a two-point alphabet with full-support \(P\), every probability law \(Q\) satisfies \(Q\ll P\).  Moreover, every member of a one-sided class satisfies \(P\ll Q\), which forces \(Q\) itself to have full support.  Thus the intersections in (50)--(53) are vacuous on every full-support binary alphabet, proving the final assertion.
