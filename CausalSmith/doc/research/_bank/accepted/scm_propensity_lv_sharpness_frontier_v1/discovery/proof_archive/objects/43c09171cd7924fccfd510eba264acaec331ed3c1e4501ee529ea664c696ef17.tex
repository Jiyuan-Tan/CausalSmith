Fix \(0<e<1\) and put \(c=1/e\).  If \(P\ll Q\) and \(r=dP/dQ\), then \(\int r\,dQ=1\).  Consequently, for every \(b\in\mathbb R\), replacing \(f\) by
\[
 f_b(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)
\]
changes neither the divergence nor the calibrated radius, because
\[
 \int(r-1)\,dQ=0,
 \qquad
 e(c-1)+(1-e)(0-1)=0. \tag{2}
\]
Convexity gives the chord inequality
\[
 f(t)\le \left(1-\frac{t}{c}\right)f(0)+\frac{t}{c}f(c),
 \qquad 0\le t\le c. \tag{3}
\]

If \(Q\in\mathfrak L_e(P)\), the mixture characterization in \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) gives \(Q\sim P\) and \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  Integrating (3), using \(\int r\,dQ=1\) and \(1/c=e\), yields
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)
 \le \left(1-\frac1c\right)f(0)+\frac1c f(c)
 =(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e). \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\) for every admissible generator.

Suppose first that \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).  By definition there is \(b\) such that \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\) vanishes on \([0,c]\) and is strictly positive on \((c,\infty)\).  In particular \(g\ge0\), and
\[
 B_g(e)=eg(c)+(1-e)g(0)=0. \tag{5}
\]
For \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\), mutual support supplies \(r=dP/dQ\), and affine invariance (2) gives
\[
 0\le \int g(r)\,dQ=D_g(P\|Q)=D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)=B_g(e)=0.
\]
Hence \(g(r)=0\), \(Q\)-almost surely, and strict positivity above \(c\) implies \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  The likelihood-ratio characterization in the preceding theorem now gives \(Q\in\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  Together with (4), this proves universal exactness.

For necessity, assume equality for every full-support binary \(P\).  Write \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\), \(p\in(0,1)\), and \(Q_q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\).  The law \(Q_{ep}\) is the mixture of \(P\) and \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0)\), and the residual is absolutely continuous with respect to full-support \(P\); hence \(Q_{ep}\in\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  For \(0<q<ep\), the likelihood ratio on the success cell is \(p/q>c\), so \(Q_q\notin\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  Exactness therefore gives
\[
 D_f(P\|Q_{ep})\le B_f(e),
 \qquad
 D_f(P\|Q_q)>B_f(e)\quad(0<q<ep). \tag{6}
\]
The binary divergence
\[
 q\longmapsto qf(p/q)+(1-q)f((1-p)/(1-q))
\]
is continuous on \((0,1)\).  Letting \(q\uparrow ep\) in (6) proves the boundary equality
\[
 epf(c)+(1-ep)f\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-ep}\right)=B_f(e). \tag{7}
\]
Letting \(p\downarrow0\) in (7), using finiteness and continuity of \(f\) and \(f(1)=0\), gives
\[
 B_f(e)=0. \tag{8}
\]

Normalize by setting \(g(t)=f(t)+f(0)(t-1)\).  By affine invariance, \(B_g(e)=0\), while
\[
 g(0)=g(1)=0,
 \qquad
 0=B_g(e)=eg(c)+(1-e)g(0)
\]
implies \(g(c)=0\).  Applying (7) to \(g\) yields
\[
 g\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-ep}\right)=0,\qquad 0<p<1. \tag{9}
\]
The argument of \(g\) in (9) ranges over \((0,1)\); continuity therefore gives \(g=0\) on \([0,1]\).

Let \(y\in(1,c)\), choose \(x\in(0,1)\), and put \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\).  Give one cell \(Q\)-mass \(\lambda\), the other mass \(1-\lambda\), and obtain \(P\) by multiplying these masses by \(y\) and \(x\), respectively.  Because
\[
 \lambda y+(1-\lambda)x=1,
\]
these are full-support probability laws, and both likelihood ratios are at most \(c\).  Hence \(Q\in\mathfrak L_e(P)\), and exactness, convexity at the mean-one likelihood ratio, and (8) give
\[
 0=g(1)\le D_g(P\|Q)
   =\lambda g(y)+(1-\lambda)g(x)\le B_g(e)=0.
\]
Since \(g(x)=0\) and \(\lambda>0\), it follows that \(g(y)=0\).  Thus \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\).

For \(t>c\), express \(c\) as a strict convex combination of \(1\) and \(t\).  Convexity and \(g(1)=g(c)=0\) imply \(g(t)\ge0\).  If \(g(t)=0\), choose any \(x\in(0,1)\) and repeat the preceding two-ratio construction with ratios \(t\) and \(x\).  It gives full-support binary laws with
\[
 D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e),
\]
so \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\), but the ratio \(t>c\) excludes \(Q\) from \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\), contradicting exactness.  Hence \(g(t)>0\) for every \(t>c\), which is exactly \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).  Universal exactness plainly implies binary exactness, and the bow-mixture theorem identifies the mixture class with the compatible causal class, completing the three-way equivalence.
