Let \(c_i=1/e_i\) for \(i=1,2\), and suppose without loss of generality that \(c_1<c_2\).  If one generator were universally exact at both propensities, \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}generator\text{-}iff\text{-}frontier}\) would give \(f\in\mathfrak H_{c_1}\cap\mathfrak H_{c_2}\).  Hence there would be slopes \(b_1,b_2\) such that
\[
 f(t)-b_i(t-1)=0\quad(0\le t\le c_i),
\]
and the first normalized representative would be strictly positive for every \(t>c_1\).  On the common interval \([0,c_1]\),
\[
 (b_1-b_2)(t-1)=0\quad\text{for every }t.
\]
Choosing any \(t\ne1\) in that interval yields \(b_1=b_2=:b\).  The second membership therefore says that the same representative \(f(t)-b(t-1)\) vanishes through \(c_2\), contradicting its required strict positivity on \((c_1,c_2]\).  Thus no fixed generator is universally exact for every observational law at two distinct propensities.

For a fixed \(e\), the adaptive generator
\[
 f_e(t)=(t-1/e)_+
\]
is finite, continuous, convex, satisfies \(f_e(1)=0\) because \(1/e>1\), vanishes on \([0,1/e]\), and is strictly positive above \(1/e\).  Taking affine slope zero shows \(f_e\in\mathfrak H_{1/e}\).  The one-sided generator frontier theorem then gives \(\mathfrak B^{\to}_{f_e,e}(P)=\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\) for every \(P\), and intersection with \(Q\ll P\) gives the mutual-AC equality.  This proves the stratumwise adaptive conclusion.
