Fix a positive integer \(n\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).  Let
\[
 E_e=\{|\widehat e_n-e|\le\delta_e\}.
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}bounded\text{-}mean}\) to the independent variables \(\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}\in[0,1]\), whose common mean is \(e\) by the definition of the propensity.  Since \(\delta_e^2=\log(4/\alpha)/(2n)\),
\[
 \Pr(E_e^c)
 \le 2\exp(-2n\delta_e^2)
 =2\exp\{-\log(4/\alpha)\}
 =\frac{\alpha}{2}. \tag{1}
\]
Because both \(e\) and \(\widehat e_n\) lie in \([0,1]\), the event \(E_e\) implies \(e\in I_e\), including when the untrimmed Hoeffding interval crosses zero or one.

For \(n_a>0\), define
\[
 E_F=\left\{\sup_{y\in\mathbb R}|\widehat F_{P,n}(y)-F_P(y)|\le\delta_F\right\}.
\]
Under \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), conditional on the treatment indicators and on \(n_a=m>0\), the \(m\) outcomes with \(A_i=a\) are independent and have common distribution function \(F_P\).  Thus \(\mathrm{lem:dkw\text{-}massart\text{-}cdf\text{-}band}\), applied conditionally, gives
\[
 \Pr(E_F^c\mid A_1,\ldots,A_n)
 \le 2\exp(-2m\delta_F^2)
 =\frac{\alpha}{2}
 \qquad\text{on }\{n_a=m>0\}. \tag{2}
\]
Summing (2) over all treatment-indicator configurations yields
\[
 \Pr(n_a>0,E_F^c)\le\frac{\alpha}{2}. \tag{3}
\]
On \(E_F\), clipping the empirical interval to \([0,1]\) cannot remove the true value \(F_P(y)\in[0,1]\), so \(F_P(y)\in I_F(y)\) simultaneously for every \(y\).
Combining (1) and (3) by the union bound,
\[
 \Pr\bigl(E_e\cap(\{n_a=0\}\cup E_F)\bigr)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{4}
\]

On \(E_e\cap E_F\) with \(n_a>0\), one has simultaneously for every \(y\in\mathbb R\)
\[
 e\in I_e,
 \qquad
 F_P(y)\in I_F(y).
\]
Therefore the generating set in \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}band}\) contains
\[
 \bigl(L_e(F_P(y)),U_e(F_P(y))\bigr)
\]
for every \(y\), and its coordinatewise rectangular hull contains that pair as well.  On \(\{n_a=0\}\), the same conclusion holds because the definition sets \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}(y)=[0,1]^2\).  Equation (4) consequently proves
\[
 \Pr\left\{
 \bigl(L_e(F_P(y)),U_e(F_P(y))\bigr)
 \in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}(y)
 \text{ for every }y\in\mathbb R
 \right\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:cdf\text{-}endpoints\text{-}sharp}\), the covered pair is the exact causal CDF endpoint pair.  The Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality is distribution-free and permits atoms.  Moreover, the last containment uses membership of the true point in the generating rectangle rather than continuity of \((\eta,p)\mapsto(L_\eta(p),U_\eta(p))\); hence it remains valid at \(p=0\), \(p=1\), and at every discontinuous face of the piecewise endpoint map.
