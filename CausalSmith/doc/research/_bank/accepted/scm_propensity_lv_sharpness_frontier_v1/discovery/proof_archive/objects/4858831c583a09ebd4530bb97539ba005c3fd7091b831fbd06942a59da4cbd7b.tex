Write \(\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P)\) for the reverse-support class defined in the conditional support-intersection proposition.  By conditional one-sided completeness and that proposition,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak M_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)
 &=\mathfrak M^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\cap\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P)\\
 &=\mathfrak L^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\cap\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P)\\
 &=\mathfrak L_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P).
\end{aligned}
\]
The one-sided theorem also gives \(P_a\ll Q_a\) and the cap, while intersection with \(\mathcal S_{\mu_X}(\boldsymbol P)\) gives \(Q_a\ll P_a\), all on the finite intersection of the common conull sets.  Conversely, mutual support and the cap make \(R_a=(Q_a-e_aP_a)/(1-e_a)\) a probability kernel by the explicit measurable construction in that theorem, and \(R_a\ll P_a\) follows from \(Q_a\ll P_a\).  Its product-kernel SCM is one simultaneous witness for both arms, proving every assertion.
