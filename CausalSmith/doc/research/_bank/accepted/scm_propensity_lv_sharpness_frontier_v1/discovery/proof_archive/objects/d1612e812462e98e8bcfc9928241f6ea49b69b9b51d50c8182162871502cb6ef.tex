Fix \(0<e<1\) and put \(c=1/e\).  We first record two consequences of the definitions.  If \(P\ll Q\) and \(r=dP/dQ\), then
\[
 \int r\,dQ=1.
\]
Consequently, replacing \(f\) by \(f_b(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\) leaves both relevant quantities unchanged:
\[
 D_{f_b}(P\|Q)=D_f(P\|Q),\qquad
 B_{f_b}(e)=B_f(e)-b\{e(c-1)-(1-e)\}=B_f(e).
\]
Also, convexity on \([0,c]\) gives the chord inequality
\[
 f(t)\le \left(1-\frac{t}{c}\right)f(0)+\frac{t}{c}f(c),\qquad 0\le t\le c. \tag{1}
\]
If \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\), then \(Q\ge eP\), so \(P\ll Q\) and \(r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely; moreover \(Q\ll P\).  Integrating (1) therefore yields
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)
 \le f(0)+\frac{f(c)-f(0)}{c}\int r\,dQ
 =(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e). \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\) for every admissible \(f\).

Suppose first that \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).  By the definition of \(\mathfrak H_c\), there is \(b\in\mathbb R\) such that \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\) is zero on \([0,c]\) and strictly positive on \((c,\infty)\).  Affine invariance gives \(B_g(e)=B_f(e)=0\).  For \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\), mutual absolute continuity supplies \(r=dP/dQ>0\), \(Q\)-almost surely, and
\[
 0\le \int g(r)\,dQ=D_f(P\|Q)\le B_f(e)=0.
\]
Hence \(g(r)=0\), \(Q\)-almost surely, and therefore \(r\le c\).  The finite measure \(Q-eP\) has density \(1-er\ge0\) with respect to \(Q\), has total mass \(1-e\), and is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\) because \(Q\ll P\).  Thus
\[
 R=\frac{Q-eP}{1-e}
\]
is a probability law satisfying \(R\ll P\), and \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\).  This proves \(\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\subseteq\mathfrak L_e(P)\); the reverse inclusion is (2).  Exactness follows for every \(P\).

Conversely, assume exactness for every full-support binary \(P\).  Fix \(p\in(0,1)\), set \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\), and let \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(ep)\).  The representation
\[
 Q_0=eP+(1-e)\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0)
\]
places \(Q_0\) in \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\), because every binary law is absolutely continuous with respect to the full-support law \(P\).  If \(0<q<ep\), then for \(Q_q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\),
\[
 \frac{P(\{1\})}{Q_q(\{1\})}=\frac pq>c,
\]
so \(Q_q\notin\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  Exactness and full support imply
\[
 D_f(P\|Q_q)>B_f(e)\quad(0<q<ep),
 \qquad D_f(P\|Q_0)\le B_f(e).
\]
The binary divergence is continuous in \(q\in(0,1)\), since \(f\) is finite and continuous.  Letting \(q\uparrow ep\) proves
\[
 D_f(P\|Q_0)=B_f(e). \tag{3}
\]
At \(Q_0\), the two likelihood ratios are \(c\) and
\[
 t_p=\frac{1-p}{1-ep},
\]
so (3) reads
\[
 ep f(c)+(1-ep)f(t_p)=B_f(e). \tag{4}
\]
Letting \(p\downarrow0\) in (4), using \(t_p\to1\), continuity, and \(f(1)=0\), gives \(B_f(e)=0\).

Set \(b=-f(0)\) and \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\).  The identity \(B_f(e)=0\), equivalently \(f(c)+(c-1)f(0)=0\), gives
\[
 g(0)=g(c)=g(1)=0. \tag{5}
\]
By affine invariance, (4) and (5) imply \(g(t_p)=0\).  As \(p\) ranges over \((0,1)\), \(t_p\) ranges over \((0,1)\); hence \(g=0\) on \([0,1]\).  Now fix \(y\in(1,c)\) and any \(x\in(0,1)\), and set
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1-x}{y-x}\in(0,1).
\]
There are full-support binary laws whose likelihood ratio \(dP/dQ\) equals \(y\) on the success cell of \(Q\)-mass \(\lambda\) and equals \(x\) on the other cell, because
\[
 \lambda y+(1-\lambda)x=1.
\]
Their likelihood ratio is at most \(c\), so the preceding measure construction places \(Q\) in \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\).  Exactness and \(B_g(e)=0\) give
\[
 0\ge D_g(P\|Q)=\lambda g(y)+(1-\lambda)g(x)=\lambda g(y).
\]
On the other hand, convexity at \(1=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x\) gives \(0=g(1)\le D_g(P\|Q)\).  Therefore \(g(y)=0\).  Together with (5), this proves \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\).

For \(t>c\), write \(c\) as a strict convex combination of \(1\) and \(t\).  Convexity and \(g(1)=g(c)=0\) imply \(g(t)\ge0\).  If \(g(t)=0\) for some \(t>c\), repeat the preceding two-ratio construction with \(y=t\).  It gives full-support binary laws with \(D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e)\), hence \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\), but with likelihood ratio \(t>c\), hence \(Q\notin\mathfrak L_e(P)\), a contradiction.  Thus \(g(t)>0\) for every \(t>c\), which is exactly \(f\in\mathfrak H_c\).

We have proved the equivalence between universal exactness, binary exactness, and membership in \(\mathfrak H_c\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) identifies \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\) with the bow-SCM compatible class \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\), so the equality characterized above is causal rather than merely an equality of abstract probability-law classes.
