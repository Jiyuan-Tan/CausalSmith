Fix \(y\in\mathbb R\), put \(B_y=(-\infty,y]\), and write \(p=P(B_y)=F_P(y)\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\) gives \(0<e<1\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), a law \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\) exactly when
\[
 Q=eP+(1-e)R
 \quad\text{for some probability law }R\ll P.
\]
Consequently, with \(r=R(B_y)\),
\[
 F_Q(y)=Q(B_y)=eP(B_y)+(1-e)R(B_y)=ep+(1-e)r. \tag{1}
\]
Suppose first that \(0<p<1\).  Absolute continuity only implies \(r\in[0,1]\), so (1) gives the inclusion
\[
 \{F_Q(y):Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\}\subseteq[ep,ep+1-e]. \tag{2}
\]
For the reverse inclusion, define the conditional probability laws
\[
 P_{B_y}(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B_y)}{p},
 \qquad
 P_{B_y^c}(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B_y^c)}{1-p}
\]
for measurable \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\).  The divisions are valid because \(0<p<1\).  For every \(r\in[0,1]\), let
\[
 R_r=rP_{B_y}+(1-r)P_{B_y^c}.
\]
Both conditional laws, and hence \(R_r\), are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), while \(R_r(B_y)=r\).  The completeness theorem therefore supplies a bow-ADMG SCM, with latent variable \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), realizing
\[
 Q_r=eP+(1-e)R_r
 \quad\text{and}
 \quad F_{Q_r}(y)=ep+(1-e)r.
\]
As \(r\) ranges over \([0,1]\), the last expression ranges over the whole interval in (2).  In particular, \(R_0=P_{B_y^c}\) and \(R_1=P_{B_y}\) attain the lower and upper endpoints and are supported on the complement of \(B_y\) and on \(B_y\), respectively.

If \(p=0\), then \(P(B_y)=0\), so \(R\ll P\) forces \(R(B_y)=0\).  Equation (1) then forces \(F_Q(y)=0\) for every compatible \(Q\).  If \(p=1\), then \(P(B_y^c)=0\), so \(R(B_y^c)=0\), \(R(B_y)=1\), and (1) forces \(F_Q(y)=1\).  In either boundary case \(R=P\) is admissible, so the unique value is attained by the latent bow construction from the completeness theorem.  By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\),
\[
 [L_e(p),U_e(p)]
 =
 \begin{cases}
 [0,0],&p=0,\\
 [ep,ep+1-e],&0<p<1,\\
 [1,1],&p=1.
 \end{cases}
\]
This proves the asserted equality and attainment.  The argument depends only on the measurable event \(B_y\), not on whether \(P\) has an atom at \(y\).  It is also exactly the mutual-absolute-continuity branch of \(\mathrm{thm:support\text{-}regime\text{-}cdf\text{-}endpoints\text{-}sharp}\), which distinguishes these boundary values from those obtained when singular residual laws are allowed.
