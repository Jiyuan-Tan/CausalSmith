As in the preceding theorem, choose a supporting slope \(b\) at \(1\), put \(g(t)=f(t)-b(t-1)\), and note that \(g\ge0\), \(g(1)=0\), \(D_g=D_f\), and \(B_g(e)=B_f(e)\).  There are exactly two cases.

First suppose that \(f\), equivalently every affine representative of \(f\), is not affine on \([0,c]\).  Then \(g\) is not identically zero there.  If both \(g(0)\) and \(g(c)\) were zero, convexity and nonnegativity would force \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\), a contradiction.  Thus
\[
 B_g(e)=\frac1c g(c)+\left(1-\frac1c\right)g(0)>0. \tag{7}
\]
Fix any \(t>c\).  For \(x\in(0,1)\), define
\[
 \lambda(t,x)=\frac{1-x}{t-x},\quad
 q(t,x)=\lambda(t,x),\quad
 p(t,x)=t\lambda(t,x).
\]
These numbers lie in \((0,1)\), and the likelihood ratios of \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p(t,x))\) relative to \(Q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q(t,x))\) are \(t\) and \(x\).  Hence the pair is illegal because \(t>c\).  Moreover,
\[
 D_g(P\|Q)=\lambda(t,x)g(t)+(1-\lambda(t,x))g(x)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }x\uparrow1. \tag{8}
\]
By (7)--(8), some \(x\) gives strict ball feasibility.  On \((0,1)^2\), the binary divergence
\[
 (p,q)\longmapsto qf(p/q)+(1-q)f((1-p)/(1-q))
\]
is continuous.  The conditions that this expression be strictly below \(B_f(e)\) and that one likelihood ratio exceed \(c\) are open.  A sufficiently small Euclidean neighborhood of the constructed pair is therefore a nonempty open set \(\mathcal O_f\) contained in \(\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\setminus\mathfrak L_e(P)\), with strict divergence inequality.

Second suppose that \(f\) is affine on \([0,c]\).  Taking \(b\) to be that affine slope makes \(g=0\) on \([0,c]\); convexity makes this line supporting, so \(g\ge0\) everywhere.  Since \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), there exists \(d>c\) with \(g(d)=0\).  Convexity and nonnegativity then force \(g=0\) on all of \([0,d]\), and consequently \(B_g(e)=0\).  Choose \(t\in(c,d)\) and \(x\in(0,1)\), and use the same \((p(t,x),q(t,x))\).  In a sufficiently small open neighborhood, the two likelihood ratios remain in \((0,d)\), while the first remains above \(c\).  Throughout that neighborhood,
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)=D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e)=B_f(e),
\]
so the neighborhood is an open illegal subset of the zero-divergence face.  The argument uses only finiteness, continuity, and convexity; no differentiability is invoked, and it applies unchanged to piecewise-linear generators.
