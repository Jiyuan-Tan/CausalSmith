Fix the setup in the statement.  Let \(\boldsymbol Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\), and let the global SCM witness membership.  Choose a countable algebra \(\mathcal C\) generating the Borel \(\sigma\)-field of \(\mathcal Y\).  For \(C\in\mathcal C\), consistency and the conditional versions in the definition give, for each arm and almost every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_a(x,C)
 &=\Pr\{Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}\\
 &=\Pr\{A=a,Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}+\Pr\{A\ne a,Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}\\
 &=e_a(x)P_a(x,C)+\Pr\{A\ne a,Y(a)\in C\mid X=x\}\\
 &\ge e_a(x)P_a(x,C).
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
\]
There are only two arms and countably many \(C\), so the exceptional sets can be united and removed to obtain one measurable \(\mu_X\)-conull set \(S\) on which (1) holds simultaneously.

For fixed \(x\in S\) and arm \(a\), let \(\lambda_{a,x}=Q_a(x,\cdot)+e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)\).  The collection of Borel sets that can be approximated in \(\lambda_{a,x}\)-symmetric-difference measure by members of \(\mathcal C\) is a monotone class containing \(\mathcal C\), hence is the whole Borel \(\sigma\)-field.  Passing (1) through such an approximation proves \(Q_a(x,B)\ge e_a(x)P_a(x,B)\) for every Borel \(B\).  Thus, fixing any probability law \(H\) on \(\mathcal Y\), define
\[
 R_a(x,B)=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{Q_a(x,B)-e_a(x)P_a(x,B)}{1-e_a(x)},&x\in S,\\[6pt]
 H(B),&x\notin S.
 \end{cases} \tag{2}
\]
The denominator is at least \(\kappa>0\).  For \(x\in S\), the numerator is a nonnegative countably additive measure of total mass \(1-e_a(x)\), so (2) is a probability law; off \(S\) it equals \(H\).  For every Borel \(B\), (2) is measurable in \(x\), so \(R_a\) is a Markov kernel.  Hence \(\boldsymbol Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_{\mu_X,\boldsymbol e}(\boldsymbol P)\).

Conversely, suppose residual Markov kernels \(R_0,R_1\) give the mixture identities on one measurable conull set \(S\).  Fix the same fallback law \(H\), and set each of \(P_a,Q_a,R_a\) equal to \(H\) on \(S^c\), leaving it unchanged on \(S\).  These pasted maps are Markov kernels, they are versions of the original conditional kernels, and their mixture identities hold for every \(x\); suppress tildes below.  Define a kernel from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Y^2\) by
\[
 K_x(\{a\}\times dy_0\times dy_1)
 =e_a(x)P_a(x,dy_a)R_{1-a}(x,dy_{1-a}),\qquad a=0,1. \tag{3}
\]
On measurable rectangles the right side is measurable in \(x\); a monotone-class argument therefore shows that \(K\) is a Markov kernel.  Its total mass is \(e_0(x)+e_1(x)=1\).  Integrating \(K_x\) against \(\mu_X(dx)\) gives one joint law of \((X,A,Y(0),Y(1))\).  Take
\[
 U=(X,A,Y(0),Y(1)),
\]
let the structural equations for \(X\) and \(A\) return their corresponding coordinates, and let the outcome equation return \(Y(A)\).  Coordinate projections are measurable, and the binary selector \((a,y_0,y_1)\mapsto y_a\) is measurable.  The stipulated conditional architecture permits this common latent parent of \(X,A,Y\), together with the observed arrows \(X\to A\), \(X\to Y\), and \(A\to Y\).  Thus the construction is compatible with that architecture and satisfies consistency.

From (3),
\[
 \Pr(A=a,Y\in B\mid X=x)=e_a(x)P_a(x,B),
\]
so overlap permits division by \(e_a(x)\) and yields \(\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a,X=x)=P_a(x,\cdot)\).  Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid X=x)
 &=e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)+e_{1-a}(x)R_a(x,\cdot)\\
 &=e_a(x)P_a(x,\cdot)+(1-e_a(x))R_a(x,\cdot)=Q_a(x,\cdot).
\end{aligned}\tag{4}
\]
This proves the global simultaneous realization and equality of the two classes.

Finally, at any \(x\in S\), the measure inequality in the theorem implies \(P_a(x,\cdot)\ll Q_a(x,\cdot)\).  If \(r_{a,x}=dP_a(x,\cdot)/dQ_a(x,\cdot)\), then \(Q_a-e_aP_a\ge0\) is equivalent to \(1-e_a(x)r_{a,x}\ge0\), \(Q_a(x,\cdot)\)-almost surely.  This is exactly the displayed likelihood-ratio cap; the converse follows by reversing the same density calculation.  No density has been selected jointly in \(x\).
