Fix the objects in the theorem.  If \(Q\in\mathfrak M_e(P)\), consistency and the observed-arm restriction give
\[
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)=P.
\]
Because \(0<e<1\), the conditional law
\[
 R=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A\ne a)
\]
is a probability law.  For every measurable \(B\subseteq\mathcal Y\), the law of total probability gives
\[
 Q(B)=\Pr\{Y(a)\in B\}=eP(B)+(1-e)R(B). \tag{1}
\]
The mutual-absolute-continuity member property says \(P\sim Q\), in particular \(Q\ll P\).  If \(P(B)=0\), then \(Q(B)=0\), and (1), nonnegativity, and \(1-e>0\) imply \(R(B)=0\).  Hence \(R\ll P\), proving \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak L_e(P)\).

Conversely, suppose
\[
 Q=eP+(1-e)R,\qquad R\ll P. \tag{2}
\]
On a standard Borel probability space, draw \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\); conditionally on \(A=a\), draw \(Y(a)\) with law \(P\), and conditionally on \(A\ne a\), draw \(Y(a)\) with law \(R\).  Draw \(Y(1-a)\), conditionally independently given \(A\), from any fixed probability law, for example \(P\).  Put \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), let the treatment structural equation return the \(A\)-coordinate of \(U\), and let the outcome structural equation return \(Y(A)\).  These measurable structural equations form a nonparametric latent-variable SCM Markov to the bow ADMG: the common latent input permits \(A\leftrightarrow Y\), and the selector equation uses the arrow \(A\to Y\).  The construction satisfies consistency, has propensity \(e\), and has observed arm law \(P\).  Equation (2) is exactly its law of \(Y(a)\).

Moreover, (2) implies \(Q\ge eP\), so \(P\ll Q\), while \(R\ll P\) implies \(Q\ll P\).  Thus \(P\sim Q\), and the constructed SCM satisfies the mutual-absolute-continuity member property.  This proves \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak M_e(P)\), hence equality.

It remains to prove the likelihood-ratio characterization.  Under (2), let \(r=dP/dQ\), which exists because \(P\ll Q\).  The measure inequality \(Q-eP\ge0\) is equivalent to
\[
 1-er\ge0\qquad Q\text{-almost surely},
\]
and therefore \(r\le1/e\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  We already proved \(Q\ll P\), so \(Q\sim P\).  Conversely, suppose \(Q\sim P\) and \(r=dP/dQ\le1/e\), \(Q\)-almost surely.  Define
\[
 R(B)=\frac{Q(B)-eP(B)}{1-e}
     =\int_B\frac{1-er}{1-e}\,dQ.
\]
The displayed density is nonnegative, and its integral is
\[
 \frac{1-e\int r\,dQ}{1-e}=1,
\]
so \(R\) is a probability law and (2) holds.  Since \(Q\ll P\), every \(P\)-null set is also \(Q\)-null and hence \(R\)-null, so \(R\ll P\).  The preceding construction realizes this \(R\), completing every asserted equivalence and the realization claim.
