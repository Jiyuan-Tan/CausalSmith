Put \(c=1/e\) and let
\[
 \ell(t)=\left(1-\frac tc\right)f(0)+\frac tc f(c),
 \qquad \ell(1)=B_f(e).
\]
If \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\), convexity implies the strict chord inequality \(f(x)<\ell(x)\) at every \(x\in(0,c)\): otherwise the nonnegative concave function \(\ell-f\), zero at both endpoints, would have an interior zero and concavity would force it to vanish everywhere.  Choose \(x\in(0,1)\), set
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1-x}{c-x},\qquad q_0=\lambda,qquad p_0=c\lambda,
\]
and take \(P_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0)\), \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_0)\).  Their likelihood ratios are \(c,x\), with respective \(Q_0\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\), and hence
\[
 D_f(P_0\|Q_0)<\lambda\ell(c)+(1-\lambda)\ell(x)=\ell(1)=B_f(e). \tag{18}
\]
Choose \(q_1<q_0=ep_0\) sufficiently close that the strict inequality in (18) persists.  By continuity, there is a nonempty open neighborhood \(\mathcal O_f\subset(0,1)^2\) of \((p_0,q_1)\) on which simultaneously \(D_f(P\|Q)<B_f(e)\) and \(p/q>c\).  Every pair has full support, so the mutual and one-sided divergence-ball support requirements both hold, while the violated likelihood-ratio cap excludes the pair from both mixture classes.

If instead \(f\) is affine on \([0,c]\), normalize it by an affine term to obtain a convex \(g\) that vanishes on \([0,c]\).  Convexity gives \(g\ge0\) above \(c\).  Since \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), there exists \(d>c\) with \(g(d)=0\), and convexity then gives \(g=0\) throughout \([0,d]\).  Choose \(x\in(0,1)\), \(y\in(c,d)\), and \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\).  The full-support binary pair with likelihood ratios \(y,x\) and \(Q\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\) satisfies \(D_f=D_g=0=B_f(e)\) and violates the cap.  A sufficiently small open neighborhood keeps both likelihood ratios in \((0,d)\) and the first above \(c\); throughout it the divergence remains zero and both mixture classes remain violated.  On full-support binary alphabets every residual law is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), so the mutual and one-sided mixture classes coincide there.  This proves the simultaneous dichotomy, including its nondifferentiable and piecewise-linear cases.  It also supplies the stable witness promised by the failure direction of \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}generator\text{-}iff\text{-}frontier}\).
