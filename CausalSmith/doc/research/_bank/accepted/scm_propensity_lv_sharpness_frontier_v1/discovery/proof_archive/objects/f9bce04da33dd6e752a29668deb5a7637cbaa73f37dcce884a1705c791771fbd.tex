We prove the proposed, total-variation-local version of the statement.  The added locality condition is necessary; a counterexample to the frozen formulation is given at the end.

For probability measures \(\mu,\nu\) on a common measurable space, write \(\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\sup_E|\mu(E)-\nu(E)|\), where the supremum is over measurable events.  Let \(\mathbb P_0\) be the single-observation law at the lower boundary, let \(\mathbb P_n\) be the single-observation law of the stated alternative, and denote their \(n\)-observation laws by \(\mathbb P_0^{(n)}\) and \(\mathbb P_n^{(n)}\).  The proposed locality hypothesis is
\[
 t_n:=\sup_E\left|\mathbb P_0^{(n)}(E)-\mathbb P_n^{(n)}(E)\right|=o(1),
\]
This definition immediately gives, for every measurable \(E\),
\[
 \mathbb P_0^{(n)}(E)\geq \mathbb P_n^{(n)}(E)-t_n. \tag{1}
\]

Write \(C_n^L(y_0)\) for the lower-endpoint coordinate projection of the rectangular band at \(y_0\).  Uniform asymptotic simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) means that there is a deterministic sequence \(r_n\downarrow0\) such that the simultaneous coverage probability under every law in the stated local class is at least \(1-\alpha-r_n\).  In particular, define
\[
 E_{0,n}:=\{1\in C_n^L(y_0)\},
 \qquad
 E_{1,n}:=\{e(1-\delta_n)\in C_n^L(y_0)\}.
\]
At the boundary, \(F_P(y_0)=1\), so \(L_e(F_P(y_0))=L_e(1)=1\) by \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\).  At the alternative, \(\delta_n>0\) and \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n<1\), hence the same definition gives
\[
 L_e(F_{P,n}(y_0))=e(1-\delta_n).
\]
These maps are the causal identified-set endpoints, rather than merely named regression functionals.  Indeed, put \(B=({-}\infty,y_0]\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), every compatible interventional law has the form \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\), and hence
\[
 Q(B)=eP(B)+(1-e)R(B). \tag{2}
\]
If \(P(B)=1\), absolute continuity forces \(R(B)=1\), so (2) gives the unique value \(1\).  For the alternatives, \(n\delta_n\to0\) implies \(0<\delta_n<1\) eventually.  Then \(0<P_n(B)<1\), and the choices \(R=P_n(\,\cdot\mid B^c)\) and \(R=P_n(\,\cdot\mid B)\) are well defined, are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P_n\), and attain \(R(B)=0\) and \(R(B)=1\), respectively.  Thus (2) verifies that the lower endpoint is exactly \(e(1-\delta_n)\).
Simultaneous coverage implies coordinatewise coverage at \(y_0\), and therefore
\[
 \mathbb P_0^{(n)}(E_{0,n})\geq1-\alpha-r_n,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_n^{(n)}(E_{1,n})\geq1-\alpha-r_n. \tag{3}
\]
Applying (1) to \(E_{1,n}\), then using the union bound with (3), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P_0^{(n)}(E_{0,n}\cap E_{1,n})
 &\geq \mathbb P_0^{(n)}(E_{0,n})+\mathbb P_0^{(n)}(E_{1,n})-1\\
 &\geq 1-2\alpha-2r_n-t_n
  =1-2\alpha-o(1). \tag{4}
 \end{aligned}
\]
On \(E_{0,n}\cap E_{1,n}\), the interval \(C_n^L(y_0)\) contains both \(1\) and \(e(1-\delta_n)\).  Rectangularity therefore implies
\[
 \operatorname{diam} C_n^L(y_0)
 \geq 1-e(1-\delta_n)
 =1-e+e\delta_n
 \geq1-e. \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) prove the asserted lower-coordinate conclusion, in fact with the non-random diameter threshold \(1-e\).

For the upper-coordinate assertion, let the boundary law satisfy \(F_P(y_0)=0\), and let the alternative satisfy \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=\delta_n>0\).  By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\),
\[
 U_e(0)=0,
 \qquad
 U_e(\delta_n)=1-e+e\delta_n.
\]
Formula (2) also verifies these as the exact causal endpoints: when \(P(B)=0\), absolute continuity forces \(R(B)=0\), while for \(0<P_n(B)=\delta_n<1\), the conditional residual laws on \(B\) and \(B^c\) attain both extreme values of \(R(B)\).  Repeating (1), (3), and (4) with the events that the upper-coordinate projection \(C_n^U(y_0)\) contains these respective values gives
\[
 \mathbb P_0^{(n)}\left\{\operatorname{diam}C_n^U(y_0)\geq1-e+e\delta_n\right\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-o(1),
\]
which proves the analogue.

The total-variation-local alternatives required above exist inside the bow-SCM model.  For the lower face, choose any boundary arm law \(P_0\) supported on \(({-}\infty,y_0]\), choose a probability law \(S\) supported on \((y_0,\infty)\), and set
\[
 P_n=(1-\delta_n)P_0+\delta_n S.
\]
Then \(F_{P_n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n\) and \(\|P_n-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\delta_n\).  Keep the propensity equal to \(e\) and keep the conditional law in the other treatment arm fixed.  The resulting single-observation observed-data laws obey
\[
 \|\mathbb P_n-\mathbb P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq e\delta_n.
\]
For probability laws \(\mu,\nu\), the telescoping identity
\[
 \mu^{\otimes n}-\nu^{\otimes n}
 =\sum_{j=1}^n
 \mu^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes(\mu-\nu)\otimes\nu^{\otimes(n-j)}
\]
and the triangle inequality for total variation give
\[
 \|\mu^{\otimes n}-\nu^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq n\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Thus \(t_n\leq ne\delta_n=o(1)\).  To realize these observed laws explicitly, draw \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), draw \(Y(a)\) independently of \(A\) with law \(P_n\), fix the law of \(Y(1-a)\) independently of \(n\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  With \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), this is the latent construction asserted in \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\).  Its interventional law is \(Q=P_n=eP_n+(1-e)P_n\), so \(R=P_n\ll P_n\), and \(\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=P_n=\mathcal L(Y(a))\); hence positivity, consistency, and the maintained mutual-absolute-continuity condition all hold.  For the upper face, interchange the two half-lines: take \(P_0\) supported on \((y_0,\infty)\) and \(S\) supported on \(({-}\infty,y_0]\).  This also supplies the requested binary stress test by taking \(y_0=0\), \(P_0\) a point mass at \(0\), and \(S\) a point mass at \(1\) for the lower face; the two endpoint values in (5) are then exact.

Because \(\alpha<1/2\) and \(1-e>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), (4)--(5) rule out \(\operatorname{diam}C_n^L(y_0)=o_{\mathbb P_0}(1)\), and the upper-face argument rules out the corresponding upper-coordinate convergence.  Any band built from the handle \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{dir}}\) that is uniformly honest over the tie-inclusive class is one of the bands covered by this argument, so it cannot be both uniformly honest and shrinking at the contact.  The finite-sample rectangle \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}\) remains an honest fallback by \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\).  The extension of \(L_e,U_e\) to \(e=0\) in \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) is used only to make that clipped inference construction algebraically well typed; the impossibility argument itself uses the maintained causal positivity condition \(0<e<1\).

Finally, the added total-variation condition cannot be inferred merely from \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n\) and \(n\delta_n\to0\).  In a three-point counterexample with \(y_0=0\), let the boundary arm law be the point mass at \(0\), but let the alternative arm law put probabilities \(1-\delta_n\) and \(\delta_n\) on \(-1\) and \(1\), respectively.  The displayed CDF condition holds, yet the boundary and alternative arm supports are disjoint.  On the two-law class, a band can inspect the first observed unit in arm \(a\): an outcome \(0\) selects the boundary endpoint function and a nonzero outcome selects the alternative endpoint function; if no arm-\(a\) unit is observed, it reports the full rectangle.  This band has simultaneous coverage one under both laws, while under the boundary law it is a singleton with probability \(1-(1-e)^n\to1\).  Taking \(Q=P\) and invoking \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\) again verifies that both laws are legal bow-SCM laws.  Hence the frozen statement without an observed-experiment locality condition is false, whereas the proposed statement proved above is valid.
