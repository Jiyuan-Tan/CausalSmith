Put \(c=1/e\) and let
\[
 \ell(t)=\left(1-\frac tc\right)f(0)+\frac tc f(c),
 \qquad \ell(1)=B_f(e).
\]
If \(f\) is not affine on \([0,c]\), the equality argument for a convex function and its endpoint chord shows that \(f(x)<\ell(x)\) at every \(x\in(0,c)\).  Choose \(x\in(0,1)\), set
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1-x}{c-x},\qquad q_0=\lambda,\qquad p_0=c\lambda,
\]
and take \(P_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0)\), \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_0)\).  Their likelihood ratios are \(c,x\) with \(Q_0\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\), so
\[
 D_f(P_0\|Q_0)
 <\lambda\ell(c)+(1-\lambda)\ell(x)
 =\ell(1)=B_f(e). \tag{45}
\]
Choose \(q_1<q_0=ep_0\) sufficiently close that (45) remains strict.  Continuity supplies a nonempty open neighborhood \(\mathcal O_f\subset(0,1)^2\) on which both \(D_f(P\|Q)<B_f(e)\) and \(p/q>c\) hold.  Full support makes both divergence-ball support requirements true, while the cap violation excludes every pair from both mixture classes.

If instead \(f\) is affine on \([0,c]\), subtract its affine representative to obtain a convex \(g\) that vanishes on \([0,c]\).  Convexity gives \(g\ge0\) above \(c\).  Since \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\), some \(d>c\) satisfies \(g(d)=0\), and convexity then gives \(g=0\) throughout \([0,d]\).  Choose \(x\in(0,1)\), \(y\in(c,d)\), and \(\lambda=(1-x)/(y-x)\).  The full-support binary pair with likelihood ratios \(y,x\) and \(Q\)-weights \(\lambda,1-\lambda\) has
\[
 D_f(P\|Q)=D_g(P\|Q)=0=B_g(e)=B_f(e)
\]
and violates the cap.  A sufficiently small open neighborhood keeps the first likelihood ratio in \((c,d)\) and the second in \((0,d)\); throughout it the divergence remains zero and both mixture caps remain violated.  On full-support binary alphabets, \(Q\ll P\) is automatic and one-sided membership already gives \(P\ll Q\), so the one-sided and mutual support regimes coincide.  This proves the simultaneous dichotomy, including all nondifferentiable and piecewise-linear cases.
