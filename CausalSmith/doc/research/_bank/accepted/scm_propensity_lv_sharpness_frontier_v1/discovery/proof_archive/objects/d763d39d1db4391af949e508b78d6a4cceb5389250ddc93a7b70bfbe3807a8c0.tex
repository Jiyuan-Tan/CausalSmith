Let \(Z_i=\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\}\), so that the independent-sampling assumption gives independent variables with \(0\le Z_i\le1\) and \(E[Z_i]=e\).  Put
\[
 E_e=\{e\in I_e\}=\{|\widehat e_n-e|\le\delta_e\}.
\]
The equality of the two events is understood after clipping to \([0,1]\): because \(e\in[0,1]\), membership in the un-clipped interval implies membership in \(I_e\).  Since \(n\ge1\) for the sampling construction and \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\delta_e>0\).  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}bounded\text{-}mean}\) with \(m=n\) and \(t=\delta_e\) yields
\[
 \Pr(E_e^c)
 \le 2\exp(-2n\delta_e^2)
 =2\exp\{-\log(4/\alpha)\}
 =\alpha/2. \tag{1}
\]

Let \(\mathcal A_n=\sigma(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\).  Conditional on \(\mathcal A_n\), on the event \(n_a=m>0\), the variables \(Y_i\) with \(A_i=a\) are independent and each has conditional law \(P=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)\).  This follows directly from the factorization of the joint law of the independent pairs \((A_i,Y_i)\) under \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\).  Thus \(\mathrm{lem:dkw\text{-}massart\text{-}cdf\text{-}band}\), conditionally with sample size \(m\) and \(t=\delta_F\), gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
   \left.\sup_{y\in\mathbb R}
   |\widehat F_{P,n}(y)-F_P(y)|>\delta_F
   \ \right|\mathcal A_n
 \right)
 \le 2\exp(-2m\delta_F^2)
 =\alpha/2. \tag{2}
\]
Define \(E_F\) to be the complementary event in (2) when \(n_a>0\), and define \(E_F\) to hold when \(n_a=0\).  Taking conditional expectations in (2) gives \(\Pr(E_F^c)\le\alpha/2\).  Consequently, the union bound and (1) imply
\[
 \Pr(E_e\cap E_F)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{3}
\]

Suppose first that \(n_a>0\).  On \(E_e\cap E_F\), simultaneously for every \(y\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 (e,F_P(y))\in I_e\times I_F(y). \tag{4}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}band}\), \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}(y)\) is the coordinatewise rectangular hull of the image of this rectangle under
\[
 (\eta,p)\longmapsto (L_\eta(p),U_\eta(p)).
\]
Substituting the particular point in (4) therefore gives, for every \(y\),
\[
 (L_e(F_P(y)),U_e(F_P(y)))
 \in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}(y). \tag{5}
\]
This argument is set inclusion and uses neither continuity nor differentiability of the piecewise maps in \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\); hence it also covers atoms and the contacts \(F_P(y)=0\) and \(F_P(y)=1\).  If \(n_a=0\), then \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}band}\) sets the band equal to \([0,1]^2\), and (5) holds because \(L_e(p),U_e(p)\in[0,1]\).  Combining (3)--(5) proves the asserted probability bound.  Finally, \(\mathrm{thm:cdf\text{-}endpoints\text{-}sharp}\) identifies the two coordinates in (5) as the exact mutual-absolute-continuity bow-SCM endpoint functionals, rather than merely as observed-law transformations.
