By \(\mathrm{ass{:}positivity}\), \(0<e<1\). Since \(p\in(0,1)\), the Bernoulli law \(P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) has full support on \(\{0,1\}\), and \(q_0=ep\) belongs to \((0,1)\). The convention \(0\log 0=0\) gives \(f_{\mathrm{KL}}(0)=0\), whereas
\[
f_{\mathrm{KL}}(1/e)=\frac{1}{e}\log(1/e).
\]
Consequently, by the definition of \(B_f(e)\),
\[
B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e)
=e f_{\mathrm{KL}}(1/e)+(1-e)f_{\mathrm{KL}}(0)
=\log(1/e)=-\log e.
\]

Let \(Q_0=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_0)\). Both \(P\) and \(Q_0\) have full support, so \(P\sim Q_0\), in particular \(P\ll Q_0\), as required in the definition of \(D_f(P\|Q)\). On the atoms \(1\) and \(0\), respectively, the likelihood ratio \(dP/dQ_0\) is \(p/q_0\) and \((1-p)/(1-q_0)\). Therefore that definition and \(q_0=ep\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(P\|Q_0)
&=q_0\frac{p}{q_0}\log\!\left(\frac{p}{q_0}\right)
 +(1-q_0)\frac{1-p}{1-q_0}
   \log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-q_0}\right)\\
&=p\log(1/e)+(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-ep}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The gap from the radius can be computed exactly:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e)-D_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(P\|Q_0)
&=(1-p)\left\{\log(1/e)
-\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-ep}\right)\right\}\\
&=(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-ep}{e(1-p)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(e(1-p)>0\), and
\[
(1-ep)-e(1-p)=1-e>0.
\]
Thus \((1-ep)/(e(1-p))>1\). Since the logarithm is strictly increasing and \(1-p>0\), the displayed gap is strictly positive. This proves
\[
D_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(P\|Q_0)<B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e).
\]

For \(q\in(0,1)\), define
\[
K_p(q)=p\log\!\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)
+(1-p)\log\!\left(\frac{1-p}{1-q}\right).
\]
The logarithm is continuous on \((0,\infty)\), so \(K_p\) is continuous on \((0,1)\). Put
\[
s=B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e)-K_p(q_0)>0.
\]
Continuity at \(q_0\) gives \(\delta>0\) such that, whenever \(q\in(0,1)\) and \(|q-q_0|<\delta\),
\[
|K_p(q)-K_p(q_0)|<s/2.
\]
Therefore every
\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\{\delta,ep\},
\qquad q=ep-\varepsilon,
\]
satisfies \(q\in(0,1)\) and
\[
D_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}\!\left(P\middle\|\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\right)
=K_p(q)<K_p(q_0)+\frac{s}{2}<B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e).
\]
The two Bernoulli laws have full support and hence are mutually absolutely continuous. By \(\mathrm{def{:}jk\text{-}ball}\),
\[
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\in\mathfrak B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}},e}(P).
\]

It remains to prove exclusion from the compatible mixture class. If a probability law \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\), then \(\mathrm{def{:}mixture\text{-}class}\) supplies a probability law \(R\ll P\) such that
\[
Q=eP+(1-e)R.
\]
Evaluating this equality on the success atom \(\{1\}\) gives
\[
Q(\{1\})=ep+(1-e)R(\{1\})\ge ep,
\]
because \(1-e>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}positivity}\) and \(R(\{1\})\ge0\) because \(R\) is a probability law. Our candidate instead has
\[
Q(\{1\})=q=ep-\varepsilon<ep,
\]
or, equivalently,
\[
\frac{p}{q}>\frac{1}{e},
\]
where division is legitimate because \(e>0\), \(p>0\), and \(q>0\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(ep-\varepsilon)
\in\mathfrak B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}},e}(P)
\setminus\mathfrak L_e(P).
\]

Finally, fix one \(\varepsilon\) in the preceding range and write \(q=ep-\varepsilon\). On the open square \(\Omega=(0,1)^2\), define
\[
\Phi(p',q')=p'\log\!\left(\frac{p'}{q'}\right)
+(1-p')\log\!\left(\frac{1-p'}{1-q'}\right).
\]
The maps
\[
(p',q')\longmapsto B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e)-\Phi(p',q')
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(p',q')\longmapsto ep'-q'
\]
are continuous on \(\Omega\), and both are strictly positive at \((p,q)\). Their positive inverse images therefore have an open intersection \(\mathcal U\subseteq\Omega\) containing \((p,q)\). For every \((p',q')\in\mathcal U\), the full-support laws \(P'=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p')\) and \(Q'=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q')\) obey
\[
D_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(P'\|Q')<B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}}}(e)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
q'<ep'.
\]
The first strict inequality and \(P'\sim Q'\) put \(Q'\) in \(\mathfrak B_{f_{\mathrm{KL}},e}(P')\). The event calculation just given, with \(p',q'\) in place of \(p,q\), excludes \(Q'\) from \(\mathfrak L_e(P')\). Thus the illegal certificate persists throughout the binary open neighborhood \(\mathcal U\), as claimed.
