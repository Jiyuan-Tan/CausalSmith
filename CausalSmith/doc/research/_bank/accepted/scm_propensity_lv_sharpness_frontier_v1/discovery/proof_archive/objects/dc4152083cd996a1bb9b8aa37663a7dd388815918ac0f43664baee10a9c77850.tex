Fix a standard Borel \(\mathcal Y\), an arm \(a\), a probability law \(P\), and \(0<e<1\).  If \(Q\in\mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)\), consistency gives
\[
 \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)=P.
\]
Since \(1-e>0\), \(R=\mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A\ne a)\) is a probability law, and total probability yields, for every measurable \(B\),
\[
 Q(B)=eP(B)+(1-e)R(B). \tag{36}
\]
Thus \(Q\in\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P)\).

Conversely, suppose (36) holds for an arbitrary probability law \(R\).  Draw \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), draw \(Y(a)\) conditionally from \(P\) on \(\{A=a\}\) and from \(R\) on \(\{A\ne a\}\), draw \(Y(1-a)\) conditionally from any fixed law, put \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  Standard Borelness ensures that these probability kernels can be realized measurably.  The common latent input and selector equation give a nonparametric SCM Markov to the bow ADMG.  It satisfies consistency, has the required propensity and observed arm law, and realizes \(Q\) by (36).  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak M^{\to}_e(P)=\mathfrak L^{\to}_e(P). \tag{37}
\]

Under (36), \(Q\ge eP\), whence \(P\ll Q\).  With \(r=dP/dQ\), the measure inequality is equivalent to
\[
 1-er\ge0\quad Q\text{-almost surely},
\]
so \(r\le1/e\).  Conversely, if \(P\ll Q\) and \(r\le1/e\), define
\[
 R(B)=\frac{Q(B)-eP(B)}{1-e}
     =\int_B\frac{1-er}{1-e}\,dQ.
\]
The density is nonnegative and integrates to one, so \(R\) is a probability law and (36) follows.  Combining this equivalence with (37) proves the likelihood-ratio description and the asserted latent realization.
