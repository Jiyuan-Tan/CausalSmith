Fix \(y\in\mathbb R\), let \(B_y=(-\infty,y]\), and put \(p=P(B_y)=F_P(y)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), a law \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M_e(P)\) exactly when
\[
 Q=eP+(1-e)R\quad\text{for a probability law }R\ll P.
\]
Writing \(r=R(B_y)\), one therefore has
\[
 F_Q(y)=Q(B_y)=ep+(1-e)r. \tag{15}
\]

If \(0<p<1\), absolute continuity permits \(r\in[0,1]\), so (15) gives the inclusion in \([ep,ep+1-e]\).  Conversely, define
\[
 P_{B_y}(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B_y)}p,
 \qquad
 P_{B_y^c}(C)=\frac{P(C\cap B_y^c)}{1-p}.
\]
The denominators are positive.  Both laws are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\), and for every \(r\in[0,1]\),
\[
 R_r=rP_{B_y}+(1-r)P_{B_y^c}
\]
satisfies \(R_r\ll P\) and \(R_r(B_y)=r\).  The completeness theorem supplies a bow-ADMG SCM attaining \(Q_r=eP+(1-e)R_r\), and (15) ranges over the entire interval.  In particular \(R_0=P_{B_y^c}\) and \(R_1=P_{B_y}\) attain the two endpoints with the stated supports.

If \(p=0\), then \(P(B_y)=0\), so \(R\ll P\) forces \(R(B_y)=0\), and (15) is uniquely zero.  If \(p=1\), then \(P(B_y^c)=0\), so \(R(B_y)=1\), and (15) is uniquely one.  In either boundary case, \(R=P\) is admissible and attains the unique value through the same latent construction.  By \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\), these three cases are exactly
\[
 [L_e(p),U_e(p)]=
 \begin{cases}
 [0,0],&p=0,\\
 [ep,ep+1-e],&0<p<1,\\
 [1,1],&p=1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The proof is eventwise and never assumes that \(P\) is atomless, so an atom at \(y\) causes no change.  The attaining SCMs satisfy every maintained support condition by the completeness theorem, and the conclusion agrees with the mutual-support branch of \(\mathrm{thm:support\text{-}regime\text{-}cdf\text{-}endpoints\text{-}sharp}\).
