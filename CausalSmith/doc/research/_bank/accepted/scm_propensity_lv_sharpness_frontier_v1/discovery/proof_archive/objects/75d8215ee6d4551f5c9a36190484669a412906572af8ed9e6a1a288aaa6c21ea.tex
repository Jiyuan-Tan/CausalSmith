Fix \(0<e<1\), put \(c=1/e\), and let \(f\) be admissible.  If \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\) with \(R\ll P\), then \(Q\sim P\), and \(r=dP/dQ\) satisfies \(0<r\le c\), \(Q\)-almost surely, and \(\int r\,dQ=1\).  Convexity on \([0,c]\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_f(P\|Q)
 &\le\int\left\{\left(1-\frac rc\right)f(0)+\frac rc f(c)\right\}dQ\\
 &=(1-e)f(0)+ef(c)=B_f(e).
 \end{aligned} \tag{16}
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\subseteq\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\).  The definition of the lawful correction and bow-mixture completeness now give
\[
 \mathfrak B^{\mathrm{law}}_{f,e}(P)
 =\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\cap\mathfrak L_e(P)
 =\mathfrak L_e(P)=\mathfrak M_e(P). \tag{17}
\]

Let \(h\in L^\infty(P)\), and write
\[
 m=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_P h,\qquad
 M=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_P h.
\]
For every lawful \(Q=eP+(1-e)R\), \(R\ll P\) gives \(m\le E_R[h]\le M\), and hence
\[
 eE_P[h]+(1-e)m\le E_Q[h]\le eE_P[h]+(1-e)M. \tag{18}
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), the definitions of essential infimum and supremum give positive-probability sets
\[
 C^-_\eta=\{h<m+\eta\},
 \qquad
 C^+_\eta=\{h>M-\eta\}.
\]
The conditional laws \(R^-_\eta=P(\,\cdot\mid C^-_\eta)\) and \(R^+_\eta=P(\,\cdot\mid C^+_\eta)\) are absolutely continuous with respect to \(P\).  Substitution into (18), followed by \(\eta\downarrow0\), proves
\[
 \underline\theta_h=eE_P[h]+(1-e)m,
 \qquad
 \overline\theta_h=eE_P[h]+(1-e)M. \tag{19}
\]

Now restrict a finite alphabet to \(\operatorname{supp}(P)\), so every retained \(p_j\) is positive.  A probability vector \(q=(q_j)_j\) belongs to \(\mathfrak L_e(P)\) if and only if
\[
 q_j\ge ep_j\quad\text{for every }j. \tag{20}
\]
The forward implication follows from \(q_j=ep_j+(1-e)r_j\).  Conversely, (20) makes
\[
 r_j=\frac{q_j-ep_j}{1-e}
\]
nonnegative, and summing gives \(\sum_jr_j=1\).  Since all retained \(p_j>0\), the resulting \(R\) also satisfies \(R\ll P\).  Thus (20) is exact.  Inequality (16) shows that the divergence constraint is redundant once the caps are imposed.  Consequently the capped divergence program has exactly the compatible mutual-support feasible set.  This set is a closed subset of the finite probability simplex and hence compact.  Restricting a feasible set weakly lowers a linear maximum and weakly raises a linear minimum.  If a relaxed optimizer is unique and violates (20), equality of the restricted and relaxed optimal values would make an optimizer over the nonempty compact capped set a second relaxed optimizer, a contradiction.  The tightening is therefore strict in the stated circumstance.  This is the mutual-support specialization of the finite-alphabet construction in \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}sided\text{-}query\text{-}legality\text{-}correction}\).

Finally suppose \(f\notin\mathfrak H_c\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:open\text{-}illegal\text{-}dichotomy}\), there is a nonempty open binary region on which \(q<ep\) while \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{f,e}(P)\).  Shrink it to an open rectangle.  Its projection contains a nonempty open interval of observational probabilities \(p\), and for each such \(p\) some divergence-feasible \(q<ep\) remains.  For the bounded success indicator \(h(z)=\mathbf 1\{z=1\}\), full support gives \(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_P h=0\), so (19) makes the lawful lower endpoint \(ep\), whereas the relaxed lower value is at most \(q<ep\).  This proves the strict legality gap on an open region of observational laws.
