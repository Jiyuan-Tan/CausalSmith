%%% FIELD proof-tie-inclusive-band-impossibility
Write the rectangular band at \(y_0\) as \(I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\), where the two factors are intervals for the lower and upper endpoint, respectively.  Uniform asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) means that there is a deterministic sequence \(\rho_n\downarrow0\) such that, for every sufficiently large \(n\), every law in the stated local class has simultaneous coverage probability at least \(1-\alpha-\rho_n\).  The product sampling laws used below follow from \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\).

First consider a boundary law \(\nu_0\) with \(F_P(y_0)=1\) and an alternative \(\nu_n\) with \(F_{P,n}(y_0)=1-\delta_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and put \(d_n=\lVert\nu_0^{\otimes n}-\nu_n^{\otimes n}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=o(1)\).  Because \(n\delta_n\to0\) and \(\delta_n>0\), there is an index \(N\) such that \(0<\delta_n<1\) for every \(n\ge N\).  Thus the piecewise formulas in \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\) give, on the common eventual tail,
\[
  L_e(1)=1,
  \qquad
  L_e(1-\delta_n)=e(1-\delta_n).
  \tag{1}
\]
Define the two endpoint pairs at \(y_0\) and their band-membership events by
\[
  z_{0n}=\bigl(L_e(1),U_e(1)\bigr),
  \qquad
  z_{1n}=\bigl(L_e(1-\delta_n),U_e(1-\delta_n)\bigr),
\]
\[
  C_{0n}=\{z_{0n}\in I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\},
  \qquad
  C_{1n}=\{z_{1n}\in I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\}.
\]
The supplied band-membership measurability premise makes \(C_{0n}\) and \(C_{1n}\) measurable.  Simultaneous coverage implies the corresponding coordinatewise coverage, so
\[
  \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n,
  \qquad
  \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})\ge1-\alpha-\rho_n.
  \tag{2}
\]
The definition of total variation, applied to the same measurable event \(C_{1n}\), yields
\[
  \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})
  \ge \Pr_{\nu_n^{\otimes n}}(C_{1n})-d_n
  \ge1-\alpha-\rho_n-d_n.
  \tag{3}
\]
The union bound under \(\nu_0^{\otimes n}\) now gives
\[
  \Pr_{\nu_0^{\otimes n}}(C_{0n}\cap C_{1n})
  \ge1-2\alpha-2\rho_n-d_n
  =1-2\alpha-o(1).
  \tag{4}
\]
On this intersection, the interval \(I_n^L(y_0)\) contains both numbers in (1), and therefore
\[
  \operatorname{diam} I_n^L(y_0)
  \ge 1-e(1-\delta_n)
  =1-e+e\delta_n
  \ge1-e.
  \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) prove the asserted lower-projection conclusion.

For the lower-support contact, let \(\nu_0\) instead satisfy \(F_{P_{\nu_0}}(y_0)=0\), and let the alternatives satisfy \(F_{P_{\nu_n}}(y_0)=\delta_n\) on the same eventual tail.  The same argument uses the other discontinuous face of \(\mathrm{def:cdf\text{-}maps}\):
\[
  U_e(F_{P_{\nu_0}}(y_0))=U_e(0)=0,
  \qquad
  U_e(F_{P_{\nu_n}}(y_0))=U_e(\delta_n)
    =e\delta_n+1-e.
  \tag{6}
\]
Define the two full endpoint pairs at probabilities zero and \(\delta_n\), and let the data events assert their respective membership in \(I_n^L(y_0)\times I_n^U(y_0)\).  The supplied band-membership premise makes these events measurable.  Repeating (2)--(4) for them shows that, under the boundary law, with probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)\), the rectangular band contains both pairs.  Its upper projection therefore contains both values in (6), and consequently
\[
  \operatorname{diam} I_n^U(y_0)
  \ge e\delta_n+1-e
  \ge1-e.
  \tag{7}
\]

It remains to verify the existence assertion inside the maintained bow-SCM class, including its eventual quantifier.  Choose \(z_-\le y_0<z_+\).  For every \(n\ge N\), define arm laws
\[
  P_0^- =\delta_{z_-},
  \qquad
  P_n^-=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_-}+\delta_n\delta_{z_+},
  \tag{8}
\]
and
\[
  P_0^+ =\delta_{z_+},
  \qquad
  P_n^+=(1-\delta_n)\delta_{z_+}+\delta_n\delta_{z_-}.
  \tag{9}
\]
Thus
\[
  F_{P_0^-}(y_0)=1,
  \quad F_{P_n^-}(y_0)=1-\delta_n,
  \qquad
  F_{P_0^+}(y_0)=0,
  \quad F_{P_n^+}(y_0)=\delta_n.
  \tag{10}
\]
For each sign and each index, take the residual law to be the arm law itself.  It is then absolutely continuous with respect to that arm law and
\[
  eP_j^\pm+(1-e)P_j^\pm=P_j^\pm.
  \tag{11}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:bow\text{-}mixture\text{-}completeness}\), (11) is realized by a bow-ADMG SCM with propensity \(e\), consistency, mutual absolute continuity, and latent variable \(U=(A,Y(0),Y(1))\).  One explicit version chooses \(A\) with \(\Pr(A=a)=e\), chooses \(Y(a)\) independently of \(A\) with law \(P_j^\pm\), fixes \(Y(1-a)\) at a common value, and sets \(Y=Y(A)\).  Then
\[
  \mathcal L(Y\mid A=a)=P_j^\pm,
  \qquad
  \mathcal L(Y(a))=P_j^\pm,
  \qquad
  \mathcal L(Y(a)\mid A=a)\sim\mathcal L(Y(a)),
  \tag{12}
\]
where the first equality uses consistency and independence, and the equivalence in (12) follows because the two laws are equal.  Thus all support conditions in \(\mathrm{def:bow\text{-}compatible}\) hold.  The common latent tuple supplies the latent representation allowed by \(\mathrm{ass:bow\text{-}structure}\).

Let \(\nu_0^\pm\) and \(\nu_n^\pm\) be the single-observation laws from these constructions, using the same fixed outside-arm outcome.  Couple them using the same treatment draw and the same outside-arm outcome.  Conditional on \(A=a\), use the common atom with probability \(1-\delta_n\) and move the remaining probability \(\delta_n\) between \(z_-\) and \(z_+\).  The coupled observations then disagree with probability exactly \(e\delta_n\).  For \(n\) independent copies, the union bound gives
\[
  \Pr\{(A_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^n
          \text{ differs between the coupled samples}\}
  \le ne\delta_n.
  \tag{13}
\]
For any measurable sample event \(H\), the two indicators \(\mathbf 1_H\) agree whenever the coupled samples agree.  Therefore
\[
  \left|\Pr_{(\nu_0^\pm)^{\otimes n}}(H)
        -\Pr_{(\nu_n^\pm)^{\otimes n}}(H)\right|
  \le ne\delta_n.
  \tag{14}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(H\) gives
\[
  \lVert(\nu_0^\pm)^{\otimes n}
          -(\nu_n^\pm)^{\otimes n}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
  \le ne\delta_n=o(1).
  \tag{15}
\]
For the finitely many indices \(n<N\), choose any fixed valid bow-SCM law, for example the corresponding boundary law.  The theorem requires the CDF identities only for all sufficiently large \(n\), so this finite-prefix choice is admissible and does not alter (15).  Equations (8)--(15) establish the claimed alternative sequences for every positive sequence satisfying \(n\delta_n\to0\).

Finally, \(1-e>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:positivity}\), and \(1-2\alpha>0\) by the stated restriction \(\alpha<1/2\).  Hence (4)--(7) rule out convergence of the relevant projection diameter to zero in probability at either boundary law while preserving uniform coverage on a class containing the corresponding alternatives.  Any use of the construction described by \(\mathrm{def:directional\text{-}handle}\) that claimed a uniformly shrinking, nonconservative band on such a tie-inclusive class would contradict this positive-probability lower bound.  It must therefore be restricted to strata separated from the contacts \(F_P(y_0)\in\{0,1\}\).  By the already established \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sample\text{-}simultaneous\text{-}band}\), \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\alpha}\) retains finite-sample simultaneous coverage and is the stated honest fallback.  The proof used strict positivity only through \(0<e<1\); the algebraic value of the endpoint map at \(e=0\) was not used.
