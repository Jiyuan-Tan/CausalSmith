Write \(m_j=|I_j|\) for \(j\in\{0,1\}\), and abbreviate \(\delta=\delta_n\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\), and define
\[
 \nu=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}],\qquad
 b_x=P(X=x,A\leq\delta)=p_xq_x(\delta),\qquad
 B=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_x=P(A\leq\delta).
 \tag{1}
\]
The first equality for \(b_x\) follows from \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) and the definition of \(q_x\), and the last equality follows because the finitely many events \(\{X=x,A\leq\delta\}\) partition \(\{A\leq\delta\}\).  By \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\),
\[
 0\leq B
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\int_0^\delta\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \leq \frac{c_+}{\kappa+1}\delta^{\kappa+1}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The division in (2) is valid because \(\kappa\geq0\), and (2) includes the boundary case \(\delta=0\), when \(B=0\).

Let \(\widehat B=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\widehat b_x\) and \(T=\widehat\nu_n+\widehat B/2\).  By \(\texttt{def:continuity-clamp-functional}\),
\[
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)
 =\nu+\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_x\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta).
 \tag{3}
\]
Since \(0\leq\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta)\leq1\), the two terms in (3) are nonnegative and their sum is at most
\(P(A>\delta)+P(A\leq\delta)=1\).  Thus the target belongs to \([0,1]\).  Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance from a point of \([0,1]\), so \(\texttt{def:continuity-fallback-estimator}\) and (3) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 &\leq |T-\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|\\
 &\leq |\widehat\nu_n-\nu|+\frac12|\widehat B-B|
   +\left|\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_x
       \left\{\frac12-\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta)\right\}\right|\\
 &\leq |\widehat\nu_n-\nu|+\frac12|\widehat B-B|+\frac B2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Here the final inequality uses \(b_x\geq0\) and
\( |1/2-\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta)|\leq1/2\).

Under \(\texttt{ass:iid-sampling}\), \(\widehat\nu_n\) and \(\widehat B\) are averages, over their respective deterministic blocks, of independent random variables in \([0,1]\), with expectations \(\nu\) and \(B\).  If \(V\in[0,1]\), then
\(\operatorname{Var}(V)\leq\mathbb E[(V-1/2)^2]\leq1/4\).  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu|\leq\frac1{2\sqrt{m_0}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat B-B|\leq\frac1{2\sqrt{m_1}}.
 \tag{5}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the declared block-size bounds imply \(m_j\geq n/8\).  Combining (2), (4), and (5), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-
       \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}\}=Cs_n.
 \tag{6}
\]
The inequality
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \tag{7}
\]
follows from the infimum over estimators in \(\texttt{def:continuity-frontier-criteria}\), because \(\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}\) is one sample-measurable estimator.

We next establish honest coverage and the upper expected-length bound.  By \(\texttt{lem:hoeffding-bounded-average}\) and the values of \(t_{j,\alpha}\) in \(\texttt{def:continuity-honest-interval}\),
\[
 P^{\otimes n}\{|\widehat\nu_n-\nu|>t_{0,\alpha}\}\leq\frac\alpha2,
 \qquad
 P^{\otimes n}\{|\widehat B-B|>t_{1,\alpha}\}\leq\frac\alpha2.
 \tag{8}
\]
No independence between the two displayed averages is required for the following union bound.  On the intersection of the complementary events in (8), \(B\leq\widehat B+t_{1,\alpha}\), and hence (4) gives
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-	heta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq t_{0,\alpha}+\frac12t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12B
 \leq t_{0,\alpha}+t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12\widehat B.
 \tag{9}
\]
The interval in \(\texttt{def:continuity-honest-interval}\) therefore contains the target with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  Intersecting an interval containing a point in \([0,1]\) with \([0,1]\) preserves containment and cannot increase length.  Since \(\mathbb E_P\widehat B=B\), equations (2) and (9) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_{n,\mathrm{cont}})]
 \leq2t_{0,\alpha}+2t_{1,\alpha}+B
 \leq Cs_n.
 \tag{10}
\]

For completeness, we derive the two testing reductions used for the converses.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two probability laws and let their scalar target values \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) have separation \(d=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\).  With \(\lambda=Q_0+Q_1\), write \(f_j=\mathrm dQ_j/\mathrm d\lambda\), and define the common subprobability measure \(H\) by \(\mathrm dH=\min(f_0,f_1)\,\mathrm d\lambda\).  Directly from the integral definition of total variation,
\[
 H(\Omega)=1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
 \tag{11}
\]
For every estimator \(T\), integration of
\(|T-\vartheta_0|+|T-\vartheta_1|\geq d\) against \(H\), followed by \(H\leq Q_j\), gives
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|T-\vartheta_j|
 \geq\frac d2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
When \(Q_1\ll Q_0\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 =\frac12\mathbb E_{Q_0}\left|\frac{\mathrm dQ_1}{\mathrm dQ_0}-1\right|
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)}.
 \tag{13}
\]
For product laws, multiplication of the independent likelihood ratios and Fubini's theorem yield
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\{1+\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)\}^n.
 \tag{14}
\]
Finally, suppose a random interval \(C_n\) covers \(\vartheta_j\) under \(Q_j\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\) for \(j=0,1\).  The defining variational inequality for total variation gives
\(Q_0\{\vartheta_1\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  A union bound under \(Q_0\) therefore yields
\[
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\in C_n\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
 \tag{15}
\]
On the event in (15), \(\operatorname{len}(C_n)\geq d\).

We now construct two pairs inside the same class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  In every stratum set
\[
 P(X=x)=\frac1J,
 \qquad
 \pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\mathbf 1\{0\leq a\leq1\}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The density in (16) integrates to one because \(\kappa+1>0\).  The declared restrictions \(p_{\min}\leq1/J\) and \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\) verify \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\) and \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), while (16) verifies \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\).  It remains in each pair only to verify that the Bernoulli conditional means lie in \([0,1]\) and are continuous on the declared threshold range.

For the global-shift pair, let
\[
 P_0(Y=1\mid X,A)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 P_1(Y=1\mid X,A)=\frac12+\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n=\gamma n^{-1/2},
 \tag{17}
\]
where \(0<\gamma\leq1/4\).  Both regressions are constant, continuous, and \([0,1]\)-valued, so both laws are in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  Since \(\{A>\delta\}\) and \(\{A\leq\delta\}\) partition the treatment space, \(\texttt{def:continuity-clamp-functional}\) gives
\[
 |\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_1)-
   \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_0)|=\varepsilon_n.
 \tag{18}
\]
Direct summation over \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) gives
\(\chi^2(P_1,P_0)=4\varepsilon_n^2\).  Thus (14) and \(1+u\leq e^u\) imply
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+4\varepsilon_n^2)^n-1
 \leq e^{4\gamma^2}-1.
 \tag{19}
\]

For the localized pair, suppose \(\delta>0\), fix \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), and put
\[
 w_n=\frac12\min\left\{\delta,,1-\bar\delta,
             \frac{\zeta}{n\delta^\kappa}\right\}>0,
 \qquad
 H_n(a)=\left(1-\frac{|a-\delta|}{w_n}\right)_+.
 \tag{20}
\]
Because \(0<\delta\leq\bar\delta<1\), we have \(w_n\leq\delta/2\) and \(w_n\leq(1-\bar\delta)/2\); consequently the support \([\delta-w_n,\delta+w_n]\) is contained in \([0,1]\).  Define
\[
 P_0(Y=1\mid X,A=a)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 P_1(Y=1\mid X,A=a)=\frac12+\eta H_n(a).
 \tag{21}
\]
These regressions are continuous and lie in \([0,1]\).  Although their modulus depends on \(n\), membership holds because \(\texttt{def:continuity-model-class}\) imposes no common modulus, Hölder exponent, or radius.  Hence both laws in (21) belong to the same class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).

Conditional Bernoulli summation, followed by the upper density envelope in \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(P_1,P_0)
 &=4\eta^2\mathbb E[H_n(A)^2]\\
 &\leq4\eta^2P(|A-\delta|\leq w_n)\\
 &\leq 8c_+\left(\frac32\right)^\kappa
       \eta^2w_n\delta^\kappa.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, on the integration interval in (22), \(a\leq\delta+w_n\leq3\delta/2\); the bound also holds at \(\kappa=0\).  Let \(C_0=8c_+(3/2)^\kappa\).  From (14), (20), (22), and \(1+u\leq e^u\),
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\exp(C_0\eta^2nw_n\delta^\kappa)-1
 \leq\exp(C_0\eta^2\zeta/2)-1.
 \tag{23}
\]
For (16), \(q_x(\delta)=\int_0^\delta(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da=\delta^{\kappa+1}\) in every stratum.  The retained-course difference is nonnegative and \(H_n(\delta)=1\), so (3) gives the target separation
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_1)-
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_0)
 &=\eta\mathbb E[H_n(A)\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
   +\eta\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\\
 &\geq\eta\delta^{\kappa+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

Choose \(\tau\in(0,1-2\alpha)\), which is possible because \(\alpha<1/2\).  Fix \(\eta\in(0,1/4]\).  The right sides of (19) and (23) converge to zero as \(\gamma\downarrow0\) and \(\zeta\downarrow0\), respectively.  We may therefore choose fixed positive \(\gamma\leq1/4\) and \(\zeta\) so that (13) bounds the product-law total variation of each pair by \(\tau\), uniformly in \(n\) and, for the localized pair, uniformly in \(\delta>0\).  Applying (12) separately to (18) and (24), and then taking the infimum over estimators, proves
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}\}
 \geq\frac c2\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}\}
 =\frac c2s_n.
 \tag{25}
\]
If \(\delta=0\), the global pair alone gives (25), because the second term is zero.  Similarly, (15), first for the global pair and then for the localized pair, gives every uniformly \((1-\alpha)\)-honest interval \(C_n\) the bound
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}\}
 \geq\frac c2s_n,
 \tag{26}
\]
because \(1-2\alpha-\tau>0\).  At \(\delta=0\), again only the global pair is needed.  Taking the infimum over all honest intervals in (26) proves the lower bound for \(L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star\).  Equations (6), (7), (10), (25), and (26) establish the asserted observed-data minimax-risk and honest expected-length frontier with constants depending only on the declared model constants and \(\alpha\).

It remains to transport the result to the causal criteria.  By \(\texttt{prop:continuity-causal-bridge}\), the causal clamp mean of every member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\) equals \(\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}\) of its observed margin.  By \(\texttt{prop:continuity-observed-margin-surjectivity}\), every observed law in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) has such a full-data lift and
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star=R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star,
 \qquad
 L_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star=L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star
 \tag{27}
\]
for every \(n\).  Because the estimator and interval are measurable functions only of the observed sample, equality of observed margins also transfers their risk, expected length, and uniform coverage.  Thus (6), (10), (25), and (26) give all asserted causal statements without changing either construction.

Finally, the two summands of \(s_n\) have the same order exactly when
\(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/[2(\kappa+1)]}\).  At \(\delta_n=0\), the atom term vanishes and the rate is \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), then \(\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\to\delta_0^{\kappa+1}>0\); equation (25) rules out uniform risk consistency, and (26) rules out vanishing worst-case expected length for uniformly honest intervals.  This proves every claimed regime statement.
