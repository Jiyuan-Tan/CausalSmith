Set \(\beta=1\).  The declared convention for integer smoothness gives \(\ell=\beta-1=0\).  Set \(\delta_n=0\), and abbreviate \(t=t_\alpha\).  The symbol declarations \(J\geq1\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\) imply \(12J/\alpha>1\), so \(t>0\).

We first prove the measurability and integrability assertions for the literal realized objective.  Fix \(n\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and a nonempty active index set \(S\subseteq I_2\), of cardinality \(s\).  On the event \(S_{z,x}=S\), write \(a=(A_i)_{i\in S}\).  By \(\text{def:exact-modulus-handle}\), degree-zero feasibility is exactly
\[
\sum_{i\in S}w_i=1,
\tag{1}
\]
with all coordinates outside \(S\) equal to zero.  Let \(\mathcal H_{1,L}\) denote the class in that definition and put
\[
B_S(a,w)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{1,L}}
\left|\sum_{i\in S}w_i f(a_i)-f(0)\right|.
\tag{2}
\]
The class \(\mathcal H_{1,L}\) is nonempty because it contains every constant function.  Its members take values in \([0,1]\) and, because \(\ell=0\), satisfy \(|f(a)-f(a')|\leq L|a-a'|\).  Hence, for any \(a,a'\) and \(w,w'\), the elementary inequalities \(|\sup_f u_f-\sup_f v_f|\leq\sup_f|u_f-v_f|\) and \(||u|-|v||\leq|u-v|\) give
\[
\left|B_S(a,w)-B_S(a',w')\right|
\leq \sum_{i\in S}|w_i-w_i'|
+L\sum_{i\in S}|w_i'|\,|a_i-a_i'|.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \((a,w)\mapsto B_S(a,w)\) is continuous.

For the uniform feasible vector \(\bar w_i=1/s\), every active \(a_i\) lies in \([0,h_n]\), and therefore
\[
B_S(a,\bar w)
=\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{1,L}}
\left|\frac1s\sum_{i\in S}\{f(a_i)-f(0)\}\right|
\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{1,L}}
\frac1s\sum_{i\in S}|f(a_i)-f(0)|
\leq Lh_n.
\tag{4}
\]
Also \(\|\bar w\|_2=s^{-1/2}\).  It follows that the infimum defining \(Q_{n,x}(z)\) is at most \(Lh_n+t/\sqrt{s}\leq L+t\).  Choose \(R=2+L/t\).  Since \(B_S\geq0\), every feasible \(w\) with \(\|w\|_2>R\) has objective larger than \(tR=L+2t>L+t\).  Therefore the defining infimum is unchanged when restricted to the nonempty compact set
\[
K_S=\left\{w\in\mathbb R^S:\sum_{i\in S}w_i=1, \|w\|_2\leq R\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equation (3), uniformly over \(w\in K_S\), now implies that
\[
a\longmapsto
q_S(a):=\inf_{w\in K_S}\{B_S(a,w)+t\|w\|_2\}
\tag{6}
\]
is continuous.  Indeed,
\[
|q_S(a)-q_S(a')|
\leq LR\sqrt{s}\,\max_{i\in S}|a_i-a_i'|.
\tag{7}
\]

There are only finitely many subsets \(S\subseteq I_2\).  Each event \(\{S_{z,x}=S\}\) is Borel because it is specified by the discrete conditions \(X_i=x\) and the interval conditions \(0\leq A_i\leq h_n\).  On a nonempty such event, \(Q_{n,x}(z)=q_S((A_i)_{i\in S})\); on \(S=\varnothing\), \(Q_{n,x}(z)=0\) by the explicit empty-feasible-set totalization in \(\text{def:exact-modulus-handle}\).  The finite piecewise-continuous representation proves that \(Q_{n,x}\) is Borel measurable.

Let \(N_x=|S_{z,x}|\).  If \(N_x\geq1\), (4) and the uniform weights give the upper bound below.  Conversely, every feasible \(w\) satisfies (1), so Cauchy--Schwarz and nonnegativity of the exact bias give
\[
1=\left|\sum_{i\in S_{z,x}}w_i\right|
\leq\sqrt{N_x}\,\|w\|_2,
\qquad
B_{S_{z,x}}(a,w)+t\|w\|_2\geq\frac{t}{\sqrt{N_x}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Taking the infimum and including the totalized case \(N_x=0\) yields the pointwise bounds
\[
\mathbf 1\{N_x\geq1\}\frac{t}{\sqrt{N_x}}
\leq Q_{n,x}(z)
\leq\mathbf 1\{N_x\geq1\}
\left(Lh_n+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N_x}}\right)
\leq Lh_n+t.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\mathcal X\) has the finite cardinality \(J\), \(\rho_n(z)=\max_xQ_{n,x}(z)\) is also Borel measurable and satisfies \(0\leq\rho_n\leq Lh_n+t\).  A real-valued Borel map has separable range and is almost everywhere strongly measurable under every probability law.  The uniform finite bound in (9) then gives
\[
\int |Q_{n,x}(z)|\,\mathrm dP_{X,A}^{\otimes n}(z)
\leq Lh_n+t<\infty,
\qquad
\int |\rho_n(z)|\,\mathrm dP_{X,A}^{\otimes n}(z)
\leq Lh_n+t<\infty.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus these are integrable in the literal real Bochner sense used by \(\text{def:exact-modulus-handle}\); in particular, the integral is not set to zero by the undefined-integral convention.

For every \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\), (9)--(10) and monotonicity of the integral imply
\[
0\leq
\operatorname{exactModulusHandle}(P,I_2,x,0,1,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},0,h_n,t)
\leq Lh_n+t.
\tag{11}
\]
The set of handles in (11) is nonempty once a member of \(\mathcal M\) is exhibited, and (11) makes it bounded above.  Hence its literal real supremum exists and is at least each of its members.

We now exhibit such a member and obtain a positive lower bound.  Define \(P_0\) by
\[
P_0(X=x)=\frac1J\quad(x\in\mathcal X),
\qquad
\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\quad(0\leq a\leq1),
\qquad
Y=\frac12\quad P_0\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{12}
\]
The density integrates to one because \(\int_0^1(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da=1\).  The declared parameter ranges give \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\), so \(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\) almost everywhere by \(\text{ass:polynomial-thinning}\).  Also \(1/J\geq p_{\min}\) by \(\text{ass:stratum-mass}\), and the constant conditional regression \(\mu_x^{P_0}=1/2\) has zero remainder and satisfies \(\text{ass:holder-regression}\).  The conditional law in (12) satisfies \(\text{ass:conditional-density-law}\) by construction.  Therefore \(P_0\in\mathcal M\) by \(\text{def:model-class}\).  This also proves the nonemptiness required after (11).

For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(n^{-1/(\kappa+3)}\leq1-\bar\delta\).  With \(\beta=1\) and \(\delta_n=0\), the set in \(\text{def:bandwidth}\) is then the interval whose left endpoint solves \(nh^{\kappa+3}=1\).  Consequently
\[
h_n=n^{-1/(\kappa+3)},
\qquad
nh_n^{\kappa+3}=1,
\qquad
nh_n^{\kappa+1}=h_n^{-2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{13}
\]

Fix \(x_0\in\mathcal X\), let \(m_n=|I_2|\), and put \(N=N_{x_0}\).  By \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\) and (12),
\[
N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(m_n,p_n),
\qquad
p_n=P_0(X=x_0,0\leq A\leq h_n)=\frac{h_n^{\kappa+1}}J,
\qquad
\lambda_n:=\mathbb E_{P_0}N=\frac{m_nh_n^{\kappa+1}}J.
\tag{14}
\]
Since \(m_n\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\lambda_n\geq\frac{n h_n^{\kappa+1}}{5J}
=\frac{h_n^{-2}}{5J}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\lambda_n\leq nh_n^{\kappa+1}=h_n^{-2}.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(E_n=\{1\leq N\leq2\lambda_n\}\), the binomial formula, \(1-u\leq e^{-u}\), and Markov's inequality give
\[
P_0(N=0)=(1-p_n)^{m_n}\leq e^{-\lambda_n},
\qquad
P_0(N>2\lambda_n)\leq\frac12,
\qquad
P_0(E_n)\geq\frac12-e^{-\lambda_n}\geq\frac13
\tag{16}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  On \(E_n\), the lower bound in (9) and the upper bound on \(\lambda_n\) in (15) yield
\[
Q_{n,x_0}(Z)
\geq\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}
\geq\frac{t}{\sqrt{2\lambda_n}}
\geq\frac{t h_n}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{17}
\]
Integrability was proved in (10), so taking the ordinary expectation in (17), rather than an extended integral or an undefined real integral, is legitimate.  Equations (16)--(17) give
\[
\mathbb E_{P_0}Q_{n,x_0}(Z)
\geq\frac{t}{3\sqrt2}h_n.
\tag{18}
\]
Set \(c_0=t_\alpha/(3\sqrt2)>0\).  Since \(\rho_n\geq Q_{n,x_0}\), (18) proves the first asserted expectation bound.  Since the handle at \((P_0,x_0)\) is a member of the nonempty bounded-above set in (11), the defining property of its real supremum proves
\[
\operatorname{worstExactModulus}_n(t_\alpha)\geq c_0h_n.
\tag{19}
\]

Finally, \(\kappa+1>0\), so \(\delta_n^{\kappa+1}=0\).  Thus \(\text{def:frontier}\) gives \(r_n=n^{-1/2}\), and (13), (19) give
\[
\frac{2\operatorname{worstExactModulus}_n(t_\alpha)}{r_n}
\geq2c_0n^{1/2-1/(\kappa+3)}
=2c_0n^{(\kappa+1)/(2(\kappa+3))}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{20}
\]
By \(\text{thm:honest-length}\), the displayed interval in that theorem is uniformly honest and has worst-case expected length at most \(Cr_n\).  Taking it as a candidate in the infimum defining \(L_n^\star\) gives \(L_n^\star/r_n\leq C\).  An untruncated symmetric interval with realized radius \(\rho_n\) has full length \(2\rho_n\), and (18) makes its worst-case expected length at least \(2c_0h_n\), whose ratio to \(r_n\) diverges by (20).  Because \(\beta=1\) and \(\delta_n=0\) are allowed, this single counterexample excludes a sharp theorem for this literal candidate that is uniform over the full declared smoothness range and every threshold regime.  Nothing in the argument proves or disproves marginal honesty, sharpness at a separately fixed \(\beta>1\), or the general exact minimax constant.
