Write \(m_j=|I_j|\) for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 b_x=P(X=x,A\leq\delta_n)=p_xq_x(\delta_n),
 \qquad B=\sum_xb_x=P(A\leq\delta_n),
 \qquad \widehat B=\sum_x\widehat b_x. \tag{36}
\]
The density upper envelope and the conditional-density identity give
\[
 B=\sum_xp_x\int_0^{\delta_n}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \leq \frac{c_+}{\kappa+1}\delta_n^{\kappa+1}. \tag{37}
\]
Put \(T=\widehat\nu_n+\widehat B/2\).  Since projection onto \([0,1]\) is a contraction toward every point of \([0,1]\), \(\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P)\in[0,1]\), and \(0\leq\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P\leq1\),
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq |\widehat\nu_n-\nu_{\delta_n}(P)|
 +\frac12|\widehat B-B|
 +\sum_xb_x\left|\frac12-\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta_n)\right|
 \leq |\widehat\nu_n-\nu_{\delta_n}(P)|+\frac12|\widehat B-B|+\frac B2. \tag{38}
\]
Both empirical quantities in (38) average variables in \([0,1]\).  Independence in \(\texttt{ass:iid-sampling}\) and the variance bound \(\operatorname{Var}(V)\leq1/4\) for \(0\leq V\leq1\) give, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_{\delta_n}(P)|\leq(2\sqrt{m_0})^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E|\widehat B-B|\leq(2\sqrt{m_1})^{-1}. \tag{39}
\]
Because \(m_j\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), (37)--(39) prove the uniform risk upper bound \(Cs_n\).

For coverage, the bounded-average concentration inequality \(\texttt{lem:hoeffding-bounded-average}\), applied to the two independent averages, yields
\[
 P\{|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_{\delta_n}(P)|>t_{0,\alpha}\}\leq\alpha/2,
 \qquad
 P\{|\widehat B-B|>t_{1,\alpha}\}\leq\alpha/2. \tag{40}
\]
On the intersection of the two events complementary to those in (40), \(B\leq\widehat B+t_{1,\alpha}\), and (38) gives
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq t_{0,\alpha}+\frac12t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12B
 \leq t_{0,\alpha}+t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12\widehat B. \tag{41}
\]
Thus the interval in \(\texttt{def:continuity-honest-interval}\) covers uniformly with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).  Intersecting with \([0,1]\) cannot increase length, so (37) and \(\mathbb E\widehat B=B\) show
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_{n,\mathrm{cont}})]
 \leq2t_{0,\alpha}+2t_{1,\alpha}+B
 \leq Cs_n. \tag{42}
\]

We now give two explicit same-class experiments.  They also establish the confidence-length converse.  In both, take \(P(X=x)=1/J\) and \(\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\) in every stratum.  These choices satisfy \(p_x\geq p_{\min}\) and \(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\) because the declared constants obey \(p_{\min}\leq1/J\) and \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\).

For the global experiment let \(P_0(Y=1\mid X,A)=1/2\) and \(P_1(Y=1\mid X,A)=1/2+\varepsilon_n\), where \(\varepsilon_n=\gamma n^{-1/2}\) and \(0<\gamma\leq1/4\) is fixed below.  Both regressions are continuous, so both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  Their target separation is
\[
 |\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P_1)-\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P_0)|=\varepsilon_n, \tag{43}
\]
because the retained and atom probabilities sum to one.  With \(P_0\) as reference, direct summation over the two Bernoulli outcomes gives one-observation chi-square divergence \(4\varepsilon_n^2\).  Independence therefore gives
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+4\varepsilon_n^2)^n-1\leq e^{4\gamma^2}-1. \tag{44}
\]

For the local experiment suppose first that \(\delta_n>0\).  Fix \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), choose
\[
 0<w_n\leq\min\left\{\frac{\delta_n}{2},\frac{1-\bar\delta}{2},
 \frac{\zeta}{n\delta_n^\kappa}\right\}, \tag{45}
\]
where \(\zeta>0\) will be fixed below, and put
\[
 H_n(a)=\left(1-\frac{|a-\delta_n|}{w_n}\right)_+,
 \qquad
 P_0(Y=1\mid X,A=a)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 P_1(Y=1\mid X,A=a)=\frac12+\eta H_n(a). \tag{46}
\]
The two regression functions are continuous and bounded in \([0,1]\); although the modulus of the second may depend on \(n\), \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) imposes no common modulus.  Thus both laws are in the same declared class.  Their one-observation chi-square divergence is
\[
 \chi^2(P_1,P_0)=4\eta^2\mathbb E[H_n(A)^2]
 \leq4\eta^2P(|A-\delta_n|\leq w_n)
 \leq C_0\eta^2w_n\delta_n^\kappa, \tag{47}
\]
where the last inequality uses (45) and the density upper envelope.  Hence
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\exp(C_0\eta^2nw_n\delta_n^\kappa)-1
 \leq\exp(C_0\eta^2\zeta)-1. \tag{48}
\]
The retained-course difference is nonnegative, and \(H_n(\delta_n)=1\).  Therefore the exact functional decomposition and the density lower envelope give
\[
 \theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P_1)-\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P_0)
 \geq\eta q_x(\delta_n)
 =\eta\delta_n^{\kappa+1}. \tag{49}
\]
Here the equality uses the displayed reference density; the same lower-bound order would follow from \(q_x(\delta_n)\geq c_-\delta_n^{\kappa+1}/(\kappa+1)\).

We record the elementary two-point reduction.  If \(Q_0,Q_1\) have target values separated by \(d\), then for every estimator \(T\), integration against the common part of the two measures gives
\[
 \max_{j=0,1}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|T-\theta_j|
 \geq\frac d2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}. \tag{50}
\]
Moreover, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)}. \tag{51}
\]
Choose \(\gamma\) and \(\zeta\) so that the right sides of (44) and (48) make (51) at most a fixed \(\tau<1-2\alpha\).  Equations (43), (49), and (50) yield
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\}
 \geq\frac c2s_n. \tag{52}
\]
When \(\delta_n=0\), the global pair alone proves (52), because the second summand of \(s_n\) is zero.

If an interval \(C_n\) covers both members of a two-point experiment with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), then under \(Q_0\)
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in C_n\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1). \tag{53}
\]
On this event its length is at least \(d\).  Apply (53) to the global and local pairs and use \(\tau<1-2\alpha\).  Taking the larger lower bound and then the infimum over honest intervals proves
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star\geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\}\geq\frac c2s_n. \tag{54}
\]
Together, (38)--(42), (52), and (54) prove the matched observed-data frontier.

Propositions \(\texttt{prop:continuity-causal-bridge}\) and \(\texttt{prop:continuity-observed-margin-surjectivity}\) identify the causal target memberwise and give exact finite-\(n\) equality of the observed and causal decision criteria.  They therefore transfer the upper bounds, coverage statement, and lower bounds without changing the estimator or interval.  Finally, the two terms in \(s_n\) are equal exactly when \(\delta_n\) has order \(n^{-1/[2(\kappa+1)]}\).  If \(\delta_n=0\), (52) is root-\(n\); if \(\delta_n\to\delta_0>0\), (52) and (54) are bounded away from zero.  This proves the elbow and the fixed-threshold impossibility statements.
