In this model, \(\beta=\kappa=1\), so
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}=n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}
 =n^{-1/[2(1+2+1+1)]}=n^{-1/10}. \tag{42}
\]
When \(\delta_n\gg n^{-1/4}\), Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} gives
\[
 h_n\asymp(n\delta_n)^{-1/3},
 \qquad h_n/\delta_n\to0. \tag{43}
\]
For the localized alternatives (20), the regression displacement has height of order \(h_n\) and support width of order \(h_n\).  If \(|g|\leq1/4\), two applications of the mean-value theorem to the Bernoulli log likelihood give
\[
 c g^2\leq
 \operatorname{KL}\{\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+g),\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)\}
 \leq Cg^2,
\]
because the second derivative is bounded above and away from zero on \([1/4,3/4]\).  Since \(\pi(a)=2a\) and (43) makes \(a\asymp\delta_n\) throughout the effective local window, the one-observation Bernoulli divergence is therefore of order
\[
 h_n^2\int_{\delta_n-h_n}^{\delta_n+h_n}2a\,\mathrm da
 \asymp\delta_nh_n^3.
\]
Independence therefore makes the product Kullback--Leibler divergence of order \(n\delta_nh_n^3\), which is constant by (43).  The clamp atom has mass
\[
 q(\delta_n)=\int_0^{\delta_n}2a\,\mathrm da=\delta_n^2.
\]
Its regression displacement is of order \(h_n\), so its target contribution is of order \(\delta_n^2h_n\); the retained-course contribution is only of order \(\delta_nh_n^2=o(\delta_n^2h_n)\) by (43).  Thus the total target separation is of order \(\delta_n^2h_n\), agreeing with Theorem \texttt{thm:minimax-risk}.  Equating this quantity with \(n^{-1/2}\), and using (43), gives
\[
 \delta_n^2(n\delta_n)^{-1/3}=n^{-1/2}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \delta_n^{5/3}=n^{-1/6}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \delta_n=n^{-1/10},
\]
which proves the stated transition and agrees with Theorem \texttt{thm:phase-diagram}.
