Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\), and let \(P\in\mathcal M\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin.  Proposition \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\) gives, for every declared threshold,
\[
 \psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})=\theta_{\delta_n}(P).
 \tag{15}
\]
Both \(\widehat\theta_n\) and \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) are measurable functions only of \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).  Hence their joint law with the observed sample under \((P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\) is the same as under \(P^{\otimes n}\).  By (15) and Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 &\leq \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 |\widehat\theta_n-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})|\\
 &\leq \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}|\widehat\theta_n-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)|
 \leq C r_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The first inequality is the definition in \(\texttt{def:causal-frontier-criteria}\), the second uses that every full-data member has an observed margin in \(\mathcal M\), and the third is the established observed-data upper bound.  Similarly, Theorem \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) and (15) give
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in\mathrm{CI}_n\}
 \geq1-\alpha
 \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 [\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]
 \leq C r_n.
 \tag{18}
\]
These upper bounds are uniform over the carrier-heterogeneous class because their right-hand sides depend only on the observed margin and the declared model constants.

It remains to show that changing the carrier quantifier does not enlarge the lower-bound claim.  The established lower-bound construction in Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\) consists of a same-class global Bernoulli shift and a same-class localized Bernoulli regression perturbation.  For either pair, write \(P_{j,n}\in\mathcal M\), \(j\in\{0,1\}\), for the two observed laws and \(\mu_{j,n}(a)\) for their continuous Bernoulli regressions.  We construct particular full-data lifts; we do not require every possible carrier to support them.  For both alternatives choose the rich standard Borel carrier \([0,1]\), take
\[
 U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]
 \quad\text{independently of }(X,A),
 \qquad
 g_{j,x}(a,u)=\mathbf 1\{u\leq\mu_{j,n}(a)\},
 \tag{19}
\]
and set
\[
 Y_j(a)=g_{j,X}(a,U),
 \qquad
 Y_j=g_{j,X}(A,U).
 \tag{20}
\]
Because \(\mu_{j,n}\) is continuous by Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), the set \(\{(a,u):u\leq\mu_{j,n}(a)\}\) is Borel, so each map in (19) is jointly Borel.  Equations (19)--(20) give the simultaneous consistency identities for every \(a\) without a dose-dependent exceptional set.  Independence in (19) implies \(A\perp\!\!\!\perp U\mid X\).  Moreover, for every \(s,t\in[0,1]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[|Y_j(t)-Y_j(s)|\mid X=x]
 &=\int_0^1
 |\mathbf 1\{u\leq\mu_{j,n}(t)\}
  -\mathbf 1\{u\leq\mu_{j,n}(s)\}|\,\mathrm du\\
 &=|\mu_{j,n}(t)-\mu_{j,n}(s)|\longrightarrow0
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
as \(t\to s\).  Thus all three full-data member conditions hold.  Conditional on \((A=a,X=x)\), (19)--(20) make \(Y_j\) Bernoulli with mean \(\mu_{j,n}(a)\), so the observed margin of this lift is exactly the original Bernoulli law \(P_{j,n}\).  Therefore each lifted law belongs to the carrier-heterogeneous class \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\).

Proposition \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\) makes the causal target separation of each lifted pair identical to its observed target separation.  The observed sample product laws, and hence every divergence or testing affinity used by the accepted lower-bound proofs, are also identical.  The global pair therefore retains separation of order \(n^{-1/2}\), while the localized pair retains separation of order \(\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\), with the same bounded product divergence.  Applying the two-point inequalities already established in Theorems \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\) and \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) to these lifted pairs gives
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\}
 \geq \frac c2 r_n
 \tag{22}
\]
and
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\}
 \geq \frac c2 r_n.
 \tag{23}
\]
Here the fixed perturbation amplitudes in the honest-length argument are chosen, as in the accepted observed proof, so that the product total-variation distance is strictly below \(1-2\alpha\); this is possible because \(\alpha<1/2\), and shrinking either fixed amplitude preserves its rate-order separation.  Equations (16)--(18) and (22)--(23) prove the stated risk, coverage, and expected-length conclusions.  Crucially, the converse uses the explicitly chosen carrier \([0,1]\); it makes no claim that an arbitrary fixed nonempty carrier can realize the Bernoulli witnesses.
