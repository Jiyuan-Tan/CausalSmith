Díaz, Williams, Hoffman, and Schenck identify threshold-LMTP means by sequential regression and show that the continuous threshold example is not pathwise differentiable. Their work supplies the structural identification template and support logic, but not the deterministic moving-threshold minimax-risk or uniformly honest expected-length frontier proved here. The present bridge uses the additional author-imposed conditional \(L^1\)-response-continuity premise: within each full-data law's own standard-Borel carrier, simultaneous latent-response consistency and latent randomization first identify the structural response mean almost everywhere, and continuity then extends equality to every dose. The pointwise equality is therefore derived, not assumed, and the carrier is not fixed globally across the class. Hoffman et al. repeat the piecewise-invertibility boundary in their applied tutorial, and Williams and Díaz document the \(\texttt{lmtp}\) user-policy workflow that consumes the result. McCoy et al.'s MTP density ratio is a Radon--Nikodym derivative with respect to an appropriate dominating measure, allowing clipping-induced boundary mass, and their classification estimator targets the clipped pushforward law directly; their stochastic-shift effect-modifier target and high-probability theory remain distinct. Gaiffas derives fixed-point regression rates under regularly varying degenerate random design. The measurable envelope \(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\) here is an explicit treatment-assignment class, not a consequence attributed to regular variation. Armstrong and Kolesár construct bias-aware honest intervals for nonparametric regression under fixed-design formulations; the atom-weighted moving-point functional and singular-Gram expected-length integration are absent. Kennedy et al. motivate two-stage causal local regression, while Bonvini and Kennedy study dose-response rates under smooth positive conditional densities rather than polynomial thinning. Van der Laan, Zhang, and Gilbert study a support-restricted stochastic region-average threshold response, and Shahu et al. study simultaneous LMTP inference under piecewise invertibility; neither supplies this deterministic-clamp frontier.
