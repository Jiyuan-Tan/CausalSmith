Put \(\delta=\delta_n\), \(h=h_n\), \(m_j=|I_j|\), and
\[
 b_x=P(X=x,A\leq\delta)=p_xq_x(\delta).
\]
Proposition \texttt{prop:pushforward-setup} gives
\[
 0\leq b_x\leq \frac{c_+}{\kappa+1}\delta^{\kappa+1}. \tag{1}
\]
We first prove the upper bound for the single sample map in Definition \texttt{def:total-gram-estimator}.  Since \(m_0,m_1\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling}, \(0\leq Y\leq1\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq1/4\) for \(0\leq Z\leq1\) imply, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|\leq Cn^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat b_x-b_x|\leq Cn^{-1/2}. \tag{2}
\]

On \(\Omega_x\), write \(S_x=\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}Y_i\).  Conditional on \(\mathscr D_2=\sigma\{(X_i,A_i):i\in I_2\}\), Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling} makes the outcomes independent, and the defining version \(\mu_x^P\) is their conditional mean at almost every realized treatment value.  Polynomial reproduction in Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} makes the Taylor polynomial of \(\mu_x^P\) about \(\delta\) contribute exactly its intercept.  Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression}, boundedness of \(Y\), and the two weight bounds in that lemma therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathbb E_P[S_x\mid\mathscr D_2]-\mu_x^P(\delta)\right|
 &\leq Lh^\beta\sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|\leq Ch^\beta,\\
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_x\mid\mathscr D_2)
 &\leq\frac14\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2\leq\frac{C}{N_x}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance from \(\mu_x^P(\delta)\in[0,1]\).

We record the count calculation needed to integrate (3).  The variable \(N_x\) is binomial with mean
\[
 \lambda_x=m_2p_x\int_\delta^{\delta+h}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\]
Assumptions \texttt{ass:stratum-mass} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}, together with the subinterval \([\delta+h/2,\delta+h]\), yield
\[
 cnh(\delta+h)^\kappa\leq\lambda_x\leq Cnh(\delta+h)^\kappa. \tag{4}
\]
For a binomial \(N\) of mean \(\lambda=mp\), exponential Markov and \(1+z\leq e^z\) give, at \(t=\log2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(N<\lambda/2)
 &\leq e^{t\lambda/2}\mathbb E e^{-tN}
 =e^{t\lambda/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^m\\
 &\leq\exp\{\lambda(t/2+e^{-t}-1)\}
 \leq e^{-\lambda/8}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Splitting according to \(N\geq\lambda/2\) consequently gives
\[
 \mathbb E[N^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N>0\}]
 \leq (2/\lambda)^{1/2}+e^{-\lambda/8}
 \leq C\lambda^{-1/2}. \tag{5}
\]
Here and below constants absorb the harmless bounded range of \(\lambda\); by (4) and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases}, \(\lambda_x\asymp h^{-2\beta}\).  On \(\Omega_x^c\), both the fallback and the true regression value lie in \([0,1]\).  Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} and \(e^{-ct}\leq C_ct^{-1/2}\) for \(t\geq1\), used with \(t=h^{-2\beta}\), therefore imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\mu_x-\mu_x^P(\delta)|
 \leq Ch^\beta+C\mathbb E_P[N_x^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N_x>0\}]
       +P(\Omega_x^c)
 \leq Ch^\beta. \tag{6}
\]

Before the final projection, add and subtract \(b_x\widetilde\mu_x\), use \(0\leq\widetilde\mu_x\leq1\), and combine (1), (2), and (6):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_n-\theta_\delta(P)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|
 +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\left\{\mathbb E_P|\widehat b_x-b_x|
 +b_x\mathbb E_P|\widetilde\mu_x-\mu_x^P(\delta)|\right\}\\
 &\leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}=Cr_n. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
The displayed inequality survives projection because \(\theta_\delta(P)\in[0,1]\).  The fixed value of \(J\) makes the constant uniform in \(P\), \(x\), and the deterministic threshold sequence.  Taking the supremum proves the claimed minimax upper bound with one data-driven estimator.

For the converse, we construct two pairs inside the same class \(\mathcal M\).  In both, take \(p_x=1/J\) and \(\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\) for every stratum.  These choices satisfy Assumptions \texttt{ass:stratum-mass} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}, because \(p_{\min}\leq1/J\) and \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\).  For the global pair, let \(Y\mid(A,X)\) be Bernoulli with mean \(1/2\) under \(P_0\) and \(1/2+a_0n^{-1/2}\) under \(P_1\), where \(a_0>0\) is a sufficiently small fixed constant.  Both regressions obey Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression}, and the two parts of the definition of \(\theta_\delta\) partition the treatment law.  Hence
\[
 |\theta_\delta(P_1)-\theta_\delta(P_0)|=a_0n^{-1/2}. \tag{8}
\]
The one-observation chi-square divergence is \(4a_0^2/n\), so independence gives
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+4a_0^2/n)^n-1\leq e^{4a_0^2}-1. \tag{9}
\]

For the localized pair, fix a nonnegative smooth bump \(\varphi\) supported on \([-1,1]\) with \(\varphi(0)=1\), for example the normalized function \(u\mapsto\exp\{-1/(1-u^2)\}\mathbf 1\{|u|<1\}\).  Direct Taylor expansion and rescaling give a finite constant \(C_{\varphi,\beta}\) such that \(h^\beta\varphi((\,cdot-\delta)/h)\), restricted to \([0,1]\), satisfies the Taylor-remainder Hölder condition with radius \(C_{\varphi,\beta}\).  Define, in every stratum,
\[
 \mu_0(a)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(a)=\frac12+a_1h^\beta\varphi((a-\delta)/h), \tag{10}
\]
where the fixed \(a_1>0\) is small enough that \(a_1C_{\varphi,\beta}\leq L\) and \(a_1\|\varphi\|_\infty\leq1/4\).  Bernoulli outcomes with these conditional means define two further members of \(\mathcal M\).  Since the Bernoulli chi-square divergence from mean \(1/2\) to mean \(1/2+g\) equals \(4g^2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(P_1,P_0)
 &=4a_1^2h^{2\beta}\int_0^1
   \varphi((a-\delta)/h)^2(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da\\
 &\leq Ca_1^2h^{2\beta+1}(\delta+h)^\kappa.
 \end{aligned} \tag{11}
\]
Definition \texttt{def:bandwidth} and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} make \(n\) times the last expression at most \(Ca_1^2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Thus
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\exp(Ca_1^2)-1. \tag{12}
\]
For this design, \(q_x(\delta)=\delta^{\kappa+1}\).  The perturbation is nonnegative, and its value at the clamp atom is \(a_1h^\beta\).  The retained-natural-course part is also nonnegative, so
\[
 \theta_\delta(P_1)-\theta_\delta(P_0)
 \geq a_1\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta. \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, if \(Q_1\ll Q_0\) has likelihood ratio \(Z\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|Q_1-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\sup_E|\mathbb E_{Q_0}[(Z-1)\mathbf 1_E]|
 \leq\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)}. \tag{14}
\]
Choose \(a_0,a_1\) so that both product total variations are at most \(1/2\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), a pair with target values \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and separation \(\Delta\), set \(E=\{|T-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\).  Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{j=0,1}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|T-\theta_j|
 &\geq\frac{\Delta}{4}\{Q_0(E^c)+Q_1(E)\}\\
 &\geq\frac{\Delta}{4}\{1-\|Q_1-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{8}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
Apply (15) separately to (8) and (13), then use \(\max(u,v)\geq(u+v)/2\).  This proves
\[
 R_n^\star\geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}
 \geq c'\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}=c'r_n. \tag{16}
\]
Together, (7) and (16) establish every assertion of the theorem, including the announced global-shift and localized-Bernoulli mechanisms.
