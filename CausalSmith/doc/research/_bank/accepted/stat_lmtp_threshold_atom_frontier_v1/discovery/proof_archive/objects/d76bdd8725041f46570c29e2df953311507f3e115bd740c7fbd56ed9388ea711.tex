Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\) be a member-level full-data structure satisfying the proposed statement, let \(P\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin, and fix \(x\in\mathcal X\). All occurrences of \(\mathcal U\), \(U\), \(g_x\), and \(Y(a)\) in this proof refer to the objects packaged by this one member. Assumption \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\) gives
\[
 P^{\mathrm F}(X=x)=P(X=x)=p_x\geq p_{\min}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus every conditional law below is well defined on the fixed stratum. Assumption \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) says that this member's carrier \(\mathcal U\) is standard Borel, that the maps \(g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1]\) are jointly Borel measurable, and that there is one \(P^{\mathrm F}\)-probability-one event on which, simultaneously for every \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Y(a)=g_X(a,U),
 \qquad
 Y=g_X(A,U).
\tag{2}
\]
The single event in (2) permits evaluation at the random argument \(A\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\) states \(A\perp\!\!\!\perp U\mid X\). Since (1) gives positive stratum mass, the defining factorization of conditional independence yields
\[
 P^{\mathrm F}_{(A,U)\mid X=x}
 =P^{\mathrm F}_{A\mid X=x}\otimes P^{\mathrm F}_{U\mid X=x}.
\tag{3}
\]
All integrands below are Borel measurable by the packaged joint measurability and are bounded by one, so every expectation and iterated product integral is finite.

Define the member-specific structural response mean
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)
 =\int_{\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm du\mid X=x)
 =\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[g_x(a,U)\mid X=x].
\tag{4}
\]
For any Borel set \(B\subseteq[0,1]\), equations (2)--(4), followed by Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 &=\int_{B\times\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,
   \{P^{\mathrm F}_{A\mid X=x}\otimes P^{\mathrm F}_{U\mid X=x}\}
   (\mathrm da,\mathrm du)\\
 &=\int_B\left\{\int_{\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,
   P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm du\mid X=x)\right\}
   P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm da\mid X=x)\\
 &=\int_Bm_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Here the first equality is the definition of integration under the conditional law, the second is the defining iterated-integral identity for the product law in (3), and the third uses (4) and the conditional-density identity. No density for \(U\), and no carrier shared with any other full-data member, is used.

We next derive the pointwise equality of response means. From (2), (4), the triangle inequality, and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\), for every \(s\in[0,1]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |m_x^{\mathrm F}(t)-m_x^{\mathrm F}(s)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}
   [|g_x(t,U)-g_x(s,U)|\mid X=x]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}
   [|Y(t)-Y(s)|\mid X=x]
 \longrightarrow0
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
as \(t\to s\). Hence \(m_x^{\mathrm F}\) is continuous on \([0,1]\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\) states that \(\mu_x^P\) is continuous and is an almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\). Therefore Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) gives, for every Borel \(B\subseteq[0,1]\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 =\int_B\mu_x^P(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(P\) is the observed margin of \(P^{\mathrm F}\), subtracting (7) from (5) yields
\[
 \int_B\{m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)-\mu_x^P(a)\}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da=0
 \quad\text{for every Borel }B\subseteq[0,1].
\tag{8}
\]
Set \(f=m_x^{\mathrm F}-\mu_x^P\). The function \(f\) is continuous by (6) and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), so the following level sets are Borel. For every integer \(j\geq1\), equation (8) with \(B=\{f\geq1/j\}\) gives
\[
 0=\int_{\{f\geq1/j\}}f(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \geq \frac1j\int_{\{f\geq1/j\}}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\tag{9}
\]
Using \(B=\{f\leq-1/j\}\) and multiplying by \(-1\) gives the same conclusion for the negative level set. Since \(\pi_x\geq0\), taking the union over \(j\) shows
\[
 f(a)=0
 \quad\text{for }\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\text{-almost every }a.
\tag{10}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), together with \(c_->0\) and \(\kappa\geq0\), gives
\[
 \pi_x(a)\geq c_-a^\kappa>0
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in(0,1].
\tag{11}
\]
Thus (10)--(11) imply \(f=0\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \((0,1]\). If \(f(a_0)\neq0\) at some \(a_0\in[0,1]\), continuity would give a relative neighborhood \(I\) of \(a_0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) on which \(|f|\geq\varepsilon\). The set \(I\cap(0,1]\) has positive Lebesgue measure, contradicting the preceding almost-everywhere equality. Consequently,
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_x^P(a)
 \quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1].
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, equality at \(a=0\) follows from continuity and is not imposed by selecting a conditional-expectation version.

Fix \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\). Definition \(\texttt{def:clamp-policy}\) and the simultaneous identities (2) imply on the same probability-one event
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Y\{d_\delta(A)\}
 &=g_X\{\max(A,\delta),U\}\\
 &=g_X(A,U)\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}
   +g_X(\delta,U)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\\
 &=Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}
   +Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
For the second term, (3), (4), (12), and Proposition \(\texttt{prop:pushforward-setup}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\mid X=x]
 &=m_x^{\mathrm F}(\delta)
   P^{\mathrm F}(A\leq\delta\mid X=x)\\
 &=\mu_x^P(\delta)q_x(\delta).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Taking expectations in (13), applying (14), using the observed-margin identity for the retained natural-course term, and summing over the finite set \(\mathcal X\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})
 &=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\{d_\delta(A)\}]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
   +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_x^P(\delta)\\
 &=\theta_\delta(P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The first and last equalities are the definitions of \(\psi_\delta\) and \(\theta_\delta\). Proposition \(\texttt{prop:policy-support}\) separately proves that \(d_\delta(A)\) belongs almost surely to the support of the conditional natural-treatment law. At \(\delta=0\), Definition \(\texttt{def:clamp-policy}\) is the identity and Proposition \(\texttt{prop:pushforward-setup}\) gives \(q_x(0)=0\); at \(\delta=\bar\delta<1\), every evaluation remains in \([0,1]\). Equations (5), (12), (13), and (15), together with the support proposition, prove every asserted conclusion fiberwise for the carrier packaged by the selected law.
