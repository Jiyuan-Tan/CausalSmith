Set \(\delta_n=0\) for every \(n\).  We first construct a member of the frozen model.  Let \(P_0(X=x)=1/J\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), let the conditional density of \(A\) given any \(X=x\) be
\[
\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa,\qquad 0\leq a\leq1,
\]
and set \(Y=1/2\) almost surely.  The density integrates to one because
\(\int_0^1(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da=1\).  The declared inequalities \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\) give
\(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\) almost everywhere.  Also \(1/J\geq p_{\min}\), and the constant regression \(\mu_x^{P_0}(a)=1/2\) has zero Taylor remainder and therefore satisfies the Hölder condition with radius \(L\).  The conditional-density identity holds by construction.  Thus \(P_0\in\mathcal M\) by \(\text{def:model-class}\), using \(\text{ass:conditional-density-law}\), \(\text{ass:stratum-mass}\), \(\text{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), and \(\text{ass:holder-regression}\).

For all sufficiently large \(n\), the number \(n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}\) is at most \(1-\bar\delta\).  Since \(h\mapsto nh^{2\beta+\kappa+1}\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), \(\text{def:bandwidth}\) then gives exactly
\[
h_n=n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)},
\qquad
nh_n^{2\beta+\kappa+1}=1,
\qquad
nh_n^{\kappa+1}=h_n^{-2\beta}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix one \(x_0\in\mathcal X\), put \(m_n=|I_2|\), and write \(N=N_{x_0}\).  By independent sampling and the preceding conditional density,
\[
N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}\!\left(m_n,J^{-1}h_n^{\kappa+1}\right),
\qquad
\mathbb E_{P_0}N=m_nJ^{-1}h_n^{\kappa+1}\leq nh_n^{\kappa+1}.
\tag{2}
\]
Markov's inequality, applied to the nonnegative random variable \(N\), yields
\[
P_0\{N>2\mathbb E_{P_0}N\}\leq\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]
On the other hand, \(\text{lem:total-gram}\), (1), and \(P_0\in\mathcal M\) imply
\[
P_0(\Omega_{x_0}^c)
\leq C\exp\{-cnh_n^{\kappa+1}\}
=C\exp\{-ch_n^{-2\beta}\}=o(1).
\tag{4}
\]
It follows from (3)--(4) that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
P_0(E_n)\geq\frac13,
\qquad
E_n=\Omega_{x_0}\cap\{N\leq2\mathbb E_{P_0}N\}.
\tag{5}
\]

We next lower-bound the realized optimization, rather than replacing it by an asymptotic surrogate.  On \(\Omega_{x_0}\), the total Gram \(G_{x_0}=V_{z,x_0}^\top V_{z,x_0}\) is invertible.  Hence the vector whose active coordinates are
\(V_{z,x_0}G_{x_0}^{-1}e_0\) belongs to \(\mathcal W_{z,x_0}\), so that affine feasible set is nonempty.  Every \(w\in\mathcal W_{z,x_0}\) satisfies its degree-zero reproduction constraint
\[
\sum_{i\in S_{z,x_0}}w_i=1.
\]
Because \(|S_{z,x_0}|=N\), the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[
1\leq \sqrt N\left(\sum_{i\in I_2}w_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{6}
\]
The exact Hölder bias is a supremum of absolute values and is therefore nonnegative.  Since \(t_\alpha>0\), (6) and \(\text{def:exact-modulus-handle}\) show on \(\Omega_{x_0}\) that
\[
\operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{z,x_0}(t_\alpha)
\geq \frac{t_\alpha}{\sqrt N}.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (2), (5), and (7), and then using (1), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_0}\operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{Z,x_0}(t_\alpha)
&\geq P_0(E_n)\frac{t_\alpha}{\sqrt{2\mathbb E_{P_0}N}}\\
&\geq \frac{t_\alpha}{3\sqrt{2nh_n^{\kappa+1}}}
=\frac{t_\alpha}{3\sqrt2}h_n^\beta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the claimed bound holds with \(c_0=t_\alpha/(3\sqrt2)>0\), both for \(\operatorname{worstExactModulus}_n(t_\alpha)\), which takes the supremum over \(P\) and \(x\), and for \(\mathbb E_{P_0}\rho_n(Z)\), since a supremum over \(x\) is at least its \(x_0\) term.

At \(\delta_n=0\), \(\text{def:frontier}\) gives \(r_n=n^{-1/2}\).  Equation (1) and (8) therefore imply
\[
\frac{2\operatorname{worstExactModulus}_n(t_\alpha)}{r_n}
\geq 2c_0 n^{1/2-\beta/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}
=2c_0n^{(\kappa+1)/[2(2\beta+\kappa+1)]}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{9}
\]
where positivity of the exponent follows from \(\kappa\geq0\).  An untruncated symmetric interval of radius \(\rho_n(Z)\) has full length \(2\rho_n(Z)\), so its worst-case expected length obeys the same lower bound.  In contrast, \(\text{thm:honest-length}\) and the definition of \(L_n^\star\) give \(L_n^\star\leq Cr_n\).  Hence the proposed radius is not even order-optimal along the regular sequence \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}=0\), and a fortiori cannot attain a sharp constant uniformly over all the regimes in the question.  The high-probability event in (5) also shows that the zero convention on empty affine feasible sets does not remove this obstruction.
