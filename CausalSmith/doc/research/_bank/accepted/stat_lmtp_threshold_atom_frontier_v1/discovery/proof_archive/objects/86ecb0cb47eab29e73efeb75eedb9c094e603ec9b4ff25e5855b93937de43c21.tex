Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\) satisfy the proposed statement, let \(P\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin, and fix \(x\in\mathcal X\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\) gives
\[
 P^{\mathrm F}(X=x)=P(X=x)=p_x\geq p_{\min}>0,
 \tag{1}
\]
so conditioning on this stratum is well defined. By Assumption \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), the maps \(g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1]\) are jointly Borel measurable and there is one \(P^{\mathrm F}\)-probability-one event on which, simultaneously for every \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Y(a)=g_X(a,U),
 \qquad
 Y=g_X(A,U).
 \tag{2}
\]
The single event in (2) permits evaluation at the random argument \(A\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\) states \(A\perp\!\!\!\perp U\mid X\). Because every stratum has positive mass by (1), the defining factorization for conditional independence gives
\[
 P^{\mathrm F}_{(A,U)\mid X=x}
 =P^{\mathrm F}_{A\mid X=x}\otimes P^{\mathrm F}_{U\mid X=x}.
 \tag{3}
\]
All integrands below are bounded by one, so their expectations and product integrals are finite.

Define
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)
 =\int_{\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,
   P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm du\mid X=x)
 =\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[g_x(a,U)\mid X=x].
 \tag{4}
\]
For a Borel set \(B\subseteq[0,1]\), equations (2)--(4), followed by Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 &=\int_{B\times\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,
   \{P^{\mathrm F}_{A\mid X=x}\otimes P^{\mathrm F}_{U\mid X=x}\}
   (\mathrm da,\mathrm du)\\
 &=\int_B\left\{\int_{\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,
   P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm du\mid X=x)\right\}
   P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm da\mid X=x)\\
 &=\int_Bm_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The first equality is the definition of expectation under the conditional law, the second is the defining integration identity for its product form in (3), and the third uses (4) and the conditional-density identity. Thus no density for \(U\) is required.

We next identify the pointwise structural response mean rather than assume it. From (2), (4), the triangle inequality, and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\), for every \(s\in[0,1]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |m_x^{\mathrm F}(t)-m_x^{\mathrm F}(s)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}
   [|g_x(t,U)-g_x(s,U)|\mid X=x]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}
   [|Y(t)-Y(s)|\mid X=x]
 \longrightarrow0
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
as \(t\to s\). Hence \(m_x^{\mathrm F}\) is continuous on \([0,1]\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\) says that \(\mu_x^P\) is continuous and is an almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\). Therefore Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) gives, for every Borel \(B\subseteq[0,1]\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 =\int_B\mu_x^P(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
 \tag{7}
\]
Since \(P\) is the observed margin of \(P^{\mathrm F}\), subtracting (7) from (5) gives
\[
 \int_B\{m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)-\mu_x^P(a)\}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da=0
 \quad\text{for every Borel }B\subseteq[0,1].
 \tag{8}
\]
Put \(f=m_x^{\mathrm F}-\mu_x^P\). The function \(f\) is continuous by (6) and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), so the sets below are Borel. For each integer \(j\geq1\), equation (8) with \(B=\{f\geq1/j\}\) gives
\[
 0=\int_{\{f\geq1/j\}}f(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \geq \frac1j\int_{\{f\geq1/j\}}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
 \tag{9}
\]
Using \(B=\{f\leq-1/j\}\) and multiplying the resulting integral by \(-1\) gives the analogous conclusion for that set. Since \(\pi_x\geq0\), (9) and its negative counterpart imply
\[
 f(a)=0
 \quad\text{for }\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\text{-almost every }a.
 \tag{10}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), together with \(c_->0\) and \(\kappa\geq0\), states that
\[
 \pi_x(a)\geq c_-a^\kappa>0
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in(0,1].
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus (10)--(11) imply \(f=0\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \((0,1]\). If \(f(a_0)\neq0\) for some \(a_0\in[0,1]\), continuity would give a relative neighborhood \(I\) of \(a_0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(|f|\geq\varepsilon\) throughout \(I\). But \(I\cap(0,1]\) has positive Lebesgue measure, contradicting the preceding almost-everywhere equality. Consequently,
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_x^P(a)
 \quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1].
 \tag{12}
\]
In particular, (12) reaches the boundary \(a=0\) by continuity; equality there was not inserted through a choice of conditional-expectation version.

Fix now \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\). Definition \(\texttt{def:clamp-policy}\) and the simultaneous identities (2) give on the same probability-one event
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Y\{d_\delta(A)\}
 &=g_X\{\max(A,\delta),U\}\\
 &=g_X(A,U)\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}
   +g_X(\delta,U)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\\
 &=Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}
   +Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
For the second term, the product factorization (3), equation (4), equation (12), and Proposition \(\texttt{prop:pushforward-setup}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\mid X=x]
 &=m_x^{\mathrm F}(\delta)
   P^{\mathrm F}(A\leq\delta\mid X=x)\\
 &=\mu_x^P(\delta)q_x(\delta).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Taking expectations in (13), applying (14), using the observed-margin identity for the retained natural-course term, and summing over the finite set \(\mathcal X\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})
 &=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\{d_\delta(A)\}]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
   +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_x^P(\delta)\\
 &=\theta_\delta(P),
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
where the first and last equalities are the definitions of \(\psi_\delta\) and \(\theta_\delta\). Proposition \(\texttt{prop:policy-support}\) proves separately that \(d_\delta(A)\) belongs almost surely to the support of the conditional natural-treatment law. At \(\delta=0\), Definition \(\texttt{def:clamp-policy}\) is the identity and Proposition \(\texttt{prop:pushforward-setup}\) gives \(q_x(0)=0\); at \(\delta=\bar\delta\), all arguments remain inside \([0,1]\). Thus (5), (12), (13), (15), and the support proposition establish every asserted conclusion for the full closed threshold range.
