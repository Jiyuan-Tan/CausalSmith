Write
\[
 D=\beta\kappa+2\beta+\kappa+1,
 \qquad
 e_n=\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n},
 \qquad
 c_n=\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}.
\]
The definitions give
\[
 \frac{c_n}{e_n}
 =n^{1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)-1/(2D)}\longrightarrow\infty, \tag{39}
\]
because \(2D-(2\beta+\kappa+1)=2\beta\kappa+2\beta+\kappa+1>0\).

When \(\delta_n\gtrsim e_n\), Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} and its balance equation give, up to constants,
\[
 \delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta
 \asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}
 \delta_n^{D/(2\beta+1)}. \tag{40}
\]
At \(\delta_n=c_n\), the right side equals \(n^{-1/2}\), since \(c_n^D=n^{-1/2}\).  When \(\delta_n=O(e_n)\), the same lemma gives
\[
 \delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta
 \leq C e_n^{\beta+\kappa+1}
 =o(n^{-1/2}), \tag{41}
\]
because \((\beta+\kappa+1)/(2\beta+\kappa+1)>1/2\).

Equations (39)--(41) settle all moving-threshold cases.  If \(\delta_n/c_n\to0\), the atom term is \(o(n^{-1/2})\), whether \(\delta_n=O(e_n)\) by (41) or \(\delta_n\gtrsim e_n\) by (40); thus \(r_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\).  If \(\delta_n/c_n\to c\in(0,\infty)\), then (39) places the sequence above the edge scale and (40) makes the atom term of order \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(\delta_n/c_n\to\infty\) while \(\delta_n\to0\), then again \(\delta_n/e_n\to\infty\), and (40) dominates the root-\(n\) term, giving
\[
 r_n\asymp\delta_n^{\kappa+1}(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
If \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} gives \(h_n\asymp n^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\); the atom term then dominates and \(r_n\asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}\).  Theorem \texttt{thm:minimax-risk} and Theorem \texttt{thm:honest-length} transfer each of these comparisons to both minimax risk and minimax honest expected length.

Finally, when \(\delta_n=0\), Proposition \texttt{prop:pushforward-setup} gives \(q_x(0)=0\).  Conditional absolute continuity gives \(P(A=0)=0\), so the definition of \(\theta_0\) yields
\[
 \theta_0(P)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>0\}]=\mathbb E_PY.
\]
In Definition \texttt{def:total-gram-estimator}, \(\widehat b_x=0\) almost surely and \(\widehat\nu_n\) is the bounded sample mean on \(I_0\); the remaining interval padding is of root-\(n\) order by (35).  Hence the construction is bounded-mean inference at rate \(n^{-1/2}\), exactly as asserted.
