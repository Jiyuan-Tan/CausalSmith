Put \(\delta=\delta_n\), \(h=h_n\), and \(b_x=p_xq_x(\delta)\).  Proposition \texttt{prop:pushforward-setup} gives
\[
 0\leq b_x\leq C\delta^{\kappa+1}. \tag{12}
\]
We first prove the upper bound for the explicit estimator in Definition \texttt{def:total-gram-estimator}.  Since all three blocks have order \(n\), Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling} and the variance bound for a \([0,1]\)-valued variable give
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|\leq Cn^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat b_x-b_x|\leq Cn^{-1/2}. \tag{13}
\]

On \(\Omega_x\), let \(S_x=\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}Y_i\).  By the polynomial reproduction identity in Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram}, the Taylor polynomial of \(\mu_x^P\) at \(\delta\) contributes exactly \(\mu_x^P(\delta)\).  Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression} and the weight bounds in that lemma therefore imply, conditionally on the realized \((X_i,A_i)\)'s,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathbb E_P[S_x\mid (X_i,A_i)_{i\in I_2}]-\mu_x^P(\delta)\right|
 &\leq Lh^\beta\sum_i|w_{ix}|\leq Ch^\beta,\\
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_x\mid (X_i,A_i)_{i\in I_2})
 &\leq\frac14\sum_iw_{ix}^2\leq\frac{C}{N_x}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{14}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance from \(\mu_x^P(\delta)\in[0,1]\).  If \(\mu_{N,x}=\mathbb E_PN_x\), a scalar exponential-Markov calculation for the binomial count gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[N_x^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N_x>0\}\right]
 \leq (2/\mu_{N,x})^{1/2}+P(N_x<\mu_{N,x}/2)
 \leq C\mu_{N,x}^{-1/2}. \tag{15}
\]
Equations (2), (4), and (5) from the preceding lemmas give \(\mu_{N,x}^{-1/2}\leq Ch^\beta\).  On \(\Omega_x^c\), both the fallback and the true regression value lie in \([0,1]\); Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} and the elementary bound \(e^{-ct}\leq C_ct^{-1/2}\), with \(t=h^{-2\beta}\), show that its expected contribution is at most \(Ch^\beta\).  Thus (14)--(15) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\mu_x-\mu_x^P(\delta)|\leq Ch^\beta. \tag{16}
\]

The three blocks are independent.  Before the final projection, add and subtract \(b_x\widetilde\mu_x\), use \(0\leq\widetilde\mu_x\leq1\), and apply (12)--(16):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_n-\theta_\delta(P)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|
 +\sum_x\left\{\mathbb E_P|\widehat b_x-b_x|
 +b_x\mathbb E_P|\widetilde\mu_x-\mu_x^P(\delta)|\right\}\\
 &\leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}=Cr_n.
\end{aligned} \tag{17}
\]
The first inequality remains valid after projection because \(\theta_\delta(P)\in[0,1]\).  Taking the supremum proves the minimax upper bound with one data-driven estimator.

For the converse, we give two explicit same-class experiments.  In both, take \(p_x=1/J\) and \(\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\) for every \(x\).  These choices obey Assumptions \texttt{ass:stratum-mass} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning} because \(p_{\min}\leq1/J\) and \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\).  First let the conditional outcome be Bernoulli with constant mean \(1/2\) under \(P_0\) and \(1/2+a_0n^{-1/2}\) under \(P_1\), for a fixed sufficiently small \(a_0>0\).  Both regressions satisfy Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression}, and the definition of \(\theta_\delta\) gives
\[
 |\theta_\delta(P_1)-\theta_\delta(P_0)|=a_0n^{-1/2}. \tag{18}
\]
The one-observation chi-square divergence is \(4a_0^2/n\), hence
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+4a_0^2/n)^n-1\leq e^{4a_0^2}-1. \tag{19}
\]

For the localized experiment, choose a nonnegative \(C^\infty\) function \(\varphi\), supported on \([-1,1]\), with \(\varphi(0)=1\).  The scaled functions \(h^\beta\varphi((\,cdot-\delta)/h)\), restricted to \([0,1]\), have Taylor-remainder Hölder norm at most a finite constant \(C_{\varphi,\beta}\): this follows directly by scaling the Taylor remainder when \(\beta\) is an integer and by scaling the Hölder modulus of the highest derivative otherwise.  Set
\[
 \mu_0(a)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(a)=\frac12+a_1h^\beta\varphi((a-\delta)/h), \tag{20}
\]
for every stratum, where \(a_1>0\) is small enough that \(a_1C_{\varphi,\beta}\leq L\) and \(a_1h^\beta\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty\leq1/4\).  Taking Bernoulli outcomes with these means produces two members of the same \(\mathcal M\).  Their one-observation chi-square divergence satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(P_1,P_0)
 &=4\int_0^1\{\mu_1(a)-\mu_0(a)\}^2(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da\\
 &\leq Ca_1^2h^{2\beta+1}(\delta+h)^\kappa.
 \end{aligned} \tag{21}
\]
Definition \texttt{def:bandwidth} and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} make \(n\) times the last expression at most \(Ca_1^2\).  Thus
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq \exp(Ca_1^2)-1. \tag{22}
\]
Since the perturbation is nonnegative and \(q_x(\delta)=\delta^{\kappa+1}\) for this design, the observed functional has separation
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta_\delta(P_1)-\theta_\delta(P_0)
 &=\int_\delta^1\{\mu_1(a)-\mu_0(a)\}(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da
   +\delta^{\kappa+1}\{\mu_1(\delta)-\mu_0(\delta)\}\\
 &\geq a_1\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{23}
\]

For completeness, if \(Q_1\ll Q_0\) with likelihood ratio \(Z\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives for every event \(E\)
\[
 |Q_1(E)-Q_0(E)|=|\mathbb E_{Q_0}[(Z-1)\mathbf 1_E]|
 \leq\{\mathbb E_{Q_0}(Z-1)^2\}^{1/2}=\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)}. \tag{24}
\]
Choose \(a_0,a_1\) so that the right sides of (19) and (22) have square roots at most \(1/2\).  For any estimator \(T\) and either pair, let \(\Delta\) denote its target separation and let \(E=\{|T-\theta(P_0)|<\Delta/2\}\).  Then
\[
 \max_{j=0,1}\mathbb E_{P_j}|T-\theta(P_j)|
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{4}\{P_0^{\otimes n}(E^c)+P_1^{\otimes n}(E)\}
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{4}(1-1/2). \tag{25}
\]
Equations (18) and (23), applied separately and then maximized, show
\[
 R_n^\star\geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}
 \geq \frac c2\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}=c'r_n. \tag{26}
\]
Together, (17) and (26) prove the theorem and explicitly exhibit the announced global-shift and localized-Bernoulli mechanisms.
