Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\).  We first construct a full-data lift and then prove the two experiment identities.

Let \(Z=(X,A)\).  The spaces \(\mathcal X\times[0,1]\) and \([0,1]\) are standard Borel because \(\mathcal X\) is finite.  By \(\texttt{lem:standard-borel-regular-conditional-law}\), there is a Borel probability kernel \(K\) from \(\mathcal X\times[0,1]\) to \([0,1]\) that is a regular conditional law of \(Y\) given \(Z\).  Put \(K_x(a,C)=K((x,a),C)\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) gives, for Borel \(B,C\subseteq[0,1]\),
\[
 P\{X=x,A\in B,Y\in C\}
 =p_x\int_B K_x(a,C)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
 \tag{1}
\]
Define the Borel function
\[
 m_x^0(a)=\int_{[0,1]}y\,K_x(a,\mathrm dy).
\]
The kernel-integration map is Borel: first this holds for indicator functions by the definition of a Borel kernel, then for nonnegative simple functions, and finally for the bounded identity function by monotone approximation.  The function \(m_x^0\) is a version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\).  By \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), \(\mu_x^P\) is another such version and is continuous.  Hence
\[
 E_x=\{a\in[0,\bar\delta]:m_x^0(a)\ne\mu_x^P(a)\}
 \tag{2}
\]
is Borel and has zero conditional exposure probability.  More explicitly, \(p_x>0\) by \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\), so uniqueness of conditional expectations and \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) imply
\[
 \int_{E_x}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da=0.
 \tag{3}
\]

Modify the kernel only on this exposure-null set:
\[
 K_x^\star(a,C)
 =\mathbf 1\{a\notin E_x\}K_x(a,C)
  +\mathbf 1\{a\in E_x\}\mathbf 1\{\mu_x^P(a)\in C\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For every Borel \(C\), the right side of (4) is Borel in \(a\), and for every \(a\) it is a probability measure in \(C\); hence \(K_x^\star\) is a Borel probability kernel.  Equations (2)--(4) give
\[
 \int y\,K_x^\star(a,\mathrm dy)=\mu_x^P(a)
 \quad\text{for every }a\in[0,\bar\delta],
 \tag{5}
\]
while (3) gives, for every Borel \(B,C\subseteq[0,1]\),
\[
 p_x\int_BK_x^\star(a,C)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 =p_x\int_BK_x(a,C)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
 \tag{6}
\]

We next realize \(K_x^\star\) without invoking an unrecorded measurable-selection step.  Let \(D=\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\), including \(1\), and set
\[
 F_x(a,t)=K_x^\star(a,[0,t]),
 \qquad
 g_x(a,u)=\inf\{r\in D:F_x(a,r)\geq u\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The set in (7) is nonempty because \(F_x(a,1)=1\).  For every real \(t\),
\[
 \{(a,u):g_x(a,u)<t\}
 =\bigcup_{\substack{r\in D\\r<t}}
   \{(a,u):u\leq F_x(a,r)\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Each set on the right is Borel because \(a\mapsto K_x^\star(a,[0,r])\) is Borel; the union is countable.  Thus \(g_x\) is jointly Borel.  For fixed \(a\), if \(t_j\downarrow t\), then \([0,t_j]\downarrow[0,t]\), so continuity from above of the probability measure \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\) gives \(F_x(a,t_j)\downarrow F_x(a,t)\).  This right continuity shows that
\[
 \{u\in[0,1]:g_x(a,u)\leq t\}
 =\{u\in[0,1]:u\leq F_x(a,t)\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, if \(u\leq F_x(a,t)\), rationals decreasing to \(t\) show \(g_x(a,u)\leq t\).  Conversely, if \(u>F_x(a,t)\), right continuity supplies \(s>t\) with \(F_x(a,s)<u\), so no rational at most \(s\) qualifies in (7), and \(g_x(a,u)>t\).  Therefore, for Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\),
\[
 \lambda\{u:g_x(a,u)\leq t\}=F_x(a,t).
 \tag{10}
\]
The intervals \([0,t]\) generate \(\mathcal B([0,1])\), so (10) proves that the pushforward of \(\lambda\) by \(u\mapsto g_x(a,u)\) is exactly \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\).

On the probability space carrying a draw \((X,A)\) from the \((X,A)\)-margin of \(P\) and an independent \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), define simultaneously for every \(a\in[0,1]\)
\[
 Y(a)=g_X(a,U),
 \qquad
 Y=g_X(A,U).
 \tag{11}
\]
Because \(\mathcal X\) is finite and every \(g_x\) is jointly Borel, \((a,\omega)\mapsto Y(a,\omega)\) is jointly measurable.  Equation (11) verifies \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) for all \(a\) on one event of probability one.  Independence of \(U\) from \((X,A)\) gives \(A\perp U\mid X\), which is \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\).  By (10), the conditional law of the last variable in (11), given \((X,A)=(x,a)\), is \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\).  Combining this fact with (1) and (6), first on rectangles and then on the generated product Borel sigma-field, shows that the observed \((X,A,Y)\)-law is exactly \(P\).

Finally, independence of \(U\) and \(X\), (5), and (10) give
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)
 =\mathbb E[g_x(a,U)\mid X=x]
 =\int_0^1g_x(a,u)\,\mathrm du
 =\int y\,K_x^\star(a,\mathrm dy)
 =\mu_x^P(a)
 \tag{12}
\]
for every \(a\in[0,\bar\delta]\).  The last function is continuous there by \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), so \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\) holds.  Thus (11), with the member-specific standard Borel carrier \(\mathcal U=[0,1]\), defines a member \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) by \(\texttt{def:full-data-model-class}\), and its observed margin is \(P\).  This establishes surjectivity without imposing a common carrier or a carrier-richness condition on \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\).

Let \(\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\) denote the observed margin.  What was just proved is
\[
 \Gamma(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F})=\mathcal M.
 \tag{13}
\]
Proposition \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\) gives, for every member and every declared threshold,
\[
 \psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})
 =\theta_{\delta_n}\{\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
If \(T\) is any estimator measurable with respect to the observed sample, its sample law under \((P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\) is \(\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\).  Hence (13)--(14) imply
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 |T-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})|
 =
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}|T-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)|.
 \tag{15}
\]
The estimator classes on the two sides are the same class of measurable maps of \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\).  Taking the infimum in (15), and using \(\texttt{def:causal-frontier-criteria}\) and the definition of \(R_n^\star\), yields
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=R_n^\star.
 \tag{16}
\]

For any observed-sample interval \(C_n\), the same two identities give both
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 (P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in C_n\}
 =
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P^{\otimes n}
 \{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in C_n\}
 \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 =
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)].
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus the coverage constraints select exactly the same intervals, and their worst-case expected-length objectives coincide.  Taking the infimum in (18) over the common feasible class in (17) proves
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=L_n^\star.
 \tag{19}
\]
Equations (13), (16), and (19) prove all claims.
