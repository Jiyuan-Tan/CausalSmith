Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\), and let \(P\in\mathcal M\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin.  Proposition \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\) gives
\[
 \psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})=\theta_{\delta_n}(P)
 \tag{14}
\]
for every declared deterministic threshold.  Both \(\widehat\theta_n\) and \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) are measurable functions only of the observed sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), by Definitions \(\texttt{def:total-gram-estimator}\) and \(\texttt{def:honest-interval}\).  Therefore their joint laws with the observed sample under \((P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\) equal their laws under \(P^{\otimes n}\).  Using (14), Definition \(\texttt{def:causal-frontier-criteria}\), Definition \(\texttt{def:full-data-model-class}\), and Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 &\leq \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 |\widehat\theta_n-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})|\\
 &\leq \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}|\widehat\theta_n-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)|
 \leq C r_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The middle inequality holds because every member of \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) has an observed margin in \(\mathcal M\).  In the same way, Theorem \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) and (14) give
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in\mathrm{CI}_n\}\geq1-\alpha
 \tag{16}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 [\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]\leq C r_n.
 \tag{17}
\]
These bounds are uniform over the carrier-heterogeneous class because the displayed quantities depend on a full-data member only through its observed margin, while the constants in the two observed-data theorems depend only on the declared model constants.

It remains to show that the observed lower-bound experiments used by the established Theorems \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\) and \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) also occur inside \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\).  Index the global Bernoulli-shift pair and localized Bernoulli-regression pair asserted in Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\) by \(s\in\{\mathrm{glob},\mathrm{loc}\}\), and write their laws as \(P_{j,s,n}\in\mathcal M\), \(j\in\{0,1\}\).  Write \(\mu_{j,s,n,x}(a)\) for the continuous Bernoulli regression in stratum \(x\).  The established constructions have target separations
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{glob},n}\geq c_0n^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\mathrm{loc},n}\geq c_0\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta,
 \tag{18}
\]
and their fixed perturbation amplitudes may be, and in the honest-length proof are, chosen so that the product total-variation distances are bounded strictly below \(1-2\alpha\).  Shrinking a fixed amplitude if necessary changes only \(c_0>0\), not either rate in (18).  Thus there are constants \(\eta>0\) and \(\eta_\alpha>0\), independent of \(n\), such that for both pairs, with \(Q_{j,s,n}=P_{j,s,n}^{\otimes n}\),
\[
 1-\|Q_{0,s,n}-Q_{1,s,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\eta,
 \qquad
 1-2\alpha-\|Q_{0,s,n}-Q_{1,s,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\eta_\alpha.
 \tag{19}
\]
These are precisely the testing-affinity properties of the already-established same-class two-point lower-bound constructions; no new restriction of \(\mathcal M\) is introduced here.

For each \((j,s,n)\), construct a full-data lift as follows.  Use the standard Borel carrier \(\mathcal U=[0,1]\); draw \((X,A)\) according to its \(P_{j,s,n}\)-marginal; draw \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) independently of \((X,A)\); and set
\[
 g_{j,s,n,x}(a,u)=\mathbf 1\{u\leq\mu_{j,s,n,x}(a)\},
 \qquad
 Y_{j,s,n}(a)=g_{j,s,n,X}(a,U),
 \qquad
 Y_{j,s,n}=g_{j,s,n,X}(A,U).
 \tag{20}
\]
Since \(\mu_{j,s,n,x}\) is continuous by the Bernoulli constructions and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), the subgraph in (20) is Borel.  Because \(\mathcal X\) is finite, \((a,u,x)\mapsto g_{j,s,n,x}(a,u)\) and \((a,\omega)\mapsto Y_{j,s,n}(a,\omega)\) are jointly measurable.  The last two equalities in (20) verify Assumption \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) simultaneously for all \(a\), without dose-dependent exceptional sets.  Independence of \(U\) from \((X,A)\) implies Assumption \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\).  Moreover, for every \(x\) and \(a\),
\[
 m_{j,s,n,x}^{\mathrm F}(a)
 =\int_0^1\mathbf 1\{u\leq\mu_{j,s,n,x}(a)\}\,\mathrm du
 =\mu_{j,s,n,x}(a).
 \tag{21}
\]
The right-hand side is continuous on \([0,1]\), hence in particular on \([0,\bar\delta]\), which verifies exactly Assumption \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\).  Conditional on \((A=a,X=x)\), equation (20) makes \(Y_{j,s,n}\) Bernoulli with success probability \(\mu_{j,s,n,x}(a)\).  Therefore the observed margin of the lift is exactly \(P_{j,s,n}\).  Definition \(\texttt{def:full-data-model-class}\) now shows that every lifted law, denoted \(P^{\mathrm F}_{j,s,n}\), belongs to \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\).  This construction uses one admissible carrier for the converse witnesses; it does not impose a common carrier on the class.

By Proposition \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\), the causal target separation of each lifted pair equals the corresponding observed target separation in (18).  Its observed product laws are exactly \(Q_{0,s,n}\) and \(Q_{1,s,n}\), so (19) is unchanged.  For completeness, the requisite two-point risk inequality follows directly.  If \(T\) is any observed-sample estimator, \(\vartheta_j\) are the two target values, \(\nu=Q_0+Q_1\), and \(q_j=\mathrm dQ_j/\mathrm d\nu\), define \(Q_0\wedge Q_1\) by \(\mathrm d(Q_0\wedge Q_1)=\min(q_0,q_1)\,\mathrm d\nu\).  Then \((Q_0\wedge Q_1)(O^n)=1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\), and the triangle inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}|T-\vartheta_0|+
 \mathbb E_{Q_1}|T-\vartheta_1|
 &\geq\int\{|T-\vartheta_0|+|T-\vartheta_1|\}\,\mathrm d(Q_0\wedge Q_1)\\
 &\geq |\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|
 \{1-\|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Thus the maximum risk on either lifted pair is at least \(\eta\Delta_{s,n}/2\).  Taking first the maximum over the two pairs and then the infimum over all observed-sample estimators yields
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\}
 \geq \frac c2 r_n.
 \tag{23}
\]

Likewise, let \(C_n\) be any observed-sample interval with coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) under every member of \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\).  On either lifted pair, total variation and the union bound imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\in C_n\}
 &\geq Q_0\{\vartheta_0\in C_n\}+Q_0\{\vartheta_1\in C_n\}-1\\
 &\geq (1-\alpha)+(1-\alpha-|Q_0-Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}})-1\\
 &\geq\eta_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Whenever both endpoints belong to an interval, its length is at least \(|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\).  Hence (24) gives \(\mathbb E_{Q_0}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]\geq\eta_\alpha\Delta_{s,n}\).  Maximizing over the two pairs and infimizing over all uniformly honest intervals gives
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\}
 \geq \frac c2 r_n.
 \tag{25}
\]
Equations (15)--(17), (23), and (25) prove the risk, coverage, and expected-length conclusions, including \(R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\asymp L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\asymp r_n\), with the displayed total-Gram estimator and bias-aware interval attaining the upper bounds.
