Again write \(\delta=\delta_n\), \(h=h_n\), and \(b_x=p_xq_x(\delta)\).  Consider the following simultaneous events:
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_0&=\{|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|\leq b_0\},\\
 E_{1x}&=\{|\widehat b_x-b_x|\leq b_1\},\\
 E_{2x}&=\Omega_x^c\ \cup\ 
 \left\{\left|\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}Y_i-\mu_x^P(\delta)\right|\leq B_x\right\}.
 \end{split} \tag{27}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling}, boundedness, and the exponential-Markov argument for a bounded sample mean give
\[
 P(E_0^c)\leq2e^{-2|I_0|b_0^2}=\frac{\alpha}{6J},
 \qquad
 P(E_{1x}^c)\leq2e^{-2|I_1|b_1^2}=\frac{\alpha}{6J}. \tag{28}
\]
On \(\Omega_x\), condition on the realized treatment design in \(I_2\).  The outcome residuals are independent, have conditional mean zero for almost every realized design point by the defining version property of \(\mu_x^P\), and each weighted residual has range length \(|w_{ix}|\).  Hence
\[
 P\left(\left.\left|\sum_iw_{ix}\{Y_i-\mu_x^P(A_i)\}\right|>
 t_\alpha\Bigl(\sum_iw_{ix}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\,\right|(X_i,A_i)_{i\in I_2}\right)
 \leq2e^{-2t_\alpha^2}=\frac{\alpha}{6J}. \tag{29}
\]
Polynomial reproduction from Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} and Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression} bound the conditional bias by \(Lh^\beta\sum_i|w_{ix}|\).  Thus (29) proves \(P(E_{2x}^c)\leq\alpha/(6J)\).  A union bound gives
\[
 P\left(E_0\cap\bigcap_x(E_{1x}\cap E_{2x})\right)
 \geq1-\frac{(2J+1)\alpha}{6J}\geq1-\alpha. \tag{30}
\]

On the event in (30), projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot enlarge the local regression error.  If \(\Omega_x\) occurs, then
\[
 |\widehat b_x\widetilde\mu_x-b_x\mu_x^P(\delta)|
 \leq b_1+(\widehat b_x+b_1)B_x=D_x. \tag{31}
\]
If \(\Omega_x^c\) occurs, the two products belong respectively to \([0,\widehat b_x]\) and \([0,b_x]\); therefore
\[
 |\widehat b_x\widetilde\mu_x-b_x\mu_x^P(\delta)|
 \leq\max\{\widehat b_x,b_x\}
 \leq\widehat b_x+b_1=D_x. \tag{32}
\]
Combining (31)--(32) with \(E_0\), and using contraction of the final projection about \(\theta_\delta(P)\in[0,1]\), yields
\[
 |\widehat\theta_n-\theta_\delta(P)|\leq b_0+\sum_xD_x. \tag{33}
\]
Definitions \texttt{def:total-gram-estimator} and \texttt{def:honest-interval} now show that (33) is exactly the event \(\theta_\delta(P)\in\mathrm{CI}_n\).  Equation (30) proves uniform finite-sample honesty for every sufficiently large \(n\).

We next bound expected length, including the singular-Gram realizations.  The inverse-binomial estimate (15) and Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_x\mathbf 1\{\Omega_x\}]
 \leq CLh^\beta+Ct_\alpha\mathbb E_P[N_x^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N_x>0\}]
 \leq Ch^\beta. \tag{34}
\]
The split makes \(\widehat b_x\) independent of \(B_x\) and \(\Omega_x\), while \(\mathbb E_P\widehat b_x=b_x\).  Therefore the two branches in Definition \texttt{def:honest-interval} give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_PD_x
 &\leq(b_x+b_1)\mathbb E_P[B_x\mathbf 1\{\Omega_x\}]+b_1
 +(b_x+b_1)P(\Omega_x^c)\\
 &\leq C\{n^{-1/2}+b_xh^\beta\}\\
 &\leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{35}
\]
Here Proposition \texttt{prop:pushforward-setup} bounds \(b_x\), Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} bounds the bad-event probability by \(Ce^{-ch^{-2\beta}}\), and \(e^{-ch^{-2\beta}}\leq Ch^\beta\).  Intersecting with \([0,1]\) cannot increase length, so (35), the fixed value of \(J\), and \(b_0=O(n^{-1/2})\) yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]
 \leq2b_0+2\sum_x\mathbb E_PD_x
 \leq Cr_n. \tag{36}
\]

It remains to prove the converse for every uniformly honest interval \(C_n\).  Reuse the two explicit same-class pairs constructed in the proof of Theorem \texttt{thm:minimax-risk}, decreasing their fixed perturbation amplitudes if necessary so that, by (19), (22), and (24), their product-law total variation is at most some \(\eta<(1-2\alpha)/2\).  Let their two target values be \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), with separation \(\Delta\).  Uniform coverage implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_0^{\otimes n}\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in C_n\}
 &\geq P_0^{\otimes n}\{\theta_0\in C_n\}
   +P_0^{\otimes n}\{\theta_1\in C_n\}-1\\
 &\geq(1-\alpha)+(1-\alpha-\eta)-1
 =1-2\alpha-\eta>0. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{37}
\]
On this event an interval has length at least \(\Delta\), and hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 \geq \Delta(1-2\alpha-\eta). \tag{38}
\]
For the global pair, \(\Delta\asymp n^{-1/2}\); for the localized pair, \(\Delta\geq c\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\).  Taking the maximum of the two lower bounds and using \(\max(u,v)\geq(u+v)/2\) proves \(L_n^\star\geq cr_n\).  Together with (30) and (36), this proves all three claims, including the atom-weighted control of singular-Gram realizations.
