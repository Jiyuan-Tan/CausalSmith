For a probability measure on \([0,1]\), call \(a\) a support point when every relatively open neighborhood of \(a\) has positive probability.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).  If \(V\) is any relatively open neighborhood of any \(a\in[0,1]\), then \(V\) contains a nondegenerate interval \(I\subseteq[0,1]\).  Assumptions \texttt{ass:conditional-density-law} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning} imply
\[
 P(A\in V\mid X=x)
 \geq \int_I\pi_x(t)\,\mathrm dt
 \geq c_-\int_I t^\kappa\,\mathrm dt>0.
\]
The last strict inequality holds also when the closure of \(I\) meets zero, because \(t^\kappa>0\) for every \(t>0\).  Thus the conditional natural-treatment support is all of \([0,1]\).  Definition \texttt{def:clamp-policy} gives \(d_\delta(A)\in[0,1]\) almost surely, so \(d_\delta(A)\) belongs to that support almost surely.  In particular, at \(\delta=0\) the map is the identity; when \(\delta>0\), the preceding display applied to every neighborhood of \(\delta\) verifies the claimed boundary support directly.
