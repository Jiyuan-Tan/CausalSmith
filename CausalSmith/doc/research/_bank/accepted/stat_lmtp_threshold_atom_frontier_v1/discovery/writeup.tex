\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}lmtp\_\allowbreak{}threshold\_\allowbreak{}atom\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The primary novel result is the matched minimax-risk and uniformly honest expected-length frontier \(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\) for the observed-data clamp functional \(\theta_{\delta_n}(P)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta_n\}]+\sum_xp_xq_x(\delta_n)\mu_x^P(\delta_n)\), where \(n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\asymp1\). On the fixed finite-stratum Hölder model with the explicit treatment-assignment envelope \(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\), a total-Gram-stabilized local polynomial, an atom-specific singular-design fallback, and same-class global-shift and localized-Bernoulli lower bounds yield the regular, critical, atom-dominated, and fixed-threshold regimes. A purpose-built full-data class satisfying simultaneous consistency, conditional exchangeability, and fiberwise structural-mean continuity maps onto the observed class; the causal bridge and this surjection make the causal risk and honest-length theorem an exact transport corollary of the observed frontier, not an independently novel frontier. On the continuity-only class, the matched rate is \(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\), with elbow \(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/[2(\kappa+1)]}\) and fixed-positive-threshold impossibility. Low's 1997 general expected-length inequality and nonadaptation corollary are the closest generic honest-inference antecedent; the contribution here is the explicit threshold-atom scaling, matched moving-threshold risk and length, and causal transport for the exact unsmoothed clamp consumed by the official \(\texttt{lmtp}\) user-policy workflow.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published LMTP identification covers the deterministic clamp, but its continuous-exposure efficiency theory does not provide rates for that non-pathwise-differentiable target. Low's 1997 theorem gives a general lower-bound inequality for the expected length of intervals honest over a distribution family and derives nonadaptation across derivative-smoothness density classes; it does not analyze a threshold-generated atom, polynomial thinning, or a deterministic moving threshold. McCoy et al. correctly accommodate clipping mass through an appropriate dominating measure and a classification estimator of the pushforward law, but their stochastic-shift effect-modifier target and high-probability regularity theory do not provide the deterministic moving-clamp minimax or uniformly honest expected-length frontier. Degenerate-design regression theory treats an unweighted fixed point, and existing bias-aware confidence-interval theory does not integrate this atom-weighted random-design problem.

\paragraph{Niche.} The unoccupied object is the observed functional \(\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\) induced by the exact unsmoothed clamp under polynomial generalized-propensity thinning, with a threshold that may follow any deterministic sequence and with inference honest across the transition where the shrinking atom becomes first-order. The associated causal statement is obtained by an explicit bridge and an onto full-data lift, so its scope is exactly the paper-defined structural class.

\paragraph{Fill.} The known mixed-measure representation fixes the observed functional \(\theta_\delta(P)\); the explicit global measurable polynomial envelope defines the treatment-assignment class; the information-balance bandwidth and total-Gram lemma give an estimator on that class; two same-class experiments certify the minimax risk and confidence-length order; and the phase diagram converts those bounds into policy-facing regimes. The continuity-only theorem separately derives the atom-mass rate, elbow, and fixed-threshold impossibility rather than claiming the generic absence of honest adaptation as new. Finally, the causal bridge and surjective observed-margin construction transport the observed frontier exactly to the purpose-built full-data class for every \(n\).

\section{Related work}

Díaz, Williams, Hoffman, and Schenck identify threshold-LMTP means by sequential regression and show that the continuous threshold example is not pathwise differentiable. Their result supplies the identification template and standard causal premises, but it does not derive a deterministic moving-threshold minimax-risk or honest expected-length frontier. The present paper's full-data class additionally imposes fiberwise structural-mean continuity; together with consistency, conditional exchangeability, the continuous observed regression, and polynomial support, the proved causal bridge yields pointwise target equality. The separately proved observed-margin surjection then makes the causal frontier theorem a transport corollary of the novel observed-data frontier, not an independent causal-frontier discovery. Hoffman et al. repeat the piecewise-invertibility boundary in their applied tutorial, and Williams and Díaz document the \(\texttt{lmtp}\) user-policy workflow that consumes the exact unsmoothed policy specification. McCoy et al.'s MTP density ratio is a Radon--Nikodym derivative with respect to an appropriate dominating measure, expressly allowing clipping-induced boundary mass, and their classification estimator targets the clipped pushforward law directly. Their object is a stochastic-shift effect-modifier target, and their high-probability regularity theory does not deliver the minimax risk or uniformly honest expected-length frontier for the deterministic clamp with an arbitrary moving threshold. For generic honest nonparametric inference, Low (1997), \(\textit{On nonparametric confidence intervals}\), proves a lower-bound inequality for expected interval length under prescribed uniform coverage and derives as a corollary that adaptation across derivative-smoothness density classes cannot improve the interval-length rate over minimax fixed-length intervals while retaining uniform coverage. That antecedent already establishes the generic nonadaptation obstruction; the continuity-only theorem here instead derives the explicit matched clamp-functional rate \(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\), its moving-threshold elbow, and fixed-positive-threshold impossibility from the threshold-generated atom mass. Gaiffas derives the fixed-point regression rate and stabilized local polynomials under regularly varying degenerate random design. The global measurable envelope \(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\) used here is an explicit treatment-assignment class, not a consequence attributed to regular variation; together with the exact conditional-density identity for every Borel set, it permits bounded measurable deviations from exact power behavior while retaining controlled boundary thinning. Armstrong and Kolesár construct bias-aware honest intervals for nonparametric regression under fixed-design formulations; the observed mixed functional \(\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\), vanishing random design, moving point, and singular-Gram expected-length integration are absent. Kennedy et al. motivate two-stage causal local regression, while Bonvini and Kennedy study dose-response rates under smooth positive conditional densities rather than polynomial thinning. Van der Laan, Zhang, and Gilbert obtain root-\(n\) bands for a support-restricted stochastic region-average threshold response, a different estimand not used as a comparator target. Shahu et al. extend simultaneous LMTP inference under piecewise invertibility rather than the continuous threshold map.

  \item \texttt{theta\_\allowbreak{}delta\_\allowbreak{}cont} --- \texttt{continuity\_\allowbreak{}only\_\allowbreak{}clamp\_\allowbreak{}functional}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{target\_\allowbreak{}estimand}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Observed-data clamp target \(\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)\) on \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Hoeffding interval \(\mathrm{CI}_{n,\mathrm{cont}}\) using the fixed atom fallback.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Minimax absolute-error risk \(R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star\) for \(\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}\) over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).
  \item \texttt{L\_\allowbreak{}n\_\allowbreak{}cont\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{continuity\_\allowbreak{}only\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}expected\_\allowbreak{}length}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{performance\_\allowbreak{}criterion}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Minimax uniformly honest expected length \(L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star\) over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Carrier-heterogeneous continuity-only full-data class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}n\_\allowbreak{}cont\_\allowbreak{}F\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{continuity\_\allowbreak{}only\_\allowbreak{}causal\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}absolute\_\allowbreak{}risk}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{causal\_\allowbreak{}performance\_\allowbreak{}criterion}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Observed-sample minimax risk \(R_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star\) for the causal clamp mean over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\).
  \item \texttt{L\_\allowbreak{}n\_\allowbreak{}cont\_\allowbreak{}F\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{continuity\_\allowbreak{}only\_\allowbreak{}causal\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}expected\_\allowbreak{}length}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{causal\_\allowbreak{}performance\_\allowbreak{}criterion}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Observed-sample minimax honest expected length \(L_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star\) for the causal clamp mean over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For a realized design \(z\), stratum \(x\), and admissible affine weights \(w\), \(\operatorname{exactHolderBias}_{z,x}(w)\) is the supremum of the absolute intercept error over the declared \([0,1]\)-valued Hölder class.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The quantity \(\operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{z,x}(t)\) is the infimum of exact Hölder bias plus \(t\) times the \(\ell_2\)-norm of admissible affine weights, totalized to zero when no such weight exists.
  \item \texttt{exactModulusHandle} --- \texttt{integrated\_\allowbreak{}exact\_\allowbreak{}modulus\_\allowbreak{}radius}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{integrated\_\allowbreak{}inference\_\allowbreak{}radius\_\allowbreak{}candidate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The quantity \(\operatorname{exactModulusHandle}(P,I_2,x,\ell,\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\delta_n,h_n,t)\) is the real-valued integral of \(\operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{z,x}(t)\) under the \(n\)-fold iid observed-design law induced by \(P\).
  \item \texttt{worstExactModulus} --- \texttt{worst\_\allowbreak{}case\_\allowbreak{}integrated\_\allowbreak{}radius}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{sharp\_\allowbreak{}constant\_\allowbreak{}candidate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The quantity \(\operatorname{worstExactModulus}_n(t)\) is the supremum of the integrated exact-modulus radius over \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).
  \item \texttt{t\_\allowbreak{}alpha} --- \texttt{conditional\_\allowbreak{}test\_\allowbreak{}multiplier}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{inference\_\allowbreak{}parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The open-question calibration is \(t_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(12J/\alpha)/2}\).
  \item \texttt{Bseq} --- \texttt{deterministic\_\allowbreak{}split\_\allowbreak{}block\_\allowbreak{}sequence}; \(\text{the set of deterministic admissible three-way sample-split sequences}\); \texttt{open\_\allowbreak{}question\_\allowbreak{}selector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The sequence \(B_{\bullet}=(B_n)_{n\geq1}\), where each \(B_n\) is an admissible deterministic three-way split used by the total-Gram construction; it may depend on the deterministic threshold sequence but not on realized data.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
The observations \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed from \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent random-design sampling, \cite{Gaiffas2005DegenerateDesign}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-density-law]\label{ass:conditional-density-law}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and Borel set \(B\subseteq[0,1]\), \(P(A\in B\mid X=x)=\int_B\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional absolute continuity for a continuous treatment, \cite{DiazWilliamsHoffmanSchenck2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
Under \(P^{\mathrm F}\), the latent variable \(U\) is \(\mathcal U\)-valued and, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1]\) is jointly Borel measurable, with one \(P^{\mathrm F}\)-null set outside which \(Y(a)=g_X(a,U)\) for every \(a\in[0,1]\) and \(Y=g_X(A,U)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (NPSEM-style simultaneous latent-response consistency, \cite{DiazWilliamsHoffmanSchenck2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exchangeability]\label{ass:exchangeability}
Under \(P^{\mathrm F}\), \(A\) and \(U\) are conditionally independent given \(X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Latent-response randomization, \cite{DiazWilliamsHoffmanSchenck2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratum-mass]\label{ass:stratum-mass}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(p_x\geq p_{\min}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-stratum overlap, \cite{DiazWilliamsHoffmanSchenck2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:polynomial-thinning]\label{ass:polynomial-thinning}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and Lebesgue-almost every \(a\in[0,1]\), \(c_-a^\kappa\leq\pi_x(a)\leq c_+a^\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The global two-sided measurable envelope is an explicit treatment-assignment design class. It contains beta-type assignment densities proportional to \(a^\kappa\) and their normalized bounded measurable tilts, representing protocols that suppress doses near a contraindicated boundary while permitting irregular assignment elsewhere. It is not asserted to follow from Gaiffas's regular-variation condition.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-regression]\label{ass:holder-regression}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), an almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\) has a continuous extension \(\mu_x^P\) satisfying \(\left|\mu_x^P(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(\mu_x^P)^{(j)}(s)(t-s)^j/j!\right|\leq L|t-s|^\beta\) for all \(s,t\in[0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Taylor-remainder formulation of Hölder outcome-regression smoothness, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:full-data-response-continuity]\label{ass:full-data-response-continuity}
Under \(P^{\mathrm F}\), for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), the function \(a\mapsto m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\) is continuous on \([0,\bar\delta]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This author-imposed scientific mean-regularity premise is weaker than conditional \(L^1\)-continuity of the full response path: it constrains only the fiberwise structural mean and only on the declared threshold range.  It is not attributed to Díaz et al.
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{kappa}; \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{beta}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:total-gram-estimator]\label{def:total-gram-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Total-\allowbreak{}Gram-\allowbreak{}stabilized local-\allowbreak{}polynomial estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Split the sample into \(I_0,I_1,I_2\). Set \(\widehat\nu_n=|I_0|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_0}Y_i\mathbf 1\{A_i>\delta_n\}\), \(\widehat b_x=|I_1|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1}\mathbf 1\{X_i=x,A_i\leq\delta_n\}\), and \(\widetilde\mu_x=\Pi_{[0,1]}(\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}Y_i)\) on \(\Omega_x\), with \(\widetilde\mu_x=1/2\) off \(\Omega_x\). Define \(\widehat\theta_n=\Pi_{[0,1]}[\widehat\nu_n+\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\widehat b_x\widetilde\mu_x]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}0}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}1}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}2}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{w\_\allowbreak{}ix}; \texttt{Omega\_\allowbreak{}x}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:honest-interval]\label{def:honest-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Bias-\allowbreak{}aware atom-\allowbreak{}fallback interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(t_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(12J/\alpha)/2}\), \(b_0=t_\alpha/\sqrt{|I_0|}\), and \(b_1=t_\alpha/\sqrt{|I_1|}\). For each \(x\), set \(B_x=Lh_n^\beta\sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|+t_\alpha(\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2)^{1/2}\) and \(D_x=(\widehat b_x+b_1)B_x+b_1\) on \(\Omega_x\), while \(D_x=\widehat b_x+b_1\) off \(\Omega_x\). Define \(\mathrm{CI}_n=[\widehat\theta_n-b_0-\sum_xD_x,\widehat\theta_n+b_0+\sum_xD_x]\cap[0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{theta\_\allowbreak{}hat\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{alpha}; \texttt{L}; \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{w\_\allowbreak{}ix}; \texttt{Omega\_\allowbreak{}x}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:exact-modulus-handle]\label{def:exact-modulus-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Realized-\allowbreak{}design exact-\allowbreak{}modulus handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), a split block \(I_2\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(\ell\), \(\beta\), \(\kappa\), \(L\), \(c_-\), \(c_+\), \(p_{\min}\), \(\delta_n\), \(h_n>0\), and a test multiplier \(t\geq0\).  For a realized design \(z=\{(X_i,A_i):i\in I_2\}\), put \(u_i=(A_i-\delta_n)/h_n\), \(S_{z,x}=\{i\in I_2:X_i=x,\ 0\leq u_i\leq1\}\), and
\[
\mathcal W_{z,x}=\left\{w=(w_i)_{i\in I_2}\in\mathbb R^{I_2}: w_i=0\ \text{for }i\notin S_{z,x},\quad \sum_{i\in I_2}w_i u_i^j=\mathbf 1\{j=0\}\ \text{for }j=0,\ldots,\ell\right\}.
\]
If \(V_{z,x}\) is the matrix whose row indexed by \(i\in S_{z,x}\) is \(v_\ell(u_i)^\top\), these are equivalently the affine realized-design constraints \(V_{z,x}^\top w_{S_{z,x}}=e_0\), and the associated total Gram is \(G_x=V_{z,x}^\top V_{z,x}\).  Let \(\mathcal H_{\beta,L}\) be the class of functions \(f:[0,1]\to[0,1]\) that are continuous and satisfy
\[
\left|f(a')-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}f^{(j)}(a)(a'-a)^j/j!\right|\leq L|a'-a|^\beta
\quad\text{for every }a,a'\in[0,1],
\]
where the derivatives are the intrinsic derivatives on \([0,1]\) used in the declared Hölder condition.  For \(w\in\mathcal W_{z,x}\), define the exact realized Hölder bias and conditional radius objective by
\[
\operatorname{exactHolderBias}_{z,x}(w)
=\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{\beta,L}}
\left|\sum_{i\in I_2}w_i f(A_i)-f(\delta_n)\right|,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{z,x,t}(w)
=\operatorname{exactHolderBias}_{z,x}(w)+t\left(\sum_{i\in I_2}w_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The realized exact modulus is the real-valued totalization
\[
\operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{z,x}(t)=
\begin{cases}
\inf_{w\in\mathcal W_{z,x}}\mathcal Q_{z,x,t}(w),&\mathcal W_{z,x}\neq\varnothing,\\
0,&\mathcal W_{z,x}=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the empty affine-feasible set has value zero, matching the real \(\operatorname{sInf}\) convention in the verified construction.  Define
\[
\operatorname{exactModulusHandle}(P,I_2,x,\ell,\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\delta_n,h_n,t)
=\int \operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{z,x}(t)\,
\mathrm d(P_{X,A}^{\otimes n})(z),
\]
the real-valued integral under the \(n\)-fold independent observed-design law induced by \(P\).  The inputs \(\kappa,c_-,c_+,p_{\min}\) enter by requiring that \(P\) satisfy the corresponding member conditions of \(\mathcal M\); they do not impose any additional smoothness on \(\pi_x\).  Finally set
\[
t_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(12J/\alpha)/2},\qquad
\operatorname{worstExactModulus}_n(t_\alpha)
=\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
\operatorname{exactModulusHandle}(P,I_2,x,\ell,\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\delta_n,h_n,t_\alpha).
\]
Both \(\operatorname{realizedExactModulus}_{z,x}(t)\) and \(\operatorname{exactModulusHandle}\) are radius, or half-length, quantities.  An untruncated symmetric interval with such radius has full length twice the radius.  The zero value on \(\mathcal W_{z,x}=\varnothing\) only totalizes this candidate handle on singular designs; no validity, nondegeneracy, honesty, or optimality of an interval using that convention is asserted here.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}model-\allowbreak{}class}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid-\allowbreak{}sampling}; \texttt{P}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}2}; \texttt{x}; \texttt{ell}; \texttt{beta}; \texttt{kappa}; \texttt{L}; \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}minus}; \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}plus}; \texttt{p\_\allowbreak{}min}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{t}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:full-data-model-class]\label{def:full-data-model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Structural full-\allowbreak{}data clamp model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) is the collection of full-data law packages \(P^{\mathrm F}\) with the following memberwise structure.  Each member carries its own standard Borel latent-response space \(\mathcal U\), an \(\mathcal U\)-valued random variable \(U\), jointly Borel maps \(g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1]\) for \(x\in\mathcal X\), and a jointly measurable process \(\{Y(a):a\in[0,1]\}\), together with a probability law for \((X,A,Y,U,\{Y(a):a\in[0,1]\})\).  Its \((X,A,Y)\)-margin is some \(P\in\mathcal M\), and, within that member's own carrier, the simultaneous latent-response consistency, latent randomization, and fiberwise structural-mean continuity on \([0,\bar\delta]\) member conditions hold.  Different members may use different standard Borel carriers; no common carrier and no carrier-richness condition is imposed.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}exchangeability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}full-\allowbreak{}data-\allowbreak{}response-\allowbreak{}continuity}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:causal-frontier-criteria]\label{def:causal-frontier-criteria}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Causal minimax risk and honest expected length}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For estimators and intervals measurable with respect to the observed sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), define
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=\inf_{\widehat\psi}\sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}|\widehat\psi-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})|
\]
and
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=
 \inf_{C_n:\,\inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 (P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha}
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)].
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{O\_\allowbreak{}i}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{alpha}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}full-\allowbreak{}data-\allowbreak{}model-\allowbreak{}class}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:continuity-model-class]\label{def:continuity-model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Continuity-\allowbreak{}only finite-\allowbreak{}stratum clamp model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) is the set of laws \(P\in\mathcal P(\mathcal X\times[0,1]\times[0,1])\) satisfying \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\), and \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), and such that, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), an almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\) admits a continuous extension to \([0,\bar\delta]\).  There is no common Hölder exponent, modulus, or radius.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}law}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}stratum-\allowbreak{}mass}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}polynomial-\allowbreak{}thinning}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:continuity-clamp-functional]\label{def:continuity-clamp-functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Continuity-\allowbreak{}only observed clamp functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) and \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\), define
\[
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)
 =\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
 +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta).
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:continuity-frontier]\label{def:continuity-frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Continuity-\allowbreak{}only clamp frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[
 s_n=n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{kappa}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:continuity-fallback-estimator]\label{def:continuity-fallback-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Continuity-\allowbreak{}only fixed atom fallback estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Using the deterministic split blocks \(I_0,I_1\), put
\[
 \widehat\nu_n=|I_0|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_0}Y_i\mathbf 1\{A_i>\delta_n\},
 \qquad
 \widehat b_x=|I_1|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1}\mathbf 1\{X_i=x,A_i\leq\delta_n\},
\]
and define the fixed-regression-fallback estimator
\[
 \widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}
 =\Pi_{[0,1]}\left\{\widehat\nu_n+\frac12\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\widehat b_x\right\}.
\]
It uses no local regression and no propensity estimate.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}0}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}1}; \texttt{delta\_\allowbreak{}n}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:continuity-honest-interval]\label{def:continuity-honest-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Continuity-\allowbreak{}only Hoeffding interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let
\[
 t_{j,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}{2|I_j|}}
 \quad(j=0,1),
 \qquad \widehat B_n=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\widehat b_x.
\]
Define the Hoeffding interval
\[
 \mathrm{CI}_{n,\mathrm{cont}}
 =\left[
 \widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-t_{0,\alpha}-t_{1,\alpha}-\frac12\widehat B_n,
 \widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}+t_{0,\alpha}+t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12\widehat B_n
 \right]\cap[0,1].
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{theta\_\allowbreak{}hat\_\allowbreak{}cont\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}0}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}1}; \texttt{alpha}.

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:pushforward-setup]\label{prop:pushforward-setup}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\), the conditional pushforward law satisfies \(P\{d_\delta(A)\in\mathrm da\mid X=x\}=\pi_x(a)\mathbf 1\{a>\delta\}\,\mathrm da+q_x(\delta)\,\delta_\delta(\mathrm da)\), with \(c_-\delta^{\kappa+1}/(\kappa+1)\leq q_x(\delta)\leq c_+\delta^{\kappa+1}/(\kappa+1)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\).  For every Borel set \(B\subseteq[0,1]\), Definition \texttt{def:clamp-policy} gives the disjoint inverse-image decomposition
\[
 \{d_\delta(A)\in B\}
 =\{A\in B\cap(\delta,1]\}
   \mathbin{\dot\cup}
   \bigl(\{A\in[0,\delta]\}\cap\{\delta\in B\}\bigr).
\]
By Assumption \texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}, conditional on \(X=x\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\{d_\delta(A)\in B\mid X=x\}
 &=\int_{B\cap(\delta,1]}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
   +\mathbf 1\{\delta\in B\}\int_0^\delta\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\\
 &=\int_B\pi_x(a)\mathbf 1\{a>\delta\}\,\mathrm da
   +q_x(\delta)\delta_\delta(B),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is the definition of \(q_x\).  Equality on every Borel set proves the asserted equality of measures.  Finally, Assumption \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning} and integration of its almost-everywhere inequalities give
\[
 \frac{c_-}{\kappa+1}\delta^{\kappa+1}
 =\int_0^\delta c_-a^\kappa\,\mathrm da
 \le q_x(\delta)
 \le\int_0^\delta c_+a^\kappa\,\mathrm da
 =\frac{c_+}{\kappa+1}\delta^{\kappa+1}.
\]
This also covers \(\delta=0\), when all three quantities vanish.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This cited setup specializes the threshold-LMTP pushforward calculation to the deterministic clamp and fixes the mixed dominating measure used to interpret the observed functional. \textit{Gap.} Díaz et al. supply threshold-LMTP identification, and McCoy et al. formulate clipped MTP ratios with an appropriate mixed dominating measure; this proposition claims no novelty beyond recording their specialization in the notation of the statistical frontier. \textit{Consumer.} The formula connects the observed-data target to the user-specified lower-threshold policy in the \(\texttt{lmtp}\) workflow.

\begin{proposition}[prop:policy-support]\label{prop:policy-support}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\), \(d_\delta(A)\) lies in the support of the conditional natural-treatment law given \(X=x\) almost surely. At \(\delta=0\) this follows from the identity map; at \(\delta>0\) every neighborhood of \(\delta\) has positive conditional probability by conditional absolute continuity and the lower polynomial envelope.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a probability measure on \([0,1]\), call \(a\) a support point when every relatively open neighborhood of \(a\) has positive probability.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).  If \(V\) is any relatively open neighborhood of any \(a\in[0,1]\), then \(V\) contains a nondegenerate interval \(I\subseteq[0,1]\).  Assumptions \texttt{ass:conditional-density-law} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning} imply
\[
 P(A\in V\mid X=x)
 \geq \int_I\pi_x(t)\,\mathrm dt
 \geq c_-\int_I t^\kappa\,\mathrm dt>0.
\]
The last strict inequality holds also when the closure of \(I\) meets zero, because \(t^\kappa>0\) for every \(t>0\).  Thus the conditional natural-treatment support is all of \([0,1]\).  Definition \texttt{def:clamp-policy} gives \(d_\delta(A)\in[0,1]\) almost surely, so \(d_\delta(A)\) belongs to that support almost surely.  In particular, at \(\delta=0\) the map is the identity; when \(\delta>0\), the preceding display applied to every neighborhood of \(\delta\) verifies the claimed boundary support directly.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The support statement records why no separate pointwise density-positivity assumption is needed for causal interpretation of the clamp. \textit{Gap.} This is the standard modified-policy support requirement specialized to a boundary-thinned continuous treatment law, not a novel statistical claim. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition discharges the positivity premise in the causal bridge while keeping causal assumptions outside the minimax model.

\begin{proposition}[prop:causal-bridge]\label{prop:causal-bridge}
Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\), let \(P\in\mathcal M\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin, and interpret \(\mathcal U\), \(U\), \(g_x\), and \(\{Y(a):a\in[0,1]\}\) as the carrier components packaged by this member.  Define \(m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[g_x(a,U)\mid X=x]\).  Then, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and Borel \(B\subseteq[0,1]\), \(\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]=\int_Bm_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\), and \(m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_x^P(a)\) for every \(a\in[0,\bar\delta]\).  Consequently, for every \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\), \(d_\delta(A)\) belongs to the support of the conditional natural-treatment law almost surely, \(Y\{d_\delta(A)\}=Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}+Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\) almost surely, and \(\psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]+\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_x^P(\delta)=\theta_\delta(P)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\), and let \(P\in\mathcal M\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin.  Definition \(\texttt{def:full-data-model-class}\) supplies, for this member, a standard Borel carrier \(\mathcal U\), an \(\mathcal U\)-valued variable \(U\), jointly Borel maps \(g_x\), and a jointly measurable process \(Y(a)\); it also imposes Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\), and \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\).  Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\) gives
\[
 P^{\mathrm F}(X=x)=P(X=x)=p_x\geq p_{\min}>0,
 \tag{1}
\]
so conditioning on \(X=x\) is well defined.

Assumption \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) gives one \(P^{\mathrm F}\)-probability-one event on which, simultaneously for every \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Y(a)=g_X(a,U),
 \qquad
 Y=g_X(A,U).
 \tag{2}
\]
By Assumption \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\), \(A\) and \(U\) are conditionally independent given \(X\).  Therefore, on the positive-probability stratum in (1),
\[
 P^{\mathrm F}_{(A,U)\mid X=x}
 =P^{\mathrm F}_{A\mid X=x}\otimes P^{\mathrm F}_{U\mid X=x}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Define, as in the proposition,
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)
 =\int_{\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm du\mid X=x).
 \tag{4}
\]
The integrand in (4) is jointly Borel and bounded in \([0,1]\) by Definition \(\texttt{def:full-data-model-class}\).  Hence its parameter integral is Borel, and the defining iterated-integral identity for the product probability in (3) applies to the nonnegative integrals below.  For every Borel set \(B\subseteq[0,1]\), equations (2)--(4), followed by Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 &=\int_{B\times\mathcal U}g_x(a,u)\,
   \{P^{\mathrm F}_{A\mid X=x}\otimes P^{\mathrm F}_{U\mid X=x}\}
   (\mathrm da,\mathrm du)\\
 &=\int_Bm_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\,P^{\mathrm F}(\mathrm da\mid X=x)\\
 &=\int_Bm_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
This proves the conditional integral identity.

We next derive, rather than assume, the pointwise equality needed at a threshold.  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\) says that \(\mu_x^P\) is continuous on \([0,1]\) and is an almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\).  Together with Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), this gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 =\int_B\mu_x^P(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \tag{6}
\]
for every Borel \(B\subseteq[0,1]\).  Since \(P\) is the observed margin of \(P^{\mathrm F}\), equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
 \int_B\{m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)-\mu_x^P(a)\}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da=0
 \quad\text{for every Borel }B\subseteq[0,1].
 \tag{7}
\]
Put \(f_x=m_x^{\mathrm F}-\mu_x^P\).  For every integer \(j\geq1\), apply (7) to the Borel sets \(B_j^+=\{a:f_x(a)\geq1/j\}\) and \(B_j^-=\{a:f_x(a)\leq-1/j\}\).  Then
\[
 0=\int_{B_j^+}f_x(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \geq \frac1j\int_{B_j^+}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da,
 \qquad
 0=\int_{B_j^-}f_x(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \leq-\frac1j\int_{B_j^-}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(f_x=0\) for \(\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\)-almost every \(a\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), together with \(c_->0\) and \(\kappa\geq0\), gives
\[
 \pi_x(a)\geq c_-a^\kappa>0
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }a\in(0,1].
 \tag{9}
\]
Consequently \(f_x=0\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \((0,\bar\delta]\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\) makes \(m_x^{\mathrm F}\) continuous on \([0,\bar\delta]\), while Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\) makes \(\mu_x^P\) continuous there.  If \(f_x(a_0)\neq0\) at some \(a_0\in[0,\bar\delta]\), relative continuity would produce a relative neighborhood of \(a_0\) on which \(|f_x|\) is bounded away from zero.  Since \(\bar\delta>0\), that neighborhood intersects \((0,\bar\delta]\) in a set of positive Lebesgue measure, contradicting the preceding almost-everywhere equality.  Hence
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_x^P(a)
 \quad\text{for every }a\in[0,\bar\delta].
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular, equality at \(a=0\) follows by continuity from punctured right neighborhoods; it is not imposed through a choice of conditional-expectation version.

Fix \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\).  Definition \(\texttt{def:clamp-policy}\) and the simultaneous identities (2) imply, on the same probability-one event,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Y\{d_\delta(A)\}
 &=g_X\{\max(A,\delta),U\}\\
 &=g_X(A,U)\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}
   +g_X(\delta,U)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\\
 &=Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}+Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
This equality also covers \(A=\delta\), because (2) then gives \(Y=Y(\delta)\).  Conditional independence in (3), equations (2), (4), and (10), and Proposition \(\texttt{prop:pushforward-setup}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\mid X=x]
 &=m_x^{\mathrm F}(\delta)P^{\mathrm F}(A\leq\delta\mid X=x)\\
 &=\mu_x^P(\delta)\int_0^\delta\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\\
 &=\mu_x^P(\delta)q_x(\delta).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Taking expectations in (11), substituting (12), using equality of observed margins for the retained-natural-course term, and summing over the finite set \(\mathcal X\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})
 &=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\{d_\delta(A)\}]\\
 &=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
   +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_x^P(\delta)\\
 &=\theta_\delta(P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The first and last equalities in (13) are the definitions of \(\psi_\delta\) and \(\theta_\delta\).  Proposition \(\texttt{prop:policy-support}\) supplies the support assertion throughout \([0,\bar\delta]\).  At \(\delta=0\), Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) gives \(q_x(0)=0\) and the clamp is the identity; at \(\delta=\bar\delta<1\), equation (10) remains valid.  This proves every asserted conclusion on the full closed threshold range.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition derives the exact deterministic clamp-policy equality fiberwise for the carrier packaged by each full-data law, transporting the observed identifying functional to the causal target without assuming pointwise mean/version equality. \textit{Gap.} Díaz et al. supply the LMTP structural template and support logic, but not the fiberwise structural-mean continuity on \([0,\bar\delta]\) used here to extend almost-everywhere regression equality pointwise over the threshold range; the carrier is quantified internally to each law. \textit{Consumer.} The causal-frontier lift uses this fiberwise bridge for the exact unsmoothed causal clamp mean; the result targets the same lower-threshold policy specification as the official \(\texttt{lmtp}\) workflow and motivates a future atom-aware implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bandwidth-phases]\label{lem:bandwidth-phases}
Uniformly over deterministic \(\delta_n\in[0,\bar\delta]\), the bandwidth satisfies \(n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\asymp1\) and the effective local count satisfies \(n h_n(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\asymp h_n^{-2\beta}\). If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\to\infty\), then \(h_n\asymp(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\); if \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}=O(1)\), then \(h_n\asymp n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(H=1-\bar\delta>0\) and
\[
 F_{n,\delta}(h)=n h^{2\beta+1}(\delta+h)^\kappa,
 \qquad 0<h\leq H.
\]
The restrictions \(\beta>0\) and \(\kappa\geq0\) make this function continuous and strictly increasing.  Moreover,
\[
 F_{n,\delta}(H)\geq nH^{2\beta+\kappa+1},
\]
uniformly in \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\).  Hence, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the set in Definition \texttt{def:bandwidth} is nonempty and its infimum is the unique solution of
\[
 n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa=1. \tag{1}
\]
This proves the first asserted comparison, in fact with equality for all such \(n\).  Dividing (1) by \(h_n^{2\beta}\) gives
\[
 n h_n(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa=h_n^{-2\beta}, \tag{2}
\]
which proves the effective-count assertion.

Let \(e_n=\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}=n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}\).  If \(\delta_n/e_n\to\infty\), set \(z_n=h_n/\delta_n\).  Equation (1) becomes
\[
 n\delta_n^{2\beta+\kappa+1}z_n^{2\beta+1}(1+z_n)^\kappa=1.
\]
Its first factor tends to infinity, so monotonicity forces \(z_n\to0\).  Substitution back into (1) yields
\[
 h_n^{2\beta+1}\sim(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1},
 \qquad
 h_n\asymp(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
If instead \(d_n=\delta_n/e_n=O(1)\) and \(s_n=h_n/e_n\), then (1) is
\[
 s_n^{2\beta+1}(d_n+s_n)^\kappa=1. \tag{3}
\]
Because \(d_n\) is bounded, (3) bounds \(s_n\) above and away from zero by constants depending only on that bound and the declared exponents.  Thus \(h_n\asymp e_n=n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}\), as claimed.  Every comparison was uniform in the deterministic threshold within its stated regime.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This balance converts polynomial thinning into the information scale that drives both estimation and inference. \textit{Gap.} Gaiffas derives the balance at a fixed degenerate point; the clamp requires a moving point \(\delta_n\) and an atom multiplier that changes the relevant phase boundary. \textit{Consumer.} The local-count identity is the normalization needed by the total-Gram probability bound and by bandwidth selection in the threshold-policy estimator.

\begin{lemma}[lem:total-gram]\label{lem:total-gram}
The constant \(\lambda_\star\) is strictly positive. There are constants \(C,c>0\), depending only on the declared model constants, such that uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and deterministic \(\delta_n\), \(P(\Omega_x^c)\leq C\exp\{-c n h_n(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\}\). On \(\Omega_x\), the exact intercept weights reproduce polynomials through degree \(\ell\) and obey \(\sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|\leq C\) and \(\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2\leq C/N_x\). No continuity or differentiability of \(\pi_x\) beyond two-sided polynomial comparability is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d=\ell+1\), \(m=|I_2|\), \(\delta=\delta_n\), and \(h=h_n\).  First consider the reference matrices.  For every nonzero \(z\in\mathbb R^d\), the polynomial \(u\mapsto z^\top v_\ell(u)\) is nonzero except on a finite set.  Consequently,
\[
 z^\top M_{\kappa,\rho}z
 =\frac{\int_0^1\{z^\top v_\ell(u)\}^2(\rho+u)^\kappa\,\mathrm du}
        {\int_0^1(\rho+u)^\kappa\,\mathrm du}>0
\]
for every \(\rho\geq0\).  The entries are continuous in \(\rho\), and after dividing numerator and denominator by \(\rho^\kappa\) they converge as \(\rho\to\infty\) to the entries of \(\int_0^1v_\ell(u)v_\ell(u)^\top\,\mathrm du\), another positive-definite matrix.  Continuity of the smallest eigenvalue on the compactification \([0,\infty]\) therefore gives
\[
 \inf_{\rho\geq0}\lambda_{\min}(M_{\kappa,\rho})>0.
\]
Since \(c_->0\) and \(c_+<\infty\), the definition of \(\lambda_\star\) proves \(\lambda_\star>0\).

Let
\[
 s_x=P\{X=x,\delta\leq A\leq\delta+h\},
 \qquad \mu_{N,x}=ms_x.
\]
Assumptions \texttt{ass:stratum-mass}, \texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}, and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning} imply
\[
 c_1h(\delta+h)^\kappa\leq s_x\leq c_2h(\delta+h)^\kappa, \tag{4}
\]
where for the lower bound one integrates over \([\delta+h/2,\delta+h]\) and uses \(a^\kappa\geq2^{-\kappa}(\delta+h)^\kappa\).  Since \(m\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), (4) and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} give
\[
 \mu_{N,x}\asymp nh(\delta+h)^\kappa\asymp h^{-2\beta}. \tag{5}
\]

By Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling}, \(N_x\) is binomial with parameters \(m,s_x\).  Conditional on \(N_x=k>0\), the \(k\) local values \(U=(A-\delta)/h\) are independent with density proportional to \(u\mapsto\pi_x(\delta+hu)\) on \([0,1]\).  Let \(Q_x\) be the conditional second-moment matrix of \(v_\ell(U)\).  For every \(z\in\mathbb R^d\), Assumption \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning} yields, with \(\rho=\delta/h\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 z^\top Q_xz
 &\geq\frac{c_-\int_0^1\{z^\top v_\ell(u)\}^2(\delta+hu)^\kappa\,\mathrm du}
              {c_+\int_0^1(\delta+hu)^\kappa\,\mathrm du}\\
 &=\frac{c_-}{c_+}z^\top M_{\kappa,\rho}z
 \geq\lambda_\star\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
 \end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
This comparison uses only the two measurable density envelopes.

Each entry of \(v_\ell(U)v_\ell(U)^\top\) lies in \([0,1]\).  Applying the scalar exponential-Markov bound to each centered bounded entry and taking a union bound over the fixed \(d^2\) entries gives
\[
 P\left(\left.\left\|k^{-1}G_x-Q_x\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\lambda_\star/2\,\right|N_x=k\right)
 \leq 2d^2\exp(-c_0k), \tag{7}
\]
because the operator norm is at most \(d\) times the largest absolute entry.  On the complement of the event in (7), (6) implies \(\lambda_{\min}(G_x)\geq k\lambda_\star/2\).  The binomial generating function now gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(\Omega_x^c)
 &\leq C_0\mathbb E[\exp(-c_0N_x)]\\
 &=C_0\{1-s_x+s_xe^{-c_0}\}^{m}
 \leq C_0\exp\{-c_1ms_x\}\\
 &\leq C\exp\{-cnh(\delta+h)^\kappa\},
 \end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
where (4) was used in the last line.  This includes the event \(N_x=0\).

It remains to verify the exact weight statements.  On \(\Omega_x\), for \(0\leq j\leq\ell\),
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}U_i^j
 =e_0^\top G_x^{-1}\left\{\sum_{i:X_i=x,\,0\leq U_i\leq1}v_\ell(U_i)U_i^j\right\}
 =e_0^\top G_x^{-1}G_xe_j
 =\mathbf 1\{j=0\}. \tag{9}
\]
Furthermore, \(\lVert v_\ell(u)\rVert_2\leq\sqrt d\) on \([0,1]\) and \(\lVert G_x^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2/(\lambda_\star N_x)\), whence
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|
 \leq N_x\frac{2\sqrt d}{\lambda_\star N_x}
 \leq C, \tag{10}
\]
and, using the definition of \(G_x\),
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2
 =e_0^\top G_x^{-1}G_xG_x^{-1}e_0
 =e_0^\top G_x^{-1}e_0
 \leq\frac{2}{\lambda_\star N_x}. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (8)--(11) prove every assertion with constants depending only on the declared model constants.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the early-kill lemma: it makes the singular-Gram fallback negligible at the atom-weighted target scale under measurable density envelopes alone. \textit{Gap.} Gaiffas stabilizes local polynomial Gram matrices under an exact regularly varying design; the present claim is uniform over every measurable density between two polynomial envelopes and controls the exact \(\ell_1\)- and \(\ell_2\)-weight norms. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma is the sole random-design input needed for the finite-sample honest interval and prevents an undocumented propensity-smoothness condition from entering the official workflow.

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-risk]\label{thm:minimax-risk}
There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the declared model constants, such that for every deterministic threshold sequence and all sufficiently large \(n\), \(c r_n\leq R_n^\star\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_n-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)|\leq C r_n\). The lower bound is the maximum, and therefore the same order as the sum, of a same-class global Bernoulli shift yielding \(n^{-1/2}\) separation and a same-class localized Bernoulli regression perturbation of width \(h_n\) yielding \(\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\) separation with bounded product divergence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\delta=\delta_n\), \(h=h_n\), \(m_j=|I_j|\), and
\[
 b_x=P(X=x,A\leq\delta)=p_xq_x(\delta).
\]
Proposition \texttt{prop:pushforward-setup} gives
\[
 0\leq b_x\leq \frac{c_+}{\kappa+1}\delta^{\kappa+1}. \tag{1}
\]
We first prove the upper bound for the single sample map in Definition \texttt{def:total-gram-estimator}.  Since \(m_0,m_1\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling}, \(0\leq Y\leq1\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq1/4\) for \(0\leq Z\leq1\) imply, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|\leq Cn^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat b_x-b_x|\leq Cn^{-1/2}. \tag{2}
\]

On \(\Omega_x\), write \(S_x=\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}Y_i\).  Conditional on \(\mathscr D_2=\sigma\{(X_i,A_i):i\in I_2\}\), Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling} makes the outcomes independent, and the defining version \(\mu_x^P\) is their conditional mean at almost every realized treatment value.  Polynomial reproduction in Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} makes the Taylor polynomial of \(\mu_x^P\) about \(\delta\) contribute exactly its intercept.  Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression}, boundedness of \(Y\), and the two weight bounds in that lemma therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathbb E_P[S_x\mid\mathscr D_2]-\mu_x^P(\delta)\right|
 &\leq Lh^\beta\sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|\leq Ch^\beta,\\
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_x\mid\mathscr D_2)
 &\leq\frac14\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2\leq\frac{C}{N_x}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance from \(\mu_x^P(\delta)\in[0,1]\).

We record the count calculation needed to integrate (3).  The variable \(N_x\) is binomial with mean
\[
 \lambda_x=m_2p_x\int_\delta^{\delta+h}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da.
\]
Assumptions \texttt{ass:stratum-mass} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}, together with the subinterval \([\delta+h/2,\delta+h]\), yield
\[
 cnh(\delta+h)^\kappa\leq\lambda_x\leq Cnh(\delta+h)^\kappa. \tag{4}
\]
For a binomial \(N\) of mean \(\lambda=mp\), exponential Markov and \(1+z\leq e^z\) give, at \(t=\log2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(N<\lambda/2)
 &\leq e^{t\lambda/2}\mathbb E e^{-tN}
 =e^{t\lambda/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^m\\
 &\leq\exp\{\lambda(t/2+e^{-t}-1)\}
 \leq e^{-\lambda/8}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Splitting according to \(N\geq\lambda/2\) consequently gives
\[
 \mathbb E[N^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N>0\}]
 \leq (2/\lambda)^{1/2}+e^{-\lambda/8}
 \leq C\lambda^{-1/2}. \tag{5}
\]
Here and below constants absorb the harmless bounded range of \(\lambda\); by (4) and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases}, \(\lambda_x\asymp h^{-2\beta}\).  On \(\Omega_x^c\), both the fallback and the true regression value lie in \([0,1]\).  Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram} and \(e^{-ct}\leq C_ct^{-1/2}\) for \(t\geq1\), used with \(t=h^{-2\beta}\), therefore imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\mu_x-\mu_x^P(\delta)|
 \leq Ch^\beta+C\mathbb E_P[N_x^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N_x>0\}]
       +P(\Omega_x^c)
 \leq Ch^\beta. \tag{6}
\]

Before the final projection, add and subtract \(b_x\widetilde\mu_x\), use \(0\leq\widetilde\mu_x\leq1\), and combine (1), (2), and (6):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_n-\theta_\delta(P)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|
 +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\left\{\mathbb E_P|\widehat b_x-b_x|
 +b_x\mathbb E_P|\widetilde\mu_x-\mu_x^P(\delta)|\right\}\\
 &\leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}=Cr_n. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
The displayed inequality survives projection because \(\theta_\delta(P)\in[0,1]\).  The fixed value of \(J\) makes the constant uniform in \(P\), \(x\), and the deterministic threshold sequence.  Taking the supremum proves the claimed minimax upper bound with one data-driven estimator.

For the converse, we construct two pairs inside the same class \(\mathcal M\).  In both, take \(p_x=1/J\) and \(\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\) for every stratum.  These choices satisfy Assumptions \texttt{ass:stratum-mass} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}, because \(p_{\min}\leq1/J\) and \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\).  For the global pair, let \(Y\mid(A,X)\) be Bernoulli with mean \(1/2\) under \(P_0\) and \(1/2+a_0n^{-1/2}\) under \(P_1\), where \(a_0>0\) is a sufficiently small fixed constant.  Both regressions obey Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression}, and the two parts of the definition of \(\theta_\delta\) partition the treatment law.  Hence
\[
 |\theta_\delta(P_1)-\theta_\delta(P_0)|=a_0n^{-1/2}. \tag{8}
\]
The one-observation chi-square divergence is \(4a_0^2/n\), so independence gives
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+4a_0^2/n)^n-1\leq e^{4a_0^2}-1. \tag{9}
\]

For the localized pair, fix a nonnegative smooth bump \(\varphi\) supported on \([-1,1]\) with \(\varphi(0)=1\), for example the normalized function \(u\mapsto\exp\{-1/(1-u^2)\}\mathbf 1\{|u|<1\}\).  Direct Taylor expansion and rescaling give a finite constant \(C_{\varphi,\beta}\) such that \(h^\beta\varphi((\,cdot-\delta)/h)\), restricted to \([0,1]\), satisfies the Taylor-remainder Hölder condition with radius \(C_{\varphi,\beta}\).  Define, in every stratum,
\[
 \mu_0(a)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_1(a)=\frac12+a_1h^\beta\varphi((a-\delta)/h), \tag{10}
\]
where the fixed \(a_1>0\) is small enough that \(a_1C_{\varphi,\beta}\leq L\) and \(a_1\|\varphi\|_\infty\leq1/4\).  Bernoulli outcomes with these conditional means define two further members of \(\mathcal M\).  Since the Bernoulli chi-square divergence from mean \(1/2\) to mean \(1/2+g\) equals \(4g^2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(P_1,P_0)
 &=4a_1^2h^{2\beta}\int_0^1
   \varphi((a-\delta)/h)^2(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\,\mathrm da\\
 &\leq Ca_1^2h^{2\beta+1}(\delta+h)^\kappa.
 \end{aligned} \tag{11}
\]
Definition \texttt{def:bandwidth} and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} make \(n\) times the last expression at most \(Ca_1^2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Thus
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\exp(Ca_1^2)-1. \tag{12}
\]
For this design, \(q_x(\delta)=\delta^{\kappa+1}\).  The perturbation is nonnegative, and its value at the clamp atom is \(a_1h^\beta\).  The retained-natural-course part is also nonnegative, so
\[
 \theta_\delta(P_1)-\theta_\delta(P_0)
 \geq a_1\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta. \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, if \(Q_1\ll Q_0\) has likelihood ratio \(Z\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|Q_1-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\sup_E|\mathbb E_{Q_0}[(Z-1)\mathbf 1_E]|
 \leq\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)}. \tag{14}
\]
Choose \(a_0,a_1\) so that both product total variations are at most \(1/2\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), a pair with target values \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and separation \(\Delta\), set \(E=\{|T-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\).  Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{j=0,1}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|T-\theta_j|
 &\geq\frac{\Delta}{4}\{Q_0(E^c)+Q_1(E)\}\\
 &\geq\frac{\Delta}{4}\{1-\|Q_1-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}
 \geq\frac{\Delta}{8}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
Apply (15) separately to (8) and (13), then use \(\max(u,v)\geq(u+v)/2\).  This proves
\[
 R_n^\star\geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}
 \geq c'\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}=c'r_n. \tag{16}
\]
Together, (7) and (16) establish every assertion of the theorem, including the announced global-shift and localized-Bernoulli mechanisms.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem is the observed-experiment backbone of the exact causal frontier: it gives a single explicit estimator and matching same-class global and localized lower bounds for the unsmoothed identifying functional. The closest accepted-bank comparison, stat\_dose\_response\_minimax/holder\_anisotropic\_converse, certifies only an interior fixed-dose, one-sided mean-squared-error lower floor; this theorem instead matches upper and lower absolute-risk bounds for a moving clamp functional whose boundary atom is weighted by q\_x(delta\_n). \textit{Gap.} Gaiffas gives an unweighted fixed-point regression rate, while LMTP identification and stochastic-shift regularity results do not give the matched risk frontier for the retained-natural-course mean plus an atom-weighted moving point evaluation. The closest accepted-bank result, stat\_dose\_response\_minimax/holder\_anisotropic\_converse, concerns an interior fixed dose under a one-sided local positivity floor and deliberately proves a lower floor without a same-class upper endpoint; it neither has polynomial boundary thinning nor a moving Dirac-weighted clamp atom. Thus it is a close converse analogue, not a substitute for the matched frontier claimed here. \textit{Consumer.} The causal lift applies this estimator unchanged to the official \(\texttt{lmtp}\) lower-threshold policy after the structural bridge proves equality of the causal and observed targets.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-length]\label{thm:honest-length}
For every \(n\) large enough, \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P\{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in\mathrm{CI}_n\}\geq1-\alpha\), and \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]\leq C r_n\). Conversely, every confidence interval with coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M\) satisfies \(L_n^\star\geq c r_n\). The interval adapts the bias-aware construction of Armstrong and Kolesár to the distinct polynomially thinned random-design clamp functional and remains honest on singular-Gram realizations through the atom fallback.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Again put \(\delta=\delta_n\), \(h=h_n\), \(b_x=p_xq_x(\delta)\), and \(m_j=|I_j|\).  Consider the simultaneous events
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_0&=\{|\widehat\nu_n-\nu_\delta(P)|\leq b_0\},\\
 E_{1x}&=\{|\widehat b_x-b_x|\leq b_1\},\\
 E_{2x}&=\Omega_x^c\ \cup\
 \left\{\left|\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}Y_i-\mu_x^P(\delta)\right|\leq B_x\right\}.
 \end{split} \tag{17}
\]
We first derive the concentration inequality used below.  If a centered random variable \(W\) lies in an interval of length \(d\), its cumulant generating function \(K(\lambda)=\log\mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\) satisfies \(K(0)=K'(0)=0\) and
\[
 K''(\lambda)=\operatorname{Var}_{\lambda}(W)\leq d^2/4,
\]
because variance under any exponentially tilted law is at most the second moment about the midpoint of that interval.  Two integrations give \(K(\lambda)\leq\lambda^2d^2/8\).  Independence, exponential Markov, and optimization over \(\lambda>0\) therefore yield, for independent \(Z_i\in[0,1]\) and deterministic \(a_i\),
\[
 P\left(\left|\sum_i a_i\{Z_i-\mathbb EZ_i\}\right|>
 t\Bigl(\sum_i a_i^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\right)\leq2e^{-2t^2}. \tag{18}
\]
This derivation also applies conditionally when the weights and conditional means are fixed.

Apply (18) with equal weights to the first two sample blocks.  The definition \(t_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(12J/\alpha)/2}\) gives
\[
 P(E_0^c)\leq2e^{-2t_\alpha^2}=\frac{\alpha}{6J},
 \qquad
 P(E_{1x}^c)\leq2e^{-2t_\alpha^2}=\frac{\alpha}{6J}. \tag{19}
\]
On \(\Omega_x\), condition on \(\mathscr D_2=\sigma\{(X_i,A_i):i\in I_2\}\).  Assumption \texttt{ass:iid-sampling} gives conditional independence.  Applying (18) to the weighted outcome residuals yields
\[
 P\left(\left.\left|\sum_iw_{ix}\{Y_i-\mu_x^P(A_i)\}\right|>
 t_\alpha\Bigl(\sum_iw_{ix}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\,\right|\mathscr D_2\right)
 \leq\frac{\alpha}{6J}. \tag{20}
\]
By Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram}, the weights reproduce every polynomial through degree \(\ell\).  Assumption \texttt{ass:holder-regression} consequently bounds the conditional bias by \(Lh^\beta\sum_i|w_{ix}|\).  Thus (20) proves \(P(E_{2x}^c)\leq\alpha/(6J)\).  A union bound and fixed finiteness of \(J\) give
\[
 P\left(E_0\cap\bigcap_{x\in\mathcal X}(E_{1x}\cap E_{2x})\right)
 \geq1-\frac{(2J+1)\alpha}{6J}\geq1-\alpha. \tag{21}
\]

On the event in (21), projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot enlarge the local regression error.  On \(\Omega_x\), \(b_x\leq\widehat b_x+b_1\) and hence
\[
 |\widehat b_x\widetilde\mu_x-b_x\mu_x^P(\delta)|
 \leq b_1+(\widehat b_x+b_1)B_x=D_x. \tag{22}
\]
On \(\Omega_x^c\), the two products lie in \([0,\widehat b_x]\) and \([0,b_x]\), respectively, so
\[
 |\widehat b_x\widetilde\mu_x-b_x\mu_x^P(\delta)|
 \leq\max\{\widehat b_x,b_x\}\leq\widehat b_x+b_1=D_x. \tag{23}
\]
Combining (22)--(23) with \(E_0\), then using contraction of the final projection about \(\theta_\delta(P)\in[0,1]\), gives
\[
 |\widehat\theta_n-\theta_\delta(P)|\leq b_0+\sum_xD_x. \tag{24}
\]
By Definition \texttt{def:honest-interval}, (24) is precisely the event \(\theta_\delta(P)\in\mathrm{CI}_n\).  Equation (21) proves the asserted uniform finite-sample coverage.

It remains to integrate the radius, including singular-Gram realizations.  The binomial calculation (4)--(5) in the proof of Theorem \texttt{thm:minimax-risk}, Lemma \texttt{lem:total-gram}, and Lemma \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[B_x\mathbf 1\{\Omega_x\}]
 &\leq Lh^\beta\mathbb E_P\!\left[\sum_i|w_{ix}|\mathbf 1\{\Omega_x\}\right]
 +t_\alpha\mathbb E_P\!\left[\Bigl(\sum_iw_{ix}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\mathbf 1\{\Omega_x\}\right]\\
 &\leq Ch^\beta+C\mathbb E_P[N_x^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{N_x>0\}]
 \leq Ch^\beta. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{25}
\]
The split makes \(\widehat b_x\) independent of \((B_x,\Omega_x)\), and \(\mathbb E_P\widehat b_x=b_x\).  The two branches in Definition \texttt{def:honest-interval} therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_PD_x
 &\leq(b_x+b_1)\mathbb E_P[B_x\mathbf 1\{\Omega_x\}]+b_1
 +(b_x+b_1)P(\Omega_x^c)\\
 &\leq C\{n^{-1/2}+b_xh^\beta\}
 \leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{26}
\]
For the second line, Proposition \texttt{prop:pushforward-setup} gives \(b_x\leq C\delta^{\kappa+1}\), while Lemmas \texttt{lem:total-gram} and \texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases} give
\[
 P(\Omega_x^c)\leq Ce^{-cnh(\delta+h)^\kappa}
 \leq Ce^{-ch^{-2\beta}}\leq Ch^\beta. \tag{27}
\]
The terms containing \(b_1\) are \(O(n^{-1/2})\), since \(h\leq1\).  Intersecting with \([0,1]\) cannot increase length.  Thus (26), fixed \(J\), and \(b_0=O(n^{-1/2})\) yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]
 \leq2b_0+2\sum_x\mathbb E_PD_x\leq Cr_n. \tag{28}
\]

For the converse, use the global and localized same-class pairs explicitly constructed in Theorem \texttt{thm:minimax-risk}.  Decrease their fixed amplitudes, if necessary, so that their product-law total variation is at most a constant \(\eta<1-2\alpha\); equations (9), (12), and (14) of that proof permit this without changing either separation order.  For either pair, let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be the product laws and let their target values have separation \(\Delta\).  Uniform coverage of an arbitrary interval \(C_n\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_0\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in C_n\}
 &\geq Q_0\{\theta_0\in C_n\}+Q_0\{\theta_1\in C_n\}-1\\
 &\geq(1-\alpha)+(1-\alpha-\eta)-1
 =1-2\alpha-\eta>0. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{29}
\]
On this event an interval has length at least \(\Delta\), whence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 \geq\Delta(1-2\alpha-\eta). \tag{30}
\]
The global pair has \(\Delta\asymp n^{-1/2}\), and the localized pair has \(\Delta\geq c\delta^{\kappa+1}h^\beta\).  Maximizing the two applications of (30) and using \(\max(u,v)\geq(u+v)/2\) proves \(L_n^\star\geq cr_n\).  Together, (21), (28), and (30) prove uniform honesty, the achieved expected length, and its minimax lower bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies finite-sample uniform coverage and minimax-order expected length for the explicit bias-aware interval, including every singular-Gram realization. \textit{Gap.} Armstrong and Kolesár provide the relevant bias-aware local-polynomial methodology, but their theorem does not subsume this one: the present target is a moving atom-weighted clamp functional under polynomially vanishing random design, and the proof must control a uniform singular-Gram fallback and expected length over every deterministic threshold sequence. Published continuous-treatment work likewise does not establish that combined honest-length result. \textit{Consumer.} Through the causal frontier lift, this interval replaces unsupported Wald output for the exact lower-threshold \(\texttt{lmtp}\) policy whenever the declared full-data premises hold.

\begin{theorem}[thm:phase-diagram]\label{thm:phase-diagram}
The phase boundary satisfies \(\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}/\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\to\infty\). If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\to0\), then \(r_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\) (regular regime). If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\to c\in(0,\infty)\), both summands of \(r_n\) have order \(n^{-1/2}\) (critical regime). If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\to\infty\) and \(\delta_n\to0\), then \(r_n\asymp\delta_n^{\kappa+1}(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}\) (atom-dominated regime). If \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), then \(r_n\asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}\) (fixed-threshold regime). At \(\delta_n=0\), \(q_x(0)=0\), \(\theta_0(P)=\mathbb E_P[Y]\), and the construction reduces to bounded-mean inference at rate \(n^{-1/2}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 D=\beta\kappa+2\beta+\kappa+1,
 \qquad e_n=\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n},
 \qquad c_n=\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}.
\]
By the definitions of the two scales,
\[
 \frac{c_n}{e_n}
 =n^{1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)-1/(2D)}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because
\[
 2D-(2\beta+\kappa+1)=2\beta\kappa+2\beta+\kappa+1>0.
\]

Write \(T_n=\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\) for the atom term in \(r_n\).  On every subsequence on which \(\delta_n/e_n\to\infty\), Lemma \(\texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases}\) gives
\[
 h_n\asymp(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/(2\beta+1)}
\]
and hence
\[
 T_n\asymp
 n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}
 \delta_n^{D/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Since \(c_n^D=n^{-1/2}\), this identity also yields
\[
 \frac{T_n}{n^{-1/2}}
 \asymp
 \left(\frac{\delta_n}{c_n}\right)^{D/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
On every subsequence on which \(\delta_n/e_n=O(1)\), the other conclusion of Lemma \(\texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases}\) gives \(h_n\asymp e_n\), and therefore
\[
 T_n\leq C e_n^{\beta+\kappa+1}
 =C n^{-(\beta+\kappa+1)/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}
 =o(n^{-1/2}),
\]
where the last relation follows from
\[
 \frac{\beta+\kappa+1}{2\beta+\kappa+1}-\frac12
 =\frac{\kappa+1}{2(2\beta+\kappa+1)}>0.
\]

Suppose first that \(\delta_n/c_n\to0\).  Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(\delta_n/e_n\) is bounded or tends to infinity.  The preceding two displays show on either such further subsequence that \(T_n=o(n^{-1/2})\).  The subsequence criterion for convergence therefore gives \(T_n=o(n^{-1/2})\) on the original sequence, so Definition \(\texttt{def:frontier}\) implies \(r_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\).

If \(\delta_n/c_n\to c\in(0,\infty)\), then \(c_n/e_n\to\infty\) implies \(\delta_n/e_n\to\infty\), and the displayed ratio for \(T_n\) is bounded above and away from zero.  Thus both summands of \(r_n\) have order \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(\delta_n/c_n\to\infty\) and \(\delta_n\to0\), then again \(\delta_n/e_n\to\infty\), while the same ratio tends to infinity.  Consequently the atom term dominates and
\[
 r_n\asymp T_n
 \asymp\delta_n^{\kappa+1}(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
If \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), then \(\delta_n/e_n\to\infty\), so Lemma \(\texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases}\) yields \(h_n\asymp n^{-1/(2\beta+1)}\).  Because \(\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\) is then bounded above and away from zero and \(\beta/(2\beta+1)<1/2\),
\[
 r_n\asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\) and Theorem \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) transfer these comparisons of \(r_n\) to minimax absolute risk and minimax honest expected length, respectively.

It remains to verify the endpoint \(\delta_n=0\).  Proposition \(\texttt{prop:pushforward-setup}\) gives \(q_x(0)=0\) for every stratum.  Its pushforward formula at zero, together with \(d_0(A)=A\), also gives \(P(A=0\mid X=x)=0\); summing over the finitely many strata gives \(P(A=0)=0\).  The definition of the observed-data target therefore gives
\[
 \theta_0(P)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>0\}]=\mathbb E_PY.
\]
In Definition \(\texttt{def:total-gram-estimator}\), \(\widehat b_x=0\) for every \(x\) almost surely, and \(\widehat\nu_n\) is the sample mean of the bounded outcomes on \(I_0\).  Thus the estimator center is exactly that bounded sample mean.  Finally, Definition \(\texttt{def:frontier}\) gives \(r_n=n^{-1/2}\) at \(\delta_n=0\), and the already established coverage and expected-length bounds in Theorem \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) show directly, without any extraneous equation reference, that the accompanying construction is honest bounded-mean inference of root-\(n\) order.  This proves every asserted regime.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The phase diagram states when the published root-\(n\) paradigm is valid, critical, or false for the exact clamp. \textit{Gap.} Existing LMTP theory separates pathwise differentiable policies from excluded threshold policies but supplies no quantitative boundary between regular and atom-dominated sequences. Crippa studies regret for threshold-policy design; it does not derive the sampling-risk or honest-confidence-length transition for the exact clamp mean that this phase diagram establishes. \textit{Consumer.} The four regimes tell an applied user whether a threshold choice supports ordinary precision, requires the bias-aware atom interval, or behaves like a fixed nonparametric point target.

\begin{proposition}[prop:one-cell-calibration]\label{prop:one-cell-calibration}
In the one-stratum model with \(\beta=\kappa=1\) and \(\pi(a)=2a\), whenever \(\delta_n\gg n^{-1/4}\), \(h_n\asymp(n\delta_n)^{-1/3}\), the localized Bernoulli alternatives have product Kullback--Leibler divergence of order \(n\delta_n h_n^3\), and their target separation is of order \(\delta_n^2h_n\). The regular-to-atom transition is exactly \(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/10}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Here \(\beta=\kappa=1\), and therefore the definitions give
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}=n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}
 =n^{-1/[2(1+2+1+1)]}=n^{-1/10}.
\]
If \(\delta_n\gg n^{-1/4}\), Lemma \(\texttt{lem:bandwidth-phases}\) gives
\[
 h_n\asymp(n\delta_n)^{-1/3}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{h_n}{\delta_n}
 \asymp(n\delta_n^4)^{-1/3}\longrightarrow0.
\]

We now make the localized Bernoulli bump construction invoked in Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\) explicit.  Let \(K(u)=(1-|u|)_+\), choose a fixed \(0<\eta\leq\min\{L,1/4\}\), and set
\[
 g_n(a)=\eta h_nK\left(\frac{a-\delta_n}{h_n}\right).
\]
Let \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) have \(X=1\) almost surely, the common conditional treatment density \(\pi(a)=2a\), and conditional outcome laws
\[
 Y\mid A=a\sim\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)
 \quad\text{under }P_0,
 \qquad
 Y\mid A=a\sim\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+g_n(a))
 \quad\text{under }P_1.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the support \([\delta_n-h_n,\delta_n+h_n]\) lies in \([0,1]\): the lower endpoint is positive because \(h_n/\delta_n\to0\), and the upper endpoint is below one because \(\delta_n\leq\bar\delta<1\) and \(h_n\to0\).  Both regressions take values in \([0,1]\).  The function \(g_n\) is \(\eta\)-Lipschitz, so the two regressions satisfy Assumption \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\) for \(\beta=1\) with radius \(L\).  The density \(\pi(a)=2a\) integrates to one, satisfies Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), and satisfies Assumption \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\) for \(\kappa=1\), because the declared ranges give \(c_-\leq2\leq c_+\).  The one-cell mass is one and hence satisfies Assumption \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\).  Thus Definition \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) shows that \(P_0,P_1\in\mathcal M\); in particular, these are same-class alternatives.

For \(0\leq g\leq1/4\), define
\[
 f(g)=\operatorname{KL}\{\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+g),\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)\}.
\]
Direct differentiation gives \(f(0)=f'(0)=0\) and
\[
 f''(g)=\frac{1}{(1/2+g)(1/2-g)}.
\]
This second derivative is bounded above and away from zero on \([0,1/4]\).  The identity
\[
 f(g)=\int_0^g(g-t)f''(t)\,\mathrm dt
\]
therefore gives constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), independent of \(g\), such that
\[
 c g^2\leq
 \operatorname{KL}\{\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+g),\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)\}
 \leq Cg^2.
\]
The two laws have the same treatment margin.  Since \(a\asymp\delta_n\) uniformly on the support of \(g_n\), conditioning on \(A\) and changing variables \(u=(a-\delta_n)/h_n\) show that the one-observation divergence satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_1,P_0)
 &\asymp \int_0^1 2a\,g_n(a)^2\,\mathrm da \\
 &\asymp \delta_n h_n^3
 \int_{-1}^1K(u)^2\,\mathrm du
 \asymp\delta_nh_n^3.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:iid-sampling}\) makes the product log-likelihood ratio the sum of its \(n\) one-observation counterparts.  Taking expectation, equivalently using the product-divergence calculation in the localized lower bound of Theorem \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\), therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =n\operatorname{KL}(P_1,P_0)
 \asymp n\delta_nh_n^3.
\]
The bandwidth relation makes this quantity of constant order.

For the target separation, the definition of \(\theta_{\delta_n}\), together with \(\pi(a)=2a\), gives
\[
 q(\delta_n)=\int_0^{\delta_n}2a\,\mathrm da=\delta_n^2
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta_{\delta_n}(P_1)-\theta_{\delta_n}(P_0)
 &=q(\delta_n)g_n(\delta_n)
   +\int_{\delta_n}^1 2a\,g_n(a)\,\mathrm da \\
 &=\eta\delta_n^2h_n+O(\delta_nh_n^2).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Both displayed contributions are nonnegative, and \(h_n/\delta_n\to0\); hence the separation is \(\asymp\delta_n^2h_n\).  This is the explicit localized bump construction used by the minimax lower bound, so the reference is unambiguous.

Finally, equating the atom separation with the root-\(n\) term and substituting the bandwidth order gives
\[
 \delta_n^2(n\delta_n)^{-1/3}=n^{-1/2}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \delta_n^{5/3}=n^{-1/6}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \delta_n=n^{-1/10}.
\]
This is exactly the transition asserted in Theorem \(\texttt{thm:phase-diagram}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This explicit calibration is the requested early-kill test and checks the general exponents against a transparent design. \textit{Gap.} No cited LMTP or degenerate-design result computes this atom-attenuated transition for the deterministic clamp mean. \textit{Consumer.} The calibration supplies a simulation benchmark and a unit test for an implementation of the proposed interval.

\begin{theorem}[thm:causal-frontier-lift]\label{thm:causal-frontier-lift}
There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the declared model constants, such that for every deterministic threshold sequence and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 c r_n\leq R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 \leq\sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}|\widehat\theta_n-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})|
 \leq C r_n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in\mathrm{CI}_n\}\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]\leq Cr_n,
\]
and every uniformly \((1-\alpha)\)-honest observed-sample interval over \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) has worst-case expected length at least \(cr_n\).  Thus \(R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\asymp L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\asymp r_n\), attained by the same total-Gram estimator and bias-aware interval as in the observed-data experiment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\) denote the observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin of \(P^{\mathrm F}\).  Proposition \(\texttt{prop:observed-margin-surjectivity}\) proves that \(\Gamma\) maps \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) onto \(\mathcal M\) and that, for every finite \(n\) and every declared deterministic threshold,
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=R_n^\star,
 \qquad
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=L_n^\star. \tag{1}
\]
Proposition \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\) gives the pointwise target identity
\[
 \psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})
 =\theta_{\delta_n}\{\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\}. \tag{2}
\]
Because every estimator considered in \(\texttt{def:causal-frontier-criteria}\) is measurable with respect to the observed sample, its sampling law under \((P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\) is \(\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\).  Combining this fact with (2), and then using the surjectivity of \(\Gamma\), gives, for every observed-sample estimator \(T\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 \left|T-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\right| \\
 &\quad=
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 \left|T-\theta_{\delta_n}\{\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\}\right| \\
 &\quad=
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 \left|T-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\right|. \tag{3}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Apply (3) with \(T=\widehat\theta_n\).  The established observed-data bound \(\texttt{thm:minimax-risk}\), followed by (1), yields constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the declared model constants, such that, for every deterministic threshold sequence and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 c r_n
 \leq R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 \leq
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 \left|\widehat\theta_n-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\right|
 \leq C r_n. \tag{4}
\]

The same argument applies to coverage and length.  For every observed-sample interval \(C_n\), equation (2), equality of the observed-sample laws, and surjectivity give
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 (P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in C_n\}
 =
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}
 P^{\otimes n}\{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in C_n\}, \tag{5}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 =
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]. \tag{6}
\]
Taking \(C_n=\mathrm{CI}_n\) in (5)--(6) and invoking \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\) proves
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 (P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in\mathrm{CI}_n\}
 \geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 [\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)]
 \leq C r_n. \tag{7}
\]
Equation (5) also shows that the class of intervals honest over \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) is exactly the class of intervals honest over \(\mathcal M\).  Hence the converse part of \(\texttt{thm:honest-length}\), together with (1), gives
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=L_n^\star\geq c r_n. \tag{8}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and (8) prove \(R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\asymp L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\asymp r_n\), while (4) and (7) prove attainment by the displayed total-Gram estimator and bias-aware interval.  The equalities in (1), (3), (5), and (6) hold for every \(n\); only the comparison with \(r_n\) invokes the sufficiently-large-\(n\) conclusions of the two observed-data theorems.  Thus the claimed causal result is the exact transport corollary of the purpose-built onto full-data lift, the causal bridge, and the observed-data frontier.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem is the exact causal transport corollary: the purpose-built full-data class maps onto the observed class, the causal bridge aligns the targets, and the observed sampling law is unchanged. The novel minimax-risk and honest expected-length frontier is established by the observed-data theorems; this result transfers it, including attainment, without claiming an independently novel causal frontier. \textit{Gap.} Published threshold-policy identification does not supply this paper's explicit onto full-data lift or the deterministic triangular-array observed frontier. The theorem closes only the transport step for the declared structural class, using the proved pointwise bridge under fiberwise structural-mean continuity and the observed-margin surjection. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem transports the observed frontier to the same exact lower-threshold policy specification accepted by the official \(\texttt{lmtp}\) user-policy workflow and motivates a future implementation of the total-Gram estimator and interval; it does not claim that workflow currently implements them.

\begin{lemma}[lem:standard-borel-regular-conditional-law]\label{lem:standard-borel-regular-conditional-law}
Let \(S\) and \(T\) be standard Borel spaces and let \((Z,W)\) be an \(S\times T\)-valued random element.  There is a Borel probability kernel \(K:S\times\mathcal B(T)\to[0,1]\) such that, writing \(P_Z\) for the law of \(Z\),
\[
 \Pr\{Z\in B,W\in C\}=\int_B K(z,C)\,P_Z(\mathrm dz)
\]
for every Borel \(B\subseteq S\) and \(C\subseteq T\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[prop:observed-margin-surjectivity]\label{prop:observed-margin-surjectivity}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), there exists \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) whose observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin is exactly \(P\).  Thus the observed-margin map from \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) onto \(\mathcal M\) is surjective.  Moreover, for every \(n\) and every declared deterministic \(\delta_n\),
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=R_n^\star
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=L_n^\star.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\).  We first construct a full-data lift and then compare the two decision problems.  Put \(Z=(X,A)\).  Since \(\mathcal X\) is finite, both \(\mathcal X\times[0,1]\) and \([0,1]\) are standard Borel spaces.  Kallenberg's regular-conditional-distribution theorem, in the exact form recorded by \(\texttt{lem:standard-borel-regular-conditional-law}\), supplies a Borel probability kernel \(K\) from \(\mathcal X\times[0,1]\) to \([0,1]\) that is a conditional law of \(Y\) given \(Z\).  Write \(K_x(a,C)=K((x,a),C)\).  By \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), for Borel \(B,C\subseteq[0,1]\),
\[
 P\{X=x,A\in B,Y\in C\}
 =p_x\int_B K_x(a,C)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da. \tag{1}
\]
The map
\[
 m_x^0(a)=\int_{[0,1]}y\,K_x(a,\mathrm dy) \tag{2}
\]
is Borel: kernel integration is Borel first for indicators, then for nonnegative simple functions, and hence for the bounded identity by monotone approximation.  It is a version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\).  By \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), \(\mu_x^P\) is a continuous version of the same conditional mean.  Thus
\[
 E_x=\{a\in[0,\bar\delta]:m_x^0(a)\ne\mu_x^P(a)\} \tag{3}
\]
is Borel.  Since \(p_x>0\) by \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\), uniqueness of conditional expectations together with \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) yields
\[
 \int_{E_x}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da=0. \tag{4}
\]
Modify the kernel only on this exposure-null set by
\[
 K_x^\star(a,C)=
 \mathbf 1\{a\notin E_x\}K_x(a,C)
 +\mathbf 1\{a\in E_x\}\mathbf 1\{\mu_x^P(a)\in C\}. \tag{5}
\]
For each Borel \(C\), (5) is Borel in \(a\), and for each \(a\) it is a probability measure in \(C\).  Equations (3)--(5) give
\[
 \int y\,K_x^\star(a,\mathrm dy)=\mu_x^P(a)
 \quad(0\leq a\leq\bar\delta), \tag{6}
\]
whereas (4) gives
\[
 p_x\int_BK_x^\star(a,C)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 =p_x\int_BK_x(a,C)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da. \tag{7}
\]

We realize the modified kernel by an explicit measurable quantile.  Let \(D=\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\), including \(1\), set \(F_x(a,t)=K_x^\star(a,[0,t])\), and define
\[
 g_x(a,u)=\inf\{r\in D:F_x(a,r)\geq u\}. \tag{8}
\]
The set in (8) is nonempty because \(F_x(a,1)=1\).  For every real \(t\),
\[
 \{(a,u):g_x(a,u)<t\}
 =\bigcup_{\substack{r\in D\\r<t}}
 \{(a,u):u\leq F_x(a,r)\}, \tag{9}
\]
a countable union of Borel sets, so \(g_x\) is jointly Borel.  Continuity from above of the probability measure \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\) makes \(t\mapsto F_x(a,t)\) right-continuous.  Consequently
\[
 \lambda\{u:g_x(a,u)\leq t\}=F_x(a,t), \tag{10}
\]
where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure on \([0,1]\).  Indeed, the two inclusions follow respectively from rationals decreasing to \(t\) and, when \(u>F_x(a,t)\), from right continuity at \(t\).  Since the intervals \([0,t]\) generate \(\mathcal B([0,1])\), (10) says that \(u\mapsto g_x(a,u)\) pushes \(\lambda\) forward to \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\).

On a probability space carrying \((X,A)\) with its \(P\)-margin and an independent \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), define simultaneously
\[
 Y(a)=g_X(a,U)\quad(0\leq a\leq1),
 \qquad Y=g_X(A,U). \tag{11}
\]
Finiteness of \(\mathcal X\) and joint Borel measurability of the \(g_x\)'s make \((a,\omega)\mapsto Y(a,\omega)\) jointly measurable.  Equation (11) verifies \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) on one probability-one event, and independence gives \(A\perp U\mid X\), namely \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\).  By (10), the conditional law of \(Y\) given \((X,A)=(x,a)\) is \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\).  Equations (1) and (7), first on measurable rectangles and then on the generated product sigma-field, therefore show that the observed \((X,A,Y)\)-law is exactly \(P\).  Finally, (6) and (10) imply
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)
 =\int_0^1g_x(a,u)\,\mathrm du
 =\int y\,K_x^\star(a,\mathrm dy)
 =\mu_x^P(a),
 \qquad 0\leq a\leq\bar\delta. \tag{12}
\]
The right side is continuous by \(\texttt{ass:holder-regression}\), so \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\) holds.  Thus (11), with the member-specific carrier \(\mathcal U=[0,1]\), is a member of \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) by \(\texttt{def:full-data-model-class}\).  This proves that the observed-margin map \(\Gamma\) satisfies
\[
 \Gamma(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F})=\mathcal M. \tag{13}
\]

By \(\texttt{prop:causal-bridge}\), for every member and every declared threshold,
\[
 \psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})
 =\theta_{\delta_n}\{\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\}. \tag{14}
\]
If \(T\) is any estimator measurable with respect to \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\), its law under \((P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\) is its law under \(\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\).  Equations (13)--(14) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 |T-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})|
 =\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}|T-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)|. \tag{15}
\]
The estimator classes are identical, because both consist of measurable maps of the observed sample.  Taking their infima in (15), using \(\texttt{def:causal-frontier-criteria}\) and the definition of \(R_n^\star\), gives
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=R_n^\star. \tag{16}
\]
For any observed-sample interval \(C_n\), the same argument gives
\[
 \inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 (P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in C_n\}
 =\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P^{\otimes n}\{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in C_n\} \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 =\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]. \tag{18}
\]
Hence the coverage constraints select the same intervals and their objectives coincide.  Taking the infimum in (18) over the common feasible set in (17) proves
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star=L_n^\star. \tag{19}
\]
Equations (13), (16), and (19) are the asserted conclusions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} For the fixed-Hölder class, the measurable kernel repair and quantile lift make the causal and observed clamp-target decision criteria exactly equivalent. \textit{Gap.} Identification alone gives a one-sided transfer; the proof supplies onto-ness of the observed-margin map. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition is the exact-equivalence input to \(\texttt{thm:causal-frontier-lift}\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:hoeffding-bounded-average]\label{lem:hoeffding-bounded-average}
If \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) are independent random variables taking values in \([0,1]\), then, for every \(t>0\), \(\Pr\{|m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m Z_i-m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E Z_i|>t\}\leq2\exp(-2mt^2)\).
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the finite-sample concentration input used by the explicit continuity-only interval. \textit{Gap.} The theorem is classical substrate rather than a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} The continuity-only frontier invokes it for the retained-course and atom-mass averages.

\begin{lemma}[lem:continuity-regression-extension-unique]\label{lem:continuity-regression-extension-unique}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\), there is exactly one continuous function on \([0,\bar\delta]\) that is an almost-everywhere version of \(\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\).  Hence \(\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P\) is well defined.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Existence is part of \(\texttt{def:continuity-model-class}\).  For uniqueness, let \(f\) and \(g\) be two continuous extensions.  Uniqueness of conditional expectations and \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) give \(f(a)=g(a)\) for \(\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\)-almost every \(a\in[0,\bar\delta]\).  By \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), \(\pi_x(a)\geq c_-a^\kappa>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\in(0,\bar\delta]\), so \(f=g\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere on that interval.  If the continuous function \(f-g\) were nonzero at some positive point, it would be bounded away from zero on a nonempty open interval, contradicting almost-everywhere equality.  Thus \(f=g\) on \((0,\bar\delta]\), and continuity at zero yields \(f(0)=g(0)\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The positive-density interior and continuity make the qualitative conditional-regression extension pointwise unique. \textit{Gap.} The continuity-only functional is well defined only after version ambiguity at the threshold is removed. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma fixes the regression value used by the continuity-only causal bridge and observed functional.

\begin{proposition}[prop:continuity-causal-bridge]\label{prop:continuity-causal-bridge}
Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\) have observed margin \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  Then \(m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and every \(a\in[0,\bar\delta]\), and, for every \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\),
\[
 \psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})
 =\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
 +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta)
 =\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a member and a stratum \(x\).  Simultaneous consistency and conditional independence, respectively \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) and \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\), imply for every Borel \(B\subseteq[0,1]\)
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 =\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[g_x(A,U)\mathbf 1\{A\in B\}\mid X=x]
 =\int_Bm_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da. \tag{26}
\]
The definition of \(\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P\), whose well-definedness is proved by \(\texttt{lem:continuity-regression-extension-unique}\), together with \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\), gives the same left side as
\[
 \int_B\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da. \tag{27}
\]
Thus the two functions agree \(\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da\)-almost everywhere.  By \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\), \(\pi_x(a)\geq c_-a^\kappa>0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(a>0\); hence they agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \((0,\bar\delta]\).  Both are continuous there, the first by \(\texttt{ass:full-data-response-continuity}\) and the second by \(\texttt{def:continuity-model-class}\).  A continuous function that vanishes almost everywhere on an interval vanishes everywhere, and continuity at zero then also gives equality at zero.  Therefore
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)
 \quad\text{for every }a\in[0,\bar\delta]. \tag{28}
\]
For \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\), the clamp definition and simultaneous consistency give
\[
 Y\{d_\delta(A)\}
 =Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}+g_X(\delta,U)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{29}
\]
Conditional independence and the conditional-density identity imply
\[
 \mathbb E[g_X(\delta,U)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}\mid X=x]
 =m_x^{\mathrm F}(\delta)q_x(\delta). \tag{30}
\]
Average (29) over \(X\), substitute (28) into (30), and use \(P(X=x)=p_x\).  The resulting expression is exactly \(\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)\) by \(\texttt{def:continuity-clamp-functional}\), proving the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Qualitative continuity upgrades exposure-almost-everywhere regression equality to the pointwise equality needed at the clamp atom. \textit{Gap.} The continuity-only class cannot reuse a Hölder-radius argument, but it still supports the exact causal bridge. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition is used by the continuity-only surjectivity and frontier results.

\begin{proposition}[prop:continuity-observed-margin-surjectivity]\label{prop:continuity-observed-margin-surjectivity}
The observed-margin map from \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\) onto \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) is surjective.  For every \(n\) and every declared deterministic \(\delta_n\), the observed-sample clamp-target criteria satisfy
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star=R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star,
 \qquad
 L_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star=L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  Apply Kallenberg's theorem as recorded by \(\texttt{lem:standard-borel-regular-conditional-law}\) to \((Z,W)=((X,A),Y)\), and write its Borel conditional kernel as \(K_x(a,\cdot)\).  Define \(m_x^0(a)=\int yK_x(a,\mathrm dy)\).  By the defining property of \(\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P\), the Borel set
\[
 E_x=\{a\in[0,\bar\delta]:m_x^0(a)\ne\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)\} \tag{31}
\]
has \(\int_{E_x}\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da=0\); here \(p_x>0\) follows from \(\texttt{ass:stratum-mass}\).  Replace \(K_x(a,\cdot)\) on \(E_x\) by the point mass at \(\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)\), obtaining a Borel kernel \(K_x^\star\).  This leaves the observed joint law unchanged and gives
\[
 \int yK_x^\star(a,\mathrm dy)=\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)
 \quad(0\leq a\leq\bar\delta). \tag{32}
\]
For completeness, use the same explicit quantile construction as in (8): with \(D=\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\),
\[
 g_x(a,u)=\inf\{r\in D:K_x^\star(a,[0,r])\geq u\}. \tag{33}
\]
The identity (9) proves joint Borel measurability, and right continuity of the distribution function proves that a uniform \(u\) is pushed forward to \(K_x^\star(a,\cdot)\).  Take \((X,A)\) with its \(P\)-margin, take \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) independently, and set \(Y(a)=g_X(a,U)\) and \(Y=g_X(A,U)\).  The rectangle identity corresponding to (7) proves that the observed margin is \(P\); independence proves exchangeability; the simultaneous definition proves consistency; and (32) proves
\[
 m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(a)
 \quad(0\leq a\leq\bar\delta). \tag{34}
\]
The right side is continuous by \(\texttt{def:continuity-model-class}\).  Hence the lift belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\), proving surjectivity.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the observed-margin map.  Surjectivity and \(\texttt{prop:continuity-causal-bridge}\) give
\[
 \Gamma(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F})=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}},
 \qquad
 \psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})
 =\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}\{\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\}. \tag{35}
\]
Every admissible decision rule is the same measurable map of \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) in the two criteria, and its sample law depends on \(P^{\mathrm F}\) only through \(\Gamma(P^{\mathrm F})\).  Thus (35) gives equality of the two worst-case risks for every estimator, and equality of worst-case coverage and expected length for every interval.  Taking the respective infima in \(\texttt{def:continuity-frontier-criteria}\) proves both displayed criterion identities.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A null-set repair and measurable quantile lift show that the qualitative continuity class is not merely a one-sided causal restriction. \textit{Gap.} Exact finite-sample causal and observed criterion identities require onto-ness of the observed-margin map. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition transfers the continuity-only matched frontier exactly to the causal clamp mean.

\begin{theorem}[thm:continuity-only-frontier]\label{thm:continuity-only-frontier}
There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the declared model constants and on \(\alpha\), such that, for every deterministic \(\delta_n\in[0,\bar\delta]\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 c s_n\leq R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star
 \leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}|\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-\theta_{\delta_n,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq Cs_n.
\]
The interval \(\mathrm{CI}_{n,\mathrm{cont}}\) has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\), has worst-case expected length at most \(Cs_n\), and every uniformly \((1-\alpha)\)-honest interval has worst-case expected length at least \(cs_n\).  The same estimator and interval obey the corresponding causal statements over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\), and
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star=R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star,
 \qquad
 L_{n,\mathrm{cont},\mathrm F}^\star=L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star.
\]
Consequently the continuity-only elbow is \(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/[2(\kappa+1)]}\); at \(\delta_n=0\) the rate is \(n^{-1/2}\), whereas if \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), neither uniform risk consistency nor vanishing uniformly honest expected length is possible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(m_j=|I_j|\) for \(j=0,1\), and abbreviate \(\delta=\delta_n\).  For \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\), define
\[
 \nu=\mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}],\qquad
 b_x=P(X=x,A\leq\delta)=p_xq_x(\delta),\qquad
 B=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_x=P(A\leq\delta),
\]
and let \(\widehat B=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\widehat b_x\).  The equality defining \(b_x\) follows from \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-law}\) and the definition of \(q_x\).  By \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-thinning}\),
\[
 B=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\int_0^\delta\pi_x(a)\,\mathrm da
 \leq \frac{c_+}{\kappa+1}\delta^{\kappa+1}. \tag{1}
\]
This also covers the boundary value \(\delta=0\), for which \(B=0\).

Put \(T=\widehat\nu_n+\widehat B/2\).  The functional in \(\texttt{def:continuity-clamp-functional}\) lies in \([0,1]\): it is the mean of a \([0,1]\)-valued retained outcome plus the disjoint atom contribution, whose regression values lie in \([0,1]\).  Projection onto \([0,1]\) is a contraction toward every point of \([0,1]\).  Hence \(\texttt{def:continuity-fallback-estimator}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 &\leq |T-\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|\\
 &\leq |\widehat\nu_n-\nu|+\frac12|\widehat B-B|
   +\left|\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_x
       \left(\frac12-\mu_{x,\mathrm{cont}}^P(\delta)\right)\right|\\
 &\leq |\widehat\nu_n-\nu|+\frac12|\widehat B-B|+\frac B2.
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
\]
Under \(\texttt{ass:iid-sampling}\), the first two centered terms in (2) are centered averages of independent \([0,1]\)-valued variables.  If \(V\in[0,1]\), then \(\operatorname{Var}(V)\leq1/4\), because \(\operatorname{Var}(V)\leq\mathbb E[(V-1/2)^2]\leq1/4\).  The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, in the elementary form \((\mathbb E|Z|)^2\leq\mathbb E[Z^2]\), therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\nu_n-\nu|\leq\frac1{2\sqrt{m_0}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat B-B|\leq\frac1{2\sqrt{m_1}}. \tag{3}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the block-size condition implies \(m_j\geq n/8\).  Combining (1)--(3) proves, uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-
       \theta_{\delta,
       \mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq C\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}\}=Cs_n. \tag{4}
\]
The left inequality \(R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star\leq
\sup_P\mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-
\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|\) follows directly from the infimum in \(\texttt{def:continuity-frontier-criteria}\).

We next prove uniform coverage.  By \(\texttt{lem:hoeffding-bounded-average}\) and the definition of \(t_{j,\alpha}\) in \(\texttt{def:continuity-honest-interval}\),
\[
 P^{\otimes n}\{|\widehat\nu_n-\nu|>t_{0,\alpha}\}\leq\frac\alpha2,
 \qquad
 P^{\otimes n}\{|\widehat B-B|>t_{1,\alpha}\}\leq\frac\alpha2. \tag{5}
\]
No independence between the two displayed averages is needed for the following union bound.  On the intersection of the complementary events in (5), \(B\leq\widehat B+t_{1,\alpha}\), so (2) implies
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{n,\mathrm{cont}}-\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P)|
 \leq t_{0,\alpha}+\frac12t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12B
 \leq t_{0,\alpha}+t_{1,\alpha}+\frac12\widehat B. \tag{6}
\]
Thus the interval in \(\texttt{def:continuity-honest-interval}\) contains the target with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over the model.  Intersecting an interval containing a point of \([0,1]\) with \([0,1]\) preserves containment and cannot increase length.  Since \(\mathbb E_P\widehat B=B\), (1) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_{n,\mathrm{cont}})]
 \leq2t_{0,\alpha}+2t_{1,\alpha}+B
 \leq Cs_n. \tag{7}
\]

It remains to prove the two minimax converses.  We first record the reduction used for both.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be two laws with scalar target values \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) separated by \(d=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\).  If \(Q_0\wedge Q_1\) denotes their common part, then its mass is \(1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  The triangle inequality, integrated against this common part, gives every estimator \(T\) the bound
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|T-\vartheta_j|
 \geq\frac d2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}. \tag{8}
\]
When \(Q_1\ll Q_0\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to its likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 =\frac12\mathbb E_{Q_0}\left|\frac{\mathrm dQ_1}{\mathrm dQ_0}-1\right|
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)}. \tag{9}
\]
Moreover, for product laws, independence and multiplication of likelihood ratios give
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\{1+\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)\}^n. \tag{10}
\]
For intervals, suppose \(C_n\) covers each \(\vartheta_j\) under \(Q_j\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).  The definition of total variation and a union bound under \(Q_0\) imply
\[
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\in C_n\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1). \tag{11}
\]
On this event \(\operatorname{len}(C_n)\geq d\).

Both lower-bound experiments use one law in every stratum: take \(P(X=x)=1/J\) and
\(\pi_x(a)=(\kappa+1)a^\kappa\) for \(0\leq a\leq1\).  This is a probability density.  The declared restrictions \(p_{\min}\leq1/J\) and \(c_-\leq\kappa+1\leq c_+\) show that the stratum-mass and two-sided polynomial-thinning conditions hold.  Thus it remains only to check boundedness and continuity of each regression constructed below.

For the global-shift experiment, let
\[
 P_0(Y=1\mid X,A)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 P_1(Y=1\mid X,A)=\frac12+\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n=\gamma n^{-1/2}, \tag{12}
\]
where \(0<\gamma\leq1/4\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), both conditional means are continuous and take values in \([0,1]\), so both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  The retained probability and atom probability partition the treatment space, and therefore the functional in \(\texttt{def:continuity-clamp-functional}\) has separation
\[
 |\theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_1)-
   \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_0)|=\varepsilon_n. \tag{13}
\]
Direct summation over \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) gives \(\chi^2(P_1,P_0)=4\varepsilon_n^2\).  Hence (10) and \(1+u\leq e^u\) give
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+4\varepsilon_n^2)^n-1
 \leq e^{4\gamma^2}-1. \tag{14}
\]

For the localized experiment, suppose \(\delta>0\), fix \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), and set
\[
 w_n=\frac12\min\left\{\delta,\,1-\bar\delta,\,
             \frac{\zeta}{n\delta^\kappa}\right\}>0,
 \qquad
 H_n(a)=\left(1-\frac{|a-\delta|}{w_n}\right)_+. \tag{15}
\]
Then \(w_n\leq\delta/2\), and the support of \(H_n\) is contained in \([0,1]\).  Define
\[
 P_0(Y=1\mid X,A=a)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 P_1(Y=1\mid X,A=a)=\frac12+\eta H_n(a). \tag{16}
\]
These regressions are continuous and lie in \([0,1]\); the model imposes no common modulus, so both laws are members of the same fixed class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\), even though \(w_n\) may shrink.  Direct Bernoulli calculation and the upper density envelope show
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(P_1,P_0)
 &=4\eta^2\mathbb E[H_n(A)^2]\\
 &\leq4\eta^2P(|A-\delta|\leq w_n)\\
 &\leq 8c_+\left(\frac32\right)^\kappa
       \eta^2w_n\delta^\kappa.
\end{aligned}\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, on the integration interval in (17), \(a\leq\delta+w_n\leq3\delta/2\).  Let \(C_0=8c_+(3/2)^\kappa\).  Equations (10), (15), and (17) yield
\[
 \chi^2(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\exp(C_0\eta^2nw_n\delta^\kappa)-1
 \leq\exp(C_0\eta^2\zeta/2)-1. \tag{18}
\]
For the reference density, \(q_x(\delta)=\delta^{\kappa+1}\) in every stratum.  The retained-course difference is nonnegative, while \(H_n(\delta)=1\).  Therefore the exact observed functional gives
\[
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_1)-
 \theta_{\delta,\mathrm{cont}}(P_0)
 =\eta\mathbb E[H_n(A)\mathbf 1\{A>\delta\}]
   +\eta\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)
 \geq\eta\delta^{\kappa+1}. \tag{19}
\]

Choose a constant \(\tau\) with \(0<\tau<1-2\alpha\).  Since the right sides of (14) and (18) tend to zero with \(\gamma\downarrow0\) and \(\zeta\downarrow0\), respectively, choose fixed positive \(\gamma\) and \(\zeta\) so that (9) bounds the product-law total variation in each experiment by \(\tau\).  Applying (8) to (13) and (19), and then taking the infimum over all estimators, gives
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}\}
 \geq\frac c2\{n^{-1/2}+\delta^{\kappa+1}\}
 =\frac c2s_n. \tag{20}
\]
When \(\delta=0\), the global pair alone proves (20), because the second term vanishes.  Likewise, (11), first for the global pair and then for the localized pair, implies every uniformly \((1-\alpha)\)-honest interval satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{len}(C_n)]
 \geq c\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta^{\kappa+1}\}
 \geq\frac c2s_n. \tag{21}
\]
Taking the infimum over honest intervals proves the asserted lower bound for \(L_{n,\mathrm{cont}}^\star\).  Equations (4), (7), (20), and (21) establish the matched observed-data risk and honest-length frontier.

Finally, \(\texttt{prop:continuity-causal-bridge}\) identifies, for each member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm F}\), its causal clamp mean with the displayed observed functional of its observed margin.  The observed-margin surjectivity and exact criterion equalities in \(\texttt{prop:continuity-observed-margin-surjectivity}\) transfer both lower bounds and both upper guarantees, including coverage, to the full-data class without altering the estimator or interval.  This is an exact finite-sample transport through the paper-defined full-data lift.

The two summands of \(s_n\) have the same order exactly when
\(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/[2(\kappa+1)]}\).  At the boundary \(\delta_n=0\), the atom term is zero and the rate is \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), then \(\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\to\delta_0^{\kappa+1}>0\), so (20) rules out uniform risk consistency and (21) rules out vanishing uniformly honest expected length.  These are the claimed regular boundary, elbow, and fixed-positive-threshold regimes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This secondary headline quantifies the exact clamp functional on the maximal declared continuity-only class. Low's 1997 expected-length inequality and smoothness-nonadaptation corollary are the generic honest-inference antecedent; the new content here is the explicit matched threshold-atom risk and length, elbow, and fixed-positive-threshold impossibility. \textit{Gap.} Low (1997) proves a general expected-length lower bound under uniform coverage and a nonadaptation corollary for derivative-smoothness density classes, but does not derive the atom-weighted rate \(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\), its deterministic moving-threshold elbow, or the corresponding clamp-functional risk theorem. Published threshold-LMTP work likewise does not provide those matched statistical frontiers. \textit{Consumer.} The result tells users of the exact one-time threshold policy why fixed positive thresholds require a declared common smoothness modulus for uniform consistency, while crediting the prior generic nonadaptation obstruction; it does not claim that current \(\texttt{lmtp}\) software implements the fallback estimator or interval.

\section{Interpretation}

The comparison between \(r_n\) and \(s_n\) isolates the statistical value of a common quantitative smoothness modulus.  Hölder control lets local information shrink the atom-regression error; qualitative continuity without a common modulus does not, because a fixed-height bump can be hidden in an arbitrarily narrow exposure window.  The continuity-only elbow \(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/[2(\kappa+1)]}\) therefore marks when the whole atom mass becomes first-order.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The expected-length theorem requires the Hölder radius and thinning exponent to be declared. Adaptation to unknown \(\beta\) and \(\kappa\) is a separate ordered-modulus problem and may require a logarithmic payment. The accepted exact-modulus construction is retained only as a candidate handle: no theorem asserting its honesty, nondegeneracy, optimality, or a sharp asymptotic minimax constant is claimed. The higher-order literal-integral issue and the general exact-constant question remain open. The primary proved claims concern the sharp minimax risk and uniformly honest expected-length order uniformly over deterministic threshold sequences.

\section{Honest scope}

The paper fixes a finite number of strata, a bounded outcome, known smoothness radius on the Hölder class, and known thinning exponent. The novel matched minimax-risk and honest-length frontier is first proved for the observed-data functional. Its causal counterpart is not positioned as an independently novel frontier: it is transported exactly through the paper-defined full-data class, whose members have observed margins in the statistical class and satisfy simultaneous latent-response consistency, conditional exchangeability, and fiberwise structural-mean continuity on the threshold range. The causal bridge and the onto observed-margin proposition prove equality of targets, sampling experiments, minimax risks, and minimax honest expected lengths on precisely that class. These statements do not extend automatically to arbitrary longitudinal histories, arbitrary latent carriers outside the declared packages, or structural laws lacking the continuity premise. The continuity-only theorem is likewise confined to its separate author-defined class. Low's generic honest-inference nonadaptation result is prior art; the continuity-only contribution is the explicit threshold-atom rate \(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\), its elbow, and the fixed-positive-threshold impossibility. The paper proves no longitudinal recursion, no simultaneous band over a continuum of thresholds, and no claim about the validity or invalidity of McCoy et al.'s dominating-measure density-ratio estimator for their stochastic-shift effect-modifier target. Neither proposed estimator nor interval is claimed to be implemented in current \(\texttt{lmtp}\) software.
