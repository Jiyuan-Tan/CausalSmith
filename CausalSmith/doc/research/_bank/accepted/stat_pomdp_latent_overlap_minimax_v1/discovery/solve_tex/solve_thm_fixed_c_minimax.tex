%%% FIELD proof-phiw-upper
Put \(r:=\log(1/\alpha)=1/t_0\) and \(D:=2r+\log L\).  Thus
\[
 \beta=\frac{2r}{D}=\frac{2}{2+t_0\zeta}.
\]
Fix a member \(M\in\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\) and an integer \(0\le k<T\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}sequential\text{-}ignorability}\), conditional on the joint state and the past, multiplication by \(\rho_j\) changes the action law at epoch \(j\) from \(b\) to \(e\).  Iterating this identity over the \(k+1\) actions in \(Z_t(k)\), and using \(\mathrm{ass{:}pomdp\text{-}kernel}\), gives
\[
 \mu_k:=E_M[Z_t(k)]=d_bP_e^kg_e .
\]
There are \(k\) state transitions here because the last ratio changes the distribution of \(A_t\), whose target-policy reward mean is \(g_e(S_t)\).  No independence between \(Y_t\) and \(S_{t+1}\) is used.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}contraction}\), iteration gives
\[
 \|d_bP_e^k-d_eP_e^k\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \alpha^k\|d_b-d_e\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le \alpha^k.
\]
Since \(d_eP_e^k=d_e\), \(|g_e|\le1\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}reward}\), and integration of a \([-1,1]\)-valued function changes by at most twice total variation, \(\mathrm{def{:}target\text{-}value}\) yields
\[
 |\mu_k-\theta(K,e)|\le2\alpha^k. \tag{1}
\]

The overlap assumption gives \(0\le\rho_j\le L\) on every behavior-supported cell and hence \(\rho_j^2\le L\rho_j\).  Repeated conditional expectation under \(\mathrm{ass{:}sequential\text{-}ignorability}\) gives expectation one for a chronological product of policy ratios.  Consequently
\[
 E_M|Z_t(k)|\le1,
 \qquad E_M[Z_t(k)^2]\le L^{k+1}. \tag{2}
\]
For \(1\le h\le k\), the two ratio products in \(Z_t(k)Z_{t+h}(k)\) overlap at exactly \(k+1-h\) actions.  Applying \(\rho^2\le L\rho\) at those actions, using \(|Y_tY_{t+h}|\le1\), and cancelling the remaining single ratios gives
\[
 E_M|Z_t(k)Z_{t+h}(k)|\le L^{k+1-h}.
\]
Because \(|\mu_k|\le1\), this implies
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_M(Z_t(k),Z_{t+h}(k))|
 \le2L^{k+1-h},\qquad 1\le h\le k. \tag{3}
\]

For \(h\ge k+1\), condition on the latent history through \(S_{t+1}\).  The Markov property and the same likelihood-ratio cancellation show that the conditional mean of the future block is
\[
 H_h(S_{t+1}),\qquad
 H_h:=P_b^{h-k-1}P_e^kg_e.
\]
For every bounded \(f\), the contraction assumption implies
\(\operatorname{osc}(P_b^mf)\le\alpha^m\operatorname{osc}(f)\).  Since \(\operatorname{osc}(P_e^kg_e)\le2\),
\[
 \operatorname{osc}(H_h)\le2\alpha^{h-k-1}.
\]
Stationarity in \(\mathrm{ass{:}stationary\text{-}start}\) gives \(E_MH_h(S_{t+1})=\mu_k\); this mean lies between the minimum and maximum of \(H_h\).  Using (2),
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_M(Z_t(k),Z_{t+h}(k))|
 =|E_M[Z_t(k)\{H_h(S_{t+1})-\mu_k\}]|
 \le2\alpha^{h-k-1}. \tag{4}
\]

Let \(N:=T-k\).  Equations (2)--(4), stationarity, and the variance identity for a finite sum give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Var}_M\!\left(N^{-1}\sum_{t=k+1}^TZ_t(k)\right)
 &\le \frac1N\left\{L^{k+1}
       +4\sum_{h=1}^kL^{k+1-h}
       +4\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha^j\right\}\\
 &\le \frac1N\left\{L^{k+1}\left(1+\frac4{L-1}\right)
       +\frac4{1-\alpha}\right\}.
 \end{split}\tag{5}
\]
This is the claimed geometric covariance sum and has no factor \(k\).

For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(k_T=\lfloor\log T/D\rfloor<T/2\).  Hence \(N\ge T/2\), and the floor inequalities imply
\[
 \alpha^{2k_T}\le \alpha^{-2}T^{-2r/D}=\alpha^{-2}T^{-\beta},
 \qquad
 T^{-1}L^{k_T+1}\le L T^{-1+(\log L)/D}=LT^{-\beta}.
\]
Also \(T^{-1}\le T^{-\beta}\) because \(0<\beta<1\).  Combining (1) and (5) bounds the mean-squared error of the unclipped average by \(c^\star T^{-\beta}\), where \(c^\star\) depends only on \(t_0\) and \(\zeta\).  Finally \(\theta(K,e)\in[-1,1]\), so projection onto \([-1,1]\) cannot increase squared distance to the target.  Taking the supremum over \(M\) proves the assertion, uniformly in both alphabet cardinalities and, a fortiori, uniformly over the additional restriction indexed by \(C\).

%%% FIELD proof-membership
Write \(p:=1/L\) and \(\nu_0(0,u):=(1+u\varepsilon_C)/2\).  The action law in \(\mathrm{def{:}signed\text{-}depth\text{-}family}\) is i.i.d. Bernoulli \(p\) under behavior and depends on neither the past nor \(U_t\).  It therefore satisfies the sequential-randomization predicate in \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\).  The displayed construction is time homogeneous and generates \((Y_t,J_{t+1},U_{t+1})\) from \((J_t,U_t,A_t)\), so it satisfies the POMDP-kernel predicate.  Moreover \(Y_t\in\{-1,+1\}\), proving bounded reward support.  Since \(e(1)=1=Lb(1)\) and \(e(0)=0\le Lb(0)\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}policy\text{-}overlap}\) holds, including its zero-cell requirement.

For either policy \(q\in\{b,e\}\), its state kernel can be written
\[
 P_q(s,\cdot)=(1-\alpha)\nu_0(\cdot)+\alpha R_{q,s}(\cdot), \tag{6}
\]
where at depth \(Q\) one may take \(R_{q,s}=\nu_0\), and below depth \(Q\), \(R_{q,s}\) is the policy-mixture of the one-step advance kernels.  Thus, for arbitrary state laws \(\nu,\nu'\), Markov-kernel nonexpansiveness of total variation and (6) give
\[
 \|\nu P_q-\nu'P_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\alpha\|\nu R_q-\nu'R_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\alpha\|\nu-\nu'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}. \tag{7}
\]
This proves the required contraction with a constant independent of \(Q\).

The depth transition is independent of the action and sign.  The probabilities
\[
 w_j=\frac{(1-\alpha)\alpha^j}{1-\alpha^{Q+1}},\qquad 0\le j\le Q,
\]
sum to one, satisfy \(w_j=\alpha w_{j-1}\) for \(j\ge1\), and satisfy
\(w_0=(1-\alpha)\sum_{j=0}^{Q-1}w_j+w_Q\).  They are therefore stationary for the depth chain.  If action one is taken independently with probability \(r\), a reset sign has mean \(\varepsilon_C\), while each advance multiplies its conditional mean by \(r\): action one retains the sign and action zero replaces it by a fair sign.  Induction on \(j\) consequently gives conditional sign mean \(\varepsilon_Cr^j\) at depth \(j\).  Hence the joint stationary law is
\[
 d_r(j,u)=\frac{w_j}{2}(1+u\varepsilon_Cr^j). \tag{8}
\]
Direct substitution in the stationarity equations also verifies (8).  All its cells are positive because \(0<\varepsilon_C\le1/2\).

Under the target \(r=1\), and under behavior \(r=p\).  For \(u=+1\),
\[
 \frac{d_e(j,+1)}{d_b(j,+1)}
 =\frac{1+\varepsilon_C}{1+\varepsilon_Cp^j}\le1+\varepsilon_C,
\]
whereas for \(u=-1\),
\[
 \frac{d_e(j,-1)}{d_b(j,-1)}
 =\frac{1-\varepsilon_C}{1-\varepsilon_Cp^j}\le1.
\]
By the definition \(\varepsilon_C=\min\{(C-1)/2,1/2\}\), one has \(1+\varepsilon_C\le C\) for every \(C>1\).  Thus the latent stationary-overlap predicate holds pointwise on the joint state.  Initialization is exactly from the behavior law (8), as required by \(\mathrm{ass{:}stationary\text{-}start}\).  Equations (6)--(8), together with the preceding support checks, prove membership in \(\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\) for every \(T\).

Finally, under the target policy the conditional sign mean at every depth is \(\varepsilon_C\).  Rewards have mean zero off depth \(Q\), and at \((Q,u)\) their mean is \(vc_0u\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{def{:}target\text{-}value}\) and (8) give
\[
 \theta(K,e)=\sum_{u\in\{-1,+1\}}d_e(Q,u)vc_0u
 =vc_0\varepsilon_Cw_Q,
\]
as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-observed-kl
Put \(p:=1/L\) and
\[
 m_t^v:=E_v[Y_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}].
\]
Every finite observed history has positive probability: the action probabilities are positive on their behavior support and every conditional reward probability lies in \([(1-c_0)/2,(1+c_0)/2]\).  Hence all following conditional probabilities are well defined.

By the reward law in \(\mathrm{def{:}signed\text{-}depth\text{-}family}\),
\[
 m_t^v=vc_0E_v[U_t\mathbf1\{J_t=Q\}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}]. \tag{9}
\]
Suppose first that \(t>Q\).  On \(\{J_t=Q\}\), the depth path during epochs \(t-Q,\ldots,t\) is necessarily \(0,1,\ldots,Q\), with no reset.  The rewards at the preceding \(Q\) epochs are therefore emitted below terminal depth and are fair; they convey no information about the episode's sign.  The sign at depth zero has mean \(\varepsilon_C\), independently of the earlier observed history, and it survives to epoch \(t\) exactly when all of \(A_{t-Q},\ldots,A_{t-1}\) equal one.  It follows that
\[
 E_v[U_t\mid J_t=Q,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 =\varepsilon_C\prod_{j=t-Q}^{t-1}\mathbf1\{A_j=1\}. \tag{10}
\]
For \(t\le Q\), terminal depth at epoch \(t\) forces initial depth \(Q-t+1\) followed by \(t-1\) advances.  Equation (8) from \(\mathrm{lem{:}signed\text{-}depth\text{-}membership}\) gives initial conditional sign mean \(\varepsilon_Cp^{Q-t+1}\); the observed preceding rewards are again all fair on this event, and each recorded action zero erases the sign.  Thus
\[
 E_v[U_t\mid J_t=Q,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 =\varepsilon_Cp^{Q-t+1}\prod_{j=1}^{t-1}\mathbf1\{A_j=1\}. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (9)--(11) and \(\mathrm{def{:}filter\text{-}quantities}\) prove, for every \(t\),
\[
 m_t^v=vc_0\varepsilon_Ca_t^{(Q)}q_t^v. \tag{12}
\]
This calculation accounts explicitly for the stationary pre-sample actions when \(t\le Q\).

We next bound \(q_t^v\) without observing resets.  For \(t>Q\), condition first on \(\mathcal F_{t-Q-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and on the intervening actions.  The joint conditional probability of \(J_t=Q\) and the realized rewards \(Y_{t-Q},\ldots,Y_{t-1}\) is
\[
 \Pr_v(J_{t-Q}=0\mid\mathcal F_{t-Q-1}^{\mathrm{obs}})\alpha^Q2^{-Q}; \tag{13}
\]
indeed, terminal arrival requires \(Q\) advances and every reward on those nonterminal depths is fair.  On the other hand, the chain rule and (12) show that the conditional probability of the realized reward window is
\[
 2^{-Q}\prod_{r=t-Q}^{t-1}(1+Y_rm_r^v). \tag{14}
\]
Dividing (13) by (14), dropping the first probability, and using
\(|m_r^v|\le c_0q_r^v\), gives
\[
 q_t^v\le\alpha^Q\prod_{r=t-Q}^{t-1}(1-c_0q_r^v)^{-1}. \tag{15}
\]
For \(t\le Q\), stationarity gives
\[
 \Pr(J_1=Q-t+1)\alpha^{t-1}=w_{Q-t+1}\alpha^{t-1}=w_Q.
\]
The same fair-reward calculation therefore replaces the numerator in (13) by \(w_Q2^{-(t-1)}\).  Since
\[
 w_Q=\frac{(1-\alpha)\alpha^Q}{1-\alpha^{Q+1}}\le\alpha^Q,
\]
inequality (15) remains valid with the product restricted to \(r=1,\ldots,t-1\).  Thus it covers every initial window.

Set \(r_0:=\alpha/(1-c_0)\).  Since \(c_0=(1-\alpha)/4\),
\[
 r_0=\frac{4\alpha}{3+\alpha}<1. \tag{16}
\]
The crude bound \(q_r^v\le1\) in (15), including its initial-window version, gives \(q_t^v\le r_0^Q\) for every \(t,v\) and every observed history.  Substituting this uniform result back into (15) yields
\[
 q_t^v\le\alpha^Q(1-c_0r_0^Q)^{-Q}.
\]
Using \(-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\), \(1-c_0r_0^Q\ge1-c_0\), and
\(Qr_0^Q\le\sum_{j\ge1}jr_0^j=r_0/(1-r_0)^2\), we obtain
\[
 q_t^v\le K_0\alpha^Q,
 \qquad
 K_0:=\exp\!\left\{\frac{c_0r_0}{(1-c_0)(1-r_0)^2}\right\}<\infty. \tag{17}
\]
This constant depends only on \(t_0\), not on \(Q,T,C\), or the observed word.

It remains to pass from the filter to the observed experiment.  If two Rademacher laws have means \(m,n\in(-1,1)\), then \(\log x\le x-1\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Rad}(m)\,\|\,\operatorname{Rad}(n))
 \le\chi^2(\operatorname{Rad}(m)\,\|\,\operatorname{Rad}(n))
 =\frac{(m-n)^2}{1-n^2}. \tag{18}
\]
At a common observed history, (12) and (17) imply
\[
 |m_t^{+1}-m_t^{-1}|
 \le2c_0\varepsilon_CK_0\alpha^Qa_t^{(Q)},
 \qquad |m_t^{-1}|\le c_0. \tag{19}
\]
The conditional action laws are identical under the two alternatives by sequential randomization.  Applying the ordinary product-density chain rule, then (18)--(19), gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{Q,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\,\|\,
                    \mathbb P_{Q,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T})
 \le \frac{4c_0^2\varepsilon_C^2K_0^2}{1-c_0^2}
       \alpha^{2Q}\sum_{t=1}^TE_{+1}[(a_t^{(Q)})^2]. \tag{20}
\]
Behavior actions are i.i.d. Bernoulli \(p\), even after conditioning on earlier observed outcomes.  With \(\ell_t=\min\{Q,t-1\}\),
\[
 E_{+1}[(a_t^{(Q)})^2]
 =p^{2(Q-\ell_t)}p^{\ell_t}=p^{2Q-\ell_t}\le p^Q=L^{-Q}. \tag{21}
\]
Combining (20)--(21) proves the asserted KL inequality with, for example,
\[
 B_0:=\frac{4c_0^2\varepsilon_C^2K_0^2}{1-c_0^2}.
\]
Its allowed dependence is only through \(t_0,\zeta,C\), and the derivation never reveals a reset or a latent state.

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-minimax
The upper inequality, with the stated observable estimator, is exactly \(\mathrm{lem{:}phiw\text{-}upper}\) combined with \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}risk}\).  We prove the converse.

Put \(r:=\log(1/\alpha)\), \(D:=2r+\log L\), so \(\beta=2r/D\).  Let \(B_0\) be the constant from \(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}path\text{-}kl}\), and, for all sufficiently large \(T\), choose
\[
 Q_T:=\left\lceil\frac{\log(8B_0T)}{D}\right\rceil\ge1. \tag{22}
\]
The two models with \(v=+1\) and \(v=-1\) belong to the class by \(\mathrm{lem{:}signed\text{-}depth\text{-}membership}\), have the same revealed policies and alphabets, and have \(2(Q_T+1)\) latent states.  The KL lemma and (22) imply
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{Q_T,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\,\|\,
                    \mathbb P_{Q_T,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T})
 \le B_0T\exp(-DQ_T)\le\frac18. \tag{23}
\]

For completeness, we derive the testing reduction used here.  For two mutually absolutely continuous probability laws \(P_+,P_-\), let \(R=dP_+/dP_-\), let
\(A:=\int\sqrt{dP_+dP_-}\), and let \(O:=\int\min(dP_+,dP_-)\).  Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 A=E_{P_+}[R^{-1/2}]
 \ge\exp\{-\operatorname{KL}(P_+\|P_-)/2\}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz, applied to \(\sqrt{\min(dP_+,dP_-)\max(dP_+,dP_-)}\), gives
\(A^2\le O(2-O)\le2O\).  Hence
\[
 O\ge\tfrac12\exp\{-\operatorname{KL}(P_+\|P_-)\}. \tag{24}
\]
For any estimator \(\widehat\theta\), classify as plus when \(\widehat\theta>0\).  The two target values are \(\theta_+=a_Q\) and \(\theta_-=-a_Q\), where
\(a_Q:=c_0\varepsilon_Cw_Q\).  On either classification error the squared loss is at least \(a_Q^2\).  The sum of the two error probabilities is at least \(O\), since pointwise the smaller density is paid by every binary decision rule.  Therefore (24) implies
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-1,+1\}}E_v[(\widehat\theta-\theta_v)^2]
 \ge\frac{a_Q^2}{4}\exp\{-\operatorname{KL}(P_+\|P_-)\}. \tag{25}
\]

Now \(w_Q\ge(1-\alpha)\alpha^Q\).  Also, by the ceiling in (22),
\[
 \alpha^{2Q_T}\ge \alpha^2(8B_0T)^{-2r/D}
 =\alpha^2(8B_0)^{-\beta}T^{-\beta}. \tag{26}
\]
Combining (23)--(26) gives, for every measurable observable-data estimator,
\[
 \max_{v\in\{-1,+1\}}E_v[(\widehat\theta-\theta_v)^2]
 \ge \frac{c_0^2\varepsilon_C^2(1-\alpha)^2\alpha^2
              (8B_0)^{-\beta}e^{-1/8}}{4}\,T^{-\beta}. \tag{27}
\]
The coefficient is strictly positive for fixed \(C>1\) and depends only on \(t_0,\zeta,C\).  Taking first the supremum over the full class, which contains this pair, and then the infimum over all estimators proves the lower inequality in \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}risk}\).  Enlarging the threshold if needed to cover the floor and ceiling conditions produces \(T_\star\).  Equations (22) and (27) also prove the asserted constant observed alphabet and \(2(Q+1)\)-state lower experiment.  Since both inequalities have exponent \(2/(2+t_0\zeta)\) and neither contains a logarithmic multiplier, bounded latent stationary occupancy with any fixed \(C>1\) leaves the partial-observability exponent unchanged.

%%% FIELD proof-unit-boundary
At \(C=1\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}latent\text{-}stationary\text{-}overlap}\) says \(d_e(s)\le d_b(s)\) for every \(s\).  Both sides sum to one on the finite joint state space, so every nonnegative difference \(d_b(s)-d_e(s)\) is zero; hence \(d_e=d_b\).

Let \(W_t:=Y_t\rho_t\), the unclipped summand defining \(\widehat\theta_0\).  Sequential randomization and the target definition give
\[
 E[W_t]=d_bg_e=d_eg_e=\theta(K,e). \tag{28}
\]
The policy-overlap inequality \(\rho_t^2\le L\rho_t\), sequential randomization from \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\), and bounded rewards give \(E[W_t^2]\le L\).  For \(h\ge1\), conditional on \(S_{t+1}\), the future conditional mean is \(P_b^{h-1}g_e(S_{t+1})\).  Uniform contraction and the stationary-start predicate from \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) therefore give
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(W_t,W_{t+h})|\le2\alpha^{h-1}. \tag{29}
\]
Equations (28)--(29) imply
\[
 E\!\left[\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TW_t-\theta(K,e)\right\}^2\right]
 \le\frac1T\left(L+\frac4{1-\alpha}\right). \tag{30}
\]
Clipping cannot increase squared loss because the target belongs to \([-1,1]\), so (30) proves the cardinality-uniform upper bound for \(\widehat\theta_0\).

For the converse, for each \(T\) take singleton observed and latent state spaces, take \(b=e=(1/2,1/2)\), and let rewards be independent Rademacher variables of mean \(v\eta_T\), where \(v\in\{-1,+1\}\) and \(\eta_T:=1/(4\sqrt T)\).  The state kernel has contraction coefficient zero, the stationary laws coincide, all rewards are bounded, and policy overlap holds, so both alternatives lie in \(\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,1)\).  Their target values are \(v\eta_T\).  The action laws coincide, and direct calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_+^T\|P_-^T)
 =T\eta_T\log\frac{1+\eta_T}{1-\eta_T}
 \le\frac{2T\eta_T^2}{1-\eta_T}\le\frac14. \tag{31}
\]
Let \(O_T:=\int\min(dP_+^T,dP_-^T)\).  With \(\Lambda_T=dP_+^T/dP_-^T\), Jensen's inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \int\sqrt{dP_+^TdP_-^T}
 =E_{P_+^T}[\Lambda_T^{-1/2}]
 \ge e^{-\operatorname{KL}(P_+^T\|P_-^T)/2},
 \qquad
 O_T\ge\tfrac12e^{-\operatorname{KL}(P_+^T\|P_-^T)}.
\]
Classifying an estimator according to the sign of its output makes squared loss at least \(\eta_T^2\) on a classification error, while the sum of the two error probabilities is at least \(O_T\).  Therefore (31) yields
\[
 R_T(t_0,\zeta,1)\ge \frac{e^{-1/4}}{4}\eta_T^2
 =\frac{e^{-1/4}}{64T}. \tag{32}
\]
Together, (30) and (32) prove \(R_T(t_0,\zeta,1)\asymp T^{-1}\).  The fixed-\(C>1\) exponent is strictly smaller because \(t_0\zeta>0\), while its lower constant contains \(\varepsilon_C^2\) and therefore need not remain bounded away from zero as \(C\downarrow1\).  This proves the stated discontinuous boundary without asserting uniformity in \(C\).

%%% FIELD statement-local-frontier
Let \(t_0>0\) and \(\zeta>0\) be fixed, and let \(\delta_T\ge0\) be any deterministic sequence with \(\delta_T\to0\).  With \(\beta=2/(2+t_0\zeta)\), there are constants \(0<c_{\mathrm{loc}}\le C_{\mathrm{loc}}<\infty\), depending only on \(t_0,\zeta\), such that, for all sufficiently large \(T\),
\[
 c_{\mathrm{loc}}\left\{T^{-1}+T^{-\beta}\delta_T^{2(1-\beta)}\right\}
 \le R_T(t_0,\zeta,1+\delta_T)
 \le C_{\mathrm{loc}}\left\{T^{-1}+T^{-\beta}\delta_T^{2(1-\beta)}\right\}.
\]
An attaining observable estimator is \(\widehat\theta_{\kappa_T}\), where \(\kappa_T=0\) if \(T\delta_T^2\le1\), and otherwise
\[
 \kappa_T:=\min\!\left\{\left\lfloor T/2\right\rfloor,
 \left\lfloor\frac{\log(T\delta_T^2)}{2\log(1/\alpha)+\log L}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Thus the exact threshold is \(\delta_T\asymp T^{-1/2}\): when \(T\delta_T^2=O(1)\), immediate weighting has parametric order, whereas when \(T\delta_T^2\to\infty\), the required history is of order \(\log(T\delta_T^2)\) and the second risk term dominates.  There is no logarithmic factor in the risk.

%%% FIELD proof-local-frontier
Put \(\delta:=\delta_T\), \(r:=\log(1/\alpha)\), \(D:=2r+\log L\), and \(x:=T\delta^2\).  Then \(\beta=2r/D\) and \(1-\beta=(\log L)/D\).

First consider the upper bound.  The pointwise inequality \(d_e\le(1+\delta)d_b\), together with normalization, implies
\[
 \|d_e-d_b\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}. \tag{33}
\]
Indeed, when \(\delta>0\),
\(\nu:=\{(1+\delta)d_b-d_e\}/\delta\) is a probability law and
\(d_b-d_e=\delta(\nu-d_e)/(1+\delta)\); when \(\delta=0\), equality follows as in \(\mathrm{prop{:}unit\text{-}overlap\text{-}boundary}\).  The likelihood-ratio cancellation and contraction calculation from \(\mathrm{lem{:}phiw\text{-}upper}\), now retaining (33), gives
\[
 |E[Z_t(k)]-\theta(K,e)|
 \le2\alpha^k\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\le2\delta\alpha^k. \tag{34}
\]
For completeness, the same calculation is uniform in \(k\): \(\rho^2\le L\rho\) makes the lag-\(h\) overlapping covariance at most \(2L^{k+1-h}\) for \(1\le h\le k\), while contraction makes a nonoverlapping lag-\(h\) covariance at most \(2\alpha^{h-k-1}\).  Summing these two geometric series supplies a constant \(A<\infty\), depending only on \(t_0,\zeta\), such that, whenever \(k\le T/2\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left((T-k)^{-1}\sum_{t=k+1}^TZ_t(k)\right)
 \le A\{T^{-1}L^k+T^{-1}\}. \tag{35}
\]
If \(x\le1\), take \(k=0\); then (34)--(35) are bounded by a constant times \(T^{-1}\).  If \(x>1\), take \(k=\lfloor\log x/D\rfloor\).  Because \(\delta_T\to0\), this is below \(T/2\) for all sufficiently large \(T\).  The floor inequalities and the two identities for \(\beta\) give
\[
 \delta^2\alpha^{2k}\le\alpha^{-2}\delta^2x^{-\beta},
 \qquad
 T^{-1}L^k\le T^{-1}x^{1-\beta}=\delta^2x^{-\beta}. \tag{36}
\]
Since
\[
 \delta^2x^{-\beta}=T^{-\beta}\delta^{2(1-\beta)}, \tag{37}
\]
(34)--(37), followed by clipping, prove the displayed upper bound for the stated observable estimator.

For the lower bound, take singleton observed and latent state spaces, \(b=e=(1/2,1/2)\), and independent Rademacher rewards of means \(\pm1/(4\sqrt T)\).  These two alternatives have \(d_e=d_b\), contraction coefficient zero, and bounded rewards, so they belong to \(\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,1+\delta)\) for every \(\delta\ge0\).  Their revealed inputs, including \(\delta\), are identical.  Their observed KL is at most \(1/4\).  Jensen's inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz therefore make their likelihood-overlap integral at least \(e^{-1/4}/2\), and sign classification gives a universal constant \(c_1>0\) such that
\[
 R_T(t_0,\zeta,1+\delta)\ge c_1T^{-1}. \tag{38}
\]
We next obtain the local hidden-state term.  For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(0\le\delta\le1\), so in the signed-depth family with \(C=1+\delta\),
\[
 \varepsilon_C=\delta/2. \tag{39}
\]
The precise conditional-mean and posterior conclusions of \(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}path\text{-}kl}\) give
\[
 |m_t^{+1}-m_t^{-1}|
 \le2c_0\varepsilon_CK_0\alpha^Qa_t^{(Q)},
 \qquad |m_t^{-1}|\le c_0.
\]
For Rademacher laws, \(\log u\le u-1\) gives conditional KL at most the squared difference of their means divided by \(1-(m_t^{-1})^2\).  The chain rule, together with
\(E[(a_t^{(Q)})^2]=L^{-(2Q-\ell_t)}\le L^{-Q}\), therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{Q,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\,\|\,
                    \mathbb P_{Q,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T})
 \le \overline B\,T\varepsilon_C^2\alpha^{2Q}L^{-Q},
 \qquad
 \overline B:=\frac{4c_0^2K_0^2}{1-c_0^2}, \tag{40}
\]
where \(\overline B\) depends only on \(t_0\).  This exposed \(\varepsilon_C^2\) factor is essential for the local calculation.

Choose a finite \(x_0\), depending only on \(t_0,\zeta\), large enough that for every \(x\ge x_0\),
\[
 Q_x:=\left\lceil D^{-1}\log(2\overline Bx)\right\rceil\ge1.
\]
By (39)--(40),
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{Q_x,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\,\|\,
                    \mathbb P_{Q_x,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T})
 \le(\overline Bx/4)e^{-DQ_x}\le\frac18. \tag{41}
\]
Membership and the target separation \(2a_Q\), where \(a_Q=c_0\varepsilon_Cw_Q\), follow from \(\mathrm{lem{:}signed\text{-}depth\text{-}membership}\).  If \(O_Q\) is the overlap integral of the two observed laws, Jensen's inequality for their likelihood ratio and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 O_Q\ge\tfrac12\exp\{-\operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{Q,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\|
                                      \mathbb P_{Q,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T})\}.
\]
Sign classification then gives maximum squared-error risk at least \(a_Q^2O_Q/2\).  Using
\(w_{Q_x}\ge(1-\alpha)\alpha^{Q_x}\), (39), (41), and
\[
 \alpha^{2Q_x}\ge\alpha^2(2\overline Bx)^{-\beta},
\]
gives a constant \(c_2>0\), depending only on \(t_0,\zeta\), such that for \(x\ge x_0\),
\[
 R_T(t_0,\zeta,1+\delta)
 \ge c_2\delta^2x^{-\beta}
 =c_2T^{-\beta}\delta^{2(1-\beta)}. \tag{42}
\]
When \(x<x_0\), the second expression in braces in the theorem is
\(T^{-1}x^{1-\beta}\le x_0^{1-\beta}T^{-1}\), so (38) already controls it.  When \(x\ge x_0\), (38) and (42) imply a constant multiple of their sum because \(\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2\).  This proves the lower bound against every measurable estimator.  Finally, the ratio of the hidden-state term to \(T^{-1}\) is exactly \(x^{1-\beta}\).  It is bounded precisely on the scale \(\delta_T=O(T^{-1/2})\) and diverges when \(T\delta_T^2\to\infty\), proving the threshold and history-depth assertions.
