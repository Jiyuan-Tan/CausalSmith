\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} On a uniformly conditioned causal VMW submodel, the primary theorem is a gap-free Lipschitz modulus from the observable moment summary to the positive quotient latent-effect law in \(W_1\), uniform through simultaneous effect merges and splits. Root-\(n\) estimation, honest root-\(n\)-diameter confidence sets, and a cluster report with explicit true-cluster association follow from that modulus. Same-class minimax corollaries give root-\(n\) quotient-law risk and effect-ordered weight risk \(\min\{1,(\sqrt n\,\delta)^{-1}\}\), using factorization-preserving pairs on an explicit full-rank unequal-weight two-class witness. Each converse also transfers to any published-VMW comparator class containing its exact two-point witness pair; no containment of an entire local neighborhood is required. The quotient collision point belongs to the published structural/finite-regularity model but lies outside the separated Theorem 7.2 recovery class, whereas the labeled pair is separated. An auxiliary class-advised admissible-summary net shows independently that the abstract modulus yields a positive collision-uniform root-\(n\) estimator, but the operative K1--K4 computational construction is the no-advice structured lattice and its outer report. Exact polynomial-time computation of the original sharp summary-inversion image remains open. The modulus, estimator uppers, confidence and cluster guarantees, and matching-minimax conclusions remain restricted to the stated uniformly conditioned classes.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Virk, Mazaheri, and Wu identify the operator moments and analyze separated eigenpair recovery, but do not give collision-uniform positive-law estimation or an evaluable confidence/report construction when empirical eigenvalues may be nonreal.

\paragraph{Niche.} The regular causal object is the quotient probability law that aggregates mass at equal effects. Effect-ordered weights remain irregular near a collision, while transport over an unresolved cluster can still be root-\(n\).

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper makes the positive-measure \(W_1\) modulus the root theorem on a uniformly conditioned causal VMW submodel, derives total root-\(n\) estimation, honest root-\(n\)-diameter sets and well-posed cluster reports, and calibrates same-class quotient-law and labeled-weight minimax risk using observed-Kullback--Leibler pairs that preserve the proxy factorization.  A class-advised admissible-summary net records the independent abstract modulus-to-estimation benchmark, while the no-advice structured lattice is the operative K1--K4 computational deliverable and supports the evaluable outer confidence set and report.  Exact computation of the original sharp summary-inversion image is left as a separate residual question.  The lower bounds transfer further to precisely those published-VMW comparator classes containing the corresponding two witness laws; whole-neighborhood containment is unnecessary, while all upper and inference guarantees retain their narrower class scope.

\section{Related work}

In their fixed-dimensional finite-sample regime, Assumption 4 imposes finite almost-sure envelopes for \(X\), \(ZX^\top\), and \(YZX^\top\), positive marginal treatment-arm probability, and a positive population singular-value margin; its other named constants are derived conditioning quantities.  Theorem 7.2 additionally assumes proxy richness, strict latent positivity, pairwise-distinct treatment effects when \(k\ge2\), and its stated sample-dependent radius conditions before delivering high-probability treatment-effect, anchor-normalized feature-matrix, and simplex-projected mixture-weight recovery.  Their structural/finite-regularity model contains the explicit quotient collision pair because it retains full proxy rank and strict positivity without requiring distinct effects, but the collision point is outside that separated recovery theorem; the labeled witness pair is separated. \section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reference-proxy-separation]\label{ass:reference-proxy-separation}
\(Z\perp (X,Y)\mid(T,U)\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW reference-proxy conditional independence, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-proxy-separation]\label{ass:target-proxy-separation}
\(X\perp (Y,T)\mid U\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW target-proxy conditional independence, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(T)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW causal consistency condition in Assumption 1, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-ignorability]\label{ass:latent-ignorability}
\(\forall t\in\{0,1\},\ Y(t)\perp T\mid U\text{ under }P.\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW armwise latent ignorability condition in Assumption 1, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:anchor]\label{ass:anchor}
\(X_1=1\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW anchor-coordinate normalization, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-x]\label{ass:bounded-x}
\(\lVert X\rVert_2\le L\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW bounded target-proxy contribution, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-proxy-product]\label{ass:bounded-proxy-product}
\(\lVert ZX^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW bounded proxy cross-moment contribution, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcome-proxy-product]\label{ass:bounded-outcome-proxy-product}
\(\lVert YZX^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW bounded outcome-weighted proxy contribution, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-arm-positivity]\label{ass:latent-arm-positivity}
\(\min_{1\le u\le k,\ t\in\{0,1\}}P(U=u,T=t)\ge\pi_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This new uniform joint latent-arm margin is satisfied when every latent class has prevalence bounded away from zero and both treatment arms have assignment probabilities bounded away from zero within each class, as in randomized or overlap-restricted latent-stratified cohorts.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:proxy-rank-margin]\label{ass:proxy-rank-margin}
\(\min\{\sigma_k(A_0),\sigma_k(A_1),\sigma_k(B)\}\ge\sigma_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This new uniform proxy-conditioning restriction is satisfied by finite proxy dictionaries whose class-conditional mean columns remain quantitatively distinct; it holds on an open neighborhood of the explicit Bernoulli collision witness.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gap-window]\label{ass:gap-window}
\(g/2\le\delta(P)\le2g\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This additional local-regime shell groups laws whose nonzero nearest-effect gap has order \(g\); it is satisfied by separated finite-class laws after choosing the dyadic shell containing their gap and is used only for the labeled-coordinate frontier.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:distinct-effects]\label{ass:distinct-effects}
\(\nu_P\) has exactly \(k\) distinct support points.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VMW spectral separation, \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-quotient-neighborhood]\label{ass:local-quotient-neighborhood}
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_O\Vert(P_0^{\mathrm{wit}})_O)\le c_{\mathrm{loc}}/n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Le Cam local Kullback--Leibler neighborhood, \cite{PolyanskiyWu2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-weight-neighborhood]\label{ass:local-weight-neighborhood}
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_O\Vert(P_{g/2}^{\mathrm{wit}})_O)\le c_{\mathrm{loc}}/n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Le Cam local Kullback--Leibler neighborhood, \cite{PolyanskiyWu2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:wasserstein-confidence-set]\label{def:wasserstein-confidence-set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{theoretical summary-\allowbreak{}inversion and computable outer Wasserstein confidence sets}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Retain the original exact summary-inversion set
\[
\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}:=
\{\overline F(q):q\in\mathcal K,\ d_S(q,\Pi(\widehat S_n))\le2r_{n,\alpha}\}
\]
when \(\mathcal K\ne\varnothing\), with \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}:=\{\delta_0\}\) otherwise.  This is the theoretical image set; no computational representation of \(\mathcal K\) or \(\overline F\) is asserted.

For the explicit lattice estimator define
\[
m_\star:=\pi_0,
\qquad R_{n,\alpha}:=C_{\mathrm{lat}}(r_{n,\alpha}+n^{-1/2}),
\]
let \(\mathcal P_{\le k,m_\star}([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}])\) be the laws on \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\) having at most \(k\) distinct atoms, each of mass at least \(m_\star\), and set
\[
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}:=
\{\xi\in\mathcal P_{\le k,m_\star}([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]):
W_1(\xi,\widehat\lambda_n)\le R_{n,\alpha}\}.
\]
This set has the following finite-dimensional constrained-program representation.  Merge repeated atoms of the computed center and write
\(\widehat\lambda_n=\sum_{b=1}^{\widehat r}\widehat w_b\delta_{\widehat x_b}\), where
\(-L_{\tau}\le\widehat x_1<\cdots<\widehat x_{\widehat r}\le L_{\tau}\) and \(1\le\widehat r\le k\).  Then \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\) is the union over \(1\le r\le k\) of the laws \(\sum_{a=1}^rw_a\delta_{x_a}\) for which there are variables
\(x_1<\cdots<x_r\), \(w_a\), and \(\gamma_{ab}\) satisfying
\[
\begin{gathered}
-L_{\tau}\le x_1<\cdots<x_r\le L_{\tau},
\qquad w_a\ge m_\star,
\qquad \sum_{a=1}^rw_a=1,\\
\gamma_{ab}\ge0,
\qquad \sum_{b=1}^{\widehat r}\gamma_{ab}=w_a,
\qquad \sum_{a=1}^{r}\gamma_{ab}=\widehat w_b,
\qquad \sum_{a=1}^{r}\sum_{b=1}^{\widehat r}
\gamma_{ab}|x_a-\widehat x_b|\le R_{n,\alpha}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\) is an evaluable fixed-dimensional finite union of ordered-support, weight, and transport-plan constraint sets.  No equality or inclusion relation between \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\) and the original \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\) is asserted.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}summary-\allowbreak{}space-\allowbreak{}repair}; \texttt{prop:\allowbreak{}polynomial-\allowbreak{}net-\allowbreak{}law-\allowbreak{}estimator}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:cluster-report]\label{def:cluster-report}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{computable cluster-\allowbreak{}adaptive support and mass report}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(E_P:=\{d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\le r_{n,\alpha}\}\) and
\(\rho_{n,\alpha}:=R_{n,\alpha}/m_\star\).  Merge repeated atoms of \(\widehat\lambda_n\), join two distinct support points when their distance is at most \(4\rho_{n,\alpha}\), and let \(\widehat{\mathscr K}_{n,\alpha}\) be the resulting connected components.  For \(C\in\widehat{\mathscr K}_{n,\alpha}\), define
\[
K_C(P):=\{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu_P):\operatorname{dist}(x,C)\le\rho_{n,\alpha}\},
\qquad
K_C(\xi):=\{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\xi):\operatorname{dist}(x,C)\le\rho_{n,\alpha}\}.
\]
Rebase the mass interval on the computable outer confidence set:
\[
I_C:=\left[
\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}}\xi(K_C(\xi)),
\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}}\xi(K_C(\xi))
\right].
\]
In the ordered-support and transport-plan representation of \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\), both endpoints are values of the same finite union of fixed-dimensional mixed-integer constrained programs: for each \(1\le r\le k\), add variables \(z_a\in\{0,1\}\), impose
\[
z_a=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\min_{c\in C}|x_a-c|\le\rho_{n,\alpha},
\]
and respectively minimize or maximize \(\sum_{a=1}^rw_az_a\) subject to all ordered-support, atom-floor, simplex, and transport constraints in \(\mathrm{def:wasserstein\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\).  Infimum and supremum are used so that atoms lying exactly on an association boundary are handled by the displayed weak inequality; no claim of an efficient exact solver is made.

The report \(\mathfrak R_{n,\alpha}\) contains, for every component \(C\), the support interval

\]
with value \(+\infty\) when the complement is empty.

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:observed-vmw-margin-inclusion]\label{prop:observed-vmw-margin-inclusion}
Every \(P\in\mathcal M\) satisfies, for \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(T=t)&\ge k\pi_0,\\
M_t(P)&=A_t\operatorname{diag}\{P(U=u\mid T=t):1\le u\le k\}B^\top,\\
\sigma_k\{M_t(P)V(P)\}&=\sigma_k\{M_t(P)\}\ge\pi_0\sigma_0^2,\\
\sigma_k\!\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_0(P)\\M_1(P)\end{bmatrix}\right)&\ge\pi_0\sigma_0^2,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\lVert M_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\ \lVert N_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\ \lVert m_X(P)\rVert_2\le L.
\]
Define \(L_{\tau}:=4L\sqrt{d_z}/\sigma_0\).  The retained assumptions further imply, for every \(u\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
|Y(t)|\le L_{\tau}/2\quad P\text{-almost surely conditional on }U=u,
\qquad |\mu_{tu}|\le L_{\tau}/2,
\qquad |\tau_u|\le L_{\tau}.
\]
With the fixed dimensions and anchor normalization, \(\mathcal M\) is a quantitatively uniform conditional-moment subclass of the published VMW parameter class.  It retains VMW Assumption 1 in its exact armwise form, including \(Y(t)\perp T\mid U\) separately for each \(t\), and strengthens the published qualitative latent positivity and full-rank requirements to the fixed margins \(\pi_0\) and \(\sigma_0\).  It imposes no additional latent conditional-moment or potential-outcome support assumption: the displayed potential-outcome envelope is derived from proxy rank, the observable envelopes, reference-proxy separation, consistency, armwise ignorability, and latent--arm positivity.  The converse inclusion and all upper, confidence, cluster, and full-class matching conclusions on the full published VMW class are not asserted; only lower bounds carried by explicit witness paths transfer upward to containing classes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Membership in \(\mathcal M\), through \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), supplies every retained assumption cited below.  Put
\[
q_t:=P(T=t),\qquad w_{tu}:=P(U=u\mid T=t).
\]
The joint latent--arm positivity condition gives
\[
q_t=\sum_{u=1}^kP(U=u,T=t)\ge k\pi_0>0,                                      \tag{1}
\]
so all armwise conditional expectations are defined.  Since \(q_t\le1\), it also gives
\[
w_{tu}=\frac{P(U=u,T=t)}{q_t}\ge P(U=u,T=t)\ge\pi_0.                         \tag{2}
\]
The anchor and observable proxy-product envelope imply
\[
\lVert Z\rVert_2=\lVert ZX^\top e_1\rVert_2
\le\lVert ZX^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L\quad\text{almost surely}.          \tag{3}
\]
By iterated expectation, reference-proxy separation, and target-proxy separation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_t(P)
&=\sum_{u=1}^kw_{tu}\,E_P[ZX^\top\mid U=u,T=t]\\
&=\sum_{u=1}^kw_{tu}\,E_P[Z\mid U=u,T=t]E_P[X\mid U=u,T=t]^\top\\
&=A_tD_tB^\top,\qquad D_t:=\operatorname{diag}(w_{t1},\ldots,w_{tk}).
\end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{4}
\]
Here target-proxy separation gives \(E[X\mid U=u,T=t]=E[X\mid U=u]=B(:,u)\).  The rank margin and (2) make all three factors in (4) full column rank.  Consequently
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_t(P)=k,
\qquad \operatorname{row}M_t(P)=\operatorname{col}B.                           \tag{5}
\]
For \(x\in\operatorname{col}B\), the singular-value variational characterization, (2), and the rank margins yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert M_t(P)x\rVert_2
&\ge\sigma_k(A_t)\lVert D_tB^\top x\rVert_2
\ge\sigma_k(A_t)\min_u w_{tu}\,\lVert B^\top x\rVert_2\\
&\ge\pi_0\sigma_0^2\lVert x\rVert_2.                                         
\end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{6}
\]
The restriction of \(B^\top\) to \(\operatorname{col}B\) has singular values equal to the nonzero singular values of \(B\), which justifies the last line.  Equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
\sigma_k\{M_t(P)\}\ge\pi_0\sigma_0^2.                                      \tag{7}
\]
Both arms have row space \(\operatorname{col}B\), hence the stacked matrix has that row space as well.  Since the columns of \(V(P)\) are an orthonormal basis of it,
\[
M_t(P)=M_t(P)V(P)V(P)^\top,
\]
and therefore \(M_t(P)V(P)\) and \(M_t(P)\) have the same \(k\) positive singular values.  Thus
\[
\sigma_k\{M_t(P)V(P)\}=\sigma_k\{M_t(P)\}\ge\pi_0\sigma_0^2.              \tag{8}
\]
Moreover, for every unit \(x\in\operatorname{col}B\), (6) gives
\[
\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}M_0(P)\\M_1(P)\end{bmatrix}x\right\rVert_2^2
=\lVert M_0(P)x\rVert_2^2+\lVert M_1(P)x\rVert_2^2
\ge(\pi_0\sigma_0^2)^2,
\]
so
\[
\sigma_k\!\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_0(P)\\M_1(P)\end{bmatrix}\right)
\ge\pi_0\sigma_0^2.                                                           \tag{9}
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality, valid by (1), and the three observable envelopes give
\[
\lVert M_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L,qquad
\lVert N_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L,qquad
\lVert m_X(P)\rVert_2\le L.                                                    \tag{10}
\]

We next derive, rather than assume, the potential-outcome envelope.  Fix \((u,t)\).  Positivity gives \(P(U=u,T=t)>0\).  If \(a_{tu}:=E_P[Z\mid U=u,T=t]=A_t(:,u)\), then
\[
\lVert a_{tu}\rVert_2=\lVert A_te_u\rVert_2\ge\sigma_k(A_t)\ge\sigma_0.
\]
Thus some coordinate \(j=j(u,t)\) satisfies
\[
|E_P[Z_j\mid U=u,T=t]|\ge\frac{\sigma_0}{\sqrt{d_z}}.                         \tag{11}
\]
Let \(a:=\sigma_0/(2\sqrt{d_z})>0\).  By (3), \(|Z_j|\le L\) almost surely.  If
\(P(|Z_j|\ge a\mid U=u,T=t)=0\), then
\[
|E_P[Z_j\mid U=u,T=t]|\le E_P[|Z_j|\mid U=u,T=t]\le a,
\]
contradicting (11).  More quantitatively, splitting at \(a\) gives
\[
E_P[|Z_j|\mid U=u,T=t]\le a+L P(|Z_j|\ge a\mid U=u,T=t),
\]
so the latter conditional probability is strictly positive.  The anchor and outcome-proxy envelope also imply
\[
|Y|\lVert Z\rVert_2
=\lVert YZX^\top e_1\rVert_2
\le\lVert YZX^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L\quad\text{almost surely}.          \tag{12}
\]
Reference-proxy separation entails \(Z_j\perp Y\mid(U,T)\).  If
\[
P\!\left(|Y|>\frac{L}{a}\,\middle|\,U=u,T=t\right)>0,
\]
conditional independence and the positive probability just proved would give positive conditional probability to the intersection of that event with \(\{|Z_j|\ge a\}\).  On the intersection \(|YZ_j|>L\), contradicting (12).  Hence
\[
|Y|\le\frac{L}{a}=\frac{2L\sqrt{d_z}}{\sigma_0}=\frac{L_{\tau}}2
\quad\text{almost surely given }(U=u,T=t).                                    \tag{13}
\]
On \(\{T=t\}\), consistency gives \(Y=Y(t)\).  Thus (13) holds for \(Y(t)\) conditional on \((U=u,T=t)\).  Since
\(P(T=t\mid U=u)>0\) by latent--arm positivity and \(Y(t)\perp T\mid U\) by the exact armwise ignorability assumption, the conditional law of \(Y(t)\) given \((U=u,T=t)\) equals its conditional law given \(U=u\).  Therefore, for every \((u,t)\),
\[
|Y(t)|\le\frac{L_{\tau}}2\quad P\text{-almost surely given }U=u,qquad
|\mu_{tu}|\le\frac{L_{\tau}}2,qquad |\tau_u|\le L_{\tau}.                  \tag{14}
\]
All divisions in this derivation are legitimate because \(d_z\ge k\ge2\), \(\sigma_0>0\), and the relevant latent arms have positive probability.

Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:vmw\text{-}model\text{-}scope}\) records the published qualitative causal, rank, and positivity conditions.  The class \(\mathcal M\) retains those conditions and strengthens only positivity and proxy rank to the fixed margins used in (1)--(9).  The support conclusion (14) is a consequence of retained conditions, not an extra member restriction.  Set enlargement transfers a lower bound carried by an explicit path, but supplies no estimator, confidence set, cluster report, or matching upper bound on the larger class.  This proves the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition derives the exact arm, compressed, stacked, and observable bounds and proves the uniform potential-outcome and effect envelope from retained hypotheses. \textit{Gap.} VMW uses qualitative rank and positivity.  The present class strengthens those two conditions quantitatively; no extra latent conditional-moment or potential-outcome support condition is imposed. \textit{Consumer.} The derived envelope supplies the common law support for every estimator and inference result while preserving the one-way subclass and lower-bound-transfer boundaries.

\begin{proposition}[prop:summary-closure-compact]\label{prop:summary-closure-compact}
The admissible summary image \(\mathscr S=S(\mathcal M)\) is uniformly bounded under \(d_S\). Hence its closure \(\mathcal K=\overline{\mathscr S}\) is a compact subset of the finite-dimensional summary space. Moreover, \(\mathcal K\) is nonempty if and only if \(\mathcal M\) is nonempty.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), (1) of \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\) permits armwise conditioning, while its observable bounds give
\[
\lVert M_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\ \lVert N_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\ 
\lVert m_X(P)\rVert_2\le L.                                                    \tag{15}
\]
Thus \(\mathscr S=S(\mathcal M)\) lies in the product of four finite-dimensional operator-norm balls of radius \(L\) and one Euclidean ball of radius \(L\), and is bounded under \(d_S\).  Its closure \(\mathcal K\) is closed and bounded.  To verify compactness without importing an undeclared result, take any sequence in \(\mathcal K\).  Every scalar coordinate is bounded by norm equivalence in the fixed finite-dimensional summary space.  Successively extract convergent subsequences for the finitely many coordinates and take the final diagonal subsequence.  Its coordinatewise limit lies in \(\mathcal K\) because \(\mathcal K\) is closed, and coordinatewise convergence is \(d_S\)-convergence by finite-dimensional norm equivalence.  Hence \(\mathcal K\) is sequentially compact and therefore compact in this metric space.  Finally, a closure is empty exactly when the original set is empty, and \(S(\mathcal M)=\varnothing\) exactly when \(\mathcal M=\varnothing\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Compactness makes nearest-summary projection exist whenever the statistical model is nonempty, while the explicit empty-class fallback makes the estimator total for incompatible constants. \textit{Gap.} VMW works with admissible population moments but does not formulate the compact summary closure or the empty-class edge case needed for a total repair through collision. \textit{Consumer.} The compact domain supports measurable nearest-point selection and the corrected confidence-set construction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:gap-free-positive-measure-modulus]\label{thm:gap-free-positive-measure-modulus}
There is a finite \(C_{\mathrm{mod}}=C_{\mathrm{mod}}(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\) such that, for every \(P,P'\in\mathcal M\), \[W_1(\nu_P,\nu_{P'})\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}d_S(S(P),S(P')).\] Equivalently, \(F\) is \(C_{\mathrm{mod}}\)-Lipschitz on \(\mathscr S\) and has a unique extension \(\overline F:\mathcal K\to\mathcal P_k([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}])\) satisfying \[W_1(\overline F(q),\overline F(q'))\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}d_S(q,q'),\qquad q,q'\in\mathcal K.\] The bound is uniform over every multiplicity pattern and simultaneous support merge or split, contains no inverse power of \(\delta(P)\) or \(\delta(P')\), and the extension always returns a positive real probability measure, including after projection of an empirical summary whose raw compressed operator has nonreal eigenvalues.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P,P'\in\mathcal M\), abbreviate their moments by \(M_t,N_t\) and \(M_t',N_t'\), and put \(w_{tu}:=P(U=u\mid T=t)\).  The two proxy separations, consistency, and armwise ignorability give
\[
N_t=A_t\operatorname{diag}(w_{t1},\ldots,w_{tk})
\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{t1},\ldots,\mu_{tk})B^\top.                           \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, reference-proxy separation factors off \(E[Z\mid U,T]\), target-proxy separation factors \(E[XY\mid U,T]\), consistency replaces \(Y\) by \(Y(t)\) on arm \(t\), and armwise ignorability replaces \(E[Y(t)\mid U,T=t]\) by \(\mu_{tu}\).  Integrability follows from (13)--(14) of \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\).

Let \(s_0:=\pi_0\sigma_0^2\).  The same proposition gives \(\sigma_k(M_t),\sigma_k(M_t')\ge s_0\) and \(\lVert N_t\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\lVert N_t'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L\).  Define the basis-free ambient operators
\[
D(P):=M_1^\dagger N_1-M_0^\dagger N_0,qquad
D(P'):=M_1'{}^\dagger N_1'-M_0'{}^\dagger N_0'.                               \tag{17}
\]
For rank-\(k\) matrices \(G,H\) whose smallest positive singular values are at least \(s_0\), the Penrose equations give the exact expansion
\[
\begin{aligned}
G^\dagger-H^\dagger={}&-G^\dagger(G-H)H^\dagger
+G^\dagger(G^\dagger)^\top(G-H)^\top(I-HH^\dagger)\\
&+(I-G^\dagger G)(G-H)^\top(H^\dagger)^\top H^\dagger.
\end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{18}
\]
Every residual factor is an orthogonal projector and both pseudoinverses have norm at most \(s_0^{-1}\).  Hence
\[
\lVert G^\dagger-H^\dagger\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le3s_0^{-2}\lVert G-H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.                                  \tag{19}
\]
The product triangle inequality, (17)--(19), and the definition of \(d_S\) imply
\[
\lVert D(P)-D(P')\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le C_1d_S(S(P),S(P')),                                                        \tag{20}
\]
where \(C_1\) depends only on \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\).

Let \(V\) be the basis in \(\mathrm{def:compressed\text{-}operator}\) and put \(R:=B^\top V\).  From the factorization proved in the observed-margin proposition, \(\operatorname{col}B=\operatorname{col}V\), so
\[
B=VR^\top,qquad \sigma_k(R)=\sigma_k(B)\ge\sigma_0,qquad
\lVert R\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt{k}L.                                   \tag{21}
\]
The last inequality follows because each column \(E[X\mid U=u]\) has norm at most \(L\).  Equations (16), the Penrose equations, and the corresponding calculation in the other arm yield
\[
V^\top D(P)V=R^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_k)R,qquad
D(P)(I-VV^\top)=0.                                                             \tag{22}
\]
Also
\[
m_X(P)=Bp=VR^\top p,qquad RV^\top e_1=B^\top e_1=\mathbf1_k.                 \tag{23}
\]
The second identity uses the anchor.  Thus \(D(P)\) is real diagonalizable, including when zero is both a signal eigenvalue and the complementary eigenvalue.  The diagonalizer \([VR^{-1},V_\perp]\) and its inverse have norms bounded by a finite \(\kappa_0=\kappa_0(k,L,\sigma_0)\).  The same holds for \(P'\), and (14) gives every signal eigenvalue in \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\).

Let \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be one-Lipschitz and replace it by \(f-f(0)\).  This does not change its integral against a difference of probability measures.  Write \(E_\lambda\) and \(E_\gamma'\) for the distinct spectral projectors of \(D(P)\) and \(D(P')\).  Their norms are at most \(\kappa_0\), and
\[
E_\lambda\{D(P)-D(P')\}E_\gamma'=(\lambda-\gamma)E_\lambda E_\gamma'.       \tag{24}
\]
For \(\lambda=\gamma\) the left side is zero.  For the other pairs, summing (24) after multiplication by the divided difference gives the exact identity
\[
f(D(P))-f(D(P'))=
\sum_{\lambda\ne\gamma}
\frac{f(\lambda)-f(\gamma)}{\lambda-\gamma}
E_\lambda\{D(P)-D(P')\}E_\gamma'.                                           \tag{25}
\]
The divided differences have absolute value at most one, and each operator has at most \(d_x\) distinct eigenvalues.  Therefore
\[
\lVert f(D(P))-f(D(P'))\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le d_x^2\kappa_0^2\lVert D(P)-D(P')\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.                    \tag{26}
\]
Direct multiplication in (22)--(23) gives
\[
m_X(P)^\top f(D(P))e_1=\sum_{u=1}^kp_uf(\tau_u)=\int f\,d\nu_P.              \tag{27}
\]
Because \(f(0)=0\), \(\lVert f(D(P))\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\kappa_0L_{\tau}\).  Combining (20), (26), (27), \(\lVert m_X(P')\rVert_2\le L\), and
\(\lVert m_X(P)-m_X(P')\rVert_2\le d_S(S(P),S(P'))\), we obtain
\[
\left|\int f\,d\nu_P-\int f\,d\nu_{P'}\right|
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}d_S(S(P),S(P')).                                         \tag{28}
\]
The constant is finite and depends only on \(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\), because \(L_{\tau}=4L\sqrt{d_z}/\sigma_0\).

For completeness, the supremum in (28) is \(W_1\) on the line.  If finite laws \(\lambda,\lambda'\) have distribution functions \(G,G'\), their increasing-quantile coupling has cost \(\int_{\mathbb R}|G-G'|\).  The absolutely continuous function
\[
f_0(x):=-\int_0^x\operatorname{sgn}\{G(s)-G'(s)\}\,ds
\]
is one-Lipschitz, and finite-sum integration by parts gives
\(\int f_0\,d(\lambda-\lambda')=\int|G-G'|\).  Conversely, every coupling bounds the integral difference of any one-Lipschitz function by its transport cost.  Taking the supremum in (28) proves
\[
W_1(\nu_P,\nu_{P'})\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}d_S(S(P),S(P')).                       \tag{29}
\]

It remains to construct the extension and verify positivity.  If \(q_m\in\mathscr S\) converges to \(q\in\mathcal K\), (29) makes \(F(q_m)\) Cauchy in \(W_1\).  Write, allowing zero weights and repeated locations,
\[
F(q_m)=\sum_{u=1}^kw_{mu}\delta_{x_{mu}},\qquad
w_m\in\Delta^{k-1},\quad x_{mu}\in[-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}].
\]
Compactness of the finite product of the simplex and the closed bounded support interval follows by the same coordinate-subsequence argument as in \(\mathrm{prop:summary\text{-}closure\text{-}compact}\).  Hence a subsequence satisfies \(w_{mu}\to w_u\) and \(x_{mu}\to x_u\) for every \(u\).  Coupling like-indexed mass and moving only unmatched mass across an interval of diameter \(2L_{\tau}\) gives
\[
W_1\!\left(F(q_m),\sum_{u=1}^kw_u\delta_{x_u}\right)
\le\max_u|x_{mu}-x_u|+L_{\tau}\sum_{u=1}^k|w_{mu}-w_u|\longrightarrow0.        \tag{30}
\]
The limit is a positive real probability law with at most \(k\) atoms.  Cauchyness makes the whole sequence converge to it.  Define that limit as \(\overline F(q)\).  Inequality (29) makes it independent of the approximating sequence, passes to the limit with the same constant, and gives uniqueness because \(\mathscr S\) is dense in \(\mathcal K\).  No step divides by an effect gap.  Projection onto \(\mathcal K\) therefore returns a positive real law even when the raw empirical compressed spectrum is nonreal, and the result is uniform through simultaneous merges and splits.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gap-free positive-measure modulus is the primary theorem and the sole stability input for the law-level statistical consequences. \textit{Gap.} Virk, Mazaheri, and Wu (2026, Section 7.1 and Theorem 7.2) provide observable-moment concentration, support localization, and separated inverse-gap weight recovery; they do not provide positive quotient-law stability through multiplicity. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem converts any valid observable-summary uncertainty region into collision-robust estimation and inference for the quotient effect law.

\begin{proposition}[prop:summary-repair-total-borel]\label{prop:summary-repair-total-borel}
If \(\mathcal K\ne\varnothing\), a Borel nearest-point selector \(\Pi(s)\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{q\in\mathcal K}d_S(q,s)\) exists by Brown and Purves (1973), and \(\widehat\nu_n=\overline F(\Pi(\widehat S_n))\). If \(\mathcal K=\varnothing\), the corrected fallback definitions set \(\Pi(s)=0\), \(\widehat\nu_n=\delta_0\), and \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}=\{\delta_0\}\). Consequently, in either case \(\widehat\nu_n\) is a total Borel positive real law-valued estimator and \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\) is nonempty on every sample.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(\mathcal K\ne\varnothing\), \(\mathrm{prop:summary\text{-}closure\text{-}compact}\) makes \(\mathcal K\) a nonempty compact subset of the finite-dimensional summary space, and \(d_S\) is continuous by \(\mathrm{def:observable\text{-}summary}\).  The established selection lemma \(\mathrm{lem:borel\text{-}nearest\text{-}point\text{-}selector}\) supplies a Borel map \(\Pi\) satisfying
\[
d_S(\Pi(s),s)=\min_{q\in\mathcal K}d_S(q,s).                         \tag{31}
\]
The empirical summary is Borel by \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}summary}\), and \(\overline F\) is continuous by \(\mathrm{thm:gap\text{-}free\text{-}positive\text{-}measure\text{-}modulus}\).  Hence \(\widehat\nu_n=\overline F\circ\Pi\circ\widehat S_n\) is a Borel map into \(\mathcal P_k([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}])\), so it is positive and real-valued.  In the retained theoretical part of \(\mathrm{def:wasserstein\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\), take \(q=\Pi(\widehat S_n)\).  Its distance from itself is zero, and therefore
\[
\widehat\nu_n=\overline F(\Pi(\widehat S_n))\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}.
\]
Thus the theoretical summary-inversion set is nonempty.  This conclusion uses neither the definition nor any property of the distinct computable outer set \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\).

If \(\mathcal K=\varnothing\), \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}space\text{-}repair}\) and the retained fallback in \(\mathrm{def:wasserstein\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\) give the constant maps
\[
\Pi(s)=0,\qquad \widehat\nu_n=\delta_0,
\qquad \mathcal C_{n,\alpha}=\{\delta_0\}.
\]
Since \(L_{\tau}>0\), \(\delta_0\in\mathcal P_k([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}])\).  Hence totality, Borel measurability, positivity, reality, and nonemptiness of the theoretical set hold in both cases, independently of the new outer construction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition discharges totality, measurability, positivity, and nonemptiness, including incompatible class-constant choices. \textit{Gap.} The published spectral estimator does not give a Borel law-valued map on empty arms, nonreal spectra, or an empty admissible-summary class. \textit{Consumer.} All finite-sample risk, coverage, and cluster-report statements can quantify over every observed sample.

\begin{theorem}[thm:collision-uniform-root-n]\label{thm:collision-uniform-root-n}
The explicit sample-evaluable lattice estimator \(\widehat\lambda_n\) and the theoretical nearest-summary estimator \(\widehat\nu_n=\overline F(\Pi(\widehat S_n))\) both estimate the quotient law at a collision-uniform root-\(n\) rate.  Specifically, there is a finite \(C=C(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\) such that, for every \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\!\left\{
\max\{W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P),W_1(\widehat\nu_n,\nu_P)\}
>C\sqrt{\frac{\log(C/\eta)}{n}}\right\}\le\eta.
\]
Both estimators are total, Borel, positive, and real-law-valued on every sample.  The first is computable by the explicit no-advice finite lattice in \(\mathrm{prop:polynomial\text{-}net\text{-}law\text{-}estimator}\); the second remains the closure-based theoretical construction used for statements requiring membership in \(\overline F(\mathcal K)\).  The simultaneous bound includes \(\{N_{t,n}=0\}\), whose probability is at most \((1-k\pi_0)^n\) for each arm, and is uniform over every effect multiplicity pattern.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(0<\eta<1/2\).  The deterministic conclusion of \(\mathrm{prop:polynomial\text{-}net\text{-}law\text{-}estimator}\) gives
\[
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)
\le C_{\mathrm{lat}}\{d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))+n^{-1/2}\}.             \tag{104}
\]
For the theoretical estimator, \(S(P)\in\mathscr S\subseteq\mathcal K\), so nearest-point optimality and the triangle inequality give
\[
d_S\{\Pi(\widehat S_n),S(P)\}
\le d_S\{\Pi(\widehat S_n),\widehat S_n\}
+d_S\{\widehat S_n,S(P)\}
\le2d_S\{\widehat S_n,S(P)\}.                                      \tag{105}
\]
Since \(\overline F(S(P))=\nu_P\), the gap-free modulus yields
\[
W_1(\widehat\nu_n,\nu_P)
\le2C_{\mathrm{mod}}d_S\{\widehat S_n,S(P)\}.                       \tag{106}
\]
The totality, Borel measurability, and positive real support of \(\widehat\nu_n\) follow from \(\mathrm{prop:summary\text{-}repair\text{-}total\text{-}borel}\); the corresponding properties of \(\widehat\lambda_n\) follow from the lattice proposition.

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\text{-}summary\text{-}concentration}\) at level \(\eta\).  On its event, both (104) and (106) are bounded by a class-dependent multiple of \(\sqrt{\log(C_0/\eta)/n}\); the additive \(n^{-1/2}\) in (104) is absorbed because \(\eta<1/2\).  Increasing \(C\) and using monotonicity of the logarithm proves the simultaneous displayed probability inequality.  The same concentration lemma gives
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\{N_{t,n}=0\}\le(1-k\pi_0)^n.
\]
Neither (104) nor (106) contains an effect gap, proving uniformity through all collisions, merges, and splits.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives the same collision-uniform root-\(n\) law rate for the explicit lattice estimator and the retained theoretical estimator. \textit{Gap.} Published VMW separated recovery does not give either this gap-free quotient-law guarantee or a total positive-real rule on arbitrary empirical summaries. \textit{Consumer.} Conditional on a separate censoring and prespecified finite-fitness bridge, the explicit estimator could support stable heterogeneity summaries for the 504-patient stage-III NSCLC PROTECT setting; the theorem does not validate that bridge.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-root-n-confidence]\label{thm:honest-root-n-confidence}
For a suitable choice of the simultaneous-concentration constant in \(r_{n,\alpha}\), both the original summary-inversion set and the computable outer set are honest:
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\{
\nu_P\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\cap\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Both sets are nonempty on every sample, and
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{W_1}(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha})
\le4C_{\mathrm{mod}}r_{n,\alpha},
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}_{W_1}(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha})
\le2R_{n,\alpha}.
\]
The second set has the explicit fixed-dimensional constrained-program representation in \(\mathrm{def:wasserstein\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\).  Coverage and root-\(n\) \(W_1\)-diameter are uniform over every collision, merge, and split stratum; neither equality nor inclusion between the two confidence sets is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(C_0\) in \(r_{n,\alpha}\) as in \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\text{-}summary\text{-}concentration}\).  Uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal M\),
\[
Q_P^{(n)}(E_P)
=Q_P^{(n)}\{d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\le r_{n,\alpha}\}
\ge1-\alpha.                                                        \tag{107}
\]
On \(E_P\), nearest-point optimality gives
\[
d_S\{S(P),\Pi(\widehat S_n)\}\le2r_{n,\alpha}.                      \tag{108}
\]
Since \(S(P)\in\mathcal K\) and \(\overline F(S(P))=\nu_P\), (108) makes \(S(P)\) feasible in the theoretical summary-inversion definition.  Hence \(\nu_P\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\).

The deterministic lattice bound gives on the same event
\[
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)
\le C_{\mathrm{lat}}(r_{n,\alpha}+n^{-1/2})
=R_{n,\alpha}.                                                       \tag{109}
\]
Latent-arm positivity implies \(p_u\ge2\pi_0\); hence every distinct atom of \(\nu_P\), after aggregation of equal effects, has mass at least \(2\pi_0\ge m_\star\).  Thus \(\nu_P\in\mathcal P_{\le k,m_\star}([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}])\), and (109) proves \(\nu_P\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\).  Together with (107), this proves simultaneous honest coverage.

For any two summaries \(q,q'\) feasible for the theoretical set,
\[
d_S(q,q')\le4r_{n,\alpha}.
\]
The gap-free modulus therefore yields
\(W_1(\overline F(q),\overline F(q'))\le4C_{\mathrm{mod}}r_{n,\alpha}\).  For any \(\xi,\xi'\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\),
\[
W_1(\xi,\xi')
\le W_1(\xi,\widehat\lambda_n)+W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\xi')
\le2R_{n,\alpha}.                                                    \tag{110}
\]
The theoretical set contains \(\widehat\nu_n\) whenever \(\mathcal K\ne\varnothing\), and equals \(\{\delta_0\}\) otherwise, by \(\mathrm{prop:summary\text{-}repair\text{-}total\text{-}borel}\).  The algorithmic set contains \(\widehat\lambda_n\), because the lattice proposition gives its atom floor and its distance from itself is zero.  Thus both sets are nonempty on every sample.

Finally, write the center and candidate in ordered atomic form.  A nonnegative array \((\gamma_{a\ell})\) with the displayed row and column sums is precisely a coupling between them, and its displayed objective is the transport cost.  Minimizing that cost over such arrays is the definition of \(W_1\) for finite atomic laws.  Enumeration of \(1\le s\le k\) therefore gives exactly the asserted fixed-dimensional constrained representation.  No step relates the two confidence sets by equality or inclusion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem simultaneously covers the retained theoretical image and an evaluable atom-floor outer set, with root-\(n\) Wasserstein diameters. \textit{Gap.} The two honest sets serve different purposes and are not claimed equal or nested. \textit{Consumer.} The outer set supports sample-evaluable aggregate latent-effect-law reporting without preliminary separation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:cluster-adaptive-report]\label{thm:cluster-adaptive-report}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), on \(E_P\), the sets \(K_C(P)\), indexed by \(C\in\widehat{\mathscr K}_{n,\alpha}\) constructed from \(\widehat\lambda_n\), form a partition of \(\operatorname{supp}(\nu_P)\).  Every \(K_C(P)\) lies in the reported support interval for \(C\), and \(I_C\), computed over \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\), contains \(\nu_P(K_C(P))\).  More explicitly, a singleton associated cluster has support-interval length at most
\[
4\rho_{n,\alpha}=4R_{n,\alpha}/m_\star,
\]
its mass interval has length at most
\[
\frac{8}{m_\star}\min\!\left\{1,\frac{R_{n,\alpha}}{\delta(P)}\right\},
\]
and every associated cluster satisfies
\[
\operatorname{length}(I_C)
\le\frac{8}{m_\star}\min\!\left\{1,\frac{R_{n,\alpha}}{\Delta_C(P)}\right\},
\]
where the right side is interpreted as zero when \(\Delta_C(P)=+\infty\).  Finally,
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}(E_P)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the entire report has a fixed-dimensional constrained-program representation, while effects unresolved at the \(\rho_{n,\alpha}\) scale are pooled without hiding the true-cluster association or its external gap.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and work on \(E_P\).  Write
\[
m_0:=m_\star=\pi_0,
\qquad R:=R_{n,\alpha},
\qquad \rho:=\rho_{n,\alpha}=R/m_0.                                  \tag{111}
\]
The lattice proposition gives every distinct atom of \(\widehat\lambda_n\) mass at least \(m_0\).  Latent-arm positivity gives
\[
p_u=\sum_{t=0}^1P(U=u,T=t)\ge2\pi_0\ge m_0,                          \tag{112}
\]
so every distinct atom of \(\nu_P\), after aggregation, also has mass at least \(m_0\).  By the deterministic lattice bound and the definition of \(E_P\),
\[
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)\le R.                                  \tag{113}
\]

We first record the atom-floor transport implication.  If probability laws \(\zeta\) and \(\zeta'\) have every distinct atom of mass at least \(m_0\) and \(W_1(\zeta,\zeta')\le R=m_0\rho\), then their supports are mutually within distance \(\rho\).  Indeed, if an atom \(x\) of \(\zeta\) had \(\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\zeta')>\rho\), every coupling would move its mass, at least \(m_0\), more than \(\rho\), at cost greater than \(R\), a contradiction.  Interchanging the laws proves the reverse inclusion.  The weak conclusion \(\le\rho\) covers equality.

Apply this implication to (113).  Every true atom lies within \(\rho\) of at least one center atom and therefore belongs to some \(K_C(P)\).  It belongs to at most one: if it were within \(\rho\) of center atoms in two components, those center atoms would be at distance at most \(2\rho\), and the \(4\rho\) joining rule would place them in the same component.  Hence the sets \(K_C(P)\) partition \(\operatorname{supp}(\nu_P)\).  Conversely, every center atom is within \(\rho\) of a true atom, so every component has a nonempty true association.  By definition, every member of \(K_C(P)\) lies in \([\min C-\rho,\max C+\rho]\).

Equations (112)--(113) and the definition of the algorithmic confidence set show that \(\nu_P\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\).  Taking \(\xi=\nu_P\) in the definition of \(I_C\) proves \(\nu_P(K_C(P))\in I_C\).

Fix arbitrary \(\xi\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\).  Every atom of \(\xi\) has mass at least \(m_0\), and
\[
W_1(\xi,\widehat\lambda_n)\le R,
\qquad W_1(\xi,\nu_P)\le2R.                                        \tag{114}
\]
The atom-floor implication applied to the first inequality shows that candidate and center supports are mutually within \(\rho\).  Consequently every candidate atom belongs to exactly one \(K_C(\xi)\), and every center component has a nonempty candidate association.  The program in the definition enumerates exactly these candidates and the binary equivalence selects exactly their associated atoms, so its objective equals \(\xi(K_C(\xi))\).  Taking its infimum and supremum therefore yields exactly \(I_C\), proving the asserted computable constrained representation.

Fix a component \(C\), put \(A:=K_C(\xi)\), \(B:=K_C(P)\), and first suppose \(\Delta:=\Delta_C(P)<+\infty\).  Then \(B\) and its complement are nonempty finite sets, so \(\Delta>0\).  Every atom of \(A\) is within \(\rho\) of a center atom in \(C\), which is within \(\rho\) of an atom of \(B\); thus every point of \(A\) is within \(2\rho\) of \(B\).  Likewise, each atom of \(A^c\) lies within \(2\rho\) of \(B^c\).

For any coupling \(\gamma\) of \(\xi\) and \(\nu_P\), marginal balance gives
\[
\xi(A)-\nu_P(B)
=\gamma(A\times B^c)-\gamma(A^c\times B).                            \tag{115}
\]
Every pair in either crossing rectangle is separated by at least \(\Delta-2\rho\).  If \(\Delta>4\rho\), (114)--(115), followed by an infimum over couplings, gives
\[
|\xi(K_C(\xi))-\nu_P(K_C(P))|
\le\frac{W_1(\xi,\nu_P)}{\Delta-2\rho}
\le\frac{4R}{\Delta}.                                                \tag{116}
\]
If \(0<\Delta\le4\rho=4R/m_0\), the left side is at most one and \(R/\Delta\ge m_0/4\).  Combining cases gives
\[
|\xi(K_C(\xi))-\nu_P(K_C(P))|
\le\frac{4}{m_0}\min\!\left\{1,\frac{R}{\Delta_C(P)}\right\}.     \tag{117}
\]
If \(\Delta_C(P)=+\infty\), then \(B\) is the whole true support.  Since the associated true sets partition that support and every component has a nonempty true association, \(C\) is the unique component.  Candidate associations also partition the candidate support, so both masses equal one and the error is zero.  Taking the range over all \(\xi\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{alg}}_{n,\alpha}\) in (117) and doubling its right side proves the interval-length bound.

If \(K_C(P)=\{x\}\), every center atom in \(C\) has a true atom within \(\rho\); that atom belongs to \(K_C(P)\) and hence equals \(x\).  Therefore \(\max C-\min C\le2\rho\), and the expanded support interval has length at most \(4\rho=4R/m_0\).  For a singleton association, \(\Delta_C(P)\ge\delta(P)\), with both sides \(+\infty\) in the one-support-point case, so its mass bound follows from the general bound.  Since \(C_0,L\ge1\) and \(\alpha<1/2\), \(n^{-1/2}\le(\log2)^{-1/2}r_{n,\alpha}\); hence \(R_{n,\alpha}\le C r_{n,\alpha}\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\text{-}summary\text{-}concentration}\) at level \(\alpha\) gives \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}(E_P)\ge1-\alpha\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit outer set yields a constrained-program report whose support and aggregate-mass errors adapt to the external cluster gap. \textit{Gap.} The report is evaluable in fixed dimension, but an efficient exact solver and exact extrema for the original theoretical image remain outside the claim. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can pool effects unresolved at the confidence radius and report separate cluster masses only when the external gap supports them.

\begin{theorem}[thm:labeled-weight-upper]\label{thm:labeled-weight-upper}
As a labeled-coordinate consequence of the root-law estimator and the additional gap-local regime, there is a finite \(C=C(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\) such that, uniformly over \(g\in(0,1/4]\), \[\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[\lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]\le C\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.\] The inverse-gap behavior concerns ordered labels and does not enter the \(W_1\) risk of \(\nu_P\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
Z_P:=W_1(\widehat\nu_n,\nu_P).
\]
By the support declarations for \(\widehat\nu_n\) and \(\nu_P\), both laws are supported on \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\), and hence
\[
0\le Z_P\le 2L_{\tau}.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Enlarge, if necessary, the constant in \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\) to a number \(A\ge2\).  For
\[
x_0:=A\sqrt{\frac{\log(4A)}{n}},
\qquad
\eta_x:=A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right),
\qquad x\ge x_0,
\]
one has \(0<\eta_x\le1/4<1/2\), and
\[
A\sqrt{\frac{\log(A/\eta_x)}{n}}=x.
\]
The simultaneous probability bound in \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\), applied to its \(\widehat\nu_n\) coordinate at level \(\eta_x\), therefore gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\{Z_P>x\}
\le A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right),
\qquad x\ge x_0.                                                            \tag{2}
\]
If \(x_0\le2L_{\tau}\), the layer-cake identity, Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative integrand, (1), and (2) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[Z_P]
&\le x_0+\int_{x_0}^{\infty}
A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right)\,dx\\
&\le \frac{A\sqrt{\log(4A)}+A^2\sqrt{\pi}/2}{\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{3}
\]
If \(x_0>2L_{\tau}\), (1) instead gives \(E[Z_P]\le2L_{\tau}<x_0\), so after increasing a constant if needed, both cases imply
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[Z_P]\le\frac{C_1}{\sqrt n},             \tag{4}
\]
where \(C_1\) depends only on \(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M(g)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:gap\text{-}stratum}\), its quotient law has exactly \(k\) distinct support points and its smallest gap \(\delta:=\delta(P)\) satisfies
\[
\delta\ge g/2.                                                              \tag{5}
\]
Relabel the latent classes so that \(\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_k\).  Since \(T\in\{0,1\}\), the latent--arm positivity assumption gives, for every \(u\),
\[
p_u=P(U=u)=\sum_{t=0}^1P(U=u,T=t)\ge2\pi_0=:m_0.                            \tag{6}
\]
Consider the event
\[
\mathcal A:=\{Z_P<m_0\delta/4\}.                                           \tag{7}
\]
For each \(u\), the open interval \(I_u:=(\tau_u-\delta/4,\tau_u+\delta/4)\) must contain a support point of \(\widehat\nu_n\) on \(\mathcal A\).  Indeed, if \(I_u\) contained none, every coupling of \(\nu_P\) and \(\widehat\nu_n\) would transport all mass \(p_u\) at least \(\delta/4\), and (6) would imply \(Z_P\ge m_0\delta/4\), contrary to (7).  The intervals \(I_1,\ldots,I_k\) are pairwise disjoint.  Because \(\widehat\nu_n\) has at most \(k\) distinct atoms, it consequently has on \(\mathcal A\) exactly one atom \(\widehat\tau_u\) in each \(I_u\) and no atom outside their union.  In particular it has exactly \(k\) atoms, so \(\mathrm{def:labeled\text{-}weight\text{-}estimator}\) returns their ordered positive masses; write these masses as \(\widehat p_u\).

Let \(\gamma=(\gamma_{uv})\) be any coupling of \(\nu_P\) and \(\widehat\nu_n\), with row index corresponding to \(\tau_u\) and column index corresponding to \(\widehat\tau_v\).  Its off-diagonal mass obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{u\ne v}\gamma_{uv}
&=1-\sum_{u=1}^k\gamma_{uu}
\ge1-\sum_{u=1}^k\min\{p_u,\widehat p_u\}\\
&=\frac12\sum_{u=1}^k|p_u-\widehat p_u|.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{8}
\]
For \(u\ne v\), the definition of the intervals and the gap \(\delta\) give
\[
|\tau_u-\widehat\tau_v|
\ge |\tau_u-\tau_v|-|\tau_v-\widehat\tau_v|
>\frac{3\delta}{4}\ge\frac{\delta}{2}.                                    \tag{9}
\]
Combining (8) and (9), every coupling has transport cost at least
\(\delta\lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1/4\).  Taking the infimum over couplings in the definition of \(W_1\) proves, on \(\mathcal A\),
\[
\lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1
\le\frac{4Z_P}{\delta}.                                                     \tag{10}
\]
On \(\mathcal A^c\), the \(\ell_1\)-diameter of the simplex is two, while (7) implies
\[
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A^c}\le\frac{4Z_P}{m_0\delta}.                        \tag{11}
\]
Thus (10), (11), and (4) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
&\le \frac4\delta E[Z_P\mathbf1_{\mathcal A}]
+2Q_P^{(n)}(\mathcal A^c)\\
&\le\left(4+\frac8{m_0}\right)\frac{E[Z_P]}{\delta}
\le\frac{C_2}{\sqrt n\,\delta}
\le\frac{2C_2}{\sqrt n\,g},
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{12}
\]
where the last inequality uses (5).  Independently, the simplex diameter gives an upper bound of two.  Consequently, with \(C:=\max\{2,2C_2\}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
\le C\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.
\]
All constants used above are independent of \(P\), \(n\), and \(g\).  The only division by the effect gap occurs in the transport-to-ordered-label implication (10); the quotient-law risk (4) itself is gap-free.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:two-class-witness-valid]\label{prop:two-class-witness-valid}

whose minimum exceeds \(\pi_0=0.1\); their column sums are

\begin{theorem}[thm:matching-local-lower-bounds]\label{thm:matching-local-lower-bounds}
For the explicit submodel \(k=d_x=d_z=2\), \(L=2\), \(\pi_0=0.1\), and \(\sigma_0=0.1\), there are a sufficiently small universal amplitude \(a>0\) and constants \(c,C>0\) such that \[\inf_{\widetilde\nu_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal L_n^{\nu}}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)]\ge\frac{c}{\sqrt n}\] and, uniformly for \(g\in(0,1/4]\), \[\inf_{\widetilde p_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal L_{n,g}^{p}}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[\lVert\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1]\ge c\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.\] Each inequality is certified by an explicit two-point path inside the displayed Bernoulli proxy factorization. The quotient-law path has one-observation Kullback--Leibler divergence at most \(C/n\) and \(W_1\)-separation at least \(c/\sqrt n\). For the labeled path, a weight displacement \(h\) preserves both proxy conditional-independence factorizations, positivity, and rank, has one-observation observed-data Kullback--Leibler divergence at most \(C g^2h^2\), and has ordered-weight separation at least \(c|h|\); taking \(|h|=a\min\{1,(\sqrt n g)^{-1}\}\) gives the second bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove both assertions by finite-support two-point arguments and derive the testing inequality used at the end.

For the quotient-law assertion, identify the observed record under the two-class witness with
\(\omega=(t,x_2,z_2,y)\in\Omega:=\{0,1\}^4\), and write
\[
 q_\varepsilon(\omega):=(P_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{wit}})_O\{\omega\}.
\]
In the construction \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}class\text{-}witness}\), every treatment, proxy, and outcome Bernoulli probability at \(\varepsilon=0\) lies strictly between zero and one, and both latent masses are positive.  Consequently
\[
 m_\nu:=\min_{\omega\in\Omega}q_0(\omega)>0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Only the two conditional means of \(Y(1)\) vary with \(\varepsilon\), and they vary affinely.  The conditional-independence factorization in that definition therefore gives numbers \(\dot q(\omega)\), independent of \(\varepsilon\), such that
\[
 q_\varepsilon(\omega)=q_0(\omega)+\varepsilon\dot q(\omega).                    \tag{2}
\]
For any probability vectors \(r,s\) on a finite set with \(s>0\), the inequality \(\log x\le x-1\) gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\Vert s)
 \le \sum_\omega r(\omega)\left\{\frac{r(\omega)}{s(\omega)}-1\right\}
 =\sum_\omega\frac{\{r(\omega)-s(\omega)\}^2}{s(\omega)}
 =:\chi^2(r\Vert s).                                                              \tag{3}
\]
Applying (3) to (1)--(2) yields the finite numerical constant
\[
 K_\nu:=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\frac{\dot q(\omega)^2}{q_0(\omega)}<\infty,
 \qquad
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\varepsilon\Vert q_0)
 \le\chi^2(q_\varepsilon\Vert q_0)=K_\nu\varepsilon^2.                         \tag{4}
\]
Fix a positive numerical constant \(a\le1/8\), to be decreased below, and set \(\varepsilon_n=a/\sqrt n\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:two\text{-}class\text{-}witness\text{-}valid}\) places both \(P_0^{\mathrm{wit}}\) and \(P_{\varepsilon_n}^{\mathrm{wit}}\) in \(\mathcal M\).  If \(K_\nu a^2\le c_{\mathrm{loc}}\), (4) and \(\mathrm{ass:local\text{-}quotient\text{-}neighborhood}\) place both laws in \(\mathcal L_n^\nu\).  The quotient laws supplied by the same proposition obey
\[
 \begin{split}
 W_1\!\left(\nu_{P_0^{\mathrm{wit}}},\nu_{P_{\varepsilon_n}^{\mathrm{wit}}}\right)
 &=W_1\!\left(\delta_{1/4},\frac25\delta_{1/4-\varepsilon_n}
                   +\frac35\delta_{1/4+\varepsilon_n}\right)\\
 &=\frac25\varepsilon_n+\frac35\varepsilon_n
 =\frac a{\sqrt n}.                                                               \tag{5}
 \end{split}
\]
The second equality holds because every coupling whose first marginal is the point mass \(\delta_{1/4}\) transports the two target masses from \(1/4\).

We next construct the labeled-weight path.  Put
\[
 b_1(h):=\frac15,
 \qquad
 b_2(h):=\frac{12/25-h/5}{3/5-h},
 \qquad
 B_h:=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ b_1(h)&b_2(h)\end{pmatrix},                            \tag{6}
\]
and define \((q_0,s_0)=(12/25,6/25)\), \((q_1,s_1)=(13/25,8/25)\),
\[
 w_t(h)^\top:=(q_t,s_t)(B_h^\top)^{-1}.                                           \tag{7}
\]
At \(h=0\), \(B_0=B\) and direct inversion gives
\[
 w_0(0)=(6/25,6/25)^\top,
 \qquad
 w_1(0)=(4/25,9/25)^\top.                                                         \tag{8}
\]
For the witness matrices \(A_t\), set
\[
 J_t:=A_t\operatorname{diag}\{w_t(0)\}B_0^\top,
 \qquad
 A_{t,h}:=J_t(B_h^\top)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\{w_t(h)\}^{-1}.                  \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7) and (9) imply, respectively,
\[
 w_t(h)^\top B_h^\top=(q_t,s_t),
 \qquad
 A_{t,h}\operatorname{diag}\{w_t(h)\}B_h^\top=J_t.                              \tag{10}
\]
The first row of \(J_t\) is \((q_t,s_t)\), because the first row of \(A_t\) is \((1,1)\).  Thus (7) and (9) also show that the first row of \(A_{t,h}\) is \((1,1)\).  Moreover, for
\[
 p(h):=(2/5+h,3/5-h)^\top,
\]
the definition of \(b_2(h)\) gives
\[
 p(h)^\top B_h^\top=(1,14/25)
 =(q_0+q_1,s_0+s_1)
 =\{w_0(h)+w_1(h)\}^\top B_h^\top.                                               \tag{11}
\]
Since \(B_h\) is invertible in a neighborhood of zero, (11) entails
\[
 w_0(h)+w_1(h)=p(h).                                                               \tag{12}
\]

Define \(P_{g,h}\) as follows.  Give \(U\) mass vector \(p(h)\), put
\[
 P_{g,h}(T=1\mid U=u):=\frac{w_{1u}(h)}{p_u(h)},
 \qquad
 E_{P_{g,h}}[Y(0)\mid U=u]=\frac14,                                                \tag{13}
\]
and set
\[
 E_{P_{g,h}}[Y(1)\mid U=1]=\frac12-\frac g2,
 \qquad
 E_{P_{g,h}}[Y(1)\mid U=2]=\frac12+\frac g2.                                     \tag{14}
\]
Conditional on \(U\), generate \(X_2,Y(0),Y(1),T\) mutually independently as Bernoulli variables, with the conditional mean of \(X_2\) given by the second row of \(B_h\).  Conditional on \((U,T=t)\), generate \(Z_2\) independently of \((X_2,Y(0),Y(1))\), with mean given by the second row of \(A_{t,h}\), and set \(Y=Y(T)\).  Thus the construction satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:target\text{-}proxy\text{-}separation}\), \(\mathrm{ass:reference\text{-}proxy\text{-}separation}\), \(\mathrm{ass:latent\text{-}ignorability}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\); its first proxy coordinate is one, as required by \(\mathrm{ass:anchor}\).

All entries in (6)--(9) are rational continuous functions of \(h\) near zero.  At zero, (8), the proxy probabilities in \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}class\text{-}witness}\), and the singular values certified by \(\mathrm{prop:two\text{-}class\text{-}witness\text{-}valid}\) have strict slack relative to zero, \(\pi_0=0.1\), and \(\sigma_0=0.1\).  Hence there is a numerical \(h_0>0\) such that, whenever \(|h|\le h_0\), all probabilities in (6)--(14) lie in \([0,1]\),
\[
 \min_{t,u}w_{tu}(h)\ge0.1,
 \qquad
 \min\{\sigma_2(B_h),\sigma_2(A_{0,h}),\sigma_2(A_{1,h})\}\ge0.1.                \tag{15}
\]
This choice is uniform over \(0<g\le1/4\): the quantities in (15) do not depend on \(g\), while the two probabilities in (14) lie in \([3/8,5/8]\).  Since \(X=(1,X_2)^\top\), \(Z=(1,Z_2)^\top\), and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \lVert X\rVert_2\le\sqrt2\le2,
 \quad
 \lVert ZX^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\lVert Z\rVert_2\lVert X\rVert_2\le2,
 \quad
 \lVert YZX^\top\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2.                                      \tag{16}
\]
Equations (12), (15), and (16) verify, respectively, that (13) is a valid treatment law, \(\mathrm{ass:latent\text{-}arm\text{-}positivity}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:proxy\text{-}rank\text{-}margin}\) hold, and all three boundedness assumptions hold with \(L=2\).  The two effects from (13)--(14) are \(1/4-g/2\) and \(1/4+g/2\); they are distinct and have gap \(g\).  Consequently \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\text{-}effects}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:gap\text{-}window}\) hold, so
\[
 P_{g,h}\in\mathcal M(g),
 \qquad 0<g\le1/4,\quad |h|\le h_0.                                             \tag{17}
\]

It remains to establish the advertised observed-data divergence.  For \(r,s\in\{0,1\}\), (10) says
\[
 J_t(r+1,s+1)
 =E_{P_{g,h}}\!\left[\mathbf 1\{T=t\}Z_2^rX_2^s\right].                         \tag{18}
\]
The four quantities in (18) determine the entire binary joint mass function of \((T=t,X_2,Z_2)\) by inclusion--exclusion.  Hence that mass function, denoted \(r_t(x,z)\), is independent of \(h\).  In arm zero, the common conditional outcome mean \(1/4\) then makes the complete observed arm-zero law independent of \(h\).  In arm one, let \(s_1=-1,s_2=1\), and let
\[
 f_{u,h}(x,z):=P_{g,h}(X_2=x\mid U=u)
               P_{g,h}(Z_2=z\mid U=u,T=1),
 \qquad
 H_{x,z}(h):=\sum_{u=1}^2s_u w_{1u}(h)f_{u,h}(x,z).                              \tag{19}
\]
The Bernoulli mass with mean \(1/2+s_ug/2\) at \(y\in\{0,1\}\) equals \(1/2+(2y-1)s_ug/2\).  Therefore the observed cell probabilities satisfy the exact identity
\[
 q_{g,h}(1,x,z,y)
 =\frac12r_1(x,z)+(2y-1)\frac g2 H_{x,z}(h).                                     \tag{20}
\]
The finitely many functions in (19) are continuously differentiable for \(|h|\le h_0\), after decreasing \(h_0\) if necessary.  Their derivatives are therefore bounded on this compact interval.  The mean-value theorem applied to (20) supplies a numerical \(K_1<\infty\), uniform in \(g\), for which
\[
 |q_{g,h}(\omega)-q_{g,0}(\omega)|\le K_1g|h|,
 \qquad \omega\in\Omega.                                                        \tag{21}
\]
At \(h=0\), the construction is exactly \(P_{g/2}^{\mathrm{wit}}\).  Every observed cell under this law is strictly positive for \(0\le g\le1/4\): all latent, treatment, and proxy probabilities are interior, the arm-zero outcome mean is \(1/4\), and the arm-one means lie in \([3/8,5/8]\).  Continuity on the compact interval gives
\[
 m_p:=\min_{0\le g\le1/4}\min_{\omega\in\Omega}q_{g,0}(\omega)>0.             \tag{22}
\]
Combining (3), (21), and (22), and noting that \(|\Omega|=16\), gives a numerical \(K_p<\infty\) such that
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{g,h})_O\Vert(P_{g,0})_O\right)
 \le\chi^2\!\left((P_{g,h})_O\Vert(P_{g,0})_O\right)
 \le K_pg^2h^2.                                                                   \tag{23}
\]
Because \(g>0\), the effects retain their displayed order.  Thus (12)--(14) give
\[
 \left\lVert p^\uparrow(P_{g,h})-p^\uparrow(P_{g,0})\right\rVert_1=2|h|.         \tag{24}
\]
Set
\[
 h(n,g):=a\min\{1,(\sqrt n\,g)^{-1}\}.                                          \tag{25}
\]
If \(a\le h_0\), then (17) applies, and
\[
 g^2h(n,g)^2\le\frac{a^2}{n}.                                                     \tag{26}
\]
If also \(K_pa^2\le c_{\mathrm{loc}}\), equations (23), (26), and \(\mathrm{ass:local\text{-}weight\text{-}neighborhood}\) place both \(P_{g,0}=P_{g/2}^{\mathrm{wit}}\) and \(P_{g,h(n,g)}\) in \(\mathcal L_{n,g}^p\), uniformly over \(g\in(0,1/4]\).

For completeness, we now derive the finite-sample testing reduction.  If \(R_0,R_1\) have densities \(r_0,r_1\) with respect to a common dominating measure, their total variation distance is
\[
 \lVert R_1-R_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\frac12\int|r_1-r_0|,
 \qquad
 \int\min(r_0,r_1)=1-\lVert R_1-R_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.                        \tag{27}
\]
For targets separated by \(\Delta\) in a metric \(d\), the triangle inequality and (27) imply, for every estimator \(\widehat\theta\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 2\max_{i=0,1}E_{R_i}d(\widehat\theta,\theta_i)
 &\ge \int\{d(\widehat\theta,\theta_0)r_0
             +d(\widehat\theta,\theta_1)r_1\}\\
 &\ge \int\min(r_0,r_1)
             \{d(\widehat\theta,\theta_0)+d(\widehat\theta,\theta_1)\}\\
 &\ge \Delta\{1-\lVert R_1-R_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.                         \tag{28}
 \end{split}
\]
If \(R_1\ll R_0\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \lVert R_1-R_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(R_1\Vert R_0)}.                                       \tag{29}
\]
For product laws, independence and the likelihood-ratio identity give
\[
 1+\chi^2(R_1^{\otimes n}\Vert R_0^{\otimes n})
 =\left\{1+\chi^2(R_1\Vert R_0)\right\}^{n}.                                  \tag{30}
\]
By (4), (23), (26), and \((1+x)^n\le e^{nx}\), both pairs constructed above satisfy
\[
 \chi^2(R_1^{\otimes n}\Vert R_0^{\otimes n})
 \le \exp\{K a^2\}-1,
 \qquad K:=\max\{K_\nu,K_p\}.                                                  \tag{31}
\]
Choose \(a>0\) small enough that all preceding restrictions hold and \(\exp\{Ka^2\}-1\le1\).  Equations (29)--(31) then make the product-experiment total variation distance at most \(1/2\) for both pairs.

For the quotient pair, insert the separation \(\Delta=a/\sqrt n\) from (5) into (28), then take the infimum over \(\widetilde\nu_n\) and the supremum over \(\mathcal L_n^\nu\), which contains both points.  This yields
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\nu_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal L_n^\nu}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)
 \ge\frac{a}{4\sqrt n}.                                                         \tag{32}
\]
For the labeled pair, (24)--(25) give \(\Delta=2a\min\{1,(\sqrt n g)^{-1}\}\).  The same use of (28) yields
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde p_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal L_{n,g}^p}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\left\lVert\widetilde p_n-p^\uparrow(P)\right\rVert_1
 \ge\frac a2\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.                       \tag{33}
\]
Thus the theorem holds with the common lower-bound constant \(c=a/4\) and, for the pathwise divergence certificates, any \(C\ge\max\{K_\nu a^2,K_p\}\).  All constants are numerical and independent of \(n\) and \(g\), as required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The verified factorization-preserving paths distinguish quotient-law regularity from labeled-coordinate irregularity in the actual observed proxy experiment. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary-mixture collision lower bounds do not preserve the VMW proxy factorization and therefore do not establish the observed-data Kullback--Leibler order required here. \textit{Consumer.} The converse calibrates both the smallest possible law-level radius and the separation needed for defensible labeled-weight reporting.

\begin{proposition}[prop:same-class-quotient-minimax]\label{prop:same-class-quotient-minimax}
For the explicit submodel \(k=d_x=d_z=2\), \(L=2\), \(\pi_0=0.1\), and \(\sigma_0=0.1\), the upper and lower bounds on the same uniformly conditioned causal VMW class imply that, for finite \(0<c<C<\infty\),
\[
\frac{c}{\sqrt n}\le\inf_{\widetilde\nu_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)]\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(k=d_x=d_z=2\), \(L=2\), \(\pi_0=0.1\), and \(\sigma_0=0.1\), as in the proposition.  Write
\[
\mathfrak R_n(\mathcal A):=
\inf_{\widetilde\nu_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal A}
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)\right]
\]
for any \(\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal M\), where the infimum is over the measurable quotient-law estimators quantified in the statement.

First consider the converse.  By \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}quotient\text{-}experiment}\), every member of \(\mathcal L_n^\nu\) is a member of \(\mathcal M\).  Hence, for every measurable \(\widetilde\nu_n\), monotonicity of the supremum gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)\right]
\ge
\sup_{P\in\mathcal L_n^\nu}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)\right].
\]
Taking the infimum over the same decision rules on both sides and then applying the first inequality of \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}local\text{-}lower\text{-}bounds}\) yields a constant \(c>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mathfrak R_n(\mathcal M)
\ge \mathfrak R_n(\mathcal L_n^\nu)
\ge \frac{c}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{1}
\]

For achievability, use the single sample-evaluable estimator \(\widehat\lambda_n\) supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\), and put
\[
Z_P:=W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P).
\]
That theorem states that \(\widehat\lambda_n\) is a total Borel probability-law-valued map.  Both \(\widehat\lambda_n\) and \(\nu_P\) are supported on \([-L_\tau,L_\tau]\), so
\[
0\le Z_P\le 2L_\tau
\tag{2}
\]
for every sample and every \(P\in\mathcal M\).  Enlarge the finite deviation constant in \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\), if necessary, to a number \(A\ge2\).  Then, for every \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\!\left\{
Z_P>A\sqrt{\frac{\log(A/\eta)}{n}}
\right\}\le\eta.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
x_0:=\frac{A\sqrt{\log(2A)}}{\sqrt n}.
\]
For any \(x\ge x_0\), the choice
\[
\eta_x:=A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right)
\]
satisfies \(0<\eta_x\le1/2\) and
\[
A\sqrt{\frac{\log(A/\eta_x)}{n}}=x.
\]
Substitution of \(\eta_x\) into (3) therefore gives the uniform tail inequality
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\{Z_P>x\}
\le A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right),
\qquad x\ge x_0.
\tag{4}
\]

The pointwise identity \(Z_P=\int_0^\infty\mathbf 1\{Z_P>x\}\,dx\), followed by nonnegative integration, (2), and (4), implies for every \(P\in\mathcal M\) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[Z_P]
&=\int_0^\infty Q_P^{(n)}\{Z_P>x\}\,dx\\
&\le x_0+A\int_{x_0}^\infty
\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right)dx\\
&=\frac{A\sqrt{\log(2A)}}{\sqrt n}
+\frac{A^2}{\sqrt n}
\int_{\sqrt{\log(2A)}}^\infty e^{-y^2}\,dy\\
&\le \frac{A\sqrt{\log(2A)}+A^2\sqrt\pi/2}{\sqrt n}
=:\frac{C}{\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The constant \(C<\infty\) depends only on the fixed model-class parameters because \(A\) does, by \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\).  Taking the supremum of (5) over \(P\in\mathcal M\), and using the admissible decision rule \(\widehat\lambda_n\) as a witness for the infimum, gives
\[
\mathfrak R_n(\mathcal M)
\le \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[Z_P]
\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (1) and (6) proves the displayed same-class minimax inequalities.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This corollary states the root-\(n\) minimax frontier on the explicit uniformly conditioned subclass containing the local quotient experiment. \textit{Gap.} VMW does not state collision-uniform quotient-law minimax risk; only the converse, not the achievability result, transfers to larger published-VMW classes containing the path. \textit{Consumer.} The result calibrates the smallest uniform Wasserstein accuracy on the displayed subclass without overstating full-class scope.

\begin{proposition}[prop:same-class-labeled-minimax]\label{prop:same-class-labeled-minimax}

\[
r_{n,g}:=\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}
\]
and define the same-class minimax risk
\[

E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\lVert\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right].                       \tag{13}
\]
Let \(R_{n,g}^{\mathrm{loc}}\) denote the same expression with \(\mathcal M(g)\) replaced by \(\mathcal L_{n,g}^{p}\), without changing the measurable decision class.  By \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}weight\text{-}experiment}\),

\]
The estimator \(\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}\) is a total Borel map into \(\Delta^{k-1}\) by \(\mathrm{def:labeled\text{-}weight\text{-}estimator}\), so it is an admissible competitor in (13).  The already proved theorem \(\mathrm{thm:labeled\text{-}weight\text{-}upper}\) supplies a finite constant \(C_+>0\), also independent of \(n\) and \(g\), for which

\le\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
\le C_+r_{n,g}.                                                             \tag{15}

Set \(c:=\min\{c_-/2,C_+/2\}\) and \(C:=C_+\).  Then \(0<c<C<\infty\), and (14)--(15) prove

c\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}
\le\inf_{\widetilde p_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
\lVert\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]

\]
This is a same-class statement: the lower experiment is a subset of the very gap stratum over which the upper bound holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:borel-nearest-point-selector]\label{lem:borel-nearest-point-selector}
If \(\mathcal K\) is a nonempty compact subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space and \(d_S\) is continuous, then the argmin correspondence \(s\mapsto\operatorname*{argmin}_{q\in\mathcal K}d_S(q,s)\) has a Borel measurable selector \(\Pi\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
\mathsf E:=\bigl(\mathbb R^{d_z\times d_x}\bigr)^4\times\mathbb R^{d_x},
\qquad D:=4d_zd_x+d_x,
\]
be the ambient summary space from \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}closure}\), equipped with its coordinate Borel structure.  Because \(d_x\) and \(d_z\) are fixed and finite, choose a coordinate linear homeomorphism
\[
\Phi:\mathsf E\longrightarrow \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D).
\tag{1}
\]
Both \(\Phi\) and \(\Phi^{-1}\) are continuous and hence Borel measurable.  Put
\[
K':=\Phi(\mathcal K).
\tag{2}
\]
The hypotheses that \(\mathcal K\) is nonempty and compact, together with continuity of \(\Phi\), imply that \(K'\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(\operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D)\).

Define the objective
\[
f:\mathsf E\times\operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D)
\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\qquad
f(s,y):=d_S\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(y),s\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
The metric \(d_S\) is continuous by hypothesis, and \(\Phi^{-1}\) is continuous by (1).  Thus \(f\) is jointly continuous, hence jointly Borel measurable, and for every \(s\in\mathsf E\) the section \(y\mapsto f(s,y)\) is continuous on \(K'\).  The parameter space \(\mathsf E\) is finite-dimensional Euclidean and therefore standard Borel.  All hypotheses of the zero-sorry in-repository theorem
\texttt{Causalean.Mathlib.MeasureTheory.borelMeasurable\_compact\_argmin\_selector}
are consequently satisfied by \(K'\) and \(f\).  That theorem supplies a Borel map
\[
\pi':\mathsf E\longrightarrow\operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D)
\tag{4}
\]
such that, for every \(s\in\mathsf E\),
\[
\pi'(s)\in K',
\qquad
f\bigl(s,\pi'(s)\bigr)\le f(s,y)
\quad\text{for every }y\in K'.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the general exact-argmin result underlying its nearest-point specialization
\texttt{Causalean.Mathlib.MeasureTheory.borelMeasurable\_nearestPoint\_selector}.

Set
\[
\Pi(s):=\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\pi'(s)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) defines a Borel map because it is a composition of the Borel maps in (1) and (4).  By (2) and the first part of (5), \(\Pi(s)\in\mathcal K\).  For arbitrary \(q\in\mathcal K\), take \(y=\Phi(q)\in K'\) in the second part of (5).  Using (3) and (6) gives
\[
d_S\bigl(\Pi(s),s\bigr)
=f\bigl(s,\pi'(s)\bigr)
\le f\bigl(s,\Phi(q)\bigr)
=d_S(q,s).
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(q\in\mathcal K\) was arbitrary, (7) proves
\[
\Pi(s)\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{q\in\mathcal K}d_S(q,s)
\quad\text{for every }s\in\mathsf E.
\]
Thus \(\Pi\) is the required Borel measurable selector.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Reference note: Brown and Purves (1973), Corollary 1(ii), DOI 10.1214/aos/1176342510, is the measurable-extrema antecedent; the logical claim here is discharged in-repository by the zero-sorry Causalean compact-argmin selector theorem.

In the published VMW model, Assumption 1 includes \(Z\perp(X,Y)\mid(T,U)\), \(X\perp(Y,T)\mid U\), causal consistency \(Y=Y(T)\), and latent ignorability \(Y(t)\perp T\mid U\).  Assumption 2 requires \(A_0,A_1,B\) to have full column rank \(k\), and Section 4.2 uses strict latent positivity \(P(T=t\mid U=u)>0\). \begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-summary-concentration]\label{lem:uniform-summary-concentration}
There is a choice of \(C_0=C_0(k,d_x,d_z,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\) such that, for every \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\!\left\{d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))>
 C_0L\sqrt{\frac{\log(C_0/\eta)}{n}}\right\}\le \eta.
\]
With \(\eta\) replaced by \(\alpha\), the radius in the display is \(r_{n,\alpha}\).  In addition, for every \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 Q_P^{(n)}\{N_{t,n}=0\}=(1-P(T=t))^n\le(1-k\pi_0)^n.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and write \(q_t:=P(T=t)\).  The observed-margin proposition gives
\[
q_t\ge q_0:=k\pi_0>0.                                                          \tag{32}
\]
We first derive the scalar concentration inequality used below.  If \(W\in[a,b]\) almost surely, \(\mu=E[W]\), \(p=(\mu-a)/(b-a)\), and \(s=\lambda(b-a)\), convexity of the exponential yields
\[
Ee^{\lambda(W-\mu)}\le(1-p)e^{-ps}+pe^{(1-p)s}.
\]
For the logarithm \(\psi_p\) of the right side, direct differentiation gives \(\psi_p(0)=\psi_p'(0)=0\) and \(0\le\psi_p''(s)\le1/4\).  Two integrations imply
\[
Ee^{\lambda(W-\mu)}\le\exp\{\lambda^2(b-a)^2/8\}.                            \tag{33}
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, optimization in \(\lambda\), and repetition for \(-W\) therefore give, for independent copies,
\[
\Pr\!\left(\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(W_i-EW_i)\right|>x\right)
\le2\exp\{-2nx^2/(b-a)^2\}.                                                   \tag{34}
\]
For \(R_i=Z_iX_i^\top\) or \(R_i=Y_iZ_iX_i^\top\), put
\[
\overline R_t:=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}R_i,qquad
\widehat q_t:=N_{t,n}/n.
\]
Every scalar entry of either \(R_i\) lies in \([-L,L]\) by the two matrix envelopes; every coordinate of \(X_i\) lies in \([-L,L]\) by the target-proxy envelope.  Apply (34) to the entries of \(\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}R_i\), the coordinates of \(X_i\), and \(\mathbf1\{T_i=t\}\), then take a union bound over the fixed \(4d_xd_z+d_x+2\) scalar quantities.  Since \(\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm F}\), there is \(C_1=C_1(d_x,d_z)\) such that, with probability at least \(1-\eta\),
\[
\max_t|\widehat q_t-q_t|
+L^{-1}\max_{t,R}\lVert\overline R_t-E\overline R_t\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
+L^{-1}\lVert\widehat m_{X,n}-m_X(P)\rVert_2
\le b_n:=C_1\sqrt{\frac{\log(C_1/\eta)}n}.                                   \tag{35}
\]
Here \(E\overline R_t=q_tM_t(P)\) or \(q_tN_t(P)\), respectively.  On \(\{b_n\le q_0/2\}\), (32) gives \(\widehat q_t\ge q_0/2\), and the total empirical-summary definition yields
\[
\widehat M_{t,n}-M_t(P)=
\frac{\overline R_t-q_tM_t(P)+(q_t-\widehat q_t)M_t(P)}{\widehat q_t},          \tag{36}
\]
with the analogous identity for \(N_t\).  The observable bounds in the margin proposition and (35)--(36) imply
\[
d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\le C_2Lq_0^{-1}
\sqrt{\frac{\log(C_1/\eta)}n}.                                                \tag{37}
\]
On \(\{b_n>q_0/2\}\), every empirical and population matrix or vector entering the summary has norm at most \(L\), including the zero matrices on an empty arm, so deterministically \(d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\le10L\).  Because \(b_n>q_0/2\), enlarging the coefficient in (37) by a constant depending only on \(q_0\) makes (37) valid in this regime too.  Choosing \(C_0\) larger than the resulting constants proves the asserted uniform inequality.  Finally, independence and (32) give
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\{N_{t,n}=0\}=(1-q_t)^n\le(1-k\pi_0)^n.
\]
Only the three retained observable envelopes were used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:polynomial-net-law-estimator]\label{thm:polynomial-net-law-estimator}
In the fixed-dimensional exact-real computational model in which the class-dependent finite library of representative summaries is precomputed, \(\widehat\nu_n^{\mathrm{net}}\) is a total Borel map with values in the positive real probability laws having at most \(k\) atoms.  The library has cardinality at most
\[
C\,n^{(4d_zd_x+d_x)/2},
\]
and exhaustive nearest-library search, including formation of \(\widehat S_n\), uses at most \(C\{n+n^{(4d_zd_x+d_x)/2}\}\) fixed-dimensional arithmetic, comparison, singular-value, and fixed-degree root-isolation operations.  Singular-value thresholding at \(\pi_0\sigma_0^2/2\) retains exactly the \(k\)-dimensional signal space for every stored representative and remains correct under operator perturbations smaller than \(\pi_0\sigma_0^2/2\).  For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), the deterministic inequality
\[
W_1(\widehat\nu_n^{\mathrm{net}},\nu_P)
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\left\{2d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))+n^{-1/2}\right\}
\]
holds.  Consequently this explicit finite-library estimator has a collision-uniform root-\(n\) high-probability guarantee, without diagonalizing a possibly nonreal empirical compressed spectrum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(\mathcal M=\varnothing\), then \(\mathscr S=\varnothing\) by \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}closure}\).  The fallback in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}net\text{-}law\text{-}estimator}\) is then the constant map \(\delta_0\), hence is Borel and takes values in the asserted space; the risk assertion is vacuous.  We therefore suppose that \(\mathcal M\ne\varnothing\).

Put
\[
D:=4d_zd_x+d_x,\qquad b:=4\sqrt{d_zd_x}+\sqrt{d_x},\qquad \epsilon_n:=n^{-1/2}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\), every matrix coordinate and every vector coordinate of every \(q\in\mathscr S\) belongs to \([-L,L]\).  If \(q,q'\) belong to the same coordinate cube of side at most \(\epsilon_n/b\), then the operator norm of each of their four matrix-coordinate differences is at most its Frobenius norm, while the Euclidean norm of their vector-coordinate difference is bounded coordinatewise.  Consequently, by the definition of \(d_S\) in \(\mathrm{def:observable\text{-}summary}\),
\[
d_S(q,q')
 \le \left(4\sqrt{d_zd_x}+\sqrt{d_x}\right)\frac{\epsilon_n}{b}
 =\epsilon_n. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the representatives in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}net\text{-}law\text{-}estimator}\) form an \(\epsilon_n\)-net of \(\mathscr S\).  The number of coordinate cubes meeting \([-L,L]^D\), and hence the number of representatives, is at most
\[
\left(2+\frac{2Lb}{\epsilon_n}\right)^D
 \le C n^{D/2}
 =C n^{(4d_zd_x+d_x)/2}, \tag{2}
\]
where \(C\) depends only on the displayed fixed dimensions and \(L\).

We next verify that the spectral formula at every stored representative returns a positive real quotient law, including at an effect collision.  Fix a representative \(q=S(P_q)\in\mathscr S\), where \(P_q\in\mathcal M\).  Write
\[
w_{tu}:=P_q(U=u\mid T=t),\qquad
D_t:=\operatorname{diag}(w_{t1},\ldots,w_{tk}),\qquad
D_{\mu,t}:=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{t1},\ldots,\mu_{tk}).
\]
The first conclusion of \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\) ensures that these conditional probabilities are defined.  The factorization conclusion of the same proposition gives
\[
M_t(P_q)=A_tD_tB^\top. \tag{3}
\]
For the outcome-weighted moment, \(\mathrm{ass:reference\text{-}proxy\text{-}separation}\) first gives
\[
N_t(P_q)
=\sum_{u=1}^k w_{tu}E_{P_q}[Z\mid U=u,T=t]
 E_{P_q}[X^\top Y\mid U=u,T=t]. \tag{4}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:target\text{-}proxy\text{-}separation}\), the last conditional expectation factors into \(E_{P_q}[X^\top\mid U=u]\,E_{P_q}[Y\mid U=u,T=t]\).  Consistency in \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) replaces \(Y\) by \(Y(t)\) on \(\{T=t\}\), and armwise latent ignorability in \(\mathrm{ass:latent\text{-}ignorability}\) gives
\[
E_{P_q}[Y\mid U=u,T=t]
=E_{P_q}[Y(t)\mid U=u,T=t]
=E_{P_q}[Y(t)\mid U=u]
=\mu_{tu}. \tag{5}
\]
Substitution of (5) into (4) therefore yields
\[
N_t(P_q)=A_tD_tD_{\mu,t}B^\top. \tag{6}
\]

The rank margin in \(\mathrm{ass:proxy\text{-}rank\text{-}margin}\) makes \(B\) have column rank \(k\).  The singular-value conclusion of \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\) makes each matrix in (3) have rank \(k\).  It follows from (3) that its row space is the \(k\)-dimensional space \(\operatorname{row}(B^\top)=\operatorname{col}(B)\); hence the vertically stacked proxy moment has the same row space.  More quantitatively, the proposition gives
\[
\sigma_k\!\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_0(P_q)\\M_1(P_q)\end{bmatrix}\right)
\ge s_0,\qquad s_0:=\pi_0\sigma_0^2. \tag{7}
\]
Thus thresholding the stored representative at \(s_0/2\) retains exactly \(k\) directions and produces an orthonormal matrix \(V\) spanning \(\operatorname{col}(B)\).  Set \(R:=B^\top V\).  This matrix is invertible: if \(Rx=0\), then \(Vx\in\operatorname{col}(B)\) and is orthogonal to every column of \(B\), so \(Vx=0\), and the orthonormality of \(V\) gives \(x=0\).

Let \(G_t:=A_tD_t\).  This matrix has full column rank because (3) has rank \(k\) and \(B^\top\) has rank \(k\).  Equations (3) and (6) imply
\[
M_t(P_q)V=G_tR,
\qquad
N_t(P_q)V=G_tD_{\mu,t}R. \tag{8}
\]
For a full-column-rank \(G_t\) and invertible \(R\), direct substitution in the Moore--Penrose formula gives
\[
(G_tR)^\dagger
=\{R^\top G_t^\top G_tR\}^{-1}R^\top G_t^\top
=R^{-1}(G_t^\top G_t)^{-1}G_t^\top
=R^{-1}G_t^\dagger. \tag{9}
\]
Combining (8)--(9) in the definition of the compressed effect operator yields
\[
\Delta
=R^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_k)R. \tag{10}
\]
In particular, every eigenvalue of \(\Delta\) is real and belongs to \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\) by \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\), and \(\Delta\) is diagonalizable even when some effects coincide.

Writing \(p:=(p_1,\ldots,p_k)^\top\), the law of total expectation and target-proxy separation give \(m_X(P_q)=Bp\).  Hence the left anchor satisfies
\[
a^\top=m_X(P_q)^\top V=p^\top B^\top V=p^\top R. \tag{11}
\]
The anchor condition \(\mathrm{ass:anchor}\) gives \(B^\top e_1=\mathbf 1_k\).  Since \(VV^\top\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}(B)\), one has \(B^\top VV^\top=B^\top\), and therefore the right anchor \(c=V^\top e_1\) obeys
\[
Rc=B^\top VV^\top e_1=B^\top e_1=\mathbf 1_k,
\qquad c=R^{-1}\mathbf 1_k. \tag{12}
\]

For a distinct eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(\Delta\), polynomial functional calculus applied directly to (10) gives
\[
E_\lambda
=\prod_{\gamma\in\operatorname{spec}(\Delta),\ \gamma\ne\lambda}
 \frac{\Delta-\gamma I_k}{\lambda-\gamma}
=R^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\{\mathbf 1(\tau_u=\lambda):1\le u\le k\}R. \tag{13}
\]
Indeed, on the \(u\)-th diagonal coordinate the product in (13) is one when \(\tau_u=\lambda\), and otherwise contains the zero factor indexed by \(\gamma=\tau_u\).  Equations (11)--(13) now show
\[
a^\top E_\lambda c
=\sum_{u:\,\tau_u=\lambda}p_u. \tag{14}
\]
These weights are nonnegative and sum to one.  Therefore, by \(\mathrm{def:quotient\text{-}law}\), \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}closure}\), and the spectral formula in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}net\text{-}law\text{-}estimator}\), the estimator's output at \(q\) is exactly
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(\Delta)}a^\top E_\lambda c\,\delta_\lambda
=\sum_{u=1}^kp_u\delta_{\tau_u}
=F(q)=\nu_{P_q}, \tag{15}
\]
where coincident effects are aggregated.  This proves, without a spectral-gap condition, that every possible output associated with a stored representative is a positive real probability law with at most \(k\) atoms.

We now prove the deterministic risk inequality.  For any \(P\in\mathcal M\), choose a stored representative \(q_0\) in the same grid cube as \(S(P)\).  By (1), \(d_S(q_0,S(P))\le\epsilon_n\).  The nearest-library definition of \(\widehat q_n\) and two applications of the triangle inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_S(\widehat q_n,S(P))
&\le d_S(\widehat q_n,\widehat S_n)+d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\\
&\le d_S(q_0,\widehat S_n)+d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\\
&\le \epsilon_n+2d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P)).
\end{aligned} \tag{16}
\]
Both \(\widehat q_n\) and \(S(P)\) belong to \(\mathscr S\).  By (15), \(\widehat\nu_n^{\mathrm{net}}=F(\widehat q_n)\), while \(F(S(P))=\nu_P\) by \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}closure}\).  Applying \(\mathrm{thm:gap\text{-}free\text{-}positive\text{-}measure\text{-}modulus}\) to (16) yields
\[
W_1(\widehat\nu_n^{\mathrm{net}},\nu_P)
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}d_S(\widehat q_n,S(P))
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\{2d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))+n^{-1/2}\}. \tag{17}
\]
In particular, on the simultaneous-concentration event supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\text{-}summary\text{-}concentration}\),
\[
W_1(\widehat\nu_n^{\mathrm{net}},\nu_P)
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\left\{2C_0L\sqrt{\frac{\log(C_0/\eta)}{n}}+n^{-1/2}\right\}, \tag{18}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\), with probability at least \(1-\eta\).  No term in (17) or (18) depends on an effect gap.

It remains to verify totality, stability, and the operation count.  For a fixed representative, the map \(s\mapsto d_S(q_{n,\ell},s)\) is continuous.  The event that index \(\ell\) is the lexicographically first minimizer is a finite intersection of weak and strict Borel inequalities between these continuous functions.  Because \(\widehat S_n\) is Borel by \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}summary}\), the finite-valued map \(\widehat q_n\), and hence the output map described in (15), is Borel.  The empty-class fallback is constant, so the estimator is total.

For the claimed threshold stability, let
\[
G:=\begin{bmatrix}M_0(P_q)\\M_1(P_q)\end{bmatrix}
\]
and let \(H\) be a conformable perturbation with \(\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}<s_0/2\).  The min--max characterization of singular values and the triangle inequality imply, for every index \(j\),
\[
|\sigma_j(G+H)-\sigma_j(G)|\le\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, for every unit vector \(x\), the inequalities \(\lVert Gx\rVert_2-\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert(G+H)x\rVert_2\le\lVert Gx\rVert_2+\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\) persist under the successive infima and suprema in that characterization.  Since (7) gives \(\sigma_k(G)\ge s_0\) and rank \(G=k\) gives \(\sigma_{k+1}(G)=0\), equation (19) yields
\[
\sigma_k(G+H)>s_0/2,
\qquad
\sigma_{k+1}(G+H)<s_0/2. \tag{20}
\]
Thus precisely \(k\) singular directions remain above the prescribed threshold.

Finally, forming \(\widehat S_n\) requires at most \(Cn\) fixed-dimensional operations.  By (2), exhaustive computation and comparison of its distance to the precomputed representatives requires at most \(Cn^{(4d_zd_x+d_x)/2}\) further fixed-dimensional operations.  Once a representative has been selected, its singular-value decomposition, the matrix operations in (8)--(13), and real root isolation for a polynomial of degree at most the fixed number \(k\) require only a fixed number of the operations allowed by the theorem's exact-real computational model.  Combining these bounds proves the asserted total operation count.  All spectral calculations occur at the admissible stored representative, for which (10) guarantees a real diagonalizable spectrum; a possibly nonreal spectrum formed directly from \(\widehat S_n\) is never diagonalized.  Equations (15), (17), (18), and (20) establish all remaining claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-library estimator attains the same collision-uniform rate with online polynomial operation count when class-dependent admissible-summary representatives are precomputed. \textit{Gap.} The finite-library search exposes polynomial online computation at the modulus scale, but its representative library is class-dependent precomputed advice and is not supplied by the data alone. \textit{Consumer.} It provides an advised computational alternative and does not claim exact computation of the full confidence or cluster report.

\begin{theorem}[thm:published-vmw-converse-transfer]\label{thm:published-vmw-converse-transfer}
Fix \(k=d_x=d_z=2\), \(L=2\), \(\pi_0=0.1\), and \(\sigma_0=0.1\).  There exist a sufficiently small universal \(a>0\) and a universal \(c>0\) such that the following holds for every \(n\ge1\).  Let \(\mathcal V_n^\nu\) be any parameter class in the published structural/finite-regularity VMW model satisfying \(\{P_0^{\mathrm{wit}},P_{a/\sqrt n}^{\mathrm{wit}}\}\subseteq\mathcal V_n^\nu\).  For \(g\in(0,1/4]\), set \(h(n,g):=a\min\{1,(\sqrt n\,g)^{-1}\}\), using the admissible positive orientation of the factorization-preserving path \(P_{g,h}\), and let \(\mathcal V_{n,g}^{p}\) be any published-VMW parameter class satisfying \(\{P_{g,0},P_{g,h(n,g)}\}\subseteq\mathcal V_{n,g}^{p}\).  Then
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\nu_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal V_n^\nu}
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)]\ge\frac c{\sqrt n}
\]
and, uniformly in \(g\in(0,1/4]\),
\[
\inf_{\widetilde p_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal V_{n,g}^{p}}
E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[\lVert\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1]
\ge c\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.
\]
Only witness-pair containment is required; neither comparator class need contain the corresponding entire local neighborhood.  The quotient pair lies in the published structural/finite-regularity VMW model, while its collision point \(P_0^{\mathrm{wit}}\) is outside the separated simple-effect recovery class of VMW Theorem 7.2; the labeled pair is separated.  This two-point transfer does not extend the gap-free modulus, either estimator upper bound, either confidence guarantee, either cluster guarantee, or either matching minimax conclusion beyond \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathcal M(g)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the two-point construction proved in \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}local\text{-}lower\text{-}bounds}\), there are numerical constants \(C_{\nu},C_p,h_0>0\), independent of \(n\) and \(g\), with the following properties.  For every \(n\ge 1\) and every \(0<a\le 1/8\), the quotient pair
\[
P_0^{\mathrm{wit}},\qquad P_{a/\sqrt n}^{\mathrm{wit}}
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal M\), and its one-observation divergence and target separation satisfy
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{a/\sqrt n}^{\mathrm{wit}})_O
 \middle\Vert (P_0^{\mathrm{wit}})_O\right)
 \le \frac{C_{\nu}a^2}{n},
\qquad
W_1\!\left(\nu_{P_{a/\sqrt n}^{\mathrm{wit}}},
             \nu_{P_0^{\mathrm{wit}}}\right)
=\frac a{\sqrt n}.                                                     \tag{110}
\]
Here the equality follows from \(\mathrm{prop:two\text{-}class\text{-}witness\text{-}valid}\): the second law is \(0.4\delta_{0.25-a/\sqrt n}+0.6\delta_{0.25+a/\sqrt n}\), whereas the first is \(\delta_{0.25}\), so every unit of mass moves distance \(a/\sqrt n\).

The same construction supplies a fixed orientation of the factorization-preserving labeled path \(P_{g,h}\).  For every \(0<g\le 1/4\) and \(|h|\le h_0\), it gives
\[
P_{g,h}\in\mathcal M(g),
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{g,h})_O\middle\Vert(P_{g,0})_O\right)
 \le C_p g^2h^2,
\qquad
\left\|p^{\uparrow}(P_{g,h})-p^{\uparrow}(P_{g,0})\right\|_1=2|h|.     \tag{111}
\]
The proof of that result derives these facts from the displayed Bernoulli factorization: on \(|h|\le h_0\), all Bernoulli parameters remain in \((0,1)\), every joint latent--arm probability is at least \(0.1\), and all three proxy signal matrices retain smallest singular value at least \(0.1\).  The two effects are \(0.25-g/2\) and \(0.25+g/2\), so their gap is exactly \(g>0\) and the ordered target in (111) is well defined.  Thus (110)--(111) use the same support, positivity, rank, and boundary conditions already verified in \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}local\text{-}lower\text{-}bounds}\), rather than adding a hypothesis.

Choose one numerical \(a>0\) such that
\[
a\le \min\{1/8,h_0\},
\qquad
\sqrt{C_{\nu}a^2/2}\le\frac12,
\qquad
\sqrt{C_pa^2/2}\le\frac12,                                             \tag{112}
\]
and fix the admissible positive orientation
\[
h(n,g):=a\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.                       \tag{113}
\]
Then \(|h(n,g)|\le a\le h_0\).  Moreover,
\[
ng^2h(n,g)^2
=a^2\min\{ng^2,1\}\le a^2.                                             \tag{114}
\]
Because Kullback--Leibler divergence tensorizes for the product sample laws defined in \(\mathrm{def:sample\text{-}experiment}\), (110), (111), and (114) imply
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(Q_{P_{a/\sqrt n}^{\mathrm{wit}}}^{(n)}
 \middle\Vert Q_{P_0^{\mathrm{wit}}}^{(n)}\right)\le C_{\nu}a^2,
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(Q_{P_{g,h(n,g)}}^{(n)}
 \middle\Vert Q_{P_{g,0}}^{(n)}\right)\le C_pa^2.                        \tag{115}
\]
The two-point inequality and its total-variation/Kullback--Leibler estimate were derived in equations (78)--(79) of \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}local\text{-}lower\text{-}bounds}\): for two sample laws \(Q_0,Q_1\), targets separated by \(\Delta\) under a metric loss \(\ell\), and any estimator \(\widehat\theta\),
\[
\max_{i\in\{0,1\}}E_{Q_i}\!\left[\ell(\widehat\theta,\theta_i)\right]
\ge \frac{\Delta}{2}\left\{1-\|Q_1-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right\},
\qquad
\|Q_1-Q_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\sqrt{\frac12D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_1\Vert Q_0)}.                         \tag{116}
\]
Combining (112), (115), and (116) shows that the braces in (116) are at least \(1/2\) for each displayed pair.

Now let \(\mathcal V_n^\nu\) contain only the required quotient pair; no containment of \(\mathcal L_n^\nu\) is used.  For every measurable quotient-law estimator \(\widetilde\nu_n\), pair containment and (110), (116) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal V_n^\nu}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)\right]
&\ge
\max_{P\in\{P_0^{\mathrm{wit}},P_{a/\sqrt n}^{\mathrm{wit}}\}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[W_1(\widetilde\nu_n,\nu_P)\right]\\
&\ge \frac{a}{4\sqrt n}.                                                  \tag{117}
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over the same set of measurable estimators proves the first assertion with \(c\le a/4\).

Likewise, if \(\mathcal V_{n,g}^p\) contains the labeled pair, then every measurable simplex-valued estimator \(\widetilde p_n\) satisfies, by (111), (113), and (116),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal V_{n,g}^p}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \|\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\|_1\right]
&\ge
\max_{P\in\{P_{g,0},P_{g,h(n,g)}\}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \|\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\|_1\right]\\
&\ge \frac{|h(n,g)|}{2}
=\frac a2\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.                    \tag{118}
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum proves the second assertion uniformly over \(0<g\le1/4\).  Choosing the single universal constant \(c:=a/4>0\) makes both (117) and (118) hold with the displayed \(c\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\), \(\mathrm{prop:two\text{-}class\text{-}witness\text{-}valid}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:vmw\text{-}model\text{-}scope}\) verify that these laws satisfy the two published proxy factorizations, causal consistency, armwise latent ignorability, strict latent positivity, and full proxy rank.  Hence both pairs lie in the published structural/finite-regularity VMW model.  The point \(P_0^{\mathrm{wit}}\) has coincident effects, and \(\mathrm{lem:vmw\text{-}separated\text{-}recovery\text{-}scope}\) records that VMW Theorem 7.2 assumes mutually distinct effects; therefore the collision point is outside that separated recovery regime.  The labeled pair has gap \(g>0\) and is separated.  Equations (117)--(118) use only containment of the two displayed pairs.  They produce no estimator or confidence procedure on either enlarged comparator class, so they transfer neither the gap-free modulus nor any estimator upper, confidence, cluster, or matching-minimax conclusion beyond \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathcal M(g)\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This records the maximal converse scope justified by exact witness-pair containment, without imposing containment of either full local neighborhood. \textit{Gap.} The subclass upper bounds do not transfer upward, but each minimax converse transfers to every published-VMW comparator class containing its exact two-point witness pair. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem tells analysts exactly which two laws a larger comparator class must contain for the converse, while preventing that transfer from being mistaken for an upper or confidence guarantee.

\begin{proposition}[prop:polynomial-net-law-estimator]\label{prop:polynomial-net-law-estimator}
Put \(m_\star:=\pi_0\), \(s_0:=\pi_0\sigma_0^2\),
\[
H_n:=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+\lceil\pi_0^{-1}\rceil+2k
+\lceil4\sqrt{d_xk}\rceil+\lceil2k/\sigma_0\rceil,
\qquad q_n:=H_n^{-1},
\]
and \(\ell_n:=\lceil m_\star H_n\rceil\).  For a rectangular matrix \(G=\sum_j\sigma_j u_jv_j^\top\), define
\[
G^\dagger_{\ge s_0/2}:=\sum_{\sigma_j\ge s_0/2}\sigma_j^{-1}v_ju_j^\top,
\qquad
\widehat D_n:=(\widehat M_{1,n})^\dagger_{\ge s_0/2}\widehat N_{1,n}
-(\widehat M_{0,n})^\dagger_{\ge s_0/2}\widehat N_{0,n}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal V_n\) consist of the polar factors \(G(G^\top G)^{-1/2}\) of all \(G\in(q_n\mathbb Z)^{d_x\times k}\) with \(|G_{ij}|\le1\) and \(\sigma_k(G)\ge1/2\).  Let \(\mathcal R_n\) consist of all \(R\in(q_n\mathbb Z)^{k\times k}\) with \(\sigma_k(R)\ge\sigma_0/2\) and \(\lVert R\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\sqrt{k}L\), and set
\[
\mathcal P_n:=\left\{H_n^{-1}(a_1,\ldots,a_k)^\top:
a_u\in\{\ell_n,\ldots,H_n\},\ \sum_{u=1}^ka_u=H_n\right\}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal T_n\) be the \(k\)-fold product of the clipped \(q_n\)-lattice in \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\).  For \(\vartheta=(V,R,p,\tau)\in\mathcal V_n\times\mathcal R_n\times\mathcal P_n\times\mathcal T_n\), define
\[
D_\vartheta:=VR^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\tau)RV^\top,
\quad m_\vartheta:=VR^\top p,
\quad b_\vartheta:=RV^\top e_1,
\quad \lambda_\vartheta:=\sum_{u=1}^kp_u\delta_{\tau_u},
\]
and
\[
J_n(\vartheta):=\lVert D_\vartheta-\widehat D_n\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
+\lVert m_\vartheta-\widehat m_{X,n}\rVert_2
+\lVert b_\vartheta-\mathbf1_k\rVert_2.
\]
Order the finite product lattice lexicographically, let \(\widehat\vartheta_n\) be its first minimizer of \(J_n\), and define the explicit estimator \(\widehat\lambda_n:=\lambda_{\widehat\vartheta_n}\).  Every distinct atom of \(\widehat\lambda_n\) has mass at least \(m_\star\).

The following displayed constants are valid and depend only on the fixed class parameters.  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_V&:=4\sqrt{d_xk},
&K_D&:=\frac{4\sqrt{k}LL_{\tau}}{\sigma_0},
&A_D&:=\frac{8}{3s_0}+\frac{32L}{s_0^2},\\
K_f&:=\frac{16\sqrt{d_x}\,kL^2}{\sigma_0^2},
&c_D&:=2K_Dc_V+\frac{4k\sqrt{k}LL_{\tau}}{\sigma_0^2}
+\frac{2\sqrt{k}L}{\sigma_0}+\frac{kL_{\tau}}{\sigma_0},\\
c_m&:=2\sqrt{k}Lc_V+k+kL,
&c_b&:=k+2\sqrt{k}Lc_V,
&c_{\mathrm{grid}}&:=c_D+c_m+c_b,\\
B_{\mathrm{lat}}&:=2(A_D+1)+c_{\mathrm{grid}},
&&
C_{\mathrm{lat}}&:=\max\!\left\{\frac{8L_{\tau}}{s_0},
(L_{\tau}+K_D+LK_f)B_{\mathrm{lat}}\right\},
\end{aligned}
\]
one has, for every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(n\ge1\), and every sample,
\[
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)
\le C_{\mathrm{lat}}\left\{d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))+n^{-1/2}\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The rule is a total Borel map on every empirical summary, including empty-arm summaries and summaries whose raw compressed operator has nonreal eigenvalues, to a positive real law with at most \(k\) atoms in \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\).  It uses no precomputed admissible-summary representative.  In the fixed-dimensional unit-cost exact-real model its candidate list has cardinality at most \(C n^{(d_xk+k^2+2k-1)/2}\), and exhaustive minimization has polynomial runtime in \(n\).  Consequently, for a finite \(C=C(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\) and every \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\!\left\{W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)>
C\sqrt{\frac{\log(C/\eta)}{n}}\right\}\le\eta.
\tag{12}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\).  By \(\mathrm{prop:observed\text{-}vmw\text{-}margin\text{-}inclusion}\), for each \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
M_t=A_tW_tB^\top,\qquad
W_t:=\operatorname{diag}\{P(U=u\mid T=t):1\le u\le k\},
\qquad \sigma_k(M_t)\ge s_0.                                      \tag{84}
\]
Reference-proxy separation factors \(Z\) from \((X,Y)\) conditional on \((T,U)\); target-proxy separation factors \(X\) from \((Y,T)\) conditional on \(U\); consistency replaces \(Y\) by \(Y(t)\) on \(\{T=t\}\); and armwise latent ignorability gives \(E_P[Y(t)\mid U=u,T=t]=\mu_{tu}\).  Therefore
\[
N_t=A_tW_t\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{t1},\ldots,\mu_{tk})B^\top.       \tag{85}
\]
Choose an orthonormal \(V\) spanning \(\operatorname{col}(B)\), put \(R:=B^\top V\), \(p:=(p_1,\ldots,p_k)^\top\), and \(\tau:=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_k)^\top\).  Full column rank gives \(B=VR^\top\).  The Penrose equations applied to (84)--(85) give
\[
D(P):=M_1^\dagger N_1-M_0^\dagger N_0
=VR^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\tau)RV^\top,
\qquad m_X(P)=VR^\top p.                                           \tag{86}
\]
The anchor assumption gives \(RV^\top e_1=\mathbf1_k\).  Latent-arm positivity, the derived outcome envelope, and boundedness of \(X\) give
\[
p_u=\sum_{t=0}^1P(U=u,T=t)\ge2\pi_0=2m_\star,
\quad |\tau_u|\le L_{\tau},
\quad \sigma_k(R)\ge\sigma_0,
\quad \lVert R\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt{k}L.                  \tag{87}
\]
No effect gap is used.

We construct a feasible grid approximation.  Since \(H_n\ge\pi_0^{-1}\), \(m_\star H_n\ge1\).  For \(x\ge1\), \(\lfloor2x\rfloor\ge\lceil x\rceil\); hence (87) implies \(\lfloor H_np_u\rfloor\ge\ell_n\).  Largest-remainder allocation gives integers \(a_u\in\{\lfloor H_np_u\rfloor,\lceil H_np_u\rceil\}\) summing to \(H_n\).  Consequently \(p_n^\circ:=H_n^{-1}(a_1,\ldots,a_k)^\top\in\mathcal P_n\) and
\[
\lVert p_n^\circ-p\rVert_2\le\sqrt{k}q_n.                           \tag{88}
\]
The lattice is nonempty because
\(k\ell_n\le k(m_\star H_n+1)\le H_n/2+k\le H_n\).

Coordinatewise clipped rounding gives \(G_n\) with \(\lVert G_n-V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt{d_xk}q_n\le1/4\).  Thus \(\sigma_k(G_n)\ge3/4\).  If \(V_n^\circ\) is its polar factor, singular-value decomposition gives
\[
\lVert V_n^\circ-V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le c_Vq_n.                  \tag{89}
\]
Rounding \(R\) coordinatewise gives \(R_n^\circ\) with \(\lVert R_n^\circ-R\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le kq_n\).  The definition of \(H_n\) makes this at most \(\sigma_0/2\), so \(\sigma_k(R_n^\circ)\ge\sigma_0/2\), and \(\lVert R_n^\circ\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\sqrt{k}L\).  Clipped rounding gives \(\lVert\tau_n^\circ-\tau\rVert_\infty\le q_n\).  Thus \(\vartheta_n^\circ=(V_n^\circ,R_n^\circ,p_n^\circ,\tau_n^\circ)\) is feasible.

The inverse identity
\[
(R_n^\circ)^{-1}-R^{-1}=(R_n^\circ)^{-1}(R-R_n^\circ)R^{-1}          \tag{90}
\]
and (87)--(89), adding and subtracting one factor at a time, yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert D_{\vartheta_n^\circ}-D(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\le c_Dq_n,\\
\lVert m_{\vartheta_n^\circ}-m_X(P)\rVert_2&\le c_mq_n,\\
\lVert b_{\vartheta_n^\circ}-\mathbf1_k\rVert_2&\le c_bq_n.
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{91}
\]
Indeed, the two basis terms in the first line total at most \(2K_Dc_Vq_n\); the inverse, effect, and direct \(R\)-perturbations are bounded respectively by the remaining three terms defining \(c_D\).  The definitions of \(c_m\) and \(c_b\) similarly bound the factorwise expansions of \(VR^\top p\) and \(RV^\top e_1\).

Put \(e:=d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))\) and first suppose \(e<s_0/4\).  Singular-value variation gives
\[
|\sigma_j(\widehat M_{t,n})-\sigma_j(M_t)|\le e.                     \tag{92}
\]
Because \(M_t\) has rank exactly \(k\), precisely the empirical signal singular values exceed \(s_0/2\).  If \(\widetilde M_{t,n}\) is that rank-\(k\) truncation, then
\[
(\widehat M_{t,n})^\dagger_{\ge s_0/2}=\widetilde M_{t,n}^\dagger,
\quad \sigma_k(\widetilde M_{t,n})\ge3s_0/4,
\quad \lVert\widetilde M_{t,n}-M_t\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2e.       \tag{93}
\]
For equal-rank matrices \(A,B\), the Penrose equations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
B^\dagger-A^\dagger={}&-B^\dagger(B-A)A^\dagger
+B^\dagger(B^\dagger)^\top(B-A)^\top(I-AA^\dagger)\\
&+(I-B^\dagger B)(B-A)^\top(A^\dagger)^\top A^\dagger.
\end{aligned}
\]
The four Penrose products are orthogonal projections, so
\[
\lVert A^\dagger-B^\dagger\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le3\max\{\lVert A^\dagger\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2,
\lVert B^\dagger\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2\}
\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.                                    \tag{94}
\]
Applying (94) to (93), using \(\lVert N_t(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L\), and summing over arms gives
\[
\lVert\widehat D_n-D(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le A_De,
\qquad
\lVert\widehat m_{X,n}-m_X(P)\rVert_2\le e.                         \tag{95}
\]
This uses only singular values and remains defined when \(\widehat D_n\) has nonreal eigenvalues.

By (91), (95), and first-minimizer optimality,
\[
J_n(\widehat\vartheta_n)\le(A_D+1)e+c_{\mathrm{grid}}q_n.             \tag{96}
\]
The triangle inequality and the definition of \(B_{\mathrm{lat}}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lVert D_{\widehat\vartheta_n}-D(P)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}&\le B_{\mathrm{lat}}(e+q_n),\\
\lVert m_{\widehat\vartheta_n}-m_X(P)\rVert_2&\le B_{\mathrm{lat}}(e+q_n),\\
\lVert b_{\widehat\vartheta_n}-\mathbf1_k\rVert_2&\le B_{\mathrm{lat}}(e+q_n).
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{97}
\]

Let \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be one-Lipschitz with \(f(0)=0\), and put \(\kappa_0:=4\sqrt{k}L/\sigma_0\).  Each structured ambient operator is diagonalizable over \(\mathbb R\), acts as zero on the orthogonal complement of its signal space, and has a full diagonalizer of condition number at most \(\kappa_0\).  If \(A=S\Lambda S^{-1}\) and \(B=T\Gamma T^{-1}\) are two such operators, then entrywise
\[
\{f(\Lambda)S^{-1}T-S^{-1}Tf(\Gamma)\}_{ij}
=d_{ij}\{\Lambda S^{-1}T-S^{-1}T\Gamma\}_{ij},
\qquad |d_{ij}|\le1,                                               \tag{98}
\]
with \(d_{ij}=0\) when the two eigenvalues coincide.  The Frobenius norm in fixed dimension therefore gives
\[
\lVert f(A)-f(B)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\sqrt{d_x}\,\kappa_0^2\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
=K_f\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.                                \tag{99}
\]
For every feasible \(\vartheta\), direct multiplication gives
\[
m_\vartheta^\top f(D_\vartheta)e_1
=\sum_{u=1}^kp_uf(\tau_u)(b_\vartheta)_u,                            \tag{100}
\]
and hence
\[
\left|\int f\,d\lambda_\vartheta-m_\vartheta^\top f(D_\vartheta)e_1\right|
\le L_{\tau}\lVert b_\vartheta-\mathbf1_k\rVert_2.                \tag{101}
\]
For the population tuple the right side is zero.  Equations (97)--(101), \(\lVert m_X(P)\rVert_2\le L\), and \(\lVert f(D_{\widehat\vartheta_n})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le K_D\) imply
\[
\left|\int f\,d\widehat\lambda_n-\int f\,d\nu_P\right|
\le(L_{\tau}+K_D+LK_f)B_{\mathrm{lat}}(e+q_n).                       \tag{102}
\]
For finite laws on the real line, summation by parts over the ordered union of their supports identifies the supremum of the left side over such \(f\) with the monotone-coupling cost \(W_1\).  Thus (11) holds when \(e<s_0/4\).

If \(e\ge s_0/4\), both laws are supported on \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\), so
\[
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)\le2L_{\tau}
\le\frac{8L_{\tau}}{s_0}e.                                         \tag{103}
\]
Since \(q_n\le n^{-1/2}\), the displayed \(C_{\mathrm{lat}}\) combines (102)--(103) and proves (11) for every sample.

Every coordinate of each \(p\in\mathcal P_n\) is at least \(m_\star\), so aggregating repeated effects preserves the atom floor.  The four grids are finite and nonempty: canonical coordinate columns furnish \(\mathcal V_n\), a rounded scalar multiple of \(I_k\) furnishes \(\mathcal R_n\), \(k\ell_n\le H_n\) handles \(\mathcal P_n\), and the clipped effect grid contains zero.  Thresholded singular-value calculus is Borel, polar factors are continuous on the full-rank grid, and first-minimizer tie breaking over a finite ordered list is Borel.  Hence \(\widehat\lambda_n\) is total and Borel, including on empty arms and threshold ties.

The four grid cardinalities are respectively \(O(H_n^{d_xk})\), \(O(H_n^{k^2})\), \(O(H_n^{k-1})\), and \(O(H_n^k)\).  Since the class parameters and dimensions are fixed and \(H_n\le C\sqrt n\), their product is at most \(C n^{(d_xk+k^2+2k-1)/2}\); all matrix operations have fixed size in the unit-cost exact-real model.  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\text{-}summary\text{-}concentration}\) applied to (11), with the additive \(n^{-1/2}\) absorbed because \(\eta<1/2\), proves (12).
\end{proof}
