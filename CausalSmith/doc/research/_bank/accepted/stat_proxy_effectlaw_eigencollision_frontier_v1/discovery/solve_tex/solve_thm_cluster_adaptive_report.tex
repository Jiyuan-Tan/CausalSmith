%%% FIELD proof-borel-nearest-point-selector
Let
\[
\mathsf E:=\bigl(\mathbb R^{d_z\times d_x}\bigr)^4\times\mathbb R^{d_x},
\qquad D:=4d_zd_x+d_x,
\]
be the ambient summary space from \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}closure}\), equipped with its coordinate Borel structure.  Because \(d_x\) and \(d_z\) are fixed and finite, choose a coordinate linear homeomorphism
\[
\Phi:\mathsf E\longrightarrow \operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D).
\tag{1}
\]
Both \(\Phi\) and \(\Phi^{-1}\) are continuous and hence Borel measurable.  Put
\[
K':=\Phi(\mathcal K).
\tag{2}
\]
The hypotheses that \(\mathcal K\) is nonempty and compact, together with continuity of \(\Phi\), imply that \(K'\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(\operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D)\).

Define the objective
\[
f:\mathsf E\times\operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D)
\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\qquad
f(s,y):=d_S\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(y),s\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
The metric \(d_S\) is continuous by hypothesis, and \(\Phi^{-1}\) is continuous by (1).  Thus \(f\) is jointly continuous, hence jointly Borel measurable, and for every \(s\in\mathsf E\) the section \(y\mapsto f(s,y)\) is continuous on \(K'\).  The parameter space \(\mathsf E\) is finite-dimensional Euclidean and therefore standard Borel.  All hypotheses of the zero-sorry in-repository theorem
\texttt{Causalean.Mathlib.MeasureTheory.borelMeasurable\_compact\_argmin\_selector}
are consequently satisfied by \(K'\) and \(f\).  That theorem supplies a Borel map
\[
\pi':\mathsf E\longrightarrow\operatorname{EuclideanSpace}(\mathbb R,\operatorname{Fin}D)
\tag{4}
\]
such that, for every \(s\in\mathsf E\),
\[
\pi'(s)\in K',
\qquad
f\bigl(s,\pi'(s)\bigr)\le f(s,y)
\quad\text{for every }y\in K'.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the general exact-argmin result underlying its nearest-point specialization
\texttt{Causalean.Mathlib.MeasureTheory.borelMeasurable\_nearestPoint\_selector}.

Set
\[
\Pi(s):=\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\pi'(s)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) defines a Borel map because it is a composition of the Borel maps in (1) and (4).  By (2) and the first part of (5), \(\Pi(s)\in\mathcal K\).  For arbitrary \(q\in\mathcal K\), take \(y=\Phi(q)\in K'\) in the second part of (5).  Using (3) and (6) gives
\[
d_S\bigl(\Pi(s),s\bigr)
=f\bigl(s,\pi'(s)\bigr)
\le f\bigl(s,\Phi(q)\bigr)
=d_S(q,s).
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(q\in\mathcal K\) was arbitrary, (7) proves
\[
\Pi(s)\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{q\in\mathcal K}d_S(q,s)
\quad\text{for every }s\in\mathsf E.
\]
Thus \(\Pi\) is the required Borel measurable selector.
