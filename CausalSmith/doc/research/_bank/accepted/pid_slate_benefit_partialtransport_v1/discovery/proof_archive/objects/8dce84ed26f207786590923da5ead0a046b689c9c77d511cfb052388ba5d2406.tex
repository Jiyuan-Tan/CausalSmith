For each cell, compute the prefix sums of \(\ell\) and \(h\), the complementary tails, \(q_0,q_1,m\), and \(\Delta q\) in one forward and one backward scan.  A scan over the \(K\) thresholds then evaluates the branch-free maximum for \(B_L\) and the minimum for \(B_U\).  This uses \(O(K)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons.

For a sparse upper-attaining coupling, compute a maximum flow on the Ferrers graph \(i<j\).  Process columns in increasing order and supply each from the largest-index residual row satisfying \(i<j\), keeping eligible residual rows on a stack.  Each row is pushed once, each exhausted row is popped once, and each assignment exhausts a row or a column.  An exchange proves maximality: if a more restrictive, larger row is sent to a later column while a less restrictive row supplies an earlier column, swapping the smaller assigned mass preserves feasibility and does not decrease benefit flow.  Iterating leaves the stated greedy order.  Thus the scan uses \(O(K)\) operations and emits at most \(2K-1\) positive assignments.

For a maximum nonbenefit subflow on \(j\le i\), reverse the order: process columns from high to low and supply them from the smallest-index eligible residual row.  The identical nested-neighborhood exchange argument proves maximality, with the same linear bound.  Extend either maximum subflow to exact mass \(m\) by a two-pointer complete-bipartite scan on its residual row and column capacities.  Each completion assignment again exhausts a row or a column, so it costs \(O(K)\) and has \(O(K)\) support.  If the maximum benefit subflow has mass below \(m\), no residual benefit edge exists; hence its completion attains \(B_U\).  If the maximum nonbenefit subflow has mass below \(m\), no residual nonbenefit edge exists; hence its completion leaves exactly \(B_L\) on benefit arcs.  These are the cut equalities established in the sharp-threshold theorem.

Therefore endpoints and sparse attaining couplings cost \(O(K)\) per cell and \(O(|\mathcal X|K)\) overall.  Initializing or writing every entry of dense \(K\times K\) arrays itself costs \(O(K^2)\) per cell and dominates the scans, giving \(O(|\mathcal X|K^2)\).  No loop count depends on any capacity magnitude, so both bounds are strongly polynomial in the stated arithmetic/comparison model.
