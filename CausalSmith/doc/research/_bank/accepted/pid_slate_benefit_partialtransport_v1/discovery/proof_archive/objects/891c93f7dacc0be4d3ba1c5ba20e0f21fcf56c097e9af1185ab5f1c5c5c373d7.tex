Let \(v\) be the finite vector of probabilities of all values of the observed element \(O\), and let \(\widehat v_n\) be its empirical vector.  Under independent sampling, the cited finite multinomial central limit theorem gives
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat v_n-v)\ \rightsquigarrow\ \mathbb Z_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every fixed cell with \(p_x>0\), instrument overlap bounds both \(P(X=x,Z=z)\) away from zero at this fixed law.  Therefore the maps from \(v\) to the conditional observed-cell probabilities, and thence to the raw contrasts \((\ell,h)\), are continuously differentiable quotient maps.

The remaining maps are finite compositions of elementary Hadamard directionally differentiable maps.  Coordinatewise projection has derivative
\[
[\Pi_{\mathfrak C}'(g)]_k=
\begin{cases}
g_k,&c_k>0,\\
\max\{g_k,0\},&c_k=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The direction margin makes the sign branch locally constant on every cell in \(\mathcal X_+(P)\).  If lower-cut affine forms are denoted by \(L_{x,t}\), including the zero form, and upper-cut affine forms by \(U_{x,t}\), including the mass cap, direct expansion of a finite maximum and minimum gives
\[
\left(\max_tL_{x,t}\right)'(g)=\max_{t\in\mathcal A_L(x)}L_{x,t}'(g),
\qquad
\left(\min_tU_{x,t}\right)'(g)=\min_{t\in\mathcal A_U(x)}U_{x,t}'(g).
\tag{3}
\]
Because the sign-separated branch has \(q_0(x)\ne q_1(x)\), the derivative of \(m(x)\) is the derivative of the uniquely smaller selected mass.  Finally, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\), writing \(N_e=\sum_xp_xB_e(x)\), the common-denominator quotient derivative is
\[
\left(\frac{N_e}{M}\right)'(g)
=\frac{N_e'(g)M-N_eM'(g)}{M^2},
\tag{4}
\]
which is well defined because \(M>0\).

It remains to justify the reduced fixed support used in (3)--(4).  Since \(\mathcal X\) is finite, the positive numbers \(r_x=p_xm(x)\) over \(\mathcal X_+(P)\) have a positive minimum.  Consistency and \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) therefore imply that every such cell is retained with probability tending to one.  If \(r_x=0\) and \(p_x=0\), then \(\widehat p_x=0\) almost surely.  If \(p_x>0\) and \(m(x)=0\), one of the two nonnegative capacity vectors has every population coordinate equal to zero.  The smooth first stage followed by the positive-part projection gives \(\widehat m(x)=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), so
\[
\widehat r_x=O_P(n^{-1/2})=o_P(\eta_n)
\]
by \(\sqrt n\eta_n\to\infty\).  A union bound over the finite cell set proves
\[
P\{\{x:\widehat r_x>\eta_n\}=\mathcal X_+(P)\}\to1.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) show that the reduced-support endpoint map is Hadamard directionally differentiable.  Applying the cited directional delta method to (1), and using (5) to replace the random screened map by the reduced fixed-support map with probability tending to one, proves the asserted joint limit.  The same argument without scaling proves \(\widehat\Psi_n\to_P\Psi(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Equations (2), (3), and (4) identify, respectively, the projection, active-cut, and quotient stages of the limit claimed in the theorem.
