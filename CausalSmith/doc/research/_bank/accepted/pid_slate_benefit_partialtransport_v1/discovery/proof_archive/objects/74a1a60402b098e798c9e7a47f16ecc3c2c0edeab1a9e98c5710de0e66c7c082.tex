\noindent\textit{Proof.}
By the definition of \(\mathcal E\), every empirical numerator and
denominator used by the plug-in estimator is the empirical probability
of an event in this fixed finite collection.  For \(E\in\mathcal E\),
write \(I_{r,E}=\mathbf 1\{O_r\in E\}\).  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}sampling}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \left\{\widehat P_n(E)-P_{\mathrm{obs}}(E)\right\}^{2}
 \right]
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(I_{1,E})}{n}
\le \frac{1}{4n},
\]
because a Bernoulli variance is \(u(1-u)\le1/4\).  The pointwise
inequality \(\mathbf 1\{|W|>v\}\le W^2/v^2\), followed by
\(\mathbf 1\{\bigcup_E F_E\}\le\sum_E\mathbf 1\{F_E\}\), therefore
yields
\[
P(\delta_n>b_{n,\alpha})
\le \sum_{E\in\mathcal E}
 \frac{\mathbb E_P[(\widehat P_n(E)-P_{\mathrm{obs}}(E))^2]}
      {b_{n,\alpha}^2}
\le\frac{|\mathcal E|}{4n b_{n,\alpha}^2}=\alpha.
\]
Thus, uniformly for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\),
\[
P\{\delta_n\le b_{n,\alpha}\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a direct finite second-moment and union argument; it invokes no
asymptotic approximation.

We next establish a deterministic stability bound on the event in
(1).  Put
\[
L_i(x)=p_x\ell_i(x),\qquad H_j(x)=p_xh_j(x),\qquad
\widehat L_i(x)=\widehat p_x\widehat\ell_i(x),\qquad
\widehat H_j(x)=\widehat p_x\widehat h_j(x).
\]
Fix a cell with \(p_x>0\) and
\(p_x\ge4\delta_n/\varepsilon_Z\).  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}instrument\text{-}overlap}\) implies that each
population denominator
\(B_{xz}=P_{\mathrm{obs}}(X=x,Z=z)\) satisfies
\(B_{xz}\ge\varepsilon_Zp_x\ge4\delta_n\).  Hence its empirical
counterpart obeys \(\widehat B_{xz}\ge B_{xz}-\delta_n\ge B_{xz}/2>0\).
For any corresponding numerator \(0\le A\le B_{xz}\), with empirical
version \(\widehat A\), the identity
\[
\frac{\widehat A}{\widehat B_{xz}}-\frac{A}{B_{xz}}
=\frac{\widehat A-A}{\widehat B_{xz}}
+\frac{A(B_{xz}-\widehat B_{xz})}
        {B_{xz}\widehat B_{xz}}
\]
and the definition of \(\delta_n\) give
\[
\left|\frac{\widehat A}{\widehat B_{xz}}-
             \frac{A}{B_{xz}}\right|
\le\frac{4\delta_n}{B_{xz}}
\le\frac{4\delta_n}{\varepsilon_Zp_x}.
\tag{2}
\]
Multiplying by \(p_x\), replacing \(p_x\) by \(\widehat p_x\), and
using \(|\widehat p_x-p_x|\le\delta_n\) bounds the error for one
weighted conditional probability by \(4\delta_n/\varepsilon_Z+\delta_n\).
Taking the difference of the two instrument arms and using
\(0<\varepsilon_Z<1/2\) gives the deliberately conservative bound
\[
\max_{i,j}\left\{|\widehat L_i(x)-L_i(x)|,
                    |\widehat H_j(x)-H_j(x)|\right\}
\le\frac{16\delta_n}{\varepsilon_Z^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Here the coordinatewise positive-part projection does not enlarge the
error: Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}capacity\text{-}identification}\),
which is in the dependency closure of Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}exact\text{-}mass\text{-}threshold\text{-}interval}\),
gives nonnegative population capacities, and \(u\mapsto\max\{u,0\}\)
is one-Lipschitz.

It remains to verify (3) when the cell is small.  If
\(0<p_x<4\delta_n/\varepsilon_Z\), then each population unconditional
capacity is at most \(p_x\), whereas each empirical unconditional
projected capacity is at most \(\widehat p_x\le p_x+\delta_n\).
Thus its error is at most \(2p_x+\delta_n\), which is bounded by the
right-hand side of (3).  If \(p_x=0\), independent sampling gives
\(\widehat p_x=0\) almost surely, so both sides are zero.  Consequently,
(3) holds simultaneously in every cell, without a lower bound on
\(p_x\).

The threshold-cut maps in \(\mathrm{def{:}threshold\text{-}cuts}\) are
positively homogeneous.  Therefore \(p_xB_e(x)\), for \(e\in\{L,U\}\),
is the same cut map evaluated at \((L(x),H(x))\), with zero contribution
when \(p_x=0\).  If every coordinate of these unconditional capacities
changes by at most \(e_0\), every prefix and tail changes by at most
\(Ke_0\), each selected-complier total by at most \(Ke_0\), and
\(\Delta q\) by at most \(2Ke_0\).  Since for finite collections
\[
|\max_r u_r-\max_r v_r|\le\max_r|u_r-v_r|,
\qquad
|\min_r u_r-\min_r v_r|\le\max_r|u_r-v_r|,
\]
the lower cut, upper cut, and survivor-mass maps each change by at most
\(4Ke_0\).  Applying (3), summing over \(x\), and writing
\(N_e=\sum_xp_xB_e(x)\) gives the unscreened bounds
\[
\left|\sum_x\widehat p_x\widehat B_e(x)-N_e\right|
\le\frac{64K|\mathcal X|}{\varepsilon_Z^2}\delta_n,
\qquad
\left|\sum_x\widehat p_x\widehat m(x)-M\right|
\le\frac{64K|\mathcal X|}{\varepsilon_Z^2}\delta_n.
\tag{4}
\]
This finite min--max Lipschitz calculation makes no choice of a unique
active threshold or selection direction, so (4) covers simultaneous cut
ties and positive-survivor zero-gap cells.

For arbitrary nonnegative capacities, the formulas in
\(\mathrm{def{:}threshold\text{-}cuts}\) satisfy
\(0\le B_e(x)\le m(x)\).  Indeed, the upper inequality is explicit for
\(B_U\); for \(B_L\), when \(q_1\ge q_0\) its inner expression is at
most \(q_0=m\), while when \(q_1<q_0\) that expression equals
\(h_{>t}-\ell_{>t}\le q_1=m\).  The same algebra applies to the
projected empirical capacities.  A screened-out cell satisfies
\(\widehat p_x\widehat m(x)=\widehat r_x\le\eta_n\), so screening changes
each empirical numerator and denominator by at most
\(|\mathcal X|\eta_n\).  Combining this fact with (4) yields
\[
|\widehat N_{e,n}-N_e|\le R_n,
\qquad
|\widehat M_n-M|\le R_n,
\qquad
R_n=\frac{64K|\mathcal X|}{\varepsilon_Z^2}\delta_n
     +|\mathcal X|\eta_n.
\tag{5}
\]
This step permits survivor masses to approach zero and does not require
uniform recovery of cell support.

On \(\{\delta_n\le b_{n,\alpha}\}\), equation (5) gives
\(R_n\le A_{n,\alpha}\).  If \(g_{n,\alpha}<1\), its definition implies
\(A_{n,\alpha}<m_{\star}/4\).  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}aggregate\text{-}survivor\text{-}bound}\)
then gives
\[
\widehat M_n\ge M-R_n
\ge m_{\star}-A_{n,\alpha}>\frac{3m_{\star}}4>0.
\]
Thus the fallback estimator is not used.  Since
\(0\le N_e\le M\), quotient algebra and (5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\frac{\widehat N_{e,n}}{\widehat M_n}-\frac{N_e}{M}\right|
&\le\frac{|\widehat N_{e,n}-N_e|}{\widehat M_n}
 +\frac{N_e|\widehat M_n-M|}{M\widehat M_n}\\
&\le\frac{2R_n}{\widehat M_n}
\le\frac{8A_{n,\alpha}}{3m_{\star}}
\le\frac{4A_{n,\alpha}}{m_{\star}}
=g_{n,\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Taking \(e=L,U\) proves the asserted sup-norm inequality.  If instead
\(g_{n,\alpha}=1\), the same inequalities
\(0\le\widehat N_{e,n}\le\widehat M_n\), together with the fallback
\((0,1)\), show that both coordinates of \(\widehat\Psi_n\) lie in
\([0,1]\).  Hence the clipped guarded set is exactly \([0,1]\).

In either case, on the event in (1), Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}exact\text{-}mass\text{-}threshold\text{-}interval}\)
gives
\(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=[\theta_L,\theta_U]\subseteq
\mathcal C_{1-\alpha,n}\).  Therefore (1) implies, for every \(n\),
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
P\{\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal C_{1-\alpha,n}\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\]

Finally, with fixed \(\alpha\) and fixed finite \(\mathcal E\),
\(b_{n,\alpha}=O(n^{-1/2})\).  The definitions then give
\(A_{n,\alpha}=O(n^{-1/2}+\eta_n)\) and, because the second argument of
the minimum defining \(g_{n,\alpha}\) tends to zero,
\[
g_{n,\alpha}=O(n^{-1/2}+\eta_n).
\]
If \(\eta_n=n^{-1/2}L_n\), \(L_n\to\infty\), and
\(L_n=o(n^{1/2})\), then \(\eta_n\downarrow0\),
\(\sqrt n\eta_n=L_n\to\infty\), and the guard padding is
\(O(n^{-1/2}L_n)\).  The localization tolerance \(a_n\) and the
multiplier process never enter the coverage argument, so the result does
not claim calibrated face-aware multiplier inference. \(\square\)
