Fix \(x\) and suppress it from the notation.  We first establish the required finite partial-transport identity directly.  For nonnegative row capacities \(a_i\), column capacities \(c_j\), and a bipartite edge set \(E\), let \(F(E;a,c)\) be the largest mass of a nonnegative flow supported on \(E\), with row sums at most \(a_i\) and column sums at most \(c_j\).  The augmenting-path proof of the finite max-flow identity gives
\[
F(E;a,c)=\min_{A\subseteq\mathcal Y}\left\{a(\mathcal Y\setminus A)+c(N_E(A))\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, the upper inequality follows by splitting every unit of flow according as its row is outside \(A\), or its row is in \(A\) and hence its column lies in \(N_E(A)\).  For the reverse inequality, start from the zero flow and augment along a source--row--column--sink path whenever one exists, using reverse arcs for previously assigned mass.  At termination, let \(A\) be the rows reachable from the source and let \(N_E(A)\) be the reachable columns.  Every source arc into \(\mathcal Y\setminus A\) and every sink arc out of \(N_E(A)\) is saturated, and flow conservation gives equality with the displayed cut.  Because the graph is finite, the same conclusion follows without a termination convention by choosing a flow of maximal value on the compact capacity box and applying the preceding residual-path argument; a residual augmenting path would contradict maximality.  This derives (1), rather than assuming a transport theorem.

For the strict-benefit graph \(E_< =\{(i,j):i<j\}\), if \(A\ne\varnothing\) and \(t=\min A\), then \(N_{E_<}(A)=\{j:j>t\}\).  For fixed \(t\), the cut is minimized by \(A=\{i:i\ge t\}\).  The empty set supplies the total-row cut.  Hence
\[
F(E_<;a,c)=\min\left\{\sum_i a_i,\ \min_{t\in\mathcal Y}left(a_{<t}+c_{>t}\right)\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For the nonbenefit graph \(E_{\ge}=\{(i,j):j\le i\}\), the analogous argument uses \(t=\max A\) and gives
\[
F(E_{\ge};a,c)=\min\left\{\sum_i a_i,\ \min_{t\in\mathcal Y}left(a_{>t}+c_{\le t}\right)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Suppose first that \(\Delta q>0\).  Then \(q_1>q_0=m\), and \(\Gamma_x=\Gamma_x^+\) has exact row margin \(\ell\) and column capacities \(h\).  Applying (2) to the benefit arcs shows that no coupling can place more than
\[
\min\left\{m,\min_t(\ell_{<t}+h_{>t})\right\}=B_U(x)
\]
on those arcs.  A maximizing benefit subflow extends to a member of \(\Gamma_x^+\): greedily send every residual row mass through the complete bipartite graph into residual column capacity, whose total is \(q_1-F\ge m-F\).  If that completion added any benefit flow, the original benefit subflow was not maximal.  Thus the displayed upper value is attained.

To minimize benefit, maximize flow on \(E_{\ge}\).  By (3), its value is
\[
F_{\ge}=\min\left\{m,\min_t(\ell_{>t}+h_{\le t})\right\}.
\]
The same complete-graph completion argument shows that the remaining mass is entirely beneficial.  Therefore
\[
\inf_{\Gamma_x^+}b_x
=m-F_{\ge}
=\max\left\{0,\max_t(\ell_{\le t}-h_{\le t})\right\}=B_L(x).
\tag{4}
\]

If \(\Delta q<0\), then \(q_0>q_1=m\), columns are exact and rows are capacitated.  Interchanging the roles of required and capacitated margins in the same two subflow completions gives from (2)
\[
\sup_{\Gamma_x^-}b_x
=\min\left\{m,\min_t(\ell_{<t}+h_{>t})\right\}=B_U(x),
\tag{5}
\]
and from (3)
\[
\inf_{\Gamma_x^-}b_x
=m-\min_t(\ell_{>t}+h_{\le t})
=\max\left\{0,\max_t(h_{>t}-\ell_{>t})\right\}=B_L(x).
\tag{6}
\]
When \(\Delta q=0\), the tie-face proposition identifies all three polytopes and makes (4) and (6) identical, while (5) is unchanged.  This proves the cut formulas, including atoms and simultaneous cut ties.

We next prove exact projection and sharpness, keeping the causal target separate from the observable cut functional.  Let \(\bar P\in\mathcal M\) be any compatible full law, whose existence is assumed in the theorem.  The capacity-identification proposition and weak selection monotonicity show that every survivor coupling produced by \(\bar P\) lies in the indicated \(\Gamma_x\).  Conversely, take any \(\gamma_x\in\Gamma_x\).  In the increasing branch, give survivor compliers mass \(\gamma_{ij,x}\) at \((Y_0,Y_1)=(i,j)\), give the \((S_0,S_1)=(0,1)\) complier stratum outcome-
\(Y_1=j\) mass
\[
h_j-\sum_i\gamma_{ij,x}\ge0,
\]
and give the remaining complier mass selection type \((0,0)\).  The residual masses total \(q_1-q_0\), and the final mass is nonnegative because \(q_1\le P(C\mid X=x)\).  In the decreasing branch, use survivor mass \(\gamma\), allocate outcome-
\(Y_0=i\) mass \(\ell_i-\sum_j\gamma_{ij,x}\) to type \((1,0)\), and put the remainder in type \((0,0)\).  On the tie face only survivor and never-selected compliers are required.  Assign arbitrary potential outcomes whenever the corresponding selection indicator is zero.  Retain from \(\bar P\) the cell probability, complier probability, all never-taker and always-taker components, and the conditional instrument law.  This construction preserves every observed complier selected-outcome submargin and hence, together with the unchanged noncompliers, induces exactly \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  It also preserves conditional instrument independence, consistency, exclusion, overlap, no defiers, and the appropriate weak selection direction.  Thus the projected feasible set is exactly \(\Gamma_x\).

The choices can be made independently over the finite set \(\mathcal X\).  For any compatible law, the causal numerator and denominator are
\[
P(Y_1>Y_0,S_0=S_1=1,C)=\sum_xp_xb_x(\gamma_x),\qquad
P(S_0=S_1=1,C)=\sum_xp_xm(x)=M.
\]
The positive-aggregate-survivor assumption permits division by \(M\).  The finite product \(\prod_x\Gamma_x\) is convex and compact, and the numerator is linear; consequently its image is the entire interval between the sums of the cellwise minima and maxima.  Cells with \(m(x)=0\) contain only the zero coupling and contribute zero.  Dividing by \(M\) proves the asserted equality for \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), not merely an outer bound.
