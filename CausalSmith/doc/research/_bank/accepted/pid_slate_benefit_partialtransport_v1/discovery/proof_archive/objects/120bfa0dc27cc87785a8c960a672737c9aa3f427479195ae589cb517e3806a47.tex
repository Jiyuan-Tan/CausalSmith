Fix a supported cell and suppress \(x\).  Put \(a_i=\ell_i\), \(c_j=h_j\), and \(m=\min\{\sum_i a_i,\sum_jc_j\}\).  We first derive the finite flow identity used below.  For a bipartite edge set \(E\), let \(F(E;a,c)\) be the greatest mass of a nonnegative flow supported on \(E\), with row capacities \(a\) and column capacities \(c\).  For every row set \(A\), splitting flow according as its row lies outside \(A\) or inside \(A\) gives
\[
F(E;a,c)\le a(\mathcal Y\setminus A)+c(N_E(A)).
\tag{1}
\]
To prove equality for some cut, choose a maximum flow, which exists because the feasible capacity box is compact.  In its residual network let \(A\) be the rows reachable from the source and let \(N_E(A)\) be the reachable columns.  No augmenting path exists.  Consequently every source arc entering \(\mathcal Y\setminus A\) and every sink arc leaving \(N_E(A)\) is saturated; otherwise the corresponding endpoint would be reachable.  Flow conservation, including reverse residual arcs, then makes (1) an equality.  Thus
\[
F(E;a,c)=\min_{A\subseteq\mathcal Y}\{a(\mathcal Y\setminus A)+c(N_E(A))\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For benefit edges \(E_< =\{(i,j):i<j\}\), a nonempty \(A\) with \(t=\min A\) has \(N_{E_<}(A)=\{j:j>t\}\); at fixed \(t\), the cut is minimized by \(A=\{i:i\ge t\}\).  The empty row set and the analogous all-column cut supply the two total-capacity cuts.  Hence
\[
F(E_<;a,c)=\min\left\{m,\ \min_{t\in\mathcal Y}(a_{<t}+c_{>t})\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For nonbenefit edges \(E_{\ge}=\{(i,j):j\le i\}\), the same argument with \(t=\max A\) gives
\[
F(E_{\ge};a,c)=\min\left\{m,\ \min_{t\in\mathcal Y}(a_{>t}+c_{\le t})\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Any subflow of mass \(f\le m\) can be extended to total mass \(m\) on the complete bipartite graph: after subtracting the subflow, both the total residual row capacity and the total residual column capacity are at least \(m-f\), and a two-pointer greedy allocation sends precisely that amount.  Apply this to a maximum benefit subflow.  If its mass is below \(m\), no residual benefit edge can carry positive flow, since that would augment the subflow.  Therefore its completion has the same benefit mass, and (3) proves
\[
\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_x^{\ast}}b_x(\gamma)
=\min\left\{m,\min_t(\ell_{<t}+h_{>t})\right\}=B_U(x).
\tag{5}
\]
Next complete a maximum nonbenefit subflow.  If its mass is below \(m\), every added unit is beneficial by the same residual-edge argument.  Thus (4) yields
\[
\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_x^{\ast}}b_x(\gamma)
=m-F(E_{\ge};\ell,h)
=\max\left\{0,\max_t(m-\ell_{>t}-h_{\le t})\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(q_0=\ell_{\le t}+\ell_{>t}\) and \(m-q_0=\min\{q_1-q_0,0\}=\min\{\Delta q,0\}\),
\[
m-\ell_{>t}-h_{\le t}
=\ell_{\le t}-h_{\le t}+\min\{\Delta q,0\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) are exactly the branch-free formula for \(B_L(x)\).  The nonnegative row and column slack identities in the tie-face proof show simultaneously that \(\Gamma_x^{\ast}\) recovers \(\Gamma_x^+\), \(\Gamma_x^-\), and \(\Gamma_x^0\) in the positive, negative, and zero-gap cases.

It remains to prove that this observable optimization equals the causal identified set.  Let \(\bar P\in\mathcal M\) induce \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Capacity identification and weak selection monotonicity imply that its survivor-complier subcoupling has row sums bounded by \(\ell\), column sums bounded by \(h\), and total mass \(m\); it therefore lies in \(\Gamma_x^{\ast}\).  Conversely, fix \(\gamma\in\Gamma_x^{\ast}\).  If \(q_1>q_0\), exact mass forces every row to bind.  Assign \(\gamma_{ij}\) to survivor compliers with outcomes \((i,j)\), assign
\[
h_j-\sum_i\gamma_{ij}\ge0
\]
to \((S_0,S_1)=(0,1)\) compliers with \(Y_1=j\), and assign the remaining complier mass \(P(C\mid X=x)-q_1\ge0\) to \((0,0)\).  If \(q_0>q_1\), exact columns bind; assign \(\ell_i-\sum_j\gamma_{ij}\) to \((1,0)\) compliers with \(Y_0=i\), and put mass \(P(C\mid X=x)-q_0\) in \((0,0)\).  On a tie both margins bind and all nonsurviving compliers use \((0,0)\).  Nonnegativity follows from the capacity constraints, while the final remainders are nonnegative because \(q_d=P(S_d=1,C\mid X=x)\le P(C\mid X=x)\).  Assign arbitrary outcomes where selection is zero and retain all noncomplier components of \(\bar P\).  This preserves the two selected-complier submargins, every noncomplier observed component, the instrument propensity, and every maintained restriction.  Thus every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_x^{\ast}\) is the projection of a compatible full law.

The construction is independent across the finite cells.  For every compatible law,
\[
P(Y_1>Y_0,S_0=S_1=1,C)=\sum_xp_xb_x(\gamma_x),
\qquad
P(S_0=S_1=1,C)=\sum_xp_xm(x)=M.
\]
The positive-aggregate-survivor assumption permits division by \(M\).  The finite product of the compact convex polytopes \(\Gamma_x^{\ast}\) is compact and convex, and the numerator is linear, so its image is the entire closed interval between its summed cellwise extrema.  A cell with \(m(x)=0\) has only the zero coupling.  Dividing (5)--(7) by \(M\) proves the stated equality for \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), including sharpness rather than merely an outer bound.
