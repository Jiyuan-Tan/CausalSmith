Let \(\mathcal O\) be the finite support of \(O\), put \(J=|\mathcal O|\), and let \(v=(P_{\mathrm{obs}}(O=o))_{o\in\mathcal O}\in\mathbb R^J\).  Its empirical counterpart is \(\widehat v_n=(\widehat P_n(O=o))_{o\in\mathcal O}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}sampling}\) and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}multinomial\text{-}central\text{-}limit}\) give
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat v_n-v)\rightsquigarrow\mathbb Z_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\quad\text{in }\mathbb R^J.
\tag{1}
\]

We first define the fixed-reduced-support map to which the directional delta method will be applied.  Set \(\mathbb D=\mathbb R^J\), \(\mathbb E=\mathbb R^2\), and \(\mathbb D_0=\mathbb D\).  For every \(w\in\mathbb D\), compute the empirical-ratio analogues of the conditional probabilities from the coordinates of \(w\), assigning the value zero whenever a conditioning denominator is zero, exactly as in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}endpoint\text{-}estimator}\).  Compute the raw capacity contrasts, apply the coordinatewise projection \(\Pi_{\mathfrak C}\), and then compute \(q_0,q_1,\Delta q,m,B_L,B_U\).  Sum the resulting numerator and denominator terms only over the fixed set \(\mathcal X_+(P)\).  Divide each numerator by the common denominator when it is nonzero and assign the fixed value \((0,1)\) otherwise.  Denote this everywhere-defined map by
\[
\Phi:\mathbb D_\phi=\mathbb D\longrightarrow\mathbb E.
\tag{2}
\]
This totalization only defines \(\Phi\) away from the population neighborhood; it does not add a probabilistic premise.

If \(x\in\mathcal X_+(P)\), then \(p_xm(x)>0\), hence \(p_x>0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}instrument\text{-}overlap}\) therefore gives
\[
P_{\mathrm{obs}}(X=x,Z=1)=p_x\pi(x)\ge p_x\varepsilon_Z>0,
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{obs}}(X=x,Z=0)=p_x\{1-\pi(x)\}\ge p_x\varepsilon_Z>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus all conditional-probability denominators used by \(\Phi\) on the fixed support are positive at \(v\) and remain positive in a neighborhood of \(v\).  On that neighborhood the map from \(w\) to \((p_x,\widetilde\ell(x),\widetilde h(x))_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}\) is continuously differentiable, because it is a finite collection of linear maps and ratios with nonzero denominators.

At the population law the raw capacities equal the nonnegative vector \(c=(\ell,h)\).  The projection in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}endpoint\text{-}estimator}\) is the coordinatewise positive-part map.  For a direction \(g\) in raw-capacity coordinates its Hadamard directional derivative is
\[
[\Pi'_{\mathfrak C,c}(g)]_k=
\begin{cases}
g_k,&c_k>0,\\
\max\{g_k,0\},&c_k=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, for \(t_r\downarrow0\) and \(g_r\to g\), division of \(\max\{c_k+t_rg_{r,k},0\}-c_k\) by \(t_r\) is eventually \(g_{r,k}\) if \(c_k>0\), and is \(\max\{g_{r,k},0\}\) if \(c_k=0\); the finite-dimensional coordinatewise limits prove (4).

We next verify, rather than assume, the directional derivatives of the cut maps.  Let \(\dot\ell_i(x)\) and \(\dot h_j(x)\) denote the capacity-direction coordinates after (4), and define
\[
\dot q_0(x)=\sum_i\dot\ell_i(x),\qquad
\dot q_1(x)=\sum_j\dot h_j(x),\qquad
\dot{\Delta q}(x)=\dot q_1(x)-\dot q_0(x).
\tag{5}
\]
For the lower endpoint put
\[
A_{x,t}=\ell_{\le t}(x)-h_{\le t}(x),\qquad
G_x=\max_{t\in\mathcal Y}A_{x,t},\qquad
s_x=\min\{\Delta q(x),0\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The active threshold set \(\mathcal A_L(x)=\{t\in\mathcal Y:A_{x,t}=G_x\}\) is nonempty because \(\mathcal Y\) is finite.  If
\[
\dot A_{x,t}=\sum_{i\le t}\dot\ell_i(x)-\sum_{j\le t}\dot h_j(x),
\]
then a direct finite active-set expansion gives
\[
\dot G_x=\max_{t\in\mathcal A_L(x)}\dot A_{x,t}.
\tag{7}
\]
To see this, inactive indices have a strictly positive gap from \(G_x\) and hence cannot maximize after a sufficiently small perturbation; maximizing the first-order terms over the remaining active indices gives (7).  Similarly,
\[
\dot s_x=
\begin{cases}
\dot{\Delta q}(x),&\Delta q(x)<0,\\
0,&\Delta q(x)>0,\\
\min\{\dot{\Delta q}(x),0\},&\Delta q(x)=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
Since Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}threshold\text{-}cuts}\) gives \(B_L(x)=\max\{0,G_x+s_x\}\), its derivative is
\[
\dot B_L(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dot G_x+\dot s_x,&G_x+s_x>0,\\
0,&G_x+s_x<0,\\
\max\{\dot G_x+\dot s_x,0\},&G_x+s_x=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) permit every simultaneous threshold tie, and (8) is the asserted active-face map when \(\Delta q(x)=0\).

For the upper endpoint define
\[
U_{x,t}=\ell_{<t}(x)+h_{>t}(x),qquad
\dot U_{x,t}=\sum_{i<t}\dot\ell_i(x)+\sum_{j>t}\dot h_j(x).
\tag{10}
\]
The mass derivative is
\[
\dot m(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dot q_0(x),&q_0(x)<q_1(x),\\
\dot q_1(x),&q_1(x)<q_0(x),\\
\min\{\dot q_0(x),\dot q_1(x)\},&q_0(x)=q_1(x).
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
Index the mass cap by \(\star\), set \(V_{x,\star}=m(x)\), \(\dot V_{x,\star}=\dot m(x)\), \(V_{x,t}=U_{x,t}\), and \(\dot V_{x,t}=\dot U_{x,t}\) for \(t\in\mathcal Y\).  Let
\[
\mathcal A_U(x)=\{a\in\{\star\}\cup\mathcal Y:V_{x,a}=B_U(x)\}.
\]
The same finite active-set argument, now for a minimum, yields
\[
\dot B_U(x)=\min_{a\in\mathcal A_U(x)}\dot V_{x,a}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus (11)--(12) include both a positive-survivor zero-gap cell and arbitrary ties between the mass cap and several threshold cuts.

The preceding calculations also prove Hadamard directional differentiability: for each finite maximum or minimum, the inactive-gap argument is valid for every convergent sequence of directions, while sums and compositions preserve the displayed first-order expansions.  Let \(\dot p_x\) be the direction of the cell probability and, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\), put
\[
N_e=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}p_xB_e(x),
\qquad
\dot N_e=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}\{\dot p_xB_e(x)+p_x\dot B_e(x)\},
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
M=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}p_xm(x),
\qquad
\dot M=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}\{\dot p_xm(x)+p_x\dot m(x)\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Cells outside \(\mathcal X_+(P)\) have \(p_xm(x)=0\), so the denominator in (14) equals the original aggregate survivor mass.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}positive\text{-}aggregate\text{-}survivors}\) gives \(M>0\); hence it also gives a neighborhood on which the denominator in (2) is nonzero.  Ordinary quotient algebra then gives, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\),
\[
\left(\frac{N_e}{M}\right)'(g)=\frac{\dot N_eM-N_e\dot M}{M^2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (3)--(15) prove that the totalized map \(\Phi\) is Hadamard directionally differentiable at \(v\), tangentially to \(\mathbb D_0=\mathbb D\), and they give its derivative explicitly.

We now check every hypothesis of the cited directional delta method.  The spaces \(\mathbb D=\mathbb R^J\) and \(\mathbb E=\mathbb R^2\) are Banach spaces; \(\mathbb D_\phi=\mathbb D\); \(\theta_0=v\); \(\widehat\theta_n=\widehat v_n\); and \(r_n=\sqrt n\uparrow\infty\).  Weak convergence is (1).  The finite-dimensional Gaussian vector \(\mathbb Z_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) is tight, and its support is a subset of \(\mathbb R^J=\mathbb D_0\).  The differentiability premise was proved in the preceding paragraph.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}hadamard\text{-}directional\text{-}delta\text{-}method}\), with these assignments, therefore yields
\[
\sqrt n\{\Phi(\widehat v_n)-\Phi(v)\}
=\Phi'_v\!\left(\sqrt n(\widehat v_n-v)\right)+o_P(1)
\rightsquigarrow\Phi'_v(\mathbb Z_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}).
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(\mathbb D_0=\mathbb D\), the continuous-extension convention in the cited lemma is not needed here: every finite-sample direction already lies in the tangent set.

It remains to relate the fixed-support map in (2) to the screened estimator.  Write \(r_x=p_xm(x)\).  Since \(\mathcal X\) is finite, if \(\mathcal X_+(P)\) is nonempty then
\[
\rho=\min_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}r_x>0.
\tag{17}
\]
It is nonempty because \(M>0\).  The law of large numbers, positivity (3), continuity of the local ratio maps, and continuity of coordinatewise positive part and finite minima imply \(\widehat r_x\to_Pr_x\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X_+(P)\).  Since \(\eta_n\downarrow0\), (17) and a finite union show that every such cell is retained with probability tending to one.

If \(p_x=0\), then \(P(X=x)=0\), so under independent sampling \(\widehat p_x=0\) almost surely and \(\widehat r_x=0\).  Finally suppose \(p_x>0\) and \(m(x)=0\).  Since \(q_0(x),q_1(x)\ge0\) and \(m(x)=\min\{q_0(x),q_1(x)\}\), at least one of these totals, say \(q_a(x)\), is zero.  The corresponding population capacity vector is coordinatewise nonnegative and has zero sum, so every one of its coordinates is zero.  By instrument overlap, the same positive-denominator argument as in (3) applies because \(p_x>0\).  Each associated raw empirical contrast is therefore a smooth function of \(\widehat v_n\) near \(v\); (1) makes it \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  The positive-part projection preserves this order, and the support size \(K\) is fixed, so
\[
0\le\widehat m(x)\le\widehat q_a(x)=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
\qquad
0\le\widehat r_x\le\widehat m(x)=O_P(n^{-1/2})=o_P(\eta_n),
\tag{18}
\]
where the last equality uses \(\sqrt n\eta_n\to\infty\).  A finite union over the remaining cells combines (17)--(18) to prove
\[
P\!\left\{\{x:\widehat r_x>\eta_n\}=\mathcal X_+(P)\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{19}
\]

On the event in (19), the three screened sums in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}endpoint\text{-}estimator}\) are exactly the fixed-support sums defining \(\Phi(\widehat v_n)\).  Their denominator is positive because the retained set is nonempty and every retained \(\widehat r_x\) exceeds \(\eta_n>0\); hence the fallback is not used.  Moreover, Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}threshold\text{-}cuts}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}identified\text{-}interval}\), together with Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}exact\text{-}mass\text{-}threshold\text{-}interval}\), give \(\Phi(v)=\Psi(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Replacing \(\Phi(\widehat v_n)\) by \(\widehat\Psi_n\) in (16) on an event whose probability tends to one proves the asserted directional limit.  Continuity of \(\Phi\) at \(v\), the law of large numbers, and the same replacement prove \(\widehat\Psi_n\to_P\Psi(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  No direction-separation, bootstrap, multiplier, or uniform-calibration claim is used. \(\square\)
