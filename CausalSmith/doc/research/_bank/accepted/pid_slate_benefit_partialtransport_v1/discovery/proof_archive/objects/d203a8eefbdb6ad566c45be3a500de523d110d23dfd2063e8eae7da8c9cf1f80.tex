Compute all prefix and tail sums of \(\ell(x)\) and \(h(x)\) in \(O(K)\) operations per cell.  Scanning the \(K\) thresholds then computes both displayed endpoints in \(O(K)\) further operations, including the separate tie branch.

To return an attaining coupling, use the two Ferrers graphs from the proof of the sharp-threshold theorem.  On the graph \(i<j\), process columns from low to high and always fill them from the largest still-positive eligible row; on the graph \(j\le i\), process columns from high to low and fill them from the smallest still-positive eligible row.  At each assignment send the smaller of the current row and column residual capacities.  The nested-neighborhood exchange argument proves maximality: if an earlier, more restrictive column is supplied by a less restrictive row while a more restrictive row is sent to a later column, swapping the smaller of the two flows preserves feasibility and never reduces total admissible flow.  Repeating exchanges yields exactly the stated greedy order.  Each assignment exhausts a row residual or a column residual, so there are at most \(2K-1\) positive assignments after the eligible indices have been ordered; even a direct \(K^2\)-entry implementation therefore uses \(O(K^2)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons.

For a lower endpoint, greedily maximize the nonbenefit subflow and fill the residual exact margin on the complete bipartite graph; for an upper endpoint, do the same with the benefit subflow.  The completion also exhausts a row or column at every step and costs \(O(K^2)\).  The proof of the sharp-threshold theorem shows that these completions attain the relevant cut values in all three sign branches.  Thus two endpoints and two couplings cost \(O(K^2)\) per cell, which is in particular \(O(K^3)\).  Multiplication by the finite number \(|\mathcal X|\) gives the claimed \(O(|\mathcal X|K^3)\) bound.  The number of scans and assignments depends only on the numbers of vertices and arcs, never on the magnitudes or bit lengths of the capacities, establishing strong polynomiality in the stated arithmetic-operation model.
