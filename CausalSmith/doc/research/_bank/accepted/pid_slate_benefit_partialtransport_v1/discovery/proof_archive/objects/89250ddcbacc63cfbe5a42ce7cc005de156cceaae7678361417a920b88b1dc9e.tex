\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Fix a supported cell \(x\), and write \(c_x=P(C\mid X=x)\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}capacity\text{-}identification}\), whose premises include conditional instrument independence, instrument overlap, and no defiers, gives
\[
q_0(x)=P(S_0=1,C\mid X=x),
\qquad
q_1(x)=P(S_1=1,C\mid X=x).
\]
If \(c_x=0\), both displayed probabilities vanish because their events are subsets of \(C\).  Moreover, every \(\ell_i(x)\) and every \(h_j(x)\) vanishes by the same proposition.  Consequently
\[
q_0(x)=q_1(x)=m(x)=\Delta q(x)=0,
\qquad
\Gamma_x^{\ast}=\Gamma_x^{0}=\{0\},
\qquad
B_L(x)=B_U(x)=0.
\]
There is no normalized survivor-complier distribution in this zero-mass cell, so every division below is restricted to \(c_x>0\), exactly as required by the conditional statement of the theorem.

Now suppose \(c_x>0\).  The no-selection restriction states
\[
P(S_0=S_1=1\mid C,X=x)=1.
\]
Since \(\{S_0=S_1=1\}\subseteq\{S_d=1\}\) for \(d\in\{0,1\}\), this restriction and the probability-one upper bound imply
\[
P(S_d=1\mid C,X=x)=1,
\qquad d\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Multiplying by the positive conditioning mass \(c_x\) yields
\[
P(S_d=1,C\mid X=x)=c_x,
\qquad d\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Substitution in the capacity-identification equalities therefore gives
\[
q_0(x)=q_1(x)=c_x,
\qquad
\Delta q(x)=q_1(x)-q_0(x)=0,
\qquad
m(x)=\min\{q_0(x),q_1(x)\}=c_x>0.
\]
Thus Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}tie\text{-}face\text{-}collapse}\) already implies \(\Gamma_x=\Gamma_x^{0}\).  For completeness, the binding argument can be read directly from Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}partial\text{-}transport\text{-}polytope}\).  If \(\gamma_x\in\Gamma_x=\Gamma_x^{\ast}\), define its row and column sums by
\[
r_i=\sum_j\gamma_{ij,x},
\qquad
s_j=\sum_i\gamma_{ij,x}.
\]
The defining inequalities give \(0\le r_i\le\ell_i(x)\) and \(0\le s_j\le h_j(x)\), while exact mass and the preceding equalities give
\[
\sum_i r_i=m(x)=q_0(x)=\sum_i\ell_i(x),
\qquad
\sum_j s_j=m(x)=q_1(x)=\sum_jh_j(x).
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_i\bigl(\ell_i(x)-r_i\bigr)=0,
\qquad
\sum_j\bigl(h_j(x)-s_j\bigr)=0.
\]
Every summand in these two finite sums is nonnegative, so every summand equals zero.  Therefore \(r_i=\ell_i(x)\) for all \(i\) and \(s_j=h_j(x)\) for all \(j\), proving \(\gamma_x\in\Gamma_x^{0}\).  Conversely, every \(\gamma_x\in\Gamma_x^{0}\) satisfies the two capacity inequalities and has total mass
\[
\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij,x}=\sum_i\ell_i(x)=m(x),
\]
so it belongs to \(\Gamma_x^{\ast}=\Gamma_x\).  This proves
\[
\Gamma_x=\Gamma_x^{\ast}=\Gamma_x^{0}.
\]

Because \(m(x)>0\), define the normalized cumulative distribution functions
\[
F_0(t)=\frac{\ell_{\le t}(x)}{m(x)},
\qquad
F_1(t)=\frac{h_{\le t}(x)}{m(x)},
\qquad
F_0(-1)=0.
\]
The capacity totals equal \(m(x)\), so \(\ell_i(x)/m(x)\) and \(h_j(x)/m(x)\) are probability mass functions on the finite ordered support \(\mathcal Y\).  Moreover, the scaling map
\[
\gamma_x\longmapsto \gamma_x/m(x)
\]
is a bijection from \(\Gamma_x^{0}\) onto the complete coupling polytope having those two marginals; its inverse multiplies a probability coupling by \(m(x)\).

Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}threshold\text{-}cuts}\), together with \(\Delta q(x)=0\), gives
\[
\frac{B_L(x)}{m(x)}
=\max\left\{0,\max_{t\in\mathcal Y}
\frac{\ell_{\le t}(x)-h_{\le t}(x)}{m(x)}\right\}
=\max\left\{0,\max_{t\in\mathcal Y}\bigl(F_0(t)-F_1(t)\bigr)\right\}.
\]
For the upper endpoint, \(\ell_{<t}(x)/m(x)=F_0(t-1)\) and \(h_{>t}(x)/m(x)=1-F_1(t)\), whence
\[
\frac{B_U(x)}{m(x)}
=\min\left\{1,\min_{t\in\mathcal Y}
\bigl(F_0(t-1)+1-F_1(t)\bigr)\right\}.
\]
At \(t=0\), the inner expression is \(h_{>0}(x)/m(x)\le1\).  Thus the separate cap by one is redundant and
\[
\frac{B_U(x)}{m(x)}
=\min_{t\in\mathcal Y}\bigl(F_0(t-1)+1-F_1(t)\bigr)
=\min_{t\in\mathcal Y}
\frac{\ell_{<t}(x)+h_{>t}(x)}{m(x)}.
\]
The revalidated cited Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}ordinal\text{-}fixed\text{-}marginal\text{-}strict\text{-}benefit}\), which is Lu, Ding, and Dasgupta's Proposition 2, equation (6), states that these are exactly the sharp lower and upper probabilities of \(Y_1>Y_0\) over all couplings of the two normalized ordinal marginals.  The scaling bijection therefore identifies \(B_L(x)/m(x)\) and \(B_U(x)/m(x)\) with the published fixed-marginal strict-benefit endpoints, cell by cell. \(\square\)
