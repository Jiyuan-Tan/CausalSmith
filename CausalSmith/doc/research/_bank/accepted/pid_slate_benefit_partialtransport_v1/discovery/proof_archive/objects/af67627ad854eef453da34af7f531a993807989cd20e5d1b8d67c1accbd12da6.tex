Let \(v\) be the finite vector of observed atomic probabilities and \(\widehat v_n\) its empirical vector.  Independent sampling and the established finite multinomial central limit theorem give
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat v_n-v)\rightsquigarrow\mathbb Z_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}.
\tag{1}
\]
For a fixed supported cell, overlap makes both \((X=x,Z=z)\) denominators positive.  The map from \(v\) to the raw capacity contrasts is therefore continuously differentiable at this law.

The projection onto \(\mathfrak C\) is coordinatewise positive part and has Hadamard directional derivative
\[
[\Pi_{\mathfrak C}'(g)]_k=
\begin{cases}
g_k,&c_k>0,\\
\max\{g_k,0\},&c_k=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
All subsequent maps are finite maxima or minima of affine maps.  Write
\[
A_{x,t}=\ell_{\le t}(x)-h_{\le t}(x),\quad
G_x=\max_t A_{x,t},\quad
s_x=\min\{\Delta q(x),0\}.
\]
Then \(B_L(x)=\max\{0,G_x+s_x\}\).  Direct first-order expansion of a finite maximum gives
\[
G_x'(g)=\max_{t:A_{x,t}=G_x}A_{x,t}'(g).
\tag{3}
\]
For the active minimum,
\[
s_x'(g)=
\begin{cases}
\Delta q_x'(g),&\Delta q(x)<0,\\
0,&\Delta q(x)>0,\\
\min\{\Delta q_x'(g),0\},&\Delta q(x)=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
The outer positive part has derivative equal to \(G_x'(g)+s_x'(g)\) when \(G_x+s_x>0\), zero when that quantity is negative, and \(\max\{G_x'(g)+s_x'(g),0\}\) on its zero face.  This is the maximum over the active lower cuts, including the zero cut, with the active minimum face in (4).

For the upper endpoint, put \(U_{x,t}=\ell_{<t}(x)+h_{>t}(x)\).  Since
\[
B_U(x)=\min\{m(x),U_{x,0},\ldots,U_{x,K-1}\},
\]
its derivative is the minimum of the derivatives of all active entries.  In particular,
\[
m_x'(g)=
\begin{cases}
q_{0,x}'(g),&q_0(x)<q_1(x),\\
q_{1,x}'(g),&q_1(x)<q_0(x),\\
\min\{q_{0,x}'(g),q_{1,x}'(g)\},&q_0(x)=q_1(x).
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) explicitly include positive-survivor zero-gap cells and require no locally fixed sign branch.

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), let \(N_e=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_+(P)}p_xB_e(x)\).  The quotient derivative is
\[
\left(\frac{N_e}{M}\right)'(g)=\frac{N_e'(g)M-N_eM'(g)}{M^2},
\tag{6}
\]
which is well defined because \(M>0\).

It remains to justify the reduced support.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal X\) gives a positive minimum of \(r_x=p_xm(x)\) over \(\mathcal X_+(P)\), so consistency and \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) retain every such cell with probability tending to one.  If \(p_x=0\), then \(\widehat p_x=0\) almost surely.  If \(p_x>0\) and \(m(x)=0\), one of the nonnegative population capacity vectors has every coordinate zero.  The smooth first stage and (2) imply \(\widehat m(x)=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), and hence
\[
\widehat r_x=O_P(n^{-1/2})=o_P(\eta_n)
\]
because \(\sqrt n\eta_n\to\infty\).  A finite union proves exact screened-support recovery.

The composite map described by (2)--(6) is Hadamard directionally differentiable.  Applying the established directional delta method to (1), then using support recovery to replace the screened map by the fixed reduced-support map with probability tending to one, proves the asserted limit.  The same composition with the unscaled law of large numbers proves consistency.
