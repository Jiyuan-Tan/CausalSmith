\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Let \(\mathcal E\) be the fixed finite collection of observed atomic and
aggregate cell events needed for all empirical conditional probabilities,
and define
\[
\delta_n=\max_{E\in\mathcal E}
|\widehat P_n(E)-P_{\mathrm{obs}}(E)|.
\]
For each indicator, independent sampling gives variance at most
\(1/(4n)\).  Markov's inequality applied to its squared centered mean and
a union bound yield, uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal P_n\),
\[
P(\delta_n>u)\le\frac{|\mathcal E|}{4nu^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(a_n=(n\eta_n)^{-1/4}\downarrow0\), and
\[
na_n^2=\sqrt{n/\eta_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus (1) implies \(\delta_n/a_n\to_P0\) uniformly.

We next use a ratio-stability calculation that does not require lower
bounds on covariate-cell probabilities.  Put
\[
L_i(x)=p_x\ell_i(x),\quad H_j(x)=p_xh_j(x),\quad
\widehat L_i(x)=\widehat p_x\widehat\ell_i(x),\quad
\widehat H_j(x)=\widehat p_x\widehat h_j(x).
\]
On a cell satisfying \(p_x\ge4\delta_n/\varepsilon_Z\), overlap makes
each population \((X=x,Z=z)\) denominator at least
\(\varepsilon_Zp_x\), and its empirical counterpart is at least half
that value.  For a numerator \(A\) bounded by its denominator \(B\), the
identity
\[
\frac{\widehat A}{\widehat B}-\frac AB
=\frac{\widehat A-A}{\widehat B}
+\frac{A(B-\widehat B)}{B\widehat B}
\]
and \(|\widehat A-A|,|\widehat B-B|\le\delta_n\) bound the error of each
conditional ratio after multiplication by \(p_x\) by
\(4\delta_n/\varepsilon_Z\).  Replacing \(p_x\) by \(\widehat p_x\)
adds at most \(\delta_n\).  Taking the contrast of two instrument arms
and using \(0<\varepsilon_Z<1/2\) therefore gives the conservative bound
\[
\max_{i,j}\{|\widehat L_i(x)-L_i(x)|,
             |\widehat H_j(x)-H_j(x)|\}
\le\frac{16\delta_n}{\varepsilon_Z^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
The coordinatewise positive-part projection does not enlarge this bound:
the population capacities are nonnegative, positive part is
one-Lipschitz, and
\(\widehat p_x\max\{u,0\}=\max\{\widehat p_xu,0\}\).  If instead
\(p_x<4\delta_n/\varepsilon_Z\), each population unconditional capacity
is at most \(p_x\), and each empirical unconditional projected capacity
is at most \(\widehat p_x\le p_x+\delta_n\); the larger right side of (2)
again dominates.  Hence (2) holds in every cell, including those with
small or zero \(p_x\).

The branch-free cut functionals are positively homogeneous.  If every
unconditional capacity coordinate changes by at most \(e\), every prefix
or tail changes by at most \(Ke\), \(\Delta q\) by at most \(2Ke\), and
the lower and upper cut values by at most \(4Ke\).  The minimum defining
the survivor mass is also covered by this constant.  Summing over cells
and applying (2) bounds the errors of each unscreened aggregate numerator
and the aggregate denominator by
\[
A_{0,n}=\frac{64K|\mathcal X|}{\varepsilon_Z^2}\delta_n.
\tag{3}
\]
This is a finite min--max Lipschitz argument and uses no sign
classification, so it remains valid at all simultaneous threshold ties
and zero-gap faces.

Screening removes only cells with \(\widehat r_x\le\eta_n\).  The sharp
transport formulas give
\(0\le\widehat B_L(x)\le\widehat B_U(x)\le\widehat m(x)\), so deletion of
all such cells changes either empirical numerator and the empirical
denominator by at most \(|\mathcal X|\eta_n\).  Consequently, with
\(N_e=\sum_xp_xB_e(x)\) for \(e=L,U\),
\[
|\widehat N_{e,n}-N_e|\le A_n,
\qquad
|\widehat M_n-M|\le A_n,
\qquad
A_n=A_{0,n}+|\mathcal X|\eta_n.
\tag{4}
\]
This step directly covers survivor masses approaching zero and does not
require uniform support recovery.

Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}aggregate\text{-}survivor\text{-}bound}\)
gives \(M\ge m_{\star}\).  On \(A_n\le m_{\star}/2\), one has
\(\widehat M_n\ge m_{\star}/2>0\), so the fallback estimator is not used.
Since \(0\le N_e\le M\), quotient algebra and (4) give
\[
\left|\frac{\widehat N_{e,n}}{\widehat M_n}-\frac{N_e}{M}\right|
\le\frac{|\widehat N_{e,n}-N_e|}{\widehat M_n}
+\frac{N_e|\widehat M_n-M|}{M\widehat M_n}
\le\frac{4A_n}{m_{\star}}.
\tag{5}
\]
On \(\{\delta_n\le a_n\}\), the last member of (5) is at most
\[
\frac{256K|\mathcal X|}{m_{\star}\varepsilon_Z^2}a_n
+\frac{4|\mathcal X|}{m_{\star}}\eta_n
\le
\frac{512K|\mathcal X|}{m_{\star}\varepsilon_Z^2}
(a_n+\eta_n)=g_n.
\tag{6}
\]
By (1), this event has probability tending to one uniformly, and
\(A_n\le m_{\star}/2\) there eventually because
\(a_n+\eta_n\to0\).  Equations (5)--(6) prove
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n}
P\!\left\{\|\widehat\Psi_n-\Psi(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\|_\infty>g_n\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

On the complementary event in (7),
\(\theta_L\ge\widehat\Psi_{n,L}-g_n\) and
\(\theta_U\le\widehat\Psi_{n,U}+g_n\).  Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}exact\text{-}mass\text{-}threshold\text{-}interval}\)
identifies \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=[\theta_L,\theta_U]\), so the
clipped guarded interval contains the entire identified set.  Taking the
infimum over \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) proves the asserted coverage limit.
No multiplier quantile occurs anywhere in this proof; the conclusion is
entirely due to the deterministic guard. \(\square\)
