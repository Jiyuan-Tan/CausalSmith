%%% FIELD sharp-frontier-statement
Fix a member of the parameterized late-ramp family and define
\[
 m_n=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor,\qquad
 V_n=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\{A_{nt}=0\},\qquad
 \eta_n=\prod_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\left(1-\frac ct\right),
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac{2\delta}{(1-a_\star)n}
   \sum_{t=1}^{n}(t/n-a_\star)_+.
\]
Adopt the convention \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\). For any possibly randomized confidence set \(D_n=D_n(O_n,U)\), where \(U\) is independent of the experiment and has the same law under both signs, let
\[
 \gamma_n=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
Write \([x]_+=\max\{x,0\}\). Then, for every \(n\) and each \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge \Delta_n\}
 \ge [\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), this inequality is sharp within this two-sign subexperiment: there is a possibly randomized complete-log confidence set \(D_{n,\alpha}^\star\) satisfying
\[
 \max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_{n,\alpha}^\star\}\le\alpha
\]
and, under each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_{n,\alpha}^\star)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =\Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_{n,\alpha}^\star)\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Moreover, \(\eta_n\to a_\star^c\), \(\Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\) for every \(n\), and \(\Delta_n\to\delta(1-a_\star)\). Hence \([a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+\) is the exact asymptotic probability--diameter frontier, both at the moving threshold \(\Delta_n\) and at the retained threshold \(\delta(1-a_\star)\), within the two-sign late-ramp subexperiment. This attainment claim is not a global minimax-sharpness claim for any larger adaptive-design class.
%%% FIELD sharp-frontier-proof
By \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family},
\[
 \tau_n^\sigma
 =\frac{\sigma\delta}{(1-a_\star)n}
   \sum_{t=1}^{n}(t/n-a_\star)_+.
\]
Subtracting the expression for \(\sigma=-1\) from that for \(\sigma=+1\) proves the displayed formula for \(\Delta_n\), which is strictly positive. The function \(f(u)=(u-a_\star)_+\) is nondecreasing. On every interval \(((t-1)/n,t/n]\), one has \(f(u)\le f(t/n)\), and therefore
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}f(t/n)
 \ge \int_0^1 f(u)\,du
 =\frac{(1-a_\star)^2}{2}.
\]
Multiplication by \(2\delta/(1-a_\star)\) yields
\[
 \Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star).
\]
The same interval comparison, using
\(0\le f(t/n)-f(u)\le 1/n\), gives
\[
 0\le \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}f(t/n)-\int_0^1f(u)\,du\le\frac1n,
\]
so \(\Delta_n\to\delta(1-a_\star)\).

We next compute the common late-arm void mass. Let
\(V_{n,s}=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{s}\{A_{nt}=0\}\), with the intersection equal to the whole sample space when \(s=m_n\). The event \(V_{n,s-1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,s-1}\)-measurable. By \texttt{ass:adaptive-assignment-law} and the deterministic propensity \(e_{ns}=c/s\) in \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family},
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr_\sigma(V_{n,s})
 &=\mathbb E_\sigma\!\left[
   \mathbf 1_{V_{n,s-1}}
   \Pr_\sigma(A_{ns}=0\mid\mathcal F_{n,s-1})
 \right]\\
 &=\left(1-\frac cs\right)\Pr_\sigma(V_{n,s-1}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Iteration gives \(\Pr_\sigma(V_n)=\eta_n\) under either sign. Every factor is positive because \(0<c<1\) and \(s\ge1\).

For completeness, the limit of this mass follows from elementary bounds. For \(0\le x<1\),
\[
 0\le-\log(1-x)-x
 =\int_0^x\frac y{1-y}\,dy
 \le\frac{x^2}{2(1-x)}.
\]
Since \(m_n/n\to a_\star>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \left|\log\eta_n+c\sum_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\frac1t\right|
 \le
 \frac{c^2}{2\{1-c/(m_n+1)\}}
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\frac1{t^2}
 =O(n^{-1}).
\]
Monotonicity of \(u\mapsto u^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \log\!\left(\frac{n+1}{m_n+1}\right)
 \le\sum_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\frac1t
 \le\log\!\left(\frac n{m_n}\right),
\]
and both outer terms tend to \(\log(1/a_\star)\). Thus
\[
 \log\eta_n\to c\log a_\star,
 \qquad \eta_n\to a_\star^c.
\]

We now construct the common subprobability measure. Under either sign, the sequential conditional law of the binary assignment is determined by the same deterministic probabilities \(c/t\); repeated conditioning shows that every assignment string has the same probability under both signs. If \(t\le m_n\), then \(t/n\le a_\star\) and both treated potential outcomes equal zero. If \(t>m_n\), the event \(V_n\) forces \(A_{nt}=0\), and then \texttt{def:observed-outcome} and \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family} give \(Y_{nt}^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)=0\). The propensity and regression logs are deterministic and identical under the two signs. Hence, for every measurable set \(H\),
\[
 \Pr_+\{(O_n,U)\in H,\ V_n\}
 =\Pr_-\{(O_n,U)\in H,\ V_n\}
 =:\nu_n(H),
\]
where independence and the common law of \(U\) preserve the equality. The measure \(\nu_n\) has mass \(\eta_n\) and is dominated by each sign-specific law.

Let \(E_{\sigma,n}=\{\tau_n^\sigma\in D_n\}\). By the definition of \(\gamma_n\),
\[
 \nu_n(E_{\sigma,n}^c)
 \le\Pr_\sigma(E_{\sigma,n}^c)
 \le\gamma_n.
\]
The union bound under the finite measure \(\nu_n\) therefore yields
\[
 \nu_n(E_{+,n}\cap E_{-,n})
 \ge\eta_n-2\gamma_n.
\]
On this intersection the same reported set contains both targets, whose distance is exactly \(\Delta_n\). Domination by either experimental law, together with nonnegativity of probability, proves
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge\max\{\eta_n-2\gamma_n,0\}
 =[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]

It remains to prove attainment. Take \(U\) uniform on \([0,1]\), independently of the experiment. On \(V_n^c\), let \(T_n\) be the first \(t>m_n\) with \(A_{nt}=1\). Such a time exists by the definition of \(V_n^c\), and
\[
 Y_{nT_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 =\sigma\delta\frac{T_n/n-a_\star}{1-a_\star}
\]
is nonzero by \texttt{def:observed-outcome}; its sign identifies \(\sigma\). On \(V_n^c\), report the singleton containing the thereby identified target \(\tau_n^\sigma\).

On \(V_n\), use the common randomizer as follows. If \(\eta_n>2\alpha\), report \(\{\tau_n^+\}\), \(\{\tau_n^-\}\), or \(\{\tau_n^+,\tau_n^-\}\) on intervals of \(U\) having respective conditional lengths
\[
 \frac\alpha{\eta_n},\qquad
 \frac\alpha{\eta_n},\qquad
 1-\frac{2\alpha}{\eta_n}.
\]
If \(\alpha<\eta_n\le2\alpha\), report \(\{\tau_n^+\}\), \(\{\tau_n^-\}\), or \(\varnothing\) on intervals having respective conditional lengths
\[
 \frac{\eta_n-\alpha}{\eta_n},\qquad
 \frac{\eta_n-\alpha}{\eta_n},\qquad
 \frac{2\alpha-\eta_n}{\eta_n}.
\]
If \(\alpha\ge\eta_n\), report \(\varnothing\) throughout \(V_n\). All displayed lengths are nonnegative in their stated cases and sum to one, so this defines a measurable possibly randomized complete-log confidence set.

In the first case, each sign receives unconditional void-event coverage
\(\alpha+(\eta_n-2\alpha)=\eta_n-\alpha\); in the second it receives \(\eta_n-\alpha\); and in the third its total miscoverage is only \(\eta_n\le\alpha\). Since coverage on \(V_n^c\) is one, the total coverage is at least \(1-\alpha\) under each sign. The reported set has positive diameter only when the two-target set is chosen, and that diameter is exactly \(\Delta_n\). Its unconditional selection probability is \(\eta_n-2\alpha\) in the first case and zero otherwise. Thus, under either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_{n,\alpha}^\star)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Because \(\Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star)>0\), the same equality holds with threshold \(\delta(1-a_\star)\). Combining this construction with the preceding lower bound proves finite-sample and asymptotic sharpness within the two-sign subexperiment. Nothing in the construction or lower bound asserts attainment over a larger adaptive-design class.
%%% FIELD parameterized-target-proof
Let \(\gamma_n=\max_{\sigma}\Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}\). The honesty premise implies
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.
\]
By \texttt{thm:sharp-two-sign-late-ramp-tradeoff}, for each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+,
 \qquad
 \eta_n\to a_\star^c,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star).
\]
Since
\(\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}\subseteq
\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}\), it follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 &\ge [a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+\\
 &\ge a_\star^c-2\alpha.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The final lower bound is positive exactly when \(a_\star^c>2\alpha\). The sharp theorem also supplies a sequence whose large-diameter probability converges to \([a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+\), but only within the two-sign late-ramp subexperiment. The derivation uses the complete logged experiments and their common void component, not a Horvitz--Thompson or AIPW distributional limit.
%%% FIELD exact-ramp-target-proof
Apply \texttt{thm:parameterized-late-ramp-frontier} to the specialization certified by \texttt{prop:late-ramp-membership}, with
\[
 c=a_\star=\frac12,\qquad
 \delta=\frac14,\qquad
 \alpha=0.05.
\]
Then
\[
 \delta(1-a_\star)=\frac18,
 \qquad
 [a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+
 =\sqrt{\frac12}-0.1
 =0.6071067811865475\ldots>0.
\]
The conclusion of \texttt{thm:parameterized-late-ramp-frontier} therefore gives, under each sign,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\frac18\right\}
 \ge\sqrt{\frac12}-0.1.
\]

As the required smallest-instance check, direct evaluation for \(n=1,2,3,4\), with \(m_n=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), gives
\[
 (\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)
 =\left(\frac12,\frac14,\frac29,\frac3{16}\right)
\]
and
\[
 (\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3,\eta_4)
 =\left(\frac12,\frac34,\frac58,\frac{35}{48}\right).
\]
Thus every checked separation is at least \(1/8\), and the void masses agree exactly with the finite product used in the proof. This corollary remains an all-confidence-set information bound and is independent of any Horvitz--Thompson or AIPW limit law.
%%% FIELD li-zhao-target-proof
The sourced node \texttt{lem:li-zhao-baseline-adaptive-class} states the relevant baseline requirements. In the binary parameterized ramp, write
\[
 e_{nt}(1)=\frac ct,
 \qquad
 e_{nt}(0)=1-\frac ct.
\]
These probabilities are deterministic, hence prespecified functions of every available history. Since \(0<c<1\) and \(t\ge1\), they are strictly positive and sum to one. Proposition \texttt{prop:parameterized-late-ramp-membership} proves that the potential outcomes are fixed and bounded and that the zero regression is predictable. Thus every parameterized late-ramp array belongs to Li and Zhao's baseline binary finite-population adaptive-design class.

It does not satisfy their additional Gaussian Condition 3(ii). At the vector level, the control residual is identically zero, so the control coordinate of the AIPW contribution is identically zero and the scaled covariance \(V_{\mathrm{aipw}}\) has a zero row and column. Hence \(\lambda_{\min}(V_{\mathrm{aipw}})=0\), and the inverse-eigenvalue expression in \texttt{lem:li-zhao-baseline-adaptive-class} cannot be \(o(1)\). The failure also persists after projecting to the scalar treated-minus-control contrast. Put
\[
 X_{nt}=(A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{r^\sigma(t/n)}{e_{nt}}.
\]
The adaptive assignment law gives \(\mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=0\), so distinct increments are uncorrelated by iterated conditioning. It also gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})
 =e_{nt}(1-e_{nt})\frac{\{r^\sigma(t/n)\}^2}{e_{nt}^2}
 =\{r^\sigma(t/n)\}^2\left(\frac tc-1\right).
\]
Therefore, if \(W_n\) denotes the variance of the \(\sqrt n\)-scaled scalar AIPW error, orthogonality of the increments yields
\[
 W_n
 =\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}
 \{r^\sigma(t/n)\}^2\left(\frac tc-1\right).
\]
Writing \(u_t=t/n\), one obtains
\[
 \frac{W_n}{n}
 =\frac{\delta^2}{c(1-a_\star)^2}
   \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}u_t(u_t-a_\star)_+^2
 -\frac{\delta^2}{(1-a_\star)^2}
   \frac1{n^2}\sum_{t=1}^{n}(u_t-a_\star)_+^2.
\]
The second term tends to zero in absolute value, while the first right Riemann sum converges, so
\[
 \frac{W_n}{n}\longrightarrow
 w_\star
 :=\frac{\delta^2}{c(1-a_\star)^2}
   \int_{a_\star}^{1}u(u-a_\star)^2\,du
 =\frac{\delta^2(a_\star+3)(1-a_\star)}{12c}>0.
\]
At \(t=n\), the outcome envelope is at least \(\delta\), the regression envelope is zero, and the smallest binary assignment probability equals \(c/n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Therefore the scalar negligible-maximum ratio has
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 W_n^{-1}n^{-1}
 \max_{1\le t\le n}
 \frac{(L_t+M_t)^2}{\underline e_t^2}
 \ge\frac{\delta^2/c^2}{w_\star}
 =\frac{12}{c(a_\star+3)(1-a_\star)}>0.
\]
This directly verifies the nonnegligible-increment obstruction recorded in \texttt{lem:li-zhao-lindeberg-premise}.

If a sequence of confidence sets is uniformly asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest over Li and Zhao's baseline class, then it is honest under both signs of every compatible late-ramp pair because those two laws are a subclass. Applying \texttt{thm:parameterized-late-ramp-frontier} gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 \ge a_\star^c-2\alpha.
\]
This transfers only the converse: the argument constructs no confidence set on Li and Zhao's larger class and invokes none of this note's positive profile-net calibration machinery. It therefore proves neither global minimax sharpness nor an extension of positive calibration on that larger class.
%%% FIELD parameterized-target-statement
Fix a member of the parameterized late-ramp family and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). For every possibly randomized confidence set \(D_n=D_n(O_n,U)\), where \(U\) is independent of the experiment and has the same law under both signs, if
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\min_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\in D_n\}\ge1-\alpha,
\]
then, for each \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr_\sigma\!\left\{
 \operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\right\}
 \ge a_\star^c-2\alpha.
\]
The bound is nontrivial when \(a_\star^c>2\alpha\). It is an all-confidence-set information bound for the complete logged experiment and does not use a Horvitz--Thompson or AIPW distributional limit.
