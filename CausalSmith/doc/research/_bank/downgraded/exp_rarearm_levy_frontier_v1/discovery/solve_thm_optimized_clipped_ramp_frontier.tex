%%% FIELD prop-membership-proposed-statement
Every member of the capped clipped-ramp family satisfies the declared fixed-outcome, outcome-boundedness, predictable-randomization, propensity-range, predictable-regression, bounded-regression, intensity-profile, and residual-profile conditions. In particular, \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\) and \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the propensity cap is inactive at every time after the profile cutoff and also at every time after the late-ramp start, both profile discrepancies in \(G_n\) are zero, the early rare-arm term and its predictable quadratic variation are zero, and hence \(G_n=\Omega\). These conclusions are eventual: they do not establish the profile-contract condition for every \(n\), and therefore do not by themselves certify membership in the full structured class.
%%% FIELD frontier-proposed-statement
Assume \(L_r>0\), and define
\[
 d_{\max}=\begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
For every \(d\) with \(0<d<d_{\max}\), set
\[
 x(d)=\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{d/L_r},&d\le M^2/L_r,\\
 \{d+M^2/L_r\}/(2M),&d>M^2/L_r,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad a(d)=1-x(d).
\]
Select the capped clipped-ramp pair with start \(a(d)\), and define
\[
 \gamma_n=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
If the selected pair satisfies \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha\), then, under each sign,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
The elbow \(d=M^2/L_r\) is interior to \((0,d_{\max})\) exactly when \(L_r>M\). Among two-sign late-void witnesses whose residual profiles vanish on \([0,a]\), are bounded in absolute value by \(M\), and have Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\), the clipped profile is pointwise optimal, and the displayed bound is the optimized late-void envelope. For each selected two-sign clipped-ramp subexperiment the bound is attained asymptotically by an exact randomized allocation, but no global minimax attainment over the full structured class is claimed. Uniform honesty over a larger class is sufficient only if that selected pair is separately known to belong to the larger class for every \(n\).

%%% FIELD prop-membership-proof
Write
\[
 g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\}.
\]
The capped clipped-ramp construction makes every \(Y_{nt}^\sigma(a)\) deterministic under the assignment law, so the finite-population fixed-outcome condition holds. Since \(0\le g(u)\le M\),
\[
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)|=0,
 \qquad
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)|=g(t/n)\le M,
\]
which verifies outcome boundedness. The construction specifies
\(\Pr_\sigma(A_{nt}=1\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=e_{nt}\), and, because \(c>0\) and \(\rho\in(0,1)\),
\[
 0<e_{nt}=\min\{\rho,c/t\}\le\rho<1.
\]
Thus the predictable assignment-law and propensity-range conditions hold. The two regressions are identically zero, hence are \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\)-measurable and satisfy \(|m_{nt}(a)|=0\le M_m\). This proves the predictable- and bounded-regression conditions.

The constructed intensity is \(\bar q^\sigma(u)=c\). Therefore \(\bar q^\sigma(0)=c\),
\[
 c/2\le \bar q^\sigma(u)=c\le K,
 \qquad
 |\bar q^\sigma(u)-\bar q^\sigma(v)|=0\le L_q|u-v|.
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal Q\), these three displayed facts give \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\). The map \(u\mapsto(u-a_\star)_+\) is one-Lipschitz, multiplication by \(L_r\) gives Lipschitz modulus \(L_r\), and truncation by the constant \(M\) is nonexpansive. Since \(B=M+M_m\ge M\), it follows that
\[
 |r^\sigma(u)|=g(u)\le M\le B,
 \qquad
 |r^\sigma(u)-r^\sigma(v)|\le L_r|u-v|.
\]
The definition of \(\mathcal R\) now yields \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\).

It remains to establish the asserted eventual good-event statement from the construction. The conditions \(n\varepsilon_n\to\infty\), \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), and \(a_\star>0\) imply that there is an integer \(N\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\),
\[
 \lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil>c/\rho,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n+1/n<a_\star,
 \qquad
 \lfloor a_\star n\rfloor+1>c/\rho.
\]
Indeed, the first inequality follows from \(n\varepsilon_n\to\infty\), the second from \(\varepsilon_n+1/n\to0\), and the third from \(a_\star n\to\infty\). If \(n\ge N\) and \(t\ge\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\), then \(t>c/\rho\), so \(c/t<\rho\) and
\[
 e_{nt}=c/t,
 \qquad
 t e_{nt}=c=\bar q^\sigma(t/n).
\]
The same cap-inactivity conclusion holds whenever \(t>\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\), by the third inequality. Moreover, for every \(t\),
\[
 r_{nt}(1)=Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)-m_{nt}(1)
 =r^\sigma(t/n),
\]
so the residual-profile discrepancy is identically zero.

For an early index \(t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\),
\[
 \frac tn<\frac{\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil}{n}
 \le\varepsilon_n+\frac1n<a_\star.
\]
Consequently \(r_{nt}(1)=0\). Every summand in
\[
 S_{n,\mathrm{rare}}^{\mathrm{early}}
 =\frac1n\sum_{t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil}
 (A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{r_{nt}(1)}{e_{nt}}
\]
is zero; hence this term is identically zero and its predictable quadratic variation is \(0\le v_{0,n}\). All three clauses in the definition of \(G_n\) therefore hold on the whole sample space for every \(n\ge N\), proving \(G_n=\Omega\) eventually.

This last conclusion is not an every-\(n\) profile-contract certificate. At a finite retained index \(t\ge\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\) for which \(c/t>\rho\), the cap gives
\[
 |t e_{nt}-\bar q^\sigma(t/n)|=c-t\rho.
\]
If \(\delta_{q,n}<c-t\rho\), the intensity clause of \(G_n\) fails. Thus eventual cap inactivity proves exactly the eventual assertion above, but it cannot by itself prove the contract required for every \(n\).
%%% FIELD frontier-proof
Let
\[
 \phi_a(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a)_+,M\}.
\]
We first certify the residual optimization. Consider any two-sign late-void witness with profiles \(\widetilde r^+=g\) and \(\widetilde r^-=-g\), where \(g=0\) on \([0,a]\), \(|g(u)|\le M\), and \(g\) has Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\). Since \(g(a)=0\), Lipschitz continuity gives, for \(u>a\),
\[
 |g(u)|=|g(u)-g(a)|\le L_r(u-a).
\]
Combining this inequality with \(|g(u)|\le M\), and using \(g(u)=0\) for \(u\le a\), yields the pointwise certificate
\[
 |g(u)|\le\phi_a(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a)_+,M\}.
\]
The capped clipped ramp attains equality everywhere. Therefore every such witness obeys
\[
 \left|2\int_0^1 g(u)\,du\right|
 \le 2\int_a^1\phi_a(u)\,du
 =2\int_0^{1-a}\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds
 =d(1-a),
\]
where direct integration gives
\[
 d(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_rx^2,&L_rx\le M,\\
 2Mx-M^2/L_r,&L_rx>M.
 \end{cases}
\]

The function \(d\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), and it is strictly increasing because
\[
 d(y)-d(x)=2\int_x^y\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds>0
 \quad\text{whenever }0\le x<y\le1.
\]
Its endpoint value is
\[
 d(1)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M,
 \end{cases}
 =d_{\max}.
\]
If \(L_r\le M\), then \(M^2/L_r\ge d_{\max}\), so every \(d\in(0,d_{\max})\) is on the quadratic branch and \(x(d)=\sqrt{d/L_r}\). If \(L_r>M\), the clipping point \(x=M/L_r\) lies in \((0,1)\), its image is \(M^2/L_r\), and inversion of the two branches gives exactly
\[
 x(d)=
 \begin{cases}
 \sqrt{d/L_r},&d\le M^2/L_r,\\
 \{d+M^2/L_r\}/(2M),&d>M^2/L_r.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus, in all cases,
\[
 x(d)\in(0,1),\qquad a(d)=1-x(d)\in(0,1),
 \qquad d(x(d))=d.
\]
The clipping elbow is interior exactly when \(L_r>M\).

Fix \(d\in(0,d_{\max})\), select the capped clipped-ramp pair with \(a_\star=a(d)\), and let \(\gamma_n\) be its maximum miscoverage probability as defined in the statement. The selected-pair premise is
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.
\]
The established sharp two-sign clipped-ramp theorem gives, for both \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+,
 \qquad
 \eta_n\longrightarrow a(d)^c,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\ge d(1-a(d))=d.
\]
Since \(\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}\subseteq
\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}\), for every \(\epsilon>0\), all sufficiently large \(n\) satisfy
\[
 \eta_n\ge a(d)^c-\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \gamma_n\le\alpha+\epsilon.
\]
Monotonicity of the positive-part map then gives
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[a(d)^c-2\alpha-3\epsilon]_+.
\]
Taking the lower limit and then letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), using continuity of \(x\mapsto[x]_+\), proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[a(d)^c-2\alpha]_+
 =[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
No structured-class inclusion is used in this implication.

For completeness, the sharp theorem's pairwise attainment can be seen directly. Put \(m_n=\lfloor a(d)n\rfloor\) and let
\[
 V_n=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^n\{A_{nt}=0\}.
\]
Under either sign, \(\Pr_\sigma(V_n)=\eta_n\). On \(V_n\), all pre-ramp treated outcomes are zero and no post-ramp treatment is observed, so the complete logged observation has the same value under the two signs. On \(V_n^c\), some \(t>m_n\) is treated and \(Y_{nt}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\sigma\phi_{a(d)}(t/n)\ne0\), so the sign is revealed. If \(\eta_n>2\alpha\), use an independent uniform randomizer on \(V_n\) to report \(\{\tau_n^+,\tau_n^-\}\) with probability \(1-2\alpha/\eta_n\), and each singleton with probability \(\alpha/\eta_n\); on \(V_n^c\), report the revealed true singleton. Each sign then has miscoverage exactly \(\alpha\), and
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =\eta_n-2\alpha.
\]
If \(\eta_n\le2\alpha\), report either singleton with probability one half on \(V_n\), and again report the revealed singleton on \(V_n^c\). Each sign then has miscoverage \(\eta_n/2\le\alpha\), and the probability of diameter at least \(\Delta_n\) is zero. Thus in both cases it equals \([\eta_n-2\alpha]_+\). The only positive-diameter report in this construction is the two-target set, whose diameter is \(\Delta_n\ge d\); hence
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+
 \longrightarrow[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
This is attainment within the selected pair, not a globally attaining confidence procedure.

Finally, let another admissible late-void pair start at \(a'=1-x'\) and have limiting causal separation at least \(d\). The pointwise certificate above implies \(d\le d(x')\). Strict monotonicity of \(d\) forces \(x'\ge x(d)\), and therefore its constant-intensity limiting void mass satisfies
\[
 (a')^c=(1-x')^c\le(1-x(d))^c.
\]
This is the matching optimality certificate for the claimed late-void envelope.

As an exact boundary check, specialize to \(c=a_\star=1/2\), \(L_r=1/2\), \(M\ge1/4\), and choose \(\rho\ge1/2\). Then
\[
 d(1/2)=1/8,
 \qquad
 [(1/2)^{1/2}-2(0.05)]_+
 =0.6071067811865475\ldots.
\]
For \(n=1,2,3,4\), the post-ramp factor sets are respectively \(\{1\}\), \(\{2\}\), \(\{2,3\}\), and \(\{3,4\}\). Because the cap is inactive on those factors when \(\rho\ge1/2\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 \eta_1=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \eta_2=\frac34,
 \qquad
 \eta_3=\frac58,
 \qquad
 \eta_4=\frac{35}{48}.
\]
The cap qualification is necessary: if \(\rho<1/2\), then \(\eta_1=1-\rho\), so these four diagnostic values are not claimed.
