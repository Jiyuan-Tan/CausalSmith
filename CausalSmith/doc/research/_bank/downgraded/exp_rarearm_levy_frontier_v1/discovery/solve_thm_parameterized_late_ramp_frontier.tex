%%% FIELD def-capped-clipped-ramp-family-construction
Fix \(c>0\), \(\rho\in(0,1)\), \(a_\star\in(0,1)\), \(M>0\), \(L_r>0\), and \(K\ge c\). For \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), put
\[
 \bar q^\sigma(u)=c,\qquad
 g_{a_\star}(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\},\qquad
 r^\sigma(u)=\sigma g_{a_\star}(u),
\]
and set
\[
 e_{nt}=\min\{\rho,c/t\},\qquad
 m_{nt}(0)=m_{nt}(1)=0,qquad
 Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)=0,qquad
 Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)=r^\sigma(t/n).
\]
The sequential randomization law is
\(\Pr_\sigma(A_{nt}=1\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=e_{nt}\), and the complete observation is \(O_n=(A_{n1:n},e_{n1:n},Y_{n1:n}^{\mathrm{obs}},m_{n1:n})\).

%%% FIELD prop-capped-clipped-ramp-membership-statement
Every member of the capped clipped-ramp family satisfies the declared fixed-outcome, outcome-boundedness, predictable-randomization, propensity-range, predictable-regression, intensity-profile, and residual-profile conditions. For all sufficiently large \(n\), the propensity cap is inactive at every time after both the profile cutoff and the late-ramp start, both profile discrepancies in \(G_n\) are zero, the early rare-arm term and its predictable quadratic variation are zero, and hence \(G_n=\Omega\). In particular, \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\), \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\), and the profile contract holds eventually with probability one.

%%% FIELD prop-capped-clipped-ramp-membership-proof
Write \(g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\}\). The arrays \(Y_{nt}^\sigma(a)\) in the construction are deterministic, so they are fixed under randomization by \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\mbox{-}outcomes}\). Since \(0\le g(u)\le M\),
\[
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)|=0,
 \qquad
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)|=g(t/n)\le M,
\]
which verifies \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\). The assignment law is the one in \(\mathrm{ass:adaptive\mbox{-}assignment\mbox{-}law}\). Moreover,
\[
 0<e_{nt}=\min\{\rho,c/t\}\le\rho<1,
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\mbox{-}range}\) holds. The zero regressions are \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\)-measurable and have absolute value zero, proving the predictable- and bounded-regression requirements.

The constant function \(\bar q^\sigma\equiv c\) has anchor \(c\), lies between \(c/2\) and \(K\), and has Lipschitz modulus zero. Thus the three intensity assumptions and \(\mathrm{def:intensity\mbox{-}class}\) give \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\). The positive-part map is one-Lipschitz, and truncation by \(M\) is nonexpansive. Consequently, for all \(u,v\in[0,1]\),
\[
 |r^\sigma(u)|=g(u)\le M\le B,
 \qquad
 |r^\sigma(u)-r^\sigma(v)|
 \le L_r|u-v|.
\]
The two residual assumptions and \(\mathrm{def:residual\mbox{-}class}\) therefore give \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\).

It remains to verify the common profile event rather than assume it. The stipulated limits \(n\varepsilon_n\to\infty\) and \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), together with \(a_\star>0\), permit an integer \(N\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\),
\[
 \lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil>c/\rho,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n+1/n<a_\star,
 \qquad
 \lfloor a_\star n\rfloor+1>c/\rho.
\]
For \(t\ge\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\), the first inequality makes the cap inactive, whence
\[
 e_{nt}=c/t,
 \qquad
 t e_{nt}=c=\bar q^\sigma(t/n).
\]
The same conclusion holds whenever \(t>\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\), by the third inequality. Also, for every \(t\),
\[
 r_{nt}(1)=Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)-m_{nt}(1)
 =r^\sigma(t/n),
\]
so the residual discrepancy is identically zero. If \(t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\), then
\[
 t/n<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil/n
 \le\varepsilon_n+1/n<a_\star,
\]
and hence \(r_{nt}(1)=0\). Every summand in \(S_{n,\mathrm{rare}}^{\mathrm{early}}\) is therefore zero, so that term is identically zero and its predictable quadratic variation is \(0\le v_{0,n}\). All three clauses of \(\mathrm{def:good\mbox{-}event}\) hold on the whole sample space. Thus \(G_n=\Omega\) for \(n\ge N\), and \(\Pr(G_n)=1\ge1-\kappa_n\), as required by \(\mathrm{ass:profile\mbox{-}contract}\).

%%% FIELD thm-sharp-clipped-ramp-tradeoff-statement
Fix a member of the capped clipped-ramp family. Define
\[
 m_n=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor,
 \qquad
 V_n=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\{A_{nt}=0\},
 \qquad
 \eta_n=\prod_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\left(1-\min\{\rho,c/t\}\right),
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^{n}\min\{L_r(t/n-a_\star)_+,M\}.
\]
For any possibly randomized complete-log confidence set \(D_n=D_n(O_n,U)\), with \(U\) independent of the experiment and identically distributed under the two signs, put
\[
 \gamma_n=max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
With \([z]_+=\max\{z,0\}\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\), for every \(n\) and each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), this inequality is attained within this two-sign subexperiment by a possibly randomized complete-log confidence set having maximum miscoverage at most \(\alpha\) and, under either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Writing \(x=1-a_\star\), one has \(\eta_n\to a_\star^c\), \(\Delta_n\ge d(x)\) for every \(n\), and \(\Delta_n\to d(x)\), where
\[
 d(x)=2\int_0^x\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds
 =\begin{cases}
 L_rx^2,&L_rx\le M,\\
 2Mx-M^2/L_r,&L_rx>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
The attainment assertion is confined to the displayed two-sign subexperiment.

%%% FIELD thm-sharp-clipped-ramp-tradeoff-proof
Let \(g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\}\). By the definition of the finite-population causal estimand and the two potential-outcome arrays,
\[
 \Delta_n
 =\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n
   \{Y_{nt}^+(1)-Y_{nt}^-(1)\}
 =\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^n g(t/n)>0.
\]
This is a contrast of fixed potential outcomes, not a distributional functional of the estimator.

The probabilities \(e_{nt}\) are deterministic. Iterated conditioning under \(\mathrm{ass:adaptive\mbox{-}assignment\mbox{-}law}\) gives
\[
 \Pr_\sigma(V_n)
 =\prod_{t=m_n+1}^n(1-e_{nt})
 =\eta_n
\]
under either sign. For \(t\le m_n\), one has \(t/n\le a_\star\), so both treated potential outcomes are zero. On \(V_n\), no later treated potential outcome is observed. The assignments and propensities have the same law under both signs, the regressions are identical, and \(U\) has the same independent law. Hence the restrictions of the two laws of \((O_n,U)\) to \(V_n\) are one and the same subprobability measure, say \(\mu_n\), of total mass \(\eta_n\).

Let \(E_\sigma=\{\tau_n^\sigma\in D_n\}\). Whenever both \(E_+\) and \(E_-\) occur, the set contains two points separated by \(\Delta_n\), so its diameter is at least \(\Delta_n\). Under either sign, commonality of \(\mu_n\) and the union bound yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 &\ge \mu_n(V_n\cap E_+\cap E_-)\\
 &\ge \eta_n-\mu_n(E_+^c)-\mu_n(E_-^c)\\
 &\ge \eta_n-
   \Pr_+\{\tau_n^+\notin D_n\}
   -\Pr_-\{\tau_n^-\notin D_n\}\\
 &\ge \eta_n-2\gamma_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The left side is nonnegative, proving the positive-part form.

We next give the finite-sample attaining rule. Off \(V_n\), at least one \(t>m_n\) is treated. Then \(t/n>a_\star\), so \(g(t/n)>0\), and the sign of the observed nonzero outcome identifies \(\sigma\) exactly. Return the corresponding singleton \(\{\tau_n^\sigma\}\). On \(V_n\), use the common auxiliary random variable to make the following conditional allocation, described by its unconditional masses inside \(V_n\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &\{\tau_n^+\}&\{\tau_n^-\}&\{\tau_n^+,\tau_n^-\}&\varnothing\\ \hline
\eta_n>2\alpha&\alpha&\alpha&\eta_n-2\alpha&0\\
\alpha<\eta_n\le2\alpha&\eta_n-\alpha&\eta_n-\alpha&0&2\alpha-\eta_n\\
\eta_n\le\alpha&0&0&0&\eta_n.
\end{array}
\]
Every row has nonnegative entries summing to \(\eta_n\), so division by \(\eta_n>0\) gives a legitimate conditional randomization. In the first two rows the miscoverage under either sign is exactly \(\alpha\); in the last it is \(\eta_n\le\alpha\). The only returned set with diameter at least \(\Delta_n\) is the two-target set, whose probability is \([\eta_n-2\alpha]_+\). This proves finite-sample sharpness within the pair.

For the deterministic separation, the function \(g\) is nondecreasing. Hence its right-endpoint Riemann sum dominates its integral:
\[
 \Delta_n=\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^n g(t/n)
 \ge2\int_0^1g(u)\,du
 =2\int_0^{1-a_\star}\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds.
\]
Continuity of \(g\) gives convergence of the same Riemann sums. If \(L_rx\le M\), direct integration gives \(d(x)=L_rx^2\). If \(L_rx>M\), splitting at \(M/L_r\) gives
\[
 d(x)=2\left\{\int_0^{M/L_r}L_rs\,ds
       +\int_{M/L_r}^xM\,ds\right\}
 =2Mx-M^2/L_r.
\]

Finally, because \(a_\star>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) every \(t>m_n\) exceeds \(c/\rho\), and thus the cap is inactive throughout the product. The elementary bound
\[
 \log(1-c/t)=-c/t+O(t^{-2})
\]
is uniform over that late range, where eventually \(c/t\le1/2\). Therefore
\[
 \log\eta_n
 =-c\sum_{t=m_n+1}^n t^{-1}
   +O\!\left(\sum_{t=m_n+1}^nt^{-2}\right)
 \longrightarrow c\log a_\star.
\]
Exponentiation gives \(\eta_n\to a_\star^c\).

%%% FIELD thm-optimized-clipped-ramp-frontier-statement
Assume \(L_r>0\), and define
\[
 d_{\max}=\begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
For every \(d\) with \(0<d<d_{\max}\), set
\[
 x(d)=\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{d/L_r},&d\le M^2/L_r,\\
 \{d+M^2/L_r\}/(2M),&d>M^2/L_r,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad a(d)=1-x(d).
\]
If a sequence of possibly randomized complete-log confidence sets is asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest uniformly over all experiments satisfying the declared structured assumptions, then, under each sign of the capped clipped-ramp member with start \(a(d)\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
The elbow \(d=M^2/L_r\) is interior to \((0,d_{\max})\) exactly when \(L_r>M\). Among two-sign late-void witnesses whose residual profiles vanish on \([0,a]\), are bounded in absolute value by \(M\), and have Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\), the clipped profile is pointwise optimal, and the displayed bound is the optimized late-void envelope. For each selected two-sign clipped-ramp subexperiment the bound is attained asymptotically by the exact randomized allocation, but no global minimax attainment over the full structured class is claimed.

%%% FIELD thm-optimized-clipped-ramp-frontier-proof
First consider any admissible two-sign late-void pair of the form \(\widetilde r^+=g\), \(\widetilde r^-=-g\), where \(g(u)=0\) on \([0,a]\), \(|g(u)|\le M\), and \(g\) is \(L_r\)-Lipschitz. For \(u>a\), the Lipschitz property and \(g(a)=0\) imply
\[
 |g(u)|\le L_r(u-a).
\]
Together with boundedness this gives the pointwise certificate
\[
 |g(u)|\le\min\{L_r(u-a)_+,M\}.
\]
The capped clipped ramp attains equality at every \(u\). Thus it maximizes the pointwise absolute two-sign separation among all such late-void witnesses, and
\[
 \left|2\int_0^1g(u)\,du\right|
 \le2\int_0^{1-a}\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds=d(1-a).
\]
This proves the claimed optimality from the primitive bound and Lipschitz conditions, rather than assuming it as part of the construction.

The function \(d(x)\) from \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}sign\mbox{-}clipped\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}tradeoff}\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,1]\). Its supremum over \(x<1\) is
\[
 d(1)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M,
 \end{cases}
 =d_{\max}.
\]
If \(L_r\le M\), then \(d_{\max}=L_r\le M^2/L_r\), so every \(d<d_{\max}\) lies on the quadratic branch and \(x(d)=\sqrt{d/L_r}\in(0,1)\). If \(L_r>M\), the clipping location \(x=M/L_r\) lies strictly inside \((0,1)\), and its image is \(M^2/L_r\). On the first branch, solving \(d=L_rx^2\) gives the displayed square-root formula. On the second, solving \(d=2Mx-M^2/L_r\) gives the displayed affine formula. In both cases \(x(d)\in(0,1)\), \(a(d)=1-x(d)\in(0,1)\), and
\[
 d(x(d))=d.
\]
This also proves that the elbow is interior exactly when \(L_r>M\).

Fix \(d\in(0,d_{\max})\) and select the capped clipped-ramp pair with \(a_\star=a(d)\). Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:capped\mbox{-}clipped\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}membership}\) puts both signs in the declared structured class. Define
\[
 \gamma_n=max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
Uniform asymptotic honesty implies, in particular for this fixed pair,
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.
\]
The sharp clipped-ramp theorem gives \(\Delta_n\ge d(x(d))=d\), \(\eta_n\to a(d)^c\), and
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
Since the event on the left is contained in \(\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}\), monotonicity and continuity of the positive-part map imply
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[a(d)^c-2\alpha]_+
 =[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]

The exact allocation in the sharp clipped-ramp theorem has maximum miscoverage at most \(\alpha\), and its only nonsingleton nonempty output is the two-target set of diameter \(\Delta_n\ge d\). Hence under either sign
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+
 \longrightarrow[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
This proves attainment inside the selected pair only.

To see that the envelope is optimized over the late-void witness method, consider any admissible start \(a'=1-x'\) whose limiting causal separation is at least \(d\). The pointwise certificate already proved gives \(d\le d(x')\). Strict monotonicity of \(d(\cdot)\) therefore forces \(x'\ge x(d)\), and hence
\[
 (a')^c=(1-x')^c\le(1-x(d))^c.
\]
Thus no admissible late-void profile with the same \(M,L_r\) constraints produces a larger common-void lower envelope at diameter \(d\).

As a check on the required exact ramp, take \(c=a_\star=1/2\), \(L_r=1/2\), and \(M\ge1/4\). Then the cap may be chosen with \(\rho\ge1/2\), the clipped profile equals \(\sigma(u-1/2)_+/2\), and
\[
 d(1/2)=1/8,
 \qquad
 [(1/2)^{1/2}-2(0.05)]_+
 =0.6071067811865475\ldots.
\]
Thus the optimized theorem preserves the \(1/8\) and \(0.60710678\) early-kill constants. It changes neither the positive calibration theorem nor its separate high-hazard radius bound.

Therefore every sequence of possibly randomized complete-log confidence sets that is asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest uniformly over that baseline class inherits, for every \(0<d<d_{\max}\) and each sign of the corresponding clipped-ramp pair,

%%% FIELD prop-li-zhao-optimized-clipped-frontier-proof
The sourced node \(\mathrm{lem:li\mbox{-}zhao\mbox{-}baseline\mbox{-}adaptive\mbox{-}class}\) records the relevant baseline requirements. In the clipped-ramp construction,
\[
 e_{nt}(1)=\min\{\rho,c/t\},
 \qquad
 e_{nt}(0)=1-e_{nt}(1).
\]
They are deterministic functions of every history, strictly positive, strictly below one, and sum to one. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:capped\mbox{-}clipped\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}membership}\) proves fixed bounded potential outcomes and predictable zero regression. Thus every such array belongs to the published baseline binary adaptive class.

We verify rather than assume failure of the Gaussian negligible-increment requirement. Put
\[
 g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\},
 \qquad
 X_{nt}=(A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{\sigma g(t/n)}{e_{nt}}.
\]
By predictable randomization,
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})
 =g(t/n)^2\{e_{nt}^{-1}-1\}.
\]
Distinct increments are orthogonal by iterated conditioning. For all sufficiently large \(n\), the cap is inactive whenever \(g(t/n)>0\). Hence, if \(W_n\) is the variance of the \(\sqrt n\)-scaled scalar AIPW error,
\[
 W_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n g(t/n)^2(t/c-1),
\]
and Riemann convergence gives
\[
 \frac{W_n}{n}\longrightarrow
 w_\star:=\frac1c\int_{a_\star}^1u g(u)^2\,du>0.
\]
At \(t=n\), the outcome envelope is at least
\(g(1)=\min\{L_r(1-a_\star),M\}>0\), the regression envelope is zero, and the smallest binary assignment probability is \(c/n\) for large \(n\). Therefore the scalar negligible-maximum ratio appearing in the sourced Condition 3(ii) satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 W_n^{-1}n^{-1}
 \max_{1\le t\le n}
 \frac{(L_t+M_t)^2}{\underline e_t^2}
 \ge\frac{g(1)^2}{c^2w_\star}>0.
\]
It cannot be \(o(1)\). At the unprojected two-arm vector level, the control contribution is identically zero, so the covariance also has a zero control row and column. This is consistent with the obstruction recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:li\mbox{-}zhao\mbox{-}lindeberg\mbox{-}premise}\).

Uniform honesty over Li and Zhao's class implies honesty on every clipped two-sign subclass by inclusion. Applying \(\mathrm{thm:optimized\mbox{-}clipped\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}frontier}\) yields the displayed lower bound. That argument constructs no procedure on the larger class, so it transfers neither pairwise attainment nor the structured positive calibration theorem.

Let
\[
 \gamma_n=max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
Because \(\gamma_n=1-\min_\sigma\Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\in D_n\}\), the honesty premise gives
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}sign\mbox{-}late\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}tradeoff}\), for each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+,
 \qquad
 \eta_n\to a_\star^c,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)
                 \ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 &\ge[a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+\\
 &\ge a_\star^c-2\alpha.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last displayed lower bound is positive exactly when \(a_\star^c>2\alpha\). The matching rule supplied by the sharp theorem attains the positive-part expression only within the selected two-sign late-ramp subexperiment. The proof uses the common component of the two complete logged experiments and does not invoke a Horvitz--Thompson or AIPW limit law.

%%% FIELD prop-li-zhao-baseline-frontier-proof
The sourced node \(\mathrm{lem:li\mbox{-}zhao\mbox{-}baseline\mbox{-}adaptive\mbox{-}class}\) gives the baseline requirements. In the binary parameterized ramp,
\[
 e_{nt}(1)=c/t,
 \qquad
 e_{nt}(0)=1-c/t.
\]
Because \(0<c<1\), these probabilities are deterministic, strictly positive, and sum to one. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:parameterized\mbox{-}late\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}membership}\) proves that the potential outcomes are fixed and bounded and that the zero regression is predictable. Hence every parameterized late-ramp array lies in Li and Zhao's baseline binary finite-population adaptive-design class.

It does not meet their Gaussian Condition 3(ii). At the vector level the control residual is identically zero, so the control coordinate of the AIPW contribution is identically zero and the scaled covariance has a zero row and column. Thus its smallest eigenvalue is zero and the inverse-eigenvalue expression in the sourced condition cannot be \(o(1)\). The obstruction also persists for the scalar treated-minus-control contrast. Put
\[
 X_{nt}=(A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{r^\sigma(t/n)}{e_{nt}}.
\]
Predictable randomization and iterated conditioning give
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})
 =\{r^\sigma(t/n)\}^2(t/c-1),
\]
and increments at distinct times are orthogonal. If \(W_n\) is the variance of the \(\sqrt n\)-scaled scalar AIPW error, then
\[
 W_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n
 \{r^\sigma(t/n)\}^2(t/c-1).
\]
With \(u_t=t/n\) and
\(r^\sigma(u)^2=\delta^2(u-a_\star)_+^2/(1-a_\star)^2\), Riemann convergence yields
\[
 \frac{W_n}{n}\longrightarrow
 w_\star
 :=\frac{\delta^2}{c(1-a_\star)^2}
   \int_{a_\star}^1u(u-a_\star)^2\,du
 =\frac{\delta^2(a_\star+3)(1-a_\star)}{12c}>0.
\]
At \(t=n\), the outcome envelope is at least \(\delta\), the regression envelope is zero, and the smallest binary assignment probability is \(c/n\) for large \(n\). Therefore
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 W_n^{-1}n^{-1}
 \max_{1\le t\le n}
 \frac{(L_t+M_t)^2}{\underline e_t^2}
 \ge\frac{\delta^2}{c^2w_\star}
 =\frac{12}{c(a_\star+3)(1-a_\star)}>0.
\]
This is the nonnegligible-increment failure recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:li\mbox{-}zhao\mbox{-}lindeberg\mbox{-}premise}\).

If confidence sets are uniformly asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest over Li and Zhao's baseline class, subclass inclusion makes them honest under both signs of every compatible parameterized late-ramp pair. Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:parameterized\mbox{-}late\mbox{-}ramp\mbox{-}frontier}\) then gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)
                 \ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 \ge a_\star^c-2\alpha.
\]
Only the converse transfers. The argument constructs no confidence set on the larger class and proves neither global minimax sharpness nor an extension of the positive profile-net calibration theorem.
