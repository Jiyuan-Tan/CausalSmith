\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} At the critical rare-arm boundary, the finite-population AIPW error need not be Gaussian. On this note's original structured binary profile subclass, a stopped adaptive-to-Bernoulli-to-Poisson transfer and directed-rational convolution give a computable atom-safe interval with uniform randomization coverage and explicit oracle slack. Separately, the retained constant-intensity clipped-ramp theorem optimizes the residual late-void obstruction under honesty for the selected pair; its eventual profile exactness is not an unconditional structured-class converse. A distinct pointwise-minimal intensity \(q_\star(u)=\max\{c-L_qu,c/2\}\) yields the jointly optimized pair-honesty envelope \(\left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+\), with residual and intensity elbows that may occur in either order. \section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Li and Zhao's baseline adaptive-design class permits positive history-dependent assignment probabilities, but their Gaussian AIPW theorem requires a negligible-maximum condition that fails at bounded cumulative hazard. Existing adaptive and i.i.d. width results neither calibrate the signed atomic compound-Poisson law nor jointly optimize the complete-log common-void obstruction. Moreover, eventual disappearance of an early propensity cap does not imply the every-\(n\) profile contract needed to turn either selected capped witness into an unconditional structured-class converse.

\paragraph{Niche.} The boundary has distinct positive and converse deliverables. Structured deterministic profiles with the supplied every-\(n\) contract support computable atom-safe calibration. For information limits, the constant-intensity clipped ramp optimizes residual separation under selected-pair honesty, while \(q_\star(u)=\max\{c-L_qu,c/2\}\) is pointwise minimal in \(\mathcal Q\) and maximizes late-void mass for each fixed start. Combining the latter intensity with the clipped residual gives a separate pair-honesty frontier with both intensity and residual elbows.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project supplies the finite adaptive-to-Poisson transfer and profile-net interval theorem on the original structured subclass, the exact constant-intensity clipped-ramp pair tradeoff, and a separate exact intensity-optimized two-sign tradeoff. Both optimized asymptotic envelopes are stated under honesty for the selected pair because the corresponding membership certificates guarantee \(G_n=\Omega\) only eventually, not the profile contract for every \(n\). Direct every-\(n\) inclusion arguments transfer converse conclusions to Li and Zhao's baseline class; pairwise attainment and positive calibration retain their separate scopes.

\section{Related work}

Li and Zhao (2026) provide the closest design-based setup: fixed finite-population potential outcomes, positive assignment probabilities that may be prespecified functions of history, and predictable AIPW regression. Their baseline binary class contains both the constant-intensity clipped ramps and the separate intensity-optimized arrays for every \(n\), but their Theorem 4(ii) assumes Condition 3; both bounded-hazard witnesses fail its negligible-maximum part because late standardized increments remain nonnegligible. The selected-pair late-void converses and their direct baseline-class transfers are logically separate from this note's structured-class positive calibration. Only converse bounds transfer; matching confidence-set constructions remain sharp inside selected two-sign experiments. Bibaut and Kallus (2025) organize adaptive inference into Gaussian repair, always-valid methods, and direct inversion of nonnormal laws. Waudby-Smith et al. (2024), Freidling, Zhao, and Gao (2026), and Cho, Bibaut, and Kallus (2025) do not supply the present finite-population signed atomic calibration or jointly optimized common-void envelope. Chen et al. (2026) obtain a compound-Poisson HT law under independent Poisson importance sampling, while Hong, Leung, and Li (2020) treat limited overlap with diverging effective information. Shekhar and Ramdas (2023) prove i.i.d. bounded-mean width bounds. Barbour and Xia (1999) support compound-Poisson error accounting, and Bhattacharjee and Goldstein (2019) identify the generalized Dickman constant-profile reduction. Liberali et al. (2025) remain the applied consumer, conditional on a prespecified expected-endpoint bridge for GUSTO-1 and EUROPA.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-outcomes]\label{ass:fixed-outcomes}
The potential-outcome array \(Y\) is fixed under the randomization law.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population design-based conditioning, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
For every \(n,t,a\), \(|Y_{nt}(a)|\le M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded finite-population potential outcomes, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:adaptive-assignment-law]\label{ass:adaptive-assignment-law}
For every \(n,t\), \(\Pr(A_{nt}=1\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=e_{nt}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Predictable adaptive randomization, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-range]\label{ass:propensity-range}
For every \(n,t\), \(0<e_{nt}\le\rho<1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (One-sided uniform overlap for the nonrare arm, \cite{HongLeungLi2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictable-regression]\label{ass:predictable-regression}
For every \(n,t,a\), \(m_{nt}(a)\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\)-measurable.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Adaptive outcome-regression predictability, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-regression]\label{ass:bounded-regression}
For every \(n,t,a\), \(|m_{nt}(a)|\le M_m\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded adaptive outcome regression, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:intensity-anchor]\label{ass:intensity-anchor}
The intensity profile satisfies \(\bar q(0)=c\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A prespecified critical allocation schedule can fix its initial exploration mass, as in clipped inverse-time exploration rules.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:intensity-bounds]\label{ass:intensity-bounds}
For every \(u\in[0,1]\), \(c/2\le\bar q(u)\le K\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded positive intensity profiles include inverse-time exploration, late ramps, and affine exploration floors used in adaptive allocation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:intensity-lipschitz]\label{ass:intensity-lipschitz}
For every \(u,v\in[0,1]\), \(|\bar q(u)-\bar q(v)|\le L_q|u-v|\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Daily or batched allocation probabilities generated from clipped smooth scores have piecewise-Lipschitz expected intensity trajectories.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-bounds]\label{ass:residual-bounds}
For every \(u\in[0,1]\), \(|r(u)|\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded outcomes and bounded prespecified endpoint predictions imply a bounded residual trajectory.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-lipschitz]\label{ass:residual-lipschitz}
For every \(u,v\in[0,1]\), \(|r(u)-r(v)|\le L_r|u-v|\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A prespecified expected-endpoint bridge under gradual calendar drift yields a uniformly continuous residual trajectory with an auditable modulus.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:profile-contract]\label{ass:profile-contract}
The event \(G_n\) satisfies \(\Pr(G_n)\ge1-\kappa_n\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The contract is checkable for adaptive rules with a prespecified expected-endpoint bridge and deterministic uniform moduli; it makes the approximation target fixed before the assignment path is realized.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:certified-inputs]\label{ass:certified-inputs}
Every numerical input to \(\mathsf{Cert}_n\) and \(\mathsf{FastCert}_n\) is rational or is supplied with a certified rational interval enclosure.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Logged probabilities and trial protocol constants are stored at finite precision, and elementary logarithms admit directed rational interval evaluation.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:logged-enclosure]\label{lem:logged-enclosure}
On \(G_n\), the true intensity profile \(\bar q\) belongs to the completed-log enclosure \(\mathcal Q_n(e)\). Moreover, every \(q'\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\) satisfies \(\|q'-\bar q\|_{\infty}\le d_{Q,n}=2\delta_{q,n}+2L_q(\varepsilon_n+1/n)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(t_0=\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\).  On \(G_n\), the first component in the definition of \(G_n\) gives
\[
 \left|\bar q(t/n)-t e_{nt}\right|\leq\delta_{q,n},
 \qquad t_0\leq t\leq n.
\]
The three defining properties of \(\mathcal Q\) follow respectively from
\(\mathrm{ass:intensity\mbox{-}anchor}\),
\(\mathrm{ass:intensity\mbox{-}bounds}\), and
\(\mathrm{ass:intensity\mbox{-}lipschitz}\).  Hence the displayed inequalities are exactly the remaining membership constraints in \(\mathcal Q_n(e)\), proving
\(\bar q\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\) on \(G_n\).

Fix \(q'\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\).  At every constrained grid point,
\[
 |q'(t/n)-\bar q(t/n)|
 \leq |q'(t/n)-t e_{nt}|+|t e_{nt}-\bar q(t/n)|
 \leq 2\delta_{q,n}.
\]
If \(u\geq t_0/n\), choose a constrained grid point \(t/n\) with
\(|u-t/n|\leq n^{-1}\).  The two Lipschitz inequalities yield
\[
 |q'(u)-\bar q(u)|
 \leq 2\delta_{q,n}+2L_q/n.
\]
If \(u<t_0/n\), both profiles equal \(c\) at zero, and therefore
\[
 |q'(u)-\bar q(u)|
 \leq |q'(u)-c|+|\bar q(u)-c|
 \leq 2L_q u
 \leq 2L_q(\varepsilon_n+n^{-1}).
\]
Both bounds are at most
\(2\delta_{q,n}+2L_q(\varepsilon_n+n^{-1})=d_{Q,n}\), which proves the asserted uniform enclosure.  No conditioning on the completed log is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This converts the completed propensity log into a simultaneous deterministic enclosure without conditioning away assignment dependence. \textit{Gap.} FreidlingZhaoGao2026 uses exact selection-event conditioning; no located result supplies a shrinking Lipschitz intensity enclosure for an approximate critical law. \textit{Consumer.} The deterministic inclusion is the selection-safety input for the certified radius used in the GUSTO-1 and EUROPA bridge analysis.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bandwidth-vanishing]\label{lem:bandwidth-vanishing}
For every \(n\), the directed-dyadic bandwidth satisfies \(x_n\le h_n\le x_n+2^{-n}\). Uniformly over the declared class, \(x_n\to0\) and hence \(h_n\to0\). In particular, \(0\le5(h_n-x_n)\le5\cdot2^{-n}\), so dyadic rounding inflates the reported radius \(a_n+5h_n\) by at most \(5\cdot2^{-n}\) relative to \(a_n+5x_n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every real \(x\), \(x\leq 2^{-n}\lceil2^n x\rceil<x+2^{-n}\).  Applying this deterministic inequality to \(x_n\) gives
\[
 x_n\leq h_n\leq x_n+2^{-n}.
\]
It remains to verify \(x_n\to0\) from the primitive sequences.  Write
\(\varepsilon_n^\star=\max\{\varepsilon_n,\delta_{q,n},n^{-1/2}\}\).
Under the corrected cutoff \(T_n\leq n+1\), one has \(0<\ell_n\leq1\);
since \(\varepsilon_n^\star\to0\), the cap is inactive eventually and
\[
 \ell_n=\frac{T_n-1}{n}\leq
 \varepsilon_n^\star+n^{-1},\qquad
 \ell_n\geq \varepsilon_n^\star-n^{-1}.
\]
In particular, eventually \(\ell_n\geq (2\sqrt n)^{-1}\), so
\(\log(1/\ell_n)=O(\log n)\) and
\((n\ell_n)^{-1}=O(n^{-1/2})\).

Let \(t_0=\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\).  Since
\(\sum_{t=t_0}^{T_n-1}t\leq T_n^2/2\),
\[
 v_{E,n}\leq v_{0,n}+\frac{2B^2}{c}(T_n/n)^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Also \(v_{C,n}=O(n^{-1})\) and
\(v_{Z,n}\leq B^2(\varepsilon_n^\star+n^{-1})^2/c\to0\).
The definitions and \(\delta_{r,n},\delta_{q,n}\to0\) give
\[
 D_{\mathrm{mark},n}\to0,\qquad d_{Q,n}\to0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 0\leq D_{\mathrm{grid},n}
 \leq n^{-1}\{L_jK\,O(\log n)+L_r\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Every summand in the definition of \(x_n\) therefore tends to zero, uniformly because all constants and modulus sequences are common to the declared class.  Thus \(x_n\to0\), and the dyadic bracket implies \(h_n\to0\).  Finally,
\[
 0\leq5(h_n-x_n)\leq5\cdot2^{-n},
\]
which is the claimed deterministic radius charge.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit dyadic bracket makes the smoothing choice effective and isolates its finite numerical contribution to interval radius. \textit{Gap.} The nearby adaptive-inference results do not track directed rounding of an atom-safe smoothing bandwidth through an oracle-radius comparison. \textit{Consumer.} The coverage theorem and clinical-log implementation receive an auditable \(5\cdot2^{-n}\) radius charge rather than an unspecified approximation error.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-transfer]\label{thm:finite-transfer}
For every \(n\) with \(T_n>K\) and \(\delta_{q,n}\le c/4\), there is an extension of the randomization probability space carrying \(Z_{r,\bar q}\) such that \(\Pr(G_n^c\cup\{|S_n-Z_{r,\bar q}|>4h_n\})\le D_{\mathrm{tr},n}\), where every term of \(D_{\mathrm{tr},n}\) is the declared computable budget and \(D_{\mathrm{tr},n}\to0\) uniformly over all experiments satisfying the declared assumptions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppress the first index \(n\), put \(u_t=t/n\), \(p_t=\bar q(u_t)/t\), and
\[
 j(u)=\frac{u r(u)}{\bar q(u)}.
\]
The restrictions \(t\geq T_n>K\geq\bar q(u_t)\) imply \(0<p_t<1\).
We construct all comparisons sequentially on an extension of the original randomization space.

\emph{Predictable Bernoulli comparison.}
For two Bernoulli probabilities \(e,p\), the common-uniform construction has mismatch probability \(|e-p|\).  Applying it at time \(t\), conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\), and using fresh independent uniforms gives variables \(B_t\) such that the \(B_t\)'s are independent with
\(\Pr(B_t=1)=p_t\), while
\[
 \Pr(A_{nt}\ne B_t\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=|e_{nt}-p_t|.
\]
Let \(\sigma_q\) be the first \(t\geq T_n\) for which
\(|t e_{nt}-\bar q(u_t)|>\delta_{q,n}\), with value \(n+1\) if there is no such time.  This is a predictable stopping rule because \(e_{nt}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\)-measurable and \(\bar q\) is deterministic.  Before \(\sigma_q\),
\[
 |e_{nt}-p_t|\leq\delta_{q,n}/t.
\]
Consequently, by the union bound and iterated expectation,
\[
 \Pr\{\exists t\geq T_n:t<\sigma_q,\ A_{nt}\ne B_t\}
 \leq\delta_{q,n}\sum_{t=T_n}^n t^{-1}
 =\delta_{q,n}H_{1,n}.
\]
On \(G_n\), \(\sigma_q=n+1\).

\emph{Replacement of predictable marks.}
On \(G_n\), for \(t\geq T_n\) the inequalities
\(\bar q(u_t)\geq c/2\) and
\(\delta_{q,n}\leq c/4\) give \(t e_{nt}\geq c/4\).
Using \(|r_{nt}(1)-r(u_t)|\leq\delta_{r,n}\) and
\(|r(u_t)|\leq B\),
\[
 \left|\frac{r_{nt}(1)}{n e_{nt}}-j(u_t)\right|
 \leq u_t\left\{\frac{4\delta_{r,n}}{c}
              +\frac{8B\delta_{q,n}}{c^2}\right\}.
\]
On \(G_n\) and on the no-mismatch event, the difference between the late rare-arm martingale and
\(\sum_{t=T_n}^n(B_t-p_t)j(u_t)\) is bounded by
\[
 \left\{\frac{4\delta_{r,n}}{c}
              +\frac{8B\delta_{q,n}}{c^2}\right\}
       \sum_{t=T_n}^nB_tu_t+\delta_{r,n},
\]
because \(p_tj(u_t)=r(u_t)/n\).  Since
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{t=T_n}^n B_tu_t
 =\frac1n\sum_{t=T_n}^n\bar q(u_t)\leq K,
\]
the expectation of this nonnegative error envelope is at most
\[
 (1+4K/c)\delta_{r,n}+(8KB/c^2)\delta_{q,n}
 =D_{\mathrm{mark},n}.
\]

\emph{Bernoulli-to-Poisson comparison.}
For \(0\leq p\leq1\), direct comparison of the masses of
\(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) and \(\operatorname{Poisson}(p)\) gives total variation
\[
 p(1-e^{-p})\leq p^2.
\]
Indeed, the positive discrepancies of the Poisson law occur at zero and at counts at least two, and their sum is \(p(1-e^{-p})\).  Coupling independently coordinate by coordinate and taking a union bound therefore replaces the \(B_t\)'s by independent \(P_t\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(p_t)\) with failure probability at most
\[
 \sum_{t=T_n}^n p_t^2\leq K^2H_{2,n}\leq2K^2H_{2,n}.
\]

\emph{Transfer from the grid to the Poisson random measure.}
For \(I_t=((t-1)/n,t/n]\), put
\(\lambda_t=\int_{I_t}\bar q(v)\,dv/v\).  For \(v\in I_t\),
\[
 \left|\frac{\bar q(v)}v-\frac{\bar q(u_t)}{u_t}\right|
 \leq \frac1n\left(\frac{L_q}{v}+\frac K{v^2}\right).
\]
Integration and summation over \((\ell_n,1]\) yield
\[
 \sum_{t=T_n}^n|\lambda_t-p_t|
 \leq n^{-1}\{L_q\log(1/\ell_n)+K(1/\ell_n-1)\}
 =I_{\mathrm{grid},n}.
\]
Two Poisson variables of means \(\lambda,\mu\) can be constructed from a common
\(\operatorname{Poisson}(\min\{\lambda,\mu\})\) count and independent excess counts; the probability of unequal counts is
\(1-e^{-|\lambda-\mu|}\leq|\lambda-\mu|\).
Thus the count vectors can be coupled with failure probability at most
\(I_{\mathrm{grid},n}\).  Conditional on the matched cell count, place the points with density proportional to \(\bar q(v)/v\) in that cell.

For \(u,v\in[0,1]\), the bounds on \(\bar q,r\) and their Lipschitz moduli give
\[
 |j(u)-j(v)|
 \leq\left\{\frac{2(B+L_r)}c+\frac{4BL_q}{c^2}\right\}|u-v|
 =L_j|u-v|.
\]
Therefore the expected absolute mark-location error is at most
\[
 \sum_{t=T_n}^n\int_{I_t}L_j|u_t-v|\frac{\bar q(v)}v\,dv
 \leq \frac{L_jK}{n}\log(1/\ell_n).
\]
The right-endpoint Riemann bound for the compensating drift is
\[
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{t=T_n}^nr(u_t)-\int_{\ell_n}^1r(v)\,dv\right|
 \leq L_r/n.
\]
Hence the grid mark and drift error has expectation at most
\(D_{\mathrm{grid},n}\).  Markov's inequality, proved by
\(\mathbb E X\geq h\Pr(X>h)\) for \(X\geq0\), allocates failure probability
\((D_{\mathrm{mark},n}+D_{\mathrm{grid},n})/h_n\) to the combined mark and grid error exceeding \(h_n\).

\emph{Early rare-arm, control-arm, and omitted Poisson terms.}
Conditional orthogonality of martingale differences gives
\[
 \mathbb E S_{n,\mathrm{control}}^2
 =\mathbb E\sum_{t=1}^n
 \frac{e_{nt}r_{nt}(0)^2}{n^2(1-e_{nt})}
 \leq\frac{B^2\rho}{n(1-\rho)}
 =v_{C,n}.
\]
Stop the early rare-arm martingale immediately before an increment whose predictable variance would make its accumulated bracket exceed the declared budget.  The stopped martingale agrees with the original one on \(G_n\).  The part before
\(t_0=\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\) has bracket at most \(v_{0,n}\) there.  For \(t_0\leq t<T_n\), stop also before the propensity-profile constraint fails.  On the retained path, \(e_{nt}\geq c/(4t)\), whence
\[
 e_{nt}(1-e_{nt})
 \left\{\frac{r_{nt}(1)}{n e_{nt}}\right\}^2
 \leq\frac{4B^2t}{cn^2}.
\]
Thus the stopped early rare-arm martingale has second moment at most \(v_{E,n}\).

For a Poisson random measure, the expectation and variance identities used here follow first for a simple integrand from independence and
\(\operatorname{Var}\{\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\}=\lambda\), and then for the present integrands by \(L^1\) and \(L^2\) approximation on \((\epsilon,1]\) followed by \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).  In particular, the centered omitted integral on \((0,\ell_n]\) has variance
\[
 \int_0^{\ell_n}\frac{u^2r(u)^2}{\bar q(u)^2}
                 \frac{\bar q(u)}u\,du
 =\int_0^{\ell_n}\frac{u r(u)^2}{\bar q(u)}\,du
 \leq \frac{B^2\ell_n^2}{c}=v_{Z,n}.
\]
For any centered square-integrable \(X\),
\(\Pr(|X|>h_n)\leq\mathbb EX^2/h_n^2\), by applying the preceding Markov argument to \(X^2\).  A union bound therefore charges
\((v_{E,n}+v_{C,n}+v_{Z,n})/h_n^2\) for these three terms.  On the complement of all charged events, the mark-grid error and the three centered remainder terms are each at most \(h_n\); hence
\(|S_n-Z_{r,\bar q}|\leq4h_n\).  Adding \(\Pr(G_n^c)\leq\kappa_n\) and all coupling failures proves exactly
\[
 \Pr\!\left(G_n^c\cup\{|S_n-Z_{r,\bar q}|>4h_n\}\right)
 \leq D_{\mathrm{tr},n}.
\]

Finally, every displayed term is an explicit finite sum or elementary expression in the supplied bounds.  To prove uniform convergence, note that eventually
\[
 H_{1,n}\leq1+\log(1/\ell_n),\qquad
 H_{2,n}\leq(n\ell_n)^{-1}=O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
and
\[
 \delta_{q,n}\log(1/\ell_n)\to0,\qquad
 I_{\mathrm{grid},n}\to0.
\]
The first limit follows because \(\ell_n\) is eventually of order at least
\(\max\{\varepsilon_n,\delta_{q,n},n^{-1/2}\}\), using
\(\delta\log(1/\delta)\to0\) at zero and the assumed
\(\delta_{q,n}\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0\).
The estimates in the bandwidth proof give
\(D_{\mathrm{mark},n}+D_{\mathrm{grid},n}\to0\) and
\(V_n:=v_{E,n}+v_{C,n}+v_{Z,n}\to0\).  Since
\[
 h_n\geq\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{mark},n}+D_{\mathrm{grid},n}},
 \qquad h_n\geq V_n^{1/4},
\]
the two ratio terms are bounded by
\(\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{mark},n}+D_{\mathrm{grid},n}}\) and
\(\sqrt{V_n}\), respectively.  Together with \(\kappa_n\to0\), this proves
\(D_{\mathrm{tr},n}\to0\) uniformly over the declared class.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:profile-continuity]\label{lem:profile-continuity}
For \(q,q'\in\mathcal Q\) and \(r,r'\in\mathcal R\), a common Poisson construction satisfies \(W_1(\mathcal L(Z_{r,q}),\mathcal L(Z_{r',q'}))\le(2K/c+1)\|r-r'\|_{\infty}+(4KB/c^2+2B/c)\|q-q'\|_{\infty}\); consequently the corresponding ramp risks differ by at most this bound divided by \(h_n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(d_r=\|r-r'\|_\infty\) and \(d_q=\|q-q'\|_\infty\).
Use a Poisson random measure on
\((0,1]\times[0,K]\) with intensity \(du\,dv/u\).  A point \((u,v)\) is retained for profile \(q\) when \(v\leq q(u)\), and for \(q'\) when \(v\leq q'(u)\).  This is a common construction of the two intensity measures.

At a point retained by both processes,
\[
 \left|\frac{ur(u)}{q(u)}-\frac{ur'(u)}{q'(u)}\right|
 \leq u\left(\frac{2d_r}{c}+\frac{4B d_q}{c^2}\right).
\]
Integrating this bound against the dominating intensity \(K\,du/u\) gives an expected common-point cost at most
\[
 \frac{2K}{c}d_r+\frac{4KB}{c^2}d_q.
\]
At a point retained by only one process, the total expected absolute mark cost from both one-sided excess regions is bounded by
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{2B}{c}|q(u)-q'(u)|\,du
 \leq\frac{2B}{c}d_q.
\]
These integrals are finite at zero.  Construct first on \((\epsilon,1]\) and let
\(\epsilon\downarrow0\); the expected omitted absolute raw-mark mass is at most
\(\int_0^\epsilon B\,du=B\epsilon\), so the coupled raw sums converge in \(L^1\).
The compensating drifts are \(\int_0^1r(u)\,du\) and
\(\int_0^1r'(u)\,du\), whose difference is at most \(d_r\).
Therefore this coupling satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E|Z_{r,q}-Z_{r',q'}|
 \leq\left(\frac{2K}{c}+1\right)d_r
 +\left(\frac{4KB}{c^2}+\frac{2B}{c}\right)d_q.
\]
Taking the infimum over couplings proves the asserted \(W_1\) bound.
Finally \(H_{b,h_n}\) is \(h_n^{-1}\)-Lipschitz, so for the displayed coupling
\[
 \left|\mathbb EH_{b,h_n}(Z_{r,q})
       -\mathbb EH_{b,h_n}(Z_{r',q'})\right|
 \leq h_n^{-1}\mathbb E|Z_{r,q}-Z_{r',q'}|,
\]
which proves the ramp-risk conclusion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This quantitative continuity makes exhaustive profile nets and directed convolution a finite certificate rather than a compactness assertion. \textit{Gap.} Classical compound-Poisson Stein bounds do not provide this near-zero real signed-mark profile metric in the logged adaptive calibration problem. \textit{Consumer.} The bound allocates profile discretization error and yields a terminating certification mesh.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certified-coverage-oracle]\label{thm:certified-coverage-oracle}
The algorithm \(\mathsf{Cert}_n\) terminates for every certified input. Uniformly over all experiments satisfying the declared assumptions, the reported interval obeys \(\Pr(\tau_n\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha\) for every \(n\), where infeasible budgets trigger \([-2M,2M]\). Moreover, for every \(\eta\in(0,\alpha)\) and \(s>0\), uniformly over the fixed compact profile classes, on \(G_n\) and for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(b_{\alpha}(\bar q)\le b_n+h_n\le b_{\alpha-\eta}(\bar q)+s+5\cdot2^{-n}\). The last term is the complete radius charge for replacing \(x_n\) by its directed-dyadic upper bracket \(h_n\). This covers the declared \(e_{nt}\asymp\bar q(t/n)/t\) profiles that fall outside the Lindeberg condition used for Gaussian coverage in LiZhao2026, Theorem 4(ii).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This proof uses the corrected dyadic threshold search in
\(\mathrm{def:certificate\mbox{-}algorithm}\).

\emph{Finite termination.}
The pointwise bounds and adjacent Lipschitz constraints on a time mesh form a finite system of rational interval inequalities.  Subdivision of the value boxes and piecewise-linear interpolation therefore gives finite outer sup-norm nets of
\(\mathcal Q_n(e)\) and \(\mathcal R\) at any prescribed rational accuracy.
For a representative pair, truncate the Poisson integral on \((0,\gamma]\).
The centered omitted term has second moment at most
\[
 \int_0^\gamma\frac{u r(u)^2}{q(u)}\,du
 \leq B^2\gamma^2/c,
\]
and hence first absolute moment at most \(B\gamma/\sqrt c\).
On \([\gamma,1]\), the total Poisson intensity is at most
\(K\log(1/\gamma)<\infty\).  Quantize time and signed marks, truncate the total count, and bound the remainder by the elementary Poisson-series inequality
\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)\geq m+1\}
 \leq\frac{\Lambda^{m+1}}
 {(m+1)!\{1-\Lambda/(m+2)\}},
 \qquad m+2>\Lambda.
\]
This follows by bounding every successive ratio in the tail series by
\(\Lambda/(m+2)\).  The continuity lemma charges the finite profile-net error.
Thus, by successively decreasing \(\gamma\) and the quantization meshes and increasing \(m\), all outward errors can be made no larger than the positive rational budget \(\zeta_n\).  Every remaining convolution is finite and uses directed rational arithmetic; logarithms are enclosed by their convergent rational series.

If a guard in the definition fires, the algorithm stops immediately.  Otherwise
\(\Delta_n<\alpha\).  For every \(q'\in\mathcal Q\) and \(r'\in\mathcal R\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z_{r',q'})
 =\int_0^1\frac{u r'(u)^2}{q'(u)}\,du
 \leq B^2/c.
\]
Consequently
\(\mathbb EH_{b,h_n}(Z_{r',q'})
\leq\Pr(|Z_{r',q'}|>b)\leq B^2/(cb^2)\).
For a sufficiently large dyadic grid point this strict upper bound is below
\(\alpha-\Delta_n\), so a finite outward calculation certifies some grid point.
The corrected increasing grid search therefore terminates and returns a well-defined \(a_n\).

\emph{Selection-safe finite coverage.}
For deterministic \(q\), define
\[
 F_q(b)=\sup_{r'\in\mathcal R}
          \mathbb EH_{b,h_n}(Z_{r',q}),\qquad
 A_q=\inf\{b\geq0:F_q(b)\leq\alpha-\Delta_n\}.
\]
The ramp is nonincreasing in \(b\) and \(h_n^{-1}\)-Lipschitz in \(b\);
therefore \(F_q\) is continuous and nonincreasing.  The preceding variance bound makes its feasible set nonempty, so the infimum is attained.
On \(G_n\), the logged-enclosure lemma gives
\(\bar q\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\).  For each realized completed log on this event, the certificate at \(a_n\) implies
\[
 F_{\bar q}(a_n)\leq\alpha-\Delta_n,
\]
and hence \(a_n\geq A_{\bar q}\).  Notice that \(A_{\bar q}\) is deterministic for the fixed experiment; no conditional distribution given the completed log has been invoked.

On the coupling supplied by the finite-transfer theorem,
\[
 \{G_n,\ |S_n|>a_n+5h_n,\ |S_n-Z_{r,\bar q}|\leq4h_n\}
 \subseteq\{|Z_{r,\bar q}|>A_{\bar q}+h_n\}.
\]
At the right-hand event the ramp \(H_{A_{\bar q},h_n}\) equals one.  Since the actual deterministic residual profile belongs to \(\mathcal R\),
\[
 \Pr(|Z_{r,\bar q}|>A_{\bar q}+h_n)
 \leq F_{\bar q}(A_{\bar q})
 \leq\alpha-\Delta_n.
\]
The transfer theorem now gives
\[
 \Pr(|\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n|>a_n+5h_n)
 \leq D_{\mathrm{tr},n}+\alpha-\Delta_n
 =\alpha-\zeta_n\leq\alpha.
\]
If a guard fires, \(C_n=[-2M,2M]\); and
\(|\tau_n|\leq2M\) by the bounded-outcome assumption.  Otherwise the last display proves coverage of the untruncated interval.  Intersecting it with
\([-2M,2M]\) cannot remove \(\tau_n\), proving
\(\Pr(\tau_n\in C_n)\geq1-\alpha\) for every \(n\).

\emph{Oracle comparison.}
The certificate itself and \(\bar q\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\) imply
\[
 \sup_{r'\in\mathcal R}
 \Pr(|Z_{r',\bar q}|>a_n+h_n)
 \leq\sup_{r'\in\mathcal R}
 \mathbb EH_{a_n,h_n}(Z_{r',\bar q})
 \leq\alpha.
\]
Thus \(b_\alpha(\bar q)\leq a_n+h_n\leq b_n+h_n\).

Fix \(\eta\in(0,\alpha)\) and \(s>0\), and put
\(b_\star=b_{\alpha-\eta}(\bar q)\).
Monotone continuity of the tail from above shows that the infimum in the oracle definition is attained, so
\[
 \sup_{r'\in\mathcal R}\Pr(|Z_{r',\bar q}|>b_\star)
 \leq\alpha-\eta.
\]
Since \(H_{b_\star,h_n}\leq{\bf1}\{|z|>b_\star\}\), the same bound holds for its robust ramp risk.  On \(G_n\), every \(q'\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\) satisfies
\(\|q'-\bar q\|_\infty\leq d_{Q,n}\).  The profile-continuity lemma, uniformly in \(r'\), gives
\[
 \sup_{\substack{q'\in\mathcal Q_n(e)\\r'\in\mathcal R}}
 \mathbb EH_{b_\star,h_n}(Z_{r',q'})
 \leq\alpha-\eta+
 \left(\frac{4KB}{c^2}+\frac{2B}{c}\right)\frac{d_{Q,n}}{h_n}.
\]
Here \(d_{Q,n}/h_n\leq\sqrt{d_{Q,n}}\to0\);
also \(D_{\mathrm{tr},n}\to0\) and \(\zeta_n\to0\).
Hence, uniformly over the fixed profile classes, the outward certificate eventually accepts the grid point
\[
 \bar b_\star=2^{-n}\lceil2^n b_\star\rceil
 \leq b_\star+2^{-n}.
\]
Minimality on the corrected grid gives \(a_n\leq\bar b_\star\).  Since
\(h_n\leq x_n+2^{-n}\) and \(x_n\to0\), eventually
\(5x_n+2^{-n}\leq s\), and therefore
\[
 b_n+h_n=a_n+5h_n
 \leq b_\star+s+5\cdot2^{-n}.
\]
This proves the two-sided oracle statement and isolates exactly
\(5(h_n-x_n)\leq5\cdot2^{-n}\) as the dyadic bandwidth charge.

Finally, the cited Li--Zhao result uses a Lindeberg negligible-increment premise for its Gaussian conclusion.  The present class includes, for example,
\(\bar q\equiv c\), \(e_{nt}=c/t\), and a nonzero constant treated residual.
For \(t/n\) bounded away from zero, a treated rare-arm increment has magnitude asymptotic to a nonzero multiple of \(t/n\), while its probability is \(c/t\).
The sum of its truncated second moments over any fixed late interval is bounded away from zero.  Thus the negligible-increment condition fails, although the finite-transfer and atom-safe coverage proof above remains valid.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:high-hazard-radius]\label{prop:high-hazard-radius}

\begin{proposition}[prop:parameterized-late-ramp-membership]\label{prop:parameterized-late-ramp-membership}

\]
for every \(n\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\). By \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family}, the potential outcomes and regressions are the deterministic arrays
\[
 Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)=0,
 \qquad
 Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)=\sigma\delta\frac{(t/n-a_\star)_+}{1-a_\star},
 \qquad
 m_{nt}(0)=m_{nt}(1)=0.
\]
They are fixed under the randomization law, which verifies the condition stated in \texttt{ass:fixed-outcomes}. Since \(0\le (t/n-a_\star)_+/(1-a_\star)\le1\) and \(0<\delta\le M\),
\[
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)|=0,
 \qquad
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)|\le\delta\le M.
\]
Thus the boundedness condition in \texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes} holds. The zero regression is \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\)-measurable and obeys \(|m_{nt}(a)|=0\le M_m\), using the declared type \(M_m\ge0\); this verifies \texttt{ass:predictable-regression} and \texttt{ass:bounded-regression}.

The definition of the sequential design gives
\[
 \Pr_\sigma(A_{nt}=1\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=e_{nt}=\frac ct.
\]
This is the conditional law in \texttt{ass:adaptive-assignment-law}. Since \(t\ge1\), \(c\in(0,1)\), and \(c\le\rho<1\),
\[
 0<e_{nt}=\frac ct\le c\le\rho<1,
\]
which verifies \texttt{ass:propensity-range}; the complementary probability \(1-c/t\) is also strictly positive.

For all \(u,v\in[0,1]\), the constant intensity profile satisfies
\[
 \bar q^\sigma(0)=c,
 \qquad
 \frac c2\le \bar q^\sigma(u)=c\le K,
 \qquad
 |\bar q^\sigma(u)-\bar q^\sigma(v)|=0\le L_q|u-v|.
\]
These are precisely the three conditions in \texttt{def:intensity-class}, so \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\). The positive-part map is one-Lipschitz. Moreover, \(B=M+M_m\ge M\ge\delta\), and the compatibility restriction gives \(L_r\ge\delta/(1-a_\star)\). Therefore
\[
 |r^\sigma(u)|\le\delta\le B,
 \qquad
 |r^\sigma(u)-r^\sigma(v)|
 \le \frac{\delta}{1-a_\star}|u-v|
 \le L_r|u-v|.
\]
By \texttt{def:residual-class}, \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\).

It remains to verify the profile contract rather than use it as a premise. From \(r_{nt}(1)=Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)-m_{nt}(1)\) and the displayed construction,
\[
 |t e_{nt}-\bar q^\sigma(t/n)|=|c-c|=0,
 \qquad
 |r_{nt}(1)-r^\sigma(t/n)|=0
\]
for every \(n,t\). Because \(\varepsilon_n\downarrow0\) and \(a_\star>0\), there is \(N_0\) such that \(\varepsilon_n<a_\star\) whenever \(n\ge N_0\). If \(n\ge N_0\) and \(t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\), then
\[
 \frac tn\le \frac{\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil-1}{n}<\varepsilon_n<a_\star.
\]

\[
 S_{n,\mathrm{rare}}^{\mathrm{early}}=0,
 \qquad
 \left\langle S_{n,\mathrm{rare}}^{\mathrm{early}}\right\rangle=0\le v_{0,n}.
\]
The declared types give \(\delta_{q,n},\delta_{r,n},v_{0,n},\kappa_n\ge0\). Thus all three events in \texttt{def:good-event} occur surely for \(n\ge N_0\), so
\[
 G_n=\Omega,
 \qquad
 \Pr_\sigma(G_n)=1\ge1-\kappa_n.
\]
This proves the eventual profile-contract condition in \texttt{ass:profile-contract}, while also proving the stronger asserted exact discrepancy and early-variation conclusions.

Finally, the definition of the finite-population causal estimand and the two signs give
\[
 \Delta_n:=\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac{2\delta}{1-a_\star}\frac1n
   \sum_{t=1}^n(t/n-a_\star)_+.
\]
The function \(f(u)=(u-a_\star)_+\) is nondecreasing. On each interval \(((t-1)/n,t/n]\), \(f(u)\le f(t/n)\); hence its right Riemann sum satisfies
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n f(t/n)
 \ge \int_0^1 f(u)\,du
 =\int_{a_\star}^1(u-a_\star)\,du
 =\frac{(1-a_\star)^2}{2}.

Substitution yields

 \Delta_n\ge
 \frac{2\delta}{1-a_\star}\frac{(1-a_\star)^2}{2}
 =\delta(1-a_\star),
\]
which proves the causal separation and completes the membership certificate.

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\[
 \gamma_n=max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.

 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.

 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+,
 \qquad
 \eta_n\to a_\star^c,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)
                 \ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 &\ge[a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+\\
 &\ge a_\star^c-2\alpha.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last displayed lower bound is positive exactly when \(a_\star^c>2\alpha\). The matching rule supplied by the sharp theorem attains the positive-part expression only within the selected two-sign late-ramp subexperiment. \begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-two-sign-late-ramp-tradeoff]\label{thm:sharp-two-sign-late-ramp-tradeoff}
Fix a member of the parameterized late-ramp family and define
\[
 m_n=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor,\qquad
 V_n=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\{A_{nt}=0\},\qquad
 \eta_n=\prod_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\left(1-\frac ct\right),
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac{2\delta}{(1-a_\star)n}
   \sum_{t=1}^{n}(t/n-a_\star)_+.
\]
Adopt the convention \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\). For any possibly randomized confidence set \(D_n=D_n(O_n,U)\), where \(U\) is independent of the experiment and has the same law under both signs, let
\[
 \gamma_n=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
Write \([x]_+=\max\{x,0\}\). Then, for every \(n\) and each \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge \Delta_n\}
 \ge [\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), this inequality is sharp within this two-sign subexperiment: there is a possibly randomized complete-log confidence set \(D_{n,\alpha}^\star\) satisfying
\[
 \max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_{n,\alpha}^\star\}\le\alpha
\]
and, under each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_{n,\alpha}^\star)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =\Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_{n,\alpha}^\star)\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Moreover, \(\eta_n\to a_\star^c\), \(\Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star)\) for every \(n\), and \(\Delta_n\to\delta(1-a_\star)\). Hence \([a_\star^c-2\alpha]_+\) is the exact asymptotic probability--diameter frontier, both at the moving threshold \(\Delta_n\) and at the retained threshold \(\delta(1-a_\star)\), within the two-sign late-ramp subexperiment. This attainment claim is not a global minimax-sharpness claim for any larger adaptive-design class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family},
\[
 \tau_n^\sigma
 =\frac{\sigma\delta}{(1-a_\star)n}
   \sum_{t=1}^{n}(t/n-a_\star)_+.
\]
Subtracting the expression for \(\sigma=-1\) from that for \(\sigma=+1\) proves the displayed formula for \(\Delta_n\), which is strictly positive. The function \(f(u)=(u-a_\star)_+\) is nondecreasing. On every interval \(((t-1)/n,t/n]\), one has \(f(u)\le f(t/n)\), and therefore
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}f(t/n)
 \ge \int_0^1 f(u)\,du
 =\frac{(1-a_\star)^2}{2}.
\]
Multiplication by \(2\delta/(1-a_\star)\) yields
\[
 \Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star).
\]
The same interval comparison, using
\(0\le f(t/n)-f(u)\le 1/n\), gives
\[
 0\le \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}f(t/n)-\int_0^1f(u)\,du\le\frac1n,
\]
so \(\Delta_n\to\delta(1-a_\star)\).

We next compute the common late-arm void mass. Let
\(V_{n,s}=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{s}\{A_{nt}=0\}\), with the intersection equal to the whole sample space when \(s=m_n\). The event \(V_{n,s-1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,s-1}\)-measurable. By \texttt{ass:adaptive-assignment-law} and the deterministic propensity \(e_{ns}=c/s\) in \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family},
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr_\sigma(V_{n,s})
 &=\mathbb E_\sigma\!\left[
   \mathbf 1_{V_{n,s-1}}
   \Pr_\sigma(A_{ns}=0\mid\mathcal F_{n,s-1})
 \right]\\
 &=\left(1-\frac cs\right)\Pr_\sigma(V_{n,s-1}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Iteration gives \(\Pr_\sigma(V_n)=\eta_n\) under either sign. Every factor is positive because \(0<c<1\) and \(s\ge1\).

For completeness, the limit of this mass follows from elementary bounds. For \(0\le x<1\),
\[
 0\le-\log(1-x)-x
 =\int_0^x\frac y{1-y}\,dy
 \le\frac{x^2}{2(1-x)}.
\]
Since \(m_n/n\to a_\star>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \left|\log\eta_n+c\sum_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\frac1t\right|
 \le
 \frac{c^2}{2\{1-c/(m_n+1)\}}
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\frac1{t^2}
 =O(n^{-1}).
\]
Monotonicity of \(u\mapsto u^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \log\!\left(\frac{n+1}{m_n+1}\right)
 \le\sum_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\frac1t
 \le\log\!\left(\frac n{m_n}\right),
\]
and both outer terms tend to \(\log(1/a_\star)\). Thus
\[
 \log\eta_n\to c\log a_\star,
 \qquad \eta_n\to a_\star^c.
\]

We now construct the common subprobability measure. Under either sign, the sequential conditional law of the binary assignment is determined by the same deterministic probabilities \(c/t\); repeated conditioning shows that every assignment string has the same probability under both signs. If \(t\le m_n\), then \(t/n\le a_\star\) and both treated potential outcomes equal zero. If \(t>m_n\), the event \(V_n\) forces \(A_{nt}=0\), and then \texttt{def:observed-outcome} and \texttt{def:parameterized-late-ramp-family} give \(Y_{nt}^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)=0\). The propensity and regression logs are deterministic and identical under the two signs. Hence, for every measurable set \(H\),
\[
 \Pr_+\{(O_n,U)\in H,\ V_n\}
 =\Pr_-\{(O_n,U)\in H,\ V_n\}
 =:\nu_n(H),
\]
where independence and the common law of \(U\) preserve the equality. The measure \(\nu_n\) has mass \(\eta_n\) and is dominated by each sign-specific law.

Let \(E_{\sigma,n}=\{\tau_n^\sigma\in D_n\}\). By the definition of \(\gamma_n\),
\[
 \nu_n(E_{\sigma,n}^c)
 \le\Pr_\sigma(E_{\sigma,n}^c)
 \le\gamma_n.
\]
The union bound under the finite measure \(\nu_n\) therefore yields
\[
 \nu_n(E_{+,n}\cap E_{-,n})
 \ge\eta_n-2\gamma_n.
\]
On this intersection the same reported set contains both targets, whose distance is exactly \(\Delta_n\). Domination by either experimental law, together with nonnegativity of probability, proves
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge\max\{\eta_n-2\gamma_n,0\}
 =[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]

It remains to prove attainment. Take \(U\) uniform on \([0,1]\), independently of the experiment. On \(V_n^c\), let \(T_n\) be the first \(t>m_n\) with \(A_{nt}=1\). Such a time exists by the definition of \(V_n^c\), and
\[
 Y_{nT_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 =\sigma\delta\frac{T_n/n-a_\star}{1-a_\star}
\]
is nonzero by \texttt{def:observed-outcome}; its sign identifies \(\sigma\). On \(V_n^c\), report the singleton containing the thereby identified target \(\tau_n^\sigma\).

On \(V_n\), use the common randomizer as follows. If \(\eta_n>2\alpha\), report \(\{\tau_n^+\}\), \(\{\tau_n^-\}\), or \(\{\tau_n^+,\tau_n^-\}\) on intervals of \(U\) having respective conditional lengths
\[
 \frac\alpha{\eta_n},\qquad
 \frac\alpha{\eta_n},\qquad
 1-\frac{2\alpha}{\eta_n}.
\]
If \(\alpha<\eta_n\le2\alpha\), report \(\{\tau_n^+\}\), \(\{\tau_n^-\}\), or \(\varnothing\) on intervals having respective conditional lengths
\[
 \frac{\eta_n-\alpha}{\eta_n},\qquad
 \frac{\eta_n-\alpha}{\eta_n},\qquad
 \frac{2\alpha-\eta_n}{\eta_n}.
\]
If \(\alpha\ge\eta_n\), report \(\varnothing\) throughout \(V_n\). All displayed lengths are nonnegative in their stated cases and sum to one, so this defines a measurable possibly randomized complete-log confidence set.

In the first case, each sign receives unconditional void-event coverage
\(\alpha+(\eta_n-2\alpha)=\eta_n-\alpha\); in the second it receives \(\eta_n-\alpha\); and in the third its total miscoverage is only \(\eta_n\le\alpha\). Since coverage on \(V_n^c\) is one, the total coverage is at least \(1-\alpha\) under each sign. The reported set has positive diameter only when the two-target set is chosen, and that diameter is exactly \(\Delta_n\). Its unconditional selection probability is \(\eta_n-2\alpha\) in the first case and zero otherwise. Thus, under either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_{n,\alpha}^\star)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Because \(\Delta_n\ge\delta(1-a_\star)>0\), the same equality holds with threshold \(\delta(1-a_\star)\). Combining this construction with the preceding lower bound proves finite-sample and asymptotic sharpness within the two-sign subexperiment. Nothing in the construction or lower bound asserts attainment over a larger adaptive-design class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem gives the exact finite-sample and asymptotic probability--diameter frontier, including a matching randomized construction, within the two-sign late-ramp subexperiment. \textit{Gap.} The common void component previously yielded only a one-sided bound; exact allocation of its mass is needed to certify sharpness in the atomic two-point experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The result separates the exact two-sign information frontier from both estimator-specific limit laws and unproved global minimax claims over larger adaptive-design classes.

\begin{proposition}[prop:capped-clipped-ramp-membership]\label{prop:capped-clipped-ramp-membership}
Every member of the capped clipped-ramp family satisfies the declared fixed-outcome, outcome-boundedness, predictable-randomization, propensity-range, predictable-regression, bounded-regression, intensity-profile, and residual-profile conditions. In particular, \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\) and \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the propensity cap is inactive at every time after the profile cutoff and also at every time after the late-ramp start, both profile discrepancies in \(G_n\) are zero, the early rare-arm term and its predictable quadratic variation are zero, and hence \(G_n=\Omega\). These conclusions are eventual: they do not establish the profile-contract condition for every \(n\), and therefore do not by themselves certify membership in the full structured class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\}.
\]
We verify the conditions in the order in which they appear in the proposition.  By the capped clipped-ramp construction, the two potential-outcome arrays are deterministic under the assignment law.  Thus the fixed-outcome assumption holds.  The same construction and the inequality \(0\le g\le M\) give, for every \(n,t\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(0)|=0,
 \qquad
 |Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)|=g(t/n)\le M,
\]
which is the bounded-outcome condition.  The construction specifies
\[
 \Pr_\sigma(A_{nt}=1\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=e_{nt},
 \qquad e_{nt}=\min\{\rho,c/t\}.
\]
Since \(c>0\), \(t\ge1\), and \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
 0<e_{nt}\le\rho<1.
\]
This proves the predictable assignment-law and propensity-range conditions.  Both regression functions are zero.  They are therefore \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\)-measurable, as required by predictable regression, and \(|m_{nt}(a)|=0\le M_m\), as required by bounded regression.

The constructed intensity is \(\bar q^\sigma(u)=c\).  Hence, using \(K\ge c\) and \(L_q\ge0\),
\[
 \bar q^\sigma(0)=c,
 \qquad
 c/2\le \bar q^\sigma(u)=c\le K,
 \qquad
 |\bar q^\sigma(u)-\bar q^\sigma(v)|=0\le L_q|u-v|.
\]
The definition of the critical intensity class now gives \(\bar q^\sigma\in\mathcal Q\).  The map \(u\mapsto(u-a_\star)_+\) is one-Lipschitz, multiplication by \(L_r\) gives Lipschitz modulus \(L_r\), and truncation from above by \(M\) is nonexpansive.  Since \(B=M+M_m\ge M\),
\[
 |r^\sigma(u)|=g(u)\le M\le B,
 \qquad
 |r^\sigma(u)-r^\sigma(v)|\le L_r|u-v|.
\]
Thus the definition of the signed residual class gives \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\).

It remains to prove the eventual assertions about the common profile event.  The conditions \(n\varepsilon_n\to\infty\), \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), and \(a_\star>0\) imply that there is an integer \(N\) such that, for all \(n\ge N\),
\[
 \lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil>c/\rho,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n+n^{-1}<a_\star,
 \qquad
 \lfloor a_\star n\rfloor+1>c/\rho.
\]
For such \(n\), if \(t\ge\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\), then \(t>c/\rho\), so the propensity cap is inactive and
\[
 e_{nt}=c/t,
 \qquad
 t e_{nt}=c=\bar q^\sigma(t/n).
\]
The same conclusion holds for every \(t>\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\) by the third displayed inequality.  Also, for every \(t\), the residual definition and the zero regression give
\[
 r_{nt}(1)=Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)-m_{nt}(1)=r^\sigma(t/n),
\]
so the residual-profile discrepancy is identically zero.

If \(t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\), then
\[
 \frac tn<\frac{\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil}{n}
 \le \varepsilon_n+\frac1n<a_\star.
\]
Consequently \(r_{nt}(1)=0\).  Every summand in
\[
 S_{n,\mathrm{rare}}^{\mathrm{early}}
 =\frac1n\sum_{t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil}
 (A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{r_{nt}(1)}{e_{nt}}
\]
is zero.  The early term is therefore identically zero, and its predictable quadratic variation is \(0\le v_{0,n}\).  All three clauses in the definition of \(G_n\) hold on the whole sample space, proving \(G_n=\Omega\) for all \(n\ge N\).

This argument is deliberately only eventual.  At a finite retained index for which \(c/t>\rho\), the cap gives
\[
 |t e_{nt}-\bar q^\sigma(t/n)|=c-t\rho.
\]
If \(\delta_{q,n}<c-t\rho\), the intensity clause in the definition of \(G_n\) fails.  Thus the preceding calculation proves the asserted eventual facts but supplies no every-\(n\) profile-contract or full structured-class certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition verifies the fixed, bounded, predictable, and profile-shape facts supplied by the capped construction, together with eventual exactness of its profile event, without promoting eventual exactness to an every-\(n\) class-membership certificate. \textit{Gap.} At a finite profile-controlled index the propensity cap can force a logged-intensity discrepancy larger than the supplied tolerance, so the all-\(n\) profile contract requires a separate argument. \textit{Consumer.} The sharp constant-intensity pair theorem uses these construction facts; any larger-class transfer must establish its own every-\(n\) inclusion or pair-honesty premise.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-two-sign-clipped-ramp-tradeoff]\label{thm:sharp-two-sign-clipped-ramp-tradeoff}
Fix a member of the capped clipped-ramp family. Define
\[
 m_n=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor,
 \qquad
 V_n=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\{A_{nt}=0\},
 \qquad
 \eta_n=\prod_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\left(1-\min\{\rho,c/t\}\right),
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^{n}\min\{L_r(t/n-a_\star)_+,M\}.
\]
For any possibly randomized complete-log confidence set \(D_n=D_n(O_n,U)\), with \(U\) independent of the experiment and identically distributed under the two signs, put
\[
 \gamma_n=max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
With \([z]_+=\max\{z,0\}\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\), for every \(n\) and each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), this inequality is attained within this two-sign subexperiment by a possibly randomized complete-log confidence set having maximum miscoverage at most \(\alpha\) and, under either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Writing \(x=1-a_\star\), one has \(\eta_n\to a_\star^c\), \(\Delta_n\ge d(x)\) for every \(n\), and \(\Delta_n\to d(x)\), where
\[
 d(x)=2\int_0^x\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds
 =\begin{cases}
 L_rx^2,&L_rx\le M,\\
 2Mx-M^2/L_r,&L_rx>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
The attainment assertion is confined to the displayed two-sign subexperiment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\}.
\]
By the definition of the finite-population causal estimand and the two fixed potential-outcome arrays,
\[
 \Delta_n
 =\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\{Y_{nt}^+(1)-Y_{nt}^-(1)\}
 =\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^n g(t/n)>0.
\]
This is a contrast of fixed potential outcomes, rather than a distributional definition involving an estimator.

The propensities are deterministic.  Successive use of the predictable adaptive-randomization assumption gives, under either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma(V_n)
 =\prod_{t=m_n+1}^n
   \Pr_\sigma(A_{nt}=0\mid A_{n,m_n+1:t-1}=0)
 =\prod_{t=m_n+1}^n(1-e_{nt})=\eta_n.
\]
For \(t\le m_n=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\), one has \(t/n\le a_\star\), so both treated potential outcomes are zero.  On \(V_n\), no later treated potential outcome is observed.  The assignments and logged propensities have the same law under both signs, the regressions coincide, and the auxiliary randomizer \(U\) has the same independent law.  By the definition of the observed outcome, the restrictions of the two laws of \((O_n,U)\) to \(V_n\) are therefore a common subprobability measure \(\mu_n\) with total mass \(\mu_n(V_n)=\eta_n\).

For \(s\in\{+,-\}\), let \(E_s=\{\tau_n^s\in D_n\}\).  On \(E_+\cap E_-\), the reported set contains two points separated by \(\Delta_n\), and hence has diameter at least \(\Delta_n\).  Commonality of \(\mu_n\), followed by the union bound, yields under either sign \(\sigma\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 &\ge \mu_n(V_n\cap E_+\cap E_-)\\
 &\ge \eta_n-\mu_n(E_+^c)-\mu_n(E_-^c)\\
 &\ge \eta_n-Pr_+(E_+^c)-\Pr_-(E_-^c)\\
 &\ge \eta_n-2\gamma_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
The probability on the left is nonnegative, proving the stated positive-part inequality.

We next certify sharpness within the two-sign experiment.  Off \(V_n\), at least one \(t>m_n\) is treated.  Then \(t/n>a_\star\), so \(g(t/n)>0\), and the sign of the corresponding nonzero observed outcome identifies \(\sigma\); report the identified singleton.  On \(V_n\), use \(U\) to allocate the following unconditional masses, each row being supported on \(V_n\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &\{\tau_n^+\}&\{\tau_n^-\}&\{\tau_n^+,\tau_n^-\}&\varnothing\\ \hline
 \eta_n>2\alpha
   &\alpha&\alpha&\eta_n-2\alpha&0\\
 \alpha<\eta_n\le2\alpha
   &\eta_n-\alpha&\eta_n-\alpha&0&2\alpha-\eta_n\\
 \eta_n\le\alpha
   &0&0&0&\eta_n.
\end{array}
\]
All entries are nonnegative and every row sums to \(\eta_n>0\); division by \(\eta_n\) therefore defines a valid conditional randomization.  In the first two rows the miscoverage probability under either sign is exactly \(\alpha\); in the last row it is \(\eta_n\le\alpha\).  The only output whose diameter is at least \(\Delta_n\) is \(\{\tau_n^+,\tau_n^-\}\), and its mass is \([\eta_n-2\alpha]_+\).  This proves finite-sample attainment, including when the observation law is atomic.

For the deterministic separation, \(g\) is nondecreasing.  Its right-endpoint Riemann sum therefore dominates its integral, and continuity gives convergence:
\[
 \Delta_n=\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^n g(t/n)
 \ge2\int_0^1g(u)\,du
 =2\int_0^{1-a_\star}\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\longrightarrow2\int_0^1g(u)\,du.
\]
Writing \(x=1-a_\star\), direct integration gives
\[
 d(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_rx^2,&L_rx\le M,\\
 2Mx-M^2/L_r,&L_rx>M.
 \end{cases}
\]

Finally, because \(a_\star>0\), for all sufficiently large \(n\), every \(t>m_n\) exceeds \(c/\rho\), and the cap is inactive throughout the product.  On that range \(c/t\le1/2\) eventually, so Taylor's formula with a uniform remainder gives
\[
 \log(1-c/t)=-c/t+O(t^{-2}).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^n t^{-1}\longrightarrow\log(1/a_\star),
 \qquad
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^n t^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \log\eta_n
 =-c\sum_{t=m_n+1}^n t^{-1}
   +O\!\left(\sum_{t=m_n+1}^n t^{-2}\right)
 \longrightarrow c\log a_\star,
\]
and exponentiation yields \(\eta_n\to a_\star^c\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The common late-arm void event yields an exact finite-sample confidence-set tradeoff for the clipped witnesses, including atomic and randomized confidence sets. \textit{Gap.} A limiting information inequality alone would neither certify the finite right-Riemann separation nor show exact allocation of the common subprobability mass. \textit{Consumer.} The optimized envelope theorem uses both the finite lower bound and the matching two-sign construction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:optimized-clipped-ramp-frontier]\label{thm:optimized-clipped-ramp-frontier}
Assume \(L_r>0\), and define
\[
 d_{\max}=\begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
For every \(d\) with \(0<d<d_{\max}\), set
\[
 x(d)=\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{d/L_r},&d\le M^2/L_r,\\
 \{d+M^2/L_r\}/(2M),&d>M^2/L_r,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad a(d)=1-x(d).
\]
Select the capped clipped-ramp pair with start \(a(d)\), and define
\[
 \gamma_n=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
If the selected pair satisfies \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha\), then, under each sign,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
The elbow \(d=M^2/L_r\) is interior to \((0,d_{\max})\) exactly when \(L_r>M\). Among two-sign late-void witnesses whose residual profiles vanish on \([0,a]\), are bounded in absolute value by \(M\), and have Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\), the clipped profile is pointwise optimal, and the displayed bound is the optimized late-void envelope. For each selected two-sign clipped-ramp subexperiment the bound is attained asymptotically by an exact randomized allocation, but no global minimax attainment over the full structured class is claimed. Uniform honesty over a larger class is sufficient only if that selected pair is separately known to belong to the larger class for every \(n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(a\in(0,1)\), define
\[
 \phi_a(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a)_+,M\}.
\]
Consider any two-sign late-void witness whose two residual profiles vanish on \([0,a]\), are bounded in absolute value by \(M\), and have Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\).  For each sign \(s\) and every \(u>a\), vanishing at \(a\) and Lipschitz continuity imply
\[
 |r^s(u)|=|r^s(u)-r^s(a)|\le L_r(u-a).
\]
Combining this with the magnitude bound gives \(|r^s(u)|\le\phi_a(u)\) for every \(u\).  Hence the absolute limiting separation of any such pair is at most
\[
 \left|\int_0^1\{r^+(u)-r^-(u)\}\,du\right|
 \le2\int_a^1\phi_a(u)\,du
 =2\int_0^{1-a}\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds
 =d(1-a).
\]
The clipped pair \(r^+=\phi_a\), \(r^-=-\phi_a\) attains equality pointwise.  Direct integration gives
\[
 d(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_rx^2,&L_rx\le M,\\
 2Mx-M^2/L_r,&L_rx>M.
 \end{cases}
\]

The function \(d\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), and for \(0\le x<y\le1\),
\[
 d(y)-d(x)=2\int_x^y\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds>0.
\]
Thus \(d\) is strictly increasing.  Its endpoint value is
\[
 d(1)=
 \begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M,
 \end{cases}
 =d_{\max}.
\]
If \(L_r\le M\), then \(M^2/L_r\ge d_{\max}\), so the inverse on \((0,d_{\max})\) is entirely on the quadratic branch.  If \(L_r>M\), the clipping point \(x=M/L_r\) lies in \((0,1)\) and has image \(M^2/L_r\).  Inverting the two displayed branches proves
\[
 x(d)=
 \begin{cases}
 \sqrt{d/L_r},&d\le M^2/L_r,\\
 \{d+M^2/L_r\}/(2M),&d>M^2/L_r,
 \end{cases}
\]
as well as
\[
 x(d)\in(0,1),
 \qquad a(d)=1-x(d)\in(0,1),
 \qquad d(x(d))=d.
\]
The residual elbow is therefore interior exactly when \(L_r>M\).

Fix \(d\in(0,d_{\max})\) and select the capped clipped-ramp pair with \(a_\star=a(d)\).  The sharp two-sign clipped-ramp theorem gives, for either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+,
 \qquad
 \eta_n\longrightarrow a(d)^c,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\ge d(1-a(d))=d.
\]
By the selected-pair honesty premise, \(\limsup_n\gamma_n\le\alpha\).  For every \(\epsilon>0\), all sufficiently large \(n\) therefore satisfy \(\eta_n\ge a(d)^c-\epsilon\) and \(\gamma_n\le\alpha+\epsilon\).  Since \(\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}\) is contained in \(\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}\), monotonicity of the positive-part map gives
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[a(d)^c-2\alpha-3\epsilon]_+
\]
eventually.  Taking the lower limit and then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
No structured-class inclusion is used.  If uniform honesty is available on a larger class and the selected pair is separately proved to belong to that class for every \(n\), then uniform honesty implies the selected-pair premise; eventual equality of \(G_n\) alone does not do so.

The sharp theorem also supplies a possibly randomized confidence set with maximum pairwise miscoverage at most \(\alpha\) and
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Its only positive-diameter output is the two-target set, whose diameter is \(\Delta_n\ge d\).  Hence for this pair-specific rule
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 =[\eta_n-2\alpha]_+
 \longrightarrow[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
This is exact asymptotic attainment within the selected pair, not global minimax attainment.

To certify optimality of the late-void envelope, let another admissible witness start at \(a'=1-x'\) and have limiting causal separation at least \(d\).  The pointwise bound proved above gives \(d\le d(x')\), so strict monotonicity forces \(x'\ge x(d)\).  The constant-intensity void-product calculation in the sharp theorem then gives limiting common void mass
\[
 (a')^c=(1-x')^c\le(1-x(d))^c.
\]
The selected clipped profile attains equality.  This is the matching upper certificate among the late-void witnesses stated in the theorem.

As an exact small-instance check, take \(c=a_\star=1/2\), \(L_r=1/2\), \(M\ge1/4\), and choose \(\rho\ge1/2\).  Then
\[
 d(1/2)=\frac18,
 \qquad
 \left[(1/2)^{1/2}-2(0.05)\right]_+
 =0.6071067811865475\ldots.
\]
For \(n=1,2,3,4\), the post-ramp factor sets are respectively \(\{1\}\), \(\{2\}\), \(\{2,3\}\), and \(\{3,4\}\).  The cap is inactive on these factors under \(\rho\ge1/2\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
 \eta_1=\frac12,
 \qquad \eta_2=\frac34,
 \qquad \eta_3=\frac58,
 \qquad \eta_4=\frac{35}{48}.
\]
The cap qualification is essential: if \(\rho<1/2\), then \(\eta_1=1-\rho\), so the four displayed diagnostic values are not asserted in that regime.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This retained theorem is the residual-optimized constant-intensity late-void converse under honesty for the selected capped two-sign pair. \textit{Gap.} Clipping exposes the residual elbow and inverse diameter map, while the capped pair's eventual profile exactness does not itself justify an all-\(n\) structured-class premise. \textit{Consumer.} Given a coverage guarantee for the selected pair, trial designers can read off the constant-intensity obstruction. Uniform honesty over a larger class supplies the premise only when that pair has separately proved every-\(n\) inclusion; exact attainment remains pairwise only.

\begin{proposition}[prop:li-zhao-optimized-clipped-frontier]\label{prop:li-zhao-optimized-clipped-frontier}
Every capped clipped-ramp member lies in Li and Zhao's baseline binary finite-population adaptive-design class and fails the negligible-increment requirement in their Gaussian Condition 3(ii). Therefore every sequence of possibly randomized complete-log confidence sets that is asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest uniformly over that baseline class inherits, for every \(0<d<d_{\max}\) and each sign of the corresponding clipped-ramp pair,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Only this converse transfers by subclass inclusion. Neither the exact two-sign attaining rule nor the positive profile-net calibration theorem is asserted on Li and Zhao's larger class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The baseline-class lemma records the relevant requirements from Li and Zhao.  In the capped clipped-ramp construction the binary assignment probabilities are
\[
 e_{nt}(1)=\min\{\rho,c/t\},
 \qquad
 e_{nt}(0)=1-e_{nt}(1).
\]
They are deterministic, hence functions of every available history, are strictly between zero and one by \(c>0\) and \(0<\rho<1\), and sum to one.  The capped-family membership proposition supplies fixed bounded potential outcomes and predictable zero regression.  Thus every member belongs, for every \(n\), to the cited baseline binary finite-population adaptive-design class.  This inclusion does not use the profile-contract event.

We next verify failure of the cited negligible-increment condition rather than assuming a non-Gaussian limit.  Write
\[
 g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\},
 \qquad
 X_{nt}=(A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{\sigma g(t/n)}{e_{nt}}.
\]
The predictable randomization law implies
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})
 =g(t/n)^2\{e_{nt}^{-1}-1\}.
\]
Different increments are orthogonal by iterated conditioning.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the cap is inactive at every index at which \(g(t/n)>0\).  Therefore the variance \(W_n\) of the \(\sqrt n\)-scaled scalar AIPW error is
\[
 W_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n g(t/n)^2(t/c-1),
\]
and the Riemann-sum calculation gives
\[
 \frac{W_n}{n}\longrightarrow
 w_\star:=\frac1c\int_{a_\star}^1u g(u)^2\,du>0.
\]
The strict inequality uses \(a_\star<1\), \(L_r>0\), and \(M>0\), which imply \(g(u)>0\) on a nonempty interval immediately to the right of \(a_\star\).

At \(t=n\), an outcome envelope must be at least \(g(1)>0\), the regression envelope is zero, and for large \(n\) the smaller binary assignment probability is \(\underline e_n=c/n\).  Thus the scalar instance of Li and Zhao's Condition 3(ii), as recorded in the cited negligible-increment premise, satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 W_n^{-1}n^{-1}
 \max_{1\le t\le n}\frac{(L_t+M_t)^2}{\underline e_t^2}
 &\ge
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{g(1)^2n}{c^2W_n}\\
 &=\frac{g(1)^2}{c^2w_\star}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
It is not \(o(1)\), so the cited Gaussian premise fails.  At an unreduced two-arm level, the identically zero control contribution also yields a zero control row and column; this does not restore the required nonsingular negligible-increment regime.

Now suppose the confidence sets are asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest uniformly over the cited baseline class.  The every-\(n\) inclusion just proved implies, for the selected constant-intensity pair,
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.
\]
Apply the finite common-subprobability argument directly to this pair.  Its common late-void event has mass \(\eta_n\), and on that event the two complete-log laws coincide.  Therefore, exactly as in the finite union-bound calculation,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
For the start \(a(d)=1-x(d)\), the clipped-ramp calculations give \(\Delta_n\ge d\) and \(\eta_n\to a(d)^c\).  Event inclusion and continuity of \([\cdot]_+\) yield
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge[(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+.
\]
This direct transfer constructs no confidence procedure on the baseline class.  It therefore transfers neither the pair-specific attaining rule nor the positive profile-net calibration theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:intensity-optimized-clipped-ramp-properties]\label{prop:intensity-optimized-clipped-ramp-properties}
Every member of the intensity-optimized clipped-ramp family has fixed potential outcomes bounded by \(M\), valid predictable probabilities \(0<e_{nt}\le\rho<1\), and predictable zero regressions. Its profiles satisfy
\[
 q_\star\in\mathcal Q,\qquad r^\sigma\in\mathcal R,
 \qquad r_{nt}(1)=r^\sigma(t/n).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the propensity cap is inactive at every \(t\ge\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\) and at every \(t>\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\); the two profile discrepancies defining \(G_n\) are zero, the early rare-arm term and its predictable quadratic variation are zero, and hence \(G_n=\Omega\). This is only an eventual assertion: the family is not asserted to satisfy the all-\(n\) profile contract or to belong to the full declared structured class for every \(n\).

Moreover, for every \(q'\in\mathcal Q\) and \(u\in[0,1]\),
\[
 q'(u)\ge q_\star(u).
\]
Consequently, for every fixed start \(a_\star\), every \(n\), and every deterministic design of the form \(e_{nt}^{q'}=\min\{\rho,q'(t/n)/t\}\),
\[
 \prod_{t=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor+1}^{n}(1-e_{nt}^{q'})
 \le
 \prod_{t=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor+1}^{n}
 \left[1-\min\{\rho,q_\star(t/n)/t\}\right].
\]
Thus \(q_\star\) pointwise maximizes the common late-void mass within this deterministic intensity-profile design family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The potential outcomes and regressions in the construction are deterministic, hence fixed under randomization and predictable, respectively. Since
\[
 0\le g_{a_\star}(u)\le M,
\]
both potential-outcome arms are bounded in absolute value by \(M\). Also \(q_\star(u)\ge c/2>0\), so
\[
 0<e_{nt}=\min\{\rho,q_\star(t/n)/t\}\le\rho<1.
\]
The displayed sequential law then gives the declared predictable binary randomization, and the two arm probabilities \(e_{nt}\) and \(1-e_{nt}\) sum to one.

We next verify the intensity conditions. Directly,
\[
 q_\star(0)=\max\{c,c/2\}=c,
 \qquad c/2\le q_\star(u)\le c\le K.
\]
The affine map \(u\mapsto c-L_qu\) is \(L_q\)-Lipschitz, and the scalar map \(x\mapsto\max\{x,c/2\}\) is nondecreasing and one-Lipschitz. Therefore
\[
 |q_\star(u)-q_\star(v)|\le L_q|u-v|,
\]
so \(q_\star\in\mathcal Q\). Likewise, \(|r^\sigma(u)|=g_{a_\star}(u)\le M\le B\), because \(B=M+M_m\) and \(M_m\ge0\). Both maps \(u\mapsto(u-a_\star)_+\) and \(x\mapsto\min\{x,M\}\) are one-Lipschitz on their respective domains. Hence
\[
 |r^\sigma(u)-r^\sigma(v)|\le L_r|u-v|,
\]
and \(r^\sigma\in\mathcal R\). Since the regression is zero, the finite residual is exactly
\[
 r_{nt}(1)=Y_{nt}^\sigma(1)-m_{nt}(1)=r^\sigma(t/n).
\]

Because \(q_\star\le c\), the cap is inactive whenever \(t\ge c/\rho\). The primitive cutoff conditions give
\[
 n\varepsilon_n\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad a_\star n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus, for all sufficiently large \(n\), both \(t\ge\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil\) and \(t>\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\) imply \(t\ge c/\rho\), and then
\[
 t e_{nt}=q_\star(t/n).
\]
The intensity discrepancy in \(G_n\) is therefore zero, and the residual discrepancy was already shown to be zero. Since \(\varepsilon_n+n^{-1}\to0<a_\star\), eventually
\[
 t<\lceil n\varepsilon_n\rceil
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 t/n<\varepsilon_n+n^{-1}<a_\star.
\]
For every such \(t\), \(r_{nt}(1)=0\). Every summand of the early rare-arm martingale and of its predictable quadratic variation is consequently zero. All three clauses in the definition of \(G_n\) hold surely, proving \(G_n=\Omega\) eventually. At a finite included index where \(q_\star(t/n)/t>\rho\), however, the intensity discrepancy equals \(q_\star(t/n)-t\rho\), which need not be bounded by \(\delta_{q,n}\). This proves precisely why the conclusion is eventual and does not establish the all-\(n\) profile contract.

Finally, take any \(q'\in\mathcal Q\). The anchor and Lipschitz conditions give
\[
 q'(u)\ge q'(0)-L_qu=c-L_qu,
\]
while the intensity floor gives \(q'(u)\ge c/2\). Hence
\[
 q'(u)\ge\max\{c-L_qu,c/2\}=q_\star(u).
\]
The function \(x\mapsto\min\{\rho,x/t\}\) is nondecreasing, so \(e_{nt}^{q'}\ge e_{nt}^{q_\star}\), and therefore \(1-e_{nt}^{q'}\le1-e_{nt}^{q_\star}\). Every factor is nonnegative. Multiplication over the late indices proves the displayed product inequality and the claimed finite-sample pointwise optimality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-two-sign-intensity-optimized-clipped-ramp-tradeoff]\label{thm:sharp-two-sign-intensity-optimized-clipped-ramp-tradeoff}
Fix a member of the intensity-optimized clipped-ramp family and define
\[
 m_n=\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor,
 \qquad V_n=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{n}\{A_{nt}=0\},
 \qquad
 \eta_n^\star=\prod_{t=m_n+1}^{n}
 \left[1-\min\{\rho,q_\star(t/n)/t\}\right],
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\tau_n^+-\tau_n^-
 =\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^{n}\min\{L_r(t/n-a_\star)_+,M\}.
\]
For any possibly randomized complete-log confidence set \(D_n=D_n(O_n,U)\), where \(U\) is independent of the experiment and identically distributed under the two signs, put
\[
 \gamma_n=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
With \([z]_+=\max\{z,0\}\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\), for every \(n\) and each sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n^\star-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), this inequality is attained within this two-sign subexperiment by a possibly randomized complete-log confidence set with maximum miscoverage at most \(\alpha\) and, under either sign,
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 =[\eta_n^\star-2\alpha]_+.
\]
Writing \(x=1-a_\star\), one has \(\Delta_n\ge d(x)\) for every \(n\) and \(\Delta_n\to d(x)\), where
\[
 d(x)=2\int_0^x\min\{L_rs,M\}\,ds
 =\begin{cases}
 L_rx^2,&L_rx\le M,\\
 2Mx-M^2/L_r,&L_rx>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \eta_n^\star\longrightarrow\omega(a_\star)=\exp\{-H(a_\star)\},
 \qquad H(a)=\int_a^1\frac{q_\star(u)}u\,du.
\]
If \(0\le L_q\le c/2\), then
\[
 H(a)=c\log(1/a)-L_q(1-a).
\]
If \(L_q>c/2\) and \(u_q=c/(2L_q)\), then
\[
 H(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 (c/2)\log(1/a),&a\ge u_q,\\
 c\log(u_q/a)-L_q(u_q-a)+(c/2)\log(1/u_q),&a<u_q.
 \end{cases}
\]
The attainment assertion is confined to the displayed two-sign subexperiment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The causal targets follow directly from the fixed potential outcomes:
\[
 \tau_n^\sigma=\frac{\sigma}{n}\sum_{t=1}^n g_{a_\star}(t/n),
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=\frac2n\sum_{t=1}^n g_{a_\star}(t/n)>0.
\]
The function \(u\mapsto g_{a_\star}(u)\) is nondecreasing. On each interval \(((t-1)/n,t/n]\), its value is at most \(g_{a_\star}(t/n)\), so the right Riemann sum satisfies
\[
 \Delta_n\ge2\int_0^1g_{a_\star}(u)\,du=d(1-a_\star).
\]
The function is continuous, hence its right Riemann sums converge to the same integral. Substitution \(s=u-a_\star\) and direct integration before and after the clipping point \(s=M/L_r\) give the two displayed formulas for \(d(x)\).

Let \(V_{n,s}=\bigcap_{t=m_n+1}^{s}\{A_{nt}=0\}\), with \(V_{n,m_n}=\Omega\). The event \(V_{n,s-1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,s-1}\)-measurable. Predictable randomization and the deterministic propensity in the family give, under either sign,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr_\sigma(V_{n,s})
 &=\mathbb E_\sigma\!\left[
 \mathbf1_{V_{n,s-1}}
 \Pr_\sigma(A_{ns}=0\mid\mathcal F_{n,s-1})
 \right]\\
 &=\left[1-\min\{\rho,q_\star(s/n)/s\}\right]
 \Pr_\sigma(V_{n,s-1}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Iteration proves \(\Pr_\sigma(V_n)=\eta_n^\star\).

On \(V_n\), every observed outcome is zero under both signs. Indeed, for \(t\le m_n\), one has \(t/n\le a_\star\), hence both treated outcomes are zero; for \(t>m_n\), the void event forces the control arm, whose potential outcome is zero. The propensity and regression logs are also identical under the two signs. Since the assignment probabilities are the same deterministic numbers under both signs, repeated conditioning shows that the restrictions of the two laws of \((O_n,U)\) to \(V_n\) coincide. Denote this common subprobability measure by \(\nu_n\); it has mass \(\eta_n^\star\) and is dominated by each full law.

For \(E_{\sigma,n}=\{\tau_n^\sigma\in D_n\}\), domination and the definition of \(\gamma_n\) imply
\[
 \nu_n(E_{\sigma,n}^c)\le\gamma_n.
\]
The union bound under \(\nu_n\) gives
\[
 \nu_n(E_{+,n}\cap E_{-,n})\ge\eta_n^\star-2\gamma_n.
\]
On this intersection the same set contains two points separated by \(\Delta_n\). Domination by either sign-specific law and nonnegativity yield
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n^\star-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]

For attainment, take an independent uniform random variable \(U\). On \(V_n^c\), the first treated time after \(m_n\) has a nonzero observed outcome whose sign identifies \(\sigma\); report the corresponding true singleton. On \(V_n\), if \(\eta_n^\star>2\alpha\), assign conditional probabilities
\[
 \frac{\alpha}{\eta_n^\star},\qquad
 \frac{\alpha}{\eta_n^\star},\qquad
 1-\frac{2\alpha}{\eta_n^\star}
\]
to the positive singleton, the negative singleton, and the two-target set. If \(\alpha<\eta_n^\star\le2\alpha\), assign conditional probabilities
\[
 \frac{\eta_n^\star-\alpha}{\eta_n^\star},\qquad
 \frac{\eta_n^\star-\alpha}{\eta_n^\star},\qquad
 \frac{2\alpha-\eta_n^\star}{\eta_n^\star}
\]
to the two singletons and the empty set. If \(\eta_n^\star\le\alpha\), report the empty set on \(V_n\). In each case the displayed probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. Direct accounting gives miscoverage at most \(\alpha\) under each sign, while the two-target set is selected with unconditional probability exactly \([\eta_n^\star-2\alpha]_+\). This proves finite-sample sharpness inside the pair.

It remains to compute the void limit. Because \(m_n/n\to a_\star>0\) and \(q_\star\le c\), the cap is inactive throughout \(t>m_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Put \(p_{nt}=q_\star(t/n)/t\). Then \(\max_{t>m_n}p_{nt}\to0\), and the elementary identity
\[
 0\le-\log(1-y)-y=\int_0^y\frac{x}{1-x}\,dx
 \le\frac{y^2}{2(1-y)},\qquad 0\le y<1,
\]
implies
\[
 \left|\log\eta_n^\star+\sum_{t=m_n+1}^np_{nt}\right|
 \le
 \frac{1}{2\{1-\max_{t>m_n}p_{nt}\}}
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^np_{nt}^2.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero because
\[
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^np_{nt}^2
 \le c^2\sum_{t=m_n+1}^nt^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \sum_{t=m_n+1}^np_{nt}
 =\frac1n\sum_{t=m_n+1}^n
 \frac{q_\star(t/n)}{t/n}
 \longrightarrow\int_{a_\star}^1\frac{q_\star(u)}u\,du=H(a_\star),
\]
by right-Riemann convergence on the compact interval bounded away from zero. Hence \(\eta_n^\star\to\exp\{-H(a_\star)\}\).

If \(0\le L_q\le c/2\), then \(c-L_qu\ge c/2\) for all \(u\in[0,1]\), so
\[
 H(a)=\int_a^1\left(\frac cu-L_q\right)du
 =c\log(1/a)-L_q(1-a).
\]
If \(L_q>c/2\), the two pieces meet at \(u_q=c/(2L_q)\in(0,1)\). For \(a\ge u_q\), the profile equals \(c/2\) throughout \([a,1]\), giving \((c/2)\log(1/a)\). For \(a<u_q\), splitting the integral at \(u_q\) gives
\[
 \int_a^{u_q}\left(\frac cu-L_q\right)du
 +\int_{u_q}^1\frac{c}{2u}\,du
 =c\log(u_q/a)-L_q(u_q-a)+(c/2)\log(1/u_q),
\]
as asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:joint-intensity-residual-clipped-ramp-frontier]\label{thm:joint-intensity-residual-clipped-ramp-frontier}
Assume \(L_r>0\), and define
\[
 d_{\max}=\begin{cases}
 L_r,&L_r\le M,\\
 2M-M^2/L_r,&L_r>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(0<d<d_{\max}\), let
\[
 x(d)=\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{d/L_r},&d\le M^2/L_r,\\
 \{d+M^2/L_r\}/(2M),&d>M^2/L_r,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad a(d)=1-x(d).
\]
Select the intensity-optimized clipped-ramp pair with start \(a(d)\), and write
\[
 \gamma_n=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\tau_n^\sigma\notin D_n\}.
\]
If this selected pair satisfies \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha\), then, under each sign,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge
 \left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+.
\]
This envelope is jointly optimal among deterministic late-void two-sign witnesses of the stated form with intensity profile in \(\mathcal Q\), residual magnitude bounded by \(M\), and residual Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\). For each selected two-sign pair, the exact randomized allocation attains the displayed asymptotic bound, but no globally attaining procedure over the full structured class is asserted.

The residual clipping elbow is \(d_r=M^2/L_r\) and is interior precisely when \(L_r>M\). If \(L_q>c/2\), the intensity profile has the additional elbow
\[
 d_q=d\!\left(1-\frac{c}{2L_q}\right).
\]
When both elbows are interior, either strict ordering \(d_q<d_r\) or \(d_r<d_q\) is possible under the declared parameter restrictions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The optimized constant-intensity theorem proves that \(d\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,1]\), that \(d(1)=d_{\max}\), and that for every \(d\in(0,d_{\max})\),
\[
 d(x(d))=d,
 \qquad x(d)\in(0,1),
 \qquad a(d)=1-x(d)\in(0,1).
\]
Fix such \(d\) and select the intensity-optimized clipped-ramp pair with start \(a(d)\).  The sharp intensity-optimized two-sign theorem gives
\[
 \Delta_n\ge d(x(d))=d,
 \qquad
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n^\star-2\gamma_n]_+,
\]
and
\[
 \eta_n^\star\longrightarrow
 \exp\{-H(a(d))\}
 =\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}.
\]
Because the event at threshold \(\Delta_n\) is contained in the event at threshold \(d\), the selected-pair premise \(\limsup_n\gamma_n\le\alpha\) and continuity and monotonicity of the positive-part map imply
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge
 \left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+.
\]

We now give the joint optimality certificate.  Consider any two-sign late-void witness of the stated deterministic form, with start \(a'\), residual profiles vanishing on \([0,a']\), residual magnitude at most \(M\), and residual Lipschitz modulus at most \(L_r\).  Vanishing at \(a'\) and the Lipschitz and magnitude bounds imply, for each sign \(s\),
\[
 |r^s(u)|\le\min\{L_r(u-a')_+,M\}.
\]
Consequently its limiting causal separation is at most \(d(1-a')\).  If that separation is at least \(d\), strict monotonicity of \(d\) forces
\[
 1-a'\ge x(d),
 \qquad a'\le a(d).
\]

For any \(q'\in\mathcal Q\), the intensity-optimized properties proposition proves the pointwise inequality \(q'(u)\ge q_\star(u)\).  Since \(x\mapsto1-\min\{\rho,x/t\}\) is nonincreasing, the finite late-void product under \(q'\) is no larger than that under \(q_\star\) at the same start.  Under \(q_\star\), its limiting value is \(\exp\{-H(a')\}\).  The integrand \(q_\star(u)/u\) is strictly positive because \(q_\star\ge c/2>0\).  Hence \(a'\le a(d)\) gives
\[
 H(a')=\int_{a'}^1\frac{q_\star(u)}u\,du
 \ge\int_{a(d)}^1\frac{q_\star(u)}u\,du=H(a(d)),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \exp\{-H(a')\}\le\exp\{-H(a(d))\}.
\]
The clipped residual at \(a(d)\) and the pointwise-minimal intensity \(q_\star\) attain equality in both comparisons.  This proves joint optimality within the witness class specified in the theorem.

The exact randomized allocation in the sharp intensity-optimized theorem has pairwise maximum miscoverage at most \(\alpha\), and its only positive-diameter output is the two-target set of diameter \(\Delta_n\ge d\).  It follows that
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 =[\eta_n^\star-2\alpha]_+
 \longrightarrow
 \left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+.
\]
This allocation depends on the selected pair and proves pairwise, not global, attainment.

It remains to locate the two elbows.  The clipped residual changes branch at \(x_r=M/L_r\), whose image is \(d_r=M^2/L_r\); this point is in \((0,1)\) exactly when \(L_r>M\).  If \(L_q>c/2\), the intensity profile changes branch at
\[
 u_q=\frac{c}{2L_q},
\]
so on the diameter scale its elbow is
\[
 d_q=d(1-u_q)=d\!\left(1-\frac{c}{2L_q}\right).
\]
When both elbows are interior, \(x_r\in(0,1)\), whereas \(x_q=1-c/(2L_q)\) ranges over \((0,1)\) as \(L_q\) ranges over \((c/2,\infty)\).  Choosing \(x_q<x_r\) or \(x_q>x_r\), and then using strict monotonicity of \(d\), gives respectively \(d_q<d_r\) or \(d_q>d_r\).  Thus the declared restrictions impose no fixed ordering.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Pointwise minimality of \(q_\star\) and pointwise maximality of the clipped residual jointly certify the strongest late-void envelope in the declared deterministic witness geometry. \textit{Gap.} The intensity lower envelope introduces a second kink that the retained constant-intensity frontier cannot reveal; arbitrary finite propensity caps prevent an all-\(n\) structured-class membership claim for the new family. \textit{Consumer.} Under honesty for a selected intensity-optimized pair, designers can compute the joint probability--diameter obstruction and locate both elbows without treating it as a global minimax guarantee.

\begin{proposition}[prop:li-zhao-joint-intensity-clipped-frontier]\label{prop:li-zhao-joint-intensity-clipped-frontier}
Every intensity-optimized clipped-ramp array belongs, for every \(n\), to Li and Zhao's baseline binary finite-population adaptive-design class, and its nonzero late rare-arm increments violate their negligible-increment Condition 3(ii). Consequently, if a sequence of possibly randomized complete-log confidence sets is asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest uniformly over that baseline class, then for every \(0<d<d_{\max}\), under each sign of the intensity-optimized clipped-ramp pair with start \(a(d)=1-x(d)\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge
 \left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+.
\]
This transfers only the converse. It transfers neither the pair-specific attaining rule nor the positive profile-net calibration theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every \(n,t\), the intensity-optimized construction has binary probabilities
\[
 e_{nt}(1)=\min\{\rho,q_\star(t/n)/t\},
 \qquad
 e_{nt}(0)=1-e_{nt}(1).
\]
They are deterministic functions of every available history and sum to one.  Because \(q_\star\ge c/2>0\) and \(0<\rho<1\), both are strictly between zero and one.  The intensity-optimized properties proposition supplies fixed bounded potential outcomes and predictable zero regressions.  The cited baseline-class lemma therefore implies every-\(n\) membership in Li and Zhao's baseline binary finite-population adaptive class.  This inclusion is independent of the eventual profile event.

We verify the failure of the cited negligible-increment condition.  For a fixed start \(a_\star\), put
\[
 g(u)=\min\{L_r(u-a_\star)_+,M\},
 \qquad
 X_{nt}=(A_{nt}-e_{nt})\frac{\sigma g(t/n)}{e_{nt}}.
\]
By predictable randomization,
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\sigma(X_{nt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1})
 =g(t/n)^2\{e_{nt}^{-1}-1\}.
\]
Distinct increments are orthogonal by iterated conditioning.  The cap is inactive at all \(t>\lfloor a_\star n\rfloor\) once \(n\) is sufficiently large, by the established intensity-optimized properties.  Thus the variance \(W_n\) of the \(\sqrt n\)-scaled scalar AIPW error is
\[
 W_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n
 g(t/n)^2\left\{\frac{t}{q_\star(t/n)}-1\right\}.
\]
The functions \(g\) and \(q_\star\) are continuous and \(q_\star\ge c/2\).  Therefore a Riemann-sum calculation gives
\[
 \frac{W_n}{n}\longrightarrow
 w_\star:=\int_{a_\star}^1
 \frac{u g(u)^2}{q_\star(u)}\,du>0.
\]
Positivity follows from \(a_\star<1\), \(L_r>0\), and \(M>0\).

At \(t=n\), an outcome envelope must be at least \(g(1)>0\), the regression envelope is zero, and for large \(n\) the smaller assignment probability is \(\underline e_n=q_\star(1)/n\).  Hence the scalar negligible-maximum ratio in the cited Condition 3(ii) obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 W_n^{-1}n^{-1}
 \max_{1\le t\le n}\frac{(L_t+M_t)^2}{\underline e_t^2}
 &\ge
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{g(1)^2n}{q_\star(1)^2W_n}\\
 &=\frac{g(1)^2}{q_\star(1)^2w_\star}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The ratio is not \(o(1)\), proving failure of the cited Gaussian premise.

Suppose now that the confidence sets are asymptotically \((1-\alpha)\)-honest uniformly over Li and Zhao's baseline class.  Every-\(n\) inclusion gives, for the selected intensity-optimized pair,
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\gamma_n\le\alpha.
\]
Use the exact finite common-subprobability inequality for that pair:
\[
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge\Delta_n\}
 \ge[\eta_n^\star-2\gamma_n]_+.
\]
At the start \(a(d)=1-x(d)\), the sharp intensity-optimized theorem gives
\[
 \Delta_n\ge d,
 \qquad
 \eta_n^\star\longrightarrow\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}.
\]
Event inclusion, the preceding bound on \(\gamma_n\), and continuity of the positive-part map yield
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_\sigma\{\operatorname{diam}(D_n)\ge d\}
 \ge
 \left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+.
\]
No procedure over the baseline class was constructed.  The argument consequently transfers only the converse, not the pair-specific attaining allocation or the positive structured-class profile-net calibration theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The intensity-optimized arrays satisfy Li and Zhao's baseline requirements for every \(n\), so baseline uniform honesty supplies the pair-honesty premise needed by the direct common-void argument. \textit{Gap.} The new family's eventual profile contract is insufficient for this note's all-\(n\) structured-class premise, but irrelevant to its every-\(n\) inclusion in Li and Zhao's broader baseline class. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition transfers only the joint converse; it does not transfer the pairwise attaining allocation or the structured positive calibration algorithm.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The finite-transfer theorem couples the AIPW statistic, not the complete experiments, and therefore does not imply the all-estimator lower bounds. Those bounds instead use exact common subprobabilities on late-arm void events. For both the constant-intensity capped family and the separate intensity-optimized family, \(G_n=\Omega\) is proved only eventually: at a finite included index where the uncapped intensity divided by time exceeds \(\rho\), the intensity discrepancy can exceed \(\delta_{q,n}\). Their optimized frontiers therefore assume honesty on the selected pair rather than inferring it from full structured-class membership. A larger-class uniform-honesty premise is sufficient only after separate every-\(n\) inclusion is proved, as in the direct Li--Zhao baseline transfers. Both matching procedures know their selected pair, so neither establishes global minimax attainment. The positive theorem still requires its supplied profile and early-variation contracts, the \(0.442M\) high-hazard result remains a subfamily upper bound, and the polynomial bit-complexity question remains open because no polynomial bound controls profile boxes straddling the certification boundary.

\section{Honest scope}

Uniform finite-experiment coverage applies to the original bounded Lipschitz intensity and signed-residual profile subclass with its supplied common profile event, early quadratic-variation envelope, and certified inputs. The constant-intensity envelope \([(1-x(d))^c-2\alpha]_+\) and the stronger joint-intensity envelope \(\left[\exp\{-H(1-x(d))\}-2\alpha\right]_+\) are selected-pair honesty conclusions: eventual \(G_n=\Omega\) does not by itself place either capped witness in the full structured class for every \(n\). Direct every-\(n\) baseline inclusion transfers the corresponding converses, but neither positive calibration nor attainment, to Li and Zhao's class. Exact attainment is confined to each selected two-sign pair; no global minimax procedure is claimed. The original linear-ramp frontier, exact \(1/8\) and \(0.60710678\) diagnostic with its \(\rho\ge1/2\) cap qualification, directed \(13/32\) certificate, and high-hazard \(0.442M\) radius remain valid. No polynomial certificate-complexity exponent is asserted. The Liberali et al. application remains conditional on a prespecified expected-endpoint bridge.
