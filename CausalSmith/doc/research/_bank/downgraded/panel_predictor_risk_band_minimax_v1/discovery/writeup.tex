\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}predictor\_\allowbreak{}risk\_\allowbreak{}band\_\allowbreak{}minimax}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Predictor weighting changes both the donor face and the future counterfactual performance of a synthetic control. This paper identifies the entire population prediction-MSE graph from the stated block-stable fixed-rank panel, constructs a uniformly valid root-\(n\) same-panel band over the predictor simplex, and computes the exact compact-local iid-Gaussian minimax expected maximum width through finite KKT-cell, conic-distance, and primal--dual linear programs with certified vanishing error. Exact attainment is local to the specified Gaussian neighborhoods; the global projection band is validity-only. Every procedure uniformly honest over the full panel class nevertheless obeys the computed local lower bound because that honesty requirement contains the Gaussian paths.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} VivesiBastida2022PredictorSelection and PouliotXieLiu2026DegreesFreedom make predictor selection operational but do not attach an honest population-risk certificate to every candidate weighting. CanenSong2026SimplexWeight treats one fixed simplex objective. FreybergerRai2018UniformBands restricts attention to bands induced by a nonrandom-shape set and develops low-level sufficient conditions and a worked approximation for weighted area; other minimum-size confidence-set results treat scalar linear targets or additive set-measure losses. None addresses an unrestricted externally randomized max-curve-diameter decision for a piecewise-affine risk curve whose KKT face moves with the local query.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is the observable graph \(G_P\) together with the exact compact-local price, measured by worst-case expected maximum interval width, of covering that graph when predictor and moment perturbations move donor faces on the same root-\(n\) scale.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper combines row-span completion, a joint block-mean and covariance expansion, exact shifted-QP inversion, critical-cone curve geometry, and certified finite primal and dual decision programs. Bidirectional vanishing-deficiency transfer between the observed iid-Gaussian panel and its Gaussian shift proves the matching compact-local lower bound and attainment. The broader mixing-class result is uniform validity of the global projection band; only the lower bound, not Gaussian-local attainment, transfers to every honesty class containing the Gaussian paths.

\section{Related work}

CanenSong2026SimplexWeight, Theorem 3.1, is a high-level fixed-objective result under uniform joint asymptotic normality, nondegeneracy, and variance consistency. Its explicit synthetic-control example instead uses repeated cross-sections with iid individuals within dates and positive group shares; the fixed-cross-section long panel here may instantiate the abstract theorem separately at a fixed predictor weight, but it is not that published example. VivesiBastida2022PredictorSelection studies an aggregate fixed-dimensional long panel with time-varying linear factors, oracle separation and injectivity, and an \(\ell_1\)-penalized bilevel predictor-selection objective, rather than this fixed-ridge block-stable class. CattaneoFengTitiunik2021PredictionIntervals allows a fixed-unit stationary weakly dependent time series but targets future-counterfactual and treatment-effect prediction intervals, not simultaneous inference for a population-MSE graph. FangSantos2019Directional explains the failure of ordinary bootstrap reasoning at nonsmooth maps. EvansHansenStark2005MinimaxConfidence, SchaferStark2009OptimalRegions, and ArmstrongKolesar2018OptimalInference provide related decision-theoretic architectures, but not the unrestricted externally randomized maximum-curve-width problem for a moving-KKT risk graph. FreybergerRai2018UniformBands studies bands induced by a nonrandom-shape set and develops its main approximation for weighted-area loss; no strict numerical separation from that work is asserted here. The present results are derived for the displayed panel class itself, not by identifying it with any of these published classes.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(c_A=\max_{i,j\in A}d_{\Psi}(u_i,u_j)\), with \(c_A=0\) when \(|A|<2\)
  \item \texttt{VNh} --- \texttt{finite randomized decision-\allowbreak{}program value}; \(V_{N,h}(q)\)
  \item \texttt{mhalf} --- \texttt{positive scalar subexperiment radius}; \((0,M]\)
  \item \texttt{ascale} --- \texttt{positive scalar hinge-\allowbreak{}path coordinate}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} In the hinge subexperiment, \(a\) multiplies the first pre-factor row and has center \(a=1\).

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-dimensions]\label{ass:fixed-dimensions}
The integers \(J,K,L,r\) are fixed, \(L\ge r\), and \(1\le r\le\min\{L,J\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed-dimensional synthetic-control factor asymptotics, \cite{Fry2024MomentsSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sampling-fractions]\label{ass:sampling-fractions}
The known blocks are contiguous, \(n_\ell/n\to p_\ell\in[c_{\mathrm{lo}},1]\) for every \(\ell\), and \(T_1/n\to\lambda\in[c_{\mathrm{lo}},C_{\mathrm{hi}}]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (proportional long-panel sampling, \cite{Fry2024MomentsSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:block-factor-means]\label{ass:block-factor-means}
For a pre-treatment date in block \(\ell\), the untreated mean vector is \((a_{0\ell},A_{\ell\cdot})=(f_\ell^{\prime}\mu_0,f_\ell^{\prime}U_D)\); in the stable post regime, the untreated target and donor means are \((f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}\mu_0,d^{\prime})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The known block-stable factor means, together with the row-span completion derived from the rank structure, are specialized restrictions of this paper; VivesiBastida2022PredictorSelection allows time-varying factors and is not support for this exact condition.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-signals]\label{ass:bounded-signals}
The entries of \(F_{\mathrm{pre}},U_D,\mu_0,f_{\mathrm{post}}\) and the operator norm of \(S\) are bounded by \(C_{\mathrm{hi}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded fixed-factor panel, \cite{Fry2024MomentsSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rank-spectrum]\label{ass:rank-spectrum}
The matrix \(A\) has rank \(r\), and each of its \(r\) nonzero singular values belongs to \([c_{\mathrm{lo}},C_{\mathrm{hi}}]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed-rank spectral separation, \cite{ChoiKwonLiao2024SyntheticControls}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stationary-errors]\label{ass:stationary-errors}
The process \((\varepsilon_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) is strictly stationary and satisfies \(\mathbb E[\varepsilon_t]=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (mean-zero strict stationarity, \cite{Fry2024MomentsSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:subgaussian-errors]\label{ass:subgaussian-errors}
The vector sub-Gaussian norm satisfies \(\lVert\varepsilon_t\rVert_{\psi_2}\le C_{\mathrm{hi}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform sub-Gaussian tails, \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021PredictionIntervals}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-spectrum]\label{ass:covariance-spectrum}
Every eigenvalue of \(\Sigma\) belongs to \([c_{\mathrm{lo}},C_{\mathrm{hi}}]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is the paper's own uniform contemporaneous-covariance regularity restriction; Canen--Song's nondegeneracy condition concerns a joint estimator-and-score covariance and does not support the displayed eigenvalue bound on the contemporaneous covariance matrix.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:geometric-mixing]\label{ass:geometric-mixing}
The absolute-regularity coefficients satisfy \(\beta(k)\le C_{\mathrm{hi}}\exp(-c_{\mathrm{lo}}k)\) for every \(k\ge0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (geometric beta mixing, \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021PredictionIntervals}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:long-run-spectrum]\label{ass:long-run-spectrum}
Every eigenvalue of \(\Omega\) belongs to \([c_{\mathrm{lo}},C_{\mathrm{hi}}]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform long-run covariance nondegeneracy, \cite{Fry2024MomentsSC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:diffuse-predictors]\label{ass:diffuse-predictors}
For predictor \(k\), every original date in block \(\ell\) enters with the known coefficient \(S_{k\ell}/n_\ell\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The exact within-block coefficients are this paper's specialized block-average construction, motivated by synthetic-control predictor averaging but not attributed as the exact known proportional-block design in Vives-i-Bastida.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-chart]\label{ass:local-chart}
The map \(\xi\) is twice continuously differentiable and locally one-to-one on the finite-dimensional rank-\(r\) Gaussian signal-and-noise manifold \(\Theta_n^{\mathrm G}\) at \(\theta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (smooth identifiable LAN chart, \cite{LeCamYang2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-gaussian-submodel]\label{ass:local-gaussian-submodel}
On the local submodel, pre-period errors and donor-only post-period errors are independent and identically distributed Gaussian vectors with the covariance submatrices induced by \(\Sigma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-dimensional Gaussian local experiment, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018OptimalInference}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:global-parameterization]\label{lem:global-parameterization}
The map \(\mathsf P_n:\Theta_n\to\mathcal M_n\), \(\theta\mapsto P_\theta\), is measurable. Every \(\theta\in\Theta_n\) induces \(P_\theta\in\mathcal M_n\), every \(P\in\mathcal M_n\) has at least one representing \(\theta\in\Theta_n\), and all representing parameters agree on \(z(\theta)\), \(w_{P_\theta}\), and \(R_{P_\theta}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a pre-treatment block \(\ell\), put
\[
m_\ell=(a_{0\ell},A_{\ell\cdot})^{\prime}.
\]
By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:block\mbox{-}factor\mbox{-}means}\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stationary\mbox{-}errors}\), and Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}map}\), the finite-dimensional distributions induced by \(\theta\in\Theta_n\) have
\[
E_\theta Y_t=m_\ell\quad(t\in\ell),\qquad
E_\theta Y_{D,t}=d\quad(t>n),
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(Y_t-m_\ell)=\Sigma.
\]
All the remaining restrictions on the induced errors, block proportions, ranks, spectra, and predictor coefficients are precisely the member properties in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}parameter\mbox{-}space}\).  They are also precisely the corresponding member properties in Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\).  It follows directly that
\[
\mathsf P_n(\theta)\in\mathcal M_n
\qquad\text{for every }\theta\in\Theta_n.
\]

Conversely, let \(P\in\mathcal M_n\).  The block-mean condition supplies matrices and vectors \(F_{\mathrm{pre}},U_D,\mu_0,f_{\mathrm{post}}\) such that
\[
A=F_{\mathrm{pre}}U_D,\qquad
a_0=F_{\mathrm{pre}}\mu_0,\qquad
d^{\prime}=f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}U_D.
\]
The stationary-error condition supplies a stationary extension with law \(\mathsf Q_\varepsilon\), mean zero, and contemporaneous covariance \(\Sigma\).  Each other member property of \(\mathcal M_n\) is one of the conditions imposed in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}parameter\mbox{-}space}\).  Thus
\[
\theta_P=(A,a_0,d,\Sigma,\mathsf Q_\varepsilon)\in\Theta_n.
\]
The pushforward formula in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}map}\) then reproduces every observed pre-treatment coordinate and every observed donor-only post-treatment coordinate, so
\[
\mathsf P_n(\theta_P)=P.
\]
This proves surjectivity; it does not assert that the stationary extension beyond the observed cylinders is unique.

We next verify measurability.  Give the path space \((\mathbb R^{J+1})^{\mathbb Z}\) its product Borel sigma-field.  For a finite set of dates \(I\), let \(Q_I\) be the corresponding marginal of \(\mathsf Q_\varepsilon\), let \(g_I(A,a_0,d)\) be the vector of the appropriate block and post means, and let \(\pi_I\) delete the unobserved target post coordinates.  For every Borel set \(B\) in the observed coordinates at the dates in \(I\),
\[
P_\theta(B)
=\int
\mathbf 1\!\left\{\pi_I e+g_I(A,a_0,d)\in B\right\}
\,dQ_I(e).
\]
The integrand is jointly Borel in the Euclidean signal coordinate and \(e\).  Approximation first by nonnegative simple functions and then by bounded monotone limits shows that its integral is measurable jointly in the signal coordinate and in the evaluation maps \(Q_I\mapsto Q_I(C)\).  Hence \(\theta\mapsto P_\theta(B)\) is measurable for every finite-dimensional cylinder \(B\).  The collection of observed-panel events for which this conclusion holds is a monotone class containing the cylinder pi-system; it is therefore the whole observed-panel sigma-field.  This is exactly measurability of
\[
\mathsf P_n:(\Theta_n,\mathcal B_\Theta)\longrightarrow
(\mathcal M_n,\mathcal E),
\]
where \(\mathcal B_\Theta\) and \(\mathcal E\) are the sigma-fields specified in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}map}\).

Finally, the observed law itself determines its block means \(m_\ell\), its donor post mean \(d\), and its pre-treatment contemporaneous covariance \(\Sigma\).  It therefore determines
\[
z(\theta)=(A,a_0,d,\operatorname{vech}\Sigma).
\]
Since \(X=SA\) and \(x=Sa_0\), the objective in Definition \(\mathrm{def:weight\mbox{-}map}\), apart from a term independent of \(w\), is
\[
\frac12 w^{\prime}H(v)w-f(v)^{\prime}w.
\]
Because \(\rho>0\),
\[
H(v)=2\{X^{\prime}\operatorname{diag}(v)X+\rho I_J\}
\succeq 2\rho I_J.
\]
Thus the objective is strictly convex on the nonempty compact simplex \(\Delta_J\), and its minimizer is unique.  Consequently \(w_{P_\theta}(v)\) depends only on \((A,a_0)\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:risk\mbox{-}graph}\) then shows that \(R_{P_\theta}(v)\) depends only on \((A,a_0,d,\Sigma)\).  Any two representations of one observed law therefore agree on \(z(\theta)\), on the entire map \(w_{P_\theta}\), and on the entire map \(R_{P_\theta}\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma makes the global confidence region and its risk projection range over one coherent admissible parameterization of observed-panel laws. \textit{Gap.} The synthetic-control moment literature states law classes and identified coordinates separately; the risk-graph projection requires an explicit measurable bridge between them. \textit{Consumer.} The global band can be implemented over rank-respecting parameter coordinates without silently projecting over objects that fail to define observed-panel laws.

\begin{lemma}[lem:row-span-completion]\label{lem:row-span-completion}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_n\), the target untreated post-regime mean is identified from observed-law means by \(c_0=d^{\prime}A^+a_0\); this value is invariant across all solutions of \(A\gamma=a_0\) and across factor rotations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_n\).  By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:block\mbox{-}factor\mbox{-}means}\),
\[
A=F_{\mathrm{pre}}U_D,\qquad
a_0=F_{\mathrm{pre}}\mu_0,\qquad
d^{\prime}=f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}U_D,
\]
and the target untreated mean in the stable post regime is
\[
c_0=f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}\mu_0.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:rank\mbox{-}spectrum}\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=r\).  Because \(F_{\mathrm{pre}}\) has \(r\) columns and \(U_D\) has \(r\) rows,
\[
r=\operatorname{rank}(F_{\mathrm{pre}}U_D)
\leq\min\{\operatorname{rank}(F_{\mathrm{pre}}),
\operatorname{rank}(U_D)\}
\leq r.
\]
Hence \(F_{\mathrm{pre}}\) has full column rank and \(U_D\) has full row rank.

In particular, full row rank of \(U_D\) provides \(\gamma\in\mathbb R^J\) with \(U_D\gamma=\mu_0\), whence
\[
A\gamma=F_{\mathrm{pre}}U_D\gamma
=F_{\mathrm{pre}}\mu_0=a_0.
\]
Thus \(a_0\in\operatorname{col}(A)\), so the Moore--Penrose identity yields
\[
AA^+a_0=a_0.
\]
Now let \(\widetilde\gamma\) be any solution of \(A\widetilde\gamma=a_0\).  Since \(F_{\mathrm{pre}}\) has trivial null space,
\[
0=A\widetilde\gamma-a_0
=F_{\mathrm{pre}}(U_D\widetilde\gamma-\mu_0)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
U_D\widetilde\gamma=\mu_0.
\]
It follows that every such solution has the same post completion:
\[
d^{\prime}\widetilde\gamma
=f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}U_D\widetilde\gamma
=f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}\mu_0=c_0.
\]
Taking the particular solution \(\widetilde\gamma=A^+a_0\) gives the observed-law identifying formula
\[
c_0=d^{\prime}A^+a_0.
\]
The right-hand side uses only the pre-block means and donor post mean, which are observed-law means under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:block\mbox{-}factor\mbox{-}means}\) and the mean-zero part of Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stationary\mbox{-}errors}\); it does not use the treated target's post-treatment outcome.

For completeness, under an invertible factor rotation \(G\), replace
\[
F_{\mathrm{pre}}\ \text{by }F_{\mathrm{pre}}G,\qquad
U_D\ \text{by }G^{-1}U_D,\qquad
\mu_0\ \text{by }G^{-1}\mu_0,\qquad
f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}\ \text{by }f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}G.
\]
These transformations leave \(A,a_0,d\), the scalar \(f_{\mathrm{post}}^{\prime}\mu_0\), and therefore \(d^{\prime}A^+a_0\) unchanged.  This proves both asserted invariances and identification.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:kkt-stratification]\label{prop:kkt-stratification}
The map \(v\mapsto w_P(v)\) admits a complete finite semialgebraic stratification indexed by \(B\) and strict-slack sign patterns. On support \(B\), the formulas \((w_B)_B=H_{BB}^{-1}(f_B-\eta_B\mathbf 1)\), \((w_B)_{B^c}=0\), and \(s_{B^c}=H_{B^cB}(w_B)_B-f_{B^c}+\eta_B\mathbf 1\) are valid exactly when \((w_B)_B>0\) and \(s_{B^c}\ge0\). Consequently, \(G_P\) is an observable semialgebraic graph.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apart from a constant independent of \(w\), the criterion equals
\[
\frac12w^\prime H(v)w-f(v)^\prime w,\qquad H(v)=2\{X^\prime\operatorname{diag}(v)X+\rho I_J\}.
\]
Since \(H(v)\succeq2\rho I_J\), there is one minimizer. With equality multiplier \(\eta\) and slack \(s\), the necessary and sufficient KKT equations are
\[
H(v)w-f(v)+\eta\mathbf1-s=0,\quad \mathbf1^\prime w=1,\quad w\geq0,\quad s\geq0,\quad w_js_j=0.
\]
Fix \(B=\{j:w_j>0\}\neq\varnothing\). Complementarity gives \(s_B=0\), whence
\[
w_B=H_{BB}^{-1}(f_B-\eta\mathbf1),\qquad
\eta=\frac{\mathbf1^\prime H_{BB}^{-1}f_B-1}{\mathbf1^\prime H_{BB}^{-1}\mathbf1}.
\]
The inverse exists and the denominator is positive because \(H_{BB}\succeq2\rho I\). On \(B^c\),
\[
s_{B^c}=H_{B^cB}w_B-f_{B^c}+\eta\mathbf1.
\]
Thus the candidate satisfies all KKT equations exactly when \(w_B>0\) and \(s_{B^c}\geq0\). Sufficiency also follows directly: for every feasible \(\widetilde w\),
\[
\{H(v)w-f(v)\}^\prime(\widetilde w-w)=(-\eta\mathbf1+s)^\prime(\widetilde w-w)=s^\prime\widetilde w\geq0.
\]

The entries of \(H,f\) are affine polynomials in \(v\). By the adjugate formula, \(H_{BB}^{-1},w_B,\eta_B,s_{B^c}\) are rational with denominators strictly positive on \(\Delta_K\). Clearing them turns all sign restrictions into polynomial inequalities. Refining each support region by zero/strict inactive-slack signs gives finitely many semialgebraic strata covering \(\Delta_K\). On each stratum \(w_P(v)\) is rational. On the fixed-rank signal set, \(A^+\) is rational on each nonvanishing \(r\)-minor chart. Substitution into \(R_P\) makes \(G_P\) a finite union of semialgebraic graphs. Its coefficients \(A,a_0,d,\Sigma\) are the observed pre and donor-post means and the observed pre covariance, proving observability.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite face atlas is the computational substrate for both the global graph and the local optimal-band problem. \textit{Gap.} CanenSong2026SimplexWeight and HsiehShiShum2022MathProgramming encode one fixed constrained optimizer; neither stratifies a continuum of predictor-indexed population objectives and its risk graph. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can enumerate exactly which donor face supports each proposed predictor weighting before reporting risk uncertainty.

\begin{lemma}[lem:joint-moment-expansion]\label{lem:joint-moment-expansion}
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_n\), the estimator \(\widehat z\) has a joint root-\(n\) asymptotic linear expansion driven by weighted contiguous-block sums of \(\phi_t\), including the covariance between mean and second-moment estimation; the block/HAC estimator \(\widehat V\) is uniformly consistent for the resulting nonsingular covariance \(V(P)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
p_{\ell n}=\frac{n_\ell}{n},\qquad
\lambda_n=\frac{T_1}{n},\qquad
\overline\varepsilon_\ell=\frac1{n_\ell}\sum_{t\in\ell}\varepsilon_t,
\qquad
q_t=\operatorname{vech}(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_t^{\prime}-\Sigma).
\]
The denominators are positive, and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sampling\mbox{-}fractions}\) gives
\[
\min_\ell p_{\ell n}\geq c>0,\qquad
c\leq\lambda_n\leq C
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over the model class.  Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:block\mbox{-}factor\mbox{-}means}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:stationary\mbox{-}errors}\) give the exact mean identities
\[
\sqrt n\{\overline Y_\ell-m_\ell\}
=\frac1{p_{\ell n}\sqrt n}
\sum_{t\in\ell}\varepsilon_t,
\qquad
\sqrt n(\widehat d-d)
=\frac1{\lambda_n\sqrt n}
\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+T_1}\varepsilon_{D,t}.
\]
The pre-block-demeaned covariance in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}moments}\) satisfies the exact matrix identity
\[
\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma
=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}
(\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_t^{\prime}-\Sigma)
-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_{\ell n}
\overline\varepsilon_\ell\overline\varepsilon_\ell^{\prime}.
\tag{1}
\]

We record the uniform moment bound needed to control the last term.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:subgaussian\mbox{-}errors}\) gives a uniform moment of every fixed order for each coordinate of \(\varepsilon_t\), and hence for each coordinate polynomial of fixed degree.  If \(U_0\) and \(V_k\) are two such centered coordinate polynomials, truncate them at level \(a>0\).  The definition of the absolute-regularity coefficient and a decomposition into the two truncated variables plus their tails give
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(U_0,V_k)|
\leq 4a^2\beta(k)
+C_m a^{-(m-2)}
\]
for every fixed \(m>2\).  Using Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:geometric\mbox{-}mixing}\), choosing \(a=\exp(\delta k)\), and then choosing \(m\) sufficiently large yields constants independent of \(P\) such that
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(U_0,V_k)|\leq C e^{-ck}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus all covariance series used below converge absolutely and uniformly.  Applying (2) to coordinates of \(\varepsilon_t\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_n}
E_P\left\|\sqrt{n_\ell}\,\overline\varepsilon_\ell\right\|_2^2
\leq C.
\]
Since \(L\) is fixed and \(p_{\ell n}\) is bounded away from zero,
\[
\sqrt n\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_{\ell n}
\left\|\overline\varepsilon_\ell
\overline\varepsilon_\ell^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P(n^{-1/2})=o_P(1)
\tag{3}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_n\).  Equations (1)--(3) therefore give
\[
\sqrt n\,\operatorname{vech}(\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma)
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}q_t+o_P(1)
\tag{4}
\]
uniformly.  The displayed mean identities and (4), followed only by the fixed coordinate reordering that separates \(a_0\) from \(A\), are the asserted joint asymptotic linear expansion of \(\widehat z\).  In particular, the same summands \(\phi_t=(\varepsilon_t^{\prime},q_t^{\prime})^{\prime}\) drive both the pre means and the covariance estimate, so their cross-covariance is retained.

We now prove the uniform Gaussian approximation for that expansion.  It suffices to consider an arbitrary fixed scalar linear combination.  Over each pre block its summand has the form
\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}
\left\{\frac{a_\ell^{\prime}}{p_{\ell n}}\varepsilon_t
+b^{\prime}q_t\right\},
\]
and over the post block it has the form
\[
\frac1{\lambda_n\sqrt n}c^{\prime}\varepsilon_{D,t}.
\]
Do not permit a big block to cross any of the finitely many regime boundaries.  Within each regime divide the dates into big blocks of length \(b_n=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), separated by gaps of length \(g_n=\lceil C_0\log n\rceil\).  By (2), the variance of the normalized sum over all gaps is
\[
O(g_n/b_n)=o(1).
\tag{5}
\]
The absolute-regularity definition expresses the total-variation distance between a new block conditional on its past and its marginal law as at most \(\beta(g_n)\).  Splitting the two measures into their common part and their two residual parts constructs, block by block, an independent copy whose mismatch probability is at most \(\beta(g_n)\).  A union bound over \(O(n/b_n)\) blocks gives
\[
P\{\text{some coupled block differs}\}
\leq C(n/b_n)e^{-c g_n}=o(1)
\tag{6}
\]
when \(C_0\) is sufficiently large.

Expanding fourth moments and using (2) to sum the resulting covariances gives, uniformly for a big-block sum \(S_{jn}\),
\[
E|S_{jn}|^4\leq Cb_n^2.
\]
Consequently the independent coupled blocks satisfy Lindeberg's condition, because for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\frac1n\sum_j
E\!\left[S_{jn}^2
\mathbf 1\{|S_{jn}|>\epsilon\sqrt n\}\right]
\leq\frac1{\epsilon^2n^2}\sum_jE|S_{jn}|^4
\leq C\frac{b_n}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
Taylor expansion of the characteristic function of each centered independent block, (7), and multiplication of the block characteristic functions show that the coupled normalized sum has characteristic function
\[
\exp\{-s^2\sigma_P^2/2+o(1)\}
\]
uniformly for bounded \(s\), where \(\sigma_P^2\) is the variance limit of the original weighted sum.  Equations (5) and (6) transfer this conclusion to the original sum.  Applying the same calculation to every fixed linear combination gives the uniform multivariate Gaussian approximation.

Absolute summability in (2) also computes the covariance.  Sums from two distinct contiguous blocks have covariance \(O(1)\) before the \(n^{-1}\) normalization, because
\[
\sum_{j\geq0}\sum_{k\geq0}e^{-c(j+k)}<\infty.
\]
Their limiting normalized covariance is therefore zero, including between the adjacent pre and post regimes.  For coefficient vectors \(a_1,\ldots,a_L,b,c\), the limiting quadratic form is
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_\ell
\begin{pmatrix}a_\ell/p_\ell\\ b\end{pmatrix}^{\!\prime}
\Omega
\begin{pmatrix}a_\ell/p_\ell\\ b\end{pmatrix}
+\lambda^{-1}c^{\prime}
\Omega_{\varepsilon_D\varepsilon_D}c.
\tag{8}
\]
This gives pre-mean blocks \(\Omega_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}/p_\ell\), the covariance-moment block \(\Omega_{qq}\), the mean--covariance blocks inherited from \(\Omega\), and the donor post-mean block \(\Omega_{\varepsilon_D\varepsilon_D}/\lambda\).  In particular, the last block is not \(\Sigma_{DD}/\lambda\) unless the relevant serial covariances vanish.  By Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:sampling\mbox{-}fractions}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:long\mbox{-}run\mbox{-}spectrum}\), (8) is bounded above by a fixed multiple of
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\|a_\ell\|_2^2+\|b\|_2^2+\|c\|_2^2
\]
and bounded below by a positive fixed multiple of the same expression.  Thus the resulting \(V(P)\) is uniformly bounded and uniformly nonsingular.

It remains to verify feasibility.  Implement the block/HAC construction in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}moments}\) with Bartlett lag weights and bandwidth \(h_n=\lceil C_1\log n\rceil\), deleting every lag pair crossing a pre-block boundary.  If the unobserved errors and \(\Sigma\) were used, (2) would give a truncation-and-taper bias
\[
O(e^{-ch_n})+O(h_n^{-1})=o(1).
\]
For a retained lag \(k\leq h_n\), another application of the truncation argument behind (2) to products of score coordinates gives variance \(O(h_n/n)\) uniformly: the two lagged products can overlap or be within their combined \(O(h_n)\) dependence neighborhood, and outside that neighborhood their covariance decays exponentially.  The triangle inequality over \(O(h_n)\) lags therefore gives the conservative stochastic error
\[
O_P(h_n^{3/2}/\sqrt n)=o_P(1)
\tag{9}
\]
uniformly.  There are at most \(C Lh_n^2\) deleted ordered pairs, so their normalized contribution is
\[
O_P(h_n^2/n)=o_P(1).
\tag{10}
\]
Replacing \(\varepsilon_t\) by \(Y_t-\overline Y_{\ell(t)}\) and \(\Sigma\) by \(\widehat\Sigma\), then expanding every product, adds terms bounded by
\[
O_P(h_n/\sqrt n+h_n^2/n)=o_P(1)
\tag{11}
\]
using (3), (4), fixed dimension, and the uniform fourth-moment bounds.  Equations (9)--(11) prove
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_n}
P\{\|\widehat V-V(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\epsilon\}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\epsilon>0).
\]
Together with (4), (7), and (8), this proves the stated uniform joint expansion, all required cross terms, uniform covariance consistency, and nonsingularity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Same-panel inference must propagate pre-weight estimation and covariance estimation jointly; fixed-weight post error variance is not the estimator variance. \textit{Gap.} Fry2024MomentsSC supplies fixed-donor mixing limits, while CattaneoFengTitiunik2021PredictionIntervals separates fitted-weight and future-outcome errors; the required joint block-mean and residual-covariance expansion for population MSE is not given there. \textit{Consumer.} The expansion supports feasible uncertainty quantification without sample splitting or target post-treatment counterfactuals.

\begin{theorem}[thm:global-risk-band]\label{thm:global-risk-band}
The band \(C_n\) is measurable and satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_n}\Pr_P\{R_P(v)\in C_n(v)\text{ for every }v\in\Delta_K\}\ge1-\alpha\), together with the explicit uniform stochastic diameter bound \(\lim_{M_{\mathrm{wid}}\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_n}\Pr_P\{\sup_{v\in\Delta_K}\sqrt n\,\operatorname{diam}C_n(v)>M_{\mathrm{wid}}\}=0\). Hence every measurable same-panel selector \(\widehat v\in\Delta_K\) obeys \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_n}\Pr_P\{R_P(\widehat v)\in C_n(\widehat v)\}\ge1-\alpha\), including sequences within \(O(n^{-1/2})\) of any donor-face change.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(q_{1-\alpha}=\chi^2_{q_z,1-\alpha}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:global\mbox{-}parameterization}\), every \(P\in\mathcal M_n\) has a representation \(\theta_P\in\Theta_n\), and all such representations have the same \(z(\theta_P)\) and risk graph.  By the uniform Gaussian approximation and nonsingularity established in \(\mathrm{lem:joint\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}expansion}\),
\[
V(P)^{-1/2}\sqrt n\{\widehat z-z(\theta_P)\}
\Longrightarrow N(0,I_{q_z})
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_n\), and
\[
\|\widehat V-V(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly.  The fixed positive-definite regularization in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}region}\) is used only on the event on which the unregularized estimate fails its fixed spectral bounds; the probability of that event tends to zero uniformly.  Therefore
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_n}
P\!\left\{
n\{\widehat z-z(\theta_P)\}^{\prime}
\widehat V^{-1}
\{\widehat z-z(\theta_P)\}
\leq q_{1-\alpha}
\right\}
\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{12}
\]
The event in (12) is exactly \(\{\theta_P\in E_n\}\).  On it \(E_n\) is nonempty and, for every \(v\in\Delta_K\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}band}\) gives
\[
\inf_{\theta\in E_n}R_{P_\theta}(v)
\leq R_{P_{\theta_P}}(v)=R_P(v)
\leq\sup_{\theta\in E_n}R_{P_\theta}(v).
\tag{13}
\]
The equality in (13) is a consequence of the identifying results \(\mathrm{lem:global\mbox{-}parameterization}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:row\mbox{-}span\mbox{-}completion}\), rather than a definition of a causal effect.  Equations (12) and (13) prove simultaneous coverage of the population prediction-risk graph.

We next derive the uniform diameter bound, including at moving donor faces.  Consider two admissible identified coordinates
\[
z=(A,a_0,d,\operatorname{vech}\Sigma),\qquad
\widetilde z=(\widetilde A,\widetilde a_0,
\widetilde d,\operatorname{vech}\widetilde\Sigma).
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}signals}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:rank\mbox{-}spectrum}\) put all signal coordinates in a fixed bounded set and give
\[
\|A^+\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq c_{\mathrm{lo}}^{-1}.
\]
For two legal rank-\(r\) matrices, direct expansion of the four Moore--Penrose equations gives the exact perturbation identity
\[
\begin{split}
\widetilde A^+-A^+
={}&-\widetilde A^+(\widetilde A-A)A^+\\
&+\widetilde A^+\widetilde A^{+\prime}
(\widetilde A-A)^{\prime}(I_L-AA^+)\\
&+(I_J-\widetilde A^+\widetilde A)
(\widetilde A-A)^{\prime}A^{+\prime}A^+.
\end{split}
\]
The two matrices have smallest nonzero singular value at least \(c_{\mathrm{lo}}\), and each matrix multiplying a difference on the outside is either a pseudoinverse or an orthogonal projection.  Taking operator norms therefore yields
\[
\|\widetilde A^+-A^+\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq 3c_{\mathrm{lo}}^{-2}
\|\widetilde A-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{14}
\]
Expanding the three factors in the completion and using (14) gives
\[
|\widetilde d^{\prime}\widetilde A^+\widetilde a_0
-d^{\prime}A^+a_0|
\leq C_c\|\widetilde z-z\|_2.
\tag{15}
\]

Let \(w=w_P(v)\) and \(\widetilde w=w_{\widetilde P}(v)\).  The two optimality variational inequalities on the same simplex are
\[
\langle H(v)w-f(v),\widetilde w-w\rangle\geq0,
\qquad
\langle\widetilde H(v)\widetilde w-widetilde f(v),
w-\widetilde w\rangle\geq0.
\]
Adding them and using \(\widetilde H(v)\succeq2\rho I_J\) gives
\[
2\rho\|\widetilde w-w\|_2^2
\leq
\left\langle
\{H(v)-\widetilde H(v)\}w+widetilde f(v)-f(v),
\widetilde w-w
\right\rangle.
\tag{16}
\]
Every simplex weight has Euclidean norm at most one.  Moreover \(X=SA\), \(x=Sa_0\), \(\|\operatorname{diag}(v)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1\), and \(S\) and all signals are uniformly bounded.  Hence
\[
\sup_{v\in\Delta_K}
\{\|\widetilde H(v)-H(v)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\widetilde f(v)-f(v)\|_2\}
\leq C_H\|\widetilde z-z\|_2.
\tag{17}
\]
Canceling one factor in (16), with the zero-difference case immediate, and using (17) proves
\[
\sup_{v\in\Delta_K}\|\widetilde w(v)-w(v)\|_2
\leq \frac{C_H}{2\rho}\|\widetilde z-z\|_2.
\tag{18}
\]
No support stability or strict complementarity is used in (16)--(18).

The risk in Definition \(\mathrm{def:risk\mbox{-}graph}\) is a quadratic polynomial in \((c_0,d,w,\Sigma)\).  Those arguments range over the fixed bounded parameter set in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}region}\).  Combining its elementary polynomial Lipschitz bound with (15) and (18) yields
\[
\sup_{v\in\Delta_K}
|R_{P_{\widetilde\theta}}(v)-R_{P_\theta}(v)|
\leq C_R\|\widetilde z-z\|_2
\tag{19}
\]
for all legal coordinates.  The constant is uniform over all donor supports and all predictor weights.

By the covariance bounds in \(\mathrm{lem:joint\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}expansion}\), there exist fixed \(0<\underline v<\overline v<\infty\) such that the event
\[
\mathcal G_n=
\{\underline v I_{q_z}\preceq\widehat V
\preceq\overline v I_{q_z}\}
\]
satisfies
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_n}P(\mathcal G_n^c)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(E_n\neq\varnothing\), \(\theta,\widetilde\theta\in E_n\), and \(\mathcal G_n\) occurs, the ellipsoid constraint and the triangle inequality imply
\[
\sqrt n\|z(\theta)-z(\widetilde\theta)\|_2
\leq2\sqrt{\overline v q_{1-\alpha}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (19) and (21) give the deterministic-on-\(\mathcal G_n\) bound
\[
\sup_{v\in\Delta_K}\sqrt n\,
\operatorname{diam}C_n(v)
\leq2C_R\sqrt{\overline v q_{1-\alpha}}.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(E_n=\varnothing\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}band}\) returns \([0,0]\), so the left side of (22) is zero.  Combining (20) and (22) proves
\[
\lim_{M_{\mathrm{wid}}\to\infty}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_n}
P\!\left\{
\sup_{v\in\Delta_K}\sqrt n\,
\operatorname{diam}C_n(v)>M_{\mathrm{wid}}
\right\}=0.
\]

We finally check measurability rather than leaving it implicit in the projection notation.  The finite-dimensional image
\[
\mathcal Z_n=\{z(\theta):\theta\in\Theta_n\}
\]
is bounded by the signal, covariance, and rank-spectrum assumptions.  It is closed: for any convergent sequence in this image, bounded factor coordinates have a convergent subsequence; the closed singular-value restrictions preserve rank \(r\); and the uniform sub-Gaussian bound makes the associated stationary path laws tight.  A weakly convergent subsequence preserves mean zero and covariance by uniform integrability, preserves stationarity on cylinders, and preserves the geometric absolute-regularity inequalities by finite-cylinder approximation.  Equation (2) makes the long-run covariance series converge uniformly, so its spectral restrictions also pass to the limit.  Hence \(\mathcal Z_n\) is compact.  The feasible identified-coordinate correspondence is the intersection of this compact set with the closed ellipsoid in Definition \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}region}\), and therefore has a Borel graph and compact values.  Joint continuity of the strongly convex optimizer, also following directly from (16), makes \((z,v)\mapsto R_z(v)\) continuous on each legal rank chart and continuous across their overlaps.  To see measurability directly, for any real \(a\), the event that the lower endpoint is less than \(a\) and the section is nonempty is the countable union over \(m\geq1\) of projections of the closed sets defined by the ellipsoid inequality and \(R_z(v)\leq a-m^{-1}\).  Each such projection is closed because its witness \(z\) ranges over compact \(\mathcal Z_n\).  The analogous superlevel description handles the upper endpoint.  Emptiness is measurable by minimizing the continuous ellipsoid criterion over compact \(\mathcal Z_n\).  Thus both endpoints of \(C_n(v)\) are measurable, including under the stated empty-set convention.

On the simultaneous event in (13), the inclusion holds for every \(v\in\Delta_K\).  It therefore holds at any measurable same-panel selector \(\widehat v\), without conditioning on or splitting off the data used to construct that selector:
\[
P\{R_P(\widehat v)\in C_n(\widehat v)\}
\geq
P\{R_P(v)\in C_n(v)\text{ for every }v\in\Delta_K\}.
\]
Taking the uniform lower limit and applying (12) gives the final selector claim.  Since neither (18), (19), nor (22) invokes a fixed active set, the result includes all sequences of queries or selectors lying within \(O(n^{-1/2})\) of any donor-face change.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the first same-panel certificate for the entire population prediction-risk graph and automatically protects unsplit predictor tuning. \textit{Gap.} VivesiBastida2022PredictorSelection and PouliotXieLiu2026DegreesFreedom tune predictors without an honest full-risk-graph band; CanenSong2026SimplexWeight covers a fixed objective rather than a continuum whose donor face moves. \textit{Consumer.} An implementation of the displayed fixed-ridge quadratic simplex estimator can compare predictor weights and retain a valid risk certificate after selecting one on the same pre panel; the theorem does not certify the different objectives implemented by Synth or SparseSC.

\begin{lemma}[lem:local-risk-map]\label{lem:local-risk-map}
Uniformly on \(\mathcal U\times\mathcal D\), \(w_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))=w_0+n^{-1/2}h(u,t)+O(n^{-1})\) and \(\sqrt n\{R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))-R_{P_{\theta_0}}(v_0)\}=\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)+o(1)\). The map \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) is continuous piecewise affine on finitely many polyhedral critical-cone cells and retains mean-completion, donor-mean, covariance-risk, and donor-weight derivatives.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\epsilon=n^{-1/2}\), write \(w_{n,u,t}=w_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))\), and set
\[
 H_{n,u,t}=H_{\theta_n(u)}(v_n(t)),\qquad
 f_{n,u,t}=f_{\theta_n(u)}(v_n(t)).
\]
The local-chart condition, the compactness of \(\mathcal U\times\mathcal D\), and twice continuous differentiability give, uniformly in \((u,t)\),
\[
 H_{n,u,t}=H_0+\epsilon\dot H(u,t)+O(\epsilon^2),
 \qquad
 f_{n,u,t}=f_0+\epsilon\dot f(u,t)+O(\epsilon^2).
\tag{1}
\]
The predictor path is feasible eventually: if \((v_0)_k=0\), the definition of \(\mathcal D\) gives \(t_k\geq0\); if \((v_0)_k>0\), compactness gives \((v_0)_k+\epsilon t_k>0\) uniformly eventually.  Since \(\rho>0\),
\[
 H_{n,u,t}\succeq 2\rho I_J,\qquad H_0\succeq2\rho I_J.
\tag{2}
\]
Ignoring a constant independent of \(w\), the criterion in Definition \(\mathrm{def:weight\mbox{-}map}\) is
\[
 q_{n,u,t}(w)=\frac12w^\prime H_{n,u,t}w-f_{n,u,t}^\prime w.
\]
Apply the variational inequalities for \(w_{n,u,t}\) and \(w_0\), and put \(r_{n,u,t}=w_{n,u,t}-w_0\).  Adding them and using (2) yields
\[
 2\rho\lVert r_{n,u,t}\rVert_2^2
 \leq
 \left\lVert (H_{n,u,t}-H_0)w_0-(f_{n,u,t}-f_0)\right\rVert_2
 \lVert r_{n,u,t}\rVert_2.
\]
Thus (1) implies
\[
 \sup_{u,t}\lVert w_{n,u,t}-w_0\rVert_2=O(\epsilon).
\tag{3}
\]
Choose \(j_\star\) with \(w_{0j_\star}>0\), which exists because \(\mathbf1^\prime w_0=1\).  By (3), \(w_{n,u,t,j_\star}>0\) uniformly eventually.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:kkt\mbox{-}stratification}\) then defines the multiplier continuously by
\[
 \eta_{n,u,t}=-(H_{n,u,t}w_{n,u,t}-f_{n,u,t})_{j_\star}.
\]
Hence \(\eta_{n,u,t}\to\eta_0\) uniformly, and
\[
 s_{n,u,t}=H_{n,u,t}w_{n,u,t}-f_{n,u,t}+\eta_{n,u,t}\mathbf1
\]
converges uniformly to \(s_0\).  If \(w_{0j}=0<s_{0j}\), the perturbed slack is positive eventually, so complementary slackness forces \(w_{n,u,t,j}=0\).  If \(w_{0j}=s_{0j}=0\), feasibility gives \(w_{n,u,t,j}\geq0\).  Consequently
\[
 k_{n,u,t}:=\epsilon^{-1}(w_{n,u,t}-w_0)\in\mathcal C_0.
\tag{4}
\]
For bounded \(k\) satisfying (4), expansion around \(w_0\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \epsilon^{-2}\{q_{n,u,t}(w_0+\epsilon k)-q_{n,u,t}(w_0)\}
 &=
 \epsilon^{-1}(H_{n,u,t}w_0-f_{n,u,t})^\prime k
 +\frac12k^\prime H_{n,u,t}k\\
 &=
 \frac12k^\prime H_0k+Q(u,t)^\prime k
 +O(\epsilon)(1+\lVert k\rVert_2^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the center KKT identity is \(H_0w_0-f_0=-\eta_0\mathbf1+s_0\).  Equality gives \(\mathbf1^\prime k=0\), and \(s_0^\prime k=0\) because positive-slack coordinates are fixed at zero and all remaining coordinates have zero slack.

Comparison of (5) at \(k_{n,u,t}\) and zero, (2), and boundedness of \(Q\) first show that \(k_{n,u,t}\) is uniformly bounded.  The limiting minimizer \(h(u,t)\) is uniformly bounded as well.  Therefore the lower bounds \(k_j\geq-w_{0j}/\epsilon\) on positive coordinates do not bind at either minimizer eventually.  The two problems may be compared on the fixed cone \(\mathcal C_0\).  Their gradients differ by \(O(\epsilon)\) on this bounded set.  Adding their variational inequalities and using \(2\rho\)-strong monotonicity gives
\[
 \sup_{u,t}\lVert k_{n,u,t}-h(u,t)\rVert_2=O(\epsilon).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
 w_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))
 =w_0+n^{-1/2}h(u,t)+O(n^{-1})
\tag{7}
\]
uniformly.  This permits any number of indices with \(w_{0j}=s_{0j}=0\); strict complementarity was not used.

For the risk expansion, fixed rank and the spectral gap make the pseudoinverse smooth along the rank-\(r\) chart.  Put \(\gamma_0=A_0^+a_{0,0}\).  Since \(a_0(\theta)\in\operatorname{col}(A(\theta))\), differentiation of \(A(\theta)\gamma(\theta)=a_0(\theta)\), for \(\gamma(\theta)=A(\theta)^+a_0(\theta)\), gives
\[
 A_0\dot\gamma(u)=\dot a_0(u)-\dot A(u)\gamma_0.
\]
Since \(d_0\in\operatorname{row}(A_0)\),
\[
 \dot c(u)
 =\dot d(u)^\prime\gamma_0
 +d_0^\prime A_0^+
   \{\dot a_0(u)-\dot A(u)\gamma_0\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Write \(\Sigma\) in target--donor blocks.  Equations (7)--(8) and a uniform second-order Taylor expansion of Definition \(\mathrm{def:risk\mbox{-}graph}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sqrt n\{R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))-R_{P_{\theta_0}}(v_0)\}\\
 &=
 2b_0\{\dot c(u)-\dot d(u)^\prime w_0\}
 +e_0^\prime\dot\Sigma(u)e_0\\
 &\quad
 +\{-2b_0d_0-2\Sigma_{D0}+2\Sigma_{DD}w_0\}^\prime h(u,t)
 +o(1)\\
 &=a(u)+g^\prime h(u,t)+o(1)
 =\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)+o(1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
uniformly.  The displayed terms separately retain completion, donor-mean, covariance-risk, and donor-weight perturbations.

Finally, \(\mathcal C_0\) is polyhedral, \(H_0\) is positive definite, and \(Q\) is linear.  For each subset of weak inequalities imposed as equalities, the KKT system gives a linear formula for \(h\), with primal and dual feasibility described by linear inequalities in \((u,t)\).  There are finitely many subsets.  Uniqueness makes their formulas agree on common boundaries.  Hence \(h\) and \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) are continuous piecewise affine on finitely many polyhedral cells.  Adding the variational inequalities for two limiting problems also gives
\[
 \lVert h(u,t)-h(u',t')\rVert_2
 \leq \frac{1}{2\rho}
 \lVert Q(u,t)-Q(u',t')\rVert_2,
\]
which supplies uniform continuity and completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The shifted critical-cone expansion is the correct local graph when moment noise and predictor queries move active faces at the same root-\(n\) scale. \textit{Gap.} Geyer1994ConstrainedM and FangSantos2019Directional analyze a fixed constraint map; this result must keep the root-\(n\) movement of the query and the KKT slack jointly and uniformly over a compact direction set. \textit{Consumer.} The local curve is the input needed to calibrate face-aware simultaneous bands rather than a pointwise fixed-cone approximation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:curve-decision-reduction]\label{prop:curve-decision-reduction}
For any admissible interval band and any finite parameter set \(F\subset\mathcal U\), let \(A_F\) be the random subset of points in \(F\) whose entire curves are covered. Then \(c(A_F)=\max_{u,u'\in A_F}d_\Psi(u,u')\) is no larger than the band's maximum width, and each grid-point coverage probability is preserved. Conversely, every measurable rule selecting a subset \(A\subseteq F\) induces the pointwise envelope band of those selected curves, with maximum width exactly \(c(A)\), and padding this envelope by the uniform \(u\)-modulus transports coverage from a finite net to \(\mathcal U\). For each pair \((u,u')\), \(d_\Psi(u,u')\) is the maximum of finitely many second-order-cone programs obtained by intersecting the two critical-cone cell systems with \(\mathcal D\). These finite-set reductions apply to every randomized measurable interval-section band and do not impose a nonrandom-shape restriction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite set \(F=\{u_1,\ldots,u_m\}\subset\mathcal U\).  Include any external randomizer in the sample point \(\omega\), and write \(C_\omega(t)\) for the realized interval section.  Define
\[
 A_F(\omega)
 =\left\{u_i\in F:\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)\in C_\omega(t)
       \text{ for every }t\in\mathcal D\right\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(C_\omega)
 =\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\operatorname{diam}C_\omega(t).
\tag{1}
\]
Admissibility in Definition \(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}band\mbox{-}class}\) includes measurability of each simultaneous-coverage event.  Since \(F\) is finite, \(A_F\) is therefore a measurable random subset of \(F\), including when the band is externally randomized.

If \(u_i,u_j\in A_F(\omega)\), then both \(\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)\) and \(\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_j,t)\) belong to the same interval \(C_\omega(t)\).  Hence, for every \(t\in\mathcal D\),
\[
 \left|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_j,t)\right|
 \leq \operatorname{diam}C_\omega(t).
\tag{2}
\]
By the definition
\[
 d_\Psi(u_i,u_j)
 =\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}
   \left|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_j,t)\right|,
\]
taking the supremum in \(t\) in (2), followed by the maximum over \(u_i,u_j\in A_F(\omega)\), gives
\[
 c\{A_F(\omega)\}
 =\max_{u_i,u_j\in A_F(\omega)}d_\Psi(u_i,u_j)
 \leq \mathcal L(C_\omega),
\tag{3}
\]
where the stipulated convention is \(c(A)=0\) when \(|A|<2\).  Moreover, for every \(u_i\in F\),
\[
 \Pr_{u_i}\{u_i\in A_F\}
 =\Pr_{u_i}\!\left\{
   \Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)\in C(Z,t)
   \text{ for every }t\in\mathcal D\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the reduction from a band to its covered subset preserves every grid-point coverage probability exactly.

Conversely, let \(A=A(Z)\subseteq F\) be any measurable subset-valued rule, with any external randomization again included in \(Z\).  When \(A\ne\varnothing\), define its envelope by
\[
 C_A(t)=
 \left[
   \min_{u_i\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t),
   \max_{u_i\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)
 \right].
\tag{5}
\]
When \(A=\varnothing\), take any fixed singleton interval.  The endpoints in (5) are measurable because they are extrema over a finite random subset of continuous functions.  On \(\{u_i\in A\}\), the entire curve indexed by \(u_i\) belongs to the envelope, so
\[
 \Pr_{u_i}\!\left\{
   \Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)\in C_A(t)
   \text{ for every }t\in\mathcal D\right\}
 \geq \Pr_{u_i}\{u_i\in A\}.
\tag{6}
\]
For every nonempty \(A\), finiteness of \(A\) permits interchange of the finite maximum and the supremum:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\operatorname{diam}C_A(t)
 &=\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\max_{u_i,u_j\in A}
   \left|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_j,t)\right|\\
 &=\max_{u_i,u_j\in A}\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}
   \left|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_j,t)\right|\\
 &=c(A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The same equality holds for \(A=\varnothing\) by the singleton and zero-cost conventions.  Consequently the subset rule produces an interval-section band having exactly the geometry-specific diameter loss, without restricting the rule to a deterministic shape.  Accidental coverage of an unselected curve can only increase coverage and does not affect (7).

We next establish the finite-net transport.  By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\), \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) is continuous and affine on every member of a finite polyhedral complex on the compact set \(\mathcal U\times\mathcal D\).  Let \(K_u<\infty\) be the maximum Euclidean norm of the coefficient of \(u\) over these finitely many affine formulas.  Partitioning the segment between \(u\) and \(u'\) at its cell crossings and summing the affine increments proves
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal D}
 \left|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u',t)\right|
 \leq K_u\lVert u-u'\rVert_2
 \qquad (u,u'\in\mathcal U).
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(F\) be a \(\delta\)-net of \(\mathcal U\), and choose a nearest point \(u_{i(u)}\in F\) using a fixed tie-breaking order.  Pad (5) to
\[
 C_A^\delta(t)=
 \left[
   \min_{u_i\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)-K_u\delta,
   \max_{u_i\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)+K_u\delta
 \right]
\tag{9}
\]
for \(A\ne\varnothing\).  If \(u_{i(u)}\in A\), inequality (8) implies
\[
 \Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)\in C_A^\delta(t)
 \quad\text{for every }t\in\mathcal D,
\tag{10}
\]
and (7) gives
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\operatorname{diam}C_A^\delta(t)
 =c(A)+2K_u\delta.
\tag{11}
\]
Writing \(P_u=N(u,I(\theta_0)^{-1})\), the elementary total-variation inequality for the measurable event \(\{u_{i(u)}\in A\}\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_u\!\left\{
   \Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)\in C_A^\delta(t)
   \text{ for every }t\in\mathcal D\right\}
 &\geq P_u\{u_{i(u)}\in A\}\\
 &\geq P_{u_{i(u)}}\{u_{i(u)}\in A\}
   -\operatorname{TV}(P_u,P_{u_{i(u)}}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus padding by the uniform \(u\)-modulus transports grid coverage to all of \(\mathcal U\), with the displayed and computable probability error and an added maximum width of at most \(2K_u\delta\).

It remains to compute each pairwise curve distance.  Since \(v_0\in\Delta_K\) and \(\rho>0\),
\[
 H_0=2\{X_0^{\prime}\operatorname{diag}(v_0)X_0+\rho I_J\}
 \succeq 2\rho I_J\succ0.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore the critical-cone quadratic program has a unique minimizer.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\) supplies finitely many closed polyhedral cells \(\Gamma_a\) on each of which \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) has one affine formula; uniqueness and continuity make formulas agree on common boundaries.

Fix \(u,u'\in\mathcal U\).  For every ordered pair of cells define
\[
 \mathcal T_{ab}(u,u')
 =\{t\in\mathcal D:(u,t)\in\Gamma_a,\ (u',t)\in\Gamma_b\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The cell restrictions in (14) are finitely many linear equalities and inequalities in \(t\), while
\[
 \mathcal D=\left\{t:\mathbf 1^{\prime}t=0,\quad
 t_k\geq0\ \text{if }(v_0)_k=0,\quad
 \lVert t\rVert_2\leq L_0\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Hence every nonempty \(\mathcal T_{ab}(u,u')\) is compact and second-order-cone representable.  On this set the difference of the two affine cell formulas has the form
\[
 \Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u',t)
 =\beta_{ab}(u,u')+\gamma_{ab}(u,u')^{\prime}t.
\tag{16}
\]
Its absolute maximum is the larger of the two finite optimal values
\[
 \max_{t}\ \sigma\{\beta_{ab}(u,u')+\gamma_{ab}(u,u')^{\prime}t\}
 \quad\text{subject to }t\in\mathcal T_{ab}(u,u'),
 \qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Each problem in (17) is a second-order-cone program: its objective is linear, its cell, tangent, and sign constraints are linear, and its sole nonlinear constraint is the Euclidean-norm cone in (15).  Compactness gives attainment.  The finitely many sets in (14) cover \(\mathcal D\), including every cell boundary, and therefore
\[
 d_\Psi(u,u')
 =\max_{a,b:\,\mathcal T_{ab}(u,u')\ne\varnothing}
   \max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}
   \max_{t\in\mathcal T_{ab}(u,u')}
   \sigma\{\beta_{ab}(u,u')+\gamma_{ab}(u,u')^{\prime}t\}.
\tag{18}
\]
This is the claimed finite conic computation and completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction replaces an infinite-dimensional band action by a confidence-set decision under a computable geometry-specific diameter loss. \textit{Gap.} FreybergerRai2018UniformBands, Definition 1, restricts bands to those induced by a nonrandom-shape set and rules out randomly selecting among valid bands. Its Section 2.3 leads with weighted area; Theorem 2 is conditional on a generic objective, while the paper supplies low-level sufficient conditions and a worked approximation specifically for weighted area. This proposition supplies the finite-subset and conic-distance reduction, and the certified Gaussian-value theorem subsequently solves the unrestricted externally randomized maximum-curve-diameter problem over all measurable interval-section bands. EvansHansenStark2005MinimaxConfidence instead uses additive set measure rather than curve diameter. \textit{Consumer.} A numerical solver can compute exact pairwise curve distances with primal and dual conic certificates.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certified-gaussian-value]\label{thm:certified-gaussian-value}
Let \(q_{\mathrm{cov}}=1-\alpha\), \(D_\star=\sup_{u,u'\in\mathcal U}d_\Psi(u,u')\), and let \(K_u\) satisfy \(\sup_t|\Psi(u,t)-\Psi(u',t)|\leq K_u\|u-u'\|_2\). For a \(\delta\)-net, put \(\epsilon_\delta=\sup_{\|u-u_i\|\leq\delta}\operatorname{TV}\{N(u,I^{-1}),N(u_i,I^{-1})\}\). For the observation partition and a parameter-independent within-cell reconstruction kernel, let \(\epsilon_h\) be its uniform deficiency, including the tail cell. Whenever \(\epsilon_h\leq q_{\mathrm{cov}}\) and \(\epsilon_\delta\leq\alpha\), certified Gaussian probabilities and conic distances give
\[
V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h)-D_\star\epsilon_h
\leq L_\alpha(\theta_0,v_0;M,L_0)
\leq V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon_\delta)+D_\star\epsilon_\delta+2K_u\delta.
\]
The primal and dual programs have the same finite value and respectively certify feasible upper and lower bounds. One may take \(\epsilon_\delta\leq\|I^{1/2}\|\delta/\sqrt{2\pi}\) and an explicit \(\epsilon_h\to0\) whenever \(R\to\infty\), \(h\to0\), and hence under \(hR\to0\). If \(\delta\to0\), \(R\to\infty\), \(hR\to0\), and numerical enclosure errors vanish, the certified gap converges to zero. The padded envelope rules are asymptotically minimax among all externally randomized measurable interval-section bands.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(I=I(\theta_0)\), \(q_{\mathrm{cov}}=1-\alpha\), \(P_u=N(u,I^{-1})\), and
\[
\mathcal L(C)=\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\operatorname{diam}C(Z,t).
\]
The covariance spectrum assumption and the positive definiteness in the definition of \(I(\theta_0)\) make every inverse and whitening operation below legitimate.  By \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\), \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) is continuous and affine on each member of a finite polyhedral complex on the compact set \(\mathcal U\times\mathcal D\).  Let \(K_u\) be the largest operator norm, over those affine pieces, of the coefficient multiplying \(u\).  Partitioning any line segment from \(u\) to \(u'\) at its finitely many cell crossings and summing its affine increments gives
\[
\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}
\left|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u',t)\right|
\le K_u\lVert u-u'\rVert_2.
\]
Thus
\[
D_\star=\sup_{u,u'\in\mathcal U}d_\Psi(u,u')
\le 2K_uM<\infty.
\]

We first make both approximation errors explicit.  Whitening two Gaussian laws with the same covariance and integrating in the single likelihood-ratio direction gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_u,P_{u_i})
=2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\lVert I^{1/2}(u-u_i)\rVert_2}{2}\right)-1.
\]
The derivative of the right-hand side, as a function of the displayed norm, is at most \(1/\sqrt{2\pi}\).  Hence, if \(\lVert u-u_i\rVert_2\le\delta\),
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_u,P_{u_i})
\le \frac{\lVert I^{1/2}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
=:\epsilon_\delta.
\]

For observation quantization, write \(P_0=N(0,I^{-1})\).  On each bounded cell \(o\), reconstruct from the parameter-independent conditional distribution \(P_0(\,\cdot\mid o)\), and use any fixed probability distribution on the tail cell.  The likelihood ratio is
\[
\ell_u(z)=\frac{dP_u}{dP_0}(z)
=\exp\!\left\{u^{\prime}Iz-\frac12u^{\prime}Iu\right\}.
\]
If \(o\) has diameter at most \(h\), then
\[
\sup_{z,z'\in o}
\left|\log\ell_u(z)-\log\ell_u(z')\right|
\le \lVert I\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}Mh.
\]
Conditionally on \(o\), the density ratio between \(P_u(\,\cdot\mid o)\) and the reconstruction law is \(\ell_u(z)/E_0[\ell_u(Z)\mid Z\in o]\).  The preceding oscillation bound therefore makes their total variation distance no larger than
\(\exp\{\lVert I\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}Mh\}-1\).  If \(R>M\), a coordinatewise Gaussian tail bound gives
\[
\tau_R:=\sup_{\lVert u\rVert_2\le M}
P_u\{Z\notin[-R,R]^q\}
\le 2q\exp\!\left\{
-\frac{(R-M)^2}{2\lambda_{\max}(I^{-1})}
\right\}.
\]
Consequently, if \(\widetilde P_u\) is the law obtained by recording the cell and applying the reconstruction kernel,
\[
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\operatorname{TV}(P_u,\widetilde P_u)
\le
\epsilon_h:=
\min\!\left\{1,\,
\exp\{\lVert I\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}Mh\}-1+\tau_R
\right\}.
\]
This bound tends to zero when \(R\to\infty\) and \(h\to0\); in particular it does so under \(R\to\infty\) and \(hR\to0\).

We next prove the lower certificate.  Let \(C\in\mathfrak C_{1-\alpha}\) be any externally randomized measurable interval-section band.  Apply \(\mathrm{prop:curve\mbox{-}decision\mbox{-}reduction}\) to \(F=\mathcal U_N\), and let \(A\) be the random subset of net points whose complete curves are covered.  Pointwise,
\[
c_A\le\mathcal L(C),
\qquad
P_{u_i}\{i\in A\}\ge q_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\]
Quantize, reconstruct, and then run this acceptance rule, including its original external randomization.  Conditional on the observed cell \(o\), let \(x_{oA}\) be the probability of selecting \(A\).  Then \(x_{oA}\ge0\) and \(\sum_Ax_{oA}=1\).  Total variation changes the probability of any event by at most \(\epsilon_h\), while \(0\le c_A\le D_\star\); hence, for every \(i\),
\[
\sum_op_{io}\sum_{A\ni i}x_{oA}
\ge q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h
\]
and
\[
\sum_op_{io}\sum_Ac_Ax_{oA}
\le E_{u_i}[\mathcal L(C)]+D_\star\epsilon_h.
\]
Thus \(x\) is feasible for the finite program at coverage \(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h\), with objective at most
\(\sup_uE_u[\mathcal L(C)]+D_\star\epsilon_h\).  Taking the infimum over \(C\) proves
\[
V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h)-D_\star\epsilon_h
\le L_\alpha(\theta_0,v_0;M,L_0).
\]

For the upper certificate, take a finite-program rule feasible at coverage \(q_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon_\delta\), with objective no larger than
\(V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon_\delta)+\eta\), where \(\eta>0\).  If the rule selects a nonempty set \(A\), output
\[
C_A^\delta(t)=
\left[
\min_{i\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)-K_u\delta,\,
\max_{i\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_i,t)+K_u\delta
\right];
\]
if \(A=\varnothing\), output a fixed singleton.  Given \(u\in\mathcal U\), choose a nearest \(u_i\), breaking ties by a fixed ordering, so that \(\lVert u-u_i\rVert_2\le\delta\).  On \(\{i\in A\}\), the Lipschitz inequality proves simultaneous coverage of the entire curve indexed by \(u\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_u\{\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)\in C_A^\delta(t)
\text{ for all }t\in\mathcal D\}
&\ge P_{u_i}\{i\in A\}
-\operatorname{TV}(P_u,P_{u_i})\\
&\ge q_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The constructed band is measurable because the partition and parameter net are finite, and it belongs to \(\mathfrak C_{1-\alpha}\).  Its loss is at most \(c_A+2K_u\delta\).  A second total-variation comparison yields
\[
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}E_u[\mathcal L(C_A^\delta)]
\le
V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon_\delta)
+\eta+D_\star\epsilon_\delta+2K_u\delta.
\]
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the stated upper certificate.

We now verify finite-program duality.  Attach nonnegative multipliers \(\ell_i\) to the coverage constraints, nonnegative multipliers \(\pi_i\) to the risk constraints, and unrestricted multipliers \(\eta_o\) to the row-normalization constraints.  The Lagrangian is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\zeta\left(1-\sum_i\pi_i\right)
+q\sum_i\ell_i+\sum_o\eta_o\\
&\quad+
\sum_{o,A}x_{oA}
\left(
c_A\sum_i\pi_ip_{io}
-\sum_{i\in A}\ell_ip_{io}
-\eta_o
\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(q\) denotes the coverage argument of the finite program.  Its infimum over unrestricted \(\zeta\) and \(x_{oA}\ge0\) is finite exactly if
\[
\sum_i\pi_i=1,
\qquad
\eta_o\le
c_A\sum_i\pi_ip_{io}
-\sum_{i\in A}\ell_ip_{io}
\quad\text{for every }(o,A),
\]
which are precisely the displayed dual constraints.  This gives weak duality.

For the reverse inequality, fix \(\gamma<V_{N,h}(q)\).  Let \(\mathcal X\) be the product of the finitely many subset-probability simplices and define
\[
a_i(x)=\sum_op_{io}\sum_{A\ni i}x_{oA},
\qquad
b_i(x)=\sum_op_{io}\sum_Ac_Ax_{oA}.
\]
The compact convex set
\[
\mathcal K=
\left\{
\bigl((q-a_i(x))_i,(b_i(x)-\gamma)_i\bigr):
x\in\mathcal X
\right\}
\]
is disjoint from the nonpositive orthant.  Let a closest pair realize the strictly positive distance between these two closed convex sets.  Expanding the squared-distance inequality after moving the point of \(\mathcal K\) toward any other point of \(\mathcal K\) gives a separating normal with nonnegative coordinates.  Hence there are \(\ell_i,\pi_i\ge0\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_i\ell_i\{q-a_i(x)\}
+\sum_i\pi_i\{b_i(x)-\gamma\}>0
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal X.
\]
The action that always selects the full net has \(a_i=1\ge q\) for every \(i\); substituting it shows \(\sum_i\pi_i>0\).  Divide all multipliers by this positive sum, so \(\sum_i\pi_i=1\), and put
\[
\eta_o=
\min_A\left\{
c_A\sum_i\pi_ip_{io}
-\sum_{i\in A}\ell_ip_{io}
\right\}.
\]
Minimizing separately over every simplex row in the strict separating inequality shows
\[
q\sum_i\ell_i+\sum_o\eta_o>\gamma.
\]
Thus the displayed \((\pi,\ell,\eta)\) is dual feasible with value exceeding every \(\gamma<V_{N,h}(q)\).  Weak duality now proves equality of the finite primal and dual values.  Primal and dual feasible points therefore provide checkable certificates.

It remains to prove convergence of the two certificates.  The hypotheses
\(\epsilon_h\le q_{\mathrm{cov}}\) and \(\epsilon_\delta\le\alpha\) ensure that both program coverage levels lie in \([0,1]\).  Mix an optimal rule at coverage \(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h\) with the rule that always selects the full net.  The mixing probability
\[
\beta=
\frac{\epsilon_\delta+\epsilon_h}{\alpha+\epsilon_h}
\]
belongs to \([0,1]\), and the mixture has coverage at least
\[
(1-\beta)(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h)+\beta
=q_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon_\delta.
\]
Because every subset cost is at most \(D_\star\),
\[
V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon_\delta)
-V_{N,h}(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\epsilon_h)
\le
D_\star\frac{\epsilon_\delta+\epsilon_h}{\alpha+\epsilon_h}.
\]
Combining this with the lower and upper certificates bounds their gap by
\[
D_\star\left\{
\frac{\epsilon_\delta+\epsilon_h}{\alpha+\epsilon_h}
+\epsilon_\delta+\epsilon_h
\right\}
+2K_u\delta,
\]
which tends to zero when \(\delta\to0\), \(R\to\infty\), and \(hR\to0\).

All inputs to these finite programs have certified finite computations.  By
\(\mathrm{prop:curve\mbox{-}decision\mbox{-}reduction}\), each \(c_A\) is obtained from finitely many second-order-cone programs; primal and conic-dual feasible points give outward numerical enclosures.  On \([-R,R]^q\), the Gaussian density and its derivatives are explicit and uniformly bounded for \(u_i\in\mathcal U\).  Interval quadrature on refined cells therefore encloses every \(p_{io}\), and \(\tau_R\) encloses the tail contribution.  Replacing costs and probabilities by outward endpoints and padding each coverage row by its rowwise probability error gives finite robust linear programs.  A rowwise probability error \(e_p\) changes a coverage row by at most \(e_p\) and a risk row by at most \(D_\star e_p\); a uniform cost error \(e_c\) changes a risk row by at most \(e_c\).  Hence the robust lower and upper numerical values converge to the exact finite-program value as \(e_p,e_c\to0\).

Finally, choose meshes and numerical enclosures satisfying the stated limits, and use each upper-program optimizer in the padded-envelope construction.  The vanishing analytic and numerical certificate gap proves that its worst expected maximum width converges to
\(L_\alpha(\theta_0,v_0;M,L_0)\).  Therefore these rules are asymptotically minimax among all externally randomized measurable interval-section bands.  If \(D_\star=0\), all curves coincide and the singleton band containing the common curve has coverage one and width zero, which proves the same conclusion directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:face-cost-comparison]\label{prop:face-cost-comparison}
Under the single criterion \(L_\alpha\), if the critical cone \(\mathcal C_0\) is a linear space, then \(\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)=a_{\star}^{\prime}u+b_{\star}^{\prime}t\) and the risk-graph decision reduces exactly to scalar Gaussian confidence-interval inference for \(a_{\star}^{\prime}u\). At a boundary face the same certified program applies, and the difference from a separated face may be positive, zero, or negative after the information and derivatives change; no compulsory positive kink penalty is asserted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(\mathcal C_0\) is a linear space, let \(\Pi_{\mathcal C_0}^{H_0}\) be orthogonal projection in the \(H_0\) inner product.  The equality-constrained first-order condition gives
\[
 h(u,t)=-\Pi_{\mathcal C_0}^{H_0}H_0^{-1}Q(u,t).
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(Q\) is linear, \(h(u,t)=H_uu+H_tt\) for fixed matrices \(H_u,H_t\).  Since \(a(u)\) is linear, vectors \(a_\star,b_\star\) exist with
\[
 \Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)=a_\star^\prime u+b_\star^\prime t.
\tag{18}
\]
Subtracting \(b_\star^\prime t\) from both endpoints of each band section preserves widths and converts simultaneous coverage into coverage of \(a_\star^\prime u\).  Adding it back gives the inverse map.  Thus the decision is exactly scalar Gaussian confidence-interval inference for \(a_\star^\prime u\), including value zero if \(a_\star=0\).

At a boundary, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\) gives a finite continuous piecewise-affine map, and Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:certified\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}value}\) applies without strict complementarity.  Two invariances follow directly from Definition \(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}decision\mbox{-}value}\):
\[
 L_\alpha(\Psi+k)=L_\alpha(\Psi)
 \quad\text{for known }k(t),\qquad
 L_\alpha(c\Psi)=|c|L_\alpha(\Psi)\quad(c\neq0).
\tag{19}
\]
Translate or scale every endpoint for one inequality and apply the inverse transformation for the other.

There are nonconstant examples of both face types.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:hinge\mbox{-}sanity\mbox{-}check}\) supplies a moving-boundary map locally injective in a scalar direction at an unsaturated query.  At an interior donor face, perturbing \(\Sigma_{00}\) while holding means fixed leaves \(h\) unchanged and gives a nonzero affine direct derivative in (18).  Their decision values are finite by the full envelope over the compact parameter set.  They are positive at every confidence level \(1-\alpha>0\): choose \(N>(1-\alpha)^{-1}\) sufficiently close parameters whose curves have minimum pairwise distance \(\Delta>0\), and whose Gaussian laws have total variation at most \(\varepsilon<(1-\alpha)-N^{-1}\) from a common center.  If \(A\) is the set of covered curves, honesty gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0|A|\geq N(1-\alpha-\varepsilon).
\]
Since \(|A|\leq1+N\mathbf1\{\sup_t\operatorname{diam}C(Z,t)\geq\Delta\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_0[\sup_t\operatorname{diam}C(Z,t)]
 \geq\Delta\{1-\alpha-\varepsilon-N^{-1}\}>0.
\tag{20}
\]

Finally multiply all outcome and mean coordinates of either experiment by \(\sqrt c\), its covariance and ridge constant by \(c>0\), and transport the chart by this bijection.  Weights and active faces are unchanged, risks and local curves are multiplied by \(c\), and the Gaussian experiments, hence information in transported coordinates, are equivalent.  By (19), the cost is multiplied by \(c\).  Starting from the two positive costs in (20), a choice of \(c\) makes boundary and separated costs equal; increasing or decreasing it realizes either ordering.  Thus their difference may be positive, zero, or negative, and there is no compulsory boundary surcharge.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:panel-experiment-transfer]\label{thm:panel-experiment-transfer}

\begin{proof}

=D\mu_g[u]^{\prime}\Sigma_g^{-1}(Y-\mu_g)
+\frac12\left\{

\Sigma_g^{-1}(Y-\mu_g)
-\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_g^{-1}D\Sigma_g[u])
\right\}.
\]

\[

and
\[

\{X^{\prime}BX-\operatorname{tr}(B\Sigma_g)\}

\right]

\qquad X\sim N(0,\Sigma_g),
\]
therefore give, group by group,
\[

\]
with exactly the two mean terms and two covariance terms in the definition of \(I(\theta_0)\).  Its assumed positive definiteness makes the limiting score nondegenerate.

\[
\log\frac{dP_{\theta_n(u)}}{dP_{\theta_0}}
=u^{\prime}\Delta_n-\frac12u^{\prime}I(\theta_0)u+r_n(u),
\qquad
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|r_n(u)|
\longrightarrow_{P_{\theta_0}}0.
\]
To justify the uniform remainder, the third derivatives of the normal log density are polynomials of fixed degree in a standardized Gaussian vector, with coefficients uniformly bounded on the spectral neighborhood.  Their sample averages are tight, while
\(\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\lVert u/\sqrt n\rVert_2^3=O(n^{-3/2})\); summing \(O(n)\) observations gives \(o_{P_{\theta_0}}(1)\).  The same polynomial-Gaussian bound supplies a common exponential moment in a neighborhood of the origin.

For completeness, this exponential moment also upgrades the score central limit to total variation.  After standardization, each score summand is a polynomial of degree at most two in a fixed-dimensional standard Gaussian vector.  Its characteristic function is analytic near the origin.  Taylor expansion there, multiplication over the \(O(n)\) independent summands, and the preceding covariance limit give the characteristic function of \(N(0,I(\theta_0))\) uniformly on every fixed frequency ball.  Outside a fixed ball, positive group proportions and nondegeneracy of \(I(\theta_0)\) provide an integrable geometric majorant after a fixed number of convolutions; the explicit Gaussian-quadratic characteristic functions provide that majorant.  Fourier inversion and then enlargement of the ball imply
\[
\left\|
\mathcal L_{\theta_0}(\Delta_n)-N(0,I(\theta_0))
\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The common exponential moment makes both the Taylor remainders and their likelihood-ratio products uniformly integrable.  Consequently the likelihood-ratio process converges in \(L^1(P_{\theta_0})\), uniformly on \(\mathcal U\), to
\[
\Lambda_u(\Delta)
=\exp\!\left\{
u^{\prime}\Delta-\frac12u^{\prime}I(\theta_0)u
\right\},
\qquad
\Delta\sim N(0,I(\theta_0)).
\]

We now construct the kernels.  Define an approximate law on the exact panel sample space by
\[
\frac{d\widetilde P_{n,u}}{dP_{\theta_0}}
=c_n(u)^{-1}
\exp\!\left\{
u^{\prime}\Delta_n-\frac12u^{\prime}I(\theta_0)u
\right\},
\]
where
\[
c_n(u)=E_{\theta_0}
\exp\!\left\{
u^{\prime}\Delta_n-\frac12u^{\prime}I(\theta_0)u
\right\}.
\]
Uniform \(L^1\) convergence gives
\[
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|c_n(u)-1|\longrightarrow0
\]
and
\[
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
\lVert\widetilde P_{n,u}-P_{\theta_n(u)}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
In the approximate experiment, \(\Delta_n\) is sufficient because every likelihood ratio is a measurable function of \(\Delta_n\).  The forward kernel reports \(\Delta_n\).  The reverse kernel draws from the center conditional law of the exact Gaussian sufficient statistic given \(\Delta_n\), and then regenerates the parameter-free ancillary residual rotations.  Such regular conditional laws exist because the sample and statistic spaces are Euclidean Borel spaces.

The center law of \(\Delta_n\) converges in total variation to \(N(0,I)\).  Multiplication by the uniformly integrable exponential tilts preserves this convergence uniformly for \(u\in\mathcal U\), so its law under \(\widetilde P_{n,u}\) converges uniformly to \(N(Iu,I)\).  A maximal common-part coupling between the center score law and \(N(0,I)\), followed by the same exponential tilt, supplies parameter-independent Markov kernels in both directions whose errors converge uniformly to zero.  Applying the linear map \(Z=I^{-1}\Delta\) yields \(Z\sim N(u,I^{-1})\).  The triangle inequality with the preceding approximation of \(P_{\theta_n(u)}\) proves that both deficiencies vanish uniformly on \(\mathcal U\).  Normal group likelihoods depend only on block means and residual scatter matrices, so these kernels can indeed be implemented from the sufficient statistics named in \(\mathrm{def:panel\mbox{-}transfer\mbox{-}handle}\); conditional simulation regenerates the discarded center-ancillary rotations.

It remains to transfer the band decision, including its target and unbounded-width issues.  By \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\),
\[
\beta_n:=
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U,\ t\in\mathcal D}
\left|
\sqrt n\{R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))
-R_{P_{\theta_0}}(v_0)\}
-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)
\right|
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Define
\[
\Psi_n(u,t)=
\sqrt n\{R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))
-R_{P_{\theta_0}}(v_0)\}
\]
and
\[
I_n(t)=
\left[
\inf_{u\in\mathcal U}\Psi_n(u,t),\,
\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\Psi_n(u,t)
\right].
\]
Compactness and uniform convergence to the continuous function \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) imply a deterministic bound \(D<\infty\) on
\(\sup_t\operatorname{diam}I_n(t)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Intersect every candidate section with \(I_n(t)\), returning a fixed point of \(I_n(t)\) if the intersection is empty.  Whenever the candidate covers a curve \(\Psi_n(u,\cdot)\), the intersection is nonempty and still covers it; width does not increase.  Thus the loss is bounded by \(D\).

If a transfer kernel has total-variation error \(\kappa_n\), it changes any simultaneous coverage probability by at most \(\kappa_n\) and any expected clipped loss by at most \(D\kappa_n\).  Padding both endpoints by \(\beta_n\) transfers between \(\Psi_n\) and \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\), adding at most \(2\beta_n\) to maximum width.  Finally, if the transferred rule has coverage \(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\gamma_n\), mix it with the full deterministic envelope with probability
\[
\frac{\gamma_n}{\alpha+\gamma_n}.
\]
The resulting coverage is at least
\[
\left(1-\frac{\gamma_n}{\alpha+\gamma_n}\right)
(q_{\mathrm{cov}}-\gamma_n)
+\frac{\gamma_n}{\alpha+\gamma_n}
=q_{\mathrm{cov}},
\]
and the extra expected width is at most
\[
D\frac{\gamma_n}{\alpha+\gamma_n}.
\]
All three errors vanish.  Applying the construction in both kernel directions proves preservation of simultaneous coverage and expected maximum width and hence equality of the compact-local Gaussian-shift and observed-panel minimax bounded curve-diameter values.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:global-honesty-local-converse]\label{prop:global-honesty-local-converse}
Let \(\{\widetilde C_n\}\) be randomized measurable observed-panel interval bands satisfying
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_n}
P\!\left\{
R_P(v)\in\widetilde C_n(v)
\text{ for every }v\in\Delta_K
\right\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For every compact Gaussian local path
\(\{\theta_n(u):u\in\mathcal U\}\subseteq\Theta_n^{\mathrm G}\), this sequence belongs, after restriction to \(v_n(\mathcal D)\), to
\(\mathfrak H^{\mathrm{panel}}_{1-\alpha}(\theta_0,v_0;M,L_0)\), and therefore
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
E_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}\!\left[
\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\sqrt n\,
\operatorname{diam}\widetilde C_n(v_n(t))
\right]
\ge L_\alpha(\theta_0,v_0;M,L_0).
\]
This implication is one-sided: attainment within the Gaussian-local honesty class does not establish attainment by a sequence uniformly honest over \(\mathcal M_n\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(u\in\mathcal U\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}parameter\mbox{-}space}\),
\(\theta_n(u)\in\Theta_n^{\mathrm G}\subseteq\Theta_n\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem:global\mbox{-}parameterization}\), the induced observed law
\(P_{\theta_n(u)}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_n\).  Also
\(v_n(t)\in\Delta_K\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over the compact tangent set \(\mathcal D\), by the defining tangent sign restrictions at zero coordinates of \(v_0\).  Hence the global simultaneous-coverage event
\[
\left\{
R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v)
\in\widetilde C_n(v)
\text{ for every }v\in\Delta_K
\right\}
\]
is contained in the local event
\[
\left\{
R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))
\in\widetilde C_n(v_n(t))
\text{ for every }t\in\mathcal D
\right\}.
\]
Taking probabilities, then the infimum over \(u\), and finally the liminf proves membership in
\(\mathfrak H^{\mathrm{panel}}_{1-\alpha}(\theta_0,v_0;M,L_0)\).  The lower-bound half of
\(\mathrm{thm:local\mbox{-}panel\mbox{-}minimax}\) gives the displayed expected-width inequality.

The set of sequences uniformly honest over \(\mathcal M_n\) is therefore a subset of the locally honest Gaussian-path sequences.  Minimizing over the larger local class can only weakly decrease the attainable value.  Thus the lower bound transfers from the Gaussian subclass to every broader honesty requirement containing those paths, whereas an attaining member of the larger local class need not belong to the smaller globally honest class.  This proves the stated one-sidedness.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The KKT atlas, critical-cone risk map, certified LP/SOCP approximation, bidirectional Gaussian-panel deficiency transfer, and compact-local minimax theorem are established in the core. The remaining limitation is one of scope rather than an open proof step: the projection band over the stationary mixing class is a validity construction only, whereas exact decision optimality and attainment are proved on compact iid-Gaussian local experiments. Uniform honesty over the larger class implies the same local lower bound, but the locally attaining Gaussian procedures have not been shown uniformly valid or attaining over that larger class. No compulsory positive cost from a face change and no strict separation from existing generic band theory is asserted.

\section{Honest scope}

The global mixing-class band and the locally optimal Gaussian-neighborhood bands are different procedures. The global projection band \(C_n\) is proved uniformly valid but is not claimed to be globally minimax. Exact optimality and attainment concern fixed-dimensional, compact iid-Gaussian local neighborhoods with known spectrally separated rank, fixed positive ridge, stable block means, and a stable donor post regime. The converse is broader: any band sequence uniformly honest over the full panel class is honest on the contained Gaussian paths and must have compact-local scaled expected maximum width at least \(L_\alpha\). The reverse implication is not proved, so Gaussian-local attainment gives no global-class attainment claim. Unknown rank, growing dimensions, singleton-date predictors, post-factor drift, shrinking ridge, direct guarantees for the different objectives in Synth or SparseSC, and optimal inference for a corrected causal treatment effect remain outside scope.
