%%% FIELD proof-local-risk-map
Put \(\epsilon=n^{-1/2}\), write \(w_{n,u,t}=w_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))\), and set
\[
 H_{n,u,t}=H_{\theta_n(u)}(v_n(t)),\qquad
 f_{n,u,t}=f_{\theta_n(u)}(v_n(t)).
\]
The local-chart condition, the compactness of \(\mathcal U\times\mathcal D\), and twice continuous differentiability give, uniformly in \((u,t)\),
\[
 H_{n,u,t}=H_0+\epsilon\dot H(u,t)+O(\epsilon^2),
 \qquad
 f_{n,u,t}=f_0+\epsilon\dot f(u,t)+O(\epsilon^2).
\tag{1}
\]
The predictor path is feasible eventually: if \((v_0)_k=0\), the definition of \(\mathcal D\) gives \(t_k\geq0\); if \((v_0)_k>0\), compactness gives \((v_0)_k+\epsilon t_k>0\) uniformly eventually.  Since \(\rho>0\),
\[
 H_{n,u,t}\succeq 2\rho I_J,\qquad H_0\succeq2\rho I_J.
\tag{2}
\]
Ignoring a constant independent of \(w\), the criterion in Definition \(\mathrm{def:weight\mbox{-}map}\) is
\[
 q_{n,u,t}(w)=\frac12w^\prime H_{n,u,t}w-f_{n,u,t}^\prime w.
\]
Apply the variational inequalities for \(w_{n,u,t}\) and \(w_0\), and put \(r_{n,u,t}=w_{n,u,t}-w_0\).  Adding them and using (2) yields
\[
 2\rho\lVert r_{n,u,t}\rVert_2^2
 \leq
 \left\lVert (H_{n,u,t}-H_0)w_0-(f_{n,u,t}-f_0)\right\rVert_2
 \lVert r_{n,u,t}\rVert_2.
\]
Thus (1) implies
\[
 \sup_{u,t}\lVert w_{n,u,t}-w_0\rVert_2=O(\epsilon).
\tag{3}
\]
Choose \(j_\star\) with \(w_{0j_\star}>0\), which exists because \(\mathbf1^\prime w_0=1\).  By (3), \(w_{n,u,t,j_\star}>0\) uniformly eventually.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:kkt\mbox{-}stratification}\) then defines the multiplier continuously by
\[
 \eta_{n,u,t}=-(H_{n,u,t}w_{n,u,t}-f_{n,u,t})_{j_\star}.
\]
Hence \(\eta_{n,u,t}\to\eta_0\) uniformly, and
\[
 s_{n,u,t}=H_{n,u,t}w_{n,u,t}-f_{n,u,t}+\eta_{n,u,t}\mathbf1
\]
converges uniformly to \(s_0\).  If \(w_{0j}=0<s_{0j}\), the perturbed slack is positive eventually, so complementary slackness forces \(w_{n,u,t,j}=0\).  If \(w_{0j}=s_{0j}=0\), feasibility gives \(w_{n,u,t,j}\geq0\).  Consequently
\[
 k_{n,u,t}:=\epsilon^{-1}(w_{n,u,t}-w_0)\in\mathcal C_0.
\tag{4}
\]
For bounded \(k\) satisfying (4), expansion around \(w_0\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \epsilon^{-2}\{q_{n,u,t}(w_0+\epsilon k)-q_{n,u,t}(w_0)\}
 &=
 \epsilon^{-1}(H_{n,u,t}w_0-f_{n,u,t})^\prime k
 +\frac12k^\prime H_{n,u,t}k\\
 &=
 \frac12k^\prime H_0k+Q(u,t)^\prime k
 +O(\epsilon)(1+\lVert k\rVert_2^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the center KKT identity is \(H_0w_0-f_0=-\eta_0\mathbf1+s_0\).  Equality gives \(\mathbf1^\prime k=0\), and \(s_0^\prime k=0\) because positive-slack coordinates are fixed at zero and all remaining coordinates have zero slack.

Comparison of (5) at \(k_{n,u,t}\) and zero, (2), and boundedness of \(Q\) first show that \(k_{n,u,t}\) is uniformly bounded.  The limiting minimizer \(h(u,t)\) is uniformly bounded as well.  Therefore the lower bounds \(k_j\geq-w_{0j}/\epsilon\) on positive coordinates do not bind at either minimizer eventually.  The two problems may be compared on the fixed cone \(\mathcal C_0\).  Their gradients differ by \(O(\epsilon)\) on this bounded set.  Adding their variational inequalities and using \(2\rho\)-strong monotonicity gives
\[
 \sup_{u,t}\lVert k_{n,u,t}-h(u,t)\rVert_2=O(\epsilon).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
 w_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))
 =w_0+n^{-1/2}h(u,t)+O(n^{-1})
\tag{7}
\]
uniformly.  This permits any number of indices with \(w_{0j}=s_{0j}=0\); strict complementarity was not used.

For the risk expansion, fixed rank and the spectral gap make the pseudoinverse smooth along the rank-\(r\) chart.  Put \(\gamma_0=A_0^+a_{0,0}\).  Since \(a_0(\theta)\in\operatorname{col}(A(\theta))\), differentiation of \(A(\theta)\gamma(\theta)=a_0(\theta)\), for \(\gamma(\theta)=A(\theta)^+a_0(\theta)\), gives
\[
 A_0\dot\gamma(u)=\dot a_0(u)-\dot A(u)\gamma_0.
\]
Since \(d_0\in\operatorname{row}(A_0)\),
\[
 \dot c(u)
 =\dot d(u)^\prime\gamma_0
 +d_0^\prime A_0^+
   \{\dot a_0(u)-\dot A(u)\gamma_0\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Write \(\Sigma\) in target--donor blocks.  Equations (7)--(8) and a uniform second-order Taylor expansion of Definition \(\mathrm{def:risk\mbox{-}graph}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sqrt n\{R_{P_{\theta_n(u)}}(v_n(t))-R_{P_{\theta_0}}(v_0)\}\\
 &=
 2b_0\{\dot c(u)-\dot d(u)^\prime w_0\}
 +e_0^\prime\dot\Sigma(u)e_0\\
 &\quad
 +\{-2b_0d_0-2\Sigma_{D0}+2\Sigma_{DD}w_0\}^\prime h(u,t)
 +o(1)\\
 &=a(u)+g^\prime h(u,t)+o(1)
 =\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)+o(1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
uniformly.  The displayed terms separately retain completion, donor-mean, covariance-risk, and donor-weight perturbations.

Finally, \(\mathcal C_0\) is polyhedral, \(H_0\) is positive definite, and \(Q\) is linear.  For each subset of weak inequalities imposed as equalities, the KKT system gives a linear formula for \(h\), with primal and dual feasibility described by linear inequalities in \((u,t)\).  There are finitely many subsets.  Uniqueness makes their formulas agree on common boundaries.  Hence \(h\) and \(\Psi_{\theta_0}\) are continuous piecewise affine on finitely many polyhedral cells.  Adding the variational inequalities for two limiting problems also gives
\[
 \lVert h(u,t)-h(u',t')\rVert_2
 \leq \frac{1}{2\rho}
 \lVert Q(u,t)-Q(u',t')\rVert_2,
\]
which supplies uniform continuity and completes the proof.

%%% FIELD proof-curve-decision-reduction
Fix finite \(F\subset\mathcal U\) and one realization of an admissible band \(C\).  Define
\[
 \mathcal A_F(C)=
 \{u\in F:\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)\in C(t)\ \text{for every }t\in\mathcal D\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(C)=\sup_{t\in\mathcal D}\operatorname{diam}C(t).
\tag{10}
\]
These events are measurable by Definition \(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}band\mbox{-}class}\).  If \(u,u'\in\mathcal A_F(C)\), both curve values lie in \(C(t)\), so
\[
 |\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u',t)|
 \leq\operatorname{diam}C(t).
\]
Taking the relevant maxima proves
\[
 c\{\mathcal A_F(C)\}\leq\mathcal L(C).
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover, \(u\in\mathcal A_F(C)\) is exactly the event that the entire curve indexed by \(u\) is covered.  Thus every grid-point coverage probability, including external randomization, is preserved.

Conversely, let a randomized measurable rule select \(A\subseteq F\).  For \(A\neq\varnothing\), set
\[
 C_A(t)=
 \left[\min_{u\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t),
       \max_{u\in A}\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)\right].
\tag{12}
\]
The finite extrema are measurable and every selected curve is covered.  Finiteness permits interchange of maxima:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_t\operatorname{diam}C_A(t)
 &=\sup_t\max_{u,u'\in A}
 |\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u',t)|\\
 &=\max_{u,u'\in A}\sup_t
 |\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u',t)|
 =c_A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
For \(A=\varnothing\), take a fixed singleton interval; its width and \(c_A\) are zero.  Accidental coverage can only improve validity.  Thus no nonrandom-shape restriction has been introduced.

If \(F=\mathcal U_N\) is a \(\delta\)-net, choose a measurable nearest point \(u_{i(u)}\) with deterministic tie breaking.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\) gives
\[
 \sup_t|\Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)-\Psi_{\theta_0}(u_{i(u)},t)|
 \leq K_u\delta.
\tag{14}
\]
Padding each nonempty envelope by \(K_u\delta\) at both endpoints makes selection of \(u_{i(u)}\) cover the curve at \(u\), and increases maximum width by at most \(2K_u\delta\).  This is the deterministic net transport; the associated probability loss is exactly the total-variation term in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:certified\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}value}\).

For computability, use the finitely many closed polyhedral cells from Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\).  For fixed \(u,u'\) and a pair of cells, their common restrictions on \(t\) have the form
\[
 \mathcal T_{ab}=
 \{t:A_{ab}t\leq b_{ab},\
 \mathbf1^\prime t=0,\
 t_k\geq0\ \text{if }(v_0)_k=0,\
 \lVert t\rVert_2\leq L_0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
On this set the curve difference is \(\beta_{ab}+\gamma_{ab}^\prime t\).  Its absolute maximum is the larger of
\[
 \max_t\ \sigma(\beta_{ab}+\gamma_{ab}^\prime t)
 \quad\text{subject to }t\in\mathcal T_{ab},
 \qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}.
\tag{16}
\]
These are second-order-cone programs because the only nonlinear constraint is the Euclidean-norm bound.  Compactness ensures attainment.  Maximizing over the finitely many nonempty cell pairs and both signs gives exactly \(d_\Psi(u,u')\).  Uniqueness of the strongly convex critical-cone minimizer makes formulas agree on shared boundaries, so closed-cell enumeration loses no boundary value.  This proves all assertions.

%%% FIELD proof-face-cost-comparison
If \(\mathcal C_0\) is a linear space, let \(\Pi_{\mathcal C_0}^{H_0}\) be orthogonal projection in the \(H_0\) inner product.  The equality-constrained first-order condition gives
\[
 h(u,t)=-\Pi_{\mathcal C_0}^{H_0}H_0^{-1}Q(u,t).
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(Q\) is linear, \(h(u,t)=H_uu+H_tt\) for fixed matrices \(H_u,H_t\).  Since \(a(u)\) is linear, vectors \(a_\star,b_\star\) exist with
\[
 \Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)=a_\star^\prime u+b_\star^\prime t.
\tag{18}
\]
Subtracting \(b_\star^\prime t\) from both endpoints of each band section preserves widths and converts simultaneous coverage into coverage of \(a_\star^\prime u\).  Adding it back gives the inverse map.  Thus the decision is exactly scalar Gaussian confidence-interval inference for \(a_\star^\prime u\), including value zero if \(a_\star=0\).

At a boundary, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}map}\) gives a finite continuous piecewise-affine map, and Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:certified\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}value}\) applies without strict complementarity.  Two invariances follow directly from Definition \(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}decision\mbox{-}value}\):
\[
 L_\alpha(\Psi+k)=L_\alpha(\Psi)
 \quad\text{for known }k(t),\qquad
 L_\alpha(c\Psi)=|c|L_\alpha(\Psi)\quad(c\neq0).
\tag{19}
\]
Translate or scale every endpoint for one inequality and apply the inverse transformation for the other.

There are nonconstant examples of both face types.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:hinge\mbox{-}sanity\mbox{-}check}\) supplies a moving-boundary map locally injective in a scalar direction at an unsaturated query.  At an interior donor face, perturbing \(\Sigma_{00}\) while holding means fixed leaves \(h\) unchanged and gives a nonzero affine direct derivative in (18).  Their decision values are finite by the full envelope over the compact parameter set.  They are positive at every confidence level \(1-\alpha>0\): choose \(N>(1-\alpha)^{-1}\) sufficiently close parameters whose curves have minimum pairwise distance \(\Delta>0\), and whose Gaussian laws have total variation at most \(\varepsilon<(1-\alpha)-N^{-1}\) from a common center.  If \(A\) is the set of covered curves, honesty gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0|A|\geq N(1-\alpha-\varepsilon).
\]
Since \(|A|\leq1+N\mathbf1\{\sup_t\operatorname{diam}C(Z,t)\geq\Delta\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_0[\sup_t\operatorname{diam}C(Z,t)]
 \geq\Delta\{1-\alpha-\varepsilon-N^{-1}\}>0.
\tag{20}
\]

Finally multiply all outcome and mean coordinates of either experiment by \(\sqrt c\), its covariance and ridge constant by \(c>0\), and transport the chart by this bijection.  Weights and active faces are unchanged, risks and local curves are multiplied by \(c\), and the Gaussian experiments, hence information in transported coordinates, are equivalent.  By (19), the cost is multiplied by \(c\).  Starting from the two positive costs in (20), a choice of \(c\) makes boundary and separated costs equal; increasing or decreasing it realizes either ordering.  Thus their difference may be positive, zero, or negative, and there is no compulsory boundary surcharge.

For \(F_{\mathrm{pre}}(a)=\operatorname{diag}(a,1)\),
\[
 A(a)=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 a_0(a)=\begin{pmatrix}3a/2\\3/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 d=(0,1,0)^\prime.
\tag{21}
\]
For \(a>0\), the two rows are nonzero and orthogonal, so the rank is two, and
\[
 A(a)^+a_0(a)=(0,3/2,3/2)^\prime,\qquad
 d^\prime A(a)^+a_0(a)=3/2.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus \(\dot c(u)=0\), and at \(a=1\) (21)--(22) give all displayed centered signal values.

Write \(v=(s,1-s)\).  On the edge \(w_1=0\), differentiating the criterion with \(w_2=z,w_3=1-z\) gives
\[
 w=(0,z,1-z),\qquad
 z=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{40s-9}{22},0,1\right).
\tag{23}
\]
The omitted donor's KKT slack equals
\[
 \eta=-\frac{40}{11}s^2+\frac{40}{11}s+\frac9{220}>0
\tag{24}
\]
on \(9/40\leq s\leq31/40\), and is at least \(27/40\) at both adjacent vertices.  The necessary and sufficient KKT conditions in Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:kkt\mbox{-}stratification}\) therefore make (23) the global three-donor solution.

The diagonal covariance gives
\[
 R(z)=(3/2-z)^2+1+4z^2+(1-z)^2
 =\frac{17}{4}-5z+6z^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Hence \(R'(1)=7\), \(s_0=31/40\), \(w_0=(0,1,0)\), and \(b_0=1/2\).  For general \(a\), direct solution before clipping gives
\[
 z_{\mathrm{unc}}(a,s)
 =\frac{30a^2s+10s-9}{2(10a^2s-10s+11)},\qquad
 s_{\mathrm{bdry}}(a)=\frac{31}{10(a^2+3)}.
\tag{26}
\]
At \((a,s)=(1,31/40)\),
\[
 \partial_a z_{\mathrm{unc}}=\frac{31}{44},\qquad
 \partial_s z_{\mathrm{unc}}=\frac{20}{11},\qquad
 \partial_a s_{\mathrm{bdry}}=-\frac{31}{80}.
\tag{27}
\]
Along \(a_n(u)=1+u/\sqrt n\), the sole changed pre-block mean derivative in target--donor order is \(Dm_1=(3/2,0,1,0)^\prime\).  Every other mean and covariance derivative is zero, and \(d\) is fixed.  Since \(p_1=1/2\) and \(\Sigma^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1/4,1)\),
\[
 I=\frac12Dm_1^\prime\Sigma^{-1}Dm_1
 =\frac12\left(\frac94+\frac14\right)=\frac54.
\tag{28}
\]
The post sample has no information about \(u\).

The signals are bounded locally, and \(A_0\) has both nonzero singular values equal to one.  Independent Gaussian errors are stationary, sub-Gaussian, and geometrically beta-mixing.  The linear and centered-quadratic blocks of \(\Omega=\operatorname{Var}(\phi_t)\) are uncorrelated by vanishing Gaussian third moments.  The linear block is \(\Sigma\), and for every nonzero symmetric \(B\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t^\prime B\varepsilon_t)
 =2\operatorname{tr}\{(\Sigma^{1/2}B\Sigma^{1/2})^2\}>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus \(\Omega\) is positive definite.  Fixed dimension supplies finite positive spectral and tail bounds, verifying all support, rank, positivity, mixing, and sampling-limit conditions of the subexperiment.

Upper clipping, (25), and (27) now give
\[
 \Psi_{\theta_0}(u,t)
 =7\min\!\left\{\frac{20}{11}t+\frac{31}{44}u,0\right\}.
\tag{30}
\]
The tangent query is \((t,-t)\), so \(|t|\leq L_0/\sqrt2\).

Put \(\sigma=\sqrt{4/5}\), \(\kappa=217/44\), and \(\ell=L_0/\sqrt2\).  For \(u\in\{-m,m\}\), (30) bounds the curve distance by \(2\kappa m\).  If \(m\leq80L_0/(31\sqrt2)\), then \(t=-\ell\) makes both hinges unsaturated and attains it, so
\[
 D_b=2\kappa m=\frac{217m}{22}.
\tag{31}
\]
The binary band problem is therefore a three-action problem: cover the minus curve only, the plus curve only, or both, with cost \(D_b\) only for the last action.

Reflection \((u,Z)\mapsto(-u,-Z)\) symmetrizes a rule without increasing maximum cost or decreasing minimum coverage.  The Gaussian monotone likelihood ratio then places the singleton actions in the tails and the both-curves action on \([-h,h]\).  Indeed, on \(z>0\), the negative-mean coverage gained per unit of symmetric cost is
\[
 \frac{2\varphi_\sigma(z+m)}
 {\varphi_\sigma(z-m)+\varphi_\sigma(z+m)}
 =\frac{2}{1+\exp(2mz/\sigma^2)},
\]
which decreases in \(z\); exchanging any outer allocation inward proves optimality.

If \(m<\sigma z_{1-\alpha}\), coverage under \(u=m\) requires
\[
 1-\alpha=\Pr_m\{Z\geq-h\}
 =\Phi\!\left(\frac{m+h}{\sigma}\right),\qquad
 h=\sigma z_{1-\alpha}-m.
\tag{32}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \Pr_m\{|Z|\leq h\}
 =\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha}-2m\sqrt{\frac54}\right)-\alpha.
\tag{33}
\]
The same holds at \(-m\).  If \(m\geq\sigma z_{1-\alpha}\), tail sign selection alone has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) and zero width.  Combining (31)--(33) gives
\[
 \frac{217m}{22}
 \max\!\left\{
 \Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha}-2m\sqrt{\frac54}\right)-\alpha,0
 \right\}.
\tag{34}
\]
For \(0<\alpha<1/2\), the maximum has a positive argument exactly when
\[
 z_{1-\alpha}-2m\sqrt{\frac54}>-z_{1-\alpha},
 \quad\text{that is,}\quad
 m<\frac{z_{1-\alpha}}{\sqrt{5/4}}.
\]
This proves the branch condition and all stated calculations.
