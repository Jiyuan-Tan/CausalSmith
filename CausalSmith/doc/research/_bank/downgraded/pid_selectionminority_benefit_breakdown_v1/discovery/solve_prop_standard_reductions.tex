We use three elementary techniques: elimination of nonnegative masses at a zero aggregate budget, the finite-marginal coupling characterization in \(\mathrm{lem:ordinal\text{-}cuts}\), and the full-law projection and lifting result in \(\mathrm{thm:projection\text{-}lifting}\).

Fix an observed array \(q\) and a reduced feasible point in some branch \(Q_o(q,\delta_D,\delta_S)\).  First suppose \(\delta_D=0\).  The defier-budget constraint in \(\mathrm{def:orientation\text{-}polytope}\), together with \(f_x\geq0\), gives
\[
  0\leq \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}f_x\leq 0.
\]
Because \(\mathcal X\) is finite, every summand vanishes: \(f_x=0\) for all \(x\).  By \(\mathrm{def:diagonal\text{-}residuals}\), all entries of \(r\) and \(s\) are nonnegative and
\[
  \sum_{w\in\mathcal V}r_{xw}=f_x=0,
  \qquad
  \sum_{w\in\mathcal V}s_{xw}=f_x=0.
\]
Thus \(r_{xw}=s_{xw}=0\) for every \((x,w)\).  Substitution in the residual identities yields, entry by entry,
\[
  d_{x0w}=q^1_{x0w},
  \qquad
  d_{x1w}=q^0_{x1w},
\]
and hence
\[
  u_{xw}=q^0_{x0w}-q^1_{x0w},
  \qquad
  v_{xw}=q^1_{x1w}-q^0_{x1w}.
\]
Consequently \(d\), \(u\), \(v\), \(c_x=\sum_wu_{xw}=\sum_wv_{xw}\), and the selected margins \(U_x\) and \(V_x\) are functions of the observable array \(q\).  Feasibility itself supplies the required nonnegativity of the displayed differences.

Now also suppose \(\delta_S=0\).  By \(\mathrm{def:selected\text{-}complier\text{-}margins}\),
\[
  a_x=U_x-h_x,
  \qquad
  b_x=V_x-h_x.
\]
The survivor constraints give \(h_x\leq U_x,V_x\), so \(a_x,b_x\geq0\).  The selection-minority constraint therefore implies
\[
  0\leq \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\min\{a_x,b_x\}
  \leq 0\cdot\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}c_x=0.
\]
Finiteness again implies \(\min\{a_x,b_x\}=0\) in every cell.  Since subtracting the common scalar \(h_x\) commutes with taking the minimum,
\[
  0=\min\{U_x-h_x,V_x-h_x\}
   =\min\{U_x,V_x\}-h_x,
\]
and therefore
\[
  h_x=\min\{U_x,V_x\}.
\]
The branch minority term is
\[
  o_xU_x+(1-o_x)V_x-h_x.
\]
It can vanish only with \(o_x=1\) when \(U_x<V_x\), only with \(o_x=0\) when \(V_x<U_x\), and with either value when \(U_x=V_x\).  Thus the smaller-margin orientation is observable, up to harmless ties.  Conversely, if there are no defiers and selection is one-sided among compliers in each cell, then \(f_x=0\) and one of \(a_x,b_x\) is zero in every cell.  Choosing \(o_x=1\) when \(a_x=0\) and \(o_x=0\) when \(b_x=0\), with either choice at a tie, makes both aggregate budget constraints equal to zero.  The zero-budget union therefore coincides exactly with no-defier, covariate-dependent one-sided selection, rather than merely being an outer relaxation of it.

The same argument gives an explicit endpoint computation before imposing complete selection.  Define
\[
  \ell_x=
  \max\!\left\{0,h_x-
     \min_{-1\leq k\leq K-1}B_{xk}\right\},
  \qquad
  \overline n_x=
  \min\!\left\{h_x,
     \min_{0\leq k\leq K}G_{xk}\right\}.
\]
At zero budgets, \(u\), \(v\), and \(h\) have just been shown to be fixed by \(q\).  The exact coupling statement \(\mathrm{lem:ordinal\text{-}cuts}\) says that the feasible values in cell \(x\) are precisely
\[
  n_x\in[\ell_x,\overline n_x].
\]
Its completion clause and \(\mathrm{thm:projection\text{-}lifting}\) show that the cellwise choices can be made simultaneously and lifted to a full response law inducing \(q\).  Hence the attainable numerators form the Minkowski sum of these finitely many intervals, namely
\[
  N\in
  \left[\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\ell_x,
        \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\overline n_x\right].
\]
Every point of this interval is attained: this follows inductively from
\([a,b]+[c,d]=[a+c,b+d]\), and lifting then supplies the corresponding full law.  If
\[
  H=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}h_x>0,
\]
the denominator is fixed and positive, so division is valid and \(\mathrm{def:identified\text{-}set}\), \(\mathrm{def:endpoints}\), and \(\mathrm{thm:attained\text{-}connected\text{-}endpoints}\) give the sharp equality
\[
  \Theta_I(q;0,0)=[L(q;0,0),U(q;0,0)],
\]
where
\[
  L(q;0,0)=
  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
    \max\!\left\{0,h_x-min_{-1\leq k\leq K-1}B_{xk}\right\}}
       {\displaystyle H},
\]
and
\[
  U(q;0,0)=
  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
    \min\!\left\{h_x,\min_{0\leq k\leq K}G_{xk}\right\}}
       {\displaystyle H}.
\]
If instead \(H=0\), nonnegativity forces every \(h_x=0\), and the positive-denominator requirement in \(\mathrm{def:identified\text{-}set}\) labels the target empty; no division is made.

The endpoint formulas require no linear program.  For each cell, one forward or backward pass through the \(K\) outcome levels computes all prefix and suffix sums occurring in the \(K+1\) values of \(B_{xk}\) and the \(K+1\) values of \(G_{xk}\).  Taking their minima, forming \(h_x=\min\{U_x,V_x\}\), and summing the cellwise endpoints therefore use \(O(K)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons per cell, hence \(O(mK)\) operations overall.

Suppose in addition that \(S_0=S_1=1\) almost surely among compliers.  Then, for every \(x\), all complier mass is selected under both treatments, so
\[
  U_x=V_x=h_x=c_x,
  \qquad
  H=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}c_x.
\]
When this common denominator is positive, the preceding formulas are exactly the sharp finite-ordinal strict-benefit interval formed from the two complier outcome margins.  Exactness follows from \(\mathrm{lem:ordinal\text{-}cuts}\), and the converse full-law construction follows from \(\mathrm{thm:projection\text{-}lifting}\); thus this is a sharp bound, not only an outer bound.

For \(K=2\), direct substitution in \(\mathrm{def:ordinal\text{-}threshold\text{-}cuts}\) gives
\[
  B_{x,-1}=U_x,
  \qquad
  B_{x,0}=u_{x1}+v_{x0},
  \qquad
  B_{x,1}=V_x,
\]
and
\[
  G_{x,0}=v_{x1},
  \qquad
  G_{x,1}=u_{x0},
  \qquad
  G_{x,2}=U_x.
\]
Because \(h_x\leq U_x,V_x\), the general cellwise interval simplifies to
\[
  \max\{0,h_x-u_{x1}-v_{x0}\}
  \leq n_x\leq
  \min\{h_x,u_{x0},v_{x1}\},
\]
which is the claimed binary reduction.

Finally, the relation to Dong--Heiler is only an anchor-class relation.  Let \(\mathcal X_+=\{x\in\mathcal X:c_x>0\}\).  Their conditional model imposes treatment monotonicity and, within each retained covariate cell, one of the two weak selection-monotonicity directions among compliers; it treats the conditional first stage and assignment overlap as support restrictions \cite[Assumptions~3.1--3.2 and the discussion following Assumption~3.1]{DongHeiler2026}.  In the present zero-budget class, treatment monotonicity follows from \(f_x=0\), and for \(x\in\mathcal X_+\), positivity of \(p_x\) permits the calculation
\[
  P(D_1\neq D_0\mid X=x)
  =P(C\mid X=x)
  =\frac{c_x}{p_x}>0.
\]
Moreover, \(a_x=0\) is equivalent to \(S_1\geq S_0\) almost surely conditional on \((C,X=x)\), while \(b_x=0\) is equivalent to the opposite inequality.  Thus, after restricting to \(\mathcal X_+\) as required by that conditional-first-stage support convention, the present zero-budget class is the finite-covariate, finite-ordinal subclass of the Dong--Heiler structural model under the common randomization, exclusion, consistency, and overlap restrictions.  Conversely, every law in that finite-ordinal subclass has \(f_x=0\) and, in each retained cell, either \(a_x=0\) or \(b_x=0\); it therefore satisfies both present budgets at \((\delta_D,\delta_S)=(0,0)\).  Cells outside \(\mathcal X_+\) have \(c_x=h_x=n_x=0\) and do not affect \(H\), \(N\), or the endpoint sums.  This proves the converse on the stated subclass and support.  It does not identify the positive-budget class with the Dong--Heiler model: positive \(\delta_D\) permits treatment defiers, and positive \(\delta_S\) permits both opposing selection-response types within a cell.
