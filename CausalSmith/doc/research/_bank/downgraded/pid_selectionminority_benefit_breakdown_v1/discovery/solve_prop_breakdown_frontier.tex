Fix an observed mass array \(q\).  For a full law with \(H>0\), the causal parameter is \(\theta=N/H\), whereas \(\Theta_I(q;\delta_D,\delta_S)\) is the observed-law sharp identified set supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:projection\text{-}lifting}\), and \(L(q;\delta_D,\delta_S)\) is its lower-endpoint functional.  Thus these objects are distinct, and, on the target-existence domain, the condition \(L(q;\delta_D,\delta_S)\ge t\) is equivalent to the assertion that every compatible value of the causal parameter is at least \(t\).

Let
\[
 \boldsymbol\delta^-=(\delta_D^-,\delta_S^-),\qquad
 \boldsymbol\delta^+=(\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+)\in\mathcal B
\]
satisfy \(\delta_D^-\leq\delta_D^+\) and \(\delta_S^-\leq\delta_S^+\).  Fix an orientation \(o\in\mathcal O\) and a reduced array
\[
 z=(d,u,v,r,s,(c_x)_x,(f_x)_x,(h_x)_x,(n_x)_x)
 \in Q_o(q,\delta_D^-,\delta_S^-).
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:orientation\text{-}polytope}\), every constraint defining this branch other than the two budget inequalities is independent of \(\boldsymbol\delta^-\).  The defier coordinates and complier coordinates are nonnegative, so
\[
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X} f_x
 \leq \delta_D^-
 \leq \delta_D^+,
 \qquad
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}c_x\geq0.
\]
The selection-minority row in the same definition and the ordering of the selection budgets give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
 \bigl\{o_xU_x+(1-o_x)V_x-h_x\bigr\}
 &\leq \delta_S^-\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}c_x \\
 &\leq \delta_S^+\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}c_x.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently \(z\in Q_o(q,\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+)\).  Since \(o\) was arbitrary,
\[
 \bigcup_{o\in\mathcal O}Q_o(q,\delta_D^-,\delta_S^-)
 \subseteq
 \bigcup_{o\in\mathcal O}Q_o(q,\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+).
\]
Restricting both sides to \(H>0\) and applying \(\mathrm{def:identified\text{-}set}\) yields
\[
 \Theta_I(q;\delta_D^-,\delta_S^-)
 \subseteq
 \Theta_I(q;\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+).
\]

Now suppose that the target exists at \(\boldsymbol\delta^-\) and that \(\boldsymbol\delta^+\in\mathcal R_t\).  The latter assertion, by \(\mathrm{def:robust\text{-}region}\), implies both that the target exists at \(\boldsymbol\delta^+\) and that \(L(q;\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+)\geq t\).  The endpoint minima are attained by \(\mathrm{thm:attained\text{-}connected\text{-}endpoints}\).  Therefore the preceding set inclusion and \(\mathrm{def:endpoints}\) imply
\[
 L(q;\delta_D^-,\delta_S^-)
 =\min\Theta_I(q;\delta_D^-,\delta_S^-)
 \geq
 \min\Theta_I(q;\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+)
 =L(q;\delta_D^+,\delta_S^+)
 \geq t.
\]
Together with target existence at \(\boldsymbol\delta^-\), this proves \(\boldsymbol\delta^-\in\mathcal R_t\).  The target-existence premise at the smaller budget is necessary for this relative statement: enlarging budgets preserves a feasible positive-denominator law, but the reverse implication need not hold.

Assume next that \(q\) is rational and fix \(t\in[0,1]\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}rational\text{-}charts}\), there is a finite semialgebraic stratification \(\{\mathcal S_j\}_{j=1}^J\) of \(\mathcal B\).  Each stratum is labeled model-infeasible, target-empty, or target-nonempty.  On every target-nonempty stratum there are polynomials \(A_j\) and \(B_j\), with rational coefficients and \(B_j\neq0\) throughout the stratum, such that
\[
 L(q;\delta_D,\delta_S)
 =\frac{A_j(\delta_D,\delta_S)}{B_j(\delta_D,\delta_S)}.
\]
Refine \(\mathcal S_j\), if necessary, by the two signs of \(B_j\).  This is still a finite semialgebraic stratification.  If \(\sigma_j\in\{-1,1\}\) is the resulting constant sign of \(B_j\), then on that stratum
\[
 L(q;\delta_D,\delta_S)\geq t
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sigma_j\bigl(A_j(\delta_D,\delta_S)-tB_j(\delta_D,\delta_S)\bigr)\geq0.
\]
The model-infeasible and target-empty strata do not belong to \(\mathcal R_t\), by \(\mathrm{def:robust\text{-}region}\).  Hence the displayed sign tests on the target-nonempty strata give an exact finite semialgebraic description of \(\mathcal R_t\).

For completeness, the boundary conclusion follows directly from this finite description and does not require a generic-position premise.  Express each semialgebraic stratum by finitely many polynomial sign conditions, collect all polynomials occurring in those descriptions together with every \(B_j\), every \(A_j-tB_j\), and the four affine polynomials defining the facets of \(\mathcal B\), and take their common sign refinement.  On every relatively open cell on which none of the collected polynomials vanishes, all stratum labels and all robust-region sign tests are constant.  Such a cell is therefore contained either in the relative interior of \(\mathcal R_t\) or in the relative interior of its complement in \(\mathcal B\).  It follows that the relative boundary of \(\mathcal R_t\) is contained in the finite union of the collected algebraic zero sets.  Intersecting those zero sets with the finitely many sign cells and retaining exactly the pieces whose closures meet both \(\mathcal R_t\) and its complement recovers the boundary exactly.  This proves that the boundary is finite piecewise algebraic, including singular and lower-dimensional pieces; the included facet polynomials give the corresponding statement for the ambient boundary as well.

Finally, the original chart labels keep the three substantive boundaries distinct.  An interface between a model-infeasible stratum and a feasible stratum is certified as a falsification boundary.  Within the model-feasible region, an interface between a target-empty stratum and a target-nonempty stratum is certified as a positive-\(H\) target-existence boundary.  On a target-nonempty stratum, the conclusion equality is exactly
\[
 A_j-tB_j=0,
\]
because \(B_j\neq0\), and hence it is exactly the chart's \(L=t\) conclusion-boundary piece.  Chart interfaces are retained in the common refinement, so no boundary piece is lost when a formula or feasibility label changes.  If two or three kinds of boundary coincide on a lower-dimensional stratum, their separate feasibility, positive-denominator, and equality certificates remain attached to that same stratum rather than being conflated.  This establishes every assertion of the proposition.

For every \(t\in[0,1]\), the robust region \(\mathcal R_t\) is relatively downward closed on its target-existence domain: whenever two budget pairs are coordinatewise ordered, the larger lies in \(\mathcal R_t\), and the target exists at the smaller, the smaller lies in \(\mathcal R_t\).  Whenever the fixed observed mass array is rational, the region has a finite piecewise-algebraic boundary obtained from the chart in \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}rational\text{-}charts}\).  The chart separately labels the falsification boundary, the positive-\(H\) target-existence boundary, and the conclusion boundary \(L=t\).
