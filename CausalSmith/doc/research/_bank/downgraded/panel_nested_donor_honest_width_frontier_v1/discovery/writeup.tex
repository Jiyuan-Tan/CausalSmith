\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}nested\_\allowbreak{}donor\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}width\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Staggered adoption creates shrinking not-yet-treated donor sets. Conditional on the declared nonempty rational fixed design, this note maps every treated-unit--horizon error into one deduplicated Gaussian contrast vector and defines the jointly attainable bias set generated by the shared calendar-time coefficients and cellwise remainder bounds. The exact honest deterministic linear-band frontier is the attained minimum, over the relevant product of donor simplexes, of the worst shifted Gaussian-cube radius over that joint bias set. A fixed-Bonferroni bias-plus-standard-error SOCP remains finite-sample honest and within the stated dimension-uniform spectral factor of this exact frontier. A legal common-donor family with rational covariance factor proves that the square-root covariance-condition-number dependence is order-sharp for fixed Bonferroni calibration. Zero-bias Loewner and diagonal row-disjoint results give computable strict nested-width gains. Separately, an in-house affine facial-reduction alternative and relative-interior strong duality yield an exact finite primal--facial-dual certificate for strict SOCP improvement on degenerate faces, together with a terminating real-algebraic construction and verifier; no quantitative runtime bound is claimed. Ben-Michael, Feller, and Rothstein provide donor geometry only; \texttt{scpi} and \texttt{augsynth::multisynth} are implementation consumers, not estimators covered by these theorems.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Ben-Michael, Feller, and Rothstein define the shrinking donor geometry and discuss deepest-pool and horizon-varying choices, but they do not supply this note's bounded-mean known-covariance experiment or an honest-width frontier for their partially pooled estimator. Cattaneo, Feng, Palomba, and Titiunik provide non-asymptotic simultaneous prediction and conic architecture, but not the exact min--max shifted Gaussian-cube frontier over jointly constrained biases or an order-sharp comparison with fixed Bonferroni calibration. Scalar convex-class interval theory does not resolve the simultaneous correlated rectangle or its donor-support comparison.

\paragraph{Niche.} Known covariance in a prespecified positive spectral band, rational fixed inputs, a nonempty treated set, and this note's bounded linear conditional-mean class define a finite-sample decision problem in which donor availability, shared-cell covariance, joint bias attainability, multiplicity, and exact width are all explicit. The targets are the exact robust frontier \(W_s\), the implementable fixed-Bonferroni SOCP value \(\Gamma_s\), strict nested-support comparisons on proved domains, and the worst-case price of the Bonferroni surrogate.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note derives the deduplicated Gaussian contrast law and exact coordinate bias support function, proves finite-sample SOCP coverage, and characterizes the full bounded-mean frontier as an attained minimum over weights of a maximum shifted Gaussian-cube radius over the joint attainable bias set. It proves the spectral comparison and constructs a legal common-donor sequence on which the SOCP-to-exact-width ratio grows as the square root of the covariance condition number. Separate zero-bias Loewner, independent-coordinate product-root, and rational-witness results identify strict exact-frontier gains. For the general degenerate SOCP comparison, an in-house separation argument produces affine exposing multipliers at every facial-reduction step, after which relative-interior strong duality and effective real algebraic methods give a finite strict-gap certificate and a terminating verifier.

\section{Related work}

Ben-Michael, Feller, and Rothstein (2022, Section 2) supply the shrinking not-yet-treated donor-set geometry and note that their estimator may use horizon-varying donors even though their exposition and application focus on a common deepest pool. They do not supply this note's bounded-mean known-covariance class, and no theorem here evaluates their partially pooled estimator. Cattaneo, Feng, Palomba, and Titiunik (2025, Sections 4.4 and 6 and Supplemental Appendix S.6) provide staggered prediction-error, simultaneous-coverage, and conic-programming architecture; they do not give the joint attainable-bias min--max frontier or the order-sharp SOCP-to-frontier ratio proved here. Armstrong and Kolesar (2018, equation (13) and Sections 3.2--3.4) provide scalar worst-case-bias plus standard-error logic over a convex Gaussian regression class. Anderson (1955, Theorem 1) supplies the weak moving-set inequality used by the separate strict zero-bias Loewner result. Kallus (2020) motivates the dual-norm bias--variance optimization analogy. Pataki (2013) supplies the minimal-face, relative-Slater, strong-duality, and dual-attainment substrate used after reduction; the stronger affine-multiplier facial-reduction alternative needed by this certificate is proved in-house. Basu, Pollack, and Roy (2006, Theorems 13.11 and 14.16) support terminating real-closed-field quantifier elimination and sign-condition sampling with the stated sample-representation bounds; the note does not attribute a bit-complexity bound for the complete certificate construction to those excerpts. The present results retain the declared known-covariance experiment; they do not claim a covariance-estimation or published-class transfer theorem. \texttt{scpi} and \texttt{augsynth::multisynth} are potential software consumers of the geometry and optimization, not covered procedures.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{it}=Y_{it}(0)\mathbf 1\{t<T_i\}+Y_{it}(1)\mathbf 1\{t\ge T_i\}\) for every \(i\in\mathcal I\) and \(t\in\mathcal T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency with absorbing staggered adoption, \cite{BenMichaelFellerRothstein2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-feasibility]\label{ass:donor-feasibility}
\(D_{jK}\ne\varnothing\) for every \(j\in\mathcal J\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonempty not-yet-treated donor pool, \cite{BenMichaelFellerRothstein2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mean-structure]\label{ass:mean-structure}
\(m_{it}=x_i^{\top}\beta_t+r_{it}\) for every \((i,t)\in\mathcal C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This tag records that the condition is imposed as this note's working bounded conditional-mean model rather than attributed to an external comparator; it does not claim historical priority for linear mean restrictions.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:coefficient-radius]\label{ass:coefficient-radius}
\(\lVert\beta_t\rVert_2\le L\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Norm-bounded convex regression class, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cellwise-misspecification]\label{ass:cellwise-misspecification}
\(|r_{it}|\le\delta/2\) for every \((i,t)\in\mathcal C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} After outcome standardization, \(\delta\) bounds residual mean differences not absorbed by observed predictors. Held-out pretreatment cells can calibrate substantively plausible values, and the same neighborhood evaluates every donor composition.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-law]\label{ass:gaussian-law}
\(Q-m\sim N(0,\Sigma_Q)\) conditional on \(x\) and \(T_{\mathrm{adopt}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known-covariance Gaussian fixed-design experiment, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-band]\label{ass:covariance-band}
\(\sigma_{\min}I_{|\mathcal C|}\preceq\Sigma_Q\preceq\sigma_{\max}I_{|\mathcal C|}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicitly chosen finite-experiment regularity restriction, not a condition attributed to Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato. In a known-covariance experiment the extremal eigenvalues are computable before outcomes are analyzed; reporting results over a prespecified band excludes nearly deterministic and explosively noisy directions, while sensitivity over candidate values of \(\sigma_{\min}\) and \(\sigma_{\max}\) gives a direct calibration interpretation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treated-set-nonempty]\label{ass:treated-set-nonempty}
\(\mathcal J\ne\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonempty treated-set nonvacuity domain condition, \cite{BenMichaelFellerRothstein2022}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:prediction-error]\label{lem:prediction-error}
For every deterministic \(w\in\mathcal F_s\) and \(e=(j,h)\in\mathcal E\), \(\widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e=a_e(w)^{\top}Q\). Conditional on the fixed inputs this error is Gaussian with mean \(a_e(w)^{\top}m\) and covariance matrix \(A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\) across \(e\in\mathcal E\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\), \(w\in\mathcal F_s\), and \(e=(j,h)\in\mathcal E\), and put \(t_e=T_j+h\).  Since \(h\geq 0\), \(t_e\geq T_j\), so \texttt{ass:consistency} gives
\[
Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{j,t_e}=Y_{j,t_e}(1).
\]
If \(w_{jh,i}>0\), then either \(i\in D_{jh}\), under the nested rule, or \(i\in D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\), under the deep rule; the inclusion follows from \(h\leq K\).  Thus \(T_i>t_e\), and the same consistency assumption gives
\[
Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,t_e}=Y_{i,t_e}(0).
\]
Using the definition of \(\tau_e\), cancellation of the treated potential outcome yields, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e
&=Y_{j,t_e}(1)-\sum_iw_{jh,i}Y_{i,t_e}(0)
  -\{Y_{j,t_e}(1)-Y_{j,t_e}(0)\}\\
&=Y_{j,t_e}(0)-\sum_iw_{jh,i}Y_{i,t_e}(0)\\
&=a_e(w)^{\top}Q.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses the definitions of the deduplicated vector \(Q\) and row \(a_e(w)\); deduplication merely makes a reused cell one common coordinate and does not change this linear contrast.  By \texttt{ass:gaussian-law}, conditional on \(x\) and \(T_{\mathrm{adopt}}\),
\[
Q=m+\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\sim N(0,\Sigma_Q).
\]
Stacking the preceding identities in the fixed order of \(\mathcal E\) therefore gives
\[
\widehat\tau(w)-\tau=A(w)m+A(w)\varepsilon
\sim N\!\left(A(w)m,\,A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\right),
\]
which proves both the coordinate identity and the asserted joint Gaussian mean and covariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The deduplicated contrast identity turns the staggered potential-outcome problem into one finite Gaussian decision problem. \textit{Gap.} CattaneoFengPalombaTitiunik2025 decomposes staggered synthetic-control prediction error, but does not specialize the entire treated-unit--horizon vector to this fixed-design known-covariance contrast experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The identity gives \texttt{scpi} a covariance-safe indexing rule for cells reused by several horizons.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bias-radius]\label{lem:bias-radius}
For every \(e=(j,h)\in\mathcal E\) and \(w\in\mathcal F_s\), \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}}|a_e(w)^{\top}m|=b_e(w)=L\lVert x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\rVert_2+\delta\). Each endpoint is attainable for that coordinate by a member of the model class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a support rule \(s\), a feasible weight array \(w\in\mathcal F_s\), and
\(e=(j,h)\in\mathcal E\).  Put \(t=T_j+h\) and
\[
 d_e(w)=x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i.
 \tag{1}
\]
The simplex constraint in the definition of \(\mathcal F_s\) gives
\(w_{jh,i}\geq0\), \(\sum_iw_{jh,i}=1\), and zero weight outside the
applicable donor set.  For the deep rule this support is also contained in
\(D_{jh}\), because \(h\leq K\) implies
\(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\).  Thus the definition of \(a_e(w)\) and
\texttt{ass:mean-structure} give, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_e(w)^{\top}m
 &=m_{jt}-\sum_iw_{jh,i}m_{it}\\
 &=x_j^{\top}\beta_t+r_{jt}
   -\sum_iw_{jh,i}(x_i^{\top}\beta_t+r_{it})\\
 &=d_e(w)^{\top}\beta_t+r_{jt}-\sum_iw_{jh,i}r_{it}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, \texttt{ass:coefficient-radius},
\texttt{ass:cellwise-misspecification}, nonnegativity of the weights, and
their unit sum,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |a_e(w)^{\top}m|
 &\leq \lVert d_e(w)\rVert_2\lVert\beta_t\rVert_2
       +|r_{jt}|+\sum_iw_{jh,i}|r_{it}|\\
 &\leq L\lVert d_e(w)\rVert_2+\frac{\delta}{2}
       +\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_iw_{jh,i}\\
 &=L\lVert d_e(w)\rVert_2+\delta
 =b_e(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

It remains to show that (3) is the exact support function, rather than only
an upper bound.  For \(\eta\in\{-1,1\}\), define the coefficient vector at
time \(t\) by
\[
 \beta_t^{(\eta)}=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle \eta L\,d_e(w)/\lVert d_e(w)\rVert_2,
     &d_e(w)\neq0,\\[3pt]
 0,&d_e(w)=0,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
and put \(\beta_{t'}^{(\eta)}=0\) for \(t'\neq t\).  Set
\[
 r_{jt}^{(\eta)}=\eta\frac{\delta}{2},
 \qquad
 r_{it}^{(\eta)}=-\eta\frac{\delta}{2}
 \quad\hbox{when }w_{jh,i}>0,
 \tag{5}
\]
and set all remaining remainder coordinates equal to zero.  These assignments
are compatible on the deduplicated cell set: every positively weighted donor
satisfies \(T_i>t\), whereas \(T_j\leq t\), so no donor cell
\((i,t)\) in (5) is the focal cell \((j,t)\).  Equations (4)--(5) satisfy
the coefficient and cellwise bounds, including the boundary cases \(L=0\)
and \(\delta=0\).  Defining \(m^{(\eta)}\) by
\texttt{ass:mean-structure} and substituting in (2) yields
\[
 a_e(w)^{\top}m^{(\eta)}
 =\eta L\lVert d_e(w)\rVert_2
   +\eta\frac{\delta}{2}
   +\eta\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_iw_{jh,i}
 =\eta b_e(w).
 \tag{6}
\]

For completeness, each mean in (6) is realized by a law, not merely by a
formal nuisance assignment.  On the fixed inputs defining
\(\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}(L,\delta,\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max})\), take
\(Q\sim N(m^{(\eta)},\Sigma_Q)\), use its deduplicated coordinates as the
untreated potential outcomes on \(\mathcal C\), extend the untreated array
arbitrarily outside \(\mathcal C\), choose arbitrary treated potential
outcomes, and define observed outcomes by \texttt{ass:consistency}.  The mean
conditions hold by (4)--(5), the Gaussian condition holds by construction,
and the fixed covariance obeys the covariance-band condition required in
\texttt{def:model-class}.  Hence this is a member of that class.  Taking
\(\eta=1\) and \(\eta=-1\) proves attainability of both endpoints in (6).
Together with (3), this proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}}
 |a_e(w)^{\top}m|=b_e(w).
\]
No limiting argument is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact support functions prevent the upper band from hiding an assumed or loose bias bound. \textit{Gap.} ArmstrongKolesar2018 gives convex-class worst-case bias for scalar affine estimators; the displayed radius exploits the staggered donor simplex and cellwise misspecification class. \textit{Consumer.} The formula lets \texttt{augsynth::multisynth} report the marginal price of predictor imbalance and declared misspecification for each horizon.

\begin{lemma}[lem:contrast-geometry]\label{lem:contrast-geometry}
For every feasible \(w\), deduplication and not-yet-treated support imply \(A(w)A(w)^{\top}\succeq I_M\). Consequently, \(A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\succeq\sigma_{\min}I_M\) and \(\sigma_{\min}\le\operatorname{sd}_e(w)^2\le2\sigma_{\max}\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(e=(j,h)\), call \(c_e=(j,T_j+h)\) its focal cell.  The cells \(c_e\) are distinct as \(e\) varies: equality of two such cells first forces the same unit \(j\), and then the same horizon.  Moreover, no \(c_e\) can be a donor cell in any contrast row.  Indeed, if the same cell were a donor cell in a row observed at calendar time \(t=T_j+h\), donor eligibility would require \(T_j>t\), whereas \(t\geq T_j\), a contradiction.  Thus, after putting the \(M\) focal-cell columns first, the deduplicated contrast matrix has the block form
\[
A(w)=\begin{bmatrix}I_M&B(w)\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Consequently
\[
A(w)A(w)^{\top}=I_M+B(w)B(w)^{\top}\succeq I_M.
\]
The simplexes in both support rules are nonempty: \texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} gives nonemptiness of \(D_{jK}\), and nesting gives \(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\).  For every feasible row, its focal coordinate is disjoint from its donor coordinates and the simplex restrictions give
\[
\lVert a_e(w)\rVert_2^2=1+\sum_iw_{jh,i}^2
\leq1+\left(\sum_iw_{jh,i}\right)^2=2.
\]
Now apply both sides of \texttt{ass:covariance-band}.  Congruence preserves the Loewner order, so
\[
A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}
\succeq\sigma_{\min}A(w)A(w)^{\top}
\succeq\sigma_{\min}I_M.
\]
For a coordinate row, the same assumption gives
\[
\sigma_{\min}\lVert a_e(w)\rVert_2^2
\leq a_e(w)^{\top}\Sigma_Qa_e(w)
=\operatorname{sd}_e(w)^2
\leq\sigma_{\max}\lVert a_e(w)\rVert_2^2.
\]
Since the focal coefficient equals one, \(\lVert a_e(w)\rVert_2^2\geq1\); combining this fact with the preceding upper norm bound proves
\[
\sigma_{\min}\leq\operatorname{sd}_e(w)^2\leq2\sigma_{\max}.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The treated-cell identity component is the early kill test for a dimension-uniform lower Gaussian-max benchmark. \textit{Gap.} ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2015 allows arbitrary Gaussian covariance, but does not derive this Loewner lower bound from staggered donor eligibility and deduplicated panel cells. \textit{Consumer.} The result guarantees that repeated donor cells cannot make simultaneous bands spuriously collapse in \texttt{scpi}.

\begin{lemma}[lem:gaussian-quantile-ratio]\label{lem:gaussian-quantile-ratio}
For every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), \(c_\alpha<\infty\). Every certified rational multiplier \(z_B\) therefore satisfies \(z_B\le c_\alpha q_\alpha(M)\), with no dimension-dependent remainder.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\overline\Phi(x)=1-\Phi(x)\), let \(\varphi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}\), and abbreviate \(q_m=q_\alpha(m)\) and \(z_m=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/(2m))\).  Independence in the definition of \(q_m\) gives
\[
1-\alpha=\{1-2\overline\Phi(q_m)\}^{m}.
\]
In particular \(q_m>0\), because \(1-\alpha>1/2\).

We first give an elementary lower bound for \(q_m\).  For every \(x\geq1\), monotonicity of \(\varphi\) on \([0,\infty)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline\Phi(x)
&\geq\int_x^{x+1/x}\varphi(u)\,du
\geq\frac1x\varphi(x+1/x)\\
&\geq c_0\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}x,
\qquad c_0=\frac{e^{-3/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last step uses \((x+1/x)^2/2\leq x^2/2+3/2\).  If \(m\geq3\) and \(q_m\leq\sqrt{\log m}\), this inequality, together with \(\overline\Phi(q_m)\geq\overline\Phi(1)\geq\varphi(2)\) when \(q_m<1\), yields in both cases
\[
\overline\Phi(q_m)\geq c_0\frac{m^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log m}}.
\]
Here the case \(q_m<1\) uses \(m^{-1/2}/\sqrt{\log m}\leq e^{-1/2}\) for \(m\geq3\) and \(c_0e^{-1/2}=\varphi(2)\).  Hence
\[
1-\alpha
\leq\exp\{-2m\overline\Phi(q_m)\}
\leq\exp\!\left\{-2c_0\sqrt{m/\log m}\right\}.
\]
Because the last expression tends to zero, there is an integer \(m_0\geq3\), depending only on \(\alpha\), for which it is strictly smaller than \(1-\alpha\) for every \(m\geq m_0\).  The supposition is then impossible, and therefore
\[
q_m>\sqrt{\log m}\qquad(m\geq m_0).
\]

For the numerator, the exponential Markov inequality applied to a standard normal \(G\) gives, for \(x\geq0\),
\[
\overline\Phi(x)=\Pr(G\geq x)
\leq e^{-x^2}\mathbb E e^{xG}=e^{-x^2/2}.
\]
Since \(z_m>0\) and \(\overline\Phi(z_m)=\alpha/(2m)\), it follows that
\[
z_m\leq\sqrt{2\log(2m/\alpha)}.
\]
For \(m\geq m_0\), the preceding two displays imply
\[
\frac{z_m+1}{q_m}
\leq
\sqrt{2\left(1+\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{\log m_0}\right)}
+\frac1{\sqrt{\log m_0}}.
\]
For \(1\leq m<m_0\), there are only finitely many positive denominators \(q_m\).  Taking the larger of the displayed constant and those finitely many ratios proves \(c_\alpha<\infty\).  Finally, the outward-rounded calibration condition in the definition of \(z_B\) gives
\[
z_B<z_M+1\leq c_\alpha q_\alpha(M),
\]
and hence the claimed weak inequality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This quantile comparison prevents the finite rational calibration required by certification from introducing a hidden multiplicity divergence. \textit{Gap.} ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2015 supplies Gaussian-max comparison and anti-concentration, while the uniform ratio for an outward-rounded Bonferroni multiplier is not stated there. \textit{Consumer.} Software can use a rationally certified multiplier while retaining a dimension-uniform efficiency comparison.

\begin{theorem}[thm:socp-coverage]\label{thm:socp-coverage}
For either support rule \(s\), every optimizer of \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} yields the rectangular band \(\widehat\tau_e(w)\pm[b_e(w)+z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w)]\), \(e\in\mathcal E\), with finite-sample coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}\). The optimization has the rational standard-form SOCP encoding in \texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}, and its optimal maximum half-width is \(\Gamma_s\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\), and for
\(w\in\mathcal F_s\) define
\[
 H_s(w)=\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \{b_e(w)+z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w)\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The condition \texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} makes every deepest donor set
\(D_{jK}\) nonempty.  Since \(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\) for
\(0\leq h\leq K\), every simplex occurring under either support rule is
nonempty.  Each is compact, and \(\mathcal E\) is finite.  Therefore
\(\mathcal F_s\) is a nonempty compact finite product of simplexes.  The
two norms defining \(b_e(w)\) and \(\operatorname{sd}_e(w)\) are continuous
norms of affine functions of \(w\); hence \(H_s\) is continuous and attains
its minimum.

The program in \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} is exactly an epigraph form of
that minimum.  Indeed, every feasible \((w,u,v,g)\) satisfies, for every
\(e=(j,h)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_e(w)+z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w)
 &=L\left\lVert x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right\rVert_2+
   \delta+z_B\lVert R_Qa_e(w)\rVert_2\\
 &\leq Lu_e+\delta+z_Bv_e
 \leq g.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Conversely, for any \(w\in\mathcal F_s\), the choices
\[
 u_e=\left\lVert x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right\rVert_2,
 \qquad
 v_e=\lVert R_Qa_e(w)\rVert_2,
 \qquad
 g=H_s(w)
 \tag{3}
\]
are feasible.  Consequently,
\[
 \Gamma_s=\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}H_s(w).
 \tag{4}
\]
In particular an optimizer exists, and if \((w,u,v,g)\) is any optimizer,
then (2)--(4) imply \(H_s(w)=g=\Gamma_s\).  Thus the maximum of the actual
half-widths in the statement, rather than of possibly slack auxiliary
variables, equals \(\Gamma_s\).

Now fix any such optimizing weight array and any
\(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}(L,\delta,\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max})\).
By \texttt{lem:prediction-error} and \texttt{lem:bias-radius}, jointly over
\(e\in\mathcal E\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e=\mu_e+Z_e,
 \qquad
 \mu_e=a_e(w)^{\top}m,
 \qquad
 |\mu_e|\leq b_e(w),
 \tag{5}
\]
where \(Z\) is centered Gaussian with covariance
\(A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\).  The row \(a_e(w)\) is nonzero because its
focal coordinate equals one, and \(R_Q\) has full rank.  Hence
\(\operatorname{sd}_e(w)=\lVert R_Qa_e(w)\rVert_2>0\), and
\(Z_e/\operatorname{sd}_e(w)\sim N(0,1)\).

Consider the event
\[
 \mathcal G=
 \bigcap_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \{|Z_e|\leq z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w)\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
On \(\mathcal G\), the triangle inequality and (5) give, simultaneously for
all coordinates,
\[
 |\widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e|
 \leq b_e(w)+z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w).
 \tag{7}
\]
No independence is required.  The union bound, the fact that
\(M=|\mathcal E|\), and the first outward-rounded certificate in the
definition of \(z_B\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(\mathcal G^c)
 &\leq\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}
       P\{|Z_e|>z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w)\}\\
 &=2M\{1-\Phi(z_B)\}
 \leq\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (7)--(8) proves simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).
The argument is finite-sample and uniform because the only law-dependent
mean quantity in (5) is bounded by the exact model-class support function.

Finally, the record in \texttt{def:finite-input} has rational scalar and
matrix entries.  For each \(e=(j,h)\), the two norm constraints are precisely
\[
 \left(u_{jh},x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right)\in\mathcal L_{p+1},
 \qquad
 (v_{jh},R_Qa_{jh}(w))\in\mathcal L_{|\mathcal C|+1}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The simplex restrictions and the epigraph inequalities are rational linear
equalities plus nonnegative slacks.  Splitting each free scalar into positive
and negative parts and retaining equalities in zero-cone blocks therefore
gives exactly the rational product-cone record of
\texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}.  Conversely, recombining split
variables and eliminating slacks recovers (2) and the simplex constraints,
so the encoding preserves the feasible objective values in both directions.
Its value is therefore \(\Gamma_s\) by (4), which completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies an implementable, outcome-independent weight rule with exact finite-sample honesty and auditable finite input. \textit{Gap.} CattaneoFengPalombaTitiunik2025 gives non-asymptotic staggered prediction intervals and conic computation, but does not choose horizon-specific weights to minimize a common honest bias-plus-standard-error envelope. \textit{Consumer.} The rational SOCP can be added as a width-optimal weighting option to \texttt{scpi} and \texttt{augsynth::multisynth}.

\begin{theorem}[thm:constant-factor-frontier]\label{thm:constant-factor-frontier}
For \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\), the exact robust deterministic linear-band frontier and the SOCP value satisfy \[W_s\le\Gamma_s\le\left(1+c_\alpha\sqrt{2\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}}\right)W_s.\] The factor is independent of \(N\), \(T\), \(K\), \(M\), the adoption pattern, the predictors, and the donor-overlap geometry.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\).  By
\texttt{thm:socp-coverage}, an optimizing SOCP weight array and the
deterministic half-widths
\[
 \ell_e=b_e(w)+z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w) \tag{51}
\]
form an honest band whose maximum half-width is \(\Gamma_s\).  Hence
\[
 W_s\leq\Gamma_s. \tag{52}
\]

For the reverse comparison, take any feasible pair \((w,\ell)\) in
\texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} and put
\[
 R=\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e. \tag{53}
\]
The nonempty-treated-set domain of \texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} and
\(K\geq0\) imply \(M=|\mathcal E|\geq1\), so this maximum and
\(q_\alpha(M)\) are defined.

First, \(b_e(w)\leq R\) for every \(e\).  Otherwise, for some \(e\), the
endpoint-attainment assertion of \texttt{lem:bias-radius} supplies a
model-class law under which the coordinate error has mean \(b_e(w)>R\).
By \texttt{lem:contrast-geometry}, its variance
\(\operatorname{sd}_e(w)^2\) is positive.  Since \(\ell_e\leq R\), writing
the error as \(b_e(w)+\operatorname{sd}_e(w)G\), with
\(G\sim N(0,1)\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{|\widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e|\leq\ell_e\}
 &\leq \Phi\!\left(
 \frac{\ell_e-b_e(w)}{\operatorname{sd}_e(w)}\right)\\
 &<\frac12<1-\alpha.
\end{aligned} \tag{54}
\]
The simultaneous event is contained in this coordinate event, contradicting
uniform honesty.  Therefore
\[
 \max_{e\in\mathcal E}b_e(w)\leq R. \tag{55}
\]
Only coordinatewise endpoint attainment is used in (54); no simultaneous
choice of all bias endpoints is asserted.

We next obtain a stochastic lower bound under one jointly valid model law.
Choose any \(e_0\in\mathcal E\) and any model-class law supplied by the
attainment part of \texttt{lem:bias-radius}.  By
\texttt{lem:prediction-error}, under this law
\[
 \widehat\tau(w)-\tau=\mu+Z,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,V),
 \qquad V=A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}, \tag{56}
\]
for one joint mean vector \(\mu=A(w)m\).  By
\texttt{lem:contrast-geometry},
\[
 V\succeq\sigma_{\min}I_M. \tag{57}
\]
Let \(X\sim N(0,\sigma_{\min}I_M)\), and independently let
\(U\sim N(0,V-\sigma_{\min}I_M)\), permitting a singular covariance for
\(U\).  Then \(X+U\) has the same law as \(Z\).

Write \(s_0=\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}}>0\).  For \(R>0\) and \(u\in\mathbb R\),
define
\[
 g_R(u)=\Pr\{|s_0G+u|\leq R\}
 =\Phi\!\left(\frac{R-u}{s_0}\right)
  +\Phi\!\left(\frac{R+u}{s_0}\right)-1. \tag{58}
\]
This function is even.  For \(u\geq0\),
\[
 g_R'(u)=\frac1{s_0}\left\{
 \varphi\!\left(\frac{R+u}{s_0}\right)
 -\varphi\!\left(\frac{R-u}{s_0}\right)\right\}\leq0, \tag{59}
\]
because \(|R+u|\geq|R-u|\) and the standard normal density decreases with
absolute value.  Hence \(g_R(u)\leq g_R(0)\) for all real \(u\).  Conditional
on \(U=u\), the coordinates of \(X\) are independent, and (58)--(59) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{\mu+X+U\in[-R,R]^M\mid U=u\}
 &=\prod_{k=1}^M g_R(u_k+\mu_k)\\
 &\leq g_R(0)^M
 =\Pr\{X\in[-R,R]^M\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{60}
\]
Integration over \(U\) proves the same inequality unconditionally.  At
\(R=0\), both relevant cube probabilities are zero because \(X\) is
nonsingular, so the inequality remains valid.

The coverage rectangle is contained in \([-R,R]^M\).  Uniform honesty under
the particular law in (56), followed by (60), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 1-\alpha
 &\leq\Pr\{|\widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e|\leq\ell_e
              \text{ for every }e\in\mathcal E\}\\
 &\leq\Pr\{\mu+Z\in[-R,R]^M\}\\
 &\leq\Pr\{X\in[-R,R]^M\}\\
 &=\Pr\left\{\max_{1\leq k\leq M}|G_k|
       \leq\frac{R}{\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}}}\right\},
\end{aligned} \tag{61}
\]
where the last-line variables are independent standard normals.  The final
probability is strictly increasing in its nonnegative threshold.  By the
definition of \(q_\alpha(M)\),
\[
 R\geq\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}}\,q_\alpha(M). \tag{62}
\]

The coordinate upper bound in \texttt{lem:contrast-geometry} and the
dimension-uniform calibration in \texttt{lem:gaussian-quantile-ratio} give
\[
 \operatorname{sd}_e(w)\leq\sqrt{2\sigma_{\max}},
 \qquad
 z_B\leq c_\alpha q_\alpha(M). \tag{63}
\]
Since \(\Gamma_s\) is the minimum SOCP envelope, (55), (62), and (63) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_s
 &\leq\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
       \{b_e(w)+z_B\operatorname{sd}_e(w)\}\\
 &\leq R+c_\alpha q_\alpha(M)\sqrt{2\sigma_{\max}}\\
 &\leq\left(1+c_\alpha
       \sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}}}\right)R.
\end{aligned} \tag{64}
\]
Taking the infimum over all honest \((w,\ell)\) and combining with (52)
proves
\[
 W_s\leq\Gamma_s\leq
 \left(1+c_\alpha
 \sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}}}\right)W_s.
\]
The denominator is positive by the declared covariance-band parameter domain.
There is no asymptotic condition in this theorem, and its factor is independent
of the panel dimensions, adoption pattern, predictors, and donor-overlap
geometry.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-sample sandwich makes the conic band decision-theoretically interpretable, and \texttt{thm:condition-number-order-sharpness} proves that its square-root spectral dependence is unavoidable for fixed Bonferroni calibration. \textit{Gap.} Armstrong and Kolesar analyze scalar linear functionals, while Cattaneo, Feng, Palomba, and Titiunik do not compare a staggered simultaneous Bonferroni SOCP with the exact robust deterministic linear-rectangle frontier or establish the sharp spectral order. \textit{Consumer.} Practitioners can use the tractable SOCP with a proved finite-sample frontier guarantee and can interpret the condition-number factor using the matching common-donor lower family; this does not assert coverage for an existing software estimator.

\begin{proposition}[prop:nested-support-boundary]\label{prop:nested-support-boundary}
The horizon-adaptive feasible sets contain their deepest-pool counterparts, so \(W_{\mathrm{nested}}\le W_{\mathrm{deep}}\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\) for every admissible fixed design.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix an admissible design and a coordinate \(e=(j,h)\in\mathcal E\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal E\), \(0\leq h\leq K\).  If \(i\in D_{jK}\), then the definition of the donor sets gives
\[
 T_i>T_j+K\geq T_j+h,
\]
so \(i\in D_{jh}\).  Hence
\[
 D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}
 \qquad\text{for every }(j,h)\in\mathcal E. \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} ensures that \(D_{jK}\) is nonempty.  Let \(q\in\Delta(D_{jK})\).  Then \(q_i\geq0\) for every \(i\), \(q_i=0\) for \(i\notin D_{jK}\), and \(\sum_{i\in D_{jK}}q_i=1\).  By (1), \(q_i=0\) for every \(i\in D_{jh}\setminus D_{jK}\) as well as for every \(i\notin D_{jh}\), and therefore
\[
 \sum_{i\in D_{jh}}q_i
 =\sum_{i\in D_{jK}}q_i
  +\sum_{i\in D_{jh}\setminus D_{jK}}q_i
 =1.
\]
Thus \(q\in\Delta(D_{jh})\), proving
\[
 \Delta(D_{jK})\subseteq\Delta(D_{jh})
 \qquad(e=(j,h)\in\mathcal E). \tag{2}
\]
Taking the product of (2) over the finite coordinate set \(\mathcal E\), according to the definition of \(\mathcal F_s\), yields
\[
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{deep}}
 =\prod_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}\Delta(D_{jK})
 \subseteq
 \prod_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}\Delta(D_{jh})
 =\mathcal F_{\mathrm{nested}}. \tag{3}
\]

For the exact frontier, let \(\mathcal H_s\) be the set of all pairs \((w,\ell)\) feasible in \texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} under support rule \(s\).  Apart from the condition \(w\in\mathcal F_s\), all requirements defining \(\mathcal H_s\) are identical for the two rules: \(\ell\in[0,\infty)^M\); both \(w\) and \(\ell\) depend only on the fixed inputs; and
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}}
 P\!\left(
   \left|\widehat\tau_e(w)-\tau_e\right|\leq\ell_e
   \text{ for every }e\in\mathcal E
 \right)\geq1-\alpha. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, neither the model class, the target \(\tau\), the estimator evaluated at a fixed numerical weight array, nor condition (4) changes when that same array is regarded as nested-feasible.  Consequently, (3) implies
\[
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{deep}}\subseteq\mathcal H_{\mathrm{nested}}. \tag{5}
\]
The objective \((w,\ell)\mapsto\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e\) is also common to the two problems.  Monotonicity of the infimum under the feasible-set inclusion (5), applied to \texttt{def:exact-width-frontier}, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}
 &=\inf_{(w,\ell)\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{nested}}}
       \max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e \\
 &\leq\inf_{(w,\ell)\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{deep}}}
       \max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e
 =W_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This comparison also covers an empty honesty-feasible set under the extended-value convention \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\) used by the codomain of \(W_s\).

For the conic frontier, let \(\mathcal S_s\) be the feasible set of quadruples \((w,u,v,g)\) in \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp}.  If \((w,u,v,g)\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{deep}}\), then (3) makes \(w\) nested-feasible.  Moreover, for each \(e=(j,h)\), the coordinates of this deep-feasible \(w_e\) outside \(D_{jK}\) vanish.  Since \(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\), the predictor sum \(\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\) and the deduplicated contrast row \(a_{jh}(w)\) have exactly the same numerical values when \(w\) is viewed as an element of either feasible set.  Thus each of the common constraints
\[
 \left\|x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right\|_2\leq u_{jh},
 \qquad
 \left\|R_Qa_{jh}(w)\right\|_2\leq v_{jh},
 \qquad
 Lu_{jh}+\delta+z_Bv_{jh}\leq g \tag{7}
\]
remains satisfied.  It follows that
\[
 \mathcal S_{\mathrm{deep}}\subseteq\mathcal S_{\mathrm{nested}}. \tag{8}
\]
The scalar objective \(g\) is the same in both programs.  Therefore (8) and the minimizations in \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} imply
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 =\min_{(w,u,v,g)\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{nested}}}g
 \leq
 \min_{(w,u,v,g)\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{deep}}}g
 =\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (6) and (9) prove both asserted boundary inequalities.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is a sanity and boundary corollary of feasible-set inclusion, included without novelty credit. \textit{Gap.} No literature gap is claimed for this tautological feasible-set implication; BenMichaelFellerRothstein2022 supplies only the shrinking-donor-set and common-support geometry anchor. \textit{Consumer.} The boundary check lets \texttt{augsynth::multisynth} verify that enabling horizon-adaptive support cannot worsen the declared honest-width objective.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-gap-sufficient]\label{prop:strict-gap-sufficient}
Every valid \(\mathsf{GapCert}\) implies \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\). Exact algebraic feasibility and dual feasibility imply weak primal and dual bounds, and \(\sup I_N<\inf I_D\) makes the inequality strict even on degenerate active faces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let a valid \(\mathsf{GapCert}\) be given, and put
\[
 p_N:=c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N,
 \qquad
 q_D:=d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D.
\]
By \texttt{def:gap-certificate},
\[
 B_{\mathrm{nested}}y_N=d_{\mathrm{nested}},
 \qquad y_N\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nested}}.
\]
Thus \(y_N\) is feasible for the nested standard-form program of
\texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}.  Since that program has value
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\), weak minimization gives
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\leq p_N. \tag{1}
\]

We next verify explicitly that every feasible vector of the original deep
program lies in the terminal face recorded by the certificate.  Let \(y\)
satisfy
\[
 B_{\mathrm{deep}}y=d_{\mathrm{deep}},
 \qquad y\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{deep}}=F_0.
\]
Suppose inductively that \(y\in F_{k-1}\).  The exposing identities in
\texttt{def:facial-dual} give
\[
 z_k=B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\eta_k,
 \qquad d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\eta_k=0,
 \qquad F_k=F_{k-1}\cap z_k^\perp.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 z_k^{\top}y
 =\eta_k^{\top}B_{\mathrm{deep}}y
 =\eta_k^{\top}d_{\mathrm{deep}}
 =0,
\]
so \(y\in F_k\).  Induction yields
\[
 y\in F_r=F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{2}
\]
The certificate's relative-interior point \(y^\circ\) also shows that the
deep feasible set is nonempty.

The facial-dual part of \texttt{def:gap-certificate} gives
\[
 s_D:=c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D
 \in F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}^*.
\]
For every deep feasible \(y\), (2), the definition of the dual cone, and the
deep affine equality imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}y-q_D
 &=\bigl(c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D\bigr)^{\top}y
   +\lambda_D^{\top}
      \bigl(B_{\mathrm{deep}}y-d_{\mathrm{deep}}\bigr)\\
 &=s_D^{\top}y\geq0.
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Taking the infimum in (3), and using the objective-preserving encoding in
\texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}, gives the facial-dual weak bound
\[
 q_D\leq\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{4}
\]
This step uses only membership in the terminal face and its dual; it does not
use ordinary Slater feasibility, strict complementarity, or an optimizer-rank
condition.

Finally, the two certified interval inclusions in
\texttt{def:gap-certificate} and their strict separation give
\[
 p_N\leq\sup I_N<\inf I_D\leq q_D. \tag{5}
\]
Combining (1), (5), and (4) yields
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \leq p_N<q_D
 \leq\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}},
\]
and hence
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\).
All affine equalities and cone memberships used above are exact algebraic
relations.  Outward rational rounding occurs only in the enclosures in (5).
The proof therefore remains valid when a donor weight is zero, an affine
constraint is redundant, or an orthant or Lorentz block lies in a proper
face.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite record yields a safe numerical strict-improvement report without trusting floating-point objective differences or ordinary Slater conditions. \textit{Gap.} Generic conic solvers report toleranced KKT residuals; Pataki2013 supplies facial strong duality but not this support-comparison certificate with exact algebraic and interval verification. \textit{Consumer.} A solver can return a machine-checkable precision-gain flag rather than a tolerance-sensitive comparison of two objective values.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-witness-gap]\label{prop:strict-witness-gap}
For \(\mathsf I_*\), equal early nested weights \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) and equal deepest-pool weights \((1/2,1/2)\) are optimal. The late contrast variance is \(3/200\), while the optimized early variances are \(4/3\) under nested support and \(3/2\) under deep support. Hence \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}=3\sqrt3/2=9/(2\sqrt3)\in[2.5980,2.5981]\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}=9\sqrt6/8\in[2.7556,2.7557]\), so \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\in[0.1575,0.1577]\). The fixed-pool comparator excludes unit \(2\) from the early contrast solely because that unit adopts at the later horizon, whereas the nested rule uses its valid untreated outcome at time \(2\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In \(\mathsf I_*\), \(L=\delta=0\) and \(x_i=0\), so the predictor-norm
constraints do not affect the objective.  By
\texttt{def:bonferroni-socp}, minimizing the epigraph variables first gives
\[
 \Gamma_s=z_B\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}
 \max_{h\in\{0,1\}}\lVert R_Qa_{1h}(w)\rVert_2. \tag{1}
\]
At calendar time \(2\), every relevant diagonal entry of \(R_Q\) is one.
Under nested support, write the early weights as
\((w_2,w_3,w_4)\).  The focal coordinate \((1,2)\) is distinct from the
three donor coordinates and has coefficient one.  Hence
\[
 \lVert R_Qa_{1,0}(w)\rVert_2^2
 =1+w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2. \tag{2}
\]
The simplex restrictions state separately that \(w_i\geq0\) and
\(w_2+w_3+w_4=1\).  Therefore
\[
 0\leq (w_2-w_3)^2+(w_2-w_4)^2+(w_3-w_4)^2
 =3(w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2)-1. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the sum of squares is at least \(1/3\), with equality if and only if
\(w_2=w_3=w_4=1/3\).  The optimized early nested variance is consequently
\(4/3\), and the displayed equal weights are its unique minimizer.

The deep early support is \(D_{1K}=\{3,4\}\).  For
\(w_3,w_4\geq0\) and \(w_3+w_4=1\),
\[
 \lVert R_Qa_{1,0}(w)\rVert_2^2
 =1+w_3^2+w_4^2
 =\frac32+\frac12(w_3-w_4)^2\geq\frac32. \tag{4}
\]
Equality holds if and only if \((w_3,w_4)=(1/2,1/2)\).

At time \(3\), both rules use \(D_{1,1}=D_{1K}=\{3,4\}\), and every
relevant diagonal entry of \(R_Q\) is \(1/10\).  Consequently,
\[
 \lVert R_Qa_{1,1}(w)\rVert_2^2
 =\frac1{100}(1+w_3^2+w_4^2)
 =\frac3{200}+\frac1{200}(w_3-w_4)^2
 \geq\frac3{200}, \tag{5}
\]
again with equality exactly at equal weights.  The positive early standard
deviations \(\sqrt{4/3}\) and \(\sqrt{3/2}\) both exceed the common late
standard deviation \(\sqrt{3/200}\).  Because \(\mathcal F_s\) is a product
of the two horizon-specific simplexes, the separate minimizers just found can
be selected simultaneously and minimize the maximum in (1).  Substitution of
the declared value \(z_B=9/4\) gives
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 =\frac94\sqrt{\frac43}
 =\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}
 =\frac9{2\sqrt3},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 =\frac94\sqrt{\frac32}
 =\frac{9\sqrt6}{8}. \tag{6}
\]

The numerical enclosures require only exact rational squaring of positive
endpoints:
\[
 2.5980^2=6.749604<\frac{27}{4}<6.75012361=2.5981^2
\]
and
\[
 2.7556^2=7.59333136<\frac{243}{32}<7.59388249=2.7557^2.
\]
Thus
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\in[2.5980,2.5981],
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\in[2.7556,2.7557].
\]
Subtracting outward endpoints yields
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \in[2.7556-2.5981,\,2.7557-2.5980]
 =[0.1575,0.1577]. \tag{7}
\]
Finally, \(T_2=3\) implies \(2\in D_{1,0}\), because \(3>2\), whereas
\(2\notin D_{1,1}=D_{1K}\), because \(3\not>3\).  Hence the nested early
rule uses unit \(2\)'s untreated time-\(2\) outcome, while the common deep
pool excludes unit \(2\) at the early horizon solely because it adopts at the
late horizon.  Every donor simplex used above is nonempty, every variance is
strictly positive, and no limiting argument is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A rational staggered instance gives an interpretable, interval-certified positive SOCP gap; the separate exact-frontier proposition proves that the same instance also has a strict \(W_s\) gap. \textit{Gap.} Ben-Michael, Feller, and Rothstein motivate the shrinking donor geometry and focus their exposition and application on the deepest pool, while explicitly allowing horizon-varying donors; this proposition does not evaluate their estimator or borrow its statistical class. \textit{Consumer.} The instance is a deterministic SOCP regression test for \(\texttt{scpi}\) and explains when an early not-yet-treated cohort carries precision that a common support discards.

\begin{proposition}[prop:support-equality-reduction]\label{prop:support-equality-reduction}
If \(D_{jh}=D_{jK}\) for every \((j,h)\in\mathcal E\), then \(W_{\mathrm{nested}}=W_{\mathrm{deep}}\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume that \(D_{jh}=D_{jK}\) for every \((j,h)\in\mathcal E\).  By the definition of the two support-restricted feasible sets, for each \((j,h)\in\mathcal E\),
\[
 \Delta(D_{jh})=\Delta(D_{jK}).
\]
The set \(\mathcal E\) is finite because \(\mathcal J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) and \(h\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\).  Taking the finite Cartesian product of the preceding equalities therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{nested}}
 =\prod_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}\Delta(D_{jh})
 =\prod_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}\Delta(D_{jK})
 =\mathcal F_{\mathrm{deep}}.
 \tag{1}
\]

For \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\), let \(\mathcal B_s\) denote the set of all pairs \((w,\ell)\) satisfying the constraints in \texttt{def:exact-width-frontier}.  In that definition, the support label \(s\) enters only through the constraint \(w\in\mathcal F_s\).  The model class, the deterministic-outcome restriction on \(w\) and \(\ell\), the estimator \(\widehat\tau(w)\), the causal target \(\tau\), and the uniform joint-coverage constraint are identical under the two labels.  Hence (1) implies
\[
 \mathcal B_{\mathrm{nested}}=\mathcal B_{\mathrm{deep}}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Both exact-frontier problems apply the same objective \((w,\ell)\mapsto\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e\) to these sets.  Thus \texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} and (2) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}
 &=\inf_{(w,\ell)\in\mathcal B_{\mathrm{nested}}}
     \max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e\\
 &=\inf_{(w,\ell)\in\mathcal B_{\mathrm{deep}}}
     \max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e
 =W_{\mathrm{deep}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]
This equality also holds under the extended-value convention if the common admissible set is empty.

Similarly, for each support label \(s\), let \(\mathcal S_s\) be the set of tuples \((w,u,v,g)\) satisfying the constraints in \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp}.  Again the only label-dependent constraint is \(w\in\mathcal F_s\); the predictor-balance norms, Gaussian-standard-error norms, scalar envelope inequalities, fixed constants, and coordinate set are otherwise the same.  Equation (1) consequently gives
\[
 \mathcal S_{\mathrm{nested}}=\mathcal S_{\mathrm{deep}}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Since the two programs minimize the same scalar objective \(g\), \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} and (4) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 &=\min_{(w,u,v,g)\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{nested}}}g\\
 &=\min_{(w,u,v,g)\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{deep}}}g
 =\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) prove the two asserted equalities.  The standing condition \(\mathcal J\ne\varnothing\) is used only because both frontier constructions are stated on such inputs.  No covariance rank or eigenvalue bound, positivity beyond the definitions' domains, overlap condition, panel-boundary convention, Gaussian distributional property, or limiting argument is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This exact reduction is a mandatory sanity check for designs without changing donor eligibility. \textit{Gap.} The equality is implicit in any fixed-support formulation and pins down the boundary of the new width comparison. \textit{Consumer.} Software regression tests can verify that enabling nested support leaves fixed-pool output unchanged when donor sets coincide.

\begin{proposition}[prop:scalar-gaussian-reduction]\label{prop:scalar-gaussian-reduction}
When \(M=1\), \(\delta=0\), and the chosen simplex weights exactly balance predictors, the SOCP band reduces to the certified known-covariance Gaussian interval \(\widehat\tau(w)\pm z_B\operatorname{sd}(w)\). Rational multipliers can approach \(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\) arbitrarily closely from above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(M=1\), \(\delta=0\), and let the selected feasible simplex weight
\(w\) exactly balance the predictors, so that
\[
 x_j-\sum_iw_ix_i=0.
 \tag{1}
\]
The definition of the exact bias radius gives
\[
 b(w)=L\left\lVert x_j-\sum_iw_ix_i\right\rVert_2+\delta=0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Substitution of (2) into the band formula in
\texttt{thm:socp-coverage} leaves
\[
 \widehat\tau(w)\ \pm\ z_B\operatorname{sd}(w).
 \tag{3}
\]
Because \(R_Q\) is full rank and the focal coefficient of the contrast is
one, \(\operatorname{sd}(w)>0\).  Thus (3) is precisely the usual
known-covariance Gaussian interval form, with the supplied certified
critical value.  No misspecification or predictor-imbalance term remains.

It remains to prove the approximation assertion about certified rational
multipliers.  Put
\[
 z_\star=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2).
 \tag{4}
\]
Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\), one has \(1-\alpha/2>1/2\), and hence
\(z_\star>0\).  Let \(\varepsilon>0\).  Density of the rationals in the
reals gives a positive \(q\in\mathbb Q\) such that
\[
 z_\star<q<z_\star+\min\{\varepsilon,1\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Strict monotonicity of the normal distribution function and (4)--(5) imply
\[
 2\{1-\Phi(q)\}<\alpha,
 \qquad
 q<\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)+1.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus \(q\) satisfies both strict inequalities required of \(z_B\) when
\(M=1\), and \(0<q-z_\star<\varepsilon\).

We finally verify that the word ``certified'' in the claim imposes no hidden
analytic assumption.  For every nonnegative rational \(y\),
\[
 \Phi(y)=\frac12+\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
             \int_0^y e^{-u^2/2}\,du.
 \tag{7}
\]
The identity
\(\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)\), together with the alternating
rational power series for \(\arctan\), gives nested rational intervals for
\(\pi\) whose widths tend to zero.  Rational bisection and reciprocation then
give such intervals for \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\).  On the rational compact interval
\([0,y]\), truncating the Taylor polynomial of \(e^{-v}\) after degree
\(n\) has remainder bounded in absolute value by
\(v^{n+1}/(n+1)!\).  Substituting \(v=u^2/2\), integrating the rational
polynomial, and integrating this uniform rational remainder bound gives
outward rational lower and upper bounds for the integral in (7), with width
tending to zero.  Hence \(\Phi(y)\) admits arbitrarily sharp, finite,
outward-rounded rational certificates.

Apply this construction at \(y=q\).  The first strict gap in (6) ensures
that a sufficiently refined lower bound for \(\Phi(q)\) certifies
\(2\{1-\Phi(q)\}<\alpha\).  For the second inequality, if \(q\leq1\), it
follows immediately from \(z_\star>0\).  If \(q>1\), (5) gives
\(q-1<z_\star\), equivalently
\[
 \Phi(q-1)<1-\alpha/2,
\]
and a sufficiently refined upper rational bound at the nonnegative rational
argument \(q-1\) certifies this strict inequality.  Thus both certificates
are finite and outward rounded.  Since \(\varepsilon\) was arbitrary,
certified rational choices of \(z_B\) approach
\(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\) arbitrarily closely from above.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The scalar balanced special case must recover a certified version of the classical known-covariance Gaussian interval. \textit{Gap.} ArmstrongKolesar2018 contains the scalar known-variance Gaussian benchmark; this proposition checks compatibility rather than claiming novelty. \textit{Consumer.} The reduction supplies a transparent unit test for single-effect implementations.

\begin{theorem}[thm:degenerate-iff-gap-certificate]\label{thm:degenerate-iff-gap-certificate}
For every finite rational input \(\mathsf{Data}\),
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{a valid finite }\mathsf{GapCert}\text{ exists}.
\]
There is an exact terminating construction and verifier: a finite disjunction of semialgebraic facial-reduction systems is decided by effective real-closed-field quantifier elimination, algebraic witnesses are represented in \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm{rep}}\), and rational objective enclosures are enumerated until they separate.  Outward-rounded rational arithmetic is used only for the two objective enclosures.  The equivalence and terminating algorithm require neither unique optimizers, positive donor weights, independent predictor columns, ordinary Slater feasibility, nor strict complementarity, and they cover tight and collapsed Lorentz-cone blocks.  No quantitative bit-complexity bound is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(P_s\) for the standard-form conic program supplied by \texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}.  We first handle infeasibility.  For every \(j\in\mathcal J\) and \(0\leq h\leq K\), the donor-set definition gives
\[
 D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh},                                                \tag{1}
\]
because \(T_i>T_j+K\) implies \(T_i>T_j+h\).  If \(P_{\mathrm{nested}}\) is feasible, its horizon-\(K\) coordinate lies in \(\Delta(D_{jK})\) for every \(j\), so every deepest donor simplex is nonempty.  Choosing an element of each \(\Delta(D_{jK})\) and reusing it at all horizons makes \(P_{\mathrm{deep}}\) feasible.  Conversely, (1) embeds every deep-feasible weight array in \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{nested}}\).  Given feasible weights, the norm epigraph variables and nonnegative scalar slacks in \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} can all be chosen finite.  Therefore
\[
 P_{\mathrm{nested}}\text{ is feasible}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P_{\mathrm{deep}}\text{ is feasible}.                                \tag{2}
\]
If (2) fails, both values are \(+\infty\), so strict inequality is false.  A valid \(\mathsf{GapCert}\) is also impossible, because its \(y_N\) is nested-primal feasible and its \(y^\circ\) is deep-primal feasible.  This proves the equivalence in the infeasible case.

Assume henceforth that the equivalent feasibility conditions in (2) hold.  For fixed feasible weights, eliminating the epigraph variables gives
\[
 G_s(w)=\max_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{
 L\left\|x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right\|_2+
 \delta+z_B\left\|R_Qa_{jh}(w)\right\|_2
 \right\}.                                                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the smallest admissible \(u_{jh}\) and \(v_{jh}\) are the corresponding norms, and the smallest admissible \(g\) is their finite maximum.  Every nonempty donor simplex is compact, so the finite product \(\mathcal F_s\) is compact.  The affine maps, Euclidean norms, and finite maximum in (3) are continuous.  Hence
\[
 \Gamma_s=\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}G_s(w)<+\infty,                       \tag{4}
\]
and the minimum is attained.  Restoring the norm values, scalar slacks, and the standard-form variables gives an attained optimizer of \(P_s\).  This step uses no division or rank condition and permits zero donor weights and nonunique minimizers.

Suppose now that
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                     \tag{5}
\]
Choose a nested primal optimizer \(y_N^*\), which exists by (4).  Abbreviate
\[
 B_D=B_{\mathrm{deep}},\quad d_D=d_{\mathrm{deep}},\quad
 \mathcal K_D=\mathcal K_{\mathrm{deep}},\quad
 H_D=\{y:B_Dy=d_D\},\quad S=H_D\cap\mathcal K_D.
\]
The set \(S\) is nonempty by (2).  Starting from \(F_0=\mathcal K_D\), construct faces inductively.  Suppose that \(F_k\) is a closed face of \(\mathcal K_D\) containing \(S\), set \(L_k=\operatorname{span}(F_k)\), and define
\[
 H_k=\{y\in L_k:B_Dy=d_D\}.                                            \tag{6}
\]
Then \(H_k\cap F_k=S\), so this intersection is nonempty.  If \(H_k\cap\operatorname{ri}F_k\ne\varnothing\), stop.  Otherwise, \texttt{lem:affine-facial-reduction-alternative} supplies \(\eta_{k+1}\) and \(z_{k+1}=B_D^{\top}\eta_{k+1}\) such that
\[
 z_{k+1}\in F_k^*,\qquad
 d_D^{\top}\eta_{k+1}=0,\qquad
 z_{k+1}\notin F_k^\perp,                                              \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 F_{k+1}=F_k\cap z_{k+1}^\perp                                         \tag{8}
\]
is a strict closed face containing \(S\).  Thus every step satisfies exactly the affine exposure identities in \texttt{def:facial-dual}; no arbitrary exposing vector has been substituted for an affine multiplier.

Every strict face inclusion lowers dimension.  Consequently, after at most \(\dim\mathcal K_D\) steps the construction stops at a face \(F_r\) and a point
\[
 y^\circ\in S\cap\operatorname{ri}F_r.                                \tag{9}
\]
We verify rather than assume that \(F_r\) is the minimal face containing \(S\).  Let \(G\) be any face of \(\mathcal K_D\) containing \(S\).  Then \(y^\circ\in G\cap\operatorname{ri}F_r\).  For every \(x\in F_r\), relative interior gives an \(\varepsilon>0\) for which
\[
 q=y^\circ+\varepsilon(y^\circ-x)\in F_r.
\]
Since
\[
 y^\circ=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}x
          +\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}q,                                   \tag{10}
\]
and both \(x,q\) lie in \(\mathcal K_D\), the face property of \(G\) implies \(x,q\in G\).  Hence \(F_r\subseteq G\).  On the other hand, every face in the constructed chain contains \(S\), so the minimal feasible face is contained in \(F_r\).  Therefore
\[
 F_r=F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}.                                           \tag{11}
\]

The feasible set is unchanged when \(\mathcal K_D\) is replaced by (11), and (9) is relative Slater feasibility on that face.  Since the deep value is finite by (4), the attained strong-duality conclusion of \texttt{lem:facial-reduction-relative-strong-duality} gives a facial-dual optimizer \(\lambda_D^*\) with
\[
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_D^{\top}\lambda_D^*
       \in F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}^*,
 \qquad
 d_D^{\top}\lambda_D^*=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                       \tag{12}
\]
Nested optimality, (5), and (12) yield
\[
 c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N^*
 <d_D^{\top}\lambda_D^*.                                               \tag{13}
\]

We next show that the witnesses can be constructed and checked exactly.  All constraints in (6)--(13) are semialgebraic over the rational input.  Orthant and zero-cone membership are given by linear signs and equalities, while Lorentz-cone membership is
\[
 (t,z)\in\mathcal L_q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 t\geq0\ \text{ and }\ t^2-\sum_{\nu=1}^{q-1}z_\nu^2\geq0.          \tag{14}
\]
The needed faces also have finite semialgebraic parameterizations.  A face of a nonnegative orthant is specified by the coordinates fixed at zero.  For \(q\geq2\), every nonzero proper face of \(\mathcal L_q\) is a boundary ray: a proper face cannot contain an interior point, and two noncollinear nonzero boundary points have a strict-triangle-inequality midpoint in the interior.  A boundary ray is parameterized by \((1,u)\) with \(\|u\|_2=1\); the zero and full faces are the remaining cases.  When \(q=1\), only the zero and full faces occur.  A face of a finite product is a product of faces.  To see this for two factors, decompose every \((x_1,x_2)\) in a face as \((x_1,0)+(0,x_2)\); the face property places both summands in the face, while closure under addition supplies every product of such coordinate summands.  Iteration handles finitely many factors.

It follows that face membership, dual-face membership, relative-interior membership, strict inclusion, and (8) are expressible by finite first-order formulas whose atoms are rational polynomial sign conditions.  Clearing finitely many denominators gives integer-coefficient atoms.  Chain length is bounded by the ambient conic dimension.  For each possible \(r\), let \(\Psi_r\) be the resulting semialgebraic sentence asserting the existence of face parameters, \(z_k,\eta_k,y^\circ,y_N,\lambda_D\) satisfying the identities in \texttt{def:facial-dual}, nested primal feasibility, facial-dual feasibility, and
\[
 c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N
 <d_D^{\top}\lambda_D.                                                  \tag{15}
\]
Equations (6)--(13) prove that (5) implies \(\bigvee_r\Psi_r\).

Conversely, assume some \(\Psi_r\) holds.  The affine exposure identities imply inductively that every deep-feasible point lies in every \(F_k\), and the asserted terminal relative-interior feasible point proves by the argument in (10)--(11) that \(F_r\) is the minimal feasible face.  If
\[
 s_D=c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_D^{\top}\lambda_D\in F_r^*,
\]
then every deep-feasible \(y_D\) satisfies
\[
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}y_D-d_D^{\top}\lambda_D
 =s_D^{\top}y_D\geq0.                                                   \tag{16}
\]
Together with nested primal feasibility and (15), this gives
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \leq c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N
 <d_D^{\top}\lambda_D
 \leq\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                                           \tag{17}
\]
Thus
\[
 \bigvee_r\Psi_r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                     \tag{18}
\]

By \texttt{lem:effective-real-algebraic-elimination}, effective real-closed-field quantifier elimination decides the finite disjunction in (18).  Let \(\mathbb A=\overline{\mathbb Q}\cap\mathbb R\), the real closure of \(\mathbb Q\) inside \(\mathbb R\).  Each \(\Psi_r\) is a sentence in the language of ordered fields with rational coefficients.  Quantifier elimination therefore gives the same quantifier-free rational sentence over the real closed fields \(\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbb A\).  Consequently, if a disjunct has witnesses over \(\mathbb R\), it has witnesses whose every coordinate lies in \(\mathbb A\).  Enumerate fairly all finite tuples of \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm{rep}}\) of the required arity, where each entry is encoded by a square-free integer polynomial, a rational isolating interval, and a root index.  For each tuple, exact real-algebraic arithmetic and sign determination verify the root encodings and every polynomial equality, weak inequality, and strict inequality in \(\Psi_r\).  Because every element of \(\mathbb A\) has such a representation, a true disjunct's algebraic witness tuple eventually occurs and is accepted.  If the disjunction is false, quantifier elimination terminates and correctly reports that no witnesses exist.  Thus (18) gives an exact terminating construction and decision procedure, with no complexity-rate assertion.  The sign-condition samples of Theorem 13.11, which lie in \(R\langle\varepsilon,\delta\rangle^k\), are not used for this witness extraction.

For a true disjunct, put
\[
 p_N=c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N,
 \qquad q_D=d_D^{\top}\lambda_D.
\]
These are algebraic numbers and (15) gives \(p_N<q_D\).  Enumerate rational quadruples \((\ell_N,u_N,\ell_D,u_D)\) and retain the first one for which exact real-closed-field checking verifies
\[
 \ell_N\leq p_N\leq u_N<\ell_D\leq q_D\leq u_D.                       \tag{19}
\]
This enumeration terminates.  Indeed, with \(\varepsilon=(q_D-p_N)/3>0\), the Archimedean property supplies a positive integer \(m\) with \(1/m<\varepsilon\); rational grid points of mesh \(1/m\) around \(p_N\) and \(q_D\) then give outer intervals whose inner endpoints are strictly separated.  Setting \(I_N=[\ell_N,u_N]\) and \(I_D=[\ell_D,u_D]\) produces
\[
 c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N\in I_N,
 \qquad d_D^{\top}\lambda_D\in I_D,
 \qquad \sup I_N<\inf I_D.                                             \tag{20}
\]
These two recorded enclosures are the only quantities for which outward-rounded rational interval arithmetic is used.  The chain, affine identities, cone signs, relative-interior condition, root data, and dual feasibility are checked exactly.

The algebraic witnesses and (20) form a valid finite \(\mathsf{GapCert}\).  This proves certificate existence from the strict gap.  The reverse implication is \texttt{prop:strict-gap-sufficient}; it also follows directly from (17) and (20).  A verifier terminates because every face, affine, cone, relative-interior, algebraic-root, and enclosure condition is a finite real-closed-field sign question.  Zero donor weights are orthant-boundary points, redundant predictor columns only repeat polynomial constraints, and the face classification above includes full, ray, and zero Lorentz faces, including collapsed blocks.  No uniqueness, positive-weight, rank, ordinary Slater, strict-complementarity, boundary exclusion, or limiting assumption has been introduced, and no quantitative bit-complexity bound is claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Strict improvement of the fixed-Bonferroni SOCP must remain decidable and independently checkable on degenerate cone faces. The in-house affine facial-reduction alternative proves that every reduction step has a multiplier of the required form; relative-interior strong duality then yields the attained facial dual used by the finite certificate. The construction and verifier terminate without any quantitative runtime claim. \textit{Consumer.} An implementer or auditor can verify \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\) from finite rational design inputs even when the original conic program lacks ordinary Slater feasibility. The certificate concerns the fixed-Bonferroni SOCP surrogate; it does not alone certify strict improvement of the exact honest-width frontier \(W_s\), and it gives no runtime guarantee for \texttt{scpi} or \texttt{augsynth::multisynth}.

\begin{lemma}[lem:effective-real-algebraic-elimination]\label{lem:effective-real-algebraic-elimination}
For a first-order formula over a real closed field whose atoms are polynomial sign conditions, effective quantifier elimination terminates and produces an equivalent quantifier-free formula.  Separately, for a finite family of \(s\) polynomials of degree at most \(D\) in \(k\) variables over a real closed field \(R\), sign-condition sampling computes real-univariate sample points in \(R\langle\varepsilon,\delta\rangle^k\) meeting every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition and computes the signs of the input polynomials at those points.  The sampled univariate representations have degree \(O(D)^k\); when the input coefficients are integers of bitsize at most \(\tau\), their coefficient bitsizes are bounded by \(\tau D^{O(k)}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:disjoint-diagonal-exact-frontier]\label{thm:disjoint-diagonal-exact-frontier}
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\).  Suppose
\(\mathcal E\ne\varnothing\), \(L=\delta=0\), \(\Sigma_Q\) is diagonal, and
the eligible row-cell sets are pairwise disjoint:
\(\mathcal C^s_e\cap\mathcal C^s_f=\varnothing\) whenever \(e\ne f\).
Then every \(v_{s,e}\) is attained and strictly positive, and \(W_s\) is the
unique positive number \(R_s\) satisfying
\[
 \prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{2\Phi\!\left(\frac{R_s}{\sqrt{v_{s,e}}}\right)-1\right\}
 =1-\alpha. \tag{8}
\]
If the displayed conditions hold for both support rules, then
\(v_{\mathrm{nested},e}\leq v_{\mathrm{deep},e}\) for every
\(e\in\mathcal E\).  If this inequality is strict for at least one
coordinate, then \(W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a support rule \(s\) and an index \(e=(j,h)\).  Assumption
\texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} gives \(D_{jK}\ne\varnothing\), while the
definition of the adoption-time donor sets and \(h\leq K\) give
\(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\).  Thus \(\Delta(D^s_e)\) is nonempty under
either rule.  It is a compact simplex.  The map
\[
 q_e(\omega)=a_e(\omega)^{\top}\Sigma_Qa_e(\omega) \tag{24}
\]
is continuous, so the minimum \(v_{s,e}\) is attained.  Because
\(\Sigma_Q\succ0\) and \(a_e(\omega)\ne0\), its focal-cell coefficient being
one,
\[
 q_e(\omega)>0\quad\text{for every }\omega\in\Delta(D^s_e),
 \qquad v_{s,e}>0. \tag{25}
\]

Under \(L=\delta=0\),
\texttt{thm:correlated-gaussian-exact-frontier} applies and gives
\[
 W_s=\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}r_\alpha
 \!\left(A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\right). \tag{26}
\]
This equality follows there from
\(\widehat\tau(w)-\tau\), not from a redefinition of the potential-outcome
target \(\tau\).

For distinct \(e,f\in\mathcal E\), row \(a_e(w)\) is supported on
\(\mathcal C^s_e\) and row \(a_f(w)\) is supported on
\(\mathcal C^s_f\).  Pairwise disjointness of these sets and diagonality of
\(\Sigma_Q\) imply
\[
 a_e(w)^{\top}\Sigma_Qa_f(w)=0. \tag{27}
\]
Consequently the centered Gaussian error covariance in (26) is diagonal,
with diagonal entry \(q_e(w_e)\) in coordinate \(e\).  Therefore, for every
\(R\geq0\),
\[
 \Pr\{\lVert Z(w)\rVert_\infty\leq R\}
 =\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{2\Phi\!\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt{q_e(w_e)}}\right)-1\right\}. \tag{28}
\]

Define
\[
 \Psi_s(R)=\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{2\Phi\!\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt{v_{s,e}}}\right)-1\right\}.
 \tag{29}
\]
The set \(\mathcal E\) is finite and nonempty and every \(v_{s,e}\) is
positive.  Hence \(\Psi_s\) is continuous and strictly increasing on
\([0,\infty)\), satisfies \(\Psi_s(0)=0\), and converges to one as
\(R\to\infty\).  Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\), there is a unique positive
\(R_s\) for which \(\Psi_s(R_s)=1-\alpha\).

For \(R>0\) and \(v>0\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dv}\left\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt v)-1\right\}
 =-Rv^{-3/2}\varphi(R/\sqrt v)<0. \tag{30}
\]
Every feasible coordinate weight satisfies \(q_e(w_e)\geq v_{s,e}\).
Equations (28)--(30) therefore show that at any fixed positive \(R\), the
largest feasible cube probability is \(\Psi_s(R)\).  Because
\(\mathcal F_s\) is the Cartesian product of the coordinate simplexes, this
upper bound is attained by choosing, independently for every \(e\), a weight
that attains \(v_{s,e}\).  Thus the minimum in (26) equals \(R_s\), proving
\[
 W_s=R_s
\]
and the product-root characterization (8).

Now suppose the displayed subclass conditions hold for both support rules.
For every \(e=(j,h)\),
\[
 D^{\mathrm{deep}}_e=D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}
 =D^{\mathrm{nested}}_e,
\]
so minimization of the same quadratic (24) over the two simplexes gives
\[
 v_{\mathrm{nested},e}\leq v_{\mathrm{deep},e}
 \qquad(e\in\mathcal E). \tag{31}
\]
Let \(\bar w_D\) and \(\bar w_N\) collect coordinatewise
variance-minimizing weights under the deep and nested rules, respectively.
The product argument above proves that \(\bar w_D\) is a deep minimizer in
(26).  Equations (27) and (31) give
\[
 V(\bar w_N)=\operatorname{diag}
   (v_{\mathrm{nested},e}:e\in\mathcal E)
 \preceq
 \operatorname{diag}
   (v_{\mathrm{deep},e}:e\in\mathcal E)
 =V(\bar w_D). \tag{32}
\]
If (31) is strict for at least one coordinate, the two matrices in (32) are
unequal.  The strict covariance criterion in
\texttt{thm:correlated-gaussian-exact-frontier} then yields
\[
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{33}
\]
All compactness and positivity statements are finite-dimensional; no rank
condition on predictors, interiority of a simplex minimizer, boundary
regularization, or asymptotic limit is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies an exact, computable frontier on a transparent observable subclass and proves when a strict coordinatewise variance gain forces a strict simultaneous-width gain. \textit{Gap.} The donor geometry comes from the staggered design; the Gaussian product-root characterization is specific to this note's bounded-mean known-covariance experiment. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can solve one monotone scalar equation after coordinatewise simplex variance minimization and use the result as an exact calibration check.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-witness-exact-frontier-gap]\label{prop:strict-witness-exact-frontier-gap}
For \(\mathsf I_*\), the coordinatewise minimum-variance vectors are
\[
 (v_{\mathrm{nested},(1,0)},v_{\mathrm{nested},(1,1)})
 =\left(\frac43,\frac3{200}\right),
 \qquad
 (v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,0)},v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,1)})
 =\left(\frac32,\frac3{200}\right).
\]
The exact frontiers are the unique positive roots of
\[
 \left\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt{v_{s,(1,0)}})-1\right\}
 \left\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt{3/200})-1\right\}=\frac{19}{20}.
\]
Since \(4/3<3/2\), \texttt{thm:disjoint-diagonal-exact-frontier} gives \(W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}\). Thus the witness has a strict exact-frontier gain as well as the strict fixed-Bonferroni SOCP gain in \texttt{prop:strict-witness-gap}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The definition of \(\mathsf I_*\) gives \(\mathcal E=\{(1,0),(1,1)\}\), so
the index set is nonempty.  Under nested support the eligible row-cell sets
are
\[
 \mathcal C^{\mathrm{nested}}_{(1,0)}
 =\{(1,2),(2,2),(3,2),(4,2)\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C^{\mathrm{nested}}_{(1,1)}
 =\{(1,3),(3,3),(4,3)\}.
\]
Under deep support the first set loses only \((2,2)\), while the second set is
unchanged.  In either rule, the two sets are disjoint because their calendar
times differ.  Moreover, \(\Sigma_Q=R_Q^{\top}R_Q\) is diagonal, with
diagonal entries one on the time-\(2\) cells and \(1/100\) on the time-\(3\)
cells; its smallest and largest eigenvalues are therefore respectively
\(1/100\) and one.  The remaining subclass conditions
\(L=\delta=0\) are part of the witness definition.

The variance minimizations in \texttt{prop:strict-witness-gap}, together with
\texttt{def:coordinate-minimum-variance}, give
\[
 v_{\mathrm{nested},(1,0)}=\frac43,
 \quad v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,0)}=\frac32,
 \quad
 v_{\mathrm{nested},(1,1)}=v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,1)}=\frac3{200}. \tag{16}
\]
The hypotheses of \texttt{thm:disjoint-diagonal-exact-frontier} now hold for
both rules.  Substituting (16) and \(1-\alpha=19/20\) in (8) gives the two
displayed root equations.  Since \(4/3<3/2\), the strict-coordinate conclusion
of that theorem proves
\[
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{17}
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition turns the exact product-root comparison into a proved positive exact-width gap for the rational witness; no numerical interval certificate is required. \textit{Gap.} No claim is made that these exact widths equal the output of the Ben-Michael--Feller--Rothstein estimator. \textit{Consumer.} The rational variance vectors and root equations provide a structural independent-Gaussian calibration check for \(\texttt{scpi}\) and \(\texttt{augsynth::multisynth}\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:anderson-ray-monotonicity]\label{lem:anderson-ray-monotonicity}
Let \(n\geq1\).  Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be convex and symmetric about the origin, and let \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) be integrable, symmetric, and have convex superlevel sets.  Then, for every \(y\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0\leq k\leq1\),
\[
 \int_C f(x+ky)\,dx\geq \int_C f(x+y)\,dx.
\]
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the precise classical moving-set inequality used to establish strict Gaussian cube comparison. \textit{Gap.} The cited theorem gives weak ray monotonicity; the strict bounded-cube conclusion needed here is derived separately. \textit{Consumer.} The strict comparison lemma uses this leaf to compare unequal covariance matrices.

\begin{lemma}[lem:strict-anderson-gaussian-cube]\label{lem:strict-anderson-gaussian-cube}
Let \(n\geq1\), \(R>0\), \(C_R=[-R,R]^n\), and \(X\sim N(0,V)\) with \(V\in\mathbb S_{++}^n\).  For every nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
 \Pr\{X+u\in C_R\}<\Pr\{X\in C_R\}.
\]
Consequently, if \(U\sim N(0,\Delta)\) is independent of \(X\), where \(\Delta\succeq0\) and \(\Delta\ne0\), then
\[
 \Pr\{X+U\in C_R\}<\Pr\{X\in C_R\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(f_V\) for the everywhere-positive density of \(N(0,V)\), and fix
\(u\ne0\).  The density is symmetric, integrable, and its nonempty
superlevel sets are centered ellipsoids, hence convex.  The cube \(C_R\) is
bounded, convex, symmetric, and has nonempty interior.  Define
\[
 h(t)=\Pr\{X+tu\in C_R\}
     =\int_{C_R}f_V(x-tu)\,dx,
 \qquad t\geq0. \tag{1}
\]
For \(0\leq s\leq t\) with \(t>0\), apply
\texttt{lem:anderson-ray-monotonicity} with \(y=-tu\) and \(k=s/t\).
It gives
\[
 h(s)=\int_{C_R}f_V(x-su)\,dx
 \geq\int_{C_R}f_V(x-tu)\,dx=h(t). \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(h\) is nonincreasing on \([0,\infty)\).

We prove that equality in (2) between zero and a positive shift is
impossible.  Put
\[
 q=u^{\top}V^{-1}u>0,
 \qquad d(x)=u^{\top}V^{-1}x.
\]
Completing the square in the Gaussian density yields, for real \(t\),
\[
 h(t)=e^{-qt^2/2}\int_{C_R}e^{t d(x)}f_V(x)\,dx. \tag{3}
\]
Because \(C_R\) is bounded, \(d\) is bounded on \(C_R\).  Consequently the
power series for the two exponentials in (3) converge uniformly on every
bounded subset of the complex \(t\)-plane and may be integrated term by
term.  Hence the right-hand side of (3) extends to an entire function of
\(t\).

If \(h(1)=h(0)\), monotonicity (2) would give \(h(t)=h(0)\) for every
\(t\in[0,1]\).  An entire function that equals a constant on a real interval
equals that constant everywhere: otherwise, at an accumulation point of its
zeros, the first nonzero coefficient of its convergent power series would
make the zero isolated.  Thus equality would force \(h(t)=h(0)\) for all
real \(t\).

This is impossible.  Let \(\lambda_{\min}(V^{-1})>0\).  Since
\(\lVert x\rVert_2\leq R\sqrt n\) on \(C_R\), for all sufficiently large
\(t\),
\[
 f_V(x-tu)
 \leq (2\pi)^{-n/2}\det(V)^{-1/2}
 exp\!\left[-\frac{\lambda_{\min}(V^{-1})}{2}
       \{t\lVert u\rVert_2-R\sqrt n\}^2\right]. \tag{4}
\]
Integrating (4) over the finite-volume cube gives \(h(t)\to0\), whereas
\(h(0)>0\) because \(f_V>0\) and \(C_R\) has positive volume.  Therefore
\[
 h(1)<h(0), \tag{5}
\]
which proves the first assertion.

For the consequence, \(\Delta\succeq0\) and \(\Delta\ne0\) imply that some
\(v\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfies \(v^{\top}\Delta v>0\).  Thus
\(v^{\top}U\) is a nondegenerate univariate normal random variable and
\[
 \Pr\{U=0\}\leq\Pr\{v^{\top}U=0\}=0. \tag{6}
\]
Conditioning on \(U\), applying (5) at every realized nonzero shift, and
using independence give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{X+U\in C_R\}
 &=\mathbb E\bigl[\Pr\{X+u\in C_R\}\vert_{u=U}\bigr]\\
 &<\Pr\{X\in C_R\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
The argument permits singular \(\Delta\); positive definiteness is required
only for \(V\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Strictness for a bounded cube turns a nonzero Loewner covariance reduction into a strict quantile reduction even when the covariance difference is singular. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary Anderson comparison is weak; the analytic equality argument rules out equality for every nonzero Gaussian shift in the setting used here. \textit{Consumer.} The correlated exact-frontier theorem uses this lemma at the deep optimizer.

\begin{theorem}[thm:correlated-gaussian-exact-frontier]\label{thm:correlated-gaussian-exact-frontier}
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\).  Suppose \(\mathcal E\ne\varnothing\) and \(L=\delta=0\).  For \(w\in\mathcal F_s\), put
\[
 V(w)=A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}.
\]
For every \(V\in\mathbb S_{++}^M\), let \(r_\alpha(V)\) be the unique positive \(R\) satisfying
\[
 \Pr_{Z\sim N(0,V)}\{\lVert Z\rVert_\infty\leq R\}=1-\alpha.
\]
Then \(r_\alpha\) is continuous on \(\mathbb S_{++}^M\), the displayed minimum below is attained, and
\[
 W_s=\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}r_\alpha\!\left(V(w)\right). \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, let \(w_D\) be a minimizer in (6) for the deep rule.  If some \(w_N\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{nested}}\) satisfies
\[
 V(w_N)\preceq V(w_D)
 \quad\text{and}\quad V(w_N)\ne V(w_D), \tag{7}
\]
then \(W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a positive-definite \(M\times M\) matrix \(V\), define
\[
 G_V(R)=\Pr_{Z\sim N(0,V)}\{Z\in[-R,R]^M\},
 \qquad R\geq0. \tag{8}
\]
The Gaussian density is everywhere positive and continuous.  It follows
that \(G_V\) is continuous, \(G_V(0)=0\), and
\(G_V(R)\to1\) as \(R\to\infty\).  If \(0\leq R_1<R_2\), the set
\([-R_2,R_2]^M\setminus[-R_1,R_1]^M\) contains a nonempty open rectangle,
so positivity of the density gives
\[
 G_V(R_1)<G_V(R_2). \tag{9}
\]
Because \(0<\alpha<1/2\), equations (8)--(9) and the intermediate-value
property give a unique \(r_\alpha(V)>0\) with
\(G_V(r_\alpha(V))=1-\alpha\).

We next verify continuity rather than assume it.  If \(V_n\to V\) in
\(\mathbb S_{++}^M\), then for every fixed \(R\), the normal densities with
covariances \(V_n\) converge pointwise on \([-R,R]^M\) to the density with
covariance \(V\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the determinants are
bounded away from zero, so those densities are bounded on the cube by one
integrable constant.  Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[
 G_{V_n}(R)\longrightarrow G_V(R). \tag{10}
\]
Write \(r=r_\alpha(V)\).  For every \(0<\varepsilon<r\), strict
monotonicity gives
\[
 G_V(r-\varepsilon)<1-\alpha<G_V(r+\varepsilon). \tag{11}
\]
By (10), both inequalities in (11) hold with \(V_n\) in place of \(V\) for
all sufficiently large \(n\).  The defining uniqueness and monotonicity of
the quantile then imply
\[
 r-\varepsilon<r_\alpha(V_n)<r+\varepsilon. \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(r_\alpha(V_n)\to r_\alpha(V)\), proving continuity.

Assumption \texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} gives
\(D_{jK}\ne\varnothing\), and the adoption-time definition gives
\(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\) for \(0\leq h\leq K\).  Hence every coordinate
simplex under either support rule is nonempty.  Each is a closed and bounded
subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, and there are finitely many
coordinates.  Therefore their product \(\mathcal F_s\) is nonempty and
compact.  The entries of \(A(w)\), and hence those of
\[
 V(w)=A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}, \tag{13}
\]
are polynomial functions of \(w\).  By
\texttt{lem:contrast-geometry},
\[
 V(w)\succeq\sigma_{\min}I_M\succ0 \tag{14}
\]
for every feasible \(w\).  Equations (12)--(14) show that
\(w\mapsto r_\alpha(V(w))\) is continuous on a nonempty compact set, so its
minimum is attained.

It remains to prove that this observed-law functional equals the causal-width
frontier rather than identify them by definition.  Under \(L=\delta=0\),
\texttt{lem:bias-radius} gives, for every feasible \(w\), every coordinate
\(e\), and every law in the model class,
\[
 a_e(w)^{\top}m=0. \tag{15}
\]
By \texttt{lem:prediction-error}, the difference between the observed linear
synthetic-control estimator and the realized causal contrast is therefore
\[
 \widehat\tau(w)-\tau=Z(w),
 \qquad Z(w)\sim N(0,V(w)). \tag{16}
\]
Thus every member of the model class induces the same centered Gaussian
error law in (16); the causal target \(\tau\) remains the potential-outcome
contrast fixed before this calculation.

Fix \(w\), a nonnegative half-width vector \(\ell\), and
\(R=\max_e\ell_e\).  The rectangular event is contained in the cube event:
\[
 \{|Z_e(w)|\leq\ell_e\text{ for every }e\}
 \subseteq\{\lVert Z(w)\rVert_\infty\leq R\}. \tag{17}
\]
If \((w,\ell)\) is honest, (16)--(17) imply
\(G_{V(w)}(R)\geq1-\alpha\), and therefore
\[
 R\geq r_\alpha(V(w)). \tag{18}
\]
Conversely, setting every half-width equal to
\(r_\alpha(V(w))\) makes the two events in (17) equal and gives coverage
exactly \(1-\alpha\) under every model-class law.  Hence the smallest possible
maximum half-width at fixed \(w\) is exactly \(r_\alpha(V(w))\).  Minimizing
over \(w\), and using attainment already proved, establishes (6).

For the strict comparison, write
\[
 V_N=V(w_N),\qquad V_D=V(w_D),\qquad
 \Delta=V_D-V_N. \tag{19}
\]
Condition (7) says \(\Delta\succeq0\) and \(\Delta\ne0\).  By (14),
\(V_N\succ0\).  On a product probability space take independent vectors
\[
 Z_N\sim N(0,V_N),\qquad U\sim N(0,\Delta). \tag{20}
\]
Then \(Z_N+U\sim N(0,V_D)\).  At the positive number
\(R_D=r_\alpha(V_D)\),
\texttt{lem:strict-anderson-gaussian-cube} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 1-\alpha
 &=\Pr\{Z_N+U\in[-R_D,R_D]^M\}\\
 &<\Pr\{Z_N\in[-R_D,R_D]^M\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{21}
\]
Strict monotonicity (9) and the defining root then yield
\[
 r_\alpha(V_N)<R_D=r_\alpha(V_D). \tag{22}
\]
Finally, \(w_D\) is a deep minimizer and \(w_N\) is nested-feasible, so (6)
and (22) imply
\[
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \leq r_\alpha(V_N)
 <r_\alpha(V_D)
 =W_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{23}
\]
No independence among the contrast coordinates, nonsingularity of
\(\Delta\), interiority of a weight vector, or limiting approximation is
used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies the exact zero-bias width frontier for arbitrary known correlated Gaussian contrasts and gives a strict covariance-order criterion. \textit{Gap.} The result optimizes a joint Gaussian-cube quantile over staggered donor simplexes; its strict comparison remains valid for singular covariance increments and degenerate simplex faces. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations may minimize the correlated Gaussian maximum quantile directly and obtain a rigorous strict-gap check from a Loewner certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:attainable-bias-exact-frontier]\label{thm:attainable-bias-exact-frontier}
For either \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\), the exact robust
deterministic linear-band frontier is attained and satisfies
\[
 W_s=\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}\ \max_{b\in\mathcal B(w)}
 r_\alpha\!\left(b,A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\right). \tag{15}
\]
For every feasible \(w\), the set \(\mathcal B(w)\) is compact and every one
of its elements is jointly attainable by a law in the declared model class.
The map \((b,V)\mapsto r_\alpha(b,V)\) is well defined and continuous on
\(\mathbb R^M\times\mathbb S_{++}^M\); both the maximum and the minimum in
(15) are therefore attained.  At an optimizing weight array, equal
half-widths equal to the right side of (15) form an honest band.  No
coordinatewise simultaneous endpoint-attainment assumption is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\).  The domain condition in
\texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} gives \(\mathcal J\ne\varnothing\), and
therefore \(M=|\mathcal E|\geq1\).  Assumption
\texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} and the inclusion
\(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\) for \(0\leq h\leq K\) make every coordinate
simplex under either support rule nonempty.  Each such simplex is closed and
bounded in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space.  Since \(\mathcal E\) is
finite, their product \(\mathcal F_s\) is nonempty and compact.

We first verify the asserted Gaussian radius.  For
\(b\in\mathbb R^M\), \(V\in\mathbb S_{++}^M\), and \(R\geq0\), put
\[
 H(b,V,R)=\Pr_{Z\sim N(b,V)}\{Z\in[-R,R]^M\}. \tag{16}
\]
The density of \(N(b,V)\) is continuous and strictly positive on all of
\(\mathbb R^M\).  Consequently \(H(b,V,0)=0\),
\(H(b,V,R)\to1\) as \(R\to\infty\), and, whenever
\(0\leq R_1<R_2\),
\[
 H(b,V,R_1)<H(b,V,R_2), \tag{17}
\]
because the difference of the two cubes contains a nonempty open rectangle.
Continuity in \(R\) follows by bounding the probability of the difference
between two nearby cubes by the integral of the bounded Gaussian density over
that difference.  Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\), (16)--(17) give a unique positive
root \(r_\alpha(b,V)\).

This root is jointly continuous.  Indeed, if
\((b_n,V_n)\to(b,V)\) with every \(V_n\) and \(V\) positive definite, then
\(V_n^{-1}\to V^{-1}\) and \(\det(V_n)\to\det(V)>0\).  On every fixed cube,
the corresponding normal densities therefore converge uniformly, so
\[
 H(b_n,V_n,R)\longrightarrow H(b,V,R)
 \quad\text{for every fixed }R\geq0. \tag{18}
\]
Let \(r=r_\alpha(b,V)>0\).  For every \(0<\varepsilon<r\), (17) gives
\[
 H(b,V,r-\varepsilon)<1-\alpha<H(b,V,r+\varepsilon). \tag{19}
\]
By (18), both strict inequalities persist for all sufficiently large \(n\).
The monotonicity and defining uniqueness of the roots then imply
\[
 r-\varepsilon<r_\alpha(b_n,V_n)<r+\varepsilon, \tag{20}
\]
which proves joint continuity.

Let
\[
 \Theta=\left\{(\beta,r):
 \lVert\beta_t\rVert_2\leq L\ (t\in\mathcal T),\quad
 |r_{it}|\leq\delta/2\ ((i,t)\in\mathcal C)\right\}. \tag{21}
\]
This is a nonempty compact finite product of closed Euclidean balls and
closed intervals.  For \(\theta=(\beta,r)\in\Theta\), define
\(m^\theta_{it}=x_i^{\top}\beta_t+r_{it}\).  The map
\[
 (w,\theta)\longmapsto
 \left(A(w)m^\theta,A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\right) \tag{22}
\]
is continuous.  Moreover, \texttt{lem:contrast-geometry} gives the uniform
positive-definiteness condition
\[
 A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\succeq\sigma_{\min}I_M\succ0
 \quad(w\in\mathcal F_s). \tag{23}
\]
It follows from (20)--(23) that
\[
 f(w,\theta)=r_\alpha\!\left(
 A(w)m^\theta,A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\right) \tag{24}
\]
is continuous on the compact set \(\mathcal F_s\times\Theta\).
For fixed \(w\), the image of \(\Theta\) under
\(\theta\mapsto A(w)m^\theta\) is exactly \(\mathcal B(w)\), so this set
is compact and the maximum of (24) over \(\theta\) is attained.  Uniform
continuity of \(f\) on the product compact set gives, for \(w,w'\) close,
\[
 \left|\max_{\theta\in\Theta}f(w,\theta)
       -\max_{\theta\in\Theta}f(w',\theta)\right|
 \leq\max_{\theta\in\Theta}|f(w,\theta)-f(w',\theta)|, \tag{25}
\]
and the right side tends to zero as \(w'\to w\).  Thus the outer objective
in (15) is continuous on \(\mathcal F_s\), and its minimum is attained.

We next establish that \(\mathcal B(w)\) is the exact joint bias set, rather
than merely an envelope.  If \(P\) belongs to
\(\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}(L,\delta,\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max})\), then
\texttt{ass:mean-structure}, \texttt{ass:coefficient-radius}, and
\texttt{ass:cellwise-misspecification} provide some \(\theta\in\Theta\)
with mean vector \(m=m^\theta\).  Hence
\[
 A(w)m\in\mathcal B(w). \tag{26}
\]
Conversely, take any \(b=A(w)m^\theta\in\mathcal B(w)\).  Let
\(Q\sim N(m^\theta,\Sigma_Q)\), assign its deduplicated coordinates to the
corresponding untreated potential outcomes, define untreated outcomes outside
\(\mathcal C\) arbitrarily, take treated potential outcomes equal to the
untreated ones, and define observed outcomes by
\texttt{ass:consistency}.  The chosen \(\theta\) satisfies each bound in
(21), while the fixed inputs already satisfy the donor and covariance-band
conditions.  This constructs a member of \texttt{def:model-class} whose
joint error mean is exactly \(b\).  Thus every element of \(\mathcal B(w)\)
is jointly attainable.  Notice that this construction respects shared
\(\beta_t\)'s and deduplicated remainders; it never combines separately
attained coordinate endpoints.

By \texttt{lem:prediction-error}, under a model law having bias
\(b=A(w)m\),
\[
 \widehat\tau(w)-\tau\sim
 N\!\left(b,V(w)\right),
 \qquad V(w)=A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}. \tag{27}
\]
This is a derived relation between the observed linear contrast and the
realized potential-outcome effect \(\tau\); it does not redefine the causal
target.

Fix \(w\), let \(\ell\in[0,\infty)^M\), and put
\(R=\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\ell_e\).  For every bias vector \(b\),
\[
 \{|Z_e|\leq\ell_e\text{ for every }e\in\mathcal E\}
 \subseteq\{\lVert Z\rVert_\infty\leq R\}. \tag{28}
\]
If \((w,\ell)\) is uniformly honest, joint attainability, (27), and (28)
imply
\[
 H(b,V(w),R)\geq1-\alpha
 \quad\text{for every }b\in\mathcal B(w), \tag{29}
\]
and hence, by (17),
\[
 R\geq\max_{b\in\mathcal B(w)}r_\alpha(b,V(w)). \tag{30}
\]
Conversely, set
\[
 R(w)=\max_{b\in\mathcal B(w)}r_\alpha(b,V(w)) \tag{31}
\]
and choose \(\ell_e=R(w)\) for every \(e\).  Then the rectangle equals the
cube in (28).  Equations (17), (26), (27), and (31) show that its coverage is
at least \(1-\alpha\) under every member of the model class.  Thus (30) is
attainable at fixed \(w\).  Minimizing (31) over the compact feasible set and
using (25) proves (15), including equal-half-width optimality and both
attainment assertions.  All arguments are finite-sample; no boundary
interiority, predictor-rank condition, independent-coordinate bias choice, or
limit passage is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the full-class exact characterization: it retains the joint nuisance geometry, proves all min--max and quantile attainment claims, and derives equal-half-width optimality from the honest-band problem. \textit{Gap.} The comparator literature does not provide this shifted correlated Gaussian-cube optimization over horizon-adaptive versus deepest-pool simplex products with jointly constrained bias vectors. \textit{Consumer.} The formula is an exact benchmark for numerical Gaussian-max calibration and for assessing the conservatism of the SOCP used in \texttt{scpi}- or \texttt{augsynth::multisynth}-adjacent workflows.

\begin{theorem}[thm:condition-number-order-sharpness]\label{thm:condition-number-order-sharpness}
Fix \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/2)\) and define the finite constant
\[
 C_\alpha=\sqrt{2\log(4/\alpha)}+
 \sqrt{2\log 2+2\log(4/\alpha)}.
\]
For every rational \(\lambda>0\) and integer \(n\geq5\), there is a legal
rational instance with
\(K=0\), \(M_n=2^{n^2}\) treated units adopting in the sole calendar period,
one shared never-treated donor, \(L=\delta=0\),
\(\sigma_{\min,n}=\lambda^2n^{-2}\), and
\(\sigma_{\max,n}=\lambda^2\), such that, for both
support rules and every supplied certified Bonferroni multiplier \(z_{B,n}\),
\[
 W_s\leq C_\alpha\lambda=C_\alpha\sqrt{\sigma_{\max,n}},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_s=z_{B,n}\lambda\sqrt{1+n^{-2}}
 \geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{\log M_n}, \tag{32}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \frac{\Gamma_s}{W_s}
 \geq\frac{\sqrt{\log2}}{2C_\alpha}\,n
 =\frac{\sqrt{\log2}}{2C_\alpha}
 \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\max,n}}{\sigma_{\min,n}}}. \tag{33}
\]
The deduplicated contrast covariance in this family is
\(\lambda^2(n^{-2}I_{M_n}+\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top})\).  Hence the square-root
condition-number dependence in \texttt{thm:constant-factor-frontier} is
order-sharp, up to constants depending only on \(\alpha\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix rational \(\lambda>0\), fix \(n\geq5\), and write \(M=2^{n^2}\).
Take \(T=1\), \(K=0\),
\(N=M+1\), and \(p=1\).  Label the first \(M\) units by
\(j=1,\ldots,M\), give each adoption date \(T_j=1\), and let
\(d=M+1\) be the sole never-treated unit, with \(T_d=\infty\).  Set
\(x_i=0\) for every unit and \(L=\delta=0\).  Then
\[
 \mathcal J=\{1,\ldots,M\},\qquad
 \mathcal E=\{(j,0):1\leq j\leq M\},\qquad
 D_{j0}=\{d\}. \tag{34}
\]
Thus donor feasibility and nonvacuity hold, the nested and deep feasible sets
coincide, and every contrast weight is forced to put mass one on \(d\).

After deduplication, the cell set is exactly
\[
 \mathcal C=\{(j,1):1\leq j\leq M\}\cup\{(d,1)\}; \tag{35}
\]
in particular, the common donor cell occurs only once.  In this ordering let
\[
 R_Q=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda n^{-1}I_M,\lambda),
 \qquad
 \Sigma_Q=R_Q^{\top}R_Q
 =\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^2n^{-2}I_M,\lambda^2). \tag{36}
\]
The factor is nonsingular and rational.  Its covariance obeys
\[
 \lambda^2n^{-2}I_{M+1}\preceq\Sigma_Q
 \preceq\lambda^2I_{M+1}, \tag{37}
\]
so the declared spectral band holds with
\(\sigma_{\min,n}=\lambda^2n^{-2}\) and
\(\sigma_{\max,n}=\lambda^2\).  This verifies that
the construction is a legal deduplicated fixed design in the model class.

Because \(L=\delta=0\), the attainable bias set is the singleton
\(\{0\}\).  Let \(U\sim N(0,\lambda^2)\) be the donor-cell noise and let
\(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_M\) be independent
\(N(0,\lambda^2n^{-2})\) treated-cell noises, also independent of \(U\).
From the
rows \(a_j=\mathbf e_j-\mathbf e_d\), the error vector has the representation
\[
 Z=(\varepsilon_1-U,\ldots,\varepsilon_M-U),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z)=\lambda^2
 (n^{-2}I_M+\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\top}). \tag{38}
\]
By \texttt{thm:attainable-bias-exact-frontier}, both support rules have the
same exact width, namely the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of
\(\max_j|\varepsilon_j-U|\).

We give a finite upper bound for that quantile.  If \(G\sim N(0,1)\), then
completing the square in its density gives
\(\mathbb E e^{tG}=e^{t^2/2}\).  On \(\{G\geq t\}\),
\(1\leq e^{tG-t^2}\); integration and symmetry therefore give, for
\(t\geq0\),
\[
 \Pr\{G\geq t\}\leq e^{-t^2/2},
 \qquad
 \Pr\{|G|\geq t\}\leq2e^{-t^2/2}. \tag{39}
\]
Put
\[
 t_0=\lambda\sqrt{2\log(4/\alpha)},
 \qquad
 t_1=\frac{\lambda}{n}\sqrt{2\log(4M/\alpha)}. \tag{40}
\]
Equation (39) and a union bound yield
\[
 \Pr\{|U|>t_0\}\leq\alpha/2,
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\max_j|\varepsilon_j|>t_1\}\leq\alpha/2. \tag{41}
\]
On the complementary event,
\(\max_j|\varepsilon_j-U|\leq t_0+t_1\).  Since
\(\log M=n^2\log2\),
\[
 t_1=\lambda\sqrt{2\log2+\frac{2}{n^2}\log(4/\alpha)}
 \leq\lambda\sqrt{2\log2+2\log(4/\alpha)}. \tag{42}
\]
Equations (40)--(42) and the exact characterization therefore prove
\[
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}=W_{\mathrm{deep}}\leq C_\alpha\lambda. \tag{43}
\]

For the SOCP, every contrast has zero bias and standard deviation
\[
 \operatorname{sd}_j=\lambda\sqrt{1+n^{-2}}. \tag{44}
\]
There is no weight choice, and taking the norm epigraph variables at equality
is feasible.  Thus \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} gives exactly
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 =z_{B,n}\lambda\sqrt{1+n^{-2}}. \tag{45}
\]
The certified tail inequality for \(z_{B,n}\) and strict monotonicity of
\(\Phi\) imply
\[
 z_{B,n}\geq\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2M}\right). \tag{46}
\]

It remains to give an explicit lower bound for this Gaussian quantile.  Let
\(\varphi\) be the standard normal density, put
\(x=\tfrac12\sqrt{\log M}\), and note that
\(\log M=n^2\log2>16\) for \(n\geq5\).  Since \(\varphi\) decreases on
\([0,\infty)\),
\[
 1-\Phi(x)=\int_x^\infty\varphi(u)\,du
 \geq\int_x^{x+1}\varphi(u)\,du
 \geq\varphi(x+1). \tag{47}
\]
For \(\log M\geq16\),
\[
 \frac{(x+1)^2}{2}
 =\frac18\log M+\frac12\sqrt{\log M}+\frac12
 \leq\frac38\log M. \tag{48}
\]
Consequently
\[
 1-\Phi(x)
 \geq(2\pi)^{-1/2}M^{-3/8}
 >\frac{\alpha}{2M}, \tag{49}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(M\geq1\), \(\alpha<1/2\), and
\(2/\sqrt{2\pi}>1/2\).  Equations (46)--(49) show that
\[
 z_{B,n}\geq\frac12\sqrt{\log M}. \tag{50}
\]
Combining (43), (45), and (50) gives (32)--(33), because
\(\log M=n^2\log2\) and
\(\sqrt{\sigma_{\max,n}/\sigma_{\min,n}}=n\).  Thus a common donor keeps the
exact Gaussian maximum at order \(\sqrt{\sigma_{\max,n}}\), while fixed
Bonferroni calibration pays \(\sqrt{\log M}\); along this legal family the
latter equals a constant times the square root of the covariance condition
number.  The statement is finite for every \(n\), and only the final
order-sharp conclusion takes \(n\to\infty\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A fully legal rational staggered-panel sequence certifies the lower order matching the spectral dependence of the general SOCP comparison. \textit{Gap.} No cited comparator establishes this simultaneous fixed-Bonferroni versus exact-width condition-number lower family. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem separates unavoidable worst-case Bonferroni conservatism from instance-specific opportunities for correlated Gaussian-max refinement.

\begin{lemma}[lem:affine-facial-reduction-alternative]\label{lem:affine-facial-reduction-alternative}
Let \(\mathcal K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be a finite product of nonnegative orthants, Lorentz cones, and zero cones, let \(F\) be a nonempty closed face of \(\mathcal K\), and put \(L_F=\operatorname{span}(F)\).  Let \(B:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^q\) be linear, let \(d\in\mathbb R^q\), and define
\[
 \mathcal H_F=\{y\in L_F:By=d\}.
\]
Assume \(\mathcal H_F\cap F\ne\varnothing\).  If \(\mathcal H_F\cap\operatorname{ri}F=\varnothing\), then there are \(\eta\in\mathbb R^q\) and \(z=B^{\top}\eta\) such that
\[
 z\in F^*,\qquad \eta^{\top}d=0,
 \qquad z\notin F^\perp.
\]
Consequently, \(F'=F\cap z^\perp\) is a strict closed face of \(F\) containing \(\mathcal H_F\cap F\).  The conclusion is valid when \(F\) lies on any degenerate product-cone face; no full-dimensionality, rank, or strict-complementarity condition is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All orthogonality and projection operations in this proof use the Euclidean inner product.  Write \(L=L_F\), \(C=\operatorname{ri}F\), and
\[
 U=C-\mathcal H_F=\{f-h:f\in C,\ h\in\mathcal H_F\}\subseteq L.       \tag{A1}
\]
Because \(F\) spans \(L\), its relative interior is its interior in \(L\); hence \(C\) is a nonempty open convex cone in \(L\).  The set \(\mathcal H_F\) is a nonempty closed affine subspace of \(L\), so \(U\) is nonempty, open, and convex.  The hypothesis \(C\cap\mathcal H_F=\varnothing\) is exactly \(0\notin U\).

We first prove, within finite-dimensional Euclidean space \(L\), that there is a nonzero \(\zeta\in L\) satisfying
\[
 \zeta^{\top}u\geq0\qquad(u\in U).                                    \tag{A2}
\]
This separation fact follows directly from metric projection.  If \(0\notin\overline U\), let \(u_*\) minimize \(\|u\|_2\) over the nonempty closed convex set \(\overline U\).  Such a minimizer exists because a minimizing sequence may be restricted to a bounded closed ball and then has a convergent subsequence.  It is nonzero, and comparison with \(u_*+t(u-u_*)\in\overline U\), followed by the right derivative at \(t=0\), gives
\[
 u_*^{\top}(u-u_*)\geq0\qquad(u\in\overline U).
\]
Thus \(\zeta=u_*\) satisfies the stronger inequality \(\zeta^{\top}u\geq\|\zeta\|_2^2>0\).

Suppose instead that \(0\in\overline U\).  Fix \(v\in U\) and set \(x_m=-v/m\).  Then \(x_m\notin\overline U\).  Indeed, if \(x_m\in\overline U\), the standard strict-segment property
\[
 a\in\overline U, b\in U, 0<t\leq1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1-t)a+tb\in U                            \tag{A3}
\]
with \(a=x_m\), \(b=v\), and \(t=1/(m+1)\) would put \(0\) in \(U\).  For completeness, (A3) follows by taking a ball about \(b\) contained in \(U\), approximating \(a\) by points of \(U\), and applying convexity; the resulting balls of radius \(t\) times the original radius eventually contain \((1-t)a+tb\).

Let \(p_m\) be the metric projection of \(x_m\) onto \(\overline U\), and put
\[
 \theta_m=\frac{p_m-x_m}{\|p_m-x_m\|_2}.
\]
The denominator is positive because \(x_m\notin\overline U\).  The projection inequality, obtained by differentiating the squared distance along segments in \(\overline U\), is
\[
 \theta_m^{\top}u\geq\theta_m^{\top}p_m
 \qquad(u\in\overline U).                                               \tag{A4}
\]
Since \(0\in\overline U\), optimality gives
\(\|p_m-x_m\|_2\leq\|x_m\|_2\), and therefore
\(\|p_m\|_2\leq2\|x_m\|_2\to0\).  A subsequence of the unit vectors \(\theta_m\) converges to a unit vector \(\zeta\in L\).  Passing to the limit in (A4) proves (A2).  This also shows directly that \(\zeta\ne0\).  Notice that the case \(L=\{0\}\) cannot occur under the two hypotheses \(\mathcal H_F\cap F\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal H_F\cap\operatorname{ri}F=\varnothing\).

By (A1)--(A2),
\[
 \zeta^{\top}f\geq\zeta^{\top}h
 \qquad(f\in C,\ h\in\mathcal H_F).                                    \tag{A5}
\]
Let
\[
 V=\{q\in L:Bq=0\}
\]
be the direction space of \(\mathcal H_F\).  Fixing \(f\in C\), \(h_0\in\mathcal H_F\), and substituting \(h_0+tq\) into (A5) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\) yields \(\zeta^{\top}q=0\).  Hence \(\zeta^{\top}h\) is constant on \(\mathcal H_F\); denote the constant by \(\gamma\).  Because \(tf\in C\) for every \(t>0\), (A5) gives
\[
 t\zeta^{\top}f\geq\gamma\qquad(t>0,f\in C).                          \tag{A6}
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow0\) gives \(\gamma\leq0\), while letting \(t\to\infty\) rules out \(\zeta^{\top}f<0\).  Thus \(\zeta^{\top}f\geq0\) on \(C\), and continuity together with \(\overline C^{\,L}=F\) gives \(\zeta^{\top}f\geq0\) on \(F\).  Choose \(y_0\in\mathcal H_F\cap F\).  Then
\[
 \gamma=\zeta^{\top}y_0\geq0.
\]
Combining this with \(\gamma\leq0\) proves
\[
 \zeta^{\top}h=0\quad(h\in\mathcal H_F),
 \qquad \zeta^{\top}f\geq0\quad(f\in F).                              \tag{A7}
\]

Let \(B_L\) be the restriction of \(B\) to \(L\), and let \(P_L\) be orthogonal projection onto \(L\).  Since \(\zeta\) annihilates \(\ker B_L\), finite-dimensional linear algebra gives
\[
 \zeta\in(\ker B_L)^\perp
       =\operatorname{range}(B_L^*)
       =\operatorname{range}(P_LB^{\top}).                              \tag{A8}
\]
Choose \(\eta\in\mathbb R^q\) with \(\zeta=P_LB^{\top}\eta\), and set \(z=B^{\top}\eta\).  Since every \(f\in F\) belongs to \(L\), (A7)--(A8) imply
\[
 z^{\top}f=\zeta^{\top}f\geq0,
\]
so \(z\in F^*\).  Moreover,
\[
 \eta^{\top}d
 =\eta^{\top}By_0
 =z^{\top}y_0
 =\zeta^{\top}y_0
 =0.                                                                    \tag{A9}
\]
If \(z\in F^\perp=L^\perp\), then \(P_Lz=0\), contradicting \(P_Lz=\zeta\ne0\).  Hence \(z\notin F^\perp\).

Finally, \(F'=F\cap z^\perp\) is a face: if \(f_1,f_2\in F\) and \(f_1+f_2\in F'\), then nonnegativity of \(z^{\top}f_1\) and \(z^{\top}f_2\) and their zero sum put both points in \(F'\).  It is strict because \(z\notin F^\perp\), and it is closed because both \(F\) and \(z^\perp\) are closed.  For every \(y\in\mathcal H_F\cap F\),
\[
 z^{\top}y=\eta^{\top}By=\eta^{\top}d=0,
\]
so every conic-feasible point lies in \(F'\), as required.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The exact full-class formula keeps the bias coordinates coupled through common calendar-time coefficients and deduplicated cell remainders. This matters because the endpoint attaining one coordinate's bias need not attain all other endpoints simultaneously; the robust width is therefore a maximum over the attainable joint image, not over a Cartesian bias box. In the order-sharp family, every treated contrast shares one donor shock while its treated-cell shock is independent and much smaller. The shared shock keeps the exact Gaussian maximum bounded on the donor-noise scale even when the number of treated units is exponential in the covariance condition number, whereas fixed Bonferroni calibration still pays a square-root log-multiplicity factor. The earlier strict witness continues to isolate the value of an early donor that remains untreated only at the early horizon. These conclusions concern deterministic linear bands in the declared experiment, not the numerical output or statistical class of an existing synthetic-control estimator.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact full-class characterization is a finite-dimensional attained min--max functional, but evaluating correlated shifted Gaussian-cube probabilities and globally optimizing that generally nonconic objective can be computationally harder than solving the Bonferroni SOCP. The order-sharp family shows that the square-root spectral condition-number loss of fixed Bonferroni calibration cannot be removed uniformly, although particular covariance and donor geometries may yield a much smaller gap. The covariance remains known and prespecified; covariance estimation, selective donor choice, outcome-dependent weights, and transfer to a published estimator class are outside this version. The facial-reduction equivalence concerns strict \(\Gamma\) improvement on arbitrary degenerate faces and is not a general strict-\(W\) criterion. Its affine exposing multipliers, minimal face, and facial-dual bound are exactly certifiable, and its algebraic construction and verifier terminate, but no quantitative bit-complexity or practical-efficiency guarantee is asserted.

\section{Honest scope}

Finite-sample SOCP coverage, the exact full bounded-mean attainable-bias frontier, the covariance-domination sandwich, its square-root condition-number order-sharpness, weak nested-support dominance, and the rational strict witnesses are theorem-level results. The exact formula uses all existing \(L\) and \(\delta\) values and maximizes over the jointly attainable bias set; it does not assume simultaneous coordinatewise endpoint attainment. On the zero-bias class, \texttt{thm:correlated-gaussian-exact-frontier} additionally gives a strict Loewner covariance criterion, while \texttt{thm:disjoint-diagonal-exact-frontier} gives the explicit product root. The exact finite certificate in \texttt{thm:degenerate-iff-gap-certificate} characterizes strict SOCP improvement on degenerate faces: its affine exposure identities are derived by an in-house separation lemma, and its real-algebraic construction and verifier terminate. It carries no quantitative runtime bound. Unknown covariance, covariance confidence sets, random donor selection, outcome-dependent weights, direct coverage claims for the Ben-Michael--Feller--Rothstein estimator, and coverage claims for \texttt{scpi} or \texttt{augsynth::multisynth} are outside the paper. Correlated Gaussian-max numerical calibration is a post-solve refinement.
