%%% FIELD affine-lemma-statement
Let \(\mathcal K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be a finite product of nonnegative orthants, Lorentz cones, and zero cones, let \(F\) be a nonempty closed face of \(\mathcal K\), and put \(L_F=\operatorname{span}(F)\).  Let \(B:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^q\) be linear, let \(d\in\mathbb R^q\), and define
\[
 \mathcal H_F=\{y\in L_F:By=d\}.
\]
Assume \(\mathcal H_F\cap F\ne\varnothing\).  If \(\mathcal H_F\cap\operatorname{ri}F=\varnothing\), then there are \(\eta\in\mathbb R^q\) and \(z=B^{\top}\eta\) such that
\[
 z\in F^*,\qquad \eta^{\top}d=0,
 \qquad z\notin F^\perp.
\]
Consequently, \(F'=F\cap z^\perp\) is a strict closed face of \(F\) containing \(\mathcal H_F\cap F\).  The conclusion is valid when \(F\) lies on any degenerate product-cone face; no full-dimensionality, rank, or strict-complementarity condition is required.

%%% FIELD affine-lemma-proof
All orthogonality and projection operations in this proof use the Euclidean inner product.  Write \(L=L_F\), \(C=\operatorname{ri}F\), and
\[
 U=C-\mathcal H_F=\{f-h:f\in C,\ h\in\mathcal H_F\}\subseteq L.       \tag{A1}
\]
Because \(F\) spans \(L\), its relative interior is its interior in \(L\); hence \(C\) is a nonempty open convex cone in \(L\).  The set \(\mathcal H_F\) is a nonempty closed affine subspace of \(L\), so \(U\) is nonempty, open, and convex.  The hypothesis \(C\cap\mathcal H_F=\varnothing\) is exactly \(0\notin U\).

We first prove, within finite-dimensional Euclidean space \(L\), that there is a nonzero \(\zeta\in L\) satisfying
\[
 \zeta^{\top}u\geq0\qquad(u\in U).                                    \tag{A2}
\]
This separation fact follows directly from metric projection.  If \(0\notin\overline U\), let \(u_*\) minimize \(\|u\|_2\) over the nonempty closed convex set \(\overline U\).  Such a minimizer exists because a minimizing sequence may be restricted to a bounded closed ball and then has a convergent subsequence.  It is nonzero, and comparison with \(u_*+t(u-u_*)\in\overline U\), followed by the right derivative at \(t=0\), gives
\[
 u_*^{\top}(u-u_*)\geq0qquad(u\in\overline U).
\]
Thus \(\zeta=u_*\) satisfies the stronger inequality \(\zeta^{\top}u\geq\|\zeta\|_2^2>0\).

Suppose instead that \(0\in\overline U\).  Fix \(v\in U\) and set \(x_m=-v/m\).  Then \(x_m\notin\overline U\).  Indeed, if \(x_m\in\overline U\), the standard strict-segment property
\[
 a\in\overline U, b\in U, 0<t\leq1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1-t)a+tb\in U                            \tag{A3}
\]
with \(a=x_m\), \(b=v\), and \(t=1/(m+1)\) would put \(0\) in \(U\).  For completeness, (A3) follows by taking a ball about \(b\) contained in \(U\), approximating \(a\) by points of \(U\), and applying convexity; the resulting balls of radius \(t\) times the original radius eventually contain \((1-t)a+tb\).

Let \(p_m\) be the metric projection of \(x_m\) onto \(\overline U\), and put
\[
 \theta_m=\frac{p_m-x_m}{\|p_m-x_m\|_2}.
\]
The denominator is positive because \(x_m\notin\overline U\).  The projection inequality, obtained by differentiating the squared distance along segments in \(\overline U\), is
\[
 \theta_m^{\top}u\geq\theta_m^{\top}p_m
 \qquad(u\in\overline U).                                               \tag{A4}
\]
Since \(0\in\overline U\), optimality gives
\(\|p_m-x_m\|_2\leq\|x_m\|_2\), and therefore
\(\|p_m\|_2\leq2\|x_m\|_2\to0\).  A subsequence of the unit vectors \(\theta_m\) converges to a unit vector \(\zeta\in L\).  Passing to the limit in (A4) proves (A2).  This also shows directly that \(\zeta\ne0\).  Notice that the case \(L=\{0\}\) cannot occur under the two hypotheses \(\mathcal H_F\cap F\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal H_F\cap\operatorname{ri}F=\varnothing\).

By (A1)--(A2),
\[
 \zeta^{\top}f\geq\zeta^{\top}h
 \qquad(f\in C,\ h\in\mathcal H_F).                                    \tag{A5}
\]
Let
\[
 V=\{q\in L:Bq=0\}
\]
be the direction space of \(\mathcal H_F\).  Fixing \(f\in C\), \(h_0\in\mathcal H_F\), and substituting \(h_0+tq\) into (A5) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\) yields \(\zeta^{\top}q=0\).  Hence \(\zeta^{\top}h\) is constant on \(\mathcal H_F\); denote the constant by \(\gamma\).  Because \(tf\in C\) for every \(t>0\), (A5) gives
\[
 t\zeta^{\top}f\geq\gamma\qquad(t>0,f\in C).                          \tag{A6}
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow0\) gives \(\gamma\leq0\), while letting \(t\to\infty\) rules out \(\zeta^{\top}f<0\).  Thus \(\zeta^{\top}f\geq0\) on \(C\), and continuity together with \(\overline C^{\,L}=F\) gives \(\zeta^{\top}f\geq0\) on \(F\).  Choose \(y_0\in\mathcal H_F\cap F\).  Then
\[
 \gamma=\zeta^{\top}y_0\geq0.
\]
Combining this with \(\gamma\leq0\) proves
\[
 \zeta^{\top}h=0\quad(h\in\mathcal H_F),
 \qquad \zeta^{\top}f\geq0\quad(f\in F).                              \tag{A7}
\]

Let \(B_L\) be the restriction of \(B\) to \(L\), and let \(P_L\) be orthogonal projection onto \(L\).  Since \(\zeta\) annihilates \(\ker B_L\), finite-dimensional linear algebra gives
\[
 \zeta\in(\ker B_L)^\perp
       =\operatorname{range}(B_L^*)
       =\operatorname{range}(P_LB^{\top}).                              \tag{A8}
\]
Choose \(\eta\in\mathbb R^q\) with \(\zeta=P_LB^{\top}\eta\), and set \(z=B^{\top}\eta\).  Since every \(f\in F\) belongs to \(L\), (A7)--(A8) imply
\[
 z^{\top}f=\zeta^{\top}f\geq0,
\]
so \(z\in F^*\).  Moreover,
\[
 \eta^{\top}d
 =\eta^{\top}By_0
 =z^{\top}y_0
 =\zeta^{\top}y_0
 =0.                                                                    \tag{A9}
\]
If \(z\in F^\perp=L^\perp\), then \(P_Lz=0\), contradicting \(P_Lz=\zeta\ne0\).  Hence \(z\notin F^\perp\).

Finally, \(F'=F\cap z^\perp\) is a face: if \(f_1,f_2\in F\) and \(f_1+f_2\in F'\), then nonnegativity of \(z^{\top}f_1\) and \(z^{\top}f_2\) and their zero sum put both points in \(F'\).  It is strict because \(z\notin F^\perp\), and it is closed because both \(F\) and \(z^\perp\) are closed.  For every \(y\in\mathcal H_F\cap F\),
\[
 z^{\top}y=\eta^{\top}By=\eta^{\top}d=0,
\]
so every conic-feasible point lies in \(F'\), as required.

%%% FIELD target-statement
For every finite rational input \(\mathsf{Data}\),
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{a valid finite }\mathsf{GapCert}\text{ exists}.
\]
There is an exact terminating construction and verifier: a finite disjunction of semialgebraic facial-reduction systems is decided by effective real-closed-field quantifier elimination, algebraic witnesses are represented in \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm{rep}}\), and rational objective enclosures are enumerated until they separate.  Outward-rounded rational arithmetic is used only for the two objective enclosures.  The equivalence and terminating algorithm require neither unique optimizers, positive donor weights, independent predictor columns, ordinary Slater feasibility, nor strict complementarity, and they cover tight and collapsed Lorentz-cone blocks.  No quantitative bit-complexity bound is asserted.

%%% FIELD target-proof
Write \(P_s\) for the standard-form conic program supplied by \texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}.  We first handle infeasibility.  For every \(j\in\mathcal J\) and \(0\leq h\leq K\), the donor-set definition gives
\[
 D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh},                                                \tag{1}
\]
because \(T_i>T_j+K\) implies \(T_i>T_j+h\).  If \(P_{\mathrm{nested}}\) is feasible, its horizon-\(K\) coordinate lies in \(\Delta(D_{jK})\) for every \(j\), so every deepest donor simplex is nonempty.  Choosing an element of each \(\Delta(D_{jK})\) and reusing it at all horizons makes \(P_{\mathrm{deep}}\) feasible.  Conversely, (1) embeds every deep-feasible weight array in \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{nested}}\).  Given feasible weights, the norm epigraph variables and nonnegative scalar slacks in \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp} can all be chosen finite.  Therefore
\[
 P_{\mathrm{nested}}\text{ is feasible}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P_{\mathrm{deep}}\text{ is feasible}.                                \tag{2}
\]
If (2) fails, both values are \(+\infty\), so strict inequality is false.  A valid \(\mathsf{GapCert}\) is also impossible, because its \(y_N\) is nested-primal feasible and its \(y^\circ\) is deep-primal feasible.  This proves the equivalence in the infeasible case.

Assume henceforth that the equivalent feasibility conditions in (2) hold.  For fixed feasible weights, eliminating the epigraph variables gives
\[
 G_s(w)=\max_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{
 L\left\|x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right\|_2+
 \delta+z_B\left\|R_Qa_{jh}(w)\right\|_2
 \right\}.                                                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the smallest admissible \(u_{jh}\) and \(v_{jh}\) are the corresponding norms, and the smallest admissible \(g\) is their finite maximum.  Every nonempty donor simplex is compact, so the finite product \(\mathcal F_s\) is compact.  The affine maps, Euclidean norms, and finite maximum in (3) are continuous.  Hence
\[
 \Gamma_s=\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}G_s(w)<+\infty,                       \tag{4}
\]
and the minimum is attained.  Restoring the norm values, scalar slacks, and the standard-form variables gives an attained optimizer of \(P_s\).  This step uses no division or rank condition and permits zero donor weights and nonunique minimizers.

Suppose now that
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                     \tag{5}
\]
Choose a nested primal optimizer \(y_N^*\), which exists by (4).  Abbreviate
\[
 B_D=B_{\mathrm{deep}},\quad d_D=d_{\mathrm{deep}},\quad
 \mathcal K_D=\mathcal K_{\mathrm{deep}},\quad
 H_D=\{y:B_Dy=d_D\},\quad S=H_D\cap\mathcal K_D.
\]
The set \(S\) is nonempty by (2).  Starting from \(F_0=\mathcal K_D\), construct faces inductively.  Suppose that \(F_k\) is a closed face of \(\mathcal K_D\) containing \(S\), set \(L_k=\operatorname{span}(F_k)\), and define
\[
 H_k=\{y\in L_k:B_Dy=d_D\}.                                            \tag{6}
\]
Then \(H_k\cap F_k=S\), so this intersection is nonempty.  If \(H_k\cap\operatorname{ri}F_k\ne\varnothing\), stop.  Otherwise, \texttt{lem:affine-facial-reduction-alternative} supplies \(\eta_{k+1}\) and \(z_{k+1}=B_D^{\top}\eta_{k+1}\) such that
\[
 z_{k+1}\in F_k^*,\qquad
 d_D^{\top}\eta_{k+1}=0,\qquad
 z_{k+1}\notin F_k^\perp,                                              \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 F_{k+1}=F_k\cap z_{k+1}^\perp                                         \tag{8}
\]
is a strict closed face containing \(S\).  Thus every step satisfies exactly the affine exposure identities in \texttt{def:facial-dual}; no arbitrary exposing vector has been substituted for an affine multiplier.

Every strict face inclusion lowers dimension.  Consequently, after at most \(\dim\mathcal K_D\) steps the construction stops at a face \(F_r\) and a point
\[
 y^\circ\in S\cap\operatorname{ri}F_r.                                \tag{9}
\]
We verify rather than assume that \(F_r\) is the minimal face containing \(S\).  Let \(G\) be any face of \(\mathcal K_D\) containing \(S\).  Then \(y^\circ\in G\cap\operatorname{ri}F_r\).  For every \(x\in F_r\), relative interior gives an \(\varepsilon>0\) for which
\[
 q=y^\circ+\varepsilon(y^\circ-x)\in F_r.
\]
Since
\[
 y^\circ=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}x
          +\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}q,                                   \tag{10}
\]
and both \(x,q\) lie in \(\mathcal K_D\), the face property of \(G\) implies \(x,q\in G\).  Hence \(F_r\subseteq G\).  On the other hand, every face in the constructed chain contains \(S\), so the minimal feasible face is contained in \(F_r\).  Therefore
\[
 F_r=F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}.                                           \tag{11}
\]

The feasible set is unchanged when \(\mathcal K_D\) is replaced by (11), and (9) is relative Slater feasibility on that face.  Since the deep value is finite by (4), the attained strong-duality conclusion of \texttt{lem:facial-reduction-relative-strong-duality} gives a facial-dual optimizer \(\lambda_D^*\) with
\[
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_D^{\top}\lambda_D^*
       \in F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}^*,
 \qquad
 d_D^{\top}\lambda_D^*=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                       \tag{12}
\]
Nested optimality, (5), and (12) yield
\[
 c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N^*
 <d_D^{\top}\lambda_D^*.                                               \tag{13}
\]

We next show that the witnesses can be constructed and checked exactly.  All constraints in (6)--(13) are semialgebraic over the rational input.  Orthant and zero-cone membership are given by linear signs and equalities, while Lorentz-cone membership is
\[
 (t,z)\in\mathcal L_q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 t\geq0\ \text{ and }\ t^2-\sum_{\nu=1}^{q-1}z_\nu^2\geq0.          \tag{14}
\]
The needed faces also have finite semialgebraic parameterizations.  A face of a nonnegative orthant is specified by the coordinates fixed at zero.  For \(q\geq2\), every nonzero proper face of \(\mathcal L_q\) is a boundary ray: a proper face cannot contain an interior point, and two noncollinear nonzero boundary points have a strict-triangle-inequality midpoint in the interior.  A boundary ray is parameterized by \((1,u)\) with \(\|u\|_2=1\); the zero and full faces are the remaining cases.  When \(q=1\), only the zero and full faces occur.  A face of a finite product is a product of faces.  To see this for two factors, decompose every \((x_1,x_2)\) in a face as \((x_1,0)+(0,x_2)\); the face property places both summands in the face, while closure under addition supplies every product of such coordinate summands.  Iteration handles finitely many factors.

It follows that face membership, dual-face membership, relative-interior membership, strict inclusion, and (8) are expressible by finite first-order formulas whose atoms are rational polynomial sign conditions.  Clearing finitely many denominators gives integer-coefficient atoms.  Chain length is bounded by the ambient conic dimension.  For each possible \(r\), let \(\Psi_r\) be the resulting semialgebraic sentence asserting the existence of face parameters, \(z_k,\eta_k,y^\circ,y_N,\lambda_D\) satisfying the identities in \texttt{def:facial-dual}, nested primal feasibility, facial-dual feasibility, and
\[
 c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N
 <d_D^{\top}\lambda_D.                                                  \tag{15}
\]
Equations (6)--(13) prove that (5) implies \(\bigvee_r\Psi_r\).

Conversely, assume some \(\Psi_r\) holds.  The affine exposure identities imply inductively that every deep-feasible point lies in every \(F_k\), and the asserted terminal relative-interior feasible point proves by the argument in (10)--(11) that \(F_r\) is the minimal feasible face.  If
\[
 s_D=c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_D^{\top}\lambda_D\in F_r^*,
\]
then every deep-feasible \(y_D\) satisfies
\[
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}y_D-d_D^{\top}\lambda_D
 =s_D^{\top}y_D\geq0.                                                   \tag{16}
\]
Together with nested primal feasibility and (15), this gives
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \leq c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N
 <d_D^{\top}\lambda_D
 \leq\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                                           \tag{17}
\]
Thus
\[
 \bigvee_r\Psi_r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}.                     \tag{18}
\]

By \texttt{lem:effective-real-algebraic-elimination}, effective real-closed-field quantifier elimination decides the finite disjunction in (18).  When it is true, sign-condition sampling applied to the finite family of polynomials defining a true \(\Psi_r\) produces a real-univariate sample point in a realizable component, hence witnesses with real algebraic coordinates.  Each coordinate can be put in \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm{rep}}\): eliminate the univariate parameter to obtain an integer polynomial for the coordinate, take its square-free factor, and isolate the selected real root by rational intervals, with the root and every required polynomial sign checked by the same effective real-closed-field procedure.  This yields exact algebraic entries for the faces, affine exposing multipliers, relative-interior point, nested optimizer, and attained facial-dual optimizer.  If the disjunction is false, the decision procedure terminates and correctly reports that no such witnesses exist.  Hence (18) gives an exact terminating construction and decision procedure, with no complexity-rate assertion.

For a true disjunct, put
\[
 p_N=c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N,
 \qquad q_D=d_D^{\top}\lambda_D.
\]
These are algebraic numbers and (15) gives \(p_N<q_D\).  Enumerate rational quadruples \((\ell_N,u_N,\ell_D,u_D)\) and retain the first one for which exact real-closed-field checking verifies
\[
 \ell_N\leq p_N\leq u_N<\ell_D\leq q_D\leq u_D.                       \tag{19}
\]
This enumeration terminates.  Indeed, with \(\varepsilon=(q_D-p_N)/3>0\), the Archimedean property supplies a positive integer \(m\) with \(1/m<\varepsilon\); rational grid points of mesh \(1/m\) around \(p_N\) and \(q_D\) then give outer intervals whose inner endpoints are strictly separated.  Setting \(I_N=[\ell_N,u_N]\) and \(I_D=[\ell_D,u_D]\) produces
\[
 c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N\in I_N,
 \qquad d_D^{\top}\lambda_D\in I_D,
 \qquad \sup I_N<\inf I_D.                                             \tag{20}
\]
These two recorded enclosures are the only quantities for which outward-rounded rational interval arithmetic is used.  The chain, affine identities, cone signs, relative-interior condition, root data, and dual feasibility are checked exactly.

The algebraic witnesses and (20) form a valid finite \(\mathsf{GapCert}\).  This proves certificate existence from the strict gap.  The reverse implication is \texttt{prop:strict-gap-sufficient}; it also follows directly from (17) and (20).  A verifier terminates because every face, affine, cone, relative-interior, algebraic-root, and enclosure condition is a finite real-closed-field sign question.  Zero donor weights are orthant-boundary points, redundant predictor columns only repeat polynomial constraints, and the face classification above includes full, ray, and zero Lorentz faces, including collapsed blocks.  No uniqueness, positive-weight, rank, ordinary Slater, strict-complementarity, boundary exclusion, or limiting assumption has been introduced, and no quantitative bit-complexity bound is claimed.
