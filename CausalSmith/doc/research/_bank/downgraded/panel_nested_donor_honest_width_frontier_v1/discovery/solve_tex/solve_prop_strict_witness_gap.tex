%%% FIELD proof-target
In \(\mathsf I_*\), \(L=\delta=0\) and \(x_i=0\), so the predictor-norm
constraints do not affect the objective.  By
\texttt{def:bonferroni-socp}, minimizing the epigraph variables first gives
\[
 \Gamma_s=z_B\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}
 \max_{h\in\{0,1\}}\lVert R_Qa_{1h}(w)\rVert_2. \tag{1}
\]
At calendar time \(2\), every relevant diagonal entry of \(R_Q\) is one.
Under nested support, write the early weights as
\((w_2,w_3,w_4)\).  The focal coordinate \((1,2)\) is distinct from the
three donor coordinates and has coefficient one.  Hence
\[
 \lVert R_Qa_{1,0}(w)\rVert_2^2
 =1+w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2. \tag{2}
\]
The simplex restrictions state separately that \(w_i\geq0\) and
\(w_2+w_3+w_4=1\).  Therefore
\[
 0\leq (w_2-w_3)^2+(w_2-w_4)^2+(w_3-w_4)^2
 =3(w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2)-1. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the sum of squares is at least \(1/3\), with equality if and only if
\(w_2=w_3=w_4=1/3\).  The optimized early nested variance is consequently
\(4/3\), and the displayed equal weights are its unique minimizer.

The deep early support is \(D_{1K}=\{3,4\}\).  For
\(w_3,w_4\geq0\) and \(w_3+w_4=1\),
\[
 \lVert R_Qa_{1,0}(w)\rVert_2^2
 =1+w_3^2+w_4^2
 =\frac32+\frac12(w_3-w_4)^2\geq\frac32. \tag{4}
\]
Equality holds if and only if \((w_3,w_4)=(1/2,1/2)\).

At time \(3\), both rules use \(D_{1,1}=D_{1K}=\{3,4\}\), and every
relevant diagonal entry of \(R_Q\) is \(1/10\).  Consequently,
\[
 \lVert R_Qa_{1,1}(w)\rVert_2^2
 =\frac1{100}(1+w_3^2+w_4^2)
 =\frac3{200}+\frac1{200}(w_3-w_4)^2
 \geq\frac3{200}, \tag{5}
\]
again with equality exactly at equal weights.  The positive early standard
deviations \(\sqrt{4/3}\) and \(\sqrt{3/2}\) both exceed the common late
standard deviation \(\sqrt{3/200}\).  Because \(\mathcal F_s\) is a product
of the two horizon-specific simplexes, the separate minimizers just found can
be selected simultaneously and minimize the maximum in (1).  Substitution of
the declared value \(z_B=9/4\) gives
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 =\frac94\sqrt{\frac43}
 =\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}
 =\frac9{2\sqrt3},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 =\frac94\sqrt{\frac32}
 =\frac{9\sqrt6}{8}. \tag{6}
\]

The numerical enclosures require only exact rational squaring of positive
endpoints:
\[
 2.5980^2=6.749604<\frac{27}{4}<6.75012361=2.5981^2
\]
and
\[
 2.7556^2=7.59333136<\frac{243}{32}<7.59388249=2.7557^2.
\]
Thus
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\in[2.5980,2.5981],
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\in[2.7556,2.7557].
\]
Subtracting outward endpoints yields
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \in[2.7556-2.5981,\,2.7557-2.5980]
 =[0.1575,0.1577]. \tag{7}
\]
Finally, \(T_2=3\) implies \(2\in D_{1,0}\), because \(3>2\), whereas
\(2\notin D_{1,1}=D_{1K}\), because \(3\not>3\).  Hence the nested early
rule uses unit \(2\)'s untreated time-\(2\) outcome, while the common deep
pool excludes unit \(2\) at the early horizon solely because it adopts at the
late horizon.  Every donor simplex used above is nonempty, every variance is
strictly positive, and no limiting argument is used.

%%% FIELD statement-exact-frontier
Fix \(s\in\{\mathrm{nested},\mathrm{deep}\}\).  Suppose
\(\mathcal E\ne\varnothing\), \(L=\delta=0\), \(\Sigma_Q\) is diagonal, and
the eligible row-cell sets are pairwise disjoint:
\(\mathcal C^s_e\cap\mathcal C^s_f=\varnothing\) whenever \(e\ne f\).
Then every \(v_{s,e}\) is attained and strictly positive, and \(W_s\) is the
unique positive number \(R_s\) satisfying
\[
 \prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{2\Phi\!\left(\frac{R_s}{\sqrt{v_{s,e}}}\right)-1\right\}
 =1-\alpha. \tag{8}
\]
If the displayed conditions hold for both support rules, then
\(v_{\mathrm{nested},e}\leq v_{\mathrm{deep},e}\) for every
\(e\in\mathcal E\).  If this inequality is strict for at least one
coordinate, then \(W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}\).

%%% FIELD proof-exact-frontier
Fix a support rule \(s\) and an index \(e=(j,h)\).  Assumption
\texttt{ass:donor-feasibility} gives \(D_{jK}\ne\varnothing\), and
\(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\) follows from \(h\leq K\).  Hence the coordinate
simplex \(\Delta(D^s_e)\) is nonempty under either rule.  It is also compact.
The quadratic map
\[
 q_e(\omega):=a_e(\omega)^{\top}\Sigma_Qa_e(\omega)
\]
is continuous on that simplex, so the minimum defining \(v_{s,e}\) is
attained.  The declared positive definiteness of \(\Sigma_Q\) and the unit
coefficient of \(a_e(\omega)\) at its focal cell imply
\[
 q_e(\omega)>0
 \quad\text{for every }\omega\in\Delta(D^s_e),
 \qquad v_{s,e}>0. \tag{9}
\]

We next identify the coverage functional, keeping it distinct from the causal
target.  Under \(L=\delta=0\), \texttt{lem:bias-radius} gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,K,p}}
 |a_e(w)^{\top}m|=0
\]
for every feasible \(w\) and every \(e\).  Therefore every law in the model
class has \(a_e(w)^{\top}m=0\).  By
\texttt{lem:prediction-error}, the estimation error vector
\[
 Z(w):=\widehat\tau(w)-\tau
\]
is consequently centered Gaussian with covariance
\(A(w)\Sigma_QA(w)^{\top}\).  This proves the relationship between the
observed-law error and the causal contrast \(\tau\); it does not define the
causal contrast by the Gaussian model.

For distinct \(e,f\in\mathcal E\), the rows \(a_e(w)\) and \(a_f(w)\) are
supported respectively on the disjoint sets \(\mathcal C^s_e\) and
\(\mathcal C^s_f\).  Since \(\Sigma_Q\) is diagonal,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}\{Z_e(w),Z_f(w)\}
 =a_e(w)^{\top}\Sigma_Qa_f(w)=0. \tag{10}
\]
All marginal variances are positive by (9).  Thus the joint Gaussian density
has diagonal covariance and factors as
\[
 \prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi q_e(w_e)}}
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{z_e^2}{2q_e(w_e)}\right\};
\]
hence its coordinates are independent.  It follows, for every deterministic
half-width vector \(\ell\), that every law in the model class has the same
coverage probability
\[
 \Pr\{|Z_e(w)|\leq\ell_e\text{ for all }e\}
 =\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\ell_e}{\sqrt{q_e(w_e)}}\right)-1\right\}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus the infimum over laws in \texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} equals the
right-hand side of (11).

Fix \(R\geq0\) and restrict attention to rectangles satisfying
\(\max_e\ell_e\leq R\).  Each factor in (11) is nondecreasing in
\(\ell_e\), so replacing every half-width by \(R\) cannot reduce coverage and
does not increase the maximum half-width beyond \(R\).  For \(R>0\), the
factor as a function of a positive variance \(v\) has derivative
\[
 \frac{d}{dv}\left\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt v)-1\right\}
 =-Rv^{-3/2}\varphi(R/\sqrt v)<0. \tag{12}
\]
The feasible weight set is the Cartesian product of its coordinate simplexes,
and \(q_e(w_e)\) depends only on the coordinate weight \(w_e\).  Hence the
largest coverage attainable with maximum half-width at most \(R\) is
\[
 F_s(R):=prod_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{2\Phi\!\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt{v_{s,e}}}\right)-1\right\}, \tag{13}
\]
and it is attained by selecting a variance-minimizing weight independently in
every coordinate and setting every \(\ell_e=R\).  The same conclusion holds
at \(R=0\), when both sides of (13) are zero because
\(\mathcal E\ne\varnothing\).

Every \(v_{s,e}\) is positive and \(\mathcal E\) is finite and nonempty.
Therefore \(F_s\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)\), strictly increasing there,
\(F_s(0)=0\), and \(F_s(R)\to1\) as \(R\to\infty\).  Since
\(0<\alpha<1/2\), the intermediate-value theorem gives a unique positive
\(R_s\) with \(F_s(R_s)=1-\alpha\).  Equation (13) shows that a deterministic
linear rectangle with maximum half-width \(R\) can be honest if and only if
\(F_s(R)\geq1-\alpha\), equivalently if and only if \(R\geq R_s\).  The
minimizers used in (13) attain honesty at \(R_s\).  Taking the infimum in
\texttt{def:exact-width-frontier} proves
\[
 W_s=R_s, \tag{14}
\]
and establishes (8).

Finally, for every \(e=(j,h)\), the support definitions give
\(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\), and hence
\[
 \Delta(D^{\mathrm{deep}}_e)
 \subseteq\Delta(D^{\mathrm{nested}}_e).
\]
Minimizing the same variance quadratic over these two sets yields
\[
 v_{\mathrm{nested},e}\leq v_{\mathrm{deep},e}. \tag{15}
\]
Suppose at least one inequality in (15) is strict and let \(R_D>0\) be the
deep root.  For fixed positive \(R_D\), (12) and (15) imply
\[
 F_{\mathrm{nested}}(R_D)
 >F_{\mathrm{deep}}(R_D)=1-\alpha.
\]
Strict monotonicity and (14) then give
\(W_{\mathrm{nested}}<R_D=W_{\mathrm{deep}}\).  No rank condition on
predictors, interiority of a weight vector, or limiting argument is used.

 \qquad
 (v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,0)},v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,1)})

The exact frontiers are the unique positive roots of

 \left\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt{v_{s,(1,0)}})-1\right\}
 \left\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt{3/200})-1\right\}=\frac{19}{20}.

They satisfy the outward-rounded enclosures

%%% FIELD proof-exact-witness
The definition of \(\mathsf I_*\) gives \(\mathcal E=\{(1,0),(1,1)\}\), so
the index set is nonempty.  Under nested support the eligible row-cell sets
are
\[
 \mathcal C^{\mathrm{nested}}_{(1,0)}
 =\{(1,2),(2,2),(3,2),(4,2)\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C^{\mathrm{nested}}_{(1,1)}
 =\{(1,3),(3,3),(4,3)\}.
\]
Under deep support the first set loses only \((2,2)\), while the second set is
unchanged.  In either rule, the two sets are disjoint because their calendar
times differ.  Moreover, \(\Sigma_Q=R_Q^{\top}R_Q\) is diagonal, with
diagonal entries one on the time-\(2\) cells and \(1/100\) on the time-\(3\)
cells; its smallest and largest eigenvalues are therefore respectively
\(1/100\) and one.  The remaining subclass conditions
\(L=\delta=0\) are part of the witness definition.

The variance minimizations in \texttt{prop:strict-witness-gap}, together with
\texttt{def:coordinate-minimum-variance}, give
\[
 v_{\mathrm{nested},(1,0)}=\frac43,
 \quad v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,0)}=\frac32,
 \quad
 v_{\mathrm{nested},(1,1)}=v_{\mathrm{deep},(1,1)}=\frac3{200}. \tag{16}
\]
The hypotheses of \texttt{thm:disjoint-diagonal-exact-frontier} now hold for
both rules.  Substituting (16) and \(1-\alpha=19/20\) in (8) gives the two
displayed root equations.  Since \(4/3<3/2\), the strict-coordinate conclusion
of that theorem proves
\[
 W_{\mathrm{nested}}<W_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{17}
\]

For completeness, the numerical assertions are finite interval statements,
not floating-point premises.  Outward-rounded rational evaluation of
\(\Phi(x)=\tfrac12+(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_0^x e^{-t^2/2}\,dt\), using rational
Taylor enclosures with the next-term remainder after range subdivision, gives
the following rational product enclosures at the four rational endpoints:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 (s,R)&
 \prod_{e\in\mathcal E}\{2\Phi(R/\sqrt{v_{s,e}})-1\}\\ \hline
 (\mathrm{nested},2.263171468)&
 [0.9499999999845,\,0.9499999999847]\\
 (\mathrm{nested},2.263171469)&
 [0.9500000000857,\,0.9500000000859]\\
 (\mathrm{deep},2.400455838)&
 [0.9499999999829,\,0.9499999999832]\\
 (\mathrm{deep},2.400455839)&
 [0.9500000000783,\,0.9500000000787].
\end{array} \tag{18}
\]
All endpoints in (18) are decimal representations of rationals; the algebraic
square roots are enclosed by exact rational squaring before the integral
evaluation.  Each lower evaluation is below \(19/20\), and each upper
evaluation is above \(19/20\).  Strict monotonicity of the root functions
therefore gives the two asserted width intervals.  Outward subtraction gives
\[
 [2.400455838-2.263171469,\,
   2.400455839-2.263171468]
 =[0.137284369,0.137284371],
\]
which proves the certified positive gap and the reported approximations.
