%%% FIELD proof-strict-gap-sufficient
Let a valid \(\mathsf{GapCert}\) be given and abbreviate
\[
 p_N=c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N,
 \qquad
 q_D=d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D.
\]
By \texttt{def:gap-certificate}, \(y_N\) satisfies
\(B_{\mathrm{nested}}y_N=d_{\mathrm{nested}}\) and
\(y_N\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nested}}\).  Hence it is feasible for the
standard-form nested program in \texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}, and
therefore
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\le p_N. \tag{1}
\]
The exposing identities in \texttt{def:facial-dual} imply that every feasible
deep vector lies in \(F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}\).  The certificate also gives
\[
 s_D:=c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D
 \in F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}^*.
\]
Consequently, for every deep feasible vector \(y\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}y-q_D
 &=\bigl(c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D\bigr)^{\top}y
   +\lambda_D^{\top}(B_{\mathrm{deep}}y-d_{\mathrm{deep}})\\
 &=s_D^{\top}y\ge0.
 \end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
The last inequality is precisely the definition of the dual cone.  Taking the
infimum in (2), and using the equality between the standard-form objective and
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\), yields
\[
 q_D\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{3}
\]
No full-dimensional Slater condition or rank condition on
\(B_{\mathrm{deep}}\) is used in (2).

The certified interval inclusions and their strict separation give
\[
 p_N\le \sup I_N<\inf I_D\le q_D. \tag{4}
\]
Combining (1), (4), and (3) proves
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \le p_N<q_D
 \le\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}},
\]
and hence \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\).  All equalities
and cone memberships preceding (4) are exact algebraic statements; outward
rational rounding is used only for the two objective enclosures.  Thus the
argument remains valid when weights vanish, constraints are redundant, or a
Lorentz block lies on a proper face.
%%% FIELD statement-facial-reduction
Let \(\mathcal K_0\) be a finite-dimensional closed convex cone and let
\(\mathcal H_0\) be an affine space with
\(\mathcal H_0\cap\mathcal K_0\ne\varnothing\).  There is a finite strict facial
reduction sequence whose successive orthogonal exposures reach the minimal
face \(\mathcal F_0\) of \(\mathcal K_0\) containing
\(\mathcal H_0\cap\mathcal K_0\), and
\(\mathcal H_0\cap\operatorname{ri}\mathcal F_0\ne\varnothing\).  Replacing
\(\mathcal K_0\) by \(\mathcal F_0\) preserves
the feasible set; for a conic linear objective with finite primal value, the
dual over \(\mathcal F_0^*\) has the same value and attains it.
%%% FIELD statement-effective-rcf
For a first-order formula over a real closed field whose atoms are polynomial
sign conditions with integer coefficients, effective quantifier elimination
terminates, produces an equivalent quantifier-free formula, and can produce
real-algebraic sample points for every nonempty sign condition.  With a fixed
number of quantifier blocks, the bit complexity and the degrees and coefficient
heights of the resulting algebraic representations are bounded by the input
bit length raised to an exponent polynomial in the block dimensions; exact
arithmetic and sign determination at the represented real-algebraic points are
effective within the same singly-exponential-in-dimension envelope.
%%% FIELD statement-degenerate-iff-certificate
For every finite rational input \(\mathsf{Data}\),
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{a valid finite }\mathsf{GapCert}\text{ exists}.
\]
There is an exact terminating construction and verifier using real-closed-field
quantifier elimination, exact polynomial reduction, and exact sign
determination on \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm{rep}}\); outward-rounded rational
arithmetic is used only for the two objective enclosures.  If \(d\) is the
total number of real variables in the two standard-form programs,
\(B\) is the binary input length, and
\(\Delta=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}>0\), put
\[
 \log_2^+(1/\Delta)=\max\{0,\log_2(1/\Delta)\}.
\]
For a universal constant \(\kappa_{\mathrm{alg}}>0\), the construction and
interval separation use
at most
\[
 \bigl(B+\lceil\log_2^+(1/\Delta)\rceil+1\bigr)^{2^{\kappa_{\mathrm{alg}}d}}
\]
bit operations.  The equivalence and algorithm require neither unique
optimizers, positive donor weights, independent predictor columns, ordinary
Slater feasibility, nor strict complementarity, and they cover tight and
collapsed Lorentz-cone blocks.  The positive part in the displayed complexity
bound is necessary because the model imposes no normalization forcing
\(\Delta\le1\).
%%% FIELD proof-degenerate-iff-certificate
Write \(P_s\) for the standard-form program in
\texttt{def:standard-conic-encoding}.  We first record two elementary facts
specific to these programs.

First, feasibility is common to the two support rules.  If the nested program
is feasible, then for every treated unit \(j\) its horizon-\(K\) simplex
\(\Delta(D_{jK})\) is nonempty.  Selecting any element of \(D_{jK}\) at every
horizon gives a feasible deep weight array, since the deep rule uses that same
set at all horizons.  Conversely, \(D_{jK}\subseteq D_{jh}\) for
\(h\le K\), because
\(T_i>T_j+K\) implies \(T_i>T_j+h\); hence deep feasibility implies nested
feasibility.  Thus the two programs are either both infeasible or both
feasible.  In particular, a strict inequality between their extended values
forces both to be feasible.

Second, whenever they are feasible, both values are finite and attained.  For
a fixed support rule, eliminate \(u\), \(v\), and \(g\) from
\texttt{def:bonferroni-socp}.  The resulting objective on the weight array is
\[
 G_s(w)=\max_{(j,h)\in\mathcal E}
 \left\{
 L\left\|x_j-\sum_iw_{jh,i}x_i\right\|_2
 +\delta+z_B\|R_Qa_{jh}(w)\|_2
 \right\}. \tag{5}
\]
Each nonempty donor simplex is closed and bounded, so their finite product
\(\mathcal F_s\) is compact.  Every affine map inside (5), the Euclidean norm,
and a finite maximum are continuous.  A minimizing sequence therefore has a
convergent subsequence in \(\mathcal F_s\), and continuity shows that its limit
attains the infimum.  Restoring the norm epigraph variables and nonnegative
slacks gives an attained optimum of \(P_s\).  Notice that this argument permits
zero weights and uses no predictor-rank condition.

We now prove the forward implication.  Assume
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}},
 \qquad
 \Delta:=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}>0. \tag{6}
\]
By the two facts above, choose a nested primal optimizer \(y_N^*\).  Apply
\texttt{lem:facial-reduction-relative-strong-duality} to the affine space
\(H_D=\{y:B_{\mathrm{deep}}y=d_{\mathrm{deep}}\}\) and the product cone
\(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{deep}}\).  It supplies an exposing chain
\[
 \mathcal K_{\mathrm{deep}}=F_0\supsetneq F_1\supsetneq\cdots
 \supsetneq F_r=F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}, \tag{7}
\]
exposing multipliers satisfying the identities in
\texttt{def:facial-dual}, and a point
\(y^\circ\in H_D\cap\operatorname{ri}F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}\).  The deep
objective is finite by (5); relative-interior strong duality therefore gives
an attained facial dual optimizer \(\lambda_D^*\) with
\[
 c_{\mathrm{deep}}-B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D^*
 \in F_{\min,\mathrm{deep}}^*,
 \qquad
 d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D^*
 =\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}. \tag{8}
\]

All objects in (7)--(8) may be chosen real algebraic.  Here is the exact
reason.  Orthant membership is a list of linear weak inequalities, zero-cone
membership is a list of polynomial equalities, and
\[
 (t,z)\in\mathcal L_q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 t\ge0\ \text{ and }\ t^2-\sum_{\nu=1}^{q-1}z_\nu^2\ge0. \tag{9}
\]
The faces of an orthant are coordinate faces.  The proper nonzero faces of a
Lorentz cone are rays, in addition to the zero face: a proper face cannot
contain an interior point, while the midpoint of two noncollinear nonzero
boundary vectors is interior by the strict triangle inequality.  A face of a
product cone is a product of faces, because decomposing a point as the sum of
its block-supported components and applying the face property puts each
component in the face.  Thus every relevant face, its relative interior, and
its dual are described by finitely many polynomial equalities and strict or
weak sign conditions with algebraic coefficients.  The finitely many face
types and chain lengths \(r\le d\) can be enumerated.  Equivalently, one can
retain the recursive orthogonal-exposure description in (7).  The requirements
\[
 z_k\in F_{k-1}^*,\quad
 z_k=B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\eta_k,\quad
 d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\eta_k=0,\quad
 F_k=F_{k-1}\cap z_k^\perp, \tag{10}
\]
together with strict face reduction, relative-interior feasibility, nested
primal optimality, and facial-dual optimality form a first-order formula over
the rationals.  Primal and dual optimality can be written with one universal
competitor block following one existential certificate block.  This formula
has a real solution by (7)--(8) and attainment.  Effective real-algebraic
elimination and sampling from
\texttt{lem:effective-real-algebraic-elimination} therefore give a solution
all of whose coordinates lie in \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm{rep}}\).  Denote the
sampled optimizers again by \(y_N^*\) and \(\lambda_D^*\).  Their objective
values
\[
 p_N:=c_{\mathrm{nested}}^{\top}y_N^*=\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}},
 \qquad
 q_D:=d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\lambda_D^*=\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}} \tag{11}
\]
are real algebraic and satisfy \(q_D-p_N=\Delta\).

Exact real-root isolation gives nested outward rational intervals around
\(p_N\) and \(q_D\).  The algorithm refines both and stops at the first exact
rational test \(\sup I_N<\inf I_D\).  This test must eventually succeed:
once both widths are less than \(\Delta/3\),
\[
 \sup I_N
 <p_N+\Delta/3
 <q_D-\Delta/3
 <\inf I_D, \tag{12}
\]
after harmless further refinement if an endpoint equality occurs.  The face
chain, the algebraic points, and the intervals at that stopping time are
exactly the fields of \(\mathsf{GapCert}\), so (6) implies existence of a valid
finite certificate.  The stopping rule itself does not require \(\Delta\) as
an additional input; \(\Delta\) only bounds the number of refinements.

Conversely, if a valid \(\mathsf{GapCert}\) exists, then
\texttt{prop:strict-gap-sufficient} gives
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}<\Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\).  This proves the
equivalence.

We next justify termination and the advertised complexity.  A chain has length
at most the dimension of the deep cone, hence at most \(d\).  Before
quantifier elimination, exact rational row reduction is applied to the linear
map
\[
 \eta\longmapsto
 \bigl(B_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\eta,\,
       d_{\mathrm{deep}}^{\top}\eta\bigr).
\]
Its range has dimension at most the number of deep primal coordinates plus
one, so each exposing multiplier can be represented during elimination by at
most \(d+1\) active coordinates; a full multiplier is recovered afterward by
setting the unused coordinates to zero in the selected column basis.  This
row reduction is valid even when the equality rows are redundant.  Storing the
elimination variables for all chain vectors, compressed exposing multipliers,
primal points, and dual points therefore uses \(O(d^2)\) real variables.  The
possibly longer displayed full multipliers have total size polynomial in
\(B\) times this bound.  The certificate-existence and optimality formula
has a fixed number of quantifier blocks, polynomially many rational atoms,
degree at most two before standard auxiliary-variable reductions, and binary
length polynomial in \(B\).  The fixed-block bound in
\texttt{lem:effective-real-algebraic-elimination} is therefore bounded by
\[
 (B+1)^{2^{C_0d}} \tag{13}
\]
for a universal constant \(C_0\); this deliberately loose envelope absorbs
every polynomial in \(d\), as well as algebraic sample construction and exact
sign determination.  Refining algebraic isolating intervals until (12) adds a
number of bit operations polynomial in the representation size and in
\(\lceil\log_2^+(1/\Delta)\rceil+1\).  Increasing the universal constant gives
\[
 \bigl(B+\lceil\log_2^+(1/\Delta)\rceil+1\bigr)^{2^{Cd}}. \tag{14}
\]
The verifier reduces every affine equality in (10) and every primal or dual
equality to an exact polynomial identity in the algebraic representations, and
uses exact sign determination for (9), orthant signs, strict face reductions,
and relative-interior signs.  It uses outward rational arithmetic only to
check the two objective enclosures and the strict rational comparison in
(12).

No part of this construction chooses an inverse, a basis of predictor columns,
a unique optimizer, or a strictly complementary solution.  A zero donor
weight is an allowed boundary point of an orthant; redundant predictors merely
repeat polynomial constraints; and tight or collapsed Lorentz blocks are
carried into the appropriate proper face in (7).  Hence all degeneracies named
in the statement are included.

Finally, without the positive part in (14), the requested expression can
decrease when \(\Delta>1\), although reading the input alone costs order
\(B\) bit operations.  Since the frozen model has no scale normalization that
forces \(\Delta\le1\), replacing \(\log_2(1/\Delta)\) by
\(\log_2^+(1/\Delta)\) is necessary and is the exact separation-sensitive
bound proved here.  This is a finite exact result; no asymptotic limit is
taken.
%%% FIELD proof-strict-witness-gap
In \(\mathsf I_*\), \(L=\delta=0\) and \(x_i=0\), so the predictor constraints
do not affect the objective.  By \texttt{def:bonferroni-socp}, after minimizing
the epigraph variables, the objective is
\[
 \Gamma_s=z_B\min_{w\in\mathcal F_s}
 \max_{h\in\{0,1\}}\|R_Qa_{1h}(w)\|_2. \tag{15}
\]
At time \(2\), every relevant diagonal entry of \(R_Q\) is one.  Under nested
support, write the early weights as \((w_2,w_3,w_4)\).  The treated coordinate
is distinct from every donor coordinate and has coefficient one, so
\[
 \|R_Qa_{1,0}(w)\|_2^2
 =1+w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2. \tag{16}
\]
The simplex restrictions give \(w_i\ge0\) and
\(w_2+w_3+w_4=1\).  Since
\[
 0\le (w_2-w_3)^2+(w_2-w_4)^2+(w_3-w_4)^2
 =3(w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2)-1,
\]
the sum of squares is at least \(1/3\), with equality if and only if
\(w_2=w_3=w_4=1/3\).  Thus the optimized early nested variance is \(4/3\).

The deep early support is \(\{3,4\}\).  For
\(w_3,w_4\ge0\) and \(w_3+w_4=1\),
\[
 \|R_Qa_{1,0}(w)\|_2^2=1+w_3^2+w_4^2
 \ge1+\frac12=\frac32, \tag{17}
\]
because \((w_3-w_4)^2\ge0\); equality holds exactly at
\((w_3,w_4)=(1/2,1/2)\).

At time \(3\), both rules use donors \(\{3,4\}\), and every relevant diagonal
entry of \(R_Q\) is \(1/10\).  Therefore
\[
 \|R_Qa_{1,1}(w)\|_2^2
 =\frac1{100}(1+w_3^2+w_4^2)
 \ge\frac1{100}\left(1+\frac12\right)=\frac3{200}, \tag{18}
\]
again with equality at equal weights.  The positive early standard deviations
\(\sqrt{4/3}\) and \(\sqrt{3/2}\) both exceed
\(\sqrt{3/200}\).  Because the feasible set is a product across horizons,
the separate equal-weight choices attain the minimum of the maximum in (15).
Using \(z_B=9/4\) gives
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 =\frac94\sqrt{\frac43}
 =\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}
 =\frac9{2\sqrt3},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}
 =\frac94\sqrt{\frac32}
 =\frac{9\sqrt6}{8}. \tag{19}
\]

The numerical enclosures follow by exact rational squaring of positive
endpoints:
\[
 2.5980^2=6.749604<\frac{27}{4}<6.75012361=2.5981^2
\]
and
\[
 2.7556^2=7.59333136<\frac{243}{32}<7.59388249=2.7557^2.
\]
Thus
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}\in[2.5980,2.5981],
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}\in[2.7556,2.7557].
\]
Subtracting outward endpoints yields
\[
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{deep}}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{nested}}
 \in[2.7556-2.5981,\,2.7557-2.5980]
 =[0.1575,0.1577]. \tag{20}
\]
Finally, \(T_2=3\) implies \(2\in D_{1,0}\) because \(3>2\), while
\(2\notin D_{1,1}=D_{1K}\) because \(3\not>3\).  Hence the nested early rule
uses unit \(2\)'s still-untreated time-\(2\) outcome, whereas the common deep
pool excludes unit \(2\) at the early horizon solely because it adopts at the
late horizon.  All supports are nonempty, every displayed variance is
strictly positive, and no limiting argument is used.
