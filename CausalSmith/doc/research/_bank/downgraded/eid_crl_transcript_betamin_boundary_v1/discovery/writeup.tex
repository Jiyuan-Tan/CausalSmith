\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} As an exact-real statistical procedure on the declared linear Gaussian perfect variance-reset class, the certificate returns the environment-labelled transitive closure \(I^{-1}\operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G)I\) and an environment-indexed encoder estimate within \(\tau\) of the parent-supported population range class, or abstains. Its per-environment sample dependence is quadratic in \(1/\tau\) and essentially linear in \(\log(1/\alpha)\), up to the displayed logarithms, and rational post-summary decisions have a proved polynomial-bit realization. The encoder output is neither asserted invertible nor a unique representative of a repeated positive eigenspace. A separate explicit bounded-kernel logistic family gives the intrinsic boundary: if a model class contains graph-changing alternatives converging to its empty-graph null in every finite-prefix total variation, every uniformly \(\alpha\)-valid randomized sequential rule returns the correct singleton empty graph there with probability at most \(\alpha\). Thus some restriction excluding such weak alternatives is necessary for high-probability finite singleton certification, while public reached gaps are one sufficient restriction on the Gaussian class; the theorem does not make the particular \(g_0,h_0\) parameters necessary, establish a minimax rate, or show failure of a named published workflow. Separately, a legal all-dimension Gaussian family has constant reached gaps but a positive unqueried equation-(22) margin that vanishes exponentially, proving no positive dimension-uniform constant lower bound on that margin is necessary and that the corresponding sufficient sample analysis is exponentially pessimistic on this family without contradicting its existential premise.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Finite singleton graph certification is not uniformly reliable at a graph-changing boundary merely because each fixed model is identifiable: a sequence of legal weak alternatives may converge to an empty-graph null on every finite data prefix. Separately, Acartürk et al.'s finite-sample analysis pays for equation (22)'s all-subset positive-eigenvalue minimum although the deterministic recovery program executes only a polynomial transcript, and its printed equation-(23) norm product is not the reciprocal lower bound supplied by actual projector geometry.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note pairs a public query-local sufficient contract for an exact-real Gaussian recover-or-abstain certificate with an intrinsic finite-return boundary showing that some model restriction must exclude graph-changing weak alternatives. The boundary concerns uniform decision validity, not comparative failure of any named algorithm.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project supplies the Gaussian certificate with public reached gaps, the cap \(B_{\mathrm{samp}}<66q_{\star}u_{\star}\), environment-labelled graph and parent-range encoder targets, explicit error bars, and polynomial-bit post-summary realization. It also supplies an explicit common-kernel logistic family, proves its intervention and source legality by a Legendre differential-operator construction, derives finite-prefix total-variation convergence, and proves the exact \(\alpha\) null singleton-return ceiling for every uniformly valid randomized adaptive sequential rule on a class containing those alternatives. This necessity statement is only the need for some exclusion; it does not privilege \(g_0,h_0\), give a minimax rate, or criticize a named workflow. The positive theorem remains confined to the Gaussian variance-reset subclass. Over the broader published class, the Gaussian witness establishes only the absence of a necessary positive dimension-uniform constant equation-(22) margin and exponential pessimism of that sufficient analysis, while preserving the source theorem's model-specific existential threshold premise.

\section{Related work}

The present Gaussian proof uses the actual subspaces and the valid reciprocal witness bound \(\rho_i(G)\); its positive certificate is a covariance-specialized result, not a theorem-level strengthening on the anchor's full smooth class, and the logistic no-return theorem is an intrinsic decision boundary rather than a claim that the anchor workflow fails. Squires et al. (2023) establish the legal linear-intervention substrate and population identifiability, while the present result addresses finite-sample transcript certification under declared observed-covariance conditions. Uhler et al. (2013) analyze observational faithfulness geometry and motivate why uniform graph recovery needs separation; the present theorem instead proves a randomized sequential finite-return ceiling in an explicit latent-interventional bounded-kernel logistic experiment, so it is neither an inclusion nor a restatement of their result. Sobczyk (2025) supplies the deterministic Hermitian spectral-projector primitive used in the post-summary bit proof; this note adapts it to zero-versus-positive clusters and composes rational rank and singular-value enclosures over the transcript. Dey et al. (2023) provide adjacent general bit-complexity results for Jordan form and spectral factorization, but are not used as a substitute for the certified Hermitian projector theorem and imply no end-to-end raw-sample complexity claim here. The explicit Gaussian family remains a source-legal witness with positive equation-(22) margin at each fixed dimension: it refutes only a necessary dimension-uniform constant lower bound and shows exponential pessimism of the equation-(22)-controlled sufficient sample bound, not the source theorem's existential threshold condition. Zhou, Shi, and Zhu (2020, supplement Appendix B) state the kernel regularity assumptions used for nonparametric score estimation: the present logistic construction verifies their B.1--B.5 using the empty finite boundary of \(\mathbb R^2\), radial logistic tail decay, uniformly bounded kernel-divergence sections (the supplement's stated stronger replacement for B.4), and bounded kernel diagonal; its proved representation \(s=L_Kf\) is their Theorem B.1 source condition at \(r=1\), for a regularizer of qualification at least one. Robins, Scheines, Spirtes, and Wasserman (2003) and Zhang and Spirtes (2003) are the closest observational pointwise-versus-uniform consistency and strong-faithfulness lineage \cite{robins2003uniform,zhang2003strong}; they establish the general need for uniform separation conditions in observational causal inference. The present result differs by giving an explicit three-environment latent-interventional family in one common bounded-kernel source class, treating adaptive randomized sampling through finite-prefix total variation, and proving the exact \(\alpha\) ceiling for a correct singleton return at the null.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:linear-gaussian-sem]\label{ass:linear-gaussian-sem}
For every \(m\in\mathcal E\), \(Z^{(m)}=B^{(m)}Z^{(m)}+\varepsilon^{(m)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (linear structural equations, \cite{squires2023linear}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-disturbances]\label{ass:gaussian-disturbances}
For every \(m\in\mathcal E\), \(\varepsilon^{(m)}\sim N(0,\Omega_m)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent Gaussian structural disturbances, \cite{squires2023linear}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dag-compatibility]\label{ass:dag-compatibility}
For distinct \(i,j\in[n]\), \(B^{(0)}_{ij}\ne0\) if and only if \(j\to i\) is an edge of \(\mathcal G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (linear SEM graph compatibility, \cite{squires2023linear}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:perfect-single-target]\label{ass:perfect-single-target}
For every \(m\in[n]\), \(B^{(m)}_{j,:}=B^{(0)}_{j,:}\) for \(j\ne I(m)\) and \(B^{(m)}_{I(m),:}=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (one stochastic perfect intervention per latent node, \cite{squires2023linear}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-reset]\label{ass:variance-reset}
For every \(m\in[n]\), \((\Omega_m)_{jj}=(\Omega_0)_{jj}\) for \(j\ne I(m)\) and \((\Omega_m)_{I(m),I(m)}\ne(\Omega_0)_{I(m),I(m)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (single-target intervention variance reset, \cite{squires2023linear}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:full-rank-mixing]\label{ass:full-rank-mixing}
The matrix \(G\) has column rank \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (injective linear observation map, \cite{acarturk2024sample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-conditioning]\label{ass:observed-conditioning}
For every \(m\in\mathcal E\), the spectrum of the fixed-coordinate covariance \(\Sigma_m\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) lies in \([\kappa^{-1},\kappa]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform covariance conditioning on the observation support, \cite{lee2026beyond}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reached-eigen-band]\label{ass:reached-eigen-band}
Every eigenvalue of every matrix occurrence \(R(M)\) in \(\mathfrak T_{\mathsf A}(P)\), including each singleton encoder query, belongs to \(\{0\}\cup[g_0,\infty)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a pre-sampling model promise on a named public computation path. It is realized with constant \(g_0\) by the uniformly conditioned bipartite family in Theorem 2 even though an unqueried subset is exponentially ill separated.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reached-projector-band]\label{ass:reached-projector-band}
Every graph-loop comparison \(c_{t,j}(P)\) in \(\mathfrak T_{\mathsf A}(P)\) belongs to \(\{0\}\cup[h_0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition is stated on actual projector geometry and is checkable from the declared population model. Coordinate-range intervention systems, including the separation family, have comparisons exactly zero or one.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-environment-sampling]\label{ass:independent-environment-sampling}
Conditional on the fixed environment labels, observations are independent within and across environments and have their corresponding laws under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent sampling across interventional environments, \cite{acarturk2024sample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:bounded-kernel-logistic-boundary-family]\label{def:bounded-kernel-logistic-boundary-family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}kernel logistic boundary family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\ell(z)=e^{-z}/(1+e^{-z})^2\) be the standard logistic density, put \(a=1/4\), and, for \(z=(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb R^2\), put \(u=\tanh(z_1/2)\), \(v=\tanh(z_2/2)\), and \(\mu(dz)=\ell(z_1)\ell(z_2)\,dz_1dz_2\).  For \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/4\), define the three-environment experiment \(P_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{log}}=(P_{\varepsilon}^{[e]}:e\in\{0,1,2\})\) by the densities, relative to \(\mu\),
\[
 w_{\varepsilon,0}=1+\varepsilon uv,\qquad
 w_{\varepsilon,1}=(1+au)(1+\varepsilon uv),\qquad
 w_{\varepsilon,2}=1+av.
\]
Equivalently, the observational root density is \(\ell(z_1)\), its second-node conditional density is \(\ell(z_2)(1+\varepsilon uv)\), intervention \(1\) replaces only the root density by \(\ell(z_1)(1+au)\), and intervention \(2\) replaces only the second-node conditional by \(\ell(z_2)(1+av)\).  The intervention-target map is the identity.  Let \(\mathcal G_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{log}}\) be the empty DAG when \(\varepsilon=0\) and the DAG with the single arrow \(1\to2\) when \(\varepsilon>0\), and set \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{log}}=\{P_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{log}}:0\le\varepsilon\le1/4\}\).

For the common source class, let \(\phi_j=\sqrt{2j+1}\,P_j\), where \(P_j\) is the degree-\(j\) Legendre polynomial, and put \(A_j=1+j(j+1)\), \(\psi_{ij}(z)=\phi_i(u)\phi_j(v)\), and
\[
 k_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z')=
 \sum_{i,j\ge0}A_i^{-6}A_j^{-6}\psi_{ij}(z)\psi_{ij}(z'),
 \qquad
 K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z')=k_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z')I_2.
\]
For \(C<\infty\), let \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{src}}(K_{\mathrm{Leg}},C)\) be the class of three-environment laws \(Q=(Q^{[e]})_{e=0}^2\) with positive continuously differentiable Lebesgue densities \(q_e\) and scores \(s_e=\nabla\log q_e\) such that: \(\sup_z\operatorname{tr}K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z)\le C\); \(\sup_z\sum_{r=1}^2\|\partial_{z_r}K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal H_{K_{\mathrm{Leg}}}}^2\le C\); \(\int\exp(\|s_e(z)\|_2)Q^{[e]}(dz)\le C\) for every \(e\); for every \(e\) and \(i,j\), \(q_e(z)\sqrt{|K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z)_{ij}|}=o(\|z\|_2^{1-d/2})=o(1)\) as \(\|z\|_2\to\infty\), with \(d=2\); for \(\xi_z=\operatorname{div}_z K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,\cdot)^\top\) and \(\bar\xi_e=\mathbb E_{Q^{[e]}}\xi_Z\), one has \(\sup_z\|\xi_z-\bar\xi_e\|_{\mathcal H_{K_{\mathrm{Leg}}}}\le C\) (the bounded stronger replacement for the Bernstein condition); and, additionally, for every \(e\), there is \(f_e\in\mathcal H_{K_{\mathrm{Leg}}}\), equivalently with two components in \(\mathcal H_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}}}\), with \(\|f_e\|_{\mathcal H_{K_{\mathrm{Leg}}}}\le C\) and
\[
 s_e(z)=L_{K_{\mathrm{Leg}},Q^{[e]}}f_e(z)
 :=\int K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z')f_e(z')\,Q^{[e]}(dz').
\] This last display is the \(r=1\) source condition, used with a regularizer of qualification at least one. For score differences \(d_m^{\mathrm{log}}=s_m-s_0\), define the generic score-correlation matrices \(R_m^{\mathrm{log}}=\mathbb E_{P_{\varepsilon}^{[0]}}[d_m^{\mathrm{log}}(Z)d_m^{\mathrm{log}}(Z)^\top]\) and \(R_A^{\mathrm{log}}=\sum_{m\in A}R_m^{\mathrm{log}}\); these definitions do not invoke the Gaussian covariance formula.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:covariance-to-correlation]\label{lem:covariance-to-correlation}
For every stage \(r\ge r_0\), if \(\max_{m\in\mathcal E}\|S_{m,r}-\Sigma_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le a_r\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\), then every \(S_{m,r}\) is invertible as an \(n\times n\) matrix and \(\max_{m\in[n]}\|\widehat R_{m,r}-R_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le40\kappa^4a_r=e_r\). Consequently, simultaneously for every \(M\subseteq[n]\), \(\|\widehat R_r(M)-R(M)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le |M|e_r\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(r\ge r_0\) and suppose
\[
 \max_{m\in\mathcal E}\|S_{m,r}-\Sigma_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le a_r\le (2\kappa)^{-1}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}observed\mbox{-}conditioning}\) gives
\(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_m)\ge\kappa^{-1}\).  Weyl's inequality, proved directly from the Rayleigh variational formula, therefore gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(S_{m,r})
 \ge \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_m)-\|S_{m,r}-\Sigma_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \ge (2\kappa)^{-1}>0.
\]
Thus \(S_{m,r}\) is invertible and
\(\|S_{m,r}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\kappa\).  Since
\(S^{-1}-\Sigma^{-1}=S^{-1}(\Sigma-S)\Sigma^{-1}\),
\[
 \|S_{m,r}^{-1}-\Theta_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le (2\kappa)a_r\kappa=2\kappa^2a_r.
\]
Put \(\widehat\Delta_{m,r}=S_{0,r}^{-1}-S_{m,r}^{-1}\).  By the preceding inequality and the triangle inequality,
\[
 \|\widehat\Delta_{m,r}-\Delta_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le4\kappa^2a_r,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\Delta_{m,r}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le4\kappa,
 \qquad
 \|\Delta_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2\kappa.
\]
The last two bounds use \(\|S_{m,r}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\kappa\) and \(\|\Theta_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\kappa\), respectively.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}primitive\mbox{-}correlations}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat R_{m,r}-R_m
 &=\widehat\Delta_{m,r}(S_{0,r}-\Sigma_0)\widehat\Delta_{m,r}\\
 &\quad +(\widehat\Delta_{m,r}-\Delta_m)\Sigma_0\widehat\Delta_{m,r}
       +\Delta_m\Sigma_0(\widehat\Delta_{m,r}-\Delta_m).
\end{aligned}
\]
Submultiplicativity and \(\|\Sigma_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\kappa\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat R_{m,r}-R_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le16\kappa^2a_r+16\kappa^4a_r+8\kappa^4a_r\\
 &\le40\kappa^4a_r=e_r,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second line uses \(\kappa\ge1\).  Finally, for every \(M\subseteq[n]\), the definitions of \(R(M)\) and \(\widehat R_r(M)\) and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \|\widehat R_r(M)-R(M)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\sum_{m\in M}\|\widehat R_{m,r}-R_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le |M|e_r.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the deterministic bridge from observable covariance confidence balls to every spectral decision used by the certificate. \textit{Gap.} Acartürk et al. control estimated score correlations; the Gaussian specialization here must include covariance inversion and a fully empirical primitive matrix. \textit{Consumer.} It provides the error budget used by interval ranks, projectors, and encoder bars in the official finite-sample implementation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:projector-geometry]\label{prop:projector-geometry}
For any \(S,T\subseteq[n]\), all projectors below act on the fixed space \(\mathbb R^n\), and \[\left\|P_{\operatorname{span}\{A^{-\top}e_j:j\in T\}}P_{\operatorname{span}\{A^{-\top}e_j:j\in S\}^{\perp}}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\subseteq S.\] For every witness label \(i\in T\setminus S\), the left-hand side is at least \(\rho_i(G)=1/(\|A^{-\top}e_i\|_2\|Ae_i\|_2)=1/(\|H^{\top}e_i\|_2\|Ge_i\|_2)>0\), and hence at least \(\rho^{\ast}(G)\). The source's printed quantity \(\gamma^{\ast}(G)=\min_i\|H_{i,:}\|_2\|G_{:,i}\|_2\) is a norm product, not this reciprocal lower bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(A=U^{\top}G\) is invertible under \(\mathrm{ass{:}full\mbox{-}rank\mbox{-}mixing}\), define
\(h_i=A^{-\top}e_i\) and \(g_i=Ae_i\).  They are dual:
\[
 h_i^{\top}g_j=e_i^{\top}A^{-1}Ae_j=\mathbf 1\{i=j\}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal U_T=\operatorname{span}\{h_i:i\in T\}\) and
\(\mathcal W_S=\operatorname{span}\{h_i:i\in S\}^{\perp}\).  If \(T\subseteq S\), then \(\mathcal U_T\subseteq\mathcal W_S^{\perp}\), so
\(P_{\mathcal U_T}P_{\mathcal W_S}=0\).  Conversely, take \(i\in T\setminus S\).  Duality gives \(g_i\in\mathcal W_S\), whereas \(h_i\in\mathcal U_T\).  Testing the projector product on \(g_i/\|g_i\|_2\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|P_{\mathcal U_T}P_{\mathcal W_S}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\ge \left\|P_{\mathcal U_T}\frac{g_i}{\|g_i\|_2}\right\|_2\\
 &\ge \left|\left\langle
       \frac{h_i}{\|h_i\|_2},\frac{g_i}{\|g_i\|_2}
       \right\rangle\right|
 =\frac{1}{\|A^{-\top}e_i\|_2\|Ae_i\|_2}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the equivalence and the stated witness bound.  Since \(U\) is an isometry from \(\mathbb R^n\) into \(\mathcal S\),
\[
 H^{\top}e_i=UA^{-\top}e_i,
 \qquad Ge_i=UAe_i,
\]
and hence the denominator above equals
\(\|H^{\top}e_i\|_2\|Ge_i\|_2\).  Taking the minimum over witness labels gives the lower bound \(\rho^{\ast}(G)\).  The declared formula for the printed \(\gamma^{\ast}(G)\) is instead the norm product itself; it is therefore not the reciprocal lower bound just derived.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The graph transcript needs a correct zero-versus-positive projector characterization under nonorthogonal mixing. \textit{Gap.} The printed norm-product expression in the closest source can exceed one and a subset-pseudoinverse identity used with it is false; this proposition uses dual vectors and actual projector geometry. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents false edge decisions in the population proof and supplies the geometric quantity to be enclosed numerically.

\begin{theorem}[thm:promised-transcript-certificate]\label{thm:promised-transcript-certificate}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathsf A}(g_0,h_0,\kappa)\), the support batch satisfies \(\Pr_P(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{sup}})=1\). On \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{sup}}\), the procedure recovers the deterministic support \(\mathcal S\) and its canonical frame \(U\) exactly, samples without inversion for \(r<r_0\), and begins covariance inversion and all spectral and projector tests in \(\mathbb R^n\) only at \(r_0=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\). Moreover, \[\Pr_P\!\left(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{sup}},\ \widehat{\mathcal G}=\operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G_{\mathcal E})=I^{-1}\operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G)I,\ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{enc}}(\widehat H,P)\le\tau,\ \text{and return occurs by }B_{\mathrm{samp}}\text{ cumulative post-support samples per environment}\right)\ge1-\alpha.\] On this event the observational environment uses at most \(B_0=n+B_{\mathrm{samp}}\) observations including the disjoint support batch, every intervention environment uses at most \(B_{\mathrm{samp}}\), and the combined budget is at most \(B_{\mathrm{tot}}=n+(n+1)B_{\mathrm{samp}}\). At any certified stage \(r\ge r_0\) with primitive radius \(e_r\), there exists \(H^{\star}\) with \(H^{\star}_{m,:}{}^{\top}\in\mathcal S\), \(\|H^{\star}_{m,:}\|_2=1\), and \(U^{\top}H^{\star}_{m,:}{}^{\top}\in\operatorname{col}(R_m)\) for every \(m\), such that \(\|\widehat H-H^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|\widehat H-H^{\star}\|_{\mathrm F}\le4\sqrt n\,e_r/g_0\), and \((H^{\star}G)_{m,I(j)}=0\) whenever \(j\ne m\) and \(j\not\to m\) in \(\mathcal G_{\mathcal E}\). No eigenvalue margin for a subset absent from \(\mathfrak T_{\mathsf A}(P)\) appears in the guarantee.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(i=I(m)\) for the latent target of intervention label \(m\).  We first recover the common support.  The vector \(Y^{(0)}\) is a nondegenerate Gaussian in \(\mathbb R^n\): \(A\) is invertible by full-column-rank mixing and every diagonal entry of \(\Omega_0\) is positive.  The determinant of the matrix formed from \(n\) independent copies of \(Y^{(0)}\) is a nonzero polynomial, whose zero set has Lebesgue measure zero.  Hence the corresponding copies of \(X^{(0)}=UY^{(0)}\) span \(\mathcal S\) almost surely and
\[
 \Pr_P(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{sup}})=1.
\]
The canonical-frame rule is deterministic in \(\mathcal S\), so it returns exactly \(U\) on this event.  The construction of \(\mathsf C\) performs no inversion for \(r<r_0\).  Since \(N_{r_0}\ge n\), the same determinant argument, a finite union over environments, and a countable union over \(r\ge r_0\) show that all later empirical covariance matrices are positive definite almost surely.  Thus every permitted inverse is an \(n\)-dimensional inverse.

For simultaneous covariance coverage, define
\[
 W_{m,r}=\Sigma_m^{-1/2}S_{m,r}\Sigma_m^{-1/2}-I_n.
\]
For a fixed unit vector \(v\), \(N_rv^{\top}(W_{m,r}+I_n)v\) has the \(\chi^2_{N_r}\) law.  Its moment generating function and Chernoff's inequality give
\[
 \Pr\!\left(\left|\frac{\chi^2_N}{N}-1\right|>
 2\sqrt{t/N}+2t/N\right)\le2e^{-t}.
 \tag{1}
\]
A maximal \(1/4\)-separated subset of the unit sphere is a \(1/4\)-net and has at most \(9^n\) elements, by the disjoint-ball volume argument.  For symmetric \(W\), approximation of a maximizing Rayleigh quotient by this net gives
\[
 \|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\max_{v\text{ in the net}}|v^{\top}Wv|.
 \tag{2}
\]
Apply (1)--(2) with \(t=t_r\), take a union over the net and the \(n+1\) environments, and use \(\|\Sigma_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\kappa\).  This produces events \(\mathcal C_r\) satisfying
\[
 \Pr_P(\mathcal C_r^c)\le\alpha2^{-r-1},\qquad
 \max_{m\in\mathcal E}\|S_{m,r}-\Sigma_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le a_r.
 \tag{3}
\]
The retained prefixes may be nested; no independence between stages is used.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal C=\bigcap_{r\ge r_0}\mathcal C_r,qquad
 \Pr_P(\mathcal C)\ge1-
 \sum_{r\ge r_0}\alpha2^{-r-1}\ge1-\alpha.
 \tag{4}
\]

We next identify the population ranges that the observable matrices encode.  In latent coordinates, let \(b_i\) be row \(i\) of \(B^{(0)}\), let \(\epsilon_i=Z_i-b_i^{\top}Z\), and write the observational and reset variances as \(\omega_i>0\) and \(\widetilde\omega_i>0\).  Cancellation of all unchanged conditional densities in the intervention-to-observation likelihood ratio yields
\[
 \nabla_z\log\frac{p_m^Z(z)}{p_0^Z(z)}
 =-\frac{\epsilon_i}{\omega_i}b_i+
 \left(\frac{\epsilon_i}{\omega_i}-\frac{z_i}{\widetilde\omega_i}\right)e_i.
 \tag{5}
\]
The disturbance \(\epsilon_i\) is independent of its parental predictor.  If \(b_i\ne0\), that predictor has positive variance and the two coefficients in (5) have nonsingular covariance; if \(b_i=0\), the variance reset \(\widetilde\omega_i\ne\omega_i\) makes the coefficient of \(e_i\) nondegenerate.  Transforming scores under \(Y=AZ\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{col}(R_m)=A^{-\top}\operatorname{span}\{e_i,b_i\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The range of a sum of positive-semidefinite matrices is the span of their ranges, because its null space is the intersection of their null spaces.

If \(V\) is ancestral, (6) gives
\[
 \operatorname{col}(R(V))=A^{-\top}\operatorname{span}\{e_i:i\in V\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
For \(k\in V\), if \(k\) is a leaf of the induced graph, no remaining parent vector has a nonzero \(k\)-coordinate, and \(\operatorname{rank}R(V\setminus\{k\})=|V|-1\).  If \(k\) is not a leaf, a remaining child contributes a parent vector with a nonzero \(k\)-coordinate; subtracting the already present other coordinate directions recovers \(e_k\), and the rank is \(|V|\).  Removing a leaf preserves ancestry.  Induction shows that the public smallest-label rule removes the smallest labelled leaf at each step, and reversing the removal order gives the population topological order.

In a graph loop, the recorded set \(D_t\) is descendant-closed by induction.  The set \(V_j\setminus D_t\) is therefore ancestral.  The same leaf calculation shows that deleting \(\pi_t\) either deletes its dual coordinate, when no surviving child exists, or leaves that coordinate in the range, when a surviving child exists.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}projector\mbox{-}geometry}\) makes the projector product zero exactly in the latter case.  The update of \(D_t\), first in increasing \(j\) and then in decreasing \(t\), records exactly all descendants.  Consequently the population graph output is
\[
 \operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G_{\mathcal E})
 =I^{-1}\operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G)I.
 \tag{8}
\]

Now work on \(\mathcal C\) at a stage satisfying \(a_r\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}covariance\mbox{-}to\mbox{-}correlation}\) gives, for every queried set \(M\),
\[
 b_M:=\|\widehat R_r(M)-R(M)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le |M|e_r.
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(b_M\le g_0/4\), the min--max formula and \(|v^{\top}(\widehat R_r(M)-R(M))v|\le b_M\) show that each ordered empirical eigenvalue is within \(b_M\) of its population counterpart; the promised spectrum \(\{0\}\cup[g_0,\infty)\) therefore certifies the exact rank.  Let \(P_M\) and \(\widehat P_M\) project onto the population positive range and the empirical eigenvectors above \(g_0/2\).  Projection of the perturbed invariant-subspace equation onto \(\operatorname{null}(R(M))\), followed by the equal-rank projector identity obtained from the singular-value decomposition of their cross-Gram matrix, gives
\[
 \|\widehat P_M-P_M\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{b_M}{g_0-b_M}\le\frac{2b_M}{g_0}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The identical bound holds for null projectors.  Hence, for the empirical graph comparison \(\widehat c_{t,j}\), the product triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\widehat c_{t,j}-c_{t,j}(P)|
 \le\frac{2(b_{V_t}+b_{M_{t,j}})}{g_0}
 \le\frac{4ne_r}{g_0}.
 \tag{11}
\]
If all previous decisions agree with the population transcript, the next query is an occurrence of \(\mathfrak T_{\mathsf A}(P)\).  The two reached-band assumptions therefore apply at that query.  Equations (9)--(11), the smallest-certified-zero rule, and induction through each restarted order and graph loop prove that every completed empirical transcript is the population transcript.  No condition has been invoked for a subset absent from that transcript.

For a singleton, (10) with its sharper denominator gives
\[
 \|\widehat P_m-P_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{e_r}{g_0-e_r}.
\]
For a unit empirical-range vector \(\widehat v_m\), normalize \(P_m\widehat v_m\) when \(e_r<g_0/2\).  The elementary normalization inequality then gives
\[
 \|\widehat v_m-v_m\|_2
 \le2\|(I-P_m)\widehat v_m\|_2
 \le\frac{2e_r}{g_0-e_r}\le\frac{4e_r}{g_0}.
 \tag{12}
\]
When \(e_r\ge g_0/2\), the same displayed upper bound follows from the distance at most two between unit vectors and the nonempty range in (6).  Map these vectors isometrically into \(\mathcal S\) by \(U\) and stack the rows.  Then
\[
 \|\widehat H-H^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|\widehat H-H^{\star}\|_{\mathrm F}
 \le\frac{4\sqrt n\,e_r}{g_0}.
 \tag{13}
\]
If \(v_m=A^{-\top}q_m\), (6) says that \(q_m\) is supported on target \(I(m)\) and its parents, and
\[
 (v_m^{\top}U^{\top})G=v_m^{\top}A=q_m^{\top}.
\]
After relabelling by \(I\), this is precisely the zero pattern required in \(\mathrm{def{:}encoder\mbox{-}loss}\).  Thus (13) bounds \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{enc}}(\widehat H,P)\).

It remains to verify the corrected terminal cap.  Put \(b=b_{\star}\), \(q=q_{\star}\), \(u=u_{\star}\), and \(B=B_{\mathrm{samp}}=2^{r_B}\).  Since \(b\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\),
\[
 q=\frac{64\kappa^2}{b^2}\ge256\kappa^4\ge256,
 \qquad u\ge\log(16q)>4.
\]
Also \(u\ge t_0\ge n\log9\), so \(32qu\ge n\).  The power-of-two definition and \(qu>1\) yield
\[
 32qu\le B<2\lceil32qu\rceil\le64qu+2<66qu.
 \tag{14}
\]
Because \(t_{r_B}=t_0+\log(2B)\), (14) gives
\[
 t_{r_B}<t_0+\log(132qu)
 =u+\log\!\left(\frac{132}{16}u\right)\le2u.
 \tag{15}
\]
The last inequality follows because \(u>4\) and the increasing function \(x-\log((132/16)x)\) is already positive at \(x=4\).  Combining \(B\ge32qu\) with (15),
\[
 \frac{t_{r_B}}{B}\le\frac1{16q}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Using \(\sqrt q=8\kappa/b\), (16), and \(b\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\), we obtain the quantitative slack
\[
 a_{r_B}
 \le4\kappa\left(\frac1{4\sqrt q}+\frac1{16q}\right)
 =\frac b8+\frac{b^2}{256\kappa}
 \le\frac{65}{512}b<\frac b7.
 \tag{17}
\]
Therefore
\[
 e_{r_B}=40\kappa^4a_{r_B}<\frac{40\kappa^4b}{7}
 \le\frac{e_{\star}}7.
 \tag{18}
\]
In particular,
\[
 ne_{r_B}<\frac{g_0h_0}{112}\le\frac{g_0}{112},
 \qquad
 \frac{4ne_{r_B}}{g_0}<\frac{h_0}{28},
 \qquad
 \frac{4\sqrt n\,e_{r_B}}{g_0}<\frac{\tau}{7}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Thus every rank and comparison interval strictly separates its two promised bands and the encoder gate holds.  On \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{sup}}\cap\mathcal C\), whose probability is at least \(1-\alpha\) by (4), the procedure returns by stage \(r_B\), (8) is its graph, and (13) gives its encoder loss.  The sample accounting is \(B_0=n+B_{\mathrm{samp}}\) in the observational environment, at most \(B_{\mathrm{samp}}\) in each intervention environment, and \(B_{\mathrm{tot}}=n+(n+1)B_{\mathrm{samp}}\) in total.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:separation-family]\label{thm:separation-family}
For every \(n\ge4\), the explicit model \(\mathfrak F_n\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathsf A}(1/40,1/2,32)\). Every fixed-coordinate covariance has spectrum in \([1/32,32]\); the transcript \(\mathfrak T_{\mathsf A}(P)\) has exactly \(n^2+2n-2\) matrix occurrences; every reached positive eigenvalue is at least \(1/40\); every reached graph comparison is zero or one; and both the printed source scale and the valid reciprocal scale satisfy \(\gamma^{\ast}(G)=\rho^{\ast}(G)=1\). The fixed source thresholds obey \(\gamma_{\mathrm{src}}=1/4\in(0,\gamma^{\ast}(G))\), whereas \(\eta_{\mathrm{src}}=1/80\) does not satisfy the source rank-threshold inequality because the subset \(\{3,4\}\) is not reached and
\[
0<\eta^{\ast}(P)\le\lambda_{\min}^{+}(R(\{3,4\}))\le2^{-2n-1}<1/80.
\]
Thus the family does not contradict the source theorem's existential premise that a threshold in \((0,\eta^{\ast}(P))\) may be chosen. Instead, it exhibits constant reached gaps together with a positive all-subset margin that tends to zero exponentially with \(n\). The family also satisfies every non-margin condition of Acartürk et al.'s graph theorem: the induced support-coordinate densities and causal conditionals are strictly positive and continuously differentiable, there is one distinct single-target intervention per node, their Assumption 1 parent-ratio condition holds, and the empirical Gaussian covariance score-difference estimator satisfies their consistency condition (15).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\epsilon=2^{-n}\).  The structural matrix of \(\mathfrak F_n\) has nonzero rows only at labels \(3\) and \(4\), with parent vectors \(b_3=(1,1)\) and \(b_4=(1,1+\epsilon)\).  Thus \((B^{(m)})^2=0\) and \(\|B^{(m)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|B^{(m)}\|_{\mathrm F}<3\) in every environment, because an intervention only deletes one row.  By the structural equations and the perfect-intervention construction,
\[
\Sigma_m=(I-B^{(m)})^{-1}\Omega_m(I-B^{(m)})^{-\top},
\qquad
(I-B^{(m)})^{-1}=I+B^{(m)},
\]
and \(I\preceq\Omega_m\preceq2I\).  Therefore
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_m)
 \le2\|I+B^{(m)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2<32,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_m)
 \ge\|(I-B^{(m)})^{\top}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-2}>1/16.
 \tag{20}
\]
In particular every fixed-coordinate covariance has spectrum in \([1/32,32]\).  The construction directly verifies the linear Gaussian DAG equations, graph compatibility, one perfect variance-reset intervention at each target, and full-rank mixing with \(G=I_n\).  Independent streams with these environment laws satisfy the sampling condition.

We next compute the primitive matrices from their definition.  For an intervention at a root \(i\in\{1,2,5,\ldots,n\}\), only the corresponding noise precision changes from \(1\) to \(1/2\).  Hence \(\Delta_i=e_ie_i^{\top}/2\), and because \(\mathbb E[Z_i^2]=1\),
\[
 R_i=\Delta_i\Sigma_0\Delta_i=\frac14e_ie_i^{\top}.
 \tag{21}
\]
For a child \(c\in\{3,4\}\) with parent vector \(b\), write \(s=b^{\top}(Z_1,Z_2)\) and \(E=Z_c-s\).  Under the observational law, \(s\) and \(E\) are independent, \(\mathbb E[E^2]=1\), and \(\mathbb E[s^2]=\|b\|_2^2\).  Direct subtraction of the observational and intervened Gaussian precision matrices gives
\[
d_c(Z)=(-bE,(E-s)/2)
\]
on the parent coordinates and coordinate \(c\).  Taking the observational second moment therefore yields
\[
 R_c=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 bb^{\top}&-b/2\\
 -b^{\top}/2&(1+\|b\|_2^2)/4
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{col}(R_c)=\operatorname{span}\{b,e_c\}.
 \tag{22}
\]
In the orthonormal basis \(b/\|b\|_2,e_c\), put \(B_0=\|b\|_2^2\in[2,5]\).  The nonzero block in (22) has determinant \(B_0^2/4\) and trace \((5B_0+1)/4\).  Since the smaller eigenvalue of a positive-definite \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix is at least its determinant divided by its trace,
\[
 \lambda_{min}^{+}(R_c)
 \ge\frac{B_0^2}{5B_0+1}\ge\frac4{11},
 \qquad
 R_c\succeq\frac4{11}P_{\operatorname{span}\{b,e_c\}}.
 \tag{23}
\]

We now bound every subset sum except the exceptional child pair.  Root-only sets have positive eigenvalues \(1/4\), and a child singleton has positive eigenvalues at least \(4/11\) by (23).  If a subset contains a root \(i\in\{1,2\}\) and a child with parent vector \(b\), then on the parent plane
\[
 \lambda_{min}^{+}(P_{e_i}+P_b)
 =1-\frac{|e_i^{\top}b|}{\|b\|_2}
 \ge1-\frac2{\sqrt5}>\frac1{10}.
\]
Combining (21) and (23) shows that the parent-plane eigenvalues are greater than \(1/40\), while (23) supplies the orthogonal child coordinate with coefficient at least \(4/11\).  Adding the other child cannot lower any existing Rayleigh quotient and supplies its new orthogonal child coordinate; adding the other root supplies \((1/4)I_2\) on the parent plane.  These cases exhaust every nonempty subset of \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) other than \(\{3,4\}\).  Each label at least \(5\) contributes the orthogonal summand \(e_je_j^{\top}/4\).  Consequently every positive eigenvalue of every such subset sum is at least \(1/40\).

The population leaf-removal order under the smallest-label rule is
\[
 (3,4,1,2,5,6,\ldots,n),
\]
and hence the reversed topological order is \(\pi=(n,n-1,\ldots,5,2,1,4,3)\).  Indeed, in the first order round, deleting either child lowers the range dimension by one, whereas deleting either root leaves both linearly independent parent vectors \(b_3,b_4\) and does not lower it.  The smallest admissible child is \(3\).  After label \(3\) is removed, the same argument selects \(4\); thereafter every remaining primitive has a distinct coordinate range and the smallest-label rule gives \(1,2,5,\ldots,n\).

In the first order round, deleting one label retains a core root whenever both children remain; after label \(3\) is removed, no later order query contains both children.  In any graph query containing both children, both core roots already lie in the relevant prefix, descendant deletion removes no root, and deletion of the current row removes at most one root.  A singleton encoder query contains only one child.  Thus no reached matrix occurrence has core intersection exactly \(\{3,4\}\).  The preceding subset bounds prove that every reached positive eigenvalue is at least \(1/40\).

For each graph query, descendant pruning leaves an ancestral set.  Its primitive ranges include the coordinate of each retained root; whenever a retained child has a parent direction not already supplied by a root, (22) supplies that direction together with the child's own coordinate.  Removing the current label deletes its coordinate exactly in the zero-comparison case and otherwise leaves it supplied by a retained descendant.  Hence the two ranges appearing in each graph comparison are coordinate subspaces, and their projector-product norm is respectively zero or one according as the relevant inclusion holds.  This proves the reached projector band with \(h_0=1/2\), using the actual orthogonal projectors; it does not use the source's printed angular expression.

The order phase evaluates \(\sum_{t=2}^nt=n(n+1)/2-1\) matrix occurrences.  The graph phase contributes \(n-1\) prefix occurrences and \(n(n-1)/2\) comparison-sum occurrences, and the encoder phase contributes \(n\) singleton occurrences.  Therefore the total is
\[
 n(n+1)/2-1+(n-1)+n(n-1)/2+n=n^2+2n-2.
 \tag{24}
\]

For the unqueried subset, the four range vectors \(e_3,b_3,e_4,b_4\) are linearly independent because \(e_3,e_4\) are orthogonal to the parent plane and \(\det(b_3,b_4)=\epsilon\ne0\).  Thus \(R(\{3,4\})\) is positive definite on the four-dimensional core.  With \(v=(1,-1,0,0)^{\top}/\sqrt2\), equation (22) gives
\[
 v^{\top}R_3v=0,
 \qquad
 v^{\top}R_4v=\epsilon^2/2.
\]
The Rayleigh variational principle therefore yields
\[
 0<\eta^{\ast}(P)
 \le\lambda_{min}^{+}(R(\{3,4\}))
 \le\epsilon^2/2=2^{-2n-1}<1/80.
 \tag{25}
\]
The strict positivity on the left also follows directly because there are finitely many subset sums and every nonzero positive-semidefinite subset sum has finitely many strictly positive eigenvalues; the preceding case analysis covers all of them.  Thus a threshold smaller than \(\eta^{\ast}(P)\) exists for each fixed \(n\), even though no fixed positive threshold works uniformly in \(n\).  Since \(G=H=I_n\), the source norm-product scale and the valid reciprocal scale both equal one: \(\gamma^{\ast}(G)=\rho^{\ast}(G)=1\).  Hence \(\gamma_{\mathrm{src}}=1/4\) lies below both scales, whereas the fixed choice \(\eta_{\mathrm{src}}=1/80\) lies above the margin in (25).

Finally, every induced Gaussian density and causal conditional is strictly positive and continuously differentiable.  Let \(\phi_v\) denote the centered Gaussian density of variance \(v\).  A root intervention has a nonconstant ratio of centered Gaussian densities.  For a child, the intervention-to-observational conditional-density ratio is
\[
 \frac{\phi_2(z_c)}{\phi_1(z_c-b^{\top}z_{\operatorname{pa}(c)})}.
\]
Its derivative in either parent coordinate is not the zero function because both entries of \(b_3\) and \(b_4\) are nonzero.  This verifies the source's Assumption 1 parent-ratio condition, and a perfect stochastic intervention is a legal special case of its smooth soft-intervention model.  Entrywise laws of large numbers give \(S_{m,r}\to\Sigma_m\) in probability.  On the uniformly positive-definite neighborhood established in (20),
\[
 S_{m,r}^{-1}-\Sigma_m^{-1}
 =S_{m,r}^{-1}(\Sigma_m-S_{m,r})\Sigma_m^{-1}
\]
gives inverse convergence, and multiplication by a Gaussian vector with finite second moment gives the source's squared-mean score-difference consistency.  Thus all promised-class assumptions and all stated non-margin source conditions hold.  Equations (20)--(25) prove the claimed constants and also show precisely why the example leaves the source's existential threshold premise intact.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-margin-improvement]\label{thm:strict-margin-improvement}

\begin{proposition}[prop:population-reduction]\label{prop:population-reduction}

\end{proposition}

The rank decisions of \(\mathsf C\) are therefore the rank decisions of \(\mathsf A\), and the smallest-certified-zero rule is exactly the public smallest-label rule.

Their column- and null-space projectors are identical, so their projector-product norms and their zero decisions coincide. \(\operatorname{col}(R_m)=A^{-\top}\operatorname{span}\{e_{I(m)},b_{I(m)}\}\). Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}projector\mbox{-}geometry}\) makes the corresponding product zero exactly in that case.  \widehat{\mathcal G}=\operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G_{\mathcal E})

\end{proof}

The answer is affirmative for post-summary computation.  Let \(Q=8(n^2+2n)\). \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(Q=8(n^2+2n)\) and \(\beta=e_{\star}/(4Q)\).  In any one restarted transcript, the public program has \(n^2+2n-2\) reached matrix occurrences.  Adding \(n+1\) covariance inversions, \(n\) primitive formations, at most two projector constructions and one product-norm computation per graph comparison, and \(n\) singleton selections is bounded by \(Q\).  Assign the \(j\)-th operation in that restart a numerical enclosure radius
\[
 \varepsilon_j\le\beta,
 \tag{28}
\]
and, for the operation's own decision, reduce it further to the minimum of \(\beta\) and the required fixed fraction of \(g_0\), \(h_0\), \(1/\sqrt n\), or \(\tau/n\).  Then, deterministically,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{Q}\varepsilon_j\le Q\beta=e_{\star}/4.
 \tag{29}
\]

We first justify rational covariance algebra.  Clear denominators in an input covariance matrix.  Its integer entries have bit length at most \(O(L)\).  The Leibniz determinant formula has at most \(n!\) products of \(n\) such entries, so every determinant minor has bit length \(O(n(L+\log n))\).  Fraction-free elimination therefore decides positive definiteness and, when the matrix is invertible, computes its adjugate-over-determinant inverse with polynomially many operations on polynomial-bit integers.  Sums and the three-factor primitive formula increase numerator and denominator lengths only polynomially in \(L\) and \(n\).  Outward rounding every entry to \(p\) fractional bits gives operator error at most \(n2^{-p}\), because the operator norm is at most the Frobenius norm.  The min--max formula and the quadratic-form bound used in (9) transfer this operator radius to every ordered eigenvalue.  Thus primitive and eigenvalue enclosure error at most any prescribed \(\xi>0\) is obtained with
\[
 p=O\!\left(L+\log n+\log(1/\xi)\right).
 \tag{30}
\]
This contains, and sharpens for exact rational centers, the prior covariance-to-primitive partial result.  If the input itself is an outward interval rather than an exact rational center, the condition \(\lambda_{\min}(S_{m,r})\ge(2\kappa)^{-1}\) and the resolvent identity give the previously established additional inverse error \(4\kappa^2n2^{-p}\), and the primitive error is at most \(Cn\kappa^4 2^{-p}\); adding \(O(\log\kappa)\) bits in (30) absorbs it.

The nonalgebraic radius terms also have certified rational enclosures at polynomial cost.  A square root of a nonnegative rational is bracketed by bisection, and range reduction writes a positive rational as \(2^jy\) with \(y\in[1,2)\).  With \(z=(y-1)/(y+1)\in[0,1/3]\),
\[
 \log y=2\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\frac{z^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell+1}+r_s,
 \qquad
 0\le r_s\le\frac{2z^{2s+3}}{(2s+3)(1-z^2)}.
\]
The same series at \(z=1/3\) encloses \(\log2\).  Thus \(O(p+L)\) rational terms suffice for \(p\) output bits, with explicit tail sign and magnitude; outward rounding gives certified values of \(t_r\) and \(a_r\) in polynomial Boolean time.

Rank certification does not require eigenvectors.  For a rational symmetric matrix \(K\) and rational \(x\), exact fraction-free symmetric elimination gives a rational block \(LDL^{\top}\) congruence of \(K-xI\).  Congruence preserves the maximal dimensions of positive and negative quadratic-form subspaces, so the signs of the one- and two-dimensional diagonal blocks determine the inertia and hence the number of eigenvalues below \(x\).  Bisection between rational endpoints gives outward eigenvalue intervals of width \(\xi\) in \(O(\log((1+\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}})/\xi))\) inertia calls.  The determinant-minor bound above proves polynomial bit growth in every call.  At the terminal stage, (18)--(19) from Theorem 1 give, for every reached \(M\),
\[
 b_M\le ne_{r_B}<g_0/112.
 \tag{31}
\]
Choosing the rational rounding radius below \(g_0/112\), the inertia counts certify that every empirical eigenvalue lies either in the zero cluster below \(g_0/28\) or in the positive cluster above \(27g_0/28\).  They consequently certify the population rank and the public forbidden band.  On an off-promise input, failure of these inequalities produces \(\bot\), not a rounded rank.

It remains to obtain whole invariant subspaces without separating eigenvalues inside a cluster.  At a certified occurrence let \(k\) be the inertia count of the empirical zero cluster.  If \(k=0\) or \(k=n\), the relevant projector is exactly \(I_n\) or \(0\).  Otherwise the only gap used is

 \tag{32}

Moreover, when \(a_r\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\),

 \quad

 \tag{33}

For a nonempty promised class, every singleton is nonzero by the variance reset and \(\|R_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le4\kappa^3\), so \(g_0\le4\kappa^3\). Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}certified\mbox{-}hermitian\mbox{-}projector}\), with \(S=I_n\), computes the projector onto the entire lower cluster to operator error \(\delta\) in Boolean time polynomial in

 n,\quad \log\kappa,\quad \log(1/g_0),\quad \log(1/\delta).
 \tag{34}

This invocation remains valid when eigenvalues are repeated within either cluster, because (32), not an internal eigengap, is its premise. \begin{proposition}[prop:logistic-boundary-legality]\label{prop:logistic-boundary-legality}

   \bigotimes_{e=0}^2(P_{\varepsilon}^{[e]})^{\otimes N},

 \le \frac{N\varepsilon}{4}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }\varepsilon\downarrow0.

\begin{proof}
The change of variables \(u=\tanh(z_1/2)\) gives
\[
 \frac{du}{dz_1}=\frac{1-u^2}{2},
 \qquad
 \ell(z_1)=\frac{1-u^2}{4},
 \qquad
 \ell(z_1)\,dz_1=\frac12\,du,
\]
and the analogous identities hold for \(v\).  Hence, under \(\mu\), the variables \(U\) and \(V\) are independent and uniform on \((-1,1)\).  Their means are zero.  It follows successively that
\[
 \int w_{\varepsilon,0}\,d\mu=1,
 \qquad
 \int w_{\varepsilon,2}\,d\mu=1,
\]
and, conditioning first on \(U\),
\[
 \int w_{\varepsilon,1}\,d\mu
 =\mathbb E\!\left[(1+aU)\mathbb E[1+\varepsilon UV\mid U]\right]
 =\mathbb E[1+aU]=1.
\]
Because \(|U|,|V|<1\), \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/4\), and \(a=1/4\),
\[
 w_{\varepsilon,0}\ge\frac34,
 \qquad
 w_{\varepsilon,2}\ge\frac34,
 \qquad
 w_{\varepsilon,1}\ge\frac9{16}.
\]
Thus every density is normalized and strictly positive.  Smoothness follows because \(\ell\), \(\tanh\), and the displayed products are smooth.

The observational marginal of \(Z_1\) is \(\ell(z_1)\), since integration over \(v\) removes \(\varepsilon uv\), and its conditional density for \(Z_2\) is
\[
 p_{\varepsilon}(z_2\mid z_1)=\ell(z_2)(1+\varepsilon uv).
 \tag{1}
\]
Use independent uniform noises and the inverse conditional distribution functions of the positive densities to generate first \(Z_1\) and then \(Z_2\) from (1).  This is an acyclic structural realization.  At \(\varepsilon=0\), (1) is independent of \(z_1\), so its minimal observational DAG is empty.  If \(\varepsilon>0\), then
\[
 \partial_{z_1}\log p_{\varepsilon}(z_2\mid z_1)
 =\frac{\varepsilon u'v}{1+\varepsilon uv},
 \qquad u'=\frac{1-u^2}{2},
\]
is not identically zero.  Thus the conditional mechanism genuinely depends on node \(1\), and the minimal DAG contains precisely \(1\to2\).  Replacing the root density by \(\ell(z_1)(1+au)\) leaves (1) unchanged and gives density \(w_{\varepsilon,1}\).  Replacing the second conditional by \(\ell(z_2)(1+av)\) leaves the root law unchanged and gives density \(w_{\varepsilon,2}\).  Both replacements differ from the corresponding observational mechanism, so they are distinct legal single-target interventions.

For the second intervention, subtraction of the observational score from the interventional score gives
\[
 d_2(u,v)=\left(-\frac{\varepsilon u'v}{1+\varepsilon uv},\;\frac{av'}{1+av}-\frac{\varepsilon uv'}{1+\varepsilon uv}\right)^\top.
\]
At \(v=0\), its parent component is zero while its target component is \((a-\varepsilon u)/2>0\). At \(u=0\) and \(v\ne0\), its parent component is \(-\varepsilon v/2\ne0\), while its target component is \(av'/(1+av)>0\). Hence the two components cannot be proportional, and the parent-to-target component ratio is not a constant function of \(z\). This is exactly Acartürk et al.'s Assumption 1 for node \(2\); node \(1\) has no parents, so its parent requirement is vacuous.

We next verify one common bounded kernel and the source representation.  Expanding the integrand and evaluating its even cosine moments gives the Laplace representation
\[
 P_j(x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
 \bigl(x+i\sqrt{1-x^2}\cos\theta\bigr)^j\,d\theta,
 \qquad -1\le x\le1.
\]
The integrand has modulus at most one, so \(\|P_j\|_\infty\le1\), with equality at \(x=1\).  Differentiating the three-term recurrence and inducting gives
\[
 P_j'(x)=\mathop{\sum_{0\le k<j}}_{j-k\,\mathrm{odd}}(2k+1)P_k(x),
\]
whose coefficients sum to \(j(j+1)/2\).  Rodrigues' formula and integration by parts give orthogonality, its normalization, and the Legendre differential equation.  Consequently the normalized Legendre polynomials satisfy
\[
 \|\phi_j\|_{\infty}=\sqrt{2j+1},
 \qquad
 -\frac{d}{du}\!\left((1-u^2)\frac{d\phi_j}{du}\right)
 =j(j+1)\phi_j,
 \qquad
 \|P_j'\|_\infty\le\frac{j(j+1)}2.
 \tag{2}
\]
Since \(A_j\ge j^2\) for \(j\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}A_j^{-6}(2j+1)<\infty.

Therefore the defining series gives

 \sup_z k_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z)
 \le\left\{\sum_{j\ge0}A_j^{-6}(2j+1)\right\}^2<\infty.
 \tag{3}

Moreover, \(|du/dz_1|\le1/2\), so (2) gives

 \left\|\frac{d}{dz_1}\phi_j(\tanh(z_1/2))\right\|_{\infty}
 \le\frac{j(j+1)\sqrt{2j+1}}4.
\]
The squared derivative-section series is consequently bounded by a constant times \(\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-7}\), times the finite undifferentiated series in (3).  The same argument applies in the second coordinate.  The supremum norm of every tail is bounded by the corresponding tail of these convergent numerical series, so the series and their first derivatives converge uniformly and their limits are continuous and uniformly bounded.  This proves the first two defining bounds of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{src}}(K_{\mathrm{Leg}},C)\) for some finite constant.

It remains to prove, rather than assume, the source representation.  On the square \([-1,1]^2\), define
\[
 \mathscr D_uF=-\partial_u\{(1-u^2)\partial_uF\},
 \qquad
 \mathscr D_vF=-\partial_v\{(1-v^2)\partial_vF\}.
\]
The factors \(1-u^2\) and \(1-v^2\) eliminate every endpoint term in integration by parts.  Hence (2), orthogonality, and repeated integration by parts imply, for every smooth rational function \(F\) whose denominators are products of positive powers of \(1+au\), \(1+av\), and \(1+\varepsilon uv\),
\[
 \|F\|_{\mathcal H_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}}}}^2
 =\sum_{i,j\ge0}A_i^6A_j^6|\langle F,\psi_{ij}\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}|^2
 =\|(I+\mathscr D_u)^3(I+\mathscr D_v)^3F\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
For completeness of this coefficient calculation on the functions used here, expand each reciprocal factor by its geometric series.  Since \(|au|,|av|,|\varepsilon uv|\le1/4\), the series and every fixed finite collection of differentiated series converge uniformly.  Their truncations are polynomials, and the Legendre polynomials span all polynomials by their nonzero leading coefficients.  Thus the relevant rational functions lie in the \(L^2(\mu)\)-closure of the tensor Legendre polynomials, which proves the last equality in (4) without an additional completeness hypothesis.

The Lebesgue score in environment \(e\) has the form
\[
 s_{\varepsilon,e}(z)=(-u,-v)^{\top}+\nabla_z\log w_{\varepsilon,e}(z).
 \tag{5}
\]
It is therefore a vector of smooth rational functions of \((u,v)\), with all denominators at least \(3/4\).  For each component \(s\) of (5), put
\[
 h=(I+\mathscr D_u)^6(I+\mathscr D_v)^6s,
 \qquad
 f=h/w_{\varepsilon,e}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Both \(h\) and \(f\) are of the type covered by (4), and hence belong to the scalar RKHS.  If \(s_{ij}=\langle s,\psi_{ij}\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}\), equations (2) and (6) give \(h_{ij}=A_i^6A_j^6s_{ij}\).  The integral operator of \(k_{\mathrm{Leg}}\) under \(\mu\) therefore satisfies
\[
 \langle L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}h,\psi_{ij}\rangle
 =A_i^{-6}A_j^{-6}h_{ij}=s_{ij}.
\]
The polynomial-closure argument following (4) yields \(L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}h=s\) in \(L^2(\mu)\), and continuity yields equality everywhere.  Since \(dP_{\varepsilon}^{[e]}=w_{\varepsilon,e}\,d\mu\),
\[
 L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},P_{\varepsilon}^{[e]}}f
 =L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}(w_{\varepsilon,e}f)
 =L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}h=s.
 \tag{7}
\]
Apply (6)--(7) componentwise to \(K_{\mathrm{Leg}}=k_{\mathrm{Leg}}I_2\).  Only finitely many derivatives occur.  Their suprema vary continuously over the compact parameter set \([0,1/4]\) and over three environments, while all denominators remain at least \(3/4\).  Equation (4) therefore bounds all source norms by one finite constant independent of \(\varepsilon\) and \(e\).  The scores in (5) are uniformly bounded, so the additional score exponential-moment bound holds. The derivative-section estimate bounds \(\xi_z=\operatorname{div}_zK_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,\cdot)^\top\) uniformly in RKHS norm; therefore \(\xi_z-\mathbb E\xi_Z\) is uniformly bounded, which is the stronger replacement for Assumption B.4 explicitly allowed by Zhou, Shi, and Zhu (2020, supplement Appendix B). Finally, every density is bounded by a constant times \(\ell(z_1)\ell(z_2)\le C_0e^{-|z_1|-|z_2|}\). Together with (3), this gives the radial \(d=2\) tail \(q_e(z)\sqrt{|K_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z)_{ij}|}=o(1)\), as required by their Assumption B.3. The empty finite boundary of \(\mathbb R^2\), smoothness and uniform first-derivative convergence, and (3) verify B.1, B.2, and B.5. Equation (7) is their separate Theorem B.1 source condition at \(r=1\), for a regularizer of qualification at least one.  Enlarging the common constant if needed proves
\(
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{log}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{src}}(K_{\mathrm{Leg}},C)
\).

For total variation, the observational densities at \(\varepsilon\) and zero differ by \(\varepsilon uv\) relative to \(\mu\), and the first-intervention densities differ by \(\varepsilon uv(1+au)\).  Symmetry gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{\varepsilon}^{[0]},P_0^{[0]})
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathbb E|UV|=\frac{\varepsilon}{8},
\]
and, because \(\mathbb E[U|U|]=0\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{\varepsilon}^{[1]},P_0^{[1]})
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathbb E[(1+aU)|UV|]
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{8}.
\]
The second-intervention law is independent of \(\varepsilon\).  The elementary product coupling bound
\(
\operatorname{TV}(\bigotimes_iQ_i,\bigotimes_iQ_i')
\le\sum_i\operatorname{TV}(Q_i,Q_i')
\)
follows by coupling each coordinate optimally and applying the union bound.  Applying it to \(N\) copies in each environment gives the claimed \(N\varepsilon/4\) bound and its limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-prefix-continuity-no-return]\label{lem:finite-prefix-continuity-no-return}
Let \(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2,\ldots\) be experiments on a fixed finite environment set \(\mathcal E\), each represented by independent infinite observation streams, and let \(\Gamma(Q)\) be its environment-labelled graph target.  Suppose \(\Gamma(Q_j)\ne\Gamma(Q_0)\) for every \(j\ge1\) and, for every finite \(N\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(
   \bigotimes_{m\in\mathcal E}Q_{j,m}^{\otimes N},
   \bigotimes_{m\in\mathcal E}Q_{0,m}^{\otimes N}
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Let \(\mathsf D\) be any randomized sequential rule that may choose environments adaptively, stop at a data-dependent finite time, and return a singleton graph, a set of graphs, or abstention.  If its probability of any incorrect finite singleton return is uniformly at most \(\alpha\), namely
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\{Q_0,Q_1,Q_2,\ldots\}}
 \Pr_Q\!\left(
   \mathsf D\text{ makes a finite singleton return }\{\widehat\Gamma\}
   \text{ with }\widehat\Gamma\ne\Gamma(Q)
 \right)\le\alpha,
\]
then
\[
 \Pr_{Q_0}\!\left(
   \mathsf D\text{ ever makes the finite singleton return }\{\Gamma(Q_0)\}
 \right)\le\alpha.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expose in advance the entire independent stream in every environment and an auxiliary random seed \(W\), independent of all streams and having the same law under every experiment.  Every adaptive sampling and randomization decision of \(\mathsf D\) is then a measurable function of these exposed objects; exposing unqueried observations changes neither the rule nor its distribution.

For \(N\ge1\), let \(E_N\) be the event that \(\mathsf D\) stops after inspecting at most \(N\) observations from each environment and returns the singleton \(\{\Gamma(Q_0)\}\).  The event \(E_N\) is measurable with respect to the first \(N\) observations of each full stream and \(W\).  Since \(\Gamma(Q_j)\ne\Gamma(Q_0)\), \(E_N\) is an incorrect finite singleton return under every \(Q_j\), and uniform validity gives
\[
 \Pr_{Q_j}(E_N)\le\alpha.
 \tag{8}
\]
Tensoring two laws with the same independent seed law does not increase total variation: for every event \(A\), integration of its seed sections gives
\[
 |(Q\otimes\mathcal L(W))(A)-(Q'\otimes\mathcal L(W))(A)|
 \le\operatorname{TV}(Q,Q').
\]
The assumed finite-prefix convergence therefore implies
\[
 |\Pr_{Q_j}(E_N)-\Pr_{Q_0}(E_N)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Letting \(j\to\infty\) in (8) yields \(\Pr_{Q_0}(E_N)\le\alpha\) for every \(N\).

The events \(E_N\) increase with \(N\).  Any finite stopping history queries only finitely many observations in every member of the finite environment set, so
\[
 \bigcup_{N\ge1}E_N
 =\{\mathsf D\text{ ever makes the finite singleton return }\{\Gamma(Q_0)\}\}.
\]
Countable additivity, applied to the disjoint increments \(E_1,E_2\setminus E_1,\ldots\), gives continuity from below.  Hence
\[
 \Pr_{Q_0}\!\left(\bigcup_{N\ge1}E_N\right)
 =\lim_{N\to\infty}\Pr_{Q_0}(E_N)\le\alpha,
\]
which includes adaptive allocation, arbitrary independent randomization, and unbounded stopping times with finite realized return time.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This finite-prefix lemma adapts the observational pointwise-versus-uniform consistency boundary of \cite{robins2003uniform,zhang2003strong} to randomized adaptive sampling across a fixed finite environment set. \textit{Gap.} The classical lineage motivates uniform separation but does not supply this pre-exposed-stream finite-prefix argument for adaptive multi-environment sampling.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-no-return-logistic-null]\label{thm:uniform-no-return-logistic-null}
Let a model class contain the logistic null \(P_0^{\mathrm{log}}\) and \(P_{\varepsilon_j}^{\mathrm{log}}\) for some sequence \(\varepsilon_j\downarrow0\) with \(\varepsilon_j>0\).  Every randomized adaptive sequential procedure whose probability of any incorrect finite singleton environment-labelled graph return is uniformly at most \(\alpha\) on that class satisfies
\[
 \Pr_{P_0^{\mathrm{log}}}\!\left(
   \text{the procedure ever returns the correct singleton empty graph at a finite time}
 \right)\le\alpha.
\]
Consequently, a uniformly \(\alpha\)-valid procedure can have null correct-singleton return probability larger than \(\alpha\) only if its model class imposes some restriction excluding such graph-changing weak alternatives.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}logistic\mbox{-}boundary\mbox{-}legality}\), the graph target at \(P_0^{\mathrm{log}}\) is the empty graph, whereas every \(P_{\varepsilon_j}^{\mathrm{log}}\) has the arrow \(1\to2\).  Thus the targets differ.  The same proposition gives, for every fixed \(N\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(
   \bigotimes_{e=0}^2(P_{\varepsilon_j}^{[e]})^{\otimes N},
   \bigotimes_{e=0}^2(P_0^{[e]})^{\otimes N}
 \right)
 \le\frac{N\varepsilon_j}{4}\longrightarrow0.
\]
All hypotheses of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\mbox{-}prefix\mbox{-}continuity\mbox{-}no\mbox{-}return}\) hold with \(Q_0=P_0^{\mathrm{log}}\) and \(Q_j=P_{\varepsilon_j}^{\mathrm{log}}\).  That lemma gives the displayed probability bound.

For the final implication, take the contrapositive.  If a uniformly valid rule returns the null's correct singleton with probability greater than \(\alpha\), the class cannot contain a graph-changing sequence satisfying the displayed finite-prefix convergence.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}logistic\mbox{-}boundary\mbox{-}legality}\) proves that the present bounded-kernel source family is one explicit such sequence.  This logical necessity is exclusion of weak graph-changing alternatives; it neither singles out a numerical exclusion parameter nor supplies a rate comparison.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This restores the proposal's intrinsic decision-theoretic side: beyond the observational uniform-consistency lineage \cite{robins2003uniform,zhang2003strong}, the explicit latent-interventional common-kernel source construction and finite-prefix lemma yield the exact \(\alpha\) null correct-singleton ceiling. \textit{Gap.} Pointwise population identifiability alone does not support a uniformly valid high-probability finite singleton declaration at a graph-changing boundary; the cited observational results do not provide this explicit latent-interventional bounded-kernel family or adaptive return bound. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations should use declared exclusions, set-valued output, or abstention rather than interpreting an empirical zero as a distribution-free certificate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:logistic-forbidden-band-exclusion]\label{prop:logistic-forbidden-band-exclusion}
At the logistic null \(P_0^{\mathrm{log}}\), the two singleton score-correlation matrices are
\[
 R_1^{\mathrm{log}}=\operatorname{diag}(c,0),\qquad
 R_2^{\mathrm{log}}=\operatorname{diag}(0,c),
 \qquad
 c=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\frac{a^2(1-u^2)^2}{4(1+au)^2}\,du
 \ge\frac{2}{375}.
\]
All reached positive eigenvalues are therefore larger than \(g_0=1/375\), and the null's sole graph-loop projector comparison is one, hence is larger than \(h_0=1/2\).  For every \(0<\varepsilon\le1/4\), the alternative's reached singleton \(R_2^{\mathrm{log}}\) is positive definite and
\[
 0<\lambda_{\min}(R_2^{\mathrm{log}})
 \le (R_2^{\mathrm{log}})_{11}
 \le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4(1-\varepsilon)^2}
 \le\frac{4\varepsilon^2}{9}.
\]
Thus every \(0<\varepsilon<\min\{1/4,3\sqrt{g_0}/2\}\) has a reached positive eigenvalue in \((0,g_0)\).  The fixed public forbidden band includes the null but excludes all sufficiently small graph-changing alternatives used in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}no\mbox{-}return\mbox{-}logistic\mbox{-}null}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At \(\varepsilon=0\), direct subtraction of the log densities in the definition gives, with \(u'=(1-u^2)/2\) and \(v'=(1-v^2)/2\),
\[
 d_1=\left(\frac{au'}{1+au},0\right)^{\!\top},
 \qquad
 d_2=\left(0,\frac{av'}{1+av}\right)^{\!\top}.
\]
Under the observational null, \(U,V\) are independent uniform variables.  Hence the generic score-correlation definition in \(\mathrm{def{:}bounded\mbox{-}kernel\mbox{-}logistic\mbox{-}boundary\mbox{-}family\) yields the two diagonal matrices in the statement, with the same positive entry \(c\).  Since \((1+aU)^2\le(1+a)^2\) and
\[
 \mathbb E(1-U^2)^2=1-\frac23+\frac15=\frac8{15},
\]
we obtain
\[
 c\ge\frac{a^2}{4(1+a)^2}\frac8{15}
 =\frac{2a^2}{15(1+a)^2}=\frac2{375}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Also \(R^{\mathrm{log}}(\{1,2\})=cI_2\), so every nonzero eigenvalue of every null matrix occurrence is at least \(2/375>g_0\).  In the two-node order step both singleton deletions have rank one, and the public smallest-label rule removes label \(1\) first.  Substitution of the coordinate ranges into the only graph-loop test gives, up to the displayed ordering, the product \(P_{\operatorname{span}(e_2)}P_{\operatorname{span}(e_2)}\), whose operator norm is one.  Thus the projector band with \(h_0=1/2\) also holds.

For \(\varepsilon>0\), subtraction of the observational score from the second-intervention score gives
\[
 d_2(u,v)=
 \left(
 -\frac{\varepsilon u'v}{1+\varepsilon uv},
 \frac{av'}{1+av}-\frac{\varepsilon uv'}{1+\varepsilon uv}
 \right)^{\!\top}.
 \tag{10}
\]
To prove positive definiteness, suppose \(x d_{2,1}+y d_{2,2}=0\) almost surely.  Positivity of the observational density and continuity make the equality valid throughout \((-1,1)^2\).  At \(v=0\), the first component vanishes and the second is \((a-\varepsilon u)/2>0\), so \(y=0\).  At \(u=0\) and \(v\ne0\), the first component is \(-\varepsilon v/2\ne0\), so \(x=0\).  Therefore every nonzero vector \(q\) has
\(
q^{\top}R_2^{\mathrm{log}}q=\mathbb E[(q^{\top}d_2)^2]>0
\), proving that \(R_2^{\mathrm{log}}\) is positive definite.

Rayleigh's principle and the first component of (10) give
\[
 0<\lambda_{\min}(R_2^{\mathrm{log}})
 \le e_1^{\top}R_2^{\mathrm{log}}e_1
 =\mathbb E_{P_{\varepsilon}^{[0]}}\!\left[
   \frac{\varepsilon^2u'^2v^2}{(1+\varepsilon uv)^2}
 \right]
 \le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4(1-\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
For \(\varepsilon\le1/4\), the last quantity is at most \(4\varepsilon^2/9\).  Every singleton is an encoder query in \(\mathfrak T_{\mathsf A}(P)\), so this eigenvalue is reached regardless of order ties.  If \(0<\varepsilon<3\sqrt{g_0}/2\), then \(4\varepsilon^2/9<g_0\).  Hence the alternative violates \(\{0\}\cup[g_0,\infty)\), whereas (9) and the projector calculation show that the null satisfies both fixed public bands.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The positive and negative theorems address different quantifiers. On the publicly promised Gaussian class, fixed reached bands make every rank and projector decision stable and yield high-probability finite graph and encoder certification. At the logistic null, uniform validity over arbitrarily weak graph-changing alternatives forces the probability of a finite correct singleton return down to at most \(\alpha\); set output, abstention, or delayed return remain available. The forbidden-band proposition connects the two sides by showing that the fixed public Gaussian-style band contains the null but excludes the very alternatives that drive the continuity argument. This certifies one sufficient design and one general exclusion requirement, not necessity of the numerical \(g_0,h_0\) formulation, a minimax phase rate, or failure of any published method.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The numerical theorem is post-summary: it assumes outward rational enclosures of exact fixed-coordinate empirical covariance summaries and, when an ambient encoder is requested, an enclosure of the canonical support frame. Raw-data processing additionally incurs representation, reading, and covariance-formation cost, including at least \(\Omega(B_{\mathrm{tot}})\) record operations. The statistical theorem remains exact-real at the sampling layer, and the encoder guarantee is distance to a unit-row parent-supported range class, not invertibility or a unique repeated-eigenspace representative. The positive guarantee is specific to Gaussian variance resets. The logistic converse controls only finite correct singleton graph returns under uniform error validity; it does not forbid honest set output, abstention, pointwise procedures, or guarantees on classes that exclude the graph-changing sequence.

\section{Honest scope}

The positive statistical and polynomial-bit post-summary theorems concern the linear Gaussian perfect variance-reset subclass in fixed coordinates on \(\mathcal S\), target only \(I^{-1}\operatorname{TC}(\mathcal G)I\) and the environment-indexed parent-supported encoder range, and do not transfer upward to the full smooth soft-intervention class. Public reached gaps \(g_0,h_0\) are sufficient for this certificate and are a pre-sampling promise, never inferred from realized empirical gaps. The logistic theorem proves a more general necessity statement only in form: a class supporting high-probability finite singleton certification at that null must exclude the exhibited graph-changing finite-prefix approximants by some restriction. It does not prove that these particular gap parameters are necessary, determine any minimax rate, or establish failure of Acartürk et al., Lee--Jin--Aragam, or any other named workflow. The rational algorithm is polynomial only after covariance summaries are supplied; raw records still cost at least \(\Omega(B_{\mathrm{tot}})\) to read. The Gaussian separation family has positive all-subset margin at every fixed dimension and therefore proves only that no positive dimension-uniform constant equation-(22) lower bound is necessary and that the equation-(22)-controlled sufficient sample bound is exponentially pessimistic relative to constant reached gaps. Actual projector geometry and \(\rho_i(G)\) replace the false printed norm-product identity. Neither an invertible encoder nor a canonical representative of a repeated positive eigenspace is claimed.
