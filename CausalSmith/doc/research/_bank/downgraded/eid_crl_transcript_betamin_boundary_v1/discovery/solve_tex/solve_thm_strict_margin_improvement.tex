The change of variables \(u=\tanh(z_1/2)\) gives

and the analogous identities hold for \(v\).  Hence, under \(\mu\), the variables \(U\) and \(V\) are independent and uniform on \((-1,1)\).  Their means are zero.  It follows successively that

 \int w_{\varepsilon,0}\,d\mu=1,
 \qquad
 \int w_{\varepsilon,2}\,d\mu=1,

and, conditioning first on \(U\),

 \int w_{\varepsilon,1}\,d\mu
 =\mathbb E\!\left[(1+aU)\mathbb E[1+\varepsilon UV\mid U]\right]

 w_{\varepsilon,0}\ge\frac34,
 \qquad
 w_{\varepsilon,2}\ge\frac34,

The observational marginal of \(Z_1\) is \(\ell(z_1)\), since integration over \(v\) removes \(\varepsilon uv\), and its conditional density for \(Z_2\) is

 p_{\varepsilon}(z_2\mid z_1)=\ell(z_2)(1+\varepsilon uv).
 \tag{1}

Use independent uniform noises and the inverse conditional distribution functions of the positive densities to generate first \(Z_1\) and then \(Z_2\) from (1).  r_{2,\varepsilon}(z_1,z_2)

We next verify one common bounded kernel and the source representation.  Expanding the integrand and evaluating its even cosine moments gives the Laplace representation
\[
 P_j(x)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
 \bigl(x+i\sqrt{1-x^2}\cos\theta\bigr)^j\,d\theta,
 \qquad -1\le x\le1.
\]
The integrand has modulus at most one, so \(\|P_j\|_\infty\le1\), with equality at \(x=1\).  Differentiating the three-term recurrence and inducting gives
\[
 P_j'(x)=\mathop{\sum_{0\le k<j}}_{j-k\,\mathrm{odd}}(2k+1)P_k(x),
\]
whose coefficients sum to \(j(j+1)/2\).  Rodrigues' formula and integration by parts give orthogonality, its normalization, and the Legendre differential equation.  Consequently the normalized Legendre polynomials satisfy
\[
 \|\phi_j\|_{\infty}=\sqrt{2j+1},
 \qquad
 -\frac{d}{du}\!\left((1-u^2)\frac{d\phi_j}{du}\right)
 =j(j+1)\phi_j,
 \qquad
 \|P_j'\|_\infty\le\frac{j(j+1)}2.
 \tag{2}
\]
Since \(A_j\ge j^2\) for \(j\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}A_j^{-6}(2j+1)<\infty.
\]
Therefore the defining series gives
\[
 \sup_z k_{\mathrm{Leg}}(z,z)
 \le\left\{\sum_{j\ge0}A_j^{-6}(2j+1)\right\}^2<\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
Moreover, \(|du/dz_1|\le1/2\), so (2) gives
\[
 \left\|\frac{d}{dz_1}\phi_j(\tanh(z_1/2))\right\|_{\infty}
 \le\frac{j(j+1)\sqrt{2j+1}}4.
\]
The squared derivative-section series is consequently bounded by a constant times \(\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-7}\), times the finite undifferentiated series in (3).  The same argument applies in the second coordinate.  The supremum norm of every tail is bounded by the corresponding tail of these convergent numerical series, so the series and their first derivatives converge uniformly and their limits are continuous and uniformly bounded.  This proves the first two defining bounds of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{src}}(K_{\mathrm{Leg}},C)\) for some finite constant.

It remains to prove, rather than assume, the source representation.  On the square \([-1,1]^2\), define
\[
 \mathscr D_uF=-\partial_u\{(1-u^2)\partial_uF\},
 \qquad
 \mathscr D_vF=-\partial_v\{(1-v^2)\partial_vF\}.
\]
The factors \(1-u^2\) and \(1-v^2\) eliminate every endpoint term in integration by parts.  Hence (2), orthogonality, and repeated integration by parts imply, for every smooth rational function \(F\) whose denominators are products of positive powers of \(1+au\), \(1+av\), and \(1+\varepsilon uv\),
\[
 \|F\|_{\mathcal H_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}}}}^2
 =\sum_{i,j\ge0}A_i^6A_j^6|\langle F,\psi_{ij}\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}|^2
 =\|(I+\mathscr D_u)^3(I+\mathscr D_v)^3F\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
For completeness of this coefficient calculation on the functions used here, expand each reciprocal factor by its geometric series.  Since \(|au|,|av|,|\varepsilon uv|\le1/4\), the series and every fixed finite collection of differentiated series converge uniformly.  Their truncations are polynomials, and the Legendre polynomials span all polynomials by their nonzero leading coefficients.  Thus the relevant rational functions lie in the \(L^2(\mu)\)-closure of the tensor Legendre polynomials, which proves the last equality in (4) without an additional completeness hypothesis.

The Lebesgue score in environment \(e\) has the form
\[
 s_{\varepsilon,e}(z)=(-u,-v)^{\top}+\nabla_z\log w_{\varepsilon,e}(z).
 \tag{5}
\]
It is therefore a vector of smooth rational functions of \((u,v)\), with all denominators at least \(3/4\).  For each component \(s\) of (5), put

 h=(I+\mathscr D_u)^6(I+\mathscr D_v)^6s,
 \qquad
 f=h/w_{\varepsilon,e}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Both \(h\) and \(f\) are of the type covered by (4), and hence belong to the scalar RKHS.  If \(s_{ij}=\langle s,\psi_{ij}\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}\), equations (2) and (6) give \(h_{ij}=A_i^6A_j^6s_{ij}\).  The integral operator of \(k_{\mathrm{Leg}}\) under \(\mu\) therefore satisfies

 \langle L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}h,\psi_{ij}\rangle
 =A_i^{-6}A_j^{-6}h_{ij}=s_{ij}.

The polynomial-closure argument following (4) yields \(L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}h=s\) in \(L^2(\mu)\), and continuity yields equality everywhere.  Since \(dP_{\varepsilon}^{[e]}=w_{\varepsilon,e}\,d\mu\),
\[
 L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},P_{\varepsilon}^{[e]}}f
 =L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}(w_{\varepsilon,e}f)
 =L_{k_{\mathrm{Leg}},\mu}h=s.
 \tag{7}

Apply (6)--(7) componentwise to \(K_{\mathrm{Leg}}=k_{\mathrm{Leg}}I_2\).  Only finitely many derivatives occur. The scores in (5) are uniformly bounded, so they have a common finite exponential-moment bound. Let \(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2,\ldots\) be experiments on a fixed finite environment set \(\mathcal E\), each represented by independent infinite observation streams, and let \(\Gamma(Q)\) be its environment-labelled graph target.  Suppose \(\Gamma(Q_j)\ne\Gamma(Q_0)\) for every \(j\ge1\) and, for every finite \(N\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(
   \bigotimes_{m\in\mathcal E}Q_{j,m}^{\otimes N},
   \bigotimes_{m\in\mathcal E}Q_{0,m}^{\otimes N}
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Let \(\mathsf D\) be any randomized sequential rule that may choose environments adaptively, stop at a data-dependent finite time, and return a singleton graph, a set of graphs, or abstention.  If its probability of any incorrect finite singleton return is uniformly at most \(\alpha\), namely
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\{Q_0,Q_1,Q_2,\ldots\}}
 \Pr_Q\!\left(
   \mathsf D\text{ makes a finite singleton return }\{\widehat\Gamma\}
   \text{ with }\widehat\Gamma\ne\Gamma(Q)
 \right)\le\alpha,
\]
then
\[
 \Pr_{Q_0}\!\left(
   \mathsf D\text{ ever makes the finite singleton return }\{\Gamma(Q_0)\}
 \right)\le\alpha.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-finite-prefix-continuity
Expose in advance the entire independent stream in every environment and an auxiliary random seed \(W\), independent of all streams and having the same law under every experiment.  Every adaptive sampling and randomization decision of \(\mathsf D\) is then a measurable function of these exposed objects; exposing unqueried observations changes neither the rule nor its distribution.

For \(N\ge1\), let \(E_N\) be the event that \(\mathsf D\) stops after inspecting at most \(N\) observations from each environment and returns the singleton \(\{\Gamma(Q_0)\}\).  The event \(E_N\) is measurable with respect to the first \(N\) observations of each full stream and \(W\).  Since \(\Gamma(Q_j)\ne\Gamma(Q_0)\), \(E_N\) is an incorrect finite singleton return under every \(Q_j\), and uniform validity gives
\[
 \Pr_{Q_j}(E_N)\le\alpha.
 \tag{8}
\]
Tensoring two laws with the same independent seed law does not increase total variation: for every event \(A\), integration of its seed sections gives
\[
 |(Q\otimes\mathcal L(W))(A)-(Q'\otimes\mathcal L(W))(A)|
 \le\operatorname{TV}(Q,Q').
\]
The assumed finite-prefix convergence therefore implies
\[
 |\Pr_{Q_j}(E_N)-\Pr_{Q_0}(E_N)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Letting \(j\to\infty\) in (8) yields \(\Pr_{Q_0}(E_N)\le\alpha\) for every \(N\).

The events \(E_N\) increase with \(N\).  Any finite stopping history queries only finitely many observations in every member of the finite environment set, so
\[
 \bigcup_{N\ge1}E_N
 =\{\mathsf D\text{ ever makes the finite singleton return }\{\Gamma(Q_0)\}\}.
\]
Countable additivity, applied to the disjoint increments \(E_1,E_2\setminus E_1,\ldots\), gives continuity from below.  Hence
\[
 \Pr_{Q_0}\!\left(\bigcup_{N\ge1}E_N\right)
 =\lim_{N\to\infty}\Pr_{Q_0}(E_N)\le\alpha,
\]
which includes adaptive allocation, arbitrary independent randomization, and unbounded stopping times with finite realized return time.

Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}projector\mbox{-}geometry}\) instead proves the valid reciprocal lower bound \(\rho_i(G)\) for any witnessed nonzero comparison.  No step above uses the false printed identity.
