Fix \(\lambda,\tau>0\) and \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and regard \(\nu\geq0\) as the parameter of the continuous extension.  Write
\[
 d=\lambda+\tau,\qquad D_\nu=d+\nu,
 \qquad q_\nu=\frac{\lambda(\tau+\nu)}{d+\nu}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Then \(D_\nu\to d\) and \(q_\nu\to q_0=\lambda\tau/d\).  The treatment drift and generator are
\[
 b_{\nu,\gamma}(x)=-dx-\nu\min\{x,\gamma\},
 \qquad
 (\mathcal L_{\nu,\gamma}g)(x)
 =b_{\nu,\gamma}(x)g'(x)+q_\nu g''(x).
 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(b_{\nu,\gamma}\to-dx\) uniformly on compact subsets of \(\mathbb R\), and at \(\nu=0\) equation (2) is exactly the control generator \(\mathcal L_0\).

We also verify convergence of the stationary objects rather than inferring it solely from the generator.  Let \(r_{\nu,\gamma}\) denote the unnormalized density appearing in the definition of \(\pi_\gamma\), with \(D\) and \(q\) replaced by \(D_\nu\) and \(q_\nu\).  Pointwise in \(x\),
\[
 r_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\longrightarrow
 r_0(x):=\exp\!\left(-\frac{d x^2}{2q_0}\right).
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, on \(x\leq\gamma\) this follows from the first quadratic piece, while on \(x>\gamma\) both terms involving \(\nu\gamma\) vanish.  Choose any fixed \(\bar\nu>0\).  Continuity and positivity of \(q_\nu\) on \([0,\bar\nu]\) give constants \(0<q_-\leq q_\nu\leq q_+<\infty\).  On the left piece,
\[
 r_{\nu,\gamma}(x)
 \leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{d x^2}{2q_+}\right),
 \tag{4}
\]
whereas on the right piece Young's inequality gives a constant \(C_\gamma<\infty\), independent of \(\nu\in[0,\bar\nu]\), such that
\[
 -\frac{d x^2}{2q_\nu}-\frac{\nu\gamma x}{q_\nu}
 +\frac{\nu\gamma^2}{2q_\nu}
 \leq -\frac{d x^2}{4q_+}+C_\gamma.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus \((1+|x|^2)r_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\) has a common integrable Gaussian envelope.  Dominated convergence applied first to the normalizing constants and then to the first two moments proves
\[
 \pi_{\nu,\gamma}\longrightarrow\pi_0
 \quad\hbox{in weighted \(L^1(1+|x|^2)\)},
 \qquad
 m_{\nu,\gamma}:=\int x\pi_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\,dx\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{6}
\]
Here the normalized version of \(r_0\) is \(\mathcal N(0,q_0/d)=\pi_0\).  Equations (2), (3), and (6) give the asserted reduction of the stationary treatment diffusion to the stationary control Ornstein--Uhlenbeck diffusion.

It remains to pass the Poisson energy to the limit.  If \(y>x\), monotonicity and the one-Lipschitz property of \(z\mapsto\min\{z,\gamma\}\) imply
\[
 -(d+\nu)(y-x)
 \leq b_{\nu,\gamma}(y)-b_{\nu,\gamma}(x)
 \leq-d(y-x).
 \tag{7}
\]
Couple two solutions of the diffusion in (2), started at \(y\) and \(x\), with the same Brownian motion.  Their difference has no martingale part, so (7) and integration of the resulting scalar differential inequalities yield
\[
 e^{-(d+\nu)t}(y-x)
 \leq X_t^y-X_t^x
 \leq e^{-dt}(y-x).
 \tag{8}
\]
The centered resolvent
\[
 \bar h_{\nu,\gamma}(x)
 =\int_0^\infty
 \{\mathbb E_xX_t-m_{\nu,\gamma}\}\,dt
 \tag{9}
\]
is finite by (8).  Integrating the difference quotients in (8), and then letting \(y\downarrow x\), gives
\[
 \frac1{d+\nu}\leq \bar h_{\nu,\gamma}'(x)\leq\frac1d.
 \tag{10}
\]
The semigroup identity applied to (9) gives
\(-\mathcal L_{\nu,\gamma}\bar h_{\nu,\gamma}=x-m_{\nu,\gamma}\).  After subtraction of its stationary mean, uniqueness of the centered Poisson solution in Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) identifies \(\bar h_{\nu,\gamma}\) with the flux construction \(h_\gamma\).  In particular,
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}\left|h_\gamma'(x)-\frac1d\right|
 \leq \frac1d-\frac1{d+\nu}
 =\frac{\nu}{d(d+\nu)}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Because \(\pi_{\nu,\gamma}\) is a probability density, (11) implies
\[
 \int\{h_\gamma'(x)\}^2\pi_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\,dx
 \longrightarrow\frac1{d^2}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Combining (1) and (12) proves
\[
 V_\gamma
 =2q_\nu\int\{h_\gamma'(x)\}^2\pi_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\,dx
 \longrightarrow \frac{2q_0}{d^2}
 =\frac{2\lambda\tau}{(\lambda+\tau)^3}.
 \tag{13}
\]

We next prove the fixed-system reduction.  Fix \(N\), an arm \(\ell\in\{0,1\}\), and an interior implemented capacity in the treatment arm.  By the two generator assumptions, every adjacent birth and death rate needed for irreducibility is positive.  Hence the finite birth--death chain is irreducible and has the stationary law \(\rho_{\ell,N}\).  On the scaled lattice define
\[
 f_{\ell,N}(x)=x-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N},
 \qquad
 -G_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}=f_{\ell,N},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}u_{\ell,N}=0.
 \tag{14}
\]
Existence and uniqueness in (14) follow because the null space of the irreducible finite-state generator consists of the constants and \(f_{\ell,N}\) has stationary mean zero.  This is the exact prelimit Poisson solution used in Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\).

Let \(r_N(x,y)\) be the transition rate from \(x\) to \(y\) for the scaled chain and put
\[
 c_{\ell,N}(x)
 =\sum_{y\ne x}r_N(x,y)
 \{u_{\ell,N}(y)-u_{\ell,N}(x)\}^2.
 \tag{15}
\]
Dynkin's identity and (14) give the square-integrable jump martingale
\[
 M_T=u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(T))-u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(0))
      +\int_0^T f_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt,
 \qquad
 \langle M\rangle_T=\int_0^T c_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt.
 \tag{16}
\]
Stationarity and the generator product identity
\[
 G_{\ell,N}(u^2)-2uG_{\ell,N}u
 =\sum_{y\ne x}r_N(x,y)\{u(y)-u(x)\}^2
 \tag{17}
\]
show that the exact stationary Poisson energy is
\[
 V_{\ell,N}
 :=\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}c_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N})
 =-2\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}
   \{u_{\ell,N}G_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}\}
 =2\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}
   \{u_{\ell,N}f_{\ell,N}\}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The birth--death chain is reversible by detailed balance.  Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) bounds its spectral gap below by \(d>0\).  Applying its finite spectral expansion to the bounded function \(c_{\ell,N}\) therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left{
 \frac1T\int_0^T c_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt
 \right}
 \leq
 \frac{2\operatorname{Var}_{\rho_{\ell,N}}(c_{\ell,N})}{dT}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Thus
\[
 T^{-1}\langle M\rangle_T\longrightarrow V_{\ell,N}
 \quad\hbox{in probability as \(T\to\infty\)}.
 \tag{20}
\]

For clarity, we derive the martingale limit from (20).  For fixed \(\xi\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
 \Psi_T(\xi)=\int_0^T\sum_{y\ne X_{t-}}r_N(X_{t-},y)
 \left[
 e^{i\xi\{u_{\ell,N}(y)-u_{\ell,N}(X_{t-})\}/\sqrt T}
 -1-\frac{i\xi\{u_{\ell,N}(y)-u_{\ell,N}(X_{t-})\}}{\sqrt T}
 \right]dt.
 \tag{21}
\]
The state space, all rates, and \(u_{\ell,N}\) are bounded because \(N\) is fixed.  Taylor's formula, (16), and (20) consequently imply
\[
 \Psi_T(\xi)
 =-\frac{\xi^2}{2T}\langle M\rangle_T+O(T^{-1/2})
 \longrightarrow-\frac{\xi^2V_{\ell,N}}2
 \quad\hbox{in probability}.
 \tag{22}
\]
A direct jump-product calculation shows that
\[
 Z_T(\xi)=\exp\{i\xi M_T/\sqrt T-\Psi_T(\xi)\}
 \tag{23}
\]
is a mean-one martingale: at a transition \(x\to y\) its multiplicative jump is the exponential in (21), and subtracting the corresponding transition-rate compensator makes its drift zero.  Moreover, \(1-\cos z\leq z^2/2\) and finiteness of the state space give \(\sup_T|Z_T(\xi)|<\infty\), while \(|e^{\Psi_T(\xi)}|\leq1\).  Hence (22), bounded convergence in probability, and \(\mathbb E Z_T(\xi)=1\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E\exp(i\xi M_T/\sqrt T)
 =\mathbb E\{Z_T(\xi)e^{\Psi_T(\xi)}\}
 \longrightarrow e^{-\xi^2V_{\ell,N}/2}.
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus \(M_T/\sqrt T\) converges in distribution to \(\mathcal N(0,V_{\ell,N})\).  Since \(u_{\ell,N}\) is bounded, the endpoint term in (16), divided by \(\sqrt T\), converges to zero.  We have therefore proved the explicit finite-state occupation limit
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt T}\int_0^T
 \{X_{\ell,N}(t)-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N}\}\,dt
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,V_{\ell,N}).
 \tag{25}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \sqrt{NT}\left[
 \frac1T\int_0^T\frac{K_{\ell,N}(t)}N\,dt
 -\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}\frac{K_{\ell,N}}N
 \right]
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,V_{\ell,N}).
 \tag{26}
\]
The energy is strictly positive: if (18) were zero, every edge difference of \(u_{\ell,N}\) would vanish; irreducibility would make \(u_{\ell,N}\) constant, contradicting (14) because the coordinate function is nonconstant.

As a smallest-instance check, take \(N=2\) and the interior treatment capacity \(M_N=1\).  The birth rates are \(2\lambda,\lambda,0\), and the death rates are \(0,\tau+\nu,2\tau+\nu\).  The unnormalized stationary weights are
\[
 1,qquad \frac{2\lambda}{\tau+\nu},qquad
 \frac{2\lambda^2}{(\tau+\nu)(2\tau+\nu)},
 \tag{27}
\]
all strictly positive.  If \(A_k=\sum_{j=0}^k\rho_{1,2}(j)f_{1,2}(j)\), then exact flux summation of (14) gives
\[
 u_{1,2}(x_{k+1})-u_{1,2}(x_k)
 =-\frac{A_k}{\rho_{1,2}(k)B_k},
 \qquad
 V_{1,2}=2\sum_{k=0}^1\frac{A_k^2}{\rho_{1,2}(k)B_k}>0.
 \tag{28}
\]
Indeed, \(A_0<0\), and centering gives \(A_1=-\rho_{1,2}(2)f_{1,2}(2)<0\).  Thus the smallest interior chain exhibits neither degeneracy nor a boundary exception to (25).

Equations (25)--(26) use the common fixed arm size and the stationary homogeneous generators imposed here.  Lemma \(\mathrm{boutilier\mbox{-}anchor\mbox{-}scope}\) records that the cited model permits distinct fixed arm populations and a separately specified capacity and presents its fixed-system occupation and many-system fluid limits separately.  The calculation above is therefore an internal reduction for the present equal-arm subclass; compatibility supplies no logical transfer of this positive result to that broader class.
