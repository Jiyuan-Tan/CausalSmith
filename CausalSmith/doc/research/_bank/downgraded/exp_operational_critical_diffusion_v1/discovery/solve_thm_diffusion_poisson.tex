Put \(x=\sqrt N\).  By \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{rate\mbox{-}bounds}}\) and \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{rate\mbox{-}membership}}\),
\[
 a(p)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\tau+\nu}
 \geq \frac{c}{3C}\geq\beta,
 \qquad
 1-a(p)=\frac{\tau+\nu}{\lambda+\tau+\nu}
 \geq\frac{2c}{C+2c}\geq\beta.                       \tag{1}
\]
The positive root of \(\beta x^2-Gx-1=0\) is
\[
 r=\frac{G+\sqrt{G^2+4\beta}}{2\beta}.
\]
By the definition of \(N_0\) and \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{large\mbox{-}enough\mbox{-}system}}\), \(N\geq N_0\geq r^2\), whence \(x\geq r\) and
\[
 \beta N-G\sqrt N\geq1.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Using \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{staffing\mbox{-}window}}\), equations (1)--(2) give
\[
 Na(p)+\gamma\sqrt N\geq \beta N-G\sqrt N\geq1              \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 Na(p)+\gamma\sqrt N
 \leq N(1-\beta)+G\sqrt N
 =N-\{\beta N-G\sqrt N\}\leq N-1.                            \tag{4}
\]
Both endpoints in (3)--(4) are integers.  Therefore taking the ceiling preserves them:
\[
 1\leq \left\lceil Na(p)+\gamma\sqrt N\right\rceil\leq N-1.
\]
Every bound used only \((c,C,G)\), so the conclusion is uniform on the stated boxes.

Write \(\delta=N^{-1/2}\).  Throughout the proof, a constant denoted by \(C_j\) may depend on \((c,C,G)\) and on a displayed compact state or time interval, but not on \(N,p,\gamma\).  This convention is valid by \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{rate\mbox{-}bounds}}\), \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{rate\mbox{-}membership}}\), and \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{staffing\mbox{-}window}}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{staffing\mbox{-}interior}\) ensures that the implemented capacity is an interior integer.

For the treatment arm put
\[
 x=\frac{k-Na}{\sqrt N},\qquad g=\gamma_N.
\]
The identities \(M_N=Na+\sqrt N g\) and
\(\lambda(1-a)=(\tau+\nu)a=q\), together with \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{treatment\mbox{-}dynamics}}\), give the exact scaled drift
\[
 \delta\{\lambda(N-k)-\tau k-\nu\min(k,M_N)\}
 =-dx-\nu\min(x,g)=:b_g(x),                                  \tag{1}
\]
and the exact local second characteristic
\[
 \delta^2\{\lambda(N-k)+\tau k+\nu\min(k,M_N)\}
 =2q+\delta\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}.                 \tag{2}
\]
Unavailable boundary jumps have rate zero, so (1)--(2) also hold at the endpoints.  For the control arm the corresponding identities, from \(\mathrm A_{\mathrm{control\mbox{-}dynamics}}\), are \(-dx\) and \(2q_0+\delta(\tau-\lambda)x\).

Taylor's formula with integral remainder, applied separately to the available jumps, now implies that for \(f\in C_c^3(\mathbb R)\) and fixed \(R_{\mathrm{loc}}<\infty\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\sup_{x\in E_{0,N}\cap[-R_{\mathrm{loc}},R_{\mathrm{loc}}]}
   |(G_{0,N}-\mathcal L_0)f(x)|\\
 &\quad+
 \sup_{x\in E_{1,N}\cap[-R_{\mathrm{loc}},R_{\mathrm{loc}}]}
   |(G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_g)f(x)|
 \leq C_f\delta.                                               \tag{3}
 \end{split}
\]
The ceiling identity gives
\[
 0\leq g-\gamma<\delta.                                       \tag{4}
\]
Since \(x\mapsto\min(x,g)\) is one-Lipschitz jointly in \(x\) and \(g\), (3)--(4) prove the asserted compact-uniform generator convergence with \(\mathcal L_{\gamma_N}\), and also with \(\mathcal L_\gamma\) after an error of order \(\delta\).

We next prove uniform stationary tightness without using a limit theorem.  Applying the treatment generator to \(x^2\) and using (1)--(2) gives
\[
 G_{1,N}x^2=2xb_g(x)+2q+\delta\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}. \tag{5}
\]
By (4), \(|g|\leq G+1\) for \(N\geq1\).  If \(x\leq g\), then \(-2\nu x\min(x,g)=-2\nu x^2\leq0\).  If \(x>g\), its positive part is at most \(2\nu(G+1)|x|\).  The remaining term in (5) is at most \(C\delta(1+|x|)\).  Young's inequality and \(d\geq2c\) therefore yield
\[
 G_{1,N}x^2\leq-c_1x^2+C_1                                  \tag{6}
\]
for constants \(c_1,C_1>0\).  The control calculation is the same without the service term.  Stationarity gives \(\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}G_{\ell,N}X_{\ell,N}^2=0\), so
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma,\ell}\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N}^2<\infty. \tag{7}
\]
More generally, expanding the two jumps in \(x^{2r}\), the term of degree \(2r\) is \(-2rdx^{2r}\) plus a nonpositive service contribution except for a term bounded by \(C_r|x|^{2r-1}\).  All lower jump terms are bounded by \(C_r(1+|x|^{2r-2})\).  Young's inequality and induction on \(r\) thus give
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma,\ell}\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}|X_{\ell,N}|^{2r}<\infty
 \quad(r\in\mathbb N).                                      \tag{8}
\]

The invariant law is now identified from detailed balance.  In the treatment arm,
\[
 \frac{\rho_{1,N}(k+1)}{\rho_{1,N}(k)}
 =\frac{\lambda(N-k)}{\tau(k+1)+\nu\min(k+1,M_N)}.            \tag{9}
\]
Uniformly for \(|x|\leq R\), expansion of the two logarithms about the common positive leading term \(qN\) gives
\[
 \log\frac{\rho_{1,N}(k+1)}{\rho_{1,N}(k)}
 =\delta\frac{b_g(x)}q+O_R(\delta^2).                          \tag{10}
\]
The remainder remains valid when \([x,x+\delta]\) crosses \(g\), because the minimum map is one-Lipschitz.  The density in the statement satisfies
\[
 q\{\log\pi_g\}'(x)=b_g(x)                                  \tag{11}
\]
on both sides of \(g\); the density and its first derivative match at \(g\).  Multiplying (10) along lattice edges and comparing the resulting sums with the integral of (11) shows that, for every fixed \(R\),
\[
 \sup_{x_k\in E_{1,N}\cap[-R,R]}
 \left|\delta^{-1}\rho_{1,N}(k)-\pi_g(x_k)\right|\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
To verify the normalization in this ratio argument, first choose \(R\) so that (7) leaves at most \(C/R^2\) mass outside \([-R,R]\), normalize the Riemann sum inside that interval, and then let \(R\to\infty\).  This also shows that no other subsequential invariant limit is possible.  Bound (8) upgrades (12) to convergence of every fixed polynomial moment.  The control invariant law is binomial with success probability \(a_0\), and the same ratio argument gives \(\pi_0\).  Compactness, (4), and the common Gaussian envelope of the displayed densities make all these conclusions uniform in \((p,\gamma)\), and replace \(\pi_g\) by \(\pi_\gamma\) in the limit.

We next pass from stationary laws to stationary paths.  Both drifts are globally Lipschitz, uniformly on the parameter box.  Let \(\zeta_R\) be the first exit time of the scaled chain from \([-R,R]\).  On the stopped interval, (1)--(2) bound the drift and local second characteristic uniformly.  The martingale decomposition of the coordinate, conditional isometry, and Cauchy--Schwarz therefore give, for every stopping time \(\sigma\leq T\) and \(0\leq u\leq1\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!left[
 |X_{\ell,N}((\sigma+u)\wedge\zeta_R)-X_{\ell,N}(\sigma)|^2
 \right]\leq C_R(u+u^2),                                    \tag{13}
\]
Stationarity and (8), applied to the maximum through the stopped martingale decomposition, imply
\[
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{N,p,\gamma}
 \mathbb P\!\left(\sup_{t\leq T}|X_{\ell,N}(t)|>R\right)=0.  \tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14), by the elementary dyadic-modulus argument and Markov's inequality, give tightness on \(D([0,T],\mathbb R)\), and the jump size \(\delta\) forces every limit to have continuous paths.

For every compactly supported \(C^3\) function, the compensated generator identity is a martingale.  Uniform integrability follows from (8); hence (3) permits passage to any subsequential limit.  The limit solves the martingale equation for \(\mathcal L_0\) or \(\mathcal L_\gamma\).  Synchronous coupling gives, for two solutions driven by the same Brownian motion,
\[
 |X_t^x-X_t^y|\leq e^{-dt}|x-y|,                              \tag{15}
\]
so solutions with a given Brownian path and initial state are unique.  The invariant initial laws were identified in (12), and therefore every subsequence has the stationary limit stated in the theorem.  If uniform bounded-Lipschitz convergence failed, a failing parameter sequence would have a convergent subsequence by compactness; (3)--(15) would identify its unique limit and yield a contradiction.  This proves uniform path convergence on every fixed time interval.

It remains to identify the Poisson solutions and energies.  For either limiting diffusion write \(b,q_*,\pi\), and \(m=\int x\pi(x)\,dx\).  The stationary equation is \(q_*\pi'=b\pi\).  With
\[
 F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x(u-m)\pi(u)\,du,
 \qquad h'(x)=-\frac{F(x)}{q_*\pi(x)},                         \tag{16}
\]
we have
\[
 q_*(\pi h')'=-(x-m)\pi,
 \qquad -\mathcal Lh=x-m.                                    \tag{17}
\]
Gaussian tails justify the integrations and the centering constant.  If two stationary-integrable centered solutions existed, their difference \(v\) would satisfy \(\mathcal Lv=0\), hence \((\pi v')'=0\) on both sides of the kink with matching flux.  Thus \(v'=C/\pi\).  If \(C\ne0\), the Gaussian tails make \(|v|\pi\) nonintegrable; therefore \(C=0\), and centering makes the remaining constant zero.

For the treatment diffusion, if \(y>x\), the secant slope of \(b_\gamma\) lies in \([-D,-d]\).  Under synchronous coupling,
\[
 e^{-Dt}(y-x)\leq X_t^y-X_t^x\leq e^{-dt}(y-x).                \tag{18}
\]
The resolvent representation
\[
 h_\gamma(x)=\int_0^\infty\{\mathbb E_xX_t-m_\gamma\}\,dt
\]
is justified by (18).  Divide its difference at \(y,x\) by \(y-x\), use (18), and let \(y\downarrow x\) to obtain
\[
 \frac1D\leq h_\gamma'(x)\leq\frac1d.                       \tag{19}
\]
For the control diffusion, (17) gives \(h_0(x)=x/d\).  Integration by parts in (17), with the Gaussian boundary terms vanishing, yields
\[
 2\int(x-m)h(x)\pi(x)\,dx
 =2q_*\int\{h'(x)\}^2\pi(x)\,dx.                             \tag{20}
\]
Thus \(V_0=2q_0/d^2=2\lambda\tau/d^3\), and (20) is the stated formula for \(V_\gamma\).

Finally, connect (20) with the exact finite systems.  For an arm with birth rates \(B_k\), death rates \(D_k\), invariant masses \(\rho_k\), and \(f_k=(k-\mathbb EK)/\sqrt N\), define
\[
 A_k=\sum_{i=0}^k\rho_i f_i,
 \qquad
 u_N(k+1)-u_N(k)=-\frac{A_k}{\rho_kB_k},
 \qquad \sum_k\rho_ku_N(k)=0.                                \tag{21}
\]
Detailed balance telescopes (21) to \(-\mathcal A_Nu_N=f\).  Its exact stationary energy is
\[
 V_N=2\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\rho_kB_k\{u_N(k+1)-u_N(k)\}^2
 =2\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{A_k^2}{\rho_kB_k}.                   \tag{22}
\]
In the ordered basic coupling of chains started one count apart, the discrepancy disappears at rate
\[
 B_k-B_{k+1}+D_{k+1}-D_k\in[d,D].
\]
The finite-chain resolvent consequently gives
\[
 0\leq u_N(k+1)-u_N(k)\leq\frac1{d\sqrt N}.                   \tag{23}
\]
On compact scaled intervals, (12), polynomial-moment convergence, and (21) imply uniformly
\[
 \sqrt N\{u_N(k+1)-u_N(k)\}\longrightarrow h_\gamma'(x_k),   \tag{24}
\]
with limit \(d^{-1}\) in the control arm.  By (23) and the rate bound \(B_k+D_k\leq3CN\), the energy on edges incident to \(|x_k|>R\) is at most a constant times \(\rho_N(|X_N|>R-1)\); (8) makes this uniformly negligible as \(R\to\infty\).  Riemann summation of (22) on \([-R,R]\), followed by \(R\to\infty\), proves
\[
 \sup_{p,|\gamma|\leq G}|V_{0,N}-V_0|\to0,
 \qquad
 \sup_{p,|\gamma|\leq G}|V_{1,N}-V_\gamma|\to0.             \tag{25}
\]
Equations (3), (12), (15), (17), (19), and (25) establish every assertion of the theorem, including support, boundary, kink, and uniformity conditions.

Write the birth and death rates in either arm as \(B_k\) and \(D_k\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{staffing\mbox{-}interior}\) and the strictly positive rate bounds make both finite chains irreducible.  Their invariant laws satisfy detailed balance,
\[
 \rho_N(k)B_k=\rho_N(k+1)D_{k+1},
 \qquad
 \rho_N(k)=\rho_N(0)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{B_j}{D_{j+1}},    \tag{1}
\]
so both chains are reversible.

We first prove the spectral-gap claim.  Let \(-\mathcal A_N\phi=\kappa\phi\), and set \(g_k=\phi(k+1)-\phi(k)\).  Subtracting the eigenvalue equations at \(k+1\) and \(k\) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 -\kappa g_k={}&B_{k+1}(g_{k+1}-g_k)+D_k(g_{k-1}-g_k)\\
 &-\{B_k-B_{k+1}+D_{k+1}-D_k\}g_k.                            \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]
Terms with unavailable indices are absent.  In the treatment arm,
\[
 B_k-B_{k+1}+D_{k+1}-D_k
 =\lambda+\tau+\nu\{\min(k+1,M_N)-\min(k,M_N)\}\geq d;       \tag{3}
\]
in the control arm it equals \(d\).  At a positive maximum of a nonzero gradient, the first two terms on the right of (2) are nonpositive, so \(\kappa\geq d\).  Replacing \(\phi\) by \(-\phi\) handles a negative minimum.  Every nonconstant eigenfunction has a nonzero gradient; reversibility makes the eigenvalues real.  Thus the spectral gap is at least \(d\).

Put \(\delta=N^{-1/2}\).  In the treatment arm, with \(x=(k-Na)/\sqrt N\) and \(g=\gamma_N\), the exact identities are
\[
 \delta(B_k-D_k)=-dx-\nu\min(x,g),
 \qquad
 \frac{\delta^2}{2}(B_k+D_k)
 =q+\frac\delta2\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}.            \tag{4}
\]
The control identities have drift \(-dx\) and leading second characteristic \(q_0\).  Since \(|g|\leq G+1\), applying the generator to \(x^{2r}\), for \(r=1,2,3\), and using
\[
 x\min(x,g)\geq-(G+1)|x|
\]
gives, by expansion and Young's inequality,
\[
 G_{\ell,N}x^{2r}\leq-c_r x^{2r}+C_r(1+x^{2r-2}).             \tag{5}
\]
Unavailable boundary jumps have zero rate, so (5) includes both endpoints.  Stationarity and induction yield
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma,\ell}
 \mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}|X_{\ell,N}|^{2r}<\infty,
 \qquad r=1,2,3.                                               \tag{6}
\]

The ordered basic coupling of chains begun in adjacent count states preserves a discrepancy of one until coalescence.  Its coalescence hazard is the expression in (3), hence at least \(d\).  For the centered resolvent
\[
 u_{\ell,N}(x)=\int_0^\infty
 \mathbb E_x\{X_{\ell,N}(t)-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N}\}\,dt,
\]
the coupling therefore gives, on every available upward edge,
\[
 0\leq u_{\ell,N}(x+\delta)-u_{\ell,N}(x)
 \leq\int_0^\infty\delta e^{-dt}\,dt=\frac\delta d.          \tag{7}
\]
This resolvent is finite by the same contraction and solves the stated centered Poisson equation.  Downward edges give the identical assertion after shifting the index.

To identify the limiting edge averages, put
\[
 f_i=x_i-\mathbb E X_{\ell,N},
 \qquad
 \Delta_i=u_{\ell,N}(x_{i+1})-u_{\ell,N}(x_i).
\]
Multiplying the Poisson equation by \(\rho_N(i)\), using (1), and summing from zero through \(k\) gives the exact flux identity
\[
 \rho_N(k)B_k\Delta_k=-\sum_{i=0}^k\rho_N(i)f_i.              \tag{8}
\]
The compact-uniform invariant-law convergence in Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) sends the numerator in (8) to the corresponding diffusion flux and sends \(\rho_N(k)B_k/\sqrt N\) to \(q_0\pi_0(x)\) or \(q\pi_\gamma(x)\), locally uniformly.  Consequently
\[
 \sqrt N\Delta_k\longrightarrow h_0'(x)=d^{-1}
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad h_\gamma'(x)                            \tag{9}
\]
locally uniformly.  Bound (7), moment bound (6), and truncation outside a compact interval permit dominated convergence in every stationary edge average.  Since
\[
 \frac{K_{0,N}}N\to a_0,
 \qquad \frac{K_{1,N}}N\to a,
 \qquad \frac{\min(K_{1,N},M_N)}N\to a                       \tag{10}
\]
in \(L^2\), equation (9) proves the asserted control average \(d^{-1}\), treatment average \(H=\int h_\gamma'\pi_\gamma\), and their downward-edge counterparts.

Finally, let \(r_N\) be any bounded normalized exposure or predictable-bracket integrand appearing in the occupation and marked-count martingales.  The rate bounds and (7) give a bound on \(\|r_N\|_\infty\) independent of \(N,p,\gamma\).  Expanding \(r_N-\mathbb E_\rho r_N\) in the finite reversible eigenbasis and using the spectral gap gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left\{T^{-1}\int_0^T r_N(K(t))\,dt\right\}
 &=\frac2{T^2}\int_0^T(T-s)
   \operatorname{Cov}_\rho\{r_N(K(0)),r_N(K(s))\}\,ds\\
 &\leq\frac{2\operatorname{Var}_\rho(r_N)}{dT}.               \tag{11}
 \end{split}
\]
Assumption \(T_N\to\infty\) and Chebyshev's inequality make (11) \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\), uniformly and without an upper relation between \(T_N\) and \(N\).  Equations (3), (6), (7), (9), and (11) prove all parts of the lemma.

Put \(\delta=N^{-1/2}\) and \(g=\gamma_N\).  The ceiling identity gives \(0\leq g-\gamma<\delta\), so \(|g|\leq G+1\).  Constants below are therefore uniform over the positive rate box and the enlarged compact staffing interval \([-G-1,G+1]\).  Write \(h_g=h_{p,g}\) and
\[
 b_g(x)=-dx-\nu\min(x,g).
\]
The synchronous-coupling argument in Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) is pointwise for every real kink location and therefore gives, at \(g=\gamma_N\),
\[
 \frac1D\leq h_g'(x)\leq\frac1d,                              \tag{1}
\]
and Definition \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) gives
\[
 qh_g''(x)=-(x-m_g)-b_g(x)h_g'(x).                             \tag{2}
\]

We first obtain derivative envelopes that remain valid at the kink.  The displayed piecewise-Gaussian invariant density has a common Gaussian tail on the compact parameter set.  Applying the stationary identity to smooth truncations of \(x^2\), then removing the truncation, gives
\[
 0=\int\mathcal L_gx^2\,d\pi_g
 =\int\{-2dx^2-2\nu x\min(x,g)+2q\}\,d\pi_g.                 \tag{3}
\]
Since \(x\min(x,g)\geq-(G+1)|x|\), Young's inequality in (3) proves
\[
 \sup_{p,|g|\leq G+1}\int x^2\pi_g(x)\,dx<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sup_{p,|g|\leq G+1}|m_g|<\infty.                            \tag{4}
\]
The quantities \(q^{-1},d,D,\nu\) are uniformly bounded on the rate box.  Equations (1)--(2) therefore imply
\[
 |h_g''(x)|\leq C_1(1+|x|).                                   \tag{5}
\]
On each open side of \(g\), differentiating (2) gives
\[
 qh_g'''(x)=-1-b_g'(x)h_g'(x)-b_g(x)h_g''(x),                 \tag{6}
\]
where \(b_g'=-D\) on the left and \(b_g'=-d\) on the right.  Thus
\[
 |h_g'''(x)|\leq C_2(1+x^2),\qquad x\ne g.                    \tag{7}
\]
The drift is continuous at \(g\); by (1)--(2), \(h_g''\) is also continuous there.  It is continuously differentiable on either side and its derivative has the local bound (7).  Hence \(h_g''\) is locally absolutely continuous across \(g\).  Taylor's integral remainder is therefore valid even when the increment interval crosses the kink; the value of \(h_g'''\) at the singleton \(\{g\}\) is irrelevant.

At \(x=(k-Na)/\sqrt N\), define the available birth and death rates
\[
 B_N(x)=\lambda(N-k),
 \qquad D_N(x)=\tau k+\nu\min(k,M_N).
\]
Direct algebra, including at the two endpoints where one rate is zero, gives
\[
 \delta\{B_N(x)-D_N(x)\}=b_g(x),                              \tag{8}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\delta^2}{2}\{B_N(x)+D_N(x)\}
 =q+\frac\delta2\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}.            \tag{9}
\]
Taylor expansion of each available lattice increment, using the absolute-continuity conclusion above, yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_{1,N}h_g(x)
 ={}&b_g(x)h_g'(x)
 +\left[q+\frac\delta2\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}\right]h_g''(x)
 +R_N(x),\\
 |R_N(x)|
 \leq{}&\frac{\delta^3}{6}\{B_N(x)+D_N(x)\}
 \mathop{\operatorname{ess\,sup}}_{|u-x|\leq\delta}|h_g'''(u)|.
                                                                    \tag{10}
 \end{split}
\]
The rate bounds give \(B_N+D_N\leq3CN\).  Equations (5), (7), and \(|\min(x,g)|\leq|x|+G+1\) therefore imply
\[
 |R_N(x)|\leq C_3\delta(1+x^2),
 \qquad
 \left|\frac\delta2\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}h_g''(x)\right|
 \leq C_4\delta(1+x^2).                                      \tag{11}
\]
Subtracting \(\mathcal L_gh_g=b_gh_g'+qh_g''\) from (10) proves the pointwise, boundary- and kink-inclusive estimate
\[
 |(G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_g)h_g(x)|
 \leq C_5N^{-1/2}(1+x^2),
 \qquad x\in E_{1,N}.                                        \tag{12}
\]

It remains to average (12) under the exact invariant law.  Applying (8)--(9) to \(x^2\) gives
\[
 G_{1,N}x^2
 =2xb_g(x)+2q+\delta\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}.      \tag{13}
\]
The inequalities used above, \(\delta\leq1\), and Young's inequality give a uniform constant \(C_6\) such that
\[
 G_{1,N}x^2\leq-dx^2+C_6.                                    \tag{14}
\]
The finite chain starts in \(\rho_{1,N}\), so stationarity implies \(\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}G_{1,N}X_{1,N}^2=0\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma}\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}X_{1,N}^2\leq C_6/d. \tag{15}
\]
Taking expectations in (12) and using (15) proves the theorem with
\[
 K_{\mathrm{res}}=C_5\left(1+\frac{C_6}{2c}\right),          \tag{16}
\]
after enlarging \(C_5,C_6\) to their compact-uniform values and using \(d\geq2c\).

The proof also performs the requested stress test.  The exact invariant product is
\[
 \rho_{1,N}(k)=Z_N^{-1}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}
 \frac{\lambda(N-j)}{\tau(j+1)+\nu\min(j+1,M_N)},             \tag{17}
\]
with empty product one.  Estimate (12) holds before weighting by (17), with the same constant at every rate-box corner and every ceiling phase \(g-\gamma\in[0,\delta)\); thus product weighting cannot create a nonvanishing scaled residual.  For \(N=2,M_N=1\), the states \(0,1,2\) are the left endpoint, kink state, and right endpoint, and (8)--(12) cover all four directed available transitions.  For arbitrary \(N\), any jump crossing \(g\) is covered by the integral remainder in (10).

Finally, stationarity and \(-\mathcal L_gh_g=x-m_g\) give the useful consequence
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}X_{1,N}-m_g\right|
 &=\left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}
   \{(G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_g)h_g\}(X_{1,N})\right|\\
 &\leq K_{\mathrm{res}}N^{-1/2}.                              \tag{18}
 \end{split}
\]
Thus the early-kill alternative is excluded at endpoints, rate-box corners, kink-crossing edges, and all ceiling phases.

%%% FIELD statement-kink-residual-resolution
The answer to the lattice-residual question is affirmative.  There is a finite constant \(K_{\mathrm{res}}\), depending only on \((c,C,G)\), such that for every \(N\geq N_0\),
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}
 \left|\{G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_{\gamma_N}\}
 h_{1,N}(X_{1,N})\right|
 \leq K_{\mathrm{res}}N^{-1/2}.
\]
All available jumps crossing \(\gamma_N\) are included.  Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}X_{1,N}-m_{\gamma_N}\right|
 \leq K_{\mathrm{res}}N^{-1/2}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-kink-residual-resolution
The first display is exactly Theorem \(\mathrm{kink\mbox{-}residual\mbox{-}bound}\), whose pointwise Taylor proof includes endpoint jumps and every lattice increment crossing \(\gamma_N\).  For the second display, stationarity gives \(\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}G_{1,N}h_{1,N}(X_{1,N})=0\), while Definition \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) gives
\[
 -\mathcal L_{\gamma_N}h_{1,N}(x)=x-m_{\gamma_N}
\]
at every lattice point.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}X_{1,N}-m_{\gamma_N}\right|
 &=\left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}
 \{G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_{\gamma_N}\}h_{1,N}(X_{1,N})\right|\\
 &\leq\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}
 \left|\{G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_{\gamma_N}\}h_{1,N}(X_{1,N})\right|\\
 &\leq K_{\mathrm{res}}N^{-1/2}.
 \end{split}
\]
This proves the affirmative answer and its centering consequence.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-fixed-capacity-derivative
The product weights in Definition \(\mathrm{fitted\mbox{-}center}\) satisfy the detailed-balance ratios of the control and treatment generators.  By Lemma \(\mathrm{staffing\mbox{-}interior}\), both chains are irreducible.  Their normalized product weights are therefore their unique invariant laws.  At \(p'=p\) and \(M=M_N\), this proves
\[
 \vartheta_N(p;M_N)=\theta_N.                                 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(\mu_{1,N}(p';M)=\sum_k(k/N)\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}(k)\).  For fixed \(M\), let \(v_N\) be the centered solution of
\[
 -\mathcal A_{1,N}^{p',M}v_N=k/N-\mu_{1,N}(p';M).             \tag{2}
\]
Differentiate the finite identity \(\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}\mathcal A_{1,N}^{p',M}=0\) in a rate coordinate \(r\), apply the differentiated signed measure to \(v_N\), and use (2).  Ordinary finite-sum algebra gives
\[
 \partial_r\mu_{1,N}(p';M)
 =\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}}
 [\{\partial_r\mathcal A_{1,N}^{p',M}\}v_N].                \tag{3}
\]
No interchange with an infinite limit occurs.

At \(p'=p,M=M_N\), equation (2) and the definition of \(u_{1,N}\) in Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) imply
\[
 v_N(k)=N^{-1/2}u_{1,N}(s_{1,N}(k)).                           \tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
 R_N^+(k)=N\{v_N(k+1)-v_N(k)\},
 \qquad
 R_N^-(k)=N\{v_N(k)-v_N(k-1)\}.
\]
Substitution of the three generator derivatives in (3) yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \partial_\lambda\mu_{1,N}
 &=\mathbb E[(1-K_{1,N}/N)R_N^+(K_{1,N})],\\
 \partial_\tau\mu_{1,N}
 &=-\mathbb E[(K_{1,N}/N)R_N^-(K_{1,N})],\\
 \partial_\nu\mu_{1,N}
 &=-\mathbb E[(\min\{K_{1,N},M_N\}/N)R_N^-(K_{1,N})].        \tag{5}
 \end{split}
\]
The edge-average limits and exposure limits in Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) now give, uniformly,
\[
 \nabla_p\mu_{1,N}(p;M_N)
 \longrightarrow((1-a)H,-aH,-aH)^{\mathsf T}.                \tag{6}
\]
The control product law is binomial with normalized mean \(a_0\), so
\[
 \nabla a_0=((1-a_0)/d,-a_0/d,0)^{\mathsf T}.                 \tag{7}
\]
Subtracting (6) from (7) proves
\[
 \nabla_{p'}\vartheta_N(p';M_N)|_{p'=p}
 \longrightarrow((1-a_0)/d-(1-a)H,-a_0/d+aH,aH)^{\mathsf T}=A. \tag{8}
\]

It remains to prove the Hessian envelope.  For either product law write \(S_r(k)=\partial_r\log w_N(k)\).  On \([c/2,2C]^3\), the one-step increments of \(S_r\) and \(\partial_sS_r\) are bounded by constants depending only on \((c,C)\).  For example,
\[
 \partial_\lambda\log w_N(k)=\frac{k}{\lambda},
 \quad
 \partial_\tau\log w_{1,N}(k)
 =-\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{j}{\tau j+\nu\min(j,M)},
\]
\[
 \partial_\nu\log w_{1,N}(k)
 =-\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{\min(j,M)}{\tau j+\nu\min(j,M)}.        \tag{9}
\]
The same claim for second derivatives follows by differentiating the summands; every denominator is at least \((c/2)j\).

For arbitrary \(1\leq M\leq N-1\), the count-scale drift \(B_k-D_k\) decreases by at least \(\lambda'+\tau'\geq c\) at every step, and the total jump rate is at most \(6CN\).  Let \(k_*\) be a sign-change point of the drift.  Expanding the generator on \((k-k_*)^{2r}\), the first-order term is at most \(-c_1|k-k_*|^{2r}\) outside a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt N\), while all jump remainders are at most \(C_rN(1+|k-k_*|^{2r-2})\).  Stationarity and induction for \(r=1,2,3\) give
\[
 \mathbb E|K-k_*|^{2r}\leq C_rN^r,
 \qquad r=1,2,3.                                               \tag{10}
\]
In particular, \(|\mathbb EK-k_*|\leq C\sqrt N\), so (10) also holds with \(k_*\) replaced by \(\mathbb EK\).  Because the scores in (9) and their parameter derivatives are uniformly Lipschitz in \(k\),
\[
 \|S_r(K)-\mathbb ES_r(K)\|_{L^6}\leq C\sqrt N,
 \quad
 \|\partial_sS_r(K)-\mathbb E\partial_sS_r(K)\|_{L^2}
 \leq C\sqrt N.                                               \tag{11}
\]

Finite-sum differentiation gives, for \(f(K)=K/N\),
\[
 \partial_r\mathbb Ef(K)=\operatorname{Cov}(f(K),S_r(K)),     \tag{12}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{split}
 \partial_s\partial_r\mathbb Ef(K)
 ={}&\mathbb E[(f-\mathbb Ef)(S_r-\mathbb ES_r)(S_s-\mathbb ES_s)]\\
 &+\operatorname{Cov}(f,\partial_sS_r).                        \tag{13}
 \end{split}
\]
Equation (10) gives \(\|f-\mathbb Ef\|_{L^6}\leq C N^{-1/2}\).  H\"older's inequality, (11), and (13) bound the first term by \(C\sqrt N\) and the second by \(C\).  Applying this to both arms proves
\[
 \sup_{p'\in[c/2,2C]^3,\,1\leq M\leq N-1}
 \|\nabla_{p'}^2\vartheta_N(p';M)\|\leq C_*\sqrt N.          \tag{14}
\]
Equations (1), (8), and (14) prove the lemma.

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-joint-law
All probabilities and expectations are under a member of \(\mathcal M\).  Thus both chains start in their invariant laws and are independent.  Put
\[
 f_{\ell,N}(x)=x-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N},
 \qquad
 -G_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}=f_{\ell,N},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}u_{\ell,N}=0.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) supplies existence, edge bounds, and moments.  Dynkin's formula gives the square-integrable martingale
\[
 M_{\ell,N}(t)=u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))-u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(0))
 -\int_0^tG_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(s))\,ds,
\]
and hence
\[
 \int_0^{T_N}f_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt
 =M_{\ell,N}(T_N)
 -\{u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(T_N))-u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(0))\}. \tag{1}
\]
The edge bound makes \(u_{\ell,N}\) uniformly \(d^{-1}\)-Lipschitz on its scaled lattice.  Stationary centering and the uniform second moment of \(X_{\ell,N}\) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma,\ell}
 \mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N})^2<\infty.    \tag{2}
\]
Since \(T_N\to\infty\), (1)--(2) give
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_N)
 =T_N^{-1/2}\{M_{0,N}(T_N)-M_{1,N}(T_N)\}+o_{\mathbb P}(1),   \tag{3}
\]
uniformly.

Define the compensated marked counts
\[
 U_{\lambda,N}=C_\lambda-\lambda E_\lambda,
 \quad
 U_{\tau,N}=C_\tau-\tau E_\tau,
 \quad
 U_{\nu,N}=C_\nu-\nu E_\nu.                                  \tag{4}
\]
The two generator assumptions give their predictable intensities, and the marked-history assumption distinguishes natural from service recoveries.  Thus the martingales in (4) are pairwise orthogonal and
\[
 \langle U_{\lambda,N}\rangle=\lambda E_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \langle U_{\tau,N}\rangle=\tau E_\tau,
 \qquad
 \langle U_{\nu,N}\rangle=\nu E_\nu.                        \tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
 s_\lambda=2-a_0-a,
 \qquad s_\tau=a_0+a,
 \qquad s_\nu=a.
\]
The control stationary mean is exactly \(Na_0\).  The invariant-law convergence and uniform moments in Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) give \(N^{-1}\mathbb E K_{1,N}\to a\).  The time-average conclusion of Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) then yields
\[
 \frac1{NT_N}(E_\lambda,E_\tau,E_\nu)
 \longrightarrow(s_\lambda,s_\tau,s_\nu)                    \tag{6}
\]
uniformly in probability.  Compact positivity gives \(\inf_{p\in\mathcal P}\min_j s_j>0\).  Hence zero-exposure fallbacks and plug-in clipping are inactive with probability tending to one, and componentwise
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat p_j-p_j)
 =\frac{U_{j,N}/\sqrt{NT_N}}{E_j/(NT_N)}
 =\frac{U_{j,N}}{s_j\sqrt{NT_N}}+o_{\mathbb P}(1).             \tag{7}
\]

We calculate the full predictable bracket before taking a limit.  Define
\[
 \mathcal Z_N(t)=\left(
 \frac{M_{0,N}(t)-M_{1,N}(t)}{\sqrt{T_N}},
 \frac{U_{\lambda,N}(t)}{s_\lambda\sqrt{NT_N}},
 \frac{U_{\tau,N}(t)}{s_\tau\sqrt{NT_N}},
 \frac{U_{\nu,N}(t)}{s_\nu\sqrt{NT_N}}
 \right)^{\mathsf T}.                                        \tag{8}
\]
The exact stationary Poisson-energy identity, arm independence, and the time-average assertion of the finite-chain lemma give
\[
 \langle\mathcal Z_N^{(1)}\rangle(T_N)
 =V_{0,N}+V_{1,N}+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \longrightarrow V_0+V_\gamma=V.                             \tag{9}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) give the lower diagonal block
\[
 \operatorname{diag}\left\{\frac\lambda{s_\lambda},
 \frac\tau{s_\tau},\frac\nu{s_\nu}\right\}=\Gamma.       \tag{10}
\]

For the cross bracket, inspect each event.  On an upward control edge, the scaled Poisson increment has stationary edge average tending to \(d^{-1}\); on an upward treatment edge it tends to \(H\).  Downward edges have the negative increments after shifting the index.  Since the first coordinate is \(M_{0,N}-M_{1,N}\), the limiting unnormalized cross-bracket rates are
\[
 \begin{split}
 c_\lambda&=\lambda\{(1-a_0)/d-(1-a)H\}=\lambda A_\lambda,\\
 c_\tau&=\tau\{-a_0/d+aH\}=\tau A_\tau,\\
 c_\nu&=\nu aH=\nu A_\nu.                                  \tag{11}
 \end{split}
\]
The exposure factors in (11) follow from (6), and the edge factors from the finite-chain lemma.  Division by \(s_\lambda,s_\tau,s_\nu\), as in (8), gives \(A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\).  Therefore
\[
 \langle\mathcal Z_N\rangle(T_N)
 \longrightarrow
 \Sigma:=\begin{pmatrix}V&A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\\
                         \Gamma A&\Gamma\end{pmatrix}        \tag{12}
\]
uniformly in probability.

We now derive, rather than assume, the Gaussian limit.  Fix \(t\in\mathbb R^4\) and put \(L_N=t^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Z_N\).  The Poisson edge bound makes a normalized occupation jump at most \(d^{-1}/\sqrt{NT_N}\), and a normalized count jump at most \((\min_j s_j)^{-1}/\sqrt{NT_N}\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_s|\Delta L_N(s)|\leq\varepsilon_N\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \langle L_N\rangle(T_N)\longrightarrow t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t. \tag{13}
\]
Let \(\nu_N(ds,dy)\) be the predictable compensator of the jumps of \(L_N\).  Stop when its quadratic integral first exceeds a fixed \(R>t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t+1\).  The compensated jump-product identity says that
\[
 \exp\!\left{iL_N(u)-
 \int_{[0,u]\times\mathbb R}(e^{iy}-1-iy)\nu_N(ds,dy)\right} \tag{14}
\]
is a mean-one martingale up to the stopping time.  Taylor's integral remainder gives, for \(|y|\leq\varepsilon_N\),
\[
 \left|e^{iy}-1-iy+\frac{y^2}{2}\right|
 \leq\frac{\varepsilon_N y^2}{6}.                             \tag{15}
\]
Since \(\int y^2\nu_N=\langle L_N\rangle\), equations (14)--(15) show that the stopped characteristic function differs from
\(\exp\{-\langle L_N\rangle(T_N)/2\}\) by at most \(C_R\varepsilon_NR\).  Equation (13) and boundedness then give
\[
 \mathbb E e^{iL_N(T_N)}longrightarrow
 \exp\{-t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t/2\};                           \tag{16}
\]
the stopping probability tends to zero by (13).  Because (16) holds for every \(t\), the joint characteristic functions converge to that of the centered Gaussian vector with covariance \(\Sigma\).  The limit is continuous at the origin, so Fourier inversion against smooth compactly supported densities, followed by approximation of bounded continuous functions, gives
\[
 \mathcal Z_N(T_N)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Sigma).            \tag{17}
\]
Combining (3), (7), and (17) proves the displayed joint law.

Uniformity follows by the sequence criterion.  If it failed, there would be a parameter sequence with a fixed positive discrepancy.  Compactness supplies a convergent subsequence.  The moment, exposure, edge, and bracket estimates above use the same constants on that subsequence; \(V,H,A,\Gamma\) are continuous Gaussian integrals.  Equations (12)--(17) then contradict the discrepancy.

It remains to studentize.  Equation (7) implies \(\widehat p-p=o_{\mathbb P}(1)\), and smoothness of \(a\) on the compact positive rate box gives
\[
 \widehat\gamma_N-\gamma_N
 =-\sqrt N\{a(\widehat p)-a(p)\}=O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma_N-\gamma<N^{-1/2}.                       \tag{18}
\]
The Gaussian integrals defining \(V\) are continuous on compact positive-rate and bounded-staffing sets.  Also
\[
 V\geq V_0=\frac{2\lambda\tau}{d^3},
 \qquad \inf_{p\in\mathcal P}V_0>0.                          \tag{19}
\]
Thus \(\widehat V/V\to1\) uniformly in probability.  Dividing the first coordinate by \(\sqrt{\widehat V}\), using \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and integrating the limiting normal density over \([-z_\alpha,z_\alpha]\) yields
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb P_{p,\gamma}\{\theta_N\in I_{\mathrm{emp}}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.                                           \tag{20}
\]
Only exact prelimit martingales on \([0,T_N]\) were used, so no upper relation between \(T_N\) and \(N\) entered.  Lemma \(\mathrm{boutilier\mbox{-}anchor\mbox{-}scope}\) supplies exactly the final scope comparison and no class-transfer premise.

%%% FIELD proof-fitted-center-wald
Work over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fit}}\).  Its primitive sampling and dynamics conditions are the same as those used in Theorem \(\mathrm{uniform\mbox{-}joint\mbox{-}law}\), and it imposes only \(N,T_N\to\infty\).  Define
\[
 S_N=\sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_N),
 \qquad R_N=\sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat p-p).
\]
The joint theorem gives, uniformly,
\[
 (S_N,R_N)\Rightarrow(S,R),
 \quad \operatorname{Var}(S)=V,
 \quad \operatorname{Var}(R)=\Gamma,
 \quad \operatorname{Cov}(R,S)=\Gamma A,                      \tag{1}
\]
and \(\widehat p-p=O_{\mathbb P}((NT_N)^{-1/2})\).

The detailed-balance calculation in Lemma \(\mathrm{exact\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}capacity\mbox{-}derivative}\) gives
\[
 \vartheta_N(p;M_N)=\theta_N.                                 \tag{2}
\]
On the event that the line segment between \(p\) and the clipped \(\widehat p\) lies in \([c/2,2C]^3\), Taylor's formula is applied while holding the implemented integer \(M_N\) fixed.  The derivative and Hessian bounds of that lemma yield
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widetilde\theta_N-\theta_N)
 &=[\nabla_{p'}\vartheta_N(p';M_N)|_{p'=p}]^{\mathsf T}R_N+\mathcal R_N,\\
 |\mathcal R_N|
 &\leq\frac{C_*}{2}\sqrt{NT_N}\sqrt N\,\|\widehat p-p\|^2
 =O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2})=o_{\mathbb P}(1).                 \tag{3}
 \end{split}
\]
Uniform consistency and the compact interior rate bounds make the complement of this event, including any fallback event, have probability tending to zero.  The derivative convergence now gives
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widetilde\theta_N-\theta_N)
 =A^{\mathsf T}R_N+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A).             \tag{4}
\]
No comparison between \(T_N\) and \(N\) appears in (3).

The derivative limit has the advertised fixed-capacity form.  Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \nabla a_0=((1-a_0)/d,-a_0/d,0)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 D\nabla a=(1-a,-a,-a)^{\mathsf T},
\]
so
\[
 \nabla a_0-DH\nabla a
 =((1-a_0)/d-(1-a)H,-a_0/d+aH,aH)^{\mathsf T}=A.              \tag{5}
\]
Thus the variance in (4) is \(Q=A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A\).

Subtracting (4) from the first coordinate in (1) gives
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\widetilde\theta_N)
 \Rightarrow S-A^{\mathsf T}R.                                \tag{6}
\]
Using every covariance identity in (1),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(S-A^{\mathsf T}R)
 =V+A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A-2A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A
 =V-A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A=W.                                 \tag{7}
\]
This also proves \(W\geq0\), because it is the variance of the displayed Gaussian linear combination.

For Wald coverage, Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) gives \(H\geq1/D\).  The service coordinate is \(A_\nu=aH\), while \(\Gamma_{\nu\nu}=\nu/a\).  Hence
\[
 Q=A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A\geq\frac\nu a(aH)^2=\nu aH^2,     \tag{8}
\]
whose infimum on the compact positive rate box is positive.  Moreover,
\[
 \widehat\gamma_N-\gamma_N
 =-\sqrt N\{a(\widehat p)-a(p)\}=O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma_N-\gamma<N^{-1/2}.                       \tag{9}
\]
The explicit Gaussian integrals defining \(H\), and therefore \(A,Q\), are continuous on compact positive-rate and bounded-staffing sets.  Equations (1), (8), and (9) imply \(\widehat Q/Q\to1\) uniformly in probability.  Studentizing (4) and integrating the standard normal density over \([-z_\alpha,z_\alpha]\) proves
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb P_{p,\gamma}\{\theta_N\in I_{\mathrm{fit}}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.                                           \tag{10}
\]
Equations (4)--(10) establish both limits, the fixed-staffing correction, and the absence of an upper horizon restriction.

%%% FIELD proof-diffusion-fitted-center-corollary
Let \(p'\in[c/2,2C]^3\), \(1\leq M\leq N-1\), and put
\[
 g'=\frac{M-Na(p')}{\sqrt N}.
\]
The control invariant mean divided by \(N\) is exactly \(a_0(p')\).  By the definition of the scaled treatment state, its invariant mean divided by \(N\) is
\[
 a(p')+N^{-1/2}\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}}X_{1,N}^{p',M}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\vartheta_N(p';M)-
 \{a_0(p')-a(p')-N^{-1/2}m_{p',g'}\}\\
 &\qquad=-N^{-1/2}
 \{\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}}X_{1,N}^{p',M}-m_{p',g'}\}. \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]

Apply Theorem \(\mathrm{kink\mbox{-}residual\mbox{-}bound}\) with the compact rate bounds \(c/2,2C\) and staffing radius \(G+1\).  Its enrollment threshold is finite, so \(N\to\infty\) eventually exceeds it.  The centering consequence proved there turns (1) into
\[
 \left|\vartheta_N(p';M)-
 \{a_0(p')-a(p')-N^{-1/2}m_{p',g'}\}\right|
 \leq K N^{-1}                                                \tag{2}
\]
uniformly whenever \(|g'|\leq G+1\).

The rate-score coordinate of Theorem \(\mathrm{uniform\mbox{-}joint\mbox{-}law}\) gives
\[
 \widehat p-p=O_{\mathbb P}((NT_N)^{-1/2}).                   \tag{3}
\]
Smoothness of \(a\) on the compact positive rate box yields
\[
 \widehat\gamma_N-\gamma_N
 =-\sqrt N\{a(\widehat p)-a(p)\}=O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma_N-\gamma<N^{-1/2}.                       \tag{4}
\]
Since \(T_N\to\infty\), the event \(\widehat p\in[c/2,2C]^3\) and \(|\widehat\gamma_N|\leq G+1\) has probability tending to one uniformly.  Take \(p'=\widehat p,M=M_N\) in (2) on this event.  Definition \(\mathrm{fitted\mbox{-}center}\) then gives
\[
 \overline\theta_N-\widetilde\theta_N=O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1}) \tag{5}
\]
uniformly.  Under \(T_N/N\to0\),
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\overline\theta_N-\widetilde\theta_N)
 =O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt{T_N/N})=o_{\mathbb P}(1).               \tag{6}
\]
Theorem \(\mathrm{fitted\mbox{-}center\mbox{-}wald}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widetilde\theta_N-\theta_N)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,Q),
 \qquad \widehat Q/Q\to1,                                    \tag{7}
\]
uniformly, with \(Q\) bounded away from zero.  Equations (6)--(7) prove
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\overline\theta_N-\theta_N)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,Q).
\]
Studentization by \(\widehat Q\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and integration of the normal density over \([-z_\alpha,z_\alpha]\) yield
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb P_{p,\gamma}\{\theta_N\in I_{\mathrm{diff}}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Equation (5) proves the claimed \(O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1})\) approximation, and (6) isolates the sole use of the fitted-horizon condition.

%%% FIELD proof-sanity-reductions
Fix \(\lambda,\tau>0\) and \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and let \(\nu\downarrow0\) through the continuous extension.  Write
\[
 d=\lambda+\tau,
 \qquad D_\nu=d+\nu,
 \qquad q_\nu=\frac{\lambda(\tau+\nu)}{d+\nu}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Then \(D_\nu\to d\) and \(q_\nu\to q_0=\lambda\tau/d\).  The treatment generator is
\[
 (\mathcal L_{\nu,\gamma}g)(x)
 =[-dx-\nu\min(x,\gamma)]g'(x)+q_\nu g''(x),                  \tag{2}
\]
which converges on compact sets to \(\mathcal L_0\), and equals \(\mathcal L_0\) at \(\nu=0\).

Let \(r_{\nu,\gamma}\) be the unnormalized density in the definition of \(\pi_\gamma\), with \(D,q\) replaced by \(D_\nu,q_\nu\), and let \(\pi_{\nu,\gamma}=r_{\nu,\gamma}/\int r_{\nu,\gamma}\).  Pointwise,
\[
 r_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\longrightarrow
 r_0(x)=\exp\{-dx^2/(2q_0)\}.                                 \tag{3}
\]
On a fixed interval \(0\leq\nu\leq\bar\nu\), positivity and continuity give \(0<q_-\leq q_\nu\leq q_+\).  On the left density branch,
\[
 r_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\leq e^{-dx^2/(2q_+)},
\]
while on the right branch Young's inequality gives a constant \(C_\gamma\) such that
\[
 -\frac{dx^2}{2q_\nu}-\frac{\nu\gamma x}{q_\nu}
 +\frac{\nu\gamma^2}{2q_\nu}
 \leq-\frac{dx^2}{4q_+}+C_\gamma.                             \tag{4}
\]
Thus \((1+x^2)r_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\) has a common integrable Gaussian envelope.  Dominated convergence for the normalizer and first two moments proves
\[
 \pi_{\nu,\gamma}\to\pi_0
 \quad\hbox{in weighted }L^1(1+x^2),
 \qquad m_{\nu,\gamma}\to0.                                 \tag{5}
\]

To pass the Poisson energy to the limit, note that for \(y>x\),
\[
 -(d+\nu)(y-x)
 \leq b_{\nu,\gamma}(y)-b_{\nu,\gamma}(x)
 \leq-d(y-x).                                                  \tag{6}
\]
Synchronous coupling therefore gives
\[
 e^{-(d+\nu)t}(y-x)
 \leq X_t^y-X_t^x\leq e^{-dt}(y-x).                            \tag{7}
\]
For the centered resolvent
\[
 h_{\nu,\gamma}(x)=
 \int_0^\infty\{\mathbb E_xX_t-m_{\nu,\gamma}\}\,dt
\]
equation (7) implies
\[
 \frac1{d+\nu}\leq h_{\nu,\gamma}'(x)\leq\frac1d.           \tag{8}
\]
It also verifies \(-\mathcal L_{\nu,\gamma}h_{\nu,\gamma}=x-m_{\nu,\gamma}\); uniqueness from Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) identifies it with the flux solution.  Hence
\[
 \sup_x\left|h_{\nu,\gamma}'(x)-\frac1d\right|
 \leq\frac\nu{d(d+\nu)}.                                    \tag{9}
\]
Equations (1), (5), and (9) yield
\[
 V_\gamma
 =2q_\nu\int\{h_{\nu,\gamma}'(x)\}^2\pi_{\nu,\gamma}(x)\,dx
 \longrightarrow\frac{2q_0}{d^2}
 =\frac{2\lambda\tau}{(\lambda+\tau)^3}.                    \tag{10}
\]
This proves the first reduction.

For the fixed-system reduction, fix \(N\), an arm \(\ell\), and an interior treatment capacity.  Strict positivity of every available birth and death rate makes the finite chain irreducible.  On its scaled lattice set
\[
 f_{\ell,N}(x)=x-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N},
 \quad
 -G_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}=f_{\ell,N},
 \quad
 \mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}u_{\ell,N}=0.                     \tag{11}
\]
The centered solution exists uniquely because the generator's null space consists of constants.  If \(r_N(x,y)\) is the transition rate, put
\[
 c_{\ell,N}(x)=\sum_{y\ne x}r_N(x,y)
 \{u_{\ell,N}(y)-u_{\ell,N}(x)\}^2.                          \tag{12}
\]
Dynkin's identity gives
\[
 M_T=u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(T))-u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(0))
 +\int_0^Tf_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt,
 \quad
 \langle M\rangle_T=\int_0^Tc_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt. \tag{13}
\]
The generator product identity and stationarity show that
\[
 \begin{split}
 V_{\ell,N}
 &:=\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}c_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N})\\
 &=-2\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}
   [u_{\ell,N}G_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}]
 =2\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}[u_{\ell,N}f_{\ell,N}].         \tag{14}
 \end{split}
\]
Reversibility and the spectral gap \(d\) from Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) give, by finite eigen-expansion,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left\{T^{-1}\int_0^T c_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt\right\}
 \leq\frac{2\operatorname{Var}_{\rho_{\ell,N}}(c_{\ell,N})}{dT}. \tag{15}
\]
For fixed \(N\), the variance in the numerator is finite, so
\[
 T^{-1}\langle M\rangle_T\longrightarrow V_{\ell,N}
 \quad\hbox{in probability}.                                  \tag{16}
\]
Every jump of \(M_T/\sqrt T\) is bounded by a fixed constant divided by \(\sqrt T\).  The compensated jump-exponential calculation used in Theorem \(\mathrm{uniform\mbox{-}joint\mbox{-}law}\), now with (16), gives
\[
 M_T/\sqrt T\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,V_{\ell,N}).             \tag{17}
\]
The endpoint term in (13), divided by \(\sqrt T\), vanishes because the state space is finite.  Therefore
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt T}\int_0^T
 \{X_{\ell,N}(t)-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N}\}\,dt
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,V_{\ell,N}),                        \tag{18}
\]
or equivalently
\[
 \sqrt{NT}\left[
 T^{-1}\int_0^T\frac{K_{\ell,N}(t)}N\,dt
 -\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}\frac{K_{\ell,N}}N\right]
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,V_{\ell,N}).                        \tag{19}
\]
The energy is positive: if (14) vanished, every edge difference of \(u_{\ell,N}\) would vanish; irreducibility would make \(u_{\ell,N}\) constant, contradicting (11) because the coordinate is nonconstant.

For the smallest interior treatment instance, \(N=2,M_N=1\), the unnormalized invariant weights are
\[
 1,qquad \frac{2\lambda}{\tau+\nu},
 \qquad\frac{2\lambda^2}{(\tau+\nu)(2\tau+\nu)}.            \tag{20}
\]
They are all positive.  If \(A_k=\sum_{j=0}^k\rho_{1,2}(j)f_{1,2}(j)\), exact flux summation gives
\[
 u_{1,2}(x_{k+1})-u_{1,2}(x_k)
 =-\frac{A_k}{\rho_{1,2}(k)B_k},
 \qquad
 V_{1,2}=2\sum_{k=0}^1\frac{A_k^2}{\rho_{1,2}(k)B_k}>0.       \tag{21}
\]
Indeed \(A_0<0\), and centering gives \(A_1=-\rho_{1,2}(2)f_{1,2}(2)<0\).  This checks the claim exactly at the smallest interior chain.  Lemma \(\mathrm{boutilier\mbox{-}anchor\mbox{-}scope}\) supplies only the stated compatibility comparison; none of (11)--(21) transfers this equal-arm result to the broader cited class.

%%% FIELD proof-stationary-poincare
Fix \(p\in\mathcal P\) and \(\gamma\in[-G,G]\), and write
\[
 b_\gamma(x)=-dx-\nu\min(x,\gamma).
\]
The displayed density satisfies \(q(\log\pi_\gamma)'=b_\gamma\) on both sides of the kink.  The one-sided derivatives agree at \(x=\gamma\), because both equal \(-D\gamma/q\).  Hence the potential \(U_\gamma=-\log\pi_\gamma\), up to its normalizing constant, is continuously differentiable and
\[
 U_\gamma''(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 D/q,&x<\gamma,\\
 d/q,&x>\gamma.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The rate assumptions give \(D>d>0\) and \(q>0\), proving the curvature assertion.

Let \(X_t^x,X_t^y\) solve the diffusion with the same Brownian path.  Monotonicity of \(u\mapsto\min(u,\gamma)\) gives
\[
 (x-y)\{b_\gamma(x)-b_\gamma(y)\}\leq-d(x-y)^2.              \tag{2}
\]
The Brownian terms cancel in the difference, so integration of (2) yields
\[
 |X_t^x-X_t^y|\leq e^{-dt}|x-y|.                              \tag{3}
\]
For smooth compactly supported \(f\), difference quotients in the initial state and (3) imply
\[
 |(P_{\gamma,t}f)'(x)|
 \leq e^{-dt}P_{\gamma,t}|f'|(x).                             \tag{4}
\]
One way to remove the single nondifferentiability point is to approximate the drift by smooth drifts whose derivatives remain in \([-D,-d]\); (3) is preserved, and dominated convergence gives (4).  Jensen's inequality and invariance of \(\pi_\gamma\) give
\[
 \int\{(P_{\gamma,t}f)'\}^2\,d\pi_\gamma
 \leq e^{-2dt}\int P_{\gamma,t}(f'^2)\,d\pi_\gamma
 =e^{-2dt}\int(f')^2\,d\pi_\gamma.                           \tag{5}
\]

The identity \(q(\log\pi_\gamma)'=b_\gamma\), integrated on the two half-lines, gives
\[
 -\int u\mathcal L_\gamma v\,d\pi_\gamma
 =q\int u'v'\,d\pi_\gamma                                  \tag{6}
\]
for smooth compactly supported \(u,v\); the two boundary terms at \(\gamma\) cancel because \(u,v,\pi_\gamma\) are continuous there.  Apply (6) with \(u=v=P_{\gamma,t}f\).  The semigroup equation gives
\[
 -\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\gamma}(P_{\gamma,t}f)
 =2q\int\{(P_{\gamma,t}f)'\}^2\,d\pi_\gamma.                \tag{7}
\]
Couple a process from a fixed initial state to an independent stationary initial state.  Equation (3) and the Gaussian second moment of \(\pi_\gamma\) show that \(P_{\gamma,t}f\to\int f\,d\pi_\gamma\) in \(L^2(\pi_\gamma)\).  Integrating (7) from zero to infinity and using (5),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\gamma}(f)
 &=2q\int_0^\infty\int\{(P_{\gamma,t}f)'\}^2\,d\pi_\gamma\,dt\\
 &\leq2q\int_0^\infty e^{-2dt}\,dt\int(f')^2\,d\pi_\gamma
 =\frac qd\int(f')^2\,d\pi_\gamma.                           \tag{8}
 \end{split}
\]
For a locally absolutely continuous \(f\) with \(f,f'\in L^2(\pi_\gamma)\), truncate and mollify.  The functions and their derivatives converge in \(L^2(\pi_\gamma)\), so both sides of (8) converge.  This proves the lemma with all boundary and integrability conditions explicit.

Fix \(p\in\mathcal P\).  Use the unnormalized invariant density
\[
 w_\gamma(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 \exp\{-Dx^2/(2q)\},&x\leq\gamma,\\
 \exp\{-dx^2/(2q)-\nu\gamma x/q+\nu\gamma^2/(2q)\},&x>\gamma.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
Its branches and their first derivatives in \(x\) agree at \(x=\gamma\).  For fixed \(x\ne\gamma\),
\[
 r_\gamma(x):=\partial_\gamma\log w_\gamma(x)
 =-\frac\nu q(x-\gamma)_+,
 \qquad
 \dot b_\gamma(x):=\partial_\gamma b_\gamma(x)
 =-\nu\mathbf1\{x>\gamma\}.                                 \tag{2}
\]
On every compact set of \(\gamma\)-values, (1)--(2) and their difference quotients are dominated by a polynomial times a common Gaussian density.  Dominated convergence therefore gives, in weighted \(L^1\),
\[
 \partial_\gamma\pi_\gamma(x)
 =\pi_\gamma(x)\{r_\gamma(x)-\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}r_\gamma(X)\}, \tag{3}
\]
and permits differentiation of all expectations below.

We justify differentiation of the Poisson solution.  For \(u\ne0\), subtract
\(-\mathcal L_{\gamma+u}h_{\gamma+u}=x-m_{\gamma+u}\) and
\(-\mathcal L_\gamma h_\gamma=x-m_\gamma\).  With difference quotients \(D_uh=(h_{\gamma+u}-h_\gamma)/u\), \(D_ub=(b_{\gamma+u}-b_\gamma)/u\), and \(D_um=(m_{\gamma+u}-m_\gamma)/u\), one gets
\[
 -\mathcal L_\gamma D_uh=D_ub\,h_{\gamma+u}'-D_um.           \tag{4}
\]
Integration against \(\pi_\gamma\) shows that the right side is centered.  Monotonicity of the kink gives \(|D_ub|\leq\nu\), and Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) gives \(0<h_{\gamma+u}'\leq d^{-1}\).  Thus the variance of the right side is at most \(\nu^2/d^2\).  Multiply (4) by the centered version of \(D_uh\), use the integration-by-parts identity in Lemma \(\mathrm{stationary\mbox{-}poincare}\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and that lemma's inequality:
\[
 \begin{split}
 q\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}\{(D_uh')^2\}
 &\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\gamma}(D_uh)}\frac\nu d\\
 &\leq\sqrt{\frac qd\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}\{(D_uh')^2\}}\frac\nu d.
                                                                    \tag{5}
 \end{split}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}\{(D_uh')^2\}
 \leq\frac{\nu^2}{qd^3}.                                     \tag{6}
\]

The flux formula, the Gaussian domination after (2), and (6) imply that the difference quotients converge in the stationary Sobolev norm.  Indeed, every weakly convergent subsequence passes in (4) to the same centered equation, while applying (5) to the difference of two such equations gives strong convergence and uniqueness.  If \(k_\gamma=\partial_\gamma h_\gamma\), the limit satisfies
\[
 -\mathcal L_\gamma k_\gamma
 =\dot b_\gamma h_\gamma'-m_\gamma',
 \qquad
 m_\gamma'=\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}(\dot b_\gamma h_\gamma'), \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}\{(k_\gamma')^2\}
 \leq\frac{\nu^2}{qd^3}.                                     \tag{8}
\]
The second identity in (7) follows by integrating the first; it also verifies the centering needed for inversion of \(-\mathcal L_\gamma\).

Since \(r_\gamma'(x)=-(\nu/q)\mathbf1\{x>\gamma\}\) almost everywhere, Lemma \(\mathrm{stationary\mbox{-}poincare}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\gamma}(r_\gamma)
 \leq\frac qd\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}\{(r_\gamma')^2\}
 \leq\frac{\nu^2}{qd}.                                       \tag{9}
\]
Also \(1/D\leq h_\gamma'\leq1/d\), so the elementary range bound for a variance gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\gamma}(h_\gamma'^2)
 \leq\frac1{4d^4}.                                            \tag{10}
\]
Differentiate \(V_\gamma=2q\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}(h_\gamma'^2)\), using (3), (7), and the strong Sobolev convergence:
\[
 V_\gamma'
 =4q\mathbb E_{\pi_\gamma}(h_\gamma'k_\gamma')
 +2q\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_\gamma}(h_\gamma'^2,r_\gamma). \tag{11}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz, (8)--(10), and \(\mathbb E h_\gamma'^2\leq d^{-2}\) imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 |V_\gamma'|
 &\leq4q\left(\frac1{d^2}\frac{\nu^2}{qd^3}\right)^{1/2}
 +2q\left(\frac1{4d^4}\frac{\nu^2}{qd}\right)^{1/2}\\
 &=\frac{5\nu\sqrt q}{d^{5/2}}.                              \tag{12}
 \end{split}
\]
The common domination in (2) and strong convergence above show that (11) is continuous in the interior of the staffing interval and has the corresponding one-sided limits at its endpoints.  Integrating (12) between \(\gamma_1\) and \(\gamma_2\) proves
\[
 |V_{\gamma_1}(p)-V_{\gamma_2}(p)|
 \leq\frac{5\nu\sqrt q}{d^{5/2}}|\gamma_1-\gamma_2|.
\]
This proves every assertion of the lemma.
