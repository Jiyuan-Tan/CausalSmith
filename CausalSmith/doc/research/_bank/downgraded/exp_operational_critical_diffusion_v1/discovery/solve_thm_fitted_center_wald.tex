%%% FIELD proof-uniform-joint-law
All probabilities and expectations in this proof are under a member of \(\mathcal M\).  In particular, the two chains start in their invariant laws and are independent by Definition \(\mathrm{model\mbox{-}class}\).  Write
\[
 f_{\ell,N}(x)=x-\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}X_{\ell,N},
 \qquad -G_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}=f_{\ell,N},
 \qquad \mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}u_{\ell,N}=0.
\]
These Poisson solutions exist and have the edge and moment bounds asserted by Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\).  Dynkin's formula gives the square-integrable martingales
\[
 M_{\ell,N}(t)=u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))-u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(0))
 -\int_0^tG_{\ell,N}u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(s))\,ds
\]
and hence the exact identity
\[
 \int_0^{T_N}f_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(t))\,dt
 =M_{\ell,N}(T_N)-
 \{u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(T_N))-u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N}(0))\}.       \tag{1}
\]
The edge bound in that lemma makes \(u_{\ell,N}\) uniformly \(d^{-1}\)-Lipschitz on its scaled lattice.  Stationary centering of \(u_{\ell,N}\), followed by the uniform second-moment bound for \(X_{\ell,N}\), therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma,\ell}\mathbb E_{\rho_{\ell,N}}u_{\ell,N}(X_{\ell,N})^2<\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Since \(T_N\to\infty\), stationarity, (1), and (2) imply, uniformly in the parameter box,
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_N)
 =T_N^{-1/2}\{M_{0,N}(T_N)-M_{1,N}(T_N)\}+o_{\mathbb P}(1).       \tag{3}
\]

Define the compensated marked counts
\[
 U_{\lambda,N}=C_\lambda-\lambda E_\lambda,
 \qquad U_{\tau,N}=C_\tau-\tau E_\tau,
 \qquad U_{\nu,N}=C_\nu-\nu E_\nu.                              \tag{4}
\]
The generator assumptions identify the intensities in (4), and the marked-history assumption makes recoveries of the two types distinguishable.  Thus these are pairwise orthogonal square-integrable martingales with predictable brackets
\[
 \langle U_{\lambda,N}\rangle=\lambda E_\lambda,
 \qquad \langle U_{\tau,N}\rangle=\tau E_\tau,
 \qquad \langle U_{\nu,N}\rangle=\nu E_\nu.                     \tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
 s_\lambda=2-a_0-a,
 \qquad s_\tau=a_0+a,
 \qquad s_\nu=a.
\]
The stationary control mean is \(Na_0\).  The invariant-law convergence in Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\), together with uniform integrability from the moment bounds, gives \(N^{-1}\mathbb E K_{1,N}\to a\) uniformly.  The time-average conclusion of Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\) then yields
\[
 \frac{1}{NT_N}(E_\lambda,E_\tau,E_\nu)
 \longrightarrow(s_\lambda,s_\tau,s_\nu)
 \quad\hbox{uniformly in probability}.                           \tag{6}
\]
Compact positivity implies \(\inf_{p\in\mathcal P}\min_j s_j>0\).  Consequently the prescribed fallbacks and clipping are inactive with probability tending to one uniformly, and, componentwise,
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat p_j-p_j)
 =\frac{U_{j,N}/\sqrt{NT_N}}{E_j/(NT_N)}
 =\frac{U_{j,N}}{s_j\sqrt{NT_N}}+o_{\mathbb P}(1).               \tag{7}
\]

We next calculate the entire limiting predictable bracket before invoking any limit theorem.  Define the four-dimensional martingale
\[
 \mathcal Z_N(t)=\left(
 \frac{M_{0,N}(t)-M_{1,N}(t)}{\sqrt{T_N}},
 \frac{U_{\lambda,N}(t)}{s_\lambda\sqrt{NT_N}},
 \frac{U_{\tau,N}(t)}{s_\tau\sqrt{NT_N}},
 \frac{U_{\nu,N}(t)}{s_\nu\sqrt{NT_N}}
 \right)^{\mathsf T}.                                           \tag{8}
\]
The stationary Poisson-energy identity and arm independence give
\[
 \langle\mathcal Z_N^{(1)}\rangle(T_N)
 =V_{0,N}+V_{1,N}+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \longrightarrow V_0+V_\gamma=V,                               \tag{9}
\]
where the last convergence is Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\), and the time-average replacement is supplied by the final assertion of Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\).  Equations (5)--(6) give the lower diagonal block
\[
 \operatorname{diag}\left\{\frac{\lambda}{s_\lambda},
 \frac{\tau}{s_\tau},\frac{\nu}{s_\nu}\right\}=\Gamma.       \tag{10}
\]

For completeness, the cross bracket can be read event by event.  On an upward control edge, the scaled Poisson increment has stationary edge average tending to \(d^{-1}\); on an upward treatment edge it has average tending to \(H\).  Downward edges have the corresponding negatives after the natural index shift.  Because the occupation coordinate in (8) is \(M_{0,N}-M_{1,N}\), the un-divided limiting cross-bracket rates with the three count martingales are
\[
 \begin{split}
 c_\lambda
 &=\lambda\{(1-a_0)/d-(1-a)H\}=\lambda A_\lambda,\\
 c_\tau
 &=\tau\{-a_0/d+aH\}=\tau A_\tau,\\
 c_\nu
 &=\nu aH=\nu A_\nu.
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{11}
\]
Here the factors \(1-a_0,a_0,1-a,a\) are the limiting normalized at-risk occupations, and every edge-average limit is exactly the one stated in Lemma \(\mathrm{finite\mbox{-}chain\mbox{-}poisson\mbox{-}controls}\).  Dividing the three entries in (11) by \(s_\lambda,s_\tau,s_\nu\), as in (8), gives \(A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\).  Combining (9)--(11),
\[
 \langle\mathcal Z_N\rangle(T_N)
 \longrightarrow
 \Sigma:=\begin{pmatrix}V&A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\\
                          \Gamma A&\Gamma\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\hbox{uniformly in probability}.                          \tag{12}
\]

We derive the Gaussian conclusion from the primitive jump and bracket conditions.  The Poisson edge bound implies that an occupation-martingale jump in (8) is at most \(d^{-1}/\sqrt{NT_N}\).  A normalized count jump is at most
\(\{\min_j s_j\}^{-1}/\sqrt{NT_N}\).  Hence, for each fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^4\), the scalar martingale \(L_N=t^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Z_N\) has maximal jump bounded by a deterministic \(\varepsilon_N\to0\), uniformly in the parameter box, and
\[
 \langle L_N\rangle(T_N)\longrightarrow t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t. \tag{13}
\]
To see directly that (13) implies the required scalar central limit theorem here, stop when the bracket first exceeds a fixed \(R>t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t+1\).  The jump formula for the exponential, together with
\[
 e^{iu}=1+iu-u^2/2+r(u),\qquad |r(u)|\leq |u|^3/6,               \tag{14}
\]
shows, after compensating the jumps, that
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\exp\left\{iL_N+\frac12\langle L_N\rangle\right\}-1\right|
 \leq C_{t,R}\varepsilon_N R                                  \tag{15}
\]
for the stopped martingale and stopped bracket.  Indeed, every cubic remainder is at most \(\varepsilon_N\) times its quadratic term, and the predictable sum of the quadratic terms is the stopped bracket; the exponential factor is bounded because that bracket is at most \(R+o(1)\).  The right-hand side of (15) tends to zero.  By (13), the stopped bracket converges in probability to \(t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t\), so bounded convergence in (15) gives the stopped characteristic-function limit \(\exp\{-t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t/2\}\).  The probability of stopping tends to zero, again by (13).  Thus
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{it^{\mathsf T}\mathcal Z_N(T_N)\}
 \longrightarrow \exp\{-t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t/2\}.             \tag{16}
\]
This argument also covers \(t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t=0\).  The Cram\'er--Wold criterion applied to (16), followed by (3) and (7), proves the displayed joint Gaussian law.

All constants used above are uniform over the compact rate and staffing set.  More explicitly, if uniform convergence failed, a failing parameter sequence would have a convergent subsequence; (2), (6), (9), (11), and the jump bounds hold on that subsequence with the same constants, while the Gaussian integrals defining \(V,H,A,\Gamma\) vary continuously.  Equations (12)--(16) would then contradict the alleged failure.  This proves the asserted uniformity.

It remains to studentize.  Equation (7) implies \(\widehat p-p=o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) uniformly, and
\[
 \widehat\gamma_N-\gamma_N
 =-\sqrt N\{a(\widehat p)-a(p)\}=O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma_N-\gamma<N^{-1/2}.                           \tag{17}
\]
The explicit Gaussian integrals defining \(V\) are continuous on every compact positive-rate and bounded-staffing set.  Moreover
\(V\geq V_0=2\lambda\tau/d^3\), whose infimum on \(\mathcal P\) is positive.  Therefore \(\widehat V/V\to1\) uniformly in probability.  Studentization of the first coordinate and continuity of the standard normal distribution function give
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb P_{p,\gamma}\{\theta_N\in I_{\mathrm{emp}}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Only exact prelimit martingales on \([0,T_N]\) were used; no path approximation on a growing interval and no upper relation between \(T_N\) and \(N\) entered the proof.  Finally, Lemma \(\mathrm{boutilier\mbox{-}anchor\mbox{-}scope}\) gives precisely the comparison in the last two sentences of the theorem and supplies no class-transfer premise.

%%% FIELD proof-fitted-center-wald
Work over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fit}}\).  Its primitive sampling and dynamics conditions are the same as those used in Theorem \(\mathrm{uniform\mbox{-}joint\mbox{-}law}\), and it imposes only the joint divergences \(N,T_N\to\infty\).  Denote
\[
 S_N=\sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_N),
 \qquad R_N=\sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat p-p).
\]
The joint theorem gives, uniformly,
\[
 (S_N,R_N)\Rightarrow(S,R),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(S)=V,quad
 \operatorname{Var}(R)=\Gamma,quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(R,S)=\Gamma A,                              \tag{17}
\]
and in particular \(\widehat p-p=O_{\mathbb P}((NT_N)^{-1/2})\).

The product weights in Definition \(\mathrm{fitted\mbox{-}center}\) are the detailed-balance weights of the two finite birth--death generators.  Hence Lemma \(\mathrm{exact\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}capacity\mbox{-}derivative}\) gives
\(\vartheta_N(p;M_N)=\theta_N\).  On the event that the clipped estimator lies in \([c/2,2C]^3\), Taylor's formula is applied to \(p'\mapsto\vartheta_N(p';M_N)\) while holding the implemented integer \(M_N\) fixed.  The same lemma yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widetilde\theta_N-\theta_N)
 &=\nabla_{p'}\vartheta_N(p';M_N)|_{p'=p}^{\mathsf T}R_N+\mathcal R_N,\\
 |\mathcal R_N|
 &\leq \frac{C_*}{2}\sqrt{NT_N}\sqrt N\,\|\widehat p-p\|^2
 =O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2})=o_{\mathbb P}(1).                   
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{18}
\]
Uniform consistency from (17) and the compact interior rate bounds show that clipping or fallback has probability tending to zero uniformly.  The derivative convergence in that lemma therefore turns (18) into
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widetilde\theta_N-\theta_N)
 =A^{\mathsf T}R_N+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A).               \tag{19}
\]
No comparison between \(T_N\) and \(N\) occurs in (18); only \(T_N\to\infty\) is required.

The formula for the derivative limit can also be checked componentwise.  Since
\[
 \nabla a_0=((1-a_0)/d,-a_0/d,0)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 D\nabla a=(1-a,-a,-a)^{\mathsf T},
\]
one has
\[
 \nabla a_0-DH\nabla a
 =((1-a_0)/d-(1-a)H,-a_0/d+aH,aH)^{\mathsf T}=A.                \tag{20}
\]
Thus (19) has variance \(Q=A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A\), exactly as stated.

Subtracting (19) from the first coordinate in (17) gives
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\widetilde\theta_N)
 \Rightarrow S-A^{\mathsf T}R.                                  \tag{21}
\]
Using all three covariance identities in (17),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(S-A^{\mathsf T}R)
 =V+A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A-2A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A
 =V-A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A=W.                                  \tag{22}
\]
In particular, (22) also proves \(W\geq0\), because it is the variance of the displayed Gaussian linear combination.

For Wald coverage, Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) gives \(H\geq1/D\).  Since the service coordinate is \(A_\nu=aH\) and \(\Gamma_{\nu\nu}=\nu/a\),
\[
 Q=A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A\geq\frac{\nu}{a}(aH)^2=\nu aH^2.     \tag{23}
\]
Compact positive rate bounds make the right-hand side uniformly positive.  Moreover,
\[
 \widehat\gamma_N-\gamma_N
 =-\sqrt N\{a(\widehat p)-a(p)\}=O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma_N-\gamma<N^{-1/2}.                          \tag{24}
\]
The explicit integral defining \(H\), and hence \(A\) and \(Q\), is continuous on compact positive-rate and bounded-staffing sets.  Equations (17), (23), and (24) thus imply
\(\widehat Q/Q\to1\) uniformly in probability.  Dividing (19) by \(\sqrt{\widehat Q}\), using the fixed quantile supplied by \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and applying continuity of the standard normal distribution function proves
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb P_{p,\gamma}\{\theta_N\in I_{\mathrm{fit}}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Together, (19)--(24) prove every assertion, including the fixed-staffing correction and the absence of an upper horizon restriction.

%%% FIELD proof-kink-residual-bound
Put \(\delta=N^{-1/2}\) and \(g=\gamma_N\).  The ceiling identity gives
\(0\leq g-\gamma<\delta\), so \(|g|\leq G+1\).  All constants below may therefore be chosen uniformly over the compact positive rate box and the enlarged staffing interval \([-G-1,G+1]\).  Write \(h_g=h_{p,g}\) and
\[
 b_g(x)=-dx-\nu\min\{x,g\}.
\]
By Theorem \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\),
\[
 \frac1D\leq h_g'(x)\leq\frac1d,                               \tag{25}
\]
and the Poisson equation in Definition \(\mathrm{diffusion\mbox{-}poisson}\) is
\[
 qh_g''(x)=-(x-m_g)-b_g(x)h_g'(x).                              \tag{26}
\]

We first establish derivative envelopes, including at the kink.  The explicit piecewise-Gaussian density has Gaussian tails uniformly on the enlarged compact parameter set, so the stationary identity for \(x^2\) follows by applying the generator identity first to smooth compactly supported truncations and then passing to the limit.  Since
\[
 \mathcal L_gx^2=2xb_g(x)+2q
 =-2dx^2-2\nu x\min\{x,g\}+2q
\]
and
\(x\min\{x,g\}\geq-(G+1)|x|\), Young's inequality gives constants \(c_1,c_2<\infty\), depending only on \((c,C,G)\), such that
\[
 0=\int\mathcal L_gx^2\,\pi_g(x)\,dx
 \leq-c_1\int x^2\pi_g(x)\,dx+c_2.                            \tag{27}
\]
Thus \(\sup_{p,g}\int x^2\pi_g(x)\,dx<\infty\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\sup_{p,g}|m_g|<\infty\).  The quantities \(q^{-1}\), \(d\), \(D\), and \(\nu\) are uniformly bounded on the rate box.  Therefore (25)--(26) imply
\[
 |h_g''(x)|\leq C_1(1+|x|).                                    \tag{28}
\]
On each open side of \(g\), differentiation of (26) gives
\[
 qh_g'''(x)=-1-b_g'(x)h_g'(x)-b_g(x)h_g''(x),                  \tag{29}
\]
where \(b_g'=-D\) on the left and \(b_g'=-d\) on the right.  Equations (25), (28), and (29) yield
\[
 |h_g'''(x)|\leq C_2(1+x^2),\qquad x\neq g.                    \tag{30}
\]
The drift \(b_g\) is continuous at \(g\), as are \(h_g'\) and the right-hand side of (26).  Hence \(h_g''\) is continuous at \(g\).  Since it is continuously differentiable on either side with the locally bounded derivative (30), \(h_g''\) is locally absolutely continuous across \(g\).  Taylor's formula with integral remainder is consequently valid even when the increment interval contains the kink; the value of \(h_g'''\) at the single point \(g\) is immaterial.

At \(x=(k-Na)/\sqrt N\), let
\[
 B_N(x)=\lambda(N-k),
 \qquad D_N(x)=\tau k+\nu\min\{k,M_N\},
\]
with the unavailable endpoint rate equal to zero.  Because
\(k=Na+\sqrt N x\), \(M_N=Na+\sqrt N g\), and \(a=\lambda/D\), direct algebra gives at every lattice state, including both endpoints,
\[
 \delta\{B_N(x)-D_N(x)\}=b_g(x),                               \tag{31}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\delta^2}{2}\{B_N(x)+D_N(x)\}
 =q+\frac\delta2\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}.             \tag{32}
\]
Taylor expansion of the two available lattice increments, using the integral remainder justified above, now gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_{1,N}h_g(x)
 &=b_g(x)h_g'(x)
 +\left[q+\frac\delta2\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}\right]h_g''(x)
 +R_N(x),\\
 |R_N(x)|
 &\leq\frac{\delta^3}{6}\{B_N(x)+D_N(x)\}
 \mathop{\operatorname{ess\,sup}}_{|u-x|\leq\delta}|h_g'''(u)|.
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{33}
\]
The rate bounds imply \(B_N+D_N\leq3CN\).  From (30),
\[
 |R_N(x)|\leq C_3\delta(1+x^2).                                \tag{34}
\]
Also \(|\min(x,g)|\leq |x|+G+1\), so (28) bounds the extra second-derivative term in (33) by \(C_4\delta(1+x^2)\).  Subtracting
\(\mathcal L_gh_g=b_gh_g'+qh_g''\) proves the pointwise, kink-inclusive estimate
\[
 |(G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_g)h_g(x)|
 \leq C_5\delta(1+x^2)                                         \tag{35}
\]
at every \(x\in E_{1,N}\).

It remains only to average (35) under the exact stationary law.  Applying (31)--(32) to the function \(x^2\) gives the exact finite-chain identity
\[
 G_{1,N}x^2
 =2xb_g(x)+2q+delta\{(\tau-\lambda)x+\nu\min(x,g)\}.          \tag{36}
\]
Using again \(x\min(x,g)\geq-(G+1)|x|\), \(|\min(x,g)|\leq|x|+G+1\), \(\delta\leq1\), and Young's inequality, there is a uniform constant \(C_6\) such that
\[
 G_{1,N}x^2\leq-dx^2+C_6.                                      \tag{37}
\]
The state space is finite, and the treatment chain starts in \(\rho_{1,N}\); hence stationarity gives \(\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}G_{1,N}X_{1,N}^2=0\).  It follows from (37) that
\[
 \sup_{N,p,\gamma}\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}X_{1,N}^2\leq C_6/d. \tag{38}
\]
Taking expectations in (35) and using (38) proves the theorem with
\(K_{\mathrm{res}}=C_5(1+C_6/d)\), enlarged if necessary to replace \(d\) by its compact-set lower bound.

The calculation also supplies the requested boundary and ceiling stress test.  The exact birth--death product law is
\[
 \rho_{1,N}(k)=Z_N^{-1}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}
 \frac{\lambda(N-j)}{\tau(j+1)+\nu\min\{j+1,M_N\}},            \tag{39}
\]
with the empty product equal to one.  Estimate (35) holds before multiplication by any weight in (39), with the same constant at all asymmetric corners of the compact rate box and for every value of the ceiling phase \(g-\gamma\in[0,\delta)\).  Thus no stationary product weighting can create a nonvanishing scaled residual.  As the smallest algebraic interior-capacity check, take \(N=2\) and \(M_N=1\): the states \(k=0,1,2\) are respectively the left endpoint, the kink state, and the right endpoint, and (31)--(35) cover every one of the four directed available transitions.  For larger \(N\), a jump crossing \(g\) is already covered by the absolute-continuity remainder in (33).

Finally, stationarity and the diffusion Poisson equation show the useful centering consequence
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}X_{1,N}-m_g\right|
 &=\left|\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}}
 \{(G_{1,N}-\mathcal L_g)h_g\}(X_{1,N})\right|\\
 &\leq K_{\mathrm{res}}N^{-1/2}.                               
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{40}
\]
This also rules out the early-kill alternative of an order-one residual at a corner, endpoint, kink-crossing edge, or ceiling phase.

%%% FIELD proof-diffusion-fitted-center-corollary
Let \(p'\in[c/2,2C]^3\), let \(1\leq M\leq N-1\), and set
\[
 g'=(M-Na(p'))/\sqrt N.
\]
The control invariant mean is exactly \(a_0(p')\).  By the definition of the scaled treatment state, the treatment invariant mean divided by \(N\) is
\[
 a(p')+N^{-1/2}\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}}X_{1,N}^{p',M}.
\]
Consequently the exact product-law effect and its diffusion approximation differ by
\[
 \vartheta_N(p';M)-\{a_0(p')-a(p')-N^{-1/2}m_{p',g'}\}
 =-N^{-1/2}\{\mathbb E_{\rho_{1,N}^{p',M}}X_{1,N}^{p',M}-m_{p',g'}\}. \tag{41}
\]

Theorem \(\mathrm{kink\mbox{-}residual\mbox{-}bound}\), and specifically its centering consequence (40), applies with new fixed compact constants to the rate box \([c/2,2C]^3\) and any fixed enlarged staffing window.  It follows from (41) that, uniformly whenever \(|g'|\leq G+1\) and for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \left|\vartheta_N(p';M)-\{a_0(p')-a(p')-N^{-1/2}m_{p',g'}\}\right|
 \leq K N^{-1}.                                                  \tag{42}
\]

Under the rate-score coordinate of Theorem \(\mathrm{uniform\mbox{-}joint\mbox{-}law}\),
\[
 \widehat p-p=O_{\mathbb P}((NT_N)^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly.  Smoothness of \(a\) on the compact positive rate box therefore gives
\[
 \widehat\gamma_N-\gamma_N
 =-\sqrt N\{a(\widehat p)-a(p)\}
 =O_{\mathbb P}(T_N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma_N-\gamma<N^{-1/2}.                          \tag{43}
\]
Since \(T_N\to\infty\), (43) implies that the event
\(\widehat p\in[c/2,2C]^3\) and \(|\widehat\gamma_N|\leq G+1\) has probability tending to one uniformly.  On this event take \(p'=\widehat p\) and \(M=M_N\) in (42).  Definitions \(\mathrm{fitted\mbox{-}center}\) then give
\[
 \overline\theta_N-\widetilde\theta_N=O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1})
 \quad\hbox{uniformly}.                                         \tag{44}
\]
Under the additional primitive condition \(T_N/N\to0\),
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\overline\theta_N-\widetilde\theta_N)
 =O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt{T_N/N})=o_{\mathbb P}(1).                 \tag{45}
\]
Theorem \(\mathrm{fitted\mbox{-}center\mbox{-}wald}\) gives
\(\sqrt{NT_N}(\widetilde\theta_N-\theta_N)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,Q)\) and \(\widehat Q/Q\to1\) uniformly, with \(Q\) bounded away from zero.  Equations (44)--(45) and Slutsky's argument therefore give
\[
 \sqrt{NT_N}(\overline\theta_N-\theta_N)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,Q).
\]
Studentization by \(\widehat Q\), the assumption \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and continuity of the standard normal distribution function yield
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P,\,|\gamma|\leq G}
 \left|\mathbb P_{p,\gamma}\{\theta_N\in I_{\mathrm{diff}}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus (44) proves the stated \(O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1})\) approximation, and (45) identifies exactly where, and only where, the additional horizon restriction is used.
