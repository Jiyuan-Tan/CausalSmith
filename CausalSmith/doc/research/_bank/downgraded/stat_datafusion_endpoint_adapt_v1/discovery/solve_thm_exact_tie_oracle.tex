%%% FIELD proof-oracle-positivity
Put \(a=a_m\) and \(s_{n,m}=\min\{a,n^{-1/2}\}\).  The deterministic pair
\[
 C_L=[-b,-b+ka],\qquad C_U=[b-ka,b]
\]
obeys the range restriction and covers every value of the two endpoint
functionals.  Hence it belongs to \(\mathcal I_{n,m}\) by
\texttt{ass:interval-map}, \texttt{ass:range-projection}, and
\texttt{ass:joint-honesty}.  By \texttt{def:length-risk} and
\texttt{def:local-oracle},
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(h)\le 2ka.                                                    \tag{1}
\]

We next derive a testing lower bound at an arbitrary \(h\in H_m\), including
faces and corners.  Set \(r=s_{n,m}/20\), and alter only profile \(v=0\).
If \(T_0(h)\ge a/2\), define
\[
 g_{0,j}=(1-2r/a)h_{0,j}.
\]
Since \(0<2r/a\le 1/10\), this vector is in the cube, every coordinate moves
by at most \(2r\), and
\[
 T_0(h)-T_0(g)=\frac{2r}{a}T_0(h)\ge r.                                  \tag{2}
\]
If \(T_0(h)<a/2\), choose \(s_j=\operatorname{sign}(h_{0,j})\), assigning
\(s_j=1\) at zero, and set
\[
 g_{0,j}=s_j\bigl(|h_{0,j}|+\min\{2r,a-|h_{0,j}|\}\bigr).
\]
Again \(g\in H_m\) and \(|g_{0,j}-h_{0,j}|\le2r\).  Because
\(\min\{2r,u\}\ge(2r/a)u\) for \(0\le u\le a\),
\[
 T_0(g)-T_0(h)
 =\frac1m\sum_j\min\{2r,a-|h_{0,j}|\}
 \ge\frac{2r}{a}\{a-T_0(h)\}>r.                                        \tag{3}
\]
Set \(g_{1,j}=h_{1,j}\) in both cases.

It remains to show that this perturbation is statistically close.  Under
\texttt{def:observed-law}, only observations with
\(V=0,J=j,S=0,T=0\) change law.  Conditional on their pairing sign \(z\),
the two Bernoulli probabilities are \(p_0+zh_{0,j}\) and \(p_0+zg_{0,j}\).
All such probabilities lie in \([1/2,2/3]\), since
\(p_0=13/22\) and \(a\le1/32\), and therefore \(p(1-p)\ge2/9\).
For Bernoulli probabilities \(p,q\), direct expansion of the likelihood
ratio gives
\[
 \mathbb E_p\left[\left(\frac{d\operatorname{Bern}(q)}
 {d\operatorname{Bern}(p)}-1\right)^2\right]
 =\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
The probability of a fixed \((v,j,z,S,T)\) informative design cell is
\(1/(16m)\).  Consequently,
\[
 \chi^2(P_{m,g},P_{m,h})
 \le \frac{9}{16m}\sum_{j=1}^m(g_{0,j}-h_{0,j})^2
 \le\frac94r^2.                                                         \tag{4}
\]
Independence in \texttt{def:observed-law} yields
\[
 1+\chi^2(P_{m,g}^n,P_{m,h}^n)
 =\{1+\chi^2(P_{m,g},P_{m,h})\}^n
 \le \exp(9nr^2/4)\le \exp(9/1600).                                    \tag{5}
\]
For any two dominated laws, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to their likelihood
ratio proves
\(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P,Q)}\).  Thus (5) gives
\[
 \|P_{m,g}^n-P_{m,h}^n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\tau_0:=\tfrac12\sqrt{e^{9/1600}-1}<0.04.                           \tag{6}
\]

Let \(C\in\mathcal I_{n,m}\), allowing the independent randomization in
\texttt{ass:interval-map} to be appended identically under both laws.
Honesty at \(h\), honesty at \(g\), and (6) imply that, under
\(P_{m,h}^n\), the interval \(C_L\) contains both \(L_m(h)\) and
\(L_m(g)\) with probability at least
\(1-2\delta_0-\tau_0\).  Equations (2)--(3) and
\texttt{def:profile-magnitudes} give
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,h}^n}\operatorname{length}(C_L)
 \ge kr(1-2\delta_0-\tau_0).
\]
Taking the infimum specified in \texttt{def:local-oracle} gives
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(h)\ge
 \frac{1-2\delta_0-\tau_0}{20}\,k\min\{a_m,n^{-1/2}\}.                \tag{7}
\]
The coefficient is positive because \(\delta_0=0.05\).  Combining (1) and
(7) proves the stated inequalities with constants depending only on the
fixed \(\delta_0,c_0\).  In particular (7) is valid on every face and corner,
so the ratio in \texttt{def:adaptation-factor} has a strictly positive
denominator.

%%% FIELD proof-nuisance-learnability
Fix \((v,j)\), write \(N=N_{v,j}\), \(q=1/(8m)\), and let
\(\widetilde h\) be the uncapped retained-cell average when \(N>0\).
By \texttt{def:observed-law}, \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\).
Conditional on retention, the variables \(R_i=Z_i(B_i-p_0)\) are independent,
have mean
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,h}}[R_i\mid V_i=v,J_i=j,S_i=0,T_i=v]
 =\frac12\sum_{z\in\{-1,1\}}z\{p_0+zh_{v,j}-p_0\}=h_{v,j},             \tag{8}
\]
and have variance at most one.  Since \(h_{v,j}\in[-a_m,a_m]\), scalar
projection in \texttt{def:capped-estimator} cannot increase the error.
It follows that
\[
 \mathbb E[(\widehat h_{v,j}-h_{v,j})^2\mid N]
 \le \mathbf1\{N=0\}a_m^2+\mathbf1\{N>0\}N^{-1}.                      \tag{9}
\]
For integer \(N\ge1\), \(N^{-1}\le2/(N+1)\), and
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{N+1}
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qt)^n\,dt
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \le\frac{8m}{n+1}.                                                     \tag{10}
\]
The event \(N=0\) contributes to the expectation in (10), and
\(a_m^2\le1\).  Hence (9)--(10) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,h}^n}(\widehat h_{v,j}-h_{v,j})^2
 \le\frac{24m}{n+1}.                                                     \tag{11}
\]
Both the estimate and the parameter lie in \([-a_m,a_m]\), so the same
expectation is at most \(4a_m^2\).  Averaging over the \(2m\) coordinates
therefore gives
\[
 \frac1{2m}\sum_{v=0}^1\sum_{j=1}^m
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,h}^n}(\widehat h_{v,j}-h_{v,j})^2
 \le24\min\{a_m^2,m/(n+1)\}.                                          \tag{12}
\]
For positive \(x,y\), \(\min(x,y)\le x^{1/3}y^{2/3}\).  Applying this
with \(x=c_0^2/m^2\) and \(y=m/(n+1)\), and using \(a_m=c_0/m\), proves
\[
 \min\{a_m^2,m/(n+1)\}
 \le c_0^{2/3}(n+1)^{-2/3}\le c_0^{2/3}n^{-2/3}.                        \tag{13}
\]

We finally prove the asserted uniform tail bound, including empty cells.
The retained response has range length at most two.  For a centered variable
in an interval of length two, convexity of the exponential and optimization
of the resulting two-point bound give
\(\mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-\mathbb EX)}\le e^{\lambda^2/2}\).
Exponential Markov applied at \(\lambda=Nt/2\) and at its negative therefore
gives, for \(0<t\le1\),
\[
 \Pr\{|\widetilde h-h_{v,j}|>t\mid N\}\mathbf1\{N>0\}
 \le2e^{-Nt^2/2}\mathbf1\{N>0\}.
\]
Adding the empty-cell event and using the binomial probability generating
function yields
\[
 \Pr\{|\widehat h_{v,j}-h_{v,j}|>t\}
 \le3\mathbb E e^{-Nt^2/2}
 =3\{1-q(1-e^{-t^2/2})\}^n
 \le3\exp\{-nt^2/(32m)\},                                               \tag{14}
\]
where \(1-e^{-t^2/2}\ge t^2/4\) for \(0<t\le1\), and projection again
cannot increase error.  A union bound over \(2m\) coordinates changes the
leading constant in (14) to \(6m\).

Let \(x=\sqrt{m\log(6m/\eta)/n}\).  If \(x<a_m\), then
\(t=\sqrt{32}\,x<1\), and (14) gives simultaneous error at most \(t\)
with probability at least \(1-\eta\).  If \(x\ge a_m\), the deterministic
bound \(|\widehat h_{v,j}-h_{v,j}|\le2a_m\) applies.  Thus, for a numerical
constant \(C_0\ge\max\{24,\sqrt{32},2,24c_0^{2/3}\}\), both (12)--(13) and
\[
 \max_{v,j}|\widehat h_{v,j}-h_{v,j}|
 \le C_0\min\left\{a_m,
       \sqrt{\frac{m\log(6m/\eta)}n}\right\}
\]
hold as claimed.  Every calculation used the sampling law in
\texttt{def:observed-law}; no nuisance-rate premise was introduced.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-tie-oracle
Write \(a=a_m\) and first prove the lower bound.  At the reference \(h=0\),
fix \(0<d\le a\), draw independent Rademacher signs \(\varepsilon_j\), and
set \(G_{0,j}=d\varepsilon_j\), \(G_{1,j}=0\).  Let \(M_{0,d}\) be the
mixture of \(P_{m,G}^n\) over these signs.  For two independent prior draws
\(G,G'\), direct multiplication of the Bernoulli likelihood ratios under
\texttt{def:observed-law} gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,0}}
 \left[\frac{dP_{m,G}}{dP_{m,0}}
       \frac{dP_{m,G'}}{dP_{m,0}}\right]
 =1+\frac{A}{m}\sum_{j=1}^mG_{0,j}G'_{0,j},
 \qquad A=\frac1{8p_0(1-p_0)}=\frac{121}{234}.                           \tag{15}
\]
The product experiment, \(1+x\le e^x\), independence of the signs, and
the elementary inequality \(\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}\) imply
\[
 1+\chi^2(M_{0,d},P_{m,0}^n)
 \le\left\{\cosh\left(\frac{nAd^2}{m}\right)\right\}^{m}
 \le\exp\left(\frac{A^2n^2d^4}{2m}\right).                              \tag{16}
\]
Choose a numerical \(\kappa>0\), depending only on \(\delta_0\), so small
that
\[
 \tau_1:=\frac12\sqrt{\exp(A^2\kappa^4/2)-1}
 \le\frac{1-2\delta_0}{2},
\]
and put \(d=\min\{a,\kappa m^{1/4}/\sqrt n\}\).  Cauchy--Schwarz and (16)
then give \(\|M_{0,d}-P_{m,0}^n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\tau_1\).
Every prior realization has \(T_0(G)=d\) and \(T_1(G)=0\).  Hence joint
honesty from \texttt{def:honest-class}, transferred from the mixture to the
null, forces \(C_L\) to contain both \(-b\) and \(-b+kd\), under the null,
with probability at least \(1-2\delta_0-\tau_1\).  Thus
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,0}^n}\operatorname{length}(C_L)
 \ge kd(1-2\delta_0-\tau_1).                                             \tag{17}
\]
Repeating (15)--(17) with independent signs in profile \(v=1\) gives the
same lower bound for \(C_U\).  Infimizing over the class in
\texttt{def:local-oracle} proves
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(0)\ge c k\min\{a,m^{1/4}/\sqrt n\}.                           \tag{18}
\]

For the matching upper bound, suppose first that \(n\ge2\).  For
\(v\in\{0,1\}\), define the proof-intermediate vector
\[
 W_{i,v,j}=8m\mathbf1\{V_i=v,J_i=j,S_i=0,T_i=v\}R_i,
 \qquad q_v=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mh_{v,j}^2,
\]
and the order-two statistic
\[
 Q_v=\frac1{mn(n-1)}\sum_{i\ne l}\sum_{j=1}^mW_{i,v,j}W_{l,v,j}.          \tag{19}
\]
Equation (8) and the informative-cell probability \(1/(8m)\) show
\(\mathbb EW_{i,v,j}=h_{v,j}\), \(\mathbb EW_{i,v,j}^2\le8m\), and
\(\mathbb EQ_v=q_v\).  Distinct coordinates of \(W_{i,v}\) cannot be
nonzero simultaneously.  For \(H(W,W')=m^{-1}\langle W,W'\rangle\), this
gives
\[
 \mathbb EH(W,W')^2\le64m,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}\{\mathbb E[H(W,W')\mid W]\}
 \le\mathbb E\frac{\langle W,h_v\rangle^2}{m^2}\le8q_v.                 \tag{20}
\]
Writing the centered order-two kernel as the sum of its two first-order
projections and its degenerate remainder makes these three terms orthogonal.
Counting diagonal and off-diagonal covariances therefore yields directly
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Q_v)
 \le\frac{4}{n}(8q_v)+\frac{2}{n(n-1)}(64m)
 \le\frac{32q_v}{n}+\frac{256m}{n^2}.                                   \tag{21}
\]

Let \(\alpha=\delta_0/2\) and
\[
 \Delta_n=\frac{32}{\alpha n}+\frac{32\sqrt{m/\alpha}}n,
 \qquad
 H_v=\min\{a^2,\max(0,2Q_v+\Delta_n)\},
 \qquad D_v=[0,\sqrt{H_v}].                                             \tag{22}
\]
From (21) and the inequality
\(\Pr(|X-\mathbb EX|>t)\le\operatorname{Var}(X)/t^2\), which follows by
integrating \((X-\mathbb EX)^2\) over that event, we have with probability
at least \(1-\alpha\)
\[
 |Q_v-q_v|\le
 \sqrt{\frac{32q_v}{\alpha n}+\frac{256m}{\alpha n^2}}
 \le\sqrt{\frac{32q_v}{\alpha n}}+\frac{16\sqrt{m/\alpha}}n.            \tag{23}
\]
Since \(2\sqrt{32q_v/(\alpha n)}\le q_v+32/(\alpha n)\), (23) implies
\(2Q_v+\Delta_n\ge q_v\).  Also \(q_v\le a^2\), so \(H_v\ge q_v\).
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 T_v(h)=\frac1m\sum_j|h_{v,j}|\le\sqrt{q_v}\le\sqrt{H_v}.               \tag{24}
\]
The union bound over \(v=0,1\) makes (24) simultaneous with probability at
least \(1-\delta_0\).  Therefore the endpoint images
\[
 C_L=-b+kD_0,\qquad C_U=b-kD_1
\]
form a measurable range-projected member of \(\mathcal I_{n,m}\).  This is
an explicit finite-sample honest rule and (19) has no empty-cell division.

At \(h=0\), (21) gives \(\mathbb E|Q_v|\le16\sqrt m/n\), while (22) gives
\(H_v\le\min\{a^2,2|Q_v|+\Delta_n\}\).  Jensen's inequality follows here
from the concavity identity for the square root, and yields
\[
 \mathbb E_0\sqrt{H_v}
 \le\min\left\{a,\sqrt{2\mathbb E_0|Q_v|+\Delta_n}\right\}
 \le C_0\min\{a,m^{1/4}/\sqrt n\}.                                      \tag{25}
\]
The summed endpoint length is \(k\sum_v\sqrt{H_v}\).  For \(n=1\), use
the two full endpoint ranges; because \(a\le1/32\) and \(m^{1/4}\ge1\),
their summed length \(2ka\) has the order on the right side of (25).
Equations (18) and (25) prove the theorem uniformly in \(n,m\).  In
particular, the upper rule is honest on the whole cube, not merely at the
tie where its expected length is evaluated.

%%% FIELD proof-low-information-profile
Write \(a=a_m\), and assume
\[
 n\le\frac{\sqrt m}{20a^2}.                                               \tag{26}
\]
Fix an arbitrary reference \(h\in H_m\).  Independently over all
\((v,j)\), draw \(G_{v,j}\in\{-a,a\}\) with
\[
 \Pr(G_{v,j}=a)=\frac{1+h_{v,j}/a}{2}.
\]
Thus \(\mathbb EG_{v,j}=h_{v,j}\), including degenerate probabilities at
cube faces, while every realization satisfies \(T_0(G)=T_1(G)=a\).  Let
\(M_h\) denote the resulting mixture of \(P_{m,G}^n\).

For independent prior draws \(G,G'\), put \(U=G-h\), \(U'=G'-h\), and
\[
 A_{v,j}(h)=\frac1{16}\sum_{z\in\{-1,1\}}
 \frac1{(p_0+zh_{v,j})(1-p_0-zh_{v,j})}.
\]
The likelihood multiplication used in (15), now at the arbitrary reference,
is exactly
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{m,h}}
 \left[\frac{dP_{m,G}}{dP_{m,h}}
       \frac{dP_{m,G'}}{dP_{m,h}}\right]
 =1+\frac1m\sum_{v=0}^1\sum_{j=1}^m
 A_{v,j}(h)U_{v,j}U'_{v,j}.                                               \tag{27}
\]
The probability bounds used in the positivity proof give
\(A_{v,j}(h)\le9/16<1\).  Moreover \(U_{v,j}U'_{v,j}\) is centered; its
possible values are \((a-h_{v,j})^2\), \((a+h_{v,j})^2\), and
\(h_{v,j}^2-a^2\), whose range has length at most \(4a^2\).

For completeness, if a centered random variable \(X\) lies in an interval
of length \(L\), convexity bounds its exponential by the chord joining the
two endpoint exponentials; maximizing the resulting Bernoulli expression
over its mean location gives
\(\mathbb Ee^{tX}\le e^{t^2L^2/8}\).  Apply this proved bound coordinatewise
to (27), after \((1+x)^n\le e^{nx}\).  Independence of all prior coordinates
then yields
\[
 1+\chi^2(M_h,P_{m,h}^n)
 \le\exp\left(\frac{4n^2a^4}{m}\right)\le e^{1/100}.                     \tag{28}
\]
Consequently, by the Cauchy--Schwarz total-variation inequality already
derived in the proof of \texttt{thm:oracle-positivity},
\[
 \|M_h-P_{m,h}^n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\tau_2:=\tfrac12\sqrt{e^{1/100}-1}<0.051.                            \tag{29}
\]

For any \(C\in\mathcal I_{n,m}\), honesty under every prior realization
implies that the common corner endpoint pair \((-b+ka,b-ka)\) is covered
under \(M_h\) with probability at least \(1-\delta_0\).  Honesty at \(h\)
and (29) imply that, under \(P_{m,h}^n\), both the reference and corner pairs
are contained in their respective intervals with probability at least
\(1-2\delta_0-\tau_2\).  On this event the summed length is at least
\[
 k\{a-T_0(h)+a-T_1(h)\}.                                                  \tag{30}
\]
Combining (30) with the lower bound of
\texttt{thm:oracle-positivity}, and using
\(\max(x,y)\ge(x+y)/2\), proves
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(h)\ge
 ck\{2a-T_0(h)-T_1(h)+\min(a,n^{-1/2})\}.                                \tag{31}
\]

We construct the pointwise-oracle upper rule without relaxing honesty.
For a fixed sign array \(s_{v,j}\in\{-1,1\}\), define
\[
 A_{i,v}^{s}=8\mathbf1\{V_i=v,S_i=0,T_i=v\}s_{v,J_i}R_i,
 \quad
 \widehat S_v^{s}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nA_{i,v}^{s},
 \quad
 S_v^{s}(g)=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^ms_{v,j}g_{v,j}.                           \tag{32}
\]
The sampling probabilities in \texttt{def:observed-law} and (8) give
\[
 \mathbb E_gA_{i,v}^{s}=S_v^{s}(g),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_g(A_{i,v}^{s})\le\mathbb E_g(A_{i,v}^{s})^2\le8.    \tag{33}
\]
For \(\alpha=\delta_0/2\), put \(r_\alpha=\sqrt{8/(\alpha n)}\) and
\[
 D_v^s=\left[
 \Pi_{[0,a]}(\widehat S_v^s-r_\alpha),\ a\right].                       \tag{34}
\]
For every \(g\in H_m\), \(S_v^s(g)\le T_v(g)\).  Equations (33) and the
second-moment tail argument used in (23) show that, except on an event of
probability \(\alpha\),
\(\widehat S_v^s-r_\alpha\le S_v^s(g)\le T_v(g)\).  Projection onto
\([0,a]\) preserves the last inequality.  A union bound makes both intervals
in (34) cover \((T_0(g),T_1(g))\) with probability at least
\(1-\delta_0\), for every \(g\).  Their endpoint images therefore define a
member of \(\mathcal I_{n,m}\) for every fixed array \(s\).

At the reference \(h\), choose the fixed constants
\(s_{v,j}=\operatorname{sign}(h_{v,j})\), assigning sign one at zero.  This
choice defines a different globally honest competitor for each point in the
pointwise infimum; it does not give the executed rule knowledge of an unknown
parameter.  Now \(S_v^s(h)=T_v(h)\).  Since scalar projection is
one-Lipschitz and \(T_v(h)\in[0,a]\), (33)--(34) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E_h\operatorname{length}(D_v^s)
 &\le\min\left\{a,
 a-T_v(h)+r_\alpha+\mathbb E_h|\widehat S_v^s-T_v(h)|\right\}\\
 &\le\min\left\{a,a-T_v(h)+
 \left(\sqrt{8/\alpha}+\sqrt8\right)n^{-1/2}\right\}.                  \tag{35}
 \end{split}
\]
If \(n^{-1/2}\ge a\), the sum of (35) is at most \(2a\); if
\(n^{-1/2}<a\), it is at most the sum of the two deficits plus a constant
times \(n^{-1/2}\).  After multiplication by \(k\), both cases give
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(h)\le
 C_0k\{2a-T_0(h)-T_1(h)+\min(a,n^{-1/2})\}.                              \tag{36}
\]
Together, (31) and (36) prove the uniform claim under (26), including exact
ties, heterogeneous coordinates, and cube boundaries.

%%% FIELD proof-low-information-adaptation
Let \(s_{n,m}=\min\{a_m,n^{-1/2}\}\), and retain the low-information
condition of \texttt{thm:low-information-profile}.  The deterministic
full-range pair used in the proof of \texttt{thm:oracle-positivity} belongs
to \(\mathcal I_{n,m}\) and has risk \(2ka_m\) at every \(h\).  The lower
bound in \texttt{thm:oracle-positivity} therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak A_{n,m}
 \le C_0\frac{a_m}{s_{n,m}}
 =C_0\max\{1,a_m\sqrt n\}.                                               \tag{37}
\]

For the converse, use the corner prior in the proof of
\texttt{thm:low-information-profile} at reference \(h=0\), now drawing all
\(2m\) coordinates independently and symmetrically from
\(\{-a_m,a_m\}\).  Equations (28)--(29) give total variation at most
\(\tau_2<0.051\) between its mixture \(M_0\) and \(P_{m,0}^n\).  For an
arbitrary \(C\in\mathcal I_{n,m}\), the common corner endpoint pair is
covered under \(M_0\) with probability at least \(1-\delta_0\).  The null
endpoint pair is covered under \(M_0\) with probability at least
\(1-\delta_0-\tau_2\).  On the intersection, the summed interval length is
at least \(2ka_m\).  Hence, writing \(\Pi\) for this corner prior,
\[
 \int R_{n,m}(C,G)\,d\Pi(G)
 =\mathbb E_{M_0}\{\operatorname{length}(C_L)+
                    \operatorname{length}(C_U)\}
 \ge2ka_m(1-2\delta_0-\tau_2).                                          \tag{38}
\]
Every corner \(G\) has \(T_0(G)=T_1(G)=a_m\).  The upper half of
\texttt{thm:low-information-profile} therefore gives
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(G)\le C_0k s_{n,m}.                                         \tag{39}
\]
If
\(Q_C=\sup_{h\in H_m}R_{n,m}(C,h)/\ell_{n,m}(h)\), positivity from
\texttt{thm:oracle-positivity} makes this ratio well defined.  Equations
(38)--(39) imply
\[
 2ka_m(1-2\delta_0-\tau_2)
 \le\int Q_C\ell_{n,m}(G)\,d\Pi(G)
 \le Q_CC_0ks_{n,m}.
\]
Thus \(Q_C\ge c a_m/s_{n,m}\) for every honest, possibly randomized rule.
Infimizing according to \texttt{def:adaptation-factor} and combining with
(37) proves
\[
 \mathfrak A_{n,m}\asymp\max\{1,a_m\sqrt n\}.
\]
At a sample size within fixed factors of
\(\sqrt m/(20a_m^2)\), one has
\(a_m\sqrt n\asymp m^{1/4}\); hence the factor reaches order
\(m^{1/4}\) at the upper boundary of the regime.  The same corner mixture
establishes the lower bound for the entire decision class, while the single
full-range pair establishes the upper bound.

%%% FIELD partial-full-profile-frontier
The closed part is
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(0)\asymp k\min\{a_m,m^{1/4}n^{-1/2}\}
 \quad\text{for all }n,m,
\]
and, uniformly over \(h\in H_m\),
\[
 \ell_{n,m}(h)\asymp
 k\{2a_m-T_0(h)-T_1(h)+\min(a_m,n^{-1/2})\},\qquad
 \mathfrak A_{n,m}\asymp\max\{1,a_m\sqrt n\},
 \quad n\le\frac{\sqrt m}{20a_m^2}.
\]
The first display is attained by the capped quadratic statistic in the proof
of \texttt{thm:exact-tie-oracle}; the second adaptation upper bound is
attained by one profile-independent full-range interval pair.

For fixed \(m\) and \(0<\xi\le a_m\), the cellwise exponential calculation
used in \texttt{thm:nuisance-learnability} gives constants \(c,C>0\) such
that
\[
 \sup_{h\in H_m(\xi)}P_{m,h}^n
 \left\{\exists(v,j):N_{v,j}=0\ \text{or}\
 \widehat s_{v,j}\ne\operatorname{sign}(h_{v,j})\right\}
 \le Cm\exp\{-cn\min(1,\xi^2)/m\}.                                      \tag{40}
\]
On the complement of this event, ratio expansion of each nonempty cell mean
gives, uniformly over the fixed compact separated set,
\[
 \sqrt n\binom{\overline L_{n,m}-L_m(h)}
                    {\overline U_{n,m}-U_m(h)}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \binom{8k\sum_j\operatorname{sign}(h_{0,j})
 \mathbf1\{V_i=0,J_i=j,S_i=0,T_i=0\}(R_i-h_{0,j})}
 {-8k\sum_j\operatorname{sign}(h_{1,j})
 \mathbf1\{V_i=1,J_i=j,S_i=0,T_i=1\}(R_i-h_{1,j})}
 +o_{P_{m,h}^n}(1).                                                       \tag{41}
\]
The two summands have zero cross-product because the strata are disjoint,
their variances are bounded away from zero, and their third moments are
uniformly bounded for fixed \(m\).  The elementary characteristic-function
expansion of this finite triangular array, together with consistency of the
empirical second moments, therefore proves the jointly independent Gaussian
limit and the stated limiting coverage of
\(C^{\mathrm{sign}}_{n,m}\).  What remains unavailable is the globally
honest full-profile family needed for the claims involving
\(\Phi_{n,m}\), \(C_{n,m}^{\star}\), and the oracle-ratio assertion.
