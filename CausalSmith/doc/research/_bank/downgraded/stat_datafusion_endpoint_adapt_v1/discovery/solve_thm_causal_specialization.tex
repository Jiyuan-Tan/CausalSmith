\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Fix \(m\geq 1\) and \(h\in H_m\).  Under
\texttt{def:observed-law}, every covariate value
\(x=(m,v,j,z)\) in the support of \(X\) has probability
\(1/(4m)>0\), and, conditionally on \(X=x\), every source--treatment
event has probability \(1/4>0\).  Thus the conditional means below
are defined.  The observed-margin requirement
\texttt{ass:observed-margin}, the identity \(Y=1/2+B\) in
\texttt{def:observed-law}, and the numerical values of the Bernoulli
probabilities give
\[
 \mu_v(x):=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid X=x,S=0,T=v]
     =\frac{12}{11}+z h_{v,j},
 \qquad
 \mu_{1-v}(x):=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid X=x,S=0,T=1-v]
     =\frac89,
 \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_Q[Y\mid X=x,S=1,T=t]=1
 \quad\text{for each }t\in\{0,1\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, \(1/2+p_0=1/2+13/22=12/11\) and
\(1/2+7/18=8/9\).  Since \(h\in H_m\) and
\(a_m=c_0/m\leq1/32\),
\[
 0<\frac{197}{352}
   =\frac{13}{22}-\frac1{32}
 \leq p_0+z h_{v,j}
 \leq\frac{13}{22}+\frac1{32}
   =\frac{219}{352}<1.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus the observed Bernoulli probabilities are uniformly interior; in
particular every observational factual mean in (1) is strictly
positive, so the ratios used below have nonzero denominators.

We first determine exactly the restrictions imposed on the unobserved
potential-outcome means.  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), write
\[
 \mu_t(x)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid X=x,S=0,T=t],\qquad
 r_t(x)=\mathbb E_Q[Y(t)\mid X=x,S=0,T=1-t],
 \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
 q_t(x)=\mathbb E_Q[Y(t)\mid X=x,S=0].
 \tag{5}
\]
By consistency, \texttt{ass:consistency}, the factual mean of
\(Y(t)\) in the event \(T=t\) is \(\mu_t(x)\).  Treatment has
conditional probability \(1/2\) in the observational source by
\texttt{def:observed-law}; hence the law of total expectation yields
\[
 q_t(x)=\frac{\mu_t(x)+r_t(x)}2.
 \tag{6}
\]
The confounding cap \texttt{ass:confounding-cap}, together with the
strict positivity following from (1)--(3), is equivalent to
\[
 \frac45\mu_t(x)\leq r_t(x)\leq\frac65\mu_t(x).
 \tag{7}
\]
Combining (6) and (7) gives
\[
 \frac9{10}\mu_t(x)\leq q_t(x)
     \leq\frac{11}{10}\mu_t(x).
 \tag{8}
\]
On the experimental source, randomization
\texttt{ass:experimental-randomization}, consistency, experimental
treatment positivity, and (2) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_Q[Y(t)\mid X=x,S=1]
 =\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid X=x,S=1,T=t]=1.
 \tag{9}
\]
The selection cap \texttt{ass:selection-cap} and (9) therefore give
\[
 \frac45\leq q_t(x)\leq\frac65.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently every \(Q\in\mathcal M_{m,h}\) must satisfy
\[
 q_t(x)\in I\{\mu_t(x)\}:=
 \left[
  \max\left\{\frac45,\frac9{10}\mu_t(x)\right\},
  \min\left\{\frac65,\frac{11}{10}\mu_t(x)\right\}
 \right].
 \tag{11}
\]

We next prove that every simultaneous selection from (11), including
its boundary, is feasible under the bounded-support condition.  For the
low observational mean \(\mu_{\ell}=8/9\), (11) becomes
\[
 I(\mu_{\ell})=\left[\frac45,\frac{44}{45}\right],
 \qquad
 2q-\mu_{\ell}\in
 \left[\frac{32}{45},\frac{16}{15}\right]\subset(0,2).
 \tag{12}
\]
For a high observational mean
\(\mu_H(s)=12/11+s\), where \(s=z h_{v,j}\) and
\(|s|\leq1/32\), one has
\((9/10)\mu_H(s)>4/5\).  Moreover,
\((11/10)\mu_H(s)\leq6/5\) exactly when \(s\leq0\).
Thus
\[
 I\{\mu_H(s)\}
 =\left[
    \frac9{10}\mu_H(s),
    \min\left\{\frac65,\frac{11}{10}\mu_H(s)\right\}
   \right].
 \tag{13}
\]
For every \(q\) in (13), the corresponding missing mean obeys
\[
 \frac{373}{440}
 =\frac45\left(\frac{12}{11}-\frac1{32}\right)
 \leq 2q-\mu_H(s)\leq\frac{72}{55}<2.
 \tag{14}
\]
The lower inequality follows from the lower endpoint of (13).  For the
upper inequality, if \(s\leq0\), then the upper endpoint gives
\(2q-\mu_H(s)\leq(6/5)\mu_H(s)\leq72/55\); if
\(s\geq0\), it gives
\(2q-\mu_H(s)\leq12/5-\mu_H(s)\leq72/55\).
Equations (12)--(14) show uniformly over the closed cube that support
truncation is inactive and every implied missing mean is strictly in
\((0,2)\).

To establish sufficiency of (11), choose arbitrarily and independently
for every \((x,t)\) a value \(q_t(x)\in I\{\mu_t(x)\}\), and set
\[
 r_t(x)=2q_t(x)-\mu_t(x).
 \tag{15}
\]
Draw \((X,S,T)\) according to its margin under
\texttt{def:observed-law}.  Given \(X=x,S=1\), draw independent
\(W_0,W_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), independently of
\(T\), and set \(Y(t)=1/2+W_t\).  Given
\(X=x,S=0,T=t\), draw
\[
 W_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\{\mu_t(x)-1/2\},
 \qquad
 Y(t)=\frac12+W_t,
 \qquad
 Y(1-t)=r_{1-t}(x),
 \tag{16}
\]
and set \(Y=Y(T)\).  The Bernoulli probability in (16) lies in
\((0,1)\) by (1)--(3).  The factual potential outcomes lie in
\(\{1/2,3/2\}\), while (12)--(15) place every missing potential
outcome strictly in \((0,2)\); hence
\texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes} holds.  The construction has observed
margin \(P_{m,h}\), satisfies consistency, and makes
\((Y(0),Y(1))\) independent of \(T\) given \(X,S=1\), as required
by \texttt{ass:experimental-randomization}.  Equation (6) holds by
construction.  Membership in (11) is equivalent, through (6) and
(15), to the pair of restrictions (7) and (10), so both sensitivity
caps hold.  This constructs a member of \(\mathcal M_{m,h}\), proves
that this class is nonempty, and proves that (11) is the exact feasible
set of observational source-marginal potential-outcome means.

It remains to optimize the causal contrast.  Source has conditional
probability \(1/2\) given \(X\) under
\texttt{def:observed-law}.  Therefore (5), (9), and the law of total
expectation give
\[
 M_t(x):=\mathbb E_Q[Y(t)\mid X=x]=\frac{1+q_t(x)}2.
 \tag{17}
\]
For the low arm, (12) and (17) imply
\[
 \underline M_{\ell}=\frac9{10},
 \qquad
 \overline M_{\ell}=\frac{89}{90}.
 \tag{18}
\]
For the high arm, (13) and (17) imply
\[
 \underline M_H(s)=\frac{109}{110}+\frac9{20}s,
 \qquad
 \overline M_H(s)=\frac{11}{10}
      +\frac{11}{40}(s-|s|).
 \tag{19}
\]
Because the construction (15)--(16) permits the choices for the two
treatments and all covariate cells to vary independently, these bounds
are variation-independent.  If \(v=0\), the high arm is treatment
zero, so the sharp conditional lower and upper contrasts are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \underline\Delta_0(s)
   &=\underline M_{\ell}-\overline M_H(s)
     =-\frac15-\frac{11}{40}s+\frac{11}{40}|s|,\\
 \overline\Delta_0(s)
   &=\overline M_{\ell}-\underline M_H(s)
     =-\frac1{495}-\frac9{20}s.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{20}
\]
If \(v=1\), the high arm is treatment one, so they are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \underline\Delta_1(s)
   &=\underline M_H(s)-\overline M_{\ell}
     =\frac1{495}+\frac9{20}s,\\
 \overline\Delta_1(s)
   &=\overline M_H(s)-\underline M_{\ell}
     =\frac15+\frac{11}{40}s-\frac{11}{40}|s|.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
The identity \(109/110-89/90=1/495\) verifies the constants in
(20)--(21).

Under \texttt{def:observed-law}, \(Z\) is symmetric and independent
of \((V,J)\).  With \(s=Zh_{v,j}\), therefore,
\[
 \mathbb E_Z[s]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_Z[|s|]=|h_{v,j}|.
 \tag{22}
\]
The law of \(J\) is uniform and the two values of \(V\) are
equiprobable.  Averaging (20)--(21) using (22), and using the
variation-independence just proved, yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \inf_{Q\in\mathcal M_{m,h}}\mathbb E_Q[Y(1)-Y(0)]
 &=\frac12\left\{-\frac15+
       \frac{11}{40m}\sum_{j=1}^m|h_{0,j}|+\frac1{495}\right\}\\
 &=-\frac{49}{495}+\frac{11}{80m}
       \sum_{j=1}^m|h_{0,j}|=L_m(h),
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{23}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{Q\in\mathcal M_{m,h}}\mathbb E_Q[Y(1)-Y(0)]
 &=\frac12\left\{-\frac1{495}+\frac15-
       \frac{11}{40m}\sum_{j=1}^m|h_{1,j}|\right\}\\
 &=\frac{49}{495}-\frac{11}{80m}
       \sum_{j=1}^m|h_{1,j}|=U_m(h).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{24}
\]
The final equalities in (23)--(24) are precisely
\texttt{def:profile-magnitudes} and \texttt{def:endpoints}.

Choosing in every cell the appropriate endpoint of (11) attains (23)
and (24) through (15)--(16), and (12)--(14) show that all missing
potential-outcome means in both endpoint-attaining completions are
strictly inside \((0,2)\).  Conversely, necessity of (11) proves that
no compatible completion can lie outside the two extrema.  Finally,
coordinatewise convex interpolation between the lower- and
upper-attaining selections remains in (11); equations (15)--(17) make
the resulting average causal contrast affine in the interpolation
parameter.  Every value between the extrema is therefore attained.
This proves that the sharp identified set is exactly
\([L_m(h),U_m(h)]\).

For comparison with the cited general data-fusion formulas, the present
experiment has observational and experimental treatment propensities
and the study propensity all fixed at \(1/2\), experimental conditional
means fixed at one, and \(\rho=\gamma=1/5\).  The compatibility
inequalities required for the max--min formulas are verified here by
\(4/5\leq(11/10)\mu_t(x)\) and
\((9/10)\mu_t(x)\leq6/5\), uniformly over both means in (1) and the
whole cube.  Hence the observational operations of
\cite[Lemma~1 and Theorem~1]{LannersRudinVolfovskyParikh2025}
specialize to \((9/10)\mu_t(x)\) and
\((11/10)\mu_t(x)\), while the experimental operations specialize to
\(4/5\) and \(6/5\).  Their pointwise maxima and minima reproduce
(11), and their treatment contrast and covariate average reproduce
(23)--(24).  Thus \(\mathcal M_{m,h}\) is a finite paired-cell
specialized subclass of that sensitivity model, and the direct
construction above independently verifies nonemptiness, sharpness, and
interior endpoint attainment on the entire subclass. \(\square\)
