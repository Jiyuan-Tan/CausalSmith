%%% FIELD proof-fixed-slope-endpoints
Fix \(c>0\), \(v\in\{\mathrm w,+\}\), and a law \(P\) satisfying \(\mathrm{ass{:}positive\text{-}observed\text{-}cells}\), and suppose that the fixed-slope fiber
\[
 I_v(P,c)=\{d:(\log c,d)\in\Theta_v(P)\}
\]
is nonempty.  Write \(z_{\mathrm w}=(m,a,b)\) and \(z_+=(m,a,b,t)\), and define
\[
 \mathcal G_v(P,c)=\{(z_v,d):z_v\in\mathcal F_c^v(P,d)\}.
\]
For fixed \(P\) and \(c\), every entry of \(H_s\), \(K_s\), and, when \(v=+\), \(R_s\), is affine in \(z_v\).  The sixteen cell restrictions are affine equalities in \(z_v\), and the APE restriction is the affine equality
\[
 d=\sum_{s\in S}L_{m_s}(N_c).
\]
The positive-semidefinite cone is convex: if \(M_0\succeq0\), \(M_1\succeq0\), and \(0\le\lambda\le1\), then for every vector \(q\),
\[
 q^{\mathsf T}\{\lambda M_0+(1-\lambda)M_1\}q
 =\lambda q^{\mathsf T}M_0q+(1-\lambda)q^{\mathsf T}M_1q\ge0.
\]
Consequently \(\mathcal G_v(P,c)\) is convex, so its image under the linear projection \((z_v,d)\mapsto d\) is an interval.  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}feedback\text{-}cone}\), this image is exactly \(I_v(P,c)\), rather than an outer approximation to that fiber.

The interval is bounded.  Put
\[
 \phi_c(u)=\frac{(c-1)u}{(1+u)(1+cu)},\qquad u>0.
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\left\{\frac{u}{(1+u)(1+cu)}\right\}
 =\frac{1-cu^2}{(1+u)^2(1+cu)^2}.
\]
The fraction in braces increases up to \(u=c^{-1/2}\), decreases thereafter, and tends to zero at both endpoints of the positive half-line.  Hence
\[
 \sup_{u>0}|\phi_c(u)|
 =\frac{|c-1|}{(1+\sqrt c)^2}
 =\frac{|\sqrt c-1|}{\sqrt c+1}.
\]
This formula also gives zero when \(c=1\).  Every \(d\in I_v(P,c)\) is, by the definition of \(\Theta_v(P)\), the integral of \(\phi_c\) with respect to a probability measure \(\mu\).  Therefore
\[
 |d|\le \frac{|\sqrt c-1|}{\sqrt c+1}.
\]
In particular, the infimum and supremum of the nonempty fiber are finite.

The two finite-dimensional optimization problems are explicitly
\[
 \underline V_v(P,c)=
 \inf_{d,z_v}\{d:z_v\in\mathcal F_c^v(P,d)\},
 \qquad
 \overline V_v(P,c)=
 \sup_{d,z_v}\{d:z_v\in\mathcal F_c^v(P,d)\}.
\]
For fixed \(c\), their objectives are linear, their equalities are affine, and their matrix inequalities are affine positive-semidefinite constraints, so they are linear semidefinite programs.  Exactness of the cone representation yields
\[
 \underline V_v(P,c)=\inf I_v(P,c)=L_c^v(P),
 \qquad
 \overline V_v(P,c)=\sup I_v(P,c)=U_c^v(P).
\]
These are sharp endpoints because \(I_v(P,c)\) is the independently model-defined legal fiber.

It remains to distinguish endpoint values from endpoint inclusion.  The lower endpoint belongs to the fiber exactly when
\[
 (\log c,L_c^v(P))\in\Theta_v(P).
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}feedback\text{-}cone}\) at that value shows that this condition is equivalent to
\[
 \mathcal F_c^v(P,L_c^v(P))\ne\varnothing.
\]
Likewise, upper inclusion is equivalent, separately, to
\[
 \mathcal F_c^v(P,U_c^v(P))\ne\varnothing.
\]
These are precisely the two tests in \(\mathrm{def{:}endpoint\text{-}attainment\text{-}test}\).  Closedness of the displayed matrix cones does not itself imply closedness of their linear images: for example,
\[
 \left\{(x,y):
 \begin{bmatrix}x&1\\1&y\end{bmatrix}\succeq0\right\}
\]
is closed, whereas its projection on the \(x\)-coordinate is \((0,\infty)\), since positive semidefiniteness is equivalent to \(x\ge0\), \(y\ge0\), and \(xy\ge1\).  Thus neither endpoint-inclusion test follows merely from the closed positive-semidefinite constraints, which proves every assertion.
%%% FIELD proof-algebraic-decision
For \(v\in\{\mathrm w,+\}\), let \(z_{\mathrm w}=(m,a,b)\) and \(z_+=(m,a,b,t)\).  Denote by
\[
 \Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)
\]
the conjunction of \(c>0\), the sixteen cell equalities, the APE equality with value \(d\), and every matrix inequality in the definition of \(\mathcal F_c^v(P,d)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}principal\text{-}minor\text{-}psd}\), each matrix inequality is equivalent to the nonnegativity of its finitely many principal minors.  The coefficients of \(A_{xz,yw}\) and \(N_c\) are polynomials with integer coefficients in \(c\), while the entries of \(H_s\), \(K_s\), and \(R_s\) are affine in the lift variables.  Thus \(\Phi_v\) is a first-order formula in the language of ordered fields whose only parameters are the entries of \(P\).  In particular, no logarithm occurs in this formula: \(c\) is the slope-odds coordinate and the corresponding structural slope is deterministically \(\log c\).

On the maintained positive-cell domain, \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}feedback\text{-}cone}\) gives the exact membership equivalence
\[
 (\log c,d)\in\Theta_v(P)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v). \tag{1}
\]
If \(P\), \(c\), and \(d\) are real algebraic, the sentence on the right of (1) has real algebraic coefficients.  The terminating procedure in \(\mathrm{lem{:}real\text{-}closed\text{-}field\text{-}elimination}\) converts it to a quantifier-free Boolean combination of polynomial sign conditions, whose signs at real algebraic arguments are decidable.  This decides membership exactly, including singular positive-semidefinite faces because principal-minor conditions use weak inequalities.

The same formula supplies both projections.  At fixed \(P\), the projection onto slope odds is represented exactly by
\[
 \left\{c:\exists d,z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\right\},
\]
and the projection onto the APE coordinate is represented exactly by
\[
 \left\{d:\exists c,z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\right\}.
\]
Here \(c>0\) is already a conjunct of \(\Phi_v\).  Eliminating the displayed quantified variables terminates and returns a finite Boolean combination of polynomial sign conditions for either projection.  It also detects the empty set, so weak infeasibility is not confused with a numerical failure.

For completeness, fix algebraic \(P\) and \(c>0\).  A real number \(\ell\) is the lower endpoint of a nonempty fiber if and only if the following ordered-field formula holds:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Inf}_v(\ell)\;:\!\Longleftrightarrow{}&
 \bigl[\exists d,z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\bigr]\\
 &{}\wedge
 \bigl[\forall d,z_v\,\{\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\Rightarrow \ell\le d\}\bigr]\\
 &{}\wedge
 \bigl[\forall\varepsilon\,\{\varepsilon>0\Rightarrow
 \exists d,z_v\,[\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\wedge d<\ell+\varepsilon]\}\bigr].
 \end{split} \tag{2}
\]
The first line asserts nonemptiness, the second that \(\ell\) is a lower bound, and the third that no larger number is a lower bound.  The corresponding upper-endpoint formula is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Sup}_v(r)\;:\!\Longleftrightarrow{}&
 \bigl[\exists d,z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\bigr]\\
 &{}\wedge
 \bigl[\forall d,z_v\,\{\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\Rightarrow d\le r\}\bigr]\\
 &{}\wedge
 \bigl[\forall\varepsilon\,\{\varepsilon>0\Rightarrow
 \exists d,z_v\,[\Phi_v(P,c,d,z_v)\wedge r-\varepsilon<d]\}\bigr].
 \end{split} \tag{3}
\]
Finiteness of both values for every nonempty fixed-\(c\) fiber follows from \(\mathrm{thm{:}fixed\text{-}slope\text{-}endpoints}\).  Quantifier elimination applied to (2) or (3) therefore gives an exact finite sign description of a singleton.  After identically zero polynomials are discarded, at least one polynomial in that description must vanish at its unique point; otherwise all signs would be locally constant there and the described set would contain an interval.  Its coefficients belong to the field of real algebraic numbers generated by the entries of \(P\) and \(c\).  Hence each finite endpoint is real algebraic, and the eliminating procedure supplies an exact algebraic description of it.

Finally, lower-endpoint inclusion and upper-endpoint inclusion are, respectively, the separate existential sentences
\[
 \exists z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,\ell,z_v),
 \qquad
 \exists z_v\,\Phi_v(P,c,r,z_v).
\]
Their equivalence to model-side inclusion follows from (1), or equivalently from \(\mathrm{def{:}endpoint\text{-}attainment\text{-}test}\) and \(\mathrm{thm{:}fixed\text{-}slope\text{-}endpoints}\).  Applying the same terminating elimination procedure decides both flags.  Thus membership, both coordinate projections, finite algebraic endpoint values, and their separate inclusion flags are all decided without a support or slope grid.
%%% FIELD proof-relative-interior-consistency
Fix \(c>0\) and \(v\in\{\mathrm w,+\}\), and abbreviate \(C=C_c^v\).  We first localize the value functions to positive observed laws, where \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}feedback\text{-}cone}\) applies.  There is a strictly positive law \(P^\star\in C\): take \(\mu\) to be unit mass at \(u=1\), set \(h_x(1)=1/2\) for both \(x\), and set \(g_{xy}(z,1)=1/2\) for every \(x,y,z\in B\).  These kernels belong even to \(\mathcal M_+(c)\), and the logit probabilities are strictly positive.  Consequently
\[
 P^\star(x,y,z,w)=\frac14p_x(y,1)p_z(w,1)>0
\]
for all sixteen cells.

Now suppose \(P\in\operatorname{ri}(C)\).  If some cell coordinate \(P_j\) were zero, then, because \(P-P^\star\) is parallel to \(\operatorname{aff}(C)\), the relative-interior property would put
\[
 P+\epsilon(P-P^\star)
\]
in \(C\) for every sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\).  Its \(j\)-th coordinate would equal \(-\epsilon P_j^\star<0\), contradicting \(C\subseteq\Delta^{15}\).  Thus every cell of \(P\) is positive.

Let
\[
 \mathcal O=\{Q\in\Delta^{15}:Q_j>0\text{ for all sixteen cells }j\},
 \qquad D=C\cap\mathcal O.
\]
The set \(D\) is convex.  Indeed, take \(Q_0,Q_1\in D\) and choose attainable effects \(d_0,d_1\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}feedback\text{-}cone}\), there are lifts \(z_j\in\mathcal F_c^v(Q_j,d_j)\).  For \(0\le\lambda\le1\), all equalities in the lift are affine and every positive-semidefinite block remains positive semidefinite under convex combination.  Hence
\[
 \lambda z_0+(1-\lambda)z_1
 \in\mathcal F_c^v\bigl(\lambda Q_0+(1-\lambda)Q_1,
 \lambda d_0+(1-\lambda)d_1\bigr).
\]
The mixed law has positive cells, so the reverse implication of the same exact-cone theorem places it in \(C\), and therefore in \(D\).

Moreover, \(P\in\operatorname{ri}(D)\) relative to \(\operatorname{aff}(C)\).  To see this, the assumption \(P\in\operatorname{ri}(C)\) gives \(\delta_0>0\) such that the relative ball of radius \(\delta_0\) about \(P\) is contained in \(C\).  Since all coordinates of \(P\) are positive, some \(\delta_1>0\) makes the corresponding relative ball lie in \(\mathcal O\).  The ball with radius \(\min\{\delta_0,\delta_1\}\) lies in \(D\), and its presence also gives \(\operatorname{aff}(D)=\operatorname{aff}(C)\).

The lower value function is convex on \(D\).  For \(Q_0,Q_1\in D\) and \(\epsilon>0\), choose \(d_j\) in the fiber over \(Q_j\) with
\[
 d_j<L_c^v(Q_j)+\epsilon.
\]
The preceding lift-mixture argument makes \(\lambda d_0+(1-\lambda)d_1\) attainable over \(\lambda Q_0+(1-\lambda)Q_1\).  Therefore
\[
 L_c^v\bigl(\lambda Q_0+(1-\lambda)Q_1\bigr)
 \le \lambda L_c^v(Q_0)+(1-\lambda)L_c^v(Q_1)+\epsilon.
\]
Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves convexity.  Choosing effects within \(\epsilon\) of the two suprema and reversing the inequality proves that \(U_c^v\) is concave on \(D\), so \(-U_c^v\) is convex there.  Both functions are finite on \(D\) by the scalar-effect bound in \(\mathrm{thm{:}fixed\text{-}slope\text{-}endpoints}\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}relative\text{-}interior\text{-}convex\text{-}continuity}\) to the proper convex extensions of \(L_c^v\) and \(-U_c^v\) that equal \(+\infty\) off \(D\).  Since \(P\in\operatorname{ri}(D)\) and \(\operatorname{aff}(D)=\operatorname{aff}(C)\), both \(L_c^v\) and \(U_c^v\) are continuous at \(P\) relative to \(\operatorname{aff}(C)\).

It remains to establish convergence of their random arguments from \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}units}\).  Fix one cell, let \(Z_i\) be its indicator, and write \(p=\mathbb E[Z_i]\).  With \(W_i=Z_i-p\), independence and centering make every term in the expansion of \(\mathbb E(\sum_{i=1}^nW_i)^4\) vanish unless each index occurs at least twice.  Thus
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right)^4\right]
 =\frac{n\mathbb E[W_1^4]+3n(n-1)\{\mathbb E[W_1^2]\}^2}{n^4}
 \le\frac{3}{n^2},
\]
where \(|W_i|\le1\) was used.  The pointwise inequality
\[
 \mathbf 1\left\{\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right|>\epsilon\right\}
 \le \epsilon^{-4}\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right|^4
\]
therefore gives
\[
 \Pr\left(\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nW_i\right|>\epsilon\right)
 \le\frac{3}{\epsilon^4n^2}.
\]
For every \(N\), countable subadditivity bounds the probability of at least one such deviation after time \(N\) by \(3\epsilon^{-4}\sum_{n\ge N}n^{-2}\), which tends to zero.  Hence deviations larger than \(\epsilon\) occur only finitely often almost surely.  Taking the intersection of these probability-one events over \(\epsilon=1/k\), \(k\in\mathbb N\), and over the sixteen cells proves
\[
 \widehat P_n\longrightarrow P\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\]

Set \(A=\operatorname{aff}(C)\).  This is a closed affine subspace of the finite-dimensional Euclidean cell space, and \(P\in A\).  Orthogonal projection onto \(A\) is nonexpansive: the orthogonal decomposition of \(\widehat P_n-P\) into its component parallel to \(A-P\) and its perpendicular residual gives
\[
 \|\widetilde P_n-P\|_2\le\|\widehat P_n-P\|_2.
\]
It follows that \(\widetilde P_n\to P\) almost surely.  By the relative ball constructed above, \(\widetilde P_n\in D\subseteq C\) eventually almost surely, proving eventual attainability.  Finally, relative continuity of both endpoint functions gives
\[
 L_c^v(\widetilde P_n)\longrightarrow L_c^v(P),
 \qquad
 U_c^v(\widetilde P_n)\longrightarrow U_c^v(P)
\]
almost surely.  The argument keeps \(c\) fixed and uses the relative-interior ball essentially; it asserts neither boundary continuity nor joint consistency through varying \(c\).
