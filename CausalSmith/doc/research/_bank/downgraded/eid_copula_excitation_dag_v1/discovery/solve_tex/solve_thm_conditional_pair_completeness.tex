%%% FIELD proof-canonical-order-reduction
Fix an order \(\pi\).  For each \(j\), abbreviate \(S=S_j^\pi\), put
\[
 \beta_j=C[j,S]C[S,S]^{-1},\qquad
 \xi_j=Z_j-\beta_j Z_S,
\]
where \(Z\) is any centered random vector with covariance \(C\).  Every principal submatrix of the positive-definite matrix \(C\) is invertible, so these quantities are defined.  The normal equations give
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\xi_j,Z_S)
 =C[j,S]-C[j,S]C[S,S]^{-1}C[S,S]=0.
\]
If \(i\) precedes \(j\), then \(\xi_i\) is a linear combination of variables that also precede \(j\); consequently \(\operatorname{Cov}(\xi_j,\xi_i)=0\).  Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_j)
 =1-C[j,S]C[S,S]^{-1}C[S,j]=v_j^\pi(C)>0,
\]
where strict positivity is the Schur-complement consequence of \(C\succ0\).  In matrix form, with \(L=I_p-B^\pi(C)\), the preceding equations are
\[
 LCL^{\mathsf T}=\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C)).
\]
The order restriction makes \(L\) unit triangular, hence invertible, and rearrangement proves
\[
 C=(I_p-B^\pi(C))^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C))(I_p-B^\pi(C))^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Conversely, in any representation of this form whose coefficient matrix respects \(\pi\), the structural residual for \(j\) is uncorrelated with every predecessor.  Its coefficient row must therefore solve the same nonsingular normal equations, and its variance is the displayed Schur complement.  This proves uniqueness without an additional rank assumption.

Now take a member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,E}(d)\), write \(h_j=(H_0)_{jj}>0\), and standardize once, in the reference units, by \(Z_e=H_0Y_e\).  Then
\[
 Z_e=(H_0BH_0^{-1})Z_e+H_0\varepsilon_e,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(H_0\varepsilon_e)
 =\operatorname{diag}(h_1^2v_1,\ldots,h_p^2v_p)D_e.
\]
The matrices \(H_0\), \(\operatorname{diag}(v)\), and \(D_e\) are diagonal, so the last identity follows directly from the innovation covariance assumption.  In environment zero, the covariance of \(Z_0\) is \(R_0\), because \(d_{0j}=1\) and by the definition of \(H_0\).  If \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Top}(G)\), then \(H_0BH_0^{-1}\) respects \(\pi_0\).  Applying the just-proved uniqueness to its reference representation yields
\[
 B^{\pi_0}(R_0)=H_0BH_0^{-1}=B^0,
 \qquad
 v^{\pi_0}(R_0)=(h_1^2v_1,\ldots,h_p^2v_p)
 =\operatorname{diag}(H_0)^2\odot v.
\]
The right sides do not depend on the selected true topological order, proving the asserted order independence.  Substitution into the environment covariance gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_e)
 =(I_p-B^0)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(v^{\pi_0}(R_0))D_e
 (I_p-B^0)^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Positive diagonal congruence does not change correlation, so
\[
 R_e=\operatorname{corr}(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_e))
 =\operatorname{corr}(\operatorname{Cov}(Z_e))
 =\Phi_e^{\pi_0}(R_0;d).
\]

Finally let \(P\) be any positive diagonal common change of raw variable units.  Then \(\Sigma'_e=P\Sigma_eP\), \(B'=PBP^{-1}\), and
\[
 H'_0=\operatorname{diag}(P\Sigma_0P)^{-1/2}=H_0P^{-1}.
\]
It follows equation by equation that
\[
 H'_0B'(H'_0)^{-1}=H_0BH_0^{-1}=B^0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{corr}(\Sigma'_e)=\operatorname{corr}(\Sigma_e)=R_e.
\]
The normalized schedule \(d\) is unchanged by a change of variable units.  Since every \(Q_\pi\) is defined only from \(R\), \(d\), and the canonical predictor, all order scores are unchanged as well.

%%% FIELD proof-tied-profile-twins
Work first on the coordinates \((i,j,k)\).  In the fork, the structural equations are
\[
 Y_i=\varepsilon_i,\qquad Y_j=\beta Y_i+\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad Y_k=\gamma Y_i+\varepsilon_k.
\]
Using independence of the innovations and the tie \(d_{ei}=d_{ej}\), its covariance in environment \(e\) is
\[
 \Sigma_e^{\mathrm F}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 ud_{ei}&\beta ud_{ei}&\gamma ud_{ei}\\
 \beta ud_{ei}&(\beta^2u+w)d_{ei}&\beta\gamma ud_{ei}\\
 \gamma ud_{ei}&\beta\gamma ud_{ei}&\gamma^2ud_{ei}+zd_{ek}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Set \(q=\beta^2u+w>0\).  The proposed chain has equations
\[
 Y'_j=\varepsilon'_j,\qquad
 Y'_i=\frac{\beta u}{q}Y'_j+\varepsilon'_i,
 \qquad Y'_k=\gamma Y'_i+\varepsilon'_k,
\]
and reference innovation variances
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon'_i)=\frac{uw}{q},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon'_j)=q,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon'_k)=z.
\]
All three are positive.  Since the first two profiles are tied,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y'_j)=qd_{ei},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_e(Y'_i,Y'_j)=\frac{\beta u}{q}qd_{ei}=\beta ud_{ei},
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y'_i)
 =\frac{\beta^2u^2}{q}d_{ei}+\frac{uw}{q}d_{ei}
 =ud_{ei}.
\]
The equation for \(Y'_k\) then gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_e(Y'_k,Y'_i)=\gamma ud_{ei},\quad
 \operatorname{Cov}_e(Y'_k,Y'_j)=\beta\gamma ud_{ei},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y'_k)=\gamma^2ud_{ei}+zd_{ek}.
\]
Thus \(\Sigma_e^{\mathrm C}=\Sigma_e^{\mathrm F}\) for every environment, and hence their released correlations agree.  In a topological order of either model, sequential regression recovers its displayed structural coefficients and positive innovation variances by the normal equations; therefore the corresponding canonical order is retained in \(\mathcal F(R,d)\).  Both exact supports are consequently in the same fiber.

The parameters range over the relatively open set
\(\{(\beta,\gamma,u,w,z):\beta\gamma\ne0,\ u,w,z>0\}\), and neither displayed edge vanishes.  The fork has a vertex of out-degree two, whereas the chain has maximum out-degree one, so the three-vertex directed graphs are nonisomorphic.  At \(\beta=\gamma=u=w=z=1\), the preceding covariance reduces to
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 d_{ei}&d_{ei}&d_{ei}\\
 d_{ei}&2d_{ei}&d_{ei}\\
 d_{ei}&d_{ei}&d_{ei}+d_{ek}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(p=2\), deletion of \(k\) leaves the two positive reverse one-edge representations just computed.  For \(p>3\), adjoining vertices with independent positive-variance innovations in both models appends identical diagonal blocks, preserves the two exact supports on \(\{i,j,k\}\), and proves the asserted embedding.

%%% FIELD proof-universal-generic-frontier
First suppose \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\).  Fix a labeled DAG \(G\), and let \(\pi\notin\operatorname{Top}(G)\).  Then some edge \(i\to j\) of \(G\) is reversed by \(\pi\).  By projective separation there is an environment \(e\) with \(d_{ei}\ne d_{ej}\).

It is useful to remove square roots before constructing the separating equation.  Apply the sequential-regression formulas to the unstandardized reference covariance \(\Sigma_0\), and denote the resulting environment prediction by \(\widetilde\Omega_e^\pi\).  If \(K\) is positive diagonal, direct substitution in the regression formula gives
\[
 B^\pi(KCK) = KB^\pi(C)K^{-1},\qquad
 v_j^\pi(KCK)=K_{jj}^2v_j^\pi(C),
\]
where the same formulas are understood on an arbitrary positive-definite covariance.  Hence the predicted covariance transforms as \(K\widetilde\Omega_e^\pi K\), and its correlation is invariant.  Taking \(K=H_0\) shows that matching the frozen predictor based on \(R_0\) implies equality of the squared correlations of \(\widetilde\Omega_e^\pi\) and \(\Sigma_e\).  In particular it implies
\[
 P_{\pi,e,ij}:=
 (\widetilde\Omega_{e,ij}^\pi)^2\Sigma_{e,ii}\Sigma_{e,jj}
 -(\Sigma_{e,ij})^2\widetilde\Omega_{e,ii}^\pi
 \widetilde\Omega_{e,jj}^\pi=0. \tag{1}
\]

On the affine coefficient space obtained by forcing coefficients off \(G\) to zero, acyclicity gives
\[
 (I_p-B)^{-1}=I_p+B+\cdots+B^{p-1}.
\]
Thus every entry of \(\Sigma_e\) is polynomial in the free coefficients and innovation variances.  Every sequential-regression coefficient is rational in those entries, with denominators that are principal minors of \(\Sigma_0\); those minors are strictly positive on the legal parameter set.  Consequently, after multiplication by a product of these denominators, (1) becomes a polynomial equation.

This polynomial is not identically zero.  Indeed, specialize in the closure of the exact-support stratum by retaining only the coefficient \(B_{ji}=a\ne0\), and retain arbitrary positive \(v_i,v_j\); all other variables are independent.  Put \(c=a^2v_i/v_j>0\).  In the true direction the squared-correlation odds of the pair are
\[
 \frac{\rho_{e,ij}^2}{1-\rho_{e,ij}^2}
 =c\frac{d_{ei}}{d_{ej}}. \tag{2}
\]
The order \(\pi\) places \(j\) before \(i\), so its reference Cholesky representation reverses this two-variable regression.  Its environment prediction has odds
\[
 \frac{(\rho_{e,ij}^{\pi})^2}{1-(\rho_{e,ij}^{\pi})^2}
 =c\frac{d_{ej}}{d_{ei}}. \tag{3}
\]
Because the multipliers are positive, equality of (2) and (3) would imply \(d_{ei}=d_{ej}\), contrary to the selected environment.  Hence (1), and therefore its denominator-cleared polynomial, is nonzero at this specialization.  Evaluation outside \(\Theta_G\) is legitimate here because it establishes that the polynomial on the ambient affine support space is not the zero polynomial.

For completeness, a nonzero real polynomial has a zero set with empty Euclidean interior: induction on the number of variables reduces along a coordinate to the fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many roots, choosing a slice on which at least one coefficient polynomial is nonzero.  The same induction, with Fubini's theorem, gives Lebesgue measure zero.  There are only finitely many nontopological orders.  The union of their matching loci is therefore contained in a proper algebraic subset of the ambient support space, and its intersection with \(\Theta_G\) is a proper algebraic exceptional subset.

Outside that subset, no nontopological order matches.  Every \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Top}(G)\) does match by the canonical-order reduction, and the same reduction gives
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(B^{\pi_0}(R_0))
 =\operatorname{supp}(H_0BH_0^{-1})=G,
\]
because positive diagonal conjugation preserves the zero pattern.  It follows directly from the definition of the fiber that \(\mathcal F(R,d)=\{G\}\).  This proves universal generic identification under \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\), including the stronger assertion that the generically matching orders are exactly \(\operatorname{Top}(G)\).

Conversely, if \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\) fails, there are \(i\ne j\) whose profiles agree in every environment.  For \(p=2\), the reverse one-edge family in the tied-profile proposition gives two distinct exact supports throughout the relatively open set of nonzero edge coefficients and positive variances.  For \(p\ge3\), use the fork on \(i\to j\) and \(i\to k\), with all other vertices isolated.  The proposition supplies a chain support with the identical covariance family for every \(\beta\gamma\ne0\) and \(u,w,z>0\), while the independent added variances may range freely over \((0,\infty)^{p-3}\).  This is a relatively open positive-parameter family in the exact fork-support stratum and its fiber is nonsingleton.  Therefore the universal generic property fails.  Both implications are proved.

The prescribed three-variable check is also exact.  With all three order-consistent coefficients and all reference innovation variances equal to one, one obtains
\[
 \Sigma_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2\\1&2&3\\2&3&6\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2\\1&5&6\\2&6&17\end{pmatrix}
\]
under \(d_1=(1,4,9)\); their determinants are respectively \(1\) and \(36\).  The actual environment-one odds for the unordered pairs \(12,13,23\) are \(1/4,4/13,36/49\), while the first-pair predictions for the six orders \(123,132,213,231,312,321\) are respectively
\[
 \frac14,\quad\frac29,\quad4,\quad\frac43,\quad18,\quad\frac{27}{4}.
\]
Only the first order's prediction equals the actual odds for its first pair; thus all five wrong orders are excluded without a marginal-scale choice outside the canonical list.

%%% FIELD proof-conditional-pair-completeness
Fix a true ancestral prefix \(S\) and put \(U=V\setminus S\).  In environment \(e\), the structural equations on \(U\), conditional on \(Y_{e,S}\), are
\[
 Y_{e,U}=B_{UU}Y_{e,U}+B_{US}Y_{e,S}+\varepsilon_{e,U}.
\]
Because \(S\) is ancestral, \(Y_{e,S}\) is a function only of \(\varepsilon_{e,S}\).  The diagonal innovation assumption makes \(\varepsilon_{e,U}\) independent of \(Y_{e,S}\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y_{e,U}\mid Y_{e,S})
 =(I-B_{UU})^{-1}\operatorname{diag}((v_jd_{ej})_{j\in U})
 (I-B_{UU})^{-\mathsf T}. \tag{4}
\]
If \(R_e=K_e\Sigma_eK_e\) for a positive diagonal \(K_e\), the Schur complement obeys
\[
 R_e/R_e[S,S]
 =K_{e,U}\bigl(\Sigma_e/\Sigma_e[S,S]\bigr)K_{e,U}.
\]
Consequently its normalization \(R_e^{(S)}\) is exactly the correlation matrix of (4).  Thus conditioning an ancestral prefix leaves an acyclic linear model on \(U\) with the same innovation multipliers.

Relabel the remaining true complete order as \(1,\ldots,q\).  The first two equations are
\(Y_{e,1}=\varepsilon_{e,1}\) and
\(Y_{e,2}=aY_{e,1}+\varepsilon_{e,2}\), where completeness gives \(a\ne0\).  Direct covariance algebra yields
\[
 \frac{(R_{e,12}^{(S)})^2}{1-(R_{e,12}^{(S)})^2}
 =\frac{a^2v_1}{v_2}\frac{d_{e1}}{d_{e2}}, \tag{5}
\]
and the sign is the fixed sign of \(a\).  Since \(d_{01}=d_{02}=1\), (5) is precisely the conditional-odds identity tested for the ordered pair \((1,2)\), in every environment.

We next show that each other ordered pair fails generically.  The conditional correlations are rational functions of the coefficients and positive variances after squared correlations are denominator-cleared, exactly as in the frontier proof.  It is therefore enough to give, for every wrong pair, one specialization in the closure of the complete-support stratum at which a tested equality fails.

If the proposed pair is \((a,b)\) with \(a>b\), retain only the true edge \(b\to a\).  Projective separation supplies an environment where the two profiles differ, and equations (2)--(3) from the reversed-edge calculation show that the proposed reverse identity fails.

If \(1<a<b\), choose \(k<a\) and retain only \(k\to a\to b\), with coefficients \(c\ne0\) and \(g\ne0\), respectively.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y_a)=v_ad_{ea}+c^2v_kd_{ek},
\]
and the squared-correlation odds for \((a,b)\) are
\[
 \frac{(R_{e,ab}^{(S)})^2}{1-(R_{e,ab}^{(S)})^2}
 =\frac{g^2(v_ad_{ea}+c^2v_kd_{ek})}{v_bd_{eb}}. \tag{6}
\]
Equality of (6) with its reference value multiplied by \(d_{ea}/d_{eb}\) would require
\[
 v_ad_{ea}+c^2v_kd_{ek}=(v_a+c^2v_k)d_{ea},
\]
or \(d_{ek}=d_{ea}\).  Pairwise separation provides an environment violating that equality.

The remaining wrong case is \((1,b)\) with \(b>2\).  Choose \(1<k<b\) and retain only \(1\to b\) and \(k\to b\), with nonzero coefficients \(g,c\).  Independence of \(Y_1\) and \(Y_k\) at this specialization gives
\[
 \frac{(R_{e,1b}^{(S)})^2}{1-(R_{e,1b}^{(S)})^2}
 =\frac{g^2v_1d_{e1}}{v_bd_{eb}+c^2v_kd_{ek}}. \tag{7}
\]
For (7) to equal its reference value multiplied by \(d_{e1}/d_{eb}\), one would need \(d_{ek}=d_{eb}\) in every environment, again contradicting projective separation.  These three cases exhaust all ordered pairs except \((1,2)\).

Each failure just exhibited makes a denominator-cleared tested polynomial nonzero.  The elementary polynomial zero-set argument from the frontier theorem, applied to the finitely many pairs and the finitely many true prefixes, shows that outside one proper real algebraic subset, \((1,2)\) is the unique passing ordered pair at every stage.  The algorithm therefore appends the next true vertex, and induction on \(|S|\) constructs \(\pi_0\).  The final canonical covariance predictions equal the released correlations by the canonical-order reduction.  They are positive definite, so their diagonals are positive, and equality of correlations implies both the squared-cross-product identities and the sign inequalities in \(\mathsf{FiberCert}\).  Hence the terminal certificate passes and the returned support is
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(B^{\pi_0}(R_0))=G.
\]
The universal generic frontier also ensures that this support is the singleton fiber on the same complement after enlarging the exceptional set if necessary.

At a stage with \(q=p-s\) remaining vertices, one rank-one Schur update and renormalization of each of the \(E\) matrices uses \(O(Eq^2)\) exact-real arithmetic operations.  There are \(q(q-1)\) ordered pairs, and testing each in all environments also uses \(O(Eq^2)\) operations.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_{q=2}^{p}O(Eq^2)=O(Ep^3).
\]
Sequential factorization and the final environment predictions and certificates require at most another \(O(Ep^3)\) operations.  This establishes the stated exact-real arithmetic, rather than bit-complexity, bound.

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-predictor-perturbation
Fix an order and one row, write \(S=S_j^\pi\), \(x=C[j,S]\), \(K=C[S,S]\), and use primes for \(C'\).  Principal-submatrix interlacing gives
\(\lambda_{\min}(K),\lambda_{\min}(K')\ge\eta\).  Since a correlation entry has absolute value at most one,
\[
 \|x-x'\|_2\le\sqrt p\,\epsilon,
 \quad \|x\|_2\vee\|x'\|_2\le\sqrt p,
 \quad \|K-K'\|_2\le p\epsilon.
\]
The inverse identity and the eigenvalue bounds imply
\[
 \|K^{-1}-(K')^{-1}\|_2
 \le\|K^{-1}\|_2\|K-K'\|_2\|(K')^{-1}\|_2
 \le p\eta^{-2}\epsilon. \tag{8}
\]
Because \(C\) and \(C'\) have trace \(p\), necessarily \(0<\eta\le1\).  From the regression formula and (8),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|xK^{-1}-x'(K')^{-1}\|_2
 &\le \sqrt p\,\eta^{-1}\epsilon
       +p^{3/2}\eta^{-2}\epsilon\\
 &\le2p^{3/2}\eta^{-2}\epsilon.
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The empty-predecessor row is identically zero.  Thus (9), for all rows, gives both
\[
 \|B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C')\|_{\max}
 \le2p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon \tag{10}
\]
and the operator-norm bound
\[
 \|B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C')\|_2
 \le\|B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C')\|_{\mathrm F}
 \le2p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon. \tag{11}
\]

For the Schur residuals, expand the difference of the two quadratic forms and use (8):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |v_j^\pi(C)-v_j^\pi(C')|
 &\le 2p\eta^{-1}\epsilon+p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon\\
 &\le3p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{12}
\]
Also, the variational formula for a Schur complement gives
\[
 v_j^\pi(C)=\min_t\operatorname{Var}(Z_j-t^{\mathsf T}Z_S),
\]
so \(\eta\le v_j^\pi(C)\le1\), and the same holds for \(C'\).  Put
\[
 A=(I_p-B^\pi(C))^{-1},\quad A'=(I_p-B^\pi(C'))^{-1},
 \quad V=\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C)),\quad V'=\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C')).
\]
Since \(C=AVA^{\mathsf T}\), \(V\succeq\eta I_p\), and \(\|C\|_2\le p\),
\[
 \|A\|_2\vee\|A'\|_2\le\sqrt{p/\eta}. \tag{13}
\]
The resolvent identity, (11), and (13) now yield
\[
 \|A-A'\|_2
 =\|A(B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C'))A'\|_2
 \le2p^3\eta^{-3}\epsilon. \tag{14}
\]

For a fixed environment, let \(\Omega=AVD_eA^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(\Omega'=A'V'D_e(A')^{\mathsf T}\).  The schedule condition and (12) give
\[
 \|VD_e\|_2\vee\|V'D_e\|_2\le M,
 \qquad
 \|(V-V')D_e\|_2\le3Mp^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon.
\]
Expanding \(\Omega-\Omega'\) into the two factor differences and the middle diagonal difference, then applying (13)--(14), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Omega-\Omega'\|_2
 &\le4Mp^{7/2}\eta^{-7/2}\epsilon
      +3Mp^3\eta^{-3}\epsilon\\
 &\le7Mp^4\eta^{-4}\epsilon. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
The last inequality uses \(p\ge2\) and \(\eta\le1\).

Because \(mI_p\preceq D_e\preceq MI_p\) and the diagonal matrices commute,
\[
 mC\preceq\Omega\preceq MC,
 \qquad mC'\preceq\Omega'\preceq MC'.
\]
In particular every diagonal entry of \(\Omega\) and \(\Omega'\) is at least \(m\).  Along the positive-definite line segment \(\Omega_t=(1-t)\Omega+t\Omega'\), differentiation of
\(\Omega_{t,ab}/\sqrt{\Omega_{t,aa}\Omega_{t,bb}}\), together with
\(|\Omega_{t,ab}|\le\sqrt{\Omega_{t,aa}\Omega_{t,bb}}\), gives
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{corr}(\Omega_t)_{ab}\right|
 \le \frac{2}{m}\|\Omega'-\Omega\|_{\max}.
\]
Integration over \(t\in[0,1]\), followed by (15) and
\(\|\cdot\|_{\max}\le\|\cdot\|_2\), proves
\[
 \|\Phi_e^\pi(C;d)-\Phi_e^\pi(C';d)\|_{\max}
 \le14(M/m)p^4\eta^{-4}\epsilon.
\]
Every bound was independent of the order and environment, so the two asserted simultaneous inequalities follow.

%%% FIELD proof-rank-summary-confidence
Let \(N=Ep(p-1)/2\).  For a fixed environment and coordinate pair, continuity of the margins makes the Kendall kernel
\[
 h((x_i,x_j),(x'_i,x'_j))
 =\operatorname{sign}((x_i-x'_i)(x_j-x'_j))
\]
take values in \(\{-1,1\}\) almost surely.  Kendall's statistic is the order-two U-statistic with this kernel.  If the latent Gaussian pair has correlation \(\rho\), invariance under strictly increasing marginal transforms reduces its expectation to the Gaussian calculation.  For a standard bivariate normal pair \((U,V)\) of correlation \(\rho\),
\[
 q(\rho):=\Pr(U>0,V>0)
 =\int_0^\infty\phi(u)\Phi\!\left(\frac{\rho u}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\right)du.
\]
Differentiation under this integrable Gaussian density gives
\(q'(\rho)=1/(2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2})\), while \(q(0)=1/4\).  Hence
\[
 \tau=4q(\rho)-1=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(\rho),
 \qquad \rho=\sin(\pi\tau/2). \tag{16}
\]

Put \(q_e=\lfloor n_e/2\rfloor\).  For a uniformly random permutation of the observations, average the kernel over the \(q_e\) disjoint adjacent pairs.  Conditional averaging over the permutation gives exactly the complete U-statistic.  The paired kernels are independent and lie in an interval of length two.  The elementary exponential convexity bound for a centered variable in such an interval gives
\(\mathbb E e^{s(W-\mathbb EW)}\le e^{s^2/2}\).  Jensen's inequality for the conditional permutation average and Chernoff optimization therefore give
\[
 \Pr(|\widehat\tau_{e,ij}-\tau_{e,ij}|>t)
 \le2\exp(-q_et^2/2). \tag{17}
\]
Since the sine map in (16) is \(\pi/2\)-Lipschitz, a union bound over the \(N\) environment-pair combinations, with
\[
 t=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2N/\alpha)}{n_\star}},
\]
shows
\[
 \Pr\!\left(\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \|\widehat R_e-R_e\|_{\max}\le
 \frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log(Ep(p-1)/\alpha)}{n_\star}}
 \right)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{18}
\]
The radius in (18) is exactly \(r\).

Call the simultaneous event in (18) \(\mathcal A\).  Its probability bound is distribution-free within the stated continuous Gaussian-copula sampling class.  On \(\mathcal A\),
\[
 \|\widehat R_0-R_0\|_2\le p r,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat R_0)
 \ge\lambda_{\min}(R_0)-pr\ge\kappa-pr, \tag{19}
\]
where the eigenvalue inequality follows by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient.  If either deterministic fallback inequality \(r\ge\kappa/(2p)\) or \(b\le2K_Br\) holds, the reported set is \(\mathfrak G_V\) and contains \(G\).  Otherwise (19) gives
\(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat R_0)>\kappa/2\), so the third fallback cannot occur on \(\mathcal A\).

Apply the uniform predictor perturbation lemma with
\(C=R_0\), \(C'=\widehat R_0\), \(\eta=\kappa/2\), and \(\epsilon=r\).  Its two constants become exactly
\[
 2p^2(\kappa/2)^{-2}=K_B,
 \qquad
 14(M/m)p^4(\kappa/2)^{-4}=L.
\]
Thus, simultaneously in \(\pi\),
\[
 \|B^\pi(\widehat R_0)-B^\pi(R_0)\|_{\max}\le K_Br, \tag{20}
\]
and the triangle inequality in every environment gives
\[
 |Q_\pi(\widehat R,d)-Q_\pi(R,d)|
 \le Lr+r=T. \tag{21}
\]

For a true topological order \(\pi_0\), the canonical-order reduction gives
\(Q_{\pi_0}(R,d)=0\) and \(B^{\pi_0}(R_0)=B^0\).  Equation (21) retains \(\pi_0\).  If \(B^0_{ji}=0\), (20) makes the estimated entry at most \(K_Br\), so the strict threshold deletes it.  If \(B^0_{ji}\ne0\), then
\[
 |B^{\pi_0}_{ji}(\widehat R_0)|
 \ge b-K_Br>K_Br
\]
because the nonfallback branch has \(b>2K_Br\).  Hence the thresholded support from \(\pi_0\) is exactly \(G\).  This proves \(G\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_\alpha\) on \(\mathcal A\), including all fallback cases, and (18) proves coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).

Assume now the three displayed strict small-error and separation inequalities in the theorem.  On \(\mathcal A\), (19) rules out the eigenvalue fallback, so the nonfallback construction is used.  If an order \(\pi\) is retained, (21) implies
\[

Fix the natural order of the vertex labels.  Run \(\mathsf{FiberCert}(R,d)\) over all permutations.  Whenever an order \(\pi\) is retained, form
\(H=\operatorname{supp}(B^\pi(R_0))\), compute the lexicographically least topological order \(\ell(H)\) by repeatedly taking the least labeled current source, and output \(H\) if and only if \(\pi=\ell(H)\).

First, the finite certificate used by \(\mathsf{FiberCert}\) is exact.  Its canonical covariance \(\Omega_e^\pi\) is positive definite because every Schur residual variance and every schedule entry is positive.  For positive diagonal entries, the squared-cross-product equation says that the predicted and observed correlations have the same absolute value.  The accompanying sign inequality selects the same sign; when the observed correlation is zero, the squared equation forces the predicted covariance entry to be zero.  Thus an order is retained exactly when \(\Phi_e^\pi(R_0;d)=R_e\) for every environment.  The supports encountered by retained orders are consequently exactly \(\mathcal F(R,d)\).

Every edge of \(H=\operatorname{supp}(B^\pi(R_0))\) points from a predecessor to a successor in \(\pi\), so \(\pi\) is topological for \(H\).  Conversely, let a retained order produce \(H\).  Its canonical coefficients and innovations define a compatible acyclic model with support \(H\).  Applying the canonical-order reduction to any \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Top}(H)\) shows that
\[
 B^\sigma(R_0)=B^\pi(R_0)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \Phi_e^\sigma(R_0;d)=R_e
\]
for every environment.  Hence \(\ell(H)\) is retained.  Exactly that occurrence passes the canonical filter, proving exact-once output.

A sequential modified-Cholesky factorization costs \(O(p^3)\); forming and checking all environment predictions costs \(O(Ep^3)\); and the greedy topological-order check costs \(O(p^2)\).  Traversing all \(p!\) permutations therefore gives the asserted \(O(p!\,Ep^3)\) arithmetic upper bound.

For the duplication witness, take \(B=0\) and arbitrary positive reference innovation variances.  Every environment covariance is diagonal, so \(R_e=I_p\).  For every order,
\[
 B^\pi(I_p)=0,\qquad v^\pi(I_p)=\mathbf 1,
 \qquad \Phi_e^\pi(I_p;d)=\operatorname{corr}(D_e)=I_p.
\]
Thus all \(p!\) orders are retained, but each yields the same empty support and
\(\mathcal F(R,d)=\{\varnothing\}\), so \(N_{\mathrm{out}}=1\).  The canonical filter removes the duplicate outputs but not the factorial traversal.  No bound on the number of distinct projective fragments represented at intermediate subsets follows from the frozen quotient handle; accordingly, the stronger output-sensitive and polynomial-delay alternatives are not asserted.
