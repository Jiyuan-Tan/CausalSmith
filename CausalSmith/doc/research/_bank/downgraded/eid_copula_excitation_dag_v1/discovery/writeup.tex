\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Known reference-normalized innovation multipliers can identify an invariant linear-Gaussian DAG even when every site releases only a Gaussian copula. The true structural coefficients are represented by the unit-free canonical matrix \(B^0=B^{\pi_0}(R_0)=H_0BH_0^{-1}\). Pairwise projective separation is the sharp universal generic frontier: wrong orders fail polynomial prediction identities, whereas tied profiles admit explicit reverse-edge and fork--chain twins. Exact all-order recovery uses signed squared-cross-product checks inside its construction, while the substantive conditional-pair theorem proves complete-DAG generic completeness and \(O(Ep^3)\) complexity. Uniform predictor bounds turn Kendall summaries into honest graph confidence sets under an explicit bounded normalized-excitation design regularity; the beta-min threshold, separation margin, and fallback are all expressed in reference-standardized units. Exact-once sparse-fiber enumeration remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Montagna2025InvariancePrinciple, Section 3, Theorem 1, is the closest multiple-environment identifiability comparator, but it uses full environment densities through score and Hessian information and needs two auxiliary environments. BACKSHIFT uses covariance differences on a common numerical scale. GnIES arXiv:2211.14897v4, Theorem 6, uses full Gaussian environment distributions; the separate Journal of Machine Learning Research article by Taeb, Gamella, Heinze-Deml, and Bühlmann treats latent variables and unknown additive interventions. Invariant prediction tests stability of a response conditional law across environments, and conditional-variance identification uses scale-bearing observational quantities. None characterizes the full DAG fiber or honest support uncertainty from independently standardized correlations and a known normalized excitation schedule, including the reference-plus-one-environment frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} Acyclicity lets the reference correlation select one canonical standardized recursive model per order. The remaining correlations become predictions indexed only by the normalized schedule, so both identification and inference live on a diagonal-gauge quotient.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the universal generic if-and-only-if frontier and tied-profile necessity family, uses the reference-standardized coordinate \(B^0=H_0BH_0^{-1}\), gives a non-tautological conditional-pair completeness and cubic-complexity theorem for generic complete DAGs, and closes rank-summary inference under explicit gauge-invariant margins and bounded calibrated design profiles. Signed polynomial checks remain part of the exhaustive algorithm specification, not a separate contribution.

\section{Related work}

Montagna2025InvariancePrinciple, Section 3, Theorem 1, is the closest multiple-environment pairwise-noise-ratio identifiability comparator. It observes full environment densities and extracts score and Hessian information, using two auxiliary environments to form two Hessian-difference groups whose diagonal ratio has distinct entries. The present observation regime releases only independently standardized correlation orbits with known normalized excitation profiles and proves a sharp reference-plus-one-environment frontier; Montagna's density-level theorem therefore informs but does not subsume the projective-copula result. RothenhauslerEtAl2015BackShift, Section 2.4, equation (10), Theorem 1, and Appendix A, is a related identification template but starts from covariance differences measured on one common scale. For any positive diagonal \(H_e\), \(\operatorname{corr}(H_e\Sigma_eH_e)=R_e\), while changing one diagonal entry of one \(H_e\) changes a covariance difference; therefore independently standardized correlations do not determine BACKSHIFT's input. This is an elementary observation about the observation regime, not a theorem contribution. PetersBuhlmann2014EqualVariance, Theorem 1, supplies a recursive triangular-factorization precedent. PetersEtAl2016InvariantPrediction, Sections 2--3, intersects predictor sets whose conditional response law remains invariant across environments and provides confidence statements for causal predictors. That intervention-invariance observation is different from observing only standardized joint correlations: the present target is the whole DAG, and its identifying variation is a known coordinatewise normalized noise-variance schedule rather than an invariant response regression. GamellaEtAl2022GnIES is the GnIES manuscript arXiv:2211.14897v4: equations (1)--(5) specify independent Gaussian innovations with environment-varying diagonal noise covariance, and Theorem 6 states that, under its assumptions, the intervention-equivalence class equals the sparsest distribution-equivalent graphs. Its full Gaussian environment distributions reveal scale-bearing variance interventions; independently standardized correlations retain only the calibrated ratios \(d\), so the canonical projective predictor is not a GnIES score specialization. TaebEtAl2024Latent is the distinct Journal of Machine Learning Research 25(293) article about latent-variable models with unknown additive interventions. Park2020ConditionalVariance, Theorem 2, uses marginal and conditional variances from a full observational law, while YinEtAl2024HNM, equation (5) and Theorem 2, uses parent-dependent conditional-variance functions in a nonlinear heteroscedastic-noise model. Those conditional-variance observation models are not available after unrelated site standardization; the present model instead uses cross-environment correlation predictions and known normalized excitation profiles. LiuEtAl2012NPNskeptic, Theorem 4.2, supplies the uniform sine-transformed Kendall correlation bound, whereas Theorem 4.3 is only its plug-in transfer to undirected precision procedures. The fixed directed-support margin here is declared directly on \(B^0\), not attributed to either theorem or to VanDeGeerBuhlmann2013DAG's different rate-dependent all-order condition; the latter is only support-recovery precedent. MitraZhang2014RankCorrelation informs simultaneous matrix control. WangKolarDrton2025ConfidenceOrderings and StriederEtAl2021ConfidenceCausalDiscovery motivate preserving causal-order uncertainty. LiEtAl2024FedCDH supplies the federated consumer but communicates covariance tensors richer than Kendall releases. Afsari2008JDSensitivity supplies broader motivation from diagonalizer conditioning and uniqueness moduli; it is not a source for the imposed bounds \(m\le d_{ej}\le M\). WangEtAl2023EquivalenceEnumeration and WienobstEtAl2023MECEnumeration motivate canonical augmentation for the open sparse quotient problem.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:acyclic-linear-structure]\label{ass:acyclic-linear-structure}
\(B_{jj}=0\) for every \(j\in V\), and \(B\) is strictly lower triangular after a simultaneous permutation of its rows and columns.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Acyclic linear structural equation model, \cite{PetersBuhlmann2014EqualVariance}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-gaussian-innovations]\label{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}
\(\varepsilon_e\sim\mathcal N(0,\operatorname{diag}(v)D_e)\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent Gaussian innovations under diagonal noise interventions, \cite{GamellaEtAl2022GnIES}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-normalized-schedule]\label{ass:known-normalized-schedule}
\(d_{ej}>0\) is known for every \(e\in\mathcal E\) and \(j\in V\), and \(d_{0j}=1\) for every \(j\in V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A calibrated experiment can record each innovation channel's variance multiplier relative to its own reference value. Absolute innovation scales and the units of observed variables need not be shared across sites.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-continuous-copula-sampling]\label{ass:iid-continuous-copula-sampling}
\((X_{e\ell})_{\ell=1}^{n_e}\) are independent and identically distributed within each \(e\in\mathcal E\), independent across environments, and have continuous margins with Gaussian copula correlation \(R_e\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian copula sampling with continuous margins, \cite{LiuEtAl2012NPNskeptic}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reference-conditioning]\label{ass:reference-conditioning}
\(\lambda_{\min}(R_0)\ge\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform positive-definiteness of a latent correlation matrix, \cite{MitraZhang2014RankCorrelation}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:schedule-conditioning]\label{ass:schedule-conditioning}
\(m\le d_{ej}\le M\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\) and \(j\in V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded reference-normalized excitation is imposed to keep perturbation constants uniform over calibrated experimental schedules. It is realistic when actuators operate inside a prespecified multiplicative range; bounded calibration error adds to the predictor discrepancy and consumes the same wrong-order score-separation budget through the predictor sensitivity modulus.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reference-standardized-beta-min]\label{ass:reference-standardized-beta-min}
\(\min_{B^0_{ji}\ne0}|B^0_{ji}|\ge b\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit compactness restriction on the identified, dimensionless reference-standardized coefficient coordinates. It is invariant to every positive change of the unreleased variable units and excludes supports arbitrarily close to edge deletion.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:wrong-order-separation]\label{ass:wrong-order-separation}
\(\Delta(R,d,G)\ge\delta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This observable correlation-scale margin holds on compact subsets separated from the finite algebraic wrong-order loci and can be audited from the order predictors themselves.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:canonical-order-reduction]\label{lem:canonical-order-reduction}
For every positive-definite correlation matrix \(C\) and order \(\pi\), \(C=(I_p-B^{\pi}(C))^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(v^{\pi}(C))(I_p-B^{\pi}(C))^{-\mathsf T}\) is the unique unit-diagonal modified-Cholesky representation in order \(\pi\). For every member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,E}(d)\) and every \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Top}(G)\), \(B^{\pi_0}(R_0)=B^0=H_0BH_0^{-1}\) and \(v^{\pi_0}(R_0)=\operatorname{diag}(H_0)^2\odot v\); hence \(B^0\) is independent of the chosen true topological order. The released family must equal \((\Phi_e^{\pi_0}(R_0;d))_{e\in\mathcal E}\). Under any positive common change of raw variable units, \(B\) and \(H_0\) transform contragrediently while \(R\), \(d\), \(B^0\), and every order score remain unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an order \(\pi\).  For each \(j\), abbreviate \(S=S_j^\pi\), put
\[
 \beta_j=C[j,S]C[S,S]^{-1},\qquad
 \xi_j=Z_j-\beta_j Z_S,
\]
where \(Z\) is any centered random vector with covariance \(C\).  Every principal submatrix of the positive-definite matrix \(C\) is invertible, so these quantities are defined.  The normal equations give
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\xi_j,Z_S)
 =C[j,S]-C[j,S]C[S,S]^{-1}C[S,S]=0.
\]
If \(i\) precedes \(j\), then \(\xi_i\) is a linear combination of variables that also precede \(j\); consequently \(\operatorname{Cov}(\xi_j,\xi_i)=0\).  Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_j)
 =1-C[j,S]C[S,S]^{-1}C[S,j]=v_j^\pi(C)>0,
\]
where strict positivity is the Schur-complement consequence of \(C\succ0\).  In matrix form, with \(L=I_p-B^\pi(C)\), the preceding equations are
\[
 LCL^{\mathsf T}=\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C)).
\]
The order restriction makes \(L\) unit triangular, hence invertible, and rearrangement proves
\[
 C=(I_p-B^\pi(C))^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C))(I_p-B^\pi(C))^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Conversely, in any representation of this form whose coefficient matrix respects \(\pi\), the structural residual for \(j\) is uncorrelated with every predecessor.  Its coefficient row must therefore solve the same nonsingular normal equations, and its variance is the displayed Schur complement.  This proves uniqueness without an additional rank assumption.

Now take a member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,E}(d)\), write \(h_j=(H_0)_{jj}>0\), and standardize once, in the reference units, by \(Z_e=H_0Y_e\).  Then
\[
 Z_e=(H_0BH_0^{-1})Z_e+H_0\varepsilon_e,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(H_0\varepsilon_e)
 =\operatorname{diag}(h_1^2v_1,\ldots,h_p^2v_p)D_e.
\]
The matrices \(H_0\), \(\operatorname{diag}(v)\), and \(D_e\) are diagonal, so the last identity follows directly from the innovation covariance assumption.  In environment zero, the covariance of \(Z_0\) is \(R_0\), because \(d_{0j}=1\) and by the definition of \(H_0\).  If \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Top}(G)\), then \(H_0BH_0^{-1}\) respects \(\pi_0\).  Applying the just-proved uniqueness to its reference representation yields
\[
 B^{\pi_0}(R_0)=H_0BH_0^{-1}=B^0,
 \qquad
 v^{\pi_0}(R_0)=(h_1^2v_1,\ldots,h_p^2v_p)
 =\operatorname{diag}(H_0)^2\odot v.
\]
The right sides do not depend on the selected true topological order, proving the asserted order independence.  Substitution into the environment covariance gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_e)
 =(I_p-B^0)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(v^{\pi_0}(R_0))D_e
 (I_p-B^0)^{-\mathsf T}.
\]
Positive diagonal congruence does not change correlation, so
\[
 R_e=\operatorname{corr}(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_e))
 =\operatorname{corr}(\operatorname{Cov}(Z_e))
 =\Phi_e^{\pi_0}(R_0;d).
\]

Finally let \(P\) be any positive diagonal common change of raw variable units.  Then \(\Sigma'_e=P\Sigma_eP\), \(B'=PBP^{-1}\), and
\[
 H'_0=\operatorname{diag}(P\Sigma_0P)^{-1/2}=H_0P^{-1}.
\]
It follows equation by equation that
\[
 H'_0B'(H'_0)^{-1}=H_0BH_0^{-1}=B^0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{corr}(\Sigma'_e)=\operatorname{corr}(\Sigma_e)=R_e.
\]
The normalized schedule \(d\) is unchanged by a change of variable units.  Since every \(Q_\pi\) is defined only from \(R\), \(d\), and the canonical predictor, all order scores are unchanged as well.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma removes continuous scale search and identifies the unique gauge-invariant coefficient coordinates on which support separation can be imposed. \textit{Gap.} PetersBuhlmann2014EqualVariance, Theorem 1, uses recursive conditional variances under equal errors, while RothenhauslerEtAl2015BackShift, Theorem 1, normalizes a joint diagonalizer of full covariance differences. Neither derives an order predictor or coefficient margin invariant to independently erased environment scales. \textit{Consumer.} A rank-only federation can validate a candidate causal order without reconstructing any site's marginal units.

\begin{proposition}[prop:tied-profile-twins]\label{prop:tied-profile-twins}
Suppose \(d_{ei}=d_{ej}\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\). For every \(\beta,\gamma\ne0\) and \(u,w,z>0\), the fork \(i\to j\), \(i\to k\), with coefficients \(\beta,\gamma\) and reference innovation variances \((u,w,z)\) in coordinate order \((i,j,k)\), has exactly the same covariance in every environment as the chain \(j\to i\to k\) with coefficients \(\beta u/(\beta^2u+w),\gamma\) and reference innovation variances \((uw/(\beta^2u+w),\beta^2u+w,z)\). Thus the two nonisomorphic exact supports form a positive-dimensional twin family. Setting \(\beta=\gamma=u=w=z=1\) gives the covariance block \(\begin{psmallmatrix}d_{ei}&d_{ei}&d_{ei}\\d_{ei}&2d_{ei}&d_{ei}\\d_{ei}&d_{ei}&d_{ei}+d_{ek}\end{psmallmatrix}\). For \(p=2\), deleting vertex \(k\) gives reverse one-edge twins; for \(p>3\), adjoining independent vertices embeds the family into the larger exact-support strata.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work first on the coordinates \((i,j,k)\).  In the fork, the structural equations are
\[
 Y_i=\varepsilon_i,\qquad Y_j=\beta Y_i+\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad Y_k=\gamma Y_i+\varepsilon_k.
\]
Using independence of the innovations and the tie \(d_{ei}=d_{ej}\), its covariance in environment \(e\) is
\[
 \Sigma_e^{\mathrm F}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 ud_{ei}&\beta ud_{ei}&\gamma ud_{ei}\\
 \beta ud_{ei}&(\beta^2u+w)d_{ei}&\beta\gamma ud_{ei}\\
 \gamma ud_{ei}&\beta\gamma ud_{ei}&\gamma^2ud_{ei}+zd_{ek}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Set \(q=\beta^2u+w>0\).  The proposed chain has equations
\[
 Y'_j=\varepsilon'_j,\qquad
 Y'_i=\frac{\beta u}{q}Y'_j+\varepsilon'_i,
 \qquad Y'_k=\gamma Y'_i+\varepsilon'_k,
\]
and reference innovation variances
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon'_i)=\frac{uw}{q},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon'_j)=q,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon'_k)=z.
\]
All three are positive.  Since the first two profiles are tied,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y'_j)=qd_{ei},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_e(Y'_i,Y'_j)=\frac{\beta u}{q}qd_{ei}=\beta ud_{ei},
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y'_i)
 =\frac{\beta^2u^2}{q}d_{ei}+\frac{uw}{q}d_{ei}
 =ud_{ei}.
\]
The equation for \(Y'_k\) then gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_e(Y'_k,Y'_i)=\gamma ud_{ei},\quad
 \operatorname{Cov}_e(Y'_k,Y'_j)=\beta\gamma ud_{ei},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y'_k)=\gamma^2ud_{ei}+zd_{ek}.
\]
Thus \(\Sigma_e^{\mathrm C}=\Sigma_e^{\mathrm F}\) for every environment, and hence their released correlations agree.  In a topological order of either model, sequential regression recovers its displayed structural coefficients and positive innovation variances by the normal equations; therefore the corresponding canonical order is retained in \(\mathcal F(R,d)\).  Both exact supports are consequently in the same fiber.

The parameters range over the relatively open set
\(\{(\beta,\gamma,u,w,z):\beta\gamma\ne0,\ u,w,z>0\}\), and neither displayed edge vanishes.  The fork has a vertex of out-degree two, whereas the chain has maximum out-degree one, so the three-vertex directed graphs are nonisomorphic.  At \(\beta=\gamma=u=w=z=1\), the preceding covariance reduces to
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 d_{ei}&d_{ei}&d_{ei}\\
 d_{ei}&2d_{ei}&d_{ei}\\
 d_{ei}&d_{ei}&d_{ei}+d_{ek}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(p=2\), deletion of \(k\) leaves the two positive reverse one-edge representations just computed.  For \(p>3\), adjoining vertices with independent positive-variance innovations in both models appends identical diagonal blocks, preserves the two exact supports on \(\{i,j,k\}\), and proves the asserted embedding.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The positive fork--chain family proves necessity without zero innovations or vanished displayed edges. \textit{Gap.} RothenhauslerEtAl2015BackShift, Appendix A, perturbs covariance-difference diagonalizers; it does not exhibit nonisomorphic acyclic models whose full covariance families coincide under tied multiplicative profiles. \textit{Consumer.} Experimental designers receive an explicit impossibility certificate for any tied schedule pair.

\begin{theorem}[thm:universal-generic-frontier]\label{thm:universal-generic-frontier}
The following are equivalent: \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\) holds; and, for every labeled DAG \(G\), every \((B,v)\in\Theta_G\) outside a proper real algebraic subset generates through \(\mathfrak M_{p,E}(d)\) a released family \(R\) satisfying \(\mathcal F(R,d)=\{G\}\). Under \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\), every nontopological order \(\pi\) violates at least one denominator-cleared correlation-matching polynomial that is not identically zero on \(\Theta_G\); hence the generically matching orders are exactly \(\operatorname{Top}(G)\), and each returns \(G\). If \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\) fails, the tied-profile constructions give a relatively open positive-parameter family in an exact-support stratum with a nonsingleton fiber.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\).  Fix a labeled DAG \(G\), and let \(\pi\notin\operatorname{Top}(G)\).  Then some edge \(i\to j\) of \(G\) is reversed by \(\pi\).  By projective separation there is an environment \(e\) with \(d_{ei}\ne d_{ej}\).

It is useful to remove square roots before constructing the separating equation.  Apply the sequential-regression formulas to the unstandardized reference covariance \(\Sigma_0\), and denote the resulting environment prediction by \(\widetilde\Omega_e^\pi\).  If \(K\) is positive diagonal, direct substitution in the regression formula gives
\[
 B^\pi(KCK) = KB^\pi(C)K^{-1},\qquad
 v_j^\pi(KCK)=K_{jj}^2v_j^\pi(C),
\]
where the same formulas are understood on an arbitrary positive-definite covariance.  Hence the predicted covariance transforms as \(K\widetilde\Omega_e^\pi K\), and its correlation is invariant.  Taking \(K=H_0\) shows that matching the frozen predictor based on \(R_0\) implies equality of the squared correlations of \(\widetilde\Omega_e^\pi\) and \(\Sigma_e\).  In particular it implies
\[
 P_{\pi,e,ij}:=
 (\widetilde\Omega_{e,ij}^\pi)^2\Sigma_{e,ii}\Sigma_{e,jj}
 -(\Sigma_{e,ij})^2\widetilde\Omega_{e,ii}^\pi
 \widetilde\Omega_{e,jj}^\pi=0. \tag{1}
\]

On the affine coefficient space obtained by forcing coefficients off \(G\) to zero, acyclicity gives
\[
 (I_p-B)^{-1}=I_p+B+\cdots+B^{p-1}.
\]
Thus every entry of \(\Sigma_e\) is polynomial in the free coefficients and innovation variances.  Every sequential-regression coefficient is rational in those entries, with denominators that are principal minors of \(\Sigma_0\); those minors are strictly positive on the legal parameter set.  Consequently, after multiplication by a product of these denominators, (1) becomes a polynomial equation.

This polynomial is not identically zero.  Indeed, specialize in the closure of the exact-support stratum by retaining only the coefficient \(B_{ji}=a\ne0\), and retain arbitrary positive \(v_i,v_j\); all other variables are independent.  Put \(c=a^2v_i/v_j>0\).  In the true direction the squared-correlation odds of the pair are
\[
 \frac{\rho_{e,ij}^2}{1-\rho_{e,ij}^2}
 =c\frac{d_{ei}}{d_{ej}}. \tag{2}
\]
The order \(\pi\) places \(j\) before \(i\), so its reference Cholesky representation reverses this two-variable regression.  Its environment prediction has odds
\[
 \frac{(\rho_{e,ij}^{\pi})^2}{1-(\rho_{e,ij}^{\pi})^2}
 =c\frac{d_{ej}}{d_{ei}}. \tag{3}
\]
Because the multipliers are positive, equality of (2) and (3) would imply \(d_{ei}=d_{ej}\), contrary to the selected environment.  Hence (1), and therefore its denominator-cleared polynomial, is nonzero at this specialization.  Evaluation outside \(\Theta_G\) is legitimate here because it establishes that the polynomial on the ambient affine support space is not the zero polynomial.

For completeness, a nonzero real polynomial has a zero set with empty Euclidean interior: induction on the number of variables reduces along a coordinate to the fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many roots, choosing a slice on which at least one coefficient polynomial is nonzero.  The same induction, with Fubini's theorem, gives Lebesgue measure zero.  There are only finitely many nontopological orders.  The union of their matching loci is therefore contained in a proper algebraic subset of the ambient support space, and its intersection with \(\Theta_G\) is a proper algebraic exceptional subset.

Outside that subset, no nontopological order matches.  Every \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Top}(G)\) does match by the canonical-order reduction, and the same reduction gives
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(B^{\pi_0}(R_0))
 =\operatorname{supp}(H_0BH_0^{-1})=G,
\]
because positive diagonal conjugation preserves the zero pattern.  It follows directly from the definition of the fiber that \(\mathcal F(R,d)=\{G\}\).  This proves universal generic identification under \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\), including the stronger assertion that the generically matching orders are exactly \(\operatorname{Top}(G)\).

Conversely, if \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\) fails, there are \(i\ne j\) whose profiles agree in every environment.  For \(p=2\), the reverse one-edge family in the tied-profile proposition gives two distinct exact supports throughout the relatively open set of nonzero edge coefficients and positive variances.  For \(p\ge3\), use the fork on \(i\to j\) and \(i\to k\), with all other vertices isolated.  The proposition supplies a chain support with the identical covariance family for every \(\beta\gamma\ne0\) and \(u,w,z>0\), while the independent added variances may range freely over \((0,\infty)^{p-3}\).  This is a relatively open positive-parameter family in the exact fork-support stratum and its fiber is nonsingleton.  Therefore the universal generic property fails.  Both implications are proved.

The prescribed three-variable check is also exact.  With all three order-consistent coefficients and all reference innovation variances equal to one, one obtains
\[
 \Sigma_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2\\1&2&3\\2&3&6\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2\\1&5&6\\2&6&17\end{pmatrix}
\]
under \(d_1=(1,4,9)\); their determinants are respectively \(1\) and \(36\).  The actual environment-one odds for the unordered pairs \(12,13,23\) are \(1/4,4/13,36/49\), while the first-pair predictions for the six orders \(123,132,213,231,312,321\) are respectively
\[
 \frac14,\quad\frac29,\quad4,\quad\frac43,\quad18,\quad\frac{27}{4}.
\]
Only the first order's prediction equals the actual odds for its first pair; thus all five wrong orders are excluded without a marginal-scale choice outside the canonical list.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the sharp universal generic excitation frontier after every marginal scale is erased. \textit{Gap.} Montagna2025InvariancePrinciple, Section 3, Theorem 1, identifies a causal graph from full environment densities through log-score and Hessian information using two auxiliary environments. GamellaEtAl2022GnIES, Theorem 6, characterizes sparsest full-distribution-equivalent graphs, RothenhauslerEtAl2015BackShift, Theorem 1 and equation (13), use pairwise noncollinearity of full covariance-difference profiles, and PetersEtAl2016InvariantPrediction, Sections 2--3, targets causal predictors through invariant conditional response laws. The present if-and-only-if result instead identifies the whole DAG from independently standardized correlation orbits and calibrated normalized schedules, permits one reference plus one auxiliary environment, and proves necessity with exact directed-support twins. \textit{Consumer.} BackShift-style and federated workflows obtain an exact test of whether sitewise standardization preserves universal generic DAG identification.

\begin{theorem}[thm:conditional-pair-completeness]\label{thm:conditional-pair-completeness}
Assume \(\mathrm{PS}(d)\) and that \(G\) is complete relative to a total order \(\pi_0\). Outside a proper real algebraic subset of \(\Theta_G\), at every true ancestral prefix \(S=\{\pi_0(1),\ldots,\pi_0(s)\}\), the ordered pair \((\pi_0(s+1),\pi_0(s+2))\) satisfies the conditional-odds profile identities, while every other ordered pair fails at least one identity. Thus \(\mathsf{PairPeel}(R,d)\) selects the next true vertex at every stage and returns \(\{G\}\). Maintaining \(E\) conditional covariance matrices by one rank-one Schur update at each stage costs \(O(E(p-s)^2)\), testing all ordered pairs costs \(O(E(p-s)^2)\), and summation over \(s=0,\ldots,p-2\), together with the final positivity, squared-cross-product, and sign checks, costs \(O(Ep^3)\) exact-real arithmetic operations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a true ancestral prefix \(S\) and put \(U=V\setminus S\).  In environment \(e\), the structural equations on \(U\), conditional on \(Y_{e,S}\), are
\[
 Y_{e,U}=B_{UU}Y_{e,U}+B_{US}Y_{e,S}+\varepsilon_{e,U}.
\]
Because \(S\) is ancestral, \(Y_{e,S}\) is a function only of \(\varepsilon_{e,S}\).  The diagonal innovation assumption makes \(\varepsilon_{e,U}\) independent of \(Y_{e,S}\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y_{e,U}\mid Y_{e,S})
 =(I-B_{UU})^{-1}\operatorname{diag}((v_jd_{ej})_{j\in U})
 (I-B_{UU})^{-\mathsf T}. \tag{4}
\]
If \(R_e=K_e\Sigma_eK_e\) for a positive diagonal \(K_e\), the Schur complement obeys
\[
 R_e/R_e[S,S]
 =K_{e,U}\bigl(\Sigma_e/\Sigma_e[S,S]\bigr)K_{e,U}.
\]
Consequently its normalization \(R_e^{(S)}\) is exactly the correlation matrix of (4).  Thus conditioning an ancestral prefix leaves an acyclic linear model on \(U\) with the same innovation multipliers.

Relabel the remaining true complete order as \(1,\ldots,q\).  The first two equations are
\(Y_{e,1}=\varepsilon_{e,1}\) and
\(Y_{e,2}=aY_{e,1}+\varepsilon_{e,2}\), where completeness gives \(a\ne0\).  Direct covariance algebra yields
\[
 \frac{(R_{e,12}^{(S)})^2}{1-(R_{e,12}^{(S)})^2}
 =\frac{a^2v_1}{v_2}\frac{d_{e1}}{d_{e2}}, \tag{5}
\]
and the sign is the fixed sign of \(a\).  Since \(d_{01}=d_{02}=1\), (5) is precisely the conditional-odds identity tested for the ordered pair \((1,2)\), in every environment.

We next show that each other ordered pair fails generically.  The conditional correlations are rational functions of the coefficients and positive variances after squared correlations are denominator-cleared, exactly as in the frontier proof.  It is therefore enough to give, for every wrong pair, one specialization in the closure of the complete-support stratum at which a tested equality fails.

If the proposed pair is \((a,b)\) with \(a>b\), retain only the true edge \(b\to a\).  Projective separation supplies an environment where the two profiles differ, and equations (2)--(3) from the reversed-edge calculation show that the proposed reverse identity fails.

If \(1<a<b\), choose \(k<a\) and retain only \(k\to a\to b\), with coefficients \(c\ne0\) and \(g\ne0\), respectively.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_e(Y_a)=v_ad_{ea}+c^2v_kd_{ek},
\]
and the squared-correlation odds for \((a,b)\) are
\[
 \frac{(R_{e,ab}^{(S)})^2}{1-(R_{e,ab}^{(S)})^2}
 =\frac{g^2(v_ad_{ea}+c^2v_kd_{ek})}{v_bd_{eb}}. \tag{6}
\]
Equality of (6) with its reference value multiplied by \(d_{ea}/d_{eb}\) would require
\[
 v_ad_{ea}+c^2v_kd_{ek}=(v_a+c^2v_k)d_{ea},
\]
or \(d_{ek}=d_{ea}\).  Pairwise separation provides an environment violating that equality.

The remaining wrong case is \((1,b)\) with \(b>2\).  Choose \(1<k<b\) and retain only \(1\to b\) and \(k\to b\), with nonzero coefficients \(g,c\).  Independence of \(Y_1\) and \(Y_k\) at this specialization gives
\[
 \frac{(R_{e,1b}^{(S)})^2}{1-(R_{e,1b}^{(S)})^2}
 =\frac{g^2v_1d_{e1}}{v_bd_{eb}+c^2v_kd_{ek}}. \tag{7}
\]
For (7) to equal its reference value multiplied by \(d_{e1}/d_{eb}\), one would need \(d_{ek}=d_{eb}\) in every environment, again contradicting projective separation.  These three cases exhaust all ordered pairs except \((1,2)\).

Each failure just exhibited makes a denominator-cleared tested polynomial nonzero.  The elementary polynomial zero-set argument from the frontier theorem, applied to the finitely many pairs and the finitely many true prefixes, shows that outside one proper real algebraic subset, \((1,2)\) is the unique passing ordered pair at every stage.  The algorithm therefore appends the next true vertex, and induction on \(|S|\) constructs \(\pi_0\).  The final canonical covariance predictions equal the released correlations by the canonical-order reduction.  They are positive definite, so their diagonals are positive, and equality of correlations implies both the squared-cross-product identities and the sign inequalities in \(\mathsf{FiberCert}\).  Hence the terminal certificate passes and the returned support is
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(B^{\pi_0}(R_0))=G.
\]
The universal generic frontier also ensures that this support is the singleton fiber on the same complement after enlarging the exceptional set if necessary.

At a stage with \(q=p-s\) remaining vertices, one rank-one Schur update and renormalization of each of the \(E\) matrices uses \(O(Eq^2)\) exact-real arithmetic operations.  There are \(q(q-1)\) ordered pairs, and testing each in all environments also uses \(O(Eq^2)\) operations.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_{q=2}^{p}O(Eq^2)=O(Ep^3).
\]
Sequential factorization and the final environment predictions and certificates require at most another \(O(Ep^3)\) operations.  This establishes the stated exact-real arithmetic, rather than bit-complexity, bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies the non-tautological local completeness argument and operation count behind cubic recovery on generic complete DAGs. \textit{Gap.} PetersBuhlmann2014EqualVariance, Theorem 1, and Park2020ConditionalVariance, Theorem 2, use scale-bearing conditional variances. The identities here use calibrated multiplier ratios and conditional correlations that survive independent site standardization. \textit{Consumer.} Dense systems can be recovered from calibrated rank-only environment summaries without factorial order traversal.

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-predictor-perturbation]\label{lem:uniform-predictor-perturbation}
Let \(C,C'\) be correlation matrices with \(\lambda_{\min}(C)\wedge\lambda_{\min}(C')\ge\eta\) and \(\lVert C-C'\rVert_{\max}\le\epsilon\). Under \(m\le d_{ej}\le M\), simultaneously for every order \(\pi\), \(\lVert B^{\pi}(C)-B^{\pi}(C')\rVert_{\max}\le2p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon\) and \(\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\lVert\Phi_e^{\pi}(C;d)-\Phi_e^{\pi}(C';d)\rVert_{\max}\le14(M/m)p^4\eta^{-4}\epsilon\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an order and one row, write \(S=S_j^\pi\), \(x=C[j,S]\), \(K=C[S,S]\), and use primes for \(C'\).  Principal-submatrix interlacing gives
\(\lambda_{\min}(K),\lambda_{\min}(K')\ge\eta\).  Since a correlation entry has absolute value at most one,
\[
 \|x-x'\|_2\le\sqrt p\,\epsilon,
 \quad \|x\|_2\vee\|x'\|_2\le\sqrt p,
 \quad \|K-K'\|_2\le p\epsilon.
\]
The inverse identity and the eigenvalue bounds imply
\[
 \|K^{-1}-(K')^{-1}\|_2
 \le\|K^{-1}\|_2\|K-K'\|_2\|(K')^{-1}\|_2
 \le p\eta^{-2}\epsilon. \tag{8}
\]
Because \(C\) and \(C'\) have trace \(p\), necessarily \(0<\eta\le1\).  From the regression formula and (8),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|xK^{-1}-x'(K')^{-1}\|_2
 &\le \sqrt p\,\eta^{-1}\epsilon
       +p^{3/2}\eta^{-2}\epsilon\\
 &\le2p^{3/2}\eta^{-2}\epsilon.
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The empty-predecessor row is identically zero.  Thus (9), for all rows, gives both
\[
 \|B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C')\|_{\max}
 \le2p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon \tag{10}
\]
and the operator-norm bound
\[
 \|B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C')\|_2
 \le\|B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C')\|_{\mathrm F}
 \le2p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon. \tag{11}
\]

For the Schur residuals, expand the difference of the two quadratic forms and use (8):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |v_j^\pi(C)-v_j^\pi(C')|
 &\le 2p\eta^{-1}\epsilon+p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon\\
 &\le3p^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{12}
\]
Also, the variational formula for a Schur complement gives
\[
 v_j^\pi(C)=\min_t\operatorname{Var}(Z_j-t^{\mathsf T}Z_S),
\]
so \(\eta\le v_j^\pi(C)\le1\), and the same holds for \(C'\).  Put
\[
 A=(I_p-B^\pi(C))^{-1},\quad A'=(I_p-B^\pi(C'))^{-1},
 \quad V=\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C)),\quad V'=\operatorname{diag}(v^\pi(C')).
\]
Since \(C=AVA^{\mathsf T}\), \(V\succeq\eta I_p\), and \(\|C\|_2\le p\),
\[
 \|A\|_2\vee\|A'\|_2\le\sqrt{p/\eta}. \tag{13}
\]
The resolvent identity, (11), and (13) now yield
\[
 \|A-A'\|_2
 =\|A(B^\pi(C)-B^\pi(C'))A'\|_2
 \le2p^3\eta^{-3}\epsilon. \tag{14}
\]

For a fixed environment, let \(\Omega=AVD_eA^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(\Omega'=A'V'D_e(A')^{\mathsf T}\).  The schedule condition and (12) give
\[
 \|VD_e\|_2\vee\|V'D_e\|_2\le M,
 \qquad
 \|(V-V')D_e\|_2\le3Mp^2\eta^{-2}\epsilon.
\]
Expanding \(\Omega-\Omega'\) into the two factor differences and the middle diagonal difference, then applying (13)--(14), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Omega-\Omega'\|_2
 &\le4Mp^{7/2}\eta^{-7/2}\epsilon
      +3Mp^3\eta^{-3}\epsilon\\
 &\le7Mp^4\eta^{-4}\epsilon. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
The last inequality uses \(p\ge2\) and \(\eta\le1\).

Because \(mI_p\preceq D_e\preceq MI_p\) and the diagonal matrices commute,
\[
 mC\preceq\Omega\preceq MC,
 \qquad mC'\preceq\Omega'\preceq MC'.
\]
In particular every diagonal entry of \(\Omega\) and \(\Omega'\) is at least \(m\).  Along the positive-definite line segment \(\Omega_t=(1-t)\Omega+t\Omega'\), differentiation of
\(\Omega_{t,ab}/\sqrt{\Omega_{t,aa}\Omega_{t,bb}}\), together with
\(|\Omega_{t,ab}|\le\sqrt{\Omega_{t,aa}\Omega_{t,bb}}\), gives
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{corr}(\Omega_t)_{ab}\right|
 \le \frac{2}{m}\|\Omega'-\Omega\|_{\max}.
\]
Integration over \(t\in[0,1]\), followed by (15) and
\(\|\cdot\|_{\max}\le\|\cdot\|_2\), proves
\[
 \|\Phi_e^\pi(C;d)-\Phi_e^\pi(C';d)\|_{\max}
 \le14(M/m)p^4\eta^{-4}\epsilon.
\]
Every bound was independent of the order and environment, so the two asserted simultaneous inequalities follow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This simultaneous modulus is the bridge from rank concentration to graph-valued inference over every candidate order. \textit{Gap.} MitraZhang2014RankCorrelation, Theorem 2, controls rank-correlation submatrices, and Afsari2008JDSensitivity studies diagonalizer sensitivity; neither propagates errors uniformly through all projective Cholesky predictors. \textit{Consumer.} Federated analysts obtain explicit penalties for weak correlation conditioning and dispersed normalized excitations.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rank-summary-confidence]\label{thm:rank-summary-confidence}
Under the continuous Gaussian-copula sampling condition, \(\Pr(\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\lVert\widehat R_e-R_e\rVert_{\max}\le r)\ge1-\alpha\). For every law in \(\mathfrak M_{p,E}(d;\kappa,m,M,b,\delta)\), \(\Pr(G\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_{\alpha})\ge1-\alpha\). If \(r<\kappa/(2p)\), \(b>2K_Br\), and \(\delta>2T\), then on the same simultaneous event \(\widehat{\mathcal G}_{\alpha}=\{G\}\). Here \(b\) is exclusively the minimum signal of \(B^0=B^{\pi_0}(R_0)\), so the threshold, fallback, coverage domain, and singleton conclusion are unchanged by arbitrary positive reference rescaling.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(N=Ep(p-1)/2\).  For a fixed environment and coordinate pair, continuity of the margins makes the Kendall kernel
\[
 h((x_i,x_j),(x'_i,x'_j))
 =\operatorname{sign}((x_i-x'_i)(x_j-x'_j))
\]
take values in \(\{-1,1\}\) almost surely.  Kendall's statistic is the order-two U-statistic with this kernel.  If the latent Gaussian pair has correlation \(\rho\), invariance under strictly increasing marginal transforms reduces its expectation to the Gaussian calculation.  For a standard bivariate normal pair \((U,V)\) of correlation \(\rho\),
\[
 q(\rho):=\Pr(U>0,V>0)
 =\int_0^\infty\phi(u)\Phi\!\left(\frac{\rho u}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\right)du.
\]
Differentiation under this integrable Gaussian density gives
\(q'(\rho)=1/(2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2})\), while \(q(0)=1/4\).  Hence
\[
 \tau=4q(\rho)-1=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(\rho),
 \qquad \rho=\sin(\pi\tau/2). \tag{16}
\]

Put \(q_e=\lfloor n_e/2\rfloor\).  For a uniformly random permutation of the observations, average the kernel over the \(q_e\) disjoint adjacent pairs.  Conditional averaging over the permutation gives exactly the complete U-statistic.  The paired kernels are independent and lie in an interval of length two.  The elementary exponential convexity bound for a centered variable in such an interval gives
\(\mathbb E e^{s(W-\mathbb EW)}\le e^{s^2/2}\).  Jensen's inequality for the conditional permutation average and Chernoff optimization therefore give
\[
 \Pr(|\widehat\tau_{e,ij}-\tau_{e,ij}|>t)
 \le2\exp(-q_et^2/2). \tag{17}
\]
Since the sine map in (16) is \(\pi/2\)-Lipschitz, a union bound over the \(N\) environment-pair combinations, with
\[
 t=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2N/\alpha)}{n_\star}},
\]
shows
\[
 \Pr\!\left(\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \|\widehat R_e-R_e\|_{\max}\le
 \frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log(Ep(p-1)/\alpha)}{n_\star}}
 \right)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{18}
\]
The radius in (18) is exactly \(r\).

Call the simultaneous event in (18) \(\mathcal A\).  Its probability bound is distribution-free within the stated continuous Gaussian-copula sampling class.  On \(\mathcal A\),
\[
 \|\widehat R_0-R_0\|_2\le p r,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat R_0)
 \ge\lambda_{\min}(R_0)-pr\ge\kappa-pr, \tag{19}
\]
where the eigenvalue inequality follows by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient.  If either deterministic fallback inequality \(r\ge\kappa/(2p)\) or \(b\le2K_Br\) holds, the reported set is \(\mathfrak G_V\) and contains \(G\).  Otherwise (19) gives
\(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat R_0)>\kappa/2\), so the third fallback cannot occur on \(\mathcal A\).

Apply the uniform predictor perturbation lemma with
\(C=R_0\), \(C'=\widehat R_0\), \(\eta=\kappa/2\), and \(\epsilon=r\).  Its two constants become exactly
\[
 2p^2(\kappa/2)^{-2}=K_B,
 \qquad
 14(M/m)p^4(\kappa/2)^{-4}=L.
\]
Thus, simultaneously in \(\pi\),
\[
 \|B^\pi(\widehat R_0)-B^\pi(R_0)\|_{\max}\le K_Br, \tag{20}
\]
and the triangle inequality in every environment gives
\[
 |Q_\pi(\widehat R,d)-Q_\pi(R,d)|
 \le Lr+r=T. \tag{21}
\]

For a true topological order \(\pi_0\), the canonical-order reduction gives
\(Q_{\pi_0}(R,d)=0\) and \(B^{\pi_0}(R_0)=B^0\).  Equation (21) retains \(\pi_0\).  If \(B^0_{ji}=0\), (20) makes the estimated entry at most \(K_Br\), so the strict threshold deletes it.  If \(B^0_{ji}\ne0\), then
\[
 |B^{\pi_0}_{ji}(\widehat R_0)|
 \ge b-K_Br>K_Br
\]
because the nonfallback branch has \(b>2K_Br\).  Hence the thresholded support from \(\pi_0\) is exactly \(G\).  This proves \(G\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_\alpha\) on \(\mathcal A\), including all fallback cases, and (18) proves coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).

Assume now the three displayed strict small-error and separation inequalities in the theorem.  On \(\mathcal A\), (19) rules out the eigenvalue fallback, so the nonfallback construction is used.  If an order \(\pi\) is retained, (21) implies
\[
 Q_\pi(R,d)\le Q_\pi(\widehat R,d)+T\le2T.
\]
A nontopological order would instead satisfy
\(Q_\pi(R,d)\ge\Delta(R,d,G)\ge\delta>2T\), a contradiction.  Every retained order is therefore topological for \(G\), and the canonical reduction plus (20) makes every thresholded support equal to \(G\).  A true order is retained, so the set is exactly \(\{G\}\).

Under a positive common reference rescaling, \(R\), \(d\), every order score, and \(B^0=H_0BH_0^{-1}\) are invariant by the canonical-order reduction.  Therefore its minimum nonzero magnitude \(b\), the fallback rules, the margin class, and both conclusions are unchanged.  No coefficient in arbitrary unreleased raw units enters the threshold.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts rank-only releases into an honest support confidence set with a measurable all-DAG fallback. \textit{Gap.} LiuEtAl2012NPNskeptic, Theorem 4.2, gives the uniform sine-transformed Kendall correlation bound, while Theorem 4.3 transfers it to undirected precision procedures. WangKolarDrton2025ConfidenceOrderings, Theorem 1, gives asymptotic ordering-set validity for residual-bootstrap tests. Neither supplies finite-sample inversion of calibrated multi-environment projective scores or a gauge-invariant directed-support margin. \textit{Consumer.} FedCDH-style sites can send Kendall matrices and sample sizes while the coordinator reports all causal supports not ruled out at level \(\alpha\).

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:sparse-output-sensitive-enumeration]\label{oeq:sparse-output-sensitive-enumeration}
Can the projective-fragment quotient handle yield an exact-real algorithm that returns each support in \(\mathcal F(R,d)\) exactly once with runtime \(2^p\operatorname{poly}(p,E)+N_{\mathrm{out}}\operatorname{poly}(p,E)\), or with polynomial delay, for arbitrary sparse fibers, together with a matching family exhibiting factorial duplication under naive order traversal?
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Sparse supports may have many topological orders representing one numerical continuation, so identification alone does not give output-sensitive computation. \textit{Gap.} WangEtAl2023EquivalenceEnumeration and WienobstEtAl2023MECEnumeration use graphical canonical augmentation, but no local congruence is known for equality of numerical projective Cholesky fragments. The proved factorial exact-once baseline supplies partial progress and a factorial-duplication witness, but does not give the requested output-sensitive bound or polynomial delay. \textit{Consumer.} Exact confidence-set software could enumerate large sparse fibers without paying once per redundant topological order.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sparse-fiber-exact-once-factorial-baseline]\label{thm:sparse-fiber-exact-once-factorial-baseline}
There is an exact-real canonical-filter variant of \(\mathsf{FiberCert}(R,d)\) that outputs every support in \(\mathcal F(R,d)\) exactly once in \(O(p!\,E p^3)\) arithmetic operations: it outputs a retained support only at its lexicographically least topological order.  For every positive schedule \(d\), the family \(R_e=I_p\) has \(N_{\mathrm{out}}=1\), but naive order traversal retains all \(p!\) orders and therefore has factorial duplication.  These conclusions do not establish either the proposed \(2^p\operatorname{poly}(p,E)+N_{\mathrm{out}}\operatorname{poly}(p,E)\) bound or polynomial delay for arbitrary sparse fibers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the natural order of the vertex labels.  Run \(\mathsf{FiberCert}(R,d)\) over all permutations.  Whenever an order \(\pi\) is retained, form
\(H=\operatorname{supp}(B^\pi(R_0))\), compute the lexicographically least topological order \(\ell(H)\) by repeatedly taking the least labeled current source, and output \(H\) if and only if \(\pi=\ell(H)\).

First, the finite certificate used by \(\mathsf{FiberCert}\) is exact.  Its canonical covariance \(\Omega_e^\pi\) is positive definite because every Schur residual variance and every schedule entry is positive.  For positive diagonal entries, the squared-cross-product equation says that the predicted and observed correlations have the same absolute value.  The accompanying sign inequality selects the same sign; when the observed correlation is zero, the squared equation forces the predicted covariance entry to be zero.  Thus an order is retained exactly when \(\Phi_e^\pi(R_0;d)=R_e\) for every environment.  The supports encountered by retained orders are consequently exactly \(\mathcal F(R,d)\).

Every edge of \(H=\operatorname{supp}(B^\pi(R_0))\) points from a predecessor to a successor in \(\pi\), so \(\pi\) is topological for \(H\).  Conversely, let a retained order produce \(H\).  Its canonical coefficients and innovations define a compatible acyclic model with support \(H\).  Applying the canonical-order reduction to any \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Top}(H)\) shows that
\[
 B^\sigma(R_0)=B^\pi(R_0)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \Phi_e^\sigma(R_0;d)=R_e
\]
for every environment.  Hence \(\ell(H)\) is retained.  Exactly that occurrence passes the canonical filter, proving exact-once output.

A sequential modified-Cholesky factorization costs \(O(p^3)\); forming and checking all environment predictions costs \(O(Ep^3)\); and the greedy topological-order check costs \(O(p^2)\).  Traversing all \(p!\) permutations therefore gives the asserted \(O(p!\,Ep^3)\) arithmetic upper bound.

For the duplication witness, take \(B=0\) and arbitrary positive reference innovation variances.  Every environment covariance is diagonal, so \(R_e=I_p\).  For every order,
\[
 B^\pi(I_p)=0,\qquad v^\pi(I_p)=\mathbf 1,
 \qquad \Phi_e^\pi(I_p;d)=\operatorname{corr}(D_e)=I_p.
\]
Thus all \(p!\) orders are retained, but each yields the same empty support and
\(\mathcal F(R,d)=\{\varnothing\}\), so \(N_{\mathrm{out}}=1\).  The canonical filter removes the duplicate outputs but not the factorial traversal.  No bound on the number of distinct projective fragments represented at intermediate subsets follows from the frozen quotient handle; accordingly, the stronger output-sensitive and polynomial-delay alternatives are not asserted.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Generic identification is relative to each exact-support stratum and does not classify its real exceptional locus. Pairwise projective separation alone gives no uniform inference rate: coverage also uses correlation conditioning, the imposed bounded normalized-excitation design, reference-standardized beta-min, and wrong-order score separation. The cubic guarantee applies only to generic complete DAGs and uses exact-real rather than bit complexity. Unknown normalized schedules, cycles, latent confounding, varying mechanisms, singular correlations, zero innovation variances, unknown vertex correspondence, and discrete margins are outside the maintained model.

\section{Honest scope}

The supporting canonical-order lemma, universal generic frontier, tied-profile family, conditional-pair completeness and operation count, predictor perturbation bound, and finite-sample confidence theorem are proof obligations. The positivity, signed squared-cross-product, and deduplication steps specify exhaustive exact-real recovery and are not counted as theorem contributions; direct enumeration uses \(p!\) orders and \(O(Ep!p^3)\) arithmetic operations, excluding coefficient bit growth. Diagonal invariance of correlation and nonrecovery of covariance differences are elementary explanatory facts, not numbered claims. The normalized schedule \(d\), not any absolute multiplier array, is the maintained design input. Bounded schedule entries are an application-facing design regularity imposed for uniform constants, not a published standard condition.
