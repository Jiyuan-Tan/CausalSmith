For \(m\in\{1,2\}\), write \(Y_x^{(m)}\) and \(Z_x^{(m)}\) for the potential responses obtained by replacing the occurrence of \(X\) in the structural equations of \(M_m\) by \(x\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{Positive\ binary\ separation\ models\ and\ catalogue}\),
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc|cc}
 &Y_0^{(m)}&Z_0^{(m)}&Y_1^{(m)}&Z_1^{(m)}\\ \hline
m=1&U_A\mathbin\oplus E_Y&U_B\mathbin\oplus E_Z&U_B\mathbin\oplus E_Y&U_C\mathbin\oplus E_Z\\
m=2&U_A\mathbin\oplus E_Y&U_B\mathbin\oplus E_Z&U_C\mathbin\oplus E_Y&U_A\mathbin\oplus E_Z.
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
That definition also states that \(U_X,U_A,U_B,U_C\) are mutually independent fair bits, that \(E_Y,E_Z\) are mutually independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)\) bits, and that the response bits and noise bits are independent.  If \(B\) is a fair bit independent of a bit \(E\), then, for each \(b\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \Pr(B\mathbin\oplus E=b)
 =\sum_{e=0}^{1}\Pr(E=e)\Pr(B=b\mathbin\oplus e)
 =\frac12\sum_{e=0}^{1}\Pr(E=e)=\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]
For every \(m\in\{1,2\}\) and \(x\in\{0,1\}\), the two pre-noise response bits appearing in \((Y_x^{(m)},Z_x^{(m)})\) in (1) are distinct independent fair bits.  The associated noise bits are independent as well.  Hence (2) and independence give
\[
 \Pr_{M_m}\!\left(Y_x^{(m)}=y,Z_x^{(m)}=z\right)=\frac14
 \quad
 (m\in\{1,2\},\ x,y,z\in\{0,1\}).
\tag{3}
\]

We next compute every ordinary regime.  Fix
\(r=(r_X,r_Y,r_Z)\in\{\mathrm{natural},0,1\}^{\{X,Y,Z\}}\), and let \(P_{m,r}\) be the law in \(M_m\) after replacing the equation for each coordinate \(W\) with \(r_W\in\{0,1\}\) by the constant \(r_W\).  Since \(Y\) and \(Z\) are sinks, fixing either sink changes no other structural equation.  If \(X\) is fixed to \(x\), (3) says that the unfixed members of \(\{Y,Z\}\) are independent fair bits.  If \(X\) is natural, then \(X=U_X\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), equation (3) gives the same uniform law for the unfixed response coordinates, independent of \(x\); moreover \(U_X\) is fair and independent of all response and noise bits.  Marginalizing any sink fixed by the regime therefore shows that all natural coordinates are mutually independent fair bits and all fixed coordinates equal their assigned values.  Thus, for every \(a=(a_X,a_Y,a_Z)\in\{0,1\}^3\),
\[
 P_{m,r}(a)
 =\prod_{W:\,r_W\ne\mathrm{natural}}\mathbf 1\{a_W=r_W\}
  \prod_{W:\,r_W=\mathrm{natural}}\frac12,
 \qquad m\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{4}
\]
There are \(3^3=27\) choices of \(r\), so (4) proves equality of the two models under all ordinary observational and simultaneous node-interventional regimes in the claim.

Both systems are recursive in the order \(X\) before \(Y,Z\).  In each system the response functions for \(Y\) and \(Z\) take \(X\) as a directed input.  In \(M_1\), \(U_B\) is shared by those two response functions, while in \(M_2\), \(U_A\) is shared by them.  This shared exogenous input is represented by \(Y\leftrightarrow Z\).  No exogenous input to \(X\) is shared with either response.  Consequently both recursive SCMs are compatible with the ADMG having \(X\to Y\), \(X\to Z\), and \(Y\leftrightarrow Z\).

At \(\varepsilon=1/10\), every atom of the six exogenous bits has strictly positive probability.  Equation (4) also shows positivity of every assignment of the natural coordinates in each ordinary regime.  For the edge-specific acquisitions used below, a pair of response coordinates based on distinct fair bits has four cells of probability \(1/4\).  If the coordinates share a fair bit \(B\), either diagonal cell has probability
\[
 \frac12\bigl((1-\varepsilon)^2+\varepsilon^2\bigr)>0,
\]
and either off-diagonal cell has probability \(\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)>0\), because \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\).  In particular, every conditioning event and READ cell used below belongs to the exact support returned by \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{src}}\).

The counterfactual joints nevertheless differ.  By (1), \(Y_0^{(1)}\) depends only on \((U_A,E_Y)\), whereas \(Z_1^{(1)}\) depends only on \((U_C,E_Z)\).  These pairs are independent, and (2) makes each response fair.  Therefore
\[
 \Pr_{M_1}(Y_0=1,Z_1=1)=\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac14.
\tag{5}
\]
In \(M_2\), the two responses share \(U_A\).  Conditioning on this fair bit and using independence of \(E_Y,E_Z\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr_{M_2}(Y_0=1,Z_1=1)
 &=\Pr(U_A=1,E_Y=0,E_Z=0)
   +\Pr(U_A=0,E_Y=1,E_Z=1)\\
 &=\frac12(1-\varepsilon)^2+\frac12\varepsilon^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Substitution of \(\varepsilon=1/10\) yields
\[
 \frac12\left(\frac9{10}\right)^2+
 \frac12\left(\frac1{10}\right)^2
 =\frac{41}{100}.
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to verify the physical acquisitions.  Under \(S_{\mathrm{ind}}\), the edge-specific action \(\chi_{Y,0}\) replaces the value transmitted from \(X\) to the mechanism for \(Y\) by zero and leaves the transmission to \(Z\) natural.  The structural equations of the acquired regime are therefore
\[
 X^{\mathrm{ind}}=U_X,
 \qquad Y^{\mathrm{ind}}=Y_0,
 \qquad Z^{\mathrm{ind}}=Z_{U_X}.
\tag{8}
\]
The event \(X^{\mathrm{ind}}=1\) has probability \(1/2>0\).  Since \(U_X\) is independent of all variables entering \((Y_0,Z_1)\), (8) implies in either model that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Pr\!\left(Y^{\mathrm{ind}}=1,Z^{\mathrm{ind}}=1
       \mid X^{\mathrm{ind}}=1,S_{\mathrm{ind}}\right)\\
 &\qquad=\Pr(Y_0=1,Z_1=1\mid U_X=1)
 =\Pr(Y_0=1,Z_1=1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
By Definition \(\mathrm{Acquired\ regime}\), the oracle returns and caches the complete law and its exact support table.  The positive denominator just established and Definition \(\mathrm{Measured\ source\ slice}\) make the READ projection conditioned on \(X=1\) a defined slice.  Its \((Y,Z)=(1,1)\) cell is the left side of (9), so this cell equals the claimed counterfactual joint in each displayed SCM.

Under \(S_{\mathrm{dir}}\), the two edge-specific actions transmit zero to the \(Y\)-mechanism and one to the \(Z\)-mechanism.  Hence
\[
 Y^{\mathrm{dir}}=Y_0,
 \qquad Z^{\mathrm{dir}}=Z_1,
\tag{10}
\]
and the unconditioned READ law directly exposes the same joint.

Finally, each displayed protocol contains exactly the fresh-iid SELECT action \(s\); each edge or READ mechanism is used at most once; the prescribed order places \(s\) before every included CTF-RAND action and those actions before the included READs; and the catalogue has no conflicts.  Equations (8) and (10) are simultaneous evaluations of the indicated child-specific transmissions on a single selected unit, so they also witness the required CTF-REALIZE compatibility on \(G^{+}\).  Definition \(\mathrm{Legal\ protocol\ class}\) therefore gives
\[
 S_{\mathrm{ind}},S_{\mathrm{dir}}\in\Pi(G^{+},\mathcal A_{\mathrm{sep}}).
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(F_{\mathrm{ind}}\) be the acquired collection containing the positive slice in (9), and let \(F_{\mathrm{dir}}\) be the acquired collection containing the unconditioned source in (10).  Their distinct-protocol sets are \(\{S_{\mathrm{ind}}\}\) and \(\{S_{\mathrm{dir}}\}\), respectively.  On this separation instance the cost function in Definition \(\mathrm{Distinct\text{-}protocol\ cost}\) is \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}\).  The catalogue costs and that definition yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cost}(F_{\mathrm{ind}})
 &=c_{\mathrm{sep}}(\chi_{Y,0})=1,\\
 \operatorname{Cost}(F_{\mathrm{dir}})
 &=c_{\mathrm{sep}}(\chi_{Y,0})+c_{\mathrm{sep}}(\chi_{Z,1})=2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
because \(s,\rho_X,\rho_Y,\rho_Z\) cost zero.  Further slices of either acquired protocol add no cost by the same definition.  Equations (4)--(12) prove all assertions of the proposition.
