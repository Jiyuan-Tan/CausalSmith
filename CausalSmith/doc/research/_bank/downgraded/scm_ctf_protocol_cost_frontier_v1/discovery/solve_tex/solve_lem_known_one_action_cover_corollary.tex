Fix an instance \((G^{+},\mathcal A,Q)\in\mathfrak C_{1}\).  Because \(\mathcal A\) is finite, \(2^{\mathcal A}\), and hence the legal-protocol class \(\Pi\subseteq 2^{\mathcal A}\) of Definition \(\text{def:legal-protocols}\), is finite.  For \(S\in\Pi\), set
\[
 w(S):=\sum_{a\in S}c(a).
\]
Assumption \(\text{ass:binary-rational-cost}\) gives \(w(S)\in\mathbb Q_{\geq 0}\).  Assumption \(\text{ass:single-paid-action}\) says that there is exactly one \(a_S\in S\) with \(c(a_S)>0\), and therefore \(w(S)=c(a_S)\).  Distinct protocol masks are nevertheless distinct cover objects even if their unique paid actions coincide: Definition \(\text{def:certificate-cost}\) charges each distinct acquired protocol once.

For \(S\in\Pi\), use the factor set in the statement,
\[
 U(S)=\{j\in[k]: C_j\text{ has an individually valid uniform certificate record using }S\}.
\]
Spelling out ``individually valid'' using Definitions \(\text{def:acquisition-certificate}\) and \(\text{def:operational-acceptance}\), membership \(j\in U(S)\) supplies a fixed literal label \(\ell\), a labelled source factor \(T\) extracted from the returned law for \(S\), and a transcript \(\tau_j\) such that the singleton-ownership record
\[
 R_{S,j}=(b_S,b_{\ell},b_{\{j\}},b_T,\tau_j)
\]
passes all catalogue, legality, and typing checks and succeeds for every branch in \(\Omega_j\).  In particular, the oracle reply is not \(\bot\), the event \(H_{\mathrm{slice}}(S,\ell)\) is defined and has strictly positive support, every retained ancestor is measured in the source, and the same \((S,\ell,T,\tau_j)\) works throughout \(\Omega_j\).  Thus no zero-probability conditional law or assignment-dependent source switch is hidden in \(U(S)\).  Positivity is used only to make the displayed source slice well typed.  No rank condition, matrix inversion, overlap ratio, or limiting argument is used below.

We first map accepted certificates to covers.  Let \(E\) satisfy \(\operatorname{Acc}(Q,E;G)=1\), and write \(\mathcal P(E)=\operatorname{Prot}(E)\).  By Definitions \(\text{def:acquisition-certificate}\) and \(\text{def:operational-acceptance}\), after empty ownership masks are discarded, the ownership masks of the records of \(E\) partition \([k]\).  Hence every \(j\in[k]\) has a unique owning record, using some protocol \(S_j\in\mathcal P(E)\), whose fixed slice, source, and transcript are valid uniformly over \(\Omega_j\).  That record witnesses \(j\in U(S_j)\).  Consequently
\[
 [k]=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal P(E)}U(S).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal P(E)\) is a set cover.  The distinct-protocol convention in Definition \(\text{def:certificate-cost}\) gives, without double charging protocols used by several records,
\[
 \operatorname{Cost}(E)
 =\sum_{S\in\mathcal P(E)}\sum_{a\in S}c(a)
 =\sum_{S\in\mathcal P(E)}w(S).
\tag{2}
\]

Conversely, let \(\mathcal P\subseteq\Pi\) satisfy
\[
 \bigcup_{S\in\mathcal P}U(S)=[k].
\tag{3}
\]
For each \(j\in[k]\), choose \(s(j)\in\mathcal P\) with \(j\in U(s(j))\), together with one witnessing label, source, and uniform transcript.  Form the singleton-ownership record for each \(j\), and let
\[
 \mathcal P':=\{s(j):j\in[k]\}\subseteq\mathcal P.
\]
Every chosen record has a legal protocol, an in-catalogue consistent label, a defined positive-support slice, a measured-ancestry source factor, and a transcript valid for every branch of its owned factor.  Its ownership mask is \(\{j\}\), so the ownership masks partition \([k]\).  Assumption \(\text{ass:protocolwise-legality}\) ensures that acquiring the protocols in \(\mathcal P'\) on distinct fresh units preserves the legality of every constituent protocol.  Assumption \(\text{ass:factorwise-no-synergy}\) ensures that the individual certification of \(C_j\) by its selected protocol is unaffected by adjoining the other acquired protocols.  Therefore the complete exhaustive run required by Definition \(\text{def:operational-acceptance}\) encounters the displayed successful source for every target factor and does not return \(\operatorname{FAIL}\).  The resulting certificate \(E(\mathcal P)\) obeys
\[
 \operatorname{Acc}(Q,E(\mathcal P);G)=1.
\tag{4}
\]
By Definition \(\text{def:certificate-cost}\) and nonnegativity of \(w\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cost}(E(\mathcal P))
 =\sum_{S\in\mathcal P'}w(S)
 \leq \sum_{S\in\mathcal P}w(S).
\tag{5}
\]

Since \(\Pi\) is finite, a feasible cover family has a minimum-cost member.  Starting from one and deleting redundant protocols terminates at an inclusion-minimal minimum-cost cover \(\mathcal P^{\star}\); deletion cannot increase cost because all weights are nonnegative, and it cannot lower the cost below the minimum.  For each \(S\in\mathcal P^{\star}\), inclusion minimality supplies a private factor
\[
 j_S\in U(S)\setminus
 \bigcup_{S'\in\mathcal P^{\star}\setminus\{S\}}U(S').
\tag{6}
\]
In the construction above, assign \(j_S\) to \(S\) and assign every other factor to an arbitrary protocol of \(\mathcal P^{\star}\) covering it.  Equation (6) forces every \(S\in\mathcal P^{\star}\) to occur in the image of \(s\); hence \(\mathcal P'=\mathcal P^{\star}\), and equality holds in (5).  Equations (1)--(5) and Definition \(\text{def:optimal-cost}\) now yield
\[
 \operatorname{Opt}
 =\min_{\mathcal P\subseteq\Pi:\,\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal P}U(S)=[k]}
   \sum_{S\in\mathcal P}w(S).
\tag{7}
\]
At the infeasible boundary, the family on the right of (7) is empty.  Then (1) rules out an accepted certificate, so both sides are \(+\infty\) under the convention in Definition \(\text{def:optimal-cost}\).

Assume next that \(\operatorname{Opt}<+\infty\).  We apply the standard weighted greedy rule named in Lemma \(\text{lem:published-weighted-greedy-approximation}\), and derive its precise \(H_k\) guarantee for the present finite cover.  First select every zero-weight protocol and put
\[
 R_0=[k]\setminus\bigcup_{S:\,w(S)=0}U(S).
\]
This preprocessing costs zero.  While \(R_{i-1}\neq\varnothing\), choose an eligible \(S_i\in\Pi\) minimizing
\[
 \frac{w(S)}{|U(S)\cap R_{i-1}|}
\tag{8}
\]
among protocols satisfying \(U(S)\cap R_{i-1}\neq\varnothing\), and set
\[
 R_i=R_{i-1}\setminus U(S_i).
\]
Feasibility in (7) makes the eligible family nonempty whenever \(R_{i-1}\neq\varnothing\), and finiteness of \(\Pi\) makes the minimum attainable.  Every zero-weight protocol covers no element of \(R_0\); hence every protocol eligible in (8) has positive numerator, while eligibility gives a positive integer denominator.  Thus every division in (8) is defined.  At least one factor is removed per iteration, so the procedure stops after at most \(k\) iterations.

Let \(r_i=|U(S_i)\cap R_{i-1}|\), and charge each factor first covered at iteration \(i\) the amount
\[
 \rho_j=\frac{w(S_i)}{r_i}.
\]
The newly covered factor sets are disjoint and have cardinalities \(r_i\), so
\[
 \sum_i w(S_i)=\sum_{j\in R_0}\rho_j.
\tag{9}
\]
Fix \(S\in\mathcal P^{\star}\), where \(\mathcal P^{\star}\) is a minimum cover in (7), and order the \(m=|U(S)\cap R_0|\) relevant factors as \(j_1,\ldots,j_m\) by the iterations at which greedy first covers them, resolving simultaneous ties arbitrarily.  Immediately before \(j_q\) is covered, at least \(m-q+1\) members of \(U(S)\cap R_0\) remain.  Thus \(S\) is eligible in (8) with denominator at least \(m-q+1\).  Greedy minimality gives
\[
 \rho_{j_q}\leq \frac{w(S)}{m-q+1}.
\]
Summing, using \(m\leq k\), and writing \(H_k=\sum_{r=1}^{k}r^{-1}\), gives
\[
 \sum_{j\in U(S)\cap R_0}\rho_j
 \leq w(S)\sum_{q=1}^{m}\frac{1}{m-q+1}
 =w(S)H_m
 \leq w(S)H_k.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(w(S)=0\), then \(U(S)\cap R_0=\varnothing\), so (10) remains valid with both sides zero.  Since \(\mathcal P^{\star}\) covers \([k]\), every \(j\in R_0\) belongs to at least one of the sets summed on the left of (10).  Charges are nonnegative; therefore (7), (9), and (10) imply
\[
 \sum_i w(S_i)
 =\sum_{j\in R_0}\rho_j
 \leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal P^{\star}}
       \sum_{j\in U(S)\cap R_0}\rho_j
 \leq H_k\sum_{S\in\mathcal P^{\star}}w(S)
 =H_k\operatorname{Opt}.
\tag{11}
\]
The zero-weight protocols together with the \(S_i\) cover \([k]\).  Applying the cover-to-certificate construction of (3)--(5), and omitting selected zero-weight protocols to which no factor was assigned, produces an accepted legal certificate \(E_{\mathrm{gr}}\) with
\[
 \operatorname{Cost}(E_{\mathrm{gr}})
 \leq\sum_i w(S_i)
 \leq H_k\operatorname{Opt}.
\]

For an exact smallest nontrivial check, take \(k=2\) and three independently legal protocols, each with one paid action, whose factor sets are \(\{1,2\}\), \(\{1\}\), and \(\{2\}\), and whose weights are \(2,1,1\), respectively.  The cover optimum and every greedy outcome both have cost \(2\), in agreement with (7) and (11).  The no-synergy condition is load-bearing for the reduction: if \(U(S_1)=U(S_2)=\varnothing\) but the two-source collection jointly certifies the target factors, an accepted acquisition exists although the induced cover instance is infeasible.  Protocolwise legality is also load-bearing: if \(U(S_1)=\{1\}\) and \(U(S_2)=\{2\}\) but acquiring the pair is forbidden, the cover is feasible although the corresponding combined certificate is not.  These finite witnesses explain the stated restriction to \(\mathfrak C_1\).
