On the signed family the observed outcome is binary, so the clipping in the definition of \(V\) is inactive and \(V=2Y-1\).  On \(C_j\), the profile construction gives \(\zeta_h=1\) and \(\psi_j=1\).  Since \(\sum_{l=1}^k\psi_l^2=1\) there and every summand is nonnegative, \(\psi_l=0\) for \(l\ne j\).  Hence \(F_\lambda=\lambda_j\) on \(C_j\).  From the definitions of \(\pi_\lambda\) and \(m_{s,\lambda}\), for every \(x\in C_j\),
\[
 \mathbb E(U\mid X=x)=2\pi_\lambda(x)-1=2a\lambda_j,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(V\mid X=x)=2m_{s,\lambda}(x)-1=2sb\lambda_j. \tag{1}
\]
Here the second equality uses
\(\mathbb E(Y\mid X=x)=\pi_\lambda(x)\mu_{1,s,\lambda}(x)+(1-\pi_\lambda(x))\mu_{0,s,\lambda}(x)=m_{s,\lambda}(x)\), which follows by substituting the two arm-mean definitions.  Independence of distinct records, from the independent sampling assumption used by the signed-family experiment, and equation \(1\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(Z_{12}\mid X_1,X_2\in C_j)
 &=\frac12\left\{
       \mathbb E(U_1\mid X_1\in C_j)\mathbb E(V_2\mid X_2\in C_j)
      +\mathbb E(V_1\mid X_1\in C_j)\mathbb E(U_2\mid X_2\in C_j)
     \right\}\\
 &=\frac12\{4sab\lambda_j^2+4sab\lambda_j^2\}=4sab. \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Conditional on \(N_j=m\ge2\), the \(m\) records in \(C_j\) are exchangeable draws from the law conditional on that cell.  Every ordered summand in \(B_j\) therefore has conditional mean \(4sab\) by equation \(2\), whereas \(B_j=0\) for \(m<2\).  Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E(B_j\mid N_j)=4sab\mathbf 1\{N_j\ge2\}. \tag{3}
\]
Exact uniform design and the fact that \(C_j\) has volume \(v\) imply \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,v)\).  By the definition of \(p_2\), summing equation \(3\) over the \(k\) cells proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{s,\lambda}^{\otimes n}}S_{\mathrm{pair}}
 =4sab\sum_{j=1}^k\Pr(N_j\ge2)=4sabkp_2. \tag{4}
\]

We next prove a sensitivity bound sharper than the one claimed.  On every bounded-outcome dataset, \(U_i\in\{-1,1\}\) and the clipped mark satisfies \(V_i\in[-1,1]\).  Thus \(Z_{ir}=Z_{ri}\in[-1,1]\).  Write \(B^{(m)}\) for a cell block containing \(m\) records.  For \(m\ge3\), let \(B^{(m-1)}\) be the block after deleting the last record and put
\[
 \overline Z_m=\frac1{m-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}Z_{im}.
\]
Rewriting the ordered-pair average as the corresponding unordered-pair average yields the exact identity
\[
 B^{(m)}=\frac{m-2}{m}B^{(m-1)}+\frac2m\overline Z_m.
\]
Since both terms on the right before weighting lie in \([-1,1]\),
\[
 |B^{(m)}-B^{(m-1)}|
 =\frac2m|\overline Z_m-B^{(m-1)}|
 \le\frac4m\le\frac43. \tag{5}
\]
For \(m=2\), deletion changes the block from \(Z_{12}\) to zero, so the change is at most one; for \(m\le1\) it is zero.  Insertion is the reverse operation, and hence also changes one cell by at most \(4/3\).

If a replaced record remains in the same core cell and that cell contains \(m\ge2\) records, only its \(m-1\) unordered incident kernels change.  Each kernel changes by at most two, and therefore
\[
 |B^{(m)}(D_n)-B^{(m)}(D_n')|
 \le\frac{2(m-1)}{\binom m2}=\frac4m\le2. \tag{6}
\]
For \(m<2\) the change is zero.  If the record moves between two distinct cells, or between a core cell and its complement, equation \(5\) bounds the deletion and insertion contributions by \(4/3\) each.  Combining the two cases gives, for every \(D_n\sim_1D_n'\),
\[
 |S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n)-S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n')|
 \le\max\left\{2,\frac83\right\}=\frac83\le4. \tag{7}
\]
Taking the supremum in equation \(7\) proves the stated \(\Delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\le4\), while retaining the sharper \(8/3\) certificate used below.

Set
\[
 I_j=\mathbf 1\{N_j\ge2\},
 \qquad
 M=\sum_{j=1}^k I_j.
\]
We first establish the occupancy transform needed below without a cardinality union bound.  If \((N_1,\ldots,N_k)\) are the counts generated by \(n\) independent categorical draws into the disjoint cells, then for arbitrary nonnegative decreasing functions \(f_j\),
\[
 \mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^k f_j(N_j)
 \le\prod_{j=1}^k\mathbb E f_j(N_j). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, condition on \(N_1=m\).  The remaining counts are generated by \(n-m\) independent categorical draws with the renormalized cell probabilities.  Coupling these counts as initial segments of one infinite categorical sequence shows that the conditional expectation of \(\prod_{j=2}^k f_j(N_j)\) is nondecreasing in \(m\): increasing \(m\) removes draws, decreases every coupled remaining count, and cannot decrease a product of nonnegative decreasing functions.  Since \(f_1(m)\) is decreasing, the covariance identity
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(g(R),h(R))
 =\frac12\mathbb E[(g(R)-g(R'))(h(R)-h(R'))]
\]
for independent copies \(R,R'\) shows that this covariance is nonpositive when \(g\) is decreasing and \(h\) is increasing.  Factoring off \(f_1\) and iterating proves equation \(1\).

Apply equation \(1\) with \(f_j(m)=\exp\{-t\mathbf 1(m\ge2)\}\).  Each \(I_j\) has mean \(p_2\), so for every \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E e^{-tM}
 &\le\prod_{j=1}^k\mathbb E e^{-tI_j}
 =\{1-p_2(1-e^{-t})\}^k\\
 &\le\exp\{-kp_2(1-e^{-t})\}. \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular, because \(1-e^{-t}\ge t/2\) for \(0\le t\le1\),
\[
 \mathbb E e^{-tM}\le\exp(-kp_2t/2),
 \qquad 0\le t\le1. \tag{3}
\]

Conditional on all covariates, the active blocks \(B_j\) depend on disjoint sets of conditionally independent outcome and treatment marks, so they are independent.  The preceding lemma and the constancy of the profiles on the cores give
\[
 \mathbb E(sB_j\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)=4abI_j,
 \qquad -1\le sB_j\le1. \tag{4}
\]
For completeness, if a random variable \(H\) lies in \([-1,1]\), then
\(g(t)=\log\mathbb E\exp\{t(H-\mathbb EH)\}\) satisfies \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\) and \(g''(t)=\operatorname{Var}_t(H)\le1\), where \(\operatorname{Var}_t\) is variance under exponential tilting.  Two integrations give
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{t(H-\mathbb EH)\}\le e^{t^2/2}. \tag{5}
\]
Applying equation \(5\) conditionally to the \(M\) active variables in equation \(4\), followed by Chernoff's inequality with \(t=2ab\), yields
\[
 \Pr\{sS_{\mathrm{pair}}\le2abM\mid X_1,ldots,X_n\}
 \le\exp(-2Ma^2b^2). \tag{6}
\]

Let \(W\) have the Laplace law in the definition of the mechanism, with scale \(8/(3\epsilon)\), independently of the data.  Apart from a null tie event, \(\phi_{\mathrm{pair}}\ne s\) means \(s(S_{\mathrm{pair}}+W)<0\).  On the complement of the event in equation \(6\), this entails \(sW<-2abM\).  Symmetry and the exact Laplace tail therefore give
\[
 \Pr(sW<-2abM\mid X_1,ldots,X_n)
 =\frac12\exp(-3\epsilon abM/4). \tag{7}
\]
Because \(a,b\le1/8\) and \(0<\epsilon\le1\), both \(2a^2b^2\) and \(3\epsilon ab/4\) lie in \([0,1]\).  Averaging equations \(6\)--\(7\) and applying equation \(3\) at these two values gives numerical constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{s,\lambda}P_{s,\lambda}^{\otimes n}
 \{\phi_{\mathrm{pair}}\ne s\}
 \le C\exp(-ckp_2a^2b^2)
      +C\exp(-c\epsilon kp_2ab). \tag{8}
\]
The constants do not depend on \(n,\epsilon,k,a,b\).  The assumption \(nv\le c_0\) is not needed for equation \(8\) itself; it is used when the occupancy probability is converted to the radius formula in the following proposition.

It remains to verify privacy and risk.  Equation \(7\) of the preceding lemma gives replacement sensitivity at most \(8/3\).  Thus the density of \(S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n)+W\), at an arbitrary release value \(z\), satisfies for neighboring datasets
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\exp\{-3\epsilon|z-S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n)|/8\}}
      {\exp\{-3\epsilon|z-S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n')|/8\}}
 &\le
 \exp\left\{\frac{3\epsilon}{8}
 |S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n)-S_{\mathrm{pair}}(D_n')|\right\}\\
 &\le e^\epsilon. \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Integration of equation \(9\) over measurable output sets proves pure \(\epsilon\)-differential privacy of the noisy query.  The sign, fair tie-break, and scalar rescaling are postprocessings; hence \(\phi_{\mathrm{pair}}\) and \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{pair}}\) are also pure \(\epsilon\)-differentially private.

Finally, the established fuzzy-family membership result gives
\[
 \theta(P_{s,\lambda})=-sA_0,
 \qquad
 A_0=\frac{ab}{1/4-a^2},
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_{\mathrm{pair}}=-\phi_{\mathrm{pair}}A_0. \tag{10}
\]
The absolute loss in equation \(10\) is zero when \(\phi_{\mathrm{pair}}=s\) and is \(2A_0\) otherwise.  Multiplying equation \(8\) by \(2A_0\) proves the displayed risk bound.  The proof uses one common score and never selects among the \(2^k\) nuisance signs; consequently no \(\log k\) or \(\log|\{-1,1\}^k|\) term occurs.

For \(z_l=(j_l,u_l,w_l)\), expand the two factors defining \((Q_{s,\lambda}^{(k)})^{\otimes2}\) and average the independent Rademacher coordinates of \(\lambda\).  Terms containing one coordinate once, or two distinct coordinates once each, vanish.  The surviving terms give
\[
 \overline\Pi_{s,k}^{\,2}(z_1,z_2)
 =\frac1{16k^2}\left[1+\mathbf 1\{j_1=j_2\}
 \left\{4a^2u_1u_2+4b^2w_1w_2
 +4sab(u_1w_2+w_1u_2)\right\}\right]. \tag{1}
\]
Summing equation \(1\) over the marks shows that the two labels are independent and uniform; hence \(\Pr(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{coll}})=1/k\).  Subtracting equation \(1\) at \(s=+1\) and \(s=-1\) gives
\[
 \overline\Pi_{+,k}^{\,2}(z_1,z_2)
 -\overline\Pi_{-,k}^{\,2}(z_1,z_2)
 =\frac{ab}{k^2}T_{2,k}(z_1,z_2). \tag{2}
\]
For each fixed common label, \(|T_{2,k}|=1\) on eight of the sixteen sign quadruples and is zero on the other eight.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\overline\Pi_{+,k}^{\,2},\overline\Pi_{-,k}^{\,2})
 =\frac12\frac{ab}{k^2}\,k\,8=\frac{4ab}{k}. \tag{3}
\]
Equation \(2\) also proves directly that the sign of \(T_{2,k}\), with a fair tie-break where the two likelihoods agree, is a Bayes rule under equal prior weights on \(s\).  Hence
\[
 \Pr(\sigma_{2,k}\ne s)=\frac{1-4ab/k}{2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(s\sigma_{2,k})=\frac{4ab}{k}. \tag{4}
\]
Put \(\rho=(e^\epsilon-1)/(e^\epsilon+1)=\tanh(\epsilon/2)\).  By the definition of randomized response,
\(\mathbb E(RR_{2,k}\mid\sigma_{2,k})=\rho\sigma_{2,k}\).  Averaging equation \(4\) therefore gives
\[
 \Pr(RR_{2,k}\ne s)
 =\frac12\left\{1-\frac{4ab}{k}\tanh(\epsilon/2)\right\}. \tag{5}
\]
The true and reported target values are respectively
\(-sab/(1/4-a^2)\) and \(-RR_{2,k}ab/(1/4-a^2)\).  Their absolute difference is twice this positive amplitude exactly on the error event in equation \(5\), proving the asserted Bayes absolute risk.

For every input, including an input at which the preliminary sign is randomized, each output probability of randomized response lies between
\(1/(1+e^\epsilon)\) and \(e^\epsilon/(1+e^\epsilon)\).  Hence the likelihood ratio for either output is at most \(e^\epsilon\) between any two inputs, and in particular between inputs adjacent under \(\sim_{\mathrm q}\).  Equality is attained on an adjacent pair: for a fixed label \(j\), take
\[
 z_1=(j,1,1),
 \qquad z_2=(j,1,1),
 \qquad z_2'=(j,-1,-1).
\]
Then \(T_{2,k}(z_1,z_2)=1\) and \(T_{2,k}(z_1,z_2')=-1\).  For output \(+1\), the corresponding likelihood ratio is exactly \(e^\epsilon\).  Thus the maximum neighboring log-likelihood ratio is exactly \(\epsilon\).

Suppose \(n\ge2\). Under the two-scale geometric restriction \(q<\gamma\), let \(a\asymp\ell^\alpha\), \(b\asymp\ell^\beta\), \(ab\asymp\delta\), \(h\asymp\delta^{1/\gamma}\), and \(\ell\asymp\delta^{1/q}\). Whenever the sparse-occupancy condition \(nv\le c_0\) holds, equivalently up to fixed profile constants \(n\delta^{d/q}\le c_*\), one has \(kp_2\asymp n^2h^d\ell^d\). For every fixed \(C\) for which \(n\{C\max(r_{\mathrm{pair}},r_F)\}^{d/q}\le c_*\), the two error exponents in Theorem 2 are bounded below by fixed multiples of \(C^{D_F+1}\) and \(C^{D_F}\), respectively, when \(\delta\ge C\max\{r_{\mathrm{pair}},r_F\}\) while sparse occupancy remains valid. At \(\delta=Cr_F\), this compatibility is equivalent up to fixed constants to \(\epsilon\ge cC^{D_F}n^{-1+q/d+q/\gamma}\). At \(\delta=Cr_{\mathrm{pair}}\), it is asymptotically feasible for a fixed \(C\) only if \(d\ge2q+qd/\gamma\); strict inequality permits every fixed \(C\) eventually, whereas equality permits only constants \(C\) satisfying the fixed occupancy bound. Thus the signed-family theorem gives a log-free fixed-error pure-private separation at the coupled radius only in these compatible sparse regimes. Outside them, and in particular under dense occupancy, it does not validate the named \(r_F\) or \(r_{\mathrm{pair}}\) branch; a new dense-occupancy argument is required. %%% FIELD proof-pair-radius
Since exact uniform design gives \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,v)\),
\[
 \binom n2v^2(1-v)^{n-2}
 \le p_2
 \le\mathbb E\binom{N_j}{2}=\binom n2v^2. \tag{1}
\]
The sparse-occupancy hypothesis \(nv\le c_0<1\) implies
\((1-v)^{n-2}\ge(1-v)^n\ge1-nv\ge1-c_0\).  Also, \(n^2/2\le n(n-1)\le n^2\) for \(n\ge2\).  Thus equation \(1\) yields
\[
 p_2\asymp n^2v^2. \tag{2}
\]
The profile definition gives \(v\asymp\ell^d\) and \(k\asymp h^d/\ell^d\).  Therefore
\[
 kp_2\asymp
 \frac{h^d}{\ell^d}n^2\ell^{2d}
 =n^2h^d\ell^d. \tag{3}
\]
All comparison constants in equations \(2\)--\(3\) depend only on the fixed profile and occupancy constants.

Because \(q=\alpha+\beta\), the amplitude calibrations imply
\[
 ab\asymp\ell^{\alpha+\beta}=\ell^q\asymp\delta,
 \qquad
 \ell\asymp\delta^{1/q},
 \qquad
 h\asymp\delta^{1/\gamma}. \tag{4}
\]
Substituting equation \(4\) into equation \(3\) and using
\(D_F=1+d/\gamma+d/q\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 kp_2a^2b^2
 &\asymp n^2\delta^{2+d/\gamma+d/q}
 =n^2\delta^{D_F+1},\\
 \epsilon kp_2ab
 &\asymp n^2\epsilon\delta^{1+d/\gamma+d/q}
 =n^2\epsilon\delta^{D_F}. \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Hence, if \(\delta\ge C\max\{r_{\mathrm{pair}},r_F\}\), the first exponent in equation \(5\) is at least a fixed multiple of \(C^{D_F+1}\), because \(r_{\mathrm{pair}}^{D_F+1}=n^{-2}\).  Under \(n^{-1}\le\epsilon\le1\) and \(n\ge2\), \(n^2\epsilon>1\), so the cap in \(r_F\) is inactive and
\[
 r_F=(n^2\epsilon)^{-1/D_F}.
\]
The second exponent in equation \(5\) is consequently at least a fixed multiple of \(C^{D_F}\).  The error estimate of Theorem 2 then gives the claimed fixed-error separation whenever the same calibrated \(\delta\) remains in the sparse-occupancy regime.  The condition \(q<\gamma\) gives
\(\ell/h\asymp\delta^{1/q-1/\gamma}\to0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\), verifying the required two-scale ordering.

We now make the sparse-regime restriction explicit.  The frozen profile geometry states \(v\asymp\ell^d\), not \(v\asymp h^d\ell^d\).  Thus \(nv\le c_0\) is equivalent, up to fixed comparison constants, to
\[
 n\ell^d\asymp n\delta^{d/q}\le c_*. \tag{6}
\]
The product \(h^d\ell^d\) occurs instead in the signal count \(kp_2/n^2\) in equation \(3\).  At \(\delta=Cr_F\), equation \(6\) becomes
\[
 C^{d/q}n(n^2\epsilon)^{-d/(qD_F)}\le c_*.
\]
Raising both sides to the positive power \(qD_F/d\) and rearranging gives
\[
 \epsilon
 \ge c_*^{-qD_F/d}C^{D_F}n^{qD_F/d-2}
 =c_*^{-qD_F/d}C^{D_F}
 n^{-1+q/d+q/\gamma}, \tag{7}
\]
where the last equality follows from
\(qD_F/d=q/d+q/\gamma+1\).  This proves the stated privacy-budget compatibility, including its asymptotic direction.

At \(\delta=Cr_{\mathrm{pair}}=Cn^{-2/(D_F+1)}\), equation \(6\) is
\[
 C^{d/q}n^{,1-2d/\{q(D_F+1)\}}\le c_*. \tag{8}
\]
For a fixed positive \(C\), equation \(8\) can hold asymptotically only if
\[
 1-\frac{2d}{q(D_F+1)}\le0.
\]
Using \(D_F+1=2+d/\gamma+d/q\), this condition is equivalent, step by step, to
\[
 q(D_F+1)\le2d
 \iff 2q+\frac{qd}{\gamma}+d\le2d
 \iff d\ge2q+\frac{qd}{\gamma}. \tag{9}
\]
If the last inequality is strict, the power of \(n\) in equation \(8\) is negative, so every fixed \(C\) is eventually compatible.  At equality that power is zero and equation \(8\) requires the fixed restriction \(C^{d/q}\le c_*\); thus one cannot make \(C\) arbitrarily large at the boundary while retaining sparse occupancy.  If inequality \(9\) fails, the left side of equation \(8\) diverges for every fixed \(C>0\).  Equations \(6\)--\(9\) prove that neither named branch is certified by this theorem outside its compatible sparse regime and isolate dense occupancy as requiring a different argument.

Finally, for \(k=1\), the profile identities on \(C_1\) give \(F_{(\lambda_1)}=\lambda_1\).  Binary outcomes make the clipping inactive, so
\[
 \mathbb E(U_i\mid X_i\in C_1)=2a\lambda_1,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(V_i\mid X_i\in C_1)=2sb\lambda_1.
\]
Independence of the two records now yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[Z_{12}\mid X_1,X_2\in C_1\right]
 &=\frac12\{(2a\lambda_1)(2sb\lambda_1)
 +(2sb\lambda_1)(2a\lambda_1)\}\\
 &=4sab,
 \end{aligned}
\]
which proves the sanity reduction.
