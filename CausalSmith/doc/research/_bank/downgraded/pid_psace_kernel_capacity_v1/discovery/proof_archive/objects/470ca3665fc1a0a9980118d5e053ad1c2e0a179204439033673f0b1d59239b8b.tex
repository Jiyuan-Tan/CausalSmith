Write the ordered states as \(1=(0,0)\), \(2=(0,1)\), \(3=(1,0)\), and \(4=(1,1)\), and abbreviate \(\bar M=\mu^{\mathrm w}\), \(\bar K=K^{\mathrm w}\), and \(\epsilon=10^{-3}\).  Fix \((\mu^0,\nu)\in\mathcal W_{\epsilon}\).  The preceding lemma supplies strictly positive marginals, a strictly positive stochastic \(K^0=M^{-1}N\), and positive first stages.

At \(d=0\), every trial row equals its center row.  If \(C\) is the matrix of center rows, marginal reproduction becomes \(MC=N\).  Nonsingularity of \(M\) forces \(C=M^{-1}N=K^0\).  Hence the class has a unique effective transition matrix and
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)=\{\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\}.
\]

We next show that every positive radius adds genuine ambiguity.  Let
\(J^0(x,z)=\mu_1^0(x)K^0_{xz}\).  For a real \(t\), perturb the four cells by
\[
 J^t=J^0+t(e_{14}+e_{31}-e_{11}-e_{34}).
\]
This four-cycle preserves every row sum and every column sum.  Strict positivity of \(J^0\) gives a number \(t_0>0\) such that \(J^t\ge0\) whenever \(|t|<t_0\).  Set the trial-one rows to \(k^t_{1x}(z)=J^t(x,z)/\mu_1^0(x)\), keep every other trial row equal to \(K^0_x\), and use \(K^0_x\) as every center row.  The only nonzero rowwise distances are
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k^t_{11},K^0_1)=|t|/\mu_1^0(1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(k^t_{13},K^0_3)=|t|/\mu_1^0(3).
\]
Thus both signs of every sufficiently small nonzero \(t\) are feasible for any fixed \(d>0\).  Only cell \((1,4)\) among the four perturbed cells enters the prevented-recurrence numerator or denominator, and it enters both with coefficient one.  Consequently
\[
 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^t)=N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t,
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^t)=D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t.
\]
Moreover \(D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)>0\), because the strictly positive table \(J^0\) assigns positive mass to prevented-recurrence cells whose effect coefficient is less than one.  Hence the derivative of \((N_1^0+t)/(D_1^0+t)\) is \((D_1^0-N_1^0)/(D_1^0+t)^2>0\).  The two signs of \(t\) give distinct ratios around \(\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\), proving
\(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)\subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\) for every \(d>0\).

For every \(J\in\mathfrak J_1\), the same binary accounting identity as in \(\texttt{lem:first-stage-denominator}\) gives \(D(J)-R(J)=\Delta_1^S>13/100\), so every benchmark denominator is positive.  Every robust trial-one table is a member of \(\mathfrak J_1\), and therefore \(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\subseteq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}\).  To make this inclusion strict uniformly for \(d\le10^{-2}\), first control all robust ratios.  In the induced row-sum matrix norm,
\[
 \|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty=2,
 \qquad
 \|M-\bar M\|_\infty<4\epsilon=1/250.
\]
Put \(B=\bar M^{-1}(M-\bar M)\).  Then \(M=\bar M(I+B)\) and \(\|B\|_\infty<1/125\).  The finite identity
\((I+B)\sum_{j=0}^J(-B)^j=I+(-1)^JB^{J+1}\), followed by the norm limit as \(J\to\infty\), proves \((I+B)^{-1}=\sum_{j\ge0}(-B)^j\) and the geometric bound
\[
 \|M^{-1}\|_\infty
 \le \frac{\|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty}
 {1-\|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty\|M-\bar M\|_\infty}
 <\frac{2}{1-1/125}=\frac{125}{62}.
\]
For a feasible system with center matrix \(C\), define the zero-sum signed row
\[
 r_{g'}=\nu_{g'}-\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)C_x
 =\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)(k_{g'x}-C_x).
\]
For any zero-sum signed vector \(v\), define its total-variation seminorm by
\(\|v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\tfrac12\sum_z|v(z)|\); for differences of probability vectors this equals the declared total-variation distance.  The triangle inequality gives \(\|r_{g'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le d\).  Since \(N=MC+r\),
\(K^0=C+M^{-1}r\); the triangle inequality for the zero-sum total-variation seminorm yields, for every \(x\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(C_x,K^0_x)
 \le d\sum_{g'}|(M^{-1})_{xg'}|
 <\frac{125}{62}d.
\]
Thus
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k_{1x},K^0_x)<\frac{187}{62}d.
\]
If \(v\) is a zero-sum signed vector, then
\(|\sum_z f(z)v(z)|\le\operatorname{osc}(f)\|v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\); this follows by subtracting \(\min f\) and separately summing the positive and negative parts of \(v\).  The row functions defining both \(N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}\) and \(D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}\) have oscillation one and vanish for control rows \(3,4\).  Therefore, for \(d\le1/100\),
\[
 |N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)|,
 \ |D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)|
 <(\mu_1^0(1)+\mu_1^0(2))\frac{187}{62}d<\frac{25}{1000}.
\]
At the witness, the common-kernel numerator and denominator are \(27/100\) and \(72/100\).  The defining neighborhood inequalities give
\[
 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)>\frac{268}{1000},
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)<\frac{724}{1000}.
\]
For completeness, the errors behind these bounds are respectively less than
\[
 2(10^{-3})(45/100)+(701/1000+101/1000)10^{-3}=1702\cdot10^{-6}
\]
and
\[
 2(10^{-3})(9/10)+2(701/1000+101/1000)10^{-3}=3404\cdot10^{-6}.
\]
It follows that every robust ratio obeys
\[
 \frac{N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}{D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}
 >\frac{243}{749}>\frac3{10}.
\]

Finally construct an unrestricted trial-one table from the current, not the witness, marginals:
\[
 \widetilde J=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \nu_1(1)&\nu_1(2)&\nu_1(3)-\mu_1^0(2)&\nu_1(4)-\mu_1^0(3)-\mu_1^0(4)\\
 0&0&\mu_1^0(2)&0\\
 0&0&0&\mu_1^0(3)\\
 0&0&0&\mu_1^0(4)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The neighborhood implies \(|\nu_1(3)-455/1000|<285/100000\) and the same bound for \(\nu_1(4)\); meanwhile \(\mu_1^0(2)<101/1000\) and \(\mu_1^0(3)+\mu_1^0(4)<202/1000\).  Hence every displayed entry is nonnegative.  Direct summation verifies row marginal \(\mu_1^0\) and column marginal \(\nu_1\), so \(\widetilde J\in\mathfrak J_1\).  Its denominator and numerator are
\[
 \widetilde D=\Delta_1^S,
 \qquad
 \widetilde N=\nu_1(4)-(1-\mu_1^0(1)).
\]
The same neighborhood bounds give \(\widetilde N<159/1000\).  The treated surrogate probability differs from its witness value \(91/100\) by less than \(57/10000\), while the control surrogate probability is below \(202/1000\); hence \(\widetilde D>702/1000\).  Consequently
\[
 \frac{\widetilde N}{\widetilde D}<\frac{159}{702}<\frac14<\frac3{10}.
\]
This value belongs to \(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}\) but to no \(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\) with \(0<d\le10^{-2}\).  Both strict inclusions therefore hold uniformly over the stated observable neighborhood.
