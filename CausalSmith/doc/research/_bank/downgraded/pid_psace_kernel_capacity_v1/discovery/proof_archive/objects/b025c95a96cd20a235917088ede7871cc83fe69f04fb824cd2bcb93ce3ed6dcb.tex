Fix \(g\) and \(d\), and suppose \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing\).  We proceed in five steps.

First, the class is a compact convex polytope.  Indeed, every stochastic row lies in the compact simplex \(\Delta_4\); marginal reproduction is affine; and each constraint
\(\operatorname{TV}(k_{g'x},k_x)\le d\) is equivalent to the existence of nonnegative variables \(u_{g'xz}\) satisfying
\[
 u_{g'xz}\ge k_{g'x}(z)-k_x(z),\qquad
 u_{g'xz}\ge k_x(z)-k_{g'x}(z),\qquad
 \frac12\sum_z u_{g'xz}\le d.
\]
Projection of this bounded finite linear system gives exactly the class in \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\).  Convexity can also be checked directly from convexity of the \(\ell_1\) norm.

Second, this polytope has the asserted causal interpretation.  For any \(\mathbf K\) in the class, define
\[
 J_{g'}(x,z)=\mu_{g'}^0(x)k_{g'x}(z),\qquad g'\in\mathcal G.
\]
The stochastic-row and reproduction equations imply that \(J_{g'}\) is a joint law with marginals \(\mu_{g'}^0\) and \(\nu_{g'}\).  Under this law,
\[
 D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\Pr\{S(1)=1,S(0)=0\mid G=g\}
\]
and
\[
 N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\mathbb E[(Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1\{S(1)=1,S(0)=0\}\mid G=g].
\]
The preceding denominator lemma yields \(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)\ge\kappa>0\), so division is legitimate and
\[
 \frac{N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}{D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}
 =\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid S(1)=1,S(0)=0,G=g].
\]
Conversely, the conditional transition rows of every joint potential-state law satisfying the maintained shared-center restrictions give such a \(\mathbf K\); rows at a zero-mass control state may be chosen as the maintained rows without changing the joint law.  For attainability from a given \(\mathbf K\), choose any strictly positive law of \(G\), use \(J_{g'}\) as the conditional law of \((X(0),X(1))\) given \(G=g'\), draw \(A\) independently of the potential states given \(G\) with probability strictly between zero and one, and set \(X^{\mathrm{obs}}=X(A)\).  This constructed law satisfies randomization, consistency, and arm overlap and has the required observed arm marginals.  Thus the displayed ratios are values of the causal target rather than a definition of that target.  The observed-law bridge \(\texttt{lem:observed-arm-identification}\) shows that the two marginals entering this construction are identified from the randomized arms.

Third, the numerator and denominator are linear in the entries of \(\mathbf K\).  Their ratio is continuous on the compact class because its denominator is at least \(\kappa\).  It consequently attains a minimum and maximum, denoted by \(L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\) and \(U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\).  If \(\mathbf K_0,\mathbf K_1\) have ratios \(r_0,r_1\), then for \(0\le t\le1\), convexity puts \(\mathbf K_t=(1-t)\mathbf K_0+t\mathbf K_1\) in the same class and
\[
 \frac{N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_t)}{D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_t)}
 =\frac{(1-t)D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_0)r_0+tD_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_1)r_1}
 {(1-t)D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_0)+tD_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_1)}.
\]
This is a continuous weighted average of \(r_0\) and \(r_1\), with strictly positive weights away from the corresponding endpoint.  It takes every value between them.  Applying this to minimizers and maximizers proves
\[
 \Theta_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)=[L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d),U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)].
\]
The joint laws just constructed show attainability of every point, so the interval is sharp, not merely an outer bound.

Fourth, both endpoints are computable by one linear program.  After the absolute-value linearization above, stack the row variables into \(v\) and write the feasible polytope as
\[
 Av=b,\qquad Cv\le e,\qquad v\ge0.
\]
Write the numerator and denominator as \(p_g^{\top}v\) and \(q_g^{\top}v\).  The denominator lemma and the fact that it is a probability give
\(\kappa\le q_g^{\top}v\le1\).  The Charnes--Cooper substitution from \(\texttt{lem:charnes-cooper-linear-fractional}\), here verified directly, is
\[
 t=(q_g^{\top}v)^{-1},\qquad y=tv.
\]
It converts endpoint minimization into the linear program
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{minimize}&p_g^{\top}y\\
 \text{subject to}&Ay=bt,\quad Cy\le et,\quad y\ge0,\\
 &q_g^{\top}y=1,\quad 1\le t\le\kappa^{-1}.
 \end{array}
\]
Maximizing the same objective gives the upper endpoint.  The forward substitution preserves the objective because \(p_g^{\top}y=(p_g^{\top}v)/(q_g^{\top}v)\).  Conversely, every feasible \((y,t)\) has \(t>0\), and \(v=y/t\) is feasible for the original polytope and has the same objective.  Thus the transformation is bijective between feasible solutions.  Its feasible set is compact: \(t\) is bounded, transformed stochastic-row coordinates are at most \(\kappa^{-1}\), and transformed absolute-value auxiliaries are at most \(2\kappa^{-1}\).  Hence each linear program returns an optimizer, and dividing its \(y\)-rows by \(t\) returns an attaining common-center system \(\mathbf K\).

Finally, the program contains the rows for all \(m\) trials simultaneously and uses one common collection \((k_x)_x\).  Therefore neither endpoint optimizes trial \(g\) after discarding the other trials: both are sharp over the single coupled class stated in the theorem.
