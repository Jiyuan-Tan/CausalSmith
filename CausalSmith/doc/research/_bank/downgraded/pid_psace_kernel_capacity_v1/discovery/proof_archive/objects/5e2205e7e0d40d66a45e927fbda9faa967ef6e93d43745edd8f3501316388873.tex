We construct the exact completion and verify each advertised output.  Throughout this proof the empirical marginals and the numerical values of \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)\), \(\kappa\), and \(d\) are rational, as required by the theorem.

First encode membership in \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\).  The simplex equations, nonnegativity constraints, and first-stage inequalities are rational linear conditions.  An inequality \(\sum_i|v_i|\le e\) is encoded exactly by adjoining variables \(a_i\) and requiring \(a_i\ge v_i\), \(a_i\ge-v_i\), \(a_i\ge0\), and \(\sum_i a_i\le e\).  Indeed, the displayed conditions imply \(a_i\ge|v_i|\), while the converse follows by taking \(a_i=|v_i|\).  Thus every confidence-region constraint is a first-order formula over the ordered real field with rational coefficients.  The region is closed and is contained in the compact product \((\Delta_4)^{2m}\), hence is compact.

For a candidate \(p\), define the proof intermediates
\[
 D_g(\mathbf K;p)=\sum_{x:s_x=0,\,z:s_z=1}p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z)
\]
and
\[
 N_g(\mathbf K;p)=\sum_{x:s_x=0,\,z:s_z=1}(y_z-y_x)p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z).
\]
For fixed \(g\) and \(d\), consider the one-free-variable formula
\[
 Q_{g,d}(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\,\exists\mathbf K\;
 \bigl[p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa},\ 
 \mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_d(p_{\cdot0},p_{\cdot1}),\ 
 N_g(\mathbf K;p)-tD_g(\mathbf K;p)=0\bigr].
\]
Simplex membership and stochastic-row equations are linear.  Marginal reproduction contains the bilinear polynomials \(p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z)\).  Each rowwise total-variation inequality is encoded exactly by the preceding absolute-value construction.  Consequently \(Q_{g,d}\) is a finite rational polynomial formula; in particular, no McCormick relaxation is used as an equality surrogate.

For every feasible pair \((p,\mathbf K)\), also put
\[
 R_g(\mathbf K;p)=\sum_{x:s_x=1,\,z:s_z=0}p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z).
\]
Marginal reproduction and stochasticity give the accounting identity used in \(\texttt{lem:first-stage-denominator}\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_g(\mathbf K;p)-R_g(\mathbf K;p)
 &=\sum_{x,z}(s_z-s_x)p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z)\\
 &=\sum_z s_zp_{g1}(z)-\sum_xs_xp_{g0}(x)
 =\Delta_g^S(p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(R_g(\mathbf K;p)\ge0\), the defining first-stage condition for \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\) now gives
\[
 D_g(\mathbf K;p)\ge \Delta_g^S(p)\ge\kappa>0.
\]
Division is therefore legitimate, and \(Q_{g,d}(t)\) holds if and only if \(t=N_g(\mathbf K;p)/D_g(\mathbf K;p)\) for some compatible \((p,\mathbf K)\).  By \(\texttt{def:confidence-objects}\), its truth set is exactly the real-valued part of \(\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d)\); incompatible fibers contribute only \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\).

All coordinates of \(p\), the center rows, and the trial rows lie in finite products of simplices.  The equations and weak inequalities defining feasible \((p,\mathbf K)\) are continuous, so that feasible set is closed and bounded, hence compact.  The ratio is continuous there because its denominator is bounded below by \(\kappa\).  Its image, namely the truth set of \(Q_{g,d}\), is consequently compact.  Apply \(\texttt{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) to \(Q_{g,d}\).  The resulting sign-invariant cylindrical algebraic decomposition gives a finite union of points and intervals with real-algebraic finite boundaries.  Compactness forces every interval endpoint belonging to the boundary of this image to be included; after merging adjacent cells, the intervals are closed and the remaining zero-dimensional components are isolated points.  Exact lifting over an included boundary cell solves the original existential formula and returns an attaining algebraic witness \((p,\mathbf K)\).  Thus every reported PSACE boundary is attained.

The incompatibility marker is handled separately by the closed formula
\[
 B_d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\;\bigl[p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\ \wedge\ 
 \neg\exists\mathbf K\;\mathbf K\in
 \mathfrak K_d(p_{\cdot0},p_{\cdot1})\bigr].
\]
This is again a rational first-order formula.  By \(\texttt{def:confidence-objects}\), \(B_d\) is true exactly when \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\) occurs in at least one projected fiber, so quantifier elimination decides the marker exactly.

It remains to profile the extended-real sign radius.  Define
\[
 F_g(p,r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\mathbf K\;\bigl[
 \mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(p_{\cdot0},p_{\cdot1})\ \wedge\ 
 N_g(\mathbf K;p)\le0\bigr]
\]
and \(F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,r)=\bigvee_{g\in\mathcal G}F_g(p,r)\).  These too are rational polynomial formulas.  For fixed \(p\), the set of pairs \((r,\mathbf K)\) satisfying a particular \(F_g\), with \(0\le r\le1\), is closed in a compact product.  Its projection onto \(r\) is therefore compact.  A finite union over \(g\) is compact as well.  Hence, if \(F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,r)\) holds for some \(r\), the least such \(r\) is attained.  Since \(\mathcal G\) is finite, that least value is
\[
 \min_{g\in\mathcal G}\inf\{r\in[0,1]:F_g(p,r)\}
 =\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p)
\]
by \(\texttt{def:radius-objects}\).  It follows that
\[
\begin{split}
 R(r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists p;[\,&p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa},\ 
 0\le r\le1,\ F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,r),\\
 &\forall s\,((0\le s\wedge s<r)\Longrightarrow
 \neg F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,s))]
\end{split}
\]
defines exactly \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\cap[0,1]\).  Applying \(\texttt{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) to \(R\) and isolating its one-dimensional boundary roots returns a finite union of real-algebraic points and intervals.  This projected value set need not be closed, so the sign decomposition, rather than compactness, supplies the exact open or closed flag of each endpoint.

For any two probability rows, total variation is at most one.  Moreover \(\mathfrak K_r\) is nested in \(r\).  Therefore a finite sign-reversal radius exists for a given \(p\) if and only if \(F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,1)\) holds.  The formula
\[
 I_{\infty}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\;[p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\ \wedge\ 
 \neg F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,1)]
\]
is consequently true exactly when \(+\infty\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\), and its truth is decided by the same elimination procedure.

If the confidence region is empty, every existential projection above is empty and both marker formulas are false, as required.  All original inequalities are retained as weak inequalities.  By \(\texttt{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\), cylindrical algebraic decomposition terminates after finitely many sign-invariant cells and retains zero-sign cells.  Thus ties among active cuts, compatibility contacts, and sign-radius boundaries require no generic-position assumption.  A face-complete exact completion of \(\mathsf{ProfileTV}\) is now explicit: McCormick boxes from \(\texttt{def:profiling-handle}\) may perform any finite amount of certified preliminary pruning, but CAD is run on every surviving box, or directly on the unpartitioned root box.  The latter choice alone proves finite termination and exactness; lifted witnesses certify included boundaries.

Finally, \(\texttt{thm:simultaneous-region-coverage}\) already proves that projection of the maintained region has the asserted simultaneous finite-sample interpretation, including ties, incompatibility, and \(+\infty\).  The construction above computes those very projections exactly and does not alter their coverage event.  This completes every part of the claim.
