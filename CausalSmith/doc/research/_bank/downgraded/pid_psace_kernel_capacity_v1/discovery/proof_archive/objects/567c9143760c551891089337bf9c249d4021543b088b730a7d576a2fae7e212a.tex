Let \(p^0=(\mu_g^0,\nu_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\). By \(\texttt{lem:observed-arm-identification}\), these are the true observed-arm probability vectors. Conditional on the sample sizes, \(\texttt{ass:multinomial-sampling}\) and \(\texttt{lem:weissman-multinomial-l1}\) give, separately for each of the \(2m\) multinomial empirical laws, the required concentration inequality.

Put \(C_4=2^4-2\). If
\[
 \sqrt{2n_{ga}^{-1}\log\!\left(C_4\,2m/\alpha\right)}<2,
\]
then the definition of \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)\) gives
\[
 \varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)^2
 =\frac{2}{n_{ga}}\log\!\left(C_4\,2m/\alpha\right).
\]
Therefore the cited multinomial inequality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Pr\!\left\{\|\widehat p_{ga}-p^0_{ga}\|_1>
 \varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)\mid(n_{g'a'})_{g',a'}\right\}\\
 &\qquad\le C_4\exp\!\left\{-\frac{n_{ga}\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)^2}{2}\right\}\\
 &\qquad=C_4\exp\!\left\{-\log\!\left(C_4\,2m/\alpha\right)\right\}
 =\frac{\alpha}{2m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If the displayed square root is at least two, then \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)=2\). For any \(u,v\in\Delta_4\), nonnegativity and unit total mass give
\[
 \|u-v\|_1\le\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}\{u(z)+v(z)\}=2,
\]
so the strict failure event \(\{\|\widehat p_{ga}-p^0_{ga}\|_1>2\}\) is empty. Thus, in either branch, the conditional failure probability for each pair \((g,a)\) is at most \(\alpha/(2m)\). The finite union bound over the \(2m\) pairs, which does not require independence, gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
 \|\widehat p_{ga}-p^0_{ga}\|_1\le\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)
 \text{ for every }g\in\mathcal G,
 \ a\in\{0,1\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}(n_{g'a'})_{g',a'}
 \right\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Averaging this conditional inequality over the sample sizes preserves the lower bound \(1-\alpha\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:positive-first-stage}\) states that the true vectors obey every first-stage inequality in \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\), while they belong to \(\Delta_4\) by definition. Hence
\[
 \Pr\{p^0\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

On the single event \(\{p^0\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\}\), \(\texttt{lem:projection-set-inclusion}\) gives, simultaneously for every \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(d\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \widetilde\Theta_g(d;p^0)\subseteq\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d),
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p^0)
 \in\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}.
\]
If \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\neq\varnothing\), then \(\texttt{thm:sharp-psace-endpoints}\) gives
\[
 \widetilde\Theta_g(d;p^0)=\Theta_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d;p^0)
 =[L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d),U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)].
\]
Consequently the interval hull of the real-valued part of the projected fiber contains both attained sharp endpoints. If the true fixed-radius class is incompatible, the same inclusion instead carries \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\). By the definition of \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\), its extended-real inclusion carries \(+\infty\) without alteration whenever \(\texttt{thm:sharp-sign-radius}\) places the true radius there.

The argument uses only deterministic set inclusion, the two exhaustive clipping branches, and a finite union bound. It never selects a unique optimizing cut or differentiates an optimum. It therefore remains valid when cuts tie, at \(d=0\) and \(d=1\), at a compatibility boundary, under incompatibility, and in the extended-real no-reversal case.
