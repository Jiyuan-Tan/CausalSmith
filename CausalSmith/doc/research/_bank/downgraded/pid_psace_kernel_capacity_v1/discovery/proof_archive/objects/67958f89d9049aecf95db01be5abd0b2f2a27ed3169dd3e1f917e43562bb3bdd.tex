\emph{Technique: explicit absolute-value linearization, phase-one linear-program duality, and determinant bounds for rational certificates.}

Write \(c_{xz}=k_x(z)\) and \(r_{gxz}=k_{gx}(z)\).  Introduce one nonnegative variable \(u_{gxz}\) for each \((g,x,z)\).  The fixed-radius compatibility system from Definition \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class} and Assumption \texttt{ass:tv-drift} is equivalent to the following finite linear system, denoted \(\mathcal L_d\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &c_{xz}\ge0,\qquad r_{gxz}\ge0,\qquad u_{gxz}\ge0,\\
 &\sum_z c_{xz}=1,\qquad \sum_z r_{gxz}=1,\\
 &u_{gxz}\ge r_{gxz}-c_{xz},\qquad
   u_{gxz}\ge c_{xz}-r_{gxz},\\
 &\sum_z u_{gxz}\le2d,\\
 &\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)r_{gxz}=\nu_g(z),
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G,\ x,z\in\mathcal X.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
If rows in \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\) are given, setting
\(u_{gxz}=|r_{gxz}-c_{xz}|\) proves feasibility of \((4)\).  Conversely, the two majorization inequalities in \((4)\) give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(r_{gx},c_x)
 =\frac12\sum_z|r_{gxz}-c_{xz}|
 \le\frac12\sum_z u_{gxz}\le d,
\]
so a solution of \((4)\) supplies exactly the kernel system required by Definition \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}.

There are \(q^2+mq^2+mq^2=(2m+1)q^2\) variables in \((c,r,u)\).  There are \((m+1)q\) row-sum equalities, \(mq\) marginal equalities, \(2mq^2\) absolute-value majorizations, \(mq\) total-variation inequalities, and \((2m+1)q^2\) nonnegativity inequalities.  Counting an equality as two inequalities still gives \(O(mq^2)\) constraints and variables; here the declared state space has \(q=4\).

For an explicit separation oracle, retain all constraints of \((4)\) except its last line and solve the always-feasible phase-one program
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \gamma^*:=\min_{c,r,u,\gamma}\quad &\gamma\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 & (c,r,u)\text{ satisfies the first four lines of }(4),\\
 &-\gamma\le \sum_x\mu_g^0(x)r_{gxz}-\nu_g(z)\le\gamma
       \quad\text{for every }g,z,\\
 &0\le\gamma\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The upper bound on \(\gamma\) is harmless because both the generated and observed marginal coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), and uniform common and trial rows give a feasible point.  Compactness of the row polytope makes the minimum attainable.  Program \((5)\) has value zero if and only if \((4)\) is feasible.  At value zero its primal optimizer therefore returns \(\mathbf K\).

For completeness, dualize the two marginal-error inequalities in \((5)\).  Give the upper inequality multiplier \(\alpha_{gz}\ge0\), the lower inequality multiplier \(\beta_{gz}\ge0\), and put \(h_{gz}=\beta_{gz}-\alpha_{gz}\).  Minimization over \(\gamma\ge0\) requires
\(\sum_{g,z}(\alpha_{gz}+\beta_{gz})\le1\), which is equivalent, after the minimal positive--negative decomposition, to \(\|h\|_1\le1\).  Minimizing the remaining Lagrangian over the row polytope gives
\[
 \langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 -\max_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}
      \langle h,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 =\langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}-H_d^*(h),
\]
where the final equality is Definition \texttt{def:direct-support}.  Exact finite elimination of the primal variables in \((5)\) gives the dual identity
\[
 \gamma^*
 =\max_{\|h\|_1\le1}
   \left\{\langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}-H_d^*(h)\right\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
One may also verify exactness of \((6)\) directly: weak duality follows from
\(\langle h,\nu-w\rangle\le\|h\|_1\|\nu-w\|_\infty\), while eliminating the finitely many inequalities of the epigraph of \((5)\) produces a supporting retained inequality with equality at its attained optimum.  This is the same finite polyhedral separation calculation used in Theorem \texttt{thm:direct-shared-center-cut}, now applied to the epigraph.

If \(\gamma^*>0\), a dual maximizer in \((6)\) is a violated cut.  To put it in the normalization used by Definition \texttt{def:direct-support}, fix any \(z_0\in\mathcal X\), set
\(\bar h_g(z)=h_g(z)-h_g(z_0)\), and then set
\(\widetilde h=\bar h/\|\bar h\|_1\).  The denominator is positive: a trialwise constant \(h\) has zero gap because every \(w_g\) and \(\nu_g\) has mass one.  Moreover,
\[
 \langle \bar h,\nu\rangle-H_d^*(\bar h)
 =\langle h,\nu\rangle-H_d^*(h)>0,
\]
and positive homogeneity of the support function preserves strict violation after normalization.  Thus the phase-one primal--dual pair returns either a compatible \(\mathbf K\) or a normalized violated support cut.  By Theorem \texttt{thm:direct-shared-center-cut}, the alternatives are exhaustive.

Finally suppose that \(d\), all \(\mu_g^0(x)\), and all \(\nu_g(z)\) are rational.  Then every coefficient in \((4)\) and \((5)\) is rational.  Clear denominators row by row.  Let \(B\) be the largest resulting coefficient bit length and let \(s\) be the number of variables plus constraints, so \(s=O(mq^2)\) and \(B\) is polynomial in the input encoding length.  A feasible rational polyhedron has a basic feasible point: choose a feasible point with minimal support after conversion to equality form with nonnegative slack variables; if its supported columns were linearly dependent, a nonzero dependence would permit a small movement in one of its two signs that preserves feasibility and removes a supported coordinate, contradicting minimality.  Its nonzero coordinates therefore solve a nonsingular square subsystem.

For any such \(r\)-by-\(r\) integer subsystem, the Leibniz expansion has \(r!\) signed products, each of magnitude at most \(2^{rB}\), and therefore
\[
 |\det A_R|\le r!\,2^{rB}.
\]
The cofactor identity \(A_R^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(A_R)/\det(A_R)\), obtained by expanding the matrix product along rows, writes every solution coordinate as a ratio of two such integer determinants.  The same expansion, with one column replaced by the integer right-hand side, bounds every numerator up to one additional input coefficient.  Hence every coordinate has
\(O(r(B+\log r))\) bits, and the complete basic solution has polynomial encoding length.  Applied to \((4)\), this yields a rational feasible kernel system whenever compatibility holds.

If compatibility fails, write \((4)\) in inequality form \(Az\le b\), including opposite inequalities for equalities.  The dual multipliers obtained from \((5)\), or equivalently the nonnegative multipliers \(y\) satisfying
\[
 y^{\mathsf T}A=0,\qquad y^{\mathsf T}b<0,
\]
form a failure certificate: multiplying and summing the asserted primal inequalities would give
\(0=y^{\mathsf T}Az\le y^{\mathsf T}b<0\).  Conversely, the violated support cut just constructed supplies such multipliers by the exact elimination leading to \((6)\).  Scale so that \(y^{\mathsf T}b=-1\), and choose a minimal-support feasible \(y\).  The preceding linear-independence and determinant argument applies again, proving that \(y\), the associated rational normalized cut, and its strictly positive rational violation all have polynomial encoding length.  This proves both the stated LP size and the constructive rational success-or-failure certificate claim.
