Write the ordered states as \(1=(0,0)\), \(2=(0,1)\), \(3=(1,0)\), and \(4=(1,1)\), and put \(\bar M=\mu^{\mathrm w}\), \(\bar K=K^{\mathrm w}\), and \(\epsilon=\epsilon_{\mathrm w}=10^{-3}\). Fix \((\mu^0,\nu)\in\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\). By \(\texttt{lem:observable-neighborhood-nondegenerate}\), every entry of \(\mu_g^0\), \(\nu_g\), and \(K^0=M^{-1}N\) is strictly positive, \(K^0\) is row-stochastic, and \(\Delta_g^S>13/100\) for every \(g\). By \(\texttt{lem:first-stage-denominator}\), every denominator considered below is at least \(\Delta_g^S\), hence is strictly positive.

First connect the causal target to the displayed optimization functional. Given a feasible kernel system \(\mathbf K\), define
\[
 J_g^{\mathbf K}(x,z)=\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}(z).
\]
The simplex constraints and marginal-reproduction equalities in \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\) give respectively
\[
 \sum_zJ_g^{\mathbf K}(x,z)=\mu_g^0(x),
 \qquad
 \sum_xJ_g^{\mathbf K}(x,z)=\nu_g(z).
\]
Thus \(J_g^{\mathbf K}\) is a joint law of \((X(0),X(1))\) with the observed arm marginals identified by \(\texttt{lem:observed-arm-identification}\). Directly from the definitions of \(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}\) and \(N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}\),
\[
 D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\Pr_{J_g^{\mathbf K}}\{S(0)=0,S(1)=1\},
\]
and
\[
 N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\mathbb E_{J_g^{\mathbf K}}
 \bigl[(Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1\{S(0)=0,S(1)=1\}\bigr].
\]
Because the denominator is positive, division yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{J_g^{\mathbf K}}
 [Y(1)-Y(0)\mid S(1)=1,S(0)=0]
 =\frac{N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}{D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}.
\]
Conversely, because every \(\mu_g^0(x)>0\), any joint law satisfying the stated rowwise shared-center restrictions determines the conditional rows \(k_{gx}(z)=J_g(x,z)/\mu_g^0(x)\). Hence the range in \(\texttt{def:psace-objects}\) is the causal identified set rather than merely an observed-law regression functional.

We next use full-rank linear algebra for the zero-drift claim. At \(d=0\), the total-variation constraints force \(k_{gx}=k_x\) for all \(g,x\). If \(C\) denotes the matrix with rows \(C_x=k_x\), marginal reproduction becomes
\[
 MC=N.
\]
The nonsingularity of \(M\), which is part of \(\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\), therefore forces
\[
 C=M^{-1}N=K^0.
\]
Conversely, taking every center and trial row equal to the positive stochastic row \(K_x^0\) satisfies marginal reproduction because \(MK^0=N\). Thus the zero-drift class is nonempty and has a unique effective transition matrix. Substitution into \(\texttt{def:psace-objects}\) and \(\texttt{def:wu-mao-functional}\) gives
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)=\{\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\}.
\]
This is the transition restriction corresponding to exact transportability on the present \(m=4\), full-rank, positive-first-stage subclass.

We now prove that every positive radius creates genuine ambiguity, using a marginal-preserving transportation cycle. Define
\[
 J^0(x,z)=\mu_1^0(x)K^0_{xz}
\]
and, for \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 J^t=J^0+t(e_{14}+e_{31}-e_{11}-e_{34}),
\]
where \(e_{xz}\) has one in cell \((x,z)\) and zero elsewhere. The signed perturbation has every row sum and every column sum equal to zero. Since \(J^0\) is strictly positive, there is \(t_0>0\) such that \(J^t\ge0\) whenever \(|t|<t_0\). For such \(t\), set
\[
 k^t_{1x}(z)=\frac{J^t(x,z)}{\mu_1^0(x)},
\]
retain \(k^t_{gx}=K_x^0\) for \(g\ne1\), and take every center row to be \(K_x^0\). Row and column preservation shows that all marginal-reproduction equalities remain valid. The only nonzero rowwise distances are
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k^t_{1,1},K_1^0)=\frac{|t|}{\mu_1^0(1)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(k^t_{1,3},K_3^0)=\frac{|t|}{\mu_1^0(3)}.
\]
For any fixed \(d>0\), both signs of every nonzero \(t\) satisfying
\[
 |t|<\min\{t_0,d\mu_1^0(1),d\mu_1^0(3)\}
\]
therefore produce systems in \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\). Among the four perturbed cells, only \((1,4)\) enters either prevented-recurrence functional, and it has coefficient one in each. Consequently
\[
 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^t)=N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t,
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^t)=D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t,
\]
where \(\mathbf K^0\) is the system whose center and trial rows all equal \(K^0\). Moreover,
\[
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)>0,
\]
because the strictly positive table \(J^0\) assigns positive mass to the prevented-recurrence cell \((1,3)\), on which the denominator coefficient is one and the numerator coefficient is zero. Hence, whenever the preceding feasibility condition holds,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}
 \frac{N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t}
      {D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t}
 =\frac{D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)}
 {(D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t)^2}>0.
\]
The zero-drift system itself is feasible at every larger radius, and the two signs of \(t\) yield distinct ratios on its two sides. Therefore
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)\subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)
 \qquad(d>0).
\]

It remains to prove that cross-trial coupling still excludes a value allowed by the unrestricted single-trial transportation polytope. Every feasible robust system induces \(J_1^{\mathbf K}\in\mathfrak J_1\), so the preceding causal-functional identity and \(\texttt{def:single-trial-benchmark}\) imply
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\subseteq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}.
\]
We make this inclusion strict uniformly for \(d\le1/100\).

Use the induced maximum-row-sum matrix norm. Since
\[
 \bar M=\frac35 I_4+\frac1{10}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \bar M^{-1}=\frac53I_4-\frac16\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},
\]
direct multiplication verifies the inverse and gives
\[
 \|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty=\frac32+3\left(\frac16\right)=2.
\]
The neighborhood restriction gives
\[
 \|M-\bar M\|_\infty<4\epsilon=\frac1{250}.
\]
Put \(B=\bar M^{-1}(M-\bar M)\). Then \(M=\bar M(I+B)\) and
\[
 \|B\|_\infty<\frac1{125}.
\]
For every integer \(J\ge0\), finite multiplication gives
\[
 (I+B)\sum_{j=0}^{J}(-B)^j=I+(-1)^JB^{J+1}.
\]
The remainder norm is at most \(\|B\|_\infty^{J+1}\), which tends to zero. Thus the series converges to \((I+B)^{-1}\), and the geometric-sum bound yields
\[
 \|M^{-1}\|_\infty
 \le \frac{\|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty}{1-\|B\|_\infty}
 <\frac{2}{1-1/125}=\frac{125}{62}.
\]

For a feasible system with center matrix \(C\), define the signed residual row
\[
 r_{g'}=\nu_{g'}-\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)C_x
 =\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)(k_{g'x}-C_x).
\]
Each \(r_{g'}\) has coordinate sum zero. For any zero-sum signed vector \(v\), write
\[
 \|v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\frac12\sum_z|v(z)|.
\]
The triangle inequality, the nonnegative weights summing to one, and the rowwise constraint in \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\) give
\[
 \|r_{g'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)\operatorname{TV}(k_{g'x},C_x)
 \le d.
\]
In matrix form \(N=MC+r\), so
\[
 K^0=C+M^{-1}r.
\]
Every row of \(M^{-1}r\) also has coordinate sum zero. Therefore, for every \(x\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(C_x,K_x^0)
 \le\sum_{g'}|(M^{-1})_{xg'}|\,\|r_{g'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 <\frac{125}{62}d.
\]
Another triangle inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k_{1x},K_x^0)<\frac{187}{62}d.
\]

For a zero-sum signed vector \(v\) and a real function \(f\) on \(\mathcal X\), subtracting \(\min_z f(z)\) and separating the positive and negative coordinates of \(v\) proves the elementary oscillation inequality
\[
 \left|\sum_zf(z)v(z)\right|
 \le\bigl(\max_zf(z)-\min_zf(z)\bigr)\|v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The row functions defining \(N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}\) and \(D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}\) have oscillation one for \(x=1,2\) and vanish for \(x=3,4\). Also the neighborhood gives \(\mu_1^0(1)+\mu_1^0(2)<802/1000\). Hence, for \(d\le1/100\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 |N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)|
 &<\frac{802}{1000}\frac{187}{62}\frac1{100}<\frac{25}{1000},\\
 |D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)|
 &<\frac{802}{1000}\frac{187}{62}\frac1{100}<\frac{25}{1000}.
 \end{split}
\]

Let \(\bar{\mathbf K}\) denote the system whose center and trial rows all equal \(\bar K\), evaluated with control marginals \(\bar M\). At the rational anchor, direct substitution gives
\[
 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\bar{\mathbf K})=\frac{27}{100},
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\bar{\mathbf K})=\frac{72}{100}.
\]
For the current law, the entrywise neighborhood bounds imply
\[
 \left|N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-\frac{27}{100}\right|
 <2\epsilon\frac{45}{100}
   +\left(\frac{701}{1000}+\frac{101}{1000}\right)\epsilon
 =\frac{1702}{10^6},
\]
and
\[
 \left|D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-\frac{72}{100}\right|
 <2\epsilon\frac9{10}
   +2\left(\frac{701}{1000}+\frac{101}{1000}\right)\epsilon
 =\frac{3404}{10^6}.
\]
Thus
\[
 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)>\frac{268}{1000},
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)<\frac{724}{1000}.
\]
Combining these inequalities with the \(25/1000\) perturbation bounds and the already-proved positivity of every denominator yields, for every robust feasible system and every \(d\le1/100\),
\[
 \frac{N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}{D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}
 >\frac{243}{749}>\frac3{10}.
\]

Finally, construct a feasible unrestricted coupling from the current marginals, not from the anchor marginals:
\[
 \widetilde J=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \nu_1(1)&\nu_1(2)&\nu_1(3)-\mu_1^0(2)&\nu_1(4)-\mu_1^0(3)-\mu_1^0(4)\\
 0&0&\mu_1^0(2)&0\\
 0&0&0&\mu_1^0(3)\\
 0&0&0&\mu_1^0(4)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(z=3,4\), the identity \(\nu_1(z)=\sum_x\mu_1^0(x)K^0_{xz}\) and the two entrywise neighborhood restrictions imply
\[
 \left|\nu_1(z)-\nu_1^{\mathrm w}(z)\right|
 \le\epsilon\sum_x\bar K_{xz}+\epsilon\sum_x\mu_1^0(x)
 =\frac{185}{100}\epsilon+\epsilon
 =\frac{285}{100000}.
\]
At the anchor, \(\nu_1^{\mathrm w}(3)=\nu_1^{\mathrm w}(4)=455/1000\). Since \(\mu_1^0(2)<101/1000\) and \(\mu_1^0(3)+\mu_1^0(4)<202/1000\), all entries displayed in \(\widetilde J\) are nonnegative. Direct summation gives row marginal \(\mu_1^0\) and column marginal \(\nu_1\), so
\[
 \widetilde J\in\mathfrak J_1.
\]
Its prevented-recurrence denominator and numerator are respectively
\[
 \widetilde D=\nu_1(3)+\nu_1(4)-\mu_1^0(3)-\mu_1^0(4)=\Delta_1^S
\]
and
\[
 \widetilde N=\nu_1(4)-\mu_1^0(2)-\mu_1^0(3)-\mu_1^0(4)
 =\nu_1(4)-(1-\mu_1^0(1)).
\]
The same bounds, together with the anchor identity \(455/1000-(1-7/10)=155/1000\), give
\[
 0<\frac{151}{1000}<\widetilde N<\frac{159}{1000}.
\]
Moreover, the treated surrogate probability differs from its anchor value \(91/100\) by less than \(57/10000\), whereas the control surrogate probability is less than \(202/1000\). Consequently
\[
 \widetilde D>\frac{702}{1000}>0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\widetilde N}{\widetilde D}
 <\frac{159}{702}<\frac14<\frac3{10}.
\]
This ratio belongs to \(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}\) but, by the uniform lower bound, belongs to no \(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\) with \(0<d\le10^{-2}\). Therefore
\[
 \{\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\}=\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)
 \subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)
 \subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}
 \qquad(0<d\le\bar d=10^{-2}),
\]
for every \((\mu^0,\nu)\in\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\), which proves both strict comparisons uniformly over the stated observable neighborhood.
