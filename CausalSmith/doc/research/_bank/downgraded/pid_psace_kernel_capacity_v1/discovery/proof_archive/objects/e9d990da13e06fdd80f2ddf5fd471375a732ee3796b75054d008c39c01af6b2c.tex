\emph{Technique: finite-dimensional polytope support and an explicit Euclidean-projection separator.}

For arrays \(u=(u_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\) and \(v=(v_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\), use the product-space inner product
\[
 \langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 :=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}u_g(z)v_g(z).
\]
Define the row polytope
\[
 \mathcal Q_d:=\left\{((c_x)_x,(r_{gx})_{g,x}):
 c_x,r_{gx}\in\Delta_4,\quad
 \frac12\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\le d
 \ \text{for all }g,x\right\}
\]
and its generated-marginal image
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mu,d}:=\left\{w\in(\Delta_4)^m:
 w_g(z)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\mu_g^0(x)r_{gx}(z)
 \ \text{for some }((c_x)_x,(r_{gx})_{g,x})\in\mathcal Q_d\right\}.
\]
These are proof intermediates.  The simplex and rowwise total-variation restrictions in Definitions \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class} and \texttt{def:direct-support} make every displayed expression well typed, using the declared identity
\(\operatorname{TV}(r_{gx},c_x)=\frac12\sum_z|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\).

The set \(\mathcal Q_d\) is nonempty: take every \(c_x\) and \(r_{gx}\) equal to the uniform vector in \(\Delta_4\).  It is compact because it is a closed subset of a finite product of compact simplices, and it is convex because every simplex constraint is convex and the \(\ell_1\)-norm is convex.  It is in fact a polytope.  Indeed, for each \((g,x,z)\), introduce \(t_{gxz}\ge0\) and replace the absolute-value condition by
\[
 t_{gxz}\ge r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z),\qquad
 t_{gxz}\ge c_x(z)-r_{gx}(z),\qquad
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal X}t_{gxz}\le2d.
\]
Existence of such \(t\) is equivalent to the total-variation inequality: necessity follows by choosing
\(t_{gxz}=|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\), and sufficiency follows from
\(|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\le t_{gxz}\) and summation.  Thus \(\mathcal Q_d\) is the projection of a bounded set described by finitely many linear equalities and inequalities.  The map from its rows to \(w\) is linear, so \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope.

By Definition \texttt{def:direct-support} and the definition of \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\),
\[
 H_d^*(h)
 =\max_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}
   \langle h,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The maximum is attained because \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\) is compact.  By the marginal equalities in Definition \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}, with \(c_x=k_x\) and \(r_{gx}=k_{gx}\),
\[
 \mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nu:=(\nu_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is a relationship proved from the two definitions, not a definition of compatibility.

Suppose first that compatibility holds.  Equations \((1)\) and \((2)\) give, for every \(h\),
\[
 \sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\langle h_g,\nu_g\rangle
 =\langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 \le \max_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}\langle h,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 =H_d^*(h).
\]

Conversely, suppose that \(\nu\notin\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Compactness gives a minimizer
\[
 w^*\in\arg\min_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}\|\nu-w\|_2,
 \qquad h^*:=\nu-w^*\ne0.
\]
For arbitrary \(w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\) and \(t\in(0,1]\), convexity puts
\(w^*+t(w-w^*)\) in \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Minimality of \(w^*\) therefore implies
\[
 0\le
 \frac{\|\nu-w^*-t(w-w^*)\|_2^2-\|\nu-w^*\|_2^2}{t}
 =-2\langle h^*,w-w^*\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
   +t\|w-w^*\|_2^2.
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow0\) yields
\(\langle h^*,w\rangle\le\langle h^*,w^*\rangle\) for every \(w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Hence, by \((1)\),
\[
 H_d^*(h^*)\le\langle h^*,w^*\rangle
 <\langle h^*,w^*\rangle+\|h^*\|_2^2
 =\langle h^*,\nu\rangle.
\]
Thus a failed compatibility system violates a displayed cut, proving the reverse implication.

It remains to prove finite normalized sufficiency.  Apply finite elimination to the linearized system above, retaining the marginal coordinates \(w\).  Each elimination step combines finitely many linear inequalities, so after finitely many row variables have been removed one obtains a finite exact half-space description of \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Tighten each retained nonredundant inequality to its support value.  It then has the form
\[
 \langle a,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}\le
 \max_{v\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 =H_d^*(a).
 \tag{3}
\]
Any affine-hull equation produced by elimination is retained as the two opposite supporting half-spaces.  In particular, rank deficiency at \(d=0\) loses no equality restriction.  Trialwise constant arrays \(a_g(z)=c_g\) impose only
\(\sum_z w_g(z)=1\), already satisfied by every observed \(\nu_g\in\Delta_4\).  For a general normal \(a\), subtracting a trialwise constant array changes both sides of \((3)\) by the same number \(\sum_g c_g\).  Positive rescaling also preserves \((3)\).  Consequently every nonconstant retained normal has a representative of \(\ell_1\)-norm one in the quotient by trialwise constants.  By Definition \texttt{def:direct-support}, the distinct resulting rays are precisely the finite set \(\mathcal R_{\mu,d}^*\).

The exact half-space description now gives
\[
 \nu\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \langle r,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}\le H_d^*(r)
 \quad\text{for every }r\in\mathcal R_{\mu,d}^*.
\]
If a supporting direction has several maximizing rows, its exposed face merely contains several points; equation \((3)\) and its support value are unchanged.  Thus no uniqueness, strict positivity, rank, or generic-position condition is used.  The uniform-row witness and compactness argument also cover both boundary radii \(d=0\) and \(d=1\), completing the proof.
