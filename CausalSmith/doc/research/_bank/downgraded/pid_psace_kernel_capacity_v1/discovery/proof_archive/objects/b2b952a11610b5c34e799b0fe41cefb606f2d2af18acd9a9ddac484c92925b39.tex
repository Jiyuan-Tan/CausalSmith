Let \(\bar M\) and \(\bar K\) denote the two matrices displayed in \(\texttt{def:rational-witness}\).  Then
\[
 \bar M=\frac35 I_4+\frac1{10}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top},
 \qquad
 \bar M^{-1}=\frac53 I_4-\frac16\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}.
\]
Thus \(\bar M\) is nonsingular.  The matrix \(\bar K\) is row-stochastic and has minimum entry \(1/40>0\).  By construction \(\nu^{\mathrm w}=\bar M\bar K\), whence the witness has \(K^0=\bar M^{-1}\nu^{\mathrm w}=\bar K\).  Both strict inequalities defining \(\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\) hold with left side zero, so \((\mu^{\mathrm w},\nu^{\mathrm w})\) belongs to that class.

Now take an arbitrary class member and put \(K^0=M^{-1}N\).  Each entry of \(M\) is larger than \(1/10-10^{-3}=99/1000\), so every control marginal is strictly positive.  Since \(M\mathbf 1=N\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\), nonsingularity gives
\[
 K^0\mathbf 1=M^{-1}N\mathbf 1=M^{-1}\mathbf 1=mathbf 1.
\]
Every entry of \(K^0\) is larger than \(1/40-10^{-3}=3/125>0\), so \(K^0\) is a strictly positive row-stochastic matrix.  The identity \(N=MK^0\), together with strict positivity of \(M\) and \(K^0\), makes every treatment marginal strictly positive as well.

For a row \(x\), set \(t_x=\sum_{z:s_z=1}K^0_{xz}\).  At the witness, \((t_x)_x=(9/10,9/10,19/20,19/20)\).  Hence the witness first stages are \(71/100,71/100,14/100,14/100\).  For a class member, \(|t_x-\bar t_x|<2\epsilon_{\mathrm w}\).  Since every row of \(M\) sums to one and \(|\bar t_x-\mathbf 1\{s_x=1\}|\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\Delta_g^S-\bar\Delta_g^S|
 &\le \sum_x|M_{gx}-\bar M_{gx}|\,
   |\bar t_x-\mathbf 1\{s_x=1\}|
   +\sum_xM_{gx}|t_x-\bar t_x|\\
 &<4\epsilon_{\mathrm w}+2\epsilon_{\mathrm w}=6/1000.
\end{aligned}
\]
The smallest witness value is \(14/100\), so every class member has \(\Delta_g^S>134/1000>13/100\).

It remains to verify nondegeneracy of the observable class.  Matrix inversion is continuous at the nonsingular \(\bar M\), and multiplication is continuous.  Therefore the map \((M,N)\mapsto M^{-1}N\) is continuous on some relative neighborhood of \((\bar M,\bar M\bar K)\).  Nonsingularity, strict positivity of all eight probability vectors, and both displayed strict inequalities in \(\texttt{def:observable-neighborhood}\) are open conditions relative to the product of the eight simplices.  Their intersection consequently contains a relative open neighborhood of the witness in the product of the eight simplex interiors.
