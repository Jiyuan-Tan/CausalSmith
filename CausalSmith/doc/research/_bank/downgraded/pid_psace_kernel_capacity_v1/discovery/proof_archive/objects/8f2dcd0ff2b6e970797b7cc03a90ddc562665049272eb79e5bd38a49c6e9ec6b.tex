Fix the observable arrays \(\mu^0=(\mu_g^0)_{g\in\mathcal G}\) and \(\nu=(\nu_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\), where every \(\mu_g^0\) and \(\nu_g\) belongs to \(\Delta_4\).  Thus each vector has four nonnegative coordinates summing to one.  Only this simplex property and the finiteness of \(\mathcal G\) and \(\mathcal X\) are used below; in particular, the argument uses no cell-positivity, overlap, rank, or uniqueness condition.

Define the joint feasibility set
\[
 \mathcal F
 =\bigl\{(r,\mathbf K):r\in[0,1],\ \mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\bigr\}.
\]
It is a subset of the finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^{1+16+16m}\): there are sixteen coordinates among the four center rows \((k_x)_x\), and \(16m\) coordinates among the trial rows \((k_{gx})_{g,x}\).  By the construction in Definition \(\mathrm{def:robust\mbox{-}kernel\mbox{-}class}\), all row variables lie in the closed and bounded simplex \(\Delta_4\).  The marginal restrictions
\[
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}(z)=\nu_g(z),
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G,\ z\in\mathcal X,
\]
are finite systems of linear equalities.  Moreover, by the definition of total variation,
\[
 (u,v,r)\longmapsto \operatorname{TV}(u,v)-r
 =\frac12\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}|u(z)-v(z)|-r
\]
is continuous, so every restriction \(\operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le r\) is closed.  Consequently \(\mathcal F\) is a closed subset of the compact finite product
\[
 [0,1]\times(\Delta_4)^{\mathcal X}
 \times(\Delta_4)^{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X},
\]
and hence \(\mathcal F\) is compact.

The set \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty.  Indeed, take any \(e\in\Delta_4\), put \(k_x=e\) for every \(x\), and put \(k_{gx}=\nu_g\) for every \((g,x)\).  For probability vectors \(u,v\in\Delta_4\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(u,v)
 =\frac12\sum_z|u(z)-v(z)|
 \le \frac12\sum_z\{u(z)+v(z)\}=1.
\]
Thus the rowwise restrictions hold at \(r=1\).  Also, for each \(g\),
\[
 \sum_x\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}
 =\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)\nu_g
 =\left(\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)\right)\nu_g
 =\nu_g.
\]
This verifies all constraints defining \(\mathfrak K_1(\mu^0,\nu)\).

Let
\[
 \mathcal S=\{r\in[0,1]:\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
It is the continuous projection of the compact set \(\mathcal F\) onto its first coordinate, so it is compact; the preceding construction shows \(1\in\mathcal S\).  Therefore \(\mathcal S\) has an attained minimum.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:radius\mbox{-}objects}\), that minimum is \(\rho^*\), and because \(\mathcal S\subseteq[0,1]\), one has \(\rho^*\in[0,1]\).

The family is monotone in its radius.  If \(r\in\mathcal S\), \(r'\in[0,1]\), and \(r'\ge r\), any \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\) obeys
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le r\le r'
\]
for every \((g,x)\), while all of its other defining constraints are unchanged.  Hence \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_{r'}(\mu^0,\nu)\), so \(r'\in\mathcal S\).  Attainment supplies some \(\mathbf K^*\in\mathfrak K_{\rho^*}(\mu^0,\nu)\).  It follows from monotonicity that every \(d\in[0,1]\) with \(d\ge\rho^*\) belongs to \(\mathcal S\).  Conversely, if \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing\), then \(d\in\mathcal S\), and the minimality of \(\rho^*\) gives \(d\ge\rho^*\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\ge\rho^*,
 \qquad d\in[0,1].
\]

It remains to identify the zero-drift boundary.  If \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_0(\mu^0,\nu)\), the zero-radius total-variation constraint in Definition \(\mathrm{def:robust\mbox{-}kernel\mbox{-}class}\) and nonnegativity of the absolute-value summands give, for every \((g,x)\),
\[
 0\le\frac12\sum_z|k_{gx}(z)-k_x(z)|
 =\operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le0.
\]
The sum is zero only if each summand is zero, so \(k_{gx}(z)=k_x(z)\) for every \(z\), equivalently \(k_{gx}=k_x\).  Conversely, equality of these rows makes every total-variation constraint hold with radius zero.  Substitution into the marginal equations yields exactly
\[
 \nu_g=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\mu_g^0(x)k_x,
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G.
\]
More explicitly, let
\[
 \mathcal C=(\Delta_4)^{\mathcal X}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \widetilde T_{\mu^0}:(\mathbb R^4)^{\mathcal X}
 \longrightarrow(\mathbb R^4)^{\mathcal G},
 \qquad
 \widetilde T_{\mu^0}((v_x)_x)
 =\left(\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)v_x\right)_{g\in\mathcal G}.
\]
The set \(\mathcal C\) is the common row-stochastic kernel polytope and \(\widetilde T_{\mu^0}\) is a linear map on the ambient vector space.  The preceding equivalence proves
\[
 \mathfrak K_0(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nu\in \widetilde T_{\mu^0}(\mathcal C),
\]
and the center-row components of \(\mathfrak K_0(\mu^0,\nu)\) form the fiber \(\widetilde T_{\mu^0}^{-1}(\{\nu\})\cap\mathcal C\).  This is precisely the ordinary linear image characterization.  No rank hypothesis is required: rank can change the dimension of the image or fiber but not either displayed equivalence.  Likewise, no unique-facet condition enters any equality, compactness argument, or boundary argument above.
