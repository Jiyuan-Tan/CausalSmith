\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}psace\_\allowbreak{}kernel\_\allowbreak{}capacity}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The model relaxes Wu and Mao's transition restriction by coupling randomized ACCT trials through one shared stochastic center and primitive trial-specific stochastic rows within rowwise TV distance \(d\), with exact marginal reproduction. The radius \(d\) is a prespecified sensitivity parameter for latent cross-world rows, not a feature identified by randomized-arm marginals. The headline results give attained coupled PSACE intervals, an explicit nondegenerate observable class on which every \(0<d\le10^{-2}\) strictly enlarges the Wu--Mao point while remaining strictly sharper than separate-trial bounds, and a global sign radius characterized by an independent primal--dual cut system with rational certificates and a \(+\infty\) branch. Direct polyhedral support supplies compatibility, and an observable first stage bounds every denominator. One maintained multinomial region gives simultaneous coverage with incompatible candidate marginals retained. Exact finite profiling exists by cylindrical algebraic decomposition for rational numerical inputs; practically tractable complexity, implementation, and the numerical ACCT reanalysis remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Wu and Mao point-identify four-state joint potential-outcome laws and PSACEs under exact transition transportability and full column rank, but do not characterize partial identification under a positive rowwise-TV relaxation. Jiang, Ding, and Geng identify PSACEs under a different exact cross-trial homogeneity restriction and give separate-trial bounds without monotonicity; Yuan, Feller, and Miratrix use a site-level zero-correlation restriction; Wu and Antonelli use continuous-intermediate outcome models with sign or dominant-observed-effect restrictions. Miratrix and coauthors and Long and Hudgens tighten principal-effect bounds using predictive baseline covariates under principal-stratum restrictions including monotonicity, while Trangucci, Chen, and Zelner obtain multicenter point identification from proxies, homogeneity, misclassification, and rank. The surrogate-resilience and historical-prediction comparators target different objects. None gives an attained positive-TV PSACE frontier together with a finite dual distance-to-sign-reversal certificate.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is a sensitivity path of coupled multi-trial PSACE intervals and distances to reversal, protected by an observable first stage and accompanied by compatibility certificates. The scientifically admissible upper range for \(d\) must be elicited rather than inferred from the trial marginals.

\paragraph{Fill.} Randomization and consistency identify the kernel-class marginals from observed-arm laws, while conditional multinomial sampling supplies concentration. The direct rowwise support LP characterizes compatibility; linear-fractional programs attain endpoints over the full prespecified \(d\)-path; an explicit open observable neighborhood gives strict intermediate identification for every \(0<d\le10^{-2}\); and a canonical primal--dual sign program returns an adversarial kernel or a finite rational certificate. One maintained multinomial region gives simultaneous coverage by inclusion, and \(\rho^*\) remains only the smallest data-compatible radius. For rational numerical inputs, cylindrical algebraic decomposition exactly profiles every bilinear PSACE and sign-radius projection, including active-cut ties and extended-real markers; practically tractable complexity, implementation, and the numerical ACCT reanalysis remain open.

\section{Related work}

Imbens and Rubin (2015, Chapter 1) supply the standard SUTVA consistency relation that, together with randomization, identifies potential-state marginals from observed arm laws; Weissman and coauthors (2003) are used only for multinomial \(L_1\) concentration. Wu and Mao (2025, arXiv v1, Section 5.1, Assumption 5 and Corollary 2) point-identify the four-state joint potential-outcome law and every PSACE under randomized assignment, their transition restriction, and full column rank of the control-marginal matrix. On this note's \(m=4\), full-rank, positive-first-stage subclass, \(d=0\) specializes to Wu--Mao Assumption 5's transition restriction; this is not a claim that the two papers' entire maintained classes are literally identical. The positive-radius neighborhood theorem strictly enlarges the resulting point for every \(0<d\le10^{-2}\) while remaining strictly inside the separate-trial interval. Jiang, Ding, and Geng (2016) provide multi-trial identification under a different cross-trial homogeneity restriction and Bayesian sensitivity analysis for deviations from that homogeneity. In the corroborating arXiv v1, Proposition 2 is the necessary condition \(N_R\ge3\) for local identification without monotonicity; the separate-trial without-monotonicity PSACE bounds are instead in Appendix A.1, Proposition A.4. The positive-radius neighborhood theorem strictly tightens those separate-trial bounds without asserting an unverified journal or online-supplement theorem number. Yuan, Feller, and Miratrix (2018, Section 4.1, Assumption 4.1, and Proposition 4.2) identify multi-site principal causal effects under the strong condition that site-specific stratum prevalence is uncorrelated with site-specific stratum effects; they neither posit a shared cross-world transition kernel nor a TV sensitivity set. Wu and Antonelli (2024, arXiv v1, Sections 2.2.2, 4, and 5) partially identify principal effects using parametric continuous-intermediate and outcome models together with same-sign or dominant-observed-effect restrictions; their target and restrictions differ from the binary multi-trial shared-kernel model. Miratrix, Furey, Feller, Grindal, and Page (2018; arXiv:1701.03139v2) construct principal-effect bounds under their principal-stratum restrictions, including monotonicity in the motivating setup, and tighten them by stratifying and averaging over predictive baseline covariates in a multisite Early College High School application. They do not pool sites through one shared cross-world transition kernel, vary a rowwise-TV radius, characterize cross-site kernel compatibility, or produce a minimum sign-reversal radius. Long and Hudgens (2013, Sections 4--6) give single-trial monotonicity-based principal-effect bounds, categorical-baseline-covariate tightening, and necessary and sufficient conditions for strict narrowing. They neither leverage cross-trial or cross-site variation nor constrain trial-specific cross-world rows by distance to a shared kernel; their bounds therefore inform the separate-trial benchmark for the strict-improvement theorem rather than the generic LP machinery. Trangucci, Chen, and Zelner (2024, Assumptions 2.4--2.6 and Theorem 3.3) exploit multicenter variation, informative categorical baseline proxies, site relevance, covariate and causal homogeneity, misclassification structure, and matrix or Kruskal-rank conditions to nonparametrically point-identify post-infection principal effects. Their genuinely close proxy and tensor-decomposition model neither contains nor subsumes sharp binary PSACE intervals under prespecified shared-row TV drift, compatibility radii, or TV distances to sign reversal. Baker, Sargent, Buyse, and Burzykowski (2012, Sections 2--4) fit historical-trial prediction models, including a principal-stratification option, and add leave-one-trial-out extrapolation error to predict the aggregate endpoint effect in a target trial observing only the surrogate. Their adjacent multi-trial prediction target does not identify or sharply bound within-principal-stratum effects through a common kernel and does not deliver a deterministic TV sensitivity or sign-radius path. Beresteanu, Molchanov, and Molinari (2011, Theorem 2.1) inform the support formulation, but the support here remains the direct rowwise-TV LP. Galichon and Henry (2011, Theorems 1--4) inform finite certificates without representing this kernel. Charnes and Cooper (1962) inform endpoint computation after the observable first-stage identity establishes positivity. Elliott, Conlon, Li, Kaciroti, and Taylor (2015, Sections 2--4) use a trial-level causal-association random-effects meta-analytic model to compute probabilities of opposite-sign surrogate and endpoint effects and a beneficial-surrogate-effect threshold. They do not target individual principal-stratum effects, a latent cross-world kernel identified set, or a deterministic minimum rowwise-TV perturbation. Hsiao, Tian, and Parast (2025) study resilience under controlled conditional-mean drift in a single prior-study setting. Hsiao and Parast (2026, Sections 2.1--2.2 and Theorem 1) directly extend that object to \(K\) completed surrogate-plus-outcome studies and infer a functional-class resilience probability for a new surrogate-only study under deviations from completed-study conditional-mean functions; this object is not a trial-specific PSACE bound or a shared latent transition-kernel TV radius. Chen, Harhay, and Li (2026) inform sensitivity motivation through principal-ignorability deviation bounds, a different deviation class and estimand. Kaido, Molinari, and Stoye (2019) and Cho and Russell (2024) inform projection inference, and McCormick (1976) supplies preliminary bilinear relaxations. Collins (1975) supplies effective cylindrical algebraic decomposition: it yields exact finite, tie-safe profiling for rational numerical inputs here, while practically tractable complexity, implementation, and the numerical ACCT reanalysis remain open.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:randomization]\label{ass:randomization}
\(A\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}(X(0),X(1))\mid G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (within-trial randomization, \cite{JiangDingGeng2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(X^{\mathrm{obs}}=X(0)\) almost surely on \(\{A=0\}\), and \(X^{\mathrm{obs}}=X(1)\) almost surely on \(\{A=1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (SUTVA consistency, \cite{ImbensRubin2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:arm-overlap]\label{ass:arm-overlap}
\(0<\Pr(A=a\mid G=g)<1\) for every \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (randomized-arm positivity, \cite{JiangDingGeng2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-first-stage]\label{ass:positive-first-stage}
\(\Delta_g^S\ge\kappa\) for every \(g\in\mathcal G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform positive surrogate first stage, \cite{JiangDingGeng2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:tv-drift]\label{ass:tv-drift}
\(\operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le d\) for every \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A deterministic total-variation radius measures the largest fraction of a four-state cross-world transition row that may be reassigned across trial populations. In commensurate trials using a common intervention, common endpoint definitions, and baseline-restricted populations, \(d\) is a prespecified sensitivity parameter whose full path can be reported. Randomized-arm marginals do not identify or bound this latent rowwise distance. Protocol or covariate similarity may motivate elicitation, but a proxy-to-TV bound is valid only under a separate explicit transport or validation assumption.
\end{assumption}

\(m=4\).

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:observed-arm-identification]\label{lem:observed-arm-identification}
Within every trial, randomization and consistency imply \(\pi_{g0}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\mu_g^0\) and \(\pi_{g1}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\nu_g\) for every \(g\in\mathcal G\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:arm-overlap}\) makes conditioning on \(G=g,A=a\) well defined.  For every \(b\in\mathcal X\), consistency gives
\[
 \Pr\{X^{\mathrm{obs}}=b\mid G=g,A=a\}
 =\Pr\{X(a)=b\mid G=g,A=a\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:randomization}\), the right side is
\(\Pr\{X(a)=b\mid G=g\}\).  Taking \(a=0\) and using the definition of \(\mu_g^0\) proves \(\pi_{g0}^{\mathrm{obs}}(b)=\mu_g^0(b)\); taking \(a=1\) and using the definition of \(\nu_g\) proves \(\pi_{g1}^{\mathrm{obs}}(b)=\nu_g(b)\).  Since this holds in every coordinate \(b\), the two asserted vector equalities follow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This bridge identifies the potential-state marginals entering the kernel class from the observed randomized-arm laws. \textit{Gap.} The bridge is standard SUTVA identification, but stating it separately prevents a concentration inequality from carrying causal identification content. \textit{Consumer.} The endpoint and coverage theorems use the identified arm laws as the control and treatment marginals.

\begin{lemma}[lem:first-stage-denominator]\label{lem:first-stage-denominator}
For every \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\), marginal reproduction gives \(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-R_g^{\mathrm{rev}}(\mathbf K)=\Delta_g^S\), so \(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)\ge\Delta_g^S\ge\kappa\) for every \(g\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(g\) and \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\), and put
\(J_g(x,z)=\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}(z)\).  By \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\), \(J_g\ge0\), its row marginal is \(\mu_g^0\), and its column marginal is \(\nu_g\).  For binary \(s_x,s_z\), the pointwise identity
\[
 \mathbf 1\{s_x=0,s_z=1\}-\mathbf 1\{s_x=1,s_z=0\}
 =s_z-s_x
\]
holds in all four cases.  Multiplication by \(J_g(x,z)\) and summation therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-R_g^{\mathrm{rev}}(\mathbf K)
 &=\sum_{x,z}(s_z-s_x)J_g(x,z)\\
 &=\sum_{z:s_z=1}\nu_g(z)-\sum_{x:s_x=1}\mu_g^0(x)
 =\Delta_g^S.
\end{aligned}
\]
Nonnegativity of \(J_g\) gives \(R_g^{\mathrm{rev}}(\mathbf K)\ge0\).  Hence
\(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)\ge\Delta_g^S\), and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:positive-first-stage}\) supplies \(\Delta_g^S\ge\kappa>0\).  This proves the identity and the uniform denominator bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The observable first stage, rather than latent entrywise positivity, totalizes every PSACE ratio. \textit{Gap.} JiangDingGeng2016 uses principal-stratum accounting, but the identity is required here uniformly over a shared-kernel sensitivity polytope. \textit{Consumer.} Every endpoint, sign-radius, and confidence-projection result uses this denominator bound.

\begin{theorem}[thm:direct-shared-center-cut]\label{thm:direct-shared-center-cut}
For every \(d\in[0,1]\), \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\neq\varnothing\) if and only if \[\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\langle h_g,\nu_g\rangle\le H_d^*(h)\quad\text{for every }h\in(\mathbb R^4)^m.\] The right side is the direct rowwise polyhedral support in \(\mathrm{def:direct-support}\), and the finite normalized set \(\mathcal R_{\mu,d}^*\) suffices, including facets exposed at tied optima.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Technique: finite-dimensional polytope support and an explicit Euclidean-projection separator.}

For arrays \(u=(u_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\) and \(v=(v_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\), use the product-space inner product
\[
 \langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 :=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}u_g(z)v_g(z).
\]
Define the row polytope
\[
 \mathcal Q_d:=\left\{((c_x)_x,(r_{gx})_{g,x}):
 c_x,r_{gx}\in\Delta_4,\quad
 \frac12\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\le d
 \ \text{for all }g,x\right\}
\]
and its generated-marginal image
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mu,d}:=\left\{w\in(\Delta_4)^m:
 w_g(z)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\mu_g^0(x)r_{gx}(z)
 \ \text{for some }((c_x)_x,(r_{gx})_{g,x})\in\mathcal Q_d\right\}.
\]
These are proof intermediates.  The simplex and rowwise total-variation restrictions in Definitions \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class} and \texttt{def:direct-support} make every displayed expression well typed, using the declared identity
\(\operatorname{TV}(r_{gx},c_x)=\frac12\sum_z|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\).

The set \(\mathcal Q_d\) is nonempty: take every \(c_x\) and \(r_{gx}\) equal to the uniform vector in \(\Delta_4\).  It is compact because it is a closed subset of a finite product of compact simplices, and it is convex because every simplex constraint is convex and the \(\ell_1\)-norm is convex.  It is in fact a polytope.  Indeed, for each \((g,x,z)\), introduce \(t_{gxz}\ge0\) and replace the absolute-value condition by
\[
 t_{gxz}\ge r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z),\qquad
 t_{gxz}\ge c_x(z)-r_{gx}(z),\qquad
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal X}t_{gxz}\le2d.
\]
Existence of such \(t\) is equivalent to the total-variation inequality: necessity follows by choosing
\(t_{gxz}=|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\), and sufficiency follows from
\(|r_{gx}(z)-c_x(z)|\le t_{gxz}\) and summation.  Thus \(\mathcal Q_d\) is the projection of a bounded set described by finitely many linear equalities and inequalities.  The map from its rows to \(w\) is linear, so \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope.

By Definition \texttt{def:direct-support} and the definition of \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\),
\[
 H_d^*(h)
 =\max_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}
   \langle h,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The maximum is attained because \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\) is compact.  By the marginal equalities in Definition \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}, with \(c_x=k_x\) and \(r_{gx}=k_{gx}\),
\[
 \mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nu:=(\nu_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is a relationship proved from the two definitions, not a definition of compatibility.

Suppose first that compatibility holds.  Equations \((1)\) and \((2)\) give, for every \(h\),
\[
 \sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\langle h_g,\nu_g\rangle
 =\langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 \le \max_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}\langle h,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 =H_d^*(h).
\]

Conversely, suppose that \(\nu\notin\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Compactness gives a minimizer
\[
 w^*\in\arg\min_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}\|\nu-w\|_2,
 \qquad h^*:=\nu-w^*\ne0.
\]
For arbitrary \(w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\) and \(t\in(0,1]\), convexity puts
\(w^*+t(w-w^*)\) in \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Minimality of \(w^*\) therefore implies
\[
 0\le
 \frac{\|\nu-w^*-t(w-w^*)\|_2^2-\|\nu-w^*\|_2^2}{t}
 =-2\langle h^*,w-w^*\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
   +t\|w-w^*\|_2^2.
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow0\) yields
\(\langle h^*,w\rangle\le\langle h^*,w^*\rangle\) for every \(w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Hence, by \((1)\),
\[
 H_d^*(h^*)\le\langle h^*,w^*\rangle
 <\langle h^*,w^*\rangle+\|h^*\|_2^2
 =\langle h^*,\nu\rangle.
\]
Thus a failed compatibility system violates a displayed cut, proving the reverse implication.

It remains to prove finite normalized sufficiency.  Apply finite elimination to the linearized system above, retaining the marginal coordinates \(w\).  Each elimination step combines finitely many linear inequalities, so after finitely many row variables have been removed one obtains a finite exact half-space description of \(\mathcal P_{\mu,d}\).  Tighten each retained nonredundant inequality to its support value.  It then has the form
\[
 \langle a,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}\le
 \max_{v\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 =H_d^*(a).
 \tag{3}
\]
Any affine-hull equation produced by elimination is retained as the two opposite supporting half-spaces.  In particular, rank deficiency at \(d=0\) loses no equality restriction.  Trialwise constant arrays \(a_g(z)=c_g\) impose only
\(\sum_z w_g(z)=1\), already satisfied by every observed \(\nu_g\in\Delta_4\).  For a general normal \(a\), subtracting a trialwise constant array changes both sides of \((3)\) by the same number \(\sum_g c_g\).  Positive rescaling also preserves \((3)\).  Consequently every nonconstant retained normal has a representative of \(\ell_1\)-norm one in the quotient by trialwise constants.  By Definition \texttt{def:direct-support}, the distinct resulting rays are precisely the finite set \(\mathcal R_{\mu,d}^*\).

The exact half-space description now gives
\[
 \nu\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \langle r,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}\le H_d^*(r)
 \quad\text{for every }r\in\mathcal R_{\mu,d}^*.
\]
If a supporting direction has several maximizing rows, its exposed face merely contains several points; equation \((3)\) and its support value are unchanged.  Thus no uniqueness, strict positivity, rank, or generic-position condition is used.  The uniform-row witness and compactness argument also cover both boundary radii \(d=0\) and \(d=1\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gives exact compatibility directly from the unrestricted simplex-row TV polytope. \textit{Gap.} BeresteanuMolchanovMolinari2011 gives abstract support domination, while JiangDingGeng2016 imposes exact homogeneity; neither states this rowwise-TV shared-kernel marginal polytope. \textit{Consumer.} The cut test falsifies a radius and supplies the feasible set for the headline bounds.

\begin{proposition}[prop:separation-certificates]\label{prop:separation-certificates}
After linearizing absolute values, fixed-radius compatibility is a linear program with \(O(mq^2)\) variables and constraints. Its dual returns either \(\mathbf K\) or a normalized violated cut. Rational inputs admit a rational feasible system or rational Farkas certificate of polynomial encoding length.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Technique: explicit absolute-value linearization, phase-one linear-program duality, and determinant bounds for rational certificates.}

Write \(c_{xz}=k_x(z)\) and \(r_{gxz}=k_{gx}(z)\).  Introduce one nonnegative variable \(u_{gxz}\) for each \((g,x,z)\).  The fixed-radius compatibility system from Definition \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class} and Assumption \texttt{ass:tv-drift} is equivalent to the following finite linear system, denoted \(\mathcal L_d\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &c_{xz}\ge0,\qquad r_{gxz}\ge0,\qquad u_{gxz}\ge0,\\
 &\sum_z c_{xz}=1,\qquad \sum_z r_{gxz}=1,\\
 &u_{gxz}\ge r_{gxz}-c_{xz},\qquad
   u_{gxz}\ge c_{xz}-r_{gxz},\\
 &\sum_z u_{gxz}\le2d,\\
 &\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)r_{gxz}=\nu_g(z),
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G,\ x,z\in\mathcal X.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
If rows in \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\) are given, setting
\(u_{gxz}=|r_{gxz}-c_{xz}|\) proves feasibility of \((4)\).  Conversely, the two majorization inequalities in \((4)\) give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(r_{gx},c_x)
 =\frac12\sum_z|r_{gxz}-c_{xz}|
 \le\frac12\sum_z u_{gxz}\le d,
\]
so a solution of \((4)\) supplies exactly the kernel system required by Definition \texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}.

There are \(q^2+mq^2+mq^2=(2m+1)q^2\) variables in \((c,r,u)\).  There are \((m+1)q\) row-sum equalities, \(mq\) marginal equalities, \(2mq^2\) absolute-value majorizations, \(mq\) total-variation inequalities, and \((2m+1)q^2\) nonnegativity inequalities.  Counting an equality as two inequalities still gives \(O(mq^2)\) constraints and variables; here the declared state space has \(q=4\).

For an explicit separation oracle, retain all constraints of \((4)\) except its last line and solve the always-feasible phase-one program
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \gamma^*:=\min_{c,r,u,\gamma}\quad &\gamma\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 & (c,r,u)\text{ satisfies the first four lines of }(4),\\
 &-\gamma\le \sum_x\mu_g^0(x)r_{gxz}-\nu_g(z)\le\gamma
       \quad\text{for every }g,z,\\
 &0\le\gamma\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The upper bound on \(\gamma\) is harmless because both the generated and observed marginal coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), and uniform common and trial rows give a feasible point.  Compactness of the row polytope makes the minimum attainable.  Program \((5)\) has value zero if and only if \((4)\) is feasible.  At value zero its primal optimizer therefore returns \(\mathbf K\).

For completeness, dualize the two marginal-error inequalities in \((5)\).  Give the upper inequality multiplier \(\alpha_{gz}\ge0\), the lower inequality multiplier \(\beta_{gz}\ge0\), and put \(h_{gz}=\beta_{gz}-\alpha_{gz}\).  Minimization over \(\gamma\ge0\) requires
\(\sum_{g,z}(\alpha_{gz}+\beta_{gz})\le1\), which is equivalent, after the minimal positive--negative decomposition, to \(\|h\|_1\le1\).  Minimizing the remaining Lagrangian over the row polytope gives
\[
 \langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 -\max_{w\in\mathcal P_{\mu,d}}
      \langle h,w\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}
 =\langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}-H_d^*(h),
\]
where the final equality is Definition \texttt{def:direct-support}.  Exact finite elimination of the primal variables in \((5)\) gives the dual identity
\[
 \gamma^*
 =\max_{\|h\|_1\le1}
   \left\{\langle h,\nu\rangle_{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X}-H_d^*(h)\right\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
One may also verify exactness of \((6)\) directly: weak duality follows from
\(\langle h,\nu-w\rangle\le\|h\|_1\|\nu-w\|_\infty\), while eliminating the finitely many inequalities of the epigraph of \((5)\) produces a supporting retained inequality with equality at its attained optimum.  This is the same finite polyhedral separation calculation used in Theorem \texttt{thm:direct-shared-center-cut}, now applied to the epigraph.

If \(\gamma^*>0\), a dual maximizer in \((6)\) is a violated cut.  To put it in the normalization used by Definition \texttt{def:direct-support}, fix any \(z_0\in\mathcal X\), set
\(\bar h_g(z)=h_g(z)-h_g(z_0)\), and then set
\(\widetilde h=\bar h/\|\bar h\|_1\).  The denominator is positive: a trialwise constant \(h\) has zero gap because every \(w_g\) and \(\nu_g\) has mass one.  Moreover,
\[
 \langle \bar h,\nu\rangle-H_d^*(\bar h)
 =\langle h,\nu\rangle-H_d^*(h)>0,
\]
and positive homogeneity of the support function preserves strict violation after normalization.  Thus the phase-one primal--dual pair returns either a compatible \(\mathbf K\) or a normalized violated support cut.  By Theorem \texttt{thm:direct-shared-center-cut}, the alternatives are exhaustive.

Finally suppose that \(d\), all \(\mu_g^0(x)\), and all \(\nu_g(z)\) are rational.  Then every coefficient in \((4)\) and \((5)\) is rational.  Clear denominators row by row.  Let \(B\) be the largest resulting coefficient bit length and let \(s\) be the number of variables plus constraints, so \(s=O(mq^2)\) and \(B\) is polynomial in the input encoding length.  A feasible rational polyhedron has a basic feasible point: choose a feasible point with minimal support after conversion to equality form with nonnegative slack variables; if its supported columns were linearly dependent, a nonzero dependence would permit a small movement in one of its two signs that preserves feasibility and removes a supported coordinate, contradicting minimality.  Its nonzero coordinates therefore solve a nonsingular square subsystem.

For any such \(r\)-by-\(r\) integer subsystem, the Leibniz expansion has \(r!\) signed products, each of magnitude at most \(2^{rB}\), and therefore
\[
 |\det A_R|\le r!\,2^{rB}.
\]
The cofactor identity \(A_R^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(A_R)/\det(A_R)\), obtained by expanding the matrix product along rows, writes every solution coordinate as a ratio of two such integer determinants.  The same expansion, with one column replaced by the integer right-hand side, bounds every numerator up to one additional input coefficient.  Hence every coordinate has
\(O(r(B+\log r))\) bits, and the complete basic solution has polynomial encoding length.  Applied to \((4)\), this yields a rational feasible kernel system whenever compatibility holds.

If compatibility fails, write \((4)\) in inequality form \(Az\le b\), including opposite inequalities for equalities.  The dual multipliers obtained from \((5)\), or equivalently the nonnegative multipliers \(y\) satisfying
\[
 y^{\mathsf T}A=0,\qquad y^{\mathsf T}b<0,
\]
form a failure certificate: multiplying and summing the asserted primal inequalities would give
\(0=y^{\mathsf T}Az\le y^{\mathsf T}b<0\).  Conversely, the violated support cut just constructed supplies such multipliers by the exact elimination leading to \((6)\).  Scale so that \(y^{\mathsf T}b=-1\), and choose a minimal-support feasible \(y\).  The preceding linear-independence and determinant argument applies again, proving that \(y\), the associated rational normalized cut, and its strictly positive rational violation all have polynomial encoding length.  This proves both the stated LP size and the constructive rational success-or-failure certificate claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite exact certificates make the model auditable. \textit{Gap.} GalichonHenry2011 gives capacity checks for correspondences; this is the dual certificate for a distinct rowwise-TV kernel system. \textit{Consumer.} Meta-analysts can archive either latent rows or a reproducible failure certificate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:compatibility-radius-zero-drift]\label{prop:compatibility-radius-zero-drift}
The minimum defining \(\rho^*\) is attained, \(\rho^*\in[0,1]\), and \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\neq\varnothing\) exactly when \(d\ge\rho^*\). At \(d=0\), every \(k_{gx}=k_x\), giving the ordinary linear image of the common row-stochastic kernel polytope without a rank or unique-facet condition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the observable arrays \(\mu^0=(\mu_g^0)_{g\in\mathcal G}\) and \(\nu=(\nu_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\), where every \(\mu_g^0\) and \(\nu_g\) belongs to \(\Delta_4\).  Thus each vector has four nonnegative coordinates summing to one.  Only this simplex property and the finiteness of \(\mathcal G\) and \(\mathcal X\) are used below; in particular, the argument uses no cell-positivity, overlap, rank, or uniqueness condition.

Define the joint feasibility set
\[
 \mathcal F
 =\bigl\{(r,\mathbf K):r\in[0,1],\ \mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\bigr\}.
\]
It is a subset of the finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^{1+16+16m}\): there are sixteen coordinates among the four center rows \((k_x)_x\), and \(16m\) coordinates among the trial rows \((k_{gx})_{g,x}\).  By the construction in Definition \(\mathrm{def:robust\mbox{-}kernel\mbox{-}class}\), all row variables lie in the closed and bounded simplex \(\Delta_4\).  The marginal restrictions
\[
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}(z)=\nu_g(z),
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G,\ z\in\mathcal X,
\]
are finite systems of linear equalities.  Moreover, by the definition of total variation,
\[
 (u,v,r)\longmapsto \operatorname{TV}(u,v)-r
 =\frac12\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}|u(z)-v(z)|-r
\]
is continuous, so every restriction \(\operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le r\) is closed.  Consequently \(\mathcal F\) is a closed subset of the compact finite product
\[
 [0,1]\times(\Delta_4)^{\mathcal X}
 \times(\Delta_4)^{\mathcal G\times\mathcal X},
\]
and hence \(\mathcal F\) is compact.

The set \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty.  Indeed, take any \(e\in\Delta_4\), put \(k_x=e\) for every \(x\), and put \(k_{gx}=\nu_g\) for every \((g,x)\).  For probability vectors \(u,v\in\Delta_4\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(u,v)
 =\frac12\sum_z|u(z)-v(z)|
 \le \frac12\sum_z\{u(z)+v(z)\}=1.
\]
Thus the rowwise restrictions hold at \(r=1\).  Also, for each \(g\),
\[
 \sum_x\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}
 =\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)\nu_g
 =\left(\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)\right)\nu_g
 =\nu_g.
\]
This verifies all constraints defining \(\mathfrak K_1(\mu^0,\nu)\).

Let
\[
 \mathcal S=\{r\in[0,1]:\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
It is the continuous projection of the compact set \(\mathcal F\) onto its first coordinate, so it is compact; the preceding construction shows \(1\in\mathcal S\).  Therefore \(\mathcal S\) has an attained minimum.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:radius\mbox{-}objects}\), that minimum is \(\rho^*\), and because \(\mathcal S\subseteq[0,1]\), one has \(\rho^*\in[0,1]\).

The family is monotone in its radius.  If \(r\in\mathcal S\), \(r'\in[0,1]\), and \(r'\ge r\), any \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\) obeys
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le r\le r'
\]
for every \((g,x)\), while all of its other defining constraints are unchanged.  Hence \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_{r'}(\mu^0,\nu)\), so \(r'\in\mathcal S\).  Attainment supplies some \(\mathbf K^*\in\mathfrak K_{\rho^*}(\mu^0,\nu)\).  It follows from monotonicity that every \(d\in[0,1]\) with \(d\ge\rho^*\) belongs to \(\mathcal S\).  Conversely, if \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing\), then \(d\in\mathcal S\), and the minimality of \(\rho^*\) gives \(d\ge\rho^*\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\ge\rho^*,
 \qquad d\in[0,1].
\]

It remains to identify the zero-drift boundary.  If \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_0(\mu^0,\nu)\), the zero-radius total-variation constraint in Definition \(\mathrm{def:robust\mbox{-}kernel\mbox{-}class}\) and nonnegativity of the absolute-value summands give, for every \((g,x)\),
\[
 0\le\frac12\sum_z|k_{gx}(z)-k_x(z)|
 =\operatorname{TV}(k_{gx},k_x)\le0.
\]
The sum is zero only if each summand is zero, so \(k_{gx}(z)=k_x(z)\) for every \(z\), equivalently \(k_{gx}=k_x\).  Conversely, equality of these rows makes every total-variation constraint hold with radius zero.  Substitution into the marginal equations yields exactly
\[
 \nu_g=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\mu_g^0(x)k_x,
 \qquad g\in\mathcal G.
\]
More explicitly, let
\[
 \mathcal C=(\Delta_4)^{\mathcal X}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \widetilde T_{\mu^0}:(\mathbb R^4)^{\mathcal X}
 \longrightarrow(\mathbb R^4)^{\mathcal G},
 \qquad
 \widetilde T_{\mu^0}((v_x)_x)
 =\left(\sum_x\mu_g^0(x)v_x\right)_{g\in\mathcal G}.
\]
The set \(\mathcal C\) is the common row-stochastic kernel polytope and \(\widetilde T_{\mu^0}\) is a linear map on the ambient vector space.  The preceding equivalence proves
\[
 \mathfrak K_0(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nu\in \widetilde T_{\mu^0}(\mathcal C),
\]
and the center-row components of \(\mathfrak K_0(\mu^0,\nu)\) form the fiber \(\widetilde T_{\mu^0}^{-1}(\{\nu\})\cap\mathcal C\).  This is precisely the ordinary linear image characterization.  No rank hypothesis is required: rank can change the dimension of the image or fiber but not either displayed equivalence.  Likewise, no unique-facet condition enters any equality, compactness argument, or boundary argument above.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separates the observable-data compatibility threshold from the prespecified sensitivity radius and supplies the zero-drift sanity check. \textit{Gap.} WuMao2025 Corollary 2 identifies the exact common transition under full column rank but does not derive a minimum deterministic rowwise distance fitting potentially incompatible trials; randomized-arm marginals do not identify the latent cross-world drift. \textit{Consumer.} The ACCT analysis can report \(\rho^*\) as a fit diagnostic and lower bound while eliciting, rather than data-calibrating, the scientifically credible upper radius.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-psace-endpoints]\label{thm:sharp-psace-endpoints}
Whenever \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\neq\varnothing\), each coupled set is the attained interval \(\Theta_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)=[L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d),U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)]\). Both endpoints are sharp over one common-center system fitting all \(m\) trials, and a Charnes--Cooper linear program returns each endpoint and an attaining \(\mathbf K\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(g\) and \(d\), and suppose \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\ne\varnothing\).  We proceed in five steps.

First, the class is a compact convex polytope.  Indeed, every stochastic row lies in the compact simplex \(\Delta_4\); marginal reproduction is affine; and each constraint
\(\operatorname{TV}(k_{g'x},k_x)\le d\) is equivalent to the existence of nonnegative variables \(u_{g'xz}\) satisfying
\[
 u_{g'xz}\ge k_{g'x}(z)-k_x(z),\qquad
 u_{g'xz}\ge k_x(z)-k_{g'x}(z),\qquad
 \frac12\sum_z u_{g'xz}\le d.
\]
Projection of this bounded finite linear system gives exactly the class in \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\).  Convexity can also be checked directly from convexity of the \(\ell_1\) norm.

Second, this polytope has the asserted causal interpretation.  For any \(\mathbf K\) in the class, define
\[
 J_{g'}(x,z)=\mu_{g'}^0(x)k_{g'x}(z),\qquad g'\in\mathcal G.
\]
The stochastic-row and reproduction equations imply that \(J_{g'}\) is a joint law with marginals \(\mu_{g'}^0\) and \(\nu_{g'}\).  Under this law,
\[
 D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\Pr\{S(1)=1,S(0)=0\mid G=g\}
\]
and
\[
 N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\mathbb E[(Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1\{S(1)=1,S(0)=0\}\mid G=g].
\]
The preceding denominator lemma yields \(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)\ge\kappa>0\), so division is legitimate and
\[
 \frac{N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}{D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}
 =\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid S(1)=1,S(0)=0,G=g].
\]
Conversely, the conditional transition rows of every joint potential-state law satisfying the maintained shared-center restrictions give such a \(\mathbf K\); rows at a zero-mass control state may be chosen as the maintained rows without changing the joint law.  For attainability from a given \(\mathbf K\), choose any strictly positive law of \(G\), use \(J_{g'}\) as the conditional law of \((X(0),X(1))\) given \(G=g'\), draw \(A\) independently of the potential states given \(G\) with probability strictly between zero and one, and set \(X^{\mathrm{obs}}=X(A)\).  This constructed law satisfies randomization, consistency, and arm overlap and has the required observed arm marginals.  Thus the displayed ratios are values of the causal target rather than a definition of that target.  The observed-law bridge \(\texttt{lem:observed-arm-identification}\) shows that the two marginals entering this construction are identified from the randomized arms.

Third, the numerator and denominator are linear in the entries of \(\mathbf K\).  Their ratio is continuous on the compact class because its denominator is at least \(\kappa\).  It consequently attains a minimum and maximum, denoted by \(L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\) and \(U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\).  If \(\mathbf K_0,\mathbf K_1\) have ratios \(r_0,r_1\), then for \(0\le t\le1\), convexity puts \(\mathbf K_t=(1-t)\mathbf K_0+t\mathbf K_1\) in the same class and
\[
 \frac{N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_t)}{D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_t)}
 =\frac{(1-t)D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_0)r_0+tD_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_1)r_1}
 {(1-t)D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_0)+tD_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K_1)}.
\]
This is a continuous weighted average of \(r_0\) and \(r_1\), with strictly positive weights away from the corresponding endpoint.  It takes every value between them.  Applying this to minimizers and maximizers proves
\[
 \Theta_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)=[L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d),U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)].
\]
The joint laws just constructed show attainability of every point, so the interval is sharp, not merely an outer bound.

Fourth, both endpoints are computable by one linear program.  After the absolute-value linearization above, stack the row variables into \(v\) and write the feasible polytope as
\[
 Av=b,\qquad Cv\le e,\qquad v\ge0.
\]
Write the numerator and denominator as \(p_g^{\top}v\) and \(q_g^{\top}v\).  The denominator lemma and the fact that it is a probability give
\(\kappa\le q_g^{\top}v\le1\).  The Charnes--Cooper substitution from \(\texttt{lem:charnes-cooper-linear-fractional}\), here verified directly, is
\[
 t=(q_g^{\top}v)^{-1},\qquad y=tv.
\]
It converts endpoint minimization into the linear program
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{minimize}&p_g^{\top}y\\
 \text{subject to}&Ay=bt,\quad Cy\le et,\quad y\ge0,\\
 &q_g^{\top}y=1,\quad 1\le t\le\kappa^{-1}.
 \end{array}
\]
Maximizing the same objective gives the upper endpoint.  The forward substitution preserves the objective because \(p_g^{\top}y=(p_g^{\top}v)/(q_g^{\top}v)\).  Conversely, every feasible \((y,t)\) has \(t>0\), and \(v=y/t\) is feasible for the original polytope and has the same objective.  Thus the transformation is bijective between feasible solutions.  Its feasible set is compact: \(t\) is bounded, transformed stochastic-row coordinates are at most \(\kappa^{-1}\), and transformed absolute-value auxiliaries are at most \(2\kappa^{-1}\).  Hence each linear program returns an optimizer, and dividing its \(y\)-rows by \(t\) returns an attaining common-center system \(\mathbf K\).

Finally, the program contains the rows for all \(m\) trials simultaneously and uses one common collection \((k_x)_x\).  Therefore neither endpoint optimizes trial \(g\) after discarding the other trials: both are sharp over the single coupled class stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The headline delivers sharp attained PSACE endpoints while preserving cross-trial coupling. \textit{Gap.} WuMao2025 Corollary 2 is the primary zero-drift comparator: exact four-state transition transportability plus full column rank point-identifies the joint law and PSACEs, but it supplies no positive-TV identified set. JiangDingGeng2016 uses a different exact cross-trial homogeneity restriction or the without-monotonicity separate-trial bounds in arXiv Appendix A.1; YuanFellerMiratrix2018 instead uses zero site-level correlation, and WuAntonelli2024v1 instead uses continuous-intermediate outcome models with sign or dominant-observed-effect restrictions. The covariate-bound, proxy-identification, and historical-prediction comparators do not impose a shared cross-world rowwise-TV sensitivity class. CharnesCooper1962 supplies optimization machinery rather than this coupled latent class. \textit{Consumer.} An ACCT reanalysis can relax exact homogeneity and still report constructive sharp effects.

\begin{lemma}[lem:observable-neighborhood-nondegenerate]\label{lem:observable-neighborhood-nondegenerate}
The observable marginals \((\mu^{\mathrm w},\nu^{\mathrm w})\) belong to \(\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\). Every member of this class has strictly positive arm-state marginals, a strictly positive row-stochastic \(K^0=M^{-1}N\), and trial surrogate first stages exceeding \(13/100\); moreover the class contains a relative open neighborhood of \((\mu^{\mathrm w},\nu^{\mathrm w})\) in the product of the eight simplex interiors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\bar M\) and \(\bar K\) denote the two matrices displayed in \(\texttt{def:rational-witness}\).  Then
\[
 \bar M=\frac35 I_4+\frac1{10}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top},
 \qquad
 \bar M^{-1}=\frac53 I_4-\frac16\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}.
\]
Thus \(\bar M\) is nonsingular.  The matrix \(\bar K\) is row-stochastic and has minimum entry \(1/40>0\).  By construction \(\nu^{\mathrm w}=\bar M\bar K\), whence the witness has \(K^0=\bar M^{-1}\nu^{\mathrm w}=\bar K\).  Both strict inequalities defining \(\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\) hold with left side zero, so \((\mu^{\mathrm w},\nu^{\mathrm w})\) belongs to that class.

Now take an arbitrary class member and put \(K^0=M^{-1}N\).  Each entry of \(M\) is larger than \(1/10-10^{-3}=99/1000\), so every control marginal is strictly positive.  Since \(M\mathbf 1=N\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\), nonsingularity gives
\[
 K^0\mathbf 1=M^{-1}N\mathbf 1=M^{-1}\mathbf 1=mathbf 1.
\]
Every entry of \(K^0\) is larger than \(1/40-10^{-3}=3/125>0\), so \(K^0\) is a strictly positive row-stochastic matrix.  The identity \(N=MK^0\), together with strict positivity of \(M\) and \(K^0\), makes every treatment marginal strictly positive as well.

For a row \(x\), set \(t_x=\sum_{z:s_z=1}K^0_{xz}\).  At the witness, \((t_x)_x=(9/10,9/10,19/20,19/20)\).  Hence the witness first stages are \(71/100,71/100,14/100,14/100\).  For a class member, \(|t_x-\bar t_x|<2\epsilon_{\mathrm w}\).  Since every row of \(M\) sums to one and \(|\bar t_x-\mathbf 1\{s_x=1\}|\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\Delta_g^S-\bar\Delta_g^S|
 &\le \sum_x|M_{gx}-\bar M_{gx}|\,
   |\bar t_x-\mathbf 1\{s_x=1\}|
   +\sum_xM_{gx}|t_x-\bar t_x|\\
 &<4\epsilon_{\mathrm w}+2\epsilon_{\mathrm w}=6/1000.
\end{aligned}
\]
The smallest witness value is \(14/100\), so every class member has \(\Delta_g^S>134/1000>13/100\).

It remains to verify nondegeneracy of the observable class.  Matrix inversion is continuous at the nonsingular \(\bar M\), and multiplication is continuous.  Therefore the map \((M,N)\mapsto M^{-1}N\) is continuous on some relative neighborhood of \((\bar M,\bar M\bar K)\).  Nonsingularity, strict positivity of all eight probability vectors, and both displayed strict inequalities in \(\texttt{def:observable-neighborhood}\) are open conditions relative to the product of the eight simplices.  Their intersection consequently contains a relative open neighborhood of the witness in the product of the eight simplex interiors.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-single-trial-improvement]\label{thm:strict-single-trial-improvement}
For every \((\mu^0,\nu)\in\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\), Wu--Mao's exact transportability model identifies \(K^0=M^{-1}N\) and \(\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)=\{\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\}\). For every \(0<d\le\bar d=10^{-2}\), the rowwise-TV model obeys the two strict inclusions \[\{\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\}=\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)\subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}.\] Both strict gaps hold uniformly over this nondegenerate observable class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write the ordered states as \(1=(0,0)\), \(2=(0,1)\), \(3=(1,0)\), and \(4=(1,1)\), and put \(\bar M=\mu^{\mathrm w}\), \(\bar K=K^{\mathrm w}\), and \(\epsilon=\epsilon_{\mathrm w}=10^{-3}\). Fix \((\mu^0,\nu)\in\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\). By \(\texttt{lem:observable-neighborhood-nondegenerate}\), every entry of \(\mu_g^0\), \(\nu_g\), and \(K^0=M^{-1}N\) is strictly positive, \(K^0\) is row-stochastic, and \(\Delta_g^S>13/100\) for every \(g\). By \(\texttt{lem:first-stage-denominator}\), every denominator considered below is at least \(\Delta_g^S\), hence is strictly positive.

First connect the causal target to the displayed optimization functional. Given a feasible kernel system \(\mathbf K\), define
\[
 J_g^{\mathbf K}(x,z)=\mu_g^0(x)k_{gx}(z).
\]
The simplex constraints and marginal-reproduction equalities in \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\) give respectively
\[
 \sum_zJ_g^{\mathbf K}(x,z)=\mu_g^0(x),
 \qquad
 \sum_xJ_g^{\mathbf K}(x,z)=\nu_g(z).
\]
Thus \(J_g^{\mathbf K}\) is a joint law of \((X(0),X(1))\) with the observed arm marginals identified by \(\texttt{lem:observed-arm-identification}\). Directly from the definitions of \(D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}\) and \(N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}\),
\[
 D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\Pr_{J_g^{\mathbf K}}\{S(0)=0,S(1)=1\},
\]
and
\[
 N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)
 =\mathbb E_{J_g^{\mathbf K}}
 \bigl[(Y(1)-Y(0))\mathbf 1\{S(0)=0,S(1)=1\}\bigr].
\]
Because the denominator is positive, division yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{J_g^{\mathbf K}}
 [Y(1)-Y(0)\mid S(1)=1,S(0)=0]
 =\frac{N_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}{D_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)}.
\]
Conversely, because every \(\mu_g^0(x)>0\), any joint law satisfying the stated rowwise shared-center restrictions determines the conditional rows \(k_{gx}(z)=J_g(x,z)/\mu_g^0(x)\). Hence the range in \(\texttt{def:psace-objects}\) is the causal identified set rather than merely an observed-law regression functional.

We next use full-rank linear algebra for the zero-drift claim. At \(d=0\), the total-variation constraints force \(k_{gx}=k_x\) for all \(g,x\). If \(C\) denotes the matrix with rows \(C_x=k_x\), marginal reproduction becomes
\[
 MC=N.
\]
The nonsingularity of \(M\), which is part of \(\mathcal W_{\epsilon_{\mathrm w}}\), therefore forces
\[
 C=M^{-1}N=K^0.
\]
Conversely, taking every center and trial row equal to the positive stochastic row \(K_x^0\) satisfies marginal reproduction because \(MK^0=N\). Thus the zero-drift class is nonempty and has a unique effective transition matrix. Substitution into \(\texttt{def:psace-objects}\) and \(\texttt{def:wu-mao-functional}\) gives
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)=\{\theta_1^{\mathrm{WM}}\}.
\]
This is the transition restriction corresponding to exact transportability on the present \(m=4\), full-rank, positive-first-stage subclass.

We now prove that every positive radius creates genuine ambiguity, using a marginal-preserving transportation cycle. Define
\[
 J^0(x,z)=\mu_1^0(x)K^0_{xz}
\]
and, for \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 J^t=J^0+t(e_{14}+e_{31}-e_{11}-e_{34}),
\]
where \(e_{xz}\) has one in cell \((x,z)\) and zero elsewhere. The signed perturbation has every row sum and every column sum equal to zero. Since \(J^0\) is strictly positive, there is \(t_0>0\) such that \(J^t\ge0\) whenever \(|t|<t_0\). For such \(t\), set
\[
 k^t_{1x}(z)=\frac{J^t(x,z)}{\mu_1^0(x)},
\]
retain \(k^t_{gx}=K_x^0\) for \(g\ne1\), and take every center row to be \(K_x^0\). Row and column preservation shows that all marginal-reproduction equalities remain valid. The only nonzero rowwise distances are
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k^t_{1,1},K_1^0)=\frac{|t|}{\mu_1^0(1)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(k^t_{1,3},K_3^0)=\frac{|t|}{\mu_1^0(3)}.
\]
For any fixed \(d>0\), both signs of every nonzero \(t\) satisfying
\[
 |t|<\min\{t_0,d\mu_1^0(1),d\mu_1^0(3)\}
\]
therefore produce systems in \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\). Among the four perturbed cells, only \((1,4)\) enters either prevented-recurrence functional, and it has coefficient one in each. Consequently
\[
 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^t)=N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t,
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^t)=D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t,
\]
where \(\mathbf K^0\) is the system whose center and trial rows all equal \(K^0\). Moreover,
\[
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)>0,
\]
because the strictly positive table \(J^0\) assigns positive mass to the prevented-recurrence cell \((1,3)\), on which the denominator coefficient is one and the numerator coefficient is zero. Hence, whenever the preceding feasibility condition holds,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}
 \frac{N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t}
      {D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t}
 =\frac{D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)}
 {(D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)+t)^2}>0.
\]
The zero-drift system itself is feasible at every larger radius, and the two signs of \(t\) yield distinct ratios on its two sides. Therefore
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(0)\subsetneq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)
 \qquad(d>0).
\]

It remains to prove that cross-trial coupling still excludes a value allowed by the unrestricted single-trial transportation polytope. Every feasible robust system induces \(J_1^{\mathbf K}\in\mathfrak J_1\), so the preceding causal-functional identity and \(\texttt{def:single-trial-benchmark}\) imply
\[
 \Theta_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)\subseteq\Theta_1^{\mathrm{ST}}.
\]
We make this inclusion strict uniformly for \(d\le1/100\).

Use the induced maximum-row-sum matrix norm. Since
\[
 \bar M=\frac35 I_4+\frac1{10}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \bar M^{-1}=\frac53I_4-\frac16\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},
\]
direct multiplication verifies the inverse and gives
\[
 \|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty=\frac32+3\left(\frac16\right)=2.
\]
The neighborhood restriction gives
\[
 \|M-\bar M\|_\infty<4\epsilon=\frac1{250}.
\]
Put \(B=\bar M^{-1}(M-\bar M)\). Then \(M=\bar M(I+B)\) and
\[
 \|B\|_\infty<\frac1{125}.
\]
For every integer \(J\ge0\), finite multiplication gives
\[
 (I+B)\sum_{j=0}^{J}(-B)^j=I+(-1)^JB^{J+1}.
\]
The remainder norm is at most \(\|B\|_\infty^{J+1}\), which tends to zero. Thus the series converges to \((I+B)^{-1}\), and the geometric-sum bound yields
\[
 \|M^{-1}\|_\infty
 \le \frac{\|\bar M^{-1}\|_\infty}{1-\|B\|_\infty}
 <\frac{2}{1-1/125}=\frac{125}{62}.
\]

For a feasible system with center matrix \(C\), define the signed residual row
\[
 r_{g'}=\nu_{g'}-\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)C_x
 =\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)(k_{g'x}-C_x).
\]
Each \(r_{g'}\) has coordinate sum zero. For any zero-sum signed vector \(v\), write
\[
 \|v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\frac12\sum_z|v(z)|.
\]
The triangle inequality, the nonnegative weights summing to one, and the rowwise constraint in \(\texttt{def:robust-kernel-class}\) give
\[
 \|r_{g'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\sum_x\mu_{g'}^0(x)\operatorname{TV}(k_{g'x},C_x)
 \le d.
\]
In matrix form \(N=MC+r\), so
\[
 K^0=C+M^{-1}r.
\]
Every row of \(M^{-1}r\) also has coordinate sum zero. Therefore, for every \(x\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(C_x,K_x^0)
 \le\sum_{g'}|(M^{-1})_{xg'}|\,\|r_{g'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 <\frac{125}{62}d.
\]
Another triangle inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(k_{1x},K_x^0)<\frac{187}{62}d.
\]

For a zero-sum signed vector \(v\) and a real function \(f\) on \(\mathcal X\), subtracting \(\min_z f(z)\) and separating the positive and negative coordinates of \(v\) proves the elementary oscillation inequality
\[
 \left|\sum_zf(z)v(z)\right|
 \le\bigl(\max_zf(z)-\min_zf(z)\bigr)\|v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The row functions defining \(N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}\) and \(D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}\) have oscillation one for \(x=1,2\) and vanish for \(x=3,4\). Also the neighborhood gives \(\mu_1^0(1)+\mu_1^0(2)<802/1000\). Hence, for \(d\le1/100\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 |N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)|
 &<\frac{802}{1000}\frac{187}{62}\frac1{100}<\frac{25}{1000},\\
 |D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K)-D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)|
 &<\frac{802}{1000}\frac{187}{62}\frac1{100}<\frac{25}{1000}.
 \end{split}
\]

Let \(\bar{\mathbf K}\) denote the system whose center and trial rows all equal \(\bar K\), evaluated with control marginals \(\bar M\). At the rational anchor, direct substitution gives

 N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\bar{\mathbf K})=\frac{27}{100},
 \qquad
 D_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\bar{\mathbf K})=\frac{72}{100}.
\]
For the current law, the entrywise neighborhood bounds imply

 \left|N_1^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbf K^0)-\frac{27}{100}\right|
 <2\epsilon\frac{45}{100}
   +\left(\frac{701}{1000}+\frac{101}{1000}\right)\epsilon
 =\frac{1702}{10^6},

and

 <2\epsilon\frac9{10}
   +2\left(\frac{701}{1000}+\frac{101}{1000}\right)\epsilon

\]
Thus

 \qquad

\]
Combining these inequalities with the \(25/1000\) perturbation bounds and the already-proved positivity of every denominator yields, for every robust feasible system and every \(d\le1/100\),

 >\frac{243}{749}>\frac3{10}.

\]
For \(z=3,4\), the identity \(\nu_1(z)=\sum_x\mu_1^0(x)K^0_{xz}\) and the two entrywise neighborhood restrictions imply

 \left|\nu_1(z)-\nu_1^{\mathrm w}(z)\right|
 \le\epsilon\sum_x\bar K_{xz}+\epsilon\sum_x\mu_1^0(x)
 =\frac{185}{100}\epsilon+\epsilon
 =\frac{285}{100000}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-sign-radius]\label{thm:sharp-sign-radius}
For each \(g\), if \(\mathsf P_g(\mu^0,\nu)\) is feasible, then \[\delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}=\min\mathsf P_g(\mu^0,\nu)=\max_{(y,\lambda)\in\mathcal B_g(\mu^0)}\Phi_g(\mu^0,\nu;y,\lambda)\in[0,1].\] The primal minimum and finite-cut maximum are attained; a primal optimizer contains an adversarial sign-reversing kernel, and a maximizing basic cut certifies that no smaller radius reverses the sign. If the primal is infeasible, then \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}=+\infty\), and a Farkas ray certifies that no sign-reversing compatible kernel exists even at \(d=1\). For rational marginals, the optimizer, dual cut, or infeasibility ray can be chosen rational. Consequently \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}=\min_g\delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}\), with the all-trial value equal to \(+\infty\) exactly when every trial has the infeasibility certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(g\).  We first identify the canonical linear program with the radius in \(\texttt{def:radius-objects}\).  Given a sign-reversing \(\mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\), set
\(u_{g'xz}=|k_{g'x}(z)-k_x(z)|\).  These values satisfy every constraint of \(\mathsf P_g(\mu^0,\nu)\) with objective \(r\).  Conversely, primal feasibility implies
\[
 |k_{g'x}(z)-k_x(z)|\le u_{g'xz},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(k_{g'x},k_x)
 \le\frac12\sum_z u_{g'xz}\le r.
\]
Together with the stochastic-row, marginal-reproduction, and sign constraints, this says exactly that the row variables form a sign-reversing member of \(\mathfrak K_r(\mu^0,\nu)\).  Thus the feasible objective values are precisely radii in \([0,1]\) at which trial \(g\) can be reversed.

When the primal is feasible, its feasible set is compact: \(0\le r\le1\), every row coordinate lies in \([0,1]\), and \(0\le u_{g'xz}\le2r\le2\).  Hence its minimum is attained.  The equivalence just proved and upward nesting of \(\mathfrak K_r\) show that this minimum equals \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:positive-first-stage}\), through \(\texttt{lem:first-stage-denominator}\), ensures that this numerator sign is also the sign of the PSACE and that no zero denominator is being classified.

Apply the finite-dimensional linear-program alternative in \(\texttt{lem:finite-linear-program-certificates}\) to the standard form from \(\texttt{def:sign-radius-dual}\).  For primal-feasible \(w\) and dual-feasible \((y,\lambda)\), weak duality is visible directly:
\[
 e(\nu)^{\top}y-f^{\top}\lambda
 \le e(\nu)^{\top}y-\lambda^{\top}F_gw
 =(E_\mu^{\top}y-F_g^{\top}\lambda)^{\top}w
 \le c^{\top}w.
\]
Strong duality gives equality at optimizers.  After quotienting the declared lineality, the dual is pointed.  A finite optimum of a pointed polyhedron is attained at a basic feasible point; there are only finitely many bases.  It follows that
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}
 =\min\mathsf P_g(\mu^0,\nu)
 =\max_{(y,\lambda)\in\mathcal B_g(\mu^0)}
   \Phi_g(\mu^0,\nu;y,\lambda)\in[0,1].
\]
The primal optimizer itself supplies the advertised adversarial kernel.  The maximizing dual cut has value \(r_*\); weak duality shows that every sign-reversing kernel has radius at least \(r_*\), so it is the advertised lower-radius certificate.

If the primal is infeasible, the first equivalence says that no sign-reversing compatible system exists for any \(r\le1\), including \(r=1\).  Hence the defining set for \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}\) is empty and the stipulated convention gives \(+\infty\).  The infeasible alternative in \(\texttt{lem:finite-linear-program-certificates}\) returns a Farkas multiplier whose checked linear combination makes the right side strictly negative while the left side is nonnegative; it therefore certifies the same impossibility.

If the marginals are rational, every coefficient in \(E_\mu,e(\nu),F_g,f,c\) is rational.  The cited linear-program alternative permits a primal optimizer and dual basic optimizer obtained by solving nonsingular rational subsystems, hence rational, and in the infeasible case a rational extreme Farkas ray.  Finally, because \(\mathcal G\) is finite,
\(\min_g\delta_{\mathrm{sign},g}=+\infty\) if and only if each term is \(+\infty\), which by the preceding equivalence is exactly the case in which every trial has its infeasibility certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The second headline turns robustness into an independently checkable primal--dual certificate rather than merely restating the radius definition. \textit{Gap.} HsiaoTianParast2025 studies resilience under single-study conditional-mean drift, and HsiaoParast2026 extends that object to a functional-class resilience probability learned from \(K\) completed studies for a new surrogate-only study. ElliottConlonLiKacirotiTaylor2015 uses trial-level causal-association random effects to estimate opposite-sign probabilities and a beneficial-surrogate threshold. ChenHarhayLi2026 uses principal-ignorability sensitivity bounds, while WuAntonelli2024v1 bounds continuous-intermediate model coefficients under same-sign or dominant-observed-effect restrictions. None targets a deterministic minimum latent rowwise shared-kernel TV distance to sign reversal across trial-specific PSACEs. \textit{Consumer.} The ACCT analysis can report how much heterogeneity is needed to overturn any trial.

\begin{lemma}[lem:projection-set-inclusion]\label{lem:projection-set-inclusion}
If \(p^0\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\), then \(\widetilde\Theta_g(d;p^0)\subseteq\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d)\) for every \(g,d\), and \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p^0)\in\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\). The inclusion also holds for \(\{\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\}\) and \(+\infty\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is a deterministic consequence of the unions in \(\texttt{def:confidence-objects}\).  If \(p^0\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\), then the summand indexed by \(p=p^0\) occurs in
\[
 \mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d)
 =\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}}
   \widetilde\Theta_g(d;p).
\]
Therefore \(\widetilde\Theta_g(d;p^0)\subseteq\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d)\).  This argument is set-theoretic and does not distinguish a real-valued compatible fiber from the singleton \(\{\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\}\).  Likewise, the definition
\[
 \mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}
 =\{\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p):
      p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\}
\]
immediately puts \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p^0)\) in that set, whether its value is finite or \(+\infty\).  No uniqueness, active-face, or boundary condition is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-region-coverage]\label{thm:simultaneous-region-coverage}
The observed-arm identification lemma and conditional multinomial concentration imply \(\Pr\{(\mu^0,\nu)\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\}\ge1-\alpha\). The set-inclusion lemma therefore gives simultaneous finite-sample coverage for all augmented PSACE fibers and \(\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}\). The interval hull of each finite projected fiber covers both sharp endpoints whenever the true fixed-radius class is compatible. Coverage remains valid at tied cuts, at \(d\in\{0,1\}\), under incompatibility, and at \(+\infty\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(p^0=(\mu_g^0,\nu_g)_{g\in\mathcal G}\). By \(\texttt{lem:observed-arm-identification}\), these are the true observed-arm probability vectors. Conditional on the sample sizes, \(\texttt{ass:multinomial-sampling}\) and \(\texttt{lem:weissman-multinomial-l1}\) give, separately for each of the \(2m\) multinomial empirical laws, the required concentration inequality.

Put \(C_4=2^4-2\). If
\[
 \sqrt{2n_{ga}^{-1}\log\!\left(C_4\,2m/\alpha\right)}<2,
\]
then the definition of \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)\) gives
\[
 \varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)^2
 =\frac{2}{n_{ga}}\log\!\left(C_4\,2m/\alpha\right).
\]
Therefore the cited multinomial inequality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Pr\!\left\{\|\widehat p_{ga}-p^0_{ga}\|_1>
 \varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)\mid(n_{g'a'})_{g',a'}\right\}\\
 &\qquad\le C_4\exp\!\left\{-\frac{n_{ga}\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)^2}{2}\right\}\\
 &\qquad=C_4\exp\!\left\{-\log\!\left(C_4\,2m/\alpha\right)\right\}
 =\frac{\alpha}{2m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If the displayed square root is at least two, then \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)=2\). For any \(u,v\in\Delta_4\), nonnegativity and unit total mass give
\[
 \|u-v\|_1\le\sum_{z\in\mathcal X}\{u(z)+v(z)\}=2,
\]
so the strict failure event \(\{\|\widehat p_{ga}-p^0_{ga}\|_1>2\}\) is empty. Thus, in either branch, the conditional failure probability for each pair \((g,a)\) is at most \(\alpha/(2m)\). The finite union bound over the \(2m\) pairs, which does not require independence, gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
 \|\widehat p_{ga}-p^0_{ga}\|_1\le\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)
 \text{ for every }g\in\mathcal G,
 \ a\in\{0,1\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}(n_{g'a'})_{g',a'}
 \right\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Averaging this conditional inequality over the sample sizes preserves the lower bound \(1-\alpha\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:positive-first-stage}\) states that the true vectors obey every first-stage inequality in \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\), while they belong to \(\Delta_4\) by definition. Hence
\[
 \Pr\{p^0\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

On the single event \(\{p^0\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\}\), \(\texttt{lem:projection-set-inclusion}\) gives, simultaneously for every \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(d\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \widetilde\Theta_g(d;p^0)\subseteq\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d),
 \qquad
 \delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p^0)
 \in\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}.
\]
If \(\mathfrak K_d(\mu^0,\nu)\neq\varnothing\), then \(\texttt{thm:sharp-psace-endpoints}\) gives
\[
 \widetilde\Theta_g(d;p^0)=\Theta_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d;p^0)
 =[L_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d),U_g^{\mathrm{pr}}(d)].
\]
Consequently the interval hull of the real-valued part of the projected fiber contains both attained sharp endpoints. If the true fixed-radius class is incompatible, the same inclusion instead carries \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\). By the definition of \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\), its extended-real inclusion carries \(+\infty\) without alteration whenever \(\texttt{thm:sharp-sign-radius}\) places the true radius there.

The argument uses only deterministic set inclusion, the two exhaustive clipping branches, and a finite union bound. It never selects a unique optimizing cut or differentiates an optimum. It therefore remains valid when cuts tie, at \(d=0\) and \(d=1\), at a compatibility boundary, under incompatibility, and in the extended-real no-reversal case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-bilinear-profiling]\label{thm:exact-bilinear-profiling}
For rational empirical marginals and rational numerical radii \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha),\kappa,d\), \(\mathsf{ProfileTV}\) has a face-complete exact completion by cylindrical algebraic decomposition.  For each \(g\), it returns the real-valued part of \(\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d)\) as a finite union of closed intervals and isolated real-algebraic points, with an attaining \((p,\mathbf K)\) for every boundary point, and it returns whether \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\) is present.  It returns \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\cap[0,1]\) as a finite union of real-algebraic points and intervals with exact open/closed endpoint flags, and returns whether \(+\infty\) is present.  The algorithm terminates after finitely many sign-invariant cells for every rational input, including empty confidence regions, tied active cuts, compatibility boundaries, and sign-radius boundaries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We construct the exact completion and verify each advertised output.  Throughout this proof the empirical marginals and the numerical values of \(\varepsilon_{ga}(\alpha)\), \(\kappa\), and \(d\) are rational, as required by the theorem.

First encode membership in \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\).  The simplex equations, nonnegativity constraints, and first-stage inequalities are rational linear conditions.  An inequality \(\sum_i|v_i|\le e\) is encoded exactly by adjoining variables \(a_i\) and requiring \(a_i\ge v_i\), \(a_i\ge-v_i\), \(a_i\ge0\), and \(\sum_i a_i\le e\).  Indeed, the displayed conditions imply \(a_i\ge|v_i|\), while the converse follows by taking \(a_i=|v_i|\).  Thus every confidence-region constraint is a first-order formula over the ordered real field with rational coefficients.  The region is closed and is contained in the compact product \((\Delta_4)^{2m}\), hence is compact.

For a candidate \(p\), define the proof intermediates
\[
 D_g(\mathbf K;p)=\sum_{x:s_x=0,\,z:s_z=1}p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z)
\]
and
\[
 N_g(\mathbf K;p)=\sum_{x:s_x=0,\,z:s_z=1}(y_z-y_x)p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z).
\]
For fixed \(g\) and \(d\), consider the one-free-variable formula
\[
 Q_{g,d}(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\,\exists\mathbf K\;
 \bigl[p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa},\ 
 \mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_d(p_{\cdot0},p_{\cdot1}),\ 
 N_g(\mathbf K;p)-tD_g(\mathbf K;p)=0\bigr].
\]
Simplex membership and stochastic-row equations are linear.  Marginal reproduction contains the bilinear polynomials \(p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z)\).  Each rowwise total-variation inequality is encoded exactly by the preceding absolute-value construction.  Consequently \(Q_{g,d}\) is a finite rational polynomial formula; in particular, no McCormick relaxation is used as an equality surrogate.

For every feasible pair \((p,\mathbf K)\), also put
\[
 R_g(\mathbf K;p)=\sum_{x:s_x=1,\,z:s_z=0}p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z).
\]
Marginal reproduction and stochasticity give the accounting identity used in \(\texttt{lem:first-stage-denominator}\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_g(\mathbf K;p)-R_g(\mathbf K;p)
 &=\sum_{x,z}(s_z-s_x)p_{g0}(x)k_{gx}(z)\\
 &=\sum_z s_zp_{g1}(z)-\sum_xs_xp_{g0}(x)
 =\Delta_g^S(p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(R_g(\mathbf K;p)\ge0\), the defining first-stage condition for \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\) now gives
\[
 D_g(\mathbf K;p)\ge \Delta_g^S(p)\ge\kappa>0.
\]
Division is therefore legitimate, and \(Q_{g,d}(t)\) holds if and only if \(t=N_g(\mathbf K;p)/D_g(\mathbf K;p)\) for some compatible \((p,\mathbf K)\).  By \(\texttt{def:confidence-objects}\), its truth set is exactly the real-valued part of \(\mathcal I_{1-\alpha,g}(d)\); incompatible fibers contribute only \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\).

All coordinates of \(p\), the center rows, and the trial rows lie in finite products of simplices.  The equations and weak inequalities defining feasible \((p,\mathbf K)\) are continuous, so that feasible set is closed and bounded, hence compact.  The ratio is continuous there because its denominator is bounded below by \(\kappa\).  Its image, namely the truth set of \(Q_{g,d}\), is consequently compact.  Apply \(\texttt{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) to \(Q_{g,d}\).  The resulting sign-invariant cylindrical algebraic decomposition gives a finite union of points and intervals with real-algebraic finite boundaries.  Compactness forces every interval endpoint belonging to the boundary of this image to be included; after merging adjacent cells, the intervals are closed and the remaining zero-dimensional components are isolated points.  Exact lifting over an included boundary cell solves the original existential formula and returns an attaining algebraic witness \((p,\mathbf K)\).  Thus every reported PSACE boundary is attained.

The incompatibility marker is handled separately by the closed formula
\[
 B_d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\;\bigl[p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\ \wedge\ 
 \neg\exists\mathbf K\;\mathbf K\in
 \mathfrak K_d(p_{\cdot0},p_{\cdot1})\bigr].
\]
This is again a rational first-order formula.  By \(\texttt{def:confidence-objects}\), \(B_d\) is true exactly when \(\bot_{\mathrm{inc}}\) occurs in at least one projected fiber, so quantifier elimination decides the marker exactly.

It remains to profile the extended-real sign radius.  Define
\[
 F_g(p,r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\mathbf K\;\bigl[
 \mathbf K\in\mathfrak K_r(p_{\cdot0},p_{\cdot1})\ \wedge\ 
 N_g(\mathbf K;p)\le0\bigr]
\]
and \(F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,r)=\bigvee_{g\in\mathcal G}F_g(p,r)\).  These too are rational polynomial formulas.  For fixed \(p\), the set of pairs \((r,\mathbf K)\) satisfying a particular \(F_g\), with \(0\le r\le1\), is closed in a compact product.  Its projection onto \(r\) is therefore compact.  A finite union over \(g\) is compact as well.  Hence, if \(F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,r)\) holds for some \(r\), the least such \(r\) is attained.  Since \(\mathcal G\) is finite, that least value is
\[
 \min_{g\in\mathcal G}\inf\{r\in[0,1]:F_g(p,r)\}
 =\delta_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{all}}(p)
\]
by \(\texttt{def:radius-objects}\).  It follows that
\[
\begin{split}
 R(r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists p;[\,&p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa},\ 
 0\le r\le1,\ F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,r),\\
 &\forall s\,((0\le s\wedge s<r)\Longrightarrow
 \neg F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,s))]
\end{split}
\]
defines exactly \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\cap[0,1]\).  Applying \(\texttt{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\) to \(R\) and isolating its one-dimensional boundary roots returns a finite union of real-algebraic points and intervals.  This projected value set need not be closed, so the sign decomposition, rather than compactness, supplies the exact open or closed flag of each endpoint.

For any two probability rows, total variation is at most one.  Moreover \(\mathfrak K_r\) is nested in \(r\).  Therefore a finite sign-reversal radius exists for a given \(p\) if and only if \(F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,1)\) holds.  The formula
\[
 I_{\infty}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\;[p\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}^{\kappa}\ \wedge\ 
 \neg F_{\mathrm{all}}(p,1)]
\]
is consequently true exactly when \(+\infty\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{all}}\), and its truth is decided by the same elimination procedure.

If the confidence region is empty, every existential projection above is empty and both marker formulas are false, as required.  All original inequalities are retained as weak inequalities.  By \(\texttt{lem:effective-real-quantifier-elimination}\), cylindrical algebraic decomposition terminates after finitely many sign-invariant cells and retains zero-sign cells.  Thus ties among active cuts, compatibility contacts, and sign-radius boundaries require no generic-position assumption.  A face-complete exact completion of \(\mathsf{ProfileTV}\) is now explicit: McCormick boxes from \(\texttt{def:profiling-handle}\) may perform any finite amount of certified preliminary pruning, but CAD is run on every surviving box, or directly on the unpartitioned root box.  The latter choice alone proves finite termination and exactness; lifted witnesses certify included boundaries.

Finally, \(\texttt{thm:simultaneous-region-coverage}\) already proves that projection of the maintained region has the asserted simultaneous finite-sample interpretation, including ties, incompatibility, and \(+\infty\).  The construction above computes those very projections exactly and does not alter their coverage event.  This completes every part of the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This turns the bilinear confidence projection into an exact, tie-safe finite computation over rational inputs. \textit{Gap.} McCormick1976 supplies relaxations, whereas exact real-algebraic elimination certifies every surviving boundary and marker. \textit{Consumer.} For rational numerical inputs, a simultaneous ACCT analysis can archive exact real-algebraic endpoint witnesses and exact incompatibility or no-reversal markers; a practical implementation and the numerical ACCT reanalysis remain open.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Varying \(p_{g0}(x)\) jointly with \(k_{gx}(z)\) creates bilinear products. Exact finite global profiling nevertheless exists by cylindrical algebraic decomposition for rational numerical inputs, including active-cut ties, compatibility boundaries, sign-radius boundaries, and the \(+\infty\) case. This establishes termination and exactness, not practically tractable complexity: an implementation, useful complexity guarantees, and the numerical ACCT reanalysis remain open. The randomized-arm marginals identify the compatibility threshold \(\rho^*\), not the latent rowwise distance or a scientifically credible upper sensitivity radius.

\section{Honest scope}

Centers and trial rows are primitive unrestricted simplex points for every control state; a trial row is interpreted as a conditional law only on control states with positive mass. No latent entry floor is imposed. Randomization and SUTVA consistency identify the potential-state marginals with the observed arm laws, and denominator positivity comes only from the maintained observable first stage. Compatibility uses the direct support LP. The radius \(d\) bounds latent cross-world rows, is not identified from randomized-arm marginals, and is prespecified for sensitivity analysis. The practical regime is a collection of commensurate trials with a common intervention, common endpoint definitions, and baseline-restricted populations; protocol or covariate similarity may motivate elicitation but does not identify or bound rowwise TV. The full endpoint and sign-robustness path is reported over \(d\in[0,1]\), with incompatible radii retained and \(\rho^*\) interpreted only as an observable-data compatibility diagnostic. On this note's \(m=4\), full-rank, positive-first-stage subclass, the \(d=0\) restriction specializes to Wu--Mao Assumption 5's transition restriction; the entire maintained classes are not claimed to be identical. The strict positive-radius result is limited to the explicitly defined observable neighborhood and \(0<d\le10^{-2}\). Fixed-margin endpoints, primal--dual sign certificates, and simultaneous coverage are theorem results. Exact finite bilinear confidence-region profiling exists by cylindrical algebraic decomposition for rational numerical inputs; practically tractable complexity, implementation, and the numerical ACCT reanalysis remain open.
