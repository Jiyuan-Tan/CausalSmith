Put \(\delta=2^{-p_n-1}\), and let \(\mathcal B\) be the coordinate box of radius \(\delta\) about \((\widetilde X,\widetilde R,\widetilde C)\). We construct the certificate and verify each assertion.

First, there is a computable coarse enclosure that remains valid when no singular subspace can be separated. Let \(T\) be a directed-rounding upper bound for the absolute values of all coordinates in \(\mathcal B\). At every point of \(\mathcal B\) at which \(G_n\) holds, the retained inverse \(Y=X_r^+\) obeys
\[
\|Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|D_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le \frac{2}{cn}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, orthonormality of the retained singular-vector matrices gives
\[
\|Z_{p,n}\|_2\le \frac{\sqrt nT}{\sigma},
\qquad
\|Z_{q,n}\|_2\le \frac{\sqrt nT}{\sigma}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since every singular value of \(D_n/\|D_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) is at most one, the definitions of \(s_{Z,n}\), \(h_n\), and \(t_{Z,n}\), followed by (2), yield
\[
t_{Z,n}\le
\sqrt{\|Z_{p,n}\|_2^2+\|Z_{q,n}\|_2^2}
\le \overline t:=\frac{\sqrt{2n}T}{\sigma}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(t\ge0\), \(\beta_\alpha(t)=\alpha/[4(1+t)]\) is decreasing. Therefore \(1-[\alpha-\beta_\alpha(t)]/2\) is decreasing, and monotonicity of \(\Phi^{-1}\) shows that \(\zeta_\alpha\) is decreasing. In particular,
\[
0<\zeta_\alpha(t)\le z_{\max}:=\Phi^{-1}(1-3\alpha/8).
\tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
\overline R=
\sqrt{C_{r,\alpha}\log(e+2\overline t+A_{r,\alpha})}
\]
and
\[
\overline W=\frac{2}{cn}
\left\{
z_{\max}(\overline t+\overline R)
+\frac{C_{r,\alpha}}2\log(e+\overline t+\overline R)
\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(t_{Z,n}\le\overline t\), the definitions of \(R_{0,n}\), \(t_{-,n}\), and \(t_{+,n}\), together with (1) and (4), imply \(W_n\le\overline W\). Also, by (1),
\[
|\widehat\theta_n|=|RYC|
\le \|R\|_2\|Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|C\|_2
\le \frac{2T^2}{c},
\tag{6}
\]
and the explicit donor allowance satisfies
\[
d_n\le \overline d:=K_0\sigma^2\ell_n n^{-2}
\left(\frac{\sqrt nT}{\sigma}+\sqrt{\ell_n}\right)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently, every on-gate exact interval generated by a point of \(\mathcal B\) lies in
\[
[-\overline H,\overline H],
\qquad
\overline H:=\frac{2T^2}{c}+\sigma^2\overline W+\overline d,
\tag{8}
\]
whereas every off-gate exact interval is \([-M_0,M_0]\). Directed rational majorants of the right sides of (2)--(8) are obtained using \(\log(e+x)\le 1+x\), \(\sqrt{x}\le1+x\), and certified outward evaluation of the fixed quantile in (4). Thus the outward hull of the intervals in (8) and \([-M_0,M_0]\) is a finite, computable fallback containing \(I_n^{\mathrm{out}}(X,R,C)\) for every \((X,R,C)\in\mathcal B\). This is the branch that makes termination independent of a spectral gap.

We next describe the sharp branch. For a particular matrix \(X\), suppose a certified rational \(\tau\) lies strictly between the squared retained and discarded singular values, and define
\[
P_R={\bf 1}_{(\tau,\infty)}(X^{\top}X),
\qquad
P_L={\bf 1}_{(\tau,\infty)}(XX^{\top}).
\]
When the certified count above \(\tau\) is \(r\),
\[
X_r=P_LXP_R,
\qquad
X_r^+=X_r^{\top}
\bigl(X_rX_r^{\top}+I-P_L\bigr)^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, write a compact singular-value decomposition \(X_r=U_rS_rV_r^{\top}\). Then \(P_L=U_rU_r^{\top}\), and \(X_rX_r^{\top}+I-P_L\) has eigenvalues \(s_j(X)^2\) on \(\operatorname{range}(P_L)\) and eigenvalue one on its orthogonal complement. Its inverse is therefore \(U_rS_r^{-2}U_r^{\top}+I-P_L\). Left multiplication by \(X_r^{\top}=V_rS_rU_r^{\top}\) proves (9).

Both projectors can be enclosed without choosing individual singular vectors. For example, put \(H_R=X^{\top}X-\tau I\), choose a certified power of two \(a\ge\|H_R\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), and apply
\[
S_0=H_R/a,
\qquad
S_{j+1}=\frac12(S_j+S_j^{-1}).
\tag{10}
\]
For a nonzero real scalar \(x\), direct algebra shows that the Cayley ratio
\[
\frac{x-\operatorname{sign}(x)}{x+\operatorname{sign}(x)}
\]
is squared by one Newton step \(x\mapsto(x+x^{-1})/2\). Functional calculus for the symmetric matrix \(H_R\) therefore shows that (10) converges first geometrically and then quadratically to \(\operatorname{sign}(H_R)\) whenever a nonzero signed spectral gap has been certified, and
\[
P_R=\frac12\{I+\operatorname{sign}(H_R)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The same construction gives \(P_L\). Every inverse in (9)--(10) is accepted only after a directed residual enclosure establishes
\[
\|I-K\widetilde K^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1.
\tag{12}
\]
Writing \(H=I-K\widetilde K^{-1}\), the absolutely convergent geometric identity
\[
(I-H)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}H^j,
\qquad
\left\|\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}H^j\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\frac{\|H\|_{mathrm{op}}^m}{1-\|H\|_{mathrm{op}}}
\tag{13}
\]
gives an explicit outward enclosure of \(K^{-1}=\widetilde K^{-1}(I-H)^{-1}\). If (12), the spectral count, or the separation from \(\tau\) has not been certified by the prescribed precision cap, the algorithm takes the coarse branch. Thus a zero or extremely small gap cannot cause nontermination.

Equations (9)--(11) depend only on the whole retained invariant subspaces. A repeated singular value inside the retained block changes neither \(P_L\), \(P_R\), nor \(X_r^+\). Furthermore, all quantities entering the exact interval are invariant under an orthogonal change of retained singular-vector basis, since
\[
\widehat\theta_n=RX_r^+C,
\qquad
s_{Z,n}=\sigma^{-1}
\sqrt{\|X_r^+C\|_2^2+
\|(X_r^+)^{\top}R^{\top}\|_2^2},
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
\|Z_{p,n}\|_2=\|P_RR^{\top}\|_2/\sigma,
\qquad
\|Z_{q,n}\|_2=\|P_LC\|_2/\sigma.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus the sharp certificate remains valid at repeated retained singular values. A tie across the retained--discarded boundary is sent to the coarse branch, whose hull contains the interval selected by every fixed measurable convention.

All scalar functions can be enclosed constructively. The identities
\[
\Phi(x)=\frac12+\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_0^x e^{-t^2/2}\,dt
\tag{16}
\]
and, by integrating the gamma density for the even integer degree \(2r\),
\[
F_{\chi^2_{2r}}(x)
=1-e^{-x/2}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{(x/2)^j}{j!},
\qquad x\ge0,
\tag{17}
\]
reduce evaluation to rational arithmetic, exponentials, square roots, and a finite sum. After binary range reduction, the exponential series with the elementary remainder estimate
\[
\left|e^y-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{y^j}{j!}\right|
\le \frac{e^{|y|}|y|^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}
\tag{18}
\]
and interval Newton steps for square roots give directed intervals of \(s\) bits using a number of bit operations polynomial in \(s\) and the binary encoding length of the argument. The Gaussian exponential-moment calculation
\[
\Pr\{Z\ge x\}
\le\inf_{t>0}e^{-tx+t^2/2}=e^{-x^2/2},
\qquad x\ge0,
\tag{19}
\]
gives a normal-quantile bracket of size \(O(\sqrt{1+\log(1/\alpha)})\). Formula (17) gives a chi-square-quantile bracket of size
\[
O_{r_0}\bigl(1+\log(1/\gamma)\bigr)
\tag{20}
\]
at tail probability \(\gamma\): after taking \(x\) to be a sufficiently large fixed multiple, depending only on \(r_0\), of \(1+\log(1/\gamma)\), every term in the finite sum in (17) is bounded by \(\gamma/r_0\).

On the relevant finite brackets the normal and chi-square densities are positive. Monotone interval bisection, increasing the function-evaluation precision whenever the comparison intervals overlap the target probability, consequently returns an interval containing either requested quantile \(Q\) and having width at most
\[
8\,2^{1-q_n}(1+|Q|).
\tag{21}
\]
The needed number of probability bits is bounded by a constant multiple of \(q_n+\log(1/\alpha)+\log(1+t)\). For the normal quantile this follows from positivity of the density and the elementary tail comparison implicit in (19); for the chi-square quantile it follows directly from the product of \(e^{-x/2}\) and a degree-\((r-1)\) polynomial in (17). Because \(r\le r_0\) is fixed, the constants are uniform in \(r\). Binary range reduction stores every possibly large scale in the arbitrary-precision signed exponent, so complexity is polynomial in the encoding length of that exponent, rather than in its numerical value.

If a public control is not dyadic, its polynomial-time refinable name is queried to the extra precision demanded by the preceding comparison tests. This representation is enough because each query at precision \(s\) has endpoint length and computation time polynomial in \(s\) and \(L_n\). A fixed nonrefinable interval would not be enough. Indeed, when \(r=1\), (17) gives
\[
F_{\chi^2_2}^{-1}(1-\gamma)=-2\log\gamma.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus an interval that allows \(\gamma\) to range from \(2^{-2^L}\) to a fixed positive number has endpoints of encoding length \(O(L)\), but its image under the quantile map has diameter of order \(2^L\). This verifies the role of the representation premise in the theorem.

Choose the deterministic working-precision cap
\[
q_n^\star=
8\bigl(n+L_n+p_n+\lceil\log_2(1/\alpha)\rceil+1\bigr)^2.
\tag{23}
\]
At successively doubled precisions no larger than \(q_n^\star\), the algorithm attempts to certify one of two sharp cases over the entire box \(\mathcal B\): either every donor matrix is off the gate, or every donor matrix is on the gate and admits a common retained spectral split. In the first case it returns the outward-rounded fallback. In the second it encloses (9), (14)--(17), all calibration expressions, and both exact-real endpoints by directed arithmetic. In every unresolved case it returns the coarse hull from (8). Hence it always terminates after at most \(O(\log q_n^\star)\) precision doublings.

The number of matrix operations at each precision is polynomial in \(n\). Whenever (10) is used, the certified gap and the scalar Cayley-ratio calculation show that it takes polynomially many iterations at the capped bit precision; otherwise the attempt is abandoned for the coarse branch. Equations (16)--(21) give polynomial-time quantile enclosures. Every mantissa has at most \(q_n^\star\) bits, and every dynamically stored exponent has polynomial encoding length by the directed-rounding model. The refinable public controls are queried only at polynomial precision. Therefore the total bit complexity and the successful working precision are polynomial in
\[
n,quad L_n,quad p_n,quad
\lceil\log_2(1/\alpha)\rceil.
\tag{24}
\]

It remains to verify the claimed deterministic inclusion and length certificate. Fix any \((X,R,C)\in\mathcal B\), and write its exact-real interval as
\[
I_n^{\mathrm{out}}(X,R,C)=[L(X,R,C),U(X,R,C)].
\]
Let the returned interval be \(I_n^{\mathrm{dyad}}=[\underline L,\overline U]\). In a sharp branch, inclusion-monotonicity of directed interval operations, the inverse residual bound (13), the quantile enclosure (21), and outward endpoint rounding give
\[
\underline L\le L(X,R,C)\le U(X,R,C)\le\overline U.
\tag{25}
\]
In an unresolved branch, (25) follows from the coarse hull proved in (8). The accept--reject--uncertain gate is sound because acceptance or rejection occurs only after interval bounds separate the relevant singular values from their gate thresholds; overlap invokes the hull, which contains both possible branch values.

Define \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{enc}}\) to be the largest certified outward displacement of either endpoint caused by replacing the point input by its coordinate and public-control boxes, including the displacement to the gate hull when a box makes the gate uncertain. Define \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{arith}}\) to be the sum of the additional nonnegative outward radii certified for (9)--(21), the scalar calibration, gate classification at finite precision, and final endpoint rounding. These are precisely the two records required by the certified handle and numerical-padding definitions. Inclusion-monotonicity and the triangle inequality for successive outward enlargements give, endpoint by endpoint,
\[
0\le L(X,R,C)-\underline L
\le\Delta_n^{\mathrm{enc}}+\Delta_n^{\mathrm{arith}},
\]
\[
0\le\overline U-U(X,R,C)
\le\Delta_n^{\mathrm{enc}}+\Delta_n^{\mathrm{arith}}.
\tag{26}
\]
Adding (26) and using
\[
\operatorname{length}(I_n^{\mathrm{dyad}})
-\operatorname{length}(I_n^{\mathrm{out}})
=\{L-\underline L\}+\{\overline U-U\}
\]
proves
\[
0\le
\operatorname{length}(I_n^{\mathrm{dyad}})
-\operatorname{length}(I_n^{\mathrm{out}}(X,R,C))
\le2(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{enc}}+\Delta_n^{\mathrm{arith}}).
\tag{27}
\]

Finally, arbitrary-precision signed exponents exclude overflow and subnormal values in \(\mathbb F_n^{\mathrm{dir}}\) by definition. For an IEEE 754 backend, exact power-of-two rescaling preserves the represented real matrix and all singular subspaces; the certificate is emitted only after widening the exponent range enough to rule out overflow and subnormal endpoints, and any overflow, subnormal, or flush-to-zero flag forces rejection. Thus no exceptional floating-point state is silently accepted. Equations (1)--(27) establish termination, polynomial bit complexity, exact containment, the excess-length bound, repeated-singular-value validity, certified quantile computation, the uncertain-gate fallback, and exceptional-state safety, completing the proof.
