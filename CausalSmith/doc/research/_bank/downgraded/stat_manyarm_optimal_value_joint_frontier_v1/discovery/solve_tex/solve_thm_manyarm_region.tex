%%% FIELD proof-poisson-lift
Fix an arm \(a\).  Introduce the exponential generating function
\[
 \mathcal H_a(u):=\sum_{h\geq 0}H_h\frac{u^h}{h!}
 =\left(1+\frac{u}{2\lambda}\right)^{N_{a1}}
  \left(1-\frac{u}{2\lambda}\right)^{N_{a0}}.
\]
The equality is coefficientwise exactly the construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}polynomial\text{-}lift}\).  The two Poisson means in the balanced experiment are \(\lambda(1/2+\theta_a)\) and \(\lambda(1/2-\theta_a)\).  Hence the Poisson probability generating function gives, in a neighborhood of zero,
\[
 E_\theta[\mathcal H_a(u)]
 =\exp\!\left\{\lambda(1/2+\theta_a)\frac{u}{2\lambda}
                 -\lambda(1/2-\theta_a)\frac{u}{2\lambda}\right\}
 =e^{\theta_a u}.
\]
Coefficient comparison yields \(E_\theta[H_h]=\theta_a^h\).  The same calculation with two indeterminates gives
\[
 E_\theta[\mathcal H_a(u)\mathcal H_a(v)]
 =\exp\!\left\{\theta_a(u+v)+\frac{uv}{4\lambda}\right\}.
\]
All coefficient extractions and expectations may be interchanged because only finitely many coefficients are needed and Poisson variables have moments of every order.  Therefore
\[
 E_\theta[H_hH_t]
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{h,t\}}
   \binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!(4\lambda)^{-\ell}
   \theta_a^{h+t-2\ell}.
\]

For a multi-index \(\gamma\), write \(H_\gamma=\prod_{a=1}^K H_{\gamma_a}\).  Independence across arms and the preceding identity imply
\[
 E_\theta[H_\gamma H_\delta]
 =\sum_{\alpha\leq\gamma\wedge\delta}
 (4\lambda)^{-|\alpha|}\alpha!
 \binom\gamma\alpha\binom\delta\alpha
 \theta^{\gamma+\delta-2\alpha}.
\]
Using the finite representation \(P_L(\theta)=\sum_\gamma c_\gamma\theta^\gamma\), the first-moment identity gives
\[
 E_\theta[U_{P_L}]=\sum_\gamma c_\gamma\theta^\gamma=P_L(\theta).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 D^\alpha P_L(\theta)
 =\sum_{\gamma\geq\alpha}c_\gamma\,\alpha!\binom\gamma\alpha
   \theta^{\gamma-\alpha}.
\]
Substitution into the preceding second-moment formula and regrouping the finite sums gives
\[
 E_\theta[U_{P_L}^2]
 =\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^K}
   \frac{(4\lambda)^{-|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}
   \{D^\alpha P_L(\theta)\}^2.
\]
The term \(\alpha=0\) is \(P_L(\theta)^2\).  Subtracting the squared expectation proves the stated variance identity.  Derivatives of order above \(L\) vanish, so the displayed sum is finite.  The coefficient table has \(M_{K,L}\) entries and the lift is the finite executable map specified in \(\mathrm{def{:}polynomial\text{-}lift}\).

%%% FIELD proof-mixture-kernel
For \(\theta\in[-1/4,1/4]^K\), all Poisson means are positive.  Relative to \(P_0\), cancellation of the Poisson exponential factors gives the likelihood ratio
\[
 \Lambda_\theta:=\frac{dP_\theta}{dP_0}
 =\prod_{a=1}^K(1+2\theta_a)^{N_{a1}}(1-2\theta_a)^{N_{a0}}.
\]
For \(\theta,\eta\in[-1/4,1/4]^K\), the Poisson probability generating function and independence over arms give
\[
 E_0[\Lambda_\theta\Lambda_\eta]
 =\prod_{a=1}^K
   \exp\!\left\{\frac\lambda2\bigl((1+2\theta_a)(1+2\eta_a)-1\bigr)
                   +\frac\lambda2\bigl((1-2\theta_a)(1-2\eta_a)-1\bigr)\right\}
 =e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}.
\]
Let \(\Delta=\nu_1-\nu_0\).  Normalization of the two priors gives \(\Delta([-1/4,1/4]^K)=0\).  By mixture linearity,
\[
 \frac{dP_{\nu_1}}{dP_0}-\frac{dP_{\nu_0}}{dP_0}
 =\int \Lambda_\theta\,d\Delta(\theta).
\]
The kernel \(e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}\) is bounded on the compact product cube.  Thus Fubini's theorem applies to the two finite signed measures, and
\[
 \left\|\frac{dP_{\nu_1}}{dP_0}-\frac{dP_{\nu_0}}{dP_0}\right\|_{L^2(P_0)}^2
 =\iint e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}\,d\Delta(\theta)d\Delta(\eta).
\]
The exponential series is absolutely and uniformly convergent on that product cube.  Its multi-index expansion therefore may be integrated term by term:
\[
 e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}
 =\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^K}
   \frac{(4\lambda)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\theta^\alpha\eta^\alpha.
\]
Consequently the last double integral equals
\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^K}
 \frac{(4\lambda)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}
 \left\{\int\theta^\alpha\,d(\nu_1-\nu_0)(\theta)\right\}^2
 =\mathfrak D_{K,\lambda}(\nu_0,\nu_1).
\]
The term of degree zero is already zero by normalization.  If all mixed moments through total degree \(L\) agree, precisely the terms with \(1\leq|\alpha|\leq L\) vanish.  No condition has been imposed on moments of larger total degree, so their nonnegative weighted tail remains exactly as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-ratio-max
Fix a patient group \(x\), and put \(q_{ay}=q_{xay}\).  By the definitions of the observed cell probabilities,
\[
 s(q)=p_x,\quad s_a(q)=p_xe_{ax},\quad q_{a1}=p_xe_{ax}\mu_{ax}.
\]
If \(p_x=0\), then \(q=0\), and both \(F_K(q)\) and \(p_xm_x\) are zero.  If \(p_x>0\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}menu\text{-}scaled\text{-}overlap}\) gives \(s_a(q)\geq s(q)/(2K)\).  Hence the denominator in \(\mathrm{def{:}ratio\text{-}max\text{-}extension}\) is \(s_a(q)\), and
\[
 F_K(q)=\max_{a\in[K]}\frac{p_x\,p_xe_{ax}\mu_{ax}}{p_xe_{ax}}
       =p_x\max_{a\in[K]}\mu_{ax}=p_xm_x.
\]
Every division here is legitimate because \(p_xe_{ax}>0\).  This proves agreement with the identified contribution on every positive-mass cell.

For the extension properties, write
\[
 f_a(q)=\frac{s(q)q_{a1}}{\max\{s_a(q),s(q)/(2K)\}},
 \quad F_K(q)=\max_a f_a(q)
\]
for \(q\neq0\).  Positive homogeneity of numerator and denominator gives \(f_a(cq)=cf_a(q)\) for \(c\geq0\), including \(c=0\) by the declared convention.  Since \(q_{a1}\leq s_a(q)\leq\max\{s_a(q),s(q)/(2K)\}\),
\[
 0\leq f_a(q)\leq s(q),
\]
and the same bounds hold for their maximum.

It remains to prove the global Lipschitz statement.  On the half-space \(s_a(q)\leq s(q)/(2K)\), one has \(f_a(q)=2Kq_{a1}\), whose gradient has \(\ell_\infty\)-norm \(2K\).  On the other half-space, put \(z=q_{a1}\) and \(t=s_a(q)>0\).  Then \(f_a(q)=s(q)z/t\), with \(z/t\leq1\) and \(s(q)/t\leq2K\).  A coordinate outside arm \(a\) has derivative \(z/t\); the success coordinate has derivative
\[
 \frac zt+\frac{s(q)}t\left(1-\frac zt\right),
\]
and the failure coordinate has derivative
\[
 \frac zt-\frac{s(q)}t\frac zt.
\]
Each absolute derivative is at most \(1+2K\).  The two formulas for \(f_a\) agree on their common boundary.  A line segment in the nonnegative orthant crosses that linear boundary at most once unless it lies in it; integrating the directional derivative on the resulting subsegments gives
\[
 |f_a(q)-f_a(q')|\leq(1+2K)\|q-q'\|_1
\]
whenever neither endpoint is the origin.  If one endpoint is the origin, the same inequality follows from \(f_a(q)\leq s(q)=\|q\|_1\).  Finally,
\[
 |F_K(q)-F_K(q')|
 \leq\max_a|f_a(q)-f_a(q')|
 \leq(1+2K)\|q-q'\|_1.
\]
Only the lower-overlap inequality was used in the agreement step; all extension properties hold on the whole nonnegative orthant.  No upper bound on \(e_{ax}\) enters.

%%% FIELD proof-observed-cell
For one coordinate \(j\), form the exponential generating function
\[
 \mathcal G_j(u):=\sum_{h\geq0}G_h(N_j;z_j,m)\frac{u^h}{h!}
 =e^{-z_ju}\left(1+\frac um\right)^{N_j}.
\]
The equality follows by multiplying the series for \(e^{-z_ju}\) and the falling-factorial expansion of \((1+u/m)^{N_j}\), and is coefficientwise the definition in \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}cell\text{-}factorial\text{-}lift}\).  Since \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(mq_j)\),
\[
 E_q[\mathcal G_j(u)]=e^{-z_ju}e^{q_ju}=e^{(q_j-z_j)u}.
\]
Thus \(E_q[G_h]=(q_j-z_j)^h\).  Likewise,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_q[\mathcal G_j(u)\mathcal G_j(v)]
 &=e^{-z_j(u+v)}
   \exp\!\left(mq_j\left[\left(1+\frac um\right)
                                 \left(1+\frac vm\right)-1\right]\right)\\
 &=\exp\!\left((q_j-z_j)(u+v)+\frac{q_j}{m}uv\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Coefficient comparison gives exactly
\[
 E_q[G_hG_t]
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{h,t\}}
   \binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!\,(q_j/m)^\ell
   (q_j-z_j)^{h+t-2\ell}.
\]
The required interchanges are finite coefficient calculations, and all Poisson factorial moments are finite.

For \(G_\alpha=\prod_{j=1}^{2K}G_{\alpha_j}(N_j;z_j,m)\), independence across the \(2K\) coordinates gives
\[
 E_q[G_\alpha]=(q-z)^\alpha
\]
and
\[
 E_q[G_\alpha G_\beta]
 =\prod_{j=1}^{2K}\left[
   \sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\}}
   \binom{\alpha_j}\ell\binom{\beta_j}\ell\ell!
   (q_j/m)^\ell(q_j-z_j)^{\alpha_j+\beta_j-2\ell}
 \right].
\]
Using the finite coefficient table in the definition therefore yields
\[
 E_q[U_{Q_L}]=\sum_\alpha\gamma_\alpha(q-z)^\alpha=Q_L(q-z),
\]
and the fully explicit finite variance
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_q(U_{Q_L})
 =\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A_{2K,L}}\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta
 \Bigg\{&\prod_{j=1}^{2K}\left[
   \sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\}}
   \binom{\alpha_j}\ell\binom{\beta_j}\ell\ell!
   (q_j/m)^\ell(q_j-z_j)^{\alpha_j+\beta_j-2\ell}
 \right]\\
 &-(q-z)^{\alpha+\beta}\Bigg\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the asserted finite double sum and includes every cross-monomial covariance.

%%% FIELD proof-or
Let \(p\) be a real polynomial on \(\mathbb R^K\) satisfying
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\{-1/4,1/4\}^K}
 \left|p(\theta)-\max_{a\in[K]}\theta_a\right|\leq\frac1{56}.
\]
Use the signed Boolean convention in which the OR function is \(+1\) only at the all-\(+1\) input and is \(-1\) otherwise.  For \(x\in\{\pm1\}^K\), define the proof-intermediate polynomial
\[
 r(x)=-4p(-x/4).
\]
If every coordinate of \(x\) is \(+1\), then \(\max_a(-x_a/4)=-1/4\); otherwise at least one coordinate equals \(-1\), and that maximum is \(+1/4\).  Therefore
\[
 -4\max_a(-x_a/4)=\operatorname{OR}_K(x)
\]
under the stated signed convention, and hence
\[
 \sup_{x\in\{\pm1\}^K}|r(x)-\operatorname{OR}_K(x)|\leq4/56=1/14.
\]
Affine substitution and scalar multiplication do not increase total degree, so \(\deg r\leq\deg p\).  The cited lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}spalek\text{-}or\text{-}approximate\text{-}degree}\) says that such an approximant has degree at least \(\sqrt K\).  Thus \(\deg p\geq\sqrt K\), proving both conclusions.

%%% FIELD proof-empirical-upper
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{d,K}\).  For a group with \(N_x=k>0\), define
\[
 Z_x=\max_{a\in[K]}|\overline Y_{xa}-\mu_{ax}|.
\]
Conditional on \(N_x=k\), the arm count \(H=N_{xa}\) is \(\operatorname{Bin}(k,e_{ax})\).  The elementary exponential-moment calculation for this binomial variable gives
\[
 P(H<ke_{ax}/2\mid N_x=k)\leq e^{-ke_{ax}/8}.
\]
Indeed, Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-(\log2)H}\), followed by \(1+u\leq e^u\), gives exponent
\(ke_{ax}\{(\log2)/2-1/2\}\leq-ke_{ax}/8\).

Given \(H=h>0\), the observed outcomes in that arm are independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(\mu_{ax}\).  For a Bernoulli variable \(W\) of mean \(\mu\), direct differentiation of
\(e^{-s\mu}(1-\mu+\mu e^s)\) gives
\(E[e^{s(W-\mu)}]\leq e^{s^2/8}\).  Markov's inequality, optimization over \(s\), and the same argument for \(-s\) therefore give
\[
 P(|\overline Y_{xa}-\mu_{ax}|>t\mid N_x=k,H=h)
 \leq2e^{-2ht^2}.
\]
For \(0<t\leq1\), splitting according to \(H<ke_{ax}/2\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(|\overline Y_{xa}-\mu_{ax}|>t\mid N_x=k)
 &\leq e^{-ke_{ax}/8}+2e^{-ke_{ax}t^2}\\
 &\leq3e^{-ke_{ax}t^2/8}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This also covers the declared value at \(H=0\), because that event belongs to \(\{H<ke_{ax}/2\}\).  By the union bound and \(e_{ax}\geq1/(2K)\),
\[
 P(Z_x>t\mid N_x=k)
 \leq\min\left\{1,3K\exp\!\left(-\frac{kt^2}{16K}\right)\right\}.
\]
Since \(0\leq Z_x\leq1\), integration of this tail with \(s=t^2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E[Z_x^2\mid N_x=k]
 &=\int_0^1P(Z_x>\sqrt s\mid N_x=k)\,ds\\
 &\leq C_0\min\left\{1,\frac{K\log(eK)}k\right\}
 \end{aligned}
\]
for an absolute \(C_0\).  To see the last inequality directly, split the integral at
\(16K\log(3K)/k\) when this number is below one; the remaining exponential integral is at most \(16K/k\).

By \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}empirical\text{-}maximum}\), decompose
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{ER}}-V^\star(P)=E_1+E_2,
\]
where
\[
 E_1=\sum_{x=1}^d\frac{N_x}{n}
 \left(\max_a\overline Y_{xa}-m_x\right),
 \quad
 E_2=\sum_{x=1}^d\left(\frac{N_x}{n}-p_x\right)m_x.
\]
The maximum map is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm, so the absolute value of the parenthesized term in \(E_1\) is at most \(Z_x\).  Since the nonnegative weights \(N_x/n\) sum to one, Jensen's inequality and the conditional bound above give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[E_1^2]
 &\leq\sum_{x=1}^d E_P\left[\frac{N_x}{n}Z_x^2\right]\\
 &\leq\frac{C_0}{n}\sum_{x=1}^d K\log(eK)
 =\frac{C_0dK\log(eK)}n.
\end{aligned}
\]
The zero-count groups contribute zero, so no conditioning at \(k=0\) or division by zero occurs.  Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}sampling}\),
\[
 E_2=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{m_{X_i}-E_P[m_X]\}.
\]
Because \(0\leq m_X\leq1\), its variance is at most \(1/(4n)\).  Thus \((E_1+E_2)^2\leq2E_1^2+2E_2^2\) implies
\[
 E_{P^n}[(\widehat V_{\mathrm{ER}}-V^\star(P))^2]
 \leq C_1\frac{dK\log(eK)}n.
\]
Both the estimator and target lie in \([0,1]\), so their squared difference is also at most one.  Combining the two bounds, taking the supremum over \(P\), and recalling \(u(d,K)=dK\log(eK)\) proves the result with an absolute constant.  The proof used neither \(p_x>0\) for every group nor an upper bound, known value, or estimator of \(e_{ax}\); ties and boundary means were never excluded.

%%% FIELD proof-centered-winner
It suffices by \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}risk}\) to construct a legal finite subexperiment.  Put \(p_x=1/d\) and \(e_{ax}=1/K\).  Under a proof-intermediate null law \(Q_0\), let every conditional outcome mean equal \(1/2\).  For each winner vector \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_d)\in[K]^d\) and \(0<\delta\leq1/4\), define an alternative law \(Q_{\mathbf j}\) by
\[
 \mu^{\mathbf j}_{ax}=\frac12+\eta_{\mathbf j,x,a},\quad
 \eta_{\mathbf j,x,a}=
 \begin{cases}
 \delta,&a=j_x,\\
 -\delta/(K-1),&a\neq j_x.
 \end{cases}
\]
These means belong to \([0,1]\), and their average over arms is exactly \(1/2\) in every group.  A full-data completion is obtained by drawing the potential outcomes conditionally on \(X=x\) as Bernoulli variables with these means, drawing \(A\) uniformly and independently of all potential outcomes conditional on \(X\), and setting \(Y=Y(A)\); give \(Q_0\) the same completion with all means equal to \(1/2\).  Every resulting law satisfies consistency, conditional exchangeability, and the overlap equality \(e_{ax}=1/K\geq1/(2K)\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal M_{d,K}\).  By \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\),
\[
 V^\star(Q_0)=\frac12,\quad V^\star(Q_{\mathbf j})=\frac12+\delta
\]
for every \(\mathbf j\).

For one observation, the likelihood ratio of \(Q_{\mathbf j}\) relative to \(Q_0\) at \((x,a,y)\) is
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathbf j}(x,a,y)=1+2(2y-1)\eta_{\mathbf j,x,a}.
\]
If \(h(\mathbf j,\mathbf l)=|\{x:j_x=l_x\}|\), direct summation over the \(2dK\) categories gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{Q_0}[\Lambda_{\mathbf j}\Lambda_{\mathbf l}]
 &=1+\frac4{dK}\sum_{x=1}^d\sum_{a=1}^K
      \eta_{\mathbf j,x,a}\eta_{\mathbf l,x,a}\\
 &=1+\frac{4\delta^2\{K h(\mathbf j,\mathbf l)-d\}}
               {d(K-1)^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the inner product in a coincident-winner group is \(\delta^2K/(K-1)\), while in a different-winner group it is \(-\delta^2K/(K-1)^2\).  Let
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{mix}}=K^{-d}\sum_{\mathbf j\in[K]^d}Q_{\mathbf j}^{\,n}.
\]
For probability laws \(Q\ll P\), write
\(\chi^2(Q,P)=E_P[(dQ/dP-1)^2]\).
Independence of the observations and averaging two independent uniform winner vectors imply the exact identity
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)
 =E\left[\left(1+\frac{4\delta^2(KH-d)}{d(K-1)^2}\right)^n\right],
 \quad H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(d,1/K).
\]
The base is positive because its minimum is \(1-4\delta^2/(K-1)^2>0\).  Define
\[
 u=\frac{4n\delta^2K}{d(K-1)^2}.
\]
Using \(1+z\leq e^z\), followed by the binomial moment generating function and \(\log(1+r)\leq r\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\{1+\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)\}
 &\leq d\log\left[e^{-u/K}\left\{1+\frac{e^u-1}{K}\right\}\right]\\
 &\leq\frac dK(e^u-1-u).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Put \(w=K/d\), \(v=\log(1+\sqrt w)\), \(c_0=1/64\), and choose
\[
 \delta^2=c_0\min\left\{1,\frac{d(K-1)^2v}{nK}\right\}.
\]
Then \(\delta\leq1/8\) and, in both the saturated and unsaturated cases, \(0\leq u\leq4c_0v\).  For \(\psi(t)=e^t-1-t\), convexity and \(\psi(0)=0\) give \(\psi(4c_0v)\leq4c_0\psi(v)\).  Moreover,
\[
 \psi(v)=\sqrt w-\log(1+\sqrt w)
 =\int_0^{\sqrt w}\frac{t}{1+t}\,dt\leq\frac w2.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \log\{1+\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)\}\leq2c_0.
\]
Consequently, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)}
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{e^{2c_0}-1}=: \eta<\frac14.
\]
Here
\(\operatorname{TV}(Q,P)=\frac12\int|dQ-dP|\); the last strict inequality follows, for example, from
\(e^{1/32}\leq(1-1/32)^{-1}=32/31\).

For any estimator, threshold it at \(1/2+\delta/2\).  On a null testing error its squared estimation loss is at least \(\delta^2/4\), and the same is true on an alternative testing error.  The sum of the null error probability and the mixture-averaged alternative error probability is at least \(1-\eta\), by the defining variational inequality for total variation.  Hence the supremum risk over the constructed laws is at least
\[
 \frac{\delta^2}{8}(1-\eta).
\]
Finally,
\[
 \frac{(K-1)^2}{K^2}\geq\frac14,
 \quad
 \delta^2\geq\frac{c_0}{4}
 \min\left\{1,\frac{dK\log(1+\sqrt{K/d})}{n}\right\}.
\]
Taking the infimum over all estimators proves the asserted lower bound with an absolute constant.  The construction permits exact ties under the null and uses only a known-uniform subexperiment of the same full class.

%%% FIELD proof-manyarm
The upper inequality follows directly from \(\mathrm{lem{:}empirical\text{-}upper}\).  For the lower inequality, \(\mathrm{lem{:}centered\text{-}winner\text{-}lower}\) gives the scale
\[
 dK\log(1+\sqrt{K/d}).
\]
If \(K\geq d^2\), then \(d\leq\sqrt K\), so \(\sqrt{K/d}\geq K^{1/4}\) and
\[
 \log(1+\sqrt{K/d})\geq\frac14\log K.
\]
Since \(K\geq2\), one has \(1+\log K\leq(1+1/\log2)\log K<\frac52\log K\).  Therefore
\[
 \log(1+\sqrt{K/d})\geq\frac1{10}\log(eK).
\]
For \(0<\rho\leq1\), \(\min\{1,\rho z\}\geq\rho\min\{1,z\}\).  Applying this with \(\rho=1/10\) converts the centered-winner lower bound to the claimed lower rate.  Both constants remain absolute.

%%% FIELD proof-onegroup
Set \(d=1\) in \(\mathrm{lem{:}empirical\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}centered\text{-}winner\text{-}lower}\).  The resulting upper scale is \(K\log(eK)\), while the lower scale is \(K\log(1+\sqrt K)\).  For \(K\geq2\),
\[
 \log(1+\sqrt K)\geq\frac12\log K
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \log(eK)=1+\log K<\frac52\log K.
\]
Thus \(\log(1+\sqrt K)\geq\frac15\log(eK)\).  The elementary relation \(\min\{1,\rho z\}\geq\rho\min\{1,z\}\) for \(0<\rho\leq1\) proves the two-sided benchmark with absolute constants.  The bibliographic scope assertion in the final clause is exactly the delivery distinction recorded in \(\mathrm{lem{:}haver\text{-}upper\text{-}bound\text{-}scope}\): that work provides estimator-specific MSE upper bounds, whereas the lower inequality just proved quantifies over all estimators on the bounded-Bernoulli class.  Its easy-instance upper guarantees are not altered by this converse.

%%% FIELD proof-binary-grouping
Start with a binary observed-data law, write \(p_x=P(X=x)\), \(\pi_x=P(B=1\mid X=x)\in[1/3,2/3]\), and let \(\mu_{bx}=E[Y\mid B=b,X=x]\).  Under \(\mathsf G_K\), an arm \(a\in G_b\) has propensity
\[
 e_{ax}=\frac{P(B=b\mid X=x)}{g_b}.
\]
Both binary block probabilities are at least \(1/3\), and \(g_b\leq\lceil K/2\rceil\).  For every \(K\geq2\),
\[
 3\lceil K/2\rceil\leq2K,
\]
where the odd case uses \(K\geq3\).  Hence \(e_{ax}\geq1/(2K)\) for every arm on every positive-mass group.  The declared completion in \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}grouping\text{-}channel}\) draws arm potential outcomes with mean \(\mu_{bx}\) for \(a\in G_b\), independently of \(A\) conditional on \(X\), and sets \(Y=Y(A)\).  It therefore satisfies conditional exchangeability and consistency, as well as the overlap condition, and belongs to \(\mathcal M_{d,K}\).

Every arm in \(G_b\) has conditional outcome mean \(\mu_{bx}\).  Thus \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\) gives
\[
 V^\star(\mathsf G_KP)
 =\sum_xp_x\max_{a\in[K]}\mu_{ax}
 =\sum_xp_x\max_{b\in\{0,1\}}\mu_{bx},
\]
which is the unrestricted binary optimal value.  Conditional on \((X,B,Y)\), the additional arm label is uniform on the fixed block \(G_B\); its law is parameter-free.

Write \(O_i^{(2)}=(X_i,B_i,Y_i)\) for the \(i\)-th binary observation.  Let \(T_K\) be any estimator in the many-arm experiment.  Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase squared loss because the target is in \([0,1]\), so we may first clip \(T_K\) and thereby make it bounded.  Apply the channel independently to each binary observation and then Rao--Blackwellize over its auxiliary uniform labels:
\[
 T_2(O_1^{(2)},\ldots,O_n^{(2)})
 =E[T_K(\mathsf G_KO_1^{(2)},\ldots,\mathsf G_KO_n^{(2)})
       \mid O_1^{(2)},\ldots,O_n^{(2)}].
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality and value preservation show that the risk of \(T_2\) at every binary law is no larger than the risk of \(T_K\) at its image law.  Taking the binary supremum and then the infimum over \(T_K\) shows that \(R(n,d,K)\) is at least the binary minimax risk.  The accepted result \texttt{stat\_discrete\_optimal\_value\_minimax\_matched/thm:matched-minimax-frontier} gives, for binary propensities in \([1/3,2/3]\), an absolute lower bound
\[
 c\min\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}.
\]
This proves the claimed transfer with a constant independent of \(K\).

%%% FIELD proof-parametric
Suppose first that \(d_n\leq D\) and \(K_n\leq K_0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(D\) and \(K_0\) are finite.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}empirical\text{-}upper}\),
\[
 R(n,d_n,K_n)
 \leq C\frac{d_nK_n\log(eK_n)}n
 \leq\frac{CDK_0\log(eK_0)}n.
\]
Thus the risk is \(O(n^{-1})\).

For the converse, first note directly from \(\mathrm{def{:}law\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}risk}\) that
\[
 R(n,d,K)\geq R(n,1,K).
\]
Indeed, embed any one-group law by assigning mass one to group \(1\) and mass zero to all other groups; the resulting data experiment is isomorphic to the one-group experiment, and all added cells are null.  If \((K_n)\) is unbounded, choose a subsequence along which \(K_n\to\infty\).  The preceding embedding and \(\mathrm{thm{:}one\text{-}group\text{-}benchmark}\) give along that subsequence
\[
 nR(n,d_n,K_n)\geq c\min\{n,K_n\log(eK_n)\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Hence \(R(n,d_n,K_n)\) cannot be \(O(n^{-1})\).

If \((d_n)\) is unbounded, choose a subsequence along which \(d_n\to\infty\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}grouping\text{-}transfer}\),
\[
 nR(n,d_n,K_n)
 \geq c\min\left\{n,\frac{d_n}{\log(ed_n)}\right\}
 \longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because both entries of the minimum diverge on that subsequence.  Thus parametric MSE also fails whenever \((d_n)\) is unbounded.  An integer sequence is bounded exactly when it has no subsequence tending to infinity, so the two arguments establish necessity without any monotonicity assumption.

%%% FIELD partial-certificate
The exact primal identities are now proved: the balanced lift has bias \(\mathcal E_K(P_L)\) and derivative-weighted variance \(\mathcal W_{K,\lambda}(P_L)\), while the observed-cell lift has the displayed finite covariance matrix for arbitrary local masses and logging.  The exact dual kernel is \(\mathfrak D_{K,\lambda}\), and uniform error below \(1/56\) on the Boolean slice forces degree at least \(\sqrt K\).  What remains unproved is an explicit family of finite coefficient tables and normalized priors whose approximation error, exact factorial covariance, target separation, and weighted moment tail have matching absolute-constant orders uniformly in \(K\), followed by localization over arbitrary \(p_x\) and legal \(e_{ax}\).

%%% FIELD partial-frontier
The proved fixed-sample bracket is
\[
 c\min\!\left\{1,\frac{\max\{d/\log(ed),\ dK\log(1+\sqrt{K/d})\}}{n}\right\}
 \leq R(n,d,K)
 \leq C\min\!\left\{1,\frac{dK\log(eK)}n\right\}.
\]
It is sharp for \(K\geq d^2\), is sharp for \(d=1\), and combines with the accepted binary frontier on \(K=2\).  The total empirical estimator attains the displayed upper bound, and the parametric-MSE condition is exactly bounded \((d_n)\) and bounded \((K_n)\).  The explicit intermediate-region rate, an attaining estimator at that sharper rate, and the necessary-and-sufficient general consistency condition remain unresolved because the dimension-uniform certificate has not been constructed.
