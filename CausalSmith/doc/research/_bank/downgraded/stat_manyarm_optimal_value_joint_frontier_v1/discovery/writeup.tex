\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}manyarm\_\allowbreak{}optimal\_\allowbreak{}value\_\allowbreak{}joint\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A growing treatment menu turns each sparse patient group into a high-dimensional nonsmooth maximum with random denominators. The headline kernel is the explicit all-regime minimax MSE \(r_\star(n,d,K)\), with a total statistically computable estimator on the full unknown-mass class under menu-scaled lower overlap alone and a matching same-class converse. The mathematical hinge is a dimension-uniform certificate that makes maximum-approximation bias, exact factorial-lift covariance, and normalized moment-prior mixture geometry share one evaluated scale, while exposing the finite coefficient count \(M_{2K,L}=\binom{2K+L}{L}\) and the estimator's combinatorial evaluation cost. Supporting claims cover the one-group and \(K\ge d^2\) rates and a new binary-to-many-arm hardness transfer; the accepted \(K=2\) frontier is used only as a cited boundary condition. The open content is the exact transition for \(2<K<d^2\) and the resulting necessary-and-sufficient consistency boundary.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The literature separates fixed-dimensional nonregular inference, restricted-class policy regret, one-group maximum-mean upper bounds, and scalar approximation-theoretic minimax estimation; none gives the joint finite-sample risk when sparse categorical groups and treatments grow together under unknown observational logging and only a menu-scaled lower propensity bound.

\paragraph{Niche.} The categorical experiment has exact Poisson factorial and likelihood-mixture identities, but its \(K\)-way tie fan, arbitrary group occupancy, and potentially unbalanced logging require a dimension-uniform primal--dual certificate with an explicit finite representation.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project evaluates that certificate, splices it to the supporting one-group and many-arm results, the new binary-grouping transfer, and the cited \(K=2\) boundary, then turns the closed-form envelope into a total computable estimator, an all-estimator lower bound, and exact consistency and parametric-MSE conditions.

\section{Related work}

LuedtkeVanderLaan2016 supplies the standard identification and nonregularity boundary: its pathwise-differentiability theorem and online estimator explain why exact ties defeat ordinary first-order inference, but identification is not an originality target here and the paper does not address growing-dimensional minimax MSE. WhitehouseAusternSyrgkanis2025 is an informed-by comparison rather than a theorem target. Its static theorem assumes independent evaluation data; a \(\delta\)-polynomial density for every suboptimality gap, allowing an atom at zero; \(\beta_n=\omega(n^{1/[2(1+\delta)]})\); \(\lVert\widehat Q_n-Q^\star\rVert_2=o_P(n^{-1/4}\beta_n^{-1/2})\); a product nuisance error of \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\); \(L^2\) consistency; uniform boundedness; and positive influence variance for the normal limit. The unrestricted finite-sample class here assumes none of the density or nuisance-rate conditions and permits both dimensions to grow. NguyenBarrettJun2025HAVER is the closest estimator comparator: its lower-confidence pivot and head averaging control passive one-group MSE, but random patient-group counts, unknown masses, causal logging, and a global converse are absent. JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015 supplies the nonsmooth--smooth split, factorial-moment lift, clipping, and fuzzy-hypothesis architecture. WuYang2016 supplies the stronger requirement that approximation and moment-matched priors be governed by the same extremal quantity, including an evaluated consistency threshold. CaiLow2011 supplies the moment-prior and chi-square template for a kinked functional. Spalek2008 is the obstruction comparator: its explicit dual polynomial shows that a uniform maximum surrogate cannot hide \(K\) in its degree. LuedtkeVanderLaan2018 studies maxima with a possibly growing index set under asymptotic remainder conditions, not sparse-table all-regime risk. ZhouAtheyWager2023 concerns learned-policy regret over restricted classes and motivates lower overlap, but it is a consumer-facing neighbor rather than a theorem-level target.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed with common observed-data margin induced by \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{LuedtkeVanderLaan2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{LuedtkeVanderLaan2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-exchangeability]\label{ass:conditional-exchangeability}
\((Y(1),\ldots,Y(K))\perp A\mid X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability for multiple treatments, \cite{LuedtkeVanderLaan2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:menu-scaled-overlap]\label{ass:menu-scaled-overlap}
\(e_{ax}\ge1/(2K)\) for every \(x\in[d]\) with \(p_x>0\) and every \(a\in[K]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Menu-scaled lower overlap, \cite{ZhouAtheyWager2023}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:poisson-lift-identity]\label{lem:poisson-lift-identity}
In the balanced Poisson comparison experiment, \(E_\theta[U_{P_L}]=P_L(\theta)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_\theta(U_{P_L})=\sum_{\alpha\ne0}(4\lambda)^{-|\alpha|}(D^\alpha P_L(\theta))^2/\alpha!\) for every \(P_L\) and every \(\theta\in[-1/4,1/4]^K\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an arm \(a\).  Introduce the exponential generating function
\[
 \mathcal H_a(u):=\sum_{h\geq 0}H_h\frac{u^h}{h!}
 =\left(1+\frac{u}{2\lambda}\right)^{N_{a1}}
  \left(1-\frac{u}{2\lambda}\right)^{N_{a0}}.
\]
The equality is coefficientwise exactly the construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}polynomial\text{-}lift}\).  The two Poisson means in the balanced experiment are \(\lambda(1/2+\theta_a)\) and \(\lambda(1/2-\theta_a)\).  Hence the Poisson probability generating function gives, in a neighborhood of zero,
\[
 E_\theta[\mathcal H_a(u)]
 =\exp\!\left\{\lambda(1/2+\theta_a)\frac{u}{2\lambda}
                 -\lambda(1/2-\theta_a)\frac{u}{2\lambda}\right\}
 =e^{\theta_a u}.
\]
Coefficient comparison yields \(E_\theta[H_h]=\theta_a^h\).  The same calculation with two indeterminates gives
\[
 E_\theta[\mathcal H_a(u)\mathcal H_a(v)]
 =\exp\!\left\{\theta_a(u+v)+\frac{uv}{4\lambda}\right\}.
\]
All coefficient extractions and expectations may be interchanged because only finitely many coefficients are needed and Poisson variables have moments of every order.  Therefore
\[
 E_\theta[H_hH_t]
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{h,t\}}
   \binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!(4\lambda)^{-\ell}
   \theta_a^{h+t-2\ell}.
\]

For a multi-index \(\gamma\), write \(H_\gamma=\prod_{a=1}^K H_{\gamma_a}\).  Independence across arms and the preceding identity imply
\[
 E_\theta[H_\gamma H_\delta]
 =\sum_{\alpha\leq\gamma\wedge\delta}
 (4\lambda)^{-|\alpha|}\alpha!
 \binom\gamma\alpha\binom\delta\alpha
 \theta^{\gamma+\delta-2\alpha}.
\]
Using the finite representation \(P_L(\theta)=\sum_\gamma c_\gamma\theta^\gamma\), the first-moment identity gives
\[
 E_\theta[U_{P_L}]=\sum_\gamma c_\gamma\theta^\gamma=P_L(\theta).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 D^\alpha P_L(\theta)
 =\sum_{\gamma\geq\alpha}c_\gamma\,\alpha!\binom\gamma\alpha
   \theta^{\gamma-\alpha}.
\]
Substitution into the preceding second-moment formula and regrouping the finite sums gives
\[
 E_\theta[U_{P_L}^2]
 =\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^K}
   \frac{(4\lambda)^{-|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}
   \{D^\alpha P_L(\theta)\}^2.
\]
The term \(\alpha=0\) is \(P_L(\theta)^2\).  Subtracting the squared expectation proves the stated variance identity.  Derivatives of order above \(L\) vanish, so the displayed sum is finite.  The coefficient table has \(M_{K,L}\) entries and the lift is the finite executable map specified in \(\mathrm{def{:}polynomial\text{-}lift}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The derivative-weighted identity exposes every treatment-menu covariance term, while the finite coefficient representation makes the corresponding statistic executable. \textit{Gap.} JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015 uses factorial moments coordinatewise; this identity is the multivariate covariance accounting needed for the \(K\)-ary maximum. \textit{Consumer.} The open joint certificate can reject approximants with acceptable uniform error but explosive lifted variance.

\begin{lemma}[lem:mixture-kernel-identity]\label{lem:mixture-kernel-identity}
For the balanced Poisson laws \(P_\theta\), \(P_0\), and \(P_{\nu_j}\), \(\lVert dP_{\nu_1}/dP_0-dP_{\nu_0}/dP_0\rVert_{L^2(P_0)}^2=\mathfrak D_{K,\lambda}(\nu_0,\nu_1)\); in particular, matching every mixed moment through total degree \(L\) deletes exactly the terms with \(1\le\lvert\alpha\rvert\le L\), without deleting the weighted higher-order tail.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(\theta\in[-1/4,1/4]^K\), all Poisson means are positive.  Relative to \(P_0\), cancellation of the Poisson exponential factors gives the likelihood ratio
\[
 \Lambda_\theta:=\frac{dP_\theta}{dP_0}
 =\prod_{a=1}^K(1+2\theta_a)^{N_{a1}}(1-2\theta_a)^{N_{a0}}.
\]
For \(\theta,\eta\in[-1/4,1/4]^K\), the Poisson probability generating function and independence over arms give
\[
 E_0[\Lambda_\theta\Lambda_\eta]
 =\prod_{a=1}^K
   \exp\!\left\{\frac\lambda2\bigl((1+2\theta_a)(1+2\eta_a)-1\bigr)
                   +\frac\lambda2\bigl((1-2\theta_a)(1-2\eta_a)-1\bigr)\right\}
 =e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}.
\]
Let \(\Delta=\nu_1-\nu_0\).  Normalization of the two priors gives \(\Delta([-1/4,1/4]^K)=0\).  By mixture linearity,
\[
 \frac{dP_{\nu_1}}{dP_0}-\frac{dP_{\nu_0}}{dP_0}
 =\int \Lambda_\theta\,d\Delta(\theta).
\]
The kernel \(e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}\) is bounded on the compact product cube.  Thus Fubini's theorem applies to the two finite signed measures, and
\[
 \left\|\frac{dP_{\nu_1}}{dP_0}-\frac{dP_{\nu_0}}{dP_0}\right\|_{L^2(P_0)}^2
 =\iint e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}\,d\Delta(\theta)d\Delta(\eta).
\]
The exponential series is absolutely and uniformly convergent on that product cube.  Its multi-index expansion therefore may be integrated term by term:
\[
 e^{4\lambda\theta\cdot\eta}
 =\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^K}
   \frac{(4\lambda)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\theta^\alpha\eta^\alpha.
\]
Consequently the last double integral equals
\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^K}
 \frac{(4\lambda)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}
 \left\{\int\theta^\alpha\,d(\nu_1-\nu_0)(\theta)\right\}^2
 =\mathfrak D_{K,\lambda}(\nu_0,\nu_1).
\]
The term of degree zero is already zero by normalization.  If all mixed moments through total degree \(L\) agree, precisely the terms with \(1\leq|\alpha|\leq L\) vanish.  No condition has been imposed on moments of larger total degree, so their nonnegative weighted tail remains exactly as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity makes mixture indistinguishability use the same weighted multi-index geometry as the estimator variance. \textit{Gap.} WuYang2016 and CaiLow2011 use scalar moment matching; neither source supplies this \(K\)-variate exponential kernel with normalized priors for the ratio-max experiment. \textit{Consumer.} A matching converse can be certified without hiding treatment-menu dependence in a generic total-variation bound.

\begin{lemma}[lem:ratio-max-extension]\label{lem:ratio-max-extension}
For a patient group with local vector \(q=(q_{xa0},q_{xa1}:a\in[K])\), menu-scaled lower overlap implies \(F_K(q)=p_xm_x\). On the entire nonnegative orthant, \(F_K\) is degree-one homogeneous, satisfies \(0\le F_K(q)\le s(q)\), and is \((1+2K)\)-Lipschitz in \(\ell_1\). None of these conclusions uses an upper bound on \(e_{ax}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a patient group \(x\), and put \(q_{ay}=q_{xay}\).  By the definitions of the observed cell probabilities,
\[
 s(q)=p_x,\quad s_a(q)=p_xe_{ax},\quad q_{a1}=p_xe_{ax}\mu_{ax}.
\]
If \(p_x=0\), then \(q=0\), and both \(F_K(q)\) and \(p_xm_x\) are zero.  If \(p_x>0\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}menu\text{-}scaled\text{-}overlap}\) gives \(s_a(q)\geq s(q)/(2K)\).  Hence the denominator in \(\mathrm{def{:}ratio\text{-}max\text{-}extension}\) is \(s_a(q)\), and
\[
 F_K(q)=\max_{a\in[K]}\frac{p_x\,p_xe_{ax}\mu_{ax}}{p_xe_{ax}}
       =p_x\max_{a\in[K]}\mu_{ax}=p_xm_x.
\]
Every division here is legitimate because \(p_xe_{ax}>0\).  This proves agreement with the identified contribution on every positive-mass cell.

For the extension properties, write
\[
 f_a(q)=\frac{s(q)q_{a1}}{\max\{s_a(q),s(q)/(2K)\}},
 \quad F_K(q)=\max_a f_a(q)
\]
for \(q\neq0\).  Positive homogeneity of numerator and denominator gives \(f_a(cq)=cf_a(q)\) for \(c\geq0\), including \(c=0\) by the declared convention.  Since \(q_{a1}\leq s_a(q)\leq\max\{s_a(q),s(q)/(2K)\}\),
\[
 0\leq f_a(q)\leq s(q),
\]
and the same bounds hold for their maximum.

It remains to prove the global Lipschitz statement.  On the half-space \(s_a(q)\leq s(q)/(2K)\), one has \(f_a(q)=2Kq_{a1}\), whose gradient has \(\ell_\infty\)-norm \(2K\).  On the other half-space, put \(z=q_{a1}\) and \(t=s_a(q)>0\).  Then \(f_a(q)=s(q)z/t\), with \(z/t\leq1\) and \(s(q)/t\leq2K\).  A coordinate outside arm \(a\) has derivative \(z/t\); the success coordinate has derivative
\[
 \frac zt+\frac{s(q)}t\left(1-\frac zt\right),
\]
and the failure coordinate has derivative
\[
 \frac zt-\frac{s(q)}t\frac zt.
\]
Each absolute derivative is at most \(1+2K\).  The two formulas for \(f_a\) agree on their common boundary.  A line segment in the nonnegative orthant crosses that linear boundary at most once unless it lies in it; integrating the directional derivative on the resulting subsegments gives
\[
 |f_a(q)-f_a(q')|\leq(1+2K)\|q-q'\|_1
\]
whenever neither endpoint is the origin.  If one endpoint is the origin, the same inequality follows from \(f_a(q)\leq s(q)=\|q\|_1\).  Finally,
\[
 |F_K(q)-F_K(q')|
 \leq\max_a|f_a(q)-f_a(q')|
 \leq(1+2K)\|q-q'\|_1.
\]
Only the lower-overlap inequality was used in the agreement step; all extension properties hold on the whole nonnegative orthant.  No upper bound on \(e_{ax}\) enters.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The extension is the full observational target seen by polynomial localization and proves that the upper logging cap is mathematically unnecessary. \textit{Gap.} Scalar factorial constructions do not address the conditional ratio at unknown patient-group and treatment masses; this lemma supplies the exact lower-overlap extension used by the joint certificate. \textit{Consumer.} The full-class estimator can localize on observed cell masses without estimating propensities or imposing an upper balance condition.

\begin{lemma}[lem:observed-cell-lift-identity]\label{lem:observed-cell-lift-identity}
For independent \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(m q_j)\), \(E_q[G_h]=(q_j-z_j)^h\) and \[E_q[G_hG_t]=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{h,t\}}\binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!\,(q_j/m)^\ell(q_j-z_j)^{h+t-2\ell}.\] Consequently \(E_q[U_{Q_L}]=Q_L(q-z)\), and \(\operatorname{Var}_q(U_{Q_L})\) is the finite double sum over \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A_{2K,L}\) obtained by multiplying the displayed coordinate second moments and subtracting \(Q_L(q-z)^2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one coordinate \(j\), form the exponential generating function
\[
 \mathcal G_j(u):=\sum_{h\geq0}G_h(N_j;z_j,m)\frac{u^h}{h!}
 =e^{-z_ju}\left(1+\frac um\right)^{N_j}.
\]
The equality follows by multiplying the series for \(e^{-z_ju}\) and the falling-factorial expansion of \((1+u/m)^{N_j}\), and is coefficientwise the definition in \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}cell\text{-}factorial\text{-}lift}\).  Since \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(mq_j)\),
\[
 E_q[\mathcal G_j(u)]=e^{-z_ju}e^{q_ju}=e^{(q_j-z_j)u}.
\]
Thus \(E_q[G_h]=(q_j-z_j)^h\).  Likewise,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_q[\mathcal G_j(u)\mathcal G_j(v)]
 &=e^{-z_j(u+v)}
   \exp\!\left(mq_j\left[\left(1+\frac um\right)
                                 \left(1+\frac vm\right)-1\right]\right)\\
 &=\exp\!\left((q_j-z_j)(u+v)+\frac{q_j}{m}uv\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Coefficient comparison gives exactly
\[
 E_q[G_hG_t]
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{h,t\}}
   \binom h\ell\binom t\ell\ell!\,(q_j/m)^\ell
   (q_j-z_j)^{h+t-2\ell}.
\]
The required interchanges are finite coefficient calculations, and all Poisson factorial moments are finite.

For \(G_\alpha=\prod_{j=1}^{2K}G_{\alpha_j}(N_j;z_j,m)\), independence across the \(2K\) coordinates gives
\[
 E_q[G_\alpha]=(q-z)^\alpha
\]
and
\[
 E_q[G_\alpha G_\beta]
 =\prod_{j=1}^{2K}\left[
   \sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\}}
   \binom{\alpha_j}\ell\binom{\beta_j}\ell\ell!
   (q_j/m)^\ell(q_j-z_j)^{\alpha_j+\beta_j-2\ell}
 \right].
\]
Using the finite coefficient table in the definition therefore yields
\[
 E_q[U_{Q_L}]=\sum_\alpha\gamma_\alpha(q-z)^\alpha=Q_L(q-z),
\]
and the fully explicit finite variance
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_q(U_{Q_L})
 =\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A_{2K,L}}\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta
 \Bigg\{&\prod_{j=1}^{2K}\left[
   \sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\}}
   \binom{\alpha_j}\ell\binom{\beta_j}\ell\ell!
   (q_j/m)^\ell(q_j-z_j)^{\alpha_j+\beta_j-2\ell}
 \right]\\
 &-(q-z)^{\alpha+\beta}\Bigg\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the asserted finite double sum and includes every cross-monomial covariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This identity gives the complete finite covariance calculation for the actual unknown-mass, unknown-logging upper-bound coordinates. \textit{Gap.} JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015 supplies coordinatewise factorial lifts for additive functionals; the joint ratio maximum requires the full mixed double sum across \(2K\) coordinates. \textit{Consumer.} The certificate can audit the exact variance of every finite candidate table before using it in the full-class estimator.

\begin{lemma}[lem:or-obstruction]\label{lem:or-obstruction}
On the Boolean restriction \(\theta_a\in\{-1/4,1/4\}\), an approximation to \(\max_{a\in[K]}\theta_a\) with uniform error at most \(1/56\) induces a \(1/14\)-approximation to signed \(\operatorname{OR}_K\); consequently its total degree is at least \(\sqrt K\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(p\) be a real polynomial on \(\mathbb R^K\) satisfying
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\{-1/4,1/4\}^K}
 \left|p(\theta)-\max_{a\in[K]}\theta_a\right|\leq\frac1{56}.
\]
Use the signed Boolean convention in which the OR function is \(+1\) only at the all-\(+1\) input and is \(-1\) otherwise.  For \(x\in\{\pm1\}^K\), define the proof-intermediate polynomial
\[
 r(x)=-4p(-x/4).
\]
If every coordinate of \(x\) is \(+1\), then \(\max_a(-x_a/4)=-1/4\); otherwise at least one coordinate equals \(-1\), and that maximum is \(+1/4\).  Therefore
\[
 -4\max_a(-x_a/4)=\operatorname{OR}_K(x)
\]
under the stated signed convention, and hence
\[
 \sup_{x\in\{\pm1\}^K}|r(x)-\operatorname{OR}_K(x)|\leq4/56=1/14.
\]
Affine substitution and scalar multiplication do not increase total degree, so \(\deg r\leq\deg p\).  The cited lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}spalek\text{-}or\text{-}approximate\text{-}degree}\) says that such an approximant has degree at least \(\sqrt K\).  Thus \(\deg p\geq\sqrt K\), proving both conclusions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the analytic early-kill test against a nominally dimension-free low-degree maximum certificate. \textit{Gap.} Spalek2008 proves the OR approximate-degree bound; the present reduction turns it into a non-representability constraint on the local statistical surrogate, while making no unsupported leap from degree to minimax risk. \textit{Consumer.} Every proposed certificate for the intermediate regime must either pay the square-root degree or exploit localization that avoids uniform Boolean approximation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:empirical-upper]\label{lem:empirical-upper}
There is an absolute constant \(C<\infty\) such that for every \(n,d,K\), \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{d,K}}E_{P^n}[(\widehat V_{\mathrm{ER}}-V^\star(P))^2]\le C\min\{1,u(d,K)/n\}\), uniformly over unknown \(p_x\), unknown \(e_{ax}\) satisfying only \(e_{ax}\ge1/(2K)\), null cells, exact ties, and boundary means.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{d,K}\).  For a group with \(N_x=k>0\), define
\[
 Z_x=\max_{a\in[K]}|\overline Y_{xa}-\mu_{ax}|.
\]
Conditional on \(N_x=k\), the arm count \(H=N_{xa}\) is \(\operatorname{Bin}(k,e_{ax})\).  The elementary exponential-moment calculation for this binomial variable gives
\[
 P(H<ke_{ax}/2\mid N_x=k)\leq e^{-ke_{ax}/8}.
\]
Indeed, Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-(\log2)H}\), followed by \(1+u\leq e^u\), gives exponent
\(ke_{ax}\{(\log2)/2-1/2\}\leq-ke_{ax}/8\).

Given \(H=h>0\), the observed outcomes in that arm are independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(\mu_{ax}\).  For a Bernoulli variable \(W\) of mean \(\mu\), direct differentiation of
\(e^{-s\mu}(1-\mu+\mu e^s)\) gives
\(E[e^{s(W-\mu)}]\leq e^{s^2/8}\).  Markov's inequality, optimization over \(s\), and the same argument for \(-s\) therefore give
\[
 P(|\overline Y_{xa}-\mu_{ax}|>t\mid N_x=k,H=h)
 \leq2e^{-2ht^2}.
\]
For \(0<t\leq1\), splitting according to \(H<ke_{ax}/2\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(|\overline Y_{xa}-\mu_{ax}|>t\mid N_x=k)
 &\leq e^{-ke_{ax}/8}+2e^{-ke_{ax}t^2}\\
 &\leq3e^{-ke_{ax}t^2/8}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This also covers the declared value at \(H=0\), because that event belongs to \(\{H<ke_{ax}/2\}\).  By the union bound and \(e_{ax}\geq1/(2K)\),
\[
 P(Z_x>t\mid N_x=k)
 \leq\min\left\{1,3K\exp\!\left(-\frac{kt^2}{16K}\right)\right\}.
\]
Since \(0\leq Z_x\leq1\), integration of this tail with \(s=t^2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E[Z_x^2\mid N_x=k]
 &=\int_0^1P(Z_x>\sqrt s\mid N_x=k)\,ds\\
 &\leq C_0\min\left\{1,\frac{K\log(eK)}k\right\}
 \end{aligned}
\]
for an absolute \(C_0\).  To see the last inequality directly, split the integral at
\(16K\log(3K)/k\) when this number is below one; the remaining exponential integral is at most \(16K/k\).

By \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}empirical\text{-}maximum}\), decompose
\[
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{ER}}-V^\star(P)=E_1+E_2,
\]
where
\[
 E_1=\sum_{x=1}^d\frac{N_x}{n}
 \left(\max_a\overline Y_{xa}-m_x\right),
 \quad
 E_2=\sum_{x=1}^d\left(\frac{N_x}{n}-p_x\right)m_x.
\]
The maximum map is one-Lipschitz in the sup norm, so the absolute value of the parenthesized term in \(E_1\) is at most \(Z_x\).  Since the nonnegative weights \(N_x/n\) sum to one, Jensen's inequality and the conditional bound above give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[E_1^2]
 &\leq\sum_{x=1}^d E_P\left[\frac{N_x}{n}Z_x^2\right]\\
 &\leq\frac{C_0}{n}\sum_{x=1}^d K\log(eK)
 =\frac{C_0dK\log(eK)}n.
\end{aligned}
\]
The zero-count groups contribute zero, so no conditioning at \(k=0\) or division by zero occurs.  Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}sampling}\),
\[
 E_2=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{m_{X_i}-E_P[m_X]\}.
\]
Because \(0\leq m_X\leq1\), its variance is at most \(1/(4n)\).  Thus \((E_1+E_2)^2\leq2E_1^2+2E_2^2\) implies
\[
 E_{P^n}[(\widehat V_{\mathrm{ER}}-V^\star(P))^2]
 \leq C_1\frac{dK\log(eK)}n.
\]
Both the estimator and target lie in \([0,1]\), so their squared difference is also at most one.  Combining the two bounds, taking the supremum over \(P\), and recalling \(u(d,K)=dK\log(eK)\) proves the result with an absolute constant.  The proof used neither \(p_x>0\) for every group nor an upper bound, known value, or estimator of \(e_{ax}\); ties and boundary means were never excluded.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proves a full-class baseline with absolute constants, no oracle knowledge of masses or logging probabilities, and no upper balance restriction on logging. \textit{Gap.} NguyenBarrettJun2025HAVER conditions on externally supplied arm counts; the result here controls random causal cell occupancy and aggregation over arbitrary group masses. \textit{Consumer.} The estimator gives an immediately implementable attainable-value error guarantee for multi-arm causal forests and related treatment-targeting systems.

\begin{lemma}[lem:centered-winner-lower]\label{lem:centered-winner-lower}
There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that for every \(n,d,K\), \(R(n,d,K)\ge c\min\{1,b(d,K)/n\}\); the lower experiment has \(p_x=1/d\), \(e_{ax}=1/K\), and centered random winners that preserve the arm-averaged conditional outcome mean in every patient group.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
It suffices by \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}risk}\) to construct a legal finite subexperiment.  Put \(p_x=1/d\) and \(e_{ax}=1/K\).  Under a proof-intermediate null law \(Q_0\), let every conditional outcome mean equal \(1/2\).  For each winner vector \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_d)\in[K]^d\) and \(0<\delta\leq1/4\), define an alternative law \(Q_{\mathbf j}\) by
\[
 \mu^{\mathbf j}_{ax}=\frac12+\eta_{\mathbf j,x,a},\quad
 \eta_{\mathbf j,x,a}=
 \begin{cases}
 \delta,&a=j_x,\\
 -\delta/(K-1),&a\neq j_x.
 \end{cases}
\]
These means belong to \([0,1]\), and their average over arms is exactly \(1/2\) in every group.  A full-data completion is obtained by drawing the potential outcomes conditionally on \(X=x\) as Bernoulli variables with these means, drawing \(A\) uniformly and independently of all potential outcomes conditional on \(X\), and setting \(Y=Y(A)\); give \(Q_0\) the same completion with all means equal to \(1/2\).  Every resulting law satisfies consistency, conditional exchangeability, and the overlap equality \(e_{ax}=1/K\geq1/(2K)\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal M_{d,K}\).  By \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\),
\[
 V^\star(Q_0)=\frac12,\quad V^\star(Q_{\mathbf j})=\frac12+\delta
\]
for every \(\mathbf j\).

For one observation, the likelihood ratio of \(Q_{\mathbf j}\) relative to \(Q_0\) at \((x,a,y)\) is
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathbf j}(x,a,y)=1+2(2y-1)\eta_{\mathbf j,x,a}.
\]
If \(h(\mathbf j,\mathbf l)=|\{x:j_x=l_x\}|\), direct summation over the \(2dK\) categories gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{Q_0}[\Lambda_{\mathbf j}\Lambda_{\mathbf l}]
 &=1+\frac4{dK}\sum_{x=1}^d\sum_{a=1}^K
      \eta_{\mathbf j,x,a}\eta_{\mathbf l,x,a}\\
 &=1+\frac{4\delta^2\{K h(\mathbf j,\mathbf l)-d\}}
               {d(K-1)^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the inner product in a coincident-winner group is \(\delta^2K/(K-1)\), while in a different-winner group it is \(-\delta^2K/(K-1)^2\).  Let
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{mix}}=K^{-d}\sum_{\mathbf j\in[K]^d}Q_{\mathbf j}^{\,n}.
\]
For probability laws \(Q\ll P\), write
\(\chi^2(Q,P)=E_P[(dQ/dP-1)^2]\).
Independence of the observations and averaging two independent uniform winner vectors imply the exact identity
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)
 =E\left[\left(1+\frac{4\delta^2(KH-d)}{d(K-1)^2}\right)^n\right],
 \quad H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(d,1/K).
\]
The base is positive because its minimum is \(1-4\delta^2/(K-1)^2>0\).  Define
\[
 u=\frac{4n\delta^2K}{d(K-1)^2}.
\]
Using \(1+z\leq e^z\), followed by the binomial moment generating function and \(\log(1+r)\leq r\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\{1+\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)\}
 &\leq d\log\left[e^{-u/K}\left\{1+\frac{e^u-1}{K}\right\}\right]\\
 &\leq\frac dK(e^u-1-u).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Put \(w=K/d\), \(v=\log(1+\sqrt w)\), \(c_0=1/64\), and choose
\[
 \delta^2=c_0\min\left\{1,\frac{d(K-1)^2v}{nK}\right\}.
\]
Then \(\delta\leq1/8\) and, in both the saturated and unsaturated cases, \(0\leq u\leq4c_0v\).  For \(\psi(t)=e^t-1-t\), convexity and \(\psi(0)=0\) give \(\psi(4c_0v)\leq4c_0\psi(v)\).  Moreover,
\[
 \psi(v)=\sqrt w-\log(1+\sqrt w)
 =\int_0^{\sqrt w}\frac{t}{1+t}\,dt\leq\frac w2.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \log\{1+\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)\}\leq2c_0.
\]
Consequently, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q_{\mathrm{mix}},Q_0^n)}
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{e^{2c_0}-1}=: \eta<\frac14.
\]
Here
\(\operatorname{TV}(Q,P)=\frac12\int|dQ-dP|\); the last strict inequality follows, for example, from
\(e^{1/32}\leq(1-1/32)^{-1}=32/31\).

For any estimator, threshold it at \(1/2+\delta/2\).  On a null testing error its squared estimation loss is at least \(\delta^2/4\), and the same is true on an alternative testing error.  The sum of the null error probability and the mixture-averaged alternative error probability is at least \(1-\eta\), by the defining variational inequality for total variation.  Hence the supremum risk over the constructed laws is at least
\[
 \frac{\delta^2}{8}(1-\eta).
\]
Finally,
\[
 \frac{(K-1)^2}{K^2}\geq\frac14,
 \quad
 \delta^2\geq\frac{c_0}{4}
 \min\left\{1,\frac{dK\log(1+\sqrt{K/d})}{n}\right\}.
\]
Taking the infimum over all estimators proves the asserted lower bound with an absolute constant.  The construction permits exact ties under the null and uses only a known-uniform subexperiment of the same full class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Centering removes the marginal outcome shift that weakens uncentered random-winner mixtures, and the uniform subexperiment remains legal under lower overlap alone. \textit{Gap.} CaiLow2011 and WuYang2016 give moment-mixture templates for other nonsmooth functionals; this exact winner-collision geometry is specific to grouped treatment maxima. \textit{Consumer.} The lower envelope rules out any analysis that obtains a falsely dimension-free or binary-like rate for expanding treatment menus.

\begin{theorem}[thm:manyarm-region]\label{thm:manyarm-region}
For absolute constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), if \(K\ge d^2\) then \(c\min\{1,dK\log(eK)/n\}\le R(n,d,K)\le C\min\{1,dK\log(eK)/n\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper inequality follows directly from \(\mathrm{lem{:}empirical\text{-}upper}\).  For the lower inequality, \(\mathrm{lem{:}centered\text{-}winner\text{-}lower}\) gives the scale
\[
 dK\log(1+\sqrt{K/d}).
\]
If \(K\geq d^2\), then \(d\leq\sqrt K\), so \(\sqrt{K/d}\geq K^{1/4}\) and
\[
 \log(1+\sqrt{K/d})\geq\frac14\log K.
\]
Since \(K\geq2\), one has \(1+\log K\leq(1+1/\log2)\log K<\frac52\log K\).  Therefore
\[
 \log(1+\sqrt{K/d})\geq\frac1{10}\log(eK).
\]
For \(0<\rho\leq1\), \(\min\{1,\rho z\}\geq\rho\min\{1,z\}\).  Applying this with \(\rho=1/10\) converts the centered-winner lower bound to the claimed lower rate.  Both constants remain absolute.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the first genuinely joint solved region and a non-negotiable splice for the full frontier. \textit{Gap.} No named published result gives minimax MSE for grouped causal optimal value in a jointly growing \(d,K\) region. \textit{Consumer.} Menu-expansion studies can read off the sample size required once the number of treatments dominates the square of patient-group count.

\begin{theorem}[thm:one-group-benchmark]\label{thm:one-group-benchmark}
For absolute constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), \(c\min\{1,K\log(eK)/n\}\le R(n,1,K)\le C\min\{1,K\log(eK)/n\}\) for every \(n\ge1\) and \(K\ge2\); this adds the global bounded-Bernoulli all-estimator converse absent from the instance-dependent upper-bound theory of Nguyen, Barrett, and Jun, while leaving their easy-instance guarantees intact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(d=1\) in \(\mathrm{lem{:}empirical\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}centered\text{-}winner\text{-}lower}\).  The resulting upper scale is \(K\log(eK)\), while the lower scale is \(K\log(1+\sqrt K)\).  For \(K\geq2\),
\[
 \log(1+\sqrt K)\geq\frac12\log K
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \log(eK)=1+\log K<\frac52\log K.
\]
Thus \(\log(1+\sqrt K)\geq\frac15\log(eK)\).  The elementary relation \(\min\{1,\rho z\}\geq\rho\min\{1,z\}\) for \(0<\rho\leq1\) proves the two-sided benchmark with absolute constants.  The bibliographic scope assertion in the final clause is exactly the delivery distinction recorded in \(\mathrm{lem{:}haver\text{-}upper\text{-}bound\text{-}scope}\): that work provides estimator-specific MSE upper bounds, whereas the lower inequality just proved quantifies over all estimators on the bounded-Bernoulli class.  Its easy-instance upper guarantees are not altered by this converse.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The passive one-group slice fixes the unavoidable \(\log K\) factor and prevents importing adaptive best-arm sample complexity. \textit{Gap.} NguyenBarrettJun2025HAVER proves sharp instance-dependent MSE upper bounds but no matching global minimax lower bound over bounded Bernoulli means. \textit{Consumer.} Maximum-mean estimation in Q-learning and Monte Carlo tree search gains a finite-sample worst-case benchmark for passive data.

\begin{lemma}[lem:binary-grouping-transfer]\label{lem:binary-grouping-transfer}
For every \(K\ge2\), the channel \(\mathsf G_K\), together with its declared causal completion, maps each binary observed-data law with \(\pi_x\in[1/3,2/3]\) into \(\mathcal M_{d,K}\), preserves the unrestricted optimal value, and appends only a parameter-free within-block treatment label. Therefore there is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that \(R(n,d,K)\ge c\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\) for every \(n,d,K\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Start with a binary observed-data law, write \(p_x=P(X=x)\), \(\pi_x=P(B=1\mid X=x)\in[1/3,2/3]\), and let \(\mu_{bx}=E[Y\mid B=b,X=x]\).  Under \(\mathsf G_K\), an arm \(a\in G_b\) has propensity
\[
 e_{ax}=\frac{P(B=b\mid X=x)}{g_b}.
\]
Both binary block probabilities are at least \(1/3\), and \(g_b\leq\lceil K/2\rceil\).  For every \(K\geq2\),
\[
 3\lceil K/2\rceil\leq2K,
\]
where the odd case uses \(K\geq3\).  Hence \(e_{ax}\geq1/(2K)\) for every arm on every positive-mass group.  The declared completion in \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}grouping\text{-}channel}\) draws arm potential outcomes with mean \(\mu_{bx}\) for \(a\in G_b\), independently of \(A\) conditional on \(X\), and sets \(Y=Y(A)\).  It therefore satisfies conditional exchangeability and consistency, as well as the overlap condition, and belongs to \(\mathcal M_{d,K}\).

Every arm in \(G_b\) has conditional outcome mean \(\mu_{bx}\).  Thus \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\) gives
\[
 V^\star(\mathsf G_KP)
 =\sum_xp_x\max_{a\in[K]}\mu_{ax}
 =\sum_xp_x\max_{b\in\{0,1\}}\mu_{bx},
\]
which is the unrestricted binary optimal value.  Conditional on \((X,B,Y)\), the additional arm label is uniform on the fixed block \(G_B\); its law is parameter-free.

Write \(O_i^{(2)}=(X_i,B_i,Y_i)\) for the \(i\)-th binary observation.  Let \(T_K\) be any estimator in the many-arm experiment.  Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase squared loss because the target is in \([0,1]\), so we may first clip \(T_K\) and thereby make it bounded.  Apply the channel independently to each binary observation and then Rao--Blackwellize over its auxiliary uniform labels:
\[
 T_2(O_1^{(2)},\ldots,O_n^{(2)})
 =E[T_K(\mathsf G_KO_1^{(2)},\ldots,\mathsf G_KO_n^{(2)})
       \mid O_1^{(2)},\ldots,O_n^{(2)}].
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality and value preservation show that the risk of \(T_2\) at every binary law is no larger than the risk of \(T_K\) at its image law.  Taking the binary supremum and then the infimum over \(T_K\) shows that \(R(n,d,K)\) is at least the binary minimax risk.  The accepted result \texttt{stat\_discrete\_optimal\_value\_minimax\_matched/thm:matched-minimax-frontier} gives, for binary propensities in \([1/3,2/3]\), an absolute lower bound
\[
 c\min\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}.
\]
This proves the claimed transfer with a constant independent of \(K\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The parameter-free arm splitting transfers the binary hardness uniformly to every treatment-menu size and is needed for the necessity of bounded patient-group complexity. \textit{Gap.} The accepted binary frontier stops at two treatments; this Blackwell reduction is a new many-arm transfer with constants independent of \(K\) and remains valid without an upper logging cap. \textit{Consumer.} The joint frontier must dominate the binary sparse-group boundary in every menu regime, ruling out formulas that lose the \(d/\log(ed)\) obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:parametric-criterion]\label{thm:parametric-criterion}
For arbitrary sequences \((d_n,K_n)\) as \(n\to\infty\), \(R(n,d_n,K_n)=O(n^{-1})\) if and only if both \(d_n\) and \(K_n\) are bounded.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that \(d_n\leq D\) and \(K_n\leq K_0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(D\) and \(K_0\) are finite.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}empirical\text{-}upper}\),
\[
 R(n,d_n,K_n)
 \leq C\frac{d_nK_n\log(eK_n)}n
 \leq\frac{CDK_0\log(eK_0)}n.
\]
Thus the risk is \(O(n^{-1})\).

For the converse, first note directly from \(\mathrm{def{:}law\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}risk}\) that
\[
 R(n,d,K)\geq R(n,1,K).
\]
Indeed, embed any one-group law by assigning mass one to group \(1\) and mass zero to all other groups; the resulting data experiment is isomorphic to the one-group experiment, and all added cells are null.  If \((K_n)\) is unbounded, choose a subsequence along which \(K_n\to\infty\).  The preceding embedding and \(\mathrm{thm{:}one\text{-}group\text{-}benchmark}\) give along that subsequence
\[
 nR(n,d_n,K_n)\geq c\min\{n,K_n\log(eK_n)\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Hence \(R(n,d_n,K_n)\) cannot be \(O(n^{-1})\).

If \((d_n)\) is unbounded, choose a subsequence along which \(d_n\to\infty\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}grouping\text{-}transfer}\),
\[
 nR(n,d_n,K_n)
 \geq c\min\left\{n,\frac{d_n}{\log(ed_n)}\right\}
 \longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because both entries of the minimum diverge on that subsequence.  Thus parametric MSE also fails whenever \((d_n)\) is unbounded.  An integer sequence is bounded exactly when it has no subsequence tending to infinity, so the two arguments establish necessity without any monotonicity assumption.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gives a complete and dimension-uniform answer for when parametric MSE is possible even before the intermediate consistency frontier is closed. \textit{Gap.} LuedtkeVanderLaan2016 studies fixed-complexity regularity. The static theorem in WhitehouseAusternSyrgkanis2025 assumes a \(\delta\)-polynomial density for each suboptimality gap, \(\beta_n=\omega(n^{1/[2(1+\delta)]})\), specified \(L^2\) nuisance rates, uniform boundedness, and positive limiting variance; neither paper gives the necessary-and-sufficient unrestricted \((d_n,K_n)\) minimax criterion. \textit{Consumer.} Practitioners can distinguish benign fixed-menu asymptotics from regimes where expanding personalization complexity necessarily destroys parametric accuracy.

\begin{proposition}[prop:unequal-logging-witness]\label{prop:unequal-logging-witness}
The constructed law \(P_{\mathrm{wit}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{2,3}\), has unequal propensities, has maximizing arm \(a=1\) at \(x=1\) and maximizing arm \(a=3\) at \(x=2\), and satisfies \(V^\star(P_{\mathrm{wit}})=43/50\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{def{:}unequal\text{-}logging\text{-}witness}\), \(d=2\), \(K=3\), and \(p_1=p_2=1/2\).  Thus both groups have positive mass and the displayed conditional treatment probabilities are
\[
(e_{11},e_{21},e_{31})=(1/2,1/3,1/6),
\qquad
(e_{12},e_{22},e_{32})=(1/6,1/3,1/2).
\]
Each vector has sum
\[
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1,
\]
so each is a probability vector.  Moreover, every displayed entry is at least
\[
\frac16=\frac{1}{2K}.
\]
Consequently the menu-scaled overlap condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}law\text{-}class}\) holds on every positive-mass group.  (In fact every entry is also at most \(1/2<2/3=2/K\).)  The two propensity vectors are unequal within each group, and they also differ across the two groups; hence the logging propensities are unequal.

The same construction draws \(A\), conditionally on \(X\), independently of the entire vector \((Y(1),Y(2),Y(3))\).  Therefore
\[
(Y(1),Y(2),Y(3))\mathrel{\perp}A\mid X,
\]
which is the conditional-exchangeability condition appearing in \(\mathrm{def{:}law\text{-}class}\).  It then sets \(Y=Y(A)\), so the consistency condition in that definition holds almost surely.  Together with the verified overlap condition, these facts give
\[
P_{\mathrm{wit}}\in\mathcal M_{2,3}.
\]

For the first group, the conditional means specified by the construction obey
\[
\mu_{11}=\frac9{10}>\mu_{21}=\frac12>\mu_{31}=\frac15.
\]
Thus the unique maximizing arm at \(x=1\) is \(a=1\), and \(m_1=9/10\) by the definition of \(m_x\).  For the second group,
\[
\mu_{32}=\frac{41}{50}>\mu_{22}=\frac35>\mu_{12}=\frac14.
\]
Thus the unique maximizing arm at \(x=2\) is \(a=3\), and \(m_2=41/50\).  Finally, the identifying formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}optimal\text{-}value}\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
V^\star(P_{\mathrm{wit}})
  &=\sum_{x=1}^{2}p_xm_x \\
  &=\frac12\left(\frac9{10}\right)
    +\frac12\left(\frac{41}{50}\right) \\
  &=\frac{45}{100}+\frac{41}{100}
   =\frac{86}{100}
   =\frac{43}{50}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves every asserted property.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit law catches accidental assumptions of uniform logging or a common maximizing treatment across groups. \textit{Gap.} This is an internal specification witness rather than a novelty claim; it tests the exact scope omitted by one-group and fixed-policy analyses. \textit{Consumer.} Formalization and estimator definitions receive a concrete regression test with switching winners and legal unequal treatment probabilities.
