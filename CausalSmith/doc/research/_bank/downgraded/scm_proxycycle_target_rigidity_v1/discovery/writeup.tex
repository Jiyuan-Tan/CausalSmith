\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}proxycycle\_\allowbreak{}target\_\allowbreak{}rigidity}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For every fixed finite four-response-type selected proxy model, PN/PS rigidity is decided directly on the raw legal cycle fiber. After the strict supplied-margin check in design A, the compiler makes one bounded-algebraic-sampling call and, when the fiber is nonempty, one one-copy unequal-target existential call. It returns incompatibility, an exact common target, or legal same-observable SCM twins. A separate exhaustive rank-two, L/R/E/D, support-face, shared-endpoint, and relevant-IV residual-forest atlas supplies optional structural labels but is not queried by the decision engine. The published regular formulas retain their existing transfer scope; the contribution is classification of additional compatible singular and boundary laws. Fixed-alphabet record and algebraic-degree bounds are singly exponential. Initial-sample degrees and second-stage degrees are controlled separately, and rational height includes the finite cost of forming the sampled target before the second call.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published regular proxy formulas do not classify PN/PS constancy when ranks fall, coefficients reach support faces, endpoints coincide, response rows become inactive, or shared-instrument equations couple unknown population margins. General decidability also does not expose the causal response-cycle obstruction or distinguish incompatibility from nonidentification.

\paragraph{Niche.} The four response types form a labeled causal cycle whose target can be rigid while nuisance factorizations vary. The normalized design-A and design-B legal fibers are bounded one-copy semialgebraic sets, so global constancy is decided by comparing every feasible point with one sampled target in a single existential query. The exhaustive orientation, rank-one, support-face, shared-endpoint, and residual-forest charts remain a separate exact explanation of every singular and boundary configuration.

\paragraph{Fill.} The compiler first checks strict supplied-margin compatibility in design A, samples once from the applicable raw legal fiber, and performs one raw-fiber unequal-target test. It returns an exact target, legal target-changing full SCMs, or incompatibility. The structural atlas can label the result and retains its finite template bound, but contributes no multiplier or auxiliary variables to mandatory decision complexity. The termination theorem uses raw degree two in design A and degree three in design B, bounds initial-sample coordinates in their sampling extension, bounds a second-stage witness by the product of the sampled-target and relative existential degrees, and composes rational height after adding the binary-length cost of summing the sampled target coordinates.

\section{Related work}

Shingaki, Yoshida, and Kuroki (2026) supply the selected response-type factorization and the regular identification results used here.  At the selected-law factorization level, the present finite four-type model specializes their Condition 1.  The fixed-margin two-proxy fiber is a restriction to which Theorem 1 applies, while the relevant-IV fiber satisfies equation (13) and relevance, so Corollary 1 applies directly.  Our contribution does not extend their regular formula or subsume their model; it classifies additional compatible singular and boundary fibers and reports incompatible inputs separately.  Shingaki and Kuroki (2023) and Kawakami (2021) provide separate experimental and IV-plus-proxy overlap results under their own conditions.  Nonnegative-factorization rigidity work supplies geometric context but not causal target constancy on this labeled response cycle.  Basu, Pollack, and Roy (2006) supply the exact bounded-algebraic-sampling, existential-realizability, real-univariate, sign, and root-isolation algorithms used by the two-call raw-fiber engine and, optionally, by structural diagnostics.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:response-consistency]\label{ass:response-consistency}
\(Y=Y_U(X)\) almost surely, with the four response maps encoded by \(U\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary response-type consistency, \cite{TianPearl2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-dependent-selection]\label{ass:outcome-dependent-selection}
\(S\perp(U,W,Z)\mid(X,Y)\) under \(P_{\vartheta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Outcome-dependent selection, \cite{ShingakiYoshidaKuroki2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:proxy-exclusion]\label{ass:proxy-exclusion}
\(W\perp(X,Y,Z,S)\mid U\) under \(P_{\vartheta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Latent-confounder proxy exclusion, \cite{ShingakiYoshidaKuroki2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selected-table-positivity]\label{ass:selected-table-positivity}
\(p_{ewz}>0\) for every \((e,w,z)\in\mathcal E\times\mathcal W\times\mathcal Z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive observed selected table, \cite{ShingakiYoshidaKuroki2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iv-exogeneity]\label{ass:iv-exogeneity}
\(Z\perp U\) under \(P_{\vartheta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Instrument exogeneity, \cite{ShingakiYoshidaKuroki2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iv-relevance]\label{ass:iv-relevance}
\(Z\not\!\perp X\) under \(P_{\vartheta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Instrument relevance, \cite{ShingakiYoshidaKuroki2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-multinomial-sampling]\label{ass:iid-multinomial-sampling}
\(\widehat p_n\) is formed from \(n\) independent draws from \(p\); when design-A margins are estimated, \(\widehat m_n\) is formed from an independent multinomial sample of size \(n_m\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent multinomial sampling, \cite{TurnerGrunwald2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:margin-sample-scaling]\label{ass:margin-sample-scaling}
When design-A margins are estimated, \(n/n_m\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent two-sample proportional asymptotics, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:compatible-margin-positivity]\label{lem:compatible-margin-positivity}
For every \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\) or \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_B(p)\), positivity of the selected table and \(c=P_{\vartheta}(S=1)>0\) imply \(P_{\vartheta}(E=e,S=1)=c r_e>0\) and therefore \(m_e=P_{\vartheta}(E=e)\ge c r_e>0\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\). Consequently, a nonempty design-A fiber forces the supplied observed margin vector \(m\) to lie in \(\Delta_{\mathcal E}^{\circ}\), and every compatible design-B SCM induces positive population margins.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\vartheta\) in either full fiber.  By the definition of \(r\) and selected-table positivity,
\[
r_e=\sum_{w,z}p_{ewz}>0\qquad(e\in\mathcal E).
\]
The definition \(c=P_{\vartheta}(S=1)>0\) and the identity
\(P_{\vartheta}(E=e\mid S=1)=r_e\) give
\[
P_{\vartheta}(E=e,S=1)
=P_{\vartheta}(S=1)P_{\vartheta}(E=e\mid S=1)
=c r_e>0.
\]
Since \(\{E=e,S=1\}\subseteq\{E=e\}\), monotonicity of probability yields
\[
m_e=P_{\vartheta}(E=e)\ge P_{\vartheta}(E=e,S=1)=c r_e>0.
\]
In design A the equality \(P_{\vartheta}(E)=m\) is part of the definition of \(\mathcal F_A(p,m)\), so a nonempty fiber forces the supplied \(m\) to be in \(\Delta_{\mathcal E}^{\circ}\).  In design B, \(m\) denotes the margin induced by \(P_{\vartheta}\), and the same calculation proves that every one of its coordinates is positive.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Population-margin positivity is a compatibility implication of observed positivity and positive selection mass, not an independent maintained condition. \textit{Gap.} The anchor imposes positive quantities in its regular formulas but does not isolate this elementary full-fiber compatibility consequence. \textit{Consumer.} The cycle lift and residual-forest theorem may use positive margin ratios without strengthening either design's assumptions.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cycle-to-scm-lift]\label{lem:cycle-to-scm-lift}
For every positive selected table \(p\) and supplied margin vector \(m\) with \(\mathcal F_A(p,m)\ne\varnothing\), the map \(\vartheta\mapsto(b,a)\) sends \(\mathcal F_A(p,m)\) onto \(\mathcal C_A(T)\) and preserves \(\tau=\tau_C\). For design B it sends \(\mathcal F_B(p)\) onto \(\mathcal C_B(T)\) and preserves \(\tau=\tau_C\). Conversely, every point of either cycle fiber has a legal full finite SCM lift, including zero response masses and boundary proxy or assignment mechanisms.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove both directions and retain boundary cases throughout.

First let \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\).  The preceding lemma gives \(m_e>0\).  Because
\[
P_{\vartheta}(E=e,S=1)=c r_e>0,
\]
conditioning on \(E=e\) is legitimate.  Outcome-dependent selection implies that the conditional law of \((U,W,Z)\) given \((E=e,S=1)\) is its conditional law given \(E=e\).  Define
\[
a_{ei}(z)=P_{\vartheta}(U=i,Z=z\mid E=e),
\qquad
b_i(w)=\text{the normalized proxy kernel for type }i\text{ in }\vartheta.
\]
For \(\pi_i>0\) this kernel equals \(P_{\vartheta}(W=w\mid U=i)\); unlike that conditional notation, the mechanism kernel remains defined when \(\pi_i=0\).
Response consistency makes \(a_{ei}=0\) unless \(i\in\mathcal I_e\).  Proxy exclusion gives, for \(\mathcal I_e=\{i,j\}\),
\[
T_e(w,z)
=P_{\vartheta}(W=w,Z=z\mid E=e,S=1)
=b_i(w)a_{ei}(z)+b_j(w)a_{ej}(z).
\]
Summing this identity over \(w\), and using \(\sum_w b_i(w)=\sum_wb_j(w)=1\), gives
\[
a_{ei}(z)+a_{ej}(z)=v_e(z).
\]
Thus \((b,a)\in\mathcal C_A(T)\).  Moreover, since \(E=11\) occurs exactly for types \(1,2\) and \(E=00\) exactly for types \(2,4\),
\[
P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=1,Y=1)=\sum_z a_{11,2}(z),
\qquad
P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=0,Y=0)=\sum_z a_{00,2}(z).
\]
Hence \(\tau(\vartheta)=\tau_C(b,a)\).

Conversely, take \((b,a)\in\mathcal C_A(T)\).  Nonemptiness of \(\mathcal F_A(p,m)\), together with the preceding lemma, supplies \(m_e>0\).  For every response type define
\[
h_i(z)=\sum_{e:i\in\mathcal I_e}m_ea_{ei}(z),
\qquad
\pi_i=\sum_z h_i(z).
\]
Because \(a_{ei}\ge0\), all these quantities are nonnegative.  Also
\[
\sum_i\pi_i
=\sum_e m_e\sum_z\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_e}a_{ei}(z)
=\sum_e m_e\sum_zv_e(z)
=\sum_e m_e=1.
\]
If \(\pi_i>0\), put \(g_i(z)=h_i(z)/\pi_i\); if \(\pi_i=0\), choose any probability vector \(g_i\).  Let \(e_1(i)\) and \(e_0(i)\) be the unique incident cells of type \(i\) having treatment coordinates one and zero, respectively.  When \(h_i(z)>0\), set
\[
e_i(z)=\frac{m_{e_1(i)}a_{e_1(i),i}(z)}{h_i(z)}.
\]
The numerator lies between zero and \(h_i(z)\), so \(e_i(z)\in[0,1]\).  When \(h_i(z)=0\), set \(e_i(z)=0\).  Nonnegativity and \(m_e>0\) then imply that both incident terms \(m_ea_{ei}(z)\) vanish, so this convention preserves
\[
\pi_i g_i(z)e_i(z)=m_{e_1(i)}a_{e_1(i),i}(z),
\qquad
\pi_i g_i(z)\{1-e_i(z)\}=m_{e_0(i)}a_{e_0(i),i}(z).
\]
Generate \(U,Z,X,Y,W\) using these kernels, the supplied \(b_i\), and \(Y=Y_U(X)\).  The last display and proxy exclusion built into the construction give, for every incident \((e,i)\),
\[
P(U=i,Z=z,E=e,W=w)=m_ea_{ei}(z)b_i(w).
\]
Consequently,
\[
P(E=e,W=w,Z=z)=m_eT_e(w,z),
\qquad P(E=e)=m_e.
\]
Since \(\sum_e r_e(m_e/r_e)=1\), one has \(\min_e(m_e/r_e)\le1\).  Thus
\[
c_0=\frac12\min_e\frac{m_e}{r_e}\in(0,1],
\qquad
s_e=\frac{c_0r_e}{m_e}\in(0,1/2].
\]
Let \(S\mid E=e\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(s_e\).  Then
\[
P(S=1)=\sum_es_em_e=c_0\sum_er_e=c_0
\]
and
\[
P(E=e,W=w,Z=z\mid S=1)
=\frac{s_em_eT_e(w,z)}{c_0}
=r_eT_e(w,z)=p_{ewz}.
\]
This is a legal member of \(\mathcal F_A(p,m)\), and the already proved conditional calculation gives \(\tau=\tau_C\).  The conventions for \(\pi_i=0\), \(h_i(z)=0\), and zero entries of \(b_i\) show explicitly that no interiority of latent mechanisms was used.

For design B, the forward calculation is unchanged.  Instrument exogeneity supplies a common marginal \(g\) and
\[
\sum_{e:i\in\mathcal I_e}m_ea_{ei}(z)
=P_{\vartheta}(U=i,Z=z)=\pi_i g(z).
\]
Observed positivity implies \(P_{\vartheta}(Z=z)>0\), hence \(g\in\Delta_{\mathcal Z}^{\circ}\).  Furthermore \(q^X_z=P_{\vartheta}(X=1,Z=z)\), so the displayed cross-product inequality in \(\mathcal C_B(T)\) is exactly \(Z\not\!\perp X\).  This proves that the forward image lies in \(\mathcal C_B(T)\).

Conversely, start from \((b,a,m,g,\pi)\in\mathcal C_B(T)\).  The shared-
\(Z\) equations say exactly that the preceding \(h_i(z)\) equals \(\pi_i g(z)\).  The same treatment-kernel construction therefore uses the common \(g\), including when \(\pi_i=0\), and produces \(Z\perp U\).  The relevance inequality produces \(Z\not\!\perp X\), while the same selection construction reproduces \(p\).  Hence this is a member of \(\mathcal F_B(p)\) with target \(\tau_C\).  Both maps are therefore onto and target preserving.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:rank-two-orientation-atlas]\label{thm:rank-two-orientation-atlas}
If every \(T_e\) has rank two, \(\mathcal C_A(T)\) is exactly the union of at most \(2^4\) products of closed rational endpoint intervals obtained by orienting each observed segment. On each nonempty product, \(\tau_C\) is constant if and only if the endpoint intervals for response types \(1,2,4\) are singletons. Global rigidity holds if and only if those constants agree across all products; otherwise rational endpoint choices yield a legal target-changing certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cell \(e\) with \(\mathcal I_e=\{i,j\}\).  Selected-table positivity gives \(v_e(z)>0\), so define its normalized column point
\[
c_{ez}=\frac{T_e(\cdot,z)}{v_e(z)}\in\Delta_{\mathcal W}.
\]
If \(T_e=b_i a_{ei}^{\top}+b_j a_{ej}^{\top}\) has rank two, then \(b_i\ne b_j\) and neither coefficient row is identically zero.  Division by \(v_e(z)=a_{ei}(z)+a_{ej}(z)\) gives
\[
c_{ez}=q_{ez}b_i+(1-q_{ez})b_j,
\qquad
q_{ez}=\frac{a_{ei}(z)}{v_e(z)}\in[0,1].
\]
Thus the two endpoints lie on \(\mathcal L_e\) and the closed segment between them contains every \(c_{ez}\).  Conversely, if distinct \(b_i,b_j\in\mathcal L_e\cap\Delta_{\mathcal W}\) have this segment-containment property, the unique barycentric coordinates \(q_{ez}\in[0,1]\) define
\[
a_{ei}(z)=v_e(z)q_{ez},
\qquad
a_{ej}(z)=v_e(z)(1-q_{ez}),
\]
and reproduce \(T_e\).  This proves the cellwise equivalence without a positivity assumption on the latent coefficients.

Because \(T_e\) has rank two, its normalized column points are not all equal.  Choose a coordinate functional \(h_e\) that is nonconstant on them and put
\[
\alpha_e=\min_z c_{ez}(h_e),
\qquad
\beta_e=\max_z c_{ez}(h_e),
\qquad \alpha_e<\beta_e.
\]
Segment containment is equivalent to one of the two orientations
\[
b_i(h_e)\le\alpha_e<\beta_e\le b_j(h_e)
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
b_j(h_e)\le\alpha_e<\beta_e\le b_i(h_e).
\]
Enumerate these choices on the four cells.  For a fixed orientation \(\sigma\), all constraints on a particular \(b_i\) are precisely: membership in the probability simplex, membership in its two incident observable lines, and the two oriented endpoint halfspaces.  There are no inequalities involving two unfixed vertices after the orientation is chosen.  Hence the endpoint fiber on that chart is exactly
\[
I_1(\sigma)\times I_2(\sigma)\times I_3(\sigma)\times I_4(\sigma).
\]
Two affine lines either have empty intersection, one intersection point, or coincide.  In the coincident case, simplex and endpoint halfspaces cut the common line into a closed interval.  Gaussian elimination and scalar linear inequalities therefore make every \(I_i(\sigma)\) empty, a point, or a closed interval whose endpoints are rational expressions in the entries of \(T\).  There are at most \(2^4\) orientations, proving the asserted finite union.

It remains to characterize target constancy.  Let
\[
\mu_e=\sum_zT_e(\cdot,z),
\qquad
q_{ei}=\sum_z a_{ei}(z).
\]
Since \(\sum_zv_e(z)=1\),
\[
\mu_e=q_{ei}b_i+(1-q_{ei})b_j.
\]
Rank two rules out \(q_{ei}=0\) and \(q_{ei}=1\), so \(\mu_e\) is strictly between the endpoints.  In an affine coordinate \(x=h_e(b)\),
\[
q_{ei}=\frac{x_j-x_{\mu}}{x_j-x_i}.
\]
With one endpoint fixed, this is strictly monotone in the other endpoint throughout the oriented feasible interval: its denominator never vanishes and its numerator is nonzero.  Consequently, a nondegenerate \(I_1(\sigma)\) changes the type-\(2\) weight in cell \(11\), a nondegenerate \(I_4(\sigma)\) changes the type-\(2\) weight in cell \(00\), and a nondegenerate \(I_2(\sigma)\) changes at least the type-\(2\) weight in cell \(11\).  Because the chart is a Cartesian product, the other endpoints can be held fixed during each variation.  Thus \(\tau_C\) is constant on a nonempty chart if and only if \(I_1(\sigma),I_2(\sigma),I_4(\sigma)\) are all singletons.  The interval for type \(3\) is irrelevant to both coordinates of \(\tau_C\).

Global constancy is equivalent to chartwise constancy plus equality of the finitely many chart constants.  If either test fails, choose two endpoint tuples differing in the relevant interval, or one tuple from each of two disagreeing charts.  Scalar interval endpoints and rational interior parameters yield exact choices over the ordered field generated by \(T\); the barycentric formula above yields their nonnegative coefficients.  The cycle-to-SCM lift then gives two legal full SCMs with the same \((p,m)\) and different targets.  This also proves the stated certificate claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the complete boundary result on the largest regular-rank branch and exhibits target rigidity without factor uniqueness. \textit{Gap.} Rank-two segment geometry is standard, but no prior result glues four labeled cells and characterizes only PN/PS constancy across orientation components. \textit{Consumer.} It certifies assignment-boundary PN/PS values on singular support faces.

\begin{theorem}[thm:iv-residual-forest]\label{thm:iv-residual-forest}
Fix \((b,a)\in\mathcal C_A(T)\) and write \(\rho_z=g(z)/g(z_0)>0\). For every response type \(i\) and its incident cells \(e,f\), define \(D_{ei}(\rho)=(a_{ei}(z)-\rho_z a_{ei}(z_0))_{z\ne z_0}\). Positive unknown margins satisfying the response-level shared-\(Z\) equations exist if and only if, at each \(i\), both residuals vanish or they are nonzero and negatively proportional; the nonzero links determine positive margin ratios, whose product equals one on every active cycle, while each forest component retains one positive scale subject to normalization. Adding \(q^X_zg(z')\ne q^X_{z'}g(z)\) for some \((z,z')\) is necessary and sufficient for membership in \(\mathcal C_B(T)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((b,a)\in\mathcal C_A(T)\), a baseline \(z_0\), and a positive projective vector \(\rho\) with \(\rho_{z_0}=1\).  For positive margins \(m\), set
\[
h_i(z)=\sum_{e:i\in\mathcal I_e}m_ea_{ei}(z).
\]
The response-level shared-\(Z\) equations are \(h_i(z)=\pi_i g(z)\).  If \(g(z)/g(z_0)=\rho_z\), subtracting \(\rho_z h_i(z_0)\) gives, for the two cells \(e,f\) incident to type \(i\),
\[
m_eD^{\mathrm{res}}_{ei}(\rho)+m_fD^{\mathrm{res}}_{fi}(\rho)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(m_e,m_f>0\), equation (1) has exactly the following possibilities.  Both residuals are zero; exactly one is zero, which is impossible; or both are nonzero and
\[
D^{\mathrm{res}}_{fi}(\rho)
=-\frac{m_e}{m_f}D^{\mathrm{res}}_{ei}(\rho).
\]
Thus the nonzero vectors are negatively proportional, and any nonzero coordinate determines the positive ratio \(m_f/m_e\).  Equality of the ratios obtained from different coordinates is exactly the vanishing of all corresponding two-by-two minors, together with the required opposite-sign pivot inequality.

Regard cells as vertices and response types as links.  The incidence graph is the four-cycle \(11-01-10-00-11\).  Along an active link the preceding ratio fixes the masses at its endpoints.  The product of directed ratios around an active cycle is one because the actual mass ratios telescope.  Every proper subgraph of a four-cycle is a forest, and on each of its connected components all masses are fixed up to one positive scale.  Conversely, if the local residual alternatives hold and the product is one whenever all four links are active, choose one positive root mass in every forest component and propagate the prescribed ratios.  Cycle consistency makes the propagated value path independent.  Finally choose positive component scales and normalize them so that \(\sum_em_e=1\).  The resulting margins satisfy (1) at every response type; zero-residual links impose no further restriction.  This proves the necessity and sufficiency of the residual-forest rules.

For the converse construction, equation (1) says
\[
h_i(z)=\rho_z h_i(z_0)\qquad(i=1,2,3,4;\ z\in\mathcal Z).
\]
Let \(R=\sum_z\rho_z\).  Using \(\sum_z\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_e}a_{ei}(z)=1\) and \(\sum_em_e=1\),
\[
1=\sum_{i,z}h_i(z)=R\sum_i h_i(z_0).
\]
Therefore
\[
g(z)=\frac{\rho_z}{R},
\qquad
\pi_i=R h_i(z_0)
\]
are respectively a strictly positive probability vector and a nonnegative probability vector, and they satisfy \(h_i(z)=\pi_i g(z)\).  This remains valid when a response type has zero mass.

Finally, under the reconstructed common \(g\),
\[
q^X_z=\sum_{e:e_X=1}m_e\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_e}a_{ei}(z)
=P(X=1,Z=z),
\qquad g(z)=P(Z=z).
\]
Because every \(g(z)>0\), \(Z\perp X\) holds if and only if all projective ratios \(q^X_z/g(z)\) are equal, equivalently if and only if
\[
q^X_zg(z')=q^X_{z'}g(z)
\quad\hbox{for every }z,z'.
\]
Its negation is precisely the relevance inequality in \(\mathcal C_B(T)\).  The cycle-to-SCM lift then proves that the residual rules plus this inequality are also sufficient for a legal design-B SCM.
\end{proof}

[K(t_0,\upsilon):K(t_0)]\le D_{E,d}.                       \tag{9}
\]
To verify the required tower bound directly, let \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) be a \(K\)-basis of \(K(t_0)\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_s\) be a \(K(t_0)\)-basis of \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\).  The \(rs\) products \(u_iv_j\) span \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\) over \(K\).  Equations (8)--(9) therefore imply

[K(t_0,\upsilon):K]
\le [K(t_0):K]\,[K(t_0,\upsilon):K(t_0)]
\le D_{S,d}D_{E,d}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Every second-stage witness coordinate belongs to \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\), so (10) bounds its degree over \(K\).  The initial coordinates are instead bounded by (7); they need not belong to \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\).  It follows that

\mathcal D_d=\max\{D_{S,d},D_{S,d}D_{E,d}\}              \tag{11}
\]
bounds every returned coordinate without asserting any containment of the initial sample field in the second-stage field.  The tower-normalization, square-free-factorization, Thom-sign, and root-isolation procedures supplied by the effective-semialgebraic lemma do not increase the relevant field degree.  Allowing two fixed decision/status records in addition to the records produced by the two calls gives

R_d=2+A_{S,d}+A_{E,d}.                                    \tag{12}
\]
If the raw fiber is empty, the second call is omitted, so (12) remains an upper bound.  Both calls are made on the one raw fiber; hence there is no chart multiplier.

\operatorname{ht}(u\pm v)\le
\operatorname{ht}(u)+\operatorname{ht}(v)+2,
\qquad

\le\operatorname{ht}(u)+\operatorname{ht}(v)+1,           \tag{17}

where the last expression is used only when \(v\ne0\).  There are at most \(4k\ell+4\le8Q\) supplied rational coordinates across the two observable simplexes. In the ratio \(T_e(w,z)=p_{ewz}/r_e\), the denominator \(B\) cancels. =\max_{d\in\{A,B\}}
  \{h_{S,d},h_{E,d},h_{F,0,d},h_{F,1,d}\}                \tag{22}

bounds its coefficient and isolating-data lengths.

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:separated-wald]\label{thm:separated-wald}
Uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathfrak R_{\varepsilon}\), under independent multinomial sampling and \(n/n_m\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\) when margins are estimated, the deterministic selector \(\widehat\chi_n\) chooses an incident first-order-compatible rigid chart and \(\widehat\gamma_n=1\) with probability tending to one. On these certified separated rigid charts, \(\widehat\Omega_n\xrightarrow{P}\Omega_{\zeta}\), \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)\Rightarrow N(0,V_{\tau})\), and \(\widehat V_n\xrightarrow{P}V_{\tau}\). Because \(\lambda_{\min}(V_{\tau})\ge\varepsilon\), scalar contrasts and the elliptical branch of \(W_n\) have asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\). If no chart is certified, the total procedure returns \(\widehat\chi_n=\bot\), \(\widehat\tau_n=(0,0)\), \(\widehat V_n=I_2\), and \(W_n=[0,1]^2\), with no covariance-consistency or Wald-coverage assertion for that fallback branch.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(H\) be a compact subset of \(\mathfrak R_{\varepsilon}\); all uniform statements below are over \(\zeta\in H\).  Under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}multinomial\text{-}sampling}\), the reduced selected-table coordinates are averages of bounded categorical vectors.  In design A with estimated margins, the margin coordinates come from an independent sample, and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}margin\text{-}sample\text{-}scaling}\) gives \(n/n_m\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\).  Consequently some finite \(C_H\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in H}
 E_{\zeta}\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2^2
 \le \frac{C_H}{n}.                                      \tag{48}
\]
The margin term is absent when margins are known.  Markov's inequality implies
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in H}P_{\zeta}
 \{\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2>\delta\}
 \le\frac{C_H}{n\delta^2},
 \qquad
 \widehat\zeta_n-\zeta=O_P(n^{-1/2})                    \tag{49}
\]
uniformly on \(H\).

The selected-table covariance is
\(\operatorname{diag}(p^-)-p^-p^{-\top}\).  Independence and the relative sample-size limit add the block
\(\lambda\{\operatorname{diag}(m^-)-m^-m^{-\top}\}\) when margins are estimated.  These are exactly the blocks of \(\Omega_{\zeta}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}multinomial\text{-}clt\text{-}and\text{-}delta}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta)
 \Rightarrow N(0,\Omega_{\zeta}).                         \tag{50}
\]
This convergence is uniform on \(H\): from any sequence in \(H\), compactness extracts a convergent subsequence; bounded categorical summands satisfy the uniform characteristic-function expansion
\[
 E_{\zeta_n}\exp\{iu^{\top}\xi_{n,1}/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{u^{\top}\Omega_{\zeta_n}u}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),    \tag{51}
\]
and multiplying the selected and independently rescaled margin characteristic functions proves (50) along the subsequence.  The subsequence criterion yields uniformity.  Since the covariance formula is continuous in the cell probabilities, (49) also gives
\[
 \widehat\Omega_n\xrightarrow{P}\Omega_{\zeta}          \tag{52}
\]
uniformly on \(H\).

For each \(\zeta\in H\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}separated\text{-}rigid\text{-}stratum}\) makes
\[
 \Gamma(\zeta)=\{\chi:d_{\chi}(\zeta)=0,
                    \ \eta_{\chi}(\zeta)\ge\varepsilon\}
\]
nonempty.  Distance to a closed set is one-Lipschitz, so for \(\chi\in\Gamma(\zeta)\),
\[
 d_{\chi}(\widehat\zeta_n)
 \le\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2.                       \tag{53}
\]
There are finitely many labels, and the normalized slack functions have the continuous extensions required by the same definition.  Compactness supplies \(\delta_H>0\) such that
\[
 \|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2<\delta_H
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \eta_{\chi}(\widehat\zeta_n)\ge\varepsilon/2
 \quad(\chi\in\Gamma(\zeta)).                           \tag{54}
\]
Because \(t_n=n^{-1/4}\), (49), (53), and (54) show uniformly that at least one incident chart passes the selector inequalities with probability tending to one.

Conversely, every incompatible chart closure is at distance at least \(\varepsilon\) from \(\zeta\).  Hence
\[
 d_{\chi}(\widehat\zeta_n)
 \ge\varepsilon-\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2.           \tag{55}
\]
On the event where the last norm is less than \(\varepsilon/2\), (55) exceeds \(t_n\) for all large \(n\).  The exhaustive atlas certification and lexicographic rule therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in H}P_{\zeta}
 \{\widehat\chi_n=\bot\ \text{or }\widehat\chi_n
   \text{ is not incident and first-order compatible}\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                       \tag{56}
\]

On the complementary event, rigidity and first-order compatibility give
\[
 F_{\widehat\chi_n}(\zeta)=\tau,
 \qquad
 J_{\widehat\chi_n}(\zeta)=J(\zeta),                     \tag{57}
\]
where \(J(\zeta)\) is the common incident Jacobian.  Twice continuous differentiability, the finite chart set, and compactness give a common Hessian bound.  Taylor's theorem and (49) yield
\[
 \widehat\tau_n-\tau
 =J(\zeta)(\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta)+R_n,
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\|R_n\|_2=o_P(1)                                \tag{58}
\]
uniformly.  Equations (50), (57), (58), and the delta method give
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)
 \Rightarrow
 N\!\left(0,J(\zeta)\Omega_{\zeta}J(\zeta)^{\top}\right)
 =N(0,V_{\tau}).                                          \tag{59}
\]

Continuity of the derivatives, (49), and (52) show that
\[
 \widetilde V_n=
 J_{\widehat\chi_n}(\widehat\zeta_n)\widehat\Omega_n
 J_{\widehat\chi_n}(\widehat\zeta_n)^{\top}
 \xrightarrow{P}V_{\tau}                                 \tag{60}
\]
uniformly.  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}separated\text{-}rigid\text{-}stratum}\) gives \(\lambda_{\min}(V_{\tau})\ge\varepsilon\).  Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and (60) imply
\[
 P_{\zeta}\{\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde V_n)>\varepsilon/2\}
 \longrightarrow1                                        \tag{61}
\]
uniformly.  Hence \(\widehat\gamma_n=1\) with probability tending to one and, on that event, \(\widehat V_n=\widetilde V_n\); therefore \(\widehat V_n\xrightarrow{P}V_{\tau}\).

For every fixed nonzero contrast vector \(h\), Slutsky's lemma and the eigenvalue bound give
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\,h^{\top}(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)}
      {(h^{\top}\widehat V_nh)^{1/2}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1),                                      \tag{62}
\]
so the corresponding two-sided scalar Wald interval has coverage tending to \(1-\alpha\).  Likewise,
\[
 n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)^{\top}\widehat V_n^{-1}
   (\widehat\tau_n-\tau)
 \Rightarrow\chi^2_2.                                    \tag{63}
\]
Because the true target lies in \([0,1]^2\), intersecting the ellipse with that square does not alter its coverage event.  Continuity of the chi-square law at \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\) proves asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\) for the elliptical branch.

If no chart is certified, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}wald\text{-}estimator}\) gives \(\widehat\chi_n=\bot\).  The total-map clauses give \(F_{\bot}=(0,0)\), \(J_{\bot}=0\), and a zero raw sandwich matrix, which is not positive definite.  Hence \(\widehat\gamma_n=0\) and
\[
 \widehat\tau_n=(0,0),\qquad
 \widehat V_n=I_2,\qquad
 W_n=[0,1]^2.                                              \tag{64}
\]
The consistency and Wald conclusions used the high-probability event (56), so no such assertion is made for this fallback branch.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The chart rule, covariance formula, and nonsingularity margin make regular-stratum Wald inference operational and falsifiable. \textit{Gap.} The anchor and earlier response-type estimators establish regular-case normality only for their inverse formulas, not for deterministically selected rational and simple algebraic rigid charts. \textit{Consumer.} Separated empirical tables obtain reproducible standard errors and Wald regions on every first-order-compatible atlas chart.

\begin{theorem}[thm:confidence-fiber-coverage]\label{thm:confidence-fiber-coverage}
For the Clopper--Pearson preliminary region \(C_n\), profile discrepancy \(\Delta_n\), and Berger--Boos calibration in \(\operatorname{pv}_n\),
\[
P_{\vartheta}\{\tau(\vartheta)\in K_n\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
at every sample size for every \(\vartheta\) in either design.  This bound holds along arbitrary sequences approaching \(\mathfrak D\), including point-to-set transitions.

The following additional Hausdorff conclusion is restricted to locally saturated separated rigid profiles.  Uniformly over any compact subset of \(\mathfrak R_{\varepsilon}\) for which there are a fixed neighborhood radius and one twice continuously differentiable map \(F\) such that every observable law \(q\) in that neighborhood is compatible, its identified set is \(\mathcal T_A(q)=\{F(q)\}\) in design A or \(\mathcal T_B(q)=\{F(q)\}\) in design B, and every incident chart continuation equals \(F\),
\[
\sqrt n\,d_{\mathrm H}(K_n,W_n)\xrightarrow{P}0.
\]
Equivalently, on these locally saturated profiles the exact local profile boundary and the Wald ellipse have the same first-order quadratic expansion and \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\) calibration.  At a law with a non-singleton identified set, without the separated-chart conditions, or without local saturation, the theorem asserts coverage only and makes no claim that \(\operatorname{diam}(K_n)\) shrinks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(q_0\) be the true observable law, including the independent margin law in design A when margins are estimated, and set \(t_0=\tau(\vartheta)\).  Each selected-table count and each applicable margin count has a binomial marginal distribution.  Exact two-sided Clopper--Pearson inversion gives the allocated marginal coverage, and the stated Bonferroni allocation therefore gives
\[
 P_{q_0}\{q_0\notin C_n\}\le\beta_n.                    \tag{65}
\]

For a fixed compatible pair \((q,t)\), define the exact nonrandomized upper-tail value
\[
 G_{q,t}=P_q\{\Delta_n^{\mathrm{rep}}(t)
                 \ge\Delta_n^{\mathrm{obs}}(t)\}.
\]
It is super-uniform.  Indeed, order the finite support of \(\Delta_n(t)\).  For \(u\in[0,1]\), let \(d\) be the least support point whose upper-tail probability is at most \(u\), if such a point exists.  Then
\(\{G_{q,t}\le u\}\subseteq\{\Delta_n^{\mathrm{obs}}(t)\ge d\}\), whence
\[
 P_q\{G_{q,t}\le u\}\le u.                              \tag{66}
\]
If no such \(d\) exists, the event on the left is empty and (66) remains true.

Compatibility gives \(t_0\in\mathcal T(q_0)\).  On \(\{q_0\in C_n\}\), the supremum in \(\operatorname{pv}_n(t_0)\) includes \(q_0\), so
\[
 \operatorname{pv}_n(t_0)
 \ge\min\{1,\beta_n+G_{q_0,t_0}\}.                       \tag{67}
\]
Since \(\beta_n\le\alpha/2<\alpha\), (65)--(67) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{q_0}\{t_0\notin K_n\}
 &\le P_{q_0}\{q_0\notin C_n\}
   +P_{q_0}\{G_{q_0,t_0}\le\alpha-\beta_n\}\\
 &\le\beta_n+(\alpha-\beta_n)=\alpha.
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{68}
\]
This is a finite-sample, pointwise argument.  Applying (68) to every member of any triangular sequence proves the same coverage along arbitrary approaches to \(\mathfrak D\), including point-to-set transitions, without any rank, support, or boundary regularity assumption.

For the Hausdorff assertion, fix a compact subset satisfying the stated local-saturation hypotheses.  Positivity and compactness bound every relevant multinomial probability away from zero.  Since eventually \(\beta_n=n^{-2}\), simultaneous Clopper--Pearson inversion and a union bound give
\[
 \sup_{q\in C_n}\|q-q_0\|_2
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)=o_P(1)      \tag{69}
\]
uniformly.  To see the asserted order, for \(x>p\), exponential Markov inequality at
\(\theta=\log\{x(1-p)/(p(1-x))\}\) gives
\[
 P_p(\widehat p\ge x)
 \le e^{-nD(x\Vert p)}
 \le e^{-2n(x-p)^2};                                      \tag{70}
\]
the last inequality follows because the second derivative of binary divergence in \(x\) is \(1/\{x(1-x)\}\ge4\), while its value and first derivative vanish at \(x=p\).  The lower tail is symmetric, and inversion at tail levels of order \(n^{-2}\) proves (69).  With probability tending uniformly to one, all laws entering the Berger--Boos supremum lie in the common saturated neighborhood, where compatibility holds and \(t\in\mathcal T(q)\) is equivalent to \(t=F(q)\).

Write \(z\) for reduced observable coordinates and \(\widehat z\) for their unrestricted multinomial maximum-likelihood estimate.  On the positive compact neighborhood, a third-order Taylor expansion of the multinomial log likelihood gives, uniformly for \(z-\widehat z=O(n^{-1/2})\),
\[
 2\{\ell_n(\widehat z)-\ell_n(z)\}
 =n(z-\widehat z)^{\top}\Omega_{\zeta}^{-1}
       (z-\widehat z)+o_P(1).                             \tag{71}
\]
For two samples, independence and \(n/n_m\to\lambda\) give the two blocks in \(\Omega_{\zeta}\).  Its inverse exists in reduced coordinates because the local cell probabilities are positive.

Let \(J=\partial F(\zeta)/\partial\zeta^{\top}\).  Twice continuous differentiability gives
\[
 F(z)-F(\widehat z)
 =J(z-\widehat z)+O(\|z-\widehat z\|_2^2).                \tag{72}
\]
Strict concavity of the interior multinomial likelihood and compactness give a positive Kullback--Leibler gap outside the fixed saturated neighborhood.  Therefore every constrained maximizer for a bounded root-\(n\) target displacement is local with probability tending uniformly to one.

Minimizing the leading quadratic form in (71) subject to the linearized constraint in (72), the Lagrange first-order condition gives
\[
 z-\widehat z
 =\Omega_{\zeta}J^{\top}
   (J\Omega_{\zeta}J^{\top})^{-1}(t-\widehat\tau_n).
\]
Substitution and \(V_{\tau}=J\Omega_{\zeta}J^{\top}\) yield
\[
 \Delta_n(t)
 =n(t-\widehat\tau_n)^{\top}V_{\tau}^{-1}
       (t-\widehat\tau_n)+o_P(1)                          \tag{73}
\]
uniformly for bounded \(\sqrt n\|t-\widehat\tau_n\|_2\).  The multiplier is unique because \(\lambda_{\min}(V_{\tau})\ge\varepsilon\).  The same likelihood expansion shows uniform divergence outside a bounded local target set.

Apply (71)--(73) to independent replicates, uniformly over the shrinking set (69), and use Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}multinomial\text{-}clt\text{-}and\text{-}delta}\).  Covariance consistency is supplied by Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}separated\text{-}wald}\).  It follows that
\[
 \sup_{q\in C_n:\,t=F(q)}
 \left|
 P_q\{\Delta_n^{\mathrm{rep}}(t)\ge\Delta_n^{\mathrm{obs}}(t)\}
 -\overline F_{\chi^2_2}\!\left(
 n(t-\widehat\tau_n)^{\top}\widehat V_n^{-1}
 (t-\widehat\tau_n)\right)
 \right|=o_P(1).                                          \tag{74}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(V_{\tau}\) forces \(J\) to have row rank two.  Since \(F\) maps a neighborhood into \([0,1]^2\), the local open-mapping property then forces \(F(\zeta)\) into the interior of the square.  Thus truncation by \([0,1]^2\) is locally inactive.

The chi-square distribution is continuous and \(\beta_n=o(1)\).  Equation (74) sandwiches \(K_n\), with probability tending uniformly to one, between ellipsoids obtained by changing \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\) by \(\pm\delta_n\) for some \(\delta_n=o_P(1)\).  The eigenvalues of \(\widehat V_n\) are uniformly bounded above and away from zero.  Radial comparison gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm H}(K_n,W_n)
 \le n^{-1/2}O_P(\delta_n)=o_P(n^{-1/2}),                 \tag{75}
\]
which is the claimed compact-uniform Hausdorff limit.  Without singleton local saturation, chart separation, or nonsingularity, (71)--(75) need not hold; the universally valid conclusion is only the finite-sample coverage (68).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite-sample coverage is universal, while the optional profile-to-Wald comparison is proved under the exact local saturation needed for regular likelihood geometry. \textit{Gap.} Smooth chart continuation alone does not make a rank-constrained observable model locally profile-saturated. \textit{Consumer.} Users retain honest transition coverage without overinterpreting Wald geometry outside regular saturated strata.

\begin{proposition}[prop:implementation-known-selection-normalization]\label{prop:implementation-known-selection-normalization}
Implementation corollary (definitional normalization). If every selection probability \(s_e=P_{\vartheta}(S=1\mid E=e)\) is known and positive, then the population margin is identified by \(m_e=(r_e/s_e)/\sum_{f\in\mathcal E}(r_f/s_f)\). The IV-plus-proxy implementation with known selection therefore intersects \(\mathcal C_B(T)\) with this normalized value of \(m\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Outcome-dependent selection makes \(s_e=P_{\vartheta}(S=1\mid E=e)\) well defined as a function of \(e\) alone.  When every \(s_e>0\), Bayes' identity gives
\[
r_e=P_{\vartheta}(E=e\mid S=1)
=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(S=1\mid E=e)P_{\vartheta}(E=e)}{P_{\vartheta}(S=1)}
=\frac{s_em_e}{c}.
\]
Thus \(m_e=cr_e/s_e\).  Summing over \(e\) and using \(\sum_em_e=1\) yields
\[
c^{-1}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal E}\frac{r_f}{s_f},
\qquad
m_e=\frac{r_e/s_e}{\sum_{f\in\mathcal E}r_f/s_f}.
\]
All divisions are legitimate by selected-table positivity and the assumed positivity of \(s_e\).  In the IV-plus-proxy construction, \(m\) is otherwise a nuisance coordinate of \(\mathcal C_B(T)\); known selection therefore imposes exactly this additional equality, which is the stated intersection.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:validation-tian-pearl-sanity-check]\label{prop:validation-tian-pearl-sanity-check}
Validation corollary (published Tian--Pearl sanity check). Let \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\). Suppose, locally for this \(\vartheta\), that \(S=1\) almost surely, \(X\perp U\) under \(P_{\vartheta}\), and \(P_{\vartheta}(U=3)=0\). Then
\[
\tau(\vartheta)=\left(
\frac{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)},
\frac{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)}
\right),
\]
where both denominators are strictly positive. If, in addition, \(\lvert\mathcal T_A(p,m)\rvert=1\), then the unique element of \(\mathcal T_A(p,m)\) is the displayed pair. These local hypotheses do not imply rigidity of the unrestricted design-A fiber and are not assumptions on either main design.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\pi_i=P_{\vartheta}(U=i)\) for \(i\in\{1,2,3,4\}\).  We first verify that every conditional probability appearing in the claim is defined.  Membership \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\), the definition \(p_{ewz}=P_{\vartheta}(E=e,W=w,Z=z\mid S=1)\), and the local condition \(P_{\vartheta}(S=1)=1\) give
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(E=e)
 =P_{\vartheta}(E=e\mid S=1)
 =\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}p_{ewz}
 =r_e.
\]
By selected-table positivity and the nonemptiness of the finite alphabets, \(r_e>0\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\).  In particular,
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(X=1)\ge P_{\vartheta}(E=11)=r_{11}>0,
 \qquad
 P_{\vartheta}(X=0)\ge P_{\vartheta}(E=00)=r_{00}>0,
\]
and hence
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)
 =\frac{r_{11}}{r_{11}+r_{10}}>0,
 \qquad
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)
 =\frac{r_{00}}{r_{00}+r_{01}}>0.
\]

By response consistency and the stated encoding of \(U\), under \(X=1\) the event \(Y=1\) occurs exactly when \(U\in\{1,2\}\), while under \(X=0\) the event \(Y=1\) occurs exactly when \(U\in\{1,3\}\), and the event \(Y=0\) occurs exactly when \(U\in\{2,4\}\).  The local independence \(X\perp U\), together with the just-established positivity of both treatment arms, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U\in\{1,2\}\mid X=1)=\pi_1+\pi_2,\\
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U\in\{1,3\}\mid X=0)=\pi_1+\pi_3,\\
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U\in\{2,4\}\mid X=0)=\pi_2+\pi_4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The local restriction \(P_{\vartheta}(U=3)=0\) says \(\pi_3=0\), so
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)=\pi_2.
\]
For type \(2\), response consistency gives \(Y=1\) when \(X=1\) and \(Y=0\) when \(X=0\).  Thus independence of \(X\) and \(U\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{PN}(\vartheta)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=1,Y=1)\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2,Y=1\mid X=1)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)}
 =\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=1)}{\pi_1+\pi_2}
 =\frac{\pi_2}{\pi_1+\pi_2}\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)},
\end{aligned}
\]
and, similarly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{PS}(\vartheta)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=0,Y=0)\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2,Y=0\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)}
 =\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=0)}{\pi_2+\pi_4}
 =\frac{\pi_2}{\pi_2+\pi_4}\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the displayed pair for the particular restricted SCM \(\vartheta\).

By the definition of the design-A target set, membership \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\) implies
\[
 \tau(\vartheta)\in
 \{\tau(\widetilde\vartheta):\widetilde\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\}
 =\mathcal T_A(p,m).
\]
Consequently, if \(\lvert\mathcal T_A(p,m)\rvert=1\), its unique element is \(\tau(\vartheta)\), which is the displayed pair.

For completeness, the local restrictions on this one member do not themselves force rigidity of its unrestricted fiber.  Here is an explicit witness.  Write \(M=\lvert\mathcal W\rvert\) and \(K=\lvert\mathcal Z\rvert\).  In both SCMs below take \(b_i(w)=1/M\), \(g_i(z)=1/K\), and \(s_e=1\) for every admissible \(i,w,z,e\).  For the first SCM take
\[

 \qquad e_i(z)=\frac12
\]
for every \(i,z\).  It satisfies \(S=1\) almost surely, \(X\perp U\), and \(P(U=3)=0\).  Directly applying the four response maps gives
\[
 (m_{11},m_{01},m_{10},m_{00})
 =\left(\frac13,\frac16,\frac16,\frac13\right),
 \qquad
 p_{ewz}=\frac{m_e}{MK}>0,
\]
and its target is \(\tau=(1/2,1/2)\).  For the second SCM take the same proxy and selection mechanisms but set
\[
 (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3,\pi_4)
 =\left(\frac13,\frac14,\frac16,\frac14\right)
\]
and, independently of \(z\), set
\[
 (e_1(z),e_2(z),e_3(z),e_4(z))
 =\left(\frac34,\frac13,\frac12,\frac13\right).
\]
The contributions of types \((1,2)\) to cell \(11\) are \((1/4,1/12)\); those of types \((1,3)\) to cell \(01\) are \((1/12,1/12)\); those of types \((3,4)\) to cell \(10\) are \((1/12,1/12)\); and those of types \((2,4)\) to cell \(00\) are \((1/6,1/6)\).  Hence this second SCM has exactly the same \(m\) and the same positive selected table \(p_{ewz}=m_e/(MK)\), while

\]
All displayed weights and assignment probabilities are nonnegative and normalized; the uniform conditional proxy mechanisms satisfy proxy exclusion, and constant selection satisfies outcome-dependent selection.  Thus both parameters are legal members of the same design-A fiber, but their targets differ.  This proves that imposing the three local restrictions on the singled-out \(\vartheta\) supplies a validation identity, not rigidity of the unrestricted fiber.  The restrictions are used only after membership in the main design has been assumed and therefore are local hypotheses of this corollary, not additional defining assumptions of either main design.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:mixed-support-elimination-complete]\label{thm:mixed-support-elimination-complete}
For every fixed finite \((\mathcal W,\mathcal Z)\), the following finite structural atlas is exhaustive.  It rejects a cell whose observed matrix has rank exceeding two.  A rank-two cell is represented by its two oriented segment modes, refined by all coefficient-support faces.  A rank-one cell is represented by the disjoint \(\mathrm L,\mathrm R,\mathrm E,\mathrm D\) modes of the rank-one normal-form lemma, with the \(\mathrm E\) mode split coordinatewise.  Products of the four local modes use one shared variable \(b_i\) at each response vertex, fix proxy supports, and retain endpoint equalities and inequalities, observed-line incidences, and guarded nonzero pivots as polynomial sign atoms.  If \(X^A_\chi\) denotes the resulting design-A charts, then
\[
\bigcup_\chi X^A_\chi=\mathcal C_A(T).
\]
In design B, adjoining \(\rho\), the zero-residual or active negatively proportional state at each response type, lexicographic nonzero pivots, forest propagation, active-cycle product-one constraints, positive component scales, normalization, and a signed relevance witness gives charts satisfying
\[
\bigcup_\chi X^B_\chi=\mathcal C_B(T).
\]
Writing \(k=|\mathcal Z|\) and \(\ell=|\mathcal W|\), the numbers of labeled templates are at most
\[
M_A=(2^{\ell}-1)^4(3^{k+1}+3)^4,
\qquad
M_B=M_A\,k(k-1)(2k-1)^4.
\]
Every chart has a finite Boolean semialgebraic description over \(\mathbb K_{\zeta}\), including every zero-support and coincident-endpoint face.  Thus the atlas is an exact field-tier structural classification and may be used to label or diagnose a raw-fiber output.  It is not a computational prerequisite for the direct raw-fiber rigidity decision made by \(\mathscr A\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}selected\text{-}table\text{-}positivity}\) gives \(r_e>0\), \(T_e(w,z)>0\), and
\[
 v_e(z)=\sum_wT_e(w,z)>0                                  \tag{76}
\]
for every \(e,z\).  Fix \(e\) with \(\mathcal I_e=\{i,j\}\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}cycle\text{-}fibers}\) gives
\[
 T_e=b_i a_{ei}^{\top}+b_j a_{ej}^{\top},
 \qquad
 a_{ei}+a_{ej}=v_e.                                       \tag{77}
\]
The first expression in (77) is a sum of two rank-one matrices, so \(\operatorname{rank}(T_e)\le2\); (76) rules out rank zero.  An observed rank exceeding two therefore makes both cycle fibers empty.

Suppose \(\operatorname{rank}(T_e)=2\) and define
\[
 c_{ez}=\frac{T_e(\cdot,z)}{v_e(z)}.
\]
Division is legal by (76).  Equation (77) gives
\[
 c_{ez}=\lambda_{ez}b_i+(1-\lambda_{ez})b_j,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{ez}=\frac{a_{ei}(z)}{v_e(z)}\in[0,1].           \tag{78}
\]
Rank two forces \(b_i\ne b_j\).  Conversely, if two endpoints on the observed affine line contain every \(c_{ez}\) in their closed segment, choose a coordinate \(h_e\) with \(b_i(h_e)\ne b_j(h_e)\).  The unique coefficients are
\[
 a_{ei}(z)=v_e(z)
 \frac{c_{ez}(h_e)-b_j(h_e)}{b_i(h_e)-b_j(h_e)},
 \qquad
 a_{ej}(z)=v_e(z)-a_{ei}(z).                              \tag{79}
\]
The denominator is nonzero by construction, and the segment inequalities make both terms nonnegative.  Reversing the endpoints gives the second orientation.  For each \(z\), the positive sum in (77) has exactly the three support alternatives \(a_{ei}(z)=0\), \(a_{ej}(z)=0\), or both positive.  Thus the two orientations and their coordinatewise support refinements exhaust the rank-two cell.

If \(\operatorname{rank}(T_e)=1\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}rank\text{-}one\text{-}lred\text{-}normal\text{-}forms}\) supplies the unique positive column point \(c_e\) and proves that the modes \(\mathrm L,\mathrm R,\mathrm E,\mathrm D\) are disjoint and exhaustive.  Its coordinatewise refinement of \(\mathrm E\) retains \(s_e(z)=0\), \(0<s_e(z)<v_e(z)\), and \(s_e(z)=v_e(z)\).  Hence inactive rows, equal endpoints, and every forced-zero boundary face are retained without division.

Take the product of the four local lists and identify the two occurrences of each shared response endpoint \(b_i\).  Endpoint-simplex and support conditions, endpoint equality or inequality, incidence with an observed affine line, segment order, and (77)--(79) become polynomial sign conditions after a denominator is cleared only on a chart recording its nonzero sign.  The exhaustive alternatives above show that every point of \(\mathcal C_A(T)\) belongs to one resulting chart.  Conversely, substitution of a chart point into (77), together with its simplex and support conditions, proves membership in \(\mathcal C_A(T)\).  Therefore
\[
 \bigcup_{\chi}X^A_{\chi}=\mathcal C_A(T).               \tag{80}
\]

For design B, fix a design-A cycle point and a positive projective coordinate \(\rho\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}iv\text{-}residual\text{-}forest}\) gives, at each response type, the exhaustive alternatives that both incident residuals vanish or that they are nonzero and negatively proportional; exactly one zero residual is infeasible.  In an active state the lexicographically first nonzero residual coordinate is a nonzero pivot, and its sign gives a finite refinement.  The induced positive margin ratios propagate from one positive scale per forest component, and an active cycle is consistent exactly when the product of its ratios is one.  Normalization fixes the remaining common scale.  Finally, relevance is the finite disjunction
\[
 q^X_zg(z')-q^X_{z'}g(z)>0
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 q^X_zg(z')-q^X_{z'}g(z)<0                               \tag{81}
\]
over unordered distinct pairs \(\{z,z'\}\).  The residual-forest theorem makes these conditions necessary and sufficient for positive margins, the response-level shared-\(Z\) equations, and relevance.  Hence
\[
 \bigcup_{\chi}X^B_{\chi}=\mathcal C_B(T).               \tag{82}
\]

It remains to count labeled templates.  Each proxy endpoint has a nonempty support, so the four shared endpoints have at most
\[
 (2^{\ell}-1)^4                                           \tag{83}
\]
support patterns.  A rank-two local library has \(2\cdot3^k\) oriented support modes.  A rank-one library has \(3^k\) refined \(\mathrm E\) modes and the three modes \(\mathrm L,\mathrm R,\mathrm D\).  Listing both possible positive ranks before consulting the observed rank therefore uses at most
\[
 2\cdot3^k+3^k+3=3^{k+1}+3                               \tag{84}
\]
local templates.  Multiplying (83) by the fourth power of (84) gives \(M_A\).  In design B, each of four response links has one zero-residual state or \(2(k-1)\) active pivot-and-sign states, giving \((2k-1)^4\) joint labels.  The unordered relevance pair and sign in (81) have \(2\binom{k}{2}=k(k-1)\) labels.  Multiplication gives \(M_B\).

Every coefficient in these polynomial conditions is obtained from \(T\) and \(v\).  Since \(T_e=p_e/r_e\) and \(r_e>0\), all coefficients belong to \(\mathbb K_{\zeta}\).  The finite list includes every support face by (78)--(82), so it is an exact field-tier structural classification and can be sampled or labeled with real-univariate and Thom data.  Equations (80) and (82) prove exhaustiveness.  The direct raw-fiber decision in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}atlas\text{-}handle}\) does not invoke this enumeration, so the atlas is diagnostic rather than a prerequisite for rigidity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem now carries exactly the exhaustive structural union and finite diagnostic template bound. \textit{Gap.} Boundary labeling requires the complete chart atlas, but causal target constancy does not require iterating those charts. \textit{Consumer.} Users may request an exact structural label for a raw-fiber output without paying for that enumeration in the minimal decision.

\begin{lemma}[lem:rank-one-lred-normal-forms]\label{lem:rank-one-lred-normal-forms}
Let \(e\in\mathcal E\) have \(\mathcal I_e=\{i,j\}\), and suppose that \(T_e\) has rank one.  There is then a unique \(c_e\in\Delta_{\mathcal W}^{\circ}\) such that \(T_e=c_ev_e^{\top}\).  Every nonnegative factorization of this cell in \(\mathcal C_A(T)\) belongs to exactly one of the following four modes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm L:& b_i\ne c_e,\quad b_j=c_e,\quad a_{ei}\equiv0,\quad a_{ej}=v_e,\\
\mathrm R:& b_i=c_e,\quad b_j\ne c_e,\quad a_{ei}=v_e,\quad a_{ej}\equiv0,\\
\mathrm E:& b_i=b_j=c_e,\quad a_{ei}=s_e,\quad a_{ej}=v_e-s_e,
             \quad 0\le s_e(z)\le v_e(z),\\
\mathrm D:& b_i\ne b_j,\quad 0<q_e<1,\quad
             c_e=q_eb_i+(1-q_e)b_j,\quad
             a_{ei}=q_ev_e,\quad a_{ej}=(1-q_e)v_e.
\end{array}
\]
In mode \(\mathrm E\), the coordinatewise alternatives \(s_e(z)=0\), \(0<s_e(z)<v_e(z)\), and \(s_e(z)=v_e(z)\) give an exhaustive finite support-face refinement.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Selected-table positivity gives \(T_e(w,z)>0\) and hence
\[
v_e(z)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}T_e(w,z)>0
\]
for every \(z\in\mathcal Z\).  Since \(T_e\) has rank one, all of its nonzero columns are proportional.  Normalizing each column by its positive sum therefore gives one common vector
\[
c_e=\frac{T_e(\cdot,z)}{v_e(z)}\in\Delta_{\mathcal W}^{\circ},
\qquad T_e=c_ev_e^{\top}.
\]
For a factorization in \(\mathcal C_A(T)\), put
\[
q_z=\frac{a_{ei}(z)}{v_e(z)}\in[0,1].
\]
The cell equation and \(a_{ei}(z)+a_{ej}(z)=v_e(z)\) yield, after division by the positive number \(v_e(z)\),
\[
c_e=q_zb_i+(1-q_z)b_j. \tag{1}
\]
If \(b_i=b_j\), equation (1) and normalization imply \(b_i=b_j=c_e\); the vector \(s_e=a_{ei}\) is otherwise arbitrary subject exactly to \(0\le s_e\le v_e\).  This is mode \(\mathrm E\).

Suppose instead that \(b_i\ne b_j\).  Choose \(w_\star\) with \(b_i(w_\star)\ne b_j(w_\star)\).  The \(w_\star\)-coordinate of (1) gives
\[
q_z=\frac{c_e(w_\star)-b_j(w_\star)}{b_i(w_\star)-b_j(w_\star)},
\]
which is independent of \(z\).  Denote it by \(q_e\).  If \(q_e=0\), equation (1) gives \(b_j=c_e\), and the inequalities \(b_i\ne b_j\) and \(a_{ei}=0\), \(a_{ej}=v_e\) give mode \(\mathrm L\).  The case \(q_e=1\) is mode \(\mathrm R\).  The remaining case \(0<q_e<1\) is mode \(\mathrm D\).  These cases are disjoint by their displayed equality and inequality conditions.  Conversely, direct substitution shows that every point in any displayed mode satisfies the cell equation and coefficient normalization.  Finally, for each of the finitely many \(z\), exactly one of \(s_e(z)=0\), \(0<s_e(z)<v_e(z)\), and \(s_e(z)=v_e(z)\) holds.  Their products over \(z\) are therefore a finite exhaustive refinement of mode \(\mathrm E\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:interior-cycle-to-positive-scm-lift]\label{lem:interior-cycle-to-positive-scm-lift}
Suppose a cycle point in design A has \(m\in\Delta_{\mathcal E}^{\circ}\), \(b_i(w)>0\) for all \((i,w)\), and \(a_{ei}(z)>0\) for every incident \((e,i,z)\).  Suppose a cycle point in design B satisfies the analogous strict inequalities, including the defining positivity of \(m\) and \(g\).  Then its full-SCM lift may be chosen with every response mass, proxy and instrument kernel, treatment probability, and selection probability strictly between its relevant boundary values.  Consequently, two target-changing cycle witnesses that both lie in interior support charts have strictly positive full-SCM lifts.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider design A first.  For every response type \(i\), define
\[
h_i(z)=\sum_{e:\,i\in\mathcal I_e}m_ea_{ei}(z),
\qquad
\pi_i=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}h_i(z).
\]
The strict positivity of \(m_e\) and of every incident coefficient gives \(h_i(z)>0\) and \(\pi_i>0\).  Hence
\[
g_i(z)=\frac{h_i(z)}{\pi_i}>0
\]
is a normalized instrument kernel.  Let \(e_1(i)\) and \(e_0(i)\) be the two incident cells of type \(i\) having treatment coordinates one and zero.  Define
\[
e_i(z)=\frac{m_{e_1(i)}a_{e_1(i),i}(z)}{h_i(z)}.
\]
Both the numerator and the omitted term \(m_{e_0(i)}a_{e_0(i),i}(z)\) are positive, so \(0<e_i(z)<1\).  The given \(b_i(w)\) are strictly positive.  Since \(p\) is positive, \(r_e>0\).  Choose
\[
c_0=\frac12\min_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac{m_e}{r_e}>0,
\qquad
s_e=\frac{c_0r_e}{m_e}.
\]
Then \(0<s_e\le1/2<1\), and
\[
\sum_e s_em_e=c_0\sum_er_e=c_0.
\]
The direct lift calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:cycle\text{-}to\text{-}scm\text{-}lift}\) now gives the required selected law and target, while the displayed inequalities prove strict positivity of every stochastic mechanism.

In design B, the shared-instrument equations give
\[
h_i(z)=\sum_{e:\,i\in\mathcal I_e}m_ea_{ei}(z)=\pi_i g(z).
\]
The left side is strictly positive, and \(g(z)>0\), so \(\pi_i>0\).  The same treatment-probability formula has a positive numerator and positive complementary incident contribution and is therefore in \((0,1)\).  The same choice of \(c_0\) makes every selection probability lie in \((0,1)\).  Thus the IV lift is also strictly positive.  Applying the construction separately to two interior cycle witnesses proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:one-sample-all-chart-rigidity]\label{lem:one-sample-all-chart-rigidity}
Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_J\) be a finite family of one-copy semialgebraic sets over an effective ordered field \(K\), put \(X=\bigcup_{j=1}^J X_j\), and let \(f:X\to\mathbb R^2\) be a polynomial map. There is an exact one-sample/all-chart procedure with the following properties. It first tests every \(X_j\) for emptiness. If \(X\neq\varnothing\), it samples one \(x_0\in X\), puts \(t_0=f(x_0)\), and, over \(K(t_0)\), tests every refinement
\[
 R_j(x_0)=\left\{x\in X_j:
 (f_1(x)-t_{0,1})^2+(f_2(x)-t_{0,2})^2>0\right\}.
\]
Then \(f(X)=\{t_0\}\) if and only if every \(R_j(x_0)\) is empty. If some refinement is nonempty, the procedure samples \(x_1\in R_j(x_0)\), and \(f(x_1)\neq f(x_0)\). The procedure uses no formula containing two independently quantified copies of \(X\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every \(X_j\) is given by finitely many polynomial sign conditions, so the bounded-algebraic-sampling algorithm in \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}semialgebraic\text{-}sampling\text{-}existential}\) decides its emptiness. Because the family is finite,
\[
 X=\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 X_j=\varnothing\ \text{for every }j. \tag{1}
\]
If the left side of (1) is false, choose the first nonempty set in the fixed ordering and let its sampled point be \(x_0\). The coordinates of \(t_0=f(x_0)\) lie in the real algebraic field generated by the sample representation. Hence each \(R_j(x_0)\) is a basic semialgebraic set over \(K(t_0)\), obtained from the formula for \(X_j\) by adding one strict polynomial inequality. The existential-realizability part of the cited lemma therefore decides its emptiness and samples it when it is nonempty.

Suppose first that every \(R_j(x_0)\) is empty. For any \(x\in X\), there is a \(j\) with \(x\in X_j\). Since \(x\notin R_j(x_0)\) and a sum of two real squares is nonnegative,
\[
 0=(f_1(x)-t_{0,1})^2+(f_2(x)-t_{0,2})^2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)=t_0. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(f(X)=\{t_0\}\). Conversely, if \(f(X)=\{t_0\}\), the strict inequality defining every \(R_j(x_0)\) is false, so every refinement is empty. Finally, if some \(R_j(x_0)\) is nonempty, its sampled point \(x_1\) satisfies
\[
 (f_1(x_1)-f_1(x_0))^2+(f_2(x_1)-f_2(x_0))^2>0,
\]
which is equivalent to \(f(x_1)\neq f(x_0)\). Only \(x\) is quantified in each refinement; \(x_0\) enters through fixed algebraic coefficients. This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The result is a theorem-level exact symbolic procedure, not a claim of a completed production implementation or favorable growing-alphabet complexity. The mandatory decision works on the raw legal fiber and makes at most one sampling and one unequal-target existential call. The finite structural atlas can be produced diagnostically but is not required for compatibility or rigidity. Over arbitrary real observables, computation is relative to effective coefficient-field arithmetic, order, sign determination, and real-root oracles. The initial sample generally belongs to its own sampling extension and is not asserted to lie in the field generated by the sampled target and a second-stage witness; the two coordinate-degree bounds are therefore proved separately. Rational height also accounts for summing up to k initial coordinates to form each target coordinate before the second-stage call. Strictly positive twins require both cycle witnesses to lie in interior support charts. The exact-coverage and locally saturated Hausdorff qualifications are unchanged. The released numerical GEUVADIS count table and binarization thresholds remain unavailable in the frozen materials, so no numerical dataset verdict is asserted.
