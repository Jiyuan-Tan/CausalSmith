Fix \(d\in\{A,B\}\).  We count a direct Boolean polynomial presentation of the raw fiber specified by Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}cycle\text{-}fibers}\).

In design A, the four endpoint vectors \(b_i\in\Delta_{\mathcal W}\) contribute \(4\ell\) variables.  Each of the four cells has two incident coefficient vectors of length \(k\), so the coefficients \(a_{ei}(z)\) contribute \(8k\) variables.  Hence
\[
N_A=4\ell+8k.                                                   \tag{1}
\]
Endpoint nonnegativity contributes \(4\ell\) sign atoms and endpoint normalization contributes four equality atoms.  Coefficient nonnegativity contributes \(8k\) sign atoms.  For each cell \(e\), the equations
\[
a_{ei}(z)+a_{ej}(z)=v_e(z),\qquad \{i,j\}=\mathcal I_e,
\]
contribute \(k\) equality atoms, hence \(4k\) in total.  Finally, the factorization equations
\[
T_e(w,z)=b_i(w)a_{ei}(z)+b_j(w)a_{ej}(z)
\]
contribute \(4k\ell\) equality atoms.  Therefore
\[
S_A=4k\ell+4\ell+12k+4.                                       \tag{2}
\]
The factorization equations are bilinear in \((b,a)\), and every other design-A atom is linear.  Thus this presentation has maximum polynomial degree
\[
D_A=2.                                                         \tag{3}
\]

The raw design-B presentation adds the four coordinates of \(m\), the \(k\) coordinates of \(g\), and the four coordinates of \(\pi\).  Consequently,
\[
N_B=N_A+4+k+4=4\ell+9k+8.                                    \tag{4}
\]
Strict positivity and normalization of \(m\) add five atoms; strict positivity and normalization of \(g\) add \(k+1\); and nonnegativity and normalization of \(\pi\) add five.  The response-level shared-\(Z\) equations
\[
\sum_{e:i\in\mathcal I_e}m_ea_{ei}(z)=\pi_i g(z),
\qquad (i,z)\in\{1,2,3,4\}\times\mathcal Z,
\]
add \(4k\) equality atoms.  For each ordered pair \((z,z')\) with \(z\ne z'\), put
\[
h_{zz'}=q^X_zg(z')-q^X_{z'}g(z).
\]
Instrument relevance is represented by the disjunction of \(h_{zz'}>0\) and \(-h_{zz'}>0\).  Retaining both signs for every ordered pair gives exactly \(2k(k-1)\) sign atoms; this intentionally redundant presentation is equivalent to the existence of one pair with \(h_{zz'}\ne0\).  Adding these atoms to (2) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_B
&=S_A+5+(k+1)+5+4k+2k(k-1)\\
&=4k\ell+2k^2+4\ell+15k+15.                              \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
The shared-\(Z\) equations have degree two.  By the definition of \(q^X\), the polynomial \(q^X_z\) is bilinear in \((m,a)\), so \(h_{zz'}\) has degree three after multiplication by a coordinate of \(g\).  All remaining new atoms are linear.  Hence
\[
D_B=3.                                                         \tag{6}
\]
The target \(\tau_C\) is linear in \(a\).  Once a sampled target \(t_0\) is fixed as a coefficient, the unequal-target refinement is the single strict sign atom
\[
(\tau_{C,1}-t_{0,1})^2+(\tau_{C,2}-t_{0,2})^2>0.
\]
It has degree two in the raw-fiber variables, so it adds one atom and does not increase either value of \(D_d\).

We next prove termination and the algebraic-degree bounds.  By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}selected\text{-}table\text{-}positivity}\),
\[
r_e=\sum_{w,z}p_{ewz}>0
\]
for every cell \(e\).  Thus all divisions used to form \(T_e=p_e/r_e\) and \(v_e=\sum_wT_e(w,\cdot)\) are by nonzero elements.  Since deleted simplex coordinates are recovered by subtraction from one, every coefficient of the raw presentations belongs to \(K=\mathbb K_{\zeta}\).

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}effective\text{-}semialgebraic\text{-}sampling\text{-}existential}\) once to the raw design-\(d\) fiber.  The call terminates, generates at most \(A_{S,d}\) algebraic records, and either certifies emptiness or returns a real-univariate representation of a point \(x_0\).  In design A the preliminary test \(m\in\Delta_{\mathcal E}^{\circ}\) consists of finitely many field sign and equality tests and therefore terminates before this call.  If \(\xi\) is the primitive element in the representation of \(x_0\), the sampling degree bound gives
\[
[K(\xi):K]\le D_{S,d}.                                      \tag{7}
\]
Every coordinate of \(x_0\) belongs to \(K(\xi)\), and hence has algebraic degree at most \(D_{S,d}\) over \(K\).  Both coordinates of \(t_0=\tau_C(x_0)\) are sums of coordinates of \(x_0\); consequently
\[
K(t_0)\subseteq K(\xi),
\qquad [K(t_0):K]\le D_{S,d}.                              \tag{8}
\]

When the raw fiber is nonempty, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}atlas\text{-}handle}\) prescribes exactly one existential-realizability call over \(K(t_0)\) for the raw fiber intersected with the preceding unequal-target sign condition.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}effective\text{-}semialgebraic\text{-}sampling\text{-}existential}\) makes this call terminating and bounds its records by \(A_{E,d}\).  If it returns a witness with primitive element \(\upsilon\), then
\[
[K(t_0,\upsilon):K(t_0)]\le D_{E,d}.                       \tag{9}
\]
To verify the required tower bound directly, let \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) be a \(K\)-basis of \(K(t_0)\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_s\) be a \(K(t_0)\)-basis of \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\).  The \(rs\) products \(u_iv_j\) span \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\) over \(K\).  Equations (8)--(9) therefore imply
\[
[K(t_0,\upsilon):K]
\le [K(t_0):K]\,[K(t_0,\upsilon):K(t_0)]
\le D_{S,d}D_{E,d}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Every second-stage witness coordinate belongs to \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\), so (10) bounds its degree over \(K\).  The initial coordinates are instead bounded by (7); they need not belong to \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\).  It follows that
\[
\mathcal D_d=\max\{D_{S,d},D_{S,d}D_{E,d}\}              \tag{11}
\]
bounds every returned coordinate without asserting any containment of the initial sample field in the second-stage field.  The tower-normalization, square-free-factorization, Thom-sign, and root-isolation procedures supplied by the effective-semialgebraic lemma do not increase the relevant field degree.  Allowing two fixed decision/status records in addition to the records produced by the two calls gives
\[
R_d=2+A_{S,d}+A_{E,d}.                                    \tag{12}
\]
If the raw fiber is empty, the second call is omitted, so (12) remains an upper bound.  Both calls are made on the one raw fiber; hence there is no chart multiplier.

The optional diagnostic enumeration is a separate finite computation.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}support\text{-}elimination\text{-}complete}\), whose rank-one cases use Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}rank\text{-}one\text{-}lred\text{-}normal\text{-}forms}\), gives
\[
M_A=(2^{\ell}-1)^4(3^{k+1}+3)^4,
\qquad
M_B=M_Ak(k-1)(2k-1)^4.                                   \tag{13}
\]
These finite lists terminate when requested, but (13) enters neither the raw variable and atom counts (1)--(6) nor the record and degree bounds (11)--(12).  Their auxiliary variables therefore do not enter \(N_d,S_d,D_d\).

Put \(Q=k\ell+k^2+\ell+1\).  Because \(k\ge2\) and \(\ell\ge3\), equations (1)--(6) imply, after choosing a universal numerical constant \(C_0\),
\[
N_d\le C_0Q,
\qquad S_d\le C_0Q,
\qquad D_d\le3.                                           \tag{14}
\]
The bounds in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}effective\text{-}semialgebraic\text{-}sampling\text{-}existential}\) are coordinatewise nondecreasing.  Its displayed singly exponential envelope, (14), and the fact that the refinement changes \(S_d\) only to \(S_d+1\) show that, for a universal \(C\ge1\),
\[
A_{S,d},D_{S,d},A_{E,d},D_{E,d}
\le 2^{CQ^C}.                                               \tag{15}
\]
Substitution of (15) into (11)--(12), followed by enlarging \(C\) once, proves
\[
R_{k,\ell}\le2^{CQ^C},
\qquad
\mathcal D_{k,\ell}\le2^{CQ^C}.                          \tag{16}
\]
The field operations, order and sign tests, sampling, existential decision, and real-root procedures invoked above are precisely the effective operations listed in the same lemma.  Hence the computation terminates over an arbitrary real input field relative to those oracles.

It remains to establish the rational-height assertions.  Suppose every rational input coordinate has numerator and denominator of binary length at most \(L\).  For rationals written as quotients of integers, direct cross multiplication gives
\[
\operatorname{ht}(u\pm v)\le
\operatorname{ht}(u)+\operatorname{ht}(v)+2,
\qquad
\operatorname{ht}(uv),\operatorname{ht}(u/v)
\le\operatorname{ht}(u)+\operatorname{ht}(v)+1,           \tag{17}
\]
where the last expression is used only when \(v\ne0\).  There are at most \(4k\ell+4\le8Q\) supplied rational coordinates across the two observable simplexes.  Let \(B\) be the product of all their positive denominators.  Then \(\operatorname{ht}(B)\le8QL\), and putting every supplied coordinate over \(B\) gives integer numerators of height at most \(8Q(L+1)\).  Recovering a deleted coordinate and summing at most \(4k\ell\) such numerators increases this bound only by \(1+\lceil\log_2(4k\ell+1)\rceil\), so every recovered probability and every \(r_e\) has a common-denominator representation of height at most \(16Q(L+1)\).  In the ratio \(T_e(w,z)=p_{ewz}/r_e\), the denominator \(B\) cancels.  For fixed \(e\), the coordinates summed to form \(v_e\) then have the same denominator, namely the integer numerator representing \(r_e\).  It follows again by integer addition that every coordinate of \(T_e\) and \(v_e\) has height at most \(32Q(L+1)\).  The raw equations have degree at most three and otherwise have coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\); clearing the displayed observable denominators in each atom therefore yields integer coefficients of height at most the conservative bound
\[
h_0=64Q^2(L+1)                                             \tag{18}
\]
dominates the binary length of every cleared integer coefficient.  No zero denominator is cleared, by selected-table positivity proved above.

Let
\[
h_{S,d}=\mathsf{Samp}_h(N_d,S_d,D_d,h_0).                  \tag{19}
\]
The real-univariate representation returned at the first stage may be chosen with a common coordinate denominator.  Each coordinate of \(t_0\) is a sum of at most \(k\) represented coordinates.  Summing their integer numerators increases the coefficient length by at most
\[
c_k=1+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil.
\]
Thus the coefficient-field description of \(t_0\) has height at most \(h_{S,d}+c_k\).  Applying the relative height envelope of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}effective\text{-}semialgebraic\text{-}sampling\text{-}existential}\) to the second call therefore gives exactly
\[
h_{E,d}=\mathsf{Exist}_h
 (N_d,S_d+1,D_d;h_0,h_{S,d}+c_k,D_{S,d}).                 \tag{20}
\]

The initial representation and the second-stage tower must be flattened separately.  The normalization part of the same lemma, with its height-bound function denoted by \(\mathsf{Flat}_h\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{F,0,d}
&=\mathsf{Flat}_h(D_{S,d},h_{S,d}+c_k+1),\\
h_{F,1,d}
&=\mathsf{Flat}_h
  (D_{S,d}D_{E,d},h_{S,d}+c_k+h_{E,d}+1).                 \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]
The first line covers \(x_0\) and the coordinate sums defining \(t_0\); the second covers the tower \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\), and in particular retains the required \(c_k\) increment.  Square-free factorization and rational root isolation then yield, for every initial-sample, target, and second-stage coordinate, a square-free integer polynomial and rational isolating data.  Equations (7), (10), and (11) bound its degree by \(\mathcal D_{k,\ell}\), while
\[
H_{k,\ell}(L)
=\max_{d\in\{A,B\}}
  \{h_{S,d},h_{E,d},h_{F,0,d},h_{F,1,d}\}                \tag{22}
\]
bounds its coefficient and isolating-data lengths.

Finally, the height envelope in the effective-semialgebraic lemma has the form of a power of its input height whose exponent is singly exponential in the variable, atom, and degree parameters.  There are only the sampling, existential, and two flattening compositions in (19)--(21).  Combining their coordinatewise monotonicity with (14)--(15), and absorbing the finite products of singly exponential exponents into a larger universal \(C\), yields
\[
H_{k,\ell}(L)
\le \bigl[64Q^2(L+2)\bigr]^{2^{CQ^C}}.                   \tag{23}
\]
This is the asserted separate rational-height envelope.  All integer polynomial arithmetic, square-free factorization, Thom-sign determination, and rational isolation operations terminate, so the rational-input procedure is a terminating Turing computation.  By the mandatory decision path in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}atlas\text{-}handle}\), it uses neither connected-component description nor target projection, diagnostic-chart iteration, or a generic two-copy classifier.  Equations (1)--(23) prove every claimed count, degree, height, and termination bound.

Let \(k=|\mathcal Z|\), \(\ell=|\mathcal W|\), and \(d\in\{A,B\}\).  The raw design-A fiber has a polynomial presentation with
\[
 N_A=4\ell+8k,\qquad
 S_A=4k\ell+4\ell+12k+4,\qquad
 D_A=2,
\]
and the raw design-B fiber has one with
\[
 N_B=4\ell+9k+8,\qquad
 S_B=4k\ell+2k^2+4\ell+15k+15,\qquad
 D_B=3.
\]
The unequal-target refinement adds one sign atom and does not increase \(D_d\).  Define
\[
 (A_{S,d},D_{S,d})=\mathsf{Samp}(N_d,S_d,D_d),
 \qquad
 (A_{E,d},D_{E,d})=\mathsf{Exist}(N_d,S_d+1,D_d),
\]
where the published bounds are coordinatewise nondecreasing, and put
\[
 R_d=2+A_{S,d}+A_{E,d},
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_d=\max\{D_{S,d},D_{S,d}D_{E,d}\},
 \qquad
 R_{k,\ell}=\max_{d\in\{A,B\}}R_d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_{k,\ell}=\max_{d\in\{A,B\}}\mathcal D_d.
\]
Then the mandatory compiler terminates after at most one raw-fiber sampling call and, if that fiber is nonempty, one unequal-target existential call.  Its record/output count is at most \(R_{k,\ell}\).  Every coordinate of the initial sample has algebraic degree at most \(D_{S,d}\) over \(\mathbb K_{\zeta}\); every coordinate of a second-stage witness has degree at most \(D_{S,d}D_{E,d}\) over \(\mathbb K_{\zeta}\); and every returned coordinate consequently has degree at most \(\mathcal D_{k,\ell}\).  No assertion is made that the initial sample lies in the field generated by the second-stage witness and its target.

The separate diagnostic structural enumerations have at most

 M_A=(2^{\ell}-1)^4(3^{k+1}+3)^4,

 M_B=M_Ak(k-1)(2k-1)^4

templates.  These counts do not multiply \(R_{k,\ell}\), and their auxiliary chart variables do not enter \(N_d,S_d,D_d\).

If \(Q=k\ell+k^2+\ell+1\), there is a universal constant \(C\ge1\), independent of \(k,\ell\) and the input, such that
\[
 R_{k,\ell}\le 2^{CQ^C},
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_{k,\ell}\le 2^{CQ^C}.
\]
Over an arbitrary real input field \(\mathbb K_{\zeta}\), the computation terminates relative to effective field arithmetic, order, sign-determination, and real-root oracles.

For rational input coordinates whose numerators and denominators have binary length at most \(L\), let
\[
 h_0=64Q^2(L+1),
 \qquad
 c_k=1+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil,
 \qquad
 h_{S,d}=\mathsf{Samp}_h(N_d,S_d,D_d,h_0),

and define

 \begin{aligned}

After flattening separately when necessary and clearing only guarded observable denominators, every initial-sample, target, or second-stage witness coordinate can be represented by a square-free integer polynomial of degree at most \(\mathcal D_{k,\ell}\) and rational isolating data of binary length at most \(H_{k,\ell}(L)\). Thus record/output count and algebraic degree have singly exponential fixed-alphabet bounds, whereas rational height obeys the separate displayed bound.
