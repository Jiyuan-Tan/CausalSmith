Put \(\pi_i=P_{\vartheta}(U=i)\) for \(i\in\{1,2,3,4\}\).  We first verify that every conditional probability appearing in the claim is defined.  Membership \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\), the definition \(p_{ewz}=P_{\vartheta}(E=e,W=w,Z=z\mid S=1)\), and the local condition \(P_{\vartheta}(S=1)=1\) give
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(E=e)
 =P_{\vartheta}(E=e\mid S=1)
 =\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}p_{ewz}
 =r_e.
\]
By selected-table positivity and the nonemptiness of the finite alphabets, \(r_e>0\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\).  In particular,
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(X=1)\ge P_{\vartheta}(E=11)=r_{11}>0,
 \qquad
 P_{\vartheta}(X=0)\ge P_{\vartheta}(E=00)=r_{00}>0,
\]
and hence
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)
 =\frac{r_{11}}{r_{11}+r_{10}}>0,
 \qquad
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)
 =\frac{r_{00}}{r_{00}+r_{01}}>0.
\]

By response consistency and the stated encoding of \(U\), under \(X=1\) the event \(Y=1\) occurs exactly when \(U\in\{1,2\}\), while under \(X=0\) the event \(Y=1\) occurs exactly when \(U\in\{1,3\}\), and the event \(Y=0\) occurs exactly when \(U\in\{2,4\}\).  The local independence \(X\perp U\), together with the just-established positivity of both treatment arms, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U\in\{1,2\}\mid X=1)=\pi_1+\pi_2,\\
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U\in\{1,3\}\mid X=0)=\pi_1+\pi_3,\\
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U\in\{2,4\}\mid X=0)=\pi_2+\pi_4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The local restriction \(P_{\vartheta}(U=3)=0\) says \(\pi_3=0\), so
\[
 P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)=\pi_2.
\]
For type \(2\), response consistency gives \(Y=1\) when \(X=1\) and \(Y=0\) when \(X=0\).  Thus independence of \(X\) and \(U\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{PN}(\vartheta)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=1,Y=1)\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2,Y=1\mid X=1)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)}
 =\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=1)}{\pi_1+\pi_2}
 =\frac{\pi_2}{\pi_1+\pi_2}\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)},
\end{aligned}
\]
and, similarly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{PS}(\vartheta)
 &=P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=0,Y=0)\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2,Y=0\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)}
 =\frac{P_{\vartheta}(U=2\mid X=0)}{\pi_2+\pi_4}
 =\frac{\pi_2}{\pi_2+\pi_4}\\
 &=\frac{P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=1)-P_{\vartheta}(Y=1\mid X=0)}{P_{\vartheta}(Y=0\mid X=0)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the displayed pair for the particular restricted SCM \(\vartheta\).

By the definition of the design-A target set, membership \(\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\) implies
\[
 \tau(\vartheta)\in
 \{\tau(\widetilde\vartheta):\widetilde\vartheta\in\mathcal F_A(p,m)\}
 =\mathcal T_A(p,m).
\]
Consequently, if \(\lvert\mathcal T_A(p,m)\rvert=1\), its unique element is \(\tau(\vartheta)\), which is the displayed pair.

For completeness, the local restrictions on this one member do not themselves force rigidity of its unrestricted fiber.  Here is an explicit witness.  Write \(M=\lvert\mathcal W\rvert\) and \(K=\lvert\mathcal Z\rvert\).  In both SCMs below take \(b_i(w)=1/M\), \(g_i(z)=1/K\), and \(s_e=1\) for every admissible \(i,w,z,e\).  For the first SCM take
\[
 (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3,\pi_4)=\left(\frac13,\frac13,0,\frac13\right),
 \qquad e_i(z)=\frac12
\]
for every \(i,z\).  It satisfies \(S=1\) almost surely, \(X\perp U\), and \(P(U=3)=0\).  Directly applying the four response maps gives
\[
 (m_{11},m_{01},m_{10},m_{00})
 =\left(\frac13,\frac16,\frac16,\frac13\right),
 \qquad
 p_{ewz}=\frac{m_e}{MK}>0,
\]
and its target is \(\tau=(1/2,1/2)\).  For the second SCM take the same proxy and selection mechanisms but set
\[
 (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3,\pi_4)
 =\left(\frac13,\frac14,\frac16,\frac14\right)
\]
and, independently of \(z\), set
\[
 (e_1(z),e_2(z),e_3(z),e_4(z))
 =\left(\frac34,\frac13,\frac12,\frac13\right).
\]
The contributions of types \((1,2)\) to cell \(11\) are \((1/4,1/12)\); those of types \((1,3)\) to cell \(01\) are \((1/12,1/12)\); those of types \((3,4)\) to cell \(10\) are \((1/12,1/12)\); and those of types \((2,4)\) to cell \(00\) are \((1/6,1/6)\).  Hence this second SCM has exactly the same \(m\) and the same positive selected table \(p_{ewz}=m_e/(MK)\), while
\[
 \tau=\left(\frac{1/12}{1/3},\frac{1/6}{1/3}\right)
 =\left(\frac14,\frac12\right).
\]
All displayed weights and assignment probabilities are nonnegative and normalized; the uniform conditional proxy mechanisms satisfy proxy exclusion, and constant selection satisfies outcome-dependent selection.  Thus both parameters are legal members of the same design-A fiber, but their targets differ.  This proves that imposing the three local restrictions on the singled-out \(\vartheta\) supplies a validation identity, not rigidity of the unrestricted fiber.  The restrictions are used only after membership in the main design has been assumed and therefore are local hypotheses of this corollary, not additional defining assumptions of either main design.
