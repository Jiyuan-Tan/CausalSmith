therefore bounds every returned coordinate without making a false field-containment assertion.  The tower-flattening, square-free-factorization, Thom-sign, and isolation procedures in the cited lemma do not increase field degree.  The two fixed status/output records and the two algorithmic record bounds give
\[
 R_d=2+A_{S,d}+A_{E,d}.                                   \tag{37}
\]
There is no chart multiplier because both calls use the single raw fiber.

Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}support\text{-}elimination\text{-}complete}\) separately gives the optional diagnostic counts
\[
 M_A=(2^{\ell}-1)^4(3^{k+1}+3)^4,
 \qquad
 M_B=M_Ak(k-1)(2k-1)^4.                                  \tag{38}
\]
The list (38) terminates when requested, but it occurs in neither (27)--(31) nor (36)--(37).

Put \(Q=k\ell+k^2+\ell+1\).  Equations (27)--(31) yield, after increasing a universal constant \(C\),

 N_d\le CQ,\qquad S_d\le CQ,\qquad D_d\le3.             \tag{39}
\]
The coordinatewise nondecreasing bounds in the effective-semialgebraic lemma then give
\[
 A_{S,d},D_{S,d},A_{E,d},D_{E,d}
 \le 2^{CQ^C}.                                             \tag{40}
\]
Substitution in (36)--(37), and enlargement of \(C\) once more, proves
\[
 R_{k,\ell}\le2^{CQ^C},
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_{k,\ell}\le2^{CQ^C}.                        \tag{41}

Selected-table positivity gives \(r_e>0\), so the only observable divisions used to form \(T_e\) and \(v_e\) are guarded.  All raw coefficients consequently lie in \(\mathbb K_{\zeta}\).  Effective field arithmetic, order, sign determination, sampling, and real-root procedures from the cited lemma terminate, proving relative termination over an arbitrary real input field.

Now suppose the input coordinates are rational with numerator and denominator binary length at most \(L\).  Reconstructing the deleted simplex coordinates, forming \(r_e\), dividing to obtain \(T_e,v_e\), and clearing the nonzero observable denominators in degree-at-most-three polynomials requires only a quadratic number of rational additions and multiplications in \(Q\).  Therefore
\[
 h_0=64Q^2(L+1)                                            \tag{42}
\]
conservatively bounds every cleared integer coefficient.  The sampling height bound is
\[
 h_{S,d}=\mathsf{Samp}_h(N_d,S_d,D_d,h_0).                 \tag{43}
\]
Each coordinate of \(t_0\) is a sum of at most \(k\) coordinates represented in the initial real-univariate representation.  Adding at most \(k\) integer numerators increases binary length by at most
\(c_k=1+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil\); consequently \(t_0\) has representation height at most \(h_{S,d}+c_k\).  The correct relative second-stage bound is therefore
\[
 h_{E,d}=\mathsf{Exist}_h
 (N_d,S_d+1,D_d;h_0,h_{S,d}+c_k,D_{S,d}).                  \tag{44}
\]

The initial representation and the second-stage tower must also be normalized separately.  Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_{F,0,d}
 &=\mathsf{Flat}_h(D_{S,d},h_{S,d}+c_k+1),\\
 h_{F,1,d}
 &=\mathsf{Flat}_h
   (D_{S,d}D_{E,d},h_{S,d}+c_k+h_{E,d}+1).
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{45}
\]
The first line covers \(x_0\) and its coordinate sums; the second covers the field \(K(t_0,\upsilon)\) and explicitly retains the increment \(c_k\).  Thus
\[
 H_{k,\ell}(L)
 =\max_{d\in\{A,B\}}
   \{h_{S,d},h_{E,d},h_{F,0,d},h_{F,1,d}\}               \tag{46}
\]
bounds the coefficient and rational-isolation lengths of every output.  The height envelopes in the cited lemma, (39), and (40), after a final enlargement of \(C\), imply
\[
 H_{k,\ell}(L)
 \le \bigl[64Q^2(L+2)\bigr]^{2^{CQ^C}}.                  \tag{47}
\]
This is a separate rational-height guarantee, not a claim that height is singly exponential in the same sense as the record and degree bounds (41).  The rational square-free and isolation subroutines are finite, so a rational-input Turing implementation terminates.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}full\text{-}rigidity\text{-}atlas}\) proves that the two raw-fiber calls decide the causal target; connected components, target projection, chart iteration, and a generic two-copy classifier are unnecessary.

%%% FIELD proof-separated-wald
Let \(H\) be a compact subset of \(\mathfrak R_{\varepsilon}\); all uniform statements below are over \(\zeta\in H\).  Under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}multinomial\text{-}sampling}\), the reduced selected-table coordinates are averages of bounded categorical vectors.  In design A with estimated margins, the margin coordinates come from an independent sample, and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}margin\text{-}sample\text{-}scaling}\) gives \(n/n_m\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\).  Consequently some finite \(C_H\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in H}
 E_{\zeta}\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2^2
 \le \frac{C_H}{n}.                                      \tag{48}
\]
The margin term is absent when margins are known.  Markov's inequality implies
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in H}P_{\zeta}
 \{\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2>\delta\}
 \le\frac{C_H}{n\delta^2},
 \qquad
 \widehat\zeta_n-\zeta=O_P(n^{-1/2})                    \tag{49}
\]
uniformly on \(H\).

The selected-table covariance is
\(\operatorname{diag}(p^-)-p^-p^{-\top}\).  Independence and the relative sample-size limit add the block
\(\lambda\{\operatorname{diag}(m^-)-m^-m^{-\top}\}\) when margins are estimated.  These are exactly the blocks of \(\Omega_{\zeta}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}multinomial\text{-}clt\text{-}and\text{-}delta}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta)
 \Rightarrow N(0,\Omega_{\zeta}).                         \tag{50}
\]
This convergence is uniform on \(H\): from any sequence in \(H\), compactness extracts a convergent subsequence; bounded categorical summands satisfy the uniform characteristic-function expansion
\[
 E_{\zeta_n}\exp\{iu^{\top}\xi_{n,1}/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{u^{\top}\Omega_{\zeta_n}u}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),    \tag{51}
\]
and multiplying the selected and independently rescaled margin characteristic functions proves (50) along the subsequence.  The subsequence criterion yields uniformity.  Since the covariance formula is continuous in the cell probabilities, (49) also gives
\[
 \widehat\Omega_n\xrightarrow{P}\Omega_{\zeta}          \tag{52}
\]
uniformly on \(H\).

For each \(\zeta\in H\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}separated\text{-}rigid\text{-}stratum}\) makes
\[
 \Gamma(\zeta)=\{\chi:d_{\chi}(\zeta)=0,
                    \ \eta_{\chi}(\zeta)\ge\varepsilon\}
\]
nonempty.  Distance to a closed set is one-Lipschitz, so for \(\chi\in\Gamma(\zeta)\),
\[
 d_{\chi}(\widehat\zeta_n)
 \le\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2.                       \tag{53}
\]
There are finitely many labels, and the normalized slack functions have the continuous extensions required by the same definition.  Compactness supplies \(\delta_H>0\) such that
\[
 \|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2<\delta_H
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \eta_{\chi}(\widehat\zeta_n)\ge\varepsilon/2
 \quad(\chi\in\Gamma(\zeta)).                           \tag{54}
\]
Because \(t_n=n^{-1/4}\), (49), (53), and (54) show uniformly that at least one incident chart passes the selector inequalities with probability tending to one.

Conversely, every incompatible chart closure is at distance at least \(\varepsilon\) from \(\zeta\).  Hence
\[
 d_{\chi}(\widehat\zeta_n)
 \ge\varepsilon-\|\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta\|_2.           \tag{55}
\]
On the event where the last norm is less than \(\varepsilon/2\), (55) exceeds \(t_n\) for all large \(n\).  The exhaustive atlas certification and lexicographic rule therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{\zeta\in H}P_{\zeta}
 \{\widehat\chi_n=\bot\ \text{or }\widehat\chi_n
   \text{ is not incident and first-order compatible}\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                       \tag{56}
\]

On the complementary event, rigidity and first-order compatibility give
\[
 F_{\widehat\chi_n}(\zeta)=\tau,
 \qquad
 J_{\widehat\chi_n}(\zeta)=J(\zeta),                     \tag{57}
\]
where \(J(\zeta)\) is the common incident Jacobian.  Twice continuous differentiability, the finite chart set, and compactness give a common Hessian bound.  Taylor's theorem and (49) yield
\[
 \widehat\tau_n-\tau
 =J(\zeta)(\widehat\zeta_n-\zeta)+R_n,
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\|R_n\|_2=o_P(1)                                \tag{58}
\]
uniformly.  Equations (50), (57), (58), and the delta method give
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)
 \Rightarrow
 N\!\left(0,J(\zeta)\Omega_{\zeta}J(\zeta)^{\top}\right)
 =N(0,V_{\tau}).                                          \tag{59}
\]

Continuity of the derivatives, (49), and (52) show that
\[
 \widetilde V_n=
 J_{\widehat\chi_n}(\widehat\zeta_n)\widehat\Omega_n
 J_{\widehat\chi_n}(\widehat\zeta_n)^{\top}
 \xrightarrow{P}V_{\tau}                                 \tag{60}
\]
uniformly.  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}separated\text{-}rigid\text{-}stratum}\) gives \(\lambda_{\min}(V_{\tau})\ge\varepsilon\).  Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and (60) imply
\[
 P_{\zeta}\{\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde V_n)>\varepsilon/2\}
 \longrightarrow1                                        \tag{61}
\]
uniformly.  Hence \(\widehat\gamma_n=1\) with probability tending to one and, on that event, \(\widehat V_n=\widetilde V_n\); therefore \(\widehat V_n\xrightarrow{P}V_{\tau}\).

For every fixed nonzero contrast vector \(h\), Slutsky's lemma and the eigenvalue bound give
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\,h^{\top}(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)}
      {(h^{\top}\widehat V_nh)^{1/2}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1),                                      \tag{62}
\]
so the corresponding two-sided scalar Wald interval has coverage tending to \(1-\alpha\).  Likewise,
\[
 n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau)^{\top}\widehat V_n^{-1}
   (\widehat\tau_n-\tau)
 \Rightarrow\chi^2_2.                                    \tag{63}
\]
Because the true target lies in \([0,1]^2\), intersecting the ellipse with that square does not alter its coverage event.  Continuity of the chi-square law at \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\) proves asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\) for the elliptical branch.

If no chart is certified, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}wald\text{-}estimator}\) gives \(\widehat\chi_n=\bot\).  The total-map clauses give \(F_{\bot}=(0,0)\), \(J_{\bot}=0\), and a zero raw sandwich matrix, which is not positive definite.  Hence \(\widehat\gamma_n=0\) and
\[
 \widehat\tau_n=(0,0),\qquad
 \widehat V_n=I_2,\qquad
 W_n=[0,1]^2.                                              \tag{64}
\]
The consistency and Wald conclusions used the high-probability event (56), so no such assertion is made for this fallback branch.

Let \(q_0\) be the true observable law, including the independent margin law in design A when margins are estimated, and set \(t_0=\tau(\vartheta)\).  Each selected-table count and each applicable margin count has a binomial marginal distribution.  Exact two-sided Clopper--Pearson inversion gives the allocated marginal coverage, and the stated Bonferroni allocation therefore gives
\[
 P_{q_0}\{q_0\notin C_n\}\le\beta_n.                    \tag{65}
\]

For a fixed compatible pair \((q,t)\), define the exact nonrandomized upper-tail value
\[
 G_{q,t}=P_q\{\Delta_n^{\mathrm{rep}}(t)
                 \ge\Delta_n^{\mathrm{obs}}(t)\}.
\]
It is super-uniform.  Indeed, order the finite support of \(\Delta_n(t)\).  For \(u\in[0,1]\), let \(d\) be the least support point whose upper-tail probability is at most \(u\), if such a point exists.  Then
\(\{G_{q,t}\le u\}\subseteq\{\Delta_n^{\mathrm{obs}}(t)\ge d\}\), whence
\[
 P_q\{G_{q,t}\le u\}\le u.                              \tag{66}
\]
If no such \(d\) exists, the event on the left is empty and (66) remains true.

Compatibility gives \(t_0\in\mathcal T(q_0)\).  On \(\{q_0\in C_n\}\), the supremum in \(\operatorname{pv}_n(t_0)\) includes \(q_0\), so
\[
 \operatorname{pv}_n(t_0)
 \ge\min\{1,\beta_n+G_{q_0,t_0}\}.                       \tag{67}
\]
Since \(\beta_n\le\alpha/2<\alpha\), (65)--(67) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{q_0}\{t_0\notin K_n\}
 &\le P_{q_0}\{q_0\notin C_n\}
   +P_{q_0}\{G_{q_0,t_0}\le\alpha-\beta_n\}\\
 &\le\beta_n+(\alpha-\beta_n)=\alpha.
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{68}
\]
This is a finite-sample, pointwise argument.  Applying (68) to every member of any triangular sequence proves the same coverage along arbitrary approaches to \(\mathfrak D\), including point-to-set transitions, without any rank, support, or boundary regularity assumption.

For the Hausdorff assertion, fix a compact subset satisfying the stated local-saturation hypotheses.  Positivity and compactness bound every relevant multinomial probability away from zero.  Since eventually \(\beta_n=n^{-2}\), simultaneous Clopper--Pearson inversion and a union bound give
\[
 \sup_{q\in C_n}\|q-q_0\|_2
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)=o_P(1)      \tag{69}
\]
uniformly.  To see the asserted order, for \(x>p\), exponential Markov inequality at
\(\theta=\log\{x(1-p)/(p(1-x))\}\) gives
\[
 P_p(\widehat p\ge x)
 \le e^{-nD(x\Vert p)}
 \le e^{-2n(x-p)^2};                                      \tag{70}
\]
the last inequality follows because the second derivative of binary divergence in \(x\) is \(1/\{x(1-x)\}\ge4\), while its value and first derivative vanish at \(x=p\).  The lower tail is symmetric, and inversion at tail levels of order \(n^{-2}\) proves (69).  With probability tending uniformly to one, all laws entering the Berger--Boos supremum lie in the common saturated neighborhood, where compatibility holds and \(t\in\mathcal T(q)\) is equivalent to \(t=F(q)\).

Write \(z\) for reduced observable coordinates and \(\widehat z\) for their unrestricted multinomial maximum-likelihood estimate.  On the positive compact neighborhood, a third-order Taylor expansion of the multinomial log likelihood gives, uniformly for \(z-\widehat z=O(n^{-1/2})\),
\[
 2\{\ell_n(\widehat z)-\ell_n(z)\}
 =n(z-\widehat z)^{\top}\Omega_{\zeta}^{-1}
       (z-\widehat z)+o_P(1).                             \tag{71}
\]
For two samples, independence and \(n/n_m\to\lambda\) give the two blocks in \(\Omega_{\zeta}\).  Its inverse exists in reduced coordinates because the local cell probabilities are positive.

Let \(J=\partial F(\zeta)/\partial\zeta^{\top}\).  Twice continuous differentiability gives
\[
 F(z)-F(\widehat z)
 =J(z-\widehat z)+O(\|z-\widehat z\|_2^2).                \tag{72}
\]
Strict concavity of the interior multinomial likelihood and compactness give a positive Kullback--Leibler gap outside the fixed saturated neighborhood.  Therefore every constrained maximizer for a bounded root-\(n\) target displacement is local with probability tending uniformly to one.

Minimizing the leading quadratic form in (71) subject to the linearized constraint in (72), the Lagrange first-order condition gives
\[
 z-\widehat z
 =\Omega_{\zeta}J^{\top}
   (J\Omega_{\zeta}J^{\top})^{-1}(t-\widehat\tau_n).
\]
Substitution and \(V_{\tau}=J\Omega_{\zeta}J^{\top}\) yield
\[
 \Delta_n(t)
 =n(t-\widehat\tau_n)^{\top}V_{\tau}^{-1}
       (t-\widehat\tau_n)+o_P(1)                          \tag{73}
\]
uniformly for bounded \(\sqrt n\|t-\widehat\tau_n\|_2\).  The multiplier is unique because \(\lambda_{\min}(V_{\tau})\ge\varepsilon\).  The same likelihood expansion shows uniform divergence outside a bounded local target set.

Apply (71)--(73) to independent replicates, uniformly over the shrinking set (69), and use Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}multinomial\text{-}clt\text{-}and\text{-}delta}\).  Covariance consistency is supplied by Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}separated\text{-}wald}\).  It follows that
\[
 \sup_{q\in C_n:\,t=F(q)}
 \left|
 P_q\{\Delta_n^{\mathrm{rep}}(t)\ge\Delta_n^{\mathrm{obs}}(t)\}
 -\overline F_{\chi^2_2}\!\left(
 n(t-\widehat\tau_n)^{\top}\widehat V_n^{-1}
 (t-\widehat\tau_n)\right)
 \right|=o_P(1).                                          \tag{74}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(V_{\tau}\) forces \(J\) to have row rank two.  Since \(F\) maps a neighborhood into \([0,1]^2\), the local open-mapping property then forces \(F(\zeta)\) into the interior of the square.  Thus truncation by \([0,1]^2\) is locally inactive.

The chi-square distribution is continuous and \(\beta_n=o(1)\).  Equation (74) sandwiches \(K_n\), with probability tending uniformly to one, between ellipsoids obtained by changing \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\) by \(\pm\delta_n\) for some \(\delta_n=o_P(1)\).  The eigenvalues of \(\widehat V_n\) are uniformly bounded above and away from zero.  Radial comparison gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm H}(K_n,W_n)
 \le n^{-1/2}O_P(\delta_n)=o_P(n^{-1/2}),                 \tag{75}
\]
which is the claimed compact-uniform Hausdorff limit.  Without singleton local saturation, chart separation, or nonsingularity, (71)--(75) need not hold; the universally valid conclusion is only the finite-sample coverage (68).

Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}selected\text{-}table\text{-}positivity}\) gives \(r_e>0\), \(T_e(w,z)>0\), and
\[
 v_e(z)=\sum_wT_e(w,z)>0                                  \tag{76}
\]
for every \(e,z\).  Fix \(e\) with \(\mathcal I_e=\{i,j\}\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}cycle\text{-}fibers}\) gives
\[
 T_e=b_i a_{ei}^{\top}+b_j a_{ej}^{\top},
 \qquad
 a_{ei}+a_{ej}=v_e.                                       \tag{77}
\]
The first expression in (77) is a sum of two rank-one matrices, so \(\operatorname{rank}(T_e)\le2\); (76) rules out rank zero.  An observed rank exceeding two therefore makes both cycle fibers empty.

Suppose \(\operatorname{rank}(T_e)=2\) and define
\[
 c_{ez}=\frac{T_e(\cdot,z)}{v_e(z)}.
\]
Division is legal by (76).  Equation (77) gives
\[
 c_{ez}=\lambda_{ez}b_i+(1-\lambda_{ez})b_j,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{ez}=\frac{a_{ei}(z)}{v_e(z)}\in[0,1].           \tag{78}
\]
Rank two forces \(b_i\ne b_j\).  Conversely, if two endpoints on the observed affine line contain every \(c_{ez}\) in their closed segment, choose a coordinate \(h_e\) with \(b_i(h_e)\ne b_j(h_e)\).  The unique coefficients are
\[
 a_{ei}(z)=v_e(z)
 \frac{c_{ez}(h_e)-b_j(h_e)}{b_i(h_e)-b_j(h_e)},
 \qquad
 a_{ej}(z)=v_e(z)-a_{ei}(z).                              \tag{79}
\]
The denominator is nonzero by construction, and the segment inequalities make both terms nonnegative.  Reversing the endpoints gives the second orientation.  For each \(z\), the positive sum in (77) has exactly the three support alternatives \(a_{ei}(z)=0\), \(a_{ej}(z)=0\), or both positive.  Thus the two orientations and their coordinatewise support refinements exhaust the rank-two cell.

If \(\operatorname{rank}(T_e)=1\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}rank\text{-}one\text{-}lred\text{-}normal\text{-}forms}\) supplies the unique positive column point \(c_e\) and proves that the modes \(\mathrm L,\mathrm R,\mathrm E,\mathrm D\) are disjoint and exhaustive.  Its coordinatewise refinement of \(\mathrm E\) retains \(s_e(z)=0\), \(0<s_e(z)<v_e(z)\), and \(s_e(z)=v_e(z)\).  Hence inactive rows, equal endpoints, and every forced-zero boundary face are retained without division.

Take the product of the four local lists and identify the two occurrences of each shared response endpoint \(b_i\).  Endpoint-simplex and support conditions, endpoint equality or inequality, incidence with an observed affine line, segment order, and (77)--(79) become polynomial sign conditions after a denominator is cleared only on a chart recording its nonzero sign.  The exhaustive alternatives above show that every point of \(\mathcal C_A(T)\) belongs to one resulting chart.  Conversely, substitution of a chart point into (77), together with its simplex and support conditions, proves membership in \(\mathcal C_A(T)\).  Therefore

 \bigcup_{\chi}X^A_{\chi}=\mathcal C_A(T).               \tag{80}

For design B, fix a design-A cycle point and a positive projective coordinate \(\rho\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}iv\text{-}residual\text{-}forest}\) gives, at each response type, the exhaustive alternatives that both incident residuals vanish or that they are nonzero and negatively proportional; exactly one zero residual is infeasible.  In an active state the lexicographically first nonzero residual coordinate is a nonzero pivot, and its sign gives a finite refinement.  The induced positive margin ratios propagate from one positive scale per forest component, and an active cycle is consistent exactly when the product of its ratios is one.  Normalization fixes the remaining common scale.  Finally, relevance is the finite disjunction

 q^X_zg(z')-q^X_{z'}g(z)>0
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 q^X_zg(z')-q^X_{z'}g(z)<0                               \tag{81}

over unordered distinct pairs \(\{z,z'\}\).  The residual-forest theorem makes these conditions necessary and sufficient for positive margins, the response-level shared-\(Z\) equations, and relevance.  Hence
\[
 \bigcup_{\chi}X^B_{\chi}=\mathcal C_B(T).               \tag{82}

It remains to count labeled templates.  Each proxy endpoint has a nonempty support, so the four shared endpoints have at most

 (2^{\ell}-1)^4                                           \tag{83}
\]
support patterns.  A rank-two local library has \(2\cdot3^k\) oriented support modes.  A rank-one library has \(3^k\) refined \(\mathrm E\) modes and the three modes \(\mathrm L,\mathrm R,\mathrm D\).  Listing both possible positive ranks before consulting the observed rank therefore uses at most

 2\cdot3^k+3^k+3=3^{k+1}+3                               \tag{84}

local templates.  Multiplying (83) by the fourth power of (84) gives \(M_A\). The finite list includes every support face by (78)--(82), so it is an exact field-tier structural classification and can be sampled or labeled with real-univariate and Thom data.
