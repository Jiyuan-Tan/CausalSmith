\textbf{Technique: local weak compactness followed by finite-dimensional convex
separation.}
Let \(\mu\) be Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R^d\), and write a Markov
kernel as
\[
 p=(p_A)_{A\in\mathcal A},\qquad
 p_A\geq0,\qquad \sum_{A\in\mathcal A}p_A=1
 \quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.
\tag{1}
\]
Denote the set in (1) by \(\mathscr K\).  This representation is exact because
\(\mathcal A=2^V\) is finite.  Put \(B_j=[-j,j]^d\), and give
\(\mathscr K\) the coordinatewise weak topology of \(L^2(B_j)\), for every
\(A\in\mathcal A\) and \(j\geq1\).

We first prove compactness rather than assume it.  For every \(p\in\mathscr K\),
\[
 \|p_A\|_{L^2(B_j)}\leq\mu(B_j)^{1/2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose a countable orthonormal basis of each \(L^2(B_j)\).  From any sequence
in \(\mathscr K\), diagonal extraction makes all its Fourier coefficients,
over the countable triples consisting of an action, a box, and a basis
element, converge.  The bounds (2) and Bessel's inequality identify the
coefficient limits with weak \(L^2(B_j)\) limits.  Limits on nested boxes
agree, because a test function on \(B_j\), extended by zero, is a test
function on \(B_{j+1}\).  They therefore define measurable functions
\(p_A\) on \(\mathbb R^d\).

The constraints in (1) are weakly closed.  Indeed, for nonnegative
\(q\in L^2(B_j)\), weak convergence gives
\(\int_{B_j}p_Aq\,d\mu\geq0\).  Taking \(q=(p_A)_-\) proves
\(p_A\geq0\); applying the same argument to \(1-p_A\) proves \(p_A\leq1\).
Testing \(\sum_Ap_A-1\) against its positive and negative parts proves
\(\sum_Ap_A=1\).  The bounded Fourier coefficients metrize this
coordinatewise local weak topology.  The diagonal argument thus proves
sequential compactness in a metric space, and hence compactness of
\(\mathscr K\).  Convexity is immediate from (1).

For \(v\in V\), \(h\in D_v\), and \(g\in H_r\), define
\[
 c_{v,h}(p)=
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\phi_\Sigma(w-h)
       \sum_{A\ni v}p_A(w)\,dw,
 \qquad
 r_g(p)=
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\phi_\Sigma(w-g)
       \sum_A\ell(A)p_A(w)\,dw.
\tag{3}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:rational\mbox{-}positive\mbox{-}covariance}\)
implies that every translated Gaussian density is in \(L^2(B_j)\).  Hence
local weak convergence gives convergence of (3) over \(B_j\).  Outside
\(B_j\), the two errors are bounded, respectively, by the translated
Gaussian tail and by \(D\) times that tail, uniformly over \(p\in\mathscr K\).
First sending the kernel index to infinity and then \(j\) to infinity proves
that every functional in (3) is continuous.

By the definition of \(\mathcal C_\eta\), its image in \(\mathscr K\) is
\[
 \mathscr K_\eta=
 \bigcap_{v\in V}\ \bigcap_{h\in D_v}
 \{p\in\mathscr K:c_{v,h}(p)\geq1-\eta\}.
\tag{4}
\]
It is compact by continuity of (3), and it is nonempty because the rule that
always reports \(V\) lies in it.  By
\(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}profile}\), the objective is
\(p\mapsto\sup_{g\in H_r}r_g(p)\).  It is lower semicontinuous as a
supremum of continuous functions.  It therefore attains its minimum on
\(\mathscr K_\eta\).  All \(N_d(h,\Sigma)\) are equivalent to \(\mu\) because
\(\Sigma\succ0\); changing the finitely many coordinates on one common null
set makes the minimizer a pointwise Markov kernel without changing any
coverage or risk.  This proves primal attainment.

We next prove weak duality and exact atomic exhaustion.  Let
\[
 \alpha_v=\sum_{j=1}^{J_v}a_{vj}\delta_{h_{vj}},
 \quad a_{vj}\geq0,\quad h_{vj}\in D_v,
 \qquad
 \beta=\sum_{i=1}^{I}b_i\delta_{g_i},
 \quad b_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i b_i=1,\quad g_i\in H_r,
\tag{5}
\]
and put
\[
 a_v(w)=\int\phi_\Sigma(w-h)\,\alpha_v(dh),
 \qquad
 b(w)=\int\phi_\Sigma(w-g)\,\beta(dg).
\tag{6}
\]
The Lagrangian for the risk epigraph and the coverage inequalities is
\[
 (1-\eta)\sum_v\alpha_v(D_v)
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\sum_{A\subseteq V}p_A(w)
 \left\{b(w)\ell(A)-\sum_{v\in A}a_v(w)\right\}\,dw .
\tag{7}
\]
At each \(w\), (1) is a finite simplex.  Selecting the first minimizing
action in a fixed enumeration is measurable.  Thus the infimum of (7) over
\(\mathscr K\) equals
\[
 \mathcal D(\alpha,\beta)=
 (1-\eta)\sum_v\alpha_v(D_v)
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\min_{A\subseteq V}
 \left\{b(w)\ell(A)-\sum_{v\in A}a_v(w)\right\}\,dw .
\tag{8}
\]
For \(p\in\mathscr K_\eta\), Tonelli's theorem applies to the nonnegative
mixtures in (6), and (3)--(8) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D(\alpha,\beta)
 &\leq
 \sum_i b_i r_{g_i}(p)
 +\sum_{v,j}a_{vj}\{(1-\eta)-c_{v,h_{vj}}(p)\}\\
 &\leq \sup_{g\in H_r}r_g(p).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore every displayed finite atomic dual is a valid lower certificate.

For the converse, fix \(t<M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\).  The open sets
\[
 \{p:r_g(p)>t\}\quad(g\in H_r),\qquad
 \{p:c_{v,h}(p)<1-\eta\}\quad(v\in V,\ h\in D_v)
\tag{10}
\]
cover \(\mathscr K\).  Indeed, an honest kernel has supremum risk at least
\(M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)>t\), whereas a nonhonest kernel violates one
inequality in (4).  Compactness supplies a finite subcover with risk points
\(g_1,\ldots,g_I\) and coverage pairs
\((v_j,h_j)\), \(j=1,\ldots,J\).  The set
\[
 Y=\left\{
 \bigl((r_{g_i}(p)-t)_{i\leq I},
 ((1-\eta)-c_{v_j,h_j}(p))_{j\leq J}\bigr):
 p\in\mathscr K
 \right\}
\tag{11}
\]
is compact and convex, and the finite-subcover property says that it is
disjoint from the closed negative orthant \(O\).

For completeness, we derive the required separator.  Choose
\(y_*\in Y\) minimizing the squared distance to \(O\), let \(z_*\) be its
coordinatewise projection on \(O\), and set \(n=y_*-z_*\).  Then
\(n\geq0\), \(n'z_*=0\), and \(n'y_*=\|n\|_2^2>0\).  For \(y\in Y\),
apply minimality to \(y_*+s(y-y_*)\in Y\), divide the resulting squared
distance inequality by \(s>0\), and let \(s\downarrow0\).  This gives
\(n'y\geq n'y_*\).  Consequently there are
\(\gamma_i,\zeta_j\geq0\), not all zero, and \(q>0\) such that
\[
 \sum_i\gamma_i\{r_{g_i}(p)-t\}
 +\sum_j\zeta_j\{(1-\eta)-c_{v_j,h_j}(p)\}
 \geq q
 \quad\text{for all }p\in\mathscr K.
\tag{12}
\]
For the kernel that always reports \(V\), every coverage deficit in (12)
equals \(-\eta\).  Hence \(G=\sum_i\gamma_i>0\), since otherwise (12)
would have a nonpositive left side.  Set \(b_i=\gamma_i/G\), and place
coverage mass \(\zeta_j/G\) at \((v_j,h_j)\).  Divide (12) by \(G\), take
the infimum over \(\mathscr K\), and use (8) to obtain
\[
 \mathcal D(\alpha,\beta)\geq t+\frac qG>t.
\tag{13}
\]
Together with (9), and then \(t\uparrow M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\), this
proves
\[
 M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)
 =\sup_{\alpha,\beta\ {\rm finite\ atomic}}\mathcal D(\alpha,\beta).
\tag{14}
\]
This argument never restricts the spatial support of either measure.

It remains to verify the effective support and value assertions.  Every
\(D_v\) is a finite union of rational polyhedra.  For a rational polyhedron
\(\{x:Ax\leq b\}\), the squared \(\Sigma^{-1}\)-distance is obtained by
partitioning according to the finitely many active sets of its strictly
convex quadratic projection problem.  On each active-set piece, primal
feasibility, stationarity, complementary slackness, and a distance
comparison with \(r^2\) are finite polynomial equalities and inequalities
in the displayed variables.  Taking the finite disjunction over components,
labels, and active sets gives a finite exact semialgebraic description of
\(H_r\).  Recursive sign decomposition of these finitely many polynomials
has two relevant constructive properties: on a rational box it terminates,
and it either certifies emptiness or isolates a sample whose coordinates
are represented by rational isolating intervals and minimal polynomials.
Enumerating rational boxes and retaining the nonempty cells therefore
produces dense, effectively enumerable, rationally encoded real-algebraic
samples in every \(D_v\) and in \(H_r\), including lower-dimensional cells.
The nonemptiness of \(H_r\) used here is
\(\mathrm{ass:separated\mbox{-}domain\mbox{-}nonempty}\).

If \(x_n\to x\), then
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}
 |\phi_\Sigma(w-x_n)-\phi_\Sigma(w-x)|\,dw
 =
 2-2\int_{\mathbb R^d}
 \min\{\phi_\Sigma(w-x_n),\phi_\Sigma(w-x)\}\,dw
 \longrightarrow0
\tag{15}
\]
by pointwise convergence and dominated convergence.  Thus finitely many
support points can be perturbed inside their respective sets, and their
weights can be perturbed to nonnegative rationals while keeping
\(\sum_i b_i=1\), so that all mixtures in (6) converge in \(L^1\).  Since
\[
 \left|\min_A x_A-\min_A y_A\right|
 \leq\max_A|x_A-y_A|,
\tag{16}
\]
the corresponding values (8) converge.  Rational weights and rationally
encoded real-algebraic support points are therefore dense among all finite
atomic certificates.

Finally, the value of each encoded certificate is a computable real.  On a
rational box, interval Taylor bounds for the finitely many Gaussian
mixtures and their derivatives give convergent rational lower and upper
Riemann bounds for the finite minimum in (8).  Off the box, the absolute
integrand is bounded by
\(D b(w)+\sum_v a_v(w)\), whose finitely many Gaussian tails have effective
outward rational bounds.  Enumerate all encoded atomic certificates and
dovetail these shrinking two-sided enclosures.  Start from any enumerated
certificate and replace the current one only when another certificate's
certified lower endpoint exceeds the current certificate's certified upper
endpoint.  The exact selected values are nondecreasing.  If their limit
were below the supremum in (14), an already enumerated certificate with a
strictly larger value would eventually have that strict inequality
certified, a contradiction.  Hence the selected finite atomic certificates
converge upward to \(M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\), proving every assertion of
the theorem.

\textbf{Technique: effective dovetailing of sound primal and dual
semidecisions.}
Assume the conclusion of
\(\mathrm{thm:effective\mbox{-}compatible\mbox{-}density}\), as stipulated
in the statement.  We construct the algorithm named in
\(\mathrm{def:certglue\mbox{-}handle}\).

At stage \(s\), compute all finite classes
\(\mathfrak F_{\mathfrak G,m}\) with \(m\leq s\).  For each member whose
finite geometry, incidence forest, protected neighborhoods, rational
partition, and rounding receipt have encoding length at most \(s\), run
outward Gaussian integration to precision \(2^{-s}\).  Accept a primal
record only when finite rational checks verify a global lower coverage
enclosure \(c^-_{q,s}\), an upper separated-risk enclosure \(r^+_{q,s}\),
and every chart, seam, tail, covariance, integration, and rounding entry of
\(\rho_{\mathrm{led}}\).  Intersect probability enclosures with \([0,1]\)
and set
\[
 \rho_{q,s}=\max\{0,1-\eta-c^-_{q,s}\},
 \qquad
 U_{q,s}=r^+_{q,s}+
 \frac{D\rho_{q,s}}{\eta+\rho_{q,s}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(c^-_{q,s}\geq0\) and \(\eta>0\),
\(0\leq\rho_{q,s}\leq1-\eta<1\).  The hypothesis of
\(\mathrm{lem:exact\mbox{-}coverage\mbox{-}repair}\) is therefore verified,
so the archived repaired rule is globally honest and
\[
 M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\leq U_{q,s}.
\tag{2}
\]

In parallel, enumerate the rational-weight, finite atomic duals with
rationally encoded real-algebraic support points in the relevant \(D_v\)'s
and in \(H_r\).  Exact polynomial sign tests verify support membership.
The effective enclosure proved in
\(\mathrm{thm:attainment\mbox{-}atomic\mbox{-}duality}\) gives a rational
lower endpoint for every such dual value.  Define
\[
 L_s=\max\{\text{verified dual lower endpoints through stage }s\},
 \qquad
 U_s=\min\{U_{q,t}:q\text{ was accepted at some }t\leq s\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The maximum or minimum is left undefined until its finite collection is
nonempty.  Weak duality from
\(\mathrm{thm:attainment\mbox{-}atomic\mbox{-}duality}\) and (2) imply,
whenever both are defined,
\[
 L_s\leq M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\leq U_s.
\tag{4}
\]

Every operation at a fixed stage terminates.  Rational polyhedral
feasibility and incidence reduce to finitely many rational linear programs.
On protected bounded sets, interval arithmetic encloses the finitely many
Gaussian integrals; Gaussian tail inequalities enclose their complements.
On an outer chart, the path comparisons in
\(\mathrm{def:weighted\mbox{-}error\mbox{-}ledger}\) reduce the claimed
bound to finitely many chart integrals and rational ledger entries.
Consequently acceptance consists only of finitely many rational
comparisons.  This proves that each archived certificate is replayable and
does not rely on a floating-point sign decision.

We prove that the stopping test is eventually satisfied.  Let \(K^*\) be
the minimizer supplied by
\(\mathrm{thm:attainment\mbox{-}atomic\mbox{-}duality}\).  Effective
compatible density gives, for every \(m\), a member
\(q_m\in\mathfrak F_{\mathfrak G,m}\) satisfying
\[
 c(q_m)\geq1-\eta-\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m),\qquad
 r(q_m)\leq M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)+D\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m),\qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{led}}(q_m)\leq\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m).
\tag{5}
\]
For every rational \(\zeta>0\), sufficiently fine outward refinement of
this finite record yields
\[
 c^-_{q_m,s}\geq c(q_m)-\zeta,\qquad
 r^+_{q_m,s}\leq r(q_m)+\zeta.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, compact interval widths, Gaussian tails, covariance enclosures,
and rational rounding errors all converge effectively to zero, while the
third inequality in (5) supplies the finite outer-chart ledger.  Equations
(1), (5), and (6) imply
\[
 U_s\leq M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)
 +D\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m)+\zeta
 +\frac{D\{\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m)+\zeta\}}
 {\eta+\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m)+\zeta}.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(\Omega_{\mathfrak G}(m)\downarrow0\), primal upper endpoints along
the dovetail approach the minimax value.  The last assertion of
\(\mathrm{thm:attainment\mbox{-}atomic\mbox{-}duality}\) supplies dual lower
endpoints approaching the same value.  Therefore a finite stage has
\(U_s-L_s\leq\varepsilon\).

It remains to expose total recursive cost bounds.  For
\[
 i\in\{\mathrm{glue},\mathrm{cut},\mathrm{Gauss},\mathrm{LP}\},
\]
let \(a_i(\mathfrak G,s)\) be the number of new operations of type \(i\)
performed at stage \(s\), and let \(b(\mathfrak G,s)\) be the largest
integer bit length appearing at that stage.  These functions are computable
because the stage is finite.  Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_i(0)&=a_i(\mathfrak G,0),&
 C_i(s+1)&=C_i(s)+a_i(\mathfrak G,s+1),\\
 B(0)&=b(\mathfrak G,0),&
 B(s+1)&=\max\{B(s),b(\mathfrak G,s+1)\},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
 s_*(\mathfrak G,\varepsilon)=
 \min\{s:L_s,U_s\text{ are defined and }U_s-L_s\leq\varepsilon\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The preceding convergence proves that the unbounded search in (9) halts
for every encoded input, so \(s_*\) is total recursive.  The promised
receipt is explicitly
\[
 N_i(\mathfrak G,\varepsilon)=
 C_i\{s_*(\mathfrak G,\varepsilon)\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Bit}(\mathfrak G,\varepsilon)=
 B\{s_*(\mathfrak G,\varepsilon)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
At the stopping stage, archive the atomic dual attaining \(L_s\) and the
repaired primal attaining \(U_s\).  Equations (4) and (9) give the outward
gap, and (8)--(10) give all five finite entries of \(\mathsf C\).  This is
the asserted termination and certificate theorem, conditional only on the
effective-compatible-density conclusion explicitly named in its statement.

\textbf{Technique: standardized facet inequalities, an exact bivariate
Gaussian comparison, and pointwise finite-action dual minimization.}
Put
\[
 q=\frac{49}{25},\qquad k=\sqrt{\frac3{10}},\qquad
 s=\sqrt{\frac{13}{10}},
\]
and use the whitening map in
\(\mathrm{def:four\mbox{-}label\mbox{-}primal\mbox{-}object}\),
\[
 z=\left(\frac a{\sqrt{1/8}},\frac b{\sqrt{3/20}}\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Every row of every displayed \(F_v\) has Euclidean norm one.  If
\(h\in D_v\), both inward standardized facet means are nonnegative.
Therefore the union bound gives
\[
 P_h\{v\notin Q_q(W)\}\leq2\Phi(-q).
\tag{2}
\]
Outward rational evaluation gives
\[
 0.0249978951482
 <\Phi(-49/25)
 <0.0249978951483.
\tag{3}
\]
For clarity, all decimal endpoints in this proof denote the corresponding
terminating rationals.  A check of (3), and of the intervals below, uses
only rational arithmetic: enclose each displayed square root by squaring
rational endpoints; enclose \(\pi\) through the alternating Machin identity
\(\pi/4=4\arctan(1/5)-\arctan(1/239)\); range-reduce the exponential; and
integrate its alternating Taylor bounds on rational subintervals, with the
first omitted term charged outward.  Thus these are interval certificates,
not floating-point evaluations.  From (2)--(3),
\[
 2\Phi(-49/25)<0.0499957902966<\frac1{20},
\]
which proves global \(0.95\) coverage, including equality faces.

Now take \(h\in H_2\) with unique label \(v\).  In whitened coordinates the
other three cones form the complement of the interior of \(D_v\).
Consequently distance at least two from their union implies that each of
the two unit inward facet means of \(D_v\) is at least two.  If both observed
inward coordinates exceed \(q\), then \(v\) is included and every rival is
excluded by its opposite facet.  Hence \(Q_q(W)=\{v\}\) on that event.
The four possible correlations between the two inward noises are \(0\),
\(\sqrt{3/13}\), or \(-\sqrt{3/13}\), and so all are at least
\[
 \rho_*=-\sqrt{\frac3{13}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(t=2-q=1/25\).  If \(\Phi_2(x,y;\rho)\) is the centered standard
bivariate Gaussian distribution function, differentiation under its
density integral and two integrations by parts give
\[
 \frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\Phi_2(t,t;\rho)
 =\phi_2(t,t;\rho)>0.
\]
Thus correlation monotonicity and the preceding sufficient event imply
\[
 P_h\{Q_q(W)=\{v\}\}\geq\Phi_2(t,t;\rho_*).
\tag{5}
\]
Differentiating on the diagonal gives
\[
 \frac d{du}\Phi_2(u,u;\rho_*)
 =2\phi(u)\Phi\left(
 u\sqrt{\frac{1-\rho_*}{1+\rho_*}}\right)
 \geq\phi(u),\qquad u\geq0.
\tag{6}
\]
A direct polar-coordinate integration of the centered Gaussian quadrant
gives
\[
 \Phi_2(0,0;\rho_*)
 =\frac14+\frac{\arcsin(\rho_*)}{2\pi}
 =\frac14-\frac{\arctan\sqrt{3/10}}{2\pi}.
\tag{7}
\]
Integrating (6) from \(0\) to \(t\), using (5), and using
\(0\leq\ell\leq1\), yields
\[
 \sup_{h\in H_2}E_h\ell\{Q_q(W)\}
 \leq
 \frac34+\frac{\arctan\sqrt{3/10}}{2\pi}
 -\{\Phi(1/25)-1/2\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The rational interval procedure described after (3) gives
\[
 0.0797514300099
 <\frac{\arctan\sqrt{3/10}}{2\pi}
 <0.0797514300101
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
 0.0159534368527
 <\Phi(1/25)-\frac12
 <0.0159534368530.
\tag{10}
\]
Substitution in (8) gives an upper bound below
\(0.8137979931574<0.813799\).

For the lower certificate, set
\(\mu_\pm=(\pm1/\sqrt2,2)\) and \(\mu_0=(0,2)\).
In whitened coordinates, \(\mu_+\) has distance exactly two from the rival
halfspace across \(a=0\), and distance greater than two from the lower
halfplane; hence \(\mu_+\in H_2\).  Symmetry gives \(\mu_-\in H_2\).
All three atoms have the same standardized second coordinate, so it
integrates out.  If \(z\) denotes the standardized first coordinate, the
risk-mixture density divided by \(\phi(z)\) is
\[
 \frac{\phi(z-2)+\phi(z+2)}{2\phi(z)}
 =e^{-2}\cosh(2z).
\tag{11}
\]
Under the discrete label metric, pointwise minimization in
\(\mathrm{def:four\mbox{-}label\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}object}\) is exactly the
minimum of the risk-mixture density and \(\lambda\phi(z)\): reporting one
of the singletons \(02\) or \(12\) costs \(\lambda\phi(z)\), reporting an
action containing both costs the risk density, and every other action is
weakly more expensive.  Put
\[
 a_0=\frac{1028}{625},\qquad
 \lambda_0=e^{-2}\cosh(2a_0).
\tag{12}
\]
The two curves cross at \(\pm a_0\).  Integration on the central and two
outer intervals, followed by the penalty
\(2\eta\lambda_0=\lambda_0/10\), gives
\[
 D_0=
 \Phi(a_0-2)-\Phi(-a_0-2)
 +\lambda_0\{2\Phi(-a_0)-1/10\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Exact outward intervals are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 1.8181887545408<\lambda_0<1.8181887545411,\\
 0.3612198823625<\Phi(a_0-2)<0.3612198823627,\\
 0.0001337999106<\Phi(-a_0-2)<0.0001337999108,\\
 0.0500055310938<\Phi(-a_0)<0.0500055310941.
 \end{gathered}
\tag{14}
\]
Rational substitution in (13) yields
\[
 D_0>0.3611061955936.
\]
For two multipliers, pointwise Lipschitz continuity of the minimum and the
penalty term imply
\[
 |D(\lambda)-D(\lambda')|
 \leq |\lambda-\lambda'|+\frac1{10}|\lambda-\lambda'|
 =\frac{11}{10}|\lambda-\lambda'|.
\tag{15}
\]
The first line of (14) and
\(\lambda_4=90909437727/50000000000\) make the right side of (15) less
than \(3753/10^{15}\).  Therefore
\[
 L_4=D(\lambda_4)>0.3611061955898>0.361100.
\tag{16}
\]
The support checks above and
\(\mathrm{thm:attainment\mbox{-}atomic\mbox{-}duality}\) make this a valid
minimax lower certificate.

Finally set \(r=0\).  All four closed regions contain the origin.  Global
honesty therefore implies
\[
 E_0|K(W)|=\sum_{v\in V_4}P_0\{v\in K(W)\}
 \geq4(1-1/20)=\frac{19}{5}.
\tag{17}
\]
For every \(A\subseteq V_4\),
\[
 |A|\leq1+3\mathbf1\{|A|\geq2\},
\tag{18}
\]
and, for the discrete metric, \(\ell(A)=\mathbf1\{|A|\geq2\}\).
Thus (17)--(18) imply
\(E_0\ell\{K(W)\}\geq14/15\).  Gaussian \(L^1\)-translation continuity,
as proved in (15) of the atomic-duality proof, makes
\(h\mapsto E_h\ell\{K(W)\}\) continuous.  Unique-label points of \(H_0\)
in each cone approach the origin, so \(R_0(K)\geq14/15\).
The rule \(K_{\mathrm{ct}}\) includes each label with probability
\(14/15+1/60=19/20\), and has loss one only when it reports \(V_4\), an
event of probability \(14/15\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:four\mbox{-}label\mbox{-}contact\mbox{-}object}\), it is
globally honest and attains \(14/15\).  This completes all parts of the
proposition.

Starting from the single ancestor \(q_s=K*\phi_{s^2\Sigma}\), the established
common-ray construction proves, for every face inclusion
\(\tau\preceq\sigma\),
\[
 q_{\sigma,L}=
 \left(\prod_{\varrho\in\mathscr R(\sigma)\setminus\mathscr R(\tau)}
 \mathsf A_{\varrho,L}\right)q_{\tau,L},
\]
independently of the incidence path, and proves the uniform translation
bounds
\[
 \|q_{\sigma,L}(w+a\varrho)-q_{\sigma,L}(w)\|_1
 \leq2\min\{1,a/L\},
 \qquad
 |F^f_{\sigma,L}(w+a\varrho)-F^f_{\sigma,L}(w)|
 \leq R_f\min\{1,a/L\}.
\]
Together with Gaussian smoothing, one-chart averaging, and weighted path
telescoping, every fixed finite library has the computable ledger terms
\[
 R_f\min\{1,s^2\sqrt{d/8}\},\qquad
 \frac{R_fk}{T\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad
 \sum_e\Gamma_{C,e}^f+\frac{R_f}{2}\omega(h).
