\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Carter's bounded-ratio comparison controls fixed positive-cell multinomial experiments on compact parameter sets, but it neither supplies finite recession families nor controls a seam escaping every compact set. Moving offsets additionally require compact-offset arrangement stability and separately supplied envelopes for replayable accounting. At recession, common-ray commutativity does not control the Gaussian discrepancy between every incident candidate on simultaneous collars. The unresolved effective-compatible-density question asks whether canonical sections and every positive-weight candidate can be certified safe, translated tails can be bounded uniformly, and a finite rational schedule can make the accumulated compatibility ledger tend to zero.

\paragraph{Niche.} Finite-label partial-identification inference combines discontinuous completed labels, intersecting unbounded recession faces, and a global honesty constraint. Equality on a geometric face does not control all candidates mixed on nearby simultaneous collars, while compact local asymptotics cannot detect a hard seam escaping to infinity. The proved compact/windowed theory and the unanswered compatibility-density question at unbounded recession seams are therefore mathematically distinct.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves common-ray operator coherence, Carter-based fixed-base compact transfer with separate semantic and covariance terms, a global minimax lower limit, and two-sided convergence on diagonally selected expanding lawful windows. It does not prove global effective compatible density. The corresponding open-ended question lists the five certificates still required. Replayable moving-offset rules and CertGluePFL termination are asserted only under an affirmative answer or a separately proved sufficient compatibility package.

\section{Related work}

Carter (2002, Theorem 1 and Sections 2--6) supplies the bounded-ratio comparison between fixed positive-cell multinomial experiments and their matched multivariate normal experiments through parameter-independent kernels. The present paper separately accounts for score maps, covariance replacement, completed-affine-arrangement stability, moving offsets, and diagonally selected expanding lawful windows. Blanco, Flores, and Flores-Lagunes (2013, Assumptions A--B and Proposition 1) motivate an always-employed wage comparison; the asserted embedding is restricted to the singleton-covariate, known rational assignment-floor, perfect-compliance, monotone-employment, finite-wage-grid submodel with positive always-employed mass, and gives neither unrestricted-class inclusion nor automatic sharpness transfer. Day and Markov--Kakutani invariant-mean theory and Namioka's 1964 Foelner conditions concern a single amenable commuting action and supply neither an input-computable modulus nor semantic coverage, separated-risk safety, translated-tail control, or branching-refinement complexity.

\(\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known nonsingular Gaussian covariance, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2021ATE}).
\end{assumption}

\(H_r\ne\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nonempty separated parameter region, \cite{GoldenshlugerJuditskyNemirovski2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:multinomial-interior]\label{ass:multinomial-interior}
\(\min_{j\in\mathcal J}(p_0)_j\ge\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (multinomial probabilities bounded away from zero, \cite{Carter2002MultinomialGaussian}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:multinomial-local-expansion]\label{ass:multinomial-local-expansion}
\(\sup_{u\in C}\|\sqrt n[\{p_n(u)-p_n(0)\}]-Bu\|_2\to0\quad\forall C\Subset\mathbb R^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This transparent premise specifies smooth finite-dimensional multinomial charts around a possibly root-\(n\)-moving base. It holds when the chart derivative is \(B\) and the centered second-order remainder is uniform on compact local-direction sets; it is not attributed to Carter.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-assignment-probabilities]\label{ass:known-assignment-probabilities}

\begin{lemma}[lem:gaussian-smoothing]\label{lem:gaussian-smoothing}
For \(K_s(w)=E[K(w+s\Sigma^{1/2}\xi)]\), \(\xi\sim N_d(0,I_d)\), and every action functional \(f\in[0,R_f]\), \[\sup_{h\in\mathbb R^d}|E_hf\{K_s(W)\}-E_hf\{K(W)\}|\le R_f\min\{1,s^2\sqrt{d/8}\},\] and \(\|K_s(w)-K_s(w')\|_1\le\sqrt{2/\pi}\|w-w'\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}/s\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(K_A(w)=K(A\mid w)\) and \(F_K^f(w)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}f(A)K_A(w)\). Then \(0\le F_K^f\le R_f\), and Fubini's theorem gives
\[
 E_hF_{K_s}^f(W)
 =\int F_K^f(x)\,dN_d\{h,(1+s^2)\Sigma\}(x).
\]
The unsmoothed expectation is the same integral with \(N_d(h,\Sigma)\). For \(P_s=N_d\{h,(1+s^2)\Sigma\}\) and \(P_0=N_d(h,\Sigma)\), direct substitution in the Gaussian likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_s\Vert P_0)
 =\frac d2\{s^2-\log(1+s^2)\}.
\]
For \(x\ge0\), \(x-\log(1+x)\le x^2/2\), because the derivative of \(x^2/2-x+\log(1+x)\) is \(x^2/(1+x)\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_s\Vert P_0)\le \frac{ds^4}{4}.
\]
Let \(A\) be a likelihood-ratio upper level set attaining total variation and put \(v=P_s(A)-P_0(A)\ge0\). The log-sum inequality reduces relative entropy to the binary experiment on \(\{A,A^c\}\). For binary probabilities \(q+v\) and \(q\), two differentiations in \(v\), followed by minimization over \(0\le q\le1-v\), give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\operatorname{Bern}(q+v)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(q)\}\ge2v^2.
\]
Thus the elementary Pinsker bound yields
\[
 \|P_s-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(P_s\Vert P_0)/2}
 \le s^2\sqrt{d/8}.
\]
The total-variation distance is also at most one. Since the difference of the expectations of a function with range in \([0,R_f]\) is at most \(R_f\|P_s-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\), this proves
\[
 \sup_h\left|E_hf\{K_s(W)\}-E_hf\{K(W)\}\right|
 \le R_f\min\{1,s^2\sqrt{d/8}\}.
\]
For the pointwise modulus, \(K_s(w)\) is obtained by applying the Markov kernel \(K\) to \(N_d(w,s^2\Sigma)\). Contraction of total variation under a Markov kernel therefore gives
\[
 \|K_s(w)-K_s(w')\|_1
 \le 2\|N_d(w,s^2\Sigma)-N_d(w',s^2\Sigma)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
If \(a=\|w-w'\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}/s\), the likelihood-ratio halfspace calculation for two equal-covariance Gaussian laws gives their total-variation distance as \(2\Phi(a/2)-1\). Since \(\Phi(x)-1/2\le x/\sqrt{2\pi}\) for \(x\ge0\),
\[
 \|K_s(w)-K_s(w')\|_1
 \le \sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\|w-w'\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}}{s}.
\]
All divisions use \(s>0\), and the Mahalanobis norm is defined because \(\Sigma\succ0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The uniform kernel-independent bound converts arbitrary measurable honest rules into quantitatively gridable rules. \textit{Gap.} Jones and Peet smooth a discontinuous terminal cost inside a fixed Gaussian integral; they do not smooth an arbitrary randomized rule uniformly over all shifted means. \textit{Consumer.} The finite-rule enumeration uses this modulus to choose observation meshes without regularity assumptions on a near-minimizer.

\begin{lemma}[lem:single-chart-averaging]\label{lem:single-chart-averaging}
For a recession stratum \(\sigma\), a \(\Sigma\)-orthogonal tangential basis \(B_{\sigma}\), and \(Q_T(w)=(2T)^{-k}\int_{[-T,T]^k}K_s(w+t u+B_{\sigma}z)\,dz\), \[\|Q_T(w+B_{\sigma}a)-Q_T(w)\|_1\le\min\{2,\|a\|_1/T\},\] and integrating out the independent residual tangential Gaussian coordinate changes any \([0,R_f]\)-valued expectation by at most \(R_fk/(T\sqrt{2\pi})\) on every declared bounded normal chart.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the tangential coordinates so that the columns of \(B_\sigma\) are orthonormal after whitening by \(\Sigma^{-1/2}\), and let \(U_T\) be uniform on \([-T,T]^k\), where \(T>0\). The displayed average is
\[
 Q_T(w)=E\{K_s(w+t u+B_\sigma U_T)\}.
\]
For \(a\in\mathbb R^k\), contraction under the kernel \(z\mapsto K_s(w+t u+B_\sigma z)\) implies
\[
 \|Q_T(w+B_\sigma a)-Q_T(w)\|_1
 \le \|\mathcal L(U_T+a)-\mathcal L(U_T)\|_1.
\]
In one coordinate the last \(L^1\)-distance is \(\min\{2,|a_i|/T\}\). Coupling the product uniforms coordinate by coordinate, or equivalently bounding the symmetric difference of the two boxes, gives
\[
 \|\mathcal L(U_T+a)-\mathcal L(U_T)\|_1
 \le \min\left\{2,\frac{\|a\|_1}{T}\right\}.
\]
This proves the first assertion without any limiting behavior of \(K\).

For the second assertion, the \(\Sigma\)-orthogonal quotient decomposition makes the residual tangential observation \(B_\sigma Z\), with \(Z\sim N_k(0,I_k)\), independent of the normal coordinate and parameter-free. If \(p,p'\) are two action distributions and \(0\le f\le R_f\), the zero-sum identity for \(p-p'\) gives
\[
 \left|\sum_A f(A)\{p_A-p'_A\}\right|
 \le \frac{R_f}{2}\|p-p'\|_1.
\]
Conditioning on the normal coordinate and using the first bound with \(a=Z\),
\[
 \left|E f\{Q_T(W_N+B_\sigma Z)\}-E f\{Q_T(W_N)\}\right|
 \le \frac{R_f}{2T}E\|Z\|_1
 =\frac{R_fk}{T\sqrt{2\pi}},
\]
because \(E|Z_i|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). The bounded-normal-chart condition is used only to ensure that the declared quotient chart and the associated coverage or separated-risk mean set are valid throughout this averaging operation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the quantitative local replacement step that neutralizes oscillatory behavior at infinity without assuming a recession limit. \textit{Gap.} Burgos Gil and Sombra control incidence geometry qualitatively, while no located statistical result supplies this rule-level tangential averaging bound. \textit{Consumer.} Each recession chart receives a finite normal-dimensional approximation with an explicit error allocation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:weighted-gluing-inequality]\label{lem:weighted-gluing-inequality}
For the glued rule \(\widetilde K(w)=\sum_i\lambda_i(w)q_i(w)\), every mean cell \(C\), anchor \(a(C)\), specified incidence paths, and \([0,R_f]\)-valued action functional \(f\), \[|E_hF_{\widetilde K}^{f}(W)-E_hF_{a(C)}^{f}(W)|\le\sum_e\Gamma_{C,e}^{f}+R_f\omega(h)/2\] uniformly on the declared coverage or separated-risk mean domain, including means centered on seams.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(F_i^f(w)=\sum_A f(A)q_{i,A}(w)\) and \(F_{\widetilde K}^f(w)=\sum_i\lambda_i(w)F_i^f(w)\). Orient every path from the anchor \(a(C)\) to \(i\). Telescoping along that path gives
\[
 F_i^f(w)-F_{a(C)}^f(w)
 =\sum_{e\in\pi_C(i)}\{F_{e^+}^f(w)-F_{e^-}^f(w)\}.
\]
Multiplication by \(\lambda_i(w)\ge0\), summation over \(i\), and the triangle inequality imply
\[
 |F_{\widetilde K}^f(w)-F_{a(C)}^f(w)|
 \le \sum_e g_{C,e}(w)|F_{e^+}^f(w)-F_{e^-}^f(w)|,
 \qquad
 g_{C,e}(w)=\sum_{i:e\in\pi_C(i)}\lambda_i(w).
\]
Both endpoint functionals lie in \([0,R_f]\). Splitting the integral for edge \(e\) over \(P_{C,e}\) and \(P_{C,e}^c\) consequently yields, for every \(h\in M_{C,f}\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_h\!\left[g_{C,e}(W)|F_{e^+}^f(W)-F_{e^-}^f(W)|\right]
 &\le \int_{P_{C,e}}g_{C,e}|F_{e^+}^f-F_{e^-}^f|\phi_\Sigma(w-h)\,dw\\
 &\quad+R_f\int_{P_{C,e}^c}g_{C,e}\phi_\Sigma(w-h)\,dw\\
 &\le \Gamma_{C,e}^f.
 \end{split}
\]
Thus the left side in the claim is at most \(\sum_e\Gamma_{C,e}^f\). The supremum is over the entire declared mean set and therefore includes seam-centered means.

For completeness, let \(d_i=\lambda_i(W)-\lambda_i(h)\). Since \(\sum_i d_i=0\) and \(0\le F_i^f\le R_f\),
\[
 \left|\sum_i d_iF_i^f(W)\right|
 \le \frac{R_f}{2}\sum_i|d_i|.
\]
Taking \(E_h\) proves the separate weight-variation estimate \(R_f\omega(h)/2\). Adding this nonnegative certified remainder to the sharper path bound proves the stated inequality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The inequality prevents uncharged off-neighborhood and weight-variation errors from invalidating a finite gluing certificate. \textit{Gap.} The polyhedral compactification literature proves that charts meet correctly but has no Gaussian-weighted rule-discrepancy inequality. \textit{Consumer.} An independent verifier can replay every seam and path charge in the primal certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:attainment-atomic-duality]\label{thm:attainment-atomic-duality}
For every \(\mathfrak G\in\mathfrak E\), the value \(M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\) is attained by a globally honest randomized rule and equals the supremum of the Lagrange dual over finite nonnegative atomic coverage measures on the regions \(D_v\) and finite atomic probability measures on \(H_r\). Every finite atomic dual is a valid lower certificate. An increasing enumeration of finite atomic certificates with rational weights and rationally encoded real-algebraic support points converges upward to \(M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\), without a bounded-support premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Technique: local weak compactness followed by finite-dimensional convex
separation.}
Let \(\mu\) be Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R^d\), and write a Markov
kernel as
\[
 p=(p_A)_{A\in\mathcal A},\qquad
 p_A\geq0,\qquad \sum_{A\in\mathcal A}p_A=1
 \quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.
\tag{1}
\]
Denote the set in (1) by \(\mathscr K\).  This representation is exact because
\(\mathcal A=2^V\) is finite.  Put \(B_j=[-j,j]^d\), and give
\(\mathscr K\) the coordinatewise weak topology of \(L^2(B_j)\), for every
\(A\in\mathcal A\) and \(j\geq1\).

We first prove compactness rather than assume it.  For every \(p\in\mathscr K\),
\[
 \|p_A\|_{L^2(B_j)}\leq\mu(B_j)^{1/2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose a countable orthonormal basis of each \(L^2(B_j)\).  From any sequence
in \(\mathscr K\), diagonal extraction makes all its Fourier coefficients,
over the countable triples consisting of an action, a box, and a basis
element, converge.  The bounds (2) and Bessel's inequality identify the
coefficient limits with weak \(L^2(B_j)\) limits.  Limits on nested boxes
agree, because a test function on \(B_j\), extended by zero, is a test
function on \(B_{j+1}\).  They therefore define measurable functions
\(p_A\) on \(\mathbb R^d\).

The constraints in (1) are weakly closed.  Indeed, for nonnegative
\(q\in L^2(B_j)\), weak convergence gives
\(\int_{B_j}p_Aq\,d\mu\geq0\).  Taking \(q=(p_A)_-\) proves
\(p_A\geq0\); applying the same argument to \(1-p_A\) proves \(p_A\leq1\).
Testing \(\sum_Ap_A-1\) against its positive and negative parts proves
\(\sum_Ap_A=1\).  The bounded Fourier coefficients metrize this
coordinatewise local weak topology.  The diagonal argument thus proves
sequential compactness in a metric space, and hence compactness of
\(\mathscr K\).  Convexity is immediate from (1).

For \(v\in V\), \(h\in D_v\), and \(g\in H_r\), define
\[
 c_{v,h}(p)=
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\phi_\Sigma(w-h)
       \sum_{A\ni v}p_A(w)\,dw,
 \qquad
 r_g(p)=
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\phi_\Sigma(w-g)
       \sum_A\ell(A)p_A(w)\,dw.
\tag{3}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:rational\mbox{-}positive\mbox{-}covariance}\)
implies that every translated Gaussian density is in \(L^2(B_j)\).  Hence
local weak convergence gives convergence of (3) over \(B_j\).  Outside
\(B_j\), the two errors are bounded, respectively, by the translated
Gaussian tail and by \(D\) times that tail, uniformly over \(p\in\mathscr K\).
First sending the kernel index to infinity and then \(j\) to infinity proves
that every functional in (3) is continuous.

By the definition of \(\mathcal C_\eta\), its image in \(\mathscr K\) is
\[
 \mathscr K_\eta=
 \bigcap_{v\in V}\ \bigcap_{h\in D_v}
 \{p\in\mathscr K:c_{v,h}(p)\geq1-\eta\}.
\tag{4}
\]
It is compact by continuity of (3), and it is nonempty because the rule that
always reports \(V\) lies in it.  By
\(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}profile}\), the objective is
\(p\mapsto\sup_{g\in H_r}r_g(p)\).  It is lower semicontinuous as a
supremum of continuous functions.  It therefore attains its minimum on
\(\mathscr K_\eta\).  All \(N_d(h,\Sigma)\) are equivalent to \(\mu\) because
\(\Sigma\succ0\); changing the finitely many coordinates on one common null
set makes the minimizer a pointwise Markov kernel without changing any
coverage or risk.  This proves primal attainment.

We next prove weak duality and exact atomic exhaustion.  Let
\[
 \alpha_v=\sum_{j=1}^{J_v}a_{vj}\delta_{h_{vj}},
 \quad a_{vj}\geq0,\quad h_{vj}\in D_v,
 \qquad
 \beta=\sum_{i=1}^{I}b_i\delta_{g_i},
 \quad b_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i b_i=1,\quad g_i\in H_r,
\tag{5}
\]
and put
\[
 a_v(w)=\int\phi_\Sigma(w-h)\,\alpha_v(dh),
 \qquad
 b(w)=\int\phi_\Sigma(w-g)\,\beta(dg).
\tag{6}
\]
The Lagrangian for the risk epigraph and the coverage inequalities is
\[
 (1-\eta)\sum_v\alpha_v(D_v)
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\sum_{A\subseteq V}p_A(w)
 \left\{b(w)\ell(A)-\sum_{v\in A}a_v(w)\right\}\,dw .
\tag{7}
\]
At each \(w\), (1) is a finite simplex.  Selecting the first minimizing
action in a fixed enumeration is measurable.  Thus the infimum of (7) over
\(\mathscr K\) equals
\[
 \mathcal D(\alpha,\beta)=
 (1-\eta)\sum_v\alpha_v(D_v)
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\min_{A\subseteq V}
 \left\{b(w)\ell(A)-\sum_{v\in A}a_v(w)\right\}\,dw .
\tag{8}
\]
For \(p\in\mathscr K_\eta\), Tonelli's theorem applies to the nonnegative
mixtures in (6), and (3)--(8) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D(\alpha,\beta)
 &\leq
 \sum_i b_i r_{g_i}(p)
 +\sum_{v,j}a_{vj}\{(1-\eta)-c_{v,h_{vj}}(p)\}\\
 &\leq \sup_{g\in H_r}r_g(p).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore every displayed finite atomic dual is a valid lower certificate.

For the converse, fix \(t<M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\).  The open sets
\[
 \{p:r_g(p)>t\}\quad(g\in H_r),\qquad
 \{p:c_{v,h}(p)<1-\eta\}\quad(v\in V,\ h\in D_v)
\tag{10}
\]
cover \(\mathscr K\).  Indeed, an honest kernel has supremum risk at least
\(M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)>t\), whereas a nonhonest kernel violates one
inequality in (4).  Compactness supplies a finite subcover with risk points
\(g_1,\ldots,g_I\) and coverage pairs
\((v_j,h_j)\), \(j=1,\ldots,J\).  The set
\[
 Y=\left\{
 \bigl((r_{g_i}(p)-t)_{i\leq I},
 ((1-\eta)-c_{v_j,h_j}(p))_{j\leq J}\bigr):
 p\in\mathscr K
 \right\}
\tag{11}
\]
is compact and convex, and the finite-subcover property says that it is
disjoint from the closed negative orthant \(O\).

For completeness, we derive the required separator.  Choose
\(y_*\in Y\) minimizing the squared distance to \(O\), let \(z_*\) be its
coordinatewise projection on \(O\), and set \(n=y_*-z_*\).  Then
\(n\geq0\), \(n'z_*=0\), and \(n'y_*=\|n\|_2^2>0\).  For \(y\in Y\),
apply minimality to \(y_*+s(y-y_*)\in Y\), divide the resulting squared
distance inequality by \(s>0\), and let \(s\downarrow0\).  This gives
\(n'y\geq n'y_*\).  Consequently there are
\(\gamma_i,\zeta_j\geq0\), not all zero, and \(q>0\) such that
\[
 \sum_i\gamma_i\{r_{g_i}(p)-t\}
 +\sum_j\zeta_j\{(1-\eta)-c_{v_j,h_j}(p)\}
 \geq q
 \quad\text{for all }p\in\mathscr K.
\tag{12}
\]
For the kernel that always reports \(V\), every coverage deficit in (12)
equals \(-\eta\).  Hence \(G=\sum_i\gamma_i>0\), since otherwise (12)
would have a nonpositive left side.  Set \(b_i=\gamma_i/G\), and place
coverage mass \(\zeta_j/G\) at \((v_j,h_j)\).  Divide (12) by \(G\), take
the infimum over \(\mathscr K\), and use (8) to obtain
\[
 \mathcal D(\alpha,\beta)\geq t+\frac qG>t.
\tag{13}
\]
Together with (9), and then \(t\uparrow M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\), this
proves
\[
 M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)
 =\sup_{\alpha,\beta\ {\rm finite\ atomic}}\mathcal D(\alpha,\beta).
\tag{14}
\]
This argument never restricts the spatial support of either measure.

It remains to verify the effective support and value assertions.  Every
\(D_v\) is a finite union of rational polyhedra.  For a rational polyhedron
\(\{x:Ax\leq b\}\), the squared \(\Sigma^{-1}\)-distance is obtained by
partitioning according to the finitely many active sets of its strictly
convex quadratic projection problem.  On each active-set piece, primal
feasibility, stationarity, complementary slackness, and a distance
comparison with \(r^2\) are finite polynomial equalities and inequalities
in the displayed variables.  Taking the finite disjunction over components,
labels, and active sets gives a finite exact semialgebraic description of
\(H_r\).  Recursive sign decomposition of these finitely many polynomials
has two relevant constructive properties: on a rational box it terminates,
and it either certifies emptiness or isolates a sample whose coordinates
are represented by rational isolating intervals and minimal polynomials.
Enumerating rational boxes and retaining the nonempty cells therefore
produces dense, effectively enumerable, rationally encoded real-algebraic
samples in every \(D_v\) and in \(H_r\), including lower-dimensional cells.
The nonemptiness of \(H_r\) used here is
\(\mathrm{ass:separated\mbox{-}domain\mbox{-}nonempty}\).

If \(x_n\to x\), then
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}
 |\phi_\Sigma(w-x_n)-\phi_\Sigma(w-x)|\,dw
 =
 2-2\int_{\mathbb R^d}
 \min\{\phi_\Sigma(w-x_n),\phi_\Sigma(w-x)\}\,dw
 \longrightarrow0
\tag{15}
\]
by pointwise convergence and dominated convergence.  Thus finitely many
support points can be perturbed inside their respective sets, and their
weights can be perturbed to nonnegative rationals while keeping
\(\sum_i b_i=1\), so that all mixtures in (6) converge in \(L^1\).  Since
\[
 \left|\min_A x_A-\min_A y_A\right|
 \leq\max_A|x_A-y_A|,
\tag{16}
\]
the corresponding values (8) converge.  Rational weights and rationally
encoded real-algebraic support points are therefore dense among all finite
atomic certificates.

Finally, the value of each encoded certificate is a computable real.  On a
rational box, interval Taylor bounds for the finitely many Gaussian
mixtures and their derivatives give convergent rational lower and upper
Riemann bounds for the finite minimum in (8).  Off the box, the absolute
integrand is bounded by
\(D b(w)+\sum_v a_v(w)\), whose finitely many Gaussian tails have effective
outward rational bounds.  Enumerate all encoded atomic certificates and
dovetail these shrinking two-sided enclosures.  Start from any enumerated
certificate and replace the current one only when another certificate's
certified lower endpoint exceeds the current certificate's certified upper
endpoint.  The exact selected values are nondecreasing.  If their limit
were below the supremum in (14), an already enumerated certificate with a
strictly larger value would eventually have that strict inequality
certified, a contradiction.  Hence the selected finite atomic certificates
converge upward to \(M_{\mathfrak G,\eta}(r)\), proving every assertion of
the theorem.
\end{proof}

\[

 \tag{24}
\]

\[
 \mathsf H^2(\mu,V;u,\Sigma_0)
 =2-2\frac{|V|^{1/4}|\Sigma_0|^{1/4}}
 {|(V+\Sigma_0)/2|^{1/2}}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac18(\mu-u)'

 \tag{25}

 =\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)

\]

\]

Then all chart and semantic correspondences on \(C\), including the directed separation-distance comparison for rival semantic unions, contribute at most

 \tag{28}

If \(W_0\sim N_d(h,\Sigma_0)\), the density of \(a_j'W_0\) is bounded, uniformly in \(h\), by \(\{2\pi a_j'\Sigma_0a_j\}^{-1/2}\).  Hence, for every \(s\ge0\),

Therefore the total geometry charge is at most
\[
 (H_C+A_0)x_n(C).
 \tag{31}

Apply \(\text{lem:bounded-semantic-transfer-by-tv}\) to Carter's kernel and to the Gaussian comparison (26), first with \(A\mapsto\mathbf1\{v\in A\}\) and then with \(A\mapsto\ell_0(A)\).  Both functions take values in \([0,1]\). This is a diagonal existence argument; the qualitative hypotheses do not provide an algorithm deciding the eventual indices in (43)--(45).

 P(X=x,Z=z,D=d,S=s,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}=y)

 \end{split}

\begin{proposition}[prop:lower-endpoint-attainment]\label{prop:lower-endpoint-attainment}

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By fiber--polytope equivalence, \(\Xi(p)\) is nonempty. It is a closed subset of a finite-dimensional probability simplex and hence compact. For \(\lambda\in\Xi(p)\), let
\[
 H_a^\lambda(j)=\sum_{x,r}\lambda_{xr}
 \mathbf 1\{r\text{ is an always-selected complier},\ Y_r(a)\le y_j\},
 \qquad
 M^\lambda=\sum_{x,r}\lambda_{xr}\mathbf 1\{r\text{ is an always-selected complier}\}.
\]
The fiber constraint gives \(M^\lambda\ge m_*>0\). On the finite ordered grid,
\[
 Q_{a,P_\lambda}(t)=y_j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_a^\lambda(j)\ge tM^\lambda
 \ \text{and}\ 
 [j=1\ \text{or}\ H_a^\lambda(j-1)<tM^\lambda]. \tag{4}
\]
All quantities are linear in \(\lambda\). For a fixed pair \((j_0,j_1)\), the common-slack program replaces each strict predecessor inequality by \(H_a^\lambda(j_a-1)\le tM^\lambda-\gamma\) and maximizes \(\gamma\). Its optimum is positive exactly when one \(\lambda\in\Xi(p)\) realizes both quantiles in (4). Every \(\lambda\) realizes one of finitely many pairs, and every feasible pair is realized by its selected basic maximizer.

The effect takes values in the finite set \(\{y_{j_1}-y_{j_0}:1\le j_0,j_1\le J\}\). Choosing the feasible pair with smallest difference therefore equals the infimum over \(\Xi(p)\), and the selected maximizer induces one \(P_t^-(p)\in\mathcal F(p)\). Applying (4) to that same law in both arms gives
\[
 Q_{1,P_t^-(p)}(t)-Q_{0,P_t^-(p)}(t)=\underline\Delta(t;p).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:causal-label-polyhedralization]\label{thm:causal-label-polyhedralization}

 \mathsf C
 :=\{D^{\mathrm{pot}}(1)>D^{\mathrm{pot}}(0)\},
 \qquad
 \mathsf A

\(a,z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \frac{p_{x,z,a,1,y_k}}{\pi_z(x)}

       Y^{\mathrm{pot}}(a)=y_k\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The division is valid because
\(\text{ass:instrument-stratum-positivity}\) gives
\(\pi_z(x)\geq\pi_*>0\).  With binary treatments,

\[
 \mathbf 1\{D^{\mathrm{pot}}(1)=1\}
 -\mathbf 1\{D^{\mathrm{pot}}(0)=1\}
 =\mathbf 1_{\mathsf C},
\tag{18}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbf 1\{D^{\mathrm{pot}}(0)=0\}
 -\mathbf 1\{D^{\mathrm{pot}}(1)=0\}
 =\mathbf 1_{\mathsf C}.
\tag{19}

Substituting (17)--(19) into the definition of \(c_{axk}(p)\) proves

\[

       Y^{\mathrm{pot}}(a)=y_k\}.
\tag{20}
\]

law; equation (20) is a consequence of the maintained restrictions,

selected-complier selection events nested.  If \(s(x)=1\), the event
\(\{S^{\mathrm{pot}}(0)=1\}\) is the smaller one; if \(s(x)=-1\), the
event \(\{S^{\mathrm{pot}}(1)=1\}\) is the smaller one.  The same
conclusion is trivial in a stratum with zero complier probability.

\[
 m_x=\min\{q_{0x},q_{1x}\}
 =P(X=x,\mathsf C,\mathsf A),
 \qquad
 M_{\mathrm{ASC}}=P(\mathsf C,\mathsf A)\geq m_*>0.
\tag{21}
\]
The final inequality is

 \qquad(a\in\{0,1\},\ t\in\mathcal T).

\]

 \end{aligned}

 \underline\Delta(t;p)

\tag{28}

\begin{lemma}[lem:violated-row-mahalanobis-separation]\label{lem:violated-row-mahalanobis-separation}

\begin{theorem}[thm:carter-positive-multinomial-gaussian-comparison]\label{thm:carter-positive-multinomial-gaussian-comparison}

\begin{lemma}[lem:bounded-semantic-transfer-by-tv]\label{lem:bounded-semantic-transfer-by-tv}

 \sup_\theta\left|P_\theta\{v\in(K\circ T)(X)\}
 -Q_\theta\{v\in K(Y)\}\right|\le\epsilon,

and for every \(g:\mathcal A\to[0,R]\),

In particular the normalized Job Corps diameter loss has error at most \(\epsilon\), and a diameter loss bounded by \(D\) has error at most \(D\epsilon\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}

\[
 \|(K\circ T)P_\theta-KQ_\theta\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \|TP_\theta-Q_\theta\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\epsilon.

Apply the defining variational inequality for total variation first to the event \(\{A:v\in A\}\). For \(0\le g\le R\), the layer-cake identity gives

 E_\mu g-E_\nu g
 =\int_0^R\{\mu(g>t)-\nu(g>t)\}\,dt,

and therefore \(|E_\mu g-E_\nu g|\le R\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\). Taking \(g=\ell_0\), whose range is \([0,1]\), or \(g=\ell\), whose range is \([0,D]\), gives the last assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:common-ray-folner-coherence]\label{lem:common-ray-folner-coherence}
The common-ray Følner operators commute. For every chain \(\tau\preceq\sigma\),
\[
 q_{\sigma,L}
 =\left(\prod_{\varrho\in\mathscr R(\sigma)\setminus\mathscr R(\tau)}
 \mathsf A_{\varrho,L}\right)q_{\tau,L},
\]
and the transition operator is independent of the incidence path. If \(a\ge0\), \(\varrho\in\mathscr R(\sigma)\), and \(q_s(w)\) is an action-probability vector, then
\[
 \|q_{\sigma,L}(w+a\varrho)-q_{\sigma,L}(w)\|_1
 \le 2\min\{1,a/L\}.
\]
For every \(f:\mathcal A\to[0,R_f]\), the corresponding scalar bound is \(R_f\min\{1,a/L\}\). Shared face vertices have exactly identical traces under the global finite-element discretization. These identities give computable \(O(L^{-1})\) translation boundary ratios, but do not assert that \(q_{\sigma,L}=q_{\tau,L}\) on a shared face.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For two rays \(\varrho_1,\varrho_2\), Tonelli's theorem applies coordinatewise because every coordinate of a Markov kernel is nonnegative and bounded by one. Thus
\[
 \mathsf A_{\varrho_1,L}\mathsf A_{\varrho_2,L}q(w,A)
 =\frac1{L^2}\int_0^L\int_0^L
 q(w+t_1\varrho_1+t_2\varrho_2,A)\,dt_2dt_1
 =\mathsf A_{\varrho_2,L}\mathsf A_{\varrho_1,L}q(w,A).
\]
Induction over the finite ray set proves commutativity. Cancelling the literally shared factors in \(\mathscr R(\tau)\subseteq\mathscr R(\sigma)\) gives the displayed face-transition identity; commutativity makes the product, and hence every composite transition, independent of the chosen chain.

Write \(q=\mathsf A_{\sigma\setminus\{\varrho\},L}q_s\). For \(0\le a\le L\),
\[
 \mathsf A_{\varrho,L}q(w+a\varrho)-\mathsf A_{\varrho,L}q(w)
 =\frac1L\left\{\int_L^{L+a}q(w+t\varrho)\,dt-
 \int_0^aq(w+t\varrho)\,dt\right\}.
\]
Since an action-probability vector has \(\ell^1\)-norm one, the triangle inequality gives \(2a/L\). The trivial distance bound two handles \(a>L\). If \(F_q^f=\sum_Af(A)q_A\in[0,R_f]\), the difference of the two boundary integrals is at most \(R_fa\), giving \(R_fa/L\), with the trivial bound \(R_f\) for \(a>L\). Finally, a global simplicial complex identifies a geometric face vertex only once; using one variable at that vertex makes all incident affine traces equal there by the uniqueness of barycentric interpolation on the face. This last assertion is algebraic and does not identify the cone-interior averages with one another.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It proves the path-independent algebra and the uniform boundary ratio supplied by literally shared ray factors. \textit{Gap.} Commuting factors alone do not prove equality between a face rule and an incident cone rule. \textit{Consumer.} The effective-compatible-density target uses it as the strongest established simultaneous-incidence ingredient.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-completed-arrangement-stability]\label{lem:finite-completed-arrangement-stability}
For the finite completed affine arrangement defining \(D_v^0(c)\), fix compact sets \(C\subset\mathbb R^d\) and \(C_c\subset\mathcal K_0\). There is a finite computable constant \(H(C,C_c)\) with the following property. If \(c,\widehat c\in C_c\), every active affine inequality is perturbed by at most \(\delta\), and \(u\in C\) has semantic label \(v\) in the perturbed completed system, then there is \(\bar u\in D_v^0(\widehat c)\) such that
\[
 \|u-\bar u\|_{\Sigma_0^{-1}}
 \le H(C,C_c)\{\delta+\|c-\widehat c\|_\infty\}.
\]
Equality faces retain all incident labels. The same finite enumeration gives directed Hausdorff bounds for rival semantic unions and hence changes every separation distance by at most twice the displayed quantity. All constants are obtained by rational active-set enumeration and outward enclosure of \(\Sigma_0\)-norms.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each completed branch is a finite weak system
\[
 A_bu\le \beta_b+G_bc,
\]
with rational \(A_b,\beta_b,G_b\). For a nonempty system, projection of \(u\) onto it and the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker equations show that the residual is generated by a linearly independent subset \(I\) of active row normals. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{\Sigma_0^{-1}}
 \bigl(u,\{x:A_bx\le\beta_b+G_b\widehat c\}\bigr)
 \le H_b\bigl\|(A_bu-\beta_b-G_b\widehat c)_+\bigr\|_\infty,
\]
where one may take \(H_b\) to be the maximum, over the finitely many linearly independent row subsets \(I\), of an outward rational enclosure of the norm of the corresponding right inverse in the \(\Sigma_0^{-1}\) metric. This is a direct finite active-set proof of the linear error bound.

If \(u\) satisfies the system with right-hand perturbation \(r_b\), \(\|r_b\|_\infty\le\delta\), at offset \(c\), then
\[
 \bigl\|(A_bu-\beta_b-G_b\widehat c)_+\bigr\|_\infty
 \le \delta+\|G_b\|_{\infty\to\infty}\|c-\widehat c\|_\infty.
\]
Empty systems are discarded. When a system becomes feasible only at a boundary value of the offset, its limiting weak equalities occur in every incident completion by construction, so the same point is assigned the same semantic label in at least one retained incident system. Taking the maximum over finitely many branches, offset cells, and semantic labels, and enlarging it to absorb \(\|G_b\|_{\infty\to\infty}\), gives \(H(C,C_c)\). Applying the point-to-set bound in both directions to every component of a rival semantic union yields directed Hausdorff error at most the displayed bound. The inequality
\[
 |\operatorname{dist}(u,E)-\operatorname{dist}(\bar u,\bar E)|
 \le \|u-\bar u\|+d_{\mathrm H}(E,\bar E)
\]
then gives the factor two for separation distances. Rational active sets are finite, and \(\Sigma_0\succ0\), so all inverses used on their ranks and all metric norms have terminating outward rational enclosures.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite rational active-set enumeration turns moving slack errors into explicit semantic and separation displacements. \textit{Gap.} This geometric perturbation result is separate from Carter's statistical comparison. \textit{Consumer.} The fixed and moving-offset transfers use its computable Hoffman constants.

\begin{lemma}[lem:escaping-boundary-kill]\label{lem:escaping-boundary-kill}
Compact-uniform local expansion does not determine an unbounded-parameter minimax value. Specifically, for every \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\) there are two two-category, two-label multinomial triangular arrays with the same positive base \(p_0\), the same full-rank tangent \(B\), and identical laws on every fixed compact for all sufficiently large \(n\), such that their zero-offset Gaussian tangent experiment has one semantic label and minimax diameter zero, whereas the second array has global honest diameter minimax value at least \(1-2\eta\) for every \(n\). Thus compact comparison cannot imply a global minimax upper bound without an expanding-domain or recession-control premise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take two categories, labels \(V=\{0,1\}\), discrete label metric, \(p_0=(1/4,3/4)\), and \(B=(1/16,-1/16)'\). For \(q\in(0,1)\), assign truth \(\{0\}\) when \(q<1/2\), truth \(\{0,1\}\) when \(q=1/2\), and truth \(\{1\}\) when \(q>1/2\). This is a completed rational polyhedral label map on the one-dimensional probability simplex. Let
\[
 q_n^A(u)=\frac14+\frac1{16}\operatorname{clip}(u/\sqrt n,-1,1),
 \qquad p_n^A(u)=(q_n^A(u),1-q_n^A(u)).
\]
Define \(p_n^B=p_n^A\) outside \(|u-n|\le\sqrt n\), while on that interval put
\[
 q_n^B(u)=\frac12+\frac{u-n}{8\sqrt n},
 \qquad p_n^B(u)=(q_n^B(u),1-q_n^B(u)).
\]
All coordinates of both arrays are at least \(3/16\). For every fixed compact \(C\), eventually \(C\cap[n-\sqrt n,n+\sqrt n]=\varnothing\) and clipping is inactive on \(C\). Therefore both arrays agree on \(C\) and
\[
 \sup_{u\in C}\left\|\sqrt n\{p_n^A(u)-p_0\}-Bu\right\|_2
 =\sup_{u\in C}\left\|\sqrt n\{p_n^B(u)-p_0\}-Bu\right\|_2=0.
\]
They have the same base, tangent, information, covariance, and every fixed-compact limiting label. Because \(q_n^A(u)\le5/16<1/2\), its sole truth is label zero; the constant action \(\{0\}\) is honest and has diameter zero.

For the second array, \(q_n^B(n)=1/2\). At this parameter, honesty of any rule \(K_n\) implies
\[
 P_{1/2}\{0\in K_n\}\ge1-\eta,
 \qquad P_{1/2}\{1\in K_n\}\ge1-\eta.
\]
The union bound therefore gives
\[
 P_{1/2}\{K_n=\{0,1\}\}
 \ge1-2\eta.
\]
With the discrete metric, the expected diameter is precisely the probability of reporting \(\{0,1\}\). For every fixed randomized rule on the finite count space this probability is a polynomial, hence continuous, in \(q\). Parameters \(u=n+t\), \(t\ne0\), have a unique truth and converge to the boundary law as \(t\to0\). For separation radius zero they belong to the risk domain. Thus
\[
 \sup_{u:\,\mathcal V_n(u)\text{ is a singleton}}
 E_{p_n^B(u)}\ell\{K_n(N_n)\}
 \ge1-2\eta.
\]
Taking the infimum over globally honest \(K_n\) proves the asserted lower bound. The inserted boundary escapes to \(u_n=n\), so no fixed-compact hypothesis detects it.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This explicit witness tests and falsifies the attempted passage from compact comparison to a global minimax upper bound. \textit{Gap.} Compact LAN hypotheses are silent about label boundaries deliberately placed at escaping local parameters. \textit{Consumer.} The corrected compact-exhaustion theorem stops at a global lower bound and proves two-sided convergence only on expanding certified windows.

\begin{lemma}[lem:moving-offset-carter-geometry]\label{lem:moving-offset-carter-geometry}

\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}\{a_j'\Sigma_0a_j\}^{1/2}}.

+\operatorname{Err}_{\Sigma,n}(C)

\qquad H_C=H(C,C_c).
\]
The upper bound \(\overline\chi_n(C)\) is arbitrary and need not be effective.  If \(\overline\chi_n(C)\to0\), then \(\Delta_n(C)\to0\).  This conclusion constructs no Gaussian rule and invokes no effective-compatible-density premise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Technique: exact affine-slack coupling, compact-offset finite-arrangement stability, and bounded Gaussian boundary density.}

\[
\zeta_{n,j}(u)-\{\widehat c_{n,j}+a_j'u\}
=(c_{n,j}-\widehat c_{n,j})+e_{n,j}(u).
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_j\sup_{u\in C}
\left|\zeta_{n,j}(u)-\{\widehat c_{n,j}+a_j'u\}\right|
\le\delta_n(C)+\xi_n\le\chi_n(C).
\tag{16}
\]
The hypothesis that \(c_n\) and \(\widehat c_n\) lie in the same fixed compact \(C_c\subset\mathcal K_0\) is now used: it permits invocation of \(\text{lem:finite-completed-arrangement-stability}\) with its single finite constant
\(H_C=H(C,C_c)\).  Together with the centered score displacement \(d_n(C)\), (16) matches every completed semantic label mean and every separated-risk mean to the \(\widehat c_n\)-offset arrangement at Mahalanobis displacement at most
\[
H_C\chi_n(C).
\tag{17}
\]
The lemma's directed Hausdorff part handles rival semantic unions, and its equality convention retains all incident labels.

For each active \(j\), positivity of \(\Sigma_0\) and \(a_j\ne0\) imply \(a_j'\Sigma_0a_j>0\).  The same density calculation as in (12) gives, uniformly over the offset and the Gaussian mean,
\[
\Pr_h\{|c_j+a_j'W_0|\le s\}
\le\min\left\{1,
\frac{2s}{\sqrt{2\pi}\{a_j'\Sigma_0a_j\}^{1/2}}\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(\mathfrak b_0(s)\le A_0s\).  In the moving-boundary comparison take the collar tolerance to be
\(\tau_n=\overline\chi_n(C)\).  Because
\(\chi_n(C)\le\overline\chi_n(C)\), the strict failure indicator
\(\mathbf1\{\chi_n(C)>\tau_n\}\) equals zero, while
\[
\mathfrak b_0\{\tau_n+\chi_n(C)\}
\le\mathfrak b_0\{2\overline\chi_n(C)\}
\le2A_0\overline\chi_n(C).
\tag{19}
\]

\(C_{2/\kappa}|\mathcal J|\log|\mathcal J|/\sqrt n\) on \(C\).  The affine score-normal replacement contributes \(\operatorname{Err}_{\Sigma,n}(C)\), and \(\text{lem:bounded-semantic-transfer-by-tv}\) applies both to coverage indicators and to \(\ell_0\in[0,1]\).  Adding these nonnegative charges to (17)--(19) proves
\[

=C_{2/\kappa}\frac{|\mathcal J|\log|\mathcal J|}{\sqrt n}
+\operatorname{Err}_{\Sigma,n}(C)

\tag{20}
\]
If \(\overline\chi_n(C)\to0\), compactness of the fixed offset set keeps \(H_C\) fixed, \(\operatorname{Err}_{\Sigma,n}(C)\to0\) by \(\text{lem:compact-moving-remainder-covariance}\), and the Carter term vanishes.  Hence \(\Delta_n(C)\to0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The Gaussian experiment is the valid local decision target on each fixed lawful direction window. Carter's comparison gives unconditional qualitative fixed-compact and moving-offset transfer once the respective compact hypotheses hold; finite replayable Gaussian-rule construction remains conditional on effective compatible density or another sufficient compatibility package, and numerical replayability also requires separately supplied effective error envelopes. Compact exhaustion gives a global minimax lower limit and two-sided convergence only along diagonally selected expanding lawful windows; it gives neither a computable window selection nor a global upper limit. The common-ray library is an algebraically coherent recession representation, not a compatibility-density theorem.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Effective compatible density is unresolved under the standing assumptions. One unproved obligation is to show that every canonical chart section remains in each required \(D_v\), \(H_r\), and declared chart-neighborhood incidence region. The remaining obligations are safety of every positive-weight candidate; exact overlap traces or a valid Gaussian-weighted face-cone discrepancy bound on simultaneous supports; translated-uniform guard and normal-base tails; and computed refinement counts \(N_j\) with schedules \(L_j,B_j,\delta_j\) for which every accumulated error vanishes and \(N_j/L_j\) tends to zero. No refinement-independent ledger constant is assumed. The four-label milliscale certificate and unconditional global CertGluePFL termination therefore remain unresolved.

\section{Honest scope}

The causal target is the same-law joint identified set and is only contained in the product of coordinatewise sharp endpoint intervals; no Cartesian joint sharpness is claimed. Carter-backed sampled-data conclusions are fixed-compact or expanding-window statements. Compact exhaustion proves a global lower limit and two-sided windowed convergence, but neither a global limsup nor a globally honest unbounded sampled-data primal. The paper proves common-ray operator identities, Gaussian smoothing, one-chart averaging, and a telescoping inequality applicable once a finite family with certified edge discrepancies has already been supplied. It does not prove existence of finite families whose total compatibility ledger vanishes. Effective compatible density remains an open-ended question, and CertGluePFL termination and replayable global Gaussian certification remain conditional.
