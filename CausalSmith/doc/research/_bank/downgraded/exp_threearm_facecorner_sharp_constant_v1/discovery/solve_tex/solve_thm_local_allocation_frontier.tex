Let \(\mathfrak S_n\) act simultaneously on unit labels in schedules, assignments, and observed data.  Fix any schedule-independent pair \((\mathcal D,\widehat\tau)\). Conditional averaging of the reported action given the observed data cannot increase squared loss, by
\[

  =\{\mathbb E(D\mid O)-\tau\}^2+\operatorname{Var}(D\mid O).
\]
Thus a deterministic invariant rule is sufficient.

An invariant assignment law is constant on each orbit of \(\mathcal A^n\). Similarly, two complete schedules are in the same orbit exactly when their eight response-type counts \(m\) agree; hence maximization over schedules becomes maximization over \(\mathcal M_n\).

\[

\]

\[
 P_m(x\mid r)
 =\frac{\prod_a r_a!}{n!}
   \sum_{h\in\mathcal H(m,r,x)}
   \prod_{t\in\mathcal T}\frac{m_t!}{\prod_a h_{t,a}!}.

For the lower construction, the label-preserving expansion gives, for the half-effect prior,
\[
B_{A,n}(\mathcal D)\geq
\frac1n-n^{-4/3}
\left[I(f)+\frac12m_1(f)+2m_1(f)
\{\bar q_1(\mathcal D)-1/2\}\right]
+O_f(n^{-5/3}).
\tag{17}
\]
The broad-prior lemma gives
\[
B_{B,n}(\mathcal D)\geq
\frac{1+k\|\bar q(\mathcal D)-q^\star\|^2}{n}
-Kn^{-9/8}.
\tag{18}
\]
Bayes risk is concave in the prior: the infimum of a mixture of risk functionals is at least the same mixture of their infima.  With \(\gamma_n=n^{-1/4}\), (17)--(18) therefore imply, writing \(d=\bar q(\mathcal D)-q^\star\),
\[
B_{\Pi_n}(\mathcal D)\geq
\frac1n-\{I(f)+m_1(f)/2\}n^{-4/3}
+k n^{-5/4}\|d\|^2
-2m_1(f)n^{-4/3}d_1
-O_f(n^{-11/8})-O_f(n^{-5/3}).
\tag{19}
\]
Completing the square gives
\[
k n^{-5/4}\|d\|^2-2m_1(f)n^{-4/3}d_1
\geq-\frac{m_1(f)^2}{k}n^{-17/12}
=o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{20}
\]
Choose smooth compactly supported even densities obtained by slowly cutting off and smoothing \(\phi_3^2\).  The Airy variational identity gives
\[
I(f)+\frac12m_1(f)\longrightarrow C_3.
\]
A diagonal cutoff sequence makes its approximation error and all remainders in (19) \(o(n^{-4/3})\), uniformly in \(\mathcal D\).  Equations (19)--(20) prove the stated all-design Bayes lower bound for the explicit complete-schedule mixture.

Finally, the grid-randomization lemma with \(M=n\) gives
\[
0\leq\Lambda_{n,n}^{\mathrm{all}}-G_n^{\mathrm{all}}
\leq\frac1{4n^2},\qquad
0\leq\Lambda_{n,n}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)-G_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)
\leq\frac1{4n^2}.
\]
Since \(n^{-2}=o(n^{-4/3})\), these are the claimed rational finite-game brackets.

The orbit proposition is exact for every integer \(n\) and retains all zero-count and zero-likelihood cells.  The upper half of the matching-constructions proposition is uniform over every complete schedule, not merely over compact local strata.  In its proof, the only schedule quantities are \(x=n^{1/3}\tau(z)\) and \(A_n=n^{-2/3}\sum_i\mu(t_i)^2\); the inequality \(A_n\geq|x|/2\) covers every mixture on the \(000\)--\(111\) edge, its endpoints, all six outgoing directions, and macroscopic departures.  It gives a primal recovery bound with error \(o(n^{-4/3})\) along the full integer sequence.

The lower half uses actual priors on complete labelled schedules.  Its half-effect component has \(\eta=0\): the positive pair \(101,110\), and respectively the negative pair \(001,010\), have cancelling \(\nu\)-coordinates.  Smooth cutoff priors converge to \(\phi_3^2\) in the target coordinate.  The broad component has mass \(n^{-1/4}\), hence disappears from the local transverse limit, while its proved quadratic coercivity keeps the Bayes inequality uniform over every schedule-independent assignment law.  Thus it supplies dual recovery concentrated, as a probability measure, on \(\eta=0\), with the same \(o(n^{-4/3})\) risk error.

The grid lemma transfers both recoveries to finite rational primal and dual games within \(1/(4n^2)=o(n^{-4/3})\).  Because it defines actions on every \((r,x)\), it does not choose arbitrary versions by discarding null cells.  The Airy fiber lemma proves the trace conditions from the one-dimensional weak Euler equation.  Finally, neither the finite risk expansion nor the dual information calculation differentiates in \(\eta\); the transverse coordinate indexes fibers and its dual is a probability measure.  Hence no \(\eta\)-Laplacian or transverse boundary condition is introduced.  These facts prove the stated value-level bridge and also delimit the stronger cellwise assertion that is not needed here.

The tangent lemma and the definition of the fiber energy give
\[
V(u,\eta)=\max\{|u|/2,|\eta|/4\}\geq |u|/2.
\]
The Airy fiber lemma therefore proves, including attainment at \(\eta=0\),
\[
\inf_{\eta}\inf_{\|\psi\|_2=1}\mathcal E_\eta(\psi)
=\inf_{\|\psi\|_2=1}
\int_{\mathbb R}\left\{4|\psi'(u)|^2+\frac{|u|}{2}|\psi(u)|^2\right\}du
=C_3.
\]
It also proves that the positive normalized minimizer is
\(\phi_3(u)\propto\operatorname{Ai}(|u|/2-C_3)\), that
\(\phi_3'(0)=0\), and that the eigenvalue condition is
\(\operatorname{Ai}'(-C_3)=0\).  Since \(a_1'\) is the largest negative derivative zero, the ground-state value is \(C_3=-a_1'\).

The matching-constructions proposition supplies
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)
\leq\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{21}
\]
Because unrestricted design includes \(\mathcal D_{q^\star}\),
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}\leq\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star).
\tag{22}
\]
For every design \(\mathcal D\), the explicit prior \(\Pi_n\) gives
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau}
\mathbb E_{z\sim\Pi_n}R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau;z)
\geq\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\tag{23}
\]
uniformly in \(\mathcal D\).  A maximum over schedules dominates an average under \(\Pi_n\); taking the infimum in (23) therefore yields the same lower bound for \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}\).  Combining (21)--(23) gives
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}

\]
The fixed-design minimax value is at least the unrestricted value, so (21) and the last display also give

=\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).

Their difference is consequently \(o(n^{-4/3})\).  The resolved singular-bridge theorem explains why these full-schedule primal and labelled-prior dual inequalities constitute a uniform value transfer through \(\eta=0\), without asserting a stronger cellwise equivalence.

%%% FIELD proof-local-frontier
Define
\[
F(q)=\frac14\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2}{q_a}.
\]
For iid assignment, direct calculation from the definition of \(T_{n,q}\) gives, for every fixed schedule,
\[
\mathbb E T_{n,q}=\tau(z),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(T_{n,q})
=\frac{F(q)}n-\frac1{n^2}\sum_i\mu(t_i)^2.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(q\) remains near \(q^\star\), the centered summands are uniformly bounded, so their averaged third absolute moment is \(O(n^{-3/2})\).  Repeating the Taylor calculation in the matching-constructions proof, now with (24), gives
\[
R_n(\mathcal D_q,\delta_{n,q};z)
=\frac{F(q)}n+n^{-4/3}
\left[-A_n-2F(q)s_3'(x)+s_3(x)^2
+2n^{-1/3}A_ns_3'(x)\right]
+O(n^{-3/2}),
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(z\), where \(A_n=n^{-2/3}\sum_i\mu(t_i)^2\) and
\(x=n^{1/3}\tau(z)\).

On the simplex tangent plane, \(q^\star\) is stationary for \(F\).  Hence, if \(q-q^\star=O(n^{-1/6})\),
\[
F(q)-1=O(n^{-1/3}).
\tag{26}
\]
Using \(s_3^2-2s_3'=|x|/2-C_3\), \(A_n\geq|x|/2\), and the decay bound on \(s_3'\), equations (25)--(26) imply
\[
\max_zR_n(\mathcal D_q,\delta_{n,q};z)
\leq\frac{F(q)}n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\tag{27}
\]
uniformly on compact sets of \(n^{1/6}(q-q^\star)\).  Projection again cannot increase loss.  The \(q\)-dependent complete-schedule prior lemma gives the reverse inequality, so
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)
=\frac{F(q_n)}n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
\tag{28}
\]
under the stated compactness condition.

Write \(d=q-q^\star\), with \(d_1+d_2+d_3=0\).  Taylor expansion, whose remainder is uniform near the interior point \(q^\star\), gives
\[
F(q^\star+d)
=1+2d_1^2+4d_2^2+4d_3^2+O(\|d\|^3).
\tag{29}
\]
Taking \(d=n^{-1/6}\zeta_n\), equations (28)--(29) yield
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)
=\frac1n+n^{-4/3}
\{\mathsf P(\zeta_n)-C_3\}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
uniformly for \(\zeta_n\) in compact subsets of the tangent plane.

By the sharp theorem, retaining the unrestricted correction means that the last coefficient equals \(-C_3+o(1)\).  If \(\zeta_n\to0\), the displayed expansion proves this.  Conversely, if \(\zeta_n\) is bounded but does not converge to zero, it has a subsequence converging to a nonzero \(\zeta\); positive definiteness of \(\mathsf P\) and the uniform expansion give the different coefficient \(\mathsf P(\zeta)-C_3\).  If \(\zeta_n\) is unbounded, select a subsequence along which its norm tends to infinity.  On that subsequence, the second bound in the \(q\)-dependent prior lemma and strong convexity of \(F\) near \(q^\star\) give constants \(k,K>0\) such that
\[
n^{4/3}\left\{\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)-\frac1n\right\}
\geq k n^{1/3}\|q_n-q^\star\|^2-K
=k\|\zeta_n\|^2-K\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus an unbounded array cannot retain the sharp correction.  When the norm of the full array tends to infinity, the same inequality holds without taking a subsequence and proves the theorem's final divergence assertion.  These alternatives establish the necessary-and-sufficient allocation frontier.
