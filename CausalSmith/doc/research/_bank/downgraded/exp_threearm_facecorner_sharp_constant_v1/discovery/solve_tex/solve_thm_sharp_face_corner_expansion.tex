%%% FIELD proof-target
By the tangent-half-wedge lemma and the definition of the fiber form,
\[
V(u,\eta)=\max\{|u|/2,|\eta|/4\}\geq |u|/2.
\]
The Airy fiber calculus therefore gives
\[
\inf_{\eta\in\mathbb R}\inf_{\substack{\psi\in H^1(\mathbb R)\\
\int\psi^2=1}}\mathcal E_\eta(\psi)
=\inf_{\substack{\psi\in H^1(\mathbb R)\\\int\psi^2=1}}
\int_{\mathbb R}\left\{4|\psi'(u)|^2+\frac{|u|}{2}|\psi(u)|^2\right\}\,du
=C_3,
\]
with equality at the fiber \(\eta=0\).  The same lemma identifies the positive normalized minimizer as
\(\phi_3(u)\propto\operatorname{Ai}(|u|/2-C_3)\), gives the Neumann trace
\(\phi_3'(0)=0\), and hence gives \(\operatorname{Ai}'(-C_3)=0\).  Since
\(a_1'\) is the largest negative zero of \(\operatorname{Ai}'\), positivity of the ground state selects the lowest eigenvalue and yields \(C_3=-a_1'\).

The matching finite-\(n\) constructions give
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)
\leq \frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{1}
\]
The feasible class in the unrestricted game contains the particular schedule-independent iid law \(\mathcal D_{q^\star}\).  Therefore
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}\leq \rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star).
\tag{2}
\]
For every schedule-independent assignment law \(\mathcal D\), the same proposition supplies the complete-schedule prior \(\Pi_n\) and the uniform inequality
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau}
\mathbb E_{z\sim\Pi_n}R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau;z)
\geq \frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{3}
\]
For each \((\mathcal D,\widehat\tau)\), a maximum over the finite support
\(\mathcal T^n\) dominates its average under \(\Pi_n\).  Taking first the infimum over \(\widehat\tau\) and then the infimum over \(\mathcal D\) in (3) thus yields
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}
\geq \frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (4) proves
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}
=\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
The fixed-design minimax value is bounded below by the unrestricted value, so (1) and the last display also imply
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)
=\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Subtracting the two expansions gives
\(\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)-\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}=o(n^{-4/3})\).
Finally, the uniform singular-value bridge supplies the stated uniform value-level passage through the coalesced fiber \(\eta=0\), retaining zero-likelihood cells and dormant primal constraints; no cellwise Le Cam equivalence is used.

%%% FIELD statement-published-reductions
In the balanced two-arm binary finite-population problem, the minimax risk of the reduced shifted-binomial experiment equals the minimax risk obtained by jointly optimizing over all schedule-independent assignment laws and all estimators; the reduced game has a unique minimax rule and a least-favorable-prior Bayes representation.

%%% FIELD statement-published-endpoint
In the published two-arm finite-population problem, for every finite \(L<U\), the unrestricted minimax squared-error risk over schedules in \([L,U]^{2n}\) is exactly \((U-L)^2\) times the binary minimax risk, and a conditional Bernoulliization of the binary minimax rule attains that value.

%%% FIELD construction-unrestricted
The binary endpoint value is
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}=\inf_{\mathcal D\in\Delta(\mathcal A^n),\,\widehat\tau:\mathcal O_n\to[-1,1]}
\max_{z\in\mathcal T^n}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\{\widehat\tau-\tau(z)\}^2.
\]
Here \(\mathcal T=\{0,1\}^3\), so this definition is literally the binary endpoint game.  Its relation to a continuous bounded schedule class is a separate minimax-value identity, not a literal eight-type orbit representation of every bounded schedule.

%%% FIELD construction-iid
For an interior allocation vector \(q\), the binary endpoint iid value is
\[
\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)=\inf_{\widehat\tau:\mathcal O_n\to[-1,1]}
\max_{z\in\mathcal T^n}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_q}\{\widehat\tau-\tau(z)\}^2.
\]
This is the iid restriction of the binary endpoint game.  The continuous bounded-schedule value is defined separately and is linked to this value by endpoint transfer rather than by treating continuous schedules as eight response types.

%%% FIELD construction-bounded-games
Fix finite endpoints \(L<U\) and write \(W=U-L\).  A bounded complete schedule is
\(z^{[L,U]}=(Y_i^{[L,U]}(a))_{i,a}\in[L,U]^{3n}\), it produces
\(Y_i^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}}=Y_i^{[L,U]}(A_i)\), and its causal contrast is
\[
\tau_{[L,U]}(z^{[L,U]})
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_aY_i^{[L,U]}(a).
\]
Because \(\sum_ac_a=0\), \(\sum_{a:c_a>0}c_a=1\), and
\(\sum_{a:c_a<0}|c_a|=1\), this target lies in \([-W,W]\).  Let
\(\mathcal O_{n,[L,U]}=(\mathcal A\times[L,U])^n\), let
\(\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}:\mathcal O_{n,[L,U]}\to[-W,W]\), and define
\[
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]})
=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\left[
\left\{\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}-\tau_{[L,U]}(z^{[L,U]})\right\}^2\right].
\]
The bounded unrestricted and iid minimax values are
\[
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{all}}
=\inf_{\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}}
\sup_{z^{[L,U]}\in[L,U]^{3n}}
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]}),
\]
and
\[
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)
=\inf_{\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}}
\sup_{z^{[L,U]}\in[L,U]^{3n}}
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D_q,\widehat\tau_{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]}).
\]
The assignment law is selected independently of the fixed bounded schedule, exactly as in the binary game.

%%% FIELD construction-bounded-constructions
Fix finite \(L<U\), put \(W=U-L\), and let \(z^{[L,U]}\) be observed under \(\mathcal D_q\).  Conditional on the realized bounded data, generate independent auxiliary variables
\[
B_i\mid A,Y_i^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}}
\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\left(
\frac{Y_i^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}}-L}{W}\right),\qquad 1\leq i\leq n.
\]
The Rao--Blackwellized bounded Airy rule is
\[
\delta_{n,q}^{[L,U]}(A,Y^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}})
=W\,\mathbb E_B\left[
\delta_{n,q}\bigl((A_i,B_i)_{i=1}^n\bigr)
\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{} A,Y^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}}
\right].
\]
It takes values in \([-W,W]\).  The bounded endpoint prior
\(\Pi_n^{[L,U]}\) is the pushforward of \(\Pi_n\) under the coordinatewise map
\(t_{i,a}\mapsto L+Wt_{i,a}\).  Thus it is a prior on fixed complete bounded schedules supported on the endpoints \(\{L,U\}^{3n}\).

%%% FIELD statement-endpoint-transfer
Fix \(n\geq1\) and finite constants \(L<U\), and put \(W=U-L\).  For every schedule-independent assignment law \(\mathcal D\), without any positivity or independence restriction on its coordinates,
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}}
\sup_{z^{[L,U]}\in[L,U]^{3n}}
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]})
=W^2\inf_{\widehat\tau}
\max_{z\in\mathcal T^n}R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau;z).
\]
Consequently the exact finite-\(n\) equalities
\[
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{all}}=W^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}},
\qquad
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)=W^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)
\]
hold for every interior \(q\).  Together with the binary orbit reduction they imply
\[
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{all}}=W^2G_n^{\mathrm{all}},
\qquad
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)=W^2G_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q).
\]
The last two equalities identify minimax values only: the eight-type kernel \(P_m(x\mid r)\) is not asserted to be a literal orbit likelihood for nonendpoint bounded schedules.

%%% FIELD proof-endpoint-transfer
Fix \(n\), \(L<U\), and a schedule-independent law \(\mathcal D\), and set \(W=U-L>0\).  The equality will first be proved with \(\mathcal D\) held fixed.

For the lower bound, take any clipped bounded rule \(d:\mathcal O_{n,[L,U]}\to[-W,W]\).  For each binary schedule \(z=(t_i)_{i=1}^n\in\mathcal T^n\), define its endpoint image by
\[
Y_i^{[L,U]}(a)=L+Wt_{i,a}.
\]
Since \(c_1+c_2+c_3=0\),
\[
\tau_{[L,U]}(z^{[L,U]})
=\frac1n\sum_{i,a}c_a(L+Wt_{i,a})
=W\tau(z).
\tag{5}
\]
The normalized rule
\[
d^{\mathrm{bin}}((A_i,t_{i,A_i})_{i=1}^n)
=W^{-1}d((A_i,L+Wt_{i,A_i})_{i=1}^n)
\]
is measurable and lies in \([-1,1]\).  Equation (5) gives, for every endpoint schedule,
\[
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,d;z^{[L,U]})
=W^2R_n(\mathcal D,d^{\mathrm{bin}};z).
\]
The endpoint schedules are a subset of \([L,U]^{3n}\); hence, after taking the supremum and then the infimum over \(d\),
\[
\inf_d\sup_{z^{[L,U]}}R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,d;z^{[L,U]})
\geq W^2\inf_{\widehat\tau}\max_{z\in\mathcal T^n}
R_n(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau;z).
\tag{6}
\]

For the reverse inequality, fix a clipped binary rule \(e:\mathcal O_n\to[-1,1]\) and an arbitrary fixed bounded schedule \(z^{[L,U]}\).  Normalize its coordinates as
\[
p_{i,a}=\frac{Y_i^{[L,U]}(a)-L}{W}\in[0,1].
\]
On an auxiliary probability space, conditional on this fixed schedule, generate all
\(B_i(a)\) independently with \(B_i(a)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{i,a})\), independently of \(A\).  Define the deterministic Rao--Blackwellized rule
\[
d_e(A,Y^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}})
=W\,\mathbb E_B\!\left[
e((A_i,B_i(A_i))_{i=1}^n)\mid A,Y^{[L,U],\mathrm{obs}}
\right].
\tag{7}
\]
Although all potential \(B_i(a)\) were introduced for the proof, the conditional expectation in (7) depends on the bounded schedule only through the observed probabilities \(p_{i,A_i}\); thus (7) is a valid map on \(\mathcal O_{n,[L,U]}\).  Convexity of \([-1,1]\) shows that it is clipped to \([-W,W]\).

Let \(B=(B_i(a))_{i,a}\) denote the auxiliary complete binary schedule.  The zero-sum identity used in (5) and independence of the Bernoulli coordinates give
\[
\mathbb E_B\tau(B)
=\frac1n\sum_{i,a}c_ap_{i,a}
=W^{-1}\tau_{[L,U]}(z^{[L,U]}).
\tag{8}
\]
For every realized assignment vector, apply
\(\mathbb E(X^2)-\{\mathbb E(X)\}^2
=\mathbb E[\{X-\mathbb E(X)\}^2]\geq0\)
conditionally to
\(X=e(A,B^{\mathrm{obs}})-\tau(B)\).  Equations (7)--(8) then yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\{d_e-W\mathbb E_B\tau(B)\right\}^2
&=W^2\left[
\mathbb E_B\{e(A,B^{\mathrm{obs}})-\tau(B)\mid A,z^{[L,U]}\}
\right]^2\\
&\leq W^2\mathbb E_B\!\left[
\{e(A,B^{\mathrm{obs}})-\tau(B)\}^2\mid A,z^{[L,U]}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Taking expectation over \(A\), exchanging the two finite sums over the supports of \(A\) and \(B\), and using that the auxiliary complete schedule \(B\) is independent of the schedule-independent assignment law, gives
\[
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,d_e;z^{[L,U]})
\leq W^2\mathbb E_B R_n(\mathcal D,e;B)
\leq W^2\max_{z\in\mathcal T^n}R_n(\mathcal D,e;z).
\tag{10}
\]
The bound is uniform in the bounded schedule.  Taking its supremum and then the infimum over binary rules \(e\) proves the reverse of (6).  Therefore the fixed-\(\mathcal D\) equality is exact.

Taking the infimum over the same schedule-independent design class proves the unrestricted equality; specializing \(\mathcal D\) to \(\mathcal D_q\) proves the iid equality.  Finally, the already-established binary orbit identities
\(\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}=G_n^{\mathrm{all}}\) and
\(\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)=G_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q)\) give the two orbit-value formulas.  Because (7)--(10) average over auxiliary endpoint schedules rather than identify each continuous schedule with an eight-type count, they prove no literal continuous-schedule orbit reduction.

%%% FIELD statement-bounded-matching
Fix finite endpoints \(L<U\), independent of \(n\), and put \(W=U-L\).  At \(q=q^\star\), the Rao--Blackwellized estimator \(\delta_{n,q^\star}^{[L,U]}\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{z^{[L,U]}\in[L,U]^{3n}}
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D_{q^\star},\delta_{n,q^\star}^{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]})
\leq W^2\left\{\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\right\}.
\]
The rescaled endpoint prior \(\Pi_n^{[L,U]}\) satisfies, uniformly over every schedule-independent assignment law \(\mathcal D\),
\[
\inf_{\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}}
\mathbb E_{z^{[L,U]}\sim\Pi_n^{[L,U]}}
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,\widehat\tau_{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]})
\geq W^2\left\{\frac1n-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\right\}.
\]
Thus both bounded procedures are explicit finite-\(n\) maps or priors, and no exact finite-\(n\) saddle attainment is asserted.

%%% FIELD proof-bounded-matching
The binary matching-constructions proposition gives the stated upper bound for \(\delta_{n,q^\star}\).  Apply the design-preserving Rao--Blackwell construction (7) with \(e=\delta_{n,q^\star}\).  Inequality (10), followed by the binary uniform upper bound, gives
\[
\sup_{z^{[L,U]}}R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D_{q^\star},
\delta_{n,q^\star}^{[L,U]};z^{[L,U]})
\leq W^2\left\{n^{-1}-C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\right\}.
\]

For the lower construction, \(\Pi_n^{[L,U]}\) is supported on endpoint schedules.  Given any bounded rule \(d\), normalize it on endpoint data exactly as in the lower-bound part of the endpoint-transfer proof to obtain a clipped binary rule \(d^{\mathrm{bin}}\).  The pushforward definition and equation (5) then give the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E_{z^{[L,U]}\sim\Pi_n^{[L,U]}}
R_{n,[L,U]}(\mathcal D,d;z^{[L,U]})
=W^2\mathbb E_{z\sim\Pi_n}R_n(\mathcal D,d^{\mathrm{bin}};z).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(d\) and applying the binary prior bound from the matching-constructions proposition proves the lower display uniformly in \(\mathcal D\).  Since \(W\) is a fixed finite positive constant, multiplying an \(o(n^{-4/3})\) remainder by \(W^2\) preserves its order.

%%% FIELD statement-bounded-sharp
For fixed finite endpoints \(L<U\), with \(W=U-L\), the bounded three-arm minimax values obey

\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{all}}
=\frac{W^2}{n}-W^2C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\qquad
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)
=\frac{W^2}{n}-W^2C_3n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Consequently
\(\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)-\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{all}}=o(n^{-4/3})\).  After normalization by \(W^2\), the half-wedge spectral value remains exactly \(C_3=-a_1'\).

%%% FIELD proof-bounded-sharp
The exact endpoint-transfer theorem gives
\(\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{all}}=W^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{all}}\) and
\(\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)=W^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q^\star)\) for every \(n\).  Substitute the two expansions from the sharp face/corner theorem.  Because \(0<W<\infty\) is fixed in \(n\), \(W^2o(n^{-4/3})=o(n^{-4/3})\).  Subtracting the exact identities proves the gap assertion, and division by \(W^2>0\) shows that normalization does not alter the dimensionless Airy constant.

%%% FIELD statement-bounded-frontier
Let fixed finite endpoints \(L<U\) have width \(W=U-L\), and let \(q_n\to q^\star\).  Uniformly whenever \(\zeta_n=n^{1/6}(q_n-q^\star)\) ranges over a compact subset of the simplex tangent plane,
\[
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)
=\frac{W^2}{n}+W^2n^{-4/3}\{\mathsf P(\zeta_n)-C_3\}
+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
Thus the bounded iid array retains the unrestricted bounded sharp correction if and only if
\(n^{1/6}(q_n-q^\star)\to0\).  If \(q_n\to q^\star\) and
\(n^{1/6}\|q_n-q^\star\|\to\infty\), then
\[
n^{4/3}\left\{\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)-W^2/n\right\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The allocation threshold is unchanged and its risk penalty is multiplied by \(W^2\).

%%% FIELD proof-bounded-frontier
For every \(n\), endpoint transfer gives
\[
\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)=W^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n).
\tag{11}
\]
Multiply the uniform expansion in the local allocation-frontier theorem by the fixed constant \(W^2\).  Its uniform remainder remains \(o(n^{-4/3})\), proving the first display.  Since \(W^2>0\), equality (11) also preserves both the necessary-and-sufficient condition for matching the unrestricted correction and the sign of every allocation penalty.  Finally,
\[
n^{4/3}\left\{\rho_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)-W^2/n\right\}
=W^2n^{4/3}\left\{\rho_n^{\mathrm{iid}}(q_n)-1/n\right\},
\]
so the divergence assertion follows directly from the binary allocation-frontier theorem.

On the bounded restricted schedule class
\(Y_i^{[L,U]}(2)=Y_i^{[L,U]}(3)\) for every \(i\), pooling labels \(2\) and \(3\) under \(q^\star\) produces balanced two-arm assignment and the causal target becomes the active-arm mean minus the pooled-comparator mean.  The endpoint subclass is represented by the published shifted-binomial experiment, while the entire bounded pooled problem has minimax risk \(W^2\) times that binary value.  Its second-order deficit coefficient is therefore \(W^2(-4^{1/3}a_1')\).  This is a value transfer; \(P_m(x\mid r)\) is not a literal likelihood for a nonendpoint bounded schedule.

%%% FIELD proof-bounded-pooled
Write the common comparator outcome as \(Y_i^{[L,U]}(0)\).  Since
\(q_1^\star=1/2\) and \(q_2^\star+q_3^\star=1/2\), pooling the two comparator labels maps \(\mathcal D_{q^\star}\) to iid balanced two-arm assignment.  Conditional on the pooled label, the split between labels \(2\) and \(3\) is ancillary because their potential outcomes agree.  Moreover,
\[
\tau_{[L,U]}(z^{[L,U]})
=\frac1n\sum_i\left\{Y_i^{[L,U]}(1)-Y_i^{[L,U]}(0)\right\}.
\]
On endpoint schedules, normalization by \(W\) gives the balanced binary two-arm problem.  The cited Theorems 2.1--2.2 identify its minimax value with the shifted-binomial value, and the cited Theorem 2.3 gives the exact factor \(W^2\) over the full bounded interval.  The cited sharp two-arm result gives the binary coefficient \(-4^{1/3}a_1'\), so rescaling gives \(W^2(-4^{1/3}a_1')\).  The endpoint-transfer proof proceeds by averaging endpoint experiments and therefore does not identify a nonendpoint bounded schedule with an eight-type count.
