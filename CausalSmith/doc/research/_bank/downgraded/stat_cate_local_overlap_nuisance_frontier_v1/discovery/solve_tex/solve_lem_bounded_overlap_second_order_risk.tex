%%% FIELD bounded-overlap-second-order-risk-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), and write \(e=e_P\), \(\mu_0=\mu_{0,P}\), and \(\tau=\tau_P\).  All expectations below are under \(P\).  By \(\mathrm{def:exact\mbox{-}model}\), \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\mbox{-}design}\), \(\mathrm{ass:local\mbox{-}propensity\mbox{-}shape}\), \(\mathrm{ass:multiplier\mbox{-}envelope}\), \(\mathrm{ass:multiplier\mbox{-}holder}\), \(\mathrm{ass:control\mbox{-}holder}\), \(\mathrm{ass:contrast\mbox{-}holder}\), \(\mathrm{ass:control\mbox{-}interiority}\), \(\mathrm{ass:treated\mbox{-}interiority}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\mbox{-}response}\), on \(D=[0,1]^d\) we have
\[
 P_X=\operatorname{Unif}(D),\qquad
 \frac14\leq e(x)=\frac{g_P(x)}4\leq\frac12,
 \qquad e\in\mathcal H_d^\alpha(L/4),
 \qquad \mu_0\in\mathcal H_d^\beta(L),
 \qquad \tau\in\mathcal H_d^\gamma(L),
 \tag{1}
\]
with \(\mu_{1,P}=\mu_0+\tau\), \(|\tau|\leq3/4\), and the stated conditional Bernoulli laws.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) makes \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) independent.

For \(s>0\), set \(m_s=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and choose
\[
 p=m_\gamma+\max\{m_\alpha,m_\beta\}.
\]
For a positive integer \(M\), partition \(D\) into the \(M^d\) half-open cubes of side \(M^{-1}\), with the closed upper-face convention on the boundary.  Let \(\mathcal B_M\) be the functions whose restriction to each cell is a polynomial of total degree at most \(p\).  This is a finite-dimensional space with
\[
 K_M=\dim(\mathcal B_M)=a_pM^d,
 \qquad a_p=\binom{p+d}{d}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Scaled tensor Legendre polynomials on each cell, restricted to multi-indices of total degree at most \(p\) and extended by zero off that cell, give an explicit Lebesgue-orthonormal basis \(b_M:D\to\mathbb R^{K_M}\).  Hence its exactly known Gram matrix is
\[
 \Omega_M=\int_D b_M(z)b_M(z)^{\mathsf T}\,dz=I_{K_M}.
 \tag{3}
\]

For the deterministic tuning proxy \(k\geq1\), put \(M=M_0+\lfloor k^{1/d}\rfloor\), where the fixed integer \(M_0\) is selected below.  Abbreviate \(b_k=b_M\), and let \(\Pi_k\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal B_M\) in \(L_2(D,dz)\).  Because \(k\geq1\) and \(M_0\) is fixed,
\[
 K_M\leq Ck,
 \qquad M^{-1}\leq Ck^{-1/d}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Repeated one-dimensional Taylor expansion along coordinate segments, with the derivative bounds and the order-\((s-m_s)\) H\"older modulus in the definition of \(\mathcal H_d^s(L)\), gives a cellwise remainder bounded by \(C(h/M)^s\) on every physical cell of side \(h/M\).  The same conclusion holds for \(s\leq1\), with the cellwise constant approximant.  Orthogonal projection has no larger \(L_2\) error than this explicit piecewise polynomial.  Multiplication by a fixed polynomial of degree at most \(m_\gamma\) raises total degree by at most \(m_\gamma\), so the choice of \(p\) keeps every required approximant in \(\mathcal B_M\).  Thus, for every component \(v_j\) of the fixed basis introduced below,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left\|(I-\Pi_k)\{v_j(e(h\,\cdot)-3/8)\}\right\|_2&\leq C\ell^\alpha,\\
 \left\|(I-\Pi_k)(\mu_0(h\,\cdot)-1/2)\right\|_2&\leq C\ell^\beta,\\
 \left\|(I-\Pi_k)\{e(h\,\cdot)v_j\}\right\|_2&\leq C\ell^\alpha,
 \end{split}
 \qquad \ell=hk^{-1/d}.
 \tag{5}
\]
All constants in (5) are uniform over the model class by (1).

Let \(v:D\to\mathbb R^q\) be a fixed Lebesgue-orthonormal basis of polynomials of total degree at most \(m_\gamma\), where \(q=\binom{m_\gamma+d}{d}\).  Define
\[
 Q=\int_Dv(z)v(z)^{\mathsf T}e(hz)\{1-e(hz)\}\,dz,
 \qquad
 R=\int_Dv(z)e(hz)\{1-e(hz)\}\tau(hz)\,dz,
 \tag{6}
\]
put \(\theta=Q^{-1}R\), and set \(\tau_h(hz)=v(z)^{\mathsf T}\theta\).  From (1) and orthonormality,
\[
 \lambda_0 I_q\preceq Q\preceq\frac14I_q,
 \qquad \lambda_0=\frac3{16}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(Q\) is invertible and \(\theta\) is uniformly bounded.

Let \(T_\tau\) be the order-\(m_\gamma\) Taylor polynomial of \(\tau\) at the corner.  The Taylor remainder argument used above yields
\[
 \sup_{z\in D}|\tau(hz)-T_\tau(hz)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
 \tag{8}
\]
Write \(T_\tau(hz)=v(z)^{\mathsf T}a_\tau\).  Subtracting \(Qa_\tau\) from \(Q\theta=R\) gives
\[
 \theta-a_\tau
 =Q^{-1}\int_Dv(z)e(hz)\{1-e(hz)\}
 \{\tau(hz)-T_\tau(hz)\}\,dz.
 \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9), boundedness of the fixed basis \(v\) on compact \(D\), and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \|\theta-a_\tau\|\leq Ch^\gamma,
 \qquad
 \sup_{z\in D}|\tau_h(hz)-T_\tau(hz)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_{z\in D}|\tau_h(hz)-\tau(hz)|\leq Ch^\gamma,
 \qquad
 |\tau_h(0)-\tau(0)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
 \tag{11}
\]

We now define the estimator.  Use the fixed bounded pilots
\[
 \bar e(x)=3/8,
 \qquad \bar\mu(x)=1/2.
 \tag{12}
\]
For \(x\in D\), put
\[
 w_h(x)=h^{-d}\mathbf1\{x\in hD\},\qquad
 v_h(x)=v(x/h)\mathbf1\{x\in hD\},\qquad
 b_{h,k}(x)=b_k(x/h)\mathbf1\{x\in hD\}.
\]
For \(o=(x,a,y)\), define
\[
 \phi_{a1}(o)=a\{a-\bar e(x)\},
 \qquad
 \phi_{y1}(o)=\{y-\bar\mu(x)\}\{a-\bar e(x)\},
 \tag{13}
\]
and, for \(o_r=(x_r,a_r,y_r)\), define
\[
 \begin{split}
 \phi_{a2}(o_1,o_2)
 &=-\{a_1-\bar e(x_1)\}
 b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)a_2,\\
 \phi_{y2}(o_1,o_2)
 &=-\{a_1-\bar e(x_1)\}
 b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)
 \{y_2-\bar\mu(x_2)\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{14}
\]
For \(\mathbb P_nf=n^{-1}\sum_i f(O_i)\) and \(\mathbb U_nH=\{n(n-1)\}^{-1}\sum_{i\ne j}H(O_i,O_j)\), set
\[
 \begin{split}
 \widetilde Q={}&\mathbb P_n\!\left[
 w_h(X)v_h(X)\phi_{a1}(O)v_h(X)^{\mathsf T}\right]\\
 &+\mathbb U_n\!\left[
 w_h(X_1)w_h(X_2)v_h(X_1)\phi_{a2}(O_1,O_2)v_h(X_2)^{\mathsf T}\right],\\
 \widetilde R={}&\mathbb P_n\!\left[
 w_h(X)v_h(X)\phi_{y1}(O)\right]\\
 &+\mathbb U_n\!\left[
 w_h(X_1)w_h(X_2)v_h(X_1)\phi_{y2}(O_1,O_2)\right].
 \end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]
The two fixed-basis factors in the matrix correction are required for the matrix cancellation below.  Since \(\widetilde Q\) need not be symmetric, choose the following deterministic Borel singular-value decomposition \(\widetilde Q=U\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_q)V^{\mathsf T}\).  Diagonalize \(\widetilde Q^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q\), and within each eigenspace project the coordinate vectors in lexicographic order, retain the first linearly independent projections, and apply Gram--Schmidt with positive first nonzero coordinate; this fixes \(V\).  If \(V_j\) is its \(j\)-th column and \(s_j>0\), set \(U_j=\widetilde QV_j/s_j\).  Complete the zero-singular-value columns of \(U\) by applying the same lexicographic projection and sign rule to the orthogonal complement of the positive-singular-value columns.  This finite rule resolves signs, tied subspaces, and zero singular values, and is Borel in the entries of \(\widetilde Q\).  Define
\[
 \widetilde Q_{\mathrm{fl}}
 =U\operatorname{diag}(s_1\vee\lambda_0/2,\ldots,s_q\vee\lambda_0/2)V^{\mathsf T}
 \tag{16}
\]
and
\[
 \widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)
 =\operatorname{clip}_{[-3/4,3/4]}
 \left\{v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q_{\mathrm{fl}}^{-1}\widetilde R\right\}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) and (12)--(17) involve only fixed polynomial evaluations, exact Lebesgue integrals, finite sample averages, an ordered finite \(U\)-statistic, and finite matrix operations.  Flooring makes the inverse exist and clipping bounds the output on every dataset, including a dataset with no point in \(hD\).  Hence (17) is a total Borel map of \(O_{1:n}\).

It remains to bound its risk.  Write \(\delta_e=e-\bar e\), \(\delta_\mu=\mu_0-\bar\mu\), and use a superscript \(h\) for stretching to \(D\), for example \(\delta_e^h(z)=\delta_e(hz)\).  Put \(\widetilde S=\widetilde R-\widetilde Q\theta\).  The conditional Bernoulli laws in \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\mbox{-}response}\) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E[\phi_{y1}(O)\mid X=x]
 &=e(x)\{1-\bar e(x)\}\tau(x)+\delta_e(x)\delta_\mu(x),\\
 \mathbb E[\phi_{a1}(O)\mid X=x]
 &=e(x)\{1-\bar e(x)\},\\
 \mathbb E[\phi_{y2}(O_1,O_2)\mid X_1=x_1,X_2=x_2]
 &=-\delta_e(x_1)b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)
 \{\delta_\mu(x_2)+e(x_2)\tau(x_2)\},\\
 \mathbb E[\phi_{a2}(O_1,O_2)\mid X_1=x_1,X_2=x_2]
 &=-\delta_e(x_1)b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)e(x_2).
 \end{split}
 \tag{18}
\]
Indeed, the first identity follows by conditioning separately on \(A=1\) and \(A=0\); the remaining identities follow directly from \(\mathbb E[A\mid X]=e(X)\) and the conditional outcome means.

Put
\[
 F=\delta_\mu^h+e^h\{\tau^h-\tau_h^h\}.
\]
After the changes of variables \(x_r=hz_r\), the first-order part of the \(j\)-th coordinate of \(\mathbb E\widetilde S\) is
\[
 \int_D v_j\delta_e^h\delta_\mu^h\,dz
 +\int_D v_j e^h(1-\bar e)\{\tau^h-\tau_h^h\}\,dz,
\]
whereas (3), (14), and (18) show that its second-order part is
\[
 -\int_Dv_j\delta_e^h\Pi_kF\,dz.
\]
Since \(\delta_e^h=e^h-\bar e\), adding and subtracting \(\int_Dv_j\delta_e^he^h(\tau^h-\tau_h^h)\,dz\) gives the complete score decomposition
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\widetilde S_j
 ={}&\int_Dv_j(z)\delta_e^h(z)(I-\Pi_k)
 \left[\delta_\mu^h+e^h\{\tau^h-\tau_h^h\}\right](z)\,dz\\
 &+\int_Dv_j(z)e^h(z)\{1-e^h(z)\}
 \{\tau^h(z)-\tau_h^h(z)\}\,dz.
 \end{split}
 \tag{19}
\]
The second line of (19) is retained in the bound.  By (1), (11), and boundedness of the fixed \(v_j\), its absolute value is at most \(Ch^\gamma\).  For the first line, \((I-\Pi_k)F\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal B_M\), whence
\[
 \int_Dv_j\delta_e^h(I-\Pi_k)F\,dz
 =\int_D(I-\Pi_k)(v_j\delta_e^h)(I-\Pi_k)F\,dz.
\]
Furthermore, (5), (11), \(e\leq1/2\), and the contraction \(\|(I-\Pi_k)f\|_2\leq\|f\|_2\) yield
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_k)F\|_2
 \leq C\ell^\beta+Ch^\gamma.
\]
Therefore Cauchy--Schwarz and (5) imply, coordinatewise and hence in the fixed dimension \(q\),
\[
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde S\|
 \leq C\{h^\gamma+\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+h^\gamma\ell^\alpha\}
 =C\{h^\gamma+\ell^S+h^\gamma\ell^\alpha\}.
 \tag{20}
\]

The same correction controls the mean matrix.  For coordinates \(j,r\), equations (6), (15), and (18) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \{\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q\}_{jr}
 &=\int_Dv_j\delta_e^h(I-\Pi_k)(e^hv_r)\,dz\\
 &=\int_D(I-\Pi_k)(v_j\delta_e^h)(I-\Pi_k)(e^hv_r)\,dz.
 \end{split}
 \tag{21}
\]
Applying the cell-width form of (5) to both factors yields
\[
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C(h/M)^{2\alpha}\leq CM_0^{-2\alpha},
 \qquad
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\ell^{2\alpha}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Choose the fixed integer \(M_0\) sufficiently large that
\[
 C(h/M)^{2\alpha}\leq CM_0^{-2\alpha}\leq\lambda_0/4
 \qquad\text{for all }h\in(0,1]\text{ and }k\geq1.
 \tag{23}
\]
For every unit vector \(a\), (7), (22), and (23) then imply
\[
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde Qa\|
 \geq\|Qa\|-\|(\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q)a\|
 \geq3\lambda_0/4.
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus \(s_{\min}(\mathbb E\widetilde Q)\geq3\lambda_0/4\).

We next derive the stochastic terms.  Because \(\mathbb P_nf=\mathbb U_n[f(O_1)]\), each coordinate of \(\widetilde S\), and each entry of \(\widetilde Q\), is an ordered second-order \(U\)-statistic; symmetrizing its kernel leaves the statistic unchanged.  For any square-integrable symmetric kernel \(\xi\), direct enumeration of pairs of index-pairs and independence from \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{\mathbb U_n\xi\}
 \leq\frac4n\mathbb E\left[\mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)\mid O_1\}^2\right]
 +\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)^2\}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Indeed, disjoint index-pairs have zero covariance; after division by \(n^2(n-1)^2\), at most \(4n(n-1)(n-2)\) ordered pair-pairs share one index and at most \(2n(n-1)\) share both, and Cauchy--Schwarz bounds their covariances by the displayed conditional and unconditional second moments.

The first-order part of each kernel has second moment at most
\[
 C\int_{hD}h^{-2d}\,dx=Ch^{-d},
 \tag{26}
\]
because \(A,Y\), the pilots, the fixed basis \(v\), and \(\tau_h\) are uniformly bounded by (1), (10), and compactness.  For the growing basis, the exact Gram identity gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\int_D\int_D
 \left\{b_k(z_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_k(z_2)\right\}^2\,dz_1\,dz_2\\
 &\qquad=\operatorname{tr}\left(
 \Omega_M^{-1}\Omega_M\Omega_M^{-1}\Omega_M\right)
 =K_M\leq Ck.
 \end{split}
 \tag{27}
\]
The two local weights and the substitutions \(x_r=hz_r\) therefore imply
\[
 \mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)^2\}\leq C\{h^{-d}+kh^{-2d}\}.
 \tag{28}
\]
For \(\widetilde S\), the second-order kernel contains the bounded second-observation factor \(Y_2-\bar\mu(X_2)-A_2\tau_h(X_2)\); for \(\widetilde Q\), it contains \(A_2v_h(X_2)^{\mathsf T}\).  Integrating out one observation produces \(\Pi_k\) applied to \(\delta_\mu^h+e^h(\tau^h-\tau_h^h)\), to a fixed-polynomial multiple of \(e^h\), or to a fixed-polynomial multiple of \(\delta_e^h\).  Projection contraction, followed by the one remaining localization integral, gives for either one-index projection, after interchanging the arguments if necessary,
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)\mid O_1\}^2\right]\leq Ch^{-d}.
 \tag{29}
\]
Combining (25)--(29) and summing the fixed number of coordinates proves
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde S-\mathbb E\widetilde S\|^2
 &\leq C\left\{\frac1{nh^d}+\frac{k}{n^2h^{2d}}\right\},\\
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde Q-\mathbb E\widetilde Q\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 &\leq C\left\{\frac1{nh^d}+\frac{k}{n^2h^{2d}}\right\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{30}
\]

Set
\[
 u=\left\{\frac1{nh^d}+\frac{k}{n^2h^{2d}}\right\}^{1/2}.
 \tag{31}
\]
If \(u\) exceeds a sufficiently small fixed constant, clipping and \(|\tau(0)|\leq3/4\) make the claimed risk bound immediate after increasing \(C\).  Otherwise define
\[
 \mathcal G=\{\|\widetilde Q-\mathbb E\widetilde Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\lambda_0/4\}.
\]
Equations (24) and (30), Markov's inequality, and \(u\leq1\) give
\[
 \mathbb P(\mathcal G^c)\leq Cu^2\leq Cu.
 \tag{32}
\]
On \(\mathcal G\), for every unit vector \(a\),
\[
 \|\widetilde Qa\|
 \geq\|\mathbb E\widetilde Qa\|-\|(\widetilde Q-\mathbb E\widetilde Q)a\|
 >\lambda_0/2.
\]
Thus \(s_{\min}(\widetilde Q)>\lambda_0/2\), flooring is inactive, and \(\|\widetilde Q^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2/\lambda_0\).  Since \(v(0)^{\mathsf T}\theta=\tau_h(0)\),
\[
 \left|v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q^{-1}\widetilde R-\tau_h(0)\right|
 =\left|v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q^{-1}\widetilde S\right|
 \leq\frac{2\|v(0)\|}{\lambda_0}\|\widetilde S\|.
 \tag{33}
\]
Clipping onto \([-3/4,3/4]\) is a contraction toward \(\tau(0)\), which lies in that interval by the two interiority assumptions.  Hence (11), (20), (30), (32), (33), and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\left|\widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)-\tau(0)\right|
 &\leq Ch^\gamma+C\|\mathbb E\widetilde S\|
 +C\left\{\mathbb E\|\widetilde S-\mathbb E\widetilde S\|^2\right\}^{1/2}
 +\frac32\mathbb P(\mathcal G^c)\\
 &\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\ell^S+h^\gamma\ell^\alpha
 +(nh^d)^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}\right\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{34}
\]
In particular, the explicit residual on the second line of (19) has been carried through (20) and is absorbed by the \(h^\gamma\) term in (34).  Since \(0<\ell\leq1\), \(h^\gamma\ell^\alpha\leq h^\gamma\).  Finally, for nonnegative \(a,b\), \(a+b\leq\sqrt{2(a^2+b^2)}\); with \(a=(nh^d)^{-1/2}\) and \(b=\sqrt{k}/(nh^d)\), this gives the second displayed inequality in the lemma after changing \(C\).  Every constant used above depends only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), so taking the supremum over \(P\) completes the proof.
