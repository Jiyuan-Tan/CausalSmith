Suppose \(\kappa=0\), put \(S=\alpha+\beta\), and let \(h\in(0,1]\) and \(k\geq1\) be deterministic.  There is an explicitly defined total Borel estimator \(\widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)\), using only \(O_{1:n}\), the known uniform design law, fixed bounded pilots, a one-sided local-polynomial basis, an exact Lebesgue projection Gram matrix, ordered second-order \(U\)-statistic corrections to both its matrix and vector moments, singular-value flooring, and clipping, such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)-\tau_P(0)\right|
 \leq C\left\{
 h^\gamma+\ell^S+h^\gamma\ell^\alpha
 +(nh^d)^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}
 \right\},
 \qquad \ell=hk^{-1/d}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)-\tau_P(0)\right|
 \leq C\left\{
 h^\gamma+\ell^S+
 \sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^d}\left(1+\frac{k}{nh^d}\right)}
 \right\}.
\]
Here \(C<\infty\) depends only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), not on \((n,h,k)\), and the estimator is defined on every sample, including samples having no observations in the localization cube.
%%% FIELD lemma-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\).  All expectations below are under \(P\).  The exact-model definition and the local-propensity, multiplier-envelope, multiplier-smoothness, control-smoothness, contrast-smoothness, interiority, and Bernoulli-response assumptions give, on \(D=[0,1]^d\),
\[
 P_X=\operatorname{Unif}(D),\qquad
 \frac14\leq e(x)=\frac{g_P(x)}4\leq\frac12,
 \qquad e\in\mathcal H_d^\alpha(L/4),
 \qquad \mu_0\in\mathcal H_d^\beta(L),
 \qquad \tau\in\mathcal H_d^\gamma(L),
 \tag{1}
\]
with \(\mu_1=\mu_0+\tau\), \(|\tau|\leq3/4\), and the stated conditional Bernoulli laws.  The sampling assumption makes \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) independent.

For \(s>0\), let \(m_s=\lceil s\rceil-1\) and choose
\[
 p=m_\gamma+\max\{m_\alpha,m_\beta\}.
\]
For a positive integer \(M\), partition \(D\) into the \(M^d\) half-open cubes of side \(M^{-1}\), using the closed upper face convention at the boundary.  Let \(\mathcal B_M\) be the functions whose restriction to each cell is a polynomial of total degree at most \(p\).  This space is finite dimensional, with
\[
 K_M=\dim(\mathcal B_M)=a_pM^d,
 \qquad a_p=\binom{p+d}{d}<\infty.
 \tag{2}
\]
Scaled tensor Legendre polynomials on each cell, restricted to multi-indices of total degree at most \(p\) and extended by zero away from that cell, give an explicit Lebesgue-orthonormal basis \(b_M:D\to\mathbb R^{K_M}\).  Its exactly known Gram matrix is
\[
 \Omega_M=\int_D b_M(z)b_M(z)^{\mathsf T}\,dz=I_{K_M}.
 \tag{3}
\]

For the tuning proxy \(k\geq1\), put \(M=M_0+\lfloor k^{1/d}\rfloor\), where the fixed integer \(M_0\) is selected below.  Abbreviate \(b_k=b_M\), and let \(\Pi_k\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal B_M\) in \(L_2(D,dz)\).  Since \(M_0\) is fixed and \(k\geq1\),
\[
 K_M\leq Ck,
 \qquad M^{-1}\leq Ck^{-1/d}.
 \tag{4}
\]
This is the required bounded-ratio rank rounding.

Repeated one-dimensional Taylor expansion along coordinate segments, using the derivatives through order \(m_s\) and the order-\((s-m_s)\) H\"older bound in the definition of \(\mathcal H_d^s(L)\), gives a uniform remainder at most \(C(h/M)^s\) on every physical cell of side \(h/M\).  Orthogonal projection has no larger \(L_2\) error than this explicit cellwise Taylor polynomial.  Multiplication by a fixed polynomial of degree at most \(m_\gamma\) raises total degree by at most \(m_\gamma\), so the choice of \(p\) ensures that the approximants remain in \(\mathcal B_M\).  Thus, for every component \(v_j\) of the fixed basis introduced below,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left\|(I-\Pi_k)\{v_j(e(h\,\cdot)-3/8)\}\right\|_2&\leq C\ell^\alpha,\\
 \left\|(I-\Pi_k)(\mu_0(h\,\cdot)-1/2)\right\|_2&\leq C\ell^\beta,\\
 \left\|(I-\Pi_k)\{e(h\,\cdot)v_j\}\right\|_2&\leq C\ell^\alpha,
 \end{split}
 \qquad \ell=hk^{-1/d}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The argument also covers \(s\leq1\), for which the cellwise approximant is constant.  All constants are uniform over the model class by (1).

Let \(v:D\to\mathbb R^q\) be a fixed Lebesgue-orthonormal basis of polynomials of total degree at most \(m_\gamma\), where \(q=\binom{m_\gamma+d}{d}\).  Define
\[
 Q=\int_Dv(z)v(z)^{\mathsf T}e(hz)\{1-e(hz)\}\,dz,
 \qquad
 R=\int_Dv(z)e(hz)\{1-e(hz)\}\tau(hz)\,dz,
 \tag{6}
\]
\(\theta=Q^{-1}R\), and \(\tau_h(hz)=v(z)^{\mathsf T}\theta\).  From (1) and orthonormality,
\[
 \lambda_0 I_q\preceq Q\preceq\frac14I_q,
 \qquad \lambda_0=\frac3{16}.
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular, \(Q\) is invertible and \(\theta\) is uniformly bounded.

Let \(T_\tau\) be the order-\(m_\gamma\) Taylor polynomial of \(\tau\) at the corner.  The same Taylor argument gives
\[
 \sup_{z\in D}|\tau(hz)-T_\tau(hz)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
 \tag{8}
\]
Write \(T_\tau(hz)=v(z)^{\mathsf T}a_\tau\).  Subtracting \(Qa_\tau\) from the equation \(Q\theta=R\) gives
\[
 \theta-a_\tau
 =Q^{-1}\int_Dv(z)e(hz)\{1-e(hz)\}
 \{\tau(hz)-T_\tau(hz)\}\,dz.
 \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9), boundedness of the fixed basis \(v\) on the compact cube, and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \|\theta-a_\tau\|\leq Ch^\gamma,
 \qquad
 \sup_{z\in D}|\tau_h(hz)-T_\tau(hz)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
 \tag{10}
\]
Combining (8) and (10) proves the uniform approximation that will be used in the projection remainder:
\[
 \sup_{z\in D}|\tau_h(hz)-\tau(hz)|\leq Ch^\gamma,
 \qquad
 |\tau_h(0)-\tau(0)|\leq Ch^\gamma.
 \tag{11}
\]

We now define the estimator.  Use the fixed bounded pilots
\[
 \bar e(x)=3/8,
 \qquad \bar\mu(x)=1/2.
 \tag{12}
\]
For \(x\in D\), put
\[
 w_h(x)=h^{-d}\mathbf1\{x\in hD\},\qquad
 v_h(x)=v(x/h)\mathbf1\{x\in hD\},\qquad
 b_{h,k}(x)=b_k(x/h)\mathbf1\{x\in hD\}.
\]
For \(o=(x,a,y)\), define
\[
 \phi_{a1}(o)=a\{a-\bar e(x)\},
 \qquad
 \phi_{y1}(o)=\{y-\bar\mu(x)\}\{a-\bar e(x)\},
 \tag{13}
\]
and, for \(o_r=(x_r,a_r,y_r)\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \phi_{a2}(o_1,o_2)
 &=-\{a_1-\bar e(x_1)\}
 b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)a_2,\\
 \phi_{y2}(o_1,o_2)
 &=-\{a_1-\bar e(x_1)\}
 b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)
 \{y_2-\bar\mu(x_2)\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{14}
\]
For \(\mathbb P_n f=n^{-1}\sum_i f(O_i)\) and \(\mathbb U_nH=\{n(n-1)\}^{-1}\sum_{i\ne j}H(O_i,O_j)\), set
\[
 \begin{split}
 \widetilde Q={}&\mathbb P_n\!\left[
 w_h(X)v_h(X)\phi_{a1}(O)v_h(X)^{\mathsf T}\right]\\
 &+\mathbb U_n\!\left[
 w_h(X_1)w_h(X_2)v_h(X_1)\phi_{a2}(O_1,O_2)v_h(X_2)^{\mathsf T}\right],\\
 \widetilde R={}&\mathbb P_n\!\left[
 w_h(X)v_h(X)\phi_{y1}(O)\right]\\
 &+\mathbb U_n\!\left[
 w_h(X_1)w_h(X_2)v_h(X_1)\phi_{y2}(O_1,O_2)\right].
 \end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]
The two fixed-basis factors \(v_h(X_1)\) and \(v_h(X_2)^{\mathsf T}\) in the matrix correction are essential for the matrix cancellation below.  The matrix \(\widetilde Q\) need not be symmetric, so stabilize its singular values.  Choose a deterministic Borel singular-value decomposition \(\widetilde Q=U\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_q)V^{\mathsf T}\): diagonalize \(\widetilde Q^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q\), and within each eigenspace project the coordinate vectors in lexicographic order, retain the first linearly independent projections, and apply Gram--Schmidt with positive first nonzero coordinate.  This finite prescription is Borel in the matrix entries and fixes signs and tied subspaces.  Put
\[
 \widetilde Q_{\mathrm{fl}}
 =U\operatorname{diag}(s_1\vee\lambda_0/2,\ldots,s_q\vee\lambda_0/2)V^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Finally, set
\[
 \widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)
 =\operatorname{clip}_{[-3/4,3/4]}
 \left\{v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q_{\mathrm{fl}}^{-1}\widetilde R\right\}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) and (12)--(17) use only fixed polynomial evaluations, exact Lebesgue integrals, sample averages, an ordered finite \(U\)-statistic, and finite matrix operations.  Singular-value flooring and clipping provide an output on every dataset, including an empty localization cube, so (17) is a total Borel estimator.

We prove its risk.  Write \(\delta_e=e-\bar e\), \(\delta_\mu=\mu_0-\bar\mu\), and use a superscript \(h\) for stretching to \(D\), for example \(\delta_e^h(z)=\delta_e(hz)\).  Put \(\widetilde S=\widetilde R-\widetilde Q\theta\).  The conditional Bernoulli laws give, equation by equation,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E[\phi_{y1}(O)\mid X=x]
 &=e(x)\{1-\bar e(x)\}\tau(x)+\delta_e(x)\delta_\mu(x),\\
 \mathbb E[\phi_{a1}(O)\mid X=x]
 &=e(x)\{1-\bar e(x)\},\\
 \mathbb E[\phi_{y2}(O_1,O_2)\mid X_1=x_1,X_2=x_2]
 &=-\delta_e(x_1)b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)
 \{\delta_\mu(x_2)+e(x_2)\tau(x_2)\},\\
 \mathbb E[\phi_{a2}(O_1,O_2)\mid X_1=x_1,X_2=x_2]
 &=-\delta_e(x_1)b_{h,k}(x_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_{h,k}(x_2)e(x_2).
 \end{split}
 \tag{18}
\]
Changing variables \(x_r=hz_r\) in (15), using the exact Gram identity (3), and subtracting \(\widetilde Q\theta\), equation (18) yields, for each coordinate \(j\),
\[
 \mathbb E\widetilde S_j
 =\int_Dv_j(z)\delta_e^h(z)(I-\Pi_k)
 \left[\delta_\mu^h+e^h\{\tau^h-\tau_h^h\}\right](z)\,dz.
 \tag{19}
\]
The factor \((I-\Pi_k)[\cdots]\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal B_M\).  Therefore (5), (11), orthogonal-projection contraction, and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \begin{split}
 |\mathbb E\widetilde S_j|
 &=\left|\int_D(I-\Pi_k)(v_j\delta_e^h)
 (I-\Pi_k)\left[\delta_\mu^h+e^h\{\tau^h-\tau_h^h\}\right]dz\right|\\
 &\leq C\ell^\alpha\{\ell^\beta+h^\gamma\}
 =C\{\ell^S+h^\gamma\ell^\alpha\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{20}
\]
This derives both nuisance-bias terms without imposing smoothness on \(\mathbb E[Y\mid X]\).

The same cancellation controls the mean of the corrected matrix.  For coordinates \(j,r\), (6), (15), and (18) give
\[
 \{\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q\}_{jr}
 =\int_Dv_j\delta_e^h(I-\Pi_k)(e^hv_r)\,dz
 =\int_D(I-\Pi_k)(v_j\delta_e^h)(I-\Pi_k)(e^hv_r)\,dz.
 \tag{21}
\]
The cell-width version of (5) implies
\[
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C(h/M)^{2\alpha}\leq C M_0^{-2\alpha},
 \qquad
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\ell^{2\alpha}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Choose the fixed \(M_0\) large enough that
\[
 C(h/M)^{2\alpha}\leq C M_0^{-2\alpha}\leq\lambda_0/4
 \qquad\text{for every }h\in(0,1]\text{ and }k\geq1.
 \tag{23}
\]
Indeed, for every unit vector \(a\), the triangle inequality, (7), and (23) give
\[
 \|\mathbb E\widetilde Q a\|
 \geq\|Qa\|-\|(\mathbb E\widetilde Q-Q)a\|
 \geq3\lambda_0/4.
\tag{24}
\]
Taking the infimum over unit vectors proves \(s_{\min}(\mathbb E\widetilde Q)\geq3\lambda_0/4\).

It remains to derive every stochastic term.  Since \(\mathbb P_nf=\mathbb U_n[f(O_1)]\), every coordinate of \(\widetilde S\), and every entry of \(\widetilde Q\), is an ordered second-order \(U\)-statistic; symmetrizing its kernel does not change the statistic.  For any square-integrable symmetric kernel \(\xi\), enumeration of pairs of index pairs gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{\mathbb U_n\xi\}
 \leq\frac4n\mathbb E\left[\mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)\mid O_1\}^2\right]
 +\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)^2\}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Indeed, terms indexed by disjoint pairs have zero covariance by independence; after division by \(n^2(n-1)^2\), at most \(4n(n-1)(n-2)\) ordered pair-pairs share one index and at most \(2n(n-1)\) share two.  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds each covariance by the displayed conditional or unconditional second moment.

The first-order part of each kernel has second moment at most
\[
 C\int_{hD}h^{-2d}\,dx=Ch^{-d}.
 \tag{26}
\]
For the second-order part, \(A\), \(Y\), the pilots, \(\tau_h\), and the fixed \(v\)-factors are bounded by (1), (10), and compactness.  The growing basis \(b_k\) is controlled instead by the exact Hilbert--Schmidt calculation
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\int_D\int_D
 \left\{b_k(z_1)^{\mathsf T}\Omega_M^{-1}b_k(z_2)\right\}^2\,dz_1\,dz_2\\
 &\qquad=\operatorname{tr}\left(
 \Omega_M^{-1}\Omega_M\Omega_M^{-1}\Omega_M\right)
 =K_M\leq Ck.
 \end{split}
 \tag{27}
\]
The two local weights and the changes of variables \(x_r=hz_r\) consequently give
\[
 \mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)^2\}\leq C\{h^{-d}+kh^{-2d}\}.
 \tag{28}
\]
For clarity, the second-order kernel for \(\widetilde S\) contains the bounded second-observation factor \(Y_2-\bar\mu(X_2)-A_2\tau_h(X_2)\); for \(\widetilde Q\) it contains \(A_2v_h(X_2)^{\mathsf T}\).  Integrating one observation therefore produces \(\Pi_k\) applied either to \(\delta_\mu^h+e^h(\tau^h-\tau_h^h)\), to a fixed-polynomial multiple of \(e^h\), or to a fixed-polynomial multiple of \(\delta_e^h\).  The contraction \(\|\Pi_k f\|_2\leq\|f\|_2\), followed by the one remaining localization, yields for both one-index projections
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\mathbb E\{\xi(O_1,O_2)\mid O_1\}^2\right]\leq Ch^{-d},
 \tag{29}
\]
after relabeling the two arguments when needed.  Combining (25)--(29), and summing the fixed number of vector or matrix coordinates, proves
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde S-\mathbb E\widetilde S\|^2
 &\leq C\left\{\frac1{nh^d}+\frac{k}{n^2h^{2d}}\right\},\\
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde Q-\mathbb E\widetilde Q\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 &\leq C\left\{\frac1{nh^d}+\frac{k}{n^2h^{2d}}\right\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{30}
\]
Thus the nondegenerate scale is \((nh^d)^{-1/2}\), whereas the degenerate projection scale is \(\sqrt{k}/(nh^d)\).

Set
\[
 u=\left\{\frac1{nh^d}+\frac{k}{n^2h^{2d}}\right\}^{1/2}.
 \tag{31}
\]
If \(u\) exceeds a sufficiently small fixed constant, clipping and \(|\tau(0)|\leq3/4\) make the claimed inequality immediate after enlarging \(C\).  Otherwise let
\[
 \mathcal G=\{\|\widetilde Q-\mathbb E\widetilde Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\lambda_0/4\}.
\]
Equations (24) and (30), Markov's inequality, and \(u\leq1\) give
\[
 \mathbb P(\mathcal G^c)\leq Cu^2\leq Cu.
 \tag{32}
\]
On \(\mathcal G\), for every unit vector \(a\), (24) and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \|\widetilde Qa\|
 \geq\|\mathbb E\widetilde Qa\|-\|(\widetilde Q-\mathbb E\widetilde Q)a\|
 >\lambda_0/2.
\]
Thus \(s_{\min}(\widetilde Q)>\lambda_0/2\), flooring is inactive, and \(\|\widetilde Q^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2/\lambda_0\).  Hence
\[
 \left|v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q^{-1}\widetilde R-\tau_h(0)\right|
 =\left|v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q^{-1}\widetilde S\right|
 \leq\frac{2\|v(0)\|}{\lambda_0}\|\widetilde S\|.
 \tag{33}
\]
Clipping is a contraction toward \(\tau(0)\in[-3/4,3/4]\).  Therefore (11), (20), (30), (32), (33), and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\left|\widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)-\tau(0)\right|
 &\leq Ch^\gamma+C\|\mathbb E\widetilde S\|
 +C\left\{\mathbb E\|\widetilde S-\mathbb E\widetilde S\|^2\right\}^{1/2}
 +\frac32\mathbb P(\mathcal G^c)\\
 &\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\ell^S+h^\gamma\ell^\alpha
 +(nh^d)^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}\right\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{34}
\]
Since \(0<\ell\leq1\), \(h^\gamma\ell^\alpha\leq h^\gamma\).  Finally, \(a+b\leq\sqrt{2(a^2+b^2)}\) for nonnegative \(a,b\); taking \(a=(nh^d)^{-1/2}\) and \(b=\sqrt{k}/(nh^d)\) gives the second displayed inequality in the statement.  Every bound is uniform in \(P\), completing the proof.
%%% FIELD theorem-statement
Suppose \(\kappa=0\), put \(S=\alpha+\beta\), and define
\[
 S_\star=\frac{d\gamma}{2\gamma+d},
 \qquad
 D_\star=1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{2S}.
\]
Then every \(P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\) satisfies
\[
 e_P(x)=g_P(x)/4\in[1/4,1/2]
 \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal X.
\]
There are constants \(0<c\leq C<\infty\), depending only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), such that for every \(n\geq2\),
\[
 c n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \leq R_n(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)
 \leq C\max\left\{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
 n^{-1/D_\star}\right\}.
\]
The upper bound is attained by a total one-sided localized second-order projection R-learner.  In particular, if \(S\geq S_\star\), then
\[
 R_n(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)
 \asymp n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
There is no logarithmic multiplier at \(S=S_\star\): at that equality the two finite bias--variance balances coincide.  If \(S<S_\star\), the result asserts the displayed bracket with upper rate \(n^{-1/D_\star}\) but does not assert a matching nuisance-dominated lower bound.
%%% FIELD theorem-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\).  The definition of \(r_\kappa\), the local-propensity-shape assumption, and the multiplier-envelope assumption give, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\),
\[
 e_P(x)=\frac{r_0(x)g_P(x)}4=\frac{g_P(x)}4,
 \qquad
 \frac14\leq e_P(x)\leq\frac12.
 \tag{35}
\]
Thus this is the original exact-uniform model on its bounded-overlap parameter slice, not a replacement class.

For the lower bound, every data-only Borel estimator \(T\) induces the admissible supplied-propensity estimator \(T^e(o_{1:n},e)=T(o_{1:n})\), which ignores its second argument and has the same loss under each law.  The unknown-risk and oracle-risk definitions therefore imply
\[
 R_n^e(d,0,\beta,\gamma,L)
 \leq R_n(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L).
 \tag{36}
\]
The supplied-propensity lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:oracle\mbox{-}minimax}\), specialized to \(\kappa=0\), and (36) give
\[
 c_e n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \leq R_n(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)
 \qquad(n\geq2).
 \tag{37}
\]
This lower bound is carried by genuine full-support uniform-design Bernoulli laws in the exact class, as stated in that established theorem.

For the upper bound invoke \(\mathrm{lem:bounded\mbox{-}overlap\mbox{-}second\mbox{-}order\mbox{-}risk}\).  Its interaction term is dominated because \(0<\ell\leq1\) implies \(h^\gamma\ell^\alpha\leq h^\gamma\).  Hence its explicit estimator satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau^{(2)}_{n,h,k}(0)-\tau_P(0)|
 \leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\ell^S+(nh^d)^{-1/2}
 +\frac{\sqrt{k}}{nh^d}\right\}.
 \tag{38}
\]

First suppose \(S<S_\star\).  Put \(\delta=D_\star^{-1}\) and choose, up to the bounded-ratio admissible-rank rounding already built into the lemma,
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/(\gamma D_\star)}=n^{-\delta/\gamma},
 \qquad
 k_n\asymp n^{(d/S-d/\gamma)/D_\star}
 =n^{\delta d(1/S-1/\gamma)}.
 \tag{39}
\]
Because \(S<S_\star<\gamma\), the exponent of \(k_n\) is positive.  With \(\ell_n=h_nk_n^{-1/d}\), equation (39) yields
\[
 \ell_n\asymp n^{-\delta/S},
 \qquad
 h_n^\gamma\asymp\ell_n^S\asymp n^{-\delta}.
 \tag{40}
\]
Moreover, the identity \(1=\delta\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(2S)\}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}
 \asymp n^{-1+\delta d/(2S)+\delta d/(2\gamma)}
 =n^{-\delta}.
 \tag{41}
\]
The exponent of the nondegenerate term \((nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\) exceeds \(\delta\) by
\[
 \left(\frac12-\frac{\delta d}{2\gamma}\right)-\delta
 =\frac{d(1/S-1/\gamma)-2}{4D_\star}\geq0,
 \tag{42}
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to \(S\leq d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)=S_\star\).  Substitution of (40)--(42) into (38) proves
\[
 R_n(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\leq Cn^{-1/D_\star}
 \qquad(S<S_\star).
 \tag{43}
\]

Now suppose \(S\geq S_\star\).  Choose
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},
 \qquad
 k_n\asymp nh_n^d,
 \tag{44}
\]
again using the lemma's deterministic rank rounding.  Then
\[
 h_n^\gamma=(nh_n^d)^{-1/2}\asymp\frac{\sqrt{k_n}}{nh_n^d}
 =n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
 \qquad
 \ell_n\asymp n^{-1/d}.
 \tag{45}
\]
The definition of \(S_\star\) gives the exact equivalence
\[
 S\geq S_\star
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac Sd\geq\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d},
\]
so (45) implies
\[
 \ell_n^S\lesssim n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
 \tag{46}
\]
Equations (38) and (44)--(46) prove the oracle-rate upper bound for \(S\geq S_\star\).  The estimator is total for every \(n\geq2\); fixed rounding and any finitely many small-sample constants are absorbed by enlarging \(C\), since clipping bounds the loss by \(3/2\).

Finally, \(D_\star^{-1}\leq\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\) exactly when \(S\leq S_\star\).  Thus (43) and the high-smoothness upper bound combine to give
\[
 R_n(d,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)
 \leq C\max\left\{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},n^{-1/D_\star}\right\}.
 \tag{47}
\]
Together with (37), this proves the asserted bracket and equality when \(S\geq S_\star\).  At \(S=S_\star\),
\[
 D_\star=2+\frac d\gamma,
 \qquad
 \frac1{D_\star}=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.
 \tag{48}
\]
Equations (45), (46), and (48) show that finitely many pure-power bias and variance terms agree; no dyadic or harmonic summation appears.  Hence no logarithmic multiplier is introduced at equality.
