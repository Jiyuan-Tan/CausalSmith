%%% FIELD proof-unique-compatible-root
Fix \(z=(u,v)\in D\) and \(\theta\in[\theta_L,\theta_U]\). By \(\mathrm{lem:assay\text{-}inversion}\),
\[
p_a=\frac{q_a-u}{v-u}\in[0,1],\qquad a\in\{0,1\},
\]
where \(v-u=d\geq\kappa>0\), so every displayed division is valid. For a proposed benefit mass \(b=p_{01}\), the two margin equations and total mass force
\[
(p_{00},p_{01},p_{10},p_{11})
=(1-p_0-b,b,b-\Delta,p_1-b),
\qquad \Delta=p_1-p_0.
\]
All four coordinates are nonnegative exactly when
\[
b\in J:=\bigl[\max(0,\Delta),\min(p_1,1-p_0)\bigr].
\]
This interval is nonempty: \(\max(0,p_1-p_0)\leq p_1\) and \(\max(0,p_1-p_0)\leq1-p_0\), using \(0\leq p_0,p_1\leq1\).

The cross-product equality is equivalent to
\[
F(b):=\theta b(b-\Delta)-(p_1-b)(1-p_0-b)
=r b^2+(1-r\Delta)b-p_1(1-p_0)=0,
\qquad r=\theta-1.
\]
At the lower endpoint of \(J\), either \(b=0\) or \(b-\Delta=0\), and hence \(F(b)\leq0\). At the upper endpoint, either \(p_1-b=0\) or \(1-p_0-b=0\), and hence \(F(b)\geq0\). Polynomial continuity and the intermediate value theorem therefore give a root in \(J\).

For every \(b\in J\), write the four displayed nonnegative cell masses. Direct differentiation gives
\[
F'(b)=\theta(p_{01}+p_{10})+p_{11}+p_{00}
\geq \min(\theta,1)
(p_{00}+p_{01}+p_{10}+p_{11})
=\min(\theta,1)>0,
\]
where positivity of \(\theta\) follows from \(0<\theta_L\leq\theta\leq\theta_U<\infty\). Thus \(F\) is strictly increasing on \(J\), and the feasible root is unique.

If \(\theta=1\), then \(r=0\) and the equation gives
\[
b=p_1(1-p_0)=C.
\]
If \(r\neq0\), put \(B=1-r\Delta\) and \(C=p_1(1-p_0)\). Completing the square yields
\[
(2rb+B)^2=B^2+4rC.
\]
At the root,
\[
2rb+B=F'(b)>0,
\]
so the positive square-root sign is forced, and
\[
b=\frac{-B+\sqrt{B^2+4rC}}{2r}=b_\theta(u,v).
\]
Membership in \(J\) proves nonnegativity of the four masses, and direct addition shows that they sum to one. Finally, \(F(b)=0\) is exactly
\[
p_{11}p_{00}=\theta p_{10}p_{01}.
\]
The argument includes structural-zero contacts because it uses nonnegativity and \(\theta>0\), never division by a cell probability.

%%% FIELD proof-root-comparative-statics
Write
\[
F(b;p_0,p_1,\theta)
=\theta b(b-p_1+p_0)-(p_1-b)(1-p_0-b).
\]
Consider any convergent sequence \((p_{0n},p_{1n},\theta_n)\to(p_0,p_1,\theta)\) in \([0,1]^2\times[\theta_L,\theta_U]\), with corresponding compatible roots \(b_n\). Compactness of \([0,1]\) gives a convergent subsequence \(b_{n_k}\to b_*\). The Fr\'echet inequalities defining each feasible interval pass to the limit, so \(b_*\in[\max(0,p_1-p_0),\min(p_1,1-p_0)]\). Polynomial continuity gives \(F(b_*;p_0,p_1,\theta)=0\). By \(\mathrm{lem:unique\text{-}compatible\text{-}root}\), \(b_*\) is the unique compatible root at the limiting arguments. Thus every convergent subsequence has that same limit, which proves continuity of \(b_\theta\), including at structural-zero contacts and at \(\theta=1\).

At every point where derivatives are taken, the unique-root proof gives the strictly positive denominator
\[
F_b=\theta(p_{01}+p_{10})+p_{11}+p_{00}>0.
\]
Holding \(b\) fixed while differentiating the defining equation gives
\[
F_\theta=p_{01}p_{10},\qquad
F_{p_0}=\theta p_{01}+p_{11},\qquad
F_{p_1}=-(\theta p_{01}+p_{00}).
\]
The implicit-function theorem applies because \(F_b>0\). Solving \(F_b\,db+F_t\,dt=0\) for each coordinate \(t\) yields
\[
\frac{\partial b}{\partial\theta}
=-\frac{p_{01}p_{10}}
{\theta(p_{01}+p_{10})+p_{11}+p_{00}},
\]
\[
\frac{\partial b}{\partial p_0}
=-\frac{\theta p_{01}+p_{11}}
{\theta(p_{01}+p_{10})+p_{11}+p_{00}},
\qquad
\frac{\partial b}{\partial p_1}
=\frac{\theta p_{01}+p_{00}}
{\theta(p_{01}+p_{10})+p_{11}+p_{00}}.
\]
Every displayed numerator is nonnegative. The common denominator minus the numerator of \(-\partial b/\partial p_0\) equals \(\theta p_{10}+p_{00}\geq0\), and the denominator minus the numerator of \(\partial b/\partial p_1\) equals \(\theta p_{10}+p_{11}\geq0\). Consequently,
\[
\frac{\partial b}{\partial\theta}\leq0,
\qquad
-1\leq\frac{\partial b}{\partial p_0}\leq0,
\qquad
0\leq\frac{\partial b}{\partial p_1}\leq1.
\]
Integrating along coordinate segments on which the arguments are interior proves that the root is nonincreasing in \(\theta\), nonincreasing in \(p_0\), and nondecreasing in \(p_1\). Approximating any boundary segment by interior segments and using continuity extends these monotonicities and the one-sided derivative bounds to boundary contacts. The same coordinate-segment integration gives, for fixed \(\theta\),
\[
|b(p_0,p_1,\theta)-b(p'_0,p'_1,\theta)|
\leq |p_0-p'_0|+|p_1-p'_1|.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-finite-sharp-envelope
Put \(x=q_0\), \(y=q_1\), \(\delta=y-x\), and \(d=v-u\). By \(\mathrm{def:channel\text{-}polygon}\), \(D\) is a closed rectangle intersected with the closed half-plane \(v-u\geq\kappa\). It is therefore compact and convex, and it has at most five vertices. The assumption \(D\neq\varnothing\), together with \(d\geq\kappa>0\), makes every corrected margin well defined.

For fixed \(\theta\), multiply the root equation by \(d^2>0\) to obtain
\[
H(u,v,b)
=r d^2b^2+(d-r\delta)d b-(y-u)(v-x)=0.
\]
The unique-root lemma gives
\[
H_b=d^2\{\theta(p_{01}+p_{10})+p_{11}+p_{00}\}>0.
\]
Thus implicit differentiation along every line segment in \(D\) is legitimate away from its endpoints, including when \(\theta=1\).

We first exclude unlisted interior extrema. If \(x\neq y\), then
\[
\det\frac{\partial(p_0,p_1)}{\partial(u,v)}
=\frac{y-x}{(v-u)^3}\neq0.
\]
At an interior channel, both corrected margins lie strictly between zero and one. Since \(0<\theta<\infty\), the strict endpoint signs in the proof of \(\mathrm{lem:unique\text{-}compatible\text{-}root}\) put the root strictly inside its Fr\'echet interval, so all four cells are positive. The margin derivatives in \(\mathrm{lem:root\text{-}comparative\text{-}statics}\) are then respectively strictly negative and strictly positive. Their nonzero gradient cannot be annihilated by the invertible Jacobian above, so no interior stationary point exists.

If \(x=y=q\), then \(p_0=p_1=p\), and the equation becomes
\[
r b^2+b=p(1-p),
\qquad
\frac{db}{dp}=\frac{1-2p}{1+2rb}.
\]
The denominator is the positive root derivative. Hence the only interior stationary set is \(p=1/2\), equivalently \(u+v=2q\), and there
\[
b=\frac{1}{2(1+\sqrt\theta)}.
\]
If this ridge meets \(D\), the endpoints of its intersection with the compact polygon lie on the boundary. They are stationary-line contacts on axis-aligned edges or the single central contact on the informativeness edge, so the candidate rule lists a representative having the ridge value.

It remains to classify every boundary edge. Direct differentiation at fixed \(b\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_u&=-2rdb^2-(2d-r\delta)b+v-x,\\
H_v&= 2rdb^2+(2d-r\delta)b-(y-u),\\
H_u+H_v&=u+v-x-y.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(r\neq0\), direct expansion of the quadratic--linear resultants gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}_b(H,H_u)
=-r^2d^2(v-x)(v-y)
\{\theta\delta^2-(u+v-x-y)^2\},
\]
\[
\operatorname{Res}_b(H,H_v)
=-r^2d^2(u-x)(u-y)
\{\theta\delta^2-(u+v-x-y)^2\}.
\]
On a horizontal edge, stationarity in its varying \(u\)-coordinate requires \(H_u=0\). Away from a margin-contact factor and using \(r\neq0\) and \(d>0\), the first resultant therefore forces
\[
u+v=x+y\pm\sqrt\theta\,|y-x|.
\]
On a vertical edge, the second resultant and \(H_v=0\) give the same two lines. When \(r=0\), the root is
\[
b=\frac{(y-u)(v-x)}{(v-u)^2},
\]
and direct differentiation gives
\[
b_u=\frac{(v-x)(2y-u-v)}{(v-u)^3},
\qquad
b_v=\frac{(y-u)(2x-u-v)}{(v-u)^3}.
\]
Thus the two stationary lines are \(u+v=2y\) and \(u+v=2x\), exactly the preceding formula at \(\theta=1\).

The excluded margin-contact edges have no hidden extrema. If \(x\leq y\), then on \(u=x\) support compatibility gives
\[
b=\frac{y-x}{v-x},
\]
and on \(v=y\) it gives
\[
b=\frac{y-x}{y-u};
\]
each expression is monotone along its edge. If \(y\leq x\), then on \(u=y\) or \(v=x\) support compatibility gives \(b=0\). Equal-risk contact edges likewise have \(b=0\). Their extrema are therefore at vertices.

Finally, on the informativeness edge \(v=u+\kappa\), implicit differentiation and the identity for \(H_u+H_v\) give
\[
\frac{d}{du}b_\theta(u,u+\kappa)
=-\frac{2u+\kappa-x-y}{H_b}.
\]
There is at most one stationary point, namely the listed point satisfying \(u+v=x+y\); vertices cover the remaining edge endpoints. The same one-dimensional argument covers a nondegenerate segment, while a singleton is itself a vertex. This proves candidate completeness.

For the count, intersecting a rectangle with one half-plane whose boundary can truncate one rectangle corner produces at most five vertices. Each of the two distinct-or-coincident slope \(-1\) stationary lines intersects the boundary of a convex polygon in at most two points. Since neither line can coincide with an axis-aligned edge or the slope \(1\) informativeness edge, each contributes at most two axis-edge contacts. The informativeness edge contributes at most one further stationary point. After duplicate points are removed,
\[
|C_\theta|\leq5+4+1=10.
\]
Without the informativeness cut, the feasible polygon is a rectangle, so the corresponding count is \(4+4=8\).

By \(\mathrm{lem:root\text{-}comparative\text{-}statics}\), the minimum over \(\theta\in[\theta_L,\theta_U]\) occurs at \(\theta_U\), and the maximum occurs at \(\theta_L\). Candidate completeness and \(\mathrm{def:sharp\text{-}endpoints}\) therefore identify the extrema of the continuous root map over \(D\times[\theta_L,\theta_U]\) as \(L\) and \(U\).

We now prove sharpness. For every \((u,v,\theta)\in D\times[\theta_L,\theta_U]\), \(\mathrm{lem:unique\text{-}compatible\text{-}root}\) makes the four masses in \(\mathrm{def:attaining\text{-}law}\) nonnegative, summing to one, and satisfying
\[
p_{11}p_{00}=\theta p_{10}p_{01}.
\]
Because \(\theta_L\leq\theta\leq\theta_U\) and \(p_{10}p_{01}\geq0\), this equality implies the closed cross-product band. Conditional Bernoulli generation gives
\[
P(W_a=1\mid Y_a=1)=v=s,
\qquad
P(W_a=0\mid Y_a=0)=1-u=c.
\]
Independent randomization and consistency then give the observed risks \(q_a=u+(v-u)p_a\). Thus every compatible-root value is attained by a law in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cl}}\).

Conversely, every law in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cl}}\) supplies a channel in \(D\) by \(\mathrm{lem:assay\text{-}inversion}\). If \(p_{10}p_{01}>0\), its closed band supplies
\[
\theta=\frac{p_{11}p_{00}}{p_{10}p_{01}}
\in[\theta_L,\theta_U].
\]
If \(p_{10}p_{01}=0\), the two closed inequalities force \(p_{11}p_{00}=0\), so the cross-product equality holds for every \(\theta\) in the nonempty band. Root uniqueness therefore makes the law's benefit equal to \(b_\theta(u,v)\).

The domain \(D\times[\theta_L,\theta_U]\) is convex and hence path-connected. The root map is continuous, so its real-valued image is connected and therefore an interval. Compactness makes this image closed and makes its extrema attainable. Since its extrema are \(L\) and \(U\), its image, and hence the identified set, is exactly \([L,U]\). The laws constructed at optimizing candidates attain both endpoints.

Assume \(D^\circ\neq\varnothing\), and fix \(z^\circ\in D^\circ\). Since \(D\) is convex, for every \(z\in D\) and \(0<t\leq1\),
\[
z_t=(1-t)z+t z^\circ\in D^\circ.
\]
Indeed, the two strict inequalities defining \(D^\circ\) hold at \(z_t\) because they hold at \(z^\circ\) and weakly at \(z\). Thus \(D^\circ\) is dense in \(D\).

By assay inversion and \(v-u\geq\kappa>0\), a channel lies in \(D^\circ\) exactly when both corrected margins lie in \((0,1)\). For finite \(0<\theta<\infty\), the endpoint signs in the unique-root proof are then strict at both ends of the Fr\'echet interval. Hence the compatible root is strictly inside that interval and all four cells are positive. Conversely, four positive cells force both margins into \((0,1)\). Therefore \(D^\circ\times[\theta_L,\theta_U]\) is precisely the channel--odds domain represented by laws in \(\mathcal M_+\).

Continuity of the root and density of \(D^\circ\) in \(D\), together with \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sharp\text{-}envelope}\), give
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_+}b(P)
=\min_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cl}}}b(P)=L,
\qquad
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_+}b(P)
=\max_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cl}}}b(P)=U.
\]

For lower-endpoint attainment, suppose first that some \(z\in C_{\theta_U}\cap D^\circ\) satisfies \(b_{\theta_U}(z)=L\). The attaining-law construction has four positive cells by the preceding argument and therefore belongs to \(\mathcal M_+\); hence it attains \(L\).

Conversely, suppose a strict law attains \(L\). Its four cells are positive, so the derivative formula gives
\[
\frac{\partial b}{\partial\theta}<0.
\]
It follows that its odds coordinate must equal \(\theta_U\): if it were smaller, increasing it to \(\theta_U\) at the same channel would strictly reduce \(b\), contradicting global minimality. Its channel is consequently a minimizer in \(D^\circ\).

If \(q_0\neq q_1\), the interior argument in \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sharp\text{-}envelope}\) excludes an interior stationary channel. A strict boundary minimizer cannot lie on a marginal-contact edge. On an axis-aligned edge it is a vertex or a stationary-line contact, and on the informativeness edge it is a vertex or its unique stationary point. Hence it belongs to \(C_{\theta_U}\cap D^\circ\).

If \(q_0=q_1=q\), the only additional possibility is the ridge \(u+v=2q\). Its intersection with \(D\), if nonempty, is a compact segment or singleton and has a boundary representative with the same objective value. Every point on the ridge satisfies \(u<q<v\), because \(v-u\geq\kappa>0\), so that representative remains in \(D^\circ\). The candidate map lists it as an axis-edge contact, an informativeness-edge stationary point, or a vertex. Degenerate polygon cases are covered by the same edge argument. This proves necessity for the lower endpoint.

Replacing minimization by maximization and \(\theta_U\) by \(\theta_L\) gives the identical equivalence for \(U\). Thus each strict endpoint is attained exactly under the stated finite-candidate criterion.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-confidence-projection
For the four primitive intervals, define
\[
E_0=\{q_0\in Q_0\},\qquad
E_1=\{q_1\in Q_1\},\qquad
E_s=\{s\in S\},\qquad
E_c=\{c\in C\}.
\]
By the four marginal binomial assumptions and \(\mathrm{lem:clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}exact\text{-}coverage}\),
\[
\Pr_P(E_0^c)\leq\beta_0,
\quad
\Pr_P(E_1^c)\leq\beta_1,
\quad
\Pr_P(E_s^c)\leq\beta_s,
\quad
\Pr_P(E_c^c)\leq\beta_c.
\]
The pointwise indicator inequality
\[
\mathbf 1_{(E_0\cap E_1\cap E_s\cap E_c)^c}
\leq
\mathbf 1_{E_0^c}+\mathbf 1_{E_1^c}
+\mathbf 1_{E_s^c}+\mathbf 1_{E_c^c}
\]
and \(\mathrm{ass:confidence\text{-}budget}\) imply
\[
\Pr_P(E_0\cap E_1\cap E_s\cap E_c)
\geq1-(\beta_0+\beta_1+\beta_s+\beta_c)
\geq1-\beta.
\]
This is a union-bound argument and uses no mutual-independence condition on the four counts.

Put \(E=E_0\cap E_1\cap E_s\cap E_c\). On \(E\), validation transport gives \(v=s\in S\) and \(1-u=c\in C\). Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cl}}\) gives the channel rectangle and \(v-u=d\geq\kappa\). Moreover, \(\mathrm{lem:assay\text{-}inversion}\) gives \(u\leq q_a\leq v\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\). Hence
\[
(q_0,q_1,u,v)\in\widehat{\mathcal F}.
\]
For every \(\theta\in[\theta_L,\theta_U]\), the compatible-root value at this true tuple belongs to the image projected in \(\mathrm{def:confidence\text{-}projection}\). By continuity and monotonicity in \(\theta\) from \(\mathrm{lem:root\text{-}comparative\text{-}statics}\), the true-channel image is
\[
\{b_\theta(u,v):\theta\in[\theta_L,\theta_U]\}
=[b_{\theta_U}(u,v),b_{\theta_L}(u,v)]
=K^\star.
\]
Since \(I_\beta\) is the interval image over the larger projected domain, \(K^\star\subseteq I_\beta\) on \(E\). The probability bound is pointwise for every compatible \(P\), so
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cl}}}
\Pr_P\{K^\star\subseteq I_\beta\}\geq1-\beta.
\]
Neither the set-inclusion argument nor the union bound invokes uniqueness or differentiability of an optimizer, or positive dimension of a feasible face. Coverage therefore remains valid at optimizer ties, marginal contacts, and collapsed faces.

We next prove the asserted finite computation. Write \(Q_a=[\ell_a,h_a]\), and define the closed validation rectangle in channel coordinates by
\[
R_{uv}
=\bigl([u_L,u_U]\cap\{1-c':c'\in C\}\bigr)
\times\bigl([v_L,v_U]\cap S\bigr).
\]
A channel permits at least one pair \((\widetilde q_0,\widetilde q_1)\in Q_0\times Q_1\) satisfying \(u\leq\widetilde q_a\leq v\) if and only if it lies in
\[
D_Q=R_{uv}\cap
\{u\leq\min(h_0,h_1),\ v\geq\max(\ell_0,\ell_1),\ v-u\geq\kappa\}.
\]
Necessity follows from \(u\leq\widetilde q_a\leq h_a\) and \(v\geq\widetilde q_a\geq\ell_a\). Conversely, these inequalities say that each pair of closed intervals \([\ell_a,h_a]\) and \([u,v]\) intersects, so a feasible \(\widetilde q_a\) can be chosen from that intersection.

Fix \((u,v)\in D_Q\). The corrected margins are increasing in their respective trial risks. The monotonicities in \(\mathrm{lem:root\text{-}comparative\text{-}statics}\) therefore give the exact profiles
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&\widetilde q_0&\widetilde q_1&\theta\\ \hline
\text{lower endpoint}&\min(h_0,v)&\max(\ell_1,u)&\theta_U\\
\text{upper endpoint}&\max(\ell_0,u)&\min(h_1,v)&\theta_L.
\end{array}
\]
For the lower endpoint, if \(D_Q\cap\{v\leq h_0\}\neq\varnothing\), the profile has \(p_0=1\); if \(D_Q\cap\{u\geq\ell_1\}\neq\varnothing\), it has \(p_1=0\). In either case the support interval in \(\mathrm{lem:unique\text{-}compatible\text{-}root}\) forces \(b=0\), the global minimum because \(b\) is a probability. Each nonemptiness question is a finite linear feasibility check. If both clipped polygons are empty, every point of \(D_Q\) has \(v>h_0\) and \(u<\ell_1\), and the lower profile fixes
\[
(\widetilde q_0,\widetilde q_1)=(h_0,\ell_1).
\]
After relabeling the endpoints of \(R_{uv}\) as the channel rectangle, the remaining domain is a channel polygon of the form in \(\mathrm{def:channel\text{-}polygon}\). The completeness result in \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}sharp\text{-}envelope}\) therefore computes its minimum with the ten-point candidate map.

For the upper endpoint, split \(D_Q\) by the two lines \(u=\ell_0\) and \(v=h_1\). On the resulting four closed polygonal regions, the profiled objective is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
u\leq\ell_0,\ v\geq h_1
&b_{\theta_L}(\ell_0,h_1;u,v)\\
u\geq\ell_0,\ v\geq h_1
&\dfrac{h_1-u}{v-u}\\
u\leq\ell_0,\ v\leq h_1
&\dfrac{v-\ell_0}{v-u}\\
u\geq\ell_0,\ v\leq h_1
&1.
\end{array}
\]
The first row is the nontrivial compatible-root region. In the second region \(p_0=0\), so support compatibility forces \(b=p_1=(h_1-u)/(v-u)\). In the third, \(p_1=1\), so it forces \(b=1-p_0=(v-\ell_0)/(v-u)\). In the fourth, \((p_0,p_1)=(0,1)\), so \(b=1\). The first region is again a channel polygon and is evaluated by the ten-point map; the fourth requires only a linear feasibility check.

On either fractional region, the denominator \(G(u,v)=v-u\) is affine and satisfies \(G\geq\kappa>0\). If \(z=\sum_i\lambda_i z_i\) is a convex combination of the vertices of that region and \(N\) denotes its affine numerator, then
\[
\frac{N(z)}{G(z)}
=\sum_i
\frac{\lambda_iG(z_i)}{\sum_k\lambda_kG(z_k)}
\frac{N(z_i)}{G(z_i)}.
\]
The coefficients on the right are nonnegative and sum to one. Hence every fractional value is a convex combination of vertex values, and each fractional-region maximum occurs at a polygon vertex. We first establish a deterministic modulus. Let \(\eta=(q_0,q_1,u,v)\) and \(\eta'=(q'_0,q'_1,u',v')\) be compatible primitive tuples, and fix \(\theta\in[\theta_L,\theta_U]\). The segment joining them remains compatible because the channel rectangle, the inequalities \(u\leq q_a\leq v\), and the floor \(v-u\geq\kappa\) are linear. Along the segment, \(d=v-u\geq\kappa>0\). Direct differentiation of
\[
p_a=\frac{q_a-u}{v-u}
\]
gives
\[
\left|\frac{\partial p_a}{\partial q_a}\right|=\frac1d,
\qquad
\left|\frac{\partial p_a}{\partial u}\right|
=\frac{v-q_a}{d^2}=\frac{1-p_a}{d},
\qquad
\left|\frac{\partial p_a}{\partial v}\right|
=\frac{q_a-u}{d^2}=\frac{p_a}{d}.
\]
Compatibility gives \(0\leq p_a\leq1\), so all three absolute derivatives are at most \(1/\kappa\). Segment integration yields
\[
|p_a(\eta)-p_a(\eta')|
\leq\frac{|q_a-q'_a|+|u-u'|+|v-v'|}{\kappa}.

The unit coordinatewise margin bounds in \(\mathrm{lem:root\text{-}comparative\text{-}statics}\), extended to boundary contacts by continuity, now give

+2|u-u'|+2|v-v'|}{\kappa}.

We next bound a Clopper--Pearson interval deterministically. Suppose \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,p)\), let \(Q_\gamma(X)=[L_X,U_X]\) be its equal-tailed interval of total noncoverage \(\gamma\), and put
\[
\varepsilon(N,\gamma)
=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\gamma)}{2N}}.
\]
For \(X<N\), its upper endpoint satisfies
\[
\Pr_{U_X}\{X'\leq X\}=\gamma/2.
\]
Because \(U_X\geq X/N\), the lower-tail half of \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}binomial\text{-}deviation}\) implies
\[
\frac\gamma2
\leq\exp\{-2N(U_X-X/N)^2\},
\]
and hence \(U_X-X/N\leq\varepsilon(N,\gamma)\). If \(X=N\), then \(U_X=1=X/N\). The symmetric upper-tail argument gives \(X/N-L_X\leq\varepsilon(N,\gamma)\), including \(X=0\), where \(L_X=0\). Consequently, for every observation,
\[
Q_\gamma(X)
\subseteq
[X/N-\varepsilon(N,\gamma),X/N+\varepsilon(N,\gamma)]\cap[0,1].
\tag{2}
\]

Index the four primitive probabilities by
\[
(p_j^\star)_j=(q_0,q_1,s,c),
\qquad
(N_j)_j=(n_0,n_1,m_1,m_0),
\qquad
(w_j)_j=(1,1,2,2),
\]
and set \(\varepsilon_j=\sqrt{\log(2/\beta_j)/(2N_j)}\). On the simultaneous coverage event \(E\) constructed in \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}confidence\text{-}projection}\), the true and every projected value of primitive coordinate \(j\) lie in the same primitive interval. By \((2)\), their distance is at most \(2\varepsilon_j\). Applying \((1)\) to any projected tuple and the true tuple, for the same \(\theta\), gives
\[
|b(\widetilde\eta,\theta)-b(\eta^\star,\theta)|
\leq R,
\qquad
R=\frac2\kappa\sum_jw_j\varepsilon_j.
\tag{3}
\]
The true tuple is feasible on \(E\), so \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}confidence\text{-}projection}\) gives the first inclusion below. As \(\theta\) varies, the true-root values fill \(K^\star\). Taking the infimum and supremum over every projected tuple and \(\theta\) in \((3)\) gives
\[
K^\star\subseteq I_\beta
\subseteq[\inf K^\star-R,\sup K^\star+R]\cap[0,1].
\]
This event has probability at least \(1-\beta\). Only marginal tail bounds and the union bound were used, so mutual independence of the counts is unnecessary.

For the expectation assertion, let \(\widehat p_j\) be the four empirical proportions and define
\[
R_{\mathrm{data}}
=\frac1\kappa\sum_jw_j
\bigl(|\widehat p_j-p_j^\star|+\varepsilon_j\bigr).
\]
The deterministic enclosure \((2)\) shows, without conditioning on coverage, that every projected value of coordinate \(j\) is within \(|\widehat p_j-p_j^\star|+\varepsilon_j\) of the truth. Equation \((1)\) therefore puts the entire projected root image inside the \(R_{\mathrm{data}}\)-enlargement of \(K^\star\). Taking the interval image, and assigning length zero when the projected feasible set is empty, yields
\[
\{\operatorname{length}(I_\beta)-\operatorname{length}(K^\star)\}_+
\leq2R_{\mathrm{data}}.
\tag{4}
\]
For each binomial empirical proportion, Cauchy--Schwarz and the binomial variance formula give
\[
E_P|\widehat p_j-p_j^\star|
\leq\sqrt{E_P(\widehat p_j-p_j^\star)^2}
=\sqrt{\frac{p_j^\star(1-p_j^\star)}{N_j}}
\leq\frac1{2\sqrt{N_j}}.
\]
Taking expectations in \((4)\) gives the uniform bound
\[
\sup_P E_P
\{\operatorname{length}(I_\beta)-\operatorname{length}(K^\star)\}_+
\leq\frac2\kappa\sum_jw_j
\left\{\frac1{2\sqrt{N_j}}+\varepsilon_j\right\}
=O\!\left(\frac1{\kappa\sqrt{N_{\min}}}\right),
\]
where the last equality uses fixed positive marginal budgets. Every displayed constant is independent of the compatible primitive probabilities, proving the claimed uniformity.
