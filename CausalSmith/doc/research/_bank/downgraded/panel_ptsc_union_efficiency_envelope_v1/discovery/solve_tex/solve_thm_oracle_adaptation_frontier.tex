%%% FIELD proof-tangent-normal-form
Write \(E_0=E_{P_{0,O}}\).  All paths below are constructed at the strict-interior, locally constant-rank point specified in the statement.  We first profile the PT restriction.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}restriction\text{-}maps}\),
\[
 q_{\mathrm{PT},x}=\delta_1^0(x)\mathbf 1_J-D(x),
 \qquad
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}=I_J-J^{-1}\mathbf 1_J\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}\mathbf 1_J=J\),
\[
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}\mathbf 1_J=0,
 \qquad
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}\dot q_{\mathrm{PT},x}=-A_{\mathrm{PT}}\dot D(x).
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus profiling the unrestricted scalar path \(\dot\delta_1^0(x)\) leaves exactly the observed derivative of \(-A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)\).  The matrix \(A_{\mathrm{PT}}\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\{v:\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}v=0\}\), so it has rank \(J-1\).  If \(\widetilde R_{D,x}\) represents \(\dot D(x)\), then \(R_{\mathrm{PT},x}=-A_{\mathrm{PT}}\widetilde R_{D,x}\), whence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_0[R_{\mathrm{PT},x}R_{\mathrm{PT},x}^{\prime}]
 \le J-1.
 \tag{3}
\]
The rank is \(J-1\) when the indicated covariance form is positive definite on \(\operatorname{range}(A_{\mathrm{PT}})\).

For SC put \(\Pi_x=B(x)\{B(x)^{\prime}B(x)\}^{-1}B(x)^{\prime}\).  The strict SC spectral margin makes this the orthogonal projector onto the \(J\)-dimensional column space of \(B(x)\).  At an SC law, \(a(x)=B(x)w(x)\).  Differentiating \(\Pi_xB(x)=B(x)\) and then \(r_{\mathrm{SC},x}=(I_T-\Pi_x)a(x)\) gives
\[
 \dot r_{\mathrm{SC},x}
 =(I_T-\Pi_x)\{\dot a(x)-\dot B(x)w(x)\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Therefore the covariance range of \(R_{\mathrm{SC},x}\) lies in \(\operatorname{range}(I_T-\Pi_x)\), which has dimension \(T-J\).  This proves the SC rank ceiling, and positive definiteness of the residualized covariance form on that range gives equality.

For every occupied cell, quotient differentiation gives
\[
 \dot\mu_{g,t}(x;s)
 =E_0\!\left[
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{G=g,X=x\}}{p_X(x)p_g(x)}
 \{Y_t-\mu_{g,t}(x)\}s\right].
 \tag{5}
\]
The denominator is positive by the strict cell and group margins.  The bracketed residual has conditional mean zero given \((G,X)\).  Both \(r_{b,x}\) and \(C_b(x)\) are differentiable finite-dimensional functions of these cell means under the strict SC rank margin.  The ordinary chain rule applied to (5) therefore supplies versions of \(R_{b,x}\) and \(g^{\circ}_{b,x}\) satisfying the covariance identities in \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) and the required conditional-residual normalization.

We next characterize the tangent spaces.  The PT observed projection is locally the zero set of \(r_{\mathrm{PT},x}=-A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)\): the equality \(A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)=0\) is equivalent to \(D(x)=c(x)\mathbf 1_J\), and the missing treated untreated final mean may be set to \(\mu_{1,T-1}(x)+c(x)\).  The SC observed projection is locally the zero set of \(r_{\mathrm{SC},x}=(I_T-\Pi_x)a(x)\), because full column rank gives
\[
 r_{\mathrm{SC},x}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a(x)=B(x)w(x).
 \tag{6}
\]
The missing treated untreated final mean may then be set to \(w(x)^{\prime}\mu_{C,T}(x)\).  No anticipation justifies the use of observed preperiod and donor means in these lifts.  Differentiating either branch equation along a branch-preserving path gives
\[
 E_0[R_{b,x}s]=0
 \quad\text{for every }x.
 \tag{7}
\]

For the converse, retain a maximal linearly independent coordinate vector \(R_b^*\).  Local constancy of rank permits the same coordinates in a neighborhood of \(P_0\), and its Gram matrix is positive definite.  Truncating and recentering its coordinates produces a bounded vector \(v\) for which \(M=E_0[R_b^*v^{\prime}]\) is nonsingular.  For a bounded mean-zero score \(s\) satisfying (7), consider the two-sided exponential tilt
\[
 \frac{dP_{O,\varepsilon,c}}{dP_{0,O}}
 =\frac{\exp\{\varepsilon s+c^{\prime}v\}}
 {E_0[\exp\{\varepsilon s+c^{\prime}v\}]},
 \qquad
 F(\varepsilon,c)=r_b^*(P_{O,\varepsilon,c}).
 \tag{8}
\]
The tilt is defined for both signs of sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).  Bounded tilting directions and the strict inequalities for cell mass, group overlap, fourth moments, the SC Gram spectrum, and the nonzero normal spectrum ensure, by continuity of the finitely many probabilities, moments, matrices, and eigenvalues, that both sides of the path remain within the displayed weak model margins.  At the origin,
\[
 F(0,0)=0,
 \qquad
 \partial_cF(0,0)=M,
 \qquad
 \partial_{\varepsilon}F(0,0)=E_0[R_b^*s]=0.
 \tag{9}
\]
Differentiability gives
\[
 F(\varepsilon,c)=F(\varepsilon,0)+Mc+\zeta(\varepsilon,c),
 \qquad
 F(\varepsilon,0)=o(\varepsilon).
\]
On a sufficiently small ball, the Lipschitz constant of \(c\mapsto M^{-1}\zeta(\varepsilon,c)\) is at most \(1/2\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal K_{\varepsilon}(c)
 =-M^{-1}\{F(\varepsilon,0)+\zeta(\varepsilon,c)\}
\]
maps a ball of radius \(C|\varepsilon|\) into itself for a fixed sufficiently large \(C\) and is a contraction.  Iteration from zero is Cauchy, and its limit \(c(\varepsilon)\) satisfies \(F(\varepsilon,c(\varepsilon))=0\) and \(c(\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon)\).  Thus the corrected path has score \(s\).  The full-data lifts described above place it in branch \(b\).  For a general square-integrable \(s\), truncate and recenter to \(\widetilde s_k\), and put
\[
 s_k=\widetilde s_k-v^{\prime}M^{-1}E_0[R_b^*\widetilde s_k].
 \tag{10}
\]
Then \(E_0[R_b^*s_k]=0\) and \(s_k\to s\) in \(L^2(P_{0,O})\).  Taking the closure in \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\text{-}tangents}\) proves
\[
 \mathcal T_b(P_0)
 =\{s\in\mathcal H_{P_0}:E_0[R_{b,x}s]=0\text{ for every }x\}.
 \tag{11}
\]

It remains to calculate the full derivative.  Put \(q_x=P_O(G=1,X=x)\).  The strict overlap conditions give \(\pi_1=\sum_xq_x>0\).  Quotient differentiation of \(\nu_x=q_x/\pi_1\) yields
\[
 \dot\nu_x(s)
 =E_0\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{G=1\}}{\pi_1}
 \{\mathbf 1\{X=x\}-\nu_x\}s\right].
 \tag{12}
\]
Since \(\theta_b=\sum_x\nu_xC_b(x)\), (12) and \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot\theta_b(s)
 &=\sum_xC_b(x)\dot\nu_x(s)+\sum_x\nu_x\dot C_b(x;s)\\
 &=E_0[\chi_bs]+\sum_xE_0[g^{\circ}_{b,x}s]
 =E_0\!\left[\left\{\chi_b+\sum_xg^{\circ}_{b,x}\right\}s\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{13}
\]

There are finitely many retained normals, so (11) implies \(\mathcal T_b(P_0)^{\perp}=\operatorname{span}\{R_{b,x}:x\in\mathcal X\}\).  Combining this identity with (13) and \(\mathrm{def{:}influence\text{-}classes}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal I_b(P_0)=\chi_b+\sum_xg^{\circ}_{b,x}
 +\operatorname{span}\{R_{b,x}:x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Conditional-residual normalization makes \(R_{b,x}\) and \(g^{\circ}_{b,x}\) orthogonal to the \((G,X)\)-measurable variable \(\chi_b\).  Let \(G_b^{\circ}=\sum_xg^{\circ}_{b,x}\), \(\Gamma_b=E_0[R_bR_b^{\prime}]\), and \(u_b=E_0[R_bG_b^{\circ}]\).  If \(c\in\ker\Gamma_b\), then \(c^{\prime}R_b=0\) almost surely, so \(c^{\prime}u_b=0\); hence \(u_b\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_b)\).  Completing the square gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\chi_b+G_b^{\circ}+R_b^{\prime}a\|_2^2
 &=\|\chi_b+G_b^{\circ}-R_b^{\prime}\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b\|_2^2\\
 &\quad +(a+\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b)^{\prime}
 \Gamma_b(a+\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
Minimizing (15) proves the displayed canonical-gradient formula, including the Moore--Penrose treatment of redundant rows.  Orthogonality to \(\chi_b\) also proves that \(V_b\) is \(E_0[\chi_b^2]\) plus the minimized centered-face variance.

 \tag{9}

\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(\text{regular limit})
 \ge \inf_{\phi\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{PT}}(P_0)\cap\mathcal I_{\mathrm{SC}}(P_0)}E_0[\phi^2]
 =E_0[\phi_U^2].
 \tag{16}
\]
This proves the sharp lower bound in the stated strict-interior local tangent experiment.  The cited Sun--Xie--Zhang result concerns one integrated ATT estimator and one multiplier construction analyzed under alternative PT and SC hypotheses, with potentially different branch influence functions and variances.  The present projection supplies neither an implemented standard error nor an attainment or multiplier-validity theorem.

Write \(E_0=E_{P_{0,O}}\) and \(\|f\|_0^2=E_0[f^2]\).  The strict-interior and locally constant-rank hypotheses place this proof in the same local tangent experiment as \(\mathrm{thm{:}union\text{-}envelope}\).  At the intersection, \(\chi_{\mathrm{PT}}=\chi_{\mathrm{SC}}=\chi_0\).  Define

 \tag{3}

 \tag{4}

Every summand is nonnegative, so the simultaneous Gram discrepancy vanishes exactly when the canonical gradients coincide in \(L^2(P_{0,O})\).

Let \(A=\mathcal I_{\mathrm{PT}}(P_0)\), \(B=\mathcal I_{\mathrm{SC}}(P_0)\), and \(C=A\cap B\). \[
 \|c\|_0^2=\|\phi_{\mathrm{PT}}\|_0^2+\|c-\phi_{\mathrm{PT}}\|_0^2.
 \tag{5}
\]
Taking the minimum over \(C\) gives
\[
 V_U=V_{\mathrm{PT}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \phi_{\mathrm{PT}}\in C
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \phi_{\mathrm{PT}}\in B.
 \tag{6}
\]
Using (1)--(3), the squared distance from \(\phi_{\mathrm{PT}}\) to \(B\) is
\[
 \min_{(d_x)}\sum_xp_X(x)c(a_x^{\mathrm{or}},d_x)^{\prime}
 \Omega_xc(a_x^{\mathrm{or}},d_x).
 \tag{7}
\]
The finite-dimensional least-squares minimum is attained; redundant coordinates are handled by the Moore--Penrose inverse.  Equations (6)--(7) prove the PT one-sided criterion.  Interchanging the branches gives
\[
 V_U=V_{\mathrm{SC}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \min_{(a_x)}\sum_xp_X(x)c(a_x,d_x^{\mathrm{or}})^{\prime}
 \Omega_xc(a_x,d_x^{\mathrm{or}})=0.
 \tag{8}
\]

If all three bounds coincide, (5) and its SC analogue force \(\phi_U=\phi_{\mathrm{PT}}=\phi_{\mathrm{SC}}\).  Conversely, if the branch gradients coincide, their common value belongs to \(C\) and has the minimum norm in each branch class; since \(C\) is contained in both classes, it also has the minimum norm in \(C\).  Thus \(V_U=V_{\mathrm{PT}}=V_{\mathrm{SC}}\).  Together with (4), this proves the simultaneous criterion.  Every discrepancy uses only \(p_X(x)\), \(\Omega_x\), and solutions of the displayed finite covariance-weighted quadratic programs, so it is identified from \(P_{0,O}\).

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-attainment-obstruction
Choose the fixed margins so that \(P^{\star}\) satisfies with strict slack the cell-mass, two-sided group-overlap, fourth-moment, SC-spectrum, nonzero-normal-spectrum, and positive local-score-variance inequalities, and so that the displayed ranks are locally constant. \[
 (1/2+\delta)(-1,-2,-2)+(1/2-\delta)(1,2,2)
 =(-2\delta,-4\delta,-4\delta).
 \tag{1}
\]

\[
 w_\delta=(1/2+\delta,1/2-\delta)^{\prime},
 \tag{2}
\]
so SC also holds.  The finitely many probabilities, fourth moments, Gram matrices, positive eigenvalues, and local-score variances vary continuously in \(\delta\).  The strict slack at \(P^{\star}\) therefore ensures that every displayed margin remains valid for all sufficiently small \(|\delta|\).  Thus \(Q_\delta\in\mathcal M_I\).

There is one covariate stratum.  On either branch \(C_b(X)=\theta_b\) is constant, so
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{PT}}(Q_\delta)=\chi_{\mathrm{SC}}(Q_\delta)=0.
 \tag{3}
\]
The branch-specific composition terms in \(\mathrm{def{:}local\text{-}score\text{-}extension}\) therefore do not affect the witness calculation.

Fix an integer \(m\).  Construct two triangular sequences that equal \(Q_\delta\) at coordinate \(m\) and \(P^{\star}\) at every other coordinate, labeling coordinate \(m\) by PT in the first and by SC in the second.  Each sequence belongs to \(\mathfrak L_U(P^{\star})\): after its single exceptional coordinate it is identically \(P^{\star}\), so the QMD score and remainder are eventually zero, and every coordinate obeys the fixed margins.  Thus, at every sample size, the frozen sequence class contains two admissibly labeled copies of the same observed law.

Let \(z_{g,t}=3\mathbf 1\{G=g\}\{Y_t-\mu_{g,t}\}\).  At \(Q_\delta\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(z_{2,3})=\frac32,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(z_{3,3})=6.
 \tag{4}
\]
At \(P^{\star}\), the common score has final donor coefficients \((-1/2,-1/2)\).  The PT extension freezes its center normal coefficient, and the donor laws are unchanged, so those coefficients remain \((-1/2,-1/2)\) at \(Q_\delta\).  The SC normal uses only preperiod coordinates, whereas (2) makes the SC final donor coefficients \((-1/2-\delta,-1/2+\delta)\).  Hence, for
\[
 \Delta_\delta
 =\phi_U^{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_\delta;\mathrm{PT},P^{\star})
 -\phi_U^{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_\delta;\mathrm{SC},P^{\star}),
 \tag{5}
\]
the final-innovation component is \(\delta(z_{2,3}-z_{3,3})\).  Every remaining component uses other group-period innovations and is orthogonal to it.  Therefore
\[
 E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta^2]
 \ge\delta^2\left(\frac32+6\right)
 =\frac{15}{2}\delta^2>0.
 \tag{6}
\]
Both chart scores are mean zero, so \(E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta]=0\).

If a single estimator had both chart expansions uniformly over the frozen class, couple the two sequences at sample size \(m\) through the same independent sample from \(Q_\delta\) and subtract their expansions. \[
 S_m=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^m\Delta_\delta(O_i)=o_{Q_\delta}(1).
 \tag{7}

\[
 E[S_m^4]
 =m^{-1}E[\Delta_\delta^4]
 +3(1-m^{-1})E[\Delta_\delta^2]^2
 \tag{8}
\]
is uniformly bounded.  Thus \((S_m^2)\) is uniformly integrable.  Convergence in probability in (7) would imply \(E[S_m^2]\to0\), contradicting (6).

The argument does not rule out a different theorem on a redesigned common neighborhood with common QMD-score and remainder bounds and without arbitrary finite-coordinate splicing.
