Write \(E_0\) for \(E_{P_{0,O}}\), put \(G_b=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}g_{b,x}\), and stack the profiled restriction maps over the finitely many strata. All expectations below are under \(P_{0,O}\).

We first profile the PT restriction. By \(\mathrm{def{:}restriction\text{-}maps}\),
\[
 q_{\mathrm{PT},x}=\delta_1^0(x)\mathbf 1_J-D(x),
 \qquad
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}=I_J-J^{-1}\mathbf 1_J\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}\mathbf 1_J
 =\mathbf 1_J-J^{-1}\mathbf 1_J(\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}\mathbf 1_J)
 =0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Therefore, along every regular path, including a path with arbitrary derivative \(\dot\delta_1^0(x)\),
\[
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}\dot q_{\mathrm{PT},x}
 =A_{\mathrm{PT}}\{\dot\delta_1^0(x)\mathbf 1_J-\dot D(x)\}
 =-A_{\mathrm{PT}}\dot D(x).
 \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3), \(\mathrm{def{:}restriction\text{-}maps}\), and \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) show that the observed profiled restriction is \(-A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)\) and its Riesz representer is \(R_{\mathrm{PT},x}\). The matrix \(A_{\mathrm{PT}}\) is symmetric and idempotent, its null space is \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_J\}\), and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm{PT}})=J-1.
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(\widetilde R_{D,x}\) represents the derivative of \(D(x)\), then (3) gives \(R_{\mathrm{PT},x}=-A_{\mathrm{PT}}\widetilde R_{D,x}\). Thus
\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_0[R_{\mathrm{PT},x}R_{\mathrm{PT},x}^{\prime}]
 \le \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm{PT}})=J-1.
 \tag{5}
\]
Under the nondegeneracy condition in the statement, the left side of (5) is \(J-1\).

The analogous SC calculation supplies the requested additional rank information without a new assumption. Put
\[
 \Pi_x=B(x)\{B(x)^{\prime}B(x)\}^{-1}B(x)^{\prime}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}sc\text{-}rank}\) makes \(B(x)^{\prime}B(x)\) invertible. Hence \(\Pi_x\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{col}B(x)\), with
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(I_T-\Pi_x)=T-J.
 \tag{7}
\]
At an SC law, \(a(x)=B(x)w(x)\). Differentiating \(\Pi_xB(x)=B(x)\) along an arbitrary regular path gives
\[
 \dot\Pi_x B(x)+\Pi_x\dot B(x)=\dot B(x),
 \qquad
 \dot\Pi_x B(x)w(x)=(I_T-\Pi_x)\dot B(x)w(x).
 \tag{8}
\]
Since \(r_{\mathrm{SC},x}=(I_T-\Pi_x)a(x)\), equations (8) and \(a(x)=B(x)w(x)\) yield
\[
 \dot r_{\mathrm{SC},x}
 =(I_T-\Pi_x)\{\dot a(x)-\dot B(x)w(x)\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus, if \(\widetilde R_{C,x}\) is any vector of Riesz representers for the coordinates of \(\dot a(x)-\dot B(x)w(x)\), then
\[
 R_{\mathrm{SC},x}=(I_T-\Pi_x)\widetilde R_{C,x}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_0[R_{\mathrm{SC},x}R_{\mathrm{SC},x}^{\prime}]
 \le T-J.
 \tag{11}
\]
Equality holds exactly when the covariance form of the residualized coordinates in (10) is positive definite on \(\operatorname{range}(I_T-\Pi_x)\); this is the stated corresponding nondegeneracy condition. Notice that (11) is a rank conclusion, not an added rank premise.

We next identify the observed projections of the causal branches. For PT, \(r_{\mathrm{PT},x}=0\) is equivalent to \(A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)=0\), and (4) makes this equivalent to
\[
 D(x)=c(x)\mathbf 1_J
 \quad\text{for some scalar }c(x).
 \tag{12}
\]
Every compatible PT law satisfies (12): no anticipation makes donor final changes observable, and conditional parallel trends equates each of them to \(\delta_1^0(x)\). Conversely, if an observed law satisfies (12), retain every observed coordinate and choose the unobserved treated-group variable \(Y_T(0)\), conditionally on \(X=x\), to have mean \(\mu_{1,T-1}(x)+c(x)\); before period \(T\), impose no anticipation. This constructs a compatible full-data PT extension without changing the observed law. Thus the observed projection of the PT branch is locally the zero set of its profiled restriction.

For SC, the rank assumption makes \(w(x)\) unique, and
\[
 r_{\mathrm{SC},x}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (I_T-\Pi_x)a(x)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a(x)=B(x)w(x).
 \tag{13}
\]
The first row of (13) is \(1=\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}w(x)\). Under no anticipation, the remaining rows are the preperiod restrictions in \(\mathrm{ass{:}synthetic\text{-}mean}\). Conversely, from an observed law satisfying (13), retain the observed coordinates and choose the missing treated-group \(Y_T(0)\), conditionally on \(X=x\), to have mean
\[
 \sum_{g=2}^{J+1}w_g(x)\mu_{g,T}(x).
 \tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) construct a compatible full-data SC extension satisfying the final-period restriction. The rank margin makes \(w(x)\) a smooth function of the observed conditional means. Hence the observed projection of the SC branch is locally the zero set of its profiled restriction.

We now prove the tangent-space equality. If \(s\) is the score of a branch-preserving quadratic-mean differentiable submodel, differentiating \(r_{b,x}=0\) and applying \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) yields
\[
 E_0[R_{b,x}s]=0
 \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal X.
 \tag{15}
\]
This proves one inclusion. For the converse, use \(\mathrm{ass{:}normal\text{-}rank}\) and the retained-coordinate convention in the definition of \(R_b\) to choose, throughout a neighborhood of \(P_0\), a maximal independent coordinate vector \(r_b^{\ast}\) with gradient vector \(R_b^{\ast}\). The other displayed coordinates impose no additional local equation. Linear independence in \(L^2(P_{0,O})\) implies that there are bounded, mean-zero functions \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) for which
\[
 M=E_0[R_b^{\ast}v^{\prime}]
 \quad\text{is nonsingular},
 \qquad v=(v_1,\ldots,v_m)^{\prime}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, the Gram matrix of \(R_b^{\ast}\) is positive definite; truncating and recentering its coordinates gives bounded functions whose cross-moment matrix converges to that Gram matrix, so a sufficiently late truncation satisfies (16).

Let \(s\) first be bounded, mean zero, and satisfy (15). Define the finite-dimensional exponential-tilt chart
\[
 \frac{dP_{O,\varepsilon,c}}{dP_{0,O}}(o)
 =\frac{\exp\{\varepsilon s(o)+c^{\prime}v(o)\}}
 {E_0[\exp\{\varepsilon s+c^{\prime}v\}]},
 \qquad
 F(\varepsilon,c)=r_b^{\ast}(P_{O,\varepsilon,c}).
 \tag{17}
\]
Boundedness in (17), finite support of \(X\) and \(G\), the positive cell margins, and the fourth-moment bound justify differentiation of every conditional mean entering \(F\). Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\), (15), and (16) give
\[
 F(0,0)=0,
 \qquad
 \partial_cF(0,0)=M,
 \qquad
 \partial_\varepsilon F(0,0)=E_0[R_b^{\ast}s]=0.
 \tag{18}
\]
Differentiability gives
\[
 F(\varepsilon,c)=F(\varepsilon,0)+Mc+\zeta(\varepsilon,c),
 \qquad
 \|\zeta(\varepsilon,c)\|=o(|\varepsilon|+\|c\|),
 \tag{19}
\]
with the derivative of \(\zeta\) in \(c\) arbitrarily small on a sufficiently small neighborhood. On a ball \(\|c\|\le C|\varepsilon|\), the map
\[
 c\longmapsto-M^{-1}\{F(\varepsilon,0)+\zeta(\varepsilon,c)\}
 \tag{20}
\]
is a contraction of that ball into itself. Its fixed point \(c(\varepsilon)\) satisfies \(F(\varepsilon,c(\varepsilon))=0\). The last equality in (18) gives \(F(\varepsilon,0)=o(\varepsilon)\), whence
\[
 c(0)=0,
 \qquad
 c(\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon).
 \tag{21}
\]
The corrected path therefore has score \(s\). Its full profiled restriction vanishes by the locally fixed maximal coordinate system, and the extensions in (12)--(14) lift it to a full-data path in branch \(b\). The fixed interior support, moment, and rank margins at \(P_0\) ensure that a sufficiently short segment remains in the branch model.

For an arbitrary \(s\in\mathcal H_{P_0}\) satisfying (15), let \(\widetilde s_k\) be its truncation at level \(k\), recentered to have mean zero, and set
\[
 s_k=\widetilde s_k-v^{\prime}M^{-1}E_0[R_b^{\ast}\widetilde s_k].
 \tag{22}
\]
Then \(s_k\) is bounded and mean zero, (16) gives \(E_0[R_b^{\ast}s_k]=0\), and Cauchy--Schwarz together with \(\widetilde s_k\to s\) in \(L^2(P_{0,O})\) gives
\[
 E_0[R_b^{\ast}\widetilde s_k]\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \|s_k-s\|_{P_0,2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{23}
\]
The bounded case puts every \(s_k\) in the branch tangent set, and \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\text{-}tangents}\) takes its \(L^2\)-closure. Combining (15) and (23) proves
\[
 \mathcal T_b(P_0)
 =\{s\in\mathcal H_{P_0}:E_0[R_{b,x}s]=0
       \text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \tag{24}
\]

There are finitely many retained normals, so elementary orthogonal-complement algebra applied to (24) gives
\[
 \mathcal T_b(P_0)^{\perp}
 =\operatorname{span}\{R_{b,x}:x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \tag{25}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\), \(E_0[G_bs]=\dot\theta_b(s)\) for every unrestricted score. Hence \(\mathrm{def{:}influence\text{-}classes}\) and (24)--(25) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal I_b(P_0)
 &=\{\phi\in\mathcal H_{P_0}:E_0[(\phi-G_b)s]=0
       \text{ for every }s\in\mathcal T_b(P_0)\}\\
 &=G_b+\mathcal T_b(P_0)^{\perp}
 =G_b+\operatorname{span}\{R_{b,x}:x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]

Finally, let
\[
 \Gamma_b=E_0[R_bR_b^{\prime}],
 \qquad
 u_b=E_0[R_bG_b].
 \tag{27}
\]
If \(c\in\ker(\Gamma_b)\), then \(E_0[(c^{\prime}R_b)^2]=0\), so \(c^{\prime}R_b=0\) almost surely and \(c^{\prime}u_b=0\). Thus \(u_b\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_b)\). For every coefficient vector \(a\), the Moore--Penrose projection identity gives
\[
 \|G_b+R_b^{\prime}a\|_{P_0,2}^2
 =\|G_b-R_b^{\prime}\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b\|_{P_0,2}^2
 +(a+\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b)^{\prime}
   \Gamma_b(a+\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b).
 \tag{28}
\]
The second term is nonnegative. Minimizing (28) over (26) proves
\[
 \phi_b
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}g_{b,x}
 -R_b^{\prime}\{E_0[R_bR_b^{\prime}]\}^{\dagger}
 E_0\!\left[R_b\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}g_{b,x}\right].
 \tag{29}
\]
Because (29) is the Hilbert-space projection itself, redundant displayed rows affect coefficient coordinates but not the represented function. Equations (5) and (11) additionally record the sharp dimensional ceilings \(J-1\) and \(T-J\) for the displayed PT and SC normal Grams, with equality under the respective residualized-coordinate nondegeneracy conditions.

 &=\dot\mu_{1,T}-w^{\prime}\dot\mu_{C,T}
   -\dot\mu_{1,T-1}+w^{\prime}\dot\mu_{C,T-1}\\
 &=L_{\mathrm{PT}}(w).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{13}
\]
The product-rule terms from differentiating \(\nu_x\) also coincide because the two conditional contrasts have the same value at the intersection. Restoring these terms and the factor \(\nu_x\), (13) produces a representer of both forms in (6). Therefore
\[
 \mathcal I_{\mathrm{PT}}(P_0)\cap\mathcal I_{\mathrm{SC}}(P_0)\ne\varnothing.
 \tag{14}
\]

For any \((a,d)\), \(\mathrm{def{:}union\text{-}qp}\) and the definition of \(h_x\) give
\[
 \psi_{\mathrm{PT},x}(a)-\psi_{\mathrm{SC},x}(d)=c(a,d)^{\prime}h_x.
 \tag{15}
\]
Since \(\Omega_x\) is positive semidefinite,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Omega_x^{1/2}c(a,d)=0
 &\Longleftrightarrow c(a,d)^{\prime}\Omega_xc(a,d)=0\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 E_0[\{\psi_{\mathrm{PT},x}(a)-\psi_{\mathrm{SC},x}(d)\}^2\mid X=x]=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Thus the QP constraint is exactly equality of the two stratum representations in \(L^2(P_{0,O}\mid X=x)\), and its objective is
\[
 E_0[\psi_{\mathrm{PT},x}(a)^2\mid X=x]
 =\ell_{\mathrm{PT}}(a)^{\prime}\Omega_x\ell_{\mathrm{PT}}(a).
 \tag{17}
\]
Feasibility follows from (13). The feasible set is closed and affine, and its image in the finite-dimensional span of \(h_x\) is a closed affine subspace. Orthogonal projection of zero onto that subspace gives a unique minimizing represented function. Redundant coordinates may yield several coefficient pairs, but the Euclidean minimum-norm tie rule defines \((a_x^U,d_x^U)\). The constant-rank and positive-spectrum assumptions ensure that the retained coordinate system is stable and that no zero eigenvalue is inverted.

Functions supported on distinct strata are orthogonal, and \(p_X(x)>0\). Hence the unconditional projection separates across strata:
\[
 \phi_U
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\psi_{\mathrm{PT},x}(a_x^U)
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\psi_{\mathrm{SC},x}(d_x^U),
 \qquad
 V_U(P_0)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_X(x)\min\mathrm{QP}_x.
 \tag{18}
\]

Finally, an estimator regular on \(\mathcal M_U\) is regular along every quadratic-mean differentiable submodel in either branch. Its regular influence component must therefore satisfy both branches' derivative covariance equations and belong to the common class in (14). Applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}semiparametric\text{-}convolution\text{-}bound}\) to finite collections of branch submodels and taking their closed span gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(\text{regular limit})
 \ge\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{PT}}(P_0)\cap\mathcal I_{\mathrm{SC}}(P_0)}E_0[\phi^2]
 =E_0[\phi_U^2]=V_U(P_0).
 \tag{19}
\]
Equation (18) shows that this lower-bound envelope is sharp in the local tangent experiment. The cited branchwise laws in \(\mathrm{lem{:}sun\text{-}xie\text{-}zhang\text{-}branchwise\text{-}laws}\) concern distinct maintained-branch procedures and variances. Equations (18)--(19) add the sharp local benchmark imposed by regularity to either identifying branch; they do not construct an attaining estimator, replace either branch variance, or establish a standard error or multiplier procedure.

%%% FIELD uniform-proposed-statement
Under the frozen definition of \(\mathfrak L_U(P_0)\), the requested uniform asymptotic-linear conclusion, and hence the requested four-part affirmative attainment result, is false in general. In particular, take \(P_0=P^{\star}\) and fixed margins that admit the witness with slack. For every sufficiently small nonzero \(\delta\), let \(Q_\delta\) retain the donor means and innovation laws of \(P^{\star}\) but replace the treated untreated mean path by \((-2\delta,-4\delta,-4\delta)\), and define
\[
 \Delta_\delta
 =\phi_U^{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_\delta;\mathrm{PT},P^{\star})
  -\phi_U^{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_\delta;\mathrm{SC},P^{\star}).
\]
Then \(Q_\delta\in\mathcal M_I\) and
\[
 E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta^2]\ge\frac{15}{2}\delta^2>0.
\]
For every sample size, finite-coordinate splicing produces two elements of \(\mathfrak L_U(P^{\star})\) having the same observed law at that coordinate but different admissible chart labels. No single estimator can have both chart expansions uniformly. This obstruction proves no affirmative sequencewise continuation theorem; it merely does not rule out such a theorem after imposing a common local neighborhood, common bounds on the QMD scores and remainders, and exclusion of arbitrary finite-coordinate replacements.

%%% FIELD uniform-proof
The obstruction is caused by the quantifiers in \(\mathrm{def{:}local\text{-}union\text{-}sequences}\), not by infeasibility of the center QP. Choose the fixed margins so that \(P^{\star}\) satisfies the overlap, fourth-moment, rank, spectral-gap, and variance conditions with strict slack. For a fixed sufficiently small \(\delta\ne0\), retain the donor means and every innovation law of \(P^{\star}\), but replace the treated untreated mean path by
\[
 (1/2+\delta)(-1,-2,-2)+(1/2-\delta)(1,2,2)
 =(-2\delta,-4\delta,-4\delta).
 \tag{1}
\]
Give the treated group the same additive final-period treatment effect as under \(P^{\star}\), and call the resulting law \(Q_\delta\). The treated and both donor untreated final changes remain zero, so conditional parallel trends holds. The donor matrix is unchanged, and (1) is reproduced by the unique weights
\[
 w_\delta=(1/2+\delta,1/2-\delta)^{\prime}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus the synthetic-mean restriction also holds. Group and stratum probabilities are unchanged, while all innovations remain bounded. The structurally zero Gram directions persist, and the nonzero eigenvalues and local-score variance are continuous in \(\delta\). The strict margins therefore persist for all sufficiently small \(|\delta|\). Hence \(Q_\delta\in\mathcal M_I\) and satisfies every coordinatewise margin in the local-sequence definition.

Fix an integer \(m\). Construct two triangular sequences. In each sequence set the law at coordinate \(m\) equal to \(Q_\delta\) and every other coordinate equal to \(P^{\star}\). Label coordinate \(m\) by PT in the first sequence and by SC in the second, using any admissible label elsewhere. Each sequence belongs to \(\mathfrak L_U(P^{\star})\): after its single exceptional coordinate it is identically \(P^{\star}\), so its QMD expansion holds eventually with score zero and remainder zero, and both possible coordinate laws obey the fixed margins. Thus, for every sample size \(m\), the supremum over the frozen sequence class contains two labeled copies of the same observed law \(Q_\delta\) at that coordinate.

For the no-covariate equal-share witness define the centered cell-mean score
\[
 z_{g,t}(O)=3\mathbf 1\{G=g\}\{Y_t-\mu_{g,t}\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Then \(E_{Q_\delta}[z_{g,t}]=0\) and, by the displayed innovation variances,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{Q_\delta}(z_{2,3})=3\sigma_{2,3}^2=\frac32,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{Q_\delta}(z_{3,3})=3\sigma_{3,3}^2=6.
 \tag{4}
\]
At \(P^{\star}\), \(\mathrm{prop{:}strict\text{-}three\text{-}group\text{-}witness}\) shows that the common score has final donor coefficients \((-1/2,-1/2)\). The PT chart extension freezes its normal coefficient at \(P^{\star}\), and the donor laws are unchanged, so its final donor coefficients at \(Q_\delta\) remain \((-1/2,-1/2)\). By contrast, the SC normal depends only on preperiod coordinates, while (2) makes the final donor coefficients of the unrestricted SC gradient \((-1/2-\delta,-1/2+\delta)\). Therefore, for
\[
 \Delta_\delta
 =\phi_U^{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_\delta;\mathrm{PT},P^{\star})
  -\phi_U^{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_\delta;\mathrm{SC},P^{\star}),
 \tag{5}
\]
the final-innovation component is \(\delta(z_{2,3}-z_{3,3})\). Every remaining component uses other group-period innovations. Disjoint group indicators and independence across periods make those components orthogonal to the displayed component. Equations (3)--(4) therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta^2]
 &\ge\delta^2\{\operatorname{Var}_{Q_\delta}(z_{2,3})
                 +\operatorname{Var}_{Q_\delta}(z_{3,3})\}\\
 &=\delta^2\left(\frac32+6\right)
 =\frac{15}{2}\delta^2>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{6}
\]
Both chart extensions in \(\mathrm{def{:}local\text{-}score\text{-}extension}\) are sums of mean-zero gradient and normal representers, so \(E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta]=0\).

Suppose one estimator satisfied the requested asymptotic-linear expansion uniformly over \(\mathfrak L_U(P^{\star})\) and every admissible chart label. At sample size \(m\), apply it to the two sequences just constructed and couple them through the identical iid sample from \(Q_\delta\). The estimator and the causal target on the left side are identical under the two labels. Subtracting the two expansions and their uniformly negligible remainders would imply
\[
 S_m:=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^m\Delta_\delta(O_i)=o_{Q_\delta}(1).
 \tag{7}
\]
By iid sampling and centering,
\[
 E_{Q_\delta}[S_m^2]=E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta^2]
 \ge\frac{15}{2}\delta^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
Moreover, \(\Delta_\delta\) is a bounded linear combination of finitely many Rademacher innovations. Expanding the fourth power of a sum of independent centered variables and retaining the terms whose indices occur in pairs gives
\[
 E_{Q_\delta}[S_m^4]
 =m^{-1}E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta^4]
  +3(1-m^{-1})E_{Q_\delta}[\Delta_\delta^2]^2,
 \tag{9}
\]
which is bounded uniformly in \(m\). In fact, if the bound in (9) is \(C\), then for every \(A>0\),
\[
 \sup_m E_{Q_\delta}[S_m^2\mathbf 1\{S_m^2>A\}]
 \le \frac{C}{A}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus \((S_m^2)_m\) is uniformly integrable. If (7) held, then for every fixed \(A\) and \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 E_{Q_\delta}[S_m^2\mathbf 1\{S_m^2\le A\}]
 \le \varepsilon+A\,Q_\delta(S_m^2>\varepsilon)\longrightarrow\varepsilon.
 \tag{11}
\]
First choose \(A\) so that the right side of (10) is arbitrarily small, then send \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) in (11). This proves \(E_{Q_\delta}[S_m^2]\to0\), contradicting (8).

No estimator can therefore possess both chart expansions uniformly over the frozen sequence class. Since asymptotic linearity is a required conjunct of the requested four-part construction, the entire affirmative result, including variance consistency and multiplier coverage, is false under the frozen definition. The branch-chart extensions used here are definitions that formulate the contradiction; this proof establishes no positive sequencewise continuation theorem. It merely leaves open whether one can prove such a result after replacing the frozen class by a genuinely uniform neighborhood with common score and remainder bounds and no arbitrary finite-coordinate replacements.
