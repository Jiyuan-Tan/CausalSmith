%%% FIELD tangent-normal-form-proof
Write \(E_0\) for \(E_{P_{0,O}}\), put \(G_b=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}g_{b,x}\), and stack the profiled restriction maps over the finitely many strata.  All expectations below are under \(P_{0,O}\).

We first profile the PT restriction.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}restriction\text{-}maps}\),
\[
 q_{\mathrm{PT},x}=\delta_1^0(x)\mathbf 1_J-D(x),
 \qquad
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}=I_J-J^{-1}\mathbf 1_J\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}\mathbf 1_J
 =\mathbf 1_J-J^{-1}\mathbf 1_J(\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}\mathbf 1_J)
 =0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Therefore, along every regular path, including paths with an arbitrary derivative \(\dot\delta_1^0(x)\) of the latent treated counterfactual change,
\[
 A_{\mathrm{PT}}\dot q_{\mathrm{PT},x}
 =A_{\mathrm{PT}}\{\dot\delta_1^0(x)\mathbf 1_J-\dot D(x)\}
 =-A_{\mathrm{PT}}\dot D(x).
 \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3), \(\mathrm{def{:}restriction\text{-}maps}\), and \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) show that the observed profiled restriction is \(-A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)\) and that its Riesz representer is \(R_{\mathrm{PT},x}\).  The matrix \(A_{\mathrm{PT}}\) is symmetric and idempotent, its null space is \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_J\}\), and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm{PT}})=J-1.
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(\widetilde R_{D,x}\) denotes the vector representing the derivative of \(D(x)\), then (3) gives \(R_{\mathrm{PT},x}=-A_{\mathrm{PT}}\widetilde R_{D,x}\).  Thus
\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_0[R_{\mathrm{PT},x}R_{\mathrm{PT},x}^{\prime}]
 \le \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm{PT}})=J-1.
 \tag{5}
\]
Under the nondegeneracy condition in the statement, the left side of (5) is \(J-1\), which proves the asserted exact-rank clause.  This qualification is necessary because a matrix projector of rank \(J-1\) can act on statistically degenerate mean gradients.

We next identify the observed projections of the causal branches.  For PT, \(r_{\mathrm{PT},x}=0\) is equivalent to \(A_{\mathrm{PT}}D(x)=0\), and (4) makes this equivalent to
\[
 D(x)=c(x)\mathbf 1_J
 \quad\text{for some scalar }c(x).
 \tag{6}
\]
Every compatible PT law satisfies (6), since no anticipation in \(\mathrm{ass{:}no\text{-}anticipation}\) makes donor final changes observable and \(\mathrm{ass{:}parallel\text{-}trends}\) equates them to the treated untreated change.  Conversely, if an observed law satisfies (6), retain every observed coordinate and choose the unobserved treated-group variable \(Y_T(0)\), conditionally on \(X=x\), to have mean \(\mu_{1,T-1}(x)+c(x)\).  Before period \(T\), impose the no-anticipation equalities.  This gives a compatible full-data PT extension without changing the observed law.  Hence the observed projection of the PT branch is locally exactly the zero set of its profiled restriction.

For SC, \(\mathrm{ass{:}sc\text{-}rank}\) implies that \(B(x)^{\prime}B(x)\) is invertible in every occupied stratum.  Thus \(w(x)=\{B(x)^{\prime}B(x)\}^{-1}B(x)^{\prime}a(x)\) is unique, and
\[
 r_{\mathrm{SC},x}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{I_T-B(x)[B(x)^{\prime}B(x)]^{-1}B(x)^{\prime}\}a(x)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a(x)=B(x)w(x).
 \tag{7}
\]
The first row in (7) is \(1=\mathbf 1_J^{\prime}w(x)\).  Under no anticipation, the remaining rows are precisely the preperiod restrictions in \(\mathrm{ass{:}synthetic\text{-}mean}\).  Conversely, from an observed law satisfying (7), retain the observed coordinates and choose the unobserved treated-group \(Y_T(0)\), conditionally on \(X=x\), to have mean
\[
 \sum_{g=2}^{J+1}w_g(x)\mu_{g,T}(x).
 \tag{8}
\]
Equations (7)--(8) give a compatible full-data SC extension satisfying the final-period mean restriction.  The rank margin makes \(w(x)\) a smooth function of the observed conditional means.  Hence the observed projection of the SC branch is locally exactly the zero set of its profiled restriction.

We now prove the tangent-space equality.  If \(s\) is the score of a branch-preserving quadratic-mean differentiable submodel, differentiating \(r_{b,x}=0\) and applying \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) yields
\[
 E_0[R_{b,x}s]=0
 \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal X.
 \tag{9}
\]
This proves one inclusion.  For the converse, use \(\mathrm{ass{:}normal\text{-}rank}\) and the retained-coordinate convention in the definition of \(R_b\) to select, throughout a neighborhood of \(P_0\), a maximal independent coordinate vector \(r_b^{\ast}\) with gradient vector \(R_b^{\ast}\).  The other displayed coordinates impose no additional local equation.  Because the coordinates of \(R_b^{\ast}\) are linearly independent in \(L^2(P_{0,O})\), there are bounded, mean-zero functions \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) for which
\[
 M=E_0[R_b^{\ast}v^{\prime}]
 \quad\text{is nonsingular},
 \qquad v=(v_1,\ldots,v_m)^{\prime}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the Gram matrix of \(R_b^{\ast}\) is positive definite; truncating and recentering its coordinates gives bounded functions whose cross-moment matrix converges to that Gram matrix, so a sufficiently late truncation satisfies (10).

Let \(s\) first be bounded, mean zero, and satisfy (9).  Define a finite-dimensional exponential-tilt chart by
\[
 \frac{dP_{O,\varepsilon,c}}{dP_{0,O}}(o)
 =\frac{\exp\{\varepsilon s(o)+c^{\prime}v(o)\}}
 {E_0[\exp\{\varepsilon s+c^{\prime}v\}]},
 \qquad
 F(\varepsilon,c)=r_b^{\ast}(P_{O,\varepsilon,c}).
 \tag{11}
\]
Boundedness in (11), the finite support of \(X\) and \(G\), the positive cell margins, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}fourth\text{-}moments}\) justify differentiation under every conditional mean entering \(F\).  Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\) and (9)--(10) give
\[
 F(0,0)=0,
 \qquad
 \partial_cF(0,0)=M,
 \qquad
 \partial_\varepsilon F(0,0)=E_0[R_b^{\ast}s]=0.
 \tag{12}
\]
For completeness, the needed local correction follows directly from (12).  Differentiability gives
\[
 F(\varepsilon,c)=F(\varepsilon,0)+Mc+\zeta(\varepsilon,c),
 \qquad
 \|\zeta(\varepsilon,c)\|=o(|\varepsilon|+\|c\|),
 \tag{13}
\]
with the derivative of \(\zeta\) in \(c\) arbitrarily small on a sufficiently small neighborhood.  On a ball \(\|c\|\le C|\varepsilon|\), the map
\[
 c\longmapsto-M^{-1}\{F(\varepsilon,0)+\zeta(\varepsilon,c)\}
 \tag{14}
\]
is then a contraction of that ball into itself.  Its fixed point \(c(\varepsilon)\) satisfies \(F(\varepsilon,c(\varepsilon))=0\).  Since the last equality in (12) gives \(F(\varepsilon,0)=o(\varepsilon)\), equations (13)--(14) also give
\[
 c(0)=0,
 \qquad
 c(\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon).
 \tag{15}
\]
Consequently the corrected path in (11) has score \(s\).  Its full profiled restriction vanishes by the locally fixed maximal coordinate system, and the extensions constructed in (6)--(8) lift it to a full-data path in branch \(b\).  The fixed interiority margins ensure that a sufficiently short segment of this path remains in the stated branch model.

Now let \(s\in\mathcal H_{P_0}\) satisfy (9), without boundedness.  Let \(\widetilde s_k\) be its truncation at level \(k\), recentered to have mean zero, and put
\[
 s_k=\widetilde s_k-v^{\prime}M^{-1}E_0[R_b^{\ast}\widetilde s_k].
 \tag{16}
\]
Each \(s_k\) is bounded and mean zero, (10) gives \(E_0[R_b^{\ast}s_k]=0\), and Cauchy--Schwarz plus \(\widetilde s_k\to s\) in \(L^2(P_{0,O})\) gives
\[
 E_0[R_b^{\ast}\widetilde s_k]\longrightarrow E_0[R_b^{\ast}s]=0,
 \qquad
 \|s_k-s\|_{P_0,2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{17}
\]
The bounded case puts every \(s_k\) in the branch tangent set, and \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\text{-}tangents}\) takes its \(L^2\)-closure.  Combining (9) and (17) proves
\[
 \mathcal T_b(P_0)
 =\{s\in\mathcal H_{P_0}:E_0[R_{b,x}s]=0
       \text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \tag{18}
\]

There are finitely many retained normals, so elementary orthogonal-complement algebra applied to (18) gives
\[
 \mathcal T_b(P_0)^{\perp}
 =\operatorname{span}\{R_{b,x}:x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \tag{19}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}normal\text{-}gradients}\), \(E_0[G_bs]=\dot\theta_b(s)\) for every unrestricted score.  Hence \(\mathrm{def{:}influence\text{-}classes}\) and (18)--(19) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal I_b(P_0)
 &=\{\phi\in\mathcal H_{P_0}:E_0[(\phi-G_b)s]=0
       \text{ for every }s\in\mathcal T_b(P_0)\}\\
 &=G_b+\mathcal T_b(P_0)^{\perp}
 =G_b+\operatorname{span}\{R_{b,x}:x\in\mathcal X\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{20}
\]

Finally, let
\[
 \Gamma_b=E_0[R_bR_b^{\prime}],
 \qquad
 u_b=E_0[R_bG_b].
 \tag{21}
\]
If \(c\in\ker(\Gamma_b)\), then \(E_0[(c^{\prime}R_b)^2]=0\), so \(c^{\prime}R_b=0\) almost surely and \(c^{\prime}u_b=0\).  Thus \(u_b\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_b)\).  The function \(R_b^{\prime}\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b\) is therefore the orthogonal projection of \(G_b\) onto the normal span.  For every coefficient vector \(a\), Pythagoras gives
\[
 \|G_b+R_b^{\prime}a\|_{P_0,2}^2
 =\|G_b-R_b^{\prime}\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b\|_{P_0,2}^2
 +(a+\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b)^{\prime}
   \Gamma_b(a+\Gamma_b^{\dagger}u_b).
 \tag{22}
\]
The second term is nonnegative.  Minimizing (22) over the affine class (20) proves
\[
 \phi_b
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}g_{b,x}
 -R_b^{\prime}\{E_0[R_bR_b^{\prime}]\}^{\dagger}
 E_0\!\left[R_b\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}g_{b,x}\right].
 \tag{23}
\]
Because (23) is the Hilbert-space projection itself, adding or deleting redundant displayed rows changes coefficient coordinates but not the represented function.

The preceding argument is pointwise and would apply at a branch-interior law \(P\in\mathcal M_b\) if the corresponding profiled restriction-gradient rank were locally constant at that \(P\).  The frozen condition \(\mathrm{ass{:}normal\text{-}rank}\), however, is stated only at the intersection center \(P_0\).  Accordingly, (18)--(23) establish the formal claim at every such \(P_0\) without silently imposing a new branchwise rank assumption.
