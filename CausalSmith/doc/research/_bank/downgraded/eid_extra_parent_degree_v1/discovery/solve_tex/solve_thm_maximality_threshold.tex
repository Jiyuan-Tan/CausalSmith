%%% FIELD proof-thm-maximality-threshold
Fix an admissible pair \(n\geq 2\) and \(k\geq 0\), as required by \(\mathrm{ass:size}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:k\mbox{-}domain}\).  First suppose that
\[
n\geq 2k+4.
\]
Then \(\mathrm{thm:large\mbox{-}size\mbox{-}frontier}\), applied to this \(n\) and \(k\), gives
\[
D_k(n)=2^{k+1}.
\tag{1}
\]

Conversely, suppose that
\[
n<2k+4.
\tag{2}
\]
Set \(N=n-1\) and \(g=k+1\).  The size condition gives \(N\geq1\), and the index condition gives \(g\geq1\).  Moreover, (2) is equivalent to
\[
N=n-1<2(k+1)+1=2g+1.
\tag{3}
\]
By \(\mathrm{prop:joint\mbox{-}orientation\mbox{-}maximality\mbox{-}threshold}\), equation (3) implies the strict inequality
\[
\vartheta_{n-1,k+1}=\vartheta_{N,g}<2^g=2^{k+1}.
\tag{4}
\]
On the other hand, \(\mathrm{thm:orientation\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}bound}\), whose graph domain is \(\mathcal G_{n,k}\) from \(\mathrm{def:graph\mbox{-}class}\), yields
\[
D_k(n)\leq \vartheta_{n-1,k+1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (4) and (5) gives
\[
D_k(n)\leq\vartheta_{n-1,k+1}<2^{k+1}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (1) proves equality throughout the regime \(n\geq2k+4\), whereas (6) rules it out throughout the complementary admissible regime \(n<2k+4\).  Hence
\[
D_k(n)=2^{k+1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n\geq2k+4.
\]
Finally, (5) is only an upper bound below the threshold; therefore this argument neither identifies \(D_k(n)\) there nor asserts \(D_k(n)=\vartheta_{n-1,k+1}\), exactly as claimed.
