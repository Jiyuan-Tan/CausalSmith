%%% FIELD proof-real-root-lifting
Fix \(G=(V,D,B)\) and write \(s=(s_{ij}:i\leq j)\) for real symmetric covariance indeterminates, \(z\) for the queried value, and \(\ell=(\ell_f:f\in D)\) for real directed-coefficient indeterminates.  For each missing bidirected pair, let \(F_{ij}(\ell,s)\) be the polynomial obtained from the residual in the definition of \(F_{ij}\).  Define the first-order formula over ordered fields
\[
 \Phi_G(s,z)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
 \exists \ell\in\mathbb R^D\;
 \left[
   \ell_e=z
   \ \wedge\!
   \bigwedge_{\substack{i\ne j\\ i\leftrightarrow j\notin B}}
       F_{ij}(\ell,s)=0
 \right].                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Every polynomial in (1) has rational coefficients: the displayed definition of \(F_{ij}\) uses only addition and multiplication with coefficients \(0,1,-1\), while \(D\) and \(B\) merely select variables and equations.  By the definition of the real retained set,
\[
 \Phi_G(\Sigma,z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 z\in\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)                                  \tag{2}
\]
for every real symmetric \(\Sigma\) and every real \(z\).

Apply the effective real quantifier-elimination lemma to (1), taking \(\ell\) as the quantified block and \((s,z)\) as the free block.  It returns finitely many \(g_{ab}\in\mathbb Q[s,z]\) and \(\epsilon_{ab}\in\{-1,0,1\}\) such that
\[
 \Psi_G(s,z)
 =\bigvee_a\bigwedge_b
   \bigl(\operatorname{sign}g_{ab}(s,z)=\epsilon_{ab}\bigr)   \tag{3}
\]
is equivalent to \(\Phi_G(s,z)\) over every real closed field.  In particular, (2)--(3) give the computable characterization
\[
 \Psi_G(\Sigma,z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 z\in\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma).                                  \tag{4}
\]
Formula (3) is obtained from the original residual equations, without division and without selecting a zero-dimensional component.  Consequently (4) continues to hold for positive-dimensional \(\Lambda\)-fibers and for components contained in a denominator-zero locus of an intermediate rational reduction.

Now impose the antecedents of the theorem: \(d_e(G)<\infty\), a valid chart \(\mathfrak C_{G,e}\), and a successful output of the conditional geometric-resolution theorem.  Let \(\Sigma\succ0\) be real, belong to the covariance-model image, and lie outside \(\mathcal E_{G,e}\).  The exceptional-set-validity theorem gives
\[
 \{z\in\mathbb C:p_e(\Sigma,z)=0\}
   =\mathcal Z_e(G,\Sigma).                                    \tag{5}
\]
Because \(h_{G,e}(\Sigma)\ne0\), its squarefreeness factor is nonzero, so every root in (5) is simple.  Thus (5) identifies the complex coordinate projection, whereas the independent test (4) decides, root by root, whether a real root has a complete real supported extension.  No conclusion about real uniqueness is drawn from \(d_e(G)\) or from a Jacobian rank.

Suppose next that the entries of \(\Sigma\) are supplied by exact real-algebraic univariate representations and isolating data.  The conditional geometric-resolution theorem represents every coefficient of \(p_e\) by a rational covariance circuit.  All certificate denominators are nonzero at \(\Sigma\), so these circuits can be evaluated exactly in the ordered real-algebraic field generated by the entries of \(\Sigma\).  After multiplying by a known nonzero common denominator, the real-algebraic-sampling lemma applied to the resulting univariate polynomial enumerates a point on every connected component of its real zero set.  Squarefreeness makes that zero set finite, hence its connected components are precisely its individual real roots.  Exact sign determination orders the roots and removes duplicate encodings.  Rational isolating intervals are obtained by exact sign bisection; termination follows because the finite set of distinct roots has positive pairwise distance and \(\mathbb Q\) is dense in \(\mathbb R\).

For an enumerated real root \(z\), exact sign determination evaluates every atom in (3).  If \(\Psi_G(\Sigma,z)\) is false, the root representation, its isolating interval, and the evaluated sign vector form a finite rejecting record.  If it is true, define the polynomial in the directed coordinates
\[
 Q_{\Sigma,z}(\ell)
 = (\ell_e-z)^2
   +\sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\i\leftrightarrow j\notin B}}
        F_{ij}(\ell,\Sigma)^2.                                 \tag{6}
\]
Over an ordered field a sum of squares is zero if and only if each summand is zero.  Hence, by (1),
\[
 Z_{\mathbb R}(Q_{\Sigma,z})\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Phi_G(\Sigma,z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 z\in\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma).                                  \tag{7}
\]
In the accepting case, apply the real-algebraic-sampling lemma to (6), over the ordered coefficient domain generated by \(\Sigma\) and \(z\).  It returns a real univariate representation of a point on every semialgebraically connected component of the zero set, irrespective of its dimension.  Select any returned point \(\ell\).  Equations (6)--(7) prove that it is a complete real lift with \(\ell_e=z\).  Conversely, any point carried by an accepting record makes every summand in (6) vanish and therefore satisfies every original residual equation.  The candidate-root representation and interval, the exact sign vector from (3), and, in the accepting case, this real lift representation constitute \(\mathsf{LiftCert}\), and the certificate accepts if and only if \(z\in\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)\).

All algebraic data just described admit the stipulated rational-univariate encoding.  Indeed, a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q\) in characteristic zero has finitely many embeddings.  For algebraic \(a,b\), equality
\[
 a_i+c b_j=a_{i'}+c b_{j'}
\]
between two distinct conjugate pairs excludes at most one rational \(c\) when \(b_j\ne b_{j'}\), and is impossible when \(b_j=b_{j'}\) and \(a_i\ne a_{i'}\).  A rational \(c\) avoiding the finitely many excluded values therefore separates all embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(a,b)\).  The element \(a+cb\) then has as many conjugates as the degree of \(\mathbb Q(a,b)\), so \(\mathbb Q(a,b)=\mathbb Q(a+cb)\).  Iterating this argument flattens the coefficient tower generated by \(\Sigma\), \(z\), and the lift coordinates to one primitive real algebraic element; exact signs and an isolating interval select its intended real embedding.

It remains to establish the support and positivity assertion.  Let \(\Lambda\in\mathbb R^D\) be an accepted lift and put \(R=I_n-\Lambda\).  By acyclicity, a topological ordering makes \(\Lambda\) strictly triangular.  Thus \(\Lambda\) is nilpotent and \(R\) is invertible.  Define
\[
 \Omega=R^{\mathsf T}\Sigma R.                                \tag{8}
\]
For every nonzero real vector \(x\), invertibility gives \(Rx\ne0\), and therefore
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}\Omega x
   =(Rx)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(Rx)>0,                              \tag{9}
\]
because \(\Sigma\succ0\).  Hence \(\Omega\succ0\).  Direct expansion of the \((i,j)\) entry in (8), using the directed support of \(\Lambda\), gives
\[
 \Omega_{ij}
 =\sigma_{ij}
  -\sum_{p\in\operatorname{pa}_D(i)}\lambda_{pi}\sigma_{pj}
  -\sum_{q\in\operatorname{pa}_D(j)}\lambda_{qj}\sigma_{iq}
  +\sum_{p\in\operatorname{pa}_D(i)}
        \sum_{q\in\operatorname{pa}_D(j)}
        \lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qj}\sigma_{pq}
 =F_{ij}(\Lambda,\Sigma).                                     \tag{10}
\]
The accepting equations make (10) zero whenever \(i\leftrightarrow j\notin B\), exactly as required by the noise-support assumption.  Finally, (8) and invertibility of \(R\) yield \(\Sigma=R^{-\mathsf T}\Omega R^{-1}\).  Thus every accepted lift is a real supported SEM realization and has positive-definite recovered disturbance covariance, completing all assertions.

Work under the finite-output and valid-chart antecedents and on the successful certificate event.  In particular, the exceptional-set-validity theorem says that \(h_{G,e}\) is nonzero on the covariance-model coordinate ring.  Put
\[
 N=|D|+|B|+n
\]
and identify the real free-entry support space of \((\Lambda,\Omega)\) with \(\mathbb R^N\).  Define
\[
 \mathcal S_+
 =\{(\Lambda,\Omega)\in\mathbb R^N:\Omega\succ0\},           \tag{11}
\]
where only the diagonal and the entries indexed by \(B\) are free in \(\Omega\).  The set \(\mathcal S_+\) is nonempty because it contains \((0,I_n)\), and it is Euclidean open in the support space because strict positive definiteness is preserved under sufficiently small perturbations.

The set \(\mathcal S_+\) is Zariski dense in the corresponding complex source space.  To see this directly, let a complex polynomial vanish on \(\mathcal S_+\).  The latter contains a real open box about \((0,I_n)\).  Holding all but one coordinate fixed shows that the polynomial, as a univariate polynomial in the remaining coordinate, has infinitely many roots and is zero.  Iterating over the \(N\) coordinates shows that every coefficient is zero.

Let
\[
 \Phi_G(\Lambda,\Omega)
 =(I_n-\Lambda)^{-\mathsf T}\Omega(I_n-\Lambda)^{-1}.          \tag{12}
\]
Acyclicity makes \(\Lambda\) nilpotent after a topological ordering, so each inverse in (12) is the finite sum \(I_n+\Lambda+\cdots+\Lambda^{n-1}\); hence \(\Phi_G\) is polynomial on source space.  Properness of \(\mathcal E_{G,e}\) on the model image says precisely that the source polynomial \(h_{G,e}\circ\Phi_G\) is not zero.  Zariski density of \(\mathcal S_+\) therefore supplies \((\Lambda_0,\Omega_0)\in\mathcal S_+\) such that
\[
 h_{G,e}\bigl(\Phi_G(\Lambda_0,\Omega_0)\bigr)\ne0.           \tag{13}
\]
With \(\Sigma_0=\Phi_G(\Lambda_0,\Omega_0)\), the definition of \(\mathcal M_G\) and (13) imply
\[
 \Sigma_0\in\mathcal M_G\setminus\mathcal E_{G,e}.           \tag{14}
\]

The model image is semialgebraic: by (12), it is the projection of the source set described by polynomial covariance equalities, the linear missing-bidirected equalities, and the strict principal-minor inequalities for \(\Omega\succ0\); the effective real quantifier-elimination lemma eliminates the source coordinates.  The complement of \(\mathcal E_{G,e}\) and every sign condition in the quantifier-free lifting formula are also semialgebraic.

For completeness, the root-count and root-label refinements can themselves be expressed semialgebraically.  Outside the leading-coefficient and squarefreeness factors contained in \(h_{G,e}\), \(p_e(\Sigma,\cdot)\) has fixed degree and only simple roots.  The assertion that \(z\) is its \(j\)-th real root is a first-order ordered-field formula: it states \(p_e(\Sigma,z)=0\) and that exactly \(j-1\) distinct real roots are smaller than \(z\).  Clearing the certified nonzero coefficient denominators and applying quantifier elimination shows that every ordered-root graph is semialgebraic.  Intersecting such a graph with the sign formula from the real-root-lifting theorem and projecting to the covariance coordinates shows that the locus on which the \(j\)-th root is extendable is semialgebraic as well.

Apply the semialgebraic-stratification lemma to a common finite refinement by the model image, the exceptional complement, the possible real-root counts, and these extendability loci, and then take connected components of its smooth strata.  The component through \(\Sigma_0\) is a connected smooth semialgebraic cell \(\mathcal C_{G,e}\subseteq\mathcal M_G\setminus\mathcal E_{G,e}\).  By construction, the number of simple real roots and the extension label of every increasingly ordered real root are constant on this cell.  This is exactly the regularity required in the definition of \(\mathcal C_{G,e}\).

Choose a smooth coordinate chart \(u:U\to u(U)\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) on this cell around \(\Sigma_0\), where \(s=\dim\mathcal C_{G,e}\).  If \(s>0\), choose \(\rho>0\) so small that the closed Euclidean ball \(\overline B(u(\Sigma_0),\rho)\) is contained in \(u(U)\), and set
\[
 \mathcal K_G
 =u^{-1}\!\left(\overline B(u(\Sigma_0),\rho)\right).         \tag{15}
\]
If \(s=0\), set \(\mathcal K_G=\{\Sigma_0\}\).  In either case \(\mathcal K_G\) is nonempty and compact, and it has nonempty relative interior in \(\mathcal C_{G,e}\), as required by the regular-class definition.

Finally fix any \(\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G\).  Since \(\Sigma\in\mathcal M_G\), the definition of the real covariance model supplies real supported \(\Lambda\) and \(\Omega\succ0\) with \(\Sigma=\Phi_G(\Lambda,\Omega)\).  Multiplication by \((I_n-\Lambda)^{\mathsf T}\) and \(I_n-\Lambda\) gives
\[
 \Omega=(I_n-\Lambda)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(I_n-\Lambda).        \tag{16}
\]
For every missing bidirected pair, the entrywise expansion already displayed in the definition of \(F_{ij}\) gives
\[
 F_{ij}(\Lambda,\Sigma)=\Omega_{ij}=0,                          \tag{17}
\]
where the last equality is the noise-support restriction built into \(\mathcal M_G\).  Thus the real number \(\lambda_e\) has a complete real supported extension satisfying all residual equations.  By the definition of \(\mathcal R_e\),
\[
 \lambda_e\in\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma),                           \tag{18}
\]
so \(\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)\ne\varnothing\) throughout \(\mathcal K_G\).

%%% FIELD proof-derived-root-separation
Let \(d=d_e(G)\).  Since \(p_e\) is a minimal polynomial, \(d\geq1\), and on the successful conditional output write its specialization as
\[
 p_e(\Sigma,t)=t^d+\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}c_a(\Sigma)t^a.              \tag{19}
\]
Each \(c_a\) is represented by a rational covariance circuit.  By the definitions of the regular class and exceptional set, \(\mathcal K_G\subseteq\mathcal M_G\setminus\mathcal E_{G,e}\).  Hence every denominator, specialized-leading-coefficient factor, and squarefreeness factor occurring in the successful certificate is nonzero on \(\mathcal K_G\).  Each factor is continuous there, so compactness gives a positive minimum for its absolute value.  Numerator circuits are continuous and bounded on \(\mathcal K_G\).  Consequently there is \(M<\infty\) such that
\[
 \max_{0\leq a<d}|c_a(\Sigma)|\leq M
 \qquad(\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G).                               \tag{20}
\]

If \(z\) is a root of (19) and \(|z|>1+M\), then
\[
 |z|^d
 =\left|\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}c_a(\Sigma)z^a\right|
 \leq M\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}|z|^a
 =M\frac{|z|^d-1}{|z|-1}
 <|z|^d,                                                        \tag{21}
\]
a contradiction.  Thus every complex root lies in the fixed disk \(|z|\leq1+M\).  The incidence set
\[
 \mathscr Z
 =\{(\Sigma,z)\in\mathcal K_G\times\mathbb C:
       p_e(\Sigma,z)=0\}                                      \tag{22}
\]
is closed in a compact set and therefore compact.

The nonvanishing squarefreeness certificate says that \(p_e(\Sigma,\cdot)\) and \(\partial_t p_e(\Sigma,\cdot)\) have no common root for any \(\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G\).  Hence \(\partial_t p_e\) is nonzero on \(\mathscr Z\), and continuity and compactness give
\[
 \eta
 =\min_{(\Sigma,z)\in\mathscr Z}
       |\partial_t p_e(\Sigma,z)|>0.                            \tag{23}
\]

Suppose, contrary to the assertion, that no uniform root-separation constant exists.  There are then \(\Sigma_\nu\in\mathcal K_G\) and distinct roots \(z_\nu,w_\nu\) of \(p_e(\Sigma_\nu,\cdot)\) such that \(|z_\nu-w_\nu|\to0\).  The disk bound and compactness permit passage to a subsequence for which
\[
 \Sigma_\nu\to\Sigma_*,\qquad z_\nu\to z_*,\qquad w_\nu\to z_*.
\]
For \(z\ne w\), define the polynomial divided difference
\[
 \mathfrak q(\Sigma,z,w)
 =\frac{p_e(\Sigma,z)-p_e(\Sigma,w)}{z-w}.
\]
It extends polynomially to the diagonal with
\(\mathfrak q(\Sigma,z,z)=\partial_t p_e(\Sigma,z)\).  Since both \(z_\nu\) and \(w_\nu\) are roots, \(\mathfrak q(\Sigma_\nu,z_\nu,w_\nu)=0\).  Taking the limit gives
\[
 p_e(\Sigma_*,z_*)=0,
 \qquad
 \partial_t p_e(\Sigma_*,z_*)=0,                               \tag{24}
\]
contradicting (23).  Therefore some \(\gamma>0\) separates every pair of distinct complex roots uniformly over \(\mathcal K_G\).  By exceptional-set validity and real-root lifting, every retained real value is one of these complex roots.  The same \(\gamma\) therefore separates every pair of distinct retained real roots.

%%% FIELD proof-regular-finite-set-inference
Let \(q=n(n+1)/2\), fix an ordering \(\operatorname{vech}\) of the symmetric covariance entries, and put
\[
 W_r
 =\operatorname{vech}\!\left(X^{(r)}X^{(r)\mathsf T}-\Sigma\right).
\]
Then
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vech}(S_m-\Sigma)
 =m^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^m W_r.                                    \tag{25}
\]
The independent Gaussian sampling assumption gives \(\mathbb E_\Sigma W_r=0\).  Differentiating the Gaussian moment-generating function \(\exp(t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t/2)\) four times at zero yields
\[
 \mathbb E_\Sigma(X_iX_jX_kX_l)
 =\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{kl}
  +\sigma_{ik}\sigma_{jl}
  +\sigma_{il}\sigma_{jk}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_\Sigma(W_{r,ij},W_{r,kl})
 =\sigma_{ik}\sigma_{jl}+\sigma_{il}\sigma_{jk}
 =(V_\Sigma)_{ij,kl}.                                          \tag{26}
\]

Compactness of \(\mathcal K_G\) bounds the eigenvalues of \(\Sigma\), hence bounds all Gaussian moments of any fixed order uniformly over \(\mathcal K_G\).  In particular, \(\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}\mathbb E_\Sigma\|W_r\|^3<\infty\).  Taylor's formula for \(e^{ix}\), applied to each fixed \(u\in\mathbb R^q\), gives uniformly in \(\Sigma\)
\[
 \mathbb E_\Sigma
 \exp\!\left(\frac{i u^{\mathsf T}W_r}{\sqrt m}\right)
 =1-\frac{u^{\mathsf T}V_\Sigma u}{2m}+O(m^{-3/2}).            \tag{27}
\]
Taking the \(m\)-th power in (27) proves convergence of the characteristic function of (25) to
\(\exp(-u^{\mathsf T}V_\Sigma u/2)\), uniformly over \(\mathcal K_G\).

Here this characteristic-function conclusion is also uniform weak convergence.  Indeed, the second moment of (25) is \(\operatorname{tr}(V_\Sigma)\), uniformly bounded by compactness, so every sequence of its laws is tight.  From any sequence \(m_\nu\to\infty\), \(\Sigma_\nu\in\mathcal K_G\), pass to a subsequence with \(\Sigma_\nu\to\Sigma_*\) and then to a weakly convergent subsequence.  Equation (27) forces the limit characteristic function to be \(\exp(-u^{\mathsf T}V_{\Sigma_*}u/2)\).  This identifies the limit uniquely: after convolution with \(N(0,\varepsilon I_q)\), Fourier inversion applies because multiplication by \(\exp(-\varepsilon\|u\|^2/2)\) is integrable; the smoothed laws are equal, and letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) recovers the original laws.  Thus
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vech}(S_m-\Sigma)
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,V_\Sigma)                                \tag{28}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K_G\).  The same uniform second-moment bound and Markov's inequality give
\[
 \|S_m-\Sigma\|=O_{\mathbb P}(m^{-1/2})                        \tag{29}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K_G\).

Let \(\widehat\Sigma_m=\pi_G(S_m)\) whenever \(S_m\) lies in the projection tube.  Since \(\pi_G(\Sigma)=\Sigma\) on \(\mathcal K_G\), smoothness of \(\pi_G\), compactness, and (29) give the uniform expansion
\[
 \operatorname{vech}(\widehat\Sigma_m-\Sigma)
 =D\pi_G(\Sigma)\operatorname{vech}(S_m-\Sigma)
  +O_{\mathbb P}(\|S_m-\Sigma\|^2).                            \tag{30}
\]

Every coefficient circuit of \(p_e\) is smooth on a neighborhood of \(\mathcal K_G\), because its finitely many denominators have positive minima there.  The derived root-separation lemma, in particular (23), bounds \(|\partial_t p_e(\Sigma,r_j(\Sigma))|\) away from zero.  Taylor expansion of the equation \(p_e(\Theta,t)=0\), with the linear coefficient in \(t\) bounded away from zero and the quadratic remainder uniformly bounded, gives by contraction on a sufficiently small rectangle a unique smooth root branch through every retained root.  Implicit differentiation of
\[
 p_e(\Sigma,r_j(\Sigma))=0
\]
then gives, for every symmetric direction \(H\),
\[
 Dr_j(\Sigma)[H]
 =-\frac{D_\Sigma p_e(\Sigma,r_j(\Sigma))[H]}
         {\partial_t p_e(\Sigma,r_j(\Sigma))}.                  \tag{31}
\]
Increasing order, the separation margin \(\gamma\), and constancy of the extension labels on the connected regular cell give the stipulated common labeling \(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_J)\).  A finite cover of \(\mathcal K_G\) by the preceding root rectangles makes the first two derivatives uniformly bounded.  Combining the Taylor expansion of \(r\) with (28)--(30) yields
\[
 \sqrt m\{r(\widehat\Sigma_m)-r(\Sigma)\}
 \rightsquigarrow
 N\!\left(0,
 Dr(\Sigma)D\pi_G(\Sigma)V_\Sigma
 D\pi_G(\Sigma)^{\mathsf T}Dr(\Sigma)^{\mathsf T}
 \right),                                                       \tag{32}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K_G\).

We now construct the events in the statement.  All certificate factors are continuous and nonzero on \(\mathcal K_G\), and the regular-cell construction makes every root-extension label locally constant along the projected model stratum.  Compactness therefore supplies a relative neighborhood \(U\) of \(\mathcal K_G\) in that stratum on which the projection is defined, all conditional certificate circuits remain valid, and the same \(J\) branches are retained.  Define
\[
 \mathcal H_m
 =\left\{
 \begin{array}{l}
 S_m\text{ lies in the projection tube},\quad
 \widehat\Sigma_m\in U,\\[2pt]
 \displaystyle
 \max_{1\leq j\leq J}
 |r_j(\widehat\Sigma_m)-r_j(\Sigma)|<\gamma/3
 \end{array}
 \right\}.                                                      \tag{33}
\]
Uniform consistency in (29)--(30), uniform continuity of the root maps, and the positive certificate and separation margins imply
\[
 \inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}
 \Pr_\Sigma(\mathcal H_m)\longrightarrow1.                     \tag{34}
\]
On \(\mathcal H_m\), the real-root-lifting characterization and the finite-set-estimator definition give
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal R}_{e,m}
 =\{r_1(\widehat\Sigma_m),\ldots,r_J(\widehat\Sigma_m)\}.      \tag{35}
\]
The inequalities in (33) and the \(\gamma\)-separation give a bijective matching with \(\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)\).  The regular-cell-existence lemma gives \(J\geq1\), so (35) is nonempty.  For the matched finite sets,
\[
 d_H\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal R}_{e,m},
              \mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)\right)
 \leq\max_{1\leq j\leq J}
       |r_j(\widehat\Sigma_m)-r_j(\Sigma)|
 =O_{\mathbb P}(m^{-1/2})                                      \tag{36}
\]
uniformly by (32).  Under the stated empty-set convention, the complement of \(\mathcal H_m\) may give infinite distance, but (34) makes its probability uniformly negligible; hence (36) remains precisely the asserted uniform \(O_{\mathbb P}(m^{-1/2})\) statement.  Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}
 \Pr_\Sigma(\widehat{\mathcal R}_{e,m}=\varnothing)
 \leq
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}\Pr_\Sigma(\mathcal H_m^c)
 \longrightarrow0.                                             \tag{37}
\]

It remains to justify simultaneous branch neighborhoods.  Let \(s\) be the dimension of the selected smooth covariance stratum and put
\[
 W_\pi(\Sigma)
 =D\pi_G(\Sigma)V_\Sigma D\pi_G(\Sigma)^{\mathsf T}.           \tag{38}
\]
The matrix \(V_\Sigma\) is positive definite on symmetric-coordinate vectors.  Indeed, a nonzero such vector corresponds to a nonzero symmetric matrix \(H\), and (26) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_\Sigma(X^{\mathsf T}HX)
 =2\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma H\Sigma H)>0                       \tag{39}
\]
because \(\Sigma\succ0\).  Since \(\pi_G\) fixes its \(s\)-dimensional smooth stratum, \(D\pi_G\) is the identity on the tangent space and has image equal to that tangent space.  Thus \(D\pi_G\) has rank \(s\), and (38)--(39) imply \(\operatorname{rank}W_\pi=s\).  The smallest positive eigenvalue of \(W_\pi\) is bounded away from zero on \(\mathcal K_G\), so its Moore--Penrose inverse is continuous there.

Let \(\widehat W_{\pi,m}\) be the plug-in version of (38) at \(\widehat\Sigma_m\).  Equations (28)--(30), continuity, and diagonalization on the rank-\(s\) image give
\[
 m\,\operatorname{vech}(\widehat\Sigma_m-\Sigma)^{\mathsf T}
 \widehat W_{\pi,m}^{+}
 \operatorname{vech}(\widehat\Sigma_m-\Sigma)
 \rightsquigarrow\chi_s^2                                    \tag{40}
\]
uniformly: if \(Z\sim N(0,W_\pi)\), then on the positive-eigenvalue coordinates \(Z^{\mathsf T}W_\pi^+Z\) is the sum of \(s\) independent squared standard normals.  Let \(E_m\) be the joint covariance ellipsoid in the regular projected neighborhood obtained by replacing \(\Sigma\) in the left side of (40) by a candidate covariance and bounding the result by the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\chi_s^2\).  It follows that
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}\inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}
 \Pr_\Sigma(\Sigma\in E_m)\geq1-\alpha.                       \tag{41}
\]
For \(s=0\), take the degenerate ellipsoid on the locally constant projected point; (41) then holds with limiting coverage one.

As prescribed by the branch-neighborhood definition, set
\[
 \mathcal A_{m,j}=r_j(E_m),\qquad 1\leq j\leq J.                \tag{42}
\]
Whenever \(\Sigma\in E_m\), all inclusions \(r_j(\Sigma)\in\mathcal A_{m,j}\) hold simultaneously.  Therefore (41)--(42) imply
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}\inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}
 \Pr_\Sigma\!\left(
   r_j(\Sigma)\in\mathcal A_{m,j}
   \text{ for every }1\leq j\leq J
 \right)\geq1-\alpha.                                         \tag{43}
\]
This covers every retained branch and does not select among them.

Assume \(\mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)=\{r_1(\Sigma)\}\) throughout \(\mathcal K_G\).  On the events \(\mathcal H_m\) supplied by the regular finite-set inference theorem, the estimated retained set is bijectively matched to the population retained set.  Hence it has exactly one element, say \(\widehat r_m\), and
\[
 \inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K_G}
 \Pr_\Sigma\!\left(
   \widehat{\mathcal R}_{e,m}=\{\widehat r_m\}
 \right)\longrightarrow1.                                     \tag{44}
\]
Define \(\widehat r_m=0\) when the estimated set is not a singleton.  By (44), this extension changes the statistic only on an event whose probability tends to zero uniformly.

For singleton sets, Hausdorff distance is absolute error.  The Hausdorff-rate conclusion of the regular finite-set inference theorem therefore gives, on \(\mathcal H_m\),
\[
 |\widehat r_m-r_1(\Sigma)|
 =d_H\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal R}_{e,m},
              \mathcal R_e(G,\Sigma)\right)
 =O_{\mathbb P}(m^{-1/2})                                      \tag{45}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K_G\).  Together with (44), this proves uniform consistency of the extended statistic.

Taking the first coordinate in the joint branch limit of the regular finite-set inference theorem yields
\[
 \sqrt m\{\widehat r_m-r_1(\Sigma)\}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,W_{11}(\Sigma)),                          \tag{46}
\]
