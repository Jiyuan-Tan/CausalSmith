%%% FIELD finite-nonsingular-perturbation-statement
Let \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\in\mathbb C[x]\) be a square polynomial system, with the variables partitioned into blocks of sizes \(r_1,\ldots,r_b\), where \(\sum_i r_i=q\), and with block-degree bounds \(\deg_i(f_\ell)\leq\alpha_{\ell i}\). If \(S\) is a finite set of distinct affine common zeros at each of which the full \(q\)-by-\(q\) Jacobian is nonsingular, then
\[
 |S|\leq
 [X_1^{r_1}\cdots X_b^{r_b}]
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{q}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{b}\alpha_{\ell i}X_i\right).
\]
This conclusion does not require the original common-zero set to be zero dimensional.
%%% FIELD finite-nonsingular-perturbation-proof
Put
\[
 C=[X_1^{r_1}\cdots X_b^{r_b}]
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{q}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{b}\alpha_{\ell i}X_i\right).
\tag{16}
\]
The assertion is immediate if \(S=\varnothing\), so suppose that \(S\ne\varnothing\), fix \(z\in S\), and write \(J_f(z)\) for the full Jacobian. Since \(\det J_f(z)\ne0\), its determinant expansion contains a nonzero term. Thus there is a permutation \(\pi\) of the \(q\) scalar variables such that
\[
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{q}
 \frac{\partial f_\ell}{\partial x_{\pi(\ell)}}(z)\ne0.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(x_{\pi(\ell)}\) belongs to block \(i(\ell)\), then (17) implies \(\alpha_{\ell,i(\ell)}\geq1\). The permutation uses exactly \(r_i\) variables from block \(i\). Hence the choices \(X_{i(\ell)}\) in the product in (16) contribute a strictly positive summand to its indicated coefficient, and therefore
\[
 C>0.
\tag{18}
\]

Homogenize each \(f_\ell\) separately in every block to multidegree \(\alpha_\ell=(\alpha_{\ell1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell b})\) on
\[
 X=\prod_{i=1}^{b}\mathbb P^{r_i}_{\mathbb C}.
\tag{19}
\]
Let \(W_\ell\) be the vector space of multihomogeneous forms of multidegree \(\alpha_\ell\), and put \(\mathcal P=\prod_{\ell=1}^{q}\mathbb P(W_\ell)\). Every \(\alpha_\ell\) has a positive entry: otherwise the \(\ell\)-th Jacobian row in (17) would vanish. Each \(W_\ell\) is therefore base-point free on \(X\). Consider the universal incidence set
\[
 \mathcal Z=
 \{(x,[g_1],\ldots,[g_q])\in X\times\mathcal P:
       g_\ell(x)=0\text{ for every }\ell\}.
\tag{20}
\]
For fixed \(x\), each equation \(g_\ell(x)=0\) is one nontrivial linear condition on \(\mathbb P(W_\ell)\). Since \(\dim X=q\), it follows directly from (20) that
\[
 \dim\mathcal Z
 =q+\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}(\dim\mathbb P(W_\ell)-1)
 =\dim\mathcal P.
\tag{21}
\]
The projection \(\mathcal Z\to\mathcal P\) is projective. The elementary fiber-dimension argument applied to (21) gives a nonempty Zariski-open set \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal P\) on which every fiber is finite or empty: if the projection is dominant, the generic fiber has dimension \(\dim\mathcal Z-\dim\mathcal P=0\), while if it is not dominant, the generic fiber is empty. For completeness, the exceptional locus is algebraic because, after choosing multihomogeneous monomial bases, positive-dimensionality is the simultaneous rank drop of the sufficiently high multigraded Macaulay maps; Noetherianity reduces these rank conditions to finitely many maximal minors. Thus the finite-fiber locus is open, and (21) shows that it is nonempty.

The variety \(\mathcal P\) is irreducible. Consequently the proper algebraic set \(\mathcal P\setminus\mathcal U\) has empty interior in the complex topology, so systems in \(\mathcal U\) occur arbitrarily close to the homogenized tuple \((f_1,\ldots,f_q)\). Choose pairwise disjoint analytic coordinate balls \(B_z\), one about each \(z\in S\), all contained in the standard affine chart used before homogenization. The holomorphic implicit-function theorem and \(\det J_f(z)\ne0\) give a coefficient neighborhood \(\mathcal N\) of \((f_1,\ldots,f_q)\) such that every system in \(\mathcal N\) has one nonsingular common zero in each \(B_z\). Shrinking the balls and \(\mathcal N\) if necessary makes these continued zeros distinct. Choose
\[
 (g_1,\ldots,g_q)\in\mathcal U\cap\mathcal N.
\tag{22}
\]
The block multidegrees in (22) are the same prescribed \(\alpha_\ell\), its projective common-zero scheme is finite, and it has at least \(|S|\) distinct nonsingular zeros in the original affine chart. Projective-infinity zeros, if present, are also finite and do not remove any of these affine continuations.

The affine common-zero set of (22) is therefore finite. Applying \(\mathrm{lem:multihomogeneous\mbox{-}bezout\mbox{-}isolated\mbox{-}bound}\) to that finite system bounds its number of distinct affine common zeros by \(C\). The continued zeros are distinct, so \(|S|\leq C\), as claimed. Notice that the cited finite-system theorem is invoked only after (22) has made the whole projective system, and hence its affine chart, finite.
%%% FIELD orientation-upper-bound-statement-replacement
For every \((G,T,e)\in\mathcal G_{n,k}\) with \(d_e(G)<\infty\),
\[
 d_e(G)
 \leq \vartheta_{n-1,\,|D|-n+2}
 \leq \vartheta_{n-1,\,k+1}.
\]
More precisely,
\[
 d_e(G)\leq\min\!\left\{2^{|D|-n+2},\tau_{n-1}\right\},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_{n-1,k+1}\leq\min\!\left\{2^{k+1},\tau_{n-1}\right\}.
\]
These inequalities hold even when the full generic parameter fiber is positive dimensional. Consequently,
\[
 D_k(n)\leq\vartheta_{n-1,k+1}\leq\min\!\left\{2^{k+1},\tau_{n-1}\right\}.
\]
For six vertices this yields
\[
 D_2(6)\leq6,\qquad D_3(6)\leq8,\qquad D_4(6)\leq12,
 \qquad D_k(6)\leq24\quad\text{for every admissible }k\geq5.
\]
%%% FIELD orientation-upper-bound-proof
Fix \((G,T,e)\in\mathcal G_{n,k}\) and suppose \(d_e(G)<\infty\). Multiplying the defining covariance identity on the left by \((I_n-\Lambda)^{\mathsf T}\) and on the right by \(I_n-\Lambda\) gives
\[
 \Omega=(I_n-\Lambda)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(I_n-\Lambda).
\tag{23}
\]
Every disturbance coordinate is therefore in \(K_G(\lambda_D)\). The reverse inclusion follows from \(K_G\subseteq L\) and \(\lambda_D\subseteq L\), so
\[
 L=K_G(\lambda_D).
\tag{24}
\]
Choose \(U\subseteq\lambda_D\setminus\{\lambda_e\}\) maximal algebraically independent over \(K_G\), put \(F=K_G(U)\), and enumerate the remaining directed coordinates as \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_q)\), with \(\lambda_e\) among the \(y_a\). Since \(\lambda_e\) is algebraic over \(K_G\), maximality of \(U\), finiteness of \(\lambda_D\), and (24) imply that \(L/F\) is finite. It is separable because \(F\) has characteristic zero.

Let \(p\in K_G[t]\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\lambda_e\). A purely transcendental extension introduces no new factorization of a polynomial over its base field: indeed, \(K_G\) is algebraically closed in \(K_G(U)\), because a rational function in algebraically independent indeterminates that is algebraic over \(K_G\) is constant; the coefficients of a factor of \(p\) are symmetric functions of a subset of its roots and hence are algebraic over \(K_G\). Thus \(p\) remains irreducible over \(F\), and
\[
 [F(\lambda_e):F]
 =[K_G(\lambda_e):K_G]
 =d_e(G).
\tag{25}
\]

By \(\mathrm{thm:incidence\mbox{-}localization}\) and \(\mathrm{def:generic\mbox{-}fiber}\), extension first to \(K_G[U]\) and then to its fraction field \(F\) gives a surjective evaluation map
\[
 \rho:F[y_1,\ldots,y_q]\longrightarrow L
\tag{26}
\]
whose kernel is generated by the images of the missing-bidirected residuals \(F_{ij}\). Surjectivity holds because (24) says that \(F[y]\) contains all generators of \(L/F\), and a finite-dimensional domain over \(F\) is a field. For each \(a\), let \(g_a\in F[t]\) be the minimal polynomial of \(y_a\). Since \(g_a(y_a)\in\ker\rho\), there are \(c_{a,ij}\in F[y]\) such that, as an identity in the ambient polynomial ring,
\[
 g_a(y_a)=
 \sum_{i\leftrightarrow j\notin B}c_{a,ij}(y)F_{ij}(y).
\tag{27}
\]
Differentiate (27) in the free cotangent space \(\Omega_{F[y]/F}\otimes_{F[y],\rho}L\). Since every residual vanishes under \(\rho\), this gives
\[
 g_a'(y_a)\,dy_a
 =\sum_{i\leftrightarrow j\notin B}c_{a,ij}(y)\,dF_{ij}.
\tag{28}
\]
Separability gives \(g_a'(y_a)\ne0\). Hence the evaluated residual differentials span every \(dy_a\), and the residual Jacobian with respect to \(y\) has rank \(q\). Select distinct residuals \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\) whose Jacobian determinant
\[
 \Delta=\det\left(\frac{\partial f_\ell}{\partial y_a}\right)_{\ell,a=1}^{q}
\tag{29}
\]
is nonzero in \(L\).

Put \(E=F(\lambda_e)\). By (25), \(E/F\) has exactly \(d_e(G)\) embeddings into an algebraic closure \(\overline F\). Every such embedding extends to an \(F\)-embedding of \(L\): for an embedding \(\eta:E\to\overline F\), the nonzero finite algebra \(L\otimes_{E,\eta}\overline F\) has a maximal ideal, and its residue map gives the extension. Applying these extensions to \(y\) produces at least \(d_e(G)\) common zeros of \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\) over \(\overline F\). Their Jacobians are nonsingular because every field embedding sends the nonzero element \(\Delta\) to a nonzero element. Embeddings with different restrictions to \(E\) have different \(\lambda_e\)-coordinates.

We next transfer this finite selected collection, not the entire possibly positive-dimensional zero set, to complex coefficients. The extension \(L/F\) is finite separable, so choose a primitive element \(\theta\) and write \(L=F(\theta)\). Let \(Q\in F[t]\) be its monic minimal polynomial, let \(h=[L:F]\), and retain the degree \(d=d_e(G)=[E:F]\). Express all coordinates \(y_a\), the determinant \(\Delta\), and \(\lambda_e\) as rational polynomials in \(\theta\). All coefficients and identities involved lie in a finitely generated integral \(\mathbb C\)-algebra \(A\subset F\). Localize \(A\) at the product of the finitely many denominators, \(\operatorname{disc}(Q)\), \(\operatorname{disc}(p)\), and the norms of those denominators and of \(\Delta\). These elements are nonzero by separability and (29). The resulting nonzero finitely generated \(\mathbb C\)-algebra has a closed point, whose residue field is \(\mathbb C\).

At such a specialization, \(Q\) has \(h\) distinct complex roots; all rational coordinate expressions are defined; the specialized residual identities still vanish; and the specialized Jacobian determinant is nonzero at every one of the \(h\) resulting points. Moreover, multiplication by \(\lambda_e\) on the \(F\)-vector space \(L\) has characteristic polynomial
\[
 \det(tI-m_{\lambda_e})=p(t)^{h/d}.
\tag{30}
\]
Indeed, every one of the \(d\) embeddings of \(E/F\) occurs exactly \([L:E]=h/d\) times among the embeddings of \(L/F\). Identity (30) survives the specialization, while the inverted discriminant of \(p\) makes its \(d\) roots distinct. Therefore the specialized square system over \(\mathbb C\) has a selected set of \(d=d_e(G)\) distinct nonsingular zeros with pairwise distinct \(\lambda_e\)-coordinates.

Partition \(y\) into its nonempty blocks indexed by child vertices, and let \(r_v\) be the number of remaining coordinates in the block indexed by \(v\). Then
\[
 1\leq r_v\leq\operatorname{indeg}_D(v).
\tag{31}
\]
The formula defining \(F_{ij}\) is affine-linear in each of its two child blocks. After the coordinates in \(U\) have been absorbed into \(F\), a selected residual meeting one remaining block is unary, and one meeting two remaining blocks has block-degree at most one in each. Decompose the bipartite equation--block incidence graph into connected components. A nonzero term in the determinant (29) is a perfect row--variable matching; every matched row and variable lie in the same incidence component. Hence every component has equally many scalar equations and scalar variables, and after permutation the Jacobian is block diagonal with a nonsingular square block for each component.

Let the component containing \(\lambda_e\) contain \(b\) variable blocks, \(u\) unary equations, and \(m_H\) binary equations. The projections of the \(d\) specialized zeros to this component remain distinct because their \(\lambda_e\)-coordinates are distinct, and the component Jacobian is nonsingular at each projection. If \(b>1\), the binary equations form a connected simple graph \(H\): connectedness is the definition of the component after unary leaves are ignored, and simplicity holds because an unordered child pair supplies only one residual polynomial. If \(b=1\), let \(H\) be the one-vertex graph. Squareness gives
\[
 \sum_{v\in V(H)}r_v=u+m_H.
\tag{32}
\]

Apply the paper-owned perturbation result \(\mathrm{lem:finite\mbox{-}nonsingular\mbox{-}perturbation\mbox{-}bound}\) to these \(d\) selected nonsingular complex zeros. Its perturbation stays within the same unary or binary block multidegrees, makes the entire multiprojective system finite, continues the selected zeros in their affine charts, and only then invokes the cited finite-system B\'ezout theorem. Consequently,
\[
 d_e(G)\leq
 [\prod_vX_v^{r_v}]
 \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{u}X_{v(\ell)}\right)
 \left(\prod_{\{v,w\}\in E(H)}(X_v+X_w)\right).
\tag{33}
\]
Write \(u_v\) for the number of unary equations in block \(v\) and put \(s_v=r_v-u_v\). The coefficient in (33) is positive because it bounds a nonempty selected set, so every \(s_v\geq0\). After removing the forced unary powers, (33) is exactly the prescribed-indegree orientation coefficient \(c_H(s)\). Therefore
\[
 d_e(G)\leq c_H(s).
\tag{34}
\]

The root of the supplied arborescence has no incoming directed edge: any such edge, together with the directed tree path from the root to its tail, would form a directed cycle, contradicting \(\mathrm{ass:acyclic}\). Thus the root supplies no nonempty child block and
\[
 b\leq n-1.
\tag{35}
\]
For \(b>1\), the cyclomatic number of \(H\) is \(g_H=m_H-b+1\). Equations (31)--(32), nonnegativity of \(u\), and the incoming tree edge at every nonroot vertex give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_H
 &=\sum_{v\in V(H)}r_v-u-b+1\\
 &\leq\sum_{v\in V(H)}\bigl(\operatorname{indeg}_D(v)-1\bigr)+1\\
 &\leq\sum_{v\ne\operatorname{root}(T)}
       \bigl(\operatorname{indeg}_D(v)-1\bigr)+1\\
 &=|D|-n+2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
The omitted summands are nonnegative because every nonroot vertex has its incoming edge from \(T\). If \(b=1\), set \(g_H=0\); then the same inequality follows from \(|D|\geq|T|=n-1\). By (34)--(36) and \(\mathrm{def:joint\mbox{-}orientation\mbox{-}frontier}\),
\[
 d_e(G)\leq\vartheta_{n-1,\,|D|-n+2}.
\tag{37}
\]

Since \(T\subseteq D\), \(|T|=n-1\), and \(\mathrm{ass:excess\mbox{-}budget}\) holds,
\[
 |D|-n+2=|D\setminus T|+1\leq k+1.
\tag{38}
\]
Monotonicity and the two bounds in \(\mathrm{lem:joint\mbox{-}orientation\mbox{-}frontier}\) now yield
\[
 d_e(G)
 \leq\vartheta_{n-1,\,|D|-n+2}
 \leq\vartheta_{n-1,k+1}
 \leq\min\{2^{k+1},\tau_{n-1}\},
\tag{39}
\]
and, before using (38),
\[
 d_e(G)\leq\min\{2^{|D|-n+2},\tau_{n-1}\}.
\tag{40}
\]
Maximizing (39) over the finite-degree members of \(\mathcal G_{n,k}\) proves
\[
 D_k(n)\leq\vartheta_{n-1,k+1}.
\tag{41}
\]
Only the finite selected conjugates of the queried component were perturbed and counted; no zero-dimensionality of the full generic parameter fiber was used.

Finally, for \(n=6\), \(\mathrm{lem:joint\mbox{-}orientation\mbox{-}frontier}\) gives \(\vartheta_{5,3}=6\), \(\vartheta_{5,4}=8\), \(\vartheta_{5,5}=12\), and \(\vartheta_{5,g}=24\) for \(g\geq6\). Substitution of \(g=k+1\) into (41) gives
\[
 D_2(6)\leq6,\qquad D_3(6)\leq8,\qquad D_4(6)\leq12,
 \qquad D_k(6)\leq24\quad(k\geq5),
\]
for every admissible \(k\).
