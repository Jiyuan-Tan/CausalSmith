%%% FIELD proof-thm-fpt-minimal-polynomial
Put
\[
r=|D\setminus T|.
\]
The integer \(r\) is read directly from the supplied graph, so the alternatives \(r=0\) and \(r>0\) are deterministic, mutually exclusive, and exhaustive.

Suppose first that \(r=0\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:tree\mbox{-}class\mbox{-}algorithm\mbox{-}transfer}\), \((G,T,e)\) belongs to exactly the published rooted-tree mixed-graph class, including arbitrary bidirected support.  Run the transferred tree procedure with failure tolerance \(\delta\).  If \(d_e(G)<\infty\), the cited transfer gives
\[
d_e(G)\in\{1,2\}
\]
and states that the returned list \((\rho_i)_{i=1}^{d_e(G)}\) exhausts the generic complex values of the queried coordinate.  Hence the output prescribed by \(\mathrm{def:certificate\mbox{-}algorithm}\) is
\[
(\operatorname{tree},d_e(G),(\rho_i)_{i=1}^{d_e(G)}).
\]
If \(d_e(G)=\infty\), the same transfer states that the procedure reports the infinite case, so the prescribed output is \((\operatorname{tree},\infty)\).  Thus the first two rows of the claimed display are correct, with error probability at most \(\delta\).

It remains to prove correctness of the positive-excess branch.  Assume \(r>0\), and enumerate the source coordinates as
\[
u=(\lambda_D,\omega_B,\omega_{\mathrm{diag}})=(u_1,\ldots,u_N),
\qquad N=|D|+|B|+n.
\]
By the definition of \(L\),
\[
L=\mathbb C(u_1,\ldots,u_N).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:acyclic}\) supplies a topological ordering in which \(\Lambda\) is strictly triangular.  Therefore \(\Lambda^n=0\), and multiplication of the finite geometric sum gives
\[
(I_n-\Lambda)^{-1}=I_n+\Lambda+\cdots+\Lambda^{n-1}.
\]
Each entry of this inverse is consequently a polynomial of degree at most \(n-1\) in \(\lambda_D\).  Since an entry of \(\Omega\) is either zero or linear in \((\omega_B,\omega_{\mathrm{diag}})\), the covariance parametrization in \(\mathrm{def:generic\mbox{-}fiber}\) has coordinates
\[
s_{ij}(u):=\phi_G(\sigma_{ij})
=\bigl[(I_n-\Lambda)^{-\mathsf T}\Omega(I_n-\Lambda)^{-1}\bigr]_{ij},
\qquad i\leq j,
\]
of total degree at most \(2n-1\).  The displayed finite matrix products give arithmetic circuits of size polynomial in \(n\) for all the \(s_{ij}\).

Let \(s=(s_{ij}:i\leq j)\), let
\[
J_0=\frac{\partial s}{\partial u},
\]
and let \(J_1\) be obtained by adjoining to \(J_0\) the row \(\partial\lambda_e/\partial u\).  Entries of \(J_0\) have degree at most \(2n-2\), while the added row has degree zero.

We next derive the rank criterion used in \(\mathrm{def:certificate\mbox{-}algorithm}\).  Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\in L\), put \(F=\mathbb C(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\), and choose a maximal algebraically independent subfamily \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\) of the \(a_i\).  Then \((b_1,\ldots,b_q)\) is a transcendence basis of \(F/\mathbb C\).  Extend it by \(c_1,\ldots,c_\ell\) to a transcendence basis of \(L/\mathbb C\).  The finitely generated extension
\[
L/\mathbb C(b_1,\ldots,b_q,c_1,\ldots,c_\ell)
\]
is finite algebraic and, because the base field has characteristic zero, separable.

For each \(j\), the derivation on the rational-function field that sends \(b_j\) to \(1\) and every other \(b\)- and \(c\)-coordinate to zero extends uniquely through this finite separable extension.  Indeed, if \(v\) has minimal polynomial \(g(t)=\sum_h g_h t^h\), differentiating \(g(v)=0\) forces
\[
g'(v)\,dv=-\sum_h v^h\,dg_h,
\]
and separability gives \(g'(v)\ne0\).  Applying these extended derivations to a relation \(\sum_j q_j\,db_j=0\), where \(q_j\in L\), gives \(q_j=0\) for every \(j\).  Thus \(db_1,\ldots,db_q\) are linearly independent over \(L\).

Conversely, maximality of the \(b_j\) makes every \(a_i\) algebraic over \(\mathbb C(b_1,\ldots,b_q)\).  Differentiation of its separable minimal polynomial expresses \(da_i\) in the \(L\)-span of \(db_1,\ldots,db_q\).  Since the \(b_j\) occur among the \(a_i\), the two spans coincide, and hence
\[
\dim_L\operatorname{span}_L\{da_1,\ldots,da_m\}
=q
=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}F.
\]

Apply this identity first to the covariance polynomials and then to those polynomials together with \(\lambda_e\).  In the differential basis \(du_1,\ldots,du_N\), the rows of \(J_0\) and \(J_1\) are the coordinate vectors of the respective differentials.  The definition
\[
K_G=\mathbb C(s_{ij}:i,j\in V)
\]
therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{rank}_L J_0=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}K_G,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_L J_1
=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}K_G(\lambda_e).
\]
Adjoining one field element leaves transcendence degree unchanged exactly when that element is algebraic, and otherwise raises it by one.  Consequently
\[
\operatorname{rank}_L J_1=\operatorname{rank}_L J_0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda_e\text{ is algebraic over }K_G,
\]
whereas
\[
\operatorname{rank}_L J_1=\operatorname{rank}_L J_0+1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda_e\text{ is transcendental over }K_G.
\]
By the definition of \(d_e\), these are precisely the finite-degree and infinite-degree alternatives.

We now bound the symbolic error in computing the two generic ranks.  Acyclicity gives \(|D|\leq n(n-1)/2\), while the definition of bidirected support gives \(|B|\leq n(n-1)/2\).  Hence
\[
N=|D|+|B|+n\leq n^2.
\]
There are at most \(n(n+1)/2+1\leq n^2+1\) rows.  A determinant witnessing either generic rank thus has size at most \(n^2+1\) and total degree at most
\[
(n^2+1)(2n-2)\leq 2n^3
\]
for \(n\geq2\), using \(\mathrm{ass:size}\) through membership in \(\mathcal G_{n,k}\).  Choose each source coordinate independently and uniformly from a finite rational set \(S\) satisfying
\[
|S|\geq\left\lceil\frac{4n^3}{\delta}\right\rceil.
\]
Evaluation cannot increase a polynomial matrix's generic rank.  If an evaluated rank is smaller than its generic rank, a fixed nonzero maximal minor has vanished.  By \(\mathrm{lem:schwartz\mbox{-}zippel\mbox{-}bound}\), this has probability at most
\[
\frac{2n^3}{|S|}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
\]
for each Jacobian.  A union bound shows that both computed ranks equal their generic ranks with probability at least \(1-\delta\).  On the complementary symbolic-error event the dispatcher may use its fixed fallback tag; this does not enlarge the stated error probability.  Exact evaluation points, pivot positions, evaluated pivot minors, and the identity-test transcript constitute \(\mathcal J_{G,e}\) as specified in \(\mathrm{def:certificate\mbox{-}algorithm}\).

On the error-free event, suppose first that the augmented rank is larger.  The rank criterion proves that \(\lambda_e\) is transcendental over \(K_G\).  Thus the homomorphism
\[
K_G[t]\longrightarrow A_K,
\qquad t\longmapsto\lambda_e,
\]
is injective.  By \(\mathrm{thm:incidence\mbox{-}localization}\), \(A_K\) is an integral finitely generated \(K_G\)-algebra with fraction field \(L\).  The injection therefore induces a dominant morphism
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(A_K)\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_{K_G}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\dim A_K
=\operatorname{trdeg}_{K_G}\operatorname{Frac}(A_K)
=\operatorname{trdeg}_{K_G}L
\geq1,
\]
because \(L\) contains \(\lambda_e\), which is transcendental over \(K_G\).  Since \(A_K\) is integral, its spectrum is an irreducible positive-dimensional generic-fiber component.  The source-parametric incidence description, the augmented pivot minor, and the rank transcripts therefore give the dominance certificate \(\mathcal N_{G,e}\) required by its definition, and the fourth output is correct.

If instead the ranks agree, the rank criterion and the definition of \(d_e\) give \(d_e(G)<\infty\).  The already established \(\mathrm{thm:orientation\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}bound}\) gives
\[
d_e(G)\leq2^{|D|-n+2}\leq2^{k+1}.
\]
Indeed, because \(T\) is a spanning arborescence, \(|T|=n-1\), so \(|D|-n+2=r+1\leq k+1\).  This proves the third output.  This argument supplies only the displayed degree ceiling: neither the rank computation nor the orientation bound constructs the exact value \(d_e(G)\), the minimal polynomial \(p_e\), the circuit family \(\mathcal Q_{G,e}\), or the bad-locus circuit \(h_{G,e}\), in agreement with the theorem's negative output clause.

Finally, the tree branch has randomized polynomial construction time and certificate length by \(\mathrm{lem:tree\mbox{-}class\mbox{-}algorithm\mbox{-}transfer}\).  On the positive-excess branch, the finite nilpotent matrix sum and the covariance matrix products use polynomially many arithmetic gates.  Differentiating a circuit gate-by-gate while storing its derivatives with respect to the at most \(n^2\) source coordinates incurs only polynomial overhead.  Exact specialization and Gaussian elimination of matrices of dimensions at most \((n^2+1)\times n^2\) are likewise polynomial in \(n\) in \(\mathsf{ArithmeticModel}\).  A sampled coordinate requires
\[
O(\log n+\log(1/\delta))
\]
bits, with rational bit complexity charged separately as stipulated by that model.  Thus one universal \(C>0\), independent of \(G,T,e,n,k,\delta\), bounds construction time and transcript length on either executed branch by
\[
n^{C(k+1)}\operatorname{poly}(\log(1/\delta)),
\]
because \(n\geq2\) and \(k\geq0\).  Only one of the exhaustive branches is run, and the branchwise analysis gives total symbolic error probability at most \(\delta\).  This proves all four mutually exclusive outputs and all asserted guarantees.
