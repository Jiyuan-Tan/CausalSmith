Each doubled terminal formula has \(2(g+hK_{\mathcal G})\) quantified response variables, \(|\mathcal X_{\mathcal O}|\) free observed-cell variables, and encoding length at most \(s\). Concatenating all terminal formulas with their Boolean separators has length at most \(S_{\mathrm{tot}}\). The common CAD eliminates only the response variables, retains the observed-cell parameters, and uses no more than \(\operatorname{CAD}(p,S_{\mathrm{tot}})\) projection, root-isolation, sign-evaluation steps and output cells. This is a symbolic bound; exact point specialization is a separate finite sign computation under \(\operatorname{Exact}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
