Assume \(\operatorname{RC}(\mathcal G,A,Y)\) and that every context graph is identifying.  By \(\text{def:root-control-functional}\), every label-context variable is observed and parentless.  Consequently intervention on \(A\) does not alter the context law:
\[
P_{\mathcal M}(C=c\mid\operatorname{do}(A=a))=P_{\mathrm{obs}}(C=c).
\tag{1}
\]
The cited result \(\text{lem:root-control-context-recovery}\) supplies, in every context, a sound functional \(f^{\mathrm{ID}}_{a,c}\) and the aggregation identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y)
&=\sum_cP_{\mathrm{obs}}(C=c)
f^{\mathrm{ID}}_{a,c}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)\\
&=f_a^{\mathrm{RC}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Positivity in the finite class ensures that every context retained in a conditional expression has supported denominators; zero-weight contexts, if any occur in the published aggregation notation, contribute zero and need no division.

Because the contexts are roots, their restriction may be performed before the context-specific ordinary ID steps: no pre-restriction parent mechanism or provenance is lost.  Thus delayed restriction adds no identifying information in this regime.  Moreover, the last expression in (2) is common to the entire observed-law fiber.  By \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-fiber-dichotomy}\), the unique TCF cell is identifying, and by \(\text{thm:tcf-soundness}\),
\[
f_{a,j}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)
=Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y)
=f_a^{\mathrm{RC}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y).
\tag{3}
\]
The cited root-control theorem is a graphwise result, while TCF also tests constancy at a particular observed law.  Therefore (3) proves the stated reduction but supplies no converse for an exceptional law, exactly as claimed.
