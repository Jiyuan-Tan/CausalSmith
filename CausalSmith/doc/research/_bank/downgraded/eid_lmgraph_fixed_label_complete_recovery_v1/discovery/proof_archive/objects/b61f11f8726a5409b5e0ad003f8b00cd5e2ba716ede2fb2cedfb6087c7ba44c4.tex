Apply \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\) to \(\mathcal M\). It gives a label-compatible representation \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\) with finite factor supports, a product exogenous law, the same full-data law as \(\mathcal M\), and the same interventional coordinates at every \((a,y)\). By \(\text{def:causal-query}\) and the finiteness of \(\mathcal X_Y\) from \(\text{ass:finite-alphabets}\), finite summation gives
\[
\tau_{s_Y}^{\mathcal M^{(0)}}
=\sum_{y\in\mathcal X_Y}s_Y(y)
\{Q_{a_1}^{\mathcal M^{(0)}}(y)-Q_{a_0}^{\mathcal M^{(0)}}(y)\}
=\tau_{s_Y}^{\mathcal M}.
\tag{1}
\]

We now reduce the already finite support of each factor while preserving only the coordinates required in the statement. Fix a factor \(h\in\mathcal J\) in a current finite product-factor representation, let \(S_h\) be its positive-probability support, and write its weights as \(\lambda_h(u_h)>0\) for \(u_h\in S_h\). Hold all other factor laws and all structural tables fixed. With \(\mu_{-h}\) denoting the product law of the other factors, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_h(u_h)
=\sum_{y\in\mathcal X_Y}s_Y(y)\Bigg\{&
\int\mathbf 1\{\Phi_Y^{a_1}(u_h,u_{-h})=y\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})\\
&-\int\mathbf 1\{\Phi_Y^{a_0}(u_h,u_{-h})=y\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})
\Bigg\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
w_h(u_h)=\left(
\left(
\int\mathbf 1\{\Phi(u_h,u_{-h})=(v,r)\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})
\right)_{(v,r)},
d_h(u_h)
\right)\in\mathbb R^{L_{\mathcal G}+1}.
\tag{3}
\]
All integrals in (2)--(3) are finite sums in \(\mathcal M^{(0)}\) and its subsequent reductions. More generally, their measurability follows from the measurable structural maps and Tonelli's theorem. Arbitrary real score values cause no integrability issue because \(\mathcal X_Y\) is finite, so \(s_Y\) is bounded.

The first \(L_{\mathcal G}\) coordinates of every \(w_h(u_h)\) sum to one. Hence the finite set \(w_h(S_h)\) is contained in an affine subspace of \(\mathbb R^{L_{\mathcal G}+1}\) of dimension at most \(L_{\mathcal G}\). Product independence and the structural definition of intervention give
\[
\sum_{u_h\in S_h}\lambda_h(u_h)w_h(u_h)
=\left(
\bigl(P(V=v,R=r)\bigr)_{(v,r)},
\tau_{s_Y}
\right)
\tag{4}
\]
for the current model: the first block follows by total probability, and substituting (2) into the last coordinate gives the finite sum in \(\text{def:causal-query}\).

If \(|S_h|>L_{\mathcal G}+1\), affine dependence supplies real coefficients \(c(u_h)\), not all zero, satisfying
\[
\sum_{u_h\in S_h}c(u_h)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{u_h\in S_h}c(u_h)w_h(u_h)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
After changing signs if necessary, the set \(S_h^+=\{u_h:c(u_h)>0\}\) is nonempty. Define
\[
t=\min_{u_h\in S_h^+}\frac{\lambda_h(u_h)}{c(u_h)},
\qquad
\lambda_h'(u_h)=\lambda_h(u_h)-t c(u_h).
\tag{6}
\]
Equation (5) implies that the new weights sum to one and preserve the vector in (4). The definition of \(t\) makes every \(\lambda_h'(u_h)\) nonnegative and makes at least one of them zero. Deleting zero-weight states and repeating (5)--(6) therefore terminates after finitely many steps with at most
\[
L_{\mathcal G}+1=K_{\tau,\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)
\tag{7}
\]
positive-probability states for factor \(h\), while preserving the current entire full-data law and the current scalar contrast.

The factor index set \(\mathcal J\) is finite because \(\mathcal G\) is finite. Apply (2)--(7) successively to every factor, always using the current laws of the other factors. Each step changes one marginal factor law only, so mutual independence is retained; equation (4) shows inductively that the original full-data law and the scalar in (1) are retained. The structural tables are only restricted to supported factor states, so every label-induced table equality and hence label compatibility are retained. The final representation \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\) has the asserted support bound and satisfies
\[
P_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(V=v,R=r)=P_{\mathcal M}(V=v,R=r),
\qquad
\tau_{s_Y}^{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}=\tau_{s_Y}^{\mathcal M}.
\tag{8}
\]
Preservation of the full law in (8) also preserves strict full-data positivity whenever the input has it. No positivity, regularity, maximality, overlap, division, inversion, limit, or rational-score premise is used. The affine count proves only the universal upper bound (7), not sharpness.
