Fix an identifying cell \(\psi_j\), a compatible law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) whose observed-cell vector \(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}}=(P_{\mathrm{obs}}(o))_o\) satisfies \(\psi_j\), a model \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and a coordinate \((a,y)\in\mathcal X_A\times\mathcal X_Y\).  By the forward direction of \(\text{thm:response-fiber-equivalence}\), there is a response vector \(\theta_{\mathcal M}\) such that
\[
\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta_{\mathcal M};\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\qquad
q_{a,y}(\theta_{\mathcal M})=Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y).
\tag{1}
\]
The first relation includes strict positivity of every full-data cell polynomial, so every trace division admitted by \(\text{def:tcf-procedure}\) is evaluated only after its nonzero denominator has been verified.

Because \(\psi_j\) is identifying, \(\mathsf I_{a,y}\) is true throughout it.  By \(\text{def:terminal-qe-test}\), at \(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}}\) this says
\[
\neg\exists\theta^0\,\exists\theta^1
\left[
\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta^0;\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}})
\wedge
\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta^1;\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}})
\wedge
q_{a,y}(\theta^0)\neq q_{a,y}(\theta^1)
\right].
\tag{2}
\]
Thus all compatible response vectors have a common \(q_{a,y}\)-value.  Since (1) supplies at least one such vector, the relation
\[
\mathsf V_{a,y}(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}},z)
\Longleftrightarrow
\exists\theta\,[\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta;\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}})\wedge z=q_{a,y}(\theta)]
\tag{3}
\]
has exactly one real solution, say \(z_*\).

There are two output cases.  If no rational trace annotation is certified on the whole cell, \(\text{def:tcf-procedure}\) stores (3), so by definition of the cellwise functional
\[
f_{a,j}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)=z_*.
\tag{4}
\]
If the compiler stores a rational trace \(r(\mathbf p)=N(\mathbf p)/D(\mathbf p)\), its whole-cell verification proves
\[
D(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}})\neq0,
\qquad
D(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}})z_*=N(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{5}
\]
The first equality licenses division, and the second then gives (4) in this case as well.  Combining (1), (3), and (4) yields
\[
f_{a,j}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)
=z_*
=q_{a,y}(\theta_{\mathcal M})
=Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y).
\tag{6}
\]
This argument takes place in the ordered field \(\mathbb R\); it uses no finite representation of the coordinates of \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).

Apply (6) at \(a=a_1\) and \(a=a_0\), multiply the equality for each \(y\) by the supplied score \(s_Y(y)\), subtract, and sum over the finite alphabet \(\mathcal X_Y\).  By \(\text{def:causal-query}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{y\in\mathcal X_Y}s_Y(y)
\{f_{a_1,j}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)-f_{a_0,j}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})(y)\}
&=\sum_{y\in\mathcal X_Y}s_Y(y)
\{Q_{a_1}^{\mathcal M}(y)-Q_{a_0}^{\mathcal M}(y)\}\\
&=\tau_{s_Y}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
This proves both assertions.
