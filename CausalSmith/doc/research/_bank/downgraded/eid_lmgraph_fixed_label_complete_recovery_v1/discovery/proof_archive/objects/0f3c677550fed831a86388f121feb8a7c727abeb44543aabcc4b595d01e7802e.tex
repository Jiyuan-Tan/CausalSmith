The universal binary-witness assertion is false, and the smallest substantive alphabet vector is not determined by the color and district structure of a colored hedge alone. There exist a finite regular-maximal graph \(\mathcal G\), a hedge in \(\operatorname{CH}(\mathcal G,A,Y)\), and a strictly positive compatible \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) for which two models in \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) have different \(Q_a\), but no model matching \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) can make every substantive variable binary. More generally, an always-observed substantive coordinate requires at least the cardinality of its observed support, and a partially observed coordinate \(Z\) requires at least \(|\operatorname{supp}(Z^{\dagger}\mid R_Z=1)|\). Under strict positivity these lower bounds equal the declared substantive alphabet cardinalities.
