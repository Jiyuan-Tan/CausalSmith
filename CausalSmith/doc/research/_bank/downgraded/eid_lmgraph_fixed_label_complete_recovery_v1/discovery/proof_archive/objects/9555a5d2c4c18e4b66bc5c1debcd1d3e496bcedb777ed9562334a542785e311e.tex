Set
\[
L=L_{\mathcal G},
\qquad
D=(L-1)+|\mathcal X_A|(|\mathcal X_Y|-1),
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)=D+1.
\tag{1}
\]
Finiteness follows from \(\text{ass:finite-alphabets}\).  The state spaces are nonempty because they are alphabets of the probability law induced by \(\mathcal M\), so every cardinality in (1) is a positive integer.

By \(\text{def:published-lm-class}\), \(\mathcal M\) is a measurable labeled-missingness SCM compatible with \(\mathcal G\).  The proof below uses its finite endogenous alphabets but permits its exogenous factor spaces to be arbitrary measurable spaces.

We first derive the finite-dimensional barycenter reduction used below.  Let \((\Omega,\mathcal A,\mu)\) be a probability space, let \(z:\Omega\to\mathbb R^m\) be bounded and measurable, and suppose \(z(\Omega)\) is contained in an affine subspace of dimension at most \(D\).  Put \(b=\int z\,d\mu\).  We claim that there are \(k\leq D+1\), points \(u_1,\ldots,u_k\in\Omega\), and weights \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\geq0\) such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i=1,
\qquad
b=\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i z(u_i).
\tag{2}
\]
This is the needed Carath\'{e}odory reduction, which we prove here rather than invoke as an undeclared result.

Let \(S=z(\Omega)\), \(C=\operatorname{conv}(S)\), and \(\overline C\) be its closure.  Partition a bounded box containing \(S\) into finitely many Borel sets of diameter at most \(1/n\).  From every set meeting \(S\), choose one point of \(S\), and replace each \(z(u)\) by the selected point in the same partition set.  The resulting finite-valued map \(z_n\) is measurable, satisfies \(\|z_n-z\|\leq1/n\), and has \(\int z_n\,d\mu\in C\).  Therefore
\[
b=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int z_n\,d\mu\in\overline C.
\tag{3}
\]

We show by induction on \(d=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\overline C)\) that the point \(b\) in (3) actually lies in \(C\).  This is immediate when \(d=0\).  If \(b\in\operatorname{ri}(\overline C)\), choose a \(d\)-simplex in \(\overline C\) having \(b\) in its relative interior.  Approximate its vertices by points of \(C\).  Affine independence and strict positivity of the barycentric coordinates of \(b\) persist under sufficiently small perturbations, so \(b\) lies in the convex hull of the approximating points and hence in \(C\).

If instead \(b\) is on the relative boundary of \(\overline C\), choose \(x_n\in\operatorname{aff}(\overline C)\setminus\overline C\) with \(x_n\to b\), and let \(p_n\) be the Euclidean projection of \(x_n\) onto the closed convex set \(\overline C\).  Then \(p_n\to b\), and the projection inequality
\[
\langle x_n-p_n,x-p_n\rangle\leq0
\qquad(x\in\overline C)
\]
holds.  Normalize \(x_n-p_n\) and pass to a convergent subsequence.  Its limit gives a nonconstant affine functional \(\ell\) on \(\operatorname{aff}(\overline C)\) such that
\[
\ell(x)\geq\ell(b)
\qquad(x\in\overline C).
\tag{4}
\]
By linearity of integration and (4),
\[
0=\ell(b)-\ell(b)
=\int\{\ell(z(u))-\ell(b)\}\,d\mu(u).
\tag{5}
\]
The integrand in (5) is nonnegative, so it is zero almost surely.  Restricting \(\Omega\) to this full-measure set leaves \(b\) unchanged and places the range of \(z\) in the proper exposed face
\[
\overline C\cap\{x:\ell(x)=\ell(b)\},
\]
whose affine dimension is strictly below \(d\).  The induction hypothesis now gives \(b\in C\).

Thus \(b\) is a finite convex combination of points in \(S\).  If a representation uses more than \(D+1\) positive weights, the corresponding augmented vectors \((1,z(u_i))\) are linearly dependent because the affine dimension of \(S\) is at most \(D\).  Hence there are coefficients \(c_i\), not all zero, with
\[
\sum_i c_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i c_i z(u_i)=0.
\]
After changing their common sign if necessary, some \(c_i>0\).  With
\[
t=\min_{i:c_i>0}\frac{\alpha_i}{c_i},
\qquad
\alpha_i'=\alpha_i-tc_i,
\]
the new weights are nonnegative, sum to one, have the same barycenter, and at least one formerly positive weight is zero.  Iteration proves (2).

We now apply (2) separately to each exogenous factor.  By \(\text{ass:semimarkovian}\), the factors indexed by
\[
\mathcal J=\mathcal E^{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal G)\sqcup\mathcal V\sqcup\mathcal R
\]
have a product law \(\bigotimes_{h\in\mathcal J}\mu_h\).  The graph is finite, so \(\mathcal J\) is finite.  By \(\text{ass:acyclic}\), the structural equations have a unique recursive deterministic evaluation
\[
\Phi(u)\in\mathcal X_{\mathcal V}\times\mathcal X_{\mathcal R}
\]
for each joint factor state \(u\).  For \(a\in\mathcal X_A\), let \(\Phi^a(u)\) denote the evaluation after replacing only the equation for \(A\) by the constant \(a\).  The equations for the missingness indicators are not replaced, in agreement with \(\text{def:causal-query}\).

Fix \(h\in\mathcal J\), temporarily holding the structural equations and all other factor laws fixed, and write \(\mu_{-h}=\bigotimes_{j\neq h}\mu_j\).  Denote the model with these current factor laws by \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\).  Define the bounded measurable vector
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_h(u_h)=\Bigg(&
\left(
\int \mathbf 1\{\Phi(u_h,u_{-h})=(v,r)\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})
\right)_{(v,r)},\\
&\left(
\int \mathbf 1\{\Phi_Y^a(u_h,u_{-h})=y\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})
\right)_{(a,y)}
\Bigg).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Measurability in (6) follows from Tonelli's theorem applied to the measurable nonnegative indicators.  Its first block is a probability vector on the \(L\) full cells.  For each fixed \(a\), its \(a\)-indexed outcome block is a probability vector on \(\mathcal X_Y\).  Therefore every point in the range of \(z_h\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{(v,r)}z_{h,v,r}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{y\in\mathcal X_Y}z_{h,a,y}=1
\quad(a\in\mathcal X_A).
\tag{7}
\]
The equations in (7) concern disjoint coordinate blocks and are linearly independent.  Consequently the affine dimension of the range of \(z_h\) is at most
\[
L+|\mathcal X_A||\mathcal X_Y|-1-|\mathcal X_A|
=(L-1)+|\mathcal X_A|(|\mathcal X_Y|-1)=D.
\tag{8}
\]

Product independence and Tonelli's theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int z_h(u_h)\,d\mu_h(u_h)
=\Big(&\bigl(P_{\mathcal M^{\circ}}(V=v,R=r)\bigr)_{(v,r)},\\
&\bigl(Q_a^{\mathcal M^{\circ}}(y)\bigr)_{(a,y)}\Big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The second equality in (9) is exactly \(\text{def:causal-query}\), applied to the current product-factor model \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\).  Applying (2) with the dimension bound (8) replaces \(\mu_h\) by an atomic probability measure supported on at most
\[
D+1=K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)
\tag{10}
\]
actual values of that factor, without changing any coordinate of (9).  Since only one marginal factor law is changed, the new joint factor law is still a product law, so mutual independence is preserved.

Enumerate the finite set \(\mathcal J\) and repeat this replacement sequentially.  At each step, define (6) using the marginal laws produced by all preceding steps.  Equation (9) shows inductively that each replacement preserves the current entire full-data law and every interventional outcome coordinate; hence their values remain equal to the corresponding coordinates of the original model \(\mathcal M\).  Let \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\) denote the model after the last replacement.  Then
\[
P_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(V=v,R=r)=P_{\mathcal M}(v,r)
\quad\text{for every }(v,r),
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
Q_a^{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(y)=Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y)
\quad\text{for every }(a,y).
\tag{12}
\]

Finally, relabel the selected support of factor \(h\) as \(\{1,\ldots,k_h\}\), where \(k_h\leq K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)\), and restrict the original structural equations to the Cartesian product of these selected factor values.  All endogenous parent alphabets and all selected latent alphabets are finite, so these restrictions are deterministic response tables.  By \(\text{ass:label-semantics}\), whenever a label requires omission of an observed parent in a context, the original mechanism is constant in that parent there; restriction to selected factor values preserves this equality.  Thus the tables are label-compatible.  Their product factor law is mutually independent, and their acyclic evaluation agrees with the reduced model on every supported factor state.  Equations (11)--(12) prove the claim.  No positivity, regularity, maximality, division, boundary limit, or additional support assumption is used.
