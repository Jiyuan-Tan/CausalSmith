We give a direct finite counterexample and then prove the support assertions.

Let \(\mathcal V=\{A,Y,Z\}\), let \(\mathcal R=\varnothing\), let \(\mathcal X_A=\mathcal X_Y=\{0,1\}\), and let \(\mathcal X_Z=\{0,1,2\}\).  Thus every substantive variable is always observed and \(\mathcal O=(A,Y,Z)\).  Let \(\mathcal G\) have the directed edge \(A\to Y\), the bidirected edge \(A\leftrightarrow Y\), isolated vertex \(Z\), and empty label set.  Its directed part is acyclic, as required by ass:acyclic.  Because \(\mathcal R=\varnothing\), there is no missingness context in which a label could occur; consequently the empty label set is regular and maximal, so ass:regular and ass:maximal hold.  Assumptions ass:proxy-consistency and ass:label-semantics are vacuous for this graph.

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the \(C\)-forest induced by \(\{A,Y\}\), and let \(\mathcal F'\) be its subforest induced by \(\{Y\}\).  The two forests have the same root set \(\{Y\}\), \(\mathcal F\cap\{A\}=\{A\}\), and \(\mathcal F'\cap\{A\}=\varnothing\).  The common root \(Y\) is an ancestor of \(Y\) after intervention on \(A\), since every vertex is its own ancestor.  Take the empty color assignment and the trace family consisting only of the empty trace.  That trace family is prefix closed, and the licensing condition is vacuous because no color occurs.  Hence, by def:colored-hedge,
\[
 H=(\mathcal F,\mathcal F',\varnothing,\{\varnothing\})
 \in \operatorname{CH}(\mathcal G,A,Y).
\]

We next construct two models.  Let \(U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) be the exogenous factor shared by \(A\) and \(Y\).  Independently, let \(E_A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)\), let \(E_Y\) be uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,16\}\), and let \(E_Z\) be uniform on \(\{0,1,2\}\).  Put
\[
 A=U\oplus E_A,
 \qquad
 Z=E_Z,
 \qquad
 t_0=\frac14,
 \qquad
 t_1=-\frac14.
\]
Here \(\oplus\) denotes addition modulo two, and the Bernoulli parameter is the probability of value one.
For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 k_i(u,a)
 =16\left[\frac12+t_i\left\{\mathbf 1(u=a)-\frac34\right\}\right]
\]
and in model \(\mathcal M_i\) set
\[
 Y=\mathbf 1\{E_Y\leq k_i(U,A)\}.
\]
The four possible thresholds are integers: \(k_0(u,a)\) is \(9\) when \(u=a\) and \(5\) otherwise, whereas \(k_1(u,a)\) is \(7\) when \(u=a\) and \(11\) otherwise.  Thus these are deterministic finite response tables.  The equation for \(A\) depends on the shared factor \(U\), and the equation for \(Y\) depends on both \(A\) and \(U\): for either model, changing whether \(u=a\) changes the threshold.  Hence the directed edge and the individual bidirected-edge factor are both active, and no edge other than those of \(\mathcal G\) is introduced.  The exogenous factors are mutually independent, so the construction satisfies ass:semimarkovian; it is compatible with the empty label set and therefore belongs to the published labeled-missingness class.

For positivity, define
\[
 \pi_i(u,a)=\frac{k_i(u,a)}{16}.
\]
The displayed threshold values give \(0<\pi_i(u,a)<1\) for every \(i,u,a\).  Moreover \(P(E_A=e)>0\) for both \(e\in\{0,1\}\).  Therefore, for every \((a,y,z)\in\mathcal X_A\times\mathcal X_Y\times\mathcal X_Z\), independence of the exogenous factors gives
\[
 P_{\mathcal M_i}(A=a,Y=y,Z=z)
 =\frac13\sum_{u=0}^1
 \frac12 P(E_A=a\oplus u)
 \pi_i(u,a)^y\{1-\pi_i(u,a)\}^{1-y}>0.
\]
This verifies ass:strict-positivity cell by cell.  All alphabets are finite, as required by ass:finite-alphabets.  It follows from def:positive-rm-class that
\[
 \mathcal M_0,\mathcal M_1\in
 \mathfrak M_{+}^{\mathrm{rm}}(\mathcal G).
\]

We now compute the observed laws.  For either \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 P_{\mathcal M_i}(A=a)
 =\sum_{u=0}^1\frac12P(E_A=a\oplus u)
 =\frac12\left(\frac34+\frac14\right)
 =\frac12,
\]
and
\[
 P_{\mathcal M_i}(U=a,A=a)
 =P(U=a,E_A=0)
 =\frac12\cdot\frac34
 =\frac38.
\]
Division is legitimate because \(P(A=a)=1/2>0\), and hence
\[
 P_{\mathcal M_i}(U=a\mid A=a)=\frac{3/8}{1/2}=\frac34.
\]
Using the response equation for \(Y\) and the law of total probability conditional on \(A=a\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{\mathcal M_i}(Y=1\mid A=a)
 &=\sum_{u=0}^1
 \left[\frac12+t_i\left\{\mathbf 1(u=a)-\frac34\right\}\right]
 P(U=u\mid A=a)\\
 &=\frac12+t_i\left\{P(U=a\mid A=a)-\frac34\right\}
 =\frac12.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(Z=E_Z\) is independent and uniform, both models consequently induce
\[
 P_{\mathcal M_i}(A=a,Y=y,Z=z)
 =\frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac13
 =\frac1{12}
\]
for every observed cell.  Let \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) denote this common uniform law.

Under \(\operatorname{do}(A=a)\), the equation for \(A\) is replaced by the constant \(a\), while the distribution of \(U\) remains Bernoulli \(\left(1/2\right)\) by the SCM intervention semantics in def:causal-query.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_a^{\mathcal M_i}(1)
 &=\sum_{u=0}^1
 \left[\frac12+t_i\left\{\mathbf 1(u=a)-\frac34\right\}\right]P(U=u)\\
 &=\frac12+t_i\left(\frac12-\frac34\right)
 =\frac12-\frac{t_i}{4}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
 Q_a^{\mathcal M_0}(1)=\frac7{16}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 Q_a^{\mathcal M_1}(1)=\frac9{16}.
\]
By def:observational-fiber, both models lie in \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), but their interventional laws differ.  This proves nonidentification for the displayed colored hedge with strictly positive witnesses.

It remains to prove that no universal binary witness can match this observed law and to establish the general support claim.  If \(W\) is always observed, define
\[
 S_W=\{w:P_{\mathrm{obs}}(W=w)>0\}.
\]
In any model inducing \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the observed value of \(W\) is its substantive value, so \(S_W\subseteq\mathcal X_W\).  Consequently
\[
 |\mathcal X_W|\geq |S_W|.
\]
For a partially observed substantive coordinate \(W\), ass:proxy-consistency implies on \(R_W=1\) that \(W^{\dagger}=W\).  Hence, whenever \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}(R_W=1)>0\),
\[
 S_W^{\dagger}
 =\operatorname{supp}_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(W^{\dagger}\mid R_W=1)
 \subseteq\mathcal X_W,
\]
and therefore
\[
 |\mathcal X_W|\geq |S_W^{\dagger}|.
\]
The required conditioning event has positive probability under ass:strict-positivity: summing the positive full-data cells with \(R_W=1\) gives \(P_{\mathcal M}(R_W=1)>0\).

Under ass:strict-positivity, both inclusions are equalities at the level of supports.  Indeed, for every \(w\in\mathcal X_W\), summing positive full-data cells with substantive coordinate \(W=w\) yields \(P_{\mathcal M}(W=w)>0\) when \(W\) is always observed.  If \(W\) is partially observed, summing positive full-data cells with \(W=w\) and \(R_W=1\) gives
\[
 P_{\mathcal M}(W=w,R_W=1)>0.
\]
By ass:proxy-consistency this is
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}(W^{\dagger}=w,R_W=1)>0,
\]
so every declared substantive value occurs in the conditional proxy support.  Conversely, proxy consistency permits no value outside \(\mathcal X_W\) on \(R_W=1\).  Thus
\[
 |S_W|=|\mathcal X_W|
 \quad\text{for always-observed }W,
 \qquad
 |S_W^{\dagger}|=|\mathcal X_W|
 \quad\text{for partially observed }W.
\]

In the constructed common law, \(S_Z=\{0,1,2\}\), so any substantive alphabet realizing that observed coordinate has at least three elements.  In particular, no model matching \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) can make every substantive variable binary.  Finally, replace the isolated alphabet \(\mathcal X_Z\) by \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) and take \(E_Z\) uniform on it, for any integer \(m\geq2\).  The same two response models on \((A,Y)\) still have identical observed laws and the same distinct query values.  The forests \(\mathcal F'\subseteq\mathcal F\), their districts, roots, colors, and trace family are unchanged, because \(Z\) is isolated and does not belong to either forest, whereas the attained support lower bound for \(Z\) is \(m\).  Since \(m\) is arbitrary, the smallest required substantive alphabet vector cannot be determined from the color and district structure of the colored hedge alone.  This completes every assertion of the theorem.
