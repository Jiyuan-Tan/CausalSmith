Put
\[
L=L_{\mathcal G},
\qquad
D=(L-1)+|\mathcal X_A|(|\mathcal X_Y|-1).
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{ass:finite-alphabets}\), these are finite integers, and the displayed definition of \(K_{\mathrm{ub}}\) gives
\[
D+1=L+|\mathcal X_A|(|\mathcal X_Y|-1)
=K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G).
\tag{2}
\]

We first prove the finite-dimensional support fact used below. Let \((\Omega,\mathcal A,\mu)\) be a probability space, let \(z:\Omega\to\mathbb R^m\) be bounded and measurable, and suppose that \(z(\Omega)\) lies in an affine subspace of dimension at most \(D\). If \(b=\int z\,d\mu\), then there are points \(u_1,\ldots,u_k\in\Omega\) and weights \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\geq0\), with \(k\leq D+1\), such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i=1,
\qquad
b=\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i z(u_i).
\tag{3}
\]
Here is a derivation, including the possible nonclosed-range case. Set \(S=z(\Omega)\), \(C=\operatorname{conv}(S)\), and let \(\overline C\) be its Euclidean closure inside its finite-dimensional affine hull. Because \(z\) is bounded, for every \(n\geq1\) a finite Borel partition of a box containing \(S\) into sets of diameter at most \(1/n\), with one selected point of \(S\) from every set meeting \(S\), produces a finite-valued measurable map \(z_n\) satisfying
\[
\|z_n-z\|\leq n^{-1},
\qquad
\int z_n\,d\mu\in C.
\]
Therefore
\[
b=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int z_n\,d\mu\in\overline C.
\tag{4}
\]

We prove \(b\in C\) by induction on \(d=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\overline C)\). The assertion is immediate for \(d=0\). If \(b\in\operatorname{ri}(\overline C)\), choose \(d+1\) points of a sufficiently small relative ball about \(b\) whose simplex contains \(b\) in its relative interior. Approximating these vertices by points of the dense convex set \(C\), sufficiently closely that affine independence and the strictly positive barycentric coordinates of \(b\) persist, puts \(b\) in a simplex contained in \(C\). Hence \(b\in C\).

It remains to treat \(b\) on the relative boundary. Choose \(x_n\in\operatorname{aff}(\overline C)\setminus\overline C\) with \(x_n\to b\), and let \(p_n\) minimize \(p\mapsto\|x_n-p\|\) over the closed convex set \(\overline C\). A minimizer exists because a minimizing sequence can be restricted to a compact ball in the finite-dimensional affine hull. For \(x\in\overline C\), convexity and the minimality of \(p_n\) imply, for \(0<t\leq1\),
\[
0\leq
\|x_n-p_n-t(x-p_n)\|^2-\|x_n-p_n\|^2.
\]
After division by \(t\) and passage to \(t\downarrow0\),
\[
\langle x_n-p_n,x-p_n\rangle\leq0
\qquad(x\in\overline C).
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(b\in\overline C\), minimality also gives \(\|x_n-p_n\|\leq\|x_n-b\|\), and thus \(p_n\to b\). Normalize \(x_n-p_n\) and pass to a convergent subsequence on the unit sphere of the direction space. The limit in (5), after changing sign, is a nonconstant affine functional \(\ell\) satisfying
\[
\ell(x)\geq\ell(b)
\qquad(x\in\overline C).
\tag{6}
\]
Linearity of the integral and (6) yield
\[
0=\ell(b)-\ell(b)
=\int\{\ell(z(u))-\ell(b)\}\,d\mu(u).
\tag{7}
\]
The integrand in (7) is nonnegative, so it is zero \(\mu\)-almost surely. Restricting to this full-measure set preserves \(b\) and puts the range of \(z\) in the proper exposed face
\[
\overline C\cap\{x:\ell(x)=\ell(b)\},
\]
whose affine dimension is strictly below \(d\). The induction hypothesis gives \(b\in C\).

Thus \(b\) is a finite convex combination of points of \(S\). If it uses more than \(D+1\) positive weights, affine dimension at most \(D\) supplies coefficients \(c_i\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_i c_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i c_i z(u_i)=0.
\tag{8}
\]
After changing all signs if necessary, some \(c_i>0\). With
\[
t=\min_{i:c_i>0}\frac{\alpha_i}{c_i},
\qquad
\alpha_i'=\alpha_i-tc_i,
\tag{9}
\]
the new weights are nonnegative, sum to one, have the same barycenter, and at least one formerly positive weight is zero. Repeating (9) proves (3). This derivation uses neither closedness of \(S\) nor an attainment assumption.

We apply (3) factor by factor. By \(\text{def:published-lm-class}\) and \(\text{ass:semimarkovian}\), the exogenous factors indexed by
\[
\mathcal J=\mathcal E^{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal G)\sqcup\mathcal V\sqcup\mathcal R
\]
have a product law \(\bigotimes_{h\in\mathcal J}\mu_h\). The index set is finite because \(\mathcal G\) is finite. By \(\text{ass:acyclic}\), the structural equations admit a unique recursive evaluation
\[
\Phi(u)\in\mathcal X_{\mathcal V}\times\mathcal X_{\mathcal R}
\]
at every joint factor state \(u\). For \(a\in\mathcal X_A\), let \(\Phi^a(u)\) be the evaluation after replacing only the equation for \(A\) by the constant \(a\). This is the intervention in \(\text{def:causal-query}\); in particular, the endogenous missingness equations remain active.

Fix \(h\in\mathcal J\), hold the structural maps and all factor laws other than \(\mu_h\) fixed, and put \(\mu_{-h}=\bigotimes_{j\neq h}\mu_j\). For the model with these current laws, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_h(u_h)=\Bigg(&
\left(
\int\mathbf 1\{\Phi(u_h,u_{-h})=(v,r)\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})
\right)_{(v,r)},\\
&\left(
\int\mathbf 1\{\Phi_Y^a(u_h,u_{-h})=y\}\,d\mu_{-h}(u_{-h})
\right)_{(a,y)}
\Bigg).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The structural maps are measurable by \(\text{def:published-lm-class}\), so Tonelli's theorem makes every coordinate in (10) measurable; all coordinates lie in \([0,1]\). The first block is a probability vector on the \(L\) full cells. For each fixed \(a\), the corresponding outcome block is a probability vector on \(\mathcal X_Y\). Hence every point in the range satisfies the linearly independent equations
\[
\sum_{(v,r)}z_{h,v,r}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{y\in\mathcal X_Y}z_{h,a,y}=1
\quad(a\in\mathcal X_A).
\tag{11}
\]
The range of (10) therefore has affine dimension at most
\[
L+|\mathcal X_A||\mathcal X_Y|-1-|\mathcal X_A|
=(L-1)+|\mathcal X_A|(|\mathcal X_Y|-1)=D.
\tag{12}
\]

Product independence and Tonelli's theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int z_h(u_h)\,d\mu_h(u_h)
=\Big(&\bigl(P(V=v,R=r)\bigr)_{(v,r)},\\
&\bigl(P(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(A=a))\bigr)_{(a,y)}\Big)
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
for the current model. In the second block, equality is exactly the structural intervention definition in \(\text{def:causal-query}\). Applying (3) with (12) replaces \(\mu_h\) by an atomic law on at most
\[
D+1=K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)
\tag{14}
\]
actual values of factor \(h\), without changing any coordinate of (13). Only one marginal factor law changes, so the joint exogenous law remains a product law.

Enumerate the finite set \(\mathcal J\) and perform this replacement sequentially, defining (10) with the factor laws current at each step. Equation (13) shows inductively that every replacement preserves the current full-data law and every interventional outcome coordinate. Thus the final model \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\) satisfies
\[
P_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(V=v,R=r)=P_{\mathcal M}(V=v,R=r)
\quad\text{for all }(v,r),
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
P_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(A=a))
=Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y)
\quad\text{for all }(a,y).
\tag{16}
\]

Relabel the selected support of factor \(h\) as \(\{1,\ldots,k_h\}\), where \(k_h\leq K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)\), and restrict the original structural equations to the selected factor values. Finite endogenous and selected exogenous alphabets make these restrictions deterministic response tables. If the common alphabet in \(\text{def:response-polynomial-fiber}\) is desired, pad to \(\{1,\ldots,K_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathcal G)\}\) with zero-weight states and copy an arbitrary supported state in every incident structural table. Padding changes neither support nor any probability. By \(\text{ass:label-semantics}\), a labeled context makes the original mechanism constant in the omitted parent there; restriction and copying preserve each such table equality. The resulting response representation is therefore label-compatible, and its factors remain mutually independent. Equations (15)--(16) prove the claim.

Because (15) preserves every full-data cell probability, strict full-data positivity is preserved if it was present. Strict positivity, regularity, maximality, overlap, division, inversion, and limiting arguments are not assumptions of the reduction, and no sharpness or support attainment claim is made.
