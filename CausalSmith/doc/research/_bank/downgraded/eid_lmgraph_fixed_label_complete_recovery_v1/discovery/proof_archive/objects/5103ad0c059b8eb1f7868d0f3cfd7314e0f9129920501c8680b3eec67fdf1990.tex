Let \(K\) be the ordered subfield of \(\mathbb R\) generated by the specialized observed coordinates \(\mathbf p=(P_{\mathrm{obs}}(o))_o\), and let \(K^{\mathrm{rc}}\) be its real closure. By \(\text{def:exact-observed-specialization}\), the exact negative-cell sample consists of Thom-encoded vectors \(\theta^0,\theta^1\) over \(K^{\mathrm{rc}}\). The statement that \(\omega\) satisfies \(\mathsf W_j\) means, by \(\text{def:terminal-qe-test}\) and \(\text{def:symbolic-observed-partition}\), that
\[
\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta^i;\mathbf p)\quad(i=0,1),
\qquad
q_{a,y}(\theta^0)\neq q_{a,y}(\theta^1).
\tag{1}
\]
This use of Thom encodings does not assert that the coordinates are algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\): they are algebraic over \(K\), as required by the corrected specialization definition.

Apply the converse direction of \(\text{thm:response-fiber-equivalence}\) separately to the two compatibility assertions in (1). It gives decoded models \(\mathcal M_i\) such that
\[
\mathcal M_i\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\qquad
p_{v,r}(\theta^i)=P_{\mathcal M_i}(v,r),
\qquad
q_{a',y'}(\theta^i)=Q_{a'}^{\mathcal M_i}(y')
\tag{2}
\]
for \(i\in\{0,1\}\) and every \((v,r,a',y')\). The equality constraints in \(\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}\) further give
\[
P_{\mathcal M_i}(\mathcal O=o)=p_o(\theta^i)=P_{\mathrm{obs}}(o)
\quad\text{for every }o\in\mathcal X_{\mathcal O}.
\tag{3}
\]
The strict inequalities in the same formula give
\[
P_{\mathcal M_i}(v,r)=p_{v,r}(\theta^i)>0
\quad\text{for every }(v,r),
\tag{4}
\]
so both decoded full-data laws satisfy the required support condition. Finally, the one-hot equations select one label-compatible response-table tuple, while the latent mass of a joint state is \(\prod_h\rho^i_{h,u_h}\). Thus the decoding in (2) retains one mutually independent factor for every individual bidirected edge and every private factor; it does not introduce a district-wide mixture.

Taking \((a',y')=(a,y)\) in (2) and substituting into the inequality in (1) yields
\[
Q_a^{\mathcal M_0}(y)=q_{a,y}(\theta^0)\neq q_{a,y}(\theta^1)=Q_a^{\mathcal M_1}(y).
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (2)--(5) prove all claims.
