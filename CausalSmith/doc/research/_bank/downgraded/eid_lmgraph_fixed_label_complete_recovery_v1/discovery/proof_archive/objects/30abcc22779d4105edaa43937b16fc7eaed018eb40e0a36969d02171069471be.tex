For each \((a,y)\), \(\mathsf I_{a,y}(\mathcal G;\mathbf p)\) is a first-order formula over a real closed field whose atomic formulas are polynomial sign conditions. By \(\text{lem:real-closed-field-quantifier-elimination}\), eliminating \(\theta^0,\theta^1\) produces a quantifier-free formula in \(\mathbf p\) with the same truth value. Apply the same result to the feasible-projection formula \(\exists\theta\,\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta;\mathbf p)\). Only finitely many polynomials occur because the graph, response-table set, latent alphabets, treatment alphabet, and outcome alphabet are finite.

By \(\text{lem:finite-sign-invariant-cad}\), those finitely many polynomials admit one finite sign-invariant cylindrical algebraic decomposition. On each cell every atomic sign and hence every Boolean combination above has a constant truth value. Intersecting with the feasible projection and discarding empty cells therefore yields finitely many pairwise-disjoint semialgebraic cells whose union is exactly
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{feas}}
=\{\mathbf p:\exists\theta\,\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta;\mathbf p)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is precisely the common-CAD construction in \(\text{def:symbolic-observed-partition}\), so its cell formulas are \(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_J\). In particular, every compatible observed-law vector in (1) belongs to exactly one such cell; the common construction does not identify states merely because their shortened graphical keys agree.

Fix a compatible \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), its unique cell, and \((a,y)\). Expanding \(\text{def:terminal-qe-test}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf I_{a,y}(\mathcal G;\mathbf p)
&\Longleftrightarrow
\neg\exists\theta^0,\theta^1\bigl[
\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta^0;\mathbf p)
\wedge\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta^1;\mathbf p)
\wedge q_{a,y}(\theta^0)\neq q_{a,y}(\theta^1)
\bigr]\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
q_{a,y}\text{ is constant on }
\Theta(\mathbf p),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\Theta(\mathbf p)=\{\theta:\mathsf C_{\mathcal G}(\theta;\mathbf p)\}\). The forward direction of \(\text{thm:response-fiber-equivalence}\) sends every \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) to an element \(\theta_{\mathcal M}\in\Theta(\mathbf p)\) and preserves
\[
q_{a,y}(\theta_{\mathcal M})=Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y).
\tag{3}
\]
Its converse sends every \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathbf p)\) to a model \(\mathcal M_\theta\) in that fiber and preserves the same equality. Consequently,
\[
q_{a,y}\text{ is constant on }\Theta(\mathbf p)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal M\longmapsto Q_a^{\mathcal M}(y)
\text{ is constant on }\mathfrak F(\mathcal G,P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{4}
\]

On the true side of (2), compatibility makes \(\Theta(\mathbf p)\) nonempty, and
\[
\mathsf V_{a,y}(\mathbf p,z)
\Longleftrightarrow
\exists\theta\in\Theta(\mathbf p)\,[z=q_{a,y}(\theta)]
\tag{5}
\]
therefore holds for exactly one \(z\). On the false side of (2), negating the universal equality yields two compatible response vectors with unequal query values; the unnegated conjunction is exactly the stored relation \(\mathsf W_j\).

Under \(\operatorname{Exact}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), \(\text{def:exact-observed-specialization}\) decides the finitely many cell signs and, on a negative cell, represents a satisfying pair by Thom encodings over the real closure of the ordered field generated by \(\mathbf p\). Applying \(\text{lem:negative-cell-model-pair}\) to that pair gives the strictly positive, individual-edge-factorized models with equal observed law and unequal causal coordinate asserted in the final sentence. Equations (1)--(5) prove the dichotomy.
