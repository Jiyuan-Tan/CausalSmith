For a Borel rule \(\delta\), define the clipped Borel rule
\[
 \bar\delta(z,y)=\operatorname{clip}(\delta(z,y)).
\]
We first verify the range of the causal target.  By \(\mathrm{def:local-target-mass}\), the cells \(\{z:z_{\Gamma_i}=x\}\), \(x\in X_i\), partition \(\Omega_n\).  Hence \(\mathrm{ass:target-product-policy}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{x\in X_i}p_{\pi',i}(x)
 =\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi'}(z)=1.
\]
Consequently, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded-schedules}\) and \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\) imply, for every \(v\in K\),
\[
 \lvert\theta(v)\rvert
 \le \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{x\in X_i}
 p_{\pi',i}(x)\lvert v_i(x)\rvert
 \le \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}1=1.
\]
Projection onto \([-1,1]\) weakly decreases distance to every point of that interval.  Thus, pointwise in \((z,v)\),
\[
 \bigl(\bar\delta(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 \le
 \bigl(\delta(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2.                                      \tag{1}
\]

Fix a genuine prior \(\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\) and let \(\mathbf V\sim\nu\).  Both \(\bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)\) and \(\theta(\mathbf V)\) are bounded by one in absolute value.  Their conditional second moments therefore exist.  For each fixed \(z\), conditioning on \(N_z\mathbf V\) and expanding the square around the conditional mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_\nu\!\left[
 \bigl(\bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)-\theta(\mathbf V)\bigr)^2
 \mid N_z\mathbf V\right]\\
 &\quad=
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V\right)
 +\left(
 \bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)
 -\mathbb E_\nu[\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V]
 \right)^2\\
 &\quad\ge
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V\right).
\end{aligned}                                                                     \tag{2}
\]
No integrability assertion about the original, possibly unbounded, rule \(\delta\) is used.  Write
\[
 r_\eta(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_\pi(z)
 \bigl(\eta(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
\]
for either rule \(\eta\).  Inequality \((1)\), integration of the bounded loss of \(\bar\delta\), the fact that a supremum dominates a prior average, and \((2)\) yield, in this order,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{v\in K}r_\delta(v)
 &\ge \sup_{v\in K}r_{\bar\delta}(v)\\
 &\ge \int_K r_{\bar\delta}(v)\,d\nu(v)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_\pi(z)
 \mathbb E_\nu\!\left[
 \bigl(\bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)-\theta(\mathbf V)\bigr)^2\right]\\
 &\ge \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_\pi(z)
 \mathbb E_\nu\!\left[
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V)\right]\\
 &=\mathcal B(\nu)=\operatorname{LB}(\nu),
\end{aligned}                                                                     \tag{3}
\]
where the first inequality remains valid in the extended nonnegative reals.  Taking the infimum of \((3)\) over all Borel \(\delta\) in \(\mathrm{def:unrestricted-frontier}\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{LB}(\nu)\le R^{\star}.                                           \tag{4}
\]

For every weight rule \(a\), the map \((z,y)\mapsto a_z^{\mathsf T}y\) is Borel.  It is therefore feasible for \(R^{\star}\), and \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) gives
\[
 R^{\star}
 \le \inf_a\sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 =F.                                                                              \tag{5}
\]
For a fixed feasible \(a\), \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{def:primal-certificate}\) similarly give
\[
 F\le
 \sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 =\operatorname{UB}(a).                                                           \tag{6}
\]

Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) supplies an attaining weight rule \(a^{\star}\).  Applying the projection inequality in \((1)\) with \(\delta(z,y)=(a_z^{\star})^{\mathsf T}y\), then using \(\mathrm{def:clipped-incidence-estimator}\), gives
\[
 \sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{inc}}(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 \le
 \sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl((a_z^{\star})^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 =F.                                                                              \tag{7}
\]
Equations \((4)\)--\((7)\) prove all assertions.
