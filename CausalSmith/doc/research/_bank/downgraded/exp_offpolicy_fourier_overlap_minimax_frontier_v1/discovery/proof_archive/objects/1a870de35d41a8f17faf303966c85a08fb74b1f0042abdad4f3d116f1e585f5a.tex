We first justify that the displayed lists are finite and exhaustive.  By \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\), \(K_i\) is the intersection, inside the finite-dimensional space \(H_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{X_i}\), of the finitely many half-spaces
\[
 -1\le v_i(x)\le1,
 \qquad x\in X_i.
\]
It contains zero, is closed, and is bounded because the displayed inequalities bound every coordinate of the function \(v_i\) on the finite set \(X_i\).  Hence \(K_i\) is compact.  We give the finite-polytope argument explicitly.  At an extreme point, the active constraint functionals span the dual of \(H_i\): otherwise a nonzero direction annihilating every active functional could be added and subtracted with sufficiently small coefficient while all inactive strict inequalities remained satisfied, contradicting extremality.  One may therefore select \(\dim(H_i)\) linearly independent active constraints, whose equalities have at most one common solution.  There are only finitely many such selections, so the extreme-point set \(\mathcal V_i\) is finite.

Let \(C_i=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_i)\).  If some \(x\in K_i\) were outside \(C_i\), compactness of the finite convex hull would give a nearest point \(y\in C_i\).  With \(c=x-y\), minimality of \(y\) along each segment from \(y\) to \(u\in C_i\) gives
\[
 \langle c,u-y\rangle\le0,
 \qquad
 \langle c,x\rangle=\langle c,y\rangle+\|c\|^2
 >\sup_{u\in C_i}\langle c,u\rangle. \tag{0}
\]
The linear functional \(c\) attains its maximum on compact \(K_i\).  Its maximizer face contains an extreme point of \(K_i\): this follows by induction on affine dimension, because a non-singleton compact convex face admits a nonconstant linear functional whose maximizer face has strictly smaller affine dimension, while an extreme point of a face is extreme in the original set.  Call such an extreme maximizer \(e\).  Since \(x\in K_i\), \(\langle c,e\rangle\ge\langle c,x\rangle\), contradicting (0) because \(e\in\mathcal V_i\subseteq C_i\).  Thus
\[
 K_i=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_i),
 \qquad |\mathcal V_i|=r_i<\infty.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:bounded-fourier-class}\), \(K=\prod_{i\in V_n}K_i\).  We next prove
\[
 \operatorname{vert}(K)=\prod_{i\in V_n}\mathcal V_i. \tag{1}
\]
If one coordinate of a tuple in \(K\) is a nontrivial convex combination of two points of its factor, holding all other coordinates fixed gives a nontrivial convex decomposition of the tuple, so a product vertex must have vertex coordinates.  Conversely, if a tuple of coordinatewise vertices is a nontrivial convex combination of two points of \(K\), projection onto each coordinate expresses a vertex of \(K_i\) as the same convex combination; every pair of projected endpoints must therefore coincide with that vertex.  The two tuples coincide, proving (1).

For a fixed weight rule \(a=(a_z)_{z\in\Omega_n}\), define
\[
 Q_a(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2.
\]
The masses \(p_{\pi}(z)\) are nonnegative by \(\mathrm{ass:source-product-design}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:source-overlap}\).  The map \(N_z\) is linear by \(\mathrm{def:observation-operator}\), and \(\theta\) is linear by its displayed formula in \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\), so each summand is the square of a linear functional of \(v\).  Therefore \(Q_a\) is convex.  Every \(v\in K\) is a convex combination \(v=\sum_e\lambda_e e\) of the vertices in (1), with \(\lambda_e\ge0\) and \(\sum_e\lambda_e=1\).  Convexity gives
\[
 Q_a(v)\le\sum_e\lambda_eQ_a(e)
 \le\max_{e\in\prod_i\mathcal V_i}Q_a(e).
\]
The reverse inequality after taking the supremum holds because every such \(e\) belongs to \(K\).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{v\in K}Q_a(v)
 =\max_{e\in\prod_i\mathcal V_i}Q_a(e). \tag{2}
\]
Substituting (2) into \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) and introducing the epigraph variable \(t\) gives exactly the displayed program.  Each constraint is convex in the decision variables because it is a nonnegative weighted sum of squares of affine functions of the coordinates of \(a\).  There are \(|\Omega_n|=2^n\) vectors \(a_z\), each with \(n\) scalar coordinates, and one scalar \(t\), hence \(n2^n+1\) scalar variables.  Equation (1) gives exactly \(\prod_i r_i=\prod_i|\operatorname{vert}(K_i)|\) constraints.  The existence assertion in \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) supplies a minimizing weight rule after the irrelevant directions \(\ker(N_z^{\ast})\) are quotiented out.  Pairing that rule with \(t=\max_eQ_a(e)\) proves attainment, and the weight component may be denoted by \(a^{\star}\).

It remains to verify that the equal-valued dual has an attained certificate, rather than merely the correct value.  The set \(K\) is compact by the preceding argument.  The map \(v\mapsto v\otimes v\) is continuous.  Let \(d\) be the dimension of the finite-dimensional vector space of self-adjoint operators on \(E\).  Every convex combination of more than \(d+1\) rank-one operators can be shortened: the participating operators are affinely dependent, so there are coefficients \(\alpha_k\), not all zero, with \(\sum_k\alpha_k=0\) and \(\sum_k\alpha_k(v_k\otimes v_k)=0\); subtracting \(t\alpha_k\) from the original weights, where \(t=\min_{\alpha_k>0}\lambda_k/\alpha_k\), preserves the represented operator and total weight, leaves every weight nonnegative, and makes at least one weight zero.  Iteration gives a representation with at most \(d+1\) terms.  Thus \(\mathfrak M(K)\), as defined in \(\mathrm{def:moment-body}\), is the continuous image of the compact set consisting of the \(d\)-simplex times \(K^{d+1}\), and is compact.

For \(M\in\mathfrak M(K)\), \(z\in\Omega_n\), and \(c\in\mathbb R^{V_n}\), put
\[
 q_z(c;M)=
 \langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}c,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}c)\rangle_E.
\]
Writing \(A=N_zMN_z^{\ast}\) and \(b=N_zM\ell\), expansion of the inner product yields
\[
 q_z(c;M)=\langle\ell,M\ell\rangle_E-2\langle c,b\rangle_{V_n}
 +\langle c,Ac\rangle_{V_n}.
\]
We now derive the required range condition.  If \(y\in\ker(A)\), then
\[
 0=\langle y,Ay\rangle_{V_n}
 =\langle N_z^{\ast}y,MN_z^{\ast}y\rangle_E.
\]
For a positive semidefinite self-adjoint operator \(M\), \(\langle u,Mu\rangle_E=0\) implies \(Mu=0\): for every \(f\in E\), nonnegativity of the quadratic polynomial \(\langle u+tf,M(u+tf)\rangle_E\) for all real \(t\), together with its zero constant term, forces \(\langle f,Mu\rangle_E=0\).  Applying this with \(u=N_z^{\ast}y\) gives \(MN_z^{\ast}y=0\), and hence
\[
 \langle y,b\rangle_{V_n}
 =\langle N_z^{\ast}y,M\ell\rangle_E=0.
\]
Thus \(b\perp\ker(A)\).  Since \(A\) is self-adjoint on finite-dimensional Euclidean space, \(\operatorname{range}(A)=(\ker A)^{\perp}\), so \(b\in\operatorname{range}(A)\).  Hence \(AA^{\dagger}b=b\), and direct completion of the square gives
\[
 q_z(c;M)=\psi_z(M)
 +\langle c-A^{\dagger}b,A(c-A^{\dagger}b)\rangle_{V_n}.
\]
Because \(A\) is positive semidefinite, the last term is nonnegative and vanishes at \(c=A^{\dagger}b\).  Therefore
\[
 \psi_z(M)=\min_{c\in\mathbb R^{V_n}}q_z(c;M). \tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(c\), the map \(M\mapsto q_z(c;M)\) is continuous and affine.  Equation (3) makes \(\psi_z\) a pointwise infimum of continuous functions, hence upper semicontinuous: if \(M_k\to M\), then for every fixed \(c\),
\[
 \limsup_k\psi_z(M_k)
 \le\lim_k q_z(c;M_k)=q_z(c;M),
\]
and taking the infimum over \(c\) gives \(\limsup_k\psi_z(M_k)\le\psi_z(M)\).  The finite nonnegative weighted sum \(M\mapsto\sum_zp_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M)\) is therefore upper semicontinuous.  A maximizing sequence in compact \(\mathfrak M(K)\) has a convergent subsequence, and upper semicontinuity shows that its limit \(\widetilde M^{\star}\) attains the maximum.  When \(F>0\), the nonempty maximizer set contains a member of minimum rank because rank takes values in the finite set \(\{0,\ldots,\dim(E)\}\); this member is the defined \(M^{\star}\), so \(\widetilde M^{\star}\) may be chosen as \(M^{\star}\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\) identifies that attained value with \(\mathcal D\), while \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) proves \(F=\mathcal D\).  This yields the asserted matching certificate.
