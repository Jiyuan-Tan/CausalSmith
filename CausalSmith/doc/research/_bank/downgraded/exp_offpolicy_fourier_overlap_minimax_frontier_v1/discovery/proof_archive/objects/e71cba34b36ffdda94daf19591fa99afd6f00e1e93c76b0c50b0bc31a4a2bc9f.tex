Let \(\mathcal V_i=\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\) and \(r_i=|\mathcal V_i|\).  By \texttt{def:local-evaluation-polytope}, each \(K_i\) is the intersection of a finite-dimensional linear space with finitely many closed half-spaces \(-1\leq v_i(x)\leq1\); it is bounded and contains zero.  Hence it is a nonempty compact polytope.  By \texttt{def:bounded-fourier-class}, \(K=\prod_iK_i\), and an elementary product argument gives
\[
 \operatorname{vert}(K)=\prod_{i\in V_n}\mathcal V_i.
\]
Indeed, if one coordinate of a point in the product is a nontrivial convex combination, then so is the product point; conversely, projecting a nontrivial convex decomposition of a tuple of coordinatewise vertices onto each coordinate forces both endpoints to equal that tuple.

For a fixed weight rule \(a\), put
\[
 Q_a(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2.
\]
The source masses are nonnegative by \texttt{ass:source-product-design}; every summand is the square of a linear functional of \(v\).  Thus \(Q_a\) is convex.  If \(v=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{vert}(K)}\lambda_e e\), where \(\lambda_e\geq0\) and \(\sum_e\lambda_e=1\), convexity yields
\[
 Q_a(v)\leq\sum_e\lambda_eQ_a(e)
 \leq\max_{e\in\operatorname{vert}(K)}Q_a(e).
\]
The reverse inequality follows because every vertex lies in \(K\).  Therefore \texttt{def:linear-frontier} and the attainment assertion in \texttt{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual} give the exact finite convex epigraph
\[
 F=\min_{a,t}t
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_ze-\theta(e)\bigr)^2\leq t
 \quad\text{for every }e\in\prod_{i\in V_n}\mathcal V_i.
\]
It has \(n2^n+1\) scalar decision variables and \(\prod_i r_i\) convex quadratic constraints before any structural compression.  The same established lemma supplies the equal-valued moment certificate \(F=\mathcal D\).  This proves exact finite computability after full expansion, but the displayed counts do not prove a complexity lower bound because many constraints may admit an implicit representation.

On the negative side, \texttt{prop:raw-incidence-bag-moment-obstruction} gives, for every even \(r\geq2\), a raw incidence star of treewidth one and two genuine priors with identical ordinary edge-bag laws but respective target second moments \((2q-1)^2/r^2\) and zero.  Consequently no exact moment formulation whose state consists only of those ordinary overlap-consistent edge-bag laws can recover the term \(\langle\ell,M\ell\rangle_E\) in \texttt{def:moment-dual}.  Its \(r=2\) case is the smallest such additive-star failure.  This obstruction does not apply to a state that also carries a global target sum, and therefore it proves neither a strict gap in \(F\) nor a lower bound against every compressed representation.
