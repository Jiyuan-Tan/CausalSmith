The previously established genuine-prior certificates give, for every admissible fixed instance,
\[
 \max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\right\}
 \le R^{\star}\le F=\mathcal D\le S_{\mathrm{fib}},
 \qquad
 \frac{F}{\min\{r_{\star},\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}\}}
 \le R^{\star}.
\]
The following exact calculation extends that partial result by completing the prescribed additive launch-or-kill test.

Fix an integer \(d\ge1\).  Under the source design let the \(d\) assignment coordinates of the nonconstant hub be independent Bernoulli variables with probability \(1/2\), and write
\[
 S_j=2Z_j-1\in\{-1,1\},
 \qquad
 S=(S_1,\ldots,S_d).
\]
The source-overlap condition holds because \(\varepsilon<1/2\).  Give the hub the downward-closed support
\[
 \{\varnothing,\{1\},\ldots,\{d\}\}.
\]
At source probability \(1/2\), the retained biased Fourier characters are \(1,S_1,\ldots,S_d\).  Thus every hub schedule has the unique form
\[
 h_{\beta}(s)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^d\beta_js_j.
\]
For every coefficient vector \(\beta=(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_d)\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^d}|h_{\beta}(s)|
 \le |\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^d|\beta_j|.
\]
Conversely, choose the signs \(s_j\) so that all nonzero terms \(\beta_js_j\) have the sign of \(\beta_0\); if \(\beta_0=0\), choose either common sign.  Equality then holds.  Consequently the bounded evaluation polytope of the hub is exactly
\[
 B_1^{d+1}=\left\{\beta\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:
 |\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^d|\beta_j|\le1\right\}.
\]
Give every other unit only its constant Fourier character.  Its constant schedule is observed under every assignment, so its contribution to the causal target can be subtracted without error.  The common factor \(n^{-1}\) multiplying the hub target multiplies both minimax risks by \(n^{-2}\) and hence does not affect their ratio.  It is therefore enough to calculate the unnormalised hub risks.

Let the target Bernoulli probabilities obey the target-overlap condition and put
\[
 m_j=2\pi'_j-1,
 \qquad
 \gamma_0=1,
 \qquad
 \gamma_j=m_j\quad(1\le j\le d).
\]
Independence under the target product policy gives
\[
 L(\beta)=\mathbb E_{\pi'}[h_{\beta}(S)]
 =\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^dm_j\beta_j
 =\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j\beta_j.
\]
For a realized source sign vector \(s\), define \(r_0(s)=1\) and \(r_j(s)=s_j\).  The observed hub outcome is the scalar
\[
 Y=h_{\beta}(S)=\sum_{j=0}^dr_j(S)\beta_j.
\]

Consider first an arbitrary assignment-indexed linear rule \(a(S)Y\), where \(a:\{-1,1\}^d\to\mathbb R\).  Its unnormalised risk is the positive semidefinite quadratic form
\[
 Q_a(\beta)=
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \left\{a(S)\sum_{j=0}^dr_j(S)\beta_j-
             \sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j\beta_j\right\}^2
 \right].
\]
Since \(B_1^{d+1}=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_0,\ldots,\pm e_d\}\) and \(Q_a\) is convex, Jensen's inequality applied to any such convex representation yields
\[
 \sup_{\beta\in B_1^{d+1}}Q_a(\beta)
 =\max_{0\le j\le d}L_j(a),
 \qquad
 L_j(a)=\mathbb E[(a(S)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2].
\]
Thus the unnormalised linear frontier is
\[
 F_d=\inf_a\max_{0\le j\le d}L_j(a).
\]

Let
\[
 \Delta_d=\left\{\lambda\in[0,1]^{d+1}:
                    \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j=1\right\}
\]
and define
\[
 G(\lambda)=
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j\gamma_j^2-
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^2\gamma_j^2
 =\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
   +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j).
\]
For every \(a\) and \(\lambda\in\Delta_d\), completing the square pointwise and using
\(\mathbb E[r_j(S)r_k(S)]=\mathbf 1\{j=k\}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_jL_j(a)
 &\ge\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_jL_j(a)\\
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[
       \left(a(S)-\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j\gamma_jr_j(S)\right)^2
     \right]+G(\lambda)\\
 &\ge G(\lambda).
 \end{aligned}
\]
It follows that \(F_d\ge\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}G(\lambda)\).  Compactness of the simplex and continuity of \(G\) give a maximizer \(\lambda^{\star}\).  Set
\[
 a^{\star}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(s),
 \qquad
 T=\sum_{k=0}^d(\lambda_k^{\star})^2\gamma_k^2.
\]
Direct orthogonality calculation gives
\[
 L_j(a^{\star})=T+\gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star}).
\]
We now verify the needed simplex optimality condition without importing it as a hypothesis.  If \(\lambda_j^{\star}>0\) and \(\lambda_k^{\star}>0\), varying \(\lambda^{\star}\) by \(t(e_j-e_k)\) for both signs of sufficiently small \(t\) shows that the two directional derivatives vanish, and hence
\[
 \gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star})
 =\gamma_k^2(1-2\lambda_k^{\star}).
\]
If \(\lambda_j^{\star}=0\) and \(\lambda_k^{\star}>0\), the feasible one-sided variation \(t(e_j-e_k)\), \(t\ge0\), cannot increase \(G\); therefore
\[
 \gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star})
 \le\gamma_k^2(1-2\lambda_k^{\star}).
\]
Hence all indices in the support of \(\lambda^{\star}\) have one common value of the displayed derivative, and all other indices have no larger value.  It follows that all supported \(L_j(a^{\star})\) are equal and dominate the unsupported ones.  Since
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}L_j(a^{\star})
 =G(\lambda^{\star}),
\]
their common value is \(G(\lambda^{\star})\).  Therefore
\[
 \max_jL_j(a^{\star})=G(\lambda^{\star}),
 \qquad
 F_d=max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}
 \left\{\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
 +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j)\right\}.
\]
For rational target probabilities this is an exact finite concave quadratic program with rational data; the displayed \(a^{\star}\) and the completed-square inequality are matching primal and dual certificates.

It remains to compare all Borel rules.  Draw an index \(J\in\{0,\ldots,d\}\) with probabilities \(\lambda_j^{\star}\), draw an independent Rademacher sign \(T_0\), and take the random coefficient vector
\[
 \mathbf B=T_0e_J.
\]
This is a countably additive symmetric probability law supported on the genuine bounded schedule body \(B_1^{d+1}\), and it is independent of the source assignment, as required for a decision-theoretic prior on fixed schedules.  Conditional on \(J=j\), the observed value is
\[
 Y=T_0r_j(S)\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
For every fixed \(s\) and every \(j\), each sign of \(Y\) has probability \(1/2\).  Bayes' formula therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P(J=j\mid S=s,Y=y)=\lambda_j^{\star}.
\]
Moreover \(T_0=Yr_J(S)\), so the target conditional on \((S,Y,J)\) is
\[
 L(\mathbf B)=Y\gamma_Jr_J(S).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E[L(\mathbf B)\mid S,Y]
 =Y\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(S)
 =Ya^{\star}(S),
\]
and its average posterior variance is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\operatorname{Var}
       (L(\mathbf B)\mid S,Y)\right]
 &=\mathbb E_S\!\left[
      \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_j^2-
      \left(\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(S)\right)^2
    \right]\\
 &=G(\lambda^{\star})=F_d.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For any Borel estimator, its worst-case risk over the bounded schedule body is at least its average risk under this genuine prior, and conditional expectation minimizes squared Bayes loss.  Hence \(R_d^{\star}\ge F_d\).  The reverse inequality \(R_d^{\star}\le F_d\) holds because every linear rule is Borel.  We conclude exactly that
\[
 R_d^{\star}=F_d
 =\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}
 \left\{\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
 +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j)\right\}.
\]
Restoring the population-average normalization multiplies both sides by \(n^{-2}\), and exactly observed constant units add no error.  Thus the ratio is one for every dimension in the prescribed additive-hub family.  This rules out that family as a vanishing-ratio witness but neither proves a dimension-free comparison for general downward-closed supports containing interactions nor supplies a counterexample there.
