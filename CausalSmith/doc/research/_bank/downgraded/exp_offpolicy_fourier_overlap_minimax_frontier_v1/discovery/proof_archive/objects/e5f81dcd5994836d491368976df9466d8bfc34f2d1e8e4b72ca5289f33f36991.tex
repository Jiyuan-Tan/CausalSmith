Fix \(n\).  By \texttt{def:indexed-hilbert-geometry} and source positivity in \texttt{ass:source-product-design} and \texttt{ass:source-overlap}, \(E_n\) is a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space and \(p_{\pi_n}(z)>0\) for every \(z\in\Omega_n\).  By \texttt{def:indexed-bounded-fourier-class}, each \(K_{n,i}\) is the intersection of a finite-dimensional linear space with finitely many closed slabs.  It is therefore a compact, centrally symmetric polytope.  It contains a neighborhood of zero relative to \(H_{n,i}\), because finitely many evaluation functionals are continuous.  Consequently \(K_n=\prod_iK_{n,i}\) is a compact, centrally symmetric polytope containing a ball
\[
 \{v_n\in E_n:\|v_n\|_{E_n}\le \rho_n\}\subseteq K_n
\]
for some \(\rho_n>0\).

The observation assertion follows directly from \texttt{def:indexed-target-selector-geometry}: conditional on assignment \(z\), the observed vector has coordinates \(v_{n,i}(z_{\Gamma_{n,i}})\), and hence is exactly \(N_{n,z}v_n\).  The fixed-schedule condition \texttt{ass:fixed-schedules} makes \(v_n\) nonrandom under this design; all expectations below are therefore only over \(z\).

Let \(\ell_n\) be the Riesz vector from \texttt{def:indexed-psd-riesz-geometry}, and put
\[
 q_z(a_{n,z})=N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n,
 \qquad
 f_v(a_n)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi_n}(z)
       \langle q_z(a_{n,z}),v\rangle_{E_n}^{,2}.
\]
The adjoint identity and \(\theta_n(v)=\langle\ell_n,v\rangle_{E_n}\) show, term by term, that \(f_v(a_n)\) is precisely the design risk of the indexed linear rule at schedule \(v\).  If \(\mathcal V_n\) denotes the finite vertex set of \(K_n\), then
\[
 \sup_{v\in K_n}f_v(a_n)=\max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n).
\]
Indeed, \(f_v(a_n)=\langle v,Q(a_n)v\rangle_{E_n}\) for the positive semidefinite operator
\[
 Q(a_n)=\sum_zp_{\pi_n}(z)q_z(a_{n,z})\otimes q_z(a_{n,z}),
\]
so \(v\mapsto f_v(a_n)\) is convex; writing a point of the polytope as a convex combination of vertices proves the display.

We next justify attainment without compactifying the coefficient space artificially.  For each \(z\), coefficient directions in \(\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})\) do not change \(q_z\), so restrict \(a_{n,z}\) to
\[
 A_{n,z}=\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})^{\perp}=\operatorname{range}(N_{n,z}).
\]
On \(A_{n,z}\), the map \(N_{n,z}^{\ast}\) is injective.  Moreover, using the ball contained in \(K_n\),
\[
 \max_{v\in K_n}f_v(a_n)
 \ge p_{\pi_n}(z)\max_{\|v\|_{E_n}\le\rho_n}
          \langle q_z(a_{n,z}),v\rangle_{E_n}^{,2}
 =p_{\pi_n}(z)\rho_n^2\|q_z(a_{n,z})\|_{E_n}^2.
\]
Finite-dimensional injectivity gives a positive smallest singular value for \(N_{n,z}^{\ast}\) on every nonzero \(A_{n,z}\).  Since every source mass is positive, the last display proves that the continuous function \(a_n\mapsto\max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n)\) is coercive on \(\prod_zA_{n,z}\).  It therefore has a minimizer \(a_n^{\star}\).  Adding arbitrary elements of \(\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})\) describes all redundant representatives.

It remains to establish the exact dual without assuming a minimax theorem on a noncompact set.  For \(M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)\), define
\[
 \Phi(a_n,M)=\sum_zp_{\pi_n}(z)
 \langle N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n,
 M(N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n)\rangle_{E_n}.
\]
If \(M=\sum_r\lambda_r(v_r\otimes v_r)\), with \(v_r\in K_n\), \(\lambda_r\ge0\), and \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\), then
\[
 \Phi(a_n,M)=\sum_r\lambda_rf_{v_r}(a_n)
 \le \max_{v\in K_n}f_v(a_n).
\]
Hence \(\max_M\inf_{a_n}\Phi(a_n,M)\le F_n\).

For the reverse inequality, let \(I\subseteq\mathcal V_n\) be the vertices active at \(a_n^{\star}\).  The origin belongs to the convex hull of \(\{\nabla f_v(a_n^{\star}):v\in I\}\).  Otherwise finite-dimensional strict separation would give a direction \(d\) having negative inner product with every active gradient.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal V_n\), continuity, and the positive gap to every inactive value would then imply
\[
 \max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n^{\star}+td)
 <\max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n^{\star})
\]
for all sufficiently small \(t>0\), contradicting minimality.  Thus there are \(v_r\in I\) and \(\lambda_r\ge0\), \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\), such that \(\sum_r\lambda_r\nabla f_{v_r}(a_n^{\star})=0\).  Set
\[
 M^{\star}=\sum_r\lambda_r(v_r\otimes v_r)\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n).
\]
The function \(a_n\mapsto\Phi(a_n,M^{\star})=\sum_r\lambda_rf_{v_r}(a_n)\) is differentiable and convex, and its gradient vanishes at \(a_n^{\star}\).  Therefore \(a_n^{\star}\) minimizes it.  Since every \(v_r\) is active,
\[
 \inf_{a_n}\Phi(a_n,M^{\star})
 =\Phi(a_n^{\star},M^{\star})
 =\sum_r\lambda_rF_n=F_n.
\]
Together with the preceding weak inequality, this proves
\[
 F_n=\max_{M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)}\inf_{a_n}\Phi(a_n,M).
\]

Finally fix \(M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)\).  The minimization separates over \(z\).  Write
\[
 A_z=N_{n,z}MN_{n,z}^{\ast},
 \qquad b_z=N_{n,z}M\ell_n.
\]
If \(r\in\ker(A_z)\), positivity gives
\[
 0=\langle r,A_zr\rangle_{V_n}
   =\|M^{1/2}N_{n,z}^{\ast}r\|_{E_n}^2,
\]
and hence
\[
 \langle r,b_z\rangle_{V_n}
 =\langle M^{1/2}N_{n,z}^{\ast}r,M^{1/2}\ell_n\rangle_{E_n}=0.
\]
Thus \(b_z\perp\ker(A_z)\), equivalently \(b_z\in\operatorname{range}(A_z)\).  Expanding the quadratic and completing the square with the Moore--Penrose inverse yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\inf_{a_{n,z}\in\mathbb R^{V_n}}
 \langle N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n,
 M(N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n)\rangle_{E_n}\\
 &\quad=\langle\ell_n,M\ell_n\rangle_{E_n}
 -\langle b_z,A_z^{\dagger}b_z\rangle_{V_n}
 =\psi_{n,z}(M).
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, \(a_{n,z}=A_z^{\dagger}b_z\) attains the infimum, with arbitrary kernel directions also allowed.  Summing with the positive source weights gives
\[
 F_n=\max_{M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)}
       \sum_zp_{\pi_n}(z)\psi_{n,z}(M)=\mathcal D_n
\]
by \texttt{def:moment-dual}.  The fixed-instance equality \(F=\mathcal D\) is the same identity under the abbreviations specified in that definition.
