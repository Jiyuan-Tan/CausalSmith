Fix \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), with no restriction that they differ, and define
\[
 A_{p,q}=(1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2,
 \qquad
 C_{p,q}=\frac{(1-q)^2}{1-p}+\frac{q^2}{p}.
\]
By \texttt{ass:source-overlap} and \texttt{ass:target-overlap}, \(0<p,q<1\).  Both summands of \(A_{p,q}\) are strictly positive, so \(A_{p,q}>0\); both denominators in \(C_{p,q}\) are strictly positive, so \(C_{p,q}<\infty\).  These constants depend only on the fixed policies, not on \(n\).

For every \(n\ge2\), \texttt{lem:hub-frontier-rate-sandwich} gives
\[
 \frac{n-1}{n^2}A_{p,q}
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n
 \le\frac{1+(n-1)C_{p,q}}{n^2}.
\]
Since \((n-1)/n^2\ge1/(2n)\) and
\(
 [1+(n-1)C_{p,q}]/n^2\le(1+C_{p,q})/n
\) for \(n\ge2\),
\[
 \frac{A_{p,q}}{2n}
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n
 \le\frac{1+C_{p,q}}{n}.
\]
In particular, each of \(R_n^{\star}\) and \(F_n\) is bounded above and below by positive finite multiples of \(n^{-1}\).  Thus
\[
 R_n^{\star}=\Theta(n^{-1})
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 F_n=\Theta(n^{-1}).
\]
This conclusion includes \(p=q\); for example, at that boundary \(A_{p,p}=p(1-p)>0\) and \(C_{p,p}=1\).

Set
\[
 E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =\frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}{n}.
\]
The strengthened \texttt{lem:hub-loomba-eckles-envelope} proves the exact identity
\[
 E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =1+n c,
 \qquad
 c=\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
For either \(X_n=F_n\) or \(X_n=R_n^{\star}\), the preceding sandwich implies
\[
 \frac{n}{1+C_{p,q}}
 \le \frac{1}{X_n}
 \le \frac{2n}{A_{p,q}}.
\]
If \(p=q\), then \(c=0\) and \(E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}=1\); multiplication of the last display by one gives
\[
 \frac{E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}}{F_n}=\Theta(n),
 \qquad
 \frac{E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}}{R_n^{\star}}=\Theta(n).
\]
If \(p\ne q\) is fixed, then \(c>0\), and \(n\ge1\) gives
\[
 cn\le E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}=1+cn\le(1+c)n.
\]
Combining this with the reciprocal bounds yields, for either permitted \(X_n\),
\[
 \frac{c}{1+C_{p,q}}n^2
 \le \frac{E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}}{X_n}
 \le \frac{2(1+c)}{A_{p,q}}n^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}}{F_n}=\Theta(n^2),
 \qquad
 \frac{E_n^{\mathrm{LE}}}{R_n^{\star}}=\Theta(n^2)
 \quad\text{when fixed }p\ne q.
\]
The neighborhoods remain genuinely overlapping in both cases because the hub intersects every periphery neighborhood.  Hence the exact frontiers exploit the signed incidence geometry at rate \(n^{-1}\), whereas the published maximum-overlap sufficient envelope is larger by a factor of order \(n\) on-policy and order \(n^2\) under a fixed nonzero policy shift.  No universal comparison between \(F_n\) and \(R_n^{\star}\) is used.
