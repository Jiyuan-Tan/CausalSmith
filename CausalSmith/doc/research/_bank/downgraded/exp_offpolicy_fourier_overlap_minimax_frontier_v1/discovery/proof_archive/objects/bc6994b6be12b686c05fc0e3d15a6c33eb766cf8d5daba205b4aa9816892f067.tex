Fix \(d\geq1\) and an instance described in the statement.  The source-product design and \(\pi_j=1/2\) imply that \(S_1,\ldots,S_d\) are independent Rademacher variables.  By \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-character}\),
\[
 \chi_{i_{\circ},\{j\}}^{\pi}(Z)
 =\frac{Z_j-1/2}{\sqrt{(1/2)(1-1/2)}}=2Z_j-1=S_j.
\]
The retained characters in \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-space}\) are therefore exactly \(1,S_1,ldots,S_d\), which are linearly independent under the positive source law.  Hence each distinguished schedule has a unique representation
\[
 h_{\beta}(s)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^d\beta_js_j.
\]
For every \(s\in\{-1,1\}^d\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 |h_{\beta}(s)|\leq |\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^d|\beta_j|.
\]
Conversely, if \(\beta_0\neq0\), choose each \(s_j\) so that \(\beta_js_j\) has the sign of \(\beta_0\); if \(\beta_0=0\), choose \(s_j\) so that \(\beta_js_j\geq0\).  In either case equality holds for that \(s\).  Consequently \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\) gives the exact coefficient body
\[
 K_{i_{\circ}}
 \cong B_1^{d+1}
 :=\left\{\beta\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:\sum_{j=0}^d|\beta_j|\leq1\right\}.
\]
This verifies rather than assumes the bounded-schedule membership of the family.  The support is downward closed, source overlap holds because \(\varepsilon<1/2\), and target overlap makes every \(m_j=2\pi'_j-1\) well defined with \(|m_j|<1\).

Under the target-product policy, \(\mathbb E_{\pi'}S_j=2\pi'_j-1=m_j\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{def:local-target-mass}\) and \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\) give the unnormalized distinguished-unit target
\[
 L(\beta)=\mathbb E_{\pi'}h_{\beta}(S)
 =\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^dm_j\beta_j
 =\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j\beta_j.
\]
The selector in \(\mathrm{def:observation-operator}\) reveals
\[
 Y=h_{\beta}(S)=\sum_{j=0}^dr_j(S)\beta_j.
\]
Assignment coordinates outside \(\{1,ldots,d\}\) are ancillary.  Indeed, if \(W\) denotes those coordinates and an assignment-indexed linear rule uses the hub coefficient \(A(S,W)\), put \(\bar a(S)=\mathbb E[A(S,W)\mid S]\).  For each \(j\), conditional Jensen gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!left[(A(S,W)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2\mid S\right]
 \geq (\bar a(S)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2.
\]
Thus ancillary assignment dependence cannot reduce any of the coordinate risks below that of a rule \(a(S)Y\).

For such a rule, define
\[
 Q_a(\beta)=\mathbb E\left[
 \left\{a(S)\sum_{j=0}^dr_j(S)\beta_j-
             \sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j\beta_j\right\}^2
 \right]
\]
and
\[
 L_j(a)=Q_a(e_j)=\mathbb E[(a(S)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2].
\]
The function \(Q_a\) is a positive semidefinite quadratic form and hence convex.  Since
\(
B_1^{d+1}=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_0,ldots,\pm e_d\}
\), convexity and \(Q_a(-e_j)=Q_a(e_j)\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\beta\in B_1^{d+1}}Q_a(\beta)=\max_{0\leq j\leq d}L_j(a).
\]
Accordingly,
\[
 F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}=\inf_a\max_{0\leq j\leq d}L_j(a).
\]

For \(\lambda\in\Delta_d\), orthogonality of the Rademacher characters gives \(\mathbb E[r_j(S)r_k(S)]=\mathbf 1\{j=k\}\).  Completing the square pointwise therefore yields, for every \(a\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_jL_j(a)
 &\geq\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_jL_j(a)\\
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[
 \left(a(S)-\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j\gamma_jr_j(S)\right)^2
 \right]+G(\lambda)\\
 &\geq G(\lambda),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G(\lambda)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j\gamma_j^2
   -\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^2\gamma_j^2\\
 &=\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
   +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(a\) and then the supremum over \(\lambda\) proves
\[
 F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\geq\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}G(\lambda).
\]
The simplex is compact and \(G\) is continuous, so a maximizer \(\lambda^{\star}\) exists.

Define
\[
 a^{\star}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(s),
 \qquad
 T=\sum_{j=0}^d(\lambda_j^{\star})^2\gamma_j^2.
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
 L_j(a^{\star})=T+\gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star}).
\]
We now certify optimality on the simplex directly.  If \(\lambda_j^{\star}>0\) and \(\lambda_k^{\star}>0\), the feasible perturbation \(\lambda^{\star}+t(e_j-e_k)\) exists for both signs of all sufficiently small \(t\).  Maximality forces the derivative at zero to vanish, so
\[
 \gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star})
 =\gamma_k^2(1-2\lambda_k^{\star}).
\]
If \(\lambda_j^{\star}=0\) and \(\lambda_k^{\star}>0\), the same perturbation is feasible for sufficiently small \(t\geq0\); its one-sided derivative cannot be positive, and hence
\[
 \gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star})
 \leq\gamma_k^2(1-2\lambda_k^{\star}).
\]
Thus the values \(L_j(a^{\star})\) are equal on the support of \(\lambda^{\star}\), and every value off that support is no larger.  Moreover, the completed-square identity at \(a=a^{\star}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}L_j(a^{\star})=G(\lambda^{\star}).
\]
The left side is the common supported value because the supported weights sum to one.  Therefore
\[
 \max_jL_j(a^{\star})=G(\lambda^{\star}),
 \qquad
 F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}=G(\lambda^{\star}).
\]
This supplies matching primal and simplex-dual certificates, not merely a candidate rule.

It remains to show that nonlinear Borel rules cannot improve the value.  Let \(J\) have probabilities \(\lambda_j^{\star}\), let \(T_0\) be an independent Rademacher sign, and set
\[
 \mathbf B=T_0e_J.
\]
Because every \(\pm e_j\) belongs to \(B_1^{d+1}\), this is a countably additive symmetric prior supported on genuine bounded schedules, and it is independent of the source assignment as required by the fixed-schedule convention.  Conditional on \(J=j\),
\[
 Y=T_0r_j(S)\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
For every \(s\), every \(j\), and every \(y\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \mathbb P(Y=y\mid S=s,J=j)=\frac12.
\]
Bayes' formula consequently gives
\[
 \mathbb P(J=j\mid S=s,Y=y)=\lambda_j^{\star}.
\]
The ancillary coordinates \(W\) are independent of \((J,T_0)\) and add no information, so the same posterior holds conditional on \((S,W,Y)\).  Since \(T_0=Yr_J(S)\),
\[
 L(\mathbf B)=Y\gamma_Jr_J(S)
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E[L(\mathbf B)\mid S,W,Y]
 =Y\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(S)
 =Ya^{\star}(S).
\]
Its average posterior variance is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\operatorname{Var}(L(\mathbf B)\mid S,W,Y)\right]
 &=\mathbb E_S\!\left[
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_j^2-
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(S)\right)^2
 \right]\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_j^2
   -\sum_{j=0}^d(\lambda_j^{\star})^2\gamma_j^2\\
 &=G(\lambda^{\star})=F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, if \(D=D(S,W,Y)\) is any Borel estimator and \(\mu=\mathbb E[L(\mathbf B)\mid S,W,Y]\), expansion followed by conditional expectation gives
\[
 \mathbb E[(D-L(\mathbf B))^2]
 =\mathbb E[(D-\mu)^2]
  +\mathbb E[(L(\mathbf B)-\mu)^2]
 \geq F_d^{\mathrm{hub}},
\]
because the cross term has conditional expectation zero.  The worst-case risk is at least this prior-average risk.  Thus \(R_d^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}\geq F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\).  Conversely, \(a^{\star}(S)Y\) is Borel, so \(R_d^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}\leq F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\), proving the unnormalized equality.

Finally, every nondistinguished schedule is constant, its realized outcome equals its target-policy mean under every assignment, and it can therefore be included with coefficient \(1/n\) without error.  The rule
\[
 \widehat\theta
 =\frac1n\left(a^{\star}(S)Y+
          \sum_{i\neq i_{\circ}}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\right)
\]
has worst-case risk \(n^{-2}G(\lambda^{\star})\).  For the reverse inequality, restrict the full schedule class to zero constants on all other units and use the preceding genuine prior on the distinguished schedule; multiplication of the target by \(n^{-1}\) multiplies squared loss by \(n^{-2}\).  This proves
\[
 R^{\star}=F=n^{-2}G(\lambda^{\star}).
\]
As the smallest exact stress test, when \(d=1\) the simplex has \(\lambda_1=1-\lambda_0\), so
\[
 R_1^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}=F_1^{\mathrm{hub}}
 =\max_{0\leq\lambda_0\leq1}(1+m_1^2)\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
 =\frac{1+m_1^2}{4}.
\]
In particular the equality remains valid at \(\pi'_1=\pi_1=1/2\).  The proof used only additive supports; it supplies no dimension-free rounding for supports containing interactions.
