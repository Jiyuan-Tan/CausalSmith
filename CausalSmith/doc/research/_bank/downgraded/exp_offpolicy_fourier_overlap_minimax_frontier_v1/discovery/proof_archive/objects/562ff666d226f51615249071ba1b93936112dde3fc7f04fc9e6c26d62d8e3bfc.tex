By \texttt{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}, the selector experiment has an attaining linear rule and \(F=\mathcal D\).  If \(\mathcal V=\operatorname{vert}(K)\), convexity of
\[
Q_a(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
\]
in \(v\) gives the exact finite epigraph
\[
F=\min_{a,t}t
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
\bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_ze-\theta(e)\bigr)^2\le t
\quad\text{for every }e\in\mathcal V.
\]
This formulation is exact but is indexed by all assignments and all vertices, so it is generally exponential rather than a \(\operatorname{poly}(L_{\mathrm{in}})2^{O(w)}\) algorithm.  The proved proposition \texttt{prop:raw-incidence-bag-moment-obstruction} shows at raw incidence treewidth one that ordinary overlap-consistent edge-bag moment records do not determine the global target second moment.  Thus ordinary raw-bag moments fail, but this does not prove optimizer nonlocality, an exact-value gap, an algorithmic lower bound, or impossibility of a compressed representation.  In particular, whether a precisely encoded global target-sum/separator state yields exact bounded-width elimination remains open.
