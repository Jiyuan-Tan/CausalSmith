Fix \(\mathfrak A\in\mathsf{Adm}\). The established rowwise upper comparison gives \(0\le R_n^{\star}\le F_n\) for every \(n\). Hence, for every \(\eta>0\), if \(F_n<\eta\) then \(R_n^{\star}<\eta\), and therefore
\[
 F_n\longrightarrow0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_n^{\star}\longrightarrow0.
\]
There is also an exact zero-risk equivalence in every fixed row. For \(z\in\Omega_n\), define the fiber radius
\[
 \rho_{n,z}=\sup\{\lvert\theta_n(h)\rvert:h\in K_n,\ N_{n,z}h=0\}.
\]
Fix \(h\in K_n\) with \(N_{n,z}h=0\), and let \(d=\delta(z,0)\) and \(t=\theta_n(h)\). Central symmetry of \(K_n\) gives \(-h\in K_n\), while linearity gives \(N_{n,z}(-h)=0\) and \(\theta_n(-h)=-t\). Therefore
\[
 (d-t)^2+(d+t)^2=2d^2+2t^2\ge2t^2.
\]
Adding the two full design risks at \(h\) and \(-h\), retaining only assignment \(z\), and then taking the larger of those two risks gives
\[
 \sup_{v_n\in K_n}\sum_{u\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi_n}(u)
 \bigl(\delta(u,N_{n,u}v_n)-\theta_n(v_n)\bigr)^2
 \ge p_{\pi_n}(z)\theta_n(h)^2.
\]
Taking the supremum over such \(h\), then the infimum over \(\delta\), yields
\[
 R_n^{\star}\ge p_{\pi_n}(z)\rho_{n,z}^2.
\]
Compactness of \(K_n\cap\ker N_{n,z}\) makes this supremum attainable. If \(h_{n,z}\) is a maximizer, then the symmetric two-point law \(\nu_{n,z}=\tfrac12\delta_{h_{n,z}}+\tfrac12\delta_{-h_{n,z}}\) is a genuine probability law on \(K_n\). Let \(\mathbf V_n\sim\nu_{n,z}\). Under assignment \(z\), both atoms have observation zero, and their target values are \(\pm\theta_n(h_{n,z})\); hence their conditional target variance is \(\rho_{n,z}^2\). The remaining assignment contributions are nonnegative, so the same lower bound has the explicit genuine-prior form
\[
 \sum_{u\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi_n}(u)\,
 \mathbb E_{\nu_{n,z}}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{n,z}}\!\left(\theta_n(\mathbf V_n)\mid N_{n,u}\mathbf V_n\right)\right]
 \ge p_{\pi_n}(z)\rho_{n,z}^2.
\]
The same radii give an explicit, though dimension-dependent, comparison in the other direction. Let \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{K_n}\) be the Minkowski norm having the centrally symmetric convex body \(K_n\) as its unit ball. This is a norm because the finitely many evaluations separate points of \(E_n\), and \(K_n\) contains a neighborhood of zero. On the subspace \(S_z=\ker N_{n,z}\), the restriction of \(\theta_n\) has norm \(\rho_{n,z}\). We now derive the required norm-preserving extension. Suppose a linear functional \(f\) on a proper subspace \(S\subset E_n\) satisfies \(\lvert f(s)\rvert\le\rho_{n,z}\lVert s\rVert_{K_n}\), and choose \(x\notin S\). Set
\[
 L=\sup_{s\in S}\{f(s)-\rho_{n,z}\lVert s-x\rVert_{K_n}\},\qquad
 U=\inf_{t\in S}\{\rho_{n,z}\lVert t+x\rVert_{K_n}-f(t)\}.
\]
For every \(s,t\in S\), linearity and the triangle inequality give
\[
 f(s)+f(t)=f(s+t)
 \le\rho_{n,z}\lVert s+t\rVert_{K_n}
 \le\rho_{n,z}\bigl(\lVert s-x\rVert_{K_n}+\lVert t+x\rVert_{K_n}\bigr),
\]
so \(L\le U\). Choose \(\alpha\in[L,U]\) and define \(f'(s+\lambda x)=f(s)+\lambda\alpha\). If \(\lambda>0\), the definition of \(U\), applied to \(s/\lambda\), gives the upper bound \(f'(s+\lambda x)\le\rho_{n,z}\lVert s+\lambda x\rVert_{K_n}\); the definition of \(L\) gives the corresponding lower bound. The case \(\lambda<0\) follows after multiplying by \(-1\), and \(\lambda=0\) is the original bound. Repeating this one-dimensional extension finitely many times produces a linear functional \(q_z\) on \(E_n\) such that
\[
 q_z\big|_{\ker N_{n,z}}=\theta_n\big|_{\ker N_{n,z}},\qquad
 \sup_{v_n\in K_n}\lvert q_z(v_n)\rvert=\rho_{n,z}.
\]
Since \(q_z-\theta_n\) vanishes on \(\ker N_{n,z}\), it factors linearly through \(N_{n,z}\). Extending that factor from \(\operatorname{range}(N_{n,z})\) to Euclidean \(\mathbb R^{V_n}\), there is \(a_{n,z}\in\mathbb R^{V_n}\) such that \(q_z(v_n)=\theta_n(v_n)-a_{n,z}^{\mathsf T}N_{n,z}v_n\). Hence
\[
 \sup_{v_n\in K_n}\left\lvert a_{n,z}^{\mathsf T}N_{n,z}v_n-\theta_n(v_n)\right\rvert
 =\rho_{n,z}.
\]
Using these coefficients assignment by assignment in the definition of \(F_n\) yields
\[
 F_n\le\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi_n}(z)\rho_{n,z}^2
 \le\lvert\Omega_n\rvert\max_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi_n}(z)\rho_{n,z}^2
 \le2^nR_n^{\star}.
\]
Thus every fixed row obeys the fully explicit nonuniform sandwich
\[
 2^{-n}F_n\le R_n^{\star}\le F_n.
\]
Because \(2^{-n}>0\), this sandwich also proves the exact fixed-row equivalence
\[
 R_n^{\star}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F_n=0
\]
for every admissible row.

If the unresolved universal comparison supplied one numerical \(c_0>0\) before quantification over \(\mathfrak A\) and \(n\), then \(c_0F_n\le R_n^{\star}\) for every row. In that event, \(R_n^{\star}\to0\) would imply \(0\le F_n\le R_n^{\star}/c_0\to0\), and the two implications would prove the stated equivalence. The proved factor \(2^{-n}\) may converge to zero and therefore does not suffice for this limit implication. Thus the displayed forward implication, the finite-row zero equivalence, and the dimension-dependent sandwich are unconditional, while the reverse asymptotic implication and the asserted common-comparison predicate are exactly the unresolved row-uniform lower comparison.
