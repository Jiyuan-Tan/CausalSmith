For a fixed weight array \(a\), put
\[
Q_a(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
\bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2.
\]
Each residual in this sum is linear in \(v\). Since \(s\mapsto s^2\) is convex and the design masses are nonnegative by \(\mathrm{ass:source-product-design}\), for \(v,w\in K\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
Q_a(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)
\le \lambda Q_a(v)+(1-\lambda)Q_a(w).
\tag{1}
\]

We next justify the vertex reduction. By \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\), \(K_i\) is the intersection of the finite-dimensional space \(H_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{X_i}\) with the finitely many slabs \(-1\le h(x)\le1\), \(x\in X_i\). It contains zero, is closed, and is bounded in the evaluation coordinates; hence it is a nonempty compact polytope. Every point of this polytope is a convex combination of its vertices. Indeed, induct on the dimension of the point's smallest face. A zero-dimensional face is a vertex. In a positive-dimensional smallest face, a line through the point in a nonzero direction of the face meets the compact face in a closed segment with two boundary endpoints; the point is a convex combination of those endpoints, and each endpoint has a smallest face of strictly smaller dimension. The induction terminates because the dimension is finite. This argument also shows that the vertex set is finite, since every vertex is the unique solution of a full-rank subsystem selected from finitely many defining equalities.

Thus write each \(v_i\in K_i\) as \(v_i=\sum_{k_i}\lambda_{i,k_i}u_{i,k_i}\), where \(u_{i,k_i}\in\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\), \(\lambda_{i,k_i}\ge0\), and \(\sum_{k_i}\lambda_{i,k_i}=1\). Expanding the product gives
\[
v=\sum_{(k_i)_{i\in V_n}}
\left(\prod_{i\in V_n}\lambda_{i,k_i}\right)
(u_{i,k_i})_{i\in V_n},
\tag{2}
\]
a convex combination of the product vertices. Applying \((1)\) repeatedly to the finite combination \((2)\) yields
\[
\sup_{v\in K}Q_a(v)
=\max_{v\in\prod_i\operatorname{vert}(K_i)}Q_a(v).
\tag{3}
\]
The upper bound in \((3)\) follows from the convex combination, and the reverse bound follows because every listed product vertex belongs to \(K\) by \(\mathrm{def:bounded-fourier-class}\).

By \(\mathrm{def:observation-operator}\) and \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\), the residual at a product vertex is exactly
\[
a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)
=\sum_{i\in V_n}(a_z)_iv_i(z_{\Gamma_i})
-\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{x\in X_i}p_{\pi',i}(x)v_i(x).
\tag{4}
\]
Substituting \((3)\) and \((4)\) into \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) gives precisely the epigraph program \((P)\). There are \(2^n\) vectors \(a_z\in\mathbb R^{V_n}\) and one scalar \(t\), hence \(n2^n+1\) real variables. Equation \((3)\) supplies one listed constraint for each element of \(\prod_i\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\), hence the claimed product count. Each constraint is a nonnegative weighted sum of squares of affine functions of \(a\), bounded above by the affine variable \(t\); it is therefore a convex quadratic constraint. The input list in the statement is exactly the list of coefficients occurring in \((4)\). By \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\), the infimum over \(a\) is attained, so the epigraph optimum is attained with \(t=F\) and weight component \(a^{\star}\).

These coefficients are computable from the declared inputs. The source mass table follows from \(\mathrm{ass:source-product-design}\); source overlap and \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-character}\) give finite real character-evaluation tables; \(\mathrm{ass:hierarchical-support}\) fixes the finite retained subfamilies; and \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\) reduces each vertex calculation to finitely many linear equalities and inequalities. Likewise, \(\mathrm{ass:target-product-policy}\) and \(\mathrm{def:local-target-mass}\) compute every \(p_{\pi',i}(x)\). Thus no unlisted schedule value or unknown distribution enters \((P)\).

It remains to exhibit the matching dual optimizer. For \(M\in\mathfrak M(K)\), the range condition in \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\) and completion of the positive-semidefinite quadratic give, for every \(z\),
\[
\psi_z(M)=\min_{b\in\mathbb R^{V_n}}
\langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}b,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}b)\rangle_E.
\tag{5}
\]
To verify \((5)\), expand its right-hand side as
\[
\langle\ell,M\ell\rangle_E
-2\langle b,N_zM\ell\rangle_{V_n}
+\langle b,(N_zMN_z^{\ast})b\rangle_{V_n}.
\]
Because \(N_zM\ell\in\operatorname{range}(N_zMN_z^{\ast})\) by \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\), the minimizers solve the normal equation, and the minimum is
\[
\langle\ell,M\ell\rangle_E
-\left\langle N_zM\ell,
(N_zMN_z^{\ast})^{\dagger}N_zM\ell\right\rangle_{V_n},
\]
which is the defining formula for \(\psi_z(M)\). Independence of the finitely many coordinates \(b_z\) in \((5)\) implies
\[
\Phi(M):=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M)
=\inf_a\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
\langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}a_z,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}a_z)\rangle_E.
\tag{6}
\]
For fixed \(a\), the expression after the infimum is continuous and affine in \(M\); hence \(\Phi\), an infimum of continuous affine functions, is upper semicontinuous.

The set \(K\) is compact by the preceding local argument and the finite-product construction in \(\mathrm{def:bounded-fourier-class}\). Therefore \(\{v\otimes v:v\in K\}\) is compact. Its convex hull \(\mathfrak M(K)\) is compact as well. To see this directly, let \(d\) be the dimension of the real vector space of self-adjoint operators on \(E\). Any convex combination of more than \(d+1\) operators has affinely dependent support: there are coefficients, not all zero, whose sum and whose weighted operator sum are both zero. Moving the mixture weights in that null direction until one first reaches zero preserves the represented operator and removes one support point. Iteration represents every element of the convex hull using at most \(d+1\) support points. Thus \(\mathfrak M(K)\) is the continuous image of the compact product of \(d+1\) copies of \(\{v\otimes v:v\in K\}\) and the compact \(d\)-simplex, and is compact.

Choose a maximizing sequence for \(\Phi\) on \(\mathfrak M(K)\). Compactness gives a convergent subsequence with limit \(M^{\circ}\in\mathfrak M(K)\), and upper semicontinuity gives
\[
\Phi(M^{\circ})\ge\limsup_k\Phi(M_k)
=\sup_{M\in\mathfrak M(K)}\Phi(M).
\]
Thus the dual maximum is attained at \(M^{\circ}\). By \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\), \(\Phi(M^{\circ})=\mathcal D\), and the exact identity in \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) gives \(\mathcal D=F\). Consequently
\[
\Phi(M^{\circ})=\mathcal D=F,
\]
which is \((D)\). If \(F>0\), the nonempty set of maximizers has a minimum integer rank, and \(\mathrm{def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity}\) names such a certificate \(M^{\star}\).

For the smallest exact stress test, take \(n=1\), \(\Gamma_1=\{1\}\), and \(\mathcal S_1=\{\varnothing,\{1\}\}\). By \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-character}\) and source overlap, the two retained characters are linearly independent and span all functions on \(X_1=\{0,1\}\). Hence in evaluation coordinates \(K_1=[-1,1]^2\) and
\[
\operatorname{vert}(K_1)=\{(-1,-1),(-1,1),(1,-1),(1,1)\}.
\]
There are two assignments, \(z=0,1\). Program \((P)\) therefore has the three variables \((a_0,a_1,t)\), exactly \(1\cdot2^1+1\), and four listed constraints, exactly \(\lvert\operatorname{vert}(K_1)\rvert\). For every fixed \((a_0,a_1)\), its risk is a convex quadratic on the square. Expressing an arbitrary point of the square as the product mixture of its four corners and applying \((1)\) proves that none of the continuum of nonvertex schedules exceeds those four enumerated risks. Thus exhaustive enumeration agrees with \((P)\) on the smallest nonconstant instance, including the variable and constraint counts.
