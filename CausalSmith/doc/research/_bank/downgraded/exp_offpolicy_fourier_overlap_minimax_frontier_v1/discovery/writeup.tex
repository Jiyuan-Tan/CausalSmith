\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}offpolicy\_\allowbreak{}fourier\_\allowbreak{}overlap\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For the fixed causal target \(\theta\) under one known product-Bernoulli source assignment, the exact assignment-indexed linear minimax risk satisfies \(F=\mathcal D\), is attained, and is computable by an explicit vertex epigraph with \(n2^n+1\) variables and \(\prod_i|\operatorname{vert}(K_i)|\) constraints. Genuine-prior and primal certificates give \(\max\{F/r_{\star},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\}\le R^{\star}\le F\le S_{\mathrm{fib}}\), while the clipped incidence rule attains risk at most \(F\). Additive hubs have the exact one-hub all-Borel equality and the broader \(n^{-1}\) frontier; on that explicit family the Loomba--Eckles envelope is separated by \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n+n^2(q_n-p_n)^2)\), with elbow \(|q_n-p_n|\asymp n^{-1/2}\). This is not a generic observable-law sharpness claim. Dimension-free linear-versus-all-Borel comparison and exact compressed bounded-treewidth computation remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy supplies unbiased Fourier transport and a degree-envelope sufficient condition, while ChenValiantValiant2020 leaves the semilinear-versus-unrestricted universal gap open. Neither gives an exact biased linear frontier with a directly solvable finite program and genuine-prior lower certificates for heterogeneous off-policy network selectors.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note treats fixed bounded local-Fourier schedules under known heterogeneous source and target product policies. Its finite-instance rank and selector-fiber geometry records information discarded by maximum dependency degree, while keeping assignment as the only source of randomness.

\paragraph{Fill.} The contribution proves \(F=\mathcal D\), lifts the exact fully expanded vertex program with attaining primal and moment certificates, proves the instance-adaptive rank/fiber comparison and its supporting consistency corollary, and establishes the additive-hub \(n^{-1}\) frontier plus the Loomba--Eckles local-policy elbow on that explicit family. It does not claim a generic observable-law sharpness failure, the unresolved dimension-free comparison, or compressed treewidth exactness.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:source-product-design]\label{ass:source-product-design}
The source assignment mass satisfies \(p_{\pi}(z)=\prod_{j\in V_n}\pi_j^{z_j}(1-\pi_j)^{1-z_j}\) for every \(z\in\Omega_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Heterogeneous product-Bernoulli randomization, \cite{LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:source-overlap]\label{ass:source-overlap}
The source probabilities satisfy \(\varepsilon\le\pi_j\le1-\varepsilon\) for every \(j\in V_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform Bernoulli overlap, \cite{LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-product-policy]\label{ass:target-product-policy}
The target assignment mass satisfies \(p_{\pi'}(z)=\prod_{j\in V_n}(\pi'_j)^{z_j}(1-\pi'_j)^{1-z_j}\) for every \(z\in\Omega_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Heterogeneous product-Bernoulli target policy, \cite{LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:target-overlap]\label{ass:target-overlap}
The target probabilities satisfy \(\varepsilon\le\pi'_j\le1-\varepsilon\) for every \(j\in V_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform target-policy positivity, \cite{ChinEcklesUgander2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hierarchical-support]\label{ass:hierarchical-support}
Each retained support is downward closed: if \(U\in\mathcal S_i\) and \(W\subseteq U\), then \(W\in\mathcal S_i\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hierarchical low-order interaction support, \cite{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fourier-membership]\label{ass:fourier-membership}
For every \(i\in V_n\), the local schedule satisfies \(v_i\in H_i\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Prespecified local Fourier-subspace restriction, \cite{LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-schedules]\label{ass:bounded-schedules}
For every \(i\in V_n\) and \(x\in X_i\), \( |v_i(x)|\le1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded finite-population potential outcomes, \cite{ChenValiantValiant2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-schedules]\label{ass:fixed-schedules}
The schedule vector \(v\) is fixed before the source assignment \(z\) is drawn.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Neyman finite-population design-based convention, \cite{KandirosHarshawSavje2026Minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:loomba-eckles-spectral-envelope]\label{def:loomba-eckles-spectral-envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Loomba-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Eckles EAO weight and least bounded-\allowbreak{}class envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every fixed instance, define the Fourier policy-shift coefficient by
\[
 \Delta_U^{\pi,\pi'}
 =\prod_{j\in U}\frac{\pi'_j-\pi_j}{\sqrt{\pi_j(1-\pi_j)}},
 \qquad \Delta_{\varnothing}^{\pi,\pi'}=1,
\]
and define the Loomba--Eckles EAO transport weight for unit \(i\) by
\[
 W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}(z)
 =\sum_{U\in\mathcal S_i}
   \Delta_U^{\pi,\pi'}\chi_{i,U}^{\pi}(z_{\Gamma_i}).
\]
The smallest uniform bounded-class second-moment envelope and the overlap degree are
\[
 B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =\max_{i\in V_n}\ \sup_{v_i\in K_i}
   \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
   \bigl(v_i(z_{\Gamma_i})W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}(z)\bigr)^2
\]
and
\[
 D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =\max_{i\in V_n}
   \left|\left\{j\in V_n\setminus\{i\}:
   \Gamma_i\cap\Gamma_j\ne\varnothing\right\}\right|.
\]
Thus \(B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}\) is the least number \(B\) for which every bounded schedule in \(K\) satisfies the unitwise second-moment premise of the Loomba--Eckles spectral-overlap theorem.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}pi}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}pi'}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal S\_\allowbreak{}i}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}chi\_\allowbreak{}\{i,\allowbreak{}U\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}pi\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma\_\allowbreak{}i}; \texttt{K\_\allowbreak{}i}; \texttt{p\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}pi\}\allowbreak{}(z)}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity]\label{def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Adaptive dual-\allowbreak{}rank and selector-\allowbreak{}fiber complexity}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a fixed admissible instance with \(F>0\), choose
\[
 M^{\star}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{M\in\mathfrak M(K)}
 \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M)
\]
having minimum rank among all maximizers, and set \(r_{\star}=\operatorname{rank}(M^{\star})\). If \(F=0\), set \(r_{\star}=1\) and do not use \(M^{\star}\). For every \(z\in\Omega_n\), define
\[
 \rho_z=\sup\{\lvert\theta(h)\rvert:h\in K,\ N_zh=0\},\qquad
 S_{\mathrm{fib}}=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\rho_z^2,
 \qquad
 m_{\mathrm{fib}}=\max_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\rho_z^2.
\]
If \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}>0\), define
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}=\frac{S_{\mathrm{fib}}}{m_{\mathrm{fib}}},
 \qquad
 \kappa=\min\{r_{\star},\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}\};
\]
if \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\), define \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}=\kappa=1\).

For every row of \(\mathfrak A\in\mathsf{Adm}\), make the same definitions with \((F,\mathcal D,M^{\star},r_{\star},\rho_z,S_{\mathrm{fib}},m_{\mathrm{fib}},\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}},\kappa)\) replaced by \((F_n,\mathcal D_n,M_n^{\star},r_n,\rho_{n,z},S_{n,\mathrm{fib}},m_{n,\mathrm{fib}},\kappa_{n,\mathrm{fib}},\kappa_n)\) and with every fixed-instance object replaced by its row-\(n\) indexed version.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{F}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal D}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak M(K)}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}psi\_\allowbreak{}z(M)}; \texttt{p\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}pi\}\allowbreak{}(z)}; \texttt{K}; \texttt{N\_\allowbreak{}z}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak A}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:selector-reduction-and-dual]\label{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}
For every fixed \(n\) and every admissible row-\(n\) instance, the observed experiment is exactly the randomized selector experiment \(z\mapsto N_{n,z}v_n\). A minimizing indexed weight rule exists after quotienting, separately for each \(z\), the coefficient directions in \(\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})\), and the exact identity is \(F_n=\mathcal D_n\). Equivalently, under the fixed-instance abbreviations, \(F=\mathcal D\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\).  By \texttt{def:indexed-hilbert-geometry} and source positivity in \texttt{ass:source-product-design} and \texttt{ass:source-overlap}, \(E_n\) is a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space and \(p_{\pi_n}(z)>0\) for every \(z\in\Omega_n\).  By \texttt{def:indexed-bounded-fourier-class}, each \(K_{n,i}\) is the intersection of a finite-dimensional linear space with finitely many closed slabs.  It is therefore a compact, centrally symmetric polytope.  It contains a neighborhood of zero relative to \(H_{n,i}\), because finitely many evaluation functionals are continuous.  Consequently \(K_n=\prod_iK_{n,i}\) is a compact, centrally symmetric polytope containing a ball
\[
 \{v_n\in E_n:\|v_n\|_{E_n}\le \rho_n\}\subseteq K_n
\]
for some \(\rho_n>0\).

The observation assertion follows directly from \texttt{def:indexed-target-selector-geometry}: conditional on assignment \(z\), the observed vector has coordinates \(v_{n,i}(z_{\Gamma_{n,i}})\), and hence is exactly \(N_{n,z}v_n\).  The fixed-schedule condition \texttt{ass:fixed-schedules} makes \(v_n\) nonrandom under this design; all expectations below are therefore only over \(z\).

Let \(\ell_n\) be the Riesz vector from \texttt{def:indexed-psd-riesz-geometry}, and put
\[
 q_z(a_{n,z})=N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n,
 \qquad
 f_v(a_n)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi_n}(z)
       \langle q_z(a_{n,z}),v\rangle_{E_n}^{2}.
\]
The adjoint identity and \(\theta_n(v)=\langle\ell_n,v\rangle_{E_n}\) show, term by term, that \(f_v(a_n)\) is precisely the design risk of the indexed linear rule at schedule \(v\).  If \(\mathcal V_n\) denotes the finite vertex set of \(K_n\), then
\[
 \sup_{v\in K_n}f_v(a_n)=\max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n).
\]
Indeed, \(f_v(a_n)=\langle v,Q(a_n)v\rangle_{E_n}\) for the positive semidefinite operator
\[
 Q(a_n)=\sum_zp_{\pi_n}(z)q_z(a_{n,z})\otimes q_z(a_{n,z}),
\]
so \(v\mapsto f_v(a_n)\) is convex; writing a point of the polytope as a convex combination of vertices proves the display.

We next justify attainment without compactifying the coefficient space artificially.  For each \(z\), coefficient directions in \(\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})\) do not change \(q_z\), so restrict \(a_{n,z}\) to
\[
 A_{n,z}=\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})^{\perp}=\operatorname{range}(N_{n,z}).
\]
On \(A_{n,z}\), the map \(N_{n,z}^{\ast}\) is injective.  Moreover, using the ball contained in \(K_n\),
\[
 \max_{v\in K_n}f_v(a_n)
 \ge p_{\pi_n}(z)\max_{\|v\|_{E_n}\le\rho_n}
          \langle q_z(a_{n,z}),v\rangle_{E_n}^{2}
 =p_{\pi_n}(z)\rho_n^2\|q_z(a_{n,z})\|_{E_n}^2.
\]
Finite-dimensional injectivity gives a positive smallest singular value for \(N_{n,z}^{\ast}\) on every nonzero \(A_{n,z}\).  Since every source mass is positive, the last display proves that the continuous function \(a_n\mapsto\max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n)\) is coercive on \(\prod_zA_{n,z}\).  It therefore has a minimizer \(a_n^{\star}\).  Adding arbitrary elements of \(\ker(N_{n,z}^{\ast})\) describes all redundant representatives.

It remains to establish the exact dual without assuming a minimax theorem on a noncompact set.  For \(M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)\), define
\[
 \Phi(a_n,M)=\sum_zp_{\pi_n}(z)
 \langle N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n,
 M(N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n)\rangle_{E_n}.
\]
If \(M=\sum_r\lambda_r(v_r\otimes v_r)\), with \(v_r\in K_n\), \(\lambda_r\ge0\), and \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\), then
\[
 \Phi(a_n,M)=\sum_r\lambda_rf_{v_r}(a_n)
 \le \max_{v\in K_n}f_v(a_n).
\]
Hence \(\max_M\inf_{a_n}\Phi(a_n,M)\le F_n\).

For the reverse inequality, let \(I\subseteq\mathcal V_n\) be the vertices active at \(a_n^{\star}\).  The origin belongs to the convex hull of \(\{\nabla f_v(a_n^{\star}):v\in I\}\).  Otherwise finite-dimensional strict separation would give a direction \(d\) having negative inner product with every active gradient.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal V_n\), continuity, and the positive gap to every inactive value would then imply
\[
 \max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n^{\star}+td)
 <\max_{v\in\mathcal V_n}f_v(a_n^{\star})
\]
for all sufficiently small \(t>0\), contradicting minimality.  Thus there are \(v_r\in I\) and \(\lambda_r\ge0\), \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\), such that \(\sum_r\lambda_r\nabla f_{v_r}(a_n^{\star})=0\).  Set
\[
 M^{\star}=\sum_r\lambda_r(v_r\otimes v_r)\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n).
\]
The function \(a_n\mapsto\Phi(a_n,M^{\star})=\sum_r\lambda_rf_{v_r}(a_n)\) is differentiable and convex, and its gradient vanishes at \(a_n^{\star}\).  Therefore \(a_n^{\star}\) minimizes it.  Since every \(v_r\) is active,
\[
 \inf_{a_n}\Phi(a_n,M^{\star})
 =\Phi(a_n^{\star},M^{\star})
 =\sum_r\lambda_rF_n=F_n.
\]
Together with the preceding weak inequality, this proves
\[
 F_n=\max_{M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)}\inf_{a_n}\Phi(a_n,M).
\]

Finally fix \(M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)\).  The minimization separates over \(z\).  Write
\[
 A_z=N_{n,z}MN_{n,z}^{\ast},
 \qquad b_z=N_{n,z}M\ell_n.
\]
If \(r\in\ker(A_z)\), positivity gives
\[
 0=\langle r,A_zr\rangle_{V_n}
   =\|M^{1/2}N_{n,z}^{\ast}r\|_{E_n}^2,
\]
and hence
\[
 \langle r,b_z\rangle_{V_n}
 =\langle M^{1/2}N_{n,z}^{\ast}r,M^{1/2}\ell_n\rangle_{E_n}=0.
\]
Thus \(b_z\perp\ker(A_z)\), equivalently \(b_z\in\operatorname{range}(A_z)\).  Expanding the quadratic and completing the square with the Moore--Penrose inverse yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\inf_{a_{n,z}\in\mathbb R^{V_n}}
 \langle N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n,
 M(N_{n,z}^{\ast}a_{n,z}-\ell_n)\rangle_{E_n}\\
 &\quad=\langle\ell_n,M\ell_n\rangle_{E_n}
 -\langle b_z,A_z^{\dagger}b_z\rangle_{V_n}
 =\psi_{n,z}(M).
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, \(a_{n,z}=A_z^{\dagger}b_z\) attains the infimum, with arbitrary kernel directions also allowed.  Summing with the positive source weights gives
\[
 F_n=\max_{M\in\mathfrak M_n(K_n)}
       \sum_zp_{\pi_n}(z)\psi_{n,z}(M)=\mathcal D_n
\]
by \texttt{def:moment-dual}.  The fixed-instance equality \(F=\mathcal D\) is the same identity under the abbreviations specified in that definition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction fixes the finite decision problem and supplies the exact dual object from which both algorithms and lower bounds must start. \textit{Gap.} ChenValiantValiant2020 gives a positive-semidefinite relaxation for full cubes, while LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy does not optimize jointly over assignment-indexed biased weights or bounded Fourier evaluation polytopes. \textit{Consumer.} The dual is the input to the genuine-prior converse and to certified risk computation for the Crépon et al. saturation design.

\begin{proposition}[prop:primal-dual-certificates]\label{prop:primal-dual-certificates}
For every feasible weight rule \(a\) and every genuine prior \(\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\), \(\operatorname{LB}(\nu)\le R^{\star}\le F\le\operatorname{UB}(a)\). The clipped incidence estimator satisfies \(\sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_{\pi}(z)(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{inc}}-\theta(v))^2\le F\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a Borel rule \(\delta\), define the clipped Borel rule
\[
 \bar\delta(z,y)=\operatorname{clip}(\delta(z,y)).
\]
We first verify the range of the causal target.  By \(\mathrm{def:local-target-mass}\), the cells \(\{z:z_{\Gamma_i}=x\}\), \(x\in X_i\), partition \(\Omega_n\).  Hence \(\mathrm{ass:target-product-policy}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{x\in X_i}p_{\pi',i}(x)
 =\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi'}(z)=1.
\]
Consequently, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded-schedules}\) and \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\) imply, for every \(v\in K\),
\[
 \lvert\theta(v)\rvert
 \le \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{x\in X_i}
 p_{\pi',i}(x)\lvert v_i(x)\rvert
 \le \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}1=1.
\]
Projection onto \([-1,1]\) weakly decreases distance to every point of that interval.  Thus, pointwise in \((z,v)\),
\[
 \bigl(\bar\delta(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 \le
 \bigl(\delta(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2.                                      \tag{1}
\]

Fix a genuine prior \(\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\) and let \(\mathbf V\sim\nu\).  Both \(\bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)\) and \(\theta(\mathbf V)\) are bounded by one in absolute value.  Their conditional second moments therefore exist.  For each fixed \(z\), conditioning on \(N_z\mathbf V\) and expanding the square around the conditional mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_\nu\!\left[
 \bigl(\bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)-\theta(\mathbf V)\bigr)^2
 \mid N_z\mathbf V\right]\\
 &\quad=
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V\right)
 +\left(
 \bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)
 -\mathbb E_\nu[\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V]
 \right)^2\\
 &\quad\ge
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V\right).
\end{aligned}                                                                     \tag{2}
\]
No integrability assertion about the original, possibly unbounded, rule \(\delta\) is used.  Write
\[
 r_\eta(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_\pi(z)
 \bigl(\eta(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
\]
for either rule \(\eta\).  Inequality \((1)\), integration of the bounded loss of \(\bar\delta\), the fact that a supremum dominates a prior average, and \((2)\) yield, in this order,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{v\in K}r_\delta(v)
 &\ge \sup_{v\in K}r_{\bar\delta}(v)\\
 &\ge \int_K r_{\bar\delta}(v)\,d\nu(v)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_\pi(z)
 \mathbb E_\nu\!\left[
 \bigl(\bar\delta(z,N_z\mathbf V)-\theta(\mathbf V)\bigr)^2\right]\\
 &\ge \sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_\pi(z)
 \mathbb E_\nu\!\left[
 \operatorname{Var}_\nu(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V)\right]\\
 &=\mathcal B(\nu)=\operatorname{LB}(\nu),
\end{aligned}                                                                     \tag{3}
\]
where the first inequality remains valid in the extended nonnegative reals.  Taking the infimum of \((3)\) over all Borel \(\delta\) in \(\mathrm{def:unrestricted-frontier}\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{LB}(\nu)\le R^{\star}.                                           \tag{4}
\]

For every weight rule \(a\), the map \((z,y)\mapsto a_z^{\mathsf T}y\) is Borel.  It is therefore feasible for \(R^{\star}\), and \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) gives
\[
 R^{\star}
 \le \inf_a\sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 =F.                                                                              \tag{5}
\]
For a fixed feasible \(a\), \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{def:primal-certificate}\) similarly give
\[
 F\le
 \sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 =\operatorname{UB}(a).                                                           \tag{6}
\]

Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) supplies an attaining weight rule \(a^{\star}\).  Applying the projection inequality in \((1)\) with \(\delta(z,y)=(a_z^{\star})^{\mathsf T}y\), then using \(\mathrm{def:clipped-incidence-estimator}\), gives
\[
 \sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{inc}}(z,N_zv)-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 \le
 \sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_\pi(z)
 \bigl((a_z^{\star})^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2
 =F.                                                                              \tag{7}
\]
Equations \((4)\)--\((7)\) prove all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The certificate pair separates a computable primal risk from a genuine-prior all-Borel lower bound, with clipping making the conditional argument valid even for initially non-square-integrable Borel rules. \textit{Gap.} BrownCohen2021 requires a combinatorial non-expansion condition; this proposition instead supplies an instance-specific posterior-variance certificate paired with the Fourier selector program. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can report certified finite-sample lower and upper risks without assuming integrability of an arbitrary competing estimator.

\begin{theorem}[thm:hub-separation]\label{thm:hub-separation}
Fix \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\). On \(\mathcal H_n^{\mathrm{hub}}\), \(F_n=\Theta(n^{-1})\) and \(R_n^{\star}=\Theta(n^{-1})\), while the Loomba--Eckles envelope is exactly
\[
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}{n}
 =1+n\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
Consequently, this envelope divided by either \(F_n\) or \(R_n^{\star}\) is \(\Theta(n)\) when \(p=q\) and \(\Theta(n^2)\) for fixed \(p\ne q\). Thus the exact frontier is strictly rate-sharper on a genuinely overlapping growing family, both on-policy and under a fixed nonzero policy shift.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\ge 2\) and \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\).  Define
\[
 A_{p,q}=(1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2,
 \qquad
 C_{p,q}=\frac{(1-q)^2}{1-p}+\frac{q^2}{p}.
\]
The overlap assumptions imply \(p,1-p,q,1-q\ge\varepsilon>0\).  Hence every denominator in \(C_{p,q}\) is positive, and
\[
 A_{p,q}
 \ge \varepsilon\{q^2+(1-q)^2\}
 \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0,
 \qquad
 C_{p,q}
 \le \frac{q^2+(1-q)^2}{\varepsilon}
 \le \frac1\varepsilon.
\]
Here the middle lower bound uses
\(q^2+(1-q)^2=2(q-1/2)^2+1/2\), and the last upper bound uses
\(q^2+(1-q)^2=1-2q(1-q)\le1\).

By \(\texttt{lem:hub-frontier-rate-sandwich}\),
\[
 \frac{n-1}{n^2}A_{p,q}
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n
 \le \frac{1+(n-1)C_{p,q}}{n^2}.
\]
Since \((n-1)/n^2\ge1/(2n)\), \(1\le1/\varepsilon\), and \(n\ge2\), the preceding display gives the uniform bounds
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon}{4n}
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n
 \le \frac{1+(n-1)/\varepsilon}{n^2}
 \le \frac1{\varepsilon n}.
\]
Thus both \(R_n^{\star}\) and \(F_n\) are bounded above and below by positive finite multiples of \(n^{-1}\); in particular,
\[
 R_n^{\star}=\Theta(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 F_n=\Theta(n^{-1}).
\]
This argument includes \(p=q\); no division by \(q-p\) has been used.

Now put
\[
 c_{p,q}=\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
The denominator is positive by \(\texttt{ass:source-overlap}\).  The exact calculation in
\(\texttt{lem:hub-loomba-eckles-envelope}\) yields
\[
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}{n}
 =1+n c_{p,q}.
\]
If \(p=q\), then \(c_{p,q}=0\), so the envelope equals one.  For either
\(X_n\in\{R_n^{\star},F_n\}\), the uniform frontier bounds imply
\[
 \varepsilon n
 \le \frac1{X_n}
 \le \frac{4n}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Multiplying by the envelope, which is one in this case, proves
\[
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)/n}{R_n^{\star}}
 =\Theta(n),
 \qquad
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)/n}{F_n}
 =\Theta(n).
\]

If \(p\ne q\) is fixed, then \(c_{p,q}>0\).  For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
 n c_{p,q}\le 1+n c_{p,q}\le n(1+c_{p,q}).
\]
Combining this with the reciprocal bounds gives, for either permitted \(X_n\),
\[
 \varepsilon c_{p,q}n^2
 \le
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)/n}{X_n}
 \le
 \frac{4(1+c_{p,q})}{\varepsilon}n^2.
\]
Consequently both ratios are \(\Theta(n^2)\) for fixed \(p\ne q\).

As the smallest-row boundary check, at \(n=2\) the four assignments all have positive source mass by \(\texttt{ass:source-overlap}\), and the two established lemmas specialize to
\[
 \frac{A_{p,q}}4\le R_2^{\star}\le F_2\le\frac{1+C_{p,q}}4,
 \qquad
 \frac{B_2^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_2^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}2=1+2c_{p,q}.
\]
Thus the smallest admissible hub row has neither a zero-risk nor a division boundary that could invalidate the comparison.  Finally, by \(\texttt{def:hub-family}\), the hub neighborhood \(\Gamma_1=V_n\) intersects every peripheral neighborhood \(\Gamma_i=\{i\}\), so the family is genuinely overlapping for every \(n\ge2\).  The displayed bounds and exact identity prove all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is an explicit minimax-family rate comparison: the additive hub exhibits an order-\(n^{-1}\) certificate while the stated Loomba--Eckles sufficient envelope is larger by order \(n\) on-policy and order \(n^2\) under a fixed nonzero policy shift. It is not a perturbation-stable generic sharpness-failure theorem about observable laws. \textit{Gap.} LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy, Theorem 5.1, assumes any \(B^{(n)}\) dominating all unit-summand second moments and multiplies it by \(D^{(n)}+1\); on the additive hub the least such bounded-class envelope is exactly \(1+n(q-p)^2/[p(1-p)]\) and \(D^{(n)}=n-1\). Establishing a nondegenerate observable-law witness and an ambient perturbation-stable class for a generic sharpness claim is outside this theorem. \textit{Consumer.} The construction is a design-specific benchmark for dense-star or influencer-network inputs: it supplies an exact finite-population rate comparison, not a claim that every nearby observable law or network family inherits the same gap.

\begin{theorem}[thm:disjoint-motif-recovery]\label{thm:disjoint-motif-recovery}
Suppose the fixed motif \(\mathfrak m\) admits a bounded assignment-indexed linear rule \(u_{\mathfrak m}\) that is design-unbiased for the motif target at every admissible motif schedule and has one-motif worst-case mean-squared error at most \(\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2\), and the symmetric genuine prior \(\nu_{\mathfrak m}\) has posterior ambiguity \(b_{\mathfrak m}>0\). Then for every \(m\ge1\), \(b_{\mathfrak m}/m\le R_m^{\star,\sqcup}\le F_m^{\sqcup}\le\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2/m\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let the copies be indexed by \(r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  For copy \(r\), write \(Z_r\) for its source assignment, \(O_r\) for its observed outcome vector, \(T_r\) for its motif target, and \(u_r=u_{\mathfrak m}(Z_r,O_r)\).  By the proposed hypothesis, \(u_{\mathfrak m}\) is assignment-indexed linear, design-unbiased at every motif schedule, and has worst-case mean-squared error at most \(\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2\).  Thus, for every fixed product schedule,
\[
 e_r:=u_r-T_r,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[e_r]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[e_r^2]\le\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2.
\]
By \texttt{def:motif-repetition}, the assignments are independent across copies.  Since the schedules are fixed in design-based risk, \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\) are independent.  The repeated-motif target is \(\overline T=m^{-1}\sum_rT_r\), and
\[
 \overline u=\frac1m\sum_{r=1}^mu_r
\]
is an assignment-indexed linear rule for that target.  Hence its risk satisfies, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[(\overline u-\overline T)^2]
 &=\frac1{m^2}\mathbb E\left[\left(\sum_{r=1}^me_r\right)^2\right]\\
 &=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{r=1}^m\mathbb E[e_r^2]
   +\frac2{m^2}\sum_{r<s}\mathbb E[e_r]\mathbb E[e_s]\\
 &\le\frac{\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2}{m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over the product schedule class and invoking \texttt{def:linear-frontier} proves \(F_m^{\sqcup}\le\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2/m\).  The middle inequality \(R_m^{\star,\sqcup}\le F_m^{\sqcup}\) follows from \texttt{prop:primal-dual-certificates} applied to the repeated instance.

For the converse, put the product prior \(\nu^{(m)}=\nu_{\mathfrak m}^{\otimes m}\) on the product schedule class.  It is countably additive and genuinely supported on bounded schedules; symmetry of the one-copy prior also makes the product prior symmetric.  Let \(\mathbf T_r\) be the random motif target in copy \(r\).  Product prior, product source assignment, and copywise observation imply conditional independence of \(\mathbf T_1,\ldots,\mathbf T_m\) given \((Z_r,O_r)_{r=1}^m\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(
 \frac1m\sum_{r=1}^m\mathbf T_r
 \mathrel{\Big|}(Z_s,O_s)_{s=1}^m\right)
 =\frac1{m^2}\sum_{r=1}^m
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathbf T_r\mid Z_r,O_r).
\]
Averaging over the product source design and product prior, and using the definition of the one-motif ambiguity, gives
\[
 \mathcal B(\nu^{(m)})
 =\frac1{m^2}\sum_{r=1}^mb_{\mathfrak m}
 =\frac{b_{\mathfrak m}}m.
\]
The lower-certificate inequality in \texttt{prop:primal-dual-certificates} now yields \(b_{\mathfrak m}/m\le R_m^{\star,\sqcup}\).  Combining the three bounds proves the proposed statement.  At \(m=1\) the calculation reduces exactly to \(b_{\mathfrak m}\le R_1^{\star,\sqcup}\le F_1^{\sqcup}\le\sigma_{\mathfrak m}^2\); at \(m=2\) the vanished cross term explicitly checks the smallest nontrivial tensorization.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Supporting tensorization corollary: once the explicitly assumed one-motif unbiased rule and positive-ambiguity prior are supplied, product-copy averaging gives these bounds by the standard variance and posterior-variance calculations. \textit{Gap.} This is not a new general overlap theorem or a standalone minimax frontier. Its limited role is to keep the required one-motif witnesses explicit when transferring a certified local calculation to disjoint copies. \textit{Consumer.} After a motif-specific solver supplies both stated witnesses, clustered village and school designs obtain an interpretable \(1/m\) calibration check.

\begin{proposition}[prop:one-binary-cell-sanity]\label{prop:one-binary-cell-sanity}
When \(n=1\), \(\Gamma_1=\{1\}\), and \(\mathcal S_1=2^{\{1\}}\), the full-class exact risks coincide and equal \(R^{\star}=F=(1-\pi_1)(\pi'_1)^2+\pi_1(1-\pi'_1)^2\). Pointwise, for every unknown schedule \(v\in K\) restricted by \(v_1(0)=v_1(1)\), the measurable rule \(\delta(z,N_zv)=(N_zv)_1\) has zero squared error under both assignments because \(\delta(z,N_zv)=\theta(v)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(p=\pi_1\) and \(q=\pi'_1\). By \texttt{ass:source-overlap}, \(0<p<1\); hence every denominator below is nonzero. By \texttt{ass:source-product-design}, the two assignments have source probabilities
\[
p_{\pi}(0)=1-p,
\qquad
p_{\pi}(1)=p.
\]
By \texttt{ass:target-product-policy} and \texttt{def:local-target-mass}, their target local-cell probabilities are \(p_{\pi',1}(0)=1-q\) and \(p_{\pi',1}(1)=q\).

We first identify the parameter set in this special case. The hypotheses give \(X_1=\{0,1\}\) and \(\mathcal S_1=\{\varnothing,\{1\}\}\). By \texttt{def:local-fourier-character},
\[
\chi_{1,\varnothing}^{\pi}(x)=1,
\qquad
\chi_{1,\{1\}}^{\pi}(x)=\frac{x-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
\]
The second character takes different values at \(0\) and \(1\), since their difference is \(1/\sqrt{p(1-p)}\ne0\). Thus these two characters are linearly independent. Because \(\mathbb R^{X_1}\) has dimension two, \texttt{def:local-fourier-space} yields \(H_1=\mathbb R^{X_1}\). It then follows from \texttt{def:bounded-fourier-class} that
\[
K=[-1,1]^{X_1}=[-1,1]^2.
\]
Write a schedule as \(v=(v_0,v_1)\), where \(v_x=v_1(x)\). By \texttt{def:observation-operator}, assignment \(z=x\) reveals \(N_xv=(v_x)\). By \texttt{def:target-value} and the target-cell probabilities above,
\[
\theta(v)=(1-q)v_0+qv_1.
\]

For the upper bound, choose the assignment-indexed linear weights \(a_0=1-q\) and \(a_1=q\). The resulting rule is
\[
\delta_a(0,N_0v)=(1-q)v_0,
\qquad
\delta_a(1,N_1v)=qv_1.
\]
Consequently its two assignment-specific errors are
\[
\delta_a(0,N_0v)-\theta(v)=-qv_1,
\qquad
\delta_a(1,N_1v)-\theta(v)=-(1-q)v_0.
\]
Its design risk therefore satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_a(v)
&=(1-p)q^2v_1^2+p(1-q)^2v_0^2\\
&\le (1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2=:A,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the inequality uses \(|v_0|,|v_1|\le1\). Equality holds, for example, at \((v_0,v_1)=(1,1)\). Hence \texttt{def:linear-frontier} gives \(F\le A\), and this displayed rule attains worst-case risk \(A\).

It remains to prove that no measurable nonlinear rule does better. Fix an arbitrary Borel rule \(\delta\), and abbreviate \(d_x(y)=\delta(x,(y))\). Average its risk over the genuine probability distribution under which \(V_0\) and \(V_1\) are independent and each is uniform on \(\{-1,1\}\). This distribution is supported on \(K\). Conditional on assignment zero, set \(T_0=d_0(V_0)-(1-q)V_0\). Independence, \(\mathbb E[V_1]=0\), and \(V_1^2=1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\!\left[\bigl(d_0(V_0)-\theta(V)\bigr)^2\right]
&=\mathbb E\!\left[(T_0-qV_1)^2\right]\\
&=\mathbb E[T_0^2]-2q\,\mathbb E[T_0]\mathbb E[V_1]+q^2\mathbb E[V_1^2]\\
&=\mathbb E[T_0^2]+q^2\\
&\ge q^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly, conditional on assignment one, put \(T_1=d_1(V_1)-qV_1\). Independence, \(\mathbb E[V_0]=0\), and \(V_0^2=1\) imply
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\bigl(d_1(V_1)-\theta(V)\bigr)^2\right]
=\mathbb E[T_1^2]+(1-q)^2
\ge(1-q)^2.
\]
Combining the two assignments with their source probabilities shows that the average, over this four-point prior, of the design risk of \(\delta\) is at least
\[
(1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2=A.
\]
The supremum of a function over \(K\) is at least its average under any probability distribution supported on \(K\). Therefore every Borel \(\delta\) has worst-case risk at least \(A\), and \texttt{def:unrestricted-frontier} yields \(R^{\star}\ge A\). Conversely, every assignment-indexed linear rule is Borel, so the two infima in \texttt{def:linear-frontier} and \texttt{def:unrestricted-frontier} satisfy \(R^{\star}\le F\). Together with \(F\le A\),
\[
A\le R^{\star}\le F\le A,
\]
which proves
\[
R^{\star}=F=(1-\pi_1)(\pi'_1)^2+\pi_1(1-\pi'_1)^2.
\]
This argument explicitly enumerates both assignments and all four support points of the lower-bound prior, so it also supplies the claimed smallest-instance stress test.

Finally, suppose \(v_1(0)=v_1(1)=c\). Boundedness gives \(|c|\le1\), and the target formula gives \(\theta(v)=(1-q)c+qc=c\). Under either \(z=0\) or \(z=1\), \texttt{def:observation-operator} gives \((N_zv)_1=c\). Thus the Borel rule \(\delta(z,N_zv)=(N_zv)_1\) equals \(\theta(v)\) under each assignment, and its squared error is identically zero.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact base case distinguishes minimax shrinkage weights from unbiased likelihood-ratio transport, while the pointwise constant-schedule calculation checks zero error without changing the full-class minimax domain. \textit{Gap.} LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy characterizes the minimum-norm unbiased transport weight, not the biased minimax rule even for one binary exposure. \textit{Consumer.} The formula is a unit test for the exact small-instance solver and for the saturation-policy implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hub-frontier-rate-sandwich]\label{lem:hub-frontier-rate-sandwich}
For every \(n\ge2\) on \(\mathcal H_n^{\mathrm{hub}}\),
\[
 \frac{n-1}{n^2}\bigl((1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2\bigr)
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n
 \le \frac{1+(n-1)\bigl((1-q)^2/(1-p)+q^2/p\bigr)}{n^2}.
\]
In particular, for fixed \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), both \(F_n\) and \(R_n^{\star}\) are \(\Theta(n^{-1})\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\ge2\).  For a peripheral unit \(i\ge2\), \texttt{def:hub-family} gives \(\Gamma_i=\{i\}\) and \(\mathcal S_i=\{\varnothing,\{i\}\}\).  By \texttt{ass:source-overlap} and \texttt{def:local-fourier-character}, the two characters \(1\) and \((x-p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}\) are linearly independent on \(\{0,1\}\).  Hence \texttt{def:local-fourier-space} gives \(H_i=\mathbb R^{\{0,1\}}\), and \texttt{def:bounded-fourier-class} gives \(K_i=[-1,1]^2\).

Write
\[
 T_i=(1-q)v_i(0)+qv_i(1)
\]
for the unscaled target contribution of unit \(i\).  Under the source product design, define the peripheral assignment-indexed linear rule
\[
 U_i=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{1-q}{1-p}v_i(0),&Z_i=0,\\[5pt]
 \dfrac{q}{p}v_i(1),&Z_i=1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The denominators are positive by \texttt{ass:source-overlap}.  Direct calculation using \texttt{ass:source-product-design} gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi}[U_i]=(1-q)v_i(0)+qv_i(1)=T_i
\]
and, since \(|v_i(0)|,|v_i(1)|\le1\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\pi}(U_i)
 \le \mathbb E_{\pi}[U_i^2]
 \le \frac{(1-q)^2}{1-p}+\frac{q^2}{p}=:A_{p,q}.
\]
Use the global semilinear estimator \(\widehat T=n^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^nU_i\), assigning weight zero to the hub outcome.  The source coordinates \(Z_2,\ldots,Z_n\) are independent, so the centered peripheral errors are independent.  The hub target contribution \(T_1\) obeys \(|T_1|\le1\), because it is a target-policy average of a schedule bounded by one.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\pi}[(\widehat T-\theta(v))^2]
 &=\frac1{n^2}\left\{
 \sum_{i=2}^n\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}(U_i)+T_1^2\right\}\\
 &\le \frac{1+(n-1)A_{p,q}}{n^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed estimator is feasible in \texttt{def:linear-frontier}; this proves the upper bound on \(F_n\).  The inequality \(R_n^{\star}\le F_n\) follows from \texttt{prop:primal-dual-certificates}.

For the lower bound, put the hub schedule identically equal to zero.  Independently for every \(i\ge2\), draw \(v_i(0)\) and \(v_i(1)\) as independent Rademacher variables.  The preceding identification of \(K_i\) shows that this is a genuine prior supported on the hub schedule class.  If \(Z_i=0\), then \(v_i(1)\) remains unobserved and contributes posterior variance \(q^2/n^2\); if \(Z_i=1\), then \(v_i(0)\) remains unobserved and contributes \((1-q)^2/n^2\).  Independence across units makes the posterior covariances vanish.  Consequently the prior certificate is exactly
\[
 \mathcal B(\nu)
 =\frac{n-1}{n^2}\bigl((1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2\bigr).
\]
The lower-certificate part of \texttt{prop:primal-dual-certificates} gives the claimed lower bound on \(R_n^{\star}\).  Finally, interior overlap implies the two constants in the sandwich are finite and strictly positive; since \((n-1)/n^2\) is between \(1/(2n)\) and \(1/n\) for \(n\ge2\), the \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) conclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:loomba-eckles-spectral-overlap-envelope]\label{lem:loomba-eckles-spectral-overlap-envelope}
For product-Bernoulli source and target policies, the retained-support spectral transport weight is
\[
 w_{\mathcal S_i}^{\pi,\pi'}(z)
 =\sum_{U\in\mathcal S_i}\Delta_U^{\pi,\pi'}
   \chi_{i,U}^{\pi}(z_{\Gamma_i}),
\]
and it transports the target expectation of every function in \(H_i\).  If square-integrable unit summands depend respectively only on the assignment coordinates in \(\Gamma_i\), if
\[
 D=\max_{i\in V_n}
 \left|\left\{k\in V_n\setminus\{i\}:\Gamma_i\cap\Gamma_k\ne\varnothing\right\}\right|,
\]
and if their second moments are all at most \(B\), then the variance of their average is at most \(B(D+1)/n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:hub-loomba-eckles-envelope]\label{lem:hub-loomba-eckles-envelope}
Fix \(n\ge2\) and \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), and put
\[
 \Delta=\frac{q-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}},
 \qquad c=\Delta^2=\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
On \(\mathcal H_n^{\mathrm{hub}}\), the Loomba--Eckles transport weights and the smallest uniform bounded-class envelope satisfy
\[
 W_1^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^n\chi_{1,\{j\}}^{\pi},
 \qquad
 W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}=1+\Delta\chi_{i,\{i\}}^{\pi}\quad(2\le i\le n),
\]
\[
 B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}=1+nc,
 \qquad D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}=n-1,
 \qquad
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}{n}=1+n\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
The last quantity equals \(1\) when \(p=q\) and is \(\Theta(n)\) for fixed \(p\ne q\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\ge 2\) and \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\).  Since \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\), \texttt{ass:source-overlap} gives \(0<p<1\), and hence
\[
 \Delta=\frac{q-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 c=\Delta^2=\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}
\]
are well defined, with \(c\ge0\).  No strict inequality between \(p\) and \(q\) is used.

Let \(Z\) have the source law in \texttt{ass:source-product-design}, and abbreviate
\[
 \chi_j=\frac{Z_j-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
\]
The two Bernoulli values give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j]
 =\frac{p(1-p)+(1-p)(-p)}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}=0
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j^2]
 =\frac{p(1-p)^2+(1-p)p^2}{p(1-p)}=1.
\]
For \(j\ne k\), product independence from \texttt{ass:source-product-design} and the first identity imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j\chi_k]
 =\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j]\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_k]=0.
\]
These are precisely the singleton characters of \texttt{def:local-fourier-character} on the homogeneous source policy.

By \texttt{def:hub-family},
\[
 \mathcal S_1=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{\{j\}:j\in V_n\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_i=\{\varnothing,\{i\}\}\quad(2\le i\le n).
\]
For a singleton, \texttt{def:loomba-eckles-spectral-envelope} gives
\(
 \Delta_{\{j\}}^{\pi,\pi'}=(q-p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}=\Delta
\), while the empty-set loading is one.  Therefore the transport formula, equivalently \texttt{lem:loomba-eckles-spectral-overlap-envelope}, yields
\[
 W_1^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =1+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^n\chi_{1,\{j\}}^{\pi},
 \qquad
 W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}
 =1+\Delta\chi_{i,\{i\}}^{\pi}\quad(2\le i\le n).
\]
Using the three moment identities above term by term,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\pi}\!\left[(W_1^{\mathrm{LE}})^2\right]
 &=1+2\Delta\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j]
   +\Delta^2\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j^2]
   +2\Delta^2\sum_{1\le j<k\le n}\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_j\chi_k]\\
 &=1+n\Delta^2=1+nc,
\end{aligned}
\]
and, for \(2\le i\le n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi}\!\left[(W_i^{\mathrm{LE}})^2\right]
 =1+2\Delta\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_i]
   +\Delta^2\mathbb E_{\pi}[\chi_i^2]
 =1+c.
\]

It remains to verify exactness of the bounded-class envelope.  For every \(v_i\in K_i\), \texttt{def:local-evaluation-polytope} gives \(|v_i(x)|\le1\) at every local cell.  Hence, pointwise in \(Z\),
\[
 \bigl(v_i(Z_{\Gamma_i})W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}(Z)\bigr)^2
 \le (W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}(Z))^2,
\]
and consequently
\[
 \sup_{v_i\in K_i}\mathbb E_{\pi}\!\left[
   \bigl(v_i(Z_{\Gamma_i})W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}(Z)\bigr)^2
 \right]
 \le \mathbb E_{\pi}\!\left[(W_i^{\mathrm{LE}})^2\right].
\]
Conversely, the constant schedule \(v_i\equiv1\) is the empty-set character.  Each support displayed above contains \(\varnothing\), so \texttt{def:local-evaluation-polytope} implies \(v_i\equiv1\in K_i\); it attains equality in the preceding display.  Thus
\[
 \sup_{v_i\in K_i}\mathbb E_{\pi}\!\left[
   \bigl(v_i(Z_{\Gamma_i})W_i^{\mathrm{LE}}(Z)\bigr)^2
 \right]
 =\mathbb E_{\pi}\!\left[(W_i^{\mathrm{LE}})^2\right].
\]
Because \(n\ge2\) and \(c\ge0\), \(1+nc\ge1+c\).  Taking the maximum over units in \texttt{def:loomba-eckles-spectral-envelope} therefore proves
\[
 B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}=1+nc.
\]

For the overlap degree, \texttt{def:hub-family} gives \(\Gamma_1=V_n\) and \(\Gamma_i=\{i\}\) for \(i\ge2\).  Hence \(\Gamma_1\cap\Gamma_k=\{k\}\ne\varnothing\) for every \(k\ne1\).  The hub has \(n-1\) intersecting partners, and no unit can have more, so \texttt{def:loomba-eckles-spectral-envelope} gives
\[
 D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}=n-1.
\]
Combining the two exact formulas,
\[
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}{n}
 =\frac{(1+nc)n}{n}
 =1+n\frac{(q-p)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
If \(p=q\), then \(c=0\), so this quantity equals one for every \(n\).  If \(p\ne q\) is fixed, source overlap makes \(c\in(0,\infty)\) independent of \(n\), and the quantity is \(\Theta(n)\).  As the smallest-instance check, \(n=2\) gives \(B_2^{\mathrm{LE}}=1+2c\), \(D_2^{\mathrm{LE}}=1\), and the same envelope value \(1+2c\), including the boundary value one when \(p=q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:raw-incidence-bag-moment-obstruction]\label{prop:raw-incidence-bag-moment-obstruction}
Fix any even integer \(r\ge 2\) and any \(q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\setminus\{1/2\}\), and put \(d=2q-1\).  There is an \(n=r\) instance with \(\pi_j=1/2\), \(\pi'_j=q\), and one additive hub whose retained support is \(\mathcal S_1=\{\varnothing,\{1\},\ldots,\{r\}\}\), such that \(\mathcal I\) is the star \(K_{1,r}\) together with isolated vertices and hence \(\operatorname{tw}(\mathcal I)=1\).  Write its hub schedule as \(h_\beta(s)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_js_j\), where \(s_j=2z_j-1\); boundedness is equivalent to \(|\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^r|\beta_j|\le1\).  For a genuine prior \(\nu\) supported on schedules with all non-hub coordinates zero, define its ordinary edge-bag record to be the collection of projected laws \(\{\mathcal L_\nu(\beta_0,\beta_j):1\le j\le r\}\), with the common \(\beta_0\)-marginal identified on overlaps.  There are two genuine, finitely supported priors \(\nu_{\mathrm A}\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm B}\) having the same ordinary edge-bag record but satisfying
\[
 \int_K\theta(v)^2\,d\nu_{\mathrm A}(v)=\frac{d^2}{r^2}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \int_K\theta(v)^2\,d\nu_{\mathrm B}(v)=0.
\]
For \(r=2\), they can be chosen as the symmetric two-point laws generated respectively by \((\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2)=(0,1/2,1/2)\) and \((0,1/2,-1/2)\); in particular their cross moments are \(1/4\) and \(-1/4\).  This is the smallest additive-star obstruction: with one leaf the sole edge-bag law is the full coefficient law and determines the squared target.  More generally, define the canonical interaction graph of the target-square polynomial by joining two coefficient variables exactly when they occur together in a nonzero monomial.  This graph contains a clique on the \(r\) leaf coefficients.  Consequently, ordinary bag moments on the raw incidence decomposition do not determine the target-moment term \(\langle\ell,M_\nu\ell\rangle_E\), where \(M_\nu=\int_K v\otimes v\,d\nu(v)\in\mathfrak M(K)\); the bag-local lift described by \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{tw}}\) therefore requires cross-leaf correlation state or an equivalent global target-sum state to be exact.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(V_r=\{1,\ldots,r\}\).  Set every source coordinate to \(\pi_j=1/2\) and every target coordinate to \(\pi'_j=q\).  The source law is product Bernoulli by \texttt{ass:source-product-design}; because \(\varepsilon<1/2\), \(1/2\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), so \texttt{ass:source-overlap} holds.  The target law is product Bernoulli by \texttt{ass:target-product-policy}, and the chosen \(q\) obeys \texttt{ass:target-overlap}.

For outcome unit \(1\), take \(\Gamma_1=V_r\) and
\[
 \mathcal S_1=\{\varnothing,\{1\},\ldots,\{r\}\}.
\]
For every outcome unit \(i\ge2\), take \(\Gamma_i=\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal S_i=\{\varnothing\}\).  These supports are downward closed, as required by \texttt{ass:hierarchical-support}.  By \texttt{def:incidence-graph}, assignment vertex \(j\) is adjacent only to outcome-factor vertex \(1\); hence \(\mathcal I\) is \(K_{1,r}\) plus isolated vertices.  The bags consisting of the hub and one leaf, connected in any tree, form a width-one tree decomposition.  Since the graph has an edge, its treewidth is not zero.  Thus \(\operatorname{tw}(\mathcal I)=1\).

At source probability \(1/2\), \texttt{def:local-fourier-character} gives
\[
 \chi_{1,\{j\}}^\pi(z)=\frac{z_j-1/2}{\sqrt{(1/2)(1/2)}}=2z_j-1=:s_j\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Therefore \texttt{def:local-fourier-space} identifies every additive hub schedule with
\[
 h_\beta(s)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_js_j.
\]
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 \max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^r}|h_\beta(s)|
 \le |\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^r|\beta_j|.
\]
For the reverse inequality, choose \(t\in\{-1,1\}\) with \(\beta_0=t|\beta_0|\), taking either value if \(\beta_0=0\), and choose \(s_j=t\operatorname{sgn}(\beta_j)\), with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=1\).  Then
\[
 h_\beta(s)=t\left(|\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^r|\beta_j|\right).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^r}|h_\beta(s)|
 =|\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^r|\beta_j|,
\]
so \texttt{def:local-evaluation-polytope} and \texttt{def:bounded-fourier-class} make the hub coefficient set exactly the displayed \(\ell_1\)-ball.

Let
\[
 b^{\mathrm A}=\left(0,\frac1r,\ldots,\frac1r\right)
\]
and let \(b^{\mathrm B}\) have zero intercept, its first \(r/2\) leaf coefficients equal to \(1/r\), and its last \(r/2\) leaf coefficients equal to \(-1/r\).  Both coefficient vectors have \(\ell_1\)-norm one.  For \(C\in\{\mathrm A,\mathrm B\}\), let \(v^{b^C}\) denote the schedule whose hub coordinate is \(h_{b^C}\) and whose other outcome coordinates are identically zero, and define the genuine prior
\[
 \nu_C=\frac12\delta_{v^{b^C}}+\frac12\delta_{-v^{b^C}}.
\]
The preceding \(\ell_1\) calculation proves that all four support schedules belong to \(K\), so these are countably additive probability laws on the actual bounded schedule class, not signed moment functionals.  They are convex-analytic certificates only: conditional on any support schedule, the potential outcomes remain fixed and the source assignment is the experiment's sole randomness, as required by \texttt{ass:fixed-schedules}.

For every leaf \(j\), under either prior \(\beta_0=0\) almost surely and \(\beta_j\) is uniform on \(\{-1/r,1/r\}\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal L_{\nu_{\mathrm A}}(\beta_0,\beta_j)
 =\frac12\delta_{(0,1/r)}+\frac12\delta_{(0,-1/r)}
 =\mathcal L_{\nu_{\mathrm B}}(\beta_0,\beta_j)
\]
for every \(j\).  Thus the two priors have identical edge-bag laws, and a fortiori identical first and second moments within every edge bag and identical overlap marginals.

Under the target product policy,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi'}[s_j]=2q-1=d.
\]
All non-hub outcome coordinates vanish under the two priors, so \texttt{def:target-value} yields, for any coefficient vector \(\beta\) in their support,
\[
 \theta(v^\beta)=\frac1r\left(\beta_0+d\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_j\right).
\]
Now \(\sum_jb_j^{\mathrm A}=1\), whereas \(\sum_jb_j^{\mathrm B}=0\).  Symmetrizing changes the sign of \(\theta\) but not its square.  Therefore
\[
 \int_K\theta(v)^2\,d\nu_{\mathrm A}(v)=\frac{d^2}{r^2},
 \qquad
 \int_K\theta(v)^2\,d\nu_{\mathrm B}(v)=0.
\]
When \(r=2\), the generating vectors are precisely \((0,1/2,1/2)\) and \((0,1/2,-1/2)\).  Enumerating all four source assignments gives the exact stress-test table
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 (s_1,s_2)&(1,1)&(1,-1)&(-1,1)&(-1,-1)\\ \hline
 h_{b^{\mathrm A}}&1&0&0&-1\\
 h_{b^{\mathrm B}}&0&1&-1&0
\end{array}
\]
and directly confirms boundedness on every realized cell.  Since multiplication is unchanged by the common sign flip in each symmetric prior,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathrm A}}[\beta_1\beta_2]=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathrm B}}[\beta_1\beta_2]=-\frac14.
\]
For \(r=1\), in contrast, the unique edge-bag record is the complete law of \((\beta_0,\beta_1)\), and the function \(r^{-2}(\beta_0+d\beta_1)^2\) is measurable with respect to that record.  Thus two leaves are minimal for this particular edge-bag ambiguity.

The failure can also be read directly from the polynomial scope.  The target square expands as
\[
 \theta(v^\beta)^2
 =\frac1{r^2}\left[
 \beta_0^2+2d\beta_0\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_j
 +d^2\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_j^2
 +2d^2\sum_{1\le j<k\le r}\beta_j\beta_k
 \right].
\]
Define the canonical coefficient-interaction graph by joining two coefficient variables when they occur together in a nonzero monomial of this polynomial.  Because \(d\ne0\), every pair \(\beta_j,\beta_k\) of distinct leaf coefficients is joined, so this graph contains \(K_r\).  To verify the resulting width lower bound directly, root the decomposition tree.  For each clique vertex, the bags containing it form a connected subtree; let its top be the vertex of that subtree nearest the root, and choose a clique vertex whose top is farthest from the root.  The subtrees for two clique vertices intersect because some bag contains their graph edge.  If \(y\) is an intersection with the selected subtree, both tops lie on the root-to-\(y\) path; maximal depth puts the selected top between the other top and \(y\).  Connectivity of the other subtree then places the selected top in it.  Thus the selected top belongs to every clique-vertex subtree, so its bag contains all \(r\) leaf variables.  The coefficient-interaction treewidth is therefore at least \(r-1\), although the raw incidence treewidth is one.

Finally, for either genuine prior define
\[
 M_{\nu_C}=\int_K v\otimes v\,d\nu_C(v).
\]
This is a convex combination of two rank-one schedule moments and hence belongs to \(\mathfrak M(K)\) by \texttt{def:moment-body}.  By the Riesz identity for \(\ell\),
\[
 \langle\ell,M_{\nu_C}\ell\rangle_E
 =\int_K\langle\ell,v\rangle_E^2\,d\nu_C(v)
 =\int_K\theta(v)^2\,d\nu_C(v).
\]
The two equal edge-bag records therefore give different values of the first target-moment term in \texttt{def:moment-dual}.  No function of those edge-bag records alone can reproduce that term for every \(M\in\mathfrak M(K)\).  Thus ordinary bag-moment consistency on the tree decomposition of \(\mathcal I\) is not an exact representation of the defined moment problem.  It must be augmented by cross-leaf correlation information or by an equivalent state carrying the global target sum, which proves the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is a self-contained representation diagnostic: it isolates the smallest exact obstruction to recovering the defined moment-body target term from ordinary raw-incidence edge-bag records. It is not a counterexample to a named published treewidth algorithm or workflow. \textit{Gap.} The obstruction concerns the particular raw edge-bag record defined in the proposition. WainwrightJordan2004 and PeyrardEtAl2019 motivate canonical scopes and elimination, but neither supplies the localized program refuted here; the result therefore does not establish a published-method failure and does not exclude a different exact algorithm using compressed global state. \textit{Consumer.} It is a constraint for any future proposed bag-local formulation of this moment program: that formulation must explicitly carry cross-leaf target-moment information or an equivalent global target-sum state.

\begin{lemma}[lem:rank-fiber-genuine-prior-certificates]\label{lem:rank-fiber-genuine-prior-certificates}
For every admissible fixed instance, if \(F>0\) there is a symmetric genuine probability law on \(K\) whose posterior-ambiguity certificate is exactly \(F/r_{\star}\). For every \(z\in\Omega_n\), there is a symmetric genuine two-point law \(\nu_z\in\mathcal P(K)\) satisfying \(\mathcal B(\nu_z)\ge p_{\pi}(z)\rho_z^2\), and there is a coefficient vector \(a_z\in\mathbb R^{V_n}\) satisfying
\[
 \sup_{v\in K}\left\lvert a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\right\rvert=\rho_z.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\right\}
 \le R^{\star}\le F=\mathcal D\le S_{\mathrm{fib}},
 \qquad
 \frac{F}{\kappa}\le R^{\star}\le F,
\]
and
\[
 1\le\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}
 \le\left\lvert\{z\in\Omega_n:\rho_z>0\}\right\rvert
 \le 2^n
\]
whenever \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}>0\), with the stated convention when \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\). In the latter case \(R^{\star}=F=\mathcal D=S_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\). The clipped incidence estimator has worst-case risk at most \(F\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an admissible finite instance. The parameter remains the fixed potential-outcome schedule \(v\in K\), the only experimental randomness is \(z\sim p_{\pi}\), and the priors constructed below are decision-theoretic lower-bound devices rather than superpopulation assumptions.

First record the geometry supplied by \(\mathrm{def:bounded-fourier-class}\). On \(E\), put
\[
 \|v\|_K=\max_{i\in V_n}\max_{x\in X_i}|v_i(x)|.
\]
If this maximum is zero, every function \(v_i\) vanishes on its finite domain, hence \(v=0\). Thus \(\|\cdot\|_K\) is a norm and \(K=\{v:\|v\|_K\le1\}\). In particular, \(K\) is compact, convex, centrally symmetric, and absolutely convex in the finite-dimensional space \(E\), and it contains a relative neighborhood of zero. Since the target probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one for every outcome unit, boundedness also gives
\[
 |\theta(v)|
 \le \frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{x\in X_i}p_{\pi',i}(x)|v_i(x)|
 \le1,
 \qquad v\in K.
\]
Consequently every compact set \(K\cap\ker N_z\) has an element attaining \(\rho_z\).

We next justify the optimizer used in \(\mathrm{def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity}\). Expanding a quadratic form and solving its normal equations gives, for every positive semidefinite \(M\),
\[
 \psi_z(M)
 =\min_{b\in\mathbb R^{V_n}}
 \langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}b,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}b)\rangle_E.
\]
Indeed, the normal equation is \(N_zMN_z^{\ast}b=N_zM\ell\); the range condition and Moore--Penrose convention in \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\) show that a solution exists and that substitution yields exactly the displayed pseudoinverse formula. Hence
\[
 \Phi(M):=\sum_zp_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M)
 =\inf_{(b_z)_z}\sum_zp_{\pi}(z)
 \langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}b_z,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}b_z)\rangle_E.
\]
For fixed \((b_z)_z\), the last expression is continuous and linear in \(M\); its infimum is therefore upper semicontinuous. The moment body is compact. To see this without importing an existence claim, let \(d\) be the dimension of the ambient self-adjoint-operator space. If a convex representation uses more than \(d+1\) atoms, affine dependence supplies coefficients summing to zero whose weighted atom sum is zero; moving the weights in one of the two possible directions until one weight first reaches zero preserves the represented point and the probability simplex. Iteration leaves at most \(d+1\) atoms. Hence \(\mathfrak M(K)\) is the continuous image of the compact set \(K^{d+1}\times\Delta_d\), allowing repeated atoms and zero weights, and is compact. Thus \(\Phi\) attains its maximum. The finite set of possible ranks then has a minimum over the nonempty maximizing set. By \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\), this maximum is \(\mathcal D=F\). If \(F>0\), a maximizing operator cannot be zero, so \(r_{\star}\ge1\).

Assume now that \(F>0\). By \(\mathrm{def:moment-body}\), a finite convex representation of the chosen optimizer is
\[
 M^{\star}=\sum_{k=1}^q\lambda_k(v_k\otimes v_k),
 \qquad
 v_k\in K,\quad \lambda_k>0,\quad \sum_{k=1}^q\lambda_k=1.
\]
Define \(B:\mathbb R^q\to E\) by
\[
 Bt=\sum_{k=1}^q\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,t_kv_k.
\]
For \(\|t\|_2\le1\), nonnegativity of
\(\sum_{k<l}(\sqrt{\lambda_l}|t_k|-\sqrt{\lambda_k}|t_l|)^2\)
gives
\[
 \left(\sum_{k=1}^q\sqrt{\lambda_k}|t_k|\right)^2
 \le\left(\sum_k\lambda_k\right)\left(\sum_kt_k^2\right)
 \le1.
\]
Absolute convexity of \(K\) therefore implies \(B\{t:\|t\|_2\le1\}\subseteq K\). Moreover,
\[
 BB^{\ast}f
 =\sum_{k=1}^q\lambda_k\langle v_k,f\rangle_Ev_k
 =M^{\star}f,
\]
so, on \(H_0=(\ker B)^{\perp}\), the dimension is
\(
\dim H_0=\operatorname{rank}(B)=\operatorname{rank}(M^{\star})=r_{\star}
\).

Let \(U\) be uniform on the unit sphere of \(H_0\), set \(\mathbf V=BU\), and denote its law by \(\nu_{\mathrm{rank}}\). This is a countably additive symmetric probability law supported on the genuine schedule class \(K\). Rotation invariance gives
\[
 \mathbb E[U\otimes U]=\frac1{r_{\star}}I_{H_0}.
\]
For completeness, the left side commutes with every orthogonal transformation of \(H_0\), hence is a scalar multiple of the identity; taking traces and using \(\|U\|_2=1\) makes that scalar \(1/r_{\star}\).

Fix \(z\), let \(A_z=N_zB|_{H_0}\), and let \(P_z\) be the orthogonal projection of \(H_0\) onto \((\ker A_z)^{\perp}=\operatorname{range}(A_z^{\ast})\). The vector \(P_zU\) is a linear measurable function of \(A_zU\), because \(A_z\) is injective on \((\ker A_z)^{\perp}\). The orthogonal reflection \(R_z=2P_z-I_{H_0}\) preserves the uniform sphere law, satisfies \(A_zR_zU=A_zU\), and negates \((I-P_z)U\). Thus, for every bounded Borel function \(g\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[g(A_zU)(I-P_z)U\right]
 =-\mathbb E\!\left[g(A_zU)(I-P_z)U\right]=0.
\]
This is the defining conditional-expectation identity, so
\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid A_zU]=P_zU.
\]
Put \(q=B^{\ast}\ell\). For every \(k\in\ker B\), \(\langle q,k\rangle=\langle\ell,Bk\rangle_E=0\), so \(q\in H_0\). Since
\(
\theta(\mathbf V)=\langle\ell,BU\rangle_E=\langle q,U\rangle_{H_0}
\), the preceding identity and the spherical second moment imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathrm{rank}}}
 \!\left[\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathrm{rank}}}
 \!\left(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V\right)\right]
 =\mathbb E\langle(I-P_z)q,U\rangle^2
 =\frac1{r_{\star}}\|(I-P_z)q\|_2^2.
\]
Now \(N_zM^{\star}\ell=A_zq\), \(N_zM^{\star}N_z^{\ast}=A_zA_z^{\ast}\), and diagonalizing the positive semidefinite operator \(A_zA_z^{\ast}\) on its range gives
\[
 A_z^{\ast}(A_zA_z^{\ast})^{\dagger}A_z=P_z.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \psi_z(M^{\star})
 =\|q\|_2^2-
 \langle A_zq,(A_zA_z^{\ast})^{\dagger}A_zq\rangle_{V_n}
 =\|(I-P_z)q\|_2^2.
\]
Summing over assignments proves the exact genuine-prior certificate
\[
 \mathcal B(\nu_{\mathrm{rank}})
 =\frac1{r_{\star}}\sum_zp_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M^{\star})
 =\frac{\mathcal D}{r_{\star}}
 =\frac{F}{r_{\star}}.
\]

We now construct the fiber certificates. Choose \(h_z\in K\cap\ker N_z\) with \(|\theta(h_z)|=\rho_z\), and let
\[
 \nu_z=\tfrac12\delta_{h_z}+\tfrac12\delta_{-h_z}.
\]
Central symmetry of \(K\) makes this a genuine symmetric prior. Under assignment \(z\), both atoms give the observation zero and their target values are opposite. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\nu_z}(\theta(\mathbf V)\mid N_z\mathbf V)
 =\theta(h_z)^2=\rho_z^2.
\]
Every other assignment contribution is nonnegative, so
\[
 \mathcal B(\nu_z)\ge p_{\pi}(z)\rho_z^2.
\]
Applying the lower certificate in \(\mathrm{prop:primal-dual-certificates}\) and maximizing over \(z\) gives
\[
 R^{\star}\ge m_{\mathrm{fib}}.
\]

It remains to prove the computable fiber upper bound. On \(S_z=\ker N_z\), the functional \(\theta|_{S_z}\) has dual norm \(\rho_z\) relative to \(\|\cdot\|_K\). We give the finite-dimensional norm-preserving extension argument explicitly. Suppose \(f\) is already defined on a proper subspace \(S\) and obeys \(|f(s)|\le\rho_z\|s\|_K\). For \(x\notin S\), set
\[
 L=\sup_{s\in S}\{f(s)-\rho_z\|s-x\|_K\},
 \qquad
 U=\inf_{t\in S}\{\rho_z\|t+x\|_K-f(t)\}.
\]
For all \(s,t\in S\),
\[
 f(s)+f(t)=f(s+t)
 \le\rho_z\|s+t\|_K
 \le\rho_z\bigl(\|s-x\|_K+\|t+x\|_K\bigr),
\]
so \(L\le U\). Choosing \(\alpha\in[L,U]\) and defining
\(
f'(s+\lambda x)=f(s)+\lambda\alpha
\)
preserves \(|f'|\le\rho_z\|\cdot\|_K\): for \(\lambda>0\) this follows from the definitions of \(L\) and \(U\) after division by \(\lambda\), for \(\lambda<0\) it follows by negation, and \(\lambda=0\) is the original bound. Iterating over a basis of \(E\) yields a linear functional \(q_z\) on \(E\) such that
\[
 q_z|_{\ker N_z}=\theta|_{\ker N_z},
 \qquad
 \sup_{v\in K}|q_z(v)|=\rho_z.
\]
The equality follows because the extension has norm at most \(\rho_z\), while its restriction has norm exactly \(\rho_z\); the case \(\rho_z=0\) is included.

The functional \(\theta-q_z\) vanishes on \(\ker N_z\), so it factors through \(N_z\): on \(\operatorname{range}(N_z)\), define \(b_z(N_zv)=\theta(v)-q_z(v)\). This is well defined, and a linear extension to Euclidean \(\mathbb R^{V_n}\) has the form \(b_z(y)=a_z^{\mathsf T}y\) for some \(a_z\in\mathbb R^{V_n}\). Thus
\[
 a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)=-q_z(v),
 \qquad
 \sup_{v\in K}|a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)|=\rho_z.
\]
Using these vectors assignment by assignment in \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) gives
\[
 F\le\sup_{v\in K}\sum_zp_{\pi}(z)\rho_z^2
 =S_{\mathrm{fib}}.
\]

When \(F>0\), the rank prior and \(\mathrm{prop:primal-dual-certificates}\) give \(R^{\star}\ge F/r_{\star}\). Together with the fiber prior, the established upper comparison, and \(F=\mathcal D\), this proves
\[
 \max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\right\}
 \le R^{\star}\le F=\mathcal D\le S_{\mathrm{fib}}.
\]
If \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}>0\), then \(S_{\mathrm{fib}}\ge m_{\mathrm{fib}}\), and every positive summand in \(S_{\mathrm{fib}}\) is at most \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}\). Therefore
\[
 1\le\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}
 =\frac{S_{\mathrm{fib}}}{m_{\mathrm{fib}}}
 \le|\{z:\rho_z>0\}|\le|\Omega_n|=2^n.
\]
Furthermore, since \(F\le S_{\mathrm{fib}}\),
\[
 \frac{F}{\kappa}
 =\max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},
                 \frac{Fm_{\mathrm{fib}}}{S_{\mathrm{fib}}}\right\}
 \le\max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\right\}
 \le R^{\star}.
\]
If \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\), then every nonnegative summand is zero, so \(S_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\); the bound \(F\le S_{\mathrm{fib}}\) forces \(F=\mathcal D=0\), and \(0\le R^{\star}\le F\) forces \(R^{\star}=0\). The convention \(r_{\star}=\kappa=1\) then makes the same sandwich valid. Finally, the clipped-incidence risk bound is exactly the last assertion of \(\mathrm{prop:primal-dual-certificates}\). This completes every case.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rank prior and selector-fiber constructions give genuine, computable all-estimator lower certificates with explicit instance-dependent losses. \textit{Gap.} The construction preserves bounded-schedule support exactly, while exposing why its rank and fiber losses do not settle the dimension-free question left open by ChenValiantValiant2020. \textit{Consumer.} The two in-place frontier theorems use this lemma to calibrate finite-instance risk and triangular-array consistency.

\begin{theorem}[thm:additive-one-hub-exact-minimax]\label{thm:additive-one-hub-exact-minimax}
For every pair of integers \(d\geq 1\) and \(n\geq d\), consider an admissible fixed \(n\)-unit instance with one distinguished outcome unit \(i_{\circ}\) having neighborhood \(\Gamma_{i_{\circ}}=\{1,\ldots,d\}\), source probabilities \(\pi_j=1/2\) on these coordinates, and retained support
\[
 \mathcal S_{i_{\circ}}=\{\varnothing,\{1\},\ldots,\{d\}\}.
\]
Write \(S_j=2Z_j-1\), \(r_0(s)=1\), and \(r_j(s)=s_j\) for \(1\leq j\leq d\).  The distinguished schedule is
\[
 h_{\beta}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^d\beta_jr_j(s),
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^d|\beta_j|\leq 1,
\]
and every other outcome unit has retained support \(\{\varnothing\}\), so its schedule class consists exactly of constants.  Put \(m_j=2\pi'_j-1\), \(\gamma_0=1\), and \(\gamma_j=m_j\) for \(1\leq j\leq d\), and let
\[
 \Delta_d=\left\{\lambda\in[0,1]^{d+1}:\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j=1\right\}.
\]
If \(F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\) and \(R_d^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}\) denote, before population-average normalization, respectively the assignment-indexed linear and all-Borel minimax mean squared errors for estimating
\[
 L(\beta)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^dm_j\beta_j
\]
from the source assignment and the observed hub response, then
\[
 R_d^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}=F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}
 =\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}
 \left\{\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
 +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j)\right\}.
\]
A maximizer \(\lambda^{\star}\) exists.  An attaining linear rule is \(a^{\star}(s)Y\), where
\[
 a^{\star}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(s),
 \qquad
 Y=h_{\beta}(S),
\]
and a matching genuine least-favorable prior is \(\mathbf B=T_0e_J\), where \(T_0\) is Rademacher, \(\mathbb P(J=j)=\lambda_j^{\star}\), and \(T_0\), \(J\), and the source assignment are mutually independent.  For the population-average target of the full instance, the frontiers in \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{def:unrestricted-frontier}\) therefore satisfy
\[
 R^{\star}=F
 =\frac{1}{n^2}\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}
 \left\{\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
 +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j)\right\}.
\]
Thus this additive one-hub family has \(R^{\star}/F=1\) whenever \(F>0\) in every dimension and cannot be a vanishing-ratio witness for the universal comparison question.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(d\geq1\) and an instance described in the statement.  The source-product design and \(\pi_j=1/2\) imply that \(S_1,\ldots,S_d\) are independent Rademacher variables.  By \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-character}\),
\[
 \chi_{i_{\circ},\{j\}}^{\pi}(Z)
 =\frac{Z_j-1/2}{\sqrt{(1/2)(1-1/2)}}=2Z_j-1=S_j.
\]
The retained characters in \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-space}\) are therefore exactly \(1,S_1,\ldots,S_d\), which are linearly independent under the positive source law.  Hence each distinguished schedule has a unique representation
\[
 h_{\beta}(s)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^d\beta_js_j.
\]
For every \(s\in\{-1,1\}^d\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 |h_{\beta}(s)|\leq |\beta_0|+\sum_{j=1}^d|\beta_j|.
\]
Conversely, if \(\beta_0\neq0\), choose each \(s_j\) so that \(\beta_js_j\) has the sign of \(\beta_0\); if \(\beta_0=0\), choose \(s_j\) so that \(\beta_js_j\geq0\).  In either case equality holds for that \(s\).  Consequently \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\) gives the exact coefficient body
\[
 K_{i_{\circ}}
 \cong B_1^{d+1}
 :=\left\{\beta\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:\sum_{j=0}^d|\beta_j|\leq1\right\}.
\]
This verifies rather than assumes the bounded-schedule membership of the family.  The support is downward closed, source overlap holds because \(\varepsilon<1/2\), and target overlap makes every \(m_j=2\pi'_j-1\) well defined with \(|m_j|<1\).

Under the target-product policy, \(\mathbb E_{\pi'}S_j=2\pi'_j-1=m_j\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{def:local-target-mass}\) and \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\) give the unnormalized distinguished-unit target
\[
 L(\beta)=\mathbb E_{\pi'}h_{\beta}(S)
 =\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^dm_j\beta_j
 =\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j\beta_j.
\]
The selector in \(\mathrm{def:observation-operator}\) reveals
\[
 Y=h_{\beta}(S)=\sum_{j=0}^dr_j(S)\beta_j.
\]
Assignment coordinates outside \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\) are ancillary.  Indeed, if \(W\) denotes those coordinates and an assignment-indexed linear rule uses the hub coefficient \(A(S,W)\), put \(\bar a(S)=\mathbb E[A(S,W)\mid S]\).  For each \(j\), conditional Jensen gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[(A(S,W)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2\mid S\right]
 \geq (\bar a(S)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2.
\]
Thus ancillary assignment dependence cannot reduce any of the coordinate risks below that of a rule \(a(S)Y\).

For such a rule, define
\[
 Q_a(\beta)=\mathbb E\left[
 \left\{a(S)\sum_{j=0}^dr_j(S)\beta_j-
             \sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j\beta_j\right\}^2
 \right]
\]
and
\[
 L_j(a)=Q_a(e_j)=\mathbb E[(a(S)r_j(S)-\gamma_j)^2].
\]
The function \(Q_a\) is a positive semidefinite quadratic form and hence convex.  Since
\(
B_1^{d+1}=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_0,\ldots,\pm e_d\}
\), convexity and \(Q_a(-e_j)=Q_a(e_j)\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\beta\in B_1^{d+1}}Q_a(\beta)=\max_{0\leq j\leq d}L_j(a).
\]
Accordingly,
\[
 F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}=\inf_a\max_{0\leq j\leq d}L_j(a).
\]

For \(\lambda\in\Delta_d\), orthogonality of the Rademacher characters gives \(\mathbb E[r_j(S)r_k(S)]=\mathbf 1\{j=k\}\).  Completing the square pointwise therefore yields, for every \(a\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_jL_j(a)
 &\geq\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_jL_j(a)\\
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[
 \left(a(S)-\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j\gamma_jr_j(S)\right)^2
 \right]+G(\lambda)\\
 &\geq G(\lambda),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G(\lambda)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j\gamma_j^2
   -\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^2\gamma_j^2\\
 &=\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
   +\sum_{j=1}^dm_j^2\lambda_j(1-\lambda_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(a\) and then the supremum over \(\lambda\) proves
\[
 F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\geq\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_d}G(\lambda).
\]
The simplex is compact and \(G\) is continuous, so a maximizer \(\lambda^{\star}\) exists.

Define
\[
 a^{\star}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(s),
 \qquad
 T=\sum_{j=0}^d(\lambda_j^{\star})^2\gamma_j^2.
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
 L_j(a^{\star})=T+\gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star}).
\]
We now certify optimality on the simplex directly.  If \(\lambda_j^{\star}>0\) and \(\lambda_k^{\star}>0\), the feasible perturbation \(\lambda^{\star}+t(e_j-e_k)\) exists for both signs of all sufficiently small \(t\).  Maximality forces the derivative at zero to vanish, so
\[
 \gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star})
 =\gamma_k^2(1-2\lambda_k^{\star}).
\]
If \(\lambda_j^{\star}=0\) and \(\lambda_k^{\star}>0\), the same perturbation is feasible for sufficiently small \(t\geq0\); its one-sided derivative cannot be positive, and hence
\[
 \gamma_j^2(1-2\lambda_j^{\star})
 \leq\gamma_k^2(1-2\lambda_k^{\star}).
\]
Thus the values \(L_j(a^{\star})\) are equal on the support of \(\lambda^{\star}\), and every value off that support is no larger.  Moreover, the completed-square identity at \(a=a^{\star}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}L_j(a^{\star})=G(\lambda^{\star}).
\]
The left side is the common supported value because the supported weights sum to one.  Therefore
\[
 \max_jL_j(a^{\star})=G(\lambda^{\star}),
 \qquad
 F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}=G(\lambda^{\star}).
\]
This supplies matching primal and simplex-dual certificates, not merely a candidate rule.

It remains to show that nonlinear Borel rules cannot improve the value.  Let \(J\) have probabilities \(\lambda_j^{\star}\), let \(T_0\) be an independent Rademacher sign, and set
\[
 \mathbf B=T_0e_J.
\]
Because every \(\pm e_j\) belongs to \(B_1^{d+1}\), this is a countably additive symmetric prior supported on genuine bounded schedules, and it is independent of the source assignment as required by the fixed-schedule convention.  Conditional on \(J=j\),
\[
 Y=T_0r_j(S)\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
For every \(s\), every \(j\), and every \(y\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \mathbb P(Y=y\mid S=s,J=j)=\frac12.
\]
Bayes' formula consequently gives
\[
 \mathbb P(J=j\mid S=s,Y=y)=\lambda_j^{\star}.
\]
The ancillary coordinates \(W\) are independent of \((J,T_0)\) and add no information, so the same posterior holds conditional on \((S,W,Y)\).  Since \(T_0=Yr_J(S)\),
\[
 L(\mathbf B)=Y\gamma_Jr_J(S)
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E[L(\mathbf B)\mid S,W,Y]
 =Y\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(S)
 =Ya^{\star}(S).
\]
Its average posterior variance is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\operatorname{Var}(L(\mathbf B)\mid S,W,Y)\right]
 &=\mathbb E_S\!\left[
 \sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_j^2-
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_jr_j(S)\right)^2
 \right]\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^d\lambda_j^{\star}\gamma_j^2
   -\sum_{j=0}^d(\lambda_j^{\star})^2\gamma_j^2\\
 &=G(\lambda^{\star})=F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, if \(D=D(S,W,Y)\) is any Borel estimator and \(\mu=\mathbb E[L(\mathbf B)\mid S,W,Y]\), expansion followed by conditional expectation gives
\[
 \mathbb E[(D-L(\mathbf B))^2]
 =\mathbb E[(D-\mu)^2]
  +\mathbb E[(L(\mathbf B)-\mu)^2]
 \geq F_d^{\mathrm{hub}},
\]
because the cross term has conditional expectation zero.  The worst-case risk is at least this prior-average risk.  Thus \(R_d^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}\geq F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\).  Conversely, \(a^{\star}(S)Y\) is Borel, so \(R_d^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}\leq F_d^{\mathrm{hub}}\), proving the unnormalized equality.

Finally, every nondistinguished schedule is constant, its realized outcome equals its target-policy mean under every assignment, and it can therefore be included with coefficient \(1/n\) without error.  The rule
\[
 \widehat\theta
 =\frac1n\left(a^{\star}(S)Y+
          \sum_{i\neq i_{\circ}}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\right)
\]
has worst-case risk \(n^{-2}G(\lambda^{\star})\).  For the reverse inequality, restrict the full schedule class to zero constants on all other units and use the preceding genuine prior on the distinguished schedule; multiplication of the target by \(n^{-1}\) multiplies squared loss by \(n^{-2}\).  This proves
\[
 R^{\star}=F=n^{-2}G(\lambda^{\star}).
\]
As the smallest exact stress test, when \(d=1\) the simplex has \(\lambda_1=1-\lambda_0\), so
\[
 R_1^{\star,\mathrm{hub}}=F_1^{\mathrm{hub}}
 =\max_{0\leq\lambda_0\leq1}(1+m_1^2)\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)
 =\frac{1+m_1^2}{4}.
\]
In particular the equality remains valid at \(\pi'_1=\pi_1=1/2\).  The proof used only additive supports; it supplies no dimension-free rounding for supports containing interactions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This exact all-Borel calculation is the prescribed additive launch-or-kill test for the remaining dimension-free comparison question. \textit{Gap.} It rules out additive one-hub schedules with the exact coefficient \(\ell_1\) body as a vanishing-ratio witness but does not cover interaction supports. \textit{Consumer.} The unresolved prior-rounding question uses this theorem to exclude the additive-hub route to a negative answer.

\begin{theorem}[thm:hub-local-policy-elbow]\label{thm:hub-local-policy-elbow}
Let \((p_n)_{n\ge2}\) and \((q_n)_{n\ge2}\) be arbitrary sequences in \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), and instantiate the row-\(n\) member of \(\mathcal H_n^{\mathrm{hub}}\) at source probability \(p_n\) and target probability \(q_n\).  Then, uniformly over \(n\ge2\),
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon}{4n}
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n\le\frac1{\varepsilon n}.
\]
Moreover, for either \(X_n\in\{R_n^{\star},F_n\}\),
\[
 \varepsilon n\bigl(1+4n(q_n-p_n)^2\bigr)
 \le
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)/n}{X_n}
 \le
 \frac{4n}{\varepsilon}
 \left(1+\frac{n(q_n-p_n)^2}{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}\right).
\]
Consequently \(R_n^{\star}=\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n^{-1})\), \(F_n=\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n^{-1})\), and both displayed envelope-to-frontier ratios are
\[
 \Theta_{\varepsilon}\!\left(n+n^2(q_n-p_n)^2\right).
\]
The local-policy elbow is \(|q_n-p_n|\asymp n^{-1/2}\): if \(|q_n-p_n|\le n^{-1/2}\), each ratio is \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n)\), whereas if \(|q_n-p_n|\ge n^{-1/2}\), each is \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n^2(q_n-p_n)^2)\).  All constants hidden by \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}\) depend only on \(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\ge2\) and abbreviate \(p=p_n\), \(q=q_n\), and \(d_n=q_n-p_n\).  Because both policy sequences lie in \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\),
\[
 p,1-p,q,1-q\ge\varepsilon.
\]
For the two quantities in \(\texttt{lem:hub-frontier-rate-sandwich}\), the same elementary estimates as in the fixed-policy theorem give
\[
 (1-p)q^2+p(1-q)^2
 \ge\varepsilon\{q^2+(1-q)^2\}
 \ge\frac\varepsilon2
\]
and
\[
 \frac{(1-q)^2}{1-p}+\frac{q^2}{p}
 \le\frac{(1-q)^2+q^2}{\varepsilon}
 \le\frac1\varepsilon.
\]
Substitution into that lemma, followed by \((n-1)/n^2\ge1/(2n)\), gives
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon}{4n}
 \le R_n^{\star}\le F_n\le\frac1{\varepsilon n}.
\]
These constants are independent of the particular sequences, proving the two uniform \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n^{-1})\) assertions.

Next, \(\texttt{lem:hub-loomba-eckles-envelope}\), applied row by row, gives the exact equality
\[
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}n
 =1+\frac{n d_n^2}{p_n(1-p_n)}.
\]
Source overlap implies
\[
 \varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)
 \le p_n(1-p_n)\le\frac14.
\]
The upper bound follows from
\(p_n(1-p_n)=1/4-(p_n-1/2)^2\le1/4\); the lower bound follows because the concave function \(t(1-t)\) takes its minimum on \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) at an endpoint.  Therefore
\[
 1+4n d_n^2
 \le
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)}n
 \le
 1+\frac{n d_n^2}{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}.
\]
For either \(X_n\in\{R_n^{\star},F_n\}\), inversion of the positive frontier bounds is legitimate and yields
\[
 \varepsilon n\le X_n^{-1}\le\frac{4n}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Multiplying the last two displays proves
\[
 \varepsilon n(1+4n d_n^2)
 \le
 \frac{B_n^{\mathrm{LE}}(D_n^{\mathrm{LE}}+1)/n}{X_n}
 \le
 \frac{4n}{\varepsilon}
 \left(1+\frac{n d_n^2}{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}\right).
\]
The lower and upper expressions are constant multiples, depending only on \(\varepsilon\), of \(n+n^2d_n^2\).  This proves the claimed uniform order.

Finally, if \(|d_n|\le n^{-1/2}\), then
\[
 n\le n+n^2d_n^2\le2n,
\]
so each ratio is \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n)\).  If \(|d_n|\ge n^{-1/2}\), then
\[
 n^2d_n^2\le n+n^2d_n^2\le2n^2d_n^2,
\]
so each ratio is \(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(n^2d_n^2)\).  The two regimes meet at \(|q_n-p_n|=n^{-1/2}\), establishing the stated elbow without any constants depending on the policy sequences.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The sequence-level corollary identifies the exact local-policy scale at which the published overlap envelope changes from an order-\(n\) to a policy-shift-dominated gap. \textit{Gap.} The fixed-policy hub theorem gives the endpoint regimes but does not expose the intermediate triangular-policy boundary \(|q_n-p_n|\asymp n^{-1/2}\). \textit{Consumer.} Designers comparing nearby source and target policies can read off when local policy drift starts to dominate the conservativeness of the maximum-overlap certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:instance-adaptive-risk-certificate]\label{thm:instance-adaptive-risk-certificate}
For every admissible fixed finite-population instance, the unrestricted and linear minimax risks obey the instance-adaptive certificate
\[
 \max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\right\}
 \le R^{\star}\le F=\mathcal D\le S_{\mathrm{fib}},
 \qquad
 \frac{F}{\kappa}\le R^{\star}\le F.                                            \tag{14}
\]
When \(F>0\), \(r_{\star}\) is the minimum rank of a dual optimizer; it has the stated value-one convention when \(F=0\).  The quantity \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}\) is the effective number of assignment fibers carrying nonzero ambiguity.  When \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}>0\),
\[
 1\le\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}
 \le\left\lvert\{z\in\Omega_n:\rho_z>0\}\right\rvert
 \le2^n.
\]
When \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\), all four values \(R^{\star},F,\mathcal D,S_{\mathrm{fib}}\) are zero.  The clipped incidence estimator has worst-case risk at most \(F\).  The factors in \((14)\) are instance dependent; no universal numerical comparison constant is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The declared lemma \(\mathrm{lem:rank-fiber-genuine-prior-certificates}\) supplies, for \(F>0\), a genuine prior with lower certificate \(F/r_{\star}\), and for every assignment fiber a genuine two-point prior with lower certificate at least \(p_\pi(z)\rho_z^2\).  Applying \(\mathrm{prop:primal-dual-certificates}\) to these priors gives
\[
 \max\left\{\frac{F}{r_{\star}},m_{\mathrm{fib}}\right\}\le R^{\star}.          \tag{15}
\]
The same lemma constructs, separately for every \(z\), a linear extension whose worst absolute error is \(\rho_z\); summing its squared errors with the design masses gives
\[
 F\le S_{\mathrm{fib}}.                                                           \tag{16}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathrm{prop:primal-dual-certificates}\) gives \(R^{\star}\le F\) and the clipped-estimator risk bound, while \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) gives \(F=\mathcal D\).  Equations \((15)\) and \((16)\) establish the first chain in \((14)\).

When \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}>0\), the definition
\[
 \kappa=\min\{r_{\star},\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}\},
 \qquad
 \kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}=\frac{S_{\mathrm{fib}}}{m_{\mathrm{fib}}}
\]
allows two cases.  If \(\kappa=r_{\star}\), \((15)\) gives \(F/\kappa\le R^{\star}\).  If \(\kappa=\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}\), then \((16)\) and the fiber lower bound give
\[
 \frac{F}{\kappa}
 \le\frac{S_{\mathrm{fib}}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}}
 =m_{\mathrm{fib}}
 \le R^{\star}.
\]
The cardinality bound and the case \(m_{\mathrm{fib}}=0\), including the conclusion that every displayed risk is zero, are exactly the boundary conclusions of \(\mathrm{lem:rank-fiber-genuine-prior-certificates}\).  This proves every assertion and also makes explicit why the comparison factor is not dimension free.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The certificate quantifies the all-Borel value of the attainable linear risk through observable finite-instance rank and selector-fiber factors without asserting dimension-free equivalence. \textit{Gap.} ChenValiantValiant2020 leaves the universal comparison open; the proved conclusion is the exact unconditional comparison supplied by minimum dual rank and effective fiber count. \textit{Consumer.} Experimenters can report the incidence upper risk together with rank and fiber lower certificates that show how informative it is for unrestricted procedures.

\begin{proposition}[prop:fully-expanded-frontier-program]\label{prop:fully-expanded-frontier-program}
For every admissible fixed instance, write \(\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\) for the vertex set of the local polytope \(K_i\). The exact linear frontier is the finite convex epigraph program
\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&\min_{\substack{t\in\mathbb R\\ a\in(\mathbb R^{V_n})^{\Omega_n}}}t\\
\text{subject to }{}&
\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
\left[
\sum_{i\in V_n}(a_z)_i v_i(z_{\Gamma_i})
-\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{x\in X_i}p_{\pi',i}(x)v_i(x)
\right]^2\le t
\quad\text{for every }v\in\prod_{i\in V_n}\operatorname{vert}(K_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{P}
\]
Its explicit numeric inputs are the tables \(p_{\pi}(z)\), \(p_{\pi',i}(x)\), the restrictions \(z_{\Gamma_i}\), and the vertex-evaluation tables \(\{(v_i(x))_{x\in X_i}:v_i\in\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\}\), all computed from the known \(\pi,\pi',\Gamma_i,\mathcal S_i\). Program \((P)\) has exactly \(n2^n+1\) real decision variables and \(\prod_{i\in V_n}\lvert\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\rvert\) listed convex quadratic constraints. An optimum exists, its weight component may be taken to be \(a^{\star}\), and there is a matching moment certificate \(M\in\mathfrak M(K)\) such that
\[
F=\mathcal D=
\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M).
\tag{D}
\]
When \(F>0\), \(M\) may be chosen to be the minimum-rank optimizer \(M^{\star}\) from \(\mathrm{def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed weight array \(a\), put
\[
Q_a(v)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
\bigl(a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)\bigr)^2.
\]
Each residual in this sum is linear in \(v\). Since \(s\mapsto s^2\) is convex and the design masses are nonnegative by \(\mathrm{ass:source-product-design}\), for \(v,w\in K\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
Q_a(\lambda v+(1-\lambda)w)
\le \lambda Q_a(v)+(1-\lambda)Q_a(w).
\tag{1}
\]

We next justify the vertex reduction. By \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\), \(K_i\) is the intersection of the finite-dimensional space \(H_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{X_i}\) with the finitely many slabs \(-1\le h(x)\le1\), \(x\in X_i\). It contains zero, is closed, and is bounded in the evaluation coordinates; hence it is a nonempty compact polytope. Every point of this polytope is a convex combination of its vertices. Indeed, induct on the dimension of the point's smallest face. A zero-dimensional face is a vertex. In a positive-dimensional smallest face, a line through the point in a nonzero direction of the face meets the compact face in a closed segment with two boundary endpoints; the point is a convex combination of those endpoints, and each endpoint has a smallest face of strictly smaller dimension. The induction terminates because the dimension is finite. This argument also shows that the vertex set is finite, since every vertex is the unique solution of a full-rank subsystem selected from finitely many defining equalities.

Thus write each \(v_i\in K_i\) as \(v_i=\sum_{k_i}\lambda_{i,k_i}u_{i,k_i}\), where \(u_{i,k_i}\in\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\), \(\lambda_{i,k_i}\ge0\), and \(\sum_{k_i}\lambda_{i,k_i}=1\). Expanding the product gives
\[
v=\sum_{(k_i)_{i\in V_n}}
\left(\prod_{i\in V_n}\lambda_{i,k_i}\right)
(u_{i,k_i})_{i\in V_n},
\tag{2}
\]
a convex combination of the product vertices. Applying \((1)\) repeatedly to the finite combination \((2)\) yields
\[
\sup_{v\in K}Q_a(v)
=\max_{v\in\prod_i\operatorname{vert}(K_i)}Q_a(v).
\tag{3}
\]
The upper bound in \((3)\) follows from the convex combination, and the reverse bound follows because every listed product vertex belongs to \(K\) by \(\mathrm{def:bounded-fourier-class}\).

By \(\mathrm{def:observation-operator}\) and \(\mathrm{def:target-value}\), the residual at a product vertex is exactly
\[
a_z^{\mathsf T}N_zv-\theta(v)
=\sum_{i\in V_n}(a_z)_iv_i(z_{\Gamma_i})
-\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}\sum_{x\in X_i}p_{\pi',i}(x)v_i(x).
\tag{4}
\]
Substituting \((3)\) and \((4)\) into \(\mathrm{def:linear-frontier}\) gives precisely the epigraph program \((P)\). There are \(2^n\) vectors \(a_z\in\mathbb R^{V_n}\) and one scalar \(t\), hence \(n2^n+1\) real variables. Equation \((3)\) supplies one listed constraint for each element of \(\prod_i\operatorname{vert}(K_i)\), hence the claimed product count. Each constraint is a nonnegative weighted sum of squares of affine functions of \(a\), bounded above by the affine variable \(t\); it is therefore a convex quadratic constraint. The input list in the statement is exactly the list of coefficients occurring in \((4)\). By \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\), the infimum over \(a\) is attained, so the epigraph optimum is attained with \(t=F\) and weight component \(a^{\star}\).

These coefficients are computable from the declared inputs. The source mass table follows from \(\mathrm{ass:source-product-design}\); source overlap and \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-character}\) give finite real character-evaluation tables; \(\mathrm{ass:hierarchical-support}\) fixes the finite retained subfamilies; and \(\mathrm{def:local-evaluation-polytope}\) reduces each vertex calculation to finitely many linear equalities and inequalities. Likewise, \(\mathrm{ass:target-product-policy}\) and \(\mathrm{def:local-target-mass}\) compute every \(p_{\pi',i}(x)\). Thus no unlisted schedule value or unknown distribution enters \((P)\).

It remains to exhibit the matching dual optimizer. For \(M\in\mathfrak M(K)\), the range condition in \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\) and completion of the positive-semidefinite quadratic give, for every \(z\),
\[
\psi_z(M)=\min_{b\in\mathbb R^{V_n}}
\langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}b,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}b)\rangle_E.
\tag{5}
\]
To verify \((5)\), expand its right-hand side as
\[
\langle\ell,M\ell\rangle_E
-2\langle b,N_zM\ell\rangle_{V_n}
+\langle b,(N_zMN_z^{\ast})b\rangle_{V_n}.
\]
Because \(N_zM\ell\in\operatorname{range}(N_zMN_z^{\ast})\) by \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\), the minimizers solve the normal equation, and the minimum is
\[
\langle\ell,M\ell\rangle_E
-\left\langle N_zM\ell,
(N_zMN_z^{\ast})^{\dagger}N_zM\ell\right\rangle_{V_n},
\]
which is the defining formula for \(\psi_z(M)\). Independence of the finitely many coordinates \(b_z\) in \((5)\) implies
\[
\Phi(M):=\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)\psi_z(M)
=\inf_a\sum_{z\in\Omega_n}p_{\pi}(z)
\langle\ell-N_z^{\ast}a_z,M(\ell-N_z^{\ast}a_z)\rangle_E.
\tag{6}
\]
For fixed \(a\), the expression after the infimum is continuous and affine in \(M\); hence \(\Phi\), an infimum of continuous affine functions, is upper semicontinuous.

The set \(K\) is compact by the preceding local argument and the finite-product construction in \(\mathrm{def:bounded-fourier-class}\). Therefore \(\{v\otimes v:v\in K\}\) is compact. Its convex hull \(\mathfrak M(K)\) is compact as well. To see this directly, let \(d\) be the dimension of the real vector space of self-adjoint operators on \(E\). Any convex combination of more than \(d+1\) operators has affinely dependent support: there are coefficients, not all zero, whose sum and whose weighted operator sum are both zero. Moving the mixture weights in that null direction until one first reaches zero preserves the represented operator and removes one support point. Iteration represents every element of the convex hull using at most \(d+1\) support points. Thus \(\mathfrak M(K)\) is the continuous image of the compact product of \(d+1\) copies of \(\{v\otimes v:v\in K\}\) and the compact \(d\)-simplex, and is compact.

Choose a maximizing sequence for \(\Phi\) on \(\mathfrak M(K)\). Compactness gives a convergent subsequence with limit \(M^{\circ}\in\mathfrak M(K)\), and upper semicontinuity gives
\[
\Phi(M^{\circ})\ge\limsup_k\Phi(M_k)
=\sup_{M\in\mathfrak M(K)}\Phi(M).
\]
Thus the dual maximum is attained at \(M^{\circ}\). By \(\mathrm{def:moment-dual}\), \(\Phi(M^{\circ})=\mathcal D\), and the exact identity in \(\mathrm{lem:selector-reduction-and-dual}\) gives \(\mathcal D=F\). Consequently
\[
\Phi(M^{\circ})=\mathcal D=F,
\]
which is \((D)\). If \(F>0\), the nonempty set of maximizers has a minimum integer rank, and \(\mathrm{def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity}\) names such a certificate \(M^{\star}\).

For the smallest exact stress test, take \(n=1\), \(\Gamma_1=\{1\}\), and \(\mathcal S_1=\{\varnothing,\{1\}\}\). By \(\mathrm{def:local-fourier-character}\) and source overlap, the two retained characters are linearly independent and span all functions on \(X_1=\{0,1\}\). Hence in evaluation coordinates \(K_1=[-1,1]^2\) and
\[
\operatorname{vert}(K_1)=\{(-1,-1),(-1,1),(1,-1),(1,1)\}.
\]
There are two assignments, \(z=0,1\). Program \((P)\) therefore has the three variables \((a_0,a_1,t)\), exactly \(1\cdot2^1+1\), and four listed constraints, exactly \(\lvert\operatorname{vert}(K_1)\rvert\). For every fixed \((a_0,a_1)\), its risk is a convex quadratic on the square. Expressing an arbitrary point of the square as the product mixture of its four corners and applying \((1)\) proves that none of the continuum of nonvertex schedules exceeds those four enumerated risks. Thus exhaustive enumeration agrees with \((P)\) on the smallest nonconstant instance, including the variable and constraint counts.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lifted vertex epigraph makes the exact frontier directly computable without presupposing any treewidth compression. \textit{Gap.} Existing treewidth motivation does not itself provide the global target-square state needed to compress this exact program. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can enumerate local vertices and solve the displayed convex quadratic epigraph with matching primal and moment certificates.

\begin{proposition}[prop:complexity-calibrated-consistency]\label{prop:complexity-calibrated-consistency}
As a rowwise corollary of the instance-adaptive risk certificate, let \(\mathfrak A\in\mathsf{Adm}\) and let \(r_n,\rho_{n,z},S_{n,\mathrm{fib}},m_{n,\mathrm{fib}},\kappa_{n,\mathrm{fib}}\), and \(\kappa_n\) be the quantities in \(\mathrm{def:adaptive-rank-fiber-complexity}\).  Then: \(\mathrm{(a)}\) \(F_n\to0\) implies \(R_n^{\star}\to0\); \(\mathrm{(b)}\) in every fixed row, \(R_n^{\star}=0\) if and only if \(F_n=0\); \(\mathrm{(c)}\) \(\kappa_nR_n^{\star}\to0\) implies \(F_n\to0\); and \(\mathrm{(d)}\) on every subfamily satisfying \(\sup_n\kappa_n<\infty\), one has \(R_n^{\star}\to0\) if and only if \(F_n\to0\).  Clause \(\mathrm{(d)}\) is conditional on that subfamily property and does not add a condition to \(\mathsf{Adm}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathfrak A\in\mathsf{Adm}\).  Applying \(\mathrm{thm:instance-adaptive-risk-certificate}\) to every row and using the rowwise definition of \(\kappa_n\) gives
\[
 0\le R_n^{\star}\le F_n\le\kappa_nR_n^{\star},
 \qquad n\ge1.                                                                    \tag{17}
\]
The factor \(\kappa_n\) is finite: if \(m_{n,\mathrm{fib}}=0\), it equals one by definition; otherwise the cardinality bound in the cited theorem gives \(\kappa_n\le\kappa_{n,\mathrm{fib}}\le2^n\).

If \(F_n\to0\), the first two inequalities in \((17)\) squeeze \(R_n^{\star}\) to zero, proving \(\mathrm{(a)}\).  In a fixed row, \(F_n=0\) implies \(R_n^{\star}=0\) by \(R_n^{\star}\le F_n\), while \(R_n^{\star}=0\) implies \(F_n=0\) by \(F_n\le\kappa_nR_n^{\star}\) and finiteness of \(\kappa_n\).  This proves \(\mathrm{(b)}\).

If \(\kappa_nR_n^{\star}\to0\), the last inequality in \((17)\) squeezes \(F_n\) to zero, proving \(\mathrm{(c)}\).  Finally, on a subfamily with \(C:=\sup_n\kappa_n<\infty\), \(R_n^{\star}\to0\) implies
\[
 0\le F_n\le\kappa_nR_n^{\star}\le C R_n^{\star}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Together with \(\mathrm{(a)}\), this proves the equivalence in \(\mathrm{(d)}\).  No reverse implication has been used or asserted when \(\kappa_n\) is unbounded.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This supporting proposition records the exact rowwise consistency consequences of the rank/fiber sandwich. \textit{Gap.} LoombaEckles2026OffPolicy gives a sufficient degree envelope and HarshawSavjeWang2022 treats the Riesz-estimator class; the reverse all-Borel implication here is explicitly restricted to uniformly bounded instance complexity. \textit{Consumer.} A vanishing linear frontier certifies unrestricted consistency on every admissible array, while bounded \(\kappa_n\) identifies subfamilies on which the converse is valid.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal object is \(\theta(v)\), the target-policy finite-population average of fixed potential outcomes. The quantities \(F\), \(\mathcal D\), and \(R^{\star}\) are design-risk functionals for estimating that object from the single source-randomized assignment; they do not redefine it. The expanded program computes the best assignment-indexed linear rule, the moment optimizer certifies its value, and genuine priors calibrate what any Borel rule must lose. The rank and fiber factors quantify the remaining nonlinear advantage instance by instance. On the explicitly specified additive hubs, signed probability-weighted incidence geometry retains an \(n^{-1}\) frontier even when the maximum-overlap envelope is order \(n\) or \(n^2(q_n-p_n)^2\) larger; this does not assert a perturbation-stable generic observable-law separation.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The proved expanded epigraph is exponential in the number of assignments and in the product of local vertex counts. The width-one result is only a self-contained failure of the proposition's ordinary raw edge-bag record to determine the squared target moment; it is not a counterexample to a named published treewidth algorithm. An exact \(\operatorname{poly}(L_{\mathrm{in}})2^{O(w)}\) algorithm still requires a proved compressed global target-sum/separator state or a genuine complexity obstruction. The comparison factors \(r_{\star}\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fib}}\) can grow, so they do not settle the universal all-estimator constant, and reverse consistency is unconditional only after multiplication by \(\kappa_n\) or on subfamilies with bounded \(\kappa_n\).

\section{Honest scope}

Established results are the selector reduction, exact moment duality \(F=\mathcal D\), attainment, the fully expanded finite convex program, clipped-incidence and genuine-prior certificates, the instance-adaptive rank/fiber comparison, its complexity-calibrated consistency corollary, exact additive one-hub equality, the broader additive-hub \(n^{-1}\) rate and envelope elbow on the specified family, and disjoint-motif recovery under its explicit unbiased-witness premise. No generic perturbation-stable observable-law sharpness failure is claimed; no published treewidth algorithm is refuted by the raw-edge-bag diagnostic; no dimension-free comparison over all admissible arrays is claimed; no unconditional reverse-consistency theorem is claimed when \(\kappa_n\) diverges; and compressed bounded-treewidth exactness remains the stated open question. Extension to the full Loomba--Eckles exposure-action-order class, correlated or adaptive assignment, studentization, and empirical reanalysis of the Cr\'epon or Chin--Eckles--Ugander applications are outside the proved scope.
