Fix \(P=P_{U,\nu_n}^{(n)}\).  Conditional expectation under the witness gives \(b_P(u)=u\).  For every positive integer \(a\), partition \([0,1]\) into the Voronoi intervals of an ordered \(a\)-codebook.  If their lengths are \(p_1,\ldots,p_a\), completing the square on every interval shows that the least distortion conditional on these lengths is \(\sum_rp_r^3/12\).  Since \(\sum_rp_r=1\), Jensen's inequality gives \(\sum_rp_r^3\geq a^{-2}\), with equality only at \(p_r=1/a\).  Hence the unique centers are the equal-cell midpoints and
\[
 W_P(a)=\frac1{12a^2}.                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Put \(r_a(u)=u-q_{P,a}(u)\) and \(d_a(u)=r_a(u)^2\).  Because \(g_K=K^{-2}-(K+1)^{-2}\), the definitions of \(\phi_W\) and \(\phi_\rho\), together with (1), give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta\phi_{P,K}
 &=12\{d_K(U)-d_{K+1}(U)-g_Kd_1(U)\}\\
 &\quad+24\{r_K(U)-r_{K+1}(U)-g_Kr_1(U)\}\zeta_P.
 \end{aligned}                                                           \tag{2}
\]
The centering constants cancel because \(W_P(K)-W_P(K+1)=g_KW_P(1)\).  Define the periodic functions \(B_1(x)=\{x\}-1/2\) and \(B_2(x)=B_1(x)^2-1/12\), away from their immaterial endpoint convention.  Equal-cell geometry gives
\[
 r_a(u)=a^{-1}B_1(au),
 \qquad d_a(u)-W_P(a)=a^{-2}B_2(au).
\]
Therefore equation (2) becomes
\[
 \Delta\phi_{P,K}=12F_{K,2}(U)+24\zeta_PF_{K,1}(U),
 \qquad F_{K,p}(u)=\sum_aw_{K,p}(a)B_p(au).                              \tag{3}
\]
Repeated indices at \(K=1\) combine according to the indicator definition of \(w_{K,p}\).

For positive integers \(a,b\), partition \([0,1]\) at the common grid of mesh \(1/\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\).  On each grid interval both periodic Bernoulli functions are polynomials.  Expanding, integrating, and using the finite power-sum identities gives
\[
 \int_0^1B_1(au)B_1(bu)\,du=\frac{\gcd(a,b)^2}{12ab},
 \qquad
 \int_0^1B_2(au)B_2(bu)\,du=\frac{\gcd(a,b)^4}{180a^2b^2}.              \tag{4}
\]
For completeness, write \(a=da_0,b=db_0\) with \(\gcd(a_0,b_0)=1\).  Grouping the \(da_0b_0\) common-grid intervals by their residues modulo \(a_0\) and \(b_0\) leaves the factors \(d^2/(ab)\) and \(d^4/(a^2b^2)\); direct integration gives \(\int_0^1B_1^2=1/12\) and \(\int_0^1B_2^2=1/180\), proving (4).

Under the randomized witness, \(\mathbb E(\zeta_P\mid U)=0\) and \(\mathbb E(\zeta_P^2\mid U)=\nu_n^2\).  Thus the two terms in (3) are uncorrelated.  Equations (3)--(4) yield
\[
 \Sigma_P(K,L)=144H_2(K,L)+576\nu_n^2H_1(K,L).                           \tag{5}
\]
Expanding the diagonal and using \(\gcd(K,K+1)=1\) gives
\[
 H_1(K,K)=\frac{(K^2+K+1)(2K^4+4K^3-K^2-3K-1)}{12K^4(K+1)^4},           \tag{6}
\]
\[
 H_2(K,K)=\frac{K^8+4K^7+10K^6+16K^5+5K^4-12K^3-14K^2-6K-1}
 {90K^6(K+1)^6}.                                                         \tag{7}
\]
Multiplying (6) by \(576\nu_n^2\) and (7) by \(144\) proves
\(\sigma_{P,K}^2=V_K^{(0)}+\nu_n^2A_K\).

It remains to prove the one-sided correlation bound.  Define
\[
 \Gamma_p(K,L)=\frac{H_p(K,L)}{\{H_p(K,K)H_p(L,L)\}^{1/2}}.
\]
For \(k\geq8\), clearing positive denominators in (6)--(7) gives
\[
 H_1(k,k)\geq\frac7{48k^2},\qquad H_2(k,k)\geq\frac1{109k^4}.           \tag{8}
\]
After the substitution \(x=k-8\), the numerator differences are
\[
 x^6+44x^5+778x^4+6948x^3+31813x^2+63808x+24764
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 19x^8+1112x^7+27964x^6+392456x^5+3331355x^4+17229864x^3\\
 {}+51279088x^2+73631346x+26318627,
 \end{aligned}
\]
so (8) follows coefficient by coefficient.

Assume \(8\leq K<L\), put \(t=K/L\), and use \(g_k\leq2/k^3\).  The sign pattern of the four nonconstant frequency pairs is load-bearing: \(w_{K,p}(K)>0\), \(w_{K,p}(K+1)<0\), \(w_{L,p}(L)>0\), and \(w_{L,p}(L+1)<0\).  Consequently the cross-divisibility cases \(K\mid(L+1)\) and \((K+1)\mid L\) multiply opposite-sign weights and contribute nonpositively to \(H_p(K,L)\).  They may therefore be discarded in an upper bound.  The only full-greatest-common-divisor terms that can increase the covariance are the same-sign cases \(K\mid L\) and \((K+1)\mid(L+1)\).

For \(p=1\), the three positive terms involving frequency one, after division by (8), total at most
\[
 \frac47\left\{\frac4{K^2L^2}+\frac2{K^2L}+\frac2{KL^2}\right\}
 \leq\frac{19}{4536}<\frac3{700}.                                      \tag{9}
\]
If neither same-sign divisibility relation holds, every remaining positive greatest common divisor is a proper divisor and is at most half its smaller argument.  If \(t\leq19/20\), their normalized sum is at most \(2t/7\), and (9) gives
\[
 \Gamma_1(K,L)\leq\frac{19}{70}+\frac3{700}=\frac{193}{700}<\frac7{25}. \tag{10}
\]
If \(t>19/20\), writing \(h=L-K<K/19\) bounds both same-sign greatest common divisors by \(h+1\); their normalized sum is below \(8/(7\cdot19^2)\), and adding (9) again gives (10).

If \(K\mid L\), write \(L=qK\).  For \(q\geq3\), the first same-sign main term is at most \(4/(7q)\).  The other has greatest common divisor \(\gcd(K+1,q-1)\), and
\[
 \frac{\min(q-1,K+1)^2}{q}\leq K+1,
\]
so its contribution is at most \(4/\{7(K+1)\}\).  With (9), the total is at most \(16/63+3/700<7/25\).  The case \((K+1)\mid(L+1)\) is symmetric.  Only \(L=2K\) and \(L=2K+1\) remain.  Direct substitution yields
\[
 H_1(K,2K)=
 \begin{cases}
 (K-1)(K+1)/(48K^4),&K\text{ even},\\
 (K^4+2K^3-3K^2-2K-1)/\{48K^4(K+1)^2\},&K\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}                                                               \tag{11}
\]
and
\[
 H_1(K,2K+1)=
 \begin{cases}
 (K^4+2K^3-3K^2-6K-3)/\{48K^2(K+1)^4\},&K\text{ even},\\
 K(K+2)/\{48(K+1)^4\},&K\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                               \tag{12}
\]
Substitution of (6), clearing positive denominators in
\((7/25)^2H_1(K,K)H_1(L,L)-H_1(K,L)^2\), and writing \(x=K-8\) gives, in the four cases of (11)--(12), polynomials whose coefficient vectors are
\[
\begin{split}
 &(2544,240672,10393928,270870520,4742210135,58729915892,527271838246,3455262502406,\\
 &\quad16388732574185,54811597404220,122528488405133,164090221734096,99322453821696),\\
 &(2544,240672,10453928,275910520,4932530135,62984545892,589628026996,4081319864906,\\
 &\quad20749481238560,75619391746720,187621442781383,284640797514096,199687969101696),\\
 &(2544,278304,13972520,425483640,8748325655,127895513844,1362728652524,10659476141778,\\
 &\quad60735769607880,245783723516520,670424622710535,1106577747715608,835717237221492),\\
 &(2544,278304,13912520,420323640,8548825655,123329183844,1294207073774,9955128761778,\\
 &\quad55713169654755,221251056581520,591874592859285,957709168083108,708907772727117).
\end{split}
\]
Every coefficient is positive, completing \(\Gamma_1(K,L)\leq7/25\).

For \(p=2\), the positive frequency-one terms are bounded after standardization by
\[
 \frac{109}{180}\left\{\frac4{KL}
 +\frac{2L^2}{K(L+1)^4}+\frac{2K^2}{L(K+1)^4}\right\}\leq\frac1{25},    \tag{13}
\]
where clearing denominators shows that the right side is maximized at \((K,L)=(8,9)\).  With neither same-sign divisibility relation, the positive main terms are at most \(109/1440\).  Under same-sign divisibility with quotient \(q\geq3\), they are at most
\[
 \frac{109}{180q^2}+\frac{109(K+2)^2}{180(K+1)^4}<\frac4{25}.            \tag{14}
\]
For \(q=2\), the companion greatest common divisor is at most two and direct substitution gives the same bound.  The opposite-sign cross-divisibility terms remain nonpositive and are discarded.  Equations (13)--(14) prove \(\Gamma_2(K,L)\leq1/5<7/25\).

Since all diagonal terms are positive by (8), Cauchy--Schwarz in \(\mathbb R^2\) and (5) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Sigma_P(K,L)
 &\leq\frac7{25}\left[144\{H_2(K,K)H_2(L,L)\}^{1/2}
 +576\nu_n^2\{H_1(K,K)H_1(L,L)\}^{1/2}\right]\\
 &\leq\frac7{25}\sigma_{P,K}\sigma_{P,L}.
 \end{aligned}                                                               \tag{15}
\]
Thus \(R_P(K,L)\leq0.28\) and \(d_P(K,L)\geq\sqrt{2-0.56}=1.2\).

Let \(m=|J_n|=(K_n-8)_+\).  For \(m\geq2\), compare \(Z=(Z_{P,K}:K\in J_n)\) with
\[
 Y_K=\sqrt{0.72}\,\varepsilon_K+\sqrt{0.28}\,\varepsilon_0,
\]
where the Gaussian variables on the right are independent and standard normal.  Marginal variances agree and (15) gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z_K,Z_L)\leq\operatorname{Cov}(Y_K,Y_L)\) off the diagonal.  For
\(f_\beta(x)=\beta^{-1}\log\sum_Ke^{\beta x_K}\), Gaussian integration by parts along the covariance interpolation gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}\mathbb Ef_\beta
 =\frac12\sum_{K,L}\{R_P(K,L)-R_Y(K,L)\}\mathbb E(\partial_{KL}f_\beta)\geq0, \tag{16}
\]
because diagonal differences vanish and \(\partial_{KL}f_\beta=-\beta p_Kp_L\leq0\) for \(K\neq L\).  Letting \(\beta\to\infty\),
\[
 \mathbb E\max_{K\in J_n}Z_{P,K}
 \geq\sqrt{0.72}\,\mathbb E\max_{j\leq m}\varepsilon_j.                \tag{17}
\]
Integration of the Gaussian density on \([t,t+t^{-1}]\) gives
\(1-\Phi(t)\geq ct^{-1}e^{-t^2/2}\) for \(t\geq1\).  Hence, for \(M_m=\max_{j\leq m}\varepsilon_j\),
\[
 \mathbb P(M_m\geq t)=1-\Phi(t)^m
 \geq1-\exp\{-m(1-\Phi(t))\}.                                           \tag{18}
\]
Taking \(t=\sqrt{\log m}\), using \(M_m\geq\varepsilon_1\) to control the negative part, and decreasing the constant for finitely many small \(m\), equations (17)--(18) yield
\[
 w_n(P)\geq c\sqrt{\log(2+K_n)}.                                        \tag{19}
\]
For the converse inequality, the Gaussian moment-generating function gives, for every \(s>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E\max_{K<K_n}|Z_{P,K}|
 \leq\frac{\log\{2(K_n-1)\}}s+\frac s2.
\]
Choosing \(s=\sqrt{2\log\{2(K_n-1)\}}\) proves the upper half of (19).  Equations (1)--(19) establish the covariance formula, diagonal variance, covariance-separated packing, semimetric separation, and Gaussian-width bounds.
