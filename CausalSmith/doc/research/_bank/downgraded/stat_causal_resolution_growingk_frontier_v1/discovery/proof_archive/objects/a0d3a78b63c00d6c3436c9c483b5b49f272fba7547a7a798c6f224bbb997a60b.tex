Write \(P=P_{U,\nu_n}^{(n)}\) and fix the row \(n\).  The witness construction gives
\[
 e_P(u)=\tfrac12,\qquad
 \mu_{1,P}(u)=u-\tfrac12,\qquad
 \mu_{0,P}(u)=\tfrac12-u .
\]
Indeed, treatment is independent of \(U\), each Rademacher mark has mean zero, and \(Y=Y(A)\).  Thus consistency holds by construction, conditional exchangeability follows from the independence of \(A,U,\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1\), and overlap follows from \(\epsilon<1/2=e_P(u)<1-\epsilon\).  Moreover,
\[
 b_P(u)=\{\mu_{1,P}(u)-\mu_{0,P}(u)+1\}/2=u,
 \qquad |\mu_{1,P}(u)-\mu_{0,P}(u)|=|2u-1|\leq1.
\]
Since \(0\leq u\leq1\) and \(0\leq\nu_n\leq1/4\), the potential outcomes and \(Y\) lie in \([-3/4,3/4]\subset[-1,1]\).  This proves the causal and support assertions from \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\), \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\), and \(\texttt{ass:bounded-feature}\).

We solve the quantization problem explicitly.  Any ordered scalar codebook induces at most \(K\) interval cells.  Let their lengths, including zeros for empty cells, be \(p_1,\ldots,p_K\), with \(p_j\geq0\) and \(\sum_jp_j=1\).  For an interval of length \(p\), completion of the square gives
\[
 \inf_c\int_a^{a+p}(u-c)^2\,du
 =\int_a^{a+p}\left(u-a-\frac p2\right)^2du
 =\frac{p^3}{12},
\]
with a unique minimizing center \(a+p/2\) when \(p>0\).  Hence strict convexity and Jensen's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\min_{c\in C}(U-c)^2
 \geq \frac1{12}\sum_{j=1}^Kp_j^3
 \geq\frac1{12K^2}.
\]
Equality requires \(p_j=1/K\) for every \(j\), then requires every center to be its cell midpoint.  Thus
\[
 c_{K,j}=\frac{2j-1}{2K},\quad
 \mathcal B_{P,K}=\left\{\frac jK:1\leq j<K\right\},\quad
 W_P(K)=\frac1{12K^2}.
\]
The centers are uniquely strictly ordered and interior.  Also \(W_P(1)=1/12\), so the denominator condition holds for every fixed \(\underline w\leq1/12\).

We verify local quadratic growth rather than presuming it.  Near this codebook, differentiation under the cell integrals is legitimate because adjacent losses agree at each moving Voronoi boundary.  At the optimum, for \(K\geq2\), the Hessian is
\[
 H_K=\frac1{2K}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&-1&0&\cdots&0\\
 -1&2&-1&\ddots&\vdots\\
 0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
 \vdots&\ddots&-1&2&-1\\
 0&\cdots&0&-1&3
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(x\in\mathbb R^K\),
\[
 2Kx^{\mathsf T}H_Kx
 =\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2+2x_1^2+2x_K^2,
\]
which is positive unless \(x=0\); for \(K=1\) the second derivative is \(2\).  Hessian continuity supplies positive \(\kappa_{P,K}\) and \(\lambda_{P,K}\) on which excess risk is at least \(\lambda_{P,K}\|C-C_{P,K}\|_2^2\).  For two ordered vectors the rearrangement inequality makes the identity permutation minimize permutation distance.  Shrinking \(\kappa_{P,K}\), if needed, proves \(\texttt{ass:quadratic-growth}\).

The maximum cell radius is \(s_{P,K}=1/(2K)\).  Take the preceding radius no larger than one.  For \(0<t\leq\kappa_{P,K}\), the intervals of radius \(t/(2K)\) about the \(K-1\) boundaries are disjoint up to endpoints, whence
\[
 P\{\operatorname{dist}(U,\mathcal B_{P,K})\leq ts_{P,K}\}
 =(K-1)t/K\leq t.
\]
Thus the margin condition holds with \(\alpha_{P,K}=1\) and \(M_{P,K}=1\).

With the declared periodic Bernoulli polynomials, outside null cell boundaries,

 U-q_{P,a}(U)=a^{-1}B_1(aU),\qquad

Substitution in the influence functions gives

 \Delta\phi_{P,K}
 =12\sum_aw_{K,2}(a)B_2(aU)
 +24\zeta_P\sum_aw_{K,1}(a)B_1(aU),

where each sum is over \(\{1,K,K+1\}\).  Direct integration, detailed in the covariance theorem, gives

 \operatorname{Var}_P(\Delta\phi_{P,K})\geq

 N_K=K^8+4K^7+10K^6+16K^5+5K^4-12K^3-14K^2-6K-1.
