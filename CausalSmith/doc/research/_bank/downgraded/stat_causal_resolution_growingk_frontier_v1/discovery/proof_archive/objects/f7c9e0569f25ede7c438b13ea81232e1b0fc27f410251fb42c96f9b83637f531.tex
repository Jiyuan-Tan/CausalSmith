Write \(P_0=P_{U,0}^{(n)}\), \(m=|J_n|\), and abbreviate row-law subscripts when no ambiguity can result.  We first verify that the assumed frontier condition supplies the growth hypotheses of the established membership and linearization theorems.  Direct integration over the \(K\) equal cells of the uniform quantizer gives
\[
 W_{P_0}(K)=\frac{1}{12K^2},\qquad s_{P_0,K}=\frac{1}{2K}.
 \tag{1}
\]
At the equal-cell codebook, differentiation of the distortion is legitimate because the two adjacent squared losses agree at every moving Voronoi boundary.  For \(K\geq2\), its Hessian is
\[
 H_K=\frac{1}{2K}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&-1&0&\cdots&0\\
 -1&2&-1&\ddots&\vdots\\
 0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
 \vdots&\ddots&-1&2&-1\\
 0&\cdots&0&-1&3
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 2Kx^{\mathsf T}H_Kx
 =\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2+2x_1^2+2x_K^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
Since \(x_j=x_1+\sum_{a<j}(x_{a+1}-x_a)\), Cauchy--Schwarz implies
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^Kx_j^2
 \leq 2Kx_1^2+2K^2\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}(x_{a+1}-x_a)^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4), together with Hessian continuity on a ball of radius proportional to the center spacing, permit the admissible choices
\[
 \lambda_{P_0,K}\asymp K^{-3},\qquad
 \kappa_{P_0,K}\asymp K^{-1},\qquad
 \alpha_{P_0,K}=1,\qquad M_{P_0,K}=1.
 \tag{5}
\]
Here and below \(\lambda_{P_0,K}\) denotes this valid, possibly nonmaximal, quadratic-growth modulus; decreasing a valid modulus preserves the quadratic-growth inequality.  The margin assertion in (5) follows equation by equation from
\[
 P_0\!\left[\operatorname{dist}\{U,\mathcal B_{P_0,K}\}\leq t s_{P_0,K}\right]
 =(K-1)t/K\leq t,\qquad 0<t\leq1.
 \tag{6}
\]
The exact covariance theorem gives
\[
 \sigma_{P_0,K}^2=V_K^{(0)}\asymp K^{-4},\qquad
 w_n(P_0)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The first comparison in (7) follows directly by dividing the degree-eight numerator of \(V_K^{(0)}\) by its degree-twelve denominator; positivity is supplied by that theorem.

Because \(r_n=0\) in the stated experiment, (1), (5), and the definition of \(\delta^C\) yield
\[
 \delta^C_{P_0,K,n}\asymp K^2L_n.
 \tag{8}
\]
Take \(K^*=\lfloor K_n/2\rfloor\), which belongs to \(J_n\) eventually because \(K_n\to\infty\).  If the validity case in the definition of \(Q^C_{P_0,K^*,n}\) fails, then \(Q^C_{P_0,K^*,n}=1\).  The nonnegative \(Q^C\)-term in \(S^G\), (7), and the two Gaussian-width factors in \(\Xi_n\) then imply a lower bound of order \((K^*)^2\log K_n\), contradicting \(\Xi_n(P_0)\to0\).  In the validity case, (5), (6), and (8) give
\[
 \{Q^C_{P_0,K^*,n}\}^2
 \geq16M_{P_0,K^*}s_{P_0,K^*}^2
 \frac{\delta^C_{P_0,K^*,n}}{s_{P_0,K^*}}
 \geq cK^*L_n.
 \tag{9}
\]
All terms in \(S^G\) and \(\Xi_n\) are nonnegative.  Hence (7)--(9) imply
\[
 \Xi_n(P_0)
 \geq c(1+w_n(P_0))w_n(P_0)
 \frac{Q^C_{P_0,K^*,n}}{\sigma_{P_0,K^*}}
 \geq cK_n^{5/2}\log K_n\,L_n^{1/2}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently the assumed convergence of \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)\) implies
\[
 K_n^5\log^2K_n\,L_n\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 K_n^5L_n\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \delta_nK_n^5\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{11}
\]
where the last implication uses
\[
 \delta_n=c_{\mathrm F}\sqrt{\frac{\log|J_n|}{n}}
 \leq c_{\mathrm F}L_n.
\]
Thus the hypotheses of \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) and \(\texttt{thm:uniform-linearization}\) hold.  In particular, every law in \(\mathcal U_n\) is in \(\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{CQ}}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), every coordinate variance is positive, and all divisions by \(W_P(1)\), \(\sigma_{P,K}\), and eventually \(\widehat\sigma_K\) below are valid.

For completeness, we also give a primitive sufficient regime for the abstract condition on \(\Xi_n\).  The density and covariance stability conclusions of \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\), the bound \(\|f_{n,j}-1\|_\infty=o(1)\), and comparison with the equal-cell codebook imply, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal U_n\) and \(K\leq K_n\),
\[
 W_P(K)\asymp K^{-2},\quad
 \lambda_{P,K}\asymp K^{-3},\quad
 s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1},\quad
 \sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2},\quad
 w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The first comparison follows because \(1/2\leq f_{n,j}\leq3/2\) eventually: for every codebook its distortion under \(P_j\) lies between one half and three halves of its uniform-law distortion.  In the noiseless experiment \(\zeta_P=0\).  Since an optimized loss is bounded by \(s_{P,K}^2\), (12) and the definitions of \(\phi_W\), \(\phi_\rho\), and \(\Delta\phi\) give
\[
 \|\Delta\phi_{P,K}\|_\infty\leq CK^{-2},\qquad
 \mathfrak E_n(P)\leq C.
 \tag{13}
\]
When the conditions displayed below hold, (11) follows, so the validity branch of \(Q^C\) applies uniformly.  Substitution into the definitions then gives
\[
 \delta^C_{P,K,n}\leq CK^2L_n,\qquad
 Q^C_{P,K,n}\leq C(KL_n)^{1/2},
 \tag{14}
\]
because \((\delta^C)^2+s\delta^C\leq C(K^4L_n^2+KL_n)\leq CKL_n\).  Similarly, since \(r_n=0\),
\[
 R_{P,K,n}\leq CKL_n^2,\quad
 E_{P,K,n}\leq C(L_n+KL_n^2),\quad
 R^G_{P,K,n}\leq CKL_n^2,\quad
 S^G_{P,K,n}\leq C(KL_n)^{1/2}.
 \tag{15}
\]
For example, the product term in \(R^G\) is bounded by
\[
 C(L_n+KL_n^2)L_n\leq C(L_n^2+KL_n^3)\leq CKL_n^2,
\]
and the remaining terms obey the same or a smaller bound by (12).  Equations (12)--(15) prove that the following three conditions are sufficient for \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)\to0\):
\[
 \frac{\log^7(nK_n)}{n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac{K_n^3\sqrt{\log K_n}\log(nK_n)}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 K_n^{5/2}\log K_n
 \left\{\frac{\log(nK_n)}{n}\right\}^{1/4}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{16}
\]
This regime is nonvacuous.  If \(r_n=0\) and \(K_n=\lfloor n^\kappa\rfloor\) with \(0<\kappa<1/10\), the second and third expressions in (16) are respectively
\[
 O\!\left(n^{3\kappa-1/2}\log^{3/2}n\right)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 O\!\left(n^{5\kappa/2-1/4}\log^{5/4}n\right),
\]
and both converge to zero; the first expression also converges to zero.  Thus every such polynomial growth sequence satisfies the sufficient regime.

We next prove the upper conclusion.  Define the oracle maximum
\[
 T_n^0=
 \max_{1\leq K<K_n}\left|
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O_{n,i})}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right|.
 \tag{17}
\]
The summands in (17) are independent by the row sampling model, centered by the influence-function definitions, have unit coordinate variances by the definition of \(\sigma_{P,K}\) and score nondegeneracy, and have standardized sub-exponential envelope \(\mathfrak E_n(P)\).  The first term of \(\Xi_n(P)\) is exactly the sufficient scale in \(\texttt{lem:high-dimensional-gaussian-multiplier-approximation}\).  That lemma therefore approximates the law of \(T_n^0\) by the law of
\[
 M_P=\max_{1\leq K<K_n}|Z_{P,K}|
\]
and gives the corresponding conditional multiplier approximation for the oracle empirically centered scores, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal U_n\).

By \(\texttt{thm:uniform-linearization}\), relative variance consistency, and the remaining nonnegative terms in \(\Xi_n\),
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat G_K-G_P(K)\}}{\widehat\sigma_K}
 -\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O_{n,i})}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right|
 =o_P\!\left(\frac1{1+w_n(P)}\right).
 \tag{18}
\]
The same bound holds conditionally for replacing the oracle multiplier scores by \(\widehat{\Delta\phi}_{i,K}/\widehat\sigma_K\).  The anti-concentration lemma, applied with \(p=K_n-1\), shows that a perturbation of the size in (18) changes the distribution function of the Gaussian maximum by \(o(1)\), because (12) gives \(w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  Combining the two oracle approximations with (18) yields
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}\left|
 P^n\!\left{
 \max_{K<K_n}\frac{\sqrt n|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)|}{\widehat\sigma_K}
 \leq t\right}
 -P\{T_n^{\#}\leq t\mid O_{n,1},\ldots,O_{n,n}\}
 \right|=o_P(1)
 \tag{19}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal U_n\).  By the quantile-infimum definition of \(q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\), (19) gives simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\).  This is precisely \(\operatorname{Honest}_\alpha(B_n^{\#},\mathcal U_n)\).

We now locate the critical value.  A Gaussian union bound gives every fixed upper quantile of \(M_P\) the bound \(C_\alpha\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  For the reverse bound, the covariance stability in \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) and the \(0.28\) separation in \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\) imply, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal U_n\),
\[
 R_P(K,L)\leq0.3,\qquad K\ne L,\quad K,L\in J_n,
 \tag{20}
\]
eventually.  We record the finite-dimensional comparison needed here.  Let \(X=(Z_{P,K}:K\in J_n)\), let \(\varepsilon_0,(\varepsilon_K:K\in J_n)\) be independent standard Gaussians, and put
\[
 Y_K=\sqrt{0.7}\,\varepsilon_K+\sqrt{0.3}\,\varepsilon_0.
\]
For a smooth nonincreasing approximation \(f\) to \(\mathbf1\{x\leq t\}\), set \(F(x)=\prod_{K\in J_n}f(x_K)\).  Along the centered Gaussian covariance interpolation from \(X\) to \(Y\), differentiating the finite-dimensional Gaussian density and integrating twice by parts gives
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\mathbb EF(X_u)
 =\frac12\sum_{K,L\in J_n}
 \{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_K,Y_L)-\operatorname{Cov}(X_K,X_L)\}
 \mathbb E\{\partial_{KL}F(X_u)\}.
 \tag{21}
\]
The diagonal summands vanish because both variances equal one.  For \(K\ne L\), the covariance difference is nonnegative by (20), while
\[
 \partial_{KL}F(x)=f'(x_K)f'(x_L)
 \prod_{a\ne K,L}f(x_a)\geq0.
\]
Integrating (21) and passing monotonically to the lower-orthant indicator proves
\[
 P\!\left(\max_{K\in J_n}X_K\leq t\right)
 \leq
 P\!\left(\max_{K\in J_n}Y_K\leq t\right).
 \tag{22}
\]
Fix \(c>0\) with \(c^2<1.4\) and take \(t=c\sqrt{\log m}\).  Conditional on \(|\varepsilon_0|\leq1\), independence and the elementary Gaussian tail bound obtained by one integration by parts give
\[
P\!\left(\max_KY_K\leq t\mid\varepsilon_0\right)
\leq
\Phi\!\left(\frac{t+\sqrt{0.3}}{\sqrt{0.7}}\right)^m
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, writing \(\varphi\) for the standard Gaussian density, integration by parts yields
\[
 1-\Phi(x)\geq\frac{x}{1+x^2}\varphi(x),\qquad x>0.
\]
Consequently \(m\{1-\Phi((t+\sqrt{0.3})/\sqrt{0.7})\}\to\infty\) because \(c^2/1.4<1\), and \(\Phi(x)^m\leq\exp[-m\{1-\Phi(x)\}]\) proves (23).  Since \(P(|\varepsilon_0|>1)<1/2<1-\alpha\), and since \(\{M_P\leq t\}\subseteq\{\max_{K\in J_n}X_K\leq t\}\), (22)--(23) show that the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(M_P\) is at least \(c_\alpha\sqrt{\log m}\).  As \(m=K_n-8\) eventually, this lower bound and the union upper bound are both of order \(\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  They also show directly, together with the same upper bound on the expectation, that \(w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  The conditional approximation in (19) and Gaussian anti-concentration transfer these two quantile bounds to
\[
 q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\asymp_P w_n(P).
 \tag{24}
\]
Finally, \(\texttt{thm:uniform-linearization}\) gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}-1\right|=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 P\!\left(\min_{K<K_n}\widehat\sigma_K>0\right)\longrightarrow1
 \tag{25}
\]
uniformly.  On the event in (25), (24) proves the asserted standardized half-width identity and order.

It remains to prove necessity.  For \(j\in J_n\), let
\[
 \theta_j=\{G_{P_j}(K):K\in J_n\},\qquad
 d_\infty(\theta_j,\theta_\ell)
 =\max_{K\in J_n}
 \frac{|G_{P_j}(K)-G_{P_\ell}(K)|}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}.
\]
The separation and divergence conclusions of \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) give
\[
 d_\infty(\theta_j,\theta_\ell)\geq a_0\delta_n
 \quad(j\ne\ell),\qquad
 \frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}
 \operatorname{KL}\{P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n}\}
 \leq Cc_{\mathrm F}^2\log m.
 \tag{26}
\]
Let the index \(J\) be uniform on \(J_n\), draw the sample from \(P_J^{\otimes n}\), and permit a decoder to use the declared \(V_n\).  Since \(V_n\) is independent of the index and sample under every law, adjoining it does not change any Kullback--Leibler divergence.  If \(Q_j\) is the joint sample-and-randomization law and \(\overline Q=m^{-1}\sum_jQ_j\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O^n,V_n)
 =\frac1m\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 \leq\frac1m\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q_0)
 \leq Cc_{\mathrm F}^2\log m.
 \tag{27}
\]
For every decoder \(\widehat J\), if \(p_e=P(\widehat J\ne J)\), the entropy chain rule and the fact that, conditional on an error, at most \(m-1\) indices remain possible imply
\[
 \log m=H(J)
 \leq I(J;O^n,V_n)+\log2+p_e\log m.
 \tag{28}
\]
Thus
\[
 \inf_{\widehat J}\frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}Q_j(\widehat J\ne j)
 \geq1-Cc_{\mathrm F}^2-\frac{\log2}{\log m}.
 \tag{29}
\]
Choose the fixed \(c_{\mathrm F}\) sufficiently small that the right-hand side is eventually greater than \((1+\alpha)/2\), as permitted by its definition.

Now let \(B_n\) be any common measurable rectangular band satisfying the stated honesty condition.  Define \(\widehat J\) to be the smallest \(j\in J_n\) for which \(\theta_j\in B_n\), using a fixed default index when no such \(j\) exists.  If \(B_n\) covers \(\theta_j\) and
\[
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{h_{n,K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}
 <\frac{a_0\delta_n}{2},
 \tag{30}
\]
then the rectangle cannot contain any \(\theta_\ell\) with \(\ell\ne j\): otherwise every coordinate difference would be at most \(2h_{n,K}\), contradicting (26).  Therefore, under \(Q_j\), a decoding error is contained in the union of the noncoverage event and the complement of (30).  Uniform honesty and (29) yield
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}
 P_j^{\otimes n}\!\left\{
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{h_{n,K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}
 \geq\frac{a_0\delta_n}{2}\right\}
 \geq \frac{1-\alpha}{2}-o(1).
 \tag{31}
\]
Hence some \(j\in J_n\) has the probability in (31) bounded below by a constant \(c_1>0\).  The uniform variance comparison in \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) replaces \(\sigma_{P_0,K}\) by \(\sigma_{P_j,K}\) at a \(1+o(1)\) factor.  Since
\[
 \sqrt n\,\delta_n=c_{\mathrm F}\sqrt{\log m},
\]
this is exactly the claimed lower bound for suitable \(c_0,c_1>0\).  Combining it with (24)--(25) and \(m\asymp K_n\) proves that the attainable and necessary radii on the specified noiseless experiment are both of order
\[
 \frac{w_n(P)}{\sqrt n}\asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log K_n}{n}}.
\]
