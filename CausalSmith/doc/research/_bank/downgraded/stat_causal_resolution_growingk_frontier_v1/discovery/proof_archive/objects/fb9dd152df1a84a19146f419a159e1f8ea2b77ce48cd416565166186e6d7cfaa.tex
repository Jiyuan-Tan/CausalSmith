Put \(P_\nu=P_{U,\nu_n}^{(n)}\).  Direct substitution of \(e=1/2\), \(\mu_1(u)=u-1/2\), and \(\mu_0(u)=1/2-u\) into the definitions of \(\zeta\), \(\phi_W\), \(\phi_\rho\), and \(\Delta\phi\) gives
\[
 \Delta\phi_{P_\nu,K}(O)
 =12F_K^{\mathrm g}(U)+24\zeta_{P_\nu}(O)F_{K,1}(U),
 \qquad
 F_{K,1}(u):=\sum_{a\in\{1,K,K+1\}}w_{K,1}(a)B_1(au).                 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, on the \(K\)-cell containing \(u\), \(u-q_K(u)=K^{-1}B_1(Ku)\), while \(\ell_K(u)-W(K)=K^{-2}B_2(Ku)\); subtracting the \(K\) and \(K+1\) profile formulas and using \(W(K)/W(1)=K^{-2}\) produces exactly the three weights in (1).  Under \(P_\nu\), conditional centering gives \(E(\zeta_{P_\nu}\mid U)=0\) and the bounded Rademacher construction gives \(|\zeta_{P_\nu}|=\nu_n\).  Hence the two terms in (1) are uncorrelated.

The Bernoulli covariance identities encoded in \(H_p\), together with \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\), yield
\[
 E_{P_\nu}F_K^{\mathrm g}=0,qquad
 144E_{P_\nu}\{F_K^{\mathrm g}\}^2=V_K^{(0)},qquad
 576E_{P_\nu}\{F_{K,1}\}^2=A_K.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Because \(|B_2|\leq1/6\), \(g_K\leq2K^{-3}\), and the absolute sum of the three \(p=2\) weights is at most \(3K^{-2}\), the definition of \(\gamma_K^{\mathrm g}\) and the explicit positive formula for \(V_K^{(0)}\) imply
\[
 E\gamma_K^{\mathrm g}=0,qquad E(\gamma_K^{\mathrm g})^2=1,qquad
 \sup_{K\geq1}\|\gamma_K^{\mathrm g}\|_\infty<\infty.                \tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) also give
\[
 \Sigma_{P_\nu}(K,L)=144H_2(K,L)+576\nu_n^2H_1(K,L),qquad
 \sigma_{P_\nu,K}^2=V_K^{(0)}+\nu_n^2A_K.                              \tag{4}
\]

The likelihood in \(\texttt{def:positive-noise-geometric-fano-family}\) is finite, normalized, and strictly positive by (3).  Since it is a function of \(U=X\) only, it cancels from every conditional distribution given \(U\).  Thus it preserves \(e=1/2\), both conditional outcome laws, \(\mu_1\), \(\mu_0\), \(b(u)=u\), bounded outcome support, consistency, and conditional exchangeability.  The zero-error estimators of the three base nuisances verify the nuisance-rate condition for every declared \(r_n\).

At \(\nu_n=0\), equation (1) says that \(\gamma_j^{\mathrm g}\) is exactly the standardized adjacent score used by \(\texttt{def:fano-family}\).  The geometric likelihood therefore induces the same marginal density of \(U\) as the law treated in \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\).  Optimized distortions and all codebook conditions depend only on this feature law.  Under \(\delta_nK_n^3\to0\), that theorem consequently gives, uniformly in \(j\in J_n\) and \(K\leq K_n\),
\[
 \|C_{P_{j,\mathrm g},K}-C_{P_\nu,K}\|_2\leq C\delta_nK^{3/2},\quad
 \lambda_{P_{j,\mathrm g},K}\geq cK^{-3},\quad
 s_{P_{j,\mathrm g},K}\asymp K^{-1},                                  \tag{5}
\]
with \(\kappa=c/K\), margin exponent one, bounded margin coefficient, unique interior centers, and \(W_{P_{j,\mathrm g}}(1)\geq1/12\).  Thus every required class condition except score stability already follows.

We next verify the asserted arithmetic separation.  Expanding \(H_2(K,K)\) and using \(\gcd(K,K+1)=1\) gives
\[
 144H_2(K,K)=V_K^{(0)},qquad \Gamma_2(K,K)=1.                           \tag{6}
\]
For \(8\leq K<L\), clearing the positive diagonal denominators in the explicit finite sum for \(H_2\) gives
\[
 H_2(K,K)\geq\frac1{109K^4}.                                             \tag{7}
\]
The summands in which at least one frequency equals one, after division by the square root of the two diagonal bounds in (7), are at most
\[
 \frac{109}{180}\left\{\frac4{KL}
 +\frac{2L^2}{K(L+1)^4}+\frac{2K^2}{L(K+1)^4}\right\}\leq\frac1{25}.    \tag{8}
\]
For the remaining four frequency pairs, every greatest common divisor is a proper divisor of the smaller frequency unless one of \(K\mid L\), \(K\mid L+1\), \(K+1\mid L\), or \(K+1\mid L+1\) holds.  In the proper-divisor case, substituting \(\gcd(a,b)\leq a/2\) into the four displayed summands of \(H_2\), and then using (7), bounds their standardized positive part by \(109/1440<4/25\).  In a divisibility case write the larger frequency as \(q\) times the smaller.  If \(q\geq3\), the same substitution gives
\[
 \frac{109}{180q^2}+\frac{109(K+2)^2}{180(K+1)^4}<\frac4{25};             \tag{9}
\]
if \(q=2\), coprimality of consecutive integers makes the companion greatest common divisor at most two, and inserting these two values in the same four-term sum also gives \(4/25\).  The four symmetric divisibility cases are identical after interchanging the two frequencies.  Negative summands can only decrease the covariance.  Equations (8)--(9) therefore prove
\[
 \sup_{K\ne L;\,K,L\geq8}\Gamma_2(K,L)\leq\frac15.                    \tag{10}
\]

The explicit rational functions in (4) satisfy \(V_K^{(0)}\asymp K^{-4}\) and \(A_K\asymp K^{-2}\), with positive comparison constants for all \(K\geq1\).  If \(\nu_nK_n\leq\bar\nu\), then for every \(K<K_n\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{V_K^{(0)}}}{\sigma_{P_\nu,K}}
 =\left\{1+\frac{\nu_n^2A_K}{V_K^{(0)}}\right\}^{-1/2}
 \geq c_{\bar\nu}>0.                                                     \tag{11}
\]
This is the nonvanishing geometric variance fraction.

It remains to control scores after reoptimization.  Along the geometric density path \(P_{t,j}\), \(0\leq t\leq\delta_n\), the cellwise first-order equations used in \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) give \(\|\partial_tC_{P_{t,j},K}\|_2\leq CK^{3/2}\).  Each optimal cell has probability \(O(K^{-1})\), its squared-residual integral is \(O(K^{-3})\), and \(|\zeta|\leq\nu_n\).  Differentiating the finite cell integrals is legitimate because adjacent squared losses agree at each moving Voronoi boundary, and gives
\[
 \|\partial_t\phi_{W,P_{t,j},K}\|_2^2
 \leq C\{K^{-3}\|\partial_tC_{P_{t,j},K}\|_2^2
 +\nu_n^2K^{-1}\|\partial_tC_{P_{t,j},K}\|_2^2+1\}
 \leq C_{\bar\nu}.                                                       \tag{12}
\]
Using \(W_{P_{t,j}}(1)\geq1/12\), \(W_{P_{t,j}}(K)\leq CK^{-2}\), and the ratio definition of \(\phi_\rho\), (12) yields
\[
 \|\partial_t\Delta\phi_{P_{t,j},K}\|_2
 \leq C_{\bar\nu}\leq C_{\bar\nu}K^2\sigma_{P_\nu,K}.                \tag{13}
\]
Differentiating expectations also contributes the bounded likelihood score from (3).  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the derivatives of variances and covariances, followed by integration over \([0,\delta_n]\), now gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\sigma_{P_{j,\mathrm g},K}}{\sigma_{P_\nu,K}}-1\right|
 +\sup_{K,L<K_n}|R_{P_{j,\mathrm g}}(K,L)-R_{P_\nu}(K,L)|
 \leq C_{\bar\nu}\delta_nK_n^2=o(1).                                  \tag{14}
\]
Here \(\delta_nK_n^2=o(1)\) follows from the stated stronger condition \(\delta_nK_n^3\to0\).  Equation (14) proves score nondegeneracy and completes class membership.

The functional \(G_P(K)\) depends only on the feature law.  Hence the gain expansion from \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) applies to the geometric density path at every \(\nu_n\).  The identity
\[
 \sigma_{P_{U,0},K}R_{P_{U,0}}(K,j)
 =\frac{144H_2(K,j)}{\sqrt{V_j^{(0)}}}
 =\sqrt{V_K^{(0)}}\,\Gamma_2(K,j)                                      \tag{15}
\]
gives exactly
\[
 G_{P_{j,\mathrm g}}(K)-G_{P_\nu}(K)
 =-\delta_n\sqrt{V_K^{(0)}}\Gamma_2(K,j)
 +O_{\bar\nu}(\delta_n^2K_n^2\sigma_{P_\nu,K}).                       \tag{16}
\]
For labels \(j\ne\ell\), use coordinate \(K=j\).  By (10)--(11) and (16),
\[
 \frac{|G_{P_{j,\mathrm g}}(j)-G_{P_{\ell,\mathrm g}}(j)|}{\sigma_{P_\nu,j}}
 \geq \delta_nc_{\bar\nu}\{1-\Gamma_2(j,\ell)\}
 -C_{\bar\nu}\delta_n^2K_n^2
 \geq a_{\bar\nu}\delta_n                                             \tag{17}
\]
eventually, for a fixed \(a_{\bar\nu}>0\).

Finally, the second derivative of the log normalizer is the variance of the bounded score under an intermediate tilt and is bounded uniformly by (3).  Taylor's theorem therefore gives a one-observation divergence at most \(C\delta_n^2\).  Additivity under independent products yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{(P_{j,\mathrm g}^{(n)})^{\otimes n},P_\nu^{\otimes n}\}
 \leq Cn\delta_n^2.                                                       \tag{18}
\]
Equations (3)--(18) prove every assertion.  The restriction \(\nu_nK_n=O(1)\) is used only to keep the geometric variance fraction and the score comparison uniform; no positive-noise upper-band assertion is made.
