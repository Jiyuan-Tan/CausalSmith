Write \(P_0=P_{U,0}^{(n)}\), and fix \(j\in J_n\).  Direct substitution of the equal-cell quantizers from \(\texttt{prop:uniform-witness-membership}\) into the definitions of \(\phi_{W,P_0,K}\), \(\phi_{\rho,P_0,K}\), and \(\Delta\phi_{P_0,K}\) gives
\[
 \Delta\phi_{P_0,j}(U)=12F_{j,2}(U),\qquad
 F_{j,2}(u)=\sum_{a\in\{1,j,j+1\}}w_{j,2}(a)B_2(au).                                      \tag{1}
\]
The centering constants cancel in (1) because
\(W_{P_0}(j)-W_{P_0}(j+1)=g_jW_{P_0}(1)\), and the residual part is zero because the outcomes are deterministic when \(\nu_n=0\).  Since \(\|B_2\|_\infty\leq1/6\), \(g_j\leq2j^{-3}\), and \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\) gives
\[
 c j^{-4}\leq\sigma_{P_0,j}^2=V_j^{(0)}\leq Cj^{-4},                                      \tag{2}
\]
the standardized direction \(h_j=\Delta\phi_{P_0,j}/\sigma_{P_0,j}\) obeys
\[
 E_{P_0}h_j=0,\qquad E_{P_0}h_j^2=1,qquad \|h_j\|_\infty\leq C_h.                         \tag{3}
\]

For \(0\leq t\leq\delta_n\), let \(P_{t,j}\) have density
\[
 f_{t,j}(u)=\frac{e^{-t h_j(u)}}{Z_{t,j}},\qquad
 Z_{t,j}=\int_0^1e^{-t h_j(v)}\,dv                                                        \tag{4}
\]
relative to the \(U\)-marginal of \(P_0\), leaving the conditional law given \(U\) unchanged.  From (3),
\[
 e^{-C_ht}\leq Z_{t,j}\leq e^{C_ht},\qquad
 e^{-2C_ht}\leq f_{t,j}(u)\leq e^{2C_ht},\qquad
 \|f_{t,j}-1\|_\infty\leq Ct.                                                           \tag{5}
\]
Thus (4) is normalized, positive, and has common support.  Because its likelihood ratio is a function of \(U=X\) only, conditioning cancels that ratio and yields
\[
 e_{P_{t,j}}(u)=\tfrac12,\quad
 \mu_{1,P_{t,j}}(u)=u-\tfrac12,\quad
 \mu_{0,P_{t,j}}(u)=\tfrac12-u,\quad b_{P_{t,j}}(u)=u.                                   \tag{6}
\]
Consequently \({\rm ass:consistency}\), \({\rm ass:exchangeability}\), \({\rm ass:overlap}\), and \({\rm ass:bounded-feature}\) are preserved, the observed outcomes remain in \([-1,1]\), and the common functions in (6) give \({\rm ass:nuisance-estimability}\) with zero error.

We next establish optimizer membership without assuming it.  For an ordered codebook \(C\), let \(I_r(C)\) be its \(r\)-th Voronoi interval, let \(p_r\) be the interval length, let \(\bar u_r\) be its midpoint, and put \(e_r=c_r-\bar u_r\).  Completion of the square gives
\[
 Q_{0,K}(C):=\int_0^1\min_{c\in C}(u-c)^2du
 =\sum_{r=1}^K\left(\frac{p_r^3}{12}+p_re_r^2\right).                                    \tag{7}
\]
For the equal-cell codebook \(C_K^0\), \(Q_{0,K}(C_K^0)=1/(12K^2)\), and
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^Kp_r^3-K^{-2}
 =\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-K^{-1})^2(p_r+2K^{-1}).                                                \tag{8}
\]
If every \(p_r\geq(2K)^{-1}\), the center locations are cumulative affine functions of the cell lengths plus the errors \(e_r\).  Cauchy--Schwarz, (7), and (8) then imply
\[
 Q_{0,K}(C)-Q_{0,K}(C_K^0)\geq cK^{-3}\|C-C_K^0\|_2^2.                                 \tag{9}
\]
If some \(p_r<(2K)^{-1}\), (8) instead gives a gap at least \(1/(24K^3)\).  Hence, for every ordered \(C\),
\[
 Q_{0,K}(C)-Q_{0,K}(C_K^0)
 \geq cK^{-3}\min\{\|C-C_K^0\|_2^2,K^{-2}\}.                                         \tag{10}
\]
In particular, outside a sufficiently small Euclidean ball of radius \(c_0/K\), the gap is at least \(cK^{-5}\).

Let \(Q_{t,j,K}(C)=\int\min_{c\in C}(u-c)^2f_{t,j}(u)du\), and let \(C_{t,j,K}\) be any minimizer, which exists by compactness of \(\mathcal C_K\).  Equations (5) and optimality first give \(Q_{0,K}(C_{t,j,K})\leq C K^{-2}\), and then give the sharper comparison
\[
 Q_{0,K}(C_{t,j,K})-Q_{0,K}(C_K^0)leq CtK^{-2}.                                         \tag{11}
\]
Comparing (11) with the off-basin gap following (10) shows that every minimizer lies in the radius-\(c_0/K\) regular neighborhood whenever \(tK^3=o(1)\).  This is the claimed basin entry and is the only place where the third power is needed.

We verify curvature separately.  On that neighborhood every cell length, center spacing, and endpoint distance is between two fixed positive multiples of \(K^{-1}\).  At the uniform law the Hessian has quadratic form
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}H_{0,K}(C)x
 =2c_1x_1^2+2(1-c_K)x_K^2
 +\frac12\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}(c_{r+1}-c_r)(x_{r+1}-x_r)^2.                                  \tag{12}
\]
The discrete inequality
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^Kx_r^2\leq CK^2\left\{x_1^2+x_K^2+
 \sum_{r=1}^{K-1}(x_{r+1}-x_r)^2\right\}                                                \tag{13}
\]
therefore yields \(H_{0,K}(C)\succeq cK^{-3}I_K\).  The perturbed Hessian consists of cell-probability terms and boundary-density terms multiplied by adjacent center spacings.  By (5), bounded cell lengths, and the tridiagonal form,
\[
 \|H_{t,j,K}(C)-H_{0,K}(C)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq CtK^{-1}.                               \tag{14}
\]
Because \(tK^3=o(1)\) implies \(tK^2=o(1)\), (12)--(14) give
\[
 H_{t,j,K}(C)\succeq cK^{-3}I_K                                                        \tag{15}
\]
throughout a fixed smaller regular neighborhood.

We now verify cell spacing and the advertised derivative bound separately.  Write the first-order equations as
\[
 \Psi_r(C,t)=2\int_{I_r(C)}(c_r-u)f_{t,j}(u)du=0,qquad 1\leq r\leq K.                   \tag{16}
\]
Moving-boundary contributions cancel in the derivative of the total distortion because adjacent squared losses agree at each Voronoi boundary.  Moreover,
\[
 \dot f_{t,j}=-(h_j-E_{P_{t,j}}h_j)f_{t,j},qquad
 \max_r|\partial_t\Psi_r(C,t)|\leq CK^{-2},qquad
 \|\partial_t\Psi(C,t)\|_2\leq CK^{-3/2}.                                             \tag{17}
\]
The first bound in (17) uses one cell of length \(O(K^{-1})\), a residual of size \(O(K^{-1})\), and the bounded density score from (3)--(5).  The implicit-function identity and (15) now give, uniformly in \(j,t,K\),
\[
 \|\partial_t C_{t,j,K}\|_2
 \leq\|H_{t,j,K}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\partial_t\Psi(C_{t,j,K},t)\|_2
 \leq CK^{3/2}.                                                                         \tag{18}
\]
After integration,
\[
 \|C_{t,j,K}-C_K^0\|_2\leq CtK^{3/2}.                                                  \tag{19}
\]
Since \(tK_n^3=o(1)\), the right side of (19) is \(o(K^{-1})\), uniformly over \(K\leq K_n\).  Thus the centers remain strictly ordered and interior and the cell radii satisfy \(s_{P_{t,j},K}\asymp K^{-1}\).  Equations (15) and (19) give quadratic growth with \(\lambda_{P_{t,j},K}\geq cK^{-3}\) on a radius \(\kappa_{P_{t,j},K}=c/K\), and strict convexity there plus global basin entry gives uniqueness.

The density in (5) is at most two eventually.  The union of the radius \(t_0s_{P_{t,j},K}\) neighborhoods of the \(K-1\) boundaries has Lebesgue measure at most \(2(K-1)t_0s_{P_{t,j},K}\).  Therefore
\[
 P_{t,j}\!\left[\operatorname{dist}\{U,\mathcal B_{P_{t,j},K}\}
 \leq t_0s_{P_{t,j},K}\right]\leq Ct_0,qquad 0<t_0\leq c/K,                           \tag{20}
\]
so \({\rm ass:growing-margin}\) holds with exponent one and a universal coefficient.

For the denominator, \(W_{P_{t,j}}(1)=\operatorname{Var}_{P_{t,j}}(U)\).  Differentiating at zero and using (1) gives the exact expression
\[
 \left.\partial_tW_{P_{t,j}}(1)\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{g_j-j^{-4}+(j+1)^{-4}}{15\sigma_{P_0,j}}\geq\frac{c}{j}.                         \tag{21}
\]
The inequality follows from \(g_j=(2j+1)/\{j^2(j+1)^2\}\), (2), and direct clearing of the positive denominator for \(j\geq8\).  Two differentiations of the variance under the bounded exponential family (4) give \(\sup_{t\leq\delta_n}|\partial_t^2W_{P_{t,j}}(1)|\leq C\).  Since \(\delta_nj=o(1)\), (21) stays nonnegative along the path, and hence
\[
 W_{P_{t,j}}(1)\geq W_{P_0}(1)=1/12.                                                     \tag{22}
\]
Thus division by the profile denominator is valid and \({\rm ass:resolution-denominator}\) holds for every \(\underline w\leq1/12\).

It remains to prove the improved covariance and gain bounds.  On each cell, away from its measure-zero moving boundary,
\[
 \partial_t\ell_{P_{t,j},K}(u)=-2\{u-c_{t,j,K,r}\}\partial_tc_{t,j,K,r}.
\]
Every cell has probability \(O(K^{-1})\) and squared-residual integral \(O(K^{-3})\).  Consequently (18) gives
\[
 \|\partial_t\ell_{P_{t,j},K}\|_{L_2(P_{t,j})}^2
 \leq CK^{-3}\|\partial_tC_{t,j,K}\|_2^2\leq C.                                      \tag{23}
\]
The envelope identity gives \(|\partial_tW_{P_{t,j}}(K)|\leq CK^{-2}\).  Differentiating the displayed definitions of \(\phi_W\), \(\phi_\rho\), and \(\Delta\phi\), using \(\zeta=0\), (22), (23), and \(W_{P_{t,j}}(K)\leq CK^{-2}\), yields
\[
 \|\partial_t\Delta\phi_{P_{t,j},K}\|_{L_2(P_{t,j})}\leq C.                          \tag{24}
\]
By (2), the right side of (24) is at most \(CK^2\sigma_{P_0,K}\).  Differentiating \(P_{t,j}(\Delta\phi_{P_{t,j},K})^2\), using the bounded density score in (17), (24), and Cauchy--Schwarz, and then integrating in \(t\), gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\sigma_{P_{t,j},K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}-1\right|
 \leq CtK_n^2.                                                                          \tag{25}
\]
The right side is \(o(1)\), so every variance remains positive.  Applying the same calculation to pairs of normalized scores and using polarization gives
\[
 \sup_{K,L<K_n}|R_{P_{t,j}}(K,L)-R_{P_0}(K,L)|\leq CtK_n^2.                              \tag{26}
\]

For the gain, the path score is \(-(h_j-E_{P_{t,j}}h_j)\).  The influence-function identity gives
\[
 \partial_tG_{P_{t,j}}(K)
 =-E_{P_{t,j}}\!\left[\Delta\phi_{P_{t,j},K}
 \{h_j-E_{P_{t,j}}h_j\}\right].                                                        \tag{27}
\]
Another differentiation of (27), followed by (3), (17), (24), and Cauchy--Schwarz, shows
\[
 \sup_{t\leq\delta_n}|\partial_t^2G_{P_{t,j}}(K)|
 \leq C\leq CK^2\sigma_{P_0,K}.                                                        \tag{28}
\]
At zero, (27) and the definition of \(R_{P_0}\) give
\[
 \left.\partial_tG_{P_{t,j}}(K)\right|_{t=0}
 =-\sigma_{P_0,K}R_{P_0}(K,j).                                                          \tag{29}
\]
Taylor's theorem with (28), evaluated at \(t=\delta_n\), proves the asserted gain expansion with remainder \(O(\delta_n^2K_n^2\sigma_{P_0,K})\).

For \(j\ne\ell\), evaluate the difference between their two expansions at coordinate \(K=j\).  By \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\), \(R_{P_0}(j,j)=1\) and \(R_{P_0}(j,\ell)\leq0.28\).  Hence
\[
 \frac{|G_{P_j}(j)-G_{P_\ell}(j)|}{\sigma_{P_0,j}}
 \geq0.72\delta_n-C\delta_n^2K_n^2\geq0.6\delta_n                                    \tag{30}
\]
eventually, because \(\delta_nK_n^2=o(1)\).  This proves separation with any fixed \(a_0\leq0.6\).

Finally, the log normalizer in (4) has second derivative \(\operatorname{Var}_{P_{t,j}}(h_j)\leq C_h^2\).  Taylor's theorem and additivity of Kullback--Leibler divergence for independent products therefore give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j,P_0)\leq C\delta_n^2,qquad
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})\leq Cn\delta_n^2.                  \tag{31}
\]
Equations (5)--(31) prove every class and packing assertion.  The regime is nonvacuous at the directed near-sixth-root scale: if
\[
 K_n=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{1/6}}{(\log n)^a}\right\rfloor,qquad a>1/6,
\]
then \(|J_n|\asymp K_n\), \(\delta_n\asymp\sqrt{\log n/n}\), and
\[
 \delta_nK_n^3\asymp(\log n)^{1/2-3a}\longrightarrow0.                                \tag{32}
\]
No necessity of the third power is claimed.
