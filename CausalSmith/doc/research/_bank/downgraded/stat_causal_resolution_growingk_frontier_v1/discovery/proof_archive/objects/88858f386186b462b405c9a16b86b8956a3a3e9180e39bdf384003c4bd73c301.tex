Fix a law \(P=P_j^{(n)}\in\mathcal U_n\) with \(\nu_n=0\).  The density and optimizer bounds in \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) are uniform in \(j\), including \(j=0\).  In particular,
\[
 cK^{-2}\leq W_P(K)\leq CK^{-2},\quad
 \lambda_{P,K}\geq cK^{-3},\quad s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1},\quad
 \sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}. \tag{17}
\]

We first prove empirical basin entry.  For an ordered codebook \(C\), the function \(u\mapsto\ell_C(u)=\min_{c\in C}(u-c)^2\) is continuous, is absolutely continuous between finitely many boundaries, and has derivative bounded in absolute value by two.  Integration by parts therefore gives, for every empirical distribution function \(F_N\) and its population counterpart \(F_P\),
\[
 \sup_{C\in\mathcal C_K}|(P_N-P)\ell_C|
 \leq2\|F_N-F_P\|_\infty. \tag{18}
\]
For a fixed \(x\), the indicator variables \(\mathbf1\{U_i\leq x\}\) are bounded Bernoulli variables.  Choose deterministic population quantiles whose distribution-function values are the grid \(\{r/N:0\leq r\leq N\}\).  Monotonicity of both distribution functions bounds the supremum between adjacent grid points by the larger endpoint error plus \(1/N\).  Applying the exponential Markov inequality to the centered Bernoulli sums at the grid points and taking a union bound yields
\[
 \mathbb P_P\{\|F_N-F_P\|_\infty>t+N^{-1}\}\leq2(N+1)e^{-2Nt^2}. \tag{19}
\]
Every training size is at least \(c_{\mathrm{tr}}n\), and the number of folds is fixed.  Taking \(t=C L_n\) in (18)--(19) shows that their left side is \(O_P(L_n)\), simultaneously over all folds, laws, and \(K\leq K_n\).  The population proof leading to (6), transferred by the density bound (2), gives an off-basin population gap \(cK^{-5}\).  Since \(K_n^5L_n\to0\), (18)--(19) force every empirical minimizer into the population basin with probability tending to one uniformly.  This establishes global entry before local quadratic growth is invoked.

Inside that basin, put \(C_{P,K}=C^star\) and \(d(C)=\|C-C^star\|_2\).  For one observation, the directional derivative of the loss is \(2\{c_r-U\}v_r\) on cell \(r\).  The disjoint cells, (17), and \(|c_r-U|\leq C/K\) give
\[
 E_P\left[\left\{\left.\frac d{dt}\ell_{C^star+tv}(U)\right|_{t=0}\right\}^2\right]
 \leq CW_P(K)\|v\|_2^2. \tag{20}
\]
The centered moment-generating-function inequality for bounded variables, applied to the cellwise gradient vector and followed by a dyadic peeling over \(d(C)\), yields, with probability at least \(1-2e^{-x}\),
\[
 \sup_{d(C)\leq r}|(P_N-P)(\ell_C-\ell_{C^star})|
 \leq C\left\{r\sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)(x+\log(nK_n))}{n}}
 +\frac{r^2(x+\log(nK_n))}{n}\right\}. \tag{21}
\]
To see that no dimension factor is hidden in (21), square the cellwise gradient norm before taking expectation: by disjointness, the sum of its \(K\) coordinate variances is \(4E_P\{U-q_{P,K}(U)\}^2=4W_P(K)\).  The boundary-switching remainder is quadratic because the two adjacent squared losses agree at their common boundary, and is absorbed by the second term.  Thus (21) follows directly from the scalar exponential bound on each dyadic shell and a union bound over \(K\) and the fixed folds.

The population quadratic-growth inequality and empirical optimality imply
\[
 \lambda_{P,K}d(\widehat C_K)^2
 \leq |(P_N-P)(\ell_{\widehat C_K}-\ell_{C^star})|.
\]
Substitution of (21), absorption of the quadratic term using \(K_n^5L_n\to0\), and division by the positive \(\lambda_{P,K}\) give
\[
 d(\widehat C_K)
 =O_P\left[\lambda_{P,K}^{-1}
 \left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/2}\right]
 =O_P(\delta^C_{P,K,n}) \tag{22}
\]
simultaneously.  A second use of (21) gives the optimized-risk remainder
\[
 \left|\widehat W_K-W_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,K}\right|
 =O_P\left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}{n\lambda_{P,K}}\right\}
 =O_P(R_{P,K,n}), \tag{23}
\]
where the nuisance terms in \(R_{P,K,n}\) vanish because the common nuisances are used exactly.  Equation (23) is uniform over folds and resolutions.

Write \(\widehat W_a=W_P(a)+Z_a+e_a\), where \(Z_a=(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,a}\), \(|e_a|=O_P(R_{P,a,n})\), and \(|Z_a|=O_P(L_n)\) uniformly by boundedness and the same exponential argument.  Since \(W_P(1)\geq1/24\) and \(|Z_1+e_1|=o_P(1)\), the exact identity
\[
 \frac{x+u}{y+v}-\frac xy-\frac uy+\frac{xv}{y^2}
 =-\frac{uv}{y(y+v)}+\frac{xv^2}{y^2(y+v)} \tag{24}
\]
is valid with \(y=W_P(1)\).  Apply (24) first to \(W_P(K)/W_P(1)\) and then to \(W_P(K+1)/W_P(1)\).  The linear terms combine to \((P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}\), while the four displayed remainder types are exactly bounded by the four terms defining \(R^G_{P,K,n}\).  This proves the asserted gain expansion.

It remains to control fitted scores.  On the event (22), a point can change its nearest center only if it lies within \(2\delta^C_{P,K,n}\) of a population Voronoi boundary.  Off this switching set the residual-map discrepancy is at most \(\delta^C_{P,K,n}\); on it both residuals are at most \(2s_{P,K}\).  The margin inequality therefore gives
\[
 \|\{U-q_{\widehat C_K}(U)\}-\{U-q_{P,K}(U)\}\|_{L_2(P)}^2
 \leq (\delta^C_{P,K,n})^2
 +16M_{P,K}s_{P,K}^2
 \left(\frac{\delta^C_{P,K,n}}{s_{P,K}}\right)^{\alpha_{P,K}}, \tag{25}
\]
when the validity condition in the definition of \(Q^C_{P,K,n}\) holds; otherwise the trivial unit bound applies.  Thus (25) is bounded by \((Q^C_{P,K,n})^2\) in both cases.  Substitution into the ratio influence-function formula, followed by the triangle inequality, gives exactly the four codebook terms in the corrected \(S^G_{P,K,n}\); the nuisance terms are zero here.  Centering by \(\overline\psi_K\) adds no larger order, because its oracle part is controlled by the bounded-score empirical mean and its fitted-minus-oracle part by the same \(L_2\) bound.  This proves the score-discrepancy assertion.

By (1), (15), and (22), the oracle standardized scores are uniformly bounded on the local experiment.  Applying the bounded-variable exponential inequality to their squares gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}|P_{\mathrm{emp},n}s_{P,K}^2-1|=O_P\{\sqrt{\log(nK_n)/n}\}=o_P(1). \tag{26}
\]
The difference of two empirical second moments is bounded by the product of the empirical \(L_2\) norm of the score difference and the sum of the two empirical \(L_2\) norms.  Hence (25)--(26) and \(\Xi_n(P)\to0\) imply \(\max_K|\widehat\sigma_K/\sigma_{P,K}-1|=o_P(1)\).  Positivity of every \(\widehat\sigma_K\) follows with probability tending to one from (17).  Finally, the definition of \(\Xi_n\) multiplies the gain remainder and score discrepancy by the Gaussian-width and anti-concentration scales needed for the maximum.  Its convergence to zero therefore makes all three feasible-oracle replacements negligible on that scale, as claimed.
