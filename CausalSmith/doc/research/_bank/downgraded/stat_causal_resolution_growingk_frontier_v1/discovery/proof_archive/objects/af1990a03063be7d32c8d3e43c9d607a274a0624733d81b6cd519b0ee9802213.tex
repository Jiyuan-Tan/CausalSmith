Fix \(P\in\mathcal U_n\), put \(p_n=K_n-1\), and suppress row-law subscripts.  Instantiate the corrected estimator with its fixed balanced folds and the common known nuisances \(e(u)=1/2\), \(\mu_1(u)=u-1/2\), and \(\mu_0(u)=1/2-u\).  The membership theorem permits the moduli used in (31) of the uniform-linearization theorem.  The exact-covariance theorem gives \(w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}\), uniformly over the experiment.  Since every summand in \(\Xi_n(P)\) is nonnegative, its score-replacement term implies
\[
 K_n^{5/2}L_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and hence \(K_n^5L_n\to0\).  Moreover, \(\delta_n=c_{\mathrm F}\sqrt{\log|J_n|/n}\), \(|J_n|\asymp K_n\), and \(L_n=\sqrt{\log(nK_n)/n}\), so \(\delta_nK_n^3\leq C K_n^3L_n\to0\).  Thus every premise of the uniform-linearization theorem holds.  It follows that
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}-1\right|=o_P(1)  \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat G_K-G_P(K)\}}{\widehat\sigma_K}
 -\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O_{n,i})}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right|=o_P\!\left(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1}\right)                              \tag{2}
\]
uniformly in \(P\).  It also gives the corresponding empirically centered score-replacement bound for the multiplier array.  Score nondegeneracy and (1) imply \(P^n(\min_K\widehat\sigma_K>0)\to1\) uniformly, so all subsequent studentization is valid on an event whose probability tends to one.

We verify the hypotheses of the cited high-dimensional approximation.  Put
\[
 X_{iK}=\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O_{n,i})/\sigma_{P,K}.
\]
The row-sampling definition makes the vectors independent.  The influence-function definitions give \(E_PX_{iK}=0\), and the definition of \(\sigma_{P,K}\) gives \(E_PX_{iK}^2=1\).  In the noiseless experiment, the optimized-loss representation, \(s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1}\), \(W_P(K)\asymp K^{-2}\), and \(\sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}\) imply
\[
 \max_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\max_{K<K_n}\|X_{iK}\|_\infty\leq C_0.           \tag{3}
\]
Indeed each adjacent loss difference is bounded by \(C K^{-2}\), while the denominator contribution is \(|D_{P,K}|\,|\phi_{W,P,1}|/W_P(1)^2\leq CK^{-2}\); division by \(\sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}\) proves (3).  Choose \(B\geq\max\{1,C_0,C_0/\log2\}\).  Then
\[
 E_P|X_{iK}|^3\leq C_0E_PX_{iK}^2\leq B,\qquad
 E_P|X_{iK}|^4\leq C_0^2E_PX_{iK}^2\leq B^2,
 \qquad E_Pe^{|X_{iK}|/B}\leq e^{C_0/B}\leq2.                              \tag{4}
\]
Thus the exact moment and sub-exponential conditions defining \(\mathfrak E_n(P)\) hold with a common constant.  Apply the cited CCK lemma to the signed \(2p_n\)-coordinate array \((X_{iK},-X_{iK})\) and choose its approximation parameter \(\gamma_n=(2nK_n)^{-1}\).  This parameter vanishes and the corresponding error is
\[
 D_n=O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log^7(2nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/6}\right)=o(1),     \tag{5}
\]
by the first term of \(\Xi_n\).  The lemma therefore approximates, uniformly in distribution functions, both the oracle maximum
\[
 T_n^0=\max_{K<K_n}\left|n^{-1/2}\sum_iX_{iK}\right|                       \tag{6}
\]
and the oracle empirically centered Gaussian-multiplier maximum by
\[
 M_P=\max_{K<K_n}|Z_{P,K}|.                                                  \tag{7}
\]

Define \(\overline X_K=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{iK}\).  The score-replacement term in \(\Xi_n\), (1), and conditional Cauchy--Schwarz show that replacing \(X_{iK}-\overline X_K\) by \(\widehat{\Delta\phi}_{i,K}/\widehat\sigma_K\) changes the conditional multiplier maximum by \(o_P(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1})\).  The Gaussian anti-concentration lemma bounds the probability of an interval of this length by \(o(1)\), because \(w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  Combining this with (2) and (5)--(7) yields
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}\left|
 P^n\!\left\{\max_{K<K_n}\frac{\sqrt n|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)|}{\widehat\sigma_K}\leq t\right\}
 -P\{T_n^{\#}\leq t\mid O_{n,1},\ldots,O_{n,n}\}
 \right|=o_P(1)                                                              \tag{8}
\]
uniformly.  On \(\min_K\widehat\sigma_K>0\), the conditional multiplier law is continuous.  Hence the conditional quantile-infimum definition of \(q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\), (8), and bounded convergence give simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\), which is precisely \(\operatorname{Honest}_{\alpha}(B_n^{\#},\mathcal U_n)\).

We next determine the critical-value order.  Covariance stability in the membership theorem, \(\delta_nK_n^2=o(1)\), and the exact-covariance packing imply eventually
\[
 R_P(K,L)\leq0.3\quad(K\neq L,\ K,L\in J_n),
 \qquad w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}.                                        \tag{9}
\]
A Gaussian union bound places the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(M_P\) below \(C_\alpha\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  For the reverse bound, let \(X=(Z_{P,K}:K\in J_n)\) and define
\[
 Y_K=\sqrt{0.7}\,\varepsilon_K+\sqrt{0.3}\,\varepsilon_0,
\]
where the displayed Gaussian variables are independent and standard normal.  Marginal variances agree and \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_K,X_L)\leq\operatorname{Cov}(Y_K,Y_L)\) off the diagonal.  For a smooth nonincreasing approximation \(f\) to \(\mathbf1\{x\leq t\}\), put \(F(x)=\prod_Kf(x_K)\).  For \(0\leq u\leq1\), let \(X_u\) be centered Gaussian with covariance \((1-u)\operatorname{Cov}(X)+u\operatorname{Cov}(Y)\).  Add \(\varepsilon I\) to both endpoint covariance matrices, differentiate the resulting nonsingular Gaussian density along this interpolation, and integrate by parts twice.  This gives
\[
 \frac d{du}E F(X_u)=\frac12\sum_{K\neq L}
 \{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_K,Y_L)-\operatorname{Cov}(X_K,X_L)\}
 E\{\partial_{KL}F(X_u)\}\geq0,                                            \tag{10}
\]
because \(\partial_{KL}F=f'(x_K)f'(x_L)\prod_{a\neq K,L}f(x_a)\geq0\).  Dominated convergence as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), followed by monotone smoothing of the indicator, yields
\[
 P(\max_{K\in J_n}X_K\leq t)\leq P(\max_{K\in J_n}Y_K\leq t).             \tag{11}
\]
Let \(m=|J_n|\), let \(\Phi\) and \(\varphi\) denote the standard normal distribution and density functions, and take \(t=c\sqrt{\log m}\) with \(c^2<1.4\).  For \(x>0\), integration by parts gives
\[
 1-\Phi(x)\geq\frac{x}{1+x^2}\varphi(x).                                   \tag{12}
\]
On \(|\varepsilon_0|\leq1\), conditioning on \(\varepsilon_0\) gives
\[
 P(\max_KY_K\leq t\mid\varepsilon_0)
 \leq\Phi\!\left(\frac{t+\sqrt{0.3}}{\sqrt{0.7}}\right)^m\longrightarrow0, \tag{13}
\]
because (12) implies that \(m[1-\Phi\{(t+\sqrt{0.3})/\sqrt{0.7}\}]\to\infty\).  The complement \(P(|\varepsilon_0|>1)<1/2<1-\alpha\), so (11)--(13) place the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(M_P\) above \(c_\alpha\sqrt{\log m}\).  Since \(m=K_n-8\), this quantile has order \(\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  Equations (5), (8), and Gaussian anti-concentration transfer that order to \(q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\).  Combining with (1) proves
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 =q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\{1+o_P(1)\}\asymp_P w_n(P).                         \tag{14}
\]

It remains to prove necessity.  For \(j\in J_n\), put \(\theta_j=(G_{P_j}(K):K\in J_n)\) and
\[
 d_\infty(\theta_j,\theta_\ell)=
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{|G_{P_j}(K)-G_{P_\ell}(K)|}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}.
\]
The separation and divergence conclusions of the membership theorem give
\[
 d_\infty(\theta_j,\theta_\ell)\geq a_0\delta_n\quad(j\neq\ell),
 \qquad
 \frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cc_{\mathrm F}^2\log m.                                               \tag{15}
\]
Let \(J\) be uniform on \(J_n\), draw the sample from \(P_J^{\otimes n}\), and adjoin the declared independent \(V_n\).  This common independent factor leaves all Kullback--Leibler divergences unchanged.  Let \(Q_j=P_j^{\otimes n}\otimes\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), let \(Q_0=P_0^{\otimes n}\otimes\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), and put \(\overline Q=m^{-1}\sum_{j\in J_n}Q_j\).  The log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O^n,V_n)=m^{-1}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 \leq m^{-1}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q_0)
 \leq Cc_{\mathrm F}^2\log m.                                               \tag{16}
\]
For any decoder \(\widehat J\), the entropy chain rule and the fact that at most \(m-1\) labels remain after an error give, with \(p_e=P(\widehat J\neq J)\),
\[
 \log m=H(J)\leq I(J;O^n,V_n)+\log2+p_e\log m.                              \tag{17}
\]
Thus the average decoding error is at least \(1-Cc_{\mathrm F}^2-\log2/\log m\).  Choose the fixed \(c_{\mathrm F}\) sufficiently small that this is eventually larger than \((1+\alpha)/2\).

Given any honest common rectangle, decode by choosing the smallest \(j\) for which \(\theta_j\) lies in the rectangle, with a fixed default if none does.  If the rectangle covers \(\theta_j\) and
\[
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{h_{n,K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}<a_0\delta_n/2,              \tag{18}
\]
then (15) says it contains no other \(\theta_\ell\), so the decoder is correct.  Uniform honesty, (17), and (18) imply
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}P_j^{\otimes n}\!\left\{
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{h_{n,K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}
 \geq a_0\delta_n/2\right\}
 \geq(1-\alpha)/2-o(1).                                                      \tag{19}
\]
Some summand is bounded below by a fixed \(c_1>0\).  The uniform variance comparison in the membership theorem replaces \(\sigma_{P_0,K}\) by \(\sigma_{P_j,K}\) at a \(1+o(1)\) factor, and \(\sqrt n\delta_n=c_{\mathrm F}\sqrt{\log m}\).  This proves the stated lower bound with fixed \(c_0,c_1>0\).  Combining (14) and (19) proves the matched standardized radius \(w_n(P)/\sqrt n\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n/n}\) on the noiseless local experiment.  No step supplies, or claims, a positive-noise achievability theorem.
