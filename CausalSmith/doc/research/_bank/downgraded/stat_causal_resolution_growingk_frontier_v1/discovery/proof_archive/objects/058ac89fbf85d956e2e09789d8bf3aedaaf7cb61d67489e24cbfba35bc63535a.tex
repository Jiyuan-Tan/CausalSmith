Write \(P_0=P_{U,0}^{(n)}\), fix \(P=P_j^{(n)}\in\mathcal U_n\), and suppress the row superscript.  Because \(K_n=\lfloor n^\kappa\rfloor\), \(0<\kappa<1/10\), and \(|J_n|\leq K_n\),
\[
 \delta_nK_n^3\leq Cn^{3\kappa-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 K_n^5L_n\leq Cn^{5\kappa-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\), together with \(\texttt{prop:uniform-witness-membership}\) and the diagonal formula in \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\), gives uniformly in \(P\) and \(K\leq K_n\)
\[
 W_P(K)\asymp K^{-2},\quad \lambda_{P,K}\geq cK^{-3},\quad
 s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1},\quad \kappa_{P,K}\geq cK^{-1},\quad
 \alpha_{P,K}=1,\quad M_{P,K}\leq C,\quad \sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}.          \tag{2}
\]
The same theorem gives \(\|f_P-1\|_\infty\leq C\delta_n\) for the density of \(U\), and
\[
 \|C_{P,K}-C_{P_0,K}\|_2\leq C\delta_nK^{3/2}=o(K^{-1}).                       \tag{3}
\]
All divisions below are valid by \(W_P(1)\geq1/12\) and \(\sigma_{P,K}>0\).  Since the known nuisance functions are used and \(\nu_n=0\), \(\widehat\zeta_v=\zeta_P=0\), so both the codebook-fitting criterion and its evaluation value are ordinary squared quantization losses.

We first force the global empirical selector into the local basin.  For the uniform law, let \(C^0_K=((2r-1)/(2K):1\leq r\leq K)\), and let the Voronoi cells of an arbitrary ordered \(C\) have lengths \(p_1,\ldots,p_K\) and midpoints \(m_1,\ldots,m_K\), allowing zero lengths.  Completing the square cell by cell gives the exact decomposition
\[
 P_0\ell_C=\sum_{r=1}^K\left\{\frac{p_r^3}{12}+p_r(c_r-m_r)^2\right\}.          \tag{4}
\]
With \(a=K^{-1}\), \(\sum_rp_r=1\) implies
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^Kp_r^3-K^{-2}
 =\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-a)^2(p_r+2a)
 \geq\frac2K\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-a)^2.                                           \tag{5}
\]
Put \(\varepsilon(C)=P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\).  If \(\varepsilon(C)\leq c_1K^{-5}\), equations (4)--(5), with \(c_1\) small, imply \(p_r\geq(2K)^{-1}\),
\[
 \sum_r(p_r-a)^2\leq6K\varepsilon(C),
 \qquad
 \sum_r(c_r-m_r)^2\leq2K\varepsilon(C).                                      \tag{6}
\]
Moreover
\[
 |m_r-(2r-1)/(2K)|
 \leq\sum_{a<r}|p_a-K^{-1}|+  frac12|p_r-K^{-1}|
 \leq C K\sqrt{\varepsilon(C)}.                                                \tag{7}
\]
Summing (7) and using (6) yields
\[
 \|C-C_K^0\|_2\leq CK^{3/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon(C)}.                             \tag{8}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed sufficiently small \(c_2>0\),
\[
 \inf_{\|C-C_K^0\|_2\geq c_2/K}\{P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\}\geq c_3K^{-5} \tag{9}
\]
for a fixed \(c_3>0\); if the premise used in (6) fails, (9) is immediate after decreasing \(c_3\), and otherwise it follows by contraposition of (8).

For a codebook loss, \(u\mapsto\ell_C(u)\) is continuous, piecewise absolutely continuous, and has derivative bounded by two.  Integration by parts therefore gives, for an empirical distribution function \(F_N\),
\[
 \sup_{C\in\mathcal C_K}|(P_N-P_0)\ell_C|
 \leq2\|F_N-F_0\|_\infty.                                                       \tag{10}
\]
Choose the deterministic grid \(u_r=r/N\), \(0\leq r\leq N\).  Between adjacent grid points, monotonicity of both distribution functions bounds their difference by the larger endpoint difference plus \(N^{-1}\).  For a Bernoulli variable \(B\), convexity of the exponential on \([0,1]\), applied once at each endpoint, gives \(E\exp\{t(B-EB)\}\leq\exp(t^2/8)\).  Exponential Markov applied to the sum and optimization over \(t\) therefore give the two-sided endpoint bound \(2e^{-2Nx^2}\).  Hence a union bound gives
\[
 P_0^N\{\|F_N-F_0\|_\infty>x+N^{-1}\}\leq2(N+1)e^{-2Nx^2}.                  \tag{11}
\]
Every codebook-fitting block has \(N\geq c n\).  Taking \(x=c_3K_n^{-5}/8\), using (1), and applying (9)--(11) over the fixed folds and \(K\leq K_n\), every global empirical minimizer lies within \(c_2/K\) of \(C_K^0\), except on an event of \(P_0^N\)-probability at most
\[
 CnK_n\exp(-c nK_n^{-10})=o(n^{-A})                                             \tag{12}
\]
for every fixed \(A>0\).

This conclusion transfers uniformly to every tilted law in \(\mathcal U_n\).  Indeed, the noiseless standardized tilt direction is uniformly bounded: the optimized loss is at most \(CK^{-2}\), (2) gives \(\sigma_{P_0,K}\asymp K^{-2}\), and hence \(\|s_{n,j}\|_\infty\leq C\).  Since \(P_0s_{n,j}=0\), Taylor's formula for the bounded exponential gives
\[
 E_{P_0}\left(\frac{dP_j}{dP_0}\right)^2\leq e^{C\delta_n^2},
 \qquad
 E_{P_0^N}\left(\frac{dP_j^N}{dP_0^N}\right)^2\leq e^{CN\delta_n^2}leq K_n^C. \tag{13}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz, (12), and (13) show that the basin event has probability tending to one uniformly in \(j\).  Combining it with (3), and decreasing the fixed basin constant if necessary, places every selected empirical codebook inside the \(c/K\) quadratic-growth basin of \(C_{P,K}\).

We next derive its rate.  Put \(C^\star=C_{P,K}\), \(d(C)=\|C-C^\star\|_2\), and let \(g(u)\) be the gradient of \(\ell_C(u)\) at \(C=C^\star\).  Exactly one coordinate of \(g(u)\) is nonzero, and it equals \(2(c_r^\star-u)\) on cell \(r\).  Thus, for every \(v\in\mathbb R^K\),
\[
 E_P\{v^{\mathsf T}g(U)\}^2\leq C W_P(K)\|v\|_2^2,
 \qquad E_P\|g(U)\|_2^2=4W_P(K).                                                \tag{14}
\]
For independent Rademacher signs \(\varepsilon_i\), disjointness of the cell coordinates gives
\[
 E\left\|N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\varepsilon_i g(U_i)\right\|_2^2
 =N^{-1}E_P\|g(U)\|_2^2\leq CW_P(K)/N.                                        \tag{15}
\]
Introduce an independent ghost sample, apply Jensen's inequality to the difference of the two empirical processes, and condition on the pooled observations; this derives the usual symmetrization inequality directly.  Equation (15), followed by the conditional exponential bound for bounded Rademacher sums, controls the linear term.  If a center perturbation has norm at most \(r\), an assignment can switch only within distance \(2r\) of a population boundary; adjacent squared losses agree at the boundary.  The density bound and the margin bound in (2) therefore make the switching contribution quadratic.  Peeling the intervals \(2^{-a-1}r<d(C)\leq2^{-a}r\) and applying the preceding exponential bound at level \(x+\log(nK_n)+2a\) gives
\[
 \sup_{d(C)\leq r}|(P_N-P)(\ell_C-\ell_{C^\star})|
 \leq C\left[r\sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)\{x+\log(nK_n)\}}{n}}
 +\frac{r^2\{x+\log(nK_n)\}}n\right]                                        \tag{16}
\]
simultaneously over the fixed folds and \(K\leq K_n\), with probability at least \(1-Ce^{-x}\).  Every term in (16) has now been traced to (14), the bounded exponential calculation, or the stated margin condition; in particular, (15) shows that no factor \(K^{1/2}\) is hidden.

Population quadratic growth, empirical optimality, and (16) imply
\[
 \lambda_{P,K}d(\widehat C_{v,K})^2
 \leq C d(\widehat C_{v,K})
 \sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n}
 +C d(\widehat C_{v,K})^2\frac{\log(nK_n)}n.                                   \tag{17}
\]
By (2) and (1), the last coefficient is \(o(\lambda_{P,K})\), so it is absorbed into the left side.  Division by the positive \(\lambda_{P,K}\) gives
\[
 d(\widehat C_{v,K})
 =O_P\!\left[\lambda_{P,K}^{-1}
 \left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/2}\right]
 =O_P(\delta^C_{P,K,n})                                                         \tag{18}
\]
uniformly.  Because the evaluation fold is independent of its fitted codebook and nuisance fit, applying (16) once more on the evaluation fold and using (18) yields
\[
 \widehat W_K-W_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,K}
 =O_P\!\left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}{n\lambda_{P,K}}\right\}
 =O_P(R_{P,K,n}).                                                                 \tag{19}
\]

Write \(\widehat W_a=W_P(a)+Z_a+e_a\), with \(Z_a=(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,a}\) and \(|e_a|=O_P(R_{P,a,n})\).  Boundedness and the exponential Markov bound give \(\max_a|Z_a|=O_P(L_n)\).  Since \(W_P(1)\geq1/12\), the denominator in the estimator is nonzero with probability tending to one.  The exact identity
\[
 \frac{x+u}{y+v}-\frac xy-\frac uy+\frac{xv}{y^2}
 =-\frac{uv}{y(y+v)}+\frac{xv^2}{y^2(y+v)}                                      \tag{20}
\]
applied at \(K\) and \(K+1\) shows that the linear terms combine to \((P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}\).  The four nonlinear terms in (20) are bounded, respectively, by the four displayed terms defining \(R^G_{P,K,n}\).  Thus
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\frac{|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}|}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 =O_P\!\left(\max_{K<K_n}\frac{R^G_{P,K,n}}{\sigma_{P,K}}\right).              \tag{21}
\]

For score replacement, on (18) a nearest-center assignment changes only within \(2\delta^C_{P,K,n}\) of a boundary.  Off that set the residual-map discrepancy is at most \(\delta^C_{P,K,n}\); on it both residuals are bounded by \(2s_{P,K}\).  The growing-margin assumption gives
\[
 \|\{U-q_{\widehat C_K}(U)\}-\{U-q_{P,K}(U)\}\|_{L_2(P)}^2
 \leq(\delta^C_{P,K,n})^2+16M_{P,K}s_{P,K}^2
 \left(\frac{\delta^C_{P,K,n}}{s_{P,K}}\right)^{\alpha_{P,K}},                 \tag{22}
\]
whenever the validity inequality holds, while the declared unit bound applies otherwise.  Hence (22) is bounded by \((Q^C_{P,K,n})^2\).  Insert this bound into the ratio influence-function formula and use the triangle inequality.  Since the nuisance discrepancy is zero, the four surviving codebook terms are exactly those in \(S^G_{P,K,n}\), and empirical centering cannot increase their order.  Therefore
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left\|
 \frac{\widehat{\Delta\phi}_K-\Delta\phi_{P,K}}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right\|_{L_2(P)}
 =O_P\!\left(\max_{K<K_n}\frac{S^G_{P,K,n}}{\sigma_{P,K}}\right).              \tag{23}
\]

It remains to verify the explicit frontier index.  Equations (1)--(3) and the exact covariance packing theorem imply uniformly over \(\mathcal U_n\)
\[
 w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n},\qquad
 \mathfrak E_n(P)\leq C,qquad
 \delta^C_{P,K,n}\leq CK^2L_n,qquad
 Q^C_{P,K,n}\leq C(KL_n)^{1/2}.                                                  \tag{24}
\]
The validity branch in the last assertion follows from \(K_n^3L_n\to0\), which is implied by (1).  Direct substitution in the declared gauges gives
\[
 R^G_{P,K,n}\leq CKL_n^2,qquad S^G_{P,K,n}\leq C(KL_n)^{1/2}.                  \tag{25}
\]
For example, the product term in \(R^G\) is at most \(C(L_n+KL_n^2)L_n\leq CKL_n^2\); every other term is no larger by (2).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)
 \leq C\left[\left\{\frac{\log^7(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/6}
 +\frac{K_n^3\sqrt{\log K_n}\log(nK_n)}{\sqrt n}
 +K_n^{5/2}\log K_n\left\{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/4}\right].          \tag{26}
\]
The three terms in (26) are respectively \(o(1)\),
\(O(n^{3\kappa-1/2}\log^{3/2}n)\), and
\(O(n^{5\kappa/2-1/4}\log^{5/4}n)\), and hence vanish because \(\kappa<1/10\).

Finally, bounded standardized oracle scores and an exponential Markov bound followed by a union bound yield
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}|P_{\mathrm{emp},n}(\Delta\phi_{P,K}/\sigma_{P,K})^2-1|=o_P(1).    \tag{27}
\]
For arrays \(a,b\), \(|P_na^2-P_nb^2|\leq\{P_n(a-b)^2\}^{1/2}\{P_n(a+b)^2\}^{1/2}\).  Apply this identity with (23), (24)--(27), and the score-replacement term in \(\Xi_n\) to obtain uniform relative variance consistency.  Positivity in (2) then implies \(\min_{K<K_n}\widehat\sigma_K>0\) with probability tending to one.  Multiplying (21) by \(\sqrt n\) and using the corresponding nonnegative term of \(\Xi_n\) proves the asserted \(o_P(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1})\) linearization.  This completes every claim in the narrowed, explicitly noiseless regime.
