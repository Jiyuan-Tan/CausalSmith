Write \(P_0=P_{U,0}^{(n)}\), fix \(P=P_j^{(n)}\in\mathcal U_n\), and suppress the row superscript.  Since \(K_n=\lfloor n^{\varkappa}\rfloor\), \(0<\varkappa<1/10\), and \(|J_n|\leq K_n\),
\[
 \delta_nK_n^3\leq Cn^{3\varkappa-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 K_n^5L_n\leq Cn^{5\varkappa-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\), \(\texttt{prop:uniform-witness-membership}\), and the diagonal identity in \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\) give, uniformly in \(P\) and \(K\leq K_n\),
\[
 W_P(K)\asymp K^{-2},\quad \lambda_{P,K}\geq cK^{-3},\quad
 s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1},\quad \kappa_{P,K}\geq cK^{-1},\quad
 \alpha_{P,K}=1,\quad M_{P,K}\leq C,\quad \sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}.          \tag{2}
\]
They also give a density \(f_P\) of \(U\) satisfying
\[
 \|f_P-1\|_\infty\leq C\delta_n,\qquad
 \|C_{P,K}-C_{P_0,K}\|_2\leq C\delta_nK^{3/2}=o(K^{-1}).                       \tag{3}
\]
Every division below is valid because \(W_P(1)\geq1/12\) and \(\sigma_{P,K}>0\).  The known nuisance functions and \(\nu_n=0\) imply \(\widehat b_v(U)=b_P(U)=U\) and \(\widehat\zeta_v=\zeta_P=0\).  Consequently the continuous plain fitting criterion and the corrected evaluation criterion in \(\texttt{def:corrected-profile-estimator}\) both reduce to squared quantization loss.

We first prove global basin entry.  Let \(C_K^0=((2r-1)/(2K):1\leq r\leq K)\).  For an arbitrary ordered \(C\), let \(p_r\) and \(m_r\) be its Voronoi-cell length and midpoint; cells of length zero are allowed.  Completing the square on each cell gives
\[
 P_0\ell_C=\sum_{r=1}^K\left\{\frac{p_r^3}{12}+p_r(c_r-m_r)^2\right\}.          \tag{4}
\]
With \(a=K^{-1}\),
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^Kp_r^3-K^{-2}
 =\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-a)^2(p_r+2a)
 \geq\frac2K\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-a)^2.                                           \tag{5}
\]
Put \(\varepsilon(C)=P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\).  If \(\varepsilon(C)\leq c_1K^{-5}\), with \(c_1\) sufficiently small, (4)--(5) imply \(p_r\geq(2K)^{-1}\) and
\[
 \sum_r(p_r-a)^2\leq6K\varepsilon(C),\qquad
 \sum_r(c_r-m_r)^2\leq2K\varepsilon(C).                                      \tag{6}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \left|m_r-\frac{2r-1}{2K}\right|
 \leq\sum_{q<r}|p_q-K^{-1}|+\frac12|p_r-K^{-1}|
 \leq CK\sqrt{\varepsilon(C)}.                                                \tag{7}
\]
Summing the squared form of (7) and using (6) yields
\[
 \|C-C_K^0\|_2\leq CK^{3/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon(C)}.                             \tag{8}
\]
By contraposition, and trivially when the premise preceding (6) fails, there are fixed \(c_2,c_3>0\) such that
\[
 \inf_{\|C-C_K^0\|_2\geq c_2/K}
 \{P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\}\geq c_3K^{-5}.                                \tag{9}
\]
If \(\|C-C_{P,K}\|_2\geq2c_2/K\), (3) implies \(\|C-C_K^0\|_2\geq c_2/K\) for all large \(n\).  Since \(0\leq\ell_C\leq1\), (3), optimality of \(C_{P,K}\), and \(P_0\ell_{C_K^0}=1/(12K^2)\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\ell_C-P\ell_{C_{P,K}}
 &\geq P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}
      -2\|f_P-1\|_\infty P_0\ell_{C_K^0}
      -2\|f_P-1\|_\infty\{P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\}\\
 &\geq \frac{c_3}{2}K^{-5}.
\end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{10}
\]
uniformly, because \(\delta_nK_n^3\to0\).  Thus the required outside-basin gap is derived from the uniform objective rather than inferred from local curvature.

For every \(C\), \(u\mapsto\ell_C(u)\) is continuous and piecewise absolutely continuous with total variation at most two.  Integration by parts therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{C\in\mathcal C_K}|(P_N-P)\ell_C|\leq2\|F_N-F_P\|_\infty.                \tag{11}
\]
To bound the right side without citing an empirical-process theorem, choose population quantiles \(u_r\) with \(F_P(u_r)=r/N\), \(0\leq r\leq N\).  Monotonicity bounds the supremum between adjacent quantiles by the larger endpoint error plus \(N^{-1}\).  For each endpoint, convexity of the exponential on \([0,1]\) gives
\[
 E\exp[t\{1(U\leq u_r)-F_P(u_r)\}]\leq e^{t^2/8}.
\]
Exponential Markov, optimization over \(t\), and a union bound therefore yield
\[
 P^N\{\|F_N-F_P\|_\infty>x+N^{-1}\}\leq2(N+1)e^{-2Nx^2}.                     \tag{12}
\]
All fitting blocks have \(N\geq cn\).  Set \(x=c_3K_n^{-5}/16\) in (11)--(12), and union over the fixed folds and \(K\leq K_n\).  By (1), the exceptional probability is bounded by
\[
 CnK_n\exp(-cnK_n^{-10})=o(1).                                                \tag{13}
\]
On its complement empirical optimality and (10)--(11) exclude every codebook outside the \(2c_2/K\) population basin.  Existence and measurability of each selector follow from the continuous compact construction (E.1).

We next control the local empirical process and explicitly account for moving Voronoi assignments.  Fix \(C^\star=C_{P,K}\), write \(v=C-C^\star\), \(r=\|v\|_2\leq c/K\), and let \(V_q^\star\) and \(b_q^\star\) denote the population cells and boundaries.  On \(V_q^\star\), define \(g_q(u)=2(c_q^\star-u)\) and set all other coordinates of \(g(u)\) to zero.  If the assignment does not change, then
\[
 \ell_C(u)-\ell_{C^\star}(u)=v^{\mathsf T}g(u)+v_q^2.                         \tag{14}
\]
The boundary between centers \(q\) and \(q+1\) moves by
\[
 \frac12|v_q+v_{q+1}|\leq r.                                                   \tag{15}
\]
Thus a switch at that boundary is possible only on
\(\{|u-b_q^\star|\leq r\}\).  Adjacent squared losses agree at \(b_q^\star\); subtracting the two quadratic polynomials and using (2) therefore bounds the remainder in (14), on this switching interval, by
\[
 Cs_{P,K}(|v_q|+|v_{q+1}|).                                                    \tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) are a direct interval-boundary decomposition of the entire moving-assignment supremum.

Let \(\varepsilon_i\) be independent Rademacher signs.  Disjointness of the population cells gives
\[
 E\left\|N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\varepsilon_i g(U_i)\right\|_2^2
 =N^{-1}E_P\|g(U)\|_2^2=\frac{4W_P(K)}N.                                     \tag{17}
\]
For the switching part, order the observations in each interval in (15) by distance from \(b_q^\star\).  The supremum over a moving endpoint is a maximum of partial Rademacher sums.  Squaring the submartingale and telescoping its conditional second moments gives
\[
 E_\varepsilon\max_{m\leq M}\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^m\varepsilon_\ell\right|
 \leq2\sqrt M.                                                                 \tag{18}
\]
Use (18), then Cauchy--Schwarz over \(q\), (16), and
\[
 \sum_{q<K}P\{|U-b_q^\star|\leq r\}
 \leq C K r\leq C                                                             \tag{19}
\]
from the density bound in (3).  The conditional Rademacher expectation of the switching supremum is at most \(Crs_{P,K}/\sqrt N\).  The quadratic terms in (14) are bounded by
\[
 r^2\max_{q\leq K}|(P_N-P)1_{V_q^\star}|
 \leq Cr^2\sqrt{\frac{x+\log K}{N}}
 \leq Crs_{P,K}\sqrt{\frac{x+\log K}{N}},                                     \tag{20}
\]
where the first inequality follows from the same bounded-exponential calculation as (12), and the second uses \(r\leq c/K\) and (2).  Symmetrization follows by adjoining an independent ghost sample and applying conditional Jensen.  Finally, changing one observation changes the supremum by at most \(Crs_{P,K}/N\), by (14)--(16); applying the bounded-exponential argument successively to the associated Doob differences adds \(Crs_{P,K}\sqrt{x/N}\).  Combining (17)--(20) proves, with probability at least \(1-Ce^{-x}\),
\[
 \sup_{\|C-C^\star\|_2\leq r}
 |(P_N-P)(\ell_C-\ell_{C^\star})|
 \leq C\left[
 r\sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)\{x+\log(nK_n)\}}N}
 +\frac{r^2\{x+\log(nK_n)\}}N\right].                                        \tag{21}
\]
This proves the previously missing supremum over moving assignments; no fixed-assignment replacement is made.

Peel the basin into \(2^{-a-1}c/K<\|C-C^\star\|_2\leq2^{-a}c/K\), apply (21) with \(x+\log(nK_n)+2a\), and sum the exceptional probabilities.  Population quadratic growth, empirical optimality, and (21) then imply
\[
 \lambda_{P,K}d(\widehat C_{v,K})^2
 \leq C d(\widehat C_{v,K})
 \sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n}
 +C d(\widehat C_{v,K})^2\frac{\log(nK_n)}n.                                  \tag{22}
\]
By (1)--(2), the last coefficient is \(o(\lambda_{P,K})\) and is absorbed.  Division by the positive \(\lambda_{P,K}\) gives
\[
 d(\widehat C_{v,K})
 =O_P\!\left[\lambda_{P,K}^{-1}
 \left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/2}\right]
 =O_P(\delta^C_{P,K,n})                                                       \tag{23}
\]
simultaneously over folds and resolutions.  Conditional on its training blocks, the evaluation fold is independent.  Applying (21) on that fold and using (23) yields
\[
 \widehat W_K-W_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,K}
 =O_P\!\left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}{n\lambda_{P,K}}\right\}
 =O_P(R_{P,K,n}).                                                              \tag{24}
\]

Write \(\widehat W_a=W_P(a)+Z_a+e_a\), where
\(Z_a=(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,a}\) and
\(|e_a|=O_P(R_{P,a,n})\).  Boundedness and the exponential calculation used in (12) give \(\max_a|Z_a|=O_P(L_n)\).  Hence \(\widehat W_1\neq0\) with probability tending to one.  The exact identity
\[
 \frac{x+u}{y+v}-\frac xy-\frac uy+\frac{xv}{y^2}
 =-\frac{uv}{y(y+v)}+\frac{xv^2}{y^2(y+v)}                                   \tag{25}
\]
applied at \(K\) and \(K+1\) shows that the first-order terms combine into
\((P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}\).  The four nonlinear products are bounded by the four terms in the definition of \(R^G_{P,K,n}\).  Therefore
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\frac{|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)
 -(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}|}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 =O_P\!\left(\max_{K<K_n}\frac{R^G_{P,K,n}}{\sigma_{P,K}}\right).              \tag{26}
\]

For score replacement, (15) shows that the residual map changes assignment only in the declared boundary neighborhoods.  Off them its discrepancy is at most \(\delta^C_{P,K,n}\); on them both residuals are at most \(2s_{P,K}\).  The margin condition in (2) gives
\[
 \|\{U-q_{\widehat C_K}(U)\}-\{U-q_{P,K}(U)\}\|_{L_2(P)}^2
 \leq(\delta^C_{P,K,n})^2
 +16M_{P,K}s_{P,K}^2
 \left(\frac{\delta^C_{P,K,n}}{s_{P,K}}\right)^{\alpha_{P,K}},                 \tag{27}
\]
on the validity branch, and the unit bound on the other branch.  Thus (27) is at most \((Q^C_{P,K,n})^2\).  Insert it into the ratio influence-function identity.  Since nuisance error is zero, its four codebook terms are exactly those defining \(S^G_{P,K,n}\), and centering is a contraction in \(L_2(P)\).  Hence
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left\|
 \frac{\widehat{\Delta\phi}_{K}-\Delta\phi_{P,K}}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right\|_{L_2(P)}
 =O_P\!\left(\max_{K<K_n}\frac{S^G_{P,K,n}}{\sigma_{P,K}}\right).              \tag{28}
\]

We now evaluate the explicit frontier.  From (1)--(3), (23), and the exact covariance theorem,
\[
 w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n},\quad \mathfrak E_n(P)\leq C,\quad
 \delta^C_{P,K,n}\leq CK^2L_n,\quad
 Q^C_{P,K,n}\leq C(KL_n)^{1/2}.                                                \tag{29}
\]
The validity branch follows from \(K_n^3L_n\to0\).  Direct substitution in the declared gauges gives
\[
 R^G_{P,K,n}\leq CKL_n^2,\qquad
 S^G_{P,K,n}\leq C(KL_n)^{1/2}.                                                \tag{30}
\]
Consequently
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)
\leq C\left[
\left\{\frac{\log^7(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/6}
+\frac{K_n^3\sqrt{\log K_n}\log(nK_n)}{\sqrt n}
+K_n^{5/2}\log K_n\left\{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/4}\right]=o(1),          \tag{31}
\]
because the last two polynomial exponents are \(3\varkappa-1/2<0\) and
\(5\varkappa/2-1/4<0\).

It remains to justify empirical variance consistency separately from the population \(L_2(P)\) statement.  Put
\[
 X_K(O)=\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O)/\sigma_{P,K},\qquad
 D_{v,K}(O)=\widehat{\Delta\phi}_{v,K}(O)/\sigma_{P,K}-X_K(O).
\]
On the basin event, (2), (23), and the cellwise formulas give
\(\sup_{v,K}\{\|X_K\|_\infty+\|D_{v,K}\|_\infty\}\leq C\).  Conditional on the two training blocks for fold \(v\), its validation observations are independent and
\[
 \max_{v,K}P D_{v,K}^2=O_P(a_n^2),\qquad
 a_n:=\max_{K<K_n}S^G_{P,K,n}/\sigma_{P,K}.                                   \tag{32}
\]
For \(0\leq Z\leq C\), Taylor's formula for \(Ee^{t(Z-EZ)}\) and
\(\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq C EZ\) gives the conditional Bernstein bound
\[
 P\{|(P_{\mathcal I_v}-P)Z|>
 C[\sqrt{PZ\,x/|\mathcal I_v|}+x/|\mathcal I_v|]\mid\mathcal T_v,\mathcal Q_v\}
 \leq2e^{-x}.                                                                  \tag{33}
\]
Apply (33) to \(Z=D_{v,K}^2\), take \(x=3\log(nK_n)\), and union over the fixed folds and \(K<K_n\).  Equations (31)--(33) give
\[
 \max_{v,K}P_{\mathcal I_v}D_{v,K}^2
 =O_P\!\left\{a_n^2+a_n\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}
 +\frac{\log(nK_n)}n\right\}=o_P(1).                                          \tag{34}
\]
Likewise, \(EX_K^2=1\), and the bounded-exponential calculation gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}|P_{\mathrm{emp},n}X_K^2-1|
 =O_P\!\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}\right\}=o_P(1),                        \tag{35}
\]
while \(\max_K|P_{\mathrm{emp},n}X_K|=o_P(1)\).  Empirical centering changes a second moment by the square of its empirical mean.  Finally,
\[
 |P_n a^2-P_n b^2|
 \leq\{P_n(a-b)^2\}^{1/2}\{P_n(a+b)^2\}^{1/2}.                                \tag{36}
\]
Use (34)--(36) with \(a=\widehat{\Delta\phi}_K/\sigma_{P,K}\) and \(b=X_K\) to obtain
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}-1\right|=o_P(1).       \tag{37}
\]
Score nondegeneracy then gives \(\min_{K<K_n}\widehat\sigma_K>0\) with probability tending to one.  Finally, (26), (30)--(31), and the nonnegative remainder term in \(\Xi_n\) give
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}
 \frac{\sqrt n\,|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}|}
 {\sigma_{P,K}}
 =o_P\!\left(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1}\right).                                        \tag{38}
\]
All exceptional probabilities above are uniform in \(P\in\mathcal U_n\), completing the proposed known-nuisance noiseless result.
