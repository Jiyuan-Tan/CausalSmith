Fix \(P=P_{U,\nu_n}^{(n)}\).  Direct conditional expectation under the declared witness gives
\[
 \mu_{1,P}(u)=u-\tfrac12,\qquad \mu_{0,P}(u)=\tfrac12-u,\qquad b_P(u)=u.
\]
Partitioning \([0,1]\) into the \(a\) nearest-center intervals and applying
\(\inf_c\int_x^{x+p}(u-c)^2du=p^3/12\), followed by
\(\sum_{j=1}^ap_j^3\geq a^{-2}\), gives \(W_P(a)=1/(12a^2)\) with equality at the equal-cell midpoints.  In particular \(W_P(1)=1/12\).  Put
\[
 r_a(u)=u-q_{P,a}(u),\qquad d_a(u)=r_a(u)^2.
\]
Since \(g_K=K^{-2}-(K+1)^{-2}\), the influence-function definitions give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta\phi_{P,K}
 &=12\{\phi_{W,P,K}-\phi_{W,P,K+1}-g_K\phi_{W,P,1}\}\\
 &=12\{d_K(U)-d_{K+1}(U)-g_Kd_1(U)\}\\
 &\quad+24\{r_K(U)-r_{K+1}(U)-g_Kr_1(U)\}\zeta_P.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The centering constants cancel because \(W_P(K)-W_P(K+1)=g_KW_P(1)=g_K/12\).  Hence
\[
 \Delta\phi_{P,K}=12F_{K,2}(U)+24\zeta_PF_{K,1}(U),\qquad
 F_{K,p}(u)=\sum_aw_{K,p}(a)B_p(au).
\]
Repeated indices at \(K=1\) combine according to the indicator definition of \(w_{K,p}\).

The \(L_2[0,1]\) Fourier series of \(B_1,B_2\), followed by Parseval's identity, yields for positive integers \(a,b\)
\[
 \int_0^1B_1(au)B_1(bu)\,du=\frac{\gcd(a,b)^2}{12ab},\qquad
 \int_0^1B_2(au)B_2(bu)\,du=\frac{\gcd(a,b)^4}{180a^2b^2}.
\]
Indeed, a shared nonzero frequency satisfies \(ma=\ell b\).  Writing \(a=da_0,b=db_0\) with coprime \(a_0,b_0\) forces \(m=b_0q,\ell=a_0q\); the remaining sums are \(2(2\pi)^{-2}\sum_{q\geq1}q^{-2}=1/12\) and \(8(2\pi)^{-4}\sum_{q\geq1}q^{-4}=1/180\).

The conditional mean-zero and variance identities for \(\zeta_P\) make the two components uncorrelated.  Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Sigma_P(K,L)
 &=144\int_0^1F_{K,2}F_{L,2}
 +576\nu_n^2\int_0^1F_{K,1}F_{L,1}\\
 &=144H_2(K,L)+576\nu_n^2H_1(K,L).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Expanding the diagonal terms and using \(\gcd(K,K+1)=1\) gives
\[
 H_1(K,K)=\frac{(K^2+K+1)(2K^4+4K^3-K^2-3K-1)}
 {12K^4(K+1)^4},
\]
\[
 H_2(K,K)=\frac{K^8+4K^7+10K^6+16K^5+5K^4-12K^3-14K^2-6K-1}
 {90K^6(K+1)^6}.
\]
Multiplying by \(576\nu_n^2\) and \(144\) proves
\(\sigma_{P,K}^2=V_K^{(0)}+\nu_n^2A_K\).

We now certify the numerical separation.  Set
\(\Gamma_p(K,L)=H_p(K,L)/\{H_p(K,K)H_p(L,L)\}^{1/2}\).  For \(k\geq8\),
\[
 H_1(k,k)\geq\frac7{48k^2},\qquad H_2(k,k)\geq\frac1{109k^4}.
\]
After clearing positive denominators and putting \(x=k-8\), these reduce respectively to
\[
 x^6+44x^5+778x^4+6948x^3+31813x^2+63808x+24764\geq0
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 19x^8+1112x^7+27964x^6+392456x^5+3331355x^4
 +17229864x^3\\
 {}+51279088x^2+73631346x+26318627\geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Assume \(8\leq K<L\), put \(t=K/L\), and use \(g_k\leq2/k^3\).  In \(H_1(K,L)\), the three positive frequency-one terms, divided by the diagonal lower bound, total at most
\[
 \frac47\left\{\frac4{K^2L^2}+\frac2{K^2L}+\frac2{KL^2}\right\}
 \leq\frac{19}{4536}<\frac3{700}.
\]
If neither \(K\mid L\) nor \(K+1\mid L+1\), each remaining positive greatest-common-divisor term has proper divisor at most half its smaller argument.  If \(t\leq19/20\), their normalized sum is at most \(2t/7\), and
\[
 \Gamma_1(K,L)\leq19/70+3/700=193/700<7/25.
\]
If \(t>19/20\), \(h=L-K<K/19\) bounds both greatest common divisors, so their normalized sum is at most
\[
 \frac{h^2}{6(7/48)KL}<\frac8{7\cdot19^2};
\]
adding \(3/700\) is again below \(7/25\).

If \(K\mid L\), write \(L=qK\).  For \(q\geq3\), the first main term is at most \(4/(7q)\).  The other greatest common divisor is \(\gcd(K+1,q-1)\), and
\[
 \frac{\min(q-1,K+1)^2}{q}\leq K+1,
\]
so its contribution is at most \(4/\{7(K+1)\}\).  Including the frequency-one terms gives at most \(16/63+3/700<7/25\).  The case \(K+1\mid L+1\) is symmetric.  Only \(L=2K\) and \(L=2K+1\) remain.  Direct substitution gives
\[
 H_1(K,2K)=
 \begin{cases}
 (K-1)(K+1)/(48K^4),&K\ {\rm even},\\
 (K^4+2K^3-3K^2-2K-1)/\{48K^4(K+1)^2\},&K\ {\rm odd},
 \end{cases}
\]
\[
 H_1(K,2K+1)=
 \begin{cases}
 (K^4+2K^3-3K^2-6K-3)/\{48K^2(K+1)^4\},&K\ {\rm even},\\
 K(K+2)/\{48(K+1)^4\},&K\ {\rm odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Substitution of the diagonal formula proves
\(H_1(K,L)^2\leq(7/25)^2H_1(K,K)H_1(L,L)\).  Explicitly, after clearing the positive denominator and setting \(x=K-8\), the four numerator polynomials have the following leading-to-constant coefficient vectors:
\[
\begin{split}
 &(2544,240672,10393928,270870520,4742210135,58729915892,527271838246,3455262502406,\\
 &\quad16388732574185,54811597404220,122528488405133,164090221734096,99322453821696),\\
 &(2544,240672,10453928,275910520,4932530135,62984545892,589628026996,4081319864906,\\
 &\quad20749481238560,75619391746720,187621442781383,284640797514096,199687969101696),\\
 &(2544,278304,13972520,425483640,8748325655,127895513844,1362728652524,10659476141778,\\
 &\quad60735769607880,245783723516520,670424622710535,1106577747715608,835717237221492),\\
 &(2544,278304,13912520,420323640,8548825655,123329183844,1294207073774,9955128761778,\\
 &\quad55713169654755,221251056581520,591874592859285,957709168083108,708907772727117).
\end{split}
\]
Every coefficient is positive, completing \(\Gamma_1(K,L)\leq7/25\).

For \(p=2\), the same exhaustive split is shorter.  The frequency-one terms are bounded after standardization by
\[
 \frac{109}{180}\left\{\frac4{KL}
 +\frac{2L^2}{K(L+1)^4}+\frac{2K^2}{L(K+1)^4}\right\}
 \leq\frac1{25},
\]
where the final inequality is maximized at the smallest admissible pair \(K=8,L=9\).  With neither divisibility, the main terms are at most \(109/1440\).  Under divisibility with quotient \(q\geq3\), they are at most
\[
 \frac{109}{180q^2}+\frac{109(K+2)^2}{180(K+1)^4}<\frac4{25};
\]
for \(q=2\), the companion greatest common divisor is at most \(2\), and the same inequality follows directly.  Therefore \(\Gamma_2(K,L)\leq1/5<7/25\).  All divisions are valid by the diagonal positivity above.

Applying the two bounds and Cauchy--Schwarz in \(\mathbb R^2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Sigma_P(K,L)
 &\leq\frac7{25}\left[144\{H_2(K,K)H_2(L,L)\}^{1/2}
 +576\nu_n^2\{H_1(K,K)H_1(L,L)\}^{1/2}\right]\\
 &\leq\frac7{25}\{\sigma_{P,K}^2\sigma_{P,L}^2\}^{1/2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(R_P(K,L)\leq0.28\) and \(d_P(K,L)\geq\sqrt{1.44}=1.2\).

Let \(m=|J_n|=(K_n-8)_+\).  For \(m\geq2\), compare \(Z=(Z_{P,K}:K\in J_n)\) with
\(Y_K=\sqrt{0.72}\varepsilon_K+\sqrt{0.28}\varepsilon_0\), where all \(\varepsilon\)'s are independent standard normals.  Marginal variances agree and off-diagonal covariances satisfy \(R_P(K,L)\leq0.28=R_Y(K,L)\).  This comparison can be derived directly: for
\(f_\beta(x)=\beta^{-1}\log\sum_Ke^{\beta x_K}\), Gaussian integration by parts along the interpolation \(\sqrt tZ+\sqrt{1-t}Y\) gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}\mathbb Ef_\beta
 =\frac12\sum_{K,L}(R_P(K,L)-R_Y(K,L))
 \mathbb E(\partial_{KL}f_\beta)\geq0,
\]
because diagonal differences vanish and \(\partial_{KL}f_\beta=-\beta p_Kp_L\leq0\) off diagonal.  Letting \(\beta\to\infty\),
\[
 \mathbb E\max_{K\in J_n}Z_{P,K}
 \geq\sqrt{0.72}\,\mathbb E\max_{j\leq m}\varepsilon_j.
\]
Integrating the Gaussian density on \([t,t+t^{-1}]\) gives
\(1-\Phi(t)\geq ct^{-1}e^{-t^2/2}\) for \(t\geq1\).  If \(M_m=\max_{j\leq m}\varepsilon_j\), independence gives
\[
 \mathbb P(M_m\geq t)=1-\Phi(t)^m
 \geq1-\exp\{-m(1-\Phi(t))\}.
\]
Moreover \(M_m\geq\varepsilon_1\), so
\[
 \mathbb EM_m\geq t\mathbb P(M_m\geq t)-\mathbb E(-M_m)_+
 \geq t\mathbb P(M_m\geq t)-\mathbb E(-\varepsilon_1)_+.
\]
Taking \(t=\sqrt{\log m}\) makes the probability tend to one and gives
\(\mathbb EM_m\geq c'\sqrt{\log m}\) after absorbing finitely many small \(m\)'s.  Since \(\max|Z_K|\geq\max_{K\in J_n}Z_K\), this proves the lower width bound for large \(K_n\); for bounded \(K_n\), reduce the universal constant and use \(\mathbb E|Z_{P,1}|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\).

For the upper bound, the Gaussian moment generating function gives, for \(s>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E\max_{1\leq K<K_n}|Z_{P,K}|
 \leq\frac{\log\{2(K_n-1)\}}s+\frac s2.
\]
Choosing \(s=\sqrt{2\log\{2(K_n-1)\}}\) completes the upper bound and hence the theorem.
