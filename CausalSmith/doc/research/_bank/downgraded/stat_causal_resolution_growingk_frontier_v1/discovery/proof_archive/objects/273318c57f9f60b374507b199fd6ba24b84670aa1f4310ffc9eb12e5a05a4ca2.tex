Write \(P_0=P_{U,0}^{(n)}\), fix \(P=P_j^{(n)}\in\mathcal U_n\), and suppress the row superscript.  The two announced localization conditions are
\[
 \delta_nK_n^3\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad K_n^5L_n\longrightarrow0.                                             \tag{1}
\]
The membership theorem, the uniform-witness proposition, and the diagonal identity in the exact-covariance theorem give, uniformly in \(P\) and \(K\leq K_n\),
\[
 W_P(K)\asymp K^{-2},\quad \lambda_{P,K}\geq cK^{-3},\quad
 s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1},\quad \kappa_{P,K}\geq cK^{-1},\quad
 \alpha_{P,K}=1,\quad M_{P,K}\leq C,\quad \sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}.        \tag{2}
\]
Here the distortion comparison follows directly from the density bound below: for every codebook \(C\), \((1-C\delta_n)P_0\ell_C\leq P\ell_C\leq(1+C\delta_n)P_0\ell_C\), and taking infima and using \(W_{P_0}(K)=1/(12K^2)\) gives the first relation in (2).  The same results give
\[
 \|f_P-1\|_\infty\leq C\delta_n,\qquad
 \|C_{P,K}-C_{P_0,K}\|_2\leq C\delta_nK^{3/2}=o(K^{-1}).                    \tag{3}
\]
Every division below is valid because the resolution-denominator assumption gives \(W_P(1)\geq\underline w>0\) and score nondegeneracy gives \(\sigma_{P,K}>0\).  The known nuisance functions and \(\nu_n=0\) imply \(\widehat b_v(U)=b_P(U)=U\) and \(\widehat\zeta_v=\zeta_P=0\).  Hence both the continuous plain fitting criterion and the corrected evaluation criterion reduce to squared quantization loss.

We first prove global basin entry.  For any ordered codebook \(C\), write \(\ell_C(u)=\min_{c\in C}(u-c)^2\), and let \(C_K^0=((2r-1)/(2K):1\leq r\leq K)\).  For such a \(C\), let \(p_r\) and \(m_r\) be its Voronoi-cell length and midpoint; zero-length cells are allowed.  Completing the square on each cell gives
\[
 P_0\ell_C=\sum_{r=1}^K\left\{\frac{p_r^3}{12}+p_r(c_r-m_r)^2\right\}.       \tag{4}
\]
With \(a=K^{-1}\),
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^Kp_r^3-K^{-2}
 =\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-a)^2(p_r+2a)
 \geq\frac2K\sum_{r=1}^K(p_r-a)^2.                                         \tag{5}
\]
Put \(\varepsilon(C)=P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\).  If \(\varepsilon(C)\leq c_1K^{-5}\), with \(c_1\) sufficiently small, (4)--(5) imply \(p_r\geq(2K)^{-1}\) and
\[
 \sum_r(p_r-a)^2\leq6K\varepsilon(C),\qquad
 \sum_r(c_r-m_r)^2\leq2K\varepsilon(C).                                    \tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \left|m_r-\frac{2r-1}{2K}\right|
 \leq\sum_{q<r}|p_q-K^{-1}|+\frac12|p_r-K^{-1}|
 \leq CK\sqrt{\varepsilon(C)}.                                              \tag{7}
\]
After squaring (7), summing over \(r\), and using (6),
\[
 \|C-C_K^0\|_2\leq CK^{3/2}\sqrt{\varepsilon(C)}.                           \tag{8}
\]
Contraposition, together with the case \(\varepsilon(C)>c_1K^{-5}\), yields fixed \(c_2,c_3>0\) such that
\[
 \inf_{\|C-C_K^0\|_2\geq c_2/K}
 \{P_0\ell_C-P_0\ell_{C_K^0}\}\geq c_3K^{-5}.                              \tag{9}
\]
If \(\|C-C_{P,K}\|_2\geq2c_2/K\), (3) implies \(\|C-C_K^0\|_2\geq c_2/K\) for all large \(n\).  Since \(C_{P,K}\) minimizes \(P\ell_C\), \(0\leq\ell_C\leq1\), and \(P_0\ell_{C_K^0}=1/(12K^2)\), (3) and (9) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P\ell_C-P\ell_{C_{P,K}}
 &\geq P\ell_C-P\ell_{C_K^0}\\
 &\geq \{1-C\delta_n\}\varepsilon(C)-2C\delta_nP_0\ell_{C_K^0}
 \geq \frac{c_3}{2}K^{-5},
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{10}
\]
uniformly, because \(\delta_nK_n^3\to0\).  Thus the outside-basin gap follows from uniform geometry and is not inferred from local curvature.

For a block of \(N\) independent observations, let \(P_N\) and \(F_N\) be its empirical law and empirical distribution function, and let \(F_P\) be the distribution function under \(P\).  For every \(C\), the map \(u\mapsto\ell_C(u)\) is continuous, piecewise absolutely continuous, and has total variation at most two.  Integration by parts therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{C\in\mathcal C_K}|(P_N-P)\ell_C|\leq2\|F_N-F_P\|_\infty.            \tag{11}
\]
To bound the right-hand side, choose population quantiles \(u_r\) satisfying \(F_P(u_r)=r/N\), \(0\leq r\leq N\); the density lower bound supplied by (3) makes these quantiles well defined for all large \(n\).  Monotonicity bounds the supremum between adjacent quantiles by the larger endpoint error plus \(N^{-1}\).  For each endpoint, Hoeffding's exponential calculation for a Bernoulli variable gives
\[
 E\exp[t\{\mathbf1(U\leq u_r)-F_P(u_r)\}]\leq e^{t^2/8}.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, optimization over \(t\), and a union bound yield
\[
 P^N\{\|F_N-F_P\|_\infty>x+N^{-1}\}\leq2(N+1)e^{-2Nx^2}.                  \tag{12}
\]
Every codebook-fitting block has \(N\geq cn\).  Set \(x=c_3K_n^{-5}/16\) in (11)--(12).  Since \(K_n^5L_n\to0\) and \(L_n\geq n^{-1/2}\), also \(N^{-1}=o(K_n^{-5})\).  Taking a union bound over the fixed folds and \(K\leq K_n\), the exceptional probability is, by (1), at most
\[
 CnK_n\exp(-cnK_n^{-10})=o(1).                                               \tag{13}
\]
On the complementary event, empirical optimality and (10)--(11) exclude every codebook outside the \(2c_2/K\) population basin.  Existence and measurability of each selector follow from the compact continuous construction in (E.1).

We next control the local empirical process, including moving Voronoi assignments.  Fix \(C^\star=C_{P,K}\), write \(v=C-C^\star\) and \(r=\|v\|_2\leq c/K\), and let \(V_q^\star\) and \(b_q^\star\) be the population cells and boundaries.  By (2)--(3), adjacent population centers are separated by at least \(c_0/K\); choose \(c<c_0/4\), so every codebook in this ball remains strictly ordered and any assignment switch crosses one adjacent boundary.  On \(V_q^\star\), define \(g_q(u)=2(c_q^\star-u)\) and set all other coordinates of \(g(u)\) to zero.  If the assignment does not change, then
\[
 \ell_C(u)-\ell_{C^\star}(u)=v^{\mathsf T}g(u)+v_q^2.                       \tag{14}
\]
The boundary between centers \(q\) and \(q+1\) moves by at most
\[
 \frac12|v_q+v_{q+1}|\leq r.                                                 \tag{15}
\]
A switch is therefore possible only when \(|u-b_q^\star|\leq r\).  Adjacent squared losses agree at \(b_q^\star\); subtracting their quadratic polynomials and using (2) bounds the remainder in (14), throughout that interval, by
\[
 Cs_{P,K}(|v_q|+|v_{q+1}|).                                                   \tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) decompose the entire moving-assignment supremum.

Let \(\varepsilon_i\) be independent Rademacher signs.  Disjointness of the population cells gives
\[
 E\left\|N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\varepsilon_i g(U_i)\right\|_2^2
 =N^{-1}E_P\|g(U)\|_2^2=\frac{4W_P(K)}N.                                   \tag{17}
\]
For a switching interval, order its observations by distance from \(b_q^\star\).  The supremum over a moving endpoint becomes a maximum of partial Rademacher sums.  If \(S_m\) is such a sum and \(\tau_t\) is the first index at which \(|S_m|\geq t\), orthogonality of increments after \(\tau_t\) gives \(ES_M^2\geq E[S_{\tau_t}^2\mathbf1\{\tau_t\leq M\}]\geq t^2P(\tau_t\leq M)\), while \(ES_M^2=M\).  Integrating \(P(\max_{m\leq M}|S_m|\geq t)\leq\min(1,M/t^2)\) gives
\[
 E_\varepsilon\max_{m\leq M}\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^m\varepsilon_\ell\right|
 \leq2\sqrt M.                                                               \tag{18}
\]
Using (18), Cauchy--Schwarz over \(q\), (16), and the density upper bound in (3),
\[
 \sum_{q<K}P\{|U-b_q^\star|\leq r\}\leq CKr\leq C,                        \tag{19}
\]
the conditional Rademacher expectation of the switching supremum is at most \(Crs_{P,K}/\sqrt N\).  The quadratic terms in (14) obey
\[
 r^2\max_{q\leq K}|(P_N-P)\mathbf1_{V_q^\star}|
 \leq Cr^2\sqrt{\frac{x+\log K}{N}}
 \leq Crs_{P,K}\sqrt{\frac{x+\log K}{N}},                                 \tag{20}
\]
by the same bounded-exponential calculation and \(r\leq c/K\).  Symmetrization follows by adjoining an independent ghost sample and applying conditional Jensen.  Changing one observation changes the supremum by at most \(Crs_{P,K}/N\), so the identical bounded-exponential calculation for its Doob differences supplies the tail term.  Consequently, with probability at least \(1-Ce^{-x}\),
\[
 \sup_{\|C-C^\star\|_2\leq r}
 |(P_N-P)(\ell_C-\ell_{C^\star})|
 \leq C\left[
 r\sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)\{x+\log(nK_n)\}}N}
 +\frac{r^2\{x+\log(nK_n)\}}N\right].                                    \tag{21}
\]

Define \(d(C)=\|C-C^\star\|_2\).  Peel the basin into shells \(2^{-a-1}c/K<d(C)\leq2^{-a}c/K\), apply (21) with the tail parameter increased by \(2a\), and sum the exceptional probabilities.  Population quadratic growth, empirical optimality, and (21) imply
\[
 \lambda_{P,K}d(\widehat C_{v,K})^2
 \leq C d(\widehat C_{v,K})
 \sqrt{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n}
 +C d(\widehat C_{v,K})^2\frac{\log(nK_n)}n.                               \tag{22}
\]
By (1)--(2), the final coefficient is \(o(\lambda_{P,K})\) and is absorbed.  Dividing by the positive modulus gives, simultaneously over folds and resolutions,
\[
 d(\widehat C_{v,K})
 =O_P\!\left[\lambda_{P,K}^{-1}
 \left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}n\right\}^{1/2}\right]
 =O_P(\delta^C_{P,K,n}).                                                     \tag{23}
\]
The balanced-fold correction gives \(|\mathcal I_v|\geq c_{\mathcal I}n\), and the estimator construction gives \(|\mathcal Q_v|\geq c_{\mathcal Q}n\).  Conditional on the fold-external observations, each validation fold is independent.  Apply (21) on each validation fold, use (23), and aggregate with weights \(|\mathcal I_v|/n\), which sum to one.  This yields
\[
 \widehat W_K-W_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,K}
 =O_P\!\left\{\frac{W_P(K)\log(nK_n)}{n\lambda_{P,K}}\right\}
 =O_P(R_{P,K,n}).                                                            \tag{24}
\]

Write \(\widehat W_a=W_P(a)+Z_a+e_a\), where \(Z_a=(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\phi_{W,P,a}\) and \(|e_a|=O_P(R_{P,a,n})\).  Boundedness and the exponential calculation used for (12) give \(\max_a|Z_a|=O_P(L_n)\).  Hence \(\widehat W_1\neq0\) with probability tending to one.  The exact identity
\[
 \frac{x+u}{y+v}-\frac xy-\frac uy+\frac{xv}{y^2}
 =-\frac{uv}{y(y+v)}+\frac{xv^2}{y^2(y+v)}                                  \tag{25}
\]
applied to the adjacent numerator shows that the first-order terms equal \((P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}\).  The four nonlinear products are bounded by the four displayed terms in \(R^G_{P,K,n}\).  Therefore
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\frac{|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)
 -(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}|}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 =O_P\!\left(\max_{K<K_n}\frac{R^G_{P,K,n}}{\sigma_{P,K}}\right).          \tag{26}
\]

For score replacement, (15) shows that the residual map changes assignment only in the declared boundary neighborhoods.  Off those neighborhoods its discrepancy is at most \(\delta^C_{P,K,n}\), while on them both residuals are at most \(2s_{P,K}\).  The margin condition gives, on its validity branch,
\[
 \|\{U-q_{\widehat C_K}(U)\}-\{U-q_{P,K}(U)\}\|_{L_2(P)}^2
 \leq(\delta^C_{P,K,n})^2
 +16M_{P,K}s_{P,K}^2
 \left(\frac{\delta^C_{P,K,n}}{s_{P,K}}\right)^{\alpha_{P,K}},             \tag{27}
\]
and the unit bound applies on the other branch.  Thus (27) is at most \((Q^C_{P,K,n})^2\).

We now keep the fold-measurable score map separate from all risk-coefficient perturbations.  Let
\[
 \mathcal F_v=\sigma(O_{n,r}:r\in\mathcal T_v\cup\mathcal Q_v),
 \qquad
 \check W_{v,a}=|\mathcal Q_v|^{-1}\sum_{r\in\mathcal Q_v}
 \ell_{\widehat C_{v,a}}(U_r).
\]
The second equality uses the known nuisances and \(\nu_n=0\).  Equations (11), (21), and (23), on \(\mathcal Q_v\), imply
\[
 \max_{v\leq V}\max_{a\leq K_n}|\check W_{v,a}-W_P(a)|
 =O_P\!\left(L_n+\max_{a\leq K_n}R_{P,a,n}\right)=O_P(L_n),                 \tag{28}
\]
because \(R_{P,a,n}\leq CaL_n^2\) and \(K_nL_n=o(1)\) follows from (1).  Hence \(\min_v\check W_{v,1}\geq\underline w/2\) with probability tending to one.  On this event, define for a fresh observation \(o\)
\[
 \psi_{v,K}^{\circ}(o)=
 \frac{m_{v,K}(o;\widehat C_{v,K})-m_{v,K+1}(o;\widehat C_{v,K+1})}{\check W_{v,1}}
 -\frac{\{\check W_{v,K}-\check W_{v,K+1}\}m_{v,1}(o;\widehat C_{v,1})}
 {\check W_{v,1}^2}.                                                         \tag{29}
\]
For \(i\in\mathcal I_v\), this is exactly \(\widetilde\psi_{i,K}\).  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_v\), (29) is fixed and the validation observations remain independent with law \(P\).

First replace the fitted residual maps in (29), holding the population coefficients fixed.  The ratio influence-function identity and (27) bound the resulting four terms by \(S^G_{P,K,n}\).  Next use (28), \(W_P(1)\geq\underline w\), \(\sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}\), and \(0\leq\ell_C\leq1\) to obtain
\[
 \max_{v,K<K_n}\frac{\|\psi_{v,K}^{\circ}-\psi_{v,K}^{C}\|_{L_2(P)}}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \leq CK_n^2L_n,                                                            \tag{30}
\]
where \(\psi_{v,K}^{C}\) is the same fitted-codebook map with every \(\check W_{v,a}\) replaced by \(W_P(a)\).  Thus no validation-dependent coefficient has been treated as conditionally fixed.

The membership theorem permits the modulus \(\lambda_{P,K}=cK^{-3}\), because a valid quadratic-growth constant remains valid when decreased, and permits a fixed \(M_{P,K}=C\), because a valid margin coefficient remains valid when enlarged.  Together with \(s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1}\) and \(\alpha_{P,K}=1\), these admissible choices give
\[
 \delta^C_{P,K,n}\asymp K^2L_n,\qquad
 Q^C_{P,K,n}\asymp(KL_n)^{1/2},\qquad
 a_n:=\max_{K<K_n}\frac{S^G_{P,K,n}}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \asymp K_n^{5/2}L_n^{1/2}.                                                 \tag{31}
\]
The validity branch in \(Q^C\) requires \(\delta^C_{P,K,n}\leq s_{P,K}\kappa_{P,K}\), which follows from \(K_n^4L_n\to0\), itself implied by (1).  Also \(K_n^2L_n=o(a_n)\).  Equations (27)--(31) consequently yield
\[
 \max_{v,K<K_n}\left\|
 \frac{\psi_{v,K}^{\circ}-\Delta\phi_{P,K}}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right\|_{L_2(P)}=O_P(a_n).                                                \tag{32}
\]

It remains to handle empirical centering and variance.  Put
\[
 X_K(O)=\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O)/\sigma_{P,K},\qquad
 D_{v,K}^{\circ}(O)=\psi_{v,K}^{\circ}(O)/\sigma_{P,K}-X_K(O).
\]
For a validation fold, let \(P_{\mathcal I_v}=|\mathcal I_v|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_v}\delta_{O_{n,i}}\).  On the basin event, the cellwise formulas, (2), (23), and (28) imply \(\sup_{v,K}\{\|X_K\|_\infty+\|D_{v,K}^{\circ}\|_\infty\}\leq C\), whereas (32) gives \(\max_{v,K}P(D_{v,K}^{\circ})^2=O_P(a_n^2)\).  For \(0\leq Z\leq C\), Taylor expansion of \(E e^{t(Z-EZ)}\), together with \(\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq CEZ\), gives conditionally on \(\mathcal F_v\)
\[
 P\!\left(\left|(P_{\mathcal I_v}-P)Z\right|>
 C\left[\sqrt{PZ\,x/|\mathcal I_v|}+x/|\mathcal I_v|\right]
 \middle|\mathcal F_v\right)\leq2e^{-x}.                                  \tag{33}
\]
Apply (33) to the fixed function \(Z=(D_{v,K}^{\circ})^2\), take \(x=3\log(nK_n)\), and use \(|\mathcal I_v|\geq c_{\mathcal I}n\) before taking the union bound over the fixed folds and \(K<K_n\).  Since \(K_n\to\infty\), (31) gives \(\sqrt{\log(nK_n)/n}=o(a_n)\), whence
\[
 \max_{v,K}P_{\mathcal I_v}(D_{v,K}^{\circ})^2
 =O_P\!\left\{a_n^2+a_n\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}
 +\frac{\log(nK_n)}n\right\}=O_P(a_n^2).                                  \tag{34}
\]
Likewise, \(PX_K=0\), \(PX_K^2=1\), and the bounded-exponential calculation gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}|P_{\mathrm{emp},n}X_K^2-1|
 +\max_{K<K_n}|P_{\mathrm{emp},n}X_K|
 =O_P\!\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}\right\}=o_P(1).                \tag{35}
\]
Applying the foldwise conditional version of the same inequality to \(D_{v,K}^{\circ}\), and using the deterministic weights \(|\mathcal I_v|/n\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and (34), gives
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\frac{|\overline\psi_K|}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 =O_P\!\left(a_n+\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}\right)=O_P(a_n).              \tag{36}
\]
Empirical centering therefore contributes only the square of (36) to a second moment.  Finally, for any finite empirical averaging operator \(\mathbb P\) and arrays \(a,b\),
\[
 |\mathbb P a^2-\mathbb P b^2|
 \leq\{\mathbb P(a-b)^2\}^{1/2}\{\mathbb P(a+b)^2\}^{1/2}.                \tag{37}
\]
Apply (37) fold by fold with \(a=\psi_{v,K}^{\circ}/\sigma_{P,K}\) and \(b=X_K\), then use (34)--(36).  This proves the asserted maximum coordinatewise \(L_2(P)\) score discrepancy and, if \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)\to0\), the nonnegative score term in \(\Xi_n\) and (31) give \(a_n=o(1)\).  Hence
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}-1\right|
 =O_P\!\left\{a_n+\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}\right\}=o_P(1).                \tag{38}
\]
Score nondegeneracy and (38) imply \(\min_{K<K_n}\widehat\sigma_K>0\) with probability tending to one.

For completeness, conditionally on the data, the Gaussian maximal inequality and (34)--(38) bound replacement of the empirically centered oracle multiplier array by the fitted array by
\[
 O_P\!\left[w_n(P)\left\{a_n+\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK_n)}n}
 +\max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}-1\right|\right\}\right]. \tag{39}
\]
The definition of \(\Xi_n\), the Gaussian union bound \(w_n(P)\leq C\sqrt{\log(2K_n)}\), and the first term of \(\Xi_n\) make (39) \(o_P(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1})\).  Separately, the global coefficients \(\widehat W_a\) occur only in \(\widehat G_K\), and their perturbations were already decomposed by (25).  Equation (26) and the ratio-remainder term of \(\Xi_n\) give
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}
 \frac{\sqrt n\,|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)-(P_{\mathrm{emp},n}-P)\Delta\phi_{P,K}|}
 {\sigma_{P,K}}
 =o_P\!\left(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1}\right).                                    \tag{40}
\]
All constants and exceptional probabilities above are uniform in \(P\in\mathcal U_n\), because the density, curvature, margin, denominator, variance, and block-size bounds used in (2)--(3) and (21)--(40) are uniform.  This proves the basin, linearization, foldwise score, empirical-variance, and intrinsic-scale conclusions under exactly the displayed positivity, boundary, and limit conditions.
