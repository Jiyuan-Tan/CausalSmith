Fix \(P\in\mathcal U_n\), put \(p_n=K_n-1\), and abbreviate row-law subscripts.  Instantiate \(\texttt{def:corrected-profile-estimator}\) with its fixed-fold construction and the common known nuisances \(e(u)=1/2\), \(\mu_1(u)=u-1/2\), and \(\mu_0(u)=1/2-u\).  By \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\), the admissible moduli may be chosen so that the calculation (31) in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-linearization}\) applies.  Since \(w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}\), the score term in \(\Xi_n(P)\) is bounded below, up to a fixed constant, by \(K_n^{5/2}L_n^{1/2}\log K_n\).  Thus \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)\to0\) implies \(K_n^5L_n\to0\); it also implies \(\delta_nK_n^3\to0\), because \(\delta_n=c_{\mathrm F}\sqrt{\log|J_n|/n}\), \(|J_n|\asymp K_n\), and \(L_n=\sqrt{\log(nK_n)/n}\).  All premises of \(\texttt{thm:uniform-linearization}\) therefore hold, and that theorem proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\Xi_n(P)\to0,
 \qquad
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_K}{\sigma_{P,K}}-1\right|=o_P(1),       \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat G_K-G_P(K)\}}{\widehat\sigma_K}
 -\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O_{n,i})}{\sigma_{P,K}}
 \right|=o_P\!\left(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1}\right)                                  \tag{2}
\]
uniformly.  It also gives the corresponding centered-score replacement bound for the multiplier array.  Score nondegeneracy and (1) imply \(P^n(\min_K\widehat\sigma_K>0)\to1\) uniformly, so every later division is valid on an event of probability tending to one.

We verify rather than assume the hypotheses of the cited high-dimensional approximation.  Put
\[
 X_{iK}=\Delta\phi_{P,K}(O_{n,i})/\sigma_{P,K}.
\]
The observations are independent by the row sampling model, the influence-function definitions give \(E_PX_{iK}=0\), and the definition of \(\sigma_{P,K}\) gives \(E_PX_{iK}^2=1\).  In the noiseless experiment, the optimized-loss representation and the bounds \(s_{P,K}\asymp K^{-1}\), \(W_P(K)\asymp K^{-2}\), and \(\sigma_{P,K}\asymp K^{-2}\) from \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) give
\[
 \max_{P\in\mathcal U_n}\max_{K<K_n}\|X_{iK}\|_\infty\leq C_0.               \tag{3}
\]
Choose \(B\geq\max\{1,C_0,C_0/\log2\}\).  Then, for \(k=1,2\),
\[
 E_P|X_{iK}|^{2+k}\leq C_0^kE_PX_{iK}^2\leq B^k,
 \qquad E_Pe^{|X_{iK}|/B}\leq e^{C_0/B}\leq2.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Thus the exact moment conditions of \(\texttt{lem:high-dimensional-gaussian-multiplier-approximation}\) hold with the common constant \(B_n=B\).  Apply that lemma to the signed \(2p_n\)-coordinate array and \(\gamma_n=(2nK_n)^{-1}\).  Since
\[
 D_n=O\!\left(\{\log^7(2nK_n)/n\}^{1/6}\right)=o(1),                            \tag{5}
\]
the oracle standardized maximum
\[
 T_n^0=\max_{K<K_n}\left|n^{-1/2}\sum_iX_{iK}\right|                           \tag{6}
\]
and the oracle empirically centered Gaussian-multiplier maximum are both approximated, uniformly in distribution functions, by
\[
 M_P=\max_{K<K_n}|Z_{P,K}|.                                                       \tag{7}
\]

The score-replacement term in \(\Xi_n\), (1), and the conditional Cauchy--Schwarz inequality show that replacing \(X_{iK}-\overline X_K\) by \(\widehat{\Delta\phi}_{i,K}/\widehat\sigma_K\) changes the conditional multiplier maximum by \(o_P(\{1+w_n(P)\}^{-1})\).  The Gaussian anti-concentration lemma, with \(p=p_n\), bounds the probability of an interval of this length by \(o(1)\), since \(w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  Combining this fact with (2), (5)--(7) gives
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}\left|
 P^n\!\left\{\max_{K<K_n}\frac{\sqrt n|\widehat G_K-G_P(K)|}{\widehat\sigma_K}\leq t\right\}
 -P\{T_n^{\#}\leq t\mid O_{n,1},\ldots,O_{n,n}\}
 \right|=o_P(1)                                                                  \tag{8}
\]
uniformly.  The conditional quantile-infimum definition of \(q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\) and (8) yield simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\), which is exactly \(\operatorname{Honest}_{\alpha}(B_n^{\#},\mathcal U_n)\).

We next determine the critical-value order.  The covariance stability conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) and \(\delta_nK_n^2=o(1)\), together with \(\texttt{thm:exact-covariance-packing}\), imply eventually
\[
 R_P(K,L)\leq0.3\quad(K\neq L,\ K,L\in J_n),
 \qquad w_n(P)\asymp\sqrt{\log K_n}.                                            \tag{9}
\]
A Gaussian union bound gives the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(M_P\) at most \(C_\alpha\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  For the reverse inequality, let \(X=(Z_{P,K}:K\in J_n)\) and
\[
 Y_K=\sqrt{0.7}\,\varepsilon_K+\sqrt{0.3}\,\varepsilon_0,
\]
where the displayed Gaussian variables are independent and standard normal.  The marginal variances agree and \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_K,X_L)\leq\operatorname{Cov}(Y_K,Y_L)\) off the diagonal by (9).  For a smooth nonincreasing approximation \(f\) to \(\mathbf1\{x\leq t\}\), define \(F(x)=\prod_Kf(x_K)\).  To derive the comparison, first add \(\varepsilon I\) to both covariance matrices.  Differentiating the explicit nonsingular Gaussian density along their affine covariance interpolation gives one half of its mixed second derivative; two ordinary integrations by parts transfer those derivatives to \(F\).  Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) by dominated convergence gives
\[
 \frac d{du}E F(X_u)=\frac12\sum_{K\neq L}
 \{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_K,Y_L)-\operatorname{Cov}(X_K,X_L)\}
 E\{\partial_{KL}F(X_u)\}\geq0,                                                 \tag{10}
\]
because \(\partial_{KL}F=f'(x_K)f'(x_L)\prod_{a\neq K,L}f(x_a)\geq0\).  Passing to the indicator yields
\[
 P(\max_{K\in J_n}X_K\leq t)\leq P(\max_{K\in J_n}Y_K\leq t).                 \tag{11}
\]
Let \(m=|J_n|\), take \(t=c\sqrt{\log m}\) with \(c^2<1.4\), and condition on \(|\varepsilon_0|\leq1\).  The elementary integration-by-parts bound
\[
 1-\Phi(x)\geq\frac{x}{1+x^2}\varphi(x),\qquad x>0,                            \tag{12}
\]
gives
\[
 P(\max_KY_K\leq t\mid\varepsilon_0)
 \leq\Phi\!\left(\frac{t+\sqrt{0.3}}{\sqrt{0.7}}\right)^m\longrightarrow0.    \tag{13}
\]
Indeed \(m\{1-\Phi((t+\sqrt{0.3})/\sqrt{0.7})\}\to\infty\), and \(\Phi(x)^m\leq e^{-m(1-\Phi(x))}\).  Since \(P(|\varepsilon_0|>1)<1/2<1-\alpha\), (11)--(13) put the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(M_P\) above \(c_\alpha\sqrt{\log m}\).  Because \(m=K_n-8\), the Gaussian quantile is of order \(\sqrt{\log K_n}\).  Equations (5), (8), and Gaussian anti-concentration transfer this order to \(q_{n,1-\alpha}^{\#}\).  Combining it with (1) proves all asserted upper half-width relations.

It remains to prove necessity.  For \(j\in J_n\), put \(\theta_j=(G_{P_j}(K):K\in J_n)\) and
\[
 d_\infty(\theta_j,\theta_\ell)=
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{|G_{P_j}(K)-G_{P_\ell}(K)|}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}.
\]
The separation and divergence assertions of \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) give
\[
 d_\infty(\theta_j,\theta_\ell)\geq a_0\delta_n\quad(j\neq\ell),
 \qquad
 \frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cc_{\mathrm F}^2\log m.                                                    \tag{14}
\]
Let an index \(J\) be uniform on \(J_n\), draw the sample from \(P_J^{\otimes n}\), and adjoin the declared independent \(V_n\).  The common independent factor leaves every Kullback--Leibler divergence unchanged.  If \(Q_j\) denotes this joint law and \(\overline Q=m^{-1}\sum_jQ_j\), the log-sum inequality gives
\[
 I(J;O^n,V_n)=m^{-1}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 \leq m^{-1}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q_0)
 \leq Cc_{\mathrm F}^2\log m.                                                    \tag{15}
\]
For any decoder \(\widehat J\), the entropy chain rule and the fact that at most \(m-1\) wrong labels remain after an error give, with \(p_e=P(\widehat J\neq J)\),
\[
 \log m=H(J)\leq I(J;O^n,V_n)+\log2+p_e\log m.                                  \tag{16}
\]
Thus the average decoding error is at least \(1-Cc_{\mathrm F}^2-\log2/\log m\).  Choose the fixed \(c_{\mathrm F}\) sufficiently small that this is eventually larger than \((1+\alpha)/2\).

Given any honest common rectangle, decode by choosing the smallest \(j\) for which \(\theta_j\) lies in the rectangle, with a fixed default if none does.  If the rectangle covers \(\theta_j\) and
\[
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{h_{n,K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}<a_0\delta_n/2,                  \tag{17}
\]
it contains no other \(\theta_\ell\), by (14), and the decoder is correct.  Uniform honesty, (16), and (17) therefore imply
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j\in J_n}P_j^{\otimes n}\!\left\{
 \max_{K\in J_n}\frac{h_{n,K}}{\sigma_{P_0,K}}\geq a_0\delta_n/2\right\}
 \geq(1-\alpha)/2-o(1).                                                          \tag{18}
\]
Some summand is bounded below by a fixed \(c_1>0\).  The uniform variance comparison in \(\texttt{thm:fano-membership}\) replaces \(\sigma_{P_0,K}\) by \(\sigma_{P_j,K}\) at a \(1+o(1)\) factor, and \(\sqrt n\delta_n=c_{\mathrm F}\sqrt{\log m}\).  This proves the displayed lower bound with a fixed \(c_0>0\).  The upper and lower conclusions are therefore matched on the stated noiseless local experiment, and no step asserts positive-noise achievability.
