Put \(p_n=K_n-1\) and
\[
 X_{iK}=\frac{\Delta\phi_{P^{(n)},K}(O_{n,i})}{\sigma_{P^{(n)},K}},
 \qquad 1\leq K<K_n.
\]
The influence-function definition gives \(\mathbb E_{P^{(n)}}X_{iK}=0\).  Independence across \(i\) follows from the row sampling model.  By the definition of \(\sigma_{P^{(n)},K}\) and 
\(\texttt{ass:score-nondegeneracy}\), every division is valid and
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb E X_{iK}^2=1.                                      \tag{1}
\]
By the definition of \(\mathfrak B_n(P^{(n)})\), for \(k=1,2\),
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb E|X_{iK}|^{2+k}
 \leq \mathfrak B_n(P^{(n)})^k,                                               \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{|X_{iK}|/\mathfrak B_n(P^{(n)})\}\leq2.                     \tag{3}
\]
Thus (1)--(3) verify conditions \(\mathrm{(M.1)}\), \(\mathrm{(M.2)}\), and \(\mathrm{(E.1)}\) in \(\texttt{lem:cck-hyperrectangle-multiplier}\), with \(b=1\) and \(B_n=\mathfrak B_n(P^{(n)})\).  The assumed displayed rate is unchanged if \(p_n\) replaces \(K_n\), since \(p_n<K_n\), and it absorbs \(\log(1/\gamma_n)\) for \(\gamma_n=(2nK_n)^{-1}\).  The cited lemma therefore gives the oracle Gaussian and empirically centered multiplier approximations for hyperrectangles.

To pass from a one-sided rectangle to an absolute maximum, replace the \(p_n\)-vector \(X_i\) by the signed \(2p_n\)-vector \((X_i,-X_i)\).  This vector has the same variance lower bound, the same moment bounds, and the same exponential bound, while
\[
 \max_{K<K_n}\left|n^{-1/2}\sum_iX_{iK}\right|
 =\max_{j\leq2p_n}n^{-1/2}\sum_iX_{ij}^{\pm}.                             \tag{4}
\]
Its Gaussian covariance is the signed doubling of \(R_{P^{(n)}}\), so the maximum in (4) has the same law as \(\max_{K<K_n}|Z_{P^{(n)},K}|\).  The multiplier construction in the cited corollary uses
\[
 n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i(X_{iK}-\overline X_K),
 \qquad \overline X_K=n^{-1}\sum_iX_{iK},                                \tag{5}
\]
which is exactly the oracle empirical centering asserted here.  Equations (1)--(5) prove the proposed statement.  No fitted-score replacement has been used; that analytically distinct step remains the responsibility of a uniform linearization and score-stability theorem.
