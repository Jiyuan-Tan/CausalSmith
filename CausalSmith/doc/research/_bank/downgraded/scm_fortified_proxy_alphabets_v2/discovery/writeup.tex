\begin{lemma}[lem:weighted-support-reduction]\label{lem:weighted-support-reduction}
For every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\), multiplication by \(p_\theta\) maps the fortified conditional-mean-zero functions bijectively onto the specialization of \(H_S\) and maps \(L^2(C)\) onto the specialization of \(R_S(x)\). Consequently, \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\) equals the rank of the composite block-column map \(\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal I_S}\mathbb R^u\xrightarrow{\oplus_y\Pi_{\theta,y}}\mathbb R^S\to\mathbb R^S/N_S(p_\theta)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_S\).  Positivity in the definition of \(\Theta_S\) gives \(p_\theta(s)>0\) for every \(s\in S\).  For a supported fiber
\[
F_{\nu,t}=\{(a,z)\in S:z_{-\nu}=t\},\qquad |\nu|=\gamma,
\]
its probability is positive.  Hence the fortified conditional-mean-zero restriction is equivalent to
\[
0=\mathbb E_{P_\theta}\{d(A,Z)\mid Z_{-\nu}=t\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0=\sum_{s\in F_{\nu,t}}p_\theta(s)d(s).
\]
Put \(q(s)=p_\theta(s)d(s)\).  The displayed restrictions are exactly the incidence equations in the specialization of \(H_S\) from \(x_s\) to \(p_\theta(s)\).  Coordinatewise division \(d(s)=q(s)/p_\theta(s)\) is valid by positivity, so multiplication by \(p_\theta\) is a bijection between the two spaces.

Likewise, if \(f\) is a function of \(C\), then
\[
[p_\theta f]_{a,b,c}=p_\theta(a,b,c)f(c)
=\sum_{c'}f(c')[r_{c'}(p_\theta)]_{a,b,c}.
\]
Thus multiplication maps \(L^2(C)\) onto \(R_S(p_\theta)\).  The supported \(c\)-sections are disjoint and their entries are positive, so the displayed spanning vectors are nonzero and every element of their span has the displayed form.

Write \(g_y=(g(v,a,c))_{v\in\mathcal U}\) for \(y=(a,c)\).  Using the factorization defining \(P_\theta\), for \(w=p_\theta(d+f)\in H_S+R_S(p_\theta)\) one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P_\theta}[(d(A,Z)+f(C))g(U,A,C)]
&=\sum_{(a,b,c)\in S}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U}
p_\theta(a,b,c)[\Pi_{\theta,(a,c)}]_{b,v}
(d(a,b,c)+f(c))g(v,a,c)\\
&=\left\langle w,\ \bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal I_S}\Pi_{\theta,y}g_y\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
The vector on the right gives the zero functional on all fortified supply vectors precisely when it lies in
\[
(H_S+R_S(p_\theta))^\perp=N_S(p_\theta).
\]
Therefore the kernel of \(\mathsf T_\theta\) is the kernel of the stated composite map to \(\mathbb R^S/N_S(p_\theta)\).  Two finite-dimensional linear maps with the same domain and the same kernel have the same rank, which proves the claim about \(M_\theta\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction removes law-dependent denominators and exposes the structural-zero problem as a product of block-subspace choices. \textit{Gap.} Yu, Shi, and Tchetgen Tchetgen define the fortified conditional moments, but their finite illustration does not derive a support-incidence representation with heterogeneous alphabets and structural zeros. \textit{Consumer.} The reduction gives a law-independent input to the Hall certificate and prevents a rank failure caused by support from being mistaken for weak association at one law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:product-block-rado]\label{thm:product-block-rado}
After one constant column is separated in every row-stochastic block, let the remaining \(u-1\) contrast columns range independently in \(\overline V_y\). The maximum rank of their images in \(\overline E_S/\overline N_S\) is \(|\mathcal D_S|-\eta(S)\). The maximizing contrast array lies in a nonempty Zariski-open set and has a rational representative; sufficiently small rational perturbation of the uniform row-stochastic blocks realizes it with strictly positive entries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
V=\overline E_S/\overline N_S,\qquad d=|\mathcal D_S|,
\qquad \rho(I)=\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\left(\sum_{(y,j)\in I}W_y\right),
\qquad \delta=\eta(S).
\]
For an arbitrary choice \(w_e\in W_e\), where \(W_{(y,j)}=W_y\), and any \(I\subseteq\mathcal D_S\),
\[
\dim\operatorname{span}\{w_e:e\in\mathcal D_S\}
\le |\mathcal D_S\setminus I|+\rho(I)
=d-\{|I|-\rho(I)\}.
\]
Taking an \(I\) attaining the definition of \(\delta\) proves the upper bound \(d-\delta\).

For the reverse inequality, let \(T\) be a \(\delta\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_S\), and, in \(V\oplus T\), put \(W'_e=W_e\oplus T\).  For every nonempty \(I\), the definition of \(\delta\) gives
\[
\dim\left(\sum_{e\in I}W'_e\right)=\rho(I)+\delta\ge |I|;
\]
the empty-set inequality is automatic.  The cited linear independent-transversal theorem therefore supplies independent vectors \(w'_e=w_e+t_e\in W_e\oplus T\) for all \(e\in\mathcal D_S\).  Their span has dimension \(d\), while the kernel of its projection to \(V\) has dimension at most \(\dim T=\delta\).  Consequently
\[
\dim\operatorname{span}\{w_e:e\in\mathcal D_S\}\ge d-\delta.
\]
Together with the upper bound this proves that the maximum is \(d-\eta(S)\).

Choose bases of the \(\overline V_y\) and lift the vectors just constructed to those bases.  A \((d-\delta)\)-minor of the resulting coordinate matrix is nonzero.  Thus the arrays attaining the maximum contain the nonempty Zariski-open set on which this minor is nonzero; conversely, no larger minor can be nonzero by the preceding upper bound.  Since \(\mathbb F_S=\mathbb Q(x_s:s\in S)\) is infinite, successive avoidance of the finitely many roots at each coordinate produces a point of this open set with coordinates in \(\mathbb F_S\), which is the asserted rational-function representative.

It remains to verify the stochastic and positivity restrictions.  In each block choose a basis of \(\mathbb F_S^u\) whose first vector is \(\mathbf 1_u\).  A row-stochastic matrix sends this vector to \(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal B_y}\); its other \(u-1\) images can be prescribed arbitrarily.  Modulo the constant image, those prescriptions range independently over \(\overline V_y\).  The uniform table sends all contrast basis vectors to zero.  Hence scaling the prescribed contrast images by a nonzero scalar does not change their span, and sufficiently small real scalings make every entry of the resulting row-stochastic tables strictly positive.

More explicitly, the nonzero minor above becomes a nonzero rational function of the \(x_s\) and of the contrast coordinates.  After clearing denominators it has a nonzero polynomial numerator with rational coefficients.  A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on a nonempty Euclidean open set: this follows by induction on the number of variables, since a nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many roots.  Therefore every sufficiently small neighborhood of the uniform tables, together with the positive orthant for the \(x_s\), contains a rational point where that numerator is nonzero.  Normalize the positive rational \(x_s\) to sum to one; common scaling does not change \(R_S(x)\), \(N_S(x)\), or rank.  This gives strictly positive rational row-stochastic blocks and proves the final realization assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the early kill test and the mathematical hinge converting local structural zeros into an exact global rank loss. \textit{Gap.} Rado characterizes independent representatives from prescribed subspaces, while the fortified problem requires simultaneous subspace choices, repeated latent contrasts, a block-constant quotient, and rational positive realization. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem supplies the missing attainment step needed by every support-specific completeness diagnostic in the proposal.

\begin{theorem}[thm:generic-rank-formula]\label{thm:generic-rank-formula}
There exists \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\) with rational positive entries such that \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)\). Moreover, \(r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)\), and the same rank holds on a nonempty relative Zariski-open, Euclidean-dense, full-Lebesgue-measure subset of \(\Theta_S\). If \(\kappa_0(S)+\eta(S)>0\), then \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta<Q_S\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(n=|\mathcal I_S|\) and \(d=n(u-1)=|\mathcal D_S|\).  By the weighted support reduction, rank is the dimension of the image of the block-column map modulo \(N_S(x)\).  In each row-stochastic block change the domain basis so that its first basis vector is \(\mathbf 1_u\).  The \(n\) first columns then span \(J_S\), whose image modulo \(N_S(x)\) has dimension
\[
\dim J_S-\dim(N_S(x)\cap J_S)=n-\kappa_0(S).
\]
After quotienting by that image, the remaining column indexed by \((y,j)\in\mathcal D_S\) ranges in
\[
(\overline V_y+\overline N_S)/\overline N_S=W_y.
\]
The product-block Rado theorem therefore gives the exact symbolic maximum
\[
(n-\kappa_0(S))+(d-\eta(S))
=un-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)
=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S). \tag{1}
\]

The realization part of that theorem supplies a positive rational specialization \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\) at which a minor of order equal to the right side of (1) is nonzero.  Thus \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}\) is at least that number.  No larger minor is nonzero over \(\mathbb F_S\), by (1).  If a larger minor were nonzero at some compatible numerical parameter, its determinant polynomial, restricted to the affine row-stochastic parameter space, would not be the zero polynomial, contradicting the symbolic maximum.  Hence equality holds at \(\theta_0\), and (1) is the generic rank.

Fix the nonzero maximal minor used above and clear its denominators.  Its nonvanishing locus inside \(\Theta_S\) is a nonempty relative Zariski-open set.  The parameter space is relatively open in the affine product of one probability simplex and the row-stochastic affine spaces.  By the elementary polynomial argument in the product-block proof, the zero set of a nonzero polynomial has empty relative interior and Lebesgue measure zero; the measure assertion follows by induction and Fubini from the corresponding univariate fact.  Its complement is therefore Euclidean dense and has full relative Lebesgue measure.  On that complement the selected minor is nonzero, while every larger minor vanishes identically, so the rank equals (1).

Finally, if \(\kappa_0(S)+\eta(S)>0\), equation (1) is strictly smaller than \(Q_S\).  Were \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta=Q_S\) at any \(\theta\in\Theta_S\), some \(Q_S\)-minor would be nonzero there and hence would not vanish identically, again contradicting (1).  Thus the failure is uniform over all compatible positive parameters.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The formula separates forced constant-direction loss from the genuine quotient Hall defect and converts an abstract rank upper bound into compatible positive tables. \textit{Gap.} Miao, Geng, and Tchetgen Tchetgen require categorical proxy rank, and the banked rectangular result treats equal alphabets with full support; neither gives rank from an arbitrary declared support. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can determine whether the fortified outcome-completeness route is generically feasible or impossible at every compatible positive law before fitting bridge models.

\begin{theorem}[thm:hall-full-rank-frontier]\label{thm:hall-full-rank-frontier}
The fortified pairing has generic full row rank if and only if \(\kappa_0(S)=0\) and \(\dim_{\mathbb F_S}(\sum_{(y,j)\in I}W_y)\ge|I|\) for every \(I\subseteq\mathcal D_S\). Equivalently, generic full row rank holds if and only if \(\kappa_0(S)=0\) and \(\eta(S)=0\). Failure of either condition is a law-uniform failure of this pairing route, not a claim that \(\tau\) is unidentified.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The generic-rank formula gives
\[
r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S).
\]
Both terms subtracted are nonnegative integers: \(\kappa_0(S)\) is a dimension, and \(\eta(S)\ge0\) because the empty set in its defining maximum has value zero.  Hence \(r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=Q_S\) if and only if both terms vanish.

For \(I\subseteq\mathcal D_S\), write
\[
\rho(I)=\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\left(\sum_{(y,j)\in I}W_y\right).
\]
By definition,
\[
\eta(S)=\max_{I\subseteq\mathcal D_S}\{|I|-\rho(I)\}.
\]
Since the maximum includes the value zero at \(I=\varnothing\), it is zero exactly when \(|I|-\rho(I)\le0\) for every \(I\), which is exactly the displayed family of Hall inequalities.  This proves both equivalences.

If either condition fails, the last part of the generic-rank theorem shows \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta<Q_S\) at every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\).  This conclusion concerns only injectivity of the fortified pairing map.  The frozen assumptions contain neither a pair of observationally equivalent structural causal models with different \(\tau\) nor a completeness theorem asserting that this pairing condition is necessary for every possible identification route.  Therefore the proved implication is precisely law-uniform failure of this pairing route and does not logically imply nonidentification of \(\tau\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The all-subfamilies inequalities are the exact structural-support frontier rather than a global supply-versus-demand count. \textit{Gap.} Published proximal rank conditions are law-level conditions, and generic partitioned-matrix theorems allow different parameter dependence than the stochastic product blocks here. \textit{Consumer.} The frontier gives a deficient subset of treatment-by-complement blocks that explains why an apparently adequate aggregate alphabet cannot support fortified completeness.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-defect-algorithm]\label{thm:exact-defect-algorithm}
Given \(S\), \(u\), and access to the quotient-rank oracle \(\mathcal O_S\), there is an exact oracle-model algorithm that returns \(\kappa_0(S)\), \(\eta(S)\), \(r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)\), and a subset \(I^*\subseteq\mathcal D_S\) attaining the maximum that defines \(\eta(S)\).  The submodular-minimization stage uses a strongly polynomial number of calls to \(\mathcal O_S\) as a function of \(|\mathcal D_S|\).  Every oracle value and the auxiliary computation of \(\kappa_0(S)\) are obtained by exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_S\).  This is an exact oracle-call and field-operation reduction; it makes no polynomial, or strongly polynomial, bit-complexity claim for representing or manipulating rational functions.  The returned \(I^*\) is a witnessing deficient subset, with no claim of a separate succinct optimality certificate beyond the correctness of the submodular-minimization algorithm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an element \(e=(y,j)\in\mathcal D_S\), write \(W_e=W_y\).  For every \(I\subseteq\mathcal D_S\), define
\[
 U_I=\sum_{e\in I}W_e,
 \qquad
 \rho(I)=\dim_{\mathbb F_S}U_I=\mathcal O_S(I),
 \qquad
 F(I)=\rho(I)-|I|.
\]
These quantities are well defined because \(\mathcal D_S\) is finite and every \(W_e\) is a subspace of the finite-dimensional quotient \(\overline E_S/\overline N_S\).  For \(I,J\subseteq\mathcal D_S\), the dimension formula for two finite-dimensional subspaces gives
\[
 \rho(I)+\rho(J)
 =\dim_{\mathbb F_S}(U_I+U_J)
  +\dim_{\mathbb F_S}(U_I\cap U_J).
\]
By the definitions of the sums, \(U_I+U_J=U_{I\cup J}\), while \(U_{I\cap J}\subseteq U_I\cap U_J\).  Monotonicity of dimension therefore yields
\[
 \rho(I)+\rho(J)
 \geq \rho(I\cup J)+\rho(I\cap J).
\]
Thus \(\rho\) is an integer-valued submodular function.  Since
\[
 |I|+|J|=|I\cup J|+|I\cap J|,
\]
the function \(I\mapsto |I|\) is modular, and consequently \(F=\rho-|\cdot|\) is integer-valued and submodular.

Apply \(\textnormal{lem:schrijver-strongly-polynomial-sfm}\) to \(F\), using one call to \(\mathcal O_S\) to obtain \(\rho(I)\) at each value-oracle query and computing \(|I|\) directly.  It returns a minimizer \(I^*\) after a number of quotient-rank-oracle calls polynomial in \(|\mathcal D_S|\).  By \(\textnormal{def:hall-defect}\),
\[
 \eta(S)
 =\max_{I\subseteq\mathcal D_S}\bigl\{|I|-\rho(I)\bigr\}
 =-\min_{I\subseteq\mathcal D_S}F(I)
 =|I^*|-\mathcal O_S(I^*).
\]
Hence both \(\eta(S)\) and a subset attaining its defining maximum are returned exactly.  This equality is the asserted witness property of \(I^*\); no additional independently checkable optimality certificate is being asserted.

It remains to compute the constant-direction defect.  From the finite support \(S\), construct over \(\mathbb F_S\) the coordinate matrices for \(D_S\), the vectors \(r_c(x)\), and a basis of \(J_S\), as specified in \(\textnormal{def:rank-oracle}\).  Exact Gaussian elimination gives successively
\[
 H_S=\ker D_S,
 \qquad
 R_S(x)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_S}\{r_c(x):c\in\operatorname{proj}_C(S)\},
 \qquad
 L_S(x)=H_S+R_S(x),
\]
and then, by solving the homogeneous linear equations for the coordinate pairing,
\[
 N_S(x)=L_S(x)^\perp.
\]
The same exact elimination applied to concatenated bases for \(N_S(x)\) and \(J_S\) computes
\[
 \kappa_0(S)
 =\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\bigl(N_S(x)\cap J_S\bigr)
 =\dim_{\mathbb F_S}N_S(x)+\dim_{\mathbb F_S}J_S
  -\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\bigl(N_S(x)+J_S\bigr).
\]
All divisions made during elimination are by nonzero elements of the field \(\mathbb F_S\), so these ranks and dimensions are exact rational-function quantities rather than numerical specializations.

Finally, \(Q_S=u|\mathcal I_S|\) is obtained by counting the declared blocks, and \(\textnormal{thm:generic-rank-formula}\) gives
\[
 r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S).
\]
This computes every claimed output.  The cited submodular-minimization result controls only the number of value-oracle calls, while the remaining work consists of finitely many exact field operations and exact Gaussian eliminations over \(\mathbb F_S\).  Because the representation size and bit cost of rational-function arithmetic have not been bounded, the argument proves no polynomial bit-complexity statement.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem turns exponentially many Hall inequalities into an executable exact certificate with a named obstruction. \textit{Gap.} Schrijver supplies oracle submodular minimization and Hirai and Iwamasa handle a narrow independently parameterized block-matrix class; neither constructs the fortified quotient rank oracle. \textit{Consumer.} Sensitivity analyses over defended proxy subsets can report the precise support blocks responsible for route failure instead of only a failed matrix-rank check.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rectangular-special-case]\label{prop:rectangular-special-case}
If \(m_i=m\) for every \(i\), \(S=\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Z\), and \(|\alpha|=\gamma\), then \(Q_S=2u m^{K-\gamma}\) and \(Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)=\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}\), where \(D_{K,m}(\gamma)=m^K+\sum_{j=K-\gamma+1}^K\binom Kj(m-1)^j+m^{K-\gamma}\). Thus the Hall frontier reduces exactly to \(D_{K,m}(\gamma)\ge Q_S\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
q=K-\gamma,\qquad b=m^\gamma,\qquad c=m^q,
\qquad
d_0=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\binom Kj(m-1)^j.
\tag{1}
\]
There are \(c\) values of \(C\) and two treatment values, so
\(\lvert\mathcal I_S\rvert=2c\).  The definition of \(Q_S\) therefore gives
\[
Q_S=u\lvert\mathcal I_S\rvert=2uc=2u m^{K-\gamma}.
\tag{2}
\]

We first compute the fortified supply.  For each coordinate \(i\), decompose the
space of functions on \(\mathcal Z_i\) as the direct sum of the constant line and
the \((m-1)\)-dimensional zero-sum contrast space \(\mathscr C_i\).  Tensoring
these decompositions gives the direct ANOVA decomposition
\[
\mathbb F_S^{\mathcal Z}
=\bigoplus_{T\subseteq[K]}\mathscr X_T,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\mathscr X_T=(m-1)^{\lvert T\rvert},
\tag{3}
\]
where functions in \(\mathscr X_T\) are contrasts precisely in the coordinates
in \(T\).  A row of \(D_S\) associated with \(\nu\) is constant in the treatment
coordinate and in all coordinates in \(\nu\).  As its fixed value
\(z_{-\nu}\) varies, those rows span all treatment-constant functions of
\(z_{-\nu}\).  It follows from the direct sum in (3) that
\[
\operatorname{row}(D_S)
=\bigoplus_{\substack{T\subseteq[K]:\ T\subseteq\nu^c
\text{ for some }\lvert\nu\rvert=\gamma}}
\mathscr X_T.
\tag{4}
\]
A set \(T\) is contained in such a \(\nu^c\) if and only if
\(\lvert T\rvert\le q\).  Thus (1), (3), and (4) imply
\[
\operatorname{rank}D_S=d_0,
\qquad
\dim H_S=2m^K-d_0
=m^K+\sum_{j=q+1}^{K}\binom Kj(m-1)^j.
\tag{5}
\]

The \(c\) vectors \(r_c(x)\) have pairwise disjoint nonempty supports and hence
are linearly independent.  If
\(\sum_c\lambda_c r_c(x)\in H_S\), use in the definition of \(H_S\) the
constraint \(\nu=\alpha\) at the fixed complement value \(c\).  It gives
\[
\lambda_c\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{b\in\prod_{i\in\alpha}\mathcal Z_i}
x_{(a,b,c)}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
The displayed sum is a nonzero element of the rational-function field
\(\mathbb F_S\), so \(\lambda_c=0\) for every \(c\).  Consequently
\(H_S\cap R_S(x)=\{0\}\), and (5) yields
\[
\dim L_S(x)=2m^K-d_0+c
=m^K+\sum_{j=q+1}^{K}\binom Kj(m-1)^j+m^q
=D_{K,m}(\gamma).
\tag{7}
\]

We also determine the block-constant kernel.  By (4), every vector in
\(H_S^\perp\) is treatment-constant and is represented by a function
\(g\) in the interaction space
\[
\mathscr G=\bigoplus_{\lvert T\rvert\le q}\mathscr X_T,
\qquad \dim\mathscr G=d_0.
\tag{8}
\]
Orthogonality to \(r_c(x)\) is the equation
\[
\ell_c(g):=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_b x_{(a,b,c)}g(b,c)=0.
\tag{9}
\]
The equations in (9) are independent: \(\mathscr G\) contains every function
of \(C\), so the indicator of any single \(C\)-cell isolates the corresponding
equation, whose coefficient sum is nonzero.  In particular,
\(\dim N_S(x)=d_0-c\).  If a vector of \(N_S(x)\) also lies in \(J_S\), treatment
constancy and block constancy make it equal to a scalar \(t_c\) throughout both
\(B\)-blocks over \(c\).  Equation (9) becomes
\[
t_c\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}=0,
\]
and hence \(t_c=0\) for every \(c\).  Therefore
\[
\kappa_0(S)=0.
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to show that the row-stochastic product blocks attain the smaller of
the demand dimension in (2) and the supply dimension in (7).  We first prove the
needed block-measurement fact.  For a complement cell \(c\), let
\(\mathscr W_c\subseteq\mathscr G^*\) be the space of restrictions to
\(\mathscr G\) of all linear functionals supported on the \(B\)-fiber over
\(c\).  Write
\[
w_c(g)=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_b x_{(a,b,c)}g(b,c)\in\mathscr W_c,
\qquad W_0=\operatorname{span}\{w_c:c\in\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}\}.
\]
For \(J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}\), set
\[
\rho(J)=\dim\left(\sum_{c\in J}\mathscr W_c\right).
\tag{11}
\]
This is a normalized, monotone, submodular rank function.  Independent
permutations of the \(m\) labels in the coordinates of \(C\) act transitively
on its \(c\) cells, preserve the interaction-order space \(\mathscr G\), and
therefore preserve \(\rho\).  Moreover, all coordinate functionals together
separate \(\mathscr G\), so \(\rho(\mathcal Z_{-\alpha})=d_0\).

We claim that
\[
\rho(J)\ge \frac{d_0}{c}\lvert J\rvert
\quad\text{for every }J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, let \(\Gamma\) be the finite transitive permutation group just
described.  The family \((\sigma J)_{\sigma\in\Gamma}\) covers each complement
cell exactly
\(k=\lvert\Gamma\rvert\lvert J\rvert/c\) times.  For completeness, the
fractional-cover inequality for a normalized monotone submodular function says
that a \(k\)-cover \((A_s)_s\) of a finite ground set \(E\) satisfies
\(\sum_s\rho(A_s)\ge k\rho(E)\).  To verify it, order the elements of \(E\),
expand each \(\rho(A_s)\) into successive marginal increments in that order,
and use diminishing returns to bound the increment contributed when an element
is added to \(A_s\) below by its increment after all earlier elements of \(E\)
have been added.  Summing counts every such full-set increment at least \(k\)
times and telescopes to \(k\rho(E)\).  Applying this inequality to the translates
of \(J\), and using \(\rho(\sigma J)=\rho(J)\), proves (12).

Now take \(r\) replicas of every \(\mathscr W_c\), where \(1\le r\le b\).
The frozen Rado independent-transversal lemma implies that the largest possible
rank of \(r\) fiber-supported rows per complement cell is
\[
R_r=rc-\max_{J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}}
\{r\lvert J\rvert-\rho(J)\}
=\min_{J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}}
\{r(c-\lvert J\rvert)+\rho(J)\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Here is the reduction from the stated all-elements form of Rado to (13).  If
\(\delta\) denotes the maximum defect in (13), every selection has rank at most
\(rc-\delta\).  Conversely, enlarge \(\mathscr G^*\) by an auxiliary
\(\delta\)-dimensional space and enlarge every replicated subspace by that same
space.  The definition of \(\delta\) makes every Rado inequality hold in the
enlarged space.  Rado therefore selects all \(rc\) vectors independently there;
their projections to \(\mathscr G^*\) span at least \(rc-\delta\) dimensions.
For a fixed support \(J\), the defect is maximized by taking all \(r\) replicas
over every cell in \(J\), which gives exactly (13).

If \(rc\le d_0\), (12) makes every term in the last minimum in (13) at least
\(rc\), and the choice \(J=\varnothing\) gives equality.  If \(rc\ge d_0\),
(12) makes every term at least \(d_0\), and \(J=\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}\) gives
equality.  Thus
\[
R_r=\min\{d_0,rc\}.
\tag{14}
\]
We now prove that the same rank is attainable when one prescribed row in each
fiber is \(w_c\), which is the constraint needed below.  The \(w_c\)'s are
independent: evaluating a linear combination on the indicator of a single
\(C\)-cell isolates its coefficient and multiplies it by the nonzero element
\(\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}\).  More generally, for every \(J\),
\[
W_0\cap\sum_{c\in J}\mathscr W_c
=\operatorname{span}\{w_c:c\in J\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The inclusion from right to left is immediate.  Conversely, if
\(\sum_c\lambda_cw_c\) is supported on the fibers in \(J\), evaluate it on the
indicator of a cell \(c_0\notin J\).  Every element of
\(\sum_{c\in J}\mathscr W_c\) vanishes there, whereas the displayed linear
combination has value
\(\lambda_{c_0}\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c_0)}\).  Hence
\(\lambda_{c_0}=0\), proving (15).

Let \(\widetilde{\mathscr W}_c\) be the image of \(\mathscr W_c\) in
\(\mathscr G^*/W_0\).  Equation (15) gives
\[
\dim\left(\sum_{c\in J}\widetilde{\mathscr W}_c\right)
=\rho(J)-|J|.
\tag{16}
\]
Apply the Rado rank formula to \(r-1\) replicas of every
\(\widetilde{\mathscr W}_c\).  Their maximum rank is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(r-1)c-\max_J\{(r-1)|J|-(\rho(J)-|J|)\}\\
&\qquad=(r-1)c-\max_J\{r|J|-\rho(J)\}=R_r-c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Lifting such representatives and adjoining the \(c\) fixed independent rows
\((w_c)_c\) therefore gives total rank \(R_r\).  This proves constrained
attainment, rather than inferring it by specializing an unrestricted generic
choice.

For completeness, make the simultaneous genericity step explicit.  Parameterize
the \(r-1\) free rows in each full \(B\)-fiber dual.  The restrictions onto
\(\mathscr G\) are surjective onto \(\mathscr W_c\), so the nonvanishing of a
maximal minor realizing the constrained rank \(R_r\) is a nonempty Zariski-open
condition on this irreducible affine parameter space.  Requiring, in every
fiber, that \(w_c\) together with its \(r-1\) free rows have row rank \(r\) is
also a nonempty Zariski-open condition because \(w_c\ne0\) and \(r\le b\).
Over the infinite field \(\mathbb F_S\), these finitely many nonempty opens
intersect.  Thus the constrained rank and all per-fiber full-row-rank conditions
hold simultaneously.

We apply this constrained form of (14) to the stochastic blocks.  First suppose
\(u<b\).  Let
\(\mathscr P_{a,c}\) be the \(u\)-dimensional column space of a full-column-rank
row-stochastic block \(\Pi_{(a,c)}\); it contains \(\mathbf 1_b\).  Define
\[
h=\max\{0,2u-b-1\},
\qquad r=b-h.
\tag{18}
\]
Choose the constrained rows above for this \(r\), and let \(\mathscr Q_c\) be
their common kernel in the \(c\)-th full fiber.  Per-fiber row rank gives
\(\dim\mathscr Q_c=b-r=h\), and the presence of the prescribed row \(w_c\)
gives \(\mathscr Q_c\subseteq\ker w_c\).

Two generic \(u\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_S^b\) containing
\(\mathbf 1_b\) intersect in dimension \(\max\{1,2u-b\}\).  Since
\(w_c^\top\mathbf 1_b\ne0\), their intersection with
\(\ker w_c^\top\) has dimension \(h\).  Conversely, every \(h\)-subspace
\(\mathscr Q_c\subseteq\ker w_c^\top\) can be realized as
\[
\mathscr Q_c
=\mathscr P_{0,c}\cap\mathscr P_{1,c}\cap\ker w_c^\top.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(h>0\), take the common intersection to be
\(\langle\mathbf 1_b\rangle\oplus\mathscr Q_c\), whose dimension is
\(2u-b\), and split its \(2(b-u)\)-dimensional complement into two
\((b-u)\)-dimensional summands.  Adjoining one summand to the common intersection
defines each \(\mathscr P_{a,c}\).  For \(h=0\), choose two
\((u-1)\)-dimensional complements to the constant line with zero mutual
intersection; this is possible because \(2(u-1)\le b-1\).

Use the simultaneously chosen constrained rows to define \(\mathscr Q_c\), and
then realize that kernel by the two block column spaces as in (19).  Equations
(8), (9), (17), and (19) now show that
\[
\dim\left(
N_S(x)\cap\bigoplus_{a,c}\mathscr P_{a,c}
\right)
=d_0-R_r
=\max\{0,d_0-rc\}.
\tag{20}
\]
By the frozen weighted-support reduction, the rank of the pairing is the
dimension of the image of \(\bigoplus_{a,c}\mathscr P_{a,c}\) modulo
\(N_S(x)\).  Its dimension is therefore
\[
2uc-\max\{0,d_0-rc\}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(2u\le b+1\), then \(h=0\), \(r=b\), and \(d_0<bc\), so (21) equals
\(2uc=Q_S\).  In this case
\[
D_{K,m}(\gamma)-Q_S
=(2b+1-2u)c-d_0\ge bc-d_0>0.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(2u>b+1\), then \(r=2(b-u)+1\), and (7) gives
\[
d_0-rc=2uc-D_{K,m}(\gamma).
\tag{23}
\]
Substitution of (23) into (21) again gives
\(\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}\).

If \(u\ge b\), a generic \(b\)-by-\(u\) row-stochastic block has row rank
\(b\), so the direct sum of its column spaces is all of \(E_S\).  The quotient
map is then surjective and its rank is \(\dim L_S(x)=D_{K,m}(\gamma)\).  Because
\(d_0\ge c\), (7) gives \(D_{K,m}(\gamma)\le2bc\le2uc=Q_S\), so this is also
the required minimum.

All attainments above are algebraic.  More explicitly, a maximal minor is
nonzero on a nonempty Zariski-open set of the cell weights and block contrasts.
A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on the open positive orthant, so the
cell weights may be chosen positive and rational.  Common rescaling of all cell
weights changes neither \(R_S(x)\), nor \(N_S(x)\), nor any rank, and therefore
normalizes those weights to the probability simplex.  For the blocks, positivity
is seen directly: given any rational subspace \(\mathscr P_{a,c}\) containing
\(\mathbf 1_b\), map \(\mathbf 1_u\) to \(\mathbf 1_b\), map a basis of the
contrast subspace of \(\mathbb Q^u\) to a basis of a complement in
\(\mathscr P_{a,c}\), and multiply the latter images by a sufficiently small
positive rational number.  The resulting matrix is a strictly positive
row-stochastic perturbation of the uniform block and has column space
\(\mathscr P_{a,c}\).  Hence the rank just computed is the generic rank over
\(\Theta_S\):
\[
r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}.
\tag{24}
\]

The established generic-rank formula and (10), (24) now imply
\[
Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)
=\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus generic full row rank holds exactly when
\(D_{K,m}(\gamma)\ge Q_S\).  By the established Hall full-rank frontier, this is
exactly the reduction of the Hall inequalities in the rectangular full-support
case.  As a smallest exact check, when \(K=2\), \(\gamma=1\), and \(m=2\), one
has \(d_0=3\), \(D_{2,2}(1)=7\); hence (25) gives rank \(7\) for \(u=2\) and
full rank \(4\) for \(u=1\), agreeing with both sides of the asserted minimum.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction checks that the support theorem contains the banked rectangular result rather than replacing it with an unrelated analogue. \textit{Gap.} The prior proposal proves only this symmetric full-support boundary and explicitly leaves heterogeneous alphabets and structural zeros unresolved. \textit{Consumer.} Existing full-support applications retain their arithmetic diagnostic, while sparse-support applications gain the strictly finer Hall certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-rank-classification]\label{thm:uniform-rank-classification}
For every nonempty declared support \(S\), the following classification holds.  If \(u\geq 2\), there is a parameter \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\) whose block tables are uniform and for which
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}\leq |\mathcal I_S|<Q_S.
\]
Consequently no support can guarantee \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta=Q_S\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\).  If, in addition, \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\), then there is a strictly positive compatible affine family \(\theta(t)\), defined for all sufficiently small positive and negative \(t\), such that \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta(0)}<Q_S\) and \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta(t)}=Q_S\) for every sufficiently small \(t\neq0\).

If \(u=1\), law-uniform full row rank holds if and only if there is no nonzero real vector \(j\in J_S\cap H_S^\perp\), where the orthogonal complement is taken after base change of the incidence equations to \(\mathbb R\), with the following support-sign property: at every \(c\) supporting only one treatment level \(a\), \(j_{a,c}=0\); and at every \(c\) supporting both treatment levels, either \(j_{0,c}=j_{1,c}=0\) or \(j_{0,c}j_{1,c}<0\).  This is a finite exact sign-feasibility characterization determined by \(S\) and \(D_S\).  Every such vector constructs a rank-deficient \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\).  If also \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\), that \(\theta_0\) can be embedded in a strictly positive compatible affine family having full row rank for every sufficiently small \(t\neq0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(N_S(p)=(H_S+R_S(p))^\perp\) for the real specialization of the symbolic block-null space at a strictly positive mass vector \(p\).  The weighted-support reduction lemma identifies \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\) with the rank of
\[
\Phi_\theta:\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal I_S}\mathbb R^u
\longrightarrow \mathbb R^S/N_S(p_\theta),
\qquad
(g_y)_y\longmapsto
\left[\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal I_S}\Pi_{\theta,y}g_y\right].
\tag{1}
\]
This reduction is applicable because the definition of \(\Theta_S\) gives \(p_\theta(s)>0\) on every \(s\in S\), which is exactly the positivity needed for the coordinatewise division in that lemma.

Suppose first that \(u\geq2\).  The support \(S\) is finite and nonempty, so set \(p_0(s)=|S|^{-1}\) for \(s\in S\), and put
\[
[\Pi_{0,y}]_{b,v}=u^{-1}
\qquad
(y\in\mathcal I_S,\ b\in\mathcal B_y,\ v\in\mathcal U).
\tag{2}
\]
All entries in (2) are positive and every row sums to one; hence \(\theta_0=(p_0,(\Pi_{0,y})_y)\) belongs to \(\Theta_S\).  The image of each block map \(\Pi_{0,y}:\mathbb R^u\to\mathbb R^{\mathcal B_y}\) is the line spanned by \(\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_y}\).  Therefore the image before the quotient in (1) has dimension at most \(|\mathcal I_S|\), and passage to a quotient cannot increase dimension.  The weighted-support reduction gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}
=\operatorname{rank}\Phi_{\theta_0}
\leq |\mathcal I_S|.
\tag{3}
\]
Nonemptiness of \(S\) implies \(|\mathcal I_S|\geq1\), while the definition of \(Q_S\) and \(u\geq2\) give
\[
|\mathcal I_S|<u|\mathcal I_S|=Q_S.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) prove law-uniform full rank impossible for every nonempty support, without a Hall-feasibility hypothesis.

Now additionally suppose \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\).  The generic-rank theorem supplies a rational positive parameter \(\theta_1\in\Theta_S\) such that \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_1}=Q_S\).  In the preceding construction choose \(p_0=p_{\theta_1}\), retain the uniform tables \(\Pi_{0,y}\), and define
\[
p_{\theta(t)}=p_{\theta_1},
\qquad
\Pi_{\theta(t),y}=\Pi_{0,y}+t(\Pi_{\theta_1,y}-\Pi_{0,y}).
\tag{5}
\]
Every row sum in (5) equals one.  Because there are finitely many table entries, their strict positivity at \(t=0\) supplies a number \(\delta>0\) such that \(\theta(t)\in\Theta_S\) whenever \(|t|<\delta\).  With \(p\) fixed, choose fixed bases for the domain in (1) and for the fortified supply \(H_S+R_S(p_{\theta_1})\).  The entries of the resulting pairing matrix are affine functions of \(t\).  Since its rank at \(t=1\) is \(Q_S\), some \(Q_S\)-minor has determinant \(\Delta(t)\) satisfying \(\Delta(1)\neq0\).  Thus \(\Delta\) is a nonzero univariate polynomial.  Equation (3) gives \(\Delta(0)=0\).  If \(k\geq1\) is the least exponent with nonzero coefficient \(c\), then \(\Delta(t)=t^k\{c+tq(t)\}\) for a polynomial \(q\).  Because \(q\) is bounded on a sufficiently small compact interval about zero, after decreasing \(\delta\) there is \(\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)\) such that \(|tq(t)|<|c|/2\) whenever \(|t|<\varepsilon\).  Hence
\[
\Delta(t)\neq0\qquad\text{whenever }0<|t|<\varepsilon.
\tag{6}
\]
The demand space has dimension \(Q_S\), so (6) is equivalent to \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta(t)}=Q_S\).  This proves the asserted two-sided crossing for \(u\geq2\).

Suppose next that \(u=1\).  Row stochasticity forces \(\Pi_{\theta,y}=\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_y}\) for every block.  The domain of (1) has one scalar coordinate \(j_{a,c}\) for every \(y=(a,c)\in\mathcal I_S\), and its image before quotienting is the block-constant vector \(j\in J_S\) with value \(j_{a,c}\) on \(\mathcal B_{(a,c)}\).  Consequently a demand vector belongs to the kernel precisely when
\[
j\in N_S(p_\theta)\cap J_S
=H_S^\perp\cap R_S(p_\theta)^\perp\cap J_S.
\tag{7}
\]
Here the equality is the elementary identity \((H_S+R_S(p_\theta))^\perp=H_S^\perp\cap R_S(p_\theta)^\perp\).

For a supported complement value \(c\), define the positive block total
\[
w_{a,c}(p_\theta)
=\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_{(a,c)}}p_\theta(a,b,c)>0
\qquad ((a,c)\in\mathcal I_S).
\tag{8}
\]
The strict inequality follows because every block is nonempty by the definition of \(\mathcal I_S\) and every supported cell mass is positive.  Since \(R_S(p_\theta)\) is spanned by the vectors \(r_c(p_\theta)\), its orthogonality condition in (7) is, for each supported \(c\),
\[
0=\langle j,r_c(p_\theta)\rangle
=\sum_{a:(a,c)\in\mathcal I_S}w_{a,c}(p_\theta)j_{a,c}.
\tag{9}
\]
If only one treatment level \(a\) is supported at \(c\), positivity in (8) makes (9) equivalent to \(j_{a,c}=0\).  If both treatment levels are supported, a positive linear combination of \(j_{0,c}\) and \(j_{1,c}\) can vanish if and only if both numbers vanish or their product is strictly negative.  Thus every rank-deficient \(\theta\) produces a nonzero vector in \(J_S\cap H_S^\perp\) with exactly the stated support-sign property.

Conversely, let such a nonzero vector \(j\) be given.  If both treatment levels are supported at \(c\) and \(j_{0,c}j_{1,c}<0\), choose
\[
w_{0,c}=|j_{1,c}|,
\qquad
w_{1,c}=|j_{0,c}|.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(w_{0,c}j_{0,c}+w_{1,c}j_{1,c}=0\).  If the two coordinates are zero, or if only one treatment block is supported, choose arbitrary positive block totals; the sign property again makes (9) hold.  Distribute each \(w_{a,c}\) strictly positively over the nonempty finite set \(\mathcal B_{(a,c)}\), and divide every resulting cell weight by their positive grand total.  This constructs a probability mass \(p_0\) that is strictly positive on exactly \(S\) and still satisfies (9).  With the forced one-column stochastic tables, it defines \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\).  Equations (7) and (9) show that the nonzero \(j\) is in the kernel, so \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}<Q_S\).

The two preceding implications prove the equivalence for every support: if no sign-feasible nonzero \(j\) exists, (7)--(9) show that the kernel is zero for every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\), and a linear map from the \(Q_S=|\mathcal I_S|\)-dimensional demand space then has full row rank.  The test is finite and exact.  Indeed, because \(D_S\) is a rational zero-one matrix,
\[
H_S^\perp=\operatorname{row}_{\mathbb R}(D_S),
\tag{11}
\]
so \(J_S\cap H_S^\perp\) is specified by rational linear equations.  There are finitely many choices, at every two-treatment \(c\), among the patterns \((0,0)\), \((+,-)\), and \((-,+)\).  For a pattern containing a strict sign, homogeneity allows rescaling so that every required positive coordinate is at least one and every required negative coordinate is at most minus one.  Hence each pattern is decided by an exact rational linear-feasibility problem, and the all-zero pattern is discarded.

Finally, assume in the \(u=1\) case that a sign-feasible vector exists and \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\).  Let \(p_0\) be the deficient mass just constructed.  The generic-rank theorem supplies a positive rational \(p_1\) whose corresponding parameter has rank \(Q_S\); the stochastic tables are uniquely fixed when \(u=1\).  Put
\[
p_t=p_0+t(p_1-p_0).
\tag{12}
\]
Normalization is preserved, and finiteness and strict positivity of \(p_0\) give \(p_t>0\) for all sufficiently small positive and negative \(t\).  Represent the pairing against demand block constants using a fixed real basis of \(H_S\) together with the generators \(r_c(p_t)\).  Every matrix entry is affine in \(t\); the matrix has rank \(Q_S\) at \(t=1\) and rank below \(Q_S\) at \(t=0\).  The nonzero-minor polynomial argument of (6) therefore gives full rank for every sufficiently small \(t\neq0\).  The weighted-support reduction transfers this rank back to \(M_{\theta(t)}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem is the generic-versus-uniform elbow: every nonempty support admits a deficient positive law when \(u\geq2\), while only the \(u=1\) case permits a support-level exact law-uniform classification. \textit{Gap.} Support counts and generic nonvanishing minors do not control the exceptional positive laws at which actual completeness is evaluated. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst must test completeness at the observed law even after the Hall frontier certifies generic feasibility; support alone cannot discharge that premise for nondegenerate \(U\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:rado-independent-transversal]\label{lem:rado-independent-transversal}
Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field and let \((V_i)_{i\in D}\) be a finite family of subspaces.  There are vectors \(v_i\in V_i\), one for every \(i\in D\), which are linearly independent if and only if
\[
\dim\left(\sum_{i\in I}V_i\right)\ge |I|
\quad\text{for every }I\subseteq D.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:schrijver-strongly-polynomial-sfm]\label{lem:schrijver-strongly-polynomial-sfm}
For a finite ground set \(D\), an integer-valued submodular function \(f:2^D\to\mathbb Z\) supplied by a value oracle can be minimized by a strongly polynomial combinatorial algorithm using a number of oracle calls polynomial in \(|D|\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:yu-fortified-anchor-display]\label{lem:yu-fortified-anchor-display}
Remark 2.3 of Yu, Shi, and Tchetgen Tchetgen's cited preprint displays a matrix \(M\in\mathbb R^{12\times15}\) and states that it is full rank.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[prop:yu-shi-tchetgen-anchor-specialization]\label{prop:yu-shi-tchetgen-anchor-specialization}
In the finite full-support specialization \(K=2\), \(\gamma=1\), \(m_1=m_2=3\), \(u=2\), \(S=\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Z\), and \(\alpha=\{1\}\), with no baseline covariate or within a fixed baseline stratum,
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}H_S=13,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}R_S(x)=3,
\qquad
H_S\cap R_S(x)=\{0\},
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}L_S(x)=16,
\qquad
Q_S=12.
\]
Thus the complete finite pairing is \(12\times16\), whereas the cited display is \(12\times15\). On a ternary complement alphabet the quadratic function of \(C\) is linearly independent of the constant and linear functions and is an internally exhibited third complement-proxy direction; the dimension comparison does not attribute a specific omitted basis vector to the cited source. Moreover \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\), so \(r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=12\), whereas every parameter with uniform binary-latent block tables satisfies \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\leq6<12\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Relabeling a three-point alphabet does not change any vector-space dimension, so the paper's values \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and the core's values \(\{1,2,3\}\) are linearly isomorphic.  The full support has
\[
|S|=2\cdot3\cdot3=18,
\qquad
|\mathcal I_S|=2\cdot3=6,
\qquad
Q_S=u|\mathcal I_S|=2\cdot6=12.
\tag{13}
\]

For \(\nu=\{1\}\), the incidence rows are the three indicators of the fixed \(Z_2\) values; for \(\nu=\{2\}\), they are the three indicators of the fixed \(Z_1\) values.  Each family spans a three-dimensional space.  On the full Cartesian product their intersection is exactly the constant line: a function that depends only on \(Z_1\) and only on \(Z_2\) is constant.  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_S}D_S=3+3-1=5,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}H_S=18-5=13.
\tag{14}
\]

The three vectors \(r_c(x)\), one for each complement value, have disjoint nonempty supports and are nonzero, so \(\dim R_S(x)=3\).  If \(q=\sum_c f_c r_c(x)\) also lies in \(H_S\), then the incidence equation for \(\nu=\alpha=\{1\}\) and fixed \(c\) gives
\[
0=\sum_{a,b}q_{a,b,c}
=f_c\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}.
\tag{15}
\]
The final sum is a nonzero polynomial and hence a nonzero element of \(\mathbb F_S\); division in this field yields \(f_c=0\) for every \(c\).  Thus \(H_S\cap R_S(x)=\{0\}\), and
\[
\dim L_S(x)=\dim H_S+\dim R_S(x)=13+3=16.
\tag{16}
\]
The cited source records a displayed \(12\times15\) full-rank matrix.  Independently, (14)--(16) compute the complete supply space here to have dimension \(16\).  Also \(1,z,z^2\) are linearly independent on \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}\): if \(a+bz+cz^2=0\) at all three values, evaluation at zero gives \(a=0\), subtraction of the equations at one and minus one gives \(b=0\), and then evaluation at one gives \(c=0\).  Hence the complete complement-proxy space has dimension three, including a quadratic direction, and the cited fifteen-column display has one fewer column than the complete supply.  This is an internal dimension comparison; it does not attribute a specific omitted basis vector to the cited source.  Together with (13), it proves the \(12\times16\) count.

It remains to verify generic full rank directly from the support objects.  The row space of \(D_S\) consists exactly of vectors
\[
n_{a,b,c}=g_b+h_c
\tag{17}
\]
that do not depend on \(a\).  If a vector of the form (17) lies in \(J_S\), then \(g_b\) is constant in \(b\), so the vector is constant within both treatment blocks at each \(c\).  Orthogonality to \(r_c(x)\) then gives its common value times \(\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}\), which vanishes only when that common value is zero.  Consequently
\[
N_S(x)\cap J_S=\{0\},
\qquad
\kappa_0(S)=0.
\tag{18}
\]

For the quotient calculation, (17) belongs to \(R_S(x)^\perp\) precisely when
\[
0=\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}(g_b+h_c)
\qquad\text{for every }c.
\tag{19}
\]
Given arbitrary \(g=(g_b)_{b=1}^3\), equation (19) has the unique solution
\[
h_c=-\frac{\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}g_b}
{\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}}.
\tag{20}
\]
The denominators are nonzero in \(\mathbb F_S\).  Modulo \(J_S\), the block restriction of (17) is therefore the same contrast class \([g]\in\mathbb F_S^3/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\) in all six blocks.  Thus \(\overline N_S\) is exactly the two-dimensional diagonal subspace
\[
\overline N_S
=\{([g],[g],[g],[g],[g],[g]):[g]\in\mathbb F_S^3/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\}.
\tag{21}
\]

Because \(u-1=1\), there is one replicated label per block.  Choose a basis \(e_1,e_2\) of the two-dimensional contrast space.  In one block choose the representative \(e_1\), and in each of the other five blocks choose \(e_2\).  If a linear combination of these six block-supported representatives lies in (21), then its value in every block must be one common contrast \(g\).  The first block makes \(g\) a multiple of \(e_1\), while any other block makes \(g\) a multiple of \(e_2\).  Linear independence of \(e_1,e_2\) forces \(g=0\), after which every coefficient is zero.  Their images in \(\overline E_S/\overline N_S\) are therefore independent.  Every subfamily is independent, so
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\left(\sum_{(y,j)\in I}W_y\right)\geq |I|
\qquad(I\subseteq\mathcal D_S).
\tag{22}
\]
The empty set attains zero in the definition of the Hall defect, while (22) makes every other deficiency nonpositive; hence \(\eta(S)=0\).  Equations (13), (18), and the generic-rank theorem now yield
\[
r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)=12.
\tag{23}
\]

Finally, at uniform binary-latent tables each block map has one-dimensional image.  The weighted-support reduction therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\leq|\mathcal I_S|=6<12=Q_S,
\tag{24}
\]
which proves the asserted actual-law deficiency and completes the specialization.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition places an independent complete-supply dimension derivation beside the cited finite display and exhibits the same support as generically full rank but rank deficient at a compatible positive law. \textit{Gap.} The cited \(12\times15\) display has one fewer column than the internally derived complete sixteen-dimensional supply space. \textit{Consumer.} The \(12\times16\) dimension calculation distinguishes the complete supply derived here from the cited \(12\times15\) display and demonstrates why generic rank is not actual-law completeness.

\section{Interpretation}

The structural support answers whether full rank is attainable generically, not whether it holds at the realized law. For every nondegenerate latent alphabet \(u\geq2\), the compatible positive model always contains uniform latent tables that collapse the \(u\) columns in each supported \((A,C)\) block to one direction. The degenerate elbow \(u=1\) is different: exact law-uniform rank is decided by whether the incidence row space contains a block-constant vector whose treatment coordinates can be canceled by positive block masses.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The defect algorithm is exact in the quotient-rank-oracle model: submodular minimization makes strongly polynomially many oracle calls, and each call is implemented by exact elimination over \(\mathbb F_S\). This does not establish polynomial bit complexity for representing or eliminating arbitrary rational functions, and the returned \(I^*\) is an exact maximizing deficient subset rather than a standalone succinct maximality certificate. The inherited nine-cell and \(135\)-case computations remain non-load-bearing because no support encodings or reproducible script were supplied.

\section{Honest scope}

The results concern finite coordinate-specific proxy alphabets, a declared support, and no baseline \(X\), or one fixed \(X\)-stratum. They give generic feasibility, law-uniform route failure, a sign characterization at \(u=1\), and an exact oracle reduction. The finite full-support subclass refutes any support/alphabet-only law-uniform completeness guarantee over the broader Yu--Shi--Tchetgen Tchetgen class when \(u\geq2\), but the positive generic theorem is not transferred to continuous or \(X\)-varying laws. Even at a generically feasible support, fortified completeness must hold at the actual law, a fortified outcome bridge must exist, and all remaining proximal causal assumptions must hold before \(\tau=\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)]\) equals \(\mathbb E\{h(W,1)-h(W,0)\}\). Failure of this pairing route does not prove that \(\tau\) is unidentified by every route. The rectangular equal-alphabet theorem remains only a special case of the support Hall result.
