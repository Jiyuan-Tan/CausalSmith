%%% FIELD rect-statement
If \(m_i=m\) for every \(i\), \(S=\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Z\), and \(|\alpha|=\gamma\), then \(Q_S=2u m^{K-\gamma}\) and \(Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)=\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}\), where \(D_{K,m}(\gamma)=m^K+\sum_{j=K-\gamma+1}^K\binom Kj(m-1)^j+m^{K-\gamma}\). Thus the Hall frontier reduces exactly to \(D_{K,m}(\gamma)\ge Q_S\).
%%% FIELD rect-proof
Put
\[
q=K-\gamma,\qquad b=m^\gamma,\qquad c=m^q,
\qquad
d_0=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\binom Kj(m-1)^j.
\tag{1}
\]
There are \(c\) values of \(C\) and two treatment values, so
\(\lvert\mathcal I_S\rvert=2c\).  The definition of \(Q_S\) therefore gives
\[
Q_S=u\lvert\mathcal I_S\rvert=2uc=2u m^{K-\gamma}.
\tag{2}
\]

We first compute the fortified supply.  For each coordinate \(i\), decompose the
space of functions on \(\mathcal Z_i\) as the direct sum of the constant line and
the \((m-1)\)-dimensional zero-sum contrast space \(\mathscr C_i\).  Tensoring
these decompositions gives the direct ANOVA decomposition
\[
\mathbb F_S^{\mathcal Z}
=\bigoplus_{T\subseteq[K]}\mathscr X_T,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\mathscr X_T=(m-1)^{\lvert T\rvert},
\tag{3}
\]
where functions in \(\mathscr X_T\) are contrasts precisely in the coordinates
in \(T\).  A row of \(D_S\) associated with \(\nu\) is constant in the treatment
coordinate and in all coordinates in \(\nu\).  As its fixed value
\(z_{-\nu}\) varies, those rows span all treatment-constant functions of
\(z_{-\nu}\).  It follows from the direct sum in (3) that
\[
\operatorname{row}(D_S)
=\bigoplus_{\substack{T\subseteq[K]:\ T\subseteq\nu^c
\text{ for some }\lvert\nu\rvert=\gamma}}
\mathscr X_T.
\tag{4}
\]
A set \(T\) is contained in such a \(\nu^c\) if and only if
\(\lvert T\rvert\le q\).  Thus (1), (3), and (4) imply
\[
\operatorname{rank}D_S=d_0,
\qquad
\dim H_S=2m^K-d_0
=m^K+\sum_{j=q+1}^{K}\binom Kj(m-1)^j.
\tag{5}
\]

The \(c\) vectors \(r_c(x)\) have pairwise disjoint nonempty supports and hence
are linearly independent.  If
\(\sum_c\lambda_c r_c(x)\in H_S\), use in the definition of \(H_S\) the
constraint \(\nu=\alpha\) at the fixed complement value \(c\).  It gives
\[
\lambda_c\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{b\in\prod_{i\in\alpha}\mathcal Z_i}
x_{(a,b,c)}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
The displayed sum is a nonzero element of the rational-function field
\(\mathbb F_S\), so \(\lambda_c=0\) for every \(c\).  Consequently
\(H_S\cap R_S(x)=\{0\}\), and (5) yields
\[
\dim L_S(x)=2m^K-d_0+c
=m^K+\sum_{j=q+1}^{K}\binom Kj(m-1)^j+m^q
=D_{K,m}(\gamma).
\tag{7}
\]

We also determine the block-constant kernel.  By (4), every vector in
\(H_S^\perp\) is treatment-constant and is represented by a function
\(g\) in the interaction space
\[
\mathscr G=\bigoplus_{\lvert T\rvert\le q}\mathscr X_T,
\qquad \dim\mathscr G=d_0.
\tag{8}
\]
Orthogonality to \(r_c(x)\) is the equation
\[
\ell_c(g):=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_b x_{(a,b,c)}g(b,c)=0.
\tag{9}
\]
The equations in (9) are independent: \(\mathscr G\) contains every function
of \(C\), so the indicator of any single \(C\)-cell isolates the corresponding
equation, whose coefficient sum is nonzero.  In particular,
\(\dim N_S(x)=d_0-c\).  If a vector of \(N_S(x)\) also lies in \(J_S\), treatment
constancy and block constancy make it equal to a scalar \(t_c\) throughout both
\(B\)-blocks over \(c\).  Equation (9) becomes
\[
t_c\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}=0,
\]
and hence \(t_c=0\) for every \(c\).  Therefore
\[
\kappa_0(S)=0.
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to show that the row-stochastic product blocks attain the smaller of
the demand dimension in (2) and the supply dimension in (7).  We first prove the
needed block-measurement fact.  For a complement cell \(c\), let
\(\mathscr W_c\subseteq\mathscr G^*\) be the space of restrictions to
\(\mathscr G\) of all linear functionals supported on the \(B\)-fiber over
\(c\), and, for \(J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}\), set
\[
\rho(J)=\dim\left(\sum_{c\in J}\mathscr W_c\right).
\tag{11}
\]
This is a normalized, monotone, submodular rank function.  Independent
permutations of the \(m\) labels in the coordinates of \(C\) act transitively
on its \(c\) cells, preserve the interaction-order space \(\mathscr G\), and
therefore preserve \(\rho\).  Moreover, all coordinate functionals together
separate \(\mathscr G\), so \(\rho(\mathcal Z_{-\alpha})=d_0\).

We claim that
\[
\rho(J)\ge \frac{d_0}{c}\lvert J\rvert
\quad\text{for every }J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, let \(\Gamma\) be the finite transitive permutation group just
described.  The family \((\sigma J)_{\sigma\in\Gamma}\) covers each complement
cell exactly
\(k=\lvert\Gamma\rvert\lvert J\rvert/c\) times.  For completeness, the
fractional-cover inequality for a normalized monotone submodular function says
that a \(k\)-cover \((A_s)_s\) of a finite ground set \(E\) satisfies
\(\sum_s\rho(A_s)\ge k\rho(E)\).  To verify it, order the elements of \(E\),
expand each \(\rho(A_s)\) into successive marginal increments in that order,
and use diminishing returns to bound the increment contributed when an element
is added to \(A_s\) below by its increment after all earlier elements of \(E\)
have been added.  Summing counts every such full-set increment at least \(k\)
times and telescopes to \(k\rho(E)\).  Applying this inequality to the translates
of \(J\), and using \(\rho(\sigma J)=\rho(J)\), proves (12).

Now take \(r\) replicas of every \(\mathscr W_c\), where \(1\le r\le b\).
The frozen Rado independent-transversal lemma implies that the largest possible
rank of \(r\) fiber-supported rows per complement cell is
\[
R_r=rc-\max_{J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}}
\{r\lvert J\rvert-\rho(J)\}
=\min_{J\subseteq\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}}
\{r(c-\lvert J\rvert)+\rho(J)\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Here is the reduction from the stated all-elements form of Rado to (13).  If
\(\delta\) denotes the maximum defect in (13), every selection has rank at most
\(rc-\delta\).  Conversely, enlarge \(\mathscr G^*\) by an auxiliary
\(\delta\)-dimensional space and enlarge every replicated subspace by that same
space.  The definition of \(\delta\) makes every Rado inequality hold in the
enlarged space.  Rado therefore selects all \(rc\) vectors independently there;
their projections to \(\mathscr G^*\) span at least \(rc-\delta\) dimensions.
For a fixed support \(J\), the defect is maximized by taking all \(r\) replicas
over every cell in \(J\), which gives exactly (13).

If \(rc\le d_0\), (12) makes every term in the last minimum in (13) at least
\(rc\), and the choice \(J=\varnothing\) gives equality.  If \(rc\ge d_0\),
(12) makes every term at least \(d_0\), and \(J=\mathcal Z_{-\alpha}\) gives
equality.  Thus
\[
R_r=\min\{d_0,rc\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The restrictions defining \(\mathscr W_c\) are surjective from the full
\(B\)-fiber dual.  Hence a nonzero maximal minor witnessing (14) is a polynomial
in the entries of the fiber rows.  Full row rank of each \(r\)-row fiber map is
another nonempty Zariski-open condition.  Since the parameter space of all rows
is irreducible over the infinite field \(\mathbb F_S\), the two open conditions
intersect.  Therefore (14) is attained by rank-\(r\) fiber maps and holds for a
nonempty Zariski-open set of them.

We apply (14) to the stochastic blocks.  First suppose \(u<b\).  Let
\(\mathscr P_{a,c}\) be the \(u\)-dimensional column space of a full-column-rank
row-stochastic block \(\Pi_{(a,c)}\); it contains \(\mathbf 1_b\).  Define
\[
w_c(b)=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}x_{(a,b,c)},
\qquad
h=\max\{0,2u-b-1\},
\qquad r=b-h.
\tag{15}
\]
Two generic \(u\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_S^b\) containing
\(\mathbf 1_b\) intersect in dimension \(\max\{1,2u-b\}\).  Since
\(w_c^\top\mathbf 1_b\ne0\), their intersection with
\(\ker w_c^\top\) has dimension \(h\).  Conversely, every \(h\)-subspace
\(\mathscr Q_c\subseteq\ker w_c^\top\) can be realized as
\[
\mathscr Q_c
=\mathscr P_{0,c}\cap\mathscr P_{1,c}\cap\ker w_c^\top.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(h>0\), take the common intersection to be
\(\langle\mathbf 1_b\rangle\oplus\mathscr Q_c\), whose dimension is
\(2u-b\), and split its \(2(b-u)\)-dimensional complement into two
\((b-u)\)-dimensional summands.  Adjoining one summand to the common intersection
defines each \(\mathscr P_{a,c}\).  For \(h=0\), choose two
\((u-1)\)-dimensional complements to the constant line with zero mutual
intersection; this is possible because \(2(u-1)\le b-1\).

The entries of all \(w_c\) are algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\): a
polynomial relation among the sums
\(x_{(0,b,c)}+x_{(1,b,c)}\) would remain a relation among independent variables
after setting all variables with treatment value one to zero.  Thus, in (14),
one of the \(r\) generic rows in each fiber may be designated as \(w_c^\top\).
Choose the remaining rows so that their common kernel is
\(\mathscr Q_c\).  Equations (8), (9), and (16) then show that
\[
\dim\left(
N_S(x)\cap\bigoplus_{a,c}\mathscr P_{a,c}
\right)
=d_0-R_r
=\max\{0,d_0-rc\}.
\tag{17}
\]
By the frozen weighted-support reduction, the rank of the pairing is the
dimension of the image of \(\bigoplus_{a,c}\mathscr P_{a,c}\) modulo
\(N_S(x)\).  Its dimension is therefore
\[
2uc-\max\{0,d_0-rc\}.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(2u\le b+1\), then \(h=0\), \(r=b\), and \(d_0<bc\), so (18) equals
\(2uc=Q_S\).  In this case
\[
D_{K,m}(\gamma)-Q_S
=(2b+1-2u)c-d_0\ge bc-d_0>0.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(2u>b+1\), then \(r=2(b-u)+1\), and (7) gives
\[
d_0-rc=2uc-D_{K,m}(\gamma).
\tag{20}
\]
Substitution of (20) into (18) again gives
\(\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}\).

If \(u\ge b\), a generic \(b\)-by-\(u\) row-stochastic block has row rank
\(b\), so the direct sum of its column spaces is all of \(E_S\).  The quotient
map is then surjective and its rank is \(\dim L_S(x)=D_{K,m}(\gamma)\).  Because
\(d_0\ge c\), (7) gives \(D_{K,m}(\gamma)\le2bc\le2uc=Q_S\), so this is also
the required minimum.

All attainments above are algebraic.  More explicitly, a maximal minor is
nonzero on a nonempty Zariski-open set of the cell weights and block contrasts.
A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on the open positive orthant, so the
cell weights may be chosen positive and rational.  Common rescaling of all cell
weights changes neither \(R_S(x)\), nor \(N_S(x)\), nor any rank, and therefore
normalizes those weights to the probability simplex.  For the blocks, positivity
is seen directly: given any rational subspace \(\mathscr P_{a,c}\) containing
\(\mathbf 1_b\), map \(\mathbf 1_u\) to \(\mathbf 1_b\), map a basis of the
contrast subspace of \(\mathbb Q^u\) to a basis of a complement in
\(\mathscr P_{a,c}\), and multiply the latter images by a sufficiently small
positive rational number.  The resulting matrix is a strictly positive
row-stochastic perturbation of the uniform block and has column space
\(\mathscr P_{a,c}\).  Hence the rank just computed is the generic rank over
\(\Theta_S\):
\[
r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}.
\tag{21}
\]

The established generic-rank formula and (10), (21) now imply
\[
Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)
=\min\{Q_S,D_{K,m}(\gamma)\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus generic full row rank holds exactly when
\(D_{K,m}(\gamma)\ge Q_S\).  By the established Hall full-rank frontier, this is
exactly the reduction of the Hall inequalities in the rectangular full-support
case.  As a smallest exact check, when \(K=2\), \(\gamma=1\), and \(m=2\), one
has \(d_0=3\), \(D_{2,2}(1)=7\); hence (22) gives rank \(7\) for \(u=2\) and
full rank \(4\) for \(u=1\), agreeing with both sides of the asserted minimum.
