%%% FIELD uniform-proposed-claim
For every nonempty declared support \(S\), the following classification holds.  If \(u\geq 2\), there is a parameter \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\) whose block tables are uniform and for which
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}\leq |\mathcal I_S|<Q_S.
\]
Consequently no support can guarantee \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta=Q_S\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\).  If, in addition, \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\), then there is a strictly positive compatible affine family \(\theta(t)\), defined for all sufficiently small positive and negative \(t\), such that \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta(0)}<Q_S\) and \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta(t)}=Q_S\) for every sufficiently small \(t\neq0\).

If \(u=1\), law-uniform full row rank holds if and only if there is no nonzero real vector \(j\in J_S\cap H_S^\perp\), where the orthogonal complement is taken after base change of the incidence equations to \(\mathbb R\), with the following support-sign property: at every \(c\) supporting only one treatment level \(a\), \(j_{a,c}=0\); and at every \(c\) supporting both treatment levels, either \(j_{0,c}=j_{1,c}=0\) or \(j_{0,c}j_{1,c}<0\).  This is a finite exact sign-feasibility characterization determined by \(S\) and \(D_S\).  Every such vector constructs a rank-deficient \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\).  If also \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\), that \(\theta_0\) can be embedded in a strictly positive compatible affine family having full row rank for every sufficiently small \(t\neq0\).

%%% FIELD uniform-proof
Write \(N_S(p)=(H_S+R_S(p))^\perp\) for the real specialization of the symbolic block-null space at a strictly positive mass vector \(p\).  The weighted-support reduction lemma identifies \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\) with the rank of
\[
\Phi_\theta:\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal I_S}\mathbb R^u
\longrightarrow \mathbb R^S/N_S(p_\theta),
\qquad
(g_y)_y\longmapsto
\left[\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal I_S}\Pi_{\theta,y}g_y\right].
\tag{1}
\]
This reduction is applicable because the definition of \(\Theta_S\) gives \(p_\theta(s)>0\) on every \(s\in S\), which is exactly the positivity needed for the coordinatewise division in that lemma.

Suppose first that \(u\geq2\).  The support \(S\) is finite and nonempty, so set \(p_0(s)=|S|^{-1}\) for \(s\in S\), and put
\[
[\Pi_{0,y}]_{b,v}=u^{-1}
\qquad
(y\in\mathcal I_S,\ b\in\mathcal B_y,\ v\in\mathcal U).
\tag{2}
\]
All entries in (2) are positive and every row sums to one; hence \(\theta_0=(p_0,(\Pi_{0,y})_y)\) belongs to \(\Theta_S\).  The image of each block map \(\Pi_{0,y}:\mathbb R^u\to\mathbb R^{\mathcal B_y}\) is the line spanned by \(\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_y}\).  Therefore the image before the quotient in (1) has dimension at most \(|\mathcal I_S|\), and passage to a quotient cannot increase dimension.  The weighted-support reduction gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}
=\operatorname{rank}\Phi_{\theta_0}
\leq |\mathcal I_S|.
\tag{3}
\]
Nonemptiness of \(S\) implies \(|\mathcal I_S|\geq1\), while the definition of \(Q_S\) and \(u\geq2\) give
\[
|\mathcal I_S|<u|\mathcal I_S|=Q_S.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) prove law-uniform full rank impossible for every nonempty support, without a Hall-feasibility hypothesis.

Now additionally suppose \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\).  The generic-rank theorem supplies a rational positive parameter \(\theta_1\in\Theta_S\) such that \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_1}=Q_S\).  In the preceding construction choose \(p_0=p_{\theta_1}\), retain the uniform tables \(\Pi_{0,y}\), and define
\[
p_{\theta(t)}=p_{\theta_1},
\qquad
\Pi_{\theta(t),y}=\Pi_{0,y}+t(\Pi_{\theta_1,y}-\Pi_{0,y}).
\tag{5}
\]
Every row sum in (5) equals one.  Because there are finitely many table entries, their strict positivity at \(t=0\) supplies a number \(\delta>0\) such that \(\theta(t)\in\Theta_S\) whenever \(|t|<\delta\).  With \(p\) fixed, choose fixed bases for the domain in (1) and for the fortified supply \(H_S+R_S(p_{\theta_1})\).  The entries of the resulting pairing matrix are affine functions of \(t\).  Since its rank at \(t=1\) is \(Q_S\), some \(Q_S\)-minor has determinant \(\Delta(t)\) satisfying \(\Delta(1)\neq0\).  Thus \(\Delta\) is a nonzero univariate polynomial.  Equation (3) gives \(\Delta(0)=0\).  If \(k\geq1\) is the least exponent with nonzero coefficient \(c\), then \(\Delta(t)=t^k\{c+tq(t)\}\) for a polynomial \(q\).  Because \(q\) is bounded on a sufficiently small compact interval about zero, after decreasing \(\delta\) there is \(\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)\) such that \(|tq(t)|<|c|/2\) whenever \(|t|<\varepsilon\).  Hence
\[
\Delta(t)\neq0\qquad\text{whenever }0<|t|<\varepsilon.
\tag{6}
\]
The demand space has dimension \(Q_S\), so (6) is equivalent to \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta(t)}=Q_S\).  This proves the asserted two-sided crossing for \(u\geq2\).

Suppose next that \(u=1\).  Row stochasticity forces \(\Pi_{\theta,y}=\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_y}\) for every block.  The domain of (1) has one scalar coordinate \(j_{a,c}\) for every \(y=(a,c)\in\mathcal I_S\), and its image before quotienting is the block-constant vector \(j\in J_S\) with value \(j_{a,c}\) on \(\mathcal B_{(a,c)}\).  Consequently a demand vector belongs to the kernel precisely when
\[
j\in N_S(p_\theta)\cap J_S
=H_S^\perp\cap R_S(p_\theta)^\perp\cap J_S.
\tag{7}
\]
Here the equality is the elementary identity \((H_S+R_S(p_\theta))^\perp=H_S^\perp\cap R_S(p_\theta)^\perp\).

For a supported complement value \(c\), define the positive block total
\[
w_{a,c}(p_\theta)
=\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_{(a,c)}}p_\theta(a,b,c)>0
\qquad ((a,c)\in\mathcal I_S).
\tag{8}
\]
The strict inequality follows because every block is nonempty by the definition of \(\mathcal I_S\) and every supported cell mass is positive.  Since \(R_S(p_\theta)\) is spanned by the vectors \(r_c(p_\theta)\), its orthogonality condition in (7) is, for each supported \(c\),
\[
0=\langle j,r_c(p_\theta)\rangle
=\sum_{a:(a,c)\in\mathcal I_S}w_{a,c}(p_\theta)j_{a,c}.
\tag{9}
\]
If only one treatment level \(a\) is supported at \(c\), positivity in (8) makes (9) equivalent to \(j_{a,c}=0\).  If both treatment levels are supported, a positive linear combination of \(j_{0,c}\) and \(j_{1,c}\) can vanish if and only if both numbers vanish or their product is strictly negative.  Thus every rank-deficient \(\theta\) produces a nonzero vector in \(J_S\cap H_S^\perp\) with exactly the stated support-sign property.

Conversely, let such a nonzero vector \(j\) be given.  If both treatment levels are supported at \(c\) and \(j_{0,c}j_{1,c}<0\), choose
\[
w_{0,c}=|j_{1,c}|,
\qquad
w_{1,c}=|j_{0,c}|.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(w_{0,c}j_{0,c}+w_{1,c}j_{1,c}=0\).  If the two coordinates are zero, or if only one treatment block is supported, choose arbitrary positive block totals; the sign property again makes (9) hold.  Distribute each \(w_{a,c}\) strictly positively over the nonempty finite set \(\mathcal B_{(a,c)}\), and divide every resulting cell weight by their positive grand total.  This constructs a probability mass \(p_0\) that is strictly positive on exactly \(S\) and still satisfies (9).  With the forced one-column stochastic tables, it defines \(\theta_0\in\Theta_S\).  Equations (7) and (9) show that the nonzero \(j\) is in the kernel, so \(\operatorname{rank}M_{\theta_0}<Q_S\).

The two preceding implications prove the equivalence for every support: if no sign-feasible nonzero \(j\) exists, (7)--(9) show that the kernel is zero for every \(\theta\in\Theta_S\), and a linear map from the \(Q_S=|\mathcal I_S|\)-dimensional demand space then has full row rank.  The test is finite and exact.  Indeed, because \(D_S\) is a rational zero-one matrix,
\[
H_S^\perp=\operatorname{row}_{\mathbb R}(D_S),
\tag{11}
\]
so \(J_S\cap H_S^\perp\) is specified by rational linear equations.  There are finitely many choices, at every two-treatment \(c\), among the patterns \((0,0)\), \((+,-)\), and \((-,+)\).  For a pattern containing a strict sign, homogeneity allows rescaling so that every required positive coordinate is at least one and every required negative coordinate is at most minus one.  Hence each pattern is decided by an exact rational linear-feasibility problem, and the all-zero pattern is discarded.

Finally, assume in the \(u=1\) case that a sign-feasible vector exists and \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\).  Let \(p_0\) be the deficient mass just constructed.  The generic-rank theorem supplies a positive rational \(p_1\) whose corresponding parameter has rank \(Q_S\); the stochastic tables are uniquely fixed when \(u=1\).  Put
\[
p_t=p_0+t(p_1-p_0).
\tag{12}
\]
Normalization is preserved, and finiteness and strict positivity of \(p_0\) give \(p_t>0\) for all sufficiently small positive and negative \(t\).  Represent the pairing against demand block constants using a fixed real basis of \(H_S\) together with the generators \(r_c(p_t)\).  Every matrix entry is affine in \(t\); the matrix has rank \(Q_S\) at \(t=1\) and rank below \(Q_S\) at \(t=0\).  The nonzero-minor polynomial argument of (6) therefore gives full rank for every sufficiently small \(t\neq0\).  The weighted-support reduction transfers this rank back to \(M_{\theta(t)}\), completing the proof.

In the finite full-support specialization \(K=2\), \(\gamma=1\), \(m_1=m_2=3\), \(u=2\), \(S=\{0,1\}\times\mathcal Z\), and \(\alpha=\{1\}\), with no baseline covariate or within a fixed baseline stratum,
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}H_S=13,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}R_S(x)=3,
\qquad
H_S\cap R_S(x)=\{0\},
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}L_S(x)=16,
\qquad
Q_S=12.
\]
Thus the complete finite pairing is \(12\times16\), not the cited \(12\times15\) display: on a ternary complement alphabet the quadratic function of \(C\) is linearly independent of the constant and linear functions and supplies the omitted direction.  Moreover \(\kappa_0(S)=\eta(S)=0\), so \(r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=12\), whereas every parameter with uniform binary-latent block tables satisfies \(\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\leq6<12\).
%%% FIELD yu-prop-proof
Relabeling a three-point alphabet does not change any vector-space dimension, so the paper's values \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and the core's values \(\{1,2,3\}\) are linearly isomorphic.  The full support has
\[
|S|=2\cdot3\cdot3=18,
\qquad
|\mathcal I_S|=2\cdot3=6,
\qquad
Q_S=u|\mathcal I_S|=2\cdot6=12.
\tag{13}
\]

For \(\nu=\{1\}\), the incidence rows are the three indicators of the fixed \(Z_2\) values; for \(\nu=\{2\}\), they are the three indicators of the fixed \(Z_1\) values.  Each family spans a three-dimensional space.  On the full Cartesian product their intersection is exactly the constant line: a function that depends only on \(Z_1\) and only on \(Z_2\) is constant.  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_S}D_S=3+3-1=5,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}H_S=18-5=13.
\tag{14}
\]

The three vectors \(r_c(x)\), one for each complement value, have disjoint nonempty supports and are nonzero, so \(\dim R_S(x)=3\).  If \(q=\sum_c f_c r_c(x)\) also lies in \(H_S\), then the incidence equation for \(\nu=\alpha=\{1\}\) and fixed \(c\) gives
\[
0=\sum_{a,b}q_{a,b,c}
=f_c\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}.
\tag{15}
\]
The final sum is a nonzero polynomial and hence a nonzero element of \(\mathbb F_S\); division in this field yields \(f_c=0\) for every \(c\).  Thus \(H_S\cap R_S(x)=\{0\}\), and
\[
\dim L_S(x)=\dim H_S+\dim R_S(x)=13+3=16.
\tag{16}
\]
The cited source records thirteen fortified columns and adjoins only the constant and linear functions of its ternary \(Z_2\), producing its displayed fifteen columns.  But \(1,z,z^2\) are linearly independent on \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}\): if \(a+bz+cz^2=0\) at all three values, evaluation at zero gives \(a=0\), subtraction of the equations at one and minus one gives \(b=0\), and then evaluation at one gives \(c=0\).  Hence the full complement-proxy space has dimension three and the quadratic direction is the missing sixteenth supply column.  Together with (13), this proves the \(12\times16\) count.

It remains to verify generic full rank directly from the support objects.  The row space of \(D_S\) consists exactly of vectors
\[
n_{a,b,c}=g_b+h_c
\tag{17}
\]
that do not depend on \(a\).  If a vector of the form (17) lies in \(J_S\), then \(g_b\) is constant in \(b\), so the vector is constant within both treatment blocks at each \(c\).  Orthogonality to \(r_c(x)\) then gives its common value times \(\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}\), which vanishes only when that common value is zero.  Consequently
\[
N_S(x)\cap J_S=\{0\},
\qquad
\kappa_0(S)=0.
\tag{18}
\]

For the quotient calculation, (17) belongs to \(R_S(x)^\perp\) precisely when
\[
0=\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}(g_b+h_c)
\qquad\text{for every }c.
\tag{19}
\]
Given arbitrary \(g=(g_b)_{b=1}^3\), equation (19) has the unique solution
\[
h_c=-\frac{\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}g_b}
{\sum_{a,b}x_{(a,b,c)}}.
\tag{20}
\]
The denominators are nonzero in \(\mathbb F_S\).  Modulo \(J_S\), the block restriction of (17) is therefore the same contrast class \([g]\in\mathbb F_S^3/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\) in all six blocks.  Thus \(\overline N_S\) is exactly the two-dimensional diagonal subspace
\[
\overline N_S
=\{([g],[g],[g],[g],[g],[g]):[g]\in\mathbb F_S^3/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\}.
\tag{21}
\]

Because \(u-1=1\), there is one replicated label per block.  Choose a basis \(e_1,e_2\) of the two-dimensional contrast space.  In one block choose the representative \(e_1\), and in each of the other five blocks choose \(e_2\).  If a linear combination of these six block-supported representatives lies in (21), then its value in every block must be one common contrast \(g\).  The first block makes \(g\) a multiple of \(e_1\), while any other block makes \(g\) a multiple of \(e_2\).  Linear independence of \(e_1,e_2\) forces \(g=0\), after which every coefficient is zero.  Their images in \(\overline E_S/\overline N_S\) are therefore independent.  Every subfamily is independent, so
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_S}\left(\sum_{(y,j)\in I}W_y\right)\geq |I|
\qquad(I\subseteq\mathcal D_S).
\tag{22}
\]
The empty set attains zero in the definition of the Hall defect, while (22) makes every other deficiency nonpositive; hence \(\eta(S)=0\).  Equations (13), (18), and the generic-rank theorem now yield
\[
r_{\mathrm{gen}}(S)=Q_S-\kappa_0(S)-\eta(S)=12.
\tag{23}
\]

Finally, at uniform binary-latent tables each block map has one-dimensional image.  The weighted-support reduction therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_\theta\leq|\mathcal I_S|=6<12=Q_S,
\tag{24}
\]
which proves the asserted actual-law deficiency and completes the specialization.
