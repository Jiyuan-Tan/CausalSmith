Fix one triangular array in \(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)\) satisfying the three displayed hypotheses.  All limits below are along this fixed array, as required by the final sentence of the theorem.  Eventual forward orientation and \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\) give
\[
X_{1,n}=\epsilon_{1,n},\qquad X_{2,n}=\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n}+\epsilon_{2,n}.
\tag{1}
\]
For brevity put
\[
U_{j,n}=U_{j,n}(t_n),\qquad a_n=\beta_nU_{1,n},\qquad b_n=U_{2,n},\qquad M_n=\max(a_n,b_n).
\tag{2}
\]
The definitions of \(c_{j,n}\) give the exact identities
\[
U_{j,n}^{\alpha_{j,n}}=c_{j,n}t_n,\qquad j\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\log\frac{U_{1,n}}{U_{2,n}}
=\frac{\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})}{\alpha_{1,n}}
-\frac{\Delta_n\{\log c_{2,n}+\log t_n\}}
{\alpha_{1,n}\alpha_{2,n}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\) implies \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\to\log c_0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\), the scale-locality hypothesis, and \(\lambda_n\to\lambda\) therefore turn (4) into
\[
\log\frac{U_{1,n}}{U_{2,n}}
\longrightarrow \frac{\log c_0}{\alpha_0}-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Together with \(\beta_n\to\beta_0>0\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\), this yields a constant \(K<\infty\) such that, eventually,
\[
K^{-1}\leq \frac{a_n}{b_n}\leq K.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(q_{2,n}=F_{2,n}^{\leftarrow}(1-1/t_n)\).  Continuity in \(\mathrm{ass{:}continuous\text{-}margins}\) implies
\[
P_{\theta,n}(X_{2,n}>q_{2,n})=t_n^{-1},
\tag{7}
\]
and the events \(\{F_{2,n}(X_{2,n})>1-1/t_n\}\) and \(\{X_{2,n}>q_{2,n}\}\) differ only by a null set.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\) also provides constants \(0<\underline\alpha<\overline\alpha<\infty\) for which
\[
\underline\alpha\leq\alpha_{j,n}\leq\overline\alpha,\qquad j\in\{1,2\},
\tag{8}
\]
eventually.  Fix \(d\in(0,1)\).  If \(M_n=a_n\), then \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}tail\text{-}normalization}\), applied at the fixed multiplier \(d\), gives
\[
t_nP_{\theta,n}(\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n}>dM_n)
=d^{-\alpha_{1,n}}\{1+o(1)\}>1
\tag{9}
\]
eventually.  If \(M_n=b_n\), the same assertion holds with \(\epsilon_{2,n}\) and \(\alpha_{2,n}\).  Positivity in (1) and (7) then imply \(q_{2,n}\geq dM_n\).

For the converse comparison, choose a fixed \(C>2\) so large that \(2(C/2)^{-\underline\alpha}<1/2\).  By (6), the dimensionless multipliers \(CM_n/(2a_n)\) and \(CM_n/(2b_n)\) range in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\); because \(M_n\geq a_n,b_n\), each is at least \(C/2\).  The union bound, (8), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}tail\text{-}normalization}\) yield
\[
\begin{split}
P_{\theta,n}(X_{2,n}>CM_n)
&\leq P_{\theta,n}(\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n}>CM_n/2)
+P_{\theta,n}(\epsilon_{2,n}>CM_n/2)\\
&<t_n^{-1}
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
eventually.  Equations (7), (9), and (10) prove the threshold-scale comparison
\[
dM_n\leq q_{2,n}\leq CM_n.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, both \(q_{2,n}/a_n\) and \(q_{2,n}/b_n\) remain bounded above and bounded away from zero.

Define
\[
p_{1,n}=P_{\theta,n}(\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n}>q_{2,n}),
\qquad
p_{2,n}=P_{\theta,n}(\epsilon_{2,n}>q_{2,n}).
\tag{12}
\]
Using (3), (11), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}tail\text{-}normalization}\) at the two compact dimensionless multiplier sequences gives
\[
\begin{split}
p_{1,n}&=t_n^{-1}\left(\frac{q_{2,n}}{a_n}\right)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}\{1+o(1)\},\\
p_{2,n}&=t_n^{-1}\left(\frac{q_{2,n}}{b_n}\right)^{-\alpha_{2,n}}\{1+o(1)\}.
\end{split}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(p_{1,n}\asymp t_n^{-1}\) and \(p_{2,n}\asymp t_n^{-1}\) along this array.

We next derive the required one-large-jump relation.  Write \(Y_n=\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n}\) and \(W_n=\epsilon_{2,n}\).  For every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), positivity and elementary event inclusions give
\[
\begin{split}
p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}-p_{1,n}p_{2,n}
&\leq P_{\theta,n}(Y_n+W_n>q_{2,n})\\
&\leq P_{\theta,n}(Y_n>(1-\delta)q_{2,n})
+P_{\theta,n}(W_n>(1-\delta)q_{2,n})\\
&\quad+P_{\theta,n}(Y_n>\delta q_{2,n})
P_{\theta,n}(W_n>\delta q_{2,n}).
\end{split}
\tag{14}
\]
The product in the last line is justified by \(\mathrm{ass{:}innovation\text{-}independence}\).  By (11), every threshold in (14), divided by its corresponding \(U_{j,n}\), lies in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\).  Hence \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}tail\text{-}normalization}\) gives
\[
\frac{P_{\theta,n}(Y_n>(1-\delta)q_{2,n})}{p_{1,n}}
=(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}+o(1),
\tag{15}
\]
with the analogous identity for \(W_n\), while the product term is \(O(t_n^{-2})\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}intermediate\text{-}sequence}\) gives \(t_n=n/k_n\to\infty\); thus the product is \(o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n})\) by (13).  Divide (14) by \(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}\), let \(n\to\infty\), and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).  The uniform index bounds (8) imply
\[
P_{\theta,n}(X_{2,n}>q_{2,n})
=p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}+o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}).
\tag{16}
\]

Put \(H_n=F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})=F_{1,n}(\epsilon_{1,n})\).  To record explicitly the probability-integral step, for \(v\in(0,1)\) let \(x_v=F_{1,n}^{\leftarrow}(v)\).  Continuity gives \(F_{1,n}(x_v)=v\), and \(\{F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})\leq v\}\) agrees almost surely with \(\{X_{1,n}\leq x_v\}\); hence \(P_{\theta,n}(H_n\leq v)=v\).  Therefore
\[
E_{P_{\theta,n}}(H_n)=\frac12.
\tag{17}
\]
On \(\{Y_n>q_{2,n}\}\), monotonicity of \(F_{1,n}\) gives
\[
0\leq1-H_n\leq P_{\theta,n}(\epsilon_{1,n}>q_{2,n}/\beta_n)=p_{1,n}.
\tag{18}
\]
It follows that
\[
E_{P_{\theta,n}}[H_n\mathbf 1\{Y_n>q_{2,n}\}]
=p_{1,n}+O(p_{1,n}^2),
\qquad
E_{P_{\theta,n}}[H_n\mathbf 1\{W_n>q_{2,n}\}]
=\frac12p_{2,n},
\tag{19}
\]
where the second equality uses \(\mathrm{ass{:}innovation\text{-}independence}\).  The intersection of the two displayed events has probability \(p_{1,n}p_{2,n}\).  Moreover, (16) shows that the event \(\{Y_n+W_n>q_{2,n}\}\setminus(\{Y_n>q_{2,n}\}\cup\{W_n>q_{2,n}\})\) has probability \(o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n})\).  Dividing the resulting numerator expansion by (16) proves
\[
\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)
=\frac{p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}/2}{p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}}+o(1)
=\frac12+\frac12\frac{p_{1,n}/p_{2,n}}{1+p_{1,n}/p_{2,n}}+o(1).
\tag{20}
\]

Equation (13) equivalently gives
\[
\frac{p_{1,n}}{p_{2,n}}
=\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}\frac{c_{1,n}}{c_{2,n}}
q_{2,n}^{-\Delta_n}\{1+o(1)\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Tail-constant balance, \(\Delta_n\to0\), and the scale-locality hypothesis imply
\[
\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}
=\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}
+\Delta_n\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\longrightarrow0.
\tag{22}
\]
Using (3), \(\lambda_n=\Delta_n\log t_n\), \(\Delta_n\log\beta_n\to0\), and (22), we obtain
\[
\Delta_n\log a_n=\frac{\lambda_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}+o(1),
\qquad
\Delta_n\log b_n=\frac{\lambda_n}{\alpha_{2,n}}+o(1).
\tag{23}
\]
Both quantities converge to \(\lambda/\alpha_0\).  Since (11) sandwiches \(q_{2,n}\) between fixed positive multiples of \(a_n\) and \(b_n\), and \(\Delta_n\to0\), it follows that
\[
\Delta_n\log q_{2,n}\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0}.
\tag{24}
\]
Now (21), \(\beta_n\to\beta_0\), \(\alpha_{1,n}\to\alpha_0\), and \(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n}\to c_0\) yield
\[
\frac{p_{1,n}}{p_{2,n}}
\longrightarrow
R(\lambda)=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}.
\tag{25}
\]
Substitution of (25) into (20) proves the asserted limit for \(\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)\).

It remains to evaluate the other rank coefficient.  By (1), continuity, and (3), the \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{1,n}\) is \(q_{1,n}=U_{1,n}\).  On \(\{X_{1,n}>U_{1,n}\}\), positivity gives \(X_{2,n}>\beta_nU_{1,n}\).  Therefore monotonicity of \(F_{2,n}\) implies
\[
0\leq1-\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)
\leq P_{\theta,n}(X_{2,n}>\beta_nU_{1,n}).
\tag{26}
\]
For the two terms in the following union bound, the relevant dimensionless multipliers relative to \(U_{1,n}\) and \(U_{2,n}\) are, respectively, \(1/2\) and \(\beta_nU_{1,n}/(2U_{2,n})\).  They lie in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\) by (6).  Thus \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}tail\text{-}normalization}\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
P_{\theta,n}(X_{2,n}>\beta_nU_{1,n})
&\leq P_{\theta,n}(\epsilon_{1,n}>U_{1,n}/2)
+P_{\theta,n}(\epsilon_{2,n}>\beta_nU_{1,n}/2)\\
&=O(t_n^{-1})=o(1).
\end{split}
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (26)--(27) prove \(\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)\to1\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\), (20), and (25) give
\[
D_n(t_n)\longrightarrow
1-\left\{\frac12+\frac{R(\lambda)}{2\{1+R(\lambda)\}}\right\}
=\frac1{2\{1+R(\lambda)\}}
=g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,c_0}(\lambda),
\]
which proves every claimed sequential conclusion.
