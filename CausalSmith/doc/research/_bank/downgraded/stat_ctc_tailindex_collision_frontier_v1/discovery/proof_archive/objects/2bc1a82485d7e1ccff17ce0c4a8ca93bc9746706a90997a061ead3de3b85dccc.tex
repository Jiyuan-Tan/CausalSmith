The map \(C_n^*\) is constant.  Hence the inverse image of each subset of its finite range is either the whole sample space or the empty set.  Both sets belong to \(\mathcal T_n\), so \(C_n^*\) is \(\mathcal T_n\)-measurable, as required by \(\mathrm{def{:}honest\text{-}direction\text{-}class}\).  The same argument applies to every \(C_n^{*,(c)}\).

Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)\).  By the definition of the orientation projection, \(o(\theta_n)\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\).  Since \(C_n^*=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\) at every sample point,
\[
 \{o(\theta_n)\in C_n^*\}=(\mathbb R_+^2)^n
 \quad\text{and therefore}\quad
 P_{\theta,n}^{\otimes n}\{o(\theta_n)\in C_n^*\}=1. \tag{1}
\]
Taking first the infimum over \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)\) and then the limit inferior in (1) gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)}
 P_{\theta,n}^{\otimes n}\{o(\theta_n)\in C_n^*\}
 =1\geq1-\eta. \tag{2}
\]
This proves \((C_n^*)\in\mathcal H_\eta(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B))\).  Nothing in (1)--(2) uses the sign or magnitude of \(\Delta_n\), so equality \(\Delta_n=0\) is covered.  Replacing \(B_n\) by \(B_n^{(c)}=cB_n\) and repeating (1)--(2) proves \((C_n^{*,(c)})\in\mathcal H_\eta(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B^{(c)}))\) for each \(c\in\mathcal C\).

Let \(\sigma\) exchange the two coordinate labels and exchange \(\mathsf{forward}\) with \(\mathsf{reverse}\).  The alphabet is invariant, \(\sigma(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}})=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\); hence \(C_n^*(\sigma z_1,\ldots,\sigma z_n)=\sigma C_n^*(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\).  This is the asserted label equivariance.  For \(c\leq c'\), both sensitivity sets equal \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\), so the required inclusion holds with equality.

Now fix \(\kappa\) and \(K\) as in the statement.  For every sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(\theta_n\in K_n(K,\kappa;B)\), constancy gives
\[
 P_{\theta,n}^{\otimes n}\{C_n^*=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\}=1. \tag{3}
\]
The set over which the supremum is taken is nonempty, so taking its supremum in (3) still gives one.  This proves the displayed oracle ambiguity value and shows directly that it cannot converge to zero, whatever values the grid and separation scores take.

It remains to explain why this is only a partial answer rather than a calibration theorem for the prose construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}adaptive\text{-}direction\text{-}handle}\).  The declared envelope assumption supplies only
\[
 \sqrt{k}\lvert A_{j,n}(n/k)\rvert\leq B_n(k),
 \qquad j\in\{1,2\},\quad k\in\mathcal G_n. \tag{4}
\]
It supplies neither a stochastic expansion for \(\widehat D_n-D_n\) nor an inequality that converts (4) into a bias bound for \(\widehat D_n\).  More decisively, \(\mathrm{thm{:}joint\text{-}collision\text{-}experiment\text{-}obstruction}\) proves that the proposed four-coordinate experiment omits a root-\(k_n\) common-index coordinate and that its two exceedance channels have a nonzero order-\(k_n\) overlap covariance.  Consequently the summed-marginal information and the simultaneous multiplier calibration named by the handle are not consequences of the frozen assumptions.  As no unlisted calibration or bias premise may be inserted, equations (1)--(4) are the strongest direction-set guarantee proved here; no nonexistence claim about procedures under an augmented experiment is made.
