Put
\[
s_{j,n}=c_{j,n}^{1/\alpha_{j,n}},\qquad
A_n=\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n},\qquad B_n'=\epsilon_{2,n}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), \(s_{j,n}\) is the lower endpoint of the scaled-Pareto law \(Q_{j,n}\).  For any \(u\) above both component supports define
\[
p_{1,n}(u)=P(A_n>u)
=c_{1,n}\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}u^{-\alpha_{1,n}},
\qquad
p_{2,n}(u)=P(B_n'>u)=c_{2,n}u^{-\alpha_{2,n}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\) implies that, for some constants \(0<a_-<a_+<\infty\),
\[
a_-\leq\alpha_{j,n}\leq a_+,
\qquad j\in\{1,2\},
\tag{2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\) similarly bounds \(\beta_n\) above and away from zero.  Hence the support condition on \(u_n\) gives
\[
\frac{u_n}{\beta_ns_{1,n}}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{u_n}{s_{2,n}}\to\infty,
\qquad
p_{1,n}(u_n)\vee p_{2,n}(u_n)\to0.
\tag{3}
\]

We first establish a ratio-free convolution-tail estimate.  Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\).  Once \(\delta u_n\) exceeds both component supports, independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}innovation\text{-}independence}\) and the inclusions
\[
\{A_n>u_n\}\cup\{B_n'>u_n\}
\subseteq\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}
\]
and
\[
\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}
\subseteq
\{A_n>(1-\delta)u_n\}\cup
\{B_n'>(1-\delta)u_n\}\cup
\bigl(\{A_n>\delta u_n\}\cap\{B_n'>\delta u_n\}\bigr)
\]
give
\[
\begin{split}
p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}-p_{1,n}p_{2,n}
&\leq P(A_n+B_n'>u_n)\\
&\leq p_{1,n}(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}
 +p_{2,n}(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{2,n}}
 +p_{1,n}p_{2,n}\delta^{-\alpha_{1,n}-\alpha_{2,n}},
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
where \(p_{j,n}=p_{j,n}(u_n)\).  Crucially, no bound on \(p_{1,n}/p_{2,n}\) is needed, because
\[
0\leq\frac{p_{1,n}p_{2,n}}{p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}}
\leq\min(p_{1,n},p_{2,n})\to0
\tag{5}
\]
by (3).  Divide (4) by \(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}\), use (2) and (5), first let \(n\to\infty\), and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).  The lower and upper bounds both tend to one, so
\[
P(A_n+B_n'>u_n)
=p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}+o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}).
\tag{6}
\]
This argument applies to every level sequence satisfying the two support-ratio limits in (3), regardless of the ratio of its two component tails.

Let \(H_n=F_{1,n}(\epsilon_{1,n})\).  The scaled-Pareto distribution is continuous, so the probability integral transform gives \(E(H_n)=1/2\).  On \(\{A_n>u_n\}\), the exact Pareto formula yields
\[
0\leq1-H_n=c_{1,n}\epsilon_{1,n}^{-\alpha_{1,n}}
\leq c_{1,n}(u_n/\beta_n)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}=p_{1,n}.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
E\!\left[H_n\mathbf1\{A_n>u_n\}\right]
=p_{1,n}+O(p_{1,n}^2).
\tag{8}
\]
Independence gives
\[
E\!\left[H_n\mathbf1\{B_n'>u_n\}\right]=\frac12p_{2,n},
\qquad
0\leq E\!\left[H_n\mathbf1\{A_n>u_n,B_n'>u_n\}\right]
\leq p_{1,n}p_{2,n}.
\tag{9}
\]
By (6), the probability of
\(\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}\setminus(\{A_n>u_n\}\cup\{B_n'>u_n\})\)
is \(o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n})\).  Since \(0\leq H_n\leq1\), equations (5), (8), and (9) therefore imply
\[
E\!\left[H_n\mathbf1\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}\right]
=p_{1,n}+\frac12p_{2,n}+o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}).
\tag{10}
\]
Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\) and the forward orientation in \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), \(X_{1,n}=\epsilon_{1,n}\) and \(X_{2,n}=A_n+B_n'\).  Dividing (10) by (6), and using
\[
\frac{p_{1,n}(u)}{p_{2,n}(u)}
=\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}\frac{c_{1,n}}{c_{2,n}}u^{-\Delta_n}
=r_n(u),
\tag{11}
\]
proves, for every level sequence in the proposed claim,
\[
E[F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})\mid X_{2,n}>u_n]
=\frac12+\frac12\frac{r_n(u_n)}{1+r_n(u_n)}+o(1).
\tag{12}
\]
The localization bound \(|\Delta_n|\log u_n\leq L\), together with \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\), also bounds \(r_n(u_n)\) above and away from zero, although the ratio-free proof of (6) did not require that fact.

We next pass to the quantile threshold in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}intermediate\text{-}sequence}\) gives \(t_n=n/k_n\to\infty\).  Define the exact \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{1,n}\) by
\[
q_{1,n}=s_{1,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}.
\tag{13}
\]
Assume now the stated scale-locality condition \(\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}\to0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\) gives \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})=O(1)\), and direct algebra gives
\[
\log\frac{q_{1,n}}{s_{2,n}}
=\frac{\log t_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}
 +\frac{\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})}{\alpha_{1,n}}
 -\frac{\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}}{\alpha_{1,n}\alpha_{2,n}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{14}
\]
Together with \(q_{1,n}/s_{1,n}=t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}\to\infty\) and the positive coefficient limit, (14) implies
\[
\frac{\beta_nq_{1,n}}{\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n}}\to\infty.
\tag{15}
\]
At the level \(v_n=\beta_nq_{1,n}\), the two component-tail probabilities in (1) satisfy
\[
p_{1,n}(v_n)=\frac1{t_n}\to0,
\qquad
p_{2,n}(v_n)=\left(\frac{s_{2,n}}{v_n}\right)^{\alpha_{2,n}}\to0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the ratio-free estimate (6), now applied at \(v_n\) using (15)--(16), gives
\[
P(X_{2,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n})
=\{p_{1,n}(v_n)+p_{2,n}(v_n)\}\{1+o(1)\}\to0.
\tag{17}
\]
On the conditioning event \(F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})>1-1/t_n\), continuity of the first Pareto law gives \(X_{1,n}>q_{1,n}\), hence \(X_{2,n}\geq\beta_nX_{1,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n}\).  Monotonicity of \(F_{2,n}\), (17), and the definition of \(\Gamma_{12,n}\) yield the audited bound
\[
0\leq1-\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)
\leq1-F_{2,n}(\beta_nq_{1,n})
=P(X_{2,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n})\to0.
\tag{18}
\]
This proves \(\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)=1+o(1)\) without imposing any boundedness condition on \(\Delta_n\log t_n\).

Finally suppose \(\lambda_n=\Delta_n\log t_n\to\lambda\in\mathbb R\), and let \(q_{2,n}\) be the exact \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{2,n}\).  Each scaled-Pareto component has a strictly positive continuous density on the interior of its support; independence therefore makes the convolution distribution continuous and strictly increasing above \(\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n}\), so this exact quantile exists and is unique.  Set
\[
a_n=\beta_ns_{1,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}},
\qquad b_n=s_{2,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{2,n}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(X_{2,n}\geq A_n\vee B_n'\), while
\(P(A_n>\beta_ns_{1,n}(2t_n)^{1/\alpha_{1,n}})=P(B_n'>s_{2,n}(2t_n)^{1/\alpha_{2,n}})=1/(2t_n)\), the union bound gives the explicit quantile sandwich
\[
\max(a_n,b_n)\leq q_{2,n}
\leq\beta_ns_{1,n}(2t_n)^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}
 +s_{2,n}(2t_n)^{1/\alpha_{2,n}}.
\tag{20}
\]
By (2), the right side is at most \(C(a_n+b_n)\leq2C\max(a_n,b_n)\) for a fixed finite \(C\).  Therefore
\[
0\leq\log q_{2,n}-\max(\log a_n,\log b_n)\leq\log(2C).
\tag{21}
\]
The lower bound in (20) also gives
\[
\frac{q_{2,n}}{\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n}}
\geq\frac12\min\!\left(t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}},t_n^{1/\alpha_{2,n}}\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{22}
\]

Scale locality and tail-constant balance imply
\[
\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}
=\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}+\Delta_n\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\to0.
\tag{23}
\]
Using (19), \(\beta_n\to\beta_0>0\), the two index limits, (23), and \(\Delta_n\log t_n\to\lambda\), we obtain
\[
\begin{split}
\Delta_n\log a_n
&=\Delta_n\log\beta_n
 +\frac{\Delta_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}(\log c_{1,n}+\log t_n)
\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0},\\
\Delta_n\log b_n
&=\frac{\Delta_n}{\alpha_{2,n}}(\log c_{2,n}+\log t_n)
\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0}.
\end{split}
\tag{24}
\]
Because \(\Delta_n\to0\), (21) and (24) prove directly, with no circular appeal to a tail equation, that
\[
\Delta_n\log q_{2,n}\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0}.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (22) and (2) show that both component tails at \(q_{2,n}\) vanish, so the ratio-free derivation of (12) applies at \(u_n=q_{2,n}\).  Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\), and (25) give
\[
r_n(q_{2,n})
=\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}\frac{c_{1,n}}{c_{2,n}}
  \exp\{-\Delta_n\log q_{2,n}\}
\longrightarrow R(\lambda)
:=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}.
\tag{26}
\]
Continuity makes \(F_{2,n}(X_{2,n})>1-1/t_n\) equivalent to \(X_{2,n}>q_{2,n}\).  Hence (12) and (26) yield
\[
\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)
=\frac12+\frac12\frac{R(\lambda)}{1+R(\lambda)}+o(1).
\tag{27}
\]
Subtracting (27) from (18), as prescribed by \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\), proves
\[
D_n(t_n)\longrightarrow
\frac1{2\{1+R(\lambda)\}}
=\{2[1+\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}]\}^{-1}
=g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,c_0}(\lambda).
\tag{28}
\]
At \(\lambda=0\), (28) is \(\{2(1+\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0)\}^{-1}\).  Since \(R(\lambda)\to0\) as \(\lambda\to+\infty\) and \(R(\lambda)\to\infty\) as \(\lambda\to-\infty\), (28) tends respectively to \(1/2\) and to zero, completing the proof.
