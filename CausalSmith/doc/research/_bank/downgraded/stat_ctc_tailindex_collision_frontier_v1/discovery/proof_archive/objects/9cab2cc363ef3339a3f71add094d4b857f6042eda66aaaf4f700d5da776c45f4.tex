Fix an array satisfying the reverse theorem.  Exchange the labels exactly by defining
\[
\widetilde X_{1,n}=X_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde X_{2,n}=X_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde\epsilon_{1,n}=\epsilon_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde\epsilon_{2,n}=\epsilon_{1,n},
\tag{1}
\]
and set
\[
\begin{gathered}
\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n},\quad
\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}=\alpha_{1,n},\quad
\widetilde c_{1,n}=c_{2,n},\quad
\widetilde c_{2,n}=c_{1,n},\\
\widetilde U_{1,n}=U_{2,n},\quad
\widetilde U_{2,n}=U_{1,n},\quad
\widetilde A_{1,n}=A_{2,n},\quad
\widetilde A_{2,n}=A_{1,n},\quad
\widetilde\rho_1=\rho_2,quad
\widetilde\rho_2=\rho_1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The reverse equations in \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\) become
\[
\widetilde X_{1,n}=\widetilde\epsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\widetilde X_{2,n}=\beta_n\widetilde X_{1,n}+\widetilde\epsilon_{2,n}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the transformed orientation is eventually forward.  Every member condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}collision\text{-}model\text{-}class}\) is preserved by (1)--(2): iid sampling and continuity are preserved by coordinate permutation; innovation independence and the coefficient limit are unchanged; the two tail-normalization, second-order, bounded-bias, and declared-envelope requirements are merely permuted in \(j\); and the intermediate sequence is unchanged.  The transformed local coordinates satisfy
\[
\widetilde\Delta_n
=\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}-\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}
=-\Delta_n,
\qquad
\widetilde\lambda_n=-\lambda_n,
\qquad
\frac{\widetilde c_{1,n}}{\widetilde c_{2,n}}
=\frac{c_{2,n}}{c_{1,n}}\longrightarrow c_0^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
The transformed scale-locality condition also follows from the stated one.  Indeed, \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\) makes \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})=O(1)\), while \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\) gives \(\Delta_n\to0\).  Hence
\[
\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}
=\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}
+\Delta_n\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\longrightarrow0
\tag{5}
\]
and therefore
\[
\widetilde\Delta_n\log\widetilde c_{2,n}
=-\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{thm{:}class\text{-}level\text{-}collision\text{-}curve}\) to this transformed array, with transformed finite limit \(-\lambda\) and transformed limiting tail-constant ratio \(c_0^{-1}\).  Its mixture ratio is
\[
\widetilde R(-\lambda)
=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0^{-1}
\exp\{-(-\lambda)/\alpha_0\}
=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0^{-1}e^{\lambda/\alpha_0}
=R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda).
\tag{7}
\]
The coordinate permutation in (1) and \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\) give the exact relations
\[
\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)=\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),
\qquad
\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)=\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),
\qquad
\widetilde D_n(t_n)=-D_n(t_n).
\tag{8}
\]
The transformed forward limits, (7), and (8) now yield
\[
\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)\to1,
\qquad
\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)\to
\frac12+\frac{R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)}{2\{1+R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)\}},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
D_n(t_n)\to
-\frac1{2\{1+R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)\}}
=-g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,1/c_0}(-\lambda).
\tag{10}
\]
This proves the reverse class formula.

Now restrict to the reverse scaled exact-Pareto subclass in the statement.  Equations (1)--(2) turn it into a forward scaled exact-Pareto tuple of the form in \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), with transformed gap \(-\Delta_n\), transformed tail-constant limit \(c_0^{-1}\), and the same positive coefficient.  Its transformed scale-locality condition is (6).  Therefore the canonical \(\mathrm{thm{:}pareto\text{-}collision\text{-}curve}\) applies with \(\widetilde\lambda=-\lambda\); no deleted extended theorem is used.

For finite \(\lambda\), that theorem and (7)--(8) give precisely (9)--(10).  If \(\lambda=-\infty\), then \(\widetilde\lambda=+\infty\), so the canonical forward triple satisfies
\[
(\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n},\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n},\widetilde D_n)
\longrightarrow(1,1/2,1/2).
\]
Equation (8) then gives
\[
(\Gamma_{21,n},\Gamma_{12,n},D_n)\longrightarrow(1,1/2,-1/2).
\]
If \(\lambda=+\infty\), then \(\widetilde\lambda=-\infty\), and the canonical forward triple tends to \((1,1,0)\); (8) gives the same ordered triple \((\Gamma_{21,n},\Gamma_{12,n},D_n)\to(1,1,0)\).  These two endpoint calculations and the finite case prove the asserted exact-Pareto extended limit.
