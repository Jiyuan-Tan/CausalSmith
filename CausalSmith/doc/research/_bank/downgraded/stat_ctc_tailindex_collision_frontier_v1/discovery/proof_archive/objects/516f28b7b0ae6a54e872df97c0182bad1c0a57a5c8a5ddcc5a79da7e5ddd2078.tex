Put \(s_{j,n}=c_{j,n}^{1/\alpha_{j,n}}\), the lower endpoint of the scaled-Pareto law \(Q_{j,n}\), and abbreviate
\[
 A_n=\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n},\qquad B_n'=\epsilon_{2,n},\qquad
 p_{1,n}(u)=c_{1,n}\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}u^{-\alpha_{1,n}},
 \qquad p_{2,n}(u)=c_{2,n}u^{-\alpha_{2,n}}.
\]
The support condition in the proposed statement implies that, at \(u=u_n\), all displayed Pareto tail formulae apply and \(p_{1,n}(u_n)\vee p_{2,n}(u_n)\to0\).  Moreover, by the definition of \(r_n\),
\[
 \frac{p_{1,n}(u)}{p_{2,n}(u)}
 =\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}\frac{c_{1,n}}{c_{2,n}}u^{-\Delta_n}
 =r_n(u).
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\), together with \(|\Delta_n|\log u_n\leq L\), therefore bound this ratio above and away from zero uniformly for all sufficiently large \(n\).

We first prove the required one-large-jump expansion directly.  For fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), independence from \(\mathrm{ass{:}innovation\text{-}independence}\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 p_{1,n}(u)+p_{2,n}(u)-p_{1,n}(u)p_{2,n}(u)
 &\leq P(A_n+B_n'>u)\\
 &\leq p_{1,n}(u)(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}
      +p_{2,n}(u)(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{2,n}}\\
 &\quad +p_{1,n}(u)p_{2,n}(u)\delta^{-\alpha_{1,n}-\alpha_{2,n}}.
\end{split}
\]
Indeed, outside \(\{A_n>(1-\delta)u\}\cup\{B_n'>(1-\delta)u\}\), the event \(A_n+B_n'>u\) forces both \(A_n>\delta u\) and \(B_n'>\delta u\).  The tail indices eventually lie in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\).  Divide the last display by \(p_{1,n}(u)+p_{2,n}(u)\), first let \(n\to\infty\), and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).  The bounded ratio and \(p_{1,n}\vee p_{2,n}\to0\) yield, uniformly over the sequences in the proposed statement,
\[
 P(A_n+B_n'>u_n)=p_{1,n}(u_n)+p_{2,n}(u_n)
 +o\{p_{1,n}(u_n)+p_{2,n}(u_n)\}. \tag{1}
\]

Because the scaled-Pareto distribution is continuous, \(F_{1,n}(\epsilon_{1,n})\) is uniform on \([0,1]\), and hence its expectation is \(1/2\).  On \(\{A_n>u\}\),
\[
 0\leq 1-F_{1,n}(\epsilon_{1,n})
 \leq p_{1,n}(u),
\]
whereas independence gives
\[
 E\!\left[F_{1,n}(\epsilon_{1,n})\mathbf 1\{B_n'>u\}\right]
 =\frac12p_{2,n}(u).
\]
The overlap of \(\{A_n>u\}\) and \(\{B_n'>u\}\) has probability \(p_{1,n}(u)p_{2,n}(u)\), and (1) shows that the part of \(\{A_n+B_n'>u\}\) outside their union is \(o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n})\).  Consequently,
\[
 E\!\left[F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})\mathbf 1\{X_{2,n}>u_n\}\right]
 =p_{1,n}(u_n)+\frac12p_{2,n}(u_n)
 +o\{p_{1,n}(u_n)+p_{2,n}(u_n)\}. \tag{2}
\]
Dividing (2) by (1), and using \(X_{1,n}=\epsilon_{1,n}\) and \(X_{2,n}=A_n+B_n'\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\), proves
\[
 E[F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})\mid X_{2,n}>u_n]
 =\frac12+\frac12\frac{r_n(u_n)}{1+r_n(u_n)}+o(1). \tag{3}
\]

It remains to connect (3), which is indexed by a level in the observation scale, to the quantile-indexed contrast in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\).  Let \(q_{1,n}=s_{1,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}\), which is the exact \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{1,n}\).  Since \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})=O(1)\), the additional scale-locality condition gives
\[
 \log\frac{q_{1,n}}{s_{2,n}}
 =\frac{\log t_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}
  +\frac{\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})}{\alpha_{1,n}}
  -\frac{\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}}{\alpha_{1,n}\alpha_{2,n}}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{4}
\]
Also \(q_{1,n}/s_{1,n}=t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}\to\infty\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}intermediate\text{-}sequence}\).  Thus
\(\beta_nq_{1,n}/(\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n})\to\infty\).  Since \(X_{2,n}\geq\beta_nX_{1,n}\), monotonicity of \(F_{2,n}\) and (1), applied at \(\beta_nq_{1,n}\), imply
\[
 0\leq1-\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)
 \leq 1-F_{2,n}(\beta_nq_{1,n})
 =P(X_{2,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n})\longrightarrow0. \tag{5}
\]

Let \(q_{2,n}\) be the \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{2,n}\).  The elementary quantile bounds obtained from
\(\max(A_n,B_n')\leq A_n+B_n'\) and
\(A_n+B_n'\leq a+b\) on \(\{A_n\leq a,B_n'\leq b\}\) show that \(q_{2,n}/(\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n})\to\infty\).  They also put \(\log q_{2,n}\) between, up to \(O(1)\), the maximum of the two component log-quantiles at levels \(t_n\) and \(2t_n\).  Hence \(|\Delta_n\log q_{2,n}|=O(1)\) when \(\lambda_n=\Delta_n\log t_n\to\lambda\) and \(\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}\to0\).  Applying (1) at \(q_{2,n}\), and using continuity of the convolution distribution, gives
\[
 \frac1{t_n}
 =c_{2,n}q_{2,n}^{-\alpha_{2,n}}
   \{1+r_n(q_{2,n})\}\{1+o(1)\}. \tag{6}
\]
The boundedness just established also bounds \(r_n(q_{2,n})\) above and away from zero.  Taking logarithms in (6) and multiplying by \(\Delta_n/\alpha_{2,n}\) yields
\[
 \Delta_n\log q_{2,n}
 =\frac{\Delta_n}{\alpha_{2,n}}
   \left[\log t_n+\log c_{2,n}
   +\log\{1+r_n(q_{2,n})\}+o(1)\right]
 \longrightarrow\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0}. \tag{7}
\]
Using \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\), and (7),
\[
 r_n(q_{2,n})
 =\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}\frac{c_{1,n}}{c_{2,n}}
   \exp\{-\Delta_n\log q_{2,n}\}
 \longrightarrow R(\lambda)
 =\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}. \tag{8}
\]
Equation (3) at \(u_n=q_{2,n}\) is exactly
\(\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)=1/2+R(\lambda)/\{2(1+R(\lambda))\}+o(1)\).  Subtracting this expression from (5), as required by \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\), proves
\[
 D_n(t_n)\longrightarrow
 \frac1{2\{1+R(\lambda)\}}
 =\{2[1+\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}]\}^{-1}.
\]
The three stated values of the curve follow by direct evaluation at \(\lambda=0\) and by taking \(\lambda\to\pm\infty\).
