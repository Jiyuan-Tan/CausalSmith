Fix one triangular array satisfying the hypotheses. Exchange the two labels by defining
\[
\widetilde X_{1,n}=X_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde X_{2,n}=X_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde\epsilon_{1,n}=\epsilon_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde\epsilon_{2,n}=\epsilon_{1,n},
\tag{1}
\]
and set
\[
\begin{gathered}
\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}=\alpha_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde c_{1,n}=c_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde c_{2,n}=c_{1,n},\\
\widetilde U_{1,n}=U_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde U_{2,n}=U_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde A_{1,n}=A_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde A_{2,n}=A_{1,n},\\
\widetilde\rho_1=\rho_2,\qquad
\widetilde\rho_2=\rho_1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\), the reverse structural equations become
\[
\widetilde X_{1,n}=\widetilde\epsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\widetilde X_{2,n}=\beta_n\widetilde X_{1,n}+\widetilde\epsilon_{2,n}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the transformed orientation is eventually forward. Every member condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}collision\text{-}model\text{-}class}\) is preserved by (1)--(2): iid sampling and continuity of the margins are preserved by coordinate permutation; innovation independence and the coefficient limit are unchanged; the two uniform tail-normalization, uniform second-order, bounded-bias, and declared-envelope conditions are permuted in \(j\); and the intermediate sequence is unchanged. Hence the transformed array belongs to \(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)\).

The transformed local coordinates satisfy, directly from their definitions,
\[
\widetilde\Delta_n
=\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}-\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}
=-\Delta_n,
\qquad
\widetilde\lambda_n
=\widetilde\Delta_n\log t_n
=-\lambda_n,
\qquad
\frac{\widetilde c_{1,n}}{\widetilde c_{2,n}}
=\frac{c_{2,n}}{c_{1,n}}\longrightarrow c_0^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
It remains to verify the transformed scale-locality premise. By \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\), \(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n}\to c_0\in(0,\infty)\), so \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})=O(1)\). By \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\), \(\Delta_n\to0\). Consequently,
\[
\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}
=\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}
+\Delta_n\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\longrightarrow0,
\tag{5}
\]
where the first term tends to zero by the hypothesis of the theorem and the second by the preceding two facts. Therefore
\[
\widetilde\Delta_n\log\widetilde c_{2,n}
=-\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{thm{:}class\text{-}level\text{-}collision\text{-}curve}\) to the transformed array, whose finite collision-coordinate limit is \(-\lambda\) and whose limiting tail-constant ratio is \(c_0^{-1}\). Its limiting mixture ratio is
\[
\widetilde R(-\lambda)
=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0^{-1}
\exp\{-(-\lambda)/\alpha_0\}
=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0^{-1}e^{\lambda/\alpha_0}
=R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda).
\tag{7}
\]
Under (1), the transformed marginal distribution functions are \(\widetilde F_{1,n}=F_{2,n}\) and \(\widetilde F_{2,n}=F_{1,n}\). Substitution into \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\) therefore gives the exact identities
\[
\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)=\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),
\qquad
\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)=\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),
\qquad
\widetilde D_n(t_n)=-D_n(t_n).
\tag{8}
\]
The transformed forward limits, (7), and (8) yield
\[
\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)\to1,
\qquad
\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)\to
\frac12+\frac{R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)}{2\{1+R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)\}},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
D_n(t_n)\to
-\frac1{2\{1+R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)\}}
=-g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,1/c_0}(-\lambda).
\tag{10}
\]
Because the argument fixed one qualifying array before applying the sequential forward theorem, (9)--(10) assert neither uniformity over arrays nor a class-uniform remainder.

Finally, restrict to the reverse scaled exact-Pareto subclass in the statement. Equations (1)--(2) transform it into a forward scaled exact-Pareto tuple of the form in \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), with gap \(-\Delta_n\), limiting tail-constant ratio \(c_0^{-1}\), the same positive coefficient, and scale locality (6). Thus \(\mathrm{thm{:}pareto\text{-}collision\text{-}curve}\) applies with \(\widetilde\lambda=-\lambda\). For finite \(\lambda\), that theorem together with (7)--(8) gives (9)--(10). If \(\lambda=-\infty\), then \(\widetilde\lambda=+\infty\), so the canonical forward triple obeys
\[
(\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),\widetilde D_n(t_n))
\longrightarrow(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12).
\]
Equation (8) gives
\[
(\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),D_n(t_n))
\longrightarrow(1,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12).
\]
If \(\lambda=+\infty\), then \(\widetilde\lambda=-\infty\), and the canonical forward triple tends to \((1,1,0)\); (8) yields the same ordered triple \((\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),D_n(t_n))\to(1,1,0)\). These two endpoint calculations and the finite case prove the asserted exact-Pareto extended limit.
