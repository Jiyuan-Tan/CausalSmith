We give two explicit certificates, both inside the exact-Pareto zero-bias subclass.  Fix the forward orientation, \(\beta_n=\beta_0\), \(c_{1,n}=c_0\), \(c_{2,n}=1\), and \(A_{1,n}=A_{2,n}=0\).  For the first array set
\(\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=\alpha_0\); for the second set
\[
 \alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n}=\alpha_0+k_n^{-1/4}.
\]
Both arrays satisfy \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\), have \(\Delta_n=0\), and therefore have the same collision coordinates
\[
 (\lambda_n,h_n,b_{1,n},b_{2,n})=(0,0,0,0).
\]
All other membership conditions in \(\mathrm{def{:}collision\text{-}model\text{-}class}\) hold: the innovations are independent continuous exact-Pareto variables, the coefficient is positive and constant, the second-order auxiliaries vanish, and every declared nonnegative bias envelope contains zero.

The marginal upper-order-statistic array belongs to \(\mathcal T_n\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}tail\text{-}rank\text{-}experiment}\).  From its first coordinate define the Hill statistic
\[
 H_n=\frac1{k_n}\sum_{j=1}^{k_n}
 \log\frac{X_{(n-j+1),1,n}}{X_{(n-k_n),1,n}}.
\]
If the common Pareto index is \(a>0\), then \(\log X_{i,1,n}\) is exponential with rate \(a\).  The elementary exponential-spacing representation gives
\[
 H_n\ \stackrel{d}{=}\ \frac1{k_n}\sum_{j=1}^{k_n}E_{j,n},
 \qquad E_{1,n},\ldots,E_{k_n,n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Exp}(a).
\]
Indeed, successive upper exponential spacings have respective rates \(a,2a,\ldots,k_na\), and multiplying the spacing with rate \(ja\) by \(j\) gives an exponential variable of rate \(a\); expanding the sum defining \(H_n\) produces exactly these multiplied spacings.  Hence
\[
 E_aH_n=\frac1a,qquad \operatorname{Var}_a(H_n)=\frac1{k_na^2}. \tag{1}
\]
For \(a_{0,n}=\alpha_0\) and \(a_{1,n}=\alpha_0+k_n^{-1/4}\),
\[
 \left|\frac1{a_{1,n}}-\frac1{a_{0,n}}\right|
 =\frac{k_n^{-1/4}}{\alpha_0(\alpha_0+k_n^{-1/4})}
 \asymp k_n^{-1/4}. \tag{2}
\]
The test that compares \(H_n\) with the midpoint of the two means has, by Chebyshev's inequality and (1)--(2), the sum of its two error probabilities bounded by \(O(k_n^{-1/2})\to0\).  Thus the total-variation distance between the two restrictions to \(\mathcal T_n\) tends to one although their entire declared finite local coordinate vectors coincide.  In particular, no likelihood expansion centered only at \((\lambda,h,b_1,b_2)\) can hold uniformly over \(K_n(K,\kappa;B)\): the omitted common-index coordinate
\(\sqrt{k_n}\{(\alpha_{1,n}+\alpha_{2,n})/2-\alpha_0\}\) equals zero in the first array and diverges in the second.

There is a separate obstruction to the proposed information form.  Keep the first array and write
\[
 Y_{a,n}=N_n^{(a)}((1,\infty)\times[0,1]),\qquad a\in\{1,2\}.
\]
Because the marginal quantiles are continuous, \(EY_{1,n}=EY_{2,n}=n/t_n=k_n\).  Here \(q_{1,n}=(c_0t_n)^{1/\alpha_0}\).  The same direct one-large-jump bounds used for two independent Pareto variables give
\[
 P(\beta_0\epsilon_{1,n}+\epsilon_{2,n}>x)
 =(\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0+1)x^{-\alpha_0}\{1+o(1)\},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \frac{q_{2,n}}{q_{1,n}}
 \longrightarrow\left(\frac{\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0+1}{c_0}\right)^{1/\alpha_0},
 \qquad
 \frac{q_{2,n}}{\beta_0q_{1,n}}
 \longrightarrow\left(1+\frac1{\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0}\right)^{1/\alpha_0}>1. \tag{3}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the event \(\{\epsilon_{1,n}>q_{2,n}/\beta_0\}\) is contained in both exceedance events.  Independence across observations from \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}triangular\text{-}sampling}\), followed by (3), yields
\[
\begin{split}
 \frac1{k_n}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1,n},Y_{2,n})
 &=\frac n{k_n}\left[P(X_{1,n}>q_{1,n},X_{2,n}>q_{2,n})-t_n^{-2}\right]\\
 &\geq t_nP(\epsilon_{1,n}>q_{2,n}/\beta_0)-t_n^{-1}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0}{1+\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0}>0.
\end{split}\tag{4}
\]
Thus the limiting compensated measures possess a nonzero cross-covariance measure.  The two marginal normalized intensities \(M_{n,\theta}^{(1)}\) and \(M_{n,\theta}^{(2)}\) do not record that measure, while the displayed candidate \(I_{rs}\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}joint\text{-}experiment\text{-}handle}\) contains no cross term.  Equations (1)--(4) prove both asserted obstructions and identify the minimum repairs required before a joint LAN calculation can be valid.
