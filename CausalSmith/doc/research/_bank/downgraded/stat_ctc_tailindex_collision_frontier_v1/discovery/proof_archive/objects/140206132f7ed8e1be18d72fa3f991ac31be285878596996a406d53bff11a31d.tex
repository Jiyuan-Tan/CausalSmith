Put
\[
s_{j,n}=c_{j,n}^{1/\alpha_{j,n}},\qquad
A_n=\beta_n\epsilon_{1,n},\qquad B_n'=\epsilon_{2,n}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), \(s_{j,n}\) is the lower support endpoint of \(Q_{j,n}\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\) supplies constants \(0<a_-<a_+<\infty\) such that
\[
a_-\leq\alpha_{j,n}\leq a_+\qquad(j=1,2)
\tag{2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}coefficient\text{-}limit}\) bounds \(\beta_n\) above and away from zero. At every level above the component supports, the exact tails in \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\) give
\[
p_{1,n}(u):=P(A_n>u)
=c_{1,n}\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}u^{-\alpha_{1,n}},
\qquad
p_{2,n}(u):=P(B_n'>u)=c_{2,n}u^{-\alpha_{2,n}}.
\tag{3}
\]
The support-ratio condition in the proposed statement and (2) imply
\[
\frac{u_n}{\beta_ns_{1,n}}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{u_n}{s_{2,n}}\to\infty,
\qquad
p_{1,n}(u_n)\vee p_{2,n}(u_n)\to0.
\tag{4}
\]

We first prove a ratio-free one-large-jump identity. Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the levels \(\delta u_n\) and \((1-\delta)u_n\) exceed both component supports by (4). Independence from \(\mathrm{ass{:}innovation\text{-}independence}\), together with
\[
\{A_n>u_n\}\cup\{B_n'>u_n\}
\subseteq\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}
\]
and
\[
\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}
\subseteq
\{A_n>(1-\delta)u_n\}\cup\{B_n'>(1-\delta)u_n\}
\cup\bigl(\{A_n>\delta u_n\}\cap\{B_n'>\delta u_n\}\bigr),
\]
yields, with \(p_{j,n}=p_{j,n}(u_n)\),
\[
\begin{split}
p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}-p_{1,n}p_{2,n}
&\leq P(A_n+B_n'>u_n)\\
&\leq p_{1,n}(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}
+p_{2,n}(1-\delta)^{-\alpha_{2,n}}
+p_{1,n}p_{2,n}\delta^{-\alpha_{1,n}-\alpha_{2,n}}.
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
0\leq\frac{p_{1,n}p_{2,n}}{p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}}
\leq\min(p_{1,n},p_{2,n})\to0
\tag{6}
\]
by (4). Divide (5) by the positive number \(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}\), use (2) and (6), first let \(n\to\infty\), and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\). This proves
\[
P(A_n+B_n'>u_n)
=p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}+o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}).
\tag{7}
\]
No bound on \(p_{1,n}/p_{2,n}\), and therefore no bound on \(|\Delta_n|\log u_n\), was used.

Let \(H_n=F_{1,n}(\epsilon_{1,n})\). The first exact-Pareto law is continuous. More explicitly, for \(0\leq y<1\), its distribution formula gives
\[
P(H_n\leq y)
=P\!\left(\epsilon_{1,n}\leq s_{1,n}(1-y)^{-1/\alpha_{1,n}}\right)=y,
\]
so \(H_n\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and \(E(H_n)=1/2\). On \(\{A_n>u_n\}\),
\[
0\leq1-H_n=c_{1,n}\epsilon_{1,n}^{-\alpha_{1,n}}
\leq c_{1,n}(u_n/\beta_n)^{-\alpha_{1,n}}=p_{1,n}.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently,
\[
E[H_n\mathbf1\{A_n>u_n\}]=p_{1,n}+O(p_{1,n}^2),
\qquad
E[H_n\mathbf1\{B_n'>u_n\}]=\frac12p_{2,n},
\tag{9}
\]
where the second equality uses \(\mathrm{ass{:}innovation\text{-}independence}\). The intersection contribution is at most \(p_{1,n}p_{2,n}\). By (7), the part of \(\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}\) outside the union of the two one-component exceedances has probability \(o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n})\). Since \(0\leq H_n\leq1\), equations (6)--(9) imply
\[
E[H_n\mathbf1\{A_n+B_n'>u_n\}]
=p_{1,n}+\frac12p_{2,n}+o(p_{1,n}+p_{2,n}).
\tag{10}
\]
Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\) and the forward orientation of \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), \(X_{1,n}=\epsilon_{1,n}\) and \(X_{2,n}=A_n+B_n'\). Dividing (10) by (7) is valid because the denominator is positive, and
\[
\frac{p_{1,n}(u)}{p_{2,n}(u)}
=\beta_n^{\alpha_{1,n}}\frac{c_{1,n}}{c_{2,n}}u^{-\Delta_n}=r_n(u).
\tag{11}
\]
This proves the arbitrary-level conditional-mixture formula.

We now impose \(\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}\to0\). By \(\mathrm{ass{:}intermediate\text{-}sequence}\), \(t_n=n/k_n\to\infty\), and the exact \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{1,n}\) is
\[
q_{1,n}=s_{1,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}.
\tag{12}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\) gives \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})=O(1)\). Hence
\[
\log\frac{q_{1,n}}{s_{2,n}}
=\frac{\log t_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}
+\frac{\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})}{\alpha_{1,n}}
-\frac{\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}}{\alpha_{1,n}\alpha_{2,n}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{13}
\]
Together with \(q_{1,n}/s_{1,n}=t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}\to\infty\) and \(\beta_n\to\beta_0>0\), this gives
\[
\frac{\beta_nq_{1,n}}{\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n}}\to\infty.
\tag{14}
\]
Apply (7) at \(u=\beta_nq_{1,n}\). The two component probabilities are \(1/t_n\) and \((s_{2,n}/(\beta_nq_{1,n}))^{\alpha_{2,n}}\), and both tend to zero by (2), (13), and \(t_n\to\infty\). Therefore
\[
P(X_{2,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n})\to0.
\tag{15}
\]
Continuity of the first margin makes \(F_{1,n}(X_{1,n})>1-1/t_n\) equivalent almost surely to \(X_{1,n}>q_{1,n}\). On this event, positivity of \(\beta_n\) and nonnegativity of \(\epsilon_{2,n}\) give \(X_{2,n}=\beta_nX_{1,n}+\epsilon_{2,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n}\). Thus \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\), monotonicity of \(F_{2,n}\), and (15) give
\[
0\leq1-\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)
\leq1-F_{2,n}(\beta_nq_{1,n})
=P(X_{2,n}>\beta_nq_{1,n})\to0.
\tag{16}
\]

Let \(q_{2,n}\) be the exact \((1-1/t_n)\)-quantile of \(X_{2,n}\). It exists uniquely because the sum of the two independent continuous scaled-Pareto variables has a continuous distribution that is strictly increasing above its lower support endpoint. Set
\[
a_n=\beta_ns_{1,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}},
\qquad b_n=s_{2,n}t_n^{1/\alpha_{2,n}},
\qquad M_n=\max(a_n,b_n).
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(X_{2,n}\geq A_n\vee B_n'\), the component tail equalities at \(a_n\) and \(b_n\) imply \(M_n\leq q_{2,n}\). On the other hand, the union bound at the two component \((1-1/(2t_n))\)-quantiles gives
\[
q_{2,n}
\leq\beta_ns_{1,n}(2t_n)^{1/\alpha_{1,n}}
+s_{2,n}(2t_n)^{1/\alpha_{2,n}}
\leq C M_n
\tag{18}
\]
for a fixed \(C<\infty\), by the lower bound in (2). The lower bound also gives
\[
\frac{q_{2,n}}{\beta_ns_{1,n}+s_{2,n}}
\geq\frac12\min\{t_n^{1/\alpha_{1,n}},t_n^{1/\alpha_{2,n}}\}
\to\infty.
\tag{19}
\]
Scale locality and \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\) imply
\[
\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}
=\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}+\Delta_n\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\to0.
\tag{20}
\]
Writing \(\lambda_n=\Delta_n\log t_n\), equations (17), (20), \(\Delta_n\to0\), and the boundedness of \(\log\beta_n\) give
\[
\Delta_n\log a_n=\frac{\lambda_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}+o(1),
\qquad
\Delta_n\log b_n=\frac{\lambda_n}{\alpha_{2,n}}+o(1).
\tag{21}
\]
Because \(\alpha_{j,n}\to\alpha_0>0\), both expressions in (21) converge to \(\lambda/\alpha_0\) when \(\lambda\) is finite, diverge to \(+\infty\) when \(\lambda=+\infty\), and diverge to \(-\infty\) when \(\lambda=-\infty\). From (18),
\[
|\Delta_n|\,|\log q_{2,n}-\log M_n|
\leq|\Delta_n|\log C\to0
\tag{22}
\]
by \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\). Since \(M_n\) equals either \(a_n\) or \(b_n\), equations (21)--(22) prove that \(\Delta_n\log q_{2,n}\) has the corresponding extended-real limit. Consequently,
\[
r_n(q_{2,n})\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
+\infty, & \lambda=-\infty,\\
\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}, & \lambda\in\mathbb R,\\
0, & \lambda=+\infty.
\end{cases}
\tag{23}
\]
By (19), the arbitrary-level formula already proved applies at \(u_n=q_{2,n}\). Continuity and strict increase make the conditioning event in \(\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)\) equal almost surely to \(\{X_{2,n}>q_{2,n}\}\). Substitution of (23) into the conditional-mixture formula yields
\[
\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
1, & \lambda=-\infty,\\
\displaystyle\frac12+\frac12\frac{\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}}{1+\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0e^{-\lambda/\alpha_0}}, & \lambda\in\mathbb R,\\
\frac12, & \lambda=+\infty.
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (16) and (24), and using \(D_n=\Gamma_{12,n}-\Gamma_{21,n}\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\), gives precisely the three asserted triples. At finite \(\lambda\), direct subtraction gives \(g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,c_0}(\lambda)\); setting \(\lambda=0\) gives the stated equality-point value.
