Fix a triangular array satisfying the first clause. Define a coordinate-exchanged array by
\[
\widetilde X_{1,n}=X_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde X_{2,n}=X_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde\epsilon_{1,n}=\epsilon_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde\epsilon_{2,n}=\epsilon_{1,n},
\tag{1}
\]
and set
\[
\begin{gathered}
\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}=\alpha_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}=\alpha_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde c_{1,n}=c_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde c_{2,n}=c_{1,n},\\
\widetilde U_{1,n}=U_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde U_{2,n}=U_{1,n},\qquad
\widetilde A_{1,n}=A_{2,n},\qquad
\widetilde A_{2,n}=A_{1,n},\\
\widetilde\rho_1=\rho_2,
\qquad
\widetilde\rho_2=\rho_1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}linear\text{-}scm}\), eventual reverse orientation and (1) give
\[
\widetilde X_{1,n}=\widetilde\epsilon_{1,n},
\qquad
\widetilde X_{2,n}=\beta_n\widetilde X_{1,n}+\widetilde\epsilon_{2,n},
\tag{3}
\]
so the exchanged array is eventually forward. We next verify its class membership rather than assuming it. By \(\mathrm{def{:}collision\text{-}model\text{-}class}\), iid sampling and continuity of both margins are preserved by the measurable coordinate permutation in (1). Innovation independence is preserved by exchanging the two innovations. The coefficient sequence is unchanged. The two uniform tail-normalization conditions, the two uniform second-order conditions, and the negativity conditions on \(\rho_1,\rho_2\) are exchanged by (2). Moreover,
\[
\widetilde b_{1,n}=\sqrt{k_n}\widetilde A_{1,n}(t_n)=b_{2,n},
\qquad
\widetilde b_{2,n}=\sqrt{k_n}\widetilde A_{2,n}(t_n)=b_{1,n},
\tag{4}
\]
so boundedness of the bias coordinates is preserved; the two inequalities in the declared bias envelope are likewise exchanged. The common intermediate sequence is unchanged. Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\) gives
\[
\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}\longrightarrow\alpha_0,
\qquad
\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}\longrightarrow\alpha_0,
\qquad
\widetilde\Delta_n
=\widetilde\alpha_{1,n}-\widetilde\alpha_{2,n}
=-\Delta_n\longrightarrow0,
\tag{5}
\]
and \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\) gives
\[
\frac{\widetilde c_{1,n}}{\widetilde c_{2,n}}
=\frac{c_{2,n}}{c_{1,n}}
\longrightarrow c_0^{-1}\in(0,\infty).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the exchanged array satisfies every defining predicate of \(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)\), with the tail-balance constant instantiated as \(\widetilde c_0=c_0^{-1}\).

The exchanged intrinsic forward coordinate is exactly, with no remainder,
\[
\widetilde\xi_n
=\widetilde\Delta_n\log\widetilde U_{2,n}(t_n)
=-\Delta_n\log U_{1,n}(t_n)
=\xi_{\mathrm{rev},n}
\longrightarrow\xi_{\mathrm{rev}}.
\tag{7}
\]
All divisions and logarithms in (6)--(7) are valid because the tail constants, tail quantiles, and \(t_n\) are positive by their declared types, and \(c_0>0\). Therefore \(\mathrm{thm{:}class\text{-}level\text{-}collision\text{-}curve}\), applied to the exchanged array with tail-balance constant \(\widetilde c_0=c_0^{-1}\), gives the mixture ratio
\[
\widetilde R_\xi
=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0^{-1}\exp(-\xi_{\mathrm{rev}})
=R_{\mathrm{rev},\xi}
\tag{8}
\]
and the limits
\[
\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)\longrightarrow
\frac12+\frac{R_{\mathrm{rev},\xi}}{2(1+R_{\mathrm{rev},\xi})},
\qquad
\widetilde D_n(t_n)\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{2(1+R_{\mathrm{rev},\xi})}.
\tag{9}
\]
The exchanged marginal distribution functions satisfy \(\widetilde F_{1,n}=F_{2,n}\) and \(\widetilde F_{2,n}=F_{1,n}\). Substitution in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}threshold\text{-}ctc}\) gives the exact identities
\[
\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n)=\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),
\qquad
\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n)=\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),
\qquad
\widetilde D_n(t_n)=-D_n(t_n).
\tag{10}
\]
Combining (9) and (10) proves all three intrinsic-coordinate limits. Because we fixed one qualifying array before applying the sequential forward theorem, the argument supplies neither a supremum over arrays nor a class-uniform remainder.

For the logarithmic-scale corollary, assume additionally that \(\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}\to0\) and \(\lambda_n\to\lambda\in\mathbb R\). The definition of \(c_{1,n}\) gives the exact identity
\[
\alpha_{1,n}\log U_{1,n}(t_n)=\log t_n+\log c_{1,n}.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n}\to c_0\in(0,\infty)\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}tail\text{-}constant\text{-}balance}\), continuity of the logarithm implies \(\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})\to\log c_0\), hence this logarithmic ratio is bounded. Since \(\Delta_n\to0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}tail\text{-}collision}\),
\[
\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}
=\Delta_n\log c_{2,n}
+\Delta_n\log(c_{1,n}/c_{2,n})
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
Multiplying (11) by \(-\Delta_n/\alpha_{1,n}\), and using \(\alpha_{1,n}\to\alpha_0>0\), yields
\[
\xi_{\mathrm{rev},n}
=-\frac{\lambda_n}{\alpha_{1,n}}
-\frac{\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}}{\alpha_{1,n}}
\longrightarrow-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_0}.
\tag{13}
\]
The eventual division by \(\alpha_{1,n}\) is legitimate because its positive limit is \(\alpha_0\). Substitution of (13) into (8) gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{rev},\xi}
=\beta_0^{\alpha_0}c_0^{-1}\exp(\lambda/\alpha_0)
=R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda).
\tag{14}
\]
By the definition of \(g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,1/c_0}\),
\[
-\frac{1}{2\{1+R_{\mathrm{rev}}(\lambda)\}}
=-g_{\alpha_0,\beta_0,1/c_0}(-\lambda),
\tag{15}
\]
which proves the stated logarithmic-scale corollary.

Finally, restrict to the reverse scaled exact-Pareto subclass in the statement. Equations (1)--(2) transform it into the forward scaled exact-Pareto tuple of \(\mathrm{def{:}pareto\text{-}witness}\), with gap \(-\Delta_n\), limiting tail-constant ratio \(c_0^{-1}\), and collision coordinate \(\widetilde\lambda_n=-\lambda_n\). The scale-locality premise required after exchange also holds: (12) implies
\[
\widetilde\Delta_n\log\widetilde c_{2,n}
=-\Delta_n\log c_{1,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{thm{:}pareto\text{-}collision\text{-}curve}\) applies. When \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), its finite forward row, (10), and (14)--(15) give the middle row in the statement. When \(\lambda=-\infty\), one has \(\widetilde\lambda=+\infty\), so that theorem gives
\[
(\widetilde\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),\widetilde\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),\widetilde D_n(t_n))
\longrightarrow(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12);
\]
then (10) gives \((\Gamma_{21,n}(t_n),\Gamma_{12,n}(t_n),D_n(t_n))\longrightarrow(1,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)\). When \(\lambda=+\infty\), one has \(\widetilde\lambda=-\infty\), the forward triple tends to \((1,1,0)\), and (10) yields the same ordered reverse triple. These cases prove the exact-Pareto extended endpoint assertion.
