For every \(n\), define the sample map
\[
 C_n^*(z_1,\ldots,z_n)=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}
 \qquad\text{for every }(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in(\mathbb R_+^2)^n,
\]
and, for every \(c\in\mathcal C\), define \(C_n^{*,(c)}=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\).  Then, for every \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\),
\[
 (C_n^*)\in\mathcal H_\eta(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B)),
 \qquad
 (C_n^{*,(c)})\in\mathcal H_\eta(\Theta_{\mathrm{CTC}}(B^{(c)}))
 \quad(c\in\mathcal C).
\]
Their coverage probability is exactly one for every member of the respective model class, including every member with \(\Delta_n=0\).  They are equivariant under exchanging the two variable labels, and
\[
 c\leq c'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 C_n^{*,(c)}=C_n^{*,(c')}=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}.
\]
For every \(\kappa\in[0,\infty]\) and compact \(K\subset\mathcal L_\kappa\) such that \(K_n(K,\kappa;B)\) is nonempty for all sufficiently large \(n\), the resulting finite-grid oracle ambiguity statement is the exact, grid-independent value
\[
 \sup_{\theta_n\in K_n(K,\kappa;B)}
 P_{\theta,n}^{\otimes n}\{C_n^*=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{dir}}\}=1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), irrespective of \(\mathcal G_n\), \(B_n(k)\), \(s_{\mathrm{CTC},n}(k)\), and \(s_{\mathrm{idx},n}(k)\).  Thus the frozen premises give an exact partial answer to the adaptive-set question, but they do not give the requested vanishing-ambiguity conclusion when the reported separations diverge.
