For every \((s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)\), suppose \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\ne\varnothing\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{s,a})=|\mathcal R|\). Then \(|\mathcal S|\ge |\mathcal R|\), the feasible set \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\) is a singleton, and its linear image \(\mathcal J_{s,a}\) is a singleton.
%%% FIELD helper-proof
Fix \((s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)\). By \(\mathrm{def{:}static\text{-}chen\text{-}cell\text{-}program}\),
\[
\mathcal W_{s,a}=\{w\in\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal R|}:A_{s,a}w=\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}\}.
\]
If \(w,w'\in\mathcal W_{s,a}\), subtraction of their defining equalities gives
\[
A_{s,a}(w-w')=\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}-\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}=0.
\]
The hypothesis \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{s,a})=|\mathcal R|\) says that the \(|\mathcal R|\) columns are linearly independent, hence \(\ker(A_{s,a})=\{0\}\). Therefore \(w-w'=0\), so \(w=w'\). Nonemptiness then makes \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\) a singleton. Since the rank of a \(|\mathcal S|\)-by-\(|\mathcal R|\) matrix is at most \(\min\{|\mathcal S|,|\mathcal R|\}\), the same rank hypothesis implies \(|\mathcal S|\ge |\mathcal R|\). Finally, \(\mathcal J_{s,a}\) is, by the same definition, the image of \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\) under the scalar-valued map
\[
w\longmapsto \mathbf 1^\top A_{s,a}\operatorname{diag}((r)_{r\in\mathcal R})(w+\mathbf 1),
\]
and the image of a singleton is a singleton.

Sanity Corollary (one-stage Chen reduction). Let \(H=1\), suppose \(\mathcal K(p)\ne\varnothing\), and let \(S_2\) play Chen's always-observed \(F\) role. For every \(s\) with \(p(S_1=s)>0\) and every \(a\in\mathcal A\), behavior positivity implies \(p(S_1=s,A_1=a)>0\). In each such cell define the observed respondent matrix and nonrespondent vector from the observed reward, response, and shadow table by \(A_{s,a}[s',r]=p(M_1=1,S_2=s',R_1=r\mid S_1=s,A_1=a)\) and \(\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}[s']=p(M_1=0,S_2=s'\mid S_1=s,A_1=a)\). Suppose cellwise response positivity holds: \(\sum_{s'\in\mathcal S}A_{s,a}[s',r]>0\) for every \((s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)\) and \(r\in\mathcal R\). Then the global fiber decomposes cellwise as \(\mathcal K(p)\cong\prod_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}\mathcal W_{s,a}\), where \(\mathcal W_{s,a}=\{w\in\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal R|}:A_{s,a}w=\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}\}\), and its value image is \(\Theta_I(p)=\{\sum_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}p(S_1=s)\pi_1(a\mid s)\theta_{s,a}:\theta_{s,a}\in\mathcal J_{s,a}\}\). Hence the endpoints are the corresponding weighted sums of the Chen cell-program endpoints. If every positive-mass cell matrix satisfies the precise full-column-rank condition \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{s,a})=|\mathcal R|\), necessarily with \(|\mathcal S|\ge|\mathcal R|\), then feasibility makes every \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\) a singleton and the one-stage value is point identified. This is the cell program of Chen, Simchi-Levi, and Xiong, arXiv:2602.16061v1, source section titled \emph{Partial Identification via Linear Programs}, equation label \(\texttt{opt:mnar-with-shadow-matrix-form-theta}\), and proposition label \(\texttt{prop:point-id-mean}\).
%%% FIELD target-proof
The proof uses four finite-dimensional techniques: cross-product algebra to obtain a cellwise odds parameterization, an explicit inverse construction to prove sharpness, importance-ratio cancellation to compute the policy value, and compact convex linear programming to obtain the endpoints. The resulting matrix program is the finite one-stage program at the cited location of Chen, Simchi-Levi, and Xiong; every property needed here is derived below.

Put \(c_{sa}=p(S_1=s,A_1=a)\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}model\text{-}compatibility}\) supplies at least one \(\bar q\in\mathcal K(p)\). By \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\text{-}fiber}\), observed-cell marginalization for \(\bar q\) and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}behavior\text{-}factorization}\) give
\[
c_{sa}=\bar q_1^O(s,a)
=\mu_1(a\mid s)\sum_{a'\in\mathcal A}\bar q_1^O(s,a')
=\mu_1(a\mid s)p(S_1=s).
\tag{1}
\]
If \(p(S_1=s)>0\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}behavior\text{-}positivity}\) yields
\[
c_{sa}\ge \epsilon p(S_1=s)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every action cell above a positive-mass state belongs to \(\mathcal X_{SA}(p)\); cells above a zero-mass state carry no mass under any \(q\in\mathcal K(p)\).

Fix \((s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)\) and \(q\in\mathcal K(p)\). Because \(c_{sa}>0\), define the conditional respondent and nonrespondent masses
\[
a_{sa}(s',r)
:=q(M_1=1,S_2=s',R_1=r\mid S_1=s,A_1=a),
\qquad
b_{sa}(s',r)
:=q(M_1=0,S_2=s',R_1=r\mid S_1=s,A_1=a).
\tag{3}
\]
Observed-cell marginalization in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\text{-}fiber}\), followed by \(O_\#q=p\), identifies
\[
a_{sa}(s',r)=A_{s,a}[s',r],
\qquad
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}b_{sa}(s',r)=\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}[s'].
\tag{4}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}weak\text{-}shadow}\) gives the unconditional cross-product equations. Dividing those equations by \(c_{sa}^2>0\), justified by (2), gives, for all \(u,s'\in\mathcal S\) and \(r\in\mathcal R\),
\[
a_{sa}(u,r)b_{sa}(s',r)=b_{sa}(u,r)a_{sa}(s',r).
\tag{5}
\]
For each \(r\), set
\[
d_{sa,r}:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal S}a_{sa}(u,r),
\qquad
e_{sa,r}:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal S}b_{sa}(u,r).
\tag{6}
\]
The stated cellwise response-positivity condition and (4) imply \(d_{sa,r}>0\). Summing (5) over \(u\) therefore yields
\[
d_{sa,r}b_{sa}(s',r)=e_{sa,r}a_{sa}(s',r),
\qquad
b_{sa}(s',r)=a_{sa}(s',r)w_{sa,r},
\quad
w_{sa,r}:=\frac{e_{sa,r}}{d_{sa,r}}\ge 0.
\tag{7}
\]
This derivation also covers cells with \(a_{sa}(s',r)=0\): equation (7), rather than division by that entry, forces the corresponding nonrespondent mass to vanish. Summing (7) over \(r\) and using (4) gives, coordinate by coordinate in \(s'\),
\[
\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}[s']
=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}A_{s,a}[s',r]w_{sa,r},
\qquad
A_{s,a}w_{sa}=\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}.
\tag{8}
\]
Hence \(w_{sa}\in\mathcal W_{s,a}\). Equation (7) and \(d_{sa,r}>0\) also show that \(w_{sa,r}\) is uniquely recovered from \(q\), so the map
\[
\Phi:\mathcal K(p)\longrightarrow
\prod_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}\mathcal W_{s,a},
\qquad
q\longmapsto (w_{sa})_{s,a},
\tag{9}
\]
is well defined.

For the converse, take any family \((w_{sa})_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}\) in the product on the right of (9). On a positive cell define
\[
q^w(R_1=r,M_1=1,S_2=s'\mid S_1=s,A_1=a)
:=A_{s,a}[s',r],
\tag{10}
\]
\[
q^w(R_1=r,M_1=0,S_2=s'\mid S_1=s,A_1=a)
:=A_{s,a}[s',r]w_{sa,r}.
\tag{11}
\]
The total mass in (10)--(11) is one, because feasibility \(A_{s,a}w_{sa}=\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}\) implies
\[
\sum_{s',r}A_{s,a}[s',r](1+w_{sa,r})
=p(M_1=1\mid S_1=s,A_1=a)+\mathbf 1^\top\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}
=1.
\tag{12}
\]
Give this conditional law baseline mass \(c_{sa}\), and give cells with \(p(S_1=s)=0\) zero mass. The resulting \(q^w\) is a probability law on \(\Omega\). Equations (10) and (8) reproduce respectively every observed respondent cell and every observed nonrespondent \(S_2\)-cell, so \(O_\#q^w=p\). Its baseline marginal is \(p(S_1=s,A_1=a)\); equation (1), which is a restriction on the compatible observed law \(p\), therefore verifies the known behavior factorization. Finally, for fixed \((s,a,r)\), its two conditional \(S_2\)-vectors are \((A_{s,a}[s',r])_{s'}\) and \((A_{s,a}[s',r]w_{sa,r})_{s'}\). Consequently
\[
A_{s,a}[u,r]A_{s,a}[s',r]w_{sa,r}
=A_{s,a}[u,r]w_{sa,r}A_{s,a}[s',r],
\tag{13}
\]
and multiplication by \(c_{sa}^2\) verifies every weak-shadow cross-product equation, including all zero cells. Thus \(q^w\in\mathcal K(p)\). Equations (7), (10), and (11) show that the two constructions are inverse to one another. This proves the cellwise bijection
\[
\mathcal K(p)\cong
\prod_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}\mathcal W_{s,a}.
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to compute the target rather than define it. Since \(H=1\), the complete history before the first action is just \(S_1\), so \(\pi_1(a\mid H_1^F)=\pi_1(a\mid s)\). By \(\mathrm{def{:}stage\text{-}value}\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}behavior\text{-}positivity}\) makes every denominator below positive, and (1), (7), and (10)--(11) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_\pi(q)
&=\sum_{s,a,r,m,s'}q(s,a,r,m,s')
  \frac{\pi_1(a\mid s)}{\mu_1(a\mid s)}r\\
&=\sum_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}
  \frac{c_{sa}\pi_1(a\mid s)}{\mu_1(a\mid s)}
  \sum_{s'\in\mathcal S}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
  rA_{s,a}[s',r](1+w_{sa,r})\\
&=\sum_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}
  p(S_1=s)\pi_1(a\mid s)\theta_{s,a},
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
\theta_{s,a}
:=\mathbf 1^\top A_{s,a}\operatorname{diag}((r)_{r\in\mathcal R})(w_{sa}+\mathbf 1)
\in\mathcal J_{s,a}
\tag{16}
\]
by \(\mathrm{def{:}static\text{-}chen\text{-}cell\text{-}program}\). Conversely, every independent selection \(\theta_{s,a}\in\mathcal J_{s,a}\) has a witnessing \(w_{sa}\in\mathcal W_{s,a}\), and (10)--(13) paste those witnesses into a single \(q^w\in\mathcal K(p)\). Combining this fact with \(\mathrm{def{:}identified\text{-}set}\) proves the exact, hence sharp, image
\[
\Theta_I(p)=
\left\{
\sum_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}
p(S_1=s)\pi_1(a\mid s)\theta_{s,a}:
\theta_{s,a}\in\mathcal J_{s,a}
\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]

For completeness, the extrema in (17) are genuine minima and maxima. Indeed, (8) implies
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}d_{sa,r}w_{sa,r}
=\mathbf 1^\top\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}.
\tag{18}
\]
Because every \(d_{sa,r}>0\), each feasible coordinate satisfies
\[
0\le w_{sa,r}\le
\frac{\mathbf 1^\top\boldsymbol\beta_{s,a}}{d_{sa,r}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus each nonempty \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\) is closed and bounded, hence compact; its convexity follows from its linear equality and nonnegativity constraints. Its scalar linear image \(\mathcal J_{s,a}\) is therefore a compact interval. Since every coefficient \(p(S_1=s)\pi_1(a\mid s)\) in (17) is nonnegative, independent selection of cellwise minimizers and maximizers gives
\[
L(p)=\sum_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}
p(S_1=s)\pi_1(a\mid s)\min\mathcal J_{s,a},
\qquad
U(p)=\sum_{(s,a)\in\mathcal X_{SA}(p)}
p(S_1=s)\pi_1(a\mid s)\max\mathcal J_{s,a}.
\tag{20}
\]
This agrees with the endpoint definitions in \(\mathrm{def{:}endpoints}\); alternatively, attainment also follows from \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}attainment}\).

Finally, if \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{s,a})=|\mathcal R|\) in every positive cell, feasibility of every \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\) follows from (9) and \(\mathcal K(p)\ne\varnothing\). Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}point\text{-}id\text{-}mean}\) then makes every \(\mathcal W_{s,a}\), and hence every \(\mathcal J_{s,a}\), a singleton and also gives \(|\mathcal S|\ge|\mathcal R|\). Equation (17) is consequently a singleton, so the policy value is point identified. Under the substitutions \(X=(S_1,A_1)\), \(F=S_2\), \(Y=R_1\), and the source's response indicator equal to \(M_1\), equations (8), (16), and (20) are exactly the matrix program and full-column-rank implication identified in the cited source location.
