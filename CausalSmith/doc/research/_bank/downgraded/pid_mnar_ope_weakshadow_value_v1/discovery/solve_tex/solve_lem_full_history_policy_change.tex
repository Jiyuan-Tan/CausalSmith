Fix \(q\in\mathcal K(p)\).  For \(t\in\mathcal T\) and \(h\in\mathcal H_t^F\), put
\[
q_t^F(h):=\sum_{a'\in\mathcal A}q_t^F(h,a').
\]
If \(q_t^F(h)>0\), division of the equality in
\(\mathrm{ass{:}behavior\text{-}factorization}\) by \(q_t^F(h)\) gives
\[
q(A_t=a\mid H_t^F=h)
=\frac{q_t^F(h,a)}{q_t^F(h)}
=\mu_t(a\mid O_t(h))
\qquad(a\in\mathcal A).
\tag{1}
\]
The same equality is one of the restrictions defining \(\mathcal K(p)\) in
\(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\text{-}fiber}\).  No division is made when
\(q_t^F(h)=0\).

Because the horizon and all alphabets are finite, the mass function of \(q\)
admits a chronological factorization.  Let \(\lambda(s_1)\) be its initial-state
mass function, and, at each positive-probability pair \((h,a)\), let
\[
e_t(r,m,s'\mid h,a)
:=q(R_t=r,M_t=m,S_{t+1}=s'\mid H_t^F=h,A_t=a).
\]
At a zero-probability pair choose any stochastic kernel \(e_t\).  Likewise, at
a zero-probability history choose the action kernel in a factorization of \(q\)
to be \(\mu_t(\cdot\mid O_t(h))\); this choice cannot change \(q\).  Equation
\((1)\) then yields, for every complete prefix
\(x_t=(s_1,a_1,r_1,m_1,\ldots,a_t,r_t,m_t,s_{t+1})\),
\[
q^{(t)}(x_t)
=\lambda(s_1)\prod_{u=1}^t
\mu_u(a_u\mid O_u(h_u^F))
e_u(r_u,m_u,s_{u+1}\mid h_u^F,a_u),
\tag{2}
\]
where \(q^{(t)}\) is the corresponding prefix marginal of \(q\).

By the definition of the policy-replaced law in the statement, the same
initial law and the same environment kernels are retained, whereas the action
kernels are replaced by \(\pi\).  Hence its prefix mass function is
\[
(q^\pi)^{(t)}(x_t)
=\lambda(s_1)\prod_{u=1}^t
\pi_u(a_u\mid h_u^F)
e_u(r_u,m_u,s_{u+1}\mid h_u^F,a_u).
\tag{3}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}behavior\text{-}positivity}\) states that
\[
\mu_u(a\mid g)\geq\epsilon>0
\tag{4}
\]
for every admissible \((u,g,a)\).  If the right-hand side of \((3)\) is
positive, then its initial factor, every selected \(\pi\)-factor, and every
selected \(e\)-factor are positive.  Replacing each selected \(\pi\)-factor by
the corresponding \(\mu\)-factor leaves a strictly positive product by
\((4)\); by \((2)\), \(q^{(t)}(x_t)>0\).  Thus
\((q^\pi)^{(t)}\ll q^{(t)}\) for every \(t\), which is the asserted absolute
continuity through every finite stage.  This argument also shows that choices
of \(e_u\) at \(q\)-null histories cannot affect \(q^\pi\).

On every prefix with positive \(q^{(t)}\)-mass, division of \((3)\) by \((2)\)
is legitimate by \((4)\) and gives
\[
\frac{(q^\pi)^{(t)}(x_t)}{q^{(t)}(x_t)}
=\prod_{u=1}^t
\frac{\pi_u(a_u\mid h_u^F)}
{\mu_u(a_u\mid O_u(h_u^F))}
=\rho_t^\pi(x_t).
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore, using finite summation over prefixes and then marginalizing the
complete trajectory law over coordinates after stage \(t\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{q^\pi}[R_t]
&=\sum_{x_t}(q^\pi)^{(t)}(x_t)r_t \\
&=\sum_{x_t}q^{(t)}(x_t)\rho_t^\pi(x_t)r_t \\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}q(\omega)\rho_t^\pi(\omega)R_t(\omega) \\
&=\ell_t(q).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The last equality is exactly \(\mathrm{def{:}stage\text{-}value}\).  Finally,
the horizon is finite, so linearity of finite sums and
\(\mathrm{def{:}identified\text{-}set}\), which uses
\(V_\pi(q)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\ell_t(q)\), imply
\[
\mathbb E_{q^\pi}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^H R_t\right]
=\sum_{t=1}^H\mathbb E_{q^\pi}[R_t]
=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\ell_t(q)
=V_\pi(q).
\tag{7}
\]
Equations \((5)\)--\((7)\) derive the equality between the causal value under
the policy-replaced law and the compatible-law functional; they do not define
that equality through an observed-law regression.
