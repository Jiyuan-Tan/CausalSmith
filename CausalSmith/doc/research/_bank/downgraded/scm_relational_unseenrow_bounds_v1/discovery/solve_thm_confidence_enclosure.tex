%%% FIELD def-finite-unsupported-parameter-space
Let \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}\) be the set of type--context keys that occur in an event-consistent target term recorded by \(Ictx\) but whose parent-context event has probability zero at every source occurrence.  If \(\mathcal Y_r\) is the finite outcome alphabet of \(r\), put
\[
 \Delta_r=\left\{(p_{r,y})_{y\in\mathcal Y_r}:p_{r,y}\geq0,\ \sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_r}p_{r,y}=1\right\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}=\prod_{r\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}}\Delta_r,
\]
where the empty product is a singleton.
%%% FIELD def-multivariate-target-polynomial
For \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\), \(\Phi(p)\) is obtained by replaying the finite truncated target factorization encoded by \(S,G,\rho,a,B,Ictx\), substituting the rational supported rows \(K\) and substituting \(p_{r,y}\) whenever an occurrence has key \(r\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}\).
%%% FIELD def-finite-unsupported-model-class
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is the class of finite RSCMs over \(S\) and \(G\) that induce the source groundings \(Rsrc\) and target grounding \(\rho\) encoded by \(Ictx\), have mutually independent ground-variable noises and type-shared finite mechanisms, reproduce \(Psrc\), use the recovered rows \(K\) at every target-relevant positive-source-support key, and leave precisely the target-relevant keys in \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}\) unrestricted over their native finite outcome alphabets.
%%% FIELD def-finite-unsupported-identified-set
For \(M\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\), let \(Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M)=P_M^{\rho}(B\mid\operatorname{do}(a))\), and define \(\Theta_{\mathrm{fin}}=\{Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M):M\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\}\).
%%% FIELD def-confidence-enclosure-corrected
Choose rational \(etahat\in Cn\) when \(Cn\neq\varnothing\), compute \(epsilon\), set
\[
 \delta=\delta_{\times}=1-\prod_r(1-\epsilon_r)^{m_r},
\]
where a zero-exponent factor is one, compute \([Lhat,Uhat]=H(etahat,[0,1])\), and return \(Kn=[\max\{0,Lhat-\delta\},\min\{1,Uhat+\delta\}]\); return \([0,1]\) when \(Cn=\varnothing\).

The set \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}\) is finite, \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\) is a nonempty finite product of finite-dimensional probability simplices, and \(\Phi\) is a multivariate polynomial with rational coefficients in the coordinates \(p_{r,y}\).  For every \(M\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\), its type-shared unsupported conditional rows form a vector \(p_M\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\) and
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M)=\Phi(p_M).
\]
%%% FIELD lem-multivariate-polynomial-reduction-proof
By \(ass:finite-domains\), every outcome alphabet is finite.  The target grounding \(\rho\) and its explicit incidence table \(Ictx\) are finite, so only finitely many type--context keys occur in event-consistent target terms.  Hence the subset \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}\) is finite.  Every simplex \(\Delta_r\) contains, for example, each point mass on \(\mathcal Y_r\), so their finite product \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\) is nonempty; if \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}=\varnothing\), this is the stipulated singleton product.

Fix an event-consistent complete assignment \(x\) of the nonintervened target nodes.  Acyclicity from \(ass:acyclic-groundings\) gives a topological ordering, and independent ground noises from \(ass:markovian-noises\) give the truncated Markov factorization
\[
 P_M^\rho(x\mid\operatorname{do}(a))
 =\prod_{v\notin\operatorname{dom}(a)}
 P_M\!\left(x_v\mid x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)}\right).
\]
By \(ass:type-sharing\), the factor at \(v\) depends on its type--context key rather than on the occurrence label.  If that key has a source occurrence with parent context \(c\) of positive probability, its row is recovered coordinatewise as
\[
 K_r(y)=\frac{Psrc(Y=y,C=c)}{Psrc(C=c)}.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive by the definition of positive source support, both numerator and denominator are rational because the tables \(Psrc\) are rational, and \(ass:source-compatibility\) makes this ratio independent of the chosen positive-support occurrence.  Thus the supported factor is uniquely recovered and rational.  Otherwise the key belongs to \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{free}}\) by \(def:finite-unsupported-parameter-space\), and the factor is one coordinate \(p_{r,x_v}\).  Hence each assignment probability is a monomial in the coordinates of \(p\), multiplied by a rational coefficient.  There are finitely many assignments, and summing the monomials for \(B\) is exactly the replay in \(def:multivariate-target-polynomial\).  Therefore \(\Phi\in\mathbb Q[(p_{r,y})]\).

Now take \(M\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\).  Each unrestricted finite categorical conditional row is a probability vector, and type sharing assigns one such vector to every key, so their collection \(p_M\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\).  Applying the preceding factorization with these actual rows makes its assignment-by-assignment sum equal both to \(Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M)\) and to \(\Phi(p_M)\).  This proves the displayed equality.
%%% FIELD thm-finite-unsupported-endpoint-models-statement
For every \(p_0\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\), there is a finite-noise model \(M_{p_0}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\) that reproduces every source law in \(Psrc\), uses independent copies of one common noise law and one common mechanism for each endogenous type, has unsupported rows \(p_0\), and satisfies \(Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M_{p_0})=\Phi(p_0)\).  If \(\mathcal C_t(p_0)\) is the set of distinct positive-probability source rows and free target rows of a type \(t\) with alphabet size \(k_t\), the construction uses at most
\[
 1+|\mathcal C_t(p_0)|(k_t-1)
\]
positive noise atoms for that type.
%%% FIELD thm-finite-unsupported-endpoint-models-proof
Fix \(p_0\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\).  For every endogenous type \(t\), order its finite alphabet as \(\{1,\ldots,k_t\}\).  Let \(\mathcal C_t(p_0)\) contain every distinct conditional row of type \(t\) recovered from a positive-probability source parent context and every row of \(p_0\) having type \(t\).  Source compatibility ensures that a row recovered at two source occurrences is the same row, so this collection is well defined.  For \(c\in\mathcal C_t(p_0)\), write
\[
 b_{c,j}=\sum_{h=1}^j p_{c,h},\qquad j=0,\ldots,k_t,
\]
with \(b_{c,0}=0\) and \(b_{c,k_t}=1\).  Sort the distinct numbers in
\[
 \{0,1\}\cup\{b_{c,j}:c\in\mathcal C_t(p_0),\ 1\leq j<k_t\}
\]
as \(0=d_{t,0}<d_{t,1}<\cdots<d_{t,h_t}=1\).  Give the type-\(t\) noise the finite atoms \(j=1,\ldots,h_t\) with masses \(d_{t,j}-d_{t,j-1}>0\).

For each atom choose any \(u_{t,j}\in(d_{t,j-1},d_{t,j})\).  Define the common type mechanism at context \(c\) to output the unique category \(y\) such that
\[
 b_{c,y-1}<u_{t,j}<b_{c,y},
\]
with empty zero-probability categories skipped.  No cumulative threshold lies in the open atom interval, so the selected category is independent of the chosen interior point.  The intervals whose output is at most \(y\) have total mass \(b_{c,y}\); subtraction at consecutive thresholds therefore shows that the probability of output \(y\) is
\[
 b_{c,y}-b_{c,y-1}=p_{c,y}.
\]
For a context absent from \(\mathcal C_t(p_0)\), choose an arbitrary fixed category.  This choice can affect neither a positive-probability source cell nor a target term using a supported or free target-relevant key.

Give distinct ground nodes mutually independent copies of the noise belonging to their type and use the single context-indexed mechanism just defined for that type.  Thus ground noises are independent and mechanisms are type shared.  On a source grounding, topological induction using \(ass:acyclic-groundings\) shows that every positive-probability parent context has exactly its recovered conditional row.  The source joint law is consequently the product of these rows.  By \(ass:source-compatibility\), this product is the given rational table in \(Psrc\); rows at zero-probability parent contexts do not change it.  Hence the constructed model belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\), and its free rows are exactly \(p_0\).

The multivariate polynomial reduction now gives
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M_{p_0})=\Phi(p_0).
\]
There are at most \(|\mathcal C_t(p_0)|(k_t-1)\) distinct interior cumulative thresholds.  Together with \(0\) and \(1\), they create at most \(1+|\mathcal C_t(p_0)|(k_t-1)\) positive adjacent-interval atoms, proving the stated atom bound.  Coincident thresholds only reduce this number.
%%% FIELD thm-sharp-finite-unsupported-rows-statement
The compatible causal set for any finite family of unsupported finite-categorical rows is
\[
 \Theta_{\mathrm{fin}}=\Phi(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}})
 =\left[\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}}\Phi(p),
         \max_{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}}\Phi(p)\right].
\]
Both endpoints are attained by finite-noise, type-shared models in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\).  This is a structural sharpness statement; it does not assert an efficient exact algorithm for optimizing a general multivariate \(\Phi\).
%%% FIELD thm-sharp-finite-unsupported-rows-proof
The multivariate reduction gives
\[
 \Theta_{\mathrm{fin}}\subseteq\Phi(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}).
\]
Conversely, for every \(p_0\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\), the finite-noise construction supplies \(M_{p_0}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\) with \(Q_{\mathrm{fin}}(M_{p_0})=\Phi(p_0)\).  Therefore
\[
 \Theta_{\mathrm{fin}}=\Phi(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}).
\]

It remains to characterize this scalar image.  Write all free row coordinates in a fixed finite vector in \([0,1]^d\).  Given any sequence in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\), repeated interval bisection produces a subsequence converging in its first coordinate; iterating over the finitely many coordinates and taking the final diagonal subsequence gives a coordinatewise convergent subsequence.  Every coordinate limit is nonnegative, and taking limits in each finite row sum preserves \(\sum_y p_{r,y}=1\).  Hence the limit remains in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\).  When \(d=0\), the product is already a singleton.  Thus every sequence in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\) has a convergent subsequence whose limit stays in the set.

Let \(u\) be the supremum of \(\Phi(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}})\) and choose \(p_j\) with \(\Phi(p_j)\to u\).  A convergent subsequence has limit \(p^+\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\).  Since a polynomial is continuous, \(\Phi(p^+)=u\).  The same argument for the infimum gives \(p^-\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{free}}\) attaining the minimum \(l\).

The product of simplices is convex: for \(t\in[0,1]\), every row of \((1-t)p^-+tp^+\) remains nonnegative and sums to one.  Therefore
\[
 g(t)=\Phi((1-t)p^-+tp^+)
\]
is a continuous univariate polynomial with \(g(0)=l\) and \(g(1)=u\).  For any \(z\in[l,u]\), bisect \([0,1]\) repeatedly, each time retaining a half whose endpoint values bracket \(z\).  The nested intervals have a common limit point \(t_z\), and continuity gives \(g(t_z)=z\).  Hence the image contains all of \([l,u]\), while the definitions of \(l\) and \(u\) give the reverse inclusion.  Finally, applying the finite-noise construction to \(p^-\) and \(p^+\) gives endpoint-attaining models in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}\).
%%% FIELD lem-multiplicative-occurrence-radius-statement
Consider any finite sequential coupling in which, conditional on no earlier disagreement, the current supported row label \(r\) may depend on the common observed history, its conditional disagreement probability is at most \(\epsilon_r\), and every no-disagreement history uses label \(r\) at most \(m_r\) times.  With a zero-exponent factor defined as one,
\[
 \Pr(\text{some disagreement})\leq
 \delta=1-\prod_r(1-\epsilon_r)^{m_r}
 \leq\min\left\{1,\sum_r m_r\epsilon_r\right\}.
\]
%%% FIELD lem-multiplicative-occurrence-radius-proof
Put \(b_r=1-\epsilon_r\in[0,1]\), with \(b_r^0=1\) also when \(b_r=0\).  View the finite sequential construction as a decision tree whose nodes are common histories reached without a disagreement.  At a history where the current label is \(r\), the total conditional probability of matched children is at least \(b_r\).  Along every root-to-leaf common history, label \(r\) occurs at most \(m_r\) times.

We prove a survival lower bound by backward induction.  At a history that has already used label \(r\) exactly \(k_r\) times, the conditional probability of reaching a leaf without disagreement is at least
\[
 \prod_r b_r^{m_r-k_r}.
\]
At a leaf this holds because the left side is one and every factor on the right lies in \([0,1]\).  At an internal history with current label \(s\), each matched child has count vector \(k+e_s\), so the induction hypothesis bounds its future survival by
\[
 \prod_r b_r^{m_r-k_r-\mathbf 1\{r=s\}}.
\]
Summing over matched children and using that their total conditional mass is at least \(b_s\) gives the claimed bound at the parent.  At the root, therefore,
\[
 \Pr(\text{no disagreement})\geq\prod_r(1-\epsilon_r)^{m_r},
\]
which proves the first inequality after taking complements.  This induction permits the label at each step to be an arbitrary function of the common history.

For the comparison with the additive radius, list \(m_r\) copies of each number \(\epsilon_r\) as \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\).  The identity
\[
 1-\prod_{j=1}^N(1-x_j)
 =1-\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-x_j)+x_N\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-x_j)
\]
and induction give \(1-\prod_j(1-x_j)\leq\sum_jx_j=\sum_rm_r\epsilon_r\).  The same left side lies in \([0,1]\), proving the clipped bound.

Under \(ass:one-unsupported-row\) and \(ass:binary-free-row\), the generalized objects specialize as

and its endpoint-model construction realizes every \(q\in[0,1]\) by a common finite-noise, type-shared RSCM reproducing \(Psrc\).  Thus this equality is compatibility sharp, not merely an outer bound.

We next identify the algebraic endpoints.  A polynomial is continuous, and the compact-attainment argument in \(thm:sharp-finite-unsupported-rows\) supplies minimizers and maximizers on \([0,1]\).  If a nonconstant \(F\) has an optimizer \(c\in(0,1)\), then for sufficiently small positive and negative \(h\), the difference quotients
\[
 \frac{F(c+h)-F(c)}{h}
\]
have opposite weak signs.  Their common limit is \(F'(c)\), so \(F'(c)=0\).  Hence every optimizer is either a boundary point or a feasible derivative root and belongs to \(Ccrit\).  If \(F\) is constant, every candidate has the same value.  In both cases, the definitions of \(L\) and \(U\) give
\[
 \min_{q\in[0,1]}F(q)=L,
 \qquad
 \max_{q\in[0,1]}F(q)=U.
\]
The continuous scalar-image argument in the generalized theorem then gives \(F([0,1])=[L,U]\), proving the first claim.

The cited scope result records that the comparator's \(\rho\)-Markovian class allows shared exogenous parents within an instance.  A model in \(Mone\) has the special case of no such shared exogenous parent because \(ass:markovian-noises\) makes all ground-variable noises mutually independent.  Its observed domains are finite by \(ass:finite-domains\), and on the fixed finite graph its relational-parent multiset size has a finite uniform maximum.  Consequently \(Mone\) is a subclass of the comparator class, and it can be a strict subclass when the graph permits the additional confounding.

If \(F\) is nonconstant, choose \(q_1,q_2\in[0,1]\) with \(F(q_1)\neq F(q_2)\).  Sharpness provides models \(M_{q_1},M_{q_2}\in Mone\) with these two causal values.  They also lie in the larger class, so that class cannot point identify the query.  A \(\mathsf{FAIL}\) report is therefore accompanied here by an exact range only for the fully Markovian subclass; no step has bounded the extra confounded models.  If \(F\) is constant, all models in \(Mone\) have the single value \(L=U\).  The same conclusion holds for the comparator class when the supplied graph rules out its extra shared-exogenous structures and the classes consequently coincide.  Without that equality, constancy on a subclass cannot determine values on its strict superclass.

Finally, unsupportedness alone cannot force failure because the target functional may cancel the free row.  The role of type sharing is load bearing: with two independent target nodes using the same unsupported binary key and event \(B\) equal to ``exactly one success,'' the shared-row polynomial is \(2q(1-q)\leq1/2\); if type sharing is removed, choosing the two occurrence probabilities as one and zero makes the event probability one, outside the asserted one-parameter range.  Conversely, a supported outcome row that is the same for both values of an unsupported upstream node makes \(F\) constant, so a complete procedure may identify the query despite the unsupported key.  These finite examples verify both the specialization and its stated qualification.
%%% FIELD confidence-enclosure-replacement-statement
For every supported-row vector \(eta\in Cn\) and every \(q\in[0,1]\), \(|H(eta,q)-H(etahat,q)|\le\delta\). Consequently, \(\Pr(\{H(eta0,q):q\in[0,1]\}\subseteq Kn)\ge 1-\alpha\). The reported \(Kn\) is a conservative confidence enclosure and is not asserted to equal the exact projection \(\bigcup_{eta\in Cn}H(eta,[0,1])\).
%%% FIELD confidence-enclosure-proof
If \(Cn=\varnothing\), the pointwise assertion is vacuous and the definition returns \(Kn=[0,1]\).  Now fix an outcome for which \(Cn\neq\varnothing\), so the deterministic feasible center \(etahat\in Cn\) exists.  For every \(eta\in Cn\), supported row \(r\), and category \(y\), both \(eta_{r,y}\) and \(\widehat\eta_{r,y}\) lie in \([\ell_{r,y},u_{r,y}]\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\eta_r,\widehat\eta_r)
 =\frac12\sum_y|\eta_{r,y}-\widehat\eta_{r,y}|
 \leq\frac12\sum_y
 \max\!\left\{|\ell_{r,y}-\widehat\eta_{r,y}|,
                  |u_{r,y}-\widehat\eta_{r,y}|\right\}
 \leq\epsilon_r.
\]
The last inequality also uses that total variation between two categorical laws is at most one, exactly as in the definition of \(\epsilon_r\).

Fix \(q\in[0,1]\) and couple the target systems with supported rows \(eta\) and \(etahat\) in a topological order of the intervened acyclic ground graph.  Intervened nodes are set equal.  Conditional on no earlier disagreement, the two systems have the same realized parents at the current node and therefore the same type--context label, although that label may depend on the common realized history.  At the unsupported key \(rstar\), use the same draw from \((1-q,q)\), which has disagreement probability zero.

At a supported row \(r\), put \(c_y=\min\{\eta_{r,y},\widehat\eta_{r,y}\}\) and \(s=\sum_yc_y\).  Assign mass \(c_y\) to the matched pair \((y,y)\).  If \(s<1\), assign residual mass
\[
 \frac{(\eta_{r,y}-c_y)(\widehat\eta_{r,z}-c_z)}{1-s}
\]
to \((y,z)\); if \(s=1\), there is no residual part.  Direct summation gives marginals \(\eta_r\) and \(\widehat\eta_r\).  For each \(y\), at least one of \(\eta_{r,y}-c_y\) and \(\widehat\eta_{r,y}-c_y\) is zero, so the residual law has no diagonal mass.  Its disagreement probability is therefore
\[
 1-s=\operatorname{TV}(\eta_r,\widehat\eta_r)\leq\epsilon_r.
\]
Fresh coupling randomness at each ground node preserves the required conditional marginal in each system, as required by \(ass:markovian-noises\).

By definition, \(m_r\) is a pathwise upper bound on the number of target occurrences carrying label \(r\).  The hypotheses of \(lem:multiplicative-occurrence-radius\) therefore hold even though the labels are history dependent.  It follows that
\[
 \Pr(\text{some target-node disagreement})
 \leq\delta
 =1-\prod_r(1-\epsilon_r)^{m_r}
 \leq\min\left\{1,\sum_rm_r\epsilon_r\right\}.
\]
The indicators of \(B\) in the two systems can differ only if some target node differs.  Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |H(eta,q)-H(etahat,q)|
 &=\left|\mathbb E\{\mathbf 1_B(X)-\mathbf 1_B(\widehat X)\}\right|\\
 &\leq\mathbb E|\mathbf 1_B(X)-\mathbf 1_B(\widehat X)|\\
 &\leq\Pr(X\neq\widehat X)\leq\delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the uniform pointwise assertion.

Let \(E_n=\{eta0\in Cn\}\).  The sampling-context positivity assumption makes every population conditional row in \(eta0\) well defined, and joint box coverage gives \(\Pr(E_n)\geq1-\alpha\).  On \(E_n\), the polytope is nonempty and the uniform inequality applies with \(eta=eta0\).  Since \(Lhat\) and \(Uhat\) are respectively the minimum and maximum of the center polynomial over \([0,1]\), for every \(q\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Lhat-\delta\leq H(eta0,q)\leq Uhat+\delta.
\]
The functional \(H\) is a probability, so intersecting this interval with \([0,1]\) preserves inclusion.  By the corrected definition of \(Kn\),
\[
 \{H(eta0,q):q\in[0,1]\}\subseteq Kn
\]
on \(E_n\).  Its probability is at least \(1-\alpha\).  The construction optimizes only the center polynomial in the unsupported coordinate and inflates it uniformly; it does not optimize jointly over \(eta\in Cn\), so no exact multivariate-projection claim is made.
