%%% FIELD proof-full-branch-geometry
Fix a primitive vector on one of the strata retained by \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\mbox{-}system}\), and fix \(j=(C,s)\in J\).  Put
\[
p_j(\rho,\vartheta)=\Pi_j(\rho,\vartheta,\ell_s),
\qquad (\rho,\vartheta)\in D_j:=I\times[0,\pi/2].
\]
This is throughout the power conditional on the complete signed event \(E_C\), not power conditional only on the active inequality in isolation; the one-dimensional integral is valid for the complete event by the factorization already built into the definition of \(\Pi_j\).

We first establish the regularity needed for the branch argument.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}derived\mbox{-}residual\mbox{-}scale}\),
\[
v_j(\rho,\vartheta)^2
=1-\kappa_j^2(1-\rho)\cos^2\vartheta
\geq \rho_0>0.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(r_j(\rho,\vartheta)\) ranges in a compact subset of \((-1,1)\).  For fixed \(s\), define the null selected-tail map
\[
H_s(q,r)
=\frac{1}{Q(c_s)}\int_{c_s}^{\infty}
  \phi(z)Q\!\left(\frac{q-rz}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\right)\,dz .           \tag{2}
\]
It has limits one and zero as \(q\) tends respectively to \(-\infty\) and \(+\infty\), and differentiation under the integral gives
\[
\frac{\partial H_s}{\partial q}(q,r)
=-\frac{1}{Q(c_s)\sqrt{1-r^2}}
  \int_{c_s}^{\infty}\phi(z)
  \phi\!\left(\frac{q-rz}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\right)\,dz<0.             \tag{3}
\]
The differentiation is justified locally uniformly by (1): every derivative of the integrand is a polynomial times a Gaussian density, and hence has an integrable Gaussian envelope when \(r\) remains in a compact subset of \((-1,1)\).  The same domination, applied to the locally convergent power series of the integrand, shows that \(H_s\) is real analytic in \((q,r)\).  Equations (2)--(3), \(0<\alpha<1\), and the analytic implicit-function theorem therefore show that the unique solution of \(H_s(q,r)=\alpha\) is analytic in \(r\).  Consequently
\[
q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\vartheta)
=q_{\alpha}\!\left(r_j(\rho,\vartheta),c_s\right)                \tag{4}
\]
is real analytic on a neighborhood of \(D_j\).

Set \(x_j(\rho)=c_s-a_j(\rho)\ell_s\).  From the displayed definition of \(\Pi_j\),
\[
p_j(\rho,\vartheta)
=\frac{1}{Q(x_j(\rho))}
  \int_{x_j(\rho)}^{\infty}\phi(z)
  Q\!\left(
    \frac{q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\vartheta)
          -d_j(\vartheta)\ell_s-r_j(\rho,\vartheta)z}
         {v_j(\rho,\vartheta)}
  \right)\,dz .                                                    \tag{5}
\]
The functions \(a_j,r_j,d_j,v_j\) are analytic on a neighborhood of their compact domains, (1) keeps every division in (5) valid, and \(Q(x_j(\rho))>0\).  After the change of variable \(z=x_j(\rho)+y\), the same Gaussian domination used above permits termwise differentiation on \(y\in[0,\infty)\).  Thus (4)--(5) prove that \(p_j\) is real analytic on a neighborhood of \(D_j\).

Let
\[
Z_j=\{(\rho,\vartheta)\in D_j:
             \partial_{\vartheta}p_j(\rho,\vartheta)=0\}.         \tag{6}
\]
The analytic function \(\partial_{\vartheta}p_j\) cannot vanish on a two-dimensional open set.  Indeed, analytic continuation from such a set would make it vanish throughout the connected neighborhood of \(D_j\), so \(p_j(\rho,\cdot)\) would be constant.  That contradicts \(\mathrm{thm{:}weight\mbox{-}side}\): if \(\Delta_j>0\), that theorem excludes \(0\) from the maximizing set; if \(\Delta_j<0\), it excludes both angular endpoints; and if \(\Delta_j=0\), it gives the unique maximizer \(\theta_{0j}\).  Hence the semianalytic set (6) has dimension at most one.

Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}semianalytic\mbox{-}finite\mbox{-}stratification}\) to (6), compatibly with projection onto the \(\rho\)-coordinate and with the boundary of \(D_j\).  There are finitely many point strata and analytic arc strata.  A rank-one projected arc is, after finitely many cuts, an analytic graph
\[
\vartheta=\vartheta_{j\ell}(\rho)                                  \tag{7}
\]
on an open interval.  Its endpoints have the Weierstrass, equivalently finite Puiseux, continuations recorded by the reduced-factor and multiple-root clauses of \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\mbox{-}system}\).  A rank-zero projected arc is vertical.  On any nontrivial angular portion of such an arc, (6) makes \(p_j\) constant in \(\vartheta\); its single projected \(\rho\)-value is therefore retained as a projected branch endpoint, while the full angular set is recovered below by a superlevel set.  The two angular endpoint graphs \(\vartheta=0\) and \(\vartheta=\pi/2\) are included as well.

For every fixed \(\rho\), continuity on \([0,\pi/2]\) gives an angular maximizer.  Fermat's rule says that every interior maximizer belongs to (6), while every other maximizer is on an angular endpoint.  It follows from (7) that, on each interval of the finite projected stratification,
\[
M_j(\rho)=\max_{\ell\in L_j}g_{j\ell}(\rho),
\qquad
g_{j\ell}(\rho)
=p_j\!\left(\rho,\vartheta_{j\ell}(\rho)\right),                 \tag{8}
\]
where \(L_j\) is finite and the endpoint branches are included.  Each \(g_{j\ell}\) is analytic on its open interval and has the Puiseux continuation just described at a ramified endpoint.  Persistent repeated factors are reduced, not deleted, so (8) also covers multiple angular roots.

There are only finitely many active cells.  Refine the projected stratification by all nonidentical crossings of the branches in (8), by nontransverse crossings, by candidate-value ties, and by all projected endpoints.  The finiteness assertion in \(\mathrm{lem{:}semianalytic\mbox{-}finite\mbox{-}stratification}\), applied also to the relevant equality sets and their relatively compact projections, makes this a finite refinement.  If two branches agree on an interval, their identical-branch predicate is retained instead of trying to order them.  On every remaining open interval the order of the branches for each \(j\), and then the order across cells, is fixed.  Therefore
\[
F(\rho)=\min_{j\in J}M_j(\rho)                                    \tag{9}
\]
equals one of the analytic or Puiseux-continuable branches there, with all ties retained.  This proves the asserted finite maximum--minimum branch representation.

Consider now a global maximizer of \(F\).  If it is in the interior of one of the refined open intervals and the active branch in (9) is nonconstant, its derivative with respect to \(\rho\) is zero.  If that branch is constant on a nonempty interval, the flat-branch identity records the whole interval whenever its value is \(V^{\star}\).  Every remaining maximizer lies at an endpoint of \(I\), a projected branch endpoint, or a crossing or tie introduced in the refinement.  Conversely, any retained point of one of these types at which \(F=V^{\star}\), and every retained flat interval on which \(F=V^{\star}\), is a global maximizing holdout by the definition of \(V^{\star}\).  Thus, with the qualification ``attaining \(V^{\star}\)'', the endpoint, stationary-root, intersection, and flat-interval list in the statement is both exhaustive and exact; multiple roots and equality strata are preserved symbolically.

It remains to identify the complete weight correspondence.  For fixed \(\rho\), compactness supplies a maximizer of each \(p_j(\rho,\cdot)\).  For every weight vector,
\[
\min_{j\in J}p_j(\rho,\theta_j)
\leq \min_{j\in J}M_j(\rho)=F(\rho),                              \tag{10}
\]
whereas choosing one cellwise maximizer for every \(j\) makes equality hold.  Hence
\[
\max_{\theta\in[0,\pi/2]^J}
   \min_{j\in J}p_j(\rho,\theta_j)=F(\rho).                       \tag{11}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}least\mbox{-}coordinate\mbox{-}reduction}\), (11) is also the original maximin problem over \(h\in H\).  A design is therefore in \(\mathcal S^{\star}\) if and only if \(\rho\) belongs to the maximizing holdout set above and
\[
p_j(\rho,\theta_j)\geq V^{\star}
\quad\hbox{for every }j\in J.                                     \tag{12}
\]
Because the angles are cell-specific, (12) is exactly
\[
\prod_{j=(C,s)\in J}
 \{\theta_j\in[0,\pi/2]:
      \Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,\ell_s)\geq V^{\star}\}.               \tag{13}
\]
This includes every continuum belonging to a slack cell.

No step of the stratification argument used a sign restriction on \(\Delta_j\).  On a positive, negative, zero, or mixed-sign stratum, \(\mathrm{thm{:}weight\mbox{-}side}\) restricts the angular graphs to the appropriate side of \(\theta_{0j}\), including its exact zero-discrepancy equality statement.  When all discrepancies are nonnegative, \(\mathrm{thm{:}global\mbox{-}endpoint}\) reduces the retained maximizing holdout set to the unique point \(1-\rho_0\); \(\mathrm{thm{:}negative\mbox{-}interior}\) shows that a retained interior branch is genuinely necessary in a negative-discrepancy regime.  Equations (8)--(13) remain necessary and sufficient in every mixed-sign case, completing the proof.
%%% FIELD proof-tolerance-certificate
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}full\mbox{-}branch\mbox{-}geometry}\), it is enough to certify on the compact design domain
\[
D=I\times[0,\pi/2]^J
\]
the continuous function
\[
\Psi(\rho,\theta)
=\min_{j=(C,s)\in J}\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,\ell_s),
\qquad
V^{\star}=\max_{x\in D}\Psi(x),                                  \tag{14}
\]
and its maximizing set.  The finite union of closed rational intervals presenting the projection of the alternative set onto coordinate \(s\) gives \(\ell_s\) as its least left endpoint.  Thus every input in (14) is rational, algebraic, or a value of an explicitly displayed elementary Gaussian operation at such inputs.

We first prove that these operations have uniform, terminating enclosures.  On a bounded rational interval, the power series for the exponential, together with its Lagrange remainder, gives outward rational enclosures of \(\phi\) and of every derivative needed below.  For \(M>0\), integration by parts gives the explicit tail bound
\[
Q(M)=\int_M^{\infty}\phi(z)\,dz
\leq \frac{1}{M}\int_M^{\infty}z\phi(z)\,dz
=\frac{\phi(M)}{M}.                                                \tag{15}
\]
Symmetry treats the negative tail.  Consequently, truncation at a computably increasing rational \(M\), bounded-interval quadrature with Taylor remainders, and (15) produce outward rational intervals of arbitrarily prescribed width for every Gaussian integral and derivative appearing in \(q_{\alpha,j}\) and \(\Pi_j\).

The critical values can be bracketed uniformly.  Let
\[
\underline q_c<\min_{s\in S}Q(c_s)
\]
be any certified positive rational lower bound.  Such a bound is obtained by the preceding enclosure and finiteness of \(S\).  Choose a rational \(M_q>0\) so large that
\[
\frac{\phi(M_q)}{M_q}
<\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\,\underline q_c.                        \tag{16}
\]
For \(W=rZ+\sqrt{1-r^2}E\), with independent standard normal \(Z,E\), the identity \(r^2+(1-r^2)=1\) gives \(W\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).  Therefore
\[
\Pr(W>M_q\mid Z>c_s)
\leq \frac{Q(M_q)}{Q(c_s)}<\alpha                                  \tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\Pr(W>-M_q\mid Z>c_s)
\geq 1-\frac{Q(M_q)}{Q(c_s)}>\alpha.                               \tag{18}
\]
Equations (17)--(18) put every \(q_{\alpha,j}\) in \([-M_q,M_q]\).

By \(\mathrm{lem{:}derived\mbox{-}residual\mbox{-}scale}\),
\[
v_j(\rho,\theta_j)\geq\sqrt{\rho_0}>0.                            \tag{19}
\]
On the compact set consisting of \(q\in[-M_q,M_q]\), the allowed correlations, and \(z\in[c_s,c_s+1]\), the integrand in the absolute value of
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial q}
\left\{
 \frac{1}{Q(c_s)}\int_{c_s}^{\infty}
 \phi(z)Q\!\left(\frac{q-rz}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\right)\,dz
\right\}
=-\frac{1}{Q(c_s)\sqrt{1-r^2}}
 \int_{c_s}^{\infty}\phi(z)
 \phi\!\left(\frac{q-rz}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\right)\,dz               \tag{20}
\]
has a certified strictly positive lower bound.  Restricting the integral in (20) to \([c_s,c_s+1]\) therefore yields an effectively computable number \(g_q>0\) such that the absolute derivative is at least \(g_q\), uniformly over all active cells and all designs.  Bisection on (17)--(18), using outward integral enclosures and (20) to resolve a bracket once it is sufficiently small, consequently terminates at every requested critical-value accuracy.

The alternative lower threshold \(c_s-a_j(\rho)\ell_s\) ranges over a computable compact interval.  Hence its denominator \(Q(c_s-a_j(\rho)\ell_s)\) has a certified positive lower bound.  Together with (19)--(20), differentiation of the displayed integral defining \(\Pi_j\) gives an effective finite bound \(L_j\) for all its first derivatives on \(I\times[0,\pi/2]\).  All differentiated integrals are enclosed by the procedure based on (15).  With
\[
L=\max_{j\in J}L_j,
\]
the elementary inequality
\[
\left|\min_j u_j-\min_j v_j\right|
\leq\max_j|u_j-v_j|                                                \tag{21}
\]
shows that \(\Psi\) has the effective modulus
\[
|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)|\leq L\lVert x-y\rVert.                          \tag{22}
\]

Fix a rational \(\varepsilon>0\), and choose a rational \(\delta\) with \(0<\delta\leq\varepsilon\).  Subdivide \(D\) into finitely many boxes of diameter small enough under (22), and evaluate the center of every box accurately enough by the terminating enclosures above, so that for every nonempty box \(B\) one obtains rational numbers \(L_B,U_B\) satisfying
\[
L_B\leq\Psi(x)\leq U_B\quad(x\in B),
\qquad U_B-L_B\leq\delta.                                        \tag{23}
\]
The subdivision is finite because the mesh prescribed by (22) is positive and \(D\) is compact.  Define
\[
L^{\star}=\max_B L_B,
\qquad U^{\star}=\max_B U_B.                                     \tag{24}
\]
Since every box is nonempty, \(L_B\leq V^{\star}\), whence \(L^{\star}\leq V^{\star}\).  A box containing a maximizer gives \(U^{\star}\geq V^{\star}\).  Moreover, (23) gives, box by box,
\[
U_B\leq L_B+\delta\leq L^{\star}+\delta,
\]
and hence
\[
L^{\star}\leq V^{\star}\leq U^{\star}
\leq L^{\star}+\delta.                                           \tag{25}
\]
Thus \([L^{\star},U^{\star}]\) is the required value enclosure of width at most \(\varepsilon\).

Retain for the outer union exactly the boxes satisfying \(U_B\geq L^{\star}\), and intersect them with \(D\).  Every optimizer lies in such a box, because its box has \(U_B\geq V^{\star}\geq L^{\star}\).  For every point \(x\) in a retained box, (23)--(25) imply
\[
\Psi(x)\geq L_B
\geq U_B-\delta
\geq L^{\star}-\delta
\geq V^{\star}-2\delta
\geq V^{\star}-2\varepsilon.                                    \tag{26}
\]
This is a finite outer union containing \(\mathcal S^{\star}\) and contained in the \(2\varepsilon\)-power superlevel set.

For the inner union, retain every box satisfying
\[
L_B\geq U^{\star}-\varepsilon.                                   \tag{27}
\]
It is finite, and it is nonempty because a box attaining \(L^{\star}\) satisfies (27) by (25) and \(\delta\leq\varepsilon\).  For every point in this union,
\[
\Psi(x)\geq L_B\geq U^{\star}-\varepsilon
\geq V^{\star}-\varepsilon,                                     \tag{28}
\]
so it is contained in the \(\varepsilon\)-power superlevel set.  All decisions in (23)--(28) compare endpoints of outward rational intervals; no equality test between arbitrary exact real values occurs.

Finally suppose the optional separation certificate in \(\mathrm{def{:}tolerance\mbox{-}handle}\) is supplied.  Interval root-uniqueness tests on its isolating boxes certify that each claimed stationary or crossing branch has exactly the stated root there.  Exclusion bounds on the complements remove every other root, strict interval comparisons remove every competing branch value, and the certified positive objective gap removes every design outside the winning boxes.  Applying the same tests to every coordinate of the product superlevel set in \(\mathrm{thm{:}full\mbox{-}branch\mbox{-}geometry}\) excludes any additional slack-cell weight.  Only when all these checks hold is the optimizer a singleton; repeated subdivision then gives arbitrarily tight boxes around that unique design.  Without this certificate, (25)--(28), and only those value and optimizer-set enclosures, are returned.  This proves both termination and the stated restriction on uniqueness claims.
