%%% FIELD proof-explicit-support
Fix \(\rho\in I\), the realized dataset, and a cell \(C=C_n(\rho)\) for which the displayed reference-cell probability is positive.  The Fisher completion assigns to every observed unit one known response under both candidate treatment labels.  Consequently, for every \((H'_{n,s},z'_{n1s})\in\Omega_{n,1s}(\rho)\), every summand in the candidate complement means and variances is known.  The exceptional cases are also imputable because the definition assigns the fixed value \(c_s-1\).  Thus \(X'_{n,s}\) and \(C'_n(\rho)\) are known for every candidate action.  The same completion determines every full-block midrank and hence \(R'_{1j}\); after a retained stratum receives a quota-respecting second-stage assignment it also determines \(R'_{2j}\) and \(T'_j\).  If \(C_s=0\), chronology supplies no second-stage coordinate.

By definition, \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\) imposes precisely the following conditions, each for every member of the fixed finite set \(S\): the holdout has size \(\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor\); the first-stage assignment has quota \(m_{n1s}\); the recomputed complete signed label is \(C\); and, exactly when \(C_s=1\), the second-stage assignment has quota \(m_{n2s}\).  The preceding paragraph proves imputability on every history satisfying these conditions.

Conversely, a quota-respecting chronological history belongs to \(\prod_{s\in S}\Omega_{n,1s}(\rho)\) at stage one.  If its recomputed label is \(C\), it lies in the same-cell fiber; chronology then supplies a second-stage vector exactly for the coordinates with \(C_s=1\).  These are all the defining conditions of \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\).  Since \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}>0\), normalization on this finite support is defined.  There is no additional event in either direction.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-level
At \(h=0\), the potential-outcome definition and no interference give
\[
Y^{(0)}_{nksi}(1)=Y^{(0)}_{nksi}(0)=B_{nksi}
\qquad(k\in K, s\in S, i\le N_{nks}).
\]
Thus the Fisher completion is the fixed baseline array on every observed coordinate and is independent of the realized action.  By the random-holdout and stage-one complete-randomization assumptions, a candidate first-stage history has mass
\[
\prod_{s\in S}
\left\{
\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}
\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}
\right\}^{-1}.
\]
The fixed thresholds make its cell the deterministic recomputation \(C'_n(\rho)\).  Conditional on \(C'_n(\rho)=C\), the second-stage design gives the independent product of uniform quota laws exactly over \(s\) with \(C_s=1\).  The explicit-support lemma therefore shows that, for every supported history \(\omega\), Bayes' formula is
\[
\mathsf P_n^{0,\rho}\{\omega\mid E_{n,C}(\rho)\}
=\frac{1}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{0,\rho},\rho)}
\prod_{s\in S}
\left\{
\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}
\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}
\right\}^{-1}
\prod_{s:C_s=1}\binom{N_{n2s}}{m_{n2s}}^{-1}.
\]
This is exactly \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\{\omega\}\).

It remains only the finite-space randomization inequality.  If a statistic has distinct values \(t_1>\cdots>t_L\) with masses \(w_l\), its nonrandomized upper-tail value is \(p(t_l)=\sum_{a\le l}w_a\).  Hence, for \(0\le u\le1\),
\[
\Pr\{p(T)\le u\}
=\sum_{l:\,\sum_{a\le l}w_a\le u}w_l\le u.
\]
Ties can therefore only make the test conservative.  Applying this identity to the conditional law just identified proves superuniformity and conditional rejection probability at most \(\alpha\).

%%% FIELD proof-full-cell-factorization
For each \(t\in S\), let \(\mathcal H_{nt}\) be its holdout and stage-one history.  The holdout and stage-one design assumptions make \((\mathcal H_{nt})_{t\in S}\) mutually independent.  The variable \(X_{n,t}\) and its signed selection event are stratum-local measurable functions.  Conditional on the combined first-stage history, the second-stage assumption supplies independent stratum-local kernels.  Equivalently one may pre-generate those kernels and reveal only the recruited coordinates.  Thus, for \(j=(C,s)\in J\) and measurable \(B_0\), finite-product independence yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\in B_0,E_{n,C}(\rho)\}\\
&\quad=\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\in B_0,X_{n,s}>c_s\}
\prod_{t\in S\setminus\{s\}}p_{nt}^{C_t}(1-p_{nt})^{1-C_t}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking \(B_0\) to be the whole space gives the corresponding cell probability, and division cancels every factor with \(t\ne s\) whenever that probability is positive.

Under \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,\mathrm{base}}\), both the candidate mass and the completed selector map factor over the fixed finite set \(S\).  Hence its signed cell probability is \(\prod_{t\in S}(p'_{nt})^{C_t}(1-p'_{nt})^{1-C_t}\).  Restriction to \(C'_n=C\) divides by this product, and the subsequent second-stage law is an independent product over retained strata.  The assumed positivity of \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}\) justifies the division.  The independent Gaussian blocks give the same calculation.

For the active coordinate write
\[
X_s=a_j(\rho)h_s+Z,
\qquad
T_j=d_j(\theta_j)h_s+r_j(\rho,\theta_j)Z+v_j(\rho,\theta_j)E,
\]
where \(Z,E\) are independent standard normals.  Under the null, selection is \(Z>c_s\), so integrating over \(E\) gives the defining equation for \(q_{\alpha,j}\).  Under the local alternative, selection is \(Z>c_s-a_j(\rho)h_s\), and the same integration gives exactly \(\Pi_j\).  Thus no signed inequality belonging to another coordinate of the complete cell has been discarded.

%%% FIELD proof-derived-max-influence
Fix a block and suppress its indices.  From \(\max_i x_i^4\le\sum_i x_i^4\) and the fourth-moment condition,
\[
\max_i(B_i-\bar B)^2
\le\left\{\sum_i(B_i-\bar B)^4\right\}^{1/2}
\le\sqrt{M_4N}.
\]
The variance margin gives \(\sum_i(B_i-\bar B)^2=(N-1)S_B^2\ge(N-1)\epsilon\), proving the first claimed ratio bound.  Since \(0\le U_i^0\le1\), one has \(\max_i(U_i^0-\bar U^0)^2\le1\), while the same margin gives a denominator at least \((N-1)\epsilon\).  Positive shares imply \(N_{nks}\to\infty\) uniformly over the fixed finite set \(K\times S\).  Both displayed ratios therefore converge uniformly to zero, and every division is licensed by \(\epsilon>0\).

%%% FIELD proof-derived-residual-scale
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the defining covariance ratio gives \(\lvert\kappa_j\rvert\le1\).  Since \(\rho\in[\rho_0,1-\rho_0]\) and \(0\le\cos^2\theta_j\le1\),
\[
v_j(\rho,\theta_j)^2
=1-\kappa_j^2(1-\rho)\cos^2\theta_j
\ge1-(1-\rho)=\rho\ge\rho_0>0.
\]
This calculation concerns only the limiting Gaussian covariance and asserts no finite-sample covariance inequality.

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-transfer
Fix one admissible baseline array \(B=(B_n)_n\).  All uniformities below are over the displayed compact parameter sets for this array; there is no supremum over different arrays.

For a stage-one block write \(N=N_{n1s}\), \(m=m_{n1s}\), \(\delta=h_s/\sqrt n\), and let \(G\) be the random complement.  The hypergeometric mean and variance formulas, the bounds \(\rho_0\le\rho\le1-\rho_0\), and balanced quotas give, uniformly in \(\rho\),
\[
\frac{g^1}{N}\xrightarrow{\mathsf P}\frac{1-\rho}{2},
\qquad
\frac{g^0}{N}\xrightarrow{\mathsf P}\frac{1-\rho}{2}.
\]
For \(f_i\) equal to \(B_i-\bar B\), \((B_i-\bar B)^2\), or \((B_i-\bar B)(U_i^0-\bar U^0)\), the simple-random-sampling variance identity and Cauchy--Schwarz with the fourth-moment bound imply
\[
|G|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G}f_i-N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Nf_i=o_{\mathsf P}(1)
\]
uniformly over the admissible complement sizes.  Conditioning on the treatment quota and applying the same identity within \(G\) gives the corresponding statement for either arm; the preceding count limit bounds the random denominators away from zero with probability tending to one.  Hence both arm variances converge to their finite-population limits, the exceptional totalization event vanishes, and direct studentization yields
\[
X_{n,s}=A_s\sqrt{1-\rho}\,h_s+X^0_{n,s}+o_{\mathsf P}(1),
\]
where \(X^0_{n,s}\) is the standardized complement contrast of the fixed baselines.

For ranks, let \(K_N(\delta)\) be the ordered-pair crossing count.  Complete-randomization inclusion probabilities give
\[
\mathsf E(\text{treated crossed-pair count})
=\frac{m(N-m)}{N(N-1)}K_N(\delta).
\]
Writing the variance as a double sum of ordered edges, pairs sharing a vertex contribute at most a constant times \(NK_N(\delta)\), whereas disjoint pairs have covariance of order \(N^{-1}\) and contribute at most a constant times \(K_N(\delta)^2/N\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\text{crossed-pair count})
\le C\{NK_N(\delta)+K_N(\delta)^2/N\}.
\]
The uniform pair-spacing condition gives \(K_N(\delta)=O(N^{3/2})\).  After division by the null rank-sum standard deviation, which is of order \(N^{3/2}\), the centered crossing term is \(o_{\mathsf P}(1)\), while its expectation converges to the stated drift.  If \(d_i\) is the number of crossings incident to unit \(i\), then
\[
\frac1N\sum_i(d_i/N)^2\le\frac{2K_N(\delta)}{N^2}=O(N^{-1/2}),
\]
so the observed ranks and baseline ranks have the same limiting variance and covariance.  The pair-spacing premise also makes the squared empirical tie masses vanish, whence the normalized-midrank variance tends to \(1/12\).  Consequently
\[
R_{1j}=b_{1j}h_s+R^0_{1j}+o_{\mathsf P}(1),
\qquad
R_{2j}=b_{2j}h_s+R^0_{2j}+o_{\mathsf P}(1).
\]

Conditional on a nonexceptional holdout, every scalar projection of \((X^0_{n,s},R^0_{1j})\) obeys the maximum-influence condition proved in \(\mathrm{lem{:}derived\mbox{-}max\mbox{-}influence}\).  The cited finite-population vector central limit theorem applies.  A covariance calculation with \(W_i=2Z_i-1\), using \(\mathsf E(W_iW_l)=-1/(N-1)\) for \(i\ne l\), gives
\[
\operatorname{Corr}(X^0_{n,s},R^0_{1j})
\longrightarrow\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho};
\]
the complement indicator contributes one factor \(1-\rho\) to covariance and one square-root factor to the selector standard deviation.  Stage two is independent conditional on the history.  Since \(S\) and \(J\) are fixed and finite, the cited vector theorem applies to the concatenated fixed-dimensional vector.  It follows that
\[
(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\Longrightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(
\begin{pmatrix}A_s\sqrt{1-\rho}h_s\\b_{1j}h_s\\b_{2j}h_s\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho}&0\\
\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho}&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
\right).
\]
Uniformity follows by subsequences: a violating sequence of \((\rho,h,\theta)\) has a convergent subsequence, and the uniform share, moment, pair-spacing, and influence bounds give the same limit along it.  The residual-scale lemma excludes singular projected limits.

For the Fisher reference, the completed stage-one values are \(B_i+\delta Z_i\).  Their centered second and fourth moments differ from those of \(B\) by \(o_{\mathsf P}(1)\), and their normalized ranks differ in empirical squared norm by \(o_{\mathsf P}(1)\) by the crossing-degree bound.  Candidate treatment labels and holdouts are freshly randomized from their product law, so every candidate contrast is null-centered; candidate stage-two assignments are independent after the candidate cell is retained.  The same finite-population argument therefore makes the pre-cell reference vector converge, in the corresponding \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\)-probability and uniformly in \((\rho,h,\theta)\), to the same Gaussian vector with all drifts zero.

Rectangle convergence at the fixed thresholds transfers both joint numerators and cell probabilities.  The sampling margin bounds the true denominators below by \(\epsilon\), and the reference margin does so with probability tending to one.  The conditioning-ratio lemma and the finite-product factorization then yield the two asserted uniform conditional-cdf limits.  The inequality \(v_j^2\ge\rho_0\), finiteness of \(S,J\), compactness of the parameters, and Gaussian tail bounds place all null critical values in one compact interval and give a positive lower density bound on a slightly larger interval.  The quantile part of the ratio lemma therefore transfers the random reference quantiles uniformly.

Let \(a_n=d_n^{\mathrm{samp}}(B)\), and let \(Z_n(\rho,h)\) denote the reference distance.  The preceding steps show \(a_n\to0\) and, for every \(u>0\),
\[
\sup_{\rho\in I,h\in H}\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{Z_n(\rho,h)>u\}\to0.
\]
Choose \(N_m\uparrow\infty\) so that for \(n\ge N_m\), \(a_n\le1/m\) and the last supremum at \(u=1/m\) is at most \(1/m\).  Defining \(\eta_n(B)=1/m\) on \([N_m,N_{m+1})\), with finitely many initial terms enlarged, gives the common decreasing remainder.

Finally bracket the rejection event on the event that its random critical value is within \(\zeta\) of \(q_{\alpha,j}\).  The complement has probability tending to zero; on the bracketed event the sampling cdf is evaluated at \(q_{\alpha,j}\pm\zeta\).  Gaussian anti-concentration, followed by \(\zeta\downarrow0\), gives the rejection limit.  The factorization lemma identifies that limit with \(\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,h_s)\).  This proves the stated field-level sampling and reference transfer with one array-dependent remainder.

%%% FIELD proof-selected-quantile-convexity
Let \(\gamma_2\) be standard Gaussian measure on \(\mathbb R^2\) and define
\[
\mathcal T(c,z)=Q^{-1}\{Q(c)Q(z)\}.
\]
Minkowski interpolation of the orthants \([c,\infty)\times[z,\infty)\) interpolates their thresholds.  The cited Ehrhard--Borell inequality and \(Q^{-1}(u)=-\Phi^{-1}(u)\) therefore show that \(\mathcal T\) is jointly convex.  Moreover, for standard normal \(Z\),
\[
\Pr\{\mathcal T(c,Z)>y\}=\frac{Q(y)}{Q(c)},\qquad y>c,
\]
so \(\mathcal T(c,Z)\) has the law of a standard normal conditioned to exceed \(c\).

Put \(v=(1-r^2)^{1/2}>0\), \(W(c,z,e)=r\mathcal T(c,z)+ve\), and
\[
\mathcal A(c,q)=\{(z,e):W(c,z,e)\le q\}.
\]
For \(r\ge0\), convexity gives
\[
(1-t)\mathcal A(c_0,q_0)+t\mathcal A(c_1,q_1)
\subseteq\mathcal A((1-t)c_0+tc_1,(1-t)q_0+tq_1).
\]
Taking \(q_i=q_\beta(r,c_i)\), both left sets have Gaussian measure \(1-\beta\).  Ehrhard--Borell, the inclusion, and uniqueness of the quantile (supplied by the independent \(vE\) term) imply
\[
q_\beta(r,(1-t)c_0+tc_1)
\le(1-t)q_\beta(r,c_0)+tq_\beta(r,c_1).
\]

If \(r=0\), this quantile is constant; if \(c_0=c_1\), chord equality is tautological.  Conversely suppose \(r>0\), \(c_0\ne c_1\), and equality holds.  Equality must then hold in the Gaussian-set comparison.  By the cited equality classification, the two closed convex sets \(\mathcal A(c_i,q_i)\) are parallel half-spaces or equal up to null sets.  Their boundaries
\[
e=\{q_i-r\mathcal T(c_i,z)\}/v
\]
are not affine because \(\mathcal T(c,z)\to c\) as \(z\to-\infty\) and tends to infinity as \(z\to\infty\).  Equality up to null sets between closed convex sets with nonempty interiors is equality, so \(\mathcal T(c_0,z)-\mathcal T(c_1,z)\) would be constant.  Its limit at \(-\infty\) is \(c_0-c_1\).  Integration by parts gives the needed Mills bounds: \(Q(x)<x^{-1}\int_x^\infty t\phi(t)\,dt=\phi(x)/x\), while \(Q(x)=\phi(x)/x-\int_x^\infty\phi(t)t^{-2}\,dt>\phi(x)/x-Q(x)/x^2\).  Hence
\[
\frac{\phi(x)}{x+x^{-1}}<Q(x)<\frac{\phi(x)}x\qquad(x>0)
\]
make the ratio of the two tails tend to zero or infinity as \(z\to\infty\), whereas the defining identity makes it the finite constant \(Q(c_0)/Q(c_1)\).  Thus the constant is zero and \(c_0=c_1\), a contradiction.

%%% FIELD proof-least-coordinate
Fix \(j=(C,s)\), \(\rho\), and \(\theta_j\), and abbreviate \(a=a_j(\rho)>0\), \(d=d_j(\theta_j)>0\), and \(r=r_j(\rho,\theta_j)\).  For \(u\in(\alpha,1)\), the selected-quantile definition gives
\[
\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,h_s)\ge u
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_u(h_s):=dh_s+q_u(r,c_s-ah_s)-q_\alpha(r,c_s)\ge0.
\]
The selected-quantile theorem makes \(B_u\) continuous and convex.  Since \(u>\alpha\), \(B_u(0)<0\).  As \(h_s\to\infty\), the selected threshold tends to \(-\infty\), its selected quantile tends to the ordinary Gaussian quantile, and \(dh_s\to\infty\); hence \(B_u(h_s)\to\infty\).  A convex function negative at zero has at most one positive transition from negative to nonnegative: at a zero \(x>0\), every right secant slope is at least \(\{B_u(x)-B_u(0)\}/x>0\).  Thus its nonnegative set on \((0,\infty)\) is an upper interval.

Compactness of \(H\) attains \(\ell_s=\min_{h\in H}h_s>0\), and every other admissible coordinate is at least \(\ell_s\).  The upper-interval property proves the stated equivalence.  The feasible pure-stage-two weight \(\theta_j=\pi/2\) has \(r=0\) and power
\[
Q\{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-b_{2j}h_s\}>\alpha.
\]
Therefore all optimized cell values and the global optimized value exceed \(\alpha\).  Equality of all superlevel predicates above \(\alpha\) implies equality of both optimized values and complete argmax sets.  This argument is coordinatewise and uses only finiteness of \(J\), not a two-coordinate property.

%%% FIELD proof-weight-side
Fix an active pair and suppress its index.  Put \(t=\sqrt{1-\rho}\), \(a=At\), \(\gamma=\kappa t\), \(d=b_1\cos\theta+b_2\sin\theta\), \(r=\gamma\cos\theta\), and \(v=\sqrt{1-r^2}\).  For \(h=\ell_s>0\), set
\[
L=c-ah,\quad K=q_\alpha(r,c)-dh,\quad
u_0=\frac{c-rq_\alpha(r,c)}v,\quad
u_h=\frac{L-rK}v,
\quad D_h=\frac{\phi(K)Q(u_h)}{Q(L)}>0.
\]
Differentiation of the Gaussian tail integral and its null critical equation gives
\[
q_r=\frac{\mu(u_0)}v,\qquad P_d=hD_h,qquad
P_r=\frac{D_h}{v}\{\mu(u_h)-\mu(u_0)\},
\]
where \(\mu(x)=\phi(x)/Q(x)\).  Every division is valid because \(v^2\ge\rho_0\) and Gaussian tails are positive.  The identity \(\mu'(x)=\mu(x)\{\mu(x)-x\}\) gives \(\mu'(x)>0\), because \(\mu(x)=\mathsf E(Z\mid Z>x)>x\).  Integration by parts also gives \(\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid Z>x)=1-\mu'(x)>0\), and hence \(\mu'(x)<1\).  Therefore the secant
\[
\bar s=\frac{\mu(u_0)-\mu(u_h)}{u_0-u_h}
\]
(with \(\mu'(u_0)\) at equality) lies in \((0,1)\), while \(u_0-u_h=h(a-rd)/v\).

With \(d'=b_2\cos\theta-b_1\sin\theta\) and \(r'=-\gamma\sin\theta\), the chain rule yields
\[
\frac{P_\theta}{hD_h}
=d'+\frac{\gamma\sin\theta\,(a-rd)\bar s}{v^2}.
\]
Using \(\cos\theta\,d=b_1+\sin\theta\,d'\) and \(\Delta=A-\kappa b_1\), this becomes
\[
\frac{P_\theta}{hD_h}
=\mathcal Ld'+\frac{\kappa t^2\sin\theta\,\Delta\bar s}{v^2},
\qquad
\mathcal L=1-\frac{\gamma^2\sin^2\theta\,\bar s}{v^2}>0,
\]
because \(v^2-\gamma^2\sin^2\theta\bar s\ge1-\gamma^2\ge\rho>0\).  Now \(d'\) is positive below \(\theta_0=\arctan(b_2/b_1)\), zero there, and negative above.  If \(\Delta>0\), the derivative is positive throughout \([0,\theta_0]\); if \(\Delta<0\), it is negative throughout \([\theta_0,\pi/2]\), while \(P_\theta(0)=hb_2D_h>0\); and if \(\Delta=0\), its sign is exactly that of \(d'\).  Compactness supplies maximizers and proves all three claimed strata.  The least-coordinate theorem transfers the result to the original compact alternative set.

%%% FIELD proof-realizability
Fix a jointly realizable active stage-one block, center its empirical baseline law, and let its limiting variance and pair slope be \(\sigma^2>0\) and \(\lambda>0\).  Convolve the empirical law with the uniform density on an interval of width \(\delta_N=u_\star/\sqrt N\), where \([0,u_\star]\) is inside the pair-spacing window.  For the resulting density \(f_N\), the ordered-pair convention gives the exact overlap identity
\[
\|f_N\|_2^2
=\frac{2}{N^2\delta_N^2}\int_0^{\delta_N}K_N(u)\,du
=\frac{2}{u_\star^2}\int_0^{u_\star}N^{-3/2}K_N(x/\sqrt N)\,dx
\longrightarrow\lambda.
\]
Its variance is \(\sigma_N^2+\delta_N^2/12\), which tends to \(\sigma^2\) by the variance and share premises.

For any centered density \(f\) of variance \(\sigma_f^2>0\), take \(a=\sqrt5\sigma_f\) and \(g(x)=3(1-x^2/a^2)_+/(4a)\).  Since \((1-x^2/a^2)_+\ge1-x^2/a^2\),
\[
\int fg\ge\frac{3}{5a},
\qquad
\int g^2=\frac{3}{5a}.
\]
The inequality \(\int(f-g)^2\ge0\) gives \(\|f\|_2^2\sigma_f\ge3/(5\sqrt5)\).  Applying it to \(f_N\) gives \(\lambda\sigma\ge3/(5\sqrt5)\).

Pair spacing removes asymptotic atoms, so the normalized midrank variance tends to \(1/12\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(B,U^0)\le\sigma/\sqrt{12}\).  Joint realizability and the definitions imply
\[
A=\frac{\sqrt{r_1}}{2\sigma},\qquad
b_1=\sqrt3\lambda\sqrt{r_1},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{12}\operatorname{Cov}(B,U^0)}\sigma.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{A\kappa}{b_1}
=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(B,U^0)}{\sigma^2\lambda}
\le\frac{1}{\sqrt{12}\sigma\lambda}
\le\frac{5\sqrt{15}}{18}.
\]
All denominators are positive by the primitive variance and pair-spacing conditions, and the argument is cellwise, hence uniform over finite \(J\).

%%% FIELD proof-global-endpoint
Fix \(j\) with \(\Delta_j\ge0\) and write \(P(\rho,\theta)=\Pi_j(\rho,\theta,\ell_s)\).  In the notation of the weight-side proof, differentiation gives, for \(r>0\),
\[
P_a=h\mu(L)\left[Q\!\left(\frac{q-dh-rL}{v}\right)-P\right]<0,
\qquad
\operatorname{sign}(P_r)=\operatorname{sign}(rd-a).
\]
The strict inequality holds because conditional rejection probability increases strictly with the selected Gaussian coordinate, so its boundary value is below its selected-tail average.  Every individually optimal angle obeys \(\theta\ge\theta_0\), and on this interval \(\cos\theta\,d\le b_1\).  Hence
\[
a-rd=t\{A-\kappa\cos\theta\,d\}\ge t\Delta\ge0,
\]
so \(P_r\le0\).  Since \(a_\rho=-A/(2t)\) and \(r_\rho=-\kappa\cos\theta/(2t)\),
\[
P_\rho=-\frac{AP_a+\kappa\cos\theta P_r}{2t}>0
\]
at every optimal non-pure angle.  At \(\theta=\pi/2\), power is the constant \(p_2=Q\{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-b_2\ell_s\}\).

At \(\rho=1-\rho_0\), the inward angular derivative from \(\pi/2\) is
\[
\phi\{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-b_2h\}
\left[b_1h-\kappa t\{\mu(c)-\mu(c-Ath)\}\right].
\]
Because \(0<\mu'<1\), its bracket exceeds \(h(b_1-\kappa At^2)\).  The realizability bound and \(t^2=\rho_0<1/2\) yield
\[
\frac{\kappa At^2}{b_1}
\le\rho_0\frac{5\sqrt{15}}{18}<1,
\]
so the pure-stage-two value is strictly improvable at the endpoint.

Thus \(M_j(\rho)\) is strictly smaller at every \(\rho<1-\rho_0\): if its optimizer is pure stage two, its value is \(p_2\), below the endpoint value; otherwise its fixed-angle power strictly increases toward the endpoint.  Choose a cell binding in the finite minimum at the endpoint.  Its strict inequality implies
\[
F(\rho)<F(1-\rho_0)\qquad(\rho<1-\rho_0),
\]
proving uniqueness of the holdout fraction.  The weight-side theorem gives the binding-cell angle location and equality stratum, while the definition of \(\mathcal S^\star\) gives the stated superlevel sets for slack cells.

%%% FIELD proof-negative-mixture-primitives
Before scaling, the stage-one density is \(16/5\) on \([-1/10,1/10]\) and \(1/5\) on the remainder of \([-1,1]\).  Direct integration gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(B)=\frac{203}{1500},\qquad
\int f^2=\frac{53}{25},\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}\{B,F(B)\}=\frac14\mathsf E|B-B'|=\frac{1393}{15000}.
\]
