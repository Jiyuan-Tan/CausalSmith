Fix \(n\), \(\rho\in I\), and the fixed potential-outcome schedule.  For each \(s\in S\), put
\[
\mathcal A_{1s}(\rho)=\left\{(d_s,z_s)\in\{0,1\}^{N_{n1s}}\times\{0,1\}^{N_{n1s}}:
\sum_i d_{si}=\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor,
\ \sum_i z_{si}=m_{n1s}\right\}.
\]
The definition \(D_{nsi}=\mathbf 1\{i\in H_{n,s}(\rho)\}\) is part of \(\mathrm{def{:}fzg\mbox{-}augmented\mbox{-}action\mbox{-}map}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}random\mbox{-}holdout}\), \(D_{ns}\) is uniform on the vectors having \(\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor\) ones, is independent across \(s\), and is independent of the fixed baseline schedule.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}stage1\mbox{-}complete\mbox{-}randomization}\), conditionally on all holdouts the vectors \(Z_{n1s}\) are independent across strata, each is uniform on the vectors having \(m_{n1s}\) ones, and this conditional law is constant in the realized holdouts.  Consequently, for every \(((d_s,z_s))_{s\in S}\),
\[
\Pr\!\left\{(D_{ns},Z_{n1s})=(d_s,z_s),\ s\in S\right\}
=q_1((d_s,z_s)_{s\in S})
=\prod_{s\in S}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{(d_s,z_s)\in\mathcal A_{1s}(\rho)\}}
{\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}}.
\]
Because \(S\) is finite and nonempty, summing the last display over its finite product support factorizes into a product of sums, each equal to one.  Thus \(q_1\) is normalized, known from \((N,m,\rho)\), and strictly positive at every supported first action.  This verifies A1 for the first augmented action.  The potential outcomes are fixed under the design, the holdout is independent of the baseline schedule by \(\mathrm{ass{:}random\mbox{-}holdout}\), and the treatment law is the fixed conditional law just displayed; hence the augmented first action is exogenous relative to the potential-outcome schedule.  This is A2.  The duplication
\[
\widetilde Y_{n1si}(d,z)=Y^{(h)}_{n1si}(z)
\]
from \(\mathrm{def{:}fzg\mbox{-}augmented\mbox{-}action\mbox{-}map}\) is well defined because \(\mathrm{ass{:}no\mbox{-}interference}\) makes the original potential outcome depend only on the unit's own treatment coordinate \(z\).

The threshold vector is fixed before randomization by \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\mbox{-}thresholds}\), so the formulas defining \(X\) and \(C\) make \(C\) a prespecified measurable function of the first augmented action and its observed outcomes.  Once \(C\) is realized, the recruited second-stage population is exactly
\[
\mathcal R_2(C)=\{(2,s,i):C_s=1,\ i\le N_{n2s}\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}stage2\mbox{-}complete\mbox{-}randomization}\), for any quota-respecting collection \(z_2=(z_{n2s})_{s:C_s=1}\),
\[
\Pr\{Z_{n2}=z_2\mid\mathcal F_{n,1}(\rho)\}
=q_2(z_2\mid C)
=\prod_{s:C_s=1}\binom{N_{n2s}}{m_{n2s}}^{-1},
\]
and the probability is zero off that support.  No second-stage coordinate exists when \(C_s=0\).  Thus both the recruited population and the second-action kernel depend on the first-stage history only through \(C\), which verifies A3.

We next identify the candidate law exactly.  Fix a realized dataset \(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho}\) and write \(C=C_n(\rho)\).  Its realized first action belongs to \(\prod_{s\in S}\mathcal A_{1s}(\rho)\).  If that same action is used as a candidate, the Fisher completion in \(\mathrm{def{:}fisher\mbox{-}null\mbox{-}reference}\) reproduces every actually observed response entering the selector.  Hence the candidate formulas give \(X'_{n,s}=X_{n,s}\) for every \(s\), including under the common deterministic degeneracy convention, and therefore \(C'_n(\rho)=C_n(\rho)=C\).  Since \(q_1\) is positive at the realized action,
\[
p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)
=\sum_{a'_1}q_1(a'_1)\mathbf 1\{C'_n(a'_1;\rho)=C\}>0.
\]
Thus the same-cell normalization is always legal for a realized cell.  Independent candidate-action resampling, restriction to that cell, and subsequent second-stage resampling assign a candidate history \((a'_1,a'_2)\) the mass
\[
\frac{q_1(a'_1)\mathbf 1\{C'_n(a'_1;\rho)=C\}}
{p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)}
q_2(a'_2\mid C).
\]
Substitution of the two product masses gives
\[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{C'_n(a'_1;\rho)=C\}}
{p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)}
\prod_{s\in S}
\left\{
\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}
\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}
\right\}^{-1}
\prod_{s:C_s=1}\binom{N_{n2s}}{m_{n2s}}^{-1}
\]
on quota-respecting histories and zero otherwise.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\mbox{-}candidate\mbox{-}support}\), the positive-mass, same-cell histories in this display are exactly \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\), with no additional imputability restriction.  The displayed mass is therefore exactly the kernel \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\) of \(\mathrm{def{:}fisher\mbox{-}null\mbox{-}reference}\).  These are precisely the independent resampling, same-selector restriction, and conditional product identities in equations (5)--(7) of the cited Freidling--Zhao--Gao construction recorded in \(\mathrm{lem{:}fzg\mbox{-}reference\mbox{-}computability}\).

It remains to separate computability from validity.  At \(h=0\), the sharp-null construction gives
\[
Y^{(0)}_{nksi}(0)=Y^{(0)}_{nksi}(1)=B_{nksi}.
\]
Accordingly, the duplicated outcome satisfies \(\widetilde Y_{n1si}(d,z)=B_{n1si}\) for every augmented action, and the Fisher completion also equals the fixed observed baseline value on every observed unit.  Relative to the constant schedule \(\mathcal G\) specified in \(\mathrm{def{:}fzg\mbox{-}augmented\mbox{-}action\mbox{-}map}\), the selector can therefore be recomputed for every candidate action, the statistics \(R'_1,R'_2,T'\) can be recomputed on the support identified above, and \(q_1,q_2\) are known functions of the design quantities.  Thus the statistic, selector, and action masses are imputable with respect to \(\mathcal G\).  Having already verified A2--A3 and A1, respectively, \(\mathrm{lem{:}fzg\mbox{-}reference\mbox{-}computability}\) applies and identifies the computable Proposition 2 reference with the kernel \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\) just derived.

Separately, \(\mathrm{lem{:}fzg\mbox{-}selective\mbox{-}validity}\) applies to the same-selector randomization \(p\)-value and gives its conditional superuniformity under the sharp null.  Hence the rejection rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}selective\mbox{-}test}\), with strict rejection above the upper \(\alpha\)-critical value, has conditional type-I error at most \(\alpha\) in every selected cell of positive probability; discreteness can only make it conservative.  This invocation of Proposition 1 does not use imputability as a premise.  The argument proves an exact specialization of the cited representation, not the converse inclusion of model classes.  Moreover, the two cited results used here assert only selective validity and reference-law computability; neither supplies a selected Gaussian limit nor any optimization over \(\rho\) or the weights \(\theta\).  Therefore no Gaussian-transfer or holdout-power optimality conclusion has been imported through the specialization.
