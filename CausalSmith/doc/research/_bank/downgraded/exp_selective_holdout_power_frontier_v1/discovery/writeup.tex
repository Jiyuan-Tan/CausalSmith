\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}selective\_\allowbreak{}holdout\_\allowbreak{}power\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For an arbitrary fixed finite nonempty stratum set \(S\), a common holdout fraction \(\rho\) indexes a chronological two-stage enrichment randomization and its same-design Fisher sharp-null reference. Stage-one randomized action is the pair of holdout and treatment vectors; this gives a formal specialization of Freidling--Zhao--Gao's augmented-action representation, with their Proposition 1 supplying validity independently of imputability and Proposition 2 supplying computability at the sharp null. A per-array common remainder transfers the shifted sampling cdf and the zero-drift data-dependent reference cdf on every complete signed cell. Finite-product factorization reduces each active cell to a scalar selected Gaussian power. Ehrhard--Borell convexity reduces arbitrary compact positive alternatives to their least coordinate, the sign of \(\Delta_j=A_s-\kappa_jb_{1j}\) fixes the side of the proportional stage weight, all nonnegative discrepancies force the largest holdout, and an explicit two-stratum mixture certifies an interior negative-discrepancy regime. Mixed signs have a complete equality-aware analytic branch characterization, while interval computation returns certified value and optimizer-set boxes and asserts uniqueness only with separation.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Freidling, Zhao, and Gao provide a broader adaptive-experiment validity and conditional-reference framework, but do not derive a finite-population selected Gaussian power limit or optimize a common holdout and cell-specific stage weights. Existing finite-population central limit theorems are unconditional, and Rosenblum, Fang, and Liu study nonnested normal model-based policies with a different expected-sample-size and familywise-error objective. None supplies the present field-level sampling/reference transfer and maximin conditional-power frontier over arbitrary fixed finite strata.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing design object is a fixed-finite-stratum, design-based maximin problem in which one scalar holdout changes both selection and retained first-stage information, while each active complete-cell coordinate has its own nonnegative unit-norm stage weight. The optimizer can be an endpoint, an interior point, or a set-valued mixed-sign correspondence.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project embeds the stage-one action as \(\widetilde Z_{n1si}=(D_{nsi},Z_{n1si})\), with \(D_{nsi}=\mathbf 1\{i\in H_{n,s}\}\), duplicates potential outcomes across the holdout label, and verifies the FZG A1--A3 and same-selector resampling equations. At the sharp null, Proposition 2 computes the explicit chronological reference and Proposition 1 separately transfers exact validity. The new contribution then derives, for arbitrary fixed finite \(S\), a common per-array split-conditional Gaussian and reference transfer, scalar complete-cell power integrals, least-coordinate reduction, the \(\Delta\)-indexed weight theorem, a unique largest-holdout region, a certified two-stratum interior witness, full mixed-sign branch geometry, and tolerance-certified optimizer sets.

\section{Related work}

Freidling, Zhao, and Gao's Definition 1 and Propositions 1--2 provide the broader adaptive-experiment representation specialized here. The exact map uses stage-one population \(\mathcal R_1\), augmented action \(D,Z_1\), selector \(\mathcal S_1=C\), selected population \(\mathcal R_2(C)\), and a constant sharp-null schedule \(\mathcal G\). Their Proposition 1 yields validity without imputability; A2--A3 in Proposition 2 yield the conditional-law representation, and A1 plus imputability yield computability. This is a specialization, not class equivalence, and their broader \(K\)-stage class supplies no selected Gaussian power frontier. Li and Ding supply the finite-population vector central limit theorem used after the present influence bounds. Shenfeld and van Handel supply Ehrhard--Borell convexity and equality cases. Rosenblum, Fang, and Liu are a nonnested architectural comparator: their normal model-based policies optimize expected sample size and familywise error rather than exact complete-cell conditional-randomization local power, so no optimality theorem transfers. Chen and Andrews concern parametric Gaussian adaptive experiments rather than fixed finite populations.

The resulting \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\) has support exactly \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\); each supported history has mass \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)^{-1}\prod_{s\in S}\{\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}\}^{-1}\prod_{s:C_s=1}\binom{N_{n2s}}{m_{n2s}}^{-1}\), and its statistic is \(T'_j(\theta_j)\).
  \item \texttt{phi} --- \texttt{function}; \(\phi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)\); \texttt{Gaussian primitive}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{standard normal density}
  \item \texttt{Phi} --- \texttt{function}; \(\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)\); \texttt{Gaussian primitive}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{standard normal distribution function}
  \item \texttt{Q} --- \texttt{function}; \(Q:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)\); \texttt{Gaussian survival function}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(Q(x)=1-\Phi(x)\)
  \item \texttt{A} --- \texttt{positive array}; \((0,\infty)^S\); \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}derived selector-\allowbreak{}drift limit}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Writing \(\bar B_{n1s}=N_{n1s}^{-1}\sum_iB_{n1si}\) and \(S^2_{B,n1s}=(N_{n1s}-1)^{-1}\sum_i(B_{n1si}-\bar B_{n1s})^2\), define \(A_s=\lim_{n\to\infty}[nS^2_{B,n1s}\{m_{n1s}^{-1}+(N_{n1s}-m_{n1s})^{-1}\}]^{-1/2}\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), define \(b_{1j}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt3K_{n1s}(h_s/\sqrt n)/(h_sN_{n1s}^{3/2})=\sqrt3\lambda_{1s}\sqrt{r_{1s}}\), using the exact crossing count that generates the local mean of \(R_{1j}\).
  \item \texttt{b2} --- \texttt{positive array}; \((0,\infty)^J\); \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}derived stage-\allowbreak{}2 rank-\allowbreak{}drift limit}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), define \(b_{2j}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt3K_{n2s}(h_s/\sqrt n)/(h_sN_{n2s}^{3/2})=\sqrt3\lambda_{2s}\sqrt{r_{2s}}\), using the exact crossing count that generates the local mean of \(R_{2j}\).
  \item \texttt{kappa} --- \texttt{array}; \((0,1]^J\); \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}derived selector-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}stage-\allowbreak{}1-\allowbreak{}rank correlation limit}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), define \(\kappa_j=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_i(B_{n1si}-\bar B_{n1s})(U^0_{n1si}-\bar U^0_{n1s})}{[\sum_i(B_{n1si}-\bar B_{n1s})^2\sum_i(U^0_{n1si}-\bar U^0_{n1s})^2]^{1/2}}\), where the bars are block averages.
  \item \texttt{Delta} --- \texttt{array}; \(\mathbb{R}^{J}\); \texttt{weight-\allowbreak{}side discrepancy}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \(\Delta_j=A_s-\kappa_j b_{1j}\)
  \item \texttt{a} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{selector drift}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \(a_j(\rho)=A_s\sqrt{1-\rho}\)
  \item \texttt{r} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{selector-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}test correlation}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(r_j(\rho,\theta_j)=\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho}\cos(\theta_j)\)
  \item \texttt{d} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{test drift}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(d_j(\theta_j)=b_{1j}\cos(\theta_j)+b_{2j}\sin(\theta_j)\)
  \item \texttt{v} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{conditional residual scale}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(v_j(\rho,\theta_j)=\sqrt{1-r_j(\rho,\theta_j)^2}\)
  \item \texttt{qbeta} --- \texttt{function}; \(q_{\beta}:[0,1)\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\); \texttt{generic selected-\allowbreak{}score upper quantile}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(q_{\beta}(r_{\mathrm g},x)\) is the unique \(q\) satisfying \(\Pr\{r_{\mathrm g}Z+\sqrt{1-r_{\mathrm g}^2}E>q\mid Z>x\}=\beta\) for independent standard normal \(Z,E\).
  \item \texttt{Eq} --- \texttt{predicate}; \(\operatorname{Eq}_{\beta,r}(c_0,c_1;t)\); \texttt{selected-\allowbreak{}quantile chord-\allowbreak{}equality predicate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(0<t<1\), \(\operatorname{Eq}_{\beta,r}(c_0,c_1;t)\) means \(q_{\beta}(r,(1-t)c_0+tc_1)=(1-t)q_{\beta}(r,c_0)+tq_{\beta}(r,c_1)\).
  \item \texttt{qalpha} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{full-\allowbreak{}cell selected sharp-\allowbreak{}null critical value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \(q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\theta_j)\) is the unique \(q\) satisfying \(\int_{c_s}^{\infty}\phi(z)Q((q-r_j(\rho,\theta_j)z)/v_j(\rho,\theta_j))\,dz=\alpha Q(c_s)\); this is the active-stratum null quantile after conditioning on the full event \(E_{n,C}(\rho)\)
  \item \texttt{Pi} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{full-\allowbreak{}cell Gaussian conditional local power}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \(\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,h_s)=Q(c_s-a_j(\rho)h_s)^{-1}\int_{c_s-a_j(\rho)h_s}^{\infty}\phi(z)Q((q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\theta_j)-d_j(\theta_j)h_s-r_j(\rho,\theta_j)z)/v_j(\rho,\theta_j))\,dz\); this is power conditional on the complete signed event \(E_{n,C}(\rho)\)
  \item \texttt{Gshift} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued distribution function}; \texttt{shifted Gaussian sampling-\allowbreak{}cdf limit}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G^h_j(x;\rho,\theta_j)\) is the cdf of \(T_j(\theta_j)\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}gaussian\mbox{-}experiment}\) with drift \(h_s\), conditional on its complete signed cell \(E_C\).
  \item \texttt{Gzero} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued distribution function}; \texttt{zero-\allowbreak{}drift Gaussian reference-\allowbreak{}cdf limit}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G^0_j(x;\rho,\theta_j)\) is the same complete-cell conditional Gaussian cdf with every drift set to zero; its upper \(\alpha\)-quantile is \(q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\theta_j)\).
  \item \texttt{ell} --- \texttt{vector}; \((0,\infty)^S\); \texttt{least alternative coordinate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\ell_s=\min\{h_s:h\in H\}\)
  \item \texttt{M} --- \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}valued function}; \texttt{cell power envelope}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(M_j(\rho)=\max_{\vartheta\in[0,\pi/2]}\Pi_j(\rho,\vartheta,\ell_s)\) for \(j=(C,s)\)
  \item \texttt{F} --- \texttt{function}; \texttt{weight-\allowbreak{}optimized worst-\allowbreak{}cell power}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(F(\rho)=\min_{j\in J}M_j(\rho)\)
  \item \texttt{Vstar} --- \texttt{scalar}; \texttt{maximin conditional-\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}power value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(V^{\star}=\max_{\rho\in I}F(\rho)\)
  \item \texttt{Sstar} --- \texttt{compact set}; \texttt{optimal design correspondence}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal{S}^{\star}=\{(\rho,(\theta_j)_{j\in J}):\min_{j\in J}\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,\ell_s)=V^{\star}\}\)
  \item \texttt{epsilon} --- \texttt{scalar}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}population variance and selection-\allowbreak{}probability margin}
  \item \texttt{M4} --- \texttt{scalar}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{baseline fourth-\allowbreak{}moment bound}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(M_4\) is the uniform empirical centered fourth-moment bound for the baseline blocks.
  \item \texttt{eta} --- \texttt{baseline-\allowbreak{}indexed sequence}; \(\{\eta_n(B):n\in\mathbb N\}\); \texttt{array-\allowbreak{}dependent transfer error}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For each admissible fixed baseline array \(B=(B_n)_n\), \(\eta_n(B)\downarrow0\) is an array-dependent common approximation remainder derived in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}selected\mbox{-}transfer}\).
  \item \texttt{dsamp} --- \texttt{nonnegative array-\allowbreak{}valued functional}; \texttt{sampling-\allowbreak{}cdf selected-\allowbreak{}law distance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(d^{\mathrm{samp}}_n(B)\) is the supremum over active \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \(\rho\in I\), \(\theta\in[0,\pi/2]^J\), \(h\in H\), and \(x\in\mathbb R\) of \(\lvert\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{T_j(\theta_j)\le x\mid E_{n,C}(\rho)\}-G^h_j(x;\rho,\theta_j)\rvert\).
  \item \texttt{Fref} --- \texttt{design-\allowbreak{}indexed random distribution function}; \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}design data-\allowbreak{}dependent Fisher-\allowbreak{}reference cdf}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \(F^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(x;\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})=\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\{T'_j(\theta_j)\le x\}\) is the Fisher-reference cdf built from the dataset realized under \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\) at that same \(\rho\).
  \item \texttt{qrefn} --- \texttt{design-\allowbreak{}indexed random scalar function}; \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}design data-\allowbreak{}dependent Fisher critical value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(q^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})=\inf\{x:F^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(x;\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})\ge1-\alpha\}\) is the upper \(\alpha\)-critical value of the same \(\rho\)-indexed Fisher-reference cdf.
  \item \texttt{dref} --- \texttt{design-\allowbreak{}indexed random nonnegative functional}; \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}design data-\allowbreak{}dependent zero-\allowbreak{}null reference distance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For a dataset \(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho}\) realized at a fixed \(\rho\), \(d^{\mathrm{ref}}_n(B,\rho,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})=\sup_{j=(C,s)\in J,\,\theta\in[0,\pi/2]^J,\,x\in\mathbb R}\max\{\lvert F^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(x;\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})-G^0_j(x;\rho,\theta_j)\rvert,\lvert q^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})-q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\theta_j)\rvert\}\).
  \item \texttt{Wmix} --- \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}population triangular-\allowbreak{}array construction}; \texttt{certified negative-\allowbreak{}discrepancy interior witness}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the deterministic-quantile two-component-uniform baseline schedule specified in \(\mathrm{def{:}negative\mbox{-}mixture\mbox{-}witness}\), with local outcomes generated from it by \(Y^{(h)}(0)=B\) and \(Y^{(h)}(1)=B+h_s/\sqrt n\)
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-interference]\label{ass:no-interference}
Each \(Y^{(h)}_{nksi}(z)\) depends only on unit \(i\)'s own treatment \(z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-population no interference, \cite{FreidlingZhaoGao2026SelectiveRI}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stage1-complete-randomization]\label{ass:stage1-complete-randomization}
After the holdouts are drawn and before recruitment is evaluated, \(\mathcal L((Z_{n1s})_{s\in S}\mid(H_{n,s}(\rho))_{s\in S})=\bigotimes_{s\in S}\mathrm{Unif}\{z\in\{0,1\}^{N_{n1s}}:\sum_i z_i=m_{n1s}\}\), and this conditional law is constant in the realized holdouts.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (stratified stage-1 complete randomization, \cite{LiDing2017FinitePopulationCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stage2-complete-randomization]\label{ass:stage2-complete-randomization}
Conditional on the realized stage-1 recruitment history, \(\mathcal L((Z_{n2s})_{s:C_{n,s}(\rho)=1}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1}(\rho))=\bigotimes_{s:C_{n,s}(\rho)=1}\mathrm{Unif}\{z\in\{0,1\}^{N_{n2s}}:\sum_i z_i=m_{n2s}\}\), and no stage-2 assignment vector is generated when \(C_{n,s}(\rho)=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (history-conditional stage-2 complete randomization, \cite{FreidlingZhaoGao2026SelectiveRI}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:random-holdout]\label{ass:random-holdout}
Before stage-1 treatment assignment, \(\{H_{n,s}(\rho):s\in S\}\) is drawn independently of the fixed baseline array \(B\), independently across strata, and uniformly over subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,N_{n1s}\}\) of size \(\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent stratified simple random sampling without replacement, \cite{LiDing2017FinitePopulationCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-compact-alternatives]\label{ass:positive-compact-alternatives}
\(H\) is nonempty and compact and satisfies \(H\subseteq[h_0,h_1]^S\) for fixed \(0<h_0\le h_1<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact local parameter set, \cite{TianTaylor2018RandomizedResponse}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-shares]\label{ass:positive-shares}
For every \((k,s)\in K\times S\), \(N_{nks}/n\to r_{ks}\) uniformly with \(\min_{k,s}r_{ks}>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (stable treatment-group proportions, \cite{LiDing2017FinitePopulationCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-fourth-moments]\label{ass:bounded-fourth-moments}
For every \(n\), \(k\in K\), and \(s\in S\), writing \(\bar B_{nks}=N_{nks}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{nks}}B_{nksi}\), the empirical centered fourth moment satisfies \(N_{nks}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{nks}}(B_{nksi}-\bar B_{nks})^4\le M_4\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded fourth-moment condition, \cite{ShiDing2025BerryEsseen}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-margin]\label{ass:variance-margin}
The finite-population variances of \(B_{nksi}\) and \(U^0_{nksi}\) are eventually at least \(\epsilon\), uniformly over \((k,s)\in K\times S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-population variance nondegeneracy condition, \cite{ShiDing2025BerryEsseen}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pair-spacing]\label{ass:pair-spacing}
Uniformly over \((k,s)\in K\times S\) and every \(u\) in the compact displacement window containing \(h_s\sqrt{r_{ks}}\) for \(h\in H\), \(N_{nks}^{-3/2}K_{nks}(u/\sqrt{N_{nks}})\to\lambda_{ks}u\), with \(0<\inf_{k,s}\lambda_{ks}\le\sup_{k,s}\lambda_{ks}<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} For each stage--stratum block, take a distribution on a bounded interval with continuously differentiable density \(f_{ks}\) satisfying \(0<\underline f\le f_{ks}\le\overline f<\infty\), and set \(B_{nksi}=F_{ks}^{-1}((i-1/2)/N_{nks})\). A uniform Riemann-sum calculation over every fixed compact displacement window gives \(N_{nks}^{-3/2}K_{nks}(u/\sqrt{N_{nks}})\to u\int f_{ks}(x)^2\,dx\). Thus the condition is realized by explicit bounded continuous-baseline quantile arrays, with \(\lambda_{ks}=\int f_{ks}^2\), and does not encode any holdout or weight conclusion.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:primitive-compactness]\label{ass:primitive-compactness}
The vector \( ((A_s)_{s\in S},(b_{1j},b_{2j},\kappa_j)_{j=(C,s)\in J},(c_s)_{s\in S})\) belongs to a fixed compact set every element of which is a joint limit, along one and the same baseline array \(B\), of the exact finite-array formulas displayed in the definitions of \(A_s\), \(b_{1j}\), \(b_{2j}\), and \(\kappa_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded continuous laws with outcome variance, local pair density, and mean--rank covariance bounded away from zero, including uniform laws and bounded two-component uniform mixtures, generate compact sets of these joint array limits. Requiring a single array to generate all coordinates excludes incompatible Gaussian tuples without stipulating any optimizer conclusion.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sampling-selection-margin]\label{ass:sampling-selection-margin}
\(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\rho\in I,h\in H,C:\exists s\,(C,s)\in J}\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{E_{n,C}(\rho)\}\ge\epsilon\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Fixed finite thresholds and bounded continuous baseline laws yield nonvanishing Gaussian probabilities for each prespecified complete signed recruitment cell. Trial protocols can omit cells whose design probability is below a declared operational minimum.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reference-selection-margin]\label{ass:reference-selection-margin}
\(\sup_{\rho\in I,h\in H}\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{\min_{C:\exists s\,(C,s)\in J}p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)<\epsilon\}\to0\text{ for each fixed admissible }B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} For bounded continuous quantile arrays and fixed thresholds, the Fisher-completed candidate selector has a nondegenerate Gaussian limit conditionally on the realized data, so its complete-cell probabilities converge in probability to positive Gaussian cell probabilities.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-thresholds]\label{ass:fixed-thresholds}
The threshold vector \(c\) is fixed before treatment assignment and does not vary with \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (prespecified enrichment rule, \cite{RosenblumFangLiu2020OptimalEnrichment}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:population-class]\label{def:population-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Uniform local-\allowbreak{}pair-\allowbreak{}spacing enrichment class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M=\{(B,N,Z_1,Z_2,H_{\mathrm{ld}}):\mathrm{ass{:}no\mbox{-}interference},\mathrm{ass{:}stage1\mbox{-}complete\mbox{-}randomization},\mathrm{ass{:}stage2\mbox{-}complete\mbox{-}randomization},\mathrm{ass{:}random\mbox{-}holdout},\mathrm{ass{:}positive\mbox{-}shares},\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\mbox{-}fourth\mbox{-}moments},\mathrm{ass{:}variance\mbox{-}margin},\mathrm{ass{:}pair\mbox{-}spacing},\mathrm{ass{:}primitive\mbox{-}compactness},\mathrm{ass{:}sampling\mbox{-}selection\mbox{-}margin},\mathrm{ass{:}reference\mbox{-}selection\mbox{-}margin},\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\mbox{-}thresholds}\text{ hold for every }n\in\mathbb N\}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}no-\allowbreak{}interference}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}stage1-\allowbreak{}complete-\allowbreak{}randomization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}stage2-\allowbreak{}complete-\allowbreak{}randomization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}random-\allowbreak{}holdout}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}positive-\allowbreak{}shares}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}fourth-\allowbreak{}moments}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}pair-\allowbreak{}spacing}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}primitive-\allowbreak{}compactness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}sampling-\allowbreak{}selection-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}reference-\allowbreak{}selection-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}thresholds}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:sequential-design]\label{def:sequential-design}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}stage selective holdout design}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\rho\in I\) and a fixed \(B_n\), construct \(Y^{(h)}\), draw the independent stratumwise holdouts, completely randomize stage one in every stratum \(s\in S\), observe \(Y^{\mathrm{obs},1}\), compute \(X_{n,s}(\rho)\), and realize \(C_n(\rho)\) and \(\mathcal F_{n,1}(\rho)\). Then recruit exactly the strata with \(C_{n,s}(\rho)=1\) and, conditional on that history, draw stage two independently by complete randomization only in those strata. This chronological construction is \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{rho}; \texttt{B}; \texttt{N}; \texttt{h}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:local-alternative-law]\label{def:local-alternative-law}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Chronological local-\allowbreak{}alternative sampling law}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each fixed \(B_n\), \(h\in H\cup\{0\}\), and \(\rho\in I\), generate outcomes from \(Y^{(h)}(0)=B\) and \(Y^{(h)}(1)=B+h_s/\sqrt n\), then execute \(\mathrm{def{:}sequential\mbox{-}design}\); the resulting probability kernel is \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{B}; \texttt{h}; \texttt{rho}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fisher-null-reference]\label{def:fisher-null-reference}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Dataset-\allowbreak{}specific Fisher sharp-\allowbreak{}null reference}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(\rho\in I\) and dataset \(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho}\) realized under \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\), form \(\widetilde Y^{0,\mathcal D(\rho)}\), enumerate \(\prod_{s\in S}\Omega_{n,1s}(\rho)\), recompute \(X'_{n,s}\) and \(C'_n(\rho)\), retain exactly \(C'_n(\rho)=C_n(\rho)=C\), and then enumerate quota-respecting stage-2 assignments only in retained strata. This gives \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\), cell probability \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\), normalized kernel \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\), cdf \(F^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(\cdot;\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})\), and critical value \(q^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{rho}; \texttt{Dobs}; \texttt{C}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:selective-test]\label{def:selective-test}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Active-\allowbreak{}pair weighted selective randomization test}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(C=C_n(\rho)\) and active \(j=(C,s)\in J\) with \(C_s=1\), reject when \(T_j(\theta_j)\) exceeds the upper \(\alpha\)-critical value under \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\), the data-dependent chronological Fisher reference supported exactly on \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{rho}; \texttt{theta}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:gaussian-experiment]\label{def:gaussian-experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}cell exchangeable square-\allowbreak{}root selected Gaussian experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each fixed label \(C\), first draw the stage-1 Gaussian blocks independently across \(t\in S\): \(X_t\sim\mathcal N(A_t\sqrt{1-\rho}h_t,1)\), and for each active \(j=(C,s)\in J\), \((X_s,R_{1j})\) has correlation \(\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho}\) and mean \((A_s\sqrt{1-\rho}h_s,b_{1j}h_s)\). Realize the signed cell from the inequalities \(X_t>c_t\). Conditional on obtaining \(C\), draw \(R_{2j}\sim\mathcal N(b_{2j}h_s,1)\) independently across active strata and independently of all stage-1 blocks. Thus, for active \(j=(C,s)\), the chronological triple has covariance \(\begin{psmallmatrix}1&\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho}&0\\\kappa_j\sqrt{1-\rho}&1&0\\0&0&1\end{psmallmatrix}\), and its selected law is restricted and normalized to \(E_C=\bigcap_{t:C_t=1}\{X_t>c_t\}\cap\bigcap_{t:C_t=0}\{X_t\le c_t\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[lem:explicit-candidate-support]\label{lem:explicit-candidate-support}
Fix \(\rho\in I\), a realized dataset \(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho}\), and \(C=C_n(\rho)\) with \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)>0\). Under \(\widetilde Y^{0,\mathcal D(\rho)}\), every history in \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\) is imputable for \(X'\), \(C'\), \(R'_1\), \(R'_2\), and \(T'\). Conversely, every quota-respecting chronological holdout--assignment history on which these statistics are imputable and whose recomputed complete signed cell equals \(C\) belongs to \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\). Therefore this explicit support is exactly the same-cell imputability support, with no additional conditioning event.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\rho\in I\), the realized dataset, and a cell \(C=C_n(\rho)\) for which the displayed reference-cell probability is positive.  The Fisher completion assigns to every observed unit one known response under both candidate treatment labels.  Consequently, for every \((H'_{n,s},z'_{n1s})\in\Omega_{n,1s}(\rho)\), every summand in the candidate complement means and variances is known.  The exceptional cases are also imputable because the definition assigns the fixed value \(c_s-1\).  Thus \(X'_{n,s}\) and \(C'_n(\rho)\) are known for every candidate action.  The same completion determines every full-block midrank and hence \(R'_{1j}\); after a retained stratum receives a quota-respecting second-stage assignment it also determines \(R'_{2j}\) and \(T'_j\).  If \(C_s=0\), chronology supplies no second-stage coordinate.

By definition, \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\) imposes precisely the following conditions, each for every member of the fixed finite set \(S\): the holdout has size \(\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor\); the first-stage assignment has quota \(m_{n1s}\); the recomputed complete signed label is \(C\); and, exactly when \(C_s=1\), the second-stage assignment has quota \(m_{n2s}\).  The preceding paragraph proves imputability on every history satisfying these conditions.

Conversely, a quota-respecting chronological history belongs to \(\prod_{s\in S}\Omega_{n,1s}(\rho)\) at stage one.  If its recomputed label is \(C\), it lies in the same-cell fiber; chronology then supplies a second-stage vector exactly for the coordinates with \(C_s=1\).  These are all the defining conditions of \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho},\rho)\).  Since \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}>0\), normalization on this finite support is defined.  There is no additional event in either direction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The support identifies every rerandomized augmented action and recomputed quantity for arbitrary fixed finite strata. \textit{Gap.} FZG Proposition 2 gives an abstract conditional reference under its hypotheses; this lemma identifies the exact combinatorial same-cell support of the present specialization. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can enumerate the reference without an unspecified fiber or hidden event.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-selective-level]\label{thm:exact-selective-level}
At the prespecified sharp-zero null \(h=0\), for every realized label \(C=C_n(\rho)\) with \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{0,\rho},\rho)>0\) and every active \(j=(C,s)\in J\), the upper-rank selective randomization \(p\)-value from \(\mathrm{def{:}selective\mbox{-}test}\) is superuniform conditional on \(E_{n,C}(\rho)\) under \(\mathsf P_n^{0,\rho}\). Because \(Y^{(0)}(1)=Y^{(0)}(0)=B\), the conditional true chronological assignment law has exactly the support \(\Omega_{n,C}(\mathcal D_n^{0,\rho},\rho)\) and equals \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\); summing its explicit product masses preserves level \(\alpha\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At \(h=0\), the potential-outcome definition and no interference give
\[
Y^{(0)}_{nksi}(1)=Y^{(0)}_{nksi}(0)=B_{nksi}
\qquad(k\in K, s\in S, i\le N_{nks}).
\]
Thus the Fisher completion is the fixed baseline array on every observed coordinate and is independent of the realized action.  By the random-holdout and stage-one complete-randomization assumptions, a candidate first-stage history has mass
\[
\prod_{s\in S}
\left\{
\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}
\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}
\right\}^{-1}.
\]
The fixed thresholds make its cell the deterministic recomputation \(C'_n(\rho)\).  Conditional on \(C'_n(\rho)=C\), the second-stage design gives the independent product of uniform quota laws exactly over \(s\) with \(C_s=1\).  The explicit-support lemma therefore shows that, for every supported history \(\omega\), Bayes' formula is
\[
\mathsf P_n^{0,\rho}\{\omega\mid E_{n,C}(\rho)\}
=\frac{1}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}(n,C,\mathcal D_n^{0,\rho},\rho)}
\prod_{s\in S}
\left\{
\binom{N_{n1s}}{\lfloor\rho N_{n1s}\rfloor}
\binom{N_{n1s}}{m_{n1s}}
\right\}^{-1}
\prod_{s:C_s=1}\binom{N_{n2s}}{m_{n2s}}^{-1}.
\]
This is exactly \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\{\omega\}\).

It remains only the finite-space randomization inequality.  If a statistic has distinct values \(t_1>\cdots>t_L\) with masses \(w_l\), its nonrandomized upper-tail value is \(p(t_l)=\sum_{a\le l}w_a\).  Hence, for \(0\le u\le1\),
\[
\Pr\{p(T)\le u\}
=\sum_{l:\,\sum_{a\le l}w_a\le u}w_l\le u.
\]
Ties can therefore only make the test conservative.  Applying this identity to the conditional law just identified proves superuniformity and conditional rejection probability at most \(\alpha\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proves finite-sample design-based validity on the same complete signed cell used throughout the power theory. \textit{Gap.} Validity specializes FZG Proposition 1, while computability of this exact reference uses their Proposition 2 only after sharp-null imputability is verified. \textit{Consumer.} The optimized design retains exact sharp-null randomization inference for any fixed finite number of strata.

\begin{lemma}[lem:full-cell-factorization]\label{lem:full-cell-factorization}
Fix a complete label \(C\) represented in \(J\), and let \(j=(C,s)\in J\), so \(C_s=1\). Under \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\), the stratumwise holdout--stage-1 histories are independent, and each history-conditional stage-2 kernel is stratum-local and independent across retained strata. Thus, for measurable \(B_0\), \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\in B_0,E_{n,C}(\rho)\}=\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\in B_0,X_{n,s}>c_s\}\prod_{t\ne s}p_{nt}^{C_t}(1-p_{nt})^{1-C_t}\), where \(p_{nt}=\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{X_{n,t}>c_t\}\), and the same identity without \(B_0\) gives the cell probability. Under \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,\mathrm{base}}\), the product combinatorial mass on \(\prod_t\Omega_{n,1t}(\rho)\) and the stratum-local map \(X'_t\) give the same signed stage-1 factorization with \(p'_{nt}=\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,\mathrm{base}}\{X'_{n,t}>c_t\}\); after restriction to \(C'_n(\rho)=C\), the stage-2 part of \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,C}\) is an independent product over retained strata. The factorization holds likewise in \(\mathrm{def{:}gaussian\mbox{-}experiment}\). Division cancels every \(t\ne s\) signed factor under the sampling, explicit reference, and Gaussian laws, yielding exactly the displayed one-dimensional \(q_{\alpha,j}\) equation and \(\Pi_j\) integral.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each \(t\in S\), let \(\mathcal H_{nt}\) be its holdout and stage-one history.  The holdout and stage-one design assumptions make \((\mathcal H_{nt})_{t\in S}\) mutually independent.  The variable \(X_{n,t}\) and its signed selection event are stratum-local measurable functions.  Conditional on the combined first-stage history, the second-stage assumption supplies independent stratum-local kernels.  Equivalently one may pre-generate those kernels and reveal only the recruited coordinates.  Thus, for \(j=(C,s)\in J\) and measurable \(B_0\), finite-product independence yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\in B_0,E_{n,C}(\rho)\}\\
&\quad=\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{(X_{n,s},R_{1j},R_{2j})\in B_0,X_{n,s}>c_s\}
\prod_{t\in S\setminus\{s\}}p_{nt}^{C_t}(1-p_{nt})^{1-C_t}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking \(B_0\) to be the whole space gives the corresponding cell probability, and division cancels every factor with \(t\ne s\) whenever that probability is positive.

Under \(\mathsf R_{n,\mathcal D(\rho)}^{0,\mathrm{base}}\), both the candidate mass and the completed selector map factor over the fixed finite set \(S\).  Hence its signed cell probability is \(\prod_{t\in S}(p'_{nt})^{C_t}(1-p'_{nt})^{1-C_t}\).  Restriction to \(C'_n=C\) divides by this product, and the subsequent second-stage law is an independent product over retained strata.  The assumed positivity of \(p_{\mathrm{ref}}\) justifies the division.  The independent Gaussian blocks give the same calculation.

For the active coordinate write
\[
X_s=a_j(\rho)h_s+Z,
\qquad
T_j=d_j(\theta_j)h_s+r_j(\rho,\theta_j)Z+v_j(\rho,\theta_j)E,
\]
where \(Z,E\) are independent standard normals.  Under the null, selection is \(Z>c_s\), so integrating over \(E\) gives the defining equation for \(q_{\alpha,j}\).  Under the local alternative, selection is \(Z>c_s-a_j(\rho)h_s\), and the same integration gives exactly \(\Pi_j\).  Thus no signed inequality belonging to another coordinate of the complete cell has been discarded.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite-product cancellation licenses the scalar Gaussian power integral without dropping any positive or negative cell inequality. \textit{Gap.} Abstract selective validity does not by itself supply the product cancellation required by the analytic power problem. \textit{Consumer.} Every cell calculation may be performed one active coordinate at a time after the complete event is retained.

\begin{lemma}[lem:derived-max-influence]\label{lem:derived-max-influence}
Write \(\bar B_{nks}=N_{nks}^{-1}\sum_iB_{nksi}\) and \(\bar U^0_{nks}=N_{nks}^{-1}\sum_iU^0_{nksi}\). Under the empirical centered fourth-moment bound, the variance margin, and positive shares, eventually and uniformly over the finite set \((k,s)\in K\times S\), \[\frac{\max_i(B_{nksi}-\bar B_{nks})^2}{\sum_i(B_{nksi}-\bar B_{nks})^2}\le\frac{\sqrt{M_4N_{nks}}}{(N_{nks}-1)\epsilon}\longrightarrow0.\] Because \(0\le U^0_{nksi}\le1\), the normalized centered midranks satisfy \[\frac{\max_i(U^0_{nksi}-\bar U^0_{nks})^2}{\sum_i(U^0_{nksi}-\bar U^0_{nks})^2}\le\frac{1}{(N_{nks}-1)\epsilon}\longrightarrow0.\] Hence both finite-population maximum-influence conditions required by the joint permutation approximation are consequences of the primitive class conditions rather than separate assumptions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block and suppress its indices.  From \(\max_i x_i^4\le\sum_i x_i^4\) and the fourth-moment condition,
\[
\max_i(B_i-\bar B)^2
\le\left\{\sum_i(B_i-\bar B)^4\right\}^{1/2}
\le\sqrt{M_4N}.
\]
The variance margin gives \(\sum_i(B_i-\bar B)^2=(N-1)S_B^2\ge(N-1)\epsilon\), proving the first claimed ratio bound.  Since \(0\le U_i^0\le1\), one has \(\max_i(U_i^0-\bar U^0)^2\le1\), while the same margin gives a denominator at least \((N-1)\epsilon\).  Positive shares imply \(N_{nks}\to\infty\) uniformly over the fixed finite set \(K\times S\).  Both displayed ratios therefore converge uniformly to zero, and every division is licensed by \(\epsilon>0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-population Lindeberg ratios needed for transfer follow quantitatively from maintained moment, variance, and share conditions. \textit{Gap.} Finite-population central limit theorems commonly state maximum-influence as a premise; this lemma records the exact implication for centered outcomes and normalized midranks in the present class. \textit{Consumer.} The selected-transfer theorem no longer relies on a redundant regularity assumption that trial populations would have to verify separately.

\begin{lemma}[lem:derived-residual-scale]\label{lem:derived-residual-scale}
For every active \(j\in J\), \(\rho\in I\), and \(\theta_j\in[0,\pi/2]\), the correlation definition and \(\lvert\kappa_j\rvert\le1\) give \[v_j(\rho,\theta_j)^2=1-\kappa_j^2(1-\rho)\cos^2(\theta_j)\ge\rho\ge\rho_0.\] This is a lower bound for the residual variance in the limiting selected Gaussian experiment; it does not assert a finite-\(n\) covariance bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the defining covariance ratio gives \(\lvert\kappa_j\rvert\le1\).  Since \(\rho\in[\rho_0,1-\rho_0]\) and \(0\le\cos^2\theta_j\le1\),
\[
v_j(\rho,\theta_j)^2
=1-\kappa_j^2(1-\rho)\cos^2\theta_j
\ge1-(1-\rho)=\rho\ge\rho_0>0.
\]
This calculation concerns only the limiting Gaussian covariance and asserts no finite-sample covariance inequality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Compact holdout support itself prevents degeneration of the Gaussian conditional residual scale. \textit{Gap.} The residual-scale margin is an algebraic consequence of the present square-root path and therefore should not be imposed as an independent positive-definiteness condition. \textit{Consumer.} All Gaussian quantile, branch, and certification bounds can use the declared design constant \(\rho_0\) directly.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-selected-transfer]\label{thm:uniform-selected-transfer}
For every fixed admissible baseline array \(B=(B_n)_n\), there is an array-dependent deterministic sequence \(\eta_n(B)\downarrow0\) with two separate conclusions. First, the sampling distance \(d_n^{\mathrm{samp}}(B)\), formed from the family of separately indexed laws \(\{\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}:\rho\in I,h\in H\}\), satisfies \(d_n^{\mathrm{samp}}(B)\le\eta_n(B)\); hence the sampling cdf conditional on \(E_{n,C}(\rho)\) converges to \(G_j^h(\cdot;\rho,\theta_j)\) uniformly over \(\rho\in I\), \(\theta\in[0,\pi/2]^J\), and \(h\in H\). Second, each \(\rho\)-indexed dataset \(\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho}\sim\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\) generates its own completion, kernel, cdf \(F^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(\cdot;\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})\), critical value \(q^{\mathrm{ref}}_{n,j}(\rho,\theta_j,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})\), and distance \(d_n^{\mathrm{ref}}(B,\rho,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})\), and \(\sup_{\rho\in I,\,h\in H}\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\{d_n^{\mathrm{ref}}(B,\rho,\mathcal D_n^{h,\rho})>\eta_n(B)\}\to0\). Thus the random Fisher-reference cdf and critical value converge in their corresponding sampling probabilities to \(G_j^0(\cdot;\rho,\theta_j)\) and \(q_{\alpha,j}(\rho,\theta_j)\), uniformly in the displayed outer supremum. The permutation approximation uses the maximum-influence bounds derived in \(\mathrm{lem{:}derived\mbox{-}max\mbox{-}influence}\), while selected Gaussian ratio and anti-concentration bounds use the common lower margin \(\min\{\epsilon,\rho_0\}\) from the selection or variance premises and \(\mathrm{lem{:}derived\mbox{-}residual\mbox{-}scale}\). After \(\mathrm{lem{:}full\mbox{-}cell\mbox{-}factorization}\), the rejection probability under each \(\mathsf P_n^{h,\rho}\) converges uniformly to \(\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,h_s)\). No deterministic rate is asserted uniformly over all baseline arrays in \(\mathcal M\), and no finite-\(n\) covariance bound is inferred from the limiting \(\rho_0\) inequality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix one admissible baseline array \(B=(B_n)_n\).  All uniformities below are over the displayed compact parameter sets for this array; there is no supremum over different arrays.

For a stage-one block write \(N=N_{n1s}\), \(m=m_{n1s}\), \(\delta=h_s/\sqrt n\), and let \(G\) be the random complement.  The hypergeometric mean and variance formulas, the bounds \(\rho_0\le\rho\le1-\rho_0\), and balanced quotas give, uniformly in \(\rho\),
\[
\frac{g^1}{N}\xrightarrow{\mathsf P}\frac{1-\rho}{2},
\qquad
\frac{g^0}{N}\xrightarrow{\mathsf P}\frac{1-\rho}{2}.
\]
For \(f_i\) equal to \(B_i-\bar B\), \((B_i-\bar B)^2\), or \((B_i-\bar B)(U_i^0-\bar U^0)\), the simple-random-sampling variance identity and Cauchy--Schwarz with the fourth-moment bound imply
\[
|G|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G}f_i-N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Nf_i=o_{\mathsf P}(1)
\]
uniformly over the admissible complement sizes.  Conditioning on the treatment quota and applying the same identity within \(G\) gives the corresponding statement for either arm; the preceding count limit bounds the random denominators away from zero with probability tending to one.  Hence both arm variances converge to their finite-population limits, the exceptional totalization event vanishes, and direct studentization yields
\[
X_{n,s}=A_s\sqrt{1-\rho}\,h_s+X^0_{n,s}+o_{\mathsf P}(1),
\]
where \(X^0_{n,s}\) is the standardized complement contrast of the fixed baselines.

For ranks, let \(K_N(\delta)\) be the ordered-pair crossing count.  Complete-randomization inclusion probabilities give
\[
\mathsf E(\text{treated crossed-pair count})
=\frac{m(N-m)}{N(N-1)}K_N(\delta).
\]
Writing the variance as a double sum of ordered edges, pairs sharing a vertex contribute at most a constant times \(NK_N(\delta)\), whereas disjoint pairs have covariance of order \(N^{-1}\) and contribute at most a constant times \(K_N(\delta)^2/N\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\text{crossed-pair count})
\le C\{NK_N(\delta)+K_N(\delta)^2/N\}.
\]
The uniform pair-spacing condition gives \(K_N(\delta)=O(N^{3/2})\).  After division by the null rank-sum standard deviation, which is of order \(N^{3/2}\), the centered crossing term is \(o_{\mathsf P}(1)\), while its expectation converges to the stated drift.  If \(d_i\) is the number of crossings incident to unit \(i\), then
\[
\frac1N\sum_i(d_i/N)^2\le\frac{2K_N(\delta)}{N^2}=O(N^{-1/2}),
\]
so the observed ranks and baseline ranks have the same limiting variance and covariance.  The pair-spacing premise also makes the squared empirical tie masses vanish, whence the normalized-midrank variance tends to \(1/12\).  Consequently
\[

\begin{theorem}[thm:selected-quantile-convexity]\label{thm:selected-quantile-convexity}
For every \(0<\beta<1\) and \(0\le r<1\), the map \(x\mapsto q_{\beta}(r,x)\) is convex on \(\mathbb R\). Moreover, for every \(c_0,c_1\in\mathbb R\) and \(0<t<1\), \(\operatorname{Eq}_{\beta,r}(c_0,c_1;t)\) holds if and only if \(r=0\) or \(c_0=c_1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\gamma_2\) be standard Gaussian measure on \(\mathbb R^2\) and define
\[
\mathcal T(c,z)=Q^{-1}\{Q(c)Q(z)\}.
\]
Minkowski interpolation of the orthants \([c,\infty)\times[z,\infty)\) interpolates their thresholds.  The cited Ehrhard--Borell inequality and \(Q^{-1}(u)=-\Phi^{-1}(u)\) therefore show that \(\mathcal T\) is jointly convex.  Moreover, for standard normal \(Z\),
\[
\Pr\{\mathcal T(c,Z)>y\}=\frac{Q(y)}{Q(c)},\qquad y>c,
\]
so \(\mathcal T(c,Z)\) has the law of a standard normal conditioned to exceed \(c\).

Put \(v=(1-r^2)^{1/2}>0\), \(W(c,z,e)=r\mathcal T(c,z)+ve\), and
\[
\mathcal A(c,q)=\{(z,e):W(c,z,e)\le q\}.
\]
For \(r\ge0\), convexity gives
\[
(1-t)\mathcal A(c_0,q_0)+t\mathcal A(c_1,q_1)
\subseteq\mathcal A((1-t)c_0+tc_1,(1-t)q_0+tq_1).
\]
Taking \(q_i=q_\beta(r,c_i)\), both left sets have Gaussian measure \(1-\beta\).  Ehrhard--Borell, the inclusion, and uniqueness of the quantile (supplied by the independent \(vE\) term) imply
\[
q_\beta(r,(1-t)c_0+tc_1)
\le(1-t)q_\beta(r,c_0)+tq_\beta(r,c_1).
\]

If \(r=0\), this quantile is constant; if \(c_0=c_1\), chord equality is tautological.  Conversely suppose \(r>0\), \(c_0\ne c_1\), and equality holds.  Equality must then hold in the Gaussian-set comparison.  By the cited equality classification, the two closed convex sets \(\mathcal A(c_i,q_i)\) are parallel half-spaces or equal up to null sets.  Their boundaries
\[
e=\{q_i-r\mathcal T(c_i,z)\}/v
\]
are not affine because \(\mathcal T(c,z)\to c\) as \(z\to-\infty\) and tends to infinity as \(z\to\infty\).  Equality up to null sets between closed convex sets with nonempty interiors is equality, so \(\mathcal T(c_0,z)-\mathcal T(c_1,z)\) would be constant.  Its limit at \(-\infty\) is \(c_0-c_1\).  Integration by parts gives the needed Mills bounds: \(Q(x)<x^{-1}\int_x^\infty t\phi(t)\,dt=\phi(x)/x\), while \(Q(x)=\phi(x)/x-\int_x^\infty\phi(t)t^{-2}\,dt>\phi(x)/x-Q(x)/x^2\).  Hence
\[
\frac{\phi(x)}{x+x^{-1}}<Q(x)<\frac{\phi(x)}x\qquad(x>0)
\]
make the ratio of the two tails tend to zero or infinity as \(z\to\infty\), whereas the defining identity makes it the finite constant \(Q(c_0)/Q(c_1)\).  Thus the constant is zero and \(c_0=c_1\), a contradiction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Convexity supplies the global single-crossing fact needed to optimize over arbitrary compact alternatives. \textit{Gap.} Shenfeld and van Handel prove Gaussian-measure convexity and equality cases but do not derive convexity of conditional selected-score quantiles. \textit{Consumer.} Power calculations need only the least clinically relevant effect coordinate, even when the prespecified alternative set is nonconvex.

\begin{theorem}[thm:least-coordinate-reduction]\label{thm:least-coordinate-reduction}
For each active \(j=(C,s)\in J\), each fixed \( (\rho,\theta_j)\), and every \(u>\alpha\), \(\min_{h\in H}\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,h_s)\ge u\) if and only if \(\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,\ell_s)\ge u\), where \(\Pi_j\) is conditional on the complete signed event \(E_{n,C}(\rho)\). Hence the maximin value and every globally optimal design are unchanged when \(H\) is replaced by its least-coordinate vector \(\ell\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(j=(C,s)\), \(\rho\), and \(\theta_j\), and abbreviate \(a=a_j(\rho)>0\), \(d=d_j(\theta_j)>0\), and \(r=r_j(\rho,\theta_j)\).  For \(u\in(\alpha,1)\), the selected-quantile definition gives
\[
\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,h_s)\ge u
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_u(h_s):=dh_s+q_u(r,c_s-ah_s)-q_\alpha(r,c_s)\ge0.
\]
The selected-quantile theorem makes \(B_u\) continuous and convex.  Since \(u>\alpha\), \(B_u(0)<0\).  As \(h_s\to\infty\), the selected threshold tends to \(-\infty\), its selected quantile tends to the ordinary Gaussian quantile, and \(dh_s\to\infty\); hence \(B_u(h_s)\to\infty\).  A convex function negative at zero has at most one positive transition from negative to nonnegative: at a zero \(x>0\), every right secant slope is at least \(\{B_u(x)-B_u(0)\}/x>0\).  Thus its nonnegative set on \((0,\infty)\) is an upper interval.

Compactness of \(H\) attains \(\ell_s=\min_{h\in H}h_s>0\), and every other admissible coordinate is at least \(\ell_s\).  The upper-interval property proves the stated equivalence.  The feasible pure-stage-two weight \(\theta_j=\pi/2\) has \(r=0\) and power
\[
Q\{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-b_{2j}h_s\}>\alpha.
\]
Therefore all optimized cell values and the global optimized value exceed \(\alpha\).  Equality of all superlevel predicates above \(\alpha\) implies equality of both optimized values and complete argmax sets.  This argument is coordinatewise and uses only finiteness of \(J\), not a two-coordinate property.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This eliminates an infinite alternative-set optimization without imposing coordinatewise monotonicity below size. \textit{Gap.} No inspected enrichment or selective-inference result gives an exact above-size least-coordinate reduction for arbitrary compact positive alternatives. \textit{Consumer.} Designers can specify a clinically meaningful compact uncertainty set without introducing a numerical inner minimization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:weight-side]\label{thm:weight-side}
Let \(\theta_{0j}=\arctan(b_{2j}/b_{1j})\). For the full-cell conditional power \(\Pi_j\), every maximizer of \(\theta_j\mapsto\Pi_j(\rho,\theta_j,\ell_s)\) lies in \( (\theta_{0j},\pi/2]\) when \(\Delta_j>0\), equals \(\theta_{0j}\) uniquely when \(\Delta_j=0\), and lies in \( (0,\theta_{0j})\) when \(\Delta_j<0\). These are exact equality strata for every active \(j=(C,s)\in J\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an active pair and suppress its index.  Put \(t=\sqrt{1-\rho}\), \(a=At\), \(\gamma=\kappa t\), \(d=b_1\cos\theta+b_2\sin\theta\), \(r=\gamma\cos\theta\), and \(v=\sqrt{1-r^2}\).  For \(h=\ell_s>0\), set
\[
L=c-ah,\quad K=q_\alpha(r,c)-dh,\quad
u_0=\frac{c-rq_\alpha(r,c)}v,\quad
u_h=\frac{L-rK}v,
\quad D_h=\frac{\phi(K)Q(u_h)}{Q(L)}>0.
\]
Differentiation of the Gaussian tail integral and its null critical equation gives
\[
q_r=\frac{\mu(u_0)}v,\qquad P_d=hD_h,qquad
P_r=\frac{D_h}{v}\{\mu(u_h)-\mu(u_0)\},
\]
where \(\mu(x)=\phi(x)/Q(x)\).  Every division is valid because \(v^2\ge\rho_0\) and Gaussian tails are positive.  The identity \(\mu'(x)=\mu(x)\{\mu(x)-x\}\) gives \(\mu'(x)>0\), because \(\mu(x)=\mathsf E(Z\mid Z>x)>x\).  Integration by parts also gives \(\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid Z>x)=1-\mu'(x)>0\), and hence \(\mu'(x)<1\).  Therefore the secant
\[
\bar s=\frac{\mu(u_0)-\mu(u_h)}{u_0-u_h}
\]
(with \(\mu'(u_0)\) at equality) lies in \((0,1)\), while \(u_0-u_h=h(a-rd)/v\).

With \(d'=b_2\cos\theta-b_1\sin\theta\) and \(r'=-\gamma\sin\theta\), the chain rule yields
\[
\frac{P_\theta}{hD_h}
=d'+\frac{\gamma\sin\theta\,(a-rd)\bar s}{v^2}.
\]
Using \(\cos\theta\,d=b_1+\sin\theta\,d'\) and \(\Delta=A-\kappa b_1\), this becomes
\[
\frac{P_\theta}{hD_h}
=\mathcal Ld'+\frac{\kappa t^2\sin\theta\,\Delta\bar s}{v^2},
\qquad
\mathcal L=1-\frac{\gamma^2\sin^2\theta\,\bar s}{v^2}>0,
\]
because \(v^2-\gamma^2\sin^2\theta\bar s\ge1-\gamma^2\ge\rho>0\).  Now \(d'\) is positive below \(\theta_0=\arctan(b_2/b_1)\), zero there, and negative above.  If \(\Delta>0\), the derivative is positive throughout \([0,\theta_0]\); if \(\Delta<0\), it is negative throughout \([\theta_0,\pi/2]\), while \(P_\theta(0)=hb_2D_h>0\); and if \(\Delta=0\), its sign is exactly that of \(d'\).  Compactness supplies maximizers and proves all three claimed strata.  The least-coordinate theorem transfers the result to the original compact alternative set.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The discrepancy index converts a two-dimensional weight search into a signed one-sided problem and identifies the proportional equality case. \textit{Gap.} Freidling, Zhao, and Gao suggest adaptive weighting, while Chen and Andrews study leftover Gaussian information; neither derives this primitive sign rule for enrichment holdouts. \textit{Consumer.} A trial designer obtains a falsifiable rule for whether stage 1 must receive more or less weight than the proportional-information benchmark.

\begin{lemma}[lem:realizability-bound]\label{lem:realizability-bound}
Every jointly realizable continuous-rank primitive vector in \(\mathcal{M}\) satisfies \(A_s\kappa_j/b_{1j}\le5\sqrt{15}/18\), uniformly over active \(j=(C,s)\in J\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a jointly realizable active stage-one block, center its empirical baseline law, and let its limiting variance and pair slope be \(\sigma^2>0\) and \(\lambda>0\).  Convolve the empirical law with the uniform density on an interval of width \(\delta_N=u_\star/\sqrt N\), where \([0,u_\star]\) is inside the pair-spacing window.  For the resulting density \(f_N\), the ordered-pair convention gives the exact overlap identity
\[
\|f_N\|_2^2
=\frac{2}{N^2\delta_N^2}\int_0^{\delta_N}K_N(u)\,du
=\frac{2}{u_\star^2}\int_0^{u_\star}N^{-3/2}K_N(x/\sqrt N)\,dx
\longrightarrow\lambda.
\]
Its variance is \(\sigma_N^2+\delta_N^2/12\), which tends to \(\sigma^2\) by the variance and share premises.

For any centered density \(f\) of variance \(\sigma_f^2>0\), take \(a=\sqrt5\sigma_f\) and \(g(x)=3(1-x^2/a^2)_+/(4a)\).  Since \((1-x^2/a^2)_+\ge1-x^2/a^2\),
\[
\int fg\ge\frac{3}{5a},
\qquad
\int g^2=\frac{3}{5a}.
\]
The inequality \(\int(f-g)^2\ge0\) gives \(\|f\|_2^2\sigma_f\ge3/(5\sqrt5)\).  Applying it to \(f_N\) gives \(\lambda\sigma\ge3/(5\sqrt5)\).

Pair spacing removes asymptotic atoms, so the normalized midrank variance tends to \(1/12\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(B,U^0)\le\sigma/\sqrt{12}\).  Joint realizability and the definitions imply
\[
A=\frac{\sqrt{r_1}}{2\sigma},\qquad
b_1=\sqrt3\lambda\sqrt{r_1},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{12}\operatorname{Cov}(B,U^0)}\sigma.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{A\kappa}{b_1}
=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(B,U^0)}{\sigma^2\lambda}
\le\frac{1}{\sqrt{12}\sigma\lambda}
\le\frac{5\sqrt{15}}{18}.
\]
All denominators are positive by the primitive variance and pair-spacing conditions, and the argument is cellwise, hence uniform over finite \(J\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This rules out a terminal pure-stage-2 plateau by linking Gaussian coordinates back to feasible finite populations. \textit{Gap.} The bound combines pair-spacing density, variance, and rank covariance and is not present in the Gaussian optimization literature. \textit{Consumer.} Endpoint optimality is certified over actual finite-population limits rather than an enlarged, potentially impossible Gaussian parameter box.

\begin{theorem}[thm:global-endpoint]\label{thm:global-endpoint}
If \(\Delta_j\ge0\) for every active \(j=(C,s)\in J\), then \(\rho^{\star}=1-\rho_0\) is the unique maximizing holdout fraction for the full-cell conditional-power objective. At that fraction every binding cell has an optimal weight in \([\theta_{0j},\pi/2)\), with equality to \(\theta_{0j}\) exactly when \(\Delta_j=0\); slack-cell weights are the full superlevel sets required by \(\mathcal{S}^{\star}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(j\) with \(\Delta_j\ge0\) and write \(P(\rho,\theta)=\Pi_j(\rho,\theta,\ell_s)\).  In the notation of the weight-side proof, differentiation gives, for \(r>0\),
\[
P_a=h\mu(L)\left[Q\!\left(\frac{q-dh-rL}{v}\right)-P\right]<0,
\qquad
\operatorname{sign}(P_r)=\operatorname{sign}(rd-a).
\]
The strict inequality holds because conditional rejection probability increases strictly with the selected Gaussian coordinate, so its boundary value is below its selected-tail average.  Every individually optimal angle obeys \(\theta\ge\theta_0\), and on this interval \(\cos\theta\,d\le b_1\).  Hence
\[
a-rd=t\{A-\kappa\cos\theta\,d\}\ge t\Delta\ge0,
\]
so \(P_r\le0\).  Since \(a_\rho=-A/(2t)\) and \(r_\rho=-\kappa\cos\theta/(2t)\),
\[
P_\rho=-\frac{AP_a+\kappa\cos\theta P_r}{2t}>0
\]
at every optimal non-pure angle.  At \(\theta=\pi/2\), power is the constant \(p_2=Q\{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-b_2\ell_s\}\).

At \(\rho=1-\rho_0\), the inward angular derivative from \(\pi/2\) is
\[
\phi\{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)-b_2h\}
\left[b_1h-\kappa t\{\mu(c)-\mu(c-Ath)\}\right].
\]
Because \(0<\mu'<1\), its bracket exceeds \(h(b_1-\kappa At^2)\).  The realizability bound and \(t^2=\rho_0<1/2\) yield
\[
\frac{\kappa At^2}{b_1}
\le\rho_0\frac{5\sqrt{15}}{18}<1,
\]
so the pure-stage-two value is strictly improvable at the endpoint.

Thus \(M_j(\rho)\) is strictly smaller at every \(\rho<1-\rho_0\): if its optimizer is pure stage two, its value is \(p_2\), below the endpoint value; otherwise its fixed-angle power strictly increases toward the endpoint.  Choose a cell binding in the finite minimum at the endpoint.  Its strict inequality implies
\[

\]
proving uniqueness of the holdout fraction.  The weight-side theorem gives the binding-cell angle location and equality stratum, while the definition of \(\mathcal S^\star\) gives the stated superlevel sets for slack cells.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A primitive sign certificate gives the unique largest-holdout decision for any finite collection of active cells. \textit{Gap.} No comparator derives this finite-population-realizable endpoint certificate. \textit{Consumer.} A design with all nonnegative discrepancies avoids global numerical holdout search.

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditioning-ratio-transfer]\label{lem:conditioning-ratio-transfer}
Let \(P_n\) and \(P\) be probability laws, let \(E_n,E\) be events, and let \(A_n(x)\subseteq E_n\) and \(A(x)\subseteq E\) be nested events indexed by \(x\in\mathbb R\). If \(P(E)\ge m>\delta\), \(\lvert P_n(E_n)-P(E)\rvert\le\delta\), and \(\sup_x\lvert P_n(A_n(x))-P(A(x))\rvert\le\delta\), then
\[
\sup_x\left\lvert P_n\{A_n(x)\mid E_n\}-P\{A(x)\mid E\}\right\rvert\le \frac{2\delta}{m-\delta}.
\]
If the conditional limit cdf has density at least \(g>0\) on a radius-\(a\) neighborhood of its \(p\)-quantile and the left side is at most \(e<ag\), then the generalized \(p\)-quantile differs from the limiting quantile by at most \(e/g\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(p_n=P_n(E_n)\), \(p=P(E)\), \(a_n(x)=P_n(A_n(x))\), and \(a(x)=P(A(x))\).  Then \(p_n\ge m-\delta>0\) and \(0\le a(x)\le p\).  Therefore
\[
\left|\frac{a_n(x)}{p_n}-\frac{a(x)}p\right|
\le\frac{|a_n(x)-a(x)|}{p_n}
+\frac{a(x)|p_n-p|}{p_np}
\le\frac{2\delta}{m-\delta}.
\]
Taking the supremum proves the cdf bound.  If \(q\) is the limiting \(p\)-quantile, the density condition gives \(F(q-t)\le p-gt\) and \(F(q+t)\ge p+gt\) for \(0<t<a\).  Whenever \(t>e/g\), uniform cdf error yields \(F_n(q-t)<p<F_n(q+t)\), so the generalized quantile satisfies \(q-t<q_n\le q+t\).  Letting \(t\downarrow e/g\) proves the quantile bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Honest scope}

The strata may form any fixed finite nonempty set, but may not grow with \(n\). The common scalar holdout is chosen from the fixed compact interval \(I\), and only active pairs in finite \(J\) are tested. Finite-sample exactness is confined to the prespecified Fisher sharp-zero null. Positive local alternatives use a shifted sampling law, while each realized dataset creates a separate zero-drift Fisher reference. The FZG relationship is a specialization of their augmented-action representation, not class equivalence; their results do not supply this note's Gaussian transfer or optimizer. Rosenblum--Fang--Liu is nonnested and transfers no optimality claim. The explicit \(W_{\mathrm{mix}}\) witness sets \(S=\{1,2\}\) only for that counterexample. General outputs remain set-valued unless an explicit separation certificate proves a unique design.
