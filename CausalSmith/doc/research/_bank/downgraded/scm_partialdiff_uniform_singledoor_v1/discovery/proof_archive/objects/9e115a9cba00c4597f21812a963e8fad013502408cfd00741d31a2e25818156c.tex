Because \(\Gamma(s_0)=\{\tau_0\}\), the accessible branch set \(\mathbb B(s_0)\) is nonempty; it is finite because \(\mathbb B_p\) is finite.  For \(b\in\mathbb B(s_0)\), define
\[
 L_{b,R}=T_b(s_0)\cap\{h\in\mathbb R^d:\|h\|_2\le R\}.
\]
Because \(b\in\mathbb B(s_0)\), the point \(s_0\) belongs to \(\overline{\mathcal S_b}\); hence the zero direction belongs to \(T_b(s_0)\), so \(L_{b,R}\) is nonempty.  The tangent cone \(T_b(s_0)\) is closed by definition, and the closed radius-\(R\) Euclidean ball is compact because \(R<\infty\).  Hence \(L_{b,R}\) is compact.  The map \(\eta_b(h)=D\theta_b(s_0)h\) is linear and therefore continuous.  By the definition of \(\Lambda_R\),
\[
 K_R=\bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}\eta_b(L_{b,R}).
\]
This is a finite union of compact subsets of \(\mathbb R^3\), and thus is compact.  In particular,
\[
 D_R:=\operatorname{diam}(K_R)<\infty.
\tag{1}
\]

We first justify the reduction to the compact action space \(\mathcal H_R\).  For a closed set \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), put
\[
 T(C)=C\cap K_R.
\tag{2}
\]
The map \(T\) is measurable from the Effros sigma-field on closed subsets of \(\mathbb R^3\) to the Borel sigma-field of \(\mathcal H_R\).  Indeed, let \(O=K_R\cap G\), where \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) is open, and define
\[
 A_m=\{x\in K_R:\operatorname{dist}(x,G^c)\ge m^{-1}\},\qquad m\ge1.
\]
With \(A_1=K_R\) when \(G^c=\varnothing\), the sets \(A_m\) are compact and \(O=\bigcup_{m\ge1}A_m\).  For every compact \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), closedness of \(C\) and compactness of \(A\) imply
\[
 \{C:C\cap A\ne\varnothing\}
 =\bigcap_{\ell\ge1}\{C:C\cap A^{1/\ell}\ne\varnothing\},
\tag{3}
\]
where \(A^{1/\ell}\) is the open \(1/\ell\)-neighborhood of \(A\).  Thus compact-hit events are Effros measurable.  Consequently
\[
 \{C:T(C)\cap O\ne\varnothing\}
 =\bigcup_{m\ge1}\{C:C\cap A_m\ne\varnothing\}
\tag{4}
\]
is measurable, and, for open \(O\subseteq K_R\),
\[
 \{C:T(C)\subseteq O\}
 =\{C:C\cap(K_R\setminus O)=\varnothing\}
\tag{5}
\]
is measurable because \(K_R\setminus O\) is compact.  We now verify directly that these events suffice for the Hausdorff Borel sigma-field.  Fix a nonempty \(A\in\mathcal H_R\), \(r>0\), and a countable dense sequence \((a_i)_{i\ge1}\) in \(A\).  For every nonempty closed \(D\subseteq K_R\),
\[
 d_H(D,A)<r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D\subseteq A^r
 \ \text{and}\ 
 \sup_{i\ge1}\operatorname{dist}(a_i,D)<r,
\tag{5a}
\]
because distance to a nonempty closed set is continuous and the supremum over compact \(A\) equals the supremum over its dense sequence.  Moreover,
\[
 \left\{C:\sup_{i\ge1}\operatorname{dist}(a_i,T(C))<r\right\}
 =\bigcup_{\substack{q\in\mathbb Q\\0<q<r}}
   \bigcap_{i\ge1}
   \left\{C:C\cap K_R\cap\overline B(a_i,q)\ne\varnothing\right\}.
\tag{5b}
\]
Every hit set on the right of (5b) is measurable by (3), while the containment condition in (5a) is measurable by (5).  Thus the preimage under \(T\) of every nonempty Hausdorff open ball is measurable.  The preimage of the isolated empty member of \(\mathcal H_R\) is \(\{C:C\cap K_R=\varnothing\}\), which is measurable by (3).  Hausdorff open balls and that isolated singleton generate the Borel sigma-field of \(\mathcal H_R\), proving measurability of \(T\).

For every \((b,h)\in\Lambda_R\), the target \(\eta_b(h)\) belongs to \(K_R\).  Therefore, pointwise in every closed \(C\),
\[
 {\bf1}\{\eta_b(h)\in T(C)\}={\bf1}\{\eta_b(h)\in C\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{diam}(T(C))\le\operatorname{diam}(C).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus truncation preserves every coverage probability and cannot increase any expected-diameter risk.  The unrestricted infimum \(v_R\) therefore equals the infimum over \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rules: one inequality follows because those rules are a subclass, and the reverse inequality follows by applying \(T\) to every unrestricted rule.  By \(\text{lem:hyperspace-compactness}\), \(\mathcal H_R\) is compact and \(C\mapsto\operatorname{diam}(C)\) is continuous on it.

We next obtain a common dominating law.  The matrix \(V_0\) is positive definite.  To verify this from its displayed covariance formula, let \(c\ne0\) be a vector in half-vector coordinates and let \(A_c\ne0\) be the corresponding symmetric coefficient matrix, with its off-diagonal entries normalized so that contraction with a symmetric matrix agrees with contraction by \(c\).  Expanding
\[
 V(\Sigma)_{AB,CD}=\Sigma_{AC}\Sigma_{BD}+\Sigma_{AD}\Sigma_{BC}
\]
gives
\[
 c^{\mathsf T}V(\Sigma)c
 =2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(\Sigma^{1/2}A_c\Sigma^{1/2})^2\right\}>0
\tag{7}
\]
for every positive-definite \(\Sigma\): the symmetric matrix \(\Sigma^{1/2}A_c\Sigma^{1/2}\) is nonzero, so the trace of its square is strictly positive.  The two covariance matrices encoded by \(s_0\in\Omega_p\) are positive definite, and \(\lambda\in(0,1)\).  Hence the block-diagonal definition of \(V_0\), together with (7), proves \(V_0\succ0\).

Let \(\varphi_0\) be the \(\mathcal N(0,V_0)\) density and put \(\mu_0=\mathcal N(0,V_0)\).  For every \(h\in\mathbb R^d\), completing the square in the two nonsingular Gaussian densities gives the continuous, strictly positive Radon--Nikodym derivative
\[
 w_h(y):=\frac{\varphi_h(y)}{\varphi_0(y)}
 =\exp\!\left\{h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}y
 -\frac12h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}h\right\},
 \qquad
 \int w_h\,d\mu_0=1.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(\delta\) be an \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued randomized rule.  Its joint measurability and the independence and uniformity of \(U\) induce the probability kernel
\[
 Q(A\mid y)=\int_0^1{\bf1}\{\delta(y,u)\in A\}\,du,
 \qquad A\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H_R).
\tag{9}
\]
Conversely, \(\text{lem:kernel-uniform-randomization}\) realizes every probability kernel from \(\mathbb R^d\) to the standard Borel space \(\mathcal H_R\) as a jointly measurable function of \((y,U)\).  Since \(w_h\varphi_0=\varphi_h\), change of measure shows that, for each \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\), the coverage and risk of \(Q\) are
\[
 G_k(Q)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathcal H_R}
 {\bf1}\{\eta_b(h)\in C\}\,Q(dC\mid y)\varphi_h(y)\,dy
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 L_k(Q)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathcal H_R}
 \operatorname{diam}(C)\,Q(dC\mid y)\varphi_h(y)\,dy.
\tag{11}
\]
It follows from (9)--(11) that the feasible kernels are exactly those satisfying \(G_k(Q)\ge1-\gamma\) for every \(k\in\Lambda_R\), and the least feasible epigraph coordinate for a fixed kernel is \(\sup_{k\in\Lambda_R}L_k(Q)\).  This proves equality between the decision problem and the displayed semi-infinite linear program, apart from attainment.  The constant rule \(C=K_R\) has coverage one and, by (1), risk \(D_R\).  Therefore
\[
 0\le v_R\le D_R<\infty.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to prove attainment.  For every integer \(j\ge1\), the definition of the infimum and (12) permit a feasible kernel \(Q_j\) such that
\[
 \sup_{k\in\Lambda_R}L_k(Q_j)\le v_R+j^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]
Define a probability measure on \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R\) by
\[
 \zeta_j(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q_j(dC\mid y).
\tag{14}
\]
Every \(\zeta_j\) has first marginal \(\mu_0\), and its second coordinate belongs to compact \(\mathcal H_R\).  Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose a compact Euclidean ball \(L_\epsilon\) with \(\mu_0(L_\epsilon)>1-\epsilon\).  Then
\[
 \zeta_j(L_\epsilon\times\mathcal H_R)>1-\epsilon
\tag{15}
\]
for every \(j\), so \((\zeta_j)_{j\ge1}\) is tight.  The product \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R\) is a separable complete metric space.  By \(\text{lem:prokhorov-tightness}\), a subsequence, not relabeled, converges weakly to a probability measure \(\zeta\).

For every bounded continuous \(g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\), weak convergence and the common first marginal give
\[
 \int g(y)\,\zeta(dy,dC)
 =\lim_{j\to\infty}\int g(y)\,\zeta_j(dy,dC)
 =\int g(y)\,\mu_0(dy).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the first marginal of \(\zeta\) is \(\mu_0\).  Both factors are standard Borel spaces, so \(\text{lem:standard-borel-disintegration}\) supplies a probability kernel \(Q\) satisfying
\[
 \zeta(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q(dC\mid y).
\tag{17}
\]

Fix \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\) and define
\[
 F_k=\{(y,C)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R:\eta_b(h)\in C\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The set \(F_k\) is closed because membership of a fixed point is preserved under Hausdorff convergence and the empty member is isolated.  For \(L>0\), let \(w_h^L=w_h\wedge L\).  Since \(w_h^L\) is nonnegative and continuous and \({\bf1}_{F_k}\) is upper semicontinuous, \(w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\) is bounded and upper semicontinuous.  Feasibility of \(Q_j\), (8), (10), and the common first marginal in (14) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j
 &=\int w_h{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j
   -\int(w_h-w_h^L){\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j\\
 &\ge1-\gamma-\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The upper-semicontinuous Portmanteau inequality \(\text{lem:portmanteau-usc}\) therefore yields
\[
 \int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta
 \ge\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j
 \ge1-\gamma-\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0.
\tag{20}
\]
By (8),
\[
 0\le\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0
 \le\int_{\{w_h>L\}}w_h\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]
Monotone convergence as \(L\to\infty\), followed by (17) and (20)--(21), proves
\[
 G_k(Q)=\int w_h{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta\ge1-\gamma.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(k\) was arbitrary, \(Q\) satisfies every coverage constraint.

For risk, the function
\[
 (y,C)\longmapsto w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)
\]
is bounded and continuous by \(\text{lem:hyperspace-compactness}\).  Weak convergence therefore implies
\[
 \int w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta
 =\lim_{j\to\infty}
 \int w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta_j.
\tag{23}
\]
Because \(\zeta_j\) and \(\zeta\) all have first marginal \(\mu_0\), while \(0\le\operatorname{diam}(C)\le D_R\) on \(\mathcal H_R\), replacing \(w_h^L\) by \(w_h\) in any integral in (23) changes it by at most
\[
 D_R\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0,
\tag{24}
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  Equations (13), (23), and (24) consequently imply
\[
 L_k(Q)=\lim_{j\to\infty}L_k(Q_j)\le v_R.
\tag{25}
\]
This holds for every \(k\in\Lambda_R\), so \(\sup_kL_k(Q)\le v_R\).  The kernel \(Q\) is feasible by (22), and \(v_R\) is the infimum over feasible rules.  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{k\in\Lambda_R}L_k(Q)=v_R.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (10)--(13) and (26) prove attainment and the exact minimum characterization in the displayed semi-infinite linear program.

By \(\text{lem:kernel-uniform-randomization}\), every minimizing kernel has a measurable realization using \(U\), and that realization is an attaining \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rule.  Conversely, the kernel induced through (9) by any attaining \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rule satisfies the program constraints and has epigraph value \(v_R\), so it is minimizing.  The likelihood ratios in (8) are everywhere strictly positive and finite.  Hence all Gaussian-shift laws have the same null sets as \(\mu_0\), proving the stated qualification up to null sets.  If an unrestricted rule attains \(v_R\), its measurable truncation \(T\circ\delta\) remains feasible by (6) and has risk at most \(v_R\).  The definition of the infimum forces equality, and the kernel induced by this truncation is therefore minimizing.

Finally, the local characterization cannot by itself imply an all-sample finite-sample comparison.  Let \((\Delta_N)_{N\ge1}\) be any proposed sequence of finite-sample pullbacks and fix one sample size \(N_0\).  Replacing only \(\Delta_{N_0}\) by the constantly empty set leaves \(\Delta_N\) unchanged for every \(N>N_0\), and therefore leaves every local asymptotic limit unchanged, but its coverage at \(N_0\) is zero.  Alternatively, replacing only \(\Delta_{N_0}\) by the constant set \(\mathbb R^3\) again leaves every local asymptotic limit unchanged, while its diameter at \(N_0\) is \(+\infty\).  Thus this local minimax characterization establishes neither finite-sample coverage nor finite-sample diameter dominance for an arbitrary pullback.
