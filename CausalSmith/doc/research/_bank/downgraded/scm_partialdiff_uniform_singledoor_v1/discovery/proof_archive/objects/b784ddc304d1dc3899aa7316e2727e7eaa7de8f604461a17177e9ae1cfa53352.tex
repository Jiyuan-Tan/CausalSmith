Fix \(p\geq 3\), integers \(n_1,n_2>p\), \(\gamma\in(0,1/2)\), and an arbitrary \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\).  The causal target is the fixed structural triple \(\tau(M)\); it is not defined to be a regression functional or a selected branch.  Define the event
\[
E_M:=\{\boldsymbol\Sigma_M\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\}.
\]
The assumptions defining \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), together with independent Gaussian sampling in \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\), meet the hypotheses of \(\text{lem:wishart-pivot}\).  Therefore
\[
\Pr_M(E_M)=1-\gamma. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, the positive innovation variances used by that lemma make each \(\Sigma_{M,e}\) positive definite, so \(s(M)=\operatorname{vech}(\boldsymbol\Sigma_M)\) is a well-typed element of \(\Omega_p\).

On \(E_M\), the identity \(\operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s(M))=\boldsymbol\Sigma_M\) gives
\[
s(M)\in\{s:\operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s)\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\}. \tag{2}
\]
Because \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), \(\text{lem:true-completion-retention}\) applies to the population ledger and compatible-completion fiber and yields
\[
M\in\mathfrak F(s(M))
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
\tau(M)\in\Psi(s(M)). \tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3), on \(E_M\) one has
\[
\tau(M)
\in
\bigcup_{s:\operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s)\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}}\Psi(s)
\subseteq
\overline{\bigcup_{s:\operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s)\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}}\Psi(s)}
=\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}, \tag{4}
\]
where the final equality is \(\text{def:confidence-union}\).  Thus (4) proves the event inclusion
\[
E_M\subseteq\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\}. \tag{5}
\]
Taking probabilities in (5) and using (1) gives
\[
\Pr_M\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\}
\geq \Pr_M(E_M)
=1-\gamma. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(M\) was arbitrary in the unchanged class \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), (6) implies
\[
\inf_{M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}}
\Pr_M\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\}
\geq 1-\gamma.
\]
The proof uses exact membership in the covariance region and unions over every compatible covariance and completion.  It neither conditions on a selected ledger or LDiffPC output nor divides by an equality or separation margin.  Consequently the same calculation applies pointwise to every element of any sequence in \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), including sequences along which nonzero regression-equality, conditional-independence, and completion-separation margins jointly approach zero; no beta-min, unique-order, or unique-branch condition has been introduced.
