Let \((a,b)\) satisfy \(0<|a|<1\) and \(0<|b|<1\).  For the chain model in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\), the structural equations in either environment are
\[
 X=\varepsilon_X,\qquad
 Y=aX+\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 W=bY+\varepsilon_W,
\]
with innovation variances \(1,1-a^2,1-b^2\).  Direct covariance calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X)=\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\operatorname{Var}(W)=1,
 \quad \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=a,
 \quad \operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)=b,
 \quad \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)=ab.
\]
For the fork model the equations are
\[
 Y=\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 X=aY+\varepsilon_X,\qquad
 W=bY+\varepsilon_W,
\]
with innovation variances \(1,1-a^2,1-b^2\) assigned respectively to \(Y,X,W\), and the same calculation gives the same covariance matrix \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\).  Its determinant is
\[
 \det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=(1-a^2)(1-b^2)>0,
\]
and its leading principal minors are positive, so it is positive definite.

Each paired construction uses identical equations in the two environments.  Hence its innovation variances are positive and invariant, its environment DAGs share the displayed topological order, baseline-parent containment and modularity hold with the environment-one baseline and empty target sets, and the paired law is the asserted product of identical Gaussian laws.  Every cross-environment regression equality holds.  Also \(D(M)\) is empty.  Under \(\text{def:diff-relevant-path}\) and \(\text{def:conditionally-diff-relevant-path}\), an empty difference DAG makes every such relevant-path family empty, so every conditioning set diff-separates every eligible pair.  Thus the equivalence in \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) holds, and both paired models belong to \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\).

Because all cross-environment regressions agree, the complete ledgers coincide and record equality in every entry.  The skeleton phase of population LDiffPC consequently deletes every adjacency, so both outputs and both difference DAGs are empty.  In the chain, the direct coefficient on \(X\to Y\) is \(a\) in both environments; in the fork there is no \(X\to Y\) edge.  Therefore
\[
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})=(a,a,0),\qquad
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})=(0,0,0),
\]
and their Euclidean distance is \(\sqrt2|a|\).

Let \(Q_N\) denote their common sample law and define the coverage events
\[
 A_N=\{(a,a,0)\in D_N\},\qquad
 B_N=\{(0,0,0)\in D_N\}.
\]
The assumed coverage at the two models says \(Q_N(A_N)\ge1-\gamma\) and \(Q_N(B_N)\ge1-\gamma\).  Hence
\[
 Q_N(A_N\cap B_N)
 \ge1-Q_N(A_N^c)-Q_N(B_N^c)
 \ge1-2\gamma.
\]
On \(A_N\cap B_N\), the closed set \(D_N\) contains both target points, so its diameter is at least \(\sqrt2|a|\).  This proves the asserted lower bound for every \(N\).
