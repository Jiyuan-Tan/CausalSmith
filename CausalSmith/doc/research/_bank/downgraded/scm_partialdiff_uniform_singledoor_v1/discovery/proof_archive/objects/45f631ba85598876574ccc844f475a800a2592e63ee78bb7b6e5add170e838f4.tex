By \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-stratification-sard}\), choose finite \(C^1\) semialgebraic Whitney stratifications \((S_j)\) of \(C\) and \((R_\ell)\) of \(\partial E\).  For each pair define the difference map
\[
 F_{j\ell}:S_j\times R_\ell\longrightarrow\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad F_{j\ell}(u,e)=u-e.
\]
Its critical values are semialgebraic by \(\text{lem:tarski-seidenberg-projection}\) and have dimension at most \(d-1\) by the semialgebraic Sard theorem in \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-stratification-sard}\).  Their closures have empty interior and equal their boundaries, so \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-boundary-null}\) makes them Lebesgue null.  Let \(B\) be the finite union of those closures.

If \(y\notin B\), every stratum of \(C\) meets every translated boundary stratum \(y+R_\ell\) transversely.  The local product property of transverse Whitney strata and the regular-closed identity \(E=\overline{\operatorname{int}E}\) then imply that each point of \(C\cap(y+\partial E)\) is a limit of points in \(C\cap(y+\operatorname{int}E)\).  Points already in the interior need no approximation.  Consequently
\[
 C\cap(y+E)=\overline{C\cap(y+\operatorname{int}E)}.
\]

Now assume \(y\) is not exceptional.  For the upper set limit, if \(x_n\in C_n\cap(y_n+E_n)\) and \(x_n\to x\), the upper parts of the two assumed set convergences give \(x\in C\cap(y+E)\).  For the lower limit, take \(x\in C\cap(y+E)\).  The closure equality supplies \(x^{(r)}\in C\cap(y+\operatorname{int}E)\) with \(x^{(r)}\to x\).  For fixed \(r\), the lower part of \(C_n\to C\) supplies \(x_n^{(r)}\in C_n\) with \(x_n^{(r)}\to x^{(r)}\); because \(x^{(r)}\) is in the interior and the translated bodies converge in Hausdorff distance, \(x_n^{(r)}\in y_n+E_n\) eventually.  A diagonal choice proves the lower set limit at \(x\).  If the limiting intersection is empty, the common compact bound and the upper argument preclude points eventually.  If it is nonempty, compactness turns the two set-limit inclusions into Hausdorff convergence.  These are respectively Fell convergence and the asserted nonempty Hausdorff refinement.
