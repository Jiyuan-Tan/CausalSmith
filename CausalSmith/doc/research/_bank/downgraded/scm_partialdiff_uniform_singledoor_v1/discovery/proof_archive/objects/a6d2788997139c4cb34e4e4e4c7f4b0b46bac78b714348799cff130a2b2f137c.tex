The branch set is finite, every \(T_b(s_0)\) is closed, and \(\|h\|_2\le R\).  Hence \(\Lambda_R\), with the branch label given the discrete topology, is compact.  Each \(\eta_b\) is linear, so
\[
 K_R=\bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}
       \eta_b\bigl(T_b(s_0)\cap\{h:\|h\|_2\le R\}\bigr)
\]
is compact and has finite diameter \(D_R\).  Intersecting any reported closed set \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) with \(K_R\) preserves every coverage event because every target belongs to \(K_R\), and it cannot increase diameter.  The intersection operation is Effros measurable: for an open \(O\), write \(K_R\cap O\) as a countable union of compact sets at positive distance from \(O^c\), and express a hit of each compact set as the complement of a Fell miss set.  It is therefore enough to optimize over \(\mathcal H_R\).  This action space is compact and its diameter loss is continuous by \(\text{lem:hyperspace-compactness}\).

The matrix \(V_0\) is positive definite by its definition: for every nonzero symmetric coordinate direction represented by \(A\), Gaussian fourth-moment expansion gives \(\operatorname{Var}(Z^{\mathsf T}AZ)=2\operatorname{tr}\{(\Sigma^{1/2}A\Sigma^{1/2})^2\}>0\), and both sample-share multipliers are positive.  Let \(\mu_0=\mathcal N(0,V_0)\).  Relative to \(\mu_0\), the law \(\mathcal N(h,V_0)\) has the continuous likelihood ratio
\[
 w_h(y)=\exp\{h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}y-  frac12h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}h\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
An Effros-measurable randomized rule induces the Markov kernel
\(Q(A\mid y)=\Pr\{\delta(y,U)\in A\}\) on \(\mathcal H_R\).  Conversely, \(\text{lem:kernel-uniform-randomization}\) realizes every such kernel by a measurable map of \((y,U)\).  Substitution of (8) shows that uniform coverage and worst-case expected diameter are exactly the constraints and objective in the displayed linear program.  The constant rule \(C=K_R\) is feasible and has risk \(D_R\), so \(0\le v_R\le D_R<\infty\).

It remains to prove attainment.  Choose feasible kernels \(Q_j\) with worst-case risks decreasing to \(v_R\), and form probability measures
\[
 \zeta_j(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q_j(dC\mid y)
 \quad\text{on }\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R.
\]
Their first marginal is fixed and their second coordinate lies in a compact space.  For every \(\epsilon>0\), choose compact \(L_\epsilon\subset\mathbb R^d\) with \(\mu_0(L_\epsilon)>1-\epsilon\); then \(L_\epsilon\times\mathcal H_R\) proves tightness.  By \(\text{lem:prokhorov-tightness}\), a subsequence converges weakly to a probability \(\zeta\).  Its first marginal is still \(\mu_0\).  Since both spaces are standard Borel, \(\text{lem:standard-borel-disintegration}\) supplies a Markov kernel \(Q\) such that \(\zeta(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q(dC\mid y)\).

Fix \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\), and let
\[
 F_k=\{(y,C):\eta_b(h)\in C\}.
\]
This is closed because Hausdorff convergence preserves membership in a fixed point.  Put \(w_h^L=w_h\wedge L\).  The function \(w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\) is bounded and upper semicontinuous.  The upper-semicontinuous form of \(\text{lem:portmanteau-usc}\) and feasibility give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta
 &\ge \limsup_j\int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j\\
 &\ge 1-\gamma-\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last integral tends to zero by monotone convergence because \(\int w_h,d\mu_0=1\).  Letting \(L\to\infty\) proves the coverage constraint for \(k\), and \(k\) was arbitrary.

For risk, \(w_h^L\operatorname{diam}(C)\) is bounded and continuous, so weak convergence gives convergence of its integrals.  The discarded tail is at most
\[
 D_R\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
It follows, for every fixed \(k\), that
\[
 \int w_h(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta
 =\lim_j\int w_h(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta_j
 \le v_R.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(k\) shows that \(Q\) is feasible with risk at most \(v_R\); the definition of the infimum forces equality.  Uniform randomization realizes this minimizing kernel as an admissible rule.  Conversely, every attaining rule induces a feasible minimizing kernel, proving the characterization.

Finally, suppose a sequence of finite-sample rules has any prescribed local asymptotic pullback.  For a fixed \(N_0\), replace only its \(N_0\)-th rule by \(\varnothing\).  The modified sequence is eventually identical to the original and hence has exactly the same local limits, but its coverage at \(N_0\) is zero.  Replacing that one rule by \(\mathbb R^3\) instead preserves coverage but gives infinite diameter.  Thus local attainment contains no information capable of certifying the all-sample-size honesty and strict-diameter comparison asked for in the second clause; those properties require a separate finite-sample optimization or admissibility argument.
