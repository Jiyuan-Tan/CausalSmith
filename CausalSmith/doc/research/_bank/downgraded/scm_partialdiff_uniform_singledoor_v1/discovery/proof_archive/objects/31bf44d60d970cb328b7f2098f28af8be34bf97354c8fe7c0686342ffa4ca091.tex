Fix \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\) and define the true-covariance event
\[
E_M=\{\boldsymbol\Sigma_M\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\}.
\]
The restrictions \(n_e>p\), \(e\in\mathbb E\), and the positive innovation variances in \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\) make both population covariance matrices positive definite, so every pivot in \(\text{def:wishart-pivot}\) is well defined.  By \(\text{lem:wishart-pivot}\), independence of the two samples and the choice \(a_\gamma=\sqrt{1-\gamma}\) give
\[
\Pr_M(E_M)
=\prod_{e=1}^2
  \Pr_M\!\left\{T_{e,n}(\Sigma_{M,e})
       \le q_{p,\nu_e}(a_\gamma)\right\}
=a_\gamma^2
=1-\gamma.
\]
On \(E_M\), the coordinate \(s(M)=\operatorname{vech}(\boldsymbol\Sigma_M)\) is among the covariance coordinates over which \(\text{def:confidence-union}\) takes its union.  The identifying relation is not imposed by definition: \(\text{lem:true-completion-retention}\), proved from population LDiffPC soundness and diff-faithfulness, gives
\[
M\in\mathfrak F(s(M))
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
\tau(M)\in\Psi(s(M)).
\]
Therefore, using the definition of the confidence union and the fact that outer closure can only enlarge a set,
\[
E_M
\subseteq
\left\{\tau(M)\in
\overline{\bigcup_{s:\operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s)\in
\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}}\Psi(s)}\right\}
=\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\}.
\]
Taking probabilities yields
\[
\Pr_M\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\}
\ge \Pr_M(E_M)=1-\gamma.
\]
This calculation holds for every \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\); hence taking the infimum over that same model class proves the displayed uniform coverage bound.

No step estimates, selects, or conditions on a ledger, topological order, or branch: on the pivotal event the union simply retains the true covariance coordinate and then \(\text{lem:true-completion-retention}\) retains the true SCM completion.  The proof uses only exact equalities at the candidate covariance and never divides by an equality, conditional-independence, or completion-separation margin.  Thus the identical argument applies to every sequence in \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\) along which any collection of nonzero such margins converges jointly to zero.  In particular, no beta-min or separation condition has been introduced.
