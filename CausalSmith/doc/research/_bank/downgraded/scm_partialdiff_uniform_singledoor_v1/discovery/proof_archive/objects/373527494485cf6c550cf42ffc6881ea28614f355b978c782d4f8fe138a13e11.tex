Put \(s_N=s(M_N)\), \(\widehat s_N=\operatorname{vech}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\Sigma})\), \(m=p(p+1)/2\), \(\lambda_1=\lambda\), and \(\lambda_2=1-\lambda\). All probability statements below are under the triangular sample law generated by \(M_N\). The local-sequence condition gives \(s_N\to s_0\). Since \(s_0\in\Omega_p\), both limiting covariance matrices \(\Sigma_{0,e}\) are positive definite.

For environment \(e\), define
\[
 X_{ei,N}=\operatorname{vech}
 \bigl(Z_{ei}Z_{ei}^{\mathsf T}-\Sigma_{M_N,e}\bigr).
\]
Gaussian fourth moments give, in half-vector coordinates,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_{ei,N})_{AB,CD}
 =\Sigma_{M_N,e,AC}\Sigma_{M_N,e,BD}
  +\Sigma_{M_N,e,AD}\Sigma_{M_N,e,BC}
 =V(\Sigma_{M_N,e})_{AB,CD}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Because \(\Sigma_{M_N,e}\to\Sigma_{0,e}\), Gaussian moments of every fixed order are uniformly bounded. Hence, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_N\!\left[
 \|X_{ei,N}\|^2
 {\bf1}\{\|X_{ei,N}\|>\epsilon\sqrt{n_e}\}
 \right]
 \le
 \frac{\mathbb E_N\|X_{ei,N}\|^4}{\epsilon^2n_e}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is the Lindeberg condition. Moreover,
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_e
 =\frac{n_e}{\nu_e}
 \left\{n_e^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}Z_{ei}Z_{ei}^{\mathsf T}
       -\overline Z_e\overline Z_e^{\mathsf T}\right\},
\]
where \(\overline Z_e\overline Z_e^{\mathsf T}=O_{\mathbb P_N}(n_e^{-1})\) and \(n_e/\nu_e-1=O(n_e^{-1})\). These two corrections are \(o_{\mathbb P_N}(n_e^{-1/2})\). Independence of the environment samples from \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\), (1)--(2), \(\text{lem:multivariate-lindeberg-feller}\), and \(n_e/N\to\lambda_e\in(0,1)\) from \(\text{ass:asymptotic-sample-balance}\) therefore yield
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat s_N-s_N)\Rightarrow\mathbb Z_0,
 \qquad
 Y_N:=\sqrt N(\widehat s_N-s_0)
 \Rightarrow y:=h+\mathbb Z_0.
 \tag{3}
\]
The covariance in (3) is \(V_0\) by definition. It is positive definite: if \(A\) is a nonzero symmetric matrix, then positivity of \(\Sigma_{0,e}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z_e^{\mathsf T}AZ_e)
 =2\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
   \{\Sigma_{0,e}^{1/2}A\Sigma_{0,e}^{1/2}\}^2
 \right]>0,
 \tag{4}
\]
and \(\lambda_e>0\). Thus \(y\) has a Lebesgue density on \(\mathbb R^d\).

We next derive the local form of the exact Wishart bodies. For deterministic \(z=(z_1,z_2)\), let \(\mathcal A_N(z)\) be the set of \(u=(u_1,u_2)\) such that, for \(e=1,2\),
\[
 S_e(u_e):=\operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s_{0,e}+u_e/\sqrt N)\succ0,
 \qquad
 T_{e,n}\{S_e(u_e);z_e\}
 \le q_{p,\nu_e}(\sqrt{1-\gamma}),
 \tag{5}
\]
where the pivot in (5) is evaluated at the sample covariance with local coordinate \(z_e\). The realized rescaled covariance region is exactly \(\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\).

Let \(g(A)=\operatorname{tr}(A)-\log\det(A)-p\). Taylor expansion at \(I_p\) gives
\[
 g(I_p+H)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(H^2)+O(\|H\|^3).
 \tag{6}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(s_0\), \(\nu_e/N\to\lambda_e\), and (6) imply, uniformly on every compact set of \((u_e,z_e)\),
\[
 T_{e,n}\{S_e(u_e);z_e\}
 =(z_e-u_e)^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}(z_e-u_e)+o(1),
 \tag{7}
\]
where \(V_{0,1}=\lambda^{-1}V(\Sigma_{0,1})\) and \(V_{0,2}=(1-\lambda)^{-1}V(\Sigma_{0,2})\). Indeed, the inverse of \(V(\Sigma_{0,e})\) represents the quadratic form
\[
 H\longmapsto\tfrac12
 \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{0,e}^{-1}H\Sigma_{0,e}^{-1}H)
 \tag{8}
\]
in half-vector coordinates, matching the factor \(1/2\) in (6).

Apply the same Gaussian covariance central limit calculation to \(W/\nu_e\) in the definition of the pivotal quantile. Together with (6), it gives convergence of the pivot to \(\chi_m^2\). Since the \(\chi_m^2\) distribution function is continuous and strictly increasing at its \(a_\gamma:=\sqrt{1-\gamma}\) quantile, quantile convergence yields
\[
 q_{p,\nu_e}(a_\gamma)\longrightarrow
 q_\infty:=q_{\chi_m^2}(a_\gamma)>0.
 \tag{9}
\]
Set
\[
 E_\gamma=\prod_{e=1}^2
 \{v_e:v_e^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}v_e\le q_\infty\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_\gamma(z)=z+E_\gamma.
 \tag{10}
\]
By (4) and (9), \(E_\gamma\) is compact, full-dimensional, semialgebraic, and regular closed. This is the Gaussian acceptance body in \(\text{def:boundary-limit-sets}\).

By \(\text{lem:skorokhod-representation}\), we may work on a coupling on which \(Y_N\to y\) almost surely. Fix such a sample path. If \(u_N\in\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\) and \(u_N\to u\), then (7)--(9) give
\[
 (y_e-u_e)^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}(y_e-u_e)\le q_\infty,
 \quad e=1,2,
 \qquad
 \limsup_N\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\subseteq\mathcal A_\gamma(y).
 \tag{11}
\]
If \(K\subset\operatorname{int}\mathcal A_\gamma(y)\) is compact, the two limiting quadratic inequalities have a common positive slack on \(K\). Equations (7), (9), and \(Y_N\to y\) then imply
\[
 K\subseteq\mathcal A_N(Y_N)
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }N.
 \tag{12}
\]

These exact bodies also have a common compact bound. Let \(a_{e1},\ldots,a_{ep}\) be the eigenvalues of \(S_e^{-1/2}\widehat\Sigma_eS_e^{-1/2}\), and put \(r(a)=a-\log a-1\). The function \(r\) is nonnegative, vanishes only at one, and is bounded away from zero outside every neighborhood of one. From (5) and (9),
\[
 0\le\sum_{j=1}^p r(a_{ej})=O(N^{-1}).
 \tag{13}
\]
Taylor's inequality for \(r\) near one gives \(\max_j|a_{ej}-1|=O(N^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the pivot sublevel. Because \(Y_N\) is bounded on the fixed path and \(\Sigma_{0,e}\succ0\), the corresponding Loewner inequalities between \(S_e\) and \(\widehat\Sigma_e\) imply
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal A_N(Y_N)}\|u-Y_N\|=O(1).
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus (11), (12), and (14) are the upper-limit, erosion, and common-compact-bound hypotheses of \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\).

For \(b\in\mathbb B(s_0)\), define
\[
 C_{b,N}=\sqrt N(\overline{\mathcal S_b}-s_0),
 \qquad
 Q_{b,N}=\overline{C_{b,N}\cap\mathcal A_N(Y_N)},
 \qquad
 Q_b(y)=T_b(s_0)\cap\mathcal A_\gamma(y).
 \tag{15}
\]
The condition \(\text{ass:tangent-cone-regularity}\), at \(t=N^{-1/2}\), states
\[
 C_{b,N}\longrightarrow T_b(s_0)
 \tag{16}
\]
locally in the Painlev\'e--Kuratowski topology. Each \(T_b(s_0)\) is semialgebraic. To see this, apply \(\text{lem:tarski-seidenberg-projection}\) to the closure of
\[
 \{(t,u):t>0,\ s_0+t u\in\overline{\mathcal S_b}\};
\]
its section at \(t=0\) is the tangent cone under (16). There are finitely many accessible branches by \(\text{thm:finite-branch-representation}\). Therefore the union over accessible \(b\) of the exceptional translation sets in \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\) is Lebesgue null. Since \(y\) has a density by (4), almost surely every accessible branch is simultaneously at a stable translation.

On this probability-one event, (11)--(16) and \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\) give
\[
 Q_{b,N}\longrightarrow Q_b(y)
 \tag{17}
\]
in the Fell topology, with Hausdorff convergence when \(Q_b(y)\ne\varnothing\). If \(Q_b(y)=\varnothing\), (14) and Fell convergence imply eventual emptiness, because otherwise a subsequence of points in the common compact set would have a limit point in \(Q_b(y)\).

Using the closed cell in (15) does not enlarge the limiting branch image. Indeed, \(\mathcal S_b\) is dense in \(\overline{\mathcal S_b}\). The lower-limit part of the intersection-stability lemma first approximates each limiting point by a point of \(T_b(s_0)\cap\operatorname{int}\mathcal A_\gamma(y)\). By (12), a small compact ball around that point lies in \(\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\) eventually; density then perturbs an approximating point of \(C_{b,N}\) inside this ball into \(\sqrt N(\mathcal S_b-s_0)\). The upper limit is unchanged because \(\mathcal S_b\subseteq\overline{\mathcal S_b}\). Hence (17) also holds for the closure of the rescaled intersection with \(\mathcal S_b\).

If \(b\notin\mathbb B(s_0)\), then the closed set \(\overline{\mathcal S_b}\) misses a neighborhood of \(s_0\). The bound (14) makes its intersection with the covariance region empty for all sufficiently large \(N\). Thus only accessible branches remain.

It remains to map (17) through the causal branch targets. The result \(\text{lem:branch-target-rational-smooth-extension}\), derived from \(\text{thm:finite-branch-representation}\), gives for every compact \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\)
\[
 \sup_{u\in K}
 \|\theta_b(s_0+u/\sqrt N)-\theta_b(s_0)\|
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{18}
\]
By \(\text{def:confidence-union}\) and the finite branch representation, the confidence union is the closure of the finite union of these branch images over the covariance intersections. Equations (14), (17), and (18), followed by the finite union, therefore give on the coupling
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\longrightarrow
 \{\theta_b(s_0):b\in\mathbb B(s_0),\ Q_b(y)\ne\varnothing\}
 =\mathcal K_h^{(0)}
 \tag{19}
\]
in the Fell topology. Almost-sure convergence of the coupled versions implies the first asserted convergence in distribution under the original triangular laws.

Finally suppose \(\Gamma(s_0)=\{\tau_0\}\). Then \(\theta_b(s_0)=\tau_0\) for every accessible \(b\). The uniform Taylor conclusion of \(\text{lem:branch-target-rational-smooth-extension}\) and the compact bound (14) give
\[
 \sup_{u\in K}
 \left\|
 \sqrt N\{\theta_b(s_0+u/\sqrt N)-\tau_0\}
 -D\theta_b(s_0)u
 \right\|\longrightarrow0
 \tag{20}
\]
on every compact \(K\). Applying (17) and (20) branch by branch and then taking the finite union yields
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}-\tau_0)
 \longrightarrow
 \bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}
 \{D\theta_b(s_0)u:u\in T_b(s_0)\cap
 \mathcal A_\gamma(h+\mathbb Z_0)\}
 =\mathcal K_h^{(1)}
 \tag{21}
\]
almost surely on the coupling, hence in distribution in the Fell topology under the original laws. Empty limiting intersections are allowed, which is why the conclusion is stated in the Fell topology rather than only on the nonempty Hausdorff hyperspace.
