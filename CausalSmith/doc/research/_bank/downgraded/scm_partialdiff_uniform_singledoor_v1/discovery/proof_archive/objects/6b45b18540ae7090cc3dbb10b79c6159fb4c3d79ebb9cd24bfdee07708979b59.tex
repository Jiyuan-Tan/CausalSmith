Fix \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(a\neq0\) and \(b\neq0\), and let \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\) and \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\) be the paired SCMs named in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\).  We first derive their observational laws.

For the chain, mutual independence and the unit innovation variances give, in either environment,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=a,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=b\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=ab,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b\operatorname{Var}(Y)=b(a^2+1),\\
 \operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2\operatorname{Var}(Y)+1=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus its covariance is
\[
 \Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&a&ab\\
  a&a^2+1&b(a^2+1)\\
  ab&b(a^2+1)&b^2(a^2+1)+1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
If
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\a&1&0\\ab&b&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
then direct multiplication yields \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=LL^{\mathsf T}\).  Since \(L\) is triangular with diagonal entries equal to one, \(\det L=1\).  Hence
\[
 \det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=(\det L)^2=1.
\]
Moreover, for every nonzero \(q\in\mathbb R^3\), invertibility of \(L^{\mathsf T}\) gives
\[
 q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)q
 =\|L^{\mathsf T}q\|_2^2>0,
\]
so the covariance is positive definite.  This argument uses no upper bound on \(|a|\) or \(|b|\).

For the fork, write \(c=a/(a^2+1)\).  The innovation variances are strictly positive because \(a^2+1>0\).  Independence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=c^2(a^2+1)+(a^2+1)^{-1}
 =\frac{a^2+1}{a^2+1}=1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=c(a^2+1)=a,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=b\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=ab,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the fork has the same covariance \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\).  All structural equations are linear combinations of independent centered Gaussian innovations, so both constructions induce \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b))\) in each environment.

We next verify membership in \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\), rather than importing it from the construction.  Each displayed graph is acyclic under its declared order.  Within each paired SCM the two environments have identical structural equations, identical positive innovation variances, a common baseline graph and coefficient matrix, and empty intervention target sets.  Consequently the linear structural-equation, baseline-parent-containment, modularity, independent-Gaussian-innovation, invariant-innovation-variance, and positive-innovation-variance conditions enumerated in \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\) hold.  It remains only to check diff-faithfulness.

For either paired SCM, \(B_1=B_2\).  Therefore \(\text{def:difference-dag}\) gives \(D(M)=\varnothing\).  With no incoming edge in \(D(M)\), neither alternative in \(\text{def:diff-relevant-path}\) can hold, and every alternative in \(\text{def:conditionally-diff-relevant-path}\) is also false.  Hence \(\text{def:diff-separation}\) gives
\[
 A\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}B\mid S
\]
for every distinct \(A,B\) and every eligible \(S\).  On the observational side the two environment covariance matrices are identical.  Positive definiteness implies \(\Sigma_{e,BB\cdot S}>0\), because this conditional variance is the Schur complement of the positive-definite principal block \(\Sigma_{e,SS}\).  Thus every partial regression is well defined and
\[
 r_1(A,B\mid S)=r_2(A,B\mid S)
\]
for all eligible triples.  Both sides of the equivalence in the diff-faithfulness assumption are therefore true for every triple, proving diff-faithfulness and hence membership of both models in \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\).

Because the two witness models have the same covariance pair, \(\text{def:exact-ledger}\) assigns them the same complete ledger; because the environments agree, every cross-environment regression-equality entry is an equality.  The population-soundness lemma \(\text{lem:ldiffpc-population-soundness}\) applies by the just-proved diff-faithfulness and perfect ledger information.  It says that the population output has the true difference-DAG skeleton and only true orientations.  The skeleton is empty, so the output is the empty partially directed graph.

In the chain the coefficient on \(X\to Y\) is \(a\) in both environments, whereas in the fork the arrow \(X\to Y\) is absent and its coefficient is zero.  The definitions of the direct-effect pair and triple therefore give
\[
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})=(a,a,0),\qquad
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})=(0,0,0),
\]
whose Euclidean separation is \(\sqrt{a^2+a^2}=\sqrt2|a|\).

Fix \(N\).  By independent Gaussian sampling and equality of both environment laws, the complete sample laws under the two witnesses coincide; call the common law \(Q_N\).  Let
\[
 A_N=\{(a,a,0)\in D_N\},\qquad
 F_N=\{(0,0,0)\in D_N\}.
\]
The two assumed coverage inequalities give \(Q_N(A_N)\ge1-\gamma\) and \(Q_N(F_N)\ge1-\gamma\).  Therefore
\[
 Q_N(A_N\cap F_N)
 \ge1-Q_N(A_N^c)-Q_N(F_N^c)
 \ge1-2\gamma.
\]
On \(A_N\cap F_N\), the closed set \(D_N\) contains both target points, so its diameter is at least \(\sqrt2|a|\).  Hence
\[
 Q_N\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\ge\sqrt2|a|\right\}\ge1-2\gamma.
\]
Since \(N\) was arbitrary, this holds for every \(N\).
