Because \(\Gamma(s_0)=\{\tau_0\}\), the accessible branch set is nonempty; it is finite because \(\mathbb B_p\) is finite.  For each accessible branch \(b\), the set
\[
L_{b,R}=T_b(s_0)\cap\{h\in\mathbb R^d:\|h\|_2\le R\}
\]
is compact: \(T_b(s_0)\) is closed by the definition of the tangent cone, and the Euclidean ball is compact.  The map \(\eta_b\) is linear by the definition of the branch-local target, hence continuous.  Consequently
\[
K_R=\bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}\eta_b(L_{b,R})
\]
is a finite union of compact sets and therefore is compact.  Write \(D_R=\operatorname{diam}(K_R)<\infty\).

We first justify restricting the action space to \(\mathcal H_R\).  Define \(T(C)=C\cap K_R\) for every closed \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^3\).  This map is Effros measurable.  Indeed, if \(O=K_R\cap G\) with \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) open, choose the compact sets
\[
A_m=\{x\in K_R:\operatorname{dist}(x,G^c)\ge m^{-1}\},\qquad m\ge1,
\]
with the evident convention when \(G^c=\varnothing\).  Then \(O=\bigcup_{m\ge1}A_m\).  For any compact \(A\), closedness of \(C\) and compactness of \(A\) give
\[
\{C:C\cap A\ne\varnothing\}
=\bigcap_{\ell\ge1}\{C:C\cap A^{1/\ell}\ne\varnothing\},
\]
where \(A^{1/\ell}\) is the open \(1/\ell\)-neighborhood of \(A\).  Thus the inverse image under \(T\) of every hit set in the Effros sigma-field of \(\mathcal H_R\) is measurable; the inverse image of its isolated empty point is measurable by the same compact-hit identity with \(A=K_R\).  This proves the claim.

Every target \(\eta_b(h)\), for \((b,h)\in\Lambda_R\), belongs to \(K_R\).  It follows pointwise that
\[
\mathbf 1\{\eta_b(h)\in T(C)\}=\mathbf 1\{\eta_b(h)\in C\},
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(T(C))\le\operatorname{diam}(C).
\]
Hence truncation preserves every coverage probability and cannot increase any expected-diameter risk.  It is therefore enough for the value and existence problem to consider rules taking values in \(\mathcal H_R\).  By \(\text{lem:hyperspace-compactness}\), this action space is compact and \(C\mapsto\operatorname{diam}(C)\) is continuous on it.

We next put all Gaussian laws over a common dominating measure.  The covariance \(V_0\) is positive definite.  To verify this rather than assume it, let \(c\ne0\) be a half-vector coordinate and let \(A_c\ne0\) be the corresponding symmetric matrix.  The displayed definition of \(V(\Sigma)\), expanded coordinate by coordinate, gives
\[
c^{\mathsf T}V(\Sigma)c
=2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(\Sigma^{1/2}A_c\Sigma^{1/2})^2\right\}>0
\]
whenever \(\Sigma\) is positive definite.  The two covariance matrices represented by \(s_0\) are positive definite, and \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), so the block-diagonal definition of \(V_0\) proves the assertion.  Put \(\mu_0=\mathcal N(0,V_0)\).  For each \(h\), the law \(\mathcal N(h,V_0)\) has the continuous, strictly positive likelihood ratio
\[
w_h(y)=\exp\!\left\{h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}y-  frac12h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}h\right\}
\quad\text{relative to }\mu_0,
\qquad
\int w_h\,d\mu_0=1.
\]

Let \(\delta\) be an \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued randomized rule.  Joint measurability and integration over the independent uniform variable define the probability kernel
\[
Q(A\mid y)=\int_0^1\mathbf 1\{\delta(y,u)\in A\}\,du,
\qquad A\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H_R).
\]
Conversely, \(\text{lem:kernel-uniform-randomization}\) represents every probability kernel \(Q\) from \(\mathbb R^d\) to the standard Borel space \(\mathcal H_R\) by such a jointly measurable function of \((y,U)\).  Changing measure from \(\mu_0\) by \(w_h\) shows, for every \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\), that its coverage and risk are respectively
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathcal H_R}
 \mathbf 1\{\eta_b(h)\in C\}\,Q(dC\mid y)\varphi_h(y)\,dy
\]
and
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathcal H_R}
 \operatorname{diam}(C)\,Q(dC\mid y)\varphi_h(y)\,dy.
\]
Therefore the feasible kernels and their smallest admissible epigraph coordinate \(t\) are exactly the variables and constraints in the displayed semi-infinite linear program.  The constant rule \(C=K_R\) satisfies every coverage constraint and has risk \(D_R\).  Thus
\[
0\le v_R\le D_R<\infty.
\]

It remains to show that the infimum is attained.  For each \(j\ge1\), choose a feasible kernel \(Q_j\) whose worst-case risk is at most \(v_R+j^{-1}\), and define
\[
\zeta_j(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q_j(dC\mid y)
\]
on \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R\).  Its first marginal is \(\mu_0\), while its second coordinate lies in the compact space \(\mathcal H_R\).  Given \(\epsilon>0\), a sufficiently large closed Euclidean ball \(L_\epsilon\) satisfies \(\mu_0(L_\epsilon)>1-\epsilon\); hence \(L_\epsilon\times\mathcal H_R\) proves tightness of \((\zeta_j)_j\).  By \(\text{lem:prokhorov-tightness}\), a subsequence, not relabeled, converges weakly to a probability measure \(\zeta\).  For every bounded continuous \(g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\), weak convergence and the fixed first marginal give
\[
\int g(y)\,\zeta(dy,dC)
=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int g(y)\,\zeta_j(dy,dC)
=\int g(y)\,\mu_0(dy).
\]
Thus the first marginal of \(\zeta\) is \(\mu_0\).  Since both factors are standard Borel spaces, \(\text{lem:standard-borel-disintegration}\) yields a probability kernel \(Q\) satisfying
\[
\zeta(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q(dC\mid y).
\]

Fix \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\) and set
\[
F_k=\{(y,C):\eta_b(h)\in C\}.
\]
The set \(F_k\) is closed because membership of a fixed point is preserved under Hausdorff convergence.  For \(L>0\), put \(w_h^L=w_h\wedge L\).  The function \(w_h^L\mathbf 1_{F_k}\) is bounded and upper semicontinuous.  The upper-semicontinuous Portmanteau inequality \(\text{lem:portmanteau-usc}\) and feasibility of every \(Q_j\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int w_h^L\mathbf 1_{F_k}\,d\zeta
&\ge \limsup_{j\to\infty}\int w_h^L\mathbf 1_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j\\
&\ge 1-\gamma-\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(w_h\) is integrable, its truncation tails satisfy
\[
0\le\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0
\le\int_{\{w_h>L\}}w_h\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(L\), the left side increases to \(\int w_h\mathbf 1_{F_k}\,d\zeta\), so the limiting kernel satisfies the coverage constraint at \(k\).  Since \(k\) was arbitrary, \(Q\) is uniformly coverage feasible.

For risk, \((y,C)\mapsto w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\) is bounded and continuous, and weak convergence therefore gives
\[
\int w_h^L\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta
=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int w_h^L\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta_j.
\]
Moreover, uniformly in \(j\), replacing \(w_h^L\) by \(w_h\) changes either integral by at most
\[
D_R\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
It follows that, for this fixed \(k\),
\[
\int w_h(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta
=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int w_h(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta_j
\le v_R.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(k\in\Lambda_R\) shows that \(Q\) has worst-case risk at most \(v_R\).  Since \(v_R\) is the infimum over all feasible rules, this risk equals \(v_R\), proving attainment and the exact linear-program characterization.

The randomization lemma realizes every minimizing kernel as an attaining \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rule.  Conversely, the kernel induced by any attaining \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rule is feasible and has objective \(v_R\), hence is minimizing.  All Gaussian shifts have strictly positive density relative to \(\mu_0\), so the relevant null sets agree.  Finally, if an unrestricted rule attains \(v_R\), its measurable truncation \(T\circ\delta\) remains feasible and has risk at most \(v_R\); the definition of \(v_R\) forces equality, and the preceding argument represents this truncation by a minimizing kernel.

For the last assertion, let \((\Delta_N)_{N\ge1}\) be any proposed sequence of finite-sample pullbacks and fix \(N_0\).  Replacing only \(\Delta_{N_0}\) by the constantly empty set leaves \(\Delta_N\) unchanged for every \(N>N_0\), hence leaves every local asymptotic limit unchanged, but its coverage at \(N_0\) is zero.  Replacing only \(\Delta_{N_0}\) by \(\mathbb R^3\) likewise leaves every local limit unchanged but gives infinite diameter at \(N_0\).  Thus neither all-sample finite-sample coverage nor finite-sample diameter dominance follows from the local attainment characterization.
