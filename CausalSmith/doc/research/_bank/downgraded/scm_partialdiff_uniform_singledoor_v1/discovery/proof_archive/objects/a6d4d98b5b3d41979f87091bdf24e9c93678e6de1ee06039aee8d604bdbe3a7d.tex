Let \((a,b)\) satisfy \(0<|a|<1\) and \(0<|b|<1\).  In either environment, the chain construction has structural equations
\[
 X=\varepsilon_X,\qquad
 Y=aX+\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 W=bY+\varepsilon_W,
\]
where the mutually independent centered Gaussian innovations have variances \(1\), \(1-a^2\), and \(1-b^2\), respectively.  By \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=1,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+(1-a^2)=1,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2+(1-b^2)=1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=a,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=ab.
\end{aligned}
\]
The fork construction has, in either environment,
\[
 Y=\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 X=aY+\varepsilon_X,\qquad
 W=bY+\varepsilon_W,
\]
where the innovation variances in coordinate order \((X,Y,W)\) are \(1-a^2,1,1-b^2\).  Independence gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X)=\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\operatorname{Var}(W)=1,
 \quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=a,
 \quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)=b,
 \quad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)=ab.
\]
Thus both constructions induce \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b))\) in each environment.  Define
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\a&1&0\\ab&b&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Delta=\operatorname{diag}(1,1-a^2,1-b^2).
\]
Direct multiplication gives \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=L\Delta L^{\mathsf T}\).  The diagonal entries of \(\Delta\) are strictly positive by the restrictions on \((a,b)\), and \(L\) is invertible because \(\det L=1\).  Hence, for every nonzero \(q\in\mathbb R^3\),
\[
 q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)q
 =(L^{\mathsf T}q)^{\mathsf T}\Delta(L^{\mathsf T}q)>0.
\]
This proves positive definiteness directly, and the same factorization gives
\[
 \det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=(1-a^2)(1-b^2)>0.
\]

We next verify membership in \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\), rather than treating it as part of the construction.  Within either paired model, the two environments use identical structural equations and identical positive innovation variances.  Take the displayed DAG as \(G^*\), take \(B^*=B_1=B_2\), and take \(\mathcal I_1=\mathcal I_2=\varnothing\).  The displayed chain order or fork order is common to the baseline and both environment DAGs.  Consequently \(\text{ass:baseline-parent-containment}\), \(\text{ass:modular-interventions}\), \(\text{ass:invariant-innovation-variances}\), and \(\text{ass:positive-innovation-variances}\) hold; the preceding equations verify \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\).

Because \(B_1=B_2\), \(\text{def:difference-dag}\) gives \(D(M)=\varnothing\).  With no edge in \(D(M)\), neither alternative in \(\text{def:diff-relevant-path}\) can hold.  Each alternative in \(\text{def:conditionally-diff-relevant-path}\) also requires a vertex with an incoming edge in \(D(M)\), so none can hold.  Hence every eligible conditioning set blocks all diff-relevant and conditionally diff-relevant paths, and \(\text{def:diff-separation}\) gives
\[
 A\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}B\mid S
\]
for every eligible \((A,B,S)\).  On the observational side, the two environment covariance matrices are identical and positive definite, so every denominator \(\Sigma_{e,BB\cdot S}\) in \(r_e(A,B\mid S)\) is strictly positive and
\[
 r_1(A,B\mid S)=r_2(A,B\mid S)
\]
for every eligible \((A,B,S)\).  Both directions of \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) therefore hold.  This proves that both paired models are members of \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\).

By \(\text{def:exact-ledger}\), equality of every cross-environment regression makes the two complete ledgers coincide and makes every equality entry positive.  The hypotheses of \(\text{lem:ldiffpc-population-soundness}\) have just been verified.  That lemma says that the population output has the true difference-DAG skeleton; because this skeleton is empty, the output itself is the empty partially directed graph.

In the chain, the coefficient on \(X\to Y\) is \(a\) in both environments.  In the fork, \(X\to Y\) is absent, so its coefficient is zero in both environments.  By the definitions of \(\alpha\) and \(\tau\),
\[
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})=(a,a,0),\qquad
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})=(0,0,0),
\]
and the Euclidean distance between these two targets is \(\sqrt{a^2+a^2}=\sqrt2|a|\).

Fix \(N\).  By \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\), the sample law under a model is \(P_{M,1}^{n_1}\otimes P_{M,2}^{n_2}\).  The complete environment law pairs of the two witnesses are the same, so these product measures coincide; denote their common value by \(Q_N\).  Define
\[
 A_N=\{(a,a,0)\in D_N\},\qquad
 B_N=\{(0,0,0)\in D_N\}.
\]
The two assumed coverage inequalities give \(Q_N(A_N)\ge1-\gamma\) and \(Q_N(B_N)\ge1-\gamma\).  The union bound therefore yields
\[
 Q_N(A_N\cap B_N)
 \ge 1-Q_N(A_N^c)-Q_N(B_N^c)
 \ge 1-2\gamma.
\]
On \(A_N\cap B_N\), the reported closed set contains both target points, and hence its diameter is at least their distance \(\sqrt2|a|\).  Thus
\[
 Q_N\{\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\ge\sqrt2|a|\}\ge1-2\gamma.
\]
Since \(N\) was arbitrary, the inequality holds for every \(N\).
