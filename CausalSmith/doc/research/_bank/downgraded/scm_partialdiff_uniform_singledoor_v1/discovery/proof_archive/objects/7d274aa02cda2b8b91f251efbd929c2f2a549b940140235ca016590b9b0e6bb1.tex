Take \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\).  By \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\), the model satisfies \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\), \(\text{ass:baseline-parent-containment}\), \(\text{ass:modular-interventions}\), and \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\), which are the four shared structural conditions in \(\text{def:bystrova-devijver-anchor-class}\).  It remains to verify causal sufficiency and invariance of the entire exogenous law.

By \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\), for each \(e\in\mathbb E\),
\[
 \varepsilon_e\sim\mathcal N(0,\operatorname{diag}(\omega)).
\]
By \(\text{ass:positive-innovation-variances}\), every \(\omega_v\) is strictly positive, so this law has density
\[
 f_e(x)=\prod_{v\in\mathbb V}
 (2\pi\omega_v)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{x_v^2}{2\omega_v}\right).
\]
The displayed product factorization proves that the coordinates of \(\varepsilon_e\) are mutually independent.  Thus the innovations are causally sufficient in the sense required by the anchor class.  By \(\text{ass:invariant-innovation-variances}\), the same vector \(\omega\) occurs in both environments.  The displayed density is therefore identical for \(e=1\) and \(e=2\), proving
\[
 \operatorname{Law}(\varepsilon_1)=\operatorname{Law}(\varepsilon_2).
\]
All defining conditions of \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{BD}}\) now hold, so \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{BD}}\).  Since \(M\) was arbitrary, the asserted inclusion follows.
