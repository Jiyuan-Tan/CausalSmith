Write \(S_e=\Sigma^{\mathrm{col}}_e\), with rows and columns ordered as \((X,W,Y)\). Direct determinant expansion gives
\[
 \det S_1
 =1+2\frac{3}{10}\frac{23}{50}\frac{8}{25}
   -\left(\frac{3}{10}\right)^2
   -\left(\frac{23}{50}\right)^2
   -\left(\frac{8}{25}\right)^2
 =\frac{4277}{6250},
\]
and
\[
 \det S_2
 =\frac{543}{500}
   -\left(\frac{57}{100}\right)^2
   -\left(\frac{3}{10}\right)^2\frac{543}{500}
   +2\frac{3}{10}\frac{2}{25}\frac{57}{100}
   -\left(\frac{2}{25}\right)^2
 =\frac{4277}{6250}.
\]
For each matrix, the leading principal minors in the displayed coordinate order are \(1\), \(91/100\), and \(4277/6250\). They are positive, so completing squares in the Gaussian quadratic form (equivalently, Sylvester's criterion) shows that both matrices are positive definite. In particular, every principal covariance matrix inverted below is nonsingular and every residual variance below is positive.

We next justify why it is enough to inspect complete ordered factorizations. The marginal covariances and the three one-variable conditional covariances are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc|ccc}
 e& S_{e,XW}&S_{e,XY}&S_{e,WY}
  &S_{e,XW\cdot Y}&S_{e,XY\cdot W}&S_{e,WY\cdot X}\\ \hline
 1&\frac3{10}&\frac{23}{50}&\frac8{25}
  &\frac{191}{1250}&\frac{91}{250}&\frac{91}{500}\\[2pt]
 2&\frac3{10}&\frac2{25}&\frac{57}{100}
  &\frac{467}{1810}&-\frac{91}{1000}&\frac{273}{500}.
\end{array}
\]
For example, \(S_{e,AB\cdot C}=S_{e,AB}-S_{e,AC}S_{e,CC}^{-1}S_{e,CB}\); all six denominators used here are positive by positive definiteness. Every displayed number is nonzero. For completeness, in a centered nonsingular Gaussian triple the conditional density of \((A,B)\) given \(C\), obtained by completing the square in the joint density, is bivariate Gaussian with off-diagonal covariance \(S_{e,AB\cdot C}\). Its density factors into an \(A\)-part and a \(B\)-part if and only if this off-diagonal entry is zero. Thus neither environment has a marginal or one-variable conditional independence.

Let \(v_1\prec v_2\prec v_3\) be a topological order of an admissible three-variable structural model and put \(P_j=\{v_1,\ldots,v_{j-1}\}\). Taking covariance between the structural equation for \(v_j\) and the predecessor vector, and using independence of its innovation from predecessor innovations, gives
\[
 b_{e,P_jv_j}=S_{e,P_jP_j}^{-1}S_{e,P_jv_j},
 \qquad
 \omega_{e,v_j}=S_{e,v_jv_j}
 -S_{e,v_jP_j}S_{e,P_jP_j}^{-1}S_{e,P_jv_j}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The inverses exist by the preceding positivity calculation. If the edge from an earlier vertex to \(v_j\) were absent, its entry in the first expression in (1) would be zero. For \(j=2\), this would force the corresponding marginal covariance to be zero. For \(j=3\), multiplying the two-predictor normal equations out shows that a zero coefficient is equivalent to the corresponding one-variable partial covariance being zero, because the remaining conditional predictor variance is positive. The preceding table excludes both possibilities. Hence every environment DAG inducing either \(S_e\) is the complete DAG for its order, and (1) uniquely determines all of its coefficients and innovation variances.

Applying (1) to the six possible orders gives the following exhaustive table. Each variance triple is listed in the order named in the first column.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{order}&(\omega_{1,v_1},\omega_{1,v_2},\omega_{1,v_3})
             &(\omega_{2,v_1},\omega_{2,v_2},\omega_{2,v_3})\\ \hline
 XWY&(1,\frac{91}{100},\frac{94}{125})
     &(1,\frac{91}{100},\frac{94}{125})\\
 XYW&(1,\frac{1971}{2500},\frac{8554}{9855})
     &(1,\frac{2699}{2500},\frac{8554}{13495})\\
 WXY&(1,\frac{91}{100},\frac{94}{125})
     &(1,\frac{91}{100},\frac{94}{125})\\
 WYX&(1,\frac{561}{625},\frac{4277}{5610})
     &(1,\frac{7611}{10000},\frac{34216}{38055})\\
 YXW&(1,\frac{1971}{2500},\frac{8554}{9855})
     &(\frac{543}{500},\frac{2699}{2715},\frac{8554}{13495})\\
 YWX&(1,\frac{561}{625},\frac{4277}{5610})
     &(\frac{543}{500},\frac{2537}{3620},\frac{34216}{38055}).
\end{array}
\]
Because the same order labels the same vertices in both columns, equality of the two displayed triples is precisely coordinatewise innovation-variance invariance. The table therefore leaves exactly \(X\prec W\prec Y\) and \(W\prec X\prec Y\). For the first order, the invariant edge coefficient is
\[
 B_{e,XW}=\frac{S_{e,XW}}{S_{e,XX}}=\frac3{10},
\]
and for the second it is \(B_{e,WX}=3/10\). In either order the two predecessors of \(Y\) are \(X,W\), so (1) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 B_{e,XY}
 &=\frac{S_{e,XY}S_{e,WW}-S_{e,WY}S_{e,XW}}
         {S_{e,XX}S_{e,WW}-S_{e,XW}^2},\\
 B_{e,WY}
 &=\frac{S_{e,WY}S_{e,XX}-S_{e,XY}S_{e,XW}}
         {S_{e,XX}S_{e,WW}-S_{e,XW}^2}.
\end{split}
\tag{2}
\]
The common denominator in (2) is \(91/100>0\). Substitution yields
\[
 (B_{1,XY},B_{1,WY})=(\tfrac25,\tfrac15),
 \qquad
 (B_{2,XY},B_{2,WY})=(-\tfrac1{10},\tfrac35).
 \tag{3}
\]
The last innovation variance is also \(\det(S_e)/(91/100)=94/125\), agreeing with the table.

We verify the class and ledger requirements rather than inferring them from the construction. For either surviving order, take the complete ordered DAG as \(G^*=G_1=G_2\), use (2)--(3) and the invariant coefficient \(3/10\) for \(B_1,B_2\), put \(B^*=B_1\), let \(\mathcal I_1=\varnothing\), and let \(\mathcal I_2=\{Y\}\). Choose in each environment independent centered Gaussian innovations with the positive variances in the first or third row of the table. Equation (1) then proves that the induced covariance is exactly \(S_e\), so the linear structural-equation assumption holds by construction. Parent containment holds with equality. Modularity holds because the mechanisms of \(X\) and \(W\) are unchanged, while \(Y\) is the sole target. The innovation laws are independent Gaussian, their variances are positive, and the table proves cross-environment invariance. It remains only to verify diff-faithfulness.

Let \(C\) denote the third vertex in each row. Direct evaluation of the regression definition gives the complete equality ledger
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 (A,B)&(r_1(A,B\mid\varnothing),r_2(A,B\mid\varnothing))
 &(r_1(A,B\mid C),r_2(A,B\mid C))\\ \hline
 (X,W)&(\frac3{10},\frac3{10})&(\frac{191}{1122},\frac{934}{2537})\\
 (W,X)&(\frac3{10},\frac3{10})&(\frac{382}{1971},\frac{1401}{5398})\\
 (X,Y)&(\frac{23}{50},\frac{40}{543})&(\frac{455}{1122},-\frac{910}{7611})\\
 (Y,X)&(\frac{23}{50},\frac2{25})&(\frac25,-\frac1{10})\\
 (W,Y)&(\frac8{25},\frac{95}{181})&(\frac{455}{1971},\frac{1365}{2699})\\
 (Y,W)&(\frac8{25},\frac{57}{100})&(\frac15,\frac35).
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
Every denominator in (4) is a positive marginal or conditional variance, as required by the definition of \(r_e\). Thus equality occurs exactly for the two directions of the pair \(X,W\) with empty conditioning set.

By (3), both coefficients into \(Y\) change and the coefficient on the ordered \(X\)--\(W\) edge does not. The definition of the difference DAG therefore gives
\[
 D(M)=X\longrightarrow Y\longleftarrow W.
 \tag{5}
\]
For \(X,W\) with empty conditioning set, the direct \(X\)--\(W\) path contains no difference edge and has no non-endpoint vertex, so it is not diff-relevant; none of the three triggers for conditional diff-relevance can hold when the conditioning set is empty. The other simple path is \(X\to Y\leftarrow W\). It contains difference edges, but it is blocked at the unconditioned collider \(Y\). Hence \(X\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}W\mid\varnothing\). Conditioning on \(Y\) opens that collider path, so \(X\not\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}W\mid Y\). For the pairs \(X,Y\) and \(W,Y\), the direct path is a difference edge and has no internal vertex, so it is unblocked for either allowed conditioning set. These are all ordered pairs and conditioning sets when \(p=3\). Comparison with (4) proves the required equality if and only if diff-separation equivalence, hence diff-faithfulness. All defining conditions of \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\) have now been checked.

Under the population LDiffPC construction, (4) removes precisely the \(X\)--\(W\) adjacency and records the empty separating set for that pair. It removes neither \(X\)--\(Y\) nor \(W\)--\(Y\). Thus \(X-Y-W\) is unshielded, and because \(Y\notin\varnothing\), the collider rule orients it as \(X\to Y\leftarrow W\); there is no further unoriented adjacency on which a Meek rule could act. Therefore both displayed representatives have the same complete ledger and the same collider-oriented \(H(s)\). Equations (5) and the definition of the completion fiber show that both representatives belong to \(\mathfrak F(\operatorname{vech}(\boldsymbol\Sigma^{\mathrm{col}}))\).

Finally, let \(\widetilde M\) be any member of this fiber. It belongs to \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\), induces \(S_1,S_2\), and has a common topological order. The nonvanishing covariance calculation above forces each of its environment DAGs to be complete. A complete acyclic graph has a unique topological order, so the order is common to the two environments. Formula (1) then makes its environment-specific coefficients and innovation variances the unique ordered Cholesky factors in the exhaustive table. Invariance restricts its order to the first or third row, and (3) therefore holds for \(\widetilde M\). This proves the asserted exhaustion at the level of environment-specific factorizations. It does not assert uniqueness of the auxiliary baseline data: for example, taking all vertices as intervention targets makes modularity vacuous and permits alternative compatible baseline coefficients without changing \(S_e\), \(D(M)\), or the environmental factorization.

Every member of the fiber consequently has
\[
 \tau(\widetilde M)
 =\left(\frac25,-\frac1{10},\frac25+\frac1{10}\right)
 =\left(\frac25,-\frac1{10},\frac12\right).
\]
The fiber is nonempty because it contains the two displayed representatives. Hence its set of effect triples is exactly this singleton, whose closure is itself. By the definition of the three-variable identified-effect set,
\[
 \Psi_3(\operatorname{vech}(\boldsymbol\Sigma^{\mathrm{col}}))
 =\left\{\left(\frac25,-\frac1{10},\frac12\right)\right\}.
\]
Every step used \(p=3\); no higher-dimensional embedding or fiber assertion follows.
