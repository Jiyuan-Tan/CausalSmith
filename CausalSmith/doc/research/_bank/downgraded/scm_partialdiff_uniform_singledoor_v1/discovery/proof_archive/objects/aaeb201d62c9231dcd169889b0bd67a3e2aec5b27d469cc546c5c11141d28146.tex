Put \(D=y+E\).  We first identify the exceptional translations.  By \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-stratification-sard}\), choose finite Whitney stratifications of \(C\) and \(\partial E\) by Nash manifolds, say \((S_j)_j\) and \((R_\ell)_\ell\).  For every pair of strata define the Nash map
\[
 F_{j\ell}:S_j\times R_\ell\longrightarrow\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad F_{j\ell}(c,e)=c-e.
\]
The product is a Nash manifold and the displayed map is the restriction of polynomial subtraction.  Hence \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-stratification-sard}\) says that its critical-value set \(B_{j\ell}\) is semialgebraic and has dimension strictly smaller than \(d\).  It consequently has empty interior and is contained in its boundary, so \(\text{lem:semialgebraic-boundary-null}\) gives Lebesgue measure zero.  Thus
\[
 B:=\bigcup_{j,\ell}B_{j\ell}
 \tag{1}
\]
is a semialgebraic Lebesgue-null set.

Fix \(y\notin B\).  If \(x\in C\cap(y+\partial E)\), choose the unique strata containing \(x\) and \(x-y\).  Since \(y=F_{j\ell}(x,x-y)\) is a regular value, the derivative of subtraction is onto, equivalently
\[
 T_xS_j+T_{x-y}R_\ell=\mathbb R^d.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here is the local consequence of (2) that is needed below.  In Nash charts at \(x\) and \(x-y\), choose a linear complement of \(T_{x-y}R_\ell\) inside the image of \(T_xS_j\).  Solving the corresponding normal-coordinate equations (the derivative in those selected coordinates is invertible by (2)) shows that the normal projection of \(S_j-y\) contains a neighborhood of the normal coordinate of \(R_\ell\).  The Whitney local product structure, together with \(E=\overline{\operatorname{int}E}\), then supplies points
\[
 x_r\in S_j\cap(y+\operatorname{int}E),
 \qquad x_r\longrightarrow x.
 \tag{3}
\]
For completeness, if \(R_\ell\) is zero-dimensional, (2) forces \(T_xS_j=\mathbb R^d\), so \(S_j\) is locally open and (3) follows directly from regular closedness of \(E\).  Points already in \(C\cap(y+\operatorname{int}E)\) need no approximation.  Therefore
\[
 C\cap D=\overline{C\cap\operatorname{int}D}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Now put \(A_n=\overline{C_n\cap D_n}\) and \(A=C\cap D\).  To prove the upper set limit, take any subsequence, still indexed by \(n\), and points \(x_n\in A_n\) with \(x_n\to x\).  Choose \(z_n\in C_n\cap D_n\) with \(\lVert z_n-x_n\rVert\le n^{-1}\).  Local Painlev\'e--Kuratowski upper convergence of \(C_n\), and the assumed inclusion \(\limsup_nD_n\subseteq D\), imply
\[
 x=\lim_nz_n\in C\cap D=A.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\limsup_nA_n\subseteq A\).

For the lower set limit, fix \(x\in A\).  By (4), choose \(x^{(r)}\in C\cap\operatorname{int}D\) with \(x^{(r)}\to x\).  Choose \(\varepsilon_r>0\) such that
\[
 K_r:=\overline B(x^{(r)},\varepsilon_r)
 \subseteq\operatorname{int}D.
 \tag{6}
\]
Local Painlev\'e--Kuratowski lower convergence gives \(x_n^{(r)}\in C_n\) with \(x_n^{(r)}\to x^{(r)}\).  For each fixed \(r\), eventually \(x_n^{(r)}\in K_r\), while the stated erosion hypothesis applied to the compact set \(K_r\) gives \(K_r\subseteq D_n\) eventually.  Hence \(x_n^{(r)}\in C_n\cap D_n\) eventually.  A diagonal choice, first in \(n\) and then in \(r\), produces points of \(A_n\) converging to \(x\), and therefore
\[
 A\subseteq\liminf_nA_n.
 \tag{7}
\]
Equations (5)--(7) prove Painlev\'e--Kuratowski, hence Fell, convergence \(A_n\to A\).

Finally, the common-compact-bound hypothesis places every sufficiently large \(A_n\) in one compact set.  If \(A=\varnothing\) and infinitely many \(A_n\) were nonempty, compactness would produce a convergent subsequence of selected points, contradicting (5); hence \(A_n=\varnothing\) eventually.  If \(A\ne\varnothing\), upper and lower convergence inside that common compact set are equivalent to Hausdorff convergence.  This proves all assertions.
