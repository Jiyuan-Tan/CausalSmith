Fix \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\) and an environment \(e\).  Acyclicity gives a permutation in which \(I-B_e^{\mathsf T}\) is triangular with unit diagonal.  Hence it is invertible, and \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\) imply
\[
 Z_e=(I-B_e^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\varepsilon_e
 \sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{M,e}),\qquad
 \Sigma_{M,e}=(I-B_e^{\mathsf T})^{-1}
          \operatorname{diag}(\omega)(I-B_e)^{-1}.
\]
The matrix is positive definite because \(\min_v\omega_v>0\).  By \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\), the \(n_e\) rows of the data matrix \(X_e\) are independent with this law.

Let \(H_e=I_{n_e}-n_e^{-1}{\bf 1}{\bf 1}^{\mathsf T}\), and choose an orthogonal \(Q_e\) satisfying
\[
 Q_e^{\mathsf T}H_eQ_e=\operatorname{diag}(0,I_{\nu_e}).
\]
Orthogonal invariance of a centered Gaussian vector with row covariance \(I_{n_e}\) shows directly from its characteristic function that the last \(\nu_e\) rows, say \(U_{e1},\ldots,U_{e\nu_e}\), of \(Q_e^{\mathsf T}X_e\) are independent \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{M,e})\) vectors.  Therefore
\[
 \nu_e\widehat\Sigma_e=X_e^{\mathsf T}H_eX_e
   =\sum_{j=1}^{\nu_e}U_{ej}U_{ej}^{\mathsf T}
   \sim\operatorname{Wishart}_p(\Sigma_{M,e},\nu_e),
\]
which is the defining Gaussian-sum construction of the Wishart law.  Independence of the two scatter matrices follows from independence of the two samples.

For \(S_e=\Sigma_{M,e}\), set
\[
 W_e=\Sigma_{M,e}^{-1/2}(\nu_e\widehat\Sigma_e)
                 \Sigma_{M,e}^{-1/2}.
\]
Then \(W_e\sim\operatorname{Wishart}_p(I_p,\nu_e)\), and similarity invariance of trace and determinant yields
\[
 T_{e,n}(\Sigma_{M,e})
 =\nu_e\left\{\operatorname{tr}(W_e/\nu_e)
      -\log\det(W_e/\nu_e)-p\right\}.
\]
Because \(n_e>p\), one has \(\nu_e\ge p\), so \(W_e\) is positive definite almost surely and has a density on the positive-definite cone.  To see directly that the displayed pivot has no atom, partition \(W_e=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&z^{\mathsf T}\\z&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).  Conditional on \((z,C)\), positive definiteness means \(x>z^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}z\), and the pivot is, up to a constant independent of \(x\),
\[
 x-\nu_e\log\{x-z^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}z\}.
\]
This strictly decreases and then strictly increases, so every level contains at most two values of \(x\).  Fubini's theorem and the matrix density show that every pivot level has probability zero.  Thus its distribution is continuous.  With \(a_\gamma=\sqrt{1-\gamma}\), the definition of \(q_{p,\nu_e}(a_\gamma)\) therefore gives
\[
 \Pr_M\{T_{e,n}(\Sigma_{M,e})\le q_{p,\nu_e}(a_\gamma)\}=a_\gamma.
\]
The two events are independent, and \(\text{def:covariance-region}\) is their product.  Thus
\[
 \Pr_M\{\boldsymbol\Sigma_M\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\}
 =a_\gamma^2=1-\gamma.
\]
