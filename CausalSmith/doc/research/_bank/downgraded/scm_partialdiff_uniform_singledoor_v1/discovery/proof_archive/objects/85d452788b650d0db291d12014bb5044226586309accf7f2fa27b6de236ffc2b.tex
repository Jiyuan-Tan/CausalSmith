Fix an order \(\pi\).  For a vertex \(v\), let \(P_\pi(v)\) be the vertices preceding \(v\), and, for a positive-definite covariance matrix \(\Sigma_e\), define the ordered regression vector and residual variance
\[
 \beta_{e,v}^{\pi}
   =\Sigma_{e,P_\pi(v),P_\pi(v)}^{-1}
      \Sigma_{e,P_\pi(v),v},\qquad
 q_{e,v}^{\pi}
   =\Sigma_{e,vv}-\Sigma_{e,v,P_\pi(v)}
      \Sigma_{e,P_\pi(v),P_\pi(v)}^{-1}
      \Sigma_{e,P_\pi(v),v}.
\]
Every principal submatrix in this display is positive definite and every Schur complement \(q_{e,v}^{\pi}\) is strictly positive.  Cramer's rule consequently makes all coordinates of \(\beta_{e,v}^{\pi}\) and \(q_{e,v}^{\pi}\) rational functions of the covariance entries, with products of positive principal minors as denominators.

Suppose first that \(M\in\mathfrak F(s)\) has common order \(\pi\).  Every predecessor of \(v\) is a function of innovations preceding \(v\), whereas \(\varepsilon_{e,v}\) is independent of all those innovations by \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\).  Taking covariances in the structural equation for \(v\) therefore gives the normal equations
\[
 \Sigma_{e,P_\pi(v),v}
 =\Sigma_{e,P_\pi(v),P_\pi(v)}c_{e,v},
\]
where \(c_{e,v,u}=B_{e,uv}\) for a parent \(u\) and \(c_{e,v,u}=0\) for every other predecessor.  Invertibility gives \(c_{e,v}=\beta_{e,v}^{\pi}\), and taking the residual variance gives \(\omega_v=q_{e,v}^{\pi}\).  Thus, once \((s,\pi)\) is fixed, both environment coefficient matrices and the common innovation variances are unique rational functions of \(s\).

There are finitely many choices of \(\pi\), the three DAGs \(G^*,G_1,G_2\), the target sets \(\mathcal I_1,\mathcal I_2\), a ledger truth table, and a compatible partially directed output.  Fix one such discrete choice.  Substitute the rational quantities above into the following finite list: the prescribed zero and nonzero coefficient conditions; the equalities \(q_{1,v}^{\pi}=q_{2,v}^{\pi}\); the baseline-parent and modularity equalities; every equality or disequality in the complete ledger; the equivalence required by diff-faithfulness for each of the finitely many triples \((A,B,S)\); and the skeleton and orientation checks against the deterministic LDiffPC output.  Conditions involving an unconstrained baseline coefficient can be eliminated explicitly: if some environment leaves the corresponding mechanism untargeted, set the baseline coefficient equal to that environment's rational coefficient and require equality with every other untargeted environment; if both environments target the mechanism, choose any baseline value compatible with the fixed baseline-edge case.  No continuous existential variable remains.

After multiplication by the positive principal-minor and conditional-variance denominators, each equality is polynomial.  Each disequality \(f\ne0\) is the disjoint union of the two strict polynomial inequalities \(f>0\) and \(-f>0\).  Splitting the finitely many signs produces an explicit quantifier-free semialgebraic cell \(\mathcal S_b\).  This proves both finiteness and the asserted effective description.

For the same branch, the direct coefficient is
\[
 \alpha_e=
 \begin{cases}
  (\beta_{e,Y}^{\pi})_X,&X\in P_\pi(Y),\ X\to Y\in G_e,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
and the branch map is \(\theta_b(s)=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_1-\alpha_2)\).  Hence every coordinate is a rational predecessor-set regression with a denominator that is positive, and in particular nonzero, on \(\mathcal S_b\).

Every model in \(\mathfrak F(s)\) selects one of these branches and has target \(\theta_b(s)\) by the normal-equation calculation.  Conversely, if \(s\in\mathcal S_b\), the ordered regressions, positive Schur complements, and the baseline choices just described construct structural equations satisfying every defining condition of \(\mathfrak F(s)\).  Thus the model has covariance \(s\) and target \(\theta_b(s)\).  At a fixed \(s\) there are only finitely many branch values, so their set is already closed.  Applying \(\text{def:identified-effect-set}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{graph}(\Psi)
 =\bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B_p}
   \{(s,\theta_b(s)):s\in\mathcal S_b\},
\]
as required.
