Fix \(B\ge1\), \(p\ge3\), \(n_1,n_2>p\), and \(0<\gamma<1/2\), and use the notation introduced in the statement.

First consider coverage.  If \(M\in\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}}\), then \(M\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\).  Therefore \(\text{lem:true-completion-retention}\) gives
\[
M\in\mathfrak F(s(M)).
\]
Membership of \(M\) in the bounded class then strengthens this to
\[
M\in\mathfrak F(s(M))\cap\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}},
\qquad
\tau(M)\in\Psi_B(s(M)).
\]
By \(\text{lem:wishart-pivot}\), the event
\[
E_M=\{\boldsymbol\Sigma_M\in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\}
\]
has probability exactly \(1-\gamma\).  On \(E_M\), the definition of \(\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma}\) includes the coordinate \(s(M)\) in its union, and hence
\[
E_M\subseteq
\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma}\}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\Pr_M\{\tau(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma}\}
\ge1-\gamma.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(M\in\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}}\) proves the finite-sample coverage assertion.  This is the same exact-pivot argument as in \(\text{thm:finite-sample-honesty}\), now with true-completion retention checked in the restricted fiber; it uses no separation or beta-min margin.

We next verify that the same-law witnesses required for the lower bound belong to the bounded class.  Specialize \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) and \(\text{thm:honesty-noncontraction-lower-bound}\) to
\[
a=B,
\qquad
b=1.
\]
These values lie in the chain--fork parameter space because \(B\ge1\).  In the chain model the only nonzero structural coefficients are \(B\) and \(1\).  In the fork model they are \(B/(B^2+1)\) and \(1\).  Since
\[
0<\frac{B}{B^2+1}\le B
\quad\text{and}\quad
1\le B,
\]
every environment coefficient in both models has absolute value at most \(B\).

The common three-dimensional covariance of the witnesses is
\[
\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(B,1)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&B&B\\
B&B^2+1&B^2+1\\
B&B^2+1&B^2+2
\end{pmatrix}
=LL^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
B&1&0\\
B&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The triangular matrix \(L\) has unit diagonal.  Hence \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(B,1)\) is positive definite and
\[
\det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(B,1)=(\det L)^2=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(B,1)=2B^2+4=K_B.
\]
Let \(0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3\) be its eigenvalues.  Positivity and the trace identity imply \(\lambda_j\le K_B\) for all \(j\).  The determinant identity then gives
\[
\lambda_1=\frac{1}{\lambda_2\lambda_3}
\ge\frac{1}{K_B^2}=\kappa_B.
\]
Thus
\[
\kappa_B I_3\preceq\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(B,1)
\preceq K_B I_3.
\]
For \(p>3\), \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) appends the identity block \(I_{p-3}\).  Since \(K_B\ge6\) and \(0<\kappa_B\le1\), the appended unit eigenvalues also lie in \([\kappa_B,K_B]\); for \(p=3\) the appended block is empty.  Therefore the full covariance in each environment of each witness obeys
\[
\kappa_B I_p\preceq
\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(B,1)\oplus I_{p-3}
\preceq K_B I_p.
\]
The established same-law theorem already proves that both witnesses are in \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\).  Together with the coefficient and covariance checks above, this proves
\[
M^{\mathrm{ch}},M^{\mathrm{fk}}
\in\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}}.
\]

Now let \(D\) be any rule in the minimax infimum.  The two witnesses induce the same law pair by \(\text{thm:honesty-noncontraction-lower-bound}\); \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\) therefore makes their complete sample laws identical.  If \(D\) uses an independent auxiliary randomizer, adjoining that same randomizer preserves equality of the two laws.  Denote the resulting common law by \(Q\).  The witness targets are
\[
t_{\mathrm{ch}}=(B,B,0),
\qquad
t_{\mathrm{fk}}=(0,0,0),
\qquad
\|t_{\mathrm{ch}}-t_{\mathrm{fk}}\|_2=\sqrt2 B.
\]
Because both witnesses belong to the bounded class, uniform coverage of \(D\) gives
\[
Q\{t_{\mathrm{ch}}\in D\}\ge1-\gamma,
\qquad
Q\{t_{\mathrm{fk}}\in D\}\ge1-\gamma.
\]
The elementary union bound yields
\[
Q\{t_{\mathrm{ch}},t_{\mathrm{fk}}\in D\}
\ge1-2\gamma.
\]
On this event the definition of diameter gives \(\operatorname{diam}(D)\ge\sqrt2 B\).  Since diameter is nonnegative, including when it takes the value \(+\infty\),
\[
\mathbb E_Q[\operatorname{diam}(D)]
\ge \sqrt2 B\,
Q\{\operatorname{diam}(D)\ge\sqrt2 B\}
\ge\sqrt2 B(1-2\gamma).
\]
Consequently
\[
\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}}}
\mathbb E_M[\operatorname{diam}(D)]
\ge\sqrt2 B(1-2\gamma).
\]
Taking the infimum over all admissible \(D\) proves the first minimax inequality.

For the upper bound, define the fixed target parallelogram
\[
\mathcal P_B
=\{(x_1,x_2,x_1-x_2): |x_1|\le B,\ |x_2|\le B\}.
\]
If \(M'\in\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}}\), then the coefficient restriction and the definition \(\alpha_e(M')=B_{e,XY}\) imply
\[
\tau(M')\in\mathcal P_B.
\]
The set \(\mathcal P_B\) is closed.  Hence the fiber closure in the definition of \(\Psi_B\), the union over the covariance region, and the final outer closure together imply, for every sample realization,
\[
\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma}\subseteq\mathcal P_B.
\]
For two points of \(\mathcal P_B\), write \(u=x_1-y_1\) and \(v=x_2-y_2\).  Then \(|u|,|v|\le2B\), and
\[
\|(x_1,x_2,x_1-x_2)-(y_1,y_2,y_1-y_2)\|_2^2
=u^2+v^2+(u-v)^2
\le24B^2.
\]
Equality is attained by \((u,v)=(2B,-2B)\), so \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal P_B)=2\sqrt6B\).  Under the frozen convention an empty confidence set has diameter zero; therefore, pointwise,
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma})
\le2\sqrt6B.
\]
Taking expectations and then the supremum over the bounded class proves the last inequality.  The coverage result already proved that \(\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma}\) is admissible for the minimax infimum, so evaluating that infimum at this rule proves the middle inequality.

Finally, write \(R^*_{n,\gamma,B}\) for the displayed minimax value.  The established lower and upper bounds give
\[
\sup_{M\in\mathcal M_{p,B}^{\mathrm{df}}}
\mathbb E_M[\operatorname{diam}(\widehat{\mathcal C}^{B}_{n,\gamma})]
\le2\sqrt6B
=\frac{2\sqrt3}{1-2\gamma}\,
  \sqrt2B(1-2\gamma)
\le\frac{2\sqrt3}{1-2\gamma}\,R^*_{n,\gamma,B}.
\]
This is the claimed finite-sample constant-factor statement.  Nothing in the argument identifies the exact minimizer or exact minimax value, and neither bound depends on a separation or beta-min condition.
