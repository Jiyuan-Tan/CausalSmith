Fix \(N\) and first take \(\mathcal C=\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{df}}\).  Set \(b=1\).  For every real \(a\neq0\), global honesty implies the two coverage inequalities required by \(\text{thm:honesty-noncontraction-lower-bound}\).  Under the common sample law \(Q_{N,a}\) of its two witnesses, that theorem gives
\[
 Q_{N,a}\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\ge\sqrt2|a|\right\}\ge1-2\gamma.
\]
For any nonnegative extended random variable \(T\) and any \(t>0\), the pointwise inequality \(T\ge t\mathbf1\{T\ge t\}\) implies \(\mathbb E[T]\ge t\Pr\{T\ge t\}\).  Apply this with \(T=\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\) and \(t=\sqrt2|a|\).  Since \(Q_{N,a}\) is the sample law under each witness and each witness belongs to \(\mathcal C\),
\[
 \sup_{M\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_M\!\left[\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\right]
 \ge \sqrt2|a|(1-2\gamma).
\]
The factor \(1-2\gamma\) is strictly positive because \(\gamma\in(0,1/2)\).  Letting \(|a|\to\infty\) proves that the supremum is infinite.

For \(\mathcal C=\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{BD}}\), the identical argument uses \(\text{thm:anchor-class-honesty-noncontraction}\) in place of the Gaussian-subclass theorem.  Thus every globally honest procedure has infinite worst-case expected diameter for either class.  The admissible collection is nonempty because the constant rule \(D_N=\mathbb R^3\) has coverage one and extended diameter infinity, so taking the infimum over globally honest rules still gives infinity.  Finally, the claimed finite and attained bounded-local value is exactly the conclusion \(v_R<\infty\) and attainment of \(\text{thm:local-minimax-attainment-characterization}\) for every finite positive \(R\).  Combining the two conclusions proves the elbow.
