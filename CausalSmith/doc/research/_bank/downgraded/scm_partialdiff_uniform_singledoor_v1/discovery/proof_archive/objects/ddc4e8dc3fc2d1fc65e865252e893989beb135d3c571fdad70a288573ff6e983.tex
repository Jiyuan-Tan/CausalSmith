Consider any sequence \((C_j)\) in \(\mathcal H(K)\).  If infinitely many terms are empty, that subsequence converges to the isolated empty set.  Otherwise discard finitely many terms and assume all \(C_j\) are nonempty.  Let \((x_r)_{r\ge1}\) be a countable dense subset of \(K\).  The numbers \(d(x_r,C_j)\) lie in the compact interval \([0,\operatorname{diam}(K)]\).  Successive subsequence extraction and a diagonal argument give a subsequence along which they converge for every \(r\).  Since every function \(x\mapsto d(x,C_j)\) is one-Lipschitz, a finite-net argument upgrades convergence on the dense set to uniform convergence on \(K\); call the limit \(f\).

Choose \(z_j\in C_j\).  Compactness gives a further subsequence with \(z_j\to z\in K\), and uniform convergence implies \(f(z)=0\).  Thus \(C=\{x\in K:f(x)=0\}\) is nonempty and closed.  The one-Lipschitz property gives \(f(x)\le d(x,C)\).  Conversely, choose \(z_j(x)\in C_j\) with \(\|x-z_j(x)\|=d(x,C_j)\), pass to a convergent subsequence, and use uniform convergence to see that its limit lies in \(C\); hence \(d(x,C)\le f(x)\).  Therefore \(f=d(\,cdot\,,C)\).  Uniform convergence of distance functions for nonempty compact sets is equivalent to Hausdorff convergence, since
\[
 d_H(C_j,C)=\sup_{x\in K}|d(x,C_j)-d(x,C)|.
\]
Every sequence thus has a convergent subsequence, and the metrizable hyperspace is compact.  Finally, for nonempty \(C,D\),
\[
 |\operatorname{diam}(C)-\operatorname{diam}(D)|\le2d_H(C,D).
\]
Continuity at the isolated empty set is automatic under the convention \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\).  This proves both assertions.
