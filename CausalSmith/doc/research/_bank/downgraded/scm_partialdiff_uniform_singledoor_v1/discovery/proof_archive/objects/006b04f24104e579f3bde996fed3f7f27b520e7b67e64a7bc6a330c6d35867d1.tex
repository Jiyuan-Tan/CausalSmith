Write \(s_N=s(M_N)\), \(\widehat s_N=\operatorname{vech}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\Sigma})\), and \(m=p(p+1)/2\).  We first derive the local covariance experiment.  For environment \(e\), replace the centered covariance temporarily by \(n_e^{-1}\sum_i Z_{ei}Z_{ei}^{\mathsf T}\).  The difference is \(\overline Z_e\overline Z_e^{\mathsf T}=O_{P_{M_N}}(n_e^{-1})\), uniformly along the displayed local sequence because \(\Sigma_{M_N,e}\to\Sigma_{0,e}\succ0\).  The half-vectorized summands are independent, centered, and have covariance
\[
 V(\Sigma_{M_N,e})_{AB,CD}
 =\Sigma_{M_N,e,AC}\Sigma_{M_N,e,BD}
  +\Sigma_{M_N,e,AD}\Sigma_{M_N,e,BC}.
\]
Their Gaussian fourth moments are locally bounded, so the Lindeberg condition in \(\text{lem:multivariate-lindeberg-feller}\) follows from Markov's inequality applied with any locally uniform moment of order \(2+\delta\).  Independence of the samples and \(n_1/N\to\lambda\in(0,1)\) then give
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat s_N-s_N)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbb Z_0,
 \qquad
 Y_N:=\sqrt N(\widehat s_N-s_0)\ \Rightarrow\ h+\mathbb Z_0.
 \tag{1}
\]
The covariance is exactly \(V_0\) from its definition.  It is positive definite: for a nonzero symmetric matrix \(A\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z^{\mathsf T}AZ)
 =2\operatorname{tr}\{(\Sigma_{0,e}^{1/2}A
             \Sigma_{0,e}^{1/2})^2\}>0,
\]
and both sample shares are positive.  Thus \(h+\mathbb Z_0\) has a Lebesgue density.

Next put
\[
 \mathcal A_N
 =\{u\in\mathbb R^d:
       \operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s_0+u/\sqrt N)
       \in\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\}.
\]
For \(g(A)=\operatorname{tr}(A)-\log\det(A)-p\), Taylor expansion at \(I_p\) gives
\[
 g(I_p+E)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(E^2)+O(\|E\|^3).
\]
Since \(s_0\) is positive definite, this expansion is uniform for \(u\) and \(Y_N\) in a fixed compact set.  Applied blockwise to \(T_{e,n}\), it yields the covariance-score quadratic
\[
 T_{e,n}(S_e(u_e))
 =(Y_{N,e}-u_e)^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}
          (Y_{N,e}-u_e)+o_{P_{M_N}}(1),
 \tag{2}
\]
uniformly on compact \(u_e\), where \(V_{0,1}=\lambda^{-1}V(\Sigma_{0,1})\) and \(V_{0,2}=(1-\lambda)^{-1}V(\Sigma_{0,2})\).  Evaluating (2) at the true covariance and using (1) shows that the pivot converges to \(\chi_m^2\).  Its distribution function is continuous and strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), hence
\[
 q_{p,\nu_e}(\sqrt{1-\gamma})
 \longrightarrow q_{\chi_m^2}(\sqrt{1-\gamma}).
\]
Moreover, \(g(A)\to\infty\) when a positive eigenvalue of \(A\) tends to zero or infinity.  Thus a bounded pivot sublevel forces \(S_e\) to be within \(O_{P_{M_N}}(N^{-1/2})\) of \(\widehat\Sigma_e\).  Equations (1)--(2) consequently imply, in the local Hausdorff topology and with stochastic boundedness globally,
\[
 \mathcal A_N\ \Rightarrow\
 \mathcal A_\gamma(h+\mathbb Z_0).
 \tag{3}
\]

For a branch \(b\), set
\[
 C_{b,N}=\sqrt N(\overline{\mathcal S_b}-s_0),\qquad
 Q_{b,N}=\overline{\sqrt N\{(\mathcal S_b\cap\mathcal R_{n,\gamma})-s_0\}}.
\]
Here the covariance region is read in half-vector coordinates.  If \(b\notin\mathbb B(s_0)\), a neighborhood of \(s_0\) misses \(\overline{\mathcal S_b}\); stochastic boundedness in (3) therefore removes that branch with probability tending to one.  If \(b\in\mathbb B(s_0)\), \(\text{ass:tangent-cone-regularity}\) says
\[
 C_{b,N}\longrightarrow T_b(s_0)
 \tag{4}
\]
locally in the Painleve--Kuratowski topology.

We record why intersections in (3)--(4) are almost surely stable.  By \(\text{thm:finite-branch-representation}\) and \(\text{lem:tarski-seidenberg-projection}\), each tangent cone is semialgebraic.  Write the limiting score body as \(\mathcal A_\gamma(y)=y+E_\gamma\), where \(E_\gamma\) is a compact full-dimensional product ellipsoid.  The translation-exception set in \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\) has Lebesgue measure zero.  There are finitely many branches, and the Gaussian center in (1) has a density, so with probability one every branch intersection is stable.

Each semialgebraic cell \(\mathcal S_b\) is dense in its closure.  On the probability-one transverse-intersection event, this density, \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\), (3), and (4) prove
\[
 Q_{b,N}
 \longrightarrow
 Q_b(y):=T_b(s_0)\cap\mathcal A_\gamma(y).
 \tag{5}
\]
Every convergent sequence from the left has its limit in both right-hand sets by (3)--(4).  For the reverse inclusion, the generic-stability lemma first approximates any boundary point of the right side by cone points in the interior of the limiting score body; (4) approximates those cone points, and (3) keeps the resulting points in \(\mathcal A_N\).  If the right side is empty, compactness and the upper parts of (3)--(4) make the left side empty on every fixed bounding ball eventually.  Compactness of the score bodies upgrades (5) to Hausdorff convergence whenever the limit is nonempty and to Fell convergence when it is empty.  This deterministic argument may be applied after the representation in \(\text{lem:skorokhod-representation}\) to (1)--(3), and therefore yields convergence in distribution in the original experiment.

It remains to map the branch intersections to the causal target.  The finite-branch representation and the outer closure in \(\text{def:confidence-union}\) give the finite union of the closed images of \(\mathcal S_b\cap\mathcal R_{n,\gamma}\).  By \(\text{ass:branch-smoothness}\), uniformly for bounded \(u\),
\[
 \theta_b(s_0+u/\sqrt N)=\theta_b(s_0)+o(1),
 \tag{6}
\]
and hence (5), finite union, and (6) give
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}
 \Rightarrow
 \{\theta_b(s_0):b\in\mathbb B(s_0),\ Q_b(h+\mathbb Z_0)\ne\varnothing\}
 =\mathcal K_h^{(0)}
\]
in the Fell topology.

If \(\Gamma(s_0)=\{\tau_0\}\), then \(\theta_b(s_0)=\tau_0\) for every accessible branch and the uniform first-order expansion is
\[
 \sqrt N\{\theta_b(s_0+u/\sqrt N)-\tau_0\}
 =D\theta_b(s_0)u+o(1).
 \tag{7}
\]
Combining (5) and (7) gives
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}-\tau_0)
 \Rightarrow
 \bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}
 \{D\theta_b(s_0)u:u\in Q_b(h+\mathbb Z_0)\}
 =\mathcal K_h^{(1)}
\]
in the Fell topology.  On every continuity realization for which the limiting union is nonempty, the same argument gives Hausdorff convergence of compact sets.  The Fell formulation is necessary without conditioning because the score body can miss all accessible cones with positive probability, in which case the limiting set is empty.
