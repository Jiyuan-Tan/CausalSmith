Fix an integer \(p\ge3\) and \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(a\neq0\) and \(b\neq0\).  Let \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\) and \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\) be the two paired SCMs in the corrected construction \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\), and write \(U=(U_1,\ldots,U_{p-3})\).  For \(p=3\), this is the empty tuple and \(I_{p-3}=I_0\) is the empty covariance block.  We verify the observational law, every condition defining \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), and the confidence lower bound.

For the chain, the structural equations and mutual independence of the unit-variance innovations give, in either environment,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=a,
 &\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=ab,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),
 &\operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, \(Y=a\varepsilon_X+\varepsilon_Y\) and \(W=ab\varepsilon_X+b\varepsilon_Y+\varepsilon_W\), so each displayed identity follows by bilinearity of covariance and independence.  Thus the \((X,Y,W)\) block is
\[
 \Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&a&ab\\
  a&a^2+1&b(a^2+1)\\
  ab&b(a^2+1)&b^2(a^2+1)+1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
With
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\a&1&0\\ab&b&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
direct multiplication gives \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=LL^{\mathsf T}\).  The matrix \(L\) is invertible because it is triangular with unit diagonal.  Hence, for every nonzero \(q\in\mathbb R^3\),
\[
 q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)q
 =\lVert L^{\mathsf T}q\rVert_2^2>0,
 \qquad
 \det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=(\det L)^2=1.
\]
Consequently the block is positive definite.  Each \(U_j\) is an independent standard Gaussian and is independent of \((X,Y,W)\), by the corrected construction.  The full chain covariance is therefore
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)
 =\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\oplus I_{p-3},
\]
which is positive definite and has determinant one.  In particular, its centered Gaussian law has full support \(\mathbb R^p\).

For the fork, put \(c=a/(a^2+1)\).  Since \(a^2+1>0\), every displayed innovation variance is strictly positive.  Independence and the fork equations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=c(a^2+1)=a,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=c^2(a^2+1)+(a^2+1)^{-1}=1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=b\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=ab,
 &\operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus its \((X,Y,W)\) covariance is the same \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\).  Its variables \(U_j\) are again independent standard Gaussians independent of that block, so its full covariance is also \(\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)\).  In either construction every observed variable is a linear combination of mutually independent centered Gaussian innovations.  Therefore each environment law is \(\mathcal N(0,\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b))\), and the two paired models have the same asserted law pair.

We next check membership in \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\) from \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\).  The chain graph has only \(X\to Y\to W\), the fork graph has only \(Y\to X\) and \(Y\to W\), and no edge is incident to any \(U_j\).  The common orders declared in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) are topological orders, so both baseline graphs and both environment graphs are acyclic.  The displayed equations are exactly the linear structural equations in \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\).  In each paired model the baseline graph and coefficient matrix equal the common environment graph and coefficient matrix, so \(\operatorname{Pa}_{G_e}(v)=\operatorname{Pa}_{G^*}(v)\) for every \(e\) and \(v\), which proves \(\text{ass:baseline-parent-containment}\).  Since \(\mathcal I_1=\mathcal I_2=\varnothing\) and every environment mechanism equals its baseline mechanism, \(\text{ass:modular-interventions}\) holds.  Mutual independence and the displayed Gaussian innovation laws prove \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\).  The two environments within each paired model use exactly the same variance vector, proving \(\text{ass:invariant-innovation-variances}\); all its entries are positive, proving \(\text{ass:positive-innovation-variances}\).

It remains to prove diff-faithfulness for all \(p\)-dimensional conditioning configurations.  In either paired model \(B_1=B_2\).  Hence \(\text{def:difference-dag}\) gives
\[
 D(M)=\varnothing.
\]
Fix arbitrary distinct ordered vertices \(A,B\in\mathbb V\) and an arbitrary \(S\subseteq\mathbb V\setminus\{A,B\}\).  Because \(D(M)\) has no edge, a path can neither contain an edge of \(D(M)\) nor contain a vertex having an ancestor with an incoming edge in \(D(M)\).  Thus no path is diff-relevant under \(\text{def:diff-relevant-path}\).  Each of the three alternatives in \(\text{def:conditionally-diff-relevant-path}\) also requires an incoming edge in \(D(M)\), so none is satisfied.  Therefore \(S\) blocks every path of either designated type, vacuously when no path exists, and \(\text{def:diff-separation}\) yields
\[
 A\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}B\mid S.
\]

For the regression side, the two environment covariance matrices within the paired model are equal to the same positive-definite matrix \(\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)\).  To justify division in \(r_e(A,B\mid S)\), let \(K=\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{SS}\).  If \(S\neq\varnothing\), then \(K\) is positive definite because extending a nonzero vector on \(S\) by zeros gives a nonzero vector in \(\mathbb R^p\); hence \(K\) is invertible.  With
\[
 v=\bigl(1,-K^{-1}\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{SB}\bigr)
\]
in coordinates \((B,S)\), positive definiteness of the corresponding principal submatrix gives
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_{p,BB\cdot S}(a,b)
 =v^{\mathsf T}\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{\{B\}\cup S,\{B\}\cup S}v>0.
\]
For \(S=\varnothing\), the same conclusion is simply \(\widetilde\Sigma_{p,BB}(a,b)>0\).  Thus every partial-regression denominator is positive.  Equality of the two covariance matrices then gives, for this arbitrary triple,
\[
 r_1(A,B\mid S)=r_2(A,B\mid S).
\]
Both sides of the equivalence in \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) are true.  Since \((A,B,S)\) was arbitrary, diff-faithfulness holds for every ordered pair and eligible conditioning set, including sets containing any of the isolated variables.  All conditions in \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\) have now been verified, so
\[
 M^{\mathrm{ch}},M^{\mathrm{fk}}\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}.
\]

The equality \(B_1=B_2\) already proved that the difference DAG of each model is empty.  Moreover, the two models have exactly the same covariance coordinate, and \(\text{def:exact-ledger}\) makes \(\mathcal L(s)\) a deterministic record of that coordinate.  Hence their complete ledgers, including every stored separating set, coincide.  Within either model every cross-environment regression difference is zero by the preceding calculation, so every equality entry in this complete ledger records equality.  The hypotheses of \(\text{lem:ldiffpc-population-soundness}\) hold by the just-proved diff-faithfulness and the exact ledger.  That lemma identifies the population output skeleton with the true difference-DAG skeleton and permits only true orientations.  Since the true skeleton is empty, the population LDiffPC output is empty for both models.

In \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\), the coefficient on \(X\to Y\) is \(a\) in each environment.  In \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\), that arrow is absent, so its coefficient is zero in each environment.  The definitions of \(\alpha_e\) and \(\tau\) give
\[
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})=(a,a,0),\qquad
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})=(0,0,0),
\]
and the Euclidean distance between these points is \(\sqrt{a^2+a^2}=\sqrt2|a|\).

Finally fix any admissible sample-size pair and its total \(N\).  By \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\) and equality of both environment laws, the complete sample distributions under the two witnesses coincide; denote the common distribution by \(Q_N\).  Define
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ch}}=\{(a,a,0)\in D_N\},\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{fk}}=\{(0,0,0)\in D_N\}.
\]
Coverage at the two models gives \(Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}})\ge1-\gamma\) and \(Q_N(E_{\mathrm{fk}})\ge1-\gamma\).  The union bound therefore yields
\[
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}\cap E_{\mathrm{fk}})
 \ge1-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}^c)-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{fk}}^c)
 \ge1-2\gamma.
\]
On this intersection, \(D_N\) contains both target points, so its extended diameter is at least \(\sqrt2|a|\).  Hence
\[
 Q_N\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\ge\sqrt2|a|\right\}\ge1-2\gamma.
\]
The argument applies to every admissible \(N\), completing the proof.
