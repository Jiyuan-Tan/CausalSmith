Fix an integer \(p\ge3\) and \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(a\neq0\) and \(b\neq0\). Let \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\) and \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\) be the paired SCMs of \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\), and write \(U=(U_1,\ldots,U_{p-3})\). When \(p=3\), this is the empty tuple and \(I_{p-3}=I_0\) is the empty covariance block.

First consider \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\). In either environment, \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) gives
\[
X=\varepsilon_X,\qquad Y=a\varepsilon_X+\varepsilon_Y,
\qquad W=ab\varepsilon_X+b\varepsilon_Y+\varepsilon_W,
\]
where the displayed innovations are independent, centered, and have variance one. Bilinearity of covariance and independence therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=a,
 &\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=ab,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),
 &\operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the \((X,Y,W)\) covariance block is \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\). More explicitly, if
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\a&1&0\\ab&b&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
then direct multiplication gives
\[
 \Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=LL^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The matrix \(L\) is triangular with unit diagonal and hence invertible. Consequently, for every nonzero \(q\in\mathbb R^3\),
\[
 q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)q
 =\lVert L^{\mathsf T}q\rVert_2^2>0,
 \qquad
 \det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=(\det L)^2=1.
\]
Therefore \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\) is positive definite. Every \(U_j\) is, by \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\), an independent standard Gaussian independent of \((X,Y,W)\). Hence the full covariance matrix is
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)
 :=\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\oplus I_{p-3},
\]
which is positive definite.

For \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\), put \(c=a/(a^2+1)\), which is well defined because \(a^2+1>0\). The fork equations and innovation variances in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) give
\[
Y=\eta_Y,\qquad X=cY+\eta_X,
\qquad W=bY+\eta_W,
\]
with mutually independent centered Gaussian innovations satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_Y)=a^2+1,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\eta_X)=(a^2+1)^{-1},
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\eta_W)=1.
\]
All three variances are strictly positive. Independence yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=c(a^2+1)=a,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=c^2(a^2+1)+(a^2+1)^{-1}=1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=b\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=ab,
 &\operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the fork has the same \((X,Y,W)\) covariance \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\). Its appended variables are again independent standard Gaussians independent of that block, so its full covariance is also \(\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)\). In both constructions every observed vector is a linear transformation of mutually independent centered Gaussian innovations. Therefore, in each environment and for each of the two models, the observed law is
\[
 \mathcal N\!\left(0,\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\oplus I_{p-3}\right).
\]
This proves the asserted equality of law pairs and also supplies the positivity needed for every covariance inversion below.

We next verify membership in \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), whose conditions are listed in \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\). The chain graph contains only \(X\to Y\to W\), the fork graph contains only \(Y\to X\) and \(Y\to W\), and no edge is incident to an appended \(U_j\). The orders specified by \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) are topological, so the baseline and environment graphs are acyclic. The displayed structural equations are precisely those required by \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\). In each paired model the baseline graph and coefficient matrix equal the graph and coefficient matrix used in both environments. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Pa}_{G_e}(v)=\operatorname{Pa}_{G^*}(v)
\]
for every \(e\in\mathbb E\) and \(v\in\mathbb V\), which implies \(\text{ass:baseline-parent-containment}\). Because \(\mathcal I_1=\mathcal I_2=\varnothing\) and every environment mechanism equals its baseline mechanism, \(\text{ass:modular-interventions}\) holds. The innovation laws in the construction verify \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\). Within either paired model the two environments use the same displayed innovation-variance vector, verifying \(\text{ass:invariant-innovation-variances}\), and every coordinate of that vector is positive, verifying \(\text{ass:positive-innovation-variances}\).

It remains to verify \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) for every ordered pair and every eligible conditioning set. In either paired model \(B_1=B_2\). Hence \(\text{def:difference-dag}\) gives
\[
 D(M)=\varnothing.
\]
Fix distinct ordered vertices \(A,B\in\mathbb V\) and \(S\subseteq\mathbb V\setminus\{A,B\}\). Because \(D(M)\) has no edges, no path can contain an edge of \(D(M)\), and no vertex can have an ancestor with an incoming edge in \(D(M)\). Thus \(\text{def:diff-relevant-path}\) designates no path as diff-relevant. Each of the three alternatives in \(\text{def:conditionally-diff-relevant-path}\) likewise requires an incoming edge in \(D(M)\), so none can occur. Every diff-relevant or conditionally diff-relevant path is therefore blocked vacuously, and \(\text{def:diff-separation}\) gives
\[
 A\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}B\mid S.
\]

For the regression-equality side, the two environment covariance matrices within the paired model are the same positive-definite matrix \(\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)\). If \(S\neq\varnothing\), its principal submatrix
\[
 K:=\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{SS}
\]
is positive definite: extending any nonzero vector on \(S\) by zeros produces a nonzero vector in \(\mathbb R^p\). Thus \(K\) is invertible. In coordinates \((B,S)\), define
\[
 v:=\left(1,-K^{-1}\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{SB}\right).
\]
The positive definiteness of the principal submatrix on \(\{B\}\cup S\) gives
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_{p,BB\cdot S}(a,b)
 =v^{\mathsf T}
 \widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{\{B\}\cup S,\{B\}\cup S}v>0.
\]
If \(S=\varnothing\), the denominator is simply \(\widetilde\Sigma_{p,BB}(a,b)>0\). Thus the division in \(r_e(A,B\mid S)\) is valid in all cases. Equality of the two environment covariance matrices now implies
\[
 r_1(A,B\mid S)=r_2(A,B\mid S).
\]
Both sides of the equivalence in \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) are true. Since \((A,B,S)\) was arbitrary, diff-faithfulness holds for every ordered pair and eligible conditioning set, including sets containing appended isolated variables. We have verified every condition in \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\), so
\[
 M^{\mathrm{ch}},M^{\mathrm{fk}}\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}.
\]

The equality \(B_1=B_2\) already proves that each model has empty difference DAG. The two models have the same covariance coordinate, and \(\text{def:exact-ledger}\) constructs \(\mathcal L(s)\) deterministically from that coordinate, so their complete equality and sepset ledgers coincide. Within either model, the preceding regression calculation shows that every cross-environment regression difference appearing in the complete ledger equals zero. The hypotheses of \(\text{lem:ldiffpc-population-soundness}\) hold because diff-faithfulness has just been proved and the ledger supplies perfect equality information. That lemma identifies the output skeleton with the true difference-DAG skeleton and permits only true orientations. Since the true skeleton is empty, the population LDiffPC output is empty for each model.

In \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\), the structural coefficient on \(X\to Y\) equals \(a\) in both environments. In \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\), that arrow is absent, so its coefficient is zero in both environments. The definitions of \(\alpha_e\) and \(\tau\) therefore give
\[
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})=(a,a,0),
 \qquad
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})=(0,0,0).
\]
Their Euclidean distance is
\[
 \left\|(a,a,0)-(0,0,0)\right\|_2=\sqrt2\lvert a\rvert.
\]

Finally, fix any admissible sample-size pair and let \(N=n_1+n_2\). By \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\) and the proved equality of both environment laws, the complete sample distributions under the two witnesses coincide; call the common distribution \(Q_N\). Define
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ch}}:=\{(a,a,0)\in D_N\},
 \qquad
 E_{\mathrm{fk}}:=\{(0,0,0)\in D_N\}.
\]
The assumed coverage at the two models gives
\[
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}})\ge1-\gamma,
 \qquad
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{fk}})\ge1-\gamma.
\]
Taking complements and using subadditivity yields
\[
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}\cap E_{\mathrm{fk}})
 =1-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}^c\cup E_{\mathrm{fk}}^c)
 \ge1-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}^c)-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{fk}}^c)
 \ge1-2\gamma.
\]
On \(E_{\mathrm{ch}}\cap E_{\mathrm{fk}}\), the set \(D_N\) contains both target points, so its Euclidean diameter is at least \(\sqrt2\lvert a\rvert\). Consequently
\[
 Q_N\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\ge\sqrt2\lvert a\rvert\right\}
 \ge1-2\gamma.
\]
This holds for every admissible \(N\), which proves the theorem.
