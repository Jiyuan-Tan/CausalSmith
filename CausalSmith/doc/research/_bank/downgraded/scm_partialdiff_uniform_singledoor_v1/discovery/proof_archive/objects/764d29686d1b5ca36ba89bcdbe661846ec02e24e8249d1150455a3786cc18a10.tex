If \(s_0\) lies in the relative interior of one branch cell and \(\mathbb B(s_0)=\{b_0\}\), then, for \(y=h+\mathbb Z_0\), \(\mathcal K_h^{(0)}=\{\theta_{b_0}(s_0)\}\) when \(T_{b_0}(s_0)\cap\mathcal A_{\gamma}(y)\neq\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal K_h^{(0)}=\varnothing\) otherwise, while \(\mathcal K_h^{(1)}=\mathcal P_{\gamma}(y;b_0,s_0)=J\bigl(T_{b_0}(s_0)\cap\mathcal A_{\gamma}(y)\bigr)\), where \(J=D\theta_{b_0}(s_0)=(J_1,J_2)\). If, in addition, \(s_0\) lies in the ambient interior of \(\mathcal S_{b_0}\), then \(T_{b_0}(s_0)=\mathbb R^d\) and \(\mathcal P_{\gamma}(y;b_0,s_0)=J\mathcal A_{\gamma}(y)=J_1\mathcal A_{1,\gamma}(y_1)\oplus J_2\mathcal A_{2,\gamma}(y_2)\); if \(\mathcal A_{e,\gamma}(y_e)=y_e+E_{e,\gamma}\), this equals \(Jy+J_1E_{1,\gamma}\oplus J_2E_{2,\gamma}\). The ambient-interior image is generally a Minkowski sum rather than a joint Wald ellipsoid. Because every branch target satisfies \(\tau_3=\tau_1-\tau_2\), \(\operatorname{rank}(J)\le2\); no rank assumption is required.
