Write \(s_N=s(M_N)\), \(\widehat s_N=\operatorname{vech}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\Sigma})\), \(m=p(p+1)/2\), and \(\lambda_1=\lambda\), \(\lambda_2=1-\lambda\).  All probability statements below are under the triangular law generated by \(M_N\).  The local-sequence condition gives \(s_N\to s_0\); because \(s_0\in\Omega_p\), both limiting covariance matrices are positive definite.

For environment \(e\), replacing the centered covariance with \(n_e^{-1}\sum_iZ_{ei}Z_{ei}^{\mathsf T}\) changes its half-vector by \(O_{P_{M_N}}(n_e^{-1})\).  Indeed, the centering term is \(\overline Z_e\overline Z_e^{\mathsf T}=O_{P_{M_N}}(n_e^{-1})\), and replacing \(n_e^{-1}\) by \(\nu_e^{-1}\) has the same order; these claims use \(\Sigma_{M_N,e}\to\Sigma_{0,e}\succ0\).  The half-vectorized centered quadratic summands have covariance
\[
 V(\Sigma_{M_N,e})_{AB,CD}
 =\Sigma_{M_N,e,AC}\Sigma_{M_N,e,BD}
  +\Sigma_{M_N,e,AD}\Sigma_{M_N,e,BC}.
\]
Gaussian moments of every fixed order are locally bounded.  Thus Markov's inequality verifies the Lindeberg condition in \(\text{lem:multivariate-lindeberg-feller}\).  Independence of the two samples from \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\), the balance condition \(n_1/N\to\lambda\in(0,1)\), and the local-sequence expansion now give
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat s_N-s_N)\Rightarrow\mathbb Z_0,
 \qquad
 Y_N:=\sqrt N(\widehat s_N-s_0)\Rightarrow y:=h+\mathbb Z_0. \tag{1}
\]
The block covariance in (1) is \(V_0\) by definition.  It is positive definite.  Indeed, if \(A\) is a nonzero symmetric matrix, then
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z^{\mathsf T}AZ)
 =2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(\Sigma_{0,e}^{1/2}A\Sigma_{0,e}^{1/2})^2\right\}>0,
\]
and \(0<\lambda_e<1\).  Hence \(y\) has a Lebesgue density on \(\mathbb R^d\).

We next establish the exact set convergence needed for intersection stability.  For a deterministic center \(z=(z_1,z_2)\), let
\[
 \mathcal A_N(z)=\left\{u:\ S_e(u_e):=
 \operatorname{vech}^{-1}(s_{0,e}+u_e/\sqrt N)\succ0,\quad
 T_{e,n}\bigl(S_e(u_e)\bigr)\le q_{p,\nu_e}(\sqrt{1-\gamma}),\ e=1,2\right\},
\]
where the pivot is evaluated at the sample covariance having local coordinate \(z\).  Thus the rescaled realized covariance region is \(\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\).

Let \(g(A)=\operatorname{tr}(A)-\log\det(A)-p\).  Taylor's formula at \(I_p\) gives
\[
 g(I_p+H)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(H^2)+O(\lVert H\rVert^3). \tag{2}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(s_0\), \(\nu_e/N\to\lambda_e\), and (2) imply the locally uniform expansion
\[
 \sup_{(u_e,z_e)\in K}
 \left|T_{e,n}\bigl(S_e(u_e);z_e\bigr)
 -(z_e-u_e)^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}(z_e-u_e)\right|\longrightarrow0 \tag{3}
\]
for every compact \(K\), where
\(V_{0,1}=\lambda^{-1}V(\Sigma_{0,1})\) and
\(V_{0,2}=(1-\lambda)^{-1}V(\Sigma_{0,2})\).  Formula (3) includes the factor \(1/2\) in (2): in half-vector coordinates the inverse of \(V(\Sigma_{0,e})\) represents the quadratic form \(H\mapsto\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\{(\Sigma_{0,e}^{-1}H)^2\}\).

At the true covariance, \(\text{lem:wishart-pivot}\), (1), and (3) imply convergence of each exact pivot to \(\chi_m^2\).  The \(\chi_m^2\) distribution function is continuous and strictly increasing at its \(\sqrt{1-\gamma}\)-quantile, so quantile convergence gives
\[
 q_{p,\nu_e}(\sqrt{1-\gamma})\longrightarrow
 q_\infty:=q_{\chi_m^2}(\sqrt{1-\gamma}). \tag{4}
\]
Define the compact product ellipsoid
\[
 E_\gamma=\prod_{e=1}^2
 \{v_e:v_e^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}v_e\le q_\infty\}.
\]
Then \(\mathcal A_\gamma(z)=z+E_\gamma\).  The set \(E_\gamma\) is full-dimensional, semialgebraic, and regular closed because \(q_\infty>0\).

Apply \(\text{lem:skorokhod-representation}\) to (1), and work temporarily on a common probability space on which \(Y_N\to y\) almost surely.  We prove three pathwise facts on each such realization.  First, if \(u_N\in\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\) and \(u_N\to u\), then (3)--(4) give
\[
 (y_e-u_e)^{\mathsf T}V_{0,e}^{-1}(y_e-u_e)\le q_\infty,
 \qquad e=1,2,
\]
so
\[
 \limsup_N\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\subseteq\mathcal A_\gamma(y). \tag{5}
\]
Second, if \(K\subset\operatorname{int}\mathcal A_\gamma(y)\) is compact, continuity gives a \(\delta>0\) such that every limiting quadratic in (3) is at most \(q_\infty-2\delta\) on \(K\).  Uniformity in (3), convergence \(Y_N\to y\), and (4) then give
\[
 K\subseteq\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }N. \tag{6}
\]
This is the required inner, or erosion, condition; it is stronger than Hausdorff convergence at the point where the earlier argument failed.

Third, the exact bodies have a common compact bound.  To see this, the scalar function \(a-\log a-1\) is nonnegative, vanishes only at \(a=1\), and is bounded away from zero off every neighborhood of one.  The pivot inequality and (4) therefore force every eigenvalue of
\(S_e(u_e)^{-1/2}\widehat\Sigma_eS_e(u_e)^{-1/2}\) to equal \(1+O(N^{-1/2})\), uniformly over the pivot sublevel.  Since \(Y_N\) is bounded and \(\Sigma_{0,e}\succ0\), the corresponding Loewner inequalities imply
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal A_N(Y_N)}\lVert u-Y_N\rVert=O(1). \tag{7}
\]
Thus (5)--(7) verify, pathwise, the upper-limit, erosion, and common-compact-bound hypotheses of the corrected \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\).

For an accessible branch \(b\in\mathbb B(s_0)\), put
\[
 C_{b,N}=\sqrt N(\overline{\mathcal S_b}-s_0),
 \qquad
 Q_{b,N}=\overline{\sqrt N\{(\mathcal S_b\cap\mathcal R_{n,\gamma})-s_0\}}.
\]
The tangent-cone assumption states exactly that
\[
 C_{b,N}\longrightarrow T_b(s_0) \tag{8}
\]
locally in the Painleve--Kuratowski topology.  Tangent cones of semialgebraic sets are semialgebraic: their graph is obtained by introducing the positive scaling variable and taking the closure, and its coordinate projection is semialgebraic by \(\text{lem:tarski-seidenberg-projection}\).  There are only finitely many branches by \(\text{thm:finite-branch-representation}\).  Therefore the union of the translation-exception sets supplied by \(\text{lem:generic-ellipsoid-intersection-stability}\), one for each \(T_b(s_0)\), is Lebesgue null.  Since \(y\) has a density, almost surely all accessible branch intersections are stable simultaneously.

On that probability-one event, apply the corrected intersection lemma to (5)--(8).  It gives
\[
 \overline{C_{b,N}\cap\mathcal A_N(Y_N)}
 \longrightarrow
 Q_b(y):=T_b(s_0)\cap\mathcal A_\gamma(y) \tag{9}
\]
in the Fell topology, and in Hausdorff distance when \(Q_b(y)\ne\varnothing\).  We must still check that the cell rather than merely its closure occurs on the left.  Every semialgebraic cell \(\mathcal S_b\) is dense in \(\overline{\mathcal S_b}\).  In the lower-limit construction in the intersection lemma, a limiting point is first approximated by a point of \(T_b(s_0)\cap\operatorname{int}\mathcal A_\gamma(y)\).  A closed ball around that point lies in the limiting interior and hence, by (6), in \(\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\) eventually.  The approximating point from \(C_{b,N}\) can consequently be perturbed inside that ball to a point of \(\sqrt N(\mathcal S_b-s_0)\) without leaving \(\mathcal A_N(Y_N)\).  The upper limit is unchanged because \(\mathcal S_b\subseteq\overline{\mathcal S_b}\).  Hence (9) is also exactly
\[
 Q_{b,N}\longrightarrow Q_b(y). \tag{10}
\]
If \(b\notin\mathbb B(s_0)\), the closed set \(\overline{\mathcal S_b}\) misses a neighborhood of \(s_0\); (7) then makes its intersection with the covariance region empty eventually.  This disposes of all inaccessible branches.

It remains to map (10) to effects.  By \(\text{thm:finite-branch-representation}\) and \(\text{def:confidence-union}\), the confidence union is the closure of the finite union of the branch images over the corresponding covariance intersections.  Branch smoothness gives, uniformly for \(u\) in the common compact set from (7),
\[
 \theta_b(s_0+u/\sqrt N)=\theta_b(s_0)+o(1). \tag{11}
\]
Combining the finite union, (10), and (11) yields
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}\Rightarrow
 \{\theta_b(s_0):b\in\mathbb B(s_0),\ Q_b(h+\mathbb Z_0)\ne\varnothing\}
 =\mathcal K_h^{(0)}
\]
in the Fell topology.  The pathwise result on the Skorokhod coupling implies the asserted convergence in distribution under the original laws.

Finally suppose \(\Gamma(s_0)=\{\tau_0\}\).  Then \(\theta_b(s_0)=\tau_0\) for every accessible branch.  Continuous differentiability and the same compact bound give the uniform expansion
\[
 \sqrt N\{\theta_b(s_0+u/\sqrt N)-\tau_0\}
 =D\theta_b(s_0)u+o(1). \tag{12}
\]
Apply (10) and (12) branch by branch and take the finite union.  The result is
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{n,\gamma}-\tau_0)
 \Rightarrow
 \bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}
 \{D\theta_b(s_0)u:u\in T_b(s_0)\cap\mathcal A_\gamma(h+\mathbb Z_0)\}
 =\mathcal K_h^{(1)}
\]
in the Fell topology.  Empty limiting intersections are allowed; this is why the theorem is stated in the Fell rather than only the nonempty Hausdorff topology.
