Because \(\Gamma(s_0)=\{\tau_0\}\), the accessible branch set \(\mathbb B(s_0)\) is nonempty; it is finite because \(\mathbb B_p\) is finite.  For \(b\in\mathbb B(s_0)\), define
\[
L_{b,R}=T_b(s_0)\cap\{h\in\mathbb R^d:\|h\|_2\le R\}.
\]
The tangent cone \(T_b(s_0)\) is closed by definition, and the closed radius-\(R\) Euclidean ball is compact because \(R<\infty\).  Hence \(L_{b,R}\) is compact.  The map \(\eta_b(h)=D\theta_b(s_0)h\) is linear and therefore continuous.  It follows that
\[
K_R=\bigcup_{b\in\mathbb B(s_0)}\eta_b(L_{b,R})
\]
is a finite union of compact subsets of \(\mathbb R^3\), and thus is compact.  In particular,
\[
D_R:=\operatorname{diam}(K_R)<\infty.
\]

We first justify the restriction to the compact action space \(\mathcal H_R\).  For a closed set \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), put \(T(C)=C\cap K_R\).  The map \(T\) is measurable from the Effros sigma-field on closed subsets of \(\mathbb R^3\) to the Borel sigma-field of \(\mathcal H_R\).  To see this directly, let \(O=K_R\cap G\), where \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) is open, and define
\[
A_m=\{x\in K_R:\operatorname{dist}(x,G^c)\ge m^{-1}\},\qquad m\ge1.
\]
With \(A_1=K_R\) when \(G^c=\varnothing\), the sets \(A_m\) are compact and \(O=\bigcup_{m\ge1}A_m\).  For any compact \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), closedness of \(C\) and compactness of \(A\) imply
\[
\{C:C\cap A\ne\varnothing\}
=\bigcap_{\ell\ge1}\{C:C\cap A^{1/\ell}\ne\varnothing\},
\]
where \(A^{1/\ell}\) is the open \(1/\ell\)-neighborhood of \(A\).  Consequently both
\[
\{C:T(C)\cap O\ne\varnothing\}
=\bigcup_{m\ge1}\{C:C\cap A_m\ne\varnothing\}
\]
and, for open \(O\subseteq K_R\),
\[
\{C:T(C)\subseteq O\}
=\{C:C\cap(K_R\setminus O)=\varnothing\}
\]
are Effros measurable.  The preimage of the isolated empty member of \(\mathcal H_R\) is \(\{C:C\cap K_R=\varnothing\}\), which is measurable by the same compact-hit identity.  The displayed hit and containment sets generate the Vietoris, equivalently Hausdorff, Borel sigma-field on the compact hyperspace, proving the asserted measurability.

For every \((b,h)\in\Lambda_R\), the target \(\eta_b(h)\) belongs to \(K_R\).  Therefore, pointwise in \(C\),
\[
{\bf1}\{\eta_b(h)\in T(C)\}={\bf1}\{\eta_b(h)\in C\},
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(T(C))\le\operatorname{diam}(C).
\]
Thus truncation preserves all coverage probabilities and cannot increase any expected-diameter risk.  It follows in both directions that the unrestricted infimum \(v_R\) equals the infimum over \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rules.  By \(\text{lem:hyperspace-compactness}\), \(\mathcal H_R\) is compact and \(C\mapsto\operatorname{diam}(C)\) is continuous on it.

We next dominate all Gaussian shifts by one law.  The matrix \(V_0\) is positive definite.  Indeed, let \(c\ne0\) be a vector in half-vector coordinates, and let \(A_c\ne0\) be the corresponding symmetric coefficient matrix, with the off-diagonal entries normalized so that contraction with a symmetric matrix agrees with contraction by \(c\) in half-vector coordinates.  Expanding the defining identity
\[
V(\Sigma)_{AB,CD}=\Sigma_{AC}\Sigma_{BD}+\Sigma_{AD}\Sigma_{BC}
\]
gives
\[
c^{\mathsf T}V(\Sigma)c
=2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(\Sigma^{1/2}A_c\Sigma^{1/2})^2\right\}>0
\]
for every positive-definite \(\Sigma\): the symmetric matrix \(\Sigma^{1/2}A_c\Sigma^{1/2}\) is nonzero, so the trace of its square is strictly positive.  The two covariance matrices encoded by \(s_0\in\Omega_p\) are positive definite, and \(\lambda\in(0,1)\); hence the block-diagonal definition of \(V_0\) proves \(V_0\succ0\).

Let \(\varphi_0\) denote the \(\mathcal N(0,V_0)\) density and put \(\mu_0=\mathcal N(0,V_0)\).  For every \(h\in\mathbb R^d\), completing the square in the two Gaussian densities yields the continuous, strictly positive Radon--Nikodym derivative
\[
w_h(y):=\frac{\varphi_h(y)}{\varphi_0(y)}
=\exp\!\left\{h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}y
-\frac12 h^{\mathsf T}V_0^{-1}h\right\},
\qquad
\int w_h\,d\mu_0=1.
\]

Let \(\delta\) be an \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued randomized rule.  Measurability of \(\delta\), followed by integration over the independent uniform randomizer, gives the probability kernel
\[
Q(A\mid y)=\int_0^1 {\bf1}\{\delta(y,u)\in A\}\,du,
\qquad A\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H_R).
\]
Conversely, \(\text{lem:kernel-uniform-randomization}\) realizes every probability kernel from \(\mathbb R^d\) to the standard Borel space \(\mathcal H_R\) as a jointly measurable function of \((y,U)\).  Since \(w_h\varphi_0=\varphi_h\), change of measure shows that for each \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\) the coverage and risk of \(Q\) are, respectively,
\[
G_k(Q)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathcal H_R}
 {\bf1}\{\eta_b(h)\in C\}\,Q(dC\mid y)\varphi_h(y)\,dy
\]
and
\[
L_k(Q)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathcal H_R}
 \operatorname{diam}(C)\,Q(dC\mid y)\varphi_h(y)\,dy.
\]
Hence the feasible kernels are exactly those satisfying \(G_k(Q)\ge1-\gamma\) for every \(k\in\Lambda_R\), and the least feasible epigraph coordinate for a fixed kernel is \(\sup_{k\in\Lambda_R}L_k(Q)\).  This proves the equality between the decision problem and the displayed semi-infinite linear program, except only for attainment.  The constant rule \(C=K_R\) has coverage one and risk \(D_R\), so
\[
0\le v_R\le D_R<\infty.
\]

We now prove attainment.  For each integer \(j\ge1\), choose a feasible kernel \(Q_j\) such that
\[
\sup_{k\in\Lambda_R}L_k(Q_j)\le v_R+j^{-1},
\]
and define a probability measure on \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R\) by
\[
\zeta_j(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q_j(dC\mid y).
\]
Every \(\zeta_j\) has first marginal \(\mu_0\), and its second coordinate belongs to compact \(\mathcal H_R\).  Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose a compact Euclidean ball \(L_\epsilon\) with \(\mu_0(L_\epsilon)>1-\epsilon\).  Then
\[
\zeta_j(L_\epsilon\times\mathcal H_R)>1-\epsilon
\]
for every \(j\), so \((\zeta_j)_{j\ge1}\) is tight.  The product is a separable complete metric space.  By \(\text{lem:prokhorov-tightness}\), a subsequence, not relabeled, converges weakly to a probability measure \(\zeta\).

For every bounded continuous \(g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\), weak convergence and the common first marginal give
\[
\int g(y)\,\zeta(dy,dC)
=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int g(y)\,\zeta_j(dy,dC)
=\int g(y)\,\mu_0(dy).
\]
Thus the first marginal of \(\zeta\) is \(\mu_0\).  Both factors are standard Borel spaces, so \(\text{lem:standard-borel-disintegration}\) supplies a probability kernel \(Q\) satisfying
\[
\zeta(dy,dC)=\mu_0(dy)Q(dC\mid y).
\]

Fix \(k=(b,h)\in\Lambda_R\) and define
\[
F_k=\{(y,C)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal H_R:\eta_b(h)\in C\}.
\]
The set \(F_k\) is closed: membership of a fixed point is preserved under Hausdorff convergence, and the empty member is isolated.  For \(L>0\), let \(w_h^L=w_h\wedge L\).  Since \(w_h^L\) is nonnegative and continuous and \({\bf1}_{F_k}\) is upper semicontinuous, \(w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\) is bounded and upper semicontinuous.  Feasibility of \(Q_j\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j
&=\int w_h{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j
  -\int(w_h-w_h^L){\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j\\
&\ge1-\gamma-\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The upper-semicontinuous Portmanteau inequality \(\text{lem:portmanteau-usc}\) therefore yields
\[
\int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta
\ge\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int w_h^L{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta_j
\ge1-\gamma-\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0.
\]
Because \(w_h\) is nonnegative with \(\int w_h\,d\mu_0=1\),
\[
0\le\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0
\le\int_{\{w_h>L\}}w_h\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
Monotone convergence as \(L\to\infty\) now gives
\[
G_k(Q)=\int w_h{\bf1}_{F_k}\,d\zeta\ge1-\gamma.
\]
Since \(k\) was arbitrary, \(Q\) satisfies every coverage constraint.

For risk, the function
\[
(y,C)\longmapsto w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)
\]
is bounded and continuous by \(\text{lem:hyperspace-compactness}\).  Weak convergence therefore implies
\[
\int w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta
=\lim_{j\to\infty}
 \int w_h^L(y)\operatorname{diam}(C)\,d\zeta_j.
\]
Because both \(\zeta_j\) and \(\zeta\) have first marginal \(\mu_0\), while \(0\le\operatorname{diam}(C)\le D_R\) on \(\mathcal H_R\), replacing \(w_h^L\) by \(w_h\) changes any one of these integrals by at most
\[
D_R\int(w_h-w_h^L)\,d\mu_0\longrightarrow0,
\]
uniformly in \(j\).  Hence
\[
L_k(Q)
=\lim_{j\to\infty}L_k(Q_j)
\le v_R.
\]
This holds for every \(k\in\Lambda_R\), so \(\sup_k L_k(Q)\le v_R\).  The kernel \(Q\) is feasible, and \(v_R\) is the infimum over feasible rules; therefore \(\sup_kL_k(Q)=v_R\).  This proves attainment and the exact minimum characterization in the displayed semi-infinite linear program.

By \(\text{lem:kernel-uniform-randomization}\), every minimizing kernel has a measurable realization using \(U\), and that realization is an attaining \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rule.  Conversely, the kernel induced by any attaining \(\mathcal H_R\)-valued rule satisfies the program constraints and has epigraph value \(v_R\), so it is minimizing.  The densities \(w_h\) are everywhere strictly positive and finite, hence all Gaussian-shift laws have the same null sets as \(\mu_0\); this proves the asserted qualification up to null sets.  If an unrestricted rule attains \(v_R\), its measurable truncation \(T\circ\delta\) remains feasible and has risk at most \(v_R\).  The definition of the infimum forces equality, and the kernel induced by this truncation is therefore minimizing.

Finally, the local characterization alone cannot imply an all-sample finite-sample comparison.  Let \((\Delta_N)_{N\ge1}\) be any proposed sequence of finite-sample pullbacks and fix one sample size \(N_0\).  Replacing only \(\Delta_{N_0}\) by the constantly empty set leaves \(\Delta_N\) unchanged for every \(N>N_0\), so it leaves every local asymptotic limit unchanged, but its coverage at \(N_0\) is zero.  Alternatively, replacing only \(\Delta_{N_0}\) by the constant set \(\mathbb R^3\) again leaves all local asymptotic limits unchanged, while its diameter at \(N_0\) is \(+\infty\), destroying any finite diameter-dominance claim there.  Thus the local minimax characterization establishes neither finite-sample coverage nor finite-sample diameter dominance for an arbitrary proposed pullback.
