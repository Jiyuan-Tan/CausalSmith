%%% FIELD statement-global-exact-factorization
For every \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\in(0,1)^3\), set \(b=p_3\), \(x=\log(p_1/p_3)\), \(z=\log(p_2/p_3)\), and \(A=xz\). Then
\[
\log H(p_1,p_2,p_3)-\log\!\left(\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}\right)
=A B(p_1,p_2,p_3),
\qquad B(p_1,p_2,p_3)<0,
\]
and
\[
H(p_1,p_2,p_3)-\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}
=\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}A C(p_1,p_2,p_3),
\qquad C(p_1,p_2,p_3)<0.
\]
Consequently, for every \(p_4\in(0,1)\), the maps \(H(p_1,p_2,p_3)-p_4\) and \(P^{\rm P}(p_1,p_2,p_3)-p_4\) agree if and only if \(p_1=p_3\) or \(p_2=p_3\).
%%% FIELD proof-global-exact-factorization
Fix \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\in(0,1)^3\).  Put
\[
b=p_3,
\qquad x=\log(p_1/p_3),
\qquad z=\log(p_2/p_3),
\]
so \(be^x=p_1\) and \(be^z=p_2\).  For \(s,t\in[0,1]\),
\[
be^{sx}=p_3^{,1-s}p_1^s\in(0,1),
\qquad
be^{tz}=p_3^{,1-t}p_2^t\in(0,1).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus every quantile and denominator used below is finite, and every inverse-Mills denominator is strictly positive.  Define on the rectangle with corners \((0,0)\) and \((x,z)\)
\[
R_b(a,d)=
\log\Phi\!\left(q(be^a)+q(be^d)-q(b)\right)-\log b-a-d.
\tag{2}
\]
The inverse-function rule, \(\Phi'=\phi>0\), and the identity \(\Phi(q(p))=p\) give
\[
q'(p)=\frac{1}{\phi(q(p))},
\qquad
\frac{d}{da}q(be^a)
=\frac{be^a}{\phi(q(be^a))}
=\frac{1}{m(q(be^a))}.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(S(a,d)=q(be^a)+q(be^d)-q(b)\), differentiation of (2), first in \(a\) and then in \(d\), yields
\[
\partial_{ad}R_b(a,d)
=\frac{m'(S(a,d))}
{m(q(be^a))m(q(be^d))}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, the derivative of \(\log\Phi(v)\) is \(m(v)\), and (3) supplies both inner derivatives.  The boundary identities are exact:
\[
R_b(a,0)=0,
\qquad R_b(0,d)=0,
\tag{5}
\]
because \(H(be^a,b,b)=be^a\) and \(H(b,be^d,b)=be^d\).  Applying the one-dimensional fundamental theorem of calculus successively to (4)--(5), with oriented integrals when \(x<0\) or \(z<0\), and then substituting \(a=sx\), \(d=tz\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_b(x,z)
&=\int_0^x\!\int_0^z\partial_{ad}R_b(a,d)\,dd\,da\\
&=xz\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
\frac{m'\!\left(q(be^{sx})+q(be^{tz})-q(b)\right)}
{m(q(be^{sx}))m(q(be^{tz}))}\,dt\,ds\\
&=A B(p_1,p_2,p_3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This derivation also covers \(x=0\) or \(z=0\): both sides of (6) then vanish, and no division by \(x\), \(z\), or \(A=xz\) has occurred.

It remains to determine the sign.  Direct differentiation of \(m(v)=\phi(v)/\Phi(v)\), using \(\phi'(v)=-v\phi(v)\), gives
\[
m'(v)=-m(v)\{v+m(v)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(v\ge0\), then \(v+m(v)>0\).  If \(v=-a<0\), the substitution \(w=a+t\) gives
\[
\Phi(-a)=\int_a^\infty\phi(w)\,dw
=\phi(a)\int_0^\infty e^{-at-t^2/2}\,dt
<\phi(a)\int_0^\infty e^{-at}\,dt
=\frac{\phi(a)}{a}.
\tag{8}
\]
Hence \(m(-a)=\phi(a)/\Phi(-a)>a\), so again \(-a+m(-a)>0\).  Equation (7) therefore proves \(m'(v)<0\) for every \(v\in\mathbb R\).  By (1), the integrand in the definition of \(B(p_1,p_2,p_3)\) is continuous and strictly negative on the compact unit square.  Consequently,
\[
B(p_1,p_2,p_3)<0.
\tag{9}
\]

By (2) and \(be^{x+z}=p_1p_2/p_3\), exponentiating (6) gives
\[
H(p_1,p_2,p_3)
=\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}e^{AB(p_1,p_2,p_3)}.
\tag{10}
\]
The defining integral \(\psi(w)=\int_0^1e^{tw}\,dt\) is strictly positive for every real \(w\), and the fundamental theorem of calculus gives \(e^w-1=w\psi(w)\), including at \(w=0\).  Subtracting \(p_1p_2/p_3\) in (10) therefore yields
\[
H(p_1,p_2,p_3)-\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}
=\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}A B(p_1,p_2,p_3)
\psi\!\left(A B(p_1,p_2,p_3)\right)
=\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}A C(p_1,p_2,p_3).
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (9) and the positivity of \(\psi\) imply \(C(p_1,p_2,p_3)<0\).  Because \(p_1p_2/p_3>0\) and \(C\ne0\), the difference in (11) is zero exactly when
\[
A=xz=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x=0\ \text{or}\ z=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p_1=p_3\ \text{or}\ p_2=p_3.
\tag{12}
\]
Subtracting the same arbitrary \(p_4\in(0,1)\) from both maps leaves their difference unchanged.  Since \(P^{\rm P}(p_1,p_2,p_3)=p_1p_2/p_3\), (12) proves the last assertion.
%%% FIELD proof-published-identification
Fix \(1\le\ell\le L\), and define the treated group's untreated post-period exceedance probability
\[
p^0_{\ell t}=\Pr\!\left(Y_{t_\ell}(0)>y_0\mid G=g_\ell\right).
\]
The no-anticipation assumption identifies the treated group's untreated pre-period exceedance probability with the observed treated-pre marginal:
\[
\Pr\!\left(Y_{s_\ell}(0)>y_0\mid G=g_\ell\right)
=\Pr\!\left(Y_{s_\ell}(1)>y_0\mid G=g_\ell\right)
=r_{\ell1}.
\tag{1}
\]
Substituting (1) and the definitions of \(r_{\ell2}\) and \(r_{\ell3}\) into normal-link parallel trends gives
\[
q(p^0_{\ell t})-q(r_{\ell1})
=q(r_{\ell2})-q(r_{\ell3}).
\tag{2}
\]
All four probabilities in (2) belong to \((0,1)\), as specified by the probability-array support of \(r\) and the domain of the parallel-trends equality.  Since \(q=\Phi^{-1}\), applying the strictly increasing function \(\Phi\) to (2) yields
\[
p^0_{\ell t}
=\Phi\!\left(q(r_{\ell1})+q(r_{\ell2})-q(r_{\ell3})\right)
=H(r_{\ell1},r_{\ell2},r_{\ell3}).
\tag{3}
\]
The treated-post observed exceedance probability is \(r_{\ell4}\).  Taking complements at the fixed threshold and using (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_\ell
&=\Pr\!\left(Y_{t_\ell}(1)\le y_0\mid G=g_\ell\right)
-\Pr\!\left(Y_{t_\ell}(0)\le y_0\mid G=g_\ell\right)\\
&=(1-r_{\ell4})-(1-p^0_{\ell t})\\
&=H(r_{\ell1},r_{\ell2},r_{\ell3})-r_{\ell4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus (4) proves, rather than defines, the equality between the causal contrast and the observed-law functional.  Averaging (4) over the fixed \(L\) pairs gives
\[
\overline\Delta=L^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^L\Delta_\ell.
\]
The two assumptions used in (1)--(2) are precisely the declared fixed-threshold standard-normal forms of Djuazon and Tsyawo's Assumptions 1--2.  Finally, an outcome-independent allocation of observations among role samples changes only the data used to estimate each marginal \(r_{\ell j}\); it does not alter any population probability in (1)--(4).
%%% FIELD statement-rare-factorization-replacement
For every \(\ell\) and every admissible \(\rho,c\), \(\log H(r_{\ell1},r_{\ell2},r_{\ell3})-\log(\rho Q_{\ell})=A_{\ell}B_{\rho,\ell}\), \(B_{\rho,\ell}<0\), and \(\Delta_{\ell}-\Delta^P_{\ell}=\rho Q_{\ell}A_{\ell}C_{\rho,\ell}\) with \(C_{\rho,\ell}<0\). Consequently, \(\Delta_{\ell}=\Delta^P_{\ell}\) if and only if \(c_{\ell1}=c_{\ell3}\) or \(c_{\ell2}=c_{\ell3}\).
%%% FIELD proof-rare-factorization-corollary
Fix \(1\le\ell\le L\).  The coefficient box, rarity-scale assumption, and positivity of \(\rho\) imply
\[
0<\rho c_{\ell j}\le4\rho\le\frac14<1,
\qquad 1\le j\le4.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\rho c_{\ell1},\rho c_{\ell2},\rho c_{\ell3})\in(0,1)^3\), so the global exact-factorization theorem applies.  Under common rarity, put \(p_j=r_{\ell j}=\rho c_{\ell j}\).  The local coordinates in that theorem satisfy
\[
b=p_3=\rho c_{\ell3},
\qquad
x=\log(p_1/p_3)=\log(c_{\ell1}/c_{\ell3})=x_\ell,
\]
\[
z=\log(p_2/p_3)=\log(c_{\ell2}/c_{\ell3})=z_\ell,
\qquad
A=xz=A_\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, the Poisson coefficient and the evaluation aliases in the generalized mixed-coefficient definition give
\[
\frac{p_1p_2}{p_3}=\rho\frac{c_{\ell1}c_{\ell2}}{c_{\ell3}}=\rho Q_\ell,
\qquad
B(p_1,p_2,p_3)=B_{\rho,\ell},
\qquad
C(p_1,p_2,p_3)=C_{\rho,\ell}.
\tag{3}
\]
Substitution of (2)--(3) into the global logarithmic identity and sign conclusions proves
\[
\log H(r_{\ell1},r_{\ell2},r_{\ell3})-\log(\rho Q_\ell)
=A_\ell B_{\rho,\ell},
\qquad
B_{\rho,\ell}<0,
\qquad C_{\rho,\ell}<0.
\tag{4}
\]
By the published identification proposition and common rarity,
\[
\Delta_\ell
=H(r_{\ell1},r_{\ell2},r_{\ell3})-r_{\ell4}
=H(r_{\ell1},r_{\ell2},r_{\ell3})-\rho c_{\ell4},
\tag{5}
\]
whereas the definition of the Poisson approximation gives
\[
\Delta^P_\ell=\rho Q_\ell-\rho c_{\ell4}.
\tag{6}
\]
Subtracting (6) from (5) cancels the treated-post term.  The global difference identity and (2)--(3) therefore yield
\[
\Delta_\ell-\Delta^P_\ell
=\rho Q_\ell A_\ell C_{\rho,\ell}.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(\rho Q_\ell>0\) and \(C_{\rho,\ell}<0\), the right-hand side of (7) vanishes exactly when \(A_\ell=0\).  By (2),
\[
A_\ell=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c_{\ell1}=c_{\ell3}\ \text{or}\ c_{\ell2}=c_{\ell3}.
\]
This proves the existing theorem as the explicit comparable-rarity corollary of the global pointwise result.
%%% FIELD statement-interior-reduction-narrowed
Under the fixed interior probabilities \(r_{\ell j,n}=p^{\circ}_{\ell j}\in[\varepsilon_{\rm int},1-\varepsilon_{\rm int}]\), mutually independent Bernoulli role observations, and \(n_{\ell j}/n\to\pi_{\ell j}>0\), the outcome-independent sixty-role split satisfies, jointly over \(1\le\ell\le L\),
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\Delta^E_{\ell}-\Delta_{\ell})
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{a=1}^{n_{\ell j}}
\iota^{\circ}_{\ell j}(E_{\ell j a,n})+o_{P}(1)
\;\Rightarrow\;N(0,\Omega^{\circ}_{\ell}),
\]
with independent coordinates, where \(\iota^{\circ}_{\ell j}(e)=\gamma^{\circ}_{\ell j}(e-p^{\circ}_{\ell j})/\pi_{\ell j}\), \(\gamma^{\circ}_{\ell1}=\phi(v^{\circ}_{\ell})/\phi(q(p^{\circ}_{\ell1}))\), \(\gamma^{\circ}_{\ell2}=\phi(v^{\circ}_{\ell})/\phi(q(p^{\circ}_{\ell2}))\), \(\gamma^{\circ}_{\ell3}=-\phi(v^{\circ}_{\ell})/\phi(q(p^{\circ}_{\ell3}))\), \(\gamma^{\circ}_{\ell4}=-1\), \(v^{\circ}_{\ell}=q(p^{\circ}_{\ell1})+q(p^{\circ}_{\ell2})-q(p^{\circ}_{\ell3})\), and \(\Omega^{\circ}_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{4}(\gamma^{\circ}_{\ell j})^2p^{\circ}_{\ell j}(1-p^{\circ}_{\ell j})/\pi_{\ell j}\). Consequently,
\[
\sqrt n\left\{L^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widehat\Delta^E_{\ell}-\overline\Delta\right\}
\Rightarrow N\!\left(0,L^{-2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Omega^{\circ}_{\ell}\right).
\]
The interior margin is the finite-threshold standard-normal analogue of the regularity imposed in Djuazon and Tsyawo's interior analysis, but the displayed limit is proved independently for the mutually independent split-role experiment. It does not assert that this split experiment, or any rare triangular array, belongs to the sampling class of their Theorem 2.
%%% FIELD proof-interior-reduction
Fix \(1\le\ell\le L\), suppress \(\ell\), and write \(p_j=p^{\circ}_{\ell j}\), \(n_j=n_{\ell j}\).  By the interior-margin assumption,
\[
p=(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)\in
[\varepsilon_{\rm int},1-\varepsilon_{\rm int}]^4\subset(0,1)^4.
\tag{1}
\]
Define the observed-law map
\[
g(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=H(a_1,a_2,a_3)-a_4.
\tag{2}
\]
The quantile function is smooth on \((0,1)\), so (1) implies that \(g\) has a bounded Hessian on a fixed open neighborhood of \(p\).  With
\[
v=q(p_1)+q(p_2)-q(p_3),
\]
the inverse-function identity \(q'(a)=1/\phi(q(a))\) and the chain rule give
\[
\nabla g(p)=
\left(
\frac{\phi(v)}{\phi(q(p_1))},
\frac{\phi(v)}{\phi(q(p_2))},
-\frac{\phi(v)}{\phi(q(p_3))},
-1
\right)
=\gamma^\circ_\ell.
\tag{3}
\]
Under the fixed-probability assumption, \(r_{\ell j,n}=p_j\).  The published identification proposition therefore gives
\[
g(p)=\Delta_\ell.
\tag{4}
\]
Equation (4) supplies the causal interpretation; it is not introduced as a definition of \(\Delta_\ell\).

For each role, the add-half estimator obeys the exact identity
\[
\widetilde r_j-p_j
=\frac{K_j-n_jp_j}{n_j+1}
+\frac{1/2-p_j}{n_j+1}.
\tag{5}
\]
The role-share limit and \(\pi_{\ell j}>0\) imply \(n_j\asymp n\).  Hence the second term in (5) is \(O(n^{-1})=o(n^{-1/2})\), while Bernoulli sampling gives
\[
K_j-n_jp_j=O_P(\sqrt{n_j})=O_P(\sqrt n).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(\widetilde r-p=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  With probability tending to one, the segment joining \(p\) and \(\widetilde r\) stays in the fixed neighborhood on which the Hessian is bounded.  Taylor's theorem, (3)--(6), and \(\sqrt n\|\widetilde r-p\|^2=o_P(1)\) then yield
\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\Delta^E_\ell-\Delta_\ell\}
=\sum_{j=1}^4\gamma^\circ_{\ell j}
\frac{\sqrt n}{n_j+1}
\sum_{a=1}^{n_j}(E_{\ell ja,n}-p_j)+o_P(1).
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(n_j/n\to\pi_{\ell j}\),
\[
\frac{\sqrt n}{n_j+1}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt n\,\pi_{\ell j}}+o(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{8}
\]
Multiplying the remainder in (8) by the centered sum in (6) gives \(o_P(1)\).  Consequently, (7) becomes
\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\Delta^E_\ell-\Delta_\ell\}
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^4\sum_{a=1}^{n_j}
\frac{\gamma^\circ_{\ell j}}{\pi_{\ell j}}
(E_{\ell ja,n}-p_j)+o_P(1),
\tag{9}
\]
which is the asserted representation because the summand equals \(\iota^\circ_{\ell j}(E_{\ell ja,n})\).

For the centered leading term in (9), mutual independence gives variance
\[
\sum_{j=1}^4\frac{n_j}{n}
\frac{(\gamma^\circ_{\ell j})^2p_j(1-p_j)}{\pi_{\ell j}^2}
\longrightarrow
\sum_{j=1}^4
\frac{(\gamma^\circ_{\ell j})^2p_j(1-p_j)}{\pi_{\ell j}}
=\Omega^\circ_\ell.
\tag{10}
\]
Each summand is centered, independent, and bounded in absolute value by a fixed multiple of \(n^{-1/2}\).  Therefore the Lindeberg condition holds directly: for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\), all summand indicators in the Lindeberg sum vanish for sufficiently large \(n\).  The triangular-array central limit theorem and (9)--(10) give
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\Delta^E_\ell-\Delta_\ell)
\Rightarrow N(0,\Omega^\circ_\ell).
\tag{11}
\]
All role observations are mutually independent, so the leading sums for distinct pairs are independent for every \(n\).  Because \(L\) is fixed, applying the same Lindeberg argument to every linear combination yields joint convergence of the \(L\) coordinates in (11), with diagonal covariance.  Averaging those coordinates and using \(\overline\Delta=L^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^L\Delta_\ell\) proves
\[
\sqrt n\left\{L^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\widehat\Delta^E_\ell-\overline\Delta\right\}
\Rightarrow
N\!\left(0,L^{-2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Omega^\circ_\ell\right).
\]
This derivation uses the declared independent Bernoulli role experiment, positive share limits, interior probabilities, and the published population identification identity.  It is therefore an independently proved split-role analogue of interior Gaussian inference and makes no sampling-class claim about Djuazon and Tsyawo's Theorem 2 or about the rare triangular array.
