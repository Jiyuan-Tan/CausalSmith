Fix \(s\in[1,2]\), and take all limits as \(n\to\infty\).  The definitions of \(T_n\) and \(\tau_n\), together with \(\kappa_n>0\), give the exact identity
\[
 T_n\kappa_n^{1-1/s}
 =n\kappa_n^{2+1/s}\kappa_n^{1-1/s}
 =n\kappa_n^3
 =\tau_n.
 \tag{1}
\]

We first establish the subsequential exhaustion in \(\text{def:recovery-regimes}\).  For any positive sequence \((a_n)\), put \(a_\star=\liminf_n a_n\in[0,\infty]\).  If \(a_\star<\infty\), then, for every integer \(j\ge1\), the definition of the limit inferior permits an index \(n_j>n_{j-1}\) such that
\[
 a_\star-j^{-1}\le a_{n_j}\le a_\star+j^{-1}.
\]
Indeed, the lower inequality holds eventually, and the upper inequality holds at arbitrarily large indices.  Hence \(a_{n_j}\to a_\star\).  If \(a_\star=\infty\), the definition of the limit inferior gives \(a_n\to\infty\).  Applying this elementary argument to \(a_n=\tau_n\) produces a subsequence on which \(\tau_n\to0\), \(\tau_n\to\tau\in(0,\infty)\), or \(\tau_n\to\infty\).  In the last case, applying the same argument to \(\lambda_n\) along that subsequence and, if necessary, passing to a further subsequence gives \(\lambda_n\to0\), \(\lambda_n\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), or \(\lambda_n\to\infty\).  These are precisely the five components in \(\text{def:recovery-regimes}\).  Their defining extended-limit conditions are pairwise incompatible, so the selected subsequence belongs to exactly one component.  The conclusion is subsequential: different subsequences of an oscillating strength sequence may belong to different components.

Suppose that \(s=1\).  The definitions immediately give
\[
 T_n=n\kappa_n^3=\tau_n,
 \qquad
 \lambda_n=\frac{T_n}{\log(e/\kappa_n)}
 =\frac{\tau_n}{\log(e/\kappa_n)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus a finite positive limit of \(\tau_n\) defines the slope face, while, conditional on \(\tau_n\to\infty\), the three possible extended limits of the ratio in (2) distinguish the location-entropy faces.  These alternatives are genuinely distinct.  To see this, let \(u_n>0\) satisfy \(u_n=o(n)\), and, for all sufficiently large \(n\), set \(\kappa_n=(u_n/n)^{1/3}\), assigning arbitrary admissible values in \((0,1/8]\) at the finitely many earlier indices.  Then
\[
 \tau_n=u_n,
 \qquad
 \lambda_n
 =\frac{u_n}{1+\{\log n-\log u_n\}/3}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The choices \(u_n=(\log n)^{1/2}\), \(u_n=(\lambda/3)\log n\) for fixed \(\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), and \(u_n=(\log n)^2\) make \(\tau_n\to\infty\) while \(\lambda_n\) tends respectively to \(0\), \(\lambda\), and \(\infty\).  A fixed positive choice of \(u_n\) produces the finite-positive slope face.

Now suppose that \(s>1\), and put \(a=1-1/s>0\).  Combining (1) with \(\lambda_n=T_n/\log(e/\kappa_n)\) yields
\[
 \tau_n
 =\lambda_n\kappa_n^a\log(e/\kappa_n).
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(\lambda_n\) is eventually bounded, then \(\kappa_n\to0\).  Otherwise there would be an \(\varepsilon>0\) and a subsequence with \(\kappa_n\ge\varepsilon\); since \(\kappa_n\le1/8\), along that subsequence
\[
 \lambda_n
 =\frac{n\kappa_n^{2+1/s}}{\log(e/\kappa_n)}
 \ge
 \frac{n\varepsilon^{2+1/s}}{\log(e/\varepsilon)}
 \longrightarrow\infty,
\]
contradicting boundedness.  Also, \(x^a\log(e/x)\to0\) as \(x\downarrow0\).  For a direct verification, set \(v=x^{-1}\).  For \(v\ge1\),
\[
 \log v=\int_1^v z^{-1}\,dz
 \le\int_1^v z^{a/2-1}\,dz
 \le\frac{2}{a}v^{a/2},
\]
and consequently
\[
 x^a\log(e/x)
 =v^{-a}(1+\log v)
 \le v^{-a}+\frac{2}{a}v^{-a/2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Equation (4), the boundedness of \(\lambda_n\), and (5) therefore imply \(\tau_n\to0\).

Conversely, define \(g_s(x)=x^a\log(e/x)\) for \(x\in(0,1/8]\) and \(g_s(0)=0\).  Equation (5) proves continuity at zero, and ordinary continuity holds on \((0,1/8]\).  Compactness therefore gives
\[
 B_s:=\sup_{0\le x\le1/8}g_s(x)<\infty.
\]
Because \(g_s(\kappa_n)>0\), equation (4) implies
\[
 \lambda_n=\frac{\tau_n}{g_s(\kappa_n)}
 \ge\frac{\tau_n}{B_s}.
\]
It follows that \(\tau_n\to\infty\) implies \(\lambda_n\to\infty\).

We next show concretely why cutoff recovery does not imply shrinking honest effect intervals.  Fix any \(s\in(1,2]\).  Since \(s/(2s+1)>1/3\), choose
\[
 \frac13<b<\frac{s}{2s+1},
 \qquad
 \kappa_n=\frac18 n^{-b}.
 \tag{6}
\]
This sequence is admissible, and
\[
 \tau_n=8^{-3}n^{1-3b}\longrightarrow0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \lambda_n
 =\frac{8^{-(2+1/s)}n^{1-b(2+1/s)}}{\log(8e)+b\log n}
 \longrightarrow\infty
\]
because \(b<s/(2s+1)=1/(2+1/s)\).  Thus \(\mathsf{CR}(s,\kappa_\bullet)\) holds by \(\text{def:recovery-regimes}\).  On the other hand, \(\tau_n\to0\) and the definition
\[
 r_n=\min\left\{1,\frac{\rho_n}{\kappa_n}+\frac{1}{\tau_n}\right\}
\]
imply that \(r_n=1\) eventually.

By \(\text{thm:integrated-and-bump-lower-frontier}\), there exists a constant \(C>0\), depending only on \(s\), \(M\), and \(\alpha\), such that
\[
 L_n^\star(\kappa_n)\ge C r_n
 \tag{7}
\]
for every \(n\ge1\) and every \(0<\kappa_n\le1/8\).  No limiting condition on \(\tau_n\) or \(\lambda_n\) occurs in the quantifiers of that theorem.  Hence (7) holds on every component of \(\mathfrak R\), including the flat and finite-positive slope faces.  For the cutoff-recovered sequence (6), \(r_n=1\) eventually, and therefore
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}L_n^\star(\kappa_n)\ge C>0.
\]
This is the claimed minimax nonshrinkage certificate.

Finally, \(\text{def:profile-resolution-schedule}\) defines \(\mathfrak R_{\mathrm{cert}}\) by first requiring membership in \(\mathfrak R_{\mathrm{recovered}}\) and then imposing three additional vanishing certificate conditions.  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\mathrm{cert}}\subseteq\mathfrak R_{\mathrm{recovered}}.
\]
That restriction concerns only the still-unproved conservative finite-grid attainment direction.  It does not restrict the global lower bound (7), and no upper-rate or attainment conclusion has been used in this proof.
