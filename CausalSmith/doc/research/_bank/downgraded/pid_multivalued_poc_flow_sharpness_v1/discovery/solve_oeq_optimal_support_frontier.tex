%%% FIELD support-frontier-statement
For every \(2\leq k\leq m\) and \(r\geq1\),
\[
mr\leq s_\star(m,r,k)\leq 2mr-m.
\]
The upper bound simultaneously controls the separately minimized endpoint supports \(s_L(o,e)\) and \(s_U(o,e)\) for every \((o,e)\in\mathcal C_{m,r,k}\).  The lower bound is witnessed by the compatible family \(o_{is}=1/(mr)\) and \(e_{js}=1/r\), for which both endpoint support minima are at least \(mr\).  Consequently \(s_\star(m,r,k)=\Theta(mr)\) uniformly over \(2\leq k\leq m\), the quadratic \(k^2\) term in \(N_\star\) is removable from a uniform exact-endpoint support guarantee, and \(s_\star(m,1,k)=m\).
%%% FIELD support-frontier-proof
Fix integers \(m,r,k\) satisfying \(2\leq k\leq m\) and \(r\geq1\), as required by \(\mathrm{ass{:}query\mbox{-}range}\) and the declared alphabet sizes.  Put
\[
R:=mr+m(r-1)=2mr-m.
\]
We first prove the uniform upper bound.  Let \((o,e)\in\mathcal C_{m,r,k}\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal C_{m,r,k}\), the pair is compatible.  The endpoint-attainment conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}interval}\) supplies finite laws \(Q_L^0,Q_U^0\in\mathcal Q(o,e)\) such that
\[
Q_L^0\!\left(\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal J}A_j\right)=L(o,e),
\qquad
Q_U^0\!\left(\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal J}A_j\right)=U(o,e).
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}certificate\mbox{-}preserving\mbox{-}endpoint\mbox{-}compression}\) separately to \(Q_L^0\) and \(Q_U^0\).  Its finite-support hypothesis holds by the preceding attainment result.  The lemma returns endpoint-attaining laws \(Q_L,Q_U\in\mathcal Q(o,e)\) satisfying
\[
Q_L\!\left(\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal J}A_j\right)=L(o,e),
\qquad
Q_U\!\left(\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal J}A_j\right)=U(o,e),
\]
and
\[
\sigma(Q_L)\leq R,
\qquad
\sigma(Q_U)\leq R.
\]
The two feasible sets in the definitions of \(s_L(o,e)\) and \(s_U(o,e)\) are thus nonempty.  Taking their minima and using the displayed laws as feasible points gives
\[
s_L(o,e)\leq\sigma(Q_L)\leq R,
\qquad
s_U(o,e)\leq\sigma(Q_U)\leq R.
\]
It follows that
\[
\max\{s_L(o,e),s_U(o,e)\}\leq R
\]
for every \((o,e)\in\mathcal C_{m,r,k}\).  Taking the supremum specified in the definition of \(s_\star\) yields
\[
s_\star(m,r,k)
=\sup_{(o,e)\in\mathcal C_{m,r,k}}
  \max\{s_L(o,e),s_U(o,e)\}
\leq R=2mr-m.
\]
This is one uniform bound for both separately minimized endpoint supports; it does not require the same law to attain both endpoints.

For the lower bound, define tables \(o^\circ\) and \(e^\circ\) by
\[
o^\circ_{is}:=\frac1{mr}
\quad(i\in\mathcal X,\ s\in\mathcal Y),
\qquad
e^\circ_{js}:=\frac1r
\quad(j\in\mathcal X,\ s\in\mathcal Y).
\]
The denominators are positive because \(m\geq2\) and \(r\geq1\).  Since \(|\mathcal X|=m\) and \(|\mathcal Y|=r\),
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}\sum_{s\in\mathcal Y}o^\circ_{is}
=mr\frac1{mr}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{s\in\mathcal Y}e^\circ_{js}
=r\frac1r=1
\quad(j\in\mathcal X).
\]
All entries are nonnegative, so \(o^\circ\in\Delta(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y)\) and \(e^\circ_{j,\cdot}\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) for every \(j\in\mathcal X\).  Moreover,
\[
e^\circ_{is}-o^\circ_{is}
=\frac1r-\frac1{mr}
=\frac{m-1}{mr}\geq0
\quad(i\in\mathcal X,\ s\in\mathcal Y).
\]
The defining characterization of \(\mathcal C_{m,r,k}\) therefore gives
\[
(o^\circ,e^\circ)\in\mathcal C_{m,r,k}.
\]
This membership calculation does not depend on the queried labels, so the same witness family applies to arbitrary repetitions among those labels.

Let \(Q\in\mathcal Q(o^\circ,e^\circ)\) be arbitrary.  For each \((i,s)\in\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y\), define the factual observational cell
\[
C_{is}:=\{(x,y_1,\ldots,y_m)\in\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y^m:x=i,\ y_i=s\}.
\]
The \(mr\) sets \(C_{is}\) are pairwise disjoint: two atoms in cells with different \(i\)'s have different factual treatments, while cells with a common \(i\) and different \(s\)'s require mutually exclusive values of \(y_i\).  They cover \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y^m\), because each response atom has exactly one factual treatment \(x=i\) and exactly one factual potential-outcome label \(y_i=s\).  By the observational-margin clause in \(\mathrm{def{:}model\mbox{-}class}\),
\[
Q(C_{is})
=\Pr_Q(X=i,Y_i=s)
=o^\circ_{is}
=\frac1{mr}>0
\quad(i\in\mathcal X,\ s\in\mathcal Y).
\]
The response space is finite.  Thus each positive-mass cell \(C_{is}\) contains at least one response atom of positive \(Q\)-mass.  Pairwise disjointness makes the chosen atoms distinct across the \(mr\) cells, and the definition of \(\sigma\) implies
\[
\sigma(Q)\geq mr
\quad\text{for every }Q\in\mathcal Q(o^\circ,e^\circ).
\]

By \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}interval}\), both endpoints \(L(o^\circ,e^\circ)\) and \(U(o^\circ,e^\circ)\) are attained by members of this model class.  Hence the minimizing sets defining \(s_L(o^\circ,e^\circ)\) and \(s_U(o^\circ,e^\circ)\) are nonempty.  The preceding inequality applies to every law in either set, so
\[
s_L(o^\circ,e^\circ)\geq mr,
\qquad
s_U(o^\circ,e^\circ)\geq mr.
\]
Because \((o^\circ,e^\circ)\in\mathcal C_{m,r,k}\), the definition of \(s_\star\) now gives
\[
s_\star(m,r,k)
\geq\max\{s_L(o^\circ,e^\circ),s_U(o^\circ,e^\circ)\}
\geq mr.
\]
Combining the two directions proves
\[
mr\leq s_\star(m,r,k)\leq2mr-m.
\]
Since \(m>0\), one has \(2mr-m\leq2mr\).  Therefore
\[
mr\leq s_\star(m,r,k)\leq2mr,
\]
with constants independent of \(k\), which is exactly \(s_\star(m,r,k)=\Theta(mr)\) uniformly over \(2\leq k\leq m\).  If \(r=1\), the proved lower and upper bounds coincide and give
\[
s_\star(m,1,k)=m.
\]

Finally, the definition of \(N_\star\) and the identity defining \(R\) give
\[
N_\star
=m+2m(r-1)+k^2+3k+1
=R+k^2+3k+1.
\]
Nevertheless, the uniform construction above provides a separate exact lower-endpoint witness and exact upper-endpoint witness with support at most \(R\), independently of \(k^2\).  Thus the quadratic \(k^2\) term is an overhead of the grid-preserving \(N_\star\) construction, not an intrinsic requirement for exact endpoint support.
