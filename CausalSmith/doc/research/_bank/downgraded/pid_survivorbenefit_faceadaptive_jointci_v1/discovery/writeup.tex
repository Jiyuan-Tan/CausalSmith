\begin{definition}[def:capacity-map]\label{def:capacity-map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Total algebraic survivor-\allowbreak{}capacity map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(u,v\in\mathbb R\), set \(u\oslash v=u/v\) if \(v\ne0\) and \(u\oslash0=0\). For every \(q\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\), set \(q_{xz}(q)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}q_{xza}\) and \(q_x(q)=\sum_{z,a}q_{xza}\). For \(k\in\mathcal Y\), define \(R_{0kx}(q)=q_{x0,(0,1,k)}\oslash q_{x0}(q)-q_{x1,(0,1,k)}\oslash q_{x1}(q)\) and \(R_{1kx}(q)=q_{x1,(1,1,k)}\oslash q_{x1}(q)-q_{x0,(1,1,k)}\oslash q_{x0}(q)\), and set \(H_x(q)=q_x(q)\{R_{0\cdot x}(q)_+,R_{1\cdot x}(q)_+\}\in\mathbb R^{2K}\). \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} an atom vector \(q\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-capacity-geometry]\label{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}
The total division operation \(\oslash\) and each coordinate of \(H_x\) are finite semialgebraic functions, while \(F_L\), \(F_U\), and \(m\) are continuous, positively homogeneous, globally Lipschitz, and piecewise linear on a finite polyhedral complex over all of \(\mathbb R^{2K}\). For every \(b\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(g\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\), \(d_b(g)=\partial_+\bar\Phi(b;g)\) exists and is finite: ordinary quotient rules apply to positive instrument cells, while positive homogeneity together with \(u\oslash0=0\) supplies the ray derivative at empty cells. The map \((b,g)\mapsto d_b(g)\) is jointly semialgebraic and Borel on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\times\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(d=|\mathcal J|\).  We first establish the assertions about the three capacity functionals directly from \(\texttt{def:capacity-map}\).  The two totals \(s_0\) and \(s_1\), every prefix sum, and every strict-cut sum are linear functions of \((\ell,h)\).  Hence
\[
m(\ell,h)=\min(s_0,s_1)
\]
is the minimum of two linear functions, and \(F_U\) is the minimum of the finite family consisting of \(m\) and the \(K\) strict-cut linear functions.  Likewise, if
\[
A_t(\ell,h)=\sum_{k\le t}\ell_k-\sum_{k\le t}h_k,
\qquad \Delta(\ell,h)=s_1-s_0,
\]
then
\[
F_L(\ell,h)=\max\!\left\{0,\max_{0\le t<K}A_t(\ell,h)+\min\{\Delta(\ell,h),0\}\right\}.
\]
A finite maximum or minimum of linear functions is continuous and piecewise linear on the common refinement of the finitely many hyperplanes on which two displayed linear functions agree.  It is globally Lipschitz, with a Lipschitz constant bounded by the largest Euclidean norm of the finitely many coefficient vectors.  Sums and finite maxima preserve these properties.  Because every displayed linear function is homogeneous of degree one, all three functionals are positively homogeneous: for every \(c\ge0\),
\[
(F_L,F_U,m)(c\ell,ch)=c(F_L,F_U,m)(\ell,h).
\]
This proves the claimed finite polyhedral geometry on all of \(\mathbb R^{2K}\), not merely on the nonnegative orthant.

The graph of total division is
\[
\{(u,v,w):v\ne0,\;vw=u\}\ \cup\ \{(u,0,0):u\in\mathbb R\},
\]
so \(\oslash\) is finite valued and semialgebraic.  The formulas in \(\texttt{def:capacity-map}\) use only finite sums, products, total division, and positive parts.  Consequently every coordinate of \(H_x\), and then \(\bar M,\bar N_L,\bar N_U\), is finite valued and semialgebraic.

Fix \(b\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(g\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\).  By instrument overlap in the legal-model definition, for each \(x\) either \(q_x(b)=0\), in which case every atom in both instrument arms of that cell is zero, or
\[
q_{xz}(b)\ge\epsilon q_x(b)>0,\qquad z=0,1.
\]
In the latter case all ratios entering \(H_x\) are ordinary rational functions on a neighborhood of \(b\).  Their ray derivatives exist by the quotient rule.  The ray derivative of positive part is
\[
\partial_+ (u)_+(v)=
\begin{cases}
v,&u>0,\\
0,&u<0,\\
\max(v,0),&u=0,
\end{cases}
\]
and finite maxima and minima have ray derivatives obtained by maximizing or minimizing the derivatives over the tied active indices.  Thus every positive-cell contribution has a finite ray derivative.

For an empty cell, write \(s_x=q_x(g)\), \(s_{xz}=q_{xz}(g)\), and let \(g_{xz,(d,s,k)}\) denote the corresponding atom coordinate.  Since \((tu)\oslash(tv)=u\oslash v\) for every \(t>0\), including \(v=0\), the capacity-map formula gives the exact identity
\[
H_x(b+tg)=t\,s_x
\left(
\left\{g_{x0,(0,1,k)}\oslash s_{x0}-g_{x1,(0,1,k)}\oslash s_{x1}\right\}_{k=0}^{K-1,+},
\left\{g_{x1,(1,1,k)}\oslash s_{x1}-g_{x0,(1,1,k)}\oslash s_{x0}\right\}_{k=0}^{K-1,+}
\right)
\]
for every \(t>0\), where the superscript \(+\) means coordinatewise positive part.  Positive homogeneity of \(F_L,F_U,m\) therefore supplies the empty-cell ray derivative, even for an ambient direction that is not a legal tangent.

Finally, \(\bar M(b)=M(b)\ge m_*>0\) by the aggregate-mass restriction in the legal-model definition.  Hence \(\bar M(b+tg)>0\) for all sufficiently small positive \(t\), and the final quotient rule yields, for \(e\in\{L,U\}\),
\[
d_{b,e}(g)=
\frac{\partial_+\bar N_e(b;g)-\Phi_e(b)\partial_+\bar M(b;g)}{\bar M(b)}.
\]
Every operation in this finite case decomposition is semialgebraic.  Its graph is obtained by finitely many sign and tie conditions, so \((b,g)\mapsto d_b(g)\) is jointly semialgebraic.  More explicitly, on each of the finitely many regions selected by those polynomial sign and tie conditions, every coordinate of \(d_b(g)\) is a rational expression with denominators having fixed nonzero signs, or is the stipulated zero-denominator value.  The inverse image of an open interval is therefore a finite Boolean combination of polynomial equalities and strict inequalities, and is Borel.  This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite capacity geometry is the reduction that avoids division by vanishing cell-specific survivor masses. \textit{Gap.} Generic directional-delta results do not supply the zero-cell rational limit or the model-specific finite capacity-face decomposition. \textit{Consumer.} The legal-face optimizer can enumerate all support and tie configurations needed for reproducible calibration.

\begin{lemma}[lem:legal-tangent-domain]\label{lem:legal-tangent-domain}
If \(b\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(p:[0,\eta)\to\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is a one-sided differentiable legal path with \(p(0)=b\), then \(\dot p(0+)\in\mathcal D_b\). For every \(g\in\mathcal D_b\) that is generated by such a legal path, \(d_b(g)\) equals the genuine one-sided derivative \(\partial_+\Phi\{p(t)\}|_{t=0}\). Thus values of \(d_b\) outside \(\mathcal D_b\) provide a total calibration convention but no ambient-to-legal realization theorem.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(p:[0,\eta)\to\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) be as in the statement, and write
\[
p(t)=b+t g+o(t),\qquad g=\dot p(0+).
\]
Suppose \(q_{xz}(b)=0\).  Every atom \(b_{xza}\) is then zero.  Since \(p_{xza}(t)\ge0\) for \(t>0\), differentiability gives \(g_{xza}\ge0\).  If in addition \(q_{xz}(g)=\sum_a g_{xza}=0\), all these nonnegative summands are zero.  This is exactly the implication defining \(g\in\mathcal D_b\) in \(\texttt{def:nearby-legal-envelope}\).

It remains to compare the derivative along the possibly curved legal path with the algebraic ray derivative.  If \(q_x(b)>0\), instrument overlap, as incorporated in \(\texttt{def:legal-model}\), gives \(q_{xz}(b)\ge\epsilon q_x(b)>0\).  Ordinary quotient expansion then gives
\[
H_x\{p(t)\}=H_x(b)+t\,\partial_+H_x(b;g)+o(t).
\]
Now suppose \(q_x(b)=0\).  Put \(s_x=q_x(g)\) and \(s_{xz}=q_{xz}(g)\).  Nonnegativity gives \(s_x,s_{xz}\ge0\), while overlap along the legal path implies
\[
\epsilon s_x\le s_{xz}\le(1-\epsilon)s_x,
\qquad z=0,1.
\]
If \(s_x>0\), both \(s_{xz}\) are positive and, for every atom,
\[
\frac{p_{xza}(t)}{q_{xz}\{p(t)\}}\longrightarrow \frac{g_{xza}}{s_{xz}}.
\]
Multiplying the resulting contrast by \(q_x\{p(t)\}=ts_x+o(t)\) proves that \(t^{-1}H_x\{p(t)\}\) converges to the empty-cell expression computed in \(\texttt{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}\).  If \(s_x=0\), then \(q_x\{p(t)\}=o(t)\).  Each selected conditional probability is between zero and one, so every coordinate of \(H_x\{p(t)\}\) has absolute value at most \(q_x\{p(t)\}=o(t)\).  The straight-ray derivative is also zero because its factor is \(s_x=0\).  Thus the same first-order expansion holds for every cell.

The globally Lipschitz, positively homogeneous capacity functionals from \(\texttt{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}\) preserve these first-order expansions.  Moreover, every \(p(t)\) is legal, so the aggregate-mass assumption gives \(\bar M\{p(t)\}\ge m_*>0\).  Applying the quotient expansion from \(\texttt{def:endpoint-map}\) therefore yields
\[
\frac{\Phi\{p(t)\}-\Phi(b)}{t}\longrightarrow d_b(g).
\]
The left side is the genuine one-sided derivative of the sharp endpoint map along the legal path, whereas the proof used no realization claim for a direction outside \(\mathcal D_b\).  This establishes both parts of the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The domain lemma separates derivatives of legal observed-law paths from algebraic directions introduced solely to make calibration total. \textit{Gap.} Generic directional calculus does not identify which zero-cell scores arise from the binary-instrument causal-law image. \textit{Consumer.} The legal realization argument can target exactly the admissible tangent scores without claiming an invalid ambient lift.

\begin{lemma}[lem:definable-measurability]\label{lem:definable-measurability}
The legal observed-law image \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is compact and semialgebraic. For each \(n\), the graph over \(\widehat p\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\) of \(\widehat p\mapsto\{b\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}:\|b-\widehat p\|_\infty\le a_n\}\) is compact and semialgebraic; for each fixed \(r>0\), the graph of \(p\mapsto\mathcal B(p,r)\) is compact and semialgebraic. Consequently definable optimization makes \(T_n(g)\) and \(E_{p,r}(g)\) finite Borel value functions of all displayed arguments. With \(G^*=\Sigma(\widehat p_n)^{1/2}W^*\) and the lower-quantile convention, \(c_n\) and the total rectangle \(C_n\) are measurable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All underlying variables have finite support.  Let \(\Omega\) be the finite set of full-data atoms for \((O,D_{\mathrm{pot}},S_{\mathrm{pot}},Y_{\mathrm{pot}})\), and represent a full law by \(w\in\Delta(\Omega)\).  Consistency and the two monotonicity restrictions set finitely many coordinates or finite coordinate sums to zero.  Conditional instrument independence is expressed by the finite polynomial identities
\[
w_{xzv}w_x=w_{xz}w_{xv}
\]
for the finitely many values \(v\) of the potential vector.  Instrument overlap is equivalent to the closed linear inequalities
\[
\epsilon w_x\le w_{x1}\le(1-\epsilon)w_x.
\]
The observed atom map \(w\mapsto p_w\) is linear.

The aggregate restriction is closed on this overlap set.  Indeed, on any positive cell, if \(A\) is a selected atom mass and \(B=q_{xz}\), then \(0\le A\le B\), \(q_x/B\le\epsilon^{-1}\), and direct differentiation gives
\[
\left|d\left(q_x\frac{A}{B}\right)[h]\right|
\le |q_x(h)|+\epsilon^{-1}\{|h_A|+|q_{xz}(h)|\}.
\]
The same expression is zero continuously when the whole cell vanishes.  Along a segment satisfying the overlap inequalities this proves a uniform Lipschitz bound for every coordinate of \(H_x\).  The Lipschitz property of \(m\) then proves continuity of \(M\).  Thus the legal full-law set is a closed subset of the compact simplex \(\Delta(\Omega)\), and is compact.  It is semialgebraic because all the preceding restrictions and the total capacity formulas have semialgebraic graphs.  The observed-law image is compact as the continuous image of this set, and it is semialgebraic by the cited Tarski--Seidenberg image lemma.  This proves the first assertion about \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).

For fixed \(n\), the graph of the nearby-base correspondence is described by
\[
b\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad -a_n\le b_j-\widehat p_j\le a_n
\quad(j\in\mathcal J).
\]
It is semialgebraic and its fibers are closed subsets of compact \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  The same argument, with \(a_n\) replaced by \(r\) and \(\widehat p\) by \(p\), applies to \(\mathcal B(p,r)\).

We next justify that the displayed suprema are finite; compactness alone would not suffice because \(d_b\) need not be continuous at an empty cell.  The positive-cell derivative bound just displayed, the finite Lipschitz constants of \(F_L,F_U,m\), and \(M(b)\ge m_*\) imply a bound \(C\|g\|_\infty\) for all positive-cell contributions to \(d_b(g)\), uniformly in legal \(b\).  For an empty cell, \(\texttt{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}\) gives an explicit finite expression depending only on the finitely many coordinates of the fixed vector \(g\), with every zero denominator interpreted by \(\oslash\).  There are finitely many cells.  Hence \(\sup_b\|d_b(g)\|_\infty<\infty\) for each fixed \(g\).

By \(\texttt{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}\), the graph of \(d_b(g)\) is semialgebraic.  The assertion that a scalar \(y\) is the supremum over a displayed fiber can be written using finitely many polynomial relations, one existential quantifier for approximate attainment, and one universal upper-bound quantifier.  The cited Tarski--Seidenberg lemma removes these quantifiers.  Therefore the finite value functions \(T_n\) and \(E_{p,r}\), including the stipulated zero value on an empty \(B_n\), are semialgebraic and hence Borel.

Finally, \(q\mapsto\Sigma(q)\) is polynomial.  The principal square root is Borel (indeed semialgebraic), because its graph is characterized uniquely by
\[
R=R^{\mathsf T},\qquad R^2=\Sigma(q),\qquad R\succeq0,
\]
and positive semidefiniteness is given by finitely many principal-minor inequalities.  Thus \((q,w)\mapsto T_{n,q}\{\Sigma(q)^{1/2}w\}\) is Borel.  Integration of its sublevel-set indicator against the fixed standard-Gaussian law shows that
\[
(q,t)\longmapsto
\Pr\!\left[T_{n,q}\{\Sigma(q)^{1/2}W^*\}\le t\right]
\]
is Borel.  The lower generalized inverse of a jointly Borel distribution function is Borel, as follows directly by testing it on rational \(t\).  Hence \(c_n\) is measurable.  The two fallback tests, the center \(\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(\widehat p_n)\), and the rectangle endpoints are Borel by the already proved algebraic measurability.  Therefore \(C_n\) is a measurable random closed set, completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The confidence procedure requires finite optimizers and a measurable conditional critical value on every empirical law. \textit{Gap.} Directional-bootstrap papers typically impose measurability of the derivative process rather than deriving it from this finite legal semialgebraic image. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem-level rectangle becomes a genuine statistic suitable for simulation and coverage analysis.

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-regular-face]\label{thm:oracle-regular-face}
If \(p_n=p\) and \(\operatorname{Reg}(p)\), then \[c_n\to_P Q_{1-\alpha}\{\|A_pG_0\|_\infty\},\qquad G_0\sim N\{0,\Sigma(p)\},\] and \(P_n\{\Phi(p)\in C_n\}\to1-\alpha\). Thus \(C_n\) is asymptotically equivalent to the oracle bivariate-Gaussian max-norm rectangle using the common covariance \(A_p\Sigma(p)A_p^{\mathsf T}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) with \(\operatorname{Reg}(p)\).  By \(\texttt{def:regular-face}\), there is \(\delta>0\) such that throughout the ambient \(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(p\) the operative positive-part signs, survivor-total comparison, and nonredundant cut optimizers are fixed.  On that neighborhood the algebraic extension in \(\texttt{def:endpoint-map}\) is an ordinary rational map whose only final denominator is bounded below by \(m_*\).  It is continuously differentiable there; write its derivative at \(b\) as \(A_b\).  The same definition gives \(A_b\to A_p\) as \(b\to p\).

The coordinatewise exponential-moment calculation for Bernoulli indicators gives
\[
P_p\!\left(\|\widehat p_n-p\|_\infty>a_n\right)
\le 2|\mathcal J|\exp(-2na_n^2)=o(1).
\]
On its complement, \(p\in B_n\), so \(B_n\ne\varnothing\); and every \(b\in B_n\) satisfies \(\|b-p\|_\infty\le2a_n<\delta\) eventually.  Consequently
\[
T_n(g)=\sup_{b\in B_n}\|A_bg\|_\infty,
\qquad
\left|T_n(g)-\|A_pg\|_\infty\right|
\le \sup_{b\in B_n}\|A_b-A_p\|_{\infty\leftarrow2}\,\|g\|_2=o_P(1)\|g\|_2.
\]
The lower inequality implicit here uses \(p\in B_n\).

Since \(\widehat p_n\to_Pp\), \(\Sigma(\widehat p_n)\to_P\Sigma(p)\).  Coupling the conditional draw and a population draw through the same \(W^*\),
\[
G^*=\Sigma(\widehat p_n)^{1/2}W^*,
\qquad
G_0^*=\Sigma(p)^{1/2}W^*,
\]
gives \(G^*-G_0^*=o_{P^*}(1)\) in probability; continuity of the positive-semidefinite square root in fixed dimension follows by diagonalizing along any convergent subsequence.  Hence, conditionally in probability,
\[
T_n(G^*)\ \Rightarrow\ \|A_pG_0^*\|_\infty.
\]
The covariance \(A_p\Sigma(p)A_p^{\mathsf T}\) is positive definite by \(\operatorname{Reg}(p)\).  The resulting bivariate Gaussian has an everywhere positive density, so the distribution of its max norm is continuous and strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\).  Its lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile, say \(q_p\), is therefore unique.  The definition of lower quantiles and the preceding conditional convergence imply
\[
c_n\to_Pq_p=Q_{1-\alpha}\{\|A_pG_0\|_\infty\},
\qquad G_0\sim N\{0,\Sigma(p)\}.
\]

For completeness, the multinomial central limit used for the center follows directly from characteristic functions: for every fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\),
\[
\left[\sum_jp_j\exp\!\left\{i t^{\mathsf T}(e_j-p)/\sqrt n\right\}\right]^n
\longrightarrow \exp\{-t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(p)t/2\}.
\]
Continuous differentiability on the separated face then gives
\[
\sqrt n\{\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(\widehat p_n)-\Phi(p)\}
=A_p\sqrt n(\widehat p_n-p)+o_P(1)
\Rightarrow A_pG_0.
\]
The fallback probability is \(o(1)\): \(B_n\ne\varnothing\) on the concentration event above, while continuity of \(M\) and \(M(p)\ge m_*>0\) imply \(M(\widehat p_n)>0\) with probability tending to one.  Therefore
\[
P_p\{\Phi(p)\in C_n\}
=P_p\!\left[\left\|\sqrt n\{\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(\widehat p_n)-\Phi(p)\}\right\|_\infty\le c_n\right]+o(1)
\longrightarrow 1-\alpha.
\]
Both endpoints use the same vector \(G_0\), so this is precisely the oracle common-covariance max-norm rectangle claimed in the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem certifies that honesty at irregular faces does not impose a first-order calibration penalty at ordinary laws. \textit{Gap.} Fang and Santos characterize derivative-aware resampling, but do not give exact oracle recovery for this legal rational capacity map with a joint endpoint rectangle. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts at well-separated laws retain the familiar efficient bivariate-Gaussian critical value rather than a least-favorable Bonferroni constant.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-rootn-width]\label{thm:uniform-rootn-width}
Uniformly over \(P_n\in\mathcal M\), \(P_n\{B_n=\varnothing\text{ or }M(\widehat p_n)=0\}\to0\) and \(c_n=O_{P_n}(1)\). On the complementary event each coordinate diameter of \(C_n\) is \(2c_n/\sqrt n\); consequently the extended random diameter, including the full-plane fallback on a uniformly negligible event, is \(O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\). This holds on arrays with covariate or survivor capacities emerging at order \(n^{-1/2}\) and with lower or upper active-cut gaps tied at order \(n^{-1/2}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(d=|\mathcal J|\) and \(d_X=|\mathcal X|\).  For every legal observed law \(p\), the empirical-coordinate exponential-moment bound gives
\[
P_p\{\|\widehat p_n-p\|_\infty>a_n\}
\le2d\exp(-2na_n^2)=o(1)
\]
uniformly in \(p\).  On the complementary event, \(p\in B_n\), because \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Thus \(P_p(B_n=\varnothing)\to0\) uniformly.

To control the other fallback branch, use \(M(p)=\sum_xm\{H_x(p)\}\ge m_*\).  There is an \(x=x(p)\) with
\[
m\{H_x(p)\}\ge m_*/d_X.
\]
Every coordinate total in \(H_x(p)\) is at most \(p_x\), so \(p_x\ge m_*/d_X\); overlap gives \(p_{xz}\ge\epsilon m_*/d_X\) for both arms.  On a fixed sufficiently small sup-norm neighborhood of such a \(p\), those two denominators stay bounded below.  The quotient calculation
\[
\left|d\left(q_x\frac{A}{q_{xz}}\right)[h]\right|
\le |q_x(h)|+\epsilon^{-1}\{|h_A|+|q_{xz}(h)|\},
\qquad 0\le A\le q_{xz},
\]
and the Lipschitz property of positive part and \(m\) give constants \(\eta_0,C_0>0\), depending only on \(d,d_X,\epsilon,m_*\), such that
\[
\|q-p\|_\infty\le\eta_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m\{H_x(q)\}\ge m_*/(2d_X)>0.
\]
The same empirical concentration bound with the fixed radius \(\eta_0\) therefore proves
\[
\sup_{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}}P_p\{M(\widehat p_n)=0\}\longrightarrow0.
\]

It remains to bound the conditional critical value.  At a legal base \(b\), every positive cell satisfies \(b_{xz}\ge\epsilon b_x\).  Differentiating each weighted conditional atom probability gives the uniform bound
\[
\left|d\left(b_x\frac{b_A}{b_{xz}}\right)[g]\right|
\le |q_x(g)|+\epsilon^{-1}\{|g_A|+|q_{xz}(g)|\}.
\]
Combining this inequality with the finite Lipschitz constants of \(F_L,F_U,m\), the quotient formula, and \(M(b)\ge m_*\), there is a finite constant \(C\) such that
\[
\|d_b(g)\|_\infty
\le C\left[\|g\|_\infty+
\sum_{x:b_x=0}|q_x(g)|
\left\{1+\sum_{z,a}|g_{xza}\oslash q_{xz}(g)|\right\}\right].
\tag{1}
\]
This also follows directly for an empty cell from the exact homogeneous formula in \(\texttt{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}\).

Condition on \(\widehat p_n=q\), and write \(G\sim N\{0,\Sigma(q)\}\).  For an instrument arm of mass \(u=q_{xz}\), put \(S_{xz}=q_{xz}(G)\).  If an atom in this arm has mass \(v\), Gaussian regression yields
\[
G_{xza}=\frac vuS_{xz}+R_{xza},
\qquad
R_{xza}\perp S_{xz},qquad
\operatorname{var}(R_{xza})=v(1-v/u)\le u,
\tag{2}
\]
when \(u>0\); when \(u=0\), all the corresponding Gaussian coordinates are zero.  Moreover \(\operatorname{var}(S_{xz})=u(1-u)\).  If some \(b\in B_n\) has \(b_x=0\), then \(q_x\le d a_n\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), this makes every such \(u\le1/2\).  Thus (2) shows that each ratio \(R_{xza}/S_{xz}\) has a centered Cauchy law with scale at most \(\sqrt2\), while
\[
q_x(G)=O_{P^*}(\sqrt{q_x})=O_{P^*}(\sqrt{a_n}).
\]
A finite union bound, first bounding the Gaussian factor and then the finitely many Cauchy ratios, proves for every \(\eta>0\) that there is \(K_\eta<\infty\) with
\[
\sup_q P^*\!\left[
\max_{x:\,\exists b\in B_n,\ b_x=0}
|q_x(G)|\left\{1+\sum_{z,a}|G_{xza}\oslash q_{xz}(G)|\right\}
>K_\eta\sqrt{a_n}\right]\le\eta.
\tag{3}
\]
Also, every coordinate variance of \(G\) is at most one, so \(\|G\|_\infty\) has uniformly bounded fixed quantiles.  Taking the supremum in (1), applying (3), and then taking the conditional lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile proves \(c_n=O_{P_n}(1)\) uniformly over the legal class.

On the nonfallback event the coordinate diameters are exactly \(2c_n/\sqrt n\) by \(\texttt{def:common-gaussian-rectangle}\).  If the full-plane branch is assigned extended diameter \(+\infty\), then for every \(\eta>0\) one can first choose a uniform critical-value bound and then use the uniformly vanishing fallback probability to obtain
\[
\limsup_n\sup_{P_n\in\mathcal M}
P_n\{\operatorname{diam}(C_n)>K_\eta n^{-1/2}\}\le\eta.
\]
This is precisely uniform \(O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\) for the extended diameter.  Because the argument made no lower-bound assumption on an individual cell or on an active-cut gap, it includes the stated root-\(n\) emerging-support and tied-cut arrays.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Rate-adaptive width prevents support robustness from degenerating into a fixed deterministic guard while retaining an honest definition on every sample. \textit{Gap.} Gabriel, Sachs, and Jensen recommend bootstrap care at boundaries but do not establish uniform root-\(n\) joint width through disappearing support and cut ties. \textit{Consumer.} Studies with rare covariate strata keep confidence widths on the sampling scale with probability tending uniformly to one while maintaining the full target population.

\begin{proposition}[prop:k4-kill-test]\label{prop:k4-kill-test}
Suppose \(K=4\), \(m_*\le1/4\), \(0\) and \(1\) are distinct elements of \(\mathcal X\), and \(\rho(0)=1\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the response-type masses in \(\texttt{def:k4-legal-array}\) are nonnegative, sum to one in each positive-probability covariate cell, satisfy conditional instrument independence, treatment and selected-outcome consistency, global no-defiers, the declared cellwise weak selection monotonicity, fair-instrument overlap, and the aggregate mass floor; hence \(Q_n^{(4)}\in\mathcal M\) and the displayed table is exactly \(p_n^{(4)}\). Its unconditional capacities are \(H_0=(1-r_n)(\ell^0,h^0)\) and \(H_1=r_n(\ell^1,h^1)\). The emerging-cell upper cuts are exactly \((.40,.30,.30,.40)\), so two cuts bind simultaneously; its lower and upper benefit masses are \(0\) and \(.30\), while the main-cell masses are \(0\) and \(.20\). The table contains the selected outcome atom \(p_n^{(4)}(0,0;0,1,0)=\lambda_{\mathrm r}/n\), and \[M(p_n^{(4)})=.25+.25r_n,\qquad \Phi(p_n^{(4)})=\left(0,\frac{.8+.4r_n}{1+r_n}\right).\] For either \(a_n=n^{-1/3}\) or \(a_n=n^{-0.49}\), the specific-array legal envelope has \(c_n\to_P\sqrt{1.28}z_{1-\alpha/2}\), the fallback probability tends to zero, \(P_n\{\Phi(p_n^{(4)})\in C_n\}\to1-\alpha\), and \[\sqrt n\{U(\widehat p_n)-U(p_n^{(4)})\}\Rightarrow N(0,1.28),\] where \(U(\widehat p_n)\) denotes the second coordinate of \(\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(\widehat p_n)\). The variance follows from the limiting main-cell ratio: its numerator event has probability \(.10\), its denominator event has probability \(.125\), and \(\{.10(.2)^2+.025(.8)^2\}/.125^2=1.28\); the emerging cell contributes deterministic root-\(n\) drift but only \(o_P(1)\) centered root-\(n\) noise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume that \(0\) and \(1\) are distinct elements of \(\mathcal X\) and that \(\rho(0)=1\), as required by the narrowed statement; all other covariate values have probability zero under the construction.  Take \(n\) so large that \(0<\delta_n<0.10\).  In cell \(X=0\), the survivor masses sum to
\[
\sum_k\ell_k^0=0.25,
\]
the selection-gain masses are
\[
h^0-\ell^0=(0.10-\delta_n,0,\delta_n,0.15),
\]
which are nonnegative and sum to \(0.25\), and the never-selected mass is \(0.50\).  Thus the cell total is one.  In cell \(X=1\),
\[
\sum_j\gamma_{ij}=2\ell_i^1\sum_jh_j^1=\ell_i^1,
\qquad
\sum_i\gamma_{ij}=2h_j^1\sum_i\ell_i^1=h_j^1,
\]
because both margins have total \(0.50\).  Hence \(\gamma\) has the displayed margins and total \(0.50\); adding the never-selected mass again gives one.

Every unit is a complier, so treatment monotonicity and treatment consistency hold.  The construction has \(S_{\mathrm{pot}}(0)\le S_{\mathrm{pot}}(1)\) in the main cell and equality in the emerging cell, proving the increasing version of cellwise weak selection monotonicity.  Conditional independence holds because the fair instrument is drawn independently of all potential variables given \(X\).  Selection and outcome consistency are imposed in the construction.  The fair propensity belongs to \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\) because \(\epsilon<1/2\).  The aggregate-mass calculation below gives \(M\ge1/4\ge m_*\).  Thus every assumption defining \(\mathcal M\) holds.

Under \(Z=0\), all units receive treatment zero, and the selected outcome distribution among compliers is \(\ell^x\).  Under \(Z=1\), all receive treatment one, and the corresponding selected distribution is \(h^x\).  The opposite-treatment selected atoms are zero.  Multiplication by \(Q(Z=z\mid X=x)=1/2\) and by the displayed covariate probability therefore gives every row of the observed table.  In particular,
\[
\frac12(1-r_n)\delta_n=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm r}}n.
\]
This proves that the table is exactly \(p_n^{(4)}\).  The same contrast calculation in \(\texttt{def:capacity-map}\) gives
\[
H_0(p_n^{(4)})=(1-r_n)(\ell^0,h^0),
\qquad
H_1(p_n^{(4)})=r_n(\ell^1,h^1).
\]

For the emerging cell, the four upper-cut expressions are
\[
0.40,\quad0.30,\quad0.30,\quad0.40.
\]
The prefix differences for the lower functional are \((-0.05,-0.05,0,0)\), and the two totals agree.  Hence
\[
F_L(\ell^1,h^1)=0,qquad F_U(\ell^1,h^1)=0.30,qquad m(\ell^1,h^1)=0.50.
\]
For the main cell, the survivor totals are \(0.25\) and \(0.50\).  Its lower prefix differences are strictly negative for large \(n\), while its upper cuts are
\[
0.40,\quad0.30+\delta_n,\quad0.30+\delta_n,\quad0.20.
\]
Consequently
\[
F_L(\ell^0,h^0)=0,qquad F_U(\ell^0,h^0)=0.20,qquad m(\ell^0,h^0)=0.25.
\]
Positive homogeneity now yields
\[
M(p_n^{(4)})=0.25(1-r_n)+0.50r_n=0.25+0.25r_n,
\]
and
\[
\Phi(p_n^{(4)})=
\left(0,\frac{0.20(1-r_n)+0.30r_n}{0.25(1-r_n)+0.50r_n}\right)
=\left(0,\frac{0.8+0.4r_n}{1+r_n}\right).
\]

We next derive the centered limit.  Let \(A_n\) be the main-cell observed event \(X=0,Z=0,S=1,Y\le2\), and let \(B_n^0\) be \(X=0,Z=0,S=1\).  Their probabilities converge to \(0.10\) and \(0.125\), respectively, and \(A_n\subset B_n^0\).  The fixed gaps just computed make the main-cell upper cut equal to its last cut with probability tending to one.  In the emerging cell the expected sample size is \(nr_n=\sqrt n\).  Its conditional proportions fluctuate at order \(n^{-1/4}\), and multiplication by its empirical cell mass makes its unconditional capacity error \(O_P(n^{-3/4})\).  The globally Lipschitz cut maps preserve this order, even at the two tied upper cuts.  The atom of probability \(\lambda_{\mathrm r}/n\) in the main cell contributes only \(O_P(n^{-1})\) to the empirical law.  It follows by the quotient expansion that, writing \(U(\widehat p_n)\) for the second coordinate of \(\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(\widehat p_n)\),
\[
\sqrt n\{U(\widehat p_n)-U(p_n^{(4)})\}
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\frac{\mathbf 1(A_{ni})-0.8\mathbf 1(B_{ni}^0)}{0.125}+o_P(1).
\tag{4}
\]
The summands are uniformly bounded and centered up to a quantity tending to zero.  Expanding their characteristic functions to second order proves the triangular-array central limit theorem.  Since \(A_n\subset B_n^0\), the limiting variance in (4) is
\[
\frac{0.10(1-0.8)^2+(0.125-0.10)(0.8)^2}{0.125^2}=1.28.
\]
The main-cell lower cut is separated at zero; the emerging-cell lower error is \(O_P(n^{-3/4})\).  Thus its centered root-\(n\) limit is zero.

It remains to check the actual nearby-base calibration.  Let \(p_0\) be the limit obtained by deleting the emerging cell and setting the rare main-cell mass to zero.  The main-cell survivor-total comparison, lower inequalities, and upper last-cut optimizer have fixed positive gaps at \(p_0\).  For a direction supported on the positive-probability main-cell atoms, the limiting derivative is obtained by differentiating the ratio \(p(A)/p(B)\):
\[
d_{p_0,L}(g)=0,
\qquad
d_{p_0,U}(g)=\frac{g(A)-0.8g(B)}{0.125}.
\tag{5}
\]
For either \(a_n=n^{-1/3}\) or \(a_n=n^{-0.49}\), empirical concentration and \(r_n=n^{-1/2}=o(a_n)\) imply that every \(b\in B_n\) is \(O_P(a_n)\) from \(p_0\).  The fixed gaps and the explicit finite quotient formulas in \(\texttt{lem:finite-capacity-geometry}\) then give, uniformly over such \(b\), equation (5) plus a remainder bounded by \(C a_n\|g\|\) on the positive-probability main coordinates.  The possible kink at the rare main-cell coordinate has Gaussian scale \(O_{P^*}(n^{-1/2})\), and changing its nearby base mass by \(O(a_n)\) changes the coefficient on common coordinates by only \(O(a_n)\).  All emerging-cell Gaussian coordinates have total variance \(O(r_n)\), so their norm is \(O_{P^*}(n^{-1/4})\).  At an empty emerging-cell base, Gaussian regression within an arm gives \(G_A=(q_A/q_{xz})q_{xz}(G)+R_A\), where \(R_A\) is independent of \(q_{xz}(G)\), its variance is at most \(q_{xz}\), and \(\operatorname{var}\{q_{xz}(G)\}=q_{xz}(1-q_{xz})\).  Thus every ratio in the empty-cell formula has a uniformly tight Cauchy remainder, while its multiplying cell total is \(O_{P^*}(\sqrt{r_n})\).  The empty-cell contribution is therefore also \(O_{P^*}(n^{-1/4})\) at every fixed conditional quantile.  Consequently, conditionally in probability,
\[
T_n(G^*)=|Z_n^*|+o_{P^*}(1),
\qquad
Z_n^*\Rightarrow N(0,1.28).
\]
The distribution of \(|N(0,1.28)|\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\).  The lower-quantile convention consequently gives
\[
c_n\to_P\sqrt{1.28}\,z_{1-\alpha/2}.
\]
The concentration argument above puts \(p_n^{(4)}\) in \(B_n\) with probability tending to one, and \(M(\widehat p_n)>0\) with probability tending to one because the main cell has fixed positive survivor mass.  Combining the critical-value limit with (4) and the negligible lower coordinate proves
\[
P_n\{\Phi(p_n^{(4)})\in C_n\}\longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\]
This proves every assertion for both prescribed choices of \(a_n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-legal-probe]\label{lem:finite-legal-probe}
The finite symmetric probe certificate is false already when \(K=4\) and \(\mathcal X\) is a singleton.  More precisely, for every \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and \(m_*\in(0,1)\), there are \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) with \(M(p)\ge m_*\), a number \(r_1>0\), and \(g\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\) such that \(E_{p,r}(g)=0\) for every \(0<r\le r_1\).  Nevertheless, for every \(\kappa>0\) and all sufficiently small such \(r\), there is \(q\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\) satisfying \(\operatorname{supp}(q)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(p)\), \(\lVert q-p\rVert_\infty<\kappa r\), and \(M(q)>0\), while \(\lVert\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q)-\Phi(p)\rVert_\infty>0\).  Consequently, no finite collection of rows with \(V(z)=\max_\nu|a_\nu^{\mathsf T}z|\) and \(V(z)\le E_{p,r}(z)\) for every \(z\) can also satisfy \(\lVert\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q)-\Phi(p)\rVert_\infty\le V(q-p)\) for every such \(q\).  Thus the original positive assertion fails before its covariance-traction condition is reached.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove the proposed negative replacement by an explicit latent response-type table.  The failure occurs before the covariance assertion \((\mathrm{P3})\): the absolute-linear functional \(V_{p,r}\) is even in its argument, whereas the legal inner envelope need not be even at a one-sided selection-total face.

Fix the declared \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and \(m_*\in(0,1)\), take \(K=4\), let \(\mathcal X=\{x\}\), and put
\[
 M_0=\frac{1+m_*}{2}\in(m_*,1),\qquad \rho(x)=1,
\]
\[

 \qquad

\tag{1}

Both vectors have total mass \(M_0\).  For \(i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), define
\[
 \gamma_{ij}=\frac{\ell_i h_j}{M_0}.
\tag{2}
\]
The complete nonzero latent response-type table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc|c|c|c}
\text{type}&d_0&d_1&s_0&s_1&y_0&y_1&\text{mass}&A_0&A_1\\ \hline
C_{ij}&0&1&1&1&i&j&\gamma_{ij}&(0,1,i)&(1,1,j)\\
N&0&0&0&0&0&0&(1-M_0)/2&(0,0,0)&(0,0,0)\\
A&1&1&0&0&0&0&(1-M_0)/2&(1,0,0)&(1,0,0)
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
where the first row represents all sixteen pairs \((i,j)\).  To make the enumeration numerical, the four entries in each row of the \(4\times4\) matrix \(\gamma\) are, respectively,

 \left(\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32}\right),

\[
 \left(\frac{3M_0}{40},\frac{3M_0}{40},\frac{3M_0}{40},\frac{3M_0}{40}\right),
 \left(\frac{9M_0}{80},\frac{9M_0}{80},\frac{9M_0}{80},\frac{9M_0}{80}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus every latent mass in \((3)\) is nonnegative and
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}+\frac{1-M_0}{2}+\frac{1-M_0}{2}
 =M_0+1-M_0=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Draw the type in \((3)\), draw \(Z\) independently of that type with \(Q(Z=1\mid X=x)=1/2\), and form \((D,S,SY)\) from the three consistency relations.  This construction satisfies \(\text{ass:conditional-iv-independence}\), \(\text{ass:consistency}\), \(\text{ass:selection-consistency}\), and \(\text{ass:outcome-consistency}\).  Every row has \(d_0\le d_1\), proving \(\text{ass:no-defiers}\).  The only compliers are the \(C_{ij}\), and they have \(s_1-s_0=0\); hence \(\rho(x)(s_1-s_0)=0\), which proves \(\text{ass:cellwise-selection-monotonicity}\).  Finally, \(1/2\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), so \(\text{ass:instrument-overlap}\) holds.

Indeed, in the lower block the selected \(D=0\) conditional mass is \(\ell_i\) under \(Z=0\) and zero under \(Z=1\); in the upper block the selected \(D=1\) conditional mass is \(h_j\) under \(Z=1\) and zero under \(Z=0\). Since the two totals agree, \(\text{def:capacity-map}\) and \((8)\) give \(F_L(\ell,h)=0\).  The four upper-cut values, in threshold order \(t=0,1,2,3\), are

by \(\text{def:endpoint-map}\).

Let \(u=(x,1,(1,1,1))\), let \(v=(x,1,(0,0,0))\), and define the ambient atom direction

 g=e_u-e_v\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}.

It satisfies
\[
 \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}g=q_{x0}(g)=q_{x1}(g)=q_x(g)=0.
\tag{12}
\]
We claim that there is \(r_1>0\) such that
\[
 E_{p,r}(g)=0,\qquad 0<r\le r_1.
\tag{13}
\]
This assertion concerns every legal base in the ball, not merely the displayed law \(p\).

To prove \((13)\), put \(J_A=\lvert\mathcal A\rvert\).  If \(b\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\) and \(\lVert b-p\rVert_\infty\le r<(4J_A)^{-1}\), then
\[
 q_{xz}(b)\ge\frac14,
 \qquad
 \lvert q_{xz}(b)-q_{xz}(p)\rvert\le J_A r.
\tag{14}
\]
For an atom numerator \(w\) in arm \(z\), \((14)\) implies
\[
 \left|\frac{b_w}{q_{xz}(b)}-\frac{p_w}{q_{xz}(p)}\right|
 \le \frac{|b_w-p_w|}{q_{xz}(b)}
 +\frac{p_w|q_{xz}(b)-q_{xz}(p)|}{q_{xz}(p)q_{xz}(b)}
 \le4(1+J_A)r.
\tag{15}
\]
Each raw contrast defining \(H_x\) contains two such ratios, and positive part is one-Lipschitz.  Since a probability law on the singleton covariate support has \(q_x(b)=1\), \((15)\) yields the coordinate bound
\[
 \lVert H_x(b)-H_x(p)\rVert_\infty\le C_Hr,
 \qquad C_H=8(1+J_A).
\tag{16}

 s_1(b)-s_0(b)
 =\sum_{t:d_0(t)<d_1(t)}Q_b(T=t)\{s_1(t)-s_0(t)\}\ge0,
\tag{17}

 \partial_+F_L\{H_x(b);D H_x(b)[g]\}=0.
\tag{19}

The unique upper cut remains \(t=2\); upper coordinate \(1\) is absent from

\tag{20}

 \partial_+m\{H_x(b);D H_x(b)[g]\}=0.
\tag{21}

 0<\tau<\min\left\{\kappa r,\frac{M_0}{8}\right\},
 \qquad q_\tau=p-\tau g.
\tag{23}

 \qquad M(q_\tau)=M_0-2\tau>0.
\tag{25}

 -\frac{M_0}{8}-2\tau,\qquad
 -\frac{M_0}{4},\qquad

\tag{26}
\]
Hence \(F_L\{H_x(q_\tau)\}=0\).  The four upper cuts and the mass cap are

 \frac{3M_0}{4}-2\tau,\quad
 \frac{5M_0}{8},\quad
 \frac{M_0}{2},\quad
 \frac{11M_0}{20},\quad

\tag{27}
\]
The restriction \(\tau<M_0/8\) makes \(M_0/2\) the minimum in \((27)\).  Equations \((25)\)--\((27)\) and \(\text{def:endpoint-map}\) give

 \Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q_\tau)
 =\left(0,\frac{M_0/2}{M_0-2\tau}\right),
\]

 \left\lVert\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q_\tau)-\Phi(p)\right\rVert_\infty
 =\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}>0.
\tag{28}

Suppose the rows required by the original assertion existed for this \(p\) and \(r\).  From \((\mathrm{P1})\) and \((13)\),

 0\le V_{p,r}(g)\le E_{p,r}(g)=0.
\tag{29}

Because \(V_{p,r}(z)=\max_\nu|a_\nu^{\mathsf T}z|\) is absolutely homogeneous and even, \((29)\) implies

 V_{p,r}(q_\tau-p)=V_{p,r}(-\tau g)=\tau V_{p,r}(g)=0.
\tag{30}

 0<\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}
 \le V_{p,r}(q_\tau-p)=0,

a contradiction.  Since \(r\) can be chosen below any proposed \(r_0>0\), no constants or finite rows satisfying the original \((\mathrm{P1})\)--\((\mathrm{P2})\) can exist.  The issue is not a missing nonnegativity budget, donor correction, zero-arm jump, or covariance estimate: all instrument cells and every capacity coordinate in this witness are uniformly positive, the latent table is legal with fixed propensity, and the contradiction precedes \((\mathrm{P3})\). \end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:scale-free-unsigned-gaussian-max]\label{lem:scale-free-unsigned-gaussian-max}
Let \(Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)\) be a centered Gaussian vector in \(\mathbb R^p\) with \(p\ge3\), let \(\sigma_{\max}^2=\max_{1\le i\le p}\mathbb E[Z_i^2]\), and let \(M^*=\max_{1\le i\le p}|Z_i|\).  Then, for any \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\delta}
 \Pr\{t\le M^*<t+\delta\}
 \le \frac{4\log(2p)}{\sigma_{\max}\vee t}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:k4-oriented-parity-certificate]\label{lem:k4-oriented-parity-certificate}
Let \(K=4\), let \(\mathcal X=\{x\}\), and let \(\rho(x)=1\).  For every \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and \(m_*\in(0,1)\), put \(M_0=(1+m_*)/2\).  There are an explicitly constructed \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), a direction \(g\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\), and \(r_0>0\) such that \(M(p)=M_0\) and, for every \(0<r\le r_0\),
\[
 E_{p,r}(g)=0,
 \qquad
 E_{p,r}(-g)\ge M_0^{-1}.
\]
Consequently the ray-restricted oriented functional \(W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(tg)=(-t)_+/M_0\) satisfies \(W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(tg)\le E_{p,r}(tg)\) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\), while \(W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(g)=0\) and \(W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(-g)=M_0^{-1}>0\).  Moreover, for every sufficiently small \(\tau\in(0,r)\), the ambient law \(q_\tau=p-\tau g\) and an explicit legal base \(b_\tau\in\mathcal B(p,r)\) obey
\[
 \left\|\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q_\tau)-\Phi(p)\right\|_\infty
 =\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}
 \le \left\|d_{b_\tau}(-\tau g)\right\|_\infty
 =\frac{\tau M_0}{(M_0-2\tau)^2}.
\]
Thus retaining orientation repairs both the parity contradiction and the finite-curvature comparison on the accepted singleton-cell witness; this lemma makes no claim about simultaneous faces outside that witness.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 \ell=M_0\left(\frac18,\frac18,\frac3{10},\frac9{20}\right),
 \qquad
 h=M_0\left(\frac14,\frac14,\frac14,\frac14\right),
 \qquad
 \gamma_{ij}=\frac{\ell_i h_j}{M_0}.
\tag{1}
\]
Give latent complier-survivor type
\[
 C_{ij}=(d_0,d_1,s_0,s_1,y_0,y_1)=(0,1,1,1,i,j)
\]
mass \(\gamma_{ij}\), give the never-taker never-selected type
\(N=(0,0,0,0,0,0)\) mass \((1-M_0)/2\), and give the always-taker never-selected type \(A=(1,1,0,0,0,0)\) mass \((1-M_0)/2\).  Since
\[
 \sum_{i,j=0}^3\gamma_{ij}
 =M_0^{-1}\left(\sum_i\ell_i\right)\left(\sum_jh_j\right)=M_0,
\tag{2}
\]
these nonnegative masses sum to one.  Draw \(Z\) independently of the latent type with probability one half, and apply the three consistency relations.  Independence proves \(\text{ass:conditional-iv-independence}\); the construction proves \(\text{ass:consistency}\), \(\text{ass:selection-consistency}\), and \(\text{ass:outcome-consistency}\).  Every type has \(d_0\le d_1\), and every complier has \(s_1-s_0=0\), proving \(\text{ass:no-defiers}\) and \(\text{ass:cellwise-selection-monotonicity}\).  Finally, \(1/2\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), so \(\text{ass:instrument-overlap}\) holds.

Let \(p\) be the induced observed law.  Substitution in \(\text{def:capacity-map}\) gives
\[
 q_{x0}(p)=q_{x1}(p)=\frac12,
 \qquad H_x(p)=(\ell,h),
 \qquad M(p)=M_0\ge m_*.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) by \(\text{def:legal-model}\) and \(\text{def:observed-law-image}\).  The lower-minus-upper prefix differences and the upper-cut values are, respectively,
\[
 M_0\left(-\frac18,-\frac14,-\frac15,0\right)
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 M_0\left(\frac34,\frac58,\frac12,\frac{11}{20}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Therefore the lower numerator is zero, the unique upper cut is \(t=2\), its numerator is \(M_0/2\), and
\[
 \Phi(p)=\left(0,\frac12\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
 u=(x,1,(1,1,1)),\qquad v=(x,1,(0,0,0)),
 \qquad g=e_u-e_v.
\tag{6}
\]
This direction preserves both instrument-arm masses.  At any sufficiently nearby legal base \(b\), all capacity coordinates remain positive, the proper prefix inequalities and the unique upper cut in \((4)\) persist, and increasing selection monotonicity gives \(s_1(b)\ge s_0(b)\).  Direct differentiation of the conditional contrasts therefore gives
\[
 D H_x(b)[g]=(0,0,0,0;0,q_{x1}(b)^{-1},0,0).
\tag{7}
\]
The direction in \((7)\) leaves the lower numerator, the unique upper numerator, and \(\min\{s_0(b),s_1(b)\}=s_0(b)\) unchanged.  The quotient rule in \(\text{def:endpoint-map}\) hence yields \(d_b(g)=0\).  Compactness of the strict inequalities in \((4)\) supplies \(r_0>0\) for which this conclusion holds for every \(b\in\mathcal B(p,r)\) and every \(0<r\le r_0\).  By \(\text{def:deterministic-inner-envelope}\),
\[
 E_{p,r}(g)=0.
\tag{8}
\]

At the tied base \(p\), direction \(-g\) lowers the upper total at rate two and leaves the active upper numerator fixed.  Hence
\[
 \partial_+M(p;-g)=-2,
 \qquad
 \partial_+\bar N_U(p;-g)=0,
 \qquad
 d_{p,U}(-g)=\frac{1}{M_0}.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(p\in\mathcal B(p,r)\), equations \((8)\)--\((9)\) and positive homogeneity in a nonnegative scalar imply
\[
 E_{p,r}(-g)\ge M_0^{-1},
 \qquad
 W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(tg)=\frac{(-t)_+}{M_0}le E_{p,r}(tg)
 \quad(t\in\mathbb R).
\tag{10}
\]
This proves the oriented parity assertion.

It remains to verify the finite increment rather than only its tangent.  Both \(u\) and \(v\) have positive \(p\)-mass.  For sufficiently small \(\tau>0\), \(q_\tau=p-\tau g\) is therefore a probability vector with support contained in that of \(p\).  Its capacity pair and endpoint are

 \qquad M(q_\tau)=M_0-2\tau,

 =\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}.
\tag{12}

 R=(0,1,0,0,0,0).

 2\tau\le\gamma_{31}=\frac{9M_0}{80}.
\tag{13}

 H_x(b_\tau)=(\ell-2\tau e_3,h-2\tau e_1),

This is the claimed explicit nearby-legal-base domination.

\begin{proposition}[prop:oriented-probe-frontier]\label{prop:oriented-probe-frontier}
Let \(K=4\), let \(\mathcal X=\{x\}\), let \(\rho(x)=1\), and fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and \(m_*\in(0,1)\).  Put \(M_0=(1+m_*)/2\).  There exist the explicit legal law \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), direction \(g\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\), and radius \(r_0>0\) constructed by \(\text{lem:finite-legal-probe}\) and \(\text{lem:k4-oriented-parity-certificate}\), with \(M(p)=M_0\), for which the following frontier holds.

First, \(E_{p,r}(g)=0\) for every \(0<r\le r_0\).  For every \(\kappa>0\) and all sufficiently small such \(r\), no choice of an integer \(N\ge1\) and rows \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}\) makes the even finite max-absolute-linear functional
\[
 V_{p,r}(z)=\max_{1\le\nu\le N}|a_\nu^{\mathsf T}z|
\]
can satisfy simultaneously
\[
 V_{p,r}(z)\le E_{p,r}(z)\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}
\tag{P1}
\]
and
\[
 \|\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q)-\Phi(p)\|_\infty
 \le V_{p,r}(q-p)
\tag{P2}
\]
for every \(q\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\) satisfying \(\operatorname{supp}(q)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(p)\), \(\|q-p\|_\infty<\kappa r\), and \(M(q)>0\).

Second, on the same one-sided legal face, define the non-even functional on \(\operatorname{span}\{g\}\) by
\[
 W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(tg)=\frac{(-t)_+}{M_0},\qquad t\in\mathbb R,
\]
satisfies
\[
 W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(tg)\le E_{p,r}(tg),\qquad
 W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(g)=0,
 \qquad
 W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(-g)=M_0^{-1}>0.
\tag{P3}
\]
For every sufficiently small \(\tau\in(0,r)\), the opposite finite displacement \(q_\tau=p-\tau g\) admits an explicit nearby legal base \(b_\tau\in\mathcal B(p,r)\) such that
\[
 \|\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q_\tau)-\Phi(p)\|_\infty
 =\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}
 \le \|d_{b_\tau}(-\tau g)\|_\infty
 =\frac{\tau M_0}{(M_0-2\tau)^2}.
\tag{P4}
\]
Thus orientation is necessary for this finite-probe strategy and repairs parity and nearby-legal derivative domination on this face.  These conclusions do not establish a global simultaneous-face comparison or uniform coverage over all triangular arrays.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose the common explicit singleton-cell law \(p\) and direction \(g\) used in \(\text{lem:finite-legal-probe}\) and \(\text{lem:k4-oriented-parity-certificate}\).  Both constructions use \(M_0=(1+m_*)/2\), the same survivor margins, the same product coupling of those margins, and the same observed direction.  Replace their two positive radii by their minimum and call it \(r_0\).

By \(\text{lem:finite-legal-probe}\), for every \(0<r\le r_0\),
\[
 E_{p,r}(g)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
That lemma further states that for every \(\kappa>0\) and every sufficiently small such \(r\), the pointwise bound in \((\mathrm{P1})\) is incompatible with domination of all endpoint increments in \((\mathrm{P2})\) by any functional of the displayed even finite max-absolute-linear form.  This proves the first assertion.

By \(\text{lem:k4-oriented-parity-certificate}\), the non-even ray functional defined in the proposition obeys, for every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 W^{\mathrm{ray}}_{p,r}(tg)=\frac{(-t)_+}{M_0}
 \le E_{p,r}(tg).
\tag{2}
\]
Putting \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\) in \((2)\) gives all three relations in \((\mathrm{P3})\).  The same lemma constructs, for every sufficiently small \(\tau\in(0,r)\), a legal \(b_\tau\in\mathcal B(p,r)\) and proves exactly
\[
 \|\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(p-\tau g)-\Phi(p)\|_\infty
 =\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}
 \le\|d_{b_\tau}(-\tau g)\|_\infty
 =\frac{\tau M_0}{(M_0-2\tau)^2},
\tag{3}
\]
which is \((\mathrm{P4})\).  Equations \((1)\)--\((3)\) prove the stated method frontier.  Neither cited lemma asserts a construction across simultaneous legal faces or a conditional critical-value transfer over arbitrary triangular arrays, so the final scope limitation follows without adding any further claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition preserves the exact same-face method frontier proved by the two explicit legal witness lemmas: even probes are impossible, while orientation repairs only that face. \textit{Gap.} It does not supply a global construction over simultaneous legal faces or a critical-value transfer over arbitrary triangular arrays. \textit{Consumer.} This is a standalone delivered proposition and the direct consumer of both witness lemmas; it records the strongest proved conclusion without implying all-array uniform coverage.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target remains the same-unit benefit probability \(\theta_B\), and the imported identification theorem gives its sharp identified set \(\Theta_I(p)=[L(p),U(p)]\). The empirical center, directional envelope, Gaussian critical value, and rectangle are observed-law procedures; they do not define the causal target. At separated regular faces the rectangle has exact oracle Gaussian calibration. These facts do not imply uniform coverage over all changing-support and simultaneous tied-face arrays; that implication is precisely the unresolved legal-relative-comparison question.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The explicit legal \(K=4\) singleton-cell construction proves that no even functional \(\max_\nu|a_\nu^{\mathsf T}z|\) lying below the legal envelope can dominate all nearby endpoint increments. A bounded oriented ray and an explicit survivor-to-never-selected transfer repair parity and finite curvature on that face. Globally, intersections of raw-capacity zeros, selection-total ties, and tied lower or upper cuts permit cancellations among numerator, denominator, and cut derivatives, so pointwise domination of every required oriented row by the full legal envelope is unproved. The legal donor transfers change both outcome margins, and a complete latent response-type transfer with row-by-row covariance traction is also unavailable. Consequently relative lower-quantile transfer for the non-even Gaussian maximum and the exact-zero-scale local-flatness branch remain unproved. The legal-relative-comparison question is therefore the sole all-array inferential target, and all-array uniform joint coverage is not established.

\section{Honest scope}

Sharp identification is imported from the accepted parent. This note proves measurability of the total procedure, exact oracle \(1-\alpha\) coverage at fixed separated regular faces, uniformly negligible fallback probability and \(O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\) finite-coordinate width, the stated \(K=4\) changing-support diagnostic, a legal singleton-cell counterexample to every even symmetric-probe reduction, and a bounded oriented repair on that singleton face.
