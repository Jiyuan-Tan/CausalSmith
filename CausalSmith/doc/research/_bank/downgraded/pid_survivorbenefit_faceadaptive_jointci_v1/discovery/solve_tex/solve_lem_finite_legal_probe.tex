We prove the proposed negative replacement by an explicit latent response-type table.  The failure occurs before the covariance assertion \((\mathrm{P3})\): the absolute-linear functional \(V_{p,r}\) is even in its argument, whereas the legal inner envelope need not be even at a one-sided selection-total face.

Fix the declared \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and \(m_*\in(0,1)\), take \(K=4\), let \(\mathcal X=\{x\}\), and put
\[
 M_0=\frac{1+m_*}{2}\in(m_*,1),\qquad \rho(x)=1,
\]
\[
 \ell=M_0\left(\frac18,\frac18,\frac3{10},\frac9{20}\right),
 \qquad
 h=M_0\left(\frac14,\frac14,\frac14,\frac14\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Both vectors have total mass \(M_0\).  For \(i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), define
\[
 \gamma_{ij}=\frac{\ell_i h_j}{M_0}.
\tag{2}
\]
The complete nonzero latent response-type table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc|c|c|c}
\text{type}&d_0&d_1&s_0&s_1&y_0&y_1&\text{mass}&A_0&A_1\\ \hline
C_{ij}&0&1&1&1&i&j&\gamma_{ij}&(0,1,i)&(1,1,j)\\
N&0&0&0&0&0&0&(1-M_0)/2&(0,0,0)&(0,0,0)\\
A&1&1&0&0&0&0&(1-M_0)/2&(1,0,0)&(1,0,0)
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
where the first row represents all sixteen pairs \((i,j)\).  To make the enumeration numerical, the four entries in each row of the \(4\times4\) matrix \(\gamma\) are, respectively,
\[
 \left(\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32}\right),
 \left(\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32},\frac{M_0}{32}\right),
\]
\[
 \left(\frac{3M_0}{40},\frac{3M_0}{40},\frac{3M_0}{40},\frac{3M_0}{40}\right),
 \left(\frac{9M_0}{80},\frac{9M_0}{80},\frac{9M_0}{80},\frac{9M_0}{80}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus every latent mass in \((3)\) is nonnegative and
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}+\frac{1-M_0}{2}+\frac{1-M_0}{2}
 =M_0+1-M_0=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Draw the type in \((3)\), draw \(Z\) independently of that type with \(Q(Z=1\mid X=x)=1/2\), and form \((D,S,SY)\) from the three consistency relations.  This construction satisfies \(\text{ass:conditional-iv-independence}\), \(\text{ass:consistency}\), \(\text{ass:selection-consistency}\), and \(\text{ass:outcome-consistency}\).  Every row has \(d_0\le d_1\), proving \(\text{ass:no-defiers}\).  The only compliers are the \(C_{ij}\), and they have \(s_1-s_0=0\); hence \(\rho(x)(s_1-s_0)=0\), which proves \(\text{ass:cellwise-selection-monotonicity}\).  Finally, \(1/2\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), so \(\text{ass:instrument-overlap}\) holds.

Let \(p\) be the induced observed atom law.  Its complete list of nonzero atoms is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{atom}&p\text{-mass}\\ \hline
(x,0,(0,1,i)),\quad i=0,1,2,3&\ell_i/2\\
(x,0,(0,0,0))&(1-M_0)/4\\
(x,0,(1,0,0))&(1-M_0)/4\\
(x,1,(1,1,j)),\quad j=0,1,2,3&h_j/2=M_0/8\\
(x,1,(0,0,0))&(1-M_0)/4\\
(x,1,(1,0,0))&(1-M_0)/4.
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]
Inserting \((6)\) into \(\text{def:capacity-map}\) gives
\[
 q_{x0}(p)=q_{x1}(p)=\frac12,\qquad q_x(p)=1,
 \qquad H_x(p)=(\ell,h).
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, in the lower block the selected \(D=0\) conditional mass is \(\ell_i\) under \(Z=0\) and zero under \(Z=1\); in the upper block the selected \(D=1\) conditional mass is \(h_j\) under \(Z=1\) and zero under \(Z=0\).  Hence \(M(p)=M_0\ge m_*\), proving \(\text{ass:aggregate-survivor-mass}\), and therefore \(p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) by \(\text{def:legal-model}\) and \(\text{def:observed-law-image}\).

We next identify the operative capacity face.  The four lower-minus-upper prefix differences at \((\ell,h)\) are
\[
 M_0\left(-\frac18,-\frac14,-\frac15,0\right).
\tag{8}
\]
Since the two totals agree, \(\text{def:capacity-map}\) and \((8)\) give \(F_L(\ell,h)=0\).  The four upper-cut values, in threshold order \(t=0,1,2,3\), are
\[
 M_0\left(\frac34,\frac58,\frac12,\frac{11}{20}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
The unique minimizing cut is \(t=2\), its value \(M_0/2\) is strictly below the mass cap \(M_0\), and therefore
\[
 \Phi(p)=\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(p)=\left(0,\frac12\right)
\tag{10}
\]
by \(\text{def:endpoint-map}\).

Let \(u=(x,1,(1,1,1))\), let \(v=(x,1,(0,0,0))\), and define the ambient atom direction
\[
 g=e_u-e_v\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal J}.
\tag{11}
\]
It satisfies
\[
 \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}g=q_{x0}(g)=q_{x1}(g)=q_x(g)=0.
\tag{12}
\]
We claim that there is \(r_1>0\) such that
\[
 E_{p,r}(g)=0,\qquad 0<r\le r_1.
\tag{13}
\]
This assertion concerns every legal base in the ball, not merely the displayed law \(p\).

To prove \((13)\), put \(J_A=\lvert\mathcal A\rvert\).  If \(b\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\) and \(\lVert b-p\rVert_\infty\le r<(4J_A)^{-1}\), then
\[
 q_{xz}(b)\ge\frac14,
 \qquad
 \lvert q_{xz}(b)-q_{xz}(p)\rvert\le J_A r.
\tag{14}
\]
For an atom numerator \(w\) in arm \(z\), \((14)\) implies
\[
 \left|\frac{b_w}{q_{xz}(b)}-\frac{p_w}{q_{xz}(p)}\right|
 \le \frac{|b_w-p_w|}{q_{xz}(b)}
 +\frac{p_w|q_{xz}(b)-q_{xz}(p)|}{q_{xz}(p)q_{xz}(b)}
 \le4(1+J_A)r.
\tag{15}
\]
Each raw contrast defining \(H_x\) contains two such ratios, and positive part is one-Lipschitz.  Since a probability law on the singleton covariate support has \(q_x(b)=1\), \((15)\) yields the coordinate bound
\[
 \lVert H_x(b)-H_x(p)\rVert_\infty\le C_Hr,
 \qquad C_H=8(1+J_A).
\tag{16}
\]

Choose \(r_1>0\) smaller than \((4J_A)^{-1}\), half each of the two positive atom masses in \((6)\) indexed by \(u\) and \(v\), and small enough that \((16)\) preserves positivity of all eight capacity coordinates, the strict proper-prefix inequalities in \((8)\), the unique-cut inequalities in \((9)\), and the strict gap between the cut value and the mass cap.  This is possible because the smallest relevant gap is positive: the capacity-coordinate minimum is \(M_0/8\), the proper-prefix gap is at least \(M_0/8\), and the unique-cut gap is \(M_0/20\).

Now fix \(b\in\mathcal B(p,r)\) with \(0<r\le r_1\).  Because \(b\) is legal and \(\rho(x)=1\), its latent-type expansion gives
\[
 s_1(b)-s_0(b)
 =\sum_{t:d_0(t)<d_1(t)}Q_b(T=t)\{s_1(t)-s_0(t)\}\ge0,
\tag{17}
\]
where \(s_0(b)\) and \(s_1(b)\) denote the totals of the lower and upper blocks of \(H_x(b)\).  Equation \((17)\) is exactly the declared cellwise selection monotonicity, summed over complier types.  Instrument overlap and the singleton support also give \(q_{xz}(b)>0\).

Using \((12)\) in the quotient derivatives from \(\text{def:capacity-map}\), and using capacity-coordinate positivity secured by the choice of \(r_1\), gives the full derivative transfer row
\[
 D H_x(b)[g]
 =\left(0,0,0,0;\ 0,q_{x1}(b)^{-1},0,0\right).
\tag{18}
\]
Thus this observed transfer increases only upper outcome margin \(1\); the survivor-to-survivor rows in \((3)\) are not involved, and no donor capacity coordinate is hidden in \((18)\).  This is precisely the one-sided total-face row that a legal survivor-to-survivor transfer cannot reverse while \(\rho(x)=1\).

The strict proper-prefix inequalities and \((17)\) imply \(F_L\{H_x(b)\}=0\).  Along the direction \((18)\), every affected lower-minus-upper prefix decreases, so
\[
 \partial_+F_L\{H_x(b);D H_x(b)[g]\}=0.
\tag{19}
\]
The unique upper cut remains \(t=2\); upper coordinate \(1\) is absent from
\(\sum_{k<2}\ell_k+\sum_{k>2}h_k\), and hence
\[
 \partial_+F_U\{H_x(b);D H_x(b)[g]\}=0.
\tag{20}
\]
Finally, \((17)\) says that the smaller total is the lower total.  Direction \((18)\) leaves that total unchanged and increases the upper total, including when the two totals are initially tied.  Consequently
\[
 \partial_+m\{H_x(b);D H_x(b)[g]\}=0.
\tag{21}
\]
The aggregate denominator is bounded away from zero by legality.  Applying the quotient rule in \(\text{def:endpoint-map}\) to \((19)\)--\((21)\) proves
\[
 d_b(g)=(0,0).
\tag{22}
\]
Since \((22)\) holds for every \(b\in\mathcal B(p,r)\), definition \(\text{def:deterministic-inner-envelope}\) proves \((13)\).

We now move in the opposite, observationally feasible but legally forbidden, direction.  Let \(\kappa>0\), take \(0<r\le r_1\), and choose
\[
 0<\tau<\min\left\{\kappa r,\frac{M_0}{8}\right\},
 \qquad q_\tau=p-\tau g.
\tag{23}
\]
Both atoms \(u\) and \(v\) have positive \(p\)-mass by \((6)\), so \((23)\) gives
\[
 q_\tau\in\Delta(\mathcal J),\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(q_\tau)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(p),
 \qquad \lVert q_\tau-p\rVert_\infty=\tau<\kappa r.
\tag{24}
\]
The arm masses remain one half.  Direct substitution therefore yields
\[
 H_x(q_\tau)=\left(\ell,h-2\tau e_1\right),
 \qquad M(q_\tau)=M_0-2\tau>0.
\tag{25}
\]
For the lower numerator, the four expressions inside its outer maximum, after adding \(\min\{(M_0-2\tau)-M_0,0\}=-2\tau\), are
\[
 -\frac{M_0}{8}-2\tau,\qquad
 -\frac{M_0}{4},\qquad
 -\frac{M_0}{5},\qquad 0.
\tag{26}
\]
Hence \(F_L\{H_x(q_\tau)\}=0\).  The four upper cuts and the mass cap are
\[
 \frac{3M_0}{4}-2\tau,\quad
 \frac{5M_0}{8},\quad
 \frac{M_0}{2},\quad
 \frac{11M_0}{20},\quad
 M_0-2\tau.
\tag{27}
\]
The restriction \(\tau<M_0/8\) makes \(M_0/2\) the minimum in \((27)\).  Equations \((25)\)--\((27)\) and \(\text{def:endpoint-map}\) give
\[
 \Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q_\tau)
 =\left(0,\frac{M_0/2}{M_0-2\tau}\right),
\]
\[
 \left\lVert\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}(q_\tau)-\Phi(p)\right\rVert_\infty
 =\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}>0.
\tag{28}
\]

Suppose the rows required by the original assertion existed for this \(p\) and \(r\).  From \((\mathrm{P1})\) and \((13)\),
\[
 0\le V_{p,r}(g)\le E_{p,r}(g)=0.
\tag{29}
\]
Because \(V_{p,r}(z)=\max_\nu|a_\nu^{\mathsf T}z|\) is absolutely homogeneous and even, \((29)\) implies
\[
 V_{p,r}(q_\tau-p)=V_{p,r}(-\tau g)=\tau V_{p,r}(g)=0.
\tag{30}
\]
Taking \(\kappa=\kappa_0\), conditions \((24)\) and \((25)\) are exactly the support, radius, and positivity premises of \((\mathrm{P2})\).  But \((\mathrm{P2})\), \((28)\), and \((30)\) would imply
\[
 0<\frac{\tau}{M_0-2\tau}
 \le V_{p,r}(q_\tau-p)=0,
\]
a contradiction.  Since \(r\) can be chosen below any proposed \(r_0>0\), no constants or finite rows satisfying the original \((\mathrm{P1})\)--\((\mathrm{P2})\) can exist.  The issue is not a missing nonnegativity budget, donor correction, zero-arm jump, or covariance estimate: all instrument cells and every capacity coordinate in this witness are uniformly positive, the latent table is legal with fixed propensity, and the contradiction precedes \((\mathrm{P3})\).  An honest repair must retain orientation, for example through positive parts of signed probe forms, and must re-prove the downstream lower-quantile comparison for that non-even envelope.
