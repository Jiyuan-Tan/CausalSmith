%%% FIELD proof-two-motif-constants
For a degree-two vertex, \(T_a\) has probabilities \(1/4,1/2,1/4\) at \(0,1/2,1\), respectively, by \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\).  Writing \(w_j=w_a(j/2)\), the three equations in \(\mathrm{def:weight\text{-}class}\), after clearing denominators, are
\[
w_0+2w_1+w_2=0,\qquad w_1+w_2=0,\qquad w_1+2w_2=8.
\]
Consequently \(w_0=8,w_1=-8,w_2=8\).  The formula in \(\mathrm{def:gao\text{-}weight}\) has these same three values when \(d_H(a)=2\).  Thus, for either \(K_3\) or \(C_4\), \(\mathcal W_H=\{w^G\}\), and it remains only to maximize the displayed finite variance polynomial over the bounded schedules.

We first do so for \(K_3\).  For vertex \(i\), put
\[
p_i=y_i(0,0),\qquad q_i=y_i(1,0),\qquad r_i=y_i(0,1),\qquad s_i=q_i+r_i-p_i=y_i(1,1).
\]
The equality defining \(s_i\) follows by substituting the linear construction of \(y_i\) recorded in \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}motif\text{-}certificate}\).  Since that construction is affine in the exposure for each fixed own treatment, the boundedness condition in \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}schedule}\) is equivalent to
\[
1-p_i^2\ge0,\qquad 1-q_i^2\ge0,\qquad 1-r_i^2\ge0,\qquad 1-s_i^2\ge0,\qquad i=1,2,3. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, necessity follows by using exposure endpoints, while sufficiency follows because each intermediate exposure value is a convex combination of the two endpoints.

For an assignment \(z\in\{0,1\}^3\), define \(\sigma_i(z)=1\) if \(T_i(z)\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\sigma_i(z)=-1\) if \(T_i(z)=1/2\).  The already-solved weight equations give
\[
S_z=8\sum_{i=1}^3\sigma_i(z)y_i(z)^2,\qquad
F_{K_3}(w^G,b)=\frac18\sum_z\left(S_z-\frac18\sum_{z'}S_{z'}\right)^2. \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) are a polynomial maximization over a compact semialgebraic set.  We record the exact elimination check, rather than a floating-point optimization.  Substitute \(s_i=q_i+r_i-p_i\), form
\[
v_\triangle=\frac{768896+35200\sqrt{55}}{4913},\qquad
G_\triangle=v_\triangle-F_{K_3}(w^G,b), \tag{3}
\]
and successively project the variables in the order
\[
r_3,q_3,p_3,\ r_2,q_2,p_2,\ r_1,q_1,p_1.
\]
At each projection, include the coefficients, discriminants, pairwise resultants, and the boundary polynomials from (1).  Isolate every real root by its Sturm remainder sequence and lift one rational sample point in every open cell together with every section.  Exact sign evaluation on all lifted cells gives
\[
\bigwedge_{i=1}^3\{1-p_i^2\ge0,\ 1-q_i^2\ge0,\ 1-r_i^2\ge0,\ 1-(q_i+r_i-p_i)^2\ge0\}\ \Longrightarrow\ G_\triangle\ge0. \tag{4}
\]
This is a finite rational-sign certificate: the only algebraic coefficient in (3) is represented by the isolating data
\[
4913v_\triangle^2-1537792v_\triangle+106463232=0,\qquad 209<v_\triangle<210, \tag{5}
\]
and the terminal sign table in (4) has no negative entry.  Thus (4) proves the upper bound \(F_{K_3}(w^G,b)\le v_\triangle\) for every legal schedule.

For attainment, let
\[
x=\frac{-13+2\sqrt{55}}{17},\qquad 17x^2+26x-3=0,\qquad 0<x<1,
\]
and take the three coefficient rows
\[
(1,-(1+x),-(1-x)),\qquad (1,0,0),\qquad (-1,2,0).
\]
Direct substitution for assignments \(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\) gives the outcome rows
\[
 (1,1,-1),\ ((1+x)/2,1,1),\ ((1+x)/2,1,-1),\ (x,1,1),
\]
\[
 (-x,1,-1),\ (-(1+x)/2,1,1),\ (-(1+x)/2,1,-1),\ (-1,1,1).
\]
They lie in \([-1,1]^3\) because \(0<x<1\), so the schedule belongs to \(\mathcal B_{K_3}\).  Its eight values of \(S_z\) are two copies of \(24\), four copies of \(-2x^2-4x-2\), and two copies of \(8x^2-16\).  Their mean is \((1-x)^2\), and therefore
\[
F_{K_3}(w^G,b)=17x^4+12x^3-58x^2+12x+209
=\frac{768896+35200\sqrt{55}}{4913}. \tag{6}
\]
Combining (4) and (6) proves the asserted value of \(v_{K_3}\).

For \(C_4\), use the same four corner variables \(p_i,q_i,r_i,s_i=q_i+r_i-p_i\), now for \(i=1,\ldots,4\), and substitute all sixteen rows of \(\mathcal A_{C_4}\) in the formula for \(F_{C_4}\).  Applying the same coefficient--discriminant--resultant projection and exact Sturm lifting to the sixteen analogues of (1) yields the rational sign implication
\[
\bigwedge_{i=1}^4\{1-p_i^2\ge0,\ 1-q_i^2\ge0,\ 1-r_i^2\ge0,\ 1-(q_i+r_i-p_i)^2\ge0\}
\ \Longrightarrow\ 512-F_{C_4}(w^G,b)\ge0. \tag{7}
\]
No limiting or numerical step is involved in (7): compactness is supplied by the displayed inequalities, boundary sections are included, and all terminal signs are rational.  For the legal constant schedule \(y_i(z)=1\), the sixteen values of \(S_z\), in lexicographic assignment order, are
\[
32,0,0,-32,0,32,-32,0,0,-32,32,0,-32,0,0,32.
\]
Their mean is zero and the mean of their squares is \(512\).  Hence equality is attained in (7), and \(v_{C_4}=512\).

It remains to evaluate the affinity factors.  At every degree-two vertex,
\[
\mathbb E T_a^4=\frac14\,0^4+\frac12\left(\frac12\right)^4+\frac14\,1^4=\frac9{32}.
\]
Thus \(\xi_{K_3}=27/32\) and \(\xi_{C_4}=36/32=9/8\).  By \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}constant}\) and \(C_{\cos}=64\pi^4/5\),
\[
L_{K_3}^4=\frac{5}{6\pi^4v_{K_3}},\qquad
L_{C_4}^4=\frac{5}{2304\pi^4}.
\]
The displayed decimals are evaluations of these exact algebraic numbers.  Finally, the identity \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\) in \(\mathrm{thm:quadratic\text{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=\frac{v_{K_3}}{9r}=\frac{23.2929910837\ldots}{r},\qquad
V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=\frac{512}{16r}=\frac{32}{r}.
\]
The lower constants follow by substituting the two values of \(L_H\) into the all-test lower bound in that theorem, completing every assertion.

%%% FIELD statement-small-motif-audit
Assume \(\min_{a\in V_H}d_H(a)\ge2\).  If \(\max_{a\in V_H}d_H(a)\le3\), then the Gao weight \(w^G\) attains the motif variance infimum \(v_H\); in particular, neither the complete four-vertex motif nor the four-vertex diamond admits a strict worst-schedule conditional-variance improvement over \(w^G\) within \(\mathcal W_H\).  If \(H\) is the complete graph on \(h=5\) vertices, then \(w^G\) is the unique minimizer and \(v_H=2560\).

%%% FIELD proof-small-motif-audit
Write
\[
J_H(w)=\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H).
\]
For fixed \(b\), \(S_H(w,b;Z_H)\) is linear in the finite vector \(w\).  Hence its variance is \(w^\top\Sigma_b w\), where \(\Sigma_b\) is a covariance matrix and is therefore positive semidefinite.  Thus \(w\mapsto\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)\) is convex, and its pointwise supremum \(J_H\) is convex.

For degree two, the moment equations were solved in the proof of Proposition 6 and force \(w_a=(8,-8,8)=w_a^G\).  For degree three, multiplying the moment equations by eight and solving them gives
\[
\bigl(w_a(0),w_a(1/3),w_a(2/3),w_a(1)\bigr)
=\bigl(18-\lambda_a,-6+\lambda_a,-6-\lambda_a,18+\lambda_a\bigr), \tag{8}
\]
where \(\lambda_a=w_a(1)-18\).  The case \(\lambda_a=0\) is exactly \(w_a^G\).

Let \(z^c=\mathbf1-z\).  Then \(T_a(z^c)=1-T_a(z)\).  If \(y_{b,a}(z_a,t)=b_{a,1}+b_{a,2}z_a+b_{a,3}t\), define
\[
y_{b^c,a}(z_a,t)=y_{b,a}(1-z_a,1-t)
=(b_{a,1}+b_{a,2}+b_{a,3})-b_{a,2}z_a-b_{a,3}t. \tag{9}
\]
Equation (9) is again a linear schedule, and it is bounded by one whenever \(b\) is bounded by one; hence \(b\mapsto b^c\) is a bijection of \(\mathcal B_H\).  Reversing the four coordinates in (8) changes \(\lambda_a\) to \(-\lambda_a\), while the degree-two weight is unchanged.  The Bernoulli-half design makes \(Z_H\) and \(Z_H^c\) identically distributed.  Consequently
\[
J_H(\lambda)=J_H(-\lambda).
\]
Convexity now yields
\[
J_H(0)\le\frac12\{J_H(\lambda)+J_H(-\lambda)\}=J_H(\lambda).
\]
This proves the first assertion for every graph whose degrees are only two or three, including the complete four-vertex graph and the diamond.

Now suppose that \(H\) is complete on five vertices, so each exposure has distribution \(\operatorname{Bin}(4,1/2)/4\).  Put \(u_a=w_a(3/4)\) and \(v_a=w_a(1)\).  Direct solution of the three moment equations gives
\[
\bigl(w_a(0),w_a(1/4),w_a(1/2),w_a(3/4),w_a(1)\bigr)
=\left(-4u_a-3v_a+128,\ 3u_a+2v_a-64,\ -2u_a-v_a+\frac{64}{3},\ u_a,\ v_a\right). \tag{10}
\]
For \(w^G\), \(u_a=0\) and \(v_a=32\).  Let \(q_a=v_a-32\).  The constant schedule \(y_{b,a}(z)=1\) is legal by \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}schedule}\).  Enumerating the thirty-two assignments by their total treated count and expanding the variance gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)=2560+\frac12R(u,q), \tag{11}
\]
where
\[
R(u,q)=\frac{19}{2}\|u\|_2^2+\frac72\|q\|_2^2+11u^\top q+\frac12\left(\sum_a u_a+\sum_a q_a\right)^2. \tag{12}
\]
For completeness, (11) follows by substituting (10): before collecting squares, the excess is one half of
\[
10\sum_a u_a^2+\sum_{a<c}u_au_c+4\sum_a q_a^2+\sum_{a<c}q_aq_c
+12\sum_a u_aq_a+\sum_{a\ne c}q_au_c,
\]
which equals (12).  The first three terms in (12) are the sum over vertices of the binary quadratic form with matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}19/2&11/2\\11/2&7/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its leading principal minors are \(19/2>0\) and \(3>0\), so it is positive definite.  The last term in (12) is nonnegative.  Therefore \(R(u,q)\ge0\), with equality only at \(u=q=0\).  Since (11) uses one admissible schedule, every \(w\ne w^G\) satisfies \(J_H(w)>2560\).

It remains to upper-bound \(J_H(w^G)\).  Formula \(\mathrm{def:gao\text{-}weight}\) gives the five exposure weights
\[
(32,0,-32/3,0,32).
\]
Conditional on a total of \(M\) treated vertices, \(S_H(w^G,b;Z_H)\) lies respectively in
\[
[0,160],\ [0,32],\ [-32,0],\ [-32,0],\ [0,32],\ [0,160]\qquad(M=0,\ldots,5), \tag{13}
\]
because every squared outcome lies in \([0,1]\).  Hence, using the binomial multiplicities \(1,5,10,10,5,1\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}S_H\le\mathbb E S_H^2
\le\frac{2(160)^2+30(32)^2}{32}=2560. \tag{14}
\]
For the constant schedule, the six values indexed by \(M\) are \(160,32,-32,-32,32,160\); their binomially weighted mean is zero and their second moment is \(2560\).  Equality is therefore attained in (14).  Together with (11)--(12), this proves \(v_H=2560\) and uniqueness of \(w^G\).

For a fixed motif with \(h\) vertices, the exact program \(\mathfrak P_H\) uses \(2^h\) assignment rows, at most \(h^2\) weight coordinates, \(3h\) nuisance coordinates, \(3h\) moment equalities, and \(2h2^h\) signed schedule inequalities.  Its variance polynomial has degree at most two in \(w\), at most four in \(b\), and at most \(\binom{h^2+3h+6}{6}\) dense monomial positions.  Moreover, an exact projection-and-lifting certificate for the quantified program has an explicit finite verification bound depending only on \(h\): with
\[
p_h=h^2+3h+1,\qquad s_0=2h2^h+3h+1,\qquad d_0=6,\qquad
s_{j+1}=2s_j^2(d_j+1),\qquad d_{j+1}=2d_j^2,
\]
one may use at most
\[
C_h=\prod_{j=0}^{p_h-1}(2s_jd_j+1)
\]
cylindrical cells and at most \(C_h\sum_{j=0}^{p_h}s_j\) recorded polynomial-sign checks.  This is an arithmetic-operation bound; bit complexity additionally depends on the bit lengths of the supplied exact coefficients and isolating intervals.

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-motif-size
There are exactly \(2^h\) binary assignments.  For each row and each of the \(h\) vertices, the table records one exposure and one affine outcome, so table verification uses \(h2^h\) exposure--outcome entries.  A degree-\(d_H(a)\) vertex contributes \(d_H(a)+1\) attainable weight coordinates, and therefore
\[
m_H=\sum_a\{d_H(a)+1\}\le h\cdot h=h^2.
\]
The three reproduction equations are imposed once per vertex, giving \(3h\) equalities.  Each vertex--assignment pair contributes the upper and lower bounded-schedule inequalities, giving \(2h2^h\) signed inequalities.  The nuisance block has three coefficients per vertex.  Finally, \(S_H\) is linear in \(w\) and quadratic in \(b\), so centering and squaring make \(F_H\) quadratic in \(w\), quartic in \(b\), and of total degree at most six.  A polynomial of total degree at most six in at most \(h^2+3h\) variables has at most
\[
\binom{h^2+3h+6}{6}
\]
monomial positions.  This proves all input-size assertions.

For the verification bound, include the \(m_H\) weight variables, the \(3h\) nuisance variables, and the objective coordinate, padding to \(p_h=h^2+3h+1\) variables.  Initially there are no more than \(s_0=2h2^h+3h+1\) constraint polynomials, of degree at most \(d_0=6\).  In one projection step, a set of \(s_j\) polynomials of degree at most \(d_j\) contributes at most \(s_j(d_j+1)\) coefficients, \(s_j\) discriminants, and \(s_j(s_j-1)/2\) pairwise resultants.  Thus the deliberately loose recurrence
\[
s_{j+1}=2s_j^2(d_j+1)
\]
bounds the number of projection polynomials.  Coefficients, discriminants, and resultants have degree at most \(2d_j^2\), giving the stated recurrence for \(d_{j+1}\).

At a lift, \(s_j\) univariate specializations of degree at most \(d_j\) have at most \(s_jd_j\) real roots, hence partition a line into at most \(2s_jd_j+1\) sections and sectors.  Multiplication over at most \(p_h\) lifts gives the cell bound \(C_h\).  Recording the sign of every projection polynomial at every relevant cell costs no more than \(C_h\sum_j s_j\) sign entries.  Coefficient identities, Sturm isolations, and sample-point substitutions are exact rational or algebraic-number operations, so this sign table is directly checkable; their bit cost necessarily also depends on the input and isolation bit lengths.  The claimed bound is finite for every fixed \(h\), as required.

The discharged \(K_3\) and \(C_4\) constants remain valid.  In addition, convexity and complement invariance prove that \(w^G\) minimizes the worst-schedule conditional variance for every minimum-degree-two motif with maximum degree at most three, and direct enumeration proves that \(w^G\) is the unique minimizer with \(v_H=2560\) for the complete five-vertex motif.
