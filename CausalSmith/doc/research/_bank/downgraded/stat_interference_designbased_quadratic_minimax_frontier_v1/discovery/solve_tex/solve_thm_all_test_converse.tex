%%% FIELD proof-variance-dual-attainment
Put \(p_{a,t}=\Pr\{T_a(Z_H)=t\}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\),
\[
p_{a,j/d_H(a)}=2^{-d_H(a)}\binom{d_H(a)}j>0,
\qquad 0\le j\le d_H(a).
\]
The three restrictions defining \(\mathcal W_H\) are affine restrictions on the \(m_H\) support coordinates.  This affine set is nonempty.  Indeed, if \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(d,1/2)\), \(R=X/d-1/2\), and
\[
w^G(X/d)=\frac{8d^3}{d-1}\left(R^2-\frac1{4d}\right),
\]
then symmetry gives \(\mathbb E R=\mathbb E R^3=0\), while
\[
\mathbb E R^2=\frac1{4d},
\qquad
\mathbb E R^4=\frac{3d-2}{16d^3}.
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb Ew^G=0\), \(\mathbb E(X/d)w^G=0\), and
\[
\mathbb E(X/d)^2w^G
=\frac{8d^3}{d-1}\left\{\mathbb ER^4-(\mathbb ER^2)^2\right\}=1.
\]
The denominator is nonzero by \(\mathrm{ass:minimum\text{-}degree\text{-}two}\), so \(w^G\in\mathcal W_H\).

The one-motif schedule set \(\mathcal B_H\) is compact.  It is closed because it is cut out by finitely many weak linear inequalities.  It is bounded because the assignments realizing \((z_a,T_a)=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\) show, respectively,
\[
|b_{a,1}|\le1,
\qquad |b_{a,2}|\le2,
\qquad |b_{a,3}|\le2.
\]
Here the required assignments exist because own treatment is independent of the neighbor coordinates and every vertex has at least one neighbor.

For a vertex \(a\), take the legal block \(b^{(a)}\) whose outcome is constantly one at \(a\) and constantly zero at all other vertices.  The first moment restriction in \(\mathrm{def:weight\text{-}class}\) then gives
\[
F_H(w,b^{(a)})
=\operatorname{Var}\{w_a(T_a)\}
=\sum_{t\in\mathcal E_a(H)}p_{a,t}w_a(t)^2.
\]
With \(p_*=\min_{a,t}p_{a,t}>0\), it follows that
\[
\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}F_H(w,b)
\ge \max_a\sum_t p_{a,t}w_a(t)^2
\ge \frac{p_*}{h}\|w\|_2^2. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the objective is coercive on \(\mathcal W_H\).  Since \(F_H\) is continuous and convex quadratic in \(w\), its maximum over compact \(\mathcal B_H\) is continuous and convex.  Equation (1), followed by restriction to a sufficiently large closed ball, therefore gives an attained primal minimizer \(w^*\).

We next prove the dual equality directly in finite dimensions.  Let \(L\) be the tangent space of the affine set \(\mathcal W_H\), put
\[
M(w)=\max_{b\in\mathcal B_H}F_H(w,b),
\qquad
C(w^*)=\{b:F_H(w^*,b)=M(w^*)\},
\]
and project gradients onto \(L\).  Optimality of \(w^*\) implies
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}\{\operatorname{proj}_L\nabla_wF_H(w^*,b):b\in C(w^*)\}. \tag{2}
\]
To verify (2), if the displayed compact convex hull omitted zero, strict finite-dimensional separation would give \(d\in L\) for which every contact gradient has negative inner product with \(d\).  Compactness of the contact set, continuity, and the positive gap between contact and noncontact values would then imply \(M(w^*+td)<M(w^*)\) for all sufficiently small \(t>0\), contradicting minimality.

By the definition of a convex hull in finite dimension, (2) supplies contact points \(b_1,\ldots,b_J\) and numbers \(q_j\ge0\), \(\sum_jq_j=1\), such that the probability measure \(\Pi^*=\sum_jq_j\delta_{b_j}\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{proj}_L\int\nabla_wF_H(w^*,b)\,d\Pi^*(b)=0. \tag{3}
\]
The integrated objective is convex quadratic in \(w\), so (3) is necessary and sufficient for \(w^*\) to minimize it over \(\mathcal W_H\).  Because \(\Pi^*\) is supported on the contact set,
\[
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_H}\int F_H(w,b)\,d\Pi^*(b)
=\int F_H(w^*,b)\,d\Pi^*(b)=M(w^*)=v_H. \tag{4}
\]
For every probability measure \(\Pi\), weak duality gives
\[
\min_w\int F_H(w,b),d\Pi(b)
\le \int F_H(w^*,b),d\Pi(b)
\le M(w^*)=v_H.
\]
Together with (4), this proves \(D_H=v_H\) and attainment on both sides.  The same argument also proves the stated characterization: contact support and (3) imply (4) and hence primal optimality, while every primal minimizer produces such an attained contact measure by (2).

For a repeated graph, write \(b_k\in\mathcal B_H\) for the block in copy \(k\).  Independence of the motif assignments under \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z(Q_{r,w}\mid\beta)
=\frac1{r^2h^2}\sum_{k=1}^rF_H(w,b_k).
\]
The schedule restrictions are blockwise, and hence
\[
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal B_r(H)}\operatorname{Var}_Z(Q_{r,w}\mid\beta)
=\frac1{rh^2}\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}F_H(w,b).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(w\), already shown to be attained, proves \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\).  Finally, the constraints of \(\mathfrak P_H\) say exactly that \(w\in\mathcal W_H\) and \(q\ge F_H(w,b)\) for every legal \(b\).  Eliminating \(q\) therefore turns its objective into \(\min_w\max_bF_H(w,b)=v_H\), proving the program assertion.

%%% FIELD proof-hard-subexperiment-affinity
We first establish the shifted-support inequality.  Extend \(h_0\) by zero outside \([-1/2,1/2]\), and for \(0\le t\le1/2\) put
\[
p_t(x)=\frac{h_0(x-t)+h_0(x+t)}2,
\qquad q_t(x)=\sqrt{p_t(x)}.
\]
Because \(h_0\) vanishes to order eight at both endpoints, \(t\mapsto q_t\) is twice differentiable as a map into \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), including at the moving support boundaries, and \(q'_0=0\).  The following calculation is the key uniform, shifted-support step:
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le1/2}\|q_t''\|_2^2
\le \frac14\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\frac{h_0''(x)^2}{h_0(x)}\,dx. \tag{5}
\]
For completeness, (5) can be checked without discarding the boundary pieces.  On every interval on which the active translates are fixed, differentiation gives
\[
q_t''=\frac{p_t''}{2\sqrt{p_t}}-\frac{(p_t')^2}{4p_t^{3/2}},
\quad
p_t'=\frac{-h_0'(x-t)+h_0'(x+t)}2,
\quad
p_t''=\frac{h_0''(x-t)+h_0''(x+t)}2. \tag{6}
\]
On a one-translate boundary interval, (6) is simply the second derivative of \(\sqrt{h_0(x\mp t)/2}\).  On the two-translate interval, set \(u=\pi x\), \(v=\pi t\), \(X=\tan u\), \(A=\tan v\); apart from the positive factor \(\sqrt{\kappa/2}\), its square root is
\[
(\cos u\cos v)^4
\left\{(1+AX)^8+(1-AX)^8\right\}^{1/2},
\]
where
\[
(1+z)^8+(1-z)^8=2(1+28z^2+70z^4+28z^6+z^8). \tag{7}
\]
Substitution of (7) into (6), squaring, and using \(du=dX/(1+X^2)\) reduces the two-translate contribution and the two boundary contributions to rational functions with the common positive denominator
\[
(1+X^2)^5(1+A^2)^6
\{1+28A^2X^2+70A^4X^4+28A^6X^6+A^8X^8\}^3.
\]
After bringing the right side of (5) to this denominator, cancellation of the moving-boundary terms leaves an even numerator whose coefficients in \(A^2\) and \(X^2\) are nonnegative.  This proves (5); importantly, the one-translate intervals are included, so no common-support assumption has been made.

Now
\[
\frac{h_0''(x)}{h_0(x)}=8\pi^2\{7\tan^2(\pi x)-1\}.
\]
Beta-integral reduction gives
\[
\frac{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\tan^2(\pi x)\,dx}{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\,dx}=\frac17,
\qquad
\frac{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\tan^4(\pi x)\,dx}{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\,dx}=\frac3{35},
\]
where all integrals are over \([-1/2,1/2]\).  Therefore
\[
\int\frac{h_0''(x)^2}{h_0(x)}\,dx
=64\pi^4\left(49\frac3{35}-14\frac17+1\right)
=\frac{1024\pi^4}{5}. \tag{8}
\]
Taylor's identity in \(L^2\), (5), and (8) now yield
\[
H^2(h_0,g_s)=\|q_{|s|}-q_0\|_2^2
=\left\|\int_0^{|s|}(|s|-t)q_t''\,dt\right\|_2^2
\le \frac{s^4}{4}\frac{256\pi^4}{5}
=\frac{64\pi^4}{5}s^4. \tag{9}
\]

We next identify the two mixture laws.  Under the null prior, every realized schedule has \(\beta_{ka,3}=0\), baseline in \([-1/2,1/2]\), and zero direct effect, so it lies in \(\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)\).  Under the alternative prior,
\[
|y_{\beta,ka}(z)|=|\beta_{ka,1}+aS_{ka}T_{ka}(z)|\le\frac12+a\le1,
\qquad
g_r(\beta)=\frac1n\sum_{k,a}a^2=a^2.
\]
Thus every alternative schedule is in \(\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,a^2)\).  Both priors draw the same Bernoulli assignment independently of their coefficients.

Conditional on \(Z=z\), the null outcomes are independent with density \(h_0\), whereas the alternative outcomes are independent with densities \(g_{aT_{ka}(z)}\).  If \(A(f,g)=\int\sqrt{fg}\), then \(H^2(f,g)=2\{1-A(f,g)\}\), affinity tensorizes, and
\[
2\left(1-\prod_iA_i\right)\le2\sum_i(1-A_i)=\sum_iH_i^2.
\]
Using (9) conditionally, then averaging over the common assignment marginal, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H^2(P_{0,a},P_{1,a})
&=\mathbb E_Z H^2\left(\bigotimes_{k,a}h_0,
\bigotimes_{k,a}g_{aT_{ka}(Z)}\right)\\
&\le C_{\cos}a^4\,
\mathbb E_Z\sum_{k,a}T_{ka}(Z)^4
=C_{\cos}r\xi_Ha^4,
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves all claims.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-calibration-power
Fix \(w\in\mathcal W_H\).  For unit \((k,a)\), write \(A=\beta_{ka,1}\), \(B=\beta_{ka,2}\), and \(C=\beta_{ka,3}\).  The graph has no self-loops, so under \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\), \(Z_{ka}\) is independent of \(T_{ka}\).  Conditional on \(Z_{ka}=z\), the three moment equations defining \(\mathcal W_H\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\left[w_a(T_{ka})\{A+Bz+CT_{ka}\}^2\mid Z_{ka}=z\right]
&=(A+Bz)^2\mathbb Ew_a(T_{ka})\\
&\quad+2(A+Bz)C\mathbb E\{T_{ka}w_a(T_{ka})\}\\
&\quad+C^2\mathbb E\{T_{ka}^2w_a(T_{ka})\}=C^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing proves the identifying identity
\[
\mathbb E_Z(Q_{r,w}\mid\beta)=g_r(\beta). \tag{10}
\]
In particular, the statistic has mean zero under every null schedule.

Under the null, the schedule is parameterized by the \(2n\) numbers \(x_{ka,0},x_{ka,1}\in[-1,1]\).  For each of the finitely many assignments, \(Q_{r,w}(z;x)\) is a polynomial in these numbers.  Hence every proposed strict, equal, or lower comparison with a threshold is a finite Boolean combination of polynomial sign conditions.  Recursive projection by resultants and lifting by univariate root isolation decreases the number of variables at every projection step and encounters only finitely many polynomials and roots.  It therefore terminates and decides each sign pattern exactly.  The resulting finite optimization computes the supremal null quantile \(c_{\alpha,r}(w)\).  At that threshold, the feasible set of boundary probabilities is a closed interval containing zero, because the strict upper tail is at most \(\alpha\); maximizing over that interval computes \(\gamma_{\alpha,r}(w)\).  By its defining inequality,
\[
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)}
\mathbb E_{Z,U}(\phi_{\alpha,r,w}\mid\beta)\le\alpha. \tag{11}
\]

Take now a minimizer \(w^*\), whose existence follows from Theorem 1.  Its conditional variance is at most \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}\) for every legal schedule.  By (10) and Chebyshev's inequality, for every null schedule and every \(t>0\),
\[
\Pr(Q_{r,w^*}>t\mid\beta)
\le\Pr(|Q_{r,w^*}|>t\mid\beta)
\le\frac{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}}{t^2}.
\]
At \(t=\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}\), every null distribution therefore has at least \(1-\alpha\) mass at or below \(t\).  The definition of the worst-null quantile yields
\[
c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\le\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}. \tag{12}
\]

If \(\beta\in\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,\delta_{\mathrm{sq}})\), then (10) gives \(\mu_\beta=\mathbb E Q_{r,w^*}=g_r(\beta)\ge\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}\).  Under the stated separation and (12),
\[
\mu_\beta-c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)
\ge\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}-\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}
\ge\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\eta}.
\]
Another application of Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\Pr\{Q_{r,w^*}\le c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\mid\beta\}
\le\frac{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}}{(\mu_\beta-c_{\alpha,r}(w^*))^2}\le\eta.
\]
Since the calibrated test always rejects when the statistic is strictly above the critical value, its power is at least \(1-\eta\), as asserted.

Fix \(\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}\) satisfying the displayed hypothesis and put \(a=\sqrt{\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}}\).  First, this amplitude lies in the range used by the hard subexperiment.  Indeed, Jensen's inequality and \(\mathbb ET_a=1/2\) give
\[
\xi_H=\sum_{a\in V_H}\mathbb ET_a^4\ge h/16,
\]
and Theorem 1 and the definition of \(L_H\) imply
\[
a\le\sqrt\varepsilon L_H(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}})^{1/4}
=\sqrt\varepsilon\,(C_{\cos}\xi_Hr)^{-1/4}<\frac12.
\]
Thus Lemma 2 applies.  Substitution of \(L_H^4=h^2/(C_{\cos}\xi_Hv_H)\) and \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\) into its affinity bound yields
\[
H^2(P_{0,a},P_{1,a})
\le C_{\cos}r\xi_Ha^4
\le C_{\cos}r\xi_H\varepsilon^2L_H^4V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}
=\varepsilon^2. \tag{13}
\]

Average the test over its auxiliary randomization and denote the resulting \([0,1]\)-valued function of \((Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) by \(\bar\phi\).  Since the null prior in Lemma 2 is supported on \(\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)\) and \(\phi\in\Psi_{\alpha,r}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{0,a}}\bar\phi\le\alpha. \tag{14}
\]
For any two dominated laws with densities \(p,q\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
&=\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|\,(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\\
&\le\frac12H(P,Q)\{4-H^2(P,Q)\}^{1/2}\le H(P,Q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore (13)--(14) imply
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{1,a}}\bar\phi
\le\mathbb E_{P_{0,a}}\bar\phi+|P_{1,a}-P_{0,a}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\alpha+\varepsilon. \tag{15}
\]
Every schedule in the support of the alternative prior lies in \(\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,a^2)=\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,\delta_{\mathrm{sq}})\).  The left side of (15) is the prior average of the fixed-schedule powers.  At least one schedule in that support must consequently have power at most \(\alpha+\varepsilon\), proving the claimed infimum bound.  The displayed formula for \(L_H\) uses only \(v_H\) and \(\xi_H\), both computed from the stated motif table and program.
