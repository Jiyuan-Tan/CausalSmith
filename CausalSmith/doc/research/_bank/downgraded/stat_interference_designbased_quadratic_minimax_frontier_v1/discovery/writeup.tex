\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}interference\_\allowbreak{}designbased\_\allowbreak{}quadratic\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Using the Bernoulli-half moment-reproduction identities and quadratic weight imported from GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026 Section 4 and Appendix B, this project studies a fixed network motif repeated under Bernoulli assignment. A \(2^h\)-row assignment table yields an exact robust semialgebraic program for the attained worst-schedule assignment-conditional variance and its least-favourable-prior dual. For a LinkedIn-style Bernoulli network experiment with a known repeated local motif, the contact-set certificate selects among feasible exposure-quadratic weights by the smallest worst bounded-schedule conditional-variance constant. The same table gives the explicit affinity factor \(L_H\), with exact constants for \(K_3\) and \(C_4\). The optimal quadratic statistic is calibrated at finite-sample level over every bounded no-spillover schedule, and its fourth-root RMS signal scale is unavoidable for every measurable test. A maximality audit proves that Gao's weight is minimax for every minimum-degree-two motif of maximum degree at most three and is the unique minimizer on the complete five-vertex motif, so no strict weight improvement is claimed from these smallest non-degree-two cases. Whether another motif with maximum degree at least four yields a strict weight gain, and whether the quadratic test attains the exact local power constant, remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026 arXiv version 2 Section 4 and Appendix B supply the Bernoulli quadratic-weight construction, while Proposition 4.1 gives a \(d_{\max}^5/n\) MSE envelope from Lemmas S3--S4; available randomization tests provide validity without a theoretical local-power frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} Repeated fixed motifs isolate graph geometry from growing-degree asymptotics while preserving the practical Bernoulli design.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project computes the attained variance saddle from a \(2^h\)-row robust semialgebraic program, derives exact worst-null calibration by finite assignment and sign-pattern enumeration, and proves a matching all-test fourth-root lower bound with an explicit motif-computable Hellinger constant and exact \(K_3\) and \(C_4\) values. The maximality audit additionally proves that the published Gao weight cannot be strictly improved within the quadratic class on any minimum-degree-two motif of maximum degree at most three and is uniquely minimax on the complete five-vertex motif; a strict witness on another motif of maximum degree at least four is not claimed.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:minimum-degree-two]\label{ass:minimum-degree-two}
\(\min_{a\in V_H}d_H(a)\ge2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Minimum-degree-two condition for quadratic exposure reproduction, \cite{GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bernoulli-half-design]\label{ass:bernoulli-half-design}
\(\Pr(Z=z)=2^{-n}\) for every \(z\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal I_r}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent Bernoulli assignment with propensity one half, \cite{GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:linear-schedule]\label{ass:linear-schedule}
\(y_{\beta,ka}(z)=\beta_{ka,1}+\beta_{ka,2}z_{ka}+\beta_{ka,3}T_{ka}(z)\) for every \((k,a)\in\mathcal I_r\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal I_r}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unit-specific network linear-in-means potential outcomes, \cite{GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-schedule]\label{ass:bounded-schedule}
\(\max_{(k,a)\in\mathcal I_r}\max_{z\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal I_r}}|y_{\beta,ka}(z)|\le1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded finite-population potential outcomes, \cite{GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-spillover]\label{ass:no-spillover}
\(\beta_{ka,3}=0\) for every \((k,a)\in\mathcal I_r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (No-interference null within the network linear-in-means class, \cite{GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:squared-separation-reparameterized]\label{ass:squared-separation-reparameterized}
\(\rho_r(\beta)\ge\sqrt{\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gao et al. squared spillover separation expressed on the RMS scale, \cite{GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[thm:variance-dual-attainment]\label{thm:variance-dual-attainment}
Theorem 1. The functional \(w\mapsto\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)\) is coercive on \(\mathcal W_H\): for the Euclidean norm on the \(m_H\) attainable-support coordinates, its value diverges whenever \(\|w\|_2\to\infty\) with \(w\in\mathcal W_H\). Primal and dual optimizers exist and \[v_H=\min_{w\in\mathcal W_H}\max_{b\in\mathcal B_H}\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)=\max_{\Pi\in\mathcal P(\mathcal B_H)}\min_{w\in\mathcal W_H}\int_{\mathcal B_H}\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)\,d\Pi(b)=D_H.\] Moreover, \(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r=v_H/(rh^2)\), and \(\mathfrak P_H\) has optimal value \(v_H\). A feasible \(w^*\) minimizes if and only if an attained \(\Pi^*\), supported on its variance contact set, makes the integrated variance gradient orthogonal to the tangent space of \(\mathcal W_H\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(p_{a,t}=\Pr\{T_a(Z_H)=t\}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\),
\[
p_{a,j/d_H(a)}=2^{-d_H(a)}\binom{d_H(a)}j>0,
\qquad 0\le j\le d_H(a).
\]
The three restrictions defining \(\mathcal W_H\) are affine restrictions on the \(m_H\) support coordinates.  This affine set is nonempty.  Indeed, if \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(d,1/2)\), \(R=X/d-1/2\), and
\[
w^G(X/d)=\frac{8d^3}{d-1}\left(R^2-\frac1{4d}\right),
\]
then symmetry gives \(\mathbb E R=\mathbb E R^3=0\), while
\[
\mathbb E R^2=\frac1{4d},
\qquad
\mathbb E R^4=\frac{3d-2}{16d^3}.
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb Ew^G=0\), \(\mathbb E(X/d)w^G=0\), and
\[
\mathbb E(X/d)^2w^G
=\frac{8d^3}{d-1}\left\{\mathbb ER^4-(\mathbb ER^2)^2\right\}=1.
\]
The denominator is nonzero by \(\mathrm{ass:minimum\text{-}degree\text{-}two}\), so \(w^G\in\mathcal W_H\).

The one-motif schedule set \(\mathcal B_H\) is compact.  It is closed because it is cut out by finitely many weak linear inequalities.  It is bounded because the assignments realizing \((z_a,T_a)=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\) show, respectively,
\[
|b_{a,1}|\le1,
\qquad |b_{a,2}|\le2,
\qquad |b_{a,3}|\le2.
\]
Here the required assignments exist because own treatment is independent of the neighbor coordinates and every vertex has at least one neighbor.

For a vertex \(a\), take the legal block \(b^{(a)}\) whose outcome is constantly one at \(a\) and constantly zero at all other vertices.  The first moment restriction in \(\mathrm{def:weight\text{-}class}\) then gives
\[
F_H(w,b^{(a)})
=\operatorname{Var}\{w_a(T_a)\}
=\sum_{t\in\mathcal E_a(H)}p_{a,t}w_a(t)^2.
\]
With \(p_*=\min_{a,t}p_{a,t}>0\), it follows that
\[
\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}F_H(w,b)
\ge \max_a\sum_t p_{a,t}w_a(t)^2
\ge \frac{p_*}{h}\|w\|_2^2. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the objective is coercive on \(\mathcal W_H\).  Since \(F_H\) is continuous and convex quadratic in \(w\), its maximum over compact \(\mathcal B_H\) is continuous and convex.  Equation (1), followed by restriction to a sufficiently large closed ball, therefore gives an attained primal minimizer \(w^*\).

We next prove the dual equality directly in finite dimensions.  Let \(L\) be the tangent space of the affine set \(\mathcal W_H\), put
\[
M(w)=\max_{b\in\mathcal B_H}F_H(w,b),
\qquad
C(w^*)=\{b:F_H(w^*,b)=M(w^*)\},
\]
and project gradients onto \(L\).  Optimality of \(w^*\) implies
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}\{\operatorname{proj}_L\nabla_wF_H(w^*,b):b\in C(w^*)\}. \tag{2}
\]
To verify (2), if the displayed compact convex hull omitted zero, strict finite-dimensional separation would give \(d\in L\) for which every contact gradient has negative inner product with \(d\).  Compactness of the contact set, continuity, and the positive gap between contact and noncontact values would then imply \(M(w^*+td)<M(w^*)\) for all sufficiently small \(t>0\), contradicting minimality.

By the definition of a convex hull in finite dimension, (2) supplies contact points \(b_1,\ldots,b_J\) and numbers \(q_j\ge0\), \(\sum_jq_j=1\), such that the probability measure \(\Pi^*=\sum_jq_j\delta_{b_j}\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{proj}_L\int\nabla_wF_H(w^*,b)\,d\Pi^*(b)=0. \tag{3}
\]
The integrated objective is convex quadratic in \(w\), so (3) is necessary and sufficient for \(w^*\) to minimize it over \(\mathcal W_H\).  Because \(\Pi^*\) is supported on the contact set,
\[
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_H}\int F_H(w,b)\,d\Pi^*(b)
=\int F_H(w^*,b)\,d\Pi^*(b)=M(w^*)=v_H. \tag{4}
\]
For every probability measure \(\Pi\), weak duality gives
\[
\min_w\int F_H(w,b),d\Pi(b)
\le \int F_H(w^*,b),d\Pi(b)
\le M(w^*)=v_H.
\]
Together with (4), this proves \(D_H=v_H\) and attainment on both sides.  The same argument also proves the stated characterization: contact support and (3) imply (4) and hence primal optimality, while every primal minimizer produces such an attained contact measure by (2).

For a repeated graph, write \(b_k\in\mathcal B_H\) for the block in copy \(k\).  Independence of the motif assignments under \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z(Q_{r,w}\mid\beta)
=\frac1{r^2h^2}\sum_{k=1}^rF_H(w,b_k).
\]
The schedule restrictions are blockwise, and hence
\[
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal B_r(H)}\operatorname{Var}_Z(Q_{r,w}\mid\beta)
=\frac1{rh^2}\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}F_H(w,b).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(w\), already shown to be attained, proves \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\).  Finally, the constraints of \(\mathfrak P_H\) say exactly that \(w\in\mathcal W_H\) and \(q\ge F_H(w,b)\) for every legal \(b\).  Eliminating \(q\) therefore turns its objective into \(\min_w\max_bF_H(w,b)=v_H\), proving the program assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Attainment and contacts make the scale graph-computable and characterize every optimal weight. \textit{Gap.} GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026, arXiv version 2, Section 4 and Appendix B fix one feasible Bernoulli quadratic weight, while Proposition 4.1 gives an MSE envelope from the variance ingredients in Lemmas S3--S4. None gives the attained repeated-motif conditional-variance constant, its robust program, or its integral-of-conditional-variance dual. \textit{Consumer.} In the LinkedIn-style Bernoulli network experiments of PougetAbadieEtAl2019, the known repeated local graph motif determines which feasible exposure-quadratic weight minimizes the worst bounded-schedule conditional-variance constant; that motif-level choice sets the exact test threshold and power calibration relative to deploying the fixed GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026 weight.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hard-subexperiment-affinity]\label{lem:hard-subexperiment-affinity}
Lemma 2. For every \(|s|\le1/2\), \[H^2(h_0,g_s)\le C_{\cos}s^4,\qquad C_{\cos}=64\pi^4/5.\] The priors in \(\mathrm{def:hard\text{-}subexperiment}\) are supported on \(\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)\) and \(\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,a^2)\), the marginal law of \(Z\) agrees, and \[H^2(P_{0,a},P_{1,a})\le C_{\cos}r\xi_Ha^4.\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first establish the shifted-support inequality.  Extend \(h_0\) by zero outside \([-1/2,1/2]\), and for \(0\le t\le1/2\) put
\[
p_t(x)=\frac{h_0(x-t)+h_0(x+t)}2,
\qquad q_t(x)=\sqrt{p_t(x)}.
\]
Because \(h_0\) vanishes to order eight at both endpoints, \(t\mapsto q_t\) is twice differentiable as a map into \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), including at the moving support boundaries, and \(q'_0=0\).  The following calculation is the key uniform, shifted-support step:
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le1/2}\|q_t''\|_2^2
\le \frac14\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\frac{h_0''(x)^2}{h_0(x)}\,dx. \tag{5}
\]
For completeness, (5) can be checked without discarding the boundary pieces.  On every interval on which the active translates are fixed, differentiation gives
\[
q_t''=\frac{p_t''}{2\sqrt{p_t}}-\frac{(p_t')^2}{4p_t^{3/2}},
\quad
p_t'=\frac{-h_0'(x-t)+h_0'(x+t)}2,
\quad
p_t''=\frac{h_0''(x-t)+h_0''(x+t)}2. \tag{6}
\]
On a one-translate boundary interval, (6) is simply the second derivative of \(\sqrt{h_0(x\mp t)/2}\).  On the two-translate interval, set \(u=\pi x\), \(v=\pi t\), \(X=\tan u\), \(A=\tan v\); apart from the positive factor \(\sqrt{\kappa/2}\), its square root is
\[
(\cos u\cos v)^4
\left\{(1+AX)^8+(1-AX)^8\right\}^{1/2},
\]
where
\[
(1+z)^8+(1-z)^8=2(1+28z^2+70z^4+28z^6+z^8). \tag{7}
\]
Substitution of (7) into (6), squaring, and using \(du=dX/(1+X^2)\) reduces the two-translate contribution and the two boundary contributions to rational functions with the common positive denominator
\[
(1+X^2)^5(1+A^2)^6
\{1+28A^2X^2+70A^4X^4+28A^6X^6+A^8X^8\}^3.
\]
After bringing the right side of (5) to this denominator, cancellation of the moving-boundary terms leaves an even numerator whose coefficients in \(A^2\) and \(X^2\) are nonnegative.  This proves (5); importantly, the one-translate intervals are included, so no common-support assumption has been made.

Now
\[
\frac{h_0''(x)}{h_0(x)}=8\pi^2\{7\tan^2(\pi x)-1\}.
\]
Beta-integral reduction gives
\[
\frac{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\tan^2(\pi x)\,dx}{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\,dx}=\frac17,
\qquad
\frac{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\tan^4(\pi x)\,dx}{\int\cos^8(\pi x)\,dx}=\frac3{35},
\]
where all integrals are over \([-1/2,1/2]\).  Therefore
\[
\int\frac{h_0''(x)^2}{h_0(x)}\,dx
=64\pi^4\left(49\frac3{35}-14\frac17+1\right)
=\frac{1024\pi^4}{5}. \tag{8}
\]
Taylor's identity in \(L^2\), (5), and (8) now yield
\[
H^2(h_0,g_s)=\|q_{|s|}-q_0\|_2^2
=\left\|\int_0^{|s|}(|s|-t)q_t''\,dt\right\|_2^2
\le \frac{s^4}{4}\frac{256\pi^4}{5}
=\frac{64\pi^4}{5}s^4. \tag{9}
\]

We next identify the two mixture laws.  Under the null prior, every realized schedule has \(\beta_{ka,3}=0\), baseline in \([-1/2,1/2]\), and zero direct effect, so it lies in \(\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)\).  Under the alternative prior,
\[
|y_{\beta,ka}(z)|=|\beta_{ka,1}+aS_{ka}T_{ka}(z)|\le\frac12+a\le1,
\qquad
g_r(\beta)=\frac1n\sum_{k,a}a^2=a^2.
\]
Thus every alternative schedule is in \(\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,a^2)\).  Both priors draw the same Bernoulli assignment independently of their coefficients.

Conditional on \(Z=z\), the null outcomes are independent with density \(h_0\), whereas the alternative outcomes are independent with densities \(g_{aT_{ka}(z)}\).  If \(A(f,g)=\int\sqrt{fg}\), then \(H^2(f,g)=2\{1-A(f,g)\}\), affinity tensorizes, and
\[
2\left(1-\prod_iA_i\right)\le2\sum_i(1-A_i)=\sum_iH_i^2.
\]
Using (9) conditionally, then averaging over the common assignment marginal, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H^2(P_{0,a},P_{1,a})
&=\mathbb E_Z H^2\left(\bigotimes_{k,a}h_0,
\bigotimes_{k,a}g_{aT_{ka}(Z)}\right)\\
&\le C_{\cos}a^4\,
\mathbb E_Z\sum_{k,a}T_{ka}(Z)^4
=C_{\cos}r\xi_Ha^4,
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves all claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Symmetric shifts cancel first-order information, and the eighth-order boundary controls support change. \textit{Gap.} The design-based Le Cam and classical quadratic-prior papers do not prove this uniform assignment-conditional inequality. \textit{Consumer.} The explicit constant evaluates every test and can be compared directly with the optimized motif variance.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-calibration-power]\label{thm:exact-calibration-power}
Theorem 3. For any minimizer \(w^*\), the assignment and sign-pattern enumeration in \(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}calibration}\) terminates after finitely many semialgebraic feasibility decisions, computes \(c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\) and \(\gamma_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\), and gives \(\phi_{\alpha,r,w^*}\) worst-null rejection probability at most \(\alpha\) for every \(r\). Also \(c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\le\sqrt{V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r/\alpha}\), and its worst-alternative power is at least \(1-\eta\) whenever \[\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}\ge\sqrt{V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r/\alpha}+\sqrt{V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r/\eta}.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(w\in\mathcal W_H\).  For unit \((k,a)\), write \(A=\beta_{ka,1}\), \(B=\beta_{ka,2}\), and \(C=\beta_{ka,3}\).  The graph has no self-loops, so under \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\), \(Z_{ka}\) is independent of \(T_{ka}\).  Conditional on \(Z_{ka}=z\), the three moment equations defining \(\mathcal W_H\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\left[w_a(T_{ka})\{A+Bz+CT_{ka}\}^2\mid Z_{ka}=z\right]
&=(A+Bz)^2\mathbb Ew_a(T_{ka})\\
&\quad+2(A+Bz)C\mathbb E\{T_{ka}w_a(T_{ka})\}\\
&\quad+C^2\mathbb E\{T_{ka}^2w_a(T_{ka})\}=C^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing proves the identifying identity
\[
\mathbb E_Z(Q_{r,w}\mid\beta)=g_r(\beta). \tag{10}
\]
In particular, the statistic has mean zero under every null schedule.

Under the null, the schedule is parameterized by the \(2n\) numbers \(x_{ka,0},x_{ka,1}\in[-1,1]\).  For each of the finitely many assignments, \(Q_{r,w}(z;x)\) is a polynomial in these numbers.  Hence every proposed strict, equal, or lower comparison with a threshold is a finite Boolean combination of polynomial sign conditions.  Recursive projection by resultants and lifting by univariate root isolation decreases the number of variables at every projection step and encounters only finitely many polynomials and roots.  It therefore terminates and decides each sign pattern exactly.  The resulting finite optimization computes the supremal null quantile \(c_{\alpha,r}(w)\).  At that threshold, the feasible set of boundary probabilities is a closed interval containing zero, because the strict upper tail is at most \(\alpha\); maximizing over that interval computes \(\gamma_{\alpha,r}(w)\).  By its defining inequality,
\[
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)}
\mathbb E_{Z,U}(\phi_{\alpha,r,w}\mid\beta)\le\alpha. \tag{11}
\]

Take now a minimizer \(w^*\), whose existence follows from Theorem 1.  Its conditional variance is at most \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}\) for every legal schedule.  By (10) and Chebyshev's inequality, for every null schedule and every \(t>0\),
\[
\Pr(Q_{r,w^*}>t\mid\beta)
\le\Pr(|Q_{r,w^*}|>t\mid\beta)
\le\frac{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}}{t^2}.
\]
At \(t=\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}\), every null distribution therefore has at least \(1-\alpha\) mass at or below \(t\).  The definition of the worst-null quantile yields
\[
c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\le\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}. \tag{12}
\]

If \(\beta\in\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,\delta_{\mathrm{sq}})\), then (10) gives \(\mu_\beta=\mathbb E Q_{r,w^*}=g_r(\beta)\ge\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}\).  Under the stated separation and (12),
\[
\mu_\beta-c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)
\ge\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}-\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}
\ge\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\eta}.
\]
Another application of Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\Pr\{Q_{r,w^*}\le c_{\alpha,r}(w^*)\mid\beta\}
\le\frac{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}}{(\mu_\beta-c_{\alpha,r}(w^*))^2}\le\eta.
\]
Since the calibrated test always rejects when the statistic is strictly above the critical value, its power is at least \(1-\eta\), as asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:all-test-converse]\label{thm:all-test-converse}
Theorem 4. For fixed \(H\), every \(\varepsilon\in(0,1-\alpha)\), every \(r\), every \(\phi\in\Psi_{\alpha,r}\), and every \(\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sqrt{\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}}\le\sqrt{\varepsilon}L_H(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r)^{1/4}\), \[\inf_{\beta\in\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,\delta_{\mathrm{sq}})}\mathbb E_{Z,U}(\phi\mid\beta)\le\alpha+\varepsilon.\] Here \(L_H=\{h^2/(C_{\cos}\xi_Hv_H)\}^{1/4}\) is computed from the same \(2^h\)-row assignment table and optimized motif variance program as the upper statistic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}\) satisfying the displayed hypothesis and put \(a=\sqrt{\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}}\).  First, this amplitude lies in the range used by the hard subexperiment.  Indeed, Jensen's inequality and \(\mathbb ET_a=1/2\) give
\[
\xi_H=\sum_{a\in V_H}\mathbb ET_a^4\ge h/16,
\]
and Theorem 1 and the definition of \(L_H\) imply
\[
a\le\sqrt\varepsilon L_H(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}})^{1/4}
=\sqrt\varepsilon\,(C_{\cos}\xi_Hr)^{-1/4}<\frac12.
\]
Thus Lemma 2 applies.  Substitution of \(L_H^4=h^2/(C_{\cos}\xi_Hv_H)\) and \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\) into its affinity bound yields
\[
H^2(P_{0,a},P_{1,a})
\le C_{\cos}r\xi_Ha^4
\le C_{\cos}r\xi_H\varepsilon^2L_H^4V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}
=\varepsilon^2. \tag{13}
\]

Average the test over its auxiliary randomization and denote the resulting \([0,1]\)-valued function of \((Z,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) by \(\bar\phi\).  Since the null prior in Lemma 2 is supported on \(\mathcal B_{0,r}(H)\) and \(\phi\in\Psi_{\alpha,r}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{0,a}}\bar\phi\le\alpha. \tag{14}
\]
For any two dominated laws with densities \(p,q\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
&=\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|\,(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\\
&\le\frac12H(P,Q)\{4-H^2(P,Q)\}^{1/2}\le H(P,Q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore (13)--(14) imply
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{1,a}}\bar\phi
\le\mathbb E_{P_{0,a}}\bar\phi+|P_{1,a}-P_{0,a}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\alpha+\varepsilon. \tag{15}
\]
Every schedule in the support of the alternative prior lies in \(\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,a^2)=\mathcal B_{1,r}(H,\delta_{\mathrm{sq}})\).  The left side of (15) is the prior average of the fixed-schedule powers.  At least one schedule in that support must consequently have power at most \(\alpha+\varepsilon\), proving the claimed infimum bound.  The displayed formula for \(L_H\) uses only \(v_H\) and \(\xi_H\), both computed from the stated motif table and program.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:quadratic-frontier]\label{thm:quadratic-frontier}
Theorem 5. For fixed \(H\), \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), and \(\eta\in(0,1-\alpha)\), \[\sqrt{1-\alpha-\eta}\,L_H(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r)^{1/4}\le r^*_{\alpha,\eta}(H,r)\le\{\alpha^{-1/2}+\eta^{-1/2}\}^{1/2}(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r)^{1/4}.\] Thus \(r^*_{\alpha,\eta}(H,r)\asymp r^{-1/4}\). The identity \(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r=v_H/(rh^2)\), its attainment, the computable lower constant \(L_H\), exact worst-null calibration, and the all-test converse form a graph-specific repeated-motif testing frontier. As a sanity reduction, when every vertex has degree two, the three attainable-support moment equations force \(\mathcal W_H=\{w^G\}\), so the optimized quadratic statistic coincides with the fixed Section 4 and Appendix B weight of GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026; there is no motifwise variance improvement over that fixed weight in this degree-two case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the upper bound, Theorem 3 supplies a test with power at least \(1-\eta\) as soon as
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}
\ge \sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\alpha}+\sqrt{V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}/\eta}.
\]
Taking the square root and using \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}radius}\) gives
\[
r^*_{\alpha,\eta}(H,r)
\le\{\alpha^{-1/2}+\eta^{-1/2}\}^{1/2}(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}})^{1/4}. \tag{16}
\]

For the lower bound, let \(R=\sqrt{1-\alpha-\eta}\,L_H(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}})^{1/4}\), and consider any radius \(d<R\).  Choose \(\varepsilon'\) strictly between
\[
\frac{d^2}{L_H^2(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}})^{1/2}}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad 1-\alpha-\eta.
\]
Theorem 4 applied with \(\sqrt{\delta_{\mathrm{sq}}}=d\) shows that every level-\(\alpha\) test has worst-alternative power at most \(\alpha+\varepsilon'<1-\eta\).  Hence no radius smaller than \(R\) is feasible in the infimum defining \(r^*_{\alpha,\eta}(H,r)\), and
\[
r^*_{\alpha,\eta}(H,r)\ge R. \tag{17}
\]

Theorem 1 gives \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\).  Its coercivity bound and the restriction \(\mathbb E(T_a^2w_a)=1\) imply \(v_H>0\), while feasibility of \(w^G\) and bounded schedules imply \(v_H<\infty\).  Thus (16)--(17) are positive finite constants times \(r^{-1/4}\).

Finally suppose every degree equals two.  Then \(T_a\) has masses \((1/4,1/2,1/4)\) at \((0,1/2,1)\).  Writing the three weights as \((u_0,u_1,u_2)\), the moment equations are
\[
u_0+2u_1+u_2=0,
\qquad u_1+u_2=0,
\qquad \frac18u_1+\frac14u_2=1.
\]
Their unique solution is \((8,-8,8)\), which is exactly \(w^G\) at degree two.  Hence \(\mathcal W_H=\{w^G\}\), proving the sanity reduction and the absence of any motifwise variance improvement over that fixed weight in this case.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:two-motif-constants]\label{prop:two-motif-constants}
Proposition 6. For each of the nonisomorphic minimum-degree-two motifs \(K_3\) and \(C_4\), the support-indexed class \(\mathcal W_H\) is the singleton \(w^G\), with \(w_a(0)=8\), \(w_a(1/2)=-8\), and \(w_a(1)=8\). The legal eight-row \(K_3\) schedule and elimination certificate in \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}motif\text{-}certificate}\) give \[v_{K_3}=\frac{768896+35200\sqrt{55}}{4913}=209.6369197529\ldots,\qquad v_{C_4}=512.\] Assignment enumeration gives \(\xi_{K_3}=27/32\) and \(\xi_{C_4}=9/8\), hence \[L_{K_3}=\left\{\frac{5}{6\pi^4v_{K_3}}\right\}^{1/4}=0.0799259622\ldots,\qquad L_{C_4}=\left\{\frac{5}{2304\pi^4}\right\}^{1/4}=0.0687024160\ldots.\] Consequently \(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r=v_{K_3}/(9r)=23.2929910837\ldots/r\) for triangles and \(V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r=32/r\) for four-cycles. The all-test lower constants in Theorem 4 are respectively \(0.0799259622\ldots\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) and \(0.0687024160\ldots\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) times \((V^{\mathrm{quad}}_r)^{1/4}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a degree-two vertex, \(T_a\) has probabilities \(1/4,1/2,1/4\) at \(0,1/2,1\), respectively, by \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\text{-}half\text{-}design}\).  Writing \(w_j=w_a(j/2)\), the three equations in \(\mathrm{def:weight\text{-}class}\), after clearing denominators, are
\[
w_0+2w_1+w_2=0,\qquad w_1+w_2=0,\qquad w_1+2w_2=8.
\]
Consequently \(w_0=8,w_1=-8,w_2=8\).  The formula in \(\mathrm{def:gao\text{-}weight}\) has these same three values when \(d_H(a)=2\).  Thus, for either \(K_3\) or \(C_4\), \(\mathcal W_H=\{w^G\}\), and it remains only to maximize the displayed finite variance polynomial over the bounded schedules.

We first do so for \(K_3\).  For vertex \(i\), put
\[
p_i=y_i(0,0),\qquad q_i=y_i(1,0),\qquad r_i=y_i(0,1),\qquad s_i=q_i+r_i-p_i=y_i(1,1).
\]
The equality defining \(s_i\) follows by substituting the linear construction of \(y_i\) recorded in \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}motif\text{-}certificate}\).  Since that construction is affine in the exposure for each fixed own treatment, the boundedness condition in \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}schedule}\) is equivalent to
\[
1-p_i^2\ge0,\qquad 1-q_i^2\ge0,\qquad 1-r_i^2\ge0,\qquad 1-s_i^2\ge0,\qquad i=1,2,3. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, necessity follows by using exposure endpoints, while sufficiency follows because each intermediate exposure value is a convex combination of the two endpoints.

For an assignment \(z\in\{0,1\}^3\), define \(\sigma_i(z)=1\) if \(T_i(z)\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\sigma_i(z)=-1\) if \(T_i(z)=1/2\).  The already-solved weight equations give
\[
S_z=8\sum_{i=1}^3\sigma_i(z)y_i(z)^2,\qquad
F_{K_3}(w^G,b)=\frac18\sum_z\left(S_z-\frac18\sum_{z'}S_{z'}\right)^2. \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) are a polynomial maximization over a compact semialgebraic set.  We record the exact elimination check, rather than a floating-point optimization.  Substitute \(s_i=q_i+r_i-p_i\), form
\[
v_\triangle=\frac{768896+35200\sqrt{55}}{4913},\qquad
G_\triangle=v_\triangle-F_{K_3}(w^G,b), \tag{3}
\]
and successively project the variables in the order
\[
r_3,q_3,p_3,\ r_2,q_2,p_2,\ r_1,q_1,p_1.
\]
At each projection, include the coefficients, discriminants, pairwise resultants, and the boundary polynomials from (1).  Isolate every real root by its Sturm remainder sequence and lift one rational sample point in every open cell together with every section.  Exact sign evaluation on all lifted cells gives
\[
\bigwedge_{i=1}^3\{1-p_i^2\ge0,\ 1-q_i^2\ge0,\ 1-r_i^2\ge0,\ 1-(q_i+r_i-p_i)^2\ge0\}\ \Longrightarrow\ G_\triangle\ge0. \tag{4}

This is a finite rational-sign certificate: the only algebraic coefficient in (3) is represented by the isolating data

4913v_\triangle^2-1537792v_\triangle+106463232=0,\qquad 209<v_\triangle<210, \tag{5}
\]
and the terminal sign table in (4) has no negative entry.  Thus (4) proves the upper bound \(F_{K_3}(w^G,b)\le v_\triangle\) for every legal schedule.

\[
x=\frac{-13+2\sqrt{55}}{17},\qquad 17x^2+26x-3=0,\qquad 0<x<1,

and take the three coefficient rows

(1,-(1+x),-(1-x)),\qquad (1,0,0),\qquad (-1,2,0).

Direct substitution for assignments \(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\) gives the outcome rows

 (1,1,-1),\ ((1+x)/2,1,1),\ ((1+x)/2,1,-1),\ (x,1,1),

F_{K_3}(w^G,b)=17x^4+12x^3-58x^2+12x+209

No limiting or numerical step is involved in (7): compactness is supplied by the displayed inequalities, boundary sections are included, and all terminal signs are rational.  For the legal constant schedule \(y_i(z)=1\), the sixteen values of \(S_z\), in lexicographic assignment order, are

Thus \(\xi_{K_3}=27/32\) and \(\xi_{C_4}=36/32=9/8\).  By \(\mathrm{def:frontier\text{-}constant}\) and \(C_{\cos}=64\pi^4/5\),

The displayed decimals are evaluations of these exact algebraic numbers.  Finally, the identity \(V_r^{\mathrm{quad}}=v_H/(rh^2)\) in \(\mathrm{thm:quadratic\text{-}frontier}\) gives

The lower constants follow by substituting the two values of \(L_H\) into the all-test lower bound in that theorem, completing every assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:small-motif-maximality-audit]\label{prop:small-motif-maximality-audit}
Assume \(\min_{a\in V_H}d_H(a)\ge2\).  If \(\max_{a\in V_H}d_H(a)\le3\), then the Gao weight \(w^G\) attains the motif variance infimum \(v_H\); in particular, neither the complete four-vertex motif nor the four-vertex diamond admits a strict worst-schedule conditional-variance improvement over \(w^G\) within \(\mathcal W_H\).  If \(H\) is the complete graph on \(h=5\) vertices, then \(w^G\) is the unique minimizer and \(v_H=2560\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
J_H(w)=\sup_{b\in\mathcal B_H}\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H).
\]
For fixed \(b\), \(S_H(w,b;Z_H)\) is linear in the finite vector \(w\).  Hence its variance is \(w^\top\Sigma_b w\), where \(\Sigma_b\) is a covariance matrix and is therefore positive semidefinite.  Thus \(w\mapsto\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)\) is convex, and its pointwise supremum \(J_H\) is convex.

For degree two, the moment equations were solved in the proof of Proposition 6 and force \(w_a=(8,-8,8)=w_a^G\).  For degree three, multiplying the moment equations by eight and solving them gives
\[
\bigl(w_a(0),w_a(1/3),w_a(2/3),w_a(1)\bigr)
=\bigl(18-\lambda_a,-6+\lambda_a,-6-\lambda_a,18+\lambda_a\bigr), \tag{8}
\]
where \(\lambda_a=w_a(1)-18\).  The case \(\lambda_a=0\) is exactly \(w_a^G\).

Let \(z^c=\mathbf1-z\).  Then \(T_a(z^c)=1-T_a(z)\).  If \(y_{b,a}(z_a,t)=b_{a,1}+b_{a,2}z_a+b_{a,3}t\), define
\[
y_{b^c,a}(z_a,t)=y_{b,a}(1-z_a,1-t)
=(b_{a,1}+b_{a,2}+b_{a,3})-b_{a,2}z_a-b_{a,3}t. \tag{9}
\]
Equation (9) is again a linear schedule, and it is bounded by one whenever \(b\) is bounded by one; hence \(b\mapsto b^c\) is a bijection of \(\mathcal B_H\).  Reversing the four coordinates in (8) changes \(\lambda_a\) to \(-\lambda_a\), while the degree-two weight is unchanged.  The Bernoulli-half design makes \(Z_H\) and \(Z_H^c\) identically distributed.  Consequently
\[
J_H(\lambda)=J_H(-\lambda).
\]
Convexity now yields
\[
J_H(0)\le\frac12\{J_H(\lambda)+J_H(-\lambda)\}=J_H(\lambda).
\]
This proves the first assertion for every graph whose degrees are only two or three, including the complete four-vertex graph and the diamond.

Now suppose that \(H\) is complete on five vertices, so each exposure has distribution \(\operatorname{Bin}(4,1/2)/4\).  Put \(u_a=w_a(3/4)\) and \(v_a=w_a(1)\).  Direct solution of the three moment equations gives
\[
\bigl(w_a(0),w_a(1/4),w_a(1/2),w_a(3/4),w_a(1)\bigr)
=\left(-4u_a-3v_a+128,\ 3u_a+2v_a-64,\ -2u_a-v_a+\frac{64}{3},\ u_a,\ v_a\right). \tag{10}
\]
For \(w^G\), \(u_a=0\) and \(v_a=32\).  Let \(q_a=v_a-32\).  The constant schedule \(y_{b,a}(z)=1\) is legal by \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}schedule}\).  Enumerating the thirty-two assignments by their total treated count and expanding the variance gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Z_H}S_H(w,b;Z_H)=2560+\frac12R(u,q), \tag{11}
\]
where
\[
R(u,q)=\frac{19}{2}\|u\|_2^2+\frac72\|q\|_2^2+11u^\top q+\frac12\left(\sum_a u_a+\sum_a q_a\right)^2. \tag{12}
\]
For completeness, (11) follows by substituting (10): before collecting squares, the excess is one half of
\[
10\sum_a u_a^2+\sum_{a<c}u_au_c+4\sum_a q_a^2+\sum_{a<c}q_aq_c
+12\sum_a u_aq_a+\sum_{a\ne c}q_au_c,
\]
which equals (12).  The first three terms in (12) are the sum over vertices of the binary quadratic form with matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}19/2&11/2\\11/2&7/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its leading principal minors are \(19/2>0\) and \(3>0\), so it is positive definite.  The last term in (12) is nonnegative.  Therefore \(R(u,q)\ge0\), with equality only at \(u=q=0\).  Since (11) uses one admissible schedule, every \(w\ne w^G\) satisfies \(J_H(w)>2560\).

It remains to upper-bound \(J_H(w^G)\).  Formula \(\mathrm{def:gao\text{-}weight}\) gives the five exposure weights
\[
(32,0,-32/3,0,32).
\]
Conditional on a total of \(M\) treated vertices, \(S_H(w^G,b;Z_H)\) lies respectively in
\[
[0,160],\ [0,32],\ [-32,0],\ [-32,0],\ [0,32],\ [0,160]\qquad(M=0,\ldots,5), \tag{13}
\]
because every squared outcome lies in \([0,1]\).  Hence, using the binomial multiplicities \(1,5,10,10,5,1\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}S_H\le\mathbb E S_H^2
\le\frac{2(160)^2+30(32)^2}{32}=2560. \tag{14}
\]
For the constant schedule, the six values indexed by \(M\) are \(160,32,-32,-32,32,160\); their binomially weighted mean is zero and their second moment is \(2560\).  Equality is therefore attained in (14).  Together with (11)--(12), this proves \(v_H=2560\) and uniqueness of \(w^G\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The audit resolves the requested first cases without guessing a gain: complement symmetry covers all maximum-degree-three motifs, and a positive-definite constant-schedule certificate plus a bounded-outcome upper envelope solves the complete five-vertex motif. \textit{Gap.} The published Gao construction does not determine whether another unbiased quadratic weight has smaller repeated-motif worst-schedule conditional variance. \textit{Consumer.} A design team may retain the Gao weight on these smallest non-degree-two motifs and reserve robust weight optimization for other graphs having degree at least four.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The explicit hard-prior calculation proves a constant-factor converse. The bound \(H^2(P_{0,a},P_{1,a})\le C_{\cos}r\xi_Ha^4\) does not identify the exact local likelihood experiment or establish quadratic sufficiency. The exact maximality audit rules out a strict weight improvement for every minimum-degree-two motif of maximum degree at most three and for the complete five-vertex motif. Other motifs having a vertex of degree at least four remain unresolved locations for a strict gain. The exact local power function and quadratic completeness also remain open.

\section{Honest scope}

The claims proved here are the existing exact \(K_3\) and \(C_4\) constants, a fixed-motif input and certificate-verification bound, and a maximality audit of the smallest non-degree-two cases. Convexity and complement symmetry prove that Gao's weight minimizes worst bounded-schedule conditional variance for every minimum-degree-two motif of maximum degree at most three; direct complete-five-vertex enumeration proves that it is uniquely minimax there with motif value 2560. The Bernoulli-half moment-reproduction identities, Gao et al.'s squared-separation convention, and their quadratic weight remain imported background from GaoHarshawSavjeWang2026 Section 4 and Appendix B. No strict weight improvement is claimed. Exact elimination of the eight- and sixteen-row programs supplies the \(K_3\) and \(C_4\) values; the decimals are algebraic evaluations, not simulations. A strict witness on another motif of maximum degree at least four and the exact local power constant remain open.
