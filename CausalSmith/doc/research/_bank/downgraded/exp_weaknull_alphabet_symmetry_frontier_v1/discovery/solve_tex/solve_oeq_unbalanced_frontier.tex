%%% FIELD thm-unbalanced-frontier-statement
Let \(n=2m\), \(K\geq 2\), \(1\leq n_1,n_0\leq n-1\), \(n_1+n_0=n\), and \(n_1\neq n_0\). Under complete randomization, the treated set is uniform over the \(\binom n{n_1}\) subsets of size \(n_1\), while the potential-outcome response table \(R\in\mathcal R_0(n,K)\), and hence the causal weak-null condition \(\tau_R=0\), is fixed. Complete the rectangular coefficient handle \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{unbal}}\) by putting
\[
 \mathcal X_{n_1,n_0,K}
 =\left\{(h,g)\in\mathbb Z_+^K\times\mathbb Z_+^K:
       \sum_a h_a=n_1,\ \sum_a g_a=n_0\right\},
\]
\[
 \mathcal T_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =\left\{T\in\mathbb Z_+^{K\times K}:0\leq T_{ab}\leq R_{ab},\quad
       \sum_bT_{ab}=h_a,\quad \sum_a(R_{ab}-T_{ab})=g_b\right\},
\]
and
\[
 W_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)}
       \prod_{a,b\in\mathcal A_K}\binom{R_{ab}}{T_{ab}},
 \qquad
 P_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)=\binom n{n_1}^{-1}W_R^{(n_1)}(h,g).
\]
For \(C,C'\in\mathcal X_{n_1,n_0,K}\), define \(C\sim_{n_1,n_0}C'\) when \(P_R^{(n_1)}(C)=P_R^{(n_1)}(C')\) for every \(R\in\mathcal R_0(n,K)\). Then
\[
 C\sim_{n_1,n_0}C'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C=C'.
\]
Consequently the maximal common bijection group
\[
 G_\star^{(n_1,n_0)}
 =\left\{f\in\operatorname{Bij}(\mathcal X_{n_1,n_0,K}):
 P_R^{(n_1)}(f(C))=P_R^{(n_1)}(C)\ \text{for every }C,R\right\}
\]
is \(\{\operatorname{id}\}\). Moreover, if
\[
 s_\star(n_1,n_0,K)
 =\max_{C\neq C'}\ \min\left\{q(R):R\in\mathcal R_0(n,K),\quad
       P_R^{(n_1)}(C)\neq P_R^{(n_1)}(C')\right\},
\]
then
\[
 s_\star(n_1,n_0,K)=2.
\]
Thus two heterogeneous units always suffice, by an explicit legal response table, and this uniform bound cannot be reduced.
%%% FIELD thm-unbalanced-frontier-proof
Write \(C=(h,g)\), \(C'=(h',g')\), and adopt the convention \(\binom uv=0\) unless \(0\leq v\leq u\). The displayed coefficient formula is first verified from the design. For a fixed response table \(R\), a treated-count matrix \(T\) specifies that exactly \(T_{ab}\) of the \(R_{ab}\) labeled units of type \((a,b)\) are treated. There are
\[
 \prod_{a,b\in\mathcal A_K}\binom{R_{ab}}{T_{ab}}
\]
such assignments. The row constraints on \(T\) give the treated histogram \(h\), and the column constraints on \(R-T\) give the control histogram \(g\). Distinct matrices \(T\) describe disjoint assignments, so summation gives \(W_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)\). Since the complete-randomization design assigns probability \(\binom n{n_1}^{-1}\) to each assignment, division by the positive number \(\binom n{n_1}\) gives \(P_R^{(n_1)}\).

For a vector \(r\in\mathbb Z_+^K\) with \(\sum_a r_a=n\), let \(D(r)\) be the diagonal table with \(D(r)_{aa}=r_a\). Its mass is \(n\), and every unit has effect zero; hence both the finite-table normalization and the weak-null condition hold, so \(D(r)\in\mathcal R_0(n,K)\). Directly from the preceding count,
\[
 W_{D(r)}^{(n_1)}(u,v)
 =\mathbf 1\{u+v=r\}\prod_{a\in\mathcal A_K}\binom{r_a}{u_a}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Set \(r=h+g\) and
\[
 Q_r(h)=\prod_a\binom{r_a}{h_a}.
\]
Every factor in \(Q_r(h)\) is positive because \(0\leq h_a\leq r_a\). If \(C\sim_{n_1,n_0}C'\), applying (1) to \(D(r)\) shows first that \(h'+g'=r\), since the count of \(C\) is positive, and then that
\[
 Q_r(h)=Q_r(h').
 \tag{2}
\]

We next extract all quadratic information by legal two-unit switches. Let \(e_a\) denote the coordinate vector at level \(a\), and let \(E_{ab}\) denote the response table of one unit of type \((a,b)\). If \(a\neq b\) and \(r_a,r_b>0\), define
\[
 R^{ab}=D(r-e_a-e_b)+E_{ab}+E_{ba}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The table in (3) has mass \(n\), while the effects of its two heterogeneous units sum to \((a-b)+(b-a)=0\). Thus it is legal and \(q(R^{ab})=2\). The two special units contribute pooled observed outcomes \(e_a+e_b\) when both are treated or both are controlled, \(2e_a\) when the \((a,b)\) unit alone is treated, and \(2e_b\) in the opposite mixed allocation. Because the diagonal filler in (3) has pooled histogram \(r-e_a-e_b\), only the first two allocations can realize a profile whose pooled histogram is \(r\). Applying the elementary identities
\[
 \binom{s-1}{t-1}=\frac ts\binom st,
 \qquad
 \binom{s-1}{t}=\frac{s-t}{s}\binom st
\]
with the strictly positive denominators \(s=r_a,r_b\) gives
\[
 W_{R^{ab}}^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =Q_r(h)\frac{h_ah_b+g_ag_b}{r_ar_b}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Define the arm-imbalance vectors
\[
 d=h-g=2h-r,\qquad d'=h'-g'=2h'-r.
\]
Since
\[
 h_ah_b+g_ag_b=\frac{r_ar_b+d_ad_b}{2},
 \tag{5}
\]
equivalence, (2), and (4) imply
\[
 d_ad_b=d'_ad'_b
 \quad\text{for all distinct }a,b\text{ with }r_a,r_b>0.
 \tag{6}
\]

The diagonal entries of the same outer product are obtained from a second placement of the switch. Fix \(a\) with \(r_a\geq2\), choose any \(b\neq a\), which is possible because \(K\geq2\), and put
\[
 \widetilde R^{a\mid b}=D(r-2e_a)+E_{ab}+E_{ba}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Again the mass is \(n\), the two nonzero effects cancel, and \(q(\widetilde R^{a\mid b})=2\). The filler in (7) has pooled histogram \(r-2e_a\). Exactly one allocation of the special units supplies the missing pooled histogram \(2e_a\): the \((a,b)\) unit is treated and the \((b,a)\) unit is controlled. The other three allocations supply \(e_a+e_b\) or \(2e_b\), which differ because \(a\neq b\). Therefore
\[
 W_{\widetilde R^{a\mid b}}^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =\prod_c\binom{r_c-2\mathbf 1\{c=a\}}
                       {h_c-\mathbf 1\{c=a\}}
 =Q_r(h)\frac{h_ag_a}{r_a(r_a-1)}.
 \tag{8}
\]
All divisions in (8) are valid because \(r_a\geq2\). Equivalence and (2) now imply \(h_ag_a=h'_ag'_a\), and hence
\[
 d_a^2=r_a^2-4h_ag_a=r_a^2-4h'_ag'_a=(d'_a)^2.
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(r_a=0\), both corresponding imbalance coordinates vanish. If \(r_a=1\), integrality and \(h_a,h'_a\in\{0,1\}\) give \(d_a^2=(d'_a)^2=1\). Thus (9) holds for every \(a\); together with (6), including the trivial products involving a zero-support coordinate, it yields
\[
 dd^{\mathsf T}=d'(d')^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{10}
\]

The unequal allocation is used precisely now. Both profile constraints give
\[
 \sum_a d_a=\sum_a d'_a=n_1-n_0=:\delta\neq0.
 \tag{11}
\]
In particular \(d\neq0\). Choose \(j\) with \(d_j\neq0\). By (10), \(d'_j=\varepsilon d_j\) for some \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), and the \((j,a)\) entries of (10) give \(d'_a=\varepsilon d_a\) for every \(a\). Summing and using (11) gives \(\delta=\varepsilon\delta\); positivity of \(|\delta|\) forces \(\varepsilon=1\). Hence \(d'=d\), and then
\[
 h'=\frac{r+d'}2=\frac{r+d}2=h,
 \qquad
 g'=\frac{r-d'}2=\frac{r-d}2=g.
\]
This proves that every equivalence class is a singleton.

The argument is constructive for every distinct pair. If the pooled histograms differ, (1) with \(D(h+g)\) separates them. If their pooled histograms agree but their values of \(Q_r\) differ, the same diagonal table separates them. Otherwise (2) holds. If some off-diagonal product in (6) differs, the table (3) separates the pair by (4). If no such product differs, distinctness and the implication (10)--(11) show that some squared coordinate must differ. Such a coordinate necessarily has \(r_a\geq2\), and (7) separates the pair by (8). Every displayed separator is legal and has \(q\leq2\). It follows immediately from the singleton partition that a common probability-preserving bijection fixes every profile, so \(G_\star^{(n_1,n_0)}=\{\operatorname{id}\}\), and the preceding construction proves
\[
 s_\star(n_1,n_0,K)\leq2.
 \tag{12}
\]

It remains to certify sharpness uniformly over all admissible \((n_1,n_0,K)\). Since \(n=2m\) and \(n_1\neq n_0\), one has \(m\geq2\). Define
\[
 \ell=\max\{0,n_1-m\},\qquad u=\min\{m,n_1\}.
\]
The bounds \(1\leq n_1\leq2m-1\) give \(\ell<u\), and a direct split into \(n_1\leq m\) and \(n_1>m\) gives \(\ell+u=n_1\). Using levels \(0,1\in\mathcal A_K\), define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h&=\ell e_0+u e_1,
 &g&=(m-\ell)e_0+(m-u)e_1,\\
 h'&=u e_0+\ell e_1,
 &g'&=(m-u)e_0+(m-\ell)e_1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{13}
\]
Both profiles have arm sizes \((n_1,n_0)\), both have pooled histogram \(r=me_0+me_1\), and they are distinct because \(\ell<u\). If a legal table has \(q(R)=0\), then it is diagonal. Formula (1) makes both counts zero unless \(R=D(r)\), while for \(R=D(r)\),
\[
 W_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)=\binom m\ell\binom mu
 =\binom mu\binom m\ell
 =W_R^{(n_1)}(h',g').
 \tag{14}
\]
There is no legal table with \(q(R)=1\): all diagonal units have zero effect, whereas the single heterogeneous unit would contribute the nonzero effect \(a-b\), contradicting the weak-null sum. Thus the pair (13) cannot be separated with fewer than two heterogeneous units. Combining this fact with (12) proves
\[
 s_\star(n_1,n_0,K)=2.
\]

For the smallest admissible instance \((n,K,n_1,n_0)=(4,2,1,3)\), (13) gives the two profiles \((h,g)=((0,1),(2,1))\) and \((h',g')=((1,0),(1,2))\). Their diagonal counts under \(D(2e_0+2e_1)\) are both \(2\), and all other diagonal tables give both count zero. The legal table \(2E_{11}+E_{01}+E_{10}\), which is (7) with \(a=0,b=1\), gives assignment counts \(0\) and \(1\), respectively, by (8). This exact enumeration checks both the upper construction and the lower obstruction at the boundary case.
