%%% FIELD proof-unbalanced-frontier
Adopt the convention \(\binom{x}{y}=0\) unless \(x\) and \(y\) are
integers satisfying \(0\leq y\leq x\).  We first verify the displayed
coefficient kernel from the randomization design.  Fix
\(R\in\mathcal R_0(n,K)\).  For each matrix \(T\) satisfying the constraints
in \(\mathcal T_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)\), one may independently choose the
\(T_{ab}\) treated units from the \(R_{ab}\) labeled units of type \((a,b)\).
The number of assignments having treated-count matrix \(T\) is therefore
\[
 \prod_{a,b\in\mathcal A_K}\binom{R_{ab}}{T_{ab}}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The row constraints on \(T\) make the treated histogram equal to \(h\), and
the column constraints on \(R-T\) make the control histogram equal to \(g\).
Conversely, every assignment producing \((h,g)\) has exactly one such
treated-count matrix.  The sets of assignments indexed by distinct matrices
\(T\) are disjoint.  Summing (1) thus proves the formula for
\(W_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)\).  By the complete-randomization hypothesis, each subset
of \(n_1\) treated units has probability \(\binom n{n_1}^{-1}\).  The
assumptions \(1\leq n_1\leq n-1\) and \(n\geq2\) imply
\(\binom n{n_1}>0\), so division gives the asserted formula for
\(P_R^{(n_1)}(h,g)\).  Notice also that \(R\) is fixed: by
\(\mathcal R_0(n,K)\), the table-mass condition and the weak-null condition
hold, and the latter gives
\[
 \tau_R
 =\frac1n\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal A_K}(a-b)R_{ab}=0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus the causal weak-null target is a fixed finite-population contrast,
whereas the kernel just derived is its assignment-induced observed-law
functional.

Write \(C=(h,g)\), \(C'=(h',g')\), and put \(r=h+g\).  For every
\(s\in\mathbb Z_+^K\) with \(\sum_a s_a=n\), the diagonal table \(D_s\)
has mass \(n\), and each of its units has individual effect zero.  Hence the
two conditions defining \(\mathcal R_0(n,K)\) show that
\(D_s\in\mathcal R_0(n,K)\).  Under this diagonal table, every unit observed
at level \(a\) is one of the \(s_a\) units of type \((a,a)\).  Consequently
\[
 W_{D_s}^{(n_1)}(u,v)
 =\mathbf 1\{u+v=s\}\prod_{a\in\mathcal A_K}\binom{s_a}{u_a}.
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
 W_{D_r}^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =Q_r(h):=\prod_a\binom{r_a}{h_a}>0,
 \tag{4}
\]
because \(0\leq h_a\leq r_a\) for every \(a\).  Suppose now that
\(C\sim_{n_1,n_0}C'\).  Equality of the two probabilities under \(D_r\),
together with the common positive normalizing constant \(\binom n{n_1}\),
implies equality of their assignment counts.  By (3)--(4), the count for
\(C'\) must be positive, so
\[
 h'+g'=r,
 \qquad
 Q_r(h')=Q_r(h)>0.
 \tag{5}
\]

We next use legal response tables with two heterogeneous units.  If
\(a\neq b\) and \(r_a,r_b>0\), define
\[
 R^{ab}=D_{\,r-e_a-e_b}+E_{ab}+E_{ba}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Its mass is \((n-2)+2=n\), and the effects of its two heterogeneous units
sum to \((a-b)+(b-a)=0\).  Thus (by the two defining conditions of
\(\mathcal R_0(n,K)\))
\[
 R^{ab}\in\mathcal R_0(n,K),
 \qquad q(R^{ab})=2.
 \tag{7}
\]
The pooled observed outcomes of the two special units are \(e_a+e_b\) if
both or neither are treated, \(2e_a\) if the \((a,b)\) unit alone is treated,
and \(2e_b\) if the \((b,a)\) unit alone is treated.  The diagonal filler has
fixed pooled histogram \(r-e_a-e_b\).  Hence a profile with pooled histogram
\(r\) can arise only when both or neither special unit is treated.  Applying
\[
 \binom{s-1}{t-1}=\frac{t}{s}\binom{s}{t},
 \qquad
 \binom{s-1}{t}=\frac{s-t}{s}\binom{s}{t}
 \tag{8}
\]
with the positive denominators \(s=r_a,r_b\), and adding those two disjoint
cases, gives
\[
 W_{R^{ab}}^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =Q_r(h)\frac{h_ah_b+g_ag_b}{r_ar_b}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The same calculation applies to \(C'\) by (5).  Define
\[
 d=h-g=2h-r,\qquad d'=h'-g'=2h'-r.
 \tag{10}
\]
Since
\[
 h_ah_b+g_ag_b=\frac{r_ar_b+d_ad_b}{2},
 \tag{11}
\]
equivalence, (5), and (9) imply
\[
 d_ad_b=d'_ad'_b
 \quad\text{whenever }a\neq b\text{ and }r_a,r_b>0.
 \tag{12}
\]

To recover the diagonal entries of the imbalance outer product, fix a level
\(a\) with \(r_a\geq2\).  Because \(K\geq2\), choose a level \(b\neq a\)
and define
\[
 \widetilde R^{a\mid b}
 =D_{\,r-2e_a}+E_{ab}+E_{ba}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The same mass and effect-sum calculation as in (7) proves
\(\widetilde R^{a\mid b}\in\mathcal R_0(n,K)\) and
\(q(\widetilde R^{a\mid b})=2\).  Its diagonal filler has pooled histogram
\(r-2e_a\).  Of the four allocations of the two special units, exactly one
supplies the missing pooled histogram \(2e_a\): the \((a,b)\) unit is
treated and the \((b,a)\) unit is controlled.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_{\widetilde R^{a\mid b}}^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 &=\binom{r_a-2}{h_a-1}
   \prod_{c\neq a}\binom{r_c}{h_c}\\
 &=Q_r(h)\frac{h_ag_a}{r_a(r_a-1)}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
The second equality follows directly by expanding factorials; it remains
valid at \(h_a=0\) and \(h_a=r_a\) under the binomial convention.  Its
denominator is positive because \(r_a\geq2\).  Equivalence and (5) applied
to (14) yield \(h_ag_a=h'_ag'_a\), and hence
\[
 d_a^2=r_a^2-4h_ag_a
       =r_a^2-4h'_ag'_a=(d'_a)^2.
 \tag{15}
\]
If \(r_a=0\), both imbalance coordinates are zero.  If \(r_a=1\), the
integrality of \(h_a,h'_a\) and (5) gives
\(d_a,d'_a\in\{-1,1\}\).  Thus (15) holds for every \(a\).  Products
involving a coordinate with \(r_a=0\) vanish, so (12) and (15) together give
\[
 dd^{\mathsf T}=d'(d')^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{16}
\]

The unequal allocation is decisive at this point.  The profile constraints
and (10) give
\[
 \sum_a d_a=\sum_a d'_a=n_1-n_0=:\delta\neq0.
 \tag{17}
\]
In particular \(d\neq0\).  Choose \(j\) with \(d_j\neq0\).  The
\((j,j)\) entry of (16) gives \(d'_j=\varepsilon d_j\) for some
\(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\).  Its \((j,a)\) entry then gives
\(d'_a=\varepsilon d_a\) for every \(a\).  Summing and using (17) yields
\(\delta=\varepsilon\delta\).  Since \(\delta\neq0\), one has
\(\varepsilon=1\).  Consequently \(d'=d\), and (5) and (10) imply
\[
 h'=\frac{r+d'}2=\frac{r+d}2=h,
 \qquad
 g'=\frac{r-d'}2=\frac{r-d}2=g.
 \tag{18}
\]
This proves
\[
 C\sim_{n_1,n_0}C'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C=C',
 \tag{19}
\]
where the reverse implication is immediate from equality of the arguments
of the kernel.

The proof also gives an explicit separator of heterogeneous-unit count at
most two for every distinct pair.  Indeed, first use \(D_{h+g}\).  Formula
(3) separates the profiles if their pooled histograms differ, and (4)
separates them if the pooled histograms agree but their \(Q_r\)-coefficients
differ.  In either case the separator has \(q=0\).  Otherwise the profiles
share \(r\) and \(Q_r\).  If some product in (12) differs, then its
coordinates necessarily have positive \(r_a,r_b\), and (9) shows that the
explicit table (6) separates them.  If no such product differs, distinctness
and the argument from (16)--(18) imply that some square in (15) differs.
Such a coordinate cannot have \(r_a=0\) or \(r_a=1\), so \(r_a\geq2\);
then (14) shows that the explicit table (13), with any \(b\neq a\),
separates them.  Every table in this case distinction is legal and has
\(q\leq2\).  It follows that
\[
 s_\star(n_1,n_0,K)\leq2.
 \tag{20}
\]

This upper bound is sharp for every admissible unequal allocation and every
\(K\geq2\).  The assumptions \(n=2m\), \(n_1+n_0=2m\), positivity of both
arm sizes, and \(n_1\neq n_0\) imply \(m\geq2\).  Put
\[
 \ell=\max\{0,n_1-m\},\qquad u=\min\{m,n_1\}.
 \tag{21}
\]
If \(n_1\leq m\), then \((\ell,u)=(0,n_1)\); if \(n_1>m\), then
\((\ell,u)=(n_1-m,m)\).  Since \(1\leq n_1\leq2m-1\), these two cases prove
\[
 \ell+u=n_1,\qquad 0\leq\ell<u\leq m.
 \tag{22}
\]
Using the two available levels \(0,1\in\mathcal A_K\), define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h&=\ell e_0+u e_1,
 &g&=(m-\ell)e_0+(m-u)e_1,\\
 h'&=u e_0+\ell e_1,
 &g'&=(m-u)e_0+(m-\ell)e_1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{23}
\]
Equations (22)--(23) show that both treated histograms have mass \(n_1\),
both control histograms have mass
\(2m-n_1=n_0\), and both pooled histograms equal
\[
 r=me_0+me_1.
 \tag{24}
\]
They are distinct because \(\ell<u\).

If a legal table \(R\) has \(q(R)=0\), all its off-diagonal entries vanish;
the table-mass condition therefore makes it \(D_s\) for some
\(s\in\mathbb Z_+^K\) with \(\sum_a s_a=n\).  Formula (3) makes the counts
of both profiles in (23) zero when \(s\neq r\).  When \(s=r\), it gives
\[
 W_{D_r}^{(n_1)}(h,g)
 =\binom m\ell\binom mu
 =\binom mu\binom m\ell
 =W_{D_r}^{(n_1)}(h',g').
 \tag{25}
\]
Thus no legal table with \(q=0\) separates this pair.  Nor can a legal table
have \(q=1\): it would contain exactly one off-diagonal unit of some type
\((a,b)\) with \(a\neq b\), all other units being diagonal, so the weak-null
sum would be \(a-b\neq0\), contrary to the weak-null condition defining
\(\mathcal R_0(n,K)\).  Hence the minimum separator size for (23) is at
least two.  Combining this witness with (20) proves
\[
 s_\star(n_1,n_0,K)=2.
 \tag{26}
\]

Finally, let \(f\in G_\star^{(n_1,n_0)}\).  Its defining invariance says that
\(P_R^{(n_1)}(f(C))=P_R^{(n_1)}(C)\) for every legal \(R\), so
\(f(C)\sim_{n_1,n_0}C\).  By (19), \(f(C)=C\) for every profile \(C\).
Thus \(f=\operatorname{id}\), while the identity plainly satisfies the
defining invariance, and therefore
\[
 G_\star^{(n_1,n_0)}=\{\operatorname{id}\}.
 \tag{27}
\]

For the smallest admissible unequal-allocation instance,
\((m,n_1,n_0,K)=(2,1,3,2)\), (23) gives
\[
 (h,g)=((0,1),(2,1)),\qquad
 (h',g')=((1,0),(1,2)).
\]
Their diagonal counts under \(D_r\), \(r=2e_0+2e_1\), are both \(2\), and
all other diagonal tables give both counts zero.  The legal table
\(2E_{11}+E_{01}+E_{10}\) has two heterogeneous units; direct substitution
in (14) gives assignment counts \(0\) and \(1\), respectively.  This exact
four-unit check confirms both sides of the sharpness argument at the
boundary.
