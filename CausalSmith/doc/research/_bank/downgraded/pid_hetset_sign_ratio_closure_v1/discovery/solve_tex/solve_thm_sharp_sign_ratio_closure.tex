%%% FIELD positive-density-condition
Under \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), for every \(i\in V\) and for \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\)-almost every \(x\), the conditional law of \((D_i,U_i)\) given \(X=x\) admits an everywhere-strictly-positive Lebesgue density on \(\mathbb R^2\), and \(E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[D_i^2+U_i^2]<\infty\).
%%% FIELD published-class-construction
Define the published Huntington--Klein specialization by
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}}),
&\text{if }0<\ell_{jk}\le 1\le u_{jk}\text{ for every }(j,k)\in\vec E,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
%%% FIELD positive-density-class-construction
\(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=\{P:\text{ass:partially-linear-model, ass:observational-equivalence, ass:bias-box, ass:positive-ratio, and ass:gaussian-observed-submodel hold for }(K,V,\vec E,s,\nu^-,\nu^+,\ell,u,P_{\mathrm{obs}},X,D,U)\}\).
%%% FIELD zero-safe-completion-construction
For \(B\in\mathcal B_G\), start with one draw of the entire observed vector from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and retain every observed coordinate unchanged.  Compute \(U_i=Y_i-s_iD_i\) and, for every \(i\in V\), set
\[
A_i^B=U_i+B_iD_i,\qquad
\theta_i^B=s_i-B_i,\qquad
\varepsilon_i^B=0.
\]
On the residualized scale set \(f_i^B(A_i^B)=A_i^B\); when lifting the equation back to the original scale, set \(f_i^B(X_i,A_i^B)\) equal to the observed-control projection removed during residualization plus \(A_i^B\).  Let \(P^B\) be the joint law of the unchanged observed vector and the resulting \(A^B\), with residualized structural equation
\[
Y_i=\theta_i^BD_i+f_i^B(A_i^B)+\varepsilon_i^B.
\]
This is one formula for all \(B_i\), including \(B_i=0\), and contains no division or auxiliary randomizer.
%%% FIELD hybrid-handle-construction
Under \(\texttt{ass:iid-triangular-sampling}\), \(\texttt{ass:primitive-moment-envelope}\), \(\texttt{ass:primitive-covariance}\), and \(\texttt{ass:regular-primitive-map}\), let \(m_n=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor\), use \(m_n\) observations as a pilot subsample, and use the independent remaining subsample of size \(N_n=n-m_n\) for inference.  On the inference subsample choose \(\varepsilon_{N_n}\to0\) with \(\sqrt{N_n}\,\varepsilon_{N_n}\to\infty\).  Define \(\mathsf H_n\) by a deterministic diagonal calibration over compact inner sieves of the finitely many strict regular cells.  The pilot selects numerical-delta inference only if a shrinking closed neighborhood of its primitive mean and covariance is contained in one inner sieve with positive denominator, ratio-order, branch-selection, primal-slack, dual-slack, reduced-cost, and endpoint-variance margins.  On that event, \(\mathsf H_n\) is the slab determined by the outer lower and upper \(q\)-support endpoints of the inference-subsample numerical-delta intervals; otherwise \(\mathsf H_n\) is the inference-subsample undivided inversion set.  The inner sieves and continuity moduli define an existence and calibrated-sieve construction; no globally implementable modulus or ball-containment algorithm is asserted.
%%% FIELD completion-membership-statement
Suppose the conditional-positive-density condition \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\) holds.  For every \(B\in\mathcal B_G\), the law \(P^B\) from \(\texttt{def:gaussian-completion}\) has observed margin exactly \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), including the full cross-setting joint law, satisfies the partially linear model, has \(B(P^B)=B\), belongs to \(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and yields \(\theta(P^B)=s-B\).  For every \(i\in V\),
\[
A_i^B=U_i+B_iD_i,
\qquad
(s_i-B_i)D_i+A_i^B=s_iD_i+U_i=Y_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{P^B}(D_i\mid X,A_i^B)>0
\]
almost surely.  If \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) for every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), then also \(P^B\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
%%% FIELD completion-membership-proof
Fix \(B\in\mathcal B_G\).  By \(\texttt{def:gaussian-completion}\), \(P^B\) is formed by adjoining \(A^B\) to one unchanged draw from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Hence the marginal law of every observed coordinate \((X,D,Y)\), and therefore their entire cross-setting joint distribution, is exactly \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  This proves \(\texttt{ass:observational-equivalence}\) and shows that the population short-regression coefficient remains \(s\).

We next verify the support condition needed by \(\texttt{ass:partially-linear-model}\).  Fix \(i\in V\).  By \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\), for almost every \(x\) the conditional law of \((D_i,U_i)\) given \(X=x\) has a density \(g_{i,x}(d,u)>0\) for every \((d,u)\in\mathbb R^2\).  For the fixed scalar \(B_i\), the linear map
\[
T_{B_i}(d,u)=(d,a)=(d,u+B_id)
\]
is bijective, has inverse \(T_{B_i}^{-1}(d,a)=(d,a-B_id)\), and has Jacobian determinant one.  The change-of-variables formula therefore gives the conditional density
\[
h_{i,x}^B(d,a)=g_{i,x}(d,a-B_id)>0
\]
of \((D_i,A_i^B)\) given \(X=x\).  Its \(A_i^B\)-marginal density
\[
m_{i,x}^B(a)=\int_{\mathbb R}h_{i,x}^B(d,a)\,\mathrm d d
\]
is strictly positive and finite for almost every \(a\) under that marginal.  At every such \((x,a)\), the conditional density of \(D_i\) is \(h_{i,x}^B(d,a)/m_{i,x}^B(a)\), which is positive for every \(d\in\mathbb R\).  It consequently assigns positive probability to each of two disjoint bounded intervals and cannot be concentrated at one point.  The finite-second-moment part of \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\), together with iterated expectation, makes its conditional second moment finite almost surely.  Thus
\[
0<\operatorname{Var}_{P^B}(D_i\mid X,A_i^B)<\infty
\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(X_i\) is contained in \(X\), the conditional variance decomposition applied after coarsening from \((X,A_i^B)\) to \((X_i,A_i^B)\) shows that the residual variance after projection on \((X_i,A_i^B)\) is positive as well.

The construction is zero-safe: for every real \(B_i\), including \(B_i=0\),
\[
(s_i-B_i)D_i+f_i^B(A_i^B)+\varepsilon_i^B
=(s_i-B_i)D_i+(U_i+B_iD_i)+0
=s_iD_i+U_i=Y_i. \tag{2}
\]
Adding back the observed-control projection gives the corresponding equality before residualization.  Since \(\varepsilon_i^B=0\),
\[
E_{P^B}[\varepsilon_i^B\mid D_i,X_i,A_i^B]=0. \tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) verify \(\texttt{ass:partially-linear-model}\).  They also make its coefficient unique: if \(R_i=D_i-E_{P^B}[D_i\mid X_i,A_i^B]\), then \(E[R_iD_i]=E[R_i^2]>0\), while \(E[R_if_i^B(X_i,A_i^B)]=0\); multiplying (2) by \(R_i\) and taking expectations therefore yields
\[
\theta_i(P^B)=\frac{E[R_iY_i]}{E[R_iD_i]}=s_i-B_i. \tag{4}
\]
Because the observed short coefficient remains \(s_i\), the defining identity \(B(P)=s-\theta(P)\) and (4) imply
\[
B_i(P^B)=s_i-\theta_i(P^B)=B_i. \tag{5}
\]

Membership \(B\in\mathcal B_G\), together with \(\texttt{lem:component-sign-decomposition}\), supplies both the coordinatewise bias boxes and witnesses \(\rho_{jk}(P^B)\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) satisfying \(B_j(P^B)=\rho_{jk}(P^B)B_k(P^B)\).  Thus \(\texttt{ass:bias-box}\) and \(\texttt{ass:positive-ratio}\) hold.  The conditional-positive-density property is a property of the unchanged observed margin.  The member-property definitions \(\texttt{def:gaussian-completion-class}\) and \(\texttt{def:hk-model-class}\) now give
\[
P^B\in\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\qquad \theta(P^B)=s-B. \tag{6}
\]
Finally, if \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) on every edge, \(\texttt{def:published-hk-model-class}\) gives \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The last assertion follows from (6).
%%% FIELD sharp-closure-statement
For every observed law and every generalized positive-ratio envelope, \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\).  Under the conditional-positive-density condition \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\),
\[
\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=s-\mathcal B_G.
\]
If additionally \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) for every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), then \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), \(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and
\[
\{\theta(P):P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
=\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=s-\mathcal B_G.
\]
Hence the affine componentwise sign union is pointwise sharp for the published Huntington--Klein specialization throughout the stated conditional-positive-density observed-law family, while the first two containments remain valid for the generalized class.  The former nonsingular jointly Gaussian family is an immediate subfamily, and the construction restricts no cross-setting dependence in the observed joint margin.
%%% FIELD sharp-closure-proof
Take \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The member properties in \(\texttt{def:hk-model-class}\) imply \(B_i(P)\in[\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+]\) for every \(i\in V\) and, for every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), the existence of \(\rho_{jk}(P)\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) satisfying
\[
B_j(P)=\rho_{jk}(P)B_k(P). \tag{1}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:component-sign-decomposition}\), these conditions are equivalent to \(B(P)\in\mathcal B_G\).  Since \(B(P)=s-\theta(P)\),
\[
\theta(P)=s-B(P)\in s-\mathcal B_G. \tag{2}
\]
Taking the image over all compatible \(P\) in \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\) proves
\[
\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq s-\mathcal B_G. \tag{3}
\]
This is an outer-bound statement and uses neither the positive-density condition nor the published-envelope specialization.

For the second containment, fix \(b\in\mathcal B_G\) and put \(\vartheta=s-b\).  The box restrictions in \(\texttt{def:positive-branch}\) and \(\texttt{def:negative-branch}\) give
\[
s_i-\nu_i^+\le \vartheta_i\le s_i-\nu_i^-\qquad(i\in V). \tag{4}
\]
For an edge \((j,k)\), \(\texttt{lem:component-sign-decomposition}\) supplies \(r_{jk}\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) with \(b_j=r_{jk}b_k\), so
\[
b_j-b_k=(r_{jk}-1)b_k. \tag{5}
\]
The function \((r,v)\mapsto(r-1)v\) is affine in either argument when the other is fixed.  Successively expressing \(r\) and \(v\) as convex combinations of their interval endpoints writes every value on \([\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\times[\nu_k^-,\nu_k^+]\) as a convex combination of its four corner values.  Its minimum and maximum are therefore the corner extrema \(c_{jk}^-\) and \(c_{jk}^+\) in \(\texttt{def:hk-outer-polytope}\).  Applying this to (5) yields
\[
c_{jk}^-\le b_j-b_k\le c_{jk}^+. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(\vartheta_j-\vartheta_k=(s_j-s_k)-(b_j-b_k)\), equations (4) and (6) are exactly the defining inequalities of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\).  Hence
\[
s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}. \tag{7}
\]
This is again a universal algebraic containment over the generalized class; it need not be sharp for the outer polytope.

Now impose \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\), whose replaced content is the conditional-positive-density condition.  For every \(b\in\mathcal B_G\), \(\texttt{prop:gaussian-completion-membership}\) constructs a law \(P^b\in\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfying \(\theta(P^b)=s-b\).  Consequently
\[
s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}}). \tag{8}
\]
Combining (3) and (8) proves equality.  Moreover, (8) proves sharpness rather than merely validity: every point of the displayed affine union is attained by an observationally equivalent full-data law.

Suppose next that \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) for every edge.  By \(\texttt{def:published-hk-model-class}\),
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}}). \tag{9}
\]
The proposition and (9) give \(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and substituting (9) into \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\) gives
\[
\{\theta(P):P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
=\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=s-\mathcal B_G. \tag{10}
\]
Thus (10) is the exact published-class transfer, while (3) and (7) retain the more general envelope scope.

Finally, a nonsingular conditional bivariate Gaussian law for \((D_i,U_i)\) has density
\[
(2\pi)^{-1}\det(\Sigma_i)^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left\{-\tfrac12(w-m_i)^\top\Sigma_i^{-1}(w-m_i)\right\}>0
\]
at every \(w\in\mathbb R^2\), and has finite second moments.  It therefore satisfies the conditional-positive-density condition, proving the Gaussian corollary.  Because every construction above leaves the entire observed vector untouched, no cross-setting covariance or other observed dependence is altered or restricted.
