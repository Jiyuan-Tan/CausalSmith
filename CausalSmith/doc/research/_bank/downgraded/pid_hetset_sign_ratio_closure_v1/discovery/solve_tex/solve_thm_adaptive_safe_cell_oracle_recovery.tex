Fix \(q\in\mathbb R^K\).  Under \texttt{ass:iid-triangular-sampling}, \texttt{ass:primitive-moment-envelope}, \texttt{ass:primitive-covariance}, and \texttt{ass:regular-primitive-map}, there exists a calibrated-sieve split-sample realization of \(\mathsf H_n\) with the following properties.  Let \(m_n=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor\) and \(N_n=n-m_n\).  On the inference subsample use the numerical-difference step \(\varepsilon_{N_n}\), for which \(\varepsilon_{N_n}\to0\) and \(\sqrt{N_n}\,\varepsilon_{N_n}\to\infty\).  A conservative pilot rule selects numerical-delta inference only when a shrinking closed neighborhood of the pilot primitive mean and covariance is contained in a compact inner sieve of one strict regular cell having nonzero denominator, ratio-order, branch-selection, primal-slack, dual-slack, reduced-cost, and endpoint-variance margins; otherwise it selects primitive-moment inversion.  The inner sieves and their continuity moduli give an existence and calibration construction and are not asserted to admit a globally implementable ball-containment algorithm.  On inversion selection, \(\mathsf H_n\) is the inference-subsample version of \(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{inv}}\).  On safe-cell selection, \(\mathsf H_n\) is the slab whose lower and upper \(q\)-support limits are, respectively, the lower endpoint of the first interval and the upper endpoint of the second interval in the inference-subsample version of \(\mathcal I_{q,N_n}^{\mathrm{ND}}\).  Then
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)}P_n\{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\subseteq\mathsf H_n\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For each fixed \(C<\infty\), the pilot selects inversion with probability tending to one uniformly over admissible arrays for which at least one denominator margin \(|d_i(\mu_n)|\), ratio-order margin \(|d_j(\mu_n)^2-d_k(\mu_n)^2|\), or nonnegative component-branch or optimizer-basis tie margin is at most \(Cn^{-1/2}\); exact zeros and exact ties are included.  At every fixed law \(P_0\) satisfying all strict-cell conditions of \texttt{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery} and having two positive endpoint variances, the pilot selects the safe cell with probability tending to one, and the two finite \(q\)-support endpoints of \(\mathsf H_n\) differ from the corresponding outer endpoints of the full-sample \(\mathcal I_{q,n}^{\mathrm{or}}\) by \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\).

Put \(m=m_n\), \(N=N_n\), and write \(\eta=(\mu,\Omega)\), equipped with the product norm
\[
 \|\eta\|_*:=\|\mu\|+\|\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Because \(m=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor\), we have \(m\to\infty\), \(N\to\infty\), \(m/n\to0\), and \(N/n\to1\).  The defining conditions on \(\varepsilon_n\) therefore imply
\[
 \varepsilon_N\to0,
 \qquad
 \sqrt N\,\varepsilon_N\to\infty. \tag{1}
\]

We first construct the calibrated safe sets.  By \texttt{thm:cycle-farkas-and-computation}, there are finitely many component-sign branches, and each endpoint problem has finitely many candidate primal--dual bases.  Let \(\mathcal G\) be the set of pairs \(\eta=(\mu,\Omega)\) for which both endpoint problems have a unique winning branch and a unique primal--dual basis, all ratio-order, branch-selection, primal-slack, dual-slack, and reduced-cost inequalities are strict, every \(d_i(\mu)\) is nonzero, and
\[
 v_r(\eta):=g_r(\mu)^\top\Omega g_r(\mu)>0,
 \qquad
 g_r(\mu):=D\psi_{q,r}(\mu),
 \qquad r\in\{1,2\}. \tag{2}
\]
Here the denominator margins are \(|d_i(\mu)|\), the ratio-order margins are \(|d_j(\mu)^2-d_k(\mu)^2|\), and the other named margins are the nonnegative distances of the finite branch and basis inequalities from their equality boundaries.  Let \(\Delta(\eta)\) be the minimum of this finite list of margins for \(\eta\in\mathcal G\), and set \(\Delta(\eta)=0\) for \(\eta\notin\mathcal G\).  The strict-cell conclusion of \texttt{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery}, together with the continuous differentiability in \texttt{ass:regular-primitive-map}, shows that \(\mathcal G\) is open and that \(g_r\) is continuous on each of its finitely many cells.

For \(j\ge1\), take \(G_j\) to be the finite union over strict cells of the closed subsets on which
\[
 \|\eta\|_*\le j,
 \quad j^{-1}I_p\preceq\Omega\preceq jI_p,
 \quad \operatorname{dist}(\eta,\mathcal G^c)\ge j^{-1},
 \quad \Delta(\eta)\ge j^{-1},
 \quad j^{-1}\le v_r(\eta)\le j,
 \quad \|g_r(\mu)\|\le j
 \quad(r=1,2). \tag{3}
\]
Replacing \(G_j\) by \(\bigcup_{k\le j}G_k\) makes the sequence nested.  Each \(G_j\) is compact, and every point of \(\mathcal G\) is in the interior of some \(G_j\), because at such a point the finite margins and the two variances in (2) are positive and the point has positive distance from the complement of its open cell.  Define the within-cell gradient modulus
\[
 \omega_j(r):=max_{a\in\{1,2\}}
 \sup\bigl\{\|g_a(\mu)-g_a(\mu')\|:
     (\mu,\Omega),(\mu',\Omega')\in G_j
     \text{ lie in one cell},\ \|\mu-\mu'\|\le r\bigr\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Compactness and continuity give \(\omega_j(r)\downarrow0\) as \(r\downarrow0\) for each fixed \(j\).

Set \(\delta_n=m^{-1/2}+\varepsilon_N\).  A diagonal choice using (1) and (4) gives deterministic integers \(j_n\uparrow\infty\) such that, with \(r_n=j_n\delta_n\),
\[
 r_n\to0,
 \qquad \frac{j_n}{\sqrt n}\to0,
 \qquad
 \sqrt{j_n}\,\omega_{j_n}(4r_n)\to0. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, for each fixed \(j\), \(j\delta_n\to0\) and \(\omega_j(4j\delta_n)\to0\); holding \(j_n=j\) until all three displayed requirements are at most \(j^{-1}\), and then increasing \(j\), proves existence.  This is the calibrated-sieve step: it uses only deterministic continuity moduli and does not assert that the global containment problem is algorithmically available.

Let \(\bar h^{\mathrm p}\) and \(\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm p}\) be the pilot mean and covariance and put \(\widehat\eta^{\mathrm p}=(\bar h^{\mathrm p},\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm p})\).  The pilot selects the safe procedure precisely when the closed ball
\[
 \overline B_*(\widehat\eta^{\mathrm p},3r_n)
\]
is contained in a single strict-cell component of \(G_{j_n}\); otherwise it selects inversion.  This is a measurable rule after a fixed deterministic tie-breaking convention, because there are finitely many cells and each \(G_j\) is closed.

We next control pilot error.  The covariance eigenvalue upper bound in \texttt{ass:primitive-covariance} and the centered \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound in \texttt{ass:primitive-moment-envelope} imply constants \(C_1,C_2<\infty\), independent of the admissible row law, such that
\[
 E\|\bar h^{\mathrm p}-\mu_n\|^2\le \frac{C_1}{m},
 \qquad
 E\|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm p}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le \frac{C_2}{m}. \tag{6}
\]
For the second inequality, expand each covariance entry as the average of centered products minus the product of centered sample means; the fourth moment bounds the squared centered products, and summing the finitely many entries gives (6).  Markov's inequality and \(r_n\ge j_nm^{-1/2}\) now yield
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\widehat\eta^{\mathrm p}-\eta_n\|_*>r_n\}
 \le \frac{C_3}{m r_n^2}
 \le \frac{C_3}{j_n^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \eta_n=(\mu_n,\Omega_n). \tag{7}
\]
This is also the quantitative covariance-consistency step underlying \texttt{lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky}.

Suppose the pilot selects the safe procedure and the event complementary to that in (7) occurs.  Then \(\eta_n\in G_{j_n}\), and the ball of radius \(2r_n\) about \(\eta_n\) stays in the selected strict cell.  In particular, (3) gives
\[
 \Delta(\eta_n)\ge j_n^{-1}. \tag{8}
\]
If, for a fixed \(C<\infty\), any named margin is at most \(Cn^{-1/2}\), then \(\Delta(\eta_n)\le Cn^{-1/2}<j_n^{-1}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where the last inequality is (5).  Thus safe selection is impossible on the high-probability event in (7).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{(P_n):\,\Delta(\eta_n)\le Cn^{-1/2}}
 P_n\{\text{safe selection}\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{9}
\]
This proves the stated uniform fallback assertion, including exact zeros and ties.

It remains to prove coverage on the safe branch.  Let \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\), \(\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm i}\), and \(\widetilde\Omega^{\mathrm i}=\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm i}+\lambda_NI_p\) be the inference-subsample quantities.  Applying the same bounds as in (6) with sample size \(N\), and using \(N/m\to\infty\), gives
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n\|>r_n\}=o(1),
 \qquad
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm i}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}>r_n\}=o(1). \tag{10}
\]
Moreover, \(\varepsilon_Nle r_n/j_n<r_n\).  Hence, on safe selection and the events in (7)--(10), \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\), every segment from \(\mu_n\) to \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\), and every numerical-difference segment from \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\) to \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}+\varepsilon_Ne_k\) stay in the same strict cell.

Write \(g_{r,n}=g_r(\mu_n)\), \(v_{r,n}=g_{r,n}^\top\Omega_ng_{r,n}\), and let \(\widehat g_{r,n}\) be row \(r\) of the numerical Jacobian computed on the inference subsample.  The mean-value formula and (4) give, for \(r=1,2\),
\[
 \psi_{q,r}(\bar h^{\mathrm i})-\psi_{q,r}(\mu_n)
 =g_{r,n}^\top(\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n)+R_{r,n},
 \qquad
 |R_{r,n}|\le \omega_{j_n}(4r_n)\|\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n\|, \tag{11}
\]
and, with a constant depending only on the fixed dimension \(p\),
\[
 \|\widehat g_{r,n}-g_{r,n}\|
 \le \sqrt p\,\omega_{j_n}(4r_n). \tag{12}
\]
Because (3) gives \(v_{r,n}\ge j_n^{-1}\), equations (5), (10), and (11) imply
\[
 \frac{\sqrt N\,R_{r,n}}{\sqrt{v_{r,n}}}=o_P(1). \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, standardization is uniform.  Put \(a_{r,n}=g_{r,n}/\sqrt{v_{r,n}}\).  The lower covariance eigenvalue in \texttt{ass:primitive-covariance} gives \(\|a_{r,n}\|\le c^{-1/2}\).  Thus the moment envelope implies, for some \(\kappa>0\),
\[
 \sup_{n,r,(P_n)}E_{P_n}|a_{r,n}^\top\{h(O_{n1})-\mu_n\}|^{2+\kappa}<\infty,
\]
and the corresponding Lyapunov remainder is bounded by a constant times \(N^{-\kappa/2}\).  The fixed-dimensional triangular-array central-limit calculation used in \texttt{lem:uniform-primitive-inversion} therefore yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt N\,g_{r,n}^\top(\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n)}{\sqrt{v_{r,n}}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1)
\]
uniformly over admissible arrays with \(\eta_n\in G_{j_n}\).  By (12), (5), (10), \(\lambda_N\to0\), and the eigenvalue bounds,
\[
 \frac{\widehat g_{r,n}\widetilde\Omega^{\mathrm i}\widehat g_{r,n}^\top}{v_{r,n}}
 \to_P1 \tag{14}
\]
uniformly on the same arrays.  Equations (11)--(14) prove the uniformly studentized numerical-delta approximation.

Let \(z_{1-\alpha/2}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\), let \(L_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\) be the lower outer endpoint for coordinate one, and let \(U_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\) be the upper outer endpoint for coordinate two, all computed on the inference subsample.  The preceding approximation gives
\[
 \sup P_n\{L_n^{\mathrm{ND}}>\underline\psi_q(\mu_n)\}
 \le \alpha/2+o(1),
 \qquad
 \sup P_n\{U_n^{\mathrm{ND}}<\overline\psi_q(\mu_n)\}
 \le \alpha/2+o(1), \tag{15}
\]
where each supremum is over admissible arrays with \(\eta_n\in G_{j_n}\).  The union bound then shows that the slab
\[
 \mathsf H_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
 =\{\theta\in\mathbb R^K:L_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\le q^\top\theta\le U_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\}
\]
contains \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) on the safe branch: by the definition of the support endpoints, every \(\theta\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\) has \(q^\top\theta\) between them.  If the pilot selects inversion, \texttt{lem:uniform-primitive-inversion}, applied to the independent inference subsample of size \(N\), gives the same lower coverage bound for its version of \(\mathcal C_N^{\mathrm{inv}}\).  Conditional on the pilot, the inference sample is independent and has the row law \(P_n\).  Combining the two conditional bounds and then using (7) proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)}
 P_n\{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\subseteq\mathsf H_n\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{16}
\]

Finally, fix a law \(P_0\) satisfying the strict-cell conditions and the two positive-variance conditions.  Then \(\eta_0=(\mu_0,\Omega_0)\) is in the interior of some \(G_J\).  Since \(j_n\to\infty\), \(r_n\to0\), and the pilot is consistent by (7), the pilot ball is eventually contained in that cell with probability tending to one.  Thus safe selection has probability tending to one.  By \texttt{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery}, used with sample size \(N\) and (1), the numerical endpoints differ from their inference-subsample analytic-Jacobian oracle endpoints by \(o_{P_0}(N^{-1/2})\).

It remains only to compare the inference-subsample oracle with the full-sample oracle.  If \(\bar h^{\mathrm f}\) is the full-sample mean, then
\[
 \bar h^{\mathrm i}-\bar h^{\mathrm f}
 =\frac{m}{n}(\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\bar h^{\mathrm p})
 =O_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt m}{n}\right)
 =o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2}). \tag{17}
\]
Continuous differentiability on the fixed cell transfers (17) to the two endpoint centers.  Both inference and full-sample covariance estimators converge to \(\Omega_0\) by \texttt{lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky}; continuity of \(J_q\), positivity of the endpoint variances, and
\[
 N^{-1/2}-n^{-1/2}=o(n^{-1/2})
\]
therefore show that the corresponding oracle standard errors differ by \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\).  Combining this fact, (17), and the inference-subsample oracle comparison proves the asserted \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\) recovery of both full-sample oracle outer endpoints.
