\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}hetset\_\allowbreak{}sign\_\allowbreak{}ratio\_\allowbreak{}closure}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Positive bounded ratios of omitted-variable biases force one globally compatible, common-sign bias vector on each connected setting component, with the all-zero vector as a closure face.  The resulting affine product of two-polyhedron unions is the exact causal effect set on the published Huntington--Klein specialization \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\), whereas the published pairwise bias-difference polytope is generally only an outer set.  Algebraic containment continues to hold for the project-maintained generalized positive-ratio class.  Converse attainment now uses the unified construction \(A_i^B=U_i+B_iD_i\), which preserves the entire observed joint margin and proves pointwise sharpness whenever each conditional pair \((D_i,U_i)\mid X\) has an everywhere-positive density and finite second moments; nonsingular conditional-Gaussian laws are an immediate corollary.  The paper also supplies log-cycle and Farkas certificates, exact branch-linear-program and zero-safe mixed-integer computation, strict support and requested-pin gaps, and a solved three-setting cycle.  For estimated supershort calibration, undivided primitive-moment inversion is uniformly valid at exact zero, \(n^{-1/2}\)-weak denominators, and branch or basis ties.  A calibrated-sieve split-sample handle falls back to that inversion near singularities and selects numerical-delta inference on certified safe cells, giving uniform coverage and pointwise first-order recovery of the known-cell oracle at every fixed strict regular law.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} HuntingtonKlein2026, Assumption 3 on pp. 8--9, imposes positive cross-setting bias ratios with intervals containing one, while Proposition 4 on p. 13 reports a polytope built from separately extremized bias differences and Definition 3 on p. 15 pins that outer set. The paper does not prove equality or converse attainment, and Assumption 4 on pp. 17--18 uses divided supershort ratios without sampling inference at weak denominators.

\paragraph{Niche.} Applied researchers need the exact joint set generated by one latent bias vector, certificates that distinguish genuine incompatibility from a numerical artifact, exact answers after several effects are pinned, and confidence sets that remain valid when the data do not identify a supershort ratio.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project supplies an if-and-only-if componentwise sign union, separates the generalized positive-ratio class from the published \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) specialization, and proves sharpness through a joint-margin-preserving zero-safe completion under conditional positive density, with the Gaussian family as a corollary.  It adds exact primal--dual computation including fixed-zero strata, analytic support and pin gaps from the published outer polytope, and a solved three-setting cycle.  For the undivided supershort sensitivity region, primitive inversion gives uniform singularity-robust coverage, while a calibrated-sieve split-sample rule combines this fallback with numerical-delta intervals on safe regular cells and achieves pointwise first-order oracle recovery.

\section{Related work}

HuntingtonKlein2026 is the theorem-level identification comparator.  Its Assumption 3 on pp. 8--9 imposes bounded positive bias ratios with \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\), and Proposition 4 on p. 13 gives membership in a pairwise bias-difference polytope.  The sharp-closure theorem proves exact componentwise sign closure and converse attainment for that published specialization under conditional positive density, while retaining universal containment for the project-maintained generalization in which intervals need not contain one. \section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:partially-linear-model]\label{ass:partially-linear-model}
For every \(i\in V\), the full law \(P\) admits \(Y_i=\theta_i(P)D_i+f_i(X_i,A_i)+\varepsilon_i\) with \(E_P[\varepsilon_i\mid D_i,X_i,A_i]=0\) and a positive residual variance for \(D_i\) after projection on \((X_i,A_i)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Partially linear OVB model, \cite{HuntingtonKlein2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observational-equivalence]\label{ass:observational-equivalence}
The observed margin of \(P\) equals \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Observed-margin compatibility, \cite{BalkePearl1997}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bias-box]\label{ass:bias-box}
For every \(i\in V\), \(B_i(P)\in[\nu^-_i,\nu^+_i]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Setting-specific bounded OVB, \cite{HuntingtonKlein2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-ratio]\label{ass:positive-ratio}
For every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), \(0<\ell_{jk}\le u_{jk}\) and there exists \(\rho_{jk}(P)\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) such that \(B_j(P)=\rho_{jk}(P)B_k(P)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Positive proportional biases with pair-specific intervals that need not contain one accommodate calibrated settings whose relative confounding direction is common but whose magnitudes are known to be ordered, as when administrative sites share an omitted risk index and external validation bounds one site's loading above another's.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-observed-submodel]\label{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}
Under \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), for every \(i\in V\) and for \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\)-almost every \(x\), the conditional law of \((D_i,U_i)\) given \(X=x\) admits an everywhere-strictly-positive Lebesgue density on \(\mathbb R^2\), and \(E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[D_i^2+U_i^2]<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Everywhere-positive conditional-density submodel, \cite{Wooldridge2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-triangular-sampling]\label{ass:iid-triangular-sampling}
For each \(n\), \(O_{n1},\ldots,O_{nn}\) are independent and identically distributed under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Row-wise i.i.d. triangular-array sampling, \cite{KaidoMolinariStoye2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:primitive-moment-envelope]\label{ass:primitive-moment-envelope}
There exist fixed \(\delta>0\) and \(C<\infty\) such that \(\sup_n E_{P_n}[\|h(O_{n1})-\mu_n\|^{4+\delta}]\le C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform Lyapunov moment condition, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:primitive-covariance]\label{ass:primitive-covariance}
There exist fixed \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that every eigenvalue of \(\Omega_n\) lies in \([c,C]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform nonsingular covariance, \cite{KaidoMolinariStoye2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:regular-primitive-map]\label{ass:regular-primitive-map}
The map \(\Gamma\) is continuously differentiable with uniformly bounded derivative on a fixed neighborhood of \(\{\mu_n:n\ge1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Regular finite-dimensional moment transformation away from regression singularity, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:component-sign-decomposition]\label{lem:component-sign-decomposition}
A vector \(b\in\prod_{i\in V}[\nu^-_i,\nu^+_i]\) admits edge witnesses \(r_{jk}\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) with \(b_j=r_{jk}b_k\) for all \((j,k)\in\vec E\) if and only if \(b\in\mathcal B_G\). In a connected component, either every coordinate is zero or every nonzero coordinate has one common sign; sign choices are independent across distinct components.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(E_c=\vec E\cap(c\times c)\).  First suppose that the edge witnesses exist.  By \texttt{ass:bias-box}, \(b_i\in[\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+]\) for every \(i\).  By \texttt{ass:positive-ratio}, for every \((j,k)\in E_c\),
\[
 b_j=r_{jk}b_k,\qquad 0<\ell_{jk}\le r_{jk}\le u_{jk}.
\]
Thus \(b_k=0\) if and only if \(b_j=0\), and, if they are nonzero, \(\operatorname{sgn}(b_j)=\operatorname{sgn}(b_k)\).  Connectivity propagates both conclusions along every undirected path in \(c\).  Hence either \(b_c=0\), or all coordinates of \(b_c\) are nonzero and have a common sign.  In the positive case multiplication of \(\ell_{jk}\le r_{jk}\le u_{jk}\) by \(b_k>0\) gives
\[
 \ell_{jk}b_k\le b_j\le u_{jk}b_k,
\]
so \(b_c\in\mathcal P_c^+\) by \texttt{def:positive-branch}.  In the negative case multiplication by \(b_k<0\) reverses the inequalities and gives
\[
 u_{jk}b_k\le b_j\le\ell_{jk}b_k,
\]
so \(b_c\in\mathcal P_c^-\) by \texttt{def:negative-branch}.  The all-zero vector belongs to either branch precisely when the componentwise boxes contain zero.  Applying this argument independently to all components and using \texttt{def:sharp-bias-set} yields \(b\in\mathcal B_G\).

Conversely, suppose \(b\in\mathcal B_G\).  Fix \(c\).  If \(b_c\in\mathcal P_c^+\), the edge inequalities and \(\ell_{jk}>0\) imply that a zero at either endpoint of an edge forces a zero at the other endpoint.  Thus connectivity implies either \(b_c=0\), or \(b_i>0\) for every \(i\in c\).  In the latter case define
\[
 r_{jk}=\frac{b_j}{b_k}\quad((j,k)\in E_c).
\]
Division is valid because \(b_k>0\), and the defining inequalities for \(\mathcal P_c^+\) give \(r_{jk}\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\).  If \(b_c=0\), choose any \(r_{jk}\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\), which is possible because the interval is nonempty by \texttt{ass:positive-ratio}.  The proof for \(b_c\in\mathcal P_c^-\) is identical after noting that division by \(b_k<0\) reverses both displayed inequalities.  The witnesses can be chosen component by component because no edge joins distinct components.  This proves both directions, including the closure point \(b_c=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:gaussian-completion-membership]\label{prop:gaussian-completion-membership}
Suppose the conditional-positive-density condition \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\) holds.  For every \(B\in\mathcal B_G\), the law \(P^B\) from \(\texttt{def:gaussian-completion}\) has observed margin exactly \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), including the full cross-setting joint law, satisfies the partially linear model, has \(B(P^B)=B\), belongs to \(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and yields \(\theta(P^B)=s-B\).  For every \(i\in V\),
\[
A_i^B=U_i+B_iD_i,
\qquad
(s_i-B_i)D_i+A_i^B=s_iD_i+U_i=Y_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{P^B}(D_i\mid X,A_i^B)>0
\]
almost surely.  If \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) for every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), then also \(P^B\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(B\in\mathcal B_G\).  By \(\texttt{def:gaussian-completion}\), \(P^B\) is formed by adjoining \(A^B\) to one unchanged draw from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Hence the marginal law of every observed coordinate \((X,D,Y)\), and therefore their entire cross-setting joint distribution, is exactly \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  This proves \(\texttt{ass:observational-equivalence}\) and shows that the population short-regression coefficient remains \(s\).

We next verify the support condition needed by \(\texttt{ass:partially-linear-model}\).  Fix \(i\in V\).  By \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\), for almost every \(x\) the conditional law of \((D_i,U_i)\) given \(X=x\) has a density \(g_{i,x}(d,u)>0\) for every \((d,u)\in\mathbb R^2\).  For the fixed scalar \(B_i\), the linear map
\[
T_{B_i}(d,u)=(d,a)=(d,u+B_id)
\]
is bijective, has inverse \(T_{B_i}^{-1}(d,a)=(d,a-B_id)\), and has Jacobian determinant one.  The change-of-variables formula therefore gives the conditional density
\[
h_{i,x}^B(d,a)=g_{i,x}(d,a-B_id)>0
\]
of \((D_i,A_i^B)\) given \(X=x\).  Its \(A_i^B\)-marginal density
\[
m_{i,x}^B(a)=\int_{\mathbb R}h_{i,x}^B(d,a)\,\mathrm d d
\]
is strictly positive and finite for almost every \(a\) under that marginal.  At every such \((x,a)\), the conditional density of \(D_i\) is \(h_{i,x}^B(d,a)/m_{i,x}^B(a)\), which is positive for every \(d\in\mathbb R\).  It consequently assigns positive probability to each of two disjoint bounded intervals and cannot be concentrated at one point.  The finite-second-moment part of \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\), together with iterated expectation, makes its conditional second moment finite almost surely.  Thus
\[
0<\operatorname{Var}_{P^B}(D_i\mid X,A_i^B)<\infty
\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(X_i\) is contained in \(X\), the conditional variance decomposition applied after coarsening from \((X,A_i^B)\) to \((X_i,A_i^B)\) shows that the residual variance after projection on \((X_i,A_i^B)\) is positive as well.

The construction is zero-safe: for every real \(B_i\), including \(B_i=0\),
\[
(s_i-B_i)D_i+f_i^B(A_i^B)+\varepsilon_i^B
=(s_i-B_i)D_i+(U_i+B_iD_i)+0
=s_iD_i+U_i=Y_i. \tag{2}
\]
Adding back the observed-control projection gives the corresponding equality before residualization.  Since \(\varepsilon_i^B=0\),
\[
E_{P^B}[\varepsilon_i^B\mid D_i,X_i,A_i^B]=0. \tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) verify \(\texttt{ass:partially-linear-model}\).  They also make its coefficient unique: if \(R_i=D_i-E_{P^B}[D_i\mid X_i,A_i^B]\), then \(E[R_iD_i]=E[R_i^2]>0\), while \(E[R_if_i^B(X_i,A_i^B)]=0\); multiplying (2) by \(R_i\) and taking expectations therefore yields
\[
\theta_i(P^B)=\frac{E[R_iY_i]}{E[R_iD_i]}=s_i-B_i. \tag{4}
\]
Because the observed short coefficient remains \(s_i\), the defining identity \(B(P)=s-\theta(P)\) and (4) imply
\[
B_i(P^B)=s_i-\theta_i(P^B)=B_i. \tag{5}
\]

Membership \(B\in\mathcal B_G\), together with \(\texttt{lem:component-sign-decomposition}\), supplies both the coordinatewise bias boxes and witnesses \(\rho_{jk}(P^B)\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) satisfying \(B_j(P^B)=\rho_{jk}(P^B)B_k(P^B)\).  Thus \(\texttt{ass:bias-box}\) and \(\texttt{ass:positive-ratio}\) hold.  The conditional-positive-density property is a property of the unchanged observed margin.  The member-property definitions \(\texttt{def:gaussian-completion-class}\) and \(\texttt{def:hk-model-class}\) now give
\[
P^B\in\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\qquad \theta(P^B)=s-B. \tag{6}
\]
Finally, if \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) on every edge, \(\texttt{def:published-hk-model-class}\) gives \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The last assertion follows from (6).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-sign-ratio-closure]\label{thm:sharp-sign-ratio-closure}
For every observed law and every generalized positive-ratio envelope, \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\).  Under the conditional-positive-density condition \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\),
\[
\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=s-\mathcal B_G.
\]
If additionally \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) for every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), then \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), \(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and
\[
\{\theta(P):P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
=\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=s-\mathcal B_G.
\]
Hence the affine componentwise sign union is pointwise sharp for the published Huntington--Klein specialization throughout the stated conditional-positive-density observed-law family, while the first two containments remain valid for the generalized class.  The former nonsingular jointly Gaussian family is an immediate subfamily, and the construction restricts no cross-setting dependence in the observed joint margin.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The member properties in \(\texttt{def:hk-model-class}\) imply \(B_i(P)\in[\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+]\) for every \(i\in V\) and, for every \((j,k)\in\vec E\), the existence of \(\rho_{jk}(P)\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) satisfying
\[
B_j(P)=\rho_{jk}(P)B_k(P). \tag{1}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:component-sign-decomposition}\), these conditions are equivalent to \(B(P)\in\mathcal B_G\).  Since \(B(P)=s-\theta(P)\),
\[
\theta(P)=s-B(P)\in s-\mathcal B_G. \tag{2}
\]
Taking the image over all compatible \(P\) in \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\) proves
\[
\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq s-\mathcal B_G. \tag{3}
\]
This is an outer-bound statement and uses neither the positive-density condition nor the published-envelope specialization.

For the second containment, fix \(b\in\mathcal B_G\) and put \(\vartheta=s-b\).  The box restrictions in \(\texttt{def:positive-branch}\) and \(\texttt{def:negative-branch}\) give
\[
s_i-\nu_i^+\le \vartheta_i\le s_i-\nu_i^-\qquad(i\in V). \tag{4}
\]
For an edge \((j,k)\), \(\texttt{lem:component-sign-decomposition}\) supplies \(r_{jk}\in[\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\) with \(b_j=r_{jk}b_k\), so
\[
b_j-b_k=(r_{jk}-1)b_k. \tag{5}
\]
The function \((r,v)\mapsto(r-1)v\) is affine in either argument when the other is fixed.  Successively expressing \(r\) and \(v\) as convex combinations of their interval endpoints writes every value on \([\ell_{jk},u_{jk}]\times[\nu_k^-,\nu_k^+]\) as a convex combination of its four corner values.  Its minimum and maximum are therefore the corner extrema \(c_{jk}^-\) and \(c_{jk}^+\) in \(\texttt{def:hk-outer-polytope}\).  Applying this to (5) yields
\[
c_{jk}^-\le b_j-b_k\le c_{jk}^+. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(\vartheta_j-\vartheta_k=(s_j-s_k)-(b_j-b_k)\), equations (4) and (6) are exactly the defining inequalities of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\).  Hence
\[
s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}. \tag{7}
\]
This is again a universal algebraic containment over the generalized class; it need not be sharp for the outer polytope.

Now impose \(\texttt{ass:gaussian-observed-submodel}\), whose replaced content is the conditional-positive-density condition.  For every \(b\in\mathcal B_G\), \(\texttt{prop:gaussian-completion-membership}\) constructs a law \(P^b\in\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfying \(\theta(P^b)=s-b\).  Consequently
\[
s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}}). \tag{8}
\]
Combining (3) and (8) proves equality.  Moreover, (8) proves sharpness rather than merely validity: every point of the displayed affine union is attained by an observationally equivalent full-data law.

Suppose next that \(0<\ell_{jk}\le1\le u_{jk}\) for every edge.  By \(\texttt{def:published-hk-model-class}\),
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}}). \tag{9}
\]
The proposition and (9) give \(\mathcal M_G(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and substituting (9) into \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\) gives
\[
\{\theta(P):P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{HK}}^{\mathrm{pub}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
=\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=s-\mathcal B_G. \tag{10}
\]
Thus (10) is the exact published-class transfer, while (3) and (7) retain the more general envelope scope.

Finally, a nonsingular conditional bivariate Gaussian law for \((D_i,U_i)\) has density
\[
(2\pi)^{-1}\det(\Sigma_i)^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left\{-\tfrac12(w-m_i)^\top\Sigma_i^{-1}(w-m_i)\right\}>0
\]
at every \(w\in\mathbb R^2\), and has finite second moments.  It therefore satisfies the conditional-positive-density condition, proving the Gaussian corollary.  Because every construction above leaves the entire observed vector untouched, no cross-setting covariance or other observed dependence is altered or restricted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:cycle-farkas-and-computation]\label{thm:cycle-farkas-and-computation}
For every component \(c\) and sign \(\zeta\), the nonzero branch \(\mathcal P_c^{\zeta}\) is nonempty if and only if the directed difference system \(\mathcal D_c^{\zeta}\) has no negative-weight directed cycle, equivalently if and only if no nonnegative Farkas circulation \(y\) satisfies \(y^\top A=0\) and \(y^\top b<0\). Together with the separately tested all-zero stratum, these certificates are closure-complete. For every \((q,H,t)\), exhaustive branch LP and def:exact-milp have exactly the feasible set \(\Theta_I(H,t)\) and identical attained extrema, including equality cycles and optimizer ties. If \(M_i=0\), the box and sign constraints force \(b_i=0\), and every active positive-ratio edge incident to \(i\) propagates zero throughout its connected component. In the exact rational check \(K=2\), \([\nu^-_1,\nu^+_1]=\{0\}\), \([\nu^-_2,\nu^+_2]=[-1,1]\), and \([\ell_{12},u_{12}]=[1/2,2]\), both the branch-LP bias projection and the MILP bias projection equal \(\{(0,0)\}\), with \(M_1=0\), \(M_2=1\), and \(M_{12}=3\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a connected component \(c\in\mathcal C_G\) and a sign \(\zeta\in\{+,-\}\).  We first use the difference-constraint technique on the all-nonzero stratum
\[
\mathcal P_{c,\ne0}^{\zeta}:=\mathcal P_c^{\zeta}\setminus\{0_c\}.
\]
For the positive and negative signs, respectively, define the admissible magnitude intervals
\[
 I_i^+=(0,\infty)\cap[\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+],
 \qquad
 I_i^-=\{-v:v\in(-\infty,0)\cap[\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+]\}.
\]
The declared strict positivity \(0<\ell_{jk}\le u_{jk}\), together with connectivity, implies that a vector in \(\mathcal P_c^{\zeta}\) which has one zero coordinate has every coordinate zero.  Consequently, every vector in \(\mathcal P_{c,\ne0}^{\zeta}\) has \(|b_i|>0\) for every \(i\in c\).  For such a vector put \(x_i=\log|b_i|\).  Both \(\texttt{def:positive-branch}\) and \(\texttt{def:negative-branch}\), after replacing a negative coordinate by its magnitude, give
\[
 \ell_{jk}|b_k|\le |b_j|\le u_{jk}|b_k|.
\]
Since the logarithm is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), this is equivalent to
\[
 x_j-x_k\le\log u_{jk},
 \qquad
 x_k-x_j\le-\log\ell_{jk}.
\tag{1}
\]

We now make the box part of \(\texttt{def:log-difference-system}\) explicit.  Adjoin an anchor vertex \(0\).  If \(I_i^{\zeta}=\varnothing\), insert the inconsistent constraint \(x_i-x_i\le-1\), which is a negative self-cycle.  Otherwise \(\log I_i^{\zeta}\) is an interval.  Every finite upper endpoint \(\overline\gamma_i\) contributes \(x_i-x_0\le\overline\gamma_i\), every finite lower endpoint \(\underline\gamma_i\) contributes \(x_0-x_i\le-\underline\gamma_i\), and an endpoint equal to \(-\infty\) contributes no inequality.  Because all original bias-box endpoints are finite, no upper endpoint is infinite.  Translation of all \(x\)-coordinates permits the normalization \(x_0=0\).  Thus \(\mathcal D_c^{\zeta}\) has the form
\[
 x_v-x_u\le d_{uv},\qquad (u,v)\in E_{c,\zeta}^{\mathrm{aug}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Exponentiation is a bijection between the solutions of (2), with \(x_0=0\), and \(\mathcal P_{c,\ne0}^{\zeta}\).  In particular, the box boundaries, including a box endpoint equal to zero, are represented without division.

We next prove the negative-cycle alternative directly.  If (2) is feasible, summing its inequalities along a directed cycle gives \(0\le\sum d_{uv}\); hence no directed cycle can have negative weight.  Conversely, suppose there is no negative directed cycle.  Add a super-source \(s_*\), with a zero-weight arc from \(s_*\) to every old vertex and no arc entering \(s_*\).  This adds no cycle.  Let \(d(v)\) be the minimum weight of a directed walk from \(s_*\) to \(v\).  The minimum exists: whenever a walk repeats a vertex, the intervening directed cycle has nonnegative weight and may be removed without increasing the walk weight, so it is enough to minimize over the finitely many simple walks.  Appending an arc \((u,v)\) to a minimizing walk for \(u\) yields
\[
 d(v)\le d(u)+d_{uv}.
\]
Therefore \(x_v=d(v)-d(0)\) satisfies every inequality in (2) and has \(x_0=0\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal P_{c,\ne0}^{\zeta}\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal D_c^{\zeta}\text{ has no negative-weight directed cycle}.
\tag{3}
\]

For the Farkas-circulation form, write (2) as \(A x\le d\), where the row belonging to \((u,v)\) is \(e_v^{\top}-e_u^{\top}\).  A vector \(y\ge0\) with \(y^{\top}A=0\) is exactly a nonnegative circulation on the augmented constraint graph.  The incidence vector of a negative directed cycle is such a circulation and satisfies \(y^{\top}d<0\).  Conversely, every nonzero nonnegative circulation is a nonnegative sum of directed-cycle incidence vectors: follow positive-flow arcs until a vertex repeats, subtract the smallest flow on that cycle, and iterate; at least one support arc disappears at every iteration, so the procedure terminates.  If \(y^{\top}d<0\), at least one cycle in that decomposition has negative weight.  Thus
\[
 \mathcal D_c^{\zeta}\text{ is feasible}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nexists y\ge0:\ y^{\top}A=0\text{ and }y^{\top}d<0.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the asserted Farkas certificate, with \(d\) denoting the right-hand-side vector called \(b\) in the theorem statement.

It remains to close the boundary.  The vector \(0_c\) satisfies all ratio and sign inequalities, and it satisfies the bias box if and only if
\[
 0\in[\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+]\qquad\text{for every }i\in c.
\tag{5}
\]
As observed above, strict positivity of every ratio lower bound rules out a partially zero vector in a connected component.  Hence (3)--(5) test all of \(\mathcal P_c^{\zeta}\), including its closure point, and are closure-complete.

We turn to exact computation, using finite disjunctive programming.  For a sign map \(\zeta:\mathcal C_G\to\{+,-\}\), define
\[
 F_{\zeta}(H,t)
 =\{b\in\mathbb R^K:b_c\in\mathcal P_c^{\zeta(c)}\ \forall c\in\mathcal C_G,\ H(s-b)=t\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:sharp-bias-set}\) and \(\texttt{def:pinned-sharp-set}\),
\[
 \Theta_I(H,t)
 =\bigcup_{\zeta:\mathcal C_G\to\{+,-\}}
   \{s-b:b\in F_{\zeta}(H,t)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
There are \(2^{|\mathcal C_G|}\) sign maps.  Each \(F_{\zeta}(H,t)\) is a closed subset of the bounded bias box and hence is compact.  Thus the linear objective \(q^{\top}(s-b)\) attains its minimum and maximum on every nonempty branch.  Taking the extrema over the finite union (6) proves the exactness of \(\texttt{def:branch-lp}\), including equality cycles, branch ties, and optimizer ties.

We finally verify the feasible projection of \(\texttt{def:exact-milp}\) equation by equation.  The box implies \(|b_i|\le M_i\).  Fix an edge \((j,k)\) in component \(c\).  If \(z_c=1\), the sign constraints imply \(0\le b_i\le M_i\), and the first edge pair reduces exactly to
\[
 \ell_{jk}b_k\le b_j\le u_{jk}b_k.
\tag{7}
\]
The second edge pair is redundant, because
\[
 u_{jk}b_k-b_j\le u_{jk}M_k\le M_{jk},
 \qquad
 b_j-\ell_{jk}b_k\le M_j\le M_{jk}.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(z_c=0\), the sign constraints imply \(-M_i\le b_i\le0\), and the second edge pair reduces exactly to
\[
 u_{jk}b_k\le b_j\le\ell_{jk}b_k.
\tag{9}
\]
The first edge pair is redundant, because
\[
 \ell_{jk}b_k-b_j\le M_j\le M_{jk},
 \qquad
 b_j-u_{jk}b_k\le u_{jk}M_k\le M_{jk}.
\tag{10}
\]
The last inequalities in (8) and (10) use only
\[
 M_{jk}=(1+u_{jk})\max\{M_j,M_k\},
 \qquad u_{jk}>0.
\]
Conversely, a point in either branch satisfies its active edge pair and, by (8) or (10), the inactive relaxed pair.  Therefore the MILP bias projection is exactly \(\mathcal B_G\).  Adding \(H(s-b)=t\) gives exactly the bias preimage in (6), so the MILP and exhaustive branch LPs have the same feasible effect set and the same attained extrema.

If \(M_i=0\), then \(\nu_i^- =\nu_i^+=0\), and the box forces \(b_i=0\).  Equations (7) or (9), according to the active sign, force the other endpoint of every incident edge to zero; connectivity propagates zero throughout the component.  In the stated rational check,
\[
 M_1=0,
 \qquad M_2=1,
 \qquad M_{12}=(1+2)\max\{0,1\}=3.
\]
The box forces \(b_1=0\), and the sole positive-ratio edge then forces \(b_2=0\).  Hence both exact formulations project to \(\{(0,0)\}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-support-relaxation-gap]\label{thm:strict-support-relaxation-gap}
Let \(K=2\), \(\nu^-_1=\nu^-_2=-a\), \(\nu^+_1=\nu^+_2=a\), \(\ell_{12}=1/R\), and \(u_{12}=R\), with \(a>0\) and \(R>1\). Then \(s-\mathcal B_G\subsetneq\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\) and the effect-difference support gap is \(\Delta(q_{12})=a\{\min(2,R-1)-(1-R^{-1})\}>0\). Hence strictness holds over the full nondegenerate parameter region \(a>0,R>1\), not at one tuned law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There is one connected component in the stated two-setting family.  Write \(\mathcal P^+=\mathcal P_{\{1,2\}}^+\).  By \(\texttt{def:positive-branch}\), this nonnegative branch consists of \(0\le b_1,b_2\le a\) satisfying
\[
 R^{-1}b_2\le b_1\le Rb_2.
\tag{11}
\]
By \(\texttt{def:negative-branch}\), negation maps its nonpositive branch bijectively to the same nonnegative branch.  Thus \(\mathcal B_G\) is centrally symmetric.  For fixed \(b_1\) in the nonnegative branch, (11) gives \(b_2\ge b_1/R\), and equality is feasible.  Consequently
\[
 \max_{b\in\mathcal P^+}(b_1-b_2)
 =\max_{0\le b_1\le a}b_1(1-R^{-1})
 =a(1-R^{-1}).
\tag{12}
\]
Interchanging the two coordinates preserves (11), so the same value is the maximum of \(b_2-b_1\) on the positive branch.  Negation and (12) therefore imply
\[
 \min_{b\in\mathcal B_G}(b_1-b_2)=-a(1-R^{-1}).
\]
Using the support-function technique and \(q_{12}=(1,-1)^{\top}\), we obtain
\[
 h_{s-\mathcal B_G}(q_{12})
 =(s_1-s_2)-\min_{b\in\mathcal B_G}(b_1-b_2)
 =(s_1-s_2)+a(1-R^{-1}).
\tag{13}
\]

We next compute the outer-polytope support from \(\texttt{def:hk-outer-polytope}\).  Since
\[
 R-1>1-R^{-1}>0,
\]
the four endpoint products \((r-1)v\), with \(r\in\{R^{-1},R\}\) and \(v\in\{-a,a\}\), have extrema
\[
 c_{12}^-=-a(R-1),
 \qquad
 c_{12}^+=a(R-1).
\tag{14}
\]
The two coordinate boxes permit an upward effect-difference displacement of at most \(2a\), while the difference inequality obtained from (14) permits at most \(a(R-1)\).  Every displacement between zero and \(2a\) is attained inside the coordinate box, for example by opposite half-displacements of the two coordinates.  Hence
\[
 h_{\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}}(q_{12})
 =(s_1-s_2)+a\min\{2,R-1\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Subtracting (13) from (15), as prescribed by \(\texttt{def:support-gap}\), gives
\[
 \Delta(q_{12})
 =a\{\min(2,R-1)-(1-R^{-1})\}.
\tag{16}
\]
This is strictly positive throughout the stated region.  If \(1<R\le3\), the expression in braces is
\[
 (R-1)-(1-R^{-1})=\frac{(R-1)^2}{R}>0;
\]
if \(R\ge3\), it is \(2-(1-R^{-1})=1+R^{-1}>0\).  The containment \(s-\mathcal B_G\subseteq\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\) follows from \(\texttt{thm:sharp-sign-ratio-closure}\).  The strict support inequality (16) shows that the two nonempty compact sets cannot be equal.  Thus the containment is strict for every \(a>0\) and \(R>1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-pin-slices]\label{thm:exact-pin-slices}
For arbitrary \((H,t)\), the sharp pinned set is exactly \(\Theta_I(H,t)\), its support endpoints are the extrema over the feasible branch LPs, and branchwise primal--dual alternatives classify each requested pin system as infeasible, redundant, or jointly binding. In the family of thm:strict-support-relaxation-gap with \(R=2\), pinning \(\theta_1=s_1-a\) yields the sharp slice \(\theta_2\in[s_2-a,s_2-a/2]\), whereas slicing the outer set as in HuntingtonKlein2026, Definition 3 on p. 15, yields \(\theta_2\in[s_2-a,s_2]\); the requested-pin upper-endpoint gap is exactly \(a/2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a sign map \(\zeta:\mathcal C_G\to\{+,-\}\), put
\[
 F_{\zeta}^0=\{b:b_c\in\mathcal P_c^{\zeta(c)}\ \forall c\in\mathcal C_G\},
 \qquad
 F_{\zeta}(H,t)=F_{\zeta}^0\cap\{b:H(s-b)=t\}.
\]
The finite-union identity proved in \(\texttt{thm:cycle-farkas-and-computation}\) is
\[
 \Theta_I(H,t)=
 \bigcup_{\zeta}\{s-b:b\in F_{\zeta}(H,t)\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Therefore minimizing or maximizing \(q^{\top}(s-b)\) over the nonempty branch linear programs and then taking the corresponding extremum over the finitely many branches gives exactly the two support endpoints of the pinned set.

We give an explicit primal--dual classification of the pins.  Write a branch as
\[
 F_{\zeta}^0=\{b:G_{\zeta}b\le g_{\zeta}\},
 \qquad h=Hs-t.
\]
Consider the cone
\[
 \mathcal K_{\zeta}
 =\{(G_{\zeta}b+r,Hb):b\in\mathbb R^K,\ r\in[0,\infty)^{m_{\zeta}}\}.
\tag{18}
\]
This cone is closed.  Indeed, it is the sum of the subspace
\(L=\{(G_{\zeta}b,Hb):b\in\mathbb R^K\}\) and the cone generated by the finitely many inequality-coordinate rays.  In the quotient by \(L\), that image is a finitely generated cone.  To see directly that such a cone is closed, represent each point using a linearly independent subset of its generators, eliminating a dependent generator while preserving nonnegative coefficients.  Along any convergent sequence, pass to a subsequence using the same one of the finitely many independent subsets; continuity of the inverse coordinate map makes the coefficients converge to nonnegative limits.  The quotient cone, and hence its inverse image \(\mathcal K_{\zeta}\), is closed.

The pinned branch is feasible exactly when \((g_{\zeta},h)\in\mathcal K_{\zeta}\).  If it is infeasible, let \(p\) be the Euclidean projection of \((g_{\zeta},h)\) onto the nonempty closed convex cone \(\mathcal K_{\zeta}\), and set \(w=p-(g_{\zeta},h)\).  Existence of \(p\) follows by restricting a minimizing sequence to a bounded ball and taking a convergent subsequence.  The projection inequality, first with arbitrary \(k\in\mathcal K_{\zeta}\) and then with \(k=0,2p\), gives
\[
 w^{\top}k\ge0\quad\forall k\in\mathcal K_{\zeta},
 \qquad
 w^{\top}(g_{\zeta},h)=-\|p-(g_{\zeta},h)\|^2<0.
\]
Writing \(w=(y,\lambda)\), varying \(b\) through both signs and \(r\) through the nonnegative coordinate rays in (18) yields the verifiable certificate
\[
 y\ge0,
 \qquad
 y^{\top}G_{\zeta}+\lambda^{\top}H=0,
 \qquad
 y^{\top}g_{\zeta}+\lambda^{\top}(Hs-t)<0.
\tag{19}
\]
Conversely, multiplying any feasible inequalities and equalities by the multipliers in (19) would give a nonnegative quantity strictly below zero.  Thus (19) is equivalent to branch infeasibility.

When a branch is nonempty, every linear optimization over it also has a primal--dual certificate.  To verify this without importing a further theorem, let \(b^*\) maximize \(c^{\top}b\) and let \(I\) index the inequalities active at \(b^*\).  If \(c\) were not in the cone generated by \(\{G_{\zeta,i}^{\top}:i\in I\}\), projection onto that closed finitely generated cone would give a vector \(d\) satisfying
\[
 G_{\zeta,i}d\le0\quad(i\in I),
 \qquad c^{\top}d>0.
\]
All inactive inequalities have positive slack, so \(b^*+\epsilon d\) would be feasible and improve the objective for sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\), a contradiction.  Hence there is \(y\ge0\), supported on \(I\), with
\[
 G_{\zeta}^{\top}y=c,
 \qquad
 g_{\zeta}^{\top}y=c^{\top}b^*.
\tag{20}
\]
Equation (20), together with weak duality, certifies optimality; minimization is obtained by replacing \(c\) with \(-c\).

Let \(\eta_r^{\top}\) denote row \(r\) of \(H\), and define
\[
 \delta_r(b)=\eta_r^{\top}(s-b)-t_r.
\]
If \(F_{\zeta}^0=\varnothing\) or the equality-constrained system is infeasible, (19) classifies the branch as infeasible.  Otherwise the pin system is redundant on the branch exactly when
\[
 \min_{b\in F_{\zeta}^0}\delta_r(b)
 =\max_{b\in F_{\zeta}^0}\delta_r(b)=0
 \qquad\text{for every row }r.
\tag{21}
\]
Compactness gives the primal optimizers, and (20) gives dual certificates for every extremum in (21).  If the pinned branch is feasible but (21) fails, it is a proper section of \(F_{\zeta}^0\), so the pins are jointly binding.  These three cases are exhaustive and computable branch by branch.

It remains to calculate the requested two-setting slice.  Put \(R=2\) and impose \(\theta_1=s_1-a\), which is equivalent to \(b_1=a\).  The nonpositive branch is impossible.  On the nonnegative branch the ratio and box restrictions are
\[
 \frac12 b_2\le b_1\le2b_2,
 \qquad 0\le b_2\le a.
\]
Substitution of \(b_1=a\) gives \(a/2\le b_2\le a\), with every value in that interval feasible.  Therefore the sharp slice is
\[
 \theta_2=s_2-b_2\in[s_2-a,s_2-a/2].
\tag{22}
\]
For \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{HK}}\), the four defining endpoint products with \(R=2\) have minimum \(-a\) and maximum \(a\), so its difference restriction is
\[
 (s_1-s_2)-a\le\theta_1-\theta_2\le(s_1-s_2)+a.
\]
Substituting \(\theta_1=s_1-a\) gives \(s_2-2a\le\theta_2\le s_2\).  Intersecting with the coordinate box \([s_2-a,s_2+a]\) yields \([s_2-a,s_2]\).  Comparing its upper endpoint with (22) gives the exact requested-pin gap \(a/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:undivided-supershort-equivalence]\label{lem:undivided-supershort-equivalence}
Fix \((j,k)\in\vec E\) and a branch with \(b_jb_k\ge0\). If \(d_j(\mu)d_k(\mu)>0\), then \(Q_{jk}(b,\mu)\le0\) holds if and only if either \(b_j=b_k=0\), or \(b_jb_k>0\) and \(b_j/b_k\) lies between the two positive reciprocal calibration ratios \(d_j(\mu)/d_k(\mu)\) and \(d_k(\mu)/d_j(\mu)\). If exactly one of \(d_j(\mu)\) and \(d_k(\mu)\) is zero, then \(Q_{jk}(b,\mu)=-b_jb_kd_m(\mu)^2\le0\), where \(m\) indexes the nonzero coordinate. If both are zero, then \(Q_{jk}(b,\mu)=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d_j=d_j(\mu)\) and \(d_k=d_k(\mu)\).  If \(d_jd_k>0\) and \(b_jb_k>0\), division by the positive number \(b_k^2d_jd_k\) gives
\[
 \frac{Q_{jk}(b,\mu)}{b_k^2d_jd_k}
 =\left(\frac{b_j}{b_k}-\frac{d_j}{d_k}\right)
  \left(\frac{b_j}{b_k}-\frac{d_k}{d_j}\right).
\]
The product of two real linear factors is nonpositive exactly between its two roots.  This proves the reciprocal-ratio assertion.  If \(b_j=b_k=0\), then \(Q_{jk}=0\).  If exactly one bias coordinate is zero while the other is not, then \(Q_{jk}=b_m^2d_jd_k>0\) for the nonzero bias coordinate \(m\); hence there are no omitted cases under \(b_jb_k\ge0\).

If \(d_k=0\) and \(d_j\ne0\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 Q_{jk}=(-b_kd_j)(b_jd_j)=-b_jb_kd_j^2\le0.
\]
If \(d_j=0\) and \(d_k\ne0\), it gives \(Q_{jk}=-b_jb_kd_k^2\le0\).  Both inequalities use the stated branch condition \(b_jb_k\ge0\).  Finally, if \(d_j=d_k=0\), both factors in the definition of \(Q_{jk}\) vanish.  These identities cover every zero-denominator case without division.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exhaustive zero-case identity gives a total polynomial replacement for divided calibration ratios on every component branch. \textit{Gap.} HuntingtonKlein2026, Assumption 4 on pp. 17--18, uses conditional divided supershort ratios; it does not state this project-maintained polynomial generalization or its exact zero-coordinate cases. \textit{Consumer.} The inference routine can evaluate every null at zero or local-to-zero coefficient changes without arbitrary clipping.

\begin{lemma}[lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky]\label{lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky}
For fixed \(p\), every deterministic \(\lambda_n>0\) with \(\lambda_n\downarrow0\), and every \(\varepsilon>0\), the admissible triangular arrays satisfy \(\sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\varepsilon\}\to0\), \(\widetilde\Omega_n\succ0\) on every sample, and \(\sup_{(P_n)}P_n\bigl(\bigl|n(\bar h_n-\mu_n)^\top\widetilde\Omega_n^{-1}(\bar h_n-\mu_n)-n(\bar h_n-\mu_n)^\top\Omega_n^{-1}(\bar h_n-\mu_n)\bigr|>\varepsilon\bigr)\to0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(X_{nr}=h(O_{nr})-\mu_n\) and \(\bar X_n=n^{-1}\sum_rX_{nr}\).  Then
\[
 \widehat\Omega_n=\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^nX_{nr}X_{nr}^\top-\bar X_n\bar X_n^\top.
\]
Because \(p\) is fixed and \(E\|X_{n1}\|^{4+\delta}\le C\), row-wise independence gives, uniformly over admissible arrays,
\[
 E\left\|\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^n
   \{X_{nr}X_{nr}^\top-\Omega_n\}\right\|_F^2
 \le \frac1n E\|X_{n1}X_{n1}^\top-\Omega_n\|_F^2
 \le\frac{C_1}{n},
\]
and
\[
 E\|\bar X_n\|^2=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Omega_n)}n\le\frac{pC}{n}.
\]
Markov's inequality and \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_F\) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\varepsilon\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(\widehat\Omega_n\) is positive semidefinite and \(\lambda_n>0\), \(\widetilde\Omega_n=\widehat\Omega_n+\lambda_nI_p\) is positive definite on every sample.

Put \(T_n=n\bar X_n^\top\Omega_n^{-1}\bar X_n\) and \(\widetilde T_n=n\bar X_n^\top\widetilde\Omega_n^{-1}\bar X_n\).  The covariance eigenvalue bound gives \(n\|\bar X_n\|^2=O_P(1)\) uniformly.  On the event \(\|\widetilde\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le c/2\), the smallest eigenvalue of \(\widetilde\Omega_n\) is at least \(c/2\), and the inverse identity yields
\[
 \|\widetilde\Omega_n^{-1}-\Omega_n^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{2}{c^2}\|\widetilde\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Covariance consistency and \(\lambda_n\to0\) make the right side \(o_P(1)\) uniformly.  Hence
\[
 |\widetilde T_n-T_n|
 \le n\|\bar X_n\|^2
 \|\widetilde\Omega_n^{-1}-\Omega_n^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =o_P(1)
\]
uniformly, which is the claimed regularized Slutsky equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Regularization makes inversion total while uniform covariance consistency guarantees that it does not alter the first-order pivotal quadratic form. \textit{Gap.} KaidoMolinariStoye2019 uses regular estimated covariance matrices in calibrated projection; it does not establish the finite-sample totalization and uniform Slutsky equivalence needed for this primitive inversion. \textit{Consumer.} The confidence-region implementation can return a defined answer even when a finite sample has collinear or duplicate primitive moments.

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-primitive-inversion]\label{lem:uniform-primitive-inversion}
For every sample, the deterministic event inclusion \(\{\mu_n\in\mathcal U_n\}\subseteq\{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\subseteq\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathcal U_n}\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu)\}\) holds by set inclusion, and the union on the right is \(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{inv}}\). Under ass:iid-triangular-sampling, ass:primitive-moment-envelope, and ass:primitive-covariance, for fixed \(p\) and \(K\), the fixed-dimensional triangular-array central limit theorem, uniform covariance consistency, and regularized Slutsky equivalence give \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)}P_n\{\mu_n\in\mathcal U_n\}\ge1-\alpha\), and therefore \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)}P_n\{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\subseteq\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{inv}}\}\ge1-\alpha\). This supporting coverage guarantee is uniform over exact-zero and \(n^{-1/2}\)-weak coordinates of \(d(\mu_n)\), component-sign ties, and branch or optimizer-basis ties; it requires no divided-ratio or endpoint-optimizer limit law.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The deterministic part is immediate from \texttt{def:inversion-confidence-set}: if \(\mu_n\in\mathcal U_n\), then the term indexed by \(\mu=\mu_n\) occurs in the union, and hence
\[
 \mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)
 \subseteq\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathcal U_n}\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu)
 =\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{inv}}.
\]

For probabilistic coverage, set \(X_{nr}=\Omega_n^{-1/2}\{h(O_{nr})-\mu_n\}\).  Then \(E X_{n1}=0\), \(E[X_{n1}X_{n1}^\top]=I_p\), and the moment and eigenvalue assumptions give a uniform bound on \(E\|X_{n1}\|^{3}\).  For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^p\), Taylor expansion of \(e^{iu}\) with its third-order remainder gives
\[
 E\exp\left\{i\frac{t^\top X_{n1}}{\sqrt n}\right\}
 =1-\frac{\|t\|^2}{2n}+R_n(t),
 \qquad
 |R_n(t)|\le \frac{C_t}{n^{3/2}},
\]
uniformly over the admissible rows.  Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power and using row-wise independence yields
\[
 E\exp\left\{it^\top n^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^nX_{nr}\right\}
 \longrightarrow e^{-\|t\|^2/2}
\]
uniformly.  A contradiction subsequence argument together with the characteristic-function continuity theorem therefore gives the uniform fixed-dimensional central limit conclusion
\[
 \sqrt n\,\Omega_n^{-1/2}(\bar h_n-\mu_n)\Rightarrow N(0,I_p).
\]
The squared Euclidean norm consequently converges uniformly to \(\chi_p^2\).  By \texttt{lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky} and continuity of the \(\chi_p^2\) distribution at its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile,
\[
 \inf_{(P_n)}P_n\{\mu_n\in\mathcal U_n\}
 =\inf_{(P_n)}P_n\!\left\{
 n(\bar h_n-\mu_n)^\top\widetilde\Omega_n^{-1}(\bar h_n-\mu_n)
 \le\chi^2_{p,1-\alpha}\right\}
 \longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\]
Combining this with the deterministic event inclusion proves the asserted lower coverage bound.  No step divided by a coordinate of \(d(\mu_n)\), selected a component sign, or selected an optimizer basis.  The argument is therefore unchanged at exact zeros, root-\(n\) weak coordinates, and all indicated ties.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery]\label{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery}

Consequently, \(\sqrt n\{\psi_q(\bar h_n)-\psi_q(\mu_0)\}=J_q(\mu_0)\sqrt n(\bar h_n-\mu_0)+o_{P_0}(1)\Rightarrow N(0,J_q(\mu_0)\Omega_0J_q(\mu_0)^\top)\). If both diagonal entries of \(J_q(\mu_0)\Omega_0J_q(\mu_0)^\top\) are positive, each lower or upper endpoint of \(\mathcal I_{q,n}^{\mathrm{ND}}\) differs from its corresponding known-cell oracle endpoint in \(\mathcal I_{q,n}^{\mathrm{or}}\) by \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\). This conclusion is pointwise at \(P_0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The nonzero conditions on \(d_i(\mu_0)\) and on \(d_j(\mu_0)^2-d_k(\mu_0)^2\) persist on a neighborhood by continuity of \(\Gamma\).  On that neighborhood \texttt{lem:undivided-supershort-equivalence} converts every quadratic edge restriction into one of two linear ratio inequalities, with the ordering of the reciprocal ratios fixed.  The coefficients of those inequalities are continuously differentiable functions of \(\mu\), since every denominator is bounded away from zero there.

Strictly positive branch-selection margins keep the same component-sign branch uniquely optimal.  Strict primal and dual slacks and strictly positive reduced-cost margins keep the same unique primal--dual basis: by continuity, the basic solution remains feasible, the nonbasic reduced costs retain their signs, and every competing branch remains separated in objective value.  On this neighborhood each endpoint is therefore the value of one fixed finite-dimensional linear-program basis.  Solving the active equalities gives a continuously differentiable rational expression in the constraint coefficients, because the basis matrix remains nonsingular.  Composition with the continuously differentiable map \(\Gamma\) proves that \(\psi_q\) is continuously differentiable there, with derivative \(J_q\).

For the fixed law, put \(X_r=\Omega_0^{-1/2}\{h(O_r)-\mu_0\}\).  The sampling and moment assumptions give \(E X_r=0\), \(E[X_rX_r^\top]=I_p\), and \(E\|X_r\|^3<\infty\).  For every fixed \(t\), the third-order expansion
\[
 E e^{it^\top X_r/\sqrt n}
 =1-\frac{\|t\|^2}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2})
\]
and independence show that the characteristic function of \(n^{-1/2}\sum_rX_r\) converges to \(e^{-\|t\|^2/2}\).  Thus
\[
 \sqrt n(\bar h_n-\mu_0)\Rightarrow N(0,\Omega_0).
\]
Differentiability yields the expansion
\[
 \psi_q(\bar h_n)-\psi_q(\mu_0)
 =J_q(\mu_0)(\bar h_n-\mu_0)+r_n,
 \qquad
 \frac{\|r_n\|}{\|\bar h_n-\mu_0\|}\to_{P_0}0,
\]
and hence the displayed Gaussian limit.

For column \(m\), the fundamental theorem of calculus on the stable neighborhood gives
\[
 \widehat J_{q,n}^{(m)}
 =\int_0^1J_q(\bar h_n+t\varepsilon_ne_m)e_m\,dt.
\]
Because \(\bar h_n\to_{P_0}\mu_0\) and \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), continuity implies \(\widehat J_{q,n}\to_{P_0}J_q(\mu_0)\).  The extra condition \(\sqrt n\varepsilon_n\to\infty\) is compatible with, but stronger than, what this fixed-cell consistency step needs.  The covariance lemma gives \(\widetilde\Omega_n\to_{P_0}\Omega_0\), so
\[
 \widehat J_{q,n}\widetilde\Omega_n\widehat J_{q,n}^\top
 -J_q(\bar h_n)\widetilde\Omega_nJ_q(\bar h_n)^\top
 \to_{P_0}0.
\]
If the two limiting diagonal variances are positive, continuity of the square root shows that the corresponding standard-error factors differ by \(o_{P_0}(1)\).  The interval centers are identical and their half-widths carry the factor \(n^{-1/2}\); every corresponding endpoint difference is therefore \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\).  All stability claims are local to the fixed law, so the conclusion is pointwise, not uniform over shrinking margins.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Strict branch and basis margins turn the apparently nonsmooth endpoint problem into one stable differentiable cell and certify first-order oracle behavior there. \textit{Gap.} HongLi2018 establishes numerical-delta validity for directionally differentiable maps, while FangSantos2019 characterizes bootstrap behavior at nondifferentiable maps; neither derives the fixed-cell Jacobian, basis-stability neighborhood, and oracle-equivalent endpoint intervals for this componentwise supershort union. \textit{Consumer.} In regular ACS specifications, software can report short Gaussian endpoint intervals with a theorem identifying exactly when they coincide to first order with a known-branch, known-basis oracle.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-safe-cell-oracle-recovery]\label{thm:adaptive-safe-cell-oracle-recovery}
Fix \(q\in\mathbb R^K\).  Under \texttt{ass:iid-triangular-sampling}, \texttt{ass:primitive-moment-envelope}, \texttt{ass:primitive-covariance}, and \texttt{ass:regular-primitive-map}, there exists a calibrated-sieve split-sample realization of \(\mathsf H_n\) with the following properties.  Let \(m_n=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor\) and \(N_n=n-m_n\).  On the inference subsample use the numerical-difference step \(\varepsilon_{N_n}\), for which \(\varepsilon_{N_n}\to0\) and \(\sqrt{N_n}\,\varepsilon_{N_n}\to\infty\).  A conservative pilot rule selects numerical-delta inference only when a shrinking closed neighborhood of the pilot primitive mean and covariance is contained in a compact inner sieve of one strict regular cell having nonzero denominator, ratio-order, branch-selection, primal-slack, dual-slack, reduced-cost, and endpoint-variance margins; otherwise it selects primitive-moment inversion.  The inner sieves and their continuity moduli give an existence and calibration construction and are not asserted to admit a globally implementable ball-containment algorithm.  On inversion selection, \(\mathsf H_n\) is the inference-subsample version of \(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{inv}}\).  On safe-cell selection, \(\mathsf H_n\) is the slab whose lower and upper \(q\)-support limits are, respectively, the lower endpoint of the first interval and the upper endpoint of the second interval in the inference-subsample version of \(\mathcal I_{q,N_n}^{\mathrm{ND}}\).  Then
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)}P_n\{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\subseteq\mathsf H_n\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For each fixed \(C<\infty\), the pilot selects inversion with probability tending to one uniformly over admissible arrays for which at least one denominator margin \(|d_i(\mu_n)|\), ratio-order margin \(|d_j(\mu_n)^2-d_k(\mu_n)^2|\), or nonnegative component-branch or optimizer-basis tie margin is at most \(Cn^{-1/2}\); exact zeros and exact ties are included.  At every fixed law \(P_0\) satisfying all strict-cell conditions of \texttt{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery} and having two positive endpoint variances, the pilot selects the safe cell with probability tending to one, and the two finite \(q\)-support endpoints of \(\mathsf H_n\) differ from the corresponding outer endpoints of the full-sample \(\mathcal I_{q,n}^{\mathrm{or}}\) by \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(m=m_n\), \(N=N_n\), and write \(\eta=(\mu,\Omega)\), equipped with the product norm
\[
 \|\eta\|_*:=\|\mu\|+\|\Omega\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Because \(m=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor\), we have \(m\to\infty\), \(N\to\infty\), \(m/n\to0\), and \(N/n\to1\).  The defining conditions on \(\varepsilon_n\) therefore imply
\[
 \varepsilon_N\to0,
 \qquad
 \sqrt N\,\varepsilon_N\to\infty. \tag{1}
\]

We first construct the calibrated safe sets.  By \texttt{thm:cycle-farkas-and-computation}, there are finitely many component-sign branches, and each endpoint problem has finitely many candidate primal--dual bases.  Let \(\mathcal G\) be the set of pairs \(\eta=(\mu,\Omega)\) for which both endpoint problems have a unique winning branch and a unique primal--dual basis, all ratio-order, branch-selection, primal-slack, dual-slack, and reduced-cost inequalities are strict, every \(d_i(\mu)\) is nonzero, and
\[
 v_r(\eta):=g_r(\mu)^\top\Omega g_r(\mu)>0,
 \qquad
 g_r(\mu):=D\psi_{q,r}(\mu),
 \qquad r\in\{1,2\}. \tag{2}
\]
Here the denominator margins are \(|d_i(\mu)|\), the ratio-order margins are \(|d_j(\mu)^2-d_k(\mu)^2|\), and the other named margins are the nonnegative distances of the finite branch and basis inequalities from their equality boundaries.  Let \(\Delta(\eta)\) be the minimum of this finite list of margins for \(\eta\in\mathcal G\), and set \(\Delta(\eta)=0\) for \(\eta\notin\mathcal G\).  The strict-cell conclusion of \texttt{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery}, together with the continuous differentiability in \texttt{ass:regular-primitive-map}, shows that \(\mathcal G\) is open and that \(g_r\) is continuous on each of its finitely many cells.

For \(j\ge1\), take \(G_j\) to be the finite union over strict cells of the closed subsets on which
\[
 \|\eta\|_*\le j,
 \quad j^{-1}I_p\preceq\Omega\preceq jI_p,
 \quad \operatorname{dist}(\eta,\mathcal G^c)\ge j^{-1},
 \quad \Delta(\eta)\ge j^{-1},
 \quad j^{-1}\le v_r(\eta)\le j,
 \quad \|g_r(\mu)\|\le j
 \quad(r=1,2). \tag{3}
\]
Replacing \(G_j\) by \(\bigcup_{k\le j}G_k\) makes the sequence nested.  Each \(G_j\) is compact, and every point of \(\mathcal G\) is in the interior of some \(G_j\), because at such a point the finite margins and the two variances in (2) are positive and the point has positive distance from the complement of its open cell.  Define the within-cell gradient modulus
\[
 \omega_j(r):=max_{a\in\{1,2\}}
 \sup\bigl\{\|g_a(\mu)-g_a(\mu')\|:
     (\mu,\Omega),(\mu',\Omega')\in G_j
     \text{ lie in one cell},\ \|\mu-\mu'\|\le r\bigr\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Compactness and continuity give \(\omega_j(r)\downarrow0\) as \(r\downarrow0\) for each fixed \(j\).

Set \(\delta_n=m^{-1/2}+\varepsilon_N\).  A diagonal choice using (1) and (4) gives deterministic integers \(j_n\uparrow\infty\) such that, with \(r_n=j_n\delta_n\),
\[
 r_n\to0,
 \qquad \frac{j_n}{\sqrt n}\to0,
 \qquad
 \sqrt{j_n}\,\omega_{j_n}(4r_n)\to0. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, for each fixed \(j\), \(j\delta_n\to0\) and \(\omega_j(4j\delta_n)\to0\); holding \(j_n=j\) until all three displayed requirements are at most \(j^{-1}\), and then increasing \(j\), proves existence.  This is the calibrated-sieve step: it uses only deterministic continuity moduli and does not assert that the global containment problem is algorithmically available.

Let \(\bar h^{\mathrm p}\) and \(\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm p}\) be the pilot mean and covariance and put \(\widehat\eta^{\mathrm p}=(\bar h^{\mathrm p},\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm p})\).  The pilot selects the safe procedure precisely when the closed ball
\[
 \overline B_*(\widehat\eta^{\mathrm p},3r_n)
\]
is contained in a single strict-cell component of \(G_{j_n}\); otherwise it selects inversion.  This is a measurable rule after a fixed deterministic tie-breaking convention, because there are finitely many cells and each \(G_j\) is closed.

We next control pilot error.  The covariance eigenvalue upper bound in \texttt{ass:primitive-covariance} and the centered \((4+\delta)\)-moment bound in \texttt{ass:primitive-moment-envelope} imply constants \(C_1,C_2<\infty\), independent of the admissible row law, such that
\[
 E\|\bar h^{\mathrm p}-\mu_n\|^2\le \frac{C_1}{m},
 \qquad
 E\|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm p}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le \frac{C_2}{m}. \tag{6}
\]
For the second inequality, expand each covariance entry as the average of centered products minus the product of centered sample means; the fourth moment bounds the squared centered products, and summing the finitely many entries gives (6).  Markov's inequality and \(r_n\ge j_nm^{-1/2}\) now yield
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\widehat\eta^{\mathrm p}-\eta_n\|_*>r_n\}
 \le \frac{C_3}{m r_n^2}
 \le \frac{C_3}{j_n^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \eta_n=(\mu_n,\Omega_n). \tag{7}
\]
This is also the quantitative covariance-consistency step underlying \texttt{lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky}.

Suppose the pilot selects the safe procedure and the event complementary to that in (7) occurs.  Then \(\eta_n\in G_{j_n}\), and the ball of radius \(2r_n\) about \(\eta_n\) stays in the selected strict cell.  In particular, (3) gives
\[
 \Delta(\eta_n)\ge j_n^{-1}. \tag{8}
\]
If, for a fixed \(C<\infty\), any named margin is at most \(Cn^{-1/2}\), then \(\Delta(\eta_n)\le Cn^{-1/2}<j_n^{-1}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where the last inequality is (5).  Thus safe selection is impossible on the high-probability event in (7).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{(P_n):\,\Delta(\eta_n)\le Cn^{-1/2}}
 P_n\{\text{safe selection}\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{9}
\]
This proves the stated uniform fallback assertion, including exact zeros and ties.

It remains to prove coverage on the safe branch.  Let \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\), \(\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm i}\), and \(\widetilde\Omega^{\mathrm i}=\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm i}+\lambda_NI_p\) be the inference-subsample quantities.  Applying the same bounds as in (6) with sample size \(N\), and using \(N/m\to\infty\), gives
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n\|>r_n\}=o(1),
 \qquad
 \sup_{(P_n)}P_n\{\|\widehat\Omega^{\mathrm i}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}>r_n\}=o(1). \tag{10}
\]
Moreover, \(\varepsilon_N\le r_n/j_n<r_n\).  Hence, on safe selection and the events in (7)--(10), \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\), every segment from \(\mu_n\) to \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\), and every numerical-difference segment from \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}\) to \(\bar h^{\mathrm i}+\varepsilon_Ne_k\) stay in the same strict cell.

Write \(g_{r,n}=g_r(\mu_n)\), \(v_{r,n}=g_{r,n}^\top\Omega_ng_{r,n}\), and let \(\widehat g_{r,n}\) be row \(r\) of the numerical Jacobian computed on the inference subsample.  The mean-value formula and (4) give, for \(r=1,2\),
\[
 \psi_{q,r}(\bar h^{\mathrm i})-\psi_{q,r}(\mu_n)
 =g_{r,n}^\top(\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n)+R_{r,n},
 \qquad
 |R_{r,n}|\le \omega_{j_n}(4r_n)\|\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n\|, \tag{11}
\]
and, with a constant depending only on the fixed dimension \(p\),
\[
 \|\widehat g_{r,n}-g_{r,n}\|
 \le \sqrt p\,\omega_{j_n}(4r_n). \tag{12}
\]
Because (3) gives \(v_{r,n}\ge j_n^{-1}\), equations (5), (10), and (11) imply
\[
 \frac{\sqrt N\,R_{r,n}}{\sqrt{v_{r,n}}}=o_P(1). \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, standardization is uniform.  Put \(a_{r,n}=g_{r,n}/\sqrt{v_{r,n}}\).  The lower covariance eigenvalue in \texttt{ass:primitive-covariance} gives \(\|a_{r,n}\|\le c^{-1/2}\).  Thus the moment envelope implies, for some \(\kappa>0\),
\[
 \sup_{n,r,(P_n)}E_{P_n}|a_{r,n}^\top\{h(O_{n1})-\mu_n\}|^{2+\kappa}<\infty,
\]
and the corresponding Lyapunov remainder is bounded by a constant times \(N^{-\kappa/2}\).  The fixed-dimensional triangular-array central-limit calculation used in \texttt{lem:uniform-primitive-inversion} therefore yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt N\,g_{r,n}^\top(\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\mu_n)}{\sqrt{v_{r,n}}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1)
\]
uniformly over admissible arrays with \(\eta_n\in G_{j_n}\).  By (12), (5), (10), \(\lambda_N\to0\), and the eigenvalue bounds,
\[
 \frac{\widehat g_{r,n}\widetilde\Omega^{\mathrm i}\widehat g_{r,n}^\top}{v_{r,n}}
 \to_P1 \tag{14}
\]
uniformly on the same arrays.  Equations (11)--(14) prove the uniformly studentized numerical-delta approximation.

Let \(z_{1-\alpha/2}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\), let \(L_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\) be the lower outer endpoint for coordinate one, and let \(U_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\) be the upper outer endpoint for coordinate two, all computed on the inference subsample.  The preceding approximation gives
\[
 \sup P_n\{L_n^{\mathrm{ND}}>\underline\psi_q(\mu_n)\}
 \le \alpha/2+o(1),
 \qquad
 \sup P_n\{U_n^{\mathrm{ND}}<\overline\psi_q(\mu_n)\}
 \le \alpha/2+o(1), \tag{15}
\]
where each supremum is over admissible arrays with \(\eta_n\in G_{j_n}\).  The union bound then shows that the slab
\[
 \mathsf H_n^{\mathrm{reg}}
 =\{\theta\in\mathbb R^K:L_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\le q^\top\theta\le U_n^{\mathrm{ND}}\}
\]
contains \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) on the safe branch: by the definition of the support endpoints, every \(\theta\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\) has \(q^\top\theta\) between them.  If the pilot selects inversion, \texttt{lem:uniform-primitive-inversion}, applied to the independent inference subsample of size \(N\), gives the same lower coverage bound for its version of \(\mathcal C_N^{\mathrm{inv}}\).  Conditional on the pilot, the inference sample is independent and has the row law \(P_n\).  Combining the two conditional bounds and then using (7) proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)}
 P_n\{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mu_n)\subseteq\mathsf H_n\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{16}
\]

Finally, fix a law \(P_0\) satisfying the strict-cell conditions and the two positive-variance conditions.  Then \(\eta_0=(\mu_0,\Omega_0)\) is in the interior of some \(G_J\).  Since \(j_n\to\infty\), \(r_n\to0\), and the pilot is consistent by (7), the pilot ball is eventually contained in that cell with probability tending to one.  Thus safe selection has probability tending to one.  By \texttt{thm:pointwise-regular-cell-oracle-recovery}, used with sample size \(N\) and (1), the numerical endpoints differ from their inference-subsample analytic-Jacobian oracle endpoints by \(o_{P_0}(N^{-1/2})\).

It remains only to compare the inference-subsample oracle with the full-sample oracle.  If \(\bar h^{\mathrm f}\) is the full-sample mean, then
\[
 \bar h^{\mathrm i}-\bar h^{\mathrm f}
 =\frac{m}{n}(\bar h^{\mathrm i}-\bar h^{\mathrm p})
 =O_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt m}{n}\right)
 =o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2}). \tag{17}
\]
Continuous differentiability on the fixed cell transfers (17) to the two endpoint centers.  Both inference and full-sample covariance estimators converge to \(\Omega_0\) by \texttt{lem:regularized-covariance-slutsky}; continuity of \(J_q\), positivity of the endpoint variances, and
\[
 N^{-1/2}-n^{-1/2}=o(n^{-1/2})
\]
therefore show that the corresponding oracle standard errors differ by \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\).  Combining this fact, (17), and the inference-subsample oracle comparison proves the asserted \(o_{P_0}(n^{-1/2})\) recovery of both full-sample oracle outer endpoints.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Universal algebraic containment does not require a density condition, but converse attainment is proved only when each \((D_i,U_i)\mid X\) has an everywhere-positive conditional density and finite second moments.  Thus arbitrary observed laws receive an outer bound, although the covered family is strictly broader than the former nonsingular Gaussian submodel and leaves cross-setting dependence unrestricted.  The adaptive safe-cell procedure is an existence and calibrated-sieve construction: its global modulus and ball-containment certificates are not claimed to be available as turnkey computations.  Uniform coverage includes singular sequences, whereas oracle equivalence is pointwise at fixed strict regular laws.

\section{Honest scope}

The identification claims cover generalized-class containment, exact componentwise closure for the published Huntington--Klein ratio-envelope specialization, and joint-margin-preserving converse attainment under conditional positive density; nonsingular conditional-Gaussian laws follow as a corollary.  The paper also covers cycle and Farkas certificates, exact branch-linear-program or mixed-integer computation, symbolic support and pin gaps, and the solved three-setting equality cycle.  The supershort object is a calibrated sensitivity region, not a causal identified set.  Undivided primitive inversion supplies uniform coverage at zero and \(n^{-1/2}\)-weak denominators or ties.  The calibrated-sieve split-sample handle combines that fallback with numerical-delta inference, is uniformly valid over the stated triangular-array class, and recovers the known-cell oracle to first order at fixed strict regular laws.  The safe-cell selector is an existence construction rather than a claim of globally implementable modulus and ball-containment checks.  The zero-safe attainment identity is \(Y_i=(s_i-B_i)D_i+(U_i+B_iD_i)\) for every \(B_i\), including zero, and it leaves the complete observed vector unchanged.
