\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cate\_\allowbreak{}homogeneity\_\allowbreak{}testing\_\allowbreak{}nuisance\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Let \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\).  The observational radius satisfies the proved all-regime upper bound \(\rho_n^*\le Cb_n\) and, for \(d\ge2s_0\), the original fixed-sample lower bound \(\rho_n^*\ge c\max\{n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)},n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}\}\), with no logarithm at \(d=2s_0\).  The no-penalty region \(s_0\ge\gamma\), \(d\le4\gamma\) remains matched.  A strict penalty is now proved whenever \(\gamma>s_0\) and \(d>2s_0\gamma/(\gamma-s_0)\): then \(\rho_n^*/\rho_{n,e}^*\ge c n^{2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}\to\infty\).  At \((d,\gamma,\alpha,\beta)=(1,1,1/10,1/10)\), the exponent is at least \(4/35\).  The complete frontier and its remaining transition boundaries are open; no common sharp-rate symbol is defined.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published CATE-homogeneity procedures and adjacent minimax results do not give the fixed-sample critical radius or the exact value-of-known-propensity frontier on this binary exact-uniform class.  The direct-Hellinger construction determines a nuisance-driven converse for \(d\ge2(\alpha+\beta)\), and comparison with the known-propensity benchmark proves strict divergence when \(\gamma>\alpha+\beta\) and \(d>2(\alpha+\beta)\gamma/(\gamma-\alpha-\beta)\).  The best proved observational upper map still does not match the converse outside the no-penalty region, so necessity and the transition boundaries remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Niche.} The live niche is the interaction among a composite unknown-constant CATE null, separately rough propensity and baseline functions, exact full-cube design, and connected repeated-cell collisions.  An attaining test must use an omnibus population functional richer than both a within-bin covariance factor and the algebraically equivalent residual of a projected four-cell law.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper supplies an explicit total profiled fourth-order test and all-regime upper bound, the known-propensity benchmark and matched no-penalty region, and a same-class fixed-\(n\) lower construction giving \(n^{-2(\alpha+\beta)/(2(\alpha+\beta)+d)}\) whenever \(d\ge2(\alpha+\beta)\).  Combining that converse with the known-propensity benchmark yields the explicit polynomial lower bound \(\rho_n^*/\rho_{n,e}^*\ge c n^{2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)-2(\alpha+\beta)/(2(\alpha+\beta)+d)}\) throughout the proved strict-penalty region, without claiming that this sufficient region is necessary or that the complete frontier is solved.

\section{Related work}

KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024 remains a method donor rather than the source of the present class: its pointwise-estimation model is larger and its binary lower submodel uses a support with holes.  The converse here uses the localized fuzzy-hypothesis idea only after proving exact full-cube uniform masses, componentwise binary margins, a common sign-conditioned singleton predictive law, and a bounded-overlap connected-graph Hellinger calculation.  NeykovBalakrishnanWasserman2021 gives collision-sensitive conditional-independence testing under a different null and metric; LundborgKimShahSamworth2024, corresponding to PCM arXiv:2211.02039, gives projected-covariance validity and spline power under common-smoothness conditions; DukesStensrudBrioschiHudson2026, arXiv:2410.00985v4, gives asymptotic structured-rule homogeneity results under nuisance conditions; and McGrathMukherjee2026, arXiv:2212.14857v5, derives separate resolutions for estimating a signed expected conditional covariance.  None supplies the componentwise constant-null fixed-sample nuisance lower map proved here, the resulting explicit sufficient strict-divergence region for unknown versus known propensity, or an omnibus test that closes the remaining same-class frontier.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-design]\label{ass:uniform-design}
\(P\circ X^{-1}=\lambda\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known random uniform design, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=A Y(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency, \cite{HernanRobins2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-exchangeability]\label{ass:conditional-exchangeability}
\((Y(0),Y(1))\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}A\mid X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability, \cite{HernanRobins2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-smoothness]\label{ass:propensity-smoothness}
The canonical continuous representative \(e\) belongs to \(\mathcal H^{\alpha}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder propensity regularity, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:baseline-smoothness]\label{ass:baseline-smoothness}
The canonical continuous representative \(m_0\) belongs to \(\mathcal H^{\beta}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder outcome-regression regularity, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cate-smoothness]\label{ass:cate-smoothness}
The function \(\tau=m_1-m_0\) formed from the canonical continuous representatives belongs to \(\mathcal H^{\gamma}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder CATE regularity, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-margin]\label{ass:propensity-margin}
\(1/4\le e(x)\le3/4\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), where \(e\) is its canonical continuous representative.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strong overlap with fixed margin, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:baseline-margin]\label{ass:baseline-margin}
\(1/4\le m_0(x)\le3/4\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), where \(m_0\) is its canonical continuous representative.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior Bernoulli regression, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treated-margin]\label{ass:treated-margin}
\(1/4\le m_1(x)\le3/4\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), where \(m_1\) is its canonical continuous representative.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior Bernoulli regression, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:sanity-witness]\label{prop:sanity-witness}
Let \(e(x)=1/2+(x_1-1/2)/16\), \(m_0(x)=3/8\), and \(\tau(x)=1/8+\eta\sin(2\pi x_1)\) with \(|\eta|\le1/32\). These observed regressions admit a binary full-data law in \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\), satisfy \(\bar\tau=1/8\), and obey \(D(P)=|\eta|/\sqrt 2\); at \(\eta=0\) the law is a nonzero homogeneous null.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the displayed functions be fixed. The propensity satisfies
\[
  \frac{15}{32}\le e(x)\le\frac{17}{32},
\]
and \(m_0(x)=3/8\). Moreover,
\[
  m_1(x)=m_0(x)+\tau(x)=\frac12+\eta\sin(2\pi x_1),
  \qquad
  \frac{15}{32}\le m_1(x)\le\frac{17}{32}.
\]
Thus all three pointwise margin assumptions hold. For \(r=\lVert x-z\rVert_2\),
\[
 |e(x)-e(z)|\le \frac1{16}\min\{r,1\}\le \frac1{16}r^\alpha\le Lr^\alpha.
\]
Here the middle inequality holds separately for \(r\le1\) and \(r\ge1\). Hence the propensity-smoothness assumption holds. The constant function \(m_0\) satisfies the baseline-smoothness assumption. Finally,
\[
 |\tau(x)-\tau(z)|
 \le |\eta|\min\{2,2\pi r\}
 \le 2\pi|\eta|\min\{r,1\}
 \le \frac{\pi}{16}r^\gamma
 \le Lr^\gamma,
\]
and \(\lVert\tau\rVert_\infty\le5/32<L\), so the CATE-smoothness assumption also holds.

It remains to exhibit a full-data law rather than only observed regressions. Draw \(X\sim\lambda\), then, conditionally on \(X=x\), draw \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e(x))\) independently of conditionally independent variables
\[
 Y(0)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_0(x)),
 \qquad
 Y(1)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_1(x)),
\]
and set \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\). This construction gives consistency and conditional exchangeability, while its observed propensity and arm regressions are exactly \(e,m_0,m_1\). Consequently it is a member of the class in \(\texttt{def:causal-law-class}\).

By Fubini's theorem and \(\int_0^1\sin(2\pi u)\,du=0\),
\[
 \bar\tau=\int_{[0,1]^d}\tau(x)\,dx=\frac18.
\]
The definition of \(D\) then gives
\[
 D(P)^2
 =\eta^2\int_{[0,1]^d}\sin^2(2\pi x_1)\,dx
 =\frac{\eta^2}{2},
 \qquad
 D(P)=\frac{|\eta|}{\sqrt2}.
\]
At \(\eta=0\), the effect is the nonzero constant \(1/8\in I\), so the constructed law belongs to \(\mathcal H_0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The witness rules out a vacuous alternative and checks that centering does not reduce the null to a zero effect. \textit{Gap.} Existing homogeneity papers allow nonzero constants, but this exact binary uniform-design witness is specific to the proposed class. \textit{Consumer.} The witness supplies a reproducible calibration case for implementations and lower-bound sanity checks.

\begin{lemma}[lem:known-propensity-benchmark]\label{lem:known-propensity-benchmark}
In the experiment supplying \(e\), the bounded pseudo-outcome \(W=\frac{(A-e(X))Y}{e(X)[1-e(X)]}\) satisfies \(\mathbb E_P[W\mid X]=\tau(X)\). Consequently the constant-CATE problem reduces to classical nonadaptive \(L^2\) goodness-of-fit testing of a \(\gamma\)-Hölder regression function, and there exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) and \(n_0\), depending only on \(\gamma,d,L\), such that \(c r_{\mathrm{or}}(n)\le\rho_{n,e}^*\le C r_{\mathrm{or}}(n)\) for every \(n\ge n_0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First, the propensity margin makes every displayed denominator positive. Conditional on \(X=x\), consistency of the observed arm regressions yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[(A-e(x))Y\mid X=x]
 &=e(x)\{1-e(x)\}m_1(x)
   -\{1-e(x)\}e(x)m_0(x)\\
 &=e(x)\{1-e(x)\}\tau(x).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Division by \(e(x)\{1-e(x)\}\ge3/16\) proves \(\mathbb E_P[W\mid X]=\tau(X)\). More sharply, on \(A=1\), \(|W|\le1/e(X)\le4\), and on \(A=0\), \(|W|\le1/\{1-e(X)\}\le4\); hence \(|W|\le4\) almost surely.

We next give an explicit upper test. Partition \(\mathcal X\) into \(J=m^d\) equal cubes and define
\[
 K_m(x,z)=J\mathbf 1\{x,z\text{ are in the same cube}\},
 \qquad K_m^0(x,z)=K_m(x,z)-1.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), put
\[
 T_m=\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}W_iW_jK_m^0(X_i,X_j).
\]
This is a measurable function of \((O^n,e_P)\). Let \(\Pi_m\) denote the \(L^2(\lambda)\)-orthogonal projection onto functions constant on the cubes. Independence, the preceding conditional-mean identity, and the uniform-design assumption give
\[
 \theta_m:=\mathbb E_PT_m
 =\lVert\Pi_m\tau-\bar\tau\rVert_2^2.
\]
Because \(\Pi_m\) preserves constants, orthogonality gives the Pythagorean identity
\[
 D(P)^2=\theta_m+\lVert\tau-\Pi_m\tau\rVert_2^2.
\]
The CATE-smoothness assumption and the cube diameter \(\sqrt d/m\) imply
\[
 \lVert\tau-\Pi_m\tau\rVert_2^2
 \le L^2d^\gamma m^{-2\gamma},
 \qquad
 \theta_m\ge D(P)^2-L^2d^\gamma m^{-2\gamma}.
\]

For completeness, expand the variance directly. The first centered projection of the symmetric order-two kernel is
\[
 W\{\Pi_m\tau(X)-\bar\tau\}-\theta_m,
\]
whose variance is at most \(16\theta_m\), since \(|W|\le4\). The squared \(L^2(P^2)\) norm of the original kernel is at most
\[
 4^4\iint K_m^0(x,z)^2\,d\lambda(x)d\lambda(z)
 =256(J-1).
\]
Subtracting projections cannot increase the squared norm. Expanding the covariance of the pair average according to whether two index pairs share one or zero observations therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(T_m)
 \le \frac{64\theta_m}{n}
      +\frac{512(J-1)}{n(n-1)}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Under \(\mathcal H_0\), \(\tau\) is constant, so \(\theta_m=0\). Thus the test
\[
 \phi_m=\mathbf 1\!\left\{T_m>
 \left(\frac{10240(J-1)}{n(n-1)}\right)^{1/2}\right\}
\]
has null rejection probability at most \(1/20=a_0\) by (1) and Chebyshev's inequality. If
\[
 D(P)^2\ge C_1\left\{m^{-2\gamma}+\frac{\sqrt J}{n}\right\},          \tag{2}
\]
where \(C_1\) depends only on \(\gamma,d,L\) and is sufficiently large, then the projection bound makes \(\theta_m\) at least a fixed large multiple of both the rejection threshold and \(1/n\). Applying Chebyshev to the lower tail in (1) gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(\phi_m=0)
 \le
 \frac{64\theta_m/n+512(J-1)/\{n(n-1)\}}
 {\left[\theta_m-\{10240(J-1)/(n(n-1))\}^{1/2}\right]^2}
 \le b_0,                                                            \tag{3}
\]
after increasing \(C_1\). Choose \(m\) to be the nearest integer at least two to \(n^{2/(4\gamma+d)}\). Then both terms on the right of (2) are bounded by a constant times \(n^{-4\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}\). Equations (2)--(3) and the definition of the known-propensity critical radius prove
\[
 \rho_{n,e}^*\le C n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}.                          \tag{4}
\]

We prove the converse on a submodel with the same supplied propensity. Fix \(e\equiv1/2\), \(m_0\equiv3/8\), and the null \(\tau_0\equiv1/8\). Choose a nonzero function \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((1/4,3/4)^d)\) with \(\int\psi=0\). For the \(J=m^d\) cubes let \(\psi_j\) be its translate and rescaling to cube \(j\), and for \(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_J)\in\{-1,1\}^J\) put
\[
 f_\lambda=\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_j\psi_j,
 \qquad
 \tau_\lambda=\frac18+\delta f_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \delta=\varepsilon m^{-\gamma}.                                    \tag{5}
\]
The supports have a fixed relative collar. Hence there is a constant \(C_\psi\) such that every \(f_\lambda\) has supremum norm at most \(C_\psi\) and Hölder-\(\gamma\) seminorm at most \(C_\psi m^\gamma\). Choosing the fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small, as a function only of \(\gamma,d,L\), makes every law in (5) satisfy the CATE Hölder restriction and all Bernoulli margins. The constant \(e\) and \(m_0\) satisfy their restrictions for every \(\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]\). The conditional-independent Bernoulli coupling used in the sanity witness supplies consistency and exchangeability. Thus the null and all alternatives belong to the unchanged causal class. Also,
\[
 \int f_\lambda=0,
 \qquad
 D(P_\lambda)^2
 =\delta^2\sum_{j=1}^J\int\psi_j^2
 =\delta^2\lVert\psi\rVert_2^2.                                    \tag{6}
\]
Let \(P_0\) be the null observed law. Only the treated outcome regression changes, and its null value is \(1/2\). Therefore the one-observation likelihood ratio is
\[
 \frac{dP_\lambda}{dP_0}(O)
 =1+2\delta f_\lambda(X)(2Y-1)\mathbf1\{A=1\}.                       \tag{7}
\]
For independent uniform sign vectors \(\lambda,\lambda'\), (7), uniform design, and disjoint supports give
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}\!\left[\frac{dP_\lambda}{dP_0}
                         \frac{dP_{\lambda'}}{dP_0}\right]
 =1+2\delta^2m^{-d}\lVert\psi\rVert_2^2
       \sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_j\lambda'_j.                              \tag{8}
\]
Let \(\overline P_n=2^{-J}\sum_\lambda P_\lambda^{\otimes n}\). Using \((1+x)^n\le e^{nx}\), then independence of the products \(\lambda_j\lambda'_j\), and finally \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\), equations (7)--(8) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 1+\chi^2(\overline P_n,P_0^{\otimes n})
 &\le\left[\cosh\{2n\delta^2m^{-d}\lVert\psi\rVert_2^2\}\right]^J\\
 &\le\exp\{2n^2\delta^4m^{-d}\lVert\psi\rVert_2^4\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{9}
\]
Take \(m\) to be an integer comparable to \(n^{2/(4\gamma+d)}\). The exponent in (9) is bounded by a constant multiple of \(\varepsilon^4\). Choose \(\varepsilon\) still smaller so that
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\overline P_n,P_0^{\otimes n})
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(\overline P_n,P_0^{\otimes n})}<\frac34.    \tag{10}
\]
The displayed total-variation inequality follows directly from Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio minus one. If a level-\(a_0\) test had type-II error at most \(b_0\) against all alternatives in (5), its rejection expectations under \(P_0^{\otimes n}\) and \(\overline P_n\) would differ by at least \(1-b_0-a_0=3/4\), contradicting (10). By (6) and integer comparability,
\[
 \rho_{n,e}^*\ge c n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}.                          \tag{11}
\]
Combining (4) and (11) proves the claimed benchmark. All constants and the threshold use only \(\gamma,d,L\) because the upper test never estimates \(e\) or \(m_0\), while the lower submodel uses constant nuisances.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This standard benchmark isolates the classical signal-detection floor against which the observational nuisance penalty is measured. \textit{Gap.} Spokoiny1996, Section 1, records the classical one-dimensional nonadaptive Hölder \(L^2\) boundary and develops its adaptive wavelet counterpart; the pseudo-outcome identity transports the standard \(d\)-dimensional analogue to the known-propensity causal experiment and is not a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma fixes the comparator experiment needed to state the unknown-versus-known propensity frontier on one unchanged causal class.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rough-prior-diagnostic]\label{prop:rough-prior-diagnostic}
For \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), and sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}\), every \(P_{0,\sigma,\ell}\) and \(P_{1,\ell}\) from def:rough-prior-diagnostic belongs to \(\mathcal P(1/10,1/10,1,L,1)\), every null component obeys \(\tau_{0,\sigma}\equiv1/8\), and averaging \(P_{0,\sigma,\ell}\) over \(\sigma\) exactly matches the one-observation law of \(P_{1,\ell}\). The first nonmatching repeated-observation likelihood moment is order two: conditional on \(X_i=x,X_k=z\), the null-mixture covariance \(\operatorname{Cov}(A_i,A_k\mid X_i=x,X_k=z)\) equals \(q_{\ell}^2\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\mathrm{diag}}}b_{j,\ell}(x)b_{j,\ell}(z)\), whereas it is zero under \(P_{1,\ell}^{\otimes2}\). Moreover \(D(P_{1,\ell})=4\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}^2\ell\{\tfrac12\int_0^1b(t)^4dt-\tfrac14(\int_0^1b(t)^2dt)^2\}^{1/2}>0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(M=M_{\mathrm{diag}}\), so \(\ell=(2M)^{-1}\), and put
\[
 F_\sigma(x)=\sum_{j=1}^M\sigma_jb_{j,\ell}(x),
 \qquad
 G(x)=\sum_{j=1}^M b_{j,\ell}(x)^2,
 \qquad
 q=q_\ell=\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}\ell^{1/2}.
\]
The bump supports are disjoint and separated by collars. The zero extension of \(b\) is Lipschitz with constant one. The elementary inequality \(\min\{1,u\}\le u^{1/10}\) for \(u\ge0\) therefore shows, first within a bump and then across the collars, that
\[
 |qF_\sigma(x)-qF_\sigma(z)|
 \le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}\ell^{2/5}|x-z|^{1/10}
 \le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}|x-z|^{1/10}.                          \tag{12}
\]
The same argument applied to \(q^2G\) gives a Hölder-\(1/10\) constant at most \(C\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}^2\ell^{9/10}\). Since \(|(b^2)'|\le2\), the function \(q^2G\) is also Lipschitz with constant at most \(2q^2/\ell=2\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}^2\). Thus, for sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}\), the null propensity and baseline meet the two Hölder-\(1/10\) restrictions, while the alternative baseline meets that restriction and the alternative CATE meets the Hölder-one restriction. The null CATE is constant. The bounds \(|F_\sigma|\le1\), \(0\le G\le1\), and \(q\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}\le1/64\) show directly that \(e,m_0,m_1\) all remain in \([1/4,3/4]\). The conditional-independent Bernoulli construction in \(\texttt{def:rough-prior-diagnostic}\) gives exact uniform design, consistency, and conditional exchangeability. Hence every displayed law belongs to \(\mathcal P(1/10,1/10,1,L,1)\), and every null draw has \(\tau\equiv1/8\).

We next verify the one-observation equality. At a fixed \(x\), at most one bump is nonzero. Write \(v=qb_{j,\ell}(x)\) on that bump and let \(\sigma=\sigma_j\). Under a null component,
\[
 e_\sigma=\frac12+\sigma v,
 \qquad m_{0,\sigma}=\frac38+\sigma v,
 \qquad m_{1,\sigma}=\frac12+\sigma v.
\]
Averaging over the symmetric sign gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_\sigma[(1-e_\sigma)m_{0,\sigma}]&=\frac3{16}-v^2,\\
 \mathbb E_\sigma[e_\sigma m_{1,\sigma}]&=\frac14+v^2,\\
 \mathbb E_\sigma[e_\sigma]&=\frac12.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{13}
\]
Under the alternative, \(e_1=1/2\), \(m_{0,1}=3/8-2v^2\), and \(m_{1,1}=1/2+2v^2\), so its corresponding three quantities in (13) are identical. Those quantities determine all four probabilities of \((A,Y)\in\{0,1\}^2\). Outside the bumps \(v=0\), and the same calculation applies. Integration against the common uniform law of \(X\) proves exact equality of the mixed-null and alternative one-observation laws.

For two observations, conditional independence holds given the common sign vector. The law of total covariance and independence of its coordinates give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\sigma}(A_i,A_k\mid X_i=x,X_k=z)
 &=\operatorname{Cov}_{\sigma}\{e_\sigma(x),e_\sigma(z)\}\\
 &=q^2\sum_{j=1}^M b_{j,\ell}(x)b_{j,\ell}(z).
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{14}
\]
The alternative is one fixed iid law, so the corresponding conditional covariance is zero. Thus (14) is the first nonmatching repeated-observation moment.

Finally, \(M\ell=1/2\), the bump supports are disjoint, and a change of variables gives
\[
 \int_0^1G(x)\,dx=\frac12\int_0^1b(t)^2\,dt,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1G(x)^2\,dx=\frac12\int_0^1b(t)^4\,dt.                       \tag{15}
\]
Since \(\tau_1=1/8+4\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}^2\ell G\), equations (15) and the definition of \(D\) yield
\[
 D(P_{1,\ell})
 =4\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}^2\ell
 \left\{\frac12\int_0^1b(t)^4\,dt
 -\frac14\left(\int_0^1b(t)^2\,dt\right)^2\right\}^{1/2}.            \tag{16}
\]
The expression in braces is positive because \(G\) is not almost everywhere constant: \(b\) is nonconstant and vanishes off a strict subinterval. This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rough symmetric case now has an explicit legal early-kill calculation rather than a promissory appeal to moment matching. \textit{Gap.} The one-observation equality does not furnish a converse because shared local signs leak through the displayed two-observation moment; matching or suppressing that term at the sharp rough-nuisance scale remains open. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition determines the first moment that every candidate fixed-sample lower prior must neutralize before its divergence calculation is credible.

\begin{proposition}[prop:single-scale-upper]\label{prop:single-scale-upper}
Put \(t=\min\{\gamma,\alpha+\beta\}\). There exist constants \(C,n_0\), depending only on \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L\), and a deterministic integer choice of \(m\) such that \(\phi_{n,m}^{(4)}\) has rejection probability at most \(a_0\) uniformly on \(\mathcal H_0\) for every \(n\), and type-II error at most \(b_0\) whenever \(D(P)\ge C n^{-2t/(4t+d)}\) if \(d\le4t\), or \(D(P)\ge C n^{-4t/(4t+3d)}\) if \(d>4t\), for every \(n\ge n_0\). Its arithmetic cost is \(O(n+J)\) and its memory cost is \(O(J)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal B_m\) be the equal-cube partition and, for \(B\in\mathcal B_m\), write \(\mathbb E_B\) for conditioning on \(X\in B\), \(e_B=\mathbb E_Be\), and \(v_B=e_B(1-e_B)\). Uniform design gives \(\lambda(B)=J^{-1}\). Direct expansion using \(m_1=m_0+\tau\) gives, for every \(c\in I\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 g_B(c)
 &:=\operatorname{Cov}(A,Y\mid X\in B)-c\operatorname{Var}(A\mid X\in B)\\
 &=\mathbb E_B[e(m_0+\tau)]-e_B\{\mathbb E_Bm_0+\mathbb E_B(e\tau)\}-cv_B\\
 &=\operatorname{Cov}_B(e,m_0)+(1-e_B)\mathbb E_B[e(\tau-c)].
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{17}
\]
For independent \(X,X'\) conditional on \(B\), the covariance identity
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_B(e,m_0)
 =\frac12\mathbb E_B[(e(X)-e(X'))(m_0(X)-m_0(X'))]
\]
and the two Hölder assumptions imply the sharper range bound
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_B(e,m_0)|
 \le B_h:=\frac{L^2}{4}d^{(\alpha+\beta)/2}h^{\alpha+\beta}.          \tag{18}
\]
Indeed, each conditional range is at most \(Ld^{s/2}h^s\), and the covariance of two variables is at most one quarter of the product of their ranges. The propensity margin gives \(3/16\le v_B\le1/4\). Put
\[
 t_B=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A,Y\mid X\in B)}{v_B}
     =\frac{\mathbb E_B(e\tau)}{e_B}
      +\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_B(e,m_0)}{v_B}.                       \tag{19}
\]
The first expression in (19) is also the difference of the two arm-specific outcome means conditional on the bin. The outcome margins therefore imply \(t_B\in I\). For every \(x\in B\), (18)--(19) and CATE smoothness give
\[
 |t_B-\tau(x)|\le E_h
 :=Ld^{\gamma/2}h^\gamma+\frac{16}{3}B_h.                            \tag{20}
\]
Define
\[
 Q_h(c)=J^{-1}\sum_{B\in\mathcal B_m}g_B(c)^2,
 \qquad q_h=\min_{c\in I}Q_h(c).
\]
Since \(g_B(c)=v_B(t_B-c)\), the distance-to-constants map is one-Lipschitz in \(L^2(\lambda)\). Applying this fact to the step function equal to \(t_B\) on \(B\), and then using (20), yields
\[
 q_h\ge\left(\frac3{16}\right)^2\{D(P)-E_h\}_+^2.                    \tag{21}
\]
Under a null with true constant \(c_0\), (17)--(18) give \(Q_h(c_0)\le B_h^2\).

We now analyze the statistic in \(\texttt{def:single-scale-fourth-order-test}\). Write \(V(c)=Y-cA\). For two independent observations conditional on the same bin,
\[
 \mathbb E[(A_1-A_2)\{V_1(c)-V_2(c)\}\mid X_1,X_2\in B]=2g_B(c).
\]
Independence of the two pairs and uniform bin probability therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_PU_{n,m}(c)
 =J^3\sum_BJ^{-4}\frac{(2g_B(c))^2}{4}=Q_h(c).                       \tag{22}
\]

We next establish the variance bound used by the fixed threshold. Let \(H_c\) be the symmetrized order-four kernel. If \(O=(x,a,y)\), \(x\in B\), \(z=y-ca\), and \(r_B=\mathbb E_B(Y-cA)\), direct conditioning of any of the three pairings gives
\[
 \mathbb E[H_c\mid O]
 =\frac{g_B(c)}2\left[(a-e_B)(z-r_B)+g_B(c)\right].                  \tag{23}
\]
The range of \(Y-cA\) is at most \(3/2\). Also, by (19), \(|g_B(c)|=v_B|t_B-c|\le1/4\). Hence the squared norm of the centered first projection is no larger than the second moment of (23), which is at most \(Q_h(c)\).

For \(r=2,3,4\), the conditional expectation of \(H_c\) given \(r\) observations vanishes unless their covariates share a bin. Its absolute value is at most \(J^{r-1}\), because the remaining \(4-r\) observations must enter that bin and the bounded unsmoothed factor has absolute value below one. The shared-bin event has probability \(J^{-(r-1)}\), so its squared norm is at most \(J^{r-1}\). To make the variance accounting explicit, define the canonical projections recursively by subtracting from each conditional expectation all lower-order projections. Conditional mean zero makes projections indexed by distinct subsets orthogonal. Expanding the average of \(H_c\) into these projections gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{U_{n,m}(c)\}
 =\sum_{r=1}^4\frac{\binom4r^2}{\binom nr}\lVert h_{r,c}\rVert_2^2.
\]
The recursive orthogonal decomposition also shows that \(\lVert h_{r,c}\rVert_2^2\) is at most the squared norm of the corresponding conditional expectation. For \(n\ge8\), the preceding bounds and elementary estimates on the four binomial coefficients give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\{U_{n,m}(c)\}
 \le1024\left\{\frac{Q_h(c)}n+\frac{J}{n^2}
 +\frac{J^2}{n^3}+\frac{J^3}{n^4}\right\}.                           \tag{24}
\]
Let \(V_{n,J}=J/n^2+J^2/n^3+J^3/n^4\). Under a null, evaluate (24) at \(c_0\), use \(Q_h(c_0)\le B_h^2\), and note that \(\min_cU_{n,m}(c)\le U_{n,m}(c_0)\). Chebyshev's inequality at the threshold in the definition then gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal H_0}\mathbb P_P\{\phi_{n,m}^{(4)}=1\}
 \le\frac{1024(B_h^2/n+V_{n,J})}{20480(B_h^2/n+V_{n,J})}
 =a_0.                                                               \tag{25}
\]
For \(n<8\), the test is identically zero, so (25) remains true.

It remains to prove uniform power despite profiling. The population quadratic \(Q_h(c)=J^{-1}\sum_Bv_B^2(t_B-c)^2\) has curvature
\[
 q_2=J^{-1}\sum_Bv_B^2\ge\frac9{256}
\]
and its unconstrained minimizer \(c_*=(\sum_Bv_B^2t_B)/(\sum_Bv_B^2)\) lies in \(I\). Thus \(Q_h'(c_*)=0\) and \(Q_h(c_*)=q_h\). Write \(U_2\) for the quadratic coefficient of \(U_{n,m}\) and \(S=U_{n,m}'(c_*)/2\). The coefficient kernel for \(U_2\) and the derivative kernel for \(S\) have the same shared-bin support and uniformly bounded factors as \(H_c\). Repeating the overlap decomposition above, now using \(\mathbb ES=0\) and \(\mathbb EU_2=q_2\), gives a universal \(C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(S)+\operatorname{Var}(U_2)
 \le C_0\{n^{-1}+V_{n,J}\}.                                        \tag{26}
\]
The empirical curvature is nonnegative term by term. On the event \(U_2\ge q_2/2\), completing the square over the real line, and then restricting to \(I\), gives
\[
 \min_{c\in I}U_{n,m}(c)
 \ge U_{n,m}(c_*)-\frac{S^2}{U_2}.                                  \tag{27}
\]
Equations (24), (26), Chebyshev's inequality, and a union bound show that, for all sufficiently large \(n\) with \(V_{n,J}\to0\), an event of probability at least \(4/5\) satisfies
\[
 \min_{c\in I}U_{n,m}(c)
 \ge q_h-C_2\left\{\sqrt{q_h/n}+\sqrt{V_{n,J}}+n^{-1}+V_{n,J}\right\}, \tag{28}
\]
where \(C_2\) is universal. Indeed, use fixed failure probability \(1/15\) for each of \(U_{n,m}(c_*)\), \(S\), and \(U_2\); the last event has that probability for large \(n\) because \(q_2\ge9/256\).

Because \(V_{n,J}\ge n^{-2}\), (21) and (28) imply that a sufficiently large constant \(C_3\), depending only on the fixed class parameters, makes the rejection probability at least \(4/5\) whenever
\[
 D(P)\ge C_3\{E_h+V_{n,J}^{1/4}\}.                                  \tag{29}
\]
To see that (29) also dominates the deterministic threshold, note that \(E_h\ge(16/3)B_h\) and
\[
 \sqrt{B_h^2/n+V_{n,J}}
 \le B_h/\sqrt n+\sqrt{V_{n,J}}
 \le\tfrac12(B_h^2+n^{-1})+\sqrt{V_{n,J}}.
\]
Thus (21) makes \(q_h\) a sufficiently large multiple of \(B_h^2+\sqrt{V_{n,J}}\), which both absorbs the error in (28) and exceeds the threshold in (25).

Set \(t=\min\{\gamma,\alpha+\beta\}\). Since \(E_h\le C_4m^{-t}\), choose deterministically
\[
 m=\left\lceil n^{2/(4t+d)}\right\rceil
 \quad\text{if }d\le4t,
 \qquad
 m=\left\lceil n^{4/(4t+3d)}\right\rceil
 \quad\text{if }d>4t.                                               \tag{30}
\]
In the first case \(J\lesssim n\), so \(V_{n,J}\asymp J/n^2\), and (29)--(30) give
\[
 E_h+V_{n,J}^{1/4}\lesssim n^{-2t/(4t+d)}.
\]
In the second case \(J\gtrsim n\), so \(V_{n,J}\asymp J^3/n^4\), and
\[
 E_h+V_{n,J}^{1/4}\lesssim n^{-4t/(4t+3d)}.
\]
At \(d=4t\), both calculations give \(m\asymp n^{1/(4t)}\), \(J\asymp n\), and rate \(n^{-1/4}\), without a logarithm. In both cases \(V_{n,J}\to0\), validating the large-\(n\) step in (28).

Finally, the quadratic coefficients are total finite-data maps. Store the four counts \(N_{B,a,y}\) in each bin. For each of the fixed \(4^4\) ordered category patterns, the number of distinct-index assignments is the product of the corresponding falling factorials. Summing its quadratic kernel value and dividing by \((n)_4\) gives exactly the ordered U-statistic; empty and low-occupancy bins contribute zero. This costs \(O(n+J)\) arithmetic operations and \(O(J)\) memory. Checking the two endpoints of \(I\) and the stationary point when the nonnegative curvature is positive computes the exact profiled minimum, including the zero-curvature case. This completes the size, power, tuning, and cost assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This explicit envelope demonstrates feasibility in every occupancy regime while keeping its possible sparse-bin suboptimality visible. \textit{Gap.} NeykovBalakrishnanWasserman2021 uses binned degenerate statistics for conditional independence, but not a profiled fourth-order CATE energy with separate propensity and baseline smoothness. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition provides a directly implementable conservative detector while the sharp multiresolution frontier is derived.

\begin{theorem}[thm:verified-no-penalty-region]\label{thm:verified-no-penalty-region}
If \(\alpha+\beta\ge\gamma\) and \(d\le4\gamma\), then constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) and \(n_0\), depending only on \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L\), satisfy \(c r_{\mathrm{or}}(n)\le\rho_n^*\le C r_{\mathrm{or}}(n)\) for every \(n\ge n_0\). In particular, \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=1/10\), and \(\beta=9/10\) yield \(\rho_n^*\asymp n^{-2/5}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(\alpha+\beta\ge\gamma\) and \(d\le4\gamma\). Then the exponent in \(\texttt{prop:single-scale-upper}\) uses \(t=\min\{\gamma,\alpha+\beta\}=\gamma\) and its dense case applies. The explicit test proved there and the definition of \(\rho_n^*\) give
\[
 \rho_n^*\le C n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}=C r_{\mathrm{or}}(n).        \tag{31}
\]
Conversely, every observational test \(\phi(O^n)\) induces a known-propensity test \((O^n,e_P)\mapsto\phi(O^n)\) by ignoring the supplied function. Its rejection probabilities are unchanged on every law in the common null and alternative classes. Hence the feasible set of known-propensity tests contains the observational feasible set and
\[
 \rho_{n,e}^*\le\rho_n^*.                                           \tag{32}
\]
The lower half of \(\texttt{lem:known-propensity-benchmark}\), followed by (32), yields
\[
 c r_{\mathrm{or}}(n)\le\rho_{n,e}^*\le\rho_n^*.                    \tag{33}
\]
Combining (31) and (33) proves the two-sided assertion. For \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=1/10\), and \(\beta=9/10\), the hypotheses hold with equality in \(\alpha+\beta\ge\gamma\), and \(2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)=2/5\), proving the stated example.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The asymmetric example proves that a first-order nuisance product condition is not the operative no-penalty mechanism. \textit{Gap.} Hahn1998 proves propensity ancillarity for regular ATE efficiency; no prior result gives this nonregular testing equality region. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem identifies a broad regime where an observational implementation pays no rate cost relative to receiving the true assignment law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:test-optimization-not-sharp]\label{thm:test-optimization-not-sharp}

 =\left(\frac{2^8\Lambda_n}{n}\right)^{1/2}

All bias and stochastic summands are nonnegative. Therefore

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:kbrw-partition-mixture-hellinger]\label{lem:kbrw-partition-mixture-hellinger}

\begin{lemma}[lem:four-atom-cross-fine-fifth-projection-corrected]\label{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-fifth-projection-corrected}

The exact fifth canonical projection of the null-minus-alternative prior-predictive likelihood ratio is
\[
 \delta^4\mathcal L_{12}\kappa_3\kappa_4\kappa_5.
 \tag{23}
\]
It is not identically zero, and its contribution to the squared \(L^2(P_\star^{\otimes n})\) norm is at least
\[
 c(n)_5h^dH^{3d}\delta^8.
 \tag{24}
\]
At \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), \(h\asymp\delta^5\), \(H\asymp\delta\), and \(\delta=\epsilon n^{-3/10}\) with fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the right side of (24) has order \(\epsilon^{16}n^{1/5}\) and diverges.  Hence this four-atom construction cannot have a support-controlled squared-likelihood connected remainder of order \(O(\epsilon^4)\) at the proposed \(n^{-3/10}\) separation.  This conclusion concerns that fixed-reference \(L^2\) comparison and does not rule out a sign-conditioned Hellinger comparison using the common predictive marginal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(S\subseteq\{3,4,5\}\), equation (21) in \(\texttt{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-third-projection-corrected}\) and conditional independence across distinct fine coordinates give
\[
 F_{12,S}=\mathbb E_{\sigma_Q}\left[
 (r\mathcal L_{12}-r^2\mathcal Q_{12})
 \prod_{i\in S}(1+\kappa_ir)\right],
 \qquad r=\delta\sigma_Q.
\]
Every marginal difference omitting observation \(1\) or \(2\) is zero.  The fifth canonical projection is therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{S\subseteq\{3,4,5\}}(-1)^{3-|S|}F_{12,S}
 &=\mathbb E_{\sigma_Q}\left[
 (r\mathcal L_{12}-r^2\mathcal Q_{12})
 \prod_{i=3}^5\{(1+\kappa_ir)-1\}\right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{\sigma_Q}\left[
 r^4\mathcal L_{12}\kappa_3\kappa_4\kappa_5
 -r^5\mathcal Q_{12}\kappa_3\kappa_4\kappa_5\right]\\
 &=\delta^4\mathcal L_{12}\kappa_3\kappa_4\kappa_5,
 \end{aligned}
\]
because the macro sign is Rademacher.  This proves (23).

If all five binary rows equal \((1,1)\), then
\[
 \mathcal L_{12}=8B_1B_2\{1-2q_e^2(B_1^2+B_2^2)\},
 \qquad \kappa_i=4B_i^2.
\]
As \(C_b\geq1\), the corrected hypothesis \(C_bq_e\leq1/4\) implies \(q_e\leq1/4\), so the expression in braces is at least \(3/4\).  On the bump cores the displayed product is bounded away from zero.  The binary event has fixed positive reference probability.  The number of ordered choices of the repeated fine cube and three distinct singleton cubes in one macro cube is
\[
 H^{-d}(H/h)^d\{(H/h)^d-1\}\{(H/h)^d-2\}\{(H/h)^d-3\}
 \asymp H^{3d}h^{-4d}.
\]
Integration over the five cores contributes \(h^{5d}\), so the squared norm of the fifth canonical kernel is at least \(ch^dH^{3d}\delta^8\).  Orthogonality across distinct five-element sample-coordinate subsets and summation over those subsets prove (24).  At the stress scaling,
\[
 (n)_5hH^3\delta^8\asymp n^5\delta^{16}
 =\epsilon^{16}n^{1/5}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The asserted fixed-reference conclusion follows.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This preserves the exact fixed-reference fifth-projection diagnostic under the corrected synthesis margins. \textit{Gap.} Its divergent squared-likelihood norm is specific to expansion around the fixed interior reference. \textit{Consumer.} The complete-frontier question retains it as a warning against fixed-reference chi-square proofs.

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-common-marginal-hellinger]\label{lem:conditional-common-marginal-hellinger}
Under the corrected four-atom construction at \(d=\gamma=1\) and \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), choose a locally finite smooth squared partition with a fixed overlap multiplicity, set \(q_e=q_b=2\delta^{1/2}\), and take integer meshes \(h\asymp\delta^5\) and \(H\asymp\delta\).  Let \(\bar P_{0,\sigma}^{(n)}\) and \(\bar P_{1,\sigma}^{(n)}\) be the null and alternative prior-predictive laws conditional on all macro signs, and let \(\bar P_k^{(n)}=\int\bar P_{k,\sigma}^{(n)}\,d\Pi(\sigma)\).  There are constants \(C<\infty\), \(\epsilon_0>0\), and \(n_0(\epsilon)\), depending only on the fixed bumps and \(L\), such that, for \(0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_0\), \(\delta=\epsilon n^{-3/10}\), and \(n\geq n_0(\epsilon)\),
\[
 H^2(\bar P_0^{(n)},\bar P_1^{(n)})
 \leq Cn^2h\delta^2
 \leq C\{n^2h\delta^4+n^3hH\delta^4+n^4h^2H\delta^4\}
 \leq C\epsilon^{10}.
 \tag{25}
\]
The first upper bound is the additional term left by conditioning and Hellinger convexity.  It equals \(O(\epsilon^7n^{-1/10})\), so it is negligible at this scaling, although it is not uniformly dominated by the right side of (25) for arbitrary unrelated \((n,h,H,\delta)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the macro signs.  By equation (18) of \(\texttt{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-third-projection-corrected}\), the null and alternative one-observation predictive densities, relative to the interior reference law, coincide and equal
\[
 \bar \ell_\sigma(x,a,y)
 =1+s_e(a)s_b(a,y)\delta\sigma_{Q(x)}G_{Q(x)}(x).
 \tag{26}
\]
The score product has absolute value at most \(16/3\).  Since \(0\leq G_Q\leq1\) and \(\delta\leq1/64\),
\[
 \frac{11}{12}\leq\bar\ell_\sigma\leq\frac{13}{12}.
 \tag{27}
\]
Thus every Radon--Nikodym denominator below is bounded away from zero.  Equivalently, the original margins imply that each conditional binary cell probability in every component is at least \(1/16\); hence the ratio of the common marginal density to any component density is at most \(9\).

Partition the covariate line into intervals \((R_r)\) of lengths at most a fixed multiple of \(h\), refining the supports of the locally finite squared partition.  On each \(R_r\), at most \(K_0<\infty\) fine-coordinate pairs are active.  Couple, independently over fine coordinates, a draw from the correlated null four-atom law and a draw from the independent alternative four-atom law.  Exact uniform design gives
\[
 p_r=P(X\in R_r)=\lambda(R_r),
 \qquad \max_rp_r\leq C_0h,
 \qquad \sum_rp_r=1.
 \tag{28}
\]

Write \(p_\lambda\) for a null component density and \(q_\lambda\) for the coupled alternative component density, conditional on the fixed macro signs.  The amplitude choices, the fixed overlap multiplicity, and the synthesis bounds give, pointwise on \(R_r\),
\[
 |p_\lambda-\bar p_\sigma|
 \leq C_1\delta^{1/2},
 \qquad
 |q_\lambda-p_\lambda|
 \leq C_1\delta^{1/2},
 \tag{29}
\]
where the densities are taken with respect to Lebesgue measure times counting measure and the constant uses only the fixed cell probabilities and bumps.  To verify (29) directly, expand (16)--(17): every term that varies with a fine atom contains at least one of \(u\) or \(v\), whose absolute values are bounded by \(C_bq_e\) and \(C_bq_b\), and all remaining factors are bounded by (12); the deterministic macro adjustment is \(O(\delta)\).  Since every component cell probability is at least \(1/16\), squaring (29), summing over the four binary cells, integrating over \(R_r\), and dividing by \(p_r\) yields
\[
 \delta_1\leq C_2\delta,
 \qquad \delta_2\leq C_2\delta.
 \tag{30}
\]
Equation (26) gives \(\bar p_\sigma=\bar q_\sigma\), so \(\delta_3=0\).  Equations (27)--(28) also give \(\bar p_\sigma/p_\lambda\leq9\) and, because \(nh\asymp n\delta^5=\epsilon^5n^{-1/2}\),
\[
 np_r\max(1,\delta_1,\delta_2)\leq9
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), after enlarging the harmless fixed constant in the cited lemma.

Equations (28)--(30) fully instantiate the numerical part of \(\texttt{lem:kbrw-partition-mixture-hellinger}\).  A literal application to the intervals \(R_r\), however, would be invalid: on a smooth transition interval one observation depends on the atoms attached to two adjacent fine coordinates, so the lemma's coordinate-locality premise is not satisfied by declaring duplicated local parameters.  We now prove the required bounded-overlap extension directly.

Fix \(X_1=x_1,\ldots,X_n=x_n\).  Let \(N(i)\) be the set of fine coordinates whose bumps are nonzero at \(x_i\), and join \(i\) and \(j\) when \(N(i)\cap N(j)\neq\varnothing\).  Conditional on the macro signs, the fine-coordinate atoms are independent.  Hence the null and alternative predictive laws of the binary observations factor over the connected components of this graph.  A singleton component has the same law under both priors by (26).

Consider a connected component \(C\) of size \(k\geq2\).  Expand the exact component likelihoods (16)--(17).  Under the independent alternative atom law, a moment \(\mathbb E_1(U^aV^b)\) vanishes unless both \(a\) and \(b\) are even.  Under the correlated null atom law,
\[
 \mathbb E_0(U^aV^b\mid\sigma_Q)-\mathbb E_1(U^aV^b)
 =\begin{cases}
 \vartheta\sigma_Qq_e^aq_b^b,&a\text{ and }b\text{ are odd},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{31}
\]
When it is nonzero, the absolute value in (31) is at most \(\vartheta q_eq_b=\delta\), because \(q_e,q_b\leq1\).  Every term introduced by the deterministic alternative adjustment contains a factor \(\delta G\).  Thus every monomial in the difference of the two \(k\)-observation predictive cell probabilities contains a factor of absolute value at most \(\delta\).  Each observation contributes only a fixed number of bounded score and bump factors, because the overlap multiplicity is \(K_0\).  Therefore, for every binary cell and fixed covariates,
\[
 |r_{0,C}-r_{1,C}|\leq C_3^k\delta.
 \tag{32}
\]
The original margins give \(r_{1,C}\geq16^{-k}\), and there are \(4^k\) binary cells.  Consequently
\[
 H^2(r_{0,C},r_{1,C})
 \leq\chi^2(r_{0,C},r_{1,C})
 \leq C_4^k\delta^2.
 \tag{33}
\]
For product laws, \(H^2(\bigotimes_C R_{0,C},\bigotimes_C R_{1,C})\leq\sum_CH^2(R_{0,C},R_{1,C})\), because the Hellinger affinities multiply and \(1-\prod_Ca_C\leq\sum_C(1-a_C)\) for \(0\leq a_C\leq1\).

It remains to average (33) over the exact uniform design.  Local support and fixed overlap imply that adjacent observations in the graph have covariates at distance at most \(C_5h\).  For a specified set of \(k\) sample indices to be connected, one of its at most \(k^{k-2}\) labeled spanning trees must have every edge this short; the tree count follows by the injective Pr\"ufer deletion code into the \(k^{k-2}\) sequences of length \(k-2\).  Conditioning successively along a rooted tree and using uniform design gives probability at most \((C_6h)^{k-1}\).  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_X\sum_{C:\,|C|\geq2}C_4^{|C|}\delta^2
 &\leq\delta^2\sum_{k=2}^n\binom nk C_4^k k^{k-2}(C_6h)^{k-1}\\
 &\leq C_7n^2h\delta^2\sum_{k=2}^n(C_8nh)^{k-2}
 \leq C_9n^2h\delta^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{34}
\]
For the second inequality, \(\binom nk\leq n^k/k!\) and \(k^{k-2}/k!\leq e^k\); the latter follows from \(\sum_{j=1}^k\log j\geq\int_1^k\log x\,dx=k\log k-k+1\).  The last inequality uses \(nh\asymp\epsilon^5n^{-1/2}\to0\).  Since the law of \(X^n\) is the same under both predictives, (33)--(34) prove, uniformly in the macro signs,
\[
 H^2(\bar P_{0,\sigma}^{(n)},\bar P_{1,\sigma}^{(n)})
 \leq Cn^2h\delta^2.
 \tag{35}
\]
Squared Hellinger distance is jointly convex: pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(\sqrt{(\int p_\sigma d\Pi)(\int q_\sigma d\Pi)}
\geq\int\sqrt{p_\sigma q_\sigma}\,d\Pi\), and integration proves the claim.  Averaging (35) over the common outer-sign prior therefore proves the first inequality in (25), with no further term.

At the prescribed scaling,
\[
 n^2h\delta^2\asymp\epsilon^7n^{-1/10},
 \quad
 n^2h\delta^4\asymp\epsilon^9n^{-7/10},
 \quad
 n^3hH\delta^4\asymp\epsilon^{10},
 \quad
 n^4h^2H\delta^4\asymp\epsilon^{15}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{36}
\]
Moreover \(nH\delta^2\asymp\epsilon^3n^{1/10}\to\infty\), so \(n^2h\delta^2\leq Cn^3hH\delta^4\) for \(n\geq n_0(\epsilon)\).  Equations (35)--(36) give the remaining inequalities in (25).  This also explains why the divergent fixed-reference fifth projection is absent: the sign-dependent common singleton likelihood (26) is the dominating marginal before the bounded-overlap comparison, rather than a factor expanded around \(P_\star\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Conditioning on the macro signs and normalizing by the exact common one-observation marginal bypasses the divergent fixed-reference odd-singleton attachments. \textit{Gap.} The calculation is at the mandated rough stress tuple and does not optimize a lower map over all smoothness regimes. \textit{Consumer.} The sharper rough-tuple fixed-sample minimax lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rough-tuple-null-supported-lower]\label{thm:rough-tuple-null-supported-lower}
For \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), and every fixed \(L\geq2\), there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\), depending only on \(L\) and the fixed bumps, such that
\[
 \rho_n^*\geq c n^{-3/10}
 \qquad\text{for every }n\geq n_0.
 \tag{33}
\]
Every component of the null prior used for (33) has exactly \(\tau\equiv1/8\), every alternative component belongs to the original binary exact-uniform-design class and has \(D(P)\geq c n^{-3/10}\), and the comparison is in the original fixed-\(n\) experiment.  Consequently, at this tuple,
\[
 c n^{-3/10}\leq\rho_n^*\leq Cn^{-4/19},
 \qquad
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}\geq c n^{1/10},
 \tag{34}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sufficiently small constant \(\epsilon>0\), put \(\delta=\epsilon n^{-3/10}\), choose the legal integer meshes and amplitudes from \(\texttt{lem:conditional-common-marginal-hellinger}\), and use the full causal laws constructed in \(\texttt{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-third-projection-corrected}\).  That lemma verifies, equation by equation, exact uniform design, consistency, conditional exchangeability, the three Hölder restrictions, overlap, both binary-regression margins, and componentwise \(\tau_0\equiv1/8\).  It also gives a constant \(c_0>0\), independent of \(n\), such that every alternative component satisfies
\[
 D(P_1)\geq c_0\delta=c_0\epsilon n^{-3/10}.
 \tag{35}
\]

Let \(\bar P_0^{(n)}\) and \(\bar P_1^{(n)}\) be the two fixed-\(n\) prior-predictive laws.  By (25), after choosing \(\epsilon\) small enough and then \(n_0\) large enough,
\[
 H^2(\bar P_0^{(n)},\bar P_1^{(n)})\leq\frac14.
\]
For the convention in (25), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P,Q)
 =\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)
 \leq H(P,Q),
\]
so the preceding display implies \(\operatorname{TV}(\bar P_0^{(n)},\bar P_1^{(n)})\leq1/2\).

For any measurable randomized test \(\phi\), the variational characterization of total variation, applied to \(0\leq\phi\leq1\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{\bar P_0^{(n)}}\phi
 +\mathbb E_{\bar P_1^{(n)}}(1-\phi)
 \geq1-\operatorname{TV}(\bar P_0^{(n)},\bar P_1^{(n)})
 \geq\frac12.
 \tag{36}
\]
If \(\phi\in\Phi_n\), every null-prior component is in \(\mathcal H_0\), so the first expectation in (36) is at most \(a_0=0.05\).  Hence the average alternative type-II error is at least \(0.45>b_0=0.20\).  By (35), this remains true for every requested separation \(\rho\leq c_0\epsilon n^{-3/10}\), since all prior alternatives then belong to \(\mathcal H_1(\rho)\).  The definition of \(\rho_n^*\) proves (33).

At this tuple \(t=\min(1,1/5)=1/5\) and \(d>4t\).  The already proved single-scale upper bound therefore gives exponent
\[
 \frac{4t}{4t+3d}=\frac{4/5}{4/5+3}=\frac4{19},
\]
which proves the upper inequality in (34).  The known-propensity benchmark gives \(\rho_{n,e}^*\leq Cn^{-2/5}\); dividing (33) by that upper bound proves the ratio lower bound in (34).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is an original fixed-\(n\), all-test converse on the unchanged exact-uniform binary class, with every null-prior component exactly homogeneous. \textit{Gap.} It closes one rough tuple but not the complete smoothness frontier or the matching upper exponent there. \textit{Consumer.} The complete-frontier question and the later known-versus-unknown propensity comparison.

\begin{proposition}[prop:pair-factor-omnibus-blind-alternative]\label{prop:pair-factor-omnibus-blind-alternative}
At \(d=\gamma=1\) and \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), fix nested integer meshes \(h\asymp\delta^5\) and \(H\asymp\delta\).  There is a legal deterministic alternative \(P_\delta\in\mathcal P(1/10,1/10,1,L,1)\), for every sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), with \(D(P_\delta)\asymp\delta\), and a constant \(c=1/8\), such that for every fine bin \(B\),
\[
 g_B(c):=\operatorname{Cov}_{P_\delta}(A,Y\mid X\in B)
          -c\operatorname{Var}_{P_\delta}(A\mid X\in B)=0.
 \tag{37}
\]
Consequently every proposed nested \(2+1\) or cross-fine \(2+2\) population kernel that contains a within-bin factor
\[
 Z_{ij}(c)=\frac{(A_i-A_j)\{Y_i-cA_i-Y_j+cA_j\}}2
\]
and multiplies it only by functions of disjoint observations and deterministic bin labels has expectation zero under \(P_\delta\).  Thus those pair-factor signals do not furnish the deterministic alternative dichotomy needed for a matching \(n^{-3/10}\) upper bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the fine bins be the intervals of length \(h\).  In each bin \(B\), choose a translated and rescaled \(C^\infty\) function \(\psi_B\), compactly supported in the interior of \(B\), with
\[
 \mathbb E_B\psi_B=0,
 \qquad \mathbb E_B\psi_B^2=\mu_\psi>0,
 \qquad \|\psi_B\|_\infty\leq1.
\]
Choose \(H^{-1}\) integral and put \(G_H(x)=\sin(2\pi x/H)\), so \(\int_0^1G_H=0\) and \(\int_0^1G_H^2=1/2\).  Let \(q=k\delta^{1/2}\), where \(k>0\) is fixed, and define
\[
 v_B=-\frac{\delta\,\mathbb E_B G_H}{q\mu_\psi},
 \qquad
 e=\frac12+q\sum_B\psi_B,
\]
\[
 m_0=\frac38+\sum_Bv_B\psi_B-2\delta G_H,
 \qquad
 \tau=\frac18+4\delta G_H.
 \tag{38}
\]
For \(X\in B\), the disjoint supports imply \(e_B=\mathbb E_Be=1/2\).  The exact binned identity gives
\[
 g_B(c)=\operatorname{Cov}_B(e,m_0)+(1-e_B)\mathbb E_B[e(\tau-c)].
\]
For completeness, this identity follows directly from
\[
 \mathbb E_B(AY)=\mathbb E_B\{e(m_0+\tau)\},
 \quad
 \mathbb E_BA=e_B,
 \quad
 \mathbb E_BY=\mathbb E_Bm_0+\mathbb E_B(e\tau),
 \quad
 \operatorname{Var}_B(A)=e_B(1-e_B),
\]
by expanding \(\operatorname{Cov}_B(A,Y)-c\operatorname{Var}_B(A)\) and collecting the \(m_0\) and \(\tau-c\) terms.
Using \(\mathbb E_B\psi_B=0\) in (38), the two terms are
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_B(e,m_0)
 =qv_B\mu_\psi-2q\delta\mathbb E_B(\psi_BG_H),
\]
and
\[
 (1-e_B)\mathbb E_B[e(\tau-c)]
 =\delta\mathbb E_BG_H+2q\delta\mathbb E_B(\psi_BG_H).
\]
Their sum is \(qv_B\mu_\psi+\delta\mathbb E_BG_H=0\) by the definition of \(v_B\), proving (37).

The rescaled bump bounds give \([\sum_B\psi_B]_{1/10}\leq C h^{-1/10}\).  Since \(|v_B|\leq\delta/(q\mu_\psi)=C\delta^{1/2}\), the two fine-scale Hölder seminorms in (38) are bounded by a constant times \(\delta^{1/2}h^{-1/10}\), which is constant when \(h\asymp\delta^5\).  Also \([\delta G_H]_1\leq2\pi\delta/H\), and its \(1/10\)-Hölder seminorm is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\delta H^{-1/10}\).  By choosing the fixed proportionality constants in \(h\asymp\delta^5\) and \(H\asymp\delta\), and then taking \(\delta\) small, the propensity, baseline, and CATE seminorms are at most \(L\); their supremum norms are at most \(L\) as well.  The perturbations in (38) converge uniformly to zero, so all three pointwise margins hold for sufficiently small \(\delta\).  Construct binary potential outcomes conditionally independently of treatment given \(X\), exactly as in the corrected diagnostic lemma, to obtain consistency, exchangeability, and exact uniform design.  Hence \(P_\delta\) is in the original class.  Finally,
\[
 D(P_\delta)=4\delta\left(\int_0^1G_H^2\right)^{1/2}
 =2\sqrt2\,\delta.
\]

For two iid observations conditioned to lie in the same fine bin, direct symmetrization gives \(\mathbb E[Z_{ij}(c)\mid X_i,X_j\in B]=g_B(c)=0\).  A multiplier depending only on disjoint observations and deterministic bin labels is conditionally independent of \((A_i,Y_i,A_j,Y_j)\) given those labels.  Iterated expectation and (37) therefore make every stated \(2+1\) or \(2+2\) population kernel zero.  Since \(D(P_\delta)>0\), no dichotomy based only on those means can be valid.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This supplies the exact blind alternative required before claiming a deterministic dichotomy for the proposed pair-factor omnibus upper test. \textit{Gap.} It refutes only the stated pair-factor population architecture, not all possible multiresolution tests. \textit{Consumer.} The complete-frontier question uses it to isolate the remaining upper-side obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sparse-nuisance-null-supported-lower]\label{thm:sparse-nuisance-null-supported-lower}
Let \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\).  If \(d\geq 2s_0\), then there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\), depending only on \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L\) and fixed bump shapes, such that
\[
 \rho_n^*\geq c\max\left\{
 n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)},
 n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}
 \right\}
\]
for every \(n\geq n_0\).  The second term is certified by original fixed-\(n\) priors on the unchanged binary exact-uniform-design class: every component of the null prior has exactly \(\tau\equiv1/8\), and every component of the alternative prior has centered \(L^2(\lambda)\) separation bounded below by a constant multiple of \(n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}\).  There is no logarithmic loss in this converse at \(d=2s_0\).  In particular, when \((d,\gamma,\alpha,\beta)=(1,1,1/10,1/10)\),
\[
 c n^{-2/7}\leq\rho_n^*\leq Cn^{-4/19},
 \qquad
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}\geq c n^{4/35}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\).  We first construct the nuisance lower bound.  Fix the bounded-overlap tensor-product bump system used in \(\texttt{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-third-projection-corrected}\).  Such a system can be obtained by taking a nonnegative smooth function that is positive on a smaller cube, normalizing its integer translates to a locally finite squared partition of unity, and multiplying the fine functions in each macro cube by a smooth macro cutoff.  If the cutoff is denoted by \(g_Q\), then the resulting fine functions satisfy \(G_Q=\sum_{j\subset Q}B_j^2=g_Q^2\).  Local finiteness gives a fixed overlap constant, rescaling gives the synthesis inequalities in equation (1) of that lemma, and the region where one normalized translate is bounded below supplies the required fine-cell cores.  All constants in what follows may depend on this fixed system and on \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L\), but not on \(n\).

Fix a sufficiently small constant \(\epsilon>0\) and set
\[
 \delta_n=\epsilon n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)},
 \qquad
 q_e=2\delta_n^{\alpha/s_0},
 \qquad
 q_b=2\delta_n^{\beta/s_0}.
\]
Then \(q_eq_b=4\delta_n\), so the four-atom correlation parameter in equation (2) of the cited construction is
\[
 \vartheta=\frac{\delta_n}{q_eq_b}=\frac14.
\]
In particular all four atom probabilities are nonnegative.  Choose dyadic mesh widths \(h_n,H_n\) as follows.  For a fixed sufficiently large \(K_h\), take the smallest dyadic number satisfying
\[
 h_n\geq K_h\delta_n^{1/s_0}.
\]
For a fixed sufficiently large \(K_H\), take the smallest dyadic number satisfying
\[
 H_n\geq\max\{4h_n,K_H\delta_n^{1/\gamma}\}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), both widths are at most one, their reciprocals are integers, and \(H_n/h_n\) is an integer at least four.  Dyadic rounding changes either displayed scale by a factor smaller than two.  Hence
\[
 h_n\asymp\delta_n^{1/s_0},
 \qquad
 H_n\asymp\max\{h_n,\delta_n^{1/\gamma}\}.
\]

We verify every construction constraint.  The fine-scale Hölder quantities obey
\[
 C_bq_eh_n^{-\alpha}\leq2C_bK_h^{-\alpha},
 \qquad
 C_bq_bh_n^{-\beta}\leq2C_bK_h^{-\beta}.
\]
Choose \(K_h\) so that both right sides are at most \(L/2\).  Since \(H_n\geq4h_n\) and \(s_0-\beta=\alpha>0\),
\[
 2C_b\delta_nH_n^{-\beta}
 \leq 2C_b\delta_n(4h_n)^{-\beta}
 \leq C\delta_n^{1-\beta/s_0}
 =C\delta_n^{\alpha/s_0}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The CATE correction satisfies
\[
 4C_b\delta_nH_n^{-\gamma}\leq4C_bK_H^{-\gamma},
\]
which is at most \(L\) after increasing \(K_H\).  Finally \(q_e,q_b,\delta_n\to0\), so for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 C_bq_e\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 C_bq_b+2\delta_n\leq\frac18.
\]
These are precisely the smoothness and margin inequalities (5) of \(\texttt{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-third-projection-corrected}\).  That lemma therefore proves that every induced law belongs to \(\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\), that every null component has \(\tau\equiv1/8\), and that every alternative component satisfies
\[
 c_D\delta_n\leq D(P_1)\leq C_D\delta_n
\]
for fixed positive constants \(c_D,C_D\).  Its construction also gives exact uniform design, consistency, conditional exchangeability, and the three pointwise interior boxes; these properties are thus componentwise, not merely properties of the prior predictives.

We next prove the fixed-sample comparison.  Condition on all macro signs \(\sigma=(\sigma_Q)\).  Under the null prior, the fine pairs are independent across fine coordinates and have the correlated four-atom law in equation (2) of the cited construction; under the alternative prior they are independent across fine coordinates and within each pair.  For nonnegative integers \(r,t\), direct summation over the four atoms gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0(U^rV^t\mid\sigma_Q)-\mathbb E_1(U^rV^t)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \vartheta\sigma_Qq_e^rq_b^t
 =\delta_n\sigma_Qq_e^{r-1}q_b^{t-1},&r,t\text{ odd},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Because \(q_e,q_b\leq1\) eventually, every nonzero moment discrepancy has absolute value at most \(\delta_n\).  The exact one-observation matching assertion of \(\texttt{lem:four-atom-cross-fine-third-projection-corrected}\) says that, conditional on \(\sigma\), the two one-observation prior-predictive laws coincide.

All Radon--Nikodym denominators are uniformly controlled.  Indeed, in every component and for every \(x\), the propensity and both arm regressions lie in \([1/4,3/4]\).  Thus each of the four conditional masses of \((A,Y)\) given \(X=x\) is at least
\[
 \min\{e(x),1-e(x)\}\min\{m_A(x),1-m_A(x)\}\geq\frac1{16}.
\]
The same lower bound holds after mixing.  Consequently every \(k\)-observation conditional binary mass used below is at least \(16^{-k}\).

Fix \(X_1=x_1,\ldots,X_n=x_n\).  Let \(N(i)\) be the set of fine-coordinate atoms whose bumps are nonzero at \(x_i\), and join sample indices \(i,j\) when \(N(i)\cap N(j)\neq\varnothing\).  Conditional on \(\sigma\), independence of the fine atoms makes each of the two predictive laws factor over the connected components of this graph.  A singleton component contributes no discrepancy because of exact one-observation matching.

Consider a connected component \(C\) of size \(k\geq2\).  Expand its conditional binary mass as a polynomial in its active \(U\)'s, \(V\)'s and the deterministic alternative corrections \(\delta_nG_Q\).  The displayed moment identity shows that a coefficient changed by the atom law contains a factor bounded by \(\delta_n\); a coefficient changed by the deterministic macro correction also contains such a factor.  All remaining amplitudes, scores, and bumps are bounded by one or by a fixed constant, and bounded overlap makes the number of monomials at most exponential in \(k\).  A telescoping expansion therefore yields, for every binary cell,
\[
 |r_{0,C}-r_{1,C}|\leq C_0^k\delta_n.
\]
Using the denominator bound and summing over the \(4^k\) binary cells gives
\[
 H^2(r_{0,C},r_{1,C})
 \leq\chi^2(r_{0,C},r_{1,C})
 \leq C_1^k\delta_n^2.
\]
For product distributions the Hellinger affinities multiply, so \(1-\prod_j a_j\leq\sum_j(1-a_j)\), for \(0\leq a_j\leq1\), implies that the squared Hellinger distance of the conditional predictive laws is at most the sum of these component bounds.

It remains to average over the common exact-uniform design.  Fixed local support implies that two adjacent vertices have covariates at Euclidean distance at most \(C_2h_n\).  A specified set of \(k\) indices can be connected only if one of its \(k^{k-2}\) labeled spanning trees has every edge this short.  Rooting a tree and integrating its vertices successively under the uniform density bounds this event by
\[
 k^{k-2}(C_3h_n^d)^{k-1}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^2(\bar P_{0,\sigma}^{(n)},\bar P_{1,\sigma}^{(n)})
 &\leq
 \delta_n^2\sum_{k=2}^n {n\choose k}C_1^k k^{k-2}(C_3h_n^d)^{k-1}\\
 &\leq C_4n^2h_n^d\delta_n^2
 \sum_{k=2}^n(C_5nh_n^d)^{k-2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \({n\choose k}\leq n^k/k!\) and \(k^{k-2}/k!\leq e^k\), the latter following from \(\sum_{j=1}^k\log j\geq\int_1^k\log x\,dx\).

At the selected scale,
\[
 nh_n^d\asymp
 \epsilon^{d/s_0}n^{(2s_0-d)/(2s_0+d)}.
\]
If \(d>2s_0\), this tends to zero.  If \(d=2s_0\), it is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\epsilon^2\).  Thus, in either case, choosing \(\epsilon\) sufficiently small makes the geometric sum uniformly bounded for all sufficiently large \(n\).  We obtain
\[
 H^2(\bar P_{0,\sigma}^{(n)},\bar P_{1,\sigma}^{(n)})
 \leq C_6n^2h_n^d\delta_n^2
 \leq C_7\epsilon^{2+d/s_0}.
\]
This bound is uniform in \(\sigma\).  Squared Hellinger distance is jointly convex: pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \sqrt{\left(\int p_\sigma\,d\Pi\right)
              \left(\int q_\sigma\,d\Pi\right)}
 \geq\int\sqrt{p_\sigma q_\sigma}\,d\Pi.
\]
Integration therefore gives the same bound after averaging over the common outer-sign prior.  In particular, no additional outer-sign term remains.  Since total variation is at most the Hellinger distance under the convention used here, decreasing \(\epsilon\) once more ensures
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\bar P_0^{(n)},\bar P_1^{(n)})
 <1-a_0-b_0=\frac34.
\]

Every component in the null mixture belongs to \(\mathcal H_0\), so a test in \(\Phi_n\) has null-mixture rejection probability at most \(a_0\).  If that test had type-II error at most \(b_0\) for every law with \(D(P)\geq c_D\delta_n\), its alternative-mixture rejection probability would be at least \(1-b_0\).  The difference of the two rejection expectations would then be at least \(1-a_0-b_0=3/4\), contradicting the total-variation bound.  Hence no such test exists and
\[
 \rho_n^*\geq c_D\epsilon n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}.
\]
The argument is an original fixed-\(n\), all-test comparison and uses only priors supported on the original class.  At \(d=2s_0\) the small fixed value of \(\epsilon\), rather than a scale union, controls the geometric series, which proves the asserted absence of a logarithm in this converse.

For the other lower term, any observational test \(\phi(O^n)\) induces the known-propensity test \((O^n,e_P)\mapsto\phi(O^n)\) with identical rejection probabilities.  Thus the definition of the two experiments gives \(\rho_{n,e}^*\leq\rho_n^*\).  The lower half of \(\texttt{lem:known-propensity-benchmark}\) yields
\[
 \rho_n^*\geq\rho_{n,e}^*\geq c n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}.
\]
Combining the two inequalities proves the displayed maximum.

Finally take \(d=\gamma=1\) and \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), so \(s_0=1/5\).  Then
\[
 \delta_n=\epsilon n^{-2/7},
 \qquad
 q_e=q_b=2\sqrt{\delta_n},
 \qquad
 h_n\asymp\delta_n^5,
 \qquad
 H_n\asymp\delta_n.
\]
Consequently
\[
 nh_n\asymp\epsilon^5n^{-3/7}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{H_n}{h_n}\asymp\delta_n^{-4},
 \qquad
 H^2(\bar P_0^{(n)},\bar P_1^{(n)})\leq C\epsilon^7.
\]
The dyadic choice makes \(H_n/h_n\) an integer while preserving these comparisons, and the preceding constraint check verifies all Hölder, margin, exact-uniform-design, and componentwise constant-null requirements.  The known-propensity upper bound in \(\texttt{lem:known-propensity-benchmark}\) is \(\rho_{n,e}^*\leq Cn^{-2/5}\).  Division of the two positive bounds gives
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \geq c n^{2/5-2/7}=c n^{4/35},
\]
as claimed.  At the same tuple, \(t=\min\{\gamma,\alpha+\beta\}=1/5\) and \(d=1>4t\), so \(\texttt{prop:single-scale-upper}\) gives
\[
 \rho_n^*\leq Cn^{-4t/(4t+3d)}=Cn^{-4/19}.
\]
This completes the stress-point bracket.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem harvests the optimized sign-conditioned common-marginal comparison and identifies a computable nuisance lower map on the unchanged class. \textit{Gap.} It is a converse only and does not determine the dense-regime lower map or an attaining upper test. \textit{Consumer.} It strengthens the complete-frontier bracket and certifies a strict value of known propensity on an explicit rough parameter region.

\begin{lemma}[lem:full-cell-bin-residual-collapse]\label{lem:full-cell-bin-residual-collapse}
For \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\), let \(\mathcal B\) be a finite measurable partition of \(\mathcal X\) whose cells have positive \(\lambda\)-measure.  For \(B\in\mathcal B\), \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), and \(c\in I\), put
\[
 p_{ay,B}=P(A=a,Y=y\mid X\in B),\qquad
 e_B=p_{10,B}+p_{11,B},
\]
and define the full four-cell plug-in residual
\[
 r_B(c)=(1-e_B)p_{11,B}-e_Bp_{01,B}-c e_B(1-e_B).
\]
Then
\[
 r_B(c)=\operatorname{Cov}_P(A,Y\mid X\in B)
          -c\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B),                                      \tag{38}
\]
and, because \(e_B\in[1/4,3/4]\),
\[
 r_B(c)=e_B(1-e_B)
 \left\{\frac{p_{11,B}}{e_B}-\frac{p_{01,B}}{1-e_B}-c\right\}.                       \tag{39}
\]
Let \(\mathcal Q\) be a finite coefficient-label set.  For arbitrary deterministic weights \(q_{QB}\), \(Q\in\mathcal Q\), define projected coefficients and their energy by
\[
 \eta_Q(c)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}q_{QB}r_B(c),
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)=\sum_Q\eta_Q(c)^2.                                       \tag{40}
\]
At \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), and every fixed \(L\ge2\), the alternatives in \(\texttt{prop:pair-factor-omnibus-blind-alternative}\) satisfy, for their fine partition \(\mathcal B\) and \(c_0=1/8\),
\[
 D(P_\delta)\asymp\delta,
 \qquad r_B(c_0)=0\ \text{for every }B\in\mathcal B,
 \qquad \mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c_0)=0.                                               \tag{41}
\]
Write \(p_{ay}(x)=P(A=a,Y=y\mid X=x)\) for the conditional cell-probability versions induced by the canonical regressions.  Nevertheless, their pointwise residual
\[
 \mathcal R_{c_0}(x)=(1-e(x))p_{11}(x)-e(x)p_{01}(x)-c_0e(x)(1-e(x))
                    =e(x)(1-e(x))\{\tau(x)-c_0\}
\]
satisfies \(\|\mathcal R_{c_0}\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\ge(3/16)D(P_\delta)\).  Consequently, if a split-sample order-four full-cell tensor statistic \(T_{\mathcal B}(c)\), whether or not its displayed kernel is separable, satisfies \(\mathbb E_P[T_{\mathcal B}(c)]=\mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)\) for every \(P\) and \(c\), then \(\mathbb E_{P_\delta}[T_{\mathcal B}(c_0)]=0\) on this legal alternative.  With \(h\asymp\delta^5\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-2/7}\), this is exactly the proposed sparse nuisance scale.  Thus the full-cell plug-in projection architecture fails the deterministic-signal and masking-witness checks; this statement does not rule out full-cell statistics having a different population target or projections resolving scales strictly below the masking cells.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\), \(\texttt{ass:uniform-design}\) gives \(P(X\in B)=\lambda(B)>0\), so every displayed conditional probability is defined.  Since \(A\) and \(Y\) are binary,
\[
 \mathbb E_P(A\mid X\in B)=p_{10,B}+p_{11,B}=e_B,
 \quad
 \mathbb E_P(Y\mid X\in B)=p_{01,B}+p_{11,B},
 \quad
 \mathbb E_P(AY\mid X\in B)=p_{11,B}.                                                  \tag{42}
\]
Also \(A^2=A\), whence \(\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B)=e_B-e_B^2\).  Substitution into the definition of \(r_B(c)\) gives, equation by equation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_B(c)
 &=p_{11,B}-e_B(p_{11,B}+p_{01,B})-c(e_B-e_B^2)\\
 &=\mathbb E_P(AY\mid X\in B)
   -\mathbb E_P(A\mid X\in B)\mathbb E_P(Y\mid X\in B)
   -c\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B)\\
 &=\operatorname{Cov}_P(A,Y\mid X\in B)
   -c\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B),
 \end{aligned}                                                                         \tag{43}
\]
which proves (38).  By \(\texttt{ass:propensity-margin}\) and averaging over \(B\), \(1/4\le e_B\le3/4\); hence both divisions below are legal.  Direct factorization yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_B(c)
 &=(1-e_B)p_{11,B}-e_Bp_{01,B}-c e_B(1-e_B)\\
 &=e_B(1-e_B)
 \left\{\frac{p_{11,B}}{e_B}-\frac{p_{01,B}}{1-e_B}-c\right\},
 \end{aligned}                                                                         \tag{44}
\]
proving (39).  In particular, the full four-cell vector lies on the binwise constant-\(c\) surface if and only if the residual in (38) is zero.  Expanding a fourth-order tensor kernel in the four cell indicators can change its sample representation, but if it is unbiased for (40), its expectation remains the polynomial \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)\); kernel-wise nonseparability cannot change that population identity.

For completeness, the pointwise four-cell identity follows from the definitions of the observed regressions.  For \(\lambda\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
 p_{11}(x)=e(x)m_1(x)=e(x)\{m_0(x)+\tau(x)\},
 \qquad
 p_{01}(x)=\{1-e(x)\}m_0(x).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_c(x)
 &=(1-e)e(m_0+\tau)-e(1-e)m_0-ce(1-e)\\
 &=e(x)\{1-e(x)\}\{\tau(x)-c\}.                                                        \tag{45}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The propensity margin implies \(e(x)\{1-e(x)\}\ge3/16\).  Moreover, the definition of \(D\) and the variance decomposition around the constant \(c\) give
\[
 \int_{\mathcal X}\{\tau(x)-c\}^2\,d\lambda(x)
 =D(P)^2+(\bar\tau-c)^2\ge D(P)^2.
\]
Combining this inequality with (45) proves
\[
 \|\mathcal R_c\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\ge\frac3{16}D(P).                                    \tag{46}
\]

Now invoke the legal deterministic alternative supplied by \(\texttt{prop:pair-factor-omnibus-blind-alternative}\).  That proved proposition gives \(c_0=1/8\), \(D(P_\delta)\asymp\delta\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{P_\delta}(A,Y\mid X\in B)
 -c_0\operatorname{Var}_{P_\delta}(A\mid X\in B)=0
\]
for every fine bin \(B\) in its nested partition.  Equation (38) therefore gives \(r_B(c_0)=0\) for every such \(B\).  Each linear combination \(\eta_Q(c_0)\) in (40) is zero, independently of the deterministic macro weights, and hence
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c_0)=0,
 \qquad
 \min_{c\in I}\mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)=0.                                           \tag{47}
\]
On the other hand, (46) and the separation part of that proposition give \(\|\mathcal R_{c_0}\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\gtrsim\delta>0\).  At the stress tuple \(s_0=\alpha+\beta=1/5\), the fine width in the proposition obeys \(h\asymp\delta^5=\delta^{1/s_0}\), so its primitive nuisance-product approximation scale is \(h^{s_0}\asymp\delta\).  Choosing \(\delta\asymp n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}=n^{-2/7}\) places (47) exactly at the candidate sparse rate.  Thus there is no nonzero projected population energy to which signal-dependent Hoeffding or sparse-occupancy bounds could be applied.  This fails the first two ordered kill checks for this architecture, so the mandated bounded attempt stops before the third check.  Since the argument assumes that the statistic targets (40), it proves no impossibility for a different population functional, as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact population diagnostic required by the final bounded richer-upper attempt: expanding the full conditional four-cell vector does not rescue a statistic whose population target is the squared residual of the projected cell law. \textit{Gap.} The lemma is deliberately architecture-specific.  It does not exclude a statistic targeting a different functional or one that resolves strictly below the masking scale. \textit{Consumer.} The open complete-frontier question uses the lemma to rule out repeating this projected-cell residual route while retaining the valid sparse converse and all-regime bracket.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-unknown-propensity-penalty]\label{prop:strict-unknown-propensity-penalty}
Put \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\).  Suppose
\[
 \gamma>s_0
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 d>\frac{2s_0\gamma}{\gamma-s_0}.
\]
Then the dimension condition implies \(d>2s_0\), and there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\), depending only on \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L\), such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \ge c n^{\kappa},
 \qquad
 \kappa=
 \frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}
 -\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}
 =\frac{2\{d(\gamma-s_0)-2s_0\gamma\}}
 {(4\gamma+d)(2s_0+d)}>0.
\]
Consequently \(\rho_n^*/\rho_{n,e}^*\to\infty\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\gamma>s_0>0\), division by \(\gamma-s_0\) preserves strict inequalities and
\[
 \frac{2s_0\gamma}{\gamma-s_0}>2s_0.
\]
Thus the assumed inequality for \(d\) gives \(d>2s_0\), so the hypothesis \(d\ge 2s_0\) of \texttt{thm:sparse-nuisance-null-supported-lower} holds.  That theorem therefore supplies constants \(c_{mathrm u}>0\) and \(n_{mathrm u}\) such that
\[
 \rho_n^*
 \ge c_{mathrm u}
 \max\left\{n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)},
 n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}\right\}
 \ge c_{mathrm u}n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}
 \tag{1}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_{mathrm u}\).  On the identical class, \texttt{lem:known-propensity-benchmark} gives constants \(0<c_{mathrm e}<C_{mathrm e}<\infty\) and \(n_{mathrm e}\) such that
\[
 0<c_{mathrm e}n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}
 \le \rho_{n,e}^*
 \le C_{mathrm e}n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}
 \tag{2}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_{mathrm e}\).  The left inequality in (2) makes division by \(\rho_{n,e}^*\) legitimate, while its right inequality and (1) yield, for \(n\ge n_0:=\max\{n_{mathrm u},n_{mathrm e}\}\),
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \ge \frac{c_{mathrm u}}{C_{mathrm e}}
 n^{\frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}-\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Putting the two fractions in (3) over their positive common denominator gives
\[
 \frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}-\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}
 =\frac{2\gamma(2s_0+d)-2s_0(4\gamma+d)}
 {(4\gamma+d)(2s_0+d)}
 =\frac{2\{d(\gamma-s_0)-2s_0\gamma\}}
 {(4\gamma+d)(2s_0+d)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The numerator in (4) is strictly positive by the assumed dimension inequality, and both denominator factors are positive because \(d,\gamma,s_0>0\).  Hence \(\kappa>0\), and (3) tends to infinity.  Taking \(c=c_{mathrm u}/C_{mathrm e}\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

In sparse rough-nuisance regimes, repeated-cell scarcity creates a detectability cost without positing a nuisance estimator.  At \((1,1,1/10,1/10)\), heterogeneity below order \(n^{-2/7}\) cannot be uniformly detected when the propensity is unknown, whereas supplying it permits order \(n^{-2/5}\); the radius ratio grows at least as \(n^{4/35}\).  The projected four-cell failure says only that one natural omnibus construction has no population signal on the masking witness.  These are worst-case experiment benchmarks, not validation of a fitted forest and not a license to interpret non-rejection as constant treatment effect.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

With \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\), the optimized lower construction takes \(h\asymp\delta^{1/s_0}\), \(H\asymp\max\{h,\delta^{1/\gamma}\}\), \(q_e=2\delta^{\alpha/s_0}\), and \(q_b=2\delta^{\beta/s_0}\).  Its sign-conditioned connected-graph bound yields \(H^2\le Cn^2h^d\delta^2\sum_{k\ge2}(Cnh^d)^{k-2}\), which closes for \(d\ge2s_0\) and not at the natural scale for \(d<2s_0\).  For the bounded richer-upper attempt, the projected full-cell residual obeys the exact identity \(r_B(c)=\operatorname{Cov}(A,Y\mid B)-c\operatorname{Var}(A\mid B)\).  At the stress tuple the legal masking alternative has \(r_B(1/8)=0\) in every fine cell at \(h\asymp\delta^5\), even though \(\|e(1-e)(\tau-1/8)\|_2\gtrsim\delta\).  Hence every tensor unbiased for squared coefficients of that projected residual has zero population target at \(\delta\asymp n^{-2/7}\); the attempt stops before Hoeffding calibration.  A different population functional and the dense-regime converse remain necessary.

\section{Honest scope}

The causal estimand and statistical class remain unchanged: binary outcomes, exact uniform design on the full cube, fixed overlap and interior margins, three fixed Hölder indices, and an unknown constant null.  No nuisance-rate, learner, or estimator-side condition was added.  The proved content is an all-regime bracket, a matched sufficient no-penalty region, the sparse-regime nuisance converse, and an architecture-specific projected four-cell collapse diagnostic.  At the rough stress tuple the exponents remain \(2/7\) and \(4/19\), so the frontier is not claimed sharp.  The strict known-versus-unknown region is sufficient, not necessary; dense-regime lower optimization, a different omnibus upper construction, and unresolved transition logarithms remain open.  No result certifies an existing causal forest, and non-rejection is not evidence of homogeneity.
