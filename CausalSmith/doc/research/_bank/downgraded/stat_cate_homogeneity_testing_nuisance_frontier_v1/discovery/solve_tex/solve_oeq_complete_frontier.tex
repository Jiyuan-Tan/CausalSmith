For \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\), let \(\mathcal B\) be a finite measurable partition of \(\mathcal X\) whose cells have positive \(\lambda\)-measure.  For \(B\in\mathcal B\), \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), and \(c\in I\), put
\[
 p_{ay,B}=P(A=a,Y=y\mid X\in B),\qquad
 e_B=p_{10,B}+p_{11,B},
\]
and define the full four-cell plug-in residual
\[
 r_B(c)=(1-e_B)p_{11,B}-e_Bp_{01,B}-c e_B(1-e_B).
\]
Then
\[
 r_B(c)=\operatorname{Cov}_P(A,Y\mid X\in B)
          -c\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B),                                      \tag{38}
\]
and, because \(e_B\in[1/4,3/4]\),
\[
 r_B(c)=e_B(1-e_B)
 \left\{\frac{p_{11,B}}{e_B}-\frac{p_{01,B}}{1-e_B}-c\right\}.                       \tag{39}
\]
Let \(\mathcal Q\) be a finite coefficient-label set.  For arbitrary deterministic weights \(q_{QB}\), \(Q\in\mathcal Q\), define projected coefficients and their energy by
\[
 \eta_Q(c)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}q_{QB}r_B(c),
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)=\sum_Q\eta_Q(c)^2.                                       \tag{40}
\]
At \(d=\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=\beta=1/10\), and every fixed \(L\ge2\), the alternatives in \(\texttt{prop:pair-factor-omnibus-blind-alternative}\) satisfy, for their fine partition \(\mathcal B\) and \(c_0=1/8\),
\[
 D(P_\delta)\asymp\delta,
 \qquad r_B(c_0)=0\ \text{for every }B\in\mathcal B,
 \qquad \mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c_0)=0.                                               \tag{41}
\]
Write \(p_{ay}(x)=P(A=a,Y=y\mid X=x)\) for the conditional cell-probability versions induced by the canonical regressions.  Nevertheless, their pointwise residual
\[
 \mathcal R_{c_0}(x)=(1-e(x))p_{11}(x)-e(x)p_{01}(x)-c_0e(x)(1-e(x))
                    =e(x)(1-e(x))\{\tau(x)-c_0\}
\]
satisfies \(\|\mathcal R_{c_0}\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\ge(3/16)D(P_\delta)\).  Consequently, if a split-sample order-four full-cell tensor statistic \(T_{\mathcal B}(c)\), whether or not its displayed kernel is separable, satisfies \(\mathbb E_P[T_{\mathcal B}(c)]=\mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)\) for every \(P\) and \(c\), then \(\mathbb E_{P_\delta}[T_{\mathcal B}(c_0)]=0\) on this legal alternative.  With \(h\asymp\delta^5\) and \(\delta\asymp n^{-2/7}\), this is exactly the proposed sparse nuisance scale.  Thus the full-cell plug-in projection architecture fails the deterministic-signal and masking-witness checks; this statement does not rule out full-cell statistics having a different population target or projections resolving scales strictly below the masking cells.
%%% FIELD lem-full-cell-bin-residual-collapse-proof
Because \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\), \(\texttt{ass:uniform-design}\) gives \(P(X\in B)=\lambda(B)>0\), so every displayed conditional probability is defined.  Since \(A\) and \(Y\) are binary,
\[
 \mathbb E_P(A\mid X\in B)=p_{10,B}+p_{11,B}=e_B,
 \quad
 \mathbb E_P(Y\mid X\in B)=p_{01,B}+p_{11,B},
 \quad
 \mathbb E_P(AY\mid X\in B)=p_{11,B}.                                                  \tag{42}
\]
Also \(A^2=A\), whence \(\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B)=e_B-e_B^2\).  Substitution into the definition of \(r_B(c)\) gives, equation by equation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_B(c)
 &=p_{11,B}-e_B(p_{11,B}+p_{01,B})-c(e_B-e_B^2)\\
 &=\mathbb E_P(AY\mid X\in B)
   -\mathbb E_P(A\mid X\in B)\mathbb E_P(Y\mid X\in B)
   -c\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B)\\
 &=\operatorname{Cov}_P(A,Y\mid X\in B)
   -c\operatorname{Var}_P(A\mid X\in B),
 \end{aligned}                                                                         \tag{43}
\]
which proves (38).  By \(\texttt{ass:propensity-margin}\) and averaging over \(B\), \(1/4\le e_B\le3/4\); hence both divisions below are legal.  Direct factorization yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_B(c)
 &=(1-e_B)p_{11,B}-e_Bp_{01,B}-c e_B(1-e_B)\\
 &=e_B(1-e_B)
 \left\{\frac{p_{11,B}}{e_B}-\frac{p_{01,B}}{1-e_B}-c\right\},
 \end{aligned}                                                                         \tag{44}
\]
proving (39).  In particular, the full four-cell vector lies on the binwise constant-\(c\) surface if and only if the residual in (38) is zero.  Expanding a fourth-order tensor kernel in the four cell indicators can change its sample representation, but if it is unbiased for (40), its expectation remains the polynomial \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)\); kernel-wise nonseparability cannot change that population identity.

For completeness, the pointwise four-cell identity follows from the definitions of the observed regressions.  For \(\lambda\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
 p_{11}(x)=e(x)m_1(x)=e(x)\{m_0(x)+\tau(x)\},
 \qquad
 p_{01}(x)=\{1-e(x)\}m_0(x).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_c(x)
 &=(1-e)e(m_0+\tau)-e(1-e)m_0-ce(1-e)\\
 &=e(x)\{1-e(x)\}\{\tau(x)-c\}.                                                        \tag{45}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The propensity margin implies \(e(x)\{1-e(x)\}\ge3/16\).  Moreover, the definition of \(D\) and the variance decomposition around the constant \(c\) give
\[
 \int_{\mathcal X}\{\tau(x)-c\}^2\,d\lambda(x)
 =D(P)^2+(\bar\tau-c)^2\ge D(P)^2.
\]
Combining this inequality with (45) proves
\[
 \|\mathcal R_c\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\ge\frac3{16}D(P).                                    \tag{46}
\]

Now invoke the legal deterministic alternative supplied by \(\texttt{prop:pair-factor-omnibus-blind-alternative}\).  That proved proposition gives \(c_0=1/8\), \(D(P_\delta)\asymp\delta\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{P_\delta}(A,Y\mid X\in B)
 -c_0\operatorname{Var}_{P_\delta}(A\mid X\in B)=0
\]
for every fine bin \(B\) in its nested partition.  Equation (38) therefore gives \(r_B(c_0)=0\) for every such \(B\).  Each linear combination \(\eta_Q(c_0)\) in (40) is zero, independently of the deterministic macro weights, and hence
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c_0)=0,
 \qquad
 \min_{c\in I}\mathcal E_{\mathcal B}(c)=0.                                           \tag{47}
\]
On the other hand, (46) and the separation part of that proposition give \(\|\mathcal R_{c_0}\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\gtrsim\delta>0\).  At the stress tuple \(s_0=\alpha+\beta=1/5\), the fine width in the proposition obeys \(h\asymp\delta^5=\delta^{1/s_0}\), so its primitive nuisance-product approximation scale is \(h^{s_0}\asymp\delta\).  Choosing \(\delta\asymp n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}=n^{-2/7}\) places (47) exactly at the candidate sparse rate.  Thus there is no nonzero projected population energy to which signal-dependent Hoeffding or sparse-occupancy bounds could be applied.  This fails the first two ordered kill checks for this architecture, so the mandated bounded attempt stops before the third check.  Since the argument assumes that the statistic targets (40), it proves no impossibility for a different population functional, as claimed.

Put \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\), \(t=\min\{\gamma,s_0\}\), and
\[
 b_n=
 \begin{cases}
 n^{-2t/(4t+d)},&d\le4t,\\
 n^{-4t/(4t+3d)},&d>4t.
 \end{cases}
\]
The proved all-regime upper bound and the known-propensity converse give
\[
 c n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}\le\rho_n^*\le Cb_n.
\]
The same-class null-supported construction strengthens the lower side, whenever \(d\ge2s_0\), to
\[
 \rho_n^*\ge c\max\left\{
 n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)},
 n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}
 \right\},                                                                           \tag{48}
\]
with no logarithmic loss at \(d=2s_0\).  If \(\gamma>s_0\) and \(d>2s_0\gamma/(\gamma-s_0)\), the nuisance term in (48) is strictly larger than the known-propensity order and
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \ge c n^{\,2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}\longrightarrow\infty.                  \tag{49}
\]
In particular, at \((d,\gamma,\alpha,\beta)=(1,1,1/10,1/10)\),
\[
 c n^{-2/7}\le\rho_n^*\le Cn^{-4/19},
 \qquad
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}\ge c n^{4/35}.                                       \tag{50}
\]
The no-penalty region \(s_0\ge\gamma\) and \(d\le4\gamma\) remains matched at order \(n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}\), with no logarithm at \(d=4\gamma\).  Outside these regions the exponents are not matched.  For \(d<2s_0\), the current null-supported collision expansion has \(nh_n^d\to\infty\), so its geometric connected-component bound does not close.  On the upper side, \(\texttt{lem:full-cell-bin-residual-collapse}\) proves that the attempted four-cell plug-in projection has population criterion zero on the legal covariance-masking alternative at the candidate scale \(n^{-2/7}\), even though the true pointwise residual has \(L^2(\lambda)\)-norm of order \(n^{-2/7}\).  Thus that attempt fails before its Hoeffding projections can support calibration or power.  This is architecture-specific: a different omnibus population functional is not ruled out.
