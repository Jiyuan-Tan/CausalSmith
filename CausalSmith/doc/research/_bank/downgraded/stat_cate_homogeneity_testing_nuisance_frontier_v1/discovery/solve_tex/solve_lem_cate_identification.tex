%%% FIELD cate-identification-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,d)\).  By
\(\texttt{def:causal-law-class}\), the law \(P\) satisfies the uniform-design,
consistency, conditional-exchangeability, and propensity-margin assumptions.  In
particular, \(P_X=\lambda\).  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 p_a(x):=P(A=a\mid X=x)
 =\begin{cases}1-e(x),&a=0,\\ e(x),&a=1,\end{cases}
\]
and let \(\mu_a\) be a version of
\(x\mapsto\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid X=x]\).  These conditional means exist and are
integrable because \(Y(a)\in\{0,1\}\).  Write \(q_0=m_0\) and \(q_1=m_1\) for
the canonical conditional-mean representatives specified in the frozen
definitions.

We first show \(q_a=\mu_a\), \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere.  Let \(g\) be an
arbitrary bounded Borel function on \(\mathcal X\).  Consistency
\(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) gives
\(\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y=\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)\) almost surely, and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[g(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y\right]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[g(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)\right]. \tag{1}
\]
By the defining property of \(q_a(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]\) and the
tower property,
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[g(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y\right]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[g(X)p_a(X)q_a(X)\right]. \tag{2}
\]
Conditional exchangeability
\(\texttt{ass:conditional-exchangeability}\) implies
\(Y(a)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}A\mid X\).  The defining factorization of
conditional independence therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)\mid X\right]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}\mid X\right]
   \mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid X]
 =p_a(X)\mu_a(X)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Applying the tower property once more gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[g(X)\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)\right]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[g(X)p_a(X)\mu_a(X)\right]. \tag{3}
\]
Combining (1)--(3) and using \(P_X=\lambda\),
\[
 \int_{\mathcal X}g(x)p_a(x)\{q_a(x)-\mu_a(x)\}\,d\lambda(x)=0
 \quad\text{for every bounded Borel }g. \tag{4}
\]
Taking in (4) the indicators of the positive and negative sets of the
integrable function \(p_a(q_a-\mu_a)\) shows
\[
 p_a(x)\{q_a(x)-\mu_a(x)\}=0
 \quad\text{for \(\lambda\)-almost every }x. \tag{5}
\]
The propensity margin \(\texttt{ass:propensity-margin}\) gives
\(p_1=e\ge 1/4\) and \(p_0=1-e\ge 1/4\) everywhere.  Division in (5) is
therefore valid and proves
\[
 m_a(x)=q_a(x)=\mu_a(x)
 \quad\text{for \(\lambda\)-almost every }x,
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\}. \tag{6}
\]

It remains only to identify the contrast.  For every bounded Borel \(g\), the
defining identities for \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_0\), together with ordinary
linearity of finite expectations, give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[g(X)\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}]
 =\mathbb E_P[g(X)\{\mu_1(X)-\mu_0(X)\}].
\]
Thus \(\mu_1-\mu_0\) is a version of
\(x\mapsto\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]\).  Finally, the definition of
\(\tau\) and (6) imply
\[
 \tau(x)=m_1(x)-m_0(x)
 =\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]
\]
for \(\lambda\)-almost every \(x\), as claimed.
