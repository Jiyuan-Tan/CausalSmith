%%% FIELD strict-penalty-statement
Put \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\).  Suppose
\[
 \gamma>s_0
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 d>\frac{2s_0\gamma}{\gamma-s_0}.
\]
Then the dimension condition implies \(d>2s_0\), and there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\), depending only on \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d,L\), such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\),
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \ge c n^{\kappa},
 \qquad
 \kappa=
 \frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}
 -\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}
 =\frac{2\{d(\gamma-s_0)-2s_0\gamma\}}
 {(4\gamma+d)(2s_0+d)}>0.
\]
Consequently \(\rho_n^*/\rho_{n,e}^*\to\infty\).
%%% FIELD strict-penalty-proof
Because \(\gamma>s_0>0\), division by \(\gamma-s_0\) preserves strict inequalities and
\[
 \frac{2s_0\gamma}{\gamma-s_0}>2s_0.
\]
Thus the assumed inequality for \(d\) gives \(d>2s_0\), so the hypothesis \(d\ge 2s_0\) of \texttt{thm:sparse-nuisance-null-supported-lower} holds.  That theorem therefore supplies constants \(c_{mathrm u}>0\) and \(n_{mathrm u}\) such that
\[
 \rho_n^*
 \ge c_{mathrm u}
 \max\left\{n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)},
 n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}\right\}
 \ge c_{mathrm u}n^{-2s_0/(2s_0+d)}
 \tag{1}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_{mathrm u}\).  On the identical class, \texttt{lem:known-propensity-benchmark} gives constants \(0<c_{mathrm e}<C_{mathrm e}<\infty\) and \(n_{mathrm e}\) such that
\[
 0<c_{mathrm e}n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}
 \le \rho_{n,e}^*
 \le C_{mathrm e}n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}
 \tag{2}
\]
for every \(n\ge n_{mathrm e}\).  The left inequality in (2) makes division by \(\rho_{n,e}^*\) legitimate, while its right inequality and (1) yield, for \(n\ge n_0:=\max\{n_{mathrm u},n_{mathrm e}\}\),
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \ge \frac{c_{mathrm u}}{C_{mathrm e}}
 n^{\frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}-\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Putting the two fractions in (3) over their positive common denominator gives
\[
 \frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}-\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}
 =\frac{2\gamma(2s_0+d)-2s_0(4\gamma+d)}
 {(4\gamma+d)(2s_0+d)}
 =\frac{2\{d(\gamma-s_0)-2s_0\gamma\}}
 {(4\gamma+d)(2s_0+d)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The numerator in (4) is strictly positive by the assumed dimension inequality, and both denominator factors are positive because \(d,\gamma,s_0>0\).  Hence \(\kappa>0\), and (3) tends to infinity.  Taking \(c=c_{mathrm u}/C_{mathrm e}\) proves the claim.
%%% FIELD propensity-frontier-partial
Put \(s_0=\alpha+\beta\).  Two sufficient regions are proved on the unchanged class.  First,
\[
 s_0\ge\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad d\le4\gamma
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \rho_n^*\asymp\rho_{n,e}^*\asymp n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}.
\]
At \(d=4\gamma\), this comparison gives \(n^{-1/4}\) without a logarithm.  Second, if
\[
 \gamma>s_0
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 d>\frac{2s_0\gamma}{\gamma-s_0},
\]
then
\[
 \frac{\rho_n^*}{\rho_{n,e}^*}
 \ge c n^{\frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}-\frac{2s_0}{2s_0+d}}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The necessary-and-sufficient equality region is not yet established.  In particular, the regimes \(s_0\ge\gamma\) with \(d>4\gamma\), and \(\gamma>s_0\) with \(d\le 2s_0\gamma/(\gamma-s_0)\), including the equality boundary and any transition logarithms there, remain unresolved.
