\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}limvam\_\allowbreak{}calibration\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} On the restrictive component-aligned Gaussian LiMVAM class, a candidate common order is compatible with an arbitrary observed view graph exactly when every observed-edge residual cross-block is diagonal and every coordinatewise partial disturbance covariance admits a positive-definite completion. The signed completion value \(\chi_G\) makes this second condition computable and stable. A new arbitrary-graph covariance-region inversion combines one sourcewise-iid paired sample per observed edge with every view marginal, without requiring cross-source independence; away from explicit feasibility and path-product margins it gives confidence-set coverage, Hausdorff coefficient-set consistency, and exact graph-essential-poset recovery. The generic elbow still shows that one preassigned view pair generically identifies the full coefficient tuple, and the memoized removed-set recursion retains its \(2^p\)-state compatibility guarantee. Duplicate-free numerical tuple enumeration beyond compatible-order enumeration is not claimed.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing multi-view LiMVAM identification results concern fully paired views under disturbance-diversity conditions, while general set-inference and Gaussian-DAG results do not handle a graph of paired covariance sources whose residual restrictions must also admit global positive-definite disturbance completion. In particular, edgewise residual diagonality and local edge positivity do not control nonchordal completion boundaries or provide finite-sample coefficient-set and common-ancestry guarantees.

\paragraph{Niche.} For the component-aligned Gaussian subclass, each candidate order determines every view-specific coefficient matrix from that view's marginal covariance. Observed edges then determine partial coordinatewise disturbance covariances. The extraction map \(A_G\), its zero-fill right inverse \(E_G\), and the signed value \(\chi_G\) separate observable residual diagonality, global noise feasibility, and coefficient recovery while allowing every unpaired marginal observation to contribute to coefficient estimation.

\paragraph{Fill.} The population graph-completion theorem identifies the full coefficient equivalence class by edge residual diagonality plus coordinatewise positive-definite completion. The completion lemma proves \(\chi_G(q)>0\) exactly for completable data and \(|\chi_G(q)-\chi_G(q')|\le m\|q-q'\|_{\max}\) by translating feasible completions with \(E_G\). The graph covariance theorem uses simultaneous Gaussian operator-norm regions over all observed edges and marginals, a combined residual/completion feasibility score, and the existing path-product argument to prove inversion coverage, exclusion of graph-incompatible orders, a \(2L_B\bar r_n\) Hausdorff bound, exact graph-essential ancestry, and consistency. A one-edge specialization yields the generic two-view singleton corollary.

\section{Related work}

HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM is the model anchor: its fully paired workflow uses cross-view disturbance diversity to identify shared causal structure, whereas the present claims concern only the narrower component-aligned Gaussian subclass and arbitrary graphs of paired covariance sources. ShimizuEtAl2011DirectLiNGAM and Shimizu2012JointLiNGAM motivate recursive residualization but do not establish removed-set path independence or partial-view positive-definite completion. ChenEtAl2024IndividualCausal and GhassamiEtAl2018MultiDomain use ICA structure or heterogeneity rather than selected paired covariance blocks. GroneEtAl1984PositiveDefiniteCompletion supplies the classical chordal completion substrate; the signed arbitrary-graph completion value, its zero-fill Lipschitz proof, and its integration with LiMVAM residuals are derived here. GhoshalHonorio2018LinearSEM uses recursive Schur updates and a score gap for one point-identified SEM, while GaoTaiAragam2022GaussianDAG studies point-identifying Gaussian-DAG estimation rather than set-valued graph compatibility. Vershynin2018HighDimensionalProbability supplies Gaussian singular-value concentration and ChernozhukovHongTamer2007SetInference supplies a general inversion comparison; neither gives the graph LiMVAM completion score or the explicit coefficient and path-product moduli. MohanPearl2021MissingData studies recoverability under missingness mechanisms, whereas the present sourcewise-iid common-population design assumes away selection and transport. TaylorEtAl2017CamCAN motivates a future MEG and fMRI diagnostic application but does not validate component alignment or the graph sampling assumptions.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:centered-gaussian-law]\label{ass:centered-gaussian-law}
The law \(P\) of \(X\) is jointly centered Gaussian.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Joint Gaussianity is an explicitly restrictive component of the maintained covariance-based subclass. It makes the observed second moments determine the maintained law and makes zero cross-covariance equivalent to independence of the coordinate disturbance stacks. It is plausible for coordinate-aligned repeated measurements with Gaussian shared shocks and readout noise, but excludes the broader non-Gaussian LiMVAM families used by several comparators.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:linear-structural-equations]\label{ass:linear-structural-equations}
For each \(a\in M\), \(X^{(a)}=B^{0,(a)}X^{(a)}+\varepsilon^{(a)}\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Linear structural equation model, \cite{HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:shared-topological-order]\label{ass:shared-topological-order}
For every \(a\in M\), \(B^{0,(a)}[\pi_0(k),\pi_0(j)]=0\) whenever \(j\ge k\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Shared-order LiMVAM condition, \cite{HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality]\label{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}
For all distinct \(i,j\in V\) and all \(a,b\in M\), \(\operatorname{Cov}_P(\varepsilon_i^{(a)},\varepsilon_j^{(b)})=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This component-alignment restriction says that cross-view dependence is carried only by disturbances with the same variable label. Under joint Gaussianity it is equivalent to mutual independence of the stacked vectors \((\varepsilon_i^{(a)})_{a\in M}\) across distinct \(i\), and, for any candidate order, it is exactly the structural restriction represented by diagonal residual cross-blocks. The concrete mechanism \(\varepsilon_i^{(a)}=\lambda_{ia}U_i+\eta_i^{(a)}\) in \(\text{def:coordinate-sensor-mechanism}\) realizes it for coordinate-aligned repeated sensors with coordinate-specific shared shocks and independent readout noise; it is not asserted for arbitrary multimodal or neuroimaging measurements.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:disturbance-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:disturbance-nondegeneracy}
For every \(j\in V\), \(\Gamma_j\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Positive definiteness places every stacked coordinate disturbance in the interior of the covariance cone and supports the inverse operations and full-view completion certificates. In the repeated-sensor mechanism, \(\Gamma_i=\operatorname{Var}(U_i)\lambda_i\lambda_i^{\mathsf T}+\operatorname{diag}_{a\in M}\operatorname{Var}(\eta_i^{(a)})\succ0\) because every readout-noise variance is positive. Singular shared-noise limits are outside the theorem.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:calibration-sampling]\label{ass:calibration-sampling}
For every observed edge \(e=\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the \(n_e\) paired vectors \((X_s^{(a)},X_s^{(b)})\), \(s=1,\ldots,n_e\), form an independent and identically distributed sample from the \((a,b)\)-marginal of \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This sourcewise-iid design generalizes one calibration clique to one paired source for every observed edge. It permits arbitrary overlap and dependence among sources, but requires every source to target the same population pair marginal and therefore excludes selection and transport.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:marginal-sampling]\label{ass:marginal-sampling}
For each \(a\in M\), the \(n_a\) available viewwise observations form an independent and identically distributed sample from the \(a\)-marginal of \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is the marginal-retention component of the calibration-plus-marginals design: additional viewwise observations come from the same target population as the calibration cohort. No independence across covariance estimators is imposed; the calibration observations may be included in a view's marginal sample, and independence across sources is available only when cohorts are disjoint. Selection and transport are excluded.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:feasibility-separation]\label{ass:feasibility-separation}
The graph feasibility margin satisfies \(\delta_{\mathrm{feas}}(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}})\ge\kappa_G>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This condition separates every excluded order in the combined residual-and-completion score. In particular it excludes incompatible orders lying exactly on a positive-definite-completion boundary; it is a stability premise, not an identifying restriction used to define compatibility.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:path-product-separation]\label{ass:path-product-separation}
The graph path-product margin satisfies \(\delta_{\mathrm{path}}(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}})\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is the arbitrary-graph version of the existing support-stratum margin: every common ancestry has a path whose product remains separated from zero in every graph-compatible tuple.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selected-joint-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:selected-joint-nondegeneracy}
For every \(e=\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the covariance \(\Sigma^e=\begin{psmallmatrix}\Sigma^{aa}&\Sigma^{ab}\\\Sigma^{ba}&\Sigma^{bb}\end{psmallmatrix}\) of the paired vector \((X^{(a)},X^{(b)})\) is positive definite.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive-definite source covariance nondegeneracy for Gaussian whitening, \cite{Vershynin2018HighDimensionalProbability}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:marginal-covariance-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:marginal-covariance-nondegeneracy}
For every \(a\in M\), \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive-definite covariance nondegeneracy for Gaussian whitening and regression, \cite{Vershynin2018HighDimensionalProbability}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:covariance-region]\label{def:covariance-region}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every edge \(e=\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}\), order its endpoints by \(a<b\), put \(Y_s^e=((X_s^{(a)})^{\mathsf T},(X_s^{(b)})^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb R^{2p}\), and set
\[
\widehat\Sigma^e=n_e^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{n_e}Y_s^e(Y_s^e)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For every \(a\in M\), set \(\widehat\Sigma^{aa}=n_a^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{n_a}X_s^{(a)}(X_s^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}\).  Let \(K_G=|E_{\mathrm{obs}}|+m\), and define
\[
\eta_{e,n}=\sqrt{2p/n_e}+\sqrt{2\log(2K_G/\alpha)/n_e},
\qquad
\eta_{a,n}=\sqrt{p/n_a}+\sqrt{2\log(2K_G/\alpha)/n_a}.
\]
Whenever every displayed \(\eta\) is smaller than one, define
\[
r_{e,n}(\alpha)=\bigl((1-\eta_{e,n})^{-2}-1\bigr)\|\widehat\Sigma^e\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad
r_{a,n}(\alpha)=\bigl((1-\eta_{a,n})^{-2}-1\bigr)\|\widehat\Sigma^{aa}\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\]
and \(\bar r_n(\alpha)=\max_{e\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}}r_{e,n}(\alpha)\vee\max_{a\in M}r_{a,n}(\alpha)\), with the edge maximum zero when the edge set is empty.  For a candidate collection \(\mathcal O'_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\), let
\[
\Sigma'^e=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Sigma'^{aa}&\Sigma'^{ab}\\
\Sigma'^{ba}&\Sigma'^{bb}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The graph covariance region \(\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\) consists of all \(\mathcal O'_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) such that
\[
\|\Sigma'^e-\widehat\Sigma^e\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le r_{e,n}(\alpha)\quad(e\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\qquad
\|\Sigma'^{aa}-\widehat\Sigma^{aa}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le r_{a,n}(\alpha)\quad(a\in M),
\]
and every \(\Sigma'^e\) and every \(\Sigma'^{aa}\) is positive definite.

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization]\label{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}
Fix \(\mathcal O_S\) generated by at least one member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\). Joint Gaussianity and component alignment imply that the stacked disturbance vectors \((\varepsilon_i^{(a)})_{a\in M}\) are mutually independent across \(i\in V\). For every permutation \(\pi\), there exists a full joint component-aligned Gaussian completion with common order \(\pi\) and observed moments \(\mathcal O_S\) if and only if \(\operatorname{offdiag}(R_\pi^{ab})=0\) for every distinct \(a,b\in S\). When this holds, the structural coefficients are uniquely \(T_\pi\), each selected disturbance block \((R_\pi^{ab}[j,j])_{a,b\in S}\) is positive definite, and every \(\Gamma_{\pi,j}\) is a full positive-definite noise certificate. Consequently, \(\Theta_S(\mathcal O_S)\) is the complete coefficient equivalence class within \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(P_S=\operatorname{diag}_{a\in S}(L_\pi^{(a)})\), with the blocks ordered by view.  Every \(\Sigma^{aa}\) is positive definite: under \(\text{def:component-aligned-limvam-class}\), \(X^{(a)}=(I_p-B^{0,(a)})^{-1}\varepsilon^{(a)}\), the matrix \(I_p-B^{0,(a)}\) is invertible by \(\text{ass:shared-topological-order}\), and the diagonal matrix \(\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon^{(a)})=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}\Gamma_j[a,a]\) is positive definite by \(\text{ass:disturbance-nondegeneracy}\).  Thus every principal matrix inverted in \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) is nonsingular.

For a fixed view \(a\), the row in \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) is the unique solution of the normal equations for the linear projection of \(X_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}\) on \(X_{\pi(1:k-1)}^{(a)}\).  Therefore the residuals \(L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}\) are mutually orthogonal within view \(a\), and
\[
R_\pi^{aa}=L_\pi^{(a)}\Sigma^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\]
is diagonal and positive definite.  Notice also that every \(L_\pi^{(a)}\) is unit triangular in the order \(\pi\), hence invertible.

We first prove necessity and uniqueness.  Suppose a full component-aligned completion with order \(\pi\), coefficient matrices \(\widetilde B^{(a)}\), and disturbances \(\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}\) has observed moments \(\mathcal O_S\).  A predecessor variable in view \(a\) is a linear combination of disturbances attached to earlier, hence different, variable labels.  By \(\text{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}\), \(\widetilde\varepsilon_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}\) is orthogonal to every predecessor.  The structural equation consequently gives the normal equations
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(X_{\pi(k)}^{(a)},X_{\pi(1:k-1)}^{(a)}\right)
=\widetilde B^{(a)}[\pi(k),\pi(1:k-1)]
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(X_{\pi(1:k-1)}^{(a)}\right).
\]
The covariance on the right is positive definite, so its inverse proves \(\widetilde B^{(a)}=B_\pi^{(a)}\) row by row.  Hence \(L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}=\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}\), and component alignment gives
\[
R_\pi^{ab}[i,j]=\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\widetilde\varepsilon_i^{(a)},\widetilde\varepsilon_j^{(b)}\right)=0,\qquad i\ne j,\quad a,b\in S.
\]
This proves necessity and the asserted uniqueness of \(T_\pi\).

For sufficiency, assume \(\operatorname{offdiag}(R_\pi^{ab})=0\) for all distinct \(a,b\in S\).  The selected joint covariance \(\Sigma^{SS}\) is positive definite because it is the covariance of an invertible linear transform of the stacked disturbances \((\varepsilon_j^{(a)}:j\in V,a\in S)\), whose covariance becomes \(\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}\Gamma_j[S,S]\succ0\) after grouping coordinates by \(j\).  Congruence therefore gives
\[
\mathscr R_{\pi,S}:=P_S\Sigma^{SS}P_S^{\mathsf T}\succ0.
\]
Within-view off-diagonal entries of \(\mathscr R_{\pi,S}\) vanish by the projection calculation above, and its cross-view, cross-variable entries vanish by the displayed compatibility hypothesis.  After a simultaneous coordinate permutation grouping equal variable labels, it follows that
\[
\mathscr R_{\pi,S}=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}\bigl(R_\pi^{ab}[j,j]\bigr)_{a,b\in S}.
\]
Every diagonal block \(\bigl(R_\pi^{ab}[j,j]\bigr)_{a,b\in S}\) is thus positive definite.  For \(a\notin S\), \(R_\pi^{aa}[j,j]>0\) because \(R_\pi^{aa}\succ0\).  Consequently \(\Gamma_{\pi,j}\) in \(\text{def:noise-certificate}\) is, after ordering \(S\) first, the block diagonal matrix
\[
\Gamma_{\pi,j}=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\bigl(R_\pi^{ab}[j,j]\bigr)_{a,b\in S},\ (R_\pi^{aa}[j,j])_{a\in M\setminus S}\right)\succ0.
\]

For each \(j\), take a centered Gaussian vector \((\widetilde\varepsilon_j^{(a)})_{a\in M}\) with covariance \(\Gamma_{\pi,j}\), independently across \(j\), and define
\[
\widetilde X^{(a)}=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\]
The coefficient matrices have common order \(\pi\), the coordinate disturbance stacks are independent, and their covariance certificates are positive definite.  Moreover, for every \(a\in M\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X^{(a)})
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}R_\pi^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-\mathsf T}=\Sigma^{aa},
\]
while for \(a,b\in S\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X^{(a)},\widetilde X^{(b)})
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}R_\pi^{ab}(L_\pi^{(b)})^{-\mathsf T}=\Sigma^{ab}.
\]
Thus this law is the required full completion of \(\mathcal O_S\).

Finally, under \(\text{ass:centered-gaussian-law}\), the collection of all disturbance stacks is jointly Gaussian because it is a linear transform of \(X\).  By \(\text{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}\), its covariance is block diagonal across \(j\).  Its characteristic function is therefore
\[
\exp\!\left\{-\frac12\sum_{j\in V}t_j^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_jt_j\right\}
=\prod_{j\in V}\exp\!\left\{-\frac12t_j^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_jt_j\right\},
\]
which proves mutual independence of the stacks.  Necessity and sufficiency show that \(\Pi_S(\mathcal O_S)\) indexes all and only the admissible coefficient tuples, and \(\text{def:identified-tuple-set}\) removes precisely their numerical duplicates.  Hence \(\Theta_S(\mathcal O_S)\) is the complete coefficient equivalence class, rather than a definition of the causal target by an observed functional.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This supporting lemma supplies the fixed-order observable criterion and positive-definite completion used by the generic theorem and the complete-set algorithms. \textit{Gap.} Section 4.6 of HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM is point-identifying under full pairing and its diversity assumption; this lemma instead records the orderwise residual characterization needed for selected pairing on the narrower component-aligned subclass, without treating that characterization as the principal novelty. \textit{Consumer.} For coordinate-aligned repeated measurements satisfying the maintained mechanism, an analyst can determine exactly which shared orders and view-specific coefficient matrices remain licensed when only a calibration subset is aligned.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-partial-calibration]\label{prop:strict-partial-calibration}
The law \(P_{\mathrm{ex}}\) in \(\text{def:strictness-example}\), together with its displayed coefficients, disturbances, covariance matrices, and order \((1,2)\), belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{2,3}^{\mathrm{CA}}\). Let \(H=\{1,2\}\subsetneq M=\{1,2,3\}\). The two orders \((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\) are both compatible with \(\mathcal O_H\), and \(\Theta_H(\mathcal O_H)=\{(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix})_{a\in M},(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1/2\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix})_{a\in M}\}\). Under full calibration, \(\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)=\{(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix})_{a\in M}\}\). The displayed structural parameter lies in \(\mathcal E_{12,(1,2)}\): the reverse-order residual restriction vanishes for the calibrated pair because \(\Gamma_1[1,2]=\Gamma_2[1,2]=1/5\), but fails for the observed full-pair block \(\{1,3\}\) because \(\Gamma_1[1,3]=1/5\ne2/5=\Gamma_2[1,3]\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(g_i^{ab}=\Gamma_i[a,b]\) for \(i\in\{1,2\}\) and \(a,b\in M\).  We first verify that the displayed construction is in the maintained model class.  By \(\text{def:strictness-example}\), the two stacked vectors \((\varepsilon_1^{(a)})_{a\in M}\) and \((\varepsilon_2^{(a)})_{a\in M}\) are independent centered Gaussian vectors.  Their concatenation is therefore centered Gaussian, and \(X\), being a linear transformation of that concatenation under the displayed structural equations, is jointly centered Gaussian.  Thus \(\text{ass:centered-gaussian-law}\) holds.  Independence of the two stacks implies, for every \(a,b\in M\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{P_{\mathrm{ex}}}(\varepsilon_1^{(a)},\varepsilon_2^{(b)})=0,
\]
which is \(\text{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}\) when \(V=\{1,2\}\).

It remains to verify disturbance nondegeneracy.  The matrix \(\Gamma_1\) has the equicorrelation form with diagonal \(1\) and off-diagonal entry \(1/5\), so its eigenvalues are
\[
1-\frac15=\frac45\quad\text{with multiplicity two},
\qquad
1+2\frac15=\frac75.
\]
Hence \(\Gamma_1\succ0\).  The leading principal minors of \(\Gamma_2\) are
\[
1,
\qquad
1-\frac1{25}=\frac{24}{25},
\qquad
\det(\Gamma_2)
=1+2\frac15\frac25\frac15-\frac1{25}-\frac4{25}-\frac1{25}
=\frac{99}{125}.
\]
All three are positive, so Sylvester's criterion gives \(\Gamma_2\succ0\).  Thus \(\text{ass:disturbance-nondegeneracy}\) holds.  Finally, the equations in \(\text{def:strictness-example}\) can be written
\[
X^{(a)}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}X^{(a)}+\varepsilon^{(a)},
\qquad a\in M,
\]
so \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\) holds, and the displayed matrix is strictly lower triangular in the order \((1,2)\), which verifies \(\text{ass:shared-topological-order}\).  Every member property in \(\text{def:component-aligned-limvam-class}\) has now been checked, proving
\[
(P_{\mathrm{ex}},B^0,\varepsilon,\Gamma,(1,2))\in\mathfrak M_{2,3}^{\mathrm{CA}}.
\]

We next calculate the observed moment restrictions.  From
\(X_1^{(a)}=\varepsilon_1^{(a)}\) and
\(X_2^{(a)}=\varepsilon_1^{(a)}+\varepsilon_2^{(a)}\), cross-variable disturbance orthogonality gives, for every \(a,b\in M\),
\[
\Sigma^{ab}
=\begin{pmatrix}
g_1^{ab}&g_1^{ab}\\
g_1^{ab}&g_1^{ab}+g_2^{ab}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both displayed disturbance covariance matrices have unit diagonal.  Consequently, for every \(a\in M\),
\[
\Sigma^{aa}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det(\Sigma^{aa})=1>0.
\]
In particular, every inverse required by \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) exists.

For the order \((1,2)\), the only nonzero order-constrained regression coefficient is
\[
B_{(1,2)}^{(a)}[2,1]
=\Sigma^{aa}[2,1]\{\Sigma^{aa}[1,1]\}^{-1}=1,
\]
and hence, in every view,
\[
B_{(1,2)}^{(a)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
L_{(1,2)}^{(a)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Using \(\text{def:residual-cross-blocks}\), direct multiplication yields
\[
R_{(1,2)}^{ab}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}g_1^{ab}&g_1^{ab}\\g_1^{ab}&g_1^{ab}+g_2^{ab}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}g_1^{ab}&0\\0&g_2^{ab}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus the true order satisfies the diagonal residual restriction for every pair of views.

For the reverse order \((2,1)\), the only nonzero coefficient is the regression coefficient of \(X_1^{(a)}\) on its predecessor \(X_2^{(a)}\):
\[
B_{(2,1)}^{(a)}[1,2]
=\Sigma^{aa}[1,2]\{\Sigma^{aa}[2,2]\}^{-1}=\frac12.
\]
Therefore, again in every view,
\[
B_{(2,1)}^{(a)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
L_{(2,1)}^{(a)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A second multiplication gives the complete reverse-order residual block
\[
R_{(2,1)}^{ab}
=\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{g_1^{ab}+g_2^{ab}}4&\dfrac{g_1^{ab}-g_2^{ab}}2\\[4pt]
\dfrac{g_1^{ab}-g_2^{ab}}2&g_1^{ab}+g_2^{ab}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[thm:removed-set-recursion]\label{thm:removed-set-recursion}
For every \(A\subseteq V\), the state \(C_A\) equals covariance after orthogonal projection on the span of the coordinates indexed by \(A\) and is independent of the order in which \(A\) was removed. A permutation \(\pi\) belongs to \(\Pi_S(\mathcal O_S)\) if and only if \(\pi(k)\) is removable at \(A_{k-1}=\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(k-1)\}\) for every \(k\). Thus source-to-sink paths in \(\mathcal D_S\) are in bijection with compatible orders, and all states and removable edges can be constructed in \(O(\operatorname{poly}(p,m)2^p)\) exact-real arithmetic operations and \(O(\operatorname{poly}(p,m)2^p)\) memory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All inverses below exist because every \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\), so each principal covariance and each Schur complement is positive definite.  Expanding \(\text{def:projected-residual}\) gives, for \(U=U_A\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_A^{ab}={}&\Sigma^{ab}[U,U]
-\Sigma^{aa}[U,A]\Sigma^{aa}[A,A]^{-1}\Sigma^{ab}[A,U]\\
&-\Sigma^{ab}[U,A]\Sigma^{bb}[A,A]^{-1}\Sigma^{bb}[A,U]\\
&+\Sigma^{aa}[U,A]\Sigma^{aa}[A,A]^{-1}\Sigma^{ab}[A,A]
\Sigma^{bb}[A,A]^{-1}\Sigma^{bb}[A,U].
\end{aligned}
\]
Every term is indexed only by \(A\), proving the claimed state path independence.

This set-indexed formula is also the result of sequential elimination.  Indeed, let \(r\in U_A\), let \(U'=U_A\setminus\{r\}\), and define
\[
\beta_{A,r}^{(a)}=\frac{C_A^{aa}[U',r]}{C_A^{aa}[r,r]}.
\]
The residual vector \(Z_{A,U'}^{(a)}-\beta_{A,r}^{(a)}Z_{A,r}^{(a)}\) is orthogonal both to \(X_A^{(a)}\) and to \(Z_{A,r}^{(a)}\).  Since
\(\operatorname{span}(X_A^{(a)},X_r^{(a)})=\operatorname{span}(X_A^{(a)},Z_{A,r}^{(a)})\), uniqueness of the orthogonal projection proves
\[
Z_{A\cup\{r\}}^{(a)}=Z_{A,U'}^{(a)}-\beta_{A,r}^{(a)}Z_{A,r}^{(a)}.
\]
Taking cross-covariances gives the Schur update
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{A\cup\{r\}}^{ab}={}&C_A^{ab}[U',U']
-\beta_{A,r}^{(a)}C_A^{ab}[r,U']
-C_A^{ab}[U',r](\beta_{A,r}^{(b)})^{\mathsf T}\\
&+\beta_{A,r}^{(a)}C_A^{ab}[r,r](\beta_{A,r}^{(b)})^{\mathsf T},
\end{aligned}
\]
again depending only on \(A\cup\{r\}\).

For \(t\in U'\) and distinct \(a,b\in S\), the score in \(\text{def:root-score}\) is exactly
\[
q_{A,r,t}^{ab}
=\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(Z_{A,r}^{(a)},
Z_{A,t}^{(b)}-\frac{C_A^{bb}[t,r]}{C_A^{bb}[r,r]}Z_{A,r}^{(b)}\right)
=\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(Z_{A,r}^{(a)},Z_{A\cup\{r\},t}^{(b)}\right).
\]
We now prove the order criterion by induction on \(\lvert U_A\rvert\).  Fix a suffix order beginning with \(r\).  The within-view Gram--Schmidt residualizer for that suffix factors into the first elimination displayed above followed by an invertible unit-triangular residualizer on \(U'\).  Consequently, for a cross-view block, its final first-to-tail row is
\[
\bigl(q_{A,r,t}^{ab}:t\in U'\bigr)K_b^{\mathsf T},
\]
where \(K_b\) is the invertible tail residualizer.  It vanishes if and only if every \(q_{A,r,t}^{ab}\) vanishes.  Once that row and, by considering the ordered pair \((b,a)\), the corresponding column vanish, the remaining final block is diagonal if and only if the recursively residualized tail block from \(C_{A\cup\{r\}}\) is diagonal.  The one-variable base case is automatic.  Thus a full Gram--Schmidt cross-block is diagonal if and only if the first variable is removable and the same condition holds recursively.  At \(A=\varnothing\), \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\) converts this algebraic criterion into
\[
\pi\in\Pi_S(\mathcal O_S)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_{A_{k-1},\pi(k),t}^{ab}=0
\ \text{for every admissible }k,t,a,b.
\]

Every source-to-sink path adds one new label at each step, hence spells one permutation; the displayed equivalence says that it spells exactly one compatible order.  Conversely every compatible order supplies all its path edges, so the correspondence is bijective.  There are at most \(2^p\) states and at most \(p2^{p-1}\) candidate edges.  For one state, the displayed covariance formula or Schur update, all root scores, and all view-pair checks require a number of exact-real arithmetic operations polynomial in \(p\) and \(m\); storing its observed covariance blocks is polynomial as well.  Memoizing each \(C_A\) once therefore gives the asserted \(O(\operatorname{poly}(p,m)2^p)\) time and memory bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:two-variable-reduction]\label{prop:two-variable-reduction}
Let \(p=2\), let the true common order be \((1,2)\), and consider calibrated views \(a,b\) with the same nonzero structural edge coefficient and equal positive within-view disturbance variances variable by variable. The reverse order \((2,1)\) is compatible if and only if \(\rho_1^{ab}=\rho_2^{ab}\). Hence unequal disturbance correlations reject the reverse order, while equal correlations reproduce the homogeneous two-view Gaussian ambiguity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the common nonzero coefficient be \(\beta\), and write
\[
v_i=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i^{(a)})=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i^{(b)})>0,\qquad
c_i=\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_i^{(a)},\varepsilon_i^{(b)}),\quad i=1,2.
\]
Then \(\rho_i^{ab}=c_i/v_i\).  Under the true order,
\[
X_1=\varepsilon_1,\qquad X_2=\beta\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2,\qquad
d:=\operatorname{Var}(X_2)=\beta^2v_1+v_2>0.
\]
For the reverse order the regression coefficient of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) is \(\gamma=\beta v_1/d\).  Hence its two innovations are
\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_2=X_2,\qquad
\widetilde\varepsilon_1=X_1-\gamma X_2=\frac{v_2}{d}\varepsilon_1-\frac{\beta v_1}{d}\varepsilon_2.
\]
Component alignment removes cross-variable covariance, and therefore
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\widetilde\varepsilon_1^{(a)},\widetilde\varepsilon_2^{(b)}\right)
=\frac{\beta v_1v_2}{d}\left(\frac{c_1}{v_1}-\frac{c_2}{v_2}\right)
=\frac{\beta v_1v_2}{d}\left(\rho_1^{ab}-\rho_2^{ab}\right).
\]
The transpose-oriented off-diagonal entry has the same zero condition.  Since \(\beta\ne0\), \(v_1,v_2>0\), and \(d>0\), both off-diagonal entries vanish if and only if \(\rho_1^{ab}=\rho_2^{ab}\).  The conclusion now follows from \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:nonzero-polynomial-null-set]\label{lem:nonzero-polynomial-null-set}
For every positive integer \(d\) and every nonzero real polynomial \(q\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), the zero set \(\{x\in\mathbb R^d:q(x)=0\}\) has \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero and empty interior.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We argue by induction on \(d\).  For \(d=1\), a nonzero polynomial has at most its degree many roots, so its zero set is finite and null.  Suppose the result holds in dimension \(d-1\).  Write \(x=(y,z)\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\times\mathbb R\) and
\[
q(y,z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{D}c_\ell(y)z^\ell.
\]
Choose \(\ell_0\) such that \(c_{\ell_0}\) is not the zero polynomial.  The exceptional set \(E=\{y:c_\ell(y)=0\text{ for every }\ell\}\) is contained in \(\{y:c_{\ell_0}(y)=0\}\), hence is null by the induction hypothesis.  For \(y\notin E\), the section \(z\mapsto q(y,z)\) is a nonzero univariate polynomial and has finitely many zeros, hence one-dimensional measure zero.  Applying the section formula for Lebesgue measure to the nonnegative indicator of the zero set gives
\[
\lambda_d(\{q=0\}\cap[-R,R]^d)
=\int_{[-R,R]^{d-1}}\lambda_1(\{z\in[-R,R]:q(y,z)=0\})\,dy=0
\]
for every positive integer \(R\): the integrand is zero outside \(E\) and is at most \(2R\) on \(E\).  The zero set is closed because \(q\) is continuous, so the section formula applies, and \(\{q=0\}\) is the countable union of these boxed intersections.  Hence it is null.  A nonempty Euclidean open set contains a ball of positive Lebesgue measure, so a null set has empty interior.  This completes the induction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This elementary algebraic-measure lemma converts nonvanishing wrong-order polynomials into a proper null exceptional set. \textit{Gap.} It is supporting mathematical infrastructure, not a causal identification claim. \textit{Consumer.} It closes the genericity conclusion of the two-view theorem.

\[
\{\theta\in\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}:
\Theta_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})\ne\{T_{\pi_0}\}\}
\]
is contained in \(\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}\), and the containment can be strict because distinct compatible orders can have the same numerical tuple.

Let \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{marg}}=(\Sigma^{aa})_{a\in M}\) contain all viewwise marginals and no cross-view block.  Define \(\Pi_{\mathrm{marg}}\) to be the set of permutations for which there exists a full member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) with that common order and observable \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{marg}}\), and define \(\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}\) to be the corresponding coefficient-tuple set with numerical duplicates removed.  Then
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{marg}}=\mathfrak S_V,
\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}=\{T_\pi:\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\}.
\]
For \(C\subseteq M\), let \(\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}\) when \(\lvert C\rvert\le1\), and let \(\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_C(\mathcal O_C)\) when \(\lvert C\rvert\ge2\).  Define the extended calibration number
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{cal}}
:=
\min\{\lvert C\rvert:C\subseteq M,\ 
\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)\}.
\]
This minimum is well defined because \(M\) is finite and \(C=M\) is feasible.
For every \(\theta\notin\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\), \(\lvert\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}\rvert\ge2\), whereas any single observed pair gives the full singleton.  Consequently one paired cross-view block is generically necessary and sufficient, \(\kappa_{\mathrm{cal}}=2\), and also \(\lvert S_{\min}\rvert=2\) under the original convention \(\lvert S\rvert\ge2\).  When \(m\ge3\) the identifying pair is a proper subset of the views; when \(m=2\) it is the full view set.  No edge-minimality condition and no diversity condition from Assumption 2 of HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM is imposed.  Every parameter law absolutely continuous with respect to relative Lebesgue measure avoids \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\) almost surely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\theta\in\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}\), and write \(A^{(a)}=(I_p-B^{(a)})^{-1}\).  Strict lower triangularity in the order \(\pi_0\) makes \(B^{(a)}\) nilpotent, so
\[
(I_p-B^{(a)})^{-1}=\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}(B^{(a)})^q.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(\Gamma_i\succ0\) implies \(\Gamma_i[a,a]>0\).  The covariance formula in \(\text{def:fixed-order-open-parameter-space}\) therefore yields
\[
\Sigma_\theta^{aa}
=A^{(a)}\operatorname{diag}_{i\in V}(\Gamma_i[a,a])(A^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\succ0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every principal submatrix inverted in \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) is positive definite and has positive determinant.

Equation (1) shows that every entry of \(\Sigma_\theta^{ab}\) is polynomial in the free coordinates of \(\theta\).  Applying
\(C^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(C)/\det(C)\) to each predecessor covariance in \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) shows that every entry of \(B_\pi^{(a)}\), and consequently every entry of
\[
R_\pi^{ab}=(I_p-B_\pi^{(a)})\Sigma_\theta^{ab}
(I_p-B_\pi^{(b)})^{\mathsf T},
\tag{3}
\]
is rational with a denominator that is a product of the positive principal minors from (2).  Hence the denominator clearing in \(\text{def:pairwise-exceptional-variety}\) preserves whether an entry is zero.

Fix \(1\le a<b\le m\) and \(\pi\ne\pi_0\).  Two distinct total orders have a discordant pair, so there are \(u,v\in V\) such that \(u\) precedes \(v\) under \(\pi_0\), whereas \(v\) precedes \(u\) under \(\pi\).  Consider \(\theta_\pi^{ab}\) from \(\text{def:wrong-order-nonvanishing-witness}\).  For \(w\in\{u,v\}\), the covariance \(\Gamma_w\), after a simultaneous permutation of view coordinates, is the direct sum of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&r_w\\ r_w&1\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad I_{m-2}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \(1-r_w\), \(1+r_w\), and \(1\) with multiplicity \(m-2\), all positive because \(|r_w|<1\).  Every other \(\Gamma_i\) is \(I_m\), and the only nonzero coefficient, \(\beta\), respects \(\pi_0\).  Thus
\[
\theta_\pi^{ab}\in\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Variables outside \(\{u,v\}\) are uncorrelated with this two-variable subsystem and therefore do not change the following population regression coefficients when they occur in a predecessor set.  In views \(a\) and \(b\),
\[
X_u=\varepsilon_u,
\qquad
X_v=\beta\varepsilon_u+\varepsilon_v,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_u)=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_v)=1.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(v\) precedes \(u\) under \(\pi\), the wrong-order innovations are
\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_v=X_v,
\qquad
\widetilde\varepsilon_u
=X_u-\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_u,X_v)}{\operatorname{Var}(X_v)}X_v
=X_u-\frac{\beta}{1+\beta^2}X_v.
\tag{6}
\]
Using cross-variable disturbance orthogonality and the witness covariances in (6) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\widetilde\varepsilon_u^{(a)},
\widetilde\varepsilon_v^{(b)}\right)
&=\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
\frac{\varepsilon_u^{(a)}-\beta\varepsilon_v^{(a)}}{1+\beta^2},
\beta\varepsilon_u^{(b)}+\varepsilon_v^{(b)}\right)\\
&=\frac{\beta}{1+\beta^2}(r_u-r_v)\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
This is, up to transpose orientation, an off-diagonal entry of \(R_\pi^{ab}\).  Its cleared numerator is nonzero at \(\theta_\pi^{ab}\), so
\[
F_\pi^{ab}\not\equiv0.
\tag{8}
\]

There are finitely many unordered view pairs and wrong permutations.  A finite product of nonzero real polynomials is nonzero, since under any monomial order the leading coefficient of the product is the product of the nonzero leading coefficients.  It follows from (8) that
\[
Q_{\mathrm{all}}\not\equiv0.
\tag{9}
\]
The set \(\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) is nonempty and relatively open in its free Euclidean coordinates: the point having \(B^{(a)}=0\) and \(\Gamma_i=I_m\) belongs to it, and the positive-definite cone is open.  By \(\text{lem:nonzero-polynomial-null-set}\), (9) implies that \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\) is proper, has empty relative interior, and has relative Lebesgue measure zero.  Any parameter law absolutely continuous with respect to that relative Lebesgue measure therefore assigns probability zero to this exceptional set.

We next identify the exact fixed-pair failure locus.  Fix \(a<b\).  Positivity of all denominator factors, the fact that a sum of real squares vanishes exactly when every summand vanishes, and (3) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\pi^{ab}(\theta)=0
&\Longleftrightarrow f_{\pi,ij}^{ab}(\theta)=0
\quad\text{for every }i\ne j\\
&\Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{offdiag}(R_\pi^{ab}(\theta))=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Also \(R_\pi^{ba}=(R_\pi^{ab})^{\mathsf T}\), because \(\Sigma^{ba}=(\Sigma^{ab})^{\mathsf T}\), so either orientation tests the unordered pair.  The true order \(\pi_0\) is compatible by \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\).  Combining that fact, (10), and \(\text{def:pairwise-exceptional-variety}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta\in\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}
&\Longleftrightarrow
F_\pi^{ab}(\theta)=0\quad\text{for at least one }\pi\ne\pi_0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\Pi_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})\ne\{\pi_0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

If \(\theta\notin\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\), every factor of \(Q_{\mathrm{all}}(\theta)\) is nonzero.  Applying (10) for every pair and every wrong order yields simultaneously
\[
\Pi_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})=\{\pi_0\}
\quad\text{for every }1\le a<b\le m.
\tag{12}
\]
The complete coefficient characterization in \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\) then proves
\[
\Theta_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})=\{T_{\pi_0}\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Full calibration retains \(\pi_0\) and imposes every pair restriction, so its compatible-order set is contained in the compatible-order set for any one pair.  Equations (12)--(13) therefore give
\[
\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)=\{T_{\pi_0}\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus (13)--(14) prove, rather than define, equality between the structural coefficient target and the observable residual-restriction functional.

If the pairwise coefficient set differs from \(\{T_{\pi_0}\}\), then its compatible-order set cannot be \(\{\pi_0\}\).  Hence the coefficient-nonidentification locus is contained in \(\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}\) by (11).  The containment can be strict.  Indeed, take \(B^{(a)}=0\) for every view and \(\Gamma_i=I_m\) for every coordinate.  Then \(\Sigma^{aa}=I_p\) and \(\Sigma^{ab}=0\) for \(a\ne b\).  For every \(\pi\), modified Cholesky gives \(B_\pi^{(a)}=0\) and \(R_\pi^{ab}=0\), whence
\[
\Pi_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})=\mathfrak S_V,
\qquad
\Theta_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})
=\{(0)_{a\in M}\}=\{T_{\pi_0}\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Because \(p\ge2\), a wrong order exists, so (15) lies in \(\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}\) but not in the coefficient-nonidentification locus.

It remains to prove the lower side of the calibration elbow.  Fix \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\) and \(a\in M\).  The rows of \(L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}\) are successive least-squares innovations.  The normal equations make the innovation at position \(k\) orthogonal to the span of the variables at positions \(1,\ldots,k-1\), while every earlier innovation lies in that span.  Distinct innovations are therefore orthogonal, and
\[
D_\pi^{(a)}:=R_\pi^{aa}
=L_\pi^{(a)}\Sigma^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\tag{16}
\]
is diagonal.  It is positive definite because \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\) and the unit order-triangular matrix \(L_\pi^{(a)}\) is invertible.  Hence every \(D_\pi^{(a)}[j,j]>0\).

For each \(j\in V\), define
\[
\Gamma_{\pi,j}^{\mathrm{marg}}
:=\operatorname{diag}_{a\in M}\bigl(D_\pi^{(a)}[j,j]\bigr)\succ0.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \((Z_j)_{j\in V}\) be independent standard Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^m\), and put
\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_j
:=\operatorname{diag}_{a\in M}\!\left(\sqrt{D_\pi^{(a)}[j,j]}\right)Z_j,
\qquad
\widetilde X^{(a)}=(I_p-B_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{18}
\]
This construction is jointly centered Gaussian, has common order \(\pi\), has mutually independent coordinate disturbance stacks, and has the positive-definite disturbance covariances (17).  By (16),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X^{(a)})
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}D_\pi^{(a)}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-\mathsf T}
=\Sigma^{aa}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus every \(\pi\) has a full component-aligned completion of the marginal-only observable.  Conversely, at a fixed order the positive-definite marginal covariance and the population normal equations uniquely determine \(B_\pi^{(a)}\).  Therefore
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{marg}}=\mathfrak S_V,
\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}=\{T_\pi:\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\}.
\tag{20}
\]

Assume again that \(\theta\notin\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\), and choose \(\pi\ne\pi_0\), possible because \(p\ge2\).  If \(T_\pi=T_{\pi_0}\), then \(L_\pi^{(a)}=L_{\pi_0}^{(a)}\) for every \(a\).  For the true order, the structural equations and component alignment give
\[
R_{\pi_0}^{ab}
=\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon^{(a)},\varepsilon^{(b)})
=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\Gamma_j[a,b])
\tag{21}
\]
for every pair.  Equality of residualizers would make \(R_\pi^{ab}=R_{\pi_0}^{ab}\), so (10) would give \(F_\pi^{ab}(\theta)=0\) for every \(a<b\).  This contradicts \(Q_{\mathrm{all}}(\theta)\ne0\).  Hence \(T_\pi\ne T_{\pi_0}\), and (20) implies
\[
|\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}|\ge2.
\tag{22}
\]

If \(|C|\le1\), there is no paired cross-view block and \(\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}\ne\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)\) by (14) and (22).  If \(|C|=2\), (12)--(14) give \(\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)=\{T_{\pi_0}\}\).  Since \(M\) is finite and \(C=M\) is feasible, the minimum exists and
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{cal}}=2.
\tag{23}
\]
The original definition of \(S_{\min}\) minimizes over sets of size at least two, so the same argument gives \(|S_{\min}|=2\).  A two-element calibration set is proper in \(M\) exactly when \(m\ge3\).  Every step used only the defining restrictions of \(\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) and \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\); neither edge minimality nor the diversity condition of HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM was invoked.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives the sharp generic calibration frontier: the absence of every paired cross-block prevents identification of the structural tuple, while any single preassigned pair identifies it, simultaneously across all pair choices outside one explicit proper algebraic set. \textit{Gap.} HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM obtains full-pair identification under edgewise diversity on a broader disturbance class. The present result is for a narrower component-aligned Gaussian class, removes that diversity requirement within this class, changes the observation regime to one arbitrary pair, and adds the matching marginal-only impossibility result; it is not a class-containment claim. \textit{Consumer.} A coordinate-aligned study can certify that exactly two jointly calibrated views are generically necessary and sufficient for order and full-tuple recovery, while using every other view marginally for its coefficients.

\begin{lemma}[lem:chordal-positive-definite-completion]\label{lem:chordal-positive-definite-completion}
Let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\) be a finite undirected graph with all diagonal positions specified.  Call \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) completable when every real symmetric partial matrix whose principal submatrix on every clique is positive definite admits a positive-definite completion.  Then \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is completable if and only if it is chordal.  Consequently, if \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is chordal, \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) admits a positive-definite completion if and only if \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})[C,C]\succ0\) for every maximal clique \(C\) of \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\); a completion can be produced by the sequential filling process for chordal partial matrices.
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the classical graph-theoretic existence criterion needed to turn coordinatewise partial disturbance covariances into full positive-definite certificates on chordal observation graphs. \textit{Gap.} GroneEtAl1984PositiveDefiniteCompletion supplies the matrix-completion substrate; it does not derive the LiMVAM residual criterion, coefficient uniqueness, or the four-cycle observed-moment obstruction proved here. \textit{Consumer.} The chordal branch of \(\text{thm:calibration-graph-completion}\) invokes this result after reducing LiMVAM feasibility to one partial covariance matrix per variable.

\begin{theorem}[thm:calibration-graph-completion]\label{thm:calibration-graph-completion}
Let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\) be an arbitrary finite simple undirected view graph, let \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) be as in \(\text{def:observed-view-graph-moments}\), and fix \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\).  There exists a full member \((P,B^0,\varepsilon,\Gamma,\pi)\in\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) whose covariance restricts to \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) if and only if both of the following conditions hold:
\[
\operatorname{offdiag}(R_\pi^{ab})=0
\quad\text{for every }\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}},
\tag{G1}
\]
and, for every \(j\in V\), the partial matrix \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) admits a positive-definite completion.  In every such full completion the coefficient tuple is uniquely \(T_\pi\), and any independent choice of positive-definite completions of the matrices \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) supplies the full disturbance-covariance certificate.

If \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is chordal, the second condition is equivalent to
\[
\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})[C,C]\succ0
\quad\text{for every }j\in V
\text{ and every maximal clique }C\text{ of }G_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\tag{G2}
\]
Under (G2), a constructive certificate for coordinate \(j\) is the unique optimizer
\[
\widehat\Gamma_{\pi,j}
=\operatorname*{argmax}_{K\in\mathbb S_+^m}
\left\{\det K:
K[a,a]=\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[a,a]\ (a\in M),\ 
K[a,b]=\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[a,b]\ (\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}})
\right\};
\tag{G3}
\]
this optimizer exists, is positive definite, and is unique.

Chordality cannot be omitted from the clique test.  When \(m=4\), take the chordless cycle with edges \(12,23,34,41\), put \(\Sigma^{aa}=I_p\), and on those four edges put \(\Sigma^{ab}=\rho_{ab}I_p\), where
\[
(\rho_{12},\rho_{23},\rho_{34},\rho_{41})=(0.9,0.9,0.9,-0.9).
\tag{G4}
\]
For every \(\pi\), all observed residual cross-blocks are diagonal and every specified two-by-two coordinate clique is positive definite, but no coordinate partial matrix in (G4) has even a positive-semidefinite completion.  Finally, when \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is the clique on \(S\) together with isolated vertices \(M\setminus S\), this theorem reduces to \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\), and the block-diagonal completion is exactly \(\Gamma_{\pi,j}\) from \(\text{def:noise-certificate}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first record consequences of the marginal positive definiteness built into \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\).  Fix \(a\in M\), and for \(k\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\) put
\[
P_k=\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(k-1)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\), every principal matrix \(\Sigma^{aa}[P_k,P_k]\) is positive definite and hence invertible.  Thus \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) gives the unique least-squares coefficient of \(X_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}\) on \(X_{P_k}^{(a)}\).  If
\[
e_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}
=X_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}
-B_\pi^{(a)}[\pi(k),P_k]X_{P_k}^{(a)},
\tag{2}
\]
the normal equations give
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P\!\left(e_{\pi(k)}^{(a)},X_{P_k}^{(a)}\right)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(r<k\), equation (2) shows that \(e_{\pi(r)}^{(a)}\) lies in the linear span of \(X_{P_k}^{(a)}\); hence (3) implies
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P\!\left(e_{\pi(k)}^{(a)},e_{\pi(r)}^{(a)}\right)=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(e^{(a)}=L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}\), equations (2)--(4) prove that
\[
R_\pi^{aa}
=L_\pi^{(a)}\Sigma^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\quad\text{is diagonal}.
\tag{5}
\]
In the order \(\pi\), \(L_\pi^{(a)}\) is unit lower triangular, so it is invertible.  Congruence in (5) therefore also gives
\[
R_\pi^{aa}\succ0,
\qquad R_\pi^{aa}[j,j]>0
\quad(a\in M,\ j\in V).
\tag{6}
\]

We prove necessity.  Suppose a full member \((P,B^0,\varepsilon,\Gamma,\pi)\in\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) restricts to \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\).  By \(\text{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}\), the within-view disturbance covariance is diagonal.  By \(\text{ass:disturbance-nondegeneracy}\), its diagonal entries satisfy
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P(\varepsilon_j^{(a)})
=\Gamma_j[a,a]>0.
\tag{7}
\]
The common-order condition makes \(I_p-B^{0,(a)}\) unit lower triangular in order \(\pi\), hence invertible.  The structural equations consequently express every variable in \(P_k\) as a linear combination of disturbances whose labels belong to \(P_k\).  Cross-variable orthogonality therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P\!\left(\varepsilon_{\pi(k)}^{(a)},X_{P_k}^{(a)}\right)=0.
\tag{8}
\]
The equation for \(X_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}\), together with (8), satisfies the population normal equations for regression on \(X_{P_k}^{(a)}\).  Since the regressor covariance is invertible, uniqueness of their solution and \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) imply
\[
B^{0,(a)}=B_\pi^{(a)},
\qquad
L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}=\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the coefficient tuple is necessarily \(T_\pi\).  For every observed edge \(\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}\), equations (9), \(\text{def:residual-cross-blocks}\), and cross-variable orthogonality give
\[
R_\pi^{ab}
=\operatorname{Cov}_P(\varepsilon^{(a)},\varepsilon^{(b)})
=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\Gamma_j[a,b]).
\tag{10}
\]
This proves (G1).  Moreover, for each \(j\in V\), the matrix \(\Gamma_j\succ0\) agrees with the diagonal and all specified edge entries of \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\), by (7), (9), and (10).  Hence \(\Gamma_j\) is a positive-definite completion of that partial matrix.  This proves necessity and coefficient uniqueness.

We prove sufficiency constructively.  Assume (G1), and for every \(j\in V\) choose a positive-definite completion \(\widetilde\Gamma_j\in\mathbb S_{++}^m\) of \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  On a product probability space, let \((E_j)_{j\in V}\) be mutually independent centered Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^m\) satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(E_j)=\widetilde\Gamma_j,
\qquad
\widetilde\varepsilon_j^{(a)}=E_j[a].
\tag{11}
\]
Define, for every \(a\in M\),
\[
\widetilde X^{(a)}
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{12}
\]
The inverse exists by unit triangularity.  Equations (11)--(12) define a jointly centered Gaussian system satisfying
\[
\widetilde X^{(a)}
=B_\pi^{(a)}\widetilde X^{(a)}+\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{13}
\]
The matrices \(B_\pi^{(a)}\) share order \(\pi\) by construction.  Independence in (11) gives cross-variable disturbance orthogonality, and \(\widetilde\Gamma_j\succ0\) gives disturbance nondegeneracy.  Thus (11)--(13) have all member properties of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\).

The diagonal specifications of the partial matrices, (5), and (11) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)})
&=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\widetilde\Gamma_j[a,a])\\
&=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(R_\pi^{aa}[j,j])
=R_\pi^{aa}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (12), (14), and \(\text{def:residual-cross-blocks}\) yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X^{(a)})
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}R_\pi^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-\mathsf T}
=\Sigma^{aa}.
\tag{15}
\]
For every observed edge \(\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the edge specifications and (G1) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)},\widetilde\varepsilon^{(b)})
&=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\widetilde\Gamma_j[a,b])\\
&=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(R_\pi^{ab}[j,j])
=R_\pi^{ab}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Multiplying (16) on the left by \((L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\) and on the right by \((L_\pi^{(b)})^{-\mathsf T}\) shows that the constructed cross-covariance equals \(\Sigma^{ab}\).  Hence the full system restricts to \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\).  Equations (11) and (16) also show directly that any independent selection of the coordinatewise completions supplies the asserted full disturbance-covariance certificate.  This proves sufficiency.

Suppose next that \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is chordal.  If \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}\) has a positive-definite completion, then its principal submatrix on each maximal clique is positive definite.  Conversely, if every displayed maximal-clique submatrix is positive definite, then every specified clique submatrix is positive definite because it is a principal submatrix of a maximal-clique submatrix.  The cited chordal completion result \(\text{lem:chordal-positive-definite-completion}\) then supplies a positive-definite completion.  This proves the equivalence with (G2), including the sequential constructive completion asserted by that lemma.

It remains to verify the optimizer in (G3).  Let \(\mathcal K_j\) denote its feasible set.  Under (G2), the preceding paragraph supplies \(K_0\in\mathcal K_j\cap\mathbb S_{++}^m\).  Thus \(\det K_0>0\).  The set \(\mathcal K_j\) is closed.  For every \(K\in\mathcal K_j\), positivity of each two-by-two principal minor gives
\[
|K[a,b]|^2\le K[a,a]K[b,b]
=\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[a,a]\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[b,b].
\tag{17}
\]
All its diagonal entries are fixed, so (17) makes \(\mathcal K_j\) bounded.  It is therefore compact, and continuity of \(\det\) gives a maximizer \(K_\star\).  Since \(\det K_\star\ge\det K_0>0\) and \(K_\star\succeq0\), necessarily \(K_\star\succ0\).  For \(K\succ0\) and a nonzero symmetric direction \(H\), direct differentiation gives
\[
D^2\log\det(K)[H,H]
=-\operatorname{tr}(K^{-1}HK^{-1}H)
=-\lVert K^{-1/2}HK^{-1/2}\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2<0.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence \(\log\det\) is strictly concave on \(\mathbb S_{++}^m\).  If two distinct matrices maximized (G3), both would be positive definite by the preceding determinant argument, and their midpoint would be feasible and would have strictly larger log-determinant by (18), a contradiction.  Thus (G3) has a unique positive-definite optimizer.

We now establish the claimed nonchordal obstruction.  For the data in (G4), \(\Sigma^{aa}=I_p\) implies, directly from \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\),
\[
B_\pi^{(a)}=0,
\qquad L_\pi^{(a)}=I_p
\quad(a\in M,\ \pi\in\mathfrak S_V).
\tag{19}
\]
Thus every observed residual block is \(R_\pi^{ab}=\rho_{ab}I_p\), which is diagonal.  The joint covariance on an observed edge is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
I_p&\rho_{ab}I_p\\
\rho_{ab}I_p&I_p
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{20}
\]
whose eigenvalues are \(1+\rho_{ab}\) and \(1-\rho_{ab}\), each with multiplicity \(p\).  Because \(|\rho_{ab}|=0.9<1\), every specified two-by-two coordinate clique is positive definite.

Assume for contradiction that one coordinate partial matrix has a positive-semidefinite completion \(K\).  Taking the rows of a square root of \(K\) gives vectors \(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4\) such that
\[
K[a,b]=\langle u_a,u_b\rangle.
\tag{21}
\]
The unit diagonal gives \(\lVert u_a\rVert=1\).  The three consecutive correlations equal to \(0.9\) imply
\[
\lVert u_1-u_2\rVert
=\lVert u_2-u_3\rVert
=\lVert u_3-u_4\rVert
=\sqrt{2-2(0.9)}=\sqrt{0.2}.
\tag{22}
\]
By the triangle inequality,
\[
\lVert u_1-u_4\rVert\le3\sqrt{0.2}.
\tag{23}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\langle u_1,u_4\rangle
=1-\frac12\lVert u_1-u_4\rVert^2
\ge 1-\frac12(3\sqrt{0.2})^2
=0.1,
\tag{24}
\]
contradicting the specified edge value \(K[1,4]=-0.9\).  No positive-semidefinite, and hence no positive-definite, completion exists.  This proves that chordality cannot be dropped from the local clique criterion.

Finally, let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) be the clique on \(S\) together with the isolated vertices \(M\setminus S\), in the data-generating setting of \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\).  The edge condition (G1) is then exactly the residual-diagonality condition defining \(\Pi_S(\mathcal O_S)\).  For an order satisfying it, that lemma gives
\[
\bigl(R_\pi^{ab}[j,j]\bigr)_{a,b\in S}\succ0
\quad(j\in V),
\tag{25}
\]
while (6) gives positive singleton entries for every isolated view.  Setting all unspecified entries to zero produces the positive-definite direct sum of (25) and those singleton blocks, exactly \(\Gamma_{\pi,j}\) from \(\text{def:noise-certificate}\).  Therefore the graph theorem specializes to \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\) for this calibration design.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem gives the complete population feasibility test for an arbitrary graph of paired views and separates edgewise component alignment from the genuinely global positive-definite completion obstruction. \textit{Gap.} The selected-clique certificate in \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\) is automatically completable, while GroneEtAl1984PositiveDefiniteCompletion addresses partial matrices without the LiMVAM residual reduction.  The theorem joins these ingredients and proves that nonchordal calibration graphs require more than positive edge blocks. \textit{Consumer.} A partially paired multi-view study can test every candidate order on its actually observed view graph, return coordinatewise max-determinant noise certificates when feasible, and detect nonchordal designs for which local edge checks are insufficient.

\begin{lemma}[lem:completion-margin-characterization]\label{lem:completion-margin-characterization}
For every finite simple observed-view graph \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\), the zero-fill map satisfies \(A_GE_G=I_{\mathcal Y_G}\) and
\[
\|E_G(d)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le m\|d\|_{\max}
\quad(d\in\mathcal Y_G).
\]
The value \(\chi_G(q)\) is finite for every \(q\in\mathcal Y_G\), and
\[
\chi_G(q)>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\text{ has a positive-definite completion}.
\]
Moreover, for all \(q,q'\in\mathcal Y_G\),
\[
|\chi_G(q)-\chi_G(q')|\le m\|q-q'\|_{\max}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity \(A_GE_G=I_{\mathcal Y_G}\) follows directly from the definitions: zero filling changes no specified coordinate.  Put \(D=E_G(d)\).  Every row of \(D\) has at most \(m\) entries, each bounded in absolute value by \(\|d\|_{\max}\).  The maximum absolute row-sum bound for a symmetric matrix therefore gives
\[
\|E_G(d)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\|D\|_{\infty}
\le m\|d\|_{\max}.
\tag{1}
\]
The feasible set in the definition of \(\chi_G(q)\) is nonempty: \(K=E_G(q)\) satisfies \(A_GK=q\) and \(K\succeq\lambda_{\min}(K)I_m\).  It is bounded above because every feasible pair \((K,t)\) obeys
\[
q_a=K[a,a]\ge t
\quad(a\in M),
\tag{2}
\]
so \(t\le\min_{a\in M}q_a\).  Hence \(\chi_G(q)\in\mathbb R\).

If \(K\succ0\) and \(A_GK=q\), then \(K\succeq\lambda_{\min}(K)I_m\) with \(\lambda_{\min}(K)>0\).  Thus
\[
\chi_G(q)\ge\lambda_{\min}(K)>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Conversely, suppose \(\chi_G(q)>0\) and choose \(s\) with \(0<s<\chi_G(q)\).  By the defining property of a supremum, some feasible \((K,t)\) has \(t>s\); otherwise \(s\) would be an upper bound.  Then
\[
K\succeq tI_m\succ0,
\qquad A_GK=q,
\tag{4}
\]
so \(K\) is a positive-definite completion.  Equations (3)--(4) prove the equivalence.

Finally fix \(q,q'\), put \(d=q'-q\), and let \((K,t)\) be feasible for \(q\).  Linearity, the right-inverse identity, and (1) give
\[
A_G(K+E_Gd)=q',
\qquad
K+E_Gd\succeq
\bigl(t-\|E_Gd\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr)I_m
\succeq
\bigl(t-m\|d\|_{\max}\bigr)I_m.
\tag{5}
\]
Taking the supremum over all feasible \(t\) in (5) yields
\[
\chi_G(q')\ge\chi_G(q)-m\|q'-q\|_{\max}.
\tag{6}
\]
Interchanging \(q\) and \(q'\) gives the reverse inequality.  Combining the two proves the asserted Lipschitz bound.  This proof uses no chordality assumption.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The completion value turns arbitrary-graph positive-definite feasibility into a continuous scalar criterion and supplies the perturbation bound needed for region inversion. \textit{Gap.} The population completion theorem characterizes existence but does not provide the max-norm modulus needed to exclude sample-induced completion failures. \textit{Consumer.} The arbitrary-graph covariance-region theorem uses this lemma to control the completion component of every wrong-order feasibility score.

\begin{theorem}[thm:graph-covariance-region-inversion]\label{thm:graph-covariance-region-inversion}
Let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\) be an arbitrary finite simple view graph, and let \(\mathcal O_G=\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) be generated by at least one member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\).  Then the observable set \(\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G)\) is exactly the set of full-view coefficient tuples among all component-aligned Gaussian completions of \(\mathcal O_G\), and \(\prec_G\) is their common ancestral relation.

Assume the centered Gaussian law, the edgewise paired sampling condition, and the marginal sampling condition.  Assume also that every edge-joint covariance \(\Sigma^e\) and every marginal covariance \(\Sigma^{aa}\) is positive definite.  No independence between distinct covariance sources is required.  If every \(\eta_{e,n}<1\) and every \(\eta_{a,n}<1\), then
\[
\Pr\{\mathcal O_G\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\}\ge1-\alpha,
\qquad
\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G)\subseteq\widehat\Theta_{G,n}(\alpha)
\quad\text{on this event}.
\]
For every positive-definite \(\mathcal O'_G\) with
\[
u=d_{\mathrm{obs}}(\mathcal O_G,\mathcal O'_G)
\le\min\{\lambda_*/2,L_B^{-1}\},
\]
every order \(\pi\), view \(a\), observed edge \(\{a,b\}\), and coordinate \(j\) satisfy
\[
\|B_\pi^{(a)}(\mathcal O'_G)-B_\pi^{(a)}(\mathcal O_G)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le L_Bu,
\]
\[
\|R_\pi^{ab}(\mathcal O'_G)-R_\pi^{ab}(\mathcal O_G)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\omega_{\mathrm{feas}}(u)=L_Ru,
\]
where the residual bound also holds for \(a=b\), and
\[
|\chi_G(\mathcal Q'_{\pi,j})-\chi_G(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j})|
\le\omega_{G,\mathrm{feas}}(u)=mL_Ru.
\]
Every fixed-order directed-path product changes by at most \(\omega_{\mathrm{path}}(u)=L_{\mathrm{path}}L_Bu\).  If \(\delta_{\mathrm{feas}}(\mathcal O_G)\ge\kappa_G>0\) and \(\omega_{G,\mathrm{feas}}(u)\le\kappa_G/2\), then
\[
\Pi_G(\mathcal O'_G)\subseteq\Pi_G(\mathcal O_G).
\]

On the coverage event, assume in addition \(\delta_{\mathrm{path}}(\mathcal O_G)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}>0\) and
\[
2\bar r_n(\alpha)\le\min\{\lambda_*/2,L_B^{-1}\},
\quad
\omega_{G,\mathrm{feas}}(2\bar r_n(\alpha))\le\kappa_G/2,
\quad
\omega_{\mathrm{path}}(2\bar r_n(\alpha))\le\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}/2.
\]
Then
\[
d_{\mathrm H}^{d_{\mathrm{coef}}}
(\widehat\Theta_{G,n}(\alpha),\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G))
\le2L_B\bar r_n(\alpha),
\qquad
\widehat\prec_{G,n}(\alpha)=\prec_G.
\]
Consequently, if every edge and marginal sample size diverges, \(\alpha_n\to0\), and
\[
\max_{e\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}}\eta_{e,n}(\alpha_n)
\vee\max_{a\in M}\eta_{a,n}(\alpha_n)\longrightarrow0,
\]
then under the two fixed positive margins the graph coefficient set is Hausdorff consistent and the graph-essential poset is recovered exactly with probability tending to one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first separate the causal target from its observable recovery map.  By \(\text{thm:calibration-graph-completion}\), a fixed order \(\pi\) supports a full member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) restricting to \(\mathcal O_G\) if and only if all observed-edge residual blocks are diagonal and every \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}\) has a positive-definite completion; in that event its coefficient tuple is uniquely \(T_\pi\).  By \(\text{lem:completion-margin-characterization}\), the latter condition is equivalent to \(\chi_G(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j})>0\) for every \(j\).  These are exactly the two tests in \(\text{def:graph-compatible-orders}\).  Therefore
\[
\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G)
=\{\text{coefficient tuples of full component-aligned Gaussian completions of }\mathcal O_G\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every \(G_T\) in (1) is acyclic because its nonzero coefficients respect its inducing order.  Its strict reachability relation is therefore irreflexive and transitive.  The intersection of these relations is again irreflexive and transitive, so \(\prec_G\) is a strict partial order; (1) and its definition show that it is precisely the ancestry shared by all full completions.

We next prove simultaneous covariance coverage.  There are \(K_G=|E_{\mathrm{obs}}|+m\) sources.  Consider one source with dimension and sample size
\[
(d,n,\Sigma_0)=
\begin{cases}
(2p,n_e,\Sigma^e),&e\in E_{\mathrm{obs}},\\
(p,n_a,\Sigma^{aa}),&a\in M.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The appropriate nondegeneracy assumption gives \(\Sigma_0\succ0\).  If \(Y\) is its \(n\times d\) data matrix, define
\[
G=Y\Sigma_0^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]
The inverse exists by the positive-definiteness premise.  The centered Gaussian assumption makes each row of \(G\) standard multivariate normal, hence its coordinates are independent standard normal variables.  The edgewise paired sampling assumption or the marginal sampling assumption, according to (2), makes the rows independent and identically distributed.  Thus all entries of \(G\) are independent standard normal variables.

Put
\[
t_\alpha=\sqrt{2\log(2K_G/\alpha)},
\qquad
\eta=\sqrt{d/n}+t_\alpha/\sqrt n.
\tag{4}
\]
The condition \(\eta<1\) implies \(n>d\), so \(\text{lem:gaussian-extreme-singular-values}\) applies.  Since \(2\exp(-t_\alpha^2/2)=\alpha/K_G\), with probability at least \(1-\alpha/K_G\),
\[
1-\eta\le s_{\min}(G)/\sqrt n
\le s_{\max}(G)/\sqrt n\le1+\eta.
\tag{5}
\]
The empirical covariance obeys
\[
\widehat\Sigma
=\frac1nY^{\mathsf T}Y
=\Sigma_0^{1/2}\frac{G^{\mathsf T}G}{n}\Sigma_0^{1/2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Squaring (5) and applying the congruence in (6), with \(\ell=(1-\eta)^2\) and \(v=(1+\eta)^2\), gives
\[
\ell\Sigma_0\preceq\widehat\Sigma\preceq v\Sigma_0.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(\ell>0\), (7) implies
\[
\Sigma_0\preceq\ell^{-1}\widehat\Sigma,
\qquad
\Sigma_0\succeq v^{-1}\widehat\Sigma.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(c=\ell^{-1}-1\), one has \(1-v^{-1}\le c\).  Hence (8) yields
\[
-c\widehat\Sigma\preceq\Sigma_0-\widehat\Sigma
\preceq c\widehat\Sigma,
\tag{9}
\]
and taking Rayleigh quotients proves
\[
\|\Sigma_0-\widehat\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\bigl((1-\eta)^{-2}-1\bigr)\|\widehat\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (10) is exactly the appropriate edge or marginal radius in \(\text{def:covariance-region}\).  A union bound over the \(K_G\) sources, which does not use independence between sources, gives an event \(\mathcal E_n(\alpha)\) with
\[
\Pr\{\mathcal E_n(\alpha)\}\ge1-K_G(\alpha/K_G)=1-\alpha.
\tag{11}
\]
On this event every regional inequality holds for the true collection.  The edge-joint and marginal nondegeneracy assumptions supply all positive-definiteness constraints, so
\[
\mathcal O_G\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha).
\tag{12}
\]
The union in \(\text{def:region-inversion}\) then gives
\[
\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G)\subseteq\widehat\Theta_{G,n}(\alpha).
\tag{13}
\]

We now prove the deterministic perturbation bounds.  Fix \(\mathcal O'_G\), put \(u=d_{\mathrm{obs}}(\mathcal O_G,\mathcal O'_G)\), and assume the displayed upper bound on \(u\).  Fix an order \(\pi\), a view \(a\), a row label \(j\), and let \(P_j\) be the predecessors of \(j\) under \(\pi\).  The row is zero when \(P_j=\varnothing\).  Otherwise, by \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\),
\[
\beta=\Sigma^{aa}[j,P_j]\Sigma^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1},
\qquad
\beta'=\Sigma'^{aa}[j,P_j]\Sigma'^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1}.
\tag{14}
\]
The marginal nondegeneracy assumption and finiteness of \(M\) give \(\lambda_*>0\).  Coordinate compression does not increase operator norm, so for every unit vector \(x\),
\[
x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma'^{aa}[P_j,P_j]x
\ge\lambda_*-u\ge\lambda_*/2.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus
\[
\|\Sigma'^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\lambda_*.
\tag{16}
\]
The resolvent identity, (16), and \(\|\Sigma^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lambda_*^{-1}\) give
\[
\|\Sigma'^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1}-\Sigma^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le2u/\lambda_*^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(\|\Sigma^{aa}[j,P_j]\|_2\le K_*\), subtracting the two equations in (14), adding and subtracting \(\Sigma^{aa}[j,P_j]\Sigma'^{aa}[P_j,P_j]^{-1}\), and using (16)--(17) yields
\[
\|\beta'-\beta\|_2
\le2u(\lambda_*^{-1}+K_*\lambda_*^{-2}).
\tag{18}
\]
There are at most \(p\) rows.  Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm, (18) and the definition of \(L_B\) imply
\[
\|B_\pi^{(a)}(\mathcal O'_G)-B_\pi^{(a)}(\mathcal O_G)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le L_Bu.
\tag{19}
\]

The same calculation and \(u\le\lambda_*/2\) give
\[
\|\beta\|_2,\|\beta'\|_2\le1+2K_*/\lambda_*.
\tag{20}
\]
Therefore the population and perturbed residualizers obey
\[
\|L_\pi^{(a)}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|L_\pi'{}^{(a)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le1+\sqrt p(1+2K_*/\lambda_*)=G_*.
\tag{21}
\]
For either \(a=b\) or \(\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}\), write the relevant residualizers as \(L_a,L_b,L'_a,L'_b\).  The residual definition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_\pi'{}^{ab}-R_\pi^{ab}
={}&L'_a(\Sigma'^{ab}-\Sigma^{ab})L_b'{}^{\mathsf T}
+(L'_a-L_a)\Sigma^{ab}L_b'{}^{\mathsf T}\\
&+L_a\Sigma^{ab}(L'_b-L_b)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Every cross block is a compression of its edge-joint covariance, so its norm and its perturbation are bounded by \(K_*\) and \(u\), respectively.  Equations (19), (21), and submultiplicativity thus imply
\[
\|R_\pi'{}^{ab}-R_\pi^{ab}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le(G_*^2+2L_BG_*K_*)u
\le L_Ru=\omega_{\mathrm{feas}}(u).
\tag{23}
\]
The specified entries of \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}\) are diagonal entries of precisely these residual blocks.  Since an entrywise change is at most the operator-norm change, (23) gives
\[
\|\mathcal Q'_{\pi,j}-\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}\|_{\max}\le L_Ru.
\tag{24}
\]
The Lipschitz conclusion of \(\text{lem:completion-margin-characterization}\) and (24) prove
\[
|\chi_G(\mathcal Q'_{\pi,j})-\chi_G(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j})|
\le mL_Ru=\omega_{G,\mathrm{feas}}(u).
\tag{25}
\]

For an ordered variable pair \((v,w)\), put \(w_{vw}(T)=\max_{a\in M}|B_{wv}^{(a)}|\).  Equation (19) implies
\[
|w_{vw}(T')-w_{vw}(T)|\le L_Bu.
\tag{26}
\]
Population weights are at most \(K_B\), and \(L_Bu\le1\) makes perturbed weights at most \(K_B+1\).  For a fixed-order path \(g=(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell)\), where \(1\le\ell\le p-1\), telescoping the product and using (26) gives
\[
\left|\prod_{h=1}^{\ell}w_{v_{h-1}v_h}(T')-
\prod_{h=1}^{\ell}w_{v_{h-1}v_h}(T)\right|
\le\ell(K_B+1)^{\ell-1}L_Bu
\le L_{\mathrm{path}}L_Bu.
\tag{27}
\]

For fixed \(\pi\), maxima over finitely many entries preserve Lipschitz bounds.  Equations (23) and (25), together with the one-Lipschitz property of \(x\mapsto[-x]_+\), imply
\[
|r_\pi(\mathcal O'_G)-r_\pi(\mathcal O_G)|\le L_Ru,
\qquad
|c_\pi(\mathcal O'_G)-c_\pi(\mathcal O_G)|\le mL_Ru.
\tag{28}
\]
Because \(m\ge2\), another finite-maximum inequality gives
\[
\left|\max\{r_\pi(\mathcal O'_G),c_\pi(\mathcal O'_G)\}
-\max\{r_\pi(\mathcal O_G),c_\pi(\mathcal O_G)\}\right|
\le mL_Ru.
\tag{29}
\]
If \(\pi\in\Pi_G(\mathcal O'_G)\), then \(r_\pi(\mathcal O'_G)=c_\pi(\mathcal O'_G)=0\).  If also \(\pi\notin\Pi_G(\mathcal O_G)\), the graph feasibility-margin assumption and its definition give
\[
\max\{r_\pi(\mathcal O_G),c_\pi(\mathcal O_G)\}\ge\kappa_G.
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (29)--(30) contradict \(mL_Ru\le\kappa_G/2\).  Therefore
\[
\Pi_G(\mathcal O'_G)\subseteq\Pi_G(\mathcal O_G).
\tag{31}
\]
The positive graph margin is used essentially here: it excludes an incompatible order with diagonal residuals and \(\min_j\chi_G(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j})=0\), for which the score in (30) would vanish.

Return to the coverage event.  If \(\mathcal O'_G\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\), then the true and candidate edge-joint blocks are each within \(r_{e,n}(\alpha)\) of the same edge empirical covariance, and the analogous assertion holds for each marginal source.  The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{obs}}(\mathcal O_G,\mathcal O'_G)
\le2\bar r_n(\alpha).
\tag{32}
\]
Under the displayed small-radius conditions, every \(T'\in\widehat\Theta_{G,n}(\alpha)\) has a representation
\[
T'=T_\pi(\mathcal O'_G),
\quad
\mathcal O'_G\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha),
\quad
\pi\in\Pi_G(\mathcal O'_G).
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (31)--(32) imply \(\pi\in\Pi_G(\mathcal O_G)\), while (19) implies
\[
d_{\mathrm{coef}}(T',T_\pi(\mathcal O_G))
\le2L_B\bar r_n(\alpha).
\tag{34}
\]
Conversely, (13) includes every population tuple in the estimated set.  Thus both directed Hausdorff distances are controlled, and
\[
d_{\mathrm H}^{d_{\mathrm{coef}}}
(\widehat\Theta_{G,n}(\alpha),\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G))
\le2L_B\bar r_n(\alpha).
\tag{35}
\]

Inclusion (13) immediately gives \(\widehat\prec_{G,n}(\alpha)\subseteq\prec_G\).  Conversely, fix \(i\prec_Gj\) and a tuple \(T'\) represented as in (33).  Its counterpart \(T=T_\pi(\mathcal O_G)\) belongs to \(\Theta_G(\mathcal O_G)\) by (31).  The graph path-margin assumption and definition provide an \(i\)-to-\(j\) path in \(G_T\) whose weight product is at least \(\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}\).  Equations (27) and (32) leave the same path product in \(T'\) at least
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}-
\omega_{\mathrm{path}}(2\bar r_n(\alpha))
\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}/2>0.
\tag{36}
\]
Every factor on this path is therefore positive, so the path occurs in \(G_{T'}\).  Since \(T'\) was arbitrary, \(i\widehat\prec_{G,n}(\alpha)j\).  Hence
\[
\widehat\prec_{G,n}(\alpha)=\prec_G.
\tag{37}
\]

For consistency, set \(\alpha=\alpha_n\) and let
\[
h_n=\max_{e\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}}\eta_{e,n}(\alpha_n)
\vee\max_{a\in M}\eta_{a,n}(\alpha_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{38}
\]
On the simultaneous singular-value event, (7) gives, source by source,
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le(1+h_n)^2\|\Sigma_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{39}
\]
There are finitely many sources and their population covariances are fixed, while \((1-h_n)^{-2}-1\to0\).  Equations (39) and the radius definitions therefore imply
\[
\bar r_n(\alpha_n)\xrightarrow{P}0.
\tag{40}
\]
The probability of the simultaneous event is at least \(1-\alpha_n\to1\).  The constants \(\lambda_*,L_B,L_R,L_{\mathrm{path}},\kappa_G,\kappa_{\mathrm{path}}\) are fixed, finite, and positive; positivity of \(\lambda_*\) follows from all-marginal nondegeneracy.  Thus (40) makes all three small-radius conditions hold with probability tending to one.  Equations (35), (37), and (40) prove Hausdorff consistency and exact graph-essential-poset recovery in probability.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem turns arbitrary-graph population compatibility into an uncertainty-aware coefficient-set and common-ancestry procedure using all paired edge sources and all marginal sources. \textit{Gap.} Neither general contour inversion nor recursive Gaussian-SEM recovery supplies a positive-definite-completion score, its graph perturbation modulus, or the resulting LiMVAM set and poset guarantees. \textit{Consumer.} A future partially paired MEG or fMRI analysis can report graph-compatible coefficient uncertainty only after checking common-target sampling, covariance nondegeneracy, component alignment, and separation from completion and path boundaries.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

All identification and inference results remain confined to the component-aligned Gaussian subclass. Arbitrary-graph inference requires positive-definite covariance for every edge source and every marginal source, plus a fixed graph feasibility margin that explicitly rules out incompatible orders lying on the boundary \(\min_j\chi_G(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j})=0\). Exact ancestry additionally requires a fixed path-product margin. The sourcewise-iid samples must share one target population, although they need not be mutually independent. Generic pair identification is relative to a fixed-order finite-dimensional parameterization and supplies no uniform distance from its exceptional algebraic set. The \(2^p\)-state recursion counts exact-real arithmetic; duplicate-free numerical tuple enumeration beyond order enumeration is not claimed.

\section{Honest scope}

The arbitrary-view-graph completion theorem, signed completion margin, graph covariance-region inversion, generic calibration elbow, fixed-order recovery, removed-set recursion, and essential-poset conclusions apply only to the component-aligned Gaussian subclass with positive-definite coordinate disturbance covariances. They do not transfer to unrestricted LiMVAMs or establish the assumptions used by Heurtebise et al. Finite-sample coverage assumes sourcewise-iid edge and marginal samples from one target law, edge-joint and all-marginal positive definiteness, and no selection or transport. Hausdorff and exact-poset recovery are off the stated completion/residual and support boundaries. Cam-CAN MEG and fMRI remain prospective diagnostic applications rather than empirical validation.
