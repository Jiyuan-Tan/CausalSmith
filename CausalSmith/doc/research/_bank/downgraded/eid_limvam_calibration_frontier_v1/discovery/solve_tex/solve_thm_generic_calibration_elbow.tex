%%% FIELD generic-elbow-proof
Fix \(\theta\in\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}\), and write \(A^{(a)}=(I_p-B^{(a)})^{-1}\).  Strict lower triangularity in the order \(\pi_0\) makes \(B^{(a)}\) nilpotent, so
\[
(I_p-B^{(a)})^{-1}=\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}(B^{(a)})^q.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(\Gamma_i\succ0\) implies \(\Gamma_i[a,a]>0\).  The covariance formula in \(\text{def:fixed-order-open-parameter-space}\) therefore yields
\[
\Sigma_\theta^{aa}
=A^{(a)}\operatorname{diag}_{i\in V}(\Gamma_i[a,a])(A^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\succ0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every principal submatrix inverted in \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) is positive definite and has positive determinant.

Equation (1) shows that every entry of \(\Sigma_\theta^{ab}\) is polynomial in the free coordinates of \(\theta\).  Applying
\(C^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(C)/\det(C)\) to each predecessor covariance in \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) shows that every entry of \(B_\pi^{(a)}\), and consequently every entry of
\[
R_\pi^{ab}=(I_p-B_\pi^{(a)})\Sigma_\theta^{ab}
(I_p-B_\pi^{(b)})^{\mathsf T},
\tag{3}
\]
is rational with a denominator that is a product of the positive principal minors from (2).  Hence the denominator clearing in \(\text{def:pairwise-exceptional-variety}\) preserves whether an entry is zero.

Fix \(1\le a<b\le m\) and \(\pi\ne\pi_0\).  Two distinct total orders have a discordant pair, so there are \(u,v\in V\) such that \(u\) precedes \(v\) under \(\pi_0\), whereas \(v\) precedes \(u\) under \(\pi\).  Consider \(\theta_\pi^{ab}\) from \(\text{def:wrong-order-nonvanishing-witness}\).  For \(w\in\{u,v\}\), the covariance \(\Gamma_w\), after a simultaneous permutation of view coordinates, is the direct sum of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&r_w\\ r_w&1\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad I_{m-2}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \(1-r_w\), \(1+r_w\), and \(1\) with multiplicity \(m-2\), all positive because \(|r_w|<1\).  Every other \(\Gamma_i\) is \(I_m\), and the only nonzero coefficient, \(\beta\), respects \(\pi_0\).  Thus
\[
\theta_\pi^{ab}\in\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Variables outside \(\{u,v\}\) are uncorrelated with this two-variable subsystem and therefore do not change the following population regression coefficients when they occur in a predecessor set.  In views \(a\) and \(b\),
\[
X_u=\varepsilon_u,
\qquad
X_v=\beta\varepsilon_u+\varepsilon_v,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_u)=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_v)=1.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(v\) precedes \(u\) under \(\pi\), the wrong-order innovations are
\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_v=X_v,
\qquad
\widetilde\varepsilon_u
=X_u-\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_u,X_v)}{\operatorname{Var}(X_v)}X_v
=X_u-\frac{\beta}{1+\beta^2}X_v.
\tag{6}
\]
Using cross-variable disturbance orthogonality and the witness covariances in (6) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\widetilde\varepsilon_u^{(a)},
\widetilde\varepsilon_v^{(b)}\right)
&=\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
\frac{\varepsilon_u^{(a)}-\beta\varepsilon_v^{(a)}}{1+\beta^2},
\beta\varepsilon_u^{(b)}+\varepsilon_v^{(b)}\right)\\
&=\frac{\beta}{1+\beta^2}(r_u-r_v)\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
This is, up to transpose orientation, an off-diagonal entry of \(R_\pi^{ab}\).  Its cleared numerator is nonzero at \(\theta_\pi^{ab}\), so
\[
F_\pi^{ab}\not\equiv0.
\tag{8}
\]

There are finitely many unordered view pairs and wrong permutations.  A finite product of nonzero real polynomials is nonzero, since under any monomial order the leading coefficient of the product is the product of the nonzero leading coefficients.  It follows from (8) that
\[
Q_{\mathrm{all}}\not\equiv0.
\tag{9}
\]
The set \(\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) is nonempty and relatively open in its free Euclidean coordinates: the point having \(B^{(a)}=0\) and \(\Gamma_i=I_m\) belongs to it, and the positive-definite cone is open.  By \(\text{lem:nonzero-polynomial-null-set}\), (9) implies that \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\) is proper, has empty relative interior, and has relative Lebesgue measure zero.  Any parameter law absolutely continuous with respect to that relative Lebesgue measure therefore assigns probability zero to this exceptional set.

We next identify the exact fixed-pair failure locus.  Fix \(a<b\).  Positivity of all denominator factors, the fact that a sum of real squares vanishes exactly when every summand vanishes, and (3) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\pi^{ab}(\theta)=0
&\Longleftrightarrow f_{\pi,ij}^{ab}(\theta)=0
\quad\text{for every }i\ne j\\
&\Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{offdiag}(R_\pi^{ab}(\theta))=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Also \(R_\pi^{ba}=(R_\pi^{ab})^{\mathsf T}\), because \(\Sigma^{ba}=(\Sigma^{ab})^{\mathsf T}\), so either orientation tests the unordered pair.  The true order \(\pi_0\) is compatible by \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\).  Combining that fact, (10), and \(\text{def:pairwise-exceptional-variety}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta\in\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}
&\Longleftrightarrow
F_\pi^{ab}(\theta)=0\quad\text{for at least one }\pi\ne\pi_0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\Pi_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})\ne\{\pi_0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

If \(\theta\notin\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\), every factor of \(Q_{\mathrm{all}}(\theta)\) is nonzero.  Applying (10) for every pair and every wrong order yields simultaneously
\[
\Pi_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})=\{\pi_0\}
\quad\text{for every }1\le a<b\le m.
\tag{12}
\]
The complete coefficient characterization in \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\) then proves
\[
\Theta_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})=\{T_{\pi_0}\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Full calibration retains \(\pi_0\) and imposes every pair restriction, so its compatible-order set is contained in the compatible-order set for any one pair.  Equations (12)--(13) therefore give
\[
\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)=\{T_{\pi_0}\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus (13)--(14) prove, rather than define, equality between the structural coefficient target and the observable residual-restriction functional.

If the pairwise coefficient set differs from \(\{T_{\pi_0}\}\), then its compatible-order set cannot be \(\{\pi_0\}\).  Hence the coefficient-nonidentification locus is contained in \(\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}\) by (11).  The containment can be strict.  Indeed, take \(B^{(a)}=0\) for every view and \(\Gamma_i=I_m\) for every coordinate.  Then \(\Sigma^{aa}=I_p\) and \(\Sigma^{ab}=0\) for \(a\ne b\).  For every \(\pi\), modified Cholesky gives \(B_\pi^{(a)}=0\) and \(R_\pi^{ab}=0\), whence
\[
\Pi_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})=\mathfrak S_V,
\qquad
\Theta_{\{a,b\}}(\mathcal O_{\{a,b\}})
=\{(0)_{a\in M}\}=\{T_{\pi_0}\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Because \(p\ge2\), a wrong order exists, so (15) lies in \(\mathcal E_{ab,\pi_0}\) but not in the coefficient-nonidentification locus.

It remains to prove the lower side of the calibration elbow.  Fix \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\) and \(a\in M\).  The rows of \(L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}\) are successive least-squares innovations.  The normal equations make the innovation at position \(k\) orthogonal to the span of the variables at positions \(1,\ldots,k-1\), while every earlier innovation lies in that span.  Distinct innovations are therefore orthogonal, and
\[
D_\pi^{(a)}:=R_\pi^{aa}
=L_\pi^{(a)}\Sigma^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\tag{16}
\]
is diagonal.  It is positive definite because \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\) and the unit order-triangular matrix \(L_\pi^{(a)}\) is invertible.  Hence every \(D_\pi^{(a)}[j,j]>0\).

For each \(j\in V\), define
\[
\Gamma_{\pi,j}^{\mathrm{marg}}
:=\operatorname{diag}_{a\in M}\bigl(D_\pi^{(a)}[j,j]\bigr)\succ0.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \((Z_j)_{j\in V}\) be independent standard Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^m\), and put
\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_j
:=\operatorname{diag}_{a\in M}\!\left(\sqrt{D_\pi^{(a)}[j,j]}\right)Z_j,
\qquad
\widetilde X^{(a)}=(I_p-B_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{18}
\]
This construction is jointly centered Gaussian, has common order \(\pi\), has mutually independent coordinate disturbance stacks, and has the positive-definite disturbance covariances (17).  By (16),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X^{(a)})
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}D_\pi^{(a)}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-\mathsf T}
=\Sigma^{aa}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus every \(\pi\) has a full component-aligned completion of the marginal-only observable.  Conversely, at a fixed order the positive-definite marginal covariance and the population normal equations uniquely determine \(B_\pi^{(a)}\).  Therefore
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{marg}}=\mathfrak S_V,
\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}=\{T_\pi:\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\}.
\tag{20}
\]

Assume again that \(\theta\notin\mathcal E_{\mathrm{all},\pi_0}\), and choose \(\pi\ne\pi_0\), possible because \(p\ge2\).  If \(T_\pi=T_{\pi_0}\), then \(L_\pi^{(a)}=L_{\pi_0}^{(a)}\) for every \(a\).  For the true order, the structural equations and component alignment give
\[
R_{\pi_0}^{ab}
=\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon^{(a)},\varepsilon^{(b)})
=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\Gamma_j[a,b])
\tag{21}
\]
for every pair.  Equality of residualizers would make \(R_\pi^{ab}=R_{\pi_0}^{ab}\), so (10) would give \(F_\pi^{ab}(\theta)=0\) for every \(a<b\).  This contradicts \(Q_{\mathrm{all}}(\theta)\ne0\).  Hence \(T_\pi\ne T_{\pi_0}\), and (20) implies
\[
|\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}|\ge2.
\tag{22}
\]

If \(|C|\le1\), there is no paired cross-view block and \(\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}\ne\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)\) by (14) and (22).  If \(|C|=2\), (12)--(14) give \(\Theta_C^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Theta_M(\mathcal O_M)=\{T_{\pi_0}\}\).  Since \(M\) is finite and \(C=M\) is feasible, the minimum exists and
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{cal}}=2.
\tag{23}
\]
The original definition of \(S_{\min}\) minimizes over sets of size at least two, so the same argument gives \(|S_{\min}|=2\).  A two-element calibration set is proper in \(M\) exactly when \(m\ge3\).  Every step used only the defining restrictions of \(\Omega_{\pi_0}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) and \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\); neither edge minimality nor the diversity condition of HeurtebiseEtAl2026LiMVAM was invoked.

Let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\) be a finite simple undirected graph.  Define
\[
\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}
=\left((\Sigma^{aa})_{a\in M},
(\Sigma^{ab})_{\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}},\,a<b}\right),
\]
where every \(\Sigma^{aa}\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\), and the opposite orientation is \(\Sigma^{ba}=(\Sigma^{ab})^{\mathsf T}\).  No cross-view block indexed by a nonedge is observed.

%%% FIELD partial-noise-construction
For \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\), a permutation \(\pi\), and \(j\in V\), define the real symmetric partial matrix \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) on \(M\) by
\[
\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})[a,a]=R_\pi^{aa}[j,j]
\quad(a\in M),
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})[a,b]
=R_\pi^{ab}[j,j]
\quad(\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\]
with the symmetric orientation used at \((b,a)\); entries indexed by nonedges are unspecified.  A positive-definite completion is a matrix \(K\in\mathbb S_{++}^m\) agreeing with every specified diagonal and edge entry.

%%% FIELD chordal-completion-statement
Let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\) be a finite undirected graph with all diagonal positions specified.  Call \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) completable when every real symmetric partial matrix whose principal submatrix on every clique is positive definite admits a positive-definite completion.  Then \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is completable if and only if it is chordal.  Consequently, if \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is chordal, \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) admits a positive-definite completion if and only if \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})[C,C]\succ0\) for every maximal clique \(C\) of \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\); a completion can be produced by the sequential filling process for chordal partial matrices.

%%% FIELD graph-completion-statement
Let \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}=(M,E_{\mathrm{obs}})\) be an arbitrary finite simple undirected view graph, let \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) be as in \(\text{def:observed-view-graph-moments}\), and fix \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_V\).  There exists a full member \((P,B^0,\varepsilon,\Gamma,\pi)\in\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) whose covariance restricts to \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) if and only if both of the following conditions hold:
\[
\operatorname{offdiag}(R_\pi^{ab})=0
\quad\text{for every }\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}},
\tag{G1}
\]
and, for every \(j\in V\), the partial matrix \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) admits a positive-definite completion.  In every such full completion the coefficient tuple is uniquely \(T_\pi\), and any independent choice of positive-definite completions of the matrices \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) supplies the full disturbance-covariance certificate.

If \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is chordal, the second condition is equivalent to
\[
\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})[C,C]\succ0
\quad\text{for every }j\in V
\text{ and every maximal clique }C\text{ of }G_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\tag{G2}
\]
Under (G2), a constructive certificate for coordinate \(j\) is the unique optimizer
\[
\widehat\Gamma_{\pi,j}
=\operatorname*{argmax}_{K\in\mathbb S_+^m}
\left\{\det K:
K[a,a]=\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[a,a]\ (a\in M),\ 
K[a,b]=\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[a,b]\ (\{a,b\}\in E_{\mathrm{obs}})
\right\};
\tag{G3}
\]
this optimizer exists, is positive definite, and is unique.

Chordality cannot be omitted from the clique test.  When \(m=4\), take the chordless cycle with edges \(12,23,34,41\), put \(\Sigma^{aa}=I_p\), and on those four edges put \(\Sigma^{ab}=\rho_{ab}I_p\), where
\[
(\rho_{12},\rho_{23},\rho_{34},\rho_{41})=(0.9,0.9,0.9,-0.9).
\tag{G4}
\]
For every \(\pi\), all observed residual cross-blocks are diagonal and every specified two-by-two coordinate clique is positive definite, but no coordinate partial matrix in (G4) has even a positive-semidefinite completion.  Finally, when \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is the clique on \(S\) together with isolated vertices \(M\setminus S\), this theorem reduces to \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\), and the block-diagonal completion is exactly \(\Gamma_{\pi,j}\) from \(\text{def:noise-certificate}\).

%%% FIELD graph-completion-proof
We first establish two orderwise facts from \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\).  Fix a view \(a\) and write \(P_k=\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(k-1)\}\).  The row indexed by \(\pi(k)\) is the unique population least-squares coefficient because \(\Sigma^{aa}[P_k,P_k]\succ0\).  Its innovation is orthogonal to the span of \(X_{P_k}^{(a)}\).  Every innovation at an earlier position belongs to that span, so distinct innovations are orthogonal.  Therefore
\[
R_\pi^{aa}=L_\pi^{(a)}\Sigma^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{\mathsf T}
\quad\text{is diagonal}.
\tag{24}
\]
The matrix \(L_\pi^{(a)}\) is unit lower triangular after ordering by \(\pi\), hence invertible.  Congruence of \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\) by this invertible matrix gives
\[
R_\pi^{aa}\succ0,
\qquad
R_\pi^{aa}[j,j]>0
\quad(j\in V).
\tag{25}
\]

Suppose first that a full member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) with common order \(\pi\) restricts to \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\).  In its equation for \(X_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}\), the disturbance \(\varepsilon_{\pi(k)}^{(a)}\) is uncorrelated with each disturbance having a different variable label by \(\text{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}\).  Order triangularity expresses \(X_{P_k}^{(a)}\) as a linear function of disturbances at labels in \(P_k\).  Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P\!\left(\varepsilon_{\pi(k)}^{(a)},X_{P_k}^{(a)}\right)=0.
\tag{26}
\]
For this class member, \(\Gamma_j\succ0\) gives \(\Gamma_j[a,a]>0\) for every \(j\).  The within-view disturbance covariance is therefore positive definite and diagonal, while \(I_p-B^{0,(a)}\) is unit order-triangular and invertible.  Congruence in the structural covariance formula yields \(\Sigma^{aa}\succ0\).  The normal equations and (26) consequently force its structural regression row to equal the unique row in \(B_\pi^{(a)}\).  Thus its full coefficient tuple is uniquely \(T_\pi\), and
\[
L_\pi^{(a)}X^{(a)}=\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{27}
\]
For an observed edge \(\{a,b\}\), equations (27) and \(\text{ass:cross-variable-disturbance-orthogonality}\) imply
\[
R_\pi^{ab}
=\operatorname{Cov}_P(\varepsilon^{(a)},\varepsilon^{(b)})
=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\Gamma_j[a,b]),
\tag{28}
\]
which proves (G1).  For each \(j\), the full matrix \(\Gamma_j\succ0\), by \(\text{ass:disturbance-nondegeneracy}\), agrees with the diagonal and every observed edge entry of \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\) by (27)--(28).  It is therefore the required positive-definite completion.  This proves necessity, including the asserted coefficient uniqueness.

Conversely, suppose (G1) holds and choose, independently for each \(j\in V\), a positive-definite completion \(\widetilde\Gamma_j\) of \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}(G_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Let \((E_j)_{j\in V}\) be mutually independent centered Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^m\) with
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(E_j)=\widetilde\Gamma_j,
\tag{29}
\]
and define \(\widetilde\varepsilon_j^{(a)}=E_j[a]\).  The coordinate stacks are mutually independent and every covariance in (29) is positive definite.  Set
\[
\widetilde X^{(a)}=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}
=(I_p-B_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)}.
\tag{30}
\]
This is a jointly centered Gaussian structural system with common order \(\pi\), cross-variable disturbance orthogonality, and disturbance nondegeneracy.

By the diagonal specifications in \(\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}\), (24), and (29),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)})
=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\widetilde\Gamma_j[a,a])
=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(R_\pi^{aa}[j,j])
=R_\pi^{aa}.
\tag{31}
\]
Consequently (30)--(31) give
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X^{(a)})
=(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}R_\pi^{aa}(L_\pi^{(a)})^{-\mathsf T}
=\Sigma^{aa}.
\tag{32}
\]
For an observed edge, the edge specifications and (G1) similarly give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\varepsilon^{(a)},\widetilde\varepsilon^{(b)})
&=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(\widetilde\Gamma_j[a,b])\\
&=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in V}(R_\pi^{ab}[j,j])
=R_\pi^{ab}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
Multiplying (33) on the left by \((L_\pi^{(a)})^{-1}\) and on the right by \((L_\pi^{(b)})^{-\mathsf T}\) yields the observed \(\Sigma^{ab}\) by \(\text{def:residual-cross-blocks}\).  Thus (30) is a full member of \(\mathfrak M_{p,m}^{\mathrm{CA}}\) restricting to \(\mathcal O_{G_{\mathrm{obs}}}\).  This proves sufficiency and the assertion that the independently chosen completions are noise certificates.

Now suppose \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is chordal.  A positive-definite completion necessarily has positive-definite principal submatrices on every maximal clique.  Conversely, (G2) makes the partial matrix positive definite on every clique, because each clique is contained in a maximal clique and principal submatrices of positive-definite matrices are positive definite.  The converse direction of \(\text{lem:chordal-positive-definite-completion}\) then supplies a positive-definite completion.  This proves the chordal equivalence.

For completeness, we verify the constructive max-determinant certificate (G3).  Let \(\mathcal K_j\) be its displayed feasible set.  The chordal result gives a matrix \(K_0\in\mathcal K_j\cap\mathbb S_{++}^m\), so \(\det K_0>0\).  The set \(\mathcal K_j\) is closed.  It is bounded because positive semidefiniteness and each two-by-two principal minor imply
\[
|K[a,b]|^2\le K[a,a]K[b,b]
=\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[a,a]\mathcal Q_{\pi,j}[b,b]
\quad(a,b\in M).
\tag{34}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal K_j\) is compact, and continuity of the determinant gives a maximizer.  Its determinant is at least \(\det K_0>0\), so the maximizer is positive definite.  If distinct positive-definite matrices \(K_1,K_2\in\mathcal K_j\) both maximized the determinant, their midpoint would also be feasible and positive definite.  For every nonzero real symmetric direction \(H\), direct matrix differentiation gives
\[
D^2\log\det(K)[H,H]
=-\operatorname{tr}(K^{-1}HK^{-1}H)<0
\quad(H\ne0)
\tag{35}
\]
because the negated trace is \(-\|K^{-1/2}HK^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm F}^2\).  Thus \(\log\det\) is strictly concave on \(\mathbb S_{++}^m\).  Applying strict concavity to \(K_1\) and \(K_2\) would make the midpoint have determinant strictly larger than their common maximum, a contradiction.  Therefore (G3) has exactly one optimizer; its positive Cholesky pivots and its equality on the displayed affine constraints give a directly checkable certificate.

We finally prove the nonchordal obstruction.  In (G4), every within-view covariance is \(I_p\), so \(\text{def:modified-cholesky}\) gives
\[
B_\pi^{(a)}=0,
\qquad L_\pi^{(a)}=I_p
\quad\text{for every }a\in M\text{ and every }\pi.
\tag{36}
\]
Hence \(R_\pi^{ab}=\rho_{ab}I_p\) on each observed edge, proving (G1).  The joint covariance of either observed edge is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}I_p&\rho_{ab}I_p\\ \rho_{ab}I_p&I_p\end{pmatrix},
\tag{37}
\]
whose eigenvalues are \(1+\rho_{ab}\) and \(1-\rho_{ab}\), each with multiplicity \(p\).  Since \(|\rho_{ab}|=0.9<1\), every observed two-view block, and in particular every specified two-by-two coordinate clique, is positive definite.

Suppose nevertheless that a coordinate partial matrix had a positive-semidefinite completion \(K\).  A Gram representation of \(K\succeq0\) gives vectors \(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4\) with \(K[a,b]=\langle u_a,u_b\rangle\).  The unit diagonal makes \(\|u_a\|=1\).  The first three correlations in (G4) therefore imply
\[
\|u_1-u_2\|=\|u_2-u_3\|=\|u_3-u_4\|=\sqrt{2-2(0.9)}=\sqrt{0.2}.
\tag{38}
\]
The triangle inequality and (38) yield
\[
\|u_1-u_4\|\le3\sqrt{0.2},
\qquad
\langle u_1,u_4\rangle
=1-\frac12\|u_1-u_4\|^2
\ge1-\frac{1.8}{2}=0.1.
\tag{39}
\]
This contradicts the specified fourth correlation \(\langle u_1,u_4\rangle=-0.9\).  Hence no positive-semidefinite, and therefore no positive-definite, completion exists.

If \(G_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is the clique on \(S\) plus isolated vertices, its maximal cliques are \(S\) and the singleton unselected views.  For an order passing (G1), \(\text{lem:fixed-order-residual-characterization}\) proves that the selected block \((R_\pi^{ab}[j,j])_{a,b\in S}\) is positive definite, while (25) proves positivity of each isolated singleton.  The completion that sets all unspecified entries to zero is their positive-definite direct sum, exactly the matrix in \(\text{def:noise-certificate}\).  Thus the earlier selected-clique characterization is precisely this chordal special case.
