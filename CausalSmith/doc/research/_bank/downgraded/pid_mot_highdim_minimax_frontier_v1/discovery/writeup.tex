\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} On the common fixed-\(d>4\), \(K\ge3\), balanced class, Gao--Ge--Qian Theorem 1 has high-dimensional term \(K^{-1/2}m^{-2/d}\), while their Proposition 2 prints a two-margin lower expression and does not establish uniformly honest one-sided expected-length optimality.  Their published framework is broader in dimension and permits \(K\ge2\), but on the overlap its arbitrary \(\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K\) class and balanced independent sampling coincide with the present armwise product experiment.  Manole--Niles-Weed leave a logarithmic all-estimator gap already at two margins; that subclass converse transfers upward to the present arbitrary-margin class after the exact \(K^{-2}\) embedding, but it does not close the multi-arm factor.

\paragraph{Niche.} The squared-sum cost has shared fixed-dimensional geometry: all potentials obey one multi-margin constraint, and optimal coupling atoms can be exchanged in arbitrary coordinate subsets.  Conversely, a hard simultaneous perturbation must preserve its endpoint separation under every alternative multi-arm rematching, so neither separate-arm entropy nor a two-margin embedding determines the residual frontier.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the deterministic residual and support-diameter theorems, a sharper empirical plug-in upper bound, and exact root-\(N\) estimation and one-sided expected-length orders on every legal subsequence with \(K_N/N^{q_d}\) bounded below by a positive constant.  It retains two distinct honest procedures: \(B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\), a fully specified no-transport baseline using only \((\bar X,N,\alpha)\), and \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\), a sharper theoretical rule calibrated by an existence constant \(C_d\) for which no numerical value is supplied.  The rational finite-support template is defined but not instantiated; matching separation, \(\operatorname{TV}_{\mathrm{mix}}\le1/4\), and \(\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{rem}}>0\) against every off-contact rematching column remain explicit targets on ratio-to-zero subsequences.  The rational-grid procedure supplies an auditable primal--dual bracket for the empirical endpoint, and the STAR audit states the exact scaling and balanced-subsampling contract.

\begin{lemma}[lem:subset-rounding]\label{lem:subset-rounding}
For every \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_K)\in(2\mathbb B_d)^K\), there exists \(I\subseteq\mathcal I_K\) such that \(\|\sum_{k\in I}a_k-A/2\|_2^2\le r\), where \(A=\sum_{k=1}^Ka_k\) and \(r=\min\{K,d\}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(B:\mathbb R^K\to\mathbb R^d\) be the linear map \(Bt=\sum_{k=1}^Kt_ka_k\), and consider
\[
Q=\{t\in[0,1]^K:Bt=A/2\}.
\]
The set \(Q\) is a nonempty compact polytope because \((1/2,\ldots,1/2)\in Q\). Choose an extreme point \(t^\star\) of \(Q\), and let \(F=\{k:0<t_k^\star<1\}\). If \(|F|>\operatorname{rank}(B)\), the columns \((a_k)_{k\in F}\) are linearly dependent. Hence there is a nonzero vector \(v\), supported on \(F\), such that \(Bv=0\). Because every coordinate in \(F\) is strictly between zero and one, some \(\eta>0\) satisfies \(t^\star\pm\eta v\in[0,1]^K\). Both perturbed points belong to \(Q\), contradicting extremality. Thus
\[
|F|\le \operatorname{rank}(B)\le d,\qquad |F|\le K.
\]
For \(k\in F\), independently draw \(\xi_k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(t_k^\star)\); for \(k\notin F\), put \(\xi_k=t_k^\star\in\{0,1\}\). Then \(I=\{k:\xi_k=1\}\) is a random subset and, using independence and the vanishing of all cross terms,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\left\|\sum_{k\in I}a_k-\frac A2\right\|_2^2
&=\mathbb E\left\|\sum_{k\in F}(\xi_k-t_k^\star)a_k\right\|_2^2\\
&=\sum_{k\in F}t_k^\star(1-t_k^\star)\|a_k\|_2^2\\
&\le |F|\cdot\frac14\cdot4
\le\min\{K,d\}=r.
\end{aligned}
\]
The random variable inside the expectation is nonnegative, so at least one realization is no larger than its expectation. Its associated subset \(I\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the dimension-sensitive replacement for summing \(K\) one-coordinate exchange inequalities. \textit{Gap.} Classical vector balancing supplies extreme-point rounding, but its insertion into the repulsive quadratic MOT support argument is not in Gao--Ge--Qian. \textit{Consumer.} The arbitrary-subset support exchange in Theorem 3 uses this bound to replace \(\sqrt K\) geometry by \(\sqrt{\min\{K,d\}}\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-residual-envelope]\label{thm:sharp-residual-envelope}
For every \(\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K\), \(0\le V(\mathbf P)\le r\), and \(\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}V(\mathbf P)=r\). Equivalently, \(\sup_{\mathbf P}K^2\{L(\mathbf P)-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2\}=\min\{K,d\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(M=\sum_{k=1}^K\mu_k=K\bar\mu\). For every coupling \(\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P)\), the sum \(S=\sum_kZ_k\) has mean \(M\), and the bias--variance identity gives
\[
\int\|S\|_2^2\,d\gamma=\|M\|_2^2+\int\|S-M\|_2^2\,d\gamma.
\]
Consequently, by the definitions of \(L\) and \(V\),
\[
V(\mathbf P)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P)}\int\|S-M\|_2^2\,d\gamma\ge0. \tag{1}
\]
First suppose that each marginal has finite support, say \(P_k=\sum_jp_{kj}\delta_{z_{kj}}\), where all \(p_{kj}>0\) and \(z_{kj}\in\mathbb B_d\) by Assumption~\texttt{ass:unit-ball-support}. Define
\[
Q=\left\{(q_{kj}):q_{kj}\ge0,\ \sum_jq_{kj}=1\ (1\le k\le K),\
\sum_{k,j}q_{kj}z_{kj}=M\right\}.
\]
The array \(p=(p_{kj})\) lies in \(Q\). The equality matrix defining \(Q\) has rank at most \(K+d\). If a point of \(Q\) has more than \(K+d\) positive coordinates, the corresponding columns of that matrix are linearly dependent, and a sufficiently small signed perturbation supported on those positive coordinates preserves both the equalities and nonnegativity. Such a point is therefore not extreme. Thus every vertex of \(Q\) has at most \(K+d\) positive coordinates. Since each of the \(K\) blocks has total mass one, at most \(d\) blocks of a vertex can have more than one positive coordinate.

The elementary finite-dimensional vertex decomposition of a bounded polytope writes \(p=\sum_s\lambda_sq^s\), where the \(q^s\) are vertices, \(\lambda_s\ge0\), and \(\sum_s\lambda_s=1\). Draw \(S_0=s\) with probability \(\lambda_s\), and, conditional on \(S_0=s\), draw \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_K\) independently with block laws \(q^s_1,\ldots,q^s_K\). The unconditional marginal of \(Z_k\) is \(P_k\), because \(\sum_s\lambda_sq^s_{kj}=p_{kj}\). The defining moment equality for \(Q\) gives \(\sum_k\mathbb E[Z_k\mid S_0=s]=M\). Conditional independence now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\|S-M\|_2^2\mid S_0=s]
&=\sum_{k=1}^K\left(\mathbb E[\|Z_k\|_2^2\mid S_0=s]
-\|\mathbb E[Z_k\mid S_0=s]\|_2^2\right)\\
&\le \#\{k:q_k^s\text{ is nondegenerate}\}\\
&\le\min\{K,d\}=r. \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
Here every summand is at most one by unit-ball support, and a one-atom block contributes zero. Averaging (2) proves \(V(\mathbf P)\le r\) for finite supports.

For general Borel marginals, fix \(\delta>0\) and choose Borel maps \(q_\delta:\mathbb B_d\to\mathbb B_d\) with finite range and \(\|q_\delta(x)-x\|_2\le\delta\); such maps exist by compactness of the unit ball. Apply the finite-support construction to \(P_{k,\delta}=(q_\delta)_{\#}P_k\), obtaining a coupling of \(Q_k=q_\delta(Z_k)\) for which
\[
\mathbb E\left\|\sum_kQ_k-\sum_k\mathbb EQ_k\right\|_2^2\le r.
\]
Lift this coupling by drawing, conditionally on each grid value \(Q_k\), from the corresponding fiber law of \(P_k\). The lifted coordinates have exactly the marginals \(P_k\). Since \(\|Z_k-Q_k\|_2\le\delta\), Minkowski's inequality gives
\[
\left(\mathbb E\left\|\sum_k(Z_k-\mu_k)\right\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt r+2K\delta.
\]
Indeed, both \(\|\sum_k(Z_k-Q_k)\|_2\) and \(\|\sum_k\mathbb E(Z_k-Q_k)\|_2\) are at most \(K\delta\). Taking the infimum over couplings in (1) and then letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves \(V(\mathbf P)\le r\).

For sharpness, let \(P_k=\tfrac12(\delta_{e_k}+\delta_{-e_k})\) for \(1\le k\le r\), and let \(P_k=\delta_0\) for \(k>r\). These laws satisfy the support assumption and have \(M=0\). Under every coupling, orthogonality gives
\[
\left\|\sum_{k=1}^KZ_k\right\|_2^2=\sum_{k=1}^r\|Z_k\|_2^2=r
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Thus \(K^2L(\mathbf P)=r\) and \(V(\mathbf P)=r\). Combining this witness with the upper bound proves both asserted suprema. Throughout this proof \(L\) remains the second-moment endpoint; \(V/K^2=L-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2\) is used only as a centered residual.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact deterministic distance from the mean floor governs the large-arm transition before any empirical-process analysis begins. \textit{Gap.} Gao--Ge--Qian's Lemma D.2 yields a dimension-blind order bound on optimal sums; Wang--Wang characterize joint mixability only for selected univariate families. \textit{Consumer.} A causal analyst can certify when the unidentified second-moment improvement over the mean floor is uniformly too small to justify solving MOT.

\begin{theorem}[thm:optimal-sum-diameter]\label{thm:optimal-sum-diameter}
For every optimizer \(\gamma^{\star}\in\Gamma(\mathbf P)\), \(\operatorname{diam}\{\sum_{k=1}^Kz_k:(z_1,\ldots,z_K)\in\operatorname{supp}(\gamma^{\star})\}\le2\sqrt r\); the constant is attained when \(K\ge d\) by orthogonal symmetric two-point margins.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(S(x)=\sum_{k=1}^Kx_k\). We first establish the support exchange inequality. For \(y,z\in\operatorname{supp}(\gamma^\star)\) and \(I\subseteq\mathcal I_K\), let \(y^I,z^I\) be obtained by swapping the coordinates indexed by \(I\) between \(y\) and \(z\). Then
\[
c_K(y)+c_K(z)\le c_K(y^I)+c_K(z^I). \tag{3}
\]
If (3) failed strictly and \(y\ne z\), continuity of \(c_K\) would provide disjoint neighborhoods \(U\ni y\) and \(W\ni z\) on which the same strict improvement holds for every pair. Both neighborhoods have positive \(\gamma^\star\)-mass by the definition of support. Remove equal positive masses from the restrictions of \(\gamma^\star\) to \(U\) and \(W\), pair the two normalized restrictions, swap the \(I\)-coordinates in every paired pair, and add the two resulting pushforwards. For each coordinate, the aggregate marginal before and after the swap is identical, while the objective decreases strictly. This contradicts optimality. When \(y=z\), (3) is immediate. Hence (3) holds on the entire support, including boundary points.

Put \(a_k=y_k-z_k\), \(A=\sum_ka_k=S(y)-S(z)\), and \(b_I=\sum_{k\in I}a_k\). Unit-ball support gives \(\|a_k\|_2\le2\), so Lemma~\texttt{lem:subset-rounding} applies. Since \(S(y^I)=S(y)-b_I\) and \(S(z^I)=S(z)+b_I\), multiplying (3) by \(K^2\) and expanding squares gives
\[
\|S(y)\|_2^2+\|S(z)\|_2^2
\le\|S(y)-b_I\|_2^2+\|S(z)+b_I\|_2^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A\mathbin\cdot b_I\le\|b_I\|_2^2. \tag{4}
\]
The algebraic identity
\[
A\mathbin\cdot b_I-\|b_I\|_2^2
=\frac{\|A\|_2^2}{4}-\left\|b_I-\frac A2\right\|_2^2 \tag{5}
\]
and the subset supplied by Lemma~\texttt{lem:subset-rounding} imply, through (4)--(5),
\[
\frac{\|S(y)-S(z)\|_2^2}{4}=\frac{\|A\|_2^2}{4}
\le\left\|b_I-\frac A2\right\|_2^2\le r.
\]
Taking the supremum over support pairs proves the diameter bound.

For attainment when \(K\ge d\), take \(P_k=\tfrac12(\delta_{e_k}+\delta_{-e_k})\) for \(k\le d\) and \(P_k=\delta_0\) otherwise. Every feasible tuple has squared sum norm \(d\); hence every coupling is optimal. The product coupling has both \((e_1,\ldots,e_d,0,\ldots,0)\) and its negative in its support, and their sums are \(2\sqrt d\) apart. Since \(r=d\), the constant is attained.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This support theorem is the shared geometry needed to localize all dual potentials at once. \textit{Gap.} Gao--Ge--Qian exchange one coordinate and obtain a \(\sqrt K\)-scale control; no located source gives the sharp fixed-dimensional diameter for this repulsive sum cost. \textit{Consumer.} The empirical-process upper bound uses the diameter to normalize dual slopes by \(K^{-2}\sqrt r\).

\begin{proposition}[prop:hypercube-parity]\label{prop:hypercube-parity}
For \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}}=(H_d,\ldots,H_d)\), \(L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})=0\) when \(K\) is even and \(L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})=K^{-2}\) when \(K\) is odd. In particular, the legal \(K=3,d=5\) witness satisfies \(L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})=1/9\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We use a pointwise lattice-parity lower bound and an explicit marginal-preserving coupling.

First suppose that \(K\) is even.  Let \(U_1,\ldots,U_{K/2}\) be random vectors with law \(H_d\), and define
\[
 Z_{2r-1}=U_r,
 \qquad
 Z_{2r}=-U_r,
 \qquad 1\le r\le K/2.
\]
(The vectors \(U_r\) may, for example, be independent.)  The map \(u\mapsto-u\) is a bijection of \(\{-1,1\}^d/\sqrt d\), so central symmetry of the uniform law gives \(-U_r\sim H_d\).  Hence every \(Z_k\) has marginal law \(H_d\), and the joint law \(\gamma_{\mathrm{even}}\) of \((Z_1,\ldots,Z_K)\) belongs to \(\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})\).  Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^K Z_k
 =\sum_{r=1}^{K/2}(U_r-U_r)=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Since the squared Euclidean norm is nonnegative and \(K\ge3\), the definition of \(L\) and the admissibility of \(\gamma_{\mathrm{even}}\) imply
\[
 0\le L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})
 \le \int K^{-2}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^Kz_k\right\|_2^2
 \,d\gamma_{\mathrm{even}}=0.
\]
Thus \(L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})=0\) for even \(K\).

Now suppose that \(K\) is odd.  Fix an arbitrary \(\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})\), and let \((Z_1,\ldots,Z_K)\) have law \(\gamma\).  Because each of the finitely many marginals is supported on \(\{-1,1\}^d/\sqrt d\), simultaneously for every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\) and \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\) we may write
\[
 Z_{k,j}=\frac{\varepsilon_{jk}}{\sqrt d},
 \qquad \varepsilon_{jk}\in\{-1,1\},
\]
almost surely.  For each fixed coordinate \(j\), the integer
\[
 S_j:=\sum_{k=1}^K\varepsilon_{jk}
\]
is a sum of an odd number of odd integers, hence is odd.  In particular \(S_j\ne0\) and \(S_j^2\ge1\).  Therefore the pointwise support argument gives
\[
 \left\|\sum_{k=1}^K Z_k\right\|_2^2
 =\sum_{j=1}^d\left(\frac{S_j}{\sqrt d}\right)^2
 =\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d S_j^2
 \ge1.
\]
Integrating, multiplying by the positive number \(K^{-2}\), and then taking the infimum over all admissible \(\gamma\) yields
\[
 L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})\ge K^{-2}.
\]

It remains to show attainability, rather than merely an outer lower bound.  Define the finite set
\[
 \mathcal A_K
 :=\left\{e=(e_1,\ldots,e_K)\in\{-1,1\}^K:
 \left|\sum_{k=1}^K e_k\right|=1\right\}.
\]
This set is nonempty because \(K\) is odd.  Independently for \(j=1,\ldots,d\), draw \(E_j=(E_{j1},\ldots,E_{jK})\) uniformly from \(\mathcal A_K\), and put
\[
 Z_k:=\frac{(E_{1k},\ldots,E_{dk})}{\sqrt d},
 \qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
\]
For any fixed \(j,k\), the fixed-point-free bijection \(e\mapsto-e\) of \(\mathcal A_K\) interchanges the events \(e_k=1\) and \(e_k=-1\).  Consequently
\[
 \Pr(E_{jk}=1)=\Pr(E_{jk}=-1)=\frac12.
\]
The rows \(E_1,\ldots,E_d\) are independent, so for every fixed arm \(k\) and every \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)\in\{-1,1\}^d\),
\[
 \Pr\bigl((E_{1k},\ldots,E_{dk})=a\bigr)
 =\prod_{j=1}^d\Pr(E_{jk}=a_j)=2^{-d}.
\]
Thus each \(Z_k\) has law \(H_d\).  The resulting joint law \(\gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}\) therefore lies in \(\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal A_K\), for every realization and every \(j\),
\[
 \left|\sum_{k=1}^K E_{jk}\right|=1,
\]
and hence
\[
 \left\|\sum_{k=1}^K Z_k\right\|_2^2
 =\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d
 \left(\sum_{k=1}^K E_{jk}\right)^2
 =1.
\]
Using this admissible coupling in the definition of \(L\) gives
\[
 L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})
 \le \int K^{-2}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^Kz_k\right\|_2^2
 \,d\gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}
 =K^{-2}.
\]
Together with the preceding lower bound, this proves \(L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})=K^{-2}\) for odd \(K\).  Finally, \(K=3\) and \(d=5\) satisfy the declared ranges, so the odd case gives \(L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{hyp}})=3^{-2}=1/9\).  No rank, overlap, conditioning, boundary-limit, or asymptotic argument is used; the only division is by the positive quantities \(K^2\) and \(d\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Parity is a complete all-rematching certificate and a destructive check against treating vector joint mixability as automatic. \textit{Gap.} The mixability literature studies selected scalar classes; the explicit vector hypercube law and its original second-moment endpoint are not supplied by those results. \textit{Consumer.} The \(K=3,d=5\) instance provides an exact finite benchmark for every empirical MOT and dual-certificate implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-margin-sanity-reduction]\label{lem:two-margin-sanity-reduction}
After substituting \(P_1=\mu\), \(P_2=(-\operatorname{Id})_{\#}\nu\), and \(P_3=\cdots=P_K=\delta_0\) into the definition of \(L\), one has definitionally \(K^2L(\mathbf P)=\inf_{\pi\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int\|x-y\|_2^2\,d\pi(x,y)=W_2^2(\mu,\nu)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Under the stated substitution, coordinates \(Z_3,\ldots,Z_K\) equal zero almost surely. If \(Z_1=X\sim\mu\) and \(Z_2=-Y\) with \(Y\sim\nu\), the joint law of \((X,Y)\) is a coupling \(\pi\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\), and conversely every such \(\pi\) induces exactly one admissible \(K\)-coupling after adjoining the deterministic zero coordinates. Therefore the definition of \(L\) gives
\[
K^2L(\mathbf P)
=\inf_{\pi\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int\|x-y\|_2^2\,d\pi(x,y)
=W_2^2(\mu,\nu).
\]
No centering term is introduced: this is an identity for the second-moment endpoint \(L\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This sanity-reduction helper checks normalization and transfers the published two-margin obstruction; it is not counted as a research contribution. \textit{Gap.} The identity is a direct definitional specialization, so no novelty is claimed for it; the unresolved work is the genuinely all-arm rematching obstruction. \textit{Consumer.} The exact-frontier question must dominate the scaled two-margin modulus and preserve its fixed-\(K\) logarithmic issue.

\begin{theorem}[thm:improved-plugin-upper]\label{thm:improved-plugin-upper}
There is \(C_d<\infty\) such that \(\sup_{\mathbf P}\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|\le C_d[N^{-1/2}+\min\{r/K^2,\ K^{-1}m^{-2/d}\}]\). Relative to Gao--Ge--Qian Theorem 1, the cap improves from \(K^{-1}\) to \(rK^{-2}\), strictly when \(K>d\), and the active high-dimensional term improves from \(K^{-1/2}m^{-2/d}\) to \(K^{-1}m^{-2/d}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(M(\mathbf Q)=\sum_k\int x\,dQ_k(x)\) and \(H(\mathbf Q)=L(\mathbf Q)-\|M(\mathbf Q)\|_2^2/K^2=V(\mathbf Q)/K^2\). We first prove a deterministic interpolation inequality for finitely supported tuples \(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q\). Let \(P_{k,t}=(1-t)P_k+tQ_k\), \(0<t<1\), and let \(S_k=\operatorname{supp}(P_k)\cup\operatorname{supp}(Q_k)\). Every atom of \(S_k\) has positive \(P_{k,t}\)-mass.

Finite linear-programming duality from Lemma~\texttt{lem:finite-lp-optimization} supplies an optimal primal plan \(\gamma_t\) and an optimal dual tuple \(f_{1,t},\ldots,f_{K,t}\) satisfying \(\sum_kf_{k,t}(x_k)\le c_K(x_1,\ldots,x_K)\) on \(\prod_kS_k\). Complementary slackness implies equality on \(\operatorname{supp}(\gamma_t)\), and every atom \(x\in S_k\) occurs in some support column because the \(k\)-th marginal is positive at \(x\).

For a support column \(z\in\operatorname{supp}(\gamma_t)\), dual feasibility implies
\[
f_{k,t}(x)\le c_K(z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1},x,z_{k+1},\ldots,z_K)
-\sum_{\ell\ne k}f_{\ell,t}(z_\ell), \qquad x\in S_k. \tag{10}
\]
Taking the infimum of the right side over support columns gives equality in (10) at every \(x\in S_k\), because a contact column through \(x\) exists. Subtract the own-quadratic and common mean-linear terms and normalize at zero:
\[
g_{k,t}(x)=f_{k,t}(x)-\frac{\|x\|_2^2}{K^2}
-\frac{2M(\mathbf P_t)\mathbin\cdot x}{K^2}-\text{constant}. \tag{11}
\]
The infimum representation following (10) extends \(g_{k,t}\) from \(S_k\) to a globally concave function whose affine slopes are
\[
\frac{2}{K^2}\{S(z)-z_k-M(\mathbf P_t)\},\qquad z\in\operatorname{supp}(\gamma_t). \tag{12}
\]
By Theorem~\texttt{thm:optimal-sum-diameter}, the image \(S(\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t)\) has diameter at most \(2\sqrt r\). Its barycenter is \(M(\mathbf P_t)\), so every support sum is within \(2\sqrt r\) of that barycenter. Unit-ball support and \(r\le d\) therefore bound the norms in (12) by
\[
\ell_{K,d}=\frac{2(2\sqrt d+1)}{K^2}. \tag{13}
\]

The finite LP value \(t\mapsto L(\mathbf P_t)\) is convex and piecewise linear, because its dual is a supremum of affine functions of the marginal masses. At every differentiability point, the dual subgradient inequality in both directions gives
\[
\frac d{dt}L(\mathbf P_t)=\sum_{k=1}^K\int f_{k,t}\,d(Q_k-P_k).
\]
Differentiating the mean square and using (11), then integrating over \(t\in(0,1)\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|H(\mathbf Q)-H(\mathbf P)|
&\le \frac1{K^2}\left|\sum_{k=1}^K\int\|x\|_2^2\,d(Q_k-P_k)\right|\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^K\sup_{g\in\mathcal C_{\ell_{K,d}}}
\left|\int g\,d(Q_k-P_k)\right|. \tag{14}
\end{aligned}
\]
The finitely many nondifferentiability points do not affect the integral, and continuity supplies the endpoints \(t=0,1\).

Apply (14) with \(\mathbf Q=\widehat{\mathbf P}\). Assumptions~\texttt{ass:balanced-array} and~\texttt{ass:product-experiment} imply that all \(N=Km\) observations are independent. Since \(\|X_{ki}\|_2\le1\),
\[
\mathbb E\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2^2\le N^{-1},\qquad
\mathbb E|\|\bar X\|_2^2-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2|\le2N^{-1/2}. \tag{15}
\]
The first term in (14) is the absolute value of a centered sum of \(N\) variables, divided by \(K^2m\), and hence has expectation at most \(1/(K\sqrt N)\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:concave-empirical-process} and (13) bound the sum of the remaining \(K\) terms by
\[
C_dK\ell_{K,d}m^{-2/d}\le C_dK^{-1}m^{-2/d}. \tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) prove
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P}\mathbb E|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|
\le C_d\{N^{-1/2}+K^{-1}m^{-2/d}\}. \tag{17}
\]
Independently, Theorem~\texttt{thm:sharp-residual-envelope} gives \(0\le H(\mathbf Q)\le r/K^2\) for every tuple. Combining this with (15) gives
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P}\mathbb E|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|
\le2N^{-1/2}+r/K^2. \tag{18}
\]
Taking the better of (17) and (18), and absorbing \(1/(K\sqrt N)\) into the parametric term, gives the claimed bound.

It remains to remove the finite-support reduction without adding a density or boundary assumption. Quantize every law by a finite-range Borel map \(q_\delta\) with \(\|q_\delta(x)-x\|_2\le\delta\), and apply the preceding argument to the quantized observations and marginals. For every tuple, the average vector moves by at most \(\delta\), so its squared norm changes by at most \((2+\delta)\delta\). Pushing a coupling forward and lifting it through the finite quantization fibers give both inequalities between the original and quantized transport values. Thus both population and empirical values converge as \(\delta\downarrow0\), uniformly by the displayed cost bound. Bounded convergence passes (17)--(18) to arbitrary Borel laws.

Finally, Lemma~\texttt{lem:gao-published-highdim-displays} records the published expressions. Since \(r/K^2<K^{-1}\) exactly when \(K>d\), the cap is then strictly smaller; and \(K^{-1}m^{-2/d}<K^{-1/2}m^{-2/d}\) for \(K>1\). This proves the comparison while retaining the separate mean contribution in (15).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The first theorem-level advance sharpens the published plug-in guarantee using shared squared-sum geometry rather than separate arm envelopes. \textit{Gap.} Gao--Ge--Qian Theorem 1 gives \(N^{-1/2}+\min\{K^{-1},K^{-1/2}m^{-2/d}\}\) when \(d>4\); no located closer paper proves the displayed \(r\)-controlled bound for arbitrary marginals. \textit{Consumer.} The rate determines how much sampling uncertainty must be deducted from an empirical causal lower endpoint.

\begin{theorem}[thm:known-risk-sandwich]\label{thm:known-risk-sandwich}
For constants \(0<c_d\le C_d<\infty\), \(c_d\max\{N^{-1/2},K^{-2}(m\log m)^{-2/d}\}\le R_d(N,K)\le C_d[N^{-1/2}+\min\{r/K^2,K^{-1}m^{-2/d}\}]\). The lower maximum is obtained from two distinct submodels and is not attributed to the printed minimum in Gao--Ge--Qian Proposition 2.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows by choosing \(T=\widehat L\) in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-risk} and applying Theorem~\texttt{thm:improved-plugin-upper}.

For the parametric lower bound, use the two laws from Lemma~\texttt{lem:bernoulli-two-point}; denote their endpoint gap by \(\Delta\ge aN^{-1/2}\) and their observation-law total variation by \(\eta\le1/4\). For any estimator \(T\), thresholding at the midpoint of the two endpoints produces a test. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_0|T-L(\mathbf P^{(0)})|+\mathbb E_1|T-L(\mathbf P^{(1)})|\\
&\qquad\ge\frac\Delta2\left\{\Pr_0(T>\text{midpoint})+\Pr_1(T\le\text{midpoint})\right\}\\
&\qquad\ge\frac\Delta2(1-\eta).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the larger of the two risks and then the infimum over \(T\) gives \(R_d(N,K)\ge3aN^{-1/2}/16\).

For the nonparametric lower bound, restrict the model to \(P_1=\mu\), \(P_2=(-\operatorname{Id})_{\#}\nu\), and \(P_3=\cdots=P_K=\delta_0\). Lemma~\texttt{lem:two-margin-sanity-reduction} gives \(K^2L=W_2^2(\mu,\nu)\), while the last \(K-2\) samples are deterministic. If \(T\) estimates \(L\), then \(K^2T\) is an estimator of \(W_2^2\) from \(m\) observations per nondegenerate margin. Lemma~\texttt{lem:two-sample-wasserstein-lower} therefore gives
\[
R_d(N,K)\ge c_dK^{-2}(m\log m)^{-2/d}. \tag{19}
\]
The constants in the two lower bounds can be decreased to a common positive \(c_d\), which yields their maximum. Lemma~\texttt{lem:gao-published-highdim-displays} shows that the printed high-dimensional expression in Gao--Ge--Qian Proposition 2 is a minimum. Here the two terms in the maximum come instead from the distinct Bernoulli and two-margin submodels just proved, so no inference from that printed minimum is being made.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The honest sandwich exposes every currently verified polynomial and logarithmic gap instead of promoting the plug-in upper bound to a minimax answer. \textit{Gap.} Manole--Niles-Weed leave logarithmic slack even for the two-margin all-estimator lower bound, while Gao--Ge--Qian do not close the \(d>4\) all-\(K\) frontier. \textit{Consumer.} A solver can see exactly which arm-count and logarithmic factors need a new construction rather than re-proving settled root-\(N\) behavior.

\begin{theorem}[thm:large-k-frontier]\label{thm:large-k-frontier}
There are constants \(c_d,C_d,c_0>0\) such that \(c_dN^{-1/2}\le R_d(N,K)\le C_d(N^{-1/2}+r/K^2)\); hence \(R_d(N,K)\asymp N^{-1/2}\) uniformly along every legal array satisfying \(K\ge c_0N^{1/4}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an observable marginal tuple \(\mathbf P=(P_1,\ldots,P_K)\), Assumption~\texttt{ass:product-experiment} specifies the observed-data law
\[
Q_{\mathbf P}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^K P_k^{\otimes m}.
\]
The unobserved object is a full-vector coupling \(\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P)\), and its multiworld second-moment parameter is
\[
\theta(\gamma)=\int c_K\,d\gamma
=K^{-2}\int\left\|\sum_{k=1}^K z_k\right\|_2^2\,d\gamma.
\]
Thus the identified set under the maintained marginal restrictions is
\[
\Theta_I(\mathbf P)=\{\theta(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P)\},
\qquad
L(\mathbf P)=\inf\Theta_I(\mathbf P).
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, the estimand in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-risk} is the second-moment endpoint \(L\), not the centered residual \(V\).

Define
\[
\widehat\mu_k=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m X_{ki},
\qquad
\bar X=K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^K\widehat\mu_k,
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{mean}}=\|\bar X\|_2^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Assumption~\texttt{ass:balanced-array} gives \(N=Km>0\), and hence
\[
\bar X=N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^m X_{ki}.
\tag{3}
\]
This is one data-driven estimator for the entire model class; it requires neither evaluation of \(\widehat L\) nor solution of a multi-marginal transport problem.

By Assumption~\texttt{ass:unit-ball-support}, \(\|X_{ki}\|_2\leq 1\) almost surely and \(\|\mu_k\|_2\leq 1\).  Assumption~\texttt{ass:product-experiment} implies independence across all pairs \((k,i)\), while the definition of \(\mu_k\) gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}X_{ki}=\mu_k\).  Therefore \(\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\bar X=\bar\mu\), all cross terms in the squared norm of the centered sum in (3) vanish, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2^2
&=N^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^m
  \mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|X_{ki}-\mu_k\|_2^2\\
&=N^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^m
  \left\{\mathbb E_{P_k}\|X_{ki}\|_2^2-\|\mu_k\|_2^2\right\}\\
&\leq N^{-2}Km=N^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The unit ball is convex, so both \(\bar X\) and \(\bar\mu\) lie in \(\mathbb B_d\).  The polarization identity, Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, and Jensen's inequality applied to (4) consequently yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\left|\|\bar X\|_2^2-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2\right|
&=\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}
  \left|\langle\bar X-\bar\mu,\bar X+\bar\mu\rangle\right|\\
&\leq 2\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2\\
&\leq 2\left(\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}
\leq 2N^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Theorem~\texttt{thm:sharp-residual-envelope} states, on the same arbitrary-margin unit-ball class,
\[
0\leq K^2\{L(\mathbf P)-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2\}=V(\mathbf P)\leq r.
\]
Because \(K\geq3\), division by \(K^2\) is valid, giving
\[
0\leq L(\mathbf P)-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2\leq \frac r{K^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (2), (5), and (6) by the triangle inequality proves the finite-sample uniform risk bound
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|T_{\mathrm{mean}}-L(\mathbf P)|
\leq 2N^{-1/2}+\frac r{K^2}
\leq 2\left(N^{-1/2}+\frac r{K^2}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
Since Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-risk} takes the infimum over all measurable estimators and \(T_{\mathrm{mean}}\) is one such estimator, (7) implies
\[
R_d(N,K)\leq 2\left(N^{-1/2}+\frac r{K^2}\right).
\tag{8}
\]

For the all-estimator lower bound, Lemma~\texttt{lem:bernoulli-two-point} supplies two admissible tuples \(\mathbf P^{(0)}\) and \(\mathbf P^{(1)}\).  Put
\[
Q_j=\bigotimes_{k=1}^K(P_k^{(j)})^{\otimes m},
\qquad
\ell_j=L(\mathbf P^{(j)}),
\qquad
\Delta=\ell_1-\ell_0.
\]
The lemma gives, for a universal \(a>0\),
\[
\Delta\geq aN^{-1/2}>0,
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14.
\tag{9}
\]
Fix an arbitrary measurable estimator \(T\), and define the measurable event
\[
D=\left\{T\geq\frac{\ell_0+\ell_1}{2}\right\}.
\]
On \(D\), \(|T-\ell_0|\geq\Delta/2\), while on \(D^c\), \(|T-\ell_1|\geq\Delta/2\).  Using the definition \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)=\sup_A|Q_0(A)-Q_1(A)|\) and then (9),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|T-\ell_0|+\mathbb E_{Q_1}|T-\ell_1|
&\geq\frac\Delta2\{Q_0(D)+Q_1(D^c)\}\\
&=\frac\Delta2\{1-(Q_1(D)-Q_0(D))\}\\
&\geq\frac\Delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
\geq\frac{3\Delta}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
At least one of the two risks in (10) is at least \(3\Delta/16\).  Both tuples belong to the supremum in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-risk}, so (9) implies, for every \(T\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|T-L(\mathbf P)|
\geq\frac{3a}{16}N^{-1/2}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(T\) gives
\[
R_d(N,K)\geq c_dN^{-1/2},
\qquad c_d:=\frac{3a}{16}>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (8) and (11) prove the asserted finite-sample sandwich with, for example, \(C_d=2\).  The lower constant is universal and therefore is also admissible as a constant depending only on fixed \(d\).

It remains to verify the growing-arm conclusion with the required quantifier.  Fix an arbitrary \(c_0>0\), and consider any balanced legal array for which \(K_N\geq c_0N^{1/4}\) eventually.  Since \(r=\min\{K,d\}\leq d\), positivity of \(c_0\) gives, eventually,
\[
\frac r{K_N^2}\leq\frac d{c_0^2}N^{-1/2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Substitution of (12) into (8), together with (11), yields
\[
c_dN^{-1/2}
\leq R_d(N,K_N)
\leq 2\left(1+\frac d{c_0^2}\right)N^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
The comparison constants in (13) are independent of \(N\), \(K_N\), and \(\mathbf P\), although the upper constant may depend on \(d\) and \(c_0\).  Assumption~\texttt{ass:legal-triangular-array} imposes \(m_N=N/K_N\to\infty\); all inequalities above are finite-sample, so applying them eventually along such an array requires no interchange of limits.  Since \(c_0>0\) was arbitrary, (13) proves \(R_d(N,K_N)\asymp N^{-1/2}\) for every fixed positive arm-growth threshold in the claimed regime.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact root-\(N\) region is already obtainable from deterministic geometry and a mean-driven Le Cam experiment. \textit{Gap.} Gao--Ge--Qian establish minimax optimality only for \(d\le4\); the theorem supplies an unrestricted \(d>4\) growing-arm region without density assumptions. \textit{Consumer.} Studies with many outcome regimes can bypass a prohibitively large MOT problem and estimate the endpoint at the parametric rate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:large-k-honest-length]\label{thm:large-k-honest-length}
For constants \(0<c_{d,\alpha}\le C_{d,\alpha}<\infty\), \(c_{d,\alpha}N^{-1/2}\le E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)\le C_{d,\alpha}(N^{-1/2}+r/K^2)\), and \(B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\in\mathcal H_\alpha\). Consequently \(E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)\asymp N^{-1/2}\) when \(K\ge c_0N^{1/4}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound uses support-moment concentration, while the converse uses an ordered two-point testing argument. Fix integers \(N,K,m\) satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:balanced-array}. The declared spaces give \(K\geq 3\) and \(m\geq 2\). Fix \(\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K\), and define
\[
\varepsilon_N=(\alpha N)^{-1/2},
\qquad
Y_{ki}=X_{ki}-\mu_k.
\]
By Assumption~\texttt{ass:unit-ball-support}, \(\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}Y_{ki}=0\), and expansion of the squared norm gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|Y_{ki}\|_2^2
=\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|X_{ki}\|_2^2-\|\mu_k\|_2^2
\leq 1. \tag{1}
\]
Assumption~\texttt{ass:product-experiment} makes the \(Y_{ki}\)'s mutually independent. Moreover, Assumption~\texttt{ass:balanced-array} gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\bar X
=\frac{m}{N}\sum_{k=1}^K\mu_k
=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\mu_k
=\bar\mu. \tag{2}
\]
The cross terms between distinct centered observations therefore have expectation zero. Using (1), (2), and \(N=Km\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2^2
&=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^m
  \mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|Y_{ki}\|_2^2\\
&\leq \frac{Km}{N^2}
=\frac1N. \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
For every nonnegative real number \(u\) and every \(t>0\), \(\mathbf 1\{u>t\}\leq u^2/t^2\). Applying this pointwise with \(u=\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2\) and the positive threshold \(t=\varepsilon_N\), then taking expectations and using (3), yields
\[
\Pr_{\mathbf P}\!\left(\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2>\varepsilon_N\right)
\leq \frac{N^{-1}}{\varepsilon_N^2}
=\alpha. \tag{4}
\]
On the complementary event, the reverse triangle inequality implies
\[
\max\{0,\|\bar X\|_2-\varepsilon_N\}
\leq \|\bar\mu\|_2. \tag{5}
\]
The lower half of Theorem~\texttt{thm:sharp-residual-envelope}, together with the definition \(V(\mathbf P)=K^2\{L(\mathbf P)-\|\bar\mu\|_2^2\}\), is exactly
\[
L(\mathbf P)\geq \|\bar\mu\|_2^2. \tag{6}
\]
Definition~\texttt{def:mean-lower-bound} and (5)--(6) show that \(B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\leq L(\mathbf P)\) on the event in (5). Hence (4) gives
\[
\Pr_{\mathbf P}\!\left(B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\leq L(\mathbf P)\right)
\geq 1-\alpha. \tag{7}
\]
Because \(\mathbf P\) was arbitrary, Definition~\texttt{def:honest-bounds} proves
\[
B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\in\mathcal H_\alpha. \tag{8}
\]

We next control the expected excess length of this particular honest rule. Put
\[
a_\mu=\|\bar\mu\|_2,
\qquad
x=\|\bar X\|_2,
\qquad
b=(x-\varepsilon_N)_+.
\]
Convexity of \(\mathbb B_d\), Assumption~\texttt{ass:unit-ball-support}, and the triangle inequality give
\[
a_\mu\leq \frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\|\mu_k\|_2\leq1,
\qquad
x\leq \frac1N\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^m\|X_{ki}\|_2\leq1,
\qquad
0\leq b\leq x. \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(a_\mu,x,b\in[0,1]\). Also, the reverse triangle inequality and \(0\leq x-(x-\varepsilon_N)_+\leq\varepsilon_N\) imply
\[
|a_\mu-b|
\leq |a_\mu-x|+|x-b|
\leq \|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2+\varepsilon_N. \tag{10}
\]
The upper half of Theorem~\texttt{thm:sharp-residual-envelope} gives
\[
L(\mathbf P)\leq a_\mu^2+\frac r{K^2}. \tag{11}
\]
Since \((u+v)_+\leq u_++v\) for \(v\geq0\), since \((a_\mu^2-b^2)_+\leq |a_\mu-b|(a_\mu+b)\), and since (9) gives \(a_\mu+b\leq2\), equations (10)--(11) yield the pointwise inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(L(\mathbf P)-B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\bigr)_+
&\leq \frac r{K^2}+(a_\mu^2-b^2)_+\\
&\leq \frac r{K^2}+2|a_\mu-b|\\
&\leq \frac r{K^2}
   +2\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2+2\varepsilon_N. \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
\]
For the nonnegative random variable \(Z=\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2\), nonnegativity of \(\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}(Z-\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}Z)^2\) gives \((\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}Z)^2\leq\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}Z^2\). Therefore (3) implies
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\|\bar X-\bar\mu\|_2\leq N^{-1/2}. \tag{13}
\]
Taking expectations in (12), using (13) and \(\varepsilon_N=\alpha^{-1/2}N^{-1/2}\), and then taking the supremum over \(\mathbf P\), gives
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\!\left[\bigl(L(\mathbf P)-B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\bigr)_+\right]
\leq \frac r{K^2}+\bigl(2+2\alpha^{-1/2}\bigr)N^{-1/2}. \tag{14}
\]
By (8) and Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-excess-length}, the infimum over all honest rules is no larger than the risk of this rule. Since \(2+2\alpha^{-1/2}>1\), equation (14) proves
\[
E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)
\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left(N^{-1/2}+\frac r{K^2}\right) \tag{15}
\]
with the finite choice \(C_{d,\alpha}=2+2\alpha^{-1/2}\).

For the converse, invoke the already established Lemma~\texttt{lem:bernoulli-two-point}, whose conditions \(N=Km\), \(K\geq3\), and \(m\geq2\) hold here. Let \(\mathbf P^{(0)},\mathbf P^{(1)}\) be the two tuples supplied by that lemma, write \(L_j=L(\mathbf P^{(j)})\), and let \(Q_j\) be the corresponding full-sample law under Assumption~\texttt{ass:product-experiment}. For a universal constant \(a>0\), the lemma gives
\[
L_1-L_0\geq aN^{-1/2},
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14. \tag{16}
\]
Fix an arbitrary \(B_N\in\mathcal H_\alpha\). Uniform honesty under \(Q_0\), followed by the defining total-variation inequality \(Q_1(A)\geq Q_0(A)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1(B_N\leq L_0)
&\geq Q_0(B_N\leq L_0)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\\
&\geq 1-\alpha-\frac14
>\frac12, \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final inequality uses the declared restriction \(0<\alpha<1/4\). On \(\{B_N\leq L_0\}\), equation (16) implies
\[
(L_1-B_N)_+\geq L_1-L_0\geq aN^{-1/2}. \tag{18}
\]
Combining (17)--(18) therefore yields
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}[(L(\mathbf P)-B_N)_+]
\geq \mathbb E_{Q_1}[(L_1-B_N)_+]
\geq \frac a2N^{-1/2}. \tag{19}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(B_N\in\mathcal H_\alpha\) proves
\[
E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)\geq c_{d,\alpha}N^{-1/2} \tag{20}
\]
with the positive choice \(c_{d,\alpha}=\min\{a/2,1\}\). This choice also satisfies \(c_{d,\alpha}\leq C_{d,\alpha}\), because the value of \(C_{d,\alpha}\) fixed after (15) is greater than one.

Finally, fix any \(c_0>0\) and consider a legal triangular array satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:legal-triangular-array} and \(K_N\geq c_0N^{1/4}\) eventually. Because \(r=\min\{K,d\}\leq d\),
\[
\frac r{K^2}
\leq \frac d{c_0^2}N^{-1/2}. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (15), (20), and (21) show
\[
c_{d,\alpha}N^{-1/2}
\leq E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)
\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left(1+\frac d{c_0^2}\right)N^{-1/2}. \tag{22}
\]
This is \(E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)\asymp N^{-1/2}\) in the asserted regime, with comparison constants allowed to depend on \(d\), \(\alpha\), and the fixed positive threshold \(c_0\). The rule establishing (8) and (14) is the fully specified independent mean-floor baseline: by Definition~\texttt{def:mean-lower-bound}, it uses only \(\bar X\), \(N\), and \(\alpha\), and requires neither computing \(\widehat L\), solving a multi-marginal optimal-transport problem, nor choosing the existential empirical-process constant \(C_d\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Uniform coverage and expected shortfall are treated as a decision problem distinct from point estimation. \textit{Gap.} Ji--Lei--Spector prove broad dual-feasible uniform validity, and Manole--Balakrishnan--Wasserman prove near-minimax intervals for sliced Wasserstein distance; neither gives this all-arm one-sided endpoint-length converse. \textit{Consumer.} A reported MOT tightening can be accompanied by a lower confidence endpoint whose worst-case conservatism is quantitatively controlled.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certified-computation]\label{thm:certified-computation}
For fixed \(d\), rational empirical input, rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), and \(N=Km\), \(\mathsf{CertMOT}_\epsilon\) returns \(\underline L_\epsilon\le L(\widehat{\mathbf P})\le\overline L_\epsilon\) with \(\overline L_\epsilon-\underline L_\epsilon\le\epsilon\), together with primal and dual witnesses, in time polynomial in the input bit length, \(N\), and \((K/\epsilon)^d\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume the rational empirical input and rational \(0<\epsilon<1\) required by the statement. Set
\[
M=\left\lceil\frac{32d}{\epsilon}\right\rceil,
\qquad h=M^{-1},
\qquad \eta=3dh.
\tag{13}
\]
Then \(M\) is a positive integer, \(h\) and \(\eta\) are rational,
\[
0<h\le\frac{\epsilon}{32d},
\qquad
M\le\frac{32d}{\epsilon}+1,
\tag{14}
\]
and all of these quantities have encoding length polynomial in the encoding length of \(\epsilon\) and \(d\).

Round each coordinate of an empirical atom \(x\) to a multiple of \(h\), with a deterministic tie rule, and denote the result by \(q(x)\). Coordinatewise rounding gives \(|q_j(x)-x_j|\le h\) for \(j=1,\ldots,d\). Since \(d\ge1\),
\[
\|q(x)-x\|_2
\le\sum_{j=1}^d|q_j(x)-x_j|
\le dh.
\tag{15}
\]
This deliberately rational bound avoids introducing \(\sqrt d\) into the certificate. For a tuple \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_K)\), define
\[
a=K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^Kx_k,
\qquad
a_q=K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^Kq(x_k).
\]
Unit-ball support gives \(\|a\|_2\le1\); (15) gives \(\|a-a_q\|_2\le dh\), and hence \(\|a_q\|_2\le1+dh\). By (14), \(dh\le\epsilon/32<1/32\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
|c_K(x)-c_K(q(x))|
&=|\|a\|_2^2-\|a_q\|_2^2|\\
&\le\|a-a_q\|_2(\|a\|_2+\|a_q\|_2)\\
&\le dh(2+dh)\\
&\le3dh=\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Every quantity on the final line is rational.

Merge atoms in each arm that have the same quantized image. Write the distinct images as \(q_{kj}\) and their rational empirical masses as \(\widehat p_{kj}\). The gridded dual linear program is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize}\quad&\sum_{k,j}\widehat p_{kj}u_{kj},\\
\text{subject to}\quad&\sum_{k=1}^Ku_{k j_k}
\le K^{-2}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^Kq_{k j_k}\right\|_2^2
\quad\text{for every }(j_1,\ldots,j_K).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Its coefficients are rational. To separate a proposed rational vector \(u\), define \(D_0(0)=0\), set all other nonexistent states to \(+\infty\), and recursively compute
\[
D_k(s)=\min_{j_1,\ldots,j_k:\,\sum_{\ell=1}^kq_{\ell j_\ell}=s}
\left(-\sum_{\ell=1}^ku_{\ell j_\ell}\right).
\tag{18}
\]
Equivalently, each transition updates
\[
D_k(s+q_{kj})
=\min\{D_k(s+q_{kj}),D_{k-1}(s)-u_{kj}\}.
\]
At the final layer,
\[
\min_s\left\{D_K(s)+K^{-2}\|s\|_2^2\right\}
=\min_{j_1,\ldots,j_K}
\left\{K^{-2}\left\|\sum_kq_{k j_k}\right\|_2^2-\sum_ku_{k j_k}\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
A negative minimizer supplies a violated constraint of (17), while a nonnegative minimum certifies every constraint. Thus (18)--(19) are an exact strong separation oracle, not a relaxation.

Every coordinate of \(q_{kj}\) is a multiple of \(h\) and has absolute value at most \(1+h\). A partial sum therefore has each coordinate in an interval of length at most \(2K(1+h)\). From (14), the number of partial-sum states at a layer is at most
\[
O_d((K/h)^d)=O_d((K/\epsilon)^d).
\tag{20}
\]
There are at most \(m\) transitions out of a state at each of the \(K\) layers. The balanced identity \(N=Km\) therefore bounds the total number of dynamic-programming transitions by \(O_d(N(K/\epsilon)^d)\). Exact additions and comparisons involve rational numbers of polynomial encoding length.

We next verify the boundedness premise needed for oracle-polynomial optimization. The quantized points satisfy \(\|q_{kj}\|_2\le1+dh<2\), so every cost in (17) lies in \([0,4]\). The product of the gridded marginals is a feasible primal coupling, and its bounded cost gives a finite primal optimum. Lemma~\texttt{lem:finite-lp-optimization} therefore supplies finite primal and dual optimizers with equal values. Starting from an optimal dual, replace one potential at a time by its finite \(c_K\)-transform
\[
u_k(q_{kj})
=\min_{(j_\ell)_{\ell\ne k}}
\left\{c_K(q_{1j_1},\ldots,q_{Kj_K})-
\sum_{\ell\ne k}u_\ell(q_{\ell j_\ell})\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Feasibility of the old tuple implies that (21) can only increase \(u_k\); the new tuple is feasible by its definition. Since the old tuple is optimal and all marginal masses are nonnegative and sum to one, the objective cannot increase strictly. Thus sequential application of (21) preserves optimality. For any two support points \(q,q'\) in margin \(k\), the elementary inequality \(|\inf A-\inf B|\le\sup|A-B|\) and the cost range give
\[
|u_k(q)-u_k(q')|
\le\sup_{q_{-k}}|c_K(q,q_{-k})-c_K(q',q_{-k})|
\le4.
\tag{22}
\]

Choose one reference point in every nonempty marginal support. Gauge shifts \(u_k\mapsto u_k+b_k\) with \(\sum_kb_k=0\) preserve both feasibility and objective. Use them to make the first \(K-1\) reference values zero. Equation (22) then bounds all values in those margins by \(4\) in absolute value. The constraint at the all-reference tuple bounds the last reference value above by \(4\). Conversely, the zero dual is feasible, so the optimal value is nonnegative. The first \(K-1\) marginal integrals are at most \(4(K-1)\), and the last marginal integral is at most its reference value plus \(4\). Hence optimality implies
\[
0\le\sum_{k,j}\widehat p_{kj}u_{kj}
\le4(K-1)+u_K(q_{K0})+4,
\]
so \(u_K(q_{K0})\ge-4K\). Together with (22), this places an optimal dual in a rational box of radius \(4K+4\), whose encoding length is polynomial.

Lemma~\texttt{lem:finite-lp-optimization}, applied to (17), this rational box, and the oracle (18)--(19), now computes an optimal gridded dual value \(L_g\) and recovers a primal optimizer with the same value and finite rational witnesses. Its running time is polynomial in the input bit length, \(N\), and the state bound (20).

Push any coupling of the original empirical laws forward coordinatewise through \(q\). This gives a feasible gridded coupling, and (16) changes its objective by at most \(\eta\). Conversely, lift the gridded primal optimizer by drawing, conditional on each quantized tuple, each original coordinate from the corresponding empirical quantization fiber with its empirical conditional probabilities. This preserves every original empirical marginal, and (16) again changes the objective by at most \(\eta\). Taking the respective infima gives
\[
L_g-\eta\le L(\widehat{\mathbf P})\le L_g+\eta.
\tag{23}
\]
The lifted primal is an explicit certificate for the upper inequality. If \(u\) is the gridded dual witness, compose every potential with \(q\) on the original atoms and subtract \(\eta\) from the first potential. By (16) and (17), for every original tuple \(x\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^Ku_k(q(x_k))-\eta
\le c_K(q(x))-\eta
\le c_K(x).
\]
Thus these shifted potentials are dual feasible for the original empirical problem and have objective \(L_g-\eta\), certifying the lower inequality in (23).

Return
\[
\underline L_\epsilon=L_g-\eta,
\qquad
\overline L_\epsilon=L_g+\eta.
\]
Equations (13)--(14) give the rational width bound
\[
\overline L_\epsilon-\underline L_\epsilon
=2\eta=6dh\le\frac{3}{16}\epsilon<\epsilon.
\]
This proves the stated bracket, witnesses, rational auditability, and complexity bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A rational mesh and rational cost allowance separate numerical error from sampling error through explicit primal and dual witnesses. \textit{Gap.} The complexity is polynomial in \((K/\epsilon)^d\) for fixed \(d\), not polynomial in a varying dimension and not a generic exact-MOT guarantee. \textit{Consumer.} The empirical endpoint and plug-in confidence correction can carry an auditable numerical error budget.

\begin{proposition}[prop:star-balanced-audit]\label{prop:star-balanced-audit}
Under Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:star-iid-superpopulation}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:star-complete-randomization}\), the randomized balanced subsample in Definition \(\mathrm{def:star-transformation}\) satisfies \(\mathcal L((X_{ki}^{\mathrm{STAR}})_{1\le k\le3,1\le i\le36})=\bigotimes_{k=1}^3(P_k^{\mathrm{STAR}})^{\otimes36}\). It therefore has \(N=108\), \(K=3\), \(m=36\), and \(d=5\), and \(\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})}\mathbb E_\gamma\|\sum_{k=1}^3Z_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}\|_2^2=9L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})\). Consequently, \(9b_Y^2L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})\) is the sharp lower endpoint for \(\mathbb E[(\sum_{k=1}^3\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}Y(k))^2]\). This empirical audit is conditional on the declared i.i.d. superpopulation law, the known bound \(B^{\mathrm{STAR}}\), the affine scaling, complete randomization, and randomized balanced subsampling; it does not validate the native-count \((161,37,36)\) plug-in calculation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Proof.}
Write \(U_j=U_j^{\mathrm{STAR}}\), \(W_j=W_j^{\mathrm{STAR}}\), and let
\(\mathcal U=\sigma(U_j:j\in\mathcal J^{\mathrm{STAR}})\).  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:star-complete-randomization}\) gives a conditional distribution of
\((W_j)_j\) that is constant as a function of \(\mathcal U\).  Thus, for every
assignment event \(C\) and every \(D\in\mathcal U\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{(W_j)_j\in C,\ D\}
 =\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf 1_D
   \mathbb P\{(W_j)_j\in C\mid\mathcal U\}\right]
 =\mathbb P\{(W_j)_j\in C\}\mathbb P(D).
\]
Hence the assignment is independent of the potential-outcome vectors.  The
auxiliary randomization in Definition \(\mathrm{def:star-transformation}\) is
also independent of those vectors.  Consequently the full selected-label
array
\[
 \mathbf J=(J_{ki}:1\leq k\leq3,\ 1\leq i\leq36)
\]
is independent of \((U_j)_j\).  Its entries are pairwise distinct: within an
arm this follows from sampling without replacement, and across arms it follows
because the three assignment cells are disjoint.  The selection is feasible
because \(36=m^{\mathrm{STAR}}\leq n_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}\) for each
\(k\in\{1,2,3\}\).

For \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\), define the measurable coordinate map
\[
 g_k(y)=\left(\frac{\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}y}{b_Y},0,0,0,0\right),
 \qquad -B^{\mathrm{STAR}}\leq y\leq B^{\mathrm{STAR}}.
\]
The displayed contrast satisfies
\(\max_k|\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}|=1\).  Since
\(B^{\mathrm{STAR}}>0\), one has \(b_Y=B^{\mathrm{STAR}}>0\), and therefore
\[
 \|g_k(y)\|_2
 =\frac{|\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}y|}{b_Y}
 \leq\frac{|\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}|B^{\mathrm{STAR}}}
 {B^{\mathrm{STAR}}\max_\ell|\beta_\ell^{\mathrm{STAR}}|}
 \leq1.
\]
This verifies the asserted closed-unit-ball support, including its boundary.
Let \(A_{ki}\subseteq\mathbb B_5\) be Borel sets.  Conditional on any possible
value \(\mathbf j=(j_{ki})\) of \(\mathbf J\), Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:star-iid-superpopulation}\), independence of \(\mathbf J\), and
distinctness of its entries give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb P\!\left\{X_{ki}^{\mathrm{STAR}}\in A_{ki}
       \text{ for all }k,i\mid\mathbf J=\mathbf j\right\} \\
 &\quad=
 \mathbb P\!\left\{g_k\bigl(Y_{j_{ki}}(k)\bigr)\in A_{ki}
       \text{ for all }k,i\right\} \\
 &\quad=\prod_{k=1}^3\prod_{i=1}^{36}
 Q^{\mathrm{STAR}}\!\left\{u:g_k(u_k)\in A_{ki}\right\}
 =\prod_{k=1}^3\prod_{i=1}^{36}P_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}(A_{ki}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The last expression does not depend on \(\mathbf j\), so averaging over
\(\mathbf J\) proves
\[
 \mathcal L\bigl((X_{ki}^{\mathrm{STAR}})_{k,i}\bigr)
 =\bigotimes_{k=1}^3(P_k^{\mathrm{STAR}})^{\otimes36}.
\]
This is a conditioning-on-random-labels argument; it does not require the
three auxiliary selections to be mutually independent.  The balanced
experiment contains \(3\cdot36=108\) observations, so its parameters are
\(N=108\), \(K=3\), \(m=36\), and, by construction of \(g_k\), \(d=5\).

We next give the population identification argument.  Let
\(Q_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}\) denote the marginal law of \(Y(k)\) under
\(Q^{\mathrm{STAR}}\), and let
\[
 \mathcal C^{\mathrm{STAR}}
 =\left\{\eta\in
 \mathcal P([-B^{\mathrm{STAR}},B^{\mathrm{STAR}}]^3):
 \eta_k=Q_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}\text{ for }k=1,2,3\right\}.
\]
Thus the observable distribution of the balanced data is
\(\bigotimes_k(P_k^{\mathrm{STAR}})^{\otimes36}\), whereas the unobserved
object is a coupling \(\eta\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{STAR}}\).  For such a
coupling define the causal target
\[
 \theta(\eta)=\int
 \left(\sum_{k=1}^3\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}y_k\right)^2
 \,d\eta(y_1,y_2,y_3).
\]
No cross-world restriction other than the displayed marginal and support
conditions is maintained here, so the identified set is
\[
 \Theta_I(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})
 =\{\theta(\eta):\eta\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{STAR}}\}.
\]

The pushforward construction now proves, rather than assumes, its relation to
the observed-law optimal-transport functional.  Put
\[
 \mathcal R_k
 =\{g_k(y):-B^{\mathrm{STAR}}\leq y\leq B^{\mathrm{STAR}}\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 h_k(z)=\frac{b_Yz_1}{\beta_k^{\mathrm{STAR}}}
 \quad(z\in\mathcal R_k).
\]
All three coefficients in
\(\beta^{\mathrm{STAR}}=(-1,1/2,1/2)\) are nonzero, so each inverse \(h_k\) is
well defined.  If \(G(y_1,y_2,y_3)=(g_1(y_1),g_2(y_2),g_3(y_3))\), then
\(G_\#\eta\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})\) for every
\(\eta\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{STAR}}\).  Conversely, each
\(\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})\) assigns probability one to
\(\mathcal R_1\times\mathcal R_2\times\mathcal R_3\), and
\((h_1,h_2,h_3)_\#\gamma\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{STAR}}\).  These two
pushforwards are inverse to one another up to null sets.  Moreover, for a
corresponding pair \((\eta,\gamma)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \theta(\eta)
 &=b_Y^2\int
 \left(\sum_{k=1}^3(g_k(y_k))_1\right)^2,d\eta(y_1,y_2,y_3)\\
 &=b_Y^2\int
 \left\|\sum_{k=1}^3z_k\right\|_2^2,d\gamma(z_1,z_2,z_3),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second equality uses the fact that every \(z_k\in\mathcal R_k\)
has four zero coordinates.  It follows that the identified set has the exact
observable-law representation
\[
 \Theta_I(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})
 =\left\{b_Y^2\int\left\|\sum_{k=1}^3z_k\right\|_2^2,d\gamma:
 \gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})\right\}.
\]

For completeness, the extrema in the last display are attained.  Here is the
direct compactness argument.  On the compact metric space
\(\mathcal X=(\mathbb B_5)^3\), take any sequence of couplings.  Choose nested
finite Borel partitions of \(\mathcal X\) whose meshes tend to zero.  Repeated
finite-dimensional subsequence extraction makes the masses of every cell in
every partition converge.  The consistent limiting cell masses define a
Borel probability measure on \(\mathcal X\).  For every continuous function
\(f\), upper and lower cell sums differ by at most the modulus of continuity
of \(f\) at the partition mesh, which tends to zero.  Thus the extracted
subsequence converges against every continuous \(f\).  Applying this first to
functions of one coordinate shows that fixed marginals are preserved, and
applying it to the continuous bounded cost
\(\|z_1+z_2+z_3\|_2^2\) shows convergence of the objective.  A minimizing and
a maximizing sequence therefore yield couplings \(\gamma_L\) and
\(\gamma_U\) attaining the two extrema.  Convex mixtures of these couplings
retain all three marginals and attain every intermediate objective value.
Consequently
\[
 \Theta_I(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})=[\theta_L,\theta_U],
 \qquad
 \theta_L=b_Y^2\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})}
 \int\left\|\sum_{k=1}^3z_k\right\|_2^2,d\gamma,
\]
with the analogous maximum defining \(\theta_U\).  This proves sharpness, not
merely an outer bound.

Finally, Definition \(c_K(z_1,\ldots,z_K)=K^{-2}\|\sum_kz_k\|_2^2\) and
\(K=3\) imply, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})
 &=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})}
   \int\frac{1}{9}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^3z_k\right\|_2^2,d\gamma,\\
 9L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})
 &=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}})}
   \int\left\|\sum_{k=1}^3z_k\right\|_2^2,d\gamma,\\
 \theta_L&=9b_Y^2L(\mathbf P^{\mathrm{STAR}}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first two lines also prove the unscaled equality claimed in the
proposition.  No overlap condition, matrix-rank condition, or asymptotic limit
is used; positivity of \(B^{\mathrm{STAR}}\), nonvanishing of the three
contrast coefficients, bounded support, and feasibility of the balanced
subsample are the division, inversion, support, and selection conditions used
above.  The argument establishes a guarantee for the randomized balanced
subsample of size \(108\).  The native array has cell sizes \((161,37,36)\),
not \((36,36,36)\), so no statement about a native-count plug-in rule follows
from this calculation without a separate unequal-cell theorem.  This proves
the final scope qualification. \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The audit separates the estimand-preserving scale transformation from the sampling contract actually required by the theory. \textit{Gap.} Gao--Ge--Qian's theoretical display is balanced, while its public STAR computation uses native unequal cell sizes and does not supply this known-bound scaling plus superpopulation-to-product-law reduction. \textit{Consumer.} A reanalysis of the scholarship-incentive contrast can state exactly which observations, scale, and endpoint its guarantee covers.

\begin{lemma}[lem:convex-entropy-and-dudley]\label{lem:convex-entropy-and-dudley}
Let \(Q_n\) be the empirical law of \(n\) independent observations from a probability law \(Q\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), and write \(\mathcal N(\varepsilon,\mathcal G,\rho)\) for a covering number. For every measurable function class \(\mathcal G\), the expected empirical supremum is bounded, up to a universal constant, by
\[
\mathbb E\inf_{\tau>0}\left\{\tau+n^{-1/2}\int_\tau^\infty
\sqrt{\log \mathcal N(\varepsilon,\mathcal G,L^2(Q_n))}\,d\varepsilon\right\}.
\]
Moreover, if \(\mathcal F_{L,U}([a,b]^d)\) is the class of convex \(L\)-Lipschitz functions on \([a,b]^d\) with absolute value at most \(U\), then for universal \(C,\varepsilon_0>0\),
\[
\log \mathcal N\!\left(\varepsilon,\mathcal F_{L,U}([a,b]^d),L^\infty\right)
\le C\left(\frac{U+L(b-a)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d/2}
\]
whenever \(0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\{U+L(b-a)\}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:concave-empirical-process]\label{lem:concave-empirical-process}
For fixed \(d>4\), let \(Q\) be any Borel probability law on \(\mathbb B_d\), let \(Q_m\) be its empirical law from \(m\) independent observations, and let \(\mathcal C_\ell\) be the restrictions to \(\mathbb B_d\) of globally concave \(\ell\)-Lipschitz functions \(g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) satisfying \(g(0)=0\). There is \(C_d<\infty\), independent of \(Q\), \(m\), and \(\ell\), such that
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{g\in\mathcal C_\ell}\left|\int g\,d(Q_m-Q)\right|
\le C_d\ell m^{-2/d}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(g\in\mathcal C_\ell\), global Lipschitzness and \(g(0)=0\) imply \(|g(x)|\le\ell\sqrt d\) on \([-1,1]^d\). The function \(-g\) is convex there. The entropy clause of Lemma~\texttt{lem:convex-entropy-and-dudley}, applied with \(L=\ell\), \(U=\ell\sqrt d\), \(a=-1\), and \(b=1\), therefore gives
\[
\log \mathcal N(\varepsilon,\mathcal C_\ell,L^\infty(\mathbb B_d))
\le C_d(\ell/\varepsilon)^{d/2}. \tag{6}
\]
The same estimate applies to the negatives of the functions in \(\mathcal C_\ell\). Applying the Dudley clause of that lemma to the union of the two classes turns the supremum into an absolute supremum and, for every \(0<\tau\le C_d\ell\), gives
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{g\in\mathcal C_\ell}\left|\int g\,d(Q_m-Q)\right|
\le C_d\left\{\tau+m^{-1/2}\ell^{d/4}\int_\tau^{C_d\ell}
\varepsilon^{-d/4}\,d\varepsilon\right\}. \tag{7}
\]
Because \(d>4\), the integral in (7) is at most \(C_d\tau^{1-d/4}\). Choosing \(\tau=\ell m^{-2/d}\) yields
\[
m^{-1/2}\ell^{d/4}\tau^{1-d/4}=\ell m^{-2/d}.
\]
Substitution in (7) proves the asserted uniform bound. The cases \(\ell=0\) and bounded \(m\) are immediate from normalization and are also covered by enlarging \(C_d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:manole-nilesweed-general-lower]\label{lem:manole-nilesweed-general-lower}
Let \(\mathcal X,\mathcal Y\subset\mathbb R^d\) be compact convex sets with nonempty interior. Suppose \(h\) is nonnegative, convex, and even, and there is a convex open neighborhood \(\mathcal Z_1\) of \(\mathcal X-\mathcal Y\) on which \(0\le h\le1\) and \(\|h\|_{\mathcal C^2(\mathcal Z_1)}<\infty\). Suppose also that there are \(x_0\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal X)\), \(y_0\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal Y)\), \(z_0=x_0-y_0\), and \(\lambda>0\) such that
\[
h(z)-h(z_0)\ge \nabla h(z_0)\mathbin\cdot(z-z_0)+\lambda\|z-z_0\|_2^2
\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal X-\mathcal Y.
\]
For the transport cost \(\mathcal T_h(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int h(x-y)\,d\pi\), there is \(C>0\), depending only on the displayed fixed objects and \(d\), such that
\[
\inf_{\widehat{\mathcal T}}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\mathcal X),\,\nu\in\mathcal P(\mathcal Y)}
\mathbb E_{\mu,\nu}|\widehat{\mathcal T}-\mathcal T_h(\mu,\nu)|
\ge C(m\log m)^{-2/d}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-sample-wasserstein-lower]\label{lem:two-sample-wasserstein-lower}
For fixed \(d\ge5\), there is \(c_d>0\) such that, from \(m\) independent observations from each of arbitrary \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)\),
\[
\inf_{\widehat W}\sup_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)}
\mathbb E_{\mu,\nu}|\widehat W-W_2^2(\mu,\nu)|
\ge c_d(m\log m)^{-2/d},
\]
where the infimum ranges over all Borel-measurable estimators.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:manole-nilesweed-general-lower} with \(\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\tfrac14\mathbb B_d\) and \(h(z)=\|z\|_2^2\). These sets are compact, convex, contained in \(\mathbb B_d\), and have nonempty interior. On the open ball \(\mathcal Z_1=\{z:\|z\|_2<3/4\}\), which contains \(\mathcal X-\mathcal Y\), \(h\) is nonnegative, at most \(9/16\), even, convex, and has finite \(\mathcal C^2\)-norm. With \(x_0=y_0=0\), \(z_0=0\), and \(\lambda=1\),
\[
h(z)-h(0)=\|z\|_2^2=\nabla h(0)\mathbin\cdot z+\lambda\|z\|_2^2.
\]
Thus every premise of the cited theorem is satisfied, and it gives the asserted lower bound over laws supported on \(\tfrac14\mathbb B_d\). This class is a subclass of \(\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)\), so enlarging the supremum preserves the lower bound. Finally \(\mathcal T_h=W_2^2\) by the definition of \(W_2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:bernoulli-two-point]\label{lem:bernoulli-two-point}
There is a universal constant \(a>0\) such that, whenever \(N=Km\), \(K\ge3\), and \(m\ge2\), two tuples \(\mathbf P^{(0)},\mathbf P^{(1)}\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K\) can be chosen with
\[
L(\mathbf P^{(1)})-L(\mathbf P^{(0)})\ge aN^{-1/2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^K(P_k^{(0)})^{\otimes m},
\bigotimes_{k=1}^K(P_k^{(1)})^{\otimes m}\right)\le\frac14.
\]
More precisely, one may take all \(K\) marginals equal to \((1-p_j)\delta_0+p_j\delta_{e_1}\), with \(p_0=1/2\) and \(p_1=1/2+aN^{-1/2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(p\in[0,1]\), take every marginal to be \((1-p)\delta_0+p\delta_{e_1}\), and let \(T=\sum_{k=1}^K\mathbf 1\{Z_k=e_1\}\). Write \(Kp=b+s\), where \(b=\lfloor Kp\rfloor\) and \(s\in[0,1)\). The affine line through \((b,b^2)\) and \((b+1,(b+1)^2)\) lies below \(t\mapsto t^2\) at every integer \(t\). Since \(\mathbb ET=Kp\), every coupling obeys
\[
\mathbb ET^2\ge(1-s)b^2+s(b+1)^2. \tag{8}
\]
Equality is attainable: draw \(T=b\) or \(b+1\) with probabilities \(1-s\) and \(s\), and, conditional on \(T\), choose uniformly a subset of \(T\) arms to equal \(e_1\). Each arm then has success probability \(\mathbb ET/K=p\). Hence
\[
L_K(p):=L(\mathbf P(p))=K^{-2}\{(1-s)b^2+s(b+1)^2\}. \tag{9}
\]
On every linear segment with \(p\ge1/2\), the slope of (9) is \((2b+1)/K\ge1\); the same inequality survives crossings of its finitely many knots by continuity and summation over segments. Thus \(L_K(p_1)-L_K(p_0)\ge p_1-p_0\).

Choose \(a=1/16\), \(p_0=1/2\), and \(p_1=1/2+aN^{-1/2}\). Since \(N=Km\ge6\), \(p_1-p_0\le1/4\). Put \(\delta=aN^{-1/2}\). The one-observation chi-square divergence, with the high law as reference, is
\[
\chi^2(\operatorname{Ber}(1/2),\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+\delta))
=\frac{\delta^2}{(1/2+\delta)(1/2-\delta)}
\le6\delta^2.
\]
For products, direct expansion of the likelihood-ratio square gives \(1+\chi^2(P^{\otimes N},Q^{\otimes N})=\{1+\chi^2(P,Q)\}^N\). Hence the product-experiment assumption and \(N=Km\) imply
\[
\chi^2(\mathbb P_0,\mathbb P_1)\le e^{6N\delta^2}-1=e^{6a^2}-1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb P_0,\mathbb P_1)
=\frac12\mathbb E_1\left|\frac{d\mathbb P_0}{d\mathbb P_1}-1\right|
\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(\mathbb P_0,\mathbb P_1)}
<\frac14.
\]
Equation (9) gives endpoint separation at least \(aN^{-1/2}\), completing the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:dimension-dependent-root-n-frontier]\label{thm:dimension-dependent-root-n-frontier}
Let
\[
q_d=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{d-4}{2(d-2)}\right\}.
\]
For fixed \(d>4\), there are constants \(0<c_d\le C_d<\infty\), depending only on \(d\), such that, whenever \(N=Km\), \(K\ge3\), and \(m\ge2\),
\[
c_dN^{-1/2}\le R_d(N,K)
\le C_d\left[N^{-1/2}+\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}\right].
\]
Moreover, for every fixed \(c_0>0\), uniformly along every legal triangular array satisfying \(K_N\ge c_0N^{q_d}\) eventually,
\[
R_d(N,K)\asymp_{d,c_0}N^{-1/2}.
\]
In particular, \(q_5=1/6\), whereas \(q_d=1/4\) for every \(d\ge6\).
Within the legal arrays, the complement of the class covered by some positive eventual lower bound is exactly
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N^{q_d}}=0.
\]
Every such array has a subsequence on which \(K_N/N^{q_d}\to0\), while every subsequence with positive lower limit is already in the proved root-\(N\) region.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the integer \(d>4\).  First take finite positive integers \(N,K,m\) satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:balanced-array}, so \(N=Km\), with \(K\ge3\) and \(m\ge2\).  Under Assumptions~\texttt{ass:product-experiment} and \texttt{ass:unit-ball-support}, Theorem~\texttt{thm:improved-plugin-upper} supplies a finite constant \(C_d\), depending only on \(d\), for which the empirical multi-marginal transport estimator in Definition~\texttt{def:empirical-endpoint} obeys
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|
\le C_d\left[N^{-1/2}+
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}\right].
\tag{1}
\]
The estimator \(\widehat L\) is measurable by the cited theorem.  Since the infimum in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-risk} ranges over every measurable estimator, substituting \(T=\widehat L\) into that definition and using (1) gives
\[
R_d(N,K)
\le C_d\left[N^{-1/2}+
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}\right].
\tag{2}
\]

For the all-estimator converse, Lemma~\texttt{lem:bernoulli-two-point}, under the same balanced product experiment, gives two tuples \(\mathbf P^{(0)},\mathbf P^{(1)}\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K\).  Put
\[
L_j=L(\mathbf P^{(j)}),
\qquad
Q_j=\bigotimes_{k=1}^K(P_k^{(j)})^{\otimes m},
\qquad
\Delta=L_1-L_0.
\]
The lemma states that, for a universal \(a>0\),
\[
\Delta\ge aN^{-1/2},
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\frac14.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(T\) be any measurable estimator and define the measurable event
\[
D=\left\{T>\frac{L_0+L_1}{2}\right\}.
\]
On \(D\), \(|T-L_0|\ge\Delta/2\), while on \(D^c\), \(|T-L_1|\ge\Delta/2\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_0}|T-L_0|+\mathbb E_{Q_1}|T-L_1|
&\ge\frac\Delta2\{Q_0(D)+Q_1(D^c)\}\\
&=\frac\Delta2\{1-(Q_1(D)-Q_0(D))\}\\
&\ge\frac\Delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}\\
&\ge\frac{3\Delta}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The penultimate inequality is the defining domination \(|Q_1(D)-Q_0(D)|\le\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  At least one summand in (4) is therefore at least \(3\Delta/16\).  Since both tuples lie in the supremum defining \(R_d(N,K)\), (3)--(4) imply, for every \(T\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|T-L(\mathbf P)|
\ge\frac{3a}{16}N^{-1/2}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(T\) proves
\[
R_d(N,K)\ge c_dN^{-1/2},
\qquad c_d:=\frac{3a}{16}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
The constant is universal and hence is admissible as a constant depending only on fixed \(d\).

It remains to prove the universally quantified elbow.  Let \(c_0>0\) be arbitrary and fixed, and consider a legal triangular array satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:legal-triangular-array} and \(K_N\ge c_0N^{q_d}\) eventually.  Positivity of \(N\), \(K\), and \(m\), together with \(N=Km\), gives
\[
\frac1K m^{-2/d}
=\frac1K\left(\frac NK\right)^{-2/d}
=N^{-2/d}K^{-(d-2)/d}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(d=5\), then
\[
q_d=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac1{2\cdot3}\right\}=\frac16.
\]
Because the minimum in (2) is no larger than its second argument, (6) and the eventual arm inequality yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}
&\le N^{-2/d}K^{-(d-2)/d}\\
&\le c_0^{-(d-2)/d}
N^{-2/d-q_d(d-2)/d}\\
&=c_0^{-3/5}N^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Here the last equality uses \(d=5\) and \(q_d=1/6\).

If \(d\ge6\), then
\[
\frac{d-4}{2(d-2)}\ge\frac14
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\ge6,
\]
so \(q_d=1/4\).  Since the definition \(r=\min\{K,d\}\) gives \(r\le d\), the minimum in (2) is no larger than its first argument, and therefore
\[
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}
\le\frac r{K^2}
\le d c_0^{-2}N^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (2), (5), and (7) gives, for \(d=5\),
\[
c_dN^{-1/2}\le R_d(N,K)
\le C_d(1+c_0^{-3/5})N^{-1/2},
\tag{9}
\]
eventually along the array.  Combining (2), (5), and (8) gives, for \(d\ge6\),
\[
c_dN^{-1/2}\le R_d(N,K)
\le C_d(1+d c_0^{-2})N^{-1/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the comparison constants are uniform in \(N\), \(K\), and \(\mathbf P\), while the upper comparison constant may depend on \(d\) and on the arbitrarily prescribed \(c_0\).  Assumption~\texttt{ass:legal-triangular-array} imposes \(m_N=N/K_N\to\infty\); it is used only to specify the legal asymptotic domain.  Equations (1)--(10) are finite-sample inequalities, so no exchange of a supremum and a limit is involved.

For completeness, put \(a_N=K_N/N^{q_d}\), which is positive.  If \(\liminf_Na_N=b>0\), the definition of the lower limit gives \(a_N\ge b/2\) eventually, so the preceding result applies with the fixed threshold \(c_0=b/2\).  Conversely, \(a_N\ge c_0>0\) eventually implies \(\liminf_Na_N\ge c_0>0\).  Therefore the complement of the arrays covered by a positive eventual lower bound is exactly \(\liminf_Na_N=0\).  In that case, the definition of the lower limit permits an increasing sequence \(N_j\) with \(a_{N_j}<1/j\), and hence \(a_{N_j}\to0\).  Applied to any subsequence, the same argument shows that a positive lower limit puts it in the proved region, while a zero lower limit yields a further ratio-to-zero subsequence.  Since \(c_0>0\) was arbitrary, this proves the stated quantifier, the residual-class characterization, and the two displayed values of \(q_d\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The active empirical term and the residual cap yield \(q_d\), while the Bernoulli pair gives an all-estimator root-\(N\) converse; the finite-sample inequalities work for every prescribed \(c_0>0\). \textit{Gap.} The theorem closes every subsequence with a positive lower bound on \(K_N/N^{q_d}\) and makes no exact-rate claim on ratio-to-zero subsequences. \textit{Consumer.} It removes every positive-lower-ratio subsequence from the residual frontier and supplies the quantifier-correct elbow calculation reused by honest length.

\begin{theorem}[thm:plugin-honest-length]\label{thm:plugin-honest-length}
Let \(q_d=\min\{1/4,(d-4)/[2(d-2)]\}\), and let \(U_{N,K}\) and \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\) be defined in Definition~\texttt{def:plugin-lower-bound}, with \(C_d\) chosen as an existence constant supplied by Theorem~\texttt{thm:improved-plugin-upper}.  For fixed \(d>4\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1/4)\), whenever \(N=Km\), \(K\ge3\), and \(m\ge2\),
\[
B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\in\mathcal H_\alpha,
\]
and there are constants \(0<c_{d,\alpha}\le C_{d,\alpha}<\infty\) such that
\[
c_{d,\alpha}N^{-1/2}
\le E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)
\le C_{d,\alpha}\left[N^{-1/2}+\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}\right].
\]
For every fixed \(c_0>0\), uniformly along every legal triangular array satisfying \(K_N\ge c_0N^{q_d}\) eventually,
\[
E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)\asymp_{d,\alpha,c_0}N^{-1/2}.
\]
Here \(q_5=1/6\) and \(q_d=1/4\) for \(d\ge6\).  The rule \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\) is theoretically calibrated: existence of a suitable \(C_d\) is proved, but no numerical value is supplied.  No matching nonparametric expected-length converse is asserted on the residual smaller-arm subsequences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(d>4\), \(\alpha\in(0,1/4)\), and positive integers \(N,K,m\) satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:balanced-array}, with \(K\ge3\) and \(m\ge2\).  Under Assumptions~\texttt{ass:product-experiment} and \texttt{ass:unit-ball-support}, choose the existence constant \(C_d\) in Definition~\texttt{def:plugin-lower-bound} so that Theorem~\texttt{thm:improved-plugin-upper} gives
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|
\le U_{N,K},
\qquad
U_{N,K}=C_d\left[N^{-1/2}+
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}\right].
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(C_d>0\), \(N>0\), and \(\alpha>0\), both \(U_{N,K}\) and \(U_{N,K}/\alpha\) are positive.  For every nonnegative random variable \(Y\) and every \(u>0\), the pointwise inequality \(\mathbf 1\{Y>u\}\le Y/u\) implies \(\Pr(Y>u)\le\mathbb EY/u\) after integration.  Apply this derived inequality to \(Y=|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|\) and \(u=U_{N,K}/\alpha\).  Equation (1) then gives, for every \(\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K\),
\[
\Pr_{\mathbf P}\left\{|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|>
\frac{U_{N,K}}\alpha\right\}
\le\frac{\alpha\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}|\widehat L-L(\mathbf P)|}{U_{N,K}}
\le\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
On the complementary event,
\[
\widehat L-\frac{U_{N,K}}\alpha\le L(\mathbf P).
\tag{3}
\]
By the definition of \(c_K\), its value is a nonnegative squared norm divided by the positive number \(K^2\).  Hence every coupling objective is nonnegative, and the definition of the endpoint gives \(L(\mathbf P)\ge0\).  Combining this fact with (3) yields
\[
B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}
=\max\left\{0,\widehat L-\frac{U_{N,K}}\alpha\right\}
\le L(\mathbf P)
\tag{4}
\]
on the event in (3).  Equations (2)--(4), uniformly over \(\mathbf P\), give
\[
\inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\Pr_{\mathbf P}\{B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\le L(\mathbf P)\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\]
This is exactly \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\in\mathcal H_\alpha\) by Definition~\texttt{def:honest-bounds}.

For expected excess length, Definition~\texttt{def:plugin-lower-bound} implies pointwise that \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\ge\widehat L-U_{N,K}/\alpha\).  The inequalities \([x+y]_+\le[x]_++y\) for \(y\ge0\) and \([x]_+\le|x|\) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
[L(\mathbf P)-B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}]_+
&\le\left[L(\mathbf P)-\widehat L+\frac{U_{N,K}}\alpha\right]_+\\
&\le|L(\mathbf P)-\widehat L|+\frac{U_{N,K}}\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Taking expectations, using (1), and then taking the supremum gives
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\!\left[(L(\mathbf P)-B_N^{\mathrm{plug}})_+\right]
\le(1+\alpha^{-1})U_{N,K}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since the particular bound \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal H_\alpha\), the infimum in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-excess-length} and (6) prove the asserted upper bound, after absorbing \(1+\alpha^{-1}\) into a constant \(C_{d,\alpha}\).

For the converse, take the ordered pair from Lemma~\texttt{lem:bernoulli-two-point}.  Write
\[
L_j=L(\mathbf P^{(j)}),
\qquad
Q_j=\bigotimes_{k=1}^K(P_k^{(j)})^{\otimes m},
\qquad
\Delta=L_1-L_0.
\]
For the universal constant \(a>0\) in that lemma,
\[
\Delta\ge aN^{-1/2},
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\frac14.
\tag{7}
\]
Let \(B_N\in\mathcal H_\alpha\) be arbitrary.  Honesty at the low-endpoint law \(Q_0\) and the defining total-variation inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1(B_N\le L_0)
&\ge Q_0(B_N\le L_0)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\\
&\ge1-\alpha-\frac14
>\frac12,
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
where the strict final inequality uses the declared restriction \(\alpha<1/4\).  On \(\{B_N\le L_0\}\), one has \([L_1-B_N]_+\ge L_1-L_0=\Delta\).  Thus (7)--(8) imply
\[
\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P(\mathbb B_d)^K}
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P}\!\left[(L(\mathbf P)-B_N)_+\right]
\ge\mathbb E_{Q_1}\!\left[(L_1-B_N)_+\right]
\ge\frac a2N^{-1/2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(B_N\in\mathcal H_\alpha\) proves the lower bound with \(c_{d,\alpha}=a/2>0\).

Finally let \(c_0>0\) be arbitrary and fixed, and consider a legal array satisfying Assumption~\texttt{ass:legal-triangular-array} and \(K_N\ge c_0N^{q_d}\) eventually.  If \(d=5\), then \(q_d=1/6\), and Assumption~\texttt{ass:balanced-array} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}
&\le N^{-2/d}K^{-(d-2)/d}\\
&\le c_0^{-3/5}N^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
If \(d\ge6\), then \(q_d=1/4\), and \(r=\min\{K,d\}\le d\) gives
\[
\min\left\{\frac r{K^2},\frac1K m^{-2/d}\right\}
\le\frac r{K^2}
\le d c_0^{-2}N^{-1/2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (6) and (9) with (10) in dimension five, or with (11) in dimensions at least six, proves
\[
E_{d,\alpha}(N,K)\asymp_{d,\alpha,c_0}N^{-1/2}
\]
eventually along every such legal array.  All bounds preceding the last display are finite-sample and uniform over the arbitrary-margin class; the condition \(m_N=N/K_N\to\infty\) only fixes the legal limiting arrays.  Since \(c_0>0\) was arbitrary, the required universal quantifier follows.  The construction uses the existence of \(C_d\) in (1); because no numerical value of that constant is supplied, the proof establishes a theoretical uniformly calibrated rule, not a directly reportable numerical calibration.  The converse (9) is only of root-\(N\) order and therefore does not establish the missing smaller-arm nonparametric expected-length lower bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Markov calibration of the uniform mean plug-in error proves coverage, the same allowance bounds expected excess length, and the elbow algebra is uniform for every fixed \(c_0>0\). \textit{Gap.} The ordered Bernoulli pair proves only the root-\(N\) converse.  The rule uses an existence constant \(C_d\) with no supplied numerical calibration, and the smaller-arm nonparametric one-sided converse remains open. \textit{Consumer.} It provides the sharper theoretical honest endpoint in the positive-lower-ratio region; numerically reportable work may instead use the fully specified mean-floor baseline.

\section{Interpretation}

The proved phase boundary is a positive-lower-ratio statement, not an assertion that a single privileged threshold separates two models.  For every fixed \(c_0>0\), any legal subsequence with \(K_N\ge c_0N^{q_d}\) eventually has root-\(N\) estimation and honest one-sided expected-length order, with constants allowed to depend on \(c_0\).  Hence an irregular legal array with \(\liminf_NK_N/N^{q_d}=0\) decomposes into already solved positive-lower-bound subsequences and genuinely unresolved subsequences along which the ratio tends to zero.  Throughout, \(L\) remains the lower endpoint for the uncentered second moment; the centered residual \(V\) is used only to prove geometry and is not substituted for the causal target.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact small-ratio rate remains open.  The finite-support perturbation template is operational, but no support, signed directions, amplitude, and pair of hypothesis priors is shown to attain the missing order while simultaneously proving ordered endpoint separation, \(\operatorname{TV}_{\mathrm{mix}}\le1/4\), and strict \(\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{rem}}>0\) for every off-contact rematching column.  The available nonparametric lower helper is the two-margin subclass converse scaled by \(K^{-2}\), whereas the theoretical plug-in upper bound can retain \(K^{-1}m^{-2/d}\); fixed-dimensional tensoring does not bridge this factor because other arms can rematch.  No aggregate localization theorem for the jointly conjugate tuple currently moves the upper side.  The plug-in confidence rule is theoretical because the proof supplies existence but no numerical value of \(C_d\); the independent mean-floor rule is fully specified but has only the baseline order \(N^{-1/2}+r/K^2\).  The certified transport computation is fixed-dimensional and polynomial in \((K/\epsilon)^d\), not a generic polynomial-time exact-MOT statement.  The STAR audit discards observations, assumes a known support bound and a superpopulation law, and does not reproduce the native unequal-cell calculation.

\section{Honest scope}

The sharp residual envelope, optimal-sum diameter, improved empirical upper bound, finite-sample risk sandwich, dimension-dependent root-\(N\) frontier, plug-in honesty and expected-length bounds, mean-floor baseline, rational computation certificate, hypercube parity witness, and conditional STAR audit are proved results.  For every fixed \(c_0>0\), estimation and uniformly honest one-sided expected excess length are of exact root-\(N\) order along legal arrays satisfying \(K_N\ge c_0N^{q_d}\) eventually, with constants allowed to depend on \(d\), \(\alpha\) where relevant, and \(c_0\).  The exact all-\(K\) frontier is not claimed: on arrays with \(\liminf_NK_N/N^{q_d}=0\), ratio-to-zero subsequences retain the fixed-\(K\) logarithmic gap, the arm-factor gap, and the missing ordered one-sided converse.  The finite-support family is a search handle, not a proved hard family, and its off-contact certificate requires \(\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{rem}}>0\).  The rule \(B_N^{\mathrm{plug}}\) is uniformly honest but only theoretically existence-calibrated; \(B_N^{\mathrm{mean}}\) is fully specified, uses only \((\bar X,N,\alpha)\), and requires neither \(\widehat L\), a transport solve, nor \(C_d\).  The note fixes \(d>4\), assumes arbitrary unit-ball marginals, balanced armwise product sampling, and \(m_N=N/K_N\to\infty\), and preserves the uncentered second-moment endpoint.  The legal \(K=3,d=5\) hypercube witness has endpoint \(1/9\).  The STAR conclusion applies only to its declared balanced subsample and scaling, not to native unequal counts.
