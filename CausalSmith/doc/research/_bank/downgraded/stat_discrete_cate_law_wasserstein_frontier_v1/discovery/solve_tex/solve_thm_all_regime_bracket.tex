%%% FIELD proof-all-regime-bracket
Fix \(n\geq 1\), \(d\geq 2\), and \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:separated\mbox{-}atom\mbox{-}lower}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:scalar\mbox{-}transfer\mbox{-}lower}\), there are constants \(c_{1,\epsilon}>0\) and \(c_{2,\epsilon}>0\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), for which
\[
 R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_{1,\epsilon}\min\!\left\{\frac dn,n^{-2/3}\right\},
 \qquad
 R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq c_{2,\epsilon}\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}.
\]
If \(r\geq c_1 a\) and \(r\geq c_2 b\) for nonnegative \(a,b,r\) and positive \(c_1,c_2\), then
\[
 r\geq \min\{c_1,c_2\}\max\{a,b\}.
\]
Applying this elementary implication and then \(\mathrm{def:benchmarks}\) gives
\[
 R_{n,d,\epsilon}\geq
 \min\{c_{1,\epsilon},c_{2,\epsilon}\}
 \max\!\left\{
   \min\!\left(1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right),
   \min\!\left(\frac dn,n^{-2/3}\right)
 \right\}
 =c_\epsilon L_{n,d},
\]
after setting \(c_\epsilon=\min\{c_{1,\epsilon},c_{2,\epsilon}\}>0\).

For the upper bound, \(\widehat G_{\mathrm{emp}}\) is, by \(\mathrm{thm:empirical\mbox{-}upper}\), a measurable \(\mathcal P([-1,1])\)-valued statistic.  It is therefore an admissible decision rule in the infimum defining \(R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}risk}\).  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 &\leq \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}}
 E_{P^n}\!\left[W_1(\widehat G_{\mathrm{emp}},G_P)^2\right] \\
 &\leq C_\epsilon\min\!\left(1,\frac dn\right)
 =C_\epsilon U_{n,d},
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the second inequality is \(\mathrm{thm:empirical\mbox{-}upper}\) and the equality is \(\mathrm{def:benchmarks}\).  All constants depend only on the fixed overlap constant \(\epsilon\), so both inequalities hold uniformly over the stated \(n\) and \(d\).

%%% FIELD statement-uniform-log-characterization
Uniformly over \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\), there are constants \(c_\epsilon>0\) and \(C_\epsilon<\infty\), depending only on \(\epsilon\), such that
\[
 c_\epsilon L_{n,d}\leq R_{n,d,\epsilon}
 \leq C_\epsilon\log(ed)L_{n,d}.
\]
Thus the explicit lower benchmark characterizes the minimax squared-Wasserstein risk to within a factor of at most \(\log(ed)\) on the original unrestricted model class.

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-log-characterization
The lower inequality is the lower inequality in \(\mathrm{thm:all\mbox{-}regime\mbox{-}bracket}\).  For the upper inequality, put \(\ell=\log(ed)\).  Since \(d\geq2\), one has \(\ell=1+\log d>1\).  For every \(a\geq0\) and every \(\ell\geq1\),
\[
 \ell\min\!\left\{1,\frac a\ell\right\}=\min\{\ell,a\}\geq\min\{1,a\}.
\]
Taking \(a=d/n\) yields the first comparison requested in the claim:
\[
 U_{n,d}=\min\!\left(1,\frac dn\right)
 \leq \log(ed)\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}.
\]
The first term in the maximum defining \(L_{n,d}\) in \(\mathrm{def:benchmarks}\) is exactly \(\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\).  Therefore
\[
 U_{n,d}
 \leq \log(ed)\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}
 \leq \log(ed)L_{n,d}.
\]
Combining this deterministic comparison with the upper inequality \(R_{n,d,\epsilon}\leq C_\epsilon U_{n,d}\) from \(\mathrm{thm:all\mbox{-}regime\mbox{-}bracket}\) proves
\[
 R_{n,d,\epsilon}\leq C_\epsilon\log(ed)L_{n,d}.
\]
The constants inherited from that theorem depend only on \(\epsilon\), which establishes uniformity.

%%% FIELD statement-lower-benchmark-phase-diagram
For every \(n>1\) and \(d\geq2\), the lower benchmark has the phase diagram
\[
L_{n,d}=
\begin{cases}
 d/n,
   & d\leq n^{1/3},\\
 n^{-2/3},
   & n^{1/3}<d\leq n^{1/3}\log(ed),\\
 d/[n\log(ed)],
   & n^{1/3}\log(ed)<d<n\log(ed),\\
 1,
   & d\geq n\log(ed).
\end{cases}
\]
At every shared boundary the adjacent formulas agree, so the displayed allocation of equality is immaterial.  The first branch condition \(d\leq n^{1/3}\) is equivalent to \(n\geq d^3\); on that branch \(\mathrm{thm:dense\mbox{-}frontier}\) gives \(c_\epsilon L_{n,d}\leq R_{n,d,\epsilon}\leq C_\epsilon L_{n,d}\).

%%% FIELD proof-lower-benchmark-phase-diagram
Fix \(n>1\) and \(d\geq2\), and abbreviate \(\ell=\log(ed)\).  Then \(\ell>1\).  By \(\mathrm{def:benchmarks}\),
\[
 L_{n,d}=\max\{A,B\},\qquad
 A=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\ell}\right\},\qquad
 B=\min\!\left\{\frac dn,n^{-2/3}\right\}.
\]
We evaluate this maximum in four exhaustive regimes.

If \(d\leq n^{1/3}\), then \(d/n\leq n^{-2/3}\), so \(B=d/n\).  Also \(\ell>1\) implies \(d/(n\ell)<d/n\leq1\), whence \(A=d/(n\ell)\leq B\).  Thus \(L_{n,d}=d/n\).

If \(n^{1/3}<d\leq n^{1/3}\ell\), then \(d/n>n^{-2/3}\), so \(B=n^{-2/3}\).  Moreover
\[
 \frac{d}{n\ell}\leq\frac{n^{1/3}\ell}{n\ell}=n^{-2/3}<1,
\]
where the last strict inequality uses \(n>1\).  Hence \(A=d/(n\ell)\leq B\), and \(L_{n,d}=n^{-2/3}\).

If \(n^{1/3}\ell<d<n\ell\), then \(d>n^{1/3}\), so \(B=n^{-2/3}\), while the upper inequality gives \(d/(n\ell)<1\), so \(A=d/(n\ell)\).  The lower inequality gives
\[
 A=\frac{d}{n\ell}>\frac{n^{1/3}\ell}{n\ell}=n^{-2/3}=B.
\]
Consequently \(L_{n,d}=d/[n\log(ed)]\).

Finally, if \(d\geq n\ell\), then \(d/(n\ell)\geq1\), so \(A=1\).  Since \(B\leq1\), it follows that \(L_{n,d}=1\).  These regimes are exhaustive because \(\ell>1\) and \(n>1\) imply
\[
 n^{1/3}<n^{1/3}\ell<n\ell.
\]
At \(d=n^{1/3}\), one has \(d/n=n^{-2/3}\); at \(d=n^{1/3}\ell\), one has \(d/(n\ell)=n^{-2/3}\); and at \(d=n\ell\), one has \(d/(n\ell)=1\).  Thus adjacent formulas coincide at all shared boundaries.

Because \(n,d>0\), cubing preserves order, and \(d\leq n^{1/3}\) is equivalent to \(d^3\leq n\).  On this first branch, \(L_{n,d}=d/n\) by the calculation above, while \(\mathrm{thm:dense\mbox{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
 c_\epsilon\frac dn\leq R_{n,d,\epsilon}\leq C_\epsilon\frac dn.
\]
Substitution of \(L_{n,d}=d/n\) proves the final assertion.

 c_\epsilon L_{n,d}\leq R_{n,d,\epsilon}
