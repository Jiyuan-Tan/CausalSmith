Fix \(L\geq 3\), \((\mu_0,\mu_1)\in\Delta_L^2\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), and put \(c=1-\alpha\).  For all nonempty \(A,B\subseteq X\),
\[
 |A-B|\geq |A|+|B|-1. \tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 k^*=1\Longrightarrow I^*=1,
 \qquad
 k^*=2\Longrightarrow I^*\leq L,
 \qquad
 I^*\leq 2L-1. \tag{11}
\]
If \(\alpha\geq 1/2\), then additionally
\[
 I^*\leq L+\lceil L/2\rceil-1. \tag{12}
\]
These conclusions allow zero marginal cells and equality at a rectangle-feasibility threshold.
%%% FIELD interval-hull-regime-bounds-proof
Write \(A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_r\}\) and \(B=\{b_1<\cdots<b_s\}\).  The list
\[
 a_1-b_s<a_2-b_s<\cdots<a_r-b_s<a_r-b_{s-1}<\cdots<a_r-b_1
\]
contains \(r+s-1\) distinct elements of \(A-B\).  This proves (10) directly from the order on the integer support; neither positivity nor an external inverse-sumset theorem is used.

If \(k^*=1\), the definition of \(k^*\) supplies a valid singleton, and every singleton is an effect interval.  Hence the definition of \(I^*\) gives \(I^*=1\).  If \(k^*=2\), the all-minimizers theorem supplies a threshold-feasible rectangle \((A,B)\in\mathcal F_\tau\) with \(|A-B|=2\).  By (10), \(|A|+|B|\leq3\).  Since \(A\) and \(B\) are nonempty and two singleton sets would have a singleton difference, one of \(A,B\) is a singleton and the other has two elements.  Consequently the two elements of \(A-B\) have span at most \(L-1\).  Its integer hull is therefore an interval of cardinality at most \(L\).  That hull contains the same threshold-feasible difference set, so it is valid by the all-minimizers theorem's validity equivalence.  Thus \(I^*\leq L\).

The full effect support \(E\) is an interval of cardinality \(2L-1\), and every coupling assigns mass one to it.  Because \(c<1\), it is valid, proving the last assertion of (11).

Finally suppose \(\alpha\geq1/2\), equivalently \(c\leq1/2\).  Partition \(X\) into two nonempty consecutive blocks, each of cardinality at most \(\lceil L/2\rceil\).  Since their \(\mu_0\)-masses sum to one, one block, call it \(B=[u,v]\cap\mathbb Z\), has \(\mu_0(B)\geq1/2\geq c\).  Take \(A=X\).  Then
\[
 \mu_1(A)+\mu_0(B)\geq1+c=\tau,
\]
so \((A,B)\in\mathcal F_\tau\).  Also
\[
 X-B=\{-v,-v+1,\ldots,L-1-u\},
 \qquad
 |X-B|=L+|B|-1\leq L+\lceil L/2\rceil-1.
\]
The all-minimizers theorem's validity equivalence makes this difference interval valid and proves (12).
%%% FIELD equality-regimes-resolved-statement
Fix \(L\geq3\), \((\mu_0,\mu_1)\in\Delta_L^2\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), and put \(\tau=2-\alpha\).  The following five assertions are equivalent:
\[
 \frac{I^*}{k^*}=\frac{2L-1}{3}; \tag{13}
\]
\[
 I^*-k^*=2L-4; \tag{14}
\]
\[
 0<\alpha<\frac12,\qquad k^*=3,\qquad I^*=2L-1; \tag{15}
\]
\[
 0<\alpha<\frac12,\qquad R_2<\tau\leq R_3,
 \qquad B_{2L-2}<\tau; \tag{16}
\]
and
\[
 0<\alpha<\frac12,\qquad R_2<\tau\leq R_3,
 \qquad
 \min\{\mu_{1,0},\mu_{1,L-1},\mu_{0,0},\mu_{0,L-1}\}>\alpha. \tag{17}
\]
Thus (16), or equivalently (17), characterizes all marginal laws and coverage levels attaining either the high-coverage multiplicative constant or the additive constant, including boundary marginal laws and threshold equalities.

In the lower-coverage phase \(1/2\leq\alpha<1\), the following three assertions are equivalent:
\[
 \frac{I^*}{k^*}=\frac L2; \tag{18}
\]
\[
 k^*=2\qquad\text{and}\qquad I^*=L; \tag{19}
\]
and
\[
 R_1<\tau\leq R_2\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{L-1}<\tau. \tag{20}
\]
The system in (15) has strictly positive nonsymmetric solutions, with \(\mu_0\neq\mu_1\), if and only if \(0<\alpha<1/2\).  Hence simultaneous equality in (13) and (14) is possible outside the symmetric endpoint-heavy family even when every marginal cell is positive.  At \(\alpha=1/2\), (18) is attained by the symmetric law placing mass \(1/2\) at each endpoint.  For every \(1/2<\alpha<1\), it is attained by the strictly positive symmetric law with endpoint masses \((1-\varepsilon)/2\) and equal interior masses \(\varepsilon/(L-2)\), for every \(0<\varepsilon<(2\alpha-1)/3\).
%%% FIELD equality-regimes-resolved-proof
The sharp interval-price theorem identifies the two regime-specific multiplicative constants and the additive constant.  We derive their complete equality conditions using the frontier profiles and the interval-hull bounds.

First consider (13)--(17), without initially restricting \(\alpha\).  If \(k^*=1\), (11) gives \(I^*=1\), so neither (13) nor (14) holds.  If \(k^*=2\), (11) and \(L\geq3\) give
\[
 \frac{I^*}{k^*}\leq\frac L2<\frac{2L-1}{3},
 \qquad
 I^*-k^*\leq L-2<2L-4, \tag{21}
\]
where the strict ratio inequality follows from
\[
 \frac{2L-1}{3}-\frac L2=\frac{L-2}{6}>0.
\]
If \(k^*\geq3\), the last bound in (11) yields
\[
 \frac{I^*}{k^*}\leq\frac{2L-1}{k^*}\leq\frac{2L-1}{3},
 \qquad
 I^*-k^*\leq2L-1-k^*\leq2L-4. \tag{22}
\]
Equality in either chain in (22) holds if and only if both inequalities in that chain are equalities, namely if and only if \(k^*=3\) and \(I^*=2L-1\).  Together with (21), this proves
\[
 \text{(13)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{(14)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{k^*=3, I^*=2L-1\}. \tag{23}
\]

The general-frontier theorem states that a valid arbitrary set of cardinality at most \(k\) exists exactly when \(R_k\geq\tau\).  Since the feasible families defining \(R_k\) are nested in \(k\), it follows that
\[
 k^*=3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_2<\tau\leq R_3. \tag{24}
\]
The same theorem states that a valid interval of cardinality at most \(k\) exists exactly when \(B_k\geq\tau\).  The full interval \(E\) is valid by (11), so
\[
 I^*=2L-1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_{2L-2}<\tau. \tag{25}
\]
Equations (24)--(25) are the inverse Hall profile condition in (16), apart from the coverage-regime restriction that will now be derived rather than assumed.

Nonnegativity of the marginal masses makes each concentration profile \(Q_d(w)\) nondecreasing in \(w\): any consecutive block of width less than \(L\) can be enlarged by one adjacent support point.  If \(1\leq a,b\leq L\) and \(a+b\leq2L-1\), then either \(a\leq L-1\) or \(b\leq L-1\).  Hence the definition of \(B_{2L-2}\), with the two feasible choices \((a,b)=(L-1,L)\) and \((L,L-1)\), gives
\[
 B_{2L-2}=\max\{Q_1(L-1)+1,1+Q_0(L-1)\}. \tag{26}
\]
For either arm,
\[
 Q_d(L-1)
 =\max\{1-\mu_{d,0},1-\mu_{d,L-1}\}
 =1-\min\{\mu_{d,0},\mu_{d,L-1}\}. \tag{27}
\]
Substitution into (26) yields the computable identity
\[
 B_{2L-2}
 =2-\min\{\mu_{1,0},\mu_{1,L-1},\mu_{0,0},\mu_{0,L-1}\}. \tag{28}
\]
Since \(\tau=2-\alpha\), (28) proves that the last inequality in (16) is equivalent to the last inequality in (17).  It also shows that \(I^*=2L-1\) forces both endpoint masses in each arm to exceed \(\alpha\).  Their sum is at most one, so \(2\alpha<1\).  Thus the regime restriction in (15)--(17) is necessary.  Conversely, (24), (25), and (28) show that either (16) or (17) implies \(k^*=3\) and \(I^*=2L-1\).  Together with (23), this proves equivalence of (13)--(17), with the strict inequalities correctly excluding equality at a competing feasibility threshold.

We next show that the high-coverage equality system has positive nonsymmetric solutions throughout, and only throughout, \(0<\alpha<1/2\).  Necessity was just proved without using positivity or symmetry.  For sufficiency fix \(0<\alpha<1/2\), choose
\[
 0<\varepsilon<\min\!\left\{\frac\alpha4,\frac{1-2\alpha}{4}\right\},
 \qquad M=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},
 \qquad 0<\delta<M-\alpha,
\]
and put \(q=\mu_0\), \(p=\mu_1\), with
\[
 q_0=q_{L-1}=M,
 \qquad p_0=M+\delta,
 \qquad p_{L-1}=M-\delta,
\]
and
\[
 p_i=q_i=\frac{\varepsilon}{L-2},
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq L-2.
\]
Both vectors sum to one, all their cells are positive, \(p\neq q\), and all four endpoint masses exceed \(\alpha\).  Thus (28) gives \(B_{2L-2}<\tau\).  Taking \(A=B=\{0,L-1\}\) gives \(|A-B|=3\) and
\[
 p(A)+q(B)=2(1-\varepsilon)>2-\alpha=\tau,
\]
so \(R_3\geq\tau\).  To control \(R_2\), (10) shows that every pair entering its maximum satisfies \(|A|+|B|\leq3\).  The two largest atoms in each arm are its endpoints because
\[
 M-\delta>\alpha>\frac{\varepsilon}{L-2}.
\]
Therefore every such rectangle obeys
\[
 p(A)+q(B)
 \leq (M+\delta)+(1-\varepsilon)
 <(2M-\alpha)+(1-\varepsilon)
 =2-2\varepsilon-\alpha
 <\tau. \tag{29}
\]
Thus \(R_2<\tau\).  Condition (17) applies, proving both equalities for a strictly positive nonsymmetric pair.

It remains to prove the lower-coverage equivalences.  Suppose \(1/2\leq\alpha<1\).  For \(k^*=1\), (11) gives ratio one, strictly below \(L/2\).  For \(k^*=2\), (11) gives \(I^*/k^*\leq L/2\), with equality exactly when \(I^*=L\).  For \(k^*\geq3\), (12) gives
\[
 \frac{I^*}{k^*}
 \leq\frac{L+\lceil L/2\rceil-1}{3}
 <\frac L2, \tag{30}
\]
because \(2\lceil L/2\rceil-2<L\) for every integer \(L\geq3\).  Hence (18) and (19) are equivalent.  The arbitrary-set part of the general-frontier theorem gives
\[
 k^*=2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_1<\tau\leq R_2. \tag{31}
\]
Conditional on \(k^*=2\), (11) gives \(I^*\leq L\), while the interval part of the general-frontier theorem gives
\[
 I^*=L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_{L-1}<\tau. \tag{32}
\]
Equations (31)--(32) prove equivalence with (20).

For completeness, the claimed lower-coverage attainments can be checked directly from these same inequalities.  At \(\alpha=1/2\), let both marginals put mass \(1/2\) at each endpoint and zero elsewhere.  Then \(\tau=3/2\), \(R_1=1<\tau\), and the rectangle \(A=\{0,L-1\}\), \(B=\{0\}\) shows \(R_2\geq3/2\).  Every proper consecutive outcome block has marginal mass at most \(1/2\); hence \(B_{L-1}=1<\tau\), and (20) holds.

If \(1/2<\alpha<1\), choose \(0<\varepsilon<(2\alpha-1)/3\), put \(M=(1-\varepsilon)/2\), and give both marginals mass \(M\) at each endpoint and mass \(r=\varepsilon/(L-2)\) at each interior point.  Here \(M>r\).  Consequently, for \(1\leq w\leq L-1\),
\[
 Q_d(w)=M+(w-1)r.
\]
It follows that
\[
 B_{L-1}
 =\max_{a+b\leq L}\{2M+(a+b-2)r\}
 =2M+(L-2)r=1<\tau. \tag{33}
\]
Moreover \(R_1=2M=1-\varepsilon<\tau\), whereas the same endpoint-by-singleton rectangle satisfies
\[
 \mu_1(\{0,L-1\})+\mu_0(\{0\})
 =1-\varepsilon+M
 =\frac32(1-\varepsilon)>2-\alpha=\tau. \tag{34}
\]
The last strict inequality is exactly \(\varepsilon<(2\alpha-1)/3\).  Thus \(R_2\geq\tau\), so (20) holds and the positive symmetric laws attain (18).  All profile maxima used above are over finite families, so the characterization is directly computable and does not require positive cells, unique optimizers, or strict slack at an attained feasibility boundary.
