&\Pr\{Y(1)-Y(0)\in I\}\geq 1-\alpha

&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad

&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h_{\mu}(I)\geq c.
\end{split}
\]
Moreover, if \(a,b\in E\), \(a\leq b\), and \(I=\{e\in E:a\leq e\leq b\}\), then \(|I|=b-a+1\).  Hence minimizing interval endpoint length \(b-a\) is equivalent to minimizing interval cardinality, and \(I^*\) is exactly the shortest-interval completion of the displayed marginal-robust validity criterion.

Because \(X\) is finite, every compatible joint law is represented uniquely by the table
\[
\gamma_{ij}=\Pr\{Y(1)=i,Y(0)=j\},\qquad i,j\in X.
\]
Its nonnegativity and marginal identities are exactly the constraints in \(\Gamma(\mu_1,\mu_0)\) from \texttt{def:coupling-polytope}.  Conversely, every table in that polytope defines a compatible joint law.  The polytope is nonempty because the product table \(\gamma_{ij}=\mu_{1,i}\mu_{0,j}\) belongs to it.  For every \(\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu_1,\mu_0)\),
\[
\Pr_{\gamma}\{Y(1)-Y(0)\in I\}
=\sum_{i-j\in I}\gamma_{ij}.
\]
Therefore requiring the probability inequality for every compatible law is equivalent to requiring its infimum over \(\Gamma(\mu_1,\mu_0)\) to be at least \(1-\alpha\).  The attainment assertion in \texttt{lem:hall-coverage} identifies this infimum with the minimum in \texttt{def:worst-coverage}, namely \(h_{\mu}(I)\).  Since \(c=1-\alpha\), the first displayed equivalence follows, including at zero marginal cells.

For the last assertion, \(E=\{-L+1,\ldots,L-1\}\) contains every integer between any two of its elements.  Thus the integers in \(I=\{e\in E:a\leq e\leq b\}\) are precisely

\]
and their number is \(b-a+1\).  The objectives \(b-a\) and \(|I|\) differ by the constant one on every nonempty interval.  They therefore have the same minimizers.  Combining this fact with \texttt{def:interval-optimum} proves the claimed shortest-oracle interpretation.

\tag{1}

\tag{2}

We next prove the interval equivalence.  Suppose first that \(B_k\geq\tau\).  The maxima defining \(Q_1,Q_0\), and \(B_k\) are over finite nonempty sets. \[
\mu_1(A)+\mu_0(B)=Q_1(a)+Q_0(b)=B_k\geq\tau.
\tag{3}
\]
Direct subtraction of the two integer intervals gives
\[
A-B=\{\min A-\max B,\ldots,\max A-\min B\},
\]
which is an effect interval of cardinality
\[
|A-B|=(\max A-\min A+1)+(\max B-\min B+1)-1=a+b-1\leq k.
\tag{4}
\]
Equation \((3)\) makes \((A,B)\in\mathcal F_{\tau}\), so \texttt{thm:all-minimizers} makes \(A-B\) valid.

\[
\overline A=\{\min A,\min A+1,\ldots,\max A\},
\qquad
\overline B=\{\min B,\min B+1,\ldots,\max B\}.
\]
The endpoint differences \(\min A-\max B\) and \(\max A-\min B\) lie in \(A-B\subseteq I\).  Since \(I\) contains every integer between any two of its points,
\[
\overline A-\overline B
=\{\min A-\max B,\ldots,\max A-\min B\}
\subseteq I.
\tag{5}
\]

\[
a+b-1=|\overline A-\overline B|\leq |I|\leq k.
\tag{6}
\]
All marginal coordinates are nonnegative, and \(A\subseteq\overline A\), \(B\subseteq\overline B\).  By \texttt{def:concentration-profiles} and feasibility of \((A,B)\),
\[
Q_1(a)+Q_0(b)
\geq\mu_1(\overline A)+\mu_0(\overline B)
\geq\mu_1(A)+\mu_0(B)
\geq\tau.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations \((6)\)--\((7)\) show \(B_k\geq\tau\).  We have proved that a valid interval of cardinality at most \(k\) exists if and only if \(B_k\geq\tau\).

Both \(R_k\) and \(B_k\) are nondecreasing in \(k\), because increasing \(k\) only enlarges their finite feasible families.  The two equivalences just proved imply

\qquad
I^*=\min\{k:B_k\geq\tau\}.
\tag{8}
\]

\[
k^*<I^*
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists k\in\{1,\ldots,2L-1\}:B_k<\tau\leq R_k.
\tag{9}
\]

Use the usual nearest-neighbor adjacency on the integer set \(E\).  A nonempty subset of \(E\) is connected exactly when it is an interval.  If \(k^*<I^*\), no member of \(\mathcal K^*\) is an interval, because such a member would be a valid interval of cardinality \(k^*<I^*\).  Hence every member of \(\mathcal K^*\) is disconnected.  Conversely, if every member of \(\mathcal K^*\) is disconnected and \(k^*=I^*\), an interval attaining \(I^*\) would belong to \(\mathcal K^*\), a contradiction.  Therefore
\[
k^*<I^*
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every member of }\mathcal K^*\text{ is disconnected}.
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to justify the corresponding assertion about the deterministic selector in both directions.  By \texttt{def:deterministic-selector}, \(S^\star\in\mathcal K^*\), so the right-hand side of \((10)\) implies that \(S^\star\) is disconnected.  For the reverse implication, suppose \(S^\star\) is disconnected and some \(J\in\mathcal K^*\) is an interval.  Both sets have \(k^*\) points.  The interval has
\[
\operatorname{span}(J)=k^*-1.
\]
A disconnected \(k^*\)-point subset of the integers omits at least one integer between its minimum and maximum, so
\[
\operatorname{span}(S^\star)+1\geq k^*+1,
\qquad
\operatorname{span}(S^\star)\geq k^*.
\]
The cardinalities tie and \(J\) has strictly smaller span, hence \(J\prec S^\star\).  This contradicts the defining minimality of \(S^\star\) among valid sets.  Thus no such \(J\) exists, and every member of \(\mathcal K^*\) is disconnected.  Combining this conclusion with \((9)\)--\((10)\) proves every assertion, including the selector equivalence that requires the span tie-break.

\tag{9}
\]
the strict inequality excludes every valid interval having at most \(L-1\) points, while \(I^*\leq L\) supplies one having at most \(L\) points.  Equations \((8)\)--\((9)\) establish the three-way low-coverage equivalence.  All profile quantities are maxima over finite families, so these conditions are computable and remain valid at zero cells and exact threshold equalities.

It remains to establish the existence assertions.  For the high-coverage equality system, necessity follows from \((6)\): \(I^*=2L-1\) forces all four endpoint masses to exceed \(\alpha\).  In either arm the two endpoint masses sum to at most one, whence
\[
2\alpha<1.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus a solution is impossible unless \(0<\alpha<1/2\), even before imposing positivity or nonsymmetry.

Conversely, fix \(0<\alpha<1/2\) and choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\{\alpha/4,(1-2\alpha)/4\},
\qquad
M=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},
\qquad
0<\delta<M-\alpha.
\]
Define \(q=\mu_0\) and \(p=\mu_1\) by
\[
q_0=q_{L-1}=M,
\qquad
p_0=M+\delta,
\qquad
p_{L-1}=M-\delta,
\]
and, for \(1\leq i\leq L-2\), by
\[
p_i=q_i=\frac{\varepsilon}{L-2}.
\]
The vectors are normalized because their coordinates sum to \(2M+\varepsilon=1\), and all coordinates are strictly positive.  Also \(p\neq q\), so the marginal pair is nonsymmetric.  The bound on \(\delta\) gives \(M-\delta>\alpha\), and the bound on \(\varepsilon\) gives \(M>\alpha\); hence all four endpoint masses exceed \(\alpha\).  Equation \((6)\) yields \(B_{2L-2}<\tau\).  With \(A=B=\{0,L-1\}\),
\[
p(A)+q(B)=2(1-\varepsilon)>2-\alpha=\tau,
\]
because \(2\varepsilon<\alpha\).  Since \(|A-B|=3\), this proves \(R_3\geq\tau\).

For every pair entering \(R_2\), the integer sumset inequality from \texttt{thm:sharp-interval-price} gives
\[
|A-B|\leq2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|A|+|B|\leq3.
\tag{11}
\]
The two largest atoms of each marginal are its endpoints: indeed,
\[
M-\delta>\alpha>\frac{\varepsilon}{L-2}.
\]
Thus a rectangle satisfying \((11)\) has mass sum no larger than
\[
(M+\delta)+(1-\varepsilon)
<(2M-\alpha)+(1-\varepsilon)
=2-2\varepsilon-\alpha
<2-\alpha=\tau.
\tag{12}
\]
It follows that \(R_2<\tau\).  The high-coverage equivalence already proved now gives \(k^*=3\) and \(I^*=2L-1\), establishing strictly positive nonsymmetric solutions for every \(0<\alpha<1/2\).  Together with necessity \((10)\), this proves the ``exactly when'' assertion.

At \(\alpha=1/2\), the symmetric two-endpoint half--half law was verified in \texttt{thm:sharp-interval-price} to have \(k^*=2\) and \(I^*=L\).  For every \(1/2<\alpha<1\), that theorem likewise verifies the stated strictly positive symmetric perturbations and the same equalities.  These are precisely the remaining attainment claims.
