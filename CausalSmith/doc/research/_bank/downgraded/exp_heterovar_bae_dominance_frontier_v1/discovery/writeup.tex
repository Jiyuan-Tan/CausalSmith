\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}heterovar\_\allowbreak{}bae\_\allowbreak{}dominance\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For every \(K\ge3\) and every known finite positive Gaussian variance vector, a fully specified proportional two-batch rule can retain any top \(m\in\{2,\ldots,K-1\}\) first-batch arms and reallocate the remaining budget proportionally among them. The exact Gaussian exponent is a finite minimum over \(\binom{K-1}{m}\) best-exclusion and \((m-1)\binom{K-1}{m-1}\) terminal branches. Weighted projections and separated matching witnesses give \(L_m(\rho)=\min\{\rho C_m,1+(1-\rho)\eta_{K-m}\}\), the unique timing \(\rho_m^\star=(1+\eta_{K-m})/(C_m+\eta_{K-m})\), and gain \(G_m>1\). Enumerating \(m\) yields the certified design \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}=\max_m G_m\); in the common-variance subclass its optimal survivor count is of order \(\sqrt K\) and the gain tends to two. The connection to Imbens, Qin, and Wager is the batched-arm-elimination motif; literal distributional and comparator overlap is the common-variance subclass.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The closest design-motif result, Imbens, Qin, and Wager's common-variance batched arm-elimination theorem, gives sufficient conditions for strict large-deviation dominance over uniform allocation. It does not give the heteroskedastic survivor-set contraction, exact terminal-branch equality, optimal retained-arm count, or dominance over Kato's variance-aware equal-gap maximin allocation.

\paragraph{Niche.} The proportional top-\(m\) family keeps the BAE motif while treating the number of survivors, the first-batch fraction, and known arm-specific variances as explicit design inputs. Literal overlap with the Imbens--Qin--Wager setting is confined to equal variances.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the exact survivor-set Gaussian branch formula, reduces every best-exclusion coefficient to at most \(K\) one-dimensional quadratic pieces, constructs matching elimination and terminal witnesses, optimizes timing for each \(m\), and then performs the finite discrete optimization over survivor counts.

\section{Related work}

Imbens, Qin, and Wager are the closest design-motif comparator: under a common Gaussian variance, their batched arm-elimination result gives sufficient conditions for strict large-deviation dominance over uniform allocation. The present paper literally overlaps that setting only in its common-variance subclass; beyond it, arm-specific variances alter both the baseline allocation and every survivor-set projection. Kato derives the generalized Neyman static comparator from a worst equal-gap Gaussian experiment, without asserting pointwise admissibility away from equal gaps. The theorem here strictly improves that variance-aware comparator, derives exact coupled branch exponents, and optimizes the number of survivors. Wang, Tzeng, and Proutiere provide an adaptive large-deviation upper-bound technique, while their completeness caveat leaves room for the finite-branch equality proved here. Lalitha et al. show how known heterogeneous variances alter elimination allocations and provide finite-budget bounds; their stagewise analysis informs, but does not imply, the survivor-set certificate. Goldberger's impossibility for matching a stronger mean-aware oracle is consistent with this paper because \(w\) depends only on variances. Jin et al. optimize a different fixed-confidence batched criterion, whereas this paper solves an exact fixed-budget robust-gain game in the proportional two-batch class.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak D\_\allowbreak{}2\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{all,\allowbreak{}multi\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}=\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}bigcup\_\allowbreak{}\{m=\allowbreak{}2\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{K-\allowbreak{}1\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}\{D(\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho,\allowbreak{}a,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}\{b\_\allowbreak{}J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal J\_\allowbreak{}m\}\allowbreak{}):\allowbreak{}0<\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho<1,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{} a\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal W,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{} b\_\allowbreak{}J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}operatorname\{int\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Sigma\_\allowbreak{}J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ for every \}\allowbreak{}J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal J\_\allowbreak{}m\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{,\allowbreak{} where batch one uses \}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho a\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{,\allowbreak{} retains its top \}\allowbreak{}m\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ empirical arms,\allowbreak{} and branch \}\allowbreak{}J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ uses \}\allowbreak{}(1-\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho)b\_\allowbreak{}J\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ over its survivors\}\allowbreak{}}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-marginals]\label{ass:gaussian-marginals}
For every \(i\in\mathcal A\) and \(t\in\mathbb N\), \(Y_{i,t}\sim\mathcal N(\mu_i,s_i)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known-variance heteroskedastic Gaussian arm model, \cite{Kato2023WorstCaseGaussian}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-streams]\label{ass:independent-streams}
The variables \(\{Y_{i,t}:i\in\mathcal A,\ t\in\mathbb N\}\) are mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent arm-reward streams, \cite{WangTzengProutiere2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-variances]\label{ass:known-variances}
The vector \(s\) is known before treatment allocation begins.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known heterogeneous reward variances, \cite{LalithaKalantariMaDeorasKveton2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:unique-best]\label{ass:unique-best}
The set \(\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal A}\mu_i\) is the singleton \(\{h\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unique best arm, \cite{Qin2022OpenProblem}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:gaussian-model]\label{def:gaussian-model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Heteroskedastic Gaussian best-\allowbreak{}arm model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal M\_\allowbreak{}K=\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}\{(\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu,\allowbreak{}s,\allowbreak{}Y):\allowbreak{}s\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in(0,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}infty)\textasciicircum{}K,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{} \textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathbb R\textasciicircum{}K,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{} Y\_\allowbreak{}\{i,\allowbreak{}t\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}sim\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal N(\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}i,\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}i)\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ independently for every \}\allowbreak{}i\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in[K],\allowbreak{}t\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathbb N,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ and \}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}arg\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}max\_\allowbreak{}\{i\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}in[K]\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}i\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}text\{ is a singleton\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}.}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}gaussian-\allowbreak{}marginals}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}independent-\allowbreak{}streams}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}unique-\allowbreak{}best}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:static-comparator]\label{def:static-comparator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Variance-\allowbreak{}aware static equal-\allowbreak{}gap comparator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Choose \(w=w^{\mathrm{GNA}}(s)\) by the lexicographically fixed maximin program in the symbol table, assign integer observations by largest-remainder rounding of \(Tw_i\), and deploy the arm with the largest sample mean.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{s}; \texttt{T}; \texttt{Y}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:proportional-design]\label{def:proportional-design}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Rounded proportional top-\(m\) two-batch design
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Given \(m\in\{2,\ldots,K-1\}\), \(T\), \(s\), \(w\), and \(0<\rho<1\), the design \(\mathsf D_{\rho,w,m}\) assigns the first-batch observations by deterministic largest-remainder rounding of \(T\rho w_i\), retains the set \(J\in\mathcal J_m:=\{J\subseteq\mathcal A:|J|=m\}\) of the \(m\) largest first-batch sample means using arm-index tie breaking, and assigns all remaining observations among \(i\in J\) by deterministic largest-remainder rounding of \((1-\rho)T w_i/W_J\), where \(W_J=\sum_{k\in J}w_k\). It deploys the survivor with the largest aggregate mean from both batches, again using arm-index tie breaking. Write \(r_i(J;\rho)=(1-\rho)w_i/W_J\) for \(i\in J\), \(r_i(J;\rho)=0\) for \(i\notin J\), and \(n_i(J;\rho)=\rho w_i+r_i(J;\rho)\). The original eliminate-one design is the specialization \(\mathsf D_{\rho,w}=\mathsf D_{\rho,w,K-1}\), with \(J=\mathcal A\setminus\{e\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}; \texttt{s}; \texttt{w}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho}; \texttt{m}; \texttt{Y}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:branch-programs]\label{def:branch-programs}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite Gaussian survivor-\allowbreak{}set branch programs}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(J\in\mathcal J_m\), define
\[
 \mathcal B_J=\{x\in\mathbb R^K:x_a\ge x_b\text{ for every }a\in J,\ b\notin J\},
\]
\[
 R_J(x,z;\mu)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\frac{\rho w_i(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}
 +\sum_{i\in J}\frac{r_i(J;\rho)(z_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i},
 \qquad
 \bar m_i(J;x,z)=\frac{\rho w_i x_i+r_i(J;\rho)z_i}{n_i(J;\rho)}\quad(i\in J).
\]
For \(J\in\mathcal J_m\) with \(h\notin J\), set
\[
 Q_J^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu)=\inf_{x\in\mathcal B_J}
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\frac{\rho w_i(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}.
\]
For \(J\in\mathcal J_m\) with \(h\in J\) and \(j\in J\setminus\{h\}\), set
\[
 Q_{J,j}^{\mathrm T}(\rho,\mu)=
 \inf\{R_J(x,z;\mu):x\in\mathcal B_J,\ \bar m_j(J;x,z)\ge \bar m_h(J;x,z)\}.
\]
When \(m=K-1\) and \(J=\mathcal A\setminus\{e\}\), these objects are respectively the original cone \(\mathcal B_e\), rate \(R_e\), aggregate means \(m_i(e)\), and branch programs \(Q_h^{\mathrm E}\) or \(Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{m}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu}; \texttt{s}; \texttt{w}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:projection-coefficient]\label{def:projection-coefficient}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Weighted top-\(m\) exclusion projection coefficients
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(m\in\{2,\ldots,K-1\}\), \(h\in\mathcal A\), and \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\setminus\{h\}\) with \(|S|=m\), define
\[
 F_{h,S}(t)=\frac{t^2}{2v_h}+\sum_{j\in S}\frac{(d_{hj}-t)_+^2}{2v_j},
 \qquad C_{h,S}(v)=\min_{t\ge0}F_{h,S}(t),
\]
and
\[
 C_m(v)=\min_{h\in\mathcal A}\ \min_{\substack{S\subseteq\mathcal A\setminus\{h\}\\|S|=m}}C_{h,S}(v).
\]
Each \(C_{h,S}\) is computed by sorting the \(m\) breakpoints \(d_{hj}\), evaluating on every induced interval the stationary point
\[
 t_A=\frac{\sum_{j\in A}d_{hj}/v_j}{1/v_h+\sum_{j\in A}1/v_j},
\]
clipped to that interval, and taking the smallest value. Thus at most \(m+1\le K\) one-dimensional quadratic pieces are required. The original coefficient satisfies \(C_h=C_{h,\mathcal A\setminus\{h\}}\) and \(C=C_{K-1}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{v}; \texttt{m}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:robust-schedule]\label{def:robust-schedule}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Balanced top-\(m\) proportional schedules and multi-survivor gain
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(\ell=K-m\), let
\[
 \zeta_\ell(w)=\min_{\substack{E\subseteq\mathcal A\\|E|=\ell}}\sum_{e\in E}w_e
\]
be the sum of the \(\ell\) smallest coordinates of \(w\), and set
\[
 \eta_\ell=\frac{\zeta_\ell}{1-\zeta_\ell},\qquad
 \rho_m^\star=\frac{1+\eta_\ell}{C_m+\eta_\ell},\qquad
 G_m=\frac{C_m(1+\eta_\ell)}{C_m+\eta_\ell}.
\]
Finally define \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}=\max_{2\le m\le K-1}G_m\) and let \(m^\star\) be the smallest maximizer. For \(m=K-1\), one has \(\ell=1\), \(\eta_1=\eta\), \(\rho_{K-1}^\star=\rho^\star\), and \(G_{K-1}=G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{w}; \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}m}; \texttt{m}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-certificate]\label{def:finite-certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Rounded survivor-\allowbreak{}set branchwise Chernoff certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each retained set \(J\), replace the proportions in the corresponding programs by the exact largest-remainder fractions \(N_{i,T}^{(1)}/T\) and \(N_{i,T}^{(2)}(J)/T\), and denote the resulting values by \(Q_{T,J}^{\mathrm E}\) and \(Q_{T,J,j}^{\mathrm T}\). Define
\[
 B_{T,m}=\sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J_m\\h\notin J}}e^{-TQ_{T,J}^{\mathrm E}}
 +\sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J_m\\h\in J}}\ \sum_{j\in J\setminus\{h\}}e^{-TQ_{T,J,j}^{\mathrm T}}.
\]
At \(m=K-1\), this is the original certificate \(B_T\), after writing the unique omitted arm as \(e\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}; \texttt{m}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu}; \texttt{s}; \texttt{w}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:full-class-handle]\label{def:full-class-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Branch-\allowbreak{}dual saddle handle for unrestricted multi-\allowbreak{}survivor two-\allowbreak{}batch designs}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(m\in\{2,\ldots,K-1\}\), parameterize an unrestricted two-batch top-\(m\) design by \(0<\rho<1\), an interior first-batch allocation \(a\in\mathcal W\), and a branch-specific allocation \(b_J\in\operatorname{int}\Sigma_J\) for every \(J\in\mathcal J_m\).  Thus \(D=D(\rho,a,\{b_J\}_{J\in\mathcal J_m})\in\mathfrak D_2^{\mathrm{all,multi}}\).  For a fixed tuple, replace \(q_i(\rho)\) by \(\rho a_i\), \(r_i(J;\rho)\) by \((1-\rho)b_{J,i}\), and \(n_i(J;\rho)\) by \(\rho a_i+(1-\rho)b_{J,i}\) in the survivor-set programs of \(\text{def:branch-programs}\), whose Gaussian objectives depend on the known variance vector \(s\).  This produces, for each best-exclusion or terminal branch, an explicit convex quadratic objective subject to linear inequalities.  The full-class handle jointly optimizes the resulting finite branch minimum over the discrete choice of \(m\), over \(\rho\), \(a\), and \(\{b_J\}\), and against all \(\mu\) having a unique best arm.  Its proposed optimality certificate consists of primal branch witnesses and feasible Lagrange multipliers for the branch polyhedra that give matching bounds on the robust ratio to \(I_w(\mu)\).  Neither a minimax interchange nor the value \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}\) is imposed by this definition.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{s}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:static-exponent]\label{lem:static-exponent}
Under the variance-aware static comparator, \[\lim_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log\mathbb P_{\mu,s}^{\mathrm{stat}}(\widehat h_T^{\mathrm{stat}}\ne h)=I_w(\mu)=\min_{j\ne h}\frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(v_h+v_j)}.\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(n_{i,T}\) be the integer count assigned to arm \(i\) by \(\text{def:static-comparator}\).  Largest-remainder rounding gives
\[
 \frac{n_{i,T}}{T}\longrightarrow w_i>0.
\]
Under \(\text{ass:gaussian-marginals}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-streams}\), for every \(j\ne h\),
\[
 D_{j,T}:=\overline Y_{j,T}-\overline Y_{h,T}
 \sim \mathcal N\!\left(-\Delta_j,\frac{s_j}{n_{j,T}}+\frac{s_h}{n_{h,T}}\right).
\]
Put \(a_{j,T}=T(s_j/n_{j,T}+s_h/n_{h,T})\).  Then \(a_{j,T}\to v_j+v_h\).  If \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), the Chernoff calculation
\[
 \mathbb P(Z\ge x)\le \inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda x+\lambda^2/2}=e^{-x^2/2}
\]
and, for \(x\ge1\), integration of the Gaussian density on \([x,x+x^{-1}]\), give
\[
 \frac{e^{-(x+x^{-1})^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,x}
 \le \mathbb P(Z\ge x)\le e^{-x^2/2}.
\]
Taking \(x=\sqrt T\,\Delta_j/\sqrt{a_{j,T}}\) therefore yields
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}-\frac1T\log\mathbb P(D_{j,T}\ge0)
 =\frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(v_h+v_j)}.
\]
The positivity \(\Delta_j>0\) used here is exactly \(\text{ass:unique-best}\).  Gaussian ties have probability zero.  Hence the static empirical-best error event is the finite union of the events \(\{D_{j,T}>0\}\), and
\[
 \max_{j\ne h}\mathbb P(D_{j,T}>0)
 \le \mathbb P_{\mu,s}^{\mathrm{stat}}(\widehat h_T^{\mathrm{stat}}\ne h)
 \le \sum_{j\ne h}\mathbb P(D_{j,T}>0).
\]
Taking logarithms, dividing by \(T\), and using that the number \(K-1\) of terms is fixed proves
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log\mathbb P_{\mu,s}^{\mathrm{stat}}(\widehat h_T^{\mathrm{stat}}\ne h)
 =\min_{j\ne h}\frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(v_h+v_j)}=I_w(\mu).
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This pins the exact variance-aware benchmark rather than an assumed stand-in. \textit{Gap.} Kato's GNA-EBA theorem uses this pairwise Gaussian benchmark in the equal-gap maximin regime; the lemma records its full instancewise value for the selected \(w\). \textit{Consumer.} The static exponent is the denominator used by experiment designers to report the adaptive gain for each fundraising-treatment mean vector.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-branch-exponent]\label{thm:finite-branch-exponent}
For every \(0<\rho<1\), the limit defining \(\Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu)\) exists and equals the finite branch minimum \[\Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu)=\min\left\{Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu),\ \min_{e\ne h}\min_{j\ne e,h}Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}(\rho,\mu)\right\}.\] Each infimum over a closed branch polyhedron equals the infimum over its strict-interior error event, and replacing ideal proportions by the largest-remainder counts \(N_T\) leaves this limit unchanged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the finite-dimensional Gaussian fact used for every branch. Fix a positive integer \(d\), positive numbers \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_d\), and independent coordinates
\[
 X_{\ell,T}\sim\mathcal N\!\left(m_\ell,\frac{\tau_\ell}{T\alpha_{\ell,T}}\right),
 \qquad \alpha_{\ell,T}\longrightarrow\alpha_\ell>0.
\]
Define
\[
 J_T(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^d\frac{\alpha_{\ell,T}(x_\ell-m_\ell)^2}{2\tau_\ell},
 \qquad
 J(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^d\frac{\alpha_\ell(x_\ell-m_\ell)^2}{2\tau_\ell}.
\]
Let \(P_T\) be a closed polyhedron whose finitely many affine defining coefficients converge to those of a closed polyhedron \(P\), and suppose that \(P\) has a strictly feasible point. Then
\[
 \inf_{x\in P_T}J_T(x)\longrightarrow\inf_{x\in P}J(x). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, positivity of the limiting \(\alpha_\ell\)'s makes the \(J_T\)'s uniformly coercive for all sufficiently large \(T\), so their minimizers over the nonempty \(P_T\)'s are bounded. Every convergent subsequence of such minimizers has a limit in \(P\), by coefficientwise convergence of the defining inequalities, and continuity gives the lower-limit inequality in (1). Conversely, mix an arbitrary \(x\in P\) with a fixed strictly feasible point of \(P\). The mixture is strictly feasible, converges to \(x\) as the mixing weight tends to zero, and, for fixed positive mixing weight, belongs to \(P_T\) for all sufficiently large \(T\). Continuity of \(J_T\) on bounded sets gives the upper-limit inequality and proves (1).

This argument also yields the exact logarithmic probability. After centering and whitening, the image \(A_T\) of \(P_T\) is a closed convex set and the squared distance from the origin to \(A_T\) is
\[
 \lVert p_T\rVert^2=2T\inf_{x\in P_T}J_T(x),
\]
where \(p_T\) is the Euclidean projection of the origin onto \(A_T\). The projection inequality gives \(p_T^{\mathsf T}z\geq\lVert p_T\rVert^2\) for every \(z\in A_T\). Thus, for a standard Gaussian vector \(Z\), the exponential Markov inequality, optimized over its scalar parameter, gives
\[
 \mathbb P(X_T\in P_T)
 \leq\mathbb P\!\left(p_T^{\mathsf T}Z\geq\lVert p_T\rVert^2\right)
 \leq \exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lVert p_T\rVert^2}{2}\right\}
 =\exp\!\left\{-T\inf_{x\in P_T}J_T(x)\right\}. \tag{2}
\]
The same conclusion is immediate when \(p_T=0\). For the reverse logarithmic inequality, choose a strictly feasible \(y\in P\) with \(J(y)\leq\inf_PJ+\varepsilon\); such a point is obtained by mixing a minimizer with a strictly feasible point. A sufficiently small fixed ball about \(y\) belongs to \(P_T\) for every sufficiently large \(T\). Integration of the product Gaussian density over that ball, coefficientwise convergence \(J_T\to J\), and the fact that the logarithm of the normalizing factor is \(O(\log T)=o(T)\) imply
\[
 \limsup_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log
 \mathbb P(X_T\in\operatorname{int}P_T)
 \leq \inf_{x\in P}J(x)+2\varepsilon.
\]
Together with (1)--(2), and then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), this proves
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log
 \mathbb P(X_T\in\operatorname{int}P_T)
 =\inf_{x\in P}J(x). \tag{3}
\]
The mixing argument also proves that the infimum over \(P\) equals the infimum over its strict interior.

We apply (3) to the design in \(\text{def:proportional-design}\). By \(\text{ass:independent-streams}\), pre-sampling the reward streams allows the first-batch means \(x_i\) and, on a putative branch \(e\), the fresh second-batch means \(z_i\), \(i\ne e\), to be considered jointly without conditioning on the elimination event. By \(\text{ass:gaussian-marginals}\), their product Gaussian density has speed-\(T\) quadratic rate
\[
 R_{T,e}(x,z;\mu)
 =\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{(N_{i,T}^{(1)}/T)(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}
 +\sum_{i\ne e}
 \frac{(N_{i,T}^{(2)}(e)/T)(z_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}.
\]
Largest-remainder rounding has bounded coordinate error. Hence
\[
 \frac{N_{i,T}^{(1)}}T\longrightarrow q_i(\rho),
 \qquad
 \frac{N_{i,T}^{(2)}(e)}T\longrightarrow r_i(e;\rho).
\]
Because \(0<\rho<1\), \(s_i>0\), and \(w_i>0\), every coefficient attached to an included branch coordinate has a positive limit. Thus \(R_{T,e}\) converges coefficientwise, and uniformly on compact sets, to \(R_e\).

All Gaussian tie events have probability zero. Therefore arm \(e\) is eliminated precisely, up to a null set, on the strict cone \(x_e<x_i\) for all \(i\ne e\). If \(e=h\), this event forces an erroneous deployment. Applying (3) to its closed cone \(\mathcal B_h\), while integrating out unused second-batch coordinates, gives exponent
\[
 Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu)
 =\inf_{x\in\mathcal B_h}
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\frac{q_i(\rho)(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}.
\]
If \(e\ne h\), a wrong deployment occurs precisely when at least one \(j\ne e,h\) satisfies \(m_j(e;x,z)>m_h(e;x,z)\), again up to a null tie event. For fixed \((e,j)\), adjoining the weak version of this inequality to \(x\in\mathcal B_e\) gives exactly the terminal closed polyhedron in \(\text{def:branch-programs}\), and (3) gives exponent \(Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}(\rho,\mu)\). Every branch polyhedron has a strictly feasible point: place \(x_e\) strictly below all other first-batch coordinates and, for a terminal branch, increase \(z_j\) until the terminal inequality is strict. Consequently its closed and strict-interior infima agree.

The error event is the union of the best-arm-elimination event and the \((K-1)(K-2)\) terminal events. For any fixed finite family \((A_{\ell,T})_{\ell=1}^L\),
\[
 \max_{\ell\leq L}\mathbb P(A_{\ell,T})
 \leq \mathbb P\!\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L A_{\ell,T}\right)
 \leq \sum_{\ell=1}^L\mathbb P(A_{\ell,T}).
\]
Equation (3) therefore makes the exponent of this finite union the minimum of its branch exponents. The defining limit exists and equals
\[
 \Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu)
 =\min\left\{Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu),
 \min_{e\ne h}\min_{j\ne e,h}Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}(\rho,\mu)\right\}.
\]
Finally, the equicoercivity and strict-feasibility argument proving (1), now applied to the rounded allocation and aggregate-mean coefficients, shows that every rounded branch value converges to its ideal-proportion value. Hence deterministic largest-remainder rounding leaves the displayed limit unchanged.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact equality, rather than a one-sided concentration exponent, is necessary for an optimality frontier. \textit{Gap.} Imbens, Qin, and Wager are the closest same-design comparator: under a common Gaussian variance, their batched arm-elimination result gives sufficient conditions for strict large-deviation dominance over uniform allocation, but it does not establish a heteroskedastic exact branch formula. The first changed steps are the weighted Gaussian contraction of each elimination branch and exact equality for the coupled terminal branches that reuse first-batch observations, including strict-interior attainment and rounding invariance. \textit{Consumer.} The equality gives ranking-and-selection software a finite list of convex programs that exactly diagnose whether elimination or final comparison drives asymptotic error.

\begin{lemma}[lem:weighted-projection]\label{lem:weighted-projection}
For each \(h\in\mathcal A\), \(C_h(v)>1\), and \[Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu)\ge\rho C_h(v)I_w(\mu).\] Equality holds at the normalized witness \(\mu_h=0\) and \(\mu_j=-d_{h j}\) for all \(j\ne h\). Moreover, the active-set construction in \(\text{def:projection-coefficient}\) evaluates \(C_h\) after at most \(K\) one-dimensional quadratic pieces.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since \(q_i(\rho)=\rho w_i\) and \(v_i=s_i/w_i\), the best-elimination program in \(\text{def:branch-programs}\) factors as
\[
 Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu)=\rho E_h(\mu),\qquad
 E_h(\mu):=\inf_{x_h\leq x_j\ (j\ne h)}
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\frac{(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2v_i}. \tag{4}
\]
Translate the means and optimization coordinates by \(-\mu_h\). By \(\text{ass:unique-best}\), this gives \(\mu_h=0\) and \(\mu_j=-\Delta_j\) with \(\Delta_j>0\). Fixing \(x_h=-t\), the projection of \(-\Delta_j\) onto \([-t,\infty)\) is \(x_j=\max\{-\Delta_j,-t\}\). Thus the objective after optimizing the other coordinates is
\[
 \frac{t^2}{2v_h}+\sum_{j\ne h}\frac{(\Delta_j-t)_+^2}{2v_j}. \tag{5}
\]
For \(t<0\), all positive-part terms are active and the derivative of (5) is
\[
 \frac{t}{v_h}+\sum_{j\ne h}\frac{t-\Delta_j}{v_j}<0.
\]
Hence no minimizer has \(t<0\), and (4)--(5) give an exact one-dimensional representation over \(t\geq0\).

Let \(I=I_w(\mu)>0\), with its value supplied by \(\text{lem:static-exponent}\). Its definition implies
\[
 \Delta_j\geq \sqrt I\,d_{hj}\qquad(j\ne h).
\]
Substitute \(t=\sqrt I\,u\) in (5) and use the monotonicity of \((a-u)_+^2\) in \(a\). Then
\[
 E_h(\mu)
 \geq I\min_{u\geq0}\left\{\frac{u^2}{2v_h}
 +\sum_{j\ne h}\frac{(d_{hj}-u)_+^2}{2v_j}\right\}
 =I C_h(v).
\]
Together with (4), this proves
\[
 Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu)\geq\rho C_h(v)I_w(\mu).
\]
For the stated witness, set \(\mu_h=0\) and \(\mu_j=-d_{hj}\) for \(j\ne h\). Every pairwise static exponent then equals one, so \(I_w(\mu)=1\), and (5) is exactly \(F_h\). Equality follows.

It remains to prove strictness and the computation claim. Fix \(j\ne h\) and put
\[
 f_j(t)=\frac{t^2}{2v_h}+\frac{(d_{hj}-t)_+^2}{2v_j}.
\]
On \([0,d_{hj}]\), its unique minimizer and minimum are
\[
 t_j=\frac{v_h}{v_h+v_j}d_{hj},
 \qquad
 f_j(t_j)=\frac{d_{hj}^2}{2(v_h+v_j)}=1,
\]
and on \([d_{hj},\infty)\) its value is strictly larger than one. Thus \(F_h(t)\geq f_j(t)\geq1\). The continuous coercive function \(F_h\) attains its minimum. If that minimum were one, its minimizer would have to be \(t_j\), and every remaining nonnegative summand would have to vanish. Since \(K\geq3\), choose \(k\ne h,j\). But
\[
 t_j=\frac{\sqrt2\,v_h}{\sqrt{v_h+v_j}}
 <\sqrt{2v_h}<\sqrt{2(v_h+v_k)}=d_{hk},
\]
so the \(k\)-summand is strictly positive. This contradiction proves \(C_h(v)>1\).

Finally, sort the \(K-1\) breakpoints \(d_{hj}\). On each of the resulting at most \(K\) intervals the active set \(A=\{j:d_{hj}>t\}\) is constant, and the derivative is
\[
 \frac{t}{v_h}+\sum_{j\in A}\frac{t-d_{hj}}{v_j}.
\]
Its zero is
\[
 t_A=\frac{\sum_{j\in A}d_{hj}/v_j}
 {1/v_h+\sum_{j\in A}1/v_j}.
\]
Convexity makes the minimizer on that interval the clipping of \(t_A\) to the interval. Evaluating these at most \(K\) candidates and taking the smallest value computes \(C_h\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The strict weighted projection coefficient is the source of improvement for every positive variance profile. \textit{Gap.} Imbens, Qin, and Wager obtain the common-variance coefficient \((K-1)/K\) inside their information lower bound; the heteroskedastic coefficient and its exact equality witness are new. \textit{Consumer.} The one-dimensional computation lets an experimental platform calculate a certified gain from its arm-specific historical variances.

\begin{theorem}[thm:universal-dominance]\label{thm:universal-dominance}
For every positive finite variance vector \(s\) and every unique-best \(\mu\), one has \(1/C<\rho^\star<1\), and the design \(\mathsf D_{\rho^\star,w}\) satisfies \[\Gamma_{\rho^\star,w}(\mu)\ge G I_w(\mu)>I_w(\mu),\qquad G=\frac{C(1+\eta)}{C+\eta}>1.\] Thus proportional late elimination strictly improves the instancewise error exponent of Kato's variance-aware static equal-gap maximin allocation throughout \((0,\infty)^K\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First, the allocation in \(\text{def:static-comparator}\) is well defined for every finite positive \(s\). Extend its maximin objective continuously to the closed simplex by assigning value zero on its boundary. The value is positive at the uniform allocation and zero on the boundary, so compactness gives a maximizer and every maximizer lies in \(\mathcal W\). The lexicographic rule selects one such maximizer. Hence every \(w_i\), and consequently every \(v_i=s_i/w_i\), is finite and positive. Assumption \(\text{ass:known-variances}\) ensures that this allocation can be chosen before rewards are observed.

Consider a terminal branch \(e\ne h\). For every survivor \(i\ne e\), \(\text{def:proportional-design}\) gives
\[
 n_i(e;\rho)=\rho w_i+\frac{(1-\rho)w_i}{1-w_e}
 =c_e(\rho)w_i,
 \qquad
 c_e(\rho):=1+(1-\rho)\frac{w_e}{1-w_e}. \tag{6}
\]
Relax the program \(Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}\) by dropping its elimination inequalities and all coordinates other than \(h,j\). For either retained arm \(i\), fixing its aggregate value at \(y_i\) gives
\[
 \inf_{q_i x_i+r_i z_i=n_i y_i}
 \left\{\frac{q_i(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}
 +\frac{r_i(z_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}\right\}
 =\frac{n_i(y_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the Lagrange equations set \(x_i=z_i=y_i\). Minimizing the sum of (7) for \(i=h,j\) under \(y_j\geq y_h\) puts the optimum on \(y_j=y_h\). Using \(n_i=c_e(\rho)w_i\) yields
\[
 Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}(\rho,\mu)
 \geq \frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(s_h/n_h+s_j/n_j)}
 =c_e(\rho)\frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(v_h+v_j)}
 \geq c_e(\rho)I_w(\mu). \tag{8}
\]
This is an unconditional relaxation of the coupled branch program; no conditional distribution has been substituted.

By \(\text{lem:weighted-projection}\),
\[
 Q_h^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu)
 \geq \rho C_h(v)I_w(\mu)
 \geq \rho C(v)I_w(\mu). \tag{9}
\]
Combining (8)--(9) with \(\text{thm:finite-branch-exponent}\) gives, for every \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
 \Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu)
 \geq \min\{\rho C,\,1+(1-\rho)\eta\}I_w(\mu). \tag{10}
\]
The strict projection result gives \(C>1\), while interiority of \(w\) gives \(\eta>0\). Therefore
\[
 \rho^\star=\frac{1+\eta}{C+\eta}<1,
 \qquad
 \rho^\star-\frac1C
 =\frac{\eta(C-1)}{C(C+\eta)}>0.
\]
At this value the two entries of the minimum in (10) agree:
\[
 \rho^\star C=1+(1-\rho^\star)\eta
 =\frac{C(1+\eta)}{C+\eta}=G.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 G-1=\frac{\eta(C-1)}{C+\eta}>0.
\]
Assumption \(\text{ass:unique-best}\) and \(\text{lem:static-exponent}\) give \(I_w(\mu)>0\). Equation (10) consequently proves
\[
 \Gamma_{\rho^\star,w}(\mu)\geq G I_w(\mu)>I_w(\mu)
\]
for every unique-best \(\mu\) and every finite positive variance vector \(s\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the theorem-level kernel: heterogeneity never creates a positive-variance exception to late-elimination admissibility. \textit{Gap.} Imbens, Qin, and Wager prove common-variance dominance over uniform allocation, while Kato proves equal-gap maximin optimality of a static heteroskedastic allocation; neither proves this strict cross-result dominance. \textit{Consumer.} A multi-arm fundraising experiment with known outcome variances can replace its generalized-Neyman static schedule by the stated two-batch schedule without sacrificing any fixed unique-best instance asymptotically.

\begin{theorem}[thm:proportional-frontier]\label{thm:proportional-frontier}
For every \(0<\rho<1\), the exact worst-instance multiplier within \(\mathfrak D_2\) is \[\inf_{\mu:\,|\arg\max_i\mu_i|=1}\frac{\Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu)}{I_w(\mu)}=\min\left\{\rho C,\ 1+(1-\rho)\eta\right\}.\] A best-elimination witness with \(h\in\arg\min_k C_k\) attains the first branch. For an \(e\in\arg\min_k w_k/(1-w_k)\), two distinct survivors and a sufficiently separated eliminated arm attain the second branch. Consequently \(\rho^\star\) uniquely maximizes the displayed value and the maximum is \(G\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(\text{thm:multi-proportional-frontier}\) with \(m=K-1\) and \(\ell=1\). The specialization identities proved there give
\[
 \mathsf D_{\rho,w,K-1}=\mathsf D_{\rho,w},\qquad
 \Gamma_{\rho,w,K-1}=\Gamma_{\rho,w},\qquad
 C_{K-1}=C,
\]
and
\[
 \eta_1=\frac{\min_e w_e}{1-\min_e w_e}
 =\min_e\frac{w_e}{1-w_e}=\eta.
\]
Its exact worst-instance formula therefore becomes
\[
 \inf_{\mu:\,|\arg\max_i\mu_i|=1}
 \frac{\Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu)}{I_w(\mu)}
 =\min\{\rho C,1+(1-\rho)\eta\}.
\]
The best-exclusion construction in that theorem has \(S=\mathcal A\setminus\{h\}\) and is precisely the stated best-elimination witness. Its separated terminal construction has singleton excluded set \(\{e\}\), with \(e\in\arg\min_k w_k/(1-w_k)\), and supplies the stated two-survivor comparison while all other survivor means are sufficiently separated.

For completeness, \(C>1\) by \(\text{lem:weighted-projection}\) and \(\eta>0\) because \(w\in\mathcal W\). Thus \(\rho\mapsto\rho C\) is strictly increasing and \(\rho\mapsto1+(1-\rho)\eta\) is strictly decreasing, and their unique intersection lies in \((0,1)\). Solving the intersection equation gives
\[
 \rho^\star=\frac{1+\eta}{C+\eta},
 \qquad
 C\rho^\star=\frac{C(1+\eta)}{C+\eta}=G.
\]
Hence \(\rho^\star\) is the unique maximizer and the maximum is \(G\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem remains the exact eliminate-one reduction of the stronger survivor-count frontier. \textit{Gap.} The broader top-\(m\) theorem closes the former material maximality gap while preserving the original result as \(m=K-1\). \textit{Consumer.} Existing eliminate-one implementations retain the original optimal schedule and matching witnesses.

\begin{proposition}[prop:radical-kill-test]\label{prop:radical-kill-test}
For \(K=3\) and \(s=(1,1,4)\), the static selector is \[w_1=w_2=\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2},\qquad w_3=2-\sqrt2.\] The active coefficient is \(C=C_3=(6+2\sqrt2)/7\), \(\eta=(2\sqrt2-1)/7\), \(\rho^\star=2\sqrt2-2\), and \(G=(8\sqrt2-4)/7\). The best-arm-elimination witness with \(h=3\) has exponent \(G I_w\), and the terminal witness eliminating arm \(1\) with survivors \(2,3\) also has exponent \(G I_w\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(r=\sqrt2\). Maximizing the static pair criterion is equivalent to minimizing
\[
 M(u)=\max_{i<j}\left(\frac{s_i}{u_i}+\frac{s_j}{u_j}\right)
\]
over the simplex. For an arbitrary allocation put \(a=u_1+u_2\). One of \(u_1,u_2\) is at most \(a/2\); pairing that arm with arm \(3\) gives
\[
 M(u)\geq \frac2a+\frac4{1-a}. \tag{11}
\]
The right-hand side has its unique minimum where \(1-a=\sqrt2a\), namely \(a=r-1\). At
\[
 w_1=w_2=\frac{r-1}{2},\qquad w_3=2-r,
\]
equality holds in (11). The remaining pair value \(2/w_1=4/a\) is smaller because \(a>1/3\). Thus this allocation globally minimizes \(M\), and the lexicographic selector in \(\text{def:static-comparator}\) returns it.

The unit-horizon variances are
\[
 v_1=v_2=2+2r=:a_0,\qquad v_3=4+2r=:b_0,
\]
and
\[
 d_{31}=d_{32}=\sqrt{2(a_0+b_0)}=a_0.
\]
For \(h=3\), both competitor terms are active at the stationary point
\[
 t_3=\frac{2a_0b_0}{a_0+2b_0}=\frac{8+12r}{7}\in(0,a_0).
\]
Direct substitution gives
\[
 C_3=\frac{t_3^2}{2b_0}+\frac{(a_0-t_3)^2}{a_0}
 =\frac{6+2r}{7}. \tag{12}
\]
To verify that this is the active coefficient, take \(h=1\); the case \(h=2\) is symmetric. Here \(d_{12}=2\sqrt{a_0}<d_{13}=a_0\). Consequently, for every \(t\geq0\),
\[
 F_1(t)\geq H(t):=\frac{t^2}{2a_0}
 +(d_{12}-t)_+^2\left(\frac1{2a_0}+\frac1{2b_0}\right).
\]
The unique minimum of \(H\) is
\[
 \frac{2(1/a_0+1/b_0)}{2/a_0+1/b_0}
 =\frac{6+2r}{7}=C_3.
\]
At the minimizer of \(H\), the inequalities \(d_{13}>d_{12}>t\) make \(F_1(t)>H(t)\). If the minima of \(F_1\) and \(H\) were equal, a minimizer of the continuous coercive \(F_1\) would also have to be the unique minimizer of \(H\), contradicting that strict inequality. Therefore \(C_1=C_2>C_3\), and (12) gives \(C=C_3\).

The least survivor expansion is attained by arm \(1\) or \(2\):
\[
 \eta=\frac{w_1}{1-w_1}
 =\frac{r-1}{3-r}
 =\frac{2r-1}{7},
\]
whereas \(w_3/(1-w_3)>1\). Substitution into \(\text{def:robust-schedule}\) gives
\[
 \rho^\star=2r-2,
 \qquad
 G=\rho^\star C=\frac{8r-4}{7}. \tag{13}
\]

We now verify both coupled witnesses exactly. For the best-elimination witness take
\[
 \mu_3=0,\qquad \mu_1=\mu_2=-a_0.
\]
Then \(I_w=1\), and the one-dimensional projection gives
\[
 Q_3^{\mathrm E}=\rho^\star C_3=G.
\]
For this paragraph define
\[
 c_e:=1+(1-\rho^\star)\frac{w_e}{1-w_e}.
\]
On every branch where the best arm survives, relaxing the elimination inequalities and minimizing the two batch costs at fixed aggregate means sets the two batch means equal. The resulting two-arm projection gives
\[
 Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}
 \geq c_e\frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(v_3+v_j)}
 \geq c_e I_w\geq G.
\]
The exact branch formula in \(\text{thm:finite-branch-exponent}\) therefore yields \(\Gamma_{\rho^\star,w}=G I_w\).

For the terminal witness, eliminate arm \(1\), retain best arm \(3\) and challenger \(2\), and set
\[
 \mu_3=0,\qquad \mu_2=-a_0,\qquad
 \mu_1=-2M,\qquad M>a_0/2.
\]
The normalized \(3\)-versus-\(2\) gap has static exponent one, whereas the \(3\)-versus-\(1\) gap has exponent \((2M/a_0)^2>1\); hence again \(I_w=1\). The unconstrained terminal optimizer has
\[
 x_2=z_2=x_3=z_3=y,\qquad
 y=-\frac{a_0b_0}{a_0+b_0},
\]
while \(x_1=\mu_1=-2M<-a_0<y\). Thus arm \(1\) is strictly the first-batch loser at this optimizer, so the elimination constraint is inactive and
\[
 Q_{1,2}^{\mathrm T}
 =c_1(\rho^\star)
 =1+(1-\rho^\star)\eta
 =G.
\]
For this same witness, the weighted projection inequality gives \(Q_3^{\mathrm E}\geq\rho^\star C_3I_w=G I_w\), and the aggregate-mean relaxation gives every terminal branch value at least \(c_eI_w\geq G I_w\). Since the \((1,2)\) branch is exactly \(G I_w\), \(\text{thm:finite-branch-exponent}\) yields \(\Gamma_{\rho^\star,w}=G I_w\). This proves both exact radical witnesses.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-budget-certificate]\label{thm:finite-budget-certificate}
For every budget large enough that each prescribed positive count is at least one, \[\mathbb P_{\mu,s}^{\rho,w}(\widehat h_T\ne h)\le B_T(\mu,s,\rho,w).\] The certificate contains one best-elimination term and exactly \((K-1)(K-2)\) terminal branch terms, uses the implemented integer counts without asymptotic replacement, and satisfies \[\lim_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log B_T(\mu,s,\rho,w)=\Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu).\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a budget for which every prescribed positive count is at least one. On the event that \(h\) is eliminated, the independent first-batch sample means are Gaussian by \(\text{ass:gaussian-marginals}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-streams}\), and the closed weak-error cone is \(\mathcal B_h\). Whitening this vector, projecting the origin onto the whitened cone, and applying the supporting-half-space exponential Markov argument proved in \(\text{thm:finite-branch-exponent}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb P(h\text{ is eliminated})
 \leq \exp\{-TQ_{T,h}^{\mathrm E}\}. \tag{14}
\]
This bounds the actual strict event because Gaussian ties have probability zero.

For \(e\ne h\) and \(j\ne e,h\), use the independent pre-sampled streams to form jointly the first-batch means and the fresh second-batch means prescribed for branch \(e\). Their exact finite-budget Gaussian quadratic rate is \(T\) times the rounded program in \(\text{def:finite-certificate}\). The event that \(e\) is the first-batch loser and that the aggregate mean of \(j\) is at least that of \(h\) is contained in its closed branch polyhedron. The same projection and scalar Chernoff calculation yields
\[
 \mathbb P(e\text{ is eliminated and }j\text{ terminally beats }h)
 \leq \exp\{-TQ_{T,e,j}^{\mathrm T}\}. \tag{15}
\]
This is an unconditional probability bound and therefore preserves the coupling through the reused first-batch observations.

The deployment error is contained in the union of the event in (14) and the events in (15). There is one event of the first kind, \(K-1\) choices of \(e\ne h\), and, for each \(e\), \(K-2\) choices of \(j\ne e,h\). The union bound and \(\text{def:finite-certificate}\) give
\[
 \mathbb P_{\mu,s}^{\rho,w}(\widehat h_T\ne h)
 \leq \exp\{-TQ_{T,h}^{\mathrm E}\}
 +\sum_{e\ne h}\sum_{j\ne e,h}\exp\{-TQ_{T,e,j}^{\mathrm T}\}
 =B_T(\mu,s,\rho,w).
\]

Largest-remainder rounding gives coefficientwise convergence of every rounded program. The equicoercivity and strict-feasibility argument in \(\text{thm:finite-branch-exponent}\) therefore gives
\[
 Q_{T,h}^{\mathrm E}\longrightarrow Q_h^{\mathrm E},
 \qquad
 Q_{T,e,j}^{\mathrm T}\longrightarrow Q_{e,j}^{\mathrm T}.
\]
For a fixed finite collection of real numbers \(a_{\ell,T}\),
\[
 \min_{\ell\leq L}a_{\ell,T}-\frac{\log L}{T}
 \leq -\frac1T\log\sum_{\ell=1}^L e^{-Ta_{\ell,T}}
 \leq \min_{\ell\leq L}a_{\ell,T}.
\]
Here \(L=1+(K-1)(K-2)\). Taking limits and using the exact branch equality in \(\text{thm:finite-branch-exponent}\) proves
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log B_T(\mu,s,\rho,w)
 =\Gamma_{\rho,w}(\mu).
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The same branch geometry should yield a conservative finite-budget deliverable without hiding rounding in asymptotic notation. \textit{Gap.} Lalitha et al. give stagewise heterogeneous-variance bounds for SHVar; this certificate instead retains the exact coupled late-elimination branch programs and their limiting exponent. \textit{Consumer.} The published \(\texttt{bbandits}\) successive-elimination workflow can expose \(B_T\) as a pre-deployment error certificate for a chosen integer fundraising budget.

\begin{proposition}[prop:equal-variance-reduction]\label{prop:equal-variance-reduction}
If \(s_i=\sigma^2\) for every arm, then \(w_i=1/K\), \(C=2(K-1)/K\), \(\eta=1/(K-1)\), \[\rho^\star=\frac{K^2}{2(K-1)^2+K},\qquad G=\frac{2K(K-1)}{2(K-1)^2+K}.\] In particular, \(K=3\) gives \(\rho^\star=9/11\) and \(G=12/11\), recovering the common-variance late-elimination improvement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(s_i=\sigma^2\) for every \(i\). Let \(u_{(1)}\leq u_{(2)}\) be the two smallest coordinates of an arbitrary interior allocation. Their average cannot exceed the average of all \(K\) coordinates, so \(u_{(1)}+u_{(2)}\leq2/K\). The arithmetic--harmonic mean inequality gives
\[
 \max_{i<j}\sigma^2\left(\frac1{u_i}+\frac1{u_j}\right)
 \geq \sigma^2\left(\frac1{u_{(1)}}+\frac1{u_{(2)}}\right)
 \geq \frac{4\sigma^2}{u_{(1)}+u_{(2)}}
 \geq2K\sigma^2. \tag{16}
\]
The uniform allocation attains (16). Equality forces \(u_{(1)}=u_{(2)}=1/K\); since these are the two smallest coordinates and all coordinates sum to one, every coordinate is then \(1/K\). Hence \(\text{def:static-comparator}\) selects
\[
 w_i=\frac1K,\qquad v_i=K\sigma^2=:V.
\]
Thus \(d_{hj}=2\sqrt V\) for every \(j\ne h\), and
\[
 F_h(t)=\frac{t^2}{2V}
 +\frac{K-1}{2V}(2\sqrt V-t)_+^2.
\]
Its minimizer is \(t=2(K-1)\sqrt V/K<2\sqrt V\), and direct substitution gives
\[
 C_h=C=\frac{2(K-1)}K.
\]
Also,
\[
 \eta=\frac{1/K}{1-1/K}=\frac1{K-1}.
\]
Substitution into \(\text{def:robust-schedule}\) yields
\[
 \rho^\star
 =\frac{K^2}{2(K-1)^2+K},
 \qquad
 G=\frac{2K(K-1)}{2(K-1)^2+K}.
\]
The strict dominance conclusion follows from \(\text{thm:universal-dominance}\). At \(K=3\), these formulas reduce to \(\rho^\star=9/11\) and \(G=12/11\).
\end{proof}

The restriction \(m=K-1\) is the nested unrestricted-allocation eliminate-one subquestion over \(\mathfrak D_2^{\mathrm{all}}\), not the full two-batch frontier.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The unrestricted multi-survivor two-batch frontier is the genuine open content beyond the exact proportional top-\(m\) theorem. \textit{Gap.} Existing batch-complexity results use different fixed-budget or fixed-confidence criteria; no cited theorem gives the joint heteroskedastic robust-gain saddle value over survivor count, first-stage allocation, and branch-specific survivor allocations. \textit{Consumer.} A solution determines whether experimental platforms lose any worst-instance exponent by enforcing the proportional survivor-allocation interface and its certified discrete survivor-count choice.

\begin{theorem}[thm:multi-proportional-frontier]\label{thm:multi-proportional-frontier}
Fix \(m\in\{2,\ldots,K-1\}\) and put \(\ell=K-m\). For every \(0<\rho<1\), the rounded top-\(m\) design has the exact exponent
\[
 \Gamma_{\rho,w,m}(\mu)=
 \min\left\{
 \min_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J_m\\h\notin J}}Q_J^{\mathrm E}(\rho,\mu),\ 
 \min_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J_m\\h\in J}}\ \min_{j\in J\setminus\{h\}}Q_{J,j}^{\mathrm T}(\rho,\mu)
 \right\}. \tag{21}
\]
There are exactly \(\binom{K-1}{m}\) best-exclusion branches and \((m-1)\binom{K-1}{m-1}\) terminal branches. Each closed-polyhedron infimum in (21) equals the infimum over the corresponding strict branch event, and deterministic largest-remainder rounding leaves (21) unchanged. Moreover,
\[
 \inf_{\mu:\,|\arg\max_i\mu_i|=1}
 \frac{\Gamma_{\rho,w,m}(\mu)}{I_w(\mu)}
 =L_m(\rho):=\min\{\rho C_m,\ 1+(1-\rho)\eta_\ell\}. \tag{22}
\]
A best-exclusion construction attains the first term whenever it is the smaller term, and a separated survivor-set terminal construction attains the second term whenever it is the smaller term. Consequently \(\rho_m^\star\) is the unique maximizer of \(L_m\) on \((0,1)\), its value is \(G_m>1\), and the certified optimum over \(\mathfrak D_2^{\mathrm{multi}}=\bigcup_{m=2}^{K-1}\mathfrak D_{2,m}\) is
\[
 \max_{2\le m\le K-1}\ \sup_{0<\rho<1}\ \inf_{\mu:\,|\arg\max_i\mu_i|=1}
 \frac{\Gamma_{\rho,w,m}(\mu)}{I_w(\mu)}=G_{\mathrm{multi}},
\]
attained by \((m^\star,\rho_{m^\star}^\star)\). For every budget for which all prescribed positive counts are at least one,
\[
 \mathbb P_{\mu,s}^{\rho,w,m}(\widehat h_{T,m}\ne h)\le B_{T,m},
 \qquad
 \lim_{T\to\infty}-T^{-1}\log B_{T,m}=\Gamma_{\rho,w,m}(\mu). \tag{23}
\]
If \(s_i=\sigma^2\) for every \(i\), then
\[
 C_m=\frac{2m}{m+1},\qquad \eta_\ell=\frac{K-m}{m},\qquad
 \rho_m^\star=\frac{K(m+1)}{m^2+(K-1)m+K},\qquad
 G_m=\frac{2mK}{m^2+(K-1)m+K}, \tag{24}
\]
and, for \(2\le m\le K-2\), \(\operatorname{sign}(G_{m+1}-G_m)=\operatorname{sign}\{K-m(m+1)\}\). Hence an optimal \(m\) is of order \(\sqrt K\) and \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}\to2\) as \(K\to\infty\). At \(m=K-1\), (21)--(24) reduce to the eliminate-one formulas.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(q_i=\rho w_i\). Since \(s_i=w_iv_i\), every first-batch quadratic cost is
\[
 \frac{q_i(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}=\rho\frac{(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2v_i}. \tag{25}
\]
We first establish the exact branch exponent rather than assume a limit law. Fix a retained set \(J\). The vector consisting of the first-batch means and the fresh second-batch means for arms in \(J\) is Gaussian by \(\text{ass:gaussian-marginals}\), has independent coordinates by \(\text{ass:independent-streams}\), and has density proportional to \(\exp\{-T R_{J,T}(x,z;\mu)\}\), where \(R_{J,T}\) is obtained from \(R_J\) by using the exact rounded fractions. Largest-remainder rounding gives coefficientwise convergence \(R_{J,T}\to R_J\).

For completeness, let \(X_T\sim\mathcal N(a,\Sigma_T/T)\) in a fixed finite dimension, with \(\Sigma_T\) positive definite and converging to a positive-definite matrix, and let \(P_T\) be one of the closed convex branch polyhedra formed with the exact rounded aggregate coefficients. Let \(x_T^\circ\) minimize \(I_T(x)=\tfrac12(x-a)^\top\Sigma_T^{-1}(x-a)\) over \(P_T\). The projection inequality gives
\[
 (x_T^\circ-a)^\top\Sigma_T^{-1}(x-x_T^\circ)\ge0\qquad(x\in P_T). \tag{26}
\]
Thus membership in \(P_T\) implies
\[
 (x_T^\circ-a)^\top\Sigma_T^{-1}(X_T-a)\ge 2I_T(x_T^\circ).
\]
The scalar on the left is centered Gaussian with variance \(2I_T(x_T^\circ)/T\); its exponential-moment bound, optimized at the corresponding one-dimensional Gaussian tilt, yields
\[
 \mathbb P(X_T\in P_T)\le e^{-T I_T(x_T^\circ)}. \tag{27}
\]
Conversely, if \(x\) lies in the strict limiting branch polyhedron, convergence of every rounded linear constraint coefficient puts a sufficiently small Euclidean ball around \(x\) inside \(P_T\) for all large \(T\). Integrating the Gaussian density on that ball gives
\[
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\log\mathbb P(X_T\in P_T)\ge-I(x), \tag{28}
\]
because the fixed-dimensional normalizing determinant and ball volume have logarithms \(o(T)\). Every branch polyhedron has a strict feasible point: separate all retained first-batch coordinates from all excluded ones, and, for a terminal branch, additionally move the fresh challenger coordinate upward. A convex combination of such a point with any weakly feasible point is strict and converges to the weakly feasible point. Continuity of the quadratic rate therefore shows that the strict and closed infima agree. Equations (27)--(28) prove the Gaussian large-deviation equality for each branch.

The rounded quadratic forms are uniformly coercive for all sufficiently large \(T\), since every limiting first-batch coefficient and every limiting survivor second-batch coefficient is positive. The rounded terminal inequalities are linear and their coefficients converge to those of the displayed aggregate comparison. Comparing minimizers with a fixed limiting strict feasible point, which remains feasible for all large \(T\), bounds the minimizers. Every convergent subsequence is feasible for the limiting weak inequalities and gives the lower limit by coefficientwise convergence. Conversely, an arbitrarily near-optimal limiting strict point remains rounded-feasible and gives the upper limit. Hence every rounded branch value converges to its displayed population-fraction program.

If \(h\notin J\), retaining \(J\) already causes error and gives one best-exclusion branch. There are \(\binom{K-1}{m}\) such sets. If \(h\in J\), an error requires at least one of the \(m-1\) other survivors to have aggregate mean at least that of \(h\); there are \(\binom{K-1}{m-1}\) such survivor sets. Gaussian ties have probability zero. Taking the exponent of this finite union, using the branch equalities just proved, gives (21), its asserted branch count, strict-interior equality, and rounding invariance.

We next lower-bound best exclusion. Put \(I=I_w(\mu)>0\). If \(h\notin J\), the constraints imply \(x_j\ge x_h\) for every \(j\in J\). After translating all means, write \(x_h=\mu_h-a\). Minimizing the other coordinates subject only to these inequalities and using (25) gives
\[
 Q_J^{\mathrm E}\ge \rho\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
 \left\{\frac{a^2}{2v_h}+\sum_{j\in J}\frac{(\Delta_j-a)_+^2}{2v_j}\right\}. \tag{29}
\]
The objective decreases when a negative \(a\) is moved toward zero, so the infimum may be restricted to \(a\ge0\). By the definition of \(I_w\), \(\Delta_j\ge\sqrt I\,d_{hj}\). Substituting \(a=\sqrt I\,t\) in (29) and using monotonicity of the positive part yields
\[
 Q_J^{\mathrm E}\ge \rho I C_{h,J}\ge \rho I C_m. \tag{30}
\]

For later use we verify strictly that \(C_m>1\). For any \(j\in J\) and \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \frac{t^2}{2v_h}+\frac{(d_{hj}-t)_+^2}{2v_j}\ge1. \tag{31}
\]
Indeed, on \(t<d_{hj}\) this is a two-variable quadratic whose minimum is \(d_{hj}^2/\{2(v_h+v_j)\}=1\), while on \(t\ge d_{hj}\) it is larger than \(d_{hj}^2/(2v_h)>1\). Equality in (31) requires
\[
 t=\frac{v_h d_{hj}}{v_h+v_j}<\sqrt{2v_h}.
\]
For every different \(k\in J\), \(d_{hk}=\sqrt{2(v_h+v_k)}>\sqrt{2v_h}>t\), so the \(k\)-term in \(F_{h,J}(t)\) is then strictly positive. Since \(|J|=m\ge2\), \(F_{h,J}(t)>1\) for all \(t\ge0\). Continuity and coercivity make its minimum attained, hence \(C_{h,J}>1\), and finiteness of the choices gives \(C_m>1\). On each interval determined by the breakpoints, differentiation gives exactly the stationary point in \(\text{def:projection-coefficient}\), proving the stated piecewise-quadratic computation.

Now consider a terminal branch with \(h,j\in J\). Put
\[
 c_J=\rho+\frac{1-\rho}{W_J}=1+(1-\rho)\frac{1-W_J}{W_J}. \tag{32}
\]
For a survivor \(i\), fixing its aggregate value \(y_i=\bar m_i(J;x,z)\) and completing the square gives the exact identity
\[
 \inf_{\rho w_i x_i+r_i z_i=n_i y_i}
 \left\{\frac{\rho w_i(x_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}
 +\frac{r_i(z_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}\right\}
 =\frac{n_i(y_i-\mu_i)^2}{2s_i}
 =c_J\frac{(y_i-\mu_i)^2}{2v_i}, \tag{33}
\]
where \(r_i=r_i(J;\rho)\) and \(n_i=c_Jw_i\). Dropping the first-batch survivor-set inequalities and applying (33) to \(h\) and \(j\), the constraint \(y_j\ge y_h\) has the two-arm projection value
\[
 Q_{J,j}^{\mathrm T}\ge c_J\frac{\Delta_j^2}{2(v_h+v_j)}\ge c_J I. \tag{34}
\]
If \(E=\mathcal A\setminus J\), then \(|E|=\ell\), \(1-W_J=\sum_{e\in E}w_e\ge\zeta_\ell\), and \(u\mapsto u/(1-u)\) is strictly increasing on \((0,1)\). Equations (32)--(34) imply
\[
 Q_{J,j}^{\mathrm T}\ge \{1+(1-\rho)\eta_\ell\}I. \tag{35}
\]
Combining (21), (30), and (35) proves that the ratio in (22) is at least \(L_m(\rho)\).

Both bounds have matching instances. Choose \((h_0,S_0)\) attaining \(C_m\), let \(\mu_{h_0}=0\), let \(\mu_j=-d_{h_0j}\) for \(j\in S_0\), and put every remaining mean equal to \(-M\). For sufficiently large \(M\), \(h_0\) is uniquely best, all pairs involving a remaining arm have static exponent at least one, and hence \(I_w=1\). At the optimizer of \(F_{h_0,S_0}\), the remaining excluded first-batch coordinates stay at \(-M\) and lie strictly below the retained coordinates. Thus the survivor-set constraints add no cost and
\[
 Q_{S_0}^{\mathrm E}=\rho C_m. \tag{36}
\]
Therefore the infimum in (22) is at most its first term.

For the terminal witness, choose \(E_0\) attaining \(\zeta_\ell\), put \(J_0=\mathcal A\setminus E_0\), and choose distinct \(h,j\in J_0\), possible because \(m\ge2\). Set
\[
 \mu_h=0,\qquad \mu_j=-d_{hj},\qquad
 \mu_k=-M\ (k\in J_0\setminus\{h,j\}),\qquad
 \mu_e=-2M\ (e\in E_0). \tag{37}
\]
For sufficiently large \(M\), again \(I_w=1\). The unconstrained optimizer in (34) sets \(x_h=z_h=x_j=z_j=y\), where
\[
 y=\frac{\mu_h/v_h+\mu_j/v_j}{1/v_h+1/v_j},
\]
keeps every unrelated survivor at \(-M\), and every excluded first-batch coordinate at \(-2M\). For large \(M\), all survivor-set inequalities are strict at this optimizer. Hence
\[
 Q_{J_0,j}^{\mathrm T}=c_{J_0}=1+(1-\rho)\eta_\ell. \tag{38}
\]
This supplies the second upper bound. Together with the universal lower bound, (36)--(38) prove (22); the appropriate witness attains the complete worst-instance ratio whenever its branch is the smaller one.

The function \(\rho C_m\) is strictly increasing and \(1+(1-\rho)\eta_\ell\) is strictly decreasing. Their unique intersection is
\[
 \rho_m^\star=\frac{1+\eta_\ell}{C_m+\eta_\ell}.
\]
Because \(C_m>1\) and \(\eta_\ell>0\), one has \(1/C_m<\rho_m^\star<1\), and the intersection value is \(G_m>1\). Maximizing the finitely many certified values over \(m\) proves the \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}\) assertion and attainment by \(m^\star\).

For the finite-budget assertion, apply (27) to each rounded closed branch and take a union bound. This gives (23) with exactly
\[
 \binom{K-1}{m}+(m-1)\binom{K-1}{m-1}
\]
terms. If these finitely many rounded branch values are \(a_{b,T}\), then
\[
 \min_b a_{b,T}-\frac{\log L}{T}
 \le-\frac1T\log\sum_{b=1}^L e^{-Ta_{b,T}}
 \le\min_b a_{b,T}.
\]
Their already proved convergence and (21) yield the limit in (23).

Finally suppose \(s_i=\sigma^2\). The maximin allocation is uniform: if \(u_{(1)}\le u_{(2)}\) are the two smallest allocation coordinates, then \(u_{(1)}+u_{(2)}\le2/K\) and
\[
 \max_{i<j}\sigma^2(1/u_i+1/u_j)\ge
 \sigma^2(1/u_{(1)}+1/u_{(2)})\ge2K\sigma^2,
\]
with equality only at \(u_i=1/K\). Thus \(v_i=K\sigma^2=:V\) and \(d_{hj}=2\sqrt V\). For every \((h,S)\),
\[
 F_{h,S}(t)=\frac{t^2}{2V}+\frac{m(2\sqrt V-t)_+^2}{2V},
\]
whose minimizer is \(2m\sqrt V/(m+1)\) and whose minimum is \(2m/(m+1)\). Also \(\zeta_\ell=\ell/K\) and \(\eta_\ell=(K-m)/m\). Substitution proves (24). Direct cross multiplication gives
\[
 \operatorname{sign}(G_{m+1}-G_m)
 =\operatorname{sign}\{(m+1)[m^2+(K-1)m+K]-m[m^2+(K+1)m+2K]\}
 =\operatorname{sign}\{K-m(m+1)\}.
\]
Hence a maximizing index straddles the positive root of \(m(m+1)=K\), so \(m\asymp\sqrt K\). Taking, for example, an admissible integer \(m\sim\sqrt K\) in (24) gives \(G_m\to2\), while \(G_m<2\) because its denominator exceeds \(mK\) by \(m^2-m+K>0\). Therefore \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}\to2\).

When \(m=K-1\), the only best-exclusion survivor set is \(\mathcal A\setminus\{h\}\), an excluded set has one arm, \(C_m=C\), and \(\eta_1=\min_e w_e/(1-w_e)=\eta\). All branch programs, schedules, witnesses, and certificates then coincide with the original eliminate-one objects. In the smallest heteroskedastic check \(K=3\), \(s=(1,1,4)\), this specialization gives exactly \(C=(6+2\sqrt2)/7\), \(\eta=(2\sqrt2-1)/7\), \(\rho^\star=2\sqrt2-2\), and \(G=(8\sqrt2-4)/7\); the best-exclusion and terminal constructions above are respectively the two exact witnesses, so the early kill test does not contradict the coupling formula.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Matching survivor-set witnesses certify both continuous timing and the discrete number of retained arms. \textit{Gap.} No cited comparator supplies the heteroskedastic exact top-\(m\) branch equality or its fixed-budget robust saddle value. \textit{Consumer.} An experiment designer can enumerate \(m\), compute \(C_m\) by one-dimensional projections, choose \(\rho_m^\star\), and evaluate the exact rounded branch certificate.

\section{Interpretation}

Retaining more arms makes erroneous exclusion of the best arm exponentially harder, through \(C_m\), but spreads the second-batch reallocation across a larger survivor weight, through \(\eta_{K-m}\). The certified schedule balances these two explicit failure modes, and \(m^\star\) chooses the best balance before outcomes are observed.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The generalized quantity \(B_{T,m}\) is a conservative upper certificate for the adaptive design's error probability, not an equality at finite \(T\). Comparing two upper certificates does not establish finite-sample risk dominance. The strict comparator claim is therefore an exact large-deviation-exponent statement; \(B_{T,m}\) is useful for planning under specified mean scenarios and for software regression tests.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact exponent, top-\(m\) projection inequality, matching survivor-set witnesses, timing optimum, discrete optimization over \(m\), equal-variance reduction, and rounded Chernoff certificate are proved for independent Gaussian streams with known finite positive variances and a unique best arm. The old eliminate-one result is the \(m=K-1\) reduction. The genuinely unrestricted residual class is now \(\mathfrak D_2^{\mathrm{all,multi}}\): it jointly ranges over the survivor count, every interior first-batch allocation, and every branch-specific interior survivor allocation. The proved proportional family embeds in this class and certifies a lower bound of \(G_{\mathrm{multi}}\), but a matching allocation-uniform upper saddle certificate is open. The former unrestricted-allocation eliminate-one class \(\mathfrak D_2^{\mathrm{all}}\) remains only the nested \(m=K-1\) subquestion. Unknown-variance adaptation, non-Gaussian rewards, variance ratios changing with budget, \(K=2\), and finite-sample risk dominance are outside the theorem.
