\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}bhar\_\allowbreak{}proxy\_\allowbreak{}modulus\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} proxy\_\allowbreak{}modulus\_\allowbreak{}v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For \(0<\lambda\le1\), the paper proves a three-channel Stat frontier for realized-path causal buy-and-hold wealth: treated replication, query-conditional calendar spacing, and full-covariance donor location. Historical proxy paths are exact and only the external event query is generated. The Gram threshold is fixed below the explicit local kernel-ball mass, and donor upper bounds require only unbiasedness and covariance ellipticity. A deterministic fixed-design rule may use known \(X,z_0,\Sigma\); every outer estimator and interval is one observable rule selected before the unknown donor mean. Panel conclusions transfer only through the labelled tuple \(\xi_P(z_0)\) at marginal-almost-every query.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Classical smooth regression and Gaussian optimal recovery do not combine finitely many exact design sites carrying replicated responses with an external full-covariance generated query. Generated-covariate and deconvolution models instead alter training coordinates, and ordinary interval theory has no donor-location expected-length converse.

\paragraph{Niche.} The center-only experiment separates three sources of difficulty. Conditional density and design mixing control occupancy of an explicit kernel ball; complete-unit concentration handles arbitrary within-history response dependence; and covariance control of donor means yields a rough-Holder \(N^{-\lambda}\) risk channel without any donor tail assumption.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note supplies a known-specification fixed-design recovery result, a feasible unknown-query marginal estimator, and a total honest interval for \(0<\lambda\le1\). Explicit treated, clustered-spacing, iid-spacing, and finite-support donor witnesses match the rates. The causal bridge is the labelled point \(\xi_P(z_0)\), constructed only on a marginally conull query set.

\section{Related work}

\textbf{Conditional frontier comparison.} Donoho's known Gaussian convex optimal-recovery experiment \cite{Donoho1994} and Stone's smooth-regression benchmark \cite{Stone1982} do not supply the bounded multiple-response fixed-design and full-covariance donor-location product experiment here. Exact training coordinates make a root-\(N\) query displacement change a merely \(\lambda\)-Holder evaluation by \(N^{-\lambda/2}\), unlike the second-order minimum-value perturbation under strong convexity in Akhavan, Gogolashvili, and Tsybakov \cite{AkhavanGogolashviliTsybakov2024}. The panel bridge is deliberately one-sided and marginal-almost-everywhere.

\textbf{Marginal phase comparison.} Stone's ordinary random-design rate uses growing distinct sites \cite{Stone1982}; here responses are replicated at only \(T\) exact sites, producing iid spacing \(T^{-2\lambda/d}\) and geometric-mixing spacing \(\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{2\lambda/d}\). The smoothing term is dominated for fixed \(S\). Mammen, Rothe, and Schienle generate training-row covariates \cite{MammenRotheSchienle2012}, and Delaigle, Hall, and Meister study noisy training coordinates and deconvolution \cite{DelaigleHallMeister2008}; neither has the center-only donor channel \(N^{-\lambda}\).

\textbf{Honest-length comparison.} Low gives the coverage-to-testing benchmark \cite{Low1997}; Armstrong and Kolesar develop ordered-modulus and bias-aware ordinary-regression intervals \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018,ArmstrongKolesar2020}; and Cattaneo, Feng, and Titiunik study dependent synthetic-control prediction intervals \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021}. The present interval concentrates one bounded complete history per unit, adds an explicit rough-Holder donor radius, and is total on geometry failures. It uses neither response-calendar mixing nor estimated-variance division, and donor-only perturbations create the additional \(N^{-\lambda/2}\) honest-length lower channel.

\section{Setup}

Target (estimand / structural parameter): At a fixed admissible query \(z_0\), each labelled point in \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) has a cohort-common treated conditional mean, and the causal target is \(\tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\mathbb E[B_{si}(1)-B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]\), whose value is independent of the replica \(i\). For a joint panel law \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), this conditional interpretation and the common-replica equality are asserted only for \(P\)-almost every query \(z_0\)..
Primary functional / estimator / diagnostic: At a fixed admissible query \(z_0\), consistency, cohort-common treated-mean replication, and event-date transport identify \(\tau(z_0)=\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}\) for every labelled point \(\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\). For \(0<\lambda\le1\), the deterministic experiment \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) treats \(X,z_0,\Sigma\) as known specifications, while every outer MSE or interval rule is one \(\mathcal O\)-measurable rule selected before the unknown donor mean and labelled point. The marginal squared-risk scale is \(R_\star=R_{\mathrm{or}}\vee N^{-\lambda}\), with the iid spacing replacement on \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\). Donor upper bounds use only unbiasedness and covariance ellipticity; no donor fourth-moment or covariance-consistency condition is imposed. For a joint panel law, only the labelled tuple \(\xi_P(z_0)=(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,\Sigma_P(z_0))\) is placed in the fixed-query class, and only for marginal-almost-every query. No panel-induced conditional converse, null-query assertion, lower local modulus, or result for \(\lambda>1\) is claimed..

  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}m\_\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}opt} --- \texttt{rough-\allowbreak{}Holder nearest-\allowbreak{}design regression estimate}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{conditional optimal-\allowbreak{}recovery nuisance estimate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat m_s^{\mathrm{opt}}=K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}\overline Q_{s,t_{s\ell}}\), with the nearest index defined in \texttt{def:nearest-design-estimator}.
  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}tau\_\allowbreak{}opt} --- \texttt{rough-\allowbreak{}Holder nearest-\allowbreak{}design causal estimator}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{conditional optimal-\allowbreak{}recovery estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}\) is the donor-fold nearest-design estimator in \texttt{def:nearest-design-estimator}.
  \item \texttt{E\_\allowbreak{}X} --- \texttt{deterministic local fixed-\allowbreak{}design product experiment}; \(2^{\mathcal P}\); \texttt{conditional statistical experiment}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) is the experiment constructed in \texttt{def:fixed-design-induced-experiment}.
  \item \texttt{E\_\allowbreak{}si} --- \texttt{treated coordinate in the deterministic fixed-\allowbreak{}design experiment}; \([-B,B]\); \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}design observed outcome}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(E_{si}\) is the observed treated-event coordinate for unit \(i\) and cohort \(s\) in \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\).
  \item \texttt{eta\_\allowbreak{}K} --- \texttt{positive kernel-\allowbreak{}ball radius}; \((0,\delta_0]\); \texttt{kernel geometry parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\eta_K\) is the fixed radius of the ball on which the kernel has its declared positive lower bound.
  \item \texttt{O\_\allowbreak{}obs} --- \texttt{common observable sigma-\allowbreak{}field}; \texttt{sigma-\allowbreak{}field}; \texttt{decision sigma-\allowbreak{}field}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal O=\sigma((X_{st})_{s,t},(Q_{sti})_{s,t,i},(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}})_{s,i},(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N})\), the information available to every fixed-query estimator and interval.
  \item \texttt{k\_\allowbreak{}K} --- \texttt{positive kernel lower bound}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{kernel geometry parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(k_K\) is the fixed lower height of the kernel on its radius-\(\eta_K\) ball.
  \item \texttt{theta\_\allowbreak{}E} --- \texttt{statistical target in the deterministic product experiment}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}design observed-\allowbreak{}law functional}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-f_s(z_s)\}\) for a labelled parameter point \(\xi=(\mathbb Q_\xi;\mu,f,z)\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\).
  \item \texttt{K} --- \texttt{bounded continuous compactly supported kernel}; \(K:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty)\); \texttt{smoothing primitive}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(K\) is a fixed continuous product kernel supported on the unit ball. Fixed constants \(\eta_K\in(0,\delta_0]\) and \(k_K>0\) satisfy \(K(v)\ge k_K\) whenever \(\|v\|_2\le\eta_K\).
  \item \texttt{G\_\allowbreak{}sT} --- \texttt{normalized local-\allowbreak{}polynomial Gram matrix}; \(\mathbb S_+^{q_r}\); \texttt{realized design geometry}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G_{sT}(u,b)=(Tb^d)^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}K((X_{st}-u)/b)b_r((X_{st}-u)/b)b_r((X_{st}-u)/b)^{\top}\).
  \item \texttt{tilde\_\allowbreak{}tau} --- \texttt{arbitrary observable-\allowbreak{}data estimator}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{decision variable}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} In \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\), \(\widetilde\tau\) is measurable in the observations and the known specifications \(X,z_0,\Sigma\), and is selected before the unknown labelled point.  In an outer fixed-query minimax display it denotes one \(\mathcal O\)-measurable rule selected before the unknown donor mean \(z_0\) and labelled kernel.  It never accesses potential outcomes or nuisance labels.
  \item \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}alpha} --- \texttt{generic observable-\allowbreak{}data random confidence interval}; \(\mathcal I(\mathbb R)\); \texttt{inference decision variable}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(I_\alpha\) is one interval-valued measurable rule based only on the observable sigma-field, selected before the query and labelled parameter point and without access to potential outcomes or nuisance labels.
  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}kernel} --- \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}query conditional causal-\allowbreak{}data kernel}; \(\mathcal P(z_0)\); \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}query experiment law}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathbb K_\xi\) denotes the kernel component of a labelled fixed-query parameter point; only its restriction to \(\mathcal O\) is observable.
  \item \texttt{v\_\allowbreak{}d} --- \texttt{positive dimension constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{small-\allowbreak{}ball volume}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(v_d\) is the Lebesgue volume of the Euclidean unit ball in \(\mathbb R^d\).
  \item \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}G} --- \texttt{positive constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{geometry parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The fixed coercivity threshold satisfies \(0<c_G<\tfrac14 k_Kc_Xv_d\eta_K^d\).
  \item \texttt{g\_\allowbreak{}sT} --- \texttt{attained dyadic Gram-\allowbreak{}coercivity radius}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{realized geometry upper-\allowbreak{}bound scale}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(g_{sT}(z_0)=\min\{b\in\mathcal B_s:\lambda_{\min}(G_{sT}(z_{0s},b))\ge c_G\}\), with value \(1\) and an empty-set record if no candidate exists.
  \item \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{geometry-\allowbreak{}clipped bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{anchor-\allowbreak{}certificate bandwidth}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(h_s^{\star}=g_{sT}(z_0)\); the dyadic grid defining \(g_{sT}\) enforces the floors \((Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\) and \(N^{-1/2}\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}D} --- \texttt{conditional realized-\allowbreak{}geometry upper-\allowbreak{}risk certificate}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{computable anchor/\allowbreak{}extrapolation certificate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_D=\{\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\}\vee[\sum_s\pi_s\{(h_s^\star)^\lambda+(Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d)^{-1/2}\}]^2\vee N^{-\kappa}\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}V} --- \texttt{known positive constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{variance certificate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A deterministic certificate constant depending only on the bounded kernel, the fixed basis dimension, and \(c_G\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}H} --- \texttt{known positive Holder-\allowbreak{}bias constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{bias calibration}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A deterministic constant depending only on \(L,d,r,K,c_\Sigma,C_\Sigma,c_G\) and dominating the local-polynomial and covariance-comparison Holder remainders.
  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}h\_\allowbreak{}sell} --- \texttt{foldwise selected bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{total bandwidth choice}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat h_{s\ell}\) is the bandwidth component of the lexicographically selected minimizer of \(\Gamma_{s\ell}(u,b)\) over the coercive anchor-bandwidth set; its fallback value is \(1\).
  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}h\_\allowbreak{}s} --- \texttt{cohort bias bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{interval bias scale}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat h_s=\max_{1\le\ell\le K_N}\widehat h_{s\ell}\).
  \item \texttt{F\_\allowbreak{}fb} --- \texttt{anchor-\allowbreak{}search fallback event}; \texttt{event}; \texttt{bad-\allowbreak{}sample record}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathsf F_{\mathrm{fb}}\) is the event that at least one fold--cohort coercive anchor-bandwidth candidate set is empty.
  \item \texttt{w\_\allowbreak{}st} --- \texttt{actual averaged selected-\allowbreak{}fit response weight}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{concentration weight}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(w_{st}\) is the donor-fold average of the intercept weights from the selected anchor-bandwidth local-polynomial fits; when \(0<\lambda\le1\), these are local-constant weights, so \(w_{st}\ge0\) and \(\sum_t w_{st}=1\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}alpha} --- \texttt{total concentration radius}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{bias-\allowbreak{}aware interval radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_\alpha=r_{\mathrm{samp}}+r_{\mathrm{bias}}+r_{\mathrm{don}}\), with the three explicit terms constructed in \texttt{def:honest-interval}.
  \item \texttt{M\_\allowbreak{}z0} --- \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}query labelled conditional-\allowbreak{}kernel class}; \texttt{set of labelled parameter points}; \texttt{pointwise statistical model}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) is the class of labelled points \(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\) constructed in \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class}; all decisions use only \(\mathcal O\).
  \item \texttt{Z\_\allowbreak{}delta} --- \texttt{admissible fixed-\allowbreak{}query set}; \(2^{(([0,1]^d)^S)}\); \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}query index set}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}=\{z\in([0,1]^d)^S:\min_s\operatorname{dist}(z_s,\partial[0,1]^d)\ge\delta_0\}\).
  \item \texttt{M\_\allowbreak{}iid} --- \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}query independent-\allowbreak{}calendar labelled subclass}; \texttt{set of labelled parameter points}; \texttt{no-\allowbreak{}log fixed-\allowbreak{}query design subclass}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) is the subclass of labelled points in \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) whose design dates are independent under the kernel component \(\mathbb K_\xi\).
  \item \texttt{theta\_\allowbreak{}K} --- \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}query observed-\allowbreak{}law functional}; \(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}:\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\to\mathbb R\); \texttt{statistical target before causal transfer}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}\); positivity and continuity make its value unique from \(\mathbb K_\xi\vert_{\mathcal O}\).
  \item \texttt{K\_\allowbreak{}P\_\allowbreak{}z0} --- \texttt{regular conditional causal-\allowbreak{}data kernel}; \(\mathcal P(z_0)\); \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}to-\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}query kernel component}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathbb K_{P,z_0}=P(\,\cdot\mid z_0)\) is a regular conditional law of the complete causal-data array given the realized query.
  \item \texttt{mu\_\allowbreak{}P} --- \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}law conditional treated-\allowbreak{}mean vector}; \(\mathbb R^S\); \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}to-\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}query mean label}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mu_P(z_0)=(\mu_{P,1}(z_0),\ldots,\mu_{P,S}(z_0))\), where \(\mu_{P,s}(z_0)=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]\), common across replicas \(i\) by \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering}.
  \item \texttt{Sigma\_\allowbreak{}P} --- \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}law conditional stacked donor covariance}; \(\mathbb S_{++}^{Sd}\); \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}to-\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}query covariance label}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\Sigma_P(z_0)=\operatorname{Var}_P(Z_j\mid z_0)\), for marginal-almost-every query at which the donor ellipticity clause holds.
  \item \texttt{xi\_\allowbreak{}P} --- \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}induced labelled fixed-\allowbreak{}query point}; \texttt{labelled parameter point}; \texttt{well-\allowbreak{}typed panel-\allowbreak{}to-\allowbreak{}kernel transfer object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\xi_P(z_0)=(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,\Sigma_P(z_0))\), the labelled tuple induced by a panel law and a chosen regular conditional kernel at the query.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
For every treated event unit, \(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (consistency for the realized treatment path, \cite{Abadie2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:event-date-transport]\label{ass:event-date-transport}
For each cohort, \(\mathbb E[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]=m_s(z_{0s})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This cross-date counterfactual equality is untestable from the observed design. Short-window institutional stability after conditioning on a rich fixed-dimensional market path can motivate it, but that story is not evidence for the equality. Rolling untreated pseudo-events can diagnose or falsify pre-event stability and cannot verify transport to the untreated event-date counterfactual.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-wealth]\label{ass:bounded-wealth}
Almost surely, \( |Q_{sti}|\vee|B_{si}(0)|\vee|B_{si}(1)|\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded nonparametric regression outcomes, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-regression]\label{ass:holder-regression}
For every cohort, \(m_s\) belongs to the isotropic Holder ball \(\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\) on \([0,1]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (isotropic Holder regression smoothness, \cite{Stone1982}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:interior-path]\label{ass:interior-path}
For every cohort, \(\operatorname{dist}(z_{0s},\partial[0,1]^d)\ge\delta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (interior pointwise regression evaluation, \cite{Fan1993}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-density]\label{ass:design-density}
For every cohort \(s\), every calendar index \(t\), and \(P\)-almost every realized query \(z_0\), the conditional law of \(X_{st}\) given \(z_0\) has a Lebesgue density \(f_{st\mid z_0}\) on \([0,1]^d\) satisfying \(c_X\le f_{st\mid z_0}(x)\le C_X\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in[0,1]^d\), with the constants uniform over cohorts, dates, laws under consideration, and all such admissible query kernels.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The ordinary density bound is strengthened explicitly to hold conditional on the realized event query and uniformly over admissible query kernels; this prevents query-dependent selection of an uncontrolled design hole.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cross-unit-independence]\label{ass:cross-unit-independence}
Conditional on the path-design array and \(z_0\), the unit histories are mutually independent across \((s,i)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional cross-unit independence in many-control panels, \cite{Ferman2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-conditional-centering]\label{ass:design-conditional-centering}
With \(\mathcal X=\sigma((X_{uv})_{1\le u\le S,\ 1\le v\le T})\), for every \(s,t,i\),
\[
 \mathbb E[Q_{sti}\mid z_0,\mathcal X]=m_s(X_{st}),
\]
and, on defining the cohort mean
\(\mu_s(z_0):=\mathbb E[B_{s1}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]\), every replica \(i\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]
 =\mu_s(z_0),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,\mathcal X]
 =\mu_s(z_0)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional mean centering for replicated random-design regression and panel sample means, \cite{Ferman2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-independence]\label{ass:donor-independence}
Conditional on \(z_0\), the donor scores \((Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\) are independent and identically distributed.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent replicated proxy measurements, \cite{DelaigleHallMeister2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:temporal-mixing]\label{ass:temporal-mixing}
For every law under consideration, every cohort \(s\), and almost every admissible realized query \(z_0\) under its marginal query law, the conditional design sequence \((X_{st})_{t=1}^{T}\) given \(z_0\) has beta-mixing coefficients satisfying \(\beta_s(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k\) for every \(k\ge1\); the same envelope is uniform over laws, cohorts, and such query values.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Only the design sequence is conditionally beta mixing; response dependence within a unit history remains unrestricted.  A panel law provides this property only at marginal-almost-every query, while the fixed-query class imposes it pointwise.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:center-only-design]\label{ass:center-only-design}
Every \(X_{st}\) is observed without donor measurement error in the response-regression sigma-field.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This data architecture occurs when historical market-state paths are recorded directly while only the event-date counterfactual path is synthesized from donors. It is empirically distinguishable from applying an estimated proxy map to every training row and from observing classically contaminated training coordinates.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-training-separation]\label{ass:donor-training-separation}
Conditional on \(z_0\), the donor-score sample \((Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\) is independent of the complete joint panel \(((X_{st})_{s,t},(Q_{sti})_{s,t,i},(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}})_{s,i})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (sample splitting for evaluation at an estimated center, \cite{AkhavanGogolashviliTsybakov2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-unbiasedness]\label{ass:donor-unbiasedness}
Conditional on \(z_0\), \(\mathbb E[Z_j\mid z_0]=z_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (unbiased replicated proxy measurement, \cite{DelaigleHallMeister2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-ellipticity]\label{ass:donor-ellipticity}
The full covariance satisfies \(c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform covariance ellipticity, \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}=\{P_{N,T,(n_s)_{s=1}^{S}}:
P_{N,T,(n_s)_{s=1}^{S}}\text{ satisfies \texttt{ass:consistency},
\texttt{ass:event-date-transport}, \texttt{ass:bounded-wealth},
\texttt{ass:holder-regression}, \texttt{ass:interior-path},
\texttt{ass:design-density}, \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence},
\texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering}, \texttt{ass:donor-independence},
\texttt{ass:temporal-mixing}, \texttt{ass:center-only-design},
\texttt{ass:donor-training-separation}, \texttt{ass:donor-unbiasedness},
\text{ and }\texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}event-\allowbreak{}date-\allowbreak{}transport}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}wealth}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}regression}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}path}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}density}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}cross-\allowbreak{}unit-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}temporal-\allowbreak{}mixing}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}center-\allowbreak{}only-\allowbreak{}design}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}training-\allowbreak{}separation}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}unbiasedness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}ellipticity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}centering}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:buyhold-level]\label{def:buyhold-level}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Realized buy-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}hold level}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(B_{si}(a)=W_{si,-1}\prod_{h=0}^{H}\{1+R_{sih}(a)\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:target]\label{def:target}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Conditional realized-\allowbreak{}path causal wealth effect}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s[\mathbb E\{B_{si}(1)\mid z_0\}-\mathbb E\{B_{si}(0)\mid z_0\}]\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:local-modulus]\label{def:local-modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}covariance event-\allowbreak{}path modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\omega_P(\delta;z_0)=\sup\{|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s[m_s(z_{0s}+u_s)-m_s(z_{0s})]|:\|\Sigma^{-1/2}u\|_2\le\delta,\ z_0+u\in([0,1]^d)^S\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:realized-geometry]\label{def:realized-geometry}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Attained pointwise dyadic Gram radius}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each cohort \(s\), let \(\mathcal B_s\) be the finite dyadic grid from \(b_{0s}\) to \(1\), and define
\[
g_{sT}(z_0)=\min\left\{b\in\mathcal B_s:\lambda_{\min}\{G_{sT}(z_{0s},b)\}\ge c_G\right\}.
\]
If the displayed set is empty, set \(g_{sT}(z_0)=1\) and record that the set was empty. Thus a value \(g_{sT}(z_0)<1\) is an attained response-independent coercive bandwidth, rather than an infimum over a nonmonotone continuum or over an unobserved tube.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:conditional-rate]\label{def:conditional-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Anchor/\allowbreak{}extrapolation risk certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Keep \(h_s^\star=g_{sT}(z_0)\), noting that the dyadic grid defining
\(g_{sT}\) already enforces
\(h_s^\star\ge(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\vee N^{-1/2}\), and define
\[
 R_D=\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}\right\}
 \vee
 \left[\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 (h_s^\star)^\lambda+
 (Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d)^{-1/2}\right\}\right]^2
 \vee N^{-\kappa}.
\]
The quantity \(R_D\) is the computable anchor/extrapolation upper-risk
certificate.  It is not the fixed-design minimax scale; that role is played
by \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\) from
\texttt{def:optimal-recovery-rate}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:marginal-rate]\label{def:marginal-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Fixed-\allowbreak{}query geometric-\allowbreak{}mixing marginal squared-\allowbreak{}risk scale}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} At a fixed admissible query \(z_0\), define
\[
 R_{\mathrm{or}}
 =\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}\right\}
 \vee
 \left[\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s(Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}\right]^2
 \vee
 \left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{2\lambda/d},
 \qquad
 R_\star=R_{\mathrm{or}}\vee N^{-\kappa}.
\]
The quantity \(R_\star\) is the marginal oracle-plus-donor squared-risk scale over \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  Its final component is the full-covariance generated-query contribution, while the last component of \(R_{\mathrm{or}}\) is the worst-case pointwise spacing scale under geometric design beta mixing.  Over \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), replace that spacing component by \(T^{-2\lambda/d}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:frontier-estimator]\label{def:frontier-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Replicated-\allowbreak{}donor anchor-\allowbreak{}extrapolation estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(K_N=\min\{2,N\}\), assign donors to the deterministic balanced folds \(I_\ell\), compute \(\overline Z_\ell\), and put \(\widetilde z_\ell=\Pi_{([0,1]^d)^S}(\overline Z_\ell)\). Form \(\overline Q_{st}=n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}Q_{sti}\). For each \((s,\ell)\), let
\[
\mathcal A_{s\ell}=\left\{(u,b)\in[0,1]^d\times\mathcal B_s:\lambda_{\min}\{G_{sT}(u,b)\}\ge c_G\right\}
\]
and minimize on this set the response-independent certificate
\[
\Gamma_{s\ell}(u,b)=C_H\|u-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\kappa+C_Hb^\lambda+C_V(Tn_sb^d)^{-1/2}.
\]
The selected pair \((\widehat u_{s\ell},\widehat h_{s\ell})\) is the lexicographically first exact minimizer, first in \(b\) and then in the coordinates of \(u\). Continuity of the fixed kernel makes \(\mathcal A_{s\ell}\) compact and \(\Gamma_{s\ell}\) continuous, so this rule is well defined and Borel measurable. At the selected pair, use the intercept of the order-\(r\) tensor local-polynomial fit and clip it to \([-B,B]\); the intercept estimates \(m_s(\widetilde z_{\ell s})\), with \(C_H\|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\kappa\) charged as extrapolation bias. If \(\mathcal A_{s\ell}\) is empty, record \(\mathsf F_{\mathrm{fb}}\) and use the clipped pooled mean, with recorded bandwidth \(1\). Average the fold values to obtain \(\widehat m_s\), and set
\[
\widehat\tau=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\widehat m_s\right\}.
\]
All searches, equality cases, projections, clipping operations, and fallbacks are deterministic and total.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:honest-interval]\label{def:honest-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Total unit-\allowbreak{}history concentration interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}=\alpha/2\).  On the regular branch, let \(w_{st}\) be the actual response weight obtained by averaging the selected foldwise intercept weights, and define
\[
 A_w=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
       \left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}|w_{st}|\right)^2,
 \qquad
 M_w=\max_{1\le s\le S}\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}
       \left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}|w_{st}|\right).
\]
The unit-history sampling radius is
\[
 r_{\mathrm{unit}}
 =B\{8A_w\log(8/\alpha_{\mathrm{st}})\}^{1/2}
  +\frac{8B}{3}M_w\log(8/\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}),
 \qquad r_{\mathrm{samp}}=r_{\mathrm{unit}}.
\]
For \(N\ge2\), form the sample covariance of the donor scores and spectrally clip it to \([c_\Sigma/2,2C_\Sigma]\), as in the definition of \(\widehat\Sigma\), and put
\[
 q_{\mathrm{don}}
 =\left\{\frac{32SdC_\Sigma}{c_\Sigma\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}}\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
Define
\[
 r_{\mathrm{bias}}
 =C_H\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_sK_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}
 \left(\|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\kappa
       +\widehat h_{s\ell}^{\lambda}\right),
 \qquad
 r_{\mathrm{don}}=C_H(q_{\mathrm{don}}/\sqrt N)^\kappa,
\]
and
\[
 R_\alpha=r_{\mathrm{samp}}+r_{\mathrm{bias}}+r_{\mathrm{don}},
 \qquad
 C_\alpha=[\widehat\tau-R_\alpha,\widehat\tau+R_\alpha]\cap[-2B,2B].
\]
If \(N<2\), some \(n_s<2\), a candidate set \(\mathcal A_{s\ell}\) is empty, or any numerical input is undefined or nonfinite, set \(C_\alpha=[-2B,2B]\).  Thus the procedure is total, uses only unit-history bounded-range concentration for its sampling radius, and never divides by an estimated variance.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:sharp-constant-handle]\label{def:sharp-constant-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Fixed-\allowbreak{}query product-\allowbreak{}experiment ordered-\allowbreak{}modulus handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} At a fixed admissible query \(z_0\), solve the ordered-modulus problem for the product of the treated-mean, bounded replicated-regression, realized-design, and full-covariance donor-location experiments indexed by labelled points
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), using \(\omega_P\) for the covariance-standardized donor coordinate.  Invert the resulting least-favorable tests using either the whole unit history as the stochastic coordinate or a valid deterministic product-experiment test.  The rate certificate \(R_D\) is not itself this exact ordered modulus, and the statistical target becomes the causal target \(\tau(z_0)\) only through consistency and transport.  Every candidate estimator and interval remains one \(\mathcal O\)-measurable rule selected before \(z_0\) and \(\xi\); it may not inspect potential outcomes or nuisance labels.  No calendar-block score, response-block independence, or query-null conditional version is part of this handle.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fixed-design-point-modulus]\label{def:fixed-design-point-modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Cohort fixed-\allowbreak{}design point modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Write
\[
 \mathcal F_{\lambda,L,B}
 =\{f\in\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L):\|f\|_\infty\le B\}.
\]
For a fixed exact design array \(X=(X_{st})\), a cohort \(s\), an
evaluation point \(x\in[0,1]^d\), and \(\varepsilon\ge0\), define
\[
 \omega_{s,X}(\varepsilon;x)
 =\sup\left\{|f(x)-g(x)|:
 f,g\in\mathcal F_{\lambda,L,B},\quad
 n_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}\{f(X_{st})-g(X_{st})\}^2\le\varepsilon^2
 \right\}.
\]
This is a fixed-design optimal-recovery modulus.  The factor \(n_s\)
records the replicated-response testing information; it is not a variance
assumption about the calendar sequence.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{X\_\allowbreak{}st}; \texttt{s}; \texttt{n\_\allowbreak{}s}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:joint-product-modulus]\label{def:joint-product-modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}covariance conditional product modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(f=(f_1,\ldots,f_S)\in\mathcal F_{\lambda,L,B}^{S}\) and
\(z=(z_1^\top,\ldots,z_S^\top)^\top\in([0,1]^d)^S\), put
\[
 \mathcal T(f,z)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s f_s(z_s).
\]
For a fixed exact design \(X=(X_{st})\), an interior reference path
\(z_0\), a full covariance \(\Sigma\), and \(\varepsilon\ge0\), define the
directed local product-experiment modulus
\[
\begin{split}
 \Omega_X(\varepsilon;z_0,\Sigma)
 =\sup\bigg\{&|\mathcal T(f,z_0)-\mathcal T(g,z)|:
 f,g\in\mathcal F_{\lambda,L,B}^{S},\ z\in([0,1]^d)^S,\\
 &\sum_{s=1}^{S}n_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}
       \{f_s(X_{st})-g_s(X_{st})\}^2
   +N\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(z-z_0)\|_2^2\le\varepsilon^2
 \bigg\}.
\end{split}
\]
Thus the response and generated-center coordinates share one constant
testing-distance budget.  The second term uses the full stacked covariance,
including every cross-coordinate, cross-horizon, and cross-cohort entry.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{X\_\allowbreak{}st}; \texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}0}; \texttt{Sigma}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:optimal-recovery-rate]\label{def:optimal-recovery-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Conditional optimal-\allowbreak{}recovery rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each cohort let
\[
 \rho_{sX}(x)=\min_{1\le t\le T}\|X_{st}-x\|_2,
 \qquad
 D_X(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\rho_{sX}(z_{0s})^\lambda,
 \qquad
 A=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}.
\]
The conditional optimal-recovery squared-risk scale is
\[
 R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)
 =A\vee\Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)^2.
\]
The finite nearest-site distances give a computable characterization of this
scale for \(0<\lambda\le1\), as proved in
\texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry}.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{X\_\allowbreak{}st}; \texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}0}; \texttt{Sigma}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:nearest-design-estimator]\label{def:nearest-design-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Rough-\allowbreak{}Holder nearest-\allowbreak{}design optimal-\allowbreak{}recovery estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every donor fold \(\ell\) and cohort \(s\), let
\[
 t_{s\ell}
 =\min\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\le t\le T}
       \|X_{st}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2,
 \qquad
 \overline Q_{st}=n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}Q_{sti},
\]
where the minimum in the first display is the smallest calendar index among
ties.  Define
\[
 \widehat m_s^{\mathrm{opt}}
 =K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}\overline Q_{s,t_{s\ell}},
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}
 =\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 -\widehat m_s^{\mathrm{opt}}\right\}.
\]
This measurable estimator is total for every \(N,T,n_s\ge1\).  It is the
rough-Holder optimal-recovery witness; the smoother anchor/extrapolation
estimator \(\widehat\tau\) remains the marginal-rate witness.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{X\_\allowbreak{}st}; \texttt{Q\_\allowbreak{}sti}; \texttt{B\_\allowbreak{}si\_\allowbreak{}obs}; \texttt{tilde\_\allowbreak{}z\_\allowbreak{}ell}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fixed-design-induced-experiment]\label{def:fixed-design-induced-experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Deterministic induced fixed-\allowbreak{}design product experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix a deterministic array \(X=(X_{st})_{1\le s\le S,1\le t\le T}\), an
interior reference path \(z_0\), and
\(\Sigma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Sd}\).  The local deterministic product
experiment \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) consists of labelled parameter
points \(\xi=(\mathbb Q_\xi;\mu,f,z)\), where \(\mathbb Q_\xi\) is a law of
\[
 \mathcal O_X=\bigl((E_{si})_{s,i},(Q_{sti})_{s,t,i},(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\bigr)
\]
labelled by \((\mu,f,z)\), where
\(\mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_S)\in[-B,B]^S\), every
\(f_s\in\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\) satisfies \(\|f_s\|_\infty\le B\), and
\[
 z\in([0,1]^d)^S,\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}(z_s,\partial[0,1]^d)\ge\delta_0,
 \qquad N\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(z-z_0)\|_2^2\le1.
\]
Under \(\mathbb Q_\xi\), \(|E_{si}|\vee|Q_{sti}|\le B\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}E_{si}=\mu_s,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}Q_{sti}=f_s(X_{st}),
\]
the histories \(((E_{si},(Q_{sti})_{t=1}^{T}))_{s,i}\) are independent
across \((s,i)\), with arbitrary dependence within a history, and the donor
sample is independent of these histories and is independent and identically
distributed with
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}Z_j=z,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb Q_\xi}(Z_j)=\Sigma.
\]
The statistical target attached to \(\xi\) is
\[
 \theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)
 =\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-f_s(z_s)\}.
\]
The deterministic specifications \(X,z_0,\Sigma\) are known inputs to a
decision rule in this experiment.  An estimator may depend on those
specifications and on \(\mathcal O_X\), and is selected only before the
unknown labelled point \(\xi\); it may not inspect \((\mu,f,z)\).  This is
distinct from the outer generated-query fixed-kernel frontier, where one
\(\mathcal O\)-measurable rule is selected before the unknown donor mean
\(z_0\) and labelled kernel.  The deterministic experiment does not select a
regular conditional version at a null design.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}wealth}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}regression}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}cross-\allowbreak{}unit-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}centering}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}training-\allowbreak{}separation}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}unbiasedness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}ellipticity}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:iid-calendar-subclass]\label{def:iid-calendar-subclass}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Fixed-\allowbreak{}query independent-\allowbreak{}calendar subclass}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each fixed admissible query \(z_0\), define

 \mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)
 =\left\{\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)

\begin{definition}[def:fixed-query-kernel-class]\label{def:fixed-query-kernel-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix a deterministic query
\(z_0=(z_{01}^{\top},\ldots,z_{0S}^{\top})^{\top}\in([0,1]^d)^S\), and let
\[
 \mathcal O=\sigma\bigl((X_{st})_{s,t},(Q_{sti})_{s,t,i},
 (B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}})_{s,i},(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\bigr)
\]
be the common observable sigma-field.  Define
\(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) to be the class of labelled points
\[
 \xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma),
 \qquad \mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_S),
 \qquad m=(m_1,\ldots,m_S),                                    \tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathbb K_\xi\) is a causal-data kernel at the fixed query whose
observable restriction is a probability law on \(\mathcal O\), and
\[
 \mu_s=\mu_{1s}(z_0)
 =\mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 \quad\text{for every }i.                                      \tag{2}
\]
For every \(s,i\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(z_{0s},\partial[0,1]^d)\ge\delta_0,
 \qquad B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)
     \ \mathbb K_\xi\text{-almost surely},
 \qquad \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}B_{si}(0)=m_s(z_{0s}),       \tag{3}
\]
\(|Q_{sti}|\vee|B_{si}(0)|\vee|B_{si}(1)|\le B\) almost
surely, \(m_s\in\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\), and every \(X_{st}\) is
observed exactly.  For every \(s,t\), \(X_{st}\) has a Lebesgue density
\(f_{st\mid z_0}\) satisfying
\[
 c_X\le f_{st\mid z_0}(x)\le C_X
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }x\in[0,1]^d,             \tag{4}
\]
and \((X_{st})_{t=1}^{T}\) has beta-mixing coefficients
\(\beta_s(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k\) for every \(k\ge1\).  With
\(\mathcal X=\sigma((X_{uv})_{u,v})\), unit histories are conditionally
independent across \((s,i)\) given \(\mathcal X\), and
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[Q_{sti}\mid\mathcal X]=m_s(X_{st}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal X]=\mu_s.
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]
The donors are independent and identically distributed, are independent of
the entire joint panel
\(((X_{st})_{s,t},(Q_{sti})_{s,t,i},(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}})_{s,i})\), and
satisfy
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}Z_j=z_0,
 \qquad \Sigma=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb K_\xi}(Z_j),
 \qquad c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}.  \tag{6}
\]
Every estimator and random interval in an outer minimax, coverage, or
expected-length display over the generated-query class is required to be
\(\mathcal O\)-measurable and to be one rule selected before both the unknown
donor mean \(z_0\) and the unknown labelled point \(\xi\).  Such a rule may
not take \(z_0\), potential outcomes, or nuisance labels \((\mu,m,\Sigma)\)
as inputs.  This outer convention differs from the deterministic experiment
\(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\), where the fixed specification
\((X,z_0,\Sigma)\) is known before the rule is selected.  The outer
convention retains the donor-location channel.  The class consists of
labelled fixed-query points and does not choose a regular conditional version
for a panel law at a query-null value.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}event-\allowbreak{}date-\allowbreak{}transport}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}wealth}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}regression}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}path}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}density}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}cross-\allowbreak{}unit-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}centering}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}temporal-\allowbreak{}mixing}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}center-\allowbreak{}only-\allowbreak{}design}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}training-\allowbreak{}separation}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}unbiasedness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}ellipticity}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fixed-query-functional]\label{def:fixed-query-functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Fixed-\allowbreak{}query observed-\allowbreak{}law functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a labelled point
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), where the treated-replica mean is common and
\[
 \mu_s=\mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 \quad\text{for every }i,
\]
define
\[
 \theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)
 =\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}.                   \tag{1}
\]
This is an observed-law functional on the common observable sigma-field.  Indeed, \(\mu_s\) is the common mean of the observed bounded treated-replica coordinates.  If \(m_s\) and \(m_s^\dagger\) are two Holder-continuous versions of
\(\mathbb E[Q_{sti}\mid X_{st}=x]\) under the same observed law, then they agree almost surely under the law of \(X_{st}\).  The lower density bound \(c_X>0\) implies that they agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere.  Their difference is continuous; if it were nonzero at any point, it would be bounded away from zero on a relative open ball of positive Lebesgue measure, a contradiction.  Thus
\(m_s=m_s^\dagger\) everywhere on \([0,1]^d\), in particular at \(z_{0s}\), so (1) is uniquely determined by the observed law.  This definition does not identify a causal target.  For a labelled causal kernel, equality with \(\tau(z_0)\) follows from Lemma \texttt{lem:causal-identification} and its consistency and event-date-transport clauses.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{M\_\allowbreak{}z0}; \texttt{z\_\allowbreak{}0}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:causal-identification]\label{lem:causal-identification}
Let \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).  Then, for almost every admissible query \(z_0\) under its marginal query law,
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]-m_s(z_{0s})\right\}.
\]
At a fixed admissible query, every labelled causal point \(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) satisfies
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)
 =\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).  By \texttt{def:target}, at every query where the relevant conditional expectations exist,
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0]-
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]\right\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} gives, outside a marginally null set of queries, a value \(\mu_s(z_0)\) independent of the replica index such that
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]=\mu_s(z_0)
 \quad\text{for every }i.                                  \tag{2}
\]
The almost-sure equality in \texttt{ass:consistency} disintegrates to
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0]
 =\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0],               \tag{3}
\]
and \texttt{ass:event-date-transport} gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]=m_s(z_{0s}).                \tag{4}
\]
All expectations are finite by the bounded-support clause incorporated in \texttt{def:panel-model-class}.  At a fixed triangular-array index there are finitely many cohorts and replicas.  Intersecting the conull sets on which (2)--(4) hold and substituting into (1) proves
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]-m_s(z_{0s})
 \right\}                                                   \tag{5}
\]
for marginal-almost-every admissible query, and also proves that the right-hand side is independent of \(i\).

Now fix an admissible query and a labelled point
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  Equations (2)--(3) of
\texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class} state, for every replica,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\mu_s,
 \qquad B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)quad
 \mathbb K_\xi\text{-almost surely},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}B_{si}(0)=m_s(z_{0s}).           \tag{6}
\]
Applying \texttt{def:target} to (6) yields
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}
 =\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0),                           \tag{7}
\]
where the last equality is exactly \texttt{def:fixed-query-functional}.  Thus the causal target is related to the observed-law functional by consistency and event-date transport; the equality is not created by the definition of the observed-law functional.  No density, mixing, donor-moment, rank, inversion, or division condition is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Consistency and transport identify the causal contrast for the labelled point \(\xi_P(z_0)\) after centering makes the treated mean common across replicas. \textit{Gap.} Observed-law uniqueness of \(m_s(z_{0s})\) does not verify event-date transport. \textit{Consumer.} Headline results first target \(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}\) and invoke this lemma only after the labelled transfer point has been constructed.

\begin{theorem}[thm:conditional-frontier]\label{thm:conditional-frontier}
Suppose \(0<\lambda\le1\), so \(\kappa=\lambda\), and fix the class
constants.  For every deterministic exact design \(X=(X_{st})\), every
interior reference path \(z_0\), and every
\(c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}\), consider the
deterministic experiment \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) in
\texttt{def:fixed-design-induced-experiment}.  Its nearest-design estimator
satisfies
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right]
 \le
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right]
 \le C\left\{\sum_s\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
          \vee D_X(z_0)^2\vee N^{-\lambda}\right\}.
\]
If \(\delta_0<1/2\), the last display is at most
\(CR_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)\), and
\[
 cR_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)
 \le
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right].
\]
Neither bound uses a lower local-modulus condition.

For every fixed admissible \(z_0\) and every
\(\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), the conditional law under
\(\mathbb K_\xi\) given the exact design, with its labels, belongs to
\(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) for \(\mathbb K_\xi\)-almost every design,
and \(\theta_{\mathfrak E}=\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\).  Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \left[\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}
 -\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2\mid(X_{st})\right]
 \le C\left\{\sum_s\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
          \vee D_X(z_0)^2\vee N^{-\lambda}\right\}
\]
for almost every design; under \(\delta_0<1/2\), its right side is at most
\(CR_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\).  If every cohort design contains \(z_{0s}\)
exactly, the displayed risk is at most
\(C\{\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\vee N^{-\lambda}\}\), and
\(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\le C\{\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\vee N^{-\lambda}\}\).
If instead \(g_{sT}(z_0)<1\) for every cohort, then
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \left[\{\widehat\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2
 \mid(X_{st})\right]\le CR_D,
 \qquad R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\le CR_D.
\]
No converse in terms of \(R_D\) is asserted.

For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), the labelled point
\(\xi_P(z_0)\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) for almost
every admissible \(z_0\) under the marginal law of the query, and
\(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0)=\tau(z_0)\) there.  Hence all
preceding conditional upper bounds transfer to \(P\) at those query values
and at \(P(\cdot\mid z_0)\)-almost every exact design; no conditional version
at a null query is selected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(q=Sd\) and abbreviate
\[
 A=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s},
 \qquad
 \rho_s=\rho_{sX}(z_{0s}),
 \qquad
 D_X=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\rho_s^\lambda.                   \tag{1}
\]
Every \(n_s\) and \(\pi_s\) is positive, so \(A>0\).  We first work in the deterministic experiment \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) from \texttt{def:fixed-design-induced-experiment}.  Here \(X,z_0,\Sigma\) are known specifications, but the decision rule is selected before the unknown label \((\mu,f,z)\).

For the estimator in \texttt{def:nearest-design-estimator}, condition on the donor sample and define
\[
 U_{si}=E_{si}-\mu_s-K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}
 \{Q_{s,t_{s\ell},i}-f_s(X_{s,t_{s\ell}})\}.               \tag{2}
\]
Donor--history independence makes the indices \(t_{s\ell}\) fixed under this conditioning.  The mean restrictions in the experiment center every \(U_{si}\), and history independence makes the collection independent across \((s,i)\).  The support restrictions imply \(|E_{si}-\mu_s|\le2B\) and \(|Q_{sti}-f_s(X_{st})|\le2B\), hence \(|U_{si}|\le4B\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb Q_\xi}\!\left(
 \sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}U_{si}
 \mathrel{\Big|}(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\right)
 \le16B^2A.                                                 \tag{3}
\]

Let \(t_s^0\) be the smallest nearest design index to \(z_{0s}\).  Nearest-site optimality, followed by two triangle inequalities, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|X_{s,t_{s\ell}}-z_s\|_2
 &\le \|X_{s,t_s^0}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2
       +\|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_s\|_2\\
 &\le \rho_s+\|z_s-z_{0s}\|_2
       +2\|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_s\|_2.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{4}
\]
Because \(0<\lambda\le1\), the map \(x\mapsto x^\lambda\) is subadditive.  The Holder restriction consequently yields
\[
 |f_s(X_{s,t_{s\ell}})-f_s(z_s)|
 \le C\left\{\rho_s^\lambda+
 \|z_s-z_{0s}\|_2^\lambda+
 \|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_s\|_2^\lambda\right\}.        \tag{5}
\]
Projection onto the closed cube is nonexpansive relative to every \(z\) in that cube.  Every balanced fold has size at least \(N/3\), including the cases \(N=1,2\).  Donor unbiasedness and \(\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)\le qC_\Sigma\) thus imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}\|\widetilde z_\ell-z\|_2^2
 \le\mathbb E\|\overline Z_\ell-z\|_2^2
 =\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)}{|I_\ell|}
 \le\frac{3qC_\Sigma}{N}.                                  \tag{6}
\]
The local experiment also imposes
\[
 \|z-z_0\|_2^2
 \le C_\Sigma\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(z-z_0)\|_2^2
 \le C_\Sigma N^{-1}.                                      \tag{7}
\]
Expanding \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\) gives the centered sum in (3) plus the weighted approximation errors in (5).  Jensen's inequality applied to (6)--(7), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}-
 \theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right]
 \le C\{A+D_X^2+N^{-\lambda}\}.                           \tag{8}
\]
The estimator uses only the observations and the known specifications, so it is an admissible witness for the infimum.  If \(\delta_0<1/2\), \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} identifies the right side of (8), up to fixed constants, with \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\).

We next prove the minimax converse.  We repeatedly use the following elementary two-point calculation.  If two laws \(P_0,P_1\) have targets \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), separation \(\Delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\), and total variation at most \(1-\eta\), then, with \(A_0=\{|\widetilde\theta-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{v=0,1}\mathbb E_v(\widetilde\theta-\theta_v)^2
 &\ge\frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{P_0(A_0^c)+P_1(A_0)\}\\
 &\ge\frac{\eta\Delta^2}{8}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{9}
\]
The first inequality follows because the two open balls of radius \(\Delta/2\) are disjoint; the second is the defining testing characterization of total variation.

For the treated-replication channel, take \(f_s=0\), \(Q_{sti}=0\), \(z=z_0\), and a common donor law with mean \(z_0\) and covariance \(\Sigma\), for example \(z_0+\Sigma^{1/2}R\) with independent Rademacher coordinates in \(R\).  Fix \(0<b<B\).  Under \(v\in\{-1,1\}\), let all \(E_{si}\) be independent, supported on \(\{-b,b\}\), and satisfy
\[
 \Pr_v(E_{si}=b)=\frac12+v\varepsilon_s,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_s=\frac{\gamma\pi_s}{bn_s\sqrt A}.            \tag{10}
\]
Since \(A\ge\pi_s^2/n_s\), a sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma>0\) puts every probability in \([1/4,3/4]\).  The two targets differ by
\[
 4b\sum_s\pi_s\varepsilon_s=4\gamma\sqrt A.                \tag{11}
\]
The Hellinger affinity of the observed products is
\[
 \prod_{s=1}^{S}(1-4\varepsilon_s^2)^{n_s/2}
 \ge \exp(-C\gamma^2/b^2)>0,                                \tag{12}
\]
because \(\sum_sn_s\varepsilon_s^2=\gamma^2/b^2\).  For any dominated laws with affinity \(a\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) gives total variation at most \(\sqrt{1-a^2}\).  Hence (9)--(12) give a lower bound \(cA\).

For the fixed-design channel, let
\[
 q_s(x)=a_0\min_{1\le t\le T}\|x-X_{st}\|_2^\lambda,
 \qquad f_s=q_s/2,
 \qquad g_s=-q_s/2,                                         \tag{13}
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is small enough for the Holder and support radii.  The distance to a nonempty finite set is one-Lipschitz, and
\(|u^\lambda-v^\lambda|\le|u-v|^\lambda\) for nonnegative \(u,v\).  Thus the two surfaces are admissible, they agree at every design site, and their values at \(z_{0s}\) differ by \(a_0\rho_s^\lambda\).  Taking deterministic zero responses, identical event laws, identical donors, and \(z=z_0\) makes the entire observation laws identical while separating the targets by \(a_0D_X\).  Equation (9) with \(\eta=1\) yields the lower bound \(cD_X^2\).

For the donor channel, fix \(\eta\in(0,1)\) and invoke \texttt{lem:full-covariance-location-pair}, reducing its displacement constant if necessary so that its Mahalanobis budget is at most one.  Choose the displaced coordinate in a cohort \(s_0\) and write it as coordinate \(k\).  Use the same regression under both alternatives,
\[
 f_{s_0}(x)=a_1|x_k-z_{0,s_0k}|^\lambda,
 \qquad f_s(x)=0\quad(s\ne s_0),                            \tag{14}
\]
with \(a_1>0\) small enough for the Holder and support bounds.  Keep all response and event laws identical and vary only the donor law.  If \(e\) is the signed displaced coordinate, the lemma and ellipticity give
\[
 |z_{1,e}-z_{0,e}|=
 \frac{c_\eta N^{-1/2}}{\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2}
 \ge c_\eta c_\Sigma^{1/2}N^{-1/2}.                       \tag{15}
\]
The donor products have total variation at most \(1-\eta\), whereas (14)--(15) separate the targets by at least \(cN^{-\lambda/2}\).  Equation (9) yields the lower bound \(cN^{-\lambda}\).  Combining the three subexperiments gives
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right]
 \ge c\{A\vee D_X^2\vee N^{-\lambda}\}.                  \tag{16}
\]
Under \(\delta_0<1/2\), \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} says that the last maximum is equivalent to \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\), proving the asserted lower bound.  Notice that no donor moment beyond the specified covariance was imposed on the experiment; the finite-support laws in \texttt{lem:full-covariance-location-pair} are only witnesses.

Now fix \(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) and condition on the exact design sigma-field \(\mathcal X\).  Definition \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class} gives donor--panel independence, conditional independence of histories across \((s,i)\), and
\[
 \mathbb E[Q_{sti}\mid\mathcal X]=m_s(X_{st}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal X]=\mu_s.     \tag{17}
\]
Thus, for almost every design, the conditional observed law with its labels is the central point \(z=z_0\) of \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\).  Because \(|Q_{sti}|\le B\), (17) gives \(|m_s(X_{st})|\le B\) almost surely.  The positive design density makes this true Lebesgue-almost everywhere, and Holder continuity extends it to every \(x\in[0,1]^d\); hence the experiment's sup-norm condition holds.  Moreover,
\[
 \theta_{\mathfrak E}
 =\sum_s\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}
 =\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0).                           \tag{18}
\]
Applying (8) conditionally proves the nearest-design upper bound.  If every cohort design contains \(z_{0s}\), then \(D_X=0\), and (8) and \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} give both exact-site conclusions.

Suppose instead that \(g_{sT}(z_0)<1\) for every cohort.  Since \(0<\lambda\le1\), the order convention gives \(r=0\).  The pair \((z_{0s},h_s^\star)\) is then feasible in every foldwise anchor search.  Optimality of the selected certificate gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &C_H\|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 +C_H\widehat h_{s\ell}^\lambda
 +C_V(Tn_s\widehat h_{s\ell}^d)^{-1/2}\\
 &\quad\le C_H\|z_{0s}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 +C_H(h_s^\star)^\lambda
 +C_V\{Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d\}^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{19}
\]
The bandwidth floor in \(\mathcal B_s\) implies
\[
 \{Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d\}^{-1/2}\le(h_s^\star)^\lambda.      \tag{20}
\]
Scalar Gram coercivity makes every selected local-constant denominator positive.  Hence its weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and clipping is inactive for responses in \([-B,B]\).  Holder continuity bounds its approximation bias by the first two terms on the left of (19).  After averaging folds, the centered response and treated terms can again be grouped into one bounded variable per unit history; (17) and cross-unit independence bound their total variance by \(CA\), without any response-calendar independence.  Finally, donor unbiasedness, projection contraction, and ellipticity give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^2
 \le\frac{3qC_\Sigma}{N}.                                  \tag{21}
\]
Using (19)--(21), Jensen's inequality for the exponent \(\lambda\le1\), and the definition of \(R_D\), we obtain
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\!\left[
 \{\widehat\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal X\right]\le CR_D.               \tag{22}
\]
The last assertion of \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} also gives \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\le CR_D\).  This is only an upper certificate and proves no converse in terms of \(R_D\).

Finally take \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).  Lemma \texttt{lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer} supplies, for marginal-almost-every admissible query, the labelled point
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)=\bigl(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,
 \Sigma_P(z_0)\bigr)\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0),    \tag{23}
\]
not membership of the bare conditional kernel.  Lemma \texttt{lem:causal-identification} gives
\(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0)=\tau(z_0)\) on the same conull set.  Equations (18), (22), and the nearest-site bound therefore transfer to the causal conditional problem at those query values and at conditional-almost-every exact design.  No regular conditional version is selected at a query-null value, and none of these one-sided upper transfers asserts a panel-induced conditional converse.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} \textbf{Comparison.} Donoho studies optimal recovery in a known Gaussian convex experiment \cite{Donoho1994}, and Stone supplies the classical smooth-regression benchmark \cite{Stone1982}. This theorem instead couples bounded multiple-response fixed-design observations to an independent full-covariance donor-location experiment. Exact historical coordinates turn a root-\(N\) external-query displacement into an order \(N^{-\lambda/2}\) rough-Holder evaluation displacement and hence an \(N^{-\lambda}\) risk channel without deconvolution. Unlike the strongly convex estimated-minimizer problem of Akhavan, Gogolashvili, and Tsybakov \cite{AkhavanGogolashviliTsybakov2024}, the perturbation is first order in the Holder modulus. The new content is the joint fixed-design optimal-recovery comparison and its one-sided marginal-almost-everywhere panel-kernel transfer, not a two-sided conditional theorem over panel-induced laws. \textit{Gap.} Known Gaussian recovery and strongly convex minimizer theory do not supply this bounded replicated-response/full-covariance product experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The marginal theorem adds query-conditional occupancy and an outer rule chosen before the donor mean.

\begin{theorem}[thm:marginal-phase-diagram]\label{thm:marginal-phase-diagram}
Suppose \(0<\lambda\le1\), so \(\kappa=\lambda\).  Uniformly over fixed
admissible query values and their conditional-kernel classes, the anchor
estimator obeys
\[
 \sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 [\{\widehat\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2]
 \le CR_\star.
\]
The estimator is a single data-measurable rule and does not use the query or
nuisance labels.  This upper bound uses no lower local-modulus condition.  If
\(\delta_0<1/2\), then, still over the base Holder class,
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau}
 \sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 [\{\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2]
 \asymp R_\star
 \asymp
 \left\{\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}\right\}
 \vee\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{2\lambda/d}
 \vee N^{-\lambda},
\]
where the infimum is over one data-measurable rule chosen before both
suprema.  Thus the nonredundant oracle/generated-proxy boundary is
\[
 N^{-\lambda}\asymp
 \left\{\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\right\}
 \vee\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{2\lambda/d}.
\]
The smoothing component in \(R_{\mathrm{or}}\) is algebraically dominated
for fixed \(S\) and is not a fourth nonredundant phase.  On the fixed-query
independent-calendar subclasses, both spacing displays replace the
logarithmic term by \(T^{-2\lambda/d}\).  In the scalar specialization
\(S=1\), \(H=0\), \(p=d=1\), and \(n_1=n\), the unreduced bookkeeping form is
\[
 n^{-1}\vee(Tn)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+1)}
 \vee T^{-2\lambda}\vee N^{-\lambda},
\]
and its second term is dominated by the maximum of the first and third.

Separately, for every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), the labelled point
\(\xi_P(z_0)=(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,\Sigma_P(z_0))\) belongs to
\(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) for almost every admissible query under
the marginal law of \(z_0\).  At each such query,
\(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0)=\tau(z_0)\), and the displayed
upper-risk conclusion applies to \(P(\cdot\mid z_0)\).  No supremum over
\(P\)-specific null conditional values is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 A=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s},\qquad
 U_s=(Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)},\qquad
 D_T=\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{2\lambda/d}.       \tag{1}
\]
All \(n_s\) and \(\pi_s\) are positive and \(S\) is fixed, so \(A>0\)
and constants below may depend on \(S\) and
\(\min_s\pi_s>0\).  Fix an admissible query \(z_0\) and a labelled point
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\) in
\(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  The decision rule in
\texttt{def:frontier-estimator} is measurable with respect to the common
observable sigma-field \(\mathcal O\): its donor folds, projections, finite
bandwidth grids, lexicographic choices, clipping, and fallbacks are all
deterministic.  In particular, the same rule is selected before the unknown
\(z_0\) and \(\xi\).

We first prove the design-occupancy bound used by the estimator.  Since
\(0<\lambda\le1\), the order-reduction convention gives \(r=0\), so the
local Gram matrix is scalar.  Put
\[
 a_K=c_Xv_d\eta_K^d.
\]
For \(0<b\le1\), the ball
\(\{x:\|x-z_{0s}\|_2\le\eta_Kb\}\) is contained in \([0,1]^d\), by
\(\eta_K\le\delta_0\) and the interior condition.  The conditional density
lower bound therefore gives, for every date,
\[
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \{\|X_{st}-z_{0s}\|_2\le\eta_Kb\}
 \ge a_Kb^d.                                                   \tag{2}
\]
Let \(M_s(b)\) be the number of these visits.  On
\(M_s(b)\ge a_KTb^d/2\), the kernel-ball bound implies
\[
 G_{sT}(z_{0s},b)
 \ge \frac{k_KM_s(b)}{Tb^d}
 \ge\frac12k_Ka_K>2c_G,                                      \tag{3}
\]
where the strict last inequality is exactly
\(0<c_G<k_Kc_Xv_d\eta_K^d/4\).  Thus a visit-count event supplies a
strictly coercive candidate.

For completeness, we control that count directly from conditional absolute
regularity.  Choose an integer
\(L_T\le T\) of order \(\log(T\vee2)\) such that
\(T\beta_0\rho_0^{L_T}\le CT^{-3}\), with small values of \(T\) absorbed
into \(C\).  Split the dates into the \(L_T\) residue classes modulo
\(L_T\).  If \(t_1<\cdots<t_m\) are in one residue class, the definition of
the beta-mixing coefficient and successive conditioning give
\[
 \left\|\mathcal L(X_{st_1},\ldots,X_{st_m}\mid z_0)
 -\bigotimes_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal L(X_{st_r}\mid z_0)
 \right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le(m-1)\beta_0\rho_0^{L_T}.                                \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, adjoining one coordinate changes the joint law from the product of
its past and its next-coordinate marginal by at most the absolute-regularity
coefficient at the separating lag; the triangle inequality iterates this
bound.  Under the product law, the visit indicators are independent and
each has success probability at least \(a_Kb^d\).  Applying exponential
Markov with \(u=\log2\) to the negative of their sum yields
\[
 \Pr\left\{\sum_{r=1}^{m}I_r<\frac12ma_Kb^d\right\}
 \le \exp(-cma_Kb^d),                                        \tag{5}
\]
because
\(\mathbb E e^{-uI_r}\le
 \exp[-a_Kb^d(1-e^{-u})]\) and
\(u/2<1-e^{-u}\).  Combining (4)--(5) over residue classes gives
\[
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \{M_s(b)<a_KTb^d/2\}
 \le L_T\exp\{-cTb^d/L_T\}+CT^{-3}.                         \tag{6}
\]
By (3), (6), and a dyadic tail summation over the grid in
\texttt{def:realized-geometry},
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}
 \le C\left\{
 (Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda}+D_T
 \right\}.                                                    \tag{7}
\]
To see the summation explicitly, the smallest grid point is between
\(b_{0s}\) and \(2b_{0s}\), while (6) bounds the upper tail at a dyadic
\(b\) by \(L_Te^{-cTb^d/L_T}+CT^{-3}\).  Splitting the dyadic sum at
\(b\asymp(L_T/T)^{1/d}\) gives
\(C\{b_{0s}^{2\lambda}+(L_T/T)^{2\lambda/d}+T^{-2}\}\), and
\(b_{0s}^{2\lambda}\le
C\{(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda}\}\).
The residual \(T^{-2}\) is dominated by the displayed spacing term because
\(2\lambda/d\le2\).  Taking \(b=1\) in the same argument also makes every
anchor set nonempty except on an event of probability at most \(CT^{-2}\).
When the dates are conditionally independent, (5) applies to all \(T\)
dates at once; the identical dyadic calculation replaces \(D_T\) by
\(T^{-2\lambda/d}\).  Only the design sequence has been mixed in this
argument.

We next establish the risk bound.  Let
\(\mathcal G=\{g_{sT}(z_0)<1\text{ for every }s\}\).  On this event,
\((z_{0s},h_s^\star)\) is an admissible comparator in every foldwise
anchor search.  At \(r=0\), Gram coercivity makes the fitted intercept a
nonnegative weighted average with weights summing to one.  Clipping is
therefore inactive for responses in \([-B,B]\).  Conditional on the exact
design and the donors, these weights are response-independent.  If
\(w_{st}^{(\ell)}\) denotes the foldwise weight, kernel support and Holder
continuity give
\[
 \left|\sum_tw_{st}^{(\ell)}m_s(X_{st})
       -m_s(\widetilde z_{\ell s})\right|
 \le C\left\{\widehat h_{s\ell}^{\lambda}
       +\|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]
Optimality of the response-independent certificate against the comparator,
and the bandwidth floor, imply
\[
 \widehat h_{s\ell}^{\lambda}
 +\|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 \le C\left\{(h_s^\star)^\lambda
       +\|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_{0s}\|_2^\lambda\right\},       \tag{9}
\]
because
\(\{Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d\}^{-1/2}\le(h_s^\star)^\lambda\).
Another Holder step from \(\widetilde z_{\ell s}\) to \(z_{0s}\) then
bounds the total conditional bias by
\[
 C\sum_s\pi_s\left\{(h_s^\star)^\lambda
 +K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}
   \|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_{0s}\|_2^\lambda\right\}.          \tag{10}
\]

The stochastic term can be grouped by complete unit history.  Conditional
on the design and donors, define
\[
 V_{si}=\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}\left[
 B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mu_s
 -K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}\sum_t w_{st}^{(\ell)}
       \{Q_{sti}-m_s(X_{st})\}\right].                          \tag{11}
\]
Design-conditional centering and donor--panel independence make (11)
centered.  Conditional cross-unit independence makes the variables
independent across \((s,i)\), while allowing arbitrary dependence between
all coordinates within one history.  Since each bracket has absolute value
at most \(4B\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \left(\sum_{s,i}V_{si}\mid(X_{st}),(Z_j)\right)
 \le16B^2A.                                                     \tag{12}
\]
No response-calendar mixing is used.

Every balanced fold has size at least \(N/3\).  Donor unbiasedness,
covariance ellipticity, and nonexpansiveness of projection onto the cube give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^2
 \le \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)}{|I_\ell|}
 \le\frac{3SdC_\Sigma}{N}.                                    \tag{13}
\]
Since \(0<\lambda\le1\), Jensen's inequality applied to the power
\(\lambda\) converts (13) into an \(O(N^{-\lambda})\) bound for the
second moment of every \(\lambda\)-Holder displacement in (10).  Equations
(10)--(13), followed by conditional Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, prove on
\(\mathcal G\) that
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 [\{\widehat\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2
   \mid(X_{st})]
 \le C\left[A+\left\{\sum_s\pi_s(h_s^\star)^\lambda\right\}^2
                  +N^{-\lambda}\right].                       \tag{14}
\]
Both the estimator and target lie in \([-2B,2B]\).  Thus the squared error
is at most \(16B^2\) on \(\mathcal G^c\).  Minkowski's inequality and (7)
give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\left\{\sum_s\pi_s(h_s^\star)^\lambda\right\}^2
 &\le C\left[\left\{\sum_s\pi_sU_s\right\}^2+D_T+N^{-\lambda}\right],\\
 \Pr(\mathcal G^c)
 &\le C\left[\left\{\sum_s\pi_sU_s\right\}^2+D_T+N^{-\lambda}\right].
                                                                    \tag{15}
 \end{split}
\]
For the second line, use \(\mathbf1\{h_s^\star=1\}\le
(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}\) and, because the positive weights and \(S\) are
fixed, \(\sum_sU_s^2\le C(\sum_s\pi_sU_s)^2\).  Integrating (14) and using
(15) proves uniformly over \(z_0\) and \(\xi\) that
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 [\{\widehat\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2]
 \le C\left[A+\left\{\sum_s\pi_sU_s\right\}^2
                 +D_T+N^{-\lambda}\right]
 \le CR_\star.                                                \tag{16}
\]
This calculation used only the donor mean and covariance; no fourth donor
moment or covariance-consistency rate occurs.

The smoothing term in (16) is redundant.  Set
\(a_s=n_s^{-1/2}\) and \(b=T^{-\lambda/d}\).  Direct exponent arithmetic
and the weighted geometric-mean inequality give
\[
 U_s=a_s^{2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}b^{d/(2\lambda+d)}
 \le a_s\vee b.                                                \tag{17}
\]
Consequently, by Cauchy--Schwarz and fixed \(S\),
\[
 \left(\sum_s\pi_sU_s\right)^2
 \le C\{A+T^{-2\lambda/d}\}
 \le C(A+D_T).                                                 \tag{18}
\]
Equations (16)--(18) give the asserted three-channel upper rate.

We now prove matching lower bounds.  We shall repeatedly use the following
elementary two-point inequality.  If observed laws \(P_0,P_1\) have target
separation \(\Delta\) and total variation at most \(1-\eta\), then every
estimator \(\widetilde\theta\) satisfies
\[
 \max_{v=0,1}\mathbb E_v(\widetilde\theta-\theta_v)^2
 \ge\frac{\eta\Delta^2}{8}.                                  \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, for
\(E=\{|\widetilde\theta-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\), squared error is at least
\(\Delta^2/4\) under \(P_0\) on \(E^c\) and under \(P_1\) on \(E\), while
\(P_0(E^c)+P_1(E)\ge1-\|P_1-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).

For the treated-replication channel set
\(z_0=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{Sd}\), use independent uniform design dates,
constant regression and untreated event wealth \(b_0\), and a common donor
law \(Z_j=z_0+U_j\) with independent Rademacher coordinates.  Choose
\(0<b<b_0\) with \(b_0+b<B\).  Under alternatives \(v\in\{-1,1\}\), let
the treated observations be independent across \((s,i)\), take values
\(b_0-b,b_0+b\), and satisfy
\[
 \Pr_v(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=b_0+b)=\frac12+v\varepsilon_s,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_s=\frac{\gamma\pi_s}{bn_s\sqrt A}.              \tag{20}
\]
Because \(A\ge\pi_s^2/n_s\), a fixed sufficiently small \(\gamma>0\)
makes \(|\varepsilon_s|\le1/4\).  Taking baseline wealth \(b_0\), untreated
return zero, the treated horizon-zero return equal to
\(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}/b_0-1>-1\), and all later returns zero verifies the
buy-and-hold construction, consistency, bounded support, and transport.
The constant regression is in the Holder ball after choosing \(b_0\) below
the fixed radii.  The uniform designs have density one and zero mixing; the
Rademacher donor has covariance \(I_{Sd}\), which is admissible since
\(c_\Sigma\le1\le C_\Sigma\), and has standardized fourth norm moment
exactly \((Sd)^2\), a property of this witness rather than a class
restriction.  All other sampling clauses hold by construction.

The two causal targets differ by \(4\gamma\sqrt A\).  The Hellinger affinity
of their treated-data laws is
\[
 \prod_{s=1}^{S}(1-4\varepsilon_s^2)^{n_s/2}
 \ge\exp(-C\gamma^2/b^2)>0,                                   \tag{21}
\]
because \(\sum_sn_s\varepsilon_s^2=\gamma^2/b^2\).  The
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
\(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\{1-(\int\sqrt{dP\,dQ})^2\}^{1/2}\) bounds total variation away from
one after decreasing \(\gamma\).  Equations (19)--(21) yield the lower bound
\(cA\).

For the donor-location channel put \(q=Sd\) and fix any
\(\epsilon_4>0\).  Set \(a_D^2=1+\epsilon_4/2\).  For \(|u|\) small define
\(A_u\in\{-a_D,0,a_D\}\) by
\[
 p_+(u)=\frac{1+u^2}{2a_D^2}+\frac{u}{2a_D},\quad
 p_-(u)=\frac{1+u^2}{2a_D^2}-\frac{u}{2a_D},\quad
 p_0(u)=1-\frac{1+u^2}{a_D^2}.                                \tag{22}
\]
These are positive near zero, and direct summation gives
\(\mathbb EA_u=u\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(A_u)=1\).  Adjoin independent
Rademacher variables \(R_2,\ldots,R_q\).  At \(u=0\),
\[
 \mathbb E\|(A_0,R_2,\ldots,R_q)\|_2^4
 =q^2-1+a_D^2=q^2+\epsilon_4/2.                               \tag{23}
\]
The centered fourth moment is continuous in \(u\), so it is at most
\(q^2+\epsilon_4\) on a smaller neighborhood.  Thus these finite-support
witnesses have exact covariance \(I_q\) and the requested local moment
slack, without imposing that slack on the model.

Let \(z^{(0)}=\tfrac12\mathbf1_q\),
\(z^{(1)}=z^{(0)}+c_*N^{-1/2}e_1\), and under alternative \(v\) give the
first donor coordinate the law \(1/2+A_{vc_*N^{-1/2}}\), with the remaining
coordinates \(1/2+R_k\).  The means are \(z^{(v)}\) and both covariances are
\(I_q\).  Strict slack \(\delta_0<1/2\) keeps both queries admissible for
large \(N\).  The square roots of the three probabilities in (22) have
bounded second derivative near zero; their one-observation affinity is
therefore at least \(1-Cc_*^2/N\), because its value and first derivative at
zero are one and zero.  Product factorization and the affinity--variation
inequality used after (21) keep the two donor-product laws a fixed distance
below total variation one when \(c_*>0\) is sufficiently small.

Use the same independent uniform training design, deterministic training
responses, and constant treated observations under both alternatives.  In
cohort one choose
\[
 m_1(x)=b_0+b_1(x_1-1/2)_+^\lambda,                           \tag{24}
\]
and use constant regressions elsewhere.  Subadditivity of the
\(\lambda\)-power verifies the \(\lambda\)-Holder condition; small positive
\(b_0,b_1\) verify the radius, wealth support, and return boundary.  Set the
unobserved untreated event wealth to \(m_s(z_s^{(v)})\), so event-date
transport holds.  The observed panel law is common, while (24) and the
donor-mean displacement separate the two causal targets by
\(\pi_1b_1c_*^\lambda N^{-\lambda/2}\).  Equation (19) yields the donor
lower bound \(cN^{-\lambda}\).  The infimum in the theorem must use one
observable rule before the query supremum, so it cannot use the differing
labels \(z^{(0)},z^{(1)}\).

For the geometric-mixing spacing channel, again take the common query
\(z_0=\tfrac12\mathbf1_q\) and the common Rademacher donor just used in
(20), whose standardized fourth norm moment is \(q^2\).  Choose
\(\gamma>0\) and put
\[
 L_T=\left\lfloor
 \frac{\log T-2\log\log(T\vee3)}{|\log\rho_0|}
 \right\rfloor\vee1,
 \qquad p_T=\frac{\gamma L_T}{T}.                             \tag{25}
\]
For all large \(T\),
\(2p_T\le\beta_0\rho_0^{L_T-1}\).  Partition the first cohort's calendar
into consecutive blocks of length \(L_T\), independently label each complete
block by \(C_b\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_T)\), and let \(A_T\) be a
cube centered at \(z_{01}\) with volume \(p_T\).  Conditional on \(C_b=1\),
draw that block's dates independently uniformly on \(A_T\); conditional on
\(C_b=0\), draw them independently uniformly on \(A_T^c\).  Every date then
has density
\[
 p_T\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T}(x)}{p_T}
 +(1-p_T)\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T^c}(x)}{1-p_T}=1                 \tag{26}
\]
almost everywhere.  At a lag below \(L_T\), past and future share at most
one block label; replacing its future copy by an independent copy costs at
most \(2p_T(1-p_T)\) in total variation.  At a lag at least \(L_T\), the
two sigma-fields use disjoint independent labels.  Hence (25) proves
\(\beta(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k\) at every lag.  Other cohorts and the
incomplete block may be drawn independently uniformly.

The event that all complete-block labels are zero, and that the incomplete
block misses a smaller concentric cube, has probability
\[
 (1-p_T)^{\lfloor T/L_T\rfloor}-o(1)\longrightarrow e^{-\gamma}>0. \tag{27}
\]
Let \(h_T\asymp p_T^{1/d}\) be the smaller cube's side scale and use the
explicit smooth bump
\[
 \phi(u)=
 \exp\!\left\{1-\frac{1}{1-\|u\|_2^2}\right\}
 \mathbf1\{\|u\|_2<1\}.
\]
It is supported on the unit ball and satisfies \(\phi(0)=1\).  The two
surfaces
\[
 m_{10}(x)=b_0,\qquad
 m_{11}(x)=b_0+ah_T^\lambda
                \phi((x-z_{01})/h_T)                         \tag{28}
\]
are uniformly \(\lambda\)-Holder when \(a>0\) is small.  Indeed, for
\(\|x-y\|_2\le h_T\), Lipschitz continuity of \(\phi\) bounds the increment
by \(Ca h_T^{\lambda-1}\|x-y\|_2\le Ca\|x-y\|_2^\lambda\);
for larger separations boundedness of \(\phi\) gives the same conclusion.
Choose \(a,b_0\) small enough for the Holder radius, bounded wealth, and
strict return boundary.  Set \(Q_{1ti}=m_{1v}(X_{1t})\), keep the treated
observations and all other regressions common and constant, and set untreated
event wealth to \(m_{sv}(z_{0s})\).  Given the exact design, the resulting
degenerate unit histories are independent and satisfy both centering
equalities.  Thus all model clauses have been verified explicitly.

On the event in (27), the two observed laws coincide, so they possess a
common subprobability measure of mass bounded away from zero.  Their causal
targets differ by
\[
 \pi_1ah_T^\lambda
 \asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}.    \tag{29}
\]
Equations (19) and (27)--(29) give the lower bound \(cD_T\).  Combining it
with the treated and donor lower bounds, and then using (18), proves
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 [\{\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2]
 \asymp A\vee D_T\vee N^{-\lambda}\asymp R_\star.          \tag{30}
\]

On the independent-calendar subclass, the independent version of the
occupancy argument gives the upper spacing term \(T^{-2\lambda/d}\).  For
the reverse inequality, use an independent uniform design and a cube of
radius \(r_T=cT^{-1/d}\) around \(z_{01}\).  Use the two surfaces in (28)
with \(h_T=r_T\).  For fixed sufficiently small \(c>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{X_{1t}\text{ misses the bump support for every }t\}
 =(1-v_dr_T^d)^T\ge c_0>0.                                   \tag{31}
\]
On this event the observed laws coincide, while their targets differ by
\(c_1T^{-\lambda/d}\).  Equation (19) proves the matching iid spacing
lower bound.

In the scalar iid specialization \(S=1\), \(H=0\), \(p=d=1\), and
\(n_1=n\), the unreduced terms in (16) are
\[
 n^{-1}\vee(Tn)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+1)}
 \vee T^{-2\lambda}\vee N^{-\lambda}.
\]
Equation (18) proves that the second is bounded by a fixed multiple of the
maximum of the first and third terms.

Finally let \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\) and choose a regular
conditional law \(\mathbb K_{P,z_0}=P(\,\cdot\mid z_0)\).  The conditional
density and design-mixing clauses hold on a marginally conull query set by
their assumptions.  Consistency, bounded support, the centering equalities,
cross-unit independence, donor independence and separation, unbiasedness,
and covariance ellipticity disintegrate on conull sets; Holder membership is
a property of the retained label.  Intersecting these finitely many sets and
the countably many lagwise mixing sets shows that
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)=(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,\Sigma_P(z_0))
 \in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)                           \tag{32}
\]
for marginal-almost-every admissible query.  This is membership of the
labelled tuple, not of the bare kernel.  On the same set, consistency and
event-date transport give, equation by equation,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \tau(z_0)
 &=\sum_s\pi_s\left\{
   \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0]
  -\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]\right\}\\
 &=\sum_s\pi_s\{\mu_{P,s}(z_0)-m_s(z_{0s})\}
 =\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0).                     \tag{33}
 \end{split}
\]
The common-replica value in (33) is supplied by design-conditional
centering.  Applying (16) to (32)--(33) proves the causal upper transfer
only on this marginally conull set.  No conditional version is selected at a
query-null value, and no converse is asserted over the panel-induced
conditional laws.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} \textbf{Comparison.} Stone's ordinary random-design benchmark \cite{Stone1982} has growing distinct sites, whereas this theorem has \(n_s\) responses at only \(T\) exact sites and hence a separate iid or geometric-mixing spacing floor. Mammen, Rothe, and Schienle generate covariates in training rows \cite{MammenRotheSchienle2012}; Delaigle, Hall, and Meister contaminate training coordinates and require deconvolution \cite{DelaigleHallMeister2008}. Exact training coordinates and an external donor mean instead add \(N^{-\lambda}\), changing the oracle/generated-query boundary. \textit{Gap.} The result is the rough-Holder \(0<\lambda\le1\) frontier for one observable rule chosen before the query, with a geometric-mixing logarithm and iid no-log corollary. \textit{Consumer.} Applications can compare the three nonredundant channels and transfer upper conclusions through \(\xi_P(z_0)\) only marginal-almost-everywhere.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-length-frontier]\label{thm:honest-length-frontier}
Suppose \(0<\lambda\le1\), so \(\kappa=\lambda\), and
\(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\).  Along every growth array with
\(N\wedge T\wedge\min_s n_s\to\infty\), the total interval \(C_\alpha\)
in \texttt{def:honest-interval} satisfies
\[
 \liminf
 \inf_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \inf_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}
 \{\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\in C_\alpha\}
 \ge1-\alpha,
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)]
 \le CR_\star^{1/2}.
\]
These conclusions use no lower local-modulus condition, count the full-range
interval on every declared bad-sample branch, and never divide by an
estimated variance.  If \(\delta_0<1/2\), every single data-measurable
random interval \(I_\alpha\) having the same uniform fixed-query-kernel
coverage obeys
\[
 \sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[\operatorname{length}(I_\alpha)]
 \ge cR_\star^{1/2}
\]
for all sufficiently large indices.  On the fixed-query
independent-calendar subclasses, both upper and lower spacing terms are
\(T^{-\lambda/d}\).  The theorem makes no higher-order claim for
\(\lambda>1\).

For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), the labelled point
\(\xi_P(z_0)\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) for almost
every query under the marginal law of \(z_0\), and
\(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0)=\tau(z_0)\) there.  Therefore the
coverage and expected-length upper conclusions apply to
\(P(\cdot\mid z_0)\) at those query values.  No claim is made about a
chosen conditional version at a null query.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}\) and a labelled point
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  Since both the treated mean and the untreated regression value lie in \([-B,B]\),
\[
 \theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\in[-2B,2B].                 \tag{1}
\]
We first prove coverage.  On the regular branch, \(r=0\), and selected Gram coercivity makes each local-constant denominator positive.  Its weights are nonnegative and sum to one, so clipping is inactive.  Conditional on the exact design and donors, all selected weights are response-independent.  Let \(w_{st}\) denote the actual fold-averaged weight and define one centered variable per complete unit history by
\[
 \zeta_{si}=\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}\left[
 B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mu_s-
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{st}\{Q_{sti}-m_s(X_{st})\}\right].       \tag{2}
\]
Donor--panel independence and the two equalities in \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} center (2).  Assumption \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} makes these variables independent across \((s,i)\) under the conditioning; no independence across dates within a history is used.  Their range lengths are at most
\[
 d_{si}=\frac{2B\pi_s}{n_s}
 \left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}|w_{st}|\right).                    \tag{3}
\]
For a random variable \(V\in[a,b]\), convexity of the exponential and integration of the chord bound give
\(\mathbb E\exp\{u(V-\mathbb EV)\}\le
\exp\{u^2(b-a)^2/8\}\).  Multiplying this inequality over the independent histories, applying exponential Markov to both tails, and optimizing over \(u>0\) yields
\[
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}\!\left\{
 \left|\sum_{s,i}\zeta_{si}\right|>r_{\mathrm{unit}}
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal X,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\right\}
 \le\alpha_{\mathrm{st}},                                  \tag{4}
\]
because \(\sum_{s,i}d_{si}^2=4B^2A_w\).  The positive linear term in the definition of \(r_{\mathrm{unit}}\) only enlarges the usual Hoeffding radius.

Write \(q=Sd\).  Spectral clipping gives
\(\widehat\Sigma\succeq(c_\Sigma/2)I_q\).  Each donor fold has size at least \(N/3\), and therefore the deterministic spectral inequality, donor unbiasedness, and \(\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_q\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\!\left[
 N\|\widehat\Sigma^{-1/2}(\overline Z_\ell-z_0)\|_2^2
 \right]
 &\le \frac{2N}{c_\Sigma}
       \mathbb E\|\overline Z_\ell-z_0\|_2^2\\
 &\le\frac{6qC_\Sigma}{c_\Sigma}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{5}
\]
No independence between the sample mean and sample covariance is used.  Markov's inequality, a union bound over \(K_N\le2\), and the definition
\(q_{\mathrm{don}}^2=32qC_\Sigma/(c_\Sigma\alpha_{\mathrm{don}})\) give
\[
 \Pr\left\{
 \max_{\ell\le K_N}\|\widehat\Sigma^{-1/2}
 (\overline Z_\ell-z_0)\|_2>
 q_{\mathrm{don}}N^{-1/2}\right\}
 \le\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}.                                 \tag{6}
\]
On the complementary event, \(\widehat\Sigma\preceq2C_\Sigma I_q\).  Projection onto the cube is nonexpansive relative to \(z_0\), so
\[
 \max_{\ell\le K_N}\|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2
 \le(2C_\Sigma)^{1/2}q_{\mathrm{don}}N^{-1/2}.             \tag{7}
\]

For a selected local-constant fit, nonnegative unit-mass weights and the support of \(K\) imply
\[
 \left|\sum_tw_{st}^{(\ell)}m_s(X_{st})-
 m_s(\widetilde z_{\ell s})\right|
 \le C\left\{
 \|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 +\widehat h_{s\ell}^\lambda\right\}.                    \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, every observation receiving positive weight is within \(\widehat h_{s\ell}\) of \(\widehat u_{s\ell}\), and Holder continuity plus subadditivity gives (8).  Holder continuity from \(\widetilde z_{\ell s}\) to \(z_{0s}\), equation (7), and the declared calibration of \(C_H\) bound the entire nonstochastic approximation error by \(r_{\mathrm{bias}}+r_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Equations (4), (6), and
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}=\alpha\) prove coverage on the regular branch.  Every declared exceptional branch returns \([-2B,2B]\), which contains the target by (1); intersecting a covering interval with this known range preserves coverage.  Consequently the asserted liminf coverage holds uniformly.  This argument uses only second moments of donors and bounded whole histories.

We next bound expected length.  On the regular branch,
\(\sum_t|w_{st}|=1\).  Thus, with \(A=\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\),
\[
 r_{\mathrm{samp}}\le C(\alpha,B)A^{1/2},                  \tag{9}
\]
where \(\max_s\pi_s/n_s\le A^{1/2}\).  Let
\(\mathcal G=\{h_s^\star<1\text{ for every }s\}\).  On \(\mathcal G\), the pairs \((z_{0s},h_s^\star)\) are feasible.  Certificate optimality and the bandwidth floor therefore give
\[
 r_{\mathrm{bias}}
 \le C\sum_s\pi_s\left\{
 (h_s^\star)^\lambda+K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}
 \|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^\lambda\right\}.            \tag{10}
\]
Projection contraction, donor unbiasedness, covariance ellipticity, and Jensen's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^\lambda
 \le\left(3qC_\Sigma/N\right)^{\lambda/2}.                 \tag{11}
\]
Moreover, since \(0<h_s^\star\le1\),
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal G^c)
 \le\sum_s\Pr(h_s^\star=1)
 \le\sum_s\mathbb E(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}.               \tag{12}
\]
The interval length is always at most \(4B\).  Apply
\texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-anchor-moment} to (10)--(12), and add the explicit term
\(r_{\mathrm{don}}=C_H(q_{\mathrm{don}}/\sqrt N)^\lambda\).  Uniformly in the fixed query and labelled point,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)
 \le C\bigg[&A^{1/2}+
 \sum_s\pi_s(Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+
 \left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}
 +N^{-\lambda/2}\bigg]+CR_\star.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{13}
\]
To see directly why the smoothing term is redundant, for each \(s\),
\[
 (Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
 =(n_s^{-1/2})^{2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
   (T^{-\lambda/d})^{d/(2\lambda+d)}
 \le n_s^{-1/2}\vee T^{-\lambda/d}.                       \tag{14}
\]
Since \(S\) is fixed,
\(\sum_s\pi_sn_s^{-1/2}\le S^{1/2}A^{1/2}\), and
\(T^{-\lambda/d}\le\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{\lambda/d}\).  Hence (13)--(14), together with \(R_\star\le R_\star^{1/2}\) for all sufficiently large indices, prove
\[
 \sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)
 \le CR_\star^{1/2}.                                       \tag{15}
\]
On the independent-calendar subclass, the anchor lemma replaces the spacing term by \(T^{-\lambda/d}\), and the same algebra applies.

It remains to prove the expected-length converse.  We first record an elementary coverage-to-testing inequality.  Suppose two observed laws \(P_0,P_1\), with targets \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), possess a common subprobability measure \(\nu\) of mass \(\eta>2\alpha\).  If an interval has coverage at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) under each law, then
\[
 \nu\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in I_\alpha\}
 \ge\eta-2\alpha-o(1),                                    \tag{16}
\]
because each noncoverage set has \(\nu\)-mass no larger than its probability under the corresponding law.  Every interval on this event has length at least \(|\theta_1-\theta_0|\), whence
\[
 \max_{v=0,1}\mathbb E_v\operatorname{length}(I_\alpha)
 \ge |\theta_1-\theta_0|\{\eta-2\alpha-o(1)\}.           \tag{17}
\]
For arbitrary laws a common measure of mass
\(1-\|P_1-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) is obtained by taking
\(\vartheta=P_0+P_1\) and
\[
 d\nu=\min\left\{\frac{dP_0}{d\vartheta},
                    \frac{dP_1}{d\vartheta}\right\}
 d\vartheta.                                                \tag{18}
\]

For the treated channel, fix a query and common design, donor, and untreated laws.  Choose constants \(0<b<b_0<B-b\), put all untreated responses and potential untreated event wealth equal to \(b_0\), and under \(v\in\{-1,1\}\) let the treated wealth take values \(b_0-b,b_0+b\) with upper probability
\[
 \frac12+v\varepsilon_s,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_s=\frac{\gamma\pi_s}{bn_s\sqrt A}.            \tag{19}
\]
Since \(A\ge\pi_s^2/n_s\), sufficiently small \(\gamma\) makes all probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\).  All wealth levels are positive and can be realized by \texttt{def:buyhold-level} using baseline one, the horizon-zero return equal to wealth minus one, and later returns zero.  The target separation equals
\(4b\sum_s\pi_s\varepsilon_s=4\gamma A^{1/2}\), while the product Hellinger affinity is at least
\(\exp(-C\gamma^2/b^2)\).  Taking \(\gamma\) sufficiently small makes the corresponding common-measure mass exceed \(2\alpha\).  Equation (17) yields the treated lower channel.

For the donor channel, choose \(\eta\in(2\alpha,1)\) and apply \texttt{lem:full-covariance-location-pair}.  Under the two alternatives use a common independent uniform exact design and a common regression in the displaced cohort,
\[
 m_{s_0}(x)=b_0+a_0|x_k-z_{0,s_0k}|^\lambda,               \tag{20}
\]
with constant regressions elsewhere and small positive \(a_0,b_0\).  Set \(Q_{sti}=m_s(X_{st})\), use one common positive treated wealth, and set untreated event wealth to \(m_s(z_s^{(v)})\).  These choices verify support, Holder smoothness, centering, consistency, and transport.  The observed training and treated coordinates coincide; only the donor law changes.  Ellipticity and the root-\(N\) one-coordinate displacement separate the targets by \(cN^{-\lambda/2}\), while the common measure has mass at least \(\eta\).  Equation (17) gives the donor lower channel.  The finite fourth moment supplied by the lemma is a property of this witness and is not used as a model restriction.

For the geometric-mixing spacing channel,
\texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-cluster-two-point-family} supplies two admissible laws with common-measure mass \(\eta_\alpha>2\alpha\) and target separation
\(c\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{\lambda/d}\).  Equation (17) gives that length lower bound.  For the independent-calendar subclass, use independent uniform dates and a cube centered at \(z_{01}\) of volume \(\gamma/T\), with \(e^{-\gamma}>2\alpha\).  A scaled Holder bump supported on a smaller concentric cube is invisible on the no-hit event, whose probability converges to \(e^{-\gamma}\), while its value at the query is of order \(T^{-\lambda/d}\).  The same construction of deterministic responses and potential wealth used for (19) verifies class membership, so (17) yields the no-log lower channel.

Taking the maximum of the treated, spacing, and donor lower bounds and using the smoothing domination (14) proves
\[
 \sup_{z_0}\sup_{\xi}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\operatorname{length}(I_\alpha)
 \ge cR_\star^{1/2}                                        \tag{21}
\]
for every single \(\mathcal O\)-measurable interval rule selected before the unknown query and label, for all sufficiently large indices.  Finally, \texttt{lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer} provides the labelled point \(\xi_P(z_0)\) for marginal-almost-every query of each panel law, and \texttt{lem:causal-identification} gives
\(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0)=\tau(z_0)\).  Applying (4)--(15) on that conull set proves the causal coverage and expected-length upper conclusions.  Nothing is asserted about a selected conditional version at a null query, and the two-point lower bounds are not transferred as panel-induced conditional converses.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} \textbf{Comparison.} Low supplies the classical coverage-to-testing benchmark \cite{Low1997}; Armstrong and Kolesar develop ordered-modulus and bias-aware ordinary-regression intervals \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018,ArmstrongKolesar2020}; and Cattaneo, Feng, and Titiunik study dependent synthetic-control prediction intervals \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021}. This interval instead concentrates one bounded variable per complete unit history, adds an explicit rough-Holder donor-location radius, and is total on geometry failures, without response-calendar mixing or division by estimated variance. Holding training and treated laws fixed while perturbing only donor location yields the additional \(N^{-\lambda/2}\) expected-length lower channel. \textit{Gap.} The cited interval results do not provide an external full-covariance generated-query radius and its matching expected-length converse. \textit{Consumer.} The procedure is conditionally honest over labelled kernels and transfers causally only marginal-almost-everywhere.

\begin{lemma}[lem:geometric-mixing-cluster-two-point-family]\label{lem:geometric-mixing-cluster-two-point-family}
Put \(q=Sd\) and suppose \(\delta_0\le1/2\).  For every
\(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) there are constants \(c_\alpha>0\) and
\(\eta_\alpha>2\alpha\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(T\), the
base Holder model class \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\) contains two laws
with the same interior realized path whose observed-data laws possess a
common subprobability measure of mass at least \(\eta_\alpha\), while their
causal targets differ by at least
\[
 c_\alpha\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}.
\]
Both laws satisfy the frozen geometric beta-mixing envelope and the
design-conditional centering assumption.  Their common donor law may be
chosen with exact covariance \(I_q\) and standardized fourth moment exactly
\(q^2\); this is a witness property, not a class restriction.  No lower
local-modulus condition is used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(q=Sd\) and choose the common realized query
\(z_0=\tfrac12\mathbf1_q\), which is admissible because \(\delta_0\le1/2\).  Use the common donor law
\[
 Z_j=z_0+U_j,                                                \tag{1}
\]
where the coordinates of \(U_j\) are independent Rademacher variables and the copies are independent across \(j\).  Then
\[
 \mathbb EZ_j=z_0,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_j)=I_q,
 \qquad \mathbb E\|Z_j-z_0\|_2^4=q^2.                     \tag{2}
\]
The covariance is elliptic because \(c_\Sigma\le1\le C_\Sigma\), and the last equality is a construction fact rather than a class assumption.  Take the donors independent of the panel.

Choose \(\gamma>0\) such that \(e^{-\gamma}>2\alpha\), and set
\[
 L_T=\left\lfloor
 \frac{\log T-2\log\log(T\vee3)}{|\log\rho_0|}
 \right\rfloor\vee1,
 \qquad
 p_T=\frac{\gamma L_T}{T}.                                 \tag{3}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(L_T\asymp\log T\), \(p_T\to0\), and
\[
 2p_T\le\beta_0\rho_0^{L_T-1}.                             \tag{4}
\]

Partition the first cohort's dates into complete consecutive blocks of length \(L_T\), plus one possible incomplete block.  Independently label every complete block by
\(C_b\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_T)\).  Let \(A_T\) be a cube centered at \(z_{01}\) with Lebesgue volume \(p_T\).  Conditional on \(C_b=1\), draw all design points in block \(b\) independently and uniformly on \(A_T\); conditional on \(C_b=0\), draw them independently and uniformly on \(A_T^c\).  Every date in a complete block has density
\[
 p_T\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T}(x)}{p_T}
 +(1-p_T)\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T^c}(x)}{1-p_T}=1               \tag{5}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\).  Draw the incomplete block and every other cohort independently and uniformly on the cube.

For a lag \(k<L_T\), the past and future sigma-fields can share at most one block label.  Replacing that future-side label by an independent copy changes their joint law with probability at most \(2p_T(1-p_T)\le2p_T\).  For \(k\ge L_T\), no block label is shared and the fields are independent.  By the total-variation definition of absolute regularity and (4),
\[
 \beta_1(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k
 \quad(k\ge1),                                             \tag{6}
\]
and the other cohorts have zero beta-mixing coefficients.  Thus the frozen geometric envelope holds.

Let \(A_T'\) be a fixed-ratio smaller concentric cube and let \(E_T\) be the event that every complete-block label is zero and that no incomplete-block point falls in \(A_T'\).  The incomplete-block failure probability is at most \(L_T|A_T'|=O(L_Tp_T)=o(1)\), while
\[
 \Pr(E_T)=(1-p_T)^{\lfloor T/L_T\rfloor}-o(1)
 \longrightarrow e^{-\gamma}.                              \tag{7}
\]
Hence, with
\(\eta_\alpha=(e^{-\gamma}+2\alpha)/2>2\alpha\), the event has probability at least \(\eta_\alpha\) for all sufficiently large \(T\).

Let \(h_T\asymp p_T^{1/d}\) be the side length of \(A_T'\), and fix a smooth bump \(\phi\) supported in the unit cube with \(\phi(0)=1\).  For sufficiently small constants \(a,b_0>0\), define
\[
 m_{10}(x)=b_0,
 \qquad
 m_{11}(x)=b_0+ah_T^\lambda
 \phi\!\left(\frac{x-z_{01}}{h_T}\right),                 \tag{8}
\]
and keep every other regression equal to \(b_0\).  A derivative of order \(j\le\lfloor\lambda\rfloor\) in the added term is bounded by a constant times \(h_T^{\lambda-j}\), and its top derivative has a uniformly bounded Holder seminorm.  Thus both surfaces belong to \(\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\); reducing \(a,b_0\) enforces the wealth bound and positivity.

Under alternative \(v\), set \(Q_{1ti}=m_{1v}(X_{1t})\), use the common constant response in other cohorts, and use the same positive constant treated observation for every replica.  Set treated potential wealth equal to that observation and untreated event wealth equal to \(m_{sv}(z_{0s})\).  Baseline one, horizon-zero return equal to wealth minus one, and later returns zero realize these levels with returns above \(-1\).  All support, density, Holder, mixing, centering, cross-unit independence, consistency, transport, and donor clauses have therefore been verified, so both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).

On \(E_T\), no observed response is evaluated in the support of the bump.  The restrictions of the two observation laws to \(E_T\) coincide, and hence form a common subprobability measure of mass at least \(\eta_\alpha\).  Lemma \texttt{lem:causal-identification}, (3), and (8) give
\[
 |\tau_1(z_0)-\tau_0(z_0)|
 =\pi_1ah_T^\lambda
 \asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}. \tag{9}
\]
This proves the required clustered-design witness and its common-measure certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The clustered design supplies the logarithmic spacing witness; its common Rademacher donor has exact covariance and standardized fourth norm moment \((Sd)^2\) as a construction fact. \textit{Gap.} Conditional independence of design dates removes this logarithmic clustering mechanism. \textit{Consumer.} Marginal MSE and honest-length lower bounds use the spacing channel.

\begin{lemma}[lem:geometric-mixing-anchor-moment]\label{lem:geometric-mixing-anchor-moment}
Suppose \(0<\lambda\le1\), the fixed kernel satisfies
\(K(v)\ge k_K\) whenever \(\|v\|_2\le\eta_K\), where
\(0<\eta_K\le\delta_0\) and \(k_K>0\), and
\[
 0<c_G<\frac14 k_Kc_Xv_d\eta_K^d.
\]
For every fixed \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}\) and cohort \(s\),
\[
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}[(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}]
 \le C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda}
 +\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right)^{2\lambda/d}\right\}.
\]
The same assertion with exponent \(\lambda\) holds after taking square
roots, and
\[
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}(\mathsf F_{\mathrm{fb}})\le CT^{-2}.
\]
On \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), the logarithmic
spacing term is replaced by \(T^{-2\lambda/d}\).  For each
\(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), these conclusions apply to the labelled
point \(\xi_P(z_0)\) for almost every admissible \(z_0\) under the marginal
law of the query.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\), \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}\), and
\(\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  All probabilities in this proof are under \(\mathbb K_\xi\).  Since \(0<\lambda\le1\), the order convention gives \(r=0\), so \(G_{sT}\) is scalar.  For \(b\in(0,1]\), let
\[
 M_b=\sum_{t=1}^{T}
 \mathbf1\{\|X_{st}-z_{0s}\|_2\le\eta_Kb\}.               \tag{1}
\]
The ball in (1) is contained in \([0,1]^d\) because
\(\eta_K\le\delta_0\) and the query is \(\delta_0\)-interior.  The density lower bound therefore gives, uniformly in \(t\),
\[
 p_{tb}:=\Pr\{\|X_{st}-z_{0s}\|_2\le\eta_Kb\}
 \ge c_Xv_d\eta_K^db^d=:c_pb^d.                            \tag{2}
\]
The declared kernel lower bound implies
\[
 G_{sT}(z_{0s},b)
 \ge\frac{k_KM_b}{Tb^d}.                                   \tag{3}
\]
In particular, on \(M_b\ge c_pTb^d/4\),
\[
 G_{sT}(z_{0s},b)\ge\frac14k_Kc_Xv_d\eta_K^d>c_G,          \tag{4}
\]
where the strict final inequality is exactly the frozen compatibility condition.  This is the step for which that precise threshold is needed.

Set
\[
 a_T=\left\lceil\frac{3\log(T\vee2)}{|\log\rho_0|}\right\rceil. \tag{5}
\]
Partition the dates into consecutive blocks of length \(a_T\), and, for each parity, retain alternating complete blocks.  Retained blocks of a fixed parity are separated by at least one complete block.  By the total-variation definition of beta mixing, successively replacing each retained block's conditional law by its marginal law changes the joint law by at most the sum of the intervening beta coefficients.  Hence, simultaneously for all statistics of the retained blocks, its total variation distance from the corresponding independent product-block law is at most
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{T}{a_T}\right\rceil
 \beta_0\rho_0^{a_T}\le CT^{-2}.                           \tag{6}
\]
This comparison involves only the design sequence.

For one block in the independent comparison law, let \(Y_j\in[0,a_T]\) count visits to the ball in (1).  The convex function \(y\mapsto e^{-y/a_T}\) lies below the chord joining its endpoint values on this interval.  Using (2),
\[
 \mathbb Ee^{-Y_j/a_T}
 \le1-\frac{1-e^{-1}}{a_T}\mathbb EY_j
 \le\exp(-c b^d).                                          \tag{7}
\]
Exponential Markov applied to each parity under the product law, followed by (6), yields constants \(c_1,C_1>0\) such that
\[
 \Pr\{M_b<c_pTb^d/4\}
 \le C_1\exp(-c_1Tb^d/a_T)+C_1T^{-2}                       \tag{8}
\]
whenever \(Tb^d\ge C_1a_T\).  Enlarging \(C_1\) makes (8) trivially valid below that scale.  Combining (4) and (8) controls Gram failure.

Define the deterministic comparison scale
\[
 b_\circ=(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\vee N^{-1/2}
 \vee(a_T/T)^{1/d}.                                        \tag{9}
\]
For every scale between \(b_\circ\) and one, the least dyadic grid point not below that scale is at most twice the scale.  Couple the retained-block design once to its independent comparison array, and on that single coupling evaluate all dyadic visit counts.  Thus the error \(CT^{-2}\) in (6) is paid once for the whole finite grid.  Applying (8) at candidates of order \(2^jb_\circ\) gives
\[
 \Pr\{h_s^\star>C2^jb_\circ\}
 \le C\exp(-c2^{jd})+CT^{-2}                               \tag{10}
\]
until the candidate scale reaches one.  Summation of the dyadic tail, using \(0<h_s^\star\le1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}
 \le Cb_\circ^{2\lambda}+CT^{-2}.                         \tag{11}
\]
If \(b_\circ\) is bounded away from zero, (11) follows directly after increasing \(C\).  Since \(a_T\le C\log(T\vee2)\) and \(2\lambda/d\le2\), the last term in (11) is absorbed by the spacing term, and (9)--(11) imply
\[
 \mathbb E(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}
 \le C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda}
 +\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right)^{2\lambda/d}\right\}. \tag{12}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the corresponding first-moment bound
\[
 \mathbb E(h_s^\star)^\lambda
 \le C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda/2}
 +\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right)^{\lambda/d}\right\}. \tag{13}
\]

At \(b=1\), equations (4) and (8) give
\(\Pr\{G_{sT}(z_{0s},1)<c_G\}\le CT^{-2}\).  If this failure occurs for no cohort, then \((z_{0s},1)\) is in every fold--cohort anchor set, so fallback is impossible.  A union bound over fixed \(S\) proves
\[
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}(\mathsf F_{\mathrm{fb}})\le CT^{-2}. \tag{14}
\]
All constants are uniform because the conditional density and mixing envelopes are uniform.  On
\(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), use single-date blocks, omit (6), and replace \((a_T/T)^{1/d}\) in (9) by \(T^{-1/d}\).  This replaces the logarithmic terms in (12)--(13) by \(T^{-2\lambda/d}\) and \(T^{-\lambda/d}\), respectively.  Finally, \texttt{lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer} places \(\xi_P(z_0)\) in the fixed-query class for marginal-almost-every admissible query of each panel law, proving the stated one-sided transfer.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact inequality \(0<c_G<k_Kc_Xv_d\eta_K^d/4\) makes the empirical visit count imply strict Gram coercivity; conditional density and design mixing alone then control occupancy. \textit{Gap.} The no-log conclusion requires conditional independence of design dates. \textit{Consumer.} Marginal risk and expected length integrate the anchor certificate through this bound.

\begin{lemma}[lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry]\label{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry}
Suppose \(0<\lambda\le1\), so \(\kappa=\lambda\).  For every fixed exact
design \(X=(X_{st})\), every admissible \(z_0\), and every
\(c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}\),
\[
 \Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)
 \le C\left\{A^{1/2}+D_X(z_0)+N^{-\lambda/2}\right\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \omega_{s,X}(1;z_{0s})
 \le n_s^{-1/2}+2L\rho_{sX}(z_{0s})^\lambda,
 \qquad
 \omega_{s,X}(1;z_{0s})\ge c\rho_{sX}(z_{0s})^\lambda,
\]
and \(\Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)\ge cD_X(z_0)\).  If
\(\delta_0<1/2\), then also
\[
 \Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)\ge cN^{-\lambda/2},
 \qquad
 R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)
 \asymp A\vee D_X(z_0)^2\vee N^{-\lambda}.
\]
Without strict interior slack, only the always-valid upper characterization
\[
 R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)
 \le C\{A\vee D_X(z_0)^2\vee N^{-\lambda}\}
\]
is asserted.  Regardless of strict slack, if \(g_{sT}(z_0)<1\) for every
\(s\), then \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)\le CR_D\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Abbreviate
\[
 \rho_s=\rho_{sX}(z_{0s}),
 \qquad D_X=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\rho_s^\lambda,
 \qquad A=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}.               \tag{1}
\]
For a feasible triple \((f,g,z)\) in
\texttt{def:joint-product-modulus}, put \(q_s=f_s-g_s\), and let \(t_s\) be a nearest design index to \(z_{0s}\).  Nonnegativity of every term in the response budget gives
\[
 |q_s(X_{st_s})|^2
 \le\sum_{t=1}^{T}|q_s(X_{st})|^2\le n_s^{-1}.             \tag{2}
\]
The difference of two functions in \(\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\) is Holder with constant at most \(2L\).  Thus
\[
 |q_s(z_{0s})|
 \le n_s^{-1/2}+2L\rho_s^\lambda.                          \tag{3}
\]
The donor part of the budget and \(\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}\) imply
\[
 \|z-z_0\|_2
 \le C_\Sigma^{1/2}\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(z-z_0)\|_2
 \le C_\Sigma^{1/2}N^{-1/2}.                              \tag{4}
\]
Splitting the functional difference at \(g_s(z_{0s})\), applying (3)--(4), Holder continuity, \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\), and
\(\sum_s\pi_sn_s^{-1/2}\le S^{1/2}A^{1/2}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathcal T(f,z_0)-\mathcal T(g,z)|
 &\le\sum_s\pi_s|q_s(z_{0s})|
      +L\sum_s\pi_s\|z_s-z_{0s}\|_2^\lambda\\
 &\le C\{A^{1/2}+D_X+N^{-\lambda/2}\}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{5}
\]
Taking the supremum proves the upper bound for \(\Omega_X\).  The same calculation for one cohort with no donor displacement proves
\[
 \omega_{s,X}(1;z_{0s})
 \le n_s^{-1/2}+2L\rho_s^\lambda.                          \tag{6}
\]

For fixed-design lower bounds let
\(\mathcal X_s=\{X_{s1},\ldots,X_{sT}\}\) and define
\[
 q_s(x)=a\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal X_s)^\lambda,      \tag{7}
\]
where \(a>0\) is small enough in terms of the fixed Holder and support radii.  Distance to a nonempty closed set is one-Lipschitz, and subadditivity gives
\[
 |u^\lambda-v^\lambda|\le|u-v|^\lambda
 \quad(u,v\ge0).                                           \tag{8}
\]
Consequently \(f_s=q_s/2\) and \(g_s=-q_s/2\) belong to
\(\mathcal F_{\lambda,L,B}\), agree at every design site, and differ at \(z_{0s}\) by \(a\rho_s^\lambda\).  Taking \(z=z_0\), first for one cohort and then simultaneously for all cohorts, gives
\[
 \omega_{s,X}(1;z_{0s})\ge a\rho_s^\lambda,
 \qquad
 \Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)\ge aD_X.                           \tag{9}
\]

Suppose now that \(\delta_0<1/2\).  Choose a signed coordinate vector \(v\) pointing from the relevant coordinate of \(z_0\) toward \(1/2\), and choose
\[
 0<a_0\le\frac12\min\{c_\Sigma^{1/2},1/2-\delta_0\},
 \qquad u_N=a_0N^{-1/2}v.                                  \tag{10}
\]
Then \(z_0+u_N\) remains in the trimmed cube, and
\[
 N\|\Sigma^{-1/2}u_N\|_2^2
 \le Nc_\Sigma^{-1}\|u_N\|_2^2\le1.                     \tag{11}
\]
In the affected cohort, a sufficiently small multiple of
\(x\mapsto\{v_s^\top(x-z_{0s})\}_+^\lambda\) belongs to
\(\mathcal F_{\lambda,L,B}\).  Use it as both \(f_s\) and \(g_s\), use zero surfaces elsewhere, and take \(z=z_0+u_N\).  The response budget is zero, while the functional values differ by at least \(cN^{-\lambda/2}\).  Hence
\[
 \Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)\ge cN^{-\lambda/2}.                \tag{12}
\]
Equations (5), (9), and (12), together with
\(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}=A\vee\Omega_X(1;z_0,\Sigma)^2\), prove
\[
 R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\asymp A\vee D_X^2\vee N^{-\lambda}.   \tag{13}
\]
Without strict interior slack, (5) still gives the asserted one-sided upper characterization.

Finally suppose \(g_{sT}(z_0)<1\) for every \(s\).  Since \(r=0\), the Gram is scalar.  Its strict positivity at \((z_{0s},h_s^\star)\), together with support of \(K\) on the unit ball, implies that at least one design point lies within \(h_s^\star\) of \(z_{0s}\); hence
\[
 \rho_s\le h_s^\star.                                     \tag{14}
\]
The grid floor gives
\[
 \{Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d\}^{-1/2}
 \le(h_s^\star)^\lambda.                                  \tag{15}
\]
Substitute
\(D_X\le\sum_s\pi_s(h_s^\star)^\lambda\) and (15) into the upper characterization following from (5), and use \texttt{def:conditional-rate}.  This yields
\[
 R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}(z_0,\Sigma)\le CR_D.                   \tag{16}
\]
Every lower construction above is explicit, and no lower local-modulus assumption was used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Nearest-site upper bounds and distance-function witnesses give a computable fixed-design modulus for rough Holder surfaces. \textit{Gap.} The donor lower direction requires strict interior slack. \textit{Consumer.} The conditional theorem compares its estimator and lower subexperiments to the modulus.

The proposed handle is the ordered modulus of the joint treated-outcome, replicated fixed-design regression, and covariance-standardized donor-location product experiment.

\begin{lemma}[lem:full-covariance-location-pair]\label{lem:full-covariance-location-pair}
Put \(q=Sd\).  Suppose \(\delta_0<1/2\),
\(c_\Sigma I_q\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_q\), and
\(\operatorname{dist}(z_{0s},\partial[0,1]^d)\ge\delta_0\) for every \(s\).
For every \(\eta\in(0,1)\) and every fixed \(\epsilon_4>0\), there are
constants \(c_\eta>0\) and \(N_\eta<\infty\), depending only on
\(\eta,\epsilon_4\) and the fixed class parameters, such that for every
\(N\ge N_\eta\) there are finite-support probability laws \(G_0,G_1\) on
\(\mathbb R^q\) with means \(z_0,z_1\), respectively, common exact
covariance \(\Sigma\), and
\[
 \mathbb E_{G_v}\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z-z_v)\|_2^4\le q^2+\epsilon_4,
 \qquad
 N\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(z_1-z_0)\|_2^2=c_\eta^2,
 \qquad v\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The displacement \(z_1-z_0\) has one nonzero coordinate, \(z_1\) obeys the
same \(\delta_0\)-interior restriction, and
\(\|G_1^{\otimes N}-G_0^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le1-\eta\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(q=Sd\), fix \(\eta\in(0,1)\) and \(\epsilon_4>0\), and choose a signed coordinate vector \(e\) pointing toward the center of its coordinate interval.  Define
\[
 w=\frac{\Sigma^{-1/2}e}{\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2},            \tag{1}
\]
and let \(O\) be an orthogonal matrix whose first column is \(w\).  Choose
\(\varepsilon\in(0,\epsilon_4/2)\), put
\(a=(1+\varepsilon)^{1/2}>1\), and, for \(|u|\) in a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood of zero, define a scalar variable \(A_u\in\{-a,0,a\}\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_+(u)&=\frac{1+u^2}{2a^2}+\frac{u}{2a},\\
 p_-(u)&=\frac{1+u^2}{2a^2}-\frac{u}{2a},\\
 p_0(u)&=1-\frac{1+u^2}{a^2}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{2}
\]
Because \(a>1\), all three expressions are positive after the neighborhood is reduced.  Direct summation gives
\[
 \mathbb EA_u=u,
 \qquad \mathbb EA_u^2=1+u^2,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(A_u)=1.                         \tag{3}
\]
Let \(R_2,\ldots,R_q\) be independent Rademacher variables, independent of \(A_u\), with the empty collection understood when \(q=1\), and put
\[
 V_u=O(A_u,R_2,\ldots,R_q)^\top.                           \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (3), and orthogonality show
\[
 \mathbb EV_u=uw,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(V_u)=I_q.                       \tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb E\|V_u-uw\|_2^4
 =\mathbb E(A_u-u)^4+2(q-1)+(q-1)^2.                       \tag{6}
\]
At \(u=0\), the right side is
\[
 a^2+2(q-1)+(q-1)^2=q^2+\varepsilon<q^2+\epsilon_4.        \tag{7}
\]
It is continuous in \(u\), so after a further fixed reduction of the neighborhood,
\[
 \mathbb E\|V_u-uw\|_2^4\le q^2+\epsilon_4.               \tag{8}
\]

Let \(G_u\) be the law of
\[
 Z_u=z_0+\Sigma^{1/2}V_u.                                  \tag{9}
\]
Its support is finite.  By (1), (5), and (8),
\[
 z(u):=\mathbb EZ_u
 =z_0+\frac{u}{\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2}e,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_u)=\Sigma,                   \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_u-z(u))\|_2^4
 \le q^2+\epsilon_4.                                      \tag{11}
\]

It remains to control testing distance.  Because the same finite support labels occur in (2), the Hellinger affinity between \(G_0\) and \(G_u\) is
\[
 a_H(u)=\sum_{r\in\{-,0,+\}}\{p_r(0)p_r(u)\}^{1/2}.       \tag{12}
\]
All probabilities are bounded away from zero on the chosen neighborhood, so \(a_H''\) is bounded there.  Also
\(a_H(0)=1\) and
\(a_H'(0)=\tfrac12\sum_rp_r'(0)=0\).  Taylor's theorem therefore gives
\[
 a_H(u)\ge1-Cu^2.                                          \tag{13}
\]
Affinity factorizes under products.  If \(CNu^2\le1/2\), then
\[
 a_H(u)^N\ge(1-Cu^2)^N\ge e^{-2CNu^2}.                    \tag{14}
\]
For dominated probability laws \(P,Q\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) proves
\[
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\left\{1-\left(\int\sqrt{dP,dQ}\right)^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{15}
\]
Choose \(c_\eta>0\) sufficiently small that (14)--(15), with
\(u=c_\eta N^{-1/2}\), give
\[
 \|G_u^{\otimes N}-G_0^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le1-\eta.                                                 \tag{16}
\]
Increase \(N_\eta\) so that this \(u\) lies in the probability and moment neighborhood for all \(N\ge N_\eta\).  Equation (10) gives the exact identity
\[
 N\|\Sigma^{-1/2}\{z(u)-z_0\}\|_2^2=c_\eta^2.           \tag{17}
\]
Finally,
\(\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2^{-1}\le C_\Sigma^{1/2}\).  Because \(\delta_0<1/2\), moving the selected coordinate toward \(1/2\) by at most \(1/2-\delta_0\) preserves the full interior restriction.  Reduce \(c_\eta\), and if necessary enlarge \(N_\eta\), so this holds.  Taking \(G_0=G_{u=0}\) and \(G_1=G_{u=c_\eta N^{-1/2}}\) proves every assertion, including exact full covariance and the arbitrary positive fourth-moment slack.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} For every prescribed elliptic full covariance, a finite-support construction supplies an overlapping root-\(N\) location pair with arbitrarily small positive excess above the covariance-implied fourth-moment floor. \textit{Gap.} The local \(\epsilon_4>0\) is witness slack at the Jensen boundary, not a model restriction. \textit{Consumer.} Conditional, marginal, and honest donor converses use the pair at the required overlap level.

\begin{lemma}[lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer]\label{lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), let
\(\mathbb K_{P,z_0}=P(\,\cdot\mid z_0)\) be any regular conditional law of
the complete causal-data array given the realized query, define
\[
 \mu_{P,s}(z_0)=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]
 \quad\text{(the same value for every \(i\))},
 \qquad
 \Sigma\_P(z\_0)=\operatorname{Var}\_P(Z\_j\mid z\_0),
\]
and retain the regression label \(m=(m_1,\ldots,m_S)\).  Then, for
\(P\)-almost every admissible query value under its marginal query law, the
labelled point
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)=\bigl(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,
                         \Sigma_P(z_0)\bigr)
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  The common-replica equality
in the first display follows from \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering}.
No membership assertion is made for the bare kernel \(\mathbb K_{P,z_0}\),
or for a chosen conditional version at a query-null value.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), choose a regular conditional law
\(\mathbb K_{P,z_0}=P(\,\cdot\mid z_0)\), and define
\[
 \mu_{P,s}(z_0)=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0],
 \qquad
 \Sigma_P(z_0)=\operatorname{Var}_P(Z_j\mid z_0).           \tag{1}
\]
Bounded wealth makes the first quantity finite.  Assumption
\texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} states that its value is common across replicas \(i\), and disintegration gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 =\mu_{P,s}(z_0).                                           \tag{2}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:consistency} disintegrates to
\(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)\) almost surely under the kernel, while \texttt{ass:event-date-transport} gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}B_{si}(0)=m_s(z_{0s}).       \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2)--(3) verify the mean-label, consistency, and transport clauses of \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class}.

Let \(\mathcal X=\sigma((X_{uv})_{u,v})\).  The conditional-mean restrictions in \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} become, under the regular conditional law,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}[Q_{sti}\mid\mathcal X]
 =m_s(X_{st}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}
 [B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal X]
 =\mu_{P,s}(z_0).                                           \tag{4}
\]
These are mean restrictions and impose neither identical replica laws nor independence between the event and training coordinates within a unit history.  Assumption \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} supplies conditional independence of the histories across \((s,i)\) given \(\mathcal X\) under the kernel.

The boundedness, exact-observation, and interior assertions disintegrate to kernel-almost-sure assertions outside a marginally null set of queries.  Holder membership is a property of the retained regression labels.  Assumptions \texttt{ass:design-density} and \texttt{ass:temporal-mixing} give, on a marginally conull set,
\[
 c_X\le f_{st\mid z_0}(x)\le C_X
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }x,
 \qquad
 \beta_s(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k                    \tag{5}
\]
for every cohort, date, and integer \(k\ge1\).

Assumptions \texttt{ass:donor-independence} and
\texttt{ass:donor-training-separation} give, under the kernel, independent and identically distributed donors independent of the complete joint panel.  Assumptions \texttt{ass:donor-unbiasedness} and \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}, together with (1), give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}Z_j=z_0,
 \qquad
 c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma_P(z_0)
 \preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}.                                  \tag{6}
\]
No donor fourth-moment statement is required by, or inferred for, the class.

There are finitely many cohorts, dates, and replicas and countably many lags.  Intersecting the conull query sets supporting (2)--(6) and the disintegrated support assertions therefore leaves a marginally conull set.  At every query in that set, the labelled tuple
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)=\bigl(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,
 \Sigma_P(z_0)\bigr)                                       \tag{7}
\]
satisfies every member property in \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class}.  Hence
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)              \tag{8}
\]
for marginal-almost-every admissible query.  Equation (8) concerns the labelled tuple, not the bare conditional law, and nothing is asserted about an arbitrarily selected version on the complementary query-null set.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Disintegration transfers exactly the active support, centering, conditional-design, independence, and covariance clauses to the labelled tuple \(\xi_P(z_0)\). \textit{Gap.} No conditional version is selected at a query-null value. \textit{Consumer.} Every causal upper transfer uses the labelled tuple rather than a bare kernel.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target and statistical functional remain distinct. The labelled fixed-query law identifies \(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\); consistency and event-date transport then prove equality with \(\tau(z_0)\). Donor uncertainty dominates when \(N^{-\lambda}\) exceeds treated replication and conditional spacing. Known \(X,z_0,\Sigma\) are available only in the deterministic experiment; revealing \(z_0\) to the outer rule would erase the donor channel. The finite-support moment calculations belong to lower-bound witnesses and are not restrictions on the model.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Every constructive headline is restricted to \(0<\lambda\le1\), fixed dimension, finitely many cohorts, and fixed admissible queries; panel-law transfer through \(\xi_P(z_0)\) is only marginal-almost-everywhere. Design-conditional centering rules out the former common-design-shock witness, so that audit is not retained as a numbered result. The active primitives nevertheless do not fix conditional treated-outcome variance or a limiting calendar long-run variance. Therefore exact constants at phase intersections remain open. Higher-order smoothness, growing dimension, adaptation, and uniform-in-query bands require separate arguments.

\section{Honest scope}

Event-date transport is untestable. The centering restriction imposes common treated means and design-invariant conditional means, but not identical replica distributions, within-history independence, or response-calendar mixing. The interval uses whole-unit bounded-range concentration, a rough-Holder donor radius based on mean-square covariance control, and the full-range fallback. The donor fourth moment is not assumed. Fixed-design decisions know \(X,z_0,\Sigma\); outer decisions do not know \(z_0\). Causal coverage and expected-length upper conclusions transfer only through the labelled tuple \(\xi_P(z_0)\) on a marginally conull query set.
