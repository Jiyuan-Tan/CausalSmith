Write \(D=D_{J_y,J_z}\), \(M=2^{dJ_z}\), \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(\nu_k=\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and
\[
b_n=(nV)^{1/2},\qquad S=S_n=A+b_n.
\tag{1}
\]
All constants below depend only on the fixed density bounds, the H\"older radius, and the chosen boundary-wavelet system.  In particular, they do not depend on \(n,J_y,J_z\), or on the member of \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\).

First reduce the estimator to its first two Hoeffding projections.  The hypotheses of \(\texttt{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}\) give
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_n,
\tag{2}
\]
where
\[
R_n=o_P\!\left(\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac A n
+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right).
\tag{3}
\]
That theorem also gives \(\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\rho_n/\sqrt n\).  Since \(V=n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\) by \(\texttt{def:pilot-tuning-and-studentizer}\),
\[
b_n\asymp \rho_n\sqrt n.
\tag{4}
\]
The assumed bias inequality and (1)--(4) therefore imply
\[
nR_n=o_P(S).
\tag{5}
\]
Notice that the two possibly non-negligible deterministic biases are subtracted in the random variable in the theorem; the \(O(S/n)\) assumption on them is used only to obtain (5).

We next identify the local first projection with the linear part of the equality Hessian.  For fixed \(z\), let \(F_{a,n}(\,\cdot\mid z)\) be the conditional distribution function.  Positivity in \(\texttt{ass:conditional-density-bounds}\) makes each inverse distribution function \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz, while
\[
\|F_{1,n}(\,\cdot\mid z)-F_{0,n}(\,\cdot\mid z)\|_\infty
\le \|\Delta_n(\,\cdot,z)\|_\infty.
\tag{6}
\]
Expanding the one-dimensional monotone transport map in (6), with the zero-mass condition \(\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)\,dy=0\), gives the Neumann equation
\[
-\partial_y\{\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_y u_n(y,z)\}=\Delta_n(y,z),\qquad
\partial_yu_n(0,z)=\partial_yu_n(1,z)=0.
\tag{7}
\]
The additive constant is fixed by \(\int_0^1u_n(y,z)\,dy=0\), so (7) is exactly \(u_n=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\,\cdot,z)\).  A second Taylor expansion of the inverse distribution functions is legitimate because \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\); after centering the two arm derivatives, it yields
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\le C\rho_n\!\left\{\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(s_{a,n}\) is the exact centered first derivative of the conditional transport endpoint and \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) is the corresponding centered derivative of the projected quadratic form \(\delta^\top H\delta\).  For completeness, the first term on the right of (8) is the Taylor remainder: the displacement in (6) is \(O(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty)\), and its product with the \(H^{-1}\)-energy norm of \(\Delta_n\), namely \(\rho_n\), bounds the derivative remainder.  For the other two terms, expand \(\Delta_n\) in the bases of \(\texttt{def:anisotropic-projection}\); the outcome coefficient of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) has the additional factor \(2^{-2j_y}\), and the H\"older coefficient bounds from \(\texttt{ass:anisotropic-conditional-smoothness}\) give respectively \(2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}\rho_n\) and \(2^{-s_zJ_z}\rho_n\).  The quadratic-form comparison in \(\texttt{prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias}\) changes these bounds only by fixed constants.  This derives (8), rather than assuming equality-Hessian alignment at unequal laws.

For evaluation fold \(e\), define the projected contrast and its whitening by
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=\frac1m\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),\qquad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Independent randomized-arm sampling in \(\texttt{ass:two-arm-sampling}\) and the covariance definition in \(\texttt{def:whitened-spectral-array}\) imply
\[
E Z_{e,n}=0,\qquad E(Z_{e,n}Z_{e,n}^{\top})=I,\qquad Z_{1,n}\ \text{and}\ Z_{2,n}\ \text{are independent}.
\tag{10}
\]
Put
\[
G_n=\sum_{e=1}^2 2\sqrt m\,\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}
H_{J_y,J_z,n}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (4), (8), and the independence in (10) show
\[
\frac{\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_{L^2}}{S}
\le C\{\|\Delta_n\|_\infty+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}=o(1).
\tag{12}
\]
If \(\rho_n=0\), positivity of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) gives \(\Delta_n=0\), and both sides before division in (12) are exactly zero.  Diagonalizing \(K\) and using the definition of \(\eta_{k,n}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2
=nV+o(S^2).
\tag{13}
\]
Thus (13) controls the entire noncentral linear shift; no truncation of the sequence \((\eta_{k,n})_k\) has been made.

We now establish the required universality for the canonical quadratic projection.  This is the point at which a fixed-\(K_0\) tail argument would be invalid if the linear variance in (13) were concentrated in high spectral coordinates.  The scale assumptions instead force the diffuse branch, as follows.  The upper quadratic-form bound and compact support imply \(\rho_n\le C\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\), so \(\rho_n\to0\).  By \(\texttt{prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias}\),
\[
A\asymp M^{1/2},\qquad M\le D=o(n^2).
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently, by (1), (4), and (14),
\[
\frac Sn\lesssim \frac{\sqrt M}{n}+\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
The assumed inequality \(2^{-2s_zJ_z}=O(S/n)\) and (15) imply \(J_z\to\infty\), hence
\[
M\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}}\lesssim M^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus a bounded-\(J_z\) subsequence cannot satisfy the theorem's local and bias-scale premises.  This explicitly disposes of the bounded branch; the remaining branch is diffuse and is treated without spectral truncation.

Index all observations in the two folds and arms by a single index \(r\), retaining their fixed fold and arm labels.  By diagonal-free construction in \(\texttt{def:anisotropic-estimator}\),
\[
Q_n:=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s),\qquad
E\{h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s)\mid W_r\}=E\{h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s)\mid W_s\}=0.
\tag{17}
\]
The coefficients in \(h_{n,rs}\) are \(O(m^{-1})\); signs distinguish within-arm and cross-arm pairs and do not affect the bounds below.  We record the moment calculation needed for (17).  The Green kernel of (7) is uniformly bounded in the outcome coordinates because \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\).  Compact support of the boundary wavelets gives a covariate projection envelope \(\kappa_{J_z}\) satisfying
\[
\iint\kappa_{J_z}(z,z')^2\,dR_n(z)dR_n(z')\le CM,\qquad
\iint\kappa_{J_z}(z,z')^4\,dR_n(z)dR_n(z')\le CM^3.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, at each scale only a fixed number of wavelets overlap; summing their squared magnitudes gives \(O(M)\), and integrating once gives the two powers in (18).  Whitening changes (18) only by fixed factors because \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma\preceq C_\Gamma I\) in \(\texttt{prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias}\).  Summing over the \(O(m^2)\) pairs in (17) therefore yields
\[
\begin{split}
&\operatorname{Var}(Q_n)=A^2,\qquad
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}E h_{n,rs}^2\le C\frac{M}{m},\\
&\sum_{r<s}E h_{n,rs}^4\le C\frac{M^3}{m^2},\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\}^2}
\le\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}\le\frac CM.
\end{split}
\tag{19}
\]
The first equality in (19) is also the variance identity \(\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb U)=A/n\) in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}\).  In particular,
\[
\frac{\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}E h_{n,rs}^2}{A^2}=O(m^{-1}),\qquad
\frac{\sum_{r<s}E h_{n,rs}^4}{A^4}=O(M/m^2)=o(1).
\tag{20}
\]
The last conclusion uses \(M\le D=o(n^2)\) and \(m=n/2\).

We give the short martingale calculation that converts (19)--(20) into the joint linear--quadratic approximation.  Order the independent observations, let \(\mathcal F_r=\sigma(W_1,\ldots,W_r)\), write \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\), and put
\[
\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(W_s,W_r).
\tag{21}
\]
Canonicality in (17) makes \((\mathcal D_{n,r},\mathcal F_r)\) a martingale-difference array and \(\sum_r\mathcal D_{n,r}=G_n+Q_n\).  From (6)--(8),
\[
\omega_n:=\max_r\|\ell_{n,r}\|_\infty/S\le C\|\Delta_n\|_\infty/S=o(1),
\tag{22}
\]
where \(S\) is bounded away from zero by (14).  Expanding the squares and fourth powers in (21), every nonzero expectation pairs each observation index at least twice.  Pairings sharing one index give the row term in (20), pairings using one edge four times give its fourth-moment term, four-edge cycles give \(\operatorname{tr}(K^4)\), and terms containing a linear factor are bounded by (22).  Therefore (19)--(22) give the explicit bounds
\[
\begin{split}
&\sum_r E\!\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal D_{n,r}}S\right)^2
\mathbf 1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\varepsilon S\}\right]\longrightarrow0\quad(\varepsilon>0),\\
&E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
-E(G_n+Q_n)^2\right\}^2\right]/S^4\\
&\hspace{35mm}\le C\left\{\frac1m+\frac{M}{m^2}+\frac1M+\omega_n^2\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
This calculation also shows why \(D=o(n^2)\), rather than a fixed-rank approximation, is the relevant high-frequency condition.

For clarity, (23) already contains a proof of the needed martingale central limit step.  Conditional Taylor expansion gives, for fixed \(t\),
\[
E\{\exp(it\mathcal D_{n,r}/S)\mid\mathcal F_{r-1}\}
=1-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}E(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})+\mathfrak r_{n,r},
\tag{24}
\]
and the first line of (23) implies \(\sum_rE|\mathfrak r_{n,r}|\to0\).  Iterating (24), and then using the second line of (23), yields
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\longrightarrow0.
\tag{25}
\]
Here the covariance between \(G_n\) and \(Q_n\) is zero by canonicality in (17), and (13) supplies the variance of \(G_n\).

It remains to compare (25) with the announced Gaussian quadratic proxy.  On the declared Gaussian array define
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k
\nu_k\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Let \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\).  Direct Gaussian integration, not a truncation argument, gives
\[
\log E\exp(it\mathcal Q_n^G/S)
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].
\tag{27}
\]
By (13), (16), and \(\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\{nV+o(S^2)\}/S^2\), expansion of (27) has a uniform remainder bounded by
\[
C_t\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\left\{\frac{A^2}{S^2}+\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2\right\}=o(1).
\tag{28}
\]
Consequently the right side of (27) equals
\[
-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}+o(1),
\tag{29}
\]
which is the exponent in (25).

To pass from characteristic functions to the stated bounded-Lipschitz distance without presuming convergence of the variance ratio, take an arbitrary subsequence.  Compactness of \(A/S\in[0,1]\) gives a further subsequence on which \(A/S\to a\); because \(b_n/S=1-A/S\), both (25) and (29) converge there to the same centered normal law with variance \(a^2+(1-a)^2\), which lies in \([1/2,1]\).  Convergence to the same continuous law makes their bounded-Lipschitz distance tend to zero on that further subsequence.  Since every subsequence has this property, the full sequence satisfies
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L((G_n+Q_n)/S),\mathcal L(\mathcal Q_n^G/S)\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{30}
\]
Combining (2), (5), (12), and (30) proves the displayed triangular approximation.

At conditional equality, positive definiteness of the Neumann inverse gives \(\Delta_n=0\), hence \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\), \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\).  Equations (16), (27), and (28), now with no linear terms, show
\[
\frac1A\sum_{e,k}\nu_k(\xi_{e,k}^2-1)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{31}
\]
The same conclusion for \(n\mathbb U/A\) follows from (23)--(30).  This proves the exact null assertion with the two-fold variance \(A^2=4\sum_k\nu_k^2\).

The qualified chaos statement follows directly from the proxy and explains why no unconditional fixed-chaos claim is made.  Let \(w_{k,n}=\nu_k/A\), pad finite arrays by zeros, and suppose along a subsequence that \(w_n\to w\) and \(\eta_n\to\eta\) in \(\ell^2\).  On one common Gaussian array,
\[
\left\|\sum_{e,k}w_{k,n}\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}
-\sum_{e,k}w_k\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_k)^2-(1+\eta_k^2)\}\right\|_{L^2}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{32}
\]
because the centered quadratic part has squared \(L^2\)-distance \(4\|w_n-w\|_2^2\), while
\[
\|w_n\eta_n-w\eta\|_2
\le\|w_n-w\|_2\|\eta_n\|_\infty+\|w\|_\infty\|\eta_n-\eta\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{33}
\]
The series on the right of (32) is therefore a well-defined centered Gaussian chaos.  Moreover (13) makes \(S/A\) bounded on such a subsequence, so (30) transfers (32) to the estimator, after passage to a further subsequence if a limit of \(S/A\) is needed.  Conversely, in the diffuse null regime (16), \(\max_k|w_{k,n}|\to0\) while \(\|w_n\|_2=1/2\), so no strongly convergent \(\ell^2\) subsequence exists.  This verifies both the sufficiency and the advertised restriction of the chaos conclusion.

We next justify the observed-kernel replacement.  Under \(D\log n/m=o(1)\), \(\texttt{lem:observable-pilot-control}\) gives uniform operator consistency of the empirical covariance and the opposite-fold Hessian.  The resolvent identity used there is relative in the elliptic energy: if \(\bar K_n^{\,\mathrm{obs}}=\{\widehat K^{(-1)}+\widehat K^{(-2)}\}/2\), then positivity \(c\le q_{a,n},r_n\le C\), \(\lambda_n\to0\), and covariance coercivity imply
\[
|x^\top(\bar K_n^{\,\mathrm{obs}}-K)y|
\le o_P(1)(x^\top Kx)^{1/2}(y^\top Ky)^{1/2}.
\tag{34}
\]
Applying (34) in an eigenbasis of \(K\) and summing squares gives
\[
\|\bar K_n^{\,\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
=o_P(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}})=o_P(A).
\tag{35}
\]
Conditional on the pilots, couple the population and observed quadratic proxies with the same standard Gaussian vector \(Z\).  Elementary Gaussian moment expansion gives
\[
E\!\left[\{Z^\top(\bar K_n^{\,\mathrm{obs}}-K)Z
-\operatorname{tr}(\bar K_n^{\,\mathrm{obs}}-K)\}^2\mid\text{pilots}\right]
=2\|\bar K_n^{\,\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=o_P(S^2).
\tag{36}
\]
The common multiplier retains the linear term and its covariance.  Equations (35)--(36) therefore permit replacement of the population kernel by the average observed kernel in (30).

Finally consider the separate fixed-unequal-law assertion.  The exact first derivative is used here, not the equality Hessian.  On the compact support, the squared transport cost is at most one; the conditional density lower bound makes the centered Kantorovich scores uniformly bounded.  Independent arm sampling therefore gives, by the same conditional Taylor calculation as (24) with no quadratic summand,
\[
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}/\sqrt{V/n}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{37}
\]
The assumed relation \(\sqrt{nV}/A\to\infty\) makes \(\mathbb U=o_P(\sqrt{V/n})\).  The stated bias and remainder hypothesis makes every other term in (2) \(o_P(\sqrt{V/n})\).  Finally \(\widehat V/V\to_P1\) implies \(\sqrt{V/\widehat V}\to_P1\); multiplying (37) by this ratio and using the preceding probability bounds proves
\[
\frac{\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n}
{\sqrt{\widehat V_{L,n}/n}}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal N(0,1).
\]
This last argument never substitutes the equality-Hessian coefficient for the fixed-law transport derivative, completing the proof of all regimes stated in the theorem.
