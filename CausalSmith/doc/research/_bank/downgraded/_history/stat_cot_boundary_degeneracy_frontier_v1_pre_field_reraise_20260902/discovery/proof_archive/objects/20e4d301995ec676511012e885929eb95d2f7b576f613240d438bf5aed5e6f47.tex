Write \(D=D_{J_y,J_z}\), \(M=2^{dJ_z}\), \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(\nu_k=\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and
\[
b_n=(nV)^{1/2},\qquad S=S_n=A+b_n.                    \tag{1}
\]
The expansion theorem gives
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_n,                        \tag{2}
\]
where \(\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L)\asymp\rho_n/\sqrt n\). Since \(V=n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L)\), \(b_n\asymp\rho_n\sqrt n\). The assumed bias inequality and the expansion remainder therefore imply
\[
nR_n=o_P(S).                                         \tag{3}
\]

Let \(s_{a,n}\) be the exact centered transport derivative and \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) the centered derivative of the projected quadratic form \(\delta^\top H\delta\). Differentiating the inverse distribution equations, using \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\), and applying the two projection-tail estimates gives
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\le C\rho_n\{\|\Delta_n\|_\infty+
2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.                       \tag{4}
\]
The first term is the local Taylor remainder; the other two are the inverse-elliptic outcome and covariate tails.

For evaluation fold \(e\), define
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=\frac1m\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\qquad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta\}.             \tag{5}
\]
Sampling independence and the covariance definition imply \(EZ_{e,n}=0\), \(E(Z_{e,n}Z_{e,n}^{\top})=I\), and independence across folds. Put
\[
G_n=\sum_{e=1}^2 2\sqrt m\,\delta^\top
H\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.                                \tag{6}
\]
Equations (4)--(6), the bias-scale assumption, and \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\) yield
\[
\frac{\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2}{S}\to0,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2
=nV+o(S^2).                                          \tag{7}
\]
When \(\rho_n=0\), positivity of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) gives \(\Delta_n=0\), and both sides in the first relation are zero.

For \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\), the energy comparison gives \(\rho_n\lesssim\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\). The spectrum proposition gives
\[
A\asymp M^{1/2},\qquad M\le D=o(n^2),\qquad S/n\to0.  \tag{8}
\]
The hypothesis \(2^{-2s_zJ_z}=O(S/n)\) and (8) force \(J_z\to\infty\). Hence
\[
M\to\infty,\qquad
\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}}
\lesssim M^{-1/2}\to0.                               \tag{9}
\]

Index all evaluation observations by \(r\). Diagonal-free construction gives
\[
Q_n=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s),\qquad
E(h_{n,rs}\mid W_r)=E(h_{n,rs}\mid W_s)=0.           \tag{10}
\]
The bounded outcome Green kernel and compact covariate-wavelet overlap imply that the covariate projection kernel has integrated square \(O(M)\) and integrated fourth power \(O(M^3)\). Since the coefficients in (10) are \(O(m^{-1})\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Q_n)=A^2\{1+o(1)\},\quad
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}Eh_{n,rs}^2\le C\frac M m,\quad
\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^4\le C\frac{M^3}{m^2},           \tag{11}
\]
and
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}
{\{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\}^2}
\le\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}
\le\frac C M.                                        \tag{12}
\]
After division by \(A^2\asymp M\) and \(A^4\asymp M^2\), the row, fourth-moment, and four-cycle ratios in (11)--(12) tend to zero because \(M=o(n^2)\).

Write \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\), let \(\mathcal F_r=\sigma(W_1,\ldots,W_r)\), and set
\[
\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+
\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(W_s,W_r).                         \tag{13}
\]
Then \((\mathcal D_{n,r},\mathcal F_r)\) is a martingale-difference array and its sum is \(G_n+Q_n\). The inverse-distribution derivative is bounded by \(C\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\), so the maximal normalized linear summand tends to zero. Expanding conditional variances and fourth moments in (13), canonicality eliminates each term with a singly occurring observation index. The remaining row, single-edge, and four-cycle terms are bounded by (11)--(12). Consequently, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sum_rE\!\left[(\mathcal D_{n,r}/S)^2
\mathbf1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\epsilon S\}\right]\to0, \tag{14}
\]
and
\[
\frac{E[\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
-E(G_n+Q_n)^2\}^2]}{S^4}
\le C\{m^{-1}+M/m^2+M^{-1}+o(1)\}\to0.              \tag{15}
\]
Conditional Taylor expansion of \(e^{it\mathcal D_{n,r}/S}\), iterated over \(r\), uses (14)--(15) to give
\[
Ee^{it(G_n+Q_n)/S}
-\exp\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\to0. \tag{16}
\]

On the declared Gaussian array define
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.       \tag{17}
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].        \tag{18}
\]
Equations (7) and (9) give \(\max_k|\nu_k|/S\to0\) and \(\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\{nV+o(S^2)\}/S^2\). Expanding (18) therefore produces the exponent in (16), with remainder bounded by
\[
C_t\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}
\left\{\frac{A^2}{S^2}+\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2\right\}=o(1). \tag{19}
\]
Along every subsequence, take a further subsequence on which \(A/S\) converges. Equations (16) and (18)--(19) then converge to the same centered normal law, with variance in \([1/2,1]\). This subsequence argument proves that their bounded-Lipschitz distance tends to zero on the full sequence. Combining with (2)--(3) and (7) proves the displayed triangular approximation.

At equality, positivity of the Neumann inverse gives \(\Delta_n=0\), hence \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\). Equations (9), (16), and (18) yield
\[
\frac{n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}}A\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{20}
\]
For the qualified chaos assertion, let \(w_{k,n}=\nu_k/A\), pad finite arrays by zero, and suppose \(w_n\to w\) and \(\eta_n\to\eta\) in \(\ell^2\). Coupling on one Gaussian array, the centered quadratic parts have squared \(L^2\) distance \(4\|w_n-w\|_2^2\), while
\[
\|w_n\eta_n-w\eta\|_2
\le\|w_n-w\|_2\|\eta_n\|_\infty+
\|w\|_\infty\|\eta_n-\eta\|_2\to0.                  \tag{21}
\]
Thus the proxy converges in \(L^2\) to the asserted centered Gaussian chaos, which proves precisely the stated subsequential qualification.

If \(D\log n/m=o(1)\), the relative elliptic-energy form of the final conclusion in \(\texttt{lem:observable-pilot-control}\) gives, for the average observed kernel \(\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\),
\[
|x^\top(\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K)y|
\le o_P(1)(x^\top Kx)^{1/2}(y^\top Ky)^{1/2}.         \tag{22}
\]
Summing (22) in a \(K\)-eigenbasis yields
\(\|\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(A)\).
Couple the two Gaussian quadratic forms with the same standard Gaussian vector \(Z\). Their conditional squared \(L^2\) distance equals
\[
2\|\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=o_P(S^2), \tag{23}
\]
so the observed-kernel replacement is valid.

Finally, at fixed unequal laws, the exact centered score is uniformly bounded because the support is compact and \(q_{a,n}\ge c\). Expanding the characteristic function of its independent sample average gives \(\mathbb L/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\). The assumed relation \(\sqrt{nV}/A\to\infty\) makes \(\mathbb U=o_P(\sqrt{V/n})\), and the displayed bias and remainder condition makes all other terms in (2) negligible on that scale. Since \(\widehat V_{L,n}/V_{L,n}\to_P1\), multiplication by \(\sqrt{V_{L,n}/\widehat V_{L,n}}\) proves the final studentized normal limit.
