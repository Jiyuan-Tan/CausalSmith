Abbreviate \(D=D_{J_y,J_z}\), \(M=2^{dJ_z}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(K=\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2}\), \(\nu_k=\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=A+\sqrt{nV}\).  The uniform expansion gives
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L+\mathbb U+B^y+B^z+R_n,
\qquad
R_n=o_P\!\left(\rho_n n^{-1/2}+A/n+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right). \tag{1}
\]
Since \(\sqrt{nV}\asymp\rho_n\sqrt n\), the bias premise implies \(nR_n=o_P(S)\).

Let \(s_{a,n}\) be the exact centered first-order score and \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) its equality-Hessian linearization after the corrected projection.  Twice differentiating the inverse-cdf identity under \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\), then applying the two wavelet tails, gives
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\le C\rho_n\{\|\Delta_n\|_\infty+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.         \tag{2}
\]
For fold \(e\), set
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi(W_{a,i,n}),
\qquad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta\}.
\]
Then \(EZ_{e,n}=0\), \(E(Z_{e,n}Z_{e,n}^{\top})=I\), and the folds are independent.  Define
\[
G_n=\sum_{e=1}^2 2\sqrt m\,\delta^{\top}B\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.             \tag{3}
\]
Since \(\eta=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\,\delta\), (3) is \(2\sum_{e,k}\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k}\) in the eigenbasis of \(K\).  Equation (2), local equality, and the bias premise give
\[
\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2=o(S),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2).           \tag{4}
\]

The spectrum proposition yields \(A\asymp M^{1/2}\), \(\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}\asymp1\), and \(M\le D=o(n^2)\), so \(S/n\to0\).  Since \(2^{-2s_zJ_z}=O(S/n)\), necessarily \(J_z\to\infty\), and
\[
M\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}}\lesssim M^{-1/2}\to0. \tag{5}
\]
Thus the theorem's premises force a diffuse spectrum.

Index the independent evaluation observations by \(r\).  Canonical centering writes \(Q_n=n\mathbb U=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s)\).  The bounded one-dimensional Green kernel and compact covariate-wavelet overlap give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Q_n)=A^2\{1+o(1)\},\quad
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}Eh_{n,rs}^2\lesssim M/m,\quad
\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^4\lesssim M^3/m^2,                                  \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\}^2}
\le\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}\lesssim M^{-1}. \tag{7}
\]
After normalization, the maximal row variance is \(O(m^{-1})\), and the fourth-moment sum is \(O(M/m^2)=o(1)\).

Order the observations, write \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\), and set \(\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(W_s,W_r)\).  This is a martingale-difference array with sum \(G_n+Q_n\).  The inverse-map bound behind (2), together with \(S\ge A\asymp M^{1/2}\), gives \(\max_r\|\ell_{n,r}\|_\infty/S=o(1)\).  Expanding conditional second and fourth moments, every nonzero term pairs each observation index at least twice.  Repeated edges, shared vertices, and four-edge cycles are bounded by (6), (7), and the last envelope.  Hence, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sum_rE[(\mathcal D_{n,r}/S)^2\mathbf1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\epsilon S\}]\to0, \tag{8}
\]
and
\[
\frac{E[\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})-E(G_n+Q_n)^2\}^2]}{S^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+M/m^2+M^{-1}+o(1)\to0.                                  \tag{9}
\]
Conditional Taylor expansion of \(e^{itx}\), using (8) for the remainder and (9) for the variance, proves
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\to0.              \tag{10}
\]

On the declared Gaussian array put
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
With \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})-
\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].                             \tag{11}
\]
By (4), (5), and \(S\ge A\), the difference between (11) and the exponent in (10) is at most
\[
C_t\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}
\left\{\frac{A^2}{S^2}+\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2\right\}=o(1).                \tag{12}
\]
Equations (10)--(12), followed by a subsequence argument for the variance in \([1/2,1]\), prove both bounded-Lipschitz claims.  Equations (1), (4), and \(nR_n=o_P(S)\) transfer them to the estimator.

At equality, \(\Delta_n=0\), hence \(V=0\), \(\eta=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\); (5), (11), and (12) give \(n\mathbb U/A\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).  Under \(D\log n/m=o(1)\), the Hilbert--Schmidt conclusion of the corrected pilot-control lemma gives, conditionally on the pilots,
\[
E[\{Z^{\top}(\overline K^{\mathrm{obs}}-K)Z-\operatorname{tr}(\overline K^{\mathrm{obs}}-K)\}^2\mid\text{pilots}]
=2\|\overline K^{\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=o_P(S^2),            \tag{13}
\]
which proves observed-kernel replacement.  At a fixed unequal law, the exact score is bounded on the compact support, so the independent-sum characteristic-function expansion gives \(\mathbb L/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).  The linear-dominance and bias hypotheses remove \(\mathbb U\) and the remaining terms in (1); finally \(\widehat V_{L,n}/V_{L,n}\to_P1\) proves the studentized conclusion.
