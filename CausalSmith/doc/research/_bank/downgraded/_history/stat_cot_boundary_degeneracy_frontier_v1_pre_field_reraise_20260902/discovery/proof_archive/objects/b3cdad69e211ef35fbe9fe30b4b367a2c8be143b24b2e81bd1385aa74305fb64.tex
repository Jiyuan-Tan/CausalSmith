Write \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and
\[
H_z(y)=F_{1,z}(y)-F_{0,z}(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv.
\]
If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integrating its Neumann equation gives
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_z(y)=H_z(y).
\]
Because \(H_z(0)=H_z(1)=0\), integration by parts yields
\[
\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)\{\partial_yu_z(y)\}^2dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy.                                \tag{1}
\]
For each \(u\in(0,1)\), the mean-value identity and \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\) give
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(u)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{u,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(u)-Q_{1,z}(u)\}
\]
for some \(\zeta_{u,z}\) between the two quantiles.  Changing variables \(u=F_{0,z}(y)\) and applying the same bounds gives
\[
c_3\int_0^1H_z(y)^2dy
\le W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
\le C_3\int_0^1H_z(y)^2dy.                                                \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1)--(2), integrating against \(R_n\), and using \(\rho_n\le\rho\), we obtain
\[
c_4\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_4\rho_n^2\le C_4\rho^2.                    \tag{3}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has root-mean-square risk at most \(C_4\rho^2\).

For the observable upper bound, put
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The Hoeffding identity underlying the expansion theorem, before imposing a little-oh condition, is
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}.                                  \tag{4}
\]
We require an \(L^2\) bound on its final term.  The number of pilot coefficients is
\[
\overline D_n\asymp2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}
\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
The coefficientwise exponential-moment calculation from the pilot-control proof, summed over \(O(\overline D_n)\) coefficients, gives for fixed \(x\in[0,1]\)
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
P\!\left(\max_e\left[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty
+\max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty\right]
>C\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+x\}\right)
\le C\exp\{-cnx^2/\overline D_n\}.                                      \tag{5}
\]
The projection bias is \(O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})\); ratios and normalization are Lipschitz on the positivity event, and clipping keeps the pilots bounded off that event.  Integrating the tail in (5) proves the corresponding fixed-moment bounds.  The deterministic Taylor and interpolation inequalities proved in the pilot-control lemma therefore imply
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}\|R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P)}
\le Cp_n+o\!\left(\frac\rho{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right).                                   \tag{6}
\]
Orthogonality of the two Hoeffding projections, their variance formulas, and \(\rho_n\le\rho\) give
\[
\|\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C\rho n^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n.                       \tag{7}
\]
At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), the spectrum, bias, and tuning results give
\[
A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z),
\qquad
|B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|\le Cr_{n,d}(s_z).                         \tag{8}
\]
Minkowski's inequality applied to (4), followed by (6)--(8), proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}.                                \tag{9}
\]
For the known class radius \(\rho\), select the zero map or this explicit sample map according to which displayed guarantee is smaller.  Equations (3) and (9) prove the asserted upper bound; in particular the zero map is selected when \(\rho\lesssim\rho_{Q,n}\).

For the converse, use the explicit sign-paired family.  Choose its fixed \(c_0\) small enough that
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1.
\]
The inequality \(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\{\chi^2(P,Q)\}^{1/2}/2\), obtained by Cauchy--Schwarz from the likelihood ratio, makes the total variation strictly smaller than one.  The null endpoint is zero, while every mixture component has endpoint at least
\[
\delta_n=c_5\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.
\]
For any estimator, thresholding at \(\delta_n/2\) produces a test, and on either testing error the squared estimation error is at least \(\delta_n^2/4\).  Since the sum of the two testing errors is at least one minus total variation, this proves
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_6\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                                      \tag{10}
\]

For the regular direction, extend \(q_{a,t}^0\) constantly in \(z\).  When \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), compare \(t=c_7\rho\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\), choosing fixed constants small enough that both inverse-elliptic radii are at most \(\rho\).  The Hellinger calculation in the mixture-check lemma leaves their testing affinity bounded away from zero.  Let \(\Theta(t)\) be the endpoint on this symmetric cosine path.  Here
\[
F_{a,t}(y)=y+(2a-1)t\sin(2\pi y)/(4\pi),
\qquad \partial_yF_{a,t}=q_{a,t}^0\ge c.
\]
Implicit differentiation gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(u)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(u)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^0\{Q_{a,t}(u)\}}.
\]
Differentiating this bounded quotient once more shows that \(\Theta''\) is continuous near zero.  The cosine lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\); hence, on a fixed small neighborhood, \(\Theta''(v)\ge1/(8\pi^2)\).  Integrating twice gives
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c_9t|t'-t|\ge c_{10}\rho n^{-1/2}.              \tag{11}
\]
The same two-point testing reduction yields a lower bound \(c_{11}\rho n^{-1/2}\).  If \(\rho<Kn^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), because \(4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), so (10) already gives order \(\rho^2\).  Splitting on these two cases proves
\[
\max\!\left[\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\},
\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right]
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\},                         \tag{12}
\]
and hence the lower frontier.

If \(p_n=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\), (9)--(12) match.  Otherwise (9) and (10)--(12) are exactly the stated honest bracket.  Finally,
\[
\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\]
and
\[
\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.
\]
Thus \(\rho_{L,n}\to0\) exactly when \(s_z>d/4\), which yields the displayed zero, quadratic, mixed, and regular zones.  If \(s_z\le d/4\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\gtrsim n^{-1/2}\), while the first projection is identically zero at equality and (10) retains quadratic difficulty of at least root-\(n\) order.  A uniformly positive-variance regular root-\(n\) limit is therefore impossible over the class.
