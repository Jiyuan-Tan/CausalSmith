\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cot\_\allowbreak{}boundary\_\allowbreak{}degeneracy\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For \(d\ge1\), conditional squared Wasserstein distance has inverse-elliptic outcome smoothing and covariate-replicated spectral rank. The exact observable two-fold estimator admits a linear--quadratic expansion, but its optimized pilot-error projection remainder yields only a certified upper rate; the explicit sign mixture supplies the strictly faster intrinsic lower frontier, so the conditional result is an honest minimax bracket rather than a matched rate in the nontrivial quadratic zone. For the infeasible population-score, population-kernel experiment, exponential Gram-inverse localization and changing-kernel contractions prove a Gaussian--quadratic proxy and force a Gaussian limit whenever \(J_z\to\infty\); no non-Gaussian chaos survives for \(d\ge1\). The separate \(d=0\) branch retains the exact two-sample Brownian-bridge quadratic law and \(n^{-1}\) scale. One observable confidence procedure honest across null, local, critical, and regular regimes remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published regular conditional-transport limit theory and unconditional Wasserstein null laws do not provide the unregularized conditional equality-local frontier with growing covariate rank. They also do not control a common observable calibration while the first-order variance vanishes and the second-order kernel is estimated.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is a primitive anisotropic smoothness-by-equality analysis that separates inverse-elliptic outcome smoothing from covariate spectral replication, distinguishes an intrinsic converse from the slower observable pilot-limited upper rate, and recovers the no-covariate quadratic-process benchmark without conflating the two regimes.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project computes the second variation, derives the ordered spectrum and explicit sign-mixture lower frontier, constructs the clipped ridge-Galerkin two-fold estimator, proves its corrected exact-target remainder and a two-sided minimax bracket, and establishes a dependency-closed Gaussian--quadratic approximation for the infeasible population experiment. It isolates observable all-regime calibration, rather than the point-estimation or population-limit calculation, as the remaining open problem.

\section{Related work}

Lin, Gao, Blanchet, and Glynn provide regular SmoothCOT theory and global conditional-transport estimation rates, which support the supplemental observable upper envelopes but not an equality-local minimax converse or an all-regime interval. Manole, Balakrishnan, Niles-Weed, and Wasserman give an unconditional unequal-law squared-Wasserstein central limit theorem whose variance degenerates at equality. Robins, Li, Tchetgen Tchetgen, and van der Vaart motivate changing-kernel Gaussian approximation, while the present proof derives the required contractions for the Gram-conjugated conditional kernel. Del Barrio, Gin\'e, and Utzet supply the unconditional weighted quantile-process law recovered in the separate \(d=0\) branch. Chen and Fang motivate derivative-aware calibration, and Sturma, Drton, and Leung motivate multiplier methods under mixed degeneracy; neither directly supplies the unresolved observable conditional coupling. Agarwal and Luedtke concern fixed-entropic causal transport rather than the unregularized frontier. Niles-Weed and Berthet, Manole and Niles-Weed, Goldfeld, Kato, Nietert, and Rioux, and Neykov, Wasserman, Kim, and Balakrishnan provide complementary negative-order, separation-sensitive, smoothed, and testing theory but do not close the conditional pilot-limited bracket or its calibration problem.

\section{Setup}

Target (estimand / structural parameter): \(\theta_n=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))dR_n(z)\).
Primary functional / estimator / diagnostic: Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\). For \(d\ge1\), use the corrected pilot-error balancing resolutions
\[
2^{J_z^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/(2s_z+d)},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2s_z/\{(s_y+1)(2s_z+d)\}}.
\]
The explicit Gram-corrected estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}\) has local RMSE at most
\[
\rho n^{-1/2}
+n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), the explicit median-aggregated aligned wavelet estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}\) has RMSE \(O_\tau\{n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}\}\), where \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\). When \(s_z\ge1\), \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}\) additionally has RMSE \(O\{n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}\}\). The certified upper map is the deterministic minimum of these guarantees and the zero-estimator guarantee \(O(\rho^2)\). The sign mixture and fixed-frequency two-point construction give the intrinsic lower frontier
\[
\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\}.
\]
The optimized observable Gram scale is strictly slower than \(n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\), so the conditional result is an honest two-sided bracket rather than a matched rate in the nontrivial quadratic zone.  The quantities \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) are true-discrepancy energy-approximation gaps and are not estimator biases or centering terms. For \(d=0\), \(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\) has local RMSE \(\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}\)..

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Equal-\allowbreak{}weight two-\allowbreak{}fold average of the centered exact population first-\allowbreak{}order transport scores evaluated on the independent evaluation observations.}

  \item \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) --- \texttt{real}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{energy approximation gap}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Outcome-frequency full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy gap, including the retained--omitted cross term, with uniform envelope \(O\{2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y}\}\).
  \item \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) --- \texttt{real}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{energy approximation gap}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Weighted-covariate full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy gap for \(d\ge1\), including its retained--omitted cross term, with uniform envelope \(O\{2^{-2s_zJ_z}\}\).

  \item \(\rho_{Q,n}\) --- \texttt{real}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{derived transition}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:two-arm-sampling]\label{ass:two-arm-sampling}
For every \(n\), the observations \(W_{a,1,n},\ldots,W_{a,n,n}\) are i.i.d. from \(P_{a,n}\) within each \(a\in\mathcal A\), and the two arm samples are independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent randomized-arm sampling, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:shared-covariate-marginal]\label{ass:shared-covariate-marginal}
The \(\mathcal Z_d\)-marginal of \(P_{a,n}\) equals \(R_n\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Common-marginal conditional transport, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-density-bounds]\label{ass:covariate-density-bounds}
For \(d\ge1\), the common marginal density satisfies \(c\le r_n(z)\le C\) for every \(z\in\mathcal Z_d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded positive covariate density, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-density-bounds]\label{ass:conditional-density-bounds}
The conditional densities satisfy \(c\le q_{a,n}(y,z)\le C\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \((y,z)\in\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Z_d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly positive bounded transport densities, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-holder-smoothness]\label{ass:covariate-holder-smoothness}
For \(d\ge1\), \(r_n\in\mathcal C^{s_z}(\mathcal Z_d;L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Covariate H\"older density smoothness, \cite{NilesWeedBerthet2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:anisotropic-conditional-smoothness]\label{ass:anisotropic-conditional-smoothness}
Each \(q_{a,n}\) belongs to the tensor anisotropic ball \(\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}(\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Z_d;L)\), with the \(z\)-condition omitted when \(d=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Tensor anisotropic H\"older smoothness, \cite{CohenDaubechiesVial1993}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-equality-radius]\label{ass:local-equality-radius}
The inverse-elliptic discrepancy satisfies \(\rho_n\le\rho\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Shrinking local neighborhood, \cite{ChenFang2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:wavelet-regularity]\label{ass:wavelet-regularity}
The bases \(\Psi_y\) and \(\Psi_z\) are compactly supported boundary wavelets with regularities strictly greater than \(s_y+1\) and \(s_z\), respectively.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Boundary-corrected wavelet regularity, \cite{CohenDaubechiesVial1993}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-radius-interior]\label{ass:holder-radius-interior}
The H\"older radius satisfies \(L>1\), so the uniform density is an interior point of the declared H\"older balls.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} standard
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:model-class]\label{def:model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Local anisotropic conditional-transport class \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)=\{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n}):\text{the declared sampling, common-marginal, density-bound, anisotropic-smoothness, and local-radius properties hold at indices }(n,d)\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}two-\allowbreak{}arm-\allowbreak{}sampling}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}shared-\allowbreak{}covariate-\allowbreak{}marginal}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}covariate-\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}bounds}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}bounds}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}covariate-\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}anisotropic-\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}equality-\allowbreak{}radius}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:anisotropic-projection]\label{def:anisotropic-projection}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Anisotropic conditional projection}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let
\[
G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}
\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(y,z)\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(y,z)^{\top}\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\]
For \(d\ge1\), define the \(L^2(dy\,dR_n)\)-orthogonal projection by
\[
\Pi_{J_y,J_z}h
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}
\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(y,z)h(y,z)\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\]
For \(d=0\), \(G_{R,J_y,0,n}=I\) and the same formula retains only the outcome-wavelet sum through \(J_y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\Psi_y\); \(\Psi_z\); \(J_y\); \(J_z\); \(R_n\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:whitened-spectral-array]\label{def:whitened-spectral-array}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}sample covariance whitening and normalized Hessian}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\) be the Gram matrix in the anisotropic-projection definition and form the inverse-elliptic bilinear-form matrix
\[
H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n;\lambda\mu}
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}\Phi_\lambda(y,z)
\{\mathcal G_{n,z}\Phi_\mu(\cdot,z)\}(y)\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\]
The Hessian in raw feature-moment coordinates is
\[
B_{J_y,J_z,n}=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}.
\]
Form \(\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}=\operatorname{Cov}_{P_{a,n}}\{\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,1,n})\}\), \(\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}=\Gamma_{0,J_y,J_z,n}+\Gamma_{1,J_y,J_z,n}\),
\[
K_{J_y,J_z,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2},
\qquad

\begin{lemma}[lem:cosine-second-variation]\label{lem:cosine-second-variation}
For \(q_{0,n}(y,z)=1-b(z)\cos(2\pi y)/2\) and \(q_{1,n}(y,z)=1+b(z)\cos(2\pi y)/2\), uniformly as \(\|b\|_{\infty}\to0\), \[\theta_n=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b(z)^2dR_n(z)+O(\|b\|_{\infty}^3),\qquad D\theta_n|_{q_{0,n}=q_{1,n}}=0,\qquad \frac12D^2\theta_n[h,h]=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}h(y,z)(\mathcal G_{n,z}h(\cdot,z))(y)dydR_n(z).\] In particular, the scalar path \(q_{0,n}=1\), \(q_{1,n}=1+t b(z)\cos(2\pi y)\) has second derivative \((4\pi^2)^{-1}\int b^2dR_n\) at \(t=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(z\) and let \(q(\cdot,z)\) be a common conditional density with \(c\le q\le C\).  For a direction \(h(\cdot,z)\) satisfying \(\int_0^1h(y,z)dy=0\), define
\[
H_h(y,z)=\int_0^y h(x,z)dx .
\]
Then \(H_h(0,z)=H_h(1,z)=0\).  Consider the symmetric path \(q_{0,t}=q-th/2\), \(q_{1,t}=q+th/2\), for \(|t|\) small enough that both densities stay positive.  If \(F_{a,t}\) and \(Q_{a,t}=F_{a,t}^{-1}\) are the corresponding distribution and quantile functions, differentiation of \(F_{a,t}\{Q_{a,t}(u,z),z\}=u\), justified by \(q_{a,t}\ge c/2\), gives
\[
\dot Q_{1,0}(u,z)-\dot Q_{0,0}(u,z)
=-\frac{H_h(Q(u,z),z)}{q(Q(u,z),z)}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
At \(t=0\) the two quantiles coincide.  Hence the first derivative of their squared \(L^2(0,1)\) distance is zero, and differentiating a second time gives
\[
\frac12D^2W_2^2(q\,dy,q\,dy)[h,h]
=\int_0^1\{\dot Q_{1,0}(u,z)-\dot Q_{0,0}(u,z)\}^2du.
\]
Changing variables \(u=F(y,z)\) in this display and using (1) yields
\[
\frac12D^2W_2^2(q\,dy,q\,dy)[h,h]
=\int_0^1\frac{H_h(y,z)^2}{q(y,z)}dy.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Let \(u_h=\mathcal G_{n,z}h\).  Integrating the defining equation
\(-\partial_y(q\partial_yu_h)=h\) from \(0\) to \(y\), and using the Neumann boundary condition at zero, gives
\[
q(y,z)\partial_yu_h(y,z)=-H_h(y,z).
\]
Integration by parts has no boundary term because \(H_h(0,z)=H_h(1,z)=0\), and therefore
\[
\int_0^1h(y,z)u_h(y,z)dy
=\int_0^1q(y,z)|\partial_yu_h(y,z)|^2dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_h(y,z)^2}{q(y,z)}dy.                                      \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3), integrated with respect to the common marginal \(R_n\), prove the asserted vanishing first derivative and inverse-elliptic second variation.  The common marginal is supplied by \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), and every division above is licensed by \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\).

For the displayed cosine pair, put \(e=b(z)/2\).  The two conditional distribution functions are
\[
F_{1,e}(y)=y+\frac{e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi y),\qquad
F_{0,e}(y)=y-\frac{e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi y).
\]
For \(|e|<1\), their derivatives are uniformly bounded away from zero.  Taylor expansion of the inverse identities, with the remainder controlled uniformly by that lower derivative bound, gives
\[
F_{1,e}^{-1}(u)-F_{0,e}^{-1}(u)
=-\frac{2e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi u)+O(|e|^2)
=-\frac{b(z)}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi u)+O(|b(z)|^2).                              \tag{4}
\]
Squaring (4), integrating over \(u\), and using \(\int_0^1\sin^2(2\pi u)du=1/2\) proves
\[
W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
=\frac{b(z)^2}{8\pi^2}+O(|b(z)|^3).                                        \tag{5}
\]
Since \(R_n\) is a probability measure, its integral remainder is \(O(\|b\|_\infty^3)\).  Finally, on the one-sided path \(q_0=1\), \(q_1=1+t b(z)\cos(2\pi y)\), equation (2) applies with \(H_h=b(z)\sin(2\pi y)/(2\pi)\).  Thus the coefficient of \(t^2\) is \(\int b^2dR_n/(8\pi^2)\), and the second derivative is \(\int b^2dR_n/(4\pi^2)\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact coefficient fixes the Hessian convention, the lower-bound separation, and the null-law constants. \textit{Gap.} LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT stops at a vanishing first derivative, while GoldmanHuesmannOtto2021 supplies deterministic Poisson linearization but not this conditional statistical second variation. \textit{Consumer.} The sign-paired lower-bound family and the \(\mathsf{STAR}\) simulation benchmark use this coefficient.

\begin{proposition}[prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias]\label{prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias}
Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\), there are fixed constants \(0<c_H<C_H<\infty\) and \(0<c_\Gamma<C_\Gamma<\infty\) such that, if \(g=\sum_{(j_y,k_y,\gamma)\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}\psi^y_{j_y,k_y}\psi^z_\gamma\), then
\[
c_H\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2
\le\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}g(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\}(y)dydR_n(z)
\le C_H\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2,
\]
and \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I\). Consequently there are fixed \(0<\underline c<\overline C<\infty\) such that, for every \(u>0\),
\[
\left|\left\{(j_y,k_y,\gamma)\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}:\underline c\,2^{-2j_y}\ge u\right\}\right|
\le N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\le
\left|\left\{(j_y,k_y,\gamma)\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}:\overline C\,2^{-2j_y}\ge u\right\}\right|.
\]
Writing \(j_0\) for the fixed coarsest retained outcome level, throughout the nonempty range \(\overline C2^{-2J_y}\le u\le\underline c2^{-2j_0}\),
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\},
\qquad
\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\},\quad 1\le k\le D_{J_y,J_z}.
\]
Consequently, for \(d\ge1\),
\[
\|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\asymp1,
\qquad \|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\asymp2^{dJ_z},
\qquad
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{\{\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\}^{1/2}}
\lesssim2^{-dJ_z/2}.
\]
The sequential full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy gaps, including their retained--omitted cross terms, satisfy
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.
\]
They compare the true discrepancy energy with its outcome and weighted-covariate projections; they are not biases of \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\). When \(d=0\), the Hilbert--Schmidt norm remains bounded above and away from zero as \(J_y\to\infty\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\Phi=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\), \(G=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(H=H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\). The tensor wavelets are Lebesgue-orthonormal by \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\). Thus, for every coefficient vector \(x\),
\[
x^\top Gx=\int(x^\top\Phi)^2r_n\,dy\,dz,
\qquad c\|x\|_2^2\le x^\top Gx\le C\|x\|_2^2.                 \tag{1}
\]
The inequalities use \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\). Outcome orthogonality gives \(G=I_y\otimes G_z\), so \(G^{-1}\) preserves every outcome level.

For \(g=x^\top\Phi\), put \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\). Every retained outcome feature has zero outcome integral. The weak Neumann equation and its boundary conditions give
\[
\int_0^1g u_z\,dy=\int_0^1\bar q_n|u_z'|^2\,dy.                 \tag{2}
\]
For zero-mean \(v\in H^1(0,1)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz give \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\). The variational characterization of (2), together with \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), therefore yields
\[
\int_0^1g\mathcal G_{n,z}g\,dy\asymp\|g(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}^2. \tag{3}
\]
The antiderivative of a normalized boundary wavelet at level \(j_y\) has squared \(L^2\)-norm comparable to \(2^{-2j_y}\). Compact support gives a uniformly bounded overlap number, including the finite boundary block. Applying (3) levelwise and integrating in \(z\) proves
\[
x^\top Hx\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and preservation of outcome levels give
\[
x^\top Bx=(G^{-1}x)^\top H(G^{-1}x)
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2.       \tag{5}
\]

For \(f=x^\top\Phi\), the covariance identity is
\[
x^\top\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int(f-v)^2q_{a,n}r_n\,dy\,dz.             \tag{6}
\]
Since \(\int f(y,z)dy=0\) at every \(z\), Lebesgue orthogonality gives \(\int(f-v)^2dy\,dz=\|x\|_2^2+v^2\). Applying both density bounds in (6), then summing the two arms, proves \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma\preceq C_\Gamma I\).

Let \(w_\lambda=2^{-2j_y(\lambda)}\), ordered decreasingly. Congruence by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\) and (5), followed by the min--max characterization on the spans of the largest reference weights and their orthogonal complements, gives
\[
\underline c\,w_k^\downarrow\le\nu_k\le\overline C\,w_k^\downarrow,
\qquad 1\le k\le D_{J_y,J_z}.                                   \tag{7}
\]
Taking superlevel sets proves the asserted counting inequalities. There are \(\asymp2^{j_y+dJ_z}\) retained indices at outcome level \(j_y\), hence on the stated threshold range
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\asymp2^{dJ_z}\sum_{j_0\le j_y\le\min\{J_y,\frac12\log_2(u^{-1})+O(1)\}}2^{j_y}
\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}.                    \tag{8}
\]
Inverting (7)--(8), with the finite endpoint blocks handled directly, gives \(\nu_k\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{D_{J_y,J_z}}\nu_k^2
\asymp2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}\asymp2^{dJ_z}. \tag{9}
\]
All spectral conclusions follow from (7)--(9). When \(d=0\), the covariate multiplicity in (9) is one, so the Hilbert--Schmidt norm stays in a finite positive range.

It remains to retain the energy cross terms. Let \(P_y\) be the outcome projector and let \(P_z^R\) be the \(L^2(dR_n)\)-orthogonal covariate projector. The tensor Gram formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}projection}\) implies
\[
\Pi_{J_y,J_z}=P_yP_z^R=P_z^RP_y.                                \tag{10}
\]
Put \(p=P_yP_z^R\Delta_n\), \(r_y=(I-P_y)\Delta_n\), and \(r_z=P_y(I-P_z^R)\Delta_n\), so \(\Delta_n=p+r_z+r_y\).

At resolution \(J_z\), write the covariate scaling-space Gram matrix as \(G_z=(\langle\phi_{J_z,k},r_n\phi_{J_z,l}\rangle)_{kl}\). Compact support makes this matrix banded in the finite-neighbor lattice metric, while \(cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI\). Put \(\alpha=2/(c+C)\) and \(R_z=I-\alpha G_z\). Then \(\|R_z\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\vartheta=(C-c)/(C+c)<1\) and
\[
G_z^{-1}=\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}R_z^j.
\]
Because \(R_z^j\) has zero \((k,l)\) entry when the lattice distance exceeds a fixed multiple of \(j\), the geometric tail gives
\[
|(G_z^{-1})_{kl}|\le C_1\vartheta^{c_1\operatorname{dist}(k,l)}. \tag{11}
\]
Consequently the row sums of \(G_z^{-1}\) are uniformly bounded. The usual scaling-space approximant and (11) give, for every \(z\)-H\"older function in a fixed ball,
\[
\|f-P_z^Rf\|_{L^2(dR_n)}+\|f-P_z^Rf\|_\infty\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z},
\qquad \|P_z^Rf\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}\lesssim1.                 \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, \(P_z^R\) is uniformly bounded on \(L^\infty\) by (11), so the first two bounds follow by applying it to a scaling-space approximation error; the last follows from the inverse inequality. The same argument applies to Banach-valued coefficient functions and boundary scaling functions. The unweighted outcome version also holds.

By \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\), (12) gives
\[
\|r_y\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_yJ_y},
\quad \|r_y\|_{H_y^{-1}}\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\quad \|r_z\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}.                            \tag{13}
\]
The projected function \(p\) has uniformly bounded anisotropic H\"older norm. If \(u=\mathcal G_{n,z}p\) and \(H_p(y,z)=\int_0^yp(v,z)dv\), integration of the Neumann equation gives
\[
u'(y,z)=-\frac{H_p(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}.                         \tag{14}
\]
The denominator is at least \(c\). The outcome antiderivative supplies one additional outcome derivative, while products and reciprocals preserve the declared H\"older orders. Equations (12)--(14) therefore imply
\[
\|(I-P_y)\mathcal G_np\|_2\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
\|(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}.            \tag{15}
\]

Define \(a(f,g)=\int f(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\}(y)dydR_n(z)\). Symmetry of the Neumann inverse and ordinary projection orthogonality give the exact identities
\[
a(p,r_y)=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_np,r_y\rangle_2,
\qquad
a(p,r_z)=\langle(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np,r_z\rangle_2.          \tag{16}
\]
Equations (13)--(16) yield
\[
|a(p,r_y)|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad |a(p,r_z)|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z},                           \tag{17}
\]
and (3), (13) give
\[
a(r_y,r_y)\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad a(r_z,r_z)\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.                           \tag{18}
\]
For the sequential decomposition,
\[
B^y_{J_y,n}=a(\Delta_n,\Delta_n)-a(P_y\Delta_n,P_y\Delta_n),
\quad
B^z_{J_z,n}=a(P_y\Delta_n,P_y\Delta_n)-a(p,p).                   \tag{19}
\]
Applying (16)--(18), first to the outcome split and then to the weighted-covariate split, proves
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad |B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.                        \tag{20}
\]
Equations (19)--(20) show directly that these are energy-approximation gaps, not terms in the mean of the exact-\(\theta_n\) estimator. Every constant depends only on the fixed support, density, H\"older-radius, and wavelet constants, proving uniformity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition proves the ordered spectrum and both retained--omitted energy cross terms while explicitly classifying \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) as approximation gaps. \textit{Gap.} The variable-coefficient inverse prevents ordinary projection orthogonality from eliminating the energy cross terms. \textit{Consumer.} The pilot-error projection proof reuses this geometry; no exact-target expansion treats these gaps as biases.

\begin{lemma}[lem:observable-pilot-control]\label{lem:observable-pilot-control}
Let \(d\ge1\) and put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\). For the opposite-fold estimator and the declared pilot resolutions, uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\max_e\left[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty+\max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty\right]=O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}),
\qquad
P\left(\bigcap_{e=1}^2\mathcal E_n^{(-e)}\right)\to1.
\]
The same bound holds in every fixed \(L^k\) norm.  The clipping is inactive on the displayed event, and conditional normalization is exact everywhere. The fixed density bounds imply
\[
\frac{c}{4C}I\preceq G_{R,J_y,J_z,n},\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}\preceq\frac{4C}{c}I
\]
for every fold and every realization. Moreover,
\[
\max_e\left[
\|\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\{\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}\}^{-1}-G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\widehat B_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-B_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\right]
=O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+\lambda_n).
\]
Conditionally on an opposite fold, the exact von Mises identity has the following sign: the conditional mean of the diagonal-free quadratic correction is the observed projected ridge-Galerkin energy of the actual arm-versus-pilot discrepancy error, whereas the second-order Taylor term for \(\theta_n\) is its full exact pilot-density energy.  Consequently their leading contribution to \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\) is the negative of the full-minus-projected pilot-error energy, plus ridge-Galerkin and H\"older Hessian-variation remainders.  This contribution is zero for an oracle pilot.  Quantitatively, with \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}=\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n-\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\),
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\lesssim
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.
\]
In particular, no term \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\) occurs in the exact-\(\theta_n\) expansion. If additionally \(D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m=o(1)\), then
\[
\max_e\|\widehat\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_P(1),
\qquad
\max_e\|\widehat K_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_P(1),
\]
and \(\max_e\|\widehat K_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(\|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}})\). This extra covariance condition is required only when the population triangular proxy is replaced by its observed spectral plug-in, not for the point estimator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(p_{a,n}=q_{a,n}r_n\), \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\), and \(\bar D_n=2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}\). Products preserve the declared anisotropic H\"older moduli, so the boundary-wavelet approximation bounds at the pilot resolutions are
\[
\max_a\|p_{a,n}-p_{a,n}^{\mathrm{proj}}\|_\infty
+\|r_n-r_n^{\mathrm{proj}}\|_\infty
\lesssim2^{-s_y\bar J_y}+2^{-s_z\bar J_z}\lesssim\varepsilon.   \tag{1}
\]
For every retained coefficient, expanding the exponential moment of its centered bounded empirical summands and using \(e^v-1-v\le v^2e^{|v|}/2\) gives Bernstein's inequality. A union bound over \(O(\{\bar D_n\})\) coefficients and finite wavelet overlap yield, uniformly over the model class,
\[
P\{E_n>C(\varepsilon+x)\}\le C\exp(-cnx^2/\bar D_n),
\quad 0\le x\le1,                                                \tag{2}
\]
where
\[
E_n=\max_e\left\{\|\widetilde r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty
+\max_a\|\widetilde p_{a,n}^{(-e)}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty\right\}.
\]
The tuning identities in \(\mathrm{def{:}pilot\text{-}tuning\text{-}and\text{-}studentizer}\) give \(\bar D_n\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\). Thus (2) implies \(E_n=O_P(\varepsilon)\), and tail integration gives \(\|E_n\|_{L^k}\le C_k\varepsilon\) for each fixed \(k\).

On \(\widetilde r\ge c/2\),
\[
\frac{\widetilde p}{\widetilde r}-\frac pr
=\frac{\widetilde p-p}{\widetilde r}
+p\frac{r-\widetilde r}{r\widetilde r}.                          \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) and the density lower bounds prove the conditional-pilot rate and \(P(\cap_e\mathcal E_n^{(-e)})\to1\). The construction normalizes every conditional density, including off this event. Since \(T(\widetilde r)\in[c/2,2C]\),
\[
\frac c{4C}\le\widehat r_n^{(-e)}\le\frac{4C}{c}.              \tag{4}
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality gives the deterministic Gram bounds. Moreover,
\[
\|\widehat G^{(-e)}-G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty,
\qquad
\widehat G^{-1}-G^{-1}=\widehat G^{-1}(G-\widehat G)G^{-1},      \tag{5}
\]
which proves the Gram perturbation assertions.

Let \(A_z=-\partial_y(\bar q_n\partial_y)\), \(A_z^{\mathrm{op}}=P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}A_zP_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\), and use hats for the pilot forms. Normalized clipping gives \(c/(4C)\le\widehat{\bar q}_n\le4C/c\). This, the true density bounds, and the zero-mean inequality \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\) give uniform coercivity. The weak forms and (2) imply
\[
\|\widehat A_z^{\mathrm{op}}-A_z^{\mathrm{op}}\|_{H^1/\mathbb R\to H^{-1}}
\lesssim E_n.                                                     \tag{6}
\]
The resolvent identity gives an \(O_P(E_n+\lambda_n)\) perturbation in energy norm. If \(u=A_z^{-1}f\), integration of the Neumann equation gives \(u'=-F/\bar q_n\), where \(F(y)=\int_0^yf(v)dv\). Since \(\bar q_n^{-1}\) is uniformly \(a_y\)-H\"older, boundary-wavelet approximation and Galerkin orthogonality give, for retained \(f,g\),
\[
|\langle f,\{(A_z^{\mathrm{op}})^{-1}P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}-A_z^{-1}\}g\rangle|
\lesssim2^{-2a_yJ_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\|f\|_2\|g\|_2
\le\varepsilon^{a_y}\|f\|_2\|g\|_2.                           \tag{7}
\]
The last inequality is the oversampling rule. Integrating against \(\widehat r\), using (2), and expanding both Gram inverses proves
\[
\|\widehat B^{(-e)}-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P(\varepsilon^{a_y}+\varepsilon+\lambda_n).                   \tag{8}
\]

We now identify the term missed by the former expansion. Fix fold \(e\) and condition on its pilot sigma-field. Define the pilot joint law
\[
d\widehat P_{a,e}^{\dagger}(y,z)
=\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}(y,z)\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)\,dy\,dz
\]
and the signed error \(\dot P_{a,e}=P_{a,n}-\widehat P_{a,e}^{\dagger}\); write \(d\widehat R_e(z)=\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)dz\) for their common pilot marginal. Both the true pair and pilot pair have a common covariate marginal.  Along the convex path \(P_{a,t}=\widehat P_{a,e}^{\dagger}+t\dot P_{a,e}\), that marginal remains common and all densities remain between fixed positive constants by (4) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\). Hence the one-dimensional quantile formula is twice directionally differentiable along the path.

Let \(\widehat\Delta_e=\widehat q_{1,n}^{(-e)}-\widehat q_{0,n}^{(-e)}\), and let the actual raw discrepancy error in pilot conditional coordinates be
\[
\zeta_e(y,z)
=\frac{r_n(z)\Delta_n(y,z)-\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)\widehat\Delta_e(y,z)}
{\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)}.                                        \tag{9}
\]
It has zero outcome integral for every \(z\). If \(d_e\) denotes the vector of retained moments of \(\dot P_{1,e}-\dot P_{0,e}\), the Gram formula gives the exact identity
\[
d_e^\top\widehat B^{(-e)}d_e
=\widehat a_e^{\mathrm{op}}(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e),       \tag{10}
\]
where \(\widehat a_e^{\mathrm{op}}(f,g)=\int f\widehat{\mathcal G}_{n,z}^{(-e)}g\,dy\,d\widehat R_e\) on the Galerkin space and \(\widehat\Pi_e\) is the \(L^2(dy\,d\widehat R_e)\) Gram projector. Direct expansion of the diagonal-free statistic also gives
\[
E(\widehat U_{e,J_y,J_z,n}\mid\mathscr F_{-e})
=d_e^\top\widehat B^{(-e)}d_e.                                  \tag{11}
\]

For clarity, we derive the comparison with the full Taylor term.  If \(F\) has density at least \(c_* >0\), a distribution-function perturbation of sup-norm \(E\) moves its inverse by at most \(E/c_*\). For \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\),
\[
F(x+w)-F(x)-q(x)w
=\int_0^w\{q(x+t)-q(x)\}dt=O(|w|^{1+a_y}).                     \tag{12}
\]
Apply (12) to the two conditional quantile maps along \(P_{a,t}\), square their difference, and integrate first in the common marginal and then in \(t\). Exact Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\[
\theta_n=\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{plug}}^{(-e)}
+D\Theta_{\widehat P_e}[\dot P_e]
+2\int_0^1(1-t)\mathcal H_{e,t}(\dot P_e,\dot P_e)dt,           \tag{13}
\]
where \(\mathcal H_{e,t}\) is one half of the second directional derivative. The score in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\) represents exactly the first derivative in (13), including the shared-marginal term. Let \(\widehat{\mathcal G}^{0}_{e,z}\) be the exact, unridged weighted Neumann inverse with coefficient \(\widehat{\bar q}_n^{(-e)}\), and put
\[
\widehat a_e^0(f,g)=\int f(y,z)\{\widehat{\mathcal G}^{0}_{e,z}g(\cdot,z)\}(y)dy\,d\widehat R_e(z).
\]
At \(t=0\), integration of the conditional Neumann equation identifies the equality Hessian with \(\widehat a_e^0\). Equations (3), (9), and (12) then give
\[
\left|2\int_0^1(1-t)\mathcal H_{e,t}(\dot P_e,\dot P_e)dt
-\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)\right|
\le C\{E_n^{2+a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}E_n^2\}.          \tag{14}
\]
Indeed, the first term is the H\"older inverse-map remainder from (12); replacing the conditional tangent by the raw error (9), replacing the unequal-law Hessian by its equality inverse-elliptic form, and varying the common marginal each contribute at most the second term. Every division in this calculation is justified by the pathwise lower density bound.

The resolvent and oversampling bounds (6)--(8), applied to the retained coefficients of \(\zeta_e\), give
\[
\left|\widehat a_e^{\mathrm{op}}(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)\right|
\le C\{E_n^{2+a_y}+E_n^3\}.                                    \tag{15}
\]
Subtracting (13) from the conditional mean of the fold estimator and using (10)--(15) yields
\[
E(\widehat\theta_{e,J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\mid\mathscr F_{-e})
=-\{\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)\}
+R_{0,e},                                                        \tag{16}
\]
with \(|R_{0,e}|\le C\{E_n^{2+a_y}+E_n^3+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}E_n^2\}\). Since \(a_y\le1\) and \(E_n\le1\) on the relevant event, \(E_n^3\le E_n^{2+a_y}\). Equation (16) is the required negative full-minus-projected sign. If the pilot is oracle, then \(\dot P_{a,e}=0\), hence \(\zeta_e=0\), and every term in (16) is zero.

It remains to bound the projection gap in (16). On the clipping-inactive event, the pilot wavelet estimates at their balanced resolutions have anisotropic H\"older norms bounded in every fixed \(L^k\): at the highest outcome level the stochastic sup-norm \(O_{L^k}(\varepsilon)\) is multiplied by \(2^{s_y\bar J_y}\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}\), and the covariate calculation is identical. Products, reciprocals of functions bounded below, and conditional normalization preserve these bounds. Thus
\[
\|\zeta_e\|_2=O_{L^k}(\varepsilon),
\qquad
\|\zeta_e\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}}=O_{L^k}(1).                 \tag{17}
\]
The complement calculation in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), now applied to \(\zeta_e\), gives
\[
|\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)|
\le C\|\zeta_e\|_2
\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.                               \tag{18}
\]
For the quadratic complement terms, use both \(\|\zeta_e\|_2=O_{L^k}(\varepsilon)\) and the smooth approximation bounds, together with \(\min(x^2,y^2)\le xy\); this yields the factor \(\|\zeta_e\|_2\) in (18). The same inequality holds in \(L^2\); the exponentially small complement of the clipping-inactive event is harmless because all densities are clipped and all transport values are bounded by one. Equations (2), (14)--(18), and the anisotropic amplitude inequality
\[
\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
\lesssim\rho_n^{2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}                              \tag{19}
\]
give the three deterministic terms in the claimed remainder bound. To verify (19), let \(A=\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\). The H\"older modulus keeps an amplitude proportional to \(A\) on an outcome interval of length proportional to \(A^{1/s_y}\) and a covariate rectangle of volume proportional to \(A^{d/s_z}\); zero outcome mass makes its antiderivative have size proportional to \(A^{1+1/s_y}\) on a comparable interval. The identity \(\rho_n^2=\int H_n^2/\bar q_n\) and the upper density bound then imply \(\rho_n^2\gtrsim A^{2+3/s_y+d/s_z}\).

Finally, subtract the conditional means in (16). The residual first-order coefficient has squared \(L^2(P_{0,n})\oplus L^2(P_{1,n})\) norm bounded by
\[
C\{E_n^{2+2a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{2a_y}E_n^2\}+o_P(\rho_n^2), \tag{20}
\]
by (12) and the energy-relative version of (8). Conditional independence makes its empirical average have variance \(m^{-1}\) times (20). Pair counting for the canonical kernel replacement gives
\[
E[\{U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)-U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(B)\}^2\mid\mathscr F_{-e}]
\le\frac C{m^2}\|\Gamma^{1/2}(\widehat B-B)\Gamma^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2. \tag{21}
\]
At outcome level \(j\), (7)--(8) bound the Galerkin part by \(C\min\{2^{-2j},\varepsilon^{a_y}\}\), and therefore the ratio in (21) to \(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2/m^2\) is \(o_P(1)\). Since \(\sqrt n\varepsilon\to\infty\), the square roots of (20)--(21) are absorbed by the deterministic terms already displayed plus
\[
o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}. \tag{22}
\]
Averaging the two folds proves the asserted \(L^2\) bound.

For the final covariance assertion, compact support gives \(\|\Phi(W)\|_2^2\lesssim D_{J_y,J_z}\). Exponential Markov bounds for scalar quadratic forms on a \(1/4\)-net yield
\[
\max_e\|\widehat\Gamma^{(-e)}-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m}+D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m\right)=o_P(1). \tag{23}
\]
Uniform positive definiteness makes the square-root map locally Lipschitz. Equations (8), (21), and (23) prove the operator and relative Hilbert--Schmidt consistency of \(\widehat K\). Condition (23) is absent from (1)--(22), so it is needed only for an observed spectral plug-in.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Conditioning on the pilot yields the exact sign of the second-order error and a uniform \(L^2\) envelope under the unchanged assumptions. \textit{Gap.} Generic pilot convergence alone does not identify the full-minus-projected Hessian term or its anisotropic balance. \textit{Consumer.} The corrected observable expansion, tuning, upper bracket, and calibration allowance use this result.

\begin{lemma}[lem:explicit-mixture-checks]\label{lem:explicit-mixture-checks}
Assume \(L>1\). For the sign-paired conditional wavelet construction and a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\), every \(b_{n,\omega}\) has zero conditional mass after multiplication by \(\cos(2\pi y)\), \(q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\ge c\), anisotropic H\"older norm at most \(L\), common marginal \(R_n(dz)=dz\), and inverse-elliptic radius at most \(\rho\). Its endpoint separation from the null equals
\[
\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}\{1+o(1)\}.
\]
The uniform Rademacher mixture and the null product law satisfy
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\exp\{C_{\chi}n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}\}-1.
\]
At \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\), the exponent is bounded and the separation is a fixed multiple of \(\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\). The fixed-frequency no-covariate construction is positive, normalized, H\"older, and has bounded product Hellinger distance for parameter increments of order \(n^{-1/2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(c_y(y)=\cos(2\pi y)\).  If \(\rho=0\), then \(\gamma_n=0\), the construction equals the null law, and all membership, separation, and divergence assertions follow immediately.  Suppose henceforth that \(\rho>0\).

Boundary-wavelet scaling and the disjointness of the supports in \(\mathcal K_{J_z}\) give constants depending only on the fixed basis such that
\[
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty
\le C_1\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2}
\le C_1c_0 2^{-s_zJ_z},
\qquad
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}
\le C_2\gamma_n2^{(s_z+d/2)J_z}
\le C_2c_0.                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Because \(L>1\), choose \(c_0\) so small that the perturbation H\"older norm is at most \(L-1\).  Shrinking this same fixed constant if necessary ensures
\[
C_1c_0\le2\min\{1-c,C-1\}.
\]
The outcome frequency is fixed, so its \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\) norm is absorbed in the constants.  Hence
\[
c\le1+(2a-1)b_{n,\omega}(z)c_y(y)/2\le C,
\qquad
\|q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}}\le L.             \tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\int_0^1q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}(y,z)dy
=1+\frac{2a-1}{2}b_{n,\omega}(z)\int_0^1c_y(y)dy=1.                       \tag{3}
\]
Thus the conditional laws are normalized.  Taking \(r_n(z)=1\) gives their common marginal and satisfies the marginal density and smoothness conditions.

For this symmetric pair, \(\bar q_n=1\) and \(\Delta_n=b_{n,\omega}c_y\).  The declared Neumann inverse is explicit:
\[
\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)
=\frac{b_{n,\omega}(z)}{4\pi^2}c_y.
\]
It has zero integral and zero derivative at both endpoints.  Orthonormality of the selected covariate wavelets gives
\[
\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b_{n,\omega}(z)^2dz
=\gamma_n^2\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k^2
=\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\rho_n^2
=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}
\le\frac{c_0^2}{8\pi^2}\rho^2
\le\rho^2,                                                               \tag{4}
\]
where the penultimate inequality is the radius branch in the definition of \(\gamma_n\), and the last follows from the fixed small choice of \(c_0\).  Equations (1)--(4), together with the sampling construction, prove membership in \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\).

By the cosine second-variation lemma,
\[
\theta_{n,\omega}
=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}+O(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty^3).           \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\asymp(\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2})^2\), whereas (1) makes \(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty=o(1)\).  Therefore the remainder in (5) is \(o(\gamma_n^2M_{J_z})\), uniformly in \(\omega\), proving the asserted endpoint separation.

Under \(\mathbb P_n^0\), let \(L_\omega\) be the likelihood ratio of the two-arm product alternative.  Arm and observation independence, \(\int_0^1c_y^2dy=1/2\), and wavelet orthonormality imply, for sign vectors \(\omega,\omega'\),
\[
E_0(L_\omega L_{\omega'})
=\left\{1+\frac18\int b_{n,\omega}b_{n,\omega'}dz\right\}^{2n}
=\left\{1+\frac{\gamma_n^2}{8}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k\omega'_k\right\}^{2n}.              \tag{6}
\]
The factor in braces is positive by (2).  Since \(\log(1+x)\le x\) for \(x>-1\), averaging (6) over independent uniform sign vectors and putting \(\epsilon_k=\omega_k\omega'_k\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)
&\le E_\epsilon\exp\left\{\frac{n\gamma_n^2}{4}
\sum_{k=1}^{M_{J_z}}\epsilon_k\right\}\\
&=\{\cosh(n\gamma_n^2/4)\}^{M_{J_z}}
\le\exp\{n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}/32\}.
\end{aligned}                                                             \tag{7}
\]
The last inequality follows from the power series bound \(\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}\).  Thus the claim holds for every fixed \(C_\chi\ge1/32\).  At \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\),
\[
n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}
\le Cc_0^4n^2 2^{-(4s_z+d)J_z}=O(c_0^4),
\]
and
\[
\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}
\asymp c_0^2\min\{2^{-2s_zJ_z},\rho^2\}
\asymp c_0^2\min\{r_{n,d}(s_z),\rho^2\}.                                \tag{8}
\]

For the no-covariate family, \(q_{a,t}^0=1+(2a-1)tc_y/2\) integrates to one.  The same \(L>1\) slack and a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood of zero give positivity, the upper density bound, and the H\"older constraint.  If \(|t'-t|\le Cn^{-1/2}\), then positivity and \((\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2\le(x-y)^2/y\) imply for one observation
\[
H^2(q_{a,t}^0,q_{a,t'}^0)
\le\int_0^1\frac{\{q_{a,t}^0(y)-q_{a,t'}^0(y)\}^2}{q_{a,t'}^0(y)}dy
\le C'(t-t')^2.
\]
For product measures, expanding \(1-\prod_j(1-H_j^2/2)\) bounds the product squared Hellinger distance by the sum of the component distances.  Across the two independent samples this is at most \(2nC'(t-t')^2=O(1)\), which completes the final assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse is tied to a finite sign family whose normalization, positivity, H\"older membership under the explicit \(L>1\) slack, shared marginal, local radius, endpoint gap, and mixture chi-square distance are all calculated. \textit{Gap.} BickelRitov1988 and IngsterSuslina2003 provide quadratic-functional mixture templates, but not this two-arm conditional-density construction preserving a common marginal and exact conditional normalization. \textit{Consumer.} The conditional minimax frontier uses the family in the zero and quadratic zones, and the no-covariate theorem reuses its fixed-frequency testing calculation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-second-order-expansion]\label{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}
Let \(d\ge1\), put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\), let \(J_y,J_z\to\infty\), let \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), and use the observable anisotropic estimator with the declared pilot tuning. Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\), the exact-\(\theta_n\) Hoeffding expansion is
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n},
\]
where
\[
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})=\{1+o(1)\}\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n,
\qquad
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0
\quad\text{at conditional equality},
\]
and
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\lesssim
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.
\]
The leading deterministic component of \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) is the negative full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy of the actual pilot discrepancy error; it vanishes for an oracle pilot.  The true-discrepancy energy gaps \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) do not occur in this expansion. At the corrected \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\),
\[
\left\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\right\|_2
\lesssim
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}
+n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
No covariance estimate enters this point-estimator expansion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each fold \(e\), condition on the opposite-fold sigma-field. By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), the evaluation observations are then independent of every fitted object.  Define \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\) to be the average of the oracle first Hoeffding projections and \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\) to be the average canonical diagonal-free projection with population raw-moment Hessian \(B_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  By the definition of \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\),
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{1}
\]
This is an algebraic identity, not an asymptotic definition of the target.  The conditional von Mises calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) shows that the conditional mean component of \(\mathbb R\) is
\[
-\{\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)\}
+O\{E_n^{2+a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}E_n^2\}.              \tag{2}
\]
Thus it vanishes for an oracle pilot and has the negative full-minus-projected sign.  In particular, the full-minus-projected energies of the true discrepancy, \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\), cannot appear in (1): with an oracle pilot the plug-in is exactly \(\theta_n\), both centered score averages have expectation zero, and the diagonal-free quadratic correction has expectation zero.

The \(L^2\) conclusion of the pilot lemma and the amplitude bound proved there give, uniformly over the model class,
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\lesssim
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac A n\right\},          \tag{3}
\]
where \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\). This proves the asserted remainder inequality.

We next verify the two stochastic scales.  Put
\[
H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv,
\qquad u_n(\cdot,z)=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z).
\]
The zero conditional mass of \(\Delta_n\), the Neumann equation, and integration by parts give
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_n(y,z)=H_n(y,z),
\qquad
\rho_n^2=\int\frac{H_n(y,z)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy\,dR_n(z).       \tag{4}
\]
The exact one-dimensional Kantorovich score has derivative equal, up to arm sign and the fixed factor two, to the displacement of the optimal quantile map.  The mean-value theorem and \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\) make that displacement comparable in \(L^2\) to \(H_n\).  Centering within an arm removes only an additive constant.  Equation (4), the common-marginal score term, and independence of the arms therefore imply
\[
n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\rho_n^2.       \tag{5}
\]
When \(\rho_n=0\), (4) gives \(H_n=0\), hence \(\Delta_n=0\) almost everywhere, the two conditional laws coincide, and the first derivative and \(\mathbb L\) vanish.

For the canonical projection, independence and diagonal deletion leave only identical or reversed observation pairs. Direct pair counting with \(m=n/2\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U)
={}&\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_0)^2\}+\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_1)^2\}}{m(m-1)}
+\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_0B\Gamma_1)}{m^2}\\
={}&\frac{4\operatorname{tr}\{(\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2})^2\}}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}
=\frac{A^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.                                   \tag{6}
\end{split}
\]
Here all matrices carry the indices \((J_y,J_z,n)\), and the last equality is the definition of \(A\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\). This proves the stochastic assertions.

At the corrected tuning, \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\) give
\[
\frac A n\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}2^{-s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}2^{-(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}. \tag{7}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{\{2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d\}/(2s_z+d)+o(1)}=o(n^2), \tag{8}
\]
because \(2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d<4s_z+2d=2(2s_z+d)\) and \(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}<n\) for all large \(n\).  Substitute (7)--(8) into (3), use \(\rho_n\le\rho\), and apply Minkowski's inequality to (1), (5), and (6). This gives the displayed root-mean-square bound. No step invokes \(\widehat\Gamma\), proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem now expands the explicit estimator directly around \(\theta_n\) as linear plus canonical quadratic fluctuation plus the actual pilot remainder. \textit{Gap.} The former true-discrepancy terms fail the oracle-pilot check and have been removed. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem supplies the corrected Gram upper envelope and the observable-center remainder bound for future inference.

\begin{theorem}[thm:mixed-spectral-transition]\label{thm:mixed-spectral-transition}
Let \(d\ge1\), \(J_y,J_z\to\infty\), and \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\).  Define the infeasible oracle-score, population-kernel statistic
\[
\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
=\theta_n+\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
\]
and put \(S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}\).  Suppose that \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\) and
\[
\sqrt n\left\{
\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}
\right\}=o(S_n).
\]
Then the finite-\(n\) Gaussian-quadratic proxy based on the population raw-moment kernel satisfies
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\left[\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal L\left[\frac1{S_n}\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}\right]
\right)\to0,
\]
where
\[
K_{J_y,J_z,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2},
\qquad
A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2=4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2,
\]
\[
\eta_{k,n}=2^{-1/2}v_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt n\,\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}.
\]
The same proxy and the normalized oracle statistic are diffuse locally Gaussian:
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\left[\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+nV_{L,n}}{S_n^2}\right)
\right)\to0.
\]
At conditional equality all shifts and the linear projection vanish, so
\[
\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}
=\frac{n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
At a fixed unequal law, if \(\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}/A_{J_y,J_z,n}\to\infty\), then
\[
\frac{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n}{\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
No fixed or subsequential non-Gaussian chaos survives in this growing-covariate-resolution regime.  This theorem concerns the infeasible population experiment; transferring it to one observable multiplier interval is not asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), abbreviate \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV}\). By \(d\ge1\), \(J_z\to\infty\), and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\),
\[
A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{A}
\lesssim M_n^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(S>0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), so every normalization below is well defined.

We first compare the exact oracle first projection with the linear part of the noncentral quadratic proxy. Define
\[
F_{a,n}(y\mid z)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(u,z)du,
\quad
T_{n,z}=F_{1,n}^{-1}(\cdot\mid z)\circ F_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),
\quad
H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(u,z)du.
\]
The conditional densities are continuous by \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) and bounded below by \(c>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), so the conditional distribution functions are strictly increasing and the map \(T_{n,z}\) is well defined. Zero conditional mass gives the boundary identities \(H_n(0,z)=H_n(1,z)=0\). If \(g=T_{n,z}(y)-y\), the one-dimensional transport identity is
\[
\int_y^{y+g}q_{1,n}(u,z)du=-H_n(y,z). \tag{2}
\]
The density lower bound implies \(|g|\le c^{-1}|H_n(y,z)|\). With \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\), the outcome H\"older condition gives
\[
\left|\int_y^{y+g}\{q_{1,n}(u,z)-q_{1,n}(y,z)\}du\right|
\le C_0|g|^{1+a_y}.
\]
Solving (2) for \(g\), using \(|q_{1,n}-\bar q_n|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty/2\) and \(|H_n|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\), yields uniformly as \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\),
\[
T_{n,z}(y)-y
=-\frac{H_n(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}
+O\!\left(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}|H_n(y,z)|\right). \tag{3}
\]

Let \(u_n(\cdot,z)=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\). Integrating its Neumann equation from \(0\) to \(y\), using \(\partial_yu_n(0,z)=0\), and then integrating by parts with \(H_n(0,z)=H_n(1,z)=0\), gives
\[
\partial_yu_n(y,z)=-\frac{H_n(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)},
\qquad
\rho_n^2=\int\Delta_nu_n\,dy\,dR_n
=\int\frac{H_n^2}{\bar q_n}\,dy\,dR_n. \tag{4}
\]
The fixed upper and lower density bounds therefore imply \(\|H_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}\asymp\rho_n\).

Let \(f_n(z)=W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))\), and let \(\ell_{a,n}\) denote the exact conditional Kantorovich derivative. Under the shared-marginal tangent restriction from \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), the exact centered arm score is
\[
s_{a,n}(y,z)
=\frac12\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}
+\ell_{a,n}(y,z)-\int_0^1\ell_{a,n}(u,z)q_{a,n}(u,z)du. \tag{5}
\]
The first term is the common-marginal component. From (2), the density bounds, and (4),
\[
f_n(z)\lesssim\int_0^1H_n(y,z)^2dy,
\quad
\int f_n\,dR_n\lesssim\rho_n^2,
\quad
\sup_z f_n(z)\lesssim\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^2.
\]
Consequently, since \(0<a_y\le1\) and \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty<1\) eventually,
\[
\|f_n-\theta_n\|_{L^2(R_n)}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
\le\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}. \tag{6}
\]
For the conditional potentials, the one-dimensional derivative identities are
\[
\partial_y\ell_{0,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}(y)\},
\qquad
\partial_y\ell_{1,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}^{-1}(y)\}.
\]
Apply (3) once as written and once after interchanging the arms. Since \(\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n\), subtracting the conditional means fixes the irrelevant additive constants. The Poincar\'e inequality on the compact interval \([0,1]\), together with the density bounds, then gives
\[
\max_a\left\|
\ell_{a,n}-\int\ell_{a,n}q_{a,n}
-(2a-1)2\left(u_n-\int u_nq_{a,n}\right)
\right\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}. \tag{7}
\]

Let \(P_z^R\) be the weighted covariate projector, put \(\Pi=P_{J_y}P_z^R\), and write \(\mathcal G_n\) for the operator that applies \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) fiberwise in \(z\). Formula (4), positivity of \(\bar q_n\), and the anisotropic H\"older bounds imply that \(u_n\) has uniformly bounded outcome modulus of order \(s_y+1\) and covariate modulus of order \(s_z\). The boundary-wavelet characterization permitted by \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\), equivalently the weighted complement argument underlying \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), therefore yields
\[
\|(I-P_{J_y})u_n\|_2\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
\|(I-P_z^R)P_{J_y}u_n\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{8}
\]
If \(\mu_a=E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,1,n})\), define the projected quadratic score
\[
s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}(w)
=(2a-1)2\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\{\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(w)-\mu_a\}.
\]
The two Gram conjugations in \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\) give the exact coordinate identity
\[
\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (5)--(9) imply
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim
\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{10}
\]

For fold \(e\), put
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\quad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}. \tag{11}
\]
The covariance bounds in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) justify the inverse. By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), the two vectors \(Z_{e,n}\) are independent and centered, and each has identity covariance. Define
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,
\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}Z_{e,n}. \tag{12}
\]
An empirical average of a centered score error having \(L^2\)-norm \(r\), after multiplication by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm at most a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt n\,r\), by independence of the \(2n\) observations. Hence (10) and the theorem's score-approximation premise give
\[
\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_2=o(S). \tag{13}
\]

Let \((\nu_k,v_k)\) be the eigenpairs of \(K\) and set \(Z_{e,k}=v_k^{\top}Z_{e,n}\). The definition of \(\eta_{k,n}\) and diagonalization give
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2. \tag{14}
\]
Because \(n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})=V\), equation (13), \(\sqrt{nV}\le S\), and the reverse triangle inequality for \(L^2\)-norms yield
\[
8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2). \tag{15}
\]

Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\). The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) hold. Indeed, \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\); the score summands in (12) are centered and uniformly bounded by the localized projected Green-kernel bound proved there together with the fixed density bounds; and (14)--(15) give \(EG_n^2=O(S^2)\). That lemma supplies a martingale-difference representation of \(G_n+Q_n\), conditional Lindeberg, and convergence of its predictable quadratic variation in \(L^2/S^2\). Canonical-kernel orthogonality, the lemma's variance identity, and (15) give
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2). \tag{16}
\]

For completeness, the limiting calculation follows directly from these bounds. For fixed \(t\), condition successively on the ordered observations and use
\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}.
\]
Conditional Lindeberg makes the sum of Taylor remainders tend to zero, and predictable-variance convergence replaces the sum of conditional second moments by (16). Iteration gives, along every subsequence on which \((A^2+nV)/S^2\) converges,
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\!\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\longrightarrow0. \tag{17}
\]

Define the Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), one-dimensional Gaussian integration gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right]. \tag{18}
\]
By (1) and (15),
\[
\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\to0,
\quad
\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\frac{nV+o(S^2)}{S^2},
\quad
\sum_{e,k}c_{e,k}^2=\frac{A^2}{2S^2}. \tag{19}
\]
Expanding (18), the total cubic remainder from the logarithm and the denominator remainder in the linear Gaussian term are each bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\max_k|\nu_k|/S\) times a bounded sum in (19). Therefore
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}(A^2+nV)+o(1). \tag{20}
\]

Set \(\sigma_n^2=(A^2+nV)/S^2\). Since \(A,\sqrt{nV}\ge0\),
\[
\frac12\le\sigma_n^2\le1. \tag{21}
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence along which \(\sigma_n^2\) converges. Along that further subsequence, (17) and (20) give the same centered normal limit for \((G_n+Q_n)/S\) and \(\mathcal Q_n^G/S\). The bounded-Lipschitz metric metrizes weak convergence on \(\mathbb R\), so the triangle inequality and the subsequence criterion prove both the Gaussian-quadratic approximation and its displayed diffuse normal approximation. Finally, (13) replaces \(G_n+Q_n\) by
\[
n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{22}
\]

At conditional equality, \(\Delta_n=0\). Hence \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\), every \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\). Taking \(S=A\) in (18)--(21) proves
\[
\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{A}
=\frac{n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}}{A}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The decisive spectral ratio is (1), so no fixed or subsequential non-Gaussian chaos can survive when \(d\ge1\) and \(J_z\to\infty\).

At a fixed unequal law, the exact scores in (5) are bounded because the support and squared-distance cost are bounded and the densities are bounded away from zero. Unequal conditional laws on a set of positive \(R_n\)-measure make at least one conditional potential nonconstant; positivity of the corresponding arm density then gives \(V>0\). Expanding the characteristic function of the independent centered score summands as in (17), now with maximal normalized summand \(O(n^{-1/2})\), proves
\[
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
Moreover, the quadratic variance identity gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})}{\sqrt{V/n}}
=\{1+o(1)\}\frac{A}{\sqrt{nV}}\longrightarrow0
\]
under the stated dominance condition. Combining this with (22) proves the final regular Gaussian limit. All claims concern \(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\); no observable multiplier or confidence interval has been introduced.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies the accepted population Gaussian--quadratic array, its noncentral linear representation, and forced Gaussianization without false bias centering. \textit{Gap.} It deliberately stops at the infeasible population-score, population-kernel experiment; observable local-shift estimation, joint multiplier coupling, and conditional quantile calibration do not follow from the contraction proof. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem is the population limit experiment underlying the remaining honest-calibration question.

\begin{theorem}[thm:conditional-minimax-frontier]\label{thm:conditional-minimax-frontier}
Assume \(L>1\), put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\), and fix \(d\ge1\). For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), put \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\). There are constants \(c_1>0\) and \(C_{1,\tau}<\infty\), depending only on the fixed class constants and, for the upper constant, on \(\tau\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_1\mathfrak R_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\le\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}\{E(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\]
\[
\le C_{1,\tau}\min\!\left\{
\rho^2,
\rho n^{-1/2}
+n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2,
n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}
\right\}.
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the additional candidate \(n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}\) may be inserted in the last minimum. Here
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)=\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\}.
\]
The nonzero upper candidates are attained, respectively, by the observable Gram-corrected estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}\) at the corrected pilot-error balancing resolutions, the median-aggregated SmoothCOT estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}\), and, when \(s_z\ge1\), the median-aggregated direct-COT estimator \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}\); the first entry is attained by the zero estimator. The quadratic and zero-zone lower bounds hold on the explicit sign-paired family. In the zone \(\rho\gg n^{-1/2}\), the constant-in-\(z\) fixed-frequency two-point family supplies the regular \(\rho n^{-1/2}\) lower bound.

The corrected Gram scale is strictly slower than the intrinsic quadratic scale:
\[
n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}
=o\!\left\{n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}\right\}.
\]
Thus the present primitive-model analysis gives an honest two-sided bracket rather than a matched conditional rate in the nontrivial quadratic zone; the SmoothCOT and direct-COT envelopes can sharpen the upper side but do not by themselves close the intrinsic exponent.  The lower frontier has the intrinsic elbow \(s_z=d/4\): when \(s_z>d/4\), \(\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/(2(4s_z+d))}\) delimit its zero, quadratic, mixed, and regular terms; when \(s_z\le d/4\), its quadratic term prevents a uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) description. Tightness of the displayed bracket outside the zero and any separately regular subregimes is not asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\) and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\). The inverses exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c>0\). If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the Neumann equation gives
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)u_z'(y)=H_z(y),
\qquad
\int_0^1\Delta_nu_z\,dy=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(F_{0,z}\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=F_{1,z}\{Q_{1,z}(v)\}=v\), the mean-value theorem gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}                \tag{2}
\]
for an intermediate point \(\zeta_{v,z}\). Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) in the quantile representation of \(W_2^2\), and using \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in (1)--(2), gives fixed \(0<c_E<C_E<\infty\) such that
\[
c_E\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy
\le W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
\le C_E\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy.              \tag{3}
\]
The shared marginal allows integration against one \(R_n\). By the definitions of \(\rho_n\), \(\theta_n\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}equality\text{-}radius}\),
\[
c_E\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_E\rho_n^2\le C_E\rho^2.           \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has uniform root-mean-square risk at most \(C_E\rho^2\).

For the observable Gram estimator, set
\[
g_n=n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\qquad
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The proved \(L^2\) expansion in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), the tuning identities, and \(\rho_n\le\rho\) give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2}+g_n+p_n.                                 \tag{5}
\]
This is one explicit sample map; no law-specific nuisance appears in its definition.

For fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), the established \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}.                       \tag{6}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.                      \tag{7}
\]
For fixed \((n,d,s_y,s_z,\rho)\), choose before observing the data the statistic among the zero, Gram-corrected, SmoothCOT, and applicable direct-COT maps having the smallest displayed deterministic bound. This remains one data-driven estimator and proves the theorem's upper minimum.

For the zero and quadratic converse, use \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\). The established \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) puts every component in the same model class and, after reducing the fixed \(c_0\) if necessary, gives
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)ge c_M\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{8}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\chi^2(P,Q)^{1/2}/2\), so the testing affinity is bounded away from zero. Thresholding any estimator halfway between the null and alternative endpoints, and using \(E|X|\le(E X^2)^{1/2}\), yields
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-  heta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                               \tag{9}
\]
This sign mixture supplies only the zero and quadratic terms.

The regular lower bound uses a separate two-point argument. Extend \(q_{a,t}^{0}\) constantly in \(z\). For \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), take \(t=c_7\min\{\rho,t_0\}\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\), with fixed sufficiently small positive constants. Both pairs have inverse-elliptic radius at most \(\rho\), and the product Hellinger bound in the mixture-check lemma leaves positive testing affinity. If \(\Theta(t)\) is the endpoint, implicit differentiation of the conditional quantiles gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}.                               \tag{10}
\]
The denominator is bounded away from zero. The established cosine second-variation lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\). Continuity of \(\Theta''\) on a small fixed neighborhood then gives
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c\min\{\rho,t_0\}n^{-1/2}
\gtrsim\rho n^{-1/2},                                           \tag{11}
\]
where the last constant is uniform because \(0\le\rho\le1\). The same testing reduction proves the regular term.

If \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}=n^{-b}\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), where \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), and (9) has order \(\rho^2\). If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), splitting according as \(\rho^2\le r_{n,d}\) or \(\rho^2>r_{n,d}\) gives
\[
\max\!\left\{\min(\rho^2,r_{n,d}),
\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right\}
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}\}.                   \tag{12}
\]
Equations (9)--(12) prove the lower frontier.

It remains to compare the two sides honestly. Write \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e\), where
\[
e=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.
\]
Since
\[
e<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d},                       \tag{13}
\]
the polynomial exponent of \(g_n\), namely \((2s_z+ed)/(2s_z+d)\), is strictly smaller than \(b\). Therefore
\[
n^{-b}=o(g_n).                                                    \tag{14}
\]
This proves that the certified Gram rate does not close the intrinsic quadratic term. Also, \(\tau<s_z\) implies
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12,                                          \tag{15}
\]
and, when \(s_z\ge1\),
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\min\!\left\{\frac12,\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}\right\}. \tag{16}
\]
Thus neither global fallback alone certifies the intrinsic exponent.

Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}\) gives
\[
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\qquad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.                          \tag{17}
\]
The latter tends to zero exactly when \(s_z>d/4\). When \(s_z\le d/4\), the equality-local quadratic lower term is at least root-\(n\) order while the first projection vanishes at equality, excluding a uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) description. Equations (4)--(17) prove every assertion, including that the remaining conclusion is a bracket rather than a matched rate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem proves the unchanged intrinsic lower frontier and the best certified explicit upper map after optimizing the corrected pilot-error remainder. \textit{Gap.} The observable Gram scale is strictly slower than the intrinsic quadratic exponent, so the result is a loose bracket rather than a matched pair. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can compare the zero, corrected Gram, SmoothCOT, and direct-COT guarantees without mistaking an unproved match for a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[thm:no-covariate-recovery]\label{thm:no-covariate-recovery}
Assume \(L>1\). When \(d=0\), use the no-covariate growing-outcome-resolution construction rather than setting \(d=0\) in the conditional tuning rule. Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}\{E(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}\asymp\mathfrak R_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)=\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.
\]
At equality under the uniform density, the full-sample bias-retaining estimator has the exact benchmark
\[
n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2,
\]
where \(J_y^0\to\infty\), \(2^{J_y^0}=o(n)\), and \(2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^0}=o(n^{-1})\). Subtracting \(2\sum_{k\le2^{J_y^0}}(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}/n\) yields the centered chaos. Thus the outcome resolution genuinely grows, the quadratic scale is \(n^{-1}\), and \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\). This is the two-sample specialization of the del Barrio--Gin\'e--Utzet weighted quantile-process law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
When \(d=0\), write \(D=Q_{1}-Q_{0}\), \(\widehat D=\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n}\), and \(P_J=P^Q_{J_y}\). Then \(\theta_n=\|D\|_2^2\) by the one-dimensional quantile representation, whereas the estimator is the explicit sample map \(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}=\|P_J\widehat D\|_2^2\).

The density lower bound implies a uniform quantile risk. Indeed, the event \(\widehat Q_a(u)>Q_a(u)+t\) is a lower tail event for a binomial variable with success probability at least \(u+ct\), and the reverse inequality is an upper tail event with the same gap. Exponential Markov applied to that binomial variable and integration in \(t\) give
\[
\sup_{a,u}E|\widehat Q_a(u)-Q_a(u)|^2\le C/n,
\qquad E\|\widehat D-D\|_2^2\le C/n.                                \tag{1}
\]
The same calculation with fourth moments gives \(E\|\widehat D-D\|_2^4\le C/n^2\). Positive \(s_y\)-H\"older densities have \((s_y+1)\)-smooth quantile maps, and the wavelet approximation bound therefore gives
\[
\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}.                            \tag{2}
\]
Expanding the two squares, using that \(P_J\) is a contraction, and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the cross term yields
\[
\{E(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\|D\|_2n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}\}.                 \tag{3}
\]
The density bounds and the energy identity used in the minimax theorem imply \(\|D\|_2\asymp\rho_n\le\rho\). At \(J_y=J_y^0\), (3) is \(O(\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1})\); the zero estimator has risk \(O(\rho^2)\).

The fixed-frequency witnesses in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) have bounded product Hellinger distance for increments no larger than \(n^{-1/2}\). Compare \(t=0\) with
\[
t'=c_0\min\{\rho,n^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Both laws have radius at most \(\rho\), and \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), with continuity of the second derivative, gives endpoint separation \(c\min\{\rho^2,n^{-1}\}\). If \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), compare a baseline amplitude \(c_1\rho\) with \(c_1\rho+c_2n^{-1/2}\); the calculation (8)--(9) in the conditional minimax proof gives separation \(c\rho n^{-1/2}\). The testing reduction and a split at \(\rho=n^{-1/2}\) prove the lower bound \(\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}\). This proves the minimax equivalence.

At equality under the uniform density, put \(u_{a,n}(t)=\sqrt n\{t-\widehat Q_{a,n}(t)\}\). The established \(\mathrm{lem{:}del\text{-}barrio\text{-}gine\text{-}utzet\text{-}uniform\text{-}quantile\text{-}limit}\), plus the binomial endpoint bound used in (1), gives \(u_{a,n}\Rightarrow\mathbb B_a\) in \(L^2(0,1)\), with independent bridges across arms. Hence
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})\Rightarrow\mathbb B_0-\mathbb B_1\stackrel d=\sqrt2\,\mathbb B. \tag{4}
\]
Put \(X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})\). The projectors are contractions and converge strongly to the identity because \(J_y^0\to\infty\). Strong convergence is uniform on compact subsets of \(L^2\); tightness of \(X_n\) in (4) therefore gives \(\|(I-P^Q_{J_y^0})X_n\|_2=o_P(1)\). Hence \(P^Q_{J_y^0}X_n\Rightarrow\mathbb B_0-\mathbb B_1\). Because \(x\mapsto\|x\|_2^2\) is continuous, weak convergence, first restricted to a compact set carrying arbitrarily high probability and then extended by tightness, yields
\[
n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt.    \tag{5}
\]
The covariance kernel \(s\wedge t-st\) is the Green kernel of the Dirichlet operator \(-d^2/dt^2\). Direct differentiation shows that its normalized eigenfunctions are \(\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt)\) with eigenvalues \((\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\). Expanding the Gaussian bridge in this orthonormal basis gives
\[
2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(2\sum_{k\le2^{J_y^0}}(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\to2\sum_{k\ge1}(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}=1/3\), subtracting its stated \(n^{-1}\) multiple centers the limit in (6). The scale is therefore \(n^{-1}\), and equating \(\rho^2\), \(\rho n^{-1/2}\), and \(n^{-1}\) gives \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:observable-pilot-feasibility-frontier]\label{prop:observable-pilot-feasibility-frontier}
Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\) and \(e=\beta/(2\beta+1)=\{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}\}^{-1}\). For \(d\ge1\), the corrected full-minus-projected pilot-error term is bounded by
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.
\]
Balancing its covariate component with \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\asymp2^{dJ_z/2}/n\), and then making the outcome component of the same order, gives exactly
\[
2^{J_z^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/(2s_z+d)},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2s_z/\{(s_y+1)(2s_z+d)\}},
\]
with optimized scale
\[
n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}.
\]
The corresponding projection dimension is \(o(n^2)\).  Moreover,
\[
e<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d},
\]
and hence, including the positive logarithmic power in \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\),
\[
\frac{n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}}{n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus there is no smoothness region in which the corrected certified Gram envelope is uniformly absorbed into \(\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\); the earlier pilot-feasibility region disappears once the actual pilot-error Hessian gap is retained.

For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), the explicit median-aggregated SmoothCOT estimator satisfies, uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\left\{E(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\lesssim_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau},
\qquad a_\tau=\frac{\tau}{2\tau+d+1}.
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the median-aggregated direct-COT estimator satisfies
\[
\left\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.
\]
Accordingly, the certified upper bracket is the deterministic minimum of the zero-estimator, corrected Gram, SmoothCOT, and applicable direct-COT envelopes.  The two global envelopes may sharpen the upper side, but \(a_\tau<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}\) and, when \(s_z\ge1\), \(1/(d+2)<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}\), so neither proves the intrinsic quadratic exponent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{def{:}pilot\text{-}tuning\text{-}and\text{-}studentizer}\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad
e=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.                                 \tag{1}
\]
The corrected pilot lemma bounds the projection part of the actual pilot-error Hessian by
\[
R_J=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.                               \tag{2}
\]
By \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), the canonical quadratic standard deviation is
\[
\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z/2}}n.                 \tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(J_y\), the right side of (2) decreases in \(J_z\) and (3) increases in \(J_z\). Their maximum is minimized, up to fixed factors, at their intersection:
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}2^{-s_zJ_z}
=\frac{2^{dJ_z/2}}n,
\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad
2^{J_z}=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/(2s_z+d)}.        \tag{4}
\]
Substitution in either side gives
\[
g_n=n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}.                      \tag{5}
\]
Choosing \(J_y\) minimally so that its term in (2) equals (5) gives
\[
2^{J_y}=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2s_z/\{(s_y+1)(2s_z+d)\}}. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (4)--(6) are the proposed tuning definition.

At these resolutions,
\[
D_{J_y,J_z}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{\{2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d\}/(2s_z+d)+o(1)}. \tag{7}
\]
The exponent in (7) is strictly less than two because
\[
\frac{2s_z}{s_y+1}+2d<4s_z+2d=2(2s_z+d).
\]
Since \(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}<n\) for large \(n\), (7) is \(o(n^2)\), as required by the expansion.

Put \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)\). From (1),
\[
e<\frac1{2+d/s_z}=\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}
<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d}.                                          \tag{8}
\]
Writing out (5),
\[
g_n=n^{-(2s_z+ed)/(2s_z+d)}
(\log n)^{ed/(2s_z+d)}.                                         \tag{9}
\]
The difference between the intrinsic exponent and the exponent in (9) is
\[
b-\frac{2s_z+ed}{2s_z+d}
=\frac{d\{2s_z-e(4s_z+d)\}}{(4s_z+d)(2s_z+d)}>0                \tag{10}
\]
by (8). Hence \(g_n/n^{-b}\to\infty\). Since \(g_n\) is already one positive term of the corrected certified Gram envelope, no choice of \((s_y,s_z)\) makes that envelope uniformly \(O(\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-b})\). This proves the disappearance of the former absorption region without strengthening the model class.

The SmoothCOT and direct-COT conclusions are exactly \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\). Taking the deterministic minimum with the zero and corrected Gram bounds gives the asserted upper envelope. Finally, for every \(\tau<s_z\),
\[
a_\tau=\frac{\tau}{2\tau+d+1}
<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12,                                         \tag{11}
\]
and, when \(s_z\ge1\),
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\min\!\left\{\frac12,\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}\right\}. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the global estimators may tighten the loose upper side but do not establish the intrinsic quadratic exponent. Equations (1)--(12) prove the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:anisotropic-smoothcot-median-envelope]\label{lem:anisotropic-smoothcot-median-envelope}
Let \(d\ge1\) and fix \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\). Put \(\epsilon_\tau=\{\min(s_y,s_z)-\tau\}/2\), \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\), and \(p_{a,n}(y,z)=q_{a,n}(y,z)r_n(z)\). Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
c^2\le p_{a,n}(y,z)\le C^2,
\qquad
p_{a,n}\in\mathcal C^{\tau+\epsilon_\tau}([0,1]^{d+1}),
\qquad
\sup_{a,n}\|p_{a,n}\|_{\mathcal C^{\tau+\epsilon_\tau}}\le H_\tau<\infty,
\]
where \(H_\tau\) depends only on \((c,C,L,s_y,s_z,d,\tau)\). The explicit estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}estimator}\) then satisfies
\[
\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\left\{E(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), with \(C_\tau\) depending only on the same fixed quantities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(s_*=\min\{s_y,s_z\}\) and \(\alpha=\tau+\epsilon_\tau=(s_*+\tau)/2<s_*\). By the common-marginal factorization and the density bounds,
\[
p_{a,n}(y,z)=q_{a,n}(y,z)r_n(z),
\qquad c^2\le p_{a,n}(y,z)\le C^2.                                 \tag{1}
\]
We verify the isotropic H\"older inclusion. Let \(k=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\), write a multi-index on \([0,1]^{d+1}\) as \(\mu=(\mu_y,\mu_z)\), and take \(|\mu|\le k\). Since \(k<\alpha<s_*\), every derivative below is licensed by \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}holder\text{-}smoothness}\). The product rule gives
\[
D^\mu p_{a,n}(y,z)
=\sum_{\nu_z\le\mu_z}{\mu_z\choose\nu_z}
\{D_y^{\mu_y}D_z^{\nu_z}q_{a,n}(y,z)\}
\{D_z^{\mu_z-\nu_z}r_n(z)\}.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Every factor on the right of (2) is uniformly bounded by the radius \(L\), so \(\|D^\mu p_{a,n}\|_\infty\le H_{0,\tau}\), where the constant depends only on \((L,d,k)\). For \(|\mu|=k\), apply the identity \(fg-f'g'=f(g-g')+g'(f-f')\) to each summand in (2), and change the coordinates from \((y,z)\) to \((y',z')\) one at a time. The anisotropic H\"older bounds and \(\alpha<s_y,s_z\) give
\[
|D^\mu p_{a,n}(y,z)-D^\mu p_{a,n}(y',z')|
\le H_{1,\tau}\left(|y-y'|^{\alpha-k}+\sum_{j=1}^d|z_j-z'_j|^{\alpha-k}\right)
\le H_{2,\tau}\|(y,z)-(y',z')\|_2^{\alpha-k}.                    \tag{3}
\]
For integer \(\alpha\), the same coordinate telescoping gives the source's integer-order modulus convention; alternatively one may reduce \(\epsilon_\tau\) by a fixed factor without changing any rate. Equations (2)--(3) are precisely the isotropic \(\mathcal C^\alpha\) norm bound, proving
\[
\sup_{a,n}\|p_{a,n}\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha}\le H_\tau.            \tag{4}
\]

Apply the cited \(\mathrm{lem{:}lin\text{-}gao\text{-}blanchet\text{-}glynn\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}rate}\) with outcome dimension one, covariate dimension \(d\), smoothness parameter \(\tau\), extra smoothness \(\epsilon_\tau\), and squared cost. On \([0,1]^2\),
\[
|(y_1-y_0)^2-(y_1'-y_0')^2|
\le2(|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|),                                      \tag{5}
\]
so its Lipschitz constant is fixed. The theorem's printed constant is law-dependent, so uniformity must be checked rather than assumed. Its proof has three law-dependent inputs: a reciprocal density bound, the joint wavelet \(L^2\) constant, and the high-probability positivity constant. By (1), the first is bounded by \(\max\{c^{-2},C^2\}\). At a generic resolution \(J\), orthogonality, the wavelet characterization of (4), and bounded variance of empirical coefficients give, uniformly in the law,
\[
E\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_2^2
\le C_\tau\left\{2^{-2\tau J}+\frac{2^{(d+1)J}}{N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}}\right\}. \tag{6}
\]
At \(J=J_{\tau,n}^{\mathrm{sm}}\), both terms in (6) are at most a constant times \((N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-2a_\tau}\). The positive-part normalization is inactive on the event \(\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty<c^2/2\). Compact wavelet support, coefficientwise Bernstein inequalities, and a union bound over \(O(2^{(d+1)J})\) coefficients give
\[
P\{\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty\ge c^2/2\}
\le C_\tau(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-2},                               \tag{7}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\): the deterministic bias is \(O(2^{-\alpha J})\), while the stochastic sup-norm scale is \(O\{(J2^{(d+1)J}/N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{1/2}\}=o(1)\) because \(\alpha>\tau\). Thus the constants in (6)--(7), and hence the Besov and conditional-density bounds in the cited theorem's proof, are controlled only by \((c,C,H_\tau,\tau,d)\).

For completeness, the only derivative-dependent marginal comparison in the source proof is unnecessary here. If \(f(z)=W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))\), \(\widehat f\) is its block analogue, and \(\widehat r\) is the aligned block marginal, then \(0\le f,\widehat f\le1\) and
\[
\left|\int\widehat f\,\widehat r-\int f r_n\right|
\le\|\widehat r-r_n\|_1+
2\int\{W_1(\widehat Q_0^z,Q_0^z)+W_1(\widehat Q_1^z,Q_1^z)\}\widehat r(z)\,dz. \tag{8}
\]
The first term is bounded by the joint \(L^2\) error in (6); the second is the conditional negative-order term bounded in the cited proof. Equations (6)--(8) therefore uniformize its conclusion:
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
E_P|\widehat V_{\ell,\tau,n}^{\mathrm{sm}}-\theta_n|
\le C_\tau(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-a_\tau}.                           \tag{9}
\]

Let \(\delta_n=C_\tau(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-a_\tau}\). Markov's inequality applied to (9) makes a block bad at threshold \(8\delta_n\) with probability at most \(1/8\). Disjoint blocks are independent by \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\). If the median is bad, at least \((K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}+1)/2\) blocks are bad; hence
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n|>8\delta_n\}
\le2^{K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}}8^{-(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}+1)/2}
\le2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Choose the fixed multiplier in \(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp\log n\) so that the last quantity is at most \(\delta_n^2\). Both the estimator and \(\theta_n\) lie in \([0,1]\), so
\[
E(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2
\le64\delta_n^2+2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}
\le65\delta_n^2.                                                   \tag{11}
\]
Finally, \(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp n/\log n\), and (11) is the asserted root-mean-square rate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:direct-cot-median-envelope]\label{lem:direct-cot-median-envelope}
Let \(d\ge1\) and \(s_z\ge1\). Choose an odd \(K_n\asymp\log n\), split each arm into \(K_n\) disjoint blocks of size \(N_n=\lfloor n/K_n\rfloor\), and on each block use covariate cells of side \(N_n^{-1/(d+2)}\). Project covariates to cell centers, form the empirical conditional outcome law at each occupied center, optimally couple the two projected empirical covariate marginals in Euclidean distance, integrate the squared Wasserstein distance between paired empirical conditional outcome laws, and take the median of the block values. The resulting explicit estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\left\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The squared cost obeys
\[
|(y_1-y_0)^2-(y_1'-y_0')^2|
\le2(|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Because \(s_z\ge1\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) gives
\[
\int_0^1|q_{a,n}(y,z)-q_{a,n}(y,z')|\,dy
\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.                                                \tag{2}
\]
Coupling common conditional mass identically and residual mass arbitrarily on the unit interval yields
\[
W_1(Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z'))
\le\frac12\int_0^1|q_{a,n}(y,z)-q_{a,n}(y,z')|\,dy
\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.                                                \tag{3}
\]
Thus the cost and conditional-kernel hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}lin\text{-}gao\text{-}blanchet\text{-}glynn\text{-}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}rate}\) hold uniformly.

On a block of size \(N\), let \(h_N=N^{-1/(d+2)}\). There are \(M_N\lesssim h_N^{-d}\) cells. If \(p_j\) is the common marginal mass of cell \(j\), binomial variance and Cauchy--Schwarz give, for each arm,
\[
E\|\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}-R_n^\varphi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{M_N}\sqrt{\frac{p_j(1-p_j)}N}
\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{M_N}{N}}
\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}.                                             \tag{4}
\]
Projection to cell centers costs at most \(\sqrt d\,h_N\). The triangle inequality through the common projected marginal and the bound \(W_1\le\sqrt d\,\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) on the unit cube therefore construct a coupling satisfying the cited theorem's coupling-gap premise with constant times \(N^{-1/(d+2)}\). The cited direct-COT theorem gives for each bounded block value
\[
E|\widehat\theta_N^{\mathrm{dir}}-\theta_n|
\le\delta_N:=C N^{-1/(d+2)}\log N.                                \tag{5}
\]

Markov's inequality makes a block bad at threshold \(8\delta_N\) with probability at most \(1/8\). The blocks are independent by the two-arm sampling assumption. If the median is bad, at least half the blocks are bad, and therefore
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n|>8\delta_{N_n}\}
\le2^{K_n}8^{-K_n/2}=2^{-K_n/2}.                                  \tag{6}
\]
Choose the fixed multiplier in \(K_n\asymp\log n\) so that the last probability is \(O(\delta_{N_n}^2)\). Since both the block median and the endpoint lie in \([0,1]\),
\[
\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim\delta_{N_n}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.                         \tag{7}
\]
All constants in (1)--(7) are uniform over the declared class, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:growing-kernel-contractions]\label{lem:growing-kernel-contractions}
Let \(d\ge1\), \(J_y,J_z\to\infty\), \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), and let \(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\) be the canonical quadratic projection obtained from the raw-moment Hessian. Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\). After indexing the independent evaluation observations by \(r\), it has an explicit representation \(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s)\), with each kernel canonical in either argument, such that
\[
\operatorname{Var}(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2\{1+o(1)\},
\]
\[
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}E h_{n,rs}^2\lesssim \frac{M_n}{m},
\qquad
\sum_{r<s}E h_{n,rs}^4\lesssim\frac{M_n^3}{m^2},
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K_{J_y,J_z,n}^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K_{J_y,J_z,n}^2)^2}\lesssim M_n^{-1}.
\]
If \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\) is the projected linear term, its summands are centered and uniformly bounded, \(E G_n^2=O(S_n^2)\), and \(S_n\ge A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), then for the filtration generated by the ordered observations and
\[
\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(W_s,W_r),
\]
the array is a martingale-difference array and, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sum_rE\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal D_{n,r}}{S_n}\right)^2
\mathbf 1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\epsilon S_n\}\right]\to0,
\]
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})-E(G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2\right\}^2\right]}{S_n^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}+M_n^{-1}+o(1)\to0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\). All constants below depend only on the density bounds, the compactly supported boundary-wavelet bases, and the fixed support dimension, and hence are uniform over the declared model class.

We first control the raw quadratic kernel without assuming that the two Gram conjugations preserve compact support. Replace the covariate wavelets spanning the levels through \(J_z\) by the orthonormal tensor boundary-scaling basis \(\{\varphi_{J_z,k}:k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}^{\mathrm{sc}}\}\) of the same space. This orthogonal coordinate change leaves the scalar kernel \(\Phi(w)^{\top}B\Phi(w')\), its moments, and the eigenvalues of \(K\) unchanged. Its covariate Gram matrix is
\[
G_z(k,l)=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\varphi_{J_z,k}(v)\varphi_{J_z,l}(v)r_n(v)\,dv. \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\),
\[
cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI. \tag{2}
\]
By compact support in \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\), there is a fixed integer \(R\) such that \(G_z(k,l)=0\) whenever the lattice distance between \(k\) and \(l\) exceeds \(R\). Put \(a=(C+c)/2\) and \(E=I-G_z/a\). Equation (2) gives
\[
\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le q:=\frac{C-c}{C+c}<1,
\]
whereas bandedness gives \((E^j)_{kl}=0\) if \(\operatorname{dist}(k,l)>Rj\). The norm-convergent Neumann series therefore yields the entrywise inverse localization
\[
|G_z^{-1}(k,l)|
=\left|a^{-1}\sum_{j\ge0}(E^j)_{kl}\right|
\le \frac{a^{-1}}{1-q}q^{\lceil\operatorname{dist}(k,l)/R\rceil}. \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, for
\[
\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)=\sum_{k,l}\varphi_{J_z,k}(z)G_z^{-1}(k,l)\varphi_{J_z,l}(v),
\]
the wavelet sup-norm bound and bounded overlap imply
\[
|\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)|
\le C_0M_n\exp\{-c_0 2^{J_z}\|z-v\|_2\}. \tag{4}
\]

For the outcome coordinate, let \(R^y_{J_y}\) be the kernel of the orthogonal projector onto the retained zero-mean outcome space. Compact support and bounded overlap give
\[
\sup_{J_y,y}\int_{\mathcal Y}|R^y_{J_y}(y,u)|\,du\le C_1. \tag{5}
\]
The Green kernel of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) is uniformly bounded. Indeed, if \(u=\mathcal G_{n,z}h\) and \(\int_0^1h=0\), integration of the differential equation and the Neumann boundary condition gives
\[
u'(y)=-\bar q_n(y,z)^{-1}\int_0^yh(v)\,dv.
\]
The lower density bound in \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), followed by the normalization \(\int_0^1u=0\), represents \(u\) as an integral of \(h\) against a kernel bounded only in terms of \(c\). Combining this representation with (5) on both sides of the inverse shows that the kernel \(g_{J_y}(y,y';z)\) of \(P_{J_y}\mathcal G_{n,z}P_{J_y}\) obeys
\[
\sup_{J_y,y,y',z}|g_{J_y}(y,y';z)|\le C_2. \tag{6}
\]

The full projection Gram matrix is the identity on the orthonormal outcome coordinates tensored with \(G_z\). Expanding both Gram inverses in \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\) therefore gives the exact identity
\[
b_J\{(y,z),(y',z')\}
:=\Phi(y,z)^{\top}B\Phi(y',z')
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)\kappa_{J_z}(z',v)
g_{J_y}(y,y';v)\,dR_n(v). \tag{7}
\]
Thus both raw-moment Gram conjugations are included. Equations (4), (6), the upper density bound, and \(\|z-v\|_2+\|z'-v\|_2\ge\|z-z'\|_2\) imply
\[
|b_J\{(y,z),(y',z')\}|
\le C_3M_n\exp\{-c_1 2^{J_z}\|z-z'\|_2\}. \tag{8}
\]
Because each joint arm density is at most \(C^2\), integration of (8) over a covariate tube of volume \(O(M_n^{-1})\) gives, for \(p\in\{2,4\}\),
\[
\max_{a,b}\iint|b_J(w,w')|^p\,dP_{a,n}(w)dP_{b,n}(w')
\le C_4M_n^{p-1},
\]
\[
\max_{a,b,w}\int|b_J(w,w')|^p\,dP_{b,n}(w')
\le C_4M_n^{p-1}. \tag{9}
\]
Let \(\mu_a=E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi(W)\) and
\[
b_{ab}^{\circ}(w,w')=
\{\Phi(w)-\mu_a\}^{\top}B\{\Phi(w')-\mu_b\}.
\]
Expanding the two centerings expresses \(b_{ab}^{\circ}\) as the raw kernel minus its two one-coordinate conditional expectations plus its expectation. Jensen's inequality applied to each conditional expectation preserves the orders in (9). Hence
\[
E|b_{ab}^{\circ}(W_a,W_b)|^2\lesssim M_n,
\qquad
E|b_{ab}^{\circ}(W_a,W_b)|^4\lesssim M_n^3, \tag{10}
\]
and the conditional second moment in either argument has \(L^2\)-norm \(O(M_n)\).

We next record the four-cycle contraction. For
\[
c_{abc}(w,w')=E_{P_{c,n}}
\{b_{ac}^{\circ}(w,W_c)b_{bc}^{\circ}(w',W_c)\},
\]
covariance algebra identifies \(\|c_{abc}\|_2^2\) with a cyclic trace containing two factors \(B\Gamma_cB\) and the two exterior arm covariances. Since \(0\preceq\Gamma_a\preceq\Gamma:=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1\), each exterior factor can be written as a contraction conjugated by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\). Cyclicity of trace and the Hilbert--Schmidt inequality then give
\[
\|c_{abc}\|_2^2\lesssim\operatorname{tr}(K^4).
\]
By \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(K^4)
\le\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\lesssim M_n,
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)^2}\lesssim M_n^{-1}. \tag{11}
\]

Index an evaluation observation by \(r=(e,a,i)\), put \(Z_r=\Phi(W_r)-E\Phi(W_r)\), and order observations fold by fold. Direct expansion of the diagonal-free statistic in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\), using \(n=2m\), gives
\[
h_{n,rs}(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2}{m-1}x^{\top}By,&e_r=e_s,\ a_r=a_s,\\[4pt]
-\dfrac{2}{m}x^{\top}By,&e_r=e_s,\ a_r\ne a_s,\\[4pt]
0,&e_r\ne e_s,
\end{cases}
\qquad
n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(Z_r,Z_s). \tag{12}
\]
Independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) and \(EZ_r=0\) show that each kernel is canonical in either argument. There are \(O(m)\) nonzero edges incident to a vertex and \(O(m^2)\) nonzero edges in total. Equations (10) and (12) therefore give
\[
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}Eh_{n,rs}^2\lesssim\frac{M_n}{m},
\qquad
\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^4\lesssim\frac{M_n^3}{m^2}. \tag{13}
\]
Canonicality makes distinct edges orthogonal, including two edges sharing exactly one vertex. Summing the edge variances and separating within-arm from cross-arm edges yields
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Var}(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
&=\frac{4m}{m-1}\sum_{a=0}^1\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_aB\Gamma_a)
+8\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_0B\Gamma_1)\\
&=4\operatorname{tr}\{B(\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1)B(\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1)\}
+O(m^{-1})\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\\
&=A^2\{1+O(m^{-1})\}.
\end{split} \tag{14}
\]
Here \(A^2=4\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\asymp M_n\) by \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), proving the variance assertion.

It remains to verify Lindeberg and predictable-variance concentration. Let
\[
q_r=\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(Z_s,Z_r),
\qquad
\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+q_r.
\]
Canonicality gives \(E(\mathcal D_{n,r}\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})=0\) and \(\sum_r\mathcal D_{n,r}=G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\). Conditional on \(W_r\), the summands in \(q_r\) are independent and centered. Thus
\[
Eq_r^4
=\sum_{s<r}Eh_{n,sr}^4
+6\sum_{s<t<r}E\!\left[E(h_{n,sr}^2\mid W_r)E(h_{n,tr}^2\mid W_r)\right]. \tag{15}
\]
The sectional bound after (10), including the \(O(m^{-1})\) edge coefficients, bounds the sum of conditional second moments in (15) by \(C_5M_n/m\). Summing over \(r\) and applying (13) gives
\[
\sum_rEq_r^4\lesssim\frac{M_n^2}{m}+\frac{M_n^3}{m^2}. \tag{16}
\]
If \(|\ell_{n,r}|\le L_0\), independence and centering imply
\[
\sum_rE\ell_{n,r}^4\le L_0^2\sum_rE\ell_{n,r}^2
=L_0^2EG_n^2=O(S_n^2).
\]
Since \(S_n\ge A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\), (16) and \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\) yield
\[
S_n^{-4}\sum_rE\mathcal D_{n,r}^4
\lesssim M_n^{-1}+m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}\longrightarrow0. \tag{17}
\]
Indeed, \(J_z\to\infty\) gives \(M_n\to\infty\), while \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)=o(m^2)\). For every \(\epsilon>0\), the bound
\[
x^2\mathbf 1\{|x|>\epsilon S_n\}\le\frac{x^4}{\epsilon^2S_n^2}
\]
and (17) prove the stated Lindeberg conclusion after summation and expectation.

Finally, direct expansion gives
\[
\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
=E(G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2+C_n+R_n+T_n, \tag{18}
\]
where
\[
\begin{split}
C_n&=2\sum_r\sum_{s<r}E\{\ell_{n,r}(W_r)h_{n,sr}(Z_s,Z_r)\mid Z_s\},\\
R_n&=\sum_r\sum_{s<r}\{E(h_{n,sr}^2\mid Z_s)-Eh_{n,sr}^2\},\\
T_n&=2\sum_r\sum_{s<t<r}E\{h_{n,sr}h_{n,tr}\mid Z_s,Z_t\}.
\end{split} \tag{19}
\]
To verify the constant in (18), canonicality gives \(E(G_n\,n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})=0\), and the orthogonality of distinct canonical edges gives \(E(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2=\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^2\). Thus the constant terms from the conditional squares are exactly \(E(G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2\).

The repeated-edge term \(R_n\) groups into independent centered functions of \(Z_s\). The conditional-second-moment \(L^2\) bound after (10), the \(O(m^{-1})\) edge coefficients, and the \(O(m)\) future edges give
\[
ER_n^2\lesssim\frac{M_n^2}{m}. \tag{20}
\]
For \(T_n\), regroup first over the unordered pair \((s,t)\). Pair kernels with disjoint edge sets are independent, and those sharing only one vertex are orthogonal by canonicality. A surviving repeated pair is the four-cycle contraction in (11); summation over possible common terminal vertices has total coefficient \(O(m^{-1})\). Since there are \(O(m^2)\) pairs,
\[
ET_n^2\lesssim m^2m^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(K^4)\lesssim M_n. \tag{21}
\]
For the mixed contraction, put \(u_r=E\{Z_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\}\). Covariance Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\|\Gamma_{a_r}^{-1/2}u_r\|_2^2\le E\ell_{n,r}^2,
\]
with the inverse understood on the covariance range. Moreover,
\[
\|\Gamma_a^{1/2}B\Gamma_b^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\|\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim1,
\]
because \(0\preceq\Gamma_a,\Gamma_b\preceq\Gamma\). Regrouping \(C_n\) over \(s\), applying Cauchy--Schwarz to its \(O(m)\) future indices, and then using independence of the resulting centered functions of \(Z_s\) proves
\[
EC_n^2\lesssim EG_n^2=O(S_n^2). \tag{22}
\]
Equations (20)--(22), \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), \(S_n^4\ge A^4\asymp M_n^2\), and \(S_n^2\gtrsim M_n\) now give
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
-E(G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2\right\}^2\right]}{S_n^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}+M_n^{-1}+o(1)\longrightarrow0. \tag{23}
\]
Equations (1)--(23) establish the claimed noncompact Gram-inverse localization, both conjugations, fourth moments, maximal influence, repeated-edge, shared-vertex, four-cycle and mixed contractions, Lindeberg condition, and predictable-variance concentration.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite Gaussian--quadratic proxy needs moment and influence control after both nonlocal raw-moment Gram conjugations. \textit{Gap.} The proof now closes exponential inverse-Gram localization, fourth moments, repeated-edge and four-cycle contractions, the mixed contraction, Lindeberg, and predictable-variance concentration from the primitive assumptions. \textit{Consumer.} The mixed spectral-transition theorem uses the lemma to compare the population quadratic statistic with its Gaussian--quadratic array and to prove diffuse Gaussianization.

\section{Interpretation}

At conditional equality, the first derivative vanishes and the second variation is an inverse-elliptic quadratic form. For \(d\ge1\), resolving the covariate coordinate replicates bounded outcome modes across \(2^{dJ_z}\) cells, so the largest Hessian eigenvalue is negligible relative to its Hilbert--Schmidt norm and the population quadratic fluctuation Gaussianizes. For \(d=0\), no such rank replication occurs and the bias-retaining empirical-quantile statistic converges to the Brownian-bridge quadratic process. Away from equality, the exact first-order transport score reappears. These population facts do not alone calibrate an interval based on estimated scores and Hessians.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact-target observable remainder contains the full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy of the actual pilot discrepancy error. Balancing that term with the quadratic fluctuation gives \(n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}\), which is strictly slower than the intrinsic converse scale \(n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\) for every finite positive smoothness pair. SmoothCOT and direct-COT estimators provide additional global upper candidates but do not establish the intrinsic equality-local exponent. The mixed limit theorem avoids this point-estimation remainder only by studying the infeasible population-score, population-kernel experiment. Observable spectral replacement is presently certified only when \(D_{J_y,J_z}\log n=o(m)\), and no uniform multiplier anti-concentration result through variance degeneracy is proved.

\section{Honest scope}

The model has scalar outcomes on \([0,1]\), two independent randomized arm samples of equal size, a shared compactly supported covariate marginal, uniformly positive bounded densities, and anisotropic H\"older smoothness. For \(d\ge1\), the note proves an intrinsic sign-mixture lower frontier and an explicit upper minimum over the zero estimator, the corrected Gram estimator, median SmoothCOT, and, when \(s_z\ge1\), median direct COT; sharpness of this bracket outside the zero and separately regular subregimes is not claimed. The Gaussian--quadratic transition is proved only for the infeasible population experiment and forces Gaussianization as covariate resolution grows. The \(d=0\) result uses a distinct empirical-quantile construction and has an exact non-Gaussian null law. Adaptation, multivariate outcomes, observational propensity estimation, unrestricted high-dimensional spectral plug-in, bracket tightness, and one honest observable mixed-regime interval remain open.
