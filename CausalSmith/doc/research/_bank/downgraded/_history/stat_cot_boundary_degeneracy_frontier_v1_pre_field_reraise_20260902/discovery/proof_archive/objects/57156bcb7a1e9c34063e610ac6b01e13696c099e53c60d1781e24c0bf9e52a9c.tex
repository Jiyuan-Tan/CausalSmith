By \(\mathrm{def{:}pilot\text{-}tuning\text{-}and\text{-}studentizer}\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad
e=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Let \(x=s_y\), \(t=d/s_z\), \(a_y=\min\{x,1\}\), \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)\), and \(\kappa=2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)\). If uniform absorption holds, setting \(\rho=0\) and retaining the positive logarithmic power forces
\[
(2+a_y)e>b.                                                       \tag{2}
\]
If \(b\le1/2\), setting \(\rho=1\) in the local pilot term forces \(2e>b\), whereas direct simplification gives
\[
b-2e=\frac{2(tx+2)}{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)}>0,                           \tag{3}
\]
a contradiction. Thus \(b>1/2\). At \(\rho_*=n^{1/2-b}\), the two denominator terms agree, and the local pilot term forces
\[
2e+a_y\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                           \tag{4}
\]
For \(0<x\le1\),
\[
b-2e-x\kappa(b-1/2)
=\frac{t^2x^3+2t^2x^2-2tx^3+5tx^2+10tx-8x^3-4x^2+8x+12}
{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)(tx+2x+3)}>0.                                    \tag{5}
\]
The coefficient of \(t^2\) is \(x^2(x+2)>0\), the coefficient of \(t\) is \(x(-2x^2+5x+10)>0\), and the constant polynomial is at least eight on \((0,1]\). Thus (4) requires \(s_y>1\). In this branch \(a_y=1\), so (2)--(4) reduce to
\[
3e>b,
\qquad
2e+\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                              \tag{6}
\]

Conversely, suppose both strict inequalities in (6) hold. The first pilot term is \(o(n^{-b})\). Since \(0<\kappa<1\), differentiation shows that
\[
f_n(\rho)=\frac{\rho^\kappa}{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-b}}
\]
is maximized at a fixed multiple of \(\rho_*\), and hence
\[
\sup_{0\le\rho\le1}f_n(\rho)\lesssim n^{b-\kappa(b-1/2)}.      \tag{7}
\]
Multiplying by \(n^{-2e}(\log n)^{2e}\) and using the second inequality in (6) proves uniform absorption. The corrected tuning changes only \(J_y^*\); by the proposed expansion it still makes both deterministic biases and the quadratic scale equal to \(n^{-b}\), so the denominator optimized here is unchanged.

The sign of the second inequality in (6) is the sign of
\[
P(x)=(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)x^2-s_z(11d+4s_z)x-12s_z^2.                \tag{8}
\]
For \(P(x)>0\), its leading coefficient must be positive, hence \(s_z>3d/4\). Its unique positive root is
\[
x_{\mathrm{crit}}=
\frac{s_z\{11d+4s_z+\sqrt{(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d)}\}}
{2(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)},                                             \tag{9}
\]
because
\[
(11d+4s_z)^2+48(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)=(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d).
\]
Moreover, \(P(1)=-3d^2-13ds_z-8s_z^2<0\), so the root lies in the already established branch \(s_y>1\), and
\[
P\!\left(\frac{4s_z}{4s_z-d}\right)
=-\frac{16s_z^2(d+2s_z)(d+4s_z)}{(4s_z-d)^2}<0                  \tag{10}
\]
shows that the second inequality in (6) implies the first. This proves the exact absorption region.

The SmoothCOT and direct-COT assertions are exactly the already proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\). Taking the deterministic minimum with the zero and corrected Gram-candidate bounds gives the asserted upper envelope. Finally,
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12,                                          \tag{11}
\]
and, when \(s_z\ge1\),
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\min\!\left\{\frac12,\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}\right\}. \tag{12}
\]
Thus either supplemental estimator can improve a larger pilot remainder but neither independently reaches the intrinsic stochastic exponent. Equations (1)--(12) prove the proposition with the corrected outcome resolution.
