Write \(\Phi=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\), \(G=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(H^{\mathrm{form}}=H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  The outcome and covariate wavelets are orthonormal for Lebesgue measure by \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\).  Therefore, for every coefficient vector \(x\),
\[
x^{\top}Gx=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}\{x^{\top}\Phi(y,z)\}^2r_n(z)\,dy\,dz.
\]
The bounds in \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) imply
\[
c\|x\|_2^2\le x^{\top}Gx\le C\|x\|_2^2.                                  \tag{1}
\]
This both proves that the inverse in the corrected projection definition exists and shows that changing from function coefficients to raw feature moments is dimension-free.  More specifically, outcome orthogonality gives the block identity \(G=I_y\otimes G_z\); hence \(G\) and \(G^{-1}\) preserve each outcome resolution level.

Let \(g=x^{\top}\Phi\), and for each \(z\) put \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\).  Every retained outcome feature has zero Lebesgue integral, so the Neumann equation in the definition of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) is solvable.  Its weak form and the boundary conditions give
\[
\int_0^1g(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)|\partial_yu_z(y)|^2\,dy.                           \tag{2}
\]
For every zero-mean \(v\in H^1(0,1)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz give \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\).  The variational identity associated with (2), together with \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), therefore yields
\[
\int_0^1g\mathcal G_{n,z}g\,dy\asymp\|g(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}^2.       \tag{3}
\]
For a boundary outcome wavelet at level \(j_y\), its antiderivative has squared \(L^2\)-norm comparable to \(2^{-2j_y}\).  Compact support gives uniformly finite overlap, including the fixed boundary block.  Applying this level by level, integrating in \(z\), and then using (1) gives
\[
x^{\top}H^{\mathrm{form}}x
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2.                 \tag{4}
\]
This is the first asserted comparison.  Because \(G^{-1}\) preserves outcome levels and is bounded above and below by (1), (4) also gives, for raw-moment coordinates,
\[
x^{\top}Bx
= (G^{-1}x)^{\top}H^{\mathrm{form}}(G^{-1}x)
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2.                 \tag{5}
\]

For an arbitrary coefficient vector \(x\), set \(f=x^{\top}\Phi\).  The covariance variational identity is
\[
x^{\top}\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int\{f(y,z)-v\}^2q_{a,n}(y,z)r_n(z)\,dy\,dz.      \tag{6}
\]
Since every retained outcome wavelet integrates to zero for every \(z\),
\[
\int\{f(y,z)-v\}^2\,dy\,dz=\|x\|_2^2+v^2.
\]
The two density-bound assumptions applied to (6) give fixed positive lower and upper multiples of \(\|x\|_2^2\).  Summing over the arms proves
\[
c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I.                 \tag{7}
\]

Congruence by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\), (5), (7), and the min--max characterization of ordered eigenvalues compare \(K=\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2}\) with the diagonal reference having outcome weight \(2^{-2j_y}\).  There are \(\asymp2^{j_y+dJ_z}\) retained coordinates at outcome level \(j_y\).  Thus, for \(2^{-2J_y}\le u\le1\),
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\asymp2^{dJ_z}\sum_{j_y\le\frac12\log_2(u^{-1})}2^{j_y}
\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}.                              \tag{8}
\]
Inverting (8) gives \(\nu_k\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}\), and hence
\[
\sum_k\nu_k^2
\asymp2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}
\asymp2^{dJ_z}.                                                           \tag{9}
\]
The operator-norm, Hilbert--Schmidt, and normalized maximal-eigenvalue conclusions follow from (9).  If \(d=0\), the multiplicity factor is one, so the Hilbert--Schmidt norm stays bounded above and away from zero as \(J_y\to\infty\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) and the vanishing moments supplied by \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\) give the anisotropic coefficient estimate
\[
|\langle\Delta_n,\psi^y_{j_y,k_y}\psi^z_\gamma\rangle_{dy\,dR_n}|
\lesssim2^{-j_y(s_y+1/2)}2^{-j_z(s_z+d/2)}.                               \tag{10}
\]
Weighted and Lebesgue coefficients are related through the uniformly coercive covariate Gram blocks in (1), so (10) has the same order in the corrected projection coordinates.  Once the first derivative is removed, truncation is quadratic in the omitted discrepancy.  The inverse-elliptic form supplies the additional outcome weight \(2^{-2j_y}\).  Squaring (10), multiplying by the level multiplicities, and summing the two geometric tails yields
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.
\]
Every comparison used only the fixed bounds and radii in the declared model class, so all constants are uniform.
