Write \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)\,dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and put
\[
H_z(y)=F_{1,z}(y)-F_{0,z}(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)\,dv.
\]
For \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the defining Neumann equation from zero to \(y\) gives \(-\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_z(y)=H_z(y)\).  Since \(H_z(0)=H_z(1)=0\), integration by parts yields
\[
\int_0^1\Delta_nu_z\,dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n|\partial_yu_z|^2\,dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy.                              \tag{1}
\]
The inverses \(Q_{a,z}\) exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c\).  For each \(v\in(0,1)\), the mean-value theorem gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}
\]
for an intermediate \(\zeta_{v,z}\).  Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) and applying \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in this identity and (1) gives constants depending only on \(c,C\) such that
\[
c_3\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy
\le W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
\le C_3\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy.                       \tag{2}
\]
The shared marginal permits integration against the same \(R_n\).  The definition of \(\rho_n\) and \(\rho_n\le\rho\) therefore imply
\[
c_4\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_4\rho_n^2\le C_4\rho^2.                    \tag{3}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has uniform root-mean-square risk at most \(C_4\rho^2\).

For the observable upper bound put
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}+
\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The corrected exact decomposition proved in the expansion theorem is
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}.                                  \tag{4}
\]
We need a second-moment, not merely probabilistic, version of its remainder.  The number of pilot coefficients is
\[
\overline D_n\asymp2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}
\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
The exponential coefficient argument used in the pilot proof, now retained with its tail parameter, gives for fixed \(x\in[0,1]\)
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
P\!\left(\max_e[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty+
\max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty]
>C\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+x\}\right)
\le C\exp\{-cnx^2/\overline D_n\}.                                     \tag{5}
\]
Clipping makes the errors bounded off the inactive event.  Integrating (5) yields fixed-moment pilot bounds.  Applying the deterministic Taylor bound (13), interpolation bound (14), Gram identity (7), resolvent bound (8), and oversampled Galerkin bound (9) from the pilot proof to these moments gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}\|R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P)}
\le Cp_n+o\!\left(\frac\rho{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+
2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right).                                   \tag{6}
\]
The Hoeffding projections are orthogonal.  Their variance calculations give
\[
\|\mathbb L\|_2\le C\rho n^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|\mathbb U\|_2\le CA_{J_y,J_z,n}/n.                                  \tag{7}
\]
At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), the corrected spectrum and approximation bounds imply
\[
A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z),
\qquad
|B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|\le Cr_{n,d}(s_z).                        \tag{8}
\]
Minkowski's inequality in (4), followed by (6)--(8), proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}.                               \tag{9}
\]
The minimax experiment treats \(\rho\) and the class constants as known.  Selecting between the zero map and the corrected sample map according to their deterministic guarantees therefore defines one estimator and proves the stated upper bound.

For the converse use \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\).  By the established \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\), a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\) puts every component in the same model class and gives
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)\ge c_5\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.        \tag{10}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio yields \(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\{\chi^2(P,Q)\}^{1/2}/2\), so the testing affinity is bounded away from zero.  Thresholding any estimator halfway between the two endpoint values converts it to a test; on either testing error its absolute estimation error is at least half the separation.  Averaging over the Rademacher mixture gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_6\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                                    \tag{11}
\]

For the regular local direction, extend \(q_{a,t}^{0}\) constantly in \(z\).  If \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), compare \(t=c_7\rho\) with \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\).  Small fixed constants preserve positivity, H\"older membership, and radius at most \(\rho\); the Hellinger calculation in the mixture-check lemma leaves their product testing affinity bounded away from zero.  Let \(\Theta(t)\) be the endpoint.  The conditional distribution functions are
\[
F_{a,t}(y)=y+(2a-1)t\sin(2\pi y)/(4\pi),
\qquad \partial_yF_{a,t}=q_{a,t}^{0}\ge c.
\]
Implicit differentiation gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}.
\]
This quotient and its first derivative are uniformly bounded near zero.  Hence \(\Theta''\) is continuous.  The established cosine second-variation lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\).  Shrinking the fixed neighborhood if necessary and integrating twice gives
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c_9t|t'-t|\ge c_{10}\rho n^{-1/2}.             \tag{12}
\]
The same two-point testing argument gives a lower bound of this order.  If \(\rho<Kn^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)=n^{-b}\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), where \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), so (11) already has order \(\rho^2\).  The elementary comparison
\[
\max\!\left[\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\},
\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right]
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}                         \tag{13}
\]
proves the stated lower frontier.

When \(p_n=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\), (9) and (13) match; otherwise they are the honest bracket.  Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) gives the displayed transition radii.  The latter tends to zero exactly when \(s_z>d/4\).  If \(s_z\le d/4\), the quadratic lower bound is at least of root-\(n\) order at equality while the first projection is identically zero, so no uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) regular procedure governs the entire class.
