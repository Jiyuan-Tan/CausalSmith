By \(\mathrm{def{:}pilot\text{-}tuning\text{-}and\text{-}studentizer}\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad
e=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.                                   \tag{1}
\]
Let \(x=s_y\), \(t=d/s_z\), and \(a_y=\min\{x,1\}\). If uniform absorption holds, setting \(\rho=0\) and retaining the positive logarithmic power forces
\[
(2+a_y)e>b.                                                         \tag{2}
\]
If \(b\le1/2\), setting \(\rho=1\) in the local pilot term forces \(2e>b\). But direct common-denominator simplification gives
\[
b-2e=\frac{2(tx+2)}{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)}>0,                              \tag{3}
\]
a contradiction. Hence \(b>1/2\). At \(\rho_*=n^{1/2-b}\in(0,1)\), the two denominator terms are equal, so the local pilot term forces
\[
2e+a_y\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                             \tag{4}
\]
For \(0<x\le1\), another common-denominator calculation gives
\[
b-2e-x\kappa(b-1/2)
=\frac{t^2x^3+2t^2x^2-2tx^3+5tx^2+10tx-8x^3-4x^2+8x+12}
{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)(tx+2x+3)}>0.                                      \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the coefficient of \(t^2\) is \(x^2(x+2)>0\), the coefficient of \(t\) is \(x(-2x^2+5x+10)>0\), and the constant polynomial is at least \(8\) on \((0,1]\). Thus (4) is impossible unless \(s_y>1\). In that branch \(a_y=1\), so (2)--(4) reduce to
\[
3e>b,
\qquad
2e+\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                                \tag{6}
\]

Conversely, suppose both strict inequalities in (6) hold. The first pilot term is \(o(n^{-b})\). Because \(0<\kappa<1\), differentiation shows that
\[
f_n(\rho)=\frac{\rho^\kappa}{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-b}}
\]
is maximized at a fixed multiple of \(\rho_*\), and hence
\[
\sup_{0\le\rho\le1}f_n(\rho)\lesssim n^{b-\kappa(b-1/2)}.         \tag{7}
\]
Multiplication by \(n^{-2e}(\log n)^{2e}\) and the second inequality in (6) proves uniform absorption.

The sign of the second inequality in (6) is the sign of
\[
P(x)=(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)x^2-s_z(11d+4s_z)x-12s_z^2.                  \tag{8}
\]
For \(P(x)>0\), its leading coefficient must be positive, so \(s_z>3d/4\). Its unique positive root is
\[
x_{\mathrm{crit}}=
\frac{s_z\{11d+4s_z+\sqrt{(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d)}\}}
{2(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)},                                               \tag{9}
\]
because
\[
(11d+4s_z)^2+48(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)=(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d).
\]
Moreover \(P(1)=-3d^2-13ds_z-8s_z^2<0\), so (9) lies in the already established branch \(s_y>1\), and
\[
P\!\left(\frac{4s_z}{4s_z-d}\right)
=-\frac{16s_z^2(d+2s_z)(d+4s_z)}{(4s_z-d)^2}<0                    \tag{10}
\]
shows that the second inequality in (6) implies the first. This proves the claimed equivalence. The Gram-corrected matching conclusion follows from \(\mathrm{thm{:}conditional\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\).

For the first supplemental estimator, \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) proves both the uniform isotropic inclusion and
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}.                       \tag{11}
\]
For the second, \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}                         \tag{12}
\]
under \(s_z\ge1\). Taking the deterministic minimum of the zero, Gram-corrected, SmoothCOT, and, where available, direct-COT guarantees proves the asserted upper envelope.

It remains to verify that no extra matching is being claimed. Since \(\tau<s_z\),
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}=b,
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12.                                            \tag{13}
\]
The middle strict inequality is equivalent to \(s_z(4s_z+3d+4)>0\). If \(s_z\ge1\), then
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\frac12,
\qquad
\frac1{d+2}<b,                                                     \tag{14}
\]
the latter being equivalent to \(4s_z(d+1)>d\). Thus the supplemental envelopes can improve a larger pilot remainder but are slower than the intrinsic stochastic scale. Equations (11)--(14) prove the supplemental and scope assertions.
