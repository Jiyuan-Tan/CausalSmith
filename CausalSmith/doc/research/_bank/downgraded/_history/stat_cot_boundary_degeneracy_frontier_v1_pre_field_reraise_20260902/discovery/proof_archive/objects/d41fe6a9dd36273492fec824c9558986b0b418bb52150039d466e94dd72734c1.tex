For \(d=0\), retain the notation \(F_a,Q_a\), and \(H=F_1-F_0\) from the proof of the conditional theorem.  Equations (1)--(3) there use only the one-dimensional density bounds, so they give
\[
 \|Q_1-Q_0\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2=\theta_n\asymp\rho_n^2\le\rho^2. \tag{13}
\]
For a pair in \(\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\), let \(P\) denote its induced two-arm product sampling law.  
Let \(D=Q_1-Q_0\), \(\widehat D=\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n}\), and \(P_J=P^Q_J\).  Orthogonality of \(P_J\) gives the exact decomposition
\[
 \widehat\theta^0_{J,n}-\theta_n
 =2\langle P_JD,P_J(\widehat D-D)\rangle
  +\|P_J(\widehat D-D)\|_2^2
  -\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2. \tag{14}
\]

We first give the required finite-sample moment bounds.  Write an observation from arm \(a\) as \(Q_a(U_i)\), where the \(U_i\) are independent uniform random variables.  For \((k-1)/n<u\le k/n\),
\(
 \widehat Q_{a,n}(u)=Q_a(U_{(k)}).
\)
Since \(q_{a,n}\ge c\), the inverse \(Q_a\) is \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz.  The beta density of \(U_{(k)}\) has mean \(k/(n+1)\), variance \(k(n+1-k)/\{(n+1)^2(n+2)\}\), and its fourth centered moment is bounded by \(C/n^2\).  Integrating over the intervals \(((k-1)/n,k/n]\) and using \(|k/(n+1)-u|\le2/n\) therefore gives, uniformly in the model class,
\[
 E\|\widehat Q_{a,n}-Q_a\|_2^2\le Cn^{-1},
 \qquad
 E\|\widehat Q_{a,n}-Q_a\|_2^4\le Cn^{-2}. \tag{15}
\]
For the second inequality, use \((\int e^2)^2\le\int e^4\) on the unit interval before applying the beta fourth-moment bound.  Independence of the arms and the triangle inequality give the same orders for \(\widehat D-D\).

By \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\), the densities are \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\); by positivity, \(Q_a'=1/(q_{a,n}\circ Q_a)\), so the usual H\"older composition and reciprocal difference inequalities, proved by iterating the defining difference quotient, give \(Q_a\in\mathcal C^{s_y+1}\) with a uniform norm bound.  The regularity and vanishing moments in \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\) then yield
\[
 \|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2\le C2^{-2(s_y+1)J}. \tag{16}
\]
Applying Minkowski and Cauchy--Schwarz to (14), and then (13), (15), and (16), gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}}
 \{E_P(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
 \le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^0}\}
 \le C'\{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}. \tag{17}
\]
The zero estimator has risk at most \(C\rho^2\) by (13).  Selecting the better of the two explicit maps proves the upper bound
\[
 C\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}. \tag{18}
\]

For the lower bound, take the fixed-frequency family in \(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}witness}\).  Comparing \(t=0\) with \(t=c_0\min\{\rho,n^{-1/2}\}\), the likelihood/Hellinger calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) is bounded and \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\) gives endpoint separation
\[
 c\min\{\rho^2,n^{-1}\}. \tag{19}
\]
For \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), compare instead \(t=c_1\rho\) with \(t'=t+c_2n^{-1/2}\).  Choosing the constants small keeps both laws in the radius-\(\rho\) class and makes their product squared Hellinger distance smaller than a fixed \(\eta<1\).  As in the proof of (11), continuity of the second derivative of the endpoint along the positive cosine path, together with \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\) from \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), gives separation at least
\[
 c_3\rho n^{-1/2}. \tag{20}
\]
The two-point testing reduction used in (10), followed by the analogue of (12) with \(r_{n,0}=n^{-1}\), combines (19)--(20) into
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}}
 \{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
 \ge c\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (18) and (21) prove the three-zone minimax assertion.

It remains to establish the exact null law.  At the uniform null, apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}del\text{-}barrio\text{-}gine\text{-}utzet\text{-}uniform\text{-}quantile\text{-}limit}\) separately to the two independent arms.  The omitted lower-tail squared norm is at most \((U_{(1)}+n^{-1})^2\), and the upper-tail bound is the same with \(1-U_{(n)}\).  The beta moment formulas used in (15) make both quantities \(o_P(1)\).  Hence the untruncated uniform quantile processes have the same \(L^2\) limits as their cited truncated versions.  Joint weak convergence and symmetry of a Brownian bridge give, in \(L^2(0,1)\),
\[
 Z_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})
 \Rightarrow \mathbb B_1-\mathbb B_0
 \stackrel{d}{=}\sqrt2\,\mathbb B. \tag{22}
\]
The projectors \(P^Q_{J_y^0}\) are contractions and converge strongly to the identity because \(J_y^0\to\infty\).  Strong convergence is uniform on every compact subset of \(L^2\); tightness of \(Z_n\), supplied by (22), therefore implies
\[
 \|(I-P^Q_{J_y^0})Z_n\|_2=o_P(1).
\]
The continuous mapping theorem and the definition of the estimator now yield
\[
 n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}
 =\|P^Q_{J_y^0}Z_n\|_2^2
 \Rightarrow 2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt. \tag{23}
\]

For completeness, the covariance kernel of \(\mathbb B\) is \(K(s,t)=s\wedge t-st\).  Direct integration, or differentiating twice with the zero boundary conditions, shows that the orthonormal functions \(\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt)\) are eigenfunctions of its covariance operator with eigenvalues \((\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\).  Gaussian orthogonality makes the corresponding coordinates independent standard normal variables.  Parseval's identity consequently gives
\[
 2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt
 \stackrel{d}{=}\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2. \tag{24}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 2\sum_{k\le2^{J_y^0}}\frac1{\pi^2k^2}\longrightarrow
 2\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac1{\pi^2k^2}=\frac13
 =E\left\{2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt\right\}.
\]
Subtracting the displayed finite sum divided by \(n\) from \(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\) therefore centers the limiting chaos in (23)--(24).  Finally, both equations \(\rho^2=n^{-1}\) and \(\rho n^{-1/2}=n^{-1}\) have solution \(\rho=n^{-1/2}\).  Hence \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\), completing the proof.
