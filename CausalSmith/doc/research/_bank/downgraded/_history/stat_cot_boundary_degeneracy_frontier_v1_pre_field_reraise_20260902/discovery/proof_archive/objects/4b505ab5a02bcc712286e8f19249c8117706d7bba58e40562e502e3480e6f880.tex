Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), abbreviate \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV}\). Since \(d\ge1\), \(J_z\to\infty\), and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) holds,
\[
A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{A}
\lesssim M_n^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(S>0\) for all large \(n\), so every displayed normalization is well defined.

We first identify the linear part of the noncentral quadratic proxy. Define
\[
F_{a,n}(y\mid z)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(u,z)\,du,
\quad
T_{n,z}=F_{1,n}^{-1}(\cdot\mid z)\circ F_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),
\quad
H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(u,z)\,du.
\]
The zero conditional mass of \(\Delta_n\) gives \(H_n(0,z)=H_n(1,z)=0\). If \(g=T_{n,z}(y)-y\), the one-dimensional transport identity is
\[
\int_y^{y+g}q_{1,n}(u,z)\,du=-H_n(y,z). \tag{2}
\]
The lower density bound gives \(|g|\le c^{-1}|H_n(y,z)|\). The outcome H\"older condition with exponent \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\) gives
\[
\left|\int_y^{y+g}\{q_{1,n}(u,z)-q_{1,n}(y,z)\}\,du\right|
\le C_0|g|^{1+a_y}.
\]
Solving (2) for \(g\), and using \(|q_{1,n}-\bar q_n|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty/2\), yields uniformly
\[
T_{n,z}(y)-y
=-\frac{H_n(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}
+O\!\left(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}|H_n(y,z)|\right). \tag{3}
\]

Let \(u_n(\cdot,z)=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\). Integrating its Neumann equation and then integrating by parts, with the boundary values of \(H_n\), gives
\[
\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n,
\qquad
\rho_n^2=\int\Delta_nu_n\,dy\,dR_n
=\int\frac{H_n^2}{\bar q_n}\,dy\,dR_n. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the fixed upper and lower density bounds imply \(\|H_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}\asymp\rho_n\).

Let \(f_n(z)=W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))\), and let \(\ell_{a,n}\) be the exact conditional Kantorovich derivative. Under the shared-marginal tangent restriction from \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), the centered arm score is
\[
s_{a,n}(y,z)
=\frac12\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}
+\ell_{a,n}(y,z)-\int_0^1\ell_{a,n}(u,z)q_{a,n}(u,z)\,du. \tag{5}
\]
Equation (2), the density bounds, (4), and \(\sup_y|H_n(y,z)|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\) imply
\[
\|f_n-\theta_n\|_{L^2(R_n)}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
\le\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y} \tag{6}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). The conditional potentials satisfy
\[
\partial_y\ell_{0,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}(y)\},
\qquad
\partial_y\ell_{1,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}^{-1}(y)\}.
\]
Apply (3) once as written and once with the arms interchanged. Since \(\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n\), the derivative of the difference between the centered potential and \((2a-1)2u_n\) has \(L^2\)-norm \(O\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}\}\). For any absolutely continuous \(v\) on \([0,1]\),
\[
v(y)-\int_0^1v(t)q_{a,n}(t,z)\,dt
=\int_0^1\left\{\mathbf 1(t\le y)-\int_t^1q_{a,n}(x,z)\,dx\right\}v'(t)\,dt,
\]
whose kernel is bounded by one. Cauchy--Schwarz therefore converts the derivative bound into
\[
\max_a\left\|
\ell_{a,n}-\int\ell_{a,n}q_{a,n}
-(2a-1)2\left(u_n-\int u_nq_{a,n}\right)
\right\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}. \tag{7}
\]

Let \(P_z^R\) be the weighted covariate projector, put \(\Pi=P_{J_y}P_z^R\), and define the fiberwise operator \((\mathcal G_nh)(y,z)=\{\mathcal G_{n,z}h(\cdot,z)\}(y)\). The weighted complement estimates proved in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), applied to \(u_n\), give
\[
\|(I-P_{J_y})u_n\|_2\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
\|(I-P_z^R)P_{J_y}u_n\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{8}
\]
If \(\mu_a=E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,1,n})\), define
\[
s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}(w)
=(2a-1)2\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\{\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(w)-\mu_a\}.
\]
The two Gram conjugations in \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\) give the exact coordinate identity
\[
\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (5)--(9), the triangle inequality, and the fixed density bounds imply
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim
\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{10}
\]
The localization bound (4) in \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) implies
\[
\sup_z\int_{\mathcal Z_d}|\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)|\,dR_n(v)\lesssim1,
\]
so the weighted covariate projector is uniformly bounded on \(L^\infty\); the analogous outcome-projector bound is (5) of that lemma. Hence the H\"older-radius assumption gives \(\|\Pi\Delta_n\|_\infty\lesssim1\). The one-dimensional Green representation following (4) maps a source with bounded \(L^1(dy)\) norm to a uniformly bounded solution, and applying the two stable projectors once more yields
\[
\|\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n\|_\infty\lesssim1.
\]
By (9), \(\sup_{a,n}\|s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_\infty<\infty\). This verifies the bounded-summand premise used below rather than adding it as an assumption.

For fold \(e\), put
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\qquad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}. \tag{11}
\]
The covariance bounds in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) justify the inverse. By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), the two \(Z_{e,n}\) are independent, centered, and have identity covariance. Define
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,
\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}Z_{e,n}. \tag{12}
\]
An average of independent centered score errors having \(L^2\)-norm \(r\), after multiplication by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm at most a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt n\,r\). Hence (10) and the theorem's score-approximation premise give
\[
\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_2=o(S). \tag{13}
\]

Let \((\nu_k,v_k)\) be the eigenpairs of \(K\) and write \(Z_{e,k}=v_k^{\top}Z_{e,n}\). The definition of \(\eta_{k,n}\) and diagonalization give
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2. \tag{14}
\]
Because \(n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})=V\), (13), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2=o(S)\) imply
\[
8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2). \tag{15}
\]

Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\). The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) hold: \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\); the score summands in (12) are centered and uniformly bounded as shown after (10); and (14)--(15) give \(EG_n^2=O(S^2)\). That lemma represents \(G_n+Q_n\) as a martingale-difference sum and proves both conditional Lindeberg and convergence of its predictable quadratic variation in \(L^2/S^2\). Canonical-kernel orthogonality, the variance identity in that lemma, and (15) give
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2). \tag{16}
\]

We derive the resulting normal approximation directly. For fixed \(t\), condition successively on the ordered observations and use
\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}.
\]
Conditional Lindeberg makes the sum of Taylor remainders tend to zero, and predictable-variance convergence replaces the sum of conditional second moments by (16). Iteration gives
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\!\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\longrightarrow0. \tag{17}
\]

Now define the Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), one-dimensional Gaussian integration gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right]. \tag{18}
\]
By (1) and (15),
\[
\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\to0,
\quad
\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\frac{nV+o(S^2)}{S^2},
\quad
\sum_{e,k}c_{e,k}^2=\frac{A^2}{2S^2}. \tag{19}
\]
Taylor expansion in (18) is uniform because the first quantity in (19) tends to zero. Each aggregate remainder is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\max_k|\nu_k|/S\) times a bounded sum in (19). Therefore
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}(A^2+nV)+o(1). \tag{20}
\]

Set \(\sigma_n^2=(A^2+nV)/S^2\). Since \(A,\sqrt{nV}\ge0\),
\[
\frac12\le\sigma_n^2\le1. \tag{21}
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(\sigma_n^2\) converges. Along that further subsequence, (17), (20), and the characteristic-function continuity argument show that both \((G_n+Q_n)/S\) and \(\mathcal Q_n^G/S\) converge to the same centered normal distribution. Their second moments are uniformly bounded by (16) and (19), hence their laws are tight. The subsequence criterion for bounded-Lipschitz distance, followed by (13), proves both
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\left[\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal L\left[\frac{\mathcal Q_n^G}{S_n}\right]\right)\to0
\]
and
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\left[\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal N(0,\sigma_n^2)\right)\to0, \tag{22}
\]
because, by definition,
\[
n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{23}
\]

At conditional equality, \(\Delta_n=0\). Hence \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\), all \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\) by the exact first-variation identity. Taking \(S=A\) in (18)--(22) gives
\[
\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}
=\frac{n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The decisive ratio is (1), so no fixed or subsequential non-Gaussian chaos can survive when the covariate resolution grows.

Finally consider the separately stated fixed unequal-law regime. The exact scores in (5) are centered and uniformly bounded because both supports and the squared-distance cost are bounded and the conditional densities are bounded away from zero. Applying the same conditional characteristic-function calculation to their independent triangular sum gives
\[
\frac{\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}}{\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The contraction lemma and Chebyshev's inequality give \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=O_P(A/n)\). Under \(\sqrt{nV}/A\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{A/n}{\sqrt{V/n}}=\frac{A}{\sqrt{nV}}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=o_P\{\sqrt{V/n}\}\). Slutsky's identity applied to (23) proves
\[
\frac{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n}{\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
which completes the proof. Every statistic in this argument uses the population score, Hessian, covariance, spectrum, and shifts; no observable calibration claim is made.
