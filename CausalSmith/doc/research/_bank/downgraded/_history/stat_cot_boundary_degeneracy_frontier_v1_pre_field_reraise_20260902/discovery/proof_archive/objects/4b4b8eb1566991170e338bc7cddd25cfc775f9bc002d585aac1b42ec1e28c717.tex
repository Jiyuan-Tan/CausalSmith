By \(\text{def:pilot-tuning-and-studentizer}\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad
e=\frac{1}{2+s_y^{-1}+d s_z^{-1}}.
\]
First suppose the displayed uniform absorption holds.  Taking \(\rho=0\) gives
\[
n^{-3e}(\log n)^{3e}=O(n^{-b}).
\]
Because \(e>0\), equality of the polynomial exponents would leave a divergent logarithmic factor.  Hence necessarily
\[
3e>b. \tag{14}
\]
Direct cross-multiplication through positive denominators shows that (14) is equivalent to
\[
s_z>d/4,
\qquad
s_y>\frac{4s_z}{4s_z-d}. \tag{15}
\]
In particular \(b>1/2\).  The radius \(\rho_n=n^{1/2-b}\) therefore belongs to \([0,1]\) for all large \(n\), and at this radius
\(\rho_n n^{-1/2}=n^{-b}\).  Applying uniform absorption to the second pilot term at \(\rho_n\) gives
\[
n^{-2e-\kappa(b-1/2)}(\log n)^{2e}=O(n^{-b}).
\]
Again the logarithm rules out equality, so
\[
2e+\kappa(b-1/2)>b. \tag{16}
\]

Conversely, assume (14) and (16).  The first term is then \(o(n^{-b})\).  Since \(0<\kappa<1\), differentiation of
\[
f_n(\rho)=\frac{\rho^\kappa}{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-b}}
\]
shows that its positive unconstrained maximizer is
\[
\rho_n^*=\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}n^{1/2-b}.
\]
Condition (14) implies \(b>1/2\), so this maximizer lies in \([0,1]\) for all large \(n\).  Consequently
\[
\sup_{0\le\rho\le1}f_n(\rho)
\lesssim n^{b-\kappa(b-1/2)}.
\]
Multiplication by \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2=n^{-2e}(\log n)^{2e}\) and (16) makes the resulting bound \(o(1)\).  Thus (14) and (16) are jointly sufficient as well as necessary.

It remains to solve these inequalities.  With all denominators positive, direct simplification gives
\[
2e+\kappa(b-1/2)-b
=\frac{s_z P(s_y)}
{(d+4s_z)(d s_y+2s_ys_z+s_z)(d s_y+2s_ys_z+3s_z)}, \tag{17}
\]
where
\[
P(x)=(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)x^2-s_z(11d+4s_z)x-12s_z^2.
\]
If \(P(x)>0\) for some \(x>0\), its quadratic coefficient must be positive, because its linear and constant coefficients are negative.  Hence \(s_z>3d/4\).  Under that condition \(P\) has exactly one positive root, and the quadratic formula, with
\[
(11d+4s_z)^2+48(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)
=(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d),
\]
gives precisely
\[
x_{\mathrm{crit}}=
\frac{s_z\!\left[11d+4s_z+\sqrt{(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d)}\right]}
{2(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)}.
\]
Therefore (16) is equivalent to \(s_z>3d/4\) and \(s_y>x_{\mathrm{crit}}\).  Moreover,
\[
P\!\left(\frac{4s_z}{4s_z-d}\right)
=-\frac{16s_z^2(d+2s_z)(d+4s_z)}{(4s_z-d)^2}<0.
\]
Thus \(P(s_y)>0\) implies the second inequality in (15), and hence also (14).  This proves the asserted exact equivalence.  The conclusion about matching now follows directly by substituting the absorption bound into \(\text{thm:conditional-minimax-frontier}\); the two different elbows are the roots \(s_z=d/4\) of the intrinsic transition and \(s_z=3d/4\) of the leading coefficient in \(P\).

We finally verify that no new law assumption is needed for the supplemental direct estimator when \(s_z\ge1\).  Compact support follows from \(\mathcal Y=[0,1]\) and \(\mathcal Z_d=[0,1]^d\).  For \(h(y_0,y_1)=(y_1-y_0)^2\),
\[
|h(y_0,y_1)-h(y_0',y_1')|
\le2\{|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|\},
\]
so the cost is Lipschitz.  Anisotropic H\"older smoothness with \(s_z\ge1\) gives, uniformly in the arm,
\[
\int_0^1|q_{a,n}(y,z)-q_{a,n}(y,z')|dy\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.
\]
On the unit outcome interval, couple the common density mass identically and couple the two residual masses arbitrarily.  The residual mass is the total-variation distance and every transport distance is at most one, so \(W_1\) is at most one half of the last \(L^1\) difference.  Hence
\[
W_1\{Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z')\}
\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2. \tag{18}
\]
The source's compact-support, Lipschitz-cost, and Wasserstein--Lipschitz-kernel premises are therefore law properties of the declared class, while independent i.i.d. arm sampling is \(\text{ass:two-arm-sampling}\).  Under the shared-marginal assumption, the population direct-COT value for this squared cost is
\[
\int_{\mathcal Z_d}W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}dR_n(z)=\theta_n
\]
by the declared identifying functional, so the source's statistical target is exactly the causal endpoint used here.

For completeness, the coupling-gap premise is also derived rather than assumed.  On a block of size \(N\), let \(h_N=N^{-1/(d+2)}\), let \(\varphi_N\) be the cell-center projection, and let \(R_n^{\varphi}=\varphi_{N\#}R_n\).  There are at most \(M_N\le C h_N^{-d}\) cells.  If \(\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}\) is the projected empirical covariate law in arm \(a\), then coupling each point to its center and then coupling measures supported on the cell centers gives
\[
W_1(\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi},R_n)
\le \sqrt d\,h_N+\sqrt d\,\|\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}-R_n^{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Writing \(p_j\) for the \(R_n\)-mass of cell \(j\), the binomial variance formula, Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\sum_jp_j=1\) imply
\[
E\|\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}-R_n^{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{M_N}\sqrt{\frac{p_j(1-p_j)}N}
\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{M_N}{N}}
\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}. \tag{19}
\]
Because both arms have the same \(R_n\), the triangle inequality and (19) show that an optimal coupling \(\widehat\pi_N\) of their projected empirical covariate laws satisfies
\[
E\!\left[\int\|z_0-z_1\|_2d\widehat\pi_N(z_0,z_1)\right]
\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}. \tag{20}
\]
Thus all hypotheses of \(\text{lem:lin-gao-blanchet-glynn-direct-cot-rate}\) hold uniformly, and each block estimator obeys
\[
E|\widehat\theta_{N}^{\mathrm{dir}}-\theta_n|
\le\delta_N:=C N^{-1/(d+2)}\log N. \tag{21}
\]
Both the block estimator and \(\theta_n\) lie in \([0,1]\).  By Markov's inequality, a block is bad with probability at most \(1/8\) when bad means an error exceeding \(8\delta_N\).  The blocks are independent by \(\text{ass:two-arm-sampling}\).  If their median is bad, at least \(K_n/2\) blocks are bad; a union bound over subsets of blocks gives
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n|>8\delta_{N_n}\}
\le2^{K_n}(1/8)^{K_n/2}=2^{-K_n/2}. \tag{22}
\]
Choose the fixed constant in the odd integer \(K_n\asymp\log n\) large enough that the last probability is \(O(\delta_{N_n}^2)\).  Boundedness and (22) yield
\[
E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2
\le64\delta_{N_n}^2+2^{-K_n/2}
\lesssim n^{-2/(d+2)}(\log n)^{2+2/(d+2)}.
\]
Taking square roots proves the direct envelope.  Since the class parameters are known in the minimax experiment, selecting the zero estimator, the anisotropic estimator, or this median estimator according to the smallest of their deterministic uniform guarantees proves the final minimum upper bound.
