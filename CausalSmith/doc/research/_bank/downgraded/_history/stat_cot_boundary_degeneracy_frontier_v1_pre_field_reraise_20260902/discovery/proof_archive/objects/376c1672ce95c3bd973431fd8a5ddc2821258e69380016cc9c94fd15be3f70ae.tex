By the pilot-tuning definition,
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad
e=\frac{1}{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.
\]
Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\).  Suppose first that uniform absorption of the corrected remainder holds.  Taking \(\rho=0\) and using the divergent logarithmic factor forces
\[
(2+a_y)e>b.                                                               \tag{1}
\]
If \(b\le1/2\), taking \(\rho=1\) in the second term would require \(2e>b\), whereas, with \(x=s_y\) and \(t=d/s_z\),
\[
b-2e=\frac{2(tx+2)}{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)}>0.
\]
Thus absorption requires \(b>1/2\).  At \(\rho_n=n^{1/2-b}\), the two denominator terms agree, so the local term requires
\[
2e+a_y\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                                    \tag{2}
\]
If \(s_y=x\le1\), then \(a_y=x\) and direct common-denominator simplification gives
\[
b-2e-x\kappa(b-1/2)
=\frac{t^2x^3+2t^2x^2-2tx^3+5tx^2+10tx-8x^3-4x^2+8x+12}
{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)(tx+2x+3)}>0.                                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the coefficient of \(t\) is \(x(-2x^2+5x+10)>0\), the coefficient of \(t^2\) is positive, and \(-8x^3-4x^2+8x+12\ge8\) for \(0<x\le1\).  Hence (2) is impossible unless \(s_y>1\).  In the remaining case \(a_y=1\), and (1)--(2) reduce to
\[
3e>b,
\qquad
2e+\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                                       \tag{4}
\]

Conversely, assume (4).  The first pilot term is \(o(n^{-b})\).  Since \(0<\kappa<1\), differentiation shows that
\[
f_n(\rho)=\frac{\rho^\kappa}{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-b}}
\]
has unconstrained maximizer \(\rho_n^*=\kappa(1-\kappa)^{-1}n^{1/2-b}\), which belongs to \([0,1]\) for large \(n\).  Consequently
\[
\sup_{0\le\rho\le1}f_n(\rho)\lesssim n^{b-\kappa(b-1/2)}.
\]
Multiplication by \(n^{-2e}(\log n)^{2e}\) and (2) makes this \(o(1)\).  Thus (4) is sufficient on \(s_y>1\); together with (1)--(3), this characterizes absorption.

Direct simplification gives
\[
2e+\kappa(b-1/2)-b
=\frac{s_zP(s_y)}
{(d+4s_z)(ds_y+2s_ys_z+s_z)(ds_y+2s_ys_z+3s_z)},                         \tag{5}
\]
where
\[
P(x)=(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)x^2-s_z(11d+4s_z)x-12s_z^2.
\]
For \(P(x)>0\) at a positive \(x\), its leading coefficient must be positive, hence \(s_z>3d/4\).  In that case the unique positive root, using
\[
(11d+4s_z)^2+48(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)=(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d),
\]
is exactly
\[
x_{\mathrm{crit}}=
\frac{s_z\{11d+4s_z+\sqrt{(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d)}\}}
{2(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
P(1)=-3d^2-13ds_z-8s_z^2<0,
\]
so the positive root is strictly larger than one and therefore lies in the branch \(a_y=1\).  Also,
\[
P\!\left(\frac{4s_z}{4s_z-d}\right)
=-\frac{16s_z^2(d+2s_z)(d+4s_z)}{(4s_z-d)^2}<0,
\]
so \(P(s_y)>0\) also implies (1).  This proves the exact equivalence and, by the corrected minimax theorem, the matching conclusion.

For the supplemental estimator assume \(s_z\ge1\).  Compact support is declared.  The squared cost obeys
\[
|(y_1-y_0)^2-(y_1'-y_0')^2|
\le2(|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|).
\]
The anisotropic H\"older condition gives
\[
\int_0^1|q_{a,n}(y,z)-q_{a,n}(y,z')|\,dy\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.
\]
Coupling common mass identically and residual mass arbitrarily on \([0,1]\) shows
\[
W_1\{Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z')\}
\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.                                                        \tag{6}
\]
Thus the compactness, Lipschitz-cost, and Wasserstein--Lipschitz-kernel premises of the established direct-COT lemma hold, and independent sampling is \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\).

It remains to derive its coupling-gap premise.  On a block of size \(N\), put \(h_N=N^{-1/(d+2)}\) and project each covariate to a cell center.  There are \(M_N\lesssim h_N^{-d}\) cells.  If \(p_j\) is the common marginal mass of cell \(j\), binomial variance and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
E\|\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}-R_n^{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{M_N}\sqrt{\frac{p_j(1-p_j)}N}
\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{M_N}{N}}\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}.                    \tag{7}
\]
Moving points to centers costs at most \(\sqrt d\,h_N\).  The shared marginal and triangle inequality therefore imply that an optimal coupling \(\widehat\pi_N\) of the two projected empirical marginals satisfies
\[
E\!\left[\int\|z_0-z_1\|_2\,d\widehat\pi_N(z_0,z_1)\right]
\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}.                                                     \tag{8}
\]
The established direct-COT lemma now gives, for a block estimator bounded in \([0,1]\),
\[
E|\widehat\theta_N^{\mathrm{dir}}-\theta_n|
\le\delta_N:=CN^{-1/(d+2)}\log N.                                       \tag{9}
\]

Take an odd \(K_n\asymp\log n\) and independent blocks of size \(N_n=\lfloor n/K_n\rfloor\).  Markov's inequality makes a block bad with probability at most \(1/8\) when its error exceeds \(8\delta_{N_n}\).  If the median is bad, at least \(K_n/2\) blocks are bad, so independence and a union bound give
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n|>8\delta_{N_n}\}
\le2^{K_n}(1/8)^{K_n/2}=2^{-K_n/2}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Choose the constant in \(K_n\) so that the last probability is \(O(\delta_{N_n}^2)\).  Boundedness and (10) then yield
\[
\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.
\]
Selecting among this estimator, the corrected anisotropic estimator, and the zero estimator by their deterministic class-wide guarantees proves the final minimum upper envelope without changing the converse.
