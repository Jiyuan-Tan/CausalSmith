Write \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and put \(H_z(y)=F_{1,z}(y)-F_{0,z}(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv\).  If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the defining Neumann equation gives
\[
 -\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_z(y)=H_z(y).
\]
An integration by parts, whose boundary term vanishes because \(H_z(0)=H_z(1)=0\), yields
\[
 \int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)dy
 =\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)\{\partial_yu_z(y)\}^2dy
 =\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy. \tag{1}
\]
For each \(u\in(0,1)\), the mean-value theorem and \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\) give
\[
 H_z\{Q_{0,z}(u)\}
 =F_{1,z}\{Q_{0,z}(u)\}-F_{1,z}\{Q_{1,z}(u)\}
 =q_{1,n}(\zeta_{u,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(u)-Q_{1,z}(u)\}
\]
for a point \(\zeta_{u,z}\) between the two quantiles.  Changing variables \(u=F_{0,z}(y)\) and using the density bounds again shows
\[
 c_3\int_0^1H_z(y)^2dy
 \le W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
 \le C_3\int_0^1H_z(y)^2dy. \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1)--(2), integrating in \(R_n\), and invoking \(\rho_n\le\rho\) gives
\[
 c_4\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_4\rho_n^2\le C_4\rho^2. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has root mean-square risk at most \(C_4\rho^2\).

For each pair \((P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\) in the model class, let \(P\) denote the induced product sampling law from \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\).  We next bound the observable estimator.  Set
\[
 p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3+
 \rho^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The Hoeffding decomposition in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), before discarding the pilot remainder, and the interpolation estimate in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) give
\[
 \widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
 =\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
   +B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{4}
\]
We record why (4) gives an \(L^2\), rather than merely an in-probability, bound.  At the declared pilot resolutions the number of retained pilot coefficients is
\[
 \overline D_n\asymp2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}
 \asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
Each coefficient is an average of bounded, compactly supported wavelets.  Applying the exponential Markov bound to the centered bounded summands and then summing over the \(O(\overline D_n)\) coefficients gives, for fixed \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
 P\!\left(\max_e\left[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty+
 \max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty\right]
 >C\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+x\}\right)
 \le C\exp\{-c n x^2/\overline D_n\}. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the deterministic projection bias is \(O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})\) by the declared anisotropic H\"older radii; on the positivity event, ratios and conditional normalization are Lipschitz because the denominator is at least \(c\), and off that event the fixed clipping map keeps the same objects bounded.  Integrating (5) shows that every fixed moment of the pilot sup-norm error is bounded by the corresponding power of \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\).  The deterministic third-order Taylor and anisotropic interpolation inequalities used in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}\|R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P)}
 \le C p_n+o\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
       +2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right). \tag{6}
\]
No covariance estimator is used in this step.

The two Hoeffding projections are orthogonal.  Their variance identities in the expansion theorem and \(\rho_n\le\rho\) give
\[
 \|\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C\rho n^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \|\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2=A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n. \tag{7}
\]
The spectral and bias bounds in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), evaluated at \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), give
\[
 A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z),\qquad
 |B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|\le C r_{n,d}(s_z). \tag{8}
\]
Minkowski's inequality applied to (4), followed by (6)--(8), proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
 \{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
 \le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}. \tag{9}
\]
Taking, for the known model radius \(\rho\), whichever of this explicit sample map and the zero map has the smaller displayed guarantee, (3) and (9) establish the asserted upper bound.  In particular the zero map is selected throughout the zero zone \(\rho\lesssim\rho_{Q,n}\).

For the converse, first use \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\).  Choose its fixed \(c_0\) still smaller so that
\[
 \chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1.
\]
Then total variation is at most \(\sqrt\eta/2<1\).  The null endpoint is zero, while every mixture component has endpoint at least
\[
 \delta_n=c_5\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.
\]
For any estimator, thresholding it at \(\delta_n/2\) turns it into a test.  On either testing error its squared estimation error is at least \(\delta_n^2/4\).  The testing identity \(\inf_\phi\{\mathbb P_n^0\phi+\overline{\mathbb P}_n(1-\phi)\}=1-\|\overline{\mathbb P}_n-\mathbb P_n^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) therefore yields
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
 \{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
 \ge c_6\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{10}
\]

For the regular direction, extend \(q_{a,t}^0\) constantly in \(z\).  If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), compare \(t=c_7\rho\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\), with fixed constants small enough that both inverse-elliptic radii are at most \(\rho\).  The fixed-frequency calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) gives product squared Hellinger distance at most \(C c_8^2\); choose \(c_8\) so this is smaller than a fixed \(\eta<1\), leaving testing affinity bounded away from zero.  Let \(\Theta(t)\) denote the endpoint on this symmetric cosine path.  Here
\[
 F_{a,t}(y)=y+(2a-1)t\sin(2\pi y)/(4\pi),
 \qquad \partial_yF_{a,t}=q_{a,t}^0\ge c.
\]
Differentiating \(F_{a,t}\{Q_{a,t}(u)\}=u\) gives
\[
 \partial_tQ_{a,t}(u)=
 -\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(u)\}}{4\pi q_{a,t}^0\{Q_{a,t}(u)\}},
\]
which is continuous and uniformly bounded; differentiating this displayed quotient once more shows directly that \(\partial_t^2Q_{a,t}\), and hence \(\Theta''(t)\), is continuous near zero.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\).  Thus, after choosing \(c_7,c_8\) so that \(0\le t<t'\) remain in a fixed sufficiently small neighborhood, \(\Theta''(v)\ge1/(8\pi^2)\) throughout that neighborhood.  Integrating this inequality first over \([0,v]\) and then over \([t,t']\) gives
\[
 |\theta(t')-\theta(t)|
 \ge c_9 t|t'-t|
 \ge c_{10}\rho n^{-1/2}. \tag{11}
\]
The two-point testing argument used for (10) therefore supplies a lower bound \(c_{11}\rho n^{-1/2}\).  When \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), one has \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), because \(4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), so (10) already gives \(c\rho^2\).  The elementary equivalence
\[
 \max\!\left[\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\},
                   \rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right]
 \asymp
 \min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\} \tag{12}
\]
now proves the lower frontier.

If \(p_n=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\), (9)--(12) match, and no such matching is asserted otherwise.  Finally,
\[
 \rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\]
and
\[
 \rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.
\]
Thus \(\rho_{L,n}\to0\) exactly when \(s_z>d/4\), yielding the stated zero, quadratic, mixed-boundary, and regular zones.  If \(s_z\le d/4\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\gtrsim n^{-1/2}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}\not\to0\).  At equality the linear projection is identically zero by \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), while the lower bound (10) keeps the quadratic difficulty at least of root-\(n\) order when \(s_z=d/4\), and larger when \(s_z<d/4\).  Hence a positive-variance regular root-\(n\) law cannot hold uniformly over the class.  This completes both sides of the honest bracket and the claimed phase calculation.
