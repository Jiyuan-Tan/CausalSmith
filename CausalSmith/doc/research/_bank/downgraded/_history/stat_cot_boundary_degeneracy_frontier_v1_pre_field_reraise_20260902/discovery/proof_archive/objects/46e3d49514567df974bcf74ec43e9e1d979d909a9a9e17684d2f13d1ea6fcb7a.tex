Condition on both pilot folds.  The sampling assumption and deterministic split make every evaluation observation independent of its opposite-fold pilot.  Apply the algebraic Hoeffding decomposition to each diagonal-free quadratic correction.  After averaging the two folds, its first projection combines with the one-step score to give \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\), its canonical second projection is \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and projection outside the retained tensor space gives \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\).  Thus
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n},                                  \tag{1}
\]
where \(R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) consists exactly of the pilot, ridge, and operator replacements.

The first-variation formula in \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), polarized about the actual discrepancy, and uniform ellipticity identify the squared \(L^2(P_{0,n})\oplus L^2(P_{1,n})\) norm of the centered first-order score with the inverse-elliptic energy up to fixed constants.  The independence and equal fold sizes therefore give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\frac{\rho_n^2}{n},         \tag{2}
\]
and the score, hence \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\), is identically zero when \(\Delta_n=0\).

For one evaluation fold, the projected two-sample feature contrast has covariance \(\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}/m\).  Whiten it and diagonalize \(K=\Gamma^{1/2}H\Gamma^{1/2}\).  Pairing the two occurrences of each observation in the diagonal-free order-two statistic gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
=\frac{4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+O(m^{-1})\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.                                 \tag{3}
\]
The only finite-sample correction in the first equality is \(m/(m-1)\) on the two within-arm terms.  Uniformity of the \(o(1)\) follows from \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), the fixed density bounds, and compact wavelet support, which bound the fourth-moment contraction by \(CD_{J_y,J_z}/n^2=o(1)\).  Thus the asymptotic standard deviation is \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\), as asserted in this asymptotic expansion.  The deterministic bias estimates are those of \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\).

The pilot-control lemma gives
\[
R_{J_y,J_z,n}
=O_P\!\left\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\right\}
+o_P(1)\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.      \tag{4}
\]
Under the theorem's displayed hypothesis, (4) is little-oh of the sum of the four expansion scales.  Substituting (2)--(4) into the identity (1) proves the uniform expansion and its variance assertions.

At the rate-balancing resolutions, the tuning identities and the spectrum proposition give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp2^{-2s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z).                                                        \tag{5}
\]
Consequently, if the last two pilot terms are negligible, the root-mean-square scale is \(\rho_n/\sqrt n+r_{n,d}(s_z)\).  Without that extra comparison, Minkowski's inequality applied to (1)--(4) gives the honest scale
\[
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+r_{n,d}(s_z)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
No covariance estimate occurs in (1)--(5), so the point-estimator conclusion does not require the separate covariance-estimation condition.
