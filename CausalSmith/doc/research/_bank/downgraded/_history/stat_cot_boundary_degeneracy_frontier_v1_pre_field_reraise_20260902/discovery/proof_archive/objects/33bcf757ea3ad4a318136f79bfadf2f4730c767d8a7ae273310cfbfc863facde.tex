Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), abbreviate \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV}\). By \(d\ge1\), \(J_z\to\infty\), and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\),
\[
A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{A}
\lesssim M_n^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0.                            \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(S>0\) for all large \(n\), so every displayed normalization is well defined.

We first compare the exact oracle first projection with the linear part of the noncentral quadratic proxy. Define
\[
F_{a,n}(y\mid z)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(u,z)du,
\quad T_{n,z}=F_{1,n}^{-1}(\cdot\mid z)\circ F_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),
\quad H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(u,z)du.
\]
Zero conditional mass gives \(H_n(0,z)=H_n(1,z)=0\). If \(g=T_{n,z}(y)-y\), the transport identity gives
\[
\int_y^{y+g}q_{1,n}(u,z)du=-H_n(y,z).                            \tag{2}
\]
The density lower bound first implies \(|g|\le c^{-1}|H_n(y,z)|\). The outcome H\"older condition then gives
\[
\left|\int_y^{y+g}\{q_{1,n}(u,z)-q_{1,n}(y,z)\}du\right|
\le C_0|g|^{1+a_y}.
\]
Solving (2) for \(g\), using \(|q_{1,n}-\bar q_n|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty/2\), yields uniformly
\[
T_{n,z}(y)-y
=-\frac{H_n(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}
+O\!\left(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}|H_n(y,z)|\right).          \tag{3}
\]

Let \(u_n(\cdot,z)=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\). Integrating its Neumann equation and then integrating by parts gives
\[
\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n,
\qquad
\rho_n^2=\int\Delta_nu_n\,dy\,dR_n
=\int\frac{H_n^2}{\bar q_n}\,dy\,dR_n.                         \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\|H_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}\asymp\rho_n\).

Let \(f_n(z)=W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))\), and let \(\ell_{a,n}\) be the exact conditional Kantorovich derivative. Under the shared-marginal tangent restriction, the exact centered arm score is
\[
s_{a,n}(y,z)
=\frac12\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}
+\ell_{a,n}(y,z)-\int_0^1\ell_{a,n}(u,z)q_{a,n}(u,z)du.          \tag{5}
\]
The first term is the common-marginal component. Equations (2), (4), and \(\sup|H_n|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\) imply
\[
\|f_n-\theta_n\|_{L^2(R_n)}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
\le\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}                           \tag{6}
\]
for all large \(n\). For the conditional potentials,
\[
\partial_y\ell_{0,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}(y)\},
\qquad
\partial_y\ell_{1,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}^{-1}(y)\}.
\]
Apply (3) once as written and once with the arms interchanged. Since \(\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n\), subtracting the conditional mean fixes the irrelevant constants, and the Poincar\'e inequality on \([0,1]\) gives
\[
\max_a\left\|
\ell_{a,n}-\int\ell_{a,n}q_{a,n}
-(2a-1)2\left(u_n-int u_nq_{a,n}\right)
\right\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}.                      \tag{7}
\]

Let \(P_z^R\) be the weighted covariate projector and put \(\Pi=P_{J_y}P_z^R\). The weighted complement argument in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\), applied to \(u_n\), gives
\[
\|(I-P_{J_y})u_n\|_2\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
\|(I-P_z^R)P_{J_y}u_n\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}.                  \tag{8}
\]
If \(\mu_a=E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,1,n})\), define the projected quadratic score
\[
s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}(w)
=(2a-1)2\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\{\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(w)-\mu_a\}.
\]
The Gram conjugation in \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\) gives the exact coordinate identity
\[
\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}.          \tag{9}
\]
Equations (5)--(9) imply
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim
\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}.                                   \tag{10}
\]

For fold \(e\), put
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\quad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}.         \tag{11}
\]
The covariance bounds in the spectrum proposition justify the inverse. The two \(Z_{e,n}\) are independent, centered, and have identity covariance. Define
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,
\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.                                \tag{12}
\]
An empirical average of a centered score error with \(L^2\)-norm \(r\), after multiplication by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm at most a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt n r\). Hence (10) and the theorem's score-approximation premise give
\[
\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_2=o(S).                         \tag{13}
\]

Let \((\nu_k,v_k)\) be the eigenpairs of \(K\) and \(Z_{e,k}=v_k^\top Z_{e,n}\). The definition of \(\eta_{k,n}\) and diagonalization give
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2.           \tag{14}
\]
Because \(n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L)=V\), (13)--(14) yield
\[
8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2).                         \tag{15}
\]

Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\). The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) hold: \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), the score summands in (12) are centered and uniformly bounded by the one-dimensional Green bound and the fixed density bounds, and (14)--(15) give \(EG_n^2=O(S^2)\). That lemma represents \(G_n+Q_n\) as a martingale-difference sum, proves conditional Lindeberg, and proves convergence of the predictable quadratic variation in \(L^2/S^2\). Canonical-kernel orthogonality, its variance identity, and (15) give
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2).                                    \tag{16}
\]

For completeness, the limiting calculation is direct. For fixed \(t\), condition successively on the ordered observations and use
\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}.
\]
Conditional Lindeberg makes the sum of Taylor remainders tend to zero, and predictable-variance convergence replaces the conditional second moments by (16). Iteration gives
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\!\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\to0. \tag{17}
\]

Define the Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), one-dimensional Gaussian integration gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].                  \tag{18}
\]
By (1) and (15),
\[
\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\to0,
\quad
\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\frac{nV+o(S^2)}{S^2},
\quad
\sum_{e,k}c_{e,k}^2=\frac{A^2}{2S^2}.                           \tag{19}
\]
Expanding (18), both total remainders are bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\max_k|\nu_k|/S\) times a bounded sum in (19), and hence
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}(A^2+nV)+o(1).                                \tag{20}
\]

Set \(\sigma_n^2=(A^2+nV)/S^2\). Since \(A,\sqrt{nV}\ge0\), \(1/2\le\sigma_n^2\le1\). Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(\sigma_n^2\) converges. Along it, (17) and (20) give the same centered normal limit for \((G_n+Q_n)/S\) and \(\mathcal Q_n^G/S\). Their second moments are uniformly bounded by (16) and (19), so the subsequence criterion for \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\) proves both displayed bounded-Lipschitz conclusions. Equation (13) replaces \(G_n+Q_n\) by
\[
n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}.                \tag{21}
\]

At conditional equality, \(\Delta_n=0\), so \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\), every \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\). Taking \(S=A\) in (18)--(20) proves the asserted standard-normal limit. The decisive ratio is (1), so no non-Gaussian chaos survives.

At a fixed unequal law, the exact scores (5) are bounded because the support and cost are bounded and the densities are bounded away from zero. The same conditional characteristic-function expansion applied to their independent sums gives \(\mathbb L/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\). The condition \(\sqrt{nV}/A\to\infty\), together with (6) in the expansion proof, makes \(\mathbb U=o_P(\sqrt{V/n})\). Slutsky's identity applied to (21) proves the last limit.
