Put \(p_{a,n}=q_{a,n}r_n\), \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\), and \(\bar D_n=2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}\). Products preserve the declared anisotropic H\"older moduli, so the boundary-wavelet approximation inequalities at the pilot resolutions are
\[
\max_a\|p_{a,n}-p_{a,n}^{\mathrm{proj}}\|_\infty
+\|r_n-r_n^{\mathrm{proj}}\|_\infty
\lesssim2^{-s_y\bar J_y}+2^{-s_z\bar J_z}\lesssim\varepsilon.       \tag{1}
\]
For a retained wavelet coefficient, expand the exponential moment of each centered bounded summand, use \(e^v-1-v\le v^2e^{|v|}/2\), multiply the independent exponential moments, and optimize the exponential parameter. A union bound over \(O(\bar D_n)\) coefficients and finite overlap in the wavelet reconstruction then give, uniformly over the model class and for \(0\le x\le1\),
\[
P\!\left(E_n>C(\varepsilon+x)\right)
\le C\exp\{-cnx^2/\bar D_n\},                                      \tag{2}
\]
where
\[
E_n=\max_e\left\{\|\widetilde r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty
+\max_a\|\widetilde p_{a,n}^{(-e)}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty\right\}.
\]
The identities defining the resolutions give \(\bar D_n\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), so (2) implies \(E_n=O_P(\varepsilon)\) and, by tail integration, \(\|E_n\|_{L^k}\le C_k\varepsilon\) for every fixed \(k\).

On \(\widetilde r\ge c/2\),
\[
\frac{\widetilde p}{\widetilde r}-\frac pr
=\frac{\widetilde p-p}{\widetilde r}+p\frac{r-\widetilde r}{r\widetilde r}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2), (3), and the density lower bounds prove the displayed conditional-pilot rate and \(P(\cap_e\mathcal E_n^{(-e)})\to1\). Normalization enforces exact mass everywhere. Since \(T(\widetilde r)\in[c/2,2C]\),
\[
\frac c{4C}\le\widehat r_n^{(-e)}\le\frac{4C}{c}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Lebesgue orthonormality therefore gives the claimed deterministic Gram bounds. Moreover,
\[
\|\widehat G^{(-e)}-G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty,
\qquad
(\widehat G^{-1}-G^{-1})=\widehat G^{-1}(G-\widehat G)G^{-1},       \tag{5}
\]
which proves both Gram perturbation assertions.

Let \(A_z=-\partial_y(\bar q_n\partial_y)\), and let \(A_z^{\mathrm{op}}=P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}A_zP_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\); put hats on these objects for the pilot coefficient. Normalized clipping gives \(c/(4C)\le\widehat{\bar q}_n\le4C/c\). This bound, \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\), and the zero-mean inequality \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\) proved in the spectrum proof give uniform coercivity. The weak forms and (2) imply
\[
\|\widehat A_z^{\mathrm{op}}-A_z^{\mathrm{op}}\|_{H^1/\mathbb R\to H^{-1}}
\lesssim E_n.
\]
The resolvent identity
\[
(\widehat A_z^{\mathrm{op}}+\lambda_nI)^{-1}-(A_z^{\mathrm{op}})^{-1}
=(\widehat A_z^{\mathrm{op}}+\lambda_nI)^{-1}
\{A_z^{\mathrm{op}}-\widehat A_z^{\mathrm{op}}-\lambda_nI\}(A_z^{\mathrm{op}})^{-1} \tag{6}
\]
therefore costs \(O_P(E_n+\lambda_n)\) in the energy norm. If \(u=A_z^{-1}f\), direct integration of the Neumann equation gives \(u'=-F/\bar q_n\), where \(F(y)=\int_0^yf(v)dv\). Since \(\bar q_n^{-1}\) is uniformly \(a_y\)-H\"older, boundary-wavelet approximation and Galerkin orthogonality yield, for retained \(f,g\),
\[
|\langle f,\{(A_z^{\mathrm{op}})^{-1}P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}-A_z^{-1}\}g\rangle|
\lesssim2^{-2a_yJ_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\|f\|_2\|g\|_2
\le\varepsilon^{a_y}\|f\|_2\|g\|_2.                              \tag{7}
\]
The last step is the declared oversampling rule. Integrating the form against \(\widehat r\), then using (2), proves
\[
\|\widehat H^{\mathrm{form},(-e)}-H^{\mathrm{form}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P(\varepsilon^{a_y}+\varepsilon+\lambda_n).                        \tag{8}
\]
Expanding the two Gram inverses in \(\widehat B=\widehat G^{-1}\widehat H^{\mathrm{form}}\widehat G^{-1}\) and applying (5), (8) proves the displayed \(\widehat B-B\) operator bound. The energy-norm part of (6) and the outcome-level preservation of the Gram matrices also give the sharper bilinear estimate
\[
|x^\top(\widehat B-B)y|
\le O_P(\varepsilon+\lambda_n)(x^\top Bx)^{1/2}(y^\top By)^{1/2}
+C\varepsilon^{a_y}\|x\|_2\|y\|_2.                                \tag{9}
\]

We now expand every stochastic term. Fix \(e\), condition on its opposite-fold sigma-field \(\mathscr F_{-e}\), and write \(\mu_a=E\Phi(W_{a,1,n})\), \(d_a=\mu_a-\widehat\mu_a\), \(d=d_1-d_0\), and \(Z_{a,i}=\Phi(W_{a,i,n})-\mu_a\). Then \(E(Z_{a,i}\mid\mathscr F_{-e})=0\), and direct expansion of the diagonal-free correction gives
\[
\widehat U_e=U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)
+2\bar Z_{1,e}^\top\widehat Bd-2\bar Z_{0,e}^\top\widehat Bd+d^\top\widehat Bd. \tag{10}
\]
In particular,
\[
E(\widehat U_e\mid\mathscr F_{-e})=d^\top\widehat Bd.                \tag{11}
\]
This is the required conditional mean; Gram conjugation makes \(d^\top Bd\) the projected inverse-elliptic quadratic Taylor term.

For the nonlinear terms, let \(F\) be a conditional distribution function and perturb it by a function of sup-norm at most \(E\). Positivity gives a quantile displacement \(|w|\le E/c\), while the H\"older condition gives
\[
F(x+w)-F(x)-q(x)w=\int_0^w\{q(x+t)-q(x)\}dt=O(|w|^{1+a_y}).          \tag{12}
\]
Applying (12) in both arms, multiplying out the squared quantile difference, and including the exactly linear common-marginal coordinate proves the deterministic conditional-mean bound
\[
|R_{0,e}|
\le C\{E_n^{2+a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}E_n^2\}.                \tag{13}
\]
The same calculation after one differentiation shows that the residual first-order coefficient \(g_{a,e}\), after combining the displayed score with the two linear terms in (10), satisfies
\[
\sum_{a=0}^1\|g_{a,e}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}^2
\le C\{E_n^{2+2a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{2a_y}E_n^2\}+o_P(\rho_n^2). \tag{14}
\]
The little-oh term in (14) is the energy-relative part of (9) applied to the true projected discrepancy. This establishes the cancellation by an equation rather than by centering alone.

More explicitly, let \(L_e^{\mathrm{or}}=m^{-1}\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}s_{a,n}(W_{a,i,n})\) be the oracle first projection and let \(B^y+B^z\) denote the deterministic projection complement. Combining the plug-in value, the two displayed score averages, all four linear terms from (10), and the conditional mean (11) gives the identity
\[
\widehat\theta_e-\theta_n-B^y-B^z-L_e^{\mathrm{or}}-U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(B)
=R_{0,e}
+\frac1m\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}g_{a,e}(W_{a,i,n})
+\{U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)-U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(B)\}.             \tag{14a}
\]
Conditional on \(\mathscr F_{-e}\), the second term in (14a) has mean zero, the last term is canonical and has mean zero, and their covariance is zero by Hoeffding orthogonality. Thus (13) is the full conditional mean and the next two displays are the two nonzero conditional variance bounds; no plug-in, score, Gram, Galerkin, ridge, or kernel term remains unclassified.

For completeness, the anisotropic H\"older modulus turns the local sup-norm into the declared energy radius. If \(A=\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\), the modulus forces amplitude of order \(A\) on an outcome interval of length \(A^{1/s_y}\) and a covariate rectangle of volume \(A^{d/s_z}\). Zero outcome mass forces the antiderivative to have order \(A^{1+1/s_y}\) on such an outcome interval. The energy identity \(\int\Delta\mathcal G\Delta=\int H^2/\bar q\) and the density bounds therefore give
\[
\rho_n^2\gtrsim A^{2+3/s_y+d/s_z},
\qquad \|\Delta_n\|_\infty\lesssim\rho_n^\kappa,
\quad \kappa=\frac{2}{2+3/s_y+d/s_z}.                                \tag{15}
\]

Conditional independence and (14) give the explicit linear conditional variance
\[
E\!\left[\left\{m^{-1}\sum_{a,i\in I_{a,e}}g_{a,e}(W_{a,i,n})\right\}^2\middle|\mathscr F_{-e}\right]
\le\frac C m\{E_n^{2+2a_y}+\rho_n^{2a_y\kappa}E_n^2\}+o_P(\rho_n^2/n). \tag{16}
\]
For the canonical kernel replacement, pair counting and independence give
\[
E[\{U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)-U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(B)\}^2\mid\mathscr F_{-e}]
\le\frac C{m^2}\|\Gamma^{1/2}(\widehat B-B)\Gamma^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2. \tag{17}
\]
At outcome level \(j\), (7)--(9) bound the Galerkin part by \(C\min\{2^{-2j},\varepsilon^{a_y}\}\). Consequently
\[
\frac{\|\Gamma^{1/2}(\widehat B-B)\Gamma^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}
\lesssim O_P((\varepsilon+\lambda_n)^2)
+\sum_{j\ge j_0}2^j\min\{2^{-4j},\varepsilon^{2a_y}\}=o_P(1).       \tag{18}
\]
Equations (16)--(18) show that the linear and canonical stochastic replacements are bounded by
\[
O_P\!\left[n^{-1/2}\{\varepsilon^{1+a_y}+\rho_n^{a_y\kappa}\varepsilon\}\right]
+o_P\!\left(\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right).     \tag{19}
\]
This is the empirical-process term suppressed in the previous informal argument. It does not change the claimed envelope: the tuning identity gives \(\sqrt n\,\varepsilon\to\infty\), and hence the first term in (19) is
\[
o_P\{\varepsilon^{2+a_y}+\rho_n^{a_y\kappa}\varepsilon^2\}.         \tag{20}
\]
Combining (13), (15), and (19)--(20) proves exactly the displayed pilot contribution and its negligibility criterion.

Finally, compact support gives \(\|\Phi(W)\|_2^2\lesssim D_{J_y,J_z}\). Applying exponential Markov to scalar quadratic forms on a \(1/4\)-net yields
\[
\max_e\|\widehat\Gamma^{(-e)}-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m}+D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m\right)=o_P(1). \tag{21}
\]
On the event furnished by (21), the square-root map is Lipschitz because \(\Gamma\) is uniformly positive definite. Equations (18), (21), and the identity defining \(\widehat K\) prove both operator consistency and \(\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}})\). The covariance condition occurs only in (21), as claimed.
