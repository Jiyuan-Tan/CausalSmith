For \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)\,dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\) and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\). The inverses exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\). If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the defining Neumann equation and the boundary condition at zero give
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)u_z'(y)=H_z(y).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Multiplying the Neumann equation by \(u_z\), integrating by parts, and using both Neumann boundary conditions yields
\[
\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)u_z'(y)^2\,dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy.                       \tag{2}
\]
Since \(F_{0,z}\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=F_{1,z}\{Q_{1,z}(v)\}=v\), the mean-value theorem gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}                 \tag{3}
\]
for an intermediate point \(\zeta_{v,z}\). Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) in \(W_2^2=\int_0^1|Q_{1,z}(v)-Q_{0,z}(v)|^2\,dv\), and using \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in (2)--(3), proves constants \(0<c_E<C_E<\infty\), depending only on \(c,C\), for which
\[
c_E\int_0^1\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy
\le W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
\le C_E\int_0^1\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy.            \tag{4}
\]
The shared-marginal assumption permits integration against the same \(R_n\). By the definitions of \(\rho_n\), \(\theta_n\), and the local-radius assumption,
\[
c_E\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_E\rho_n^2\le C_E\rho^2.             \tag{5}
\]
Thus the zero statistic has uniform root-mean-square risk at most \(C_E\rho^2\).

Put
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The decomposition and bounded-moment estimates in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\), before imposing its displayed absorption condition, give for the explicit estimator of \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\)
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n},                            \tag{6}
\]
where, uniformly over the model class,
\[
\|R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C p_n+o\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right). \tag{7}
\]
Here the \(L^2\) form follows from the same bounded wavelet-coefficient and clipped-pilot concentration estimates used in that lemma: every summand is bounded on the compact support, and integration of its exponential tail gives the displayed second moment. The variance conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\) and the spectrum and bias conclusions of \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) imply, at \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\),
\[
\|\mathbb L\|_2\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2},\qquad
\|\mathbb U\|_2\lesssim r_{n,d}(s_z),\qquad
|B^y|+|B^z|\lesssim r_{n,d}(s_z).                                  \tag{8}
\]
Minkowski's inequality applied to (6)--(8) proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}.                         \tag{9}
\]

Fix \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\). The proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives the single observable sample map
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}.                         \tag{10}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) similarly gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.                            \tag{11}
\]
The minimax experiment fixes \((n,d,s_y,s_z,\rho)\). Selecting, before seeing the data, the zero statistic or the one estimator among (9)--(11) with the smallest deterministic guarantee is therefore a single data-driven estimator and proves the asserted upper bounds; no law-specific nuisance enters this choice.

For the converse, use \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\). The proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) places every mixture component in the same model class and, after reducing the fixed \(c_0\) if necessary, supplies
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)ge c_M\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.  \tag{12}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives
\(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac12\chi^2(P,Q)^{1/2}\), so (12) has testing affinity bounded away from zero. Thresholding an arbitrary estimator halfway between the null value and the common separated alternative value turns it into a test; on a testing error its absolute estimation error is at least half the separation. Averaging over the Rademacher mixture and then using Jensen's inequality proves
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                                 \tag{13}
\]

For the regular direction, extend \(q_{a,t}^{0}\) constantly in \(z\). When \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), choose fixed small constants so that \(t=c_7\rho\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\) both have inverse-elliptic radius at most \(\rho\); this is possible by (4) and the cosine energy calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\). The product Hellinger bound in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) leaves positive testing affinity. Writing the endpoint as \(\Theta(t)\), implicit differentiation of \(F_{a,t}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}=v\) gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}.                                \tag{14}
\]
The denominator is bounded away from zero and the numerator is smooth, so \(\Theta''\) is continuous in a fixed neighborhood of zero. The cosine lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\). Shrinking the fixed neighborhood and integrating \(\Theta''\) twice yields
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c\,t|t'-t|ge c'\rho n^{-1/2}.          \tag{15}
\]
The same testing reduction supplies this regular lower bound. If \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)=n^{-b}\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), because \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), so (13) already has order \(\rho^2\). If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), split according as \(\rho^2\le r_{n,d}\) or \(\rho^2>r_{n,d}\); in the latter case \(\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}\lesssim\rho^2\). These cases give
\[
\max\!\left\{\min(\rho^2,r_{n,d}),\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\}\right\}
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}\}.                     \tag{16}
\]
Equations (13)--(16) prove the lower bound.

Combining (9) and (16) proves the stated pilot matching condition. The supplemental envelopes do not create an unproved matching claim. Indeed, \(\tau<s_z\) implies
\[
a_\tau=\frac{\tau}{2\tau+d+1}
<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}
<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}=b,
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12,                                             \tag{17}
\]
where the second strict inequality follows after cross multiplication from \(s_z(4s_z+3d+4)>0\). When \(s_z\ge1\),
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\frac12,
\qquad
\frac1{d+2}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},                                  \tag{18}
\]
because \(4s_z(d+1)>d\). Thus both global rates are asymptotically larger than the intrinsic stochastic denominator uniformly in \(\rho\), although either may be smaller than the pilot remainder.

Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}\) gives
\[
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\qquad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/(2(4s_z+d))}.                               \tag{19}
\]
The latter tends to zero exactly when \(s_z>d/4\). If \(s_z\le d/4\), the equality quadratic lower bound is at least root-\(n\) order while the first projection vanishes at equality, ruling out a uniformly positive-variance regular root-\(n\) description of the whole class. This proves every assertion.
