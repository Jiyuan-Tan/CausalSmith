Fix an evaluation fold \(e\) and condition on its opposite-fold pilots. By \(\texttt{ass:two-arm-sampling}\), the evaluation observations are then independent. Write \(e_a=q_{a,n}-\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}\), with the common-marginal error included in \(e\). Differentiating the inverse equations \(F_a(Q_a(u))=u\) three times on the fixed density interval \([c/2,2C]\) gives the deterministic Taylor identity
\[
\theta(q)-\theta(\widehat q)
=D\theta_{\widehat q}[e]
+\mathcal H_{\widehat{\bar q}}[e_1-e_0,e_1-e_0]+R_e,  \tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathcal H_{\widehat{\bar q}}\) is the equality inverse-elliptic quadratic form. The same differentiation, with every denominator at least \(c/2\), gives
\[
|R_e|\le C\{\|e\|_\infty^3+
\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\|e\|_\infty^2\}.                  \tag{2}
\]
The score in \(\texttt{def:anisotropic-estimator}\) is the centered empirical representation of \(D\theta_{\widehat q}[e]\), including the common-marginal derivative. Conditional on the pilot, independence of distinct observations and the centering in \(X_{a,i}^{(-e)}\) give
\[
E\widehat U_{e,J_y,J_z,n}
=\langle\Pi_{J_y,J_z}(e_1-e_0),
\widehat{\mathcal G}^{(-e)}
\Pi_{J_y,J_z}(e_1-e_0)\rangle_{L^2(dy\,d\widehat R)}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the two within-arm expectations in (3) are \(e_a^\top\widehat He_a\), while the cross-arm expectation is \(-2e_1^\top\widehat He_0\). Thus the diagonal-free statistic estimates the projected quadratic term in (1) without a diagonal bias.

Subtract conditional expectations in (1)--(3) and expand the quadratic statistic algebraically into its two projections. After averaging the folds, the first projection combines with the empirical score to give \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\), the canonical second projection is \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and the two omitted-frequency terms are \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\). Hence
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}.              \tag{4}
\]
The first two stochastic terms are orthogonal because the second-order kernel has zero conditional expectation in either argument.

To compute the linear variance, put \(H_z(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv\). The inverse-equation derivative equals \(H_z/q_{a,n}\), followed by centering. Changing variables between quantile and outcome coordinates and using \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\) compares the squared two-arm score norm with \(\int H_z^2dy\,dR_n\). The energy identity in the cosine proof compares the latter with \(\rho_n^2\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})
\asymp\frac{\rho_n^2}{n}.                             \tag{5}
\]
If \(\Delta_n=0\), then \(H_z=0\), so \(\mathbb L\) is identically zero.

For the canonical quadratic projection, a one-fold feature contrast multiplied by \(\sqrt m\) has covariance \(\Gamma\). Whitening and diagonalizing \(K=\Gamma^{1/2}H\Gamma^{1/2}\), followed by direct expansion of the diagonal-free second moment, gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
=\frac{4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.              \tag{6}
\]
The finite-\(m\) factor is \(1+O(m^{-1})\). Compact wavelet support bounds the remaining fourth-moment contraction by \(O(D_{J_y,J_z}/n^2)=o(1)\), using \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\). Thus \(A/n\) is the asserted uniform asymptotic standard-deviation scale.

The spectrum proposition supplies the deterministic bias bounds. Equations (1)--(3), the interpolation and operator bounds in \(\texttt{lem:observable-pilot-control}\), and sample splitting give
\[
R_{J_y,J_z,n}
=O_P\!\left\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3+
\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\right\}
+o_P(1)\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+
\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.                       \tag{7}
\]
Under the theorem's displayed condition, (7) is little-oh of the sum of the four expansion scales. Substitution into (4), together with (5)--(6), proves the uniform expansion.

At the rate-balancing resolutions, the spectrum proposition and the tuning identities give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp2^{-2s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z).                                  \tag{8}
\]
Equations (4)--(8) give the displayed observable root-mean-square scale. When the two pilot terms are no larger than \(\rho_n/\sqrt n+r_{n,d}(s_z)\), they are absorbed. No covariance estimate enters any of (1)--(8).
