Abbreviate \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV}\). The proposed uniform expansion and the corrected bias premise give
\[
n\{\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n-B^y_{J_y,n}-B^z_{J_z,n}\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+o_P(S).          \tag{1}
\]

Let \(s_{a,n}\) be the exact centered first derivative and let \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) be the projected inverse-elliptic derivative. Positivity makes the inverse distribution maps uniformly Lipschitz. Expanding their defining identities around \(\bar q_n\) bounds the nonlinear part by \(C\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\) in \(L^2(P_{a,n})\). The outcome antiderivative and weighted projection estimates in equations (13)--(15) of \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) bound the two projection tails. Thus
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\le C\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}. \tag{2}
\]

For fold \(e\), put \(\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n})\), \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(\delta=\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and
\[
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta\},
\quad
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,\delta^\top B_{J_y,J_z,n}\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}. \tag{3}
\]
The two \(Z_{e,n}\) are independent, centered, and have covariance identity. With \(Z_{e,k}=v_k^\top Z_{e,n}\) and \(\eta_{k,n}=v_k^\top\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\,\delta\), diagonalization gives
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2.             \tag{4}
\]
An empirical average of a centered score error with \(L^2\)-norm \(r_n\), multiplied by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm at most \(C\sqrt n r_n\). Hence (2) gives
\[
\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2
\le C\sqrt n\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}. \tag{5}
\]
The first term is \(o(S)\) because \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\), \(\rho_n\sqrt n\asymp\sqrt{nV}\), and \(S\ge\sqrt{nV}\). For \(x_n=2^{-J_y}\), the corrected bias premise implies
\[
n x_n^{2s_y+2}=\{n x_n^{2s_y+1}\}x_n=O(Sx_n),
\]
so \(\sqrt n,2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}=O(\sqrt{Sx_n})=o(S)\). The same premise gives \(\sqrt n,2^{-s_zJ_z}=O(\sqrt S)=o(S)\), because the spectral proposition gives \(A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\to\infty\). Therefore
\[
\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2=o(S),
\qquad
8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2).                           \tag{6}
\]

Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\). The proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) applies because \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), the projected linear summands in (3) are uniformly bounded by the compact support and density bounds, and \(E G_n^2=O(S^2)\) by (6). It supplies a martingale-difference array summing to \(G_n+Q_n\), its Lindeberg condition, and predictable conditional-variance convergence. Hoeffding orthogonality, (6), and the variance part of that lemma give
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2).                                     \tag{7}
\]
For fixed \(t\), conditionally apply
\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}
\]
to each martingale increment. Lindeberg makes the summed remainders vanish, and predictable-variance convergence permits replacement by (7). Iteration over the observations yields
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\to0.     \tag{8}
\]

Define the finite Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].                   \tag{9}
\]
Equations (6) and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) imply
\[
\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\frac{nV+o(S^2)}{S^2},
\qquad
\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\le\frac{\max_k|\nu_k|}{A}\lesssim M_n^{-1/2}\to0. \tag{10}
\]
Taylor expansion in (9), uniformly for fixed \(t\), gives
\[
\left|\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}+\frac{t^2(A^2+nV)}{2S^2}\right|=o(1). \tag{11}
\]
The variance \(\sigma_n^2=(A^2+nV)/S^2\) lies in \([1/2,1]\). Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which it converges; (8), (11), and uniform second moments then make both laws converge to the same centered normal law. The subsequence criterion for bounded-Lipschitz distance proves both the finite-proxy and diffuse-normal conclusions. Equations (1) and (6) change the normalized statistic by \(o_P(1)\), so the same conclusions hold for the estimator.

At equality, \(\Delta_n=0\), hence \(V=0\), every \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\). Taking \(S=A\) in (9)--(11) gives \(n\mathbb U/A\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\). The vanishing maximal eigenvalue ratio in (10) forces this Gaussianization.

If \(D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m=o(1)\), the pilot lemma gives \(\|\widehat K^{(-e)}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}})\). Conditionally on the pilots, for standard Gaussian \(Z\) and \(E_e=\widehat K^{(-e)}-K\),
\[
E\{Z^\top E_eZ-\operatorname{tr}(E_e)\}^2=2\|E_e\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=o_P(A^2), \tag{12}
\]
which proves the observed-kernel replacement.

Finally, at a fixed unequal law, the centered Kantorovich scores are bounded and have variance \(V_{L,n}\). The ordinary independent-sum characteristic-function expansion gives \(\mathbb L/\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\). The assumed relation \(\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}/A\to\infty\) makes \(\mathbb U=o_P(\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n})\) by the quadratic variance formula. The corrected bias and remainder hypotheses remove the remaining terms, and \(\widehat V_{L,n}/V_{L,n}\to_P1\) permits studentization. This proves the last assertion.
