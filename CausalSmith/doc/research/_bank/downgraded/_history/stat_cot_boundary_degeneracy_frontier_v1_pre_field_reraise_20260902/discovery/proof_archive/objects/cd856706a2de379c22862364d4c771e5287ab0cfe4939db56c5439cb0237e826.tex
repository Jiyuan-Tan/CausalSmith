For \(d=0\), retain \(F_a,Q_a\), and \(H=F_1-F_0\) from the preceding proof. Equations (1)--(3) there use only the one-dimensional density bounds, so
\[
\|Q_1-Q_0\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2=\theta_n\asymp\rho_n^2\le\rho^2. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(D=Q_1-Q_0\), \(\widehat D=\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n}\), and \(P_J=P^Q_J\). Orthogonality gives the exact decomposition
\[
\widehat\theta^0_{J,n}-\theta_n
=2\langle P_JD,P_J(\widehat D-D)\rangle
+\|P_J(\widehat D-D)\|_2^2-\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2.          \tag{2}
\]

Represent an arm-\(a\) observation as \(Q_a(U_i)\), with \(U_i\) uniform. On \(((k-1)/n,k/n]\), \(\widehat Q_{a,n}(u)=Q_a(U_{(k)})\). Since \(q_{a,n}\ge c\), \(Q_a\) is \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz. Direct beta-integral calculation gives
\[
E U_{(k)}=\frac{k}{n+1},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(U_{(k)})
=\frac{k(n+1-k)}{(n+1)^2(n+2)},
\]
and \(E|U_{(k)}-EU_{(k)}|^4\le Cn^{-2}\). Since \(|k/(n+1)-u|\le2/n\) on the indicated interval, integration over \(u\) gives uniformly
\[
E\|\widehat Q_{a,n}-Q_a\|_2^2\le Cn^{-1},\qquad
E\|\widehat Q_{a,n}-Q_a\|_2^4\le Cn^{-2}.             \tag{3}
\]
For the second inequality use \((\int e^2)^2\le\int e^4\) on the unit interval. Independence and the triangle inequality give the same orders for \(\widehat D-D\).

The density H\"older assumption and positivity imply
\[
Q_a'(u)=1/q_{a,n}\{Q_a(u)\},
\]
so reciprocal and composition difference quotients give \(Q_a\in\mathcal C^{s_y+1}\) with a uniform radius. Wavelet regularity then gives
\[
\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2\le C2^{-2(s_y+1)J}.                 \tag{4}
\]
Minkowski and Cauchy--Schwarz in (2), followed by (1), (3), and (4), yield
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^0}\}
\le C'\{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.                      \tag{5}
\]
The zero estimator has risk at most \(C\rho^2\) by (1). Selecting the better explicit map proves the upper bound \(C\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}\).

For the lower bound, use \(\texttt{def:no-covariate-witness}\). Comparing \(t=0\) with \(t=c_0\min\{\rho,n^{-1/2}\}\), the bounded Hellinger calculation and the cosine lemma give endpoint separation
\[
c\min\{\rho^2,n^{-1}\}.                              \tag{6}
\]
For \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), compare \(t=c_1\rho\) with \(t'=t+c_2n^{-1/2}\). Small fixed constants keep both laws in the radius-\(\rho\) class and their testing affinity positive. The continuity and positive lower bound for the endpoint second derivative established in (11)--(12) give separation at least
\[
c_3\rho n^{-1/2}.                                    \tag{7}
\]
The threshold-test reduction used above combines (6)--(7) into
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.             \tag{8}
\]
Together with (5), this proves the minimax assertion.

It remains to prove the null law. At the uniform null, apply the cited quantile-process lemma independently in the two arms. The omitted lower-tail squared norm is bounded by \((U_{(1)}+n^{-1})^2\), and the upper-tail bound is analogous with \(1-U_{(n)}\). The beta moments in (3) make both \(o_P(1)\). Hence the untruncated processes have the same \(L^2\) limits as their cited truncations. Arm independence and Brownian-bridge symmetry give
\[
Z_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})
\Rightarrow\mathbb B_1-\mathbb B_0
\stackrel d=\sqrt2\,\mathbb B
\quad\text{in }L^2(0,1).                              \tag{9}
\]
The projectors \(P^Q_{J_y^0}\) are contractions and converge strongly to the identity because \(J_y^0\to\infty\). Strong convergence is uniform on compact subsets of \(L^2\); tightness from (9) therefore gives \(\|(I-P^Q_{J_y^0})Z_n\|_2=o_P(1)\). Consequently
\[
n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}
=\|P^Q_{J_y^0}Z_n\|_2^2
\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt.                \tag{10}
\]

The covariance kernel of \(\mathbb B\) is \(K(s,t)=s\wedge t-st\). Direct differentiation twice with zero boundary values shows that \(\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt)\) are its orthonormal eigenfunctions with eigenvalues \((\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\). Gaussian orthogonality makes the corresponding coordinates independent standard normals, so
\[
2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt
\stackrel d=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2. \tag{11}
\]
Moreover,
\[
2\sum_{k\le2^{J_y^0}}\frac1{\pi^2k^2}\longrightarrow
2\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac1{\pi^2k^2}=\frac13,
\]
which is the mean of the limit in (11). Subtracting the displayed finite sum divided by \(n\) therefore yields the centered chaos. Finally, both equations \(\rho^2=n^{-1}\) and \(\rho n^{-1/2}=n^{-1}\) give \(\rho=n^{-1/2}\), proving \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\).
