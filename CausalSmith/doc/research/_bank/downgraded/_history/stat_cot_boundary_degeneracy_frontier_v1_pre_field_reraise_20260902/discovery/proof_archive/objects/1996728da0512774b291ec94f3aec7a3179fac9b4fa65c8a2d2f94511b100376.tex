By the tuning definition,
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad e=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.
\]
Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\). If uniform absorption holds, taking \(\rho=0\) and retaining the logarithmic factor forces
\[
(2+a_y)e>b.                                                            \tag{1}
\]
If \(b\le1/2\), taking \(\rho=1\) in the local term would require \(2e>b\), but, for \(x=s_y\) and \(t=d/s_z\),
\[
b-2e=\frac{2(tx+2)}{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)}>0.
\]
Thus \(b>1/2\). At \(\rho_n=n^{1/2-b}\), the two denominator terms agree, so the local term forces
\[
2e+a_y\kappa(b-1/2)>b.                                                 \tag{2}
\]
For \(0<x=s_y\le1\), direct common-denominator simplification gives
\[
b-2e-x\kappa(b-1/2)
=\frac{t^2x^3+2t^2x^2-2tx^3+5tx^2+10tx-8x^3-4x^2+8x+12}
{(t+4)(tx+2x+1)(tx+2x+3)}>0.                                          \tag{3}
\]
The coefficient of \(t\) is \(x(-2x^2+5x+10)>0\), the coefficient of \(t^2\) is positive, and the constant polynomial is at least \(8\) on \((0,1]\). Hence (2) is impossible unless \(s_y>1\). In that branch \(a_y=1\), so (1)--(2) become \(3e>b\) and \(2e+\kappa(b-1/2)>b\).

Conversely, assume those strict inequalities. The first pilot term is \(o(n^{-b})\). Since \(0<\kappa<1\), differentiation shows that
\[
f_n(\rho)=\frac{\rho^\kappa}{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-b}}
\]
is maximized at a fixed multiple of \(n^{1/2-b}\), and therefore \(\sup_{0\le\rho\le1}f_n(\rho)\lesssim n^{b-\kappa(b-1/2)}\). Multiplication by \(n^{-2e}(\log n)^{2e}\) and (2) gives absorption.

The second strict inequality has sign equal to the polynomial
\[
P(x)=(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)x^2-s_z(11d+4s_z)x-12s_z^2.                    \tag{4}
\]
For \(P(x)>0\), its leading coefficient must be positive, so \(s_z>3d/4\). The unique positive root is
\[
x_{\mathrm{crit}}=
\frac{s_z\{11d+4s_z+\sqrt{(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d)}\}}
{2(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)},                                                  \tag{5}
\]
because \((11d+4s_z)^2+48(4s_z-3d)(2s_z+d)=(4s_z-d)(100s_z+23d)\). Also \(P(1)=-3d^2-13ds_z-8s_z^2<0\), so (5) is in the branch \(s_y>1\), and
\[
P\!\left(\frac{4s_z}{4s_z-d}\right)
=-\frac{16s_z^2(d+2s_z)(d+4s_z)}{(4s_z-d)^2}<0
\]
shows that (2) implies (1). This proves the exact equivalence. The matching conclusion follows from the proved conditional minimax theorem.

For the supplemental estimator assume \(s_z\ge1\). The cost satisfies
\[
|(y_1-y_0)^2-(y_1'-y_0')^2|\le2(|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|),              \tag{6}
\]
and the anisotropic H\"older condition gives \(\int|q_a(y,z)-q_a(y,z')|dy\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2\). Coupling common mass identically and residual mass arbitrarily on \([0,1]\) gives the required Wasserstein--1 Lipschitz kernel bound.

On a block of size \(N\), use cells of side \(h_N=N^{-1/(d+2)}\). There are \(M_N\lesssim h_N^{-d}\) cells. If \(p_j\) is the common marginal mass of cell \(j\), binomial variance and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
E\|\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}-R_n^\varphi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{M_N}\sqrt{p_j(1-p_j)/N}
\le\frac12\sqrt{M_N/N}\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}.                        \tag{7}
\]
Projection to cell centers costs at most \(\sqrt d h_N\). The common marginal and the triangle inequality therefore furnish a coupling satisfying the premise of \(\mathrm{lem{:}lin\text{-}gao\text{-}blanchet\text{-}glynn\text{-}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}rate}\). That established result gives for a bounded block estimator
\[
E|\widehat\theta_N^{\mathrm{dir}}-\theta_n|le\delta_N:=CN^{-1/(d+2)}\log N. \tag{8}
\]
Take odd \(K_n\asymp\log n\) and independent blocks of size \(N_n=\lfloor n/K_n\rfloor\). Markov's inequality makes a block bad with probability at most \(1/8\) at threshold \(8\delta_{N_n}\). If the median is bad, at least half the blocks are bad, whence
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n|>8\delta_{N_n}\}
\le2^{K_n}(1/8)^{K_n/2}=2^{-K_n/2}.                                  \tag{9}
\]
Choosing the fixed multiplier in \(K_n\) so that the last quantity is \(O(\delta_{N_n}^2)\), and using boundedness off this event, proves
\[
\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.
\]
Taking the deterministic minimum of this guarantee, the anisotropic guarantee, and the zero-estimator guarantee proves the final envelope without changing the converse.
