Fix an arbitrary triangular sequence of laws in the declared model class satisfying the hypotheses; proving bounds uniformly along every such sequence proves the asserted uniform conclusions.  Abbreviate
\[
D=D_{J_y,J_z},\qquad M=2^{dJ_z},\qquad
\Gamma=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n},\qquad B=B_{J_y,J_z,n},\qquad K=K_{J_y,J_z,n},\qquad
A=A_{J_y,J_z,n},\qquad V=V_{L,n},\qquad S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV},
\]
and put \(t_y=2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}\) and \(t_z=2^{-s_zJ_z}\).  Constants below depend only on the fixed density, smoothness, support, and wavelet constants.

By \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}second\mbox{-}order\mbox{-}expansion}\),
\[
n\{\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n-B^y_{J_y,n}-B^z_{J_z,n}\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathcal R_n,
\qquad
n\mathcal R_n=o_P\{\rho_n\sqrt n+A+n(t_y^2+t_z^2)\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The same theorem gives \(\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\rho_n/\sqrt n\).  Since \(V=n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\),
\[
\sqrt{nV}=n\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\rho_n\sqrt n.
\]
The bias premise says \(t_y^2+t_z^2=O(S/n)\), so (1) implies \(n\mathcal R_n=o_P(S)\).

We next repair the high-frequency part of the linear projection.  Let \(s_{a,n}\) denote the exact centered first-order score and let \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) denote the equality-Hessian score after the declared Gram-corrected projection.  For fixed \(z\), write \(F_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z)\) for the conditional distribution function.  The positivity assumption gives
\[
\|F_{a,n}^{-1}(\cdot\mid z)\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\le c^{-1},
\qquad
\|F_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)-F_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z)\|_\infty
\le \|\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\|_\infty.
\]
Taylor expansion of the inverse identity \(F_{a,n}\{F_{a,n}^{-1}(u\mid z)\mid z\}=u\), whose denominator is bounded below by \(c\), shows that the linear displacement is \(u_n(\cdot,z)=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\); its boundary derivative vanishes because \(\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)dy=0\), and the zero-integral normalization selects the declared Neumann inverse.  A second Taylor remainder is bounded in \(L^2(P_{a,n})\) by \(C\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\): one factor is the inverse-elliptic energy norm \(\rho_n\), and the other is the uniformly bounded inverse displacement.

It remains to approximate the linear displacement itself.  In the outcome direction, the inverse-elliptic comparison in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\mbox{-}spectrum\mbox{-}bias}\) attaches weight \(2^{-2j_y}\) to level \(j_y\).  The anisotropic H\"older coefficient bound and the boundary-wavelet regularity therefore give
\[
\sum_{j_y>J_y,\,k_y,\,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}|\langle\Delta_n,
\psi^y_{j_y,k_y}\psi^z_\gamma\rangle|^2\le C t_y^2.
\]
The corresponding covariate coefficient tail is at most \(Ct_z^2\).  The common-marginal and conditional density bounds convert these coefficient norms to either arm's \(L^2(P_{a,n})\) norm with fixed constants.  Taking square roots and combining the nonlinear and projection parts proves the corrected bound
\[
\boxed{\quad
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\le C\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty+t_y+t_z\}.
\quad}                                                                  \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the two tail terms in (2) are not multiplied by \(\rho_n\).

Before using (2), note that it nevertheless gives the required \(n\)-scale approximation.  The spectrum proposition yields \(A\asymp M^{1/2}\), while \(M\le D=o(n^2)\).  Also \(\rho_n\le\rho\le1\), hence \(V=O(1)\).  Consequently
\[
\frac Sn\le \frac An+\sqrt{\frac Vn}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Because \(t_z^2=O(S/n)\), this forces \(J_z\to\infty\), and then \(M\to\infty\), \(A\to\infty\), and \(S\to\infty\).  Moreover,
\[
\sqrt n(t_y+t_z)\le\{2n(t_y^2+t_z^2)\}^{1/2}=O(\sqrt S)=o(S).           \tag{3}
\]

For fold \(e\), define
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\qquad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}.
\]
The covariance lower bound in the spectrum proposition makes the inverse square root well defined.  Independent randomized-arm sampling and the definition of \(\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\) give \(EZ_{e,n}=0\), \(E Z_{e,n}Z_{e,n}^{\top}=I\), and independence of the two folds.  Put
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}
B\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.
\]
The difference \(n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\) is a fixed-weight sum of centered copies of the score difference in (2).  Independence therefore bounds its squared \(L^2\) norm by \(Cn\) times the square of the right side of (2).  Since \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\), \(\rho_n\sqrt n=O(\sqrt{nV})\), and (3) holds,
\[
\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_2=o(S),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=nV+o(S^2).                                    \tag{4}
\]
When \(\rho_n=0\), positivity of the inverse-elliptic form implies \(\Delta_n=0\), so both linear quantities in (4) vanish.  Diagonalizing \(K=\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2}\), and using \(m=n/2\), gives the exact coordinate formula
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
8\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2).                    \tag{5}
\]

We now control the canonical quadratic projection.  Write \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  Expanding this declared diagonal-free canonical second Hoeffding projection and centering each argument gives, after indexing the independent evaluation observations by \(r\),
\[
Q_n=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s),
\qquad
E\{h_{n,rs}\mid W_r\}=E\{h_{n,rs}\mid W_s\}=0.                    \tag{6}
\]
The weighted Neumann equation gives explicitly
\[
\partial_y\{\mathcal G_{n,z}g\}(y)
=-\bar q_n(y,z)^{-1}\int_0^y g(v)dv.
\]
Thus the Green kernel is uniformly bounded because \(\bar q_n\ge c\); the zero-mass condition supplies the Neumann boundary value at \(y=1\).  In the covariate direction, compact support of the boundary wavelets and bounded overlap at each scale imply that their projection kernel \(\kappa_{J_z}\) satisfies
\[
\iint\kappa_{J_z}(z,z')^2dR_n(z)dR_n(z')\le CM,
\qquad
\iint\kappa_{J_z}(z,z')^4dR_n(z)dR_n(z')\le CM^3.
\]
The Gram and covariance eigenvalue bounds in the spectrum proposition preserve these orders under raw-moment conjugation and whitening.  Direct pairing of the indices in (6), with the diagonal excluded, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{Var}(Q_n)=A^2\{1+o(1)\},
\qquad \max_r\sum_{s\ne r}E h_{n,rs}^2\le CM/m,\\
&\sum_{r<s}E h_{n,rs}^4\le CM^3/m^2,
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)^2}
\le\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}\le C/M.
\end{aligned}                                                           \tag{7}
\]
Here the outcome-frequency contractions are summable because the inverse-elliptic weights are \(2^{-2j_y}\), and only coincident or finitely neighboring covariate supports survive.  Since \(M\le D=o(n^2)\) and \(m=n/2\), the fourth-moment ratio \(M/m^2\) tends to zero.  The same spectrum result gives the forced diffusion
\[
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{\{\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\}^{1/2}}
\le CM^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0.                                        \tag{8}
\]

Write \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\), order the independent observations, let \(\mathcal F_r=\sigma(W_1,\ldots,W_r)\), and set
\[
\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(W_s,W_r).
\]
Canonicality in (6) makes this a martingale-difference array whose sum is \(G_n+Q_n\).  The squared cost is bounded on \([0,1]\), positivity bounds the inverse-map score, and compactly supported wavelet projection is uniformly bounded on the declared H\"older ball; hence \(\max_r\|\ell_{n,r}\|_\infty/S=o(1)\) because \(S\to\infty\).  Expanding conditional squares and fourth powers, every nonzero term pairs each observation index at least twice.  Repeated edges, shared-index pairs, and four-edge cycles are respectively bounded by the last three quantities in (7).  Therefore, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sum_rE\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal D_{n,r}}S\right)^2
\mathbf 1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\epsilon S\}\right]\longrightarrow0,
\]
and
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
-E(G_n+Q_n)^2\right\}^2\right]}{S^4}
\le C\{m^{-1}+M/m^2+M^{-1}+o(1)\}\longrightarrow0.                  \tag{9}
\]
Conditional Taylor expansion of \(e^{itx}\), using the first part of (9) for the summed Taylor remainder and the second part for the conditional variance, gives
\[
E e^{it(G_n+Q_n)/S}
-\exp\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
The covariance between \(G_n\) and \(Q_n\) is zero by canonicality, while (4) and (7) give the two displayed variance terms.

Define the stated Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
With \(c_{e,k}=\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\eta_{k,n}/S\), independence and direct one-dimensional Gaussian integration give
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].                         \tag{11}
\]
Equations (5) and (8) imply
\[
\left|\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
+\frac{t^2}{2S^2}(A^2+nV)\right|
\le C_t\max_k\frac{|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{S}
\left\{\frac{A^2}{S^2}+\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2\right\}+o(1)
=o(1).                                                                  \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, \(A^2=4\sum_k\nu_k^2\), \(\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=(nV+o(S^2))/S^2\), and \(\max_k|\nu_k|/S\le\max_k|\nu_k|/A\to0\).

The moving variance
\[
\sigma_n^2=\frac{A^2+nV}{(A+\sqrt{nV})^2}
\]
lies in \([1/2,1]\).  Along every subsequence it has a convergent further subsequence.  For a compactly supported smooth test function, Fourier inversion turns (10)--(12) into convergence of its expectations to those under the corresponding centered Gaussian law.  Truncation outside a common compact set is uniform because all displayed laws have bounded second moments, and compactly supported smooth functions approximate bounded Lipschitz functions on that set.  This proves, along every such further subsequence and hence along the original sequence, both
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L((G_n+Q_n)/S),\mathcal L(\mathcal Q_n^G/S)\}\to0
\]
and
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L((G_n+Q_n)/S),\mathcal N(0,\sigma_n^2)\}\to0.
\]
Combining these relations with (1), (3), and (4) proves the two asserted approximations for the normalized estimator, including the exact twice-repeated spectrum and shift constants.

At conditional equality, positivity of the inverse form gives \(\Delta_n=0\).  Hence \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\), every \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\).  Now \(S=A\), so (8), (11), and (12) give
\[
A^{-1}\sum_{e,k}\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}(\xi_{e,k}^2-1)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
and the first bounded-Lipschitz approximation transfers this limit to \(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}/A\).  Because (8) is forced by the theorem's premises, no fixed or subsequential non-Gaussian chaos occurs in this \(d\ge1\), growing-covariate-resolution branch.

Suppose next that \(D\log n/m=o(1)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\mbox{-}pilot\mbox{-}control}\), for the foldwise observed kernels and their average \(\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\),
\[
\|\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}})=o_P(A).
\]
The relative elliptic-resolvent estimate used in that lemma, applied to the local mean \(g_n=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\,\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\), also gives
\[
\|(\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K)g_n\|=o_P(S);
\]
this is precisely the local-mean part of the lemma's Gram, ridge, and operator contribution, multiplied by \(n\), and its deterministic pilot terms are \(o(S)\) by the expansion condition and the bias premise.  For a self-adjoint matrix \(H\) and a vector \(g\), define the coordinate-free proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G(H,g)=\sum_{e=1}^2\{(\xi_e+g)^\top H(\xi_e+g)-\operatorname{tr}(H)-g^\top Hg\},
\]
where each \(\xi_e\) is standard Gaussian in the retained feature space.  In the eigenbasis of \(K\), \(\mathcal Q_n^G(K,g_n)\) is exactly the declared proxy whose log characteristic function is (11).  Coupling the population and observed proxies with the same standard Gaussian vectors, direct Gaussian moments give
\[
E_\xi|\mathcal Q_n^G(\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}},g_n)-\mathcal Q_n^G(K,g_n)|^2
\le C\{\|\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
+\|(\bar K_n^{\mathrm{obs}}-K)g_n\|^2\}=o_P(S^2).
\]
This proves the stated observed-kernel replacement.

Finally consider the separate fixed-unequal-law branch.  The exact centered first-order score is bounded because the support and squared cost are bounded and the conditional densities are bounded below by \(c\).  Write its independent centered contributions after division by \(\sqrt{V/n}\) as \(X_{n,r}\).  At a fixed unequal law, \(\max_r\|X_{n,r}\|_\infty=O(n^{-1/2})\), \(\sum_rEX_{n,r}^2=1\), and \(\sum_rE|X_{n,r}|^3=O(n^{-1/2})\).  Independence and the third-order expansion of each characteristic function give
\[
\log E\exp\{it\sum_rX_{n,r}\}=-t^2/2+O_t(n^{-1/2}).
\]
The Fourier smoothing and second-moment truncation argument used above therefore yields
\[
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The hypothesis \(\sqrt{nV}/A\to\infty\), together with \(\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb U)=\{1+o(1)\}A/n\), makes the quadratic term \(o_P(\sqrt{V/n})\).  The stated bias and remainder hypothesis removes the deterministic and residual terms on the same scale.  Multiplication by \(\sqrt{V/\widehat V_{L,n}}\to_P1\) then proves
\[
\frac{\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n}
{\sqrt{\widehat V_{L,n}/n}}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
as claimed.
