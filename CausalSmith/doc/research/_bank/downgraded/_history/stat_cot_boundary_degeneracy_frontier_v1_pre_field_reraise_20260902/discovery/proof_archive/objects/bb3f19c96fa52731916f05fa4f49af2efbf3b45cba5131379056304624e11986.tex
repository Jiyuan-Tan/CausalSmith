Let \(g\) be the finite wavelet sum in the proposition and, for each \(z\), put \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\).  Every retained outcome feature has zero integral, so the Neumann problem is solvable.  Its weak equation gives
\[
\langle g,u_z\rangle_{L^2(dy)}
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)|\partial_yu_z(y)|^2dy.                              \tag{1}
\]
For every zero-mean \(v\in H^1(0,1)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz give \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\).  Consequently the variational formula
\[
\langle g,\mathcal G_{n,z}g\rangle
=\sup_{v:\int v=0}\left\{2\langle g,v\rangle-int_0^1\bar q_n|v'|^2dy\right\}
\]
and \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) imply, with constants depending only on \(c,C\),
\[
\langle g,\mathcal G_{n,z}g\rangle
\asymp\|g(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}^2.                                    \tag{2}
\]
For a compactly supported boundary outcome wavelet at level \(j_y\), its antiderivative has \(L^2\)-norm comparable to \(2^{-j_y}\); only a uniformly bounded number of such antiderivatives overlap at a point, and the boundary scaling block has fixed dimension.  Applying these facts level by level and then using orthogonality of the covariate wavelets gives
\[
\|g\|_{L^2(dR_n;H^{-1}_y)}^2
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2.                  \tag{3}
\]
The bounds \(c\le r_n\le C\) transfer the corresponding Lebesgue tensor calculation to \(dR_n\).  Combining (1)--(3) proves the first displayed quadratic-form comparison.

For \(d\ge1\), write \(p_{a,n}=q_{a,n}r_n\).  If \(x\) is the coefficient vector of \(g\), then
\[
x^\top\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int\{g(y,z)-v\}^2p_{a,n}(y,z)dydz.                 \tag{4}
\]
Because \(\int_0^1g(y,z)dy=0\) for each \(z\),
\[
\int\{g(y,z)-v\}^2dydz=\int g(y,z)^2dydz+v^2.
\]
The two density lower bounds and Parseval's identity therefore bound (4) below by a fixed positive multiple of \(\|x\|_2^2\), while the upper density bounds give the corresponding upper bound.  For \(d=0\), the same calculation uses only the conditional-density bounds.  Summing the two arms proves \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I\).

Congruence by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\), (3), and the variational characterization of ordered eigenvalues compare the eigenvalues of \(K\) with those of the diagonal form having weight \(2^{-2j_y}\).  There are \(\asymp2^{j_y+dJ_z}\) coordinates at outcome level \(j_y\).  Hence, for \(2^{-2J_y}\le u\le1\),
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\asymp2^{dJ_z}\sum_{j_y\le(1/2)\log_2(u^{-1})}2^{j_y}
\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}.                              \tag{5}
\]
Inverting this monotone counting relation yields
\[
\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2
\asymp2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}
\asymp2^{dJ_z},                                                            \tag{6}
\]
whereas the largest eigenvalue is comparable to one.  This proves the operator, Hilbert--Schmidt, and normalized maximal-eigenvalue assertions.  When \(d=0\), the multiplicity factor in (6) is one, so the sum remains bounded above and away from zero as \(J_y\to\infty\).

It remains to bound truncation.  The tensor H\"older and wavelet-regularity assumptions give, by applying the defining H\"older difference bounds and the vanishing moments in each coordinate,
\[
|\langle\Delta_n,\psi^y_{j_y,k_y}\psi^z_\gamma\rangle|
\lesssim2^{-j_y(s_y+1/2)}2^{-j_z(s_z+d/2)}.                                \tag{7}
\]
After the first derivative has been removed, each truncation remainder is quadratic in the omitted discrepancy.  The inverse-elliptic form contributes weight \(2^{-2j_y}\) to an outcome coefficient.  Squaring (7), multiplying by the level multiplicities, and summing the geometric tails gives
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.
\]
All constants are uniform over the fixed-radius model class because the density, H\"older-radius, and wavelet-overlap constants are uniform.
