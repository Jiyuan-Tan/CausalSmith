Condition on both pilot folds.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) and the deterministic split in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\), the observations in evaluation fold \(e\) are independent of all quantities estimated from fold \(3-e\).  Thus, conditionally on the pilot sigma-field, every pilot score and pilot Hessian is fixed.

Apply the algebraic Hoeffding decomposition to each diagonal-free quadratic correction in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\).  This is an identity: add and subtract the two population feature means in every factor, collect the terms containing exactly one centered evaluation observation, and leave the product of two centered observations as the canonical term.  After averaging the two folds, the terms containing one centered observation combine with the one-step score to give \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\), the products of two centered observations give \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and projection outside the retained tensor space gives \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\).  Therefore
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n},                                  \tag{1}
\]
where \(R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) consists precisely of the pilot, ridge, and operator-replacement terms.

For completeness, consider the oracle first-order score obtained by replacing every opposite-fold pilot in the displayed score of \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\) by the corresponding true density.  Differentiating the one-dimensional inverse-cdf representation of conditional squared Wasserstein distance and centering the resulting conditional Kantorovich potentials gives the first Hoeffding projection in (1).  The uniform bounds \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\), the common marginal in \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), and the weak energy identity for \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) identify its squared \(L^2(P_{0,n})\oplus L^2(P_{1,n})\) norm, up to constants depending only on \(c\) and \(C\), with
\[
\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}
\Delta_n(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\}(y),dy,dR_n(z)
=\rho_n^2.                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The two folds have size \(m=n/2\), and the two arm samples are independent by \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\).  Consequently the variance of their equal-weight average satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\frac{\rho_n^2}{n}.       \tag{3}
\]
If \(\Delta_n=0\), both the conditional transport value and its centered first derivative vanish, so \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\).

For the quadratic term, let \(Z_{a,i}=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n})-E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n})\).  Within a fixed evaluation fold its oracle canonical component is the sum of
\[
\frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i\ne j}Z_{a,i}^{\top}H_{J_y,J_z,n}Z_{a,j}
\quad\text{and}\quad
-\frac{2}{m^2}\sum_{i,j}Z_{1,i}^{\top}H_{J_y,J_z,n}Z_{0,j}.              \tag{4}
\]
Independence and centering make every covariance between summands in (4) vanish unless the two ordered pairs coincide or are reversed.  Writing \(H=H_{J_y,J_z,n}\) and \(\Gamma_a=\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\), direct counting therefore gives, after averaging the two independent evaluation folds,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{(H\Gamma_0)^2\}+\operatorname{tr}\{(H\Gamma_1)^2\}}{m(m-1)}
+\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(H\Gamma_0H\Gamma_1)}{m^2}\\
&=\frac{4\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\{\Gamma^{1/2}H\Gamma^{1/2}\}^2\right]}{n^2}\{1+O(m^{-1})\}\\
&=\frac{4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+O(m^{-1})\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.
\end{aligned}                                                            \tag{5}
\]
where \(\Gamma=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1\), \(n=2m\), and the second equality uses the definition of \(K_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  The sole finite-sample discrepancy is the factor \(m/(m-1)\) on the two within-arm contractions.  Thus the standard deviation at the asymptotic expansion scale is \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\), as stated.  Compact wavelet support and the fixed density bounds bound the remaining fourth-moment contraction by a constant times \(D_{J_y,J_z}/n^2\), which is \(o(1)\) under \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\); hence the remainder in (5) is uniform over the declared model class.

By \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\),
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.                                      \tag{6}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) and \(\lambda_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\), the remaining term in (1) obeys
\[
R_{J_y,J_z,n}
=O_P\!\left\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\right\}
+o_P(1)\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.    \tag{7}
\]
The hypothesis of the theorem says that the first brace in (7) is little-oh of
\[
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}.                                         \tag{8}
\]
The second term in (7) is also little-oh of (8).  Substitution of (6)--(7) into the exact identity (1), together with (3) and (5), proves the asserted uniform second-order expansion, both fluctuation scales, and the equality degeneracy.

Finally, at the resolutions of \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\), that definition and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) yield
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp2^{-2s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z).                                                      \tag{9}
\]
Minkowski's inequality applied to (1), with (3), (5), (6), (7), and (9), gives the root-mean-square scale
\[
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+r_{n,d}(s_z)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
When the final two terms are no larger than the first two, this reduces to \(\rho_n/\sqrt n+r_{n,d}(s_z)\).  Equations (1)--(9) use the population covariance only to describe the fluctuation scale; no covariance estimate enters the point estimator or its expansion.  The separate condition for replacing the population spectral proxy by an observed one is therefore unnecessary here.
