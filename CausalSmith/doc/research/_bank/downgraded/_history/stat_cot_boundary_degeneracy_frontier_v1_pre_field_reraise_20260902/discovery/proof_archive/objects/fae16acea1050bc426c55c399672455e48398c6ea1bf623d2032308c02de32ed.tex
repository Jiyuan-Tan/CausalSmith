Write \(\Phi=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\), \(G=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(H=H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\). The tensor wavelets are Lebesgue-orthonormal by \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\). Hence, for every coefficient vector \(x\),
\[
x^\top Gx=\int(x^\top\Phi)^2r_n\,dy\,dz,
\qquad c\|x\|_2^2\le x^\top Gx\le C\|x\|_2^2,                 \tag{1}
\]
where the inequalities use \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\). Outcome orthogonality also gives \(G=I_y\otimes G_z\), so \(G^{-1}\) preserves each outcome level.

For \(g=x^\top\Phi\), put \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\). Every retained outcome wavelet has zero outcome integral. The weak Neumann equation and its boundary conditions therefore give
\[
\int_0^1g u_z\,dy=\int_0^1\bar q_n|u_z'|^2\,dy.                    \tag{2}
\]
For zero-mean \(v\in H^1(0,1)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz imply \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\). The variational characterization of (2), together with \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), thus yields
\[
\int_0^1g\mathcal G_{n,z}g\,dy\asymp\|g(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}^2. \tag{3}
\]
The antiderivative of a normalized boundary wavelet at level \(j_y\) has squared \(L^2\)-norm comparable to \(2^{-2j_y}\). Compact support gives a uniformly bounded overlap number, including the finite boundary block. Applying (3) levelwise and integrating in \(z\) proves
\[
x^\top Hx\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and preservation of outcome levels give the same comparison for the Gram-conjugated raw-moment Hessian:
\[
x^\top Bx=(G^{-1}x)^\top H(G^{-1}x)
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2.          \tag{5}
\]

Let \(f=x^\top\Phi\). For each arm the covariance identity is
\[
x^\top\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int(f-v)^2q_{a,n}r_n\,dy\,dz.                \tag{6}
\]
Because \(\int f(y,z)dy=0\) for every \(z\), Lebesgue orthogonality gives \(\int(f-v)^2dy\,dz=\|x\|_2^2+v^2\). Applying both density bounds in (6), and then summing the arms, proves \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma\preceq C_\Gamma I\).

Let \(w_\lambda=2^{-2j_y(\lambda)}\) for \(\lambda\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}\), ordered decreasingly. Congruence by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\) and (5) bound the Rayleigh quotient of \(K\) above and below by fixed multiples of the diagonal reference quotient. To obtain the \(k\)-th lower bound, take the span of the \(k\) reference coordinates with largest weights. If \(K\) had fewer than \(k\) eigenvalues above \(\underline c w_k^\downarrow\), that span would intersect the spectral subspace of \(K\) below this threshold, contradicting the lower quadratic-form bound. Applying the same dimension argument to the orthogonal complement of the first \(k-1\) reference coordinates gives the upper bound. Thus
\[
\underline c\,w_k^\downarrow\le\nu_k\le\overline C\,w_k^\downarrow,
\qquad 1\le k\le D_{J_y,J_z}.                                      \tag{7}
\]
Taking superlevel sets in (7) gives the asserted constant-rescaled counting inequalities for every \(u>0\); this avoids the false literal endpoint comparison at a threshold above the largest eigenvalue. There are \(\asymp2^{j_y+dJ_z}\) indices at outcome level \(j_y\). Thus, on the stated interior threshold range,
\[
N(u)\asymp2^{dJ_z}\sum_{j_0\le j_y\le\min\{J_y,\frac12\log_2(u^{-1})+O(1)\}}2^{j_y}
\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}.                       \tag{8}
\]
Inversion of (7)--(8), including the two endpoint blocks directly, gives \(\nu_k\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}\). Hence
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{D}\nu_k^2
\asymp2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}\asymp2^{dJ_z}.      \tag{9}
\]
The operator, Hilbert--Schmidt, and diffuse-spectrum conclusions follow. For \(d=0\), (9) has multiplicity one and converges to a finite positive constant.

Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) and the wavelet vanishing moments give, with the usual omission of the \(z\)-factor for a coarse covariate index,
\[
|\langle\Delta_n,\psi^y_{j_y,k_y}\psi^z_{j_z,k_z}\rangle_{dy\,dR_n}|
\lesssim2^{-j_y(s_y+1/2)}2^{-j_z(s_z+d/2)}.                          \tag{10}
\]
The coercive Gram blocks in (1) preserve this order in projection coordinates. The first derivative vanishes at equality by \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\); consequently the projection error is the inverse-elliptic quadratic tail. Squaring (10), multiplying by the level multiplicities, inserting the outcome weight \(2^{-2j_y}\) from (4), and summing the two geometric tails yields
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad |B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.
\]
Every constant used above depends only on the fixed support, density, radius, and wavelet constants, so the bounds are uniform over the declared model class.
