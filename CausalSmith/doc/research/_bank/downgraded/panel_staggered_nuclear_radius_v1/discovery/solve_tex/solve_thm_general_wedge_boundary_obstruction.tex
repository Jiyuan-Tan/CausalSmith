%%% FIELD boundary-frontier-statement
Assume the balanced rank-one one-block L-mask conditions, \(\mu=1\), \(0<\kappa\le 1\), and \(0<s_-\le s_+\).  Then both normalized overlap Grams in the unique balanced chart equal \(1\), so the value of \(\kappa\) does not further restrict this boundary class.  For all sufficiently large even \(n\), the minimax expected honest radius has the following complete boundary characterization.
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
s_-<s_+:& c_\alpha\sigma\le \rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}\le C_\alpha\sigma,
\quad\text{hence }\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}\asymp\sigma,\\[3pt]
s_-=s_+:& \rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}=0,
\end{array}
\]
where \(0<c_\alpha<C_\alpha<\infty\) depend only on \(\alpha\).  The first upper bound and the zero-radius result are attained by deterministic \(O(n^2)\)-time procedures in \(\mathcal H_{\alpha,n}\); in the second line the common factor level is known because the parameter-space constants coincide.  In both cases
\[
\mathfrak R_n\ge \sigma\sqrt{2n}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}}{\mathfrak R_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently, no positive constant can make an all-procedure inequality \(\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}\ge c\mathfrak R_n\) hold over every parameter choice and wedge allowed by the model.  The obstruction is that at \(\mu=1\) the admissible rank-one singular vectors have only coordinatewise sign choices, whereas the unrestricted rank-manifold tangent space still contains continuous directions.
%%% FIELD boundary-frontier-proof
Put \(n=2m\).  By the balanced-dimension, even-benchmark, balanced-L-mask, and one-chart assumptions, the unique chart has
\[
C=P=\{1,\ldots,m\},\qquad I=Q=\{m+1,\ldots,2m\},
\]
and
\[
W_n=I\times Q,qquad
\Omega_n=(C\times P)\mathbin{\sqcup}(I\times P)
\mathbin{\sqcup}(C\times Q),qquad |\Omega_n|=3m^2.       \tag{1}
\]

We first identify the entire \(\mu=1\) boundary class.  The rank-one and strong-factor assumptions give, for every \(\vartheta_n=(L_n,D_n)\in\Theta_n\), a singular-value decomposition
\[
L_n=s_nu_nv_n^{\top},\qquad
\lVert u_n\rVert_2=\lVert v_n\rVert_2=1,qquad
s_-n\le s_n\le s_+n.                                      \tag{2}
\]
At \(\mu=1\), incoherence says
\[
|u_{n,i}|^2\le n^{-1}\quad(1\le i\le n),
\qquad |v_{n,t}|^2\le n^{-1}\quad(1\le t\le n).          \tag{3}
\]
The \(n\) nonnegative quantities on each side of (3) sum to one.  Thus every inequality in (3) is an equality.  Hence there are signs \(p_i,q_t\in\{-1,1\}\) and a level
\(\ell_n=s_n/n\in[s_-,s_+]\) such that
\[
u_{n,i}=\frac{p_i}{\sqrt n},\qquad
v_{n,t}=\frac{q_t}{\sqrt n},\qquad
L_{n,it}=\ell_np_iq_t.                                    \tag{4}
\]
The normalized row and column Grams in the unique balanced chart are well defined because \(|C|=|P|=m>0\), and (4) yields
\[
\frac{n}{|C|}u_{n,C}^{\top}u_{n,C}
=\frac{n}{m}\sum_{i\in C}\frac1n=1,
\qquad
\frac{n}{|P|}v_{n,P}^{\top}v_{n,P}
=\frac{n}{m}\sum_{t\in P}\frac1n=1.                     \tag{5}
\]
Thus every boundary matrix satisfies the overlap-Gram restriction for every \(0<\kappa\le1\), not merely for \(\kappa=1\).

We next preserve the lower bound for the geometric modulus.  The class is nonempty: take \(p_i=q_t=1\), \(\ell_n=s_-\), and \(D_n=0\).  Equations (2)--(5), together with treatment support, verify membership in \(\Theta_n\).  For this matrix let \(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)^{\top}\), and take any vector \(h\in\mathbb R^n\) supported on \(I\).  Since \(v_n=q/\sqrt n\),
\[
\Delta=hq^{\top}=(\sqrt n\,h)v_n^{\top}\in\mathcal T_n(L_n)          \tag{6}
\]
by the definition of the tangent space.  The decomposition (1) and the support of \(h\) give
\[
\lVert P_{\Omega_n}\Delta\rVert_F
=\lVert h_Iq_P^{\top}\rVert_F
=\sqrt m\,\lVert h\rVert_2,
\qquad
\lVert P_{W_n}\Delta\rVert_*
=\lVert h_Iq_Q^{\top}\rVert_*
=\sqrt m\,\lVert h\rVert_2.                              \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the only nonzero singular value of a rank-one matrix \(ab^{\top}\) is
\(\lVert a\rVert_2\lVert b\rVert_2\), which proves the nuclear-norm equality in (7).  Choose \(h\) so that \(\sqrt m\lVert h\rVert_2=\sigma\sqrt{2n}\).  Since \(r=1\) and \(N_n=T_n=n\), this \(\Delta\) is feasible in the definition of \(\omega_n(L_n,\Omega_n)\).  Therefore
\[
\mathfrak R_n\ge \omega_n(L_n,\Omega_n)
\ge\sigma\sqrt{2n}.                                      \tag{8}
\]

We now construct a common relative-sign pilot.  Fix \(i_0\in C\), \(t_0\in P\), set \(\operatorname{sign}(0)=1\), and define from observed cells only
\[
\widehat p_i=\operatorname{sign}\!\left(\sum_{t\in P}X_{n,it}X_{n,i_0t}\right),
\qquad
\widehat q_t=\operatorname{sign}\!\left(\sum_{i\in C}X_{n,it}X_{n,it_0}\right).       \tag{9}
\]
All cells used in (9) lie in \(\Omega_n\) by (1).  Treatment support and the observed-experiment definition therefore give \(X_{n,it}=L_{n,it}+E_{n,it}\) at each such cell.  For \(i\ne i_0\), multiplication of the row score in (9) by \(p_ip_{i_0}\), followed by sign invariance of centered Gaussian errors, gives the distributional identity
\[
S_i=\sum_{t=1}^{m}(\ell_n+\varepsilon_t)(\ell_n+\delta_t)
=m\ell_n^2+G_i+H_i,                                      \tag{10}
\]
where the pairs \((\varepsilon_t,\delta_t)\) are independent over \(t\), their two coordinates are independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) variables, and
\[
G_i=\ell_n\sum_{t=1}^{m}(\varepsilon_t+\delta_t)
\sim\mathcal N(0,2m\ell_n^2\sigma^2),
\qquad H_i=\sum_{t=1}^{m}\varepsilon_t\delta_t.           \tag{11}
\]
For \(G\sim\mathcal N(0,v)\), exponential Markov inequality and minimization over \(a>0\) give
\[
\Pr(G\le-x)\le\inf_{a>0}\exp\{-ax+a^2v/2\}
=\exp\{-x^2/(2v)\}.                                     \tag{12}
\]
Applying (12) to (11) yields
\[
\Pr\!\left(G_i\le-\frac{m\ell_n^2}{2}\right)
\le\exp\!\left\{-\frac{m\ell_n^2}{16\sigma^2}\right\}.             \tag{13}
\]
For \(0<a<\sigma^{-2}\), conditioning on one Gaussian and integrating the other gives
\[
\mathbb E\exp(-a\varepsilon_t\delta_t)
=(1-\sigma^4a^2)^{-1/2}.                                 \tag{14}
\]
If \(0<a\le(2\sigma^2)^{-1}\), then
\(-\tfrac12\log(1-\sigma^4a^2)\le\sigma^4a^2\).  Independence, (14), and exponential Markov inequality imply
\[
\Pr\!\left(H_i\le-\frac{m\ell_n^2}{2}\right)
\le\exp\!\left\{-\frac{ma\ell_n^2}{2}+m\sigma^4a^2\right\}.         \tag{15}
\]
Taking \(a=\min\{\ell_n^2/(4\sigma^4),(2\sigma^2)^{-1}\}\), and using \(\ell_n\ge s_->0\), gives
\[
\Pr\!\left(H_i\le-\frac{m\ell_n^2}{2}\right)\le e^{-mc_2},
\quad
c_2=\min\!\left\{\frac{s_-^4}{16\sigma^4},\frac14\right\}>0.       \tag{16}
\]
If \(S_i\le0\), either event in (13) and (16) occurs.  Thus, with
\[
c_1=\frac{s_-^2}{16\sigma^2},
\qquad c_\star=\min\{c_1,c_2\}>0,
\]
we have
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb Q_{\vartheta,n}}\{\widehat p_i\ne p_ip_{i_0}\}
\le e^{-mc_1}+e^{-mc_2}\quad(i\ne i_0).                  \tag{17}
\]
For \(i=i_0\), the row score is a sum of squares and is positive almost surely.  The identical argument with rows and columns interchanged proves (17) for \(\widehat q_t\ne q_tq_{t_0}\), while the reference-column score is again positive almost surely.  Consequently the event
\[
\mathcal S_n=
\{\widehat p_i=p_ip_{i_0}\ \text{for all }i,
\ \widehat q_t=q_tq_{t_0}\ \text{for all }t\}
\]
satisfies the uniform bound
\[
\sup_{\vartheta_n\in\Theta_n}
\mathbb Q_{\vartheta,n}(\mathcal S_n^c)
\le2(n-1)(e^{-mc_1}+e^{-mc_2})
\le4ne^{-mc_\star}.                                      \tag{18}
\]
No independence among the comparisons is used in this union bound.

Suppose first that \(s_-<s_+\).  Let \(\Phi\) denote the standard-normal distribution function and put \(z_\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/4)\).  Define
\[
\widehat\ell_n=\frac1{3m^2}\sum_{(i,t)\in\Omega_n}
X_{n,it}\widehat p_i\widehat q_t,
\qquad
\widehat L_{n,it}=\widehat\ell_n\widehat p_i\widehat q_t,
\qquad
R_n=\frac{z_\alpha}{\sqrt3}\sigma.                       \tag{19}
\]
On \(\mathcal S_n\), let \(c=p_{i_0}q_{t_0}\).  Then
\(\widehat p_i\widehat q_t=cp_iq_t\), and (4), (19), and the independent Gaussian-cell assumption give
\[
\widehat\ell_n=c(\ell_n+Z_n),
\qquad
Z_n=\frac1{3m^2}\sum_{(i,t)\in\Omega_n}p_iq_tE_{n,it}
\sim\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^2}{3m^2}\right).                \tag{20}
\]
It follows that on \(\mathcal S_n\),
\[
P_{W_n}(\widehat L_n-L_n)=Z_np_Iq_Q^{\top},
\qquad
\lVert P_{W_n}(\widehat L_n-L_n)\rVert_*=m|Z_n|.          \tag{21}
\]
By the definition of \(z_\alpha\),
\[
\mathbb Q_{\vartheta,n}\{m|Z_n|>R_n\}=\alpha/2.          \tag{22}
\]
For all sufficiently large even \(n\), the right side of (18) is at most \(\alpha/2\).  Combining (18), (21), and (22), without requiring independence between \(\mathcal S_n\) and \(Z_n\), proves
\[
\inf_{\vartheta_n\in\Theta_n}
\Pr_{\mathbb Q_{\vartheta,n},\Xi_n,\Zeta_n}
\{\lVert P_{W_n}(\widehat L_n-L_n)\rVert_*\le R_n\}
\ge1-\alpha.                                               \tag{23}
\]
Thus (19) belongs to \(\mathcal H_{\alpha,n}\).  Its two families of correlations, its signed average, and its outer-product completion require \(O(n^2)\) arithmetic operations.  Since \(R_n\) is deterministic, the minimax definition gives
\[
\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}\le \frac{z_\alpha}{\sqrt3}\sigma.           \tag{24}
\]

For the matching all-procedure lower bound, retain the fixed signs \(p_i=q_t=1\).  Set
\[
a_\alpha=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\sqrt3}>0,
\qquad
\delta_n=\frac{a_\alpha\sigma}{m},
\qquad
\ell_0=s_-,\qquad \ell_1=s_-+\delta_n.                  \tag{25}
\]
Because \(s_-<s_+\), one has \(\ell_1\le s_+\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Let
\(L^{(j)}=\ell_j\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^{\top}\) and \(D^{(j)}=0\) for \(j=0,1\).  Equations (2)--(5) and treatment support show that both tuples belong to \(\Theta_n\).  Their level separation is \(a_\alpha\sigma/m\), as required, and their observed Gaussian means differ by \(\delta_n\) in each of the \(3m^2\) coordinates of \(\Omega_n\).

Let \(f_0,f_1\) be the two observed Gaussian densities.  Completing the square coordinatewise gives their Hellinger affinity
\[
A:=\int\sqrt{f_0f_1}
=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lVert
\operatorname{vec}_{\Omega_n}(L^{(1)}-L^{(0)})\rVert_2^2}{8\sigma^2}\right\}
=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{3a_\alpha^2}{8}\right\}.           \tag{26}
\]
Writing total variation as one half of the \(L^1\) distance and applying Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathbb Q_0,\mathbb Q_1)
&=\frac12\int|\sqrt{f_0}-\sqrt{f_1}|(\sqrt{f_0}+\sqrt{f_1})\\
&\le\frac12\{(2-2A)(2+2A)\}^{1/2}
=\sqrt{1-A^2}\\
&\le\frac{\sqrt3a_\alpha}{2}
=\frac{1-2\alpha}{4}.                                    \tag{27}
\end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate inequality follows from \(1-e^{-x}\le x\) for \(x\ge0\).  Adjoining the common independent laws of \(\Xi_n\) and \(\Zeta_n\) leaves total variation unchanged, because integration over the common product factor multiplies the \(L^1\) distance by one.

Take any \((\widehat L_n,R_n)\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,n}\), and under the joint law associated with tuple \(j\) define
\[
\mathcal A_j=
\{\lVert P_{W_n}(\widehat L_n-L^{(j)})\rVert_*\le R_n\}.
\]
Uniform honesty gives \(\Pr_j(\mathcal A_j)\ge1-\alpha\).  By (27),
\[
\Pr_0(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1)
\ge1-2\alpha-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathbb Q_0,\mathbb Q_1)
\ge\frac34(1-2\alpha).                                   \tag{28}
\]
On this intersection, the triangle inequality for the nuclear norm gives
\[
2R_n\ge\lVert P_{W_n}(L^{(1)}-L^{(0)})\rVert_*
=\delta_n\lVert\mathbf1_I\mathbf1_Q^{\top}\rVert_*
=m\delta_n=a_\alpha\sigma.                               \tag{29}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sup_{\vartheta_n\in\Theta_n}
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_{\vartheta,n},\Xi_n,\Zeta_n}[R_n]
\ge\mathbb E_0R_n
\ge\frac{3a_\alpha(1-2\alpha)}8\sigma
=\frac{3(1-2\alpha)^2}{16\sqrt3}\sigma.                 \tag{30}
\]
Taking the infimum over all honest procedures in (30), and combining with (24), proves the asserted \(\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}\asymp\sigma\) characterization.  Notice that (25)--(30) use two fixed-sign matrices and vary only their positive factor level; the treatment-effect component is fixed at zero, and no causal treatment effect is identified by definition.

It remains to treat the elbow \(s_-=s_+\).  Write the known common value as \(s_0:=s_-=s_+>0\).  Use the same relative signs (9), and set
\[
\overline X_n^{\mathrm{sgn}}
=\frac1{3m^2}\sum_{(i,t)\in\Omega_n}X_{n,it}\widehat p_i\widehat q_t,
\qquad
\widehat c_n=\operatorname{sign}(\overline X_n^{\mathrm{sgn}}),
\]
\[
\widehat L_{n,it}=s_0\widehat c_n\widehat p_i\widehat q_t,
\qquad R_n=0.                                              \tag{31}
\]
On \(\mathcal S_n\), equation (20) becomes
\[
\overline X_n^{\mathrm{sgn}}=c(s_0+Z_n),
\qquad Z_n\sim\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^2}{3m^2}\right).     \tag{32}
\]
Thus \(\widehat c_n=c\) whenever \(\mathcal S_n\) occurs and \(Z_n>-s_0\), and then (31) equals \(L_n\) at every cell.  Applying the elementary Gaussian bound (12) to (32),
\[
\sup_{\vartheta_n\in\Theta_n}
\Pr\{P_{W_n}\widehat L_n\ne P_{W_n}L_n\}
\le4ne^{-mc_\star}
+\exp\!\left\{-\frac{3m^2s_0^2}{2\sigma^2}\right\}.     \tag{33}
\]
The right side is at most \(\alpha\) for all sufficiently large even \(n\).  Hence the deterministic \(O(n^2)\)-time pair (31) lies in \(\mathcal H_{\alpha,n}\).  Its radius is identically zero.  Since every admissible radius is nonnegative by definition,
\[
0\le\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}\le0,                         \tag{34}
\]
which proves \(\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}=0\).

Finally, (8) and (24) give in the nondegenerate case
\[
0\le\frac{\rho_{\alpha,n}^{\star}}{\mathfrak R_n}
\le\frac{z_\alpha}{\sqrt{6n}}\longrightarrow0,           \tag{35}
\]
while the ratio is identically zero in the known-level case by (34).  This proves the universal lower-comparison obstruction.  Equations (4) and (6) also isolate its source: the active incoherence boundary makes admissible singular-vector magnitudes fixed, but the tangent modulus still optimizes over directions that do not remain in that boundary class.
