\textit{Step 1: the compatible block submodel.}
Let \(U_i\) be independent and identically distributed uniform variables on \([0,1]\), use the cells, functions, and kernel displayed in the statement, and write \(T_0\) for the integral operator with kernel \(W_0\).  The intervals form a partition up to null endpoints.  Hence, for all \(1\leq k,\ell\leq r\),
\[
 \int_0^1\psi_k(u)\psi_\ell(u)\,du
 =r\,|I_k\cap I_\ell|=\mathbf 1\{k=\ell\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 (T_0\psi_k)(u)
 =c_0\mathbf 1\{u\in I_k\}\int_{I_k}\sqrt r\,dv
 =\frac{c_0}{r}\psi_k(u).
\tag{2}
\]
The ranges of the \(r\) functions in (1) span the range of \(T_0\), so (2) proves that \(W_0\) has exact rank \(r\) and that every nonzero eigenvalue equals \(c_0/r\).  By the definition of \(c_0\),
\[
 \frac{c_0}{r}\geq\underline\lambda,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1W_0(u,v)\,dv=\frac{c_0}{r}\geq\kappa^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
The local condition \(c_0\leq\kappa\) gives \(0\leq W_0\leq\kappa\).  Together with \({\rm ass:edge-probability}\), it also gives
\[
 0\leq\rho_nW_0(u,v)\leq\rho_n\kappa\leq1,
\tag{4}
\]
so all Bernoulli edge probabilities are valid.

For completeness, write out the Bernstein condition used in \({\rm ass:eigenfunction-bernstein}\): for a centered variable \(X\), it is
\[
 \mathbb E|X|^q\leq\frac{q!}{2}\mathbb E[X^2]b_\psi^{q-2},
 \qquad q\geq2.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(\mathbb E\psi_k(U_i)=r^{-1/2}\).  If \(r\geq2\), then
\[
 \left|\psi_k(U_i)-r^{-1/2}\right|
 \leq\frac{r-1}{\sqrt r}\leq b_\psi.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, raising the bound in (6) to the power \(q-2\), multiplying by the square, and taking expectations gives
\[
 \mathbb E\left|\psi_k(U_i)-r^{-1/2}\right|^q
 \leq b_\psi^{q-2}\mathbb E\left[\left\{\psi_k(U_i)-r^{-1/2}\right\}^2\right]
 \leq\frac{q!}{2}b_\psi^{q-2}\mathbb E\left[\left\{\psi_k(U_i)-r^{-1/2}\right\}^2\right].
\tag{7}
\]
For \(r=1\) the centered variable is zero, so (5) is immediate.  Equations (1)--(7) verify orthonormality, exact rank, spectral strength, kernel bounds, row-mass regularity, the Bernstein moment condition, and edge-probability feasibility.  The independent latent types, the graph drawn conditionally according to \(W_0\), and the independent Bernoulli assignment then satisfy \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\), \({\rm ass:graphon-draw}\), and \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\).

Set \(s_0=B/4\) and let the independent intercepts \(\xi_i\), independent of all graph and assignment variables, have density
\[
p_{s_0}(t)=s_0^{-1}\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2s_0}\right)\mathbf 1\{|t|\leq s_0\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}p_{s_0}(t)\,dt
 =s_0^{-1}\int_{-s_0}^{s_0}\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2s_0}\right)dt=1.
\]
For any deterministic \(g\in C^3[0,1]\), define the two response laws by
\[
 f_i^\pm(a,x)=\xi_i\pm g(x),
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\},\quad x\in[0,1].
\tag{9}
\]
Every construction below chooses \(g\) so that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \|g\|_\infty\leq3B/4,
 \quad \|g'\|_\infty\leq B,
 \quad \|g''\|_\infty\leq B,
 \quad \|g'''\|_\infty\leq L.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(|\xi_i|\leq B/4\), (10) verifies \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) and \({\rm ass:third-derivative-bound}\); (9) verifies anonymous interference when inserted in \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\).  Thus, after (10) is checked separately for each \(g\), the induced laws are members of \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\), rather than merely oracle experiments outside the model.

Under (9), the target in \({\rm def:target}\) is nonrandom and equals
\[
 \theta_n^\pm=\pm g'(1/2),
 \qquad
 \Delta_g:=|\theta_n^+-\theta_n^-|=2|g'(1/2)|.
\tag{11}
\]

\textit{Step 2: a common experiment-distance inequality.}
Let \(q_{s_0}=\sqrt{p_{s_0}}\).  The function \(q_{s_0}(t)=s_0^{-1/2}\cos(\pi t/(2s_0))\mathbf 1\{|t|\leq s_0\}\) is absolutely continuous after extension by zero, because it vanishes at both endpoints.  Its weak derivative satisfies
\[
 \|q_{s_0}'\|_2^2
 =\frac1{s_0}\left(\frac{\pi}{2s_0}\right)^2
 \int_{-s_0}^{s_0}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2s_0}\right)dt
 =\frac{\pi^2}{4s_0^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The fundamental theorem of calculus followed by Minkowski's integral inequality therefore yields, for every \(\eta_1,\eta_2\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 H^2\{p_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_1),p_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_2)\}
 =\|q_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_1)-q_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_2)\|_2^2
 \leq(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2\|q_{s_0}'\|_2^2
 =\frac{\pi^2(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2}{4s_0^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
We use the convention in (13) for squared Hellinger distance.  Conditional on \((A,Z)\), the responses are independent translated intercepts.  If \(Q_g^+\) and \(Q_g^-\) denote the two observed-data laws, the product-affinity identity and \(1-\prod_i(1-x_i)\leq\sum_i x_i\) give, from (13) with translations differing by \(2g(X_i)\),
\[
 H^2(Q_g^+,Q_g^-)
 \leq\frac{\pi^2}{s_0^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^ng(X_i)^2,
 \qquad
 X_i=\begin{cases}1/2+V_i,&N_i>0,\\0,&N_i=0.\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
The outer expectation in (14) is valid because the law of \((A,Z)\) is identical in the two experiments.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_g^+,Q_g^-)
 \leq H(Q_g^+,Q_g^-).
\tag{15}
\]

\textit{Step 3: the linear graph-floor pair.}
Put \(\bar c=c_0/r\).  Conditional on \(U_i\), each other unit belongs to the same block with probability \(1/r\), independently, and the edge is then present with probability \(\rho_nc_0\).  Thus
\[
 N_i\sim{\rm Binomial}(n-1,p_n),
 \qquad p_n=\frac{c_0d}{rn}=\frac{\bar c d}{n},
 \qquad \mu_n:=(n-1)p_n\asymp d.
\tag{16}
\]
Conditional on \(N_i=N>0\), Bernoulli randomization gives \(M_i\sim{\rm Binomial}(N,1/2)\), whence
\[
 \mathbb E_Z[V_i^2\mid A,N_i=N]=\frac1{4N}.
\tag{17}
\]
For a binomial variable with \(n-1\) trials,
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{N_i+1}
 =\frac{1-(1-p_n)^n}{np_n}\leq\frac1{np_n}.
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(N^{-1}\leq2(N+1)^{-1}\) for \(N\geq1\), (17)--(18) imply
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^nV_i^2\mathbf 1\{N_i>0\}
 =\frac n4\mathbb E\{N_i^{-1}\mathbf 1(N_i>0)\}
 \leq\frac{n}{2np_n}=O(n/d).
\tag{19}
\]
Also,
\[
 n\Pr(N_i=0)=n(1-p_n)^{n-1}
 \leq n e^{-\mu_n}=O(ne^{-c d})
\tag{20}
\]
for a constant \(c>0\) depending only on the fixed block parameters.

Take
\[
 g_G(x)=c_GB\sqrt{d/n}\,(x-1/2).
\tag{21}
\]
Because \(d/n\to0\), choosing \(c_G\leq1\) verifies all four inequalities in (10) for large \(n\).  By (19)--(21),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\sum_i g_G(X_i)^2
 &=c_G^2B^2\frac dn
 \left\{\mathbb E\sum_iV_i^2\mathbf 1(N_i>0)
       +\frac n4\Pr(N_i=0)\right\}\\
 &\leq Cc_G^2B^2\{1+d e^{-cd}\}\leq C'c_G^2B^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(s_0=B/4\), (14)--(15) and (22) show that \(c_G>0\) may be chosen, independently of \(n,d\), so that
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_G^+,Q_G^-)\leq\eta,
 \qquad \eta:=(1-2\alpha)/2>0.
\tag{23}
\]
By (11) and (21), this pair has separation
\[
 \Delta_G=2c_GB\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{24}
\]

\textit{Step 4: the interior nonlinear bump.}
Fix the explicit odd function
\[
 \phi(t)=t(1-4t^2)^4\mathbf 1\{|t|<1/2\}.
\tag{25}
\]
Its derivatives through order three match zero at \(\{-1/2,1/2\}\), so \(\phi\in C^3(\mathbb R)\), its support lies strictly inside \((-1,1)\), and \(\phi'(0)=1\).  For \(0\leq j_0\leq3\), let \(C_{j_0}=\|\phi^{(j_0)}\|_\infty<\infty\).  For \(d^{-1}\leq h\leq d^{-1/2}\), set
\[
 g_h(x)=c_ILh^3\phi\!\left(\frac{x-1/2}{h}\right).
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
 \|g_h^{(j_0)}\|_\infty=c_ILh^{3-j_0}C_{j_0},
 \qquad 0\leq j_0\leq3.
\tag{27}
\]
Choose \(c_I C_3\leq1\).  Since \(d\geq C_{\log}\log n\) implies \(h\leq d^{-1/2}\to0\), (27) verifies (10) for all sufficiently large \(n\), with fixed \(B,L\).

We next prove the local binomial-mass bound used in (14).  For \(N\geq1\), the largest atom of \({\rm Binomial}(N,1/2)\) is at most \(C/\sqrt N\).  Indeed, for \(N=2q_0\), if \(a_{q_0}={2q_0\choose q_0}4^{-q_0}\), then \(a_0=1\),
\[
 \frac{a_{q_0+1}}{a_{q_0}}=\frac{2q_0+1}{2q_0+2},
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{2q_0+1}{2q_0+2}\right)^2\leq\frac{q_0+1}{q_0+2},
\tag{28}
\]
so induction gives \(a_{q_0}\leq(q_0+1)^{-1/2}\); the odd case is bounded by the preceding even case.  The interval \(|M/N-1/2|\leq h\) contains at most \(2Nh+2\) integers.  Therefore
\[
 \Pr\{|M/N-1/2|\leq h\mid N\}
 \leq C(\sqrt N h+N^{-1/2}).
\tag{29}
\]
For \(N_i\) in (16), exponential Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN_i}\) with \(t=\log2\) gives \(\Pr(N_i<\mu_n/2)\leq e^{-\mu_n/8}\).  Jensen's inequality and a split at \(\mu_n/2\) then give
\[
 \mathbb E\sqrt{N_i}\leq\sqrt{\mu_n},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{N_i^{-1/2}\mathbf 1(N_i>0)\}
 \leq\sqrt{2/\mu_n}+e^{-\mu_n/8}.
\tag{30}
\]
Combining (29)--(30), using \(\mu_n\asymp d\) and \(h\geq d^{-1}\), yields the uniform bound
\[
 \Pr(|V_i|\leq h,N_i>0)\leq C\sqrt d\,h.
\tag{31}
\]
The bump is zero for isolates and outside the event in (31).  Hence (14), (26), and (31) imply
\[
 H^2(Q_h^+,Q_h^-)
 \leq Cc_I^2n\sqrt d\,L^2h^7.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(L>0\), define the proof-intermediate bandwidth
\[
 h_0=\left(\frac{L^{-2}}{n\sqrt d}\right)^{1/7}.
\tag{33}
\]
When \(d^{-1}<h_0<d^{-1/2}\), substituting \(h=h_0\) in (32) leaves the right-hand side bounded by \(Cc_I^2\).  Decreasing \(c_I\), if necessary, makes the total variation at most \(\eta\).  Equations (11), (26), and (33) give
\[
 \Delta_I=2c_ILh_0^2
 =2c_IL^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{34}
\]

\textit{Step 5: the invisible degree-lattice bump.}
Let \(D_n=\lceil Kd\rceil\), where \(K>e\bar c\) is fixed sufficiently large.  For \(t>0\), exponential Markov's inequality and \(1+y\leq e^y\) give
\[
 \Pr(N_i>D_n)
 \leq e^{-tD_n}(1-p_n+p_ne^t)^{n-1}
 \leq\exp\{-tD_n+\mu_n(e^t-1)\}.
\]
Taking \(t=\log(D_n/\mu_n)\) when \(D_n\geq\mu_n\) yields
\[
 \Pr(N_i>D_n)\leq\left(\frac{e\mu_n}{D_n}\right)^{D_n}
 \leq e^{-a_Kd}.
\tag{35}
\]
Choose \(K\) so large that \(a_KC_{\log}>2\).  The union bound and \({\rm ass:log-degree}\) give
\[
 \Pr\left(\max_iN_i>D_n\right)
 \leq ne^{-a_Kd}=o(1).
\tag{36}
\]
Put \(w_n=(4D_n)^{-1}\) and
\[
 g_D(x)=c_D Lw_n^3\phi\!\left(\frac{x-1/2}{w_n}\right),
\tag{37}
\]
where \(c_DC_3\leq1\).  The analogue of (27), together with \(D_n\to\infty\), verifies (10) for large \(n\).  If \(1\leq N_i\leq D_n\), every noncentral point of the exposure lattice has
\[
 |V_i|\geq\begin{cases}N_i^{-1},&N_i\text{ even},\\(2N_i)^{-1},&N_i\text{ odd},\end{cases}
 \geq(2D_n)^{-1}>w_n.
\tag{38}
\]
At the central point \(V_i=0\), oddness gives \(g_D(1/2)=0\); for an isolate, \(g_D(0)=0\).  Thus on \(E_n=\{\max_iN_i\leq D_n\}\), every observed response equals \(\xi_i\) under both signs.  The restrictions of the two observed laws to \(E_n\) are identical, and (36) consequently implies
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_D^+,Q_D^-)
 \leq\Pr(E_n^c)=o(1).
\tag{39}
\]
On the other hand, (11) and (37) give
\[
 \Delta_D=2c_D Lw_n^2\asymp Ld^{-2}.
\tag{40}
\]
This proves invisibility from the actual lattice, rather than putting that property into the construction by definition.

\textit{Step 6: risk and honest-length reductions.}
Consider any one of the three pairs above and abbreviate its targets by \(\theta^+,\theta^-\), its observed laws by \(Q^+,Q^-\), and its separation by \(\Delta\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), let \(A_T=\{|T-\theta^+|\geq\Delta/2\}\).  On \(A_T^c\), the triangle inequality gives \(|T-\theta^-|\geq\Delta/2\).  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q^+}|T-\theta^+|+\mathbb E_{Q^-}|T-\theta^-|
 &\geq\frac\Delta2\{Q^+(A_T)+Q^-(A_T^c)\}\\
 &\geq\frac\Delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
Thus the larger of the two risks is at least \(\Delta\{1-\operatorname{TV}\}/4\).

Now let \(\mathcal C\) be any confidence interval having coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) under every member of the class.  Total variation and the union bound imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q^+\{\theta^+,\theta^-\in\mathcal C\}
 &\geq Q^+\{\theta^+\in\mathcal C\}
       +Q^+\{\theta^-\in\mathcal C\}-1\\
 &\geq(1-\alpha)+(1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-))-1\\
 &=1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
An interval containing both target values has length at least \(\Delta\), so
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q^+}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C)
 \geq\Delta\{1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-)\}.
\tag{43}
\]
By (23), (32)--(34), and (39), all pairwise total variations are eventually at most \(\eta=(1-2\alpha)/2\).  Equations (41) and (43), followed by the infima in \({\rm def:minimax-criteria}\), therefore yield the lower orders in (24), (34), and (40) for both \(R^*_{n,d,L}\) and \(\Lambda^*_{n,d,L}(\alpha)\).

\textit{Step 7: comparison with the bandwidth envelope.}
Write \(a_n=(n\sqrt d)^{-1/2}\).  For \(L>0\), (33) gives
\[
 Lh_0^2=a_nh_0^{-3/2}=L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{44}
\]
If \(h_0\leq d^{-1}\), evaluate the infimum in \({\rm def:frontier-envelope}\) at \(h=d^{-1}\).  The condition \(h_0\leq d^{-1}\) is equivalent to \(L^2\geq d^{13/2}/n\), and hence
\[
 a_nd^{3/2}=d^{5/4}n^{-1/2}\leq Ld^{-2},
 \qquad
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}\leq2Ld^{-2}.
\tag{45}
\]
If \(d^{-1}<h_0<d^{-1/2}\), evaluation at \(h_0\), using (44), gives
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{46}
\]
If \(h_0\geq d^{-1/2}\), then \(L^2\leq d^3/n\).  Evaluation at \(h=d^{-1/2}\) gives
\[
 Ld^{-1}\leq\sqrt{d/n},
 \qquad
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{47}
\]
For \(L=0\), the infimum is attained at \(d^{-1/2}\) and equals \(\sqrt{d/n}\).  In the first regime, combine the graph pair (24) and lattice pair (40); in the second, combine (24) and (34); and in the third and in the case \(L=0\), use (24).  Since \(\max\{x,y\}\geq(x+y)/2\), (45)--(47) show that both minimax criteria are bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of
\[
 \sqrt{d/n}+\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}
 \left\{Lh^2+(n\sqrt d\,h^3)^{-1/2}\right\}
 =\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}.
\tag{48}
\]
All constants used to make the experiment distances small depend only on the fixed class constants and \(\alpha\), not on \(n,d\).  This completes the conditional block-submodel converse and does not extend it beyond the two sufficient compatibility inequalities in the statement.  The final assertion about the benchmark on every eventually nonempty repaired class follows separately from \({\rm thm:unrestricted-supported-converse}\), exactly as stated.
