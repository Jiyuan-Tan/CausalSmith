Fix \(A\), the preassignment response functions, and \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  All expectations and variances below are over the independent assignment coordinates in \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\).  Put
\[
 X=Z-\frac12\mathbf1_n,\qquad P_A=\widehat\Pi_r(A),\qquad C_A=P_AA,
 \qquad F_h(Z)=\widehat\theta_h.
\tag{1}
\]

We first prove the variance certificate.  If \(z^{(j)}\) is obtained from \(z\) by flipping coordinate \(j\), the finite maximum in \({\rm def:pc-handle}\) gives
\[
 |q_{i,h}(z)-q_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|\leq D_{ij,h}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the difference is zero when \(A_{ij}=0\); when \(A_{ij}=1\), the treated-neighbor count moves between two consecutive integers appearing in that maximum.  The normalizer is positive for \(N_i>0\) by \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\).  Since \(|X_j|=1/2\), the definitions of \(Q_{i,h}\), \(\Gamma_{i,h}\), and \(\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}\) give
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)|
 \leq Q_{i,h}+2\sum_{j'}|(C_A)_{ij'}|=\Gamma_{i,h},
\tag{3}
\]
and, because a flip changes \(X_j\) by one in absolute value,
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)-\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq D_{ij,h}+4|(C_A)_{ij}|.
\tag{4}
\]

The response-level part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) gives \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)|\leq B\).  If \(j=i\), a flip changes only the own-treatment argument, so the two response values differ by at most \(2B\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), then \(j\ne i\), the exposure fraction changes by \(N_i^{-1}\), and
\[
 |f_i(z_i,x+N_i^{-1})-f_i(z_i,x)|
 =\left|\int_x^{x+N_i^{-1}}\partial_t f_i(z_i,t)\,dt\right|
 \leq B/N_i
\]
by the derivative part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\).  Otherwise \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) makes the change zero.  With the convention in \({\rm def:pc-handle}\),
\[
 |Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)-Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq B\left\{2\mathbf1\{i=j\}+\frac{A_{ij}}{N_i}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Applying \(|gy-g'y'|\leq|g-g'||y|+|g'||y-y'|\) to (3)--(5) proves
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)
 -\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{6}
\]
Summation and \({\rm def:estimator}\) yield
\[
 |F_h(z)-F_h(z^{(j)})|
 \leq c_{j,h}:=n^{-1}\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathcal F_j=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_j)\), \(M_j=\mathbb E_Z(F_h\mid\mathcal F_j)\), and \(E_j=M_j-M_{j-1}\).  If \(j<k\), the tower property gives \(\mathbb E_Z(E_jE_k)=\mathbb E_Z[E_j\mathbb E_Z(E_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1})]=0\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(F_h)=\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E_ZE_j^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_{j-1}\), the difference between the two values of \(M_j\) obtained by setting \(Z_j=1\) and \(Z_j=0\) is an average over future coordinates of differences bounded by (7), hence is at most \(c_{j,h}\).  A two-point variable with equal masses at values \(a\) and \(b\) has variance \((a-b)^2/4\).  Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \leq\frac14\sum_jc_{j,h}^2
 =\frac1{4n^2}\sum_j\left(\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}\right)^2
 =\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}=\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{9}
\]

We next prove the bias certificate.  For \(N_i>0\), \(q_{i,h}\) depends only on neighbor assignments and is independent of \(Z_i\).  Conditioning first on \(Z_i=a\), applying \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) and \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\), and averaging the two equiprobable own-treatment values gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 -\frac{\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)+\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)}2\right|
 \leq\frac L6h_i(h)^2.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(N_i=0\), \(q_{i,h}=0\), while the derivative part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) bounds the absolute target contribution by \(B\).  Thus the score part has conditional bias at most
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}h_i(h)^2
 +\frac Bn\sum_i\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}.
\tag{11}
\]

It remains to bound the PC subtraction.  Centering and \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}|\leq B\) imply
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|\leq B/2.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(j\ne i\) with \(A_{ij}=1\), condition on all assignments except \(Z_j\).  The response change bound in (5) and the two equiprobable values of \(Z_j\) yield
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|
 =\frac14\left|\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=1]
 -\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=0]\right|
 \leq\frac B{4N_i}.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(j\ne i\) and \(A_{ij}=0\), the expectation is zero by \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) and \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac4n\sum_i\left|\sum_j(C_A)_{ij}\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right|
 &\leq\frac{2B}{n}\sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|\\
 &\quad+\frac Bn\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
       \sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|\\
 &=\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (11)--(14) with \({\rm def:target}\), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:radii}\) proves
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\mid A,(f_i)_i)\right|
 \leq\overline b_h(A).
\tag{15}
\]

Because \(P_A\) is a spectral projector of the symmetric matrix \(A\), it commutes with \(A\), so \(C_A=P_AA\) is symmetric.  If \(C_A=\sum_t\nu_tu_tu_t^\top\), orthonormality gives
\[
 \sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|
 \leq\sum_t|\nu_t|\sum_i u_{t,i}^2
 =\sum_t|\nu_t|=\|C_A\|_*.
\tag{16}
\]
For each positive-degree row, expanding the nonnegative square
\(\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(|(C_A)_{ij}|-c)^2\) at its minimizing constant proves the finite-sum Cauchy--Schwarz bound.  Applying it within rows and then once more across the at most \(n\) positive-degree rows gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 &\leq\sum_{i:N_i>0}
 \left\{\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(C_A)_{ij}^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\leq\sqrt{nG_A(C_A)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Substitution into \({\rm def:pc-handle}\) proves
\[
 \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \leq\frac{2B}{n}\|C_A\|_*+B\sqrt{\frac{G_A(C_A)}n}.
\tag{18}
\]

Put \(E_h=\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\) and \(m_h=\mathbb E_Z(E_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)\).  If \(\overline s_h(A)>0\), then pointwise
\[
 \mathbf1\{|E_h-m_h|>\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha\}
 \leq\frac{\alpha(E_h-m_h)^2}{\overline s_h(A)^2}.
\]
Taking the conditional expectation and using (9) shows that the conditional probability on the left is at most \(\alpha\).  If \(\overline s_h(A)=0\), (9) makes \(E_h=m_h\) almost surely and the same conclusion holds.  Together with (15),
\[
 \Pr_Z\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|>
 \overline b_h(A)+\frac{\overline s_h(A)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A,(f_i)_i\right)\leq\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
The derivative envelope and \({\rm def:target}\) give \(|\theta_n|\leq B\); intersecting with \([-B,B]\) therefore cannot remove the target on the event certified in (19).  Integrating (19) under any \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\) proves the asserted uniform coverage.

Finally, for each realized degree the truncated score changes only when \(h\) crosses one of finitely many lattice distances, so there are at most \(O(n^2)\) breakpoints over all units.  Exact finite binomial sums compute \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\), the finite maxima compute \(Q_{i,h}\) and \(D_{ij,h}\), one eigendecomposition computes \(C_A\), and matrix and finite-sum arithmetic compute the remaining certificates.  Thus every distinct score and certificate is a polynomial-time sample map.  By \({\rm lem:exact-score-hoeffding-projections}\), the first projection of \(q_h-4P_AAX\) is \(4(I_n-P_A)AX\) and its second projection is zero, with neither projection depending on \(h\).  Equations (9), (15), (18), and (19) prove every asserted certificate without imposing a sharper stochastic order.
