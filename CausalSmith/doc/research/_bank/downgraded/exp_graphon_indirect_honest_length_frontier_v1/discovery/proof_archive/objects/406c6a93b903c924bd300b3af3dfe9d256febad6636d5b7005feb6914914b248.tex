Fix \(i\) and condition on \(A\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), the \(N_i\) neighbor contrasts are exchangeable.  By \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\) and \(V_i=N_i^{-1}\sum_{k:A_{ik}=1}X_k\),
\[
 1=\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}V_i\mid A]
 =N_i^{-1}\sum_{k:A_{ik}=1}\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}X_k\mid A]
 =\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}X_j\mid A].
\]
If \(A_{ij}=0\), then \(q_{i,h}\) is independent of the centered variable \(X_j\), so the same expectation is zero.  Since \(\mathbb E_ZX_j^2=1/4\), the orthogonal projection of \(q_{i,h}\) onto the span of \(X_j\) is \(4A_{ij}X_j\).  Summing over \(j\) proves \(q_h^{(1)}=4AX\), uniformly in \(h\).

For the second projection, the simultaneous sign reversal of all neighbor contrasts sends \(V_i\) to \(-V_i\), hence sends the odd score \(q_{i,h}\) to \(-q_{i,h}\).  For distinct \(j,k\), integrate over all contrasts except \((X_j,X_k)\).  The resulting function on \(\{-1/2,1/2\}^2\) is odd under \((X_j,X_k)\mapsto(-X_j,-X_k)\).  Its Walsh expansion therefore contains only the two degree-one monomials and no constant or degree-two monomial.  After subtraction of the first projections, the \(\{j,k\}\) interaction is zero.  This proves \(q_h^{(2)}=0\).

An \(A\)-measurable linear map preserves Hoeffding order, proving the two displayed identities with \(I_n-\Pi\).  Finally, conditional independence and \(\mathbb E_Z[XX^\top\mid A]=I_n/4\) give
\[
 \mathbb E_Z\|4(I_n-\Pi)AX\|_2^2
 =16\operatorname{tr}\!\left((I_n-\Pi)AA^\top(I_n-\Pi)\frac{I_n}{4}\right)
 =4\|(I_n-\Pi)A\|_{\mathrm F}^2,
\]
where symmetry of \(A\) and idempotence of \(I_n-\Pi\) justify the last equality.
