For a realized set of isolates, the deterministic interval pays the worst-case contribution \(Bn^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf 1\{N_i=0\}\).  If the unobserved derivative perturbations on those units were conditionally centered independent signs of amplitude \(B\), their conditional absolute aggregate fluctuation would instead be of order
\[
 Bn^{-1}\sqrt{\sum_i\mathbf 1\{N_i=0\}},
\]
whose expectation is at most \(B\sqrt{\pi_{0,n}/n}\) by Jensen's inequality.  This explains the testing scale but does not supply a uniformly valid imputation estimator.  The frozen model permits the response law to depend arbitrarily on the latent graphon type, and it supplies neither a response-regression smoothness condition in that type nor an explicit, uniformly stable inverse-observation probability estimator for isolated units.  Estimated principal-component cells alone do not establish either ingredient, especially because no embedding is observed for an isolate.  Thus the naive upper term and the smaller centered testing fluctuation are rigorously separated, while a same-class estimator attaining the latter remains unconstructed.
