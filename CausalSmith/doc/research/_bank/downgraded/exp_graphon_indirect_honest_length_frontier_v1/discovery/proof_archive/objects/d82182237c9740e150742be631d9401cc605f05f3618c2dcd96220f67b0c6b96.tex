For \(m\geq1\), define the explicit odd function
\[
 \phi(t)=t(1-4t^2)^4\mathbf1\{|t|\leq1/2\}.
\]
The fourth-order zero at each endpoint makes \(\phi\) a globally \(C^3\) function, and \(\phi'(0)=1\).  Let \(C_s=\|\phi^{(s)}\|_\infty\) for \(0\leq s\leq3\), choose a universal \(c_0>0\) with \(c_0\max_s C_s\leq1\), and set
\[
 g_m(x)=c_0a_m m^{-1}\phi\{m(x-1/2)\}.
\]
Because \(a_m\leq Bm^{-1}\) and \(a_m\leq Lm^{-2}\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \|g_m\|_\infty\leq B,\quad
 \|g_m'\|_\infty\leq B,\quad
 \|g_m''\|_\infty\leq B,\quad
 \|g_m'''\|_\infty\leq L,
\]
after decreasing \(c_0\) if necessary.  Also \(g_m'(1/2)=c_0a_m\).  Every noncentral exposure point of a degree \(N\leq m\) is at distance at least \((2N)^{-1}\geq(2m)^{-1}\) from one half; the central point is a zero of this odd bump.  Thus \(g_m\) vanishes at every such lattice point.  For \(m=0\), take \(g_0(x)=Bx\), which obeys all envelopes and vanishes at the only exposure of an isolate.

Adjoin independent Rademacher variables \(\varepsilon_i\), independent of \((U,A,Z)\), and let
\[
 f_i(a,x)=\varepsilon_i g_m(x),\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The construction is i.i.d. across units and, by the preceding derivative bounds, satisfies \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\), \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\), \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\), and \({\rm ass:third-derivative-bound}\); retaining the admissible graph marginal preserves every other member property of \({\rm def:model-class}\).  Its target is
\[
 \theta_n=\frac{\beta_m}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad \beta_m=g_m'(1/2),
\]
with \(|\beta_m|=c_0a_m\) for \(m\geq1\) and \(|\beta_0|=B\).

If \(N_i\leq m\), then \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=0\) for every assignment, so the data contain no information about \(\varepsilon_i\).  No other unit's outcome contains \(f_i\), hence it contains no information about that sign either.  Enlarge the experiment by revealing every sign with \(N_i>m\).  Conditional on the enlarged data and \(A\), the remaining \(K_m(A)\) signs are still independent Rademacher variables.  Additional information can only decrease optimal risk, and zero is a median of their symmetric sum.  Consequently every estimator has conditional absolute risk at least
\[
 \frac{|\beta_m|}{n}\mathbb E\left[\left|\sum_{j=1}^{K_m(A)}\varepsilon_j\right|\Bigm|A\right].
\]
For \(S_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\varepsilon_j\), independence gives \(\mathbb ES_k^2=k\) and \(\mathbb ES_k^4=3k^2-2k\leq3k^2\).  If \(p_k=P\{S_k^2\geq k/2\}\), then decomposition at this event and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 k=\mathbb ES_k^2
 \leq\frac{k}{2}+(\mathbb ES_k^4)^{1/2}p_k^{1/2}
 \leq\frac{k}{2}+\sqrt3\,k p_k^{1/2}.
\]
Thus \(p_k\geq1/12\), and consequently
\[
 P\{|S_k|\geq\sqrt{k/2}\}\geq\frac1{12},
\]
so \(\mathbb E|S_k|\geq(12\sqrt2)^{-1}\sqrt k\).  Averaging over \(A\) proves the risk lower bound.

Finally, Jensen's inequality and the definition of \(\pi_{m,n}\) give
\[
 \frac1n\mathbb E_P\sqrt{K_m(A)}
 \leq\frac1n\sqrt{\mathbb E_PK_m(A)}
 \leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{m,n}}n}.
\]
The inequality is one-sided.  For example, if \(K\) is Bernoulli with mean \(p\) and \(np\to0\), then \(n^{-1}\mathbb E\sqrt K=p/n\), whereas \(\sqrt{(p/n)/n}=\sqrt p/n\); their ratio tends to zero.  Thus a first-moment definition cannot supply the reverse inequality required by the frozen claim.
