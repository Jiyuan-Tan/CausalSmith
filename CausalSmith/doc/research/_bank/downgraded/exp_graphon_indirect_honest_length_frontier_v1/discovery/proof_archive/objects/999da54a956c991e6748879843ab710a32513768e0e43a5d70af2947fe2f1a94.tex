Let
\[
 X=Z-\frac12\mathbf 1_n,\qquad
 P_A=\widehat\Pi_r(A),\qquad
 C_A=P_AA.
\]
All expectations and variances in the next four paragraphs are conditional on \(A\) and \((f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\).  By \({\rm def:empirical-projector}\), \(P_A\) is an orthogonal spectral projector of the symmetric matrix \(A\).  Hence \(P_AA=AP_A\).  By \({\rm lem:exact-score-hoeffding-projections}\), if
\[
 q_h^{(1)}=4AX,
 \qquad
 \eta_h=q_h-q_h^{(1)},
\]
then \(\eta_h\) is orthogonal in conditional \(L^2\) to every first-order function of the independent assignment coordinates and
\[
 \widetilde\gamma_h=q_h-4P_AAX
 =(I_n-P_A)q_h^{(1)}+\eta_h.
\tag{1}
\]
The two summands in (1) are orthogonal.  Because an orthogonal projector is a contraction, \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_Z\|\widetilde\gamma_h\|_2^2
 &=\mathbb E_Z\|(I_n-P_A)q_h^{(1)}\|_2^2
   +\mathbb E_Z\|\eta_h\|_2^2\\
 &\leq\mathbb E_Z\|q_h^{(1)}\|_2^2
   +\mathbb E_Z\|\eta_h\|_2^2\\
 &=\mathbb E_Z\|q_h\|_2^2
 =\sum_{i:N_i>0}\mu_{2,i}(h)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Here every inverse is well defined because \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\) proves \(\mu_{2,i}(h)>0\) whenever \(N_i>0\); isolates have score zero by \({\rm def:discrete-score}\).

The response envelope gives \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}|\leq B\).  Therefore pointwise Euclidean Cauchy--Schwarz and \({\rm def:estimator}\) imply
\[
 \widehat\theta_h^2
 =\left(\frac1n\sum_i\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\right)^2
 \leq\frac{B^2}{n}\|\widetilde\gamma_h\|_2^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(\operatorname{Var}(T)\leq\mathbb E[T^2]\), equations (2)--(3), \({\rm def:pc-handle}\), and the nonnegativity of every term in \({\rm def:radii}\) yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \leq\frac{B^2}{n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}\mu_{2,i}(h)^{-1}
 =\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \leq\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the advertised conservative variance certificate; no asymptotic order for \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}\) is used.

We next prove the conditional bias bound.  For \(N_i>0\), \(q_{i,h}\) is a function only of the neighbor assignments and is therefore independent of \(Z_i\) under \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\).  Combining this independence, \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\), and \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\), first condition on \(Z_i=a\) and then average over its two equiprobable values to obtain
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 -\frac{\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)+\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)}2
 \right|
 \leq\frac L6h_i(h)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(N_i=0\), then \(q_{i,h}=0\), while \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) bounds the absolute value of the corresponding summand of the target in \({\rm def:target}\) by \(B\).  Thus the score part of the estimator has conditional bias at most
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}h_i(h)^2
 +\frac Bn\sum_i\mathbf 1\{N_i=0\}.
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to bound the PC subtraction.  Centering and boundedness give
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[X_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right|
 \leq\frac B2.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(j\ne i\) with \(A_{ij}=1\), condition on all assignment coordinates except \(Z_j\).  Changing \(Z_j\) changes unit \(i\)'s treated-neighbor fraction by exactly \(N_i^{-1}\).  The mean-value theorem and the first-derivative part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) therefore give
\[
 |Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(Z_j=1)-Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(Z_j=0)|
 \leq\frac B{N_i}.
\]
Since \(X_j\) takes values \(-1/2\) and \(1/2\) with probability one half,
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right|
 =\frac14\left|
  \mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=1]
 -\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=0]
 \right|
 \leq\frac B{4N_i}.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(j\ne i\) and \(A_{ij}=0\), anonymous interference makes \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) independent of the centered coordinate \(X_j\), so the expectation is zero.  Applying (7)--(8) row by row gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\frac4n\sum_i\left|
   \sum_j(C_A)_{ij}\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]
  \right|\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \frac{2B}{n}\sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|
 +\frac Bn\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
      \sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 =\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (6), (9), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:radii}\) proves
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\mid A,(f_i)_i)\right|
 \leq\overline b_h(A).
\tag{10}
\]

We now derive the deterministic graph-norm bound.  Let \(D\) be the diagonal matrix whose \(i\)-th diagonal entry is the sign of \((C_A)_{ii}\), with sign zero defined as zero.  Since \(\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1\), nuclear--operator duality gives
\[
 \sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|=\operatorname{tr}(DC_A)
 \leq\|C_A\|_*.
\tag{11}
\]
For each positive-degree row, Cauchy--Schwarz over its \(N_i\) incident entries, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz over rows, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
    \sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 &\leq\sum_{i:N_i>0}
  \left\{\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(C_A)_{ij}^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\leq\sqrt{nG_A(C_A)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Substitution of (11)--(12) into \({\rm def:pc-handle}\) yields
\[
 \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \leq\frac{2B}{n}\|C_A\|_*
 +B\sqrt{\frac{G_A(C_A)}n}.
\tag{13}
\]

Conditional Chebyshev's inequality applied to (4) and (10) shows
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|>
 \overline b_h(A)+\frac{\overline s_h(A)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A,(f_i)_i
 \right)\leq\alpha.
\tag{14}
\]
The response envelope and \({\rm def:target}\) imply \(|\theta_n|\leq B\).  Hence intersecting the interval in (14) with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove \(\theta_n\).  Integrating (14) over the graph and preassignment draws proves
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}P\{\theta_n\in\mathcal C_h\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{15}
\]

Finally fix a realized degree \(N_i\).  The indicator in \({\rm def:discrete-score}\), and hence both its numerator and its exact binomial normalizer, changes only when \(h\) crosses one of the at most \(N_i+1\) distances of the degree-\(N_i\) exposure lattice from one half.  Across all units there are at most \(n^2\) such breakpoints.  Exact finite binomial sums compute every \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\) at them; one eigendecomposition computes \(P_A\); and ordinary matrix arithmetic then computes every distinct score and certificate.  This is a polynomial-time, piecewise-constant representation over \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  Equations (1)--(2) also show directly that the exact zero second projection produces no additional bandwidth-dependent term.  The argument proves only the computable quantities in (4), (10), and (13), and nowhere asserts \(O_P(d/n)\) rates for either feasible-PC remainder.
