Fix \(A\), the preassignment response functions, and \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  All expectations and variances in this proof are over the independent assignment coordinates from \({\rm ass:bernoulli\text{-}design}\).  Put
\[
 X=Z-\frac12\mathbf1_n,\qquad
 P_A=\widehat\Pi_r(A),\qquad
 C_A=P_AA,\qquad
 F_h(Z)=\widehat\theta_h.
\tag{1}
\]
The corrected \({\rm def:empirical\text{-}projector}\) uses \(m_{r,n}=\min\{r,n\}\) eigenvectors.  Hence \(P_A\) is an \(n\times n\) orthogonal spectral projector for every finite \(r,n\); no condition \(r\leq n\) is needed below.

We first prove the variance certificate.  If \(z^{(j)}\) is obtained from \(z\) by flipping coordinate \(j\), the finite maximum in \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) gives
\[
 |q_{i,h}(z)-q_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|\leq D_{ij,h}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the difference is zero when \(A_{ij}=0\); when \(A_{ij}=1\), the treated-neighbor count moves between two consecutive integers occurring in that maximum.  The division by \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\) is valid for \(N_i>0\) by \({\rm lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}moments}\), and both sides of (2) are zero under the definitions when \(N_i=0\).  Since \(|X_j|=1/2\), the definitions of \(Q_{i,h}\), \(\Gamma_{i,h}\), and \(\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}\) imply
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)|
 \leq Q_{i,h}+2\sum_{j'}|(C_A)_{ij'}|
 =\Gamma_{i,h}.
\tag{3}
\]
A flip changes \(X_j\) by one in absolute value, and therefore
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)-\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq D_{ij,h}+4|(C_A)_{ij}|.
\tag{4}
\]

The response-level part of \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) gives \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)|\leq B\).  If \(j=i\), a flip changes only the own-treatment argument, so the two response values differ by at most \(2B\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), then \(j\ne i\), \(N_i>0\), and the exposure fraction changes by \(N_i^{-1}\).  The first-derivative part of \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) and the fundamental theorem of calculus give
\[
 |f_i(z_i,x+N_i^{-1})-f_i(z_i,x)|
 =\left|\int_x^{x+N_i^{-1}}\partial_t f_i(z_i,t)\,dt\right|
 \leq B/N_i.
\]
In every other case \({\rm ass:anonymous\text{-}interference}\) makes the change zero.  With the zero-degree convention in \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\),
\[
 |Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)-Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq B\left\{2\mathbf1\{i=j\}+\frac{A_{ij}}{N_i}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Applying \(|gy-g'y'|\leq|g-g'||y|+|g'||y-y'|\) to (3)--(5) proves
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)
 -\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{6}
\]
Summing (6) and using \({\rm def:estimator}\) yields
\[
 |F_h(z)-F_h(z^{(j)})|
 \leq c_{j,h}:=\frac1n\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathcal F_j=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_j)\), \(M_j=\mathbb E_Z(F_h\mid\mathcal F_j)\), and \(E_j=M_j-M_{j-1}\).  If \(j<k\), the tower property gives
\[
 \mathbb E_Z(E_jE_k)
 =\mathbb E_Z\{E_j\mathbb E_Z(E_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1})\}=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(F_h)=\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E_ZE_j^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_{j-1}\), the difference between the two values of \(M_j\) obtained by setting \(Z_j=1\) and \(Z_j=0\) is an average over future coordinates of differences bounded by (7), and hence is at most \(c_{j,h}\).  By \({\rm ass:bernoulli\text{-}design}\), these two values have equal conditional probabilities.  The variance of two equal-mass values \(a,b\) is \((a-b)^2/4\), so
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \leq\frac14\sum_jc_{j,h}^2
 =\frac1{4n^2}\sum_j\left(\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}\right)^2
 =\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 =\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{9}
\]

We next prove the bias certificate.  For \(N_i>0\), \(q_{i,h}\) depends only on neighbor assignments and is independent of \(Z_i\), because \(A_{ii}=0\) under \({\rm ass:graphon\text{-}draw}\).  Conditioning first on \(Z_i=a\), applying \({\rm ass:anonymous\text{-}interference}\) and \({\rm lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}moments}\), and averaging the two equiprobable own-treatment values gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 -\frac{\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)+\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)}2\right|
 \leq\frac L6h_i(h)^2.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(N_i=0\), \(q_{i,h}=0\), while the first-derivative part of \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) bounds the absolute target summand by \(B\).  Thus the conditional bias of the score part is at most
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}h_i(h)^2
 +\frac Bn\sum_i\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}.
\tag{11}
\]

It remains to bound the PC subtraction.  Centering and \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}|\leq B\) imply
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|\leq B/2.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(j\ne i\) with \(A_{ij}=1\), condition on all assignments except \(Z_j\).  The response-change bound in (5) and the two equiprobable values of \(Z_j\) give
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|
 =\frac14\left|\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=1]
 -\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=0]\right|
 \leq\frac B{4N_i}.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(j\ne i\) and \(A_{ij}=0\), the expectation is zero by \({\rm ass:anonymous\text{-}interference}\) and \({\rm ass:bernoulli\text{-}design}\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac4n\sum_i\left|\sum_j(C_A)_{ij}
       \mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right|
 &\leq\frac{2B}{n}\sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|\\
 &\quad+\frac Bn\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
       \sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|\\
 &=\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (11)--(14) with \({\rm def:target}\), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:radii}\) proves
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\mid A,(f_i)_i)\right|
 \leq\overline b_h(A).
\tag{15}
\]

Because \(P_A\) is a spectral projector of the symmetric matrix \(A\), it commutes with \(A\); hence \(C_A=P_AA\) is symmetric.  If \(C_A=\sum_t\nu_tu_tu_t^\top\) is its spectral decomposition, orthonormality gives
\[
 \sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|
 \leq\sum_t|\nu_t|\sum_i u_{t,i}^2
 =\sum_t|\nu_t|=\|C_A\|_*.
\tag{16}
\]
Finite-sum Cauchy--Schwarz within every positive-degree row and then across the at most \(n\) such rows gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 &\leq\sum_{i:N_i>0}
 \left\{\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(C_A)_{ij}^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\leq\sqrt{nG_A(C_A)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Substitution of (16)--(17) into \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) proves
\[
 \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \leq\frac{2B}{n}\|C_A\|_*+B\sqrt{\frac{G_A(C_A)}n}.
\tag{18}
\]

Put \(E_h=\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\) and \(m_h=\mathbb E_Z(E_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)\).  If \(\overline s_h(A)>0\), then pointwise
\[
 \mathbf1\{|E_h-m_h|>\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha\}
 \leq\frac{\alpha(E_h-m_h)^2}{\overline s_h(A)^2}.
\]
Taking conditional expectations and using (9) bounds the probability on the left by \(\alpha\).  If \(\overline s_h(A)=0\), (9) gives \(E_h=m_h\) conditionally almost surely.  Together with (15), in either case
\[
 \Pr_Z\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|>
 \overline b_h(A)+\frac{\overline s_h(A)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A,(f_i)_i\right)\leq\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
The first-derivative envelope and \({\rm def:target}\) give \(|\theta_n|\leq B\); intersection with \([-B,B]\) therefore cannot remove the target on the event certified in (19).  Integrating the conditional inequality under any \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\) proves the asserted uniform coverage.

Finally, for each realized degree the truncated score changes only when \(h\) crosses one of finitely many lattice distances.  Across all units there are at most \(O(n^2)\) such breakpoints.  Exact finite binomial sums compute \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\), the finite maxima compute \(Q_{i,h}\) and \(D_{ij,h}\), one eigendecomposition with the leading \(\min\{r,n\}\) convention computes \(C_A\), and matrix and finite-sum arithmetic compute the remaining certificates.  Thus every distinct score and every certificate at a supplied bandwidth are polynomial-time sample maps.  By \({\rm lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}hoeffding\text{-}projections}\), the first projection of \(q_h-4P_AAX\) is
\[
 4(I_n-P_A)AX,
\]
and its second projection is zero.  The construction therefore subtracts the empirical leading-eigenspace component; it does not remove the residual first projection.  Neither projection depends on \(h\).  Equations (9), (15), (18), and (19) prove every asserted certificate without imposing a sharper stochastic order.
