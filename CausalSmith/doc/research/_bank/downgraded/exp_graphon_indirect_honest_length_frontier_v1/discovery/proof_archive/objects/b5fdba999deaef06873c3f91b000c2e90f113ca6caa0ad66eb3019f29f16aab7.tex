Fix a graph \(A\) in the realized support and write \(I_0=\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}\).  Consider the admissible response law
\[
 f_i(a,x)=\varepsilon_iBx,
 \quad \Pr(\varepsilon_i=1)=\Pr(\varepsilon_i=-1)=\tfrac12,
\tag{1}
\]
where the signs are independent across units and independent of the uniform latent types.  The pairs \((U_i,f_i)\) are therefore independent and identically distributed as required by \({\rm ass:unit\text{-}sampling}\).  For every \(a,x\), (1) has absolute level at most \(B\), absolute first derivative \(B\), zero second derivative, and zero third derivative.  Hence \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) and \({\rm ass:third\text{-}derivative\text{-}bound}\) hold for every \(L\geq0\); all graph properties of any pre-existing member of the nonempty class are unchanged.  This proves admissibility whenever \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\) is nonempty.

For an isolate, \({\rm ass:anonymous\text{-}interference}\) gives \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=f_i(Z_i,0)=0\), while its contribution to \({\rm def:target}\) is \(\varepsilon_iB\).  Fix all nonisolate response functions.  Two sign configurations that put respectively \(+1\) and \(-1\) on every isolate induce the same conditional observed-data law because every isolate outcome is zero, but their targets differ by \(2BI_0/n\).  If \(m\) is the common conditional expectation of any data-measurable estimator under these two configurations, the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \max\{|m-\theta_+|,|m-\theta_-|\}
 \geq\frac{|\theta_+-\theta_-|}{2}=\frac Bn I_0.
\tag{2}
\]
Both configurations have positive conditional-support probability under (1), independently of the graph.  Thus a graph-only radius required to dominate the conditional bias for every supported response configuration must satisfy \(b(A)\geq BI_0(A)/n\) on every realized graph.

Now give a genie all nonisolate signs.  Conditional on \(A\), the remaining isolate signs are independent Rademacher variables and are independent of the entire observed sample, because their outcomes are identically zero.  After subtracting the known nonisolate contribution, estimating \(\theta_n\) is therefore exactly the location problem
\[
 \frac Bn S_{I_0},\quad S_m=\sum_{k=1}^m\varepsilon_k.
\tag{3}
\]
The distribution of \(S_m\) is symmetric, so zero is a median and minimizes absolute loss.  Consequently the conditional Bayes risk equals \((B/n)\mathbb E|S_{I_0}|\).  Independence and centering give
\[
 \mathbb ES_m^2=m,\quad
 \mathbb ES_m^4=m+6\binom m2=3m^2-2m\leq3m^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\mathbb E|S_m|\leq\sqrt m\).  Also, Hölder's inequality in the form
\[
 \mathbb ES_m^2
 \leq(\mathbb E|S_m|)^{2/3}(\mathbb ES_m^4)^{1/3}
\]
and (4) give \(\mathbb E|S_m|\geq\sqrt{m/3}\).  The conclusion also holds at \(m=0\).  Substitution in (3) proves the two displayed Bayes-risk bounds.  Averaging over \(A\) gives the fluctuation order \(Bn^{-1}\mathbb E\sqrt{I_0(A)}\), and \(\sqrt m\leq m\) for integer \(m\geq1\) shows that it is no larger than \(Bn^{-1}\mathbb EI_0(A)\).  The argument concerns a conditional graph-only bias requirement and a genie-aided Bayes subproblem; it does not construct, or prove a universal length lower bound for, an unconditionally honest interval.
