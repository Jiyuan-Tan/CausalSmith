Work first conditionally on \(A\) and \((f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\), and introduce the proof-intermediate function
\[
 g_i(x):=\frac{f_i(1,x)+f_i(0,x)}{2},\qquad x\in[0,1].
\]
By \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\), conditional on \(A\) and \((U_i,f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\), each assignment vector has probability \(2^{-n}\).  Because this conditional probability does not depend on the latent types, the tower property gives, for every \(z\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal V_n}\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{Z=z\mid A,(f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\right\}
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
   \Pr\!\left\{Z=z\mid A,(U_i,f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\right\}
   \mathrel{\big|}A,(f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}
 \right]
 =2^{-n}.
\]
Thus the coordinates of \(Z\) remain independent \({\rm Bernoulli}(1/2)\) variables under the conditional law used in \({\rm def:li-wager-target}\).

Fix \(i\in\mathcal V_n\) with \(N_i=m>0\).  Since \(A_{ii}=0\), the treated-neighbor count \(M_i\) is a function only of \((Z_j)_{j\ne i}\).  The preceding product law therefore gives
\[
 M_i\sim{\rm Binomial}(m,1/2),
 \qquad Z_i\ \text{is independent of }M_i.
\]
Define the unit contribution
\[
 \beta_i:=4\,\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
 Y_i(Z)(M_i-m/2)\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}
 \right].
\]
By \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\), \(Y_i(Z)=f_i(Z_i,1/2+V_i)\) for this positive-degree unit.  Averaging first over the independent variable \(Z_i\), and then using \(M_i-m/2=mV_i\), yields
\[
 \beta_i
 =4m\,\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
 g_i(1/2+V_i)V_i\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}
 \right].
\]
For \(N_i=0\), the factor \(M_i-N_i/2\) vanishes, so set \(\beta_i=0\).  The construction in \({\rm def:li-wager-target}\) then gives
\[
 \bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\beta_i.
\]

For the fixed positive-degree unit, let \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) be independent variables taking the values \(-1/2\) and \(1/2\) with equal probabilities, and put \(S_m=\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\).  The binomial law above identifies the conditional distribution of \(V_i\) with that of \(S_m/m\).  Centering and independence imply
\[
 \mathbb E S_m^2=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^2=\frac m4,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E S_m^3=0.
\]
In the fourth-power expansion, only the one-index terms and the terms formed by two index pairs have nonzero expectation.  Since \(\mathbb E X_j^2=1/4\) and \(\mathbb E X_j^4=1/16\),
\[
 \mathbb E S_m^4
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^4
 +6\sum_{1\le j<k\le m}\mathbb E X_j^2\mathbb E X_k^2
 =\frac{3m^2-2m}{16}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E V_i=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E V_i^2=\frac1{4m},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E V_i^3=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E V_i^4=\frac{3m^2-2m}{16m^4}\le\frac3{16m^2}.
\]

By \({\rm ass:third-derivative-bound}\) and the definition of \(g_i\),
\[
 \sup_{x\in[0,1]}|g_i^{(3)}(x)|\le L.
\]
Taylor's identity with integral remainder on the segment joining \(1/2\) and \(1/2+v\), which is contained in \([0,1]\) for \(|v|\le1/2\), gives
\[
 g_i(1/2+v)
 =g_i(1/2)+g_i'(1/2)v+\frac12g_i''(1/2)v^2+R_i(v),
 \qquad
 |R_i(v)|\le\frac L6|v|^3.
\]
Multiplication by \(v\), conditional expectation, and the four moment identities cancel the constant and quadratic Taylor terms and use \(4m\mathbb E V_i^2=1\).  Hence
\[
 \beta_i-g_i'(1/2)
 =4m\,\mathbb E\!\left[V_iR_i(V_i)\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right],
\]
so
\[
 |\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
 \le\frac{4mL}{6}\mathbb E V_i^4
 \le\frac{4mL}{6}\frac3{16m^2}
 =\frac{L}{8m}.
\]
By \({\rm def:target}\), the contribution of unit \(i\) to \(\theta_n\) is \(g_i'(1/2)\).  If \(N_i=0\), \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) gives
\[
 |g_i'(1/2)|
 \le\frac{|\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)|+|\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)|}{2}
 \le B.
\]
Separating positive-degree vertices from isolates and applying the triangle inequality proves the finite-sample bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|
 &\le\frac1n\sum_{i:N_i>0}|\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
 +\frac1n\sum_{i:N_i=0}|g_i'(1/2)|\\
 &\le\frac{L}{8n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
 +\frac Bn\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the maintained-class asymptotic approximation, use the lower-order envelope rather than fixing \(L\).  By \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\), \(\sup_x|g_i''(x)|\le B\), and the once-integrated Taylor identity gives
\[
 g_i(1/2+v)=g_i(1/2)+g_i'(1/2)v+T_i(v),
 \qquad |T_i(v)|\le\frac B2v^2.
\]
The same moment cancellation, followed by Lyapunov's inequality \(\mathbb E|V_i|^3\le(\mathbb EV_i^4)^{3/4}\), therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
 &\le2mB\,\mathbb E|V_i|^3
 \le2mB(\mathbb EV_i^4)^{3/4}\\
 &\le2mB\left(\frac3{16m^2}\right)^{3/4}
 =\frac{3^{3/4}B}{4\sqrt m}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now consider any maintained triangular-array sequence with fixed \(B\) and \(\kappa\) and
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_n}{\log n}>-\frac12.
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) strictly smaller than the gap between this lower limit and \(-1/2\).  Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \rho_n\ge n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.
\]
Fix a vertex \(i\).  By \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\) and \({\rm ass:graphon-draw}\), conditional only on \(U_i=u\), the pairs consisting of \(U_j\) and the edge draw \(A_{ij}\), for \(j\ne i\), are independent and identically distributed.  Marginalizing each \(U_j\) gives
\[
 N_i\mid U_i=u\sim{\rm Binomial}(n-1,p_n(u)),
 \qquad
 p_n(u):=\rho_n\int_0^1W(u,v)\,dv.
\]
The conditions \({\rm ass:kernel-bound}\) and \({\rm ass:edge-probability}\) imply \(0\le p_n(u)\le\rho_n\kappa\le1\), while \({\rm ass:degree-regularity}\) gives, for almost every \(u\),
\[
 \mu_i(u):=\mathbb E[N_i\mid U_i=u]
 =(n-1)p_n(u)
 \ge\frac{(n-1)\rho_n}{\kappa}.
\]
Since the \(U_i\) are uniform by \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\), this almost-everywhere bound holds simultaneously for all finitely many sampled types with probability one at each \(n\).

We derive the needed binomial tail bound.  If \(S\sim{\rm Binomial}(m,p)\), \(\mu=mp\), and \(t=\log2\), then
\[
 \mathbf1\{S\le\mu/2\}\le\exp\{t(\mu/2-S)\}.
\]
Taking expectations, using independence, and applying \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr(S\le\mu/2)
 &\le e^{t\mu/2}\mathbb Ee^{-tS}
 =e^{t\mu/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^m\\
 &\le\exp\!\left\{\frac{t\mu}{2}-\mu(1-e^{-t})\right\}
 =\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1-\log2}{2}\mu\right\}
 \le e^{-\mu/8},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality uses \((1-\log2)/2\ge1/8\).  Applying this inequality conditionally on each \(U_i\), then integrating and taking a union bound, shows that the event
\[
 \mathcal E_n:=\left\{\min_{i\in\mathcal V_n}N_i
 \ge\frac{(n-1)\rho_n}{2\kappa}\right\}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le n\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(n-1)\rho_n}{8\kappa}\right\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed, \(n\rho_n\ge n^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) eventually, so the negative exponential term dominates \(\log n\).

On \(\mathcal E_n\) there are no isolates, and
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\frac1{\sqrt{N_i}}
 \le\left(\frac{2\kappa}{(n-1)\rho_n}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The lower-order bound therefore implies
\[
 \frac{|\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|}{\sqrt{\rho_n}}
 \le\frac{3^{3/4}B\sqrt{2\kappa}}{4\sqrt{n-1}\,\rho_n}
 \le\frac{3^{3/4}B\sqrt{2\kappa}}{4\sqrt{1-1/n}\,n^\varepsilon}
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad\text{on }\mathcal E_n.
\]
Together with \(\Pr(\mathcal E_n^c)\to0\), this proves
\[
 \bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n=o_P(\sqrt{\rho_n}).
\]

Finally, restrict to the intersection with every source condition recorded in \({\rm lem:li-wager-theorem-six-regime}\), and specialize the treatment probability to one half.  The cited lemma supplies the equation~(43) Gaussian limit centered at \(\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}\), with complete standardizer \(\sqrt{\rho_n}\sigma_{\mathrm{IND},n}\).  Under the additional stated nondegeneracy condition, there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\) such that \(\sigma_{\mathrm{IND},n}\ge c\) for \(n\ge n_0\).  Hence
\[
 R_n:=\frac{\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n}
 {\sqrt{\rho_n}\sigma_{\mathrm{IND},n}}=o_P(1).
\]
Writing \(T_n\) for the equation~(43) statistic centered at \(\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}\), the corresponding statistic centered at \(\theta_n\) is exactly \(T_n+R_n\).  To verify the additive limit step without invoking any undeclared theorem, let \(\Phi\) be the standard-normal distribution function.  For every real \(x\) and every \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \Pr(T_n\le x-\eta)-\Pr(|R_n|>\eta)
 \le\Pr(T_n+R_n\le x)
 \le\Pr(T_n\le x+\eta)+\Pr(|R_n|>\eta).
\]
The cited limit gives \(T_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), and the preceding display gives \(R_n=o_P(1)\).  Taking lower and upper limits and then letting \(\eta\downarrow0\), using continuity of \(\Phi\), proves \(T_n+R_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).  Thus the transfer is valid under the full-standardizer nondegeneracy condition and only on the stated source-class intersection.  The nonzero degree-exact finite-sample remainder proved above also shows that the two targets were not identified by definition.
