If \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\) is empty, the assertion quantified over admissible laws is vacuous, and the submodel clause is not invoked.  Suppose henceforth that \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\) is nonempty.  Retain the latent-type and graph law of any one member and replace its response draw by
\[
 f_i(a,x)=\varepsilon_i Bx,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_i\mathrel{\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}}{\rm Unif}\{-1,1\},
\tag{1}
\]
where the signs are independent of all latent types, graph variables, and assignments.  The pairs \((U_i,f_i)\) are then independent and identically distributed and have uniform \(U_i\)-marginal, so (1) satisfies \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\).  Moreover, for every \(i\), \(a\), and \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
 |f_i(a,x)|\le B,
 \qquad
 |\partial_x f_i(a,x)|=B,
 \qquad
 \partial_x^2 f_i(a,x)=\partial_x^3 f_i(a,x)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus (2) verifies \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) and \({\rm ass:third-derivative-bound}\) for every \(L\ge0\).  Because only the response law was replaced, every graph, rank, support, and randomization member property listed in \({\rm def:model-class}\) is unchanged.  Hence (1) is an admissible submodel.

Fix a graph \(A\) in the support of this submodel and define
\[
 S_0(A)=\{i\in\mathcal V_n:N_i=0\},
 \qquad
 |S_0(A)|=I_0(A).
\tag{3}
\]
Consider two sign vectors in the conditional support of (1) which agree on \(S_0(A)^c\), with every sign in \(S_0(A)\) equal to \(+1\) in the first vector and to \(-1\) in the second.  Independence of the signs gives each vector strictly positive conditional probability \(2^{-n}\).  If \(i\in S_0(A)\), then \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) and (1) give, for every assignment,
\[
 Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=f_i(Z_i,0)=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The two sign vectors agree at every nonisolate, so the nonisolate responses also agree pointwise for every assignment.  Consequently the complete conditional observed-data laws of any data-measurable estimator \(\widetilde\theta\) are identical under the two response configurations.  Write \(m(A)\) for their common value of \(\mathbb E_Z(\widetilde\theta\mid A,(f_i)_i)\).

By (1) and \({\rm def:target}\), a sign vector \(\varepsilon\) has target
\[
 \theta_n(\varepsilon)=\frac{B}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\varepsilon_i.
\tag{5}
\]
The two target values therefore satisfy
\[
 |\theta_n^+-\theta_n^-|=\frac{2B}{n}I_0(A).
\tag{6}
\]
If the stated graph-only radius is valid for every response configuration in the conditional support, it bounds both \(|m(A)-\theta_n^+|\) and \(|m(A)-\theta_n^-|\).  The triangle inequality and (6) then yield
\[
 2b(A)
 \ge |m(A)-\theta_n^+|+|m(A)-\theta_n^-|
 \ge |\theta_n^+-\theta_n^-|
 =\frac{2B}{n}I_0(A),
\tag{7}
\]
which proves \(b(A)\ge BI_0(A)/n\) on every realized graph in the support.

For the Bayes calculation, give the estimator \(A\) and every sign in \(S_0(A)^c\).  Conditional on this genie information, the known nonisolate part of (5) may be subtracted.  By independence in (1) and (4), all remaining observed data are independent of the unknown target contribution
\[
 T_0(A)=\frac{B}{n}\sum_{i\in S_0(A)}\varepsilon_i.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the conditional genie Bayes absolute-error risk equals \(B n^{-1}\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}\mathbb E|S_K-a|\), where \(K=I_0(A)\) and \(S_K=\sum_{k=1}^K\varepsilon_k\).  The distribution of \(S_K\) is symmetric, and for every \(s,a\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \frac{|s-a|+|-s-a|}{2}\ge |s|.
\tag{9}
\]
Averaging (9) over the symmetric law proves that zero is a Bayes action and hence that the risk is exactly \(Bn^{-1}\mathbb E|S_K|\).

For \(K\ge1\), independence, centering, and \(\varepsilon_k^2=1\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E S_K^2=K,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E S_K^4=K+6{K\choose2}=3K^2-2K\le3K^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\mathbb E|S_K|\le\sqrt K\).  H\"older's inequality applied to \(|S_K|^{2/3}|S_K|^{4/3}\), followed by (10), gives
\[
 K=\mathbb E S_K^2
 \le (\mathbb E|S_K|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|S_K|^4)^{1/3},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E|S_K|
 \ge \frac{(\mathbb E S_K^2)^{3/2}}{(\mathbb E S_K^4)^{1/2}}
 \ge\sqrt{K/3}.
\tag{11}
\]
Both bounds also hold when \(K=0\).  Combining (8)--(11) with \(K=I_0(A)\) proves the two displayed conditional Bayes-risk bounds.  Averaging them over \(A\) shows that the unconditional missing-sign fluctuation lies between \(B(\sqrt3\,n)^{-1}\mathbb E\sqrt{I_0(A)}\) and \(Bn^{-1}\mathbb E\sqrt{I_0(A)}\).  Finally, \(\sqrt K\le K\) for every nonnegative integer \(K\), so this fluctuation is no larger than \(Bn^{-1}\mathbb E I_0(A)\).  The argument constructs neither an unconditionally honest interval nor a lower bound applying to every such interval, which proves the theorem's final scope sentence.
