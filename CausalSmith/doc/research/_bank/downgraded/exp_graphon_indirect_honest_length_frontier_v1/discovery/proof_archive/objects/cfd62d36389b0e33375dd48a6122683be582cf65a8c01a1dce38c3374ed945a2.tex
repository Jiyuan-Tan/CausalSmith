Fix an index for which \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\ne\varnothing\), and define
\[
 J(h)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)\right],
 \qquad h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}.
\tag{1}
\]
We first prove carefully that the smallest global minimizer in \({\rm def:exact\text{-}optimal\text{-}bandwidth}\) exists.  By \({\rm def:bandwidth\text{-}set}\), nonemptiness implies
\[
 \mathcal H_{n,d}=[\ell,u],\qquad \ell=d^{-1},\quad u=\min\{d^{-1/2},1/2\},
 \qquad 0<\ell\leq u\leq\tfrac12.
\tag{2}
\]
At fixed \(n\), the graph sample space is finite.  For every realized graph and every positive degree, \({\rm lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}moments}\) gives \(\mu_{2,i}(h)>0\); hence every quotient and every finite-lattice maximum in \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) is finite.  The response envelope in \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\), together with the finite sums in \({\rm def:radii}\), then gives a finite deterministic bound for \(\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)\), uniformly over the finite graph sample space and over \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\).  Therefore
\[
 0\leq J(h)<\infty\qquad(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}).
\tag{3}
\]

For a degree \(N\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\), every indicator entering \(q_{i,h}\), \(Q_{i,h}\), or \(D_{ij,h}\) has the form
\[
 \mathbf 1\{|m/N-1/2|\leq\max(h,\delta_N)\}
\tag{4}
\]
for an integer \(m\) in a finite range.  Consequently these score-dependent quantities can change only when \(h\) crosses one of finitely many degree-lattice distances.  Let \(\mathcal K\) consist of \(\ell\), \(u\), and all such distances lying in \([\ell,u]\).  List its distinct elements increasingly as \(t_0,\ldots,t_K\), with \(t_0=\ell\) and \(t_K=u\).  If \(K\geq1\), then
\[
 \ell=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_K=u.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(K=0\), then \(\mathcal H_{n,d}=\{\ell\}=\{u\}\), whose unique point is already its smallest global minimizer, and (8)--(10) below are immediate.  For the cell argument suppose \(K\geq1\).
Because the inequality in (4) is weak, all score-dependent terms in \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) are constant on every half-closed cell \([t_k,t_{k+1})\).  The matrix \(C_A\) and \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}\) are bandwidth-free, while the finite-lattice formula for \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}\) depends on \(h\) only through those score terms.  Thus both certificates are constant on each such cell.  The only possibly varying term in \(\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)\) is
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}\max\{h,\delta_{N_i}\}^2,
\tag{6}
\]
which is nondecreasing in \(h\) because \(L\geq0\).  Thus, for every realized \(A\), every \(P\), and \(t_k\leq h<t_{k+1}\),
\[
 \overline b_{t_k}(A)+\overline s_{t_k}(A)
 \leq \overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A),
 \quad
 \mathbb E_P[\overline b_{t_k}(A)+\overline s_{t_k}(A)]
 \leq \mathbb E_P[\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)].
\tag{7}
\]
Taking the supremum over the same nonempty class in (7) preserves the inequality, so \(J(t_k)\leq J(h)\).  The terminal point \(u\) itself belongs to \(\mathcal K\).  It follows that
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}J(h)=\min_{t\in\mathcal K}J(t).
\tag{8}
\]
The finite set on the right has at least one minimizer.  Let \(t^\dagger\) be its smallest minimizing element.  Equation (8) makes \(t^\dagger\) a global minimizer of \(J\) on \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  If a global minimizer \(h'<t^\dagger\) existed, then either \(h'=u\), which is impossible, or \(h'\in[t_k,t_{k+1})\) for some \(k\) with \(t_k\leq h'<t^\dagger\).  By (7),
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}J(h)
 \leq J(t_k)\leq J(h')
 =\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}J(h),
\tag{9}
\]
so \(t_k\) would be a minimizing candidate strictly earlier than \(t^\dagger\), a contradiction.  Hence \(t^\dagger\) is the smallest global minimizer, even though the full global argmin can contain intervals.  Therefore
\[
 h^\dagger=t^\dagger
 =\min\operatorname*{argmin}_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}J(h)
\tag{10}
\]
is well defined exactly as required by \({\rm def:exact\text{-}optimal\text{-}bandwidth}\).  Since (1) contains the full-class supremum, \(h^\dagger\) is an oracle class-level value rather than an operational data-driven tuning rule.

Now fix any prespecified \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\), any \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\), and put
\[
 \mathcal G=\sigma\!\left(A,(f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\right),
 \qquad E_h=\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n,
 \qquad m_h=\mathbb E_P(E_h\mid\mathcal G).
\tag{11}
\]
By \({\rm thm:explicit\text{-}pc\text{-}conditional\text{-}certificate}\),
\[
 |m_h|\leq\overline b_h(A),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[(E_h-m_h)^2\mid\mathcal G\right]
 \leq\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{12}
\]
On \(\{\overline s_h(A)>0\}\), conditional Markov's inequality applied to the nonnegative variable \((E_h-m_h)^2\), followed by (12), yields
\[
 \Pr_Z\!\left\{|E_h-m_h|>overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha\mid\mathcal G\right\}
 \leq
 \frac{\alpha\,\mathbb E_P[(E_h-m_h)^2\mid\mathcal G]}
      {\overline s_h(A)^2}
 \leq\alpha.
\tag{13}
\]
On \(\{\overline s_h(A)=0\}\), (12) implies \(E_h=m_h\) conditionally almost surely.  Thus in both cases, the triangle inequality and the first inequality in (12) give
\[
 \Pr_Z\!\left\{
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|
 \leq\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal G\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{14}
\]
By \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) and \({\rm def:target}\),
\[
 |\theta_n|
 \leq\frac1{2n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}
 \left\{|\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)|+|\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)|\right\}
 \leq B.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore intersecting the untruncated interval with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove \(\theta_n\) on the event in (14).  This proves the asserted conditional coverage for almost every preassignment realization.  Polynomial-time reportability follows from the polynomial-time sample maps in \({\rm thm:explicit\text{-}pc\text{-}conditional\text{-}certificate}\), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:honest\text{-}interval}\).  Before intersection, \({\rm def:honest\text{-}interval}\) gives the pointwise half-length exactly as
\[
 \overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha.
\tag{16}
\]
The sums in \({\rm def:radii}\) include every positive realized degree and the term \(Bn^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}\), so no degree or isolate is discarded.  Moreover, (2) implies \(d^{-1}=\ell\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  Hence \(h=d^{-1}\) is an admissible prespecified operational choice, but (10) supplies no identity between that endpoint and \(h^\dagger\).

It remains to establish the integrated oracle bounds.  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and (12) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P(|E_h-m_h|\mid\mathcal G)
 \leq
 \left\{\mathbb E_P[(E_h-m_h)^2\mid\mathcal G]\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq\overline s_h(A).
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (17) with the triangle inequality and (12), then taking expectations, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|
 \leq\mathbb E_P[\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)].
\tag{18}
\]
Set \(h=h^\dagger\), take the supremum over the nonempty model class, and use \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\).  Then
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{h^\dagger}-\theta_n|
 \leq\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(h^\dagger\) is deterministic at the class level, integrating (14) over the preassignment variables and the graph gives
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 P\{\theta_n\in\mathcal C_{h^\dagger}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{20}
\]
Intersection with \([-B,B]\) can only shorten the untruncated interval.  Nonnegativity of both radii therefore yields, pointwise,
\[
 \operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_h)
 \leq2\{\overline b_h(A)+\alpha^{-1/2}\overline s_h(A)\}
 \leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}
       \{\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Taking expectations and the class supremum at \(h=h^\dagger\), and again invoking \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\), proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_{h^\dagger})]
 \leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) are the operational finite-sample conditional certificate at any prespecified admissible bandwidth; (19)--(22) are integrated guarantees at the oracle class-level minimizer.  No step identifies \(h^\dagger\) with \(d^{-1}\), establishes a bandwidth oracle inequality, or compares \(\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}\) with \(\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\).
