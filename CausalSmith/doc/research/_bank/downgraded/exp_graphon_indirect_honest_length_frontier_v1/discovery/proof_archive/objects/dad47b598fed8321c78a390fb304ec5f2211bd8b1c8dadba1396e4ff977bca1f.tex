Assume \(L>0\), and write
\[
 c_{n,d}=n^{-1/2}d^{-1/4},
 \qquad g(h)=Lh^2+c_{n,d}h^{-3/2},
 \qquad \ell=d^{-1},
 \qquad u=d^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Along the stipulated triangular array, \({\rm ass:log\text{-}degree}\) and \(C_{\log}>0\) imply \(d\to\infty\).  Hence, at every sufficiently large index, \(d\geq4\), so \(0<\ell\leq u\leq1/2\).  By \({\rm def:bandwidth\text{-}set}\),
\[
 \mathcal H_{n,d}=[\ell,u].
\tag{2}
\]
The definition of \(h_0\) in \({\rm def:optimal\text{-}bandwidth}\) gives
\[
 h_0^7=\frac{L^{-2}}{n\sqrt d}.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(c_{n,d}^2=(n\sqrt d)^{-1}\) and all quantities are positive, (3) implies the exact balance identity
\[
 Lh_0^2=c_{n,d}h_0^{-3/2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Suppose first that \(h_0\leq\ell\).  For every \(h\in[\ell,u]\), nonnegativity of the second summand in \(g\) gives \(g(h)\geq L\ell^2\).  Since \(t\mapsto t^{-3/2}\) is decreasing and \(t\mapsto t^2\) is increasing, (4) gives
\[
 c_{n,d}\ell^{-3/2}
 \leq c_{n,d}h_0^{-3/2}
 =Lh_0^2
 \leq L\ell^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Evaluating at \(\ell\), we obtain
\[
 Ld^{-2}
 \leq\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}g(h)
 \leq g(\ell)
 \leq2Ld^{-2}.
\tag{6}
\]

Next suppose that \(\ell<h_0<u\).  If \(h\geq h_0\), then \(Lh^2\geq Lh_0^2\); if \(h\leq h_0\), then \(c_{n,d}h^{-3/2}\geq c_{n,d}h_0^{-3/2}=Lh_0^2\).  Therefore \(g(h)\geq Lh_0^2\) throughout \([\ell,u]\), while \(g(h_0)=2Lh_0^2\).  Hence
\[
 Lh_0^2
 \leq\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}g(h)
 \leq2Lh_0^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Equation (3) yields
\[
 Lh_0^2=L^{3/7}n^{-2/7}d^{-1/7}
 =L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{8}
\]

Finally suppose that \(h_0\geq u\).  For every \(h\in[\ell,u]\),
\[
 g(h)\geq c_{n,d}u^{-3/2}
 =n^{-1/2}d^{-1/4}d^{3/4}
 =\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(u\leq h_0\), monotonicity and (4) give
\[
 Lu^2\leq Lh_0^2=c_{n,d}h_0^{-3/2}
 \leq c_{n,d}u^{-3/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Evaluating at \(u\) thus proves
\[
 \sqrt{d/n}
 \leq\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}g(h)
 \leq g(u)
 \leq2\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{11}
\]

By \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\),
\[
 \mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}
 =\sqrt{d/n}+\inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}g(h).
\tag{12}
\]
Adding \(\sqrt{d/n}\) to (6), (7), and (11), and using (8) in the middle case, proves respectively
\[
 \mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}
 \asymp Ld^{-2}+\sqrt{d/n},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}
 \asymp L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}+\sqrt{d/n},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\asymp\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{13}
\]
The comparison constants in (13) may be chosen independently of \(n\) and \(d\).

For the upper half of the decision-theoretic bracket, fix an index for which both \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\ne\varnothing\).  The settled \({\rm thm:honest\text{-}upper}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{h^\dagger}-\theta_n|
 \leq\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{14}
\]
The oracle value \(h^\dagger\) is deterministic at the class level.  The totalized Borel sample map in \({\rm def:empirical\text{-}projector}\), together with \({\rm def:estimator}\), makes \(\widehat\theta_{h^\dagger}\) a real-valued measurable function of the observed data for every finite \(r,n\).  It is therefore admissible in the estimator infimum of \({\rm def:minimax\text{-}criteria}\), and (14) implies
\[
 R^*_{n,d,L}\leq\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{15}
\]

For the interval criterion, introduce the untruncated data-dependent interval
\[
 \mathcal J_{h^\dagger}(D)=
 \left[
 \widehat\theta_{h^\dagger}-\overline b_{h^\dagger}(A)
 -\frac{\overline s_{h^\dagger}(A)}{\sqrt\alpha},
 \widehat\theta_{h^\dagger}+\overline b_{h^\dagger}(A)
 +\frac{\overline s_{h^\dagger}(A)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \right].
\tag{16}
\]
The radii are finite, nonnegative, graph-measurable sample maps by \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) and \({\rm def:radii}\); also \(0<\alpha<1/2\).  Thus (16) is a data-measurable closed interval with finite ordered endpoints, as required by \({\rm def:minimax\text{-}criteria}\).  Since \({\rm def:honest\text{-}interval}\) gives \(\mathcal C_{h^\dagger}\subseteq\mathcal J_{h^\dagger}\), the coverage conclusion of \({\rm thm:honest\text{-}upper}\) implies
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 P\{\theta_n\in\mathcal J_{h^\dagger}(D)\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{17}
\]
Pointwise nonnegativity of the radii gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{length}(\mathcal J_{h^\dagger})
 &=2\{\overline b_{h^\dagger}(A)
 +\alpha^{-1/2}\overline s_{h^\dagger}(A)\}\\
 &\leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}
 \{\overline b_{h^\dagger}(A)+\overline s_{h^\dagger}(A)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Taking expectations and the supremum over the nonempty class, and applying \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\), turns (18) into
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(\mathcal J_{h^\dagger})]
 \leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{19}
\]
By (17), \(\mathcal J_{h^\dagger}\) is admissible in the interval infimum of \({\rm def:minimax\text{-}criteria}\).  Hence
\[
 \Lambda^*_{n,d,L}(\alpha)
 \leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{20}
\]

Along every sequence covered by \({\rm thm:unrestricted\text{-}supported\text{-}converse}\), that theorem supplies a constant \(c>0\), independent of \(n,d\), such that, for every sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R^*_{n,d,L}\geq c\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L},
 \qquad
 \Lambda^*_{n,d,L}(\alpha)
 \geq c\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (15), (20), and (21) are exactly the asserted conservative lower--upper bracket.  Separately, \({\rm thm:conditional\text{-}isolate\text{-}wall}\) proves on every realized graph that any graph-only conditional-bias certificate of the stated uniform kind satisfies
\[
 b(A)\geq\frac{B}{n}
 \sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}.
\tag{22}
\]
The upper bounds (15) and (20) involve the oracle conservative envelope \(\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}\), whereas the lower bounds (21) involve \(\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\).  No declared result proves \(\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}\lesssim\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\).  Therefore (13) is tuning geometry and a minimax lower benchmark, not a sharp minimax rate or an achieved honest expected-length frontier.
