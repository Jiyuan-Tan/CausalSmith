Fix \(A\), the preassignment response functions, and \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  All expectations and variances below are over the independent coordinates of \(Z\), as specified by \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\).  Put
\[
 X=Z-\frac12\mathbf1_n,\qquad P_A=\widehat\Pi_r(A),\qquad C_A=P_AA,
 \qquad F_h(Z)=\widehat\theta_h.
\tag{1}
\]

We first prove that the corrected variance certificate in \({\rm def:pc-handle}\) is valid.  If \(z^{(j)}\) is obtained from \(z\) by flipping coordinate \(j\), the finite maximum defining \(D_{ij,h}\) gives
\[
 |q_{i,h}(z)-q_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|\le D_{ij,h}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the left side is zero when \(A_{ij}=0\); when \(A_{ij}=1\), the treated-neighbor count changes from some \(m\in\{0,\ldots,N_i-1\}\) to \(m+1\), which is exactly one of the pairs in that maximum.  Since \(|X_k|=1/2\), the definitions of \(Q_{i,h}\), \(\Gamma_{i,h}\), and \(\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}\) also give, for every assignment,
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)|\le Q_{i,h}+2\sum_{j'\in\mathcal V_n}|(C_A)_{ij'}|=\Gamma_{i,h}
\tag{3}
\]
and, because flipping \(Z_j\) changes \(X_j\) by one in absolute value,
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)-\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|
 \le D_{ij,h}+4|(C_A)_{ij}|.
\tag{4}
\]

The response-level part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) gives \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)|\le B\).  If \(j=i\), flipping \(Z_j\) changes only the own-treatment argument of unit \(i\), so the two response levels differ by at most \(2B\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), then \(j\ne i\), and the neighbor fraction changes by exactly \(N_i^{-1}\); the derivative part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) and the mean-value theorem give a change of at most \(B/N_i\).  If neither condition holds, \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) makes the observed response unchanged.  Thus, using the zero-denominator convention in \({\rm def:pc-handle}\),
\[
 |Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)-Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \le B\left\{2\mathbf1\{i=j\}+\frac{A_{ij}}{N_i}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For real numbers \(g,g',y,y'\), \(|gy-g'y'|\le |g-g'||y|+|g'||y-y'|\).  Applying this inequality with (3)--(5) proves
\[
 \left|\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)
 -\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})\right|
 \le\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{6}
\]
Summing (6) and using \({\rm def:estimator}\) yields the coordinate oscillation bound
\[
 |F_h(z)-F_h(z^{(j)})|
 \le c_{j,h}:=\frac1n\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, we derive rather than invoke the Boolean variance inequality.  Let \(\mathcal F_j=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_j)\), \(M_j=\mathbb E_Z(F_h\mid\mathcal F_j)\), and \(E_j=M_j-M_{j-1}\), with \(M_0=\mathbb E_ZF_h\).  Conditional centering gives \(\mathbb E_Z(E_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})=0\); hence for \(j<k\),
\[
 \mathbb E_Z(E_jE_k)=\mathbb E_Z\{E_j\mathbb E_Z(E_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1})\}=0.
\tag{8}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(F_h)=\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E_ZE_j^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_{j-1}\), the difference between the two values of \(M_j\) obtained by setting \(Z_j=1\) and \(Z_j=0\) is an average over the future coordinates of differences bounded by (7), and is therefore at most \(c_{j,h}\).  Since \(Z_j\) is Bernoulli one half, its conditional variance is at most \(c_{j,h}^2/4\).  Combining this fact with (9) proves
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \le\frac14\sum_jc_{j,h}^2
 =\frac1{4n^2}\sum_j\left(\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}\right)^2
 =\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 =\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{10}
\]

We next prove the bias certificate.  For \(N_i>0\), \(q_{i,h}\) depends only on neighbor assignments and is independent of \(Z_i\).  By \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) and \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\), conditioning first on \(Z_i=a\) and then averaging over its two equiprobable values gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 -\frac{\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)+\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)}2\right|
 \le\frac L6h_i(h)^2.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(N_i=0\), \(q_{i,h}=0\), while the derivative part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) bounds the absolute target contribution by \(B\).  Consequently the score part has conditional bias at most
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}h_i(h)^2
 +\frac Bn\sum_i\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to control the PC subtraction.  Centering and \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}|\le B\) imply
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|\le B/2.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(j\ne i\) with \(A_{ij}=1\), condition on all coordinates except \(Z_j\).  The same mean-value argument as in (5), followed by the two-point Bernoulli calculation, gives
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|
 =\frac14\left|\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=1]
 -\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=0]\right|
 \le\frac B{4N_i}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(j\ne i\) with \(A_{ij}=0\), the expectation is zero by anonymous interference and independence.  Equations (13)--(14) therefore imply
\[
 \frac4n\sum_i\left|\sum_j(C_A)_{ij}\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right|
 \le\frac{2B}{n}\sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|
 +\frac Bn\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 =\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}.
\tag{15}
\]
Combining (12)--(15) with \({\rm def:target}\), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:radii}\) proves
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\mid A,(f_i)_i)\right|
 \le\overline b_h(A).
\tag{16}
\]

We also prove the displayed deterministic graph-norm bound.  Because \(P_A\) is a spectral projector of the symmetric matrix \(A\), it commutes with \(A\), and \(C_A=P_AA\) is symmetric.  Write its spectral decomposition as \(C_A=\sum_t\nu_tu_tu_t^\top\).  Then
\[
 \sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|
 \le\sum_t|\nu_t|\sum_i u_{t,i}^2
 =\sum_t|\nu_t|=\|C_A\|_*.
\tag{17}
\]
For the edge-restricted term, Cauchy--Schwarz within each positive-degree row and then across rows gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 &\le\sum_{i:N_i>0}
 \left\{\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(C_A)_{ij}^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le\sqrt{nG_A(C_A)}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Substitution of (17)--(18) into the definition of \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}\) proves
\[
 \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \le\frac{2B}{n}\|C_A\|_*+B\sqrt{\frac{G_A(C_A)}n}.
\tag{19}
\]

Conditional Chebyshev applied to (10) and (16) yields
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|>
 \overline b_h(A)+\frac{\overline s_h(A)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A,(f_i)_i\right)\le\alpha.
\tag{20}
\]
The derivative envelope and \({\rm def:target}\) give \(|\theta_n|\le B\), so intersecting with \([-B,B]\) does not remove the target.  Integrating (20) proves the asserted uniform coverage.

Finally, the truncated score for a degree \(N_i\) changes only when \(h\) crosses one of at most \(N_i+1\) lattice distances.  Thus all units together create at most \(n^2\) breakpoints.  Exact finite binomial sums compute \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\); the finite maxima compute \(Q_{i,h}\) and \(D_{ij,h}\); one eigendecomposition computes \(C_A\); and matrix and finite-sum arithmetic compute \(\Gamma_{i,h}\), \(\beta_{ij,h}\), and both certificates.  This is polynomial in \(n\).  By \({\rm lem:exact-score-hoeffding-projections}\),
\[
 q_h^{(1)}=4AX,\qquad q_h^{(2)}=0,
\]
so the first projection of \(q_h-4P_AAX\) is \(4(I_n-P_A)AX\) and its second projection is zero, with neither expression depending on \(h\).  The corrected bounded-difference certificate controls all remaining product terms without asserting the unavailable sharp asymptotic order.  This proves every claim.
