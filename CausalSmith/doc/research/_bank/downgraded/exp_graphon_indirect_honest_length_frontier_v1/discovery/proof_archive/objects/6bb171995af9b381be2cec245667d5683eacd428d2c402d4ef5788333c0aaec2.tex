Work first conditionally on \(A\) and \((f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\).  For every \(i\in\mathcal V_n\), define the proof-intermediate function
\[
g_i(x):=\frac{f_i(1,x)+f_i(0,x)}{2},\qquad x\in[0,1].
\]
Fix \(i\) with \(N_i=m>0\).  By \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-design}\), conditional on \(A\) and the preassignment variables, \(M_i\sim {\rm Binomial}(m,1/2)\), while \(Z_i\) is independent of \(M_i\).  Hence, by \(\texttt{ass:anonymous-interference}\) and \(\texttt{def:li-wager-target}\), the contribution of unit \(i\) to \(\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_i
&:=4\,\mathbb E_Z\!\left[Y_i(Z)(M_i-m/2)\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right]\\
&=4m\,\mathbb E_Z\!\left[g_i(1/2+V_i)V_i\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(m=0\), the definition instead gives \(\beta_i=0\), since \(M_i-N_i/2=0\).  Therefore
\[
\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\beta_i.
\]

For \(m>0\), let \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) be independent variables taking the values \(-1/2\) and \(1/2\) with equal probabilities, and put \(S_m=\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\).  Conditionally on the quantities fixed above, \(V_i\) has the law of \(S_m/m\).  Independence, centering, and \(\mathbb E X_j^2=1/4\), \(\mathbb E X_j^4=1/16\) give
\[
\mathbb E S_m^2=\frac m4,\qquad
\mathbb E S_m^3=0,
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E S_m^4
=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^4
+6\sum_{1\le j<k\le m}\mathbb E X_j^2\mathbb E X_k^2
=\frac{3m^2-2m}{16}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\mathbb E V_i=0,\qquad
\mathbb E V_i^2=\frac1{4m},\qquad
\mathbb E V_i^3=0,\qquad
\mathbb E V_i^4=\frac{3m^2-2m}{16m^4}\le\frac3{16m^2}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:third-derivative-bound}\), the averaging defining \(g_i\) satisfies \(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|g_i^{(3)}(x)|\le L\).  Since \(1/2+V_i\in[0,1]\), Taylor's theorem with its Lagrange remainder yields
\[
g_i(1/2+v)
=g_i(1/2)+g_i'(1/2)v+\frac12g_i''(1/2)v^2+R_i(v),
\qquad |R_i(v)|\le\frac L6|v|^3.
\]
Multiplication by \(v\), conditional expectation, and the four moment identities above show that the constant and quadratic Taylor terms vanish, whereas \(4m\mathbb E V_i^2=1\).  Thus
\[
\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)=4m\,\mathbb E\!\left[V_iR_i(V_i)\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right]
\]
and consequently
\[
|\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
\le \frac{4mL}{6}\mathbb E V_i^4
\le \frac{4mL}{6}\frac3{16m^2}
=\frac{L}{8m}.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:target}\), the unit-level contribution to \(\theta_n\) is \(g_i'(1/2)\).  If \(N_i=0\), \(\texttt{ass:response-envelope}\) gives
\[
|g_i'(1/2)|
\le\frac{|\partial_x f_i(1,1/2)|+|\partial_x f_i(0,1/2)|}{2}
\le B.
\]
Separating nonisolated and isolated vertices and applying the triangle inequality proves the finite-sample assertion:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|
&\le \frac1n\sum_{i:N_i>0}|\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
+\frac1n\sum_{i:N_i=0}|g_i'(1/2)|\\
&\le \frac{L}{8n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
+\frac Bn\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\mathbf 1\{N_i=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the asymptotic assertion, now take a sequence satisfying every hypothesis explicitly stipulated in the proposed statement.  In particular, its latent types are independent with the \({\rm Uniform}[0,1]\) marginal, as required by the Li--Wager random-graph model, and \(\texttt{lem:li-wager-theorem-six-regime}\) supplies
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\rho_n}{\log n}>-\frac12.
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) so that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\rho_n\ge n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:unit-sampling}\), the latent types of distinct units are independent; by the uniform-marginal hypothesis, \(U_j\) has Lebesgue law.  Integrating the conditionally independent edge variables in \(\texttt{ass:graphon-draw}\) over the independent \(U_j\)'s therefore shows that, conditional on \(U_i=u\),
\[
N_i\sim {\rm Binomial}(n-1,p_n(u)),
\qquad
p_n(u)=\rho_n\int_0^1W(u,v)\,dv.
\]
The expression is a valid probability by \(\texttt{ass:edge-probability}\) and \(\texttt{ass:kernel-bound}\).  Moreover, \(\texttt{ass:degree-regularity}\) implies, for Lebesgue-almost every \(u\),
\[
\mu_i(u):=\mathbb E[N_i\mid U_i=u]
=(n-1)p_n(u)
\ge \frac{(n-1)\rho_n}{\kappa}.
\]
Because \(U_i\) is uniform, all realized \(U_i\)'s lie in this full-measure set almost surely.

We next derive the only tail inequality needed.  If \(S\sim{\rm Binomial}(m,p)\), \(\mu=mp\), and \(t=\log2\), exponential Markov's inequality and \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(S\le\mu/2)
&\le e^{t\mu/2}\mathbb E e^{-tS}
=e^{t\mu/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^m\\
&\le \exp\!\left\{\frac{t\mu}{2}-\mu(1-e^{-t})\right\}
=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1-\log2}{2}\mu\right\}
\le e^{-\mu/8}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying this inequality conditionally on each \(U_i\), using the displayed lower bound for \(\mu_i(U_i)\), and then taking a union bound yields, for
\[
\mathcal E_n:=\left\{\min_{i\in\mathcal V_n}N_i\ge\frac{(n-1)\rho_n}{2\kappa}\right\},
\]
the bound
\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal E_n^c)
\le n\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(n-1)\rho_n}{8\kappa}\right\}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
because \(n\rho_n\ge n^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) eventually.  On \(\mathcal E_n\) there are no isolates and the finite-sample inequality gives
\[
\frac{|\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|}{\sqrt{\rho_n}}
\le \frac{L\kappa}{4(n-1)\rho_n^{3/2}}.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero, since
\[
(n-1)\rho_n^{3/2}
\ge (1-o(1))n^{1/4+3\varepsilon/2}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The constants \(L\) and \(\kappa\) are fixed class constants.  Combining the last two displays with \(\mathbb P(\mathcal E_n^c)\to0\) proves
\[
\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n=o_P(\sqrt{\rho_n}).
\]
Finally, \(\texttt{lem:li-wager-theorem-six-regime}\) records that the published PC-balancing Gaussian theorem is centered at \(\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}\) on this same scale.  Hence the displayed \(o_P(\sqrt{\rho_n})\) comparison makes that theorem an asymptotic benchmark for \(\theta_n\) along the stated sequences.  The two finite-sample causal targets remain distinct by \(\texttt{def:target}\) and \(\texttt{def:li-wager-target}\), as the degree-exact inequality itself makes explicit.
