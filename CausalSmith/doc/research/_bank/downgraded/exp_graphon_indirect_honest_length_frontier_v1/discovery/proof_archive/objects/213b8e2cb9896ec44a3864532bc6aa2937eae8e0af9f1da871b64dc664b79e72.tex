Put \(m_{r,n}=\min\{r,n\}\).  Order the \(n\) eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix \(A\) by decreasing absolute value, breaking equal-absolute-value ties by decreasing signed value.  Within every remaining repeated eigenspace, choose the orthonormal basis obtained by applying Gram--Schmidt to the nonzero vectors in the ordered list of projections of \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) onto that eigenspace.  Let \(\widehat\Psi_r(A)\in\mathbb R^{n\times m_{r,n}}\) contain the first \(m_{r,n}\) resulting orthonormal eigenvectors and define
\[
 \widehat\Pi_r(A)=\widehat\Psi_r(A)\widehat\Psi_r(A)^\top.
\]
Thus \(\widehat\Pi_r(A)\) is the orthogonal spectral projector onto the leading \(\min\{r,n\}\)-dimensional eigenspace selected by this convention.  The deterministic tie rule makes \(A\mapsto\widehat\Pi_r(A)\) a fixed Borel-measurable sample map for every \(r,n\in\mathbb N\), without requiring \(r\leq n\).
