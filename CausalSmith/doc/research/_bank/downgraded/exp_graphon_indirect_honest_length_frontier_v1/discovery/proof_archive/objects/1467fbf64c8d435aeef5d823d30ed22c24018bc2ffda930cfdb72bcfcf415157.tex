Fix a sufficiently large triangular-array index for which \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\neq\varnothing\), and select the kernel and spectral data of one member.  Throughout the proof, all constants are independent of \(n\) and \(d\); they may depend on the fixed constants displayed in the theorem.

\textit{Step 1: an exact Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi submodel obtained from a null row.}
Let \(T_W\) be the integral operator represented by \(W\), and define
\[
 b_k=\int_0^1\psi_k(u)\,du,\qquad 1\leq k\leq r.
\]
The integrals exist by Cauchy--Schwarz and \({\rm ass:eigenfunction\text{-}orthonormality}\).  Integrating the finite expansion in \({\rm ass:finite\text{-}rank}\) and using \({\rm ass:degree\text{-}regularity}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^r\lambda_kb_k^2
 =\int_0^1\!\int_0^1W(u,v)\,du\,dv
 \geq\kappa^{-1}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus some eigenvalue is positive; fix \(k_+\) such that \(\lambda_{k_+}>0\).  Choose \(u_0\in[0,1]\), replace the eigenfunction representatives only at \(u_0\) by
\[
 \psi_{k_+}^*(u_0)=\lambda_{k_+}^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \psi_k^*(u_0)=0\quad(k\neq k_+),
 \qquad
 \psi_k^*(u)=\psi_k(u)\quad(u\neq u_0),
\tag{2}
\]
and set
\[
 W^*(u,v)=\sum_{k=1}^r\lambda_k\psi_k^*(u)\psi_k^*(v).
\tag{3}
\]
The modification is measurable and changes \(W\) only on
\(\{u_0\}\times[0,1]\cup[0,1]\times\{u_0\}\), a set of two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero.  Consequently \(W^*=W\) in \(L^2([0,1]^2)\); hence its integral operator, exact rank, nonzero eigenvalues, and the orthonormality of the displayed eigenfunctions are unchanged.  The almost-everywhere conditions \({\rm ass:kernel\text{-}bound}\) and \({\rm ass:degree\text{-}regularity}\), as well as \({\rm ass:spectral\text{-}strength}\), are also unchanged.  On the exceptional point, however,
\[
 W^*(u_0,u_0)=\lambda_{k_+}\lambda_{k_+}^{-1}=1.
\tag{4}
\]

Take \(U_i=u_0\) almost surely, independently over \(i\).  Each centered random variable \(\psi_k^*(U_i)-\mathbb E\psi_k^*(U_i)\) is zero, so the Bernstein moment condition in \({\rm ass:eigenfunction\text{-}bernstein}\) holds with the prescribed positive parameter \(b_\psi\).  By \({\rm ass:graphon\text{-}draw}\), (4), and \(\rho_n=d/n\),
\[
 A_{ij}\overset{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}{\rm Bernoulli}(p_n),
 \qquad p_n=d/n,
 \qquad i<j.
\tag{5}
\]
The probability is in \((0,1)\) because \(0<d<n\).  Thus the atomic latent-type law satisfies \({\rm ass:unit\text{-}sampling}\), and (2)--(5), together with \({\rm ass:bernoulli\text{-}design}\), satisfy every graph, rank, support, and design member-property in \({\rm def:model\text{-}class}\).  This construction uses only nonemptiness of the original class to obtain one admissible integral operator; no block-compatibility inequality is used.

\textit{Step 2: a common two-point distance calculation.}
Let \(s_0=B/4\), and let \((\xi_i)_{i=1}^n\), independently of \((U,A,Z)\), be independent with density
\[
 p_{s_0}(t)=s_0^{-1}\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2s_0}\right)
 \mathbf 1\{|t|\leq s_0\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The change of variable \(y=\pi t/(2s_0)\) shows that (6) integrates to one.  For a deterministic \(g\in C^3[0,1]\), define two response laws by
\[
 f_i^\pm(a,x)=\xi_i\pm g(x),
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\},\quad x\in[0,1].
\tag{7}
\]
If
\[
 \|g\|_\infty\leq 3B/4,
 \qquad
 \max_{1\leq j\leq2}\|g^{(j)}\|_\infty\leq B,
 \qquad
 \|g^{(3)}\|_\infty\leq L,
\tag{8}
\]
then \(|\xi_i|\leq B/4\), (7), and (8) verify \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) and \({\rm ass:third\text{-}derivative\text{-}bound}\).  The pairs \((U_i,f_i^\pm)\) are independent and identically distributed, as required by \({\rm ass:unit\text{-}sampling}\), and \({\rm ass:anonymous\text{-}interference}\) defines the corresponding potential outcomes.  Hence both signs give laws in \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\).  By \({\rm def:target}\),
\[
 \theta_n^\pm=\pm g'(1/2),
 \qquad
 \Delta_g:=|\theta_n^+-\theta_n^-|=2|g'(1/2)|.
\tag{9}
\]

Write \(q_{s_0}=\sqrt{p_{s_0}}\), extended by zero outside \([-s_0,s_0]\).  Because the cosine vanishes at both endpoints, \(q_{s_0}\) is absolutely continuous, and direct integration yields
\[
 \|q_{s_0}'\|_2^2=\frac{\pi^2}{4s_0^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
For translations \(\eta_1,\eta_2\), the identity
\(q_{s_0}(t-\eta_1)-q_{s_0}(t-\eta_2)
=-\int_{\eta_2}^{\eta_1}q_{s_0}'(t-a)\,da\) and the triangle inequality in \(L^2\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^2\{p_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_1),p_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_2)\}
 &=\|q_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_1)-q_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_2)\|_2^2\\
 &\leq(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2\|q_{s_0}'\|_2^2
 =\frac{\pi^2(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2}{4s_0^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(H^2(p,q)=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\).  Define the observed exposure
\[
 X_i=\begin{cases}1/2+V_i,&N_i>0,\\0,&N_i=0.\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]
Conditional on \((A,Z)\), the outcomes under (7) are independent translates of (6), and the two translations for unit \(i\) differ by \(2g(X_i)\).  For product densities, affinity multiplies; since
\(H^2=2(1-\text{affinity})\) and \(1-\prod_i(1-x_i)\leq\sum_i x_i\) for \(0\leq x_i\leq1\), equation (11) gives, after averaging over the common \((A,Z)\) marginal,
\[
 H^2(Q_g^+,Q_g^-)
 \leq\frac{\pi^2}{s_0^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^ng(X_i)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Finally, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_g^+,Q_g^-)
 \leq H(Q_g^+,Q_g^-).
\tag{14}
\]

\textit{Step 3: degree and exposure-lattice estimates.}
Under (5),
\[
 N_i\sim{\rm Binomial}(n-1,p_n),
 \qquad
 \mu_n:=\mathbb EN_i=(n-1)d/n.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(n\geq2\), \(d/2\leq\mu_n\leq d\).  Conditional on \(N_i=N>0\), \({\rm ass:bernoulli\text{-}design}\) gives \(M_i\sim{\rm Binomial}(N,1/2)\), and therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_Z(V_i^2\mid A,N_i=N)=\frac1{4N}.
\tag{16}
\]
Expanding the binomial law and using \({n-1\choose k}/(k+1)={n\choose k+1}/n\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{N_i+1}
 =\frac{1-(1-p_n)^n}{np_n}leq\frac1d.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(N^{-1}\leq2(N+1)^{-1}\) for \(N\geq1\), (16)--(17) imply
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^nV_i^2\mathbf1\{N_i>0\}
 \leq\frac n{2d}.
\tag{18}
\]
Also,
\[
 n\Pr(N_i=0)=n(1-d/n)^{n-1}
 \leq ne^{-\mu_n}\leq ne^{-d/2}.
\tag{19}
\]

We next prove a local binomial-mass bound.  For \(N=2q\), put
\(a_q={2q\choose q}4^{-q}\).  Then
\[
 \frac{a_{q+1}}{a_q}=\frac{2q+1}{2q+2},
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{2q+1}{2q+2}\right)^2\leq\frac{q+1}{q+2}.
\tag{20}
\]
Induction from \(a_0=1\) yields \(a_q\leq(q+1)^{-1/2}\); comparison with the adjacent even case gives the same order for odd \(N\).  Thus every atom of \({\rm Binomial}(N,1/2)\) is at most \(C_0N^{-1/2}\).  Since \(|M/N-1/2|\leq h\) contains at most \(2Nh+2\) integer values,
\[
 \Pr\{|M/N-1/2|\leq h\mid N\}
 \leq C_1(\sqrt N\,h+N^{-1/2}).
\tag{21}
\]
For completeness, exponential Markov with \(t=\log2\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N_i<\mu_n/2)
 \leq e^{t\mu_n/2}(1-p_n+p_ne^{-t})^{n-1}
 \leq\exp\{\mu_n(t/2+e^{-t}-1)\}
 \leq e^{-c_0\mu_n}
\tag{22}
\]
for a universal \(c_0>0\).  Splitting at \(N_i=\mu_n/2\), and using \(N_i^{-1/2}\leq1\) when \(N_i>0\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E\{N_i^{-1/2}\mathbf1(N_i>0)\}
 \leq\sqrt{2/\mu_n}+e^{-c_0\mu_n}
 \leq C_2d^{-1/2}.
\tag{23}
\]
Moreover, concavity gives \(\mathbb E\sqrt{N_i}\leq\sqrt{\mu_n}\leq\sqrt d\).  Combining (21)--(23) and \(h\geq d^{-1}\) from \({\rm def:bandwidth\text{-}set}\), we obtain, uniformly over \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\),
\[
 \Pr(|V_i|\leq h,N_i>0)\leq C_3\sqrt d\,h.
\tag{24}
\]

Choose a fixed \(K>e\) so large that
\(a_K:=K\log(K/e)>2/C_{\log}\), and put \(D_n=\lceil Kd\rceil\).  Exponential Markov optimized at \(t=\log(D_n/\mu_n)>0\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N_i>D_n)
 \leq\left(\frac{e\mu_n}{D_n}\right)^{D_n}
 \leq e^{-a_Kd}.
\tag{25}
\]
The union bound and \({\rm ass:log\text{-}degree}\) therefore imply
\[
 \Pr\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}N_i>D_n\right)
 \leq ne^{-a_Kd}
 \leq n^{1-a_KC_{\log}}=o(1).
\tag{26}
\]

\textit{Step 4: the linear graph-floor pair.}
Define
\[
 g_G(x)=c_GB\sqrt{d/n}\,(x-1/2).
\tag{27}
\]
Because \({\rm ass:sparse\text{-}limit}\) gives \(d/n\to0\), (8) holds for all sufficiently large \(n\) when \(0<c_G\leq1\).  Equations (12), (18), (19), and (27) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^ng_G(X_i)^2
 &=c_G^2B^2\frac dn
 \left\{\mathbb E\sum_iV_i^2\mathbf1(N_i>0)
       +\frac n4\Pr(N_i=0)\right\}\\
 &\leq c_G^2B^2\left\{\frac12+\frac d4e^{-d/2}\right\}
 \leq C_4c_G^2B^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Set
\[
 \eta=(1-2\alpha)/2>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Because \(s_0=B/4\), (13), (14), and (28) permit a fixed choice of \(c_G>0\) for which
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_G^+,Q_G^-)\leq\eta.
\tag{30}
\]
The target separation is, by (9),
\[
 \Delta_G=2c_GB\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{31}
\]

\textit{Step 5: the local \(C^3\)-bump pair.}
Suppose \(L>0\), and define
\[
 \phi(t)=t(1-4t^2)^4\mathbf1\{|t|<1/2\}.
\tag{32}
\]
The fourth-order zeros at the endpoints make the zero extension in (32) a \(C^3(\mathbb R)\) function, and \(\phi'(0)=1\).  Let \(C_j=\|\phi^{(j)}\|_\infty\) for \(0\leq j\leq3\), and define, for \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\),
\[
 g_h(x)=c_ILh^3\phi\!\left(\frac{x-1/2}{h}\right).
\tag{33}
\]
Then
\[
 \|g_h^{(j)}\|_\infty=c_ILh^{3-j}C_j,
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq3.
\tag{34}
\]
Choose \(c_IC_3\leq1\).  The lower-degree assumption implies \(d\to\infty\), while \(h\leq d^{-1/2}\); hence (34), with fixed \(B,L\), verifies (8) for every sufficiently large \(n\).  The function is zero at the isolate exposure \(0\), and its nonzero support is contained in \(|V_i|\leq h\).  Equations (13), (24), and (33) therefore give
\[
 H^2(Q_h^+,Q_h^-)
 \leq C_5c_I^2n\sqrt d\,L^2h^7.
\tag{35}
\]
Put
\[
 h_0=\{L^{-2}/(n\sqrt d)\}^{1/7}.
\tag{36}
\]
If \(d^{-1}<h_0<d^{-1/2}\), substitution in (35) and a fixed further decrease of \(c_I\) give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_{h_0}^+,Q_{h_0}^-)\leq\eta.
\tag{37}
\]
By (9), (32), and (33),
\[
 \Delta_I=2c_ILh_0^2
 =2c_IL^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{38}
\]

\textit{Step 6: the degree-lattice bump pair.}
Continue to suppose \(L>0\).  Let \(w_n=(4D_n)^{-1}\), and set
\[
 g_D(x)=c_DLw_n^3\phi\!\left(\frac{x-1/2}{w_n}\right),
\tag{39}
\]
where \(c_DC_3\leq1\).  Since \(D_n\to\infty\), the analogue of (34) verifies (8) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  If \(1\leq N_i\leq D_n\), every noncentral point on the exposure lattice satisfies
\[
 |V_i|\geq
 \begin{cases}N_i^{-1},&N_i\text{ is even},\\(2N_i)^{-1},&N_i\text{ is odd},\end{cases}
 \geq(2D_n)^{-1}>w_n.
\tag{40}
\]
At the only possible central point, oddness gives \(g_D(1/2)=0\), and \(g_D(0)=0\) for isolates.  Hence, on
\(E_n=\{\max_iN_i\leq D_n\}\), the complete observed outcomes under both signs in (7) equal \(\xi_i\).  The restrictions of the two data laws to \(E_n\) are identical, so (26) implies
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_D^+,Q_D^-)
 \leq\Pr(E_n^c)=o(1)\leq\eta
\tag{41}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  On the other hand, (9) and (39) give
\[
 \Delta_D=2c_DLw_n^2\asymp Ld^{-2},
\tag{42}
\]
where the two comparison constants depend only on the fixed \(K\).

\textit{Step 7: reduction to absolute risk and honest expected length.}
For any one of the preceding pairs, abbreviate the target values by \(\theta^+,\theta^-\), the data laws by \(Q^+,Q^-\), and their separation by \(\Delta\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), let \(A_T=\{|T-\theta^+|\geq\Delta/2\}\).  On \(A_T^c\), the triangle inequality gives \(|T-\theta^-|\geq\Delta/2\).  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q^+}|T-\theta^+|+\mathbb E_{Q^-}|T-\theta^-|
 &\geq\frac\Delta2\{Q^+(A_T)+Q^-(A_T^c)\}\\
 &\geq\frac\Delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
Thus the larger of the two risks is at least \(\Delta(1-\eta)/4\) whenever the pairwise total variation is at most \(\eta\).

Let \(\mathcal C\) be any interval-valued procedure admissible in the honest-length criterion of \({\rm def:minimax\text{-}criteria}\).  Uniform coverage and total variation imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q^+\{\theta^+,\theta^-\in\mathcal C\}
 &\geq Q^+\{\theta^+\in\mathcal C\}
      +Q^+\{\theta^-\in\mathcal C\}-1\\
 &\geq(1-\alpha)+(1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-))-1\\
 &\geq1-2\alpha-\eta=(1-2\alpha)/2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
An interval containing both target values has length at least \(\Delta\), and consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q^+}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C)
 \geq\Delta(1-2\alpha)/2.
\tag{45}
\]
Taking the infima in \({\rm def:minimax\text{-}criteria}\), (30)--(31), (37)--(38), and (41)--(42) show that both minimax criteria are bounded below, up to fixed positive constants, by \(\sqrt{d/n}\), by \(L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}\) when (36) is interior, and by \(Ld^{-2}\), respectively.

\textit{Step 8: comparison with the supported envelope.}
Let
\[
 a_n=(n\sqrt d)^{-1/2}.
\]
For \(L>0\), (36) gives
\[
 Lh_0^2=a_nh_0^{-3/2}=L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{46}
\]
If \(h_0\leq d^{-1}\), then \(L^2\geq d^{13/2}/n\), so
\(a_nd^{3/2}=d^{5/4}n^{-1/2}\leq Ld^{-2}\).  Evaluation at \(h=d^{-1}\) yields
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2Ld^{-2}.
\tag{47}
\]
If \(d^{-1}<h_0<d^{-1/2}\), evaluation at \(h_0\) and (46) give
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{48}
\]
If \(h_0\geq d^{-1/2}\), then \(L^2\leq d^3/n\), whence \(Ld^{-1}\leq\sqrt{d/n}\); at \(h=d^{-1/2}\), the stochastic term also equals \(\sqrt{d/n}\).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{49}
\]
When \(L=0\), the infimum is attained at \(d^{-1/2}\) and equals \(\sqrt{d/n}\).

In the regime of (47), combine the graph and lattice pairs; in the regime of (48), combine the graph and interior pairs; and in the regime of (49), or when \(L=0\), use the graph pair.  If a minimax criterion is at least \(c_1x\) and at least \(c_2y\), then it is at least \(\min(c_1,c_2)(x+y)/2\).  Thus (31), (38), (42), and (47)--(49) yield a fixed \(c>0\) such that
\[
 R^*_{n,d,L}\geq c\left[\sqrt{d/n}+
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+(n\sqrt d\,h^3)^{-1/2}\}\right],
\tag{50}
\]
and the identical bound holds with \(R^*_{n,d,L}\) replaced by \(\Lambda^*_{n,d,L}(\alpha)\).  The bracket in (50) is \(\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\) by \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\).  The constructed laws obey all member-properties of \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\), and every division above has a positive denominator because \(B>0\), \(d>0\), \(L>0\) in Steps 5--6, and \(N_i>0\) wherever \(N_i^{-1}\) occurs.  This proves both asserted inequalities and the stated unrestricted-submodel certificate.
