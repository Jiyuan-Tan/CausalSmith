The frozen amplitude misses a constraint already present in \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\).  To see this, let \(g\) be the difference used by a lattice-hidden response perturbation and suppose that it is invisible at the three exposure points \(1/2-\delta\), \(1/2\), and \(1/2+\delta\).  Thus
\[
 g(1/2-\delta)=g(1/2)=g(1/2+\delta)=0.
\]
Writing \(a=g'(1/2)\), Taylor's integral formula on the right gives
\[
 0=\delta a+\int_0^\delta(\delta-t)g''(1/2+t)\,dt,
\]
and hence, when \(\|g''\|_\infty\leq C B\),
\[
 |a|\leq \frac{CB}{2}\delta.
\]
Subtracting the third-order Taylor formulas at \(\pm\delta\) cancels the value and second-derivative terms and, when \(\|g'''\|_\infty\leq C L\), gives
\[
 |a|\leq \frac{CL}{6}\delta^2.
\]
For every degree \(N\leq m\), the closest nonzero exposure points have distance at least \((2m)^{-1}\) from \(1/2\); a bump supported strictly between them has width proportional to \(m^{-1}\).  Conversely, scaling any fixed odd \(C^3\) bump of unit support by
\[
 g_m(x)=c\,a_m m^{-1}\phi\bigl(m(x-1/2)\bigr)
\]
with a sufficiently small universal \(c>0\) shows that the simultaneous bounds on the first three derivatives are met when
\[
 a_m\asymp \min\{Bm^{-1},Lm^{-2}\}.
\]
Thus the second-derivative envelope forces the missing \(Bm^{-1}\) term.  The case \(m=0\) remains \(a_0=B\), because an isolated unit has no neighbor-exposure observation.
