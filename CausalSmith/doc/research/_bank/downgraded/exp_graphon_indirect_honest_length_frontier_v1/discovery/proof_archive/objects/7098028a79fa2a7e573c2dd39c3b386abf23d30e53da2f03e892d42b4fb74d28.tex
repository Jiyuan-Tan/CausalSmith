Work throughout conditionally on \(A\) and \((f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\).  Define the proof-intermediate function
\[
g_i(x):=\frac{f_i(1,x)+f_i(0,x)}{2},\qquad x\in[0,1].
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-design}\), if \(N_i=m>0\), then \(M_i\sim {\rm Binomial}(m,1/2)\), \(Z_i\) is independent of \(M_i\), and
\[
V_i=\frac{M_i-m/2}{m}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:anonymous-interference}\), \(\texttt{def:li-wager-target}\), and the preceding independence, the contribution of unit \(i\) to \(\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}\) is
\[
\beta_i
:=4\,\mathbb E_Z\!\left[Y_i(Z)(M_i-m/2)\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right]
=4m\,\mathbb E_Z\!\left[g_i(1/2+V_i)V_i\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right].
\]
For \(m=0\), the same definition gives \(\beta_i=0\), because then \(M_i-N_i/2=0\).  Thus
\[
\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\beta_i.
\]

For \(m>0\), write \(S_m=\sum_{j=1}^m X_j\), where the \(X_j\) are independent and take the values \(-1/2\) and \(1/2\) with equal probability.  Then \(V_i\) has the same conditional law as \(S_m/m\).  Independence and centering give
\[
\mathbb E S_m^2=\frac m4,qquad
\mathbb E S_m^3=0,qquad
\mathbb E S_m^4
=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E X_j^4
+6\sum_{1\le j<k\le m}\mathbb E X_j^2\,\mathbb E X_k^2
=\frac{3m^2-2m}{16}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E V_i=0,qquad
\mathbb E V_i^2=\frac1{4m},qquad
\mathbb E V_i^3=0,qquad
\mathbb E V_i^4=\frac{3m^2-2m}{16m^4}\le \frac{3}{16m^2}.
\]
The averaging defining \(g_i\), together with \(\texttt{ass:third-derivative-bound}\), yields
\[
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|g_i^{(3)}(x)|\le L.
\]
Taylor's formula on \([0,1]\), applicable because \(1/2+V_i\in[0,1]\), therefore gives
\[
g_i(1/2+v)
=g_i(1/2)+g_i'(1/2)v+\frac12g_i''(1/2)v^2+R_i(v),
\qquad |R_i(v)|\le\frac L6|v|^3.
\]
Multiplying by \(v\), taking the conditional expectation, and using the four displayed moments gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)
&=4m\,\mathbb E\!\left[V_iR_i(V_i)\mid A,(f_j)_{j\in\mathcal V_n}\right],\\
|\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
&\le \frac{4mL}{6}\mathbb E V_i^4
\le \frac{4mL}{6}\frac{3}{16m^2}
=\frac{L}{8m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the coefficient of \(g_i'(1/2)\) is exactly \(4m\mathbb E V_i^2=1\), while the constant and quadratic Taylor terms vanish because \(\mathbb E V_i=\mathbb E V_i^3=0\).

By \(\texttt{def:target}\), the contribution of unit \(i\) to \(\theta_n\) is \(g_i'(1/2)\).  If \(N_i=0\), \(\texttt{ass:response-envelope}\) gives
\[
|g_i'(1/2)|
\le \frac{|\partial_x f_i(1,1/2)|+|\partial_x f_i(0,1/2)|}{2}
\le B.
\]
The triangle inequality, separated over nonisolated and isolated vertices, now proves the finite-sample claim:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|
&\le \frac1n\sum_{i:N_i>0}|\beta_i-g_i'(1/2)|
+\frac1n\sum_{i:N_i=0}|g_i'(1/2)|\\
&\le \frac{L}{8n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}
+\frac Bn\sum_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\mathbf 1\{N_i=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to verify the stated scale comparison.  The conditions referred to as the Li--Wager Theorem~6 regime are recorded in \(\texttt{lem:li-wager-theorem-six-regime}\).  In particular, there is \(\varepsilon>0\) such that, eventually,
\[
\rho_n\ge n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.
\]
Indeed, this follows directly from \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\log(\rho_n)/\log(n)>-1/2\).  In the same regime, conditional on \(U_i=u\), the degree is binomial with \(n-1\) trials and success probability
\[
p_n(u)=\int_0^1\min\{1,\rho_nW(u,v)\}\,dv.
\]
Because \(0<\rho_n\le1\) and \(W\ge0\),
\[
\min\{1,\rho_nW(u,v)\}\ge \rho_n\min\{1,W(u,v)\}.
\]
The uniformly positive row-integral condition in that regime therefore supplies a constant \(c_{\rm deg}>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\mu_i(u):=\mathbb E[N_i\mid U_i=u]=(n-1)p_n(u)\ge c_{\rm deg}(n-1)\rho_n.
\]
For completeness, the needed lower-tail bound follows directly from exponential Markov.  If \(S\sim{\rm Binomial}(m,p)\), \(\mu=mp\), and \(t=\log 2\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(S\le\mu/2)
&\le e^{t\mu/2}\mathbb E e^{-tS}
=e^{t\mu/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^m\\
&\le \exp\!\left\{\frac{t\mu}{2}-\mu(1-e^{-t})\right\}
\le e^{-\mu/8},
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) and \((1-\log 2)/2\ge1/8\) justify the last two inequalities.  Applying this bound conditionally on each \(U_i\) and then using the union bound yields, for
\[
\mathcal E_n:=\left\{\min_{i\in\mathcal V_n}N_i\ge \frac{c_{\rm deg}}2(n-1)\rho_n\right\},
\]
the probability bound
\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal E_n^c)
\le n\exp\!\left\{-\frac{c_{\rm deg}}8(n-1)\rho_n\right\}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
because \(n\rho_n\ge n^{1/2+\varepsilon}\).  On \(\mathcal E_n\) there are no isolates, and the finite-sample inequality already proved implies
\[
\frac{|\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|}{\sqrt{\rho_n}}
\le \frac{L}{4c_{\rm deg}(n-1)\rho_n^{3/2}}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
since
\[
(n-1)\rho_n^{3/2}\ge (1-o(1))n^{1/4+3\varepsilon/2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The radii \(B\) and \(L\) are fixed model-class constants, as declared in the frozen core.  Hence, for every \(\eta>0\), the event in the preceding display is eventually contained in \(\{|\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n|/\sqrt{\rho_n}\le\eta\}\), while \(\mathbb P(\mathcal E_n^c)\to0\).  This is precisely
\[
\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}-\theta_n=o_P(\sqrt{\rho_n}).
\]
The cited theorem is centered at \(\bar\tau_{\mathrm{IND},n}\) on the \(\sqrt{\rho_n}\) scale.  The last display makes its centering asymptotically compatible with \(\theta_n\) at that scale, while \(\texttt{def:target}\) and \(\texttt{def:li-wager-target}\) remain distinct finite-sample causal functionals.  This proves the final interpretation in the proposition.
