Fix first an index for which \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\ne\varnothing\), and define
\[
 J(h)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P[\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)],
 \qquad h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}.
\tag{1}
\]
We verify that the oracle optimizer exists.  By \({\rm def:bandwidth\text{-}set}\), the nonempty set \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\) is a closed bounded interval.  At fixed \(n\), the graph sample space is finite.  For every realized graph, positivity of every positive-degree normalizer follows from \({\rm lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}moments}\), and all sums and finite maxima in \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) are therefore finite.  The response envelope makes the remaining terms in \({\rm def:radii}\) finite.  Taking the maximum over the finite graph sample space produces a finite deterministic upper bound uniform over \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\), so \(J(h)<\infty\).

Across all possible degrees \(1,\ldots,n-1\), the score indicators and finite maxima in \({\rm def:pc\text{-}handle}\) have finitely many common bandwidth breakpoints.  Between successive breakpoints every score-dependent certificate is constant, while each \(h_i(h)^2\) in the Taylor term of \({\rm def:radii}\) is continuous and nondecreasing.  Thus, on each half-closed cell beginning at an included breakpoint, \(\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)\) is nondecreasing for every \(A\).  Expectation under each \(P\), followed by the nonempty-class supremum in (1), preserves this monotonicity.  The minimum of \(J\) is therefore attained among the finitely many included left endpoints and the terminal endpoint.  Its argmin is nonempty and finite, so its smallest element exists.  This proves that \(h^\dagger\) in \({\rm def:exact\text{-}optimal\text{-}bandwidth}\) is well defined.  Because (1) depends on the full class rather than only on the realized data, \(h^\dagger\) is an oracle class-level tuning value.

Now fix any prespecified \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\), any \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\), and put
\[
 \mathcal G=\sigma\!\left(A,(f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\right),
 \qquad E_h=\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n,
 \qquad m_h=\mathbb E_P(E_h\mid\mathcal G).
\tag{2}
\]
By \({\rm thm:explicit\text{-}pc\text{-}conditional\text{-}certificate}\),
\[
 |m_h|\leq\overline b_h(A),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\{(E_h-m_h)^2\mid\mathcal G\}
 \leq\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(\overline s_h(A)>0\), the pointwise indicator inequality
\[
 \mathbf1\{|E_h-m_h|>\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha\}
 \leq\frac{\alpha(E_h-m_h)^2}{\overline s_h(A)^2}
\]
and (3) show that the conditional probability of the event on the left is at most \(\alpha\).  If \(\overline s_h(A)=0\), the second part of (3) gives \(E_h=m_h\) conditionally almost surely.  In both cases, the triangle inequality and the first part of (3) yield
\[
 \Pr_Z\!\left\{
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|
 \leq\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal G\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
By \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) and \({\rm def:target}\), \(|\theta_n|\leq B\).  Hence intersection with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove \(\theta_n\) on the event in (4), proving the stated finite-sample conditional coverage.  Polynomial-time reportability follows from \({\rm thm:explicit\text{-}pc\text{-}conditional\text{-}certificate}\), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:honest\text{-}interval}\).  The last definition shows directly that, before intersection, the pointwise half-length equals
\[
 \overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha.
\tag{5}
\]
Every realized degree and every isolate occurs explicitly in \({\rm def:radii}\).  Moreover, nonemptiness of \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\) implies \(d^{-1}\leq1/2\) and \(d^{-1}\leq d^{-1/2}\), so \({\rm def:bandwidth\text{-}set}\) gives \(d^{-1}\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  This proves the operational prespecified-bandwidth clause.  It supplies no identity between that endpoint and the class-level minimizer \(h^\dagger\).

For the oracle integrated bound, the nonnegative conditional variance of \(|E_h-m_h|\) gives
\[
 \{\mathbb E_P(|E_h-m_h|\mid\mathcal G)\}^2
 \leq\mathbb E_P\{(E_h-m_h)^2\mid\mathcal G\}.
\]
Thus (3) and the triangle inequality imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P(|E_h|\mid\mathcal G)
 \leq |m_h|+\mathbb E_P(|E_h-m_h|\mid\mathcal G)
 \leq\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A).
\tag{6}
\]
Taking expectations, setting \(h=h^\dagger\), taking the supremum over the nonempty class, and using \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{h^\dagger}-\theta_n|
 \leq\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{7}
\]
Integrating (4) at the deterministic class-level value \(h^\dagger\) gives
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 P\{\theta_n\in\mathcal C_{h^\dagger}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{8}
\]
Intersection with \([-B,B]\) can only shorten the untruncated interval, and (5) therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_h)
 \leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}
 \{\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Taking expectations and the class supremum at \(h=h^\dagger\), then applying \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\), proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_{h^\dagger})]
 \leq2\max\{1,\alpha^{-1/2}\}\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) are the operational conditional certificate, while (7)--(10) are integrated bounds at an oracle class-level minimizer.  No step proves a bandwidth oracle inequality or compares \(\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}\) with \(\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\).
