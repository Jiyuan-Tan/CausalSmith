Write
\[
 a_\alpha=\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}.
\tag{1}
\]
The maintained restriction \(0<\alpha<1/2\) implies \(a_\alpha>1\).  Fix first an index for which \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\ne\varnothing\).  By \({\rm def:bandwidth\text{-}set}\),
\[
 \mathcal H_{n,d}=[\ell,u],\qquad
 \ell=d^{-1},\qquad u=\min\{d^{-1/2},1/2\},
 \qquad 0<\ell\leq u\leq1/2.
\tag{2}
\]

We prove the pointwise candidate inequality that drives the operational result.  For a fixed realized graph, list the distinct points of \(\mathcal K(A)\) from \({\rm def:operational\text{-}bandwidth}\) as
\[
 \ell=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_K=u,
\tag{3}
\]
with the evident single-point interpretation if \(\ell=u\).  Every indicator entering \(q_{i,h}\), \(Q_{i,h}\), or \(D_{ij,h}\) has the form
\[
 \mathbf1\{|m/N_i-1/2|\leq\max(h,\delta_{N_i})\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Because the inequality in (4) is weak, all score-dependent terms are constant on each half-closed cell \([t_k,t_{k+1})\).  The matrix \(C_A\), the isolate term, and \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}\) are bandwidth independent; \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}\), and hence \(\overline s_h(A)\), is constant on such a cell.  The only varying term is
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}\max\{h,\delta_{N_i}\}^2,
\tag{5}
\]
which is nondecreasing because \(L\geq0\).  Hence, for every \(h\in[t_k,t_{k+1})\),
\[
 \overline b_{t_k}(A)+\overline s_{t_k}(A)
 \leq\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A).
\tag{6}
\]
The terminal point \(u\) is itself a candidate.  Minimization over \(\mathcal K(A)\), followed by (6), proves the pointwise inequality
\[
 \overline b_{\widehat h(A)}(A)+\overline s_{\widehat h(A)}(A)
 \leq\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)
 \qquad\text{for every }h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}.
\tag{7}
\]
The existence, Borel measurability, and polynomial-time computation of \(\widehat h(A)\) were proved in \({\rm thm:explicit\text{-}pc\text{-}conditional\text{-}certificate}\).

Now assume also that \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\ne\varnothing\), and define
\[
 J(h)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P[\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)].
\tag{8}
\]
We verify that the class-level comparison bandwidth \(h^\dagger\) exists.  Let
\[
 \mathcal K_n=\{\ell,u\}\cup
 \left\{\left|\frac mN-\frac12\right|:
 1\leq N\leq n-1,\ 0\leq m\leq N,\
 \ell\leq\left|\frac mN-\frac12\right|\leq u\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This deterministic set is finite and contains every possible graph-specific breakpoint.  The positive normalizers from \({\rm lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}moments}\), the finite graph sample space, and the finite formulas in \({\rm def:radii}\) imply \(0\leq J(h)<\infty\) for every \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  Repeating the cell argument (3)--(6) on the universal partition induced by \(\mathcal K_n\), then taking the expectation under a fixed \(P\) and the supremum over the same nonempty class, shows that the left endpoint of every cell has no larger \(J\)-value than any point in the cell.  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}J(h)=\min_{t\in\mathcal K_n}J(t).
\tag{10}
\]
The right side is a minimum of finitely many finite real numbers.  Its smallest minimizer is also the smallest global minimizer on \(\mathcal H_{n,d}\) by the same left-endpoint argument.  This is exactly \(h^\dagger\) in \({\rm def:exact\text{-}optimal\text{-}bandwidth}\), so \(h^\dagger\) is well defined.  It remains a class-level object because (8) requires the full-class supremum.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\) and let
\[
 \mathcal G=\sigma\!\left(A,(f_i)_{i\in\mathcal V_n}\right),
 \qquad E_h=\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n,
 \qquad m_h=\mathbb E_P(E_h\mid\mathcal G).
\tag{11}
\]
For every prespecified \(h\), \({\rm thm:explicit\text{-}pc\text{-}conditional\text{-}certificate}\) gives
\[
 |m_h|\leq\overline b_h(A),\qquad
 \mathbb E_P[(E_h-m_h)^2\mid\mathcal G]\leq\overline s_h(A)^2,
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
 \Pr_Z\{|E_h|\leq\overline b_h(A)+a_\alpha\overline s_h(A)\mid\mathcal G\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
\tag{13}
\]
The same theorem proves that \(\widehat h(A)\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable and does not depend on \(Z\).  Consequently (12)--(13) may be evaluated at \(h=\widehat h(A)\), with no multiple-testing or selection penalty.  By \({\rm ass:response\text{-}envelope}\) and \({\rm def:target}\),
\[
 |\theta_n|
 \leq\frac1{2n}\sum_i
 \{|\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)|+|\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)|\}
 \leq B.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus intersection with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove \(\theta_n\) on the event in (13).  The corrected \({\rm def:honest\text{-}interval}\) now proves the fixed-bandwidth and selected conditional coverage claims.  Its untruncated half-length is exactly \(\overline b_h(A)+a_\alpha\overline s_h(A)\); every positive degree occurs in the first sum of \({\rm def:radii}\), and every isolate occurs in its explicit isolate term.  Equation (2) also gives \(d^{-1}=\ell\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\), so this endpoint remains an admissible prespecified choice.

Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz in (12) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(|E_h-m_h|\mid\mathcal G)\leq\overline s_h(A).
\]
Together with the bias part of (12),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(|E_h|\mid\mathcal G)
 \leq\overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A).
\tag{15}
\]
Evaluate (15) at the selected bandwidth, use (7) with the deterministic class-level value \(h=h^\dagger\), and take expectations.  Then, for every member law,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{\widehat h(A)}-\theta_n|
 &\leq\mathbb E_P[\overline b_{\widehat h(A)}(A)+\overline s_{\widehat h(A)}(A)]\\
 &\leq\mathbb E_P[\overline b_{h^\dagger}(A)+\overline s_{h^\dagger}(A)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(P\) and invoking \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\) yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\theta_{\widehat h(A)}-\theta_n|
 \leq\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{17}
\]
Integrating the selected version of (13) gives
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 P\{\theta_n\in\mathcal C_{\widehat h(A)}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{18}
\]

Finally, intersection can only shorten the untruncated set, and both radii are nonnegative.  Since \(a_\alpha>1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_{\widehat h(A)})
 &\leq2\{\overline b_{\widehat h(A)}(A)
      +a_\alpha\overline s_{\widehat h(A)}(A)\}\\
 &\leq2a_\alpha
 \{\overline b_{\widehat h(A)}(A)+\overline s_{\widehat h(A)}(A)\}\\
 &\leq2a_\alpha
 \{\overline b_{h^\dagger}(A)+\overline s_{h^\dagger}(A)\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
where the last step is (7).  Expectations, the class supremum, (1), and \({\rm def:frontier\text{-}envelope}\) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d,L}}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_{\widehat h(A)})]
 \leq2\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}\,\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}.
\tag{20}
\]
The operational conclusions (17)--(20) come from the pointwise candidate inequality (7), not from identifying \(\widehat h(A)\) with \(h^\dagger\).  No step compares \(\mathfrak u_{n,d,L}\) to \(\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\), so the asserted frontier disclaimer follows.
