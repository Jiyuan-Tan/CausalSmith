Fix a sufficiently large index for which \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\neq\varnothing\), and select the graph primitives \(W\), \((\lambda_k,\psi_k)_{k=1}^r\), and the graph sampling law from one member.  We retain those primitives throughout.  All constants below are independent of \(n\) and \(d\), although they may depend on the fixed constants in the theorem.

\textit{Step 1: uniform conditional-degree bounds.}
Define
\[
 w(u)=\int_0^1W(u,v)\,dv,
 \qquad
 p_n(u)=\rho_nw(u).
\tag{1}
\]
By \({\rm ass:kernel-bound}\), \({\rm ass:degree-regularity}\), and \({\rm ass:edge-probability}\), there is a Lebesgue-null set \(E\subset[0,1]\) such that, for every \(u\notin E\),
\[
 \kappa^{-1}\leq w(u)\leq\kappa,
 \qquad 0<p_n(u)\leq1.
\tag{2}
\]
The uniform marginal in \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\) gives \(P(U_i\in E)=0\).  Conditional on \(U_i=u\notin E\), the pairs consisting of \(U_j\) and the edge randomizer for \(A_{ij}\), over \(j\neq i\), are independent.  Integrating \({\rm ass:graphon-draw}\) over the independent \({\rm Uniform}[0,1]\) variable \(U_j\) therefore gives
\[
 \Pr(A_{ij}=1\mid U_i=u)
 =\rho_n\int_0^1W(u,v)\,dv=p_n(u),
\tag{3}
\]
and the variables \((A_{ij})_{j\neq i}\) are conditionally independent.  Consequently
\[
 N_i\mid U_i=u\sim{\rm Binomial}(n-1,p_n(u)),
 \qquad
 m_n(u):=\mathbb E(N_i\mid U_i=u)=(n-1)p_n(u).
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(\rho_n=d/n\), (2)--(4) imply
\[
 \frac{(n-1)d}{n\kappa}\leq m_n(u)
 \leq\frac{(n-1)d\kappa}{n}.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, for \(n\geq2\),
\[
 \frac{d}{2\kappa}\leq m_n(u)\leq\kappa d.
\tag{6}
\]

We record four consequences of (4)--(6).  If \(N\sim{\rm Binomial}(n-1,p)\), expansion of the binomial mass function and the identity \({n-1\choose k}/(k+1)={n\choose k+1}/n\) give
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{N+1}
 =\frac{1-(1-p)^n}{np}\leq\frac1{np}.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(u\notin E\), (2), (7), and \(N^{-1}\mathbf1\{N>0\}\leq2(N+1)^{-1}\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E\left\{N_i^{-1}\mathbf1(N_i>0)\mid U_i=u\right\}
 \leq\frac{2\kappa}{d}.
\tag{8}
\]
Also, (4)--(6) and \(1-x\leq e^{-x}\) give
\[
 \Pr(N_i=0\mid U_i=u)=(1-p_n(u))^{n-1}
 \leq e^{-m_n(u)}\leq e^{-d/(2\kappa)}.
\tag{9}
\]

Conditional on \(A\) and \(N_i=N>0\), \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\) gives \(M_i\sim{\rm Binomial}(N,1/2)\), whence
\[
 \mathbb E_Z(V_i^2\mid A,N_i=N)=\frac1{4N}.
\tag{10}
\]
Integrating (10), then using (8), yields
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^nV_i^2\mathbf1\{N_i>0\}
 \leq\frac{n\kappa}{2d}.
\tag{11}
\]

We next bound local exposure mass.  For \(M\sim{\rm Binomial}(N,1/2)\), the maximal atom is bounded by \(C_0N^{-1/2}\).  Indeed, for \(N=2q\), if \(a_q={2q\choose q}4^{-q}\), then
\[
 \frac{a_{q+1}}{a_q}=\frac{2q+1}{2q+2},
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{2q+1}{2q+2}\right)^2\leq\frac{q+1}{q+2},
\tag{12}
\]
so induction gives \(a_q\leq(q+1)^{-1/2}\); an odd-binomial central atom is bounded by an adjacent even central atom.  The event \(|M/N-1/2|\leq h\) contains at most \(2Nh+2\) lattice points.  Hence
\[
 \Pr\{|M/N-1/2|\leq h\mid N\}
 \leq C_1\{\sqrt N\,h+N^{-1/2}\}.
\tag{13}
\]
For a binomial variable with mean \(m\), exponential Markov's inequality at \(t=\log2\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N<m/2)
 \leq e^{tm/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^{n-1}
 \leq\exp\{-c_0m\},
 \qquad c_0=(1-\log2)/2>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Splitting according to \(N_i\geq m_n(U_i)/2\), and using (6), (14), and \(d\to\infty\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{N_i^{-1/2}\mathbf1(N_i>0)\}
 \leq C_2d^{-1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Jensen's inequality and (6) also give
\[
 \mathbb E\sqrt{N_i}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb EN_i}\leq\sqrt{\kappa d}.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (13), (15), and (16), and using \(h\geq d^{-1}\) from \({\rm def:bandwidth-set}\), we obtain uniformly for \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\),
\[
 \Pr(|V_i|\leq h,N_i>0)
 \leq C_3\{\sqrt d\,h+d^{-1/2}\}
 \leq2C_3\sqrt d\,h.
\tag{17}
\]

Finally choose \(K>e\kappa\) so large that
\[
 a_K:=K\log\{K/(e\kappa)\}>2/C_{\log},
 \qquad D_n=\lceil Kd\rceil.
\tag{18}
\]
The binomial Chernoff bound obtained by optimizing exponential Markov's inequality, together with \(m_n(u)\leq\kappa d\), gives
\[
 \Pr(N_i>D_n\mid U_i=u)
 \leq\left\{\frac{e m_n(u)}{D_n}\right\}^{D_n}
 \leq e^{-a_Kd}.
\tag{19}
\]
No independence among the degrees is needed for the union bound.  By \({\rm ass:log-degree}\),
\[
 \Pr\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}N_i>D_n\right)
 \leq ne^{-a_Kd}
 \leq n^{1-a_KC_{\log}}=o(1).
\tag{20}
\]

\textit{Step 2: response submodels and a common distance bound.}
Let \(s_0=B/4\), and let \((\xi_i)_{i=1}^n\), independently of \((U,A,Z)\), be independent with density
\[
 p_{s_0}(t)=s_0^{-1}\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2s_0}\right)
 \mathbf1\{|t|\leq s_0\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The substitution \(y=\pi t/(2s_0)\) verifies that (21) integrates to one.  For a deterministic \(g\in C^3[0,1]\), define
\[
 f_i^\pm(a,x)=\xi_i\pm g(x),
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\},\quad x\in[0,1].
\tag{22}
\]
If
\[
 \|g\|_\infty\leq3B/4,
 \qquad
 \max_{1\leq j\leq2}\|g^{(j)}\|_\infty\leq B,
 \qquad
 \|g^{(3)}\|_\infty\leq L,
\tag{23}
\]
then (21)--(23) verify \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) and \({\rm ass:third-derivative-bound}\).  Moreover, \((U_i,f_i^\pm)\) are independent and identically distributed, and their \(U_i\)-marginal remains \({\rm Uniform}[0,1]\).  Thus \({\rm ass:unit-sampling}\) holds; the retained graph primitives satisfy every graph condition of \({\rm def:model-class}\), and \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) defines the potential outcomes.  Each sign in (22) therefore produces a law in \(\mathcal M_{n,d,L}\).  By \({\rm def:target}\),
\[
 \theta_n^\pm=\pm g'(1/2),
 \qquad
 \Delta_g:=|\theta_n^+-\theta_n^-|=2|g'(1/2)|.
\tag{24}
\]

Let \(q_{s_0}=\sqrt{p_{s_0}}\), extended by zero outside \([-s_0,s_0]\).  The cosine vanishes at the endpoints, so \(q_{s_0}\) is absolutely continuous, and direct integration gives
\[
 \|q_{s_0}'\|_2^2=\frac{\pi^2}{4s_0^2}.
\tag{25}
\]
For translations \(\eta_1,\eta_2\), the fundamental theorem of calculus followed by Minkowski's integral inequality gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^2\{p_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_1),p_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_2)\}
 &=\|q_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_1)-q_{s_0}(\mathord\cdot-\eta_2)\|_2^2\\
 &\leq(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2\|q_{s_0}'\|_2^2
 =\frac{\pi^2(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2}{4s_0^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Here \(H^2(p,q)=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\).  Define the observed neighbor fraction
\[
 X_i=\begin{cases}1/2+V_i,&N_i>0,\\0,&N_i=0.\end{cases}
\tag{27}
\]
Conditional on \((A,Z)\), the outcomes under (22) are independent translates of (21), and the two translations for unit \(i\) differ by \(2g(X_i)\).  Hellinger affinity multiplies for product laws.  Since \(H^2=2(1-\text{affinity})\) and \(1-\prod_i(1-x_i)\leq\sum_i x_i\) for \(0\leq x_i\leq1\), (26), followed by averaging over the common \((A,Z)\) marginal, gives
\[
 H^2(Q_g^+,Q_g^-)
 \leq\frac{\pi^2}{s_0^2}\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^ng(X_i)^2.
\tag{28}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_g^+,Q_g^-)
 \leq H(Q_g^+,Q_g^-).
\tag{29}
\]

\textit{Step 3: the linear graph-floor pair.}
Let
\[
 g_G(x)=c_GB\sqrt{d/n}\,(x-1/2).
\tag{30}
\]
Because \({\rm ass:sparse-limit}\) gives \(d/n\to0\), condition (23) holds for all sufficiently large \(n\) whenever \(0<c_G\leq1\).  Equations (9), (11), (27), and (30) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^ng_G(X_i)^2
 &=c_G^2B^2\frac dn
 \left\{\mathbb E\sum_iV_i^2\mathbf1(N_i>0)
 +\frac n4\Pr(N_i=0)\right\}\\
 &\leq c_G^2B^2
 \left\{\frac\kappa2+\frac d4e^{-d/(2\kappa)}\right\}
 \leq C_4c_G^2B^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Put
\[
 \eta=(1-2\alpha)/2>0.
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(s_0=B/4\), (28), (29), and (31) permit a fixed choice of \(c_G>0\) for which
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_G^+,Q_G^-)\leq\eta.
\tag{33}
\]
Equation (24) gives the target separation
\[
 \Delta_G=2c_GB\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{34}
\]

\textit{Step 4: the local \(C^3\)-bump pair.}
Suppose \(L>0\), and define
\[
 \phi(t)=t(1-4t^2)^4\mathbf1\{|t|<1/2\}.
\tag{35}
\]
The fourth-order zeros at the endpoints make the zero extension a \(C^3(\mathbb R)\) function, and \(\phi'(0)=1\).  Put \(C_j=\|\phi^{(j)}\|_\infty\), \(0\leq j\leq3\), and, for \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\), let
\[
 g_h(x)=c_ILh^3\phi\!\left(\frac{x-1/2}{h}\right).
\tag{36}
\]
Then
\[
 \|g_h^{(j)}\|_\infty=c_ILh^{3-j}C_j,
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq3.
\tag{37}
\]
Choose \(c_IC_3\leq1\).  Since \({\rm ass:log-degree}\) implies \(d\to\infty\) and \(h\leq d^{-1/2}\), (37) verifies (23) for every sufficiently large \(n\).  The bump vanishes at the isolate exposure \(0\), and its nonzero support is contained in \(|V_i|\leq h\).  Therefore (17), (28), and (36) give
\[
 H^2(Q_h^+,Q_h^-)
 \leq C_5c_I^2n\sqrt d\,L^2h^7.
\tag{38}
\]
Let
\[
 h_0=\{L^{-2}/(n\sqrt d)\}^{1/7}.
\tag{39}
\]
If \(d^{-1}<h_0<d^{-1/2}\), substitution of (39) into (38), and a fixed further decrease of \(c_I\), give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_{h_0}^+,Q_{h_0}^-)\leq\eta.
\tag{40}
\]
By (24), (35), and (36),
\[
 \Delta_I=2c_ILh_0^2
 =2c_IL^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{41}
\]

\textit{Step 5: the degree-lattice pair.}
Continue to suppose \(L>0\).  With \(D_n\) from (18), put \(w_n=(4D_n)^{-1}\) and
\[
 g_D(x)=c_DLw_n^3\phi\!\left(\frac{x-1/2}{w_n}\right),
 \qquad c_DC_3\leq1.
\tag{42}
\]
Because \(D_n\to\infty\), the analogue of (37) verifies (23) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  If \(1\leq N_i\leq D_n\), every noncentral exposure point satisfies
\[
 |V_i|\geq
 \begin{cases}N_i^{-1},&N_i\text{ is even},\\(2N_i)^{-1},&N_i\text{ is odd},\end{cases}
 \geq(2D_n)^{-1}>w_n.
\tag{43}
\]
At a central point, oddness of \(\phi\) gives \(g_D(1/2)=0\), and at an isolate \(g_D(0)=0\).  Hence, on \(F_n=\{\max_iN_i\leq D_n\}\), the complete observed outcomes under both signs in (22) equal \(\xi_i\).  The two data laws agree on \(F_n\), while the event has the same probability under both laws.  Thus (20) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_D^+,Q_D^-)
 \leq\Pr(F_n^c)=o(1)\leq\eta
\tag{44}
\]
for every sufficiently large \(n\).  From (24), (35), and (42),
\[
 \Delta_D=2c_DLw_n^2\asymp Ld^{-2},
\tag{45}
\]
where the comparison constants depend only on the fixed \(K\).

\textit{Step 6: risk and honest-length reductions.}
For any one of the preceding pairs, write its target values as \(\theta^+,\theta^-\), its data laws as \(Q^+,Q^-\), and its separation as \(\Delta\).  For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), define \(A_T=\{|T-\theta^+|\geq\Delta/2\}\).  On \(A_T^c\), the triangle inequality gives \(|T-\theta^-|\geq\Delta/2\).  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q^+}|T-\theta^+|+\mathbb E_{Q^-}|T-\theta^-|
 &\geq\frac\Delta2\{Q^+(A_T)+Q^-(A_T^c)\}\\
 &\geq\frac\Delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]
If the total variation is at most \(\eta\), the larger risk is at least \(\Delta(1-\eta)/4\).

Let \(\mathcal C\) be any interval-valued procedure admissible in the honest-length criterion of \({\rm def:minimax-criteria}\).  Uniform coverage and the definition of total variation imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q^+\{\theta^+,\theta^-\in\mathcal C\}
 &\geq Q^+\{\theta^+\in\mathcal C\}
      +Q^+\{\theta^-\in\mathcal C\}-1\\
 &\geq(1-\alpha)+(1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q^+,Q^-))-1\\
 &\geq1-2\alpha-\eta=(1-2\alpha)/2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
An interval containing both target values has length at least \(\Delta\), so
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q^+}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C)
 \geq\Delta(1-2\alpha)/2.
\tag{48}
\]
Taking the infima in \({\rm def:minimax-criteria}\), (33)--(34), (40)--(41), and (44)--(45) show that each minimax criterion is bounded below, up to fixed positive constants, by \(\sqrt{d/n}\), by \(L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}\) in the interior regime, and by \(Ld^{-2}\), respectively.

\textit{Step 7: comparison with the supported envelope.}
Set \(a_n=(n\sqrt d)^{-1/2}\).  For \(L>0\), (39) gives
\[
 Lh_0^2=a_nh_0^{-3/2}=L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{49}
\]
If \(h_0\leq d^{-1}\), then \(L^2\geq d^{13/2}/n\), and hence \(a_nd^{3/2}\leq Ld^{-2}\).  Evaluation at \(h=d^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2Ld^{-2}.
\tag{50}
\]
If \(d^{-1}<h_0<d^{-1/2}\), evaluation at \(h_0\) and (49) gives
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}.
\tag{51}
\]
If \(h_0\geq d^{-1/2}\), then \(L^2\leq d^3/n\), so \(Ld^{-1}\leq\sqrt{d/n}\); at \(h=d^{-1/2}\), the stochastic term also equals \(\sqrt{d/n}\).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+a_nh^{-3/2}\}
 \leq2\sqrt{d/n}.
\tag{52}
\]
When \(L=0\), the infimum is attained at \(d^{-1/2}\) and equals \(\sqrt{d/n}\).

In the regime of (50), combine the graph and lattice pairs; in the regime of (51), combine the graph and interior pairs; in the regime of (52), or when \(L=0\), use the graph pair.  If a nonnegative criterion is at least \(c_1x\) and at least \(c_2y\), it is at least \(\min(c_1,c_2)(x+y)/2\).  Equations (34), (41), (45), and (50)--(52) therefore give a fixed \(c>0\) such that
\[
 R^*_{n,d,L}\geq c\left[\sqrt{d/n}+
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}}\{Lh^2+(n\sqrt d\,h^3)^{-1/2}\}\right].
\tag{53}
\]
The same argument using (48) gives (53) with \(R^*_{n,d,L}\) replaced by \(\Lambda^*_{n,d,L}(\alpha)\).  The bracket is \(\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}\) by \({\rm def:frontier-envelope}\).  Every constructed response law preserves the selected member's kernel and independent uniform latent types.  Divisions by \(B\), \(d\), and \(N_i\) are legitimate because \(B>0\), \(d>0\), and the factor \(N_i^{-1}\) is used only on \(\{N_i>0\}\); Steps 4--5 are invoked only when \(L>0\).  This proves both inequalities and the final construction claims.
