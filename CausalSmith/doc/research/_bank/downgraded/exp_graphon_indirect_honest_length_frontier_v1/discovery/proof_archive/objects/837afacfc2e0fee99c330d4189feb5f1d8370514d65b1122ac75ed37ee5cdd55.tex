Define \(\lambda_k=c_0/r\).  Since the intervals \(I_k\) partition \([0,1]\) up to endpoints of measure zero,
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^r\lambda_k\psi_k(u)\psi_k(v)
 =c_0\mathbf1\{u\text{ and }v\text{ are in the same }I_k\}=W_0(u,v)
\tag{1}
\]
almost everywhere.  The functions are orthonormal because
\[
 \int_0^1\psi_k(u)\psi_\ell(u)\,du=\mathbf1\{k=\ell\}.
\tag{2}
\]
All \(r\) eigenvalues are nonzero, and the definition of \(c_0\) gives
\[
 |\lambda_k|=c_0/r\geq\underline\lambda,
 \quad
 \int_0^1W_0(u,v)\,dv=c_0/r\geq\kappa^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
The added compatibility condition \(c_0\leq\kappa\) gives \(0\leq W_0\leq\kappa\); together with \({\rm ass:edge\text{-}probability}\), all edge probabilities lie in \([0,1]\).

For completeness, let \(\overline\psi_k=\int_0^1\psi_k(u)\,du=1/\sqrt r\).  The centered variable \(\psi_k(U)-\overline\psi_k\) takes values \((r-1)/\sqrt r\) and \(-1/\sqrt r\).  Hence its absolute value is at most \((r-1)/\sqrt r\) when \(r\geq2\), while it is identically zero when \(r=1\).  For every integer \(m\geq2\),
\[
 \mathbb E|\psi_k(U)-\overline\psi_k|^m
 \leq b_\psi^{m-2}\operatorname{Var}\{\psi_k(U)\}
 \leq\frac{m!}{2}b_\psi^{m-2}\operatorname{Var}\{\psi_k(U)\},
\tag{4}
\]
where the first inequality uses \(b_\psi\geq(r-1)/\sqrt r\).  Thus the declared Bernstein moment property holds.  Equations (1)--(4), uniform latent types, and the maintained graph and assignment assumptions show that \(W_0\) supplies the advertised rank-\(r\) block graph submodel.

Put \(s_0=B/4\).  The density
\[
 p_{s_0}(t)=s_0^{-1}\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2s_0}\right)
 \mathbf1\{|t|\leq s_0\}
\tag{5}
\]
integrates to one and is supported on \([-B/4,B/4]\).  Specialize the three response constructions in the proof of \({\rm thm:unrestricted\text{-}supported\text{-}converse}\) to this fixed \(W_0\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_G(x)&=a_G(x-1/2),\\
 g_I(x)&=\gamma Lh_0^3\phi((x-1/2)/h_0),\\
 g_L(x)&=\gamma L(4Kd)^{-3}\phi(4Kd(x-1/2)),
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\phi(t)=t(1-t^2)^4\mathbf1\{|t|\leq1\}\), \(a_G\) is a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(B\sqrt{d/n}\), and the fixed constants \(\gamma,K\) are chosen there.  Use \(g_I\) only in the interior phase; when \(L=0\), the graph pair alone is needed.  The derivative and level calculations in that proof show that
\[
 f_i^\pm(a,x)=\xi_i\pm g(x),\quad \xi_i\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}p_{s_0},
\tag{7}
\]
satisfy both response radii for all sufficiently large \(n\).  They are independent and identically distributed jointly with the uniform types, so (7) does not disturb any member property.

The same proof bounds the total variation distance of each pair away from one and derives, respectively, parameter separations of orders \(\sqrt{d/n}\), \(L^{3/7}(n^2d)^{-1/7}\), and \(Ld^{-2}\).  Its testing inequalities apply with the graph law fixed at \(W_0\), so these are lower experiments inside this single explicit block submodel, for both absolute risk and honest expected length.  Its final phase comparison proves
\[
 R^*_{n,d,L}\geq c\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L},
 \quad
 \Lambda^*_{n,d,L}(\alpha)\geq c\mathfrak r^{\mathrm{sup}}_{n,d,L}
\tag{8}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), with \(c\) independent of \(n,d\).  The compatibility inequalities were used only in (3)--(4) to certify this convenient block witness.  Independently, \({\rm thm:unrestricted\text{-}supported\text{-}converse}\) proves (8) by retaining the graph primitives of an arbitrary admissible member, so those sufficient block inequalities do not characterize class nonemptiness.
