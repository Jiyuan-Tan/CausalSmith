Fix \(A\), the preassignment response functions, and \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,d}\).  All expectations and variances below are over the independent assignment coordinates from \({\rm ass:bernoulli-design}\).  Put
\[
 X=Z-\frac12\mathbf1_n,\qquad P_A=\widehat\Pi_r(A),\qquad C_A=P_AA,
 \qquad F_h(Z)=\widehat\theta_h.
\]

We first prove the variance certificate.  If \(z^{(j)}\) is obtained from \(z\) by flipping coordinate \(j\), the finite maximum in \({\rm def:pc-handle}\) gives
\[
 |q_{i,h}(z)-q_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|\leq D_{ij,h}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the difference is zero when \(A_{ij}=0\); when \(A_{ij}=1\), the treated-neighbor count moves between two consecutive integers appearing in that maximum.  The normalizer is positive for \(N_i>0\) by \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\).  Since \(|X_j|=1/2\), the definitions of \(Q_{i,h}\), \(\Gamma_{i,h}\), and \(\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}\) give
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)|\leq Q_{i,h}+2\sum_{j'}|(C_A)_{ij'}|=\Gamma_{i,h},
\tag{2}
\]
and, because a flip changes \(X_j\) by one in absolute value,
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)-\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq D_{ij,h}+4|(C_A)_{ij}|.
\tag{3}
\]

The response-level part of \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) gives \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)|\leq B\).  If \(j=i\), a flip changes only the own-treatment argument, so the two response values differ by at most \(2B\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), then \(j\ne i\), the exposure fraction changes by \(N_i^{-1}\), and the derivative envelope plus the fundamental theorem of calculus bounds the change by \(B/N_i\).  Otherwise anonymous interference makes it zero.  With the convention in \({\rm def:pc-handle}\),
\[
 |Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)-Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq B\left\{2\mathbf1\{i=j\}+\frac{A_{ij}}{N_i}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Applying \(|gy-g'y'|\leq|g-g'||y|+|g'||y-y'|\) to (2)--(4) proves
\[
 |\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z)Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z)
 -\widetilde\gamma_{i,h}(z^{(j)})Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}(z^{(j)})|
 \leq\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{5}
\]
Summation and \({\rm def:estimator}\) yield
\[
 |F_h(z)-F_h(z^{(j)})|
 \leq c_{j,h}:=n^{-1}\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}.
\tag{6}
\]

For completeness, let \(\mathcal F_j=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_j)\), \(M_j=\mathbb E_Z(F_h\mid\mathcal F_j)\), and \(E_j=M_j-M_{j-1}\).  Martingale differences are orthogonal, so
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(F_h)=\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E_ZE_j^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_{j-1}\), the difference between the two values of \(M_j\) obtained by setting \(Z_j=1\) and \(Z_j=0\) is an average over future coordinates of differences bounded by (6), hence is at most \(c_{j,h}\).  A two-point variable with equal masses and range at most \(c_{j,h}\) has variance at most \(c_{j,h}^2/4\).  Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \leq\frac14\sum_jc_{j,h}^2
 =\frac1{4n^2}\sum_j\left(\sum_i\beta_{ij,h}\right)^2
 =\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}=\overline s_h(A)^2.
\tag{8}
\]

We next prove the bias certificate.  For \(N_i>0\), \(q_{i,h}\) depends only on neighbor assignments and is independent of \(Z_i\).  Conditioning first on \(Z_i=a\), applying \({\rm ass:anonymous-interference}\) and \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\), and averaging the two equiprobable own-treatment values gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]
 -\frac{\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)+\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)}2\right|
 \leq\frac L6h_i(h)^2.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(N_i=0\), \(q_{i,h}=0\), while the derivative envelope bounds the absolute target contribution by \(B\).  Thus the score part has conditional bias at most
\[
 \frac{L}{6n}\sum_{i:N_i>0}h_i(h)^2
 +\frac Bn\sum_i\mathbf1\{N_i=0\}.
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to bound the PC subtraction.  Centering and \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}|\leq B\) imply
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|\leq B/2.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(j\ne i\) with \(A_{ij}=1\), condition on all assignments except \(Z_j\).  The response change bound in (4) and the two-point calculation yield
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]|
 =\frac14\left|\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=1]
 -\mathbb E_Z[Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid Z_j=0]\right|
 \leq\frac B{4N_i}.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(j\ne i\) and \(A_{ij}=0\), the expectation is zero by anonymous interference and assignment independence.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac4n\sum_i\left|\sum_j(C_A)_{ij}\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right|
 &\leq\frac{2B}{n}\sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|\\
 &\quad+\frac Bn\sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 =\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Combining (10)--(13) with \({\rm def:target}\), \({\rm def:estimator}\), and \({\rm def:radii}\) proves
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\mid A,(f_i)_i)\right|
 \leq\overline b_h(A).
\tag{14}
\]

Because \(P_A\) is a spectral projector of the symmetric matrix \(A\), it commutes with \(A\), so \(C_A=P_AA\) is symmetric.  If \(C_A=\sum_t\nu_tu_tu_t^\top\), orthonormality gives
\[
 \sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|
 \leq\sum_t|\nu_t|\sum_i u_{t,i}^2=\sum_t|\nu_t|=\|C_A\|_*.
\tag{15}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz first within each positive-degree row and then across rows gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 &\leq\sum_{i:N_i>0}
 \left\{\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(C_A)_{ij}^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\leq\sqrt{nG_A(C_A)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Substitution into the definition of \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}\) proves
\[
 \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \leq\frac{2B}{n}\|C_A\|_*+B\sqrt{\frac{G_A(C_A)}n}.
\tag{17}
\]

Conditional Chebyshev applied to (8) and (14) gives
\[
 \Pr_Z\!\left(
 |\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|>
 \overline b_h(A)+\frac{\overline s_h(A)}{\sqrt\alpha}
 \mathrel{\Big|}A,(f_i)_i\right)\leq\alpha.
\tag{18}
\]
When \(\overline s_h(A)=0\), (8) makes the centered fluctuation zero almost surely, so (18) remains valid.  The derivative envelope and \({\rm def:target}\) give \(|\theta_n|\leq B\); intersecting with \([-B,B]\) therefore cannot remove the target.  Integrating (18) proves uniform coverage.

Finally, for each realized degree the truncated score changes only when \(h\) crosses one of finitely many lattice distances, so there are at most \(n^2\) breakpoints over all units.  Exact finite binomial sums compute \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\), the finite maxima compute \(Q_{i,h}\) and \(D_{ij,h}\), one eigendecomposition computes \(C_A\), and matrix and finite-sum arithmetic compute the remaining certificates.  This is polynomial time.  By \({\rm lem:exact-score-hoeffding-projections}\), the first projection of \(q_h-4P_AAX\) is \(4(I_n-P_A)AX\) and its second projection is zero, with neither projection depending on \(h\).  Equations (8), (14), (17), and (18) prove every asserted certificate without imposing a sharper stochastic order.
