Let \(X=Z-\mathbf1_n/2\), \(P_A=\widehat\Pi_r(A)\), and \(C_A=P_AA\).  The matrix \(P_A\) is an orthogonal spectral projector of the symmetric matrix \(A\), so it commutes with \(A\).  By \({\rm lem:exact-score-hoeffding-projections}\), if \(q_h^{(1)}=4AX\) and \(\eta_h=q_h-q_h^{(1)}\), then
\[
 \widetilde\gamma_h=(I_n-P_A)q_h^{(1)}+\eta_h,
\]
and \(\eta_h\) is conditionally orthogonal in \(L^2\) to every first-order function of \(Z\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_Z\|\widetilde\gamma_h\|_2^2
 &=\mathbb E_Z\|(I_n-P_A)q_h^{(1)}\|_2^2
   +\mathbb E_Z\|\eta_h\|_2^2\\
 &\leq\mathbb E_Z\|q_h^{(1)}\|_2^2+\mathbb E_Z\|\eta_h\|_2^2
 =\mathbb E_Z\|q_h\|_2^2
 =\sum_{i:N_i>0}\mu_{2,i}(h)^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last identity is \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\).  Since \(|Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}|\leq B\) by \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\), pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_h^2
 \leq \frac{B^2}{n}\|\widetilde\gamma_h\|_2^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_h\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \leq\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h^2\mid A,(f_i)_i)
 \leq\widehat V_{\mathrm{pc},h}
 \leq\overline s_h(A)^2.
\]

We next prove the bias inequality.  The score \(q_{i,h}\) depends only on neighbor assignments and is independent of \(Z_i\).  Thus \({\rm lem:exact-score-moments}\) implies, for \(N_i>0\),
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_Z[q_{i,h}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid A,(f_i)_i]
 -\frac{\partial_xf_i(1,1/2)+\partial_xf_i(0,1/2)}2\right|
 \leq\frac L6h_i(h)^2.
\]
For \(N_i=0\), \(q_{i,h}=0\) and the omitted target summand has magnitude at most \(B\) by \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\).

It remains to bound the subtraction \(4(C_AX)_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Pointwise boundedness gives
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid A,(f_i)_i]|\leq B/2.
\]
If \(A_{ij}=1\), condition on all assignments except \(Z_j\).  Changing \(Z_j\) changes unit \(i\)'s exposure by \(N_i^{-1}\); the derivative bound in \({\rm ass:response-envelope}\) and the two equiprobable values of \(X_j\) give
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid A,(f_i)_i]|\leq \frac{B}{4N_i}.
\]
If \(j\neq i\) and \(A_{ij}=0\), then \(X_j\) is independent of \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and is centered, so this covariance is zero.  Consequently
\[
 \frac4n\sum_i\left|\sum_j(C_A)_{ij}
 \mathbb E_Z[X_jY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid A,(f_i)_i]\right|
 \leq\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{pc},h}.
\]
Combining this display with the positive-degree Taylor bounds and the isolate bound proves
\[
 |\mathbb E_Z(\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n\mid A,(f_i)_i)|\leq\overline b_h(A).
\]

For the graph-norm display, duality of nuclear and operator norm gives
\(\sum_i|(C_A)_{ii}|\leq\|C_A\|_*\).  Rowwise Cauchy--Schwarz and then Cauchy--Schwarz over rows give
\[
 \sum_{i:N_i>0}\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}|(C_A)_{ij}|
 \leq\sum_{i:N_i>0}\left\{\frac1{N_i}\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}(C_A)_{ij}^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq\sqrt{nG_A(C_A)}.
\]
Substitution proves the deterministic inequality.

Conditional Chebyshev applied to the proved bias and variance bounds yields
\[
 P\!\left(|\widehat\theta_h-\theta_n|>
 \overline b_h(A)+\overline s_h(A)/\sqrt\alpha
 \mid A,(f_i)_i\right)\leq\alpha.
\]
Because \(|\theta_n|\leq B\), intersecting with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove the target, and integration proves coverage.

Finally, for a fixed degree \(N_i\), both the observed numerator and \(\mu_{2,i}(h)\) change only when \(h\) crosses one of at most \(N_i+1\) binomial-lattice distances.  The union over units has at most \(n^2\) breakpoints.  Binomial sums at these breakpoints, one spectral decomposition, and ordinary matrix operations give a polynomial-time piecewise-constant representation of all distinct scores and certificates.  The final two sentences of the theorem are scope statements: the proof used neither an entrywise bound implying \(G_A(C_A)=O_P(d^2/n)\) nor a sharp product-ANOVA inequality, so no sharper stochastic order follows from this argument.
