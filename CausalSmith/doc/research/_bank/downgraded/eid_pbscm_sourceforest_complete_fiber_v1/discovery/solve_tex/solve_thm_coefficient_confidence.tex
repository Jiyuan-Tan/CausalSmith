%%% FIELD genealogy-proof
Fix \((G,\mu,\alpha,P)\in\mathcal M_d^\circ\).  We first make the occurrence bookkeeping explicit.  Start with one individual carrying label \(i\).  At each occurrence carrying label \(a\), perform once, independently, the Bernoulli trial attached to each edge \(a\to b\); an open trial creates one child occurrence carrying label \(b\), and a closed trial creates none.  Because \(G\) is acyclic, every lineage has length at most \(d-1\).  Since every vertex has finite outdegree, the total number of occurrences and the number of possible open--closed histories are finite.  Write \(K_i(\omega)\in\mathbb N_0^V\) for the vector counting labels in a history \(\omega\) rooted at \(i\).

We prove by reverse topological induction that
\[
 H_i(z)=\mathbb E\!\left[z^{K_i(\omega)}\right].
\]
The assertion is immediate at a sink, where both sides equal \(z_i\).  At a general vertex, the initial occurrence contributes \(z_i\).  For each \(j\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(i)\), independence of the edge trial and of the descendant trials gives the factor \((1-\alpha_{ij})+\alpha_{ij}H_j(z)\).  Independence across outgoing edges therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[z^{K_i(\omega)}\right]
 =z_i\prod_{j\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(i)}
       \bigl(1-\alpha_{ij}+\alpha_{ij}H_j(z)\bigr)
 =H_i(z),
\]
where the last equality is the root-cluster recursion.  This induction does not merge occurrences having the same label: two open directed paths ending at the same vertex give two distinct occurrences and hence add two to the corresponding coordinate of \(K_i(\omega)\).

Regard \(O_1(\omega)\) and \(O_0(\omega)\) as occurrence-indexed sets of trials, so that two trials of the same graph edge made by different occurrences are distinct elements.  The independent branching-law assumption then gives
\[
 \Pr\{\omega\}
 =\prod_{e\in O_1(\omega)}\alpha_e
   \prod_{e\in O_0(\omega)}(1-\alpha_e)
 =w_\omega.
\]
Consequently, coefficient extraction from the preceding finite probability-generating polynomial yields
\[
 [z^k]H_i(z)
 =\sum_{\omega:K_i(\omega)=k}\Pr\{\omega\}
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_i(k)}w_\gamma.
\]
The independent Poisson innovations and the structural recursion give the root-cluster log-PGF identity in the frozen definition,
\[
 \Psi_P(z)=\sum_{i\in V}\mu_i\bigl(H_i(z)-1\bigr).
\]
For nonzero \(k\), the constants on the right have zero \(z^k\)-coefficient.  The definition of \(c_k\) therefore gives, without any cancellation,
\[
 c_k=[z^k]\bigl(\Psi_P(z)-\Psi_P(0)\bigr)
 =\sum_{i\in V}\mu_i[z^k]H_i(z)
 =\sum_{i\in V}\mu_i\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_i(k)}w_\gamma.
\]
Strictness, namely \(\mu_i>0\) and \(0<\alpha_e<1\), also shows that every displayed genealogy weight is positive.  This proves the claimed formula, including the case of repeated observed labels produced by overlapping downstream paths.

%%% FIELD local-signatures-proof
Fix a strict representation \((G,\mu,\alpha,P)\).  By the genealogy coefficient formula, \(c_k>0\) holds exactly when at least one one-innovation genealogy has label-multiplicity vector \(k\).  The root label occurs in every such genealogy, and every vertex on an open directed path occurs at least once.

Consider distinct \(i,j\).  A genealogy with multiplicity \(e_i+e_j\) must be rooted at \(i\) or \(j\).  Any open path between the two labels of length greater than one would contribute the label of an intermediate vertex, so such a genealogy can exist only when \(i\) and \(j\) are adjacent.  Conversely, if \(i\to j\), open that edge and close every other outgoing trial made by the occurrences at \(i\) and \(j\).  This event has positive probability by strictness and has multiplicity \(e_i+e_j\).  The same argument applies when \(j\to i\).  Thus
\[
 c_{e_i+e_j}>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{i,j\}\text{ is an edge of the undirected skeleton of }G.
\]
In particular, the definition of \(S(P)\) recovers that skeleton.  For later reference, if
\[
 \beta_a:=\prod_{h\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(a)}(1-\alpha_{ah}),
\]
then the same exact-support argument gives
\[
 c_{e_a}=\mu_a\beta_a>0,
 \qquad
 c_{e_i+e_j}
 =\mu_i\alpha_{ij}
   \prod_{h\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(i)\setminus\{j\}}(1-\alpha_{ih})\,\beta_j
 =\frac{c_{e_i}\alpha_{ij}\beta_j}{1-\alpha_{ij}}
\tag{1}
\]
whenever \(i\to j\).  Shared descendants cause no additional term in (1), because opening an edge to any third label would violate exact support.

Now suppose that \(i-k-j\) is an induced path.  Under \(i\to k\leftarrow j\), no vertex among \(i,j,k\) reaches all three within the induced subgraph.  A root outside the triple would itself occur, and a path leaving the triple would contribute an outside label.  Hence no genealogy has multiplicity \(e_i+e_j+e_k\), and its coefficient is zero.  Every other acyclic orientation of the two-edge path is an out-tree rooted at one of its three vertices.  Opening its two tree edges and closing all other outgoing trials of the three resulting occurrences produces each label exactly once with positive probability.  Therefore
\[
 c_{e_i+e_j+e_k}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\to k\leftarrow j
\tag{2}
\]
on an induced path \(i-k-j\).

Every acyclic orientation of a triangle is transitive.  Denote its unique order by \(s\to m\to r\), with the additional edge \(s\to r\).  Opening all three triangle edges from a cluster rooted at \(s\), and closing all other outgoing trials of the resulting occurrences, produces multiplicity
\[
 e_s+e_m+2e_r;
\]
the event has positive probability.  Conversely, in a genealogy supported on the triangle, each occurrence of a label \(a\) corresponds to an open directed path from the genealogy root to \(a\), with the empty path included for the root.  In a transitive triangle, the only label reachable from a root by two distinct directed paths is the sink \(r\), and this occurs only from \(s\), via \(s\to r\) and \(s\to m\to r\).  Hence
\[
 c_{e_i+e_j+2e_k}>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\to k\leftarrow j
\tag{3}
\]
when \(i,j,k\) form a triangle.  This path argument counts occurrences before equal labels are merged, so it remains valid with overlapping descendants.

Equations (2) and (3), together with skeleton recovery, show that \(\mathsf{Head}_P(i,j;k)\) holds exactly when \(i\) and \(j\) are two distinct parents of \(k\).  Indeed, if they are nonadjacent, (2) applies, and if they are adjacent, (3) applies.  Thus every incoming arrow \(i\to k\) at a vertex with indegree at least two is placed in \(A(P)\), by pairing \(i\) with any other parent of \(k\).  Conversely, every arrow placed in \(A(P)\) is certified by one of (2) or (3), so it really enters a vertex having at least two parents.  Since all the coefficients involved depend only on \(P\), the same conclusion holds in every strict representation of \(P\), hence in every member of \(\mathcal D(P)\).  No conditional-independence faithfulness assumption is used.

%%% FIELD source-reversal-proof
Put \(t=\alpha_{uv}\).  If there were a directed path from \(u\) to \(v\) other than the edge \(u\to v\), its last edge would enter \(v\) from a parent different from \(u\), contradicting \(\operatorname{Pa}_G(v)=\{u\}\).  Replacing \(u\to v\) by \(v\to u\) therefore cannot create a directed cycle.  Moreover, after reversal \(v\) is a source and \(u\) has sole parent \(v\).

Define the following formal polynomials using the unchanged descendant root-cluster polynomials:
\[
 U:=z_u\prod_{a\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(u)\setminus\{v\}}
       (1-\alpha_{ua}+\alpha_{ua}H_a),
 \qquad
 W:=z_v\prod_{b\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(v)}
       (1-\alpha_{vb}+\alpha_{vb}H_b).
\]
Acyclicity implies that neither product contains \(u\) or \(v\) as a descendant.  The products may involve arbitrarily many common descendant variables; no disjointness is being assumed.  The old root polynomials are
\[
 H_u=U(1-t+tW),\qquad H_v=W.
\]
With
\[
 \mu'_u=\mu_u(1-t),\qquad
 \mu'_v=\mu_v+\mu_ut,
 \qquad
 s:=\alpha'_{vu}=\frac{\mu_ut}{\mu_v+\mu_ut},
\]
the new root polynomials are
\[
 H'_u=U,\qquad H'_v=W(1-s+sU).
\]
No third vertex is an ancestor of \(u\), because \(u\) is a source, and no third vertex is an ancestor of \(v\), because its sole parent is \(u\).  The same assertion holds after reversal with \(u,v\) interchanged.  Therefore every \(H_a\) with \(a\notin\{u,v\}\) is unchanged.

The two nonconstant contributions satisfy the formal-polynomial identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mu_uH_u+\mu_vH_v
 &=\mu_uU(1-t+tW)+\mu_vW\\
 &=\mu_u(1-t)U+\mu_vW+\mu_utUW\\
 &=\mu'_uU+\mu'_vW(1-s+sU)\\
 &=\mu'_uH'_u+\mu'_vH'_v,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\mu'_v(1-s)=\mu_v\) and \(\mu'_vs=\mu_ut\).  Also
\[
 \mu'_u+\mu'_v=\mu_u+\mu_v.
\]
Substituting these two identities into
\(\Psi(z)=\sum_a\mu_a(H_a(z)-1)\) proves equality of the complete log-PGFs, including the constant coefficient.

Finally, \(\mu'_u>0\), \(\mu'_v>0\), and
\[
 0<\frac{\mu_ut}{\mu_v+\mu_ut}<1
\]
because \(\mu_u,\mu_v>0\) and \(0<t<1\); every unchanged parameter remains strict.  This proves all assertions, and the algebra explicitly permits overlapping descendant sets.

%%% FIELD fixed-decoder-proof
Let \(G\in\mathcal D(P)\).  By the definition of the observational fiber, at least one strict parameter point \((\mu,\alpha)\) on \(G\) induces \(P\).  Define
\[
 \beta_i:=\prod_{j\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(i)}(1-\alpha_{ij}).
\]
The coefficient of \(z_i\) in the root-cluster formula can only come from the innovation at \(i\), with every outgoing trial closed.  Hence
\[
 c_{e_i}=\mu_i\beta_i>0.
\tag{4}
\]
For an edge \(i\to j\), exact support \(\{i,j\}\) requires the trial \(i\to j\) to be open and every other outgoing trial from the occurrences at \(i\) and \(j\) to be closed.  Acyclicity excludes a contribution rooted at \(j\).  Therefore
\[
 c_{e_i+e_j}
 =\mu_i\alpha_{ij}
   \prod_{h\in\operatorname{Ch}_G(i)\setminus\{j\}}(1-\alpha_{ih})\,\beta_j
 =\frac{c_{e_i}\alpha_{ij}\beta_j}{1-\alpha_{ij}}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Solving (5), using \(1-\alpha_{ij}>0\), gives
\[
 \alpha_{ij}
 =\frac{c_{e_i+e_j}}
        {c_{e_i+e_j}+c_{e_i}\beta_j}.
\tag{6}
\]

Evaluate (6) in reverse topological order.  At a sink, \(\beta_i=1\).  Once all children of \(i\) have been processed, every \(\beta_j\) on the right of (6) is known, so all outgoing \(\alpha_{ij}\) are determined; then \(\beta_i\) is determined by its product definition, and (4) gives
\[
 \mu_i=\frac{c_{e_i}}{\beta_i}.
\tag{7}
\]
Every denominator in (6) is positive by (4)--(5), the quotient in (6) lies strictly between zero and one, every \(\beta_i\) is positive, and (7) is positive.  Induction over the reverse topological order proves that the recursion returns the generating parameter point.  Applying the same argument to any second parameter point on \(G\) inducing \(P\) shows equality edge by edge and then vertex by vertex, proving uniqueness.

For the rank claim, let \(m=|E(G)|\), let \(\Theta_G=(0,\infty)^V\times(0,1)^{E(G)}\), and let \(\Phi_G\) be the fixed-DAG coefficient map.  Let \(\pi\Phi_G\) retain the \(d\) singleton coordinates and the \(m\) pair coordinates indexed by edges of \(G\).  Equations (6)--(7) define a rational map \(R_G\) on a neighborhood of every point of \(\pi\Phi_G(\Theta_G)\), since all its displayed denominators are positive, and the preceding calculation proves
\[
 R_G\circ\pi\Phi_G=\operatorname{id}_{\Theta_G}.
\]
Both maps are differentiable on the strict domain.  Differentiating gives
\[
 D R_G\,D(\pi\Phi_G)=I_{d+m}.
\]
Thus \(D(\pi\Phi_G)\), and hence the full coefficient Jacobian \(D\Phi_G\), has column rank \(d+m\) everywhere on \(\Theta_G\).

%%% FIELD source-forest-proof
Fix \(P\) generated by the strict model and fix any \(G\in\mathcal D(P)\).  The local-signature theorem shows simultaneously that \(S(P)\) is the undirected skeleton of \(G\) and that \(A(P)\) consists exactly of all arrows of \(G\) whose head has indegree at least two.  Remove those arrows and retain the orientations on the remaining skeleton \(F(P)\).  A vertex in \(M(P)\) then has residual indegree zero, while every vertex outside \(M(P)\) has residual indegree at most one.

Suppose that the undirected residual graph contained a cycle.  Choose the latest vertex of that cycle in a topological ordering of \(G\).  Both incident cycle edges point into that vertex, giving residual indegree at least two, a contradiction.  Hence \(F(P)\) is a forest.

Let \(T\) be a connected component of \(F(P)\), with \(r=|T|\).  Since \(T\) is a tree, it has \(r-1\) edges, and therefore
\[
 \sum_{v\in T}\operatorname{indeg}_{T}(v)=r-1.
\]
Every summand is at most one, so exactly one vertex has residual indegree zero.  Every member of \(T\cap M(P)\) has residual indegree zero, proving
\[
 |T\cap M(P)|\le 1.
\]
If \(T\cap M(P)=\{m\}\), then \(m\) is the unique residual root.  Along the unique path starting at \(m\), the first edge must point away from \(m\); after a vertex has received its unique incoming edge, the next path edge must point away from it.  Induction along paths shows that all of \(T\) is directed away from \(m\).  If \(T\cap M(P)=\varnothing\), the same argument shows that \(T\) is directed away from its unique residual root, which may be any vertex of \(T\).

There is no arrow entering a free component \(T\) from outside.  Indeed, a removed arrow has its head in \(M(P)\), whereas \(T\cap M(P)=\varnothing\); and an unremoved edge incident to \(T\) would be an edge of \(F(P)\), putting its other endpoint in the same component.  Thus the residual root of a free component is a global source of \(G\), and every other vertex in that component has its tree predecessor as its sole parent.  The preceding arguments apply to every \(G\in\mathcal D(P)\), because \(S(P)\), \(A(P)\), and \(M(P)\) are law functionals.  They prove the necessary inclusion
\[
 \mathcal D(P)\subseteq
 \{G_\rho:\rho(T)\in T\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal T_0(P)\}.
\tag{8}
\]

For sufficiency, start from the fixed strict representation \(G\).  In a free tree with current root \(v_0\), let
\(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_\ell\) be the unique path to a desired root \(v_\ell\).  Initially \(v_0\) is a global source and \(v_1\) has sole parent \(v_0\), so the source-reversal theorem applies to \(v_0\to v_1\).  After that reversal, \(v_1\) is the root; inductively, at step \(h\), \(v_h\) is a global source and \(v_{h+1}\) has sole parent \(v_h\).  Thus the finite sequence
\[
 \mathsf R_{v_0\to v_1},\ 
 \mathsf R_{v_1\to v_2},\ \ldots,\ 
 \mathsf R_{v_{\ell-1}\to v_\ell}
\]
preserves the complete law and keeps every parameter strict.  It changes no arrow outside that free tree.  The same construction can consequently be performed independently in every free component.  Arbitrarily overlapping descendants cause no difficulty because the source-reversal identity made no disjointness assumption.  The resulting graph is exactly \(G_\rho\), and it has a strict parameter point inducing \(P\).  This proves the reverse inclusion in (8), hence
\[
 \mathcal D(P)=
 \{G_\rho:\rho(T)\in T\text{ for every }T\in\mathcal T_0(P)\}.
\]
The result is necessary and sufficient and enumerates all admissible completions; in this precise sense it strictly adds a global converse and an exhaustive fiber to the local/pathwise sufficient orientation conclusions of Xiang et al.\ \cite[Theorem~7]{xiang2024identifiability} and Qiao et al.\ \cite[Theorem~7]{qiao2024causal}.

%%% FIELD cardinality-proof
By the source-forest classification, a compatible graph is specified by choosing exactly one root in each free tree, with anchored components fixed.  Choices in distinct components affect disjoint residual edge sets.  Moreover, two different roots in the same tree give different orientations: the root is the unique vertex of residual indegree zero.  Hence the root-choice map is injective and surjective, and finiteness gives
\[
 |\mathcal D(P)|=\prod_{T\in\mathcal T_0(P)}|T|,
\]
with the empty product equal to one.

Every arrow in \(A(P)\) is compelled by its local head signature.  In an anchored residual tree, the anchor is fixed by such a signature, and every residual edge is forced away from the anchor along its unique tree path; that path together with the anchor signature is a certificate of its orientation.  Thus every edge outside a free tree is compelled.

Conversely, let \(\{a,b\}\) be an edge of a free tree.  Choosing \(a\) as the component root orients it as \(a\to b\), while choosing \(b\) as the component root orients it as \(b\to a\).  Both completions belong to \(\mathcal D(P)\), so the edge is not compelled.  More constructively, from any current root one moves the root along the unique path first to \(a\) and then, if desired, across \(a-b\) to \(b\).  At each step the current root is a global source and the next vertex has it as sole parent, so the corresponding finite sequence of maps \(\mathsf R_{u\to v}\) is valid and preserves the complete law with strict parameters.  This supplies the claimed ambiguity certificates and proves the if-and-only-if assertion.

%%% FIELD degree-four-proof
The reverse implication is immediate: if \(P=P'\), then their log-PGFs and hence all coordinates in \(t_4\) coincide.  For the forward implication, assume \(t_4(P)=t_4(P')\).  The definitions of \(S(P)\) and \(\mathsf{Head}_P\) use only singleton, pair, degree-three, and degree-four coordinates.  The local-signature theorem therefore gives identical \(S\), \(A\), \(M\), \(F\), and free-component collections for the two laws.  Applying the source-forest theorem to both laws yields
\[
 \mathcal D(P)=\{G_\rho\}=\mathcal D(P').
\tag{9}
\]
The set in (9) is nonempty because both laws have strict representations.  Fix any \(G\) in this common set.  The rational decoder uses only the common singleton and pair coefficients.  Its uniqueness theorem gives
\[
 \theta_G(P)=\theta_G(P').
\tag{10}
\]
Substitution of (10) into the root-cluster formula gives equality of the complete log-PGFs, including their constant terms:
\[
 \Psi_P(z)=\sum_{i\in V}\mu_i(H_i(z)-1)=\Psi_{P'}(z).
\]
Exponentiating shows equality of the probability-generating functions.  Since
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\prod_{i\in V}z_i^{X_i}\right]
 =\sum_{x\in\mathbb N_0^V}P(X=x)z^x,
\]
coefficient equality gives \(P(X=x)=P'(X=x)\) for every \(x\), hence \(P=P'\).  Equations (9) and (10) prove the two additional fiber and parameter conclusions.

%%% FIELD coefficient-confidence-proof
Let
\[
 G_P(z):=\mathbb E_P\!\left[\prod_{i\in V}z_i^{X_i}\right]
 =\sum_{x\in\mathbb N_0^V}p_xz^x.
\]
Because \(p_0>0\), in the ring of formal power series we may write
\[
 G_P(z)=p_0(1+R(z)),\qquad
 R(z)=\sum_{x\ne0}\frac{p_x}{p_0}z^x.
\]
Every monomial of \(R\) has positive total degree.  Therefore, for a multi-index \(k\) with \(1\le |k|\le4\), only the powers \(R^\ell\) with \(1\le\ell\le |k|\) can contribute to \([z^k]\log(1+R)\), and every contributing \(x_a\) satisfies \(|x_a|\le4\).  The formal identity \(\log(1+R)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}(-1)^{\ell+1}R^\ell/\ell\) consequently gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_k
 &=[z^k]\bigl(\log G_P(z)-\log p_0\bigr)\\
 &=\sum_{\ell=1}^{|k|}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{\ell}
   \sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_\ell=k\\x_a\ne0}}
   \prod_{a=1}^{\ell}\frac{p_{x_a}}{p_0}
 =T_k((p_x)_{x\in I_4}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The inner sum is over ordered tuples, exactly as in the definition of \(T\).  Thus no analytic radius-of-convergence or omitted higher-count cell is involved.

It remains to prove simultaneous coverage.  Put \(L=|I_4|\), and for fixed \(x\in I_4\) set \(Y_b=\mathbf 1\{X^{(b)}=x\}\).  Under independent sampling, the \(Y_b\) are independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(p_x\).  For completeness, define
\[
 f(\lambda)=\log\mathbb E\exp\{\lambda(Y_b-p_x)\}.
\]
Under exponential tilting, \(f''(\lambda)\) is the variance of a Bernoulli variable, and hence \(f''(\lambda)\le1/4\).  Since \(f(0)=f'(0)=0\), integration twice gives
\[
 f(\lambda)\le\frac{\lambda^2}{8}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
\]
Independence and Markov's inequality then imply, for \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{\widehat p_x-p_x\ge r\}
 &\le\inf_{\lambda>0}
   \exp\left(-\lambda nr+\frac{n\lambda^2}{8}\right)
 =e^{-2nr^2},\\
 \Pr\{p_x-\widehat p_x\ge r\}&\le e^{-2nr^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the first infimum is attained at \(\lambda=4r\), and the second line applies the same argument to \(-Y_b\).  A union bound at \(r=\epsilon_n\) yields
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{
   \max_{x\in I_4}|\widehat p_x-p_x|>\epsilon_n
 \right\}
 \le 2L e^{-2n\epsilon_n^2}=\eta.
\tag{12}
\]
On the complementary event, the vector \(q=(p_x)_{x\in I_4}\) is feasible for \(Q_n\): it is coordinatewise nonnegative, its truncated sum is at most one, \(q_0=p_0>0\), and it satisfies every interval constraint.  By (11), \(T(q)=t_4(P)\), whence \(t_4(P)\in C_n\).  Combining this with (12) proves
\[
 \Pr_P\{t_4(P)\in C_n\}\ge1-\eta.
\]
The bound (12) is distribution-free and contains no division by a lower bound for \(p_0\).  It is therefore uniform over \(\mathcal M_d^\circ\), although the random set \(C_n\) may be unbounded when its feasible values of \(q_0\) approach zero.

%%% FIELD outer-confidence-proof
Let
\[
 \mathcal E_n:=\{t_4(P)\in C_n\}.
\]
The coefficient-confidence theorem gives \(\Pr_P(\mathcal E_n)\ge1-\eta\), uniformly over the strict model.  On \(\mathcal E_n\), take any \(G\in\mathcal D(P)\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal D(P)\), there is a strict parameter point on \(G\) inducing \(P\).  Hence \(G\in\mathcal D(t_4(P))\).  Since \(t_4(P)\in C_n\), the definition of the outward inverse gives
\[
 G\in\mathcal D(t_4(P))
 \subseteq\bigcup_{t\in C_n}\mathcal D(t)=\mathcal D_n.
\]
This holds for every \(G\in\mathcal D(P)\), so
\[
 \Pr_P\{\mathcal D(P)\subseteq\mathcal D_n\}
 \ge\Pr_P(\mathcal E_n)\ge1-\eta.
\tag{13}
\]

Define the reporting rule to output no arrow when \(\mathcal D_n=\varnothing\), and otherwise to output only an arrow that occurs with the same orientation in every \(G\in\mathcal D_n\).  If (13)'s inclusion event holds, every reported arrow occurs with that orientation in every member of the subset \(\mathcal D(P)\); therefore it is genuinely compelled throughout the population fiber.  Thus
\[
 \{\text{at least one falsely compelled arrow is reported}\}
 \subseteq
 \{\mathcal D(P)\nsubseteq\mathcal D_n\},
\]
and (13) proves that the probability of any false compulsion is at most \(\eta\), uniformly over \(\mathcal M_d^\circ\).  This is an unconditional familywise statement for a rule that acts only when the outer set is nonempty; division by \(\Pr_P\{\mathcal D_n\ne\varnothing\}\) is neither used nor justified.

The outward inverse satisfies \(\Pr_P\{\mathcal D(P)\subseteq\mathcal D_n\}\ge1-\eta\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M_d^\circ\). If the reporting rule outputs no arrow when \(\mathcal D_n=\varnothing\) and otherwise reports only arrows present with the same orientation in every \(G\in\mathcal D_n\), then the unconditional probability that it reports any arrow not compelled throughout \(\mathcal D(P)\) is at most \(\eta\).

%%% FIELD oeq-partial
For each fixed labeled DAG \(G\), membership \(G\in\mathcal D_n\) reduces to one finite existential feasibility problem over \(q\), \(\mu\), and \(\alpha\): impose \(q\in Q_n\), \(\mu_i>0\), \(0<\alpha_e<1\), and \(t_4(P_{G,\mu,\alpha})=T(q)\).  The root-cluster recursion makes the left side polynomial in \((\mu,\alpha)\), and multiplication by the positive powers of \(q_0\) appearing in \(T\) clears every rational denominator.  Thus an exact feasibility oracle followed by finite enumeration of labeled DAGs returns exactly \(\mathcal D_n\), and because it returns the set in the frozen definition, the coverage theorem remains valid.  This reduction supplies neither polynomial delay nor a polynomial-size inclusion or exclusion certificate.
