Fix a returned certificate \(\kappa_H\) and first work on the normalized expansion.  By \(\textnormal{thm:normalization-equivalence}\), for every \(\theta\in\Theta_H\) there is a unique \(\eta=\nu_H(\theta)\in\mathcal I_H\) such that
\[
 O_H^N(\eta)=O_H(\theta),
 \qquad
 T_{a,H}^N(\eta)=T_{a,H}(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose first that \(\kappa_H=(F_H,\mathcal D_H^{\mathrm{den}},\mathfrak D_H)\).  The accepting computation is finite by \(\textnormal{thm:transition-soundness-termination}\).  Induct over its transition log.  The induction invariant is that every current labelled kernel is the normalized structural kernel represented by that label and that each displayed rational equality is valid after multiplication by the product of the divisors already entered in the ledger.  The base is the normalized observation kernel \(o_H^N(\eta)\).  The context step preserves the invariant by evaluating a known zero--one selector multiplexer at a value \(s\in H\).  A truncated-factorization step preserves it by the finite chain rule.  A fixing step records the actual current conditional \(m\), forms \(K'=K/m\), and records the reversible identity
\[
 mK'=K.
\tag{2}
\]
The Algorithm--2 hybrid step and the final district product and marginalization preserve the represented kernel by the local identities proved in \(\textnormal{thm:transition-soundness-termination}\).  Finally, a residual projection is accepted only when, componentwise, its generated numerator \(P\), denominator product \(D\), and the retained constraint polynomials \(J_1,\ldots,J_M\) satisfy an exact polynomial identity
\[
 D(\xi)t_{a,H}^N(\xi)-P\{o_H^N(\xi)\}
 =\sum_{j=1}^M h_j(\xi)J_j(\xi).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\eta\in\mathcal I_H\), every \(J_j(\eta)=0\) by the intrinsic definition of \(\mathcal I_H\).  Thus the same invariant holds whether the formula was assembled by the nonresidual cases or accepted by the residual projection verifier.

By \(\textnormal{lem:restricted-support-denominator-positivity}\), every actual context, chain-rule, fixing, or elimination divisor recorded on this branch is positive at \(O_H^N(\eta)=O_H(\theta)\), and every branch-required supported cell is positive.  Hence
\[
 O_H(\Theta_H)\subseteq\mathfrak D_H.
\tag{4}
\]
All cross-multiplications in the transition induction, including (2) and (3), are therefore reversible on the compatible image.  At the terminal label the represented normalized kernel is \(t_{a,H}^N\), so (1) and (4) give
\[
 F_H\{O_H(\theta)\}
 =F_H\{O_H^N(\eta)\}
 =T_{a,H}^N(\eta)
 =T_{a,H}(\theta),
 \qquad \theta\in\Theta_H.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(F_H\) is defined only on \(\mathfrak D_H\), equation (5) asserts nothing at an ambient table outside that branch domain.

Suppose next that \(\kappa_H\) is negative, and fix \(b\in\mathcal B_H\).  Let \(\eta_b^+\) and \(\eta_b^-\) be the two rational-affine normalized records generated before gate reinsertion.  The verifier checks, for each sign, all primitive simplex inequalities and all equalities \(g_H=0\), as well as

 s_H^N(\eta_b^\pm)(s)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s\in H
\tag{6}
\]
and

 o_H^N(\eta_b^\pm)(c)>0

\tag{7}

These are precisely the intrinsic membership conditions for \(\mathcal I_H\), including the retained root-factorization and gate-sharing equations.  Therefore \(\eta_b^\pm\in\mathcal I_H\), and the well-typed reinsertion maps give
\[
 \theta_b^\pm:=\rho_H(\eta_b^\pm)\in\Theta_H.
\tag{8}
\]
The other two acceptance tests are equality of every supported observation polynomial and a target-difference polynomial having a certified nonzero constant sign throughout the open rational box.  Consequently
\[
 O_H^N(\eta_b^+)=O_H^N(\eta_b^-),
 \qquad
 T_{a,H}^N(\eta_b^+)\ne T_{a,H}^N(\eta_b^-).
\tag{9}
\]
Applying the mutual-inverse identities of \(\textnormal{thm:normalization-equivalence}\) to (8)--(9) yields
\[
 O_H(\theta_b^+)=O_H(\theta_b^-),
 \qquad
 T_{a,H}(\theta_b^+)\ne T_{a,H}(\theta_b^-).
\tag{10}
\]
Membership in \(\Theta_H\), together with (6)--(7), also gives selector support exactly \(H\) and strict positivity on every structurally admissible supported cell.  Equations (4)--(5) and (8)--(10) prove the two asserted certificate alternatives.

Fix a positive certificate, \(\theta\in\Theta_H\), and \(p_H=O_H(\theta)\), and suppose that \(\mathbf p_H\) lies in the differentiable interior of the branch domain \(\mathfrak D_H\).  Put \(C=\mathcal C_H^{\mathrm{adm}}(G^+)\).  The declared finite categorical model makes \(m_H=|C|<\infty\).  For \(X_i=(V_i,S_i)\), the empirical vector satisfies
\[
 \widehat{\mathbf p}_H=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n e_{X_i}^C,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{p_H}(e_{X_i}^C)=\mathbf p_H.
\tag{1}
\]
The branch domain is given by finitely many strict coordinate and registered-denominator inequalities.  Those defining functions are continuous.  Interiority therefore gives a relative neighborhood \(N\) of \(\mathbf p_H\) in the simplex and \(\delta>0\) such that the relative \(\ell_\infty\)-ball of radius \(\delta\) is contained in \(N\subseteq\mathfrak D_H\).  Since
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H(c)\}
 =\frac{p_H(c)\{1-p_H(c)\}}{n},
\]
Chebyshev's inequality in each of the finitely many coordinates and a union bound imply
\[
 \Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\notin N\}
 \le
 \frac1{n\delta^2}\sum_{c\in C}p_H(c)\{1-p_H(c)\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(\Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H\notin\mathfrak D_H\}\to0\).

All registered denominators remain nonzero on a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(\mathbf p_H\).  The finite rational circuit \(F_H\) thus has a continuously differentiable ambient-coordinate extension there.  Taylor expansion, (1), and the fact that
\[
 \mathbb E_{p_H}\left\|\sqrt n
 (\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H)\right\|_2^2
 =\operatorname{tr}\{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf p_H)
 -\mathbf p_H\mathbf p_H^{\mathsf T}\}<\infty
\]
give, on the on-domain event,
\[
 F_H(\widehat p_H)-F_H(p_H)
 =J_H(\mathbf p_H)(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H)
 +o_p(n^{-1/2})
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{IF}_H(X_i)+o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{3}
\]
The influence function in (3) has mean zero by (1) and finite variance because \(C\) is finite.  The same differentiability calculation along every local multinomial submodel establishes regularity.  Thus \(\textnormal{lem:asymptotic-linear-normality}\), after premultiplication by any fixed \(c\in\mathbb R^{k_Y}\), gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,c^{\mathsf T}
 \{F_H(\widehat p_H)-F_H(p_H)\}
 \rightsquigarrow
 N\bigl(0,c^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Hc\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

On the reporting event, expansion of the defined empirical influence functions gives the exact identity
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_H
 =J_H(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H)
 \{\operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H)
 -\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\widehat{\mathbf p}_H^{\mathsf T}\}
 J_H(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) gives \(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\to\mathbf p_H\) in probability, and the Jacobian of a rational circuit is continuous wherever its registered denominators are nonzero.  Hence
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_H\longrightarrow
 J_H(\mathbf p_H)
 \{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf p_H)-\mathbf p_H\mathbf p_H^{\mathsf T}\}
 J_H(\mathbf p_H)^{\mathsf T}
 =\Sigma_H
 \quad\text{in probability},
\tag{6}
\]
where the last equality is the variance of the influence function in (3).

By \(\textnormal{thm:certificate-soundness}\), \(F_H(p_H)=Q_{a,H}\).  For fixed \(y\in\mathcal X_Y\), take \(c=e_y^Y\) in (4).  If
\[
 v_{H,y}:=(e_y^Y)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_H e_y^Y>0,
\]
then (6) gives \(\widehat v_{H,y}/v_{H,y}\to1\) in probability and \(\Pr(\widehat v_{H,y}>0)\to1\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat Q_{a,H}(y)-Q_{a,H}(y)\}}
 {\sqrt{\widehat v_{H,y}}}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,1).
\tag{7}
\]
The definition of \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\), equations (2) and (7), and the fact that no interval is reported off the domain imply
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H\in\mathfrak D_H,
 Q_{a,H}(y)\in CI_y\}=1-\alpha.
\tag{8}
\]

For a fixed nonzero \(\lambda\) with \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_H\lambda>0\), take \(c=\lambda\) in (4) and premultiply (6) by \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\lambda\).  The same studentization argument yields
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H\in\mathfrak D_H,
 \lambda^{\mathsf T}Q_{a,H}\in CI_{\lambda}\}=1-\alpha.
\tag{9}
\]
The neighborhoods used in (2) depend on the fixed interior law.  Thus (8)--(9) are pointwise statements and make no uniform claim as a supported cell or registered denominator approaches zero; on the off-domain event the output is \(\bot_H\), not an estimate.

Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_H\) and write \(p_H=O_H(\theta)\).  Order the entries of the accepting branch ledger by creation time.  We prove simultaneously, by induction over this order, that every recorded divisor is positive and that every current-kernel cell marked branch-reachable by the ledger is positive.

 d_s=p_H(S=s)=
 \sum_{v:(v,s)\in\mathcal C_H^{\mathrm{adm}}(G^+)}p_H(v,s).
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\textnormal{ass:exact-selector-support}\), \(d_s>0\), so the displayed summation set is nonempty. Evaluating a known selector gate changes no probability by division and therefore preserves this base claim.

Assume the simultaneous claim through ledger entry \(j-1\).  A denominator introduced by \(\operatorname{TF}_{\prec}\) is, by \(\textnormal{def:kernel-operations}\), the actual marginal of the current positive kernel over a nonempty branch-reachable conditioning fiber.  It therefore has the form
\[
 d_j=\sum_{x\in B_j}K_j(x)>0,
 \qquad B_j\ne\varnothing,
\tag{2}
\]
because every summand is positive by the induction hypothesis.  The resulting conditional is a ratio of positive quantities and its reachable cells remain positive.

At a computed fixing step, the divisor is the actual current nested conditional, constructed from current-kernel marginals.  Its numerator and conditioning marginal are nonempty sums of positive reachable cells, so both are positive by the preceding paragraph; the conditional is therefore positive.  The successor identity is recorded as
\[
 d_jK_{j+1}=K_j.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every reachable successor cell is positive.  The same conclusion holds for a hybrid product because it is a finite product of positive truncated conditional factors, and ancestor marginalization preserves positivity by a nonempty finite sum.

Finally, a residual-elimination divisor is eligible only after cancellation and sign normalization have produced the exact ledger factorization
\[
 d_j(p_H)=r_j
 \prod_{i\in E_j}d_i(p_H)
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{L_j}
 \left\{\sum_{x\in B_{j\ell}}K_{j\ell}(p_H,x)\right\},
 \qquad r_j\in\mathbb Q_{>0},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(E_j\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\), every \(B_{j\ell}\) is nonempty, and every displayed kernel cell is marked branch-reachable.  The first product in (4) is positive by induction, and every sum in the second product is positive by the simultaneous kernel-cell claim.  Hence \(d_j(p_H)>0\).

Finite induction proves positivity of every actual divisor retained in \(\mathcal D_H^{\mathrm{den}}\), including any intermediate divisor later cancelled from the reduced terminal expression.  The branch-required coordinate inequalities also hold by \(\textnormal{ass:admissible-cell-interior}\).  Therefore \(p_H\in\mathfrak D_H\).  Since \(\theta\) was arbitrary,
\[
 O_H(\Theta_H)\subseteq\mathfrak D_H.
\]
All inequalities are pointwise at the chosen compatible law; no common positive lower bound has been used.
