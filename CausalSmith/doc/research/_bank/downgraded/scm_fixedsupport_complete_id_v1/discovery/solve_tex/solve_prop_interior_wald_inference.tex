Fix a positive certificate and a compatible parameter \(\theta\in\Theta_H\), and write \(p_H=O_H(\theta)\).  Assume, as in the proposition, that \(\mathbf p_H\) is in the differentiable interior of the branch domain \(\mathfrak D_H\).  Put \(C=\mathcal C_H^{\mathrm{adm}}(G^+)\), and let \(m_H=|C|<\infty\); finiteness follows from the fixed finite alphabets.  For \(X_i=(V_i,S_i)\), the vectorized empirical table satisfies
\[
 \widehat{\mathbf p}_H=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n e_{X_i}^C,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{p_H}(e_{X_i}^C)=\mathbf p_H.
\]
In particular, for every \(c\in C\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H(c)\}=p_H(c),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H(c)\}
 =\frac{p_H(c)\{1-p_H(c)\}}{n}.
\]

The definition of \(\mathfrak D_H\) imposes finitely many strict inequalities, each involving either a coordinate or one of the registered polynomial denominators.  Those functions are continuous.  Thus interiority supplies a relative-open neighborhood \(N\) of \(\mathbf p_H\) in the probability simplex such that \(N\subseteq\mathfrak D_H\).  Choose \(\delta>0\) so that the relative \(\ell_\infty\)-ball of radius \(\delta\) about \(\mathbf p_H\) is contained in \(N\).  Chebyshev's inequality, applied coordinatewise, and the union bound give
\[
 \Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\notin N\}
 \leq
 \sum_{c\in C}
 \Pr_{p_H}\{|\widehat p_H(c)-p_H(c)|\geq\delta\}
 \leq
 \frac{1}{n\delta^2}\sum_{c\in C}p_H(c)\{1-p_H(c)\}
 \longrightarrow 0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H\notin\mathfrak D_H\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{1}
\]

All registered denominators are positive on \(N\).  Since \(F_H\) is a finite rational circuit whose only divisions are by those denominators, it has a continuously differentiable ambient-coordinate extension on an open neighborhood of \(N\).  Fr\'{e}chet differentiability at \(\mathbf p_H\) therefore yields, on \(\{\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\in N\}\),
\[
 F_H(\widehat p_H)-F_H(p_H)
 =J_H(\mathbf p_H)(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H)+r_n,
 \qquad
 \frac{\|r_n\|}{\|\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H\|}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}. \tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb E_{p_H}\!\left[\left\|\sqrt n
 (\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H)\right\|_2^2\right]
 =\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf p_H)
 -\mathbf p_H\mathbf p_H^{\mathsf T}\right\}<\infty,
\]
so \(\sqrt n(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H)=O_p(1)\).  Equation (2), together with (1), then gives \(r_n=o_p(n^{-1/2})\).  Finally,
\[
 J_H(\mathbf p_H)(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H-\mathbf p_H)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 J_H(\mathbf p_H)(e_{X_i}^C-\mathbf p_H)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{IF}_H(X_i). \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) prove the asserted asymptotic linearity.  Formally, one may define the local smooth extension off the reporting event; it agrees with the reported estimator whenever \(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\in N\), an event whose probability tends to one by (1), so this extension does not alter the asserted on-domain expansion.

For completeness, the influence function in (3) has mean zero because
\[
 \mathbb E_{p_H}\{\operatorname{IF}_H(X_i)\}
 =J_H(\mathbf p_H)\{\mathbb E_{p_H}(e_{X_i}^C)-\mathbf p_H\}=0,
\]
and it has finite second moment because \(C\) is finite.  The smooth plug-in is regular at this interior law: for every tangent vector \(h\in\mathbb R^{m_H}\) with \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}h=0\) for which \(\mathbf p_H+t h\) remains in the simplex for sufficiently small \(|t|\), the same expansion gives
\[
 F_H(\mathbf p_H+t h)-F_H(\mathbf p_H)
 =tJ_H(\mathbf p_H)h+o(t),
\]
so its first-order law is stable along every local interior multinomial submodel.  Applying \(\textnormal{lem:asymptotic-linear-normality}\) to (3), after premultiplication by any fixed \(c\in\mathbb R^{k_Y}\), gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,c^{\mathsf T}\{F_H(\widehat p_H)-F_H(p_H)\}
 \ \rightsquigarrow\ 
 N\!\left(0,c^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Hc\right), \tag{4}
\]
because the scalar influence function is \(c^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{IF}_H\) and its variance is \(c^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Hc\).

It remains to justify studentization.  Direct expansion of the definition of \(\widehat\Sigma_H\) gives, on the reporting event,
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_H
 =J_H(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H)
 \left\{\operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H)
 -\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\widehat{\mathbf p}_H^{\mathsf T}\right\}
 J_H(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{5}
\]
The Jacobian of a rational circuit is continuous wherever its registered denominators are nonzero.  Hence (1), consistency of \(\widehat{\mathbf p}_H\), continuity of \(J_H\), and (5) imply
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_H\longrightarrow
 J_H(\mathbf p_H)
 \left\{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf p_H)
 -\mathbf p_H\mathbf p_H^{\mathsf T}\right\}
 J_H(\mathbf p_H)^{\mathsf T}
 =\Sigma_H
 \quad\text{in probability}. \tag{6}
\]
The last equality follows by taking the variance of the displayed definition of \(\operatorname{IF}_H(X_i)\).

By \(\textnormal{thm:certificate-soundness}\), \(F_H(p_H)=Q_{a,H}\).  Fix \(y\in\mathcal X_Y\), let \(c=e_y^Y\), and put
\[
 v_{H,y}=(e_y^Y)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_H e_y^Y.
\]
Under the stated condition \(v_{H,y}>0\), (6) gives \(\widehat v_{H,y}/v_{H,y}\to1\) in probability and, in particular, \(\Pr(\widehat v_{H,y}>0)\to1\).  Combining this fact with (4) shows that the studentized statistic
\[
 Z_{n,y}=
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat Q_{a,H}(y)-Q_{a,H}(y)\}}
 {\sqrt{\widehat v_{H,y}}}
\]
converges in distribution to \(N(0,1)\) on an event whose probability tends to one.  This conclusion may be obtained without an additional limit theorem: on the event \(|\widehat v_{H,y}/v_{H,y}-1|\leq\varepsilon\), the absolute studentized statistic is bounded between the corresponding fixed rescalings by \((1\pm\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\); apply (4), then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), using continuity of the normal distribution function.  Therefore, by the defining quantile identity for \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\),
\[
 \Pr_{p_H}\{|Z_{n,y}|\leq z_{1-\alpha/2}\}\longrightarrow1-\alpha. \tag{7}
\]
The event in (7), where the interval is defined, is exactly \(\{Q_{a,H}(y)\in CI_y\}\).  Intersecting with \(\{\widehat p_H\in\mathfrak D_H\}\) changes its probability by at most the probability in (1).  Hence
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H\in\mathfrak D_H,
 \ Q_{a,H}(y)\in CI_y\}=1-\alpha. \tag{8}
\]

Now fix nonzero \(\lambda\) with \(v_{H,\lambda}=\lambda^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_H\lambda>0\).  Taking \(c=\lambda\) in (4) and premultiplying (6) by \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\lambda\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,\lambda^{\mathsf T}\{F_H(\widehat p_H)-Q_{a,H}\}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,v_{H,\lambda}),
 \qquad
 \lambda^{\mathsf T}\widehat\Sigma_H\lambda
 \longrightarrow v_{H,\lambda}
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Repeating the studentization and intersection argument used for (7)--(8) proves
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr_{p_H}\{\widehat p_H\in\mathfrak D_H,
 \ \lambda^{\mathsf T}Q_{a,H}\in CI_{\lambda}\}=1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the first interval covers only the specified component and the second covers only the specified fixed linear contrast, both pointwise at the fixed interior law.  If \(\widehat p_H\notin\mathfrak D_H\), the definition of the procedure returns \(\bot_H\) and reports no interval.  The argument used the fixed interior neighborhood \(N\) and supplies neither a uniform lower bound on the registered denominators nor coverage uniform over a positivity boundary.
