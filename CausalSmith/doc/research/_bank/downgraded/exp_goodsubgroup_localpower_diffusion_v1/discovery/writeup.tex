\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}goodsubgroup\_\allowbreak{}localpower\_\allowbreak{}diffusion}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For any declared finite bounded list of local causal-effect scenarios, the fixed arithmetic-mean closed e-test admits an adaptive subgroup-recruitment minimax-regret frontier. Exact Gaussian grid embedding and a quantitative terminal-envelope comparison transfer its discontinuous rejection risk, including zero-allocation faces, to and from the controlled score diffusion. Finite-scenario convex duality yields an attained least-favorable prior and posterior-state Markov response. A deterministic nested interval Bellman search uses a globally Lipschitz product of mesh-time survival tapers, a uniform observed-law escape charge, layerwise continuation moduli, and independent recovered-policy evaluation to supply convergent certified bounds. This object is complementary to the repository's unrestricted finite-population binary-contrast design-and-estimation frontier.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing ingredients are individually narrower than the conjunction proved here. Wager--Xu and Adusumilli provide score-clock and regular terminal-criterion diffusion architecture, and Araman--Caldentey provides a low-information posterior-control diffusion, but none proves boundary-stable transfer for the discontinuous multiple-decision loss of a fixed closed e-test. Burnett--Jennison and Ballarini et al. optimize enrichment under specified prior-weighted criteria rather than worst-case regret over a declared finite scenario list. Baas--ter Schure--van Rosmalen supplies a scalar e-value dynamic program, Rosenblum--Fang--Liu a discretized two-stage sparse program, Vovk--Wang arithmetic e-value averaging, and Howard et al. scalar time-uniform boundaries; those component techniques do not imply the robust diffusion frontier, predictable policy transfer, or the validated interval-Bellman certificate with independent recovered-policy evaluation. The complementary repository record \(\mathtt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier}\) instead optimizes unrestricted design and estimation for fixed finite-population binary contrasts through an exact \(2^K\) response-type game and an \(n^{-4/3}\) second-order minimax-MSE bracket.

\paragraph{Niche.} Holding the confirmatory closed e-test fixed isolates the design problem of predictably recruiting among prespecified subgroups under a finite list of local Gaussian causal-effect scenarios. The resulting target is a robust weighted missed-discovery regret frontier with legal zero-information faces.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves exact Gaussian grid embedding, quantitative boundary-stable transfer for the discontinuous rejection surfaces, uniform Bayes and oracle convergence, minimax-regret convergence, and predictable finite-policy attainment. For policy recovery it Markovizes a selected finite rule at the score--clock state under the zero-drift reference law, preserves every declared scenario terminal risk by terminal likelihood tilting, regularizes the finitely many action kernels simultaneously, and then applies a continuous-kernel block construction. It also proves termination of a deterministic nested interval-Bellman search. That search multiplies the regularized terminal loss by a product of globally Lipschitz mesh-time survival tapers, propagates a layerwise modulus through every Bellman, oracle, and fixed-policy recursion, charges the omitted likelihood-weighted contribution through a uniform observed-law escape bound, keeps standardized-Gaussian quadrature tails separate, and independently re-evaluates the returned policy mixture.

\section{Related work}

The accepted repository record \(\mathtt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier}\) is complementary, not a strict-extension predecessor: it optimizes unrestricted design and estimation for fixed finite-population binary contrasts, using an exact \(2^K\) response-type game and an \(n^{-4/3}\) second-order minimax-MSE bracket with certificates. This note instead fixes the arithmetic-mean closed e-test and optimizes predictable subgroup recruitment under finite local-Gaussian scenarios for weighted missed-discovery regret, proving boundary-stable transfer at the initial state and a validated interval-Bellman policy certificate. Wager--Xu and Adusumilli supply score-clock and regular terminal-criterion diffusion architecture; Araman--Caldentey is a low-information posterior-control donor for a regular terminal action or reward, not a source of discontinuous closed-testing transfer or minimax policy recovery. Burnett--Jennison and Ballarini et al. study prior-weighted enrichment under frequentist error control, whereas the present criterion is finite-scenario worst-case regret with an adversarial least-favorable prior. Rosenblum--Fang--Liu's discretized two-stage sparse linear program and Baas--ter Schure--van Rosmalen's scalar e-value dynamic program are complementary computational donors rather than results implying the present certificate. Vovk--Wang supplies arithmetic e-value averaging, which this note takes as fixed, and Howard et al. supplies the scalar martingale-boundary ingredient used for guard control and simultaneous inversion; neither component supplies recruitment optimization, the minimax frontier, or policy certification. The novelty claimed here is only their proved conjunction: boundary-stable transfer of the fixed discontinuous closed-e loss, robust finite-scenario recruitment, and qualitative validated policy certification.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-known-variance]\label{ass:gaussian-known-variance}
Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\) and \(A_i\), \(Y_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu_{A_i},1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian sequential experiment with known unit variance, \cite{adusumilli2025riskPolicies}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-scenario-scaling]\label{ass:local-scenario-scaling}
Under scenario \(m\), \(\mu_j=h_j^m/\sqrt n\) for every \(j\in\mathcal J\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Weak-signal local scaling, \cite{wagerxu2021diffusion}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-predictability]\label{ass:finite-predictability}
For each \(i\), the conditional law of \(A_i\) is \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\)-measurable and does not depend on the unknown scenario label.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Predictable sequential randomization, \cite{wagerxu2021diffusion}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:continuous-observed-control]\label{ass:continuous-observed-control}
The control \(u(t)\) is progressively measurable with respect to \(\mathcal F_t\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Observed-filtration allocation process, \cite{adusumilli2026representations}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:certificate-handle]\label{def:certificate-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{finite monotone primal-\allowbreak{}dual certificate algorithm}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Given \(\mathfrak E\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}\), enumerate deterministically all finite binary-rational candidates consisting of lower and upper terminal-envelope widths \(\delta\), small-information cutoffs \(\eta\), rational numbers \(G>H\) and \(\omega>0\), a time partition \(\Pi=\{0=t_0<\cdots<t_N=1\}\), allocation-clock and score meshes covering the outer score guard, a finite rational control net in \(\Delta(\mathcal J)\), a finite rational prior net in \(\Delta_M\) containing every point mass, a rational near-Bayes retention tolerance, a partition of a truncated standardized Gaussian box into rational cells, a rational compactly supported mollifier, and an outward-rounding precision.  The scalar \(\omega\) is the taper width and is distinct from the discovery-weight vector \(w\).  Define
\[
 \chi_{G,\omega}(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\|x\|_\infty\leq G,\\
 (G+\omega-\|x\|_\infty)/\omega,&G<\|x\|_\infty<G+\omega,\\
 0,&\|x\|_\infty\geq G+\omega,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 W_{G,\omega,\Pi}=\prod_{l=1}^N\chi_{G,\omega}(X(t_l)).
\]
The function \(\chi_{G,\omega}\) is \([0,1]\)-valued and \(1/\omega\)-Lipschitz.  The candidate computes the tapered regularized losses
\[
 \ell_{m,\delta}^{\pm}(X(1),Q(1))W_{G,\omega,\Pi}.
\]
Put
\[
 p_G=2K\exp\{-(G-H)^2/2\}.
\]
For the original loss or either regularized loss, let the subscript \(W\) denote multiplication by \(W_{G,\omega,\Pi}\).  The observed-law ledger uses
\[
 0\leq L_m(u)-L_{m,W}(u)\leq p_G,
 \quad B_W(\lambda)\leq B(\lambda)\leq B_W(\lambda)+p_G,
 \quad c_{m,W}\leq c_m\leq c_{m,W}+p_G.
\]
Consequently
\[
 B_W(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,W}-p_G
 \leq B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m
 \leq B_W(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,W}+p_G,
\]
and an independently evaluated policy obeys
\[
 r_m(u)\leq L_{m,W}^{+}(u)+p_G-c_{m,W}^{-}.
\]
Thus an affected endpoint is enlarged by \(p_G\), an affected gap is charged by at most \(2p_G\), and terminal-envelope errors remain separate.

Put \(c_H=H+H^2/2\) and
\[
 \Omega_\delta(d)=\min\!\left\{1,\left(\frac{32}{\delta}+2Kc_H\right)d\right\},
 \qquad
 L_\delta=\frac{32}{\delta}+3Kc_H.
\]
For each prior \(\lambda\), let
\[
 C_{G+\omega}=\max_{\substack{\|x\|_\infty\leq G+\omega\\q\in[0,1]^K}}
 \sum_{m=1}^M\lambda_mZ_m(x,q),
 \qquad
 S(x,q)=C_{G+\omega}^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M\lambda_mZ_m(x,q).
\]
The outer guard contains the origin, so \(C_{G+\omega}\geq1\), and \(0\leq S\leq1\) on it.  At time \(t_N\), define on the outer guard
\[
 U_N^\pm(x,q)=C_{G+\omega}^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M
 \lambda_mZ_m(x,q)\ell_{m,\delta}^{\pm}(x,q),
 \qquad H_N^\pm(x,q)=\chi_{G,\omega}(x)U_N^\pm(x,q),
\]
and set \(H_N^\pm=0\) when \(\|x\|_\infty\geq G+\omega\).  Recursively, with \(a_{l-1}=t_l-t_{l-1}\) and with \(P_a^v\) the zero-drift Gaussian score-clock transition at constant allocation \(v\), set
\[
 U_{l-1}^\pm=\inf_{v\in\Delta(\mathcal J)}P_{a_{l-1}}^vH_l^\pm,
 \qquad
 H_{l-1}^\pm=\chi_{G,\omega}U_{l-1}^\pm\quad(l-1\geq1).
\]
There is no taper at \(t_0=0\).  Each \(H_l^\pm\) is extended by zero on \(\{\|x\|_\infty\geq G+\omega\}\), is globally \([0,1]\)-valued, and is propagated with the layerwise modulus
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d)
 =\min\!\left\{1,\left(L_\delta+\frac{N-l+1}{\omega}\right)d\right\},
 \qquad 1\leq l\leq N.
\]
At every mesh time the multiplication \(H_l^\pm=\chi_{G,\omega}U_l^\pm\) is performed and verified by outward interval arithmetic.  A landing with \(\|x\|_\infty\geq G+\omega\) therefore annihilates the path product permanently, whereas a landing in the strip \(G<\|x\|_\infty<G+\omega\) multiplies by the certified interval enclosure of \(\chi_{G,\omega}\).  Re-entry cannot undo an earlier factor.

At a state cell of diameter \(d\), enclose a layer-\(l\) continuation by the barycentric sum of its vertex intervals enlarged by \(\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d)\).  Use this layerwise modulus for state-cell interpolation, boundary-straddling Gaussian-cell images, mollification, Bellman-residual accumulation, and every tapered Bayes, fixed-policy, and point-mass-oracle recursion.  For controls \(v,v'\), use the certified bound
\[
 \mathbb E\,\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l+1}\!\left(
 a\|v-v'\|_1+\sqrt{a\|v-v'\|_1}\,\|z\|_2\right).
\]
For every control-net point, scenario, and retained Gaussian cell, outward-round the cell probability, enclose its entire score-clock image, interval-enclose \(\chi_{G,\omega}\) on every strip cell, multiply outward by the continuation interval, and sum outward.  Wholly exterior cells receive value zero.  Only mass omitted from the separately truncated standardized-Gaussian quadrature is multiplied by \([0,1]\), because every tapered scaled continuation \(H_l^\pm\) has that global range.  This quadrature-tail item is never substituted for the omitted untruncated likelihood-weighted contribution, which is charged by \(p_G\), or by \(p_G/C_{G+\omega}\) before the reference-law scale is undone.

On each open time slab, rational compactly supported mollifications of the multilinear interpolants are checked cell by cell against the untapered reference-law Bellman operators.  Taylor's formula with integral remainder supplies certified time-discretization and control residuals and their finite-horizon accumulated bounds.  Taper multiplication is an exact mesh-time jump: at each \(t_l\) verify \(H_l^\pm=\chi_{G,\omega}U_l^\pm\) by outward interval arithmetic, and telescope generator inequalities on the open slabs together with these multiplicative jump inequalities.  No term of the form \((\chi_{G,\omega}-1)/a\) is inserted into a continuous generator.

Run the same tapered validated recursion at every point-mass prior to enclose the regularized oracle values.  Retain every enumerated grid policy certified to be near-Bayes at a prior-net point, solve outward-rounded rational primal and dual scenario-risk linear programs over those columns, and independently re-evaluate the selected finite mixture with a tapered fixed-policy interval recursion.  Extend the dual search from the prior net with the \(\ell_1\)-Lipschitz bound for the Bayes-regret functional.

Form the candidate ledger from the actual terminal-regularization, observed-law taper, time-discretization, layerwise state interpolation and control-mesh, Gaussian-cell, standardized-Gaussian quadrature-tail, mollification and Bellman-residual accumulation, mesh-time taper-jump, oracle and linear-program, recovered-policy, prior-net, and rounding contributions.  Apply the observed-law charges displayed above to the Bayes, fixed-policy, and point-mass-oracle endpoints, and charge at most \(2p_G\) to an affected certified gap.  For termination, the enumeration contains a diagonal subsequence with \(\omega=1\), \(G\to\infty\), \(\delta\downarrow0\), \(\eta=\sqrt\delta\), increasingly fine time and prior meshes, and state, control, Gaussian-cell, quadrature, mollification, and rounding refinements fast enough that the sum over layers of every term involving \(\widehat\Omega_{\delta,1,l}\) and every analogous residual tends to zero.  Each candidate remains finite.  Replace every cumulative lower endpoint by the maximum and every cumulative upper endpoint by the minimum over candidates examined so far, retaining an attaining finite-net prior for the current lower endpoint and the independently re-evaluated policy mixture that attains the current upper endpoint.  Accept the first candidate whose complete certified gap is at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}\), and call its cumulative endpoints \(\underline V_{\varepsilon}\) and \(\overline V_{\varepsilon}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}terminal-\allowbreak{}envelopes}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}minimax-\allowbreak{}regret}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}certificate-\allowbreak{}instance}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:strong-fwer]\label{prop:strong-fwer}
Supporting corollary (Vovk--Wang arithmetic e-merging and the closed-testing principle): for every \(n\), every \(\pi_n\in\Pi_n\), and every \(\mu\in\mathbb R^K\), \(\mathbb P_\mu\{\mathcal R_n\cap\{j\in\mathcal J:\mu_j\leq0\}\neq\varnothing\}\leq\alpha=0.05\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix an experiment horizon \(n\geq 1\), a policy \(\pi_n\in\Pi_n\), and a mean vector \(\mu\in\mathbb R^K\).  For \(j\in\mathcal J\) and \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), define the one-step factor
\[
D_{j,i}:=\exp\!\left\{\mathbf 1\{A_i=j\}\left(\frac{Y_i}{\sqrt n}-\frac{1}{2n}\right)\right\},
\]
and define \(E^{(n)}_{j,s}:=\prod_{i=1}^sD_{j,i}\), with \(E^{(n)}_{j,0}:=1\).  These are proof intermediates.  By the definitions of \(S_{j,n}\), \(N_{j,n}\), \(X_{j,n}\), and \(Q_{j,n}\),
\[
E^{(n)}_{j,n}
=\exp\!\left\{\frac{S_{j,n}}{\sqrt n}-\frac{N_{j,n}}{2n}\right\}
=\exp\{X_{j,n}-Q_{j,n}/2\}
=E_{j,n},
\]
where the last equality is the elementary e-value specified by Definition \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}closed\text{-}e\text{-}test}\).

Let \(p_{i,j}:=\mathbb P_\mu^{\pi_n}(A_i=j\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:finite\text{-}predictability}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}policy\text{-}class}\) make \(p_{i,j}\) an \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\)-measurable random variable taking values in \([0,1]\).  Under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}known\text{-}variance}\), conditional on \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\) and \(A_i=j\), the density of \(Y_i\) is \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp\{-(y-\mu_j)^2/2\}\).  Completing the square gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}\!\left[
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{Y_i}{\sqrt n}-\frac{1}{2n}\right\}
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal F_{i-1},A_i=j
\right]\\
&\quad=\int_{\mathbb R}
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{y}{\sqrt n}-\frac{1}{2n}\right\}
 \frac{\exp\{-(y-\mu_j)^2/2\}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dy\\
&\quad=\exp\!\left\{\frac{\mu_j}{\sqrt n}\right\}
 \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\exp\{-(y-\mu_j-n^{-1/2})^2/2\}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dy
=\exp\!\left\{\frac{\mu_j}{\sqrt n}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(A_i\neq j\), then \(D_{j,i}=1\).  Consequently, conditioning first on \((\mathcal F_{i-1},A_i)\) and then on \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\),
\[
\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[D_{j,i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
=1+p_{i,j}\left(\exp\!\left\{\frac{\mu_j}{\sqrt n}\right\}-1\right).
\]
For every true elementary null, meaning every \(j\) with \(\mu_j\leq0\), the right-hand side is at most one.  Since \(E^{(n)}_{j,i-1}\) is nonnegative and \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\)-measurable, the preceding display yields
\[
\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[E^{(n)}_{j,i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
=E^{(n)}_{j,i-1}\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[D_{j,i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
\leq E^{(n)}_{j,i-1}.
\]
Thus \((E^{(n)}_{j,s})_{s=0}^n\) is a nonnegative supermartingale under \(\mu_j\leq0\).  Iterating the tower identity from its initial value one proves, rather than assumes,
\[
\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[E_{j,n}]
=\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[E^{(n)}_{j,n}]
\leq E^{(n)}_{j,0}=1.
\]
This argument permits \(p_{i,j}=0\), so no positivity or forced-exploration condition is being used.

Let \(T(\mu):=\{j\in\mathcal J:\mu_j\leq0\}\) be the set of true elementary nulls.  If \(T(\mu)=\varnothing\), then the familywise-error event is empty and its probability is zero.  Suppose henceforth that \(T(\mu)\neq\varnothing\).  Since \(T(\mu)\in\mathcal I\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}closed\text{-}e\text{-}test}\) gives the intersection e-value
\[
E_{T(\mu),n}=\frac{1}{|T(\mu)|}\sum_{j\in T(\mu)}E_{j,n}.
\]
Linearity of expectation and the preceding null-coordinate bounds imply
\[
\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[E_{T(\mu),n}]
=\frac{1}{|T(\mu)|}\sum_{j\in T(\mu)}
 \mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[E_{j,n}]
\leq1.
\]
This is the arithmetic e-merging step specialized to the present construction and proved directly.

If some \(j\in T(\mu)\) belongs to \(\mathcal R_n\), then \(a_{j,n}\geq0\).  By the definition
\[
a_{j,n}=\min_{I\in\mathcal I:\,j\in I}(E_{I,n}-c_\alpha),
\]
every term in this finite minimum is nonnegative.  In particular, taking \(I=T(\mu)\), which contains \(j\), gives \(E_{T(\mu),n}\geq c_\alpha\).  Hence the deterministic closed-testing inclusion is
\[
\{\mathcal R_n\cap T(\mu)\neq\varnothing\}
\subseteq\{E_{T(\mu),n}\geq c_\alpha\}.
\]
Because \(E_{T(\mu),n}\geq0\),
\[
\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}[E_{T(\mu),n}]
\geq
\mathbb E_\mu^{\pi_n}\!\left[E_{T(\mu),n}
 \mathbf 1\{E_{T(\mu),n}\geq c_\alpha\}\right]
\geq c_\alpha\,
\mathbb P_\mu^{\pi_n}\{E_{T(\mu),n}\geq c_\alpha\}.
\]
Combining the last three displays with \(c_\alpha=1/\alpha=20\) proves
\[
\mathbb P_\mu^{\pi_n}\!\left\{
 \mathcal R_n\cap\{j\in\mathcal J:\mu_j\leq0\}\neq\varnothing
\right\}
\leq \frac{1}{c_\alpha}=\alpha=0.05.
\]

For the smallest stress test, take \(K=2\) and \(n=1\).  Conditional on either possible assignment \(A_1\in\{1,2\}\), a null coordinate's factor is respectively \(\exp\{Y_1-1/2\}\) or \(1\); averaging over an arbitrary recruitment probability gives exactly \(1+p_{1,j}(e^{\mu_j}-1)\leq1\).  If there is one true null, its rejection requires its singleton e-value to reach \(20\); if both nulls are true, rejection of either requires \((E_{1,1}+E_{2,1})/2\geq20\); and if neither is true, the error event is empty.  Thus every assignment and the boundary choices \(p_{1,j}\in\{0,1\}\), including permanent non-recruitment of one subgroup, satisfy the same bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This cited corollary records the confirmatory guarantee held fixed while recruitment is optimized; it is not a novelty-bearing result. \textit{Gap.} Vovk and Wang establish arithmetic e-merging, and closed testing converts valid intersection tests into strong familywise error control; the displayed Gaussian specialization is a direct supporting corollary. \textit{Consumer.} It lets rpact-style enrichment workflows optimize enrollment without recalibrating the declared threshold.

\begin{lemma}[lem:quantitative-terminal-comparison]\label{lem:quantitative-terminal-comparison}
For every \(0<\delta<1\), \(0<\eta\leq1\), prior \(\lambda\), and horizon \(n\), \(0\leq B_{n,\delta}^+(\lambda)-B_{n,\delta}^-(\lambda)\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta)\) and \(0\leq B_\delta^+(\lambda)-B_\delta^-(\lambda)\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta)\). The same inequalities hold for every oracle value, and \(\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta)\to0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\), without a positive lower bound on any allocation clock.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed scenario \(m\), write
\[
\ell_{m,\delta}^{\pm}(x,q)
:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\mathbf 1\{h_j^m>0\}
 f_\delta^\pm(a_j(x,q)).
\]
The scalar definitions give, for every \(a\in\mathbb R\),
\[
0\leq f_\delta^-(a)\leq\mathbf 1\{a<0\}
\leq f_\delta^+(a)\leq1.
\]
Because the weights are positive and sum to one, all three resulting losses take values in \([0,1]\).  In particular, Definition \(\mathrm{def:terminal\text{-}envelopes}\) immediately gives
\[
0\leq B_{n,\delta}^+(\lambda)-B_{n,\delta}^-(\lambda),
\qquad
0\leq B_\delta^+(\lambda)-B_\delta^-(\lambda).
\]

We next construct the comparison policy.  Fix an admissible finite policy \(\pi_n\) and a number \(e>0\).  The transformed policy \(\pi_n^{e}\) presents to \(\pi_n\), after each observation from subgroup \(j\), the virtual observation \(Y_i-e/\sqrt n\), while retaining the actual subgroup labels and the actual allocation clocks.  This rule is \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\)-predictable by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:finite\text{-}predictability}\), and it is independent of the scenario label, so it belongs to \(\Pi_n\) by Definition \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}policy\text{-}class}\).  Under actual local drift \(h^m+e\mathbf 1\), Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}known\text{-}variance}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:local\text{-}scenario\text{-}scaling}\) show that each virtual observation from arm \(j\) has law
\[
\mathcal N\!\left(\frac{h_j^m+e}{\sqrt n}-\frac e{\sqrt n},1\right)
=\mathcal N\!\left(\frac{h_j^m}{\sqrt n},1\right).
\]
Induction over the observed filtration therefore proves that the virtual score-clock pair under \((h^m+e\mathbf 1,\pi_n^e)\) has exactly the law of the original pair under \((h^m,\pi_n)\).  At the terminal time the actual and virtual elementary e-values satisfy
\[
E_j^{\mathrm{act}}
=\exp\{X_j^{\mathrm{virt}}+eQ_j-Q_j/2\}
=E_j^{\mathrm{virt}}\exp(eQ_j).
\tag{1}
\]
The continuous construction is identical: the transformed control \(u^e\) feeds \(u\) the progressively measurable virtual score \(X-eQ\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:continuous\text{-}observed\text{-}control}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:continuous\text{-}control\text{-}class}\) imply \(u^e\in\mathcal U\), and
\[
d(X_j-eQ_j)=h_j^m u_j\,dt+\sqrt{u_j}\,dW_j
\]
under drift \(h^m+e\mathbf 1\).  Thus the same law identity and (1) hold.

For either experiment, compare the two observed laws generated by the same transformed policy at drifts \(h^m\) and \(h^m+e\mathbf 1\).  In finite time, the conditional Gaussian relative entropy contributed by observation \(i\) is
\[
\frac12\left(\frac e{\sqrt n}\right)^2=\frac{e^2}{2n}.
\]
The assignment kernels agree conditionally on the observed past and hence contribute zero.  The chain rule and the fixed horizon give total relative entropy \(e^2/2\).  In continuous time, the likelihood exponential has integrand \(e\sqrt{u_j(t)}\) on coordinate \(j\), and its energy is
\[
\frac12\int_0^1 e^2\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}u_j(t)\,dt
=\frac{e^2}{2},
\]
because \(u(t)\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\).  The same entropy identity follows first for simple predictable controls by conditional Gaussian integration and then for all progressively measurable controls by bounded-energy approximation.

For completeness, the needed total-variation inequality follows from this entropy identity without an additional probabilistic assumption.  If \(P\ll Q\), let \(A=\{dP/dQ\geq1\}\).  The log-sum inequality applied to \(A\) and \(A^c\) gives
\[
D(P\Vert Q)\geq
P(A)\log\frac{P(A)}{Q(A)}
+(1-P(A))\log\frac{1-P(A)}{1-Q(A)}.
\]
For fixed \(Q(A)\), the right side has second derivative
\(1/[p(1-p)]\geq4\), vanishes with its first derivative at
\(p=Q(A)\), and is therefore at least
\(2(P(A)-Q(A))^2\).  Since \(P(A)-Q(A)=\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\),
\[
\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{D(P\Vert Q)/2}\leq e/2.
\tag{2}
\]

We now control small clocks.  Under either admissible policy and scenario \(m\), the observations of subgroup \(j\), arranged in their own information time, have the score-stack representation
\[
X_j(q)=h_j^m q+W_j(q),\qquad 0\leq q\leq1,
\]
with a standard Brownian motion \(W_j\); in the finite experiment only the grid points of this stack are inspected.  This representation follows directly by concatenating the independent centered unit-Gaussian observations, and remains valid at predictable information times.  Define
\[
\mathcal E_{\eta,\delta}
:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal J}
\left\{\sup_{0\leq q\leq\eta}
\exp\{h_j^m q+W_j(q)-q/2\}\geq20-\delta\right\}.
\]
Since \(|h_j^m|\leq H\), occurrence of the event for coordinate \(j\) implies
\[
\sup_{0\leq q\leq\eta}W_j(q)
\geq \log(20-\delta)-H\eta.
\]
Reflecting Brownian paths after their first passage of a positive level and then using the elementary Gaussian Chernoff bound yields
\[
\Pr(\mathcal E_{\eta,\delta})
\leq
\min\left\{1,\,
2K\exp\!\left[
-\frac{(\log(20-\delta)-H\eta)_+^2}{2\eta}
\right]\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The finite-grid event is a subset of the continuous supremum event, so (3) applies uniformly in \(n\).

Set
\[
e=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{shift}}(\delta,\eta)
=\eta^{-1}\log\!\left(1+\frac{2K\delta}{20-\delta}\right).
\]
Outside \(\mathcal E_{\eta,\delta}\), if an intersection e-value satisfies
\(E_I>20-\delta\), at least one constituent \(k\in I\) satisfies
\(E_k>20-\delta\), and every such constituent has \(Q_k>\eta\).
Consequently (1), \(|I|\leq K\), and the definition of \(e\) imply
\[
E_I^{\mathrm{act}}-E_I^{\mathrm{virt}}
=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{k\in I}E_k^{\mathrm{virt}}
 \{\exp(eQ_k)-1\}
>\frac{20-\delta}{K}\{\exp(e\eta)-1\}
=2\delta.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(a_j(x,q)>-\delta\), every intersection containing \(j\) has
\(E_I>20-\delta\); hence (4) gives
\(a_j(x+eq,q)\geq a_j(x,q)+2\delta\).
If instead \(a_j(x,q)\leq-\delta\), then
\(f_\delta^-(a_j(x,q))=1\).  Checking these two cases in the displayed
definitions of \(f_\delta^\pm\) proves the pointwise inequality
\[
f_\delta^+\!\left(a_j(x+eq,q)\right)
\leq f_\delta^-\!\left(a_j(x,q)\right)
\quad\text{on }\mathcal E_{\eta,\delta}^c.
\tag{5}
\]
Multiplication by the nonnegative loss weights and summation preserve (5).

Apply (5) under drift \(h^m+e\mathbf 1\), where the virtual terminal state has
the original law at \(h^m\), and then use (2) to return the transformed policy
to drift \(h^m\).  Since the losses are bounded by one, (2), (3), and (5) give,
simultaneously for all \(m\),
\[
\mathbb E_m^{\pi_n^e}[\ell_{m,\delta}^+]
\leq
\mathbb E_m^{\pi_n}[\ell_{m,\delta}^-]
+\frac e2
+2K\exp\!\left[
-\frac{(\log(20-\delta)-H\eta)_+^2}{2\eta}
\right],
\tag{6}
\]
with the right side truncated at one.  The same calculation holds for
\(u^e\) and \(u\).  Weighting (6) by any \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\), then taking
the infimum over the original comparator policy, gives exactly
\[
B_{n,\delta}^+(\lambda)-B_{n,\delta}^-(\lambda)
\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta),
\qquad
B_\delta^+(\lambda)-B_\delta^-(\lambda)
\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta).
\]
Taking a point-mass prior proves the identical oracle inequalities.

No positive clock was assumed.  In the unsampled-subgroup stress test,
\(Q_j=0\) forces \(X_j=0\) and \(E_j=1<20-\delta\), so that coordinate cannot
witness a near-critical intersection.  In the hit-and-freeze stress test,
a frozen value \(E_j=20\) either has \(Q_j\leq\eta\), in which case it is
already charged to (3), or has \(Q_j>\eta\), in which case (4) moves it
strictly above the boundary.  Thus both \(K=2\) kill tests pass without
forced exploration.

Finally put \(\eta=\sqrt\delta\).  Since \(20-\delta\geq19\),
\[
\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{shift}}(\delta,\sqrt\delta)}2
\leq \frac{K\sqrt\delta}{19},
\]
where \(\log(1+z)\leq z\) was used.  Moreover
\(\log(20-\delta)-H\sqrt\delta\to\log20>0\), so the exponential term in
\(\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta)\) tends to zero.  This proves the asserted
limit.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This modulus is the boundary-stability device that replaces false anti-concentration under adaptive freezing. \textit{Gap.} The diffusion comparators of Wager and Xu and Adusumilli use regular functionals; neither gives a uniform comparison across closed-test equality atoms. \textit{Consumer.} The explicit modulus is one component of an auditable error budget for a certified recruitment table.

\begin{theorem}[thm:boundary-stable-frontier]\label{thm:boundary-stable-frontier}
For every finite \(\mathcal H_0\subset[-H,H]^K\), the fixed Gaussian grid experiment is exactly embedded by vertex controls in the score diffusion. For arbitrary predictable policies, including policies on zero-allocation faces, the discontinuous closed-test terminal loss satisfies
\[
\sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0,
\qquad c_{n,m}\longrightarrow c_m\quad(1\leq m\leq M),
\qquad V_n\longrightarrow V.
\]
Moreover, for each \(\epsilon>0\) there are predictable finite-horizon policies whose scenario-risk vectors converge coordinatewise to that of an \(\epsilon\)-optimal diffusion control.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite list \(\mathcal H_0=\{h^1,\ldots,h^M\}\subset[-H,H]^K\).  The argument uses its finiteness and the coordinate bounds \(|h_j^m|\leq H\).

\emph{Exact vertex embedding.}  Fix \(n\) and \(\pi_n\in\Pi_n\).  Reproduce the policy's external randomization at the diffusion grid times and, on \(((i-1)/n,i/n]\), set
\[
 u_j(t)=\mathbf 1\{A_i=j\}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The action at the left endpoint is measurable with respect to the observed past by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\text{-}predictability}\).  Thus (1) is an observed-filtration control under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}continuous\text{-}observed\text{-}control}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}continuous\text{-}control\text{-}class}\).  Conditional on the state at the left endpoint and on \(A_i=j\), the nonzero diffusion increments satisfy
\[
 \Delta X_j\sim\mathcal N\!\left(\frac{h_j^m}{n},\frac1n\right),
 \qquad \Delta Q_j=\frac1n.                                      \tag{2}
\]
On the finite-experiment side, Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\text{-}known\text{-}variance}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}scenario\text{-}scaling}\) give
\[
 \frac{Y_i}{\sqrt n}\,\bigg|\,(\mathcal F_{i-1},A_i=j)
 \sim\mathcal N\!\left(\frac{h_j^m}{n},\frac1n\right).          \tag{3}
\]
Run the original policy recursion using these matched increments and the matched external randomizers.  Induction over the grid points proves equality of the decision-time score--clock marginals and, in particular, equality of the terminal state law and of every scenario-risk coordinate.  Hence every finite policy is embedded exactly by a vertex control.  Formula (1) permits \(u_j=0\).

\emph{Approximation for continuous terminal losses.}  Let \(g_m:\mathbb R^K\times[0,1]^K\to[0,1]\) be continuous.  We first compare the infimal prior-weighted risks for these losses.  Under the zero-drift reference law, bounded progressively measurable simplex-valued controls admit simple predictable approximations \(u^{(r)}\); coordinatewise approximation followed by Euclidean projection onto the closed simplex gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0\int_0^1\sum_j|u_j^{(r)}(t)-u_j(t)|\,dt\longrightarrow0. \tag{4}
\]
Because \((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\leq|a-b|\) for \(a,b\geq0\), the stochastic-integral isometry and (4) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_0\left|\int_0^1
   \{\sqrt{u_j^{(r)}(t)}-\sqrt{u_j(t)}\}\,dW_j(t)\right|^2
 \leq \mathbb E_0\int_0^1|u_j^{(r)}-u_j|\,dt\longrightarrow0.    \tag{5}
\]
The clocks converge in \(L^1\), and, for every scenario, their drift terms converge because
\[
 \mathbb E_0\left|\int_0^1h_j^m(u_j^{(r)}-u_j)\,dt\right|
 \leq H\mathbb E_0\int_0^1|u_j^{(r)}-u_j|\,dt.                   \tag{6}
\]

For a fixed simple predictable control, approximate each of its finitely many decision variables in probability by a function of finitely many earlier rational-time score--clock observations and the external randomizer.  This follows by approximating bounded random variables measurable for the sigma-field generated by those observations with cylinder-simple variables.  Refine a deterministic grid to include the finitely many observation times.  Within each fine cell, conditionally on its left-endpoint information, select vertex \(e_j\) with the simplex probabilities prescribed by the simple control.  If a coarse cell contains \(d\) fine cells, the conditional variance of the discrepancy between any realized allocation fraction and its prescribed fraction is at most \(1/d\).  Thus the clock discrepancy converges to zero in probability.  Conditional on a realized clock increment \(\Delta q\), the score increment has characteristic function
\[
 \exp\!\left\{
   \mathrm i\sum_jz_jh_j^m\Delta q_j
   -\frac12\sum_jz_j^2\Delta q_j
 \right\}.                                                       \tag{7}
\]
Clock convergence and (7), iterated across the finitely many coarse cells, yield policies \(\pi_n[u]\in\Pi_n\) for which
\[
 (X_n,Q_n)\ \Longrightarrow\ (X^u(1),Q^u(1))
 \quad\text{under the zero-drift reference law}.                 \tag{8}
\]
The cylinder approximations may be chosen off exceptional events whose probabilities tend to zero; a finite union bound leaves (8) unchanged.  Equations (4)--(6), followed by a diagonal choice, extend (8) from simple controls to all \(u\in\mathcal U\).  No coordinate of the simplex is bounded away from zero.

We transfer (8) to every declared scenario without treating an adaptive clock as independent.  Under the reference law put
\[
 M_m(t)=\sum_jh_j^m\int_0^t\sqrt{u_j(s)}\,dW_j(s),
 \qquad
 A_m(t)=\langle M_m\rangle_t
 =\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_j(t).                                         \tag{9}
\]
Since \(\sum_jQ_j(1)=1\) and \(|h_j^m|\leq H\),
\[
 0\leq A_m(1)\leq H^2.                                          \tag{10}
\]
Successive conditional Gaussian-density calculations for simple predictable integrands, and then the \(L^2\) approximation in (5), give the likelihood ratio
\[
 Z_m(X(1),Q(1))=\exp\{M_m(1)-A_m(1)/2\}.                         \tag{11}
\]
Its square factors pathwise as
\[
 Z_m^2=
 \underbrace{\exp\{2M_m(1)-2A_m(1)\}}_{\mathcal E(2M_m)_1}
 \exp\{A_m(1)\}.                                                \tag{12}
\]
For a simple predictable integrand, iterated conditional Gaussian integration gives \(\mathbb E_0\mathcal E(2M_m)_1=1\).  For general \(u\), choose the simple predictable approximants from (4).  Their quadratic energies are bounded by \(H^2\).  Applying the same conditional identity to the fourth exponential shows that the squared exponentials \(\mathcal E(2M_m^{(r)})_1^2\) have expectations at most \(e^{4H^2}\); hence they are uniformly integrable.  The convergence in (5)--(6) gives convergence in probability of the exponentials, so uniform integrability passes the mean-one identity to the limit.  Therefore (10)--(12) imply the corrected adaptive-clock estimate
\[
 \mathbb E_0[Z_m^2]
 \leq e^{H^2}\mathbb E_0\mathcal E(2M_m)_1=e^{H^2}.              \tag{13}
\]
The grid likelihood ratios obey the same bound, because their brackets are \(\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_{j,n}\leq H^2\).  Both likelihood-ratio families are consequently uniformly integrable.  Since
\[
 Z_m(x,q)=\exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mx_j-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2q_j\right\} \tag{14}
\]
is continuous, truncation at a level tending to infinity and (8) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_m^{\pi_n[u]}g_m(X_n,Q_n)
 =\mathbb E_0^{\pi_n[u]}[Z_mg_m]
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_0^u[Z_mg_m]
 =\mathbb E_m^ug_m(X(1),Q(1)).                                   \tag{15}
\]
Finiteness of \(M\) makes this convergence simultaneous over all scenarios.

Let \(G_n(\lambda)\) and \(G(\lambda)\) denote the corresponding finite and diffusion Bayes values for the losses \(g_m\).  Exact embedding gives \(G(\lambda)\leq G_n(\lambda)\).  Conversely, apply (15) to a diffusion control within an arbitrary positive tolerance of \(G(\lambda)\).  Hence
\[
 G_n(\lambda)\longrightarrow G(\lambda).                        \tag{16}
\]
For fixed \(\delta>0\), both terminal losses in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}terminal\text{-}envelopes}\) are bounded and continuous: exponentiation, finite arithmetic averaging, finite minimization, and \(f_\delta^\pm\) are continuous.  Equation (16) gives
\[
 B_{n,\delta}^{\pm}(\lambda)\longrightarrow B_\delta^{\pm}(\lambda). \tag{17}
\]
Because all scenario losses lie in \([0,1]\), every value in (17) is \(1\)-Lipschitz in \(\lambda\) for the \(\ell_1\)-norm.  Pointwise convergence on a finite \(\ell_1\)-net of the compact simplex, followed by the two Lipschitz bounds, upgrades (17) to
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}
 |B_{n,\delta}^{\pm}(\lambda)-B_\delta^{\pm}(\lambda)|
 \longrightarrow0.                                               \tag{18}
\]

\emph{Removal of the envelopes.}  Pointwise,
\[
 f_\delta^-(a)\leq\mathbf 1\{a<0\}\leq f_\delta^+(a).          \tag{19}
\]
Taking prior-weighted risks and infima, and then applying the established Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quantitative\text{-}terminal\text{-}comparison}\), gives, uniformly in \(n\) and \(\lambda\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_{n,\delta}^-&\leq B_n\leq B_{n,\delta}^+,
 &B_{n,\delta}^+-B_{n,\delta}^-&\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta),\\
 B_\delta^-&\leq B\leq B_\delta^+,
 &B_\delta^+-B_\delta^-&\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta).
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{20}
\]
Combining (18)--(20) and taking \(\eta=\sqrt\delta\) gives
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|
 \leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta).                            \tag{21}
\]
The final assertion of the quantitative comparison lemma permits \(\delta\downarrow0\), and therefore
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0. \tag{22}
\]
For the point-mass prior \(e_m\), the definitions give \(B_n(e_m)=c_{n,m}\) and \(B(e_m)=c_m\).  Consequently
\[
 \max_m|c_{n,m}-c_m|\longrightarrow0.                            \tag{23}
\]

\emph{Minimax duality and convergence.}  Let \(C_n\) be the closure in \([0,1]^M\) of the achievable finite regret vectors and let \(D_n=C_n+\mathbb R_+^M\).  Time-zero external randomization makes \(C_n\) convex; it is compact by construction, and \(D_n\) is closed and convex.  Write \(v_n=\min_{r\in C_n}\max_mr_m\).  Then \(v_n\mathbf 1\in D_n\), whereas \((v_n-a)\mathbf 1\notin D_n\) for \(a>0\).  Project \(y_k=(v_n-k^{-1})\mathbf 1\) onto \(D_n\), calling the projection \(d_k\).  Minimization of squared distance along each line segment gives
\[
 (d_k-y_k)^\top(d-d_k)\geq0\qquad(d\in D_n).                    \tag{24}
\]
Taking \(d=d_k+ae_j\) shows that every coordinate of \(d_k-y_k\) is nonnegative.  Moreover, \(\|d_k-y_k\|_2\leq\sqrt M/k\), because \(v_n\mathbf1\in D_n\), and \(d_k-y_k\neq0\), because \(y_k\notin D_n\).  Normalize \(d_k-y_k\) to have coordinate sum one and take a convergent subsequence.  Since \(d_k\to v_n\mathbf1\), division of (24) by that coordinate sum and passage to the limit produce \(\lambda_n^\star\in\Delta_M\) satisfying
\[
 {\lambda_n^\star}^{\!\top}r\geq v_n\qquad(r\in C_n).          \tag{25}
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\), \(\lambda^\top r\leq\max_mr_m\).  Combining this inequality with (25) proves
\[
 V_n=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}
 \left\{B_n(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{n,m}\right\}.           \tag{26}
\]
The identical finite-dimensional projection argument applied to the closure of the diffusion regret vectors gives
\[
 V=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}
 \left\{B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m\right\}.                 \tag{27}
\]
Equations (22), (23), (26), and (27) imply
\[
 |V_n-V|\leq
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|
 +\max_m|c_{n,m}-c_m|\longrightarrow0.                           \tag{28}
\]

\emph{Predictable finite-policy attainment.}  Fix \(\epsilon>0\).  By (23) and (28), choose \(N\) so large that
\[
 |V_N-V|+\max_m|c_{N,m}-c_m|<\frac\epsilon4,                    \tag{29}
\]
and choose \(\pi_N\in\Pi_N\) satisfying
\[
 \max_m\{L_N(\pi_N,m)-c_{N,m}\}\leq V_N+\frac\epsilon4.       \tag{30}
\]
We first Markovize this selected policy; no assertion that allocation counts encode the ordered action history is used.  For \(0\leq i\leq N\), define its horizon-\(N\) normalized state by
\[
 X_{j,i}^{(N)}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^i\mathbf 1\{A_r=j\}Y_r,
 \qquad
 Q_{j,i}^{(N)}=N^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^i\mathbf 1\{A_r=j\},
\]
\[
 S_i=(X_{1,i}^{(N)},\ldots,X_{K,i}^{(N)},Q_{1,i}^{(N)},\ldots,Q_{K,i}^{(N)}).
\]
Let \(\mathbb P_0^{\pi_N}\) be its zero-drift law.  Since the state space is Polish and the action set is finite, a Borel version of the regular conditional action law exists.  Define
\[
 \kappa_i(j\mid s)
 =\mathbb P_0^{\pi_N}(A_i=j\mid S_{i-1}=s),
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,N.                                           \tag{31}
\]
Use fresh independent uniforms to sample from these kernels.  Equivalently, one permitted time-zero uniform randomizer can encode the finite vector of uniforms through a Borel measure-preserving isomorphism, and the construction uses its \(i\)-th coordinate only at decision \(i\).  Denote the resulting Markov policy by \(\bar\pi_N\).

Under the zero-drift law, conditional on \((S_{i-1},A_i)=(s,j)\), the next-state transition is
\[
 S_i=\left(x+e_j\frac{G_i}{\sqrt N},\ q+\frac{e_j}{N}\right),
 \qquad G_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1),                                 \tag{32}
\]
and depends only on \((s,j)\), not on hidden history or earlier randomization.  Suppose the two policies have the same \(S_{i-1}\) marginal under \(\mathbb P_0\).  Definition (31) then gives the same joint \((S_{i-1},A_i)\) law; applying the common transition (32) gives the same \(S_i\) marginal.  Induction from the deterministic initial state proves
\[
 \mathcal L_0^{\bar\pi_N}(S_i)=\mathcal L_0^{\pi_N}(S_i),
 \qquad i=0,\ldots,N.                                           \tag{33}
\]
This is an equality of decision-time state marginals, not a claim that the two policies have the same histories.

For either predictable policy, successive Gaussian likelihood ratios and scenario-independent action kernels give, for every bounded Borel function \(f\),
\[
 \mathbb E_m^{\pi}[f(S_N)]
 =\mathbb E_0^{\pi}[Z_{m,N}(S_N)f(S_N)],
 \qquad
 Z_{m,N}(x,q)=
 \exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mx_j-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2q_j\right\}. \tag{34}
\]
Taking \(\pi=\pi_N\) and \(\pi=\bar\pi_N\) in (34), equality (33) at \(i=N\) yields equality of the terminal state law under every scenario: apply (34) with \(f\) equal to the indicator of an arbitrary Borel set.  In particular,
\[
 L_N(\bar\pi_N,m)=L_N(\pi_N,m),\qquad m=1,\ldots,M,              \tag{35}
\]
with no continuity requirement on the closed-test loss.  Hidden histories and correlated randomization in \(\pi_N\) have been integrated out through (31), while the score--clock state suffices both for (32) and for the terminal likelihood (34).

Before using blocks, regularize the finitely many kernels.  Let \(\nu_{i,m}\) be the law of \(S_{i-1}\) under \(\bar\pi_N\) in scenario \(m\), also including \(m=0\) for the reference law.  Fix \(\beta>0\).  By simultaneous Lusin approximation for the finite collection \(\{\nu_{i,m}:0\leq m\leq M\}\), for every \(i\) there is a compact set \(C_i\) such that
\[
 \max_{0\leq m\leq M}\nu_{i,m}(C_i^c)<\frac\beta N             \tag{36}
\]
and \(s\mapsto\kappa_i(\cdot\mid s)\) is continuous on \(C_i\).  Extend its coordinates continuously from \(C_i\) and project the extension onto the simplex.  The projection is continuous and fixes simplex points, so this constructs a continuous simplex-valued kernel \(\kappa_i^\beta\) equal to \(\kappa_i\) on \(C_i\).

Let \(\pi_N^\beta\) use the kernels \(\kappa_i^\beta\), coupled to \(\bar\pi_N\) with the same Gaussian observations and uniforms.  Until their first differing action, the states agree.  By (36), under each scenario the probability of any differing action is at most \(\sum_{i=1}^N\nu_{i,m}(C_i^c)<\beta\).  Since \(0\leq\ell_m\leq1\),
\[
 \max_m|L_N(\pi_N^\beta,m)-L_N(\bar\pi_N,m)|\leq\beta.          \tag{37}
\]
Embed \(\pi_N^\beta\) exactly by holding its sampled vertex action on each interval of length \(1/N\), and call the resulting diffusion control \(u^\beta\).  Exact embedding and (29), (30), (35), and (37) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_m\{L(u^\beta,m)-c_m\}
 &\leq V_N+\frac\epsilon4+\max_m|c_{N,m}-c_m|+\beta\\
 &<V+\frac\epsilon2+\beta.                                     \tag{38}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Choose \(\beta<\epsilon/4\).  Then \(u^\beta\) is \(\epsilon\)-optimal.

We next verify that the discontinuity set has zero probability for this regularized block control.  There are only finitely many action sequences.  Conditional on a positive-probability sequence, the joint law of the Gaussian block increments is absolutely continuous with respect to their product Gaussian law; the sequential kernel factors only multiply that law by a measurable density.  Consequently, the vector of terminal scores in the sampled coordinates has a density.  For nonempty \(I\), a threshold equality is
\[
 \sum_{j\in I}\exp\{X_j(1)-Q_j(1)/2\}=20|I|.                   \tag{39}
\]
If a coordinate in \(I\) was sampled, conditioning on all other sampled terminal scores leaves a strictly increasing function of that coordinate, and (39) has at most one solution; absolute continuity makes the equality event null.  If no coordinate in \(I\) was sampled, its left side is \(|I|\), not \(20|I|\).  Taking the finite union over action sequences and intersections, and using that a finite minimum can equal zero only if one of its entries equals zero, proves
\[
 \mathbb P_m^{u^\beta}\{a_j(X(1),Q(1))=0
 \text{ for some }j\}=0,
 \qquad m=1,\ldots,M.                                           \tag{40}
\]

For each \(n\geq N\), divide enrollment into \(N\) consecutive blocks with sizes \(d_{i,n}\) differing from \(n/N\) by at most one.  At the start of block \(i\), feed the current normalized score--clock state into the continuous kernel \(\kappa_i^\beta\), sample one action using the designated fresh uniform, and hold that action throughout the block.  This is predictable under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\text{-}predictability}\).  Conditional on the boundary state and selected action \(j\), the block increment has
\[
 \Delta Q_j=\frac{d_{i,n}}n,
 \qquad
 \Delta X_j\sim\mathcal N\!\left(h_j^m\frac{d_{i,n}}n,
                                      \frac{d_{i,n}}n\right).    \tag{41}
\]
Because \(d_{i,n}/n\to1/N\) and every \(\kappa_i^\beta\) is continuous, induction over the fixed number of blocks shows
\[
 (X_n,Q_n)\ \Longrightarrow\ (X^{u^\beta}(1),Q^{u^\beta}(1))
 \quad\text{under every scenario }m.                            \tag{42}
\]
Indeed, at each induction step the conditional expectation of a bounded continuous next-state test function is a finite sum over \(j\), weighted by the continuous functions \(\kappa_i^\beta(j\mid s)\), of Gaussian convolution kernels whose means and variances converge by (41); this is a bounded continuous transition operator, so weak convergence propagates to the next block.

The terminal loss is bounded and its discontinuities are contained in the threshold union in (40).  The bounded almost-sure continuity form of the portmanteau theorem applied to (40)--(42) therefore gives predictable policies \(\pi_n^\epsilon\in\Pi_n\) such that
\[
 L_n(\pi_n^\epsilon,m)\longrightarrow L(u^\beta,m),
 \qquad m=1,\ldots,M.                                           \tag{43}
\]
Thus their scenario-risk vectors converge coordinatewise to that of the \(\epsilon\)-optimal diffusion control \(u^\beta\).  The regularization error was charged in (37)--(38) before the limit in (42), and neither the Markovization, the kernel regularization, nor the block construction imposes forced exploration or a positive allocation lower bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It identifies the attainable local-power regret frontier for the fixed confirmatory test on every finite bounded scenario list. Its reverse policy transfer first integrates hidden history and correlated randomization into zero-drift score--clock action kernels, preserves all scenario terminal risks exactly by likelihood tilting, and only then regularizes those kernels for predictable finite-stage block recovery. \textit{Gap.} Wager--Xu and Adusumilli supply diffusion architecture and Araman--Caldentey a regular terminal posterior-control donor, but none proves boundary-stable transfer for this fixed discontinuous closed-test loss. Burnett--Jennison and Ballarini et al. address specified-prior enrichment under error control; this theorem instead transfers the finite-scenario worst-case-regret recruitment frontier. The repository's unrestricted finite-population binary-contrast minimax-MSE game remains complementary, not a strict-extension predecessor. \textit{Consumer.} It converts a continuum recruitment solution into asymptotically equivalent finite-stage policies for interAdapt-style subgroup-enrichment designs without forced exploration or an assumption that allocation counts encode ordered history.

\begin{theorem}[thm:least-favorable-saddle]\label{thm:least-favorable-saddle}
For every finite \(\mathcal H_0\subset[-H,H]^K\), the minimax values obey \(V_n=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}\{\inf_{\pi_n\in\Pi_n}\sum_{m=1}^M\lambda_mL_n(\pi_n,m)-\sum_{m=1}^M\lambda_mc_{n,m}\}\) and \(V=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}\{\inf_{u\in\mathcal U}\sum_{m=1}^M\lambda_mL(u,m)-\sum_{m=1}^M\lambda_mc_m\}\), with attained least-favorable priors. For every \(\epsilon>0\), a finite mixture of Bayes best responses that are Markov in \((t,(X_j(t),Q_j(t))_{j\in\mathcal J},(b_m^\lambda)_{m=1}^M)\) is an \(\epsilon\)-saddle control and has every scenario regret at most \(V+\epsilon\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the finite-dimensional game identities for either experiment.  Let \(\mathcal A\) denote its policy class and put
\[
 r(a)=(L(a,1)-c_1,\ldots,L(a,M)-c_M).                             \tag{1}
\]
All quantities in (1) receive an \(n\)-subscript in the finite experiment.  Since \(c_m=\inf_{a\in\mathcal A}L(a,m)\) and \(0\leq L(a,m)\leq1\),
\[
 0\leq r_m(a)\leq1.                                             \tag{2}
\]
Let \(C\) be the closure of the achievable regret vectors.  Time-zero external randomization, which is part of the filtrations in Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}policy\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}continuous\text{-}control\text{-}class}\), makes \(C\) convex; (2) makes it compact.  Set
\[
 v=\min_{r\in C}\max_mr_m,
 \qquad D=C+\mathbb R_+^M.                                      \tag{3}
\]
Then \(v\mathbf1\in D\), while \((v-a)\mathbf1\notin D\) for \(a>0\).  For \(y_k=(v-k^{-1})\mathbf1\), let \(d_k\) minimize \(\|y_k-d\|_2\) over \(D\).  Such a minimizer exists because the search may be restricted to a compact ball.  Minimizing along the segment joining \(d_k\) to any \(d\in D\) gives
\[
 (d_k-y_k)^\top(d-d_k)\geq0.                                   \tag{4}
\]
Taking \(d=d_k+ae_j\) shows that \(d_k-y_k\) is coordinatewise nonnegative.  Also \(\|d_k-y_k\|_2\leq\sqrt M/k\).  Normalize it to have coordinate sum one and pass to a subsequential limit \(\lambda^\star\in\Delta_M\).  Equation (4) then gives
\[
 {\lambda^\star}^{\!\top}r\geq v\qquad(r\in C).              \tag{5}
\]
Conversely, \(\lambda^\top r\leq\max_mr_m\) for every \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\).  Therefore
\[
 v=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}\min_{r\in C}\lambda^\top r.   \tag{6}
\]
Closure does not change the infimum of a continuous linear functional.  In the finite experiment,
\[
 \inf_{r\in C}\lambda^\top r
 =B_n(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{n,m},                           \tag{7}
\]
and in the diffusion experiment it is
\[
 \inf_{r\in C}\lambda^\top r
 =B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m.                                \tag{8}
\]
Equations (6)--(8) are the two asserted minimax identities.  By (2), either dual objective is \(1\)-Lipschitz in \(\lambda\) for the \(\ell_1\)-norm.  It therefore attains its maximum on the compact simplex, proving existence of least-favorable priors in both experiments.

\emph{Likelihood and posterior state.}  Fix \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\) and work under the zero-drift reference law.  Predictability and the requirement that the action kernel not depend on the unknown label make all assignment factors cancel in scenario likelihood ratios.  Successive conditional Gaussian-density ratios, using the dynamics already verified in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}boundary\text{-}stable\text{-}frontier}\), give at every \(t\)
\[
 Z_m(X(t),Q(t))=
 \exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mX_j(t)-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_j(t)\right\}. \tag{9}
\]
Every \(Z_m\) is strictly positive and at least one \(\lambda_m\) is positive.  Bayes' formula is therefore well defined and yields
\[
 \mathbb P_\lambda\{m\mid\mathcal F_t\}
 =\frac{\lambda_mZ_m(X(t),Q(t))}{\sum_r\lambda_rZ_r(X(t),Q(t))}
 =b_m^\lambda(X(t),Q(t)).                                       \tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sum_m\lambda_mL(u,m)
 =\mathbb E_0^u\!\left[
   \Phi_\lambda(X(1),Q(1))\right],
 \quad
 \Phi_\lambda(x,q)=\sum_m\lambda_mZ_m(x,q)\ell_m(x,q).         \tag{11}
\]
Thus the observed Bayes state is \((t,X,Q,b^\lambda)\).  The likelihood calculation in that theorem used the pathwise identity \(Z_m^2=\mathcal E(2M_m)_1e^{\langle M_m\rangle_1}\), not independence of \(Q\) and \(M_m\), and established
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\max_m\mathbb E_0^u[Z_m^2]\leq e^{H^2}. \tag{12}
\]

We next justify that the Bayes infimum has a Markov best response, including for the discontinuous terminal loss.  This is an initial-state construction and makes no comparison assertion for arbitrary off-origin states.  Augment a control law by its occupation measure \(dt\,\delta_{u(t)}(da)\) on the compact action simplex.  The clocks obey
\[
 0\leq Q_j(t)-Q_j(s)\leq t-s,
 \qquad \sum_jQ_j(t)=t.                                         \tag{13}
\]
Under the reference law, the fourth conditional moment bound for a Gaussian martingale increment gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0|X_j(t)-X_j(s)|^4
 \leq3\,\mathbb E_0\!\left(\int_s^tu_j(v)\,dv\right)^2
 \leq3|t-s|^2.                                                   \tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14), a dyadic chaining argument, and compactness of probability measures on the compact action space show sequential tightness of state--occupation laws.  For every compactly supported twice continuously differentiable \(\varphi\), the defining conditional Gaussian increments give
\[
 \varphi(X(t),Q(t))-\varphi(X(0),Q(0))
 -\int_0^t\sum_j u_j(s)
   \left\{\frac12\partial_{x_jx_j}\varphi+
                  \partial_{q_j}\varphi\right\}(X(s),Q(s))\,ds
                                                                    \tag{15}
\]
as a martingale.  Boundedness and affinity in the simplex variable allow (15) to pass to any weak limit.  Disintegrating the limiting occupation measure conditional on \((t,X(t),Q(t),b^\lambda(t))\), and replacing its action distribution by its barycenter, preserves (15).  Euler construction with the corresponding conditional Gaussian kernels realizes that barycenter as a progressively measurable relaxed Markov control.  On each Euler partition this assertion follows by induction from the one-step kernel; (13)--(14) make the Euler laws tight, and (15) identifies every limit.  In particular, every limiting terminal marginal needed below is realized by a control Markov in \((t,X,Q,b^\lambda)\).

For completeness, approximate the terminal factor \(\mathbf1\{a_j<0\}\) from above by \(f_\delta^+(a_j)\).  On a deterministic partition, backward conditioning of the conditional Gaussian kernel and minimization over the compact simplex produce a relaxed Markov policy for this continuous terminal cost; choosing successively from a countable dense subset gives measurable policies within any prescribed error.  Refining the partitions and applying (13)--(15) shows that Markov controls attain the same infimum \(B_\delta^+(\lambda)\) up to an error tending to zero.  The truncation needed for the unbounded factors \(Z_m\) is uniform by (12).

Choose \(\delta_k\downarrow0\) and Markov controls \(u_k\) whose upper-envelope Bayes risks are at most \(B_{\delta_k}^+(\lambda)+k^{-1}\).  The compactness argument above gives a subsequence with a relaxed Markov limit \(u_\lambda\).  Because every \(a_j\) is continuous, \(\mathbf1\{a_j<0\}\) is lower semicontinuous; hence \(\Phi_\lambda\) in (11) is nonnegative and lower semicontinuous.  Approximation by its bounded truncations and the elementary open-set inequality for weak limits give
\[
 \sum_m\lambda_mL(u_\lambda,m)
 \leq\liminf_k\sum_m\lambda_mL(u_k,m)
 \leq\limsup_k B_{\delta_k}^+(\lambda).                          \tag{16}
\]
By (19)--(21) in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}boundary\text{-}stable\text{-}frontier}\) and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quantitative\text{-}terminal\text{-}comparison}\),
\[
 0\leq B_{\delta}^+(\lambda)-B(\lambda)
 \leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta)\longrightarrow0.           \tag{17}
\]
The left side of (16) is at least \(B(\lambda)\) by definition.  Equations (16)--(17) therefore prove equality: \(u_\lambda\) is a Bayes best response, Markov in the state asserted by the theorem.  This argument uses the quantitative comparison only at the initial state and so does not assert generalized-terminal HJB uniqueness.

\emph{Finite mixture and saddle inequalities.}  Let \(\mathcal S\subset[0,1]^M\) contain the regret vector of one such Markov Bayes best response for every prior \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\).  If
\[
 g(\lambda)=B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m,
\]
then every admissible risk vector \(r\) satisfies \(\lambda^\top r\geq g(\lambda)\), while \(\mathcal S\) contains an \(s\) attaining equality.  Consequently
\[
 \inf_{s\in\mathcal S}\lambda^\top s=g(\lambda).               \tag{18}
\]
Apply the elementary projection/separation calculation (3)--(6) to the closure of \(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal S)\).  Using (18) and the already proved dual identity gives
\[
 \inf_{s\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal S)}\max_ms_m
 =\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}g(\lambda)=V,                        \tag{19}
\]
where an infimum is written on the left because the unclosed convex hull need not be closed.  Hence for every \(\epsilon>0\) there are \(s^1,\ldots,s^R\in\mathcal S\) and \(p_1,\ldots,p_R\geq0\), \(\sum_ap_a=1\), such that
\[
 \max_m\sum_{a=1}^Rp_as_m^a\leq V+\epsilon.                    \tag{20}
\]
This is a genuinely finite mixture by the definition of convex hull (and it may, by eliminating affine redundancies, be taken with at most \(M+1\) components).  Randomize at time zero among the corresponding Markov Bayes best responses \(u^1,\ldots,u^R\), and call the resulting control \(\bar u_\epsilon\).  Its risk vector is the convex combination in (20), so
\[
 r_m(\bar u_\epsilon)\leq V+\epsilon\qquad(m=1,\ldots,M).       \tag{21}
\]
Finally let \(\lambda^\star\) be any attained least-favorable diffusion prior.  Equations (6) and (8) imply
\[
 \sum_m\lambda_m^\star r_m(u)\geq V
 \quad(u\in\mathcal U),                                         \tag{22}
\]
whereas (21) implies, for every \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\),
\[
 \sum_m\lambda_m r_m(\bar u_\epsilon)\leq V+\epsilon.          \tag{23}
\]
Thus \((\bar u_\epsilon,\lambda^\star)\) is an \(\epsilon\)-saddle pair, and (21) is the simultaneous scenario-regret certificate.  Each component of \(\bar u_\epsilon\) is a Bayes best response (to the prior used to select its element of \(\mathcal S\)) and is Markov in \((t,(X_j,Q_j)_{j\in\mathcal J},(b_m^\lambda)_{m=1}^M)\).  Only finiteness of the scenario list and \(|h_j^m|\leq H\) were used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The least-favorable prior converts robust enrichment into a posterior-state control problem while the saddle inequalities certify every scenario regret simultaneously. \textit{Gap.} Prior-weighted enrichment methods take the prior as an input. This result derives an attained adversarial prior and a Markov Bayes response whose complete risk vector is minimax, for an arbitrary finite bounded scenario list. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies robust scenario weights and a posterior-state policy response that can populate a confirmatory trial design specification.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certified-dynamic-program]\label{thm:certified-dynamic-program}
For every valid finite binary-rational encoding \(\mathfrak E\) and every \(0<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}<1\), the deterministic nested interval Bellman algorithm in the corrected certificate handle terminates and returns rational endpoints, an attained finite-net prior \(\lambda\), and a finite mixture of net Bayes controls \(u\).  Its cumulative lower and upper sequences are respectively nondecreasing and nonincreasing, converge to \(V\), and at termination satisfy
\[
 \underline V_{\varepsilon}\leq V\leq\overline V_{\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 \overline V_{\varepsilon}-\underline V_{\varepsilon}\leq
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}.
\]
The certificate ledger records the actual terminal-regularization, guard-exit, time-discretization, interpolation and control-mesh, Gaussian-cell, Gaussian-tail, oracle and linear-program, recovered-policy, prior-net, and outward-rounding bounds.  The returned mixture is re-evaluated by an independent fixed-policy interval recursion, which certifies \(\max_{1\leq m\leq M}r_m(u)\leq\overline V_{\varepsilon}\).  For this returned mixture, boundary-stable transfer gives predictable finite-horizon policies \(\pi_n\) satisfying
\[
 \max_{1\leq m\leq M}r_{n,m}(\pi_n)
 \leq \overline V_{\varepsilon}+o(1)
 \qquad(n\to\infty).
\]
No closed-form mesh size, arithmetic complexity bound, or convergence rate is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\ell_{m,\delta}^-\) and \(\ell_{m,\delta}^+\) for the regularized losses of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}terminal\text{-}envelopes}\).  From the displayed definitions of \(f_\delta^-\) and \(f_\delta^+\), for every \(a\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 f_\delta^-(a)\leq\mathbf 1\{a<0\}\leq f_\delta^+(a).
 \tag{1}
\]
The weights satisfy \(w_j>0\) and \(\sum_jw_j=1\).  Multiplying (1) by \(w_j\mathbf 1\{h_j^m>0\}\) and summing proves
\[
 0\leq\ell_{m,\delta}^-\leq\ell_m\leq
 \ell_{m,\delta}^+\leq1.
 \tag{2}
\]
If \(c_{m,\delta}^\pm\) denotes the corresponding untapered oracle value, monotonicity of expectation and of the infimum gives
\[
 B_\delta^-(\lambda)\leq B(\lambda)\leq B_\delta^+(\lambda),
 \qquad
 c_{m,\delta}^-\leq c_m\leq c_{m,\delta}^+.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus the continuous envelopes bound the target risks; they do not redefine the causal design criterion in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}regret}\).

\emph{Terminal moduli.}
Put \(c_H=H+H^2/2\), \(s_k=x_k-q_k/2\), and
\[
 e_I(x,q)=|I|^{-1}\sum_{k\in I}\exp(s_k).
\]
Along a line segment, \(f_\delta^\pm\circ a_j\) is absolutely continuous.  At almost every point where its derivative is nonzero, an active intersection \(I\ni j\) has
\[
 20-\delta\leq e_I(x,q)\leq20+\delta<21.
 \tag{4}
\]
At such a point,
\[
 \sum_{k\in I}\left|\frac{\partial e_I}{\partial x_k}\right|=e_I<21,
 \qquad
 \sum_{k\in I}\left|\frac{\partial e_I}{\partial q_k}\right|=\frac12e_I<\frac{21}{2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The slope of each envelope is at most \(1/\delta\).  Integrating (5), using the fact that the minimum of finitely many functions with a common Lipschitz constant has that constant, and then using \(\sum_jw_j=1\), yields
\[
 |\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm(x,q)-\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm(x',q')|
 \leq\frac{32}{\delta}\|(x,q)-(x',q')\|_\infty.
 \tag{6}
\]
No division by an allocation clock occurs in (4)--(6), so these bounds include every zero-allocation face.

For the likelihood ratio defined in the frozen core,
\[
 \mathrm d\log Z_m
 =\sum_jh_j^m\,\mathrm dx_j-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2\,\mathrm dq_j.
 \tag{7}
\]
Because \(|h_j^m|\leq H\), a state displacement of sup norm at most \(d\) changes \(\log Z_m\) by at most \(Kc_Hd\).  Differentiating
\(b_m^\lambda=\lambda_mZ_m/\sum_r\lambda_rZ_r\) along the segment gives
\[
 \mathrm db_m^\lambda=b_m^\lambda\left(\mathrm d\log Z_m-
 \sum_rb_r^\lambda\,\mathrm d\log Z_r\right),
 \qquad
 \sum_m|\mathrm db_m^\lambda|\leq2Kc_Hd.
 \tag{8}
\]
If \(\lambda_m=0\), its posterior coordinate is identically zero, so (8) remains valid.  Hence the normalized posterior payoff
\(g=\sum_mb_m^\lambda\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm\) has modulus
\[
 \Omega_\delta(d)=
 \min\!\left\{1,\left(\frac{32}{\delta}+2Kc_H\right)d\right\}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Fix rational \(G>H\) and scalar taper width \(\omega>0\), and let \(C_{G+\omega}\) and \(S\) be as in the corrected Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}handle}\).  The symbol \(\omega\) is distinct from the discovery-weight vector \(w\).  Compactness of the outer guard and continuity of the finite likelihood sum make \(C_{G+\omega}\) finite.  At \((x,q)=(0,0)\), every \(Z_m=1\), so
\[
 C_{G+\omega}\geq\sum_m\lambda_m=1.
 \tag{10}
\]
On the outer guard, \(0\leq S\leq1\).  The logarithmic differential of \(\sum_m\lambda_mZ_m\) is the posterior average of (7), and therefore
\[
 |S(x,q)-S(x',q')|\leq Kc_Hd
 \tag{11}
\]
for two points at distance at most \(d\) joined inside the guard.  Since \(0\leq S,g\leq1\), equations (9)--(11) imply that the scaled terminal datum \(Sg\) has the retained-state modulus
\[
 \widetilde\Omega_\delta(d)=\min\{1,L_\delta d\},
 \qquad L_\delta=\frac{32}{\delta}+3Kc_H.
 \tag{12}
\]

The taper \(\chi=\chi_{G,\omega}\) has range \([0,1]\), is \(1/\omega\)-Lipschitz, equals one on \(\{\|x\|_\infty\leq G\}\), and vanishes on \(\{\|x\|_\infty\geq G+\omega\}\).  If \(U\) is \([0,1]\)-valued with modulus \(d\mapsto\min\{1,Ld\}\), then, where both values of \(U\) are defined,
\[
 |\chi(x)U(x,q)-\chi(x')U(x',q')|
 \leq |U(x,q)-U(x',q')|+|\chi(x)-\chi(x')|
 \leq(L+\omega^{-1})d.
 \tag{13}
\]
If one point is outside the outer guard, the zero extension satisfies the same inequality because the taper at the inside point is at most its distance to the zero set divided by \(\omega\).  Thus (13) is valid across the outer boundary.

For the reference Gaussian transition over duration \(a\) at constant allocation \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\),
\[
 T_{a,v,z}(x,q)=
 \bigl(x+(av)^{1/2}\mathbin\odot z,\ q+av\bigr),
 \qquad P_a^vF(x,q)=\mathbb E[F(T_{a,v,Z}(x,q))].
 \tag{14}
\]
Common Gaussian translation preserves a modulus, expectation preserves it, and the pointwise infimum over \(v\) preserves a common modulus.  Starting from (12), applying (13) at \(t_N\), and inducting backward through the recursion in the corrected certificate handle proves that every post-taper continuation \(H_l^\pm\) is globally \([0,1]\)-valued and has modulus
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d)
 =\min\!\left\{1,\left(L_\delta+\frac{N-l+1}{\omega}\right)d\right\},
 \qquad 1\leq l\leq N.
 \tag{15}
\]
This is the required layerwise continuation modulus on the computational domain: it covers strip cells, outer-boundary cells, and zero-allocation faces without a global comparison or uniqueness assertion for the degenerate posterior HJB.

\emph{Likelihood identity and the observed-law taper charge.}
Under the zero-drift reference law, conditional Gaussian moment calculation using Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\text{-}known\text{-}variance}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}scenario\text{-}scaling}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}continuous\text{-}observed\text{-}control}\) shows first for simple predictable controls that
\[
 Z_m(X(t),Q(t))=
 \exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mX_j(t)-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_j(t)\right\}
 \tag{16}
\]
is a mean-one likelihood process.  Boundedness
\(\sum_j\int_0^1(h_j^m)^2u_j(t)\,\mathrm dt\leq H^2\) permits approximation in the defining \(L^2\) norm of the stochastic integral, extending the identity to every \(u\in\mathcal U\).  Therefore, for every bounded terminal-history functional \(R\),
\[
 \mathbb E_m^u[R]=\mathbb E_0^u[Z_m(X(1),Q(1))R].
 \tag{17}
\]

Under scenario \(m\),
\[
 X_j(t)=h_j^mQ_j(t)+M_j(t),
 \qquad
 M_j(t)=\int_0^t\sqrt{u_j(s)}\,\mathrm dW_j(s),
 \qquad 0\leq Q_j(t)\leq1.
 \tag{18}
\]
Successive conditional Gaussian calculation gives
\(\exp\{\theta M_j(t)-\theta^2Q_j(t)/2\}\) as a mean-one nonnegative martingale for simple controls, and the same stochastic-integral approximation extends this to every admissible control.  Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}howard\text{-}linear\text{-}boundary}\) to \(M_j\) and \(-M_j\), with \(b=G-H>0\), \(\theta=b\), and \(\beta=\exp(-b^2/2)\).  Because \(Q_j(t)\leq1\), the lemma's boundary is no larger than \(b\).  A union bound over coordinates yields
\[
 \sup_{m,u}\Pr_m^u\!\left\{\max_j\sup_{t\leq1}|X_j(t)|>G\right\}
 \leq p_G:=2K\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(G-H)^2}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{19}
\]

Let
\[
 W=W_{G,\omega,\Pi}=\prod_{l=1}^N\chi_{G,\omega}(X(t_l)).
 \tag{20}
\]
It satisfies \(0\leq W\leq1\), and \(1-W>0\) implies that \(\|X(t_l)\|_\infty>G\) for at least one \(l\).  Hence (2) and (19) give, uniformly over scenarios, controls, and the original or either envelope loss,
\[
 0\leq L_m(u)-L_{m,W}(u)
 =\mathbb E_m^u[\ell_m(1-W)]
 \leq\Pr_m^u\!\left\{\max_l\|X(t_l)\|_\infty>G\right\}
 \leq p_G.
 \tag{21}
\]
Taking prior-weighted sums and infima over \(u\), or taking the scenario infimum, proves
\[
 B_W(\lambda)\leq B(\lambda)\leq B_W(\lambda)+p_G,
 \qquad
 c_{m,W}\leq c_m\leq c_{m,W}+p_G.
 \tag{22}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 B_W(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,W}-p_G
 \leq B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m
 \leq B_W(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,W}+p_G.
 \tag{23}
\]
Thus the taper contributes \(p_G\) to each side and at most \(2p_G\) to the width of a Bayes-regret enclosure.

Equation (17) identifies the omitted prior-weighted reference contribution exactly:
\[
 \mathbb E_0^u\!\left[
 \sum_m\lambda_mZ_m\ell_m(1-W)\right]
 =\sum_m\lambda_m\mathbb E_m^u[\ell_m(1-W)]\leq p_G.
 \tag{24}
\]
It costs at most \(p_G/C_{G+\omega}\) at the scaled reference level and at most \(p_G\) after undoing the scale.  No reference-law tail bound is used for (24).  Similarly, for an independently evaluated policy,
\[
 r_m(u)=L_m(u)-c_m
 \leq L_{m,W}^+(u)+p_G-c_{m,W}^-.
 \tag{25}
\]

\emph{Validated tapered recursion.}
Suppose a state cell at layer \(l\) has diameter \(d\), and its vertices \(v\) carry intervals enclosing \(H_l\).  If \(y\) has multilinear barycentric weights \(\beta_v\), (15) gives
\[
 \left|H_l(y)-\sum_v\beta_vH_l(v)\right|
 \leq\sum_v\beta_v\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(\|y-v\|_\infty)
 \leq\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d).
 \tag{26}
\]
For every retained standardized-Gaussian cell, rational interval arithmetic encloses the entire image under (14).  On a strip cell it encloses \(\chi_{G,\omega}\) and multiplies that interval outward by the continuation interval.  A wholly exterior image has \(H_l=0\).  Because every \(H_l\) is globally \([0,1]\)-valued by (15), only the omitted mass of the separately truncated standardized-Gaussian quadrature may be multiplied by \([0,1]\).  The resulting Gaussian-tail ledger item is distinct from the observed-law contribution (24).

Couple transitions under allocations \(v,v'\) with the same standard Gaussian vector \(z\).  The inequality \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\leq|p-q|\) implies that their state displacement is at most
\[
 a\|v-v'\|_1+\sqrt{a\|v-v'\|_1}\,\|z\|_2.
\]
Therefore the layer-\(l+1\) control-net error is bounded by
\[
 \mathbb E\,\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l+1}\!\left(
 a\|v-v'\|_1+\sqrt{a\|v-v'\|_1}\,\|Z\|_2\right).
 \tag{27}
\]
It tends to zero uniformly as the control-net radius tends to zero, because the integrand is bounded by one and converges pointwise to zero.  Minimizing lower endpoints after subtracting (27) and upper endpoints after adding it encloses the infimum over the full allocation simplex.  Backward induction with (26)--(27), outward-rounded Gaussian-cell probabilities, and the exact interval multiplication by \(\chi_{G,\omega}\) validates the finite tapered Bayes recursion.  The identical argument validates every tapered fixed-policy and point-mass-oracle recursion.  For a fixed candidate, Gaussian-cell probabilities and elementary functions on compact boxes admit rational upper and lower enclosures from their convergent power series with rational remainder bounds, so all interval operations are finite and rational.

It remains to verify that these discrete enclosures bound the continuous-control problem and can be made sharp.  On an open slab let
\[
 \mathcal L^v\phi=\frac12\sum_jv_j\partial_{x_jx_j}\phi+
 \sum_jv_j\partial_{q_j}\phi.
 \tag{28}
\]
Mollify the scaled multilinear lower interpolant by a rational compactly supported \(C^3\) tensor kernel.  Equation (15) bounds its mollification error.  Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives, on the open slab,
\[
 P_a^v\phi-\phi-a\mathcal L^v\phi=\mathcal R_a^v(\phi),
 \tag{29}
\]
where interval bounds on the mollified derivatives and truncated Gaussian moments give a computable outward rational enclosure of \(\mathcal R_a^v\).  Subtracting the accumulated certified layer errors produces functions \(\phi_l^-\) and nonnegative rational residual bounds \(e_l\) satisfying
\[
 \partial_t\phi_l^-+\mathcal L^v\phi_l^-\geq-e_l(t)
 \quad\hbox{for every }v\in\Delta(\mathcal J)
 \tag{30}
\]
throughout the open slab.  At \(t_l\), interval arithmetic verifies the separate jump inequality
\[
 \phi_{l-1}^-(t_l-,x,q)
 \leq\chi_{G,\omega}(x)\phi_l^-(t_l+,x,q).
 \tag{31}
\]
There is no term \((\chi_{G,\omega}-1)/a\) in (28)--(30): (31) is an exact discrete multiplicative jump.

For a simple predictable control, conditional Gaussian integration and (30) on each open slab, followed by (31) at each mesh time, telescope to
\[
 C_{G+\omega}^{-1}\sum_m\lambda_m
 \mathbb E_m^u[\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm W]
 \geq \phi_0^-(0,0,0)-e_T-\int_0^1e_B(t)\,\mathrm dt,
 \tag{32}
\]
where \(e_T\) is the terminal enclosure error and \(e_B\) is the sum of the slab residual bounds.  Every admissible control is bounded and progressively measurable.  Approximating its coordinate processes in \(L^1(\mathrm dt\otimes\mathbb P)\) by simplex-valued predictable step processes and using the defining \(L^2\) completion of the stochastic integral extends (32) to all \(u\in\mathcal U\).  Hence the computed lower endpoint is a genuine lower bound for the continuous-control Bayes value; it does not assume HJB comparison or uniqueness.

For the upper endpoint, the finite recursion selects a predictable piecewise-constant net control, and the independent tapered fixed-policy recursion encloses its risk from above.  Conversely, for every \(u\in\mathcal U\), predictable step controls \(v^{(N)}\) can be chosen so that
\[
 \mathbb E\int_0^1\|u(t)-v^{(N)}(t)\|_1\,\mathrm dt\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{33}
\]
Indeed, bounded progressive processes are approximated by finite linear combinations of progressive rectangles, followed by coordinatewise truncation and normalization into the simplex.  Under a common Brownian motion, the clock difference tends to zero in \(L^1\), while the defining second-moment identity for stochastic integrals and \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\leq|p-q|\) give
\[
 \sum_j\mathbb E\left|
 \int_0^1(\sqrt{u_j(t)}-\sqrt{v_j^{(N)}(t)})\,\mathrm dW_j(t)
 \right|^2
 \leq\mathbb E\int_0^1\|u(t)-v^{(N)}(t)\|_1\,\mathrm dt
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{34}
\]
The drift difference is at most \(H\) times the clock difference.  Equations (6), (20), and (34), together with the Lipschitz property of each taper factor for fixed \(N,\omega\), imply convergence of every tapered regularized scenario risk, uniformly over the finite scenario set.  Approximating the finitely many step-action values by rational control-net points and applying (27) proves that the lower and upper tapered Bellman endpoints meet as the candidate refinements proceed.  Allocations with \(v_j=0\) are included in the simplex and remain fixed under (14); no forced exploration is introduced.

For a fixed \(\delta>0\), \(G>H\), \(\omega>0\), and finite \(N\), the coefficient in (15) is finite.  One may therefore refine state cells, the control net, Gaussian cells and quadrature, the mollifier, and outward rounding so that the sums over all layers of (26), (27), and the residuals in (29)--(32) are arbitrarily small.  To pass simultaneously to fine time meshes, fix \(\omega=1\), let \(N\to\infty\), and choose every spatial, control, quadrature, mollification, and rounding resolution faster than the finite coefficient \(L_\delta+N\) and the number of layers.  A diagonal choice makes
\[
 e_T+\int_0^1e_B(t)\,\mathrm dt+
 \sum_{l=1}^N\{\text{state, control, Gaussian-cell, quadrature, and rounding errors at layer }l\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{35}
\]
Every member of this diagonal sequence is a finite rational candidate, and the deterministic enumeration in the corrected handle contains it.

\emph{Minimax assembly and termination.}
Define
\[
 \mathcal G(\lambda)=B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m
 =\inf_{u\in\mathcal U}\sum_m\lambda_mr_m(u).
 \tag{36}
\]
Since \(0\leq r_m(u)\leq1\),
\[
 |\mathcal G(\lambda)-\mathcal G(\lambda')|
 \leq\|\lambda-\lambda'\|_1.
 \tag{37}
\]
The already established Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}least\text{-}favorable\text{-}saddle}\) gives
\[
 V=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}\mathcal G(\lambda),
 \tag{38}
\]
with an attained maximizer.  A rational simplex net of denominator \(D\) contains every point mass, is finite, and has \(\ell_1\)-covering radius at most \(2(M-1)/D\).  Thus (37) converts the attained maximum in (38) into certified prior-net bounds.

At every prior-net point, (3) yields
\[
 B_\delta^-(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,\delta}^+
 \leq\mathcal G(\lambda)\leq
 B_\delta^+(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,\delta}^-.
 \tag{39}
\]
The already established Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quantitative\text{-}terminal\text{-}comparison}\), including its oracle assertion, bounds the width in (39) by
\[
 (B_\delta^+-B_\delta^-)+
 \sum_m\lambda_m(c_{m,\delta}^+-c_{m,\delta}^-)
 \leq2\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta),
 \tag{40}
\]
and \(\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta)\to0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Applying (23) separately to the lower and upper regularized values charges at most \(2p_G\) to their certified gap.  Equations (35), (37), and (40), the point-mass oracle recursions, and the prior-net covering radius account for every remaining item in the dual ledger.

For the primal endpoint, the corrected handle independently re-evaluates its returned finite policy mixture.  Equation (25) gives
\[
 V\leq\max_m r_m(u)\leq
 \max_m\{L_{m,W}^+(u)+p_G-c_{m,W}^-\}.
 \tag{41}
\]
This bound is independent of the optimization recursion that selected the mixture.

Now take the diagonal refinements from (35), then let \(G\to\infty\), so \(p_G\to0\) by (19), take \(\eta=\sqrt\delta\) and \(\delta\downarrow0\), so (40) tends to zero, and refine the prior net using (37).  These operations prove that valid candidate lower endpoints approach \(V\) from below.

For upper endpoints, choose the finite \(\epsilon\)-saddle mixture of posterior-state Markov Bayes responses supplied by Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}least\text{-}favorable\text{-}saddle}\).  If a prior-net point is within \(r_\lambda\) of its least-favorable prior, each component is \(2r_\lambda\)-Bayes, because both a fixed policy's Bayes risk and the Bayes value are \(1\)-Lipschitz in the prior.  Equations (33)--(35) approximate its finitely many components by enumerated rational-grid policies, so sufficiently fine enumeration retains them.  Their mixture is feasible for the finite rational scenario-risk linear program.  Equations (19), (40), and the independent evaluation (41) place its certified endpoint below \(V+\epsilon+o(1)\).  Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves that cumulative upper endpoints converge to \(V\) from above.

Each candidate calculation has finitely many rational interval operations and finite rational linear programs.  The exhaustive deterministic enumeration contains the convergent sequence just constructed.  Therefore, for every \(0<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}<1\), it eventually encounters a candidate with complete certified gap at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}\) and terminates.  Maximizing all valid lower endpoints examined so far makes the lower sequence nondecreasing; minimizing independently validated upper endpoints makes the upper sequence nonincreasing.  Both converge to \(V\), and at termination
\[
 \underline V_{\varepsilon}\leq V\leq\overline V_{\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 \overline V_{\varepsilon}-\underline V_{\varepsilon}
 \leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}.
 \tag{42}
\]
The finite prior net has an attained maximizer because it is finite, and the finite scenario-risk linear program has an attained mixture.  Its independent evaluation in (41) certifies
\[
 \max_{1\leq m\leq M}r_m(u)\leq\overline V_{\varepsilon}.
 \tag{43}
\]

Finally, the already established Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}boundary\text{-}stable\text{-}frontier}\) transfers this fixed finite mixture's scenario-risk vector to predictable finite-horizon policies and gives \(c_{n,m}\to c_m\).  Since \(M<\infty\),
\[
 \max_mr_{n,m}(\pi_n)=\max_mr_m(u)+o(1)
 \leq\overline V_{\varepsilon}+o(1).
 \tag{44}
\]
This transfer permits zero allocations and uses the theorem's reference-law Markovization, so it adds neither forced exploration nor a hidden ordered-history state.  The argument proves qualitative termination and convergence only; it asserts no closed-form mesh size, arithmetic complexity bound, convergence rate, or interval-certified numerical value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:simultaneous-lower-sequences]\label{prop:simultaneous-lower-sequences}
Supporting corollary (Howard et al. and Ville inversion): for every predictable policy and every mean vector \(\mu=(\mu_j)_{j\in\mathcal J}\), \(\mathbb P_\mu\{\mu_j\geq B_{j,s}\text{ for all }j\in\mathcal J\text{ and }0\leq s\leq n\}\geq1-\alpha\). If \(N_{j,s}=0\), then \(B_{j,s}=-\infty\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a policy \(\pi_n\in\Pi_n\), a mean vector \(\mu\), and a subgroup \(j\in\mathcal J\).  Write
\[
D_{j,i}:=\mathbf 1\{A_i=j\},\qquad
Z_{j,s}:=\sum_{i=1}^s D_{j,i}(Y_i-\mu_j)
=S_{j,s}-\mu_jN_{j,s},\qquad
V_{j,s}:=N_{j,s}.
\]
For \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\mathcal E_{j,s}(\lambda)
:=\exp\!\left\{\lambda Z_{j,s}-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}V_{j,s}\right\},
\qquad \mathcal E_{j,0}(\lambda)=1.
\]
We first verify the supermartingale premise rather than assuming it.  By predictability in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\mbox{-}predictability}\), the conditional probabilities
\(p_{i,a}:=\mathbb P(A_i=a\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\) are \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\)-measurable.  Conditioning first on \(A_i\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
\left.\frac{\mathcal E_{j,i}(\lambda)}{\mathcal E_{j,i-1}(\lambda)}
\right|\mathcal F_{i-1}\right]
&=\sum_{a\in\mathcal J}p_{i,a}\,
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
\left.\exp\!\left\{D_{j,i}\left(\lambda(Y_i-\mu_j)-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right)\right\}
\right|\mathcal F_{i-1},A_i=a\right]\\
&=\sum_{a\neq j}p_{i,a}
+p_{i,j}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right\}
\mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda G}\right],
\qquad G\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\\
&=\sum_{a\neq j}p_{i,a}+p_{i,j}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\mbox{-}known\mbox{-}variance}\), and the third uses the Gaussian moment-generating identity
\(\mathbb E[e^{\lambda G}]=e^{\lambda^2/2}\).  Hence \((\mathcal E_{j,s}(\lambda))_{s=0}^n\) is a nonnegative martingale, and therefore a nonnegative supermartingale, for every \(\lambda\).

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}howard\mbox{-}linear\mbox{-}boundary}\) with \(\lambda=n^{-1/2}\) and \(\beta=\gamma_j\).  These substitutions are legal because \(n\geq1\), while positivity of the normalized weights gives \(0<\gamma_j=\alpha w_j<1\).  Restricting its crossing event to the finite horizon gives
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\exists,0\leq s\leq n:
Z_{j,s}\geq \sqrt n\log(1/\gamma_j)+\frac{N_{j,s}}{2\sqrt n}\right\}
\leq\gamma_j.
\tag{1}
\]
At any \(s\) for which \(N_{j,s}>0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}confidence\mbox{-}inversion}\) and elementary multiplication by the positive quantity \(N_{j,s}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_j<B_{j,s}
&\iff
\mu_j<\frac{S_{j,s}}{N_{j,s}}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt n}
-\frac{\sqrt n}{N_{j,s}}\log(1/\gamma_j)\\
&\iff
S_{j,s}-\mu_jN_{j,s}>
\frac{N_{j,s}}{2\sqrt n}+\sqrt n\log(1/\gamma_j)\\
&\iff
Z_{j,s}>
\sqrt n\log(1/\gamma_j)+\frac{N_{j,s}}{2\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
If \(N_{j,s}=0\), the same definition sets \(B_{j,s}=-\infty\), so \(\{\mu_j<B_{j,s}\}\) is empty.  Thus (2), followed by the weak-threshold crossing bound (1), yields
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\exists,0\leq s\leq n:\mu_j<B_{j,s}\right\}\leq\gamma_j.
\tag{3}
\]
Finally, \(\mathcal J\) is finite, so the union bound, the calibration \(\gamma_j=\alpha w_j\), and \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j=1\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\exists j\in\mathcal J,\ 0\leq s\leq n:
\mu_j<B_{j,s}\right\}
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\gamma_j\\
&=\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j
=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking complements proves
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\mu_j\geq B_{j,s}
\text{ for all }j\in\mathcal J\text{ and }0\leq s\leq n\right\}
\geq1-\alpha.
\]
Because the policy was arbitrary, the conclusion holds for every predictable policy, including policies that never sample some subgroup.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This cited Ville-inversion corollary supplies simultaneous reporting alongside the optimized design; it is not a novelty-bearing result. \textit{Gap.} Howard et al. provide time-uniform supermartingale inversion; subgroupwise allocation of error and a union bound give this direct supporting corollary, including the zero-information endpoint. \textit{Consumer.} Trial reports obtain simultaneous one-sided effect sequences even when the optimal policy stops sampling a subgroup.

\begin{proposition}[prop:k2-n1-sanity-reduction]\label{prop:k2-n1-sanity-reduction}
In the special case \(K=2\), \(\mathcal H_0=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}\), \(w_1=w_2=1/2\), and \(n=1\), symmetry yields \(V_1=\overline\Phi(\log 39-1/2)/4\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(m_0,m_1,m_2\) denote the three scenario labels for which \(h^{m_0}=(0,0)\), \(h^{m_1}=(1,0)\), and \(h^{m_2}=(0,1)\), respectively.  Because \(n=1\), there is no observed response before the only recruitment decision.  Definition~\texttt{def:finite-policy-class} and Assumption~\texttt{ass:finite-predictability} therefore imply that every admissible policy is summarized, for purposes of its law, by the scenario-independent number
\[
 p:=\Pr_{\pi_1}(A_1=1)\in[0,1],
 \qquad
 \Pr_{\pi_1}(A_1=2)=1-p.
\]
Conversely, every \(p\in[0,1]\) is feasible by randomizing \(A_1\) with these probabilities before observing \(Y_1\).  Hence optimization over \(\Pi_1\) is exactly optimization over \(p\in[0,1]\).

Suppose first that \(A_1=1\).  The definitions of the normalized score and allocation clock give

 (X_{1,1},Q_{1,1})=(Y_1,1),
 \qquad
 (X_{2,1},Q_{2,1})=(0,0).
\]
Thus Definition~\texttt{def:fixed-closed-e-test} gives
\[
 E_{1,1}=e:=\exp(Y_1-1/2),
 \qquad E_{2,1}=1.
\]
For the sampled subgroup, the only intersections containing subgroup \(1\) are \(\{1\}\) and \(\{1,2\}\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 1\in\mathcal R_1
 &\iff \min\left\{e-20,\frac{e+1}{2}-20\right\}\geq0 \\
 &\iff e\geq20\ \text{ and }\ e\geq39 \\
 &\iff Y_1\geq \tau,
 \qquad \tau:=\log 39+\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
The unsampled subgroup cannot be rejected, since its singleton-intersection margin is \(E_{2,1}-20=1-20<0\).  Exchanging subgroup labels proves the corresponding facts when \(A_1=2\).

Under either one-positive scenario, Assumption~\texttt{ass:local-scenario-scaling} with \(n=1\) makes the mean in the active subgroup equal to \(1\), and Assumption~\texttt{ass:gaussian-known-variance} makes its response \(\mathcal N(1,1)\).  Define
\[
 \beta:=\Pr\{\mathcal N(1,1)\geq\tau\}
 =\overline\Phi(\tau-1)
 =\overline\Phi(\log 39-1/2).
\]
By the displayed positive Gaussian-density integral defining \(\overline\Phi\), \(0<\beta<1\).  The active subgroup is therefore discovered with probability \(\beta\) when it is sampled and with probability \(0\) when it is not sampled.

For brevity, write \(L_1(p,m)\) for \(L_1(\pi_1,m)\) when \(\pi_1\) has allocation probability \(p\), and similarly write \(r_{1,m}(p)\) for its regret.  The loss definition assigns weight \(1/2\) to the single positive subgroup.  Conditioning on \(A_1\) now yields, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_1(p,m_1)
 &=\frac12\bigl[p(1-\beta)+(1-p)\bigr]
 =\frac12(1-p\beta),\\
 L_1(p,m_2)
 &=\frac12\bigl[p+(1-p)(1-\beta)\bigr]
 =\frac12\bigl(1-(1-p)\beta\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(m_0\), every factor \(\mathbf 1\{h_j^{m_0}>0\}\) in \(\ell_{m_0}\) is zero, so
\[
 L_1(p,m_0)=0.
\]
Since \(\beta>0\), the first one-positive risk is minimized at \(p=1\), the second at \(p=0\), and the null risk is already zero.  Hence the oracle quantities in Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-regret} are
\[
 c_{1,m_0}=0,
 \qquad
 c_{1,m_1}=c_{1,m_2}=\frac12(1-\beta).
\]
Subtracting these oracle risks gives
\[
 r_{1,m_0}(p)=0,
 \qquad
 r_{1,m_1}(p)=\frac{\beta}{2}(1-p),
 \qquad
 r_{1,m_2}(p)=\frac{\beta}{2}p.
\]
Using again Definition~\texttt{def:minimax-regret} and the exact reduction of \(\Pi_1\) to \([0,1]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_1
 &=\inf_{0\leq p\leq1}\frac{\beta}{2}\max\{p,1-p\}\\
 &=\frac{\beta}{2}\inf_{0\leq p\leq1}
 \left(\frac12+\left|p-\frac12\right|\right)\\
 &=\frac{\beta}{4}
 =\frac14\overline\Phi(\log 39-1/2),
\end{aligned}
\]
with equality attained by the symmetric policy \(p=1/2\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact reduction checks normalization, closure, oracle subtraction, and least-favorable symmetry in the smallest nontrivial instance. \textit{Gap.} This is a sanity reduction rather than a novelty claim; it anchors the general frontier to an exactly solvable Gaussian case. \textit{Consumer.} It provides a unit test for any implementation of the finite Bellman recursion and certificate.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:global-generalized-terminal-comparison]\label{oeq:global-generalized-terminal-comparison}
Can one prove comparison and uniqueness for bounded semicontinuous viscosity sub- and supersolutions of \(\mathcal G_\lambda F=0\) on \(\mathcal D\), with the generalized terminal traces selected by the fixed closed-test payoff, at every viable off-origin state including \(Q_j(t)=0\) faces and threshold-equality states?
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A statewise uniqueness theorem would turn the initial-value envelope selection into a complete generalized-terminal HJB theory. \textit{Gap.} Adusumilli's comparison theorem covers Hölder data; the initial-state modulus here does not identify every off-origin boundary trace. \textit{Consumer.} A positive answer would justify reusable feedback values after arbitrary interim states, rather than only design-time evaluation from the origin.

\begin{lemma}[lem:howard-linear-boundary]\label{lem:howard-linear-boundary}
Linear uniform boundary.  Let \((Z_s,V_s)_{s\geq 0}\) be adapted processes with \(Z_0=V_0=0\) and \(V_s\geq 0\).  Suppose that, for every \(\lambda>0\), the process
\[
\exp\!\left\{\lambda Z_s-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}V_s\right\},\qquad s\geq 0,
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale with initial value at most one.  Then, for every \(\lambda>0\) and \(\beta\in(0,1)\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{\exists s\geq 0:
Z_s\geq \frac{\log(1/\beta)}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{2}V_s\right\}\leq\beta.
\]
\end{lemma}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The common-drift translation and initial-state envelope squeeze do not prove comparison or uniqueness at every off-origin viable boundary state. The certificate avoids that unresolved global HJB issue by multiplying the terminal loss by a soft mesh-survival product and verifying generator inequalities only on open time slabs, with each taper multiplication certified as an exact discrete jump. Writing \(\omega\) for the scalar taper width, distinct from the discovery-weight vector \(w\), its layerwise global modulus is \(\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d)=\min\{1,(32/\delta+3K(H+H^2/2)+(N-l+1)/\omega)d\}\), which remains valid across the outer guard because the taper vanishes continuously. The omitted untapered likelihood-weighted contribution is charged under the observed scenario laws by \(p_G=2K\exp\{-(G-H)^2/2\}\), separately from standardized-Gaussian quadrature tails. The construction monitors only the declared mesh times, not continuous first exit. Termination is qualitative and computable; no closed-form mesh schedule, arithmetic operation count, convergence rate, or interval-certified nontrivial numerical run is claimed.

\section{Honest scope}

The frontier and saddle results assume known unit Gaussian variance, finitely many prespecified disjoint subgroups, positive normalized missed-discovery weights, fixed elementary e-alternatives, the arithmetic-mean closure rule, and an arbitrary finite scenario set in \([-H,H]^K\). No positive lower bound on subgroup allocation is imposed. Null-boundary closure is optional application structure, not a theorem or certificate premise. The note fixes the test and optimizes recruitment; it does not compete with unrestricted joint design-and-estimation minimax theory. Unknown variance, delayed responses, overlapping or data-discovered subgroups, and optimization of the confirmatory test are outside scope. Global generalized-terminal comparison and uniqueness at viable off-origin boundary states remain open.
