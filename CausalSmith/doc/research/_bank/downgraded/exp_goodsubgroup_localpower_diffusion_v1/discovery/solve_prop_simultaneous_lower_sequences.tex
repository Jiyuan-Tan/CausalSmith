%%% FIELD lem-howard-linear-boundary-statement
Linear uniform boundary.  Let \((Z_s,V_s)_{s\geq 0}\) be adapted processes with \(Z_0=V_0=0\) and \(V_s\geq 0\).  Suppose that, for every \(\lambda>0\), the process
\[
\exp\!\left\{\lambda Z_s-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}V_s\right\},\qquad s\geq 0,
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale with initial value at most one.  Then, for every \(\lambda>0\) and \(\beta\in(0,1)\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{\exists s\geq 0:
Z_s\geq \frac{\log(1/\beta)}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{2}V_s\right\}\leq\beta.
\]
%%% FIELD prop-simultaneous-lower-sequences-statement
Supporting corollary (Howard et al. and Ville inversion): for every predictable policy and every mean vector \(\mu=(\mu_j)_{j\in\mathcal J}\), \(\mathbb P_\mu\{\mu_j\geq B_{j,s}\text{ for all }j\in\mathcal J\text{ and }0\leq s\leq n\}\geq1-\alpha\). If \(N_{j,s}=0\), then \(B_{j,s}=-\infty\).
%%% FIELD prop-simultaneous-lower-sequences-proof
Fix a policy \(\pi_n\in\Pi_n\), a mean vector \(\mu\), and a subgroup \(j\in\mathcal J\).  Write
\[
D_{j,i}:=\mathbf 1\{A_i=j\},\qquad
Z_{j,s}:=\sum_{i=1}^s D_{j,i}(Y_i-\mu_j)
=S_{j,s}-\mu_jN_{j,s},\qquad
V_{j,s}:=N_{j,s}.
\]
For \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\mathcal E_{j,s}(\lambda)
:=\exp\!\left\{\lambda Z_{j,s}-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}V_{j,s}\right\},
\qquad \mathcal E_{j,0}(\lambda)=1.
\]
We first verify the supermartingale premise rather than assuming it.  By predictability in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\mbox{-}predictability}\), the conditional probabilities
\(p_{i,a}:=\mathbb P(A_i=a\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\) are \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\)-measurable.  Conditioning first on \(A_i\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
\left.\frac{\mathcal E_{j,i}(\lambda)}{\mathcal E_{j,i-1}(\lambda)}
\right|\mathcal F_{i-1}\right]
&=\sum_{a\in\mathcal J}p_{i,a}\,
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
\left.\exp\!\left\{D_{j,i}\left(\lambda(Y_i-\mu_j)-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right)\right\}
\right|\mathcal F_{i-1},A_i=a\right]\\
&=\sum_{a\neq j}p_{i,a}
+p_{i,j}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right\}
\mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda G}\right],
\qquad G\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\\
&=\sum_{a\neq j}p_{i,a}+p_{i,j}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\mbox{-}known\mbox{-}variance}\), and the third uses the Gaussian moment-generating identity
\(\mathbb E[e^{\lambda G}]=e^{\lambda^2/2}\).  Hence \((\mathcal E_{j,s}(\lambda))_{s=0}^n\) is a nonnegative martingale, and therefore a nonnegative supermartingale, for every \(\lambda\).

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}howard\mbox{-}linear\mbox{-}boundary}\) with \(\lambda=n^{-1/2}\) and \(\beta=\gamma_j\).  These substitutions are legal because \(n\geq1\), while positivity of the normalized weights gives \(0<\gamma_j=\alpha w_j<1\).  Restricting its crossing event to the finite horizon gives
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\exists,0\leq s\leq n:
Z_{j,s}\geq \sqrt n\log(1/\gamma_j)+\frac{N_{j,s}}{2\sqrt n}\right\}
\leq\gamma_j.
\tag{1}
\]
At any \(s\) for which \(N_{j,s}>0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}confidence\mbox{-}inversion}\) and elementary multiplication by the positive quantity \(N_{j,s}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_j<B_{j,s}
&\iff
\mu_j<\frac{S_{j,s}}{N_{j,s}}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt n}
-\frac{\sqrt n}{N_{j,s}}\log(1/\gamma_j)\\
&\iff
S_{j,s}-\mu_jN_{j,s}>
\frac{N_{j,s}}{2\sqrt n}+\sqrt n\log(1/\gamma_j)\\
&\iff
Z_{j,s}>
\sqrt n\log(1/\gamma_j)+\frac{N_{j,s}}{2\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
If \(N_{j,s}=0\), the same definition sets \(B_{j,s}=-\infty\), so \(\{\mu_j<B_{j,s}\}\) is empty.  Thus (2), followed by the weak-threshold crossing bound (1), yields
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\exists,0\leq s\leq n:\mu_j<B_{j,s}\right\}\leq\gamma_j.
\tag{3}
\]
Finally, \(\mathcal J\) is finite, so the union bound, the calibration \(\gamma_j=\alpha w_j\), and \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j=1\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\exists j\in\mathcal J,\ 0\leq s\leq n:
\mu_j<B_{j,s}\right\}
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\gamma_j\\
&=\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j
=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking complements proves
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left\{\mu_j\geq B_{j,s}
\text{ for all }j\in\mathcal J\text{ and }0\leq s\leq n\right\}
\geq1-\alpha.
\]
Because the policy was arbitrary, the conclusion holds for every predictable policy, including policies that never sample some subgroup.
