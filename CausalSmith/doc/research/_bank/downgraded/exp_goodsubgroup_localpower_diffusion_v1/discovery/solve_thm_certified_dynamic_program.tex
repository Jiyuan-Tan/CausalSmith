Write \(\ell_{m,\delta}^-\) and \(\ell_{m,\delta}^+\) for the regularized losses of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}terminal\text{-}envelopes}\).  From the displayed definitions of \(f_\delta^-\) and \(f_\delta^+\), for every \(a\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 f_\delta^-(a)\leq\mathbf 1\{a<0\}\leq f_\delta^+(a).
 \tag{1}
\]
The weights satisfy \(w_j>0\) and \(\sum_jw_j=1\).  Multiplying (1) by \(w_j\mathbf 1\{h_j^m>0\}\) and summing proves
\[
 0\leq\ell_{m,\delta}^-\leq\ell_m\leq
 \ell_{m,\delta}^+\leq1.
 \tag{2}
\]
If \(c_{m,\delta}^\pm\) denotes the corresponding untapered oracle value, monotonicity of expectation and of the infimum gives
\[
 B_\delta^-(\lambda)\leq B(\lambda)\leq B_\delta^+(\lambda),
 \qquad
 c_{m,\delta}^-\leq c_m\leq c_{m,\delta}^+.
 \tag{3}

Thus the continuous envelopes bound the target risks; they do not redefine the causal design criterion in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}minimax\text{-}regret}\).

\emph{Terminal moduli.}
Put \(c_H=H+H^2/2\), \(s_k=x_k-q_k/2\), and
\[
 e_I(x,q)=|I|^{-1}\sum_{k\in I}\exp(s_k).
\]
Along a line segment, \(f_\delta^\pm\circ a_j\) is absolutely continuous.  At almost every point where its derivative is nonzero, an active intersection \(I\ni j\) has
\[
 20-\delta\leq e_I(x,q)\leq20+\delta<21.
 \tag{4}
\]
At such a point,
\[
 \sum_{k\in I}\left|\frac{\partial e_I}{\partial x_k}\right|=e_I<21,
 \qquad
 \sum_{k\in I}\left|\frac{\partial e_I}{\partial q_k}\right|=\frac12e_I<\frac{21}{2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The slope of each envelope is at most \(1/\delta\).  Integrating (5), using the fact that the minimum of finitely many functions with a common Lipschitz constant has that constant, and then using \(\sum_jw_j=1\), yields
\[
 |\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm(x,q)-\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm(x',q')|
 \leq\frac{32}{\delta}\|(x,q)-(x',q')\|_\infty.
 \tag{6}
\]
No division by an allocation clock occurs in (4)--(6), so these bounds include every zero-allocation face.

For the likelihood ratio defined in the frozen core,
\[
 \mathrm d\log Z_m
 =\sum_jh_j^m\,\mathrm dx_j-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2\,\mathrm dq_j.
 \tag{7}
\]
Because \(|h_j^m|\leq H\), a state displacement of sup norm at most \(d\) changes \(\log Z_m\) by at most \(Kc_Hd\).  Differentiating
\(b_m^\lambda=\lambda_mZ_m/\sum_r\lambda_rZ_r\) along the segment gives
\[
 \mathrm db_m^\lambda=b_m^\lambda\left(\mathrm d\log Z_m-
 \sum_rb_r^\lambda\,\mathrm d\log Z_r\right),
 \qquad
 \sum_m|\mathrm db_m^\lambda|\leq2Kc_Hd.
 \tag{8}
\]
If \(\lambda_m=0\), its posterior coordinate is identically zero, so (8) remains valid.  Hence the normalized posterior payoff
\(g=\sum_mb_m^\lambda\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm\) has modulus
\[
 \Omega_\delta(d)=
 \min\!\left\{1,\left(\frac{32}{\delta}+2Kc_H\right)d\right\}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Fix rational \(G>H\) and scalar taper width \(\omega>0\), and let \(C_{G+\omega}\) and \(S\) be as in the corrected Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}handle}\).  The symbol \(\omega\) is distinct from the discovery-weight vector \(w\).  Compactness of the outer guard and continuity of the finite likelihood sum make \(C_{G+\omega}\) finite.  At \((x,q)=(0,0)\), every \(Z_m=1\), so
\[
 C_{G+\omega}\geq\sum_m\lambda_m=1.
 \tag{10}
\]
On the outer guard, \(0\leq S\leq1\).  The logarithmic differential of \(\sum_m\lambda_mZ_m\) is the posterior average of (7), and therefore
\[
 |S(x,q)-S(x',q')|\leq Kc_Hd
 \tag{11}
\]
for two points at distance at most \(d\) joined inside the guard.  Since \(0\leq S,g\leq1\), equations (9)--(11) imply that the scaled terminal datum \(Sg\) has the retained-state modulus
\[
 \widetilde\Omega_\delta(d)=\min\{1,L_\delta d\},
 \qquad L_\delta=\frac{32}{\delta}+3Kc_H.
 \tag{12}
\]

The taper \(\chi=\chi_{G,\omega}\) has range \([0,1]\), is \(1/\omega\)-Lipschitz, equals one on \(\{\|x\|_\infty\leq G\}\), and vanishes on \(\{\|x\|_\infty\geq G+\omega\}\).  If \(U\) is \([0,1]\)-valued with modulus \(d\mapsto\min\{1,Ld\}\), then, where both values of \(U\) are defined,
\[
 |\chi(x)U(x,q)-\chi(x')U(x',q')|
 \leq |U(x,q)-U(x',q')|+|\chi(x)-\chi(x')|
 \leq(L+\omega^{-1})d.
 \tag{13}
\]
If one point is outside the outer guard, the zero extension satisfies the same inequality because the taper at the inside point is at most its distance to the zero set divided by \(w\).  Thus (13) is valid across the outer boundary.

For the reference Gaussian transition over duration \(a\) at constant allocation \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal J)\),
\[
 T_{a,v,z}(x,q)=
 \bigl(x+(av)^{1/2}\mathbin\odot z,\ q+av\bigr),
 \qquad P_a^vF(x,q)=\mathbb E[F(T_{a,v,Z}(x,q))].
 \tag{14}
\]
Common Gaussian translation preserves a modulus, expectation preserves it, and the pointwise infimum over \(v\) preserves a common modulus.  Starting from (12), applying (13) at \(t_N\), and inducting backward through the recursion in the corrected certificate handle proves that every post-taper continuation \(H_l^\pm\) is globally \([0,1]\)-valued and has modulus
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d)
 =\min\!\left\{1,\left(L_\delta+\frac{N-l+1}{\omega}\right)d\right\},
 \qquad 1\leq l\leq N.
 \tag{15}
\]
This is the required layerwise continuation modulus on the computational domain: it covers strip cells, outer-boundary cells, and zero-allocation faces without a global comparison or uniqueness assertion for the degenerate posterior HJB.

\emph{Likelihood identity and the observed-law taper charge.}
Under the zero-drift reference law, conditional Gaussian moment calculation using Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\text{-}known\text{-}variance}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}scenario\text{-}scaling}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}continuous\text{-}observed\text{-}control}\) shows first for simple predictable controls that
\[
 Z_m(X(t),Q(t))=
 \exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mX_j(t)-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_j(t)\right\}
 \tag{16}
\]
is a mean-one likelihood process.  Boundedness
\(\sum_j\int_0^1(h_j^m)^2u_j(t)\,\mathrm dt\leq H^2\) permits approximation in the defining \(L^2\) norm of the stochastic integral, extending the identity to every \(u\in\mathcal U\).  Therefore, for every bounded terminal-history functional \(R\),
\[
 \mathbb E_m^u[R]=\mathbb E_0^u[Z_m(X(1),Q(1))R].
 \tag{17}
\]

Under scenario \(m\),
\[
 X_j(t)=h_j^mQ_j(t)+M_j(t),
 \qquad
 M_j(t)=\int_0^t\sqrt{u_j(s)}\,\mathrm dW_j(s),
 \qquad 0\leq Q_j(t)\leq1.
 \tag{18}
\]
Successive conditional Gaussian calculation gives
\(\exp\{\theta M_j(t)-\theta^2Q_j(t)/2\}\) as a mean-one nonnegative martingale for simple controls, and the same stochastic-integral approximation extends this to every admissible control.  Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}howard\text{-}linear\text{-}boundary}\) to \(M_j\) and \(-M_j\), with \(b=G-H>0\), \(\theta=b\), and \(\beta=\exp(-b^2/2)\).  Because \(Q_j(t)\leq1\), the lemma's boundary is no larger than \(b\).  A union bound over coordinates yields
\[
 \sup_{m,u}\Pr_m^u\!\left\{\max_j\sup_{t\leq1}|X_j(t)|>G\right\}
 \leq p_G:=2K\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(G-H)^2}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{19}
\]

Let
\[
 W=W_{G,\omega,\Pi}=\prod_{l=1}^N\chi_{G,\omega}(X(t_l)).
 \tag{20}
\]
It satisfies \(0\leq W\leq1\), and \(1-W>0\) implies that \(\|X(t_l)\|_\infty>G\) for at least one \(l\).  Hence (2) and (19) give, uniformly over scenarios, controls, and the original or either envelope loss,
\[
 0\leq L_m(u)-L_{m,W}(u)
 =\mathbb E_m^u[\ell_m(1-W)]
 \leq\Pr_m^u\!\left\{\max_l\|X(t_l)\|_\infty>G\right\}
 \leq p_G.
 \tag{21}
\]
Taking prior-weighted sums and infima over \(u\), or taking the scenario infimum, proves
\[
 B_W(\lambda)\leq B(\lambda)\leq B_W(\lambda)+p_G,
 \qquad
 c_{m,W}\leq c_m\leq c_{m,W}+p_G.
 \tag{22}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 B_W(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,W}-p_G
 \leq B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m
 \leq B_W(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,W}+p_G.
 \tag{23}
\]
Thus the taper contributes \(p_G\) to each side and at most \(2p_G\) to the width of a Bayes-regret enclosure.

Equation (17) identifies the omitted prior-weighted reference contribution exactly:
\[
 \mathbb E_0^u\!\left[
 \sum_m\lambda_mZ_m\ell_m(1-W)\right]
 =\sum_m\lambda_m\mathbb E_m^u[\ell_m(1-W)]\leq p_G.
 \tag{24}
\]
It costs at most \(p_G/C_{G+\omega}\) at the scaled reference level and at most \(p_G\) after undoing the scale.  No reference-law tail bound is used for (24).  Similarly, for an independently evaluated policy,
\[
 r_m(u)=L_m(u)-c_m
 \leq L_{m,W}^+(u)+p_G-c_{m,W}^-.
 \tag{25}
\]

\emph{Validated tapered recursion.}
Suppose a state cell at layer \(l\) has diameter \(d\), and its vertices \(v\) carry intervals enclosing \(H_l\).  If \(y\) has multilinear barycentric weights \(\beta_v\), (15) gives
\[
 \left|H_l(y)-\sum_v\beta_vH_l(v)\right|
 \leq\sum_v\beta_v\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(\|y-v\|_\infty)
 \leq\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l}(d).
 \tag{26}
\]
For every retained standardized-Gaussian cell, rational interval arithmetic encloses the entire image under (14).  On a strip cell it encloses \(\chi_{G,\omega}\) and multiplies that interval outward by the continuation interval.  A wholly exterior image has \(H_l=0\).  Because every \(H_l\) is globally \([0,1]\)-valued by (15), only the omitted mass of the separately truncated standardized-Gaussian quadrature may be multiplied by \([0,1]\).  The resulting Gaussian-tail ledger item is distinct from the observed-law contribution (24).

Couple transitions under allocations \(v,v'\) with the same standard Gaussian vector \(z\).  The inequality \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\leq|p-q|\) implies that their state displacement is at most
\[
 a\|v-v'\|_1+\sqrt{a\|v-v'\|_1}\,\|z\|_2.
\]
Therefore the layer-\(l+1\) control-net error is bounded by
\[
 \mathbb E\,\widehat\Omega_{\delta,\omega,l+1}\!\left(
 a\|v-v'\|_1+\sqrt{a\|v-v'\|_1}\,\|Z\|_2\right).
 \tag{27}
\]
It tends to zero uniformly as the control-net radius tends to zero, because the integrand is bounded by one and converges pointwise to zero.  Minimizing lower endpoints after subtracting (27) and upper endpoints after adding it encloses the infimum over the full allocation simplex.  Backward induction with (26)--(27), outward-rounded Gaussian-cell probabilities, and the exact interval multiplication by \(\chi_{G,\omega}\) validates the finite tapered Bayes recursion.  The identical argument validates every tapered fixed-policy and point-mass-oracle recursion.  For a fixed candidate, Gaussian-cell probabilities and elementary functions on compact boxes admit rational upper and lower enclosures from their convergent power series with rational remainder bounds, so all interval operations are finite and rational.

It remains to verify that these discrete enclosures bound the continuous-control problem and can be made sharp.  On an open slab let
\[
 \mathcal L^v\phi=\frac12\sum_jv_j\partial_{x_jx_j}\phi+
 \sum_jv_j\partial_{q_j}\phi.
 \tag{28}
\]
Mollify the scaled multilinear lower interpolant by a rational compactly supported \(C^3\) tensor kernel.  Equation (15) bounds its mollification error.  Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives, on the open slab,
\[
 P_a^v\phi-\phi-a\mathcal L^v\phi=\mathcal R_a^v(\phi),
 \tag{29}
\]
where interval bounds on the mollified derivatives and truncated Gaussian moments give a computable outward rational enclosure of \(\mathcal R_a^v\).  Subtracting the accumulated certified layer errors produces functions \(\phi_l^-\) and nonnegative rational residual bounds \(e_l\) satisfying
\[
 \partial_t\phi_l^-+\mathcal L^v\phi_l^-\geq-e_l(t)
 \quad\hbox{for every }v\in\Delta(\mathcal J)
 \tag{30}
\]
throughout the open slab.  At \(t_l\), interval arithmetic verifies the separate jump inequality
\[
 \phi_{l-1}^-(t_l-,x,q)
 \leq\chi_{G,\omega}(x)\phi_l^-(t_l+,x,q).
 \tag{31}
\]
There is no term \((\chi_{G,\omega}-1)/a\) in (28)--(30): (31) is an exact discrete multiplicative jump.

For a simple predictable control, conditional Gaussian integration and (30) on each open slab, followed by (31) at each mesh time, telescope to
\[
 C_{G+\omega}^{-1}\sum_m\lambda_m
 \mathbb E_m^u[\ell_{m,\delta}^\pm W]
 \geq \phi_0^-(0,0,0)-e_T-\int_0^1e_B(t)\,\mathrm dt,
 \tag{32}
\]
where \(e_T\) is the terminal enclosure error and \(e_B\) is the sum of the slab residual bounds.  Every admissible control is bounded and progressively measurable.  Approximating its coordinate processes in \(L^1(\mathrm dt\otimes\mathbb P)\) by simplex-valued predictable step processes and using the defining \(L^2\) completion of the stochastic integral extends (32) to all \(u\in\mathcal U\).  Hence the computed lower endpoint is a genuine lower bound for the continuous-control Bayes value; it does not assume HJB comparison or uniqueness.

For the upper endpoint, the finite recursion selects a predictable piecewise-constant net control, and the independent tapered fixed-policy recursion encloses its risk from above.  Conversely, for every \(u\in\mathcal U\), predictable step controls \(v^{(N)}\) can be chosen so that
\[
 \mathbb E\int_0^1\|u(t)-v^{(N)}(t)\|_1\,\mathrm dt\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{33}
\]
Indeed, bounded progressive processes are approximated by finite linear combinations of progressive rectangles, followed by coordinatewise truncation and normalization into the simplex.  Under a common Brownian motion, the clock difference tends to zero in \(L^1\), while the defining second-moment identity for stochastic integrals and \((\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\leq|p-q|\) give
\[
 \sum_j\mathbb E\left|
 \int_0^1(\sqrt{u_j(t)}-\sqrt{v_j^{(N)}(t)})\,\mathrm dW_j(t)
 \right|^2
 \leq\mathbb E\int_0^1\|u(t)-v^{(N)}(t)\|_1\,\mathrm dt
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{34}
\]
The drift difference is at most \(H\) times the clock difference.  Equations (6), (20), and (34), together with the Lipschitz property of each taper factor for fixed \(N,\omega\), imply convergence of every tapered regularized scenario risk, uniformly over the finite scenario set.  Approximating the finitely many step-action values by rational control-net points and applying (27) proves that the lower and upper tapered Bellman endpoints meet as the candidate refinements proceed.  Allocations with \(v_j=0\) are included in the simplex and remain fixed under (14); no forced exploration is introduced.

For a fixed \(\delta>0\), \(G>H\), \(\omega>0\), and finite \(N\), the coefficient in (15) is finite.  One may therefore refine state cells, the control net, Gaussian cells and quadrature, the mollifier, and outward rounding so that the sums over all layers of (26), (27), and the residuals in (29)--(32) are arbitrarily small.  To pass simultaneously to fine time meshes, fix \(\omega=1\), let \(N\to\infty\), and choose every spatial, control, quadrature, mollification, and rounding resolution faster than the finite coefficient \(L_\delta+N\) and the number of layers.  A diagonal choice makes
\[
 e_T+\int_0^1e_B(t)\,\mathrm dt+
 \sum_{l=1}^N\{\text{state, control, Gaussian-cell, quadrature, and rounding errors at layer }l\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{35}
\]
Every member of this diagonal sequence is a finite rational candidate, and the deterministic enumeration in the corrected handle contains it.

\emph{Minimax assembly and termination.}
Define
\[
 \mathcal G(\lambda)=B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m
 =\inf_{u\in\mathcal U}\sum_m\lambda_mr_m(u).
 \tag{36}
\]
Since \(0\leq r_m(u)\leq1\),
\[
 |\mathcal G(\lambda)-\mathcal G(\lambda')|
 \leq\|\lambda-\lambda'\|_1.
 \tag{37}
\]
The already established Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}least\text{-}favorable\text{-}saddle}\) gives
\[
 V=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}\mathcal G(\lambda),
 \tag{38}
\]
with an attained maximizer.  A rational simplex net of denominator \(D\) contains every point mass, is finite, and has \(\ell_1\)-covering radius at most \(2(M-1)/D\).  Thus (37) converts the attained maximum in (38) into certified prior-net bounds.

At every prior-net point, (3) yields
\[
 B_\delta^-(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,\delta}^+
 \leq\mathcal G(\lambda)\leq
 B_\delta^+(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{m,\delta}^-.
 \tag{39}
\]
The already established Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quantitative\text{-}terminal\text{-}comparison}\), including its oracle assertion, bounds the width in (39) by
\[
 (B_\delta^+-B_\delta^-)+
 \sum_m\lambda_m(c_{m,\delta}^+-c_{m,\delta}^-)
 \leq2\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta),
 \tag{40}
\]
and \(\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta)\to0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Applying (23) separately to the lower and upper regularized values charges at most \(2p_G\) to their certified gap.  Equations (35), (37), and (40), the point-mass oracle recursions, and the prior-net covering radius account for every remaining item in the dual ledger.

For the primal endpoint, the corrected handle independently re-evaluates its returned finite policy mixture.  Equation (25) gives
\[
 V\leq\max_m r_m(u)leq
 \max_m\{L_{m,W}^+(u)+p_G-c_{m,W}^-\}.
 \tag{41}
\]
This bound is independent of the optimization recursion that selected the mixture.

Now take the diagonal refinements from (35), then let \(G\to\infty\), so \(p_G\to0\) by (19), take \(\eta=\sqrt\delta\) and \(\delta\downarrow0\), so (40) tends to zero, and refine the prior net using (37).  These operations prove that valid candidate lower endpoints approach \(V\) from below.

For upper endpoints, choose the finite \(\epsilon\)-saddle mixture of posterior-state Markov Bayes responses supplied by Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}least\text{-}favorable\text{-}saddle}\).  If a prior-net point is within \(r_\lambda\) of its least-favorable prior, each component is \(2r_\lambda\)-Bayes, because both a fixed policy's Bayes risk and the Bayes value are \(1\)-Lipschitz in the prior.  Equations (33)--(35) approximate its finitely many components by enumerated rational-grid policies, so sufficiently fine enumeration retains them.  Their mixture is feasible for the finite rational scenario-risk linear program.  Equations (19), (40), and the independent evaluation (41) place its certified endpoint below \(V+\epsilon+o(1)\).  Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves that cumulative upper endpoints converge to \(V\) from above.

Each candidate calculation has finitely many rational interval operations and finite rational linear programs.  The exhaustive deterministic enumeration contains the convergent sequence just constructed.  Therefore, for every \(0<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}<1\), it eventually encounters a candidate with complete certified gap at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}\) and terminates.  Maximizing all valid lower endpoints examined so far makes the lower sequence nondecreasing; minimizing independently validated upper endpoints makes the upper sequence nonincreasing.  Both converge to \(V\), and at termination
\[
 \underline V_{\varepsilon}\leq V\leq\overline V_{\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 \overline V_{\varepsilon}-\underline V_{\varepsilon}
 \leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}}.
 \tag{42}
\]
The finite prior net has an attained maximizer because it is finite, and the finite scenario-risk linear program has an attained mixture.  Its independent evaluation in (41) certifies
\[
 \max_{1\leq m\leq M}r_m(u)\leq\overline V_{\varepsilon}.
 \tag{43}
\]

Finally, the already established Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}boundary\text{-}stable\text{-}frontier}\) transfers this fixed finite mixture's scenario-risk vector to predictable finite-horizon policies and gives \(c_{n,m}\to c_m\).  Since \(M<\infty\),
\[
 \max_mr_{n,m}(\pi_n)=\max_mr_m(u)+o(1)
 \leq\overline V_{\varepsilon}+o(1).
 \tag{44}
\]
This transfer permits zero allocations and uses the theorem's reference-law Markovization, so it adds neither forced exploration nor a hidden ordered-history state.  The argument proves qualitative termination and convergence only; it asserts no closed-form mesh size, arithmetic complexity bound, convergence rate, or interval-certified numerical value.
