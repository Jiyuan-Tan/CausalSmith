Fix a finite list \(\mathcal H_0=\{h^1,\ldots,h^M\}\subset[-H,H]^K\).  The argument uses its finiteness and the coordinate bounds \(|h_j^m|\leq H\).

\emph{Exact vertex embedding.}  Fix \(n\) and \(\pi_n\in\Pi_n\).  Reproduce the policy's external randomization at the diffusion grid times and, on \(((i-1)/n,i/n]\), set
\[
 u_j(t)=\mathbf 1\{A_i=j\}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The action at the left endpoint is measurable with respect to the observed past by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\text{-}predictability}\).  Thus (1) is an observed-filtration control under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}continuous\text{-}observed\text{-}control}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}continuous\text{-}control\text{-}class}\).  Conditional on the state at the left endpoint and on \(A_i=j\), the nonzero diffusion increments satisfy
\[
 \Delta X_j\sim\mathcal N\!\left(\frac{h_j^m}{n},\frac1n\right),
 \qquad \Delta Q_j=\frac1n.                                      \tag{2}
\]
On the finite-experiment side, Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\text{-}known\text{-}variance}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}scenario\text{-}scaling}\) give
\[
 \frac{Y_i}{\sqrt n}\,\bigg|\,(\mathcal F_{i-1},A_i=j)
 \sim\mathcal N\!\left(\frac{h_j^m}{n},\frac1n\right).          \tag{3}
\]
Run the original policy recursion using these matched increments and the matched external randomizers.  Induction over the grid points proves equality of the decision-time score--clock marginals and, in particular, equality of the terminal state law and of every scenario-risk coordinate.  Hence every finite policy is embedded exactly by a vertex control.  Formula (1) permits \(u_j=0\).

\emph{Approximation for continuous terminal losses.}  Let \(g_m:\mathbb R^K\times[0,1]^K\to[0,1]\) be continuous.  We first compare the infimal prior-weighted risks for these losses.  Under the zero-drift reference law, bounded progressively measurable simplex-valued controls admit simple predictable approximations \(u^{(r)}\); coordinatewise approximation followed by Euclidean projection onto the closed simplex gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0\int_0^1\sum_j|u_j^{(r)}(t)-u_j(t)|\,dt\longrightarrow0. \tag{4}
\]
Because \((\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2\leq|a-b|\) for \(a,b\geq0\), the stochastic-integral isometry and (4) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_0\left|\int_0^1
   \{\sqrt{u_j^{(r)}(t)}-\sqrt{u_j(t)}\}\,dW_j(t)\right|^2
 \leq \mathbb E_0\int_0^1|u_j^{(r)}-u_j|\,dt\longrightarrow0.    \tag{5}
\]
The clocks converge in \(L^1\), and, for every scenario, their drift terms converge because
\[
 \mathbb E_0\left|\int_0^1h_j^m(u_j^{(r)}-u_j)\,dt\right|
 \leq H\mathbb E_0\int_0^1|u_j^{(r)}-u_j|\,dt.                   \tag{6}
\]

For a fixed simple predictable control, approximate each of its finitely many decision variables in probability by a function of finitely many earlier rational-time score--clock observations and the external randomizer.  This follows by approximating bounded random variables measurable for the sigma-field generated by those observations with cylinder-simple variables.  Refine a deterministic grid to include the finitely many observation times.  Within each fine cell, conditionally on its left-endpoint information, select vertex \(e_j\) with the simplex probabilities prescribed by the simple control.  If a coarse cell contains \(d\) fine cells, the conditional variance of the discrepancy between any realized allocation fraction and its prescribed fraction is at most \(1/d\).  Thus the clock discrepancy converges to zero in probability.  Conditional on a realized clock increment \(\Delta q\), the score increment has characteristic function
\[
 \exp\!\left\{
   \mathrm i\sum_jz_jh_j^m\Delta q_j
   -\frac12\sum_jz_j^2\Delta q_j
 \right\}.                                                       \tag{7}
\]
Clock convergence and (7), iterated across the finitely many coarse cells, yield policies \(\pi_n[u]\in\Pi_n\) for which
\[
 (X_n,Q_n)\ \Longrightarrow\ (X^u(1),Q^u(1))
 \quad\text{under the zero-drift reference law}.                 \tag{8}
\]
The cylinder approximations may be chosen off exceptional events whose probabilities tend to zero; a finite union bound leaves (8) unchanged.  Equations (4)--(6), followed by a diagonal choice, extend (8) from simple controls to all \(u\in\mathcal U\).  No coordinate of the simplex is bounded away from zero.

We transfer (8) to every declared scenario without treating an adaptive clock as independent.  Under the reference law put
\[
 M_m(t)=\sum_jh_j^m\int_0^t\sqrt{u_j(s)}\,dW_j(s),
 \qquad
 A_m(t)=\langle M_m\rangle_t
 =\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_j(t).                                         \tag{9}
\]
Since \(\sum_jQ_j(1)=1\) and \(|h_j^m|\leq H\),
\[
 0\leq A_m(1)\leq H^2.                                          \tag{10}
\]
Successive conditional Gaussian-density calculations for simple predictable integrands, and then the \(L^2\) approximation in (5), give the likelihood ratio
\[
 Z_m(X(1),Q(1))=\exp\{M_m(1)-A_m(1)/2\}.                         \tag{11}
\]
Its square factors pathwise as
\[
 Z_m^2=
 \underbrace{\exp\{2M_m(1)-2A_m(1)\}}_{\mathcal E(2M_m)_1}
 \exp\{A_m(1)\}.                                                \tag{12}
\]
For a simple predictable integrand, iterated conditional Gaussian integration gives \(\mathbb E_0\mathcal E(2M_m)_1=1\).  For general \(u\), choose the simple predictable approximants from (4).  Their quadratic energies are bounded by \(H^2\).  Applying the same conditional identity to the fourth exponential shows that the squared exponentials \(\mathcal E(2M_m^{(r)})_1^2\) have expectations at most \(e^{4H^2}\); hence they are uniformly integrable.  The convergence in (5)--(6) gives convergence in probability of the exponentials, so uniform integrability passes the mean-one identity to the limit.  Therefore (10)--(12) imply the corrected adaptive-clock estimate
\[
 \mathbb E_0[Z_m^2]
 \leq e^{H^2}\mathbb E_0\mathcal E(2M_m)_1=e^{H^2}.              \tag{13}
\]
The grid likelihood ratios obey the same bound, because their brackets are \(\sum_j(h_j^m)^2Q_{j,n}\leq H^2\).  Both likelihood-ratio families are consequently uniformly integrable.  Since
\[
 Z_m(x,q)=\exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mx_j-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2q_j\right\} \tag{14}
\]
is continuous, truncation at a level tending to infinity and (8) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_m^{\pi_n[u]}g_m(X_n,Q_n)
 =\mathbb E_0^{\pi_n[u]}[Z_mg_m]
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_0^u[Z_mg_m]
 =\mathbb E_m^ug_m(X(1),Q(1)).                                   \tag{15}
\]
Finiteness of \(M\) makes this convergence simultaneous over all scenarios.

Let \(G_n(\lambda)\) and \(G(\lambda)\) denote the corresponding finite and diffusion Bayes values for the losses \(g_m\).  Exact embedding gives \(G(\lambda)\leq G_n(\lambda)\).  Conversely, apply (15) to a diffusion control within an arbitrary positive tolerance of \(G(\lambda)\).  Hence
\[
 G_n(\lambda)\longrightarrow G(\lambda).                        \tag{16}
\]
For fixed \(\delta>0\), both terminal losses in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}terminal\text{-}envelopes}\) are bounded and continuous: exponentiation, finite arithmetic averaging, finite minimization, and \(f_\delta^\pm\) are continuous.  Equation (16) gives
\[
 B_{n,\delta}^{\pm}(\lambda)\longrightarrow B_\delta^{\pm}(\lambda). \tag{17}
\]
Because all scenario losses lie in \([0,1]\), every value in (17) is \(1\)-Lipschitz in \(\lambda\) for the \(\ell_1\)-norm.  Pointwise convergence on a finite \(\ell_1\)-net of the compact simplex, followed by the two Lipschitz bounds, upgrades (17) to
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}
 |B_{n,\delta}^{\pm}(\lambda)-B_\delta^{\pm}(\lambda)|
 \longrightarrow0.                                               \tag{18}
\]

\emph{Removal of the envelopes.}  Pointwise,
\[
 f_\delta^-(a)\leq\mathbf 1\{a<0\}\leq f_\delta^+(a).          \tag{19}
\]
Taking prior-weighted risks and infima, and then applying the established Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quantitative\text{-}terminal\text{-}comparison}\), gives, uniformly in \(n\) and \(\lambda\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_{n,\delta}^-&\leq B_n\leq B_{n,\delta}^+,
 &B_{n,\delta}^+-B_{n,\delta}^-&\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta),\\
 B_\delta^-&\leq B\leq B_\delta^+,
 &B_\delta^+-B_\delta^-&\leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\eta).
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{20}
\]
Combining (18)--(20) and taking \(\eta=\sqrt\delta\) gives
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|
 \leq\rho_{H,K}(\delta,\sqrt\delta).                            \tag{21}
\]
The final assertion of the quantitative comparison lemma permits \(\delta\downarrow0\), and therefore
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0. \tag{22}
\]
For the point-mass prior \(e_m\), the definitions give \(B_n(e_m)=c_{n,m}\) and \(B(e_m)=c_m\).  Consequently
\[
 \max_m|c_{n,m}-c_m|\longrightarrow0.                            \tag{23}
\]

\emph{Minimax duality and convergence.}  Let \(C_n\) be the closure in \([0,1]^M\) of the achievable finite regret vectors and let \(D_n=C_n+\mathbb R_+^M\).  Time-zero external randomization makes \(C_n\) convex; it is compact by construction, and \(D_n\) is closed and convex.  Write \(v_n=\min_{r\in C_n}\max_mr_m\).  Then \(v_n\mathbf 1\in D_n\), whereas \((v_n-a)\mathbf 1\notin D_n\) for \(a>0\).  Project \(y_k=(v_n-k^{-1})\mathbf 1\) onto \(D_n\), calling the projection \(d_k\).  Minimization of squared distance along each line segment gives
\[
 (d_k-y_k)^\top(d-d_k)\geq0\qquad(d\in D_n).                    \tag{24}
\]
Taking \(d=d_k+ae_j\) shows that every coordinate of \(d_k-y_k\) is nonnegative.  Moreover, \(\|d_k-y_k\|_2\leq\sqrt M/k\), because \(v_n\mathbf1\in D_n\), and \(d_k-y_k\neq0\), because \(y_k\notin D_n\).  Normalize \(d_k-y_k\) to have coordinate sum one and take a convergent subsequence.  Since \(d_k\to v_n\mathbf1\), division of (24) by that coordinate sum and passage to the limit produce \(\lambda_n^\star\in\Delta_M\) satisfying
\[
 {\lambda_n^\star}^{\!\top}r\geq v_n\qquad(r\in C_n).          \tag{25}
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\Delta_M\), \(\lambda^\top r\leq\max_mr_m\).  Combining this inequality with (25) proves
\[
 V_n=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}
 \left\{B_n(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_{n,m}\right\}.           \tag{26}
\]
The identical finite-dimensional projection argument applied to the closure of the diffusion regret vectors gives
\[
 V=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}
 \left\{B(\lambda)-\sum_m\lambda_mc_m\right\}.                 \tag{27}
\]
Equations (22), (23), (26), and (27) imply
\[
 |V_n-V|\leq
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Delta_M}|B_n(\lambda)-B(\lambda)|
 +\max_m|c_{n,m}-c_m|\longrightarrow0.                           \tag{28}
\]

\emph{Predictable finite-policy attainment.}  Fix \(\epsilon>0\).  By (23) and (28), choose \(N\) so large that
\[
 |V_N-V|+\max_m|c_{N,m}-c_m|<\frac\epsilon4,                    \tag{29}
\]
and choose \(\pi_N\in\Pi_N\) satisfying
\[
 \max_m\{L_N(\pi_N,m)-c_{N,m}\}\leq V_N+\frac\epsilon4.       \tag{30}
\]
We first Markovize this selected policy; no assertion that allocation counts encode the ordered action history is used.  For \(0\leq i\leq N\), define its horizon-\(N\) normalized state by
\[
 X_{j,i}^{(N)}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^i\mathbf 1\{A_r=j\}Y_r,
 \qquad
 Q_{j,i}^{(N)}=N^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^i\mathbf 1\{A_r=j\},
\]
\[
 S_i=(X_{1,i}^{(N)},\ldots,X_{K,i}^{(N)},Q_{1,i}^{(N)},\ldots,Q_{K,i}^{(N)}).
\]
Let \(\mathbb P_0^{\pi_N}\) be its zero-drift law.  Since the state space is Polish and the action set is finite, a Borel version of the regular conditional action law exists.  Define
\[
 \kappa_i(j\mid s)
 =\mathbb P_0^{\pi_N}(A_i=j\mid S_{i-1}=s),
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,N.                                           \tag{31}
\]
Use fresh independent uniforms to sample from these kernels.  Equivalently, one permitted time-zero uniform randomizer can encode the finite vector of uniforms through a Borel measure-preserving isomorphism, and the construction uses its \(i\)-th coordinate only at decision \(i\).  Denote the resulting Markov policy by \(\bar\pi_N\).

Under the zero-drift law, conditional on \((S_{i-1},A_i)=(s,j)\), the next-state transition is
\[
 S_i=\left(x+e_j\frac{G_i}{\sqrt N},\ q+\frac{e_j}{N}\right),
 \qquad G_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1),                                 \tag{32}
\]
and depends only on \((s,j)\), not on hidden history or earlier randomization.  Suppose the two policies have the same \(S_{i-1}\) marginal under \(\mathbb P_0\).  Definition (31) then gives the same joint \((S_{i-1},A_i)\) law; applying the common transition (32) gives the same \(S_i\) marginal.  Induction from the deterministic initial state proves
\[
 \mathcal L_0^{\bar\pi_N}(S_i)=\mathcal L_0^{\pi_N}(S_i),
 \qquad i=0,\ldots,N.                                           \tag{33}
\]
This is an equality of decision-time state marginals, not a claim that the two policies have the same histories.

For either predictable policy, successive Gaussian likelihood ratios and scenario-independent action kernels give, for every bounded Borel function \(f\),
\[
 \mathbb E_m^{\pi}[f(S_N)]
 =\mathbb E_0^{\pi}[Z_{m,N}(S_N)f(S_N)],
 \qquad
 Z_{m,N}(x,q)=
 \exp\!\left\{\sum_jh_j^mx_j-\frac12\sum_j(h_j^m)^2q_j\right\}. \tag{34}
\]
Taking \(\pi=\pi_N\) and \(\pi=\bar\pi_N\) in (34), equality (33) at \(i=N\) yields equality of the terminal state law under every scenario: apply (34) with \(f\) equal to the indicator of an arbitrary Borel set.  In particular,
\[
 L_N(\bar\pi_N,m)=L_N(\pi_N,m),\qquad m=1,\ldots,M,              \tag{35}
\]
with no continuity requirement on the closed-test loss.  Hidden histories and correlated randomization in \(\pi_N\) have been integrated out through (31), while the score--clock state suffices both for (32) and for the terminal likelihood (34).

Before using blocks, regularize the finitely many kernels.  Let \(\nu_{i,m}\) be the law of \(S_{i-1}\) under \(\bar\pi_N\) in scenario \(m\), also including \(m=0\) for the reference law.  Fix \(\beta>0\).  By simultaneous Lusin approximation for the finite collection \(\{\nu_{i,m}:0\leq m\leq M\}\), for every \(i\) there is a compact set \(C_i\) such that
\[
 \max_{0\leq m\leq M}\nu_{i,m}(C_i^c)<\frac\beta N             \tag{36}
\]
and \(s\mapsto\kappa_i(\cdot\mid s)\) is continuous on \(C_i\).  Extend its coordinates continuously from \(C_i\) and project the extension onto the simplex.  The projection is continuous and fixes simplex points, so this constructs a continuous simplex-valued kernel \(\kappa_i^\beta\) equal to \(\kappa_i\) on \(C_i\).

Let \(\pi_N^\beta\) use the kernels \(\kappa_i^\beta\), coupled to \(\bar\pi_N\) with the same Gaussian observations and uniforms.  Until their first differing action, the states agree.  By (36), under each scenario the probability of any differing action is at most \(\sum_{i=1}^N\nu_{i,m}(C_i^c)<\beta\).  Since \(0\leq\ell_m\leq1\),
\[
 \max_m|L_N(\pi_N^\beta,m)-L_N(\bar\pi_N,m)|\leq\beta.          \tag{37}
\]
Embed \(\pi_N^\beta\) exactly by holding its sampled vertex action on each interval of length \(1/N\), and call the resulting diffusion control \(u^\beta\).  Exact embedding and (29), (30), (35), and (37) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_m\{L(u^\beta,m)-c_m\}
 &\leq V_N+\frac\epsilon4+\max_m|c_{N,m}-c_m|+\beta\\
 &<V+\frac\epsilon2+\beta.                                     \tag{38}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Choose \(\beta<\epsilon/4\).  Then \(u^\beta\) is \(\epsilon\)-optimal.

We next verify that the discontinuity set has zero probability for this regularized block control.  There are only finitely many action sequences.  Conditional on a positive-probability sequence, the joint law of the Gaussian block increments is absolutely continuous with respect to their product Gaussian law; the sequential kernel factors only multiply that law by a measurable density.  Consequently, the vector of terminal scores in the sampled coordinates has a density.  For nonempty \(I\), a threshold equality is
\[
 \sum_{j\in I}\exp\{X_j(1)-Q_j(1)/2\}=20|I|.                   \tag{39}
\]
If a coordinate in \(I\) was sampled, conditioning on all other sampled terminal scores leaves a strictly increasing function of that coordinate, and (39) has at most one solution; absolute continuity makes the equality event null.  If no coordinate in \(I\) was sampled, its left side is \(|I|\), not \(20|I|\).  Taking the finite union over action sequences and intersections, and using that a finite minimum can equal zero only if one of its entries equals zero, proves
\[
 \mathbb P_m^{u^\beta}\{a_j(X(1),Q(1))=0
 \text{ for some }j\}=0,
 \qquad m=1,\ldots,M.                                           \tag{40}
\]

For each \(n\geq N\), divide enrollment into \(N\) consecutive blocks with sizes \(d_{i,n}\) differing from \(n/N\) by at most one.  At the start of block \(i\), feed the current normalized score--clock state into the continuous kernel \(\kappa_i^\beta\), sample one action using the designated fresh uniform, and hold that action throughout the block.  This is predictable under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\text{-}predictability}\).  Conditional on the boundary state and selected action \(j\), the block increment has
\[
 \Delta Q_j=\frac{d_{i,n}}n,
 \qquad
 \Delta X_j\sim\mathcal N\!\left(h_j^m\frac{d_{i,n}}n,
                                      \frac{d_{i,n}}n\right).    \tag{41}
\]
Because \(d_{i,n}/n\to1/N\) and every \(\kappa_i^\beta\) is continuous, induction over the fixed number of blocks shows
\[
 (X_n,Q_n)\ \Longrightarrow\ (X^{u^\beta}(1),Q^{u^\beta}(1))
 \quad\text{under every scenario }m.                            \tag{42}
\]
Indeed, at each induction step the conditional expectation of a bounded continuous next-state test function is a finite sum over \(j\), weighted by the continuous functions \(\kappa_i^\beta(j\mid s)\), of Gaussian convolution kernels whose means and variances converge by (41); this is a bounded continuous transition operator, so weak convergence propagates to the next block.

The terminal loss is bounded and its discontinuities are contained in the threshold union in (40).  The bounded almost-sure continuity form of the portmanteau theorem applied to (40)--(42) therefore gives predictable policies \(\pi_n^\epsilon\in\Pi_n\) such that
\[
 L_n(\pi_n^\epsilon,m)\longrightarrow L(u^\beta,m),
 \qquad m=1,\ldots,M.                                           \tag{43}
\]
Thus their scenario-risk vectors converge coordinatewise to that of the \(\epsilon\)-optimal diffusion control \(u^\beta\).  The regularization error was charged in (37)--(38) before the limit in (42), and neither the Markovization, the kernel regularization, nor the block construction imposes forced exploration or a positive allocation lower bound.
