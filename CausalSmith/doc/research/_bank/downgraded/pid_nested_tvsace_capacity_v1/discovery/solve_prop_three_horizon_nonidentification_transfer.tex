Assume \(0<\eta\leq 1/4\).  Write \(\mathbb P_-\) and \(\mathbb P_+\) for the two full laws obtained from the arrays \(q^-_{ihj}\) and \(q^+_{ihj}\), respectively, in the definition of \(\mathfrak E_3\).  The finite-table identities certified by the strict cross-cohort support proposition are
\[
 \left(\sum_h q^-_{ihj}\right)_{ij}=f^-_{ij},\qquad
 \left(\sum_h q^+_{ihj}\right)_{ij}=f^+_{ij},
 \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \left(\sum_i q^-_{ihj}\right)_{hj}
 =\left(\sum_i q^+_{ihj}\right)_{hj}
 =\mu_{1,hj}^{\star}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus (1) gives the respective \((D(0),R(1))\)-margins, while (2) proves that both laws have the same treated within-arm margin \(\mu_1^\star\).  The row sums of both \(f^-\) and \(f^+\) are \((1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\).  After attaching \(R(0)=\infty\), as prescribed by the exhibit, the resulting \((D(0),R(0))\)-margin under either sign is therefore exactly the displayed control table \(\mu_0^\star\).

Every positive entry listed for either \(q^-_{ihj}\) or \(q^+_{ihj}\) has \(i\leq h\).  Since the death categories \(d_0^\star<d_1^\star<d_2^\star<d_3^\star=\infty\) are ordered, this proves
\[
 \mathbb P_\pm\{D(0)\leq D(1)\}=1.
 \tag{3}
\]
The within-arm tables also obey the semicompeting-risk order: every cell of \(\mu_1^\star\) with finite readmission time has \(r_j^\star<d_h^\star\), and under control \(R(0)=\infty\), to which the finite-readmission clause does not apply.  Hence the two arrays define admissible monotone full-law lifts with the asserted within-arm margins.

The lifts are distinct.  Indeed, marginalization in (1) and the displayed arrays give
\[
 \mathbb P_-\{D(0)=d_0^\star,R(1)=r_0^\star\}
 =f^-_{00}=\frac{1}{10}
 \neq \frac{1}{20}=f^+_{00}
 =\mathbb P_+\{D(0)=d_0^\star,R(1)=r_0^\star\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

It remains to prove observational equivalence, which does not follow merely from distinct projected margins.  In both constructions \(X\) is degenerate, \(Z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) is independent of the complete potential path, and \(C=3=\tau\).  Let \(\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\) denote the deterministic observed history through \(\tau\) generated by treatment \(z\), factual terminal time \(d\), factual readmission time \(r\), and censoring time \(3\).  By the consistency assumption, treatment arm \(z\) reveals \((D(z),R(z))\).  Consequently, for every measurable set \(A\) in the observed-path space and each \(\sigma\in\{-,+\}\),
\[
 \mathbb P_\sigma(O\in A)
 =\frac12\sum_{z=0}^1
   \int \mathbf 1\{\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\in A\}\,
          d\mu_z^\star(d,r).
 \tag{5}
\]
The right-hand side of (5) is independent of \(\sigma\), because (1)--(2) show that both signs have the same two factual within-arm laws \(\mu_0^\star\) and \(\mu_1^\star\), and the treatment and censoring mechanisms are common.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_-}(O)=\mathcal L_{\mathbb P_+}(O).
 \tag{6}
\]
For completeness, independence of \(Z\) from the potential path gives conditional exchangeability; \(e=1/2\) gives treatment overlap because \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\); deterministic \(C=\tau\) gives arm-specific independent censoring and \(G_z(u)=1\geq\gamma\) for every \(u\in[0,\tau]\); and the sole covariate cell has mass one.  Thus (6) is equality of observed laws under the maintained sampling restrictions, not merely equality of uncensored margins.

The control-survivor masses in the exhibit are
\[
 S(1)=\frac34,\qquad S(2)=\frac12,\qquad S(3)=\frac14.
 \tag{7}
\]
Since \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), (7) and the definition of \(\mathcal H_\eta\) place \((1,1)\), \((2,2)\), and \((3,3)\) in \(\mathcal H_\eta\); hence the signed functional in the definition of \(\ell_\star\) is well defined for both lifts.  The strict cross-cohort support proposition computes its values as
\[
 \ell_\star(f^-)=-\frac1{20},\qquad
 \ell_\star(f^+)=\frac14.
 \tag{8}
\]

Now let \(\mathfrak C\) be any nonparametric causal model class containing both atomic laws.  At the common observable distribution (6), the identified set for \(\ell_\star\) over \(\mathfrak C\) contains both values in (8).  Their difference is
\[
 \frac14-\left(-\frac1{20}\right)=\frac3{10}>0,
 \tag{9}
\]
so \(\ell_\star\), and therefore the joint surface from which it is computed, is not point identified in \(\mathfrak C\).  This is an inclusion argument: enlarging the model class retains both compatible witnesses.  It does not establish that every additional law in a larger, possibly nonmonotone class is represented by the marked-capacity fiber, and therefore it implies no sharpness claim for that enlarged class.
