Let \(\mathsf A\) and \(\mathsf B\) be compact metric spaces, let \(\alpha\in\mathcal P(\mathsf A)\) and \(\beta\in\mathcal P(\mathsf B)\), and let \(\mathcal R\subseteq\mathsf A\times\mathsf B\) be closed.  There exists a coupling \(\pi\in\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)\) with \(\pi(\mathcal R)=1\) if and only if \(\alpha(A)\leq\beta(\mathcal R(A))\) for every compact \(A\subseteq\mathsf A\), where \(\mathcal R(A)=\{b\in\mathsf B:(a,b)\in\mathcal R\text{ for some }a\in A\}\).
%%% FIELD gluing-statement
Let \(\alpha\in\mathcal P(\mathcal E^2)\) be the law of \((A,B)\), and let \(\beta\in\mathcal P(\mathcal E^3)\) be the law of \((B',C_1,C_2)\).  If the \(B\)-margin of \(\alpha\) equals the \(B'\)-margin of \(\beta\), then there is \(\gamma\in\mathcal P(\mathcal E^4)\) whose \((A,B)\)-margin is \(\alpha\) and whose \((B,C_1,C_2)\)-margin is \(\beta\).
%%% FIELD gluing-proof
Equip the compact metric carrier \(\mathcal E\) with a bounded compatible metric \(d\).  For every \(n\), choose a finite Borel partition \(\mathcal P_n\) that isolates \(\{\infty\}\), whose other cells have \(d\)-diameter at most \(1/n\).  Write \(\zeta\) for the common \(B\)-margin.  On \(\mathcal E^2\times\mathcal E^3\), define
\[
 \widetilde\gamma_n
 =\sum_{H\in\mathcal P_n:\,\zeta(H)>0}
 \frac{\alpha\vert_{\{B\in H\}}\otimes
       \beta\vert_{\{B'\in H\}}}{\zeta(H)}.
\]
The total mass of the summand indexed by \(H\) is \(\zeta(H)\), so \(\widetilde\gamma_n\) is a probability measure.  For every bounded measurable \(g\) on \(\mathcal E^2\),
\[
 \int g(A,B)\,d\widetilde\gamma_n
 =\sum_{H:\,\zeta(H)>0}
   \frac{\int_{\{B\in H\}}g(A,B)\,d\alpha}{\zeta(H)}
   \beta(B'\in H)
 =\int g\,d\alpha.
\]
The analogous calculation shows that the \((B',C_1,C_2)\)-margin is exactly \(\beta\).  Moreover,
\[
 d(B,B')\leq 1/n
 \quad\widetilde\gamma_n\text{-almost surely},
\]
because both coordinates lie in the same partition cell, including the isolated cell \(\{\infty\}\).

By compactness of \(\mathcal P(\mathcal E^5)\), supplied by the cited weak-compactness lemma, a subsequence converges weakly to some \(\widetilde\gamma\).  Coordinate projections are continuous, so their pushforward laws converge; since the two relevant margins are constant along the sequence, the corresponding margins of \(\widetilde\gamma\) remain \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).  The function \((b,b')\mapsto d(b,b')\) is bounded and continuous, and hence
\[
 \int d(B,B')\,d\widetilde\gamma
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}\int d(B,B')\,d\widetilde\gamma_n=0.
\]
Thus \(B=B'\) \(\widetilde\gamma\)-almost surely.  Identifying the two equal coordinates and pushing \(\widetilde\gamma\) forward to \((A,B,C_1,C_2)\) gives the asserted \(\gamma\).  This argument uses only compactness and a vanishing-mesh finite-partition limit; it invokes neither densities nor positivity.

%%% FIELD target-proof
Define the latent compatibility class independently of the observed-law functional \(\Theta_I(P)\).  For a fixed \(x\in\mathcal X\), call a probability law \(Q_x\) on \(\mathcal E^4\) compatible if its \((D(0),R(0))\)-margin is \(\mu_{0x}\), its \((D(1),R(1))\)-margin is \(\mu_{1x}\), and it assigns probability one to \(D(0)\leq D(1)\).  The within-arm margins already obey the semicompeting-risk order by the maintained assumption.  The causal identified surface set is the collection of target surfaces induced by families \((Q_x:x\in\mathcal X)\) that, together with the observed treatment and censoring mechanisms, reproduce \(P\).  This definition precedes, and does not use, the formula defining \(\Theta_I(P)\).

First, the complete within-arm laws used below are observable-law functionals.  Positive cell support and treatment overlap give
\[
 \Pr_P(Z=z,X=x)>0
 \qquad(z\in\{0,1\},\ x\in\mathcal X),
\]
while full-horizon censoring support gives \(G_{zx}(L)\geq\gamma>0\).  Consequently every division in the IPCW identity is legal.  By the established IPCW recovery lemma, consistency, conditional exchangeability, arm-specific independent censoring, and these support conditions identify \(\mu_{zx}\), including its atom at \(\infty\).  Hence \(d_x\), \(\zeta_x\), \(b_x\), \(K_x\), \(s_x\), and \(S\) are functionals of \(P\).

We next prove necessity by a set-inclusion argument.  Let \(Q_x\) be compatible and let \(\nu_x\) be its \((D(0),R(1))\)-margin.  Its margins are \(d_x\) and \(\zeta_x\).  For every \(u\in[0,\tau]\) and every Borel \(B\subseteq\mathcal E\), terminal monotonicity gives
\[
 \{D(0)>u,R(1)\in B\}
 \subseteq
 \{D(1)>u,R(1)\in B\}.
\]
Taking \(Q_x\)-probabilities and using the treated within-arm margin yields
\[
 \nu_x((u,\infty]\times B)
 \leq \mu_{1x}((u,\infty]\times B)
 =K_x(u,B).
\]
Thus \(\nu_x\in\mathcal N_x\).  The argument uses every readmission-mark set \(B\), not merely the unmarked death-survival inequality.

For sufficiency, fix \(x\) and \(\nu_x\in\mathcal N_x\).  Put \(\alpha=\nu_x\) and \(\beta=\mu_{1x}\), both regarded as laws on \(\mathcal E^2\); they have the same second-coordinate margin \(\zeta_x\).  On two copies of \(\mathcal E^2\), introduce the closed relation
\[
 \mathcal R
 =\{((d_0,v),(d_1,v')):v=v',\ d_0\leq d_1\}.
\]
Closedness uses the closed diagonal of the compact metric space \(\mathcal E\) and the closed order graph on \([0,\tau]\sqcup\{\infty\}\).

We verify the compact-set inequalities required by the cited closed-relation coupling theorem.  The defining capacity inequality first implies its closed-tail version:
\[
 \alpha([u,\infty]\times B)\leq\beta([u,\infty]\times B)
 \qquad(u\in\mathcal E,\ B\in\mathscr B(\mathcal E)).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, for \(0<u\leq\tau\), take \(u_k\uparrow u\) with \(u_k<u\) and use continuity from above on \((u_k,\infty]\times B\).  At \(u=0\), (1) is equality because the second margins coincide.  At \(u=\infty\), it is precisely the defining inequality at the boundary cut \(u=\tau\), since \((\tau,\infty]=\{\infty\}\).  Thus neither the horizon atom nor the zero boundary is omitted.

Let \(A\subseteq\mathcal E^2\) be compact and put \(U=\mathcal R(A)\).  Compactness of \(A\) and closedness of \(\mathcal R\) on a compact product make \(U\) compact.  For each mark \(v\), the section \(U_v\) is empty or a closed upper interval \([h(v),\infty]\).  Give an empty section a separate cemetery label.  For finite \(a\),
\[
 \{v:h(v)\leq a\}
 =\operatorname{proj}_v\!left(A\cap([0,a]\times\mathcal E)\right),
\]
which is compact; hence \(h\) is Borel measurable on the nonempty sections.  Choose nested finite grids of \([0,\tau]\) with mesh decreasing to zero.  Replace every finite \(h(v)\) by the largest grid point \(\underline h_n(v)\leq h(v)\), retain the section \(\{\infty\}\) when \(h(v)=\infty\), and retain empty sections.  The resulting sets \(U_n\) decrease to \(U\).  Each \(U_n\) is a finite disjoint union of sets \([q,\infty]\times B_{nq}\), together possibly with \(\{\infty\}\times B_{n\infty}\), for Borel and pairwise disjoint mark sets \(B_{nq}\).  Summing (1) over these disjoint pieces gives \(\alpha(U_n)\leq\beta(U_n)\).  Continuity from above now gives
\[
 \alpha(A)\leq\alpha(U)leq\beta(U)=\beta(\mathcal R(A)).                                  \tag{2}
\]
The cited theorem applied to (2) supplies a coupling of \((D(0),R(1))\sim\nu_x\) and \((D(1),R'(1))\sim\mu_{1x}\) supported on \(R(1)=R'(1)\) and \(D(0)\leq D(1)\).  After identifying the equal mark coordinates, denote its law on \((D(0),D(1),R(1))\) by \(\pi_x\).

It remains to attach the control readmission without altering either constructed margin.  Reorder \(\mu_{0x}\) as the law of \((R(0),D(0))\).  This law and \(\pi_x\) have the same \(D(0)\)-margin \(d_x\).  The proved compact-coordinate gluing lemma therefore gives a law \(Q_x\) of \((R(0),D(0),D(1),R(1))\) whose \((D(0),R(0))\)-margin is \(\mu_{0x}\) and whose \((D(0),D(1),R(1))\)-margin is \(\pi_x\).  It follows that
\[
 \mathcal L_{Q_x}(D(0),R(0))=\mu_{0x},\qquad
 \mathcal L_{Q_x}(D(1),R(1))=\mu_{1x},\qquad
 Q_x\{D(0)\leq D(1)\}=1.                                                               \tag{3}
\]
The strict within-arm event orders are inherited from the fixed margins \(\mu_{0x}\) and \(\mu_{1x}\).  No rank or density condition is used in this lifting step.

Because \(\mathcal X\) is finite, the cellwise collection \(x\mapsto Q_x\) is automatically a measurable probability kernel.  Draw \(X\) with masses \(p_x\); conditional on \(X=x\), draw the potential path from \(Q_x\), draw \(Z\) independently with probability \(e(x)\), and draw \(C\) from its observed conditional law given \((Z,X)\), independently of the potential path.  Apply consistency to form the factual event path.  In arm \(z\) and cell \(x\), (3) gives exactly \(\mu_{zx}\), and the construction also preserves the observed laws of \((X,Z,C)\).  Since the at-risk, terminal-event, and first-readmission histories through \(\tau\) are measurable functions of these variables, the resulting law of \(O\) is exactly \(P\).  Thus every family \(\nu_x\in\mathcal N_x\) has a measurable compatible full-law lift reproducing the observed distribution.

Finally, we calculate the causal targets under an arbitrary compatible lift.  Terminal monotonicity gives the event identity
\[
 \{D(0)>t,D(1)>t\}=\{D(0)>t\}.                                                            \tag{4}
\]
For \((r,t)\in\mathcal H_\eta\), the target-domain definition gives \(S(t)\geq\eta>0\), so conditioning and division by \(S(t)\) are valid.  Using (4), conditioning first on \(X\), and using the definitions of \(\nu_x\) and \(b_x\), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\Pr\{R(1)\leq r\mid D(0)>t,D(1)>t\}
 -\Pr\{R(0)\leq r\mid D(0)>t,D(1)>t\}\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x
 \left[\nu_x((t,\infty]\times[0,r])-b_x(r,t)\right]}{S(t)}
 =\theta_\nu(r,t).                                                                        \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(0\leq a\leq t\), the pointwise identity
\[
 \min\{q,a\}=a-\int_0^a\mathbf 1\{q\leq r\}\,dr,
 \qquad q\in\mathcal E,
\]
also holds at \(q=\infty\).  The integrand is nonnegative and bounded, so Tonelli's theorem applies.  Subtracting the two conditional expectations and using (5) yields
\[
 E[\min\{R(1),a\}-\min\{R(0),a\}\mid D(0)>t,D(1)>t]
 =-\int_0^a\theta_\nu(r,t)\,dr
 =\rho_\nu(a,t).                                                                          \tag{6}
\]

Necessity shows that every monotonicity-compatible causal surface belongs to the observed-law image \(\Theta_I(P)\); the full-law construction shows that every member of that image is attained by a law satisfying all maintained restrictions and reproducing \(P\).  Equations (5)--(6) prove the relationship between the causal estimands and the observed-law functional rather than defining that relationship.  Therefore the bound is sharp---indeed, it is an exact set characterization---for the nonparametric class with \(D(0)\leq D(1)\).  If terminal monotonicity is removed, both the inclusion used in necessity and the denominator identity (4) can fail, so this proof makes no equality claim for that larger class.

The finite-table certificate in the strict-support proposition gives
\[
 \left(\sum_hq^-_{ihj}\right)_{ij}=f^-_{ij},\qquad
 \left(\sum_hq^+_{ihj}\right)_{ij}=f^+_{ij},\qquad
 \left(\sum_iq^-_{ihj}\right)_{hj}
 =\left(\sum_iq^+_{ihj}\right)_{hj}=\mu_{1,hj}^\star.                                    \tag{7}
\]
After attaching \(R(0)=\infty\), both laws have control margin \(\mu_0^\star\).  Every positive entry listed in the construction has \(i\leq h\), so both laws satisfy \(D(0)\leq D(1)\).  They are distinct: for example,
\[
 f^-_{00}=1/10\neq1/20=f^+_{00}.                                                           \tag{8}
\]

The construction uses the same degenerate covariate, \(Z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) independent of the potential path, and \(C=\tau=3\) under both signs.  By consistency, treatment arm \(z\) reveals only \((D(z),R(z))\).  Thus, for every measurable set \(A\) of observed paths, if \(\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\) denotes the deterministic observed history generated from treatment \(z\), event times \((d,r)\), and censoring time \(3\), then (7) gives
\[
 \Pr_\pm(O\in A)
 =\frac12\sum_{z=0}^1
   \int\mathbf 1\{\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\in A\}\,d\mu_z^\star(d,r),                       \tag{9}
\]
which is independent of the sign.  This also verifies conditional exchangeability directly, while \(e=1/2\) and \(G_z(u)=1\) supply treatment and full-horizon censoring overlap.

The same finite-table proposition computes
\[
 \ell_\star(f^-)=-\frac1{20},
 \qquad
 \ell_\star(f^+)=\frac14.                                                                \tag{10}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak C\) be any nonparametric causal class containing both constructed laws.  At the common observable distribution in (9), the identified set for \(\ell_\star\) over \(\mathfrak C\) contains both values in (10); by (10) they differ by \(3/10>0\).  Therefore \(\ell_\star\) is not point identified in \(\mathfrak C\).  Enlarging a model class can only add compatible full laws, so the two-law witness survives every such enlargement.  This set-inclusion argument does not show that all additional, possibly nonmonotone laws are represented by \(\mathcal N_x\), and accordingly proves no enlarged-class sharpness claim.
