The proof uses nested-deadline matching, suffix cuts, and compact limiting.  Fix \(x\) and \(t\in[0,\tau]\), write \(s=s_x(t)\), and use the boundary convention \(K_x(\infty,B)=s_x(\infty)=0\).  If \(s=0\), monotonicity of \(s_x\) gives \(s_x(u)=0\) for every \(u\geq t\); the cut at \(u=\infty\), together with nonnegativity, makes both deadline ranks zero.  Hence the unstandardized claim is immediate.  Assume below that \(s>0\).

For any compatible full law, let \(w\) be the treated-record submeasure selected by \(D(0)>t\):
\[
w(H)=\Pr\{(D(1),R(1))\in H,D(0)>t\mid X=x\}.
\]
It has mass \(s\), is dominated by \(\mu_{1x}\), and is supported on \(D(1)>t\).  For every \(u\geq t\), terminal monotonicity gives
\[
w(D(1)>u)\geq\Pr(D(0)>u\mid X=x)=s_x(u).                             \tag{12}
\]
For a Borel mark set \(B\), split the selected records at deadline \(u\).  Equation (12) permits at most \(s-s_x(u)\) selected records at or before \(u\), while domination by \(\mu_{1x}\) permits at most \(K_x(u,B)\) available \(B\)-marked records after \(u\).  Therefore
\[
w(R(1)\in B)\leq s-s_x(u)+K_x(u,B).
\]
This also holds at \(u=\infty\) by the declared boundary convention.  Taking the infimum gives the upper deadline bound.

We next prove sharpness on a finite rational grid.  Multiply every mass by a common denominator.  Make one demand copy for each unit of control mass with \(D(0)>t\) and one resource copy for each unit of treated mass with \(D(1)>t\).  Join a demand at death level \(i\) to a resource at death level \(h\) precisely when \(i\leq h\).  Taking the full mark set in the survivor-capacity inequalities gives every suffix condition
\[
\#\{\text{resources later than }u\}
\geq\#\{\text{demands later than }u\}.                              \tag{13}
\]
Sorting demands from latest to earliest and assigning an unused admissible resource at each step proves by induction from (13) that all demands can be matched.

Fix a mark set \(B\).  Starting from a full matching, use alternating-path augmentation to maximize the number of selected \(B\)-resources.  Because demand neighborhoods are nested suffixes, if no augmentation is possible, the demands reached by alternating paths may be enlarged to the suffix beginning at their earliest death level without enlarging their resource neighborhood.  At the stopping suffix \(u\), every selected \(B\)-resource is either used by a demand at or before \(u\), of which there are \(s-s_x(u)\) after rescaling, or is an available \(B\)-resource after \(u\), of which there are \(K_x(u,B)\).  Thus equality is attained in the smallest of the valid cut bounds
\[
\#\{\text{selected \(B\)-resources}\}
\leq\#\{\text{demands at or before }u\}
 +\#\{\text{available \(B\)-resources after }u\}.                 \tag{14}
\]
Hence the maximum selected \(B\)-mass is \(\min_u\{s-s_x(u)+K_x(u,B)\}\).  Superposing the maximizing partial matching with a full matching and augmenting on alternating paths and cycles extends it to a full survivor-demand matching without deleting a selected \(B\)-resource.

The family of matchable resource subsets has the augmentation property.  Indeed, superpose matchings of two matchable sets of different sizes.  A component starting from a resource used only by the larger matching must be an alternating path with an unmatched endpoint, and switching that path augments the smaller set.  Order resources by increasing readmission category and greedily retain a resource exactly when the retained set remains matchable.  The augmentation property implies after each prefix that the retained set has maximum cardinality among resources in that prefix.  Extending the final retained set to a matching of all survivor demands therefore produces one coupling attaining the upper cut formula simultaneously for every readmission prefix.  Reversing the mark order maximizes every complementary suffix and hence produces one coupling minimizing every prefix simultaneously.

The unselected treated records can be paired with control deaths at or before \(t\).  Put \(T_x(u)=\Pr(D(1)>u\mid X=x)\).  For \(u\leq t\), every selected resource has \(D(1)>t\), so its tail mass at \(u\) is \(s\).  The residual treated and control tails are respectively \(T_x(u)-s\) and \(s_x(u)-s\).  Taking the full mark set in the survivor-capacity inequality gives \(T_x(u)\geq s_x(u)\), and hence
\[
T_x(u)-s\geq s_x(u)-s.                                               \tag{15}
\]
For \(u>t\), the residual control tail is zero.  The same latest-to-earliest suffix induction therefore completes the terminal-monotone matching without changing the selected cohort.

q_{kj}=\zeta_x(j)-\sum_{i\leq k}f_{ij}
=\sum_{i>k}f_{ij}.                                                    \tag{9}
