%%% FIELD proof-strict-cross-cohort-support
All probabilities in this proof are conditional on the sole value of \(X\).  By the construction in \(\mathfrak E_3\), \(R(0)=\infty\), so the control numerator \(b_x(r,t)\) in the definition of \(\theta_\nu\) is zero at every finite \(r\).  The four control-death rows each have mass \(1/4\); hence, by the definitions of \(s_x\) and \(S\),
\[
 S(1)=\frac34,\qquad S(2)=\frac12,\qquad S(3)=\frac14. \tag{1}
\]
The premise \(0<\eta\leq 1/4\), (1), and the definition of \(\mathcal H_\eta\) therefore imply that \((1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\in\mathcal H_\eta\).  In particular, every division below is by a strictly positive survivor mass.

For a feasible projected table \(f=(f_{ij})\), set
\[
 A_1=\sum_{i>0}f_{i0},\qquad
 A_2=\sum_{\substack{i>1\\ j\leq1}}f_{ij},\qquad
 A_3=\sum_{j\leq2}f_{3j}. \tag{2}
\]
The quadratic chain-flow theorem identifies feasibility with the two margin equations and the singleton inequalities
\[
 \sum_{i>k}f_{ij}\leq\kappa^\star_{kj}\quad(k=0,1,2;\ j=0,1,2,3). \tag{3}
\]
At the first cut, the row demands put total mass \(3/4\) beyond the cut.  The capacities of columns \(1,2,3\) there sum to \(20/100+15/100+35/100=7/10\), whereas the column-\(0\) capacity is \(20/100=1/5\).  Thus (3) gives
\[
 \frac1{20}=\frac34-\frac7{10}\leq A_1\leq\kappa^\star_{00}=\frac15. \tag{4}
\]
At the second cut, total mass beyond the cut is \(1/2\).  The capacities in columns \(2,3\) sum to \(15/100+30/100=9/20\), while those in columns \(0,1\) sum to \(15/100+10/100=1/4\).  Hence
\[
 \frac1{20}=\frac12-\frac9{20}\leq A_2
 \leq\kappa^\star_{10}+\kappa^\star_{11}=\frac14. \tag{5}
\]
Finally the last row has mass \(1/4\).  Since \(f_{33}\leq\kappa^\star_{23}=1/5\), and since the capacities in columns \(0,1,2\) at the third cut sum to \(10/100+5/100+5/100=1/5\),
\[
 \frac1{20}=\frac14-\frac15\leq A_3
 \leq\sum_{j=0}^2\kappa^\star_{2j}=\frac15. \tag{6}
\]

These endpoints are attainable.  The displayed table \(f^+\) attains the upper endpoints in (4) and (6), \(f^-\) attains the upper endpoint in (5) and the lower endpoint in (6), and the remaining two lower endpoints are attained by
\[
100g^{(1)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
20&0&0&5\\
0&15&5&5\\
0&0&5&20\\
5&5&5&10
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
100g^{(2)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
20&0&0&5\\
0&20&0&5\\
0&0&10&15\\
5&0&5&15
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{7}
\]
Both tables in (7) have row sums \((25,25,25,25)/100\) and column sums \((25,20,15,40)/100\).  Their complete singleton chain loads are, respectively,
\[
\frac1{100}
\begin{pmatrix}
5&20&15&35\\
5&5&10&30\\
5&5&5&10
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\frac1{100}
\begin{pmatrix}
5&20&15&35\\
5&0&15&30\\
5&0&5&15
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{8}
\]
Every entry in (8) is bounded by the corresponding entry of \(\kappa^\star\).  The quadratic chain-flow theorem therefore proves feasibility; direct substitution gives \(A_1(g^{(1)})=1/20\) and \(A_2(g^{(2)})=1/20\).  The feasible flow polytope is convex, and each \(A_j\) is linear, so its image is an interval.  Consequently (4)--(6) are the exact coordinate ranges.

By the definition of \(\theta_\nu\), the vanishing control numerator, (1), and (2),
\[
 \theta_\nu(1,1)=\frac{A_1}{3/4},\qquad
 \theta_\nu(2,2)=\frac{A_2}{1/2},\qquad
 \theta_\nu(3,3)=\frac{A_3}{1/4}. \tag{9}
\]
Combining (4)--(6) with (9) proves
\[
 \theta_\nu(1,1)\in\left[\frac1{15},\frac4{15}\right],\qquad
 \theta_\nu(2,2)\in\left[\frac1{10},\frac12\right],\qquad
 \theta_\nu(3,3)\in\left[\frac15,\frac45\right]. \tag{10}
\]

We next verify the two full-law certificates.  Summing the explicitly listed entries of \(q^-\) and \(q^+\) over the treated-death index gives
\[
100\left(\sum_hq^-_{ihj}\right)_{ij}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
10&0&10&5\\
0&10&5&10\\
15&5&0&5\\
0&5&0&20
\end{pmatrix}
=100f^-,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
100\left(\sum_hq^+_{ihj}\right)_{ij}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
5&0&10&10\\
10&15&0&0\\
0&0&0&25\\
10&5&5&5
\end{pmatrix}
=100f^+.
\tag{12}
\]
Summing instead over the control-death index gives, for both signs,
\[
100\left(\sum_iq^\pm_{ihj}\right)_{hj}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
5&0&0&5\\
5&10&0&5\\
5&5&10&10\\
10&5&5&20
\end{pmatrix}
=100\mu_1^\star. \tag{13}
\]
All listed entries are nonnegative.  Equations (11)--(13) prove the asserted \((D(0),R(1))\) and \((D(1),R(1))\) margins.  The row sums in (11)--(12) are \((1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\); after attaching \(R(0)=\infty\), they give exactly \(\mu_0^\star\).  Every positive listed triple satisfies \(i\leq h\), and the death categories are ordered, so both laws put probability one on \(D(0)\leq D(1)\).  Moreover every positive cell with finite treated readmission has \(r_j^\star<d_h^\star\), while the control readmission is infinite; hence the semicompeting-order restriction also holds.

For completeness, the complete singleton chain loads of the two projected margins are
\[
100\left(\sum_{i>k}f^-_{ij}\right)_{kj}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
15&20&5&35\\
15&10&0&25\\
0&5&0&20
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
100\left(\sum_{i>k}f^+_{ij}\right)_{kj}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
20&20&5&30\\
10&5&5&30\\
10&5&5&5
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{14}
\]
Comparison with the displayed matrix \(100\kappa^\star\) proves every singleton inequality in (3).  Any mark set on this finite carrier is a union of singleton columns, so summing the corresponding inequalities in (14) proves every readmission-mark capacity constraint.

Using (1), (2), and the definition of \(\ell_\star\) yields
\[
 \ell_\star(\nu)=A_1-A_2+A_3
 =f_{10}-f_{21}+f_{30}+f_{32}. \tag{15}
\]
By nonnegativity and (3),
\[
 \ell_\star(\nu)
 \leq f_{10}+f_{30}+f_{32}
 \leq\kappa^\star_{00}+\kappa^\star_{22}
 =\frac14. \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, \(f_{10}+f_{30}\leq\sum_{i>0}f_{i0}\leq\kappa^\star_{00}\) and \(f_{32}\leq\kappa^\star_{22}\).  At the second cut in column \(1\), and at the third cut in column \(3\), (3) and the last-row margin give
\[
 f_{21}+f_{31}\leq\frac1{10},\qquad
 f_{30}+f_{31}+f_{32}
 =\frac14-f_{33}\geq\frac14-\frac15=\frac1{20}. \tag{17}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \ell_\star(\nu)
 =f_{10}-(f_{21}+f_{31})+(f_{30}+f_{31}+f_{32})
 \geq-\frac1{20}. \tag{18}
\]
Direct substitution into (15) gives \(\ell_\star(f^-)=-1/20\) and \(\ell_\star(f^+)=1/4\).  The lifts (11)--(13) attain those endpoints, and every mixture of the two lifts preserves both within-arm margins and all support restrictions.  Thus the sharp signed interval is
\[
 \left[-\frac1{20},\frac14\right]. \tag{19}
\]
In contrast, optimizing the signed direction over the product of the three coordinate intervals in (10) gives
\[
 \frac34\frac1{15}-\frac12\frac12+\frac14\frac15=-\frac3{20},
 \qquad
 \frac34\frac4{15}-\frac12\frac1{10}+\frac14\frac45=\frac7{20}. \tag{20}
\]
Hence the coordinate rectangle gives \([-3/20,7/20]\), which strictly contains (19), with a gap \(1/10\) at each endpoint.  This establishes strict cross-cohort support rather than merely coordinatewise nonidentification.

It remains to verify the bounded-Lipschitz version and its exact regime.  Fix the density and bandwidth in the statement and define
\[
 \varphi_\delta(u)=\delta^{-1}\varphi(u/\delta),\qquad
 b_\delta=\|\varphi_\delta\|_\infty,\qquad
 l_\delta=\|\varphi_\delta'\|_\infty,
\tag{21}
\]
\[
 M_\delta=\max\{b_\delta,b_\delta^2\},\qquad
 L_\delta=\max\{l_\delta,2b_\delta l_\delta\}. \tag{22}
\]
Because \(\varphi\) is \(C^1\) with compact support, all four quantities in (21)--(22) are finite.  Replace each finite death or readmission category by the translate of \(\varphi_\delta\) centered at that category, retaining the atom at \(\infty\).  Conditional on a positive triple \((i,h,j)\), use independent death-bump draws if \(i<h\), use one common death-bump draw if \(i=h\), and use an independent readmission-bump draw; retain \(R(0)=\infty\).

Distinct finite death centers differ by at least one, so \(2\delta<1\) preserves \(D(0)<D(1)\) in every cell with \(i<h\), while the common draw preserves equality in cells with \(i=h\).  The smallest finite treated readmission--death center gap in a positive cell is \(1/4\); since \(2\delta<1/4\), every such smoothed cell satisfies \(R(1)<D(1)\).  Thus both smoothed laws preserve terminal monotonicity and the semicompeting order pointwise.

Every finite-part within-arm density is a convex mixture of translates of \(\varphi_\delta\) or of \(\varphi_\delta\otimes\varphi_\delta\).  The one-dimensional densities have sup-norm at most \(b_\delta\) and Lipschitz constant at most \(l_\delta\).  For a product density \(p(u,v)=\varphi_\delta(u-a)\varphi_\delta(v-b)\), the product rule and the mean-value inequality give
\[
 \|p\|_\infty\leq b_\delta^2,\qquad
 |p(u,v)-p(u',v')|
 \leq b_\delta l_\delta\bigl(|u-u'|+|v-v'|\bigr)
 \leq2b_\delta l_\delta\|(u,v)-(u',v')\|_\infty. \tag{23}
\]
Convex mixtures do not increase these bounds.  Equations (22)--(23) prove membership in every declared envelope class satisfying
\[
 M\geq M_\delta,\qquad L_{\mathrm{Lip}}\geq L_\delta. \tag{24}
\]
This is a nonempty bounded-Lipschitz regime; the argument makes no assertion when either inequality in (24) fails.

No bump support crosses any of the thresholds \(1,2,3\).  Consequently, binning \(D(0)\) and \(R(1)\) by their separated bump supports sends every compatible smoothed coupling to a categorical table with the same margins and all inequalities (3), and every surface numerator and denominator used in (4)--(20) equals its binned categorical counterpart.  Conversely, smoothing either endpoint lift by the just-described kernels preserves its margins and order restrictions.  Thus the exact signed interval (19), the coordinate rectangle (20), and their strict gaps survive smoothing throughout (24).

Finally fix \(\varphi\), \(\delta\), and the structurally permitted incidence pattern.  The relevant lower and upper support problems are finite linear programs whose constraint matrix is fixed and whose right-hand sides depend linearly on the mixture weights.  At the displayed law every structurally permitted within-arm cell has positive weight, so its right-hand side is in the relative interior of the corresponding feasible polyhedral domain.  Eliminating equality variables and enumerating the finitely many bases shows that, on a relative-interior neighborhood, each finite optimal value is a finite piecewise-affine, hence continuous, function of those weights.  The survivor denominators in (1) remain positive on a sufficiently small neighborhood, so the standardized coordinate endpoints are continuous there as well.  Each of the two strict gaps equals \(1/10\) at the displayed law by (19)--(20); continuity therefore leaves both gaps positive on some relatively open neighborhood.  The kernels remain fixed and the weights remain nonnegative and sum to one, so (23)--(24) continue to hold throughout that neighborhood.  This proves every assertion of the proposition.

%%% FIELD proof-three-horizon-nonidentification-transfer
Assume \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), and let \(\mathbb P_-\) and \(\mathbb P_+\) denote the full laws represented by \(q^-_{ihj}\) and \(q^+_{ihj}\), respectively.  The strict cross-cohort support proposition proves the finite-table identities
\[
 \left(\sum_hq^-_{ihj}\right)_{ij}=f^-_{ij},\qquad
 \left(\sum_hq^+_{ihj}\right)_{ij}=f^+_{ij}. \tag{1}
\]
It also proves
\[
 \left(\sum_iq^-_{ihj}\right)_{hj}
 =\left(\sum_iq^+_{ihj}\right)_{hj}
 =\mu_{1,hj}^\star. \tag{2}
\]
Thus (1) gives the two distinct \((D(0),R(1))\) margins, while (2) proves that both laws have treated within-arm margin \(\mu_1^\star\).  The row sums of both tables in (1) are \((1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\).  Attaching \(R(0)=\infty\), as required by the definition of \(\mathfrak E_3\), therefore makes the control within-arm margin under both signs equal to \(\mu_0^\star\).

Every positive listed triple has \(i\leq h\).  Since \(d_0^\star<d_1^\star<d_2^\star<d_3^\star=\infty\),
\[
 \mathbb P_\pm\{D(0)\leq D(1)\}=1. \tag{3}
\]
The laws are distinct because (1) and the displayed projected arrays give
\[
 \mathbb P_-\{D(0)=d_0^\star,R(1)=r_0^\star\}
 =f^-_{00}=\frac1{10}
 \neq\frac1{20}=f^+_{00}
 =\mathbb P_+\{D(0)=d_0^\star,R(1)=r_0^\star\}. \tag{4}
\]

We now prove observational equivalence rather than infer it from the projected margins.  In both constructions \(X\) is degenerate, \(Z\) is Bernoulli with probability \(1/2\) and independent of all potential times, and \(C=3=\tau\).  Let \(\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\) denote the deterministic observed history through \(\tau\) generated by treatment \(z\), factual terminal time \(d\), factual readmission time \(r\), and censoring time \(3\).  By consistency, arm \(z\) reveals \((D(z),R(z))\).  Hence, for every measurable observed-path set \(A\) and each \(\sigma\in\{-,+\}\),
\[
 \mathbb P_\sigma(O\in A)
 =\frac12\sum_{z=0}^1
 \int\mathbf 1\{\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\in A\}\,
 d\mu_z^\star(d,r). \tag{5}
\]
The right side of (5) does not depend on \(\sigma\), because (1)--(2) give the same two factual within-arm laws and the treatment and censoring mechanisms are common.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_-}(O)=\mathcal L_{\mathbb P_+}(O). \tag{6}
\]

The exhibit has survivor masses
\[
 S(1)=\frac34,\qquad S(2)=\frac12,\qquad S(3)=\frac14. \tag{7}
\]
The premise on \(\eta\), (7), and the definition of \(\mathcal H_\eta\) show that all three coordinates in \(\ell_\star\) are well defined.  The strict cross-cohort support proposition then gives
\[
 \ell_\star(f^-)=-\frac1{20},\qquad
 \ell_\star(f^+)=\frac14. \tag{8}
\]

Let \(\mathfrak C\) be any nonparametric causal model class containing both atomic laws.  At the common observed distribution in (6), its identified set for \(\ell_\star\) contains both values in (8), whose separation is
\[
 \frac14-\left(-\frac1{20}\right)=\frac3{10}>0. \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\ell_\star\), and hence the joint surface from which it is computed, is not point identified in \(\mathfrak C\).  This conclusion uses only inclusion of the two observationally equivalent witnesses.  It does not say that every law added by enlarging the class is represented by the marked-capacity fiber and therefore does not assert sharpness after terminal monotonicity is removed.

%%% FIELD proof-three-horizon-smooth-nonidentification-transfer
Fix the density \(\varphi\), a bandwidth \(0<\delta<1/8\), and the constants \(M_\delta\) and \(L_\delta\) in the statement, and assume
\[
 M\geq M_\delta,\qquad L_{\mathrm{Lip}}\geq L_\delta. \tag{1}
\]
Use the same smoothing kernels under both signs.  Write \(H_{i,\delta}\) for the death-bump law centered at \(d_i^\star\), \(J_{j,\delta}\) for the readmission-bump law centered at \(r_j^\star\), and retain point masses for the \(\infty\) categories.

Under either sign, the smoothed control within-arm law is
\[
 \mu_{0,\delta}^{\pm}
 =\sum_i\left(\sum_{h,j}q^\pm_{ihj}\right)
 \bigl(H_{i,\delta}\otimes\delta_\infty\bigr)
 =\sum_i\frac14\bigl(H_{i,\delta}\otimes\delta_\infty\bigr). \tag{2}
\]
Thus the smoothed control law is common.  For the treated arm, conditional on the categorical pair \((h,j)\), the marginal law of \((D(1),R(1))\) is \(H_{h,\delta}\otimes J_{j,\delta}\).  This remains true in an equal-death cell, where sharing the two cross-world death draws changes their joint law but not the treated marginal.  The marginal identity for the atomic lifts therefore gives
\[
 \mu_{1,\delta}^{\pm}
 =\sum_{h,j}\left(\sum_iq^\pm_{ihj}\right)
 \bigl(H_{h,\delta}\otimes J_{j,\delta}\bigr)
 =\sum_{h,j}\mu_{1,hj}^\star
 \bigl(H_{h,\delta}\otimes J_{j,\delta}\bigr). \tag{3}
\]
Thus the complete treated within-arm law is also common.

The strict cross-cohort support proposition proves, from separation of the bump supports, that both smoothed laws satisfy \(D(0)\leq D(1)\) and \(R(z)<D(z)\) whenever \(R(z)<\infty\).  It also proves that their finite-part within-arm densities are bounded by \(M_\delta\) and are \(L_\delta\)-Lipschitz.  The two inequalities in (1) therefore put both witnesses in the declared bounded-Lipschitz envelope class.

Give the two laws the same degenerate covariate, independent Bernoulli treatment with probability \(1/2\), and deterministic censoring \(C=\tau=3\).  Let \(\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\) be the deterministic observed history through \(\tau\).  By consistency, for every measurable observed-path set \(A\),
\[
 \Pr_{\pm,\delta}(O\in A)
 =\frac12\sum_{z=0}^1
 \int\mathbf 1\{\mathcal O(z,d,r,3)\in A\}\,
 d\mu_{z,\delta}^{\star}(d,r), \tag{4}
\]
where the common laws on the right are those in (2)--(3).  Hence the right side of (4) is independent of the sign, and the two smoothed full laws are observationally equivalent.

No bump support crosses a threshold \(1,2,\) or \(3\).  The survivor denominators and the three threshold numerators entering \(\ell_\star\) consequently retain their categorical values.  Since \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), the three coordinates lie in \(\mathcal H_\eta\), and the strict-support calculation gives
\[
 \ell_\star(\mathbb P_{-,\delta})=-\frac1{20},\qquad
 \ell_\star(\mathbb P_{+,\delta})=\frac14. \tag{5}
\]
Every causal class satisfying (1) and containing both smoothed laws therefore has, at their common observed distribution, an identified set for \(\ell_\star\) containing the two values in (5).  They differ by \(3/10\), proving non-point-identification.  The argument establishes neither envelope membership below the explicit bounds in (1) nor sharpness of the marked-capacity image in a larger class without terminal monotonicity.
