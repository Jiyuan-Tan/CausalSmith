%%% FIELD continuous-grid-proof
Fix the identified within-arm margins.  By the proved recovery lemma, these margins are functionals of \(P\).  The argument below uses the bounded finite-part coordinate-density envelope \(M\), but nowhere uses continuity or Lipschitz regularity of a density.

For each \(x\in\mathcal X\), let \(\mathcal Q_x\) be the set of probability laws \(Q\) on \(\mathcal E^4\) such that
\[
 Q_{(D(0),R(0))}=\mu_{0x},\qquad
 Q_{(D(1),R(1))}=\mu_{1x},\qquad
 Q\{D(0)\leq D(1)\}=1. \tag{1}
\]
The maintained full law shows that \(\mathcal Q_x\) is nonempty.  The compact-carrier lemma makes \(\mathcal P(\mathcal E^4)\) weakly compact.  The two marginal equalities in (1) define weakly closed sets, because they are equivalent to equality of integrals of every continuous function on \(\mathcal E^2\).  The order restriction is weakly closed as well.  Indeed, the relation
\[
 \mathcal R_D=\{(d_0,r_0,d_1,r_1):d_0\leq d_1\}
\]
is closed.  If \(Q_n(\mathcal R_D)=1\) and \(Q_n\Rightarrow Q\), then, for every positive integer \(k\), the continuous function
\[
 g_k(y)=\min\{1,k\,\operatorname{dist}(y,\mathcal R_D)\}
\]
has \(\int g_k\,dQ=\lim_n\int g_k\,dQ_n=0\).  Since \(g_k\) increases pointwise to \(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal R_D^c}\), it follows that \(Q(\mathcal R_D)=1\).  Thus \(\mathcal Q_x\) is compact.  The fixed within-arm margins in (1) already assign probability one to \(R(z)<D(z)\) whenever \(R(z)<\infty\), so the strict within-arm order creates no additional closure issue.

Projection of a law in \(\mathcal Q_x\) onto \((D(0),R(1))\) is weakly continuous.  The proved capacity-projection theorem says that the image of this projection is exactly \(\mathcal N_x\).  Hence \(\mathcal N_x\) is compact.  It is convex because its two marginal equalities and all its marked-capacity inequalities are linear in \(\nu_x\).  Since \(\mathcal X\) is finite, \(\prod_{x\in\mathcal X}\mathcal N_x\) is compact and convex.

We next prove a modulus uniform over this product.  Put
\[
 U_\nu(r,t)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x
 \left\{\nu_x((t,\infty]\times[0,r])-b_x(r,t)\right\},
 \qquad
 \theta_\nu(r,t)=\frac{U_\nu(r,t)}{S(t)}. \tag{2}
\]
The density-envelope assumption implies that, for every finite interval \(I\subseteq[0,\tau]\),
\[
 d_x(I)\leq M\,\operatorname{Leb}(I),
 \qquad
 \zeta_x(I)\leq M\,\operatorname{Leb}(I), \tag{3}
\]
and the same bound holds for the finite-part \(D(0)\)- and \(R(0)\)-coordinate margins of \(\mu_{0x}\).  Atoms at \(\infty\) do not enter any interval in (3).  For two indices \((r,t),(r',t')\in\mathcal H_\eta\), a symmetric-difference decomposition into one death strip and one readmission strip gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\left|\nu_x((t,\infty]\times[0,r])
 -\nu_x((t',\infty]\times[0,r'])\right|\\
 &\quad\leq d_x((t\wedge t',t\vee t'])
       +\zeta_x((r\wedge r',r\vee r'])\\
 &\quad\leq M\{|t-t'|+|r-r'|\}. 
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Applying the same decomposition to \(b_x(r,t)=\mu_{0x}\{R(0)\leq r,D(0)>t\}\), then summing with \(\sum_xp_x=1\), yields
\[
 |U_\nu(r,t)-U_\nu(r',t')|
 \leq 2M\{|t-t'|+|r-r'|\}. \tag{5}
\]
Likewise,
\[
 |S(t)-S(t')|\leq M|t-t'|. \tag{6}
\]
For each \(x\), both probabilities whose difference appears in (2) lie in \([0,s_x(t)]\).  Therefore
\[
 |U_\nu(r,t)|\leq S(t)\leq1. \tag{7}
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal H_\eta\), both denominators below are at least \(\eta>0\).  Equations (5)--(7) and quotient algebra give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\theta_\nu(r,t)-\theta_\nu(r',t')|
 &\leq \frac{|U_\nu(r,t)-U_\nu(r',t')|}{S(t)}
 +\frac{|U_\nu(r',t')|\,|S(t)-S(t')|}{S(t)S(t')}\\
 &\leq \frac{2M}{\eta}|r-r'|+\frac{3M}{\eta}|t-t'|\\
 &\leq C_\theta\{|r-r'|+|t-t'|\},
 \qquad C_\theta=\frac{3M}{\eta}. 
\end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
In particular, only the envelope \(M\), not \(L_{\mathrm{Lip}}\), appears in the modulus.

Equation (6) makes \(S\) continuous.  Because \(S\) is nonincreasing, \(\mathcal H_\eta\) is either empty or a closed triangle of the form
\[
 \{(r,t):0\leq r\leq t\leq T_\eta\}
\]
for some \(T_\eta\in[0,\tau]\); hence it is compact.  If it is empty, its continuous-function space contains a unique function and all the conclusions are immediate, so assume it is nonempty.  Since \(|\theta_\nu|\leq1\), the RM definition and (8) imply, for \((a,t),(a',t')\in\mathcal H_\eta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\rho_\nu(a,t)-\rho_\nu(a',t')|
 &\leq |a-a'|
 +\int_0^{a\wedge a'}|\theta_\nu(r,t)-\theta_\nu(r,t')|\,dr\\
 &\leq |a-a'|+\tau C_\theta|t-t'|. 
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
Thus all surface pairs have a common modulus depending only on \((M,\eta,\tau)\).

Let \(\nu_x^{(n)}\Rightarrow\nu_x\) for every \(x\).  For fixed finite \((r,t)\), the boundary of \((t,\infty]\times[0,r]\) is contained in
\[
 \{D(0)=t\}\cup\{R(1)=r\}. \tag{10}
\]
Both sets in (10) have probability zero by (3).  For completeness, rectangle convergence follows directly from weak convergence: choose continuous lower and upper piecewise-linear ramps for the two threshold indicators whose products bracket the rectangle indicator and differ only when \(D(0)\in[t-\delta,t+\delta]\) or \(R(1)\in[r-\delta,r+\delta]\), with intervals truncated to \([0,\tau]\) and the isolated point \(\infty\) assigned its exact indicator value.  Their integral gap is at most \(4M\delta\) by (3).  Weak convergence passes the two continuous ramp integrals to the limit, and letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves
\[
 \nu_x^{(n)}((t,\infty]\times[0,r])
 \longrightarrow \nu_x((t,\infty]\times[0,r]). \tag{11}
\]
Consequently \(\theta_{\nu^{(n)}}\) converges pointwise to \(\theta_\nu\).  A finite net of the compact domain, together with the common modulus (8), upgrades this convergence to uniform convergence.  The integral definition gives
\[
 \|\rho_{\nu^{(n)}}-\rho_\nu\|_\infty
 \leq \tau\|\theta_{\nu^{(n)}}-\theta_\nu\|_\infty\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
The surface map from \(\prod_x\mathcal N_x\) to \(C(\mathcal H_\eta)^2\) is therefore continuous.  Its image \(\Theta_I(P)\) is compact.  It is convex because (2) and the RM integral are affine in \((\nu_x)_x\).  Every continuous signed linear functional has a compact real image of \(\Theta_I(P)\), so its upper and lower support values are attained.

It remains to compare the continuous fiber with exact finite grids.  Choose nested partitions \(\Pi_m\) of \([0,\tau]\), containing \(T_\eta\), whose maximum cell width \(\delta_m\) tends to zero, and retain \(\infty\) as an isolated category.  For finite \(u\), let \(\pi_m(u)\) be the least grid knot not smaller than \(u\), and set \(\pi_m(\infty)=\infty\).  At every grid knot \(v\),
\[
 \{\pi_m(D)>v\}=\{D>v\},
 \qquad
 \{\pi_m(R)\leq v\}=\{R\leq v\}. \tag{13}
\]
Push the fixed within-arm laws forward by \(\pi_m\).  When two within-arm event coordinates enter the same cell, retain their finite order mark; this keeps the strict semicompeting-risk order available to the full-law lift but does not change either event in (13).  Use the proved chain-flow theorem for these coarsened margins, interpolate every exact knotwise TV surface piecewise affinely over \(\mathcal H_\eta\), and define the corresponding RM coordinate by exact integration of that interpolant.  Denote the resulting surface set by \(K_m\), and put \(K=\Theta_I(P)\).  Each \(K_m\) is compact because the chain-flow feasible set is a closed bounded finite-dimensional polytope and interpolation is continuous.  The chain-flow theorem identifies every exact signed knot support with the primal support value \(h_m(c)\) and its stated dual infimum.

For the first directed approximation, take \(q=(\theta_\nu,\rho_\nu)\in K\) and use the capacity-projection theorem to choose a compatible full-law lift.  Applying \(\pi_m\) to all four event coordinates preserves \(D(0)\leq D(1)\), because \(\pi_m\) is nondecreasing, and the retained within-cell marks preserve the strict within-arm order.  The resulting finite law is feasible for the exact chain flow.  By (13), its TV value at every knot is exactly the continuous TV value there.  Every point and the vertices used to interpolate it are at coordinate distance at most \(\delta_m\).  Thus (8), followed by the integral bound in (12), gives a constant \(C_1=C_1(M,\eta,\tau)<\infty\), independent of \(q\) and \(m\), such that
\[
 \sup_{q\in K}\inf_{q_m\in K_m}
 \|q-q_m\|_\infty
 \leq C_1\delta_m\longrightarrow0. \tag{14}
\]

For the reverse direction, choose an arbitrary sequence \(q_m\in K_m\).  The chain-flow theorem supplies, for every \(x\), a finite-grid full-law lift.  Embed every finite category at a representative in its source cell, choosing representatives according to the retained order mark so that \(R(z)<D(z)\) whenever \(R(z)<\infty\).  Call the embedded law \(Q_{mx}\).  Every representative is within \(\delta_m\) of its cell, and grid death order implies
\[
 Q_{mx}\{D(0)\leq D(1)+\delta_m\}=1. \tag{15}
\]
Weak compactness and finiteness of \(\mathcal X\) give a common subsequence such that
\[
 Q_{mx}\Rightarrow Q_x\qquad\text{for every }x. \tag{16}
\]
Uniform continuity of a continuous test function shows that each within-arm margin of \(Q_{mx}\) differs from the corresponding original margin by at most the test function's modulus at \(\delta_m\).  Hence the within-arm margins of \(Q_x\) are \(\mu_{0x}\) and \(\mu_{1x}\).

To pass (15) to the limit without a boundary assumption, use the closed relation \(\mathcal R_D\) from (1).  The support in (15) lies within distance \(\delta_m\) of \(\mathcal R_D\).  Therefore, for the functions \(g_k\) used above,
\[
 \int g_k\,dQ_{mx}\leq k\delta_m.
\]
Weak convergence first gives \(\int g_k\,dQ_x=0\) for every \(k\), and then \(Q_x(\mathcal R_D)=1\).  Thus \(Q_x\in\mathcal Q_x\), and the surface \(q\) induced by its \((D(0),R(1))\)-projection belongs to \(K\).

It remains to identify the surface limit.  Across adjacent knots, a grid rectangle changes by no more than one death-bin mass plus one readmission-bin mass.  Each such mass is the mass of the corresponding original marginal interval and is at most \(M\) times that interval's width by (3).  The identical assertion holds for \(b_x\) and \(S\).  Since every interpolation vertex belongs to the domain where \(S\geq\eta\), the quotient calculation (8) gives all interpolated grid TV surfaces a common modulus depending only on \((M,\eta)\); (9) gives the corresponding RM modulus.  This establishes equicontinuity using only \(M\).

For any fixed \((r,t)\in\mathcal H_\eta\), choose interpolation knots \((r_m,t_m)\to(r,t)\).  Replacing the moving thresholds by \((r,t)\) changes the associated grid rectangle probability by at most the mass of the death and readmission marginal cells meeting the two intervening threshold intervals.  Their total source-interval width is at most \(|r_m-r|+|t_m-t|+4\delta_m\), so (3) bounds the probability change by \(M\{|r_m-r|+|t_m-t|+4\delta_m\}\).  The fixed-threshold rectangle has the null boundary (10), so the continuous-ramp argument used for (11), now applied to (16), gives convergence to its probability under \(Q_x\).  The known terms \(b_x(r_m,t_m)\) and \(S(t_m)\) converge by (3) and (6), and their denominators remain at least \(\eta\).  Therefore the knotwise TV values converge to \(\theta_q(r,t)\); interpolation changes them by a quantity tending to zero.  Exact integration then gives pointwise RM convergence.  A finite-net argument with the common moduli yields
\[
 \|q_m-q\|_\infty\longrightarrow0. \tag{17}
\]
If the reverse directed distance failed to converge to zero, some subsequence would satisfy \(\inf_{q\in K}\|q_m-q\|_\infty\geq\epsilon\) for a fixed \(\epsilon>0\).  Applying (16)--(17) to that subsequence would produce a limit in \(K\), a contradiction.  Hence
\[
 \sup_{q_m\in K_m}\inf_{q\in K}\|q_m-q\|_\infty\longrightarrow0. \tag{18}
\]
Combining (14) and (18) proves
\[
 d_H(K_m,\Theta_I(P))\longrightarrow0. \tag{19}
\]

Finally, for every continuous signed linear functional \(\Lambda\),
\[
 \left|\sup_{q_m\in K_m}\Lambda(q_m)
       -\sup_{q\in K}\Lambda(q)\right|
 \leq \|\Lambda\|\,d_H(K_m,K)\longrightarrow0, \tag{20}
\]
and the same argument applies to lower supports.  When the restriction of \(\Lambda\) to the grid is represented by cell costs \(c\), the chain-flow theorem makes \(h_m(c)\) the unknown-coupling part of this exact grid support, with the identified affine terms added separately; thus these support certificates converge as asserted.  Evaluation of \(\rho_\nu(a,t)\) at a fixed positive-mass cohort is continuous in the uniform norm.  The proved common-extremizer theorem identifies its two sharp endpoints with the integrals of the opposite common TV envelopes, both on each exact grid and in the continuous model.  Equation (19) therefore implies convergence of the sharp fixed-cohort RM integrals.  Every modulus, boundary-null argument, compactness step, and directed grid approximation above uses only the bounded-density constant \(M\); no step uses \(L_{\mathrm{Lip}}\).
%%% FIELD continuous-grid-statement
For fixed identified within-arm margins, \(\Theta_I(P)\) is compact and convex in the uniform norm on \(\mathcal H_\eta\), every continuous signed support functional is attained, and the interpolated exact grid surface sets from nested meshes converge to \(\Theta_I(P)\) in Hausdorff distance. Consequently their support values and sharp fixed-cohort RM integrals converge to the continuous-support values.

Every finite-part coordinate density of \(\mu_{zx}\) is \(L_{\mathrm{Lip}}\)-Lipschitz.
