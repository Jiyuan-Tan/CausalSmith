%%% FIELD proof-retained-edge-sanity-reduction
Take the graph and target data in \(\mathrm{def:retained\mbox{-}edge\mbox{-}sanity\mbox{-}specification}\), and let every endogenous variable have alphabet \(\{0,1\}\).  We first construct one fully specified model.  In both regimes \(j\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
 \kappa _1^{(j)}(1)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \kappa _2^{(j)}(x_2\mid x_1)
 =
 \begin{cases}
  3/4,&x_2=x_1,\\
  1/4,&x_2\ne x_1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \kappa _s^{(j)}(1\mid x_1)=\frac12.
 \tag{1}
\]
For the isolated target vertex, set
\[
 \kappa _3^{(0)}(1)=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \kappa _3^{(1)}(1)=\frac34.
 \tag{2}
\]
Denote the resulting tuple by \(\eta^\dagger\).  Equations (1)--(2) specify kernels for every vertex of \(V\cup S\) in every regime.  They factorize along the edges \(1\to2\) and \(1\to s\), so the factorization in \(\mathrm{ass:markov\mbox{-}factorization}\) holds.  The graph is finite and acyclic, which verifies \(\mathrm{ass:basal\mbox{-}dag}\); the selection vertex \(s\) is childless, which verifies \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}structure}\); and \(\operatorname{pa}_{G_0}(s)=\{1\}\subseteq V\), which verifies \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}parent\mbox{-}domain}\).  The kernels may be generated from mutually independent uniform exogenous variables, one for each of \(1,2,3,s\).  Thus no latent variable is a common cause of two vertices in \(V\), as required by \(\mathrm{ass:causal\mbox{-}sufficiency}\).  Generate the basal selection variable from \(X_1\) before selecting the regime-specific kernel in (2); this gives the temporal ordering in \(\mathrm{ass:pretreatment\mbox{-}selection}\).  Since \(T_1=\{3\}\), (1) also shows
\[
 \kappa_v^{(1)}=\kappa_v^{(0)}
 \quad\text{for every }v\in V\setminus T_1=\{1,2\},
 \tag{3}
\]
which is \(\mathrm{ass:soft\mbox{-}intervention}\).  Finally, (1) gives
\[
 \Pr_{\eta^\dagger}(S_s^*=1)
 =\sum_{x_1\in\{0,1\}}\frac12\Pr(X_1=x_1)
 =\frac12>0,
 \tag{4}
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}event\mbox{-}positivity}\) holds.

It remains to verify rather than assume pooled twin faithfulness.  Because the selection probability in (1) is constant in \(x_1\), for each \(j\in\{0,1\}\) and every \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\),
\[
 \Pr(X=x\mid S_s^*=1,\text{ regime }j)
 =\frac{\Pr(X=x, S_s^*=1\mid\text{ regime }j)}
        {\Pr(S_s^*=1\mid\text{ regime }j)}
 =\Pr(X=x\mid\text{ regime }j).
 \tag{5}
\]
Write \(p_{12}\) for the joint mass function of \((X_1,X_2)\), and write \(p_j\) for the regime-\(j\) mass function of \(X_3\).  Equations (1), (2), and (5) imply
\[
 P_{\eta^\dagger,1}^{\mathrm{pool}}(x_1,x_2,x_3,z)
 =p_{12}(x_1,x_2)q(z,x_3),
 \qquad
 q(z,x_3)=\frac12p_z(x_3).
 \tag{6}
\]
With rows and columns ordered by \(0,1\), the two component tables in (6) are
\[
 \bigl(p_{12}(x_1,x_2)\bigr)_{x_1,x_2}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}3/8&1/8\\[2pt]1/8&3/8\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \bigl(q(z,x_3)\bigr)_{z,x_3}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}3/8&1/8\\[2pt]1/8&3/8\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{7}
\]
In the twin graph for this specification, the unchanged basal edge gives \(1\to2\), and the change of the kernel at the sole target gives \(\zeta_1\to3\).  The conditioned selection leaf \(s\) has only the parent \(1\), so eliminating it creates no additional adjacency.  Vertex \(3\) is in a different basal component from \(\{1,2,s\}\), and no other target or basal edge exists.  Hence direct application of the latent-projection definition of \(M_1\) gives
\[
 E\bigl(M_1(\eta^\dagger)\bigr)
 =\{1\to2,\ \zeta_1\to3\}.
 \tag{8}
\]

We check faithfulness to (8) for arbitrary conditioning sets.  Let \(C_1=\{1,2\}\) and \(C_2=\{\zeta_1,3\}\).  The product in (6) makes \(X_{C_1}\) independent of \(X_{C_2}\), and all cells in (7) are positive.  Moreover, each table in (7) has determinant
\[
 \frac{3}{8}\frac{3}{8}-\frac{1}{8}\frac{1}{8}
 =\frac18\ne0.
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus the two endpoints within either \(C_i\) are dependent; indeed, for a positive binary table independence would give the cross-product equality whose left side minus right side is the determinant in (9).

Let \(R_1,R_2,W\subseteq U_1\) be pairwise disjoint.  In the graph (8), \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) are \(m\)-separated by \(W\) exactly when no component \(C_i\) has one endpoint in \(R_1\) and its other endpoint in \(R_2\), because the only paths are the two one-edge paths.  If no component is split in this way, conditioning and marginalizing the two independent factors in (6) preserves a product between the coordinates in \(R_1\) and those in \(R_2\); hence
\[
 X_{R_1}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W.
 \tag{10}
\]
Conversely, suppose a component \(C_i=\{a,b\}\) has \(a\in R_1\) and \(b\in R_2\).  Disjointness gives \(a,b\notin W\).  Conditional on every positive-probability value of \(X_W\), the law of \((X_a,X_b)\) is its table in (7), because \(W\) can contain only vertices from the other independent component.  By (9), \(X_a\) and \(X_b\) are then dependent.  Conditional independence of \(X_{R_1}\) and \(X_{R_2}\) would imply conditional independence of these coordinate projections, a contradiction.  This proves the converse to (10), and therefore verifies \(\mathrm{ass:twin\mbox{-}faithfulness}\) for the only comparison \(k=1\).

All nine conditions listed in \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) have now been checked.  Consequently
\[
 \eta^\dagger\in\mathfrak M_{3,1}^{\mathrm{Dai}},
 \tag{11}
\]
and (1)--(2) show explicitly that this is a finite-alphabet member with graph and target data \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{san}}\).

We next establish the claimed graphical conclusions for every faithful model \(\widetilde\eta\in\mathfrak M_{3,1}^{\mathrm{Dai}}\) having these same graph and target data.  They do not depend on the particular numerical kernels above.  Since \(\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\widetilde\eta)}(S)=\{1\}\), and the inclusive ancestral set of \(1\) in the specified basal DAG is \(\{1\}\), the definition of \(A\) gives
\[
 A(\widetilde\eta)
 =\operatorname{An}_{G_0(\widetilde\eta)}(\{1\})\cap V
 =\{1\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The target \(3\) is isolated in the basal DAG and hence has no affected observed vertex other than itself.  Therefore \(F_1(\widetilde\eta)=\{3\}\), and the indicator-neighbor identity in \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Z_1
 &=T_1(\widetilde\eta)\cup
   \bigl((F_1(\widetilde\eta)\setminus T_1(\widetilde\eta))
          \cap A(\widetilde\eta)\bigr)\\
 &=\{3\}\cup(\varnothing\cap\{1\})
 =\{3\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{13}
\]
The basal edge \(1\to2\) creates the observational adjacency \(12\).  By (12), that adjacency meets \(V\setminus A(\widetilde\eta)\) at vertex \(2\); the final observational-orientation clause of \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) therefore gives the basal orientation \(1\to2\) in \(M_0(\widetilde\eta)\).  The retained-directed-edge clause of the same cited lemma then gives
\[
 1\to2\quad\text{in }M_1(\widetilde\eta).
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus the endpoint at \(2\) on this regime adjacency is \(\mathtt{arrow}\), whereas (13) says \(2\notin Z_1\).  The edge \(12\) belongs to the observational skeleton \(H\), so it is retained rather than intervention-created.  Hence an equivalence between an arrowhead at an endpoint \(u\) and membership \(u\in Z_k\) is false if retained edges \(uv\in H\) are admitted: (13)--(14), with \(u=2\), are an explicit counterexample.  Accordingly, the corrected endpoint frontier can apply that target-membership equivalence only to intervention-created adjacencies \(uv\notin H\), and it makes no such equivalence for retained adjacencies \(uv\in H\), as claimed.  \(\square\)
