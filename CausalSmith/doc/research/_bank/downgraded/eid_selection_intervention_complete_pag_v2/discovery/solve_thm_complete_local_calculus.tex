%%% FIELD prop-exact-saturation-statement
Fix a finite list \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathrm{SI}}\) whose rule schemata are independent of \(D\) and \(K\). Suppose every rule premise and every path predicate is uniformly decidable in time polynomial in \(D\), \(K\), and the encoding length of \(\mathcal O\), and every strict conclusion monotonically replaces at least one reported circle endpoint by a tail or an arrowhead, with no endpoint subsequently changed. Then saturation computes \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\) in polynomial time and makes at most
\[
 (K+1)D(D-1)
\]
strict reported-endpoint updates. The observational skeleton and unshielded colliders on \(U_0=V\), and the complete skeletons and unshielded-collider families of the faithful pooled selected-law signatures on \(U_k\), \(k\in[K]\), including all indicator adjacencies and indicator-involving unshielded colliders, remain immutable inputs. If in addition \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)\), the polynomial-time output is the canonical SI-PAG and the procedure does not enumerate \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\).
%%% FIELD prop-exact-saturation-proof
Let \(L\) be the encoding length of the immutable oracle input \(\mathcal O\). In one regime there are at most \(\binom D2\) observed--observed adjacencies, and each such adjacency has two reported endpoint positions. There are \(K+1\) regimes. Therefore the number \(N_{\mathrm{end}}\) of reported endpoint positions obeys
\[
 N_{\mathrm{end}}
 \le (K+1)\,2\binom D2
 =(K+1)D(D-1).
\tag{1}
\]
This count includes no endpoint at an intervention indicator: by the endpoint-domain convention, regime \(0\) reports endpoints on \(U_0=V\), while every regime \(k\in[K]\) reports only observed--observed endpoints from \(U_k=V\cup\{\zeta_k\}\).

By the hypothesis on rule conclusions, every strict endpoint update replaces one circle by one element of \(\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\), and monotonicity prevents a second strict update at that endpoint. Charging each strict endpoint update to its unique changed position and using (1) gives
\[
 N_{\mathrm{upd}}
 \le N_{\mathrm{end}}
 \le (K+1)D(D-1).
\tag{2}
\]

It remains to bound the work used to find these updates. Because \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\) is fixed and finite and each schema has finitely many typed vertex and regime placeholders, there are constants \(c,q,r\in\mathbb N_0\), depending only on the rule list, such that one exhaustive scan has at most
\[
 cD^q(K+1)^r
\tag{3}
\]
instantiated rule premises. The uniform premise-decider hypothesis and finiteness of the list provide a polynomial \(p\) for which each instantiated premise, including each path predicate occurring in it, is decided in at most \(p(D,K,L)\) operations. Hence one exhaustive scan costs at most
\[
 cD^q(K+1)^r p(D,K,L).
\tag{4}
\]

Use the deterministic procedure that scans instances in a fixed order, applies the first instance having a strict conclusion, restarts the scan, and stops after a scan with no strict conclusion. Every nonterminal scan causes at least one strict endpoint update, so (2) bounds the number of scans, including the final scan, by \((K+1)D(D-1)+1\). Combining this fact with (4) gives the polynomial bound
\[
 \bigl((K+1)D(D-1)+1\bigr)cD^q(K+1)^r p(D,K,L).
\tag{5}
\]

The initialization is the one in the definition of \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}\). Rule conclusions refine only reported observed endpoints, so the skeleton and unshielded-collider predicates supplied by the complete signatures on every \(U_k\), including predicates involving \(\zeta_k\), are never modified. At termination the state is closed under every rule instance. To see that it is the least closed extension, let \(Q\) be any closed state extending the same initialization. Induct on the performed strict rule applications. The initialization is contained in \(Q\). If the current state is contained in \(Q\), monotonicity of the selected rule carries its satisfied premise to \(Q\), and closedness of \(Q\) supplies the rule's conclusion. Thus the updated state is also contained in \(Q\). The terminal state is consequently contained in every closed extension, and so equals \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\) by the definition of the least monotone closure.

Finally, under the additional equality \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)\), substitution identifies the computed closure with the canonical endpoint-invariance SI-PAG. The described procedure reads only \(\mathcal O\), the current endpoint marks, and the rule predicates; it never queries or enumerates \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\). This proves the proposition.
%%% FIELD thm-conditional-completeness-statement
Let \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathrm{SI}}\) be a finite candidate rule list whose schemata are independent of \(D\) and \(K\), and fix a compatible full signature \(\mathcal O\). Assume the following three conditions. First, for every \(\xi\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and every graph \(B\) admissible for the graphical replacement lemma, \(\operatorname{Real}(\xi,B)\ne\varnothing\). Second, the closure is sound: if \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\) has a definite mark \(m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\) at a reported endpoint \(e\), then \(\mu_\xi(e)=m\) for every \(\xi\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\). Third, for every endpoint \(e\) left circular by the closure and every \(m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\), there are \(\xi_{e,m}\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and a graph \(B_{e,m}\) admissible for the graphical replacement lemma such that every \(\widetilde\xi\in\operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,m},B_{e,m})\) satisfies \(\mu_{\widetilde\xi}(e)=m\). The rule premises use neither latent \(A(\xi)\), a compatible-model or realizability query, nor a global model enumerator. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)
\]
on all reported observed endpoints. If, additionally, every rule premise and path predicate is uniformly decidable in time polynomial in \(D\), \(K\), and the encoding length of \(\mathcal O\), then saturation is polynomial and makes at most \((K+1)D(D-1)\) strict reported-endpoint updates. This is a conditional completeness reduction: it asserts neither nonempty faithful realization for the replacement graphs nor the existence of a finite rule list satisfying the observable unknown-\(A\) extension conditions.
%%% FIELD thm-conditional-completeness-proof
Fix an arbitrary reported endpoint \(e\in\mathcal E(\mathcal O)\). The closure assigns either a definite mark or a circle at \(e\).

Suppose first that
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)(e)
 =m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The soundness hypothesis applied to (1) yields
\[
 \mu_\xi(e)=m
 \qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O).
\tag{2}
\]
By the definition of the canonical endpoint-invariance SI-PAG, (2) is precisely the condition implying
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)(e)=m.
\tag{3}
\]

Suppose instead that
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)(e)=\mathtt{circle}.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply the opposing-construction hypothesis to (4), once with \(m=\mathtt{tail}\) and once with \(m=\mathtt{arrow}\). It supplies compatible bases \(\xi_{e,\mathtt{tail}},\xi_{e,\mathtt{arrow}}\) and admissible graphs \(B_{e,\mathtt{tail}},B_{e,\mathtt{arrow}}\). The nonempty-realization hypothesis gives
\[
 \operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,\mathtt{tail}},B_{e,\mathtt{tail}})\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,\mathtt{arrow}},B_{e,\mathtt{arrow}})\ne\varnothing.
\tag{5}
\]
Choose respective members \(\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{tail}}\) and \(\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{arrow}}\) from the two sets in (5). The replacement-preservation lemma implies
\[
 \widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{tail}},\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{arrow}}
 \in\mathcal C(\mathcal O).
\tag{6}
\]
The opposing-construction hypothesis, which applies to every member of each realization set, also gives
\[
 \mu_{\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{tail}}}(e)=\mathtt{tail},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{arrow}}}(e)=\mathtt{arrow}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the endpoint mark at \(e\) is not invariant on \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\). The circle clause in the definition of the canonical SI-PAG and (7) imply
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)(e)=\mathtt{circle}.
\tag{8}
\]

Equations (3) and (8) show equality of the two endpoint marks for the arbitrary \(e\). Both partial graphs have the same reported endpoint domain and the same skeleton fixed by \(\mathcal O\); hence endpointwise equality proves
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O).
\tag{9}
\]

Under the additional uniform polynomial-time premise-decider condition, the exact conditional polynomial-saturation proposition applies to the fixed finite list \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\). It yields polynomial running time and at most \((K+1)D(D-1)\) strict reported-endpoint updates. The proof of (9) used nonempty faithful realization and the observable unknown-\(A\) extension properties only as explicit hypotheses; it did not establish either hypothesis or construct the rule list. This proves the theorem.
