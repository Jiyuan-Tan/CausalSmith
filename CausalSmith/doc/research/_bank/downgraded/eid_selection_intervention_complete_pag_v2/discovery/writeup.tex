\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}selection\_\allowbreak{}intervention\_\allowbreak{}complete\_\allowbreak{}pag}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Selection before perturbation couples every pooled regime through one basal selected DAG and one shared-response twin SCM. The proved structural result is the exact fixed-\(A\) graphical identity \(\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}=\mathcal G_A\). At law level, the faithful mark set is nonempty and contained in that graphical set, so singleton graphical sets give equality automatically; two-mark faithful witnesses require nonempty realization fibers. On any supplied directly coupled nonempty open rational parameter family, atomic nonzero determinant certificates imply simultaneous pooled faithfulness by a finite exceptional-set argument. Constructing that parameter family, proving the atomic certificates, finding a finite observable unknown-\(A\) extension, establishing canonical endpoint equality, and obtaining simultaneous separation-margin confidence SI-PAGs remain open. The achieved result is therefore below a complete-calculus result. Neither faithful realization nor the unknown-\(A\) extension follows from graphical inclusion.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Dai et al.'s graphical endpoint semantics do not furnish two faithful representatives from one basal selected DAG and one coupled shared-response twin SCM. The law-level gap has two parts: for every admissible replacement, construct a directly coupled nonempty open rational parameter family satisfying all model conditions except possibly pooled twin faithfulness, and prove a nonzero cleared determinant for every \(m\)-connected query on that family. Separately, no finite observable unknown-\(A\) extension theorem synchronizes the common exterior completion across regimes. The parameter construction, atomic certificates, finite rule list, canonical equality, and separation-margin confidence result all remain open.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note delivers the exact fixed-\(A\) structural characterization \(\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}=\mathcal G_A\), then cleanly separates it from the nonempty faithful inclusion and from the open unknown-\(A\) problem. Singleton graphical mark sets identify the faithful endpoint, whereas a two-mark graphical set reaches law-level equality only when both required realization fibers are nonempty. This is a technical foundation below a complete-calculus result.

\paragraph{Fill.} Explicit empty-graph and signature-monotone-path replacements establish the two graphical marks while preserving the full regime signatures. The faithful-model mark set is nonempty and lies inside the exact graphical set, giving automatic equality in singleton cases and only a conditional equality in the two-mark case. For a supplied directly coupled rational parameterization, the determinant proof shows that atomic nontriviality is sufficient and necessary for a common simultaneously faithful parameter. If faithful realization and a finite observable unknown-\(A\) extension are later established, the existing conditional theorem yields canonical endpoint equality and at most \((K+1)D(D-1)\) strict reported-endpoint updates. Neither antecedent is a consequence of graphical inclusion, and no simultaneous confidence SI-PAG theorem is proved.

\section{Related work}

Dai et al.'s Lemmas 3--6 provide the graphical twin-MAG interface for causally sufficient, childless pre-treatment selection, while their Theorem 3 establishes CDIS soundness and their discussion leaves completeness uncertain. The present note uses a finite-alphabet, positive-selection, pooled-law-faithful shared-response subclass of those graphical models and does not claim equality with Dai et al.'s full law class. Zhang's Sections 3--4 and Theorem 2 establish arrowhead and tail completeness for a single observational PAG, but not simultaneous common-basal pooled regimes. Ali, Richardson, and Spirtes provide single-MAG equivalence invariants that motivate full-signature preservation, not faithful common-basal realization. The public Zhou--Kocaoglu abstract concerns known-target completion with enumeration, which is excluded here as a headline calculus. Wang, Du, and Zhou inform computational context; the present computable statement is only the conditional bound of \((K+1)D(D-1)\) strict endpoint updates for a supplied fixed rule list. Jaber et al. treat unknown targets without selection, and Luo et al. concern a distinct post-treatment-selection setting. Relative to these comparators, the result is an exact fixed-\(A\) graphical theorem and a conditional law-level implication, not completeness.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:basal-dag]\label{ass:basal-dag}
\(G_0(\eta)\) is a finite directed acyclic graph on the disjoint union \(V\cup S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite basal DAG, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:causal-sufficiency]\label{ass:causal-sufficiency}
No latent variable is a common cause of two vertices in \(V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Causal sufficiency among measured variables, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-structure]\label{ass:selection-structure}
Every node in \(S\) is childless in \(G_0(\eta)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Childless selection nodes, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-parent-domain]\label{ass:selection-parent-domain}
For every \(s\in S\), \(\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\eta)}(s)\subseteq V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Observed-parent selection structure, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:markov-factorization]\label{ass:markov-factorization}
For every \(\eta\) there exist mutually independent possibly vector-valued exogenous variables \((U_v)_{v\in V\mathbin{\dot\cup}S_\eta}\) on standard-Borel spaces with one product law and the same realized vector used in every world, measurable responses \(f_v^{(j)}\) and basal selection responses \(f_s^*\), satisfying \(X_v^{(j)}=f_v^{(j)}(X_{\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\eta)}(v)\cap V}^{(j)},U_v)\), \(S_{\eta,s}^*=f_s^*(X_{\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\eta)}(s)}^{(0)},U_s)\), \(f_v^{(k)}=f_v^{(0)}\) for \(v\notin T_k(\eta)\), \(\kappa_v^{(j)}(\eta)\) equal to the observed-node structural kernels induced by these responses, every regime-indexed selection kernel \(\kappa_s^{(j)}(\eta)\) equal to the single basal kernel \(\kappa_s^*\) induced by \(f_s^*\), and \(P_\eta^{(j)}=\mathcal L_\eta((X_v^{(j)})_{v\in V}\mid S_\eta^*=\mathbf1)\) for \(j=0,\ldots,K\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Shared-exogenous twin-SCM Markov representation, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-event-positivity]\label{ass:selection-event-positivity}
\(\Pr_{\eta}(S^*=\mathbf 1)>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive selection event, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pretreatment-selection]\label{ass:pretreatment-selection}
The event determined by \(S\) is generated in the basal world before every regime indexed by \(T(\eta)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Common pre-treatment selection, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:soft-intervention]\label{ass:soft-intervention}
For every \(k\in[K]\) and \(v\in V\setminus T_k(\eta)\), \(\kappa_v^{(k)}(\eta)=\kappa_v^{(0)}(\eta)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unknown-target soft-intervention semantics, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:twin-faithfulness]\label{ass:twin-faithfulness}
For every comparison \(k\in[K]\), the pooled selected law \(P_{\eta,k}^{\mathrm{pool}}(dx,dz)=\tfrac12P_{\eta}^{(0)}(dx)\delta_0(dz)+\tfrac12P_{\eta}^{(k)}(dx)\delta_1(dz)\) on \(X\cup\{\zeta_k\}\) is faithful to \(M_k(\eta)\): for every disjoint vertex sets \(R_1,R_2,W\subseteq U_k\), \(X_{R_1}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W\) under \(P_{\eta,k}^{\mathrm{pool}}\) if and only if \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) are \(m\)-separated by \(W\) in \(M_k(\eta)\), with \(X_{\zeta_k}:=\zeta_k\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Pooled twin-graph faithfulness, \cite{DaiEtAl2025SelectionIntervention}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:graphical-replacement-preservation]\label{lem:graphical-replacement-preservation}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\). If \(B\) is acyclic, every edge of \(B\) lies in \(H[A(\eta)]\), \(B\) creates no unshielded collider whose endpoints are nonadjacent in \(H\), and every \(x\to y\in B\) satisfies \(\sigma(x)\subseteq\sigma(y)\), then the basal DAG \(\widetilde G_0\) in \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{An}_{\widetilde G_0}(\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde G_0}(S))\cap V=A(\eta)\). For every \(k=0,\ldots,K\), the twin graph determined by \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)\) has a latent-projection MAG on \(U_k\) with exactly the adjacency and unshielded-collider signature of \(M_k(\eta)\), including every adjacency and every unshielded collider involving \(\zeta_k\). For \(k\in[K]\), its target and descendant data realize \(Z_k=\widetilde T_k\cup((\operatorname{de}_{\widetilde G_0}(\widetilde T_k)\cap V\setminus\widetilde T_k)\cap A(\eta))\), with the two terms representing direct targets and indirectly affected ancestrally selected vertices separately.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(A_0=A(\eta)\), \(W=V\setminus A_0\), and let \(\widetilde S\), \(\widetilde G_0\), and \((\widetilde T_k)_{k=1}^K\) be the three components of \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)\).  Membership of \(\eta\) in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\), by \(\mathrm{def:compatibility\mbox{-}fiber}\), implies membership in \(\mathfrak M_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai}}\).  Hence \(G_0(\eta)\) is acyclic by \(\mathrm{ass:basal\mbox{-}dag}\), its selection vertices are childless by \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}structure}\), and their parents are observed by \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}parent\mbox{-}domain}\).

No basal edge can point from \(W\) into \(A_0\).  Indeed, if \(w\to a\) with \(w\in W\) and \(a\in A_0\), the definition of \(A_0\) supplies a directed path from \(a\) to a parent of an old selection vertex.  Prepending \(w\to a\) would put \(w\) in \(A_0\), a contradiction.  This no-entry property persists in \(\widetilde G_0\), because the only changed observed--observed edges have both endpoints in \(A_0\).  A directed cycle in the observed part of \(\widetilde G_0\) therefore cannot cross from \(W\) into \(A_0\): a cycle in \(W\) would be a cycle of \(G_0(\eta)\), and a cycle in \(A_0\) would be a cycle of \(B\).  Both are impossible.  Every new selection vertex is childless, so no such vertex lies on a directed cycle.  Thus \(\widetilde G_0\) is acyclic.

The fresh selection family is finite because \(A_0\) and \(H[A_0]\) are finite.  Every \(a\in A_0\) directly parents the fresh singleton selection vertex \(s_a\); hence
\[
 A_0\subseteq
 \operatorname{An}_{\widetilde G_0}
 \bigl(\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde G_0}(\widetilde S)\bigr)\cap V.
\]
Conversely, every parent of an old or fresh selection vertex is in \(A_0\).  The no-entry property shows that no element of \(W\) can reach any such parent.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{An}_{\widetilde G_0}
 \bigl(\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde G_0}(\widetilde S)\bigr)\cap V
 =A_0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here and below, the selection set appearing in a property of the replacement graph is \(\widetilde S\).

We next identify its observational projection using \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\).  If a pair is not wholly contained in \(A_0\), its basal edge and every old selection-generating configuration are unchanged, while no fresh selection vertex has a parent in \(W\).  If \(x,y\in A_0\) and \(xy\in H[A_0]\), the fresh vertex \(s_{xy}\) has parents \(x,y\), so the replacement observational projection contains \(xy\).  Conversely, if \(xy\notin H[A_0]\), then \(xy\notin E(B)\) and no fresh selection vertex has both \(x,y\) as parents.  A new observed common-child configuration in \(B\) could generate \(xy\) only through a collider \(x\to c\leftarrow y\) ancestral to selection; it would be an unshielded collider with endpoints nonadjacent in \(H\), contrary to the hypothesis on \(B\).  Thus the retained old part can reproduce only an adjacency already in \(H\), and
\[
 \operatorname{skel}(\widetilde M_0)=H,
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(\widetilde M_0\) denotes the observational latent projection of the replacement specification.  By (1) and the observational endpoint clause of the cited interface, every endpoint on an adjacency inside \(A_0\) is a tail.  An observational adjacency meeting \(W\) cannot be selection-generated, since that would make its endpoint in \(W\) ancestral to selection; it is a retained basal edge with its original basal orientation.  Consequently the entire observational endpoint pattern, and hence every observational unshielded collider, agrees with that of \(M_0(\eta)\).

Fix \(k\in[K]\) and put
\[
 R_k:=Z_k\cap A_0.
\]
The indicator-neighbor identity in \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\), together with \(T_k(\eta)\subseteq F_k(\eta)\), gives
\[
 R_k=F_k(\eta)\cap A_0.
 \tag{3}
\]
If \(x\to y\in B\) and \(x\in R_k\), then \(k\in\sigma(x)\subseteq\sigma(y)\), so \(y\in R_k\).  Thus \(R_k\) is descendant-closed in \(B\).  Since \(B\) is a finite DAG, every element of \(R_k\) descends in \(B\) from an element of \(R_k\) that is not a proper descendant of another element of \(R_k\).  No retained target in \(W\) can reach \(A_0\), by the no-entry property.  The definition of \(\widetilde T_k\) therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{de}_{\widetilde G_0}(\widetilde T_k)\cap A_0=R_k.
 \tag{4}
\]

The affected set is unchanged on \(W\) as well.  For an old target-to-\(w\) path with \(w\in W\), a target in \(W\) has a path that never enters \(A_0\), hence a wholly retained path.  A path from a target in \(A_0\) has a last vertex \(r\in A_0\); this vertex lies in \(R_k\), equation (4) supplies a replacement target-to-\(r\) prefix, and the suffix after \(r\) is retained.  This proves the old-to-new inclusion.  Conversely, a new target-to-\(w\) path either begins at a retained target in \(W\), or has a last vertex \(r\in A_0\) before leaving \(A_0\).  In the latter case \(r\in R_k\), and (3) supplies an old target-to-\(r\) prefix; the remaining suffix is retained.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{de}_{\widetilde G_0}(\widetilde T_k)\cap V=F_k(\eta).
 \tag{5}
\]
On \(W\), indicator neighbors are exactly the retained direct targets; on \(A_0\), (4) and the indicator formula give exactly \(R_k\).  Therefore
\[
 \widetilde T_k\cup
 \left(\left(
 \operatorname{de}_{\widetilde G_0}(\widetilde T_k)\cap V
 \right)\setminus\widetilde T_k\right)\cap A_0
 =Z_k.
 \tag{6}
\]
The first term and the second term in (6) are, respectively, the direct-target and indirectly affected ancestrally selected contributions; they have not been conflated.

The regime-adjacency clause of \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) depends only on \(H\), \(A_0\), and \(F_k\cap A_0=R_k\).  Equations (1)--(5) therefore give equality of the observed regime-\(k\) skeletons, while (6) gives equality of all adjacencies incident to \(\zeta_k\).

It remains to compare unshielded colliders.  Every observed--observed regime adjacency incident to \(W\) is a retained observational directed edge, because every intervention-created observed adjacency has both endpoints in \(A_0\); its endpoint marks are unchanged.  Every indicator adjacency incident to \(W\) is likewise preserved by (6) and is directed out of \(\zeta_k\).  Thus every unshielded-collider status centered in \(W\) is unchanged.  If \(c\in A_0\), every observed edge between \(c\) and \(W\) has a tail at \(c\), by the no-entry property, so such an edge cannot participate in a collider centered at \(c\).  If \(c\notin R_k\), the endpoint rule in the cited interface gives a tail at \(c\) on every regime edge joining \(c\) to another vertex of \(A_0\), so again no collider is centered at \(c\).  If \(c\in R_k\) and two vertices \(a,b\in A_0\) are regime neighbors of \(c\), concatenate their witnessing paths in \(H[A_0]\).  All internal vertices of the two paths, and the concatenation vertex \(c\), lie in \(R_k\).  Deleting loops gives an \(a\)-to-\(b\) path with internal vertices in \(R_k\), so \(a,b\) are regime adjacent; any collider with these outer vertices is shielded.

Finally consider a triple \(a,c,\zeta_k\) centered at \(c\in A_0\).  Its existence forces \(c\in R_k\).  If \(a\in A_0\cap R_k\), then \(a\) is adjacent to \(\zeta_k\), so the triple is shielded.  If \(a\in A_0\setminus R_k\), then \(a\) is not adjacent to \(\zeta_k\), and the endpoint rule gives \(a\to c\leftarrow\zeta_k\); hence it is an unshielded collider.  This criterion depends only on the already preserved skeleton and \(R_k\).  If \(a\in W\), the edge at \(c\) has a tail and the triple is not a collider.  No collider can be centered at \(\zeta_k\), because every indicator edge has a tail there.  These cases exhaust all centers and establish equality of every unshielded-collider family on \(U_k\).

Since \(k\) was arbitrary, (1), (2), (6), and the collider analysis prove all asserted properties on \(U_0,\ldots,U_K\).  The argument is purely graphical and uses no division, rank, support, boundary, or limiting assertion.  \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma isolates the graph-theoretic obligation: the replacement specification preserves the full oracle signature before any probability law is chosen. \textit{Gap.} Dai et al., Lemmas 4--6 on pp. 6--7, give the intervention-created adjacency, indicator-neighbor, and endpoint semantics used here, but do not give a synchronized common-basal graph replacement preserving every indicator-involving signature constraint. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the graphical half of each opposing selected-endpoint witness used by the complete calculus.

\begin{openendedquestion}[lem:simultaneous-faithful-realization]\label{lem:simultaneous-faithful-realization}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and an admissible \(B\) in the sense of the graphical replacement lemma. Does the replacement graphical specification admit finite-alphabet shared-response-function kernels with positive selection probability and non-target kernel stability such that every pooled selected law \(P_{\widetilde\eta,k}^{\mathrm{pool}}\), \(k\in[K]\), is faithful to its replacement MAG? For any supplied finite-dimensional rational parameterization satisfying the Markov direction, this existence question reduces to atomic determinant nontriviality: for every \(k\in[K]\) and every conditional-independence query that is \(m\)-connected in the replacement MAG, at least one cleared conditional-independence determinant must be a nonzero polynomial on the equality-constrained shared-response-function parameter space. The finite-union exceptional-set argument is valid once these atomic certificates are available; the atomic certificates themselves remain open.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This open question separates law-level faithful promotion from the exact fixed-\(A\) graphical identity. \textit{Gap.} For arbitrary admissible replacements, both a directly coupled nonempty open rational parameterization and one nonzero determinant certificate per \(m\)-connected query remain unproved. \textit{Consumer.} An affirmative answer would promote the two graphical endpoint constructions to faithful law-level witnesses, but would not itself provide the finite observable unknown-\(A\) extension needed for canonical equality.

\begin{lemma}[lem:replacement-preservation]\label{lem:replacement-preservation}
For every \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\), every admissible \(B\), and every \(\widetilde\eta\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B)\), one has \(A(\widetilde\eta)=A(\eta)\), \(\mathcal O(\widetilde\eta)=\mathcal O(\eta)=\mathcal O\), and hence \(\widetilde\eta\in\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta)\). Every adjacency and unshielded collider on every full domain \(U_k\), including all \(\zeta_k\)-involving instances, is preserved, and for every \(k\in[K]\),
\[
 Z_k=T_k(\widetilde\eta)\cup
 \bigl((F_k(\widetilde\eta)\setminus T_k(\widetilde\eta))
 \cap A(\widetilde\eta)\bigr).
\]
Consequently, if \(\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B)\ne\varnothing\), then a fully specified faithful replacement witness with all these properties exists. No equality between \(\kappa(\widetilde\eta)\) and \(\kappa(\eta)\) is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\widetilde\eta\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:replacement\mbox{-}realization\mbox{-}fiber}\), if
\[
 \operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)
 =\bigl(\widetilde S,\widetilde G_0,(\widetilde T_k)_{k=1}^K\bigr),
\]
then
\[
 \widetilde\eta\in\mathfrak M_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai}},\qquad
 S_{\widetilde\eta}=\widetilde S,\qquad
 G_0(\widetilde\eta)=\widetilde G_0,
 \qquad
 T_k(\widetilde\eta)=\widetilde T_k\quad(k\in[K]).
 \tag{1}
\]
The admissibility of \(B\) is precisely the graphical hypothesis of \(\mathrm{lem:graphical\mbox{-}replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\).  Applying that lemma to the graph-and-target equalities in (1) gives
\[
 A(\widetilde\eta)=A(\eta)
 \tag{2}
\]
and, for every \(j=0,\ldots,K\),
\[
 \operatorname{skel}(M_j(\widetilde\eta))
 =\operatorname{skel}(M_j(\eta)),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{UC}(M_j(\widetilde\eta))
 =\operatorname{UC}(M_j(\eta))
 \tag{3}
\]
on the complete domain \(U_j\).  In particular, for \(j\geq1\), (3) includes all adjacencies and all unshielded colliders involving \(\zeta_j\).

Membership in \(\mathfrak M_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai}}\) in (1) supplies positive selection probability, so all selected laws used by the signature are defined, and supplies faithfulness of every pooled selected law to its corresponding MAG.  By \(\mathrm{def:oracle\mbox{-}signature}\), equation (3) consequently yields
\[
 \mathcal O(\widetilde\eta)
 =\bigl(\operatorname{skel}(M_j(\widetilde\eta)),
         \operatorname{UC}(M_j(\widetilde\eta))\bigr)_{j=0}^K
 =\mathcal O(\eta)=\mathcal O.
 \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2) and (4), together with \(\mathrm{def:compatibility\mbox{-}fiber}\) and \(\mathrm{def:fixed\mbox{-}a\mbox{-}fiber}\), imply
\[
 \widetilde\eta\in\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta).
 \tag{5}
\]
The target-and-descendant conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:graphical\mbox{-}replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\), translated through (1)--(2), gives for every \(k\in[K]\)
\[
 Z_k=T_k(\widetilde\eta)\cup
 \bigl((F_k(\widetilde\eta)\setminus T_k(\widetilde\eta))
       \cap A(\widetilde\eta)\bigr).
 \tag{6}
\]
This proves the assertion for every member of the realization fiber.  If that fiber is nonempty, choosing one member gives the stated existence conclusion.  The proof uses only equality of graph, selection-set, and target components from (1); it nowhere assumes \(\kappa(\widetilde\eta)=\kappa(\eta)\).  \(\square\)
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proved lemma characterizes every member of a replacement realization fiber and makes existence conditional on nonemptiness. \textit{Gap.} It does not establish realization-fiber nonemptiness. \textit{Consumer.} It transfers any separately supplied faithful realization into the fixed-\(A\) compatibility fiber.

\begin{proposition}[prop:retained-edge-sanity-reduction]\label{prop:retained-edge-sanity-reduction}
There exists a finite-alphabet model \(\eta^\dagger\in\mathfrak M_{3,1}^{\mathrm{Dai}}\) whose graph and target data equal \(\mathsf G_{\mathrm{san}}\). For every such faithful model, \(A(\eta^\dagger)=\{1\}\), \(Z_1=\{3\}\), and Dai et al.'s retained-edge result gives \(1\to2\) in \(M_1(\eta^\dagger)\). Consequently, the endpoint at vertex \(2\) on that retained adjacency is an arrowhead although \(2\notin Z_1\). The corrected endpoint frontier therefore makes its arrowhead equivalence only for intervention-created adjacencies \(uv\notin H\); it makes no target-membership equivalence for retained adjacencies \(uv\in H\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the graph and target data of \(\mathrm{def:retained\mbox{-}edge\mbox{-}sanity\mbox{-}specification}\), and give every endogenous variable alphabet \(\{0,1\}\).  Let \(U_1,U_2,U_3,U_s\) be independent \(\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) variables.  In both regimes \(j\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 X_1^{(j)}=\boldsymbol 1\{U_1\leq 1/2\},
 \qquad
 X_2^{(j)}=
 \begin{cases}
 X_1^{(j)},&U_2\leq3/4,\\
 1-X_1^{(j)},&U_2>3/4,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 S_s^*=\boldsymbol 1\{U_s\leq1/2\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For the isolated target vertex, set
\[
 X_3^{(0)}=\boldsymbol 1\{U_3\leq1/4\},
 \qquad
 X_3^{(1)}=\boldsymbol 1\{U_3\leq3/4\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The response for \(S_s^*\) is allowed to ignore its displayed parent \(X_1^{(0)}\).  Equations (1)--(2) use one product exogenous law and the same realized exogenous vector in both worlds.  Their induced kernels are
\[
 \kappa_1^{(j)}(1)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \kappa_2^{(j)}(x_2\mid x_1)=
 \begin{cases}
 3/4,&x_2=x_1,\\
 1/4,&x_2\ne x_1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \kappa_s^*(1\mid x_1)=\frac12,
 \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 \kappa_3^{(0)}(1)=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \kappa_3^{(1)}(1)=\frac34.
 \tag{4}
\]
Call the resulting tuple \(\eta^\dagger\).  Its graph is the finite acyclic graph with edges \(1\to2\) and \(1\to s\), verifying \(\mathrm{ass:basal\mbox{-}dag}\).  The selection vertex is childless and has the observed parent set \(\{1\}\), verifying \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}structure}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}parent\mbox{-}domain}\).  Independence of the four exogenous variables gives \(\mathrm{ass:causal\mbox{-}sufficiency}\), while (1) generates selection in the basal world before the intervention and hence gives \(\mathrm{ass:pretreatment\mbox{-}selection}\).  Because \(T_1=\{3\}\), equations (3)--(4) give
\[
 \kappa_v^{(1)}=\kappa_v^{(0)}
 \quad\text{for every }v\in V\setminus T_1=\{1,2\},
 \tag{5}
\]
which is \(\mathrm{ass:soft\mbox{-}intervention}\).  The structural equations and induced kernels give \(\mathrm{ass:markov\mbox{-}factorization}\), including its shared-exogenous and shared-non-target-response requirements.  Finally,
\[
 \Pr_{\eta^\dagger}(S_s^*=1)=\frac12>0,
 \tag{6}
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}event\mbox{-}positivity}\) holds.

We verify pooled twin faithfulness explicitly.  Selection is independent of \(X\), so for \(j\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \Pr(X=x\mid S_s^*=1,\text{ regime }j)
 =\Pr(X=x\mid\text{ regime }j).
 \tag{7}
\]
Let \(p_{12}\) be the mass function of \((X_1,X_2)\), and let \(p_j\) be the regime-\(j\) mass function of \(X_3\).  Equations (1)--(4) and (7) yield
\[
 P_{\eta^\dagger,1}^{\mathrm{pool}}(x_1,x_2,x_3,z)
 =p_{12}(x_1,x_2)q(z,x_3),
 \qquad q(z,x_3)=\frac12p_z(x_3).
 \tag{8}
\]
With rows and columns ordered by \(0,1\), both factor tables are
\[
 \bigl(p_{12}(x_1,x_2)\bigr)_{x_1,x_2}
 =
 \bigl(q(z,x_3)\bigr)_{z,x_3}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}3/8&1/8\\[2pt]1/8&3/8\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{9}
\]
The twin MAG has exactly the two edges
\[
 E(M_1(\eta^\dagger))=\{1\to2,\ \zeta_1\to3\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the unchanged basal edge is retained, the sole changed kernel creates \(\zeta_1\to3\), the conditioned leaf \(s\) has only one parent and therefore creates no adjacency, and the two basal components are otherwise disjoint.  This also follows directly from \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\).

Put \(C_1=\{1,2\}\) and \(C_2=\{\zeta_1,3\}\).  The factorization in (8) makes the two vectors \(X_{C_1}\) and \(X_{C_2}\) independent, all cells in (9) are positive, and within each factor the endpoint variables are dependent because
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}3/8&1/8\\[2pt]1/8&3/8\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac18\ne0.
 \tag{11}
\]
For pairwise disjoint \(R_1,R_2,W\subseteq U_1\), the graph in (10) \(m\)-separates \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) given \(W\) exactly when neither two-vertex component \(C_i\) has one endpoint in \(R_1\) and the other in \(R_2\).  If no component is split, conditioning and marginalizing the positive product in (8) leaves a product between the coordinates in \(R_1\) and those in \(R_2\), proving conditional independence.  If a component \(C_i=\{a,b\}\) is split with \(a\in R_1\) and \(b\in R_2\), then disjointness gives \(a,b\notin W\).  Conditional on every value of \(X_W\), the law of \((X_a,X_b)\) remains the table in (9), because \(W\) can contain only variables from the other independent component.  Equation (11) makes \(X_a\) and \(X_b\) dependent.  Conditional independence of the two vectors would imply conditional independence of their coordinate projections, a contradiction.  Thus conditional independence holds exactly for the \(m\)-separations in (10), proving \(\mathrm{ass:twin\mbox{-}faithfulness}\).

All conditions in \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) are now verified, so
\[
 \eta^\dagger\in\mathfrak M_{3,1}^{\mathrm{Dai}}.
 \tag{12}
\]
This proves the required finite-alphabet existence.

Now let \(\widetilde\eta\) be any faithful model with the same graph and target data.  Its only selection parent is \(1\), whose inclusive ancestral set is \(\{1\}\), so
\[
 A(\widetilde\eta)=\{1\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The isolated target \(3\) affects only itself, whence \(F_1(\widetilde\eta)=\{3\}\).  The indicator-neighbor identity in \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) gives
\[
 Z_1
 =\{3\}\cup\bigl((\{3\}\setminus\{3\})\cap\{1\}\bigr)
 =\{3\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
The basal edge gives \(1\to2\) in the observational MAG, and the retained-directed-edge clause of the same interface gives
\[
 1\to2\quad\text{in }M_1(\widetilde\eta).
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus the endpoint at \(2\) is an arrowhead although \(2\notin Z_1\).  Since \(12\in H\), this is a retained rather than intervention-created adjacency.  Equations (14)--(15) are therefore an explicit witness that target-neighborhood membership cannot characterize endpoint marks on retained adjacencies.  Restricting the frontier's target-membership equivalences to \(uv\notin H\) is necessary, as asserted.  \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-a-endpoint-frontier]\label{thm:fixed-a-endpoint-frontier}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\), \(k\in[K]\), and an intervention-created observed adjacency \(uv\), where \(u,v\in A(\eta)\), \(uv\notin H\), and \(uv\) is present in the regime-\(k\) skeleton. Let \(e\) be the endpoint at \(u\) on \(uv\). Then
\[
 \varnothing\ne\mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)
 \subseteq \mathcal G_A(u,v;k).
\]
Thus, if \(u\notin Z_k\), then \(\mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)=\{\mathtt{tail}\}\); if \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_{A(\eta)}(u,v)=0\), then \(\mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)=\{\mathtt{arrow}\}\). If \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_{A(\eta)}(u,v)=1\), the empty graph \(B_0\) on \(A(\eta)\) and the directed-edge graph \(B_P\) of any simple \(u\)-to-\(v\) path in \(L_{A(\eta)}\) are admissible and give two common-basal graphical replacement specifications preserving \(A(\eta)\) and every regime skeleton and unshielded collider on the full domains \(U_j\). The former has an arrowhead and the latter a tail at \(e\). In this two-mark case,
\[
 \operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_0)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_P)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)=\mathcal G_A(u,v;k)
 =\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
\]
Here \(u\in Z_k\) denotes direct targeting or indirect affectedness within \(A(\eta)\), not necessarily \(u\in T_k(\eta)\). Retained adjacencies \(uv\in H\) are outside this intervention-created-edge bracket.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(g=\operatorname{gr}(\eta)\).  Because \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\), the definition of the fixed-\(A\) fiber gives \(\eta\in\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta)\).  Hence
\[
 \mu_\eta(e)\in\mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta),
 \qquad
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)\ne\varnothing.
 \tag{1}
\]
The hypotheses that \(u,v\in A(\eta)\), \(uv\notin H\), and \(uv\) occurs in the regime-\(k\) skeleton are precisely the intervention-created-adjacency hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:exact\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}a\mbox{-}graphical\mbox{-}frontier}\).  That lemma, together with \(g=\operatorname{gr}(\eta)\), yields
\[
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)
 \subseteq
 \mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(e;\mathcal O,g)
 =\mathcal G_A(u,v;k).
 \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) proves the asserted nonempty outer bracket.

Suppose first that \(u\notin Z_k\).  By the three-case definition in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\mbox{-}a\mbox{-}mark\mbox{-}sets}\),
\[
 \mathcal G_A(u,v;k)=\{\mathtt{tail}\}.
\]
A nonempty subset of a singleton is that singleton, so (1)--(2) imply
\[
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)=\{\mathtt{tail}\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Suppose instead that \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_{A(\eta)}(u,v)=0\).  The same definition gives
\[
 \mathcal G_A(u,v;k)=\{\mathtt{arrow}\},
\]
and therefore (1)--(2) give
\[
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)=\{\mathtt{arrow}\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

It remains to establish the conditional conclusion in the reachability case.  Assume
\[
 u\in Z_k,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Reach}_{A(\eta)}(u,v)=1.
 \tag{5}
\]
By the definition of nonempty reachability in \(L_{A(\eta)}\), there is a directed \(u\)-to-\(v\) path.  Since \(A(\eta)\subseteq V\) and \(V\) is finite, deleting repeated directed cycles produces a simple directed path \(P\).  Let \(B_0\) be the empty directed graph on \(A(\eta)\), and let \(B_P\) contain exactly the directed edges of \(P\).  The reachability part of \(\mathrm{lem:exact\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}a\mbox{-}graphical\mbox{-}frontier}\) states that both \(B_0\) and \(B_P\) are admissible and that
\[
 \operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_0),\ 
 \operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_P)
 \in\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g).
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus both replacement specifications have ancestral selected set \(A(\eta)\) and preserve the skeleton and the unshielded-collider set of every regime projection on its full domain \(U_j\), including all instances involving \(\zeta_j\).  The same lemma computes their marks at \(e\) as
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_0)}(e)
   =\mathtt{arrow},
 \qquad
 \mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_P)}(e)
   =\mathtt{tail}.
 \tag{7}
\]
This proves the two asserted common-basal graphical witness statements without promoting either specification to a law-level model.

Now impose the two antecedents in the theorem's displayed implication:
\[
 \operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_0)\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_P)\ne\varnothing.
 \tag{8}
\]
Choose
\[
 \eta_{\mathtt{arrow}}\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_0),
 \qquad
 \eta_{\mathtt{tail}}\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_P).
 \tag{9}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\), each model in (9) is a fully specified faithful member of \(\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta)\).  Its graphical reduct is the corresponding replacement specification, so (7) and the definition of \(\mathcal M_A\) imply
\[
 \{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}
 \subseteq\mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta).
 \tag{10}
\]
Under (5), the third case of \(\mathrm{def:fixed\mbox{-}a\mbox{-}mark\mbox{-}sets}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal G_A(u,v;k)=\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Equations (2), (10), and (11) therefore establish
\[
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)
 =\mathcal G_A(u,v;k)
 =\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
\]

Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) gives
\[
 Z_k=T_k(\eta)\cup
 \bigl((F_k(\eta)\setminus T_k(\eta))\cap A(\eta)\bigr),
\]
so the proof uses \(u\in Z_k\), which allows either direct targeting or indirect affectedness in \(A(\eta)\), and never replaces this condition by \(u\in T_k(\eta)\).  Moreover, \(\mathrm{prop:retained\mbox{-}edge\mbox{-}sanity\mbox{-}reduction}\) exhibits a retained adjacency with an arrowhead endpoint outside \(Z_k\).  Consequently the restriction \(uv\notin H\) is necessary for the stated intervention-created-edge bracket.  This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:dai-visible-selectedness]\label{lem:dai-visible-selectedness}

\begin{proof}

uv\notin E(H),

\tag{1}

\begin{lemma}[lem:visible-selectedness-certificates]\label{lem:visible-selectedness-certificates}
For every \(\eta\in\mathfrak M_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai}}\), with \(\mathcal O=\mathcal O(\eta)\) formed from the faithful pooled selected laws \(P_{\eta,k}^{\mathrm{pool}}\), the entirely observable closure satisfies \(\mathsf C_{\mathrm{sel}}(\mathcal O)\subseteq A(\eta)\). \end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathfrak M_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai}}\), put \(A=A(\eta)\), and let \(\mathcal O=\mathcal O(\eta)\).  For brevity write
\[
C^{(r)}=C^{(r)}(\mathcal O),\qquad 0\le r\le |V|,
\]
for the sets in \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}selectedness\mbox{-}closure}\).  We prove by induction on \(r\) that
\[
C^{(r)}\subseteq A.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(r=0\), every \(q\in C^{(0)}\) is, by the defining seed rule, an endpoint of an observed--observed adjacency absent from \(H\) and present in some intervention-regime skeleton.  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}visible\mbox{-}selectedness}\) to that adjacency gives \(q\in A\).  Thus (3) holds at \(r=0\).

Suppose (3) holds for some \(r<|V|\).  An element of \(C^{(r+1)}\setminus C^{(r)}\) is a vertex \(x\) for which there are nonadjacent vertices \(i,j\in C^{(r)}\) such that
\[
i-x-j
\tag{4}
\]
is an unshielded noncollider in the observational MAG \(M_0(\eta)\).  The induction hypothesis yields
\[
i,j\in A.
\tag{5}
\]
Assume, for contradiction, that \(x\notin A\).

Because \(ix\in E(H)\), the observational-adjacency clause of \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) says that this adjacency is generated either by a basal observed edge or by an observed common child ancestral to selection.  The latter alternative would imply \(x\in A\): indeed, if \(c\) is such a common child, then \(x\to c\) and \(c\) has a directed path to a selection vertex.  By \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}structure}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:selection\mbox{-}parent\mbox{-}domain}\), either \(x\) itself is an observed parent of that selection vertex or the path has an observed penultimate vertex \(p\in\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\eta)}(S_\eta)\); in both cases
\[
x\in\operatorname{An}_{G_0(\eta)}
   \bigl(\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\eta)}(S_\eta)\bigr)\cap V
=A,
\]
contrary to the supposition.  Hence \(ix\) is a basal observed edge.

That basal edge cannot have direction \(x\to i\).  If it did, (5) and the definition of \(A\) would give
\[
x\in\operatorname{An}_{G_0(\eta)}(i)
\subseteq
\operatorname{An}_{G_0(\eta)}
   \bigl(\operatorname{pa}_{G_0(\eta)}(S_\eta)\bigr),
\]
and therefore \(x\in A\), again a contradiction.  Thus its basal direction is \(i\to x\).  Since this observational adjacency meets \(V\setminus A\), the retained-orientation clause of \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) implies that its observational-MAG orientation is also \(i\to x\).  Applying the same argument to \(jx\) gives \(j\to x\).  Together with the nonadjacency of \(i\) and \(j\), this makes (4) the unshielded collider
\[
i\to x\leftarrow j,
\]
contradicting the premise that (4) is a noncollider.  Therefore \(x\in A\), and (3) follows at stage \(r+1\).

Induction gives (3) for every \(0\le r\le |V|\).  Taking the union specified in \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}selectedness\mbox{-}closure}\) yields

=\bigcup_{r=0}^{|V|}C^{(r)}(\mathcal O)
\subseteq A(\eta).

Finally, the seed adjacencies, observational unshielded-noncollider statuses, and recursion are all functions of the full oracle signature \(\mathcal O\) by \(\mathrm{def:oracle\mbox{-}signature}\) and \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}selectedness\mbox{-}closure}\).  Thus the certificate is observable and never queries \(A(\eta)\) or another compatible model in its computation.  As above, the proof is finite and graphical; it uses no positivity denominator, rank condition, or limiting argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:simultaneous-faithfulness-exceptional-set-reduction]\label{lem:simultaneous-faithfulness-exceptional-set-reduction}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and a graph \(B\) admissible for the graphical replacement lemma. Suppose that a nonempty open set \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^r\) directly parameterizes finite-alphabet ambient tuples \(\eta_\theta\) whose selection-set, basal-graph, and target components jointly equal \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)\). Assume that every \(\eta_\theta\) satisfies every member condition in \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) except possibly pooled twin faithfulness. In particular, each tuple is already represented by one product law of mutually independent exogenous variables whose same realized vector is used in every world, measurable response functions shared across regimes at every non-target vertex, one basal selection response generated before intervention, and positive basal selection probability; these coupled-response objects are part of the parameterized tuple and are not reconstructed from regime-local conditional tables. For \(k\in[K]\), let \(p_{\theta,k}\) be the mass function of the pooled selected law \(P_{\eta_\theta,k}^{\mathrm{pool}}\), and let the replacement MAG be the latent projection on \(U_k\) determined by \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)\). Assume that every cell of \(p_{\theta,k}\) is strictly positive and rational in \(\theta\), with all denominators positive on \(\Theta\), and that \(m\)-separation in every replacement MAG implies the corresponding conditional independence under \(p_{\theta,k}\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta\). For every \(k\in[K]\), every triple of pairwise disjoint sets \(R_1,R_2,W\subseteq U_k\), and every cell assignment \(a=(r_1,r_2,w)\), clear positive denominators in
\[
 p_{\theta,k}(r_1,r_2,w)p_{\theta,k}(w)
 -p_{\theta,k}(r_1,w)p_{\theta,k}(r_2,w)
\]
and denote the resulting polynomial by \(q_{k,R_1,R_2,W,a}\). There exists \(\theta^\star\in\Theta\) for which all pooled selected laws are simultaneously faithful to their replacement MAGs if and only if, for every query \((k,R_1,R_2,W)\) that is \(m\)-connected in its replacement MAG, at least one polynomial \(q_{k,R_1,R_2,W,a}\) is not the zero polynomial. Every such simultaneously faithful \(\eta_{\theta^\star}\) belongs to \(\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B)\). Thus the conclusion is conditional on the supplied directly coupled rational parameterization and reduces faithful realization within that family to atomic determinant nontriviality; it constructs neither the parameterization nor the atomic certificates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal Q\) be the collection of quadruples
\[
 Q=(k,R_1,R_2,W),
 \qquad k\in[K],
 \qquad R_1,R_2,W\subseteq U_k,
\]
for which \(R_1,R_2,W\) are pairwise disjoint and \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) are \(m\)-connected given \(W\) in the replacement MAG on \(U_k\).  This collection is finite: \([K]\) and every \(U_k\) are finite.

Fix \(Q=(k,R_1,R_2,W)\in\mathcal Q\) and a joint cell assignment \(a=(r_1,r_2,w)\).  Define
\[
 \Delta_{Q,a}(\theta)
 :=p_{\theta,k}(r_1,r_2,w)p_{\theta,k}(w)
   -p_{\theta,k}(r_1,w)p_{\theta,k}(r_2,w).
 \tag{1}
\]
Here each displayed probability is the appropriate marginal of the pooled mass function from the same coupled-response model \(\eta_\theta\).  Strict positivity of every full cell implies \(p_{\theta,k}(w)>0\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta\) and every \(w\); if \(W=\varnothing\), this marginal equals \(1\).  Consequently, the finite-table definition of conditional independence gives
\[
 X_{R_1}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p_{\theta,k}(r_1,r_2,w)p_{\theta,k}(w)
 =p_{\theta,k}(r_1,w)p_{\theta,k}(r_2,w)
 \quad\text{for every }a.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, division by \(p_{\theta,k}(w)^2\) turns the equality on the right into the usual factorization of the conditional joint mass, and positivity justifies that division.  Thus (1)--(2) imply
\[
 X_{R_1}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta_{Q,a}(\theta)=0\quad\text{for every }a.
 \tag{3}
\]

All full-cell masses are rational in \(\theta\), so their finite sums, and hence all marginals in (1), are rational in \(\theta\).  By the stipulated positivity of all denominators on \(\Theta\), clearing denominators in (1) yields the polynomial named in the statement and a function \(d_{Q,a}:\Theta\to(0,\infty)\) such that
\[
 q_{Q,a}(\theta)=d_{Q,a}(\theta)\Delta_{Q,a}(\theta).
 \tag{4}
\]
It follows that, on \(\Theta\),
\[
 q_{Q,a}(\theta)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta_{Q,a}(\theta)=0.
 \tag{5}
\]

Assume first that for every \(Q\in\mathcal Q\) there is at least one assignment \(a(Q)\) for which \(q_{Q,a(Q)}\) is not the zero polynomial.  Set
\[
 q_*(\theta):=\prod_{Q\in\mathcal Q}q_{Q,a(Q)}(\theta),
 \tag{6}
\]
where the empty product is \(1\).  The ring \(\mathbb R[\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r]\) is an integral domain, so finiteness of \(\mathcal Q\) makes \(q_*\) a nonzero polynomial.

We prove directly the elementary zero-set fact needed here.  A nonzero real polynomial in \(r\) variables cannot vanish on a nonempty open subset of \(\mathbb R^r\).  For \(r=0\) this says that a nonzero constant does not vanish.  For \(r=1\), a nonzero polynomial has at most its degree many distinct roots, by repeated division by \(t-t_0\).  Inductively, write
\[
 h(t_1,\ldots,t_r)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}h_j(t_1,\ldots,t_{r-1})t_r^j.
 \tag{7}
\]
If \(h\) vanished on an open box, then at every point in the box's projection the resulting univariate polynomial in \(t_r\) would vanish on an interval.  Every coefficient \(h_j\) would therefore vanish throughout the projected open box, and the induction hypothesis would make every \(h_j\) the zero polynomial, contradicting \(h\ne0\).  Since every nonempty open subset of Euclidean space contains an open box, the fact follows.

Apply this fact to the nonzero polynomial \(q_*\) and the nonempty open set \(\Theta\).  There is \(\theta^\star\in\Theta\) such that
\[
 q_*(\theta^\star)\ne0.
 \tag{8}
\]
For every \(Q\in\mathcal Q\), equations (6) and (8) give \(q_{Q,a(Q)}(\theta^\star)\ne0\).  Equations (3)--(5) then yield
\[
 X_{R_1}\not\!\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W
 \quad\text{under }P_{\eta_{\theta^\star},k}^{\mathrm{pool}}
 \qquad\text{for every }Q\in\mathcal Q.
 \tag{9}
\]
For every query that is \(m\)-separated in its replacement MAG, the Markov-direction premise gives the corresponding conditional independence.  A query not \(m\)-separated is, by definition, one of the \(m\)-connected queries covered by (9).  Hence, for every \(k\in[K]\) and every pairwise disjoint \(R_1,R_2,W\subseteq U_k\),
\[
 X_{R_1}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R_1\text{ and }R_2\text{ are }m\text{-separated by }W
 \text{ in the replacement MAG}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus all pooled selected laws are simultaneously faithful to their replacement MAGs.

By hypothesis, the directly parameterized tuple \(\eta_{\theta^\star}\) already satisfies every other member condition of \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\): in particular it has one product exogenous law with the same realized exogenous vector in every world, shared response functions at non-target vertices, one pre-intervention basal selection response, and positive basal selection probability.  Equation (10) supplies the only condition that was allowed to be absent, namely pooled twin faithfulness.  Therefore
\[
 \eta_{\theta^\star}\in\mathfrak M_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai}}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The family premise also says that the selection-set, basal-graph, and target components of \(\eta_{\theta^\star}\) jointly equal \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B)\).  Applying \(\mathrm{def:replacement\mbox{-}realization\mbox{-}fiber}\) to (11) consequently gives
\[
 \eta_{\theta^\star}\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B).
 \tag{12}
\]
The cited frozen result \(\mathrm{lem:graphical\mbox{-}replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\) guarantees that this replacement specification has the asserted selected-DAG and regime-signature properties.  It is not used to construct or recouple the shared-response law; that law is supplied by the parameter-family premise.

Conversely, suppose that some \(\theta^\star\in\Theta\) makes every pooled selected law faithful to its replacement MAG.  Fix \(Q\in\mathcal Q\).  Because this query is \(m\)-connected, faithfulness implies
\[
 X_{R_1}\not\!\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{R_2}\mid X_W.
\]
By (3), there is a cell assignment \(a\) for which \(\Delta_{Q,a}(\theta^\star)\ne0\).  Equations (4)--(5) imply
\[
 q_{Q,a}(\theta^\star)\ne0,
 \tag{13}
\]
and therefore \(q_{Q,a}\) is not the zero polynomial.  Since \(Q\) was arbitrary, the required atomic nontriviality holds for every \(m\)-connected query.

Finally, if \(K=0\), there are no pooled laws and \(\mathcal Q=\varnothing\).  The atomic condition and simultaneous-faithfulness condition are both vacuous, (6) gives \(q_*=1\), and any point of the nonempty set \(\Theta\) satisfies all member conditions because pooled twin faithfulness has no instances.  The same component-equality argument then gives (12).  This proves both directions, including the boundary case.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma proves the finite algebraic exceptional-set step for a supplied directly coupled rational shared-response family; the coupling is a premise, not something reconstructed from regime-local tables. \textit{Gap.} It constructs neither the directly coupled nonempty open parameter family nor a nonzero determinant certificate for any \(m\)-connected query. \textit{Consumer.} The faithful-realization question uses the lemma to isolate its two remaining tasks: parameter-family construction and atomic determinant nontriviality.

\begin{lemma}[lem:exact-fixed-a-graphical-frontier]\label{lem:exact-fixed-a-graphical-frontier}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\), \(k\in[K]\), and an intervention-created observed adjacency \(uv\), where \(u,v\in A(\eta)\), \(uv\notin H\), and \(uv\) is present in the regime-\(k\) skeleton.  Let \(e=(k,u\mid uv)\) and put \(g=\operatorname{gr}(\eta)\).  Then
\[
 \varnothing\ne
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)
 \subseteq
 \mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(e;\mathcal O,g)
 =\mathcal G_A(u,v;k).
\]
Consequently, if \(u\notin Z_k\), both the law-level and graphical mark sets equal \(\{\mathtt{tail}\}\), while if \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_{A(\eta)}(u,v)=0\), both equal \(\{\mathtt{arrow}\}\).  If \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_{A(\eta)}(u,v)=1\), the specifications \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_0)\) and \(\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_P)\), where \(B_0\) is empty and \(B_P\) is the directed-edge graph of any simple \(u\)-to-\(v\) path in \(L_{A(\eta)}\), belong to \(\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g)\) and attain respectively \(\mathtt{arrow}\) and \(\mathtt{tail}\) at \(e\).  Hence the graphical equality is unconditional.  In this two-mark case, the further law-level equality
\[
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)
 =\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(e;\mathcal O,g)
 =\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}
\]
follows if \(\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_0)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_P)\ne\varnothing\).  No graphical member is promoted to a faithful law-level member merely from its membership in \(\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}\), and no target-membership equivalence is asserted for retained adjacencies \(uv\in H\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(A=A(\eta)\) and \(g=\operatorname{gr}(\eta)\).  The adjacency, indicator-neighbor, and endpoint clauses of \(\mathrm{lem:dai\mbox{-}twin\mbox{-}mag\mbox{-}interface}\) are graphical consequences of the Dai twin construction; by \(\mathrm{def:graphical\mbox{-}dai\mbox{-}specifications}\), the same construction defines the projections of every member of \(\mathfrak G_{D,K}^{\mathrm{Dai,gr}}\).

Take any \(\widetilde g\in\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g)\), set \(\widetilde A=A_{\mathrm{gr}}(\widetilde g)=A\), and write \(\widetilde F_\ell=\operatorname{de}_{G_{\widetilde g}}(T_{\widetilde g,\ell})\cap V\).  Equality \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{gr}}(\widetilde g)=\mathcal O\) fixes its indicator-neighbor set as \(Z_\ell\).  The indicator identity gives
\[
 Z_\ell
 =T_{\widetilde g,\ell}\cup
 \bigl((\widetilde F_\ell\setminus T_{\widetilde g,\ell})
       \cap\widetilde A\bigr).
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(T_{\widetilde g,\ell}\subseteq\widetilde F_\ell\), equation (1) yields, for every \(x\in A\),
\[
 x\in Z_\ell\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\widetilde F_\ell.
 \tag{2}
\]

The adjacency \(uv\) is absent from the observational skeleton and present in regime \(k\), so it is not retained.  The exhaustive non-retained endpoint cases in the interface, together with (2), imply
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\widetilde g}(e)=\mathtt{arrow}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\in Z_k,
 \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\widetilde g}(e)=\mathtt{tail}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 u\notin Z_k\ \text{or}\
 u\in\operatorname{An}_{G_{\widetilde g}}(v).
 \tag{4}
\]
Suppose the second alternative in (4) holds.  Because \(u\ne v\), there is a nonempty directed path
\[
 u=x_0\to x_1\to\cdots\to x_r=v
 \tag{5}
\]
in \(G_{\widetilde g}\).  No selection vertex occurs on (5), since every selection vertex is childless.  Moreover, \(v\in\widetilde A\) is an ancestor of a selection parent, so every ancestor \(x_i\) of \(v\) is also in \(\widetilde A=A\).  Every edge \(x_ix_{i+1}\) is a basal observed edge and hence an adjacency in the observational skeleton \(H[A]\).

Fix \(\ell\in[K]\).  If \(\ell\in\sigma(x_i)\), then \(x_i\in Z_\ell\), so (2) gives \(x_i\in\widetilde F_\ell\).  Descendant closure along \(x_i\to x_{i+1}\), followed again by (2), gives \(x_{i+1}\in Z_\ell\).  Therefore
\[
 \sigma(x_i)\subseteq\sigma(x_{i+1}),
 \qquad i=0,\ldots,r-1.
 \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) exhibit a directed path from \(u\) to \(v\) in \(L_A\), and hence
\[
 u\in\operatorname{An}_{G_{\widetilde g}}(v)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Reach}_A(u,v)=1.
 \tag{7}
\]
Combining (3), (4), and (7) with the three cases in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\mbox{-}a\mbox{-}mark\mbox{-}sets}\) proves
\[
 \mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(e;\mathcal O,g)
 \subseteq\mathcal G_A(u,v;k).
 \tag{8}
\]

The reduct \(g=\operatorname{gr}(\eta)\) belongs to \(\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g)\), because \(A_{\mathrm{gr}}(g)=A(\eta)\) and \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{gr}}(g)=\mathcal O(\eta)=\mathcal O\).  Thus the left side of (8) is nonempty.  If \(u\notin Z_k\), (8) gives \(\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}=\{\mathtt{tail}\}\).  If \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_A(u,v)=0\), it gives \(\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}=\{\mathtt{arrow}\}\).

For the remaining case, assume \(u\in Z_k\) and \(\operatorname{Reach}_A(u,v)=1\).  Let \(B_0\) have vertex set \(A\) and no edges.  It is acyclic, is a subgraph of \(H[A]\), satisfies signature monotonicity vacuously, and creates no unshielded collider.  Hence \(\mathrm{lem:graphical\mbox{-}replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\) applies.  In the replacement graph there is no directed path from \(u\) to \(v\): there is no edge within \(A\), no edge enters \(A\) from \(V\setminus A\), and a childless selection vertex cannot return to \(V\).  The preservation lemma also gives the unchanged indicator-neighbor set, so (2) applied to the replacement makes \(u\) affected in regime \(k\).  The non-retained endpoint rule therefore puts an arrowhead at \(u\), and
\[
 \operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_0)
 \in\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g),
 \qquad
 \mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_0)}(e)
 =\mathtt{arrow}.
 \tag{9}
\]
This is a purely graphical membership statement and asserts no kernel realization.

Finiteness of \(A\) and reachability provide a simple path
\[
 P:u=y_0\to y_1\to\cdots\to y_s=v
 \tag{10}
\]
in \(L_A\).  Let \(B_P\) consist exactly of the directed edges in (10).  It is acyclic; every edge lies in \(H[A]\) and satisfies \(\sigma(y_i)\subseteq\sigma(y_{i+1})\); and every vertex has at most one incoming edge, so it creates no collider.  Thus \(B_P\) is admissible.  In its replacement, \(u\) is an ancestor of \(v\).  The preserved indicator identity makes \(u\) affected, and signature monotonicity along (10) makes every \(y_i\), including \(v\), affected in regime \(k\).  The non-retained endpoint rule gives \(u\to v\), so
\[
 \operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_P)
 \in\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g),
 \qquad
 \mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\operatorname{RepGraph}(\eta,B_P)}(e)
 =\mathtt{tail}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Membership in (9) and (11) follows from the preservation of \(A\) and of every skeleton and unshielded collider on every full domain \(U_j\).  Equations (8)--(11) prove in all cases that
\[
 \mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(e;\mathcal O,g)
 =\mathcal G_A(u,v;k).
 \tag{12}
\]

It remains to compare the structural fiber with the law-level fiber.  If \(\eta'\in\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta)\), then
\[
 A_{\mathrm{gr}}(\operatorname{gr}(\eta'))=A(\eta')=A(\eta),
 \qquad
 \mathcal O_{\mathrm{gr}}(\operatorname{gr}(\eta'))
 =\mathcal O(\eta')=\mathcal O.
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{gr}(\eta')\in\mathcal C_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathcal O,g)\), and the reduct identities in \(\mathrm{def:graphical\mbox{-}dai\mbox{-}specifications}\) give
\(\mu_{\eta'}(e)=\mu^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\operatorname{gr}(\eta')}(e)\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal M_A(e;\mathcal O,\eta)
 \subseteq\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(e;\mathcal O,g).
 \tag{14}
\]
The left side is nonempty because \(\eta\in\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta)\).  Equations (12)--(14) prove the displayed nonempty chain and the automatic law-level equality in both singleton cases.

Finally, if both realization fibers are nonempty, choose
\[
 \eta_{\mathtt{arrow}}\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_0),
 \qquad
 \eta_{\mathtt{tail}}\in\operatorname{Real}(\eta,B_P).
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\), both are fully specified faithful members of \(\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\eta)\).  Their graphical components are precisely the specifications in (9) and (11), so they contribute respectively \(\mathtt{arrow}\) and \(\mathtt{tail}\) to the law-level mark set.  Combining this fact with (12)--(14) gives the asserted two-mark law-level equality.  This last step uses nonemptiness of the realization fibers; graphical membership alone does not imply it.  The restriction \(uv\notin H\) is essential by \(\mathrm{prop:retained\mbox{-}edge\mbox{-}sanity\mbox{-}reduction}\).  \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:exact-conditional-polynomial-saturation]\label{prop:exact-conditional-polynomial-saturation}
Fix a finite list \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathrm{SI}}\) whose rule schemata are independent of \(D\) and \(K\). Suppose every rule premise and every path predicate is uniformly decidable in time polynomial in \(D\), \(K\), and the encoding length of \(\mathcal O\), and every strict conclusion monotonically replaces at least one reported circle endpoint by a tail or an arrowhead, with no endpoint subsequently changed. Then saturation computes \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\) in polynomial time and makes at most
\[
 (K+1)D(D-1)
\]
strict reported-endpoint updates. The observational skeleton and unshielded colliders on \(U_0=V\), and the complete skeletons and unshielded-collider families of the faithful pooled selected-law signatures on \(U_k\), \(k\in[K]\), including all indicator adjacencies and indicator-involving unshielded colliders, remain immutable inputs. If in addition \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)\), the polynomial-time output is the canonical SI-PAG and the procedure does not enumerate \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(L\) be the encoding length of the immutable input \(\mathcal O\).  In one regime there are at most \(\binom D2\) observed--observed adjacencies and two reported endpoint positions per adjacency.  Since there are \(K+1\) regimes, the number of reported endpoint positions satisfies
\[
 N_{\mathrm{end}}
 \leq (K+1)2\binom D2
 =(K+1)D(D-1).
 \tag{1}
\]
No endpoint at an intervention indicator is counted, by the definition of \(\mathcal E(\mathcal O)\).

Every strict update replaces a circle at one reported position by one element of \(\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\), and the monotonicity hypothesis forbids a later change at that position.  Charging each strict update to its unique changed position gives
\[
 N_{\mathrm{upd}}\leq (K+1)D(D-1).
 \tag{2}
\]

Because \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\) is fixed and finite and every schema has finitely many typed vertex and regime placeholders, there are constants \(c,q,r\in\mathbb N_0\), depending only on the rule list, such that an exhaustive scan has at most
\[
 cD^q(K+1)^r
 \tag{3}
\]
instances.  The uniform premise-decider hypothesis supplies a polynomial \(p\) such that each instantiated premise, including every path predicate it invokes, is decided within \(p(D,K,L)\) operations.  One scan therefore costs at most
\[
 cD^q(K+1)^r p(D,K,L).
 \tag{4}
\]

Scan instances in a fixed order, apply the first instance with a strict conclusion, restart, and stop after a scan with no strict conclusion.  Every nonterminal scan makes at least one strict update, so (2) bounds the total number of scans, including the final scan, by \((K+1)D(D-1)+1\).  The running time is consequently at most
\[
 \bigl((K+1)D(D-1)+1\bigr)
 cD^q(K+1)^r p(D,K,L),
 \tag{5}
\]
which is polynomial in \(D\), \(K\), and \(L\).

Initialization is exactly that of \(\mathrm{def:monotone\mbox{-}closure}\).  Rule conclusions refine only reported observed endpoints, so every skeleton and unshielded-collider predicate in the complete signatures on \(U_0,\ldots,U_K\), including those involving \(\zeta_k\), remains immutable.  At termination the state is closed under all rule instances.  To prove leastness, let \(Q\) be any closed state extending the same initialization.  Induct over strict rule applications.  The initial state is contained in \(Q\).  If the current state is contained in \(Q\), monotonicity carries the satisfied premise of the next rule to \(Q\), and closedness supplies its conclusion in \(Q\).  Thus the updated state remains contained in \(Q\).  The terminal state is contained in every closed extension and therefore equals \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\).

If additionally \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)\), substitution identifies this polynomial-time output with the canonical SI-PAG.  The procedure reads only \(\mathcal O\), current endpoint marks, and the fixed rule predicates; it never enumerates or queries \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\).  \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:conditional-completeness-reduction]\label{thm:conditional-completeness-reduction}
Let \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathrm{SI}}\) be a finite candidate rule list whose schemata are independent of \(D\) and \(K\), and fix a compatible full signature \(\mathcal O\). Assume the following three conditions. First, for every \(\xi\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and every graph \(B\) admissible for the graphical replacement lemma, \(\operatorname{Real}(\xi,B)\ne\varnothing\). Second, the closure is sound: if \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\) has a definite mark \(m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\) at a reported endpoint \(e\), then \(\mu_\xi(e)=m\) for every \(\xi\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\). Third, for every endpoint \(e\) left circular by the closure and every \(m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\), there are \(\xi_{e,m}\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and a graph \(B_{e,m}\) admissible for the graphical replacement lemma such that every \(\widetilde\xi\in\operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,m},B_{e,m})\) satisfies \(\mu_{\widetilde\xi}(e)=m\). The rule premises use neither latent \(A(\xi)\), a compatible-model or realizability query, nor a global model enumerator. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)
\]
on all reported observed endpoints. If, additionally, every rule premise and path predicate is uniformly decidable in time polynomial in \(D\), \(K\), and the encoding length of \(\mathcal O\), then saturation is polynomial and makes at most \((K+1)D(D-1)\) strict reported-endpoint updates. This is a conditional completeness reduction: it asserts neither nonempty faithful realization for the replacement graphs nor the existence of a finite rule list satisfying the observable unknown-\(A\) extension conditions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\mathcal O\) is compatible, \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\neq\varnothing\).  Fix an arbitrary reported endpoint \(e\in\mathcal E(\mathcal O)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:monotone\mbox{-}closure}\), the mark assigned by \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)\) at \(e\) is either a member of \(\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\) or \(\mathtt{circle}\).  We treat these two exhaustive cases.

Suppose first that
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)(e)=m,
 \qquad m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The soundness condition in the theorem gives
\[
 \mu_\xi(e)=m
 \qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O).
 \tag{2}
\]
By the invariant-mark clause of \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}si\mbox{-}pag}\), equation \((2)\) implies
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)(e)=m
 =\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)(e).
 \tag{3}
\]

Suppose instead that
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)(e)=\mathtt{circle}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Apply the opposing-extension condition first with \(m=\mathtt{tail}\) and then with \(m=\mathtt{arrow}\).  For each \(m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}\), it supplies a base model \(\xi_{e,m}\in\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\) and a graph \(B_{e,m}\) admissible for \(\mathrm{lem:graphical\mbox{-}replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\) such that
\[
 \mu_{\widetilde\xi}(e)=m
 \quad\text{for every }
 \widetilde\xi\in\operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,m},B_{e,m}).
 \tag{5}
\]
The faithful-realization condition gives
\[
 \operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,m},B_{e,m})\neq\varnothing
 \qquad\text{for each }m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Choose
\[
 \widetilde\xi_m\in
 \operatorname{Real}(\xi_{e,m},B_{e,m})
 \qquad\text{for each }m\in\{\mathtt{tail},\mathtt{arrow}\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Since each \(B_{e,m}\) is admissible, \(\mathrm{lem:replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\), whose graphical premise is supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:graphical\mbox{-}replacement\mbox{-}preservation}\), yields
\[
 \mathcal O(\widetilde\xi_m)=\mathcal O,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\xi_m\in\mathcal C_A(\mathcal O,\xi_{e,m})
 \subseteq\mathcal C(\mathcal O).
 \tag{8}
\]
Combining \((5)\)--\((8)\) gives two members of the same compatibility fiber with opposing marks:
\[
 \mu_{\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{tail}}}(e)=\mathtt{tail},
 \qquad
 \mu_{\widetilde\xi_{\mathtt{arrow}}}(e)=\mathtt{arrow}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus the endpoint mark is not invariant over \(\mathcal C(\mathcal O)\).  The circle clause of \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}si\mbox{-}pag}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O)(e)=\mathtt{circle}
 =\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)(e).
 \tag{10}
\]

Equations \((3)\) and \((10)\) establish equality at every \(e\in\mathcal E(\mathcal O)\).  Both objects are partial mixed-graph families on the reported domain fixed by \(\mathcal O\), by \(\mathrm{def:canonical\mbox{-}si\mbox{-}pag}\) and \(\mathrm{def:monotone\mbox{-}closure}\).  Hence endpointwise equality implies
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal O)
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal O).
 \tag{11}
\]

Finally, suppose that every rule premise and path predicate has the stated uniform polynomial-time decision procedure.  The list \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SI}}\) is finite and its schemata are independent of \(D\) and \(K\) by hypothesis.  Membership in \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{SI}}\), together with \(\mathrm{def:monotone\mbox{-}closure}\), ensures that each strict conclusion replaces at least one reported circle by a definite endpoint and that no definite endpoint is subsequently changed.  Therefore all hypotheses of \(\mathrm{prop:exact\mbox{-}conditional\mbox{-}polynomial\mbox{-}saturation}\) hold.  That proposition yields polynomial-time saturation and at most
\[
 (K+1)D(D-1)
\]
strict reported-endpoint updates.  The argument uses the faithful-realization and opposing-extension conditions only as explicit antecedents; it neither establishes them nor constructs the candidate rule list.  This proves the conditional claim.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The replacement construction specifies a selection set, basal DAG, and intervention targets, but no law-level coupling. The exceptional-set lemma now starts only after a nonempty open rational family of tuples is supplied directly, with one product exogenous law, the same realized vector across worlds, response functions shared off target, one basal selection response, positive selection probability, positive pooled cells, positive rational denominators, and the Markov direction. It adds pooled twin faithfulness when the atomic determinants are nontrivial; it does not construct that family or certify those determinants. The exact graphical identity does not imply two-mark faithful realization, and neither faithful realization nor graphical inclusion supplies a finite observable unknown-\(A\) extension. No separation margin or finite-sample testing theorem is supplied, so canonical equality and simultaneous confidence SI-PAGs remain open.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved structural content is the exact fixed-selection-ancestry identity \(\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}=\mathcal G_A\) on the purely graphical Dai fiber. At law level, \(\varnothing\ne\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{faith}}\subseteq\mathcal M_A^{\mathrm{gr}}\); singleton \(\mathcal G_A\) gives equality, while two-mark equality requires nonempty faithful realization fibers. The algebraic lemma is conditional on a supplied directly coupled nonempty open rational parameter family satisfying positivity and the Markov direction, plus one nonzero determinant certificate per \(m\)-connected query. Constructing the family, proving those certificates, producing opposing faithful two-mark witnesses, finding a finite observable unknown-\(A\) extension rule list, proving unconditional canonical equality, and deriving simultaneous finite-sample confidence guarantees are open. Neither the faithful-realization antecedent nor the unknown-\(A\) extension antecedent follows from graphical inclusion. Thus the result is below a complete-calculus result; a global basal-DAG enumerator is not offered as the headline method.
