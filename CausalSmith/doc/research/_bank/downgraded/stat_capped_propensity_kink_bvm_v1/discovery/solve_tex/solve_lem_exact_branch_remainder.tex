%%% FIELD proof-exact-branch-remainder
Put \(q(r)=\min\{1,\delta r\}\), \(I_s(x)=\mathbf 1\{s(x)<c\}\), \(I_e(x)=\mathbf 1\{e(x)<c\}\), and \(d_s(x)=\delta I_s(x)\).  Thus \(d_s\) is the slope selected by the candidate signal; in particular, the saturated slope \(d_s=0\) is selected when \(s=c\).  Also put \(v_a=u_a-\mu_a\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  All equalities below involving \(X\) hold \(P_X\)-almost surely.

The definitions of \(e\) and \(\mu_a\), with the arbitrary bounded versions on unsupported arms understood, imply the everywhere-valid weighted identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[A(Y-u_1(X))\mid X]
 &=e(X)\{\mu_1(X)-u_1(X)\}=-e(X)v_1(X),\\
 \mathbb E_P[(1-A)(Y-u_0(X))\mid X]
 &=(1-e(X))\{\mu_0(X)-u_0(X)\}=-(1-e(X))v_0(X),\\
 \mathbb E_P[A-s(X)\mid X]&=e(X)-s(X).
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, the first equality remains valid when \(e(X)=0\), because both sides are then zero; hence no conditional mean on an unsupported arm is being divided by its arm probability.

The branch-weight definitions give, pointwise on \([0,1]\),
\[
 q(s)=s w_1(s),\qquad 1-q(s)=(1-s)w_0(s).
\]
Indeed, for \(s<c\) these identities read \(\delta s=s\delta\) and \(1-\delta s=(1-s)(1-\delta s)/(1-s)\), while for \(s\ge c\) they read \(1=s(1/s)\) and \(0=(1-s)0\); at \(s=c\), \(w_1(c)=\delta=1/c\).  Conditional expectation of the candidate signal and the preceding weighted identities therefore yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P[m_\eta(O)\mid X]-\{\mu_0(X)+\tau(X)q(e(X))\}\\
 &\quad=\tau(X)\{q(s(X))+d_s(X)(e(X)-s(X))-q(e(X))\}\\
 &\qquad +(e(X)-s(X))
 \bigl[\{d_s(X)-w_1(s(X))\}v_1(X)
       +\{w_0(s(X))-d_s(X)\}v_0(X)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
To verify (1) directly, substitute \(u_a=\mu_a+v_a\).  The coefficient of \(\tau\) is \(q(s)+d_s(e-s)-q(e)\); the coefficient of \(v_1\) is
\[
 q(s)+d_s(e-s)-e w_1(s)=(e-s)\{d_s-w_1(s)\},
\]
and the coefficient of \(v_0\) is
\[
 1-q(s)-d_s(e-s)-(1-e)w_0(s)=(e-s)\{w_0(s)-d_s\}.
\]

The hinge has the following exact selected-tangent gap:
\[
 q(s)+d_s(e-s)-q(e)
 =\delta |e-c|\mathbf 1\{I_s\ne I_e\}.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(e\) and \(s\) are on the same branch, both sides of (2) vanish.  If \(s<c\le e\), its left-hand side is \(\delta s+\delta(e-s)-1=\delta(e-c)\).  If \(e<c\le s\), it is \(1-\delta e=\delta(c-e)\).  These cases include the relevant ties because both indicators use strict inequality; when \(e=c\), the right-hand side and the corresponding gap are both zero.

It remains to simplify the outcome-error term in (1).  On the lower branch \(s<c\),
\[
 d_s-w_1(s)=0,
 \qquad
 w_0(s)-d_s=\frac{1-\delta s}{1-s}-\delta
 =-\frac{\delta-1}{1-s}.
\tag{3}
\]
On the saturated branch \(s\ge c\),
\[
 d_s=w_0(s)=0,
 \qquad w_1(s)=\frac1s,
\tag{4}
\]
where (4) also holds at \(s=c\) because \(w_1(c)=\delta=1/c\).  Substitution of (2)--(4) into (1), followed by integration with respect to \(P_X\), and then use of the definition \(R(P,\eta)=P[m_\eta]-\Psi_\delta(P)\), proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
R(P,\eta)
&=\delta\mathbb E_P\!\left[\tau(X)|e(X)-c|
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{\mathbf 1\{s(X)<c\}\ne\mathbf 1\{e(X)<c\}\right\}\right]\\
&\quad+\mathbb E_P\!\left[(s(X)-e(X))\left\{
 \mathbf 1\{s(X)<c\}\frac{(\delta-1)(u_0(X)-\mu_0(X))}{1-s(X)}
 +\mathbf 1\{s(X)\ge c\}\frac{u_1(X)-\mu_1(X)}{s(X)}
 \right\}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The braces in (5) are an everywhere-defined branchwise function: only the quotient belonging to the active branch is evaluated.  On the lower branch,
\[
 1-s>1-c=\frac{\delta-1}{\delta},
 \qquad \frac{\delta-1}{1-s}<\delta,
\tag{6}
\]
whereas on the saturated branch
\[
 s\ge c=\frac1\delta,
 \qquad \frac1s\le\delta.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus every active denominator is bounded away from zero, including at the specified tie \(s=c\).

For the rate assertion, write the stated branch-relevant \(L_2(P_X)\) error condition explicitly as
\[
 \|I_s v_0\|_{L_2(P_X)}+\|(1-I_s)v_1\|_{L_2(P_X)}\le\varepsilon.
\tag{8}
\]
By (6)--(8) and \(\|s-e\|_\infty\le\rho\), the absolute value of the second expectation in (5) is at most
\[
 \delta\rho\left{\mathbb E_P[|I_s v_0|]
 +\mathbb E_P[|(1-I_s)v_1|]\right}
 \le \delta\rho\varepsilon.
\tag{9}
\]
The last inequality follows directly from
\(0\le\mathbb E_P[(|Z|-\mathbb E_P|Z|)^2]
=\mathbb E_P[Z^2]-(\mathbb E_P|Z|)^2\), applied to each summand, so no additional moment condition is used.

If the two branch indicators disagree and \(e(X)\ne c\), then \(c\) lies between \(s(X)\) and \(e(X)\); hence
\[
 0<|e(X)-c|\le |e(X)-s(X)|\le\rho.
\tag{10}
\]
When \(e(X)=c\), the factor \(|e(X)-c|\) is zero.  Since \(u_a\) and \(\mu_a\) take values in \([-M,M]\), in particular \(|\tau|\le2M\).  Using (10), the punctured-margin condition, and the required range \(0<\rho\le c\wedge(1-c)\), the absolute value of the first expectation in (5) is therefore at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta\mathbb E_P\!\left[|\tau(X)|\,|e(X)-c|\mathbf 1\{I_s\ne I_e\}\right]
 &\le 2\delta M\rho\,P\{0<|e(X)-c|\le\rho\}\\
 &\le 2\delta M C_{\mathrm m}\rho^{1+\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (9) and (11) proves
\[
 |R(P,\eta)|
 \le \delta\rho\varepsilon+2\delta M C_{\mathrm m}\rho^{1+\alpha}
 \le C_{\delta,M,C_{\mathrm m}}\{\rho\varepsilon+\rho^{1+\alpha}\},
\]
for example with \(C_{\delta,M,C_{\mathrm m}}=\delta+2\delta M C_{\mathrm m}\), as claimed.
