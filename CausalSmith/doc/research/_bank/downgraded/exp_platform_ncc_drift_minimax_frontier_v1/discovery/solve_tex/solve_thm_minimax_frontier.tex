%%% FIELD optimized-envelope-statement
Every experiment in the target class satisfies the optimized observed-law total-variation lower envelope
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 \sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
In particular, for \(\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\),
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha).
\]
%%% FIELD optimized-envelope-proof
Fix \(0\le\eta<1-2\alpha\).  For every \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mixture\text{-}modulus\text{-}length\text{-}lower\text{-}bound}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]
 \ge
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
The right-hand side depends on the experiment and \(\eta\), but not on \(K\).  Taking the infimum over \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) and using Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
This inequality holds for every \(\eta\) in the displayed nonempty range; hence taking the supremum over \(\eta\) proves the first claim.  Assumption \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) gives
\[
 0<\eta_\alpha=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}<1-2\alpha,
 \qquad
 1-2\alpha-\eta_\alpha=\eta_\alpha.
\]
Substitution of this admissible value in the pointwise inequality proves the convenient corollary.
%%% FIELD masked-profile-statement
For \(0\le\gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\), let \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\) be the all-zero schedule.  For every \(S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}\), put
\[
 w(S)=\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b},
 \qquad
 E_S=\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\},
 \qquad
 q_S=P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(E_S).
\]
There are two schedules \(Y^-,Y^+\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\) with
\[
 \tau_W(Y^-)=-Mw(S),
 \qquad
 \tau_W(Y^+)=Mw(S),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^-},P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^+}\right)=1-q_S.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(1-q_S)\ge2Mw(S).
\]
The number \(q_S\) is computable by finite enumeration of the known adaptive assignment tree.  In particular, if \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\), then \(\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta_\alpha)\ge2Mw(S)\).
%%% FIELD masked-profile-proof
Fix \(\gamma\) and \(S\) as in the statement.  Define two fixed schedules by
\[
 Y^-_{bi}(z_1)=-M,
 \qquad
 Y^+_{bi}(z_1)=M
 \quad\text{for }(b,i)\in S,
\]
and set every other available potential outcome, including every control outcome and every focal outcome outside \(S\), equal to zero under both schedules.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) holds because all displayed values lie in \([-M,M]\).  Under either schedule, every control block mean equals zero, and hence
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^{B-1}|\mu_{b+1}-\mu_b|=0\le\gamma.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:schedule\text{-}family}\) therefore gives \(Y^-,Y^+\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\) ensures that \(z_1\) is available at every position in \(S\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) then gives
\[
 \tau_W(Y^-)=-M\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}=-Mw(S),
 \qquad
 \tau_W(Y^+)=Mw(S).
\]

We next identify the observed-law overlap exactly.  On an assignment prefix that has not assigned \(z_1\) at a position in \(S\), every outcome observed so far under \(Y^-\), \(Y^+\), and \(Y^{(0)}\) is zero.  Starting from the empty history and proceeding through the finite lexicographic order on \(\mathcal T\), suppose such prefixes agree.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) then evaluates the same known probability vector \(p_{bi}(\cdot\mid H_{bi})\) at the same observed history under all three schedules.  Induction over the enrollment positions proves
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^-}(E_S)
 =P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^+}(E_S)
 =P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(E_S)=q_S,
\]
and it also proves equality of the restrictions of the first two observed-data laws to \(E_S\).

On \(E_S^c\), let \((b,i)\) be the first position in \(S\) assigned to \(z_1\).  At that position the full observed record contains \(-M\) under \(Y^-\) and \(M\) under \(Y^+\).  Since \(M>0\), the restrictions of the two laws to \(E_S^c\) have disjoint supports, irrespective of how this different observed outcome changes later predictable propensities.  Thus the two probability laws have a common restriction of mass \(q_S\) and mutually singular complementary restrictions of mass \(1-q_S\).  The finite-space definition of total variation gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^-},P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^+}\right)=1-q_S.
\]

In Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}information\text{-}modulus}\), choose the point-mass priors \(\Pi_0=\delta_{Y^-}\) and \(\Pi_1=\delta_{Y^+}\).  Their supports lie in \(\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\), their observed-law total variation is \(1-q_S\), and their target supports are separated by \(2Mw(S)\).  Hence
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(1-q_S)\ge2Mw(S).
\]
If \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\), then \(1-q_S\le\eta_\alpha\).  The feasible prior pairs in the definition of \(\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta)\) are nested as \(\eta\) increases, so
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \ge\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(1-q_S)
 \ge2Mw(S).
\]

Finally, let \(\mathcal A_S\) be the finite collection of complete assignment paths that avoid assigning \(z_1\) at every position in \(S\).  If \(h^0_{bi}(z)\) is the earlier assignment prefix along path \(z\), together with its zero observed outcomes, sequential randomization gives
\[
 q_S
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal A_S}
   \prod_{(b,i)\in\mathcal T}
   p_{bi}(z_{bi}\mid h^0_{bi}(z)).
\]
The enrollment set and all availability sets are finite and every propensity is known, so this sum is explicitly computable.
%%% FIELD quantitative-obstruction-statement
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(M>0\).  There is a sequence of admissible experiments \(\mathcal D_n\) with \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a strictly positive propensity floor in every experiment such that, for all sufficiently large integers \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
Thus the ratio tends to zero, and no constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) can satisfy \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) uniformly over the primitive adaptive-design class.
%%% FIELD quantitative-obstruction-proof
Put
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 \delta_n=\frac{\varepsilon}{n},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{\alpha-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}
       =\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}.
\]
For each positive integer \(n\), take \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(q_2=1\), and block sizes \((n_1,n_2,n_3)=(1,n,1)\).  Let the arm set be \(\{z_0,z_1,a\}\), with
\[
 A_1=\{z_0,a\},
 \qquad
 A_2=\{z_0,z_1\},
 \qquad
 A_3=\{z_0\}.
\]
At the unique position in block \(1\), assign \(a\) with probability \(\varepsilon\) and \(z_0\) with probability \(1-\varepsilon\), and let \(E=\{Z_{11}=a\}\).  At every position in block \(2\), set
\[
 p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})=
 \begin{cases}
  \delta_n,&E,\\
  1/2,&E^c,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 p_{2i}(z_0\mid H_{2i})=1-p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i}),
\]
and assign \(z_0\) with probability one in block \(3\).  Arms outside the displayed availability sets receive probability zero.

The event \(E\) is observed before block \(2\), so these probabilities are predictable, and \(P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(E)=\varepsilon\) for every fixed schedule \(Y\).  The construction therefore satisfies Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\).  The displayed support and availability sets verify Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}support}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\).  Since \(0<\delta_n\le\varepsilon<1/2\), every available-arm probability is at least \(p_{\min,n}=\delta_n>0\), verifying Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\).  We use the schedule class \(\mathcal Y_{M,0}\), whose members have bounded outcomes by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and equal control block means by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}tv}\).  Thus every experiment is admissible under the primitive conditions.

Definition \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}weights}\) gives \(u_2=1\) and \(u_1=u_3=0\).  The focal-arm part of the scale in Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) depends only on \(E\), and every omitted part is nonnegative.  Hence, for every \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\) and every schedule in \(\mathcal Y_{M,0}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}(v)
 &\ge
 \frac{8M^2}{n}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 \left[
  \frac{1-p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})}
       {p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})}
 \right]\\
 &=\frac{8M^2}{n}
 \left\{
  \varepsilon\frac{1-\delta_n}{\delta_n}
  +(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1-1/2}{1/2}
 \right\}\\
 &=8M^2\left\{1+\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{n}\right\}
 \ge8M^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In the first line the common expectation is multiplied by the sum of the \(n\) coefficients \(n^{-2}\), which is \(n^{-1}\).  Since \(\mathsf B_0(v)\ge0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}frontier}\) and \(\alpha<1/2\) give, for every feasible \(v\),
\[
 \Psi_{\alpha,0}(v)
 \ge\sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}(v)/\alpha}
 \ge M\sqrt{8/\alpha}>2M.
\]
The clipping in that definition therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M.
\]

It remains to upper-bound the all-interval minimax length.  On \(E^c\), define the observable variables
\[
 X_i=2\mathbf 1\{Z_{2i}=z_1\}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{2i}
     -2\mathbf 1\{Z_{2i}=z_0\}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{2i},
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i.
\]
The two block-\(2\) arms have conditional probabilities \(1/2\) on \(E^c\).  Sequential randomization and the observation map imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[X_i\mid H_{2i},E^c]
 =Y_{2i}(z_1)-Y_{2i}(z_0).
\]
Bounded outcomes give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[X_i^2\mid H_{2i},E^c]
 =2Y_{2i}(z_1)^2+2Y_{2i}(z_0)^2
 \le4M^2.
\]
Because \(q_2=1\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) identifies the average of the conditional means with \(\tau_W\).  Thus the centered summands are martingale differences under the conditional design law given \(E^c\).  For \(i<j\), iterated conditioning gives zero cross moment, and conditional variance is bounded by conditional second moment.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 \left[(\widehat\theta_n-\tau_W)^2\mid E^c\right]
 \le\frac{4M^2}{n}
\]
uniformly over \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\).

Set \(t_n=2M/\sqrt{n\beta}\).  For every sufficiently large \(n\), \(t_n<2M\).  Consider the deterministic schedule-independent interval kernel that reports the singleton \(\{0\}\) on \(E\), and on \(E^c\) reports
\[
 J_n=
 [\Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)-t_n,
   \Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)+t_n]
 \cap[-2M,2M].
\]
This is a nonempty closed interval.  Bounded outcomes and Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) imply \(\tau_W\in[-2M,2M]\), while projection onto this interval gives
\[
 |\Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)-\tau_W|
 \le|\widehat\theta_n-\tau_W|.
\]
Applying the pointwise inequality \(\mathbf 1\{|x|>t_n\}\le x^2/t_n^2\) to the conditional second-moment bound yields
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(\tau_W\notin J_n\mid E^c)
 \le\frac{4M^2/n}{t_n^2}=\beta.
\]
We claim no coverage on \(E\).  Nevertheless,
\[
 P^K_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(\tau_W\in I_K)
 \ge(1-\varepsilon)(1-\beta)=1-\alpha
\]
for every schedule.  Hence this kernel belongs to \(\mathcal C_\alpha\).  Its interval length is zero on \(E\) and at most \(2t_n\) on \(E^c\), so Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star
 \le(1-\varepsilon)2t_n
 =\frac{4M(1-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{n\beta}}.
\]
Dividing by \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M\) and substituting \(\varepsilon=\alpha/2\) and \(\beta=\alpha/(2-\alpha)\) proves
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
The right-hand side converges to zero.  If a positive \(c_\alpha\) lower-compared \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\) throughout the primitive class, applying it to this sequence and passing to the limit would give \(c_\alpha\le0\), a contradiction.
%%% FIELD adaptive-sandwich-statement
Under the primitive sequential-randomization, availability, positivity, boundedness, and control-total-variation conditions, every admissible experiment obeys the adaptive minimax sandwich
\[
 \sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta)
 \le \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star.
\]
For the all-zero schedule \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\), define, for every \(S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}\),
\[
 w(S)=\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b},
 \qquad
 q_S=P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\}.
\]
Then the sharper computable certificate is
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 \max_{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}}
 2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+,
 \qquad [x]_+=\max\{x,0\}.
\]
The maximum is computable by finite enumeration of the known adaptive assignment tree.  No constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) lower-compares \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\) uniformly on the primitive adaptive class.  Indeed, there is an admissible three-block sequence with \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a positive propensity floor in every experiment such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
Coverage and expected length in every assertion use the same extended design law, and the minimax infimum ranges over all schedule-independent auxiliary-randomized, variable-length observed-data interval kernels.
%%% FIELD adaptive-sandwich-proof
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:optimized\text{-}mixture\text{-}envelope}\) proves the optimized lower envelope and its \(\eta_\alpha\) corollary.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}upper\text{-}frontier}\) constructs one schedule-independent kernel \(K^\star\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) whose worst-schedule expected length is at most \(2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\).  Taking the infimum over all \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) gives the upper half of the sandwich.

For the masked certificate, fix \(S\) and abbreviate its displayed probability by \(q_S\).  If \(q_S>2\alpha\), put \(\eta_S=1-q_S\).  Since \(0\le q_S\le1\),
\[
 0\le\eta_S<1-2\alpha,
 \qquad
 1-2\alpha-\eta_S=q_S-2\alpha>0.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:masked\text{-}focal\text{-}profile}\), used at \(\gamma=\Gamma\), gives
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_S)\ge2Mw(S).
\]
The pointwise lower bound proved in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:optimized\text{-}mixture\text{-}envelope}\) therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge(q_S-2\alpha)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_S)
 \ge2Mw(S)(q_S-2\alpha).
\]
If \(q_S\le2\alpha\), the claimed right-hand side is zero, while interval lengths and hence \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) are nonnegative by Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\).  Thus, in both cases,
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\ge2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+.
\]
The collection of subsets of the finite focal-window enrollment set is finite, so taking its maximum preserves the inequality.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:masked\text{-}focal\text{-}profile}\) expresses every \(q_S\) as a finite sum of products of known sequential propensities; hence the maximum is computable by finite enumeration.

Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:rare\text{-}history\text{-}quantitative\text{-}obstruction}\) supplies the displayed three-block sequence and quantitative ratio bound.  Since its right-hand side tends to zero, any uniform inequality \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(c_\alpha>0\) would contradict that sequence.  Finally, Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) defines both coverage and length under \(P^K_{\mathcal D,Y}\), includes the independent auxiliary randomizer in the interval action, and takes the infimum over every schedule-independent observed-data Markov kernel of the stated type.  This verifies the procedural scope in the last sentence.
%%% FIELD target-current-statement
Under the primitive sequential-randomization, availability, positivity, boundedness, and control-total-variation conditions, every admissible experiment satisfies
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le 2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
Let \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\) be the all-zero fixed schedule. The same experiment also has the computable masked-history lower certificate
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge \eta_\alpha
 \max_{\substack{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}:\\ P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\}\ge1-\eta_\alpha}}

%%% FIELD target-proof
Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\text{-}minimax\text{-}sandwich}\) directly gives
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
It also gives the claimed absence of an \(\alpha\)-only comparison and, more strongly, constructs a sequence for which
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

It remains only to derive the older thresholded form of the masked certificate from the stronger positive-part form.  Let \(S\) satisfy the probability constraint in the statement and write that probability as \(q_S\).  Since \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\) and \(\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\),
\[
 q_S-2\alpha
 \ge1-\eta_\alpha-2\alpha
 =\eta_\alpha>0.
\]
Therefore the positive-part certificate in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\text{-}minimax\text{-}sandwich}\) gives, for every feasible \(S\),
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+
 \ge\eta_\alpha\,2M
 \sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}.
\]
Taking the maximum over the feasible collection proves the displayed thresholded certificate.  That collection is nonempty because \(S=\varnothing\) has \(w(S)=0\) and \(q_S=1\).  Its finite-enumeration computability, as well as the common-extended-law and all-kernel scope, are also part of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\text{-}minimax\text{-}sandwich}\).  This proves every assertion of the target theorem.
