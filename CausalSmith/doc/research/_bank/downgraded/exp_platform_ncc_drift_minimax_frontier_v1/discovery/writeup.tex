\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}platform\_\allowbreak{}ncc\_\allowbreak{}drift\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For the window-weighted finite-population focal-arm effect, the exact cumulative-discrepancy program gives the sharp bounded-drift borrowing bias, and a finite convex program balances that bias against an assignment-law-averaged predictable-AIPW martingale envelope. The projected interval is uniformly design-honest and has worst-case expected length at most twice the clipped optimized radius. The all-kernel lower side is the optimized observed-law total-variation envelope \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\ge\sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}(1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta)\), with the fixed \(\eta_\alpha\) bound as a corollary. Exact focal-mask overlap yields the computable certificate \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\ge\max_S2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+\). This is an adaptive minimax sandwich, not a matching frontier: an explicit positive-per-experiment three-block sequence has \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M\) and ratio at most \(2(1-\alpha/2)/\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}\). The fixed-common-floor comparison remains an unanswered, nondependent question.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Platform-control papers study parametric or semiparametric calendar adjustment through bias, type-I error, power, or superpopulation efficiency, while Gaussian convex-class work studies minimax expected length in nonadaptive observation experiments. Those cited classes do not transfer an adaptive randomization converse by inclusion. On the present primitive class, an \(\alpha\)-only comparison between every honest variable-length interval and the predictable inverse-propensity radius is false: the quantitative rare-history sequence refutes it despite positive propensities in every finite experiment. Its floors shrink, leaving the fixed-common-floor comparison genuinely open.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note makes the adaptive assignment tree and fixed potential-outcome schedule explicit. It combines the sharp bounded-total-variation drift support function, an implementable predictable-AIPW upper interval, the optimized all-kernel observed-law total-variation lower envelope, an exact finite-tree focal-mask overlap certificate, and a quantitative rare-history obstruction. The residual fixed-floor question is isolated outside the proved dependency spine.

\paragraph{Fill.} The proved contribution is an adaptive minimax sandwich: the projected convex-program interval has worst-case expected length at most twice its clipped radius, while every schedule-independent randomized interval obeys the optimized observed-law modulus lower envelope and the computable positive-part mask certificate. The explicit three-block inequality quantifies why the upper radius is not an unrestricted minimax equivalent. The optimizer certificate compares nonconcurrent-control and concurrent-only AIPW radii, not all-kernel minimax values. For the NCC package and ACTT-style calibration, the radius is a certified conservative design-based scale; constant-factor minimax calibration under one common propensity floor remains open and unused.

\section{Related work}

LeeWason2020, BofillRoigEtAl2022, and KrotkaEtAl2025TemporalDrift compare all-control and concurrent-only platform procedures under parametric calendar models through bias, type-I error, and power. MarschnerSchou2024 formalizes concurrent cohorts, and SantacatterinaEtAl2025 studies superpopulation efficiency for a concurrent estimand. These works motivate the platform setting but do not give uniform fixed-schedule coverage over the present total-variation drift class. LiZhao2026 supplies the predictable-AIPW martingale template and positivity for every analyzed treatment; the present finite-horizon condition adapts positivity to available arms and permits structural support zeros off the focal window. ChenLiZhangYe2025, ArmstrongKolesar2018, and CaiLow2004 supply Gaussian bias--variance or modulus analogies. Their nonadaptive experiment classes do not transfer a converse here by inclusion once observed outcomes change later propensities. The proved lower result is instead an observed-law total-variation envelope under the actual adaptive assignment law, sharpened by an exact masked-history overlap calculation. The quantitative rare-history sequence rules out an \(\alpha\)-only comparison with the AIPW radius on the primitive class. Whether a fixed-common-floor subclass admits a constant depending on \(\alpha\) and that floor remains open and unused. SawadaNomuraShinozaki2026 concerns estimated bias caps and bootstrap inference under a different estimand and model, while AronowLopatto2026 and SudijonoDobribanTchetgen2026 do not combine staggered availability, bounded calendar drift, and adaptive-history masking.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sequential-randomization]\label{ass:sequential-randomization}
The design obeys \(\Pr(Z_{bi}=z\mid H_{bi})=p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})\) for every \((b,i)\in\mathcal T\) and \(z\in\mathcal Z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known history-adaptive randomization, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:availability-window]\label{ass:availability-window}
The availability sets satisfy \(z_0\in A_b\) and \(z_1\in A_b\Longleftrightarrow b\in W\) for every \(b\in\mathcal B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (staggered platform-arm availability, \cite{BofillRoigEtAl2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:availability-support]\label{ass:availability-support}
The propensity support satisfies \(p_{bi}(z\mid h)>0\Longleftrightarrow z\in A_b\) for every history \(h\) compatible with the design.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (availability-restricted positivity, \cite{SantacatterinaEtAl2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-floor]\label{ass:propensity-floor}
Available-arm propensities satisfy \(p_{bi}(z\mid h)\ge p_{\min}\) for every \(z\in A_b\) and every compatible history \(h\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform adaptive-design positivity, \cite{LiZhao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
The fixed schedule satisfies \(Y_{bi}(z)\in[-M,M]\) for every \((b,i)\in\mathcal T\) and \(z\in A_b\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded finite-population outcomes, \cite{AronowLopatto2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:control-tv]\label{ass:control-tv}
The fixed control means satisfy \(\sum_{b=1}^{B-1}|\mu_{b+1}-\mu_b|\le\Gamma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} An aggregate calendar-drift budget is interpretable for trials with a small number of prespecified calendar blocks: one standard-of-care change of size at most \(\Gamma\), or several smaller changes with the same total movement, satisfies the condition without imposing a parametric trend or equality across treatment arms.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:aipw-martingale]\label{lem:aipw-martingale}
For every deterministic \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\), \(\widehat\tau(v)-\tau_W=\sum_{(b,i)\in\mathcal T}\xi_{bi}(v)-\sum_{b=1}^B d_b(v)\mu_b\), where \(\{\xi_{bi}(v),H_{bi}\}\) is a design-based martingale-difference array. Its conditional variances obey \(\sum_{b,i}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\xi_{bi}(v)^2\mid H_{bi}]\le\mathsf G_{\mathcal D}(v;H)\) almost surely and hence \(\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}\sum_{b,i}\xi_{bi}(v)^2\le\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(v)\) uniformly over \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\).  Put \(u_b=q_b\mathbf 1\{b\in W\}\), and, for an arm \(z\in A_b\), define
\[
e_{bi}(z)=Y_{bi}(z)-m_{bi}(z),
\qquad
\Delta_{bi}(z)=\phi_{bi}(z)-Y_{bi}(z).
\]
Consistency of the observed outcome and Definition \(\mathrm{def:aipw\text{-}score}\) give
\[
\Delta_{bi}(z)
=e_{bi}(z)\left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_{bi}=z\}}
 {p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})}-1\right\}.
\]
The denominator is positive by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}support}\).  Both \(m_{bi}(z)\) and \(p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})\) are \(H_{bi}\)-measurable.  Hence Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) implies, conditionally on \(H_{bi}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\Delta_{bi}(z)\mid H_{bi}]=0
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\Delta_{bi}(z)^2\mid H_{bi}]
&=e_{bi}(z)^2\left[
p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})
 \left\{\frac1{p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})}-1\right\}^{2}
+1-p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})\right]\\
&=e_{bi}(z)^2
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})}\\
&\le 4M^2
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality follows from Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and the stipulated bound \(m_{bi}(z)\in[-M,M]\).  Define the increment, with its first term interpreted as zero when \(b\notin W\), by
\[
\xi_{bi}(v)
=\frac{u_b}{n_b}\Delta_{bi}(z_1)
-\frac{v_b}{n_b}\Delta_{bi}(z_0).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\) makes every score occurring with nonzero coefficient available.  The preceding conditional-mean identity shows that the lexicographically ordered array 
\(\{\xi_{bi}(v),H_{bi}\}_{(b,i)\in\mathcal T}\) is a martingale-difference array.  Moreover, \((x-y)^2\le 2x^2+2y^2\) and the preceding second-moment calculation yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\xi_{bi}(v)^2\mid H_{bi}]
&\le 8M^2\left\{
\frac{u_b^2}{n_b^2}
\frac{1-p_{bi}(z_1\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_1\mid H_{bi})}
+\frac{v_b^2}{n_b^2}
\frac{1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}
\right\},
\end{aligned}
\]
again taking the first summand to be zero off \(W\).  Summing gives exactly
\[
\sum_{b,i}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\xi_{bi}(v)^2\mid H_{bi}]
\le \mathsf G_{\mathcal D}(v;H).
\]

It remains to identify the deterministic center.  By Definitions \(\mathrm{def:target}\), \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}weights}\), and \(\mathrm{def:borrowing\text{-}estimator}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau(v)-\tau_W
&=\sum_{b,i}\xi_{bi}(v)
+\sum_{b\in W}\frac{q_b}{n_b}\sum_iY_{bi}(z_1)
-\sum_b\frac{v_b}{n_b}\sum_iY_{bi}(z_0)\\
&\qquad-
\sum_{b\in W}\frac{q_b}{n_b}\sum_i
 \{Y_{bi}(z_1)-Y_{bi}(z_0)\}\\
&=\sum_{b,i}\xi_{bi}(v)-\sum_b(v_b-u_b)\mu_b\\
&=\sum_{b,i}\xi_{bi}(v)-\sum_bd_b(v)\mu_b.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, taking expectations in the conditional-variance bound and using Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) gives, for every \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}\sum_{b,i}\xi_{bi}(v)^2
\le \mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\mathsf G_{\mathcal D}(v;H)]
\le\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(v),
\]
which proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The decomposition separates deterministic calendar bias from assignment noise while retaining the actual probability of each adaptive history. \textit{Gap.} LiZhao2026, equation (3) and Theorem 4, center AIPW at a full-support finite-population contrast; the present decomposition permits focal-arm support zeros off \(W\) and exposes both NCC drift and assignment-law-averaged information. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the auditable estimating equation and variance accounting needed for an NCC package implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:tv-support-dual]\label{lem:tv-support-dual}
For every \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\) and every \(0\le\gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\), summation by parts gives \(\sum_bd_b(v)m_b=-\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}D_j(v)(m_{j+1}-m_j)\). Consequently, \(\mathsf B_\gamma(v)\) equals the value of the finite linear program over increments \(\delta_j=m_{j+1}-m_j\) with \(\sum_j|\delta_j|\le\gamma\) and \(-M\le c+\sum_{k<b}\delta_k\le M\); an endpoint optimizer yields an extremal fixed control schedule. In particular, \(\mathsf B_\gamma(v)=\gamma\max_{j<B}|D_j(v)|\) when \(0\le\gamma\le2M\), and \(\mathsf B_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}(v)=M\sum_b|d_b(v)|\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\) and set \(D_0(v)=0\) and \(D_B(v)=\sum_{b=1}^Bd_b(v)\).  Both \(v\) and \(u\) are probability vectors, so \(D_B(v)=0\).  Since \(d_b(v)=D_b(v)-D_{b-1}(v)\), finite summation by parts gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{b=1}^Bd_b(v)m_b
&=\sum_{b=1}^B\{D_b(v)-D_{b-1}(v)\}m_b\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}D_j(v)(m_j-m_{j+1})\\
&=-\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}D_j(v)(m_{j+1}-m_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
Write \(c=m_1\) and \(\delta_j=m_{j+1}-m_j\).  Then
\[
m_b=c+\sum_{k<b}\delta_k.
\]
Thus the feasible set in Definition \(\mathrm{def:drift\text{-}support\text{-}family}\) is represented exactly by
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}|\delta_j|\le\gamma,
\qquad
-M\le c+\sum_{k<b}\delta_k\le M
\quad(1\le b\le B).
\]
After replacing each \(\delta_j\) by the difference of two nonnegative variables, the absolute-value constraint is linear.  Maximizing each of the two signs of
\(-\sum_jD_j(v)\delta_j\) therefore gives two finite linear programs, and their larger value is precisely \(\mathsf B_\gamma(v)\).  The feasible polytope is nonempty and compact, so a maximizing extreme point exists.  Given any maximizing \(m\), the fixed schedule with \(Y_{bi}(z_0)=m_b\) for every \(i\), and with every other available potential outcome set to zero, is bounded by \(M\), has control means \(m_b\), and obeys the same total-variation constraint.  It is therefore an extremal fixed control schedule in \(\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\); no stochastic or superpopulation construction is being used.

For \(0\le\gamma\le2M\), the summation-by-parts identity and the variation constraint imply
\[
\left|\sum_bd_b(v)m_b\right|
\le \max_{j<B}|D_j(v)|\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}|\delta_j|
\le\gamma\max_{j<B}|D_j(v)|.
\]
Choose \(j_\star\) attaining the maximum.  If \(D_{j_\star}(v)\ne0\), put
\[
\delta_{j_\star}=-\gamma\operatorname{sgn}(D_{j_\star}(v)),
\qquad \delta_j=0\ (j\ne j_\star),
\qquad c=\frac{\gamma}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(D_{j_\star}(v)).
\]
The resulting block means equal \(c\) before the jump and \(-c\) after the jump, so they lie in \([-M,M]\) because \(\gamma\le2M\), and the absolute objective is
\(\gamma|D_{j_\star}(v)|\).  If every \(D_j(v)\) is zero, both sides are zero.  Hence
\[
\mathsf B_\gamma(v)=\gamma\max_{j<B}|D_j(v)|,
\qquad 0\le\gamma\le2M.
\]

Finally, every \(m\in[-M,M]^B\) has
\(\sum_{j<B}|m_{j+1}-m_j|\le2M(B-1)=\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\).  The variation constraint is therefore vacuous at that budget.  Choosing \(m_b=M\operatorname{sgn}(d_b(v))\), with an arbitrary sign when \(d_b(v)=0\), attains the elementary box support function, and consequently
\[
\mathsf B_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}(v)
=\max_{m\in[-M,M]^B}\left|\sum_bd_b(v)m_b\right|
=M\sum_b|d_b(v)|.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The cumulative-discrepancy map turns calendar robustness into an exact finite bias penalty for every sensitivity budget. \textit{Gap.} ArmstrongKolesar2018 optimizes worst-case bias over convex regression classes, while BofillRoigEtAl2022 reports model-dependent trend bias; neither gives this ordered-block finite-population support function. \textit{Consumer.} ACTT analysts can translate a clinically chosen drift budget into an exact bias allowance for any NCC weight vector.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rare-history-stress-test]\label{prop:rare-history-stress-test}
Fix any enrollment position \((b,i)\), schedule \(Y\), and arm \(z\) such that \(p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})>0\) holds \(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}\)-almost surely, and fix any predictable event \(E\in H_{bi}\).  The event's contribution to the average inverse-propensity factor is exactly 
\(\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\mathbf 1_E\{1-p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})\}/p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})]\).  If \(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}(E)\le\epsilon\) and \(p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})\ge\delta\) on \(E\), this contribution is at most \(\epsilon(1-\delta)/\delta\).  Thus a rare \(\epsilon\)-probability branch is charged through its assignment probability rather than by the worst-path factor \(1/\delta\) alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(p=p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})\).  The added positivity condition makes the displayed random variable well defined.  Its contribution on \(E\) is, by restriction of the expectation under the actual design law,
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}\left[\mathbf 1_E\frac{1-p}{p}\right].
\]
On \(E\), the hypothesis \(p\ge\delta>0\) and monotonicity of \(x\mapsto x^{-1}-1\) on \((0,1]\) give
\[
0\le\frac{1-p}{p}\le\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}\left[\mathbf 1_E\frac{1-p}{p}\right]
\le \frac{1-\delta}{\delta}P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}(E)
\le\epsilon\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}.
\]
This is an assignment-law average: the factor contributed by the branch is multiplied by its actual probability.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\) guarantees the positivity premise, with \(p\ge p_{\min}\), whenever the fixed arm is available.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This calculation is the required low-propensity audit and prevents an information lower bound from treating negligible histories as certain. \textit{Gap.} Worst-path martingale bounds are valid for coverage but do not by themselves characterize worst-case expected length under adaptive randomization. \textit{Consumer.} It tells trial software how rare adaptive branches enter the borrowing penalty and guards against severe overconservatism.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-upper-frontier]\label{thm:honest-upper-frontier}
For every \(0\le\gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\), the objective \(\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}\) is convex and lower semicontinuous on \(\Delta(\mathcal B)\), so an optimizer \(v_\gamma^\star\) exists. At the target budget \(\Gamma\), the projected interval \(I^\star\) is a nonempty closed subinterval of \([-2M,2M]\) on every assignment path and induces the schedule-independent observed-data Markov kernel \(K^\star(dI\mid O)=\delta_{I^\star(O)}(dI)\). Because \(\tau_W\in[-2M,2M]\) and \(\lvert\Pi_M(x)-\tau_W\rvert\le\lvert x-\tau_W\rvert\), it satisfies \(\inf_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}P^{K^\star}_{\mathcal D,Y}(\tau_W\in I^\star)\ge1-\alpha\). Its worst-case expected length is at most \(2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\): the projected branch has length at most \(2\Psi_\alpha(v^\star)\), the full-space branch has length \(4M\), and the empty-intersection fallback event has probability zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first establish existence of the optimizer from the primitive design conditions.  For a fixed schedule \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\), taking expectation in the displayed definition of \(\mathsf G_{\mathcal D}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\mathsf G_{\mathcal D}(v;H)]
=c_Y+\sum_{b=1}^Ba_{b,Y}v_b^2,
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_Y&=8M^2\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}
 \sum_{b,i}\frac{u_b^2}{n_b^2}
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_1\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_1\mid H_{bi})},\\
a_{b,Y}&=8M^2\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}
 \sum_{i=1}^{n_b}\frac1{n_b^2}
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(c_Y\ge0\) and \(a_{b,Y}\ge0\).  Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\), \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}support}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\) make every nonzero displayed denominator at least \(p_{\min}\); hence these coefficients are finite and uniformly bounded over the schedule class.  Since the square root is increasing,
\[
\sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(v)/\alpha}
=\sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}}
 \alpha^{-1/2}\sqrt{c_Y+\sum_ba_{b,Y}v_b^2}.
\]
For each fixed \(Y\), the expression after the supremum is the Euclidean norm of
\(\alpha^{-1/2}(\sqrt{c_Y},\sqrt{a_{1,Y}}v_1,\ldots,\sqrt{a_{B,Y}}v_B)\), so it is convex and continuous in \(v\).  Its supremum over \(Y\) is convex and lower semicontinuous.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:drift\text{-}support\text{-}family}\) expresses \(\mathsf B_\gamma\) as the support function of a nonempty compact set under an affine map of \(v\); it is finite, convex, and continuous.  Consequently \(\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}\) is finite, convex, and lower semicontinuous.  The simplex \(\Delta(\mathcal B)\) is nonempty and compact, so a minimizing sequence has a convergent subsequence and lower semicontinuity shows that its limit is an optimizer \(v_\gamma^\star\).

Now fix \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\), put \(v=v^\star\), and write \(S=\sum_{b,i}\xi_{bi}(v)\).  For two lexicographically ordered increments with the first preceding the second, the tower property gives \(\mathbb E[\xi_s\xi_t]=\mathbb E[\xi_s\mathbb E(\xi_t\mid H_t)]=0\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:aipw\text{-}martingale}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) therefore imply
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[S^2]
=\sum_{b,i}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\xi_{bi}(v)^2]
\le\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(v).
\]
The same lemma and the definition of the sharp support functional give
\[
\widehat\tau(v)-\tau_W
=S-\sum_bd_b(v)\mu_b,
\qquad
\left|\sum_bd_b(v)\mu_b\right|\le\mathsf B_\Gamma(v),
\]
where the last inequality uses Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}tv}\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\), and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:tv\text{-}support\text{-}dual}\).  If \(\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(v)>0\), nonnegativity gives
\[
\mathbb E[S^2]\ge
\frac{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(v)}{\alpha}
P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}\left\{
S^2>\frac{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(v)}{\alpha}
\right\}.
\]
Combining this display with the second-moment bound yields
\[
P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}\left(
 |S|>\sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(v)/\alpha}
\right)\le\alpha.
\]
If the variance envelope is zero, the same second-moment bound gives \(S=0\) almost surely, so the corresponding conclusion holds with probability one.  Thus in all cases
\[
P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}\left(
 |\widehat\tau(v)-\tau_W|\le
 \mathsf B_\Gamma(v)+
 \sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(v)/\alpha}
\right)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The radius on the right is \(\Psi_\alpha(v^\star)\).

Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\), the positivity and unit-sum properties of \(q\), and Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) imply \(\tau_W\in[-2M,2M]\).  For every \(t\in[-2M,2M]\), the scalar projection satisfies
\[
|\Pi_M(x)-t|\le|x-t|;
\]
this follows directly by considering \(x<-2M\), \(x\in[-2M,2M]\), and \(x>2M\).  Therefore the preceding event implies
\(|\Pi_M(\widehat\tau(v^\star))-\tau_W|\le\Psi_\alpha(v^\star)\).  If the radius is below \(2M\), the interval centered at the projected value contains \(\tau_W\), and intersecting with \([-2M,2M]\) does not remove it.  This intersection is nonempty on every path because its center lies in \([-2M,2M]\).  If the radius is at least \(2M\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}aware\text{-}interval}\) returns all of \([-2M,2M]\), which contains \(\tau_W\) surely.  Hence
\[
\inf_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}
P^{K^\star}_{\mathcal D,Y}(\tau_W\in I^\star)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
All ingredients of \(v^\star\) are fixed by the known design and the prespecified schedule class, while \(I^\star\) is a measurable function of the observed scores.  It therefore induces the schedule-independent Dirac Markov kernel in Definition \(\mathrm{def:projected\text{-}kernel}\).

Finally, on the projected branch,
\(\operatorname{len}(I^\star)\le2\Psi_\alpha(v^\star)\); on the full-space branch its length is \(4M\).  Both cases are summarized by
\[
\operatorname{len}(I^\star)
\le2\min\{2M,\Psi_\alpha(v^\star)\}
=2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star
\]
on every path.  Taking expectations and then the supremum over the fixed-schedule class proves the asserted worst-case expected-length bound.  The nonempty construction shows that the claimed empty-intersection fallback event has probability zero.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the constructive half of the borrowing frontier and gives finite-sample design-based coverage using average rather than worst-path information. \textit{Gap.} LeeWason2020 compares all-control and concurrent-only analyses in a two-stage adding-arm platform under parametric linear and step trends, LiZhao2026 proves asymptotic Wald coverage for centered adaptive AIPW, SawadaNomuraShinozaki2026 controls an estimated external-control bias cap, ChenLiZhangYe2025 Theorem 4 gives the minimax confidence-interval-length rate and an attaining upper construction for a Gaussian RCT--observational model under a scalar confounding-bias bound, and KrotkaEtAl2025TemporalDrift studies fixed-effect, mixed-effect, and spline time adjustment through simulated type-I error and power. None supplies an everywhere-nonempty uniform fixed-schedule randomization interval over a TV drift class with an assignment-law-averaged platform-borrowing criterion. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem provides a prespecifiable sensitivity-analysis engine for the NCC package and ACTT-style analyses.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:adaptive-all-interval-converse]\label{oeq:adaptive-all-interval-converse}
Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:rare\text{-}history\text{-}alpha\text{-}only\text{-}obstruction}\), together with Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mixture\text{-}modulus\text{-}length\text{-}lower\text{-}bound}\), shows that on the primitive adaptive-design class no finite constant \(C_\alpha\) depending only on \(\alpha\) can satisfy \[\mathfrak R_{\alpha,\gamma}^\star\le C_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta_\alpha)\] uniformly. Indeed, that comparison and the lemma would imply \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star/\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\ge\eta_\alpha/C_\alpha>0\), whereas the proposition's admissible sequence has \(p_{\min,n}>0\) in every experiment, \(p_{\min,n}\downarrow0\), and \(\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star/\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star\to0\). The honest residual question is the following. Fix \(\underline p\in(0,1/2]\) independently of the block counts, block sizes, drift budget, and experiment, and restrict attention to collections of admissible designs satisfying \(p_{bi}(z\mid h)\ge\underline p\) for every \((b,i)\in\mathcal T\), every \(z\in A_b\), and every compatible history \(h\), with the same \(\underline p\) for the whole collection. Does there exist a finite \(C_{\alpha,\underline p}\), depending only on \(\alpha\) and \(\underline p\), such that, uniformly over \(0\le\gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\) and every design in this fixed-floor subclass, \[\mathfrak R_{\alpha,\gamma}^\star\le C_{\alpha,\underline p}\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta_\alpha)?\] Equivalently, can the fixed-floor condition be used to construct two priors supported almost surely on \(\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\) whose assignment-averaged observed-data mixture laws have total variation at most \(\eta_\alpha\) and whose \(\tau_W\)-supports are separated by at least \(C_{\alpha,\underline p}^{-1}\mathfrak R_{\alpha,\gamma}^\star\), with the sequential likelihood-ratio or deficiency bound derived from the fixed-floor condition rather than by assuming the target separation? This residual question is not a premise of any proved headline result.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The unrestricted comparison is already refuted. This node isolates the strongest directly motivated residual problem suggested by that witness: whether a propensity floor fixed uniformly across the design class is enough to recover a constant-factor comparison, with the constant allowed to depend on that floor. \textit{Gap.} The counterexample uses \(p_{\min,n}\downarrow0\), so it does not decide the fixed-floor subclass. Even there, arbitrary outcome-adaptive propensity updates can reweight future histories, and no schedule-supported bounded-total-variation fuzzy-prior construction currently supplies the required sequential mixture-law bound. \textit{Consumer.} A positive answer would justify constant-factor minimax calibration only for platform trials that enforce one announced propensity floor uniformly over the compared design family. Until then, the NCC implementation must use the optimized radius only as its proved constructive upper scale.

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-frontier]\label{thm:minimax-frontier}
Under the primitive sequential-randomization, availability, positivity, boundedness, and control-total-variation conditions, every admissible experiment satisfies
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le 2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
Let \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\) be the all-zero fixed schedule. The same experiment also has the computable masked-history lower certificate
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge \eta_\alpha
 \max_{\substack{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}:\\ P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\}\ge1-\eta_\alpha}}
 \left\{2M\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}\right\}.
\]
The feasible collection is nonempty and the maximum is computable by finite enumeration. No constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) can strengthen the first lower bound to \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) uniformly over the primitive adaptive-design class. Indeed, for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(M>0\), there is a sequence of admissible experiments with \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a positive propensity floor in every experiment such that \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M\) and \(\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star/\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star\to0\). Coverage and expected length use the same extended design law, and the minimax infimum ranges over every schedule-independent auxiliary-randomized, variable-length observed-data interval kernel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\text{-}minimax\text{-}sandwich}\) directly gives
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
It also gives the claimed absence of an \(\alpha\)-only comparison and, more strongly, constructs a sequence for which
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

It remains only to derive the older thresholded form of the masked certificate from the stronger positive-part form.  Let \(S\) satisfy the probability constraint in the statement and write that probability as \(q_S\).  Since \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\) and \(\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\),
\[
 q_S-2\alpha
 \ge1-\eta_\alpha-2\alpha
 =\eta_\alpha>0.
\]
Therefore the positive-part certificate in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\text{-}minimax\text{-}sandwich}\) gives, for every feasible \(S\),
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+
 \ge\eta_\alpha\,2M
 \sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}.
\]
Taking the maximum over the feasible collection proves the displayed thresholded certificate.  That collection is nonempty because \(S=\varnothing\) has \(w(S)=0\) and \(q_S=1\).  Its finite-enumeration computability, as well as the common-extended-law and all-kernel scope, are also part of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\text{-}minimax\text{-}sandwich}\).  This proves every assertion of the target theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem is the convenient fixed-radius and thresholded-mask corollary of the optimized adaptive minimax sandwich, while retaining the explicit primitive-class separation from the AIPW radius. \textit{Gap.} The proved lower side is an observed-law total-variation bound. A constant-factor comparison with the AIPW radius is false on the primitive class, and the fixed-common-floor comparison remains open and unused. \textit{Consumer.} The NCC implementation may report the projected honest interval, the optimized observed-law and masked-history lower benchmarks, and the quantitative warning that the AIPW radius is only a conservative upper scale on the primitive adaptive class.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-improvement-iff]\label{prop:strict-improvement-iff}
For each \(0\le\gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\) with \(\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(u)<2M\), the certified NCC AIPW radius \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha,\gamma}^\star\) is strictly shorter than the certified concurrent-only AIPW radius \(\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(u)\) if and only if \(u\notin\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)}\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(v)\). This is equivalent to \(0\notin\partial\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(u)+N_{\Delta(\mathcal B)}(u)\), and equivalent to the existence of \(h\in T_u\) with \(\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}'(u;h)<0\). The set \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{cc}}\) and permanent threshold \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{cc}}\) are therefore computed from the same pointwise optimizer certificates without assuming that adaptive average-information terms are monotone in \(\gamma\). This certificate compares the two AIPW constructions; the exact all-kernel concurrent benchmark is \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\gamma\) and abbreviate \(f(v)=\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(v)\) and \(C=\Delta(\mathcal B)\).  For every fixed schedule \(Y\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) writes the expected variation envelope as \(c_Y+\sum_ba_{b,Y}v_b^2\), with nonnegative coefficients uniformly bounded by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\).  Hence its square root is the supremum of the norms
\(\alpha^{-1/2}\sqrt{c_Y+\sum_ba_{b,Y}v_b^2}\), and is convex and uniformly Lipschitz on bounded sets.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:drift\text{-}support\text{-}family}\) makes the bias term a support function of a bounded set, so it too is convex and Lipschitz.  Thus \(f\) is a finite continuous convex function on finite-dimensional space.  Since \(f(u)<2M\) and \(\min_Cf\le f(u)\), clipping is inactive at the optimum, and therefore
\[
\mathfrak R_{\alpha,\gamma}^\star=\min_{v\in C}f(v).
\]
It follows immediately that
\[
\mathfrak R_{\alpha,\gamma}^\star<f(u)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u\notin\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in C}f(v).
\]

For completeness, use the normal-cone convention
\[
N_C(u)=\{n:\langle n,v-u\rangle\le0\text{ for every }v\in C\}.
\]
If \(0=g+n\) with \(g\in\partial f(u)\) and \(n\in N_C(u)\), then for every \(v\in C\),
\[
f(v)\ge f(u)+\langle g,v-u\rangle
=f(u)-\langle n,v-u\rangle\ge f(u),
\]
so \(u\) minimizes \(f\) over \(C\).  For the converse, let
\[
A=\partial f(u),
\qquad
K=-N_C(u)=\{g:\langle g,h\rangle\ge0\text{ for every }h\in T_u\}.
\]
Because \(f\) is finite and continuous on finite-dimensional space, its secant inequalities give a nonempty compact convex subdifferential \(A\), and
\[
f'(u;h)=\max_{g\in A}\langle g,h\rangle.
\]
The latter identity follows directly by taking subgradients at \(u+t h\): local Lipschitz continuity bounds those subgradients; along any sequence \(t\downarrow0\), a convergent subsequence and the two subgradient inequalities at \(u\) and \(u+t h\) sandwich the difference quotient between the limiting inner products.

Suppose that \(u\) minimizes \(f\) on \(C\) but \(A\cap K=\varnothing\).  Choose \(a_0\in A\) and \(k_0\in K\) minimizing \(\|a-k\|\), and put \(w=a_0-k_0\ne0\).  Comparing the minimizing pair with \((a,k_0)\) and \((a_0,k)\), then taking one-sided derivatives of the squared distance, gives
\[
\langle w,a-a_0\rangle\ge0\quad(a\in A),
\qquad
\langle w,k-k_0\rangle\le0\quad(k\in K).
\]
Because \(K\) is a cone, the second inequality with \(k=0\) and \(k=2k_0\) gives \(\langle w,k_0\rangle=0\), and scaling any \(k\in K\) gives \(\langle w,k\rangle\le0\).  Hence, for \(h=-w\),
\[
\sup_{a\in A}\langle a,h\rangle
\le-\langle a_0,w\rangle
=-\|w\|^2<0,
\qquad
\langle k,h\rangle\ge0\quad(k\in K).
\]
The explicit form of \(K\) forces \(\sum_bh_b=0\) and \(h_b\ge0\) whenever \(u_b=0\): constants of both signs belong to \(K\), and so does every nonnegative coordinate vector off the support of \(u\).  Thus \(h\in T_u\).  The directional-derivative identity now gives \(f'(u;h)<0\), contradicting optimality along a sufficiently short feasible segment.  Therefore \(A\cap K\ne\varnothing\); taking \(g\in A\cap K\) gives \(-g\in N_C(u)\), and
\[
u\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in C}f(v)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0\in\partial f(u)+N_C(u).
\]

The cone of feasible one-sided directions at \(u\) is exactly
\[
T_u=\{h:\sum_bh_b=0,\ h_b\ge0\text{ whenever }u_b=0\}.
\]
To verify this, necessity follows from \(u+th\in C\) for arbitrarily small \(t>0\); conversely, for \(h\in T_u\), choosing
\(t>0\) below every positive ratio \(u_b/(-h_b)\) with \(h_b<0\) ensures \(u+th\in C\).  If \(u\) is not a minimizer, choose \(v\in C\) with \(f(v)<f(u)\) and let \(h=v-u\in T_u\).  Convexity of \(t\mapsto f(u+th)\) gives
\[
f'(u;h)\le f(v)-f(u)<0.
\]
Conversely, if \(h\in T_u\) and \(f'(u;h)<0\), the definition of the one-sided directional derivative gives \(f(u+th)<f(u)\) for all sufficiently small positive feasible \(t\).  Hence
\[
u\notin\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in C}f(v)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists h\in T_u:\ f'(u;h)<0.
\]
Combining the three equivalences proves the certificate.  The definitions of \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{cc}}\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{cc}}\) merely apply this certificate separately at each budget; no monotonicity of the schedule supremum in the stochastic term is used.  Finally, the optimization compares the full-record AIPW weight \(v_\gamma^\star\) with the concurrent AIPW weight \(u\); Definition \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}only\text{-}benchmark}\) separately defines the all-kernel value \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A necessary-and-sufficient KKT certificate replaces informal claims that more controls must improve precision and remains valid under outcome-adaptive assignment. \textit{Gap.} BofillRoigEtAl2022 gives a two-period variance reduction under a common-trend regression model, while SantacatterinaEtAl2025 finds no first-order robust gain with deterministic availability; neither characterizes gain under a quantitative drift class and an average adaptive-design criterion. \textit{Consumer.} Software can report a reproducible pointwise borrowing decision and the budget beyond which concurrent-only weighting remains optimal.

\begin{proposition}[prop:boundary-regimes]\label{prop:boundary-regimes}
At \(\gamma=0\), \(\mathsf B_0(v)=0\) for every \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\), and hence \(v_0^\star\) minimizes \(\sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,0}(v)/\alpha}\) over \(\Delta(\mathcal B)\).  If this stochastic radius is strictly convex and
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}\!\left\{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})<1\right\}>0
 \quad\text{for every }Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\text{ and every }(b,i)\in\mathcal T,
\]
then every coordinate of every minimizer, in particular every coordinate of \(v_0^\star\), is strictly positive.  At every \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{cc}}\), the concurrent vector \(u\) minimizes \(\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}\).  For every target budget \(0\le\Gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\),
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}.
\]
Suppose additionally that \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\) and that, at every enrollment position, the predictable propensity vector is measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated by preceding assignments and preceding observed outcomes from blocks in \(W\); thus it is unchanged when off-window potential outcomes are changed while assignments and window potential outcomes are held fixed.  Then every \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) has an \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\)-measurable kernel \(K^{\mathrm{cc}}\in\mathcal C_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\) satisfying
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}}
 \mathbb E^{K^{\mathrm{cc}}}_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_{K^{\mathrm{cc}}})]
 \le
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)].
\]
Consequently \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star=\mathfrak L_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\) under this additional off-window-outcome-ignorability condition.  Finally,
\[
 \widehat\tau(u)=
 \sum_{b\in W}q_b n_b^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_b}
 \{\phi_{bi}(z_1)-\phi_{bi}(z_0)\},
\]
so substitution of \(v=u\) removes every off-window block-score term.  This is an algebraic concurrent-score identity; it is \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\)-measurable when the window score predictions \(m_{bi}(z)\) are themselves \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\)-measurable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First consider \(\gamma=0\).  Since \(v\) and \(u\) are probability vectors,
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^B d_b(v)=\sum_{b=1}^B(v_b-u_b)=0.
\]
Every vector feasible in Definition \(\mathrm{def:drift\text{-}support\text{-}family}\) at budget zero has \(m_{b+1}=m_b\) for every \(b<B\), because a sum of nonnegative absolute increments can vanish only when each increment vanishes.  Thus it is constant, say \(m_b=c\), and
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^B d_b(v)m_b=c\sum_{b=1}^B d_b(v)=0.
\]
Taking the maximum in that definition gives \(\mathsf B_0(v)=0\).  This also follows from Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:tv\text{-}support\text{-}dual}\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}frontier}\) therefore reduces the zero-budget objective to
\[
 \Psi_{\alpha,0}(v)=
 f_0(v):=\sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D,0}(v)/\alpha},
\]
so every selected \(v_0^\star\) minimizes \(f_0\) on the simplex.

We next prove the asserted interiority from the primitive randomization conditions.  Let
\[
 N=\sum_{b=1}^B n_b
\]
and let \(r(b,i)\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) be the lexicographic rank of \((b,i)\).  For a fixed \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}[\mathsf G_{\mathcal D}(v;H)]
 =c_Y+\sum_{b=1}^B a_{b,Y}v_b^2,
\]
where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_Y&=8M^2\mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}
 \sum_{b,i}\frac{u_b^2}{n_b^2}
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_1\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_1\mid H_{bi})},\\
 a_{b,Y}&=\frac{8M^2}{n_b^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n_b}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}\!\left[
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}
 \right].
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first summand is understood to be zero off \(W\), exactly as in the definition of \(\mathsf G_{\mathcal D}\).

Fix \((b,i)\).  Under a fixed schedule there are only finitely many assignment-prefix histories.  The displayed nondegeneracy premise says that the event
\[
 E_{bi,Y}=\{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})<1\}
\]
has positive design probability, so it contains a compatible prefix-history atom \(h\).  By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\), the probability of that atom is the product of the propensities of its \(r(b,i)-1\) preceding realized assignments.  Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}support}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\) give
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D,Y}(H_{bi}=h)\ge p_{\min}^{,r(b,i)-1}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\) makes \(z_0\) available, and hence its propensity is at least \(p_{\min}\).  On \(h\), its propensity is also below one.  Because the finite propensity vector sums to one, some other arm has positive propensity; availability support and the propensity floor then imply
\[
 1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid h)\ge p_{\min}.
\]
As \(p_{bi}(z_0\mid h)\le1\),
\[
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid h)}{p_{bi}(z_0\mid h)}\ge p_{\min}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D,Y}\!\left[
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}
 \right]
 \ge p_{\min}^{,r(b,i)}\ge p_{\min}^{,N}.
\]
The same assumptions give the uniform upper bound
\[
 0\le
 \frac{1-p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}{p_{bi}(z_0\mid H_{bi})}
 \le p_{\min}^{-1}.
\]
Writing \(n_{\max}=\max_b n_b\), we have therefore proved, uniformly in \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\) and \(b\),
\[
 0<a_-:=\frac{8M^2p_{\min}^{,N}}{n_{\max}}
 \le a_{b,Y}
 \le a_+:=\frac{8M^2}{p_{\min}}<\infty.
\]
All divisions are valid because \(M>0\), \(n_b\ge1\), and \(p_{\min}>0\).

Put \(Q_Y(v)=c_Y+\sum_ba_{b,Y}v_b^2\).  Since the square root is increasing on the nonnegative reals,
\[
 f_0(v)^2=\alpha^{-1}\sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}}Q_Y(v).
\]
Suppose a minimizer \(v\) had \(v_j=0\).  Since its coordinates sum to one, some \(k\) has \(v_k>0\).  For
\[
 w(t)=v+t(e_j-e_k),
 \qquad
 0<t<\min\!\left\{v_k,\frac{a_-v_k}{2a_+}\right\},
\]
we have \(w(t)\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\) and, for every \(Y\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_Y(w(t))-Q_Y(v)
 &=a_{j,Y}t^2+a_{k,Y}\{(v_k-t)^2-v_k^2\}\\
 &=-2a_{k,Y}v_kt+(a_{j,Y}+a_{k,Y})t^2\\
 &\le-2a_-v_kt+2a_+t^2\\
 &<-a_-v_kt.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The decrease is uniform in \(Y\), so
\[
 \sup_YQ_Y(w(t))
 \le\sup_YQ_Y(v)-a_-v_kt
 <\sup_YQ_Y(v).
\]
Thus \(f_0(w(t))<f_0(v)\), contradicting minimality.  Every minimizer is therefore interior.  The proof does not need strict convexity; hence it proves the stated implication, whose premise also assumes strict convexity.

Now let \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{cc}}\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:strict\text{-}gain\text{-}certificate}\) gives
\[
 0\in\partial\Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(u)
      +N_{\Delta(\mathcal B)}(u).
\]
Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:strict\text{-}improvement\text{-}iff}\) proves that this pointwise convex optimality certificate is equivalent to
\[
 u\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)}
 \Psi_{\alpha,\gamma}(v).
\]
This proves the no-borrow assertion without any monotonicity premise in \(\gamma\).

For the one-sided comparison of all interval kernels, Definition \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}only\text{-}benchmark}\) gives the literal inclusion
\[
 \mathcal C_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\subseteq\mathcal C_\alpha.
\]
The constant full-range interval belongs to both classes because Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) imply \(\tau_W\in[-2M,2M]\).  Thus both infima are finite, and taking the infimum of the same worst-case expected-length functional over the larger class gives
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}.
\]

We prove the reverse inequality at the vacuous budget under the additional off-window-outcome-ignorability condition.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) implies \(\mu_b\in[-M,M]\), and hence
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^{B-1}|\mu_{b+1}-\mu_b|
 \le 2M(B-1)=\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}.
\]
Consequently, at this budget the schedule class contains every coordinatewise bounded schedule and is rectangular between its window and off-window coordinates.

For a bounded window schedule \(Y_W\), let \(Y^0(Y_W)\) agree with \(Y_W\) at all positions in \(W\) and set every available potential outcome outside \(W\) equal to zero.  The preceding display shows that \(Y^0(Y_W)\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}\), and Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) gives
\[
 \tau_W(Y^0(Y_W))=\tau_W(Y)
\]
whenever \(Y\) has window restriction \(Y_W\).

Under the additional design condition, the law of \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\) depends on a schedule only through \(Y_W\).  Indeed, couple \(Y\) and \(Y^0(Y_W)\) by the same sequential assignment uniforms.  Inductively, equality of the preceding assignments and preceding window outcomes makes the two propensity vectors equal.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) then gives the same next assignment under the coupling, and the observed outcome retained by \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\) is the same whenever the next block lies in \(W\).  Induction over the finite ordered set \(\mathcal T\) proves
\[
 \mathcal L_{\mathcal D,Y}(O^{\mathrm{cc}})
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal D,Y^0(Y_W)}(O^{\mathrm{cc}}).
\]

Let \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\).  Define the measurable completion map \(\iota\) from a concurrent record to a full record by retaining all assignments and window outcomes and imputing the value zero for every observed off-window outcome.  Define
\[
 K^{\mathrm{cc}}(dI\mid r)=K(dI\mid\iota(r)).
\]
This is a schedule-independent Markov kernel measurable with respect to \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\).  Under \(Y^0(Y_W)\), all observed off-window outcomes equal zero, so
\[
 O=\iota(O^{\mathrm{cc}})
\]
on every assignment path.  Combining this pathwise identity with the preceding equality of concurrent-record laws yields, for every \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P^{K^{\mathrm{cc}}}_{\mathcal D,Y}
 \{\tau_W(Y)\in I_{K^{\mathrm{cc}}}\}
 &=P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^0(Y_W)}
 \{\tau_W(Y^0(Y_W))\in I_K\}\\
 &\ge1-\alpha,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the inequality is uniform honesty of \(K\).  Hence \(K^{\mathrm{cc}}\in\mathcal C_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\).  The same law identity gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}}
 \mathbb E^{K^{\mathrm{cc}}}_{\mathcal D,Y}
 [\operatorname{len}(I_{K^{\mathrm{cc}}})]
 &=\sup_{Y_W}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y^0(Y_W)}
 [\operatorname{len}(I_K)]\\
 &\le\sup_{Y'\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}}}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y'}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) proves
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^{\mathrm{cc}}\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star.
\]
Together with the already proved reverse inequality, this gives equality.  Notice why the added condition is load-bearing: without it, replacing off-window outcomes can change later propensity vectors and therefore the distribution of \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\), so the displayed law identity need not hold.

Finally, Definition \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}weights}\) gives \(u_b=q_b\mathbf 1\{b\in W\}\).  Substitution in Definition \(\mathrm{def:borrowing\text{-}estimator}\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\tau(u)
 &=\sum_{b\in W}q_bn_b^{-1}\sum_i\phi_{bi}(z_1)
 -\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}q_b\mathbf 1\{b\in W\}n_b^{-1}\sum_i\phi_{bi}(z_0)\\
 &=\sum_{b\in W}q_bn_b^{-1}\sum_i
 \{\phi_{bi}(z_1)-\phi_{bi}(z_0)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus no off-window block-score is used.  Moreover \(d_b(u)=0\) for every \(b\), so Definition \(\mathrm{def:drift\text{-}support\text{-}family}\) gives \(\mathsf B_\gamma(u)=0\) for every budget.  The score formula in Definition \(\mathrm{def:aipw\text{-}score}\) uses assignments, propensities, observed window outcomes, and the predictions \(m_{bi}(z)\).  The first three are retained in \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\); therefore the displayed contrast is \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\)-measurable when its window predictions are, as claimed.

As a smallest exact check, take \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(n_1=n_2=n_3=1\), and suppose every block has two available arms with fixed probability \(1/2\) each.  For the control component, the displayed coefficient formula gives \(a_{b,Y}=8M^2\) for all \(b\) and all schedules.  At zero drift the control-dependent part of the squared stochastic radius is therefore proportional to
\[
 v_1^2+v_2^2+v_3^2
 =\frac13+\sum_{b=1}^3\left(v_b-\frac13\right)^2,
\]
so its unique minimizer is \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\), confirming the interior conclusion exactly.  For the vacuous-budget reduction in the same three-block experiment, outcome-oblivious propensities make every assignment path have the same probability under \(Y\) and \(Y^0(Y_W)\), while the zero-completion map makes the two full records identical after completion; hence the two displayed coverage and length identities hold path by path.  If the block-two propensity is instead allowed to depend on the omitted block-one outcome, changing that outcome changes the law of \(O^{\mathrm{cc}}\), so this check also locates precisely the boundary at which the reduction proof ceases to apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:three-block-kill-test]\label{prop:three-block-kill-test}
The randomized interval problem for \(\mathcal E_3\) is exactly the finite linear program specified by the three-block LP construction. At \(\alpha=0.1\) and \(\gamma=0\), with all available potential outcomes in \(\{-M,0,M\}\), its minimax expected length is exactly \(\mathfrak L^{(3)}_{0.1,0}=0.7M\). The hull-plus-singletons policy and analytic dual in the zero-drift certificate are respectively primal and dual feasible with common value \(0.7M\). On the control-assignment record each of the three observationally indistinguishable target values has marginal conditional coverage \(0.8\), but auxiliary randomization need not cover the two extreme targets jointly; therefore those marginal constraints do not imply conditional expected length \(1.6M\). No exact value is asserted here for positive drift budgets or for the concurrent-only restriction without a displayed matching primal--dual certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first prove that the unrestricted randomized-interval problem reduces exactly to the stated finite linear program.  Fix \(\gamma\in[0,\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}]\).  By the three-block experiment definition and the bounded-outcomes assumption, a grid-valued schedule is a vector
\[
s=(c_1,c_2,t,c_3)\in\{-M,0,M\}^{4}
\]
satisfying \(|c_2-c_1|+|c_3-c_2|\le\gamma\).  Denote the finite set of such schedules by \(\mathcal S_\gamma\).  By the target definition, its target value is
\[
\tau_s=t-c_2\in\mathcal G_M:=\{-2M,-M,0,M,2M\}.
\]
The randomization in \(\mathcal E_3\) is deterministic outside block \(2\), while the two block-\(2\) arms each have probability \(1/2\).  Hence the attainable record set \(\mathcal R_\gamma\) is finite; write \(P_s(r)\) for the design probability of record \(r\) under schedule \(s\).

Let \(I\subseteq[-2M,2M]\) be any nonempty closed interval and define
\[
d(I)=
\begin{cases}
[\min(I\cap\mathcal G_M),\max(I\cap\mathcal G_M)],&I\cap\mathcal G_M\ne\varnothing,\\
\{-2M\},&I\cap\mathcal G_M=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\]
This is an interval whose endpoints belong to \(\mathcal G_M\).  If \(g\in\mathcal G_M\), then
\[
\mathbf 1\{g\in d(I)\}\ge \mathbf 1\{g\in I\},
\qquad
\operatorname{len}(d(I))\le \operatorname{len}(I).
\]
Indeed, the first inequality is immediate when \(g\in I\), because then \(g\) lies between the smallest and largest elements of \(I\cap\mathcal G_M\); when \(I\cap\mathcal G_M=\varnothing\), its right-hand side is zero for every \(g\in\mathcal G_M\).  For the second inequality, the two extreme points of a nonempty \(I\cap\mathcal G_M\) lie in \(I\), while in the empty-intersection case \(d(I)\) has length zero.

Given any admissible Markov kernel from the honest-minimax-length definition, push its interval action forward through \(d\).  The two displayed inequalities show, schedule by schedule, that this operation weakly increases coverage and weakly decreases expected length.  Conversely, every kernel supported on the finite set
\[
\mathcal A_M:=\{[a,b]:a,b\in\mathcal G_M,\ a\le b\}
\]
is an admissible kernel in the original problem.  Thus restricting the action space to \(\mathcal A_M\) does not change the minimax value.  For \(r\in\mathcal R_\gamma\) and \(I\in\mathcal A_M\), let \(x_{rI}\) be the conditional probability of action \(I\) at record \(r\).  The honest-minimax-length definition then gives exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{minimize }&L,\\
\text{subject to }&x_{rI}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{I\in\mathcal A_M}x_{rI}=1 &&(r\in\mathcal R_\gamma),\\
&\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_\gamma}P_s(r)\sum_{I\in\mathcal A_M}x_{rI}\mathbf 1\{\tau_s\in I\}\ge1-\alpha &&(s\in\mathcal S_\gamma),\\
&\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_\gamma}P_s(r)\sum_{I\in\mathcal A_M}x_{rI}\operatorname{len}(I)\le L &&(s\in\mathcal S_\gamma),\\
&L\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the finite linear program in the three-block LP construction.  The auxiliary uniform randomizer realizes any feasible finite probability vector \((x_{rI})_{I\in\mathcal A_M}\), so the reduction works in both directions and is exact.

We now set \(\alpha=0.1\) and \(\gamma=0\).  By the control-TV assumption, the sum of the two nonnegative increments is at most zero, so both increments vanish.  Therefore \(c_1=c_2=c_3=:c\), and every schedule is uniquely indexed by
\[
s=(c,t)\in\{-M,0,M\}^{2},\qquad \tau_s=t-c.
\]
Under this schedule, the control-assignment record
\[
r_c^0=(c,z_0,c,c)
\]
has probability \(1/2\) and is independent of \(t\), while the treatment-assignment record
\[
r_{c,t}^1=(c,z_1,t,c)
\]
has probability \(1/2\).

Consider the following primal policy from the zero-drift certificate.  At \(r_{c,t}^1\), report the singleton \(\{t-c\}\).  At \(r_c^0\), report
\[
[-M-c,M-c]\quad\text{with probability }\frac7{10},
\]
and report each of the three singletons \(\{-M-c\}\), \(\{-c\}\), and \(\{M-c\}\) with probability \(1/10\).  All endpoints belong to \(\mathcal G_M\), all actions lie in \([-2M,2M]\), and the four probabilities sum to one.  For schedule \((c,t)\), the treatment branch covers \(t-c\) with probability one.  On the control branch, the hull covers \(t-c\), as does exactly the singleton indexed by \(t\), so its conditional coverage is
\[
\frac7{10}+\frac1{10}=\frac8{10}.
\]
Consequently the unconditional coverage is
\[
\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot\frac8{10}=\frac9{10}=1-\alpha.
\]
Only the hull has positive length, and its length is \(2M\).  Thus every schedule has expected length
\[
\frac12\left(\frac7{10}\right)(2M)=\frac7{10}M.
\]
This proves \(\mathfrak L^{(3)}_{0.1,0}\le0.7M\).

For the matching lower bound, first normalize \(M=1\).  This loses no generality because division of every outcome and interval endpoint by the declared positive number \(M\) preserves assignment probabilities and coverage and divides every interval length by \(M\); multiplication by \(M\) gives the inverse transformation.

We verify the dual certificate directly, including weak duality.  For a primal-feasible pair \((x,L)\), write \(C_s\) and \(E_s\) for its coverage and expected length under schedule \(s\).  Suppose numbers \(\lambda_s,\rho_s\ge0\) and unrestricted \(z_r\) satisfy
\[
\sum_sP_s(r)\left\{\lambda_s\mathbf 1\{\tau_s\in I\}-\rho_s\operatorname{len}(I)\right\}+z_r\le0
\]
for every record--action pair and satisfy \(\sum_s\rho_s\le1\).  Multiplication by \(x_{rI}\), summation, primal normalization, the coverage constraints, the length constraints, and \(L\ge0\), in that order, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\ge \sum_{r,I}x_{rI}\left[\sum_sP_s(r)\{\lambda_s\mathbf 1(\tau_s\in I)-\rho_s\operatorname{len}(I)\}+z_r\right]\\
&=\sum_s\lambda_s C_s-\sum_s\rho_sE_s+\sum_rz_r\\
&\ge \frac9{10}\sum_s\lambda_s-L\sum_s\rho_s+\sum_rz_r\\
&\ge \frac9{10}\sum_s\lambda_s-L+\sum_rz_r.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence every such certificate gives
\[
L\ge\frac9{10}\sum_s\lambda_s+\sum_rz_r.
\]

Set \(\lambda_{(0,t)}=1\) for \(t\in\{-1,0,1\}\), set \(\rho_{(0,-1)}=1\), and set every other \(\lambda_s\) and \(\rho_s\) to zero.  Put \(z_r=-1/2\) at \(r_0^0\) and at each of \(r_{0,-1}^1,r_{0,0}^1,r_{0,1}^1\), and put \(z_r=0\) elsewhere.  Then \(\sum_s\rho_s=1\).  At a record outside these four records, every term is zero.  At \(r_0^0\), let
\[
k(I)=|I\cap\{-1,0,1\}|.
\]
The left side of the dual constraint is
\[
\frac{k(I)-\operatorname{len}(I)-1}{2}.
\]
Every grid interval satisfies \(\operatorname{len}(I)\ge k(I)-1\): this is immediate for \(k(I)\le1\), while for \(k(I)\ge2\) the distance between the smallest and largest included points is at least \(k(I)-1\).  Thus the control-record constraint holds.  At \(r_{0,-1}^1\), the left side is
\[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{-1\in I\}-\operatorname{len}(I)-1}{2}\le0.
\]
At \(r_{0,t}^1\) for \(t\in\{0,1\}\), it is
\[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{t\in I\}-1}{2}\le0.
\]
The certificate is therefore dual feasible, and its objective is
\[
\frac9{10}\cdot3+4\left(-\frac12\right)=\frac7{10}.
\]
The direct weak-duality calculation yields \(L\ge0.7\) for every normalized primal procedure.  Together with the primal policy and scale equivariance, this proves
\[
\mathfrak L^{(3)}_{0.1,0}=0.7M.
\]

Finally, conditional on \(r_c^0\), each of the three observationally compatible targets has marginal coverage \(0.8\).  The two extreme targets are covered jointly only when the hull is selected, an event of conditional probability \(0.7\), and the conditional expected length is \((7/10)(2M)=1.4M\), not \(1.6M\).  Thus the displayed feasible policy itself proves that the three marginal constraints do not entail the claimed larger conditional-length bound.  All value calculations above use \(\gamma=0\) and the unrestricted finite action set; they provide no matching primal--dual certificate, and the proposition makes no exact-value claim, for positive drift or for a concurrent-only restriction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:mixture-modulus-length-lower-bound]\label{lem:mixture-modulus-length-lower-bound}
For every \(0\le\eta<1-2\alpha\), every schedule-independent kernel \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}
\mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]
\ge (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
\ge \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), \(0\le\eta<1-2\alpha\), and any admissible pair of priors \(\Pi_0,\Pi_1\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}information\text{-}modulus}\) with target separation \(\Delta\) and mixture-law total variation at most \(\eta\).  Include the independent auxiliary randomizer in the data variable \(X=(O,U_{\mathrm{aux}})\), and denote its two mixture laws by \(Q_0,Q_1\).  Tensoring both laws with the same independent uniform distribution does not change their total variation, so \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\eta\).

For \(j\in\{0,1\}\), let \(a_j(x)\) be the conditional probability, under the \(j\)-th prior and its extended experiment, that the interval \(I_K(x)\) covers the corresponding random value \(\tau_W(Y)\).  Pointwise honesty and averaging over \(\Pi_j\) give
\[
\int a_j\,dQ_j\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Let \(\lambda=Q_0+Q_1\), let \(q_j\) be the density of \(Q_j\) with respect to \(\lambda\), and define the common subprobability measure \(\nu\) by
\(d\nu=\min(q_0,q_1)d\lambda\).  Directly from the definition of total variation,
\[
\nu(\mathsf X)=1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1),
\qquad \nu\le Q_j,
\qquad (Q_j-\nu)(\mathsf X)=\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
If \(\operatorname{len}(I_K(x))<\Delta\), the support-separation condition prevents this interval from containing a target value from both prior supports.  Therefore
\[
\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{len}(I_K(x))\ge\Delta\}
\ge a_0(x)+a_1(x)-1.
\]
Since \(0\le a_j\le1\), domination by the common part gives
\[
\int a_j\,d\nu
\ge\int a_j\,dQ_j-(Q_j-\nu)(\mathsf X)
\ge1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Integrating the indicator inequality against \(\nu\) yields
\[
\nu\{\operatorname{len}(I_K)\ge\Delta\}
\ge1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
\ge1-2\alpha-\eta.
\]
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]
&\ge \int \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]\,d\Pi_0(Y)\\
&=\int\operatorname{len}(I_K(x))\,dQ_0(x)\\
&\ge\int\operatorname{len}(I_K(x))\,d\nu(x)\\
&\ge\Delta(1-2\alpha-\eta).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over all admissible prior pairs and separations, then the infimum over \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), proves the first display.  At \(\eta=\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\), its multiplier is exactly \(\eta_\alpha\), proving the second display.  The proof covers variable-length kernels and auxiliary randomization because both are included in \(X\) and the same extended law is used in the coverage and length calculations.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The overlap-measure argument converts the observed-law testing modulus into a lower bound for every honest randomized variable-length interval. \textit{Gap.} The argument is experiment-generic and isolates the genuinely design-specific task as comparison of the adaptive randomization modulus with the optimized borrowing radius. \textit{Consumer.} It is the all-kernel testing half needed by the minimax frontier theorem.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rare-history-alpha-only-obstruction]\label{prop:rare-history-alpha-only-obstruction}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(M>0\).  There is a sequence of admissible three-block adaptive platform experiments \(\mathcal D_n\), each having \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a strictly positive propensity floor \(p_{\min,n}\), such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}
      {\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently, over the primitive adaptive-design class there is no constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) for which \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) holds uniformly.  The obstruction is a low-propensity history whose probability is below the permitted miscoverage level, not a failure of positivity in any member of the sequence.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 \delta_n=\frac{\varepsilon}{n}.
\]
For every sufficiently large integer \(n\), take \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(q_2=1\), and \((n_1,n_2,n_3)=(1,n,1)\).  Take the platform-arm set \(\mathcal Z_n=\{z_0,z_1,a\}\), where \(a\notin\{z_0,z_1\}\), and set
\[
 A_1=\{z_0,a\},\qquad
 A_2=\{z_0,z_1\},\qquad
 A_3=\{z_0\}.
\]
At the only position in block \(1\), assign \(a\) with probability \(\varepsilon\) and \(z_0\) with probability \(1-\varepsilon\), and define
\[
 E=\{Z_{11}=a\}.
\]
Thus \(E\) is known before block \(2\), and \(P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(E)=\varepsilon\) for every fixed schedule \(Y\).  At every position in block \(2\), set
\[
 p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})=
 \begin{cases}
  \delta_n,&E,\\
  1/2,&E^c,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 p_{2i}(z_0\mid H_{2i})=1-p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i}),
\]
and assign \(z_0\) with probability one in block \(3\); every arm outside the displayed availability set receives probability zero.  These probabilities are measurable with respect to the pre-assignment histories, so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) holds by construction.  Their support is exactly the displayed availability set, and \(z_1\) is available exactly in \(W\), establishing Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}support}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 0<\delta_n\le
 \min\{\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon,1/2,1-\delta_n,1\},
\]
so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\) holds with \(p_{\min,n}=\delta_n>0\).  We use the class \(\mathcal Y_{M,0}\) from Definition \(\mathrm{def:schedule\text{-}family}\): Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}tv}\) require bounded fixed potential outcomes and equal control block means, respectively.  Hence each constructed experiment is in the primitive class.

We first evaluate its frozen optimized radius.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}weights}\) gives \(u_2=1\) and \(u_1=u_3=0\).  The focal-arm summand in Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) is schedule independent because its propensity is a function only of \(E\).  All other summands in \(\mathsf G_{\mathcal D_n}\) are nonnegative.  Therefore, for every \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}(v)
 &\ge
 8M^2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n^2}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 \left[
  \frac{1-p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})}
       {p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})}
 \right]\\
 &=\frac{8M^2}{n}
 \left\{
  \varepsilon\frac{1-\delta_n}{\delta_n}
  +(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1-1/2}{1/2}
 \right\}\\
 &=8M^2\left\{1+\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{n}\right\}
 \ge 8M^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The expectation is the same for every \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\), so it remains a valid lower bound after the schedule supremum defining \(\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}\).  The middle equality is also the rare-branch assignment-law averaging identity in Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:rare\text{-}history\text{-}stress\text{-}test}\).  Since \(\mathsf B_0(v)\ge0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}frontier}\) and \(\alpha<1/2\) imply
\[
 \Psi_{\alpha,0}(v)
 \ge \sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}(v)/\alpha}
 \ge M\sqrt{8/\alpha}>2M.
\]
This holds for every feasible \(v\), and clipping in that definition therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M.
\]

We next construct an honest observed-data interval with vanishing worst-case expected length.  Work under the conditional design law given \(E^c\).  Since \(E\) is determined before block \(2\), it belongs to every \(H_{2i}\).  Define the observable variables
\[
 X_i=2\mathbf 1\{Z_{2i}=z_1\}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{2i}
     -2\mathbf 1\{Z_{2i}=z_0\}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{2i},
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i.
\]
On \(E^c\), both available arms have conditional probability \(1/2\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) and the observation map therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[X_i\mid H_{2i},E^c]
 =Y_{2i}(z_1)-Y_{2i}(z_0)
\]
and, by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[X_i^2\mid H_{2i},E^c]
 =2Y_{2i}(z_1)^2+2Y_{2i}(z_0)^2
 \le4M^2.
\]
Because \(q_2=1\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) identifies the average of the first display with \(\tau_W\).  Hence
\[
 \Delta_i=X_i-\{Y_{2i}(z_1)-Y_{2i}(z_0)\}
\]
is a martingale difference under the conditional law given \(E^c\).  If \(i<j\), then \(\Delta_i\) is \(H_{2j}\)-measurable, so iterated conditioning gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[\Delta_i\Delta_j\mid E^c]
 =\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 \left[
  \Delta_i\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[\Delta_j\mid H_{2j},E^c]
  \,\middle|\,E^c
 \right]=0.
\]
The increments are orthogonal.  Conditional variance is bounded by conditional second moment, so, uniformly over \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 [ (\widehat\theta_n-\tau_W)^2\mid E^c]
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
   \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[\Delta_i^2\mid E^c]\\
 &\le\frac{4M^2}{n}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Set
\[
 \beta=\frac{\alpha-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}
       =\frac{\alpha/2}{1-\alpha/2}>0,
 \qquad
 t_n=\frac{2M}{\sqrt{n\beta}}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(t_n<2M\).  Define a deterministic, schedule-independent observed-data kernel that reports the singleton \(\{0\}\) on \(E\), and on \(E^c\) reports
\[
 J_n=
 [\Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)-t_n,
   \Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)+t_n]
 \cap[-2M,2M].
\]
This is always a nonempty closed interval because its projected center belongs to \([-2M,2M]\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) give \(\tau_W\in[-2M,2M]\).  The metric projection inequality used in Definition \(\mathrm{def:bias\text{-}aware\text{-}interval}\) therefore gives
\[
 |\Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)-\tau_W|
 \le|\widehat\theta_n-\tau_W|.
\]
For \(t_n>0\), the pointwise inequality \(\mathbf 1\{|x|>t_n\}\le x^2/t_n^2\) and the preceding second-moment bound imply
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(\tau_W\notin J_n\mid E^c)
 \le\frac{4M^2/n}{t_n^2}=\beta.
\]
We make no coverage claim on \(E\).  Nevertheless, because \(P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(E)=\varepsilon\) for every schedule,
\[
 P^K_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(\tau_W\in I_K)
 \ge(1-\varepsilon)(1-\beta)=1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the kernel belongs to \(\mathcal C_\alpha\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\).  Its singleton branch has length zero and \(\operatorname{len}(J_n)\le2t_n\), whence, uniformly over the schedule class,
\[
 \mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star
 \le(1-\varepsilon)2t_n
 =\frac{4M(1-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{n\beta}}.
\]
Combining this inequality with \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M\) yields
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}
      {\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{n\beta}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Every member of the sequence satisfies all primitive assumptions with \(p_{\min,n}=\varepsilon/n>0\).  If a positive comparison constant depending only on \(\alpha\) existed, applying it to this sequence and taking the displayed limit would be a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:optimized-mixture-envelope]\label{lem:optimized-mixture-envelope}
Every experiment in the target class satisfies the optimized observed-law total-variation lower envelope
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 \sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
In particular, for \(\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\),
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0\le\eta<1-2\alpha\).  For every \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:mixture\text{-}modulus\text{-}length\text{-}lower\text{-}bound}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]
 \ge
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
The right-hand side depends on the experiment and \(\eta\), but not on \(K\).  Taking the infimum over \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) and using Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta).
\]
This inequality holds for every \(\eta\) in the displayed nonempty range; hence taking the supremum over \(\eta\) proves the first claim.  Assumption \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) gives
\[
 0<\eta_\alpha=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}<1-2\alpha,
 \qquad
 1-2\alpha-\eta_\alpha=\eta_\alpha.
\]
Substitution of this admissible value in the pointwise inequality proves the convenient corollary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This takes the pointwise all-kernel testing inequality over every admissible total-variation radius, rather than fixing one convenient radius. \textit{Gap.} It is an observed-law total-variation lower envelope; it does not compare the modulus with the predictable-AIPW radius. \textit{Consumer.} The adaptive minimax sandwich uses this optimized lower side and retains the fixed-radius display as a corollary.

\begin{lemma}[lem:masked-focal-profile]\label{lem:masked-focal-profile}
For \(0\le\gamma\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{vac}}\), let \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\) be the all-zero schedule.  For every \(S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}\), put
\[
 w(S)=\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b},
 \qquad
 E_S=\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\},
 \qquad
 q_S=P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(E_S).
\]
There are two schedules \(Y^-,Y^+\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\) with
\[
 \tau_W(Y^-)=-Mw(S),
 \qquad
 \tau_W(Y^+)=Mw(S),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^-},P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^+}\right)=1-q_S.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(1-q_S)\ge2Mw(S).
\]
The number \(q_S\) is computable by finite enumeration of the known adaptive assignment tree.  In particular, if \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\), then \(\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta_\alpha)\ge2Mw(S)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\gamma\) and \(S\) as in the statement.  Define two fixed schedules by
\[
 Y^-_{bi}(z_1)=-M,
 \qquad
 Y^+_{bi}(z_1)=M
 \quad\text{for }(b,i)\in S,
\]
and set every other available potential outcome, including every control outcome and every focal outcome outside \(S\), equal to zero under both schedules.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) holds because all displayed values lie in \([-M,M]\).  Under either schedule, every control block mean equals zero, and hence
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^{B-1}|\mu_{b+1}-\mu_b|=0\le\gamma.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:schedule\text{-}family}\) therefore gives \(Y^-,Y^+\in\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\) ensures that \(z_1\) is available at every position in \(S\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) then gives
\[
 \tau_W(Y^-)=-M\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}=-Mw(S),
 \qquad
 \tau_W(Y^+)=Mw(S).
\]

We next identify the observed-law overlap exactly.  On an assignment prefix that has not assigned \(z_1\) at a position in \(S\), every outcome observed so far under \(Y^-\), \(Y^+\), and \(Y^{(0)}\) is zero.  Starting from the empty history and proceeding through the finite lexicographic order on \(\mathcal T\), suppose such prefixes agree.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) then evaluates the same known probability vector \(p_{bi}(\cdot\mid H_{bi})\) at the same observed history under all three schedules.  Induction over the enrollment positions proves
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^-}(E_S)
 =P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^+}(E_S)
 =P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(E_S)=q_S,
\]
and it also proves equality of the restrictions of the first two observed-data laws to \(E_S\).

On \(E_S^c\), let \((b,i)\) be the first position in \(S\) assigned to \(z_1\).  At that position the full observed record contains \(-M\) under \(Y^-\) and \(M\) under \(Y^+\).  Since \(M>0\), the restrictions of the two laws to \(E_S^c\) have disjoint supports, irrespective of how this different observed outcome changes later predictable propensities.  Thus the two probability laws have a common restriction of mass \(q_S\) and mutually singular complementary restrictions of mass \(1-q_S\).  The finite-space definition of total variation gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^-},P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^+}\right)=1-q_S.
\]

In Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}information\text{-}modulus}\), choose the point-mass priors \(\Pi_0=\delta_{Y^-}\) and \(\Pi_1=\delta_{Y^+}\).  Their supports lie in \(\mathcal Y_{M,\gamma}\), their observed-law total variation is \(1-q_S\), and their target supports are separated by \(2Mw(S)\).  Hence
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(1-q_S)\ge2Mw(S).
\]
If \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\), then \(1-q_S\le\eta_\alpha\).  The feasible prior pairs in the definition of \(\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta)\) are nested as \(\eta\) increases, so
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \ge\Omega_{\mathcal D,\gamma}(1-q_S)
 \ge2Mw(S).
\]

Finally, let \(\mathcal A_S\) be the finite collection of complete assignment paths that avoid assigning \(z_1\) at every position in \(S\).  If \(h^0_{bi}(z)\) is the earlier assignment prefix along path \(z\), together with its zero observed outcomes, sequential randomization gives
\[
 q_S
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal A_S}
   \prod_{(b,i)\in\mathcal T}
   p_{bi}(z_{bi}\mid h^0_{bi}(z)).
\]
The enrollment set and all availability sets are finite and every propensity is known, so this sum is explicitly computable.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The two explicit focal schedules identify the mask overlap exactly, permitting the testing radius to adapt to each mask probability. \textit{Gap.} This is a computable sufficient testing certificate, not a necessary characterization of the full observed-law modulus. \textit{Consumer.} The package can enumerate mask probabilities and evaluate the resulting positive-part lower certificate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rare-history-quantitative-obstruction]\label{prop:rare-history-quantitative-obstruction}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(M>0\).  There is a sequence of admissible experiments \(\mathcal D_n\) with \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a strictly positive propensity floor in every experiment such that, for all sufficiently large integers \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
Thus the ratio tends to zero, and no constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) can satisfy \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) uniformly over the primitive adaptive-design class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{\alpha}{2},
 \qquad
 \delta_n=\frac{\varepsilon}{n},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{\alpha-\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}
       =\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}.
\]
For each positive integer \(n\), take \(B=3\), \(W=\{2\}\), \(q_2=1\), and block sizes \((n_1,n_2,n_3)=(1,n,1)\).  Let the arm set be \(\{z_0,z_1,a\}\), with
\[
 A_1=\{z_0,a\},
 \qquad
 A_2=\{z_0,z_1\},
 \qquad
 A_3=\{z_0\}.
\]
At the unique position in block \(1\), assign \(a\) with probability \(\varepsilon\) and \(z_0\) with probability \(1-\varepsilon\), and let \(E=\{Z_{11}=a\}\).  At every position in block \(2\), set
\[
 p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})=
 \begin{cases}
  \delta_n,&E,\\
  1/2,&E^c,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 p_{2i}(z_0\mid H_{2i})=1-p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i}),
\]
and assign \(z_0\) with probability one in block \(3\).  Arms outside the displayed availability sets receive probability zero.

The event \(E\) is observed before block \(2\), so these probabilities are predictable, and \(P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(E)=\varepsilon\) for every fixed schedule \(Y\).  The construction therefore satisfies Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\).  The displayed support and availability sets verify Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}support}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\).  Since \(0<\delta_n\le\varepsilon<1/2\), every available-arm probability is at least \(p_{\min,n}=\delta_n>0\), verifying Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\text{-}floor}\).  We use the schedule class \(\mathcal Y_{M,0}\), whose members have bounded outcomes by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and equal control block means by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:control\text{-}tv}\).  Thus every experiment is admissible under the primitive conditions.

Definition \(\mathrm{def:concurrent\text{-}weights}\) gives \(u_2=1\) and \(u_1=u_3=0\).  The focal-arm part of the scale in Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}design\text{-}scale}\) depends only on \(E\), and every omitted part is nonnegative.  Hence, for every \(v\in\Delta(\mathcal B)\) and every schedule in \(\mathcal Y_{M,0}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}(v)
 &\ge
 \frac{8M^2}{n}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 \left[
  \frac{1-p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})}
       {p_{2i}(z_1\mid H_{2i})}
 \right]\\
 &=\frac{8M^2}{n}
 \left\{
  \varepsilon\frac{1-\delta_n}{\delta_n}
  +(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1-1/2}{1/2}
 \right\}\\
 &=8M^2\left\{1+\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{n}\right\}
 \ge8M^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In the first line the common expectation is multiplied by the sum of the \(n\) coefficients \(n^{-2}\), which is \(n^{-1}\).  Since \(\mathsf B_0(v)\ge0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:average\text{-}frontier}\) and \(\alpha<1/2\) give, for every feasible \(v\),
\[
 \Psi_{\alpha,0}(v)
 \ge\sqrt{\overline{\mathsf V}_{\mathcal D_n,0}(v)/\alpha}
 \ge M\sqrt{8/\alpha}>2M.
\]
The clipping in that definition therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M.
\]

It remains to upper-bound the all-interval minimax length.  On \(E^c\), define the observable variables
\[
 X_i=2\mathbf 1\{Z_{2i}=z_1\}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{2i}
     -2\mathbf 1\{Z_{2i}=z_0\}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{2i},
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i.
\]
The two block-\(2\) arms have conditional probabilities \(1/2\) on \(E^c\).  Sequential randomization and the observation map imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[X_i\mid H_{2i},E^c]
 =Y_{2i}(z_1)-Y_{2i}(z_0).
\]
Bounded outcomes give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}[X_i^2\mid H_{2i},E^c]
 =2Y_{2i}(z_1)^2+2Y_{2i}(z_0)^2
 \le4M^2.
\]
Because \(q_2=1\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) identifies the average of the conditional means with \(\tau_W\).  Thus the centered summands are martingale differences under the conditional design law given \(E^c\).  For \(i<j\), iterated conditioning gives zero cross moment, and conditional variance is bounded by conditional second moment.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,Y}
 \left[(\widehat\theta_n-\tau_W)^2\mid E^c\right]
 \le\frac{4M^2}{n}
\]
uniformly over \(Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,0}\).

Set \(t_n=2M/\sqrt{n\beta}\).  For every sufficiently large \(n\), \(t_n<2M\).  Consider the deterministic schedule-independent interval kernel that reports the singleton \(\{0\}\) on \(E\), and on \(E^c\) reports
\[
 J_n=
 [\Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)-t_n,
   \Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)+t_n]
 \cap[-2M,2M].
\]
This is a nonempty closed interval.  Bounded outcomes and Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) imply \(\tau_W\in[-2M,2M]\), while projection onto this interval gives
\[
 |\Pi_M(\widehat\theta_n)-\tau_W|
 \le|\widehat\theta_n-\tau_W|.
\]
Applying the pointwise inequality \(\mathbf 1\{|x|>t_n\}\le x^2/t_n^2\) to the conditional second-moment bound yields
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(\tau_W\notin J_n\mid E^c)
 \le\frac{4M^2/n}{t_n^2}=\beta.
\]
We claim no coverage on \(E\).  Nevertheless,
\[
 P^K_{\mathcal D_n,Y}(\tau_W\in I_K)
 \ge(1-\varepsilon)(1-\beta)=1-\alpha
\]
for every schedule.  Hence this kernel belongs to \(\mathcal C_\alpha\).  Its interval length is zero on \(E\) and at most \(2t_n\) on \(E^c\), so Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star
 \le(1-\varepsilon)2t_n
 =\frac{4M(1-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{n\beta}}.
\]
Dividing by \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M\) and substituting \(\varepsilon=\alpha/2\) and \(\beta=\alpha/(2-\alpha)\) proves
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
The right-hand side converges to zero.  If a positive \(c_\alpha\) lower-compared \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\) throughout the primitive class, applying it to this sequence and passing to the limit would give \(c_\alpha\le0\), a contradiction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit rate shows quantitatively how a rare low-propensity branch separates a second-moment AIPW radius from unrestricted variable-length honest intervals. \textit{Gap.} The witness has a positive floor in every finite experiment but its floors shrink with the sequence, so it does not decide the fixed-common-floor subclass. \textit{Consumer.} The optimized radius must be reported as a conservative implementable scale on the primitive adaptive class, not as a constant-factor minimax length.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-minimax-sandwich]\label{thm:adaptive-minimax-sandwich}
Under the primitive sequential-randomization, availability, positivity, boundedness, and control-total-variation conditions, every admissible experiment obeys the adaptive minimax sandwich
\[
 \sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta)
 \le \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star.
\]
For the all-zero schedule \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\), define, for every \(S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}\),
\[
 w(S)=\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b},
 \qquad
 q_S=P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\}.
\]
Then the sharper computable certificate is
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 \max_{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}}
 2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+,
 \qquad [x]_+=\max\{x,0\}.
\]
The maximum is computable by finite enumeration of the known adaptive assignment tree.  No constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) lower-compares \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\) uniformly on the primitive adaptive class.  Indeed, there is an admissible three-block sequence with \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a positive propensity floor in every experiment such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
Coverage and expected length in every assertion use the same extended design law, and the minimax infimum ranges over all schedule-independent auxiliary-randomized, variable-length observed-data interval kernels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:optimized\text{-}mixture\text{-}envelope}\) proves the optimized lower envelope and its \(\eta_\alpha\) corollary.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}upper\text{-}frontier}\) constructs one schedule-independent kernel \(K^\star\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) whose worst-schedule expected length is at most \(2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\).  Taking the infimum over all \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) gives the upper half of the sandwich.

For the masked certificate, fix \(S\) and abbreviate its displayed probability by \(q_S\).  If \(q_S>2\alpha\), put \(\eta_S=1-q_S\).  Since \(0\le q_S\le1\),
\[
 0\le\eta_S<1-2\alpha,
 \qquad
 1-2\alpha-\eta_S=q_S-2\alpha>0.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:masked\text{-}focal\text{-}profile}\), used at \(\gamma=\Gamma\), gives
\[
 \Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_S)\ge2Mw(S).
\]
The pointwise lower bound proved in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:optimized\text{-}mixture\text{-}envelope}\) therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge(q_S-2\alpha)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_S)
 \ge2Mw(S)(q_S-2\alpha).
\]
If \(q_S\le2\alpha\), the claimed right-hand side is zero, while interval lengths and hence \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) are nonnegative by Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\).  Thus, in both cases,
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\ge2Mw(S)[q_S-2\alpha]_+.
\]
The collection of subsets of the finite focal-window enrollment set is finite, so taking its maximum preserves the inequality.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:masked\text{-}focal\text{-}profile}\) expresses every \(q_S\) as a finite sum of products of known sequential propensities; hence the maximum is computable by finite enumeration.

Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:rare\text{-}history\text{-}quantitative\text{-}obstruction}\) supplies the displayed three-block sequence and quantitative ratio bound.  Since its right-hand side tends to zero, any uniform inequality \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(c_\alpha>0\) would contradict that sequence.  Finally, Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) defines both coverage and length under \(P^K_{\mathcal D,Y}\), includes the independent auxiliary randomizer in the interval action, and takes the infimum over every schedule-independent observed-data Markov kernel of the stated type.  This verifies the procedural scope in the last sentence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the strengthened headline supported by the primitive adaptive class: an optimized observed-law lower envelope, a constructive AIPW upper radius, a computable mask certificate, and a quantitative separation between the latter radius and unrestricted minimax length. \textit{Gap.} A comparison of the modulus with the AIPW radius remains open only after imposing one fixed propensity floor across the entire design family; it is not a premise here. \textit{Consumer.} The NCC package can report the projected interval and enumerate the lower certificate while labeling the optimized radius as a conservative constructive scale.

\begin{lemma}[lem:tonelli-nonnegative]\label{lem:tonelli-nonnegative}
Let \((X_1,\Sigma_1,\mu_1)\) and \((X_2,\Sigma_2,\mu_2)\) be \(\sigma\)-finite measure spaces.  If \(f:X_1\times X_2\to[0,\infty]\) is measurable, then
\[
 \int_{X_1\times X_2} f\,d(\mu_1\otimes\mu_2)
 =\int_{X_1}\!\left(\int_{X_2}f(x_1,x_2)\,d\mu_2(x_2)\right)d\mu_1(x_1)
 =\int_{X_2}\!\left(\int_{X_1}f(x_1,x_2)\,d\mu_1(x_1)\right)d\mu_2(x_2).
\]
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the nonnegative product-integration theorem used to exchange expectation and Lebesgue integration in the common-mass length argument. \textit{Gap.} None; only the stated nonnegative, measurable, \(\sigma\)-finite case is used. \textit{Consumer.} The masked-continuum lemma applies the theorem to an interval-membership indicator under one extended probability law and finite Lebesgue measure.

\begin{lemma}[lem:masked-continuum-common-mass]\label{lem:masked-continuum-common-mass}
Let \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\) be the all-zero schedule.  For every
\(S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}\), define
\[
 w(S)=\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b},
 \qquad
 E_S=\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\},
 \qquad
 q_S=P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(E_S).
\]
If \(w(S)>0\), then, for each \(t\in[-Mw(S),Mw(S)]\), the fixed schedule
\[
 Y^{(t)}_{bi}(z_1)=\frac{t}{w(S)}\quad ((b,i)\in S),
 \qquad
 Y^{(t)}_{bi}(z)=0\quad\text{for every other }(b,i,z)\text{ with }z\in A_b
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\) and satisfies \(\tau_W(Y^{(t)})=t\).  For every \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), the extended laws \(P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}\) have a common subprobability restriction \(\nu_{S,K}\) on \(E_S\), of mass \(q_S\), and
\[
 \nu_{S,K}\{t\in I_K\}\ge[q_S-\alpha]_+
 \qquad\text{for every }t\in[-Mw(S),Mw(S)].
\]
Consequently, for every reference \(t_0\in[-Mw(S),Mw(S)]\),
\[
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t_0)}}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]
 \ge 2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+.
\]
The same last inequality is valid when \(w(S)=0\).  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 \max_{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}}
 2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+,
\]
and this finite maximum is computable by enumeration of the known adaptive assignment tree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(S\).  First suppose \(w(S)>0\), and let \(t\in[-Mw(S),Mw(S)]\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:availability\text{-}window}\) ensures that \(z_1\in A_b\) at every \((b,i)\in S\), so the displayed schedule is well typed.  Moreover,
\[
 \left|Y^{(t)}_{bi}(z_1)\right|=\frac{|t|}{w(S)}\le M
 \quad ((b,i)\in S),
\]
and every other available potential outcome is zero.  Thus the boundedness condition in Definition \(\mathrm{def:schedule\text{-}family}\) holds.  Every control potential outcome is zero, so every control block mean is \(\mu_b=0\), and therefore
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^{B-1}|\mu_{b+1}-\mu_b|=0\le\Gamma.
\]
The nonnegativity of \(\Gamma\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:schedule\text{-}family}\) give \(Y^{(t)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:target}\) now yields, equation by equation,
\[
 \tau_W(Y^{(t)})
 =\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}\frac{t}{w(S)}
 =\frac{t}{w(S)}w(S)
 =t.
\]

We next establish common extended-law mass.  Fix a complete assignment path belonging to \(E_S\).  Along that path, no position in \(S\) reveals its \(z_1\)-potential outcome; all other potential outcomes under every \(Y^{(t)}\), including the all-zero schedule \(Y^{(0)}\), equal zero.  Hence all schedules produce exactly the same observed outcome after every assignment on this path.  Starting at the empty history, suppose the assignment and observed-outcome prefixes agree through the position preceding \((b,i)\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) then gives the same next-assignment probability
\[
 p_{bi}(z\mid H_{bi})
\]
under every \(Y^{(t)}\), because the realized history \(H_{bi}\) is the same.  Induction over the finite lexicographic order on \(\mathcal T\) proves that the restrictions of all observed-data laws \(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}\) to \(E_S\) coincide with the restriction of \(P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}\).  Their common mass is therefore
\[
 P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}(E_S)
 =P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(E_S)
 =q_S.
\]

Now fix one schedule-independent kernel \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\).  Under Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\), the auxiliary variable \(U_{\mathrm{aux}}\) has the same independent uniform law under every schedule, and \(I_K=F_K(O,U_{\mathrm{aux}})\) uses the same measurable map \(F_K\).  Tensoring the common restricted observed law with this uniform law and pushing it forward through the common map preserves equality of restrictions.  Thus, on the extended data--action space, define
\[
 \nu_{S,K}(A)
 =P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}(A\cap E_S)
\]
for every measurable event \(A\), with \(E_S\) understood as its pullback from the observed record.  The preceding equality proves, for every admissible \(t\),
\[
 \nu_{S,K}(A)=P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}(A\cap E_S),
 \qquad
 \nu_{S,K}(\mathsf X)=q_S,
\]
where \(\mathsf X\) denotes the extended sample space.  In particular, \(\nu_{S,K}\le P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}\) as measures.

Because \(Y^{(t)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\), \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), and \(\tau_W(Y^{(t)})=t\), uniform honesty gives
\[
 P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}\{t\in I_K\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The complement of the mask has probability \(1-q_S\), so
\begin{align*}
 \nu_{S,K}\{t\in I_K\}
 &=P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}\{t\in I_K,E_S\}\\
 &\ge P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}\{t\in I_K\}
       -P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t)}}(E_S^c)\\
 &\ge (1-\alpha)-(1-q_S)
 =q_S-\alpha.
\end{align*}
The left side is nonnegative, proving
\[
 \nu_{S,K}\{t\in I_K\}\ge[q_S-\alpha]_+.
\]

Fix any \(t_0\in[-Mw(S),Mw(S)]\).  Since \(I_K\) is a measurable random closed interval contained in \([-2M,2M]\), its length obeys the pathwise identity
\[
 \operatorname{len}(I_K)
 =\int_{-2M}^{2M}\mathbf 1\{s\in I_K\}\,ds.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative and jointly measurable.  The probability measure \(P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t_0)}}\) and Lebesgue measure restricted to \([-2M,2M]\) are finite, hence \(\sigma\)-finite.  Applying Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:tonelli\text{-}nonnegative}\), then using \(P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t_0)}}\ge\nu_{S,K}\), gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t_0)}}[\operatorname{len}(I_K)]
 &=\int_{-2M}^{2M}
 P^K_{\mathcal D,Y^{(t_0)}}\{s\in I_K\}\,ds\\
 &\ge\int_{-Mw(S)}^{Mw(S)}\nu_{S,K}\{s\in I_K\}\,ds\\
 &\ge\int_{-Mw(S)}^{Mw(S)}[q_S-\alpha]_+\,ds\\
 &=2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+.
\end{align*}
Thus the worst-schedule expected length of this arbitrary \(K\) is at least the displayed quantity.  Taking the infimum over \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), as prescribed by Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\), proves
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\ge2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+.
\]

If \(w(S)=0\), the right side is zero; interval lengths are nonnegative, so the same inequality holds without dividing by \(w(S)\).  There are finitely many subsets of the finite focal-window enrollment set.  Taking their maximum therefore preserves the inequality.  Finally, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:masked\text{-}focal\text{-}profile}\) expresses each \(q_S\) as a finite sum of products of known sequential propensities along the all-zero assignment tree, establishing computability.

For the smallest nontrivial check, take one focal-window enrollment position and let \(S\) contain only that position.  The assignment tree partitions exhaustively into \(E_S\), of mass \(q_S\), and its complement, of mass \(1-q_S\).  On the first branch all schedules in the continuum reveal zero and hence have the common restriction above; on the second branch the coverage contribution is at most \(1-q_S\).  The preceding subtraction is therefore exact at the branch level and again leaves the common-event requirement \([q_S-\alpha]_+\), so the one-position enumeration does not revert to the two-point coefficient \([q_S-2\alpha]_+\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The continuum construction converts common masked-history probability into an integrated all-parameter coverage requirement, improving the two-point loss from \(2\alpha\) to \(\alpha\). \textit{Gap.} This is a computable lower certificate and is not asserted to characterize the full observed-law modulus or the optimized predictable-AIPW radius. \textit{Consumer.} The NCC implementation can enumerate \(q_S\) and report \(2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+\) for every focal mask.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-minimax-sandwich-sharp-mask]\label{thm:adaptive-minimax-sandwich-sharp-mask}
Under the primitive sequential-randomization, availability, positivity, boundedness, and control-total-variation conditions, every admissible experiment obeys the adaptive minimax sandwich
\[
 \sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta)
 \le \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \eta_\alpha\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta_\alpha)
 \le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star.
\]
For the all-zero schedule \(Y^{(0)}\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}\), define, for every \(S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}\),
\[
 w(S)=\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b},
 \qquad
 q_S=P^O_{\mathcal D,Y^{(0)}}\{Z_{bi}\ne z_1\text{ for every }(b,i)\in S\}.
\]
Then the sharper computable common-mass certificate is
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge
 \max_{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}}
 2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+,
 \qquad [x]_+=\max\{x,0\}.
\]
Consequently, the thresholded \(\eta_\alpha\)-corollary is
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
 \ge \frac12
 \max_{\substack{S\subseteq\{(b,i)\in\mathcal T:b\in W\}:\\
 q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha}}
 \left\{2M\sum_{(b,i)\in S}\frac{q_b}{n_b}\right\}.
\]
Both maxima are computable by finite enumeration of the known adaptive assignment tree, and the feasible collection in the second display contains \(S=\varnothing\).  No constant \(c_\alpha>0\) depending only on \(\alpha\) lower-compares \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) with \(\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\) uniformly on the primitive adaptive class.  Indeed, there is an admissible three-block sequence with \(W=\{2\}\), \(\Gamma=0\), and a positive propensity floor in every experiment such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star=2M,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak L_{\alpha,n}^\star}{\mathfrak R_{\alpha,n}^\star}
 \le
 \frac{2(1-\alpha/2)}{\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}}.
\]
Coverage and expected length in every assertion use the same extended design law, and the minimax infimum ranges over all schedule-independent auxiliary-randomized, variable-length observed-data interval kernels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:optimized\text{-}mixture\text{-}envelope}\) proves
\[
 \sup_{0\le\eta<1-2\alpha}
 (1-2\alpha-\eta)\Omega_{\mathcal D,\Gamma}(\eta)
 \le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star
\]
and, at \(\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\), its displayed fixed-radius corollary.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}upper\text{-}frontier}\) constructs a schedule-independent kernel \(K^\star\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) with
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{M,\Gamma}}
 \mathbb E^{K^\star}_{\mathcal D,Y}[\operatorname{len}(I^\star)]
 \le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(K\in\mathcal C_\alpha\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) gives \(\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\le2\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\), completing the sandwich.

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:masked\text{-}continuum\text{-}common\text{-}mass}\) applies to every focal mask \(S\) and gives directly
\[
 \mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\ge2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+.
\]
The set of masks is finite, so maximizing over \(S\) proves the common-mass certificate, and the same lemma proves finite-tree computability.  For the thresholded corollary, if \(q_S\ge1-\eta_\alpha\), then the definition of \(\eta_\alpha\) gives
\[
 q_S-\alpha
 \ge1-\eta_\alpha-\alpha
 =1-\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}-\alpha
 =\frac12>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
 2Mw(S)[q_S-\alpha]_+
 \ge\frac12\,2Mw(S).
\]
Taking the maximum over threshold-feasible masks proves the stated factor-\(1/2\) display.  The empty mask has \(w(\varnothing)=0\), \(E_\varnothing\) equal to the whole observed-record space, and \(q_\varnothing=1\), so this feasible collection is nonempty.

Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:rare\text{-}history\text{-}quantitative\text{-}obstruction}\) supplies the stated admissible three-block sequence and its quantitative ratio bound.  The right side of that bound converges to zero.  Hence, if a constant \(c_\alpha>0\) satisfied \(c_\alpha\mathfrak R_\alpha^\star\le\mathfrak L_\alpha^\star\) for every experiment in the primitive adaptive class, applying it along the sequence would give a positive lower bound \(c_\alpha\) for a ratio that converges to zero, a contradiction.  Finally, Definition \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}minimax\text{-}length}\) places both coverage and length under the same extended law, includes independent auxiliary randomization, and ranges over every schedule-independent observed-data interval kernel.  This verifies the procedural scope of the last sentence without using the open fixed-common-propensity-floor question.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the sharp-mask adaptive headline: it combines the optimized observed-law lower envelope, the constructive AIPW upper radius, the continuum common-mass certificate, and the quantitative separation of that radius from unrestricted minimax length. \textit{Gap.} A comparison of the modulus with the AIPW radius remains open only after imposing one fixed propensity floor across the entire design family; that residual question is not a premise. \textit{Consumer.} The NCC package can report the projected interval and the sharper \([q_S-\alpha]_+\) certificate while labeling the optimized radius as a conservative constructive scale.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The optimized predictable-AIPW radius is a valid constructive upper scale but is not the unrestricted minimax expected-length scale. The quantitative counterexample exploits an \(\alpha/2\)-probability branch whose focal propensity is \(\alpha/(2n)\): the second-moment radius clips at \(2M\), whereas an honest variable-length interval has ratio at most \(2(1-\alpha/2)/\sqrt{n\alpha/(2-\alpha)}\). Every experiment is positive, but the floor decreases with \(n\). Fixing one floor across a design family removes this witness without making outcome-adaptive assignment likelihoods comparable; schedule-supported fuzzy priors with a uniform sequential likelihood-ratio bound remain unavailable. The exact masked positive-part expression is a computable lower certificate, not a necessary characterization of the modulus. The vacuous-drift reduction still requires structural invariance of propensity updates to off-window outcome changes.

\section{Honest scope}

The causal target is the window-weighted finite-population focal-arm-versus-control contrast, and assignment is the experiment's only source of randomness. The projected AIPW interval is uniformly honest under the primitive support, boundedness, and drift conditions without regression consistency or an assumed limit law. Every schedule-independent auxiliary-randomized variable-length observed-data interval obeys the optimized observed-law total-variation lower envelope and the finite-tree positive-part mask certificate. No universal \(\alpha\)-only equivalence with the optimized AIPW radius is claimed; the explicit three-block rate refutes it while retaining positivity in each experiment. Because that sequence has shrinking floors, the sole residual converse question fixes a common \(\underline p>0\) and allows its constant to depend on that floor; it is open and absent from the proved dependency closure. Comparator results are templates or analogies, not class-inclusion converses. The KKT result concerns AIPW radii. Zero drift leaves information-optimal borrowing, while the vacuous-drift all-kernel equality requires structural invariance of propensity updates to off-window potential-outcome changes. No claim is made that the drift budget is learned, that the mask certificate is necessary, or that the fixed-floor question is solved.
