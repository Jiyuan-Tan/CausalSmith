%%% FIELD proof-support-rank
Write \(q_a=\Pi_{s_a}\), \(D_a=D_{s_a}\), and \(\overline D=\sum_aq_aD_a=M(\Pi)\).  For arbitrary matrices \(X_a\) of the same dimension and \(\overline X=\sum_aq_aX_a\), expansion of both sides gives the finite weighted-variance identity
\[
 \sum_aq_a(X_a-\overline X)^{\top}J(X_a-\overline X)
 =\frac12\sum_{a,b}q_aq_b(X_a-X_b)^{\top}J(X_a-X_b).
\]
Indeed, the left side and the right side both equal
\[
 \sum_aq_aX_a^{\top}JX_a-\overline X^{\top}J\overline X,
\]
where \(\sum_aq_a=1\) is used.  Taking \(X_a=D_a\), deleting the zero terms with \(a=b\), and combining the two equal terms for \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\), the definition of \(G(\Pi)\) gives
\[
 G(\Pi)
 =\frac1T\sum_{a<b}q_aq_b(D_a-D_b)^{\top}J(D_a-D_b)
 =\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab}.
\]

For the loading numerator, fix \((t,\ell)\), put \(x_a=(D_a)_{t,\ell}\), and use the corresponding finite cross-covariance identity
\[
 \sum_aq_a(D_a-\overline D)^{\top}Je_tx_a
 =\sum_{a<b}q_aq_b(D_a-D_b)^{\top}Je_t(x_a-x_b).
\]
This identity follows by expanding the right side as
\[
 \sum_aq_aD_a^{\top}Je_tx_a-\overline D^{\top}Je_t\sum_aq_ax_a,
\]
which is also the expansion of the left side.  Since \(R_{s_a}=J(D_a-\overline D)\), \(x_a-x_b=(E_{ab})_{t,\ell}\), and \(E_{ab}=D_a-D_b\), division by \(T\) yields
\[
 h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bb_{ab,t,\ell}.
\]

The matrix \(J\) is symmetric and idempotent.  Hence, for every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 v^{\top}B_{ab}v
 =T^{-1}(E_{ab}v)^{\top}J(E_{ab}v)
 =T^{-1}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2.
\]
The probability floor gives \(q_aq_b\geq\epsilon^2>0\).  It follows from the pair representation that
\[
 v^{\top}G(\Pi)v=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 JE_{ab}v=0\ \text{for every }a<b.
\]
The same sum-of-squares calculation without the weights gives
\[
 v^{\top}H_{\mathcal S}v
 =T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \ker G(\Pi)=\bigcap_{a<b}\ker(JE_{ab})=\ker H_{\mathcal S}.
\]
The right side depends on the support and not on the positive values of the \(q_a\).  Thus \(G(\Pi)\) is nonsingular for one positive law exactly when it is nonsingular for every positive law on \(\mathcal S\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-calendar-lag
All conditional expectations below exist by the definitions of \(\mu_t(s,x)\) and the population averages \(\tau_{t,\ell}\).  By path overlap, \(\pi_s(X)\geq c_{\pi}>0\), so every inverse propensity in \(\theta(\Pi)\) is well defined.  Conditional on \(X\), expand over the finite support:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\left.\frac{\Pi_W}{\pi_W(X)}A_W(\Pi)Y\right|X\right]
 &=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\pi_s(X)\frac{\Pi_s}{\pi_s(X)}A_s(\Pi)
       \mathbb E_P[Y\mid W=s,X]\\
 &=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sA_s(\Pi)\mu(s,X).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses consistency, which gives \(Y_t=Y_t(s)\) on \(\{W=s\}\), and pathwise conditional mean random assignment, which gives
\(
\mathbb E_P[Y_t(s)\mid W=s,X]=\mu_t(s,X)
\)
for every \(t\).

Let \(m(X)=(m_1(X),\ldots,m_T(X))^{\top}\).  The calendar-memory condition is the vector identity
\[
 \mu(s,X)=m(X)+\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{\ell=0}^p
 e_t\tau_{t,\ell}(X)(D_s)_{t,\ell}.
\]
Moreover, the definitions of \(R_s(\Pi)\) and \(M(\Pi)\) imply
\[
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)
 =J\left\{\sum_s\Pi_sD_s-M(\Pi)\sum_s\Pi_s\right\}=0.
\]
Consequently the unrestricted baseline cancels exactly:
\[
 \sum_s\Pi_sA_s(\Pi)m(X)
 =T^{-1}G(\Pi)^{-1}\left\{\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}\right\}m(X)=0.
\]
For each effect cell, the remaining coefficient is
\[
 \sum_s\Pi_sA_s(\Pi)e_t(D_s)_{t,\ell}
 =G(\Pi)^{-1}\left\{T^{-1}\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}e_t(D_s)_{t,\ell}\right\}
 =L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi).
\]
Taking expectation over \(X\), using the finite sums and \(\tau_{t,\ell}=\mathbb E_P[\tau_{t,\ell}(X)]\), proves
\[
 \theta(\Pi)=\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{\ell=0}^pL_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)\tau_{t,\ell}.
\]
The inverse is legitimate by the residual full-rank assumption; equivalently, the support-rank lemma shows that the common support kernel is trivial.

It remains to derive the displayed Green formulas.  Under the auxiliary finite law that assigns probability \(\Pi_s\) to path \(s\), define
\[
 Z_u(s)=\mathbf 1\{a(s)\leq u\},
\]
with \(Z_u=0\) for \(u\leq0\).  Absorption and the boundary convention imply
\[
 (D_s)_{t,j}=Z_{t-j}(s).
\]
Thus, directly from the definition of \(F_{\Pi}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi}(Z_u,Z_v)
 =F_{\Pi}(\min\{u,v\})-F_{\Pi}(u)F_{\Pi}(v)
 =K_{\Pi}(u,v).
\]
Writing \(J_{tv}=\mathbf 1\{t=v\}-T^{-1}\), expansion of \(G\) as a covariance across paths gives, for \(j,r\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_{jr}(\Pi)
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{v=1}^TJ_{tv}
       \operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi}(Z_{t-j},Z_{v-r})\\
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TK_{\Pi}(t-j,t-r)
   -T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{v=1}^TK_{\Pi}(t-j,v-r).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Likewise, the \(j\)-th coordinate of the loading numerator is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_{t,\ell,j}(\Pi)
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^TJ_{vt}
       \operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi}(Z_{v-j},Z_{t-\ell})\\
 &=T^{-1}\left\{K_{\Pi}(t-j,t-\ell)
       -T^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^TK_{\Pi}(v-j,t-\ell)\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Finally, if \(e_{\ell}\in\mathbb R^d\) denotes the lag-coordinate vector, then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{t=1}^Th_{t,\ell}(\Pi)
 &=T^{-1}\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}D_se_{\ell}\\
 &=T^{-1}\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}\{D_s-M(\Pi)\}e_{\ell}\\
 &=T^{-1}\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}R_s(\Pi)e_{\ell}
 =G(\Pi)e_{\ell}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality again uses \(\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)=0\); the third uses \(R_s=J(D_s-M)\), symmetry and idempotence of \(J\).  Multiplication by \(G(\Pi)^{-1}\) therefore yields
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^TL_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=e_j^{\top}e_{\ell}=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-homogeneous
Suppose the stated calendar-homogeneity restriction holds and write its common value at lag \(\ell\) as \(\overline\tau_{\ell}\).  The calendar-lag loading theorem and its normalization identity give, coordinate by coordinate,
\[
 \theta_j(\Pi)
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^p\sum_{t=1}^TL_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)\overline\tau_{\ell}
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^p\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}\overline\tau_{\ell}
 =\overline\tau_j.
\]
Hence \(\theta_j(\Pi)=\tau_{t,j}\) at every legal calendar date \(t\).  This is the homogeneous distributed-lag recovery asserted by the cited comparator theorem, while the equality here has been derived directly from this core's loading formula.

For strictness, take the support and law in the statement, in the displayed order.  The three lag-design matrices and their mean are
\[
 D_{000}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_{001}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_{011}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1/3&0\\2/3&1/3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With
\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&-1/3&-1/3\\-1/3&2/3&-1/3\\-1/3&-1/3&2/3\end{pmatrix},
\]
substitution into the definitions of \(R_s\), \(G\), and \(h\) gives
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}2/27&0\\0&4/81\end{pmatrix},
\]
and the only nonzero loading vectors are
\[
 L_{\cdot,2,0}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\-1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L_{\cdot,3,0}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L_{\cdot,3,1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For completeness, these follow by multiplying \(G^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(27/2,81/4)\) by
\[
 h_{2,0}=\begin{pmatrix}1/27\\-2/81\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 h_{3,0}=\begin{pmatrix}1/27\\2/81\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 h_{3,1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\4/81\end{pmatrix},
\]
while all other numerator vectors are zero.  The causal representation therefore becomes
\[
 \theta_0=\frac{\tau_{2,0}+\tau_{3,0}}2,
 \qquad
 \theta_1=\frac{-\tau_{2,0}+\tau_{3,0}}2+\tau_{3,1}.
\]
The second coordinate depends on two distinct calendar-specific current effects even after the lag-one effect is held fixed.  Thus outside calendar homogeneity the coefficient vector is not determined by a time-homogeneous lag-effect vector, establishing the asserted strict extension.

%%% FIELD proof-forward
All entries of every listed \(D_s\) and \(E_{ab}\) are integers, and
\(
J=I_T-\mathbf 1_T\mathbf 1_T^{\top}/T
\)
has rational entries.  Hence \(B_{ab}\), \(b_{ab,t,\ell}\), and \(H_{\mathcal S}\) are rational matrices or vectors computable by finitely many rational operations.

Perform exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb Q\) on \(H_{\mathcal S}\).  This terminates because the matrix has the finite dimension \(d\).  If it is singular, the elimination supplies a nonzero vector \(v\in\mathbb Q^d\) with \(H_{\mathcal S}v=0\).  The sum-of-squares identity in the support-rank lemma then gives
\[
 0=v^{\top}H_{\mathcal S}v
   =T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2,
\]
so \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every pair.  This is the required exact singularity certificate.

Otherwise \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is nonsingular.  The support-rank lemma and the probability floor \(\Pi_s\geq\epsilon>0\) imply that \(G(\Pi)\) is nonsingular.  Since \(\Pi\) is rational, the pair identities
\[
 G(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}B_{ab},
 \qquad
 h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}b_{ab,t,\ell}
\]
show that all entries of \(G\) and \(h\) are rational.  Exact elimination computes \(G^{-1}\), and hence every rational entry
\[
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)=G(\Pi)^{-1}h_{t,\ell}(\Pi).
\]
The rational weights \(\omega_{\ell,t}\) make every entry of \(Q\) rational.  For each of the finitely many triples \((j,t,\ell)\), comparison of the rational number \(L_{j,t,\ell}-Q_{j,t,\ell}\) with zero returns its exact sign, including equality.  Taking its rational absolute value and summing gives the exact rational value of \(\mathcal C(\Pi,Q)\).  Thus both branches terminate and return precisely the certificate claimed.

%%% FIELD statement-adjacent-sign-rule
Order the distinct absorbing paths as \(\mathcal S=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\), write \(u_a=a(s_a)\), and require \(u_a<u_b\) whenever \(a<b\).  Suppose the support-rank condition \(\ker H_{\mathcal S}=\{0\}\) holds.  For a law satisfying \(\Pi_{s_a}\geq\epsilon>0\), define \(\rho_a=\Pi_{s_{a+1}}/\Pi_{s_a}\), \(z_1=1\), \(z_a=\prod_{r=1}^{a-1}\rho_r\), and \(Z=\sum_{a=1}^kz_a\).  Define the endpoint matrices \(I_{ab}\in\{0,1\}^{T\times d}\) by \((I_{ab})_{r,j}=\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq r-j<u_b\}\), and set
\[
 \Gamma(\rho)=T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}z_az_bI_{ab}^{\top}JI_{ab},\qquad
 g_{t,\ell}(\rho)=T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}z_az_bI_{ab}^{\top}Je_t(I_{ab})_{t,\ell}.
\]
Then \(\det\Gamma(\rho)>0\).  If \(\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)\) is the determinant of \(\Gamma(\rho)\) after replacing its \(j\)-th column by \(g_{t,\ell}(\rho)\), then, for every \(j,\ell\in\{0,ldots,p\}\) and \(t\in\{1,ldots,T\}\),
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=\operatorname{sgn}\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho),
 \qquad
 L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)=0.
\]
In particular, all off-diagonal causal-lag loadings vanish if and only if
\[
 \Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)=0
 \quad\text{for every }t\in\{1,ldots,T\}\text{ and every }j\ne\ell.
\]
The floor is equivalently \(z_a\geq\epsilon Z\) for every \(a\).  Thus the displayed determinant equalities and strict sign inequalities are a necessary-and-sufficient rule using only adjacent adoption-mass ratios and the ordered interval endpoints.  They remain valid when shifted boundary coordinates repeat or fall outside the panel.  On rationally represented inputs their evaluation is a terminating exact rational-arithmetic procedure.

%%% FIELD proof-adjacent-sign-rule
An absorbing binary path is uniquely determined by its adoption date: for \(u=a(s)\), one has \(s_r=\mathbf 1\{u\leq r\}\), with \(u=T+1\) for the never-treated path.  Distinct paths therefore have distinct adoption dates, so the asserted strict ordering exists.  For \(a<b\), absorption and the convention that treatment is zero before the panel give, for every \(r\) and \(j\),
\[
 (E_{ab})_{r,j}
 =(D_{s_a})_{r,j}-(D_{s_b})_{r,j}
 =\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq r-j\}-\mathbf 1\{u_b\leq r-j\}
 =\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq r-j<u_b\}.
\]
Hence \(E_{ab}=I_{ab}\), including when \(r-j\leq0\), when an endpoint is \(T+1\), or when several shifted arguments produce the same cumulative coordinate.  No inverse of a matrix of distinct cumulative coordinates is required.

Positivity makes every adjacent ratio well defined.  Recursion gives
\[
 \frac{\Pi_{s_a}}{\Pi_{s_1}}=\prod_{r=1}^{a-1}\rho_r=z_a.
\]
Since the probabilities sum to one, this implies
\[
 \Pi_{s_a}=\frac{z_a}{Z},
 \qquad
 \Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}=\frac{z_az_b}{Z^2}.
\]
Substituting these identities and \(E_{ab}=I_{ab}\) into the pair-mass formulas of the support-rank lemma yields
\[
 G(\Pi)=Z^{-2}\Gamma(\rho),
 \qquad
 h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=Z^{-2}g_{t,\ell}(\rho).
\]
The support-rank condition and positivity imply \(G(\Pi)\succ0\); therefore \(\Gamma(\rho)=Z^2G(\Pi)\succ0\) and \(\det\Gamma(\rho)>0\).  The common positive scale cancels from the loading equation:
\[
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)=\Gamma(\rho)^{-1}g_{t,\ell}(\rho).
\]

To avoid invoking an undeclared inversion rule, expand the determinant of a matrix along its \(j\)-th column.  The cofactor vector in that expansion is the \(j\)-th row of the adjugate, and the same cofactor expansion applied after replacing column \(j\) by \(g_{t,\ell}\) gives
\[
 e_j^{\top}\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)g_{t,\ell}
 =\Delta_{j,t,\ell}.
\]
The elementary cofactor identity \(\Gamma\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)=\det(\Gamma)I_d\), verified entrywise by the same expansion and by cancellation when two columns coincide, then gives
\[
 L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)
 =\frac{\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)}{\det\Gamma(\rho)}.
\]
Because the denominator is strictly positive, the asserted sign and zero equivalences follow.  Taking the conjunction over the finite set of cells with \(j\ne\ell\) proves the off-diagonal characterization.

Finally, \(\Pi_{s_a}=z_a/Z\) shows directly that \(\Pi_{s_a}\geq\epsilon\) is equivalent to \(z_a\geq\epsilon Z\).  Each entry of \(I_{ab}\) is determined solely by the integer endpoints \((u_a,u_b)\); each \(z_a\) is a monomial in adjacent ratios.  Thus every entry of \(\Gamma\) and \(g\), and every determinant \(\Delta\), is an explicit finite polynomial in those ratios with rational coefficients fixed by the endpoints.  When the endpoints and ratios are rational, finite exact determinant evaluation and rational sign comparison terminate.  This also proves that repeated or boundary Green coordinates cause no exceptional stratum: they merely repeat, or zero out, rows of the already defined interval matrices.
