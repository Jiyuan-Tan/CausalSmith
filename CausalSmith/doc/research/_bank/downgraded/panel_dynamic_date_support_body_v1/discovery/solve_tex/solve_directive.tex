%%% FIELD construction-positive-loading-body-two-case
Define the full-rank positive loading body by
\[
 \mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}
 :=
 \begin{cases}
 \left\{\mathsf X\in\mathbb R^{d\times T\times d}:
 \text{there exists }q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)
 \text{ such that }
 G(q)\mathsf X_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
 \text{ for every }t,\ell\right\},
 & \det H_{\mathcal S}\neq0,\\[6pt]
 \varnothing,
 & \det H_{\mathcal S}=0,
 \end{cases}
\]
where
\[
 G(q):=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab},
 \qquad
 h_{t,\ell}(q):=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bb_{ab,t,\ell},
\]
with \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\) and \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\).
%%% FIELD statement-exact-loading-body-inverse-optimization
Fix integer \(T,p\), an explicitly listed binary support \(\mathcal S=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\), rational \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon\leq1/k\), and a rational target tensor \(Q\).  The following exact procedure terminates.

First it computes \(H_{\mathcal S}\).  If \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is singular, it returns a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Q^d\) such that \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every \(a<b\); then \(G(q)\) is singular for every \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\) and \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}=\varnothing\).

If \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is nonsingular, then
\[
 H_{\mathcal S}\succ0,
 \qquad
 G(q)\succeq\epsilon^2H_{\mathcal S},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}\{G(q)\}\geq
 \epsilon^2\lambda_{\min}(H_{\mathcal S})>0
\]
for every \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\).  The body \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\) is then a compact semialgebraic set, with no convexity assertion.  Cylindrical algebraic decomposition decides exactly whether \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\) from the existential polynomial formula
\[
 \exists q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\quad
 G(q)Q_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
 \quad\text{for every }t,\ell.
\]
In the feasible case it returns a witnessing law as exact real-algebraic data, which need not be rational.

In the nonsingular branch, exact real-algebraic optimization returns
\[
 c^{\star}(Q)=\min_{q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)}\mathcal C(q,Q),
\]
an algebraic minimizing law, its closest loading tensor, and a cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition certificate of the universal lower bound.  Membership holds if and only if \(c^{\star}(Q)=0\).  If \(c^{\star}(Q)>0\), the procedure also returns a rational isolating interval for \(c^{\star}(Q)\) with positive lower endpoint; together with the universal lower-bound certificate, this is a metric-separation certificate.

For every rational \(B\geq0\),
\[
 W_B(q,Q)
 =B\max_{0\leq j\leq p}
       \sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{\ell=0}^p
       |L_{j,t,\ell}(q)-Q_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]
The procedure computes this value exactly at a closest law.  If the minimizer of \(\mathcal C(\cdot,Q)\) is not unique, it returns, by a secondary exact optimization, a law minimizing \(W_B(\cdot,Q)\) over all closest laws.  The procedure is exact and terminating on every finite rational input, but generic cylindrical algebraic decomposition can be doubly exponential in the number of variables.
%%% FIELD proof-exact-loading-body-inverse-optimization
Write \(q_a\) for the coordinate assigned to \(s_a\).  The definitions of \(D_s\), \(J\), and the pair contributions show that \(B_{ab}\), \(b_{ab,t,\ell}\), and \(H_{\mathcal S}\) are rational matrices or vectors computable by finitely many exact operations.  Moreover, because \(J=J^{\top}=J^2\), for every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 x^{\top}B_{ab}x
 =\frac1T x^{\top}E_{ab}^{\top}JE_{ab}x
 =\frac1T\lVert JE_{ab}x\rVert_2^2\geq0.
\]
Thus every \(B_{ab}\) and their sum \(H_{\mathcal S}\) are positive semidefinite.

Suppose first that \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is singular.  Rational Gaussian elimination returns a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Q^d\) with \(H_{\mathcal S}v=0\).  The preceding sum-of-squares identity gives
\[
 0=v^{\top}H_{\mathcal S}v
 =\frac1T\sum_{a<b}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2,
\]
so \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every pair.  For every \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\), the established pair identity yields
\[
 G(q)v=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab}v=0,
\]
because \(B_{ab}v=T^{-1}E_{ab}^{\top}J(JE_{ab}v)=0\).  Hence every such \(G(q)\) is singular.  The defining condition \(\det H_{\mathcal S}\neq0\) for the full-rank body fails, so \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}=\varnothing\).  This proves the first branch and its rational certificate.

Now suppose that \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is nonsingular.  A nonsingular positive-semidefinite symmetric matrix is positive definite, so \(H_{\mathcal S}\succ0\).  If \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\), then \(q_aq_b\geq\epsilon^2\) for every pair.  Therefore, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x^{\top}\{G(q)-\epsilon^2H_{\mathcal S}\}x
 &=\sum_{a<b}(q_aq_b-\epsilon^2)x^{\top}B_{ab}x\\
 &\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \(G(q)\succeq\epsilon^2H_{\mathcal S}\).  Taking the infimum of the Rayleigh quotient over \(\lVert x\rVert_2=1\) gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{G(q)\}
 \geq\epsilon^2\lambda_{\min}(H_{\mathcal S})>0.
\]
Thus every \(G(q)\) is invertible, uniformly over the probability-floor domain.

The set \(\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\) is nonempty because its uniform point has coordinates \(1/k\geq\epsilon\).  It is closed and bounded in the finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^k\), hence compact.  The maps
\[
 q\longmapsto G(q)=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab},
 \qquad
 q\longmapsto h_{t,\ell}(q)=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bb_{ab,t,\ell}
\]
are polynomial.  The displayed eigenvalue bound keeps \(\det G(q)\) away from zero, so the adjugate formula
\[
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(q)
 =\frac{\operatorname{adj}\{G(q)\}h_{t,\ell}(q)}{\det G(q)}
\]
is continuous on the whole domain.  The polynomial graph in the definition of \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\) has exactly one tensor \(\mathsf X=L(q)\) over each \(q\).  Consequently
\[
 \mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}
 =\{L(q):q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\}
\]
is the continuous image of a compact set and is compact.  The graph is described by the rational polynomial equalities
\[
 G(q)\mathsf X_{\cdot,t,\ell}-h_{t,\ell}(q)=0
\]
and by the linear equality and inequalities defining \(\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\).  The cited cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition theorem applied to the existential projection onto \(\mathsf X\) therefore proves that the body is semialgebraic and computes a defining quantifier-free formula.  Compactness does not imply convexity, and no convexity step has been used.

Substitution of the rational tensor \(Q\) for \(\mathsf X\) gives the finite first-order formula
\[
 \exists(q_1,\ldots,q_k)\quad
 \left[
   \sum_{a=1}^kq_a=1
   \ \wedge\ 
   \bigwedge_{a=1}^k q_a\geq\epsilon
   \ \wedge\ 
   \bigwedge_{t=1}^T\bigwedge_{\ell=0}^p
   G(q)Q_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
 \right].
\]
Every scalar equality in this formula is polynomial with rational coefficients.  Cylindrical algebraic decomposition therefore terminates and decides it.  When it is true, projection and lifting return a sample point in the feasible cell.  Its coordinates are represented exactly by defining polynomials together with isolating intervals and signs; such a real-algebraic witness need not have rational coordinates.

It remains to certify the closest point.  On the compact domain, \(q\mapsto L(q)\) is continuous, and a finite sum of absolute values is continuous.  Hence \(q\mapsto\mathcal C(q,Q)\) attains its minimum \(c^{\star}(Q)\).  To expose the exact polynomial optimization, introduce a tensor \(\mathsf X\), nonnegative auxiliary variables \(u_{j,t,\ell}\), and a scalar \(z\), and form the formula
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi(z):\quad \exists q,\mathsf X,u\quad
 &q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S),\\
 &G(q)\mathsf X_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
       &&\text{for every }t,\ell,\\
 &u_{j,t,\ell}\geq \mathsf X_{j,t,\ell}-Q_{j,t,\ell},\quad
  u_{j,t,\ell}\geq Q_{j,t,\ell}-\mathsf X_{j,t,\ell}
       &&\text{for every }j,t,\ell,\\
 &\sum_{j,t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}\leq z.
\end{aligned}
\]
For fixed \(q\), the smallest possible value of the displayed sum is exactly
\[
 \sum_{j,t,\ell}|L_{j,t,\ell}(q)-Q_{j,t,\ell}|
 =\mathcal C(q,Q).
\]
It follows in both directions that
\[
 \Phi(z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there exists }q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)
 \text{ with }\mathcal C(q,Q)\leq z.
\]
Cylindrical algebraic decomposition eliminates \(q,\mathsf X,u\) and decomposes the real \(z\)-line into cells on which \(\Phi\) has constant truth value.  Since the minimum is attained, the least true endpoint is \(c^{\star}(Q)\); the endpoint is real algebraic and is returned with defining polynomial and rational isolating interval.  Lifting over the endpoint returns an algebraic minimizer \(q^{\star}\) and its exact loading \(L(q^{\star})\).  The same sign-invariant decomposition and truth table certify
\[
 \neg\exists q,\mathsf X,u\quad
 \left[
  q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S),\quad
  G(q)\mathsf X=h(q),\quad
  u\geq\pm(\mathsf X-Q),\quad
  \sum_{j,t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}<c^{\star}(Q)
 \right],
\]
which is the promised universal lower-bound certificate.

Because \(\mathcal C(q,Q)\geq0\), membership of \(Q\) gives \(c^{\star}(Q)=0\).  Conversely, if \(c^{\star}(Q)=0\), attainment gives a \(q^{\star}\) for which every nonnegative summand in \(\mathcal C(q^{\star},Q)\) is zero, so \(L(q^{\star})=Q\) and \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\).  If \(c^{\star}(Q)>0\), exact algebraic-number isolation returns rationals \(r_-<c^{\star}(Q)<r_+\) with \(r_->0\).  The universal certificate implies
\[
 \lVert \mathsf X-Q\rVert_1\geq c^{\star}(Q)>r_-
 \quad\text{for every }\mathsf X\in\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon},
\]
so \((r_-,r_+)\), the minimizing witness, and the cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition truth certificate separate \(Q\) metrically from the body.  This conclusion does not assert the existence of a linear separating hyperplane.

Finally fix \(B\geq0\), put
\[
 a_{j,t,\ell}=L_{j,t,\ell}(q)-Q_{j,t,\ell},
\]
and let \(|\zeta_{t,\ell}|\leq B\).  For each row \(j\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{t,\ell}a_{j,t,\ell}\zeta_{t,\ell}\right|
 \leq B\sum_{t,\ell}|a_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(j\) and then the supremum over \(\zeta\) proves the upper bound
\[
 W_B(q,Q)\leq
 B\max_j\sum_{t,\ell}|a_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]
Choose a maximizing row \(j^{\star}\) and set
\[
 \zeta_{t,\ell}=B\operatorname{sgn}(a_{j^{\star},t,\ell}),
\]
with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\).  This admissible effect array attains the right-hand side in row \(j^{\star}\), proving
\[
 W_B(q,Q)=B\max_j\sum_{t,\ell}|a_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]

To make tie-breaking among closest laws exact, describe absolute values by nonnegative variables \(u_{j,t,\ell}\) satisfying
\[
 u_{j,t,\ell}^2=(\mathsf X_{j,t,\ell}-Q_{j,t,\ell})^2
\]
and impose \(\sum_{j,t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}=c^{\star}(Q)\).  This is precisely the compact semialgebraic set of closest laws and their loadings.  Introduce \(w\) with
\[
 w\geq B\sum_{t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}
 \quad\text{for every }j
\]
and minimize \(w\) over that set.  The same elimination and lifting argument returns an algebraic minimizer and the exact secondary optimum, which equals \(W_B\) by the formula just proved.  All formulas use finitely many rational polynomials; algebraic endpoints from the first optimization can equivalently be constrained by their defining polynomials and isolating sign conditions.  Thus the entire procedure terminates.  The cited general algorithm is potentially doubly exponential in the number of variables, so this proof supplies an exact finite-instance inverse analysis, not a closed ordered/combinatorial chamber law or a polynomial-time guarantee.
