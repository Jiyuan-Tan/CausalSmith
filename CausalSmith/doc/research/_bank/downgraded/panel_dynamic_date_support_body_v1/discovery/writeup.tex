\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For any finite binary-path support, the paper identifies the reshaped distributed-lag fixed-effect coefficient as an exact linear functional of calendar-specific current and carryover effects and gives support-only rank and exact forward diagnostics. On the full-rank probability-floor domain, the complete loading image is a compact semialgebraic body. A terminating exact real-algebraic procedure decides target membership and returns either an algebraic feasible law or an attained closest loading, a metric-separation certificate, and exact worst-case contamination over coordinatewise bounded effects; generic cylindrical algebraic decomposition is potentially doubly exponential. The separate published unit-indexed layer retains the tensor approximation rate \(O_P(n^{-q/2})\), with a block witness showing that no uniformly faster coefficient rate follows from its stated conditions. The remaining open problem is a support-uniform ordered/combinatorial description of chamber counts, signs, incidences, and simultaneous off-diagonal-zero strata, not finite-instance decidability.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published RIPW theory establishes dynamic consistency under calendar-homogeneous effects and leaves its calendar-varying DATE equation beyond scope. Its class and the base i.i.d. conditional-mean layer remain incomparable. Existing event-study decompositions and supplied-law numerical diagnostics do not provide the complete finite-memory calendar-by-lag loading body, exact inverse membership, certified closest loading, or exact bounded-effect contamination on an arbitrary listed support.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing objects are an arbitrary-path loading tensor shared algebraically across two explicitly separated sampling layers and an exact finite-instance inverse analysis that respects probability-product compatibility on every exceptional stratum. Ordered absorption structure remains useful for a sharper structural chamber law, but is not needed for exact decidability.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves arbitrary-path covariance and pair-mass identities, exact normalization, rational supplied-law diagnostics, and the positive-law support-rank law. It encodes the complete full-rank probability-floor loading body by its polynomial probability-vector graph, proves compact semialgebraicity and uniform invertibility, and uses exact real-algebraic elimination to return membership witnesses or an attained closest loading with a metric-separation certificate and exact bounded-effect worst-case contamination. The generic algorithm terminates but may be doubly exponential and does not replace the open ordered/combinatorial chamber classification. In the separate published triangular-array layer, the calendar-varying tensor approximation remains \(O_P(n^{-q/2})\), and the block construction rules out any uniformly faster rate from the stated conditions alone.

\section{Related work}

Arkhangelsky, Imbens, Lei, and Luo (2024) allow arbitrary binary-path support, unit-specific known generalized propensities conditional on full potential-outcome schedules, non-identical units, and weak cross-unit dependence. The base i.i.d. conditional-mean class is not their published class and is incomparable with it. The separate unit-indexed theorem retains their class exactly upon taking the reshaping support to be \(\mathcal S^*\), extends the coefficient approximation to calendar-varying finite-memory effects at rate \(O_P(n^{-q/2})\), and directly recovers their homogeneous theorem; its block construction shows that no uniformly faster coefficient rate follows from their stated conditions alone. Sun and Abraham (2021) decompose conventional event-study coefficients but do not analyze assignment-law reshaping or its complete inverse image. Bojinov, Rambachan, and Shephard (2021) motivate treatment-history indexing under known experimental assignment, while de Chaisemartin and D'Haultfoeuille (2025) study time-constant random-coefficient distributed lags. Wright (2026) supplies adjacent numerical diagnostic evidence and Ailigeng, Lu, and Wang (2023) supply time-varying distributed-lag modeling precedent; none supplies the present exact probability-graph loading body, membership or metric-separation certificate, and closest-loading bounded-effect contamination calculation. Collins (1975) supplies the standard generic cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition algorithm; the contribution here is the exact loading-tensor encoding and certified inverse analysis, not generic quantifier elimination itself.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reshaping-floor]\label{ass:reshaping-floor}
\(\Pi_s\ge\epsilon\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-support overlap for a reshaped assignment law, \cite{ArkhangelskyImbensLeiLuo2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:path-overlap]\label{ass:path-overlap}
\(\pi_s(X)\ge c_{\pi}\) almost surely for every \(s\in\mathcal S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Generalized propensity-score overlap, \cite{ArkhangelskyImbensLeiLuo2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y_t=Y_t(W)\) almost surely for every \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency of observed and potential outcomes, \cite{BojinovRambachanShephard2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:path-mean-random-assignment]\label{ass:path-mean-random-assignment}
\(\mathbb E_P[Y_t(s)\mid W=s,X]=\mu_t(s,X)\) almost surely for every \(s\in\mathcal S\) and \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional pathwise mean random assignment, \cite{ArkhangelskyImbensLeiLuo2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:calendar-memory-mean]\label{ass:calendar-memory-mean}
\(\mu_t(s,x)=m_t(x)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\tau_{t,\ell}(x)(D_s)_{t,\ell}\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Calendar-varying finite-memory linear distributed-lag conditional mean, \cite{AiligengLuWang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-full-rank]\label{ass:residual-full-rank}
\(G(\Pi)\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Full rank of the residualized distributed-lag design, \cite{ArkhangelskyImbensLeiLuo2024}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:model-class]\label{def:model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Calendar-\allowbreak{}varying finite-\allowbreak{}memory RIPW class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M(T,p,\mathcal S,\epsilon)\) is the set of tuples \((P,\Pi,\mu,m,\tau)\) such that \(\mathcal S\subseteq\{0,1\}^{T}\) is finite and nonempty, \(\Pi_s\geq\epsilon\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(\pi_s(X)\geq c_{\pi}\) almost surely for every \(s\in\mathcal S\), consistency and pathwise conditional mean random assignment hold, \(\mu_t(s,X)=m_t(X)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\tau_{t,\ell}(X)(D_s)_{t,\ell}\) for every \(s\) and \(t\), and \(G(\Pi)\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}reshaping-\allowbreak{}floor}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}path-\allowbreak{}overlap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}path-\allowbreak{}mean-\allowbreak{}random-\allowbreak{}assignment}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}calendar-\allowbreak{}memory-\allowbreak{}mean}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}full-\allowbreak{}rank}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:lag-design]\label{def:lag-design}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Lag-\allowbreak{}design matrices}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(s\in\mathcal S\), set \((D_s)_{t,j}=s_{t-j}\) for \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\) and \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\), using \(s_u=0\) for \(u\le0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}; \texttt{p}; \texttt{S}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:time-residualizer]\label{def:time-residualizer}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Time residualizer}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(J=I_T-\mathbf 1_T\mathbf 1_T^{\top}/T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:reshaped-fwl]\label{def:reshaped-fwl}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Reshaped Frisch-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Waugh-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Lovell objects}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(M(\Pi)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sD_s\), \(R_s(\Pi)=J\{D_s-M(\Pi)\}\), and \(G(\Pi)=T^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}R_s(\Pi)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Pi}; \texttt{D}; \texttt{J}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:loading-tensor]\label{def:loading-tensor}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Calendar-\allowbreak{}by-\allowbreak{}causal-\allowbreak{}lag loading tensor}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\) and \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\), set \(h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=T^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}e_t(D_s)_{t,\ell}\) and \(L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)=G(\Pi)^{-1}h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Pi}; \texttt{R}; \texttt{D}; \texttt{G}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:coefficient-functional]\label{def:coefficient-functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Reshaped fixed-\allowbreak{}effect coefficient}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every \(s\in\mathcal S\), set \(A_s(\Pi)=T^{-1}G(\Pi)^{-1}R_s(\Pi)^{\top}\), and set \(\theta(\Pi)=\mathbb E_P[\{\Pi_W/\pi_W(X)\}A_W(\Pi)Y]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{Pi}; \texttt{pi}; \texttt{G}; \texttt{R}.
\end{definition}

For \(1\leq a<b\leq k\), \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), and \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\), set \(E_{ab}=D_{s_a}-D_{s_b}\), \(B_{ab}=T^{-1}E_{ab}^{\top}JE_{ab}\), \(b_{ab,t,\ell}=T^{-1}E_{ab}^{\top}Je_t(E_{ab})_{t,\ell}\), and \(H_{\mathcal S}=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq k}B_{ab}\).

\begin{definition}[def:path-covariance]\label{def:path-covariance}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Extend every \(s\in\mathcal S\) by \(s_u=0\) for \(u\notin\{1,\ldots,T\}\), put \(\overline s_u=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s s_u\), and define

 C_{\Pi}(u,v)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s(s_u-\overline s_u)(s_v-\overline s_v),
 \qquad (u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2.

Thus \(C_{\Pi}\) is the coordinate covariance kernel under the finite law \(s\sim\Pi\), without an absorbing-path restriction.

For every unordered pair of distinct adoption dates \(u_a=a(s_a)\), \(u_b=a(s_b)\), and every \(t\in\{2,\ldots,T\}\), put \(r=t-u_a\) and \(q=t-u_b\). \begin{definition}[def:published-ripw-dynamic-estimator]\label{def:published-ripw-dynamic-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Published-\allowbreak{}class distributed-\allowbreak{}lag RIPW estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(n\), let \(\{(\mathscr Y_i,W_i):i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}\) be a possibly dependent triangular array, where \(W_i\in\{0,1\}^{T}\), \(\mathscr Y_i=\{Y_{it}(w):t\in\{1,\ldots,T\},\ w\in\{0,1\}^{T}\}\), and \(Y_{it}=Y_{it}(W_i)\).  Define the known unit-specific generalized propensity
\[
 \pi_i(w)=\mathbb P_n(W_i=w\mid\mathscr Y_i)
\]
and let \(\rho_{ij}\) be the maximal correlation between measurable square-integrable functions of \((\mathscr Y_i,W_i)\) and \((\mathscr Y_j,W_j)\).  The published regularity conditions are: for some \(q\in(0,1]\),
\[
 n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{ij}=O(n^{-q});
\]
for some \(c_{*}>0\), \(\pi_i(s)\geq c_{*}\) for every \(i\) and every \(s\in\mathcal S\); and for some \(M_{2}<\infty\),
\[
 \max_{i,t,w}\mathbb E[Y_{it}(w)^2]\leq M_{2}.
\]
For a reshaping law \(\Pi\) supported on \(\mathcal S\), define
\[
 r_i(w)=
 \begin{cases}
 \Pi_w/\pi_i(w),&w\in\mathcal S,\\
 0,&w\notin\mathcal S.
 \end{cases}
\]
The published-class distributed-lag RIPW estimator, with the grand intercept retained, is any coefficient vector \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}=(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}_{0},\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}_{-1},\ldots,\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}_{-p})^{\top}\) solving
\[
 \min_{\substack{\beta\in\mathbb R^{d},\ \mu_0\in\mathbb R,\
                   \alpha\in\mathbb R^n,\ \lambda\in\mathbb R^T\\
                   \sum_i\alpha_i=0,\ \sum_t\lambda_t=0}}
 \sum_{i=1}^{n}r_i(W_i)
 \left\|Y_i-\mu_0\mathbf 1_T-\alpha_i\mathbf 1_T-\lambda-D_{W_i}\beta\right\|_2^2.
\]
These are Arkhangelsky, Imbens, Lei, and Luo's known-propensity design and Assumptions \(2.1\)--\(2.3\), specialized only by taking the reshaping support to be \(\mathcal S\), and their equation \((4.2)\) with \(D_{W_i}\) collecting the current treatment and its \(p\) lags.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Pn}; \texttt{Wn}; \texttt{Yn}; \texttt{Ypotn}; \texttt{Pi}; \texttt{S}; \texttt{D}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:support-rank]\label{lem:support-rank}
For every positive reshaping law \(\Pi\), \(G(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}B_{ab}\), \(h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}b_{ab,t,\ell}\), and \(\ker G(\Pi)=\bigcap_{a<b}\ker(JE_{ab})=\ker H_{\mathcal S}\). Hence nonsingularity is a support-only property and holds for one positive law if and only if it holds for every positive law on \(\mathcal S\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(q_a=\Pi_{s_a}\), \(D_a=D_{s_a}\), and \(\overline D=\sum_aq_aD_a=M(\Pi)\).  For matrices \(X_a\) of a common dimension and \(\overline X=\sum_aq_aX_a\), direct expansion using \(\sum_aq_a=1\) gives
\[
 \sum_aq_a(X_a-\overline X)^{\top}J(X_a-\overline X)
 =\frac12\sum_{a,b}q_aq_b(X_a-X_b)^{\top}J(X_a-X_b).
\]
Indeed, each side equals
\[
 \sum_aq_aX_a^{\top}JX_a-\overline X^{\top}J\overline X.
\]
Taking \(X_a=D_a\), deleting the terms with \(a=b\), pairing \((a,b)\) with \((b,a)\), and applying the definitions of \(G\), \(E_{ab}\), and \(B_{ab}\), we obtain
\[
 G(\Pi)=\frac1T\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bE_{ab}^{\top}JE_{ab}
 =\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab}.
\]

Fix \((t,\ell)\), set \(x_a=(D_a)_{t,\ell}\), and expand similarly:
\[
 \sum_aq_a(D_a-\overline D)^{\top}Je_tx_a
 =\sum_{a<b}q_aq_b(D_a-D_b)^{\top}Je_t(x_a-x_b).
\]
Both sides equal
\[
 \sum_aq_aD_a^{\top}Je_tx_a-\overline D^{\top}Je_t\sum_aq_ax_a.
\]
Since \(R_{s_a}=J(D_a-\overline D)\), \(x_a-x_b=(E_{ab})_{t,\ell}\), and \(J\) is symmetric, division by \(T\) yields
\[
 h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bb_{ab,t,\ell}.
\]

The matrix \(J=I_T-\mathbf 1_T\mathbf 1_T^{\top}/T\) is symmetric and idempotent.  Therefore, for every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 v^{\top}B_{ab}v=T^{-1}(E_{ab}v)^{\top}J(E_{ab}v)
 =T^{-1}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2.
\]
The reshaping floor implies \(q_aq_b\geq\epsilon^2>0\).  Hence the pair formula gives
\[
 v^{\top}G(\Pi)v=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 JE_{ab}v=0\quad\hbox{for every }a<b.
\]
Likewise,
\[
 v^{\top}H_{\mathcal S}v
 =T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2.
\]
Thus
\[
 \ker G(\Pi)=\bigcap_{a<b}\ker(JE_{ab})=\ker H_{\mathcal S}.
\]
No step used absorption or the numerical values of the positive masses.  The common kernel depends only on the listed support, proving that nonsingularity for one positive law is equivalent to nonsingularity for every positive law on \(\mathcal S\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The pair identities and common kernel hold for every finite binary-path support and separate support identification from the numerical positive reshaping masses. \textit{Gap.} Published dynamic RIPW assumes positive definiteness but does not give this arbitrary-support pair decomposition. \textit{Consumer.} The public \(\texttt{ripw}\) workflow can reject an underidentified lag specification before fitting an assignment model.

\begin{theorem}[thm:calendar-lag-loading]\label{thm:calendar-lag-loading}
For every law in \(\mathcal M(T,p,\mathcal S,\epsilon)\), with \(\mathcal S\) any finite binary-path support, the reshaped fixed-effect coefficient satisfies
\[
 \theta(\Pi)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)\tau_{t,\ell}.
\]
For \(j,r,\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\), the arbitrary-path covariance kernel obeys
\[
 G_{jr}(\Pi)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}C_{\Pi}(t-j,t-r)
 -T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{v=1}^{T}C_{\Pi}(t-j,v-r),
\]
\[
 h_{t,\ell,j}(\Pi)=T^{-1}\left\{C_{\Pi}(t-j,t-\ell)
 -T^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^{T}C_{\Pi}(v-j,t-\ell)\right\},
\]
and \(\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}\).  If every path in \(\mathcal S\) is absorbing, then \(C_{\Pi}=K_{\Pi}\), so the same two formulas hold with \(K_{\Pi}\) in place of \(C_{\Pi}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\pi_s(X)\geq c_{\pi}>0\) almost surely for every \(s\in\mathcal S\), every ratio in the definition of \(\theta(\Pi)\) is well defined and bounded by \(c_{\pi}^{-1}\).  The conditional means defining \(\mu_t(s,X)\) ensure integrability of every supported potential-outcome coordinate; finite support, finite \(T\), and \(G(\Pi)\succ0\) then ensure that the expectations and finite interchanges below are well defined.  Write \(\mu(s,X)=(\mu_1(s,X),\ldots,\mu_T(s,X))^{\top}\).  Conditional on \(X\), consistency and pathwise conditional mean random assignment give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[\left.\frac{\Pi_W}{\pi_W(X)}A_W(\Pi)Y\right|X\right]
&=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\pi_s(X)\frac{\Pi_s}{\pi_s(X)}A_s(\Pi)
  \mathbb E_P[Y\mid W=s,X]\\
&=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sA_s(\Pi)\mu(s,X).
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, on \(\{W=s\}\), consistency gives \(Y_t=Y_t(s)\), and the random-assignment assumption then gives \(\mathbb E_P[Y_t\mid W=s,X]=\mu_t(s,X)\) for every coordinate \(t\).

Let \(m(X)=(m_1(X),\ldots,m_T(X))^{\top}\).  The calendar-memory assumption is equivalently
\[
 \mu(s,X)=m(X)+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}
 e_t\tau_{t,\ell}(X)(D_s)_{t,\ell}.
\]
Since \(\Pi\) is a probability law and \(M(\Pi)=\sum_s\Pi_sD_s\),
\[
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)
 =J\left\{\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sD_s-M(\Pi)\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s\right\}=0.
\]
Consequently the unrestricted baseline cancels exactly:
\[
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sA_s(\Pi)m(X)
 =T^{-1}G(\Pi)^{-1}
   \left\{\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}\right\}m(X)=0.
\]
For every effect cell \((t,\ell)\), the definitions of \(A_s\), \(h_{t,\ell}\), and \(L\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sA_s(\Pi)e_t(D_s)_{t,\ell}
 &=G(\Pi)^{-1}\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}
   \Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}e_t(D_s)_{t,\ell}\right\}\\
 &=G(\Pi)^{-1}h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)
 =L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi).
\end{aligned}
\]
The inverse exists by \(G(\Pi)\succ0\).  Taking expectation over \(X\), interchanging only finite sums, and using \(\tau_{t,\ell}=\mathbb E_P[\tau_{t,\ell}(X)]\), proves
\[
 \theta(\Pi)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)\tau_{t,\ell}.
\]
Thus the causal target array is kept distinct from the observed-law functional \(\theta(\Pi)\), and the displayed equality follows from the stated assignment and outcome-mean conditions rather than by definition.

It remains to derive the design identities.  Regard \(s\) as a random path with law \(\Pi\), and put \(\widetilde D_s=D_s-M(\Pi)\).  Since \(J^{\top}=J\) and \(J^2=J\),
\[
 G(\Pi)=T^{-1}\mathbb E_{\Pi}[\widetilde D_s^{\top}J\widetilde D_s].
\]
Because \((D_s)_{t,j}=s_{t-j}\), with out-of-panel coordinates set to zero, its \((j,r)\) entry is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G_{jr}(\Pi)
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{u=1}^{T}\sum_{v=1}^{T}J_{uv}
   \operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi}(s_{u-j},s_{v-r})\\
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}C_{\Pi}(t-j,t-r)
   -T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{v=1}^{T}C_{\Pi}(t-j,v-r),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(J_{uv}=\mathbf 1\{u=v\}-T^{-1}\).  Similarly, centering the first factor and observing that its product with the uncentered second factor has the same expectation as the corresponding covariance,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 h_{t,\ell,j}(\Pi)
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^{T}J_{vt}
   \operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi}(s_{v-j},s_{t-\ell})\\
 &=T^{-1}\left\{C_{\Pi}(t-j,t-\ell)
   -T^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^{T}C_{\Pi}(v-j,t-\ell)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These two calculations require only the finite binary-path support.

If every supported path is absorbing, then for every coordinate occurring above,
\(s_u=\mathbf 1\{a(s)\leq u\}\), with value zero for \(u\leq0\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{\Pi}(u,v)
 &=\mathbb E_{\Pi}[s_us_v]-\mathbb E_{\Pi}[s_u]\mathbb E_{\Pi}[s_v]\\
 &=F_{\Pi}(\min\{u,v\})-F_{\Pi}(u)F_{\Pi}(v)
 =K_{\Pi}(u,v).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the ordered-Green specialization.

Finally, let \(e_{\ell}\in\mathbb R^d\).  Summing the numerator over calendar time gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}D_se_{\ell}\\
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}
   \{D_s-M(\Pi)\}e_{\ell}\\
 &=T^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)^{\top}R_s(\Pi)e_{\ell}\\
 &=G(\Pi)e_{\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses \(\sum_s\Pi_sR_s(\Pi)=0\); the third uses \(R_s=J\widetilde D_s\), \(J^{\top}=J\), and \(J^2=J\).  Multiplication by \(G(\Pi)^{-1}\) therefore yields
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)
 =e_j^{\top}e_{\ell}=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\},
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact coefficient-by-calendar-by-causal-lag map on arbitrary binary support; the ordered Green formula is correctly isolated as an absorbing corollary. \textit{Gap.} Published RIPW dynamic recovery is calendar homogeneous and does not state the general covariance-kernel tensor. \textit{Consumer.} The OpenTable analysis can report the exact calendar and causal-lag sources of each coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[thm:homogeneous-strict-extension]\label{thm:homogeneous-strict-extension}
If \(\tau_{t,\ell}=\tau_{r,\ell}\) for every \(t,r\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\) and \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\), then \(\theta_j(\Pi)=\tau_{t,j}\) for every \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\) and every legal \(t\).  For \(T=3\), \(p=1\), \(\mathcal S=\{000,001,011\}\), and \(\Pi=(1/3,1/3,1/3)\), the calendar-varying model yields
\[
 \theta_0=(\tau_{2,0}+\tau_{3,0})/2,
 \qquad
 \theta_1=(-\tau_{2,0}+\tau_{3,0})/2+\tau_{3,1}.
\]
Thus the coefficient vector is not determined by a time-homogeneous lag-effect vector.  The first equality is coefficient algebra in the present superpopulation class, not a class-inclusion proof of Arkhangelsky, Imbens, Lei, and Luo's Theorem \(4.1\).  The published-class calendar-loading theorem supplies the faithful statistical recovery of that theorem, and the displayed witness admits an independent randomized-assignment embedding satisfying its hypotheses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume calendar homogeneity and denote the common effect at lag \(\ell\) by \(\overline\tau_{\ell}\), so that \(\tau_{t,\ell}=\overline\tau_{\ell}\) for every legal calendar index.  The causal loading representation in \(\mathrm{thm{:}calendar\text{-}lag\text{-}loading}\), followed by its normalization identity, gives for every \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \theta_j(\Pi)
 &=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
   L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)\overline\tau_{\ell}\\
 &=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}\overline\tau_{\ell}
 =\overline\tau_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\theta_j(\Pi)=\tau_{t,j}\) for every legal \(t\).  This conclusion is coefficient algebra in the present superpopulation model.  Independently, \(\mathrm{thm{:}published\text{-}class\text{-}calendar\text{-}loading}\) applies under the published unit-indexed known-propensity and weak-dependence conditions and, under the same calendar homogeneity restriction, gives the published-class recovery statement with remainder \(O_P(n^{-q/2})\).  Therefore no class-inclusion argument is used.

For the strict calendar-varying witness, take \(T=3\), \(p=1\), \(\mathcal S=\{000,001,011\}\), and put mass \(1/3\) on each listed path.  In that listing order,
\[
 D_{000}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_{001}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D_{011}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and hence
\[
 M(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1/3&0\\2/3&1/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&-1/3&-1/3\\-1/3&2/3&-1/3\\-1/3&-1/3&2/3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Substitution into \(R_s=J(D_s-M(\Pi))\), followed by finite matrix multiplication in the definitions of \(G\) and \(h\), gives
\[
 G(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}2/27&0\\0&4/81\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 h_{2,0}(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}1/27\\-2/81\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 h_{3,0}(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}1/27\\2/81\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 h_{3,1}(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\4/81\end{pmatrix},
\]
with every other \(h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\) equal to zero.  In particular, \(G(\Pi)\succ0\), and multiplication by
\(G(\Pi)^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(27/2,81/4)\) yields
\[
 L_{\cdot,2,0}(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\-1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L_{\cdot,3,0}(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L_{\cdot,3,1}(\Pi)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The already-proved causal loading representation now gives
\[
 \theta_0=\frac{\tau_{2,0}+\tau_{3,0}}{2},\qquad
 \theta_1=\frac{-\tau_{2,0}+\tau_{3,0}}{2}+\tau_{3,1}.
\]
Thus, holding \(\tau_{3,1}\) fixed while varying either calendar-specific current effect changes the lag-one coefficient, so no time-homogeneous lag-effect vector determines the coefficient on this calendar-varying class.

The same witness has an independent randomized-assignment embedding in the published sampling setup.  For each unit, draw \(W_i\) independently from \(\Pi\), and define bounded deterministic potential outcomes for every binary path \(w\) by
\[
 Y_{it}(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{1}\tau_{t,\ell}w_{t-\ell},
 \qquad w_u=0\text{ outside }\{1,2,3\}.
\]
Then the propensity conditional on the full potential-outcome schedule equals \(\pi_i(s)=1/3\) for \(s\in\mathcal S\), so published overlap holds with \(c_*=1/3\).  Independence gives \(\rho_{ij}=0\) for \(i\neq j\), while \(\rho_{ii}=1\), and therefore
\[
 n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{ij}=n^{-1}.
\]
The finite collection of deterministic outcomes has a uniform finite second-moment bound, and the displayed \(G(\Pi)\) supplies full rank.  Hence all published regularity conditions hold with \(q=1\), and \(\mathrm{thm{:}published\text{-}class\text{-}calendar\text{-}loading}\) transfers the same calendar-varying nonreduction to the published estimator up to its \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) remainder.  Under calendar homogeneity, that same theorem instead gives the exact published Theorem \(4.1\) recovery conclusion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Normalization proves homogeneous coefficient algebra, while the published-class theorem separately supplies exact comparator recovery and the randomized witness proves nonreduction under calendar variation. \textit{Gap.} The earlier wording conflated algebra in an i.i.d. mean-ignorability class with the published weak-dependence theorem. \textit{Consumer.} Applied users can distinguish model interpretation from sampling guarantees.

\begin{proposition}[prop:exact-forward-diagnostic]\label{prop:exact-forward-diagnostic}
For integer \(T,p\), an explicitly listed binary support \(\mathcal S\), and rational \(\epsilon,\Pi,\omega\), there is a terminating exact rational-arithmetic procedure that returns either a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Q^{d}\) with \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every \(a<b\), certifying singularity, or the exact tensor \(L(\Pi)\), the exact value \(\mathcal C(\Pi,Q)\), and the complete sign-and-zero table of \(L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)-Q_{j,t,\ell}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every listed \(D_s\) and \(E_{ab}\) is integral, while
\[
 J=I_T-\frac{\mathbf 1_T\mathbf 1_T^{\top}}{T}
\]
has rational entries.  Consequently the definitions of \(B_{ab}\), \(b_{ab,t,\ell}\), and \(H_{\mathcal S}\) use only finitely many rational arithmetic operations.

Apply Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb Q\) to \(H_{\mathcal S}\).  If this matrix is singular, elimination returns a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Q^d\) satisfying \(H_{\mathcal S}v=0\).  Since \(J\) is symmetric and idempotent, the definition of \(B_{ab}\) gives
\[
 0=v^{\top}H_{\mathcal S}v
 =\sum_{a<b}v^{\top}B_{ab}v
 =\frac1T\sum_{a<b}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, so \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every \(a<b\).  Thus \(v\) is the claimed exact singularity certificate.

Suppose instead that \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is nonsingular.  The reshaping floor gives \(\Pi_{s_a}\geq\epsilon>0\) for every \(a\).  The established support-rank lemma therefore implies
\[
 \ker G(\Pi)=\ker H_{\mathcal S}=\{0\}.
\]
Thus \(G(\Pi)\) is invertible.  Rationality of \(\Pi\), together with the pair identities in that lemma, gives
\[
 G(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}B_{ab}\in\mathbb Q^{d\times d},
 \qquad
 h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}b_{ab,t,\ell}\in\mathbb Q^d.
\]
Exact elimination over \(\mathbb Q\) consequently computes \(G(\Pi)^{-1}\) and, for every one of the finitely many pairs \((t,\ell)\),
\[
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)=G(\Pi)^{-1}h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\in\mathbb Q^d.
\]
The rational weights \(\omega_{\ell,t}\) make every entry of \(Q\) rational.  Exact comparison in the ordered field \(\mathbb Q\) therefore returns the sign, including equality to zero, of every rational number \(L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)-Q_{j,t,\ell}\).  Summing their exact absolute values gives \(\mathcal C(\Pi,Q)\).  Both branches terminate because Gaussian elimination and the subsequent arithmetic operate on finite rational matrices and finitely many tensor cells.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite rational elimination provides auditable rank, loading, sign, zero, and contamination certificates at any supplied rational reshaping law. \textit{Gap.} A supplied-law forward certificate alone does not characterize or optimize over the complete probability-floor loading body. \textit{Consumer.} The public workflow can export a machine-checkable loading table and use it as the pointwise forward check inside the exact inverse routine.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adjacent-ratio-determinant-sign-rule]\label{thm:adjacent-ratio-determinant-sign-rule}
Suppose every path in \(\mathcal S\) is absorbing.  Order the distinct paths as \(\mathcal S=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\), put \(u_a=a(s_a)\), and require \(u_a<u_b\) for \(a<b\).  Suppose \(\ker H_{\mathcal S}=\{0\}\), equivalently every lag vertex of \(\mathfrak G_{\mathcal S,p}\) is connected to \(\bot\).  For a fixed law with \(\Pi_{s_a}\geq\epsilon>0\), define \(\rho_a=\Pi_{s_{a+1}}/\Pi_{s_a}\), \(z_1=1\), \(z_a=\prod_{r=1}^{a-1}\rho_r\), and \(Z=\sum_{a=1}^{k}z_a\).  Define \(I_{ab}\in\{0,1\}^{T\times d}\) by \((I_{ab})_{r,j}=\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq r-j<u_b\}\), and set
\[
 \Gamma(\rho)=T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}z_az_bI_{ab}^{\top}JI_{ab},
 \qquad
 g_{t,\ell}(\rho)=T^{-1}\sum_{a<b}z_az_bI_{ab}^{\top}Je_t(I_{ab})_{t,\ell}.
\]
Then \(\det\Gamma(\rho)>0\).  If \(\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)\) is the determinant obtained by replacing column \(j\) of \(\Gamma(\rho)\) by \(g_{t,\ell}(\rho)\), then for every \(j,\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\) and \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\),
\[
 L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=\frac{\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)}{\det\Gamma(\rho)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sgn}L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=\operatorname{sgn}\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho),
\]
and \(L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=0\) if and only if \(\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)=0\).  Hence all off-diagonal causal-lag loadings vanish at this law if and only if these determinants vanish for every \(t\) and every \(j\neq\ell\).  The floor is equivalent to \(z_a\geq\epsilon Z\) for every \(a\).  For rational endpoints and ratios, all displayed quantities and signs are computable by terminating exact rational arithmetic, including shifted boundary coordinates.  This is a pointwise certificate for a supplied law; it does not classify the global sign chambers over all admissible ratios.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because an absorbing binary path is determined by its adoption date, distinct supported paths have distinct adoption dates.  Under the stated ordering, if \(u_a=a(s_a)<u_b=a(s_b)\), then the boundary convention implies for every row \(r\) and lag column \(j\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (E_{ab})_{r,j}
 &=(D_{s_a})_{r,j}-(D_{s_b})_{r,j}\\
 &=\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq r-j\}-\mathbf 1\{u_b\leq r-j\}\\
 &=\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq r-j<u_b\}
 =(I_{ab})_{r,j}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(E_{ab}=I_{ab}\), including coordinates shifted outside the panel.  The established absorbing graph-rank lemma gives
\[
 \ker H_{\mathcal S}=\{0\}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{every lag vertex of }\mathfrak G_{\mathcal S,p}\text{ is connected to }\bot.
\]

The floor \(\Pi_{s_a}\geq\epsilon>0\) makes every adjacent ratio \(\rho_a\) strictly positive and well defined.  Recursion in the ratios and normalization of \(\Pi\) give
\[
 \frac{\Pi_{s_a}}{\Pi_{s_1}}
 =\prod_{r=1}^{a-1}\rho_r=z_a,
 \qquad
 1=\sum_{a=1}^{k}\Pi_{s_a}=\Pi_{s_1}Z,
 \qquad
 \Pi_{s_a}=\frac{z_a}{Z}.
\]
Using \(E_{ab}=I_{ab}\) in the pair contributions and then applying the established pair-mass identities yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G(\Pi)
 &=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}B_{ab}
 =Z^{-2}\Gamma(\rho),\\
 h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)
 &=\sum_{a<b}\Pi_{s_a}\Pi_{s_b}b_{ab,t,\ell}
 =Z^{-2}g_{t,\ell}(\rho).
\end{aligned}
\]
By construction \(G(\Pi)\) is symmetric positive semidefinite.  Positivity of all masses and the support-rank lemma give
\(\ker G(\Pi)=\ker H_{\mathcal S}=\{0\}\); hence \(G(\Pi)\succ0\).  It follows that
\[
 \Gamma(\rho)=Z^2G(\Pi)\succ0,
 \qquad \det\Gamma(\rho)>0,
\]
and the common positive scale cancels from the loading:
\[
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)=\Gamma(\rho)^{-1}g_{t,\ell}(\rho).
\]

For completeness, the required Cramer identity follows from determinant expansion.  Let \(\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)\) be the cofactor adjugate.  Cofactor expansion gives
\[
 \Gamma\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)=\det(\Gamma)I_d:
\]
on a diagonal entry this is the determinant expansion, whereas an off-diagonal entry is the same expansion for a matrix with two equal columns and is zero.  Expanding the determinant of the matrix formed by replacing column \(j\) of \(\Gamma\) by \(g_{t,\ell}\) gives
\[
 e_j^{\top}\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)g_{t,\ell}
 =\Delta_{j,t,\ell}.
\]
Since \(\det\Gamma>0\), these identities imply
\[
 L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)
 =\frac{e_j^{\top}\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma(\rho))g_{t,\ell}(\rho)}
        {\det\Gamma(\rho)}
 =\frac{\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)}{\det\Gamma(\rho)}.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive, so
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)
 =\operatorname{sgn}\Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho),
 \qquad
 L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)=0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \Delta_{j,t,\ell}(\rho)=0.
\]
Taking the finite conjunction over all \(t\) and all \(j\neq\ell\) proves the stated characterization of vanishing off-diagonal causal-lag loadings.

Finally, \(\Pi_{s_a}=z_a/Z\) proves, in both directions,
\[
 \Pi_{s_a}\geq\epsilon\text{ for every }a
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 z_a\geq\epsilon Z\text{ for every }a.
\]
Each \(z_a\) is a finite monomial in the adjacent ratios, while every endpoint \(u_a\) is an integer and every entry of \(I_{ab}\) and \(J\) is rational.  Thus, on rationally represented ratios, all entries of \(\Gamma\) and \(g\), the floor comparisons, and all determinants \(\Delta_{j,t,\ell}\) are computable by finitely many exact rational operations.  Exact determinant evaluation and comparison with zero terminate, including for shifted boundary coordinates.  This supplies a pointwise certificate for the given law; the argument does not enumerate the global semialgebraic sign chambers over the admissible ratio domain.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} At any supplied positive absorbing law, adjacent ratios and interval endpoints determine every loading sign and zero by a positive-denominator Cramer formula. \textit{Gap.} The theorem is not a global enumeration of sign chambers over the ratio-floor domain. \textit{Consumer.} The workflow can certify a chosen law exactly while labeling global chamber search as unresolved.

\begin{lemma}[lem:absorbing-graph-rank]\label{lem:absorbing-graph-rank}
Suppose every path in \(\mathcal S\) is absorbing and \(\Pi_s>0\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\).  Then
\[
 \dim\ker G(\Pi)
 =\#\{\text{connected components of }\mathfrak G_{\mathcal S,p}\text{ not containing }\bot\}.
\]
In particular, \(G(\Pi)\) is positive definite if and only if every vertex \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\) is connected to \(\bot\).  The equality need not hold for a law on the boundary of the simplex.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order the absorbing paths by increasing adoption date.  For a pair with dates \(u_a<u_b\), put \(x=E_{ab}v\) and extend the coordinates of \(v\) by \(v_j=0\) for \(j\notin\{0,\ldots,p\}\).  The interval representation of \(E_{ab}\) gives
\[
 x_t=\sum_{j=0}^{p}v_j\mathbf 1\{u_a\leq t-j<u_b\}.
\]
Taking the difference of consecutive rows, for every \(t\in\{2,\ldots,T\}\), yields
\[
 x_t-x_{t-1}=v_{t-u_a}-v_{t-u_b}.
\]
Because \(Jx=0\) if and only if \(x\) is constant, we have
\[
 JE_{ab}v=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_{t-u_a}=v_{t-u_b}\quad\text{for every }t\in\{2,\ldots,T\}.
\]
If both indices are in \(\{0,\ldots,p\}\), this is precisely equality across the corresponding graph edge.  If exactly one is in range, the other coordinate is zero and the equation is equality with the value at \(\bot\), which is fixed to zero.  If neither is in range, the equation is \(0=0\) and adds no restriction.

The support-rank lemma is applicable because every mass is positive, and therefore identifies \(\ker G(\Pi)\) with the intersection of these pairwise constraints.  A vector in that intersection is constant on every connected component of \(\mathfrak G_{\mathcal S,p}\), and its constant is zero on the component containing \(\bot\).  Conversely, assigning an arbitrary constant to each other component and zero to the component of \(\bot\) satisfies every edge equation.  These component indicators are linearly independent and span the kernel, proving the dimension formula and the positive-definiteness criterion.

Positivity is essential.  Take \(T=2\), \(p=0\), \(\mathcal S=\{00,01\}\), and the boundary law \(\Pi_{00}=1\), \(\Pi_{01}=0\).  At \(t=2\), the two adoption dates produce an edge joining lag vertex \(0\) to \(\bot\), so the graph has no component away from \(\bot\).  But \(D_W=D_{00}=0\) almost surely under this boundary law, whence \(G(\Pi)=0\) and \(\dim\ker G(\Pi)=1\).  This is the asserted boundary counterexample.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} First differences turn the absorbing-support kernel into equality constraints on a finite graph, including the boundary-zero vertex. \textit{Gap.} A matrix rank test does not reveal the exact number or location of unidentified lag directions. \textit{Consumer.} Users receive a combinatorial explanation of rank failure, not only a zero determinant.

\begin{theorem}[thm:published-class-calendar-loading]\label{thm:published-class-calendar-loading}
In the published-class setup of the distributed-lag RIPW estimator, suppose its three stated regularity conditions hold, the unit-specific generalized propensities are known, \(G(\Pi)\succ0\), and
\[
 \mathbb E[Y_{it}(w)]=\mu_{it}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\tau_{i,t,\ell}w_{t-\ell}
\]
for every \(i\), \(t\), and \(w\), with \(w_u=0\) outside \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\). Put \(\overline\tau_{t,\ell,n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i,t,\ell}\). Then
\[
 \widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}-
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)\overline\tau_{t,\ell,n}
 =O_P(n^{-q/2}).
\]
If \(\tau_{i,t,\ell}=\tau_{i,-\ell}\) for every legal \(t\), then, for each \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\),
\[
 \widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}_{-\ell}
 =n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i,-\ell}+O_P(n^{-q/2}),
\]
and hence the conclusion of Arkhangelsky, Imbens, Lei, and Luo's Theorem \(4.1\) follows under their unit-indexed known-propensity setup and Assumptions \(2.1\)--\(2.3\).
Moreover, the rate cannot be improved uniformly over the stated published class: for every \(q\in(0,1]\), there is a two-period block-dependent triangular array satisfying all stated conditions, with zero tensor target, for which \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}\) is not \(o_P(n^{-q/2})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\delta_n=n^{-q/2}\), write \(r_i=r_i(W_i)\), and write \(M=M(\Pi)\). By the overlap condition in the published setup, \(r_i=0\) off \(\mathcal S\) and
\[
0\leq r_i\leq c_*^{-1}.
\]
The weighted outcome terms also have uniformly bounded second moments. Indeed, consistency, conditioning on \(\mathscr Y_i\), and \(\pi_i(s)\geq c_*\) give, for every \(t\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\!\left[r_i^2Y_{it}^2\right]
&=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\mathbb E\!\left[
  \mathbf 1\{W_i=s\}\frac{\Pi_s^2}{\pi_i(s)^2}Y_{it}(s)^2
\right]\\
&=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s^2
  \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{Y_{it}(s)^2}{\pi_i(s)}\right]
\leq c_*^{-1}M_2\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s^2
\leq c_*^{-1}M_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(T\) and \(p\) are fixed and every entry of \(D_s\) and \(J\) is bounded, every scalar entry needed below from
\[
r_i,\quad r_iD_{W_i},\quad r_iD_{W_i}^{\top}JD_{W_i},
\quad r_iJY_i,\quad r_iD_{W_i}^{\top}JY_i
\]
therefore has a second moment bounded by a constant independent of \(i\) and \(n\).

Let \(U_i\) be any one of these scalar entries. By the definition of the maximal-correlation coefficient,
\[
\left|\operatorname{Cov}(U_i,U_j)\right|
\leq \rho_{ij}
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(U_i)\operatorname{Var}(U_j)}
\leq C\rho_{ij}.
\]
Consequently the published weak-dependence condition implies
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left\{n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
(U_i-\mathbb E U_i)\right\}
\leq Cn^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{ij}
=O(n^{-q})=O(\delta_n^2).
\]
For every \(A>0\), the elementary inequality
\(\mathbf 1\{|Z|>A\delta_n\}\leq Z^2/(A^2\delta_n^2)\)
therefore yields
\[
\mathbb P_n\!\left(
\left|n^{-1}\sum_i(U_i-\mathbb E U_i)\right|>A\delta_n
\right)\leq \frac{C}{A^2}.
\]
Thus every required scalar sample moment equals its expectation plus \(O_P(\delta_n)\). The same statement holds for the finitely many entries of every required vector and matrix.

For every supported path \(s\) and every integrable function \(f(\mathscr Y_i)\), the definition of the known propensity gives the exact change-of-law identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\!\left[r_i\mathbf 1\{W_i=s\}f(\mathscr Y_i)\right]
&=\mathbb E\!\left[
  \mathbb E\!\left[
   \left.\mathbf 1\{W_i=s\}\frac{\Pi_s}{\pi_i(s)}f(\mathscr Y_i)
   \right|\mathscr Y_i\right]
\right]\\
&=\Pi_s\mathbb E[f(\mathscr Y_i)].
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, with
\[
s_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_i,
\qquad
S_{D,n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_iD_{W_i},
\]
the preceding moment bound gives
\[
s_n=1+O_P(\delta_n),
\qquad
S_{D,n}=M+O_P(\delta_n).
\]
Since \(s_n\) is bounded away from zero with probability tending to one,
\[
\overline D_n:=\frac{\sum_i r_iD_{W_i}}{\sum_i r_i}
=\frac{S_{D,n}}{s_n}=M+O_P(\delta_n).
\]

The grand intercept together with \(\sum_i\alpha_i=0\) and \(\sum_t\lambda_t=0\) spans all unit constants and common time effects. Eliminating these nuisance coefficients first applies \(J\) within each unit and then removes the weighted cross-unit mean. Hence, whenever the resulting normal matrix is nonsingular, the weighted least-squares normal equations give
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}=\widehat G_n^{-1}\widehat g_n,
\]
where
\[
\widehat G_n=\frac{1}{nT}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_i
(D_{W_i}-\overline D_n)^{\top}J(D_{W_i}-\overline D_n)
\]
and
\[
\widehat g_n=\frac{1}{nT}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_i
(D_{W_i}-\overline D_n)^{\top}JY_i.
\]
The weighted outcome mean disappears from the latter equation because
\(\sum_i r_i(D_{W_i}-\overline D_n)=0\). Expanding the first display and using the exact weighted mean gives
\[
\widehat G_n
=\frac{1}{nT}\sum_i r_iD_{W_i}^{\top}JD_{W_i}
  -\frac{s_n}{T}\overline D_n^{\top}J\overline D_n.
\]
The change-of-law identity, the moment rate, and
\(s_n=1+O_P(\delta_n)\), \(\overline D_n=M+O_P(\delta_n)\) now imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat G_n
&=\frac1T\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_sD_s^{\top}JD_s
  -\frac1T M^{\top}JM+O_P(\delta_n)\\
&=\frac1T\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s
  (D_s-M)^{\top}J(D_s-M)+O_P(\delta_n)\\
&=G(\Pi)+O_P(\delta_n).
\end{aligned}
\]

For the numerator, add and subtract \(M\):
\[
\widehat g_n
=\frac{1}{nT}\sum_i r_i(D_{W_i}-M)^{\top}JY_i
 -(\overline D_n-M)^{\top}
   \left\{\frac{1}{nT}\sum_i r_iJY_i\right\}.
\]
The vector in braces is \(O_P(1)\): its centered part is \(O_P(\delta_n)\), and its expectation is uniformly bounded by the displayed second-moment bound and Cauchy--Schwarz. The second term is therefore \(O_P(\delta_n)\). For the first term, the change-of-law identity and the stated unconditional mean model give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\!\left[\frac1T r_i(D_{W_i}-M)^{\top}JY_i\right]\\
&\quad=\frac1T\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pi_s(D_s-M)^{\top}J
\left\{\mu_i+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}
e_t\tau_{i,t,\ell}(D_s)_{t,\ell}\right\}\\
&\quad=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}
h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\tau_{i,t,\ell},
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\mu_i=(\mu_{i1},\ldots,\mu_{iT})^{\top}\). The baseline term vanishes because
\(\sum_s\Pi_s(D_s-M)=0\), and the last equality is the definition of \(h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\). Averaging over \(i\) and applying the moment rate proves
\[
\widehat g_n=b_n+O_P(\delta_n),
\qquad
b_n:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}
h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\overline\tau_{t,\ell,n}.
\]

The deterministic moving target \(b_n\) is uniformly bounded under the stated conditions. To see this without an additional effect-size assumption, let \(w^{(0)}\) be the all-zero path. The second-moment bound and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
|\mu_{it}|=|\mathbb E Y_{it}(w^{(0)})|\leq M_2^{1/2}.
\]
For every legal pair \((t,\ell)\), meaning \(1\leq t-\ell\leq T\), let \(w^{(t,\ell)}\) have its sole nonzero coordinate at \(t-\ell\). The mean model then gives
\[
\tau_{i,t,\ell}
=\mathbb E Y_{it}(w^{(t,\ell)})-\mathbb E Y_{it}(w^{(0)}),
\qquad
|\tau_{i,t,\ell}|\leq 2M_2^{1/2}.
\]
For an illegal pair, \((D_s)_{t,\ell}=0\) for every \(s\), so \(h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)=0\). Since there are finitely many legal pairs and \(h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\) is fixed,
\[
\sup_n\|b_n\|
\leq 2M_2^{1/2}\sum_{\substack{1\leq t\leq T,\ 0\leq\ell\leq p\\1\leq t-\ell\leq T}}
\|h_{t,\ell}(\Pi)\|<\infty.
\]

Let \(\lambda_{\min}(G)>0\) be the smallest eigenvalue of \(G=G(\Pi)\). Since the dimension \(d\) is fixed and \(\widehat G_n-G=O_P(\delta_n)=o_P(1)\), with probability tending to one
\(\|\widehat G_n-G\|<\lambda_{\min}(G)/2\). On this event, \(\widehat G_n\) is nonsingular,
\[
\|\widehat G_n^{-1}\|\leq \frac{2}{\lambda_{\min}(G)},
\qquad
\widehat G_n^{-1}-G^{-1}
=\widehat G_n^{-1}(G-\widehat G_n)G^{-1}
=O_P(\delta_n).
\]
Thus the definition of the loading tensor yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}-G^{-1}b_n
&=\widehat G_n^{-1}(\widehat g_n-b_n)
  +(\widehat G_n^{-1}-G^{-1})b_n
=O_P(\delta_n),\\
G^{-1}b_n
&=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}
L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(\Pi)\overline\tau_{t,\ell,n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the first conclusion, including the stated rate. If
\(\tau_{i,t,\ell}=\tau_{i,-\ell}\) for every legal \(t\), the normalization in \(\text{thm:calendar-lag-loading}\) gives, for every \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_j^{\top}G^{-1}b_n
&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{j,t,\ell}(\Pi)\right\}
  \left(n^{-1}\sum_i\tau_{i,-\ell}\right)\\
&=n^{-1}\sum_i\tau_{i,-j}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coordinatewise homogeneous conclusion follows because \(d\) is fixed.

Finally, the exponent is sharp for the moment control available from the three published regularity conditions. Fix \(T=2\), \(p=0\), \(\mathcal S=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}\), and \(\Pi_{(0,0)}=\Pi_{(0,1)}=1/2\); direct substitution gives \(G(\Pi)=1/16>0\). Let \(B_n=\lceil n^q\rceil\) and partition the units into \(B_n\) blocks whose sizes \(m_{b,n}\) differ by at most one. Independently across blocks, draw a Rademacher variable \(V_b\) and a Bernoulli variable \(Z_b\) with success probability \(1/2\), independently of each other. For every unit in block \(b\), set \(W_i=(0,Z_b)\) and set
\[
Y_{i1}(w)=0,\qquad Y_{i2}(w)=V_b
\quad\text{for every }w\in\{0,1\}^2.
\]
Then \(\pi_i((0,0))=\pi_i((0,1))=1/2\) conditional on the full schedule, the second moments are bounded by one, and the mean model holds with \(\mu_{it}=0\) and \(\tau_{i,t,0}=0\). Within a block the observed schedule--treatment pairs are identical and nondegenerate, so \(\rho_{ij}=1\); across blocks they are independent, so \(\rho_{ij}=0\). Hence
\[
n^{-2}\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}
=n^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}m_{b,n}^2
=\Theta(B_n^{-1})=\Theta(n^{-q}).
\]
The required weighted numerator moment
\(r_iD_{W_i}^{\top}JY_i=Z_bV_b/2\) is constant inside each block, centered, nondegenerate, and has sample-mean standard deviation of order \(n^{-q/2}\). Moreover, this fluctuation survives in the coefficient. Put
\[
\overline Z_n=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_i,\qquad
\overline V_n=n^{-1}\sum_iV_i,\qquad
S_n=n^{-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\tfrac12)V_i.
\]
Since \(\xi_b=(2Z_b-1)V_b\) are independent Rademacher variables,
\[
S_n=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}m_{b,n}\xi_b,
\qquad
\sigma_n^2:=\mathbb E[S_n^2]
=\frac{1}{4n^2}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}m_{b,n}^2
=\Theta(n^{-q}).
\]
Independence and centering across blocks give
\(\mathbb E[S_n^4]\leq3\sigma_n^4\). This follows by expanding the fourth power: only terms with indices occurring in pairs remain, and each Rademacher fourth moment equals the squared second moment. With
\(A_n=\{S_n^2\geq\sigma_n^2/2\}\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sigma_n^2=\mathbb E[S_n^2]
\leq \frac{\sigma_n^2}{2}
  +\{\mathbb E[S_n^4]\mathbb P_n(A_n)\}^{1/2},
\]
and consequently \(\mathbb P_n(A_n)\geq1/12\). Thus
\(S_n\) is not \(o_P(n^{-q/2})\), and it cannot be \(O_P(n^{-r})\) for any \(r>q/2\).

In this two-period design, let
\[
E_n=\{0<\overline Z_n<1\}.
\]
There are \(B_n\) independent block-level treatment draws and every block has positive size, so
\[
\mathbb P_n(E_n^c)=2^{1-B_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
On \(E_n\), the sample normal matrix is nonsingular and the exact fixed-effect coefficient is the slope from the cross-unit regression of the outcome change \(V_i\) on the treatment change \(Z_i\):
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}
=\frac{n^{-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\overline Z_n)V_i}
       {n^{-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\overline Z_n)^2}.
\]
On \(E_n^c\), retain any coefficient selected by the definition's arbitrary-minimizer convention; because \(\mathbb P_n(E_n^c)\to0\), this selection does not alter any conclusion in probability below. The same block-variance calculation gives
\(\overline Z_n-\tfrac12=O_P(n^{-q/2})\) and
\(\overline V_n=O_P(n^{-q/2})\). Hence, on \(E_n\),
\[
n^{-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\overline Z_n)V_i
=S_n-(\overline Z_n-\tfrac12)\overline V_n
=S_n+O_P(n^{-q}),
\]
whereas the binary identity gives
\[
n^{-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\overline Z_n)^2
=\overline Z_n(1-\overline Z_n)
=\frac14+O_P(n^{-q}).
\]
The coefficient target is zero, so, on \(E_n\),
\(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}=4S_n+o_P(n^{-q/2})\). Since \(\mathbb P_n(E_n^c)\to0\), the preceding fourth-moment lower bound for \(S_n\) implies that \(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{ex}}\) is not \(o_P(n^{-q/2})\), irrespective of the arbitrary minimizer selected on \(E_n^c\). This block construction proves that no uniformly faster coefficient rate follows from the three published regularity conditions alone; it invokes no central limit theorem and makes no asymptotic-normality claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-loading-body-inverse-optimization]\label{thm:exact-loading-body-inverse-optimization}
Fix integer \(T,p\), an explicitly listed binary support \(\mathcal S=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\), rational \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon\leq1/k\), and a rational target tensor \(Q\).  The following exact procedure terminates.

First it computes \(H_{\mathcal S}\).  If \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is singular, it returns a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Q^d\) such that \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every \(a<b\); then \(G(q)\) is singular for every \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\) and \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}=\varnothing\).

If \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is nonsingular, then
\[
 H_{\mathcal S}\succ0,
 \qquad
 G(q)\succeq\epsilon^2H_{\mathcal S},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}\{G(q)\}\geq
 \epsilon^2\lambda_{\min}(H_{\mathcal S})>0
\]
for every \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\).  The body \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\) is then a compact semialgebraic set, with no convexity assertion.  Cylindrical algebraic decomposition decides exactly whether \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\) from the existential polynomial formula
\[
 \exists q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\quad
 G(q)Q_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
 \quad\text{for every }t,\ell.
\]
In the feasible case it returns a witnessing law as exact real-algebraic data, which need not be rational.

In the nonsingular branch, exact real-algebraic optimization returns
\[
 c^{\star}(Q)=\min_{q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)}\mathcal C(q,Q),
\]
an algebraic minimizing law, its closest loading tensor, and a cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition certificate of the universal lower bound.  Membership holds if and only if \(c^{\star}(Q)=0\).  If \(c^{\star}(Q)>0\), the procedure also returns a rational isolating interval for \(c^{\star}(Q)\) with positive lower endpoint; together with the universal lower-bound certificate, this is a metric-separation certificate.

For every rational \(B\geq0\),
\[
 W_B(q,Q)
 =B\max_{0\leq j\leq p}
       \sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{\ell=0}^p
       |L_{j,t,\ell}(q)-Q_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]
The procedure computes this value exactly at a closest law.  If the minimizer of \(\mathcal C(\cdot,Q)\) is not unique, it returns, by a secondary exact optimization, a law minimizing \(W_B(\cdot,Q)\) over all closest laws.  The procedure is exact and terminating on every finite rational input, but generic cylindrical algebraic decomposition can be doubly exponential in the number of variables.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(q_a\) for the coordinate assigned to \(s_a\).  The definitions of \(D_s\), \(J\), and the pair contributions show that \(B_{ab}\), \(b_{ab,t,\ell}\), and \(H_{\mathcal S}\) are rational matrices or vectors computable by finitely many exact operations.  Moreover, because \(J=J^{\top}=J^2\), for every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 x^{\top}B_{ab}x
 =\frac1T x^{\top}E_{ab}^{\top}JE_{ab}x
 =\frac1T\lVert JE_{ab}x\rVert_2^2\geq0.
\]
Thus every \(B_{ab}\) and their sum \(H_{\mathcal S}\) are positive semidefinite.

Suppose first that \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is singular.  Rational Gaussian elimination returns a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Q^d\) with \(H_{\mathcal S}v=0\).  The preceding sum-of-squares identity gives
\[
 0=v^{\top}H_{\mathcal S}v
 =\frac1T\sum_{a<b}\lVert JE_{ab}v\rVert_2^2,
\]
so \(JE_{ab}v=0\) for every pair.  For every \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\), the established pair identity yields
\[
 G(q)v=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab}v=0,
\]
because \(B_{ab}v=T^{-1}E_{ab}^{\top}J(JE_{ab}v)=0\).  Hence every such \(G(q)\) is singular.  The defining condition \(\det H_{\mathcal S}\neq0\) for the full-rank body fails, so \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}=\varnothing\).  This proves the first branch and its rational certificate.

Now suppose that \(H_{\mathcal S}\) is nonsingular.  A nonsingular positive-semidefinite symmetric matrix is positive definite, so \(H_{\mathcal S}\succ0\).  If \(q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\), then \(q_aq_b\geq\epsilon^2\) for every pair.  Therefore, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x^{\top}\{G(q)-\epsilon^2H_{\mathcal S}\}x
 &=\sum_{a<b}(q_aq_b-\epsilon^2)x^{\top}B_{ab}x\\
 &\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \(G(q)\succeq\epsilon^2H_{\mathcal S}\).  Taking the infimum of the Rayleigh quotient over \(\lVert x\rVert_2=1\) gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{G(q)\}
 \geq\epsilon^2\lambda_{\min}(H_{\mathcal S})>0.
\]
Thus every \(G(q)\) is invertible, uniformly over the probability-floor domain.

The set \(\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\) is nonempty because its uniform point has coordinates \(1/k\geq\epsilon\).  It is closed and bounded in the finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^k\), hence compact.  The maps
\[
 q\longmapsto G(q)=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bB_{ab},
 \qquad
 q\longmapsto h_{t,\ell}(q)=\sum_{a<b}q_aq_bb_{ab,t,\ell}
\]
are polynomial.  The displayed eigenvalue bound keeps \(\det G(q)\) away from zero, so the adjugate formula
\[
 L_{\cdot,t,\ell}(q)
 =\frac{\operatorname{adj}\{G(q)\}h_{t,\ell}(q)}{\det G(q)}
\]
is continuous on the whole domain.  The polynomial graph in the definition of \(\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\) has exactly one tensor \(\mathsf X=L(q)\) over each \(q\).  Consequently
\[
 \mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}
 =\{L(q):q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\}
\]
is the continuous image of a compact set and is compact.  The graph is described by the rational polynomial equalities
\[
 G(q)\mathsf X_{\cdot,t,\ell}-h_{t,\ell}(q)=0
\]
and by the linear equality and inequalities defining \(\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)\).  The cited cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition theorem applied to the existential projection onto \(\mathsf X\) therefore proves that the body is semialgebraic and computes a defining quantifier-free formula.  Compactness does not imply convexity, and no convexity step has been used.

Substitution of the rational tensor \(Q\) for \(\mathsf X\) gives the finite first-order formula
\[
 \exists(q_1,\ldots,q_k)\quad
 \left[
   \sum_{a=1}^kq_a=1
   \ \wedge\ 
   \bigwedge_{a=1}^k q_a\geq\epsilon
   \ \wedge\ 
   \bigwedge_{t=1}^T\bigwedge_{\ell=0}^p
   G(q)Q_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
 \right].
\]
Every scalar equality in this formula is polynomial with rational coefficients.  Cylindrical algebraic decomposition therefore terminates and decides it.  When it is true, projection and lifting return a sample point in the feasible cell.  Its coordinates are represented exactly by defining polynomials together with isolating intervals and signs; such a real-algebraic witness need not have rational coordinates.

It remains to certify the closest point.  On the compact domain, \(q\mapsto L(q)\) is continuous, and a finite sum of absolute values is continuous.  Hence \(q\mapsto\mathcal C(q,Q)\) attains its minimum \(c^{\star}(Q)\).  To expose the exact polynomial optimization, introduce a tensor \(\mathsf X\), nonnegative auxiliary variables \(u_{j,t,\ell}\), and a scalar \(z\), and form the formula
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi(z):\quad \exists q,\mathsf X,u\quad
 &q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S),\\
 &G(q)\mathsf X_{\cdot,t,\ell}=h_{t,\ell}(q)
       &&\text{for every }t,\ell,\\
 &u_{j,t,\ell}\geq \mathsf X_{j,t,\ell}-Q_{j,t,\ell},\quad
  u_{j,t,\ell}\geq Q_{j,t,\ell}-\mathsf X_{j,t,\ell}
       &&\text{for every }j,t,\ell,\\
 &\sum_{j,t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}\leq z.
\end{aligned}
\]
For fixed \(q\), the smallest possible value of the displayed sum is exactly
\[
 \sum_{j,t,\ell}|L_{j,t,\ell}(q)-Q_{j,t,\ell}|
 =\mathcal C(q,Q).
\]
It follows in both directions that
\[
 \Phi(z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there exists }q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S)
 \text{ with }\mathcal C(q,Q)\leq z.
\]
Cylindrical algebraic decomposition eliminates \(q,\mathsf X,u\) and decomposes the real \(z\)-line into cells on which \(\Phi\) has constant truth value.  Since the minimum is attained, the least true endpoint is \(c^{\star}(Q)\); the endpoint is real algebraic and is returned with defining polynomial and rational isolating interval.  Lifting over the endpoint returns an algebraic minimizer \(q^{\star}\) and its exact loading \(L(q^{\star})\).  The same sign-invariant decomposition and truth table certify
\[
 \neg\exists q,\mathsf X,u\quad
 \left[
  q\in\Delta_{\epsilon}(\mathcal S),\quad
  G(q)\mathsf X=h(q),\quad
  u\geq\pm(\mathsf X-Q),\quad
  \sum_{j,t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}<c^{\star}(Q)
 \right],
\]
which is the promised universal lower-bound certificate.

Because \(\mathcal C(q,Q)\geq0\), membership of \(Q\) gives \(c^{\star}(Q)=0\).  Conversely, if \(c^{\star}(Q)=0\), attainment gives a \(q^{\star}\) for which every nonnegative summand in \(\mathcal C(q^{\star},Q)\) is zero, so \(L(q^{\star})=Q\) and \(Q\in\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon}\).  If \(c^{\star}(Q)>0\), exact algebraic-number isolation returns rationals \(r_-<c^{\star}(Q)<r_+\) with \(r_->0\).  The universal certificate implies
\[
 \lVert \mathsf X-Q\rVert_1\geq c^{\star}(Q)>r_-
 \quad\text{for every }\mathsf X\in\mathfrak B_{\mathcal S,p,\epsilon},
\]
so \((r_-,r_+)\), the minimizing witness, and the cylindrical-algebraic-decomposition truth certificate separate \(Q\) metrically from the body.  This conclusion does not assert the existence of a linear separating hyperplane.

Finally fix \(B\geq0\), put
\[
 a_{j,t,\ell}=L_{j,t,\ell}(q)-Q_{j,t,\ell},
\]
and let \(|\zeta_{t,\ell}|\leq B\).  For each row \(j\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{t,\ell}a_{j,t,\ell}\zeta_{t,\ell}\right|
 \leq B\sum_{t,\ell}|a_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(j\) and then the supremum over \(\zeta\) proves the upper bound
\[
 W_B(q,Q)\leq
 B\max_j\sum_{t,\ell}|a_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]
Choose a maximizing row \(j^{\star}\) and set
\[
 \zeta_{t,\ell}=B\operatorname{sgn}(a_{j^{\star},t,\ell}),
\]
with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\).  This admissible effect array attains the right-hand side in row \(j^{\star}\), proving
\[
 W_B(q,Q)=B\max_j\sum_{t,\ell}|a_{j,t,\ell}|.
\]

To make tie-breaking among closest laws exact, describe absolute values by nonnegative variables \(u_{j,t,\ell}\) satisfying
\[
 u_{j,t,\ell}^2=(\mathsf X_{j,t,\ell}-Q_{j,t,\ell})^2
\]
and impose \(\sum_{j,t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}=c^{\star}(Q)\).  This is precisely the compact semialgebraic set of closest laws and their loadings.  Introduce \(w\) with
\[
 w\geq B\sum_{t,\ell}u_{j,t,\ell}
 \quad\text{for every }j
\]
and minimize \(w\) over that set.  The same elimination and lifting argument returns an algebraic minimizer and the exact secondary optimum, which equals \(W_B\) by the formula just proved.  All formulas use finitely many rational polynomials; algebraic endpoints from the first optimization can equivalently be constrained by their defining polynomials and isolating sign conditions.  Thus the entire procedure terminates.  The cited general algorithm is potentially doubly exponential in the number of variables, so this proof supplies an exact finite-instance inverse analysis, not a closed ordered/combinatorial chamber law or a polynomial-time guarantee.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The probability-vector graph preserves all pair-product compatibility constraints and turns loading-body membership, closest-loading loss, and bounded-effect contamination into exact finite real-algebraic problems. \textit{Gap.} Pointwise rational diagnostics and low-dimensional pair-mass inverses do not decide membership or optimize contamination on every exceptional finite-support stratum. \textit{Consumer.} The OpenTable state-of-emergency analysis and public reshaped inverse-probability workflow can return an exact feasible law or a certified closest loading with its worst-case bounded-effect contamination.
