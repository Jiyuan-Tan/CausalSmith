\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Heterogeneous sites may supply observational, interventional, and genuine Layer-3 partial-copy laws.  Certified extraction yields local district rows, while exact graph-compatible canonical fibers retain every cross-district complete-menu equation on the original observed alphabets.  The hybrid procedure returns an allowed rational functional when the local recursion identifies the query and otherwise gives an exact real-semialgebraic range or an evidence-status rejection.  Global regime-block acquisition may itself establish conditional definability: only the full acquired fiber must be uniformly positive, every candidate subset is screened exactly, and exhaustive search returns all least-cost subsets preserving the full-menu arm set.  The local LP remains the cheaper special case with a checked positive evidence bound on its base face, and the Boolean pushforward witness shows that neither local nor global arm elimination is submodular in general.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} CTFTR treats heterogeneous Layer-1 and Layer-2 transport, CTFIDU+ treats positive one-specification Layer-3 identification, and transported bandit work does not give an exact finite sparse-ADMG acquisition objective whose purchased menu may establish conditional definability.

\paragraph{Niche.} District-closed response-block invariance supports a fast local row calculus, while the exact canonical fallback retains all complete cross-menu polynomial information.  Subset-specific positivity separates the causal question of whether a conditional is defined from the numerical question of which arms remain optimal.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves exact saturated and sparse-canonical fixed-alphabet range characterizations, a terminating sound rational fast path with zero-denominator rejection, sharp conditional bounds and pruning certificates, a checked-positive local LP acquisition layer, and an exact global acquisition layer that screens every augmented fiber and permits the full menu to create uniform definability.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:recursive-compatibility]\label{ass:recursive-compatibility}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(M^s\) is a recursive finite-state SCM compatible with \(G\) on \(\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (recursive finite-state SCM compatibility, \cite{Pearl2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:district-factorization]\label{ass:district-factorization}
In every domain \(s\), the exogenous response blocks associated with distinct elements of \(\mathcal D(G)\) are mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (semi-Markovian district independence, \cite{ShpitserPearl2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:district-kernel-invariance]\label{ass:district-kernel-invariance}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\) and \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\) with \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), the joint law of the structural response maps on \(D\) in \(M^s\) equals its law in \(M^\star\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A shared laboratory pipeline, record-linkage platform, or policy module can preserve the entire latent response block for a confounded district across sites. This is deliberately stronger than ordinary vertex-level selection-diagram invariance because Layer-3 laws depend on cross-world coupling inside the district.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:district-closure]\label{ass:district-closure}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\) and \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\), either \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\) or \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Site changes often act on an entire confounded measurement or policy block. Whole-district selection permits arbitrary changes inside that block while ruling out an unsupported splice of cross-world dependence across only part of it.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:typed-menu-realizability]\label{ass:typed-menu-realizability}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\) and \(a\in\mathcal A_s\), descriptor \(a\) declares a finite counterfactual tuple \(T_a\), a finite output alphabet \(\mathcal Y_a\), and a deterministic map \(f_a\) into \(\mathcal Y_a\), and
\[
P_a^s=(f_a)_\#\mathcal L_{M^s}(T_a),
\]
where an unpostprocessed descriptor has the identity output map.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (counterfactual-regime realizability, \cite{RaghavanBareinboim2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:dctftr-grammar]\label{def:dctftr-grammar}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{DCTFTR syntax,\allowbreak{} configurations,\allowbreak{} and terminals}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix canonical finite orders on vertices, domains, descriptors, districts, assignments, complete response profiles, and generated work-DAG identifiers.  The counterfactual syntax is
\[
\mathsf{Atom}::=[s]V_\alpha=v,
\qquad
\mathsf{Event}::=\top\mid\bot\mid\mathsf{Atom}
\mid\mathsf{Event}\wedge\mathsf{Event}
\mid\mathsf{Event}\vee\mathsf{Event},
\]
where \(\alpha\) is a finite well-typed intervention or partial-copy term.  A descriptor declares a finite returned counterfactual tuple \(T_a\), a finite output alphabet \(\mathcal Y_a\), and a deterministic map \(f_a\).  Its output cell is the event \(\{f_a(T_a)=y\}\); the parser uses the pointwise preimage identity
\[
\mathbf 1\{f_a(T_a)=y\}
=\sum_{t:f_a(t)=y}\mathbf 1\{T_a=t\}
\tag{1}
\]
only for normalization, locality tables, and response-profile evaluation.  The expression leaf for this cell is the directly supplied number \(P_a^s(y)\), not a collection of unsupplied tuple-cell probabilities.  Normalized events are canonical disjoint normal forms of consistent unnested atoms, including contradiction and sure-event forms.

Identified expressions have grammar
\[
\mathsf{Expr}::=
\operatorname{Cell}(s,a,y)\mid\operatorname{Rat}(r)
\mid\sum_{j\in J}\mathsf{Expr}_j
\mid\prod_{j\in J}\mathsf{Expr}_j
\mid\operatorname{Quot}(F,H,\nu_H),
\]
where \(J\) is finite, \(r\in\mathbb Q\), and \(\nu_H\) is an independently checked nonzero certificate on the declared fiber.  The phase-indexed work-node grammar is
\[
\begin{split}
\mathsf{Goal}::={}&
\operatorname{Raw}(\tau,e)
\mid\operatorname{Normed}(\tau,e,\nu)
\mid\operatorname{AncClosed}(\tau,H,e,\alpha)
\mid\operatorname{NeedRows}(\tau,D,C,b,\ell)\\
&\mid\operatorname{NeedFace}(\tau,D,b,A,m,Z,\kappa)
\mid\operatorname{Sum}(J,\mathrm{children})
\mid\operatorname{Prod}(\mathrm{children})
\mid\operatorname{Resolved}(F,\delta),
\end{split}
\]
with \(\tau\in\{\mathrm{num},\mathrm{den}\}\) and an accepting locality trace \(\ell\) on every row-needed node.  A configuration \(\mathsf{Config}\) is a finite well-typed acyclic DAG of these nodes, two distinguished roots, and a checked transition trace.

For \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), raw, normalized, and ancestor-closed nodes denote their target-domain event probabilities.  A row or face node at \((D,b)\) denotes \(b^{\mathsf T}q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\), a resolved node denotes \(F(\mathcal P)\), and sum and product nodes denote the finite sum and product of their children.  Configuration denotation is the ordered pair of root denotations.  Normalization derivations check consistency, unnesting, \((1)\), and finite disjoint Boolean identities pointwise.  Ancestor derivations enumerate every marginalized assignment.  Split derivations expand over the original districts \(\mathcal D(G)\), never over districts newly formed in an induced subgraph.  Extraction derivations are finite \(\mathsf{XEvent}\)- and \(\mathsf{XExpr}\)-trees with exhaustive \(\operatorname{SupportLocal}_D\) tables, separate numerator and context rows, and independent positivity certificates; they contain no semantic row equality.  A face node is well typed only after literal verification of every equation in \(A_Dq_D^\star=m_D\).  Its row-space and forced-zero certificates use exact rational or real-algebraic linear arithmetic.  Zero, nonzero, and strict-positivity certificates are independently checked rational identities, exact linear certificates, or displayed finite preordering identities; none may consist of the conclusion it certifies.

The terminals are
\[
\mathsf{Result}::=
\operatorname{ID}(F,\delta)
\mid\operatorname{ZERO\_EVIDENCE}(F_E,z)
\mid\operatorname{FAIL}(t).
\]
Failure traces include ill-typed normalization or deterministic-preimage parsing, undefined original-district splitting, failed locality or extraction, \(\operatorname{FACE\_NOT\_IDENTIFIED}\), \(\operatorname{NO\_ADMISSIBLE\_FACTOR}\), \(\operatorname{DENOMINATOR\_MAY\_VANISH}\), \(\operatorname{DENOMINATOR\_POSITIVITY\_UNCERTIFIED}\), and \(\operatorname{STUCK}\).  For a nonterminal configuration define
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{lex}}
=(r_N,r_A,r_S,r_X,r_F,r_\Sigma,r_\Pi,r_C)\in\mathbb N^8,
\]
counting respectively raw, normalized, ancestor-closed, row-needed, face-needed, unresolved-sum, unresolved-product nodes, and one terminalization flag; every terminal has rank zero.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{G}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal X}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal P}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:cross-menu-preservation-handle]\label{def:cross-menu-preservation-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Cross-\allowbreak{}menu face-\allowbreak{}preservation handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{preserve}}\) starts from the exact feasible face \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) and tracks both its affine hull and forced-zero coordinates through ancestor restriction, fixing, district splitting, and marginalization in \(\mathsf{DCTFTR}\).

where the declared fiber has equations \(h_i=0\), inequalities \(g_j\geq0\), and every \(\sigma_J\) is checked coefficient by coefficient to be a finite sum of squares.  All polynomial equalities are checked by exact integer or rational coefficient comparison.

\begin{lemma}[lem:district-cell-linearity]\label{lem:district-cell-linearity}

=r_{s,a,D,h,c}(\omega_D),

\qquad
P_{M^s}(H_h)=v_{s,a,D,h}^{\mathsf T}q_D^s.
\tag{3}

\[

=\frac{N_{s,a,D,h,c}}{H_{s,a,D,h}}
=\frac{r_{s,a,D,h,c}^{\mathsf T}q_D^s}

\tag{4}

All district tables and laws in the designated source are the same for \(i=0\) and \(i=1\): the focal source law is \(\delta_{\omega_0}\) by (3), and every nonfocal law is common by (5).  Therefore
\[
M^{s,(0)}=M^{s,(1)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Every observational, interventional, or declared same-source Layer-3 output is a deterministic recursive evaluation followed by marginalization under this source SCM.  Equation (6) therefore implies equality of all complete laws supplied from domain \(s\), not merely equality of one reported row.  In particular,
\[
\langle\ell,q_D^{s,(0)}\rangle
=\langle\ell,q_D^{s,(1)}\rangle
=\alpha.
\tag{7}
\]
The target values, however, are
\[
\langle\ell,q_D^{\star,(0)}\rangle=\alpha,
\qquad
\langle\ell,q_D^{\star,(1)}\rangle=\beta,
\tag{8}
\]
and they differ by (2).  Equations (3)--(8) are the promised equal-source-law, unequal-target, original-alphabet certificate for every nonconstant real row.

Conversely, if \(\ell\equiv c\) on \(\Omega_D\), normalization of every \(q\in\Delta(\Omega_D)\) gives
\[
\langle\ell,q\rangle
=c\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}q(\omega)
=c.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the target value of a constant row is known without any cross-domain information.  Equations (2)--(8) exclude universal source-only determination for every other row, while (9) proves it for constant rows.  This proves the first equivalence.  Notice that the pair is allowed to differ in a target-domain law and in a further source law whose unselected focal district must equal the target.  Accordingly, the conclusion concerns only information supplied from the designated partially selected source; it does not survive automatically if either kind of extra law is observed.

Now add the premise that the response-profile marginal on \(I\) is invariant between the designated source and target.  Let
\[
\pi_I:\Omega_D\longrightarrow\pi_I(\Omega_D)
\]
be coordinate restriction to the response maps of vertices in \(I\).  The added premise is the finite pushforward equality
\[
(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^s=(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^\star.
\tag{10}
\]
Suppose that \(\ell\) is constant on every fiber of \(\pi_I\).  Then the function
\[
\widetilde\ell(u)=\ell(\omega)
\quad\text{for any }\omega\text{ with }\pi_I(\omega)=u
\tag{11}
\]
is well defined on \(\pi_I(\Omega_D)\), and \(\ell=\widetilde\ell\circ\pi_I\).  Grouping finite sums by projection fibers and using (10) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle\ell,q_D^s\rangle
&=\sum_{u\in\pi_I(\Omega_D)}
  \widetilde\ell(u)(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^s(u)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\pi_I(\Omega_D)}
  \widetilde\ell(u)(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^\star(u)\\
&=\langle\ell,q_D^\star\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Hence fiber constancy is sufficient.

For necessity, suppose that fiber constancy fails.  There are then \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) such that
\[
\pi_I(\omega_0)=\pi_I(\omega_1),
\qquad
\ell(\omega_0)\ne\ell(\omega_1).
\tag{13}
\]
Repeat the construction (3)--(6) with this pair.  In the model \(\mathbf M^{(i)}\), the source and target pushforwards are respectively
\[
(\pi_I)_{\#}\delta_{\omega_0}
=\delta_{\pi_I(\omega_0)}
=\delta_{\pi_I(\omega_1)}
=(\pi_I)_{\#}\delta_{\omega_i}.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus each model satisfies the added marginal-invariance premise (10), the two models still have identical complete laws in source \(s\), and their target row values differ by (13).  This proves necessity and the second scalar equivalence.

Let \(L\in\mathbb R^{k\times|\Omega_D|}\) be any finite matrix with row functions \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k\), and define
\[
Lq=
\bigl(\langle\ell_1,q\rangle,\ldots,
      \langle\ell_k,q\rangle\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{15}
\]
If every row is fiber-constant, equation (12), applied separately to each \(\ell_j\), gives \(Lq_D^s=Lq_D^\star\).  If some row \(\ell_j\) is not fiber-constant, choose a failing pair for that row and apply (13)--(14); the \(j\)-th target components then differ although the complete designated-source laws agree.  This proves the componentwise matrix equivalence under (10).  Without (10), the same argument uses (2)--(9) and shows that componentwise universal transport is equivalent to every row being constant on all of \(\Omega_D\).

The characterizations are finite algorithms.  Under (10), enumerate, for each row \(j\), all pairs \((\omega,\omega')\in\Omega_D^2\) satisfying \(\pi_I(\omega)=\pi_I(\omega')\), and compare \(\ell_j(\omega)\) with \(\ell_j(\omega')\).  Without (10), enumerate all pairs.  This uses at most \(k|\Omega_D|^2\) equality comparisons.  Rational equality is decided exactly by integer cross multiplication after choosing fraction representations.  For an effectively represented real-algebraic entry, exact equality comparison is part of the stipulated effective representation, so the same finite loop is exact.  Exhaustion without a failure certifies the required constancy.  A failure returns \((j,\omega_0,\omega_1)\), and equations (3)--(8), or (3)--(8) together with (14), construct the two-Dirac certificate.  This is the finite leakage test named by Definition \(\mathrm{def:partial\text{-}district\text{-}leakage\text{-}handle}\).

By Definition \(\mathrm{def:incidence\text{-}row}\), binary event cells are scalar rows and hence are a direct special case.  Rows with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), including pairwise arm gaps, arbitrary real-valued reward rows on the finite profile set, and rows used in finite linear certificates are also scalar rows in \(\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}\); the proved equivalences therefore apply to them without alteration.

As the smallest exact stress check, take binary vertices \(X\) and \(Y\) with ADMG \(X\leftrightarrow Y\), let \(D=\{X,Y\}\), and let \(\Delta_s=\{X\}\).  Choose constant response profiles \(\omega_0=(X=0,Y=0)\) and \(\omega_1=(X=1,Y=0)\), and take \(\ell(\omega)=\mathbf 1\{X(\omega)=1\}\).  The two source laws are both \(\delta_{\omega_0}\), whereas the target laws \(\delta_{\omega_0}\) and \(\delta_{\omega_1}\) give exact target values zero and one.  Their \(I=\{Y\}\) marginals are identical, so the same pair also witnesses the fiber criterion.  Restoring district closure excludes this selection pattern because neither \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\) nor \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\) holds.  This verifies on the smallest two-profile instance that the omitted condition is load-bearing.  No division, conditioning, positivity premise, or limiting argument is used anywhere in the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:structural-separation-implies-active-slice]\label{lem:structural-separation-implies-active-slice}
For every district \(D\),
\[
\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P).
\]
The implication is strict in general.  In particular, take two independent binary response coordinates \(Y_0\) and \(Z\) generated by distinct singleton districts, let \(D\) be the district generating \(Y_0\), and supply in the target domain both the complete marginal law of \(Y_0\) and the complete joint law of \((Y_0,Z)\).  The resulting menu satisfies \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) but not \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  If a complete supplied output-cell indicator is independent of \(\omega_D\), then every focal slice is either the zero row or the normalization row.  If it depends only on \(\omega_D\), then every slice is its accepted raw row.  For a deterministic postprocessor this conclusion follows by evaluating its finite preimage indicator, not by adding tuple-cell probabilities to the menu.  In both cases every slice belongs to \(R_D(m_D)\), proving \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).

For strictness, let \(Y_0\) and \(Z\) be independent binary response coordinates in distinct singleton districts, let \(D\) generate \(Y_0\), and supply in the target domain the complete marginal law of \(Y_0\) and the complete joint law of \((Y_0,Z)\).  Write
\[
h_y(\omega_D)=\mathbf 1\{Y_0=y\},
\qquad
k_z(\omega_{-D})=\mathbf 1\{Z=z\}.
\]
The marginal menu retains \(h_y\), whereas a joint cell has cross-district indicator \(h_yk_z\), so structural separation fails.  After fixing \(\omega_{-D}\), however, its focal slice is either zero or \(h_y\), both in the active row space.  Hence active-slice separation holds.  Independent Bernoulli response blocks realize this menu on the original binary alphabets, proving strictness.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proof covers deterministic-preimage output cells as ordinary finite indicators. \textit{Gap.} Structural separation is easier to check but stronger than slice separation. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a baseline-free sufficient route to exact focal projection.

\begin{lemma}[lem:active-slice-implies-baseline-averaged]\label{lem:active-slice-implies-baseline-averaged}
For every district \(D\),
\[
\bigl\{\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing
\text{ and }\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\bigr\}
\Longrightarrow \mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P).
\]
Together with \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\), this gives the convenient hierarchy
\[
\bigl\{\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing
\text{ and }\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\bigr\}
\Longrightarrow
\bigl\{\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing
\text{ and }\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\bigr\}
\Longrightarrow \mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P).
\]
The second implication is strict on finite original alphabets: there is a three-vertex binary DAG and a complete target-domain menu for which \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) holds but \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) fails because two Boolean focal slices outside \(R_D(m_D)\) average to a row inside it under the witnessing nonfocal product law.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\).  District factorization gives the finite nonfocal product law \(\nu_{-D}^{0,s}\).  Under \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\), every focal slice lies in the linear space \(R_D(m_D)\), so
\[
\bar\chi_C^{s,\mathbf M^0}
=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}
\nu_{-D}^{0,s}(\omega_{-D})
\chi_{C,\omega_{-D}}
\in R_D(m_D).
\tag{1}
\]
The same baseline works for every cell and domain, proving \(\mathsf{BASep}_D\).  Composition with the structural-separation lemma gives the full hierarchy.

For strictness, take independent fair binary roots \(X,Z\) feeding the deterministic binary child \(Y=\mathbf 1\{X=Z\}\), and let \(D=\{X\}\).  Supply only the complete target-domain output law of \(Y\).  Then \(A_D=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\) and
\[
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}.
\]
The two focal slices of \(\{Y=1\}\) are \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\), so active-slice separation fails.  Their average under the fair nonfocal law of \(Z\) is \((1/2,1/2)\in R_D(m_D)\); the complementary output cell is analogous.  Focal saturation permits every law of \(X\) while the fair law of \(Z\) and the structural table of \(Y\) stay fixed, so this single compatible baseline proves \(\mathsf{BASep}_D\).  Thus the second implication is strict on the original binary alphabets.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The hierarchy isolates the weakest single-baseline condition actually used by the lift. \textit{Gap.} Pointwise slice membership can fail even when its fixed-law average is active. \textit{Consumer.} Downstream exact local clauses use baseline-averaged separation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-layer3-acquisition-obstruction]\label{thm:finite-layer3-acquisition-obstruction}
For every finite \(\mathsf{CheckedLocalArms}_D\) instance with a finite experiment set \(\mathcal U\), effectively rational or real-algebraic rows, menu values, and positive costs, and a jointly realizable complete experiment menu \(\mathcal K_{\mathcal U}\ne\varnothing\), all minimum-cost Layer-3 regime-block solutions are characterized by enumerating \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\) and, for each arm, solving the finite linear feasibility problem defining \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\).  In general the elimination function \(f\) is not submodular.  In particular, there is a realizable one-domain binary one-district checked instance with two unit-cost experiments, each revealing one complete Boolean postprocessed Layer-3 output law, for which
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1\})
=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_2\})=\{\rho,\sigma\},
\qquad
\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1,e_2\})=
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\{\sigma\}.
\]
Thus the marginal gain of \(e_2\) is zero at \(S=\varnothing\) and one at \(T_{\mathrm{exp}}=\{e_1\}\), violating diminishing returns.  For this instance the exact acquisition problem is
\[
\min_{x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}}x_1+x_2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad x_1+x_2\geq2,
\]
with unique solution \((1,1)\) and cost two; the multiplier \(y=1\) for the covering inequality certifies the matching lower bound for its continuous relaxation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite checked instance satisfying the hypotheses and fix
\(S\subseteq\mathcal U\).  Joint realizability and
\(\mathrm{def:regime\text{-}block\text{-}acquisition}\) give
\[
\varnothing\ne\mathcal K_{\mathcal U}
\subseteq\mathcal K_S\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\tag{1}
\]
The checked-local-arms record gives the two separate conditions
\(\varepsilon_D>0\) and
\[
d_D^{\mathsf T}q\geq\varepsilon_D
\qquad(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus division is legal throughout every subface in \((1)\), and for any two arms
\[
Q_\rho(q)\geq Q_\sigma(q)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{n_{\rho,D}^{\mathsf T}q}{d_D^{\mathsf T}q}
\geq
\frac{n_{\sigma,D}^{\mathsf T}q}{d_D^{\mathsf T}q}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\geq0.
\tag{3}
\]
The last step multiplies by the common strictly positive denominator from \((2)\);
it does not define the causal conditional by its numerator.

For a fixed arm, the membership test is exactly
\[
\rho\in\operatorname{PO}(S)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists q\in\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}\quad
\begin{cases}
q(\omega)\geq0,&\omega\in\Omega_D,\\
A_Dq=m_D,\\
R_eq=p_e,&e\in S,\\
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\geq0,
&\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
This is a finite linear feasibility problem.  Its feasible set is closed and bounded
because \(A_D\) contains the normalization row with value one.  If it is nonempty,
successively minimizing its finitely many coordinates on the current compact face
produces a singleton face, hence an extreme point.  At an extreme point the equality
rows together with some active inequalities have full column rank: otherwise a
nonzero vector orthogonal to all active rows would allow sufficiently small
movements in both signs while preserving every equality and inequality, contradicting
extremality.  Enumerate the finitely many full-rank active-row subsets.  Gaussian
elimination gives the unique candidate for each subset, and exact sign tests verify
the remaining inequalities.  Effective rational or real-algebraic arithmetic makes
all rank, equality and sign decisions exact.  Hence \((4)\) is computable without a
limit or numerical tolerance.

There are finitely many pairs \((S,\rho)\).  Computing each test \((4)\) determines
every \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\), including
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\operatorname{PO}(\mathcal U)\).  Exact finite cost
comparison then retains all subsets satisfying the equality constraint and having
minimum total cost, including all ties.  This proves the general characterization.

For the obstruction, consider the one-domain binary bow graph on
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\) and \(Y\), with
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\) and \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\leftrightarrow Y\).  It is acyclic
and has one district \(D=\{T_{\mathrm{tr}},Y\}\).  A deterministic district profile
is
\[
\omega=(t^{\mathrm{nat}},Y_0,Y_1)\in\{0,1\}^3.
\tag{5}
\]
Supply the complete observational law
\[
P(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=0)=1,
\qquad P(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=1)=0.
\tag{6}
\]
The corresponding raw rows and nonnegativity force zero mass on profiles with
\(t^{\mathrm{nat}}=1\) and impose no restriction on the four active profiles
\[
\omega_1=(0,0,0),\quad
\omega_2=(0,0,1),\quad
\omega_3=(0,1,0),\quad
\omega_4=(0,1,1).
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the base face is the simplex spanned by their four point masses.

This entire face is realizable on the declared binary alphabets.  Given any law
\(q\) on \((7)\), take one finite district latent block \(U_D\sim q\), set
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=t^{\mathrm{nat}}(U_D)\), and set
\(Y=Y_{T_{\mathrm{tr}}}(U_D)\); under intervention
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=t\), the latter equation returns \(Y_t(U_D)\).  The shared block
is compatible with the bidirected edge and the last equation with the directed edge.
There is one domain, so no transport equality remains to check.  Equivalently, all
cells are local to the only district; nonemptiness and
\(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\) realize every
boundary point as an original-alphabet focal-saturated SCM.

Let \(E=\top\) and take the two arm reward events

B_\rho=\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(1,1)\},
\qquad
B_\sigma=\{(Y_0,Y_1)=(1,1)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
On the active profiles, their numerator and evidence rows are
\[
n_{\rho,D}=(1,1,1,0),\qquad
n_{\sigma,D}=(0,0,0,1),\qquad
d_D=(1,1,1,1).
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d_D^{\mathsf T}q=1\) and \(\varepsilon_D=1\) is a valid checked lower
bound; zero evidence is impossible.

Let \(\mathcal U=\{e_1,e_2\}\) with unit costs.  Experiment \(e_i\) declares the

\[
F_1=\mathbf1\{Y_0=Y_1\},
\qquad
F_2=\mathbf1\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(0,0)\},
\tag{10}
\]
and reveals the complete laws \(P(F_i=1)=1\) and \(P(F_i=0)=0\).  These are
complete Boolean pushforwards, not a disclosure of the unsupplied joint tuple law.
Their output-one rows on \((7)\) are

r_1=(1,0,0,1),
\qquad
r_2=(0,1,1,1),
\tag{11}
\]
and their output-zero rows are the complements.  Normalization makes each complement
equation exactly redundant with the corresponding output-one equation.  Hence

     \delta_{\omega_3},\delta_{\omega_4}\},\\
\mathcal K_{\{e_1\}}

\mathcal K_{\{e_2\}}

\mathcal K_{\{e_1,e_2\}}

\end{aligned}
\tag{12}

The last point mass realizes both complete experiment laws, proving joint

\tag{14}
\]

\(x_i=\mathbf1\{e_i\in S\}\), the exact problem is

\min_{x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}}x_1+x_2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad x_1+x_2\geq2.
\tag{15}

nonnegative multiplier \(y=1\) on the covering inequality proves
\(x_1+x_2\geq2y=2\), and \((1,1)\) attains this lower bound.  This is the claimed

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-fixing-calculus-soundness]\label{lem:finite-fixing-calculus-soundness}
Every finite fixing trace accepted by the extraction calculus preserves the represented event probability.  More precisely, if the trace expands its input and output into finite response-profile expressions, uses division only by a separately certified positive context probability, and its exact checker verifies the cleared-denominator polynomial identity, then the input and output expressions agree in every \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an accepted finite fixing trace \(\tau\), fix a domain label \(s\) occurring in the trace, and fix \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). By \(\mathrm{def:compatibility\text{-}fiber}\), the family \(\mathbf M\) satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\). Acyclicity and the finite response carrier of \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\) imply that recursive substitution assigns to every event \(A\) occurring in the trace a total deterministic indicator
\[
 I_A:\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}\Omega_D\longrightarrow\{0,1\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The vertex set, its district partition, every declared state space, and every response-profile set are finite; hence the product and every sum below are finite. Independence of the district response blocks, which is \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\), gives
\[
 P_{M^s}(A)
 =\sum_{\omega\in\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}\Omega_D}
 I_A(\omega)\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}q_D^s(\omega_D).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus each unconditional response-profile event probability is a polynomial with integer coefficients in the finitely many coordinates \(q_D^s(\omega_D)\). The same argument applies separately to every source-labelled probability leaf. Algebraic combinations of leaves are therefore polynomials in the union of their coordinate families; this observation does not posit any joint probability law across distinct domain labels.

We first justify every strict-positivity certificate that can guard a divisor in \(\tau\). Let \(x\) collect the finitely many response-law coordinates named by such a certificate. The typing rules of \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\) require its declared compatibility fiber to be represented by checked constraints
\[
 h_i(x)=0\quad(1\le i\le r),
 \qquad
 g_j(x)\ge 0\quad(1\le j\le t),
\tag{3}
\]
and require the response coordinates of every model in that fiber to satisfy these constraints. For a rational-constant certificate, exact normalization verifies a rational lower bound \(\varepsilon>0\) for the certified polynomial \(p\). For a linear face certificate, the checker verifies rational multipliers and the polynomial identity
\[
 p(x)-\varepsilon
 =\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_i h_i(x)
  +\sum_{j=1}^{t}\lambda_j g_j(x),
 \qquad \varepsilon>0,\quad \lambda_j\ge 0.
\tag{4}
\]
For a preordering certificate, it verifies
\[
 p(x)-\varepsilon
 =\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tau_i(x)h_i(x)
  +\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}}
    \sigma_J(x)\prod_{j\in J}g_j(x),
 \qquad \varepsilon>0,
\tag{5}
\]
where every displayed \(\sigma_J\) is exactly checked to be a finite sum of squares of rational polynomials. Substitution of (3) into (4) gives \(p(x)\ge\varepsilon>0\). In (5), the equality terms vanish, each factor \(g_j(x)\) is nonnegative, every finite product of those factors is nonnegative, and every \(\sigma_J(x)\) is nonnegative; hence again \(p(x)\ge\varepsilon>0\). The rational-constant case has the same conclusion by its exact comparison. These cases exhaust the positivity constructors in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\). Consequently every context probability used as a divisor by the accepted trace is strictly positive at \(\mathbf M\).

We next verify the probability constructors. The false and true events have indicators zero and one. For a finite pairwise-disjoint family \((A_j:j\in J)\), pointwise equality of indicators gives
\[
 I_{\bigsqcup_{j\in J}A_j}=\sum_{j\in J}I_{A_j},
\]
and substitution in (2) yields
\[
 P_{M^s}\!\left(\bigsqcup_{j\in J}A_j\right)
 =\sum_{j\in J}P_{M^s}(A_j).
\tag{6}
\]
Marginalization is (6) applied to the finite partition indexed by the marginalized variable's declared state space. Pointwise \(I_{A^{\mathsf c}}=1-I_A\), so normalization of (2) gives
\[
 P_{M^s}(A^{\mathsf c})=1-P_{M^s}(A).
\tag{7}
\]
An algebraic product constructor preserves equality of already interpreted scalar expressions. If a product is annotated as the probability of a district-split event, its annotation is valid because \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) makes the indicated district response blocks independent. A type-correct substitution is a homomorphism for rational constants, finite sums, differences, and products, and therefore preserves every checked polynomial identity.

For a conditional constructor with numerator event \(A\cap H\) and context event \(H\), the certificate argument gives
\[
 P_{M^s}(H)>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The quotient is therefore defined, and the definition of conditional probability gives
\[
 P_{M^s}(A\mid H)
 =\frac{P_{M^s}(A\cap H)}{P_{M^s}(H)}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (2), (6), (7), and (9), by structural induction over the finite trace syntax in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\), identify the semantic value of every subexpression of \(\tau\).

Let \(L_\tau\) and \(R_\tau\) be the resulting response-profile expressions at the input and output of the trace, and let \(H_1,\ldots,H_J\) be its distinct divisor expressions. Clearing denominators uses a multiplier
\[
 \Delta_\tau(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{J}H_j(x)^{k_j},
 \qquad k_j\in\mathbb N_{>0},
\tag{10}
\]
with the empty product interpreted as one. By (8),
\[
 \Delta_\tau(x(\mathbf M))>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Acceptance of \(\tau\), by the exact-checker clause of \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\), means that rational normalization and coefficient comparison verify the formal polynomial identity
\[
 \Delta_\tau L_\tau-\Delta_\tau R_\tau=0.
\tag{12}
\]
All coordinate sets and sums are finite, so substitution of the response-law coordinates \(x(\mathbf M)\) into (12) is valid and yields
\[
 \Delta_\tau(x(\mathbf M))L_\tau(x(\mathbf M))
 =\Delta_\tau(x(\mathbf M))R_\tau(x(\mathbf M)).
\]
Division by the strictly positive quantity in (11) proves
\[
 L_\tau(x(\mathbf M))=R_\tau(x(\mathbf M)).
\]
Since \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) was arbitrary, every accepted finite fixing trace preserves its represented event probability throughout the compatibility fiber, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-extraction-calculus-soundness]\label{lem:finite-extraction-calculus-soundness}
Every checked derivation \(\delta:A\Downarrow F\) in the finite extraction calculus satisfies
\[
F(\mathcal P)=P_{M^s}(A)
\]
for every \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  Every accepted positivity certificate is valid on its declared compatibility fiber.  Moreover, \(\operatorname{SupportLocal}_D(A)\) terminates and accepts if and only if the recursive indicator of \(A\) is independent of the nonfocal response profiles; upon acceptance its constructed row \(\operatorname{row}_D(A)\) equals that indicator for every nonfocal profile.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a domain label \(s\), a checked derivation \(\delta:A\Downarrow F\), and \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). We first prove the positivity assertion needed by quotient nodes. For any accepted positivity certificate, let \(x\) be its finite vector of response-law coordinates and write the checked description of its declared compatibility fiber as
\[
 h_i(x)=0\quad(1\le i\le r),
 \qquad
 g_j(x)\ge 0\quad(1\le j\le t).
\tag{13}
\]
The typing clause of \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\) ensures that the coordinates of every model in the declared fiber satisfy (13). An accepted rational-constant certificate exactly verifies \(p(x)\ge\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\in\mathbb Q\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). An accepted linear face certificate exactly verifies
\[
 p(x)-\varepsilon
 =\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_i h_i(x)
  +\sum_{j=1}^{t}\lambda_j g_j(x),
 \qquad \varepsilon>0,\quad\lambda_j\ge 0,
\tag{14}
\]
and an accepted preordering certificate exactly verifies
\[
 p(x)-\varepsilon
 =\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tau_i(x)h_i(x)
  +\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}}
    \sigma_J(x)\prod_{j\in J}g_j(x),
 \qquad \varepsilon>0,
\tag{15}
\]
where each \(\sigma_J\) is exactly checked to be a finite sum of squares of rational polynomials. On (13), the equality terms in (14) and (15) vanish, all remaining factors are nonnegative, and sums of squares are nonnegative. Therefore every admitted constructor gives
\[
 p(x)\ge\varepsilon>0.
\tag{16}
\]
Exact coefficient comparison establishes (14) and (15) as polynomial identities, so substitution of model coordinates cannot invalidate them. Since these cases exhaust the admitted positivity-certificate grammar, every accepted positivity certificate is valid on its declared compatibility fiber.

We now prove
\[
 F(\mathcal P)=P_{M^s}(A)
\tag{17}
\]
by structural induction on the finite derivation tree in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\). At a supplied-cell leaf, descriptor \(a\) declares a finite primitive tuple \(T_a\), a deterministic finite map \(f_a\), and the returned output \(f_a(T_a)\). By \(\mathrm{ass:typed\text{-}menu\text{-}realizability}\), together with the descriptor semantics in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\), its supplied law is the pushforward
\[
 P_a^s=(f_a)_\#\operatorname{Law}_{M^s}(T_a).
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently, for each declared output cell \(y\), the leaf generator has the direct semantics
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cell}(s,a,y)(\mathcal P)
 &=P_a^s(y)\\
 &=\operatorname{Law}_{M^s}\!\bigl(f_a(T_a)\bigr)(y)\\
 &=P_{M^s}\!\bigl(f_a(T_a)=y\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
This uses the supplied output law itself. The deterministic preimage equality
\[
 \mathbf 1\{f_a(T_a)=y\}
 =\sum_{t:\,f_a(t)=y}\mathbf 1\{T_a=t\}
\tag{20}
\]
is used only to evaluate event indicators, locality truth tables, and response-profile polynomials. In particular, (20) does not make any probability \(P_{M^s}(T_a=t)\) a supplied menu generator. Such a tuple-cell probability is directly available only if the menu separately contains an identity-output descriptor. This proves the induction claim for every supplied leaf without enlarging \(\mathcal P\). Since the deterministic map \(f_a\) is evaluated after \(T_a\) is returned, this evaluation visits no additional structural parent configuration; the carrier in \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\) therefore suffices for (20).

The contradiction and sure-event leaves have probabilities zero and one. If the induction claim holds for \(A_0\), finite complementarity gives
\[
 P_{M^s}(A_0^{\mathsf c})
 =1-P_{M^s}(A_0)
 =1-F_0(\mathcal P).
\tag{21}
\]
If it holds for a finite pairwise-disjoint family \((A_j:j\in J)\), finite additivity gives
\[
 P_{M^s}\!\left(\bigsqcup_{j\in J}A_j\right)
 =\sum_{j\in J}P_{M^s}(A_j)
 =\sum_{j\in J}F_j(\mathcal P).
\tag{22}
\]
The Boolean-normalization constructor forms a finite disjoint truth-table partition, so (21)--(22) prove its case. A marginalization constructor enumerates the finite partition indexed by the marginalized variable's declared nonempty state space and is another application of (22).

For a fixing constructor, the derivation contains an accepted fixing trace and type-correct substitutions for its leaves. By \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}fixing\text{-}calculus\text{-}soundness}\), the trace preserves the represented event probability. Equations (19), (21), and (22), together with the induction hypotheses, identify the semantic values of all substituted leaves, while type-correct substitution preserves rational constants, finite sums, differences, and products. Thus the fixing constructor preserves (17).

An internal kernel quotient is admitted only after checked derivations of its numerator event \(A_0\cap H\) and context event \(H\), together with an independently accepted strict-positivity certificate for that context. The induction hypotheses and (16) give
\[
 F_H(\mathcal P)=P_{M^s}(H)>0.
\tag{23}
\]
Division is therefore legitimate, and the definition of conditional probability yields
\[
 \frac{F_{A_0\cap H}(\mathcal P)}{F_H(\mathcal P)}
 =\frac{P_{M^s}(A_0\cap H)}{P_{M^s}(H)}
 =P_{M^s}(A_0\mid H).
\tag{24}
\]
There is no unguarded division constructor. The supplied-cell, constant, Boolean-normalization, marginalization, fixing, and certified-quotient cases exhaust the checked derivation grammar. This completes the induction and proves (17).

It remains to prove the assertion about \(\operatorname{SupportLocal}_D\). Fix \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\) and define
\[
 \Omega_{-D}
 =\prod_{D'\in\mathcal D(G),\,D'\ne D}\Omega_{D'},
\tag{25}
\]
where an empty product is a singleton. By \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\) and \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\), every factor is finite and recursive evaluation is total. Hence \(A\) has a deterministic indicator
\[
 I_A:\Omega_D\times\Omega_{-D}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}.
\tag{26}
\]
By the exhaustive-checker clause of \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\), \(\operatorname{SupportLocal}_D(A)\) compares
\[
 I_A(\omega_D,\omega_{-D})
 =I_A(\omega_D,\omega'_{-D})
\tag{27}
\]
for every \(\omega_D\in\Omega_D\) and every \(\omega_{-D},\omega'_{-D}\in\Omega_{-D}\). The enumerated set is finite, so the checker terminates. If it accepts, all equalities (27) hold, which is exactly independence of the recursive indicator from the nonfocal response profiles. Conversely, that independence makes every equality (27) true, so the exhaustive checker accepts.

Upon acceptance, let \(\omega_{-D}^{0}\) be the checker's fixed reference profile. Its constructed row is
\[
 \operatorname{row}_D(A)(\omega_D)
 =I_A(\omega_D,\omega_{-D}^{0}).
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (27) and (28) gives, for every \(\omega_D\in\Omega_D\) and every \(\omega_{-D}\in\Omega_{-D}\),
\[
 \operatorname{row}_D(A)(\omega_D)
 =I_A(\omega_D,\omega_{-D}).
\]
If the checker rejects, its first failed comparison in the fixed finite enumeration supplies an explicit triple \((\omega_D,\omega_{-D},\omega'_{-D})\) witnessing dependence on the nonfocal profile. This proves termination, the if-and-only-if acceptance characterization, and correctness of the row constructed upon acceptance.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:global-canonical-acquisition-completeness]\label{thm:global-canonical-acquisition-completeness}
Assume that the exact global acquisition instance of \(\mathrm{def:global\text{-}regime\text{-}block\text{-}acquisition}\) is admissible.  Then \(\Lambda_S\ne\varnothing\) for every \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\), and
\[
S\subseteq T_{\mathrm{exp}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_{T_{\mathrm{exp}}}\subseteq\Lambda_S.
\]
The full-fiber evidence test on \(\Lambda_{\mathcal U}\) returns uniform zero evidence or mixed zero and positive evidence exactly in those two cases and then defines no numerical full-menu arm-ranking benchmark.  In the remaining case \(\operatorname{Pos}_G(\mathcal U)\) holds and the acquisition problem is defined, even if \(\operatorname{Pos}_G(\varnothing)\) fails.

For every \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\), \(\operatorname{Pos}_G(S)\) is decided exactly by one closed quantifier-elimination call.  Whenever it holds, there exists \(\epsilon_S>0\) such that
\[
\Phi_{E,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star)\geq\epsilon_S
\qquad\text{for every }\vartheta\in\Lambda_S,
\]
and, for each \(\rho\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}\), membership \(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\) is decided exactly by one closed existential formula over the reals.  Moreover,
\[
S\subseteq T_{\mathrm{exp}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl(\operatorname{Pos}_G(S)\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Pos}_G(T_{\mathrm{exp}})\bigr),
\]
and, when both possibly-optimal sets are defined,
\[
\operatorname{PO}_G(T_{\mathrm{exp}})
\subseteq\operatorname{PO}_G(S).
\]
Consequently the feasible design family is upward closed.  Exhaustive enumeration returns every minimum-cost solution, including every cost tie, using at most
\[
2^{|\mathcal U|}
\]
subset-positivity quantifier-elimination calls and at most
\[
2^{|\mathcal U|}|\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}|
\]
membership calls, in addition to one joint-realizability call and a fixed full-fiber status classification.

For every positive subset \(S\), \(\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\) is exactly the set of arms that are conditionally optimal in at least one original-alphabet SCM family in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P_S)\).  Thus the subset-specific ranking semantics are exact for every finite sparse ADMG.

Whenever the same augmented information is represented by a local \(\mathsf{CheckedLocalArms}_D\) system, its checked base-face evidence lower bound makes every subset positive and broad compatibility implies
\[
\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\subseteq\operatorname{PO}(S).
\]
If, for that augmented menu, the checked arm events are focal-district local and the focal projection hypotheses of \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\) hold, then equality holds.  Hence the existing local LP acquisition is the uniformly-positive special case and remains an outer pruning relaxation outside those projection hypotheses.

The two-experiment Boolean-pushforward instance of \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}layer3\text{-}acquisition\text{-}obstruction}\) has \(E=\top\), so every subset is positive, its displayed possibly-optimal table also holds with \(\operatorname{PO}_G\), and \(f_G\) is not submodular in general; its unique exact broad-model solution buys both experiments at total cost two.  Separately, there is a realizable one-domain binary singleton-district instance with one complete Boolean-output experiment for which \(\Lambda_{\varnothing}=[0,1]\), the evidence map is \(e(q)=q\), and the acquired law fixes \(q=1\).  Thus base evidence is mixed while the full fiber is uniformly positive; this second instance demonstrates acquisition of definability and is not a second non-submodularity witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(S\subseteq T_{\mathrm{exp}}\subseteq\mathcal U\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:global\text{-}regime\text{-}block\text{-}acquisition}\), the menu
\(\mathcal P_{T_{\mathrm{exp}}}\) contains every complete cell equation in
\(\mathcal P_S\).  Applying the same equations in
\(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}global\text{-}canonical\text{-}fiber}\) therefore gives
\[
\Lambda_{\mathcal U}\subseteq
\Lambda_{T_{\mathrm{exp}}}\subseteq\Lambda_S.
\tag{1}
\]
Admissibility gives \(\Lambda_{\mathcal U}\ne\varnothing\), so every fiber in
\((1)\) is nonempty.  Each is a closed subset of the finite canonical parameter
space and hence is compact; all of its constraints are polynomial equalities and
inequalities.

Put
\[
e(\vartheta)=
\Phi_{E,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star).
\tag{2}
\]
Recursive probability semantics gives \(e\geq0\) on every canonical fiber.  Apply
the reusable block in \(\mathrm{def:hybrid\text{-}result\text{-}grammar}\) to the
nonempty full fiber \(\Lambda_{\mathcal U}\).  Exact quantifier elimination from
\(\mathrm{lem:basu\text{-}mohammad\text{-}nezhad\text{-}quantifier\text{-}elimination}\)
decides precisely the mutually exclusive alternatives
\[
 e\equiv0,
\qquad
\bigl(\min e=0<\max e\bigr),
\qquad
e>0\text{ everywhere on }\Lambda_{\mathcal U}.
\tag{3}
\]
The first two alternatives are exactly the two payload-free failures prescribed by
the acquisition definition.  The last alternative is
\(\operatorname{Pos}_G(\mathcal U)\) by
\(\mathrm{def:global\text{-}subset\text{-}positivity}\), so and only so the numerical
full-menu benchmark is defined.

Fix any \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\).  The defining formula
\[
\neg\exists\vartheta\,
\left[\vartheta\in\Lambda_S\wedge e(\vartheta)=0\right]
\tag{4}
\]
is a closed first-order formula over the reals with coefficients in the effective
ordered ring generated by the complete augmented menu.  The cited
quantifier-elimination result makes \((4)\) one exact decision.  If \((4)\) holds,
continuity of \(e\), compactness and nonemptiness give an attained minimum
\[
\epsilon_S:=\min_{\vartheta\in\Lambda_S}e(\vartheta)>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Conversely, \((5)\) excludes a zero and hence implies \((4)\).  Thus the displayed
predicate is exactly uniform strict positivity, including on a boundary fiber.

The inclusion in \((1)\) proves the positivity monotonicity: if
\(\operatorname{Pos}_G(S)\) holds and \(S\subseteq T_{\mathrm{exp}}\), a zero of
\(e\) in \(\Lambda_{T_{\mathrm{exp}}}\) would also be a zero in \(\Lambda_S\), a
contradiction.  Suppose both possibly-optimal sets are defined and
\(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}_G(T_{\mathrm{exp}})\).  There is then
\(\vartheta\in\Lambda_{T_{\mathrm{exp}}}\subseteq\Lambda_S\) satisfying
\[
n_\rho(\vartheta)-n_\sigma(\vartheta)\geq0
\qquad(\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}),
\tag{6}
\]
so the same point witnesses \(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\).  Hence
\[
\operatorname{PO}_G(T_{\mathrm{exp}})
\subseteq\operatorname{PO}_G(S).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(S\) is feasible and \(S\subseteq T_{\mathrm{exp}}\), positivity monotonicity
and \((7)\), applied also to \(T_{\mathrm{exp}}\subseteq\mathcal U\), yield
\[
\operatorname{PO}_G(\mathcal U)
\subseteq\operatorname{PO}_G(T_{\mathrm{exp}})
\subseteq\operatorname{PO}_G(S)
=\operatorname{PO}_G(\mathcal U).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(T_{\mathrm{exp}}\) is feasible, proving upward closure.

For a positive \(S\) and fixed \(\rho\), the membership condition is the single
closed existential formula
\[
\exists\vartheta\left[
\vartheta\in\Lambda_S\ \wedge\
\bigwedge_{\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}}
\{n_\rho(\vartheta)-n_\sigma(\vartheta)\geq0\}
\right].
\tag{9}
\]
Every constraint in \((9)\) is polynomial over the same effective ordered
coefficient ring, so quantifier elimination decides it exactly.  Enumerating the
\(2^{|\mathcal U|}\) subsets uses at most that many calls for \((4)\) and at most
\(2^{|\mathcal U|}|\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}|\) calls for \((9)\).  One existential
call verifies \(\Lambda_{\mathcal U}\ne\varnothing\), and a fixed number of calls
classifies the full fiber as in \((3)\).  Once the Boolean answers are known, exact
finite comparison of the effectively comparable positive costs retains every
feasible set of least total cost, including ties.  The full set is feasible under
\(\operatorname{Pos}_G(\mathcal U)\), so the optimization has a solution.

We next prove that this numerical program ranks causal conditionals rather than
merely canonical numerator polynomials.  For a positive \(S\), apply
\(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}global\text{-}canonical\text{-}frontier}\) to the complete
augmented menu \(\mathcal P_S\) and to the joint finite cell collection
\[
\{B_\tau\cap E:\tau\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}\}\cup\{E\}.
\tag{10}
\]
It gives equality of the entire range in \((10)\) between \(\Lambda_S\) and
original-alphabet families in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P_S)\).  By \((5)\), for every
pair of arms,
\[
\frac{n_\rho(\vartheta)}{e(\vartheta)}
\geq
\frac{n_\sigma(\vartheta)}{e(\vartheta)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n_\rho(\vartheta)-n_\sigma(\vartheta)\geq0.
\tag{11}
\]
The equivalence uses multiplication by the common strictly positive denominator,
not a definition of the causal target.  Joint range equality and \((11)\) prove that
\(\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\) is exactly the set of arms conditionally optimal in some
broad compatible original-alphabet family.

Suppose now that the augmented information is also represented by a checked local
row system.  By \(\mathrm{def:target\text{-}arm\text{-}rows}\), its base-face
certificate gives
\[
d_D^{\mathsf T}q\geq\varepsilon_D>0
\qquad(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)).
\tag{12}
\]
Every acquired subface lies inside the base face.  Moreover,
\(\mathrm{lem:district\text{-}cell\text{-}linearity}\) maps every broad compatible
model to its local response law in \(\mathcal K_S\) and maps evidence to
\(d_D^{\mathsf T}q\).  Thus \((12)\) makes every corresponding global subset
positive.  A broad witness for \(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\) supplies a
\(q\in\mathcal K_S\) satisfying all local numerator inequalities, proving
\[
\operatorname{PO}_G(S)\subseteq\operatorname{PO}(S).
\tag{13}
\]
Under the augmented focal projection hypotheses,
\(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\) realizes every
\(q\in\mathcal K_S\) by a family in
\(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P_S)\subseteq\mathcal C(\mathcal P_S)\).
The same numerator and evidence equations reverse \((13)\).  This proves the local
bridge without identifying the row face with the broad fiber outside those
hypotheses.

For non-submodularity, use the one-domain binary bow construction in
\(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}layer3\text{-}acquisition\text{-}obstruction}\).  Its
common evidence is \(\top\), so \(e=1\) on every augmented fiber.  Its explicit
response-block realization gives, for every \(S\subseteq\{e_1,e_2\}\),
\[
\{q_D^\star(\vartheta):\vartheta\in\Lambda_S\}=\mathcal K_S.
\tag{14}
\]
The possibly-optimal table and the increasing marginal gain proved there therefore
hold verbatim for \(\operatorname{PO}_G\) and \(f_G\).  Both experiments, and only
both, reproduce the full possibly-optimal set, so the unique cost is two.

Finally, the promised definability-acquisition check uses the one-domain graph with
one parentless binary vertex \(Y\), hence one singleton district and response
profiles \(Y=0\) and \(Y=1\).  Let
\[
q=P(Y=1),\qquad E=\{Y=1\},
\tag{15}
\]
and let the base menu contain only a constant Boolean output, which imposes no
constraint on \(q\).  Thus \(\Lambda_{\varnothing}=[0,1]\) and \(e(q)=q\).  Take a
single candidate experiment that discloses the complete law of the Boolean output
\(Y\), with \(P(Y=1)=1\) and \(P(Y=0)=0\).  The two complete cells impose exactly
\(q=1\), so \(\Lambda_{\mathcal U}=\{1\}\).  Both endpoint response laws are finite
recursive SCMs on the original binary alphabet, so the menu is realizable.  The
base fiber contains both a zero-evidence and a positive-evidence law, whereas the
full fiber has evidence one.  With a singleton arm set the full numerical benchmark
is well defined.  This proves the claimed strict usefulness of full-menu positivity;
with one experiment and one arm it makes no separate non-submodularity claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem makes sparse-ADMG acquisition exact on every positive augmented broad-model fiber and allows the acquired full menu itself to establish conditional definability. \textit{Gap.} Existing transported-bandit and Layer-3 identification results do not give subset-specific evidence screening inside an exact heterogeneous canonical acquisition objective. \textit{Consumer.} A user can buy the least-cost complete regime blocks that both make evidence uniformly positive and preserve the full-menu possibly-optimal arm set.

\section{Interpretation}

A purchased regime block has two logically separate effects.  It can remove canonical models on which evidence vanishes, thereby making the conditional query uniformly defined, and it can remove models on which a competing arm is optimal.  Because augmented fibers are nested, both positivity and arm elimination are monotone with acquisition, so the feasible design family is upward closed.  Complementary experiments can nevertheless have increasing marginal pruning gains, which rules out a general submodular shortcut.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The local active row space remains boundary sensitive and is exact only with focal-district saturation plus baseline-averaged separation or a stronger stated certificate; otherwise it is an outer relaxation of the exact sparse canonical program.  The global Route-B fallback preserves every complete output cell, deterministic pushforward, graph-compatible latent restriction, and arbitrary real canonical parameter, but quantifier elimination returns formulas and decisions rather than explicit endpoint numbers, canonical samples, or paired SCM objects.  Exhaustive acquisition is exponential in \(|\mathcal U|\), and non-submodularity prevents a general greedy guarantee.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact global results hold for finite recursive SCMs on the declared original alphabets and effectively rational or real-algebraic supplied cell probabilities, with quantifier-free syntax interpreted over \(\mathbb R\).  Non-singleton ranges guarantee two same-menu original-alphabet SCMs existentially; the algorithm is not claimed to extract them.  Global acquisition defines numerical arm rankings only on subsets satisfying \(\operatorname{Pos}_G(S)\), although a base menu may have mixed evidence; the full acquired fiber must be positive.  The local LP is unchanged and assumes a checked base-face evidence bound.  The Boolean postprocessors disclose complete output laws, not unsupplied joint tuple cells.  No published CTFTR, CTFIDU+, selection-diagram, or bandit completeness theorem is claimed to cover this district-closed heterogeneous Layer-3 class.
