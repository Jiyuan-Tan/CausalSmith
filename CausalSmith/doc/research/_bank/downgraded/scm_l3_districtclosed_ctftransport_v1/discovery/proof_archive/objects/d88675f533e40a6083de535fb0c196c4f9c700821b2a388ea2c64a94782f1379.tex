Fix \(D\) and \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), and put \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\).  Completeness of \(\Omega_D\) gives \(q\ge0\) and \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}q=1\).  Typed-menu realizability, total probability, admissibility, and district-kernel invariance give each accepted raw equation.  The district-cell lemma gives each separately accepted numerator and context equation.  These equations, and only these equations, are the rows of \(A_Dq=m_D\).  Thus
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\tag{1}
\]

Assume \(\mathsf{BASep}_D\) and take its single baseline \(\mathbf M^0\).  Write \(K=\mathcal K_D(m_D)\), \(q_0=q_D^\star(\mathbf M^0)\), \(Z=Z_D(m_D)\), and
\[
L=\{v:A_Dv=0,\ v(\omega)=0\text{ for }\omega\in Z\}.
\]
Every difference in \(K-K\) lies in \(L\).  For each \(\omega\notin Z\), choose \(q^\omega\in K\) positive at \(\omega\), and average these finitely many laws to obtain \(\bar q\in K\) strictly positive outside \(Z\).  For \(v\in L\), a sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\) has \(\bar q\pm\epsilon v\ge0\), so both perturbations are in \(K\) and \(v\in\operatorname{span}(K-K)\).  Therefore
\[
R_D(m_D)=L^\perp=\operatorname{span}(K-K)^\perp.
\tag{2}
\]

For arbitrary \(q\in K\), focal saturation permits replacement of the complete target block law by \(q\), with the same replacement in every unchanged source; keep the baseline focal law in changed sources and keep every nonfocal block fixed.  District closure makes these cases exhaustive.  For a transport-admissible complete supplied cell, finite total probability under the fixed nonfocal product law gives
\[
P_{M^{q,s}}(C)-P_{M^{0,s}}(C)
=(\bar\chi_C^{s,\mathbf M^0})^{\mathsf T}(q-q_0)=0,
\tag{3}
\]
because the averaged row belongs to \(R_D(m_D)\) and (2) annihilates \(q-q_0\).  In a changed source the focal law was not altered.  Hence the new family preserves every menu law, lies in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\), and induces \(q\).  Combining this with (1) proves exact projection, including boundary points.  The two sufficient conditions follow from the separation lemmas.
