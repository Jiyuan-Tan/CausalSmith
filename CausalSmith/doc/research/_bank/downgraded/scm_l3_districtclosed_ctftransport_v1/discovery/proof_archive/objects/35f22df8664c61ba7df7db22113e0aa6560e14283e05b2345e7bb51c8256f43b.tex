Write \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4)^\top\) in the response-profile order of \(\mathrm{def:binary\text{-}check}\). The equations \(A_Dq=m_D\) are
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4=1,
\qquad q_3+q_4=\frac12,
\qquad q_2+q_4=\frac12,
\qquad q_j\geq0.
\]
Setting \(p=q_4\) gives
\[
q_1=p,
\qquad q_2=q_3=\frac12-p,
\qquad 0\leq p\leq\frac12,
\]
which proves the unique parameterization \(q(p)\). Since \(d_Dq(p)=1\), direct row multiplication gives
\[
Q_\rho(q(p))=q_2+q_3=1-2p,
\qquad
Q_\sigma(q(p))=q_2+q_4=\frac12.
\]
The candidate row gives \(r_D^\dagger q(p)=q_4=p\), so
\[
I_D=[0,1/2],
\qquad
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}q(p)=-q_3+q_4=2p-\frac12,
\]
and therefore \(\mathcal J_{\rho\prec\sigma}^+=(1/4,1/2]\).

In the augmented row order,
\[
(0,-1,0,2)
\begin{bmatrix}A_D\\ r_D^\dagger\end{bmatrix}
=(0,0,-1,1)=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}.
\]
The vector is dual feasible with equality, and its objective is
\[
(0,-1,0,2)(1,1/2,1/2,p)^\top=2p-\frac12.
\]
At \(p=3/8\) this value is \(1/4\).

For every \(p\in[0,1/2]\), let a binary response-block variable \(U_Y\) have profile law \(q(p)\), and set \(Y_t=f_t(U_Y)\) for \(t\in\{0,1\}\). Use the same \(U_Y\) law in target and source because \(D=\{Y\}\) is unchanged. Let \(Z=0\) deterministically in the target and \(Z=1\) deterministically in the source, and choose an independent treatment mechanism. The system is recursive, all observed outputs remain binary, exogenous blocks of distinct singleton districts are independent, \(\Delta_s=\{Z\}\) is district closed, and the response law of \(Y\) is invariant. Thus
\[
q(1/4)=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4),
\quad q(0)=(0,1/2,1/2,0),
\quad q(1/2)=(1/2,0,0,1/2)
\]
are realized on the original binary alphabets and have gaps \(0,-1/2,1/2\), respectively.

With no experiment, \(p=0\) makes \(\rho\) strictly optimal and \(p=1/2\) makes \(\sigma\) strictly optimal, so
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\{\rho,\sigma\}.
\]
Acquiring the sole block imposes \(q_4=3/8\), and the unique parameterization gives
\[
\mathcal K_{\{e^\dagger\}}=\{q(3/8)\}.
\]
At this law \(Q_\rho=1/4<1/2=Q_\sigma\). Since \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}=\{\rho,\sigma\}\),
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\{e^\dagger\})=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\{\sigma\}.
