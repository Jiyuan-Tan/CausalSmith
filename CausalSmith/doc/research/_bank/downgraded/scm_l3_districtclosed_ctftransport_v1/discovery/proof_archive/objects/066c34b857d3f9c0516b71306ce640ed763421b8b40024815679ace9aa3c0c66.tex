Fix \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\). First let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) and set \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\). Since \(q\) is the law of the finite response profile, \(q\geq0\) and \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\). Each raw row retained in \(A_D\) is transport-admissible and district-local. Typed-menu realizability and finite total probability identify its product with \(q\) as its supplied complete-cell probability. Each retained derived row satisfies the corresponding equality by the district-cell-linearity lemma, after district-kernel invariance is applied only in a transport-admissible source. These are exactly the rows stacked by the definition of \((A_D,m_D)\). Consequently
\[
A_Dq=m_D,\qquad q\geq0,\tag{1}
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).\tag{2}
\]
This conclusion is also valid when the broad fiber is empty, in which case the left side is empty.

Now suppose \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) holds. By its definition there is a single witness
\(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\), so the latter fiber is nonempty. Put
\[
K=\mathcal K_D(m_D),\qquad q_0=q_D^\star(\mathbf M^0),\qquad
Z=Z_D(m_D),\qquad
L=\{v\in\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}:A_Dv=0,\ v(\omega)=0\text{ for all }\omega\in Z\}.
\]
For \(q,q'\in K\), one has \(A_D(q-q')=0\), and both laws vanish on \(Z\); hence
\[
\operatorname{span}(K-K)\subseteq L.\tag{3}
\]
For every \(\omega\notin Z\), the definition of \(Z\) supplies \(q^\omega\in K\) with \(q^\omega(\omega)>0\). Averaging these finitely many laws gives \(\bar q\in K\) such that
\(\bar q(\omega)>0\) for every \(\omega\notin Z\). If \(v\in L\), choose
\[
0<\epsilon<\min_{\substack{\omega\notin Z\\v(\omega)\ne0}}
\frac{\bar q(\omega)}{|v(\omega)|},\tag{4}
\]
with any positive \(\epsilon\) if the displayed index set is empty. Because \(v=0\) on \(Z\), equation (4) implies \(\bar q\pm\epsilon v\geq0\); moreover \(A_D(\bar q\pm\epsilon v)=m_D\). Thus both perturbations belong to \(K\), and
\[
v=\frac{(\bar q+\epsilon v)-(\bar q-\epsilon v)}{2\epsilon}\in\operatorname{span}(K-K).\tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) yield \(\operatorname{span}(K-K)=L\). Taking orthogonal complements and using
\(\ker(A_D)^\perp=\operatorname{row}(A_D)\) and
\(\{v:v|_Z=0\}^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{e_\omega:\omega\in Z\}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{span}(K-K)^\perp
=L^\perp
=\operatorname{row}(A_D)+\operatorname{span}\{e_\omega:\omega\in Z\}
=R_D(m_D).\tag{6}
\]

It remains to prove surjectivity onto \(K\). Fix \(q\in K\). Every restricted response profile in \(\Omega_D\) extends to a full profile in \(\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\), because each omitted structural-table entry has a nonempty finite output alphabet. Choose one such extension for each profile. Focal-district saturation permits replacing the target law of \(U_D^\star\) by the lifted law of \(q\), and it requires the identical replacement in every source satisfying \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\). Keep the baseline focal law in every source satisfying \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), and keep every nonfocal mechanism and exogenous law equal to its value in \(\mathbf M^0\). District closure makes these two source cases exhaustive, and district factorization is preserved because only one independent block law was replaced. Denote the resulting family by \(\mathbf M^q\).

Consider a complete supplied cell \(C_{a,v}\) in a transport-admissible domain \(s\). The nonfocal product law remains \(\nu_{-D}^{0,s}\), so finite total probability and the slice definition imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{M^{q,s}}(C_{a,v})-P_{M^{0,s}}(C_{a,v})
&=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}\nu_{-D}^{0,s}(\omega_{-D})
\sum_{\omega_D}\chi_{C_{a,v},\omega_{-D}}(\omega_D)\,[q(\omega_D)-q_0(\omega_D)]\\
&=\bar\chi_{C_{a,v}}^{s,\mathbf M^0}(q-q_0).
\end{aligned}\tag{7}
\]
The baseline-averaged predicate puts the row in \(R_D(m_D)\), while \(q-q_0\in\operatorname{span}(K-K)\); equation (6) therefore makes (7) zero. In a non-admissible source district closure gives \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), and its focal law was not changed, so its supplied cells are also preserved. Hence every complete cell probability, and therefore every supplied law, is unchanged. We have proved
\[
\mathbf M^q\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P),\qquad
q_D^\star(\mathbf M^q)=q.\tag{8}
\]
The broad inclusion (2), applied to the focal subclass, gives the reverse containment, so (8) proves
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal K_D(m_D).\tag{9}
\]
This construction includes boundary laws because no strict positivity of \(q\) was used. It changes no nonfocal district. Finally, nonemptiness together with \(\mathsf{ASep}_D\) implies \(\mathsf{BASep}_D\) by the active-slice lemma, and \(\mathsf{Sep}_D\) implies \(\mathsf{ASep}_D\) by the structural-separation lemma, proving both advertised sufficient conditions.
