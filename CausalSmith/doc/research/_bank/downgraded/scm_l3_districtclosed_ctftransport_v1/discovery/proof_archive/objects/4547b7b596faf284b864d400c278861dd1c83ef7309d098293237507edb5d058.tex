Write
\[
K=\mathcal K_D(m_D),\quad A=A_D,\quad m=m_D,
\quad Z=Z_D(m_D),\quad J=\Omega_D\setminus Z.
\]
The normalization row implies \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\) on \(K\), so \(J\ne\varnothing\). For each \(\omega\in J\), the definition of \(Z\) supplies \(q^\omega\in K\) with \(q^\omega(\omega)>0\). Hence
\[
\bar q=\frac1{|J|}\sum_{\omega\in J}q^\omega
\]
belongs to \(K\), vanishes on \(Z\), and is strictly positive on \(J\).

Define
\[
L=\{v\in\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}:Av=0,\ v(\omega)=0\text{ for all }\omega\in Z\}.
\]
For \(v\in L\), finiteness of \(J\) and positivity of \(\bar q\) allow \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
\varepsilon |v(\omega)|\leq \frac12\bar q(\omega)
\quad(\omega\in J).
\]
Then \(\bar q\pm\varepsilon v\geq0\) and \(A(\bar q\pm\varepsilon v)=m\), so both laws belong to \(K\). Conversely, every difference of two points in \(K\) lies in \(L\). Thus \(L\) is the direction space of the affine hull of \(K\).

Let \(\widetilde A\) be obtained by appending to \(A\) the rows \(e_\omega\), \(\omega\in Z\). Then \(L=\ker(\widetilde A)\). Finite-dimensional row reduction yields
\[
L^\perp=(\ker\widetilde A)^\perp
=\operatorname{row}(\widetilde A)
=\operatorname{row}(A)+\operatorname{span}\{e_\omega:\omega\in Z\}.
\]

For broad sufficiency, let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). The projection theorem gives \(q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\in K\). If
\[
b_D=\lambda^\top A+\sum_{\omega\in Z}\zeta_\omega e_\omega,
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_Dq_D^\star(\mathbf M)
&=\lambda^\top A q_D^\star(\mathbf M)
+\sum_{\omega\in Z}\zeta_\omega q_D^\star(\mathbf M)(\omega)\\
&=\lambda^\top m,
\end{aligned}
\]
because every feasible law vanishes on \(Z\). Thus the target is identified throughout the broad fiber.

Under \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), exact saturated projection gives \(\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)=K\). The target is constant on this fiber exactly when \(b_Dv=0\) for every \(v\in L\), equivalently when \(b_D\in L^\perp\), which is the displayed active-face row space. If membership fails, choose \(v\in L\) with \(b_Dv\ne0\) and choose \(\varepsilon\) as above. Then
\[
q_+=\bar q+\varepsilon v,
\qquad q_-=\bar q-\varepsilon v
\]
belong to \(K\) and satisfy \(b_Dq_+-b_Dq_-=2\varepsilon b_Dv\ne0\). Exact saturated projection supplies two families in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) on \(\mathcal X\) inducing these laws. Their membership in the same compatibility fiber makes every supplied law equal, while their target probabilities differ. Without exact projection, the construction proves only local row-face variation.
