We prove the two inclusions in the displayed equality of counterfactual ranges, then derive the optimization statements.

First fix \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  By the definition of the compatibility fiber and assumption \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), each \(M^s\) is a finite-state recursive SCM compatible with \(G\) on \(\mathcal X\).  Apply the finite canonical counterfactual-equivalence lemma in each domain.  Its construction partitions exogenous values according to their finite deterministic response tables.  We apply that partition district by district.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) gives, for each \(s\), a product law across distinct districts, so the canonical exogenous mass in domain \(s\) has the product form used in \(\operatorname{Can}(G,\mathcal X)\).

This domainwise construction can be synchronized across invariant districts.  Indeed, if \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) states equality of the joint structural-response-map laws on \(D\) in domains \(s\) and \(\star\).  The canonical partition label is precisely the finite structural response profile.  We therefore assign the same mass to each such label in the two domains, obtaining
\[
 q_D^s(\cdot;\theta^s)=q_D^\star(\cdot;\theta^\star).
\]
If instead \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), no equality is imposed.  These are the only two cases by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}closure}\).  Thus no partial-district response law is spliced.

The cited canonical equivalence preserves every counterfactual cell, and assumption \(\mathrm{ass:typed\text{-}menu\text{-}realizability}\) gives
\[
 \Phi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)
 =P_{M^s}^{a}(v)=P_a^s(v)
\]
for every \(s\), \(a\), and \(v\in\mathcal X_{W_a}\).  Hence the synchronized tuple \(\theta\) belongs to \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  For every target cell \(C\), canonical equivalence also yields
\[
 P_{M^\star}(C)=\Phi_C^\star(\theta^\star).
\]
This proves that the left-hand range is contained in the right-hand range, simultaneously for every finite \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, fix \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  The one-hot constraints select deterministic structural tables with outputs in the original alphabets \(\mathcal X_V\), the simplex constraints define the finite exogenous laws, and recursive evaluation in a topological order is well defined because \(G\) is acyclic.  The graph-compatibility restrictions in \(\operatorname{Can}(G,\mathcal X)\) give assumption \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), while the product restriction across district blocks gives assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\).  The fixed selection sets obey assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}closure}\), and the equalities
\(
q_D^s=q_D^\star
\)
for every unchanged district give assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\).  Finally, the full collection of equations
\(
\Phi_{C_{a,v}}^s=P_a^s(v)
\)
over all \(v\in\mathcal X_{W_a}\) proves equality of the complete induced law and \(P_a^s\), not merely equality of a marginal or selected row.  Thus canonical realization of \(\theta\) is a family \(\mathbf M_\theta\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), and by construction
\[
 P_{M_\theta^\star}(C)=\Phi_C^\star(\theta^\star)
\]
for every target cell \(C\).  This proves the reverse range inclusion and also proves preservation of the declared observed alphabets.

Nonemptiness of \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) follows from the first inclusion and the assumed nonemptiness of \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  There are finitely many one-hot structural-table coordinates because \(G\), \(\mathcal V\), and every alphabet \(\mathcal X_V\) are finite.  For each fixed one-hot table assignment, the remaining coordinates lie in a finite product of probability simplices.  This product is closed and bounded in finite-dimensional Euclidean space.  The invariance and complete-menu restrictions are inverse images of singletons under the polynomial maps \(q_D^s-q_D^\star\) and \(\Phi_{C_{a,v}}^s\), hence are closed.  There are only finitely many one-hot assignments.  Therefore \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) is a finite union of closed subsets of compact simplex products and is compact.  The recursive formula defining every \(\Phi_C^s\) is a finite sum of finite products of parameter coordinates, so it is polynomial and continuous.  It follows that its minimum and maximum on the nonempty compact fiber are attained.

Apply the exact range equality with \(\mathcal F=\{T\}\).  Every compatible target value lies in
\(
[L_T(\mathcal P),U_T(\mathcal P)]
\), and the canonical realizations of a minimizer and maximizer attain the endpoints.  Thus the bounds are sharp.  Since every probability lies in \([0,1]\), this sharp interval is strictly smaller than the logical interval exactly when its lower endpoint is positive or its upper endpoint is less than one; both conditions are outputs of the same finite program.  The target is constant on the compatibility fiber exactly when its minimum equals its maximum.  If the inequality is strict, the two optimizing realizations agree on all supplied complete-law cells by membership in \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\), retain the original alphabets by construction, and disagree on \(T\).  This is the required paired-model certificate.

The evidence polynomial is nonnegative.  Compactness gives attained values \(e_-\) and \(e_+\).  If \(e_+=0\), then \(\Phi_E^\star=0\) everywhere.  If \(e_-=0<e_+\), an attaining canonical realization has zero evidence while another has positive evidence, so positivity on the full compatibility fiber is false.  Division is therefore rejected in both cases.  If \(e_->0\), then for every compatible model the denominator is positive and the continuous ratio satisfies
\[
 Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_\theta)
 =\frac{\Phi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(\theta^\star)}
        {\Phi_E^\star(\theta^\star)}.
\]
The ratio is continuous on the compact fiber because its denominator is bounded below by \(e_->0\), so both displayed conditional endpoints are attained; exact range equality makes them sharp.

For the two arms the denominator is common.  Hence, when \(e_->0\),
\[
 Q_\sigma^\star(\mathbf M_\theta)-Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_\theta)
 =\frac{\mathcal G_{\rho,\sigma}(\theta)}
        {\Phi_E^\star(\theta^\star)},
\]
and the sign of the conditional difference equals the sign of \(\mathcal G_{\rho,\sigma}(\theta)\).  If the difference is positive for every compatible model, exact range equality makes \(\mathcal G_{\rho,\sigma}>0\) at every point of the compact canonical fiber; its attained minimum is therefore strictly positive.  The converse is immediate.  Reversing the sign proves the maximum criterion for uniform \(\rho\)-dominance.  Finally, an attained negative minimum and positive maximum give canonical compatible realizations with opposite strict rankings, while any such pair forces the two strict inequalities.  This proves all three arm-order equivalences.
Safe pruning is now immediate: the first strict minimum condition says exactly that every compatible target-domain SCM ranks \(\sigma\) above \(\rho\), so removing \(\rho\) cannot remove an optimal compatible arm.

It remains to compare the global fiber with a local face.  Fix \(D\) and \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  Its canonical realization lies in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  For every retained row, the district-cell linearity lemma gives the corresponding equality
\(
r_{s,a,D,h,c}q_D^\star=K_{s,a,D}(c\mid h)
\); the normalization row gives \(\mathbf 1^\top q_D^\star=1\), and nonnegativity follows because \(q_D^\star\) is a probability law.  Stacking these equations yields
\[
 A_Dq_D^\star(\cdot;\theta^\star)=m_D,
 \qquad q_D^\star(\cdot;\theta^\star)\geq0,
\]
so the global projection is contained in \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  By the already proved exact range equivalence, a proposed local law lifts precisely when it lies in this global projection.  The one-district fiber-projection theorem identifies the projection with \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) under its hypotheses.  No reverse inclusion has been proved in the multi-district case, where the complete menu equations \(\Phi_{C_{a,v}}^s=P_a^s(v)\) retain polynomial sum-product constraints across districts.  Therefore a local face-failure witness is only a relaxation witness; distinct global optimizers, and only those optimizers in this argument, supply the claimed original-alphabet countermodels.
