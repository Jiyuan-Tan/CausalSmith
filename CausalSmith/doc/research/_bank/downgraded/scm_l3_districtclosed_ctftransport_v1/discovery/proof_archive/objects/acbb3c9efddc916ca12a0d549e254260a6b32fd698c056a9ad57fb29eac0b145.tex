There are two exact finite frontiers with different scopes.

First, assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\). Then \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) is a nonempty compact semialgebraic set and, for every finite collection \(\mathcal F\) of declared target-domain counterfactual cells,
\[
\{(P_{M^\star}(C):C\in\mathcal F):\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\}
=
\{(\Psi_C^\star(\theta^\star):C\in\mathcal F):\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\}.
\]
Every \(\Psi_C^s\) is multi-affine in the district response-law blocks and has total degree at most the number of districts visited by \(C\). Every point of the right-hand range is realized by a district-saturated SCM family on the original observed alphabets \(\mathcal X\).

Second, for an arbitrary acyclic directed mixed graph, if \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\), then \(\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\) is a nonempty compact semialgebraic set and
\[
\{(P_{M^\star}(C):C\in\mathcal F):\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\}
=
\{(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star):C\in\mathcal F):
\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\}.
\]
The canonical realization also uses the original observed alphabets and retains the internal latent-factor restrictions of \(G\).

For either exact row
\[
(\mathfrak C,\Lambda,\Xi)
\in
\{(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P),\Theta(\mathcal P),\Psi),
(\mathcal C(\mathcal P),\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P),\Phi_{\mathrm{can}})\},
\]
provided \(\mathfrak C\ne\varnothing\), the quantities
\[
\min_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_T^\star(x^\star)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\max_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_T^\star(x^\star)
\]
are respectively the attained sharp lower and upper bounds for the target cell. At least one bound is strictly inside the logical interval \([0,1]\) exactly when the lower endpoint is positive or the upper endpoint is less than one, and the cell is identified exactly when the two extrema agree. If the extrema differ, minimizing and maximizing realizations give two original-alphabet same-menu SCM families attaining different endpoint values. No assertion is made that every scalar value between the two extrema is attained. These are finite semialgebraic characterizations.

For the same row let
\[
e_-(\Lambda)=\min_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_E^\star(x^\star),
\qquad e_+(\Lambda)=\max_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_E^\star(x^\star).
\]
If \(e_+(\Lambda)=0\), evidence is zero throughout \(\mathfrak C\); if \(e_-(\Lambda)=0<e_+(\Lambda)\), positivity is not uniform. In either case a conditional functional on all of \(\mathfrak C\) is rejected before division. If \(e_-(\Lambda)>0\), the quantities
\[
\min_{x\in\Lambda}
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\max_{x\in\Lambda}
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}
\]
are the attained sharp lower and upper conditional bounds. If they differ, their endpoint models are distinct; no interval-filling property of the conditional image is asserted. Under this positivity condition, \(Q_\sigma^\star>Q_\rho^\star\) throughout \(\mathfrak C\) if and only if
\[
\min_{x\in\Lambda}
\{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star(x^\star)-\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)\}>0,
\]
and opposite strict rankings occur in two same-menu original-alphabet models if and only if the minimum of the displayed gap is negative and its maximum is positive. Thus this strict minimum is the exact safe-pruning certificate for the chosen fiber.

Finally, for every district \(D\),
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]
If \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) holds, the one-district fiber-projection theorem gives the exact local equality
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal K_D(m_D),
\]
and \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) itself entails a nonempty focal-district-saturated compatibility fiber. This equality requires saturation only of the focal district and preserves the graph-compatible restrictions of every \(D'\ne D\). Cross-district coupling of a complete supplied cell is harmless for the fixed-baseline replacement exactly when its baseline-averaged focal row lies in \(R_D(m_D)\), because
\[
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}\{q-q':q,q'\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\}^{\perp}.
\]
Nonemptiness of \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\) together with \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\), and hence with \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), is a baseline-free sufficient certificate for this equality. The baseline-averaged predicate is generally baseline-dependent and not directly checkable from menu cells alone; allowing a different baseline for each candidate \(q\) is not asserted. Outside focal-district saturation and baseline-averaged separation, \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) is certified only as an outer relaxation. This local equality is distinct from the first, all-district-saturated global row, whose Cartesian district variation makes the multi-affine program exact. Consequently a failed local face test is not a global nonidentification certificate outside the focal projection theorem's scope; distinct endpoint optimizers of the appropriate exact global program are the paired-model certificate whenever their objective values differ.
