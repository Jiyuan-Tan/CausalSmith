For the general computability claim, fix \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\).  The set \(\mathcal K_S\) is given by the finite equalities \(A_Dq=m_D\), \(R_eq=p_e\) for \(e\in S\), and inequalities \(q\geq0\).  For a candidate arm \(\rho\), common evidence reduces the comparison \(Q_\rho(q)\geq Q_\sigma(q)\), on \(d_Dq>0\), to the linear inequality
\[
 (n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})q\geq0.
\]
Positive evidence is checked by maximizing \(d_Dq\) subject to \(q\in\mathcal K_S\) and all of these arm-comparison inequalities, and requiring a positive optimum.  Thus membership of \(\rho\) in \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\) is decided by one finite linear program.  There are only \(2^{|\mathcal U|}\) sets \(S\), so enumerating them, retaining those with \(\operatorname{PO}(S)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}\), and minimizing \(\sum_{e\in S}c_e\) returns all minimum-cost solutions.

We construct the obstruction on the binary graph \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\), with the singleton district \(D=\{Y\}\), sure evidence, and response order
\(
 \Omega_D=((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)).
\)
Take the base face to be the whole simplex.  Let \(\rho\) be treatment zero and \(\sigma\) treatment one with reward \(Y=1\); hence
\[
 n_{\rho,D}=(0,0,1,1),
 \qquad n_{\sigma,D}=(0,1,0,1),
 \qquad g_{\rho,\sigma,D}=(0,1,-1,0).
\]
Use the realizable target response law
\[
 q^\star=(1/4,3/8,1/8,1/4).
\]
Experiment \(e_1\) reveals the genuine Layer-3 cell
\(
 q_2=P(Y_0=0,Y_1=1)=3/8
\), and \(e_2\) reveals
\(
 q_3=P(Y_0=1,Y_1=0)=1/8
\); each has unit cost.  These are zero-one incidence rows and are realized by the displayed finite response law, so the typed blocks are admissible.

With no experiment, laws with \(q_2-q_3<0\) and with \(q_2-q_3>0\) both occur.  Under \(e_1\) alone, the feasible choices \((q_2,q_3)=(3/8,5/8)\) and \((3/8,0)\), with the remaining mass zero or assigned to a zero-gap coordinate, give opposite strict rankings.  Under \(e_2\) alone, the choices \((q_2,q_3)=(0,1/8)\) and \((7/8,1/8)\) likewise give opposite rankings.  Hence both arms are possibly optimal in the first three faces.  When both blocks are acquired, every feasible law satisfies
\[
 g_{\rho,\sigma,D}q=q_2-q_3=\frac38-\frac18=\frac14>0,
\]
so only \(\sigma\) is possibly optimal.  The one-district projection construction realizes every displayed witness on the original binary alphabets.

Therefore
\[
 f(\{e_2\})-f(\varnothing)=0
 <1=f(\{e_1,e_2\})-f(\{e_1\}),
\]
which refutes submodularity with \(S=\varnothing\), \(T_{\mathrm{exp}}=\{e_1\}\), and \(e=e_2\).  Finally, matching the full-information set requires both equalities: omitting either one leaves both arms possibly optimal by the witnesses above.  With binary acquisition variables this is exactly \(x_1+x_2\geq2\); enumeration of its four integer points certifies the unique optimum \((1,1)\) and objective two.
For the continuous relaxation, the dual of \(\min x_1+x_2\) subject to \(x_1+x_2\geq2\) and \(x_i\geq0\) is
\[
 \max_{0\leq y\leq1}2y.
\]
The dual multiplier \(y=1\) gives the lower bound two, matched by the integer point \((1,1)\).  This is an exact mixed-integer optimality certificate, not merely an exhaustive numerical claim.
