Assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\). Choose one
\(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\). District factorization makes the nonfocal response law in each domain the product probability \(\nu_{-D}^{0,s}\). For every transport-admissible complete cell, active-slice separation gives
\[
\chi_{C,\omega_{-D}}\in R_D(m_D)\qquad\text{for every }\omega_{-D}.\tag{1}
\]
Because \(R_D(m_D)\) is a linear space, averaging (1) with the nonnegative weights summing to one yields
\[
\bar\chi_C^{s,\mathbf M^0}
=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}\nu_{-D}^{0,s}(\omega_{-D})\chi_{C,\omega_{-D}}
\in R_D(m_D).\tag{2}
\]
The same fixed \(\mathbf M^0\) works for every cell and domain, so (2) is precisely \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\). Composing this implication with structural separation proves the displayed hierarchy.

To prove strictness of the second implication, use the binary DAG with independent root districts \(X\) and \(Z\), both pointing to \(Y\), and structural equation
\[
Y=\mathbf1\{X=Z\}.\tag{3}
\]
Let \(D=\{X\}\), let the baseline \(Z\) be fair, and supply only the complete target-domain marginal law of \(Y\). The indicator of either \(Y\)-cell depends jointly on \(X\) and \(Z\), so no nonnormalization district-local row for \(D\) is retained; hence
\[
A_D=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}.\tag{4}
\]
For \(C=\{Y=1\}\), the two slices at \(Z=0\) and \(Z=1\) are \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Neither lies in (4), so \(\mathsf{ASep}_D\) fails. Their fair baseline average is
\((\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\in R_D(m_D)\); the complementary cell has the same property. Moreover, for every law of \(X\), equation (3) with fair independent \(Z\) gives \(P(Y=1)=\tfrac12\). Thus focal saturation permits every replacement law of \(X\) while preserving the menu, and the chosen baseline is in the required compatibility fiber. Therefore \(\mathsf{BASep}_D\) holds although \(\mathsf{ASep}_D\) fails, on the original finite alphabets.
