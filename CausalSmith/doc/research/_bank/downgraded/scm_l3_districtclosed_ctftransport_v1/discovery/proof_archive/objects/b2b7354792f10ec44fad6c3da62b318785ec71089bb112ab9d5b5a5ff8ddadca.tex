Put \(K=\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) and \(g=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}\).  The normalization row of \(A_D\) gives \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\) on \(K\).  Thus the assumed nonempty set \(K\) is a closed subset of the finite simplex and is compact.  The primal minimum and maximum are consequently attained.

Apply finite linear-programming strong duality to the primal variables \(q\), equality constraint \(A_Dq=m_D\), nonnegativity constraint \(q\geq0\), and cost column \(g^\top\).  Its dual variable \(\eta\) is unrestricted because the primal constraint is an equality, and the nonnegative primal variables give the coordinatewise constraint \(A_D^\top\eta\leq g^\top\).  Therefore
\[
 \Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(m_D)
 =\min_{q\in K}gq
 =\max_{\eta:A_D^\top\eta\leq g^\top}m_D^\top\eta.
\]
Weak duality is visible directly: for feasible \(q,\eta\),
\[
 m_D^\top\eta=q^\top A_D^\top\eta\leq gq.
\]

For every \(q\in K\), common positive evidence and the target-row identities give
\[
 Q_\sigma(q)-Q_\rho(q)
 =\frac{n_{\sigma,D}q-n_{\rho,D}q}{d_Dq}
 =\frac{gq}{d_Dq}.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive by hypothesis, so the sign of the conditional difference is the sign of \(gq\).  The one-district fiber-projection theorem says that these \(q\)'s are exactly the target district laws of compatible SCM families.  Hence every compatible SCM has \(Q_\sigma^\star>Q_\rho^\star\) if and only if \(gq>0\) for every \(q\in K\).  Compactness makes this equivalent to \(\min_{q\in K}gq>0\), and dual attainment makes it equivalent to a feasible \(\eta\) with \(m_D^\top\eta>0\).

If \(\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(m_D)<0\), let \(q_-\) be a primal minimizer.  Fiber projection realizes \(q_-\) by an SCM family on \(\mathcal X\), and the displayed ratio has negative numerator, proving \(Q_\sigma^\star<Q_\rho^\star\).  If in addition \(\overline\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(m_D)>0\), a maximizer \(q_+\) is likewise realized and has the opposite strict ranking.  Both families lie in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), so they agree on every supplied law and use the original observed alphabets.
