Assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\), and choose \(\mathbf M^0\) in that fiber.  District factorization gives the nonfocal product law \(\nu_{-D}^{0,s}\).  Every focal slice of every transport-admissible supplied cell belongs to \(R_D(m_D)\).  Because this is a linear subspace and the sum is finite,
\[
\bar\chi_{C_{a,v}}^{s,\mathbf M^0}
=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}\nu_{-D}^{0,s}(\omega_{-D})
\chi_{C_{a,v},\omega_{-D}}
\in R_D(m_D).
\]
The same \(\mathbf M^0\) works for every cell, so it witnesses \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  Composing this implication with structural separation proves the displayed hierarchy.

For strictness, take a binary DAG with two independent root vertices \(X,Z\) feeding a binary child \(Y\), with no bidirected edges, and let \(D=\{X\}\).  At the target baseline let \(Z\) be fair and let \(Y=\mathbf 1\{X=Z\}\).  Supply only the complete marginal law of \(Y\), which is Bernoulli \(1/2\).  The complete focal carrier is \(\Omega_D=\{0,1\}\).  The child marginal is cross-district, so no raw focal row is accepted, and it supplies no sound extraction exposing \(X\); hence
\[
A_D=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},\quad
\mathcal K_D(m_D)=\Delta(\{0,1\}),\quad
Z_D(m_D)=\varnothing,\quad
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}.
\]
For the cell \(Y=1\), fixing \(Z=0\) and \(Z=1\) gives focal slices \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\), neither in \(R_D(m_D)\); therefore active-slice separation fails.  Under the single fair nonfocal baseline their average is
\[
\tfrac12(1,0)+\tfrac12(0,1)=\tfrac12(1,1)\in R_D(m_D).
\]
The failure cell has the slices interchanged and the same average.  A singleton district is bidirected complete, and focal saturation permits every law on \(X\)'s response block while the fair law of \(Z\) and the deterministic table of \(Y\) remain fixed.  Thus the displayed baseline lies in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\) and witnesses baseline-averaged separation although active-slice separation fails.
