Fix \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\). The set \(\mathcal K_S\) is described by the finitely many equations
\[
A_Dq=m_D,
\qquad R_eq=p_e\quad(e\in S),
\]
and the inequalities \(q\geq0\). For a candidate arm \(\rho\), whenever the common denominator is positive,
\[
Q_\rho(q)\geq Q_\sigma(q)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})q\geq0.
\]
Therefore \(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}(S)\) exactly when the linear program that maximizes \(d_Dq\) over \(q\in\mathcal K_S\) subject to all pairwise numerator-gap inequalities has a strictly positive optimum. This gives a finite LP characterization for every arm and every \(S\). Enumerating the finite set of \(2^{|\mathcal U|}\) subsets, comparing each \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\) with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}\), and minimizing \(\sum_{e\in S}c_e\) characterizes every minimum-cost solution. If the coefficients are effectively rational or real algebraic, exact ordered-field arithmetic and finite LP pivoting decide each equality, inequality, and strict positivity test, so the characterization is algorithmic. No such effective assertion is made for an uneffectively specified real coefficient.

For the non-submodularity witness, take the binary graph \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\), the singleton district \(D=\{Y\}\), sure evidence, and response-profile order
\[
\Omega_D=((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)).
\]
Let the base face be the whole simplex. Let \(\rho\) be treatment zero and \(\sigma\) treatment one, with reward \(Y=1\). Then
\[
n_{\rho,D}=(0,0,1,1),
\qquad n_{\sigma,D}=(0,1,0,1),
\qquad g_{\rho,\sigma,D}=(0,1,-1,0).
\]
Choose the realizable response law
\[
q^\star=(1/4,3/8,1/8,1/4).
\]
Experiment \(e_1\) reveals the Layer-3 cell \(q_2=P(Y_0=0,Y_1=1)=3/8\), and \(e_2\) reveals \(q_3=P(Y_0=1,Y_1=0)=1/8\); each has unit cost. A one-district response-block SCM with law \(q^\star\) realizes both cells on the original binary alphabets.

With no experiment, simplex laws with \(q_2-q_3<0\) and with \(q_2-q_3>0\) exist. Under \(e_1\) alone, the feasible pairs
\[
(q_2,q_3)=(3/8,5/8),
\qquad (q_2,q_3)=(3/8,0)
\]
have opposite gap signs. Under \(e_2\) alone, the feasible pairs
\[
(q_2,q_3)=(0,1/8),
\qquad (q_2,q_3)=(7/8,1/8)
\]
also have opposite signs. In each display the remaining nonnegative mass is assigned to coordinate one or four, whose gap coefficient is zero. The one-district response-block construction realizes every displayed law. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1\})
=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_2\})=\{\rho,\sigma\}.
\]
With both blocks, every feasible law satisfies
\[
g_{\rho,\sigma,D}q=q_2-q_3=\frac38-\frac18=\frac14>0,
\]
so \(\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1,e_2\})=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\{\sigma\}\). Hence
\[
f(\{e_2\})-f(\varnothing)=0
<1=f(\{e_1,e_2\})-f(\{e_1\}),
\]
which violates diminishing returns for \(S=\varnothing\), \(T_{\mathrm{exp}}=\{e_1\}\), and \(e=e_2\).

Omitting either experiment leaves both arms possibly optimal by the displayed witnesses. Full-information pruning therefore requires both acquisition variables, and the exact integer program is
\[
\min_{x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}}x_1+x_2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad x_1+x_2\geq2.
\]
Its unique feasible optimum is \((1,1)\) with value two. For every point of the continuous relaxation, the covering constraint itself gives \(x_1+x_2\geq2\); multiplying it by \(y=1\) is a valid dual lower bound of two, matching the feasible point \((1,1)\).
