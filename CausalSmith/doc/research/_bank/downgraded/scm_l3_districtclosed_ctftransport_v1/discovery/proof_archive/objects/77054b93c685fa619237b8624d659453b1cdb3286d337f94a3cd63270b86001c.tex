Fix \(D\), \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), and write \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\).  Completeness of \(\Omega_D\) gives \(q\geq0\) and \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}q=1\).  By the district-cell lemma, every accepted raw, numerator, and context row in \(A_D\) has the value recorded in \(m_D\), after its recorded source-admissibility proof.  Therefore
\[
A_Dq=m_D,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\tag{1}
\]

Assume \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) and choose the single baseline \(\mathbf M^0\) in its definition.  This choice itself proves \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\).  Put
\[
K=\mathcal K_D(m_D),\quad q_0=q_D^\star(\mathbf M^0),\quad Z=Z_D(m_D),
\]
and
\[
L=\{v\in\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}:A_Dv=0,
\ v(\omega)=0\text{ for every }\omega\in Z\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Every difference of two points of \(K\) lies in \(L\).  Conversely, for each \(\omega\notin Z\), choose \(q^\omega\in K\) with \(q^\omega(\omega)>0\).  Their finite average \(\bar q\in K\) is positive on every coordinate outside \(Z\).  If \(v\in L\), sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\) makes \(\bar q\pm\epsilon v\geq0\); both perturbations remain in \(K\).  Hence
\[
\operatorname{span}(K-K)=L.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking orthogonal complements gives
\[
\operatorname{span}(K-K)^\perp
=L^\perp
=\operatorname{row}(A_D)+
\operatorname{span}\{e_\omega:\omega\in Z\}
=R_D(m_D).
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(q\in K\).  Focal-district saturation permits replacement of the target law of the complete block \(U_D^\star\) by \(q\), and the same replacement in every unchanged source, while holding the focal law fixed in changed sources and holding every nonfocal structural table and latent law fixed.  District closure makes these cases exhaustive.  For a complete supplied output cell \(C_{a,y}=\{f_a(T_a)=y\}\) in a domain admissible for \(D\), finite total probability under the fixed nonfocal product law gives
\[
P_{M^{q,s}}(C_{a,y})-P_{M^{0,s}}(C_{a,y})
=(\bar\chi_{C_{a,y}}^{s,\mathbf M^0})^{\mathsf T}(q-q_0).
\tag{5}
\]
The deterministic preimage is used only to evaluate the indicator defining the averaged row.  Baseline-averaged separation puts this row in \(R_D(m_D)\), while \((4)\) makes the right side of \((5)\) zero.  A changed-source focal law was not altered.  Thus every directly supplied output law is preserved.  The resulting family remains on \(\mathcal X\), belongs to \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\), and induces \(q\).  Combining this surjection with \((1)\) yields
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=K.
\]
The two separation lemmas give the stated baseline-free sufficient conditions.  Without the displayed focal saturation and one-baseline preservation condition, only \((1)\) has been proved.
