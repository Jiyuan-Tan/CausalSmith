Fix \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\). Let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) and put \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\). Since \(q\) is a probability law, \(q\geq0\) and the normalization row gives \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\). Every retained raw row has a checked proof that its complete supplied cell is district-local and that either \(s=\star\) or \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\). Typed-menu realizability, recursive evaluation, and district-kernel invariance give
\[
P_a^s(v)=r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q_D^s
=r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q.
\]
For every retained derived row, \(\mathrm{lem:district\text{-}cell\text{-}linearity}\) gives
\[
K_{s,a,D}(c\mid h)=r_{s,a,D,h,c}q.
\]
Stacking these equalities and normalization yields \(A_Dq=m_D\). Hence
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]

Now assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\). The preceding inclusion and \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) imply
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]
Choose a baseline \(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) and an arbitrary \(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\). In the response-block representation of \(\mathbf M^0\), leave every district law other than that of \(D\) unchanged. For each \(\omega_D\in\Omega_D\), complete its deterministic maps at every unvisited parent configuration by a fixed element of the corresponding nonempty alphabet \(\mathcal X_V\). This gives an injection into \(\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\). In the target domain replace the law of \(U_D^\star\) by the pushforward of \(q\) through this injection. Use the same completed law in every source satisfying \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), and retain the baseline law in every source satisfying \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\). District closure makes these the only two cases. Denote the resulting family by \(\mathbf M^q\).

The structural tables still take values in \(\mathcal X\) and remain recursive because \(G\) is acyclic. The response blocks remain independent across districts. The defining construction of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\) admits every such full district-response law, and its bidirected-completeness clause makes the replacement graph-compatible within that class. The construction enforces full response-law equality across every unchanged district, so district-kernel invariance holds.

It remains to verify the complete supplied laws. Consider a complete cell in a domain for which \(D\) is transport-admissible. By \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), its recursive indicator is either independent of \(\omega_D\) or depends only on \(\omega_D\). In the first case its probability is unchanged because all other district laws retain their baseline values. In the second case its raw incidence row occurs in \(A_D\), so
\[
P_{M^{q,s}}^a(v)=r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q=P_a^s(v),
\]
where the last equality is one coordinate of \(A_Dq=m_D\). If \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), the entire source response block at \(D\) was left at its baseline law, and every supplied cell in that source is unchanged. Thus all complete cells, and hence all supplied laws, agree with \(\mathcal P\). Therefore \(\mathbf M^q\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) and its target district law is \(q\). This proves the reverse inclusion. No step required a coordinate of \(q\) to be positive, so every boundary point, including a point with forced-zero profiles, is realized on the original alphabets.
