Assume \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), and fix a transport-admissible complete supplied cell \(C\) and a nonfocal profile \(\omega_{-D}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}separated\text{-}menu}\), at least one of the two permitted structural cases applies.  If the recursive indicator of \(C\) is independent of \(\omega_D\), then its fixed-\(\omega_{-D}\) slice is either the zero row or \(\mathbf 1^\top\).  The zero row belongs to every row space, and \(\mathbf 1^\top\in\operatorname{row}(A_D)\) because \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}menu\text{-}system}\) prepends the normalization row.  Hence the slice lies in \(R_D(m_D)\).

If instead the indicator depends only on \(\omega_D\), \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}separated\text{-}menu}\) requires its raw row to occur in \(A_D\).  Every fixed-\(\omega_{-D}\) slice is that raw row, so it belongs to \(\operatorname{row}(A_D)\subseteq R_D(m_D)\).  These cases cover every admissible cell and every nonfocal profile, proving \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).

For strictness, consider two binary response coordinates \(Y_0\) and \(Z\) generated by independent singleton district blocks, and put \(D=D_{Y_0}\).  For \(y,z\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
h_y(\omega_D)=\mathbf 1\{Y_0(\omega_D)=y\},
\qquad
k_z(\omega_{-D})=\mathbf 1\{Z(\omega_{-D})=z\}.
\]
Supplying the complete marginal law of \(Y_0\) retains both rows \(h_0,h_1\) in \(A_D\).  The complete joint cell \(\{Y_0=y,Z=z\}\) has the genuine cross-district indicator
\[
h_y(\omega_D)k_z(\omega_{-D}).
\]
Because the binary profile sets contain profiles producing both values, this indicator is neither independent of \(\omega_D\) nor a function of \(\omega_D\) alone.  Thus \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\) fails.  On fixing \(\omega_{-D}\), however, its focal slice is \(0\) when \(k_z(\omega_{-D})=0\) and is \(h_y\) when \(k_z(\omega_{-D})=1\).  Both rows belong to \(\operatorname{row}(A_D)\).  The marginal cells have slices \(h_y\), and the same argument applies to every joint cell.  Therefore every required slice belongs to \(R_D(m_D)\), so \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) holds.  Independent Bernoulli laws on the two finite singleton response blocks realize the supplied marginal and joint laws on the declared binary alphabets, completing the promised strict example.
