Fix an accepted finite fixing trace and fix
\(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  We prove preservation at this
arbitrary model.  By \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), recursive
evaluation in each domain terminates and every well-typed finite multiworld
event is a zero--one function of the complete deterministic response profiles.
For a domain \(t\in\mathcal S\), put
\[
 \widehat\Omega^t=\prod_{D'\in\mathcal D(G)}
 \Omega_{D'}^{\mathrm{all}},
 \qquad
 I_A^t(\omega)=\mathbf 1\{A\text{ is true after recursive evaluation at }
 \omega\}.
\]
All factors are finite and nonempty: the alphabets are finite and nonempty by
\(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), and a deterministic table can
be chosen at every one of the finitely many parent configurations.  Write
\(\widehat q_{D'}^t\) for the law induced by \(M^t\) on the complete profile of
district \(D'\).  The product-law clause
\(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) and finite total probability
give, for every such event,
\[
 P_{M^t}(A)
 =p_A^t(\widehat q^t)
 :=\sum_{\omega\in\widehat\Omega^t}
 I_A^t(\omega)
 \prod_{D'\in\mathcal D(G)}
 \widehat q_{D'}^t(\omega_{D'}).
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(p_A^t\) is a finite polynomial with zero--one coefficients.  Partial
copies create additional recursive evaluations of the same deterministic
tables, but do not alter (1).  A supplied-cell leaf is also covered: membership
of \(\mathbf M\) in the fiber defined by
\(\mathrm{def:compatibility\text{-}fiber}\) makes its numerical menu value
equal to the corresponding polynomial (1).

We next record the algebraic invariant checked by the finite calculus.  After
response-profile expansion, associate to every expression \(F\) two ordinary
polynomials \((N_F,D_F)\).  For a rational constant or an event-probability
leaf, set \(D_F=1\) and take \(N_F\) to be the constant or the polynomial in
(1).  For the constructors in
\(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\), the association is
recursive:
\[
\begin{array}{rclcrcl}
 N_{\sum_{j\in J}F_j}
 &=&\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}N_{F_j}
       \prod_{k\in J\setminus\{j\}}D_{F_k},
 &\quad&
 D_{\sum_{j\in J}F_j}&=&\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}D_{F_j},\\[4pt]
 N_{\prod_{j\in J}F_j}&=&\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}N_{F_j},
 &&D_{\prod_{j\in J}F_j}&=&\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}D_{F_j},\\[4pt]
 N_{1-F}&=&D_F-N_F,
 &&D_{1-F}&=&D_F,\\[4pt]
 N_{F/H}&=&N_FD_H,
 &&D_{F/H}&=&D_FN_H.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
The empty product in (2) is one.  A conditional constructor is the last line
with \(F\) the expression for the joint event and \(H\) the expression for its
context.  It is admitted only when the trace contains an independently checked
certificate that \(H>0\) on its declared fiber.  Induction on the expression
grammar, using (2), proves simultaneously that every admitted denominator is
nonzero and
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf M}(F)
 =\frac{N_F(\widehat q)}{D_F(\widehat q)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For the quotient step, the induction hypotheses give
\(D_F(\widehat q)\ne0\), \(D_H(\widehat q)\ne0\), and
\(N_H(\widehat q)/D_H(\widehat q)>0\); hence
\(N_H(\widehat q)\ne0\), so the displayed division in (3) is legal.  This is
the only point at which division is used.

We now induct on the accepted trace constructors specified in
\(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}extraction\text{-}calculus}\).  The induction
invariant is equality of the evaluations of the input and output expressions
at every compatible response-law tuple.

For a finite disjoint-sum or marginalization constructor, its event indicators
satisfy pointwise
\[
 I_{\bigsqcup_{j\in J}A_j}^t(\omega)
 =\sum_{j\in J}I_{A_j}^t(\omega),
\]
so substitution in (1) gives
\[
 P_{M^t}\!\left(\bigsqcup_{j\in J}A_j\right)
 =\sum_{j\in J}P_{M^t}(A_j).
 \tag{4}
\]
The marginalization case is (4) for the finite partition indexed by the
enumerated values of the marginalized variables.  For the complement
constructor, \(I_{A^c}^t=1-I_A^t\); normalization of each district law and
(1) therefore give
\[
 P_{M^t}(A^c)=1-P_{M^t}(A).
 \tag{5}
\]
For an arithmetic product constructor, preservation follows directly from the
product line of (2) and the child induction hypotheses.  When that constructor
also asserts a probability factorization, its checker has partitioned the
event into factors measurable with respect to disjoint original-district
response blocks.  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) then gives
\[
 P_{M^t}\!\left(\bigcap_{j\in J}A_j\right)
 =\prod_{j\in J}P_{M^t}(A_j).
 \tag{6}
\]
No product over overlapping district supports is an accepted probability
identity.  For a conditional constructor with context \(H\), its certificate
gives \(P_{M^t}(H)>0\), and hence the definition of finite conditional
probability gives
\[
 P_{M^t}(A\mid H)P_{M^t}(H)=P_{M^t}(A\cap H),
 \qquad
 P_{M^t}(A\mid H)
 =\frac{P_{M^t}(A\cap H)}{P_{M^t}(H)}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Equations (4)--(7) prove the corresponding primitive registry entries.

The remaining registry and substitution constructors do not assume a semantic
equality.  The checker first expands both sides using (1) and (2), multiplies
by their recorded finite denominator products, expands every finite sum and
product in the fixed monomial order, applies the displayed response-law
normalization equalities when one is present, and compares every rational
coefficient.  Acceptance therefore supplies the exact identity
\[
 N_LD_R-N_RD_L\equiv0.
 \tag{8}
\]
For a registry leaf, (8), (3), and the certified nonvanishing of \(D_LD_R\)
give \(\operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf M}(L)=
\operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf M}(R)\).  A well-typed substitution preserves (8)
because replacing the indeterminates in the zero polynomial by the checked
expression polynomials again gives the zero polynomial; equivalently, the
checker repeats coefficient comparison after substitution.  Congruence under
finite sums, products, complements, and certified quotients follows from (2),
and composition follows by transitivity.  These are all trace constructors,
so structural induction proves the invariant for the complete finite trace.

Applying the invariant to its endpoints proves that its input and output
expressions agree at the arbitrary
\(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  Since the model was arbitrary and
every cancellation used a separately certified positive context probability,
the claimed fixing-calculus soundness follows.
