For fixed \(S\) and \(\rho\), membership in \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\) is exactly feasibility of
\[
q\geq0,\quad A_Dq=m_D,\quad R_eq=p_e\ (e\in S),\quad
d_D^{\mathsf T}q>0,\quad
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\geq0
\quad(\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}).
\tag{1}
\]
The strict condition is decided by maximizing \(d_D^{\mathsf T}q\) over the closed face defined by the remaining constraints and testing whether the maximum is positive.  Hence finite subset enumeration and rational or real-algebraic linear programming compute every possibly-optimal set and every minimum-cost solution.

For the obstruction, let a genuine partial-copy regime return the binary tuple \(T=(Y_0,Y_1)\), whose four deterministic profiles are
\[
\omega_1=(0,0),\quad\omega_2=(0,1),\quad
\omega_3=(1,0),\quad\omega_4=(1,1).
\]
Experiment \(e_1\) uses the declared output map \(f_1(y_0,y_1)=\mathbf1\{y_0=y_1\}\), and \(e_2\) uses \(f_2(y_0,y_1)=\mathbf1\{(y_0,y_1)\ne(0,0)\}\).  By deterministic-preimage expansion, their output-one complete cells are exactly
\[
C_1=\{Y_0=Y_1\},\qquad
C_2=\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(0,0)\}.
\]
Evaluating these legal singleton output cells, together with \(B_\rho=\{T\ne(1,1)\}\), \(B_\sigma=\{T=(1,1)\}\), gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
&n_\rho&n_\sigma&r_1&r_2\\\hline
\omega_1&1&0&1&0\\
\omega_2&1&0&0&1\\
\omega_3&1&0&0&1\\
\omega_4&0&1&1&1
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
exactly.  Each complete binary output law adds only its output-one row and normalization-dependent complement.  Give both output-one cells value one at unit cost.  Their joint menu is realized by \(q=\delta_{\omega_4}\), and
\[
\mathcal K_{\{e_1\}}=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_1},\delta_{\omega_4}\},\quad
\mathcal K_{\{e_2\}}=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_2},\delta_{\omega_3},\delta_{\omega_4}\},\quad
\mathcal K_{\{e_1,e_2\}}=\{\delta_{\omega_4}\}.
\]
The first three point masses make \(\rho\) uniquely optimal and the fourth makes \(\sigma\) uniquely optimal.  Therefore both singleton acquisitions leave both arms possibly optimal, whereas the pair leaves only \(\sigma\).  The marginal gain of \(e_2\) rises from zero to one, violating submodularity.  Both experiments are necessary, so the acquisition program is \(\min x_1+x_2\) subject to \(x_1+x_2\geq2\) and \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\).  Its unique optimum is \((1,1)\); multiplier one gives the matching continuous-relaxation lower bound two.
