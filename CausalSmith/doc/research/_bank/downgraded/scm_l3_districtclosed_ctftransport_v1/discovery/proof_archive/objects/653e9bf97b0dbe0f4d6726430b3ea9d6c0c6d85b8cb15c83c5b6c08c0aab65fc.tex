Write \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4)^\top\) in the response-profile order of the binary definition.  The equations \(A_Dq=m_D\) are
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4=1,
\qquad q_3+q_4=\frac12,
\qquad q_2+q_4=\frac12,
\qquad q_j\geq0.
\]
Setting \(p=q_4\) gives
\[
q_1=p,
\qquad q_2=q_3=\frac12-p,
\qquad 0\leq p\leq\frac12,
\]
and proves the unique parameterization \(q(p)\).  Since \(d_Dq(p)=1\), direct row multiplication gives
\[
Q_\rho(q(p))=q_2+q_3=1-2p,
\qquad
Q_\sigma(q(p))=q_2+q_4=\frac12.
\]
The candidate row gives \(r_D^\dagger q(p)=q_4=p\), whence \(I_D=[0,1/2]\), and
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}q(p)
=-q_3+q_4=2p-\frac12.
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal J_{\rho\prec\sigma}^+=(1/4,1/2]\).

In the augmented row order,
\[
(0,-1,0,2)
\begin{bmatrix}A_D\\ r_D^\dagger\end{bmatrix}
=(0,0,-1,1)=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}.
\]
Thus the vector is dual feasible with equality, and its objective is
\[
(0,-1,0,2)(1,1/2,1/2,p)^\top=2p-\frac12.
\]
At \(p=3/8\) this is \(1/4\).
