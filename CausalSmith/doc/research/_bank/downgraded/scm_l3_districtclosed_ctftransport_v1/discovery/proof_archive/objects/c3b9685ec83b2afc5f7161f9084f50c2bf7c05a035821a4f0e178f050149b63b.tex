Assume \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), and fix a transport-admissible complete supplied cell \(C\) and a nonfocal profile \(\omega_{-D}\). By the district-separated definition there are two cases. If the indicator of \(C\) is independent of \(\omega_D\), then its focal slice is either the zero row or the normalization row \(\mathbf1^\top\). Both belong to \(\operatorname{row}(A_D)\subseteq R_D(m_D)\). If the indicator depends only on \(\omega_D\), every slice equals the raw row retained in \(A_D\), and again lies in \(R_D(m_D)\). Thus every required slice is active and
\[
\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P).\tag{1}
\]

For strictness, take two independent binary response coordinates \(Y_0\) and \(Z\) in distinct singleton districts, with \(D\) the district containing \(Y_0\). Supply both the complete marginal law of \(Y_0\) and the complete joint law of \((Y_0,Z)\). Write
\[
h_y(\omega_D)=\mathbf1\{Y_0=y\},\qquad
k_z(\omega_{-D})=\mathbf1\{Z=z\}.
\]
The marginal cells retain both rows \(h_y\) in \(A_D\). A joint cell has indicator \(h_yk_z\), which depends on both district profiles and therefore violates the two alternatives in \(\mathsf{Sep}_D\). After \(\omega_{-D}\) is fixed, however, its focal slice is either zero or \(h_y\), both in \(R_D(m_D)\). This holds for every complete joint cell, so \(\mathsf{ASep}_D\) holds. Independent Bernoulli response blocks realize the menu on the original binary alphabets, establishing strictness.
