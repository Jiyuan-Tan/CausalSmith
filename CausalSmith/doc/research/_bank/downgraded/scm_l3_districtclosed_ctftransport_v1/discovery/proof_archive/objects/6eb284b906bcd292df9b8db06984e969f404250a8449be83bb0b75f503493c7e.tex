Write
\[
K=\mathcal K_D(m_D),\qquad A=A_D,\qquad m=m_D,
\qquad Z=Z_D(m_D),\qquad J=\Omega_D\setminus Z.
\]
For broad sufficiency, let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\), \(q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\in K\).  If
\[
b_D=\lambda^\top A+
\sum_{\omega\in Z}\zeta_\omega e_\omega,
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_Dq_D^\star(\mathbf M)
&=\lambda^\top A q_D^\star(\mathbf M)
+\sum_{\omega\in Z}\zeta_\omega q_D^\star(\mathbf M)(\omega)\\
&=\lambda^\top m,
\end{aligned}
\]
because each feasible law vanishes on \(Z\).  The displayed expression is a function only of the supplied menu, proving identification on the broad fiber.

Now assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  The focal projection equality gives \(\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=K\), so \(K\ne\varnothing\).  The normalization row in \(A\) implies \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\) for every \(q\in K\), hence \(J\ne\varnothing\).  For each \(\omega\in J\), the definition of \(Z\) supplies \(q^\omega\in K\) with \(q^\omega(\omega)>0\).  Therefore
\[
\bar q=\frac{1}{\lvert J\rvert}\sum_{\omega\in J}q^\omega
\]
belongs to \(K\), vanishes on \(Z\), and is strictly positive on \(J\).

Define
\[
L=\{v\in\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}:Av=0,
\ v(\omega)=0\text{ for every }\omega\in Z\}.
\]
For \(v\in L\), finiteness of \(J\) and positivity of \(\bar q\) permit \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
\varepsilon\lvert v(\omega)\rvert\leq\frac12\bar q(\omega)
\qquad(\omega\in J).
\]
Then \(\bar q\pm\varepsilon v\geq0\) and \(A(\bar q\pm\varepsilon v)=m\), so both points lie in \(K\).  Conversely, every difference of two points of \(K\) lies in \(L\).  Thus \(L\) is the direction space of the affine hull of \(K\).

Let \(\widetilde A\) be obtained by appending the rows \(e_\omega\), \(\omega\in Z\), to \(A\).  Then \(L=\ker(\widetilde A)\).  Elementary finite-dimensional row reduction gives
\[
L^\perp=(\ker\widetilde A)^\perp
=\operatorname{row}(\widetilde A)
=\operatorname{row}(A)+\operatorname{span}\{e_\omega:\omega\in Z\}.
\]
The target is constant on the focal saturated fiber exactly when \(b_Dv=0\) for every \(v\in L\), equivalently exactly when \(b_D\in L^\perp\), which is the displayed active-face row space.  If membership fails, choose \(v\in L\) with \(b_Dv\ne0\) and choose \(\varepsilon>0\) as above.  Then
\[
q_+=\bar q+\varepsilon v,
\qquad q_-=\bar q-\varepsilon v
\]
belong to \(K\) and satisfy
\[
b_Dq_+-b_Dq_-=2\varepsilon b_Dv\ne0.
\]
The focal projection theorem supplies \(\mathbf M_+,\mathbf M_-\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\) on \(\mathcal X\) inducing \(q_+,q_-\), respectively.  They agree on every supplied law because they lie in the same compatibility fiber, and they disagree on the target cell.  Its construction alters only the response law at \(D\), so all other districts retain their graph-compatible baseline restrictions.  Outside the exact focal-projection hypotheses, the proof yields only broad sufficiency and variation in the local row-face relaxation.
