Put
\[
J=D\cap\Delta_s,
\qquad
I=D\setminus\Delta_s.
\]
The displayed partial-selection hypothesis gives \(J\ne\varnothing\), \(I\ne\varnothing\), and \(D\cap\Delta_s\ne\varnothing\). Hence the antecedent \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\) in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) is false for the pair \((s,D)\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\) constrains each domain separately, and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) constrains distinct districts within a domain; neither equates the response law of this partially selected district across domains. We now give an explicit construction showing that no such equality is a consequence of the retained assumptions.

By Definition \(\mathrm{def:incidence\text{-}row}\), a binary incidence row on \(\Omega_D\) is a function \(\ell:\Omega_D\to\{0,1\}\). Let \(\ell\) be nonconstant. Since \(\Omega_D\) is finite, there exist \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) such that
\[
\ell(\omega_0)=0,
\qquad
\ell(\omega_1)=1.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), define a source-labelled family \(\mathbf M^{(i)}\) whose focal-district response laws satisfy
\[
q_D^{s,(i)}=\delta_{\omega_0},
\qquad
q_D^{\star,(i)}=\delta_{\omega_i},
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\delta_\omega\) is the point mass at \(\omega\). For every further domain \(t\in\mathcal S\setminus\{s,\star\}\), set
\[
q_D^{t,(i)}=
\begin{cases}
\delta_{\omega_i},&D\cap\Delta_t=\varnothing,\\
\delta_{\omega_0},&D\cap\Delta_t\ne\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every instance of Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) involving \(D\) and having a true antecedent is satisfied. For each district \(D'\ne D\), choose one deterministic response profile \(\bar\omega_{D'}\) and put \(q_{D'}^{t,(i)}=\delta_{\bar\omega_{D'}}\) for every domain \(t\) and both \(i\). These common choices satisfy every remaining instance of district-kernel invariance.

It remains to verify that these response laws are realized by SCMs on the declared alphabets rather than on enlarged state spaces. By Definition \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\), each coordinate of \(\omega_i\) is a deterministic structural response map with codomain \(\mathcal X_V\) at every parent configuration visited by the target and the typed menus. For each unvisited graph-allowed parent configuration of vertex \(V\), choose an arbitrary \(x_V^\circ\in\mathcal X_V\); such a value exists because every declared alphabet is nonempty. Extend each map by \(x_V^\circ\), and extend every \(\bar\omega_{D'}\) in the same way. Use these completed tables as the structural equations and one-point exogenous laws. No additional observed value is introduced. Acyclicity of \(G\) gives a unique recursive evaluation in a topological order, so the resulting finite SCM is compatible with \(G\) and \(\mathcal X\), as required by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\). All exogenous laws are degenerate and may be chosen independently across distinct districts, so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) holds. Equations (2)--(3) verify Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) whenever its antecedent is true. Therefore both \(\mathbf M^{(0)}\) and \(\mathbf M^{(1)}\) satisfy all retained frozen assumptions on the original state spaces.

The complete SCM in the designated partially selected source is identical in the two families:
\[
M^{s,(0)}=M^{s,(1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, for every descriptor \(a\in\mathcal A_s\), every observational, interventional, or declared same-source counterfactual law supplied from domain \(s\) is identical under the two families. In particular, the value of the source incidence row is the same:
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{s,(0)}(\omega)
=
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{s,(1)}(\omega)
=\ell(\omega_0)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
By contrast, the two target values are
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{\star,(0)}(\omega)
=\ell(\omega_0)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{\star,(1)}(\omega)
=\ell(\omega_1)=1.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (2)--(6) are an explicit equal-designated-source-law, unequal-target paired-model certificate. This is the source-row-only information configuration stated in the theorem: adding a target-domain law or a wholly unselected source law that already copies the target district would be additional statistical information, not a graph-only replacement for district closure. Any graph-only replacement for district closure that still admits this proper partial-selection pattern admits the same graph and selection specification for both members of the pair, and therefore cannot make this nonconstant row universally transportable from that source alone.

Conversely, if \(\ell\equiv c\) is constant, then normalization of every \(q\in\Delta(\Omega_D)\) gives
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q(\omega)
=c\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}q(\omega)
=c.
\tag{7}
\]
Hence every constant row transports without any cross-domain equality, while (1)--(6) exclude every nonconstant binary row. This proves the first characterization.

Now impose the additional premise that the marginal response law on \(I\) is invariant between the designated source and target. Let
\[
\pi_I:\Omega_D\longrightarrow\pi_I(\Omega_D)
\]
be coordinate restriction to the response maps of the vertices in \(I\). The additional premise is the well-typed equality
\[
(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^s=(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^\star,
\tag{8}
\]
where \((\pi_I)_{\#}\) denotes finite pushforward.

Suppose first that \(\ell\) is constant on every fiber of \(\pi_I\). Define \(\widetilde\ell:\pi_I(\Omega_D)\to\{0,1\}\) by \(\widetilde\ell(u)=\ell(\omega)\) for any \(\omega\) satisfying \(\pi_I(\omega)=u\). Fiber constancy makes this definition independent of the choice of \(\omega\), and \(\ell=\widetilde\ell\circ\pi_I\). Grouping the finite sums by projection fibers and using (8) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^s(\omega)
&=\sum_{u\in\pi_I(\Omega_D)}
  \widetilde\ell(u)(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^s(u)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\pi_I(\Omega_D)}
  \widetilde\ell(u)(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^\star(u)\\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^\star(\omega).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore fiber constancy is sufficient for transport under the marginal-invariance premise.

For necessity, suppose that \(\ell\) is not constant on the fibers of \(\pi_I\). Then there exist \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) such that
\[
\pi_I(\omega_0)=\pi_I(\omega_1),
\qquad
\ell(\omega_0)\ne\ell(\omega_1).
\tag{10}
\]
Repeat the paired construction (2)--(4) with these two profiles. In both members of the pair, the source marginal is the point mass at \(\pi_I(\omega_0)\), and
\[
(\pi_I)_{\#}\delta_{\omega_0}
=\delta_{\pi_I(\omega_0)}
=\delta_{\pi_I(\omega_1)}
=(\pi_I)_{\#}\delta_{\omega_1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus each member satisfies the added source--target marginal-invariance premise, their complete supplied laws from source \(s\) remain identical, and their target row values are \(\ell(\omega_0)\) and \(\ell(\omega_1)\), which differ by (10). Hence universal transport under (8) implies fiber constancy. Together with (9), this proves the claimed equivalence.

The condition is decidable because \(\Omega_D\) is finite. Enumerate the finite set \(\Omega_D^2\) and reject exactly when a pair \((\omega,\omega')\) satisfies
\[
\pi_I(\omega)=\pi_I(\omega')
\quad\text{and}\quad
\ell(\omega)\ne\ell(\omega').
\tag{12}
\]
If enumeration returns no pair, exhaustion certifies fiber constancy. If it returns a pair, the two Dirac laws at that pair, completed on the original alphabets as above, are the promised two-point leakage certificate. This is precisely the finite coordinate-fiber test named by Definition \(\mathrm{def:partial\text{-}district\text{-}leakage\text{-}handle}\).

For the smallest stress check, take the binary two-vertex ADMG \(X\leftrightarrow Y\), let \(D=\{X,Y\}\), \(\Delta_s=\{X\}\), choose constant response profiles \(\omega_0=(X=0,Y=0)\) and \(\omega_1=(X=1,Y=0)\), and put \(\ell(\omega)=\mathbf 1\{X(\omega)=1\}\). Both source laws are \(\delta_{\omega_0}\), whereas target laws \(\delta_{\omega_0}\) and \(\delta_{\omega_1}\) give row values zero and one. They have the same marginal response law on \(I=\{Y\}\). Restoring district closure excludes \(\Delta_s=\{X\}\), since neither \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\) nor \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\) holds. Thus the stress check also confirms that district closure is load-bearing.
