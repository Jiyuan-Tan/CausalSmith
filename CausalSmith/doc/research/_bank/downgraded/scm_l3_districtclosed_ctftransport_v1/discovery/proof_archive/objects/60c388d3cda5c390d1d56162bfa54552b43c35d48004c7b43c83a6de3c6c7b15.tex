Let \(\mathbb D\) be the effective ordered coefficient ring generated by the finitely many supplied rational or real-algebraic output-cell probabilities.  Their stipulated effective presentations give exact arithmetic and sign comparison in \(\mathbb D\), and an order-preserving embedding \(\mathbb D\hookrightarrow\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}\) supplies exact coefficient representations.  A \(\mathsf{QEFormula}\) is finite syntax for a quantifier-free Boolean combination of polynomial sign tests with coefficients in \(\mathbb D\).  For every real-closed field \(K\) containing an ordered copy of \(\mathbb D\), this syntax has the usual realization \(\llbracket\chi\rrbracket_K\) in its displayed free variables.  In particular, the causal realization used below is \(\llbracket\chi\rrbracket_{\mathbb R}\) over the ordinary real field; neither canonical probability variables nor free target-value variables are restricted to \(\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}\).

The hybrid terminal grammar is exactly
\[
\begin{split}
\mathsf{HybridResult}::={}&
\operatorname{ID\_RAT}(F,\delta)
\mid\operatorname{UNCOND\_QE}(\chi_T,\chi_{\min},\chi_{\max},\mathsf{isSingleton})\\
&\mid\operatorname{ZERO\_EVIDENCE\_QE}
\mid\operatorname{NOT\_UNIFORMLY\_DEFINED\_QE}\\
&\mid\operatorname{COND\_QE}(\chi_Q,\chi_{\min},\chi_{\max},\mathsf{isSingleton}),
\end{split}
\tag{1}
\]
where each displayed \(\chi\) is a \(\mathsf{QEFormula}\) and \(\mathsf{isSingleton}\) is the exact Boolean output of the associated closed first-order sentence.

The deterministic procedure \(\mathsf{DCTFTR\text{-}CAN}\) first runs \(\mathsf{DCTFTR}\).  A successful rational identification is returned as \(\operatorname{ID\_RAT}\).  Every other terminal invokes the graph-compatible canonical fiber
\[
S=\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P).
\]
Every quantifier-elimination call below is made in the first-order language of real-closed fields with coefficients in \(\mathbb D\); its computation performs only exact coefficient operations in \(\mathbb D\), and its returned syntax is interpreted over \(\mathbb R\).  For \(p_T=\Phi_{T,\mathrm{can}}^\star\), apply quantifier elimination to
\[
\varphi_T(t):=\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge t-p_T(x)=0]
\tag{2}
\]
and denote the output by \(\chi_T(t)\).  Apply it to the closed sentence
\[
\exists t_1,t_2\,[\chi_T(t_1)\wedge\chi_T(t_2)\wedge t_1\ne t_2]
\tag{3}
\]
and to
\[
\chi_T(\ell)\wedge\forall t\,[\chi_T(t)\Longrightarrow\ell\leq t],
\qquad
\chi_T(u)\wedge\forall t\,[\chi_T(t)\Longrightarrow t\leq u]
\tag{4}
\]
to form the singleton Boolean and endpoint formulas.

For a conditional target put \(e=\Phi_{E,\mathrm{can}}^\star\) and \(n=\Phi_{B_\rho\cap E,\mathrm{can}}^\star\).  Define
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathsf Z:&\neg\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)>0],\\
\mathsf N:&\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)=0]
\ \wedge\ \exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)>0],\\
\mathsf P:&\neg\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)=0].
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Three quantifier-elimination calls yield closed quantifier-free formulas whose exact constant sign evaluations decide \(\mathsf Z\), \(\mathsf N\), and \(\mathsf P\).  The first two return the corresponding payload-free evidence terminals.  Only in case \(\mathsf P\) is quantifier elimination applied to
\[
\varphi_Q(t):=\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)>0\wedge e(x)t-n(x)=0]
\tag{6}
\]
to form \(\chi_Q(t)\), its singleton Boolean, and endpoint formulas as in \((3)\)--\((4)\).  The procedure returns formulas and decisions only: it returns no explicit endpoint value, algebraic sample, cylindrical algebraic decomposition trace, canonical parameter, or paired SCM.
