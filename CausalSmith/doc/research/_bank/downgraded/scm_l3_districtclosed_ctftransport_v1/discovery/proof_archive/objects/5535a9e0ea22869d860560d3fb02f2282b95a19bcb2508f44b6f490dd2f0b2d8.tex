Write \(K=\mathcal K_D(m_D)\), \(r=r_D^\dagger\), and \(g=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}\).  The normalization row makes \(K\) a nonempty compact convex polytope in the probability simplex.  Its image under the linear map \(q\mapsto rq\) is therefore a nonempty compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R\), hence a closed interval \(I_D\).  For each \(p\in I_D\),
\[
K(p)=\{q\in K:rq=p\}
\]
is nonempty and compact, so the defining minimum is attained.

Let \(p_0,p_1\in I_D\), choose minimizers \(q_i\in K(p_i)\), and let \(0\leq t\leq1\).  Then \(tq_0+(1-t)q_1\in K(tp_0+(1-t)p_1)\), so
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(tp_0+(1-t)p_1;m_D)
\leq t\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p_0;m_D)
+(1-t)\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p_1;m_D).
\]
Thus the value function is convex.

Append \(r\) to \(A_D\) and apply finite linear-programming strong duality to the nonempty bounded primal fiber \(K(p)\).  This gives
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)
=\max_{(\eta,\beta):A_D^\top\eta+\beta r^\top\leq g^\top}
\{m_D^\top\eta+\beta p\}.
\]
The dual feasible polyhedron is independent of \((m_D,p)\) and is described by finitely many rational inequalities, because \(A_D,r,g\) have entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).  We now justify the finite-basis representation rather than assume it.  Quotient its lineality space \(L_0=\{(u,t):A_D^\top u+t r^\top=0\}\).  Primal feasibility implies \(m_D^\top u+tp=0\) on \(L_0\), so the dual objective is well defined on the quotient.  Starting from an attained dual optimum, if the active constraint normals do not span the quotient, choose a nonzero quotient direction annihilated by all active normals.  Both sufficiently small signs of this direction remain feasible; optimality forces zero objective slope.  Moving in a sign that is not a recession direction until the first inactive inequality becomes tight preserves optimality and increases the span of the active normals.  Such a sign exists, since otherwise the direction would lie in \(L_0\).  Finitely many repetitions reach an optimal point determined modulo \(L_0\) by a spanning subset of active constraints.  There are only finitely many such subsets.  Solving each corresponding nonsingular subsystem after a rational choice of complement to \(L_0\) gives a rational pair \((\eta_B,\beta_B)\).  Therefore, over all \(p\in I_D\),
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)
=\max_B\{m_D^\top\eta_B+\beta_Bp\}
\]
for a finite collection of feasible bases \(B\).  This proves that the value is finite, continuous, convex, and piecewise affine, with rational slopes and intercepts that are rational linear, hence rational affine, functions of \(m_D\).

The sublevel set
\[
N=\{p\in I_D:\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)\leq0\}
\]
is closed by continuity and convex by convexity.  A closed convex subset of a real interval is empty, a point, or a closed interval.  Its relative complement is \(\mathcal J_{\rho\prec\sigma}^+\), so this set has zero, one, or two relative boundaries.  At a boundary \(p_0\), continuity gives \(\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p_0;m_D)=0\).  Primal attainment supplies \(q_0\in K(p_0)\) with \(gq_0=0\).  Since only finitely many dual pieces occur, one piece active along a sequence on any adjacent positive component recurs infinitely often; its affine objective converges to zero at \(p_0\).  Its feasibility does not depend on \(p\), so it is a zero-valued dual menu certificate at the boundary and is positive on the adjacent piece.

Finally augment the supplied menu by the equality \(rq=p_0\).  If its focal-district-saturated compatibility fiber is nonempty and its \(\mathsf{Sep}_D\) predicate holds, the focal projection theorem applied to the augmented row system realizes \(q_0\) by an original-alphabet compatible SCM that is saturated only at \(D\).  The stated condition \(d_Dq_0>0\) permits division, and
\[
Q_\sigma^\star-Q_\rho^\star=\frac{gq_0}{d_Dq_0}=0.
\]
The realization changes no other district's graph-compatible structure.  Without these focal exact-projection hypotheses, primal attainment establishes only a boundary law of the local row-face relaxation.
