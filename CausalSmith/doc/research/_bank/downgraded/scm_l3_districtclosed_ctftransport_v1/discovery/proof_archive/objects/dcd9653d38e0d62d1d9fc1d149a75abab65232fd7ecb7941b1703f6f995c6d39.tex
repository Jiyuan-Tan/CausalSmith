We prove the two exact finite range equalities separately.

First assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\). For every domain \(s\) and district \(D\), push the law of the full response block \(U_D^s\in\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) forward to the profile coordinates \(\Omega_D\) visited by the declared menus and target cells; call the resulting law \(q_D^s\). Independence of the response blocks for distinct districts gives their product law. Thus, for every declared finite counterfactual cell \(C\), finite recursive evaluation and total probability give
\[
P_{M^s}(C)
=\sum_{\omega\in\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}\Omega_D}
\mathbf 1\{C\text{ is true under }\omega\}
\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}q_D^s(\omega_D)
=\Psi_C^s(\theta^s).
\]
If \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), district-kernel invariance gives \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\). Typed-menu realizability gives, for every complete supplied cell,
\[
\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_{M^s}^{a}(v)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Hence \(\theta=(q_D^s)_{s,D}\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\), and the target-cell equality above proves the left-to-right saturated range inclusion simultaneously for every finite \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, take \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\). For every partial profile \(\omega_D\in\Omega_D\), assign at each unvisited parent configuration an arbitrary fixed element of the nonempty alphabet \(\mathcal X_V\). This constructs an injective completion map \(\Omega_D\to\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\). Push each law \(q_D^s\) through this map and use the resulting law as the law of \(U_D^s\). Make the response-block variables independent across districts, and let each structural equation read the component response map indexed by \(U_D^s\). Acyclicity of \(G\) makes recursive evaluation unique, and every output lies in its declared alphabet. By the self-contained construction of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\), bidirected completeness, a single full response block per district, independence across districts, and unrestricted permitted response-law choices make this a member of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\). Because the same completion map is used in every domain, each equality \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\) for an unchanged district becomes equality of the completed response-block laws.

The complete-cell equations in \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) now give
\[
P_{M_\theta^s}^{a}(v)
=\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)
=P_a^s(v)
\]
for every \(s,a,v\). Equality of all complete cells is equality of each supplied law. Therefore \(\mathbf M_\theta\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\), and its target cells equal the corresponding \(\Psi\)-values. This proves the reverse saturated inclusion and original-alphabet realization.

Now consider the arbitrary-ADMG frontier. Take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). Recursive compatibility makes each \(M^s\) a finite-state recursive SCM on \((G,\mathcal X)\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}canonical\text{-}counterfactual\text{-}equivalence}\) in every domain. The resulting graph-compatible canonical model has the same observed alphabets and preserves every finite joint counterfactual law, in particular every district response-profile law used here. Hence district-kernel invariance in \(\mathbf M\) implies
\[
q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\cdot;\vartheta^s)
=q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\cdot;\vartheta^\star)
\quad\text{when }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing.
\]
Preservation of the complete supplied cells and typed-menu realizability give
\[
\Phi_{C_{a,v},\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Thus \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\), and preservation of all target cells proves one canonical range inclusion.

Conversely, a point \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\) consists of graph-compatible one-hot structural tables and finite canonical exogenous probability vectors. The one-hot constraints select exactly one output in \(\mathcal X_V\) for every structural input, the simplex constraints define probability laws, and the graph-compatible latent restrictions and product law across districts give recursive compatibility and district factorization. The displayed response-law equalities give district-kernel invariance, while district closure is part of the fixed problem instance. Finally, the equation for every complete supplied cell gives each entire supplied law. The canonical realization therefore belongs to \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), stays on the original alphabets, and has target-cell probabilities \(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star)\). This proves the reverse canonical inclusion.

Both parameter fibers are finite dimensional. The saturated fiber is a closed subset of a finite product of probability simplices cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities. The graph-compatible canonical fiber is a closed subset of a finite union of products of probability simplices and finite one-hot sets, cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities. Both are bounded, hence compact, and both are semialgebraic by their displayed finite polynomial descriptions. Their nonemptiness follows from the corresponding assumed nonempty compatibility fiber and the already proved forward inclusion. In the formula for \(\Psi_C^s\), every summand contains at most one coordinate from each district block visited by \(C\); hence \(\Psi_C^s\) is multi-affine and has total degree at most the number of visited districts.

Fix either exact range equality and a target cell \(T\). Its target polynomial is continuous on a nonempty compact fiber, so it attains a minimum and a maximum. Exact range equality shows that these two numbers are respectively the greatest valid lower bound and least valid upper bound over the corresponding SCM compatibility fiber: each is attained by an original-alphabet compatible SCM. Since cell probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), at least one bound is strictly inside the logical interval exactly when the lower endpoint is positive or the upper endpoint is below one. The cell is constant, and hence identified, exactly when the two extrema agree. If they differ, an attaining minimizer and an attaining maximizer realize two distinct target values while satisfying the same complete menu. This proves endpoint sharpness without requiring the scalar image between them to be an interval.

Apply the same argument to the nonnegative evidence polynomial. Its minimum and maximum are attained. If the maximum is zero, nonnegativity makes evidence null throughout the fiber. If the minimum is zero and the maximum is positive, a minimizing compatible model has zero evidence, so positivity is not uniform. In either case the conditional is not defined on the whole fiber and division must be rejected. If the evidence minimum is strictly positive, the corresponding ratio
\[
x\longmapsto
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)},
\]
where \((x,\Xi)\) is respectively \((\theta,\Psi)\) or \((\vartheta,\Phi_{\mathrm{can}})\), is continuous on the compact fiber. Its minimum and maximum are therefore attained, and exact range equality makes them sharp lower and upper conditional bounds. If those values differ, the minimizing and maximizing realizations are distinct original-alphabet same-menu SCM families. Again, only endpoint sharpness is used.

For two arms the denominator is common. Under the same strict positivity condition,
\[
Q_\sigma^\star(\mathbf M_x)-Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_x)
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star(x^\star)
-\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}.
\]
The denominator is positive, so the signs agree. A continuous numerator-gap polynomial that is positive at every point of a compact fiber has a strictly positive attained minimum, and the converse is immediate. The analogous statement with a negative attained maximum proves uniform reverse ordering. A negative minimum and positive maximum give two attaining, same-menu, original-alphabet models with opposite strict rankings. Conversely, any such two models force the corresponding strict inequalities for the extrema. This proves the exact pruning tests.

Finally, let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). The broad part of the projection theorem stacks normalization, every admissible district-local raw cell, and every certified derived row to give
\[
q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\geq0,
\qquad A_Dq_D^\star(\mathbf M)=m_D.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D)\). The reverse inclusion is available for \(\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) only under the nonempty saturated-fiber and separation hypotheses. In a sparse or cross-district problem, latent-factor restrictions or complete cross-district menu equations can make the local row face a strict outer relaxation. Therefore local failure alone supplies no global pair; distinct endpoint optimizers of either exact global polynomial program do supply original-alphabet same-menu countermodels whenever their objective values differ.
