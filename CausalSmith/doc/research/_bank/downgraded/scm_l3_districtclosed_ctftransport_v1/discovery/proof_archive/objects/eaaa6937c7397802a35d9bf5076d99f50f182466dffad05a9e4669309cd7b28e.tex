For every finite checked local-arm acquisition instance, all minimum-cost Layer-3 regime-block solutions are characterized by enumerating \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\) and solving, for each arm, the finite linear feasibility system defining \(\mathcal K_S\) together with the pairwise numerator-gap inequalities; the checked common evidence certificate makes these inequalities equivalent to arm-value comparisons.  The computation is effective for rational or real-algebraic entries and costs.  The elimination function need not be submodular.  Explicitly, a one-domain binary graph \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\) with four response maps and two unit-cost partial-copy cell experiments has
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1\})=
\operatorname{PO}(\{e_2\})=\{\rho,\sigma\},\qquad
\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1,e_2\})=\{\sigma\}.
\]
Its exact acquisition problem is \(\min_{x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}}x_1+x_2\) subject to \(x_1+x_2\ge2\), with unique solution \((1,1)\), cost two, and continuous-relaxation dual multiplier one.
