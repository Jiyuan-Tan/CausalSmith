Put
\[
J=D\cap\Delta_s,
\qquad
I=D\setminus\Delta_s.
\]
The displayed partial-selection hypothesis gives \(J\ne\varnothing\), \(I\ne\varnothing\), and \(D\cap\Delta_s\ne\varnothing\).  Therefore the antecedent \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\) in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) is false for the pair \((s,D)\).  Once district closure is omitted, the three retained assumptions consequently impose no equality between \(q_D^s\) and \(q_D^\star\).  In particular, they imply neither equality of the complete district response laws nor equality of their marginals on \(I\).

By Definition \(\mathrm{def:incidence\text{-}row}\), a binary incidence row is a function from \(\Omega_D\) to \(\{0,1\}\).  Let \(\ell:\Omega_D\to\{0,1\}\) be an arbitrary nonconstant such row.  Nonconstancy supplies \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) such that
\[
\ell(\omega_0)=0,
\qquad
\ell(\omega_1)=1.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), construct a source-labelled family \(\mathbf M^{(i)}\) by assigning the focal-district response laws
\[
q_D^{s,(i)}=\delta_{\omega_0},
\qquad
q_D^{\star,(i)}=\delta_{\omega_i}.
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(\delta_\omega\) denotes the probability law assigning mass one to \(\omega\).
For each further domain \(t\in\mathcal S\setminus\{s,\star\}\), assign
\[
q_D^{t,(i)}=
\begin{cases}
\delta_{\omega_i},&D\cap\Delta_t=\varnothing,\\
\delta_{\omega_0},&D\cap\Delta_t\ne\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every instance of district-kernel invariance with district \(D\) and true antecedent is satisfied.  For every district \(D'\ne D\), choose a deterministic response profile \(\bar\omega_{D'}\) and set \(q_{D'}^{t,(i)}=\delta_{\bar\omega_{D'}}\) for every domain \(t\) and both values of \(i\).  These assignments satisfy all remaining instances of district-kernel invariance.

We verify that (2)--(3) describe SCMs on the declared alphabets, rather than laws on an enlarged carrier.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\), each coordinate of \(\omega_i\) is a deterministic structural response map whose codomain is \(\mathcal X_V\).  For every graph-allowed parent configuration not visited in the definition of \(\Omega_D\), choose a fixed value \(x_V^\circ\in\mathcal X_V\), which exists because each declared state space is finite and nonempty, and extend the response map by that value.  Do the same for each \(\bar\omega_{D'}\).  Use the completed tables as structural equations, and use one-point exogenous laws.  Acyclicity of \(G\), together with Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), permits recursive evaluation in a topological order; every table has values in the original \(\mathcal X_V\) and uses only graph-allowed parents.  Hence the construction is a finite original-alphabet SCM compatible with \(G\).  Because every district block is degenerate, its joint law is the product of the district marginals, so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\) holds.  Equations (2)--(3) and the common laws outside \(D\) verify Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) whenever its antecedent is true.  Its antecedent for \((s,D)\) is false.  Thus both constructed families satisfy every retained frozen assumption.

The entire SCM in the partially selected source is the same in the two families:
\[
M^{s,(0)}=M^{s,(1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently every supplied observational, interventional, or same-source declared counterfactual law used to form the source row is identical in the two families.  In particular, the supplied row value is
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{s,(0)}(\omega)
=
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{s,(1)}(\omega)
=\ell(\omega_0)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The source-row-only information configuration in the theorem contains no target-domain law, and no additional wholly unselected source law that would copy the target district law.  Such a law would be an additional statistical input, not a graph-only replacement for district closure.  On the other hand, the target values are
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{\star,(0)}(\omega)
=\ell(\omega_0)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^{\star,(1)}(\omega)
=\ell(\omega_1)=1.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (4)--(6) are the required equal-source-law, unequal-target paired-model certificate.  Conversely, if \(\ell\equiv c\) is constant, normalization of every response law \(q\in\Delta(\Omega_D)\) gives
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q(\omega)
=c\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}q(\omega)=c.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus constant rows transport universally, whereas (1)--(6) refute universal transport for every nonconstant binary row.  This proves the first characterization.

Now add the premise that the source and target marginal response laws on \(I\) are invariant.  Let
\[
\pi_I:\Omega_D\longrightarrow\pi_I(\Omega_D)
\]
be restriction to the response-map coordinates belonging to vertices in \(I\), and write \((\pi_I)_{\#}q\) for the pushforward of a law \(q\in\Delta(\Omega_D)\) under \(\pi_I\).  The additional premise is the well-typed equality
\[
(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^s=(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^\star.
\tag{8}
\]
Suppose first that \(\ell\) is constant on every fiber of \(\pi_I\).  The function
\[
\widetilde\ell(u)=\ell(\omega)
\quad\text{for any }\omega\in\Omega_D\text{ satisfying }\pi_I(\omega)=u
\]
is then well defined on \(\pi_I(\Omega_D)\), and \(\ell=\widetilde\ell\circ\pi_I\).  Grouping the finite sums by projection fibers and applying (8) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^s(\omega)
&=\sum_{u\in\pi_I(\Omega_D)}
  \widetilde\ell(u)(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^s(u)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\pi_I(\Omega_D)}
  \widetilde\ell(u)(\pi_I)_{\#}q_D^\star(u)\\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\ell(\omega)q_D^\star(\omega).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Hence fiber constancy is sufficient.

For necessity, suppose that \(\ell\) is not constant on the fibers.  Then there are \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) with
\[
\pi_I(\omega_0)=\pi_I(\omega_1),
\qquad
\ell(\omega_0)\ne\ell(\omega_1).
\tag{10}
\]
Use \(q_D^s=\delta_{\omega_0}\) and \(q_D^\star=\delta_{\omega_1}\), and complete the two-point SCM as in (2)--(3).  Equality of the restricted profiles in (10) implies
\[
(\pi_I)_{\#}\delta_{\omega_0}
=\delta_{\pi_I(\omega_0)}
=\delta_{\pi_I(\omega_1)}
=(\pi_I)_{\#}\delta_{\omega_1},
\tag{11}
\]
so the added marginal-invariance premise holds, while the source and target row values are respectively \(\ell(\omega_0)\) and \(\ell(\omega_1)\), which differ by (10).  Therefore a row transports for every compatible pair satisfying (8) only if it is constant on every projection fiber.  Together with (9), this proves the asserted equivalence.

The test is finite and computable because \(\Omega_D\) is finite.  Enumerate all ordered pairs \((\omega,\omega')\in\Omega_D^2\), and reject precisely when
\[
\pi_I(\omega)=\pi_I(\omega')
\quad\text{and}\quad
\ell(\omega)\ne\ell(\omega').
\tag{12}
\]
If no pair is returned, exhaustive enumeration certifies fiber constancy.  If a pair is returned, the two Dirac laws at that pair, with the original-alphabet completion above, give the promised leakage certificate.

Finally, consider the smallest stress check: the binary two-vertex ADMG \(X\leftrightarrow Y\), with \(D=\{X,Y\}\), \(\Delta_s=\{X\}\), profiles \(\omega_0=(X=0,Y=0)\) and \(\omega_1=(X=1,Y=0)\), and row \(\ell(\omega)=\mathbf 1\{X(\omega)=1\}\).  The two source laws are both \(\delta_{\omega_0}\), while target laws \(\delta_{\omega_0}\) and \(\delta_{\omega_1}\) give row values zero and one.  Both target laws have the same \(I=\{Y\}\) marginal.  Thus this instance verifies both the graph-only obstruction and the failure of a non-fiber-constant row under marginal invariance.  Restoring district closure forbids \(\Delta_s=\{X\}\), because neither \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\) nor \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\) holds, confirming that district closure is load-bearing.
