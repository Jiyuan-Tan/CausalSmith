We prove the two exact finite range equalities separately and then derive their optimization consequences.

First assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\).  For every domain \(s\) and district \(D\), push the law of the full response block \(U_D^s\in\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) forward to the profile coordinates \(\Omega_D\) visited by the declared menus and target cells; call the resulting law \(q_D^s\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\), the joint law of the district blocks is their product.  Therefore finite recursive evaluation and total probability give, for every declared counterfactual cell \(C\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{M^s}(C)
&=\sum_{\omega\in\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}\Omega_D}
\mathbf 1\{C\text{ is true under recursive evaluation of }\omega\}
\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}q_D^s(\omega_D)\\
&=\Psi_C^s(\theta^s).
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) gives \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\).  For every complete supplied cell, \(\mathrm{ass:typed\text{-}menu\text{-}realizability}\) gives
\[
\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_{M^s}^{a}(v)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Hence \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\), proving the left-to-right saturated range inclusion simultaneously for every finite \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, take \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  Nonemptiness of every alphabet permits an injective deterministic completion \(\iota_D:\Omega_D\to\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) at the finitely many unvisited parent configurations.  Push each \(q_D^s\) forward through \(\iota_D\), take these district blocks independently, and let each structural equation read its component response map.  Acyclicity gives unique recursive evaluation, and every output remains in \(\mathcal X\).  The defining clauses of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\) admit every such independent collection of full district-response laws because every district is bidirected complete.  Using the same \(\iota_D\) in every domain turns each equality \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\) for an unchanged district into equality of its completed full-block laws.  The complete-cell equations in \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) then imply
\[
P_{M_\theta^s}^{a}(v)
=\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)
=P_a^s(v)
\]
for every \(s,a,v\).  Thus \(\mathbf M_\theta\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\), remains on the original alphabets, and realizes every corresponding \(\Psi\)-value.  This proves the reverse saturated range inclusion.

For the arbitrary-ADMG row, take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), each \(M^s\) is a finite recursive SCM on \((G,\mathcal X)\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}canonical\text{-}counterfactual\text{-}equivalence}\) in each domain to the finite joint collection containing all response-map counterfactuals visited here, all supplied cells, and all target cells in \(\mathcal F\).  The resulting graph-compatible canonical model has the same observed alphabets and the same joint counterfactual law.  In particular it preserves each district response-profile law, so \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) implies
\[
q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\mathord\cdot;\vartheta^s)
=q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\mathord\cdot;\vartheta^\star)
\quad\text{when }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing.
\]
Preservation of complete supplied cells gives
\[
\Phi_{C_{a,v},\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Hence \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\), and preservation of every target cell proves one canonical range inclusion.

Conversely, a point \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\) consists of graph-compatible one-hot structural tables and finite canonical exogenous probability vectors.  The one-hot constraints choose one value in \(\mathcal X_V\) for every structural input; the simplex constraints define probability laws; and the graph-compatible latent restrictions and product law across distinct districts give recursive compatibility and district factorization.  The response-law equalities in the definition give district-kernel invariance, while district closure is a fixed condition of the model-class instance.  The equality for every complete supplied cell gives each entire supplied law.  The canonical realization thus lies in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), remains on the original alphabets, and has target-cell probabilities \(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star)\).  This proves the reverse canonical inclusion.

Both parameter fibers are finite dimensional.  The saturated fiber is a closed subset of a finite product of probability simplices cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities.  The graph-compatible canonical fiber is a closed subset of a finite union of products of probability simplices and finite one-hot sets cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities.  Both are bounded, hence compact, and both are semialgebraic by these finite polynomial descriptions.  Their nonemptiness follows from the corresponding assumed nonempty compatibility fiber and the proved forward inclusion.  In each summand defining \(\Psi_C^s\), at most one coordinate occurs from each district block visited by \(C\).  Thus \(\Psi_C^s\) is multi-affine and has total degree at most the number of visited districts.

Fix either exact range equality and a target cell \(T\).  The target polynomial is continuous on a nonempty compact fiber, so it attains a minimum and a maximum.  Exact range equality shows that these are respectively the sharp lower and upper bounds over the corresponding SCM fiber, and both are realized on the original alphabets.  Since cell probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), at least one endpoint is strictly inside the logical interval exactly when the lower endpoint is positive or the upper endpoint is below one.  The cell is identified exactly when the extrema agree.  If they differ, attaining minimizers and maximizers are same-menu SCM families with unequal target values.  This proves endpoint sharpness and does not assert that intermediate scalar values are attained.

Apply the same compactness argument to the nonnegative evidence polynomial.  If its attained maximum is zero, evidence is zero throughout the fiber.  If its attained minimum is zero and its maximum is positive, a compatible minimizing model has zero evidence, so positivity is not uniform.  In either case the conditional is not defined on the whole fiber and division is rejected.  If the evidence minimum is strictly positive, the ratio
\[
x\longmapsto
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}
\]
is continuous on the compact fiber.  It therefore attains its sharp lower and upper conditional bounds, with endpoint models supplied by the exact range equality.  Distinct extrema yield distinct original-alphabet same-menu models; again no interval-filling claim is needed.

For two arms the denominator is common.  Under the same strict positivity condition,
\[
Q_\sigma^\star(\mathbf M_x)-Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_x)
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star(x^\star)
-\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}.
\]
The denominator is positive, so the signs agree.  A continuous numerator-gap polynomial positive at every point of a compact fiber has a strictly positive attained minimum, and the converse is immediate.  A negative minimum and positive maximum are respectively attained by two same-menu, original-alphabet models with opposite strict rankings; conversely any such models force the two strict endpoint inequalities.  This proves the exact pruning test.

Finally, the broad part of \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\) gives, for every \(D\),
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]
If \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), the focal part of that theorem strengthens this to
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]
Its construction replaces only the full response-block law at \(D\) and its unchanged-source copies; every \(D'\ne D\) is frozen at a graph-compatible baseline.  This is strictly a local realization statement.  The first exact global row above instead uses \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\), where every district is saturated, because simultaneous Cartesian variation of all district laws is needed for the global multi-affine fiber.  In a sparse graph or under an unrecorded cross-district menu equation, the local face can be a strict outer relaxation, so failed local membership alone gives no global countermodel.  Whenever the objective extrema of the appropriate exact global program differ, however, the already proved exact range equality supplies the required pair of original-alphabet same-menu SCMs.
