Write a vector in \(\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}\) in the coordinate order \((00,01,10,11)\) fixed by the complete binary acquisition and strictness instance definition, and write
\[
q=(q_{00},q_{01},q_{10},q_{11})^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By the compatible-face definition and the displayed matrix and value vector in that instance, \(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) if and only if \(q\geq0\) and
\[
q_{00}+q_{01}+q_{10}+q_{11}=1,
\qquad
q_{10}+q_{11}=\frac12,
\qquad
q_{01}+q_{11}=\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]
Set \(p=q_{11}\).  The last two equations in \((2)\) give
\[
q_{10}=\frac12-p,
\qquad
q_{01}=\frac12-p,
\]
and substitution in the first equation gives
\[
q_{00}=1-\left(\frac12-p\right)-\left(\frac12-p\right)-p=p.
\]
Thus every feasible vector equals
\[
q(p)=\left(p,\frac12-p,\frac12-p,p\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{3}
\]
Conversely, the vector in \((3)\) satisfies all three equations in \((2)\), and it is coordinatewise nonnegative exactly when \(0\leq p\leq1/2\).  This proves both uniqueness of the parameterization and
\[
\mathcal K_D(m_D)=\{q(p):0\leq p\leq1/2\}.
\tag{4}
\]

The checked-local-arms data in the binary-instance definition give \(d_D=(1,1,1,1)\).  Hence, for every law in \((4)\),
\[
d_D^{\mathsf T}q(p)=1>0.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, no conditional value below has a zero denominator.  Using the two numerator rows from the same definition and the common-evidence arm-row definition,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_\rho(q(p))
&=\frac{q_{01}+q_{10}}{d_D^{\mathsf T}q(p)}
=\left(\frac12-p\right)+\left(\frac12-p\right)
=1-2p,\\
Q_\sigma(q(p))
&=\frac{q_{01}+q_{11}}{d_D^{\mathsf T}q(p)}
=\left(\frac12-p\right)+p
=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The additional output-one row is \(r_D^\dagger=(0,0,0,1)\).  Therefore
\[
(r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}q(p)=q_{11}=p.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations \((4)\) and \((7)\), together with the one-cell frontier definition, imply
\[
I_D=[0,1/2].
\tag{8}
\]
For a fixed value \(p\in I_D\), the extra equality in that definition and \((7)\) leave the singleton feasible set \(\{q(p)\}\).  Since \(g_{\rho,\sigma,D}=(0,0,-1,1)\), direct evaluation gives
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}^{\mathsf T}q(p)
=-q_{10}+q_{11}
=-\left(\frac12-p\right)+p
=2p-\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently, by the definition of the strict dominance regime,
\[
\mathcal J_{\rho\prec\sigma}^+
=\{p\in[0,1/2]:2p-1/2>0\}
=(1/4,1/2].
\tag{10}
\]

For completeness, form the augmented matrix and value vector
\[
\widetilde A_D=
\begin{bmatrix}A_D\\(r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\widetilde m_D=(1,1/2,1/2,p)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
In the stipulated augmented row order, let
\[
\eta=(0,-1,0,2)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Multiplication, using the four displayed rows of \(\widetilde A_D\), yields
\[
\widetilde A_D^{\mathsf T}\eta
=0(1,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}
-(0,0,1,1)^{\mathsf T}
+0(0,1,0,1)^{\mathsf T}
+2(0,0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}
=(0,0,-1,1)^{\mathsf T}
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the dual inequality holds with equality, so \(\eta\) is a feasible menu-dual certificate.  Its certified value is
\[
\widetilde m_D^{\mathsf T}\eta
=-\frac12+2p
=2p-\frac12,
\tag{12}
\]
which agrees with the primal value in \((9)\).  At the supplied experimental value \(p=3/8\), equation \((12)\) equals
\[
2\cdot\frac38-\frac12=\frac14.
\tag{13}
\]

At the sign boundary, \((3)\) gives
\[
q(1/4)=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)^{\mathsf T},
\]
and \((6)\) gives equality of the two arm values.  At the two endpoints,
\[
Q_\rho(q(0))=1>\frac12=Q_\sigma(q(0)),
\qquad
Q_\sigma(q(1/2))=\frac12>0=Q_\rho(q(1/2)).
\tag{14}
\]
These response laws are realized on the original binary alphabets, not merely on an enlarged canonical space.  Indeed, for any \(p\in[0,1/2]\), take a finite exogenous variable \(U_Y\) with values in the four deterministic binary maps \(t\mapsto(Y_0,Y_1)\) and law \(q(p)\), and define \(Y=U_Y(T_{\mathrm{tr}})\).  Use the same law of \(U_Y\) in the target and source domains, take an independent binary exogenous block for \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\) with the same law in both domains, and take independent degenerate blocks satisfying \(Z=0\) in the target and \(Z=1\) in the source.  This is a recursive finite SCM for the graph \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\) with isolated \(Z\); its singleton district blocks are independent, \(\Delta_s=\{Z\}\) is district closed, and every unselected district law is invariant.  Its two base row probabilities are \(1/2\) by \((2)\), and its additional output-one probability is \(p\) by \((7)\).  Hence this construction supplies original-alphabet SCM realizations of the boundary and both ranking witnesses in \((14)\), while preserving the prescribed selection shift.

It remains to verify the acquisition conclusion.  With no acquired block, the regime-block acquisition definition and \((4)\) give \(\mathcal K_\varnothing=\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  The first inequality in \((14)\) witnesses that \(\rho\) is possibly optimal at \(q(0)\), and the second witnesses that \(\sigma\) is possibly optimal at \(q(1/2)\).  Since the arm set is exactly \(\{\rho,\sigma\}\),
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\{\rho,\sigma\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The sole experiment supplies its complete Boolean output law.  Its output-one equation is \((r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}q=3/8\), while its output-zero equation is the complementary equation \(1-(r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}q=5/8\) and is therefore redundant given normalization.  Equations \((3)\) and \((7)\) consequently give
\[
\mathcal K_{\{e^\dagger\}}=\{q(3/8)\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Finally, substituting \(p=3/8\) in \((6)\) yields
\[
Q_\rho(q(3/8))=\frac14
<\frac12=Q_\sigma(q(3/8)).
\tag{17}
\]
Thus \(\sigma\) is the unique possibly optimal arm after acquisition.  Because \(\mathcal U=\{e^\dagger\}\) and there are no other arms, the full-information definition, \((16)\), and \((17)\) imply
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\{e^\dagger\})
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}
=\{\sigma\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations \((3)\)--\((18)\) establish every asserted identity, certificate, realization, and pruning conclusion.
