The hybrid algorithm \(\mathsf{DCTFTR\text{-}CAN}\) first runs \(\mathsf{DCTFTR}\).  Its finite certified output grammar is
\[
\begin{split}
\mathsf{HybridResult}::={}&\operatorname{ID\_RAT}(F,\delta)
\mid\operatorname{ID\_CAN}(v,\zeta)
\mid\operatorname{PAIR\_CAN}(\vartheta_-,\vartheta_+,\zeta)\\
&\mid\operatorname{ZERO\_EVIDENCE}(\vartheta_0,\zeta_0)
\mid\operatorname{NOT\_UNIFORMLY\_DEFINED}(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_+,\zeta)
\mid\operatorname{FAIL\_CHECK}(t).
\end{split}
\]
A successful rational terminal is relabelled \(\operatorname{ID\_RAT}\).  A proved uniformly zero fast-path terminal is relabelled zero evidence.  Every unresolved local trace invokes CAD on \(\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\).  For an unconditional target, equal attained extrema return \(\operatorname{ID\_CAN}\), and unequal extrema return their two endpoint parameters.  For a conditional target, CAD first computes \(e_-\) and \(e_+\): \(e_+=0\) returns zero evidence; \(e_-=0<e_+\) returns zero- and positive-evidence witnesses; only \(e_->0\) permits optimization of the numerator/evidence ratio, with equal extrema returning \(\operatorname{ID\_CAN}\) and unequal extrema returning their endpoint pair.

Every \(\zeta\) stores the ordered input polynomials, a legal projection sequence, isolating intervals or Thom encodings for algebraic coordinates, lifted cell sample points, sign tables, the quantifier-free output, and substitutions verifying feasibility and the reported endpoint comparisons.  A verifier recomputes the projection polynomials, checks root isolation and every sign table by exact real-algebraic arithmetic, checks the quantified-to-quantifier-free propagation, and substitutes each witness.  Different accepted legal CAD choices may yield different traces or endpoint witnesses; the algorithm asserts termination and semantic validity of every accepted trace, not uniqueness of its representation.
