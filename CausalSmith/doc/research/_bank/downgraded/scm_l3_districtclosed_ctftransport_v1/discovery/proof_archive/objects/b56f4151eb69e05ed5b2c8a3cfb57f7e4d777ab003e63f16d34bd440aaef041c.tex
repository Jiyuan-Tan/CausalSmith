Assume first that \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  Choose any \(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\), for each domain \(s\) the nonfocal response profiles have the product probability law \(\nu_{-D}^{0,s}\) in \(\mathrm{def:baseline\text{-}averaged\text{-}active\text{-}slice\text{-}menu}\).  For every transport-admissible complete supplied cell and every \(\omega_{-D}\), \(\mathrm{def:active\text{-}slice\text{-}separated\text{-}menu}\) gives
\[
\chi_{C_{a,v},\omega_{-D}}\in R_D(m_D).
\]
Because \(R_D(m_D)\) is a linear subspace and the sum defining \(\bar\chi_{C_{a,v}}^{s,\mathbf M^0}\) is finite,
\[
\bar\chi_{C_{a,v}}^{s,\mathbf M^0}
=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}\nu_{-D}^{0,s}(\omega_{-D})
\chi_{C_{a,v},\omega_{-D}}
\in R_D(m_D).
\]
Thus the chosen \(\mathbf M^0\) witnesses \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  The first implication in the displayed hierarchy is \(\mathrm{lem:structural\text{-}separation\text{-}implies\text{-}active\text{-}slice}\), and composing it with the implication just proved gives the stated hierarchy.

For strictness of the second implication, take a binary DAG with two root vertices feeding a third vertex and no bidirected edges.  Every district is therefore a singleton.  Let \(D\) be the district of the first root.  In a target-domain baseline, let the second root be a fair Bernoulli variable, let the two roots be independent, and let the third vertex be the deterministic indicator that the roots are equal.  Supply only the complete marginal law of the third vertex, which is Bernoulli with success probability \(1/2\).  The observed alphabets are the original binary alphabets.

For the focal root, \(\Omega_D=\{0,1\}\).  The supplied marginal cell is cross-district, so \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}menu\text{-}system}\) retains no raw district-local row; and the marginal law of the child alone provides neither a context nor a fixing quotient exposing the root, so \(\mathrm{def:typed\text{-}extraction\text{-}judgment}\) supplies no derived focal row.  Hence \(A_D=\mathbf 1^\top\),
\[
\mathcal K_D(m_D)=\Delta(\{0,1\}),\qquad
Z_D(m_D)=\varnothing,\qquad
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)\}.
\]
Fix the baseline response profile of the third vertex at the stated equality table.  For the supplied success cell, fixing the second root at zero gives the focal slice \((1,0)\), while fixing it at one gives \((0,1)\).  Neither row belongs to \(R_D(m_D)\), so \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) fails.  The baseline nonfocal law assigns probability \(1/2\) to each of these two relevant profiles, and therefore
\[
\bar\chi^{\star,\mathbf M^0}_{\{\text{third vertex}=1\}}
=\tfrac12(1,0)+\tfrac12(0,1)
=\tfrac12(1,1)\in R_D(m_D).
\]
For the failure cell the two slices are interchanged, so its average is also \(\tfrac12(1,1)\).  These are all complete cells of the supplied law.  A singleton district is bidirected complete, and \(\mathrm{def:focal\text{-}district\text{-}saturated\text{-}model\text{-}class}\) permits every law on the focal root response block while the fair second-root law and deterministic child table remain fixed.  Consequently the displayed baseline belongs to \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\) and witnesses \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  Thus baseline-averaged separation can hold when active-slice separation fails.
