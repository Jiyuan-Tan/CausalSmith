We prove the saturated and graph-compatible assertions separately.

For \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\), let \(q_D^s\) be the law of the finite response profile in district \(D\). The district blocks are mutually independent, and recursive acyclicity makes every finite counterfactual cell \(C\) a deterministic function of their profiles. Finite total probability therefore gives
\[
P_{M^s}(C)
=\sum_{\omega\in\prod_D\Omega_D}\mathbf1\{C\text{ is true under }\omega\}
\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}q_D^s(\omega_D)
=\Psi_C^s(\theta^s).\tag{1}
\]
Every declared cell uses only the parent configurations included in the response carriers. If its indicator visits \(k\) districts, terms from unvisited districts sum to one in (1), leaving one linear factor from each of the \(k\) visited blocks. Hence \(\Psi_C^s\) is multi-affine and has total degree at most \(k\).

District-kernel invariance gives \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\) whenever
\(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), and typed-menu realizability applied to (1) gives every complete supplied-cell equation. Thus the response tuple of \(\mathbf M\) belongs to \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\).

Conversely, take \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\). Each restricted profile in \(\Omega_D\) extends to a full deterministic table in \(\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\), since all omitted output alphabets are nonempty; fix a section of this restriction map. Push \(q_D^s\) forward through that section to obtain a full response-block law. Use the same section in every domain, so an equality \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\) becomes equality of the full lifted laws. Draw the district blocks independently and let each structural equation read its stored table entry. This is an acyclic district-saturated SCM family on the original alphabets. The equations defining \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) and (1) show that it induces every supplied law, while its declared target-cell probabilities equal the corresponding \(\Psi\)-values. Therefore
\[
\{(P_{M^\star}(C):C\in\mathcal F):\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\}
=\{(\Psi_C^\star(\theta^\star):C\in\mathcal F):\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\}.\tag{2}
\]
The assumed nonempty saturated fiber maps into \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\). The latter is cut out in a finite product of simplices by finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities, so it is semialgebraic, closed, bounded, nonempty, and therefore compact.

Now let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) for an arbitrary acyclic directed mixed graph. Apply finite canonical counterfactual equivalence in each domain to all structural response tables. Because the full response-profile tuple is itself a finite joint counterfactual object, this preserves every complete district response law, as well as all supplied and target cells. Hence cross-domain equality of an unchanged district response law is preserved. The resulting source-labelled tuple lies in \(\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\), and
\[
P_{M^s}(C)=\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s).\tag{3}
\]
Conversely, a point \(\vartheta\) of that canonical fiber consists of one-hot recursive structural tables, normalized nonnegative canonical exogenous laws, the graph-compatible latent-factor restrictions, and product laws across distinct districts. These data define a recursive SCM in each domain. The response-law equalities impose district-kernel invariance, the fixed selection sets satisfy district closure, and the complete-cell equations reproduce every supplied law. Thus the tuple is in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). This proves
\[
\{(P_{M^\star}(C):C\in\mathcal F):\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\}
=\{(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star):C\in\mathcal F):\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\}.\tag{4}
\]
The assumed broad compatibility supplies nonemptiness. The canonical space is a finite union of products of compact simplices and finite one-hot table sets, while graph, invariance, and menu constraints are polynomial equalities and inequalities. Hence the canonical fiber is compact and semialgebraic. The reverse construction in both (2) and (4) retains the declared observed alphabets.

Fix either exact row \((\mathfrak C,\Lambda,\Xi)\) in the statement. A cell probability is continuous on compact \(\Lambda\), so it attains
\[
\ell_T=\min_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_T^\star(x^\star),\qquad
u_T=\max_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_T^\star(x^\star).\tag{5}
\]
Range equality makes (5) the sharp lower and upper SCM bounds and lifts both optimizers to original-alphabet compatible families. Since all cell probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), at least one endpoint is strictly internal exactly when \(\ell_T>0\) or \(u_T<1\). The cell is constant on the fiber exactly when \(\ell_T=u_T\); if they differ, the two attaining SCMs are the required same-menu paired certificate. Nothing in this argument asserts that the image between the endpoints is an interval.

Apply the same reasoning to evidence and write
\[
e_-=\min_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_E^\star(x^\star),\qquad
e_+=\max_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_E^\star(x^\star).\tag{6}
\]
Evidence probabilities are nonnegative. Thus \(e_+=0\) means zero evidence throughout the fiber, whereas \(e_-=0<e_+\) supplies both a zero-evidence and a positive-evidence model. In either case the conditional is not a functional defined on all of \(\mathfrak C\), so division is rejected. If \(e_->0\), the ratio
\[
x\longmapsto
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}\tag{7}
\]
is continuous on compact \(\Lambda\). Its attained minimum and maximum are therefore sharp conditional bounds by range equality. Equality of these endpoints is identification; inequality supplies two endpoint SCMs with unequal conditional values.

Under the same strict positivity, the common denominator yields
\[
Q_\sigma^\star-Q_\rho^\star
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star-\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star}
{\Xi_E^\star}.\tag{8}
\]
Consequently strict dominance throughout the fiber is equivalent, by compactness and attainment, to a strictly positive minimum of the numerator gap. Opposite strict rankings occur in two compatible models exactly when the attained minimum is negative and the attained maximum is positive. This proves the exact safe-pruning characterization.

Finally, the one-district projection theorem supplies
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D),\qquad
\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\Longrightarrow
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal K_D(m_D),\tag{9}
\]
together with
\[
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}\{q-q':q,q'\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\}^\perp.\tag{10}
\]
Its fixed-baseline calculation shows that a cross-district complete cell is preserved exactly when its averaged focal row annihilates all face differences, equivalently when that row is in (10). The active-slice and structural-separation lemmas give the two baseline-free sufficient checks. These local facts neither discard the cross-district polynomial equalities in the exact global fibers nor promote a local outer-face witness outside the focal projection scope. Hence a broad paired-model certificate must come from distinct optimizers of the appropriate exact global program unless the hypotheses in (9) have separately been established.
