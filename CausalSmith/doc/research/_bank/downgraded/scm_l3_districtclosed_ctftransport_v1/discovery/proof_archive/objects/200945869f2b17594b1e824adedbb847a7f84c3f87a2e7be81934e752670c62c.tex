We prove each exact range equality in both directions and then derive the optimization conclusions.

For the saturated frontier, first take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\).  For every domain \(s\) and district \(D\), push the law of the full response block \(U_D^s\in\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) forward to the profile coordinates \(\Omega_D\) visited by the menus and targets; call the resulting law \(q_D^s\).  Independence of the district blocks gives the product law in the definition of \(\Psi_C^s\).  If \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), district-kernel invariance gives \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\).  Typed-menu realizability gives, for every complete supplied cell,
\[
\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_{M^s}^{a}(v)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Thus \(\theta=(q_D^s)_{s,D}\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\), and recursive evaluation gives
\[
P_{M^\star}(C)=\Psi_C^\star(\theta^\star)
\]
for every target cell \(C\).  This proves the saturated left-to-right range inclusion simultaneously for every finite \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, take \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  For every partial profile \(\omega_D\in\Omega_D\), choose one extension to \(\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) by assigning an arbitrary fixed element of \(\mathcal X_V\) at each unvisited parent configuration.  Such choices exist because every alphabet is nonempty.  Push each \(q_D^s\) through this injection and use the resulting law as the law of \(U_D^s\).  Let the variables \((U_D^s:D\in\mathcal D(G))\) be mutually independent and let every structural equation read its component of \(U_D^s\).  The district-bidirected-clique and response-block assumptions make this a member of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\); acyclicity permits recursive evaluation; all outputs remain in \(\mathcal X\).  The invariance equations in \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) impose equality of the full completed response-block laws for unchanged districts.  The complete-cell equations impose
\[
P_{M_  heta^s}^{a}(v)=\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_a^s(v)
\]
for every \(v\), hence equality of each complete supplied law.  Therefore \(\mathbf M_\theta\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\), and its target cell probabilities equal the corresponding \(\Psi\)-values.  This proves the reverse saturated inclusion and original-alphabet realization.

For the arbitrary-ADMG frontier, take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  By recursive compatibility, each \(M^s\) is a finite-state recursive SCM on \((G,\mathcal X)\).  Apply finite canonical counterfactual equivalence in every domain.  The resulting canonical model has the same causal diagram and observed alphabets and preserves every finite joint counterfactual law.  In particular, it preserves each district response-profile law.  Hence district-kernel invariance in \(\mathbf M\) implies
\[
q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\cdot;\vartheta^s)
=q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\cdot;\vartheta^\star)
\quad\text{when }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing.
\]
Preservation of every supplied cell and typed-menu realizability give
\[
\Phi_{C_{a,v},\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Thus \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\), and target cells are preserved.  This proves one inclusion.  Conversely, a point of \(\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\) consists of graph-compatible one-hot structural tables and finite canonical exogenous probability vectors.  The one-hot constraints select one output in \(\mathcal X_V\) for every input, the simplex constraints define probability laws, graph compatibility and cross-district products give recursive compatibility and district factorization, the displayed response-law equalities give district-kernel invariance, and district closure is part of the fixed instance.  The full list of complete-cell equations gives every supplied law, not merely selected marginals.  Hence its canonical realization belongs to \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) and has target cell probabilities \(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^\star\).  This proves the second equality.

Both fibers are finite dimensional.  The saturated fiber is a closed subset of a finite product of probability simplices, cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities.  The sparse canonical fiber is a closed subset of a finite union of products of probability simplices and finite one-hot sets, again cut out by polynomial equalities and inequalities.  They are therefore compact semialgebraic sets.  Their asserted nonemptiness follows from the corresponding assumed nonempty compatibility fiber and the first inclusion.  The defining formula for \(\Psi_C^s\) is a finite sum of terms each containing one coordinate from every visited district, so it is multi-affine and has total degree at most the number of such districts.  Both \(\Psi\) and \(\Phi_{\mathrm{can}}\) are polynomial and therefore continuous.  Rational or algebraic menu probabilities give a finite exact coefficient encoding of these displayed semialgebraic systems; no effective exact claim is made for an uneffectively specified real input.

Now fix either row \((\mathfrak C,\Lambda,\Xi)\) in the statement.  Exact range equality and compactness show that
\[
\left[
\min_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_T^\star(x^\star),
\max_{x\in\Lambda}\Xi_T^\star(x^\star)
\right]
\]
is the attained sharp target-cell range on \(\mathfrak C\).  Since all cell probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), this interval is strictly smaller than the logical interval exactly when its lower endpoint is positive or its upper endpoint is below one.  It is a singleton exactly when the target is constant on \(\mathfrak C\).  If the endpoints differ, realizing a minimizer and a maximizer through the appropriate range equality gives two distinct original-alphabet SCM families in the same menu fiber.

The evidence polynomial is nonnegative and continuous.  Thus its extrema \(e_-(\Lambda)\) and \(e_+(\Lambda)\) are attained.  If \(e_+=0\), it vanishes throughout the fiber.  If \(e_-=0<e_+\), an attaining compatible model has zero evidence, so strict positivity is not uniform.  In either case the conditional is not a well-defined functional on all of \(\mathfrak C\), and division is rejected.  If \(e_->0\), the denominator is uniformly positive and
\[
Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_x)
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
       {\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}.
\]
This ratio is continuous on compact \(\Lambda\), so its minimum and maximum are attained; exact range equality makes them sharp.

For two arms the denominator is common.  Under \(e_->0\),
\[
Q_\sigma^\star(\mathbf M_x)-Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_x)
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star(x^\star)
-\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}.
\]
The denominator is positive, so the signs agree.  A continuous function that is positive at every point of a compact set has an attained strictly positive minimum, and conversely.  This proves the exact strict-dominance and safe-pruning criterion.  An attained negative minimum and positive maximum of the gap give two same-menu original-alphabet models with opposite strict rankings; any such pair forces those two strict inequalities, proving the converse.

Finally, let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  The proof of the broad part of the projection theorem stacks normalization, raw district-local cell equalities, and certified derived equalities to give
\[
q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\geq0,
\qquad A_Dq_D^\star(\mathbf M)=m_D.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  The reverse inclusion is supplied only for \(\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) under \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  Therefore sparse latent-factor restrictions or cross-district menu equations may make the row face a strict outer relaxation, and a local failure does not by itself furnish global countermodels.  Distinct optimizers of either exact global fiber do furnish such models by the two range equalities already proved.
