Fix a finite checked instance satisfying the hypotheses and fix
\(S\subseteq\mathcal U\).  Joint realizability and the definition of the
subfaces give
\[
\varnothing\ne\mathcal K_{\mathcal U}\subseteq\mathcal K_S
\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\tag{1}
\]
The checked-local-arms certificate in
\(\mathrm{def:target\text{-}arm\text{-}rows}\) states, one condition at a
time, that \(\varepsilon_D>0\) and that
\(d_D^{\mathsf T}q\geq\varepsilon_D\) for every
\(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  Hence the denominator is nonzero throughout
every subface in \((1)\).  Consequently, for every
\(q\in\mathcal K_S\) and every pair of arms \(\rho,\sigma\),
\[
Q_\rho(q)\geq Q_\sigma(q)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{n_{\rho,D}^{\mathsf T}q}{d_D^{\mathsf T}q}
\geq
\frac{n_{\sigma,D}^{\mathsf T}q}{d_D^{\mathsf T}q}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\geq0.
\tag{2}
\]
The second equivalence uses only multiplication by the common strictly
positive denominator.  Thus the causal conditional ranking is not being
defined by the numerator comparison; equation \((2)\) proves their equality
on the checked positive-evidence face.

By \(\mathrm{def:regime\text{-}block\text{-}acquisition}\), for a fixed
arm \(\rho\),
\[
\rho\in\operatorname{PO}(S)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists q\in\mathbb R^{\Omega_D}\quad
\begin{cases}
q(\omega)\geq0,&\omega\in\Omega_D,\\
A_Dq=m_D,\\
R_eq=p_e,&e\in S,\\
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\geq0,
&\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
This is a finite linear feasibility problem.  Its feasible set is contained
in the probability simplex because the normalization row
\(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\) with value one is a row of \(A_D\).  It is
therefore bounded and closed.  If it is nonempty, it has an extreme point:
indeed, successively minimize the finitely many coordinates on the current
nonempty compact face; after the last coordinate the resulting face is a
singleton, which is an extreme point of the original polytope.  At an extreme
point, the equality rows together with some active inequality rows have full
column rank.  Otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to all active rows would
permit sufficiently small movements in both signs while preserving the
equalities and all inequalities, contradicting extremality.  Hence feasibility
of \((3)\) is equivalent to feasibility of at least one of the finitely many
systems obtained by declaring a full-rank subset of its inequalities active.
For each such subset, Gaussian elimination and substitution produce its unique
candidate, and exact sign tests check all remaining inequalities.  Effective
rational and real-algebraic arithmetic supplies exact equality, rank, and sign
tests, so this procedure decides \((3)\) without a limiting or numerical
tolerance argument.

There are finitely many pairs \((S,\rho)\).  Applying the preceding decision
procedure to each pair computes every \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\), including
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\operatorname{PO}(\mathcal U)\).  Retain
exactly those subsets for which
\(\operatorname{PO}(S)=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}\), sum their positive
effectively represented costs, and compare the finitely many sums exactly.
This returns all, including all ties, of the minimum-cost solutions claimed in
the first sentence of the theorem.

It remains to prove that no general submodularity claim can strengthen this
finite characterization.  Consider the one-domain binary bow graph on
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\) and \(Y\), with
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\) and
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\leftrightarrow Y\).  It is acyclic and has the single
district \(D=\{T_{\mathrm{tr}},Y\}\).  Both endogenous state spaces are
\(\{0,1\}\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\), a deterministic
district response profile is
\[
\omega=(t^{\mathrm{nat}},Y_0,Y_1)\in\{0,1\}^3,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(t^{\mathrm{nat}}\) is the response of the parentless treatment and
\(Y_t\) is the response of \(Y\) when \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\) is set to
\(t\).  Supply in the base menu the complete observational law
\[
P(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=0)=1,
\qquad P(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=1)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The two raw rows from \((5)\), nonnegativity, and normalization force zero
mass on every profile with \(t^{\mathrm{nat}}=1\), and impose no restriction
on the remaining four profiles.  Write these active profiles, in order, as
\[
\omega_1=(0,0,0),\quad
\omega_2=(0,0,1),\quad
\omega_3=(0,1,0),\quad
\omega_4=(0,1,1).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the base row face is
\(\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_1},\delta_{\omega_2},
\delta_{\omega_3},\delta_{\omega_4}\}\).

This face is realized on the stated binary alphabets.  For any law \(q\) on
the four profiles in \((6)\), take one finite district latent block
\(U_D\sim q\), set \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=t^{\mathrm{nat}}(U_D)\), and set
\(Y=Y_{T_{\mathrm{tr}}}(U_D)\); under intervention
\(T_{\mathrm{tr}}=t\), the latter equation returns \(Y_t(U_D)\).
The common latent block is compatible with the bidirected edge, and the last
equation is compatible with the directed edge.  There is only one domain, so
there is no cross-domain invariance condition to check.  Equivalently, every
complete supplied cell below is local to the only district and is stacked, so
the structural-separation hypothesis holds for each augmented menu; the
explicit construction gives nonemptiness, and
\(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\)
certifies that each displayed row face is the exact focal-saturated SCM
projection, including its boundary points.

Let the common evidence event be \(E=\top\), and declare two realizable
counterfactual arms whose favorable reward events are
\[
B_\rho=\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(1,1)\},
\qquad
B_\sigma=\{(Y_0,Y_1)=(1,1)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Because there is only one district, the exhaustive locality checks accept.
On the active profiles their numerator and evidence rows are
\[
n_{\rho,D}=(1,1,1,0),\qquad
n_{\sigma,D}=(0,0,0,1),\qquad
d_D=(1,1,1,1).
\tag{8}
\]
The identity \(d_D^{\mathsf T}q=1\) on the base face proves the checked
certificate with \(\varepsilon_D=1\); in particular, no zero-denominator
case is admitted.

Let \(\mathcal U=\{e_1,e_2\}\), with \(c_{e_1}=c_{e_2}=1\).  Experiment
\(e_i\) declares the Layer-3 tuple \((Y_0,Y_1)\), applies the Boolean
postprocessor
\[
F_1=\mathbf 1\{Y_0=Y_1\},
\qquad
F_2=\mathbf 1\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(0,0)\},
\tag{9}
\]
and reveals its complete output law
\(P(F_i=1)=1\), \(P(F_i=0)=0\).  These are genuine counterfactual
output laws: only the Boolean pushforwards in \((9)\), not the joint law of
\((Y_0,Y_1)\), are supplied.  The output-one rows on \((6)\) are
\[
r_1=(1,0,0,1),
\qquad
r_2=(0,1,1,1),
\tag{10}
\]
and the output-zero rows are their complements.  Since normalization is
already imposed, a complement row adds exactly the complementary equation and
no further restriction.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_{\varnothing}
 &=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_1},\delta_{\omega_2},
     \delta_{\omega_3},\delta_{\omega_4}\},\\
\mathcal K_{\{e_1\}}
 &=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_1},\delta_{\omega_4}\},\\
\mathcal K_{\{e_2\}}
 &=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_2},\delta_{\omega_3},
     \delta_{\omega_4}\},\\
\mathcal K_{\{e_1,e_2\}}
 &=\{\delta_{\omega_4}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The last point mass realizes the complete laws of both experiments, so the
whole experiment menu is jointly realizable.  Equations \((8)\) and \((11)\)
show that each of \(\delta_{\omega_1},\delta_{\omega_2},
\delta_{\omega_3}\) makes \(\rho\) uniquely optimal, whereas
\(\delta_{\omega_4}\) makes \(\sigma\) uniquely optimal.  Since there are
exactly two arms,
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=
\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1\})=
\operatorname{PO}(\{e_2\})=\{\rho,\sigma\},
\qquad
\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1,e_2\})=
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\{\sigma\}.
\tag{12}
\]
It follows from the definition of \(f\) that
\[
f(\{e_2\})-f(\varnothing)=0
<1=f(\{e_1,e_2\})-f(\{e_1\}).
\tag{13}
\]
Taking \(S=\varnothing\),
\(T_{\mathrm{exp}}=\{e_1\}\), and \(e=e_2\), equation \((13)\)
contradicts the diminishing-returns inequality required for submodularity.

Finally, \((12)\) shows that a selected set preserves the full-information
possibly-optimal set if and only if it contains both experiments.  With
\(x_i=\mathbf 1\{e_i\in S\}\), the exact acquisition problem is therefore
\[
\min_{x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}}x_1+x_2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad x_1+x_2\geq2.
\tag{14}
\]
Its only feasible binary point is \((1,1)\), of cost two.  In the continuous
relaxation \(0\leq x_i\leq1\), the nonnegative multiplier \(y=1\) on the
covering inequality gives the lower bound
\(x_1+x_2\geq2y=2\), and \((1,1)\) attains it.  This proves the claimed
matching certificate and completes the construction.
