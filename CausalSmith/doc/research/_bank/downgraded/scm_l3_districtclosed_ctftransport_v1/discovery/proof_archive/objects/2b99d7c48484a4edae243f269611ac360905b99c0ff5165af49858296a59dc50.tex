For a response-profile tuple \(\theta=(q_D^s:s\in\mathcal S,\ D\in\mathcal D(G))\) with \(q_D^s\in\Delta(\Omega_D)\), define, for every finite counterfactual cell \(C\),
\[
\Psi_C^s(\theta^s)=
\sum_{\omega\in\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}\Omega_D}
\mathbf 1\{C\text{ is true under recursive evaluation of }\omega\}
\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}q_D^s(\omega_D).
\]
If the recursive indicator of \(C\) depends on the profiles in \(k\) districts, then \(\Psi_C^s\) is multi-affine in the district probability blocks and has total degree at most \(k\).  Writing \(C_{a,v}\) for the complete cell in which descriptor \(a\) returns \(v\in\mathcal X_{W_a}\), define the saturated response-profile fiber
\[
\begin{split}
\Theta(\mathcal P)=\bigl\{\theta:{}&q_D^s\in\Delta(\Omega_D)
\quad(s\in\mathcal S,\ D\in\mathcal D(G)),\\
&q_D^s=q_D^\star\quad\text{if }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing,\\
&\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_a^s(v)
\quad\text{for every }s\in\mathcal S,\ a\in\mathcal A_s,
\ v\in\mathcal X_{W_a}\bigr\}.
\end{split}
\]
For a target cell \(T\), set
\[
L_T(\mathcal P)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)}\Psi_T^\star(\theta^\star),
\qquad
U_T(\mathcal P)=\max_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)}\Psi_T^\star(\theta^\star),
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal G_{\rho,\sigma}(\theta)
=\Psi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star(\theta^\star)
-\Psi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(\theta^\star).
\]

For an arbitrary, possibly nonsaturated ADMG \(G\), retain a separate graph-compatible canonical parameter \(\vartheta^s=(\mu^s,p^s)\in\operatorname{Can}(G,\mathcal X)\).  Its simplex and one-hot coordinates satisfy
\[
p_U^s(u)\geq0,\qquad \sum_up_U^s(u)=1,
\qquad \mu_{V,v\mid pa,u_V}^s\in\{0,1\},
\qquad \sum_{v\in\mathcal X_V}\mu_{V,v\mid pa,u_V}^s=1,
\]
with the graph-compatible latent-factor restrictions and product law across distinct districts retained.  Let \(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s)\) be the finite recursive canonical cell polynomial and let \(q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\cdot;\vartheta^s)\) be its induced joint response-profile law on \(D\).  Define
\[
\begin{split}
\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)=\bigl\{\vartheta:{}&
\vartheta^s\in\operatorname{Can}(G,\mathcal X)\quad(s\in\mathcal S),\\
&q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\cdot;\vartheta^s)=q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\cdot;\vartheta^\star)
\quad\text{if }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing,\\
&\Phi_{C_{a,v},\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s)=P_a^s(v)
\quad\text{for every complete supplied cell}\bigr\}.
\end{split}
\]
The first fiber deliberately uses district-saturated response-block semantics; the second retains all internal latent-factor restrictions of a sparse \(G\).
