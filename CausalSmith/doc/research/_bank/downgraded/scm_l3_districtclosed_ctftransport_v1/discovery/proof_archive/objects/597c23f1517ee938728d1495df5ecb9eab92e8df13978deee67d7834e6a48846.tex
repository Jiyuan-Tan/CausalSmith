We prove the two exact finite range equalities separately and then derive their optimization consequences.

First assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\).  For every domain \(s\) and district \(D\), push the law of the full response block \(U_D^s\in\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) forward to the profile coordinates \(\Omega_D\) visited by the declared menus and target cells; call the resulting law \(q_D^s\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\), the joint law of the district blocks is their product.  Thus finite recursive evaluation and total probability give, for every declared counterfactual cell \(C\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{M^s}(C)
&=\sum_{\omega\in\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}\Omega_D}
\mathbf 1\{C\text{ is true under recursive evaluation of }\omega\}
\prod_{D\in\mathcal D(G)}q_D^s(\omega_D)\\
&=\Psi_C^s(\theta^s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
If \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) gives \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\).  For each complete supplied cell, \(\mathrm{ass:typed\text{-}menu\text{-}realizability}\) and (5) give
\[
\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_{M^s}^{a}(v)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Hence \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\), proving the left-to-right saturated range inclusion simultaneously for every finite \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, take \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  Nonemptiness of every alphabet permits an injective deterministic completion \(\iota_D:\Omega_D\to\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\) at the finitely many unvisited parent configurations.  Push each \(q_D^s\) forward through \(\iota_D\), take the district blocks independently, and let each structural equation read its component response map.  Acyclicity of \(G\) gives unique recursive evaluation, and every output lies in \(\mathcal X\).  The defining clauses of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\) admit each independent collection of full district-response laws because every district is bidirected complete and every law on its full response block is permitted.  Using the same \(\iota_D\) in every domain turns each equality \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\) for an unchanged district into equality of its completed full-block laws.  The complete-cell equations in \(\Theta(\mathcal P)\) imply
\[
P_{M_\theta^s}^{a}(v)
=\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)
=P_a^s(v)
\]
for all \(s,a,v\).  Therefore \(\mathbf M_\theta\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\), uses the original alphabets, and realizes every corresponding \(\Psi\)-value.  This proves the reverse saturated range inclusion.

For the arbitrary-ADMG row, take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:recursive\text{-}compatibility}\), each \(M^s\) is a finite recursive SCM on \((G,\mathcal X)\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}canonical\text{-}counterfactual\text{-}equivalence}\) in each domain to the finite joint collection containing all response-map counterfactuals visited by the supplied cells and the target cells in \(\mathcal F\).  The resulting graph-compatible canonical model has the same observed alphabets and the same joint counterfactual law.  In particular it preserves each district response-profile law, so \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) gives
\[
q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\mathord\cdot;\vartheta^s)
=q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\mathord\cdot;\vartheta^\star)
\quad\text{whenever }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing.
\]
Preservation of every complete supplied cell gives
\[
\Phi_{C_{a,v},\mathrm{can}}^s(\vartheta^s)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Thus \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\), and preservation of all cells in \(\mathcal F\) proves one canonical range inclusion.

Conversely, a point \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\) consists of graph-compatible one-hot structural tables and finite canonical exogenous probability vectors.  The one-hot constraints select one value in \(\mathcal X_V\) for every structural input; the simplex constraints define probability laws; and the graph-compatible latent restrictions and the product law across districts give recursive compatibility and district factorization.  The response-law equalities give district-kernel invariance, while district closure is a fixed condition of the model-class instance.  The equation for every complete supplied cell gives the entire corresponding supplied law.  Hence the canonical realization lies in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), uses the original alphabets, and has target-cell probabilities \(\Phi_{C,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\vartheta^\star)\).  This proves the reverse canonical inclusion.

Both parameter fibers are finite-dimensional.  The saturated fiber is a closed subset of a finite product of probability simplices cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities.  The graph-compatible canonical fiber is a closed subset of a finite union of products of probability simplices and finite one-hot sets cut out by finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities.  Both are bounded, hence compact, and are semialgebraic by their displayed finite polynomial descriptions.  Their nonemptiness follows from the assumed nonempty SCM fiber and the corresponding forward inclusion.  In each summand in (5), at most one coordinate occurs from each district block visited by \(C\).  Therefore \(\Psi_C^s\) is multi-affine and has total degree at most the number of visited districts.

Fix either exact range equality and a target cell \(T\).  The target polynomial is continuous on a nonempty compact fiber, so it attains a minimum and maximum.  The range equality makes these respectively the sharp lower and upper bounds over the corresponding SCM fiber, with both endpoints realized on the original alphabets.  Cell probabilities lie in \([0,1]\); hence at least one endpoint is strictly inside the logical interval exactly when the lower endpoint is positive or the upper endpoint is less than one.  The cell is identified exactly when the extrema agree.  If they differ, attaining minimizers and maximizers yield same-menu original-alphabet SCM families with unequal target values.  This proves endpoint sharpness without asserting that intermediate values are attained.

Apply the same compactness argument to the nonnegative evidence polynomial.  If its attained maximum is zero, evidence is zero throughout the fiber.  If its attained minimum is zero and its maximum is positive, a compatible minimizing model has zero evidence, so positivity is not uniform.  In both cases a conditional functional on the entire fiber is rejected before division.  If the evidence minimum is strictly positive, the ratio
\[
x\longmapsto
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}
\]
is continuous on the compact fiber.  It attains its sharp lower and upper conditional bounds, and the exact range equality supplies endpoint SCMs.  Distinct extrema give distinct same-menu original-alphabet models; no interval-filling property is used.

For two arms, the denominator is common.  Under the same strict positivity condition,
\[
Q_\sigma^\star(\mathbf M_x)-Q_\rho^\star(\mathbf M_x)
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star(x^\star)
-\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}
{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}.
\]
The denominator is positive, so the two sides have the sign of the numerator gap.  A continuous numerator-gap polynomial that is positive everywhere on a compact fiber has a strictly positive attained minimum, and the converse is immediate.  A negative minimum and a positive maximum are attained by two same-menu original-alphabet models with opposite strict rankings; conversely, any such pair forces the two strict endpoint inequalities.  This proves the exact pruning test.

Finally, the broad part of \(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\) gives
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D)
\]
for every \(D\).  Under \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\), its focal part strengthens this to
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]
The proof freezes every nonfocal response law.  For a transport-admissible complete supplied cell \(C\), changing the focal law from \(q_0\) to \(q\) changes its probability by
\[
\sum_{\omega_{-D}}\nu^s(\omega_{-D})
\chi_{C,\omega_{-D}}(q-q_0),
\]
and each summand vanishes because \(\chi_{C,\omega_{-D}}\in R_D(m_D)\) while \(q-q_0\) annihilates \(R_D(m_D)\).  Thus a cross-district indicator is compatible with exact local projection whenever its active slices satisfy this condition; cross-district coupling alone is not an obstruction.  The strictness part of \(\mathrm{lem:structural\text{-}separation\text{-}implies\text{-}active\text{-}slice}\) shows why \(\mathsf{Sep}_D\) is sufficient but not necessary.

This focal statement changes only the full response-block law at \(D\) and its unchanged-source copies, leaving every \(D'\ne D\) at a graph-compatible baseline.  The first exact global row instead uses \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\), in which every district is saturated, because its full multi-affine fiber permits simultaneous Cartesian variation of all district laws.  Outside the focal projection theorem's hypotheses, the local face is certified only as an outer relaxation, so failed local membership is not a global nonidentification certificate.  Whenever the extrema of the appropriate exact global program differ, the proved exact range equality supplies the required pair of original-alphabet same-menu SCMs.
