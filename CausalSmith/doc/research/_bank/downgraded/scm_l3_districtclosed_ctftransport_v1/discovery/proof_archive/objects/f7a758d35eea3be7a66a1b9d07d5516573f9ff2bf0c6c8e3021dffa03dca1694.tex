Fix \(s\in\mathcal S\) and \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\) with
\[
0<\lvert D\cap\Delta_s\rvert<\lvert D\rvert,
\qquad I:=D\setminus\Delta_s.
\]
We consider the model class obtained by deleting district closure and retaining the three frozen model conditions: recursive finite-state compatibility, district factorization, and district-kernel invariance.  The last condition has the form
\[
D'\cap\Delta_t=\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q_{D'}^t=q_{D'}^\star.
\]
For \((t,D')=(s,D)\), its antecedent is false because \(D\cap\Delta_s\ne\varnothing\).  Thus district-kernel invariance imposes no equality between \(q_D^s\) and \(q_D^\star\).  Recursive compatibility is a within-domain condition, and district factorization imposes independence between distinct districts within a domain; neither condition adds a cross-domain equality for this partially selected district.

Let \(r\in\{0,1\}^{\Omega_D}\) be nonconstant.  Finiteness of \(\Omega_D\), from the canonical district response-profile definition, gives profiles \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) such that, after relabeling,
\[
r(\omega_0)=0,
\qquad
r(\omega_1)=1.
\]
We now construct the promised two-model certificate.  Each \(\omega_j\) is a tuple of deterministic structural response maps at every parent configuration used by the menus and the target.  Because every declared alphabet \(\mathcal X_V\) is nonempty, extend each map arbitrarily to every unvisited parent configuration.  The resulting maps are structural equations on the original alphabets \(\mathcal X\); acyclicity of \(G\) makes their recursive evaluation well defined.

Use the same source-domain SCM \(M^s\) in both families and give its \(D\)-block the degenerate response law
\[
q_D^s=\delta_{\omega_0}.
\]
In target family \(\mathbf M_0\), set \(q_D^\star=\delta_{\omega_0}\), whereas in target family \(\mathbf M_1\), set \(q_D^\star=\delta_{\omega_1}\).  In every district \(D'\ne D\), choose any constant structural response profile, possible because the alphabets are nonempty, and use its point mass in both domains and both families.  Take the exogenous response blocks of distinct districts to be independent.  Hence district factorization holds.  The construction uses only finite deterministic maps and finite point-mass exogenous laws, so recursive finite-state compatibility holds.  On each wholly unselected district the response laws are identical across domains, as required by district-kernel invariance; on \(D\) the invariance antecedent is false.  Point masses lie on the boundary of the permitted finite probability simplices, so this construction uses no unstated positivity or overlap condition.

The whole source SCM \(M^s\) is identical in \(\mathbf M_0\) and \(\mathbf M_1\).  Consequently recursive substitution under every supplied observational, interventional, or declared partial-copy regime in that source gives exactly the same supplied source law in the two families.  In particular, by the incidence-row and district response-law definitions, the supplied source-row value is
\[
r q_D^s
=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}r(\omega)\delta_{\omega_0}(\omega)
=r(\omega_0)
=0
\]
in both families.  Their target values differ:
\[
r q_{D,0}^\star=r(\omega_0)=0,
\qquad
r q_{D,1}^\star=r(\omega_1)=1.
\]
This is an original-alphabet, two-domain counterexample for the source row alone.  Such a two-domain instance is sufficient to refute any graph-only replacement claimed to transport the row universally after district closure is removed.

Conversely, if \(r\) is constant, say \(r(\omega)=k\in\{0,1\}\) for every \(\omega\in\Omega_D\), then every probability law \(q\in\Delta(\Omega_D)\) satisfies
\[
r q
=k\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}q(\omega)
=k.
\]
Thus a constant row has the same source and target value under every compatible pair.  Together with the preceding certificate, this proves that the universally transportable rows from a source row alone are exactly the constant rows.

It remains to prove the stated characterization under the additional marginal response-law invariance premise.  Let
\[
\pi_I:\Omega_D\longrightarrow\Omega_I:=\pi_I(\Omega_D)
\]
be coordinate restriction to the response maps of vertices in \(I\), and assume explicitly that
\[
(\pi_I)_\#q_D^s=(\pi_I)_\#q_D^\star.
\tag{MI}
\]
Suppose first that \(r\) is constant on every fiber of \(\pi_I\).  Then the formula
\[
\widetilde r(u):=r(\omega)\quad\text{for any }\omega\text{ with }\pi_I(\omega)=u
\]
is well defined on \(\Omega_I\), and \(r=\widetilde r\circ\pi_I\).  For \(t\in\{s,\star\}\), finite total probability gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
r q_D^t
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_D}\widetilde r(\pi_I(\omega))q_D^t(\omega)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\Omega_I}\widetilde r(u)
   \sum_{\omega:\,\pi_I(\omega)=u}q_D^t(\omega)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\Omega_I}\widetilde r(u)
   \bigl((\pi_I)_\#q_D^t\bigr)(u).
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying \((\mathrm{MI})\) to the last expression yields \(r q_D^s=r q_D^\star\).  This proves sufficiency without division or a support-positivity premise.

For necessity, suppose \(r\) is not constant on a fiber.  Then there are \(\omega_0,\omega_1\in\Omega_D\) with
\[
\pi_I(\omega_0)=\pi_I(\omega_1)=:u,
\qquad
r(\omega_0)\ne r(\omega_1).
\]
Use the same original-alphabet construction as above.  In both families set the source law to \(q_D^s=\delta_{\omega_0}\); set the target laws to \(q_{D,0}^\star=\delta_{\omega_0}\) and \(q_{D,1}^\star=\delta_{\omega_1}\).  At unvisited configurations, choose the extensions of the response maps on \(I\) identically; this is possible because the two restricted profiles agree.  In each family the added marginal premise holds, since
\[
(\pi_I)_\#q_D^s
=\delta_u
=(\pi_I)_\#q_{D,j}^\star,
\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
but the target row values are \(r(\omega_0)\) and \(r(\omega_1)\), respectively.  Hence no row that varies within a projection fiber transports for every compatible pair.  Finally, \(\Omega_D\) is finite, so one decides the condition by enumerating all pairs \((\omega,\omega')\) with \(\pi_I(\omega)=\pi_I(\omega')\) and checking whether \(r(\omega)=r(\omega')\).  Any failed comparison is exactly the displayed two-point leakage certificate, which is the finite test prescribed by the partial-district leakage handle.
