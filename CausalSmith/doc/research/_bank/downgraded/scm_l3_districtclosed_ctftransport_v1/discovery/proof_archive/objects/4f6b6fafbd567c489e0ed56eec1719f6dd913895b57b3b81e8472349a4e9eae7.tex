Let \(q_D^\star=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\) for an arbitrary \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\). District-cell linearity gives the district-local target as \(b_Dq_D^\star\), and the broad projection gives
\[
A_Dq_D^\star=m_D,\qquad
q_D^\star(\omega)=0\quad(\omega\in Z_D(m_D)).\tag{1}
\]
If
\[
b_D=\lambda^\top A_D+\sum_{\omega\in Z_D(m_D)}\zeta_\omega e_\omega,\tag{2}
\]
then multiplication of (2) by \(q_D^\star\) and use of (1) yield
\[
b_Dq_D^\star
=\lambda^\top A_Dq_D^\star+\sum_{\omega\in Z_D(m_D)}\zeta_\omega q_D^\star(\omega)
=\lambda^\top m_D.\tag{3}
\]
The right side is a function only of the supplied menu, so this proves broad-fiber identification and gives the stated explicit functional.

Suppose next that \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\) holds. The one-district projection theorem gives
\[
\{q_D^\star(\mathbf M):\mathbf M\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\}
=K:=\mathcal K_D(m_D),\qquad
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{span}(K-K)^\perp.\tag{4}
\]
The functional \(q\mapsto b_Dq\) is constant on \(K\) if and only if
\(b_D(q-q')=0\) for all \(q,q'\in K\), which by (4) is equivalent to
\(b_D\in R_D(m_D)\). Thus active-row membership is necessary as well as sufficient on the focal-district-saturated fiber.

If membership fails, the second equality in (4) supplies \(q,q'\in K\) such that
\[
b_Dq\ne b_Dq'.\tag{5}
\]
The first equality in (4) lifts these laws to two members of
\(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\). They use the declared alphabets \(\mathcal X\), agree on every supplied law, and differ on the target by (5); the construction in the projection theorem keeps every nonfocal district fixed and graph-compatible. The two stronger sufficient checks follow from the two separation implications. If the baseline-averaged projection premise is absent, only (1)--(3) remain valid, and a point of the local row face need not lift to the broad SCM fiber; hence no broader nonidentification conclusion is drawn.
