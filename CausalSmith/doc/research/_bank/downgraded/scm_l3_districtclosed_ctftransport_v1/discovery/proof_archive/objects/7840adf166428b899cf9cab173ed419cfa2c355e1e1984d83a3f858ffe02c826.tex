Fix a checked, jointly realizable instance and \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\).  Because the checked record proves \(d_D^{\mathsf T}q\geq\varepsilon_D>0\) on the base face, it proves the same inequality on every subface \(\mathcal K_S\).  The common denominator may therefore be cancelled without changing arm order.  For a fixed arm \(\rho\), the definition gives
\[
\rho\in\operatorname{PO}(S)
\Longleftrightarrow
\exists q\ \left\{
\begin{array}{l}
q\geq0,\quad A_Dq=m_D,\\
R_eq=p_e\quad(e\in S),\\
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\geq0
\quad(\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}).
\end{array}\right.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a finite linear feasibility problem contained in the simplex because the normalization row is part of \(A_D\).  If its feasible polytope is nonempty, start at any feasible point; whenever it is not extreme, move in a nonzero feasible affine direction until an additional inequality becomes active.  Boundedness makes both movement endpoints finite, and after at most \(|\Omega_D|\) repetitions the active equalities have full affine rank, giving a vertex.  Consequently feasibility is equivalent to feasibility of one of the finitely many systems obtained by selecting a full-rank active set.  Gaussian elimination and sign comparison over the effectively presented real-algebraic field decide each such system exactly.  Enumerating these active sets and then the finite power set of \(\mathcal U\) computes every \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\), and exact comparison of the finitely many algebraic costs computes all minimum-cost feasible sets.

For the obstruction, take the one-domain binary graph \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\), use the complete one-district response carrier
\[
\omega_1=(0,0),\quad \omega_2=(0,1),\quad
\omega_3=(1,0),\quad \omega_4=(1,1)
\tag{2}
\]
for \((Y_0,Y_1)\), and give the focal block an arbitrary law on these four profiles.  Let \(E=\top\), let
\[
B_\rho=\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(1,1)\},
\qquad
B_\sigma=\{(Y_0,Y_1)=(1,1)\}.
\]
Both indicators are local, \(d_D=(1,1,1,1)\), and \(\varepsilon_D=1\), so they form a total checked-local-arms record.  The two experiments return only the Boolean outputs
\[
f_1(Y_0,Y_1)=\mathbf 1\{Y_0=Y_1\},
\qquad
f_2(Y_0,Y_1)=\mathbf 1\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(0,0)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Their complete supplied output laws put probability one on output one and probability zero on output zero.  By typed-menu realizability, these are directly the laws of the outputs in \((3)\); no tuple law is supplied.  The deterministic preimage identity shows that their output-one events are
\[
C_1=\{Y_0=Y_1\},
\qquad
C_2=\{(Y_0,Y_1)\ne(0,0)\},
\]
and their output-zero events are exactly the complements.  Evaluating all four event indicators on \((2)\) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
&n_\rho&n_\sigma&r_1&r_2\\\hline
\omega_1&1&0&1&0\\
\omega_2&1&0&0&1\\
\omega_3&1&0&0&1\\
\omega_4&0&1&1&1
\end{array}.
\tag{4}
\]
The output-zero rows are \(\mathbf 1-r_1\) and \(\mathbf 1-r_2\), so complete-law normalization adds no further restriction.  The joint menu is realized by \(q=\delta_{\omega_4}\), proving the required joint realizability.  Directly from \((4)\),
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal K_{\{e_1\}}&=
\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_1},\delta_{\omega_4}\},\\
\mathcal K_{\{e_2\}}&=
\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_{\omega_2},\delta_{\omega_3},
\delta_{\omega_4}\},\\
\mathcal K_{\{e_1,e_2\}}&=\{\delta_{\omega_4}\}.
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
The first three point masses make \(\rho\) uniquely optimal and the fourth makes \(\sigma\) uniquely optimal.  Thus the empty and singleton experiment sets leave both arms possibly optimal, whereas the pair leaves only \(\sigma\).  Hence
\[
f(\{e_2\})-f(\varnothing)=0
<1=f(\{e_1,e_2\})-f(\{e_1\}),
\]
which violates diminishing returns.  Each experiment is necessary, so the integer program is exactly the displayed two-element covering problem.  Its unique feasible optimum is \((1,1)\).  For the continuous relaxation, multiplying \(x_1+x_2\geq2\) by \(y=1\) gives objective at least two, matched at \((1,1)\).  This proves both the computation and the explicit obstruction.
