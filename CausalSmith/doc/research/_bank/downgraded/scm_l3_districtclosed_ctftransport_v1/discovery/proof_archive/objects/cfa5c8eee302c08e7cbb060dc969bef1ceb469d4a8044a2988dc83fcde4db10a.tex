Fix \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\).  For the broad inclusion, let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) and put \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\).  This is a probability law, so \(q\geq0\) and the normalization row gives \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\).  Each retained raw row has a checked proof that its complete cell is district-local and that either \(s=\star\) or \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\).  Typed-menu realizability, recursive finite evaluation, and district-kernel invariance give
\[
P_a^s(v)=r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q_D^s
=r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q.
\]
For every retained derived row, district-cell linearity gives the analogous equality
\[
K_{s,a,D}(c\mid h)=r_{s,a,D,h,c}q.
\]
Stacking these equalities yields \(A_Dq=m_D\).  Therefore \(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\), proving
\(
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D)
\).

Now assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  The preceding inclusion gives
\(
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D)
\)
because \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  For the reverse inclusion, choose a baseline \(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) and an arbitrary \(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  In the response-block representation of \(\mathbf M^0\), leave every district law except that of \(D\) unchanged.  In the target domain replace the law of \(U_D^\star\), restricted to the finitely visited profiles \(\Omega_D\), by \(q\).  Complete each profile deterministically at parent configurations not visited by the menus or targets; nonemptiness of each alphabet supplies such a completion.  Use the same completed law for every source with \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), and retain the baseline source law when \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\).  District closure makes these the only two source cases.  Call the resulting family \(\mathbf M^q\).

The structural equations still take values in the declared alphabets, the graph remains acyclic, and the response-block variables remain independent across districts.  Bidirected completeness and the district-response-block assumption make the replaced law graph-compatible within the saturated subclass.  The construction enforces unchanged-district response-law equality, so \(\mathbf M^q\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sat}}\).

It remains to verify every complete supplied law.  Consider a complete supplied cell in a domain for which \(D\) is transport-admissible.  By \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), its recursive indicator is either independent of \(\omega_D\) or depends only on \(\omega_D\).  In the first case its probability is unchanged because all other district laws equal their baseline laws.  In the second case its raw incidence row occurs in \(A_D\), and hence its probability under \(\mathbf M^q\) is
\[
r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q=P_a^s(v)
\]
because \(A_Dq=m_D\).  If \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), the entire source response-block law was left at its baseline value, so all supplied cells in that source are unchanged.  Thus every cell of every complete supplied law equals its specified value, and \(\mathbf M^q\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\).  Its target district law is \(q\), proving
\[
\mathcal K_D(m_D)\subseteq\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P).
\]
The construction did not require strict positivity of any coordinate of \(q\), so it realizes boundary points, including forced-zero faces, on the original alphabets.
