Let \(\mathbb D\) be the effective ordered coefficient ring generated by the finitely many supplied rational or real-algebraic output-cell probabilities, with exact arithmetic and sign comparison supplied by their stipulated effective presentations.  Embed \(\mathbb D\) into \(\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}\), the real closed field of all real algebraic numbers.  The carrier \(\mathsf{QEFormula}\) is the finite syntax of quantifier-free Boolean combinations of polynomial sign tests with coefficients in \(\mathbb D\), interpreted over \(\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}\) in the displayed free variables.

The hybrid terminal grammar is exactly
\[
\begin{split}
\mathsf{HybridResult}::={}&
\operatorname{ID\_RAT}(F,\delta)
\mid\operatorname{UNCOND\_QE}(\chi_T,\chi_{\min},\chi_{\max},\mathsf{isSingleton})\\
&\mid\operatorname{ZERO\_EVIDENCE\_QE}
\mid\operatorname{NOT\_UNIFORMLY\_DEFINED\_QE}\\
&\mid\operatorname{COND\_QE}(\chi_Q,\chi_{\min},\chi_{\max},\mathsf{isSingleton}),
\end{split}
\tag{1}
\]
where each displayed \(\chi\) is a \(\mathsf{QEFormula}\) and \(\mathsf{isSingleton}\) is the exact Boolean output of the associated closed first-order sentence.

The deterministic procedure \(\mathsf{DCTFTR\text{-}CAN}\) first runs \(\mathsf{DCTFTR}\).  A successful rational identification is returned as \(\operatorname{ID\_RAT}\).  Every other terminal invokes the graph-compatible canonical fiber
\[
S=\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P),
\]
viewing all its constraints and every query polynomial over \(\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}\) with coefficients in \(\mathbb D\).  For an unconditional target polynomial \(p_T=\Phi_{T,\mathrm{can}}^\star\), quantifier elimination is applied to
\[
\varphi_T(t):=\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge t-p_T(x)=0]
\tag{2}
\]
to obtain the equivalent range formula \(\chi_T(t)\).  It decides singleton status by eliminating the closed sentence
\[
\exists t_1,t_2\,[\chi_T(t_1)\wedge\chi_T(t_2)\wedge t_1\ne t_2]
\tag{3}
\]
and obtains \(\chi_{\min}(\ell)\) and \(\chi_{\max}(u)\) by eliminating, respectively,
\[
\chi_T(\ell)\wedge\forall t\,[\chi_T(t)\Longrightarrow\ell\leq t],
\qquad
\chi_T(u)\wedge\forall t\,[\chi_T(t)\Longrightarrow t\leq u].
\tag{4}
\]

For a conditional target put \(e=\Phi_{E,\mathrm{can}}^\star\) and \(n=\Phi_{B_\rho\cap E,\mathrm{can}}^\star\).  Define the three closed first-order sentences
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathsf Z:&\neg\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)>0],\\
\mathsf N:&\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)=0]
\ \wedge\ \exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)>0],\\
\mathsf P:&\neg\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)=0].
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Three finite quantifier-elimination calls produce closed quantifier-free formulas; exact evaluation of their constant sign tests in \(\mathbb D\) gives the Boolean outputs deciding \(\mathsf Z\), \(\mathsf N\), and \(\mathsf P\).  A true \(\mathsf Z\) returns \(\operatorname{ZERO\_EVIDENCE\_QE}\), and a true \(\mathsf N\) returns \(\operatorname{NOT\_UNIFORMLY\_DEFINED\_QE}\); neither terminal carries a range formula.  Only when \(\mathsf P\) is true is quantifier elimination applied to
\[
\varphi_Q(t):=
\exists x\,[x\in S\wedge e(x)>0\wedge e(x)t-n(x)=0]
\tag{6}
\]
to obtain \(\chi_Q(t)\), followed by the singleton decision and endpoint formulas constructed as in \((3)\)--\((4)\).  The procedure returns formulas and decisions only: it returns no explicit endpoint value, algebraic sample, CAD trace, canonical parameter, or paired SCM.
