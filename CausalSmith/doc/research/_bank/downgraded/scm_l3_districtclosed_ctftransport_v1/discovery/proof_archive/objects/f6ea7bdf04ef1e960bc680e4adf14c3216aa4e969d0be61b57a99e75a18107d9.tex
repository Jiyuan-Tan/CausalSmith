Because \(\mathcal U\) is finite, every candidate \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\) can be enumerated.  Under the checked common-district reduction and positive common denominator, an arm \(\rho\) belongs to \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\) exactly when there exists \(q\) satisfying
\[
q\ge0,\quad A_Dq=m_D,\quad R_eq=p_e\ (e\in S),
\quad (n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})^{\mathsf T}q\ge0
\quad(\sigma\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}).
\tag{1}
\]
This is finite linear feasibility.  Hence (1) computes every \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\), after which direct comparison with \(\operatorname{PO}(\mathcal U)\) and enumeration of the finite costs characterizes every minimum-cost solution.  Exact rational or real-algebraic Gaussian elimination, sign comparison, and linear feasibility make the computation effective.

For the counterexample, take four deterministic profiles \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_4\) in one bidirected-complete binary district and choose declared arm and Layer-3 cell events with incidence table
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
&n_\rho&n_\sigma&r_1&r_2\\\hline
\omega_1&1&0&1&0\\
\omega_2&1&0&0&1\\
\omega_3&0&1&1&1\\
\omega_4&0&1&0&0
\end{array}
\]
and sure evidence.  The district-saturated response-block construction realizes this finite truth table on original binary variables by assigning the four displayed deterministic response maps; declare \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) as the measured cells of two partial-copy regimes.  Take no base equality beyond normalization, so \(\mathcal K_\varnothing=\Delta_4\), and let experiment \(e_i\) reveal \(r_i^{\mathsf T}q=1\) at unit cost.

Then
\[
\mathcal K_{\{e_1\}}=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_1,\delta_3\},
\qquad
\mathcal K_{\{e_2\}}=\operatorname{conv}\{\delta_2,\delta_3\},
\qquad
\mathcal K_{\{e_1,e_2\}}=\{\delta_3\}.
\]
Here \(\delta_i\) denotes the unit probability law at \(\omega_i\).  At \(\delta_1\) and \(\delta_2\), arm \(\rho\) is uniquely optimal; at \(\delta_3\), arm \(\sigma\) is uniquely optimal.  Therefore the four possibly-optimal sets are exactly those in the statement.  Hence
\[
f(\{e_2\})-f(\varnothing)=0
<1=f(\{e_1,e_2\})-f(\{e_1\}),
\]
which violates diminishing returns.  Full information eliminates \(\rho\) only if both experiments are bought, so the binary covering constraint is \(x_1+x_2\ge2\).  Its only binary feasible point is \((1,1)\), of cost two.  For the continuous relaxation, multiplying the covering inequality by \(y=1\) gives the lower bound two, matching that feasible point.
