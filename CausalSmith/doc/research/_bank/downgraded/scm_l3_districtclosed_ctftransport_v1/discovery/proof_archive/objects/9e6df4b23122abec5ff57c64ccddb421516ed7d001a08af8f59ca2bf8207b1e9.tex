Fix \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\).  For \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), put \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\).  Completeness of the response carrier gives \(q\ge0\) and \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}q=1\).  Each retained raw row has transport-admissibility and accepted support-locality proofs, so typed-menu realizability, total probability, and district-kernel invariance give
\[
P_a^s(v)=(r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v})^{\mathsf T}q.
\tag{1}
\]
For every retained row-eligible context--cell pair, district-cell linearity gives the two separate equations
\[
N_{s,a,D,h,c}=u_{s,a,D,h,c}^{\mathsf T}q,
\qquad H_{s,a,D,h}=v_{s,a,D,h}^{\mathsf T}q.
\tag{2}
\]
By the corrected stacking definition, normalization, (1), and (2) are precisely \(A_Dq=m_D\).  Thus
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\tag{3}
\]

Assume \(\mathsf{BASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  Its definition supplies one baseline \(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\).  Write \(K=\mathcal K_D(m_D)\), \(q_0=q_D^\star(\mathbf M^0)\), and \(Z=Z_D(m_D)\).  By (3), \(q_0\in K\).

Let \(J=\Omega_D\setminus Z\).  For each \(\omega\in J\), the definition of \(Z\) supplies \(q^\omega\in K\) with \(q^\omega(\omega)>0\).  Their average \(\bar q\) belongs to \(K\), is zero on \(Z\), and is strictly positive on \(J\).  Define
\[
L=\{v:A_Dv=0,\ v(\omega)=0\text{ for }\omega\in Z\}.
\]
Every difference of two points of \(K\) lies in \(L\).  Conversely, if \(v\in L\), finiteness of \(J\) and positivity of \(\bar q\) permit \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(\bar q\pm\varepsilon v\ge0\).  Both perturbations belong to \(K\), whence
\[
v=(2\varepsilon)^{-1}\{(\bar q+\varepsilon v)-(\bar q-\varepsilon v)\}.
\]
Thus \(L=\operatorname{span}(K-K)\).  Appending the rows \(e_\omega^{\mathsf T}\), \(\omega\in Z\), to \(A_D\) gives \(\widetilde A_D\) with \(L=\ker\widetilde A_D\), so finite-dimensional row reduction yields
\[
R_D(m_D)=\operatorname{row}(\widetilde A_D)
=L^\perp=\operatorname{span}(K-K)^\perp.
\tag{4}
\]

Fix \(q\in K\).  Because \(\Omega_D=\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\), replace the target law of the complete focal block by \(q\), and make the same replacement in every unchanged source.  Keep the baseline focal law in every changed source and keep every nonfocal mechanism and law fixed.  District closure makes the changed/unchanged cases exhaustive; focal saturation permits the replacement on the original alphabets; block independence and common replacement preserve district factorization and district-kernel invariance.  Call the resulting family \(\mathbf M^q\).

For a transport-admissible supplied complete cell, finite total probability under the fixed nonfocal product law gives
\[
P_{M^{q,s}}^a(v)-P_{M^{0,s}}^a(v)
=(\bar\chi_{C_{a,v}}^{s,\mathbf M^0})^{\mathsf T}(q-q_0).
\tag{5}
\]
The baseline-averaged predicate puts the first factor in \(R_D(m_D)\), while (4) puts \(q-q_0\) in its orthogonal complement, so (5) is zero.  A nonadmissible cell belongs to a changed source whose focal law was left fixed, so it is also preserved.  Therefore \(\mathbf M^q\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\) and \(q_D^\star(\mathbf M^q)=q\).  Since \(q\) was arbitrary, (3) gives
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)=K.
\]
Every boundary point is included and every nonfocal graph restriction remains fixed.  The active-slice and structural-separation lemmas give the two stated stronger sufficient conditions.
