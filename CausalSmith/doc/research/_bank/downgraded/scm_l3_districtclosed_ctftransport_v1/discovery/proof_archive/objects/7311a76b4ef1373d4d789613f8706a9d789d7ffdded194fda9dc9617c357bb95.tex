For the general computability claim, fix \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\).  The set \(\mathcal K_S\) is defined by the finite equalities \(A_Dq=m_D\), \(R_eq=p_e\) for \(e\in S\), and inequalities \(q\geq0\).  For a candidate arm \(\rho\), common evidence reduces \(Q_\rho(q)\geq Q_\sigma(q)\), whenever \(d_Dq>0\), to
\[
(n_{\rho,D}-n_{\sigma,D})q\geq0.
\]
To decide whether \(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}(S)\), maximize \(d_Dq\) subject to \(q\in\mathcal K_S\) and all these arm-comparison inequalities; membership holds exactly when the optimum is positive.  This is a finite linear program.  Since \(\mathcal U\) is finite, enumeration of its \(2^{|\mathcal U|}\) subsets, followed by comparison of \(\operatorname{PO}(S)\) with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}\) and minimization of \(\sum_{e\in S}c_e\), returns every minimum-cost solution.

For the obstruction use the binary graph \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\to Y\), singleton district \(D=\{Y\}\), sure evidence, and profile order
\(
\Omega_D=((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1))
\).
Let the base face be the whole simplex.  Let \(\rho\) be treatment zero and \(\sigma\) treatment one with reward \(Y=1\), so
\[
n_{\rho,D}=(0,0,1,1),
\qquad n_{\sigma,D}=(0,1,0,1),
\qquad g_{\rho,\sigma,D}=(0,1,-1,0).
\]
Take the realizable response law
\[
q^\star=(1/4,3/8,1/8,1/4).
\]
Experiment \(e_1\) reveals the Layer-3 cell \(q_2=P(Y_0=0,Y_1=1)=3/8\), and \(e_2\) reveals \(q_3=P(Y_0=1,Y_1=0)=1/8\); both have unit cost.  Each is a zero-one incidence row, and the displayed response-block SCM realizes both on the original binary alphabets.

With no experiment, simplex laws with \(q_2-q_3<0\) and with \(q_2-q_3>0\) exist.  Under \(e_1\) alone, the feasible pairs \((q_2,q_3)=(3/8,5/8)\) and \((3/8,0)\) have opposite gap signs.  Under \(e_2\) alone, the feasible pairs \((q_2,q_3)=(0,1/8)\) and \((7/8,1/8)\) do likewise.  Remaining mass is nonnegative and may be placed in coordinate one or four, whose gap coefficient is zero.  Singleton-district response-block construction realizes all these laws, so
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1\})
=\operatorname{PO}(\{e_2\})=\{\rho,\sigma\}.
\]
With both blocks, every feasible law satisfies
\[
g_{\rho,\sigma,D}q=q_2-q_3=\frac38-\frac18=\frac14>0,
\]
so \(\operatorname{PO}(\{e_1,e_2\})=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\{\sigma\}\).  Consequently
\[
f(\{e_2\})-f(\varnothing)=0
<1=f(\{e_1,e_2\})-f(\{e_1\}),
\]
violating submodularity for \(S=\varnothing\), \(T_{\mathrm{exp}}=\{e_1\}\), and \(e=e_2\).

Omitting either experiment leaves both arms possibly optimal by the displayed witnesses, so full-information pruning requires both acquisition variables.  The exact integer program is therefore
\[
\min_{x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}}x_1+x_2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad x_1+x_2\geq2,
\]
whose unique feasible optimum is \((1,1)\) with value two.  The continuous relaxation has dual
\[
\max_{0\leq y\leq1}2y.
\]
The multiplier \(y=1\) supplies the matching lower bound two, certifying the integer optimum.
