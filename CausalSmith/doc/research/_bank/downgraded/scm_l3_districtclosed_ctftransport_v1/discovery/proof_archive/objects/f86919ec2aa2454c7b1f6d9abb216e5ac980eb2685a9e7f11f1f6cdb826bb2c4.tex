Assume \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\), and fix a transport-admissible complete supplied cell \(C\) and a nonfocal profile \(\omega_{-D}\).  If the recursive indicator is independent of \(\omega_D\), its focal slice is either the zero row or \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\); both lie in \(R_D(m_D)\), since normalization is a row of \(A_D\).  If the indicator depends only on \(\omega_D\), the district-separated definition requires its accepted raw row to occur in \(A_D\), and every focal slice is that row.  These cases prove \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).

For strictness, take independent binary singleton response districts generating \(Y_0\) and \(Z\), with \(D\) the first district.  Supply the complete marginal law of \(Y_0\) and the complete joint law of \((Y_0,Z)\).  For \(y,z\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
h_y(\omega_D)=\mathbf 1\{Y_0(\omega_D)=y\},
\qquad k_z(\omega_{-D})=\mathbf 1\{Z(\omega_{-D})=z\}.
\]
The marginal law retains \(h_0,h_1\) in \(A_D\).  A joint cell has cross-district indicator \(h_yk_z\), which is neither independent of \(\omega_D\) nor a function of \(\omega_D\) alone because both binary carriers realize both values.  Hence \(\mathsf{Sep}_D\) fails.  After fixing \(\omega_{-D}\), however, the focal slice is either \(0\) or \(h_y\), both in \(\operatorname{row}(A_D)\).  Thus every joint and marginal slice lies in \(R_D(m_D)\), so \(\mathsf{ASep}_D\) holds.  Independent Bernoulli laws on the original binary blocks realize the menu.
