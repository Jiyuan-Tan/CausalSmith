We prove the saturated, sparse-canonical, optimization, and focal claims in turn.

Assume first \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\), and take \(\mathbf M\) in that fiber.  In domain \(s\), let \(q_D^s\) be the law of the complete response block \(U_D^s\in\Omega_D=\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}\).  District factorization gives the product law across the original districts.  Consequently, for every finite counterfactual cell \(C\), recursive substitution and finite total probability give
\[
P_{M^s}(C)
=\sum_{\omega\in\prod_D\Omega_D}
\mathbf 1\{C(\omega)=1\}\prod_Dq_D^s(\omega_D)
=\Psi_C^s(\theta^s).
\tag{1}
\]
The complete carrier represents every allowed parent configuration, including those appearing only in \(C\).  If \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), district-kernel invariance gives \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\).  Typed-menu realizability and (1) give every complete supplied-cell equation, so \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  This proves the forward saturated range inclusion simultaneously for every \(C\in\mathcal F\).

Conversely, let \(\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal P)\).  In each domain take mutually independent district blocks with laws \(q_D^s\) on the full response-map sets and let every structural equation read its component of the relevant block.  Acyclicity gives unique recursive values in the original alphabets.  The saturated model-class definition permits every such product of block laws, and the equalities \(q_D^s=q_D^\star\) preserve unchanged districts.  The defining menu equations and (1) give
\[
P_{M_\theta^s}^a(v)=\Psi_{C_{a,v}}^s(\theta^s)=P_a^s(v).
\]
Thus \(\mathbf M_\theta\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal P)\) and realizes the complete vector of \(\Psi\)-values.  This proves the reverse saturated inclusion without any completion of unrepresented response coordinates.

For the arbitrary-ADMG row, take \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\).  There are finitely many endogenous variables, values, and parent assignments, so the collection consisting of every response-map coordinate, every supplied cell, and every cell in \(\mathcal F\) is finite.  Apply finite canonical counterfactual equivalence in each domain to this joint collection.  The resulting canonical SCMs have the same diagram and observed alphabets and preserve its entire joint law.  In particular they preserve each complete district response-map law. q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^s(\cdot;\vartheta^s)
=q_{D,\mathrm{can}}^\star(\cdot;\vartheta^\star)
\quad\text{when }D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing.
\tag{2}
\]
They also preserve every supplied complete-cell probability, so \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\), and preservation of \(\mathcal F\) proves the forward canonical range inclusion.

Conversely, a point \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G^{\mathrm{can}}(\mathcal P)\) consists of graph-compatible one-hot structural tables and canonical exogenous probability vectors.  The one-hot constraints choose a unique value in \(\mathcal X_V\) at every structural input, the simplex constraints give probability laws, and the retained latent-factor restrictions and original-district product restrictions give recursive compatibility and district factorization.  Equations (2) give district-kernel invariance, district closure is part of the fixed instance, and the complete supplied-cell equations give every law in \(\mathcal P\).  Its canonical realization therefore lies in \(\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\), uses the original alphabets, and realizes the displayed \(\Phi_{\mathrm{can}}\)-values.  This proves the reverse canonical inclusion.

Both parameter fibers are finite-dimensional.  The saturated fiber is a closed subset of a finite product of simplices cut out by polynomial equalities.  The graph-compatible canonical fiber is a closed subset of a finite union of products of simplices and finite one-hot table sets, cut out by polynomial equalities and inequalities.  Both are bounded, hence compact, and both are semialgebraic.  Nonemptiness follows from the respective assumed SCM fiber and the forward constructions.  In (1), each monomial contains at most one coordinate from each visited original district, proving multi-affinity and the asserted degree bound.

Fix either exact range row \((\mathfrak C,\Lambda,\Xi)\) and a target cell \(T\).  The continuous target polynomial attains a minimum and maximum on the nonempty compact set \(\Lambda\).  Exact range equality makes these the attained sharp SCM bounds.  A cell probability lies in \([0,1]\), so at least one endpoint improves on the logical bounds exactly when its minimum is positive or its maximum is below one.  Equality of the extrema is equivalent to constancy and hence identification.  If the extrema differ, their attaining parameters realize two original-alphabet same-menu SCMs with unequal values.  No connectedness, and therefore no interval-filling conclusion, is used.

The nonnegative evidence polynomial also attains its extrema.  If its maximum is zero, it is zero throughout the fiber.  If its minimum is zero and maximum is positive, an attaining compatible model has zero evidence and another has positive evidence, so the conditional is not uniformly defined.  Neither case permits division on the whole fiber.  If its minimum is strictly positive, the ratio
\[
x\longmapsto
\frac{\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star(x^\star)}{\Xi_E^\star(x^\star)}
\tag{3}
\]
is continuous on \(\Lambda\) and attains its sharp lower and upper bounds.  Exact range equality realizes its endpoint parameters by compatible original-alphabet SCMs.

For two arms with the same strictly positive evidence denominator,
\[
Q_\sigma^\star-Q_\rho^\star
=\frac{\Xi_{B_\sigma\cap E}^\star-
\Xi_{B_\rho\cap E}^\star}{\Xi_E^\star}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus strict dominance throughout the fiber is equivalent to strict positivity of the attained minimum numerator gap.  A negative minimum and positive maximum are attained exactly when two compatible endpoint models give opposite strict rankings.  Equation (4) proves the safe-pruning characterization without conflating numerator optimization with the causal conditional.
