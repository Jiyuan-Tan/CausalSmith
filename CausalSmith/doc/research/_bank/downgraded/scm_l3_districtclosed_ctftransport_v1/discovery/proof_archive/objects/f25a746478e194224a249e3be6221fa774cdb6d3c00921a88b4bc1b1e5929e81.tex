Fix \(D\in\mathcal D(G)\).  Let \(\mathbf M\in\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) and put \(q=q_D^\star(\mathbf M)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:district\text{-}response\text{-}profile}\), \(q\geq0\) and \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\).  Consider a raw row retained in \(A_D\).  By \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}menu\text{-}system}\), its retention includes proofs that its supplied complete cell is district-local and that \(s=\star\) or \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\).  Recursive evaluation and \(\mathrm{ass:typed\text{-}menu\text{-}realizability}\) give
\[
P_a^s(v)=r^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,a,D,v}q_D^s.
\]
If \(s=\star\), then \(q_D^s=q\) by definition; if \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), then \(q_D^s=q\) by \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\).  Thus every retained raw equality holds at \(q\).  For a retained derived row, \(\mathrm{lem:district\text{-}cell\text{-}linearity}\), together with the same admissibility proof, gives
\[
K_{s,a,D}(c\mid h)=r_{s,a,D,h,c}q.
\]
Stacking normalization, raw, and derived equalities in the order specified by \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}menu\text{-}system}\) yields \(A_Dq=m_D\).  Hence
\[
\mathcal Q_D(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]

Now assume \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\ne\varnothing\) and \(\mathsf{ASep}_D(\mathcal P)\).  The preceding inclusion and \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal C(\mathcal P)\) imply
\[
\mathcal Q_D^{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal K_D(m_D).
\]
For the reverse inclusion, choose a baseline \(\mathbf M^0\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P)\), write \(q_0=q_D^\star(\mathbf M^0)\), and fix arbitrary \(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  The broad inclusion already proved gives \(q_0\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\), and therefore
\[
A_D(q-q_0)=0,
\qquad
(q-q_0)(\omega)=0\quad\text{for every }\omega\in Z_D(m_D).
\]
Consequently, from the definition of \(R_D(m_D)\),
\[
h(q-q_0)=0\qquad\text{for every }h\in R_D(m_D).
\tag{1}
\]

Every alphabet in \(\mathcal X\) is finite and nonempty.  Complete each partial profile \(\omega_D\in\Omega_D\) at every unvisited parent configuration by a fixed element of the appropriate alphabet, and denote the resulting injection by
\[
\iota_D:\Omega_D\longrightarrow\Omega_D^{\mathrm{all}}.
\]
In the target domain replace the law of \(U_D^\star\) by \((\iota_D)_\#q\).  Make the identical replacement in every source satisfying \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), retain the baseline law of \(U_D^s\) in every source satisfying \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), and hold fixed every structural table and latent law in every district \(D'\ne D\).  These two source cases exhaust all possibilities by \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}closure}\).  Call the resulting family \(\mathbf M^q\).

The component-reading structural equations at \(D\) are unchanged, so acyclicity of \(G\) preserves recursive evaluation and every output remains in \(\mathcal X\).  The restrictions at every \(D'\ne D\) are literally those of \(\mathbf M^0\).  Taking the new \(D\)-block marginal independently of the unchanged nonfocal blocks preserves \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\).  The bidirected completeness, full-block representation, and unrestricted-law clause of \(\mathrm{def:focal\text{-}district\text{-}saturated\text{-}model\text{-}class}\) admit \((\iota_D)_\#q\) on the same graph and alphabets.  Equality between the target replacement and each unchanged-source replacement gives \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}kernel\text{-}invariance}\) at \(D\); every other required equality remains true because its blocks were held fixed.  Thus \(\mathbf M^q\) belongs to the focal-district-saturated model class before checking the menu.

Fix a complete supplied cell \(C_{a,v}\) in domain \(s\).  Suppose first that it is transport-admissible for \(D\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:district\text{-}factorization}\), the baseline law of the nonfocal profile is a product probability law; denote it by \(\nu^s\).  It is unchanged in \(\mathbf M^q\).  Finite total probability and the definition of the focal slice give the exact difference
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{M^{q,s}}^a(v)-P_{M^{0,s}}^a(v)
&=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}
\nu^s(\omega_{-D})
\sum_{\omega_D}
\chi_{C_{a,v},\omega_{-D}}(\omega_D)
\{q(\omega_D)-q_0(\omega_D)\}\\
&=\sum_{\omega_{-D}}
\nu^s(\omega_{-D})
\chi_{C_{a,v},\omega_{-D}}(q-q_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Active-slice separation places every slice in \(R_D(m_D)\), so every summand is zero by (1).  Therefore this supplied cell retains its baseline probability \(P_a^s(v)\).

If the cell is not transport-admissible for \(D\), then \(s\ne\star\) and \(D\cap\Delta_s\ne\varnothing\).  District closure gives \(D\subseteq\Delta_s\), so the \(D\)-block and every nonfocal block in that source were all left at their baseline laws.  Its cell probability is again unchanged.  Thus every complete cell, and hence every supplied law, equals \(\mathcal P\).  We have shown
\[
\mathbf M^q\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sat},D}(\mathcal P),
\qquad q_D^\star(\mathbf M^q)=q.
\]
This proves the reverse inclusion.  The construction uses only \(q\geq0\) and \(A_Dq=m_D\); it therefore realizes boundary points, preserves the original alphabets, and leaves every \(D'\ne D\) at its graph-compatible baseline.  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:structural\text{-}separation\text{-}implies\text{-}active\text{-}slice}\) shows that \(\mathsf{Sep}_D(\mathcal P)\) remains a sufficient, though not necessary, check for the new hypothesis.
