\paragraph{Proof.}
Write a generic law on the ordered response-profile carrier in the complete binary instance as
\[
q=(q_{00},q_{01},q_{10},q_{11})^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By the definitions of the binary instance and the compatible face, membership in \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) is equivalent to coordinatewise nonnegativity together with
\[
q_{00}+q_{01}+q_{10}+q_{11}=1,
\qquad
q_{10}+q_{11}=\frac12,
\qquad
q_{01}+q_{11}=\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]
Set \(p=q_{11}\).  The last two equations in \((2)\) give
\[
q_{10}=\frac12-p,
\qquad
q_{01}=\frac12-p,
\]
and substitution in the first equation gives
\[
q_{00}
=1-\left(\frac12-p\right)-\left(\frac12-p\right)-p
=p.
\]
Thus every feasible law equals
\[
q(p)=\left(p,\frac12-p,\frac12-p,p\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{3}
\]
Coordinatewise nonnegativity in \((3)\) is equivalent to \(0\leq p\leq 1/2\).  Conversely, for every \(p\) in this interval, \((3)\) is nonnegative and satisfies all three equations in \((2)\).  This proves both uniqueness of the parameterization and
\[
\mathcal K_D(m_D)=\{q(p):0\leq p\leq1/2\}.
\tag{4}
\]

The checked-local-arms record in the binary-instance definition supplies
\[
d_D=(1,1,1,1),
\quad
n_{\rho,D}=(0,1,1,0),
\quad
n_{\sigma,D}=(0,1,0,1),
\]
and certifies \(d_D^{\mathsf T}q=1>0\) throughout the face.  Hence the divisions in the definition of the two conditional arm values are valid.  Direct substitution of \((3)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_\rho(q(p))
&=\frac{q_{01}+q_{10}}{1}
=\left(\frac12-p\right)+\left(\frac12-p\right)
=1-2p,\\
Q_\sigma(q(p))
&=\frac{q_{01}+q_{11}}{1}
=\left(\frac12-p\right)+p
=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

By the additional-cell definition, \(r_D^\dagger=(0,0,0,1)\), and therefore
\[
(r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}q(p)=q_{11}=p.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations \((4)\) and \((6)\) yield \(I_D=[0,1/2]\).  For any fixed \(p\in I_D\), the row-augmented feasible set contains exactly the single law \(q(p)\).  Since
\[
g_{\rho,\sigma,D}=n_{\sigma,D}-n_{\rho,D}=(0,0,-1,1),
\]
the definition of the parametric transported margin and \((3)\) imply
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D}^{\mathsf T}q(p)
=-q_{10}+q_{11}
=-\left(\frac12-p\right)+p
=2p-\frac12.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathcal J_{\rho\prec\sigma}^+
=\left\{p\in[0,1/2]:2p-\frac12>0\right\}
=\left(\frac14,\frac12\right].
\tag{8}
\]

For completeness, let \(\widetilde A_D\) be \(A_D\) with \(r_D^\dagger\) appended as its fourth row and let
\[
\widetilde m_D(p)=\left(1,\frac12,\frac12,p\right)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\eta=(0,-1,0,2)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
An exact coordinate calculation gives
\[
\widetilde A_D^{\mathsf T}\eta
=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix}
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D},
\qquad
\widetilde m_D(p)^{\mathsf T}\eta
=-\frac12+2p.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\eta\) is dual feasible with equality in every coordinate.  Moreover, every \(q\) satisfying the augmented row equations obeys
\[
g_{\rho,\sigma,D}^{\mathsf T}q
=\eta^{\mathsf T}\widetilde A_Dq
=\eta^{\mathsf T}\widetilde m_D(p)
=2p-\frac12,
\]
so the certificate is exact, in agreement with \((7)\).  At the realized value \(p=3/8\), its certified value is
\[
2\left(\frac38\right)-\frac12=\frac14.
\tag{10}
\]

The endpoint and boundary laws are genuine SCM laws on the declared binary alphabets, rather than formal points introduced by enlarging a state space.  Indeed, for any \(p\in[0,1/2]\), take a finite latent response variable \(U_D\) with values \((Y_0,Y_1)\in\{0,1\}^2\) and law \(q(p)\), and define the binary structural equation for \(Y\) by selecting the coordinate \(Y_{T_{\mathrm{tr}}}\).  Use the same response law in the target and source because \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\), keep all nonfocal response blocks independent and fixed, and retain the target and source mechanisms for \(Z\) stated in the binary-instance definition.  The selection set \(\Delta_s=\{Z\}\) is a union of the singleton district of \(Z\), so district closure holds; the independent construction gives district factorization.  Because \((2)\) is exactly the base row system, every such SCM induces the base menu.  The additional Boolean output equals one precisely at response type \((1,1)\), and hence its complete source output law is
\[
P_{e^\dagger}^s(1)=q_{11}=p,
\qquad
P_{e^\dagger}^s(0)=1-p.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, \(p=3/8\) gives the declared probabilities \(3/8\) and \(5/8\).  At the sign boundary, \((3)\) gives
\[
q(1/4)=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4),
\]
and \((5)\) gives equality of the two arm values.  At the two endpoints,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&Q_\rho&Q_\sigma\\ \hline
p=0&1&1/2\\
p=1/2&0&1/2
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
so \(q(0)\) makes \(\rho\) strictly optimal and \(q(1/2)\) makes \(\sigma\) strictly optimal, both in original-alphabet SCMs.

It remains to check the acquisition conclusion.  Before acquisition, \((12)\) witnesses that each of the two arms is optimal somewhere on \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\).  Since the declared arm set is exactly \(\{\rho,\sigma\}\), the definition of the possibly-optimal set therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\{\rho,\sigma\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Acquiring the sole block imposes \(q_{11}=3/8\) by \((11)\).  The unique parameterization \((3)\) then yields
\[
\mathcal K_{\{e^\dagger\}}
=\{q(3/8)\}
=\left\{\left(\frac38,\frac18,\frac18,\frac38\right)^{\mathsf T}\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
At this law, equation \((5)\) gives
\[
Q_\rho(q(3/8))=\frac14<\frac12=Q_\sigma(q(3/8)).
\tag{15}
\]
There are no other arms.  Thus the acquisition and full-information definitions, together with \((14)\)--\((15)\), give
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\{e^\dagger\})
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}
=\{\sigma\}.
\]
All conditional values used above are defined because the checked evidence denominator is identically one.  This proves every asserted identity and pruning claim.
