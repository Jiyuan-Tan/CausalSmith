%%% FIELD proof-global-inversion-coverage
Fix \(n\in\mathbb N\) and a compatible observed law \(q\in\overline{\mathcal Q}\).  Put
\[
M_z:=\sum_{d=0}^1\sum_{k=1}^K N_{zdk},
\qquad
\delta:=\frac{\alpha}{4K},
\qquad
E_{zdk}:=\{q_z(d,k)\in I^{\mathrm{CP}}_{zdk}(\delta)\}.
\]
These quantities are well defined because \(K\ge2\), \(0<\alpha<1/2\), and \(n<\infty\).  By independent sampling in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), conditional on \(M_z=m\), the count \(N_{zdk}\) has the binomial law
\[
N_{zdk}\mid M_z=m\ \sim\ \operatorname{Bin}\bigl(m,q_z(d,k)\bigr).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:positive\text{-}instrument\text{-}shares}\) ensures that each conditional arm law \(q_z\) is well defined.  The defining exact-coverage property of the two-sided Clopper--Pearson interval in Definition \(\mathrm{def:observed\text{-}confidence\text{-}region}\) is, for every \(m\ge1\) and every \(r\in[0,1]\),
\[
\Pr_{B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,r)}
 \{r\notin I^{\mathrm{CP}}(B,m;\delta)\}\le\delta.
\]
This property includes the boundary values \(r=0\) and \(r=1\), so no positivity of an individual multinomial cell is required.  If \(M_z=0\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:observed\text{-}confidence\text{-}region}\) uses the full arm simplex, and hence \(E_{zdk}\) holds surely.  Therefore the law of total probability gives, for every \((z,d,k)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_q(E_{zdk}^{c})
&=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\Pr_q(M_z=m)
  \Pr_q(E_{zdk}^{c}\mid M_z=m)\\
&\le \delta\sum_{m=1}^{n}\Pr_q(M_z=m)
\le\delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
There are \(2\cdot2\cdot K=4K\) such cell events.  The union bound, followed by the definition of \(R_n\), yields
\[
\Pr_q\{q\notin R_n\}
 =\Pr_q\!\left(\bigcup_{z=0}^1\bigcup_{d=0}^1
                    \bigcup_{k=1}^K E_{zdk}^{c}\right)
 \le\sum_{z,d,k}\Pr_q(E_{zdk}^{c})
 \le4K\delta=\alpha,
\]
and consequently
\[
\Pr_q\{q\in R_n\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{1}
\]
This is the simultaneous observed-law coverage step; it uses only marginal exact binomial coverage and therefore does not require independence among the \(4K\) cell events.

On the event \(\{q\in R_n\}\), compatibility gives
\[
q\in R_n\cap\overline{\mathcal Q}.
\]
Thus this intersection is nonempty, and the non-fallback branch of Definition \(\mathrm{def:outer\text{-}inversion}\) gives the two deterministic inclusions
\[
S(q)\subseteq
 \bigcup_{q'\in R_n\cap\overline{\mathcal Q}}S(q')=C_n,
\qquad
S(q)\in
 \{S(q'):q'\in R_n\cap\overline{\mathcal Q}\}=\mathcal H_n. \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2), by monotonicity of probability,
\[
\Pr_q\{S(q)\subseteq C_n\}
 \ge\Pr_q\{q\in R_n\}\ge1-\alpha,
\qquad
\Pr_q\{S(q)\in\mathcal H_n\}
 \ge\Pr_q\{q\in R_n\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

Finally, the argument is unchanged on every structural-zero sampling face: the exact binomial coverage property used above is valid at \(q_z(d,k)=0\) and \(q_z(d,k)=1\).  It is also unchanged when \(M_z=0\), because the full-simplex convention makes all events for that arm automatic.  No step assumes that the empirical proportions satisfy the monotonicity compatibility inequalities.  Indeed, even when the empirical cell differences are incompatible, \(q\in R_n\) still implies \(q\in R_n\cap\overline{\mathcal Q}\); if the latter intersection is empty, then \(\{q\in R_n\}\) cannot occur for a compatible \(q\), while the fallback clause in Definition \(\mathrm{def:outer\text{-}inversion}\) keeps both reported objects well defined.  Hence all asserted finite-sample guarantees persist in the stated boundary and incompatibility cases.
