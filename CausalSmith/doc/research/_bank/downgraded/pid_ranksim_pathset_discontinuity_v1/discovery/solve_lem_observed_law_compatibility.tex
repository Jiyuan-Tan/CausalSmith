Fix \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\).  The positive-share assumption \(\pi_z>0\) ensures that each conditional probability defining \(q_z\) is well defined.  For \(z\in\{0,1\}\), latent selection and exclusion imply the event identities
\[
\{D=1,Y=y_k\}=\{U\le p_z,Y_1=y_k\},\qquad
\{D=0,Y=y_k\}=\{U>p_z,Y_0=y_k\}
\]
within the event \(\{Z=z\}\).  Hence, by the definition of \(q\),
\[
q_z(1,k)=\Pr(U\le p_z,Y_1=y_k\mid Z=z),\qquad
q_z(0,k)=\Pr(U>p_z,Y_0=y_k\mid Z=z).
\]
Both events on the right are measurable with respect to \((U,Y_0,Y_1)\).  Instrument independence therefore gives, for each \(z\),
\[
q_z(1,k)=\Pr(U\le p_z,Y_1=y_k),\qquad
q_z(0,k)=\Pr(U>p_z,Y_0=y_k).
\]
The strict propensity order \(p_0<p_1\) yields the exact set differences
\[
\{U\le p_1\}\setminus\{U\le p_0\}=\{p_0<U\le p_1\},
\qquad
\{U>p_0\}\setminus\{U>p_1\}=\{p_0<U\le p_1\}.
\]
These identities already account for both endpoints, so no unmentioned boundary condition is required.  Because the smaller event is contained in the larger event in each difference, finite additivity on the corresponding disjoint unions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{1k}
&=q_1(1,k)-q_0(1,k)\\
&=\Pr(U\le p_1,Y_1=y_k)-\Pr(U\le p_0,Y_1=y_k)\\
&=\Pr(p_0<U\le p_1,Y_1=y_k)\ge0,
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{0k}
&=q_0(0,k)-q_1(0,k)\\
&=\Pr(U>p_0,Y_0=y_k)-\Pr(U>p_1,Y_0=y_k)\\
&=\Pr(p_0<U\le p_1,Y_0=y_k)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first equality in each display is the definition of the observed balances.  Since \(k\) was arbitrary, \((c_0(q),c_1(q))\in\mathbb R_+^{2K}\).  Moreover, \(q\in\Delta_{2K-1}^2\) because its two arms are conditional multinomial probability vectors.  The definition of \(\overline{\mathcal Q}\) now implies \(q\in\overline{\mathcal Q}\).  Thus the nonnegativity inequalities are consequences of the maintained latent model and observable through \(q\); they have not been imposed as additional assumptions.  The uniform-resistance normalization supplies the declared support \(U\in[0,1]\), but the difference argument itself uses neither a density calculation nor division by a latent probability.
