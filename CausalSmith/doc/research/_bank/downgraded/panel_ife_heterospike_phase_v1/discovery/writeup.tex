\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}ife\_\allowbreak{}heterospike\_\allowbreak{}phase}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} This paper studies an abstract exact-oracle panel experiment in which four finite-array orthogonality restrictions remove ordinary baseline confounding without fixing the profiled coefficient.  The full-sample IFE coefficient has a uniformly localized two-by-two spiked-SVD profile with an always-separated singleton minimizer.  It equals the ATT coordinate exactly when \(|h|\le h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\), and beyond that threshold it moves strictly toward the common effect.  The absorbed phase is relatively open and is nonempty exactly when \(\overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\); on its compact subsets the conventional residualized IFE Wald interval has asymptotically zero coverage.  Masked fitting remains an internal benchmark control.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing panel theory covers regular strong factors, generic weak-factor robustness, or a strong bad-control endpoint, while rectangular random-matrix theory fixes the spike before taking limits.  It does not supply the globally selected coefficient-indexed phase boundary \(h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\) after baseline-confounding terms are removed by defining restrictions.

\paragraph{Niche.} An exact-oracle noise-scale treatment factor creates a nonregular but fully classifiable transition: ATT is retained through the spectral edge and is partially absorbed strictly beyond it.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives \(\Phi_{\eta,h}\), proves uniform bounded-local profile convergence and an always-separated singleton minimizer, identifies \(h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)=c^{1/4}/[\kappa s_1(C_\eta)]\), proves that the absorbed phase is nonempty exactly under the stated reach inequality, and derives the explicit residual-design overlap and conventional-Wald zero-coverage law there.

\section{Related work}

Bai (2009) supplies the regular profiled-IFE and strong-factor framework, but not a coefficient-indexed noise-scale treatment spike.  Moon and Weidner (2015) study harmless rank overestimation under conditions excluding this low-rank regressor geometry.  Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi (2012) supply the fixed-spike rectangular outlier and singular-vector-overlap maps; this paper embeds those maps in the global coefficient profile and proves that the ATT coordinate is selected exactly through \(|h|=h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\).  Bloemendal and Virag (2013) study near-edge laws, whereas the critical point here remains a unique first-order selection.  Armstrong, Weidner, and Zeleneev (2026) motivate weak-factor-robust inference but exclude the rank-one block treatment through non-collinearity.  Cardoso, Ferman, and Fernandes (2026) establish the strong treatment-factor bad-control endpoint, not the bounded local phase or its exact reach condition.  Bai and Ng (2021) motivate observed-block imputation, and Gobillon and Magnac (2016) motivate comparing whole-sample and counterfactual factor fits; the present exact-oracle result is a sensitivity benchmark rather than a direct empirical identification claim.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:aspect-bound]\label{ass:aspect-bound}
\(0<\underline c\le c_N\le\overline c<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Proportional rectangular asymptotics, \cite{BenaychGeorgesNadakuditi2012}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:aspect-limit]\label{ass:aspect-limit}
\(c_N\to c\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Aspect-ratio convergence, \cite{BenaychGeorgesNadakuditi2012}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-noise]\label{ass:gaussian-noise}
\(\{E_{it}/\sigma:1\le i\le N,\ 1\le t\le T\}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian rectangular noise, \cite{Onatski2012}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:noise-scale]\label{ass:noise-scale}
\(0<\underline\sigma\le\sigma\le\overline\sigma<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform variance bounds, \cite{ArmstrongWeidnerZeleneev2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:loading-gram]\label{ass:loading-gram}
\(N^{-1}\Lambda^{\top}\Lambda\to\Sigma_{\Lambda}\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strong loading Gram limit, \cite{Bai2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:factor-gram]\label{ass:factor-gram}
\(T^{-1}F^{\top}F\to\Sigma_F\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strong factor Gram limit, \cite{Bai2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:loading-gram-envelope]\label{ass:loading-gram-envelope}
\(0<\underline\lambda\le\lambda_{\min}(N^{-1}\Lambda^{\top}\Lambda)\le\lambda_{\max}(N^{-1}\Lambda^{\top}\Lambda)\le\overline\lambda<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This paper maintains a deterministic uniform strong-loading envelope, satisfied by fixed-rank panels whose empirical loading Gram matrices remain in a compact positive-definite set.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:factor-gram-envelope]\label{ass:factor-gram-envelope}
\(0<\underline f\le\lambda_{\min}(T^{-1}F^{\top}F)\le\lambda_{\max}(T^{-1}F^{\top}F)\le\overline f<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This paper maintains a deterministic uniform strong-factor envelope, satisfied by fixed-rank common-state arrays with uniformly nondegenerate empirical covariance.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:oracle-class-nonempty]\label{prop:oracle-class-nonempty}
For every fixed \(r\) and every \(c\in(\underline c,\overline c)\), there exist dimensions \(N,T\to\infty\) and deterministic arrays \(d,qvec,a,b,\Lambda,F\) such that \(c_N\to c\), \(\eta_{NT}\to\eta\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}})\), \(\Lambda^{\top}d=\Lambda^{\top}a_S=0\), \(F^{\top}qvec=F^{\top}b_S=0\), the full Gram limits \(N^{-1}\Lambda^{\top}\Lambda\) and \(T^{-1}F^{\top}F\) are positive definite, the selector-restricted limits \(N^{-1}\Lambda^{\top}M_d\Lambda\) and \(T^{-1}F^{\top}M_{qvec}F\) are positive definite, and the loading, factor, and treatment-score rows are uniformly bounded. Hence the exact-oracle triangular-array class is nonempty without imposing any rule for selecting a minimizer of \(\Phi_{\eta,h}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(r\) and \(c\in(\underline c,\overline c)\). Define
\[
 \mu^\circ=\frac{\underline\mu+\overline\mu}{2},
 \qquad
 v^\circ=\frac{\underline v+\overline v}{2}.
\]
The defining inequalities \(0<\underline\mu<\overline\mu\) and \(0<\underline v<\overline v\) imply
\[
 \mu^\circ\in(\underline\mu,\overline\mu),
 \qquad
 v^\circ\in(\underline v,\overline v).
\]
For every sufficiently large integer \(k\), put
\[
 s_k=\lfloor ck\rfloor,
 \qquad
 N_k=4s_k,
 \qquad
 T_k=4k.
\]
Because \(c>0\), both dimensions tend to infinity. Moreover, \(0\le ck-s_k<1\), so the definition \(c_N=N/T\) gives
\[
 c_{N_k}=\frac{N_k}{T_k}=\frac{s_k}{k}\longrightarrow c.
\]
Since \(c\in(\underline c,\overline c)\), this also yields \(c_{N_k}\in(\underline c,\overline c)\) for all sufficiently large \(k\).

Suppress \(k\) in the array notation. Define the support indicators by
\[
 d_i=\mathbf 1\{1\le i\le2s_k\},
 \qquad
 qvec_t=\mathbf 1\{1\le t\le2k\}.
\]
Thus, by the definitions of \(n_1,t_1,p_N,q_T\),
\[
 n_1=2s_k,
 \qquad t_1=2k,
 \qquad p_N=\frac{2s_k}{4s_k}=\frac12,
 \qquad q_T=\frac{2k}{4k}=\frac12.
\]
Define the score arrays by
\[
 a_i=
 \begin{cases}
  \mu^\circ+(-1)^i\sqrt{v^\circ},&1\le i\le2s_k,\\
  0,&2s_k<i\le4s_k,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 b_t=
 \begin{cases}
  \mu^\circ+(-1)^t\sqrt{v^\circ},&1\le t\le2k,\\
  0,&2k<t\le4k.
 \end{cases}
\]
These arrays vanish off their respective supports, hence the definitions \(a_S=d\odot a\) and \(b_S=qvec\odot b\) give \(a_S=a\) and \(b_S=b\). Each supported block has even cardinality and therefore contains equally many signs of each type. The definitions of the supported means yield
\[
 \mu_{a,N}
 =\frac{1}{2s_k}\sum_{i=1}^{2s_k}
   \bigl(\mu^\circ+(-1)^i\sqrt{v^\circ}\bigr)
 =\mu^\circ,
 \qquad
 \mu_{b,T}
 =\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{t=1}^{2k}
   \bigl(\mu^\circ+(-1)^t\sqrt{v^\circ}\bigr)
 =\mu^\circ.
\]
After centering by these means, every supported squared deviation is \(v^\circ\). Consequently, the definitions of \(v_{a,N}\) and \(v_{b,T}\) give
\[
 v_{a,N}
 =\frac{1}{2s_k}\sum_{i=1}^{2s_k}v^\circ
 =v^\circ,
 \qquad
 v_{b,T}
 =\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{t=1}^{2k}v^\circ
 =v^\circ.
\]

Suppose first that \(r\ge1\). Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^r\), and define
\[
 \rho(j)=1+((j-1)\bmod r).
\]
For sufficiently large \(k\), both \(2s_k\ge r\) and \(2k\ge r\). Define the rows of the loading and factor matrices by
\[
 \Lambda_{i\cdot}=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&1\le i\le2s_k,\\
  e_{\rho(i-2s_k)}^{\top},&2s_k<i\le4s_k,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 F_{t\cdot}=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&1\le t\le2k,\\
  e_{\rho(t-2k)}^{\top},&2k<t\le4k.
 \end{cases}
\]
The nonzero rows of \(\Lambda\) occur only where \(d_i=0\), while \(a_S\) is zero there. Hence, term by term,
\[
 \Lambda^{\top}d
 =\sum_{i=1}^{N_k}\Lambda_{i\cdot}^{\top}d_i=0,
 \qquad
 \Lambda^{\top}a_S
 =\sum_{i=1}^{N_k}\Lambda_{i\cdot}^{\top}(a_S)_i=0.
\]
Similarly, the nonzero rows of \(F\) occur only where \(qvec_t=0\), while \(b_S\) is zero there, and thus
\[
 F^{\top}qvec=0,
 \qquad
 F^{\top}b_S=0.
\]

For \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\), let
\[
 n_{\ell,k}=\#\{1\le j\le2s_k:\rho(j)=\ell\},
 \qquad
 f_{\ell,k}=\#\{1\le j\le2k:\rho(j)=\ell\}.
\]
The cyclic allocation gives
\[
 \left|n_{\ell,k}-\frac{2s_k}{r}\right|\le1,
 \qquad
 \left|f_{\ell,k}-\frac{2k}{r}\right|\le1.
\]
Direct multiplication of the displayed row arrays therefore gives
\[
 \frac{1}{N_k}\Lambda^{\top}\Lambda
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
   \frac{n_{1,k}}{4s_k},\ldots,\frac{n_{r,k}}{4s_k}
  \right)
 \longrightarrow \frac{1}{2r}I_r
\]
and
\[
 \frac{1}{T_k}F^{\top}F
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
   \frac{f_{1,k}}{4k},\ldots,\frac{f_{r,k}}{4k}
  \right)
 \longrightarrow \frac{1}{2r}I_r.
\]
Both limiting matrices are positive definite. By the definitions \(M_d=\operatorname{diag}(1-d_1,\ldots,1-d_N)\) and \(M_{qvec}=\operatorname{diag}(1-qvec_1,\ldots,1-qvec_T)\), the support placement implies
\[
 M_d\Lambda=\Lambda,
 \qquad
 M_{qvec}F=F.
\]
It follows that the restricted Gram matrices satisfy
\[
 \frac{1}{N_k}\Lambda^{\top}M_d\Lambda
 =\frac{1}{N_k}\Lambda^{\top}\Lambda
 \longrightarrow\frac{1}{2r}I_r,
 \qquad
 \frac{1}{T_k}F^{\top}M_{qvec}F
 =\frac{1}{T_k}F^{\top}F
 \longrightarrow\frac{1}{2r}I_r,
\]
so their limits are positive definite as well. For sufficiently large \(k\), every cyclic count is positive. Hence \(\Lambda\) and \(F\) each have column rank \(r\), and \(\Lambda F^{\top}\) has rank \(r\).

If the convention \(0\in\mathbb N\) is used and \(r=0\), take \(\Lambda\) and \(F\) to be the unique \(N_k\times0\) and \(T_k\times0\) matrices. Then the four orthogonality identities hold, the baseline has rank zero, and the four Gram conclusions hold under the zero-dimensional convention, in which positive definiteness is vacuous because there is no nonzero vector in \(\mathbb R^0\).

For \(r\ge1\), every row of \(\Lambda\) and \(F\) has Euclidean norm at most one; the same assertion is immediate in the rank-zero case. The score construction and the triangle inequality give, uniformly in \(k\),
\[
 \max_{1\le i\le N_k}|a_i|
 \le\mu^\circ+\sqrt{v^\circ},
 \qquad
 \max_{1\le t\le T_k}|b_t|
 \le\mu^\circ+\sqrt{v^\circ}.
\]
Thus all loading, factor, and treatment-score rows are uniformly bounded.

Finally, the definition of \(\eta_{NT}\) and the calculations above show
\[
 \eta_{N_kT_k}
 =\left(c_{N_k},\frac12,\frac12,
         \mu^\circ,v^\circ,\mu^\circ,v^\circ\right)
 \longrightarrow
 \eta
 =\left(c,\frac12,\frac12,
         \mu^\circ,v^\circ,\mu^\circ,v^\circ\right).
\]
Because \(\underline\pi\in(0,1/2)\), one has \(1/2\in(\underline\pi,1-\underline\pi)\). The strict inclusions for \(c,\mu^\circ,v^\circ\) established above place every coordinate of \(\eta\) in the interior of its corresponding factor in the definition of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\); for each mean coordinate, \(\mu^\circ\) lies in the interior of the positive component \([\underline\mu,\overline\mu]\). Therefore
\[
 \eta\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}).
\]
The construction depends only on \(c,r\) and the fixed envelope constants. In particular, it does not use \(\Phi_{\eta,h}\), \(h\), any minimizer of \(\Phi_{\eta,h}\), or any selection from its argmin. This proves the asserted nonemptiness independently of a profile-minimizer selection rule.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The sensitivity experiment must contain concrete deterministic sequences rather than rely on mutually inconsistent oracle restrictions. \textit{Gap.} This is a nonvacuity certificate for the paper's abstract class, not a claim that the restrictions are observable or operationally constructible. \textit{Consumer.} It validates that the absorption phase law is evaluated on a nonempty confounding-free oracle class.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-profile]\label{thm:uniform-profile}
Suppose \(\eta_{NT}\to\eta\).  Uniformly over compact subsets of
\(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h]\) and every finite \(M\),
\[
\sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
\frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)-Q_F(\gamma)}{\sigma^2T}
-\{\Phi_{NT,h}(x)-\Phi_{NT,h}(0)\}
\right|\xrightarrow{p}0.
\]
Moreover, \(\widehat x_F=O_p(1)\) uniformly,
\[
\operatorname{dist}\{\widehat x_F,\mathcal S_{NT}(h)\}\xrightarrow{p}0,
\qquad
\widehat x_F-x_\star(\eta,h)\xrightarrow{p}0
\]
uniformly on those compact sets.  Here \(h=\theta/\sigma\).  The global singleton theorem implies
\(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h]\),
so no genuine limiting tie occurs anywhere in the bounded experiment.  No stochastic assertion is made for \(|h|>\overline h\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(U_0=\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)\) and \(V_0=\operatorname{col}(F)\).  The loading and factor Gram envelopes imply that both spaces have dimension \(r\).  Moreover,
\[
 s_r\!\left(\frac{\Lambda F^{\top}}{\sigma\sqrt T}\right)
 \geq
 \frac{\sqrt{N\underline\lambda}\sqrt{T\underline f}}
 {\overline\sigma\sqrt T}
 \geq C_0\sqrt m,
 \qquad C_0=\frac{\sqrt{\underline\lambda\underline f}}{\overline\sigma}>0. \tag{1}
\]
All bounds below are uniform when \((\eta,h)\) ranges over a fixed compact subset \(\mathcal K\) of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h]\).  The aspect, share, score, and noise-scale envelopes make all constants uniform on \(\mathcal K\).

The share and finite supported-variance envelopes make the vectors
\[
 e_1=\frac d{\sqrt{n_1}},\qquad
 e_2=\frac{a_S-\mu_{a,N}d}{\sqrt{n_1v_{a,N}}},\qquad
 f_1=\frac{qvec}{\sqrt{t_1}},\qquad
 f_2=\frac{b_S-\mu_{b,T}qvec}{\sqrt{t_1v_{b,T}}} \tag{2}
\]
well defined.  The definitions of the supported means and variances give
\[
 e_j^{\top}e_k=f_j^{\top}f_k=\mathbf 1\{j=k\},\qquad 1\leq j,k\leq2. \tag{3}
\]
The four exact orthogonality assumptions give
\[
 e_1,e_2\in U_0^{\perp},\qquad f_1,f_2\in V_0^{\perp}. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, for example,
\(\Lambda^{\top}(a_S-\mu_{a,N}d)=0\) follows from the left-score and left-support restrictions, and the other three inclusions are identical calculations.

For \(\beta=\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m\), define
\[
 P_x=\frac{H-(\beta-\gamma)D}{\sigma\sqrt T}.
\]
Substitution of the definitions of \(H\), \(D\), and (2) gives the exact identity
\[
 P_x=(e_1,e_2)B_{NT,h}(x)(f_1,f_2)^{\top}. \tag{5}
\]
To check its normalization, the heterogeneous term is multiplied by
\[
 \frac{\theta\sqrt{n_1t_1}}{\sigma\sqrt{mT}}
 =h\sqrt{\frac Nm}\sqrt{p_Nq_T}=h\kappa_{NT}, \tag{6}
\]
and the coefficient displacement is multiplied by \(x\kappa_{NT}\).  For every fixed \(M\), the coefficient-domain assumption and \(\sigma\leq\overline\sigma\) imply that \(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\) for all \(|x|\leq M\) and all sufficiently large panels.

Complete orthonormal bases beginning with bases of \(U_0\) and \(V_0\).  Gaussian orthogonal invariance, (4), and (5) yield
\[
 W_x:=\frac{Y-(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)D}{\sigma\sqrt T}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 G+Z_{11}&Z_{12}\\
 Z_{21}&Z_{22}+P_x
 \end{pmatrix}, \tag{7}
\]
where \(s_r(G)\geq C_0\sqrt m\), every entry of \(Z\) is independently \(\mathcal N(0,1/T)\), and the nonzero coordinates of \(P_x\) are the two-by-two matrix in (5).  The rectangular Gaussian outlier lemma with zero perturbation, transposed and rescaled when \(c_N>1\), gives
\[
 \|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_p(1). \tag{8}
\]
Also \(\sup_{|x|\leq M}\|P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O(1)\).  Weyl's inequality and (1) therefore put the first \(r\) singular values of \(W_x\) above \(C_0\sqrt m/2\) with probability tending to one, uniformly for \(|x|\leq M\).

Write a corresponding unit singular pair as \((u_1,u_2)\) and \((v_1,v_2)\), partitioned as in (7).  The lower block singular equations imply
\[
 \|u_2\|_2\leq
 \frac{\|Z_{21}|_{\mathrm{op}}+|Z_{22}+P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|v_2\|_2}{s_j(W_x)},
 \qquad
 \|v_2\|_2\leq
 \frac{\|Z_{12}|_{\mathrm{op}}+|Z_{22}+P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|u_2\|_2}{s_j(W_x)}.
\]
Equations (1) and (8) hence give
\[
 \max_{1\leq j\leq r}\sup_{|x|\leq M}
 \bigl(\|u_{j,2}(x)\|_2+\|v_{j,2}(x)\|_2\bigr)
 =O_p(m^{-1/2}). \tag{9}
\]
Along the line segment from \(P_0\) to \(P_x\), the Ky Fan sum of the first \(r\) squared singular values is absolutely continuous.  At every differentiability point its derivative is a sum of terms
\(2s_j u_{j,2}^{\top}(P_x-P_0)v_{j,2}\).  Since \(r\) is fixed, (9) bounds the derivative by \(O_p(m^{-1/2})\), uniformly on the segment.  Integration gives
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 \sum_{j=1}^r s_j(W_x)^2-sum_{j=1}^r s_j(W_0)^2
 \right|=o_p(1). \tag{10}
\]
This argument also covers singular-value crossings because the Ky Fan sum is locally Lipschitz and the derivative bound holds almost everywhere.

For the next singular value, apply the singular equations to the symmetric dilation of (7).  On the orthogonal complement of its \(2r\) strong eigenspaces, solving the strong-coordinate equations introduces the Schur-complement term
\[
 O_p\!\left(\frac{\|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2+|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}\sup_{|x|\leq M}\|P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
 {s_r(G)}\right)=o_p(1).
\]
The min--max characterization of singular values then yields
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}
 \left|s_{r+1}(W_x)-s_1(Z_{22}+P_x)\right|=o_p(1). \tag{11}
\]

We next make the outlier limit uniform over \(|x|\leq M\).  The matrices \(B_{NT,h}(x)\) range over a compact family.  For any two deterministic perturbations,
\[
 |s_1(Z_{22}+P)-s_1(Z_{22}+\widetilde P)|
 \leq\|P-\widetilde P\|_F. \tag{12}
\]
Choose a finite Frobenius \(\epsilon\)-net of the corresponding two-by-two matrices.  At its finitely many points, the rectangular Gaussian outlier lemma applies.  The function \((\rho,s)\mapsto\psi_\rho(s)\) is continuous at the edge because its two displayed branches agree at \(s=\rho^{1/4}\).  Equation (12), the finite net, and then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) give, when \(c\leq1\),
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 s_1(Z_{22}+P_x)^2-psi_{c_N}\{s_1(B_{NT,h}(x))\}
 \right|\xrightarrow{p}0. \tag{13}
\]
When \(c>1\), apply the same lemma to \(c_N^{-1/2}(Z_{22}+P_x)^{\top}\), whose aspect ratio tends to \(1/c\).  Multiplying its squared limit by \(c\) gives
\[
 c\left\{\frac{s^2}{c}+1+\frac1c+\frac1{s^2}\right\}
 =s^2+1+c+\frac c{s^2},
\]
with threshold \(s=c^{1/4}\), so (13) again follows.  Removing the fixed number \(r\) of rows and columns changes the aspect ratio and entry normalization by \(o(1)\); continuity of \(\psi\) absorbs this change.

The Frobenius part has an exact expansion.  Orthogonality of the baseline block to \(P_x\) gives
\[
 \|W_x\|_F^2-\|W_0\|_F^2
 =\|B_{NT,h}(x)\|_F^2-\|B_{NT,h}(0)\|_F^2
 +2\langle Z,P_x-P_0\rangle_F. \tag{14}
\]
The last term is affine in \(x\), has mean zero, and its supremum on \([-M,M]\) has variance bounded by \(C_M/T\).  It is therefore \(o_p(1)\).  By the full-profile definition,
\[
 \frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)}{\sigma^2T}
 =\|W_x\|_F^2-\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}s_j(W_x)^2.
\]
Combining (10), (11), (13), and (14) proves
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 \frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)-Q_F(\gamma)}{\sigma^2T}
 -\{\Phi_{NT,h}(x)-\Phi_{NT,h}(0)\}
 \right|\xrightarrow{p}0. \tag{15}
\]

It remains to prove global localization rather than merely compact convergence.  Put
\[
 R=\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b),
 \qquad d_0^2=\kappa^2h^2v_av_b,
 \qquad L_\infty=d_0^2-(1+c). \tag{16}
\]
At \(x=0\), \(B_{\eta,h}(0)\) has rank one and nonzero squared singular value \(R\).  Direct substitution in the two branches of \(\psi_c\) gives
\[
 L_\infty-\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)=
 \begin{cases}
 d_0^2+2\sqrt c-R,&R\leq\sqrt c,\\
 d_0^2+c/R,&R>\sqrt c.
 \end{cases} \tag{17}
\]
The first expression is at least \(d_0^2+\sqrt c\), and the second is positive.  Compactness of \(\mathcal K\), the positive aspect bound, and the upper bound on \(R\) therefore give a common constant \(4g_{\mathcal K}>0\) below the right-hand side of (17).

For completeness, the stochastic tail has the same value \(L_\infty\).  In the lower block write
\[
 Z_{22}+P_x=-t e_1f_1^{\top}+A,
 \qquad t=\kappa_{NT}x,
 \qquad A=Z_{22}+P_0.
\]
For \(|t|\to\infty\), the eigenvalue equation, expanded around its simple eigenvalue \(t^2\), gives uniformly after \(|t|\) exceeds a fixed constant,
\[
 s_1(-t e_1f_1^{\top}+A)^2
 =t^2-2t e_1^{\top}Af_1+|A^{\top}e_1\|_2^2
 +\|(I-e_1e_1^{\top})Af_1\|_2^2+O_p(|t|^{-1}). \tag{18}
\]
The two Gaussian quadratic terms converge to \(1\) and \(c\); all Gaussian--spike cross terms converge to zero.  In the criterion centered at \(x=0\), subtracting (18) from the Frobenius expansion cancels the terms in \(t^2\) and \(t e_1^{\top}Af_1\).  The remaining lower-block difference converges to
\[
 \psi_c\{s_1(B_{\eta,h}(0))\}-(1+c)
 -\|P_0^{\top}e_1\|_2^2
 -\|(I-e_1e_1^{\top})P_0f_1\|_2^2. \tag{19}
\]
The orthogonal block decomposition of \(P_0\), together with \(\|P_0\|_F=\|B_{\eta,h}(0)\|_F\), gives
\[
 \|B_{\eta,h}(0)\|_F^2
 =\|P_0^{\top}e_1\|_2^2
 +\|(I-e_1e_1^{\top})P_0f_1\|_2^2+d_0^2.
\]
Consequently, (19) equals
\[
 d_0^2-(1+c)-\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)
 =L_\infty-\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)\geq4g_{\mathcal K}. \tag{20}
\]
For larger \(|x|\), the same singular equations are applied jointly to the \(r\) baseline directions and the treatment direction.  Their deterministic left and right spaces are orthogonal by (4); the baseline singular values are bounded below by \(C_0\sqrt m\), while the treatment singular value is \(\kappa_{NT}|x|\).  Splitting at a sequence \(M_N\to\infty\) with \(M_N=o(\sqrt m)\), the first calculation covers \(M\leq|x|\leq M_N\), and on \(|x|>M_N\) every Schur-complement denominator is bounded below by a constant times \(\min\{\sqrt m,M_N\}\to\infty\).  Thus the joint error is \(o_p(1)\), and the cancellation in (19) is unchanged.  Consequently,
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\liminf_{N,T\to\infty}
 \Pr\!\left[
 \inf_{\substack{|x|\geq M\\ \gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m\in\mathcal B}}
 \left\{
 \frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)-Q_F(\gamma)}{\sigma^2T}
 \right\}\geq2g_{\mathcal K}
 \right]=1. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (17)--(21) exclude every diverging feasible coefficient sequence.  Hence
\[
 \widehat x_F=\frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)}\sigma=O_p(1) \tag{22}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).

On the common compact set supplied by (21), (15) applies.  The corrected global-profile singleton theorem gives a continuous unique minimizer and a positive uniform gap outside every fixed neighborhood.  Since \(\eta_{NT}\to\eta\), uniform continuity of \(\Phi\) and the same tail gap give
\[
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal S_{NT}(h)}|y-x_\star(\eta,h)|\longrightarrow0. \tag{23}
\]
If either \(\operatorname{dist}\{\widehat x_F,\mathcal S_{NT}(h)\}\to_p0\) or \(\widehat x_F-x_\star(\eta,h)\to_p0\) failed, (15), (21), and the corresponding deterministic separation gap would contradict global minimality.  This proves both argmin conclusions uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).  Finally, the global singleton theorem applies on all of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\), so
\[
 \mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}}=
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h],
\]
and there is no limiting tie in the bounded experiment.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem links the actual full-sample IFE objective to the explicit two-by-two profile and, using global singleton separation, localizes the selected coefficient uniformly throughout the bounded experiment. \textit{Gap.} Fixed-spike random-matrix limits do not themselves control a coefficient-indexed global argmin. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies direct convergence to the unique profile minimizer for the exact phase and Wald calculations.

\begin{theorem}[thm:subcritical-no-absorption]\label{thm:subcritical-no-absorption}
Uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}}\) satisfying \(\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2v_b+v_a\mu_b^2+v_av_b)<\sqrt c\), \(\mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x_{\operatorname{ATT}}\}\) and \(\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})/\sigma\xrightarrow{p}0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(x_A=h\mu_a\mu_b=x_{\operatorname{ATT}}\).  The local geometry assumption gives \(c>0\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(v_a,v_b>0\).  At \(x_A\), direct substitution in the local-spike profile gives
\[
 \|B_{\eta,h}(x_A)\|_F^2
 =\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2v_b+v_a\mu_b^2+v_av_b)<\sqrt c. \tag{1}
\]
Therefore \(s_1\{B_{\eta,h}(x_A)\}<c^{1/4}\).  At every subcritical point, the outlier-map definition gives
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)=\|B_{\eta,h}(x)\|_F^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2. \tag{2}
\]
The only \(x\)-dependent term in the squared Frobenius norm is
\(\kappa^2(h\mu_a\mu_b-x)^2\).  Hence (2) is uniquely minimized at \(x=x_A\) among subcritical points.

For any supercritical \(x\), write \(s_j=s_j\{B_{\eta,h}(x)\}\).  Since \(s_1^2>\sqrt c\),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)
 =s_2^2-(1+c)-\frac c{s_1^2}
 >-(1+c)-\sqrt c. \tag{3}
\]
By (1) and (2),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x_A)
 <\sqrt c-(1+\sqrt c)^2
 =-(1+c)-\sqrt c. \tag{4}
\]
Thus no supercritical point can minimize, and the within-branch calculation proves
\[
 \mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x_A\}. \tag{5}
\]

On any compact set in the statement, the strict inequality in (1) has a common positive margin.  The uniform-profile theorem and (5) therefore give \(\widehat x_F-x_A\to_p0\) uniformly.  The definitions of \(\widehat x_F\), \(h\), and the finite-array target give the exact identity
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 =\widehat x_F-h\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}. \tag{6}
\]
The two moment assumptions imply
\(\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}\to\mu_a\mu_b\).  Combining this convergence with (5) and (6) proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}\xrightarrow{p}0
\]
uniformly on the stated compact subsets.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result certifies a nonempty positive-signal region in which fitting the extra factor is harmless at the local ATT scale. \textit{Gap.} Cardoso, Ferman, and Fernandes (2026) analyze an asymptotically strong heterogeneous factor and do not contain a bounded subcritical no-absorption region. \textit{Consumer.} It identifies the harmless side of the theoretical absorption frontier within the exact-oracle class.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:global-selection]\label{oeq:global-selection}
Classify \(\mathcal S(\eta,h)\) outside the stable-singleton restriction. Either establish uniqueness and separation, or enumerate every genuine separated tie and derive a measurable next-order selection functional of the complete nuisance-drift path and the coefficient-indexed limiting noise process. The classification must include opposite admissible \(m^{-1/4}\) drifts toward a tie.
\end{openendedquestion}
\begin{proof}
Theorem \(\textnormal{thm:global-profile-singleton}\) proves that \(\mathcal S(\eta,h)\) is a separated singleton for every \((\eta,h)\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\), gives its explicit measurable selector, and derives the one-sided critical expansion under opposite admissible \(m^{-1/4}\) drifts. Hence every alternative requested by this open-ended question is resolved by that theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Global selection is the irreducible open content because deterministic uniform convergence cannot choose between separated equal minima. \textit{Gap.} Neither coefficient profiling nor spiked-matrix edge theory supplies a measurable selector at a drift-sensitive tie of this endogenous scalar potential. \textit{Consumer.} The answer determines whether the abstract full-sample coefficient has a stable limit at every detectability boundary.

\begin{theorem}[thm:absorption-law]\label{thm:absorption-law}
Uniformly over every compact subset of
\(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h]\),
\[
\frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
\xrightarrow{p}x_\star(\eta,h)-h\mu_a\mu_b.
\]
The exact phase classification is
\[
x_\star(\eta,h)=h\mu_a\mu_b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|h|\leq h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta).
\]
If \(|h|>h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\), then the minimizer lies strictly between zero and the ATT coordinate in its signed direction:
\[
0<x_\star(\eta,h)<h\mu_a\mu_b
\quad\text{when }h\mu_a\mu_b>0,
\]
and
\[
h\mu_a\mu_b<x_\star(\eta,h)<0
\quad\text{when }h\mu_a\mu_b<0.
\]
The set \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\) is relatively open in
\(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h]\), and
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta).
\]
No assertion is made for \(|h|>\overline h\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The definitions of \(\widehat x_F\), \(h\), and \(\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\) give the exact finite-array identity
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 =\widehat x_F-h\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}. \tag{1}
\]
The uniform-profile theorem gives
\(\widehat x_F-x_\star(\eta,h)\to_p0\) uniformly on compact subsets of the bounded experiment.  The two moment assumptions give
\(\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}\to\mu_a\mu_b\).  Substitution in (1) proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 \xrightarrow{p}x_\star(\eta,h)-h\mu_a\mu_b. \tag{2}
\]

It remains to identify the phase.  At
\(x_{\operatorname{ATT}}=h\mu_a\mu_b\),
\[
 B_{\eta,h}(x_{\operatorname{ATT}})
 =\kappa h
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&\mu_a\sqrt{v_b}\\
 \sqrt{v_a}\mu_b&\sqrt{v_av_b}
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{3}
\]
Row and column sign changes are orthogonal transformations.  Thus, with \(C_\eta\) from the absorption-phase definition,
\[
 s_1\{B_{\eta,h}(x_{\operatorname{ATT}})\}
 =\kappa|h|s_1(C_\eta). \tag{4}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\), \(\kappa>0\), \(v_a,v_b>0\), and \(|\mu_a|,|\mu_b|>0\).  Hence \(s_1(C_\eta)>0\), so every division below is valid.

In the scalar coordinates of the corrected global-profile singleton theorem, \(z=0\) is exactly \(x=x_{\operatorname{ATT}}\), and \(\tau(0)\) is the squared largest singular value in (4).  Its exact selection rule gives \(z_\star=0\) if and only if \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\).  By (4),
\[
 \tau(0)^2\leq c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \kappa^4h^4s_1(C_\eta)^4\leq c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |h|\leq\frac{c^{1/4}}{\kappa s_1(C_\eta)}
 =h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta). \tag{5}
\]
This includes \(h=0\) and equality at the spectral edge.  The inverse scalar coordinate then proves
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)=h\mu_a\mu_b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |h|\leq h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta). \tag{6}
\]

If \(|h|>h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\), the same theorem gives \(0<z_\star<z_0\) and
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)
 =\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b)}\kappa(z_0-z_\star),
 \qquad
 h\mu_a\mu_b
 =\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b)}\kappa z_0. \tag{7}
\]
Because \(\kappa>0\), equation (7) gives
\[
 0<x_\star(\eta,h)<h\mu_a\mu_b
 \quad\text{if }h\mu_a\mu_b>0,
\]
and
\[
 h\mu_a\mu_b<x_\star(\eta,h)<0
 \quad\text{if }h\mu_a\mu_b<0. \tag{8}
\]

The entries of \(C_\eta\), its largest singular value, \(c^{1/4}\), and \(\kappa\) are continuous on \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\).  The denominator in (5) is positive and, by compactness, bounded away from zero.  Thus \(h_{\mathrm{crit}}\) is continuous and positive.  The strict inverse image
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}
 =\{(\eta,h):|h|>h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\}
 \cap\{(\eta,h):\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}},\ |h|\leq\overline h\}
\]
is relatively open.  Compactness of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) implies that \(h_{\mathrm{crit}}\) attains its minimum.  If \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\neq\varnothing\), its defining inequality implies
\[
 \overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta). \tag{9}
\]
Conversely, if (9) holds, choose a minimizer \(\eta_0\) and any \(|h|\in(h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta_0),\overline h]\); then \((\eta_0,h)\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\).  This proves the exact nonemptiness equivalence without an additional reach assumption.  All stochastic uses above invoke \(|h|\leq\overline h\), so no conclusion has been extended beyond the bounded experiment.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact local ATT-absorption phase law, including the critical selection and the necessary-and-sufficient reach condition for nonemptiness. \textit{Gap.} The strong-signal endpoint does not identify the bounded rectangular threshold or the selected transition path. \textit{Consumer.} Within the abstract exact-oracle sensitivity benchmark, it identifies exactly when treatment-supported heterogeneity remains in the coefficient and when fitted factors absorb part of it.

\begin{proposition}[prop:deterministic-large-signal-endpoint]\label{prop:deterministic-large-signal-endpoint}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\). Along any deterministic branch with \((\eta,h)\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}}\) for all sufficiently large \(|h|\), \(x_\star(\eta,h)=c\mu_a\mu_b\{\kappa^4v_av_b(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b)\}^{-1}h^{-3}+o(h^{-3})\) as \(|h|\to\infty\), and hence \(x_\star(\eta,h)\to0\). This is a calculation for the deterministic profile and is not a stochastic triangular-array limit under \(\theta\in[-K,K]\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\), and abbreviate
\[
 M=\mu_a\mu_b,\qquad U=\mu_a^2+v_a,\qquad V=\mu_b^2+v_b,\qquad R=v_av_b,\qquad L=UV.
\]
The definition of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) gives \(v_a>0\), \(v_b>0\), \(c>0\), \(p>0\), and \(q>0\).  Hence \(R>0\), \(L>0\), and \(\kappa=\sqrt{\max\{c,1\}pq}>0\).

For \(h\ne0\), put \(y=x/h\) and
\[
 C(y)=A_\eta-ye_{11}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 M-y&\mu_a\sqrt{v_b}\\
 \sqrt{v_a}\mu_b&\sqrt{R}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(\tau(y)\) and \(\upsilon(y)\) be, respectively, the larger and smaller squared singular values of \(C(y)\).  Direct calculation gives
\[
 \|C(y)\|_F^2=L-2My+y^2,
 \qquad
 \det C(y)=-y\sqrt R.
\]
Consequently, using the trace and determinant identities for the two eigenvalues of \(C(y)C(y)^{\top}\),
\[
 \tau(y)+\upsilon(y)=L-2My+y^2,
 \qquad
 \tau(y)\upsilon(y)=Ry^2.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, the lower-right entry of \(C(y)\) is \(\sqrt R\), so
\[
 \tau(y)=s_1\{C(y)\}^2\ge R                                      \tag{2}
\]
for every \(y\in\mathbb R\).  At \(y=0\), equation \((1)\) yields
\[
 \tau(0)=L,\qquad \upsilon(0)=0.                                  \tag{3}
\]
Since \(R>0\), equations \((1)\)--\((2)\) also show that \(\upsilon(y)=0\) if and only if \(y=0\).

We next establish the global localization needed to justify using the local expansion.  The bounds
\[
 (M-y)^2\le \tau(y)\le \|C(y)\|_F^2=L-2My+y^2
\]
imply \(\tau(y)/y^2\to1\) as \(|y|\to\infty\).  By \((1)\), therefore,
\[
 \upsilon(y)=\frac{Ry^2}{\tau(y)}\longrightarrow R
 \quad\text{as }|y|\to\infty.                                    \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\upsilon\) is continuous, has the unique zero \(0\), and is separated from zero off every neighborhood of zero: for each \(d>0\),
\[
 a_d:=\inf_{|y|\ge d}\upsilon(y)>0.                                \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, \((4)\) controls the tails, while continuity and the unique-zero property control the remaining compact set.

By the local-spike-profile definition,
\[
 B_{\eta,h}(hy)=\kappa h C(y).
\]
It follows from \((2)\) that
\[
 s_1\{B_{\eta,h}(hy)\}=\kappa|h|\sqrt{\tau(y)}
 \ge \kappa|h|\sqrt R.
\]
Hence, whenever \(\kappa|h|\sqrt R>c^{1/4}\), every \(y\) lies on the supercritical branch in the definition of \(\psi_c\).  On that branch,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(hy)
 &=\kappa^2h^2\{\tau(y)+\upsilon(y)\}
   -\left\{\kappa^2h^2\tau(y)+1+c+
   \frac{c}{\kappa^2h^2\tau(y)}\right\}\\
 &=\kappa^2h^2\left\{\upsilon(y)-
   \frac{\varepsilon_h}{\tau(y)}\right\}-(1+c),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_h:=\frac{c}{\kappa^4h^4}.                            \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus minimizing \(\Phi_{\eta,h}\) over \(x\in\mathbb R\) is equivalent to minimizing
\[
 g_\varepsilon(y):=\upsilon(y)-\frac{\varepsilon}{\tau(y)}         \tag{7}
\]
over \(y\in\mathbb R\), with \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_h\).

We now prove that this minimizer is unique and global for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\).  At \(y=0\), equations \((3)\) and \((7)\) give
\[
 g_\varepsilon(0)=-\frac{\varepsilon}{L}<0.                        \tag{8}
\]
On the other hand, \((2)\), \((5)\), and \((7)\) give, for \(|y|\ge d\),
\[
 g_\varepsilon(y)\ge a_d-\frac{\varepsilon}{R}.                   \tag{9}
\]
Choose \(d>0\) small enough that the two roots in \((1)\) remain distinct on \([-d,d]\).  There, they are twice continuously differentiable, as is seen directly from the quadratic formula applied to \((1)\).  Differentiating \((1)\) at zero and using \((3)\) gives
\[
 \upsilon'(0)=0,\qquad \tau'(0)=-2M,
 \qquad \upsilon''(0)=\frac{2R}{L}>0.                              \tag{10}
\]
After decreasing \(d\) if necessary, \(\upsilon''\) is bounded below by a positive constant on \([-d,d]\).  Because \(\tau\ge R\) and \(\tau\) is twice continuously differentiable there, the second derivative of \(-\varepsilon/\tau\) is uniformly of order \(\varepsilon\).  Therefore \(g_\varepsilon\) is strictly convex on \([-d,d]\) for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).  Equations \((8)\)--\((9)\), with \(\varepsilon<a_dR/2\), place every global minimizer in \((-d,d)\).  Such a minimizer exists by continuity on \([-d,d]\), and strict convexity makes it unique; denote it by \(y_\varepsilon\).

This uniqueness also supplies the separation required in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}}\).  Indeed, \((4)\) and \((7)\) give \(g_\varepsilon(y)\to R>g_\varepsilon(y_\varepsilon)\) as \(|y|\to\infty\).  On every compact set excluding a fixed neighborhood of \(y_\varepsilon\), continuity and uniqueness give a strictly positive objective gap.  The change of variables \(x=hy\) and the positive rescaling in \((6)\) preserve this property.  Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(|h|\),
\[
 (\eta,h)\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}},
 \qquad x_\star(\eta,h)=h y_{\varepsilon_h}.                       \tag{11}
\]
This proves, in particular, the global localization that is needed rather than merely assuming a local stationary branch.

It remains to compute the branch.  Since \(y_{\varepsilon}\in(-d,d)\), its first-order condition is
\[
 0=g_\varepsilon'(y_\varepsilon)
 =\upsilon'(y_\varepsilon)
  +\varepsilon\frac{\tau'(y_\varepsilon)}{\tau(y_\varepsilon)^2}. \tag{12}
\]
The positive lower bound on \(\upsilon''\), together with \(\upsilon'(0)=0\), and the boundedness of \(\tau'/\tau^2\) near zero imply from \((12)\) that \(y_\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon)\).  Expanding \((12)\) with \((10)\) and \((3)\) therefore yields
\[
 0=\frac{2R}{L}y_\varepsilon
   -\frac{2M}{L^2}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),
\]
and hence
\[
 y_\varepsilon=\frac{M}{RL}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon).            \tag{13}
\]
Substituting \(\varepsilon_h=c/(\kappa^4h^4)\), \(M=\mu_a\mu_b\), \(R=v_av_b\), and \(L=(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b)\) into \((11)\)--\((13)\) gives
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)
 =\frac{c\mu_a\mu_b}
 {\kappa^4v_av_b(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b)}h^{-3}
 +o(h^{-3})
\]
as \(|h|\to\infty\), for either sign of \(h\).  In particular, \(x_\star(\eta,h)\to0\).  The argument varied only the deterministic scalar \(h\) in the limiting profile; it did not take a stochastic triangular-array limit.  It therefore establishes precisely the stated deterministic endpoint and makes no additional assertion for the bounded local-amplitude experiment \(\theta\in[-K,K]\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Separating the deterministic endpoint records how the profile approaches complete absorption without mixing an unbounded signal with the bounded local experiment. \textit{Gap.} The strong-factor endpoint in Cardoso, Ferman, and Fernandes (2026) does not provide this deterministic profile expansion. \textit{Consumer.} The endpoint checks that the bounded-local profile has the correct deterministic connection to complete absorption.

\begin{theorem}[thm:wald-failure]\label{thm:wald-failure}
At \((\eta,h)\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\), put
\[
B_\star=B_{\eta,h}\{x_\star(\eta,h)\},
\qquad s_\star=s_1(B_\star),
\]
and let \(u_\star,v_\star\in\mathbb R^2\) be unit left and right singular vectors for \(s_\star\), with first coordinates \(u_{\star,1}\) and \(v_{\star,1}\).  The top singular value is simple and \(s_\star>c^{1/4}\).  Define
\[
\ell_c(s)=\frac{s^4-c}{s^2(s^2+c)},
\qquad
r_c(s)=\frac{s^4-c}{s^2(s^2+1)},
\]
and
\[
a_D(\eta,h)=\sqrt{pq}
\left\{1-\ell_c(s_\star)u_{\star,1}^2\right\}^{1/2}
\left\{1-r_c(s_\star)v_{\star,1}^2\right\}^{1/2}>0.
\]
For every compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\), uniformly over \((\eta,h)\in\mathcal K\),
\[
\frac{\|\widetilde D\|_F}{\sqrt{NT}}\xrightarrow{p}a_D(\eta,h),
\qquad
\widehat\sigma_F\xrightarrow{p}\sigma,
\qquad
\Pr\{\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\in\mathrm{CI}_F\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus conventional full-sample IFE Wald coverage is strictly below \(1-\alpha\), indeed asymptotically zero, throughout compact subsets of the absorption phase.  This phase is relatively open and is nonempty exactly when
\[
\overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta).
\]
All conclusions concern only the bounded experiment \(h=\theta/\sigma\in[-\overline h,\overline h]\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\).  By the absorption law, every point of \(\mathcal K\) is strictly beyond the threshold and \(\widehat x_F\to_p x_\star\) uniformly.  In the sign-rotated representation of the corrected singleton theorem, the selected matrix is
\[
 M_\star=\begin{pmatrix}z_\star&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<z_\star<z_0,
\]
and all four displayed entries are positive.  The off-diagonal entry of \(M_\star M_\star^{\top}\) is \(z_\star c_0+b_0d_0>0\); hence its eigenvalues are distinct and the top singular value \(s_\star\) is simple.  Moreover, increasing the positive entry \(z\) strictly increases the Perron eigenvalue of \(M(z)M(z)^{\top}\).  Since the ATT-coordinate matrix at \(z=0\) is supercritical on \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\),
\[
 s_\star>s_1\{M(0)\}>c^{1/4}. \tag{1}
\]
Continuity and compactness supply uniform gaps from both the spectral edge and the second singular value on \(\mathcal K\).

Use the treatment-support vectors \(e_1,f_1\) from (2) in the proof of the uniform-profile theorem.  The strong-block singular equations show that the first \(r\) selected left and right singular spaces converge to \(U_0\) and \(V_0\).  The additional selected pair is the leading outlier pair from \(Z_{22}+P_{x_\star}\): replacing \(\widehat x_F\) by \(x_\star\) changes the perturbation by \(o_p(1)\) in operator norm.  For \(c\leq1\), the rectangular Gaussian singular-vector-overlap lemma therefore gives
\[
 |\langle\widehat u,e_1\rangle|^2
 \xrightarrow{p}\ell_c(s_\star)u_{\star,1}^2,
 \qquad
 |\langle\widehat v,f_1\rangle|^2
 \xrightarrow{p}r_c(s_\star)v_{\star,1}^2. \tag{2}
\]
The factors \(u_{\star,1}^2\) and \(v_{\star,1}^2\) arise by resolving \(e_1\) and \(f_1\) in the two population singular-vector bases; the cited lemma makes projections onto every distinct population singular direction vanish.  For \(c>1\), apply that lemma to \(c^{-1/2}(Z_{22}+P_{x_\star})^{\top}\).  The aspect ratio becomes \(1/c\), the spike becomes \(s_\star/\sqrt c\), and left and right vectors interchange.  Algebra gives
\[
 \frac{s_\star^4/c^2-1/c}{(s_\star^2/c)(s_\star^2/c+1/c)}
 =\frac{s_\star^4-c}{s_\star^2(s_\star^2+1)}=r_c(s_\star),
\]
\[
 \frac{s_\star^4/c^2-1/c}{(s_\star^2/c)(s_\star^2/c+1)}
 =\frac{s_\star^4-c}{s_\star^2(s_\star^2+c)}=\ell_c(s_\star). \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) holds for every \(c>0\), uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).

Because \(D=\sqrt{n_1t_1}\,e_1f_1^{\top}\), and because \(e_1\perp U_0\) and \(f_1\perp V_0\),
\[
 \frac{\|\widetilde D\|_F}{\sqrt{NT}}
 =\sqrt{p_Nq_T}\,
 \|(I-P_{\widehat U})e_1\|_2
 \|(I-P_{\widehat V})f_1\|_2. \tag{4}
\]
Substitution of (2) into (4), followed by the two share limits, proves
\[
 \frac{\|\widetilde D\|_F}{\sqrt{NT}}
 \xrightarrow{p}
 \sqrt{pq}\{1-\ell_c(s_\star)u_{\star,1}^2\}^{1/2}
 \{1-r_c(s_\star)v_{\star,1}^2\}^{1/2}
 =a_D(\eta,h). \tag{5}
\]
For every finite \(s>c^{1/4}\), direct subtraction gives
\[
 0<\ell_c(s)<1,
 \qquad 0<r_c(s)<1. \tag{6}
\]
Thus (5) is strictly positive, even if one of the first population singular-vector coordinates has absolute value one.  Compactness gives a common positive lower bound on \(\mathcal K\).
In particular, \(\Pr\{\|\widetilde D\|_F>0\}\to1\) uniformly on \(\mathcal K\), so the finite-width branch of the interval definition is used with probability tending to one.

We next prove variance consistency.  The absorption law implies
\(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma=O_p(m^{-1/2})\).  Put
\[
 R_{NT}=H-(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)D.
\]
The bounded-score, aspect, and share assumptions imply
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(R_{NT})\leq2,
 \qquad \|R_{NT}\|_F=O_p(\sqrt T). \tag{7}
\]
Choosing \(L=\Lambda F^{\top}\), whose rank is at most \(r\), in the profiled residual problem gives
\[
 \|Y-\widehat\beta_FD-\widehat L_F\|_F^2
 \leq\|E+R_{NT}\|_F^2
 =\|E\|_F^2+o_p(NT), \tag{8}
\]
because \(\|E\|_F=O_p(\sqrt{NT})\) and (7) makes both the cross term and \(\|R_{NT}\|_F^2\) negligible relative to \(NT\).

For the reverse inequality, write
\[
 Y-\widehat\beta_FD-\widehat L_F=E-K_{NT},
 \qquad
 K_{NT}=\widehat L_F-(\Lambda F^{\top}+R_{NT}).
\]
Then \(\operatorname{rank}(K_{NT})\leq2r+3\).  The singular-value variational characterization gives
\[
 \|E-K_{NT}\|_F^2
 \geq\|E\|_F^2-\sum_{j=1}^{2r+3}s_j(E)^2. \tag{9}
\]
The Gaussian law of large numbers and the zero-perturbation case of the rectangular outlier bound give
\[
 \frac{\|E\|_F^2}{NT}\xrightarrow{p}\sigma^2,
 \qquad
 \frac1{NT}\sum_{j=1}^{2r+3}s_j(E)^2
 =O_p(N^{-1})=o_p(1), \tag{10}
\]
uniformly under the noise-scale and aspect bounds.  Equations (8)--(10) imply
\[
 \frac{\|Y-\widehat\beta_FD-\widehat L_F\|_F^2}{NT}
 \xrightarrow{p}\sigma^2. \tag{11}
\]
Since \(r\) is fixed, the aspect bound gives \(\nu_F/(NT)\to1\), and \(\nu_F>0\) eventually.  The nonnegative-root definition therefore gives
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F\xrightarrow{p}\sigma. \tag{12}
\]

Finally define
\[
 b(\eta,h)=x_\star(\eta,h)-h\mu_a\mu_b.
\]
The phase classification makes \(b\) continuous and nonzero on \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\), so
\[
 \inf_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}|b(\eta,h)|>0. \tag{13}
\]
The absorption law yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 \xrightarrow{p}b(\eta,h), \tag{14}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).  On the other hand, (5), (12), and \(NT\asymp m^2\) yield
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m}{\sigma}
 \frac{z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat\sigma_F}{\|\widetilde D\|_F}
 =O_p(m^{-1/2})\longrightarrow0. \tag{15}
\]
If \(\mathrm{CI}_F\) contains \(\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\), the absolute value on the left of (14) is at most the quantity in (15).  Equations (13)--(15) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\in\mathrm{CI}_F\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{16}
\]
The absorption law already proves that \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\) is relatively open and is nonempty if and only if
\(\overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\).  This completes every asserted conclusion within the bounded experiment.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Strictly beyond the same threshold, the order-\(m^{-1/2}\) absorption bias dominates an order-\(m^{-1}\) conventional standard error. \textit{Gap.} Generic weak-factor results do not provide this rank-one treatment overlap formula or its exact nonempty phase. \textit{Consumer.} It converts the phase law into uniform zero coverage for conventional full-sample IFE Wald inference on every compact absorbed subregion.

\begin{lemma}[lem:baseline-mask-feasible]\label{lem:baseline-mask-feasible}
For all sufficiently large \(N,T\), \(G_F\succ0\), and \(\Lambda F^{\top}=(\Lambda G_F^{1/2})(FG_F^{-1/2})^{\top}\) satisfies \(T^{-1}(FG_F^{-1/2})^{\top}(FG_F^{-1/2})=I_r\), \(\max_i\|(\Lambda G_F^{1/2})_{i\cdot}\|_2<C_A\), and \(\max_t\|(FG_F^{-1/2})_{t\cdot}\|_2<C_B\). Hence the true baseline factorization belongs to the masked fitting class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 A^\circ=\Lambda G_F^{1/2},\qquad B^\circ=F G_F^{-1/2}.
\]
The factor-Gram envelope gives \(\lambda_{\min}(G_F)\geq\underline f>0\), so the two symmetric square roots in this display exist.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 A^\circ(B^\circ)^{\top}
 =\Lambda G_F^{1/2}G_F^{-1/2}F^{\top}
 =\Lambda F^{\top}
\]
and
\[
 T^{-1}(B^\circ)^{\top}B^\circ
 =G_F^{-1/2}(T^{-1}F^{\top}F)G_F^{-1/2}
 =I_r.
\]
For every row \(i\), the loading-row bound and the upper factor-Gram envelope imply
\[
 \|A^\circ_{i\cdot}\|_2
 \leq \|\Lambda_{i\cdot}\|_2\|G_F^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq \overline\ell\sqrt{\overline f}<C_A.
\]
Likewise, for every row \(t\), the factor-row bound and the lower factor-Gram envelope imply
\[
 \|B^\circ_{t\cdot}\|_2
 \leq \|F_{t\cdot}\|_2\|G_F^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq \frac{\overline g}{\sqrt{\underline f}}<C_B.
\]
Thus \((A^\circ,B^\circ)\) is feasible, with fixed positive slack in both row-radius restrictions.  In particular, it is feasible for all arrays satisfying the stated envelopes, hence certainly for all sufficiently large \(N,T\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The normalization and radii must contain the data-generating baseline rather than merely define a compact optimization domain. \textit{Gap.} Bai and Ng (2021) normalize factors for tall-wide rotation, but feasibility under these explicit deterministic radii must be recorded. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma validates the optimization domain of the internal masked benchmark before its coverage theorem is invoked.

\begin{theorem}[thm:masked-coverage]\label{thm:masked-coverage}
Uniformly over \(\theta\in[-K,K]\), \(\sqrt{n_1t_1}(\widehat{\operatorname{ATT}}_M-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})/\widehat\sigma_M\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\), \(\widehat\sigma_M\xrightarrow{p}\sigma\), and \(\inf_{\theta\in[-K,K]}\Pr\{\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\in\mathrm{CI}_M\}\to1-\alpha\). In addition, \((n_1t_1)^{-1}\sum_{i:d_i=1}\sum_{t:qvec_t=1}\widehat L_{M,it}=o_p((n_1t_1)^{-1/2})\) uniformly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 I_1=\{i:d_i=1\},\quad I_0=\{i:d_i=0\},\qquad
 J_1=\{t:qvec_t=1\},\quad J_0=\{t:qvec_t=0\}.
\]
The share envelope and the proportional-asymptotics envelope imply that the four cardinalities \(|I_1|,|I_0|,|J_1|,|J_0|\) are respectively of orders \(N,N,T,T\), uniformly along the triangular array.  Write \(P_{\Omega_0}\) for entrywise restriction to \(\Omega_0\), with zeros outside \(\Omega_0\), and put \(L^\circ=\Lambda F^{\top}\).

First observe that the training law contains no treatment signal.  If \((i,t)\in\Omega_0\), then \(d_iqvec_t=0\).  Consequently
\[
 D_{it}=0,\qquad
 H_{it}=\delta(d_i a_i)(qvec_t b_t)=0,\qquad
 Y_{it}=L^\circ_{it}+E_{it}.
\tag{1}
\]
This identity holds for every value of \(\theta\).

We next establish the only rate about the masked fit that will be needed.  Use the feasible normalization from the preceding lemma,
\[
 A^\circ=\Lambda G_F^{1/2},\qquad B^\circ=F G_F^{-1/2},\qquad
 L^\circ=A^\circ(B^\circ)^{\top}.
\tag{2}
\]
Subscripts \(0\) and \(1\) on these factor matrices denote restriction to the corresponding index sets.  The restricted-Gram envelopes and the factor-Gram envelope yield fixed constants \(c_A,c_B>0\) such that, eventually,
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\bigl((A^\circ_0)^{\top}A^\circ_0\bigr)
 \geq c_A N,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}\bigl((B^\circ_0)^{\top}B^\circ_0\bigr)
 \geq c_B T.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 N^{-1}(A^\circ_0)^{\top}A^\circ_0
 =G_F^{1/2}(N^{-1}\Lambda^{\top}M_d\Lambda)G_F^{1/2}
\]
has smallest eigenvalue at least \(\underline f\,\underline\lambda_0\), while
\[
 T^{-1}(B^\circ_0)^{\top}B^\circ_0
 =G_F^{-1/2}(T^{-1}F^{\top}M_{qvec}F)G_F^{-1/2}
\]
has smallest eigenvalue at least \(\underline f_0/\overline f\).

Let \(\widehat L_M=\widehat A\widehat B^{\top}\) be any global minimizer in the masked fitting class, in particular the lexicographically selected one, and define
\[
 \Delta_L=\widehat L_M-L^\circ,\qquad Z=P_{\Omega_0}E.
\]
Existence follows because the feasible parameter set is nonempty by the preceding lemma, closed, and bounded, and the criterion is continuous.  Comparing the minimizer with the feasible pair in (2), and using (1), gives the basic inequality
\[
 \|P_{\Omega_0}\Delta_L\|_F^2
 \leq 2\langle Z,P_{\Omega_0}\Delta_L\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(\operatorname{rank}(\Delta_L)\leq2r\) and deleting the rectangle \(I_1\times J_1\) adds at most another \(2r\) to rank,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(P_{\Omega_0}\Delta_L)\leq4r.
\tag{5}
\]
For completeness, a one-quarter-net argument on the two Euclidean unit spheres gives
\[
 \|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_p\bigl(\sigma(\sqrt N+\sqrt T)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]
To see this directly, for fixed unit vectors \(u,v\), the variable \(u^{\top}Zv\) is centered Gaussian with variance at most \(\sigma^2\); union bounding its Gaussian exponential tail over nets of cardinalities at most \(9^N\) and \(9^T\), and then using the deterministic net approximation for the operator norm, proves (6).  Hölder's inequality for the nuclear and operator norms, followed by (5), now turns (4) into
\[
 \|P_{\Omega_0}\Delta_L\|_F
 \leq4\sqrt r\,\|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =O_p(\sqrt m),
\tag{7}
\]
where the last equality uses the noise-scale and aspect-ratio envelopes.

We record the factor consequence of (3) and (7), including its derivation.  The complete control-row block \(I_0\times\{1,\ldots,T\}\) is observed.  If \(P_{\widehat B}\) is the orthogonal projector onto the columns of \(\widehat B\), then
\[
 \|A^\circ_0(B^\circ)^{\top}(I-P_{\widehat B})\|_F
 \leq \|P_{\Omega_0}\Delta_L\|_F.
\]
Using the first bound in (3) and dividing by \(\sqrt T\) shows that the sine distance between the column spaces of \(B^\circ\) and \(\widehat B\) is \(O_p(m^{-1/2})\).  Similarly, the complete pre-period block \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\times J_0\), together with the second bound in (3), shows that the sine distance between the column spaces of \(A^\circ\) and \(\widehat A\) is \(O_p(m^{-1/2})\).  Both fitted factor matrices have full column rank with probability tending to one, since otherwise either projection inequality would contradict (3) and (7).  Orthogonal Procrustes alignment, followed by multiplication of the two observed block errors by the inverses of the two Grams in (3), therefore supplies an orthogonal matrix \(R\) for which
\[
 \|\widehat A-A^\circ R\|_F=O_p(1),
 \qquad
 \|\widehat B-B^\circ R\|_F=O_p(1).
\tag{8}
\]
Here is the elementary scaling in the last step.  The normalized sine error is \(O_p(m^{-1/2})\), multiplication by \(\sqrt N\) or \(\sqrt T\) gives an \(O_p(1)\) factor error under proportional asymptotics, and the remaining coefficient error is at most the observed matrix error in (7) divided by the relevant smallest singular value, which is of order \(\sqrt m\) by (3).  Thus (8) follows without a rate theorem external to this proof.

The deterministic row-radius slack proved in the preceding lemma makes the constraints asymptotically inactive.  We justify this rather than assume it.  Conditional least squares for any row uses at least a fixed fraction of the opposite panel dimension by the share envelope and (3).  Decompose its score into the score at \((A^\circ R,B^\circ R)\) and the remainder in (8).  Gaussian exponential tails and a union bound over \(O(m)\) rows give
\[
 \max_i\|\widehat A_{i\cdot}-(A^\circ R)_{i\cdot}\|_2
 +\max_t\|\widehat B_{t\cdot}-(B^\circ R)_{t\cdot}\|_2
 =O_p\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log m}{m}}+\frac1{\sqrt m}\right)=o_p(1).
\tag{9}
\]
For example, the fixed part of an \(A\)-row score is Gaussian with covariance bounded by a constant times \(T\), so its maximum divided by a Gram of order \(T\) is \(O_p(\sqrt{\log m/m})\); the remainder is bounded by \(\|E_{i\cdot}\|_2\|\widehat B-B^\circ R\|_F/T=O_p(m^{-1/2})\), uniformly, using (8) and the same Gaussian union bound.  The transposed calculation gives the \(B\)-row bound.  Since the two true row bounds are separated from \(C_A,C_B\) by fixed positive constants, (9) proves inactivity with probability tending to one, uniformly over all global minimizers.

On this event, the usual unconstrained rank-\(r\) normal equations are valid.  The normalization of \(\widehat B\) causes no restriction here: every sufficiently small rank-\(r\) matrix perturbation can be renormalized by an invertible right change of coordinates, and (9) keeps the resulting factorization inside both row-radius bounds.  Hence
\[
 \widehat A_1
 =Y_{10}\widehat B_0(\widehat B_0^{\top}\widehat B_0)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \widehat B_1
 =Y_{01}^{\top}\widehat A_0(\widehat A_0^{\top}\widehat A_0)^{-1},
\tag{10}
\]
where \(Y_{10}=Y_{I_1,J_0}\) and \(Y_{01}=Y_{I_0,J_1}\).  Equations (3) and (8) also imply
\[
 \| (\widehat B_0^{\top}\widehat B_0)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_p(T^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \| (\widehat A_0^{\top}\widehat A_0)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_p(N^{-1}).
\tag{11}
\]

Exact orthogonality now produces the sharper aggregate rate.  By left-support orthogonality,
\[
 n_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{I_1}^{\top}A^\circ_1
 =n_1^{-1}d^{\top}\Lambda G_F^{1/2}=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus, with \(\overline E_{10}=n_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{I_1}^{\top}E_{10}\), the first equation in (10) gives
\[
 n_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{I_1}^{\top}\widehat A_1
 =\overline E_{10}\widehat B_0(\widehat B_0^{\top}\widehat B_0)^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]
The vector \(\overline E_{10}B^\circ_0R\) is centered Gaussian with covariance
\[
 \frac{\sigma^2}{n_1}R^{\top}(B^\circ_0)^{\top}B^\circ_0R=O(1),
\]
while \(\|\overline E_{10}\|_2=O_p(1)\) and \(\|\widehat B_0-B^\circ_0R\|_F=O_p(1)\) by (8).  No independence is needed for the latter product.  Combining these facts with (11) proves
\[
 \left\|n_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{I_1}^{\top}\widehat A_1\right\|_2=O_p(T^{-1}).
\tag{14}
\]
The right-support orthogonality similarly gives
\[
 t_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{J_1}^{\top}B^\circ_1
 =t_1^{-1}qvec^{\top}F G_F^{-1/2}=0.
\tag{15}
\]
Applying the transposed argument to the second equation in (10) yields
\[
 \left\|t_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{J_1}^{\top}\widehat B_1\right\|_2=O_p(N^{-1}).
\tag{16}
\]
Because the mean of a rank-\(r\) product over a rectangle factors into the product of its two factor means, (14)--(16) imply
\[
 \frac1{n_1t_1}\sum_{i\in I_1}\sum_{t\in J_1}\widehat L_{M,it}
 =\left(n_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{I_1}^{\top}\widehat A_1\right)
   \left(t_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{J_1}^{\top}\widehat B_1\right)^{\top}
 =O_p((NT)^{-1})
 =o_p((n_1t_1)^{-1/2}).
\tag{17}
\]

It remains to verify studentization.  Let \(R_M=\sum_{(i,t)\in\Omega_0}(Y_{it}-\widehat L_{M,it})^2\).  From (4)--(7), (5), and the same nuclear-norm inequality,
\[
 |R_M-\|Z\|_F^2|=O_p(m).
\tag{18}
\]
The share envelope gives \(|\Omega_0|=NT-n_1t_1\asymp NT\), whereas fixed \(r\) gives \(r(N+T-r)=O(m)\).  Therefore \(\nu_M>0\) eventually and \(\nu_M/|\Omega_0|\to1\).  Moreover, Gaussianity gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\|Z\|_F^2}{\sigma^2|\Omega_0|}\right]=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{\|Z\|_F^2}{\sigma^2|\Omega_0|}\right)=\frac2{|\Omega_0|}\to0.
\]
Equation (18), divided by \(\nu_M\), consequently proves
\[
 \widehat\sigma_M^2\xrightarrow{p}\sigma^2,
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_M\xrightarrow{p}\sigma,
\tag{19}
\]
where the second conclusion uses \(\sigma\geq\underline\sigma>0\).

Finally, exact orthogonality gives an exact expression for the treated-post mean of the data.  Namely,
\[
 \frac1{n_1t_1}\sum_{i\in I_1,t\in J_1}L^\circ_{it}=0
\]
by either (12) or (15), and the definitions of the supported moments give
\[
 \frac1{n_1t_1}\sum_{i\in I_1,t\in J_1}H_{it}
 =\delta\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}.
\]
Hence, writing \(\overline E_{11}=(n_1t_1)^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_1,t\in J_1}E_{it}\),
\[
 \widehat{\operatorname{ATT}}_M-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}
 =\overline E_{11}
 -\frac1{n_1t_1}\sum_{i\in I_1,t\in J_1}\widehat L_{M,it}.
\tag{20}
\]
The first term satisfies \(\sqrt{n_1t_1}\,\overline E_{11}/\sigma\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) exactly.  Equations (17), (19), and (20), followed by Slutsky's argument, prove
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{n_1t_1}(\widehat{\operatorname{ATT}}_M-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\widehat\sigma_M}
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{21}
\]
All random quantities on the right side of (20), including the masked fit and its residual variance, have a finite-sample law independent of \(\theta\) by (1); the \(\theta\)-dependent treated-post mean has been removed exactly through \(\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\).  Thus every remainder bound above is the same for every \(\theta\in[-K,K]\), and (17), (19), and (21) hold uniformly on that compact interval.  Taking the probability of the event that the absolute value in (21) is at most \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\), and using continuity of the standard-normal distribution, gives
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in[-K,K]}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\in\mathrm{CI}_M\}\longrightarrow1-\alpha,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} As an internal control, masking removes the heterogeneous treatment factor from every training cell, so uniformity spans the bounded absorption transition. \textit{Gap.} Bai and Ng (2021) give pointwise block-imputation normality, not this compact-amplitude result paired with full-sample absorption. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies an internal benchmark showing that the full-sample failure is caused by fitting the treated-post spike rather than by the Gaussian oracle experiment itself.

\begin{proposition}[prop:zero-heterogeneity-reduction]\label{prop:zero-heterogeneity-reduction}
At \(\theta=0\), \(h=0\), \(\mathcal S(\eta,0)=\{0\}\), \(\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)/\sigma\xrightarrow{p}0\), and \(\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}=\gamma\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At \(\theta=0\), the positive noise-scale assumption implies \(h=0\), while the target definition gives
\[
 \operatorname{ATT}_{NT}=\gamma. \tag{1}
\]
The local-spike profile becomes
\[
 B_{\eta,0}(x)=-\kappa xe_{11},
 \qquad s_1\{B_{\eta,0}(x)\}=\kappa|x|. \tag{2}
\]
If \(\kappa|x|\leq c^{1/4}\), then the outlier-map definition gives
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=\kappa^2x^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2,
\]
which is uniquely minimized at \(x=0\) on that region.  If \(\kappa|x|>c^{1/4}\), then
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=-(1+c)-\frac{c}{\kappa^2x^2}
 >-(1+c)-\sqrt c
 >-(1+c)-2\sqrt c=\Phi_{\eta,0}(0). \tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal S(\eta,0)=\{0\}. \tag{4}
\]
The uniform-profile theorem applied at \(h=0\) yields
\[
 \widehat x_F=\frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)}\sigma\xrightarrow{p}0. \tag{5}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and (5) prove all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This sanity reduction prevents spurious absorption bias when heterogeneous treatment variation is absent. \textit{Gap.} Bai (2009) supplies the homogeneous-effect benchmark but not its embedding as the zero-signal point of a local profile. \textit{Consumer.} The theoretical sensitivity map reduces to the familiar homogeneous-effect benchmark at zero amplitude.

\begin{lemma}[lem:rectangular-gaussian-outlier]\label{lem:rectangular-gaussian-outlier}
Suppose \(0<c\leq 1\), \(N/T\to c\), and \(Z_{NT}=E/(\sigma\sqrt T)\).  Let \(P_{NT}\) be deterministic of fixed rank, independent of \(Z_{NT}\), and suppose its largest singular value converges to \(s\).  Then
\[
s_1(Z_{NT}+P_{NT})^2\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
(1+\sqrt c)^2,&s\leq c^{1/4},\\
s^2+1+c+c/s^2,&s>c^{1/4},
\end{cases}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:rectangular-gaussian-singular-vector-overlap]\label{lem:rectangular-gaussian-singular-vector-overlap}
Suppose \(0<c\leq1\), \(N/T\to c\), and \(Z_{NT}=E/(\sigma\sqrt T)\).  Let a deterministic fixed-rank perturbation have a simple largest singular value \(s>c^{1/4}\), with corresponding unit left and right singular vectors \(u\) and \(v\).  If \(\widehat u\) and \(\widehat v\) are the singular vectors associated with the resulting largest outlier, then
\[
|\langle\widehat u,u\rangle|^2\longrightarrow
\frac{s^4-c}{s^2(s^2+c)},\qquad
|\langle\widehat v,v\rangle|^2\longrightarrow
\frac{s^4-c}{s^2(s^2+1)}
\]
almost surely, while their projections on population singular-vector spaces belonging to distinct spikes converge to zero.  If every perturbation singular value is strictly below \(c^{1/4}\), the projections of an edge singular vector on the fixed perturbation spaces converge to zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:global-profile-singleton]\label{thm:global-profile-singleton}
Suppose \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) and \(h\in\mathbb R\).  Then \((\eta,h)\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}}\): the set \(\mathcal S(\eta,h)\) is always a separated singleton.  Its member \(x_\star(\eta,h)\) is given by the following explicit scalar map.  If \(h=0\), then \(x_\star(\eta,0)=0\).  If \(h\neq0\), define
\[
 s=\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b),\quad
 z_0=\kappa|h\mu_a\mu_b|,\quad
 b_0=\kappa|h\mu_a|\sqrt{v_b},\quad
 c_0=\kappa|h\mu_b|\sqrt{v_a},\quad
 d_0=\kappa|h|\sqrt{v_av_b},
\]
and, for \(0\leq z\leq z_0\), put
\[
 w=z_0-z,\qquad Q(z)=z^2+b_0^2+c_0^2+d_0^2,
\]
\[
 \tau(z)=\frac{Q(z)+\sqrt{Q(z)^2-4d_0^2w^2}}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal R(z)=\frac{d_0^2w\tau(z)\{\tau(z)+zw\}}
 {z\tau(z)+d_0^2w}.
\]
Let \(z_\star=0\) when \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\); when \(\tau(0)^2>c\), let \(z_\star\) be the unique root of \(\mathcal R(z)=c\) in \((0,z_0)\).  Then
\[
 \mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x_\star(\eta,h)\},\qquad
 x_\star(\eta,h)=\frac{s}{\kappa}(z_0-z_\star).
\]
The map \((\eta,h)\mapsto x_\star(\eta,h)\) is continuous.  For every compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \inf_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}\ \inf_{|x-x_\star(\eta,h)|\geq\varepsilon}
 \left\{\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)-\Phi_{\eta,h}\bigl(x_\star(\eta,h)\bigr)\right\}>0. \tag{8}
\]
Consequently, let \((\eta_n,h_n)\in\mathcal K\), let \(C_n:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be random criteria, and let \(\widehat x_n\) be any measurable global minimizer.  If \(\widehat x_n\) is uniformly tight and, for every finite \(M\),
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 \{C_n(x)-C_n(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n))\}
 -\{\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x)-\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n))\}
 \right|\xrightarrow{p}0,
\]
then \(\widehat x_n-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\xrightarrow{p}0\).  Thus no coefficient-indexed next-order noise process is needed to break a first-order tie.

The critical selection is one-sided but remains unique.  At a point with \(h\neq0\) and \(\tau(0)^2=c\), consider any admissible path indexed by \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) for which
\[
 \Delta_\epsilon=\tau_\epsilon(0)^2-c_\epsilon
 =\epsilon\dot\Delta+o(\epsilon).
\]
Writing \(\mathcal R_0'(0)<0\) for the derivative at the limiting point, one has
\[
 z_{\star,\epsilon}
 =\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}+o(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 x_{\star,\epsilon}-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
 =-\frac{s}{\kappa}\epsilon
 \frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}+o(\epsilon).
\]
In particular, opposite admissible \(m^{-1/4}\) drifts can change this local directional derivative, but both selections converge to the same critical minimizer; they cannot produce different separated limiting selections with identical limiting nuisance coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\), the coordinates \(c,p,q,v_a,v_b\) are strictly positive, \(\mu_a\mu_b\neq0\), and
\[
 \kappa=\sqrt{\max\{c,1\}pq}>0.
\]
First suppose \(h\neq0\).  Define the orthogonal sign matrices
\[
 U=\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{sgn}(\mu_a),1\},
 \qquad
 V=\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_b),\operatorname{sgn}(h)\}.
\]
With \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b)\), direct substitution in the definition of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\) gives
\[
 UB_{\eta,h}(x)V
 =\begin{pmatrix}z&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad z=z_0-s\kappa x,
\]
where
\[
 z_0=\kappa|h\mu_a\mu_b|,
 \quad b_0=\kappa|h\mu_a|\sqrt{v_b},
 \quad c_0=\kappa|h\mu_b|\sqrt{v_a},
 \quad d_0=\kappa|h|\sqrt{v_av_b}.
\]
All four displayed scalars are strictly positive, and
\[
 \frac{b_0c_0}{d_0}=z_0.
\]
The division is valid because \(d_0>0\).  Left and right multiplication by orthogonal matrices preserves the Frobenius norm and all singular values.  Moreover, \(x\mapsto z_0-s\kappa x\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb R\), since \(s\kappa\neq0\).  Hence \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\) is exactly the criterion in \(\textnormal{lem:scalar-profile-singleton}\), with \(\rho=c\), after this affine change of coordinate.  That lemma shows that its unique global minimizer is \(z_\star=0\) when \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\), while, when \(\tau(0)^2>c\), it is the unique \(z_\star\in(0,z_0)\) satisfying \(\mathcal R(z_\star)=c\).  Inverting the coordinate change yields
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)=\frac{s}{\kappa}(z_0-z_\star).
\]
In particular, since \(sz_0/\kappa=h\mu_a\mu_b\), the first case gives \(x_\star=h\mu_a\mu_b\), whereas in the second case \(x_\star\) lies strictly between \(0\) and \(h\mu_a\mu_b\) in their common signed direction.

If \(h=0\), then \(B_{\eta,0}(x)=-\kappa xe_{11}\).  On the spectral-edge branch \(\kappa^2x^2\leq\sqrt c\),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=\kappa^2x^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2,
\]
which is uniquely minimized at \(x=0\).  On the outlier branch \(\kappa^2x^2>\sqrt c\),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=-(1+c)-\frac{c}{\kappa^2x^2}
 >-(1+\sqrt c)^2=\Phi_{\eta,0}(0),
\]
where the strict inequality follows from \(c/(\kappa^2x^2)<\sqrt c\).  Thus \(x_\star(\eta,0)=0\), uniquely.

We next establish continuity and uniform separation.  The map \((\eta,h,x)\mapsto\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\) is continuous: the entries of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\) and its singular values are continuous, and the two formulas defining \(\psi_c\) agree when \(s=c^{1/4}\).  Consequently, convergence of the parameters implies uniform convergence of the profiles on every bounded set of \(x\)'s.

Let \(\lambda_1(\eta,h,x)\geq\lambda_2(\eta,h,x)\geq0\) be the squared singular values of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\), and write
\[
 d_0^2=\kappa^2h^2v_av_b,
 \qquad
 S=\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b).
\]
The rank-one identity for \(A_\eta\) gives
\[
 \det B_{\eta,h}(x)=-\kappa^2xh\sqrt{v_av_b}.
\]
On any compact parameter set, \(\kappa\) is bounded above and bounded away from zero, all other coordinates are bounded, and therefore
\[
 \lambda_1+\lambda_2
 =\|B_{\eta,h}(x)\|_F^2
 =\kappa^2x^2+O(|x|+1),
 \qquad
 \lambda_1\lambda_2=\kappa^4x^2h^2v_av_b,
\]
uniformly in the parameters.  The first relation implies \(\lambda_1\geq Cx^2\) for sufficiently large \(|x|\), with \(C>0\) uniform on the compact set.  The product relation then first gives \(\lambda_2=O(1)\), and substitution back into the sum and product gives
\[
 \lambda_1(\eta,h,x)\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \lambda_2(\eta,h,x)\longrightarrow d_0^2
 \quad\text{as }|x|\longrightarrow\infty, \tag{1}
\]
uniformly on that compact set.  The outlier branch of \(\psi_c\) is therefore active in the tails, and (1) yields
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)longrightarrow d_0^2-(1+c). \tag{2}
\]
At \(x=0\), the matrix \(B_{\eta,h}(0)=\kappa hA_\eta\) has rank at most one, and its only possibly nonzero squared singular value is \(S\).  Subtracting \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)\) from the limit in (2) gives
\[
 G(\eta,h)=
 \begin{cases}
 d_0^2+2\sqrt c-S,&S\leq\sqrt c,\\
 d_0^2+c/S,&S>\sqrt c.
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
The second branch uses only \(S>\sqrt c>0\), so its division is valid.  The two branches agree at \(S=\sqrt c\).  Moreover, on the first branch \(G\geq d_0^2+\sqrt c>0\), and positivity on the second branch is immediate.  Hence continuity, compactness, and the uniform convergence in (2) imply that, for every compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\), there are \(M<\infty\) and \(g>0\) such that
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\geq\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)+g,
 \qquad (\eta,h)\in\mathcal K,\quad |x|>M. \tag{4}
\]

Let \((\eta_n,h_n)\to(\eta,h)\) and set \(x_n=x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\).  Applying (4) to the compact closure of this convergent parameter sequence shows that \((x_n)\) is bounded, because \(\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)\leq\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(0)\).  Along any subsequence for which \(x_n\to\bar x\), local uniform convergence and
\[
 \Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)\leq\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(y),
 \qquad y\in\mathbb R,
\]
give \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(\bar x)\leq\Phi_{\eta,h}(y)\) for every \(y\in\mathbb R\).  The already proved uniqueness forces \(\bar x=x_\star(\eta,h)\).  Every convergent subsequence has this same limit, so \(x_n\to x_\star(\eta,h)\).  This proves continuity, including at \(h=0\) and at the spectral edge.

Fix a compact \(\mathcal K\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).  If the asserted uniform separation failed, there would exist \((\eta_n,h_n)\in\mathcal K\) and \(x_n\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 |x_n-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)|\geq\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)-
 \Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}\{x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{5}
\]
If \((x_n)\) were bounded along a subsequence, compactness, continuity of \(x_\star\), and local uniform convergence would produce a point distinct from the unique limiting minimizer but having the same criterion value.  If \((x_n)\) were unbounded along a subsequence, (4) and \(\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}\{x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\}\leq\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(0)\) would bound the difference in (5) below by \(g\).  Both alternatives are contradictions.  This proves (8), and hence the asserted membership in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}}\).

For the stochastic consequence, let \(g_\varepsilon>0\) denote the deterministic gap in (8).  Uniform tightness supplies a finite \(M\) such that \(\widehat x_n\in[-M,M]\) with probability tending to one.  Continuity and compactness bound \(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\), so, after enlarging \(M\), these deterministic minimizers also belong to \([-M,M]\).  On the event that the centered criterion approximation is less than \(g_\varepsilon/2\) uniformly on this interval, any \(x\) in the interval with \(|x-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)|\geq\varepsilon\) satisfies
\[
 C_n(x)-C_n\{x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\}>g_\varepsilon/2>0.
\]
Such an \(x\) cannot be a global minimizer.  It follows that
\[
 \Pr\{|\widehat x_n-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)|\geq\varepsilon\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is the claimed convergence in probability and shows that no first-order tie-breaking process is needed.

It remains to prove the critical expansion.  Fix a critical point with \(h\neq0\) and \(\tau(0)^2=c\).  At \(z=0\), let \(t=\tau(0)\), let \(u\) be the smaller squared singular value of \(M(0)\), and put \(w=z_0\).  Positivity of \(b_0,c_0,d_0,w\) gives
\[
 t+u=Q(0),
 \qquad
 tu=d_0^2w^2.
\]
The off-diagonal entry \(c_0d_0\) of \(M(0)^{\top}M(0)\) is nonzero, so its two eigenvalues are distinct; in particular, \(t>u>0\).  Differentiating
\[
 \tau(z)^2-Q(z)\tau(z)+d_0^2w(z)^2=0
\]
at zero, using \(Q'(0)=0\) and \(w'(0)=-1\), gives
\[
 \tau'(0)=\frac{2d_0^2w}{t-u}. \tag{6}
\]
All denominators in (6) are nonzero by the preceding positivity and strict-eigenvalue statements.  Since \(\mathcal R(0)=t^2\), logarithmic differentiation of \(\mathcal R\), followed by (6) and \(tu=d_0^2w^2\), yields
\[
 \frac{\mathcal R'(0)}{\mathcal R(0)}
 =\frac{2\tau'(0)}t+\frac wt-\frac{t}{d_0^2w}
 =\frac{4u}{w(t-u)}-\frac{w(t-u)}{tu}. \tag{7}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 M(0)^{\top}M(0)
 =\begin{pmatrix}c_0^2&c_0d_0\\c_0d_0&b_0^2+d_0^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Rayleigh quotient at the first coordinate vector is \(c_0^2\), and that vector is not an eigenvector because \(c_0d_0\neq0\).  The smaller eigenvalue therefore satisfies \(u<c_0^2\).  Consequently,
\[
 (t-u)^2-4d_0^2u
 =(c_0^2-b_0^2-d_0^2)^2+4d_0^2(c_0^2-u)>0. \tag{8}
\]
Using \(tu=d_0^2w^2\), inequality (8) is equivalent to the right-hand side of (7) being negative.  Since \(\mathcal R(0)=t^2>0\), this proves
\[
 \mathcal R'_0(0)<0. \tag{9}
\]

Now take an admissible path satisfying
\[
 \Delta_\epsilon=\tau_\epsilon(0)^2-c_\epsilon
 =\epsilon\dot\Delta+o(\epsilon).
\]
Because \(\mathcal R_\epsilon(0)=\tau_\epsilon(0)^2\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\epsilon(0)-c_\epsilon=\Delta_\epsilon. \tag{10}
\]
The exact selection rule gives \(z_{\star,\epsilon}=0\) when \(\Delta_\epsilon\leq0\).  When \(\Delta_\epsilon>0\), it gives \(\mathcal R_\epsilon(z_{\star,\epsilon})=c_\epsilon\).  Continuity of the minimizer, already proved above, implies \(z_{\star,\epsilon}\to0\).  The mean-value theorem applied to (10), together with continuity of \(\mathcal R_\epsilon'\) near the critical point and (9), therefore gives
\[
 z_{\star,\epsilon}
 =\frac{(\Delta_\epsilon)_+}{-\mathcal R'_0(0)}+o(\epsilon)
 =\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R'_0(0)}+o(\epsilon). \tag{11}
\]
The limiting \(h,\mu_a,\mu_b\) are nonzero, so their signs are locally constant along the admissible path, and \(\kappa_\epsilon\to\kappa>0\).  The exact inverse-coordinate identity
\[
 x_{\star,\epsilon}-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
 =-\frac{s_\epsilon}{\kappa_\epsilon}z_{\star,\epsilon}
\]
and (11) establish
\[
 x_{\star,\epsilon}-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
 =-\frac{s}{\kappa}\epsilon
 \frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R'_0(0)}+o(\epsilon).
\]
For \(\epsilon=m^{-1/4}\), opposite admissible drifts can therefore alter this vanishing directional term, but both selections converge to the same unique critical minimizer.  They cannot yield different separated limiting selections with the same limiting nuisance coordinates.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:scalar-profile-singleton]\label{lem:scalar-profile-singleton}
Let \(\rho,b_0,c_0,d_0>0\), let \(z_0=b_0c_0/d_0\), and set
\[
 M(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z&b_0\\ c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \varphi_\rho(z)=\|M(z)\|_F^2-\Psi_\rho\{s_1(M(z))\},
\]
where
\[
 \Psi_\rho(s)=(1+\sqrt\rho)^2\mathbf 1\{s\leq \rho^{1/4}\}
 +\left(s^2+1+\rho+\frac{\rho}{s^2}\right)\mathbf 1\{s>\rho^{1/4}\}.
\]
For \(0\leq z\leq z_0\), write \(w=z_0-z\),
\[
 Q(z)=z^2+b_0^2+c_0^2+d_0^2,\qquad
 \tau(z)=\frac{Q(z)+\sqrt{Q(z)^2-4d_0^2w^2}}{2},
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal R(z)=\frac{d_0^2w\tau(z)\{\tau(z)+zw\}}
 {z\tau(z)+d_0^2w}.
\]
Then \(\varphi_\rho\) has exactly one global minimizer.  It is \(z_\star=0\) if \(\tau(0)^2\leq\rho\).  If \(\tau(0)^2>\rho\), it is the unique \(z_\star\in(0,z_0)\) satisfying \(\mathcal R(z_\star)=\rho\).  In either case, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \inf_{|z-z_\star|\geq\varepsilon}
 \{\varphi_\rho(z)-\varphi_\rho(z_\star)\}>0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\tau(z)=s_1\{M(z)\}^2\) and \(u(z)=s_2\{M(z)\}^2\).  The trace and determinant identities for the two eigenvalues of \(M(z)M(z)^{\top}\) give
\[
 \tau+u=Q(z),\qquad \tau u=d_0^2(z-z_0)^2.
\]
This proves the displayed formula for \(\tau\), and, whenever \(\tau>0\), gives \(u=d_0^2(z-z_0)^2/\tau\).  Define
\[
 G_\rho(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&t\leq\sqrt\rho,\\
 t+\rho/t-2\sqrt\rho,&t>\sqrt\rho.
 \end{cases}
\]
Direct substitution in the definition of \(\Psi_\rho\) yields
\[
 \varphi_\rho(z)=z^2+b_0^2+c_0^2+d_0^2-(1+\sqrt\rho)^2-G_\rho\{\tau(z)\}. \tag{1}
\]
The function \(G_\rho\) is nondecreasing, is continuously differentiable, and has derivative zero below the edge and \(1-\rho/t^2\) above the edge.

We first localize the minimum.  If \(q>0\), then \(Q(-q)=Q(q)\), whereas
\[
 |\det M(-q)|=d_0(q+z_0)\geq d_0|q-z_0|=|\det M(q)|.
\]
For two nonnegative roots with fixed sum \(Q(q)\), the larger root is nonincreasing in their product.  Hence \(\tau(-q)\leq\tau(q)\), and (1) implies \(\varphi_\rho(-q)\geq\varphi_\rho(q)\).

For \(z>z_0\), the top squared singular value is simple because the off-diagonal entry \(zc_0+b_0d_0\) of \(M(z)M(z)^{\top}\) is positive.  Put \(v=z-z_0>0\).  Differentiating
\[
 \tau^2-Q(z)\tau+d_0^2v^2=0
\]
gives
\[
 \tau'(z)=\frac{2\{z\tau-d_0^2v\}}{\tau-u}.
\]
Moreover, \(\tau\geq z^2+c_0^2>zv\), so
\[
 2z-\tau'(z)=\frac{2d_0^2v\{1-zv/\tau\}}{\tau-u}>0. \tag{2}
\]
Below the spectral edge, (1) has derivative \(2z>0\).  Above the edge its derivative is
\[
 2z-\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\tau^2}\right)\tau'(z),
\]
which is positive by (2), whether \(\tau'\) is nonnegative or negative.  Thus \(\varphi_\rho\) is strictly increasing on \((z_0,\infty)\).  Together with the preceding reflection comparison, every global minimizer lies in \([0,z_0]\).

It remains to analyze this interval.  Put \(w=z_0-z\).  Here the same characteristic equation gives
\[
 \tau'(z)=\frac{2\{z\tau+d_0^2w\}}{\tau-u}>0. \tag{3}
\]
The strict inequality follows because every term in the numerator is nonnegative and \(d_0^2w>0\) away from \(z_0\); continuity covers the endpoint.  In the supercritical region, (1) and (3) imply
\[
 \varphi_\rho'(z)
 =\frac{\tau'(z)}{\tau(z)^2}\{\rho-\mathcal R(z)\}, \tag{4}
\]
because
\[
 \tau^2\left(1-\frac{2z}{\tau'}\right)
 =\frac{d_0^2w\tau(\tau+zw)}{z\tau+d_0^2w}
 =\mathcal R(z). \tag{5}
\]
Also, \(\mathcal R(0)=\tau(0)^2\) and \(\lim_{z\uparrow z_0}\mathcal R(z)=0\).

We now prove the load-bearing monotonicity rather than assuming it.  For \(0\leq z<z_0\), define
\[
 \alpha=\frac{d_0}{w},\qquad \xi=\frac{z}{w},\qquad
 y=\frac{\tau}{d_0w},\qquad e=\frac{(b_0-c_0)^2}{w^2}.
\]
The two singular values cannot be equal, since \(zc_0+b_0d_0>0\); hence \(y>1\).  The characteristic equation and \(b_0c_0=d_0(z+w)\) imply
\[
 \alpha\left(y+\frac1y\right)
 =\xi^2+\alpha^2+2\alpha(1+\xi)+e. \tag{6}
\]
Differentiate the right side of (5), use (3), and then use (6).  Collecting terms gives the exact factorization
\[
 \mathcal R'(z)
 =-\frac{d_0^5\tau w^6}
 {(\tau^2-d_0^2w^2)(d_0^2w+\tau z)^2}
 \left[\alpha(1+\xi)(y-1)^3(y+3)+4ey\right]<0. \tag{7}
\]
Every denominator in (7) is positive, and the bracket is strictly positive because \(\alpha>0\), \(1+\xi>0\), and \(y>1\).  Thus \(\mathcal R\) decreases strictly from \(\tau(0)^2\) to zero.

If \(\tau(0)^2\leq\rho\), then (3) shows that any subcritical portion has derivative \(2z>0\), while on any supercritical portion (4) and (7) give \(\varphi_\rho'(z)>0\) for \(z>0\).  Hence zero is the unique minimizer.  If \(\tau(0)^2>\rho\), then zero is supercritical and (4) is negative at zero.  Equations (4) and (7) show that the derivative changes sign exactly once, at the unique solution of \(\mathcal R(z)=\rho\).  At that solution \(\rho=\mathcal R(z)<\tau(z)^2\), so it indeed lies in the supercritical region.  The derivative is negative before this root and positive after it, proving global uniqueness.

Finally,
\[
 \lim_{|z|\to\infty}\varphi_\rho(z)=d_0^2-(1+\rho).
\]
At the rank-one point \(z_0\), put \(S=z_0^2+b_0^2+c_0^2+d_0^2\).  If \(S\leq\sqrt\rho\), the difference between this tail limit and \(\varphi_\rho(z_0)\) is \(d_0^2+2\sqrt\rho-S\geq d_0^2+\sqrt\rho>0\).  If \(S>\sqrt\rho\), that difference is \(d_0^2+\rho/S>0\).  The unique minimum is no larger than \(\varphi_\rho(z_0)\), so it is strictly below the tail limit.  Continuity, uniqueness on bounded sets, and this strict tail gap prove the asserted separation.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The four exact orthogonality restrictions define the admissible triangular-array class.  They eliminate baseline-factor cross terms and isolate absorption by the fitted singular direction, but they are not observable residualization conditions.  Within this class the scalar profile has an always-separated singleton, including at \(|h|=h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\).  An absorbed region exists in the bounded experiment only when \(\overline h>\min_{\eta}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\); no signal-reach assumption is imposed to force that inequality.

\section{Honest scope}

The proposal is an abstract exact-oracle sensitivity benchmark.  All stochastic profile, selection, absorption, and Wald conclusions concern \(\theta\in[-K,K]\) and \(h=\theta/\sigma\in[-\overline h,\overline h]\).  There is no genuine tie in this domain: the coefficient equals the ATT coordinate at and below the explicit threshold and lies strictly between the ATT coordinate and zero above it.  The absorbed phase may be empty, and it is nonempty exactly when \(\overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\).  Exact finite-array orthogonality is a defining restriction, not an observable procedure.  Masked fitting is only an internal control, not a claimed new practical estimator, and no stochastic conclusion is extended to unbounded \(|h|\).
