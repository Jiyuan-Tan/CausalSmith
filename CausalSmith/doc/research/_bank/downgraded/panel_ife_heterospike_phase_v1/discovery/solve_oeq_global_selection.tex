Assume \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) and \(h\in\mathbb R\).  Then \(\mathcal S(\eta,h)\) is a separated singleton for every such pair; in particular, there is no genuine separated tie.  Its unique member \(x^\dagger(\eta,h)\) has the following explicit form.  If \(h=0\), then \(x^\dagger(\eta,0)=0\).  If \(h\neq0\), define
\[
s=\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b),\qquad
z_0=\kappa|h\mu_a\mu_b|,\qquad
b_0=\kappa|h\mu_a|\sqrt{v_b},
\]
\[
c_0=\kappa|h\mu_b|\sqrt{v_a},\qquad
d_0=\kappa|h|\sqrt{v_av_b}.
\]
For \(0\leq z\leq z_0\), put
\[
w(z)=z_0-z,\qquad
Q(z)=z^2+b_0^2+c_0^2+d_0^2,
\]
\[
\tau(z)=\frac{Q(z)+\sqrt{Q(z)^2-4d_0^2w(z)^2}}{2},
\qquad
\mathcal R(z)=
\frac{d_0^2w(z)\tau(z)\{\tau(z)+zw(z)\}}
{z\tau(z)+d_0^2w(z)}.
\]
Let \(z^\dagger=0\) if \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\).  If \(\tau(0)^2>c\), let \(z^\dagger\) be the unique solution of \(\mathcal R(z)=c\) in \((0,z_0)\).  Then
\[
\mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x^\dagger(\eta,h)\},
\qquad
x^\dagger(\eta,h)=\frac{s}{\kappa}(z_0-z^\dagger).
\]
The map \((\eta,h)\mapsto x^\dagger(\eta,h)\) is continuous.  For every compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\inf_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}
\inf_{|x-x^\dagger(\eta,h)|\geq\varepsilon}
\left[\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)-
\Phi_{\eta,h}\{x^\dagger(\eta,h)\}\right]>0.
\]
At a critical point \((\eta_0,h_0)\) with \(h_0\neq0\), let \((\eta_\epsilon,h_\epsilon)\to(\eta_0,h_0)\) be any path.  Write \(c_\epsilon^{\mathrm{asp}}\), \(\mu_{a,\epsilon}\), and \(\mu_{b,\epsilon}\) for the corresponding coordinates of \(\eta_\epsilon\), and let \(\tau_\epsilon\), \(\mathcal R_\epsilon\), and \(\kappa_\epsilon\) denote the preceding constructions evaluated at \((\eta_\epsilon,h_\epsilon)\).  Suppose \(\tau_0(0)^2=c_0^{\mathrm{asp}}\) and
\[
\Delta_\epsilon=\tau_\epsilon(0)^2-c_\epsilon^{\mathrm{asp}}
=\epsilon\dot\Delta+o(\epsilon),
\qquad \epsilon\downarrow0.
\]
Writing \(s_0=\operatorname{sgn}(h_0\mu_{a,0}\mu_{b,0})\), and writing \(\mathcal R_0'(0)<0\) for the derivative at the critical point, one has
\[
z^\dagger_\epsilon=
\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}+o(\epsilon),
\]
\[
x^\dagger(\eta_\epsilon,h_\epsilon)
-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
=-\frac{s_0}{\kappa_0}\,
\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}+o(\epsilon).
\]
Thus opposite admissible \(m^{-1/4}\) drifts can alter the one-sided local derivative, but both selectors converge to the same critical minimizer.  A coefficient-indexed next-order noise process is therefore not needed to choose among limiting global minima.
%%% FIELD global-selection-proof
By \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\), the quantities \(c,p,q,v_a,v_b\) are strictly positive and \(\mu_a\mu_b\neq0\).  Hence
\[
\kappa=\sqrt{\max\{c,1\}pq}>0. \tag{1}
\]
We first treat \(h\neq0\).  The sign rotation specified by the global-selection handle is made explicit by
\[
U=\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{sgn}(\mu_a),1\},
\qquad
V=\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_b),
\operatorname{sgn}(h)\}.
\]
Both matrices are orthogonal.  Substitution of the definition
\(B_{\eta,h}(x)=\kappa(hA_\eta-xe_{11})\) gives
\[
UB_{\eta,h}(x)V
=\begin{pmatrix}z&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad z=z_0-s\kappa x, \tag{2}
\]
with \(s,z_0,b_0,c_0,d_0\) as in the statement.  All four of
\(z_0,b_0,c_0,d_0\) are positive, and direct multiplication yields
\[
\frac{b_0c_0}{d_0}=z_0. \tag{3}
\]
Orthogonal left and right multiplication preserves the Frobenius norm and all singular values.  Moreover, because \(s\kappa\neq0\), the map
\(x\mapsto z_0-s\kappa x\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb R\).  Therefore the profile \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\), under (2), is exactly the scalar profile in \(\text{lem:scalar-profile-singleton}\) with
\[
\rho=c,\qquad M(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4}
\]
That lemma and (3) show that the scalar criterion has exactly one global minimizer: it is zero when \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\), and otherwise it is the unique root of \(\mathcal R(z)=c\) in \((0,z_0)\).  Inverting (2) proves
\[
\mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x^\dagger(\eta,h)\},
\qquad
x^\dagger(\eta,h)=\frac{s}{\kappa}(z_0-z^\dagger). \tag{5}
\]
Because \(sz_0/\kappa=h\mu_a\mu_b\), (5) also gives
\[
x^\dagger(\eta,h)=h\mu_a\mu_b
\quad\text{when }\tau(0)^2\leq c, \tag{6}
\]
whereas in the other case it lies strictly between zero and
\(h\mu_a\mu_b\), in their common signed direction.

If \(h=0\), then
\[
B_{\eta,0}(x)=-\kappa xe_{11}. \tag{7}
\]
When \(\kappa^2x^2\leq\sqrt c\), the definition of \(\psi_c\) gives
\[
\Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=\kappa^2x^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2, \tag{8}
\]
which is uniquely minimized at zero on this region.  When
\(\kappa^2x^2>\sqrt c\), the outlier branch gives
\[
\Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=-(1+c)-\frac{c}{\kappa^2x^2}
>-(1+\sqrt c)^2=\Phi_{\eta,0}(0). \tag{9}
\]
The strict inequality follows from
\(\kappa^2x^2>\sqrt c\), which implies
\(c/(\kappa^2x^2)<\sqrt c\).  Equations (8)--(9) prove that zero is the unique minimizer when \(h=0\).

We next establish continuity and compact-uniform separation.  The entries of
\(B_{\eta,h}(x)\) are continuous in \((\eta,h,x)\).  Singular values are continuous in the entries, and the two branches of \(\psi_c\) agree when the largest singular value equals \(c^{1/4}\).  Hence
\[
(\eta,h,x)\longmapsto\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)
\quad\text{is continuous}, \tag{10}
\]
and parameter convergence gives uniform convergence on bounded
\(x\)-intervals.

Let \(\lambda_1(\eta,h,x)\geq\lambda_2(\eta,h,x)\geq0\) be the squared singular values of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\), and define
\[
d_0^2=\kappa^2h^2v_av_b,
\qquad
S=\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b). \tag{11}
\]
Since \(A_\eta\) has rank one,
\[
\det B_{\eta,h}(x)=-\kappa^2xh\sqrt{v_av_b},
\qquad
\lambda_1\lambda_2=\kappa^4x^2h^2v_av_b. \tag{12}
\]
Also
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2
=\|B_{\eta,h}(x)\|_F^2
=\kappa^2x^2+O(|x|+1), \tag{13}
\]
uniformly when \((\eta,h)\) ranges over a compact subset of
\(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\).  The quadratic formula applied to (12)--(13) yields, uniformly on such a compact set,
\[
\lambda_1(\eta,h,x)\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\lambda_2(\eta,h,x)\longrightarrow d_0^2
\quad\text{as }|x|\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{14}
\]
For sufficiently large \(|x|\), the first singular value is above the spectral edge.  Substitution of (14) into the outlier branch of \(\psi_c\) therefore gives
\[
\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\longrightarrow d_0^2-(1+c). \tag{15}
\]
At \(x=0\), the matrix \(B_{\eta,h}(0)=\kappa hA_\eta\) has rank at most one and its nonzero squared singular value is \(S\).  Consequently, the difference between the limit in (15) and \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)\) is
\[
G(\eta,h)=
\begin{cases}
d_0^2+2\sqrt c-S,&S\leq\sqrt c,\\
d_0^2+c/S,&S>\sqrt c.
\end{cases} \tag{16}
\]
On the first branch, \(G(\eta,h)\geq d_0^2+\sqrt c>0\); on the second branch it is positive as well.  The two expressions agree at \(S=\sqrt c\), so \(G\) is continuous.  Compactness, (14)--(16), and positivity of \(c\) thus imply that for every compact
\(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\), there are \(M<\infty\) and \(g>0\) such that
\[
\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\geq\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)+g,
\qquad
(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K,quad |x|>M. \tag{17}
\]

Let \((\eta_n,h_n)\to(\eta,h)\) and put
\(x_n=x^\dagger(\eta_n,h_n)\).  Inequality (17) and
\(\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)\leq\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(0)\) show that \((x_n)\) is bounded.  If a subsequence converges to \(\bar x\), then global minimality and (10) imply, for each \(y\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\Phi_{\eta,h}(\bar x)
\leq\Phi_{\eta,h}(y). \tag{18}
\]
The uniqueness already proved forces
\(\bar x=x^\dagger(\eta,h)\).  Every convergent subsequence has this limit, so the entire sequence converges.  This proves continuity of \(x^\dagger\), including at \(h=0\) and at the spectral edge.

Fix a compact \(\mathcal K\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).  If the claimed uniform gap failed, there would be
\((\eta_n,h_n)\in\mathcal K\) and \(x_n\) such that
\[
|x_n-x^\dagger(\eta_n,h_n)|\geq\varepsilon,
\qquad
\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)-
\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}\{x^\dagger(\eta_n,h_n)\}
\longrightarrow0. \tag{19}
\]
If \((x_n)\) is bounded along a subsequence, compactness, continuity of the selector, and (10) produce a point distinct from the unique limiting minimizer but having the same criterion value.  If \((x_n)\) is unbounded along a subsequence, (17) and
\(\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}\{x^\dagger(\eta_n,h_n)\}\leq
\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(0)\) bound the left side of (19) below by \(g\).  Both conclusions contradict (19).  This proves the compact-uniform separation and hence the absence of a genuine separated tie.

It remains to classify opposite local drifts at the critical boundary.  Fix a critical point with \(h_0\neq0\), and denote the values of \(b_0,c_0,d_0,z_0\) at this point by \(b_{0,0},c_{0,0},d_{0,0},z_{0,0}\), respectively.  At \(z=0\), abbreviate
\[
t=\tau_0(0),\qquad
u=s_2\{M_0(0)\}^2,\qquad
w=z_{0,0}. \tag{20}
\]
The trace and determinant identities give
\[
t+u=Q_0(0),
\qquad
tu=d_{0,0}^2w^2. \tag{21}
\]
Both values are positive because \(b_{0,0}c_{0,0}>0\).  They are unequal because the off-diagonal entry \(c_{0,0}d_{0,0}\) of
\(M_0(0)^{\top}M_0(0)\) is nonzero; hence \(t>u>0\).

Differentiate the characteristic identity
\[
\tau(z)^2-Q(z)\tau(z)+d_0^2w(z)^2=0. \tag{22}
\]
At zero, \(Q'(0)=0\) and \(w'(0)=-1\), so (21)--(22) imply
\[
\tau_0'(0)=\frac{2d_{0,0}^2w}{t-u}. \tag{23}
\]
Because \(\mathcal R_0(0)=t^2\), logarithmic differentiation of \(\mathcal R\), followed by (21) and (23), gives
\[
\frac{\mathcal R_0'(0)}{\mathcal R_0(0)}
=\frac{2\tau_0'(0)}t+\frac wt-
\frac{t}{d_{0,0}^2w}
=\frac{4u}{w(t-u)}-
\frac{w(t-u)}{tu}. \tag{24}
\]
Now
\[
M_0(0)^{\top}M_0(0)=
\begin{pmatrix}
c_{0,0}^2&c_{0,0}d_{0,0}\\
c_{0,0}d_{0,0}&b_{0,0}^2+d_{0,0}^2
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{25}
\]
The first coordinate vector has Rayleigh quotient \(c_{0,0}^2\) and is not an eigenvector, so the smaller eigenvalue satisfies
\(u<c_{0,0}^2\).  Therefore
\[
(t-u)^2-4d_{0,0}^2u
=(c_{0,0}^2-b_{0,0}^2-d_{0,0}^2)^2
+4d_{0,0}^2(c_{0,0}^2-u)>0. \tag{26}
\]
Using \(tu=d_{0,0}^2w^2\), inequality (26) is equivalent to the right side of (24) being negative.  Thus
\[
\mathcal R_0'(0)<0. \tag{27}
\]

Along the path in the statement,
\[
\mathcal R_\epsilon(0)-c_\epsilon^{\mathrm{asp}}
=\tau_\epsilon(0)^2-c_\epsilon^{\mathrm{asp}}
=\epsilon\dot\Delta+o(\epsilon). \tag{28}
\]
The exact selection rule gives \(z^\dagger_\epsilon=0\) whenever the left side of (28) is nonpositive.  When it is positive, the selected point is the unique solution of
\[
\mathcal R_\epsilon(z^\dagger_\epsilon)-c_\epsilon^{\mathrm{asp}}=0. \tag{29}
\]
Continuity of the selector implies
\(z^\dagger_\epsilon\to0\).  The mean-value theorem applied to (29), together with (27)--(28) and continuity of
\(\mathcal R_\epsilon'\) near zero, yields in all three cases
\(\dot\Delta>0\), \(\dot\Delta<0\), and \(\dot\Delta=0\),
\[
z^\dagger_\epsilon
=\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}
+o(\epsilon). \tag{30}
\]
Finally, the exact inverse-coordinate identity from (2) is
\[
x^\dagger(\eta_\epsilon,h_\epsilon)
-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
=-\frac{s_\epsilon}{\kappa_\epsilon}z^\dagger_\epsilon. \tag{31}
\]
The limiting values \(h_0,\mu_{a,0},\mu_{b,0}\) are nonzero, so
\(s_\epsilon=s_0\) for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), while
\(\kappa_\epsilon\to\kappa_0>0\).  Equations (30)--(31) give the claimed second expansion.  Taking \(\epsilon=m^{-1/4}\) shows that opposite drifts change only a vanishing directional term.  They cannot produce different separated limiting selectors at the same limiting nuisance coordinates, which completes the classification.
