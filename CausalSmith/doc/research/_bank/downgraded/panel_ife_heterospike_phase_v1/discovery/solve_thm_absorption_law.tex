Put \(U_0=\operatorname{col}(\Lambda)\) and \(V_0=\operatorname{col}(F)\).  The loading and factor Gram envelopes imply that both spaces have dimension \(r\).  Moreover,
\[
 s_r\!\left(\frac{\Lambda F^{\top}}{\sigma\sqrt T}\right)
 \geq
 \frac{\sqrt{N\underline\lambda}\sqrt{T\underline f}}
 {\overline\sigma\sqrt T}
 \geq C_0\sqrt m,
 \qquad C_0=\frac{\sqrt{\underline\lambda\underline f}}{\overline\sigma}>0. \tag{1}
\]
All bounds below are uniform when \((\eta,h)\) ranges over a fixed compact subset \(\mathcal K\) of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h]\).  The aspect, share, score, and noise-scale envelopes make all constants uniform on \(\mathcal K\).

The share and finite supported-variance envelopes make the vectors
\[
 e_1=\frac d{\sqrt{n_1}},\qquad
 e_2=\frac{a_S-\mu_{a,N}d}{\sqrt{n_1v_{a,N}}},\qquad
 f_1=\frac{qvec}{\sqrt{t_1}},\qquad
 f_2=\frac{b_S-\mu_{b,T}qvec}{\sqrt{t_1v_{b,T}}} \tag{2}
\]
well defined.  The definitions of the supported means and variances give
\[
 e_j^{\top}e_k=f_j^{\top}f_k=\mathbf 1\{j=k\},\qquad 1\leq j,k\leq2. \tag{3}
\]
The four exact orthogonality assumptions give
\[
 e_1,e_2\in U_0^{\perp},\qquad f_1,f_2\in V_0^{\perp}. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, for example,
\(\Lambda^{\top}(a_S-\mu_{a,N}d)=0\) follows from the left-score and left-support restrictions, and the other three inclusions are identical calculations.

For \(\beta=\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m\), define
\[
 P_x=\frac{H-(\beta-\gamma)D}{\sigma\sqrt T}.
\]
Substitution of the definitions of \(H\), \(D\), and (2) gives the exact identity
\[
 P_x=(e_1,e_2)B_{NT,h}(x)(f_1,f_2)^{\top}. \tag{5}
\]
To check its normalization, the heterogeneous term is multiplied by
\[
 \frac{\theta\sqrt{n_1t_1}}{\sigma\sqrt{mT}}
 =h\sqrt{\frac Nm}\sqrt{p_Nq_T}=h\kappa_{NT}, \tag{6}
\]
and the coefficient displacement is multiplied by \(x\kappa_{NT}\).  For every fixed \(M\), the coefficient-domain assumption and \(\sigma\leq\overline\sigma\) imply that \(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\) for all \(|x|\leq M\) and all sufficiently large panels.

Complete orthonormal bases beginning with bases of \(U_0\) and \(V_0\).  Gaussian orthogonal invariance, (4), and (5) yield
\[
 W_x:=\frac{Y-(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)D}{\sigma\sqrt T}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 G+Z_{11}&Z_{12}\\
 Z_{21}&Z_{22}+P_x
 \end{pmatrix}, \tag{7}
\]
where \(s_r(G)\geq C_0\sqrt m\), every entry of \(Z\) is independently \(\mathcal N(0,1/T)\), and the nonzero coordinates of \(P_x\) are the two-by-two matrix in (5).  The rectangular Gaussian outlier lemma with zero perturbation, transposed and rescaled when \(c_N>1\), gives
\[
 \|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_p(1). \tag{8}
\]
Also \(\sup_{|x|\leq M}\|P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O(1)\).  Weyl's inequality and (1) therefore put the first \(r\) singular values of \(W_x\) above \(C_0\sqrt m/2\) with probability tending to one, uniformly for \(|x|\leq M\).

Write a corresponding unit singular pair as \((u_1,u_2)\) and \((v_1,v_2)\), partitioned as in (7).  The lower block singular equations imply
\[
 \|u_2\|_2\leq
 \frac{\|Z_{21}|_{\mathrm{op}}+|Z_{22}+P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|v_2\|_2}{s_j(W_x)},
 \qquad
 \|v_2\|_2\leq
 \frac{\|Z_{12}|_{\mathrm{op}}+|Z_{22}+P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|u_2\|_2}{s_j(W_x)}.
\]
Equations (1) and (8) hence give
\[
 \max_{1\leq j\leq r}\sup_{|x|\leq M}
 \bigl(\|u_{j,2}(x)\|_2+\|v_{j,2}(x)\|_2\bigr)
 =O_p(m^{-1/2}). \tag{9}
\]
Along the line segment from \(P_0\) to \(P_x\), the Ky Fan sum of the first \(r\) squared singular values is absolutely continuous.  At every differentiability point its derivative is a sum of terms
\(2s_j u_{j,2}^{\top}(P_x-P_0)v_{j,2}\).  Since \(r\) is fixed, (9) bounds the derivative by \(O_p(m^{-1/2})\), uniformly on the segment.  Integration gives
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 \sum_{j=1}^r s_j(W_x)^2-sum_{j=1}^r s_j(W_0)^2
 \right|=o_p(1). \tag{10}
\]
This argument also covers singular-value crossings because the Ky Fan sum is locally Lipschitz and the derivative bound holds almost everywhere.

For the next singular value, apply the singular equations to the symmetric dilation of (7).  On the orthogonal complement of its \(2r\) strong eigenspaces, solving the strong-coordinate equations introduces the Schur-complement term
\[
 O_p\!\left(\frac{\|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2+|Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}\sup_{|x|\leq M}\|P_x\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
 {s_r(G)}\right)=o_p(1).
\]
The min--max characterization of singular values then yields
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}
 \left|s_{r+1}(W_x)-s_1(Z_{22}+P_x)\right|=o_p(1). \tag{11}
\]

We next make the outlier limit uniform over \(|x|\leq M\).  The matrices \(B_{NT,h}(x)\) range over a compact family.  For any two deterministic perturbations,
\[
 |s_1(Z_{22}+P)-s_1(Z_{22}+\widetilde P)|
 \leq\|P-\widetilde P\|_F. \tag{12}
\]
Choose a finite Frobenius \(\epsilon\)-net of the corresponding two-by-two matrices.  At its finitely many points, the rectangular Gaussian outlier lemma applies.  The function \((\rho,s)\mapsto\psi_\rho(s)\) is continuous at the edge because its two displayed branches agree at \(s=\rho^{1/4}\).  Equation (12), the finite net, and then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) give, when \(c\leq1\),
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 s_1(Z_{22}+P_x)^2-psi_{c_N}\{s_1(B_{NT,h}(x))\}
 \right|\xrightarrow{p}0. \tag{13}
\]
When \(c>1\), apply the same lemma to \(c_N^{-1/2}(Z_{22}+P_x)^{\top}\), whose aspect ratio tends to \(1/c\).  Multiplying its squared limit by \(c\) gives
\[
 c\left\{\frac{s^2}{c}+1+\frac1c+\frac1{s^2}\right\}
 =s^2+1+c+\frac c{s^2},
\]
with threshold \(s=c^{1/4}\), so (13) again follows.  Removing the fixed number \(r\) of rows and columns changes the aspect ratio and entry normalization by \(o(1)\); continuity of \(\psi\) absorbs this change.

The Frobenius part has an exact expansion.  Orthogonality of the baseline block to \(P_x\) gives
\[
 \|W_x\|_F^2-\|W_0\|_F^2
 =\|B_{NT,h}(x)\|_F^2-\|B_{NT,h}(0)\|_F^2
 +2\langle Z,P_x-P_0\rangle_F. \tag{14}
\]
The last term is affine in \(x\), has mean zero, and its supremum on \([-M,M]\) has variance bounded by \(C_M/T\).  It is therefore \(o_p(1)\).  By the full-profile definition,
\[
 \frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)}{\sigma^2T}
 =\|W_x\|_F^2-\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}s_j(W_x)^2.
\]
Combining (10), (11), (13), and (14) proves
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 \frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)-Q_F(\gamma)}{\sigma^2T}
 -\{\Phi_{NT,h}(x)-\Phi_{NT,h}(0)\}
 \right|\xrightarrow{p}0. \tag{15}
\]

It remains to prove global localization rather than merely compact convergence.  Put
\[
 R=\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b),
 \qquad d_0^2=\kappa^2h^2v_av_b,
 \qquad L_\infty=d_0^2-(1+c). \tag{16}
\]
At \(x=0\), \(B_{\eta,h}(0)\) has rank one and nonzero squared singular value \(R\).  Direct substitution in the two branches of \(\psi_c\) gives
\[
 L_\infty-\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)=
 \begin{cases}
 d_0^2+2\sqrt c-R,&R\leq\sqrt c,\\
 d_0^2+c/R,&R>\sqrt c.
 \end{cases} \tag{17}
\]
The first expression is at least \(d_0^2+\sqrt c\), and the second is positive.  Compactness of \(\mathcal K\), the positive aspect bound, and the upper bound on \(R\) therefore give a common constant \(4g_{\mathcal K}>0\) below the right-hand side of (17).

For completeness, the stochastic tail has the same value \(L_\infty\).  In the lower block write
\[
 Z_{22}+P_x=-t e_1f_1^{\top}+A,
 \qquad t=\kappa_{NT}x,
 \qquad A=Z_{22}+P_0.
\]
For \(|t|\to\infty\), the eigenvalue equation, expanded around its simple eigenvalue \(t^2\), gives uniformly after \(|t|\) exceeds a fixed constant,
\[
 s_1(-t e_1f_1^{\top}+A)^2
 =t^2-2t e_1^{\top}Af_1+|A^{\top}e_1\|_2^2
 +\|(I-e_1e_1^{\top})Af_1\|_2^2+O_p(|t|^{-1}). \tag{18}
\]
The two Gaussian quadratic terms converge to \(1\) and \(c\); all Gaussian--spike cross terms converge to zero.  In the criterion centered at \(x=0\), subtracting (18) from the Frobenius expansion cancels the terms in \(t^2\) and \(t e_1^{\top}Af_1\).  The remaining lower-block difference converges to
\[
 \psi_c\{s_1(B_{\eta,h}(0))\}-(1+c)
 -\|P_0^{\top}e_1\|_2^2
 -\|(I-e_1e_1^{\top})P_0f_1\|_2^2. \tag{19}
\]
The orthogonal block decomposition of \(P_0\), together with \(\|P_0\|_F=\|B_{\eta,h}(0)\|_F\), gives
\[
 \|B_{\eta,h}(0)\|_F^2
 =\|P_0^{\top}e_1\|_2^2
 +\|(I-e_1e_1^{\top})P_0f_1\|_2^2+d_0^2.
\]
Consequently, (19) equals
\[
 d_0^2-(1+c)-\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)
 =L_\infty-\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)\geq4g_{\mathcal K}. \tag{20}
\]
For larger \(|x|\), the same singular equations are applied jointly to the \(r\) baseline directions and the treatment direction.  Their deterministic left and right spaces are orthogonal by (4); the baseline singular values are bounded below by \(C_0\sqrt m\), while the treatment singular value is \(\kappa_{NT}|x|\).  Splitting at a sequence \(M_N\to\infty\) with \(M_N=o(\sqrt m)\), the first calculation covers \(M\leq|x|\leq M_N\), and on \(|x|>M_N\) every Schur-complement denominator is bounded below by a constant times \(\min\{\sqrt m,M_N\}\to\infty\).  Thus the joint error is \(o_p(1)\), and the cancellation in (19) is unchanged.  Consequently,
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\liminf_{N,T\to\infty}
 \Pr\!\left[
 \inf_{\substack{|x|\geq M\\ \gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m\in\mathcal B}}
 \left\{
 \frac{Q_F(\gamma+\sigma x/\sqrt m)-Q_F(\gamma)}{\sigma^2T}
 \right\}\geq2g_{\mathcal K}
 \right]=1. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (17)--(21) exclude every diverging feasible coefficient sequence.  Hence
\[
 \widehat x_F=\frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)}\sigma=O_p(1) \tag{22}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).

On the common compact set supplied by (21), (15) applies.  The corrected global-profile singleton theorem gives a continuous unique minimizer and a positive uniform gap outside every fixed neighborhood.  Since \(\eta_{NT}\to\eta\), uniform continuity of \(\Phi\) and the same tail gap give
\[
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal S_{NT}(h)}|y-x_\star(\eta,h)|\longrightarrow0. \tag{23}
\]
If either \(\operatorname{dist}\{\widehat x_F,\mathcal S_{NT}(h)\}\to_p0\) or \(\widehat x_F-x_\star(\eta,h)\to_p0\) failed, (15), (21), and the corresponding deterministic separation gap would contradict global minimality.  This proves both argmin conclusions uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).  Finally, the global singleton theorem applies on all of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\), so
\[
 \mathcal G_{\mathrm{loc}}=
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times[-\overline h,\overline h],
\]
and there is no limiting tie in the bounded experiment.

Let \(x_A=h\mu_a\mu_b=x_{\operatorname{ATT}}\).  The local geometry assumption gives \(c>0\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(v_a,v_b>0\).  At \(x_A\), direct substitution in the local-spike profile gives
\[
 \|B_{\eta,h}(x_A)\|_F^2
 =\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2v_b+v_a\mu_b^2+v_av_b)<\sqrt c. \tag{1}
\]
Therefore \(s_1\{B_{\eta,h}(x_A)\}<c^{1/4}\).  At every subcritical point, the outlier-map definition gives
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)=\|B_{\eta,h}(x)\|_F^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2. \tag{2}
\]
The only \(x\)-dependent term in the squared Frobenius norm is
\(\kappa^2(h\mu_a\mu_b-x)^2\).  Hence (2) is uniquely minimized at \(x=x_A\) among subcritical points.

For any supercritical \(x\), write \(s_j=s_j\{B_{\eta,h}(x)\}\).  Since \(s_1^2>\sqrt c\),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)
 =s_2^2-(1+c)-\frac c{s_1^2}
 >-(1+c)-\sqrt c. \tag{3}
\]
By (1) and (2),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x_A)
 <\sqrt c-(1+\sqrt c)^2
 =-(1+c)-\sqrt c. \tag{4}
\]
Thus no supercritical point can minimize, and the within-branch calculation proves
\[
 \mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x_A\}. \tag{5}
\]

On any compact set in the statement, the strict inequality in (1) has a common positive margin.  The uniform-profile theorem and (5) therefore give \(\widehat x_F-x_A\to_p0\) uniformly.  The definitions of \(\widehat x_F\), \(h\), and the finite-array target give the exact identity
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 =\widehat x_F-h\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}. \tag{6}
\]
The two moment assumptions imply
\(\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}\to\mu_a\mu_b\).  Combining this convergence with (5) and (6) proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}\xrightarrow{p}0
\]
uniformly on the stated compact subsets.

%%% FIELD proof-absorption-law
The definitions of \(\widehat x_F\), \(h\), and \(\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\) give the exact finite-array identity
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 =\widehat x_F-h\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}. \tag{1}
\]
The uniform-profile theorem gives
\(\widehat x_F-x_\star(\eta,h)\to_p0\) uniformly on compact subsets of the bounded experiment.  The two moment assumptions give
\(\mu_{a,N}\mu_{b,T}\to\mu_a\mu_b\).  Substitution in (1) proves
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 \xrightarrow{p}x_\star(\eta,h)-h\mu_a\mu_b. \tag{2}
\]

It remains to identify the phase.  At
\(x_{\operatorname{ATT}}=h\mu_a\mu_b\),
\[
 B_{\eta,h}(x_{\operatorname{ATT}})
 =\kappa h
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&\mu_a\sqrt{v_b}\\
 \sqrt{v_a}\mu_b&\sqrt{v_av_b}
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{3}
\]
Row and column sign changes are orthogonal transformations.  Thus, with \(C_\eta\) from the absorption-phase definition,
\[
 s_1\{B_{\eta,h}(x_{\operatorname{ATT}})\}
 =\kappa|h|s_1(C_\eta). \tag{4}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\), \(\kappa>0\), \(v_a,v_b>0\), and \(|\mu_a|,|\mu_b|>0\).  Hence \(s_1(C_\eta)>0\), so every division below is valid.

In the scalar coordinates of the corrected global-profile singleton theorem, \(z=0\) is exactly \(x=x_{\operatorname{ATT}}\), and \(\tau(0)\) is the squared largest singular value in (4).  Its exact selection rule gives \(z_\star=0\) if and only if \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\).  By (4),
\[
 \tau(0)^2\leq c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \kappa^4h^4s_1(C_\eta)^4\leq c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |h|\leq\frac{c^{1/4}}{\kappa s_1(C_\eta)}
 =h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta). \tag{5}
\]
This includes \(h=0\) and equality at the spectral edge.  The inverse scalar coordinate then proves
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)=h\mu_a\mu_b
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |h|\leq h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta). \tag{6}
\]

If \(|h|>h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\), the same theorem gives \(0<z_\star<z_0\) and
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)
 =\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b)}\kappa(z_0-z_\star),
 \qquad
 h\mu_a\mu_b
 =\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b)}\kappa z_0. \tag{7}
\]
Because \(\kappa>0\), equation (7) gives
\[
 0<x_\star(\eta,h)<h\mu_a\mu_b
 \quad\text{if }h\mu_a\mu_b>0,
\]
and
\[
 h\mu_a\mu_b<x_\star(\eta,h)<0
 \quad\text{if }h\mu_a\mu_b<0. \tag{8}
\]

The entries of \(C_\eta\), its largest singular value, \(c^{1/4}\), and \(\kappa\) are continuous on \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\).  The denominator in (5) is positive and, by compactness, bounded away from zero.  Thus \(h_{\mathrm{crit}}\) is continuous and positive.  The strict inverse image
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}
 =\{(\eta,h):|h|>h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\}
 \cap\{(\eta,h):\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}},\ |h|\leq\overline h\}
\]
is relatively open.  Compactness of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) implies that \(h_{\mathrm{crit}}\) attains its minimum.  If \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\neq\varnothing\), its defining inequality implies
\[
 \overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta). \tag{9}
\]
Conversely, if (9) holds, choose a minimizer \(\eta_0\) and any \(|h|\in(h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta_0),\overline h]\); then \((\eta_0,h)\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\).  This proves the exact nonemptiness equivalence without an additional reach assumption.  All stochastic uses above invoke \(|h|\leq\overline h\), so no conclusion has been extended beyond the bounded experiment.

%%% FIELD proof-wald-failure
Fix a compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\).  By the absorption law, every point of \(\mathcal K\) is strictly beyond the threshold and \(\widehat x_F\to_p x_\star\) uniformly.  In the sign-rotated representation of the corrected singleton theorem, the selected matrix is
\[
 M_\star=\begin{pmatrix}z_\star&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<z_\star<z_0,
\]
and all four displayed entries are positive.  The off-diagonal entry of \(M_\star M_\star^{\top}\) is \(z_\star c_0+b_0d_0>0\); hence its eigenvalues are distinct and the top singular value \(s_\star\) is simple.  Moreover, increasing the positive entry \(z\) strictly increases the Perron eigenvalue of \(M(z)M(z)^{\top}\).  Since the ATT-coordinate matrix at \(z=0\) is supercritical on \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\),
\[
 s_\star>s_1\{M(0)\}>c^{1/4}. \tag{1}
\]
Continuity and compactness supply uniform gaps from both the spectral edge and the second singular value on \(\mathcal K\).

Use the treatment-support vectors \(e_1,f_1\) from (2) in the proof of the uniform-profile theorem.  The strong-block singular equations show that the first \(r\) selected left and right singular spaces converge to \(U_0\) and \(V_0\).  The additional selected pair is the leading outlier pair from \(Z_{22}+P_{x_\star}\): replacing \(\widehat x_F\) by \(x_\star\) changes the perturbation by \(o_p(1)\) in operator norm.  For \(c\leq1\), the rectangular Gaussian singular-vector-overlap lemma therefore gives
\[
 |\langle\widehat u,e_1\rangle|^2
 \xrightarrow{p}\ell_c(s_\star)u_{\star,1}^2,
 \qquad
 |\langle\widehat v,f_1\rangle|^2
 \xrightarrow{p}r_c(s_\star)v_{\star,1}^2. \tag{2}
\]
The factors \(u_{\star,1}^2\) and \(v_{\star,1}^2\) arise by resolving \(e_1\) and \(f_1\) in the two population singular-vector bases; the cited lemma makes projections onto every distinct population singular direction vanish.  For \(c>1\), apply that lemma to \(c^{-1/2}(Z_{22}+P_{x_\star})^{\top}\).  The aspect ratio becomes \(1/c\), the spike becomes \(s_\star/\sqrt c\), and left and right vectors interchange.  Algebra gives
\[
 \frac{s_\star^4/c^2-1/c}{(s_\star^2/c)(s_\star^2/c+1/c)}
 =\frac{s_\star^4-c}{s_\star^2(s_\star^2+1)}=r_c(s_\star),
\]
\[
 \frac{s_\star^4/c^2-1/c}{(s_\star^2/c)(s_\star^2/c+1)}
 =\frac{s_\star^4-c}{s_\star^2(s_\star^2+c)}=\ell_c(s_\star). \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) holds for every \(c>0\), uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).

Because \(D=\sqrt{n_1t_1}\,e_1f_1^{\top}\), and because \(e_1\perp U_0\) and \(f_1\perp V_0\),
\[
 \frac{\|\widetilde D\|_F}{\sqrt{NT}}
 =\sqrt{p_Nq_T}\,
 \|(I-P_{\widehat U})e_1\|_2
 \|(I-P_{\widehat V})f_1\|_2. \tag{4}
\]
Substitution of (2) into (4), followed by the two share limits, proves
\[
 \frac{\|\widetilde D\|_F}{\sqrt{NT}}
 \xrightarrow{p}
 \sqrt{pq}\{1-\ell_c(s_\star)u_{\star,1}^2\}^{1/2}
 \{1-r_c(s_\star)v_{\star,1}^2\}^{1/2}
 =a_D(\eta,h). \tag{5}
\]
For every finite \(s>c^{1/4}\), direct subtraction gives
\[
 0<\ell_c(s)<1,
 \qquad 0<r_c(s)<1. \tag{6}
\]
Thus (5) is strictly positive, even if one of the first population singular-vector coordinates has absolute value one.  Compactness gives a common positive lower bound on \(\mathcal K\).
In particular, \(\Pr\{\|\widetilde D\|_F>0\}\to1\) uniformly on \(\mathcal K\), so the finite-width branch of the interval definition is used with probability tending to one.

We next prove variance consistency.  The absorption law implies
\(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma=O_p(m^{-1/2})\).  Put
\[
 R_{NT}=H-(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)D.
\]
The bounded-score, aspect, and share assumptions imply
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(R_{NT})\leq2,
 \qquad \|R_{NT}\|_F=O_p(\sqrt T). \tag{7}
\]
Choosing \(L=\Lambda F^{\top}\), whose rank is at most \(r\), in the profiled residual problem gives
\[
 \|Y-\widehat\beta_FD-\widehat L_F\|_F^2
 \leq\|E+R_{NT}\|_F^2
 =\|E\|_F^2+o_p(NT), \tag{8}
\]
because \(\|E\|_F=O_p(\sqrt{NT})\) and (7) makes both the cross term and \(\|R_{NT}\|_F^2\) negligible relative to \(NT\).

For the reverse inequality, write
\[
 Y-\widehat\beta_FD-\widehat L_F=E-K_{NT},
 \qquad
 K_{NT}=\widehat L_F-(\Lambda F^{\top}+R_{NT}).
\]
Then \(\operatorname{rank}(K_{NT})\leq2r+3\).  The singular-value variational characterization gives
\[
 \|E-K_{NT}\|_F^2
 \geq\|E\|_F^2-\sum_{j=1}^{2r+3}s_j(E)^2. \tag{9}
\]
The Gaussian law of large numbers and the zero-perturbation case of the rectangular outlier bound give
\[
 \frac{\|E\|_F^2}{NT}\xrightarrow{p}\sigma^2,
 \qquad
 \frac1{NT}\sum_{j=1}^{2r+3}s_j(E)^2
 =O_p(N^{-1})=o_p(1), \tag{10}
\]
uniformly under the noise-scale and aspect bounds.  Equations (8)--(10) imply
\[
 \frac{\|Y-\widehat\beta_FD-\widehat L_F\|_F^2}{NT}
 \xrightarrow{p}\sigma^2. \tag{11}
\]
Since \(r\) is fixed, the aspect bound gives \(\nu_F/(NT)\to1\), and \(\nu_F>0\) eventually.  The nonnegative-root definition therefore gives
\[
 \widehat\sigma_F\xrightarrow{p}\sigma. \tag{12}
\]

Finally define
\[
 b(\eta,h)=x_\star(\eta,h)-h\mu_a\mu_b.
\]
The phase classification makes \(b\) continuous and nonzero on \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\), so
\[
 \inf_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}|b(\eta,h)|>0. \tag{13}
\]
The absorption law yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\operatorname{ATT}_{NT})}{\sigma}
 \xrightarrow{p}b(\eta,h), \tag{14}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).  On the other hand, (5), (12), and \(NT\asymp m^2\) yield
\[
 \frac{\sqrt m}{\sigma}
 \frac{z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat\sigma_F}{\|\widetilde D\|_F}
 =O_p(m^{-1/2})\longrightarrow0. \tag{15}
\]
If \(\mathrm{CI}_F\) contains \(\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\), the absolute value on the left of (14) is at most the quantity in (15).  Equations (13)--(15) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{ATT}_{NT}\in\mathrm{CI}_F\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{16}
\]
The absorption law already proves that \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{abs}}\) is relatively open and is nonempty if and only if
\(\overline h>\min_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}}h_{\mathrm{crit}}(\eta)\).  This completes every asserted conclusion within the bounded experiment.

At \(\theta=0\), the positive noise-scale assumption implies \(h=0\), while the target definition gives
\[
 \operatorname{ATT}_{NT}=\gamma. \tag{1}
\]
The local-spike profile becomes
\[
 B_{\eta,0}(x)=-\kappa xe_{11},
 \qquad s_1\{B_{\eta,0}(x)\}=\kappa|x|. \tag{2}
\]
If \(\kappa|x|\leq c^{1/4}\), then the outlier-map definition gives
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=\kappa^2x^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2,
\]
which is uniquely minimized at \(x=0\) on that region.  If \(\kappa|x|>c^{1/4}\), then
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=-(1+c)-\frac{c}{\kappa^2x^2}
 >-(1+c)-\sqrt c
 >-(1+c)-2\sqrt c=\Phi_{\eta,0}(0). \tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal S(\eta,0)=\{0\}. \tag{4}
\]
The uniform-profile theorem applied at \(h=0\) yields
\[
 \widehat x_F=\frac{\sqrt m(\widehat\beta_F-\gamma)}\sigma\xrightarrow{p}0. \tag{5}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and (5) prove all assertions.

%%% FIELD statement-global-profile-singleton-corrected
Suppose \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) and \(h\in\mathbb R\).  Then \((\eta,h)\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}}\): the set \(\mathcal S(\eta,h)\) is always a separated singleton.  Its member \(x_\star(\eta,h)\) is given by the following explicit scalar map.  If \(h=0\), then \(x_\star(\eta,0)=0\).  If \(h\neq0\), define
\[
 s=\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b),\quad
 z_0=\kappa|h\mu_a\mu_b|,\quad
 b_0=\kappa|h\mu_a|\sqrt{v_b},\quad
 c_0=\kappa|h\mu_b|\sqrt{v_a},\quad
 d_0=\kappa|h|\sqrt{v_av_b},
\]
and, for \(0\leq z\leq z_0\), put
\[
 w=z_0-z,\qquad Q(z)=z^2+b_0^2+c_0^2+d_0^2,
\]
\[
 \tau(z)=\frac{Q(z)+\sqrt{Q(z)^2-4d_0^2w^2}}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal R(z)=\frac{d_0^2w\tau(z)\{\tau(z)+zw\}}
 {z\tau(z)+d_0^2w}.
\]
Let \(z_\star=0\) when \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\); when \(\tau(0)^2>c\), let \(z_\star\) be the unique root of \(\mathcal R(z)=c\) in \((0,z_0)\).  Then
\[
 \mathcal S(\eta,h)=\{x_\star(\eta,h)\},\qquad
 x_\star(\eta,h)=\frac{s}{\kappa}(z_0-z_\star).
\]
The map \((\eta,h)\mapsto x_\star(\eta,h)\) is continuous.  For every compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \inf_{(\eta,h)\in\mathcal K}\ \inf_{|x-x_\star(\eta,h)|\geq\varepsilon}
 \left\{\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)-\Phi_{\eta,h}\bigl(x_\star(\eta,h)\bigr)\right\}>0. \tag{8}
\]
Consequently, let \((\eta_n,h_n)\in\mathcal K\), let \(C_n:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be random criteria, and let \(\widehat x_n\) be any measurable global minimizer.  If \(\widehat x_n\) is uniformly tight and, for every finite \(M\),
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq M}\left|
 \{C_n(x)-C_n(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n))\}
 -\{\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x)-\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n))\}
 \right|\xrightarrow{p}0,
\]
then \(\widehat x_n-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\xrightarrow{p}0\).  Thus no coefficient-indexed next-order noise process is needed to break a first-order tie.

The critical selection is one-sided but remains unique.  At a point with \(h\neq0\) and \(\tau(0)^2=c\), consider any admissible path indexed by \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) for which
\[
 \Delta_\epsilon=\tau_\epsilon(0)^2-c_\epsilon
 =\epsilon\dot\Delta+o(\epsilon).
\]
Writing \(\mathcal R_0'(0)<0\) for the derivative at the limiting point, one has
\[
 z_{\star,\epsilon}
 =\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}+o(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 x_{\star,\epsilon}-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
 =-\frac{s}{\kappa}\epsilon
 \frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R_0'(0)}+o(\epsilon).
\]
In particular, opposite admissible \(m^{-1/4}\) drifts can change this local directional derivative, but both selections converge to the same critical minimizer; they cannot produce different separated limiting selections with identical limiting nuisance coordinates.

%%% FIELD proof-global-profile-singleton
Because \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\), the coordinates \(c,p,q,v_a,v_b\) are strictly positive, \(\mu_a\mu_b\neq0\), and
\[
 \kappa=\sqrt{\max\{c,1\}pq}>0.
\]
First suppose \(h\neq0\), and define the orthogonal sign matrices
\[
 U=\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{sgn}(\mu_a),1\},
 \qquad
 V=\operatorname{diag}\{\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_b),\operatorname{sgn}(h)\}.
\]
Writing \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(h\mu_a\mu_b)\), direct substitution in the definition of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\) gives
\[
 UB_{\eta,h}(x)V
 =\begin{pmatrix}z&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad z=z_0-s\kappa x,
\]
where
\[
 z_0=\kappa|h\mu_a\mu_b|,
 \quad b_0=\kappa|h\mu_a|\sqrt{v_b},
 \quad c_0=\kappa|h\mu_b|\sqrt{v_a},
 \quad d_0=\kappa|h|\sqrt{v_av_b}.
\]
All of \(b_0,c_0,d_0,z_0\) are strictly positive, and
\[
 \frac{b_0c_0}{d_0}=z_0.
\]
Left and right multiplication by orthogonal matrices preserves the Frobenius norm and every singular value.  Moreover, \(x\mapsto z_0-s\kappa x\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb R\).  Thus \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\) is exactly the criterion of the already established scalar-profile singleton lemma, with \(\rho=c\), under this affine change of coordinate.  That lemma proves that the unique global minimizer is \(z_\star=0\) when \(\tau(0)^2\leq c\), and otherwise is the unique \(z_\star\in(0,z_0)\) satisfying \(\mathcal R(z_\star)=c\).  Inverting the affine change gives
\[
 x_\star(\eta,h)=\frac{s}{\kappa}(z_0-z_\star).
\]
Since \(sz_0/\kappa=h\mu_a\mu_b\), the minimizer equals \(h\mu_a\mu_b\) in the first case and lies strictly between \(0\) and \(h\mu_a\mu_b\), in the signed direction, in the second case.

If \(h=0\), then \(B_{\eta,0}(x)=-\kappa xe_{11}\).  When \(\kappa^2x^2\leq\sqrt c\),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=\kappa^2x^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2,
\]
whose unique minimum on this region is at \(x=0\).  When \(\kappa^2x^2>\sqrt c\),
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,0}(x)=-(1+c)-\frac{c}{\kappa^2x^2}
 >-(1+\sqrt c)^2=\Phi_{\eta,0}(0).
\]
Hence \(x_\star(\eta,0)=0\), uniquely.

We next prove continuity and uniform separation.  The map \((\eta,h,x)\mapsto\Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\) is continuous because the entries of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\) and its singular values are continuous and the two branches of \(\psi_c\) agree at \(s=c^{1/4}\).  Thus parameter convergence implies uniform convergence of the profiles on bounded \(x\)-sets.

Let \(\lambda_1(\eta,h,x)\geq\lambda_2(\eta,h,x)\geq0\) be the squared singular values of \(B_{\eta,h}(x)\), and put
\[
 d_0^2=\kappa^2h^2v_av_b,
 \qquad
 S=\kappa^2h^2(\mu_a^2+v_a)(\mu_b^2+v_b).
\]
The rank-one identity for \(A_\eta\) gives
\[
 \det B_{\eta,h}(x)=-\kappa^2xh\sqrt{v_av_b}.
\]
The squared Frobenius norm is \(\kappa^2x^2+O(|x|+1)\), uniformly on compact parameter sets, and
\(\lambda_1\lambda_2=\kappa^4x^2h^2v_av_b\).  The quadratic formula therefore gives, uniformly on every compact parameter set,
\[
 \lambda_1(\eta,h,x)\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \lambda_2(\eta,h,x)\longrightarrow d_0^2
 \quad\text{as }|x|\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{1}
\]
The outlier branch of \(\psi_c\) then implies
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\longrightarrow d_0^2-(1+c). \tag{2}
\]
At \(x=0\), the matrix \(B_{\eta,h}(0)=\kappa hA_\eta\) has rank at most one and nonzero squared singular value \(S\).  Subtracting \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)\) from (2) gives
\[
 G(\eta,h)=
 \begin{cases}
 d_0^2+2\sqrt c-S,&S\leq\sqrt c,\\
 d_0^2+c/S,&S>\sqrt c.
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
This function is continuous across \(S=\sqrt c\) and strictly positive: on the first branch it is at least \(d_0^2+\sqrt c\), and on the second branch positivity is immediate.  Hence every compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal E_{\mathrm{loc}}\times\mathbb R\) admits \(M<\infty\) and \(g>0\) such that
\[
 \Phi_{\eta,h}(x)\geq\Phi_{\eta,h}(0)+g,
 \qquad (\eta,h)\in\mathcal K,\quad |x|>M. \tag{4}
\]

Let \((\eta_n,h_n)\to(\eta,h)\) and \(x_n=x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\).  Equation (4) bounds \((x_n)\).  Along any convergent subsequence with limit \(\bar x\), local uniform convergence and
\[
 \Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)\leq\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(y),\qquad y\in\mathbb R,
\]
give \(\Phi_{\eta,h}(\bar x)\leq\Phi_{\eta,h}(y)\) for every \(y\).  Uniqueness forces \(\bar x=x_\star(\eta,h)\), so the entire sequence converges.  This proves continuity of \(x_\star\), including at \(h=0\) and at the spectral edge.

Fix a compact \(\mathcal K\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).  If the uniform separation in (8) failed, there would be \((\eta_n,h_n)\in\mathcal K\) and \(x_n\) satisfying
\[
 |x_n-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)|\geq\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(x_n)-
 \Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}\{x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{5}
\]
If \((x_n)\) were bounded, compactness and continuity would produce a point distinct from the unique limiting minimizer but with the same criterion value.  If it were unbounded along a subsequence, (4) and
\(\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}\{x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\}\leq\Phi_{\eta_n,h_n}(0)\) would bound (5) below by \(g\).  Both are contradictions.  This proves (8) and membership in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stab}}\).

For the stochastic consequence, let \(g_\varepsilon>0\) be the deterministic gap from (8).  Uniform tightness permits an \(M\) for which \(\widehat x_n\in[-M,M]\) with probability tending to one; continuity and compactness put every \(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\) in the same interval after enlarging it.  On the event that the centered criterion approximation is smaller than \(g_\varepsilon/2\) uniformly on this interval, no point at distance at least \(\varepsilon\) from \(x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)\) can be a global minimizer.  Therefore
\[
 \Pr\{|\widehat x_n-x_\star(\eta_n,h_n)|\geq\varepsilon\}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Finally, fix a critical point with \(h\neq0\) and \(\tau(0)^2=c\).  At \(z=0\), let \(t=\tau(0)\), let \(u\) be the smaller squared singular value of \(M(0)\), and put \(w=z_0\).  Then
\[
 t+u=Q(0),\qquad tu=d_0^2w^2,
\]
and the nonzero off-diagonal entry of \(M(0)^{\top}M(0)\) gives \(t>u>0\).  Differentiating
\[
 \tau(z)^2-Q(z)\tau(z)+d_0^2w(z)^2=0
\]
at zero, with \(Q'(0)=0\) and \(w'(0)=-1\), gives
\[
 \tau'(0)=\frac{2d_0^2w}{t-u}.
\]
Since \(\mathcal R(0)=t^2\), logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal R'(0)}{\mathcal R(0)}
 =\frac{2\tau'(0)}t+\frac wt-\frac{t}{d_0^2w}
 =\frac{4u}{w(t-u)}-\frac{w(t-u)}{tu}. \tag{6}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 M(0)^{\top}M(0)
 =\begin{pmatrix}c_0^2&c_0d_0\\c_0d_0&b_0^2+d_0^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its smaller eigenvalue satisfies \(u<c_0^2\), because the first coordinate vector has Rayleigh quotient \(c_0^2\) and is not an eigenvector.  Hence
\[
 (t-u)^2-4d_0^2u
 =(c_0^2-b_0^2-d_0^2)^2+4d_0^2(c_0^2-u)>0. \tag{7}
\]
Using \(tu=d_0^2w^2\), inequality (7) is equivalent to the right-hand side of (6) being negative.  Thus \(\mathcal R'_0(0)<0\).

Along the stated admissible path,
\[
 \mathcal R_\epsilon(0)-c_\epsilon
 =\Delta_\epsilon
 =\epsilon\dot\Delta+o(\epsilon). \tag{8}
\]
The exact selection rule gives \(z_{\star,\epsilon}=0\) whenever \(\Delta_\epsilon\leq0\).  When \(\Delta_\epsilon>0\),
\[
 0=\mathcal R_\epsilon(z_{\star,\epsilon})-c_\epsilon.
\]
Continuity of the minimizer gives \(z_{\star,\epsilon}\to0\); the mean-value theorem, (8), and continuity of \(\mathcal R_\epsilon'\) near zero therefore give
\[
 z_{\star,\epsilon}
 =\epsilon\frac{(\dot\Delta)_+}{-\mathcal R'_0(0)}+o(\epsilon). \tag{9}
\]
The limiting \(h\), \(\mu_a\), and \(\mu_b\) are nonzero, so their sign is locally constant, while \(\kappa_\epsilon\to\kappa\).  The exact inverse-coordinate identity
\[
 x_{\star,\epsilon}-h_\epsilon\mu_{a,\epsilon}\mu_{b,\epsilon}
 =-\frac{s_\epsilon}{\kappa_\epsilon}z_{\star,\epsilon}
\]
and (9) prove the second expansion.  In particular, setting \(\epsilon=m^{-1/4}\) changes a vanishing directional term but not the unique limiting selection.  Opposite admissible drifts therefore cannot produce different separated limits at identical limiting nuisance coordinates.
