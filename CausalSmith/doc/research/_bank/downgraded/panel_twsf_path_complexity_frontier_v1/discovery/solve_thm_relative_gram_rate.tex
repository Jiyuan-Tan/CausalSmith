Fix a mode and condition throughout on \( (\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}) \).  This conditioning is legitimate because \(\mathrm{ass{:}independent\mbox{-}selection}\) makes the errors in the selected inference blocks independent of the selection errors, and \(\lvert\mathcal D_{m,n}\rvert\) is bounded.  Put
\[
 b_n=\log(nH_n),\qquad w_n=\bar W_n^{\top}\beta_n .
\]
All constants below depend only on the radii in \(\mathrm{def{:}panel\mbox{-}class}\), never on the selected layout or on the member of \(\mathcal C_n\).

We first construct one uniform event.  Let \(E\) be any Gaussian design-error matrix having dimensions between fixed multiples of \(n\).  For fixed unit vectors \(u,v\), \(u^{\top}Ev\) is \(N(0,\sigma_n^2)\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\mbox{-}cells}\).  Taking \(1/4\)-nets of the two unit spheres, each of cardinality at most \(9^d\), and using \(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\max_{u,v}|u^{\top}Ev|\) on those nets gives
\[
 \Pr\{\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}>x\mid\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}\}
 \le 2\,9^{d_1+d_2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{8\sigma_n^2}\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
The dimension bounds in \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\mbox{-}dimensions}\), the scale bounds in \(\mathrm{ass{:}noise\mbox{-}scale}\), and a union bound over the finitely many selected design blocks therefore imply
\[
 \max\{\|E_{Y,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|E_{Z,m,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}},
             \|E_{W,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}\le K\sqrt n
 \tag{2}
\]
except on an event of probability at most \(K\exp(-c n)\).

We record the deterministic spectral consequences of (2).  Write \(\bar X\) for either \(\bar Y_n\) or a selected \(\bar Z_{m,n}\), write \(X=\bar X+E\), and let \(\widehat X\) be its declared rank truncation.  Weyl's inequality follows directly from
\(\lvert\|Xv\|_2-\|\bar Xv\|_2\rvert\le\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) and the min--max formula for singular values.  Hence \(\sigma_r(\widehat X)\ge c_s n/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), by \(\mathrm{ass{:}strong\mbox{-}spectrum}\).  If \(P_L,P_R\) and \(\widehat P_L,\widehat P_R\) are the population and fitted left and right singular-space projectors, projecting the equations \(X=\bar X+E\) onto the orthogonal complements of the two singular spaces yields
\[
 \|\widehat P_L-P_L\|_{\mathrm{op}}\vee
 \|\widehat P_R-P_R\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le K n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For completeness, the Moore--Penrose equations give, for equal-rank matrices \(A,B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B^\dagger-A^\dagger
={}&-B^\dagger(B-A)A^\dagger
 +B^\dagger B^{\dagger\top}(B-A)^\top(I-AA^\dagger)\\
 &+(I-B^\dagger B)(B-A)^\top A^{\dagger\top}A^\dagger .
\end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
Substituting \(A=\bar X\), \(B=\widehat X\), using \(\|\widehat X-\bar X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), and using the two lower singular-value bounds gives
\[
 \|\widehat X^\dagger\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le K n^{-1},\qquad
 \|\widehat X^\dagger-\bar X^\dagger\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le K n^{-3/2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus repeated nonzero singular values cause no problem: only the gap between the retained fixed-rank space and zero is used.  The deterministic spectral-tie convention in \(\mathrm{def{:}pcr\mbox{-}map}\) makes the maps measurable.

Consider any one of the unit, direct-response, or recursive-response PCR regressions.  In generic notation let \(\gamma=\bar X^\dagger\bar u\), \(\widehat\gamma=\widehat X^\dagger u\), \(u=\bar u+e\), and \(r=(I-P_L)\bar u\).  Since \(\gamma=P_R\gamma\) and \(\widehat X^\dagger X=\widehat P_R\), exact addition and subtraction gives
\[
 \widehat\gamma-\gamma
 = (\widehat P_R-P_R)\gamma
   -\widehat X^\dagger E\gamma
   +\widehat X^\dagger r
   +\widehat X^\dagger e .
 \tag{6}
\]
Bounded mean entries and (5) imply
\[
 \|\gamma\|_2\le K n^{-1/2},\qquad \|r\|_2\le K\sqrt n.
 \tag{7}
\]
Moreover, \(P_Lr=0\), so (3)--(5) give
\[
 \|\widehat X^\dagger r\|_2
 \le \|\widehat X^\dagger\|_{\mathrm{op}}
      \|\widehat P_L-P_L\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|r\|_2
 \le K n^{-1}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Conditional on \(X\), the last term of (6) is a Gaussian vector of rank at most \(r_0\), with largest covariance eigenvalue at most \(K n^{-2}\).  The exact moment-generating function
\(\mathbb E e^{t\chi_r^2}=(1-2t)^{-r/2}\), \(0<t<1/2\), and Chernoff's inequality therefore give
\[
 \Pr\{\|\widehat X^\dagger e\|_2>K\sqrt{b_n}/n\mid X,\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}\}
 \le K e^{-A_0b_n}
 \tag{9}
\]
for any fixed \(A_0>0\), after increasing \(K=K(A_0)\).  The response/design independence needed here is exactly \(\mathrm{ass{:}disjoint\mbox{-}inference\mbox{-}blocks}\).  Equations (2)--(9), followed by a union bound over the \(H_n\) direct responses, yield one event \(\mathcal E_n(A_0)\) on which
\[
 \|\widehat\beta_n-\beta_n\|_2
 \vee\|\widehat a_n-a_n\|_2
 \vee\max_{\ell\le H_n}\|\widehat a_{n,\ell}-a_{n,\ell}\|_2
 \le K\frac{\sqrt{b_n}}n,
 \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 \inf_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal C_n}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P}\{\mathcal E_n(A_0)\mid\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}\}
 \ge1-K(nH_n)^{-A_0}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The stated growing-path convention \(H_n=\lfloor n^{1/8}\rfloor\) makes the exponentially small probability in (2) smaller than the right-hand failure probability in (11).

We next propagate (10) through the actual observed terminal block and the companion derivative.  Bounded entries give \(\|\bar W_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le Kn\); hence (2), (7), and (10) imply
\[
 \|w_n\|_2\le K\sqrt n,\qquad
 \|W_n^\top\widehat\beta_n-w_n\|_2
 \le\|E_{W,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\beta_n\|_2
    +\|W_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\widehat\beta_n-\beta_n\|_2
 \le K\sqrt{b_n}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Let \(A_n=\Pi_n(a_n)\), \(\widehat A_n=\Pi_n(\widehat a_n)\), and \(u_n=K\sqrt{b_n}/n\).  Since
\[
 g_{n,1}=a_n,\qquad
 g_{n,j+1}=S_n^\top g_{n,j}+a_n e_{L_n}^\top g_{n,j},
\]
(7) gives
\[
 \max_{\ell\le H_n}\|g_{n,\ell}\|_2
 \le \|a_n\|_2(1+\|a_n\|_2)^{H_n-1}\le K n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The same inequality holds for \(\widehat g_{n,\ell}\).  Indeed, \(H_nu_n=o(1)\).  Also, the telescoping identity
\[
 \widehat A_n^j-A_n^j
 =\sum_{h=0}^{j-1}\widehat A_n^h(\widehat A_n-A_n)A_n^{j-1-h}
 \tag{14}
\]
together with \(\mathrm{ass{:}stable\mbox{-}short\mbox{-}recursion}\) first gives
\(\max_{j\le H_n}\|\widehat A_n^j\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le K\), and then gives
\[
 \max_{\ell\le H_n}\|\widehat g_{n,\ell}-g_{n,\ell}\|_2
 \le KH_nu_n.
 \tag{15}
\]
Differentiating the finite sum in (14) once shows
\[
 \max_{\ell\le H_n}\|J_{n,\ell}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KH_n.
 \tag{16}
\]
Subtracting the two differentiated sums, and applying (14) to each changed power, leaves at most \(2H_n^2\) summands, each bounded by \(Ku_n\); consequently
\[
 \max_{\ell\le H_n}\|\widehat J_{n,\ell}-J_{n,\ell}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le KH_n^2u_n.
 \tag{17}
\]
This displays every horizon factor rather than absorbing it into a fixed-horizon constant.

The population definitions, (5), and (7), (12)--(17) imply
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \|\beta_n\|_2\vee\|a_n\|_2\vee
 \max_\ell\{\|a_{n,\ell}\|_2,\|g_{n,\ell}\|_2,
                 \|p_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}\|_2,
                 \|p_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}\|_2\}\le K n^{-1/2},\\
 \max_\ell\|q_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}\|_2\le K n^{-1/2},\qquad
 \max_\ell\|q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}\|_2\le KH_n n^{-1/2}.
\end{gathered}
 \tag{18}
\]
Here the direct temporal representer is common across leads, but retaining the index is harmless.  Expanding each fitted Riesz vector around its population counterpart gives
\[
 \max_\ell\bigl(
 \|\widehat p_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}-p_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}\|_2+
 \|\widehat q_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}-q_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}\|_2\bigr)
 \le K\frac{\sqrt{b_n}}n,
 \tag{19}
\]
and
\[
 \max_\ell\|\widehat p_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}-p_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}\|_2
 \le K\frac{H_n\sqrt{b_n}}n.
 \tag{20}
\]
For example, (20) follows from the exact three-term decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat p_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}-p_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}
={}&(\widehat Y_n^{\dagger\top}-\bar Y_n^{\dagger\top})\bar W_ng_{n,\ell}
 +\widehat Y_n^{\dagger\top}E_{W,n}g_{n,\ell}\\
 &+\widehat Y_n^{\dagger\top}W_n(\widehat g_{n,\ell}-g_{n,\ell}),
\end{aligned}
\]
whose terms are respectively \(O(n^{-1})\), \(O(n^{-1})\), and \(O(H_n\sqrt{b_n}/n)\).  Most importantly, the actual recursive temporal representer, with neither an oracle terminal block nor an oracle Jacobian, satisfies the exact three-piece perturbation identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}-q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}
={}&(\widehat Z_{\mathrm{rec},n}^{\dagger\top}
       -\bar Z_{\mathrm{rec},n}^{\dagger\top})J_{n,\ell}^{\top}w_n\\
 &+\widehat Z_{\mathrm{rec},n}^{\dagger\top}J_{n,\ell}^{\top}
      (W_n^\top\widehat\beta_n-w_n)\\
 &+\widehat Z_{\mathrm{rec},n}^{\dagger\top}
      (\widehat J_{n,\ell}-J_{n,\ell})^{\top}W_n^\top\widehat\beta_n .
\end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
By (5), (12), and (16)--(17), its three lines are bounded respectively by
\[
 K\frac{H_n}{n},\qquad
 K\frac{H_n\sqrt{b_n}}n,\qquad
 K\frac{H_n^2\sqrt{b_n}}{n^{3/2}}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Since \(H_n\le n^{1/8}\), (22) proves
\[
 \max_{\ell\le H_n}
 \|\widehat q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}-q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}\|_2
 \le K\frac{H_n\sqrt{b_n}}n.
 \tag{23}
\]

We now expose the covariance map.  Independence of the primitive blocks in \(\mathrm{ass{:}gaussian\mbox{-}cells}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}disjoint\mbox{-}inference\mbox{-}blocks}\), applied to \(\mathrm{def{:}stacked\mbox{-}scores}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{\Sigma_{\mathrm{dir},n}(\ell,k)}{\sigma_n^2}
={}&\|\beta_n\|_2^2a_{n,\ell}^{\top}a_{n,k}
 +(1+\|\beta_n\|_2^2)p_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}^{\top}p_{\mathrm{dir},n,k}\\
 &+q_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}^{\top}q_{\mathrm{dir},n,k}
   \{\mathbf 1\{\ell=k\}+a_{n,\ell}^{\top}a_{n,k}\},
\end{aligned}
 \tag{24}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{\Sigma_{\mathrm{rec},n}(\ell,k)}{\sigma_n^2}
={}&\|\beta_n\|_2^2g_{n,\ell}^{\top}g_{n,k}
 +(1+\|\beta_n\|_2^2)p_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}^{\top}p_{\mathrm{rec},n,k}\\
 &+(1+\|a_n\|_2^2)q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}^{\top}q_{\mathrm{rec},n,k}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{25}
\]
For instance,
\(\operatorname{Cov}(e_{\mathrm{dir},n,\ell}-E_{Z,\mathrm{dir},n}a_{n,\ell},
e_{\mathrm{dir},n,k}-E_{Z,\mathrm{dir},n}a_{n,k})
=\sigma_n^2\{\mathbf 1\{\ell=k\}+a_{n,\ell}^{\top}a_{n,k}\}I\), which proves the last line of (24); the other lines follow identically.  Thus the fitted coefficient Gram matrices are obtained from (24)--(25) by putting hats on every coefficient.  In particular, with \(\widehat K_{m,n}=\widehat Q_{m,n}\widehat Q_{m,n}^{\top}\),
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{m,n}=\widehat\sigma_n^2\widehat K_{m,n}
 =\widehat\sigma_n^2\widehat Q_{m,n}\widehat Q_{m,n}^{\top}\succeq0.
 \tag{26}
\]
Every quantity on the right of (26) is computed from the observed blocks listed in \(\mathrm{def{:}gram\mbox{-}covariance}\), so the map is observable.  It remains positive semidefinite when singular.

For vectors \(x_\ell,x_k\) and perturbations \(d_\ell,d_k\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \bigl|(x_\ell+d_\ell)^\top(x_k+d_k)-x_\ell^\top x_k\bigr|
 \le\|x_\ell\|_2\|d_k\|_2+
      \|x_k\|_2\|d_\ell\|_2+
      \|d_\ell\|_2\|d_k\|_2.
 \tag{27}
\]
Applying (27) to every Gram term in (24)--(25), and using (10), (15), and (18)--(23), proves on \(\mathcal E_n(A_0)\)
\[
 \max_{\ell,k\le H_n}
 |\widehat K_{\mathrm{dir},n}(\ell,k)-K_{\mathrm{dir},n}(\ell,k)|
 \le K\frac{\sqrt{b_n}}{n^{3/2}},
 \tag{28}
\]
\[
 \max_{\ell,k\le H_n}
 |\widehat K_{\mathrm{rec},n}(\ell,k)-K_{\mathrm{rec},n}(\ell,k)|
 \le K\frac{H_n^2\sqrt{b_n}}{n^{3/2}},
 \tag{29}
\]
where \(K_{m,n}=\Sigma_{m,n}/\sigma_n^2\).  To see the largest term in (29), use
\(\|q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}\|_2\le KH_n/\sqrt n\) and
\(\|\widehat q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}-q_{\mathrm{rec},n,\ell}\|_2
\le KH_n\sqrt{b_n}/n\) in (27); their product is
\(KH_n^2\sqrt{b_n}/n^{3/2}\).  All remaining terms have no larger order.  This is a simultaneous pairwise bound and therefore incurs no additional \(H_n^2\) union factor.

It remains to audit \(\widehat\sigma_n^2\).  For either of the two regressions pooled in \(\mathrm{def{:}gram\mbox{-}covariance}\), let \(\widehat P_X\) be its fitted observation-space projector and write
\[
 (I-\widehat P_X)u=v+(I-\widehat P_X)e,\qquad
 v=(I-\widehat P_X)\bar u.
 \tag{30}
\]
The invariant-unit factor equation in \(\mathrm{ass{:}factor\mbox{-}model}\) and the corresponding temporal response-span equation in \(\mathrm{ass{:}direct\mbox{-}response\mbox{-}span}\) or \(\mathrm{ass{:}recursive\mbox{-}recovery}\) put \(\bar u\) in the population observation space used by that regression.  Therefore (3), bounded means, and balanced dimensions imply
\[
 \|v\|_2=\|(P_L-\widehat P_X)\bar u\|_2\le K.
 \tag{31}
\]
Conditional on its design, \(\|(I-\widehat P_X)e\|_2^2/\sigma_n^2\) is chi-square with residual degrees of freedom equal to the response dimension minus the retained rank.  These degrees of freedom are positive and of order \(n\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\mbox{-}dimensions}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\mbox{-}rank}\).  The cross term in the square of (30) is centered Gaussian with variance at most \(4\sigma_n^2\|v\|_2^2\), and \(\|v\|_2^2\) contributes \(O(n^{-1})\) after division by the pooled degrees of freedom.  Applying the same chi-square moment-generating-function calculation as in (9) to the two independent residual blocks gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\widehat\sigma_n^2}{\sigma_n^2}-1\right|
 \le K\sqrt{\frac{b_n}{n}}
 \tag{32}
\]
on an enlargement of \(\mathcal E_n(A_0)\) preserving (11).

Let
\[
 B_{m,n}=\max_{\ell,k\le H_n}
 \frac{|\widehat\Sigma_{m,n}(\ell,k)-\Sigma_{m,n}(\ell,k)|}
 {s_{m,n,\ell}s_{m,n,k}}.
\]
From (26),
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{m,n}-\Sigma_{m,n}
 =\widehat\sigma_n^2(\widehat K_{m,n}-K_{m,n})
   +(\widehat\sigma_n^2-\sigma_n^2)K_{m,n}.
 \tag{33}
\]
The covariance Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\(\lvert\Sigma_{m,n}(\ell,k)\rvert\le s_{m,n,\ell}s_{m,n,k}\), while \(\mathrm{ass{:}variance\mbox{-}floor}\) gives
\(s_{m,n,\ell}s_{m,n,k}\ge c_v^2/n\).  Combining (28)--(33) therefore yields
\[
 B_{\mathrm{dir},n}=O_{\Pr}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{b_n}{n}}\right),\qquad
 B_{\mathrm{rec},n}=O_{\Pr}\!\left(H_n^2\sqrt{\frac{b_n}{n}}\right),
 \tag{34}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal C_n\).

Finally suppose \(B_{m,n}\le1/2\), an event whose probability tends uniformly to one in the rate regime used by the band theorem.  Put
\(e_\ell=\widehat\Sigma_{m,n}(\ell,\ell)/s_{m,n,\ell}^2-1\).  Then
\[
 \left|\frac{\widehat s_{m,n,\ell}}{s_{m,n,\ell}}-1\right|
 =|\sqrt{1+e_\ell}-1|\le|e_\ell|\le B_{m,n},
\]
so \(t_{m,n}\le B_{m,n}\).  Writing \(r_\ell=\widehat s_{m,n,\ell}/s_{m,n,\ell}\in[1-B_{m,n},1+B_{m,n}]\), we also have
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat R_{m,n}(\ell,k)-R_{m,n}(\ell,k)|
 \le{}&\frac{B_{m,n}}{r_\ell r_k}
 +|R_{m,n}(\ell,k)|\left|\frac1{r_\ell r_k}-1\right|\\
 \le{}&K B_{m,n},
\end{aligned}
 \tag{35}
\]
because \(|R_{m,n}(\ell,k)|\le1\).  Hence
\[
 t_{m,n}\vee\Delta_{m,n}\le K B_{m,n}.
 \tag{36}
\]
Equations (26), (34), and (36) prove all parts of the theorem, including singular fitted and population covariance matrices; no covariance inverse or lower eigenvalue bound was used.

Fix a prespecified mode \(m\) and first condition on \((\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n})\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}stacked\mbox{-}orthogonal\mbox{-}expansion}\),
\[
 \widehat\theta_{m,n,\ell}-\theta_{n,\ell}
 =G_{m,n,\ell}+r_{m,n,\ell},\qquad 1\le\ell\le H_n,
 \tag{37}
\]
and the standardized leading vector is exactly \(N(0,R_{m,n})\).  Define
\[
 T_n=\max_{\ell\le H_n}\left|\frac{G_{m,n,\ell}}{s_{m,n,\ell}}\right|,\qquad
 F_n(x)=\Pr(T_n\le x\mid\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}).
 \tag{38}
\]
Every coordinate in (38) has variance one by \(\mathrm{ass{:}variance\mbox{-}floor}\).  Consequently \(F_n\) is continuous even when \(R_{m,n}\) is singular: for every \(x\ge0\), the event \(\{T_n=x\}\) is contained in the finite union of the null events
\(\{|G_{m,n,\ell}|=xs_{m,n,\ell}\}\); for \(x<0\) the assertion is immediate.

Let \(\widehat F_n\) be the conditional distribution function of the multiplier maximum in \(\mathrm{def{:}multiplier\mbox{-}band}\), and let \(\widehat c_n\) be its lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile.  Choose deterministic numbers \(e\ge0\), \(0\le t<1/2\), and
\(0\le d<\frac12\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\), and work on the event
\[
 \varepsilon_{m,n}\le e,\qquad t_{m,n}\le t,\qquad d_{m,n}\le d.
 \tag{39}
\]
With the lower-quantile convention \(F_n^{-1}(u)=\inf\{x:F_n(x)\ge u\}\), put
\[
 c_-=F_n^{-1}(1-\alpha-d),\qquad
 c_+=F_n^{-1}(1-\alpha+d).
 \tag{40}
\]
The definition of Kolmogorov distance gives, pathwise,
\[
 c_-\le\widehat c_n\le c_+.
 \tag{41}
\]
Indeed, if \(x<c_-\), then \(\widehat F_n(x)\le F_n(x)+d<1-\alpha\); and
\(\widehat F_n(c_+)\ge F_n(c_+)-d\ge1-\alpha\).

The expansion (37), the scale inequalities
\(1-t\le\widehat s_{m,n,\ell}/s_{m,n,\ell}\le1+t\), and (41) imply the deterministic inclusions
\[
 \{T_n\le(1-t)c_- -e\}
 \subseteq\{\boldsymbol\theta_n\in\mathcal B_{m,n}\}
 \subseteq\{T_n\le(1+t)c_+ +e\}.
 \tag{42}
\]
For the first inclusion, coordinatewise,
\[
 \frac{|\widehat\theta_{m,n,\ell}-\theta_{n,\ell}|}{s_{m,n,\ell}}
 \le T_n+e\le(1-t)c_-
 \le\widehat c_n\frac{\widehat s_{m,n,\ell}}{s_{m,n,\ell}}.
\]
For the second, coverage and the reverse triangle inequality give
\[
 \frac{|G_{m,n,\ell}|}{s_{m,n,\ell}}
 \le\widehat c_n\frac{\widehat s_{m,n,\ell}}{s_{m,n,\ell}}+e
 \le(1+t)c_++e
\]
for every coordinate.

Because \(T_n\ge0\) and \(\mathbb ET_n=\kappa_{m,n}\), Markov's inequality gives an upper bound for the deterministic quantile in (40):
\[
 c_+\le\frac{\kappa_{m,n}}{\alpha-d}\le C_\alpha(\kappa_{m,n}+1).
 \tag{43}
\]
Continuity gives \(F_n(c_-)=1-\alpha-d\) and \(F_n(c_+)=1-\alpha+d\).  Apply the anti-concentration part of
\(\mathrm{lem{:}gaussian\mbox{-}max\mbox{-}comparison\mbox{-}anticoncentration}\) to the two threshold displacements in (42).  Equations (42)--(43) then yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\left|\Pr\{\boldsymbol\theta_n\in\mathcal B_{m,n}\mid
       \mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\\
 &\qquad\le C_\alpha\left\{
 d+(\kappa_{m,n}+1)e+(\kappa_{m,n}+1)^2t\right\}
\end{aligned}
 \tag{44}
\]
apart from the conditional probability of the complement of (39).  This is a deterministic \(F_n\)-quantile sandwich; it never conditions the Gaussian score on the fitted covariance and is therefore valid even though those two objects use common data.

We make the passage from the stochastic premise to (44) explicit.  Let
\[
 A_{m,n}=(\kappa_{m,n}+1)\varepsilon_{m,n}
 +(\kappa_{m,n}+1)^2t_{m,n}+d_{m,n}.
\]
Uniform \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) in the conditional class means that there are deterministic \(a_n\downarrow0\) and \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal C_n}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P}\{A_{m,n}>a_n\mid\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}\}
 \le\eta_n.
 \tag{45}
\]
On \(\{A_{m,n}\le a_n\}\), use (39) with
\[
 d=a_n,\qquad e=\frac{a_n}{\kappa_{m,n}+1},\qquad
 t=\frac{a_n}{(\kappa_{m,n}+1)^2}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), these values satisfy the restrictions following (39).  Equations (43)--(45) give
\[
 \sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal C_n}
 \left|\Pr_{\mathbb P}\{\boldsymbol\theta_n\in\mathcal B_{m,n}\mid
       \mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n}\}-(1-\alpha)\right|
 \le C_\alpha a_n+\eta_n=o(1).
 \tag{46}
\]
Finally average (46) over \(\widehat d_{m,n}\) conditional on \(\mathcal F_n\).  The finite-menu uniformity and \(\mathrm{ass{:}independent\mbox{-}selection}\) preserve the same bound, proving the claimed conditional-on-\(\mathcal F_n\) coverage.  Neither (38)--(46) nor the cited Gaussian lemma uses \(R_{m,n}^{-1}\) or a positive minimum eigenvalue.

It remains to verify the premise at the advertised horizon.  For any correlation matrix, a union bound gives
\[
 \Pr(T_n>x\mid\mathcal F_n,\widehat d_{m,n})\le2H_n e^{-x^2/2}.
\]
Integrating this tail at \(x_0=\sqrt{2\log(2H_n)}\) yields
\[
 \kappa_{m,n}\le x_0+\int_{x_0}^{\infty}2H_ne^{-x^2/2}\,dx
 \le C\sqrt{\log(2H_n)}.
 \tag{47}
\]
At \(H_n=\lfloor n^{1/8}\rfloor\), \(b_n=\log(nH_n)\asymp\log n\).  The established expansion theorem and \(\mathrm{thm{:}relative\mbox{-}gram\mbox{-}rate}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{dir},n}
   &=O_{\Pr}(n^{-1/2}\log n),
 &t_{\mathrm{dir},n}\vee\Delta_{\mathrm{dir},n}
   &=O_{\Pr}(n^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}),\\
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rec},n}
   &=O_{\Pr}(n^{-3/8}\log n+n^{-3/4}\log n),
 &t_{\mathrm{rec},n}\vee\Delta_{\mathrm{rec},n}
   &=O_{\Pr}(n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log n}).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{48}
\]
The comparison part of
\(\mathrm{lem{:}gaussian\mbox{-}max\mbox{-}comparison\mbox{-}anticoncentration}\), with the value zero at \(\Delta_{m,n}=0\), now gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{dir},n}
 =O_{\Pr}\!\left(n^{-1/6}(\log n)^{5/6}\right),\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{rec},n}
 =O_{\Pr}\!\left(n^{-1/12}(\log n)^{5/6}\right).
 \tag{49}
\]
Combining (47)--(49), the largest recursive remainder and scale contributions are respectively
\[
 O_{\Pr}\!\left(n^{-3/8}(\log n)^{3/2}
                  +n^{-3/4}(\log n)^{3/2}\right),\qquad
 O_{\Pr}\!\left(n^{-1/4}(\log n)^{3/2}\right),
\]
and both tend to zero; the direct contributions are smaller.  Equation (49) tends to zero as well, so the premise of the theorem is verified.

When \(H_n=1\), the multiplier maximum is \(|N(0,1)|\), its critical value is \(\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\), and the construction reduces to the corresponding scalar Gaussian TWSF interval.  For \(H_n>1\), (46) controls the intersection of all coordinate coverage events, which is strictly stronger than separate pointwise guarantees.  Throughout, the target is the conditional-mean path \(\boldsymbol\theta_n\); no future realized prediction shock is included.
