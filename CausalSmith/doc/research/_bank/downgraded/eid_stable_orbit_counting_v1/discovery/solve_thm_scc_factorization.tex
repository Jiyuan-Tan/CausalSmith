%%% FIELD thm-scc-factorization-real-statement
For every positive integer \(q\) and every real matrix \(W\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}\), put \(B=I_q-W\). Restriction to the strongly connected components of the directed support graph of \(B\) gives bijections
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C).
\]
The inverse maps concatenate the local permutations in any fixed topological order of the condensation graph. Consequently,
\[
 N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_C.
\]
No rationality, invertibility, unit-diagonal, or reference-stability condition on \(W\) is required.
%%% FIELD thm-scc-factorization-real-proof
Fix a positive integer \(q\) and a matrix \(W\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}\), and set \(B=I_q-W\). Let \(D(B)\) be the directed support graph with an arrow \(j\to i\) exactly when \(B_{ij}\neq0\). For distinct indices \(i,r\in[q]\), the off-diagonal entries of the identity matrix vanish, and hence
\[
 B_{ri}=(I_q)_{ri}-W_{ri}=-W_{ri}.
\]
It follows directly from the definition of \(D(B)\) that
\[
 W_{ri}\neq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_{ri}\neq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\to r\text{ is an arrow of }D(B)
 \qquad(i\neq r). \tag{1}
\]

We first prove the admissible product decomposition. Take \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\). By the definition of the admissible orbit,
\[
 W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0\qquad\text{for every }i\in[q]. \tag{2}
\]
Consider a nontrivial cycle of \(\pi\), written with distinct vertices as
\[
 i_0\mapsto i_1\mapsto\cdots\mapsto i_{s-1}\mapsto i_0,
 \qquad s\ge2,
\]
where indices on the \(i_t\)'s are read modulo \(s\). Equations (1) and (2), applied to \(\pi(i_t)=i_{t+1}\), give the directed cycle
\[
 i_0\to i_1\to\cdots\to i_{s-1}\to i_0
\]
in \(D(B)\). Every two vertices on this cycle are mutually reachable, so the entire permutation cycle is contained in one strongly connected component of \(D(B)\). A fixed point of \(\pi\) is contained in its own strongly connected component without requiring any self-loop. Since the cycles partition \([q]\), we have
\[
 \pi(C)=C
 \qquad\text{for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B). \tag{3}
\]

Identify each component with its inherited increasing ordering. By (3), the restriction \(\pi_C:=\pi|_C\) is a permutation of \(C\). For \(i\in C\), principality of \(W_C=W[C,C]\) and (2) yield
\[
 (W_C)_{\pi_C(i),i}=W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0. \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\pi_C\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\), and restriction defines a map
\[
 R_{\mathrm{adm}}:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C),
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{adm}}(\pi)=(\pi_C)_C. \tag{5}
\]
Conversely, let \((\sigma_C)_C\) belong to the product on the right of (5). Define \(\sigma\) on the disjoint union \([q]=\bigsqcup_C C\) by \(\sigma(i)=\sigma_C(i)\) for \(i\in C\), concatenating blocks in a fixed topological order. Since every \(\sigma_C\) is a bijection of \(C\), \(\sigma\) is a permutation of \([q]\). Local admissibility gives, for every \(i\in C\),
\[
 W_{\sigma(i),i}=(W_C)_{\sigma_C(i),i}\neq0. \tag{6}
\]
Hence \(\sigma\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\). The constructions in (5) and (6) are inverse because restriction followed by disjoint-union concatenation, and concatenation followed by restriction, preserve every coordinate. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \cong
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C). \tag{7}
\]
All sets in (7) are finite. Taking cardinalities and applying the definition of \(N_{\mathrm{adm}}\) proves
\[
 N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C). \tag{8}
\]

We now prove that the same bijection factors the stability filter. Put the strongly connected components in the same topological order. If a block of \(B\) strictly violates the corresponding triangular pattern, one of its nonzero entries would give an arrow in the condensation graph directed opposite to that topological order. Therefore \(B\) is block triangular in this ordering. Since \(W=I_q-B\), the matrix \(W\) has the same block-triangular pattern and diagonal blocks \(W_C\).

Let \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\). Equation (3) says that \(P_\pi\) is block diagonal. The normalization matrix
\[
 D_\pi^{-1}:=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(W_{\pi(1),1}^{-1},\ldots,W_{\pi(q),q}^{-1}\bigr)
\]
exists by (2) and is also block diagonal. Thus \(T_\pi=D_\pi^{-1}P_\pi\) is block diagonal, so \(W_\pi=T_\pi W\) is block triangular. For every component \(C\), equation (4) shows that the global and local diagonal normalizers agree on \(C\), and consequently
\[
 (W_\pi)[C,C]
 =T_{\pi_C}W_C. \tag{9}
\]
Subtracting (9) from the corresponding identity block shows that \(B_\pi=I_q-W_\pi\) is block triangular with diagonal block
\[
 (B_\pi)[C,C]
 =I_{|C|}-T_{\pi_C}W_C
 =:B^C_{\pi_C}, \tag{10}
\]
where the last matrix is exactly the normalized coefficient candidate constructed from \(W_C\) and \(\pi_C\).

Applying the determinant expansion successively to the leading diagonal blocks of the block-triangular matrix in (10) gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \det(zI_q-B_\pi)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
   \det\bigl(zI_{|C|}-B^C_{\pi_C}\bigr). \tag{11}
\]
Indeed, for two diagonal blocks the only nonzero terms in the Leibniz expansion pair rows and columns within the same diagonal block, yielding the product of the two block determinants; induction gives (11). Hence the roots of the characteristic polynomial of \(B_\pi\), counted with algebraic multiplicity, are the multiset union of the roots of the characteristic polynomials of the matrices \(B^C_{\pi_C}\). By the definition of spectral radius,
\[
 \rho(B_\pi)
 =\max_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\rho(B^C_{\pi_C}), \tag{12}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \rho(B_\pi)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho(B^C_{\pi_C})<1
 \text{ for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B). \tag{13}
\]
By the definitions of the local normalized candidate and stable orbit, the right-hand side of (13) is equivalent to
\[
 \pi_C\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)
 \qquad\text{for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B). \tag{14}
\]
Thus the admissible bijection (7) restricts, in both directions, to the bijection
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 \cong
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C). \tag{15}
\]
Taking cardinalities in (15), then using the definitions \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)=|\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)|\) and \(N_C=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)\), gives
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_C. \tag{16}
\]
The argument used only the displayed real-matrix identities and the orbit definitions. In particular, it did not invoke rationality, invertibility of \(W\), unit diagonal, or stability of the reference matrix \(B\), which proves the asserted scope.
