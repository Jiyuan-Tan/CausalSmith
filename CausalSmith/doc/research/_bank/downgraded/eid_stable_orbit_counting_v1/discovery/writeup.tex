\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}stable\_\allowbreak{}orbit\_\allowbreak{}counting}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A fully observed cyclic LiNG law identifies a finite monomial row-assignment orbit, while equilibrium stability imposes a global spectral filter on that orbit. The explicit rational \(3n\)-variable construction \(W_G\) has complete stable orbit \(\{\pi_S:S\in\mathcal I(G)\}\), with graph-uniform radial margins established by a pole-free, multiplicity-preserving analytic determinant homotopy. On fixed-labelled gadget images, exact counting is in \(\mathrm{FP}\) through degree two and \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete already at degree three; approximation has a deterministic FPTAS through degree five and no FPRAS at degree six unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\). Hardness also persists on coned gadget images whose coefficient-support digraph is one SCC, without a bounded-degree promise, so it is not caused by multiplying SCC-local counts. In contrast, the unfiltered orbit is a permanent with an FPRAS. Admissible and stable orbit counts factor exactly over SCCs for every real equation matrix; only the enumerator and its Lyapunov and bit-complexity guarantees are restricted to rational stable-reference inputs. Symmetrized FOBI-region inversion gives pointwise signed-orbit-honest count coverage, without a uniform graph-support or stability-boundary claim.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published cyclic-LiNG methods describe the normalized row-assignment fiber and may screen individual candidates by stability, but they do not classify the stability-filtered count, locate its bounded-degree exact and approximation thresholds, or show whether hardness survives inside one feedback SCC. Donor results for independent sets and permanent-based sampling do not themselves provide a coefficient-valid cyclic-LiNG transfer.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing bridge is a rational coefficient construction that controls normalization, all unintended permutations, analytic multiplicity, Schur-complement poles, polynomial-bit spectral margins, and the support-SCC geometry uniformly over the donor graph.

\paragraph{Fill.} The graph gadget identifies its complete stable orbit with independent sets. On fixed-labelled gadget images this yields the exact degree-two/three and approximation degree-five/six elbows, while a cone reduction proves exact and approximate hardness on one-SCC images. The note separately proves admissible and stable SCC factorization for arbitrary real equation matrices, an output-sensitive local enumerator on rational stable-reference inputs, and pointwise symmetrized FOBI confidence-image coverage modulo the signed row orbit.

\section{Related work}

Lacerda, Spirtes, Ramsey, and Hoyer characterize the cyclic LiNG row-permutation fiber and use Schur stability to screen candidates, but do not realize arbitrary independent-set systems or classify the filtered count. Sharifian, Salehkaleybar, and Kiyavash represent the ambiguity by perfect matchings, establish SCC invariance, and use unfiltered matching samples for intervention rewards; their sampler does not impose equilibrium stability, and must be conditioned on the stable orbit when stability is additionally required. Valiant supplies the general exact-counting donor, Greenhill proves exact independent-set hardness at degree three, Weitz gives the deterministic hard-core approximation scheme applicable at activity one through degree five, and Sly supplies the degree-six inapproximability donor. Those are graph results: the coefficient-valid, count-preserving transfer and the exclusion of unintended normalized assignments come from the present realization theorem. Jerrum, Sinclair, and Vigoda provide the nonnegative-permanent FPRAS for the unfiltered benchmark. The FOBI limit uses distinct centered standardized fourth cumulants and is symmetrized over signed row assignments, so covariance convergence is required only modulo signed-row conjugacy.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:linear-sem]\label{ass:linear-sem}
The observational vector satisfies \(WX=e\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (square linear structural equation model, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:unit-diagonal]\label{ass:unit-diagonal}
The equation matrix obeys \(W_{ii}=1\) for every \(i\in[q]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (no-self-loop normalization, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:invertible-equations]\label{ass:invertible-equations}
The matrix \(W\) is invertible.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (unique reduced-form solvability, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-errors]\label{ass:independent-errors}
The coordinates \(e_1,\ldots,e_q\) are mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent-component errors, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:non-gaussian-errors]\label{ass:non-gaussian-errors}
At most one coordinate of \(e\) has a Gaussian distribution.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (square ICA non-Gaussianity, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonzero-error-variances]\label{ass:nonzero-error-variances}
Every structural error coordinate has nonzero variance: \(0<\operatorname{Var}(e_i)<\infty\) for every \(i\in[q]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate square ICA components, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stable-reference]\label{ass:stable-reference}
The reference coefficient matrix satisfies \(\rho(B)<1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Schur equilibrium stability, \cite{LacerdaSpirtesRamseyHoyer2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rational-input]\label{ass:rational-input}
Every entry of \(W\) is rational with total binary encoding length \(L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (binary rational Turing input, \cite{Valiant1979}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
The observations \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) are independent and identically distributed according to \(P_X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent sampling for FOBI, \cite{MiettinenTaskinenNordhausenOja2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:eighth-moments]\label{ass:eighth-moments}
The moment \(\mathbb E[\|X\|_2^8]\) is finite.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded eighth-moment FOBI condition, \cite{MiettinenTaskinenNordhausenOja2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:distinct-kurtoses]\label{ass:distinct-kurtoses}
The coordinates of \(\kappa\) are pairwise distinct.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (distinct fourth moments for FOBI, \cite{MiettinenTaskinenNordhausenOja2015}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:population-orbit-identification]\label{prop:population-orbit-identification}
For every law in \(\mathcal M_q\), including the nondegeneracy condition \(0<\operatorname{Var}(e_i)<\infty\) for every \(i\in[q]\), square ICA identifies \([W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\) from \(P_X\). If \(\widetilde W=MW\) for an invertible monomial matrix \(M\), re-referencing induces a bijection between the admissible normalized assignments of \(W\) and \(\widetilde W\) that preserves their normalized coefficient matrices. Consequently \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(\widetilde W)=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\), so the stable count is a population-law functional rather than a labeling-dependent count.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \((P_X,W,e)\in\mathcal M_q\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:invertible\mbox{-}equations}\) and the structural equation give the square mixing representation
\[
 X=Ae,\qquad A=W^{-1}.
\]
The coordinates of \(e\) are mutually independent by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:independent\mbox{-}errors}\), have positive finite variances by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonzero\mbox{-}error\mbox{-}variances}\), and at most one is Gaussian by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:non\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}errors}\). The cited square-ICA identification lemma therefore implies that \(P_X\) determines \([W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\). This is identification of a structural orbit from the observed law; it does not define the orbit to equal an observed functional.

It remains to prove that the count does not depend on the representative of this identified orbit. Let
\[
 \widetilde W=MW,
 \qquad
 M=D P_\sigma,
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_q),
 \qquad a_i\neq0,
\]
where \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_q\). For \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_q\), define \(\tau=\sigma\circ\pi\). By the convention \((P_\pi)_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{j=\pi(i)\}\), the \(i\)-th row of \(P_\pi D P_\sigma W\) is \(a_{\pi(i)}\) times row \(\sigma(\pi(i))\) of \(W\). Hence
\[
 \widetilde W_{\pi(i),i}
 =a_{\pi(i)}W_{\sigma(\pi(i)),i}
 =a_{\pi(i)}W_{\tau(i),i}.
\]
Because every \(a_{\pi(i)}\) is nonzero, this proves the equivalence
\[
 \pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(\widetilde W)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \tau\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W).
\]
The map \(\pi\mapsto\sigma\circ\pi\) is a bijection of \(\mathfrak S_q\), with inverse \(\tau\mapsto\sigma^{-1}\circ\tau\), so it is a bijection of the two admissible sets.

For an admissible \(\pi\), direct substitution into the normalization gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde W_\pi
 &=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
      \frac{1}{a_{\pi(i)}W_{\tau(i),i}}:i\in[q]
    \right)P_\pi D P_\sigma W\\
 &=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
      \frac{1}{W_{\tau(i),i}}:i\in[q]
    \right)P_\tau W
 =W_\tau.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Therefore \(I_q-\widetilde W_\pi=I_q-W_\tau\), and so
\[
 \rho(I_q-\widetilde W_\pi)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho(I_q-W_\tau)<1.
\]
The admissible-set bijection consequently restricts to a bijection
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(\widetilde W)
 \longleftrightarrow
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
\]
that preserves the normalized coefficient matrix itself. Taking finite cardinalities yields
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(\widetilde W)=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W).
\]
Thus the explicitly identified map is
\[
 P_X\longmapsto[W]_{\mathrm{mon}}
       \longmapsto N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W),
\]
and the second arrow is well defined by the proved representative invariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition closes the identification step that turns a matrix-representative count into a causal functional of the observational law. \textit{Gap.} The ICA literature states sign, scale, and permutation indeterminacy, but stable-orbit counting requires an explicit normalization-preserving bijection across representatives. \textit{Consumer.} Any population or sample procedure may start from an arbitrary ICA labeling without changing the reported stable count.

\begin{proposition}[prop:gadget-law-validity]\label{prop:gadget-law-validity}
For every simple \(n\)-vertex graph \(G\), let \(e\) have independent, centered, variance-one, continuous non-Gaussian coordinates and set \(X=W_G^{-1}e\). Then \((P_X,W_G,e)\in\mathcal M_{3n}\). For every \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_G)\), the transformed errors \(T_\pi e\) remain independent, centered, continuous, and non-Gaussian with positive coordinate variances, so every normalized assignment is a legal same-law cyclic LiNG mechanism.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The graph-gadget definition gives \((B_G)_{ii}=0\), because \(G\) is simple and each local block has zero diagonal. Hence \((W_G)_{ii}=1\), which verifies the unit-diagonal assumption. To verify invertibility without using the later stability theorem, eliminate \(v_i,w_i\) from \((I_{3n}-B_G)x=0\). At \(z=1\), the unswitched calculation gives
\[
 \det(I_{3n}-B_G)
 =\det\!\left(\left(1-p-r\right)I_n-gA_G\right).
\]
Now
\[
 1-p-r=\frac{13}{8}+\frac{15}{16}\delta,
 \qquad \|gA_G\|_2\leq g(n-1)<1,
\]
so every eigenvalue of the displayed symmetric matrix is positive. Thus \(W_G\) is invertible.

By the hypothesis of the proposition, the coordinates of \(e\) are mutually independent, centered, continuous, non-Gaussian, and have variance one. Defining \(X=W_G^{-1}e\) gives \(W_GX=e\), so the linear-SEM assumption holds. The preceding two paragraphs verify the unit-diagonal and invertibility assumptions, and the stated properties of \(e\) verify independent errors, at most one Gaussian coordinate, and positive finite error variances. Therefore the definition of the square cyclic LiNG class gives
\[
 (P_X,W_G,e)\in\mathcal M_{3n}.
\]

Fix \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_G)\). By admissibility, every \(d_{\pi,i}=W_G[\pi(i),i]\) is nonzero, so the normalized-candidate definition makes \(T_\pi\) an invertible monomial matrix and
\[
 (T_\pi e)_i=d_{\pi,i}^{-1}e_{\pi(i)}.
\]
A permutation preserves mutual independence, and multiplication by a nonzero constant preserves centering, continuity, and non-Gaussianity. Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}((T_\pi e)_i)=d_{\pi,i}^{-2}>0
\]
is finite. Normalization gives
\[
 (W_\pi)_{ii}=d_{\pi,i}^{-1}W_G[\pi(i),i]=1,
\]
and \(W_\pi=T_\pi W_G\) is invertible. Finally,
\[
 W_\pi X=T_\pi W_GX=T_\pi e.
\]
Thus every admissible normalized assignment supplies a legal structural representation of the same observational vector \(X\), with all claimed error-law properties.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The counting reduction must live inside the causal model rather than only inside rational matrix algebra. \textit{Gap.} The matching representation permits row rescaling, but the reduction still must verify that its chosen error family is closed under every normalization in the complete orbit. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition licenses interpreting each counted assignment as a distribution-equivalent cyclic LiNG mechanism.

\begin{theorem}[thm:stable-orbit-realization]\label{thm:stable-orbit-realization}
For every simple graph \(G\) on \(n\geq1\) vertices, the rational construction with \(\varepsilon_n=[2^{40}(n+1)^4]^{-1}\), \(g=\varepsilon_n^2\), \(\delta=\varepsilon_n^3\), \(p=-25/16\), and \(r=(15/16)(1-\delta)\) satisfies \(\rho(B_G)<1\) and \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)=\{\pi_S:S\in\mathcal I(G)\}\). Every independent intended assignment obeys \(\rho(B_{\pi_S})\leq\max\{9/10,1-\delta/10\}\); every non-independent intended assignment has an eigenvalue of modulus at least \(1+g/20\); every other admissible assignment is unstable; and \(W_G\) has \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits per nonzero entry.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a simple graph \(G\) on \(n\geq1\) vertices. The resolved analytic-homotopy theorem applies to the graph gadget because all of its constants are exactly those in \(\mathrm{def:graph\mbox{-}gadget}\) and no additional model hypothesis is required.

First let \(S\in\mathcal I(G)\). The theorem places all noncritical eigenvalues of \(B_{\pi_S}\) strictly inside \(|z|<9/10\), and it places both critical clusters inside
\[
 |z|\leq1-\frac{\delta}{10}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \rho(B_{\pi_S})
 \leq\max\!\left\{\frac9{10},1-\frac{\delta}{10}\right\}<1.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus every independent intended assignment belongs to
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)\).

If \(S\notin\mathcal I(G)\), the same theorem supplies an eigenvalue satisfying
\[
 |z|\geq1+\frac g{20}>1,
\]
so \(\pi_S\notin\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)\). It also proves that every admissible assignment not of the form \(\pi_S\) is unstable: an assignment containing a root-only cycle has an eigenvalue of modulus greater than \(1/(2g)\), and, in the absence of such a cycle, an assignment containing a \(u_i\)--\(w_i\) swap has an eigenvalue with real part greater than \(5/4\). The exhaustive support classification in that theorem leaves no other admissible assignment. Hence
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)
 =\{\pi_S:S\in\mathcal I(G)\}.
\tag{31}
\]

The empty set is independent. Taking \(S=\varnothing\) in (30) gives
\[
 \rho(B_G)=\rho(B_{\pi_\varnothing})<1.
\tag{32}
\]
In particular, \(1\) is not an eigenvalue of \(B_G\), so
\[
 W_G=I_{3n}-B_G
\]
is invertible. The graph-gadget definition has zero diagonal in \(B_G\), since \(G\) is simple, and hence \(W_G\) has unit diagonal. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:gadget\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}validity}\) then confirms that the construction and all its normalized admissible assignments are legal same-law cyclic LiNG mechanisms for the stated non-Gaussian error law; this separates mechanism validity from the spectral classification in (31).

It remains to verify the encoding claim. The only nonintegral inputs are
\[
 \varepsilon_n=\frac1{2^{40}(n+1)^4},\qquad
 g=\frac1{2^{80}(n+1)^8},\qquad
 \delta=\frac1{2^{120}(n+1)^{12}},\qquad
 p=-\frac{25}{16},\qquad
 r=\frac{15}{16}(1-\delta).
\]
Their numerators and denominators have \(O(\log(n+1))\) binary digits. Every nonzero entry of \(B_G\) and \(W_G\) is one of these numbers, their negatives, or an integer obtained by adding the identity diagonal. Thus \(W_G\in\mathbb Q^{3n\times3n}\), every nonzero entry has \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits, and the full matrix is produced from the adjacency matrix in polynomial time. Equations (30)--(32) prove all asserted margins, reference stability, and the complete orbit equality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem turns a global spectral filter on a causal equivalence class into an exact coefficient-valid combinatorial family with explicit margins. \textit{Gap.} Existing stability screens do not realize arbitrary independent-set systems or control every unintended normalized row assignment. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the stability-conditioned orbit oracle boundary needed by matching-frequency calculations in cyclic-LiNG intervention design.

\begin{theorem}[thm:counting-separation]\label{thm:counting-separation}
On \(\mathcal R_q\), computing \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\) is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\), no FPRAS exists even on matrices \(W_G\) obtained from graphs of maximum degree six. In contrast, \(N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)=\operatorname{per}(\mathbf 1\{W\neq0\})\) has an FPRAS. Membership in \(\#\mathrm P\) is witnessed by a permutation and verified exactly by rational normalization followed by the Lyapunov certificate \(H-B_\pi^{\mathsf T}HB_\pi=I_q\) with \(H\succ0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first construct an exact polynomial-time verifier for the stability-filtered count. Let \(C\in\mathbb Q^{q\times q}\), and consider the rational linear system in the \(q^2\) entries of \(H\),
\[
 H-C^{\mathsf T}HC=I_q. \tag{1}
\]
We claim that
\[
 \rho(C)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{equation (1) has a real symmetric solution \(H\succ0\)}, \tag{2}
\]
and that the solution is unique when \(\rho(C)<1\).

Suppose first that \(\rho(C)<1\). Choose a real number \(a\) with \(\rho(C)<a<1\). Complex Jordan normal form and the binomial formula for powers of a Jordan block imply that there is a finite constant \(K_a\) such that
\[
 \lVert C^k\rVert_2\leq K_a a^k
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\): the factors \(\binom{k}{j}\), where \(0\leq j<q\), are absorbed by the strict gap between every eigenvalue modulus and \(a\). Consequently
\[
 H_C:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(C^k)^{\mathsf T}C^k \tag{3}
\]
converges absolutely in operator norm. Every summand is real symmetric positive semidefinite, and the zeroth summand is \(I_q\); hence \(H_C\succ0\). Absolute convergence permits an index shift, which gives
\[
 C^{\mathsf T}H_CC
 =\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(C^k)^{\mathsf T}C^k
 =H_C-I_q.
\]
Thus \(H_C\) solves (1). If \(K-C^{\mathsf T}KC=0\), iteration yields
\[
 K=(C^m)^{\mathsf T}KC^m,
 \qquad
 \lVert K\rVert_2
 \leq \lVert C^m\rVert_2^2\lVert K\rVert_2
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore \(K=0\), proving uniqueness.

Conversely, suppose that a real symmetric matrix \(H\succ0\) solves (1). If \(Cv=\lambda v\) for a nonzero complex vector \(v\), complexifying the real quadratic form in (1) gives
\[
 (1-|\lambda|^2)v^*Hv
 =v^*(H-C^{\mathsf T}HC)v
 =v^*v>0. \tag{4}
\]
Positive definiteness gives \(v^*Hv>0\), so division in (4) is valid and yields \(|\lambda|<1\). This holds for every eigenvalue of \(C\), and hence \(\rho(C)<1\). This proves (2). It also shows that if the coefficient operator of (1) is singular, then \(C\) cannot be stable, because stability would have implied uniqueness.

The test in (2) has polynomial bit complexity. After clearing denominators, (1) is an integer linear system of dimension \(d=q^2\); the bit length of every coefficient is polynomial in \(q\) and the encoding length of \(C\). If those integer coefficients have absolute value at most \(2^K\), every \(d\)-by-\(d\) determinant arising in fraction-free elimination has absolute value at most
\[
 d!\,2^{dK}
\]
by the Leibniz formula, and therefore has at most \(dK+\lceil\log_2(d!)\rceil+1\) bits. The same estimate applies to the relevant minors and Cramer numerators. Fraction-free elimination thus decides singularity and computes the unique rational solution, when it exists, using polynomially many operations on polynomial-bit integers. Symmetry is checked exactly. For a symmetric solution, Sylvester's criterion follows by successive completion of the square: writing
\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}h&b^{\mathsf T}\\ b&D\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad h>0,
\]
one has
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}s\\y\end{pmatrix}^{\!\mathsf T}
 H
 \begin{pmatrix}s\\y\end{pmatrix}
 =h\left(s+h^{-1}b^{\mathsf T}y\right)^2
  +y^{\mathsf T}\left(D-h^{-1}bb^{\mathsf T}\right)y.
\]
Induction shows that \(H\succ0\) exactly when all leading principal minors are positive. These signs are decidable by exact determinant computation under the same bit bound. Applying this procedure to \(I_q-W\) also decides the reference-stability condition in the definition of \(\mathcal R_q\) in polynomial time; rationality, unit diagonal, and invertibility are decidable directly by exact arithmetic.

We now prove membership in \(\#\mathrm P\). Extend the promised counting function to all binary strings by returning zero unless the input parses as a square rational matrix in \(\mathcal R_q\), a condition decidable by the preceding paragraph. On an input \(W\in\mathcal R_q\), a nondeterministic branch guesses a fixed-length canonical list encoding of a map \(\pi:[q]\to[q]\), checks that the list is a permutation, and checks
\[
 d_{\pi,i}=W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq q). \tag{5}
\]
Only after the nonzero checks in (5) does the verifier form
\[
 B_\pi
 =I_q-
 \operatorname{diag}\bigl(d_{\pi,1}^{-1},\ldots,d_{\pi,q}^{-1}\bigr)
 P_\pi W. \tag{6}
\]
All arithmetic in (6) is exact. Inverting a checked nonzero rational and multiplying input rationals increases bit length by at most a polynomial in \(q+L\), so the entries of \(B_\pi\) have polynomial bit length. The verifier accepts precisely when the Lyapunov test (1)--(4), with \(C=B_\pi\), returns a positive-definite solution. By (2), this is equivalent to \(\rho(B_\pi)<1\). The canonical encoding supplies exactly one branch for each permutation, so the number of accepting branches is exactly
\[
 |\{\pi\in\mathfrak S_q:d_{\pi,i}\neq0\text{ for every }i,\ 
 \rho(B_\pi)<1\}|
 =N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W).
\]
Thus stable-orbit counting on \(\mathcal R_q\) belongs to \(\#\mathrm P\).

For hardness, let \(G\) be a simple graph on \(n\geq1\) vertices and compute the frozen rational gadget \(W_G\). The stable-orbit realization theorem gives
\[
 \rho(B_G)<1,
 \qquad
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)=|\mathcal I(G)|, \tag{7}
\]
and gives \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits per nonzero entry. The graph-gadget definition has zero diagonal in \(B_G\), because each local block has zero diagonal and the simple-graph adjacency matrix has zero diagonal; hence \(W_G=I_{3n}-B_G\) has unit diagonal. If \(W_Gz=0\) for a nonzero complex vector \(z\), then \(B_Gz=z\), contradicting \(\rho(B_G)<1\). Therefore \(W_G\) is invertible. It follows from the definition of \(\mathcal R_{3n}\) that \(W_G\in\mathcal R_{3n}\), and its complete matrix encoding has length polynomial in \(n\). Since \(G\mapsto|\mathcal I(G)|\) is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions, (7) is a polynomial-time one-query count-preserving reduction. Together with membership, this proves \(\#\mathrm P\)-completeness on \(\mathcal R_q\).

Next suppose that an FPRAS for \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}\) existed on the gadget images \(W_G\) of graphs having maximum degree at most six. Given such a graph \(G\), construct \(W_G\), invoke the scheme with the requested relative tolerance and failure probability, and return its output. The positive exact equality in (7) preserves both the relative-error event and its probability. This would be an FPRAS for independent-set counting on graphs of maximum degree at most six, contrary to the cited bounded-degree inapproximability lemma unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\).

It remains to identify the unfiltered count. Define the zero--one support matrix
\[
 A(W)_{ri}:=\mathbf 1\{W_{ri}\neq0\}.
\]
The definition of the admissible orbit gives, for every \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_q\),
\[
 \pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \prod_{i=1}^q A(W)_{\pi(i),i}=1. \tag{8}
\]
Inversion is a bijection of \(\mathfrak S_q\). Setting \(\sigma=\pi^{-1}\) in the finite sum and using (8), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 &=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_q}
   \prod_{i=1}^q A(W)_{\pi(i),i}\\
 &=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_q}
   \prod_{r=1}^q A(W)_{r,\sigma(r)}
 =\operatorname{per}(A(W)).
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The matrix \(A(W)\) is nonnegative. The cited nonnegative-permanent lemma therefore supplies an FPRAS for (9). Equations (7) and (9) prove the asserted exact and approximate separation between the Schur-stable orbit count and the unfiltered normalized-assignment count.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem proves that the stability filter changes the computational character of the same normalized-assignment representation whose unfiltered size is a permanent; the bounded-degree phase diagram and the one-SCC corollary sharpen where and why that separation persists. \textit{Gap.} Independent-set hardness, Greenhill's and Sly's bounded-degree donors, Weitz's approximation theorem, and the permanent FPRAS do not themselves produce a legal cyclic-LiNG coefficient map, exclude unintended normalized assignments, preserve Schur margins, or establish hardness within one support SCC. \textit{Consumer.} The matching-frequency sampler and adaptive intervention rule of Sharifian, Salehkaleybar, and Kiyavash are unfiltered. If equilibrium stability is additionally imposed, they must condition on the stable orbit; neither the permanent FPRAS nor SCC decomposition removes the hard within-component filter.

\begin{theorem}[thm:scc-factorization]\label{thm:scc-factorization}
For every \(W\in\mathcal R_q\), admissibility and stability factor over the original support SCCs: \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\cong\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)\) and \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)=\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_C\). The bijection is restriction and concatenation in a fixed topological SCC order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(W\in\mathcal R_q\), and let \(D(B)\) be the directed support graph having an arrow \(j\to i\) exactly when \(B_{ij}\neq 0\). By \(B=I_q-W\) and assumption \(\mathrm{ass:unit\mbox{-}diagonal}\), for distinct \(i,r\in[q]\) one has
\[
 W_{ri}\neq 0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_{ri}=-W_{ri}\neq 0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\to r\text{ is an arrow of }D(B).
\]
Let \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\). Thus \(W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0\) for every \(i\). Write a nontrivial cycle of \(\pi\) as
\[
 i_0\mapsto i_1\mapsto\cdots\mapsto i_{s-1}\mapsto i_0.
\]
The preceding equivalence gives the directed cycle
\[
 i_0\to i_1\to\cdots\to i_{s-1}\to i_0
\]
in \(D(B)\). Hence all vertices of that permutation cycle lie in one strongly connected component. Trivial permutation cycles also lie in one component. It follows that
\[
 \pi(C)=C\qquad\text{for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B).
\]
Consequently restriction defines a map
\[
 R:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C),
 \qquad
 R(\pi)=(\pi|_C)_C.
\]
For each component \(C\), the local diagonal normalizer uses precisely the entries \(W_{\pi(i),i}\) with \(i\in C\); these are the same entries in \(W_C\). Thus every restriction is locally admissible. Conversely, given locally admissible permutations \((\sigma_C)_C\), their concatenation \(\sigma\), taken in a fixed topological order of the condensation graph, is a permutation of \([q]\) satisfying
\[
 W_{\sigma(i),i}=(W_C)_{\sigma_C(i),i}\neq0
 \qquad(i\in C).
\]
It is therefore globally admissible. Restriction and concatenation are inverse, so \(R\) is a bijection on admissible fibers.

It remains to identify the stability filter. Put the SCCs in a topological order. The matrices \(B\) and \(W=I_q-B\) are block triangular in this order. Since every admissible \(\pi\) preserves every component, both \(P_\pi\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(d_{\pi,1}^{-1},\ldots,d_{\pi,q}^{-1})\) are block diagonal. Hence
\[
 W_\pi=T_\pi W
\]
is block triangular, and its diagonal block indexed by \(C\) is
\[
 (W_\pi)[C,C]
 =T_{\pi|_C}W_C.
\]
Therefore \(B_\pi=I_q-W_\pi\) is block triangular with diagonal blocks
\[
 B^C_{\pi|_C}:=I_{|C|}-T_{\pi|_C}W_C.
\]
Expanding the determinant of a block-triangular matrix along its diagonal blocks gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 \det(zI_q-B_\pi)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
   \det\!\left(zI_{|C|}-B^C_{\pi|_C}\right).
\]
Thus the spectrum of \(B_\pi\), including algebraic multiplicities, is the multiset union of the spectra of its diagonal blocks, and consequently
\[
 \rho(B_\pi)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho\!\left(B^C_{\pi|_C}\right)<1
 \text{ for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B).
\]
By the definitions of the local normalized candidate and the stable orbit, the right-hand side says exactly that \(\pi|_C\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)\) for every \(C\). The admissible-fiber bijection therefore restricts to
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 \cong
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C).
\]
All sets are finite. Taking cardinalities and using \(N_C=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)\) yields
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_C,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies the exact modular unit of stable causal ambiguity rather than merely an invariant graph partition. \textit{Gap.} Sharifian et al. prove SCC invariance, and Lacerda et al. use block triangularity for disjoint cycles, but neither proves a Cartesian product of normalized stable assignment fibers. \textit{Consumer.} Small feedback modules can be counted and audited independently before their stable alternatives are combined for intervention planning.

\begin{proposition}[prop:local-enumerator]\label{prop:local-enumerator}
For every \(W\in\mathcal R_q\), the algorithm \(\mathsf{Enum}(W)\) emits every member of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\) exactly once. If \(c=|C|\) and \(e_C\) is the number of admissibility edges in block \(C\), its local screening time is \(\sum_C O((c^{1/2}e_C+cN_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C))\operatorname{poly}(c,L))\), after which Cartesian-product output has \(O(q)\) delay per stable assignment. The claim is output-sensitive in the enumerated local matching fibers and does not assert polynomial delay in \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\) alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(W\in\mathcal R_q\).  By \(\mathrm{def:rational\mbox{-}input\mbox{-}class}\), assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:unit\mbox{-}diagonal}\), \(\mathrm{ass:invertible\mbox{-}equations}\), \(\mathrm{ass:stable\mbox{-}reference}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:rational\mbox{-}input}\) hold.  In particular, \(B=I_q-W\) has rational entries.  Equality of a rational entry to zero is decidable by inspecting its reduced numerator, so the directed support graph of \(B\) is computed exactly in time polynomial in \(q\) and \(L\).  Graph searches compute reachability, and mutual reachability is an equivalence relation whose classes are precisely \(\operatorname{SCC}(B)\).  Hence the first step of \(\mathsf{Enum}(W)\), as specified by \(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}enumerator}\), is exact and has polynomial bit complexity.

Fix a topological order of the condensation graph.  For \(C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)\), write \(c=|C|\) and \(B_C=B[C,C]\).  In this order, \(B\), and therefore \(W=I_q-B\), is block triangular with diagonal blocks \(B_C\) and \(W_C=I_c-B_C\).  Consequently,
\[
 \det(tI_q-B)=\prod_{D\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \det(tI_{|D|}-B_D),
 \qquad
 \det(W)=\prod_{D\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\det(W_D).
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stable\mbox{-}reference}\) and the first identity give \(\rho(B_C)<1\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:invertible\mbox{-}equations}\) and the second identity give \(\det(W_C)\ne0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:rational\mbox{-}input}\) passes rationality and an encoding-length bound no larger than \(L\) to \(W_C\), while \(\mathrm{ass:unit\mbox{-}diagonal}\) gives \((W_C)_{ii}=1\).  Thus \(W_C\in\mathcal R_c\).  The same unit-diagonal condition also implies that the admissibility graph below has at least \(c\) edges.

Let \(H_C\) be the bipartite graph with a column copy and a row copy of \(C\), and put an edge between column \(i\) and row \(r\) exactly when \((W_C)_{ri}\ne0\).  Let \(e_C\) be its number of edges.  A perfect matching of \(H_C\) is a bijection \(i\mapsto\pi_C(i)\) such that
\[
 (W_C)_{\pi_C(i),i}\ne0\qquad(i\in C).
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:admissible\mbox{-}orbit}\), these bijections are exactly the elements of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\).

We next give the exact recursion used in \(\mathsf{Enum}(W)\).  Order the columns and the incident rows of every column.  At a prefix that injectively assigns the first \(k\) columns, inspect every available incident row in order and recurse precisely when the resulting residual bipartite graph has a perfect matching.  Residual feasibility is decided by augmenting paths.  Starting with any matching, an alternating-path search from the unmatched columns either reaches an unmatched row, in which case flipping the path increases the matching size by one, or reaches no unmatched row.  In the latter case let \(D\) be the reached columns and \(R\) the reached rows.  Every neighbor of \(D\) lies in \(R\); every row of \(R\) is matched, for otherwise an augmenting path would have been found; and following the matching edge from \(R\) injects \(R\) into \(D\) while omitting every initially unmatched column.  Therefore
\[
 |N(D)|=|R|<|D|.
\]
No matching can cover \(D\), since its distinct columns would need distinct representatives in \(N(D)\).  Conversely, if augmentation matches every residual column, the resulting edges are a perfect matching.  Hence the feasibility test is exact.  It uses at most \(c\) augmentations, each scanning at most \(e_C\) edges, and therefore costs \(O(ce_C)\) graph operations.

Every perfect matching determines one and only one root-to-leaf sequence of ordered prefix choices, and every reached leaf is a perfect matching because every prefix passed the exact residual-feasibility test.  Thus the recursion emits every element of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\) exactly once.  It is an alternating-cycle recursion in the sense used in \(\mathrm{def:local\mbox{-}enumerator}\): the symmetric difference of two perfect matchings has degree zero or two at each vertex, hence is a disjoint union of even cycles alternating between the two matchings, and ordered branching selects the first edge on which the corresponding extensions differ.

Put \(N_C^{\mathrm{adm}}=N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\).  Associate with every feasible prefix its lexicographically least perfect-matching extension.  For a fixed perfect matching and a fixed depth, at most one prefix has that matching as its extension, because a matching has exactly one prefix at that depth.  Since there are \(c+1\) depths, the number of feasible prefixes is at most
\[
 (c+1)N_C^{\mathrm{adm}}.
\]
Each feasible prefix examines at most \(c\) candidate children.  If the root is infeasible, only the root feasibility test is performed.  Since \(c\le e_C\le c^2\), construction of \(H_C\), the root test, all child tests, and the elementary rational zero tests together take
\[
 O\!\left(\bigl(c^{1/2}e_C+cN_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\bigr)
          \operatorname{poly}(c,L)\right)
\]
bit operations.  Here the displayed polynomial absorbs the polynomial factors in \(c\) and \(e_C\) from the augmenting-path tests; it is independent of the number of emitted matchings.

It remains to prove that the Schur screening is exact and polynomial per admissible matching.  Fix \(\pi_C\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:normalized\mbox{-}candidate}\), its assigned coefficient matrix is the rational matrix
\[
 A=B_{\pi_C}
 =I_c-
   \operatorname{diag}\!\left(
   (W_C)_{\pi_C(i),i}^{-1}:i\in C\right)P_{\pi_C}W_C.
\]
Every denominator displayed here is nonzero by admissibility, and the entries of \(A\) have binary length polynomial in \(c\) and \(L\).  Consider the \(c^2\)-variable rational linear system
\[
 P-A^{\mathsf T}PA=I_c. \tag{1}
\]
If \(\rho(A)<1\), complex Jordan form implies \(A^k\to0\) geometrically up to a polynomial factor in \(k\).  Hence the series
\[
 P_\star=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(A^k)^{\mathsf T}A^k
\]
converges entrywise.  Shifting its index gives
\[
 P_\star-A^{\mathsf T}P_\star A=I_c.
\]
For every nonzero \(x\in\mathbb R^c\),
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}P_\star x
 =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lVert A^kx\rVert_2^2
 \ge \lVert x\rVert_2^2>0,
\]
so \(P_\star\) is positive definite.  This solution is unique: if \(H=A^{\mathsf T}HA\), then iteration yields
\[
 H=(A^k)^{\mathsf T}HA^k\longrightarrow0,
\]
and hence \(H=0\).

Conversely, suppose a real symmetric positive-definite matrix \(P\) solves (1).  For any complex eigenpair \(Av=\lambda v\), with \(v\ne0\), sesquilinear extension of the real quadratic form gives
\[
 (1-|\lambda|^2)v^*Pv
 =v^*(P-A^{\mathsf T}PA)v
 =v^*v>0.
\]
Since \(v^*Pv>0\), it follows that \(|\lambda|<1\).  This holds for every eigenvalue, and therefore \(\rho(A)<1\).  We have proved the boundary-sensitive equivalence
\[
 \rho(A)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{(1) has a positive-definite solution}. \tag{2}
\]
In particular, (2) rejects every boundary case \(\rho(A)=1\).

Fraction-free rational elimination decides whether (1) has a unique solution and computes it when it does.  If its coefficient system is singular, the candidate may be rejected without losing a stable assignment, because stability implies uniqueness by the homogeneous argument above.  If the solution is unique, transposing (1) shows that \(P^{\mathsf T}\) is another solution, so uniqueness gives \(P=P^{\mathsf T}\).  Positive definiteness is then decided by exact rational Schur complements.  Indeed, for
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}a&b^{\mathsf T}\\ b&D\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{with }a>0,
\]
completion of the square gives
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}s\\y\end{pmatrix}^{\!\mathsf T}
 P
 \begin{pmatrix}s\\y\end{pmatrix}
 =a\left(s+a^{-1}b^{\mathsf T}y\right)^2
  +y^{\mathsf T}\left(D-a^{-1}bb^{\mathsf T}\right)y.
\]
Thus \(P\) is positive definite exactly when \(a>0\) and \(D-a^{-1}bb^{\mathsf T}\) is positive definite; if \(a\le0\), the first coordinate vector already rules out positive definiteness.  Induction therefore reduces the decision to exact signs of rational pivots.

For completeness, the bit complexity of these exact operations is polynomial.  After clearing row denominators, (1) is a linear system of dimension \(c^2\) whose integer coefficients have bit length polynomial in \(c\) and \(L\).  Every minor arising in fraction-free elimination is a sum of at most \((c^2)!\) products of at most \(c^2\) such integers.  Its bit length is bounded by the sum of \(c^2\) times the coefficient bit bound and \(\log((c^2)!)\), hence is polynomial in \(c\) and \(L\).  The same holds for every rational pivot and Schur complement.  Since the number of arithmetic operations is polynomial in \(c\), normalization, solution of (1), and all sign tests take \(\operatorname{poly}(c,L)\) bit operations per admissible matching.  Combining this with the exact-once matching recursion yields total local screening time
\[
 \sum_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 O\!\left(\bigl(c^{1/2}e_C+cN_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\bigr)
          \operatorname{poly}(c,L)\right).
\]

For every component, the preceding construction retains every member of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)\) exactly once by (2).  The already established \(\mathrm{thm:scc\mbox{-}factorization\mbox{-}real}\), and hence its specialization \(\mathrm{thm:scc\mbox{-}factorization}\) to \(\mathcal R_q\), states that restriction and concatenation in the fixed topological component order give a bijection
\[
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W).
\]
A lexicographic mixed-radix counter visits every tuple in this product exactly once.  Advancing it touches at most the number of components, and materializing the concatenated permutation writes exactly \(q\) entries, so the delay after local screening is \(O(q)\) per stable global assignment.  The screening bound depends on \(N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\), including admissible local matchings rejected by (2).  It therefore makes no polynomial-delay assertion in \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\) alone, exactly as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The bound makes the positive algorithmic content exact without hiding rejected local matchings behind a polynomial-delay claim. \textit{Gap.} Uno enumerates unfiltered perfect matchings, while prior local causal discovery does not integrate normalized Schur screening or SCC products. \textit{Consumer.} The procedure is an exact fallback for stable matching frequencies when the largest feedback SCC is small.

\begin{theorem}[thm:pointwise-count-inference]\label{thm:pointwise-count-inference}
For every fixed law in \(\mathcal M_q^{\mathrm{FOBI}}\), whose structural errors satisfy \(0<\operatorname{Var}(e_i)<\infty\) for every \(i\in[q]\), define \(\mathcal H_q\) to be the finite group of orthogonal maps on \(\mathbb R^{q^2}\) given by
\[
 Q_{J,\sigma}\operatorname{vec}(A)=\operatorname{vec}(J P_\sigma A),
 \qquad
 \sigma\in\mathfrak S_q,
 \quad
 J=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_q),
 \quad s_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
The plug-in covariance estimator is orbit-consistent in the sense that
\[
 \min_{Q\in\mathcal H_q}
 \|\widehat V_m-QV_{\mathrm{FOBI}}Q^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm F}
 \ \longrightarrow_{\Pr}\ 0.
\]
The matrix-valued symmetrized set satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \Pr\{N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\in C_m^N\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
and
\[
 d_{\mathrm H,F}(K_m,\mathcal O_U(W))=O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2})
\]
in the normalized-row Frobenius topology; in particular the underlying estimator contracts in the quotient metric \(d_{\mathrm{orb}}\). These assertions are pointwise in the fixed law. No uniform graph-support, shrinking-kurtosis-gap, zero-normalization-boundary, or unit-circle-boundary guarantee is made.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix one law in \(\mathcal M_q^{\mathrm{FOBI}}\). All constants below may depend on this law. With the corrected central-moment definition of \(\kappa\), put
\[
 \mu_e=\mathbb E[e],
 \qquad
 \sigma_i^2=\operatorname{Var}(e_i),
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_q),
 \qquad
 \xi=D^{-1}(e-\mu_e).
\]
The nonzero-variance assumption makes every division by \(\sigma_i\) valid. Independence of the coordinates of \(e\) gives independence of the coordinates of \(\xi\), and
\[
 \mathbb E[\xi]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\xi\xi^{\mathsf T}]=I_q,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\xi_i^4]-3=\kappa_i.
\]
Moreover \(e=WX\), so \(\|e\|_2\leq\|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|X\|_2\). The finite eighth-moment assumption therefore implies \(\mathbb E\|\xi\|_2^8<\infty\). Centering the structural equation yields
\[
 X-\mathbb E[X]=\Omega\xi,
 \qquad
 \Omega=W^{-1}D.
\]
Both \(W\) and \(D\) are nonsingular, and hence
\[
 \Sigma:=\operatorname{Var}(X)=\Omega\Omega^{\mathsf T}\succ0.
\]
This establishes the positivity needed for whitening. We will repeatedly use the following elementary iid averaging argument. If \(h(X)\) is integrable, put \(h_K=h\mathbf 1\{|h|\leq K\}\). Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  \left|m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m(h_K(X_j)-\mathbb E[h_K(X)])\right|>\varepsilon
 \right)
 \leq\frac{K^2}{m\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m|h(X_j)-h_K(X_j)|>\varepsilon
 \right)
 \leq\frac{\mathbb E|h(X)-h_K(X)|}{\varepsilon}.
\]
The expectation on the right tends to zero as \(K\to\infty\). Taking \(K\to\infty\) after \(m\to\infty\), and using \(|\mathbb E[h-h_K]|\leq\mathbb E|h-h_K|\), proves
\[
 m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m h(X_j)\longrightarrow_{\Pr}\mathbb E[h(X)].
\]
Apply this result to the coordinate functions \(X_a\) and \(X_aX_b\), which are integrable under the eighth-moment condition. The algebraic identity expressing sample covariance through empirical first and second raw moments then gives \(\widehat\Sigma_m\to_{\Pr}\Sigma\). Consequently \(\Pr(\widehat\Sigma_m\succ0)\to1\), so the fallback branch in the estimator definition has asymptotically vanishing probability.

The population whitening calculation also verifies the FOBI rank condition explicitly. Set
\[
 H=\Sigma^{-1/2}\Omega,
 \qquad
 Y=\Sigma^{-1/2}(X-\mathbb E[X])=H\xi.
\]
Since \(HH^{\mathsf T}=I_q\), the square matrix \(H\) is orthogonal. Independence and centering give, for \(i\neq j\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|\xi\|_2^2\xi_i\xi_j\right]=0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|\xi\|_2^2\xi_i^2\right]
 =\mathbb E[\xi_i^4]+
   \sum_{k\neq i}\mathbb E[\xi_i^2]\mathbb E[\xi_k^2]
 =\kappa_i+q+2.
\]
It follows that the population FOBI matrix is
\[
 F:=\mathbb E[\|Y\|_2^2YY^{\mathsf T}]
 =H\operatorname{diag}(\kappa_1+q+2,\ldots,\kappa_q+q+2)H^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The pairwise-distinct-kurtosis assumption makes all eigenvalues in this display simple. Thus all inverse eigengaps used below are nonzero. If \(O_0\) is the population eigenvector matrix in decreasing-eigenvalue order, then for some permutation matrix \(P\) and sign matrix \(J\),
\[
 O_0=HPJ,
 \qquad
 A_0:=O_0^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1/2}
 =JP^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}
 =JP^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}W.
\]
Hence \(A_0\in[W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\), and its row-normalization
\[
 U_0:=\ell(A_0)
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal O_U(W)\).

By the cited FOBI pointwise-limit lemma, the empirical eigenvectors can be aligned with this population chart by signed row permutations. Thus there are signed permutation matrices \(H_m\) and their induced orthogonal vectorization actions
\[
 Q_m\operatorname{vec}(A)=\operatorname{vec}(H_mA),
 \qquad Q_m\in\mathcal H_q,
\]
such that, with \(\overline A_m=H_m\widehat A_m^{\mathrm F}\),
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vec}(\overline A_m-A_0)
 =m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_A(X_j)+o_{\Pr}(1).
\]
For a matrix \(A\) with nonzero rows, the row-normalization map is differentiable, with rowwise differential
\[
 \bigl(D\ell_A[H]\bigr)_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}
 =\frac{H_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}}{\|A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}\|_2}
 -\frac{A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}H_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}^{\mathsf T}}
  {\|A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}\|_2^3}A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}.
\]
Every row of the nonsingular matrix \(A_0\) is nonzero, so Taylor expansion of this displayed formula gives
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vec}(H_m\widehat U_m-U_0)
 =m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_{\mathrm F}(X_j;P_X)+o_{\Pr}(1)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ Z,
 \qquad
 Z\sim N(0,V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}).
\]
This derives the normalized-row limit from the cited unmixing limit rather than assuming it. In particular,
\[
 \|H_m\widehat U_m-U_0\|_{\mathrm F}=O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2}).
\]

We next prove covariance consistency, including the plug-in step. In a neighborhood of the fixed law on which \(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)>0\) and all kurtosis gaps are bounded away from zero, the FOBI influence function is a polynomial of degree at most four in the centered observation whose coefficients are continuous functions of the mean, covariance, fourth moments, and simple eigensystem. Apply the iid averaging argument above to every monomial of degree at most four; its integrability follows from the eighth-moment condition. The empirical counterparts of all those coefficients therefore converge in probability by continuity of inversion, the positive-definite square root, and simple-eigenvalue projectors. Hence, on events whose probabilities tend to one, there is a random \(a_m\to_{\Pr}0\) such that
\[
 \|Q_m\widehat\psi_{m,j}-\psi_{\mathrm F}(X_j;P_X)\|_2
 \leq a_m(1+\|X_j\|_2^4)
 \qquad(1\leq j\leq m).
\]
The eighth-moment assumption and Markov's inequality imply
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m
 \|Q_m\widehat\psi_{m,j}-\psi_{\mathrm F}(X_j;P_X)\|_2^2
 \leq
 a_m^2\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m(1+\|X_j\|_2^4)^2
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
Indeed, the sample average on the right is \(O_{\Pr}(1)\), because its expectation is the fixed finite number \(\mathbb E[(1+\|X\|_2^4)^2]\). Since the influence function has mean zero and finite second moment, apply the proved iid averaging result to each integrable product of two influence coordinates, and use Cauchy--Schwarz for the plug-in cross terms. This gives
\[
 Q_m\widehat V_mQ_m^{\mathsf T}
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}
 \mathbb E[\psi_{\mathrm F}(X;P_X)\psi_{\mathrm F}(X;P_X)^{\mathsf T}]
 =V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}.
\]
Because \(Q_m\in\mathcal H_q\) and this finite group is closed under transposition, the last display is equivalent to
\[
 \min_{Q\in\mathcal H_q}
 \|\widehat V_m-QV_{\mathrm{FOBI}}Q^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm F}
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]

For the Gaussian-radius calibration, first suppose \(q\geq2\). The limiting covariance is not the zero matrix. Indeed, at standardized independent components, Theorem 8's off-diagonal influence coordinates are, for \(k\neq l\),
\[
 \phi_{kl}(\xi)
 =\frac{
 (\xi_k^3-\gamma_k)\xi_l+(\xi_l^3-\gamma_l)\xi_k
 +\sum_{s\neq k,l}\xi_s^2\xi_k\xi_l
 -(\kappa_k+q+4)\xi_k\xi_l
 }{\kappa_k-\kappa_l},
 \qquad
 \gamma_k=\mathbb E[\xi_k^3].
\]
Each coordinate has mean zero, and direct subtraction gives
\[
 \phi_{lk}(\xi)=-\phi_{kl}(\xi)-\xi_k\xi_l.
\]
If both variances were zero, both mean-zero variables would vanish almost surely, forcing \(\xi_k\xi_l=0\) almost surely. This contradicts independence and unit variances, because
\[
 \mathbb E[\xi_k^2\xi_l^2]
 =\mathbb E[\xi_k^2]\mathbb E[\xi_l^2]=1.
\]
The differential of row normalization removes only the radial diagonal coordinate at the identity chart and retains these off-diagonal coordinates. Hence \(V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}\neq0\).

Let \(c_{1-\alpha}\) be the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\|Z\|_2\) for \(Z\sim N(0,V_{\mathrm{FOBI}})\). A nonzero positive-semidefinite Gaussian covariance has at least one positive eigenvalue, so \(\|Z\|_2\) has a continuous distribution, with no atom at \(c_{1-\alpha}>0\). Conditional on the sample, multiplication of \(Z_m^*\) by \(Q_m\) preserves its Euclidean norm and changes its covariance to \(Q_m\widehat V_mQ_m^{\mathsf T}\). Continuity of the positive-semidefinite square root and the proved covariance convergence therefore give
\[
 c_{m,1-\alpha}\longrightarrow_{\Pr}c_{1-\alpha}.
\]
Combining this with the normalized-row limit and the absence of a boundary atom yields
\[
 \Pr\!\left{
  \sqrt m\,\|H_m\widehat U_m-U_0\|_{\mathrm F}
  \leq c_{m,1-\alpha}
 \right}
 \longrightarrow 1-\alpha.
\]

On the event in braces, set \(R_m=H_m^{\mathsf T}U_0\). Signed row permutations preserve \(\mathcal U_q\), so \(R_m\in\mathcal O_U(W)\), and orthogonality gives
\[
 \sqrt m\,\|R_m-\widehat U_m\|_{\mathrm F}
 =\sqrt m\,\|U_0-H_m\widehat U_m\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq c_{m,1-\alpha}.
\]
Thus \(R_m\in\mathcal E_m\). By symmetrization, \(K_m\) then contains the entire normalized monomial orbit of \(R_m\), which equals \(\mathcal O_U(W)\). In particular \(\ell(W)\in K_m\). The matrix \(\ell(W)\) is invertible because it is obtained from the invertible \(W\) by nonzero row scaling. The proved representative invariance gives
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(\ell(W))=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W).
\]
By the definition of the confidence image, the preceding inclusions imply
\[
 \left\{
  \sqrt m\,\|H_m\widehat U_m-U_0\|_{\mathrm F}
  \leq c_{m,1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 \subseteq
 \{N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\in C_m^N\}.
\]
Taking lower limits proves
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \Pr\{N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\in C_m^N\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
Notice that this is set inversion: it uses no continuity of the integer-valued count.

It remains to prove contraction of the whole random set. Write
\[
 r_m=\frac{c_{m,1-\alpha}}{\sqrt m},
 \qquad
 d_m=\|\widehat U_m-R_m\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
The covariance convergence makes \(c_{m,1-\alpha}=O_{\Pr}(1)\), while the normalized-row limit gives \(d_m=O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2})\). Each element of \(\mathcal E_m\) lies within \(r_m\) of \(\widehat U_m\). Signed row permutations act isometrically, so each element of \(K_m\) lies within \(r_m+d_m\) of the corresponding image of \(R_m\), which belongs to \(\mathcal O_U(W)\). Conversely, \(\widehat U_m\in\mathcal E_m\), and every point of \(\mathcal O_U(W)\) is an image of \(R_m\); the corresponding image of \(\widehat U_m\) belongs to \(K_m\) and is within \(d_m\). Therefore
\[
 d_{\mathrm H,F}(K_m,\mathcal O_U(W))
 \leq r_m+d_m
 =O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2}).
\]
The same alignment gives directly
\[
 d_{\mathrm{orb}}(\widehat A_m^{\mathrm F},W)
 \leq\|\widehat U_m-R_m\|_{\mathrm F}
 =O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2}).
\]

If \(q=1\), assumption \(W_{11}=1\) gives \(W=(1)\) and \(\mathcal O_U(W)=\{-1,1\}\). Row normalization in one dimension is locally constant, its differential and \(V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}\) are zero, and \(\widehat U_m\in\{-1,1\}\) on both branches of its definition. Hence \(\widehat V_m=0\), \(c_{m,1-\alpha}=0\), and symmetrization gives \(K_m=\{-1,1\}\) exactly. All three conclusions follow with probability one.

Finally, every use of a covariance eigenvalue, a kurtosis gap, and an eighth-moment envelope above concerns the one fixed law. The coverage event only inserts a true orbit representative into \(K_m\); it neither selects a support nor requires normalization denominators to stay uniformly away from zero, and it never invokes local constancy across the unit-circle stability boundary. This proves precisely the stated pointwise, orbit-honest scope.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:analytic-boundary-zero-count]\label{lem:analytic-boundary-zero-count}
Let \(D\subset\mathbb C\) be a bounded simply connected domain with piecewise \(C^1\) boundary, and let \(F:[0,1]\times\overline D\to\mathbb C\) be continuous, continuously differentiable in the homotopy parameter, and holomorphic in a neighborhood of \(\overline D\) in its second argument. If \(F(t,z)\neq0\) for every \((t,z)\in[0,1]\times\partial D\), then the number of zeros of \(F(t,\cdot)\) in \(D\), counted with analytic multiplicity, is independent of \(t\). The same conclusion applies to \(F(t,z)=\det M_t(z)\) for a square analytic matrix pencil that is nonsingular on \(\partial D\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a positively oriented parametrization of \(\partial D\). Since the boundary is compact and \(F\) is nonzero there, the logarithmic-derivative integral
\[
 N(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}\int_{\partial D}
       \frac{\partial_zF(t,z)}{F(t,z)}\,dz
\]
is well defined and continuous in \(t\). For a fixed \(t\), factor \(F(t,z)=(z-z_0)^mG(z)\) in a small disk about each zero \(z_0\), where \(G(z_0)\neq0\). Integration of the logarithmic derivative around the small disk gives \(m\): the term \(m/(z-z_0)\) integrates to \(2\pi\mathrm i m\), while the integral of \(G'/G\) is zero because a nonvanishing holomorphic function on a disk has a holomorphic logarithm, obtained by integrating \(G'/G\) from the center. Deforming \(\partial D\) to the disjoint union of these small circles therefore shows that \(N(t)\) is exactly the sum of the zero multiplicities in \(D\). In particular, \(N(t)\) is integer valued. A continuous integer-valued function on \([0,1]\) is constant, proving the first assertion. For an analytic matrix pencil, its determinant is holomorphic, is nonzero exactly when the pencil is nonsingular, and its analytic zero order is the algebraic multiplicity used in the claim; applying the scalar conclusion proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:monotone-two-sat-counting-complete]\label{lem:monotone-two-sat-counting-complete}
Valiant's counting problem \(\mathrm{MONOTONE\ 2\mbox{-}SAT}\), which asks for the number of satisfying assignments of a conjunction of positive two-variable clauses, is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time oracle reductions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:independent-set-counting-complete]\label{lem:independent-set-counting-complete}
The function \(G\mapsto|\mathcal I(G)|\), the number of independent sets of a finite simple graph, is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Membership in \(\#\mathrm P\) is immediate: a vertex subset is a polynomial-size witness, and every edge can be checked in polynomial time. For hardness, start with an instance of Valiant's \(\mathrm{MONOTONE\ 2\mbox{-}SAT}\) problem. A clause of the form \(x_i\vee x_i\) forces \(x_i=1\); set every such variable to one and delete all clauses thereby satisfied. This preserves the number of satisfying assignments because each forced variable has exactly one possible value. Delete duplicate remaining clauses, and form the simple graph with one vertex per remaining variable and edge \(ij\) for the clause \(x_i\vee x_j\). A truth assignment satisfies all clauses exactly when its set of true variables is a vertex cover. Complementation is a bijection from vertex covers to independent sets, including arbitrary choices of isolated vertices. If preprocessing removes every variable, the answer is the directly computable value one; otherwise the graph is nonempty. Thus this is a polynomial-time count-preserving Turing reduction. Composing it with the cited \(\#\mathrm P\)-completeness of \(\mathrm{MONOTONE\ 2\mbox{-}SAT}\) proves \(\#\mathrm P\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:analytic-homotopy-certificate]\label{thm:analytic-homotopy-certificate}
For every simple graph \(G\) on \(n\geq1\) vertices and every \(S\subseteq V\), the exact-pencil analytic homotopy handle \(H_{\mathrm{hom}}\) is valid uniformly at the frozen values of \(g,\delta,p,r\). In particular, with \(T=V\setminus S\), \(a=16/25\), \(h_0(z)=z^2-p-r\), \(h_1(z)=z^3+az-ar\), and \(c_i=a\) on \(S\) and \(c_i=1\) on \(T\), one has the degree-\(3n\) polynomial identity
\[
 \det(zI_{3n}-B_{\pi_S})
 =z^{\,n-|S|}
   \det\!\left(
     \operatorname{diag}\bigl(h_1(z)I_S,h_0(z)I_T\bigr)
     -zg\operatorname{diag}(c_i)A_G
   \right).
\]
On both critical-root disks the \(T\)-block is uniformly invertible, the critical scalar coordinate has a one-to-one analytic inverse with nonzero derivative, and analytic zero multiplicities pass unchanged through the Schur complement and this inverse. The resulting negative-mode rectangle contains a zero, counted with multiplicity, if and only if \(S\notin\mathcal I(G)\). If \(S\in\mathcal I(G)\), every critical root has modulus at most \(1-\delta/10\); if \(S\notin\mathcal I(G)\), some critical root has modulus at least \(1+g/20\). Moreover, every admissible assignment not of the form \(\pi_S\) is unstable: a root-only permutation cycle produces an eigenvalue of modulus greater than \(1/(2g)\), while a root--third-coordinate swap produces an eigenvalue with real part greater than \(5/4\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 a=\frac{16}{25},\qquad r_0=\frac{15}{16},\qquad
 \zeta=\frac{-3+\mathrm i\sqrt{91}}{10}.
\]
The frozen choice of \(\varepsilon_n\) gives, for every \(n\geq1\),
\[
 0<\varepsilon_n\leq2^{-40},\qquad
 g=\varepsilon_n^2,\qquad
 \delta=\varepsilon_n^3,\qquad
 gn<2^{-80},\qquad
 \delta\leq2^{-120}.
\tag{1}
\]
All inversions and contour estimates below will be justified from these inequalities.

\emph{Classification of admissible permutations.}
By the graph-gadget definition, a leaf coordinate \(v_i\) or \(w_i\) has nonzero entries only in its own equation row and the row indexed by \(u_i\). Every cycle of an admissible permutation follows nonzero entries of \(W_G\). Hence a nontrivial permutation cycle containing \(v_i\) or \(w_i\) is respectively the two-cycle \((u_i\,v_i)\) or \((u_i\,w_i)\). Every other nontrivial cycle uses root coordinates only and follows edges of \(G\). Thus an admissible assignment is a disjoint union of local \(u_i\)--\(v_i\) swaps, local \(u_i\)--\(w_i\) swaps, root-only cycles, and fixed points. This exhausts the admissible orbit.

\emph{Root-only cycles.}
Suppose that \(C\neq\varnothing\) is the set of roots in root-only permutation cycles. After putting the coordinates indexed by \(C\) first, direct use of the normalization denominator \(-g\) gives
\[
 B_\pi=g^{-1}H+E,\qquad
 H=\begin{pmatrix}P&R\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\tag{2}
\]
where \(P\) is the permutation matrix of the root cycles. Each selected original root row contributes \(-1\) at each of its two distinct leaves to \(R\), so
\[
 \|P^{-1}R\|_2=\sqrt2.
\]
The remaining graph entries have magnitude at most one, all rows normalized by one of \(1,-1,-p,-r\) have entries of magnitude at most two, and the local rows are sparse. Consequently
\[
 \|E\|_2\leq\|E\|_{\mathrm F}\leq6n.
\tag{3}
\]
With
\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}I&-P^{-1}R\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
\]
one has
\[
 Q^{-1}HQ=\operatorname{diag}(P,0),
 \qquad
 \|Q\|_2\|Q^{-1}\|_2
 \leq(1+\sqrt2)^2<6.
\]
It follows from (2)--(3) that
\[
 Q^{-1}(gB_\pi)Q=\operatorname{diag}(P,0)+F,
 \qquad \|F\|_2<36gn.
\]
On \(|z|=1/2\), the normal matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(P,0)\) has resolvent norm at most \(2\). By (1), \(2\|F\|_2<72gn<1\). The Neumann-series criterion therefore makes
\[
 zI-\operatorname{diag}(P,0)-tF
\]
invertible on that circle for every \(t\in[0,1]\). The analytic boundary-zero-count lemma preserves the number of eigenvalues inside the circle, including algebraic multiplicity. Since \(\operatorname{diag}(P,0)\) has exactly \(|C|\) eigenvalues outside the circle, so does \(gB_\pi\). Therefore
\[
 \rho(B_\pi)>\frac1{2g}>1.
\tag{4}
\]
This conclusion permits arbitrary simultaneous local swaps outside \(C\).

\emph{Root--third-coordinate swaps.}
It remains to treat assignments without root-only cycles. At zero graph coupling their local blocks are
\[
 M_0(\delta)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\p&0&0\\r&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 M_1(\delta)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1/p&0\\1&0&-1\\r&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 M_2(\delta)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0&1/r\\p&0&0\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
They correspond respectively to a fixed root, a \(u_i\)--\(v_i\) swap, and a \(u_i\)--\(w_i\) swap. The change from \(\delta=0\) in the block diagonal matrix has norm at most \(2\delta\), while graph coupling has norm at most \(g\|A_G\|_2\leq gn\). Thus the full perturbation has norm at most \(2\delta+gn\).

At \(\delta=0\), the three characteristic polynomials are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \det(zI-M_0(0))&=z(z^2+5/8),\\
 \det(zI-M_1(0))&=(z-3/5)(z^2+(3/5)z+1),\\
 \det(zI-M_2(0))&=z^3-(16/15)z-5/3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The last polynomial has one positive root \(\lambda\in(7/5,3/2)\); its other roots form a conjugate pair with real part \(-\lambda/2\). On \(|z-3/2|=1/4\), the root-distance bounds from (6) give
\[
 |\det(zI-M_2(0))|
 >\frac3{20}\left(\frac{39}{20}\right)^2>\frac12.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(M_0(0)\), all three root distances on this circle are at least \(5/4\). For \(M_1(0)\), the distance to \(3/5\) is at least \(13/20\), and those to the two remaining roots are at least \(31/20\). Every \(2\)-by-\(2\) cofactor of the matrices in (5) on this circle has modulus at most \(8\), so every local adjugate has operator norm at most \(24\). Equations (6)--(7) therefore bound all three local resolvents by \(64\). Because
\[
 64(2\delta+gn)<1
\]
by (1), the block-diagonal-to-coupled homotopy is invertible on the circle. The analytic boundary-zero-count lemma shows that each \(M_2\) block contributes one eigenvalue to its interior, whereas \(M_0\) and \(M_1\) contribute none. Every point in that disk has real part greater than \(5/4\). Hence every assignment containing a root--third-coordinate swap is unstable.

\emph{Exact elimination for intended switches.}
Fix \(S\subseteq V\) and write \(T=V\setminus S\). For an unswitched vertex, the eigenvalue equations imply
\[
 zu_i=v_i+w_i+g\sum_j(A_G)_{ij}u_j,\qquad
 zv_i=pu_i,\qquad zw_i=ru_i,
\]
and hence
\[
 h_0(z)u_i=zg\sum_j(A_G)_{ij}u_j,
 \qquad h_0(z)=z^2-p-r.
\tag{8}
\]
For a switched vertex they are
\[
 zu_i=p^{-1}v_i,\qquad
 zv_i=u_i-w_i-g\sum_j(A_G)_{ij}u_j,\qquad
 zw_i=ru_i,
\]
so multiplication and the identity \(p=-1/a\) give
\[
 h_1(z)u_i=azg\sum_j(A_G)_{ij}u_j,
 \qquad h_1(z)=z^3+az-ar.
\tag{9}
\]
Eliminating the leaf coordinates in the determinant gives, initially for \(z\neq0\),
\[
 \det(zI_{3n}-B_{\pi_S})
 =z^{\,n-|S|}
   \det\!\left(
      \operatorname{diag}\bigl(h_1(z)I_S,h_0(z)I_T\bigr)
      -zg\operatorname{diag}(aI_S,I_T)A_G
   \right).
\tag{10}
\]
Both sides of (10) are monic polynomials of degree \(3n\), and they agree for every nonzero \(z\); hence they agree identically. In particular, the factor in (10) is the degree-correct factor \(z^{n-|S|}\).

Define
\[
 b=-p-r=\frac58+\frac{15}{16}\delta,\qquad
 d_0(z)=\frac{h_0(z)}z=z+\frac bz,\qquad
 f_\delta(z)=\frac{h_1(z)}{az}=\frac{z^2}{a}+1-\frac rz.
\tag{11}
\]
Whenever
\[
 R_T(z):=d_0(z)I_T-gA_G[T,T]
\]
is invertible, block elimination in (10) shows that every critical root is a zero of
\[
 \det\!\left[
 f_\delta(z)I_S-gA_G[S,S]
 -g^2A_G[S,T]R_T(z)^{-1}A_G[T,S]
 \right].
\tag{12}
\]
For \(T=\varnothing\), the last term is defined to be zero.

\emph{Uniform localization and absence of poles.}
At \(g=\delta=0\), the roots belonging to \(M_1(0)\) outside \(|z|<9/10\) are
\[
 \zeta=\frac{-3+\mathrm i\sqrt{91}}{10}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad \overline\zeta,
 \qquad |\zeta|=1,
\]
one pair for each member of \(S\). The other roots are \(0\), \(\pm\mathrm i\sqrt{5/8}\), and \(3/5\). Use the three disjoint contours
\[
 |z|=\frac9{10},\qquad |z-\zeta|=\frac1{16},\qquad
 |z-\overline\zeta|=\frac1{16}.
\tag{13}
\]
On the first contour the root distances in (6) yield
\[
 |\det(zI-M_1(0))|\geq\frac3{10}\left(\frac1{10}\right)^2
 =\frac3{1000},
\]
and
\[
 |\det(zI-M_0(0))|
 \geq\frac9{10}\left(\frac9{10}-\sqrt{\frac58}\right)^2
 >\frac3{1000}.
\]
On either critical circle, the explicit distances to the roots in (6) give the same lower bound \(3/1000\). The cofactor estimate used above then gives the deliberately loose common resolvent bound \(8192\) on all contours in (13). Since
\[
 8192(2\delta+gn)<1,
\]
the matrix homotopy from the zero-coupling blocks to \(B_{\pi_S}\) is boundary-invertible on all three contours. The analytic boundary-zero-count lemma therefore gives, with multiplicity,
\[
 3n-2|S|\ \hbox{roots in }|z|<9/10,
\quad |S|\ \hbox{roots in each critical disk}.
\tag{14}
\]
These counts sum to \(3n\), so (14) exhausts every root.

On \(|z-\zeta|\leq1/16\), one has \(15/16\leq|z|\leq17/16\). Hence (11) gives
\[
 |d_0(z)|
 \geq \frac{15}{16}
  -\frac{5/8+(15/16)\delta}{15/16}
 =\frac{13}{48}-\delta.
\]
Since \(\|gA_G[T,T]\|_2\leq gn\), (1) and the Neumann criterion imply
\[
 \|R_T(z)^{-1}\|_2\leq4.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(R_T\) is analytic and pole-free throughout the disk. Set
\[
 E(z)=gA_G[S,T]R_T(z)^{-1}A_G[T,S].
\]
Using \(\|A_G[S,T]\|_2,\|A_G[T,S]\|_2\leq n\), (15) gives the uniform analytic bound
\[
 \|E(z)\|_2\leq4gn^2=:\eta.
\tag{16}
\]
The conjugate disk has the conjugate bounds. This proves the required uniform \(T\)-block invertibility and excludes hidden Schur-complement poles.

\emph{Analytic inverse coordinate and multiplicity transfer.}
Let \(F=f_0'(\zeta)\). Direct algebra from \(\zeta^2+(3/5)\zeta+1=0\) gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 |F|^2=\frac{3913}{256}>\left(\frac{39}{10}\right)^2,\qquad
 \beta=\frac1F=\frac{a\zeta}{3\zeta^2+a},\qquad
 \gamma=-\frac{3/5}{3\zeta^2+a},\\
 \overline\zeta\,\beta
 =-\frac8{43}+\mathrm i\frac{72\sqrt{91}}{3913},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Re}(\overline\zeta\,\gamma)=-\frac9{43}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{17}
\]
On the critical disk, \(|f_0''(z)|<6\). Writing \(v=z-\zeta\), Taylor's formula bounds the quadratic remainder by \(3|v|^2\), and
\[
 \left|\frac{r_0\delta}{z}-\frac{r_0\delta}{\zeta}\right|
 \leq\delta|v|.
\]
For every \(|y|\leq1/8\), on \(|v|=1/16\) the linear term satisfies
\[
 |Fv|>\frac{39}{160},
\]
whereas the sum of \(3|v|^2\), the \(\delta\)-term, and \(|y|\) is strictly smaller. Apply the analytic boundary-zero-count lemma to the homotopy from \(Fv\) to \(f_\delta(\zeta+v)-y\). Its boundary is nonzero by the preceding strict inequality, so the final function, like \(Fv\), has exactly one zero counted with multiplicity. Thus there is exactly one solution \(z=z_\delta(y)\) in the critical disk to
\[
 f_\delta(z)=y.
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover \(f_\delta'\) cannot vanish there, because its deviation from \(F\) is less than \(6/16+2\delta<1/2\), while \(|F|>39/10\). To verify analytic dependence without an additional premise, fix \(y_0\) and its root \(z_0\). Factoring the convergent local power series gives
\[
 f_\delta(z_0+h)-f_\delta(z_0)
 =h\bigl(f_\delta'(z_0)+h\,k(h)\bigr)
\]
for an analytic \(k\). On a sufficiently small disk the equation for \(h\), with \(y-y_0\) on the right, is a contraction; its iterates are analytic in \(y\) and converge uniformly there. Their limit is therefore analytic and, by global uniqueness, equals \(z_\delta(y)-z_0\). Differentiating (18) then gives
\[
 z_\delta'(y)=\frac1{f_\delta'(z_\delta(y))}\neq0.
\]
Thus \(z_\delta(y)\) is analytic on \(|y|<1/8\), with nonzero derivative. Rearranging Taylor's formula yields the disk-uniform estimates
\[
 |v|\leq\frac{|y|+\delta}{3},
 \qquad
 |z_\delta(y)-\zeta-\beta y-\gamma\delta|
 \leq(|y|+\delta)^2.
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(|\zeta|=1\) and \(|v|\leq1/16\),
\[
 \bigl||\zeta+v|-1-\operatorname{Re}(\overline\zeta v)\bigr|
 \leq |v|^2.
\]
Combining this with (17)--(19) gives
\[
 |z_\delta(y)|
 =1-\frac9{43}\delta
  +\operatorname{Re}\!\left[
    \left(-\frac8{43}
      +\mathrm i\frac{72\sqrt{91}}{3913}\right)y
   \right]+R(y),
\qquad
 |R(y)|\leq4(|y|+\delta)^2.
\tag{20}
\]

At a zero of (12), a unit nullvector and (16) imply
\[
 |f_\delta(z)|\leq gn+4g^2n^2<\frac18.
\]
Set \(f_\delta(z)=gw\) and apply (18). Equation (12) is then equivalent to
\[
 \det\!\left[
 wI_S-A_G[S,S]-E(z_\delta(gw))
 \right]=0.
\tag{21}
\]
Throughout \(|gw|<1/8\), the matrix correction in (21) is analytic and has norm at most \(\eta\). In either critical disk, the factors removed from (10) and (12) are nonzero by \(z\neq0\) and (15). The map \(w\mapsto z_\delta(gw)\) has nonzero derivative. Therefore the order of each determinant zero, and hence its analytic multiplicity, is unchanged by both the Schur complement and the inverse-coordinate substitution. This establishes multiplicity transfer rather than merely setwise root correspondence.

\emph{Independent switch sets.}
If \(S\in\mathcal I(G)\), then \(A_G[S,S]=0\). At a zero of (21), a unit nullvector gives
\[
 |w|\leq\eta,\qquad |y|=g|w|\leq4g^2n^2.
\]
Using \(|\overline\zeta\beta|<1\) in (20),
\[
 |z|
 \leq1-\frac9{43}\delta+4g^2n^2
       +4(4g^2n^2+\delta)^2
 \leq1-\frac{\delta}{10}.
\tag{22}
\]
For the last inequality, divide the positive error terms by \(\delta\) and use
\[
 \frac{g^2n^2}{\delta}=\varepsilon_n n^2\leq2^{-40},
 \qquad \delta\leq2^{-120}.
\]
The conjugate critical disk obeys the same estimate. All other roots have modulus less than \(9/10\) by (14).

\emph{Non-independent switch sets and the negative-mode homotopy.}
If \(S\notin\mathcal I(G)\), choose an edge \(\{i,j\}\) of \(G[S]\). The unit vector supported on \(i,j\) with opposite coordinates has Rayleigh quotient \(-1\); hence, by the variational characterization for the real symmetric matrix \(A_G[S,S]\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(A_G[S,S])\leq-1.
\tag{23}
\]
The interval \([-3/4,-1/2]\) has length \(1/4\). The union of the at most \(n\) intervals of radius \(1/[8(n+1)]\) about the eigenvalues has total length less than \(1/4\). Hence there exists
\[
 t\in[-3/4,-1/2],\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}\bigl(t,\operatorname{spec}(A_G[S,S])\bigr)
 \geq\frac1{8(n+1)}.
\tag{24}
\]
Consider the rectangle
\[
 \mathcal D=\{w\in\mathbb C:-n-1<\operatorname{Re}w<t,\ 
                         |\operatorname{Im}w|<1\}.
\tag{25}
\]
All eigenvalues of an adjacency matrix on at most \(n\) vertices lie in \([-n,n]\). Because the matrix is real symmetric, its resolvent on \(\partial\mathcal D\) is bounded by the reciprocal distance to its real spectrum. Equations (24)--(25) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{w\in\partial\mathcal D}
 \|(wI_S-A_G[S,S])^{-1}\|_2\leq8(n+1).
\tag{26}
\]
By (1) and (16),
\[
 8(n+1)\eta\leq32gn^2(n+1)<1.
\tag{27}
\]
Also \(|w|\leq2(n+1)\) on \(\overline{\mathcal D}\), so \(|gw|<1/8\). Thus the inverse coordinate, \(R_T^{-1}\), and \(E(z_\delta(gw))\) are analytic throughout the closed rectangle. From (26)--(27), the matrix pencil
\[
 wI_S-A_G[S,S]-sE(z_\delta(gw)),\qquad 0\leq s\leq1,
\tag{28}
\]
is nonsingular on \(\partial\mathcal D\). By the analytic boundary-zero-count lemma, its determinant has the same number of zeros in \(\mathcal D\), counted with multiplicity, for every \(s\). At \(s=0\), this number is positive by (23), because an eigenvalue at most \(-1\) lies strictly between the vertical sides of (25). Conversely, if \(S\in\mathcal I(G)\), then \(A_G[S,S]=0\), and its only eigenvalue lies to the right of \(t\); the same homotopy has zero zeros in \(\mathcal D\). This proves the asserted \emph{if and only if} negative-mode persistence.

At a retained zero of (21), normal-resolvent exclusion gives
\[
 \operatorname{dist}\bigl(w,\operatorname{spec}(A_G[S,S])\bigr)\leq\eta.
\]
In particular,
\[
 |\operatorname{Im}w|\leq\eta,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}w<t\leq-\frac12,\qquad
 |w|\leq2(n+1).
\]
Substitution of \(y=gw\) in (20), together with
\(\bigl|72\sqrt{91}/3913\bigr|<1\), gives
\[
 |z|
 \geq1+\frac4{43}g-\frac9{43}\delta
       -4g^2n^2-4(2g(n+1)+\delta)^2
 \geq1+\frac g{20}.
\tag{29}
\]
For the final inequality divide by \(g\), use
\(\delta/g=\varepsilon_n\leq2^{-40}\) and
\(g(n+1)^2\leq2^{-80}\), and note that the available coefficient
\(4/43-1/20>0\) exceeds the sum of the resulting error bounds.

Equations (4), (7), (10), (15), (18), (21), (28), and (29) respectively establish exclusion of root cycles, exclusion of root--third-coordinate swaps, the degree-correct exact pencil, uniform pole-freeness, analytic invertibility, multiplicity-preserving reduction, negative-mode persistence, and the claimed radial margins. The classification at the start of the proof covers every admissible permutation, completing the certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem certifies the graph-uniform no-pole and multiplicity-preserving step that turns the exact pencil into a valid spectral classification. \textit{Gap.} The prior cyclic-LiNG literature does not supply the inverse-coordinate rectangle homotopy or the all-permutation exclusions required by this gadget. \textit{Consumer.} It is the load-bearing certificate for the complete stable-orbit realization and therefore for the counting reduction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:bounded-degree-phase-diagram]\label{thm:bounded-degree-phase-diagram}
For every integer \(\Delta\geq0\), let
\[
 \mathcal W_\Delta
 :=\{W_G:G\text{ is a finite simple graph and }\max_{v\in V}\deg_G(v)\leq\Delta\}.
\]
On the promised rational encodings in this fixed-labelled gadget image, the following sharp bounded-degree phase diagram holds.  Exact computation of \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}\) is in \(\mathrm{FP}\) on \(\mathcal W_\Delta\) for \(\Delta\leq2\), and is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions already on \(\mathcal W_3\).  There is a deterministic \(\mathrm{FPTAS}\) for \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}\) on \(\mathcal W_\Delta\) for \(\Delta\leq5\), whereas no \(\mathrm{FPRAS}\) exists on \(\mathcal W_6\) unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\).  The labelled matrix \(W_G\) determines \(G\) in polynomial time, and every transfer is count preserving through \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)=|\mathcal I(G)|\).  Define the rationally encoded square cyclic-LiNG input class
\[
 \mathcal C_{\mathrm{rat}}
 :=\bigcup_{q\geq1}
 \left\{W\in\mathbb Q^{q\times q}:
 \exists P_X,e\ \text{such that }(P_X,W,e)\in\mathcal M_q\right\}.
\]
Its admissible normalized row assignments are represented by perfect matchings in the support matrix.  The lower-bound gadget images form a strict subclass of \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rat}}\): they have a stable reference and admit independent centered variance-one continuous non-Gaussian errors.  Thus the lower bounds transfer by subclass inclusion to this rationally encoded published cyclic-LiNG/perfect-matching class; stability is an additional restriction imposed on the counted subfamily.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\Delta\geq0\).  We first verify that the graph can be recovered from the promised matrix input.  If \(W=W_G\in\mathcal W_\Delta\), its dimension is \(q=3n\), and the fixed coordinate labels identify \(u_i,v_i,w_i\) for \(i\in[n]\).  The frozen construction has, for \(i\neq j\),
\[
 (W_G)_{u_i u_j}=-(B_G)_{u_i u_j}=-g(A_G)_{ij},
 \qquad
 g=\bigl[2^{40}(n+1)^4\bigr]^{-2}>0. \tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
 (A_G)_{ij}=-\frac{(W_G)_{u_i u_j}}{g}\quad(i\neq j),
 \qquad (A_G)_{ii}=0. \tag{7}
\]
The division in (7) is licensed by \(g>0\).  Computing \(n=q/3\), \(g\), and all entries in (7) uses polynomially many exact rational operations on polynomial-bit numbers.  Hence the fixed-labelled encoding permits polynomial-time recovery of \(G\).  Conversely, the graph-gadget definition and its bit bound compute \(W_G\) from \(G\) in polynomial time.  The stable-orbit realization theorem supplies the exact equality
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)=|\mathcal I(G)|. \tag{8}
\]

Suppose first that \(\Delta\leq2\).  Every connected component of \(G\) is a path or a cycle: if a component is acyclic and has maximum degree at most two, following its unique possible continuation from an endpoint lists a path; if it contains a cycle, every vertex on that cycle already has degree two, so connectedness leaves no vertex outside the cycle.  Let \(P_k\) be the path on \(k\) vertices, including \(P_0\), and define Fibonacci numbers by \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), and \(F_{k+1}=F_k+F_{k-1}\) for \(k\geq1\).  Splitting independent sets of \(P_k\) according as the first vertex is excluded or included gives
\[
 |\mathcal I(P_0)|=1,\qquad |\mathcal I(P_1)|=2,\qquad
 |\mathcal I(P_k)|=|\mathcal I(P_{k-1})|+|\mathcal I(P_{k-2})|=F_{k+2},
 \qquad k\geq2. \tag{9}
\]
For a cycle \(C_k\), \(k\geq3\), split on a fixed vertex.  Excluding it leaves \(P_{k-1}\); including it forces its two neighbors out and leaves \(P_{k-3}\).  Hence
\[
 |\mathcal I(C_k)|=F_{k+1}+F_{k-1}. \tag{10}
\]
Independent choices on distinct connected components are in Cartesian product, so \(|\mathcal I(G)|\) is the product of the quantities in (9)--(10).  Component discovery and all recurrences use \(O(n)\) graph operations and \(O(n)\) integer additions or multiplications.  Every intermediate count is at most \(2^n\), hence has at most \(n+1\) bits.  This is a deterministic polynomial-time exact algorithm.  Graph recovery and (8) therefore put exact \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}\) computation in \(\mathrm{FP}\) on \(\mathcal W_\Delta\) for \(\Delta\leq2\).

For the next regime, the cited Greenhill theorem states that exact independent-set counting is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete even on \(3\)-regular graphs.  Such graphs have maximum degree three.  The forward map \(G\mapsto W_G\), followed by the one equality (8), is therefore a polynomial-time, count-preserving, one-query reduction to exact stable-orbit counting on \(\mathcal W_3\).  The counting-separation theorem already supplies \(\#\mathrm P\) membership for these rational stable-reference inputs.  Thus the restriction to \(\mathcal W_3\) is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

For approximation, take any graph of maximum degree at most five.  In the cited Weitz theorem set \(b=4\) and \(\lambda=1\).  Its strict threshold premise holds because
\[
 1<\frac{4^4}{3^5}=\frac{256}{243}. \tag{11}
\]
At activity one the hard-core partition function is exactly
\[
 Z_G(1)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal I(G)}1^{|I|}=|\mathcal I(G)|. \tag{12}
\]
The deterministic scheme from that theorem, applied after the recovery (7), is therefore a deterministic \(\mathrm{FPTAS}\) for \(|\mathcal I(G)|\), and (8) transfers the same relative bounds without any postprocessing loss to \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_G)\).  This proves the claim for every \(\Delta\leq5\).

Conversely, suppose an \(\mathrm{FPRAS}\) existed on \(\mathcal W_6\).  Given a maximum-degree-six graph \(G\), compute \(W_G\), invoke that scheme once with the desired accuracy and failure probability, and return the result.  By (8), the returned relative-error event and success probability are exactly those for \(|\mathcal I(G)|\).  The cited Sly theorem rules out such an \(\mathrm{FPRAS}\) unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\).  This proves the approximation elbow between degrees five and six.

It remains to record the class transfer.  The stable-orbit realization proof shows that each \(W_G\) is rational, unit diagonal, invertible, and has stable reference \(B_G\); the gadget-law proposition supplies independent centered variance-one continuous non-Gaussian errors and legal same-law normalized assignments.  Hence every hard image belongs to the explicitly defined class \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rat}}\).  For any \(W\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rat}}\), the support matrix has an edge from row \(r\) to column \(i\) exactly when \(W_{ri}\neq0\).  By the admissible-orbit definition, selecting one incident edge at each row and column is equivalent to selecting a permutation \(\pi\) for which all \(W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0\); hence admissible normalized assignments are exactly support perfect matchings.  The gadget subclass is strict: for example, \(W=I_{3n}\) with the same type of independent errors belongs to \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rat}}\) but cannot equal any \(W_G\), since every gadget has the nonzero local entry \((W_G)_{u_i v_i}=-1\).  Any exact or approximate algorithm valid on the larger published input class would restrict to the gadget images, so both lower bounds transfer by subclass inclusion.  This uses stability only as the additional filter defining the counted subfamily; it does not attribute a stability assumption to the unfiltered matching formulation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corollary exposes the sharp bounded-degree elbows hidden by the unrestricted hardness statement: exact complexity changes between degrees two and three, while approximation changes between degrees five and six. \textit{Gap.} Greenhill, Weitz, and Sly establish the donor-graph regimes, but none gives a coefficient-valid, fixed-labelled cyclic-LiNG realization or proves that all four regimes transfer without loss to the Schur-stable normalized-assignment count. \textit{Consumer.} It tells a stability-conditioned matching-frequency or adaptive-intervention procedure exactly when the gadget subproblem is polynomially countable, deterministically approximable, or provably resistant to an FPRAS.

\begin{theorem}[thm:scc-factorization-real]\label{thm:scc-factorization-real}
For every positive integer \(q\) and every real matrix \(W\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}\), put \(B=I_q-W\). Restriction to the strongly connected components of the directed support graph of \(B\) gives bijections
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C).
\]
The inverse maps concatenate the local permutations in any fixed topological order of the condensation graph. Consequently,
\[
 N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_C.
\]
No rationality, invertibility, unit-diagonal, or reference-stability condition on \(W\) is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(q\in\mathbb N\) with \(q\geq 1\) and \(W\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}\), and set \(B=I_q-W\).  Let \(D(B)\) be the directed support graph from Definition~\(\mathrm{def:scc\text{-}local\text{-}objects}\): it has the arrow \(j\to i\) precisely when \(B_{ij}\neq0\).  If \(i\neq r\), then \((I_q)_{ri}=0\), so
\[
 B_{ri}=(I_q)_{ri}-W_{ri}=-W_{ri}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 W_{ri}\neq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_{ri}\neq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\to r\text{ is an arrow of }D(B)
 \qquad(i\neq r). \tag{1}
\]

We first establish the admissible-orbit bijection.  Let \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\).  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}orbit}\),
\[
 W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0
 \qquad\text{for every }i\in[q]. \tag{2}
\]
Consider any cycle of \(\pi\) of length \(s\geq2\), and write it as
\[
 i_0\longmapsto i_1\longmapsto\cdots
 \longmapsto i_{s-1}\longmapsto i_0,
 \qquad \pi(i_t)=i_{t+1},
\]
where subscripts are read modulo \(s\).  Since consecutive vertices are distinct, (1) and (2) give
\[
 i_0\longrightarrow i_1\longrightarrow\cdots
 \longrightarrow i_{s-1}\longrightarrow i_0
\]
as a directed cycle in \(D(B)\).  Hence all vertices in this permutation cycle are mutually reachable and lie in one strongly connected component.  A one-cycle consists of one vertex and is likewise contained in its unique strongly connected component.  Because the cycles of \(\pi\) partition \([q]\), every cycle is contained in a component, and therefore
\[
 \pi(C)=C
 \qquad\text{for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B). \tag{3}
\]

Identify each \(C\) with \([|C|]\) through its inherited increasing order.  Equation (3) makes \(\pi_C:=\pi|_C\) a permutation of \(C\).  For every \(i\in C\), Definition~\(\mathrm{def:scc\text{-}local\text{-}objects}\), (2), and principality of \(W_C=W[C,C]\) imply
\[
 (W_C)_{\pi_C(i),i}=W_{\pi(i),i}\neq0. \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\pi_C\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C)\), and restriction defines
\[
 R:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C),
 \qquad
 R(\pi)=(\pi_C)_C. \tag{5}
\]

Conversely, take a tuple \((\sigma_C)_C\) in the product on the right of (5).  On the disjoint union \([q]=\bigsqcup_C C\), define
\[
 \sigma(i)=\sigma_C(i)\qquad(i\in C). \tag{6}
\]
Each \(\sigma_C\) is a bijection of \(C\), so (6) is a bijection of \([q]\), hence \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_q\).  Local admissibility and the definition of a principal submatrix yield
\[
 W_{\sigma(i),i}=(W_C)_{\sigma_C(i),i}\neq0
 \qquad(i\in C). \tag{7}
\]
Definition~\(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}orbit}\) therefore gives \(\sigma\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\).  Equations (5)--(7) show coordinatewise that restriction and disjoint-union concatenation are inverse maps.  Listing the components in any fixed topological order of the condensation graph merely fixes the order in which those disjoint local permutations are concatenated.  We have proved
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C). \tag{8}
\]
Since all sets in (8) are finite, Definition~\(\mathrm{def:orbit\text{-}counts}\) gives
\[
 N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_{\mathrm{adm}}(W_C). \tag{9}
\]

It remains to show that (8) respects Schur stability.  Order the strongly connected components as \(C_1,\ldots,C_k\) topologically in the condensation graph, with the source of every intercomponent arrow preceding its target.  If \(a>b\), then there can be no arrow from a vertex of \(C_a\) to a vertex of \(C_b\).  Recalling that a nonzero entry \(B_{ij}\) represents the arrow \(j\to i\), this says
\[
 B[C_b,C_a]=0\qquad(a>b).
\]
Thus, in this component order, \(B\) is block lower triangular (equivalently, reversing the order makes it block upper triangular).  Since \(W=I_q-B\), \(W\) has the same block-triangular pattern and diagonal blocks \(W_C\).

For \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\), define
\[
 D_\pi=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(W_{\pi(1),1},\ldots,
 W_{\pi(q),q}\bigr).
\]
Every diagonal entry of \(D_\pi\) is nonzero by (2), so \(D_\pi^{-1}\) exists.  Equation (3) implies that \(P_\pi\) is block diagonal in the component partition, while \(D_\pi^{-1}\) is diagonal and hence block diagonal.  Therefore the normalizer \(T_\pi=D_\pi^{-1}P_\pi\) from Definition~\(\mathrm{def:normalized\text{-}candidate}\) is block diagonal.  Multiplication on the left by this block-diagonal matrix preserves the block-triangular pattern of \(W\), and (4) shows that its restriction to each diagonal block is exactly the local normalizer.  Hence
\[
 (W_\pi)[C,C]=T_{\pi_C}W_C. \tag{10}
\]
By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:normalized\text{-}candidate}\), \(B_\pi=I_q-W_\pi\).  It follows from (10) that \(B_\pi\) is block triangular with diagonal blocks
\[
 B_\pi[C,C]
 =I_{|C|}-T_{\pi_C}W_C
 =:B^C_{\pi_C}, \tag{11}
\]
where \(B^C_{\pi_C}\) is precisely the normalized coefficient candidate formed from \(W_C\) and \(\pi_C\).

For completeness, the characteristic-polynomial factorization used next follows directly from the determinant definition.  For a two-block lower-triangular matrix
\(
 \begin{psmallmatrix}A&0\\ E&D\end{psmallmatrix}
\), every nonzero term in the Leibniz formula must match the rows of the first block with the columns of the first block; the remaining rows are then matched with the remaining columns.  The sum of those terms is \(\det(A)\det(D)\).  Induction over the number of diagonal blocks, applied to \(zI_q-B_\pi\), therefore gives
\[
 \det(zI_q-B_\pi)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \det\bigl(zI_{|C|}-B^C_{\pi_C}\bigr)
 \qquad(z\in\mathbb C). \tag{12}
\]
Thus the roots on the left of (12), with algebraic multiplicity, are exactly the multiset union of the roots of the factors on the right.  By the definition of \(\rho\),
\[
 \rho(B_\pi)
 =\max_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \rho(B^C_{\pi_C}). \tag{13}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \rho(B_\pi)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho(B^C_{\pi_C})<1
 \text{ for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B). \tag{14}
\]
By Definition~\(\mathrm{def:stable\text{-}orbit}\), the right side of (14) is equivalent to
\[
 \pi_C\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)
 \qquad\text{for every }C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B). \tag{15}
\]
Therefore the bijection (8) restricts in both directions to
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C). \tag{16}
\]
Taking finite cardinalities in (16), using Definition~\(\mathrm{def:orbit\text{-}counts}\), and then using \(N_C=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)\) from Definition~\(\mathrm{def:scc\text{-}local\text{-}objects}\), yields
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_C)
 =\prod_{C\in\operatorname{SCC}(B)}N_C. \tag{17}
\]
Only real-matrix identities, nonzero entries required separately for each admissible normalization, and the five stated definitions were used.  In particular, the proof never assumes that \(W\) is rational or invertible, that it has unit diagonal, or that the reference matrix \(I_q-W\) is Schur stable.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:one-scc-counting-hardness]\label{thm:one-scc-counting-hardness}
For every simple graph \(G\) on \(n\geq1\) labelled vertices, let \(G^+\) be its cone, obtained by adjoining one new vertex \(x\) adjacent to every vertex of \(G\). Then \(G^+\) is connected,
\[
 |\mathcal I(G^+)|=|\mathcal I(G)|+1,
\]
\(W_{G^+}\in\mathcal R_{3(n+1)}\), and the directed support graph of \(B_{G^+}\) has exactly one strongly connected component. Computing \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}\) remains \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions on rational stable-reference gadget matrices whose support digraph has one strongly connected component. Unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\), there is no FPRAS on this one-component gadget class. No bounded-degree promise is asserted for the coned images.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(V=[n]\) and \(n\geq1\). Let \(x\notin V\), and define the cone \(G^+\) to have vertex set \(V\cup\{x\}\) and edge set
\[
 E\cup\bigl\{\{x,i\}:i\in V\bigr\}. \tag{10}
\]
Every original vertex is adjacent to \(x\), so any two vertices of \(G^+\) are joined by a path of length at most two. Thus \(G^+\) is connected.

Partition the independent sets of \(G^+\) according as they contain \(x\). If \(x\) is absent, restriction to \(V\) is an arbitrary member of \(\mathcal I(G)\), and every member of \(\mathcal I(G)\) extends uniquely in this way. If \(x\) is present, (10) forbids every vertex of \(V\), so the only such independent set is \(\{x\}\). The two cases are disjoint; hence
\[
 \mathcal I(G^+)
 =\mathcal I(G)\,\dot\cup\,\bigl\{\{x\}\bigr\},
 \qquad
 |\mathcal I(G^+)|=|\mathcal I(G)|+1. \tag{11}
\]

We next verify the single-component assertion directly from the frozen gadget support. For a graph \(H\), define its coefficient-support digraph to have an arrow \(b\to a\) when \((B_H)_{ab}\neq0\). In the local coordinates \((u_i,v_i,w_i)\), the gadget block is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&1\\
 p&0&0\\
 r&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{12}
\]
Here \(p=-25/16\neq0\), and \(r=(15/16)(1-\delta)>0\), because \(0<\delta<1\) at the frozen value. Thus (12) gives all four arrows
\[
 u_i\longleftrightarrow v_i,
 \qquad
 u_i\longleftrightarrow w_i. \tag{13}
\]
Each local triple is therefore strongly connected. If \(\{i,j\}\) is an undirected edge of \(H\), symmetry of its adjacency matrix and \(g>0\) give
\[
 (B_H)_{u_i u_j}=g=(B_H)_{u_j u_i}, \tag{14}
\]
so (14) supplies both arrows \(u_i\to u_j\) and \(u_j\to u_i\). When \(H\) is connected, a graph path, (14), and transitivity of directed reachability show that every pair of root coordinates is mutually reachable. Equations (13) then show that every coordinate in every local triple is mutually reachable from every other coordinate. Hence the support digraph of \(B_H\) has one strongly connected component. Applying this conclusion to the connected graph \(H=G^+\) proves the asserted support property.

The stable-orbit realization theorem, applied to \(G^+\), gives
\[
 \rho(B_{G^+})<1,
 \qquad
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_{G^+})
 =|\mathcal I(G^+)|, \tag{15}
\]
and rational entries of polynomial encoding length. The diagonal of \(B_{G^+}\) is zero by the gadget definition, so \(W_{G^+}\) has unit diagonal. If \(W_{G^+}z=0\) for nonzero \(z\), then \(B_{G^+}z=z\), contradicting (15). Thus \(W_{G^+}\) is invertible, and all defining conditions of \(\mathcal R_{3(n+1)}\) hold.

For exact hardness, let an oracle compute \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}\) on the promised one-SCC gadget class. Given arbitrary \(G\), construct \(G^+\) and \(W_{G^+}\), make one oracle call, and return
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_{G^+})-1
 =|\mathcal I(G^+)|-1
 =|\mathcal I(G)|, \tag{16}
\]
where the first equality uses (15) and the second uses (11). Both constructions have polynomial size. The cited independent-set counting theorem makes (16) a polynomial-time one-query Turing reduction from a \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete function. Membership in \(\#\mathrm P\) follows by restricting the verifier proved in \(\mathrm{thm:counting\mbox{-}separation}\) to this subclass of \(\mathcal R_q\). Therefore the restricted problem is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete.

For approximation hardness, suppose instead that an FPRAS exists on this one-SCC gadget class. Take a maximum-degree-six donor graph \(G\), put
\[
 Z=|\mathcal I(G)|,
 \qquad
 Z'=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W_{G^+})=Z+1.
\]
The empty set is independent, so \(Z\geq1\), and consequently
\[
 Z'=Z+1\leq2Z. \tag{17}
\]
For a requested \(0<\epsilon<1\), invoke the assumed scheme for \(Z'\) with relative tolerance \(\epsilon/2\), and let its output be \(Y\). Return
\[
 \widehat Z=\max\{0,Y-1\}. \tag{18}
\]
On the scheme's success event,
\[
 |Y-Z'|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}Z'. \tag{19}
\]
Projection onto \([0,\infty)\) cannot increase distance from the nonnegative number \(Z\): if \(Y-1\geq0\) this is equality, while if \(Y-1<0\), then \(Z\leq Z-(Y-1)\). Using (17)--(19),
\[
 |\widehat Z-Z|
 \leq |(Y-1)-Z|
 =|Y-Z'|
 \leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}Z'
 \leq\epsilon Z. \tag{20}
\]
The success probability is unchanged, and replacing \(\epsilon\) by \(\epsilon/2\) preserves polynomial running time. Thus (18) would be an FPRAS for independent-set counting on maximum-degree-six graphs, contradicting the cited inapproximability lemma unless \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\). The cone vertex has degree \(n\), so this argument intentionally makes no bounded-degree claim about the output matrices. Since every hard output has a single support SCC, the hardness cannot be attributed to multiplying many SCC-local counts.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The cone reduction proves that exact and approximate stable-orbit hardness persists inside a single feedback component, so SCC product multiplication is not the source of the obstruction. \textit{Gap.} Global independent-set hardness does not by itself rule out tractability when the coefficient support has only one strongly connected component. \textit{Consumer.} Any stability-conditioned matching sampler must confront hard within-component filtering even when SCC decomposition produces only one local problem.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target is the number of Schur-stable mechanisms in the population-identified normalized ICA row orbit, not an arbitrary count attached to one ICA labeling and not an effect estimand. On the gadget image, equality with \(|\mathcal I(G)|\) is proved rather than stipulated. The fixed-labelled images exhibit exact degree-two/three and approximation degree-five/six thresholds, and the cone construction shows that hardness remains when the coefficient-support digraph itself has one SCC. Exact SCC factorization for arbitrary real equation matrices therefore isolates components algebraically but does not make the within-component stability filter easy. The matching-frequency sampler and adaptive intervention rule of Sharifian, Salehkaleybar, and Kiyavash use the unfiltered matching orbit; when equilibrium stability is additionally imposed, their frequencies and rewards must instead be conditioned on the stable orbit. The permanent FPRAS for all admissible assignments does not approximate those conditioned frequencies by itself.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The degree-two/three exact and degree-five/six approximation phase diagram is proved only on fixed-labelled gadget images, where the donor graph is recoverable in polynomial time. The one-SCC cone reduction deliberately carries no bounded-degree promise because its universal vertex has unbounded degree. Admissible and stable SCC factorization is algebraic and holds for every real equation matrix, but the local enumerator, exact Lyapunov screening, and stated bit-complexity bounds apply only to the rational stable-reference class \(\mathcal R_q\) and are output-sensitive in local admissible matchings rather than stable outputs alone. The analytic proof uses conservative contour constants; the complete-orbit computations for \(K_2\), \(P_5\), \(C_5\), \(K_{1,4}\), and \(K_5\) are finite sanity checks, not substitutes for the uniform proof. FOBI covariance convergence is only modulo signed-row conjugacy, and confidence-image coverage is pointwise, not uniform near graph-support, eigengap, normalization, or unit-circle boundaries.

\section{Honest scope}

The phase diagram concerns the promised fixed-labelled gadget images, not all sparse cyclic LiNG matrices. The one-SCC result establishes hardness persistence but makes no bounded-degree claim. Admissible and Schur-stable orbit factorization holds for arbitrary real equation matrices without rationality, invertibility, unit diagonal, or stable-reference assumptions. Enumeration, exact Lyapunov certification, and their bit bounds are asserted only on \(\mathcal R_q\), with work depending on enumerated local admissible matchings. The note does not provide an almost-uniform generator for the stable orbit, stability-conditioned adaptive submodularity, or a uniform graph-support recovery theorem. The FOBI result assumes distinct centered standardized fourth cumulants and gives pointwise signed-orbit-honest confidence-image coverage; it makes no uniform singleton-count claim at discontinuity boundaries.
