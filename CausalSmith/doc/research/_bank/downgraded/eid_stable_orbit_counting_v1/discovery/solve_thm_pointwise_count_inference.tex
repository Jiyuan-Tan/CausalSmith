%%% FIELD stmt-square-ica-monomial-identification
In the square model \(X=W^{-1}e\), if \(W\) is nonsingular, the coordinates of \(e\) are independent and nondegenerate, and at most one coordinate of \(e\) is Gaussian, then the observational law \(P_X\) determines the monomial row orbit \([W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\).

%%% FIELD stmt-fobi-pointwise-limit
For every fixed element \((P_X,W,e,X^{(m)})\in\mathcal M_q^{\mathrm{FOBI}}\), the FOBI unmixing estimator \(\widehat A_m^{\mathrm F}\) has, in a locally aligned signed-row chart, a population limit \(A_0\in[W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\) and a mean-zero influence function \(\psi_A\) with finite second moment such that
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vec}(\widehat A_m^{\mathrm F}-A_0)
 =m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_A(X_j)+o_{\Pr}(1)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,V_A),
 \qquad
 V_A=\mathbb E[\psi_A(X;P_X)\psi_A(X;P_X)^{\mathsf T}],
\]
where the equality is understood in that local chart. The expansion is a linear combination of empirical moments of degrees at most four, with coefficients continuous whenever the covariance is positive definite and the centered kurtosis gaps are nonzero.

%%% FIELD proof-prop-population-orbit-identification
Let \((P_X,W,e)\in\mathcal M_q\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:invertible\mbox{-}equations}\) and the structural equation give the square mixing representation
\[
 X=Ae,\qquad A=W^{-1}.
\]
The coordinates of \(e\) are mutually independent by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:independent\mbox{-}errors}\), have positive finite variances by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonzero\mbox{-}error\mbox{-}variances}\), and at most one is Gaussian by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:non\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}errors}\). The cited square-ICA identification lemma therefore implies that \(P_X\) determines \([W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\). This is identification of a structural orbit from the observed law; it does not define the orbit to equal an observed functional.

It remains to prove that the count does not depend on the representative of this identified orbit. Let
\[
 \widetilde W=MW,
 \qquad
 M=D P_\sigma,
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_q),
 \qquad a_i\neq0,
\]
where \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_q\). For \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_q\), define \(\tau=\sigma\circ\pi\). By the convention \((P_\pi)_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{j=\pi(i)\}\), the \(i\)-th row of \(P_\pi D P_\sigma W\) is \(a_{\pi(i)}\) times row \(\sigma(\pi(i))\) of \(W\). Hence
\[
 \widetilde W_{\pi(i),i}
 =a_{\pi(i)}W_{\sigma(\pi(i)),i}
 =a_{\pi(i)}W_{\tau(i),i}.
\]
Because every \(a_{\pi(i)}\) is nonzero, this proves the equivalence
\[
 \pi\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(\widetilde W)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \tau\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{adm}}(W).
\]
The map \(\pi\mapsto\sigma\circ\pi\) is a bijection of \(\mathfrak S_q\), with inverse \(\tau\mapsto\sigma^{-1}\circ\tau\), so it is a bijection of the two admissible sets.

For an admissible \(\pi\), direct substitution into the normalization gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde W_\pi
 &=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
      \frac{1}{a_{\pi(i)}W_{\tau(i),i}}:i\in[q]
    \right)P_\pi D P_\sigma W\\
 &=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
      \frac{1}{W_{\tau(i),i}}:i\in[q]
    \right)P_\tau W
 =W_\tau.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Therefore \(I_q-\widetilde W_\pi=I_q-W_\tau\), and so
\[
 \rho(I_q-\widetilde W_\pi)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rho(I_q-W_\tau)<1.
\]
The admissible-set bijection consequently restricts to a bijection
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(\widetilde W)
 \longleftrightarrow
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)
\]
that preserves the normalized coefficient matrix itself. Taking finite cardinalities yields
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(\widetilde W)=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W).
\]
Thus the explicitly identified map is
\[
 P_X\longmapsto[W]_{\mathrm{mon}}
       \longmapsto N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W),
\]
and the second arrow is well defined by the proved representative invariance.

%%% FIELD proposed-thm-pointwise-count-inference
For every fixed law in \(\mathcal M_q^{\mathrm{FOBI}}\), whose structural errors satisfy \(0<\operatorname{Var}(e_i)<\infty\) for every \(i\in[q]\), define \(\mathcal H_q\) to be the finite group of orthogonal maps on \(\mathbb R^{q^2}\) given by
\[
 Q_{J,\sigma}\operatorname{vec}(A)=\operatorname{vec}(J P_\sigma A),
 \qquad
 \sigma\in\mathfrak S_q,
 \quad
 J=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_q),
 \quad s_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
The plug-in covariance estimator is orbit-consistent in the sense that
\[
 \min_{Q\in\mathcal H_q}
 \|\widehat V_m-QV_{\mathrm{FOBI}}Q^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm F}
 \ \longrightarrow_{\Pr}\ 0.
\]
The matrix-valued symmetrized set satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \Pr\{N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\in C_m^N\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
and
\[
 d_{\mathrm H,F}(K_m,\mathcal O_U(W))=O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2})
\]
in the normalized-row Frobenius topology; in particular the underlying estimator contracts in the quotient metric \(d_{\mathrm{orb}}\). These assertions are pointwise in the fixed law. No uniform graph-support, shrinking-kurtosis-gap, zero-normalization-boundary, or unit-circle-boundary guarantee is made.

%%% FIELD replacement-prop-population-orbit-identification
For every law in \(\mathcal M_q\), including the nondegeneracy condition \(0<\operatorname{Var}(e_i)<\infty\) for every \(i\in[q]\), square ICA identifies \([W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\) from \(P_X\). If \(\widetilde W=MW\) for an invertible monomial matrix \(M\), re-referencing induces a bijection between the admissible normalized assignments of \(W\) and \(\widetilde W\) that preserves their normalized coefficient matrices. Consequently \(N_{\mathrm{stab}}(\widetilde W)=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\), so the stable count is a population-law functional rather than a labeling-dependent count.

%%% FIELD proof-thm-pointwise-count-inference
Fix one law in \(\mathcal M_q^{\mathrm{FOBI}}\). All constants below may depend on this law. With the corrected central-moment definition of \(\kappa\), put
\[
 \mu_e=\mathbb E[e],
 \qquad
 \sigma_i^2=\operatorname{Var}(e_i),
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_q),
 \qquad
 \xi=D^{-1}(e-\mu_e).
\]
The nonzero-variance assumption makes every division by \(\sigma_i\) valid. Independence of the coordinates of \(e\) gives independence of the coordinates of \(\xi\), and
\[
 \mathbb E[\xi]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\xi\xi^{\mathsf T}]=I_q,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\xi_i^4]-3=\kappa_i.
\]
Moreover \(e=WX\), so \(\|e\|_2\leq\|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|X\|_2\). The finite eighth-moment assumption therefore implies \(\mathbb E\|\xi\|_2^8<\infty\). Centering the structural equation yields
\[
 X-\mathbb E[X]=\Omega\xi,
 \qquad
 \Omega=W^{-1}D.
\]
Both \(W\) and \(D\) are nonsingular, and hence
\[
 \Sigma:=\operatorname{Var}(X)=\Omega\Omega^{\mathsf T}\succ0.
\]
This establishes the positivity needed for whitening. We will repeatedly use the following elementary iid averaging argument. If \(h(X)\) is integrable, put \(h_K=h\mathbf 1\{|h|\leq K\}\). Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  \left|m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m(h_K(X_j)-\mathbb E[h_K(X)])\right|>\varepsilon
 \right)
 \leq\frac{K^2}{m\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m|h(X_j)-h_K(X_j)|>\varepsilon
 \right)
 \leq\frac{\mathbb E|h(X)-h_K(X)|}{\varepsilon}.
\]
The expectation on the right tends to zero as \(K\to\infty\). Taking \(K\to\infty\) after \(m\to\infty\), and using \(|\mathbb E[h-h_K]|\leq\mathbb E|h-h_K|\), proves
\[
 m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m h(X_j)\longrightarrow_{\Pr}\mathbb E[h(X)].
\]
Apply this result to the coordinate functions \(X_a\) and \(X_aX_b\), which are integrable under the eighth-moment condition. The algebraic identity expressing sample covariance through empirical first and second raw moments then gives \(\widehat\Sigma_m\to_{\Pr}\Sigma\). Consequently \(\Pr(\widehat\Sigma_m\succ0)\to1\), so the fallback branch in the estimator definition has asymptotically vanishing probability.

The population whitening calculation also verifies the FOBI rank condition explicitly. Set
\[
 H=\Sigma^{-1/2}\Omega,
 \qquad
 Y=\Sigma^{-1/2}(X-\mathbb E[X])=H\xi.
\]
Since \(HH^{\mathsf T}=I_q\), the square matrix \(H\) is orthogonal. Independence and centering give, for \(i\neq j\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|\xi\|_2^2\xi_i\xi_j\right]=0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|\xi\|_2^2\xi_i^2\right]
 =\mathbb E[\xi_i^4]+
   \sum_{k\neq i}\mathbb E[\xi_i^2]\mathbb E[\xi_k^2]
 =\kappa_i+q+2.
\]
It follows that the population FOBI matrix is
\[
 F:=\mathbb E[\|Y\|_2^2YY^{\mathsf T}]
 =H\operatorname{diag}(\kappa_1+q+2,\ldots,\kappa_q+q+2)H^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The pairwise-distinct-kurtosis assumption makes all eigenvalues in this display simple. Thus all inverse eigengaps used below are nonzero. If \(O_0\) is the population eigenvector matrix in decreasing-eigenvalue order, then for some permutation matrix \(P\) and sign matrix \(J\),
\[
 O_0=HPJ,
 \qquad
 A_0:=O_0^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1/2}
 =JP^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}
 =JP^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}W.
\]
Hence \(A_0\in[W]_{\mathrm{mon}}\), and its row-normalization
\[
 U_0:=\ell(A_0)
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal O_U(W)\).

By the cited FOBI pointwise-limit lemma, the empirical eigenvectors can be aligned with this population chart by signed row permutations. Thus there are signed permutation matrices \(H_m\) and their induced orthogonal vectorization actions
\[
 Q_m\operatorname{vec}(A)=\operatorname{vec}(H_mA),
 \qquad Q_m\in\mathcal H_q,
\]
such that, with \(\overline A_m=H_m\widehat A_m^{\mathrm F}\),
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vec}(\overline A_m-A_0)
 =m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_A(X_j)+o_{\Pr}(1).
\]
For a matrix \(A\) with nonzero rows, the row-normalization map is differentiable, with rowwise differential
\[
 \bigl(D\ell_A[H]\bigr)_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}
 =\frac{H_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}}{\|A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}\|_2}
 -\frac{A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}H_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}^{\mathsf T}}
  {\|A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}\|_2^3}A_{i\boldsymbol\cdot}.
\]
Every row of the nonsingular matrix \(A_0\) is nonzero, so Taylor expansion of this displayed formula gives
\[
 \sqrt m\,\operatorname{vec}(H_m\widehat U_m-U_0)
 =m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_{\mathrm F}(X_j;P_X)+o_{\Pr}(1)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ Z,
 \qquad
 Z\sim N(0,V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}).
\]
This derives the normalized-row limit from the cited unmixing limit rather than assuming it. In particular,
\[
 \|H_m\widehat U_m-U_0\|_{\mathrm F}=O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2}).
\]

We next prove covariance consistency, including the plug-in step. In a neighborhood of the fixed law on which \(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)>0\) and all kurtosis gaps are bounded away from zero, the FOBI influence function is a polynomial of degree at most four in the centered observation whose coefficients are continuous functions of the mean, covariance, fourth moments, and simple eigensystem. Apply the iid averaging argument above to every monomial of degree at most four; its integrability follows from the eighth-moment condition. The empirical counterparts of all those coefficients therefore converge in probability by continuity of inversion, the positive-definite square root, and simple-eigenvalue projectors. Hence, on events whose probabilities tend to one, there is a random \(a_m\to_{\Pr}0\) such that
\[
 \|Q_m\widehat\psi_{m,j}-\psi_{\mathrm F}(X_j;P_X)\|_2
 \leq a_m(1+\|X_j\|_2^4)
 \qquad(1\leq j\leq m).
\]
The eighth-moment assumption and Markov's inequality imply
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m
 \|Q_m\widehat\psi_{m,j}-\psi_{\mathrm F}(X_j;P_X)\|_2^2
 \leq
 a_m^2\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m(1+\|X_j\|_2^4)^2
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
Indeed, the sample average on the right is \(O_{\Pr}(1)\), because its expectation is the fixed finite number \(\mathbb E[(1+\|X\|_2^4)^2]\). Since the influence function has mean zero and finite second moment, apply the proved iid averaging result to each integrable product of two influence coordinates, and use Cauchy--Schwarz for the plug-in cross terms. This gives
\[
 Q_m\widehat V_mQ_m^{\mathsf T}
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}
 \mathbb E[\psi_{\mathrm F}(X;P_X)\psi_{\mathrm F}(X;P_X)^{\mathsf T}]
 =V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}.
\]
Because \(Q_m\in\mathcal H_q\) and this finite group is closed under transposition, the last display is equivalent to
\[
 \min_{Q\in\mathcal H_q}
 \|\widehat V_m-QV_{\mathrm{FOBI}}Q^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm F}
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]

For the Gaussian-radius calibration, first suppose \(q\geq2\). The limiting covariance is not the zero matrix. Indeed, at standardized independent components, Theorem 8's off-diagonal influence coordinates are, for \(k\neq l\),
\[
 \phi_{kl}(\xi)
 =\frac{
 (\xi_k^3-\gamma_k)\xi_l+(\xi_l^3-\gamma_l)\xi_k
 +\sum_{s\neq k,l}\xi_s^2\xi_k\xi_l
 -(\kappa_k+q+4)\xi_k\xi_l
 }{\kappa_k-\kappa_l},
 \qquad
 \gamma_k=\mathbb E[\xi_k^3].
\]
Each coordinate has mean zero, and direct subtraction gives
\[
 \phi_{lk}(\xi)=-\phi_{kl}(\xi)-\xi_k\xi_l.
\]
If both variances were zero, both mean-zero variables would vanish almost surely, forcing \(\xi_k\xi_l=0\) almost surely. This contradicts independence and unit variances, because
\[
 \mathbb E[\xi_k^2\xi_l^2]
 =\mathbb E[\xi_k^2]\mathbb E[\xi_l^2]=1.
\]
The differential of row normalization removes only the radial diagonal coordinate at the identity chart and retains these off-diagonal coordinates. Hence \(V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}\neq0\).

Let \(c_{1-\alpha}\) be the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\|Z\|_2\) for \(Z\sim N(0,V_{\mathrm{FOBI}})\). A nonzero positive-semidefinite Gaussian covariance has at least one positive eigenvalue, so \(\|Z\|_2\) has a continuous distribution, with no atom at \(c_{1-\alpha}>0\). Conditional on the sample, multiplication of \(Z_m^*\) by \(Q_m\) preserves its Euclidean norm and changes its covariance to \(Q_m\widehat V_mQ_m^{\mathsf T}\). Continuity of the positive-semidefinite square root and the proved covariance convergence therefore give
\[
 c_{m,1-\alpha}\longrightarrow_{\Pr}c_{1-\alpha}.
\]
Combining this with the normalized-row limit and the absence of a boundary atom yields
\[
 \Pr\!\left{
  \sqrt m\,\|H_m\widehat U_m-U_0\|_{\mathrm F}
  \leq c_{m,1-\alpha}
 \right}
 \longrightarrow 1-\alpha.
\]

On the event in braces, set \(R_m=H_m^{\mathsf T}U_0\). Signed row permutations preserve \(\mathcal U_q\), so \(R_m\in\mathcal O_U(W)\), and orthogonality gives
\[
 \sqrt m\,\|R_m-\widehat U_m\|_{\mathrm F}
 =\sqrt m\,\|U_0-H_m\widehat U_m\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq c_{m,1-\alpha}.
\]
Thus \(R_m\in\mathcal E_m\). By symmetrization, \(K_m\) then contains the entire normalized monomial orbit of \(R_m\), which equals \(\mathcal O_U(W)\). In particular \(\ell(W)\in K_m\). The matrix \(\ell(W)\) is invertible because it is obtained from the invertible \(W\) by nonzero row scaling. The proved representative invariance gives
\[
 N_{\mathrm{stab}}(\ell(W))=N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W).
\]
By the definition of the confidence image, the preceding inclusions imply
\[
 \left\{
  \sqrt m\,\|H_m\widehat U_m-U_0\|_{\mathrm F}
  \leq c_{m,1-\alpha}
 \right\}
 \subseteq
 \{N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\in C_m^N\}.
\]
Taking lower limits proves
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \Pr\{N_{\mathrm{stab}}(W)\in C_m^N\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
Notice that this is set inversion: it uses no continuity of the integer-valued count.

It remains to prove contraction of the whole random set. Write
\[
 r_m=\frac{c_{m,1-\alpha}}{\sqrt m},
 \qquad
 d_m=\|\widehat U_m-R_m\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
The covariance convergence makes \(c_{m,1-\alpha}=O_{\Pr}(1)\), while the normalized-row limit gives \(d_m=O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2})\). Each element of \(\mathcal E_m\) lies within \(r_m\) of \(\widehat U_m\). Signed row permutations act isometrically, so each element of \(K_m\) lies within \(r_m+d_m\) of the corresponding image of \(R_m\), which belongs to \(\mathcal O_U(W)\). Conversely, \(\widehat U_m\in\mathcal E_m\), and every point of \(\mathcal O_U(W)\) is an image of \(R_m\); the corresponding image of \(\widehat U_m\) belongs to \(K_m\) and is within \(d_m\). Therefore
\[
 d_{\mathrm H,F}(K_m,\mathcal O_U(W))
 \leq r_m+d_m
 =O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2}).
\]
The same alignment gives directly
\[
 d_{\mathrm{orb}}(\widehat A_m^{\mathrm F},W)
 \leq\|\widehat U_m-R_m\|_{\mathrm F}
 =O_{\Pr}(m^{-1/2}).
\]

If \(q=1\), assumption \(W_{11}=1\) gives \(W=(1)\) and \(\mathcal O_U(W)=\{-1,1\}\). Row normalization in one dimension is locally constant, its differential and \(V_{\mathrm{FOBI}}\) are zero, and \(\widehat U_m\in\{-1,1\}\) on both branches of its definition. Hence \(\widehat V_m=0\), \(c_{m,1-\alpha}=0\), and symmetrization gives \(K_m=\{-1,1\}\) exactly. All three conclusions follow with probability one.

Finally, every use of a covariance eigenvalue, a kurtosis gap, and an eighth-moment envelope above concerns the one fixed law. The coverage event only inserts a true orbit representative into \(K_m\); it neither selects a support nor requires normalization denominators to stay uniformly away from zero, and it never invokes local constancy across the unit-circle stability boundary. This proves precisely the stated pointwise, orbit-honest scope.
