\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}ate\_\allowbreak{}overlap\_\allowbreak{}decay}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For fixed one-sided dyadic-shell constants satisfying both \(\texttt{ass:lower-shell-richness}\) and \(\texttt{ass:upper-shell-richness}\), the absolute-risk, root-MSE, fixed-probability for each fixed \(\delta\in(0,\delta_0]\), and honest expected-length for each fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1/4)\) frontiers all equal the four-channel rate \(\rho_n\) up to constants.  The clipped estimator, five-block median, and interval upper bounds hold on the broader uniform one-sided-shell class with those two richness conditions deleted.  An exported all-\(N\) inequality gives \(E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda,N}-\tau(P)|\leq C_{\mathrm{FT}}U_N(\lambda)\), the explicit oracle comparison gives \(U_n(\lambda_n^\star)\leq C_{\mathrm{opt}}\rho_n\), and \(C_\star=C_{\mathrm{FT}}C_{\mathrm{opt}}\) yields the computable interval multiplier \(K_\alpha=C_\star\max\{\sqrt{10}(10/\alpha)^{1/3},\alpha^{-1}\}\).  Adaptation to unknown shell and nuisance certificates remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} JinSyrgkanis2024StructureAgnosticDR supplies WATE lower-bound templates in its estimator-bounded source class, whereas BonviniKennedyDukesBalakrishnan2024StructureAgnosticATE supplies a lower-rate statement for one mean functional in a different uniformly overlapped structure-agnostic source class. Neither published class is nested with the present dyadic-shell public-function experiment, so neither citation supports class inclusion or a transferred minimax theorem. The accepted-bank runs \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match} and \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_heterogeneity\_frontier\_v1} also study the full-population binary ATE, but under fixed two-sided overlap and finite discrete covariate occupancy. With \(d\) denoting the number of covariate cells, the former establishes the binary-outcome rare-cell MSE frontier \(n^{-1}+d^2/[n^2\log^2 n]\); the latter gives a radius-indexed MSE bracket for real outcomes under bounded conditional means and approximate cell-effect homogeneity. Their obstructions are unknown discrete cell occupancy and cell-effect heterogeneity, whereas the present experiment has an infinite standard-Borel covariate support, dyadic propensity decay, deterministic public nuisance functions, and only global \(L^2(P_X)\) certificates. These law and information classes are non-nested, and neither accepted-bank result transfers a converse, rate, or honest-length theorem to the present class. Weak-overlap papers give trimming rules, irregular limits, or sufficient Wald conditions rather than this finite-sample four-risk frontier with an evaluable honest interval.

\paragraph{Niche.} The public-function model isolates the statistical price of knowing only global prediction-error guarantees when treated observations vanish at a boundary.  Its all-\(N\), all-threshold bound keeps the public certificates indexed by the original experiment, so the same inequality controls the full sample and every median block.  Upper estimation and coverage require only the uniform one-sided-shell law; the matching converse requires both explicit reservoir-richness inequalities.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper supplies the clipped estimator, five-block median, and a bias-aware interval with the closed-form constants \(C_{\mathrm{FT}}\), \(C_{\mathrm{opt}}\), \(C_\star\), and \(K_\alpha\).  On the broader shell class these objects attain the four-channel upper rate.  On the reservoir-rich subclass, the established signal-only four-channel construction supplies matching absolute-risk, root-MSE, fixed-probability, and honest mean-length lower bounds.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid]\label{ass:iid}
The coordinates of \(O^{(n)}\) are independent and identically distributed under the observed marginal induced by \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (i.i.d. sampling, \cite{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=D Y_1+(1-D)Y_0\) holds \(P\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (consistency, \cite{HiranoImbensRidder2003EfficientATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:ignorability]\label{ass:ignorability}
\((Y_0,Y_1)\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}D\mid X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional ignorability, \cite{HiranoImbensRidder2003EfficientATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positivity]\label{ass:positivity}
\(P\{e(X)>0\}=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (almost-sure positivity, \cite{KhanTamer2010Irregular}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-shell]\label{ass:uniform-shell}
\(c_-u^{\kappa}\le P\{u/2<e(X)\le u\}\le c_+u^{\kappa}\) for every \(u\in(0,\lambda_0]\), with \(c_-\) and \(c_+\) fixed uniformly over the class and over \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition is satisfied by Beta-type propensity tails whose density is comparable to \(u^{\kappa-1}\), including rare-eligibility designs with a regularly varying participation margin.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:right-overlap]\label{ass:right-overlap}
\(e(X)\le 1-\eta_+\) holds \(P\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (one-sided overlap, \cite{Dorn2025WeakOverlapTStatistics}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treated-residual-variance]\label{ass:treated-residual-variance}
\(E_P[(Y-g_1(X))^2\mid D=1,X]\le\sigma_1^2\) holds \(P\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform treated residual second moment, \cite{Dorn2025WeakOverlapTStatistics}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:control-residual-variance]\label{ass:control-residual-variance}
\(E_P[(Y-g_0(X))^2\mid D=0,X]\le\sigma_0^2\) holds \(P\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform control residual second moment, \cite{Dorn2025WeakOverlapTStatistics}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-treatment-effect-signal]\label{ass:bounded-treatment-effect-signal}
\(E_P[(g_1(X)-g_0(X))^2]\le M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded conditional treatment-effect second moment, \cite{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:public-g0-radius]\label{ass:public-g0-radius}
\(\lVert m_0-g_0\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\le r_{0n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (structure-agnostic outcome-regression certificate, \cite{JinSyrgkanis2024StructureAgnosticDR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:public-g1-radius]\label{ass:public-g1-radius}
\(\lVert m_1-g_1\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\le r_{1n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (structure-agnostic outcome-regression certificate, \cite{JinSyrgkanis2024StructureAgnosticDR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:public-e-radius]\label{ass:public-e-radius}
\(\lVert p-e\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\le r_{en}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (structure-agnostic propensity certificate, \cite{BonviniKennedyDukesBalakrishnan2024StructureAgnosticATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:lower-shell-richness]\label{ass:lower-shell-richness}
\(c_-\le (1-2^{-\kappa})/(4\lambda_0^{\kappa})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The inequality holds for mixtures with two equal regularly varying tail reservoirs and a nonvanishing interior stratum, a concrete design class in which rare treatment arises in more than one covariate subgroup.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:upper-shell-richness]\label{ass:upper-shell-richness}
\(c_+\ge 2^{\kappa-1}/\lambda_0^{\kappa}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The inequality is satisfied by the same two-reservoir Beta-type mixtures after normalization and permits fixed interior mass without making the tail law degenerate.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-overlap-comparator]\label{ass:uniform-overlap-comparator}
\(\eta_-\le e(X)\le1-\eta_+\) holds \(P\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (two-sided uniform overlap, \cite{JinSyrgkanis2024StructureAgnosticDR}).
\end{assumption}

for \(\ell\geq1\), mass \(b_\ell=c_-q_{\ell-1}^\kappa\) at propensity
\(q_\ell\).  Let \(B_{\mathrm{bg}}=\sum_{\ell\geq0}b_\ell\).  Choose
\[
0<A<\min\left\{
{c_+-2^\kappa c_-\over2^{\kappa+1}},
{1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}\over2s_0^\kappa},
{c_+\{(2\gamma)^\kappa-1\}
 \over2^{\kappa+1}(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa}
\right\},
\]
and set
\[
t_j=s_02^{-j},\quad\quad
w_j=A(1-2^{-\kappa})t_j^\kappa,\quad\quad j\geq0.
\]
For every finite \(N\), use mutually distinct signal points \(x_{j,k}\),
balancing companions \(\widetilde x_{j,k}\), background points, and completion
points, where \(j\geq0\) and \(1\leq k\leq N\).  Give each signal point and
its companion mass \(w_j/N\), and give both public baseline propensity
\(e_0=t_j\).  Define the two reservoirs
\[
S_N=\bigcup_{j\geq0}\{x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\},\quad\quad
C_N=\bigcup_{j\geq0}\{\widetilde x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\}.
\]
Reserve their combined mass \(R_{J+j}=2w_j\) from the virtual endpoint
\(q_{J+j}\), and put \(R_\ell=0\) otherwise.  Define
\[
d_\ell=c_+q_\ell^\kappa-b_\ell-R_\ell,\quad\quad
H=1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}-2As_0^\kappa,\quad\quad
D_{\mathrm{cap}}=\sum_{\ell\geq0}d_\ell,\quad\quad
\pi_\ell={H\over D_{\mathrm{cap}}}d_\ell,
\]
and put completion mass \(\pi_\ell\) at its distinct point of propensity
\(q_\ell\).  In unchanged families both members of every active pair have
propensity \(t_j\).  In a treated fuzzy cell, with
\(a_{\theta,k}=\theta\delta Z_k\), use propensities
\(t_j/(1+a_{\theta,k})\) and \(t_j/(1-a_{\theta,k})\) on the signal and
companion points, respectively, and perturb the treated regression only on the
signal point.  In a control fuzzy cell, with \(a_{\theta,k}=\theta tZ_k\), use
propensities \((t_j+a_{\theta,k})/(1+a_{\theta,k})\) and
\((t_j-a_{\theta,k})/(1-a_{\theta,k})\), respectively, and perturb the control
regression only on the signal point.  Thus each fixed-sign pair has a
sign-invariant propensity multiset.

All localization sets are signal-only.  Write
\[
A_s^S=S_N\cap\{e_0\leq s\},\quad\quad
B_s^S=S_N\cap\{s<e_0\leq2s\},
\]
and define its companion set by
\(C_s^S=\{\widetilde x_{j,k}:x_{j,k}\in B_s^S\}\).  For a fixed
\(j_c\geq1\), put \(s_c=t_{j_c+1}\) and
\[
B_c^S=S_N\cap\{s_c<e_0\leq2s_c\}
=\{x_{j_c,k}:1\leq k\leq N\},\quad\quad
C_c^S=\{\widetilde x_{j_c,k}:1\leq k\leq N\}.
\]
Background and completion atoms whose propensities lie in the same numerical
bands are not in \(S_N\), remain unperturbed, and never enter a regression
localization.  On this completed support build the four subexperiments in
\(\mathcal M_n\): shell-localized treated Gaussian mean shifts for
\(Q_\kappa\); an untreated-observation coupling at scale
\(n^{-1/(\kappa+1)}\); a treated signed-cell fuzzy mixture on the unique signal
level \(B_s^S\), with \(s\asymp r_{en}^{2/(\kappa+2)}\); and a control
signed-cell fuzzy mixture on \(B_c^S\), whose signal mass is
\(P_X(B_c^S)=w_{j_c}\).  In the control family set
\(g_0=h_0Z_k\), \(h_0=c_A r_{0n}/\sqrt{P_X(B_c^S)}\), \(m_0=0\), \(p=e_0\),
and
\[
1-e_{\theta,Z}={1-e_0\over1+\theta tZ_k},\quad\quad
t=c_A\min\left\{{r_{en}\over\sqrt{P_X(B_c^S)}},{1\over4}\right\}
\]
on signal cells, using the opposite tilt on their companions.  Keep the public
functions fixed within each subexperiment, take \(N\) large enough to make
pair-index collisions and prior-target fluctuations negligible, and combine
the four component separations into \(\rho_n\) by the maximum-to-sum comparison.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal M\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}kappa}; \texttt{r\_\allowbreak{}\{0n\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{r\_\allowbreak{}\{1n\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{r\_\allowbreak{}\{en\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{M}; \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}+\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}lambda\_\allowbreak{}0}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}eta\_\allowbreak{}+\allowbreak{}}.
\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Use a dyadic family of \(\widehat\tau_{\lambda}\), sample-split empirical shell counts, and held-out prediction-risk certificates in a Lepski or aggregation comparison to derive a data-driven threshold and the smallest uniform oracle-inequality penalty relative to \(\rho_n\).

\begin{definition}[def:finite-threshold-envelope]\label{def:finite-threshold-envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{U\_\allowbreak{}N}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\) and an i.i.d. observed-law sample
\(O_1,\ldots,O_N\), define
\[
\widehat\tau_{\lambda,N}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_\lambda(O_i)
\]
using the public functions and the \(n\)-indexed certificates
\((r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})\).  Thus \(N\) is only the number of observations
averaged.  Set
\[
v_\kappa(\lambda)=
\begin{cases}
\lambda^{-(1-\kappa)/2},&0<\kappa<1,\\
\sqrt{1+\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)},&\kappa=1,\\
1,&\kappa>1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\overline c_\kappa=\frac{c_+}{1-2^{-\kappa}},
\]
and
\[
H_\kappa=
\begin{cases}
\lambda_0^{-1}+\overline c_\kappa/(1-\kappa),&0<\kappa<1,\\
\lambda_0^{-1}+\overline c_1,&\kappa=1,\\
\lambda_0^{-1}+\overline c_\kappa\lambda_0^{\kappa-1}/(\kappa-1),&\kappa>1.
\end{cases}
\]
Define the finite-threshold envelope
\[
\begin{split}
U_N(\lambda)={}&
\frac{v_\kappa(\lambda)}{\sqrt N}
+\frac{r_{1n}}{\sqrt{N\lambda}}
+\frac{r_{en}}{\sqrt N\,\lambda^{3/2}}
+r_{1n}\lambda^{\kappa/2}
+\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}{\lambda}
+r_{0n}r_{en}
\end{split}
\]
and the explicit score constant
\[
\begin{split}
C_{\mathrm{FT}}={}&1+\sqrt M+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2H_\kappa}
+\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_0^2}{\eta_+}}
+\frac{\bar r}{\sqrt{\eta_+}}
+\frac{2\bar r\sqrt{\sigma_0^2}}{\eta_+^{3/2}}\\
&+\sqrt2+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2}+\sqrt{\overline c_\kappa}
+\frac2{\eta_+}.
\end{split}
\]
For later oracle calibration, put \(v_0=v_\kappa(\lambda_0)\) and define
\[
C_{\mathrm{oe}}
=v_0+\bar r\lambda_0^{-1/2}+\bar r\lambda_0^{-3/2}
+2+\lambda_0^{-1}\bar r^{2/(\kappa+2)},
\]
\[
C_{\mathrm g}
=v_0+\lambda_0^{-1}+2\bar r\lambda_0^{-3/2}
+\lambda_0^{-(1-\kappa)/2}+1,
\]
\[
C_{\mathrm{opt}}=
\begin{cases}
4+C_{\mathrm{oe}}+C_{\mathrm g},&0<\kappa\leq1,\\
4+C_{\mathrm{oe}},&\kappa>1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
C_\star=C_{\mathrm{FT}}C_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\]

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-shell-calculus]\label{lem:uniform-shell-calculus}
There are constants depending only on \((c_-,c_+,\kappa,\lambda_0)\) such that, uniformly over \(\mathcal M_n\) and \(0<\lambda\le\lambda_0\), \(P\{e(X)\le\lambda\}\asymp\lambda^{\kappa}\) and \(E_P[(e(X)\vee\lambda)^{-1}]\asymp v_{\kappa}(\lambda)^2\), where \(v_{\kappa}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-(1-\kappa)/2}\) for \(0<\kappa<1\), \(v_1(\lambda)=\sqrt{1+\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)}\), and \(v_{\kappa}(\lambda)=1\) for \(\kappa>1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\mathcal M_n\) and \(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\).  By \(\texttt{def:public-weak-overlap-class}\), the law \(P\) satisfies \(\texttt{ass:positivity}\) and \(\texttt{ass:uniform-shell}\).  Positivity and disjointness give
\[
 \{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 =\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \{2^{-j-1}\lambda<e(X)\leq 2^{-j}\lambda\}
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Consequently, the upper shell inequality and countable additivity imply
\[
 P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \leq c_+\lambda^\kappa\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\kappa}
 =\frac{c_+}{1-2^{-\kappa}}\lambda^\kappa.
\]
The shell with upper endpoint \(\lambda\) and the lower shell inequality give
\[
 P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \geq P\{\lambda/2<e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \geq c_-\lambda^\kappa.
\]
Thus \(P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}\asymp\lambda^\kappa\), uniformly in the chosen class member and in \(\lambda\).

It remains to control
\[
 H_\lambda:=E_P\!\left[(e(X)\vee\lambda)^{-1}\right].
\]
Write \(C_F=c_+/(1-2^{-\kappa})\).  The preceding tail bounds first show
\[
 c_-\lambda^{\kappa-1}
 \leq \lambda^{-1}P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \leq C_F\lambda^{\kappa-1}.
\]
For a uniform upper bound on the remaining part, set
\[
 J=\left\lfloor\log_2(\lambda_0/\lambda)\right\rfloor.
\]
Then \(J\geq0\), \(2^J\lambda\leq\lambda_0<2^{J+1}\lambda\), and the events
\[
 \{e(X)\leq\lambda\},\qquad
 A_j:=\{2^j\lambda<e(X)\leq2^{j+1}\lambda\}
 \quad(0\leq j<J),
\]
together with \(A_{\mathrm{top}}:=\{2^J\lambda<e(X)\leq\lambda_0\}\) and \(A_+:=\{e(X)>\lambda_0\}\), form a partition up to null sets.  For every \(0\leq j<J\), the upper endpoint \(2^{j+1}\lambda\) is at most \(\lambda_0\), so the upper shell inequality yields
\[
 E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_j}}{e(X)}\right]
 \leq \frac{P(A_j)}{2^j\lambda}
 \leq c_+2^\kappa\lambda^{\kappa-1}2^{j(\kappa-1)}.
\]
Moreover, the already proved tail upper bound and \(2^J\lambda>\lambda_0/2\) give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_{\mathrm{top}}}}{e(X)}\right]
 \leq \frac{P\{e(X)\leq\lambda_0\}}{2^J\lambda}
 \leq 2C_F\lambda_0^{\kappa-1},
\]
where the inequality is also valid when \(A_{\mathrm{top}}\) is empty.  On \(A_+\), one has \(e(X)^{-1}<\lambda_0^{-1}\).  Combining these bounds with the contribution below \(\lambda\) proves
\[
 H_\lambda
 \leq C\left{1+\lambda^{\kappa-1}
 \left(1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^{j(\kappa-1)}\right)\right},
\]
where an empty sum is zero and \(C\) depends only on \((c_+,\kappa,\lambda_0)\).

Suppose first that \(0<\kappa<1\).  The geometric sum in the last display is bounded by \((1-2^{\kappa-1})^{-1}\).  Since \(0<\lambda<1\), \(\lambda^{\kappa-1}\geq1\), and hence
\[
 H_\lambda\leq C\lambda^{\kappa-1}.
\]
The contribution from \(\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}\) in the earlier lower bound gives \(H_\lambda\geq c_-\lambda^{\kappa-1}\).  Therefore \(H_\lambda\asymp\lambda^{\kappa-1}=v_\kappa(\lambda)^2\).

Suppose next that \(\kappa=1\).  The upper bound becomes \(H_\lambda\leq C(J+1)\).  For each \(0\leq j<J\), the lower shell inequality gives
\[
 E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_j}}{e(X)}\right]
 \geq \frac{P(A_j)}{2^{j+1}\lambda}
 \geq c_-.
\]
Together with the below-threshold contribution, which is at least \(c_-\), this yields \(H_\lambda\geq c_-(J+1)\).  Because
\[
 J\leq\log_2(\lambda_0/\lambda)<J+1,
\]
the quantity \(J+1\) is comparable, by absolute constants, to \(1+\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)\).  Hence
\[
 H_\lambda\asymp 1+\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)=v_1(\lambda)^2.
\]

Finally, suppose that \(\kappa>1\).  The finite geometric sum satisfies
\[
 \lambda^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^{j(\kappa-1)}
 \leq \frac{(2^J\lambda)^{\kappa-1}}{2^{\kappa-1}-1}
 \leq \frac{\lambda_0^{\kappa-1}}{2^{\kappa-1}-1},
\]
so the displayed upper bound for \(H_\lambda\) is a constant.  Since the declared range of the propensity score and \(\lambda_0<1\) imply \(0<e(X)\vee\lambda\leq1\) almost surely, \(H_\lambda\geq1\).  Thus \(H_\lambda\asymp1=v_\kappa(\lambda)^2\).

All lower and upper constants obtained above depend only on \((c_-,c_+,\kappa,\lambda_0)\), and not on \(P\), \(n\), or \(\lambda\).  This proves both asserted uniform equivalences.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This converts the primitive dyadic law into the two uniform tail functionals used on both sides of the frontier. \textit{Gap.} KhanTamer2010Irregular gives tail-rate intuition but not this class-uniform dyadic-shell equivalence. \textit{Consumer.} The clipped-score upper bound and every shell-localized lower family use these uniform comparisons.

\begin{theorem}[thm:clipped-absolute-upper]\label{thm:clipped-absolute-upper}
For every admissible radius triple \((r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})\in[0,\bar r]^3\), let \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\) denote the broader class obtained from \texttt{def:public-weak-overlap-class} by retaining all of its member conditions and deleting exactly \texttt{ass:lower-shell-richness} and \texttt{ass:upper-shell-richness}.  The rule \(\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^{\star}}\) from \texttt{def:clipped-score} and \texttt{def:oracle-threshold} belongs to \(\mathcal T_n\).  There is a constant \(C<\infty\), depending only on the retained fixed class constants, including \(M\) and \(\bar r\), such that
\[
\sup_{(P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n}
E_P\!\left|\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^{\star}}-\tau(P)\right|
\leq C\rho_n.
\]
For every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), the same estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{(P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n}
P\!\left\{\left|\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^{\star}}-\tau(P)\right|
>C\rho_n/\delta\right\}\leq\delta.
\]
Since \(\mathcal M_n\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\), both risk conclusions remain valid with \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\) replaced by the reservoir-rich class \(\mathcal M_n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\) be the broader class in the statement.
The causal target is the frozen identified functional
\(\tau(P)=E_P[g_1(X)-g_0(X)]\); the statistic below is a sample map and is not
a definition of that target.  Its ATE interpretation follows from
\texttt{ass:consistency} and \texttt{ass:ignorability}.

Apply \texttt{lem:finite-threshold-clipped-bound} with \(N=n\).  For every
\(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\),
\[
\sup_{(P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n}
E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda,n}-\tau(P)|
\leq C_{\mathrm{FT}}U_n(\lambda).                                  \tag{14}
\]
It remains only to evaluate the explicit envelope at the frozen oracle
threshold.  Put
\[
b_o=n^{-1/(\kappa+1)},\qquad
b_e=r_{en}^{2/(\kappa+2)},\qquad
b_g=(nr_{1n}^2)^{-1},
\]
with \(b_g=+\infty\) when \(r_{1n}=0\), and let \(L\) be the active maximum
in \texttt{def:oracle-threshold}.  Also abbreviate
\[
S_o=r_{1n}n^{-\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]},\qquad
S_e=r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)},\qquad
S_0=r_{0n}r_{en}.
\]
Then \(\rho_n=Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})+S_o+S_e+S_0\).

Suppose first that \(L<\lambda_0\), so
\(\lambda_n^\star=L\).  Since \(L\geq b_o\),
\[
\frac{r_{1n}}{\sqrt{nL}}
\leq r_{1n}L^{\kappa/2}.                                           \tag{15}
\]
Since \(L\geq b_e\), with the assertion trivial when \(r_{en}=0\),
\[
\frac{r_{en}}{\sqrt nL^{3/2}}
\leq\frac{L^{(\kappa-1)/2}}{\sqrt n}
\leq Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n}),
\qquad
\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}L\leq S_e.                                     \tag{16}
\]
For the second inequality in the first part of (16), if \(0<\kappa<1\),
use \(L\geq b_g\) to obtain
\(n^{-1/2}L^{-(1-\kappa)/2}
\leq n^{-\kappa/2}r_{1n}^{1-\kappa}\); if \(\kappa\geq1\), use
\(L<\lambda_0<1\).  The same comparison with \(b_g\) gives
\[
\frac{v_\kappa(L)}{\sqrt n}\leq Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})                 \tag{17}
\]
for \(0<\kappa\leq1\).  At \(\kappa=1\), indeed,
\(\log(\lambda_0/L)\leq\log_+(nr_{1n}^2)\).  For \(\kappa>1\),
(17) is equality because both sides equal \(n^{-1/2}\).

Taking the positive \(\kappa/2\)-power of the active maximum and bounding a
maximum by a sum yields
\[
r_{1n}L^{\kappa/2}
\leq S_o+S_e+Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n}).                                    \tag{18}
\]
When \(0<\kappa<1\), the contribution of \(b_g\) in (18) is exactly
\(n^{-\kappa/2}r_{1n}^{1-\kappa}\); when \(\kappa=1\), it is
\(n^{-1/2}\); and when \(\kappa>1\), \(b_g\) is not active.  Combining
(15)--(18) term by term in \(U_n(L)\) gives the explicit non-cap bound
\[
U_n(L)\leq4Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})+2S_o+3S_e+S_0
\leq4\rho_n.                                                       \tag{19}
\]

Now suppose the cap binds, so \(L\geq\lambda_0\) and
\(\lambda_n^\star=\lambda_0\).  At least one active component is at least
\(\lambda_0\).  If \(b_o\geq\lambda_0\), then the fourth term of
\(U_n(\lambda_0)\) is at most \(S_o\).  If
\(b_e\geq\lambda_0\), that term is at most \(S_e\).  In either case,
using \(Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})\geq n^{-1/2}\), both radius caps, and
\[
\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}{\lambda_0}
\leq\lambda_0^{-1}\bar r^{2/(\kappa+2)}S_e,
\]
we obtain directly from the six terms of \(U_n\)
\[
U_n(\lambda_0)\leq C_{\mathrm{oe}}\rho_n.                           \tag{20}
\]
If \(0<\kappa\leq1\) and \(b_g\geq\lambda_0\), then
\(r_{1n}\leq(n\lambda_0)^{-1/2}\), including the case \(r_{1n}=0\).
Consequently
\[
\begin{gathered}
\frac{r_{1n}}{\sqrt{n\lambda_0}}
\leq\lambda_0^{-1}n^{-1/2},\qquad
r_{1n}\lambda_0^{\kappa/2}
\leq\lambda_0^{-(1-\kappa)/2}n^{-1/2},\\
\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}{\lambda_0}
\leq\bar r\lambda_0^{-3/2}n^{-1/2}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Together with the first and third stochastic terms and \(S_0\), these
inequalities give
\[
U_n(\lambda_0)\leq C_{\mathrm g}\rho_n.                            \tag{21}
\]
The cases (20)--(21) exhaust the active maximum.  Equations (19)--(21) and the
definition of \(C_{\mathrm{opt}}\) prove the fully explicit optimization
inequality
\[
U_n(\lambda_n^\star)\leq C_{\mathrm{opt}}\rho_n.                    \tag{22}
\]

Taking \(\lambda=\lambda_n^\star\) in (14), using (22), and recalling
\(C_\star=C_{\mathrm{FT}}C_{\mathrm{opt}}\), gives
\[
\sup_{(P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n}
E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^\star}-\tau(P)|
\leq C_\star\rho_n.                                               \tag{23}
\]
Thus the unnamed constant \(C\) in the theorem statement may be taken to be
the computable algebraic constant \(C_\star\) from
\texttt{def:finite-threshold-envelope}.  The statistic is measurable in the
observed sample, public functions, and certified radii, so
\texttt{def:estimator-class} gives
\(\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^\star}\in\mathcal T_n\).

For \(W=|\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^\star}-\tau(P)|\), Markov's inequality and
(23) yield, for every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\),
\[
P\{W>C_\star\rho_n/\delta\}
\leq\frac{\delta E_PW}{C_\star\rho_n}\leq\delta.                   \tag{24}
\]
Finally, \(\mathcal M_n\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\) because the former
adds exactly the two richness conditions and changes no retained member
condition.  Restricting the suprema in (23)--(24) proves both conclusions on
\(\mathcal M_n\).  Neither richness condition was used for the upper bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exported finite-threshold lemma proves a uniform all-block inequality with explicit \(C_{\mathrm{FT}}\), and the theorem's cap analysis supplies explicit \(C_{\mathrm{opt}}\), so \(C_\star\) is a computable upper-risk witness on the broader shell class. \textit{Gap.} Existing weak-overlap asymptotic results do not provide this finite-risk public-function envelope with one constant valid for every averaging size and threshold. \textit{Consumer.} The full-sample estimator, five-block median, and confidence interval can now use the same declared and evaluable constant chain.

\begin{lemma}[lem:median-risk-upgrade]\label{lem:median-risk-upgrade}
The corrected median rule \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal T_n\). If \(n\ge5\) and each of its five independent-block copies of \(\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^{\star}}\) has mean absolute error at most \(K\rho_n\), then \(P\{|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)|>u\}\le\min\{1,10(K\rho_n/u)^3\}\) and \(\left\{E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P))^2\right]\right\}^{1/2}\le\sqrt{21}\,K\rho_n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If \(n\geq 5\), the five blocks in \texttt{def:median-estimator} are nonempty and are generated by disjoint coordinate sets.  By \texttt{ass:iid}, their sample vectors are independent.  Each block statistic is measurable in its block sample and in the deterministic public inputs by \texttt{def:clipped-score}; moreover, the median of five real numbers is a Borel function of those numbers.  If \(1\leq n<5\), \texttt{def:median-estimator} instead uses the measurable full-sample clipped statistic.  Thus \texttt{def:estimator-class} gives
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}\in\mathcal T_n
\]
in both cases.

Suppose now that \(n\geq5\), and, for \(1\leq j\leq5\), put
\[
Z_j=\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^{\star}}^{(j)}-\tau(P),
\qquad a=K\rho_n.
\]
The premise is \(E_P|Z_j|\leq a\) for every \(j\).  For every \(u>0\), Markov's inequality, in its elementary form
\[
E_P|Z_j|\geq uP\{|Z_j|>u\},
\]
therefore gives \(P\{|Z_j|>u\}\leq a/u\).  If
\(\left|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)\right|>u\), at least three of the five values lie at distance greater than \(u\) from \(\tau(P)\).  Indeed, if at least three values belonged to the closed interval \([\tau(P)-u,\tau(P)+u]\), their median would belong to that interval.  Hence, using block independence and taking the union over the \({5\choose3}=10\) triples,
\[
\begin{split}
P\!\left\{\left|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)\right|>u\right\}
&\leq \sum_{1\leq j_1<j_2<j_3\leq5}
P\!\left(\bigcap_{r=1}^3\{|Z_{j_r}|>u\}\right)\\
&\leq 10(a/u)^3.
\end{split}
\]
Combining this inequality with the trivial probability bound by one proves
\[
P\!\left\{\left|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)\right|>u\right\}
\leq\min\{1,10(K\rho_n/u)^3\}.
\]

Let \(W=|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)|\).  If \(a=0\), the premise implies \(Z_j=0\) almost surely for every \(j\), so both conclusions are immediate.  Suppose \(a>0\).  Tonelli's theorem applied to the nonnegative identity \(W^2=\int_0^W2u\,du\), followed by the preceding tail bound, gives
\[
\begin{split}
E_P\!\left[W^2\right]
&=\int_0^\infty 2uP(W>u)\,du\\
&\leq\int_0^a2u\,du+
\int_a^\infty20a^3u^{-2}\,du
=21a^2.
\end{split}
\]
The right-hand side is finite, so the equality and all integrations are valid also as extended nonnegative integrals.  Taking square roots and substituting \(a=K\rho_n\) yields
\[
\left\{E_P\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P))^2\right]\right\}^{1/2}
\leq\sqrt{21}\,K\rho_n,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:four-channel-converse]\label{thm:four-channel-converse}
For every admissible radius triple \( (r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})\in[0,\bar r]^3 \) for which \(\mathcal M_n\) is nonempty, the handle \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{lb}}\) yields constants \(c>0\) and \(\delta_0\in(0,1/4)\), uniform in \(n\), such that every rule \(T\in\mathcal T_n\), including every rule using \(O^{(n)}\), \(m_0\), \(m_1\), \(p\), and the certified radii, obeys \(\sup_{(P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\mathcal M_n}P\{|T(O^{(n)},m_0,m_1,p,r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})-\tau(P)|\ge c\rho_n\}\ge\delta_0\). Consequently \(\mathfrak R_n^{(1)}\ge c\delta_0\rho_n\) and \(\mathfrak R_n^{(2)}\ge c\sqrt{\delta_0}\rho_n\). Every fixed-sign law used by the fuzzy mixtures, rather than merely the mixtures themselves, belongs to \(\mathcal M_n\). Each of the four subexperiment families satisfies \(E_P[(g_1(X)-g_0(X))^2]\le M\) by regression-amplitude calibration within the applicable nuisance radius or a sufficiently small fixed constant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(L=1-\eta_+\).  Nonemptiness of \(\mathcal M_n\),
\texttt{ass:uniform-shell} at \(u=\lambda_0\), and
\texttt{ass:right-overlap} imply
\[
L>{\lambda_0\over2}.                                      \tag{21}
\]
Indeed, if \(L\leq\lambda_0/2\), the shell
\(\{\lambda_0/2<e(X)\leq\lambda_0\}\) is empty, contrary to its lower bound
\(c_-\lambda_0^\kappa>0\).  Positivity and the lower shell bound also imply
that \(\mathcal X\) is infinite: finitely many positive propensity values have
a positive minimum, leaving all sufficiently small shells empty.  Every
infinite standard Borel space contains a countably infinite set of distinct
points, and its singletons are measurable.  Thus the countable atomic support
specified by \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{lb}}\) is measurable.

Set \(a=\min\{L,\lambda_0\}\) and \(q_\ell=a2^{-\ell}\).  By (21),
\(\lambda_0/2<a\leq\lambda_0\).  Put background mass
\(b_0=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa\) at \(q_0\), and for \(\ell\geq1\) put
\[
b_\ell=c_-q_{\ell-1}^\kappa=2^\kappa c_-q_\ell^\kappa.     \tag{22}
\]
For \(0<u<a\), there is a unique \(\ell\geq1\) with
\(q_\ell\leq u<q_{\ell-1}\), and exactly \(q_\ell\) is in \((u/2,u]\).
Hence
\[
c_-u^\kappa<b_\ell\leq2^\kappa c_-u^\kappa.               \tag{23}
\]
At a dyadic endpoint the adjacent-index convention gives the same weak
inequalities.  For \(a\leq u\leq\lambda_0\), the point \(q_0=a\) is in the
shell because \(u<2a\), and
\[
c_-u^\kappa\leq b_0=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa
<2^\kappa c_-a^\kappa.                                    \tag{24}
\]
Thus the background supplies every lower shell bound and has upper coefficient
\(2^\kappa c_-\).  Its total mass is
\[
B_{\mathrm{bg}}
=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa+{c_-a^\kappa\over1-2^{-\kappa}}
\leq {1-2^{-\kappa}\over4}+{1\over4}< {1\over2},           \tag{25}
\]
by \texttt{ass:lower-shell-richness}.

Let \(\Delta_c=c_+-2^\kappa c_-\).  The two richness inequalities imply
\(\Delta_c>0\).  Fix \(J\geq3\), \(\zeta=7/10\),
\(s_0=\zeta q_J\), \(\gamma=21/40\), and choose \(A\) below the three positive
caps in \texttt{def:lower-bound-handle}.  Put
\[
t_j=s_02^{-j},\quad\quad w_j=A(1-2^{-\kappa})t_j^\kappa.
\]
For each finite \(N\), give every signal point \(x_{j,k}\) and companion
\(\widetilde x_{j,k}\) mass \(w_j/N\).  The signal mass and companion mass are
separately
\[
\sum_{j\geq0}w_j
=A(1-2^{-\kappa})s_0^\kappa\sum_{j\geq0}2^{-j\kappa}
=As_0^\kappa.                                            \tag{26}
\]
Reserve their combined mass by \(R_{J+j}=2w_j\).  With
\(d_\ell=c_+q_\ell^\kappa-b_\ell-R_\ell\), one has
\(d_\ell\geq0\): this is immediate below level \(J\), including \(\ell=0\)
from the richness bounds, and for \(\ell\geq J\) it follows from
\[
b_\ell+R_\ell
=\{2^\kappa c_-+2A(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa\}q_\ell^\kappa
\leq c_+q_\ell^\kappa.                                    \tag{27}
\]

Let \(S=c_+a^\kappa/(1-2^{-\kappa})\).  We claim \(S\geq1\).  If
\(a=L\leq\lambda_0\), take a member of the nonempty class.  Positivity and
right overlap make the disjoint shells
\(\{q_{\ell+1}<e(X)\leq q_\ell\}\), \(\ell\geq0\), a partition up to null
sets, so their upper shell bounds imply
\[
1\leq\sum_{\ell\geq0}c_+q_\ell^\kappa=S.                 \tag{28}
\]
If \(a=\lambda_0<L\), \texttt{ass:upper-shell-richness} gives
\[
S\geq{2^{\kappa-1}\over1-2^{-\kappa}}\geq1.               \tag{29}
\]
Consequently,
\[
D_{\mathrm{cap}}:=\sum_{\ell\geq0}d_\ell
=S-B_{\mathrm{bg}}-2As_0^\kappa
\geq1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}-2As_0^\kappa=:H>0.                 \tag{30}
\]
Put \(\pi_\ell=Hd_\ell/D_{\mathrm{cap}}\) at a distinct completion point of
propensity \(q_\ell\).  Then \(0\leq\pi_\ell\leq d_\ell\),
\(\sum_\ell\pi_\ell=H\), and the exact total-mass identity is
\[
\sum_{\ell\geq0}b_\ell+2\sum_{j\geq0}w_j+
\sum_{\ell\geq0}\pi_\ell
=B_{\mathrm{bg}}+2As_0^\kappa+H=1.                       \tag{31}
\]

For the shell upper bound, first put the active reserved mass back at its
virtual endpoint.  Its total endpoint mass is
\[
M_\ell=b_\ell+\pi_\ell+R_\ell\leq c_+q_\ell^\kappa.       \tag{32}
\]
The actual law relocates \(R_\ell\) into \([\gamma q_\ell,q_\ell)\).  This
contains unchanged active propensities because \(t_j=\zeta q_{J+j}\), treated
fuzzy ratios in \([4/5,4/3]\), and control fuzzy ratios in \([3/4,5/4]\), since
\[
{4\over5}\zeta>{21\over40},\quad {4\over3}\zeta<1,
\quad\quad {3\over4}\zeta={21\over40},\quad {5\over4}\zeta<1. \tag{33}
\]
The signal point receives one tilt and its companion the opposite tilt, so the
unordered pair of relocated propensities is independent of the fixed sign and
of \(\theta\).

Fix \(0<u<a\) and the unique \(\ell\geq1\) with
\(q_\ell\leq u<q_{\ell-1}\).  Relocation from \(q_\ell\) adds no more than
the mass removed there; all lower endpoints remain at or below \(u/2\).  Only
relocation from \(q_{\ell-1}\) can add further mass, and only when
\(u\geq2\gamma q_\ell\).  Thus
\[
P\{u/2<e(X)\leq u\}
\leq M_\ell+R_{\ell-1}\mathbf 1\{u\geq2\gamma q_\ell\}.   \tag{34}
\]
When the indicator is one, the third cap on \(A\) gives
\[
R_{\ell-1}
\leq2^{\kappa+1}A(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa q_\ell^\kappa
\leq c_+\{(2\gamma)^\kappa-1\}q_\ell^\kappa
\leq c_+(u^\kappa-q_\ell^\kappa).                         \tag{35}
\]
Equations (32)--(35) prove the upper cap.  When \(a\leq u\leq\lambda_0\),
all active propensities are below \(q_J<a/2\), and the endpoint capacity at
\(q_0=a\) gives the same cap.  Because background atoms are never relocated,
(23)--(24) give the lower cap for every fixed-sign law.  Hence
\[
c_-u^\kappa\leq P\{u/2<e(X)\leq u\}\leq c_+u^\kappa,
\quad\quad0<u\leq\lambda_0.                                  \tag{36}
\]

We now isolate the signal reservoir
\(S_N=\bigcup_{j\geq0}\{x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\}\); the companion reservoir
is \(C_N=\bigcup_{j\geq0}\{\widetilde x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\}\).
For \(0<v\leq s_0\), let \(j(v)=\min\{j:t_j\leq v\}\).  Then
\(v/2<t_{j(v)}\leq v\), and exact geometric summation gives
\[
P_X(S_N\cap\{e_0\leq v\})
=\sum_{j\geq j(v)}w_j=A t_{j(v)}^\kappa,
\quad A2^{-\kappa}v^\kappa<P_X(S_N\cap\{e_0\leq v\})\leq Av^\kappa, \tag{37}
\]
and
\[
E_P[e_0\mathbf 1\{X\in S_N,e_0\leq v\}]
={A(1-2^{-\kappa})\over1-2^{-(\kappa+1)}}
t_{j(v)}^{\kappa+1}\asymp v^{\kappa+1}.                    \tag{38}
\]
For every \(0<s\leq s_0/2\), the half-open interval \((s,2s]\) contains
exactly one active dyadic level.  If \(s=t_j\), that level is
\(t_{j-1}=2s\); otherwise the unique \(j\) with
\(t_j<2s<t_{j-1}\) has \(t_j\in(s,2s)\).  Therefore
\[
B_s^S:=S_N\cap\{s<e_0\leq2s\}=\{x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\},
\quad m:=P_X(B_s^S)=w_j\asymp s^\kappa,                    \tag{39}
\]
and its separately defined companion set
\(C_s^S=\{\widetilde x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\}\) also has mass \(m\).
In particular, for fixed \(j_c\geq1\) and \(s_c=t_{j_c+1}\),
\[
B_c^S:=S_N\cap\{s_c<e_0\leq2s_c\}
=\{x_{j_c,k}:1\leq k\leq N\},\quad
m_*:=P_X(B_c^S)=w_{j_c}>0.                              \tag{40}
\]
The companion set \(C_c^S\) has the same mass.  Background and completion
atoms, including those at \(q_{J+j_c+1}\) in the numerical band in (40), are
outside \(S_N\), remain unperturbed, and are absent from (37)--(40).

We use the following elementary testing reduction.  If sample laws \(Q_+\) and
\(Q_-\) have targets separated by at least \(2d\), the two events of estimation
within \(d\) of the respective targets are disjoint.  The variational
definition of total variation gives
\[
Q_+\{|T-\tau_+|\geq d\}+Q_-\{|T-\tau_-|\geq d\}
\geq1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_+,Q_-).                         \tag{41}
\]
For prior mixtures, if their sample total variation is at most \(\Gamma\), and
each prior target is within \(d/4\) of centers separated by \(2d\) except with
prior probability \(\varepsilon\), testing the nearer center gives error at
least \((1-\Gamma-2\varepsilon)/2\) for one mixture.  Since mixture
probability is an average of fixed-law probabilities, a fixed law in its
support inherits the bound.

For every family, generate \(D\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e(X))\),
generate \((Y_0,Y_1)\) conditionally independently of \(D\) given \(X\), set
\(Y=DY_1+(1-D)Y_0\), and take \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) independently.  This verifies
\texttt{ass:ignorability}, \texttt{ass:consistency}, and \texttt{ass:iid}.
For Gaussian treated mean-shift families use centered treated residual noise
with a fixed variance \(0<\sigma^2\leq\sigma_1^2\); use zero residual noise in
all other arms and families.  Choose a fixed \(c_A>0\), depending only on class
constants, so small that \(c_A\bar r\leq\sqrt M/4\), every displayed nuisance
radius inequality below holds, and \(c_A\leq\min\{1/4,s_c\}\).  Every
treatment-effect signal then has squared \(L^2(P_X)\) norm at most \(M\), and
the two residual-variance assumptions hold.  No separate moment bound on
\(g_0\), \(g_1\), or \(Y\) is used.  Equations (32)--(36) give positivity and
the shell law; (33) and \(q_J<L\) give right overlap.

For the parametric obstruction, split the top background mass \(b_0\) between
two distinct points of the same propensity.  Give them fixed treatment effects
\(+c_A\) and \(-c_A\), set public nuisances equal to the true nuisances, and
under \(\theta\in\{-1,1\}\) assign their masses
\(b_0/2+\theta\epsilon_n\) and \(b_0/2-\theta\epsilon_n\), where
\(\epsilon_n=c_pn^{-1/2}\) and \(0<c_p<b_0/4\).  All propensity shells are
unchanged.  The one-observation categorical divergence is
\[
2\epsilon_n\log{b_0/2+\epsilon_n\over b_0/2-\epsilon_n}
\leq{16\epsilon_n^2\over b_0},                            \tag{42}
\]
so product divergence is at most \(16c_p^2/b_0\).  If \(f=dQ_+/dQ_-\), the
pointwise inequality \(f\log f-f+1\geq(\sqrt f-1)^2\), integration, and
Cauchy--Schwarz give
\(\operatorname{TV}(Q_+,Q_-)^2\leq\operatorname{KL}(Q_+,Q_-)\).
Decreasing \(c_p\) makes product total variation at most \(1/4\).  The targets
differ by \(4c_Ac_pn^{-1/2}\), so (41) proves the root-\(n\) obstruction.

For \(0<\kappa<1\) and \(r_{1n}>0\), first suppose

\(E[e_0\mathbf 1_{A_s^S}]\leq Csm_s\), and direct Gaussian integration gives

\operatorname{KL}(P_+^n,P_-^n)

If \(s>s_0\), this scale is a class-constant multiple of at most

At \(\kappa=1\), first assume \(r_{1n}>0\) and set

Because \(s_02^{-J_s}\geq s>s_02^{-(J_s+1)}\), the first comparison is exact

\operatorname{KL}(P_+^n,P_-^n)

\quad
2E[f]\asymp n^{-1/2}\sqrt{1+\log_+(nr_{1n}^2)}.            \tag{48}

If \(s>s_0\), the logarithm is bounded and (42) applies.  If \(r_{1n}=0\),

For the empty-treated-shell channel, choose a fixed small \(c_s\leq s_0\) and

The component is zero when \(r_{1n}=0\).

hm\asymp r_{1n}s^{\kappa/2}
\asymp r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}.                   \tag{59}

If \(r_{en}=0\) or \(r_{1n}=0\), this component is zero.

\]

\]

r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)},\quad\quad r_{0n}r_{en}.       \tag{69}

\geq c\rho_n\sup_{\mathcal M_n}
P\{|T-\tau(P)|\geq c\rho_n\}
\geq c\delta_0\rho_n,

\quad\quad

\begin{proposition}[prop:polynomial-phase-diagram]\label{prop:polynomial-phase-diagram}
If \(r_{0n}=n^{-a_0}\), \(r_{1n}=n^{-a_1}\), and \(r_{en}=n^{-a_e}\) with \(a_0,a_1,a_e\ge0\), then the bounded-radius regime holds for \(\bar r\ge1\), and \(\rho_n=n^{-\beta_{\kappa}}\) up to the boundary logarithm specified below. If \(B\le a_1\), weak overlap causes no polynomial deterioration. If \(B>a_1\), then \(\beta_{\kappa}<B\) holds exactly when \(0<\kappa<\kappa_{\dagger}\). At \(\kappa=1\), the additional factor is \(\sqrt{1+\log_+(n r_{1n}^2)}\), and it changes the leading root-\(n\) rate only when \(a_1<1/2\). The empty-shell exponent \(a_1+\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]\) tends to \(a_1+1/2\), so its surviving fast-tail term never worsens the benchmark \(B\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the polynomial-radius specialization in the statement and the symbol declarations, \(n\in\mathbb N\) and \(a_0,a_1,a_e\geq 0\).  Hence
\[
0<r_{jn}=n^{-a_j}\leq 1\leq \bar r,
\qquad j\in\{0,1,e\},
\]
so the three radius bounds required by the bounded-radius regime hold.

We first extract the polynomial order of every summand in \(\rho_n\).  Define, only within this proof,
\[
t_\kappa:=\frac{\kappa}{2}+(1-\kappa)a_1,
\qquad
u_\kappa:=a_1+\frac{\kappa}{2(\kappa+1)},
\qquad
w_\kappa:=a_1+\frac{\kappa a_e}{\kappa+2},
\qquad
c:=a_0+a_e.
\]
The definition of \(Q_\kappa\) and the equality \(r_{1n}=n^{-a_1}\) give, when \(0<\kappa<1\),
\[
Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})
=n^{-1/2}\vee n^{-\kappa/2}(n^{-a_1})^{1-\kappa}
=n^{-1/2}\vee n^{-t_\kappa}.
\]
The remaining three terms in the definition of \(\rho_n\) satisfy
\[
r_{1n}n^{-\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]}=n^{-u_\kappa},
\qquad
r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}=n^{-w_\kappa},
\qquad
r_{0n}r_{en}=n^{-c}.
\]
For \(n\geq1\), the largest of finitely many powers \(n^{-s}\) is the power having the smallest exponent.  Therefore Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}phase\mbox{-}exponent}\) gives, for \(0<\kappa<1\),
\[
\beta_\kappa=\min\{1/2,t_\kappa,u_\kappa,w_\kappa,c\}.
\]
For \(\kappa>1\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}tail\mbox{-}noise\mbox{-}rate}\) gives \(Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})=n^{-1/2}\), and hence
\[
\beta_\kappa=\min\{1/2,u_\kappa,w_\kappa,c\}.
\]
In either case, the summand whose exponent equals \(\beta_\kappa\) supplies the lower bound, while each of the four summands in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}sharp\mbox{-}rate}\) is at most \(n^{-\beta_\kappa}\).  Thus
\[
n^{-\beta_\kappa}\leq \rho_n\leq 4n^{-\beta_\kappa},
\qquad \kappa\neq1.
\]

At \(\kappa=1\), put
\[
L_n:=\sqrt{1+\log_+(nr_{1n}^2)}.
\]
Then \(Q_1(n,r_{1n})=n^{-1/2}L_n\), \(L_n\geq1\), and
\[
\beta_1=\min\{1/2,a_1+1/4,a_1+a_e/3,c\}.
\]
The same comparison, now using \(L_n\geq1\), yields
\[
n^{-\beta_1}\leq \rho_n\leq4L_n n^{-\beta_1}.
\]
This proves the asserted polynomial order, including the stated possible boundary logarithm.

It remains to characterize strict deterioration relative to \(B\).  First suppose \(B\leq a_1\).  For \(0<\kappa<1\),
\[
t_\kappa=(1-\kappa)a_1+\kappa/2\geq B,
\]
because both \(a_1\) and \(1/2\) are at least \(B\).  For every \(\kappa>0\),
\[
u_\kappa\geq a_1\geq B,
\qquad
w_\kappa\geq a_1\geq B.
\]
Also \(1/2\geq B\) and \(c=a_0+a_e\geq B\) by the definition
\(B=\min\{1/2,a_1+a_e,a_0+a_e\}\).  Thus every entry defining \(\beta_\kappa\) is at least \(B\).  Conversely, if the minimum defining \(B\) is attained by \(1/2\) or by \(c\), that same entry occurs in \(\beta_\kappa\).  In the remaining case \(B=a_1+a_e\); the inequalities \(B\leq a_1\) and \(a_e\geq0\) then force \(a_e=0\) and \(B=a_1=w_\kappa\).  Hence
\[
\beta_\kappa=B
\]
whenever \(B\leq a_1\), proving that there is no polynomial deterioration in this case.

Now suppose \(B>a_1\), and set \(x:=B-a_1>0\) as in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}deterioration\mbox{-}threshold}\).  Since \(B\leq1/2\) and \(B\leq a_1+a_e\),
\[
0<x\leq 1/2-a_1,
\qquad
0<x\leq a_e.
\]
In particular, \(a_1<1/2\), the first denominator below is positive, and \(a_e-x\geq0\).  Define the three extended-real cutoffs
\[
A_1:=\frac{x}{1/2-a_1},
\qquad
A_2:=\frac{2x}{1-2x},
\qquad
A_3:=\frac{2x}{a_e-x},
\]
with a zero denominator interpreted as \(+\infty\), exactly as in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}deterioration\mbox{-}threshold}\).  Notice that \(A_1\leq1\).

The tail-noise exponent is present only for \(0<\kappa<1\), and direct subtraction gives
\[
t_\kappa<B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa(1/2-a_1)<x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa<A_1.
\]
Because \(A_1\leq1\), the condition \(0<\kappa<A_1\) automatically lies in the range where this entry is present.  For the empty-shell exponent, if \(x<1/2\), multiplication by the positive number \(2(\kappa+1)\) gives
\[
u_\kappa<B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\kappa}{2(\kappa+1)}<x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa(1-2x)<2x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa<A_2.
\]
If \(x=1/2\), the strict inequality \(\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]<x\) holds for every finite \(\kappa>0\), and the convention \(A_2=+\infty\) gives the same equivalence.  Finally, if \(a_e>x\),
\[
w_\kappa<B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\kappa a_e}{\kappa+2}<x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa(a_e-x)<2x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa<A_3.
\]
If \(a_e=x\), then \(\kappa a_e/(\kappa+2)<a_e=x\) for every finite \(\kappa>0\), again agreeing with \(A_3=+\infty\).

The two entries \(1/2\) and \(c\) are at least \(B\) by definition.  Consequently, the preceding three displays exhaust all ways in which an entry of \(\beta_\kappa\) can be strictly smaller than \(B\).  Taking their union,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_\kappa<B
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\kappa<A_1)\ \text{or}\ (\kappa<A_2)\ \text{or}\ (\kappa<A_3)\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0<\kappa<\max\{A_1,A_2,A_3\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0<\kappa<\kappa_\dagger,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equivalence is Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}deterioration\mbox{-}threshold}\).  The strict inequalities also show that a finite boundary \(\kappa=\kappa_\dagger\) is not part of the deterioration region.

For completeness, the logarithmic qualification at \(\kappa=1\) is exact.  Since \(r_{1n}=n^{-a_1}\),
\[
L_n=\sqrt{1+\log_+(n^{1-2a_1})}.
\]
If \(a_1\geq1/2\), then \(n^{1-2a_1}\leq1\) and \(L_n=1\).  If \(a_1<1/2\), then \(L_n\) is of order \(\sqrt{\log n}\).  When \(\beta_1<1/2\), writing \(d:=1/2-\beta_1>0\) shows
\[
\frac{n^{-1/2}L_n}{n^{-\beta_1}}=n^{-d}L_n\longrightarrow0;
\]
indeed, after setting \(s=\log n\), the bound \(e^{ds}\geq (ds)^2/2\) proves the limit.  Hence the logarithm does not change the leading rate in that case.  When \(\beta_1=1/2\), all three non-noise terms are at most \(n^{-1/2}\), so
\[
n^{-1/2}L_n\leq\rho_n\leq4n^{-1/2}L_n.
\]
Thus the boundary factor changes a leading root-\(n\) rate exactly when \(a_1<1/2\) and \(\beta_1=1/2\), which in particular proves the necessary condition stated in the proposition.

Finally,
\[
\frac{d}{d\kappa}u_\kappa=\frac{1}{2(\kappa+1)^2}>0,
\qquad
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}u_\kappa=a_1+1/2\geq1/2\geq B.
\]
Therefore the empty-shell channel has no surviving deterioration in the fast-tail limit; any deterioration at finite \(\kappa\) is already exactly accounted for by the cutoff \(A_2\) above.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:regular-benchmark-reduction]\label{prop:regular-benchmark-reduction}
For every fixed \(\kappa>0\), \(\mathcal M_n\cap\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\varnothing\): the uniform-shell condition places positive mass below every positive propensity threshold, whereas the uniform-overlap comparator imposes \(e(X)\ge\eta_-\) almost surely. For fixed \(n\) and \((r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})\in[0,\bar r]^3\),
\[
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\rho_n=(1+r_{1n})n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}+r_{0n}r_{en}\asymp n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}+r_{0n}r_{en},
\]
uniformly under \(r_{1n}\le\bar r\). Writing the final rate form as \(C_n=n^{-1/2}+r_{0n}r_{en}+r_{1n}r_{en}\), the constant-weight Jin--Syrgkanis lower-bound template after its stated interpretive radius substitutions is \(J_n=n^{-1/2}+\max\{r_{0n},r_{1n}\}r_{en}\), and \(J_n\le C_n\le2J_n\). Separately, the Bonvini--Kennedy--Dukes--Balakrishnan one-functional template becomes only the treated-side form \(L_{1,n}=n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}\le C_n\) under the purely interpretive substitution \(\varepsilon_n=r_{1n}\), \(\delta_n=r_{en}\); it supplies no control-side term. These are only algebraic source-class sanity comparisons: they prove neither a minimax equivalence for \(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) nor any class-inclusion transfer.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\kappa>0\).  Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\((P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\mathcal M_n\cap\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), and define
\[
u_\star=\frac12\min\{\lambda_0,\eta_-\}.
\]
The declared ranges \(\lambda_0\in(0,1)\) and \(\eta_-\in(0,1)\) imply
\(0<u_\star\le\lambda_0\) and \(u_\star<\eta_-\).  Membership in
\(\mathcal M_n\), by \texttt{def:public-weak-overlap-class}, entails
\texttt{ass:uniform-shell}.  Its lower shell inequality, applied at
\(u=u_\star\), gives
\[
P\!\left\{\frac{u_\star}{2}<e(X)\le u_\star\right\}
\ge c_-u_\star^\kappa>0,
\]
where strict positivity follows from \(c_->0\), \(u_\star>0\), and
\(\kappa>0\).  Membership in \(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), by
\texttt{def:regular-overlap-class}, entails
\texttt{ass:uniform-overlap-comparator}, and therefore
\[
P\{e(X)\ge\eta_-\}=1.
\]
Since \(u_\star<\eta_-\), however,
\[
\left\{\frac{u_\star}{2}<e(X)\le u_\star\right\}
\subseteq\{e(X)<\eta_-\},
\]
so the same shell has probability zero.  This contradiction proves
\[
\mathcal M_n\cap\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}=\varnothing.
\]

Now fix \(n\in\mathbb N\) and
\((r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})\in[0,\bar r]^3\).  In particular, \(n\ge1\) and
the three radii are nonnegative.  For every \(\kappa>1\),
\texttt{def:tail-noise-rate} yields
\[
Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\frac{\kappa}{2(\kappa+1)}\longrightarrow\frac12,
\qquad
\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+2}\longrightarrow1
\qquad\text{as }\kappa\to\infty.
\]
Because \(n\ge1\), continuity of \(a\mapsto n^{-a}\) gives
\[
r_{1n}n^{-\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]}
\longrightarrow r_{1n}n^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(r_{en}>0\), continuity of \(a\mapsto r_{en}^{a}\) gives
\(r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}\to r_{en}\).  If \(r_{en}=0\), then
\(r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}=0=r_{en}\), since
\(\kappa/(\kappa+2)>0\).  Hence in either case
\[
r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}
\longrightarrow r_{1n}r_{en}.
\]
The term \(r_{0n}r_{en}\) is independent of \(\kappa\).  Substituting these
limits into the finite sum in \texttt{def:sharp-rate} proves
\[
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\rho_n
=(1+r_{1n})n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}+r_{0n}r_{en}.
\]

For the asserted uniform comparison, set
\[
A_n=r_{1n}r_{en}+r_{0n}r_{en}\ge0,
\qquad
B_n=n^{-1/2}+A_n>0.
\]
Since \(0\le r_{1n}\le\bar r\),
\[
B_n
\le (1+r_{1n})n^{-1/2}+A_n
\le (1+\bar r)n^{-1/2}+A_n
\le (1+\bar r)B_n.
\]
The comparison constants \(1\) and \(1+\bar r\) are independent of \(n\)
and of all three radii.  Consequently
\[
(1+r_{1n})n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}+r_{0n}r_{en}
\asymp n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}+r_{0n}r_{en}
\]
uniformly over the declared radius range, and in particular uniformly under
\(r_{1n}\le\bar r\).

It remains to prove the two algebraic source-class comparisons.  Define
\[
C_n=n^{-1/2}+r_{0n}r_{en}+r_{1n}r_{en}.
\]
By \texttt{lem:jin-syrgkanis-regular-benchmark}, the constant-weight
square-root lower-bound template after the stated interpretive substitutions is
\[
J_n=n^{-1/2}+\max\{r_{0n},r_{1n}\}r_{en}.
\]
Nonnegativity of \(r_{0n}\), \(r_{1n}\), and \(r_{en}\) implies
\[
\max\{r_{0n},r_{1n}\}r_{en}
\le (r_{0n}+r_{1n})r_{en}
\le 2\max\{r_{0n},r_{1n}\}r_{en}.
\]
After adding \(n^{-1/2}\) to the first inequality, and using
\(n^{-1/2}\le2n^{-1/2}\) with the second, we obtain
\[
J_n\le C_n\le2J_n.
\]

Finally, \texttt{lem:bonvini-et-al-regular-benchmark} supplies only its
one-functional lower-rate template.  Under the stated treated-side
interpretive substitutions \(\varepsilon_n=r_{1n}\) and
\(\delta_n=r_{en}\), this becomes
\[
L_{1,n}=n^{-1/2}+r_{1n}r_{en}.
\]
Since \(r_{0n}r_{en}\ge0\),
\[
L_{1,n}\le C_n.
\]
No control-side term or sum of two armwise risks has been inferred from that
cited result.  The two cited lemmas retain their distinct source classes and
lower-bound risk conventions; neither asserts a matching upper bound or a joint
two-arm minimax theorem for \(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).  Together with the
proved disjointness of \(\mathcal M_n\) and
\(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\), these calculations are therefore only
algebraic source-class sanity comparisons.  They establish neither minimax
equivalence for \(\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) nor any class-inclusion
transfer.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-threshold-clipped-bound]\label{lem:finite-threshold-clipped-bound}
For every admissible radius triple
\((r_{0n},r_{1n},r_{en})\in[0,\bar r]^3\), let
\(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\) be the class obtained from
\texttt{def:public-weak-overlap-class} by deleting exactly
\texttt{ass:lower-shell-richness} and
\texttt{ass:upper-shell-richness}.  For every
\((P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\), every integer \(N\geq1\),
every i.i.d. observed-law sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_N\), and every
\(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\), the block estimator and envelope from
\texttt{def:finite-threshold-envelope} satisfy the exact finite-threshold
inequality
\[
E_P\left|\widehat\tau_{\lambda,N}-\tau(P)\right|
\leq C_{\mathrm{FT}}U_N(\lambda).
\]
The public functions and all three approximation certificates in this display
remain indexed by \(n\); no membership assertion about any
sample-size-\(N\) model class is made.  Neither richness condition is used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a member of \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\), an integer \(N\geq1\), and
\(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\).  All expectations below are under its observed
marginal.  First derive the two shell bounds required by the score calculation.
Write \(F(t)=P\{e(X)\leq t\}\).  By \texttt{ass:positivity}, the dyadic
shells \((2^{-j-1}t,2^{-j}t]\), \(j\geq0\), partition
\(\{0<e(X)\leq t\}\) up to a null set.  Therefore
\texttt{ass:uniform-shell} gives, for \(0<t\leq\lambda_0\),
\[
F(t)
\leq c_+t^\kappa\sum_{j=0}^\infty2^{-j\kappa}
=\overline c_\kappa t^\kappa.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Layer cake applied to the bounded nonnegative variable
\((e(X)\vee\lambda)^{-1}\), followed by the change of variables
\(s=t^{-1}\), gives
\[
E_P[(e(X)\vee\lambda)^{-1}]
=1+\int_\lambda^1\frac{P\{e(X)<t\}}{t^2}\,dt.                     \tag{2}
\]
On \([\lambda,\lambda_0]\) use (1), and on
\([\lambda_0,1]\) use the probability bound by one.  Evaluating the
resulting elementary integral separately for \(0<\kappa<1\), \(\kappa=1\),
and \(\kappa>1\) yields, with the constants defined in
\texttt{def:finite-threshold-envelope},
\[
E_P[(e(X)\vee\lambda)^{-1}]
\leq H_\kappa v_\kappa(\lambda)^2.                                  \tag{3}
\]
For example, when \(0<\kappa<1\), the right side before the final comparison
is at most
\(\lambda_0^{-1}+\overline c_\kappa\lambda^{\kappa-1}/(1-\kappa)\),
and \(v_\kappa(\lambda)^2=\lambda^{\kappa-1}\geq1\).  When
\(\kappa=1\), it is at most
\(\lambda_0^{-1}+\overline c_1\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)\).  When
\(\kappa>1\), it is at most
\(\lambda_0^{-1}+\overline c_\kappa\lambda_0^{\kappa-1}/(\kappa-1)\).
This proves (3) without either richness assumption.

Put
\[
\Delta=g_1-g_0,\qquad h_a=m_a-g_a,
\qquad \varepsilon_a=Y-g_a(X)\quad\hbox{on }\{D=a\},
\qquad e_\lambda=e\vee\lambda.
\]
The definitions of \(g_a\) give
\(E_P(\varepsilon_a\mid D=a,X)=0\).  Since
\texttt{ass:right-overlap} places \(e(X)\) in
\([0,1-\eta_+]\), projection onto \([0,1-\eta_+/2]\) and the map
\(z\mapsto z\vee\lambda\) are contractions at \(e(X)\).  Hence
\[
|p^\circ-e|\leq|p-e|,
\qquad |q_\lambda-e_\lambda|\leq|p-e|,
\qquad 1-p^\circ\geq\eta_+/2.                                      \tag{4}
\]
All denominators below are positive by \(\lambda>0\),
\texttt{ass:positivity}, and \texttt{ass:right-overlap}.

Substitution in \texttt{def:clipped-score}, followed by adding and subtracting
the true clipped treated denominator and the true control denominator, gives
the pointwise identity
\[
\psi_\lambda-\tau(P)=A+B_1+B_0+C_1+C_0+R_1+R_0,                    \tag{5}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=\Delta-\tau(P)+\frac{D\varepsilon_1}{e_\lambda}
-\frac{(1-D)\varepsilon_0}{1-e},\\
B_1&=h_1\left(1-\frac D{e_\lambda}\right),
&B_0&=-h_0\left(1-\frac{1-D}{1-e}\right),\\
C_1&=D\varepsilon_1\left(\frac1{q_\lambda}-\frac1{e_\lambda}\right),
&C_0&=-(1-D)\varepsilon_0
\left(\frac1{1-p^\circ}-\frac1{1-e}\right),\\
R_1&=Dh_1\left(\frac1{e_\lambda}-\frac1{q_\lambda}\right),
&R_0&=(1-D)h_0
\left(\frac1{1-p^\circ}-\frac1{1-e}\right).
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{6}
\]
Conditional mean zero of the residuals shows
\(E_PA=E_PB_0=E_PC_1=E_PC_0=0\).  The only clipping mean is
\[
E_PB_1
=E_P\!\left[h_1(X)\left\{1-\frac{e(X)}{e_\lambda(X)}\right\}\right].
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, \texttt{ass:public-g1-radius}, and (1),
\[
|E_PB_1|
\leq r_{1n}F(\lambda)^{1/2}
\leq\sqrt{\overline c_\kappa}\,r_{1n}\lambda^{\kappa/2}.          \tag{7}
\]

We next give every second-moment calculation used for the centered empirical
terms.  By Minkowski, \texttt{ass:bounded-treatment-effect-signal}, the two
conditional residual-variance assumptions, \texttt{ass:right-overlap}, and
(3),
\[
\begin{split}
\|A\|_{P,2}
&\leq\sqrt M
+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2E_P[e_\lambda^{-1}]}
+\sqrt{\sigma_0^2/\eta_+}\\
&\leq
\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2H_\kappa}
+\sqrt{\sigma_0^2/\eta_+}\right)v_\kappa(\lambda),
\end{split} \tag{8}
\]
where \(v_\kappa(\lambda)\geq1\).  Conditional on \(X\),
\[
E_P\!\left[\left.h_1^2\left(1-\frac D{e_\lambda}\right)^2
\right|X\right]
\leq h_1^2(1+\lambda^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
E_P\!\left[\left.h_0^2\left(1-\frac{1-D}{1-e}\right)^2
\right|X\right]
=h_0^2\frac e{1-e}\leq h_0^2\eta_+^{-1}.
\]
The public regression certificates and \(\lambda\leq\lambda_0<1\) thus imply
\[
\|B_1\|_{P,2}\leq\sqrt2\,r_{1n}\lambda^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|B_0\|_{P,2}\leq r_{0n}\eta_+^{-1/2}.                            \tag{9}
\]
Using (4), \(e\leq e_\lambda\), the public propensity certificate, and the
conditional residual-variance bounds gives
\[
\begin{split}
E_PC_1^2
&\leq\sigma_1^2E_P\!\left[
\frac{e(p-e)^2}{q_\lambda^2e_\lambda^2}\right]
\leq\sigma_1^2r_{en}^2\lambda^{-3},\\
E_PC_0^2
&\leq\sigma_0^2E_P\!\left[
\frac{(p^\circ-e)^2}{(1-p^\circ)^2(1-e)}\right]
\leq4\sigma_0^2r_{en}^2\eta_+^{-3}.
\end{split} \tag{10}
\]
Finally, conditioning on \(X\), using (4), and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the
two certified \(L^2(P_X)\) errors yields the first-moment remainder bounds
\[
E_P|R_1|\leq\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}\lambda,
\qquad
E_P|R_0|\leq\frac2{\eta_+}r_{0n}r_{en}.                             \tag{11}
\]

For every mean-zero square-integrable random variable \(Z\), independence of
the \(N\) observations and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
E_P\left|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^NZ_i\right|
\leq\left\{E_P\left[\left(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^NZ_i\right)^2\right]\right\}^{1/2}
=\frac{\|Z\|_{P,2}}{\sqrt N}.                                     \tag{12}
\]
Apply (12) to \(A,B_0,C_1,C_0\) and to \(B_1-E_PB_1\), use
\(\|B_1-E_PB_1\|_{P,2}\leq\|B_1\|_{P,2}\), retain (7), and use
\(E_P|N^{-1}\sum_iR_{a,i}|\leq E_P|R_a|\).  Since
\(r_{0n},r_{en}\leq\bar r\) and \(v_\kappa(\lambda)\geq1\),
(8)--(12) give
\[
\begin{split}
E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda,N}-\tau(P)|
\leq{}&K_v\frac{v_\kappa(\lambda)}{\sqrt N}
+\sqrt2\frac{r_{1n}}{\sqrt{N\lambda}}
+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2}\frac{r_{en}}{\sqrt N\lambda^{3/2}}\\
&+\sqrt{\overline c_\kappa}r_{1n}\lambda^{\kappa/2}
+\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}\lambda
+\frac2{\eta_+}r_{0n}r_{en},
\end{split} \tag{13}
\]
where
\[
K_v=\sqrt M+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2H_\kappa}
+\sqrt{\sigma_0^2/\eta_+}
+\bar r\eta_+^{-1/2}
+2\bar r\sqrt{\sigma_0^2}\eta_+^{-3/2}.
\]
By its displayed algebraic definition, \(C_{\mathrm{FT}}\) is at least each
of \(K_v,\sqrt2,\sqrt{\sigma_1^2},
\sqrt{\overline c_\kappa},1,2/\eta_+\).  Comparing (13) term by term with
\(U_N(\lambda)\) proves
\[
E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda,N}-\tau(P)|
\leq C_{\mathrm{FT}}U_N(\lambda),
\]
as claimed.  Every condition used above is retained in
\(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\), and neither richness inequality entered any
step.
\end{proof}

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
b_o=n^{-1/(\kappa+1)},\qquad
b_e=r_{en}^{2/(\kappa+2)},\qquad
b_g=(nr_{1n}^2)^{-1},
\]
with \(b_g=+\infty\) when \(r_{1n}=0\), and let \(L\) be the active maximum in \texttt{def:oracle-threshold}.  Write

S_e=r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)},\qquad
S_0=r_{0n}r_{en}.
\]
By \texttt{def:sharp-rate},
\[
\rho_n=Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})+S_o+S_e+S_0. \tag{1}
\]

First suppose \(L<\lambda_0\), so \(\lambda_n^\star=L\).  Since \(L\geq b_o\),
\[
\frac{r_{1n}}{\sqrt{nL}}\leq r_{1n}L^{\kappa/2}. \tag{2}
\]
Since \(L\geq b_e\), with both conclusions trivial if \(r_{en}=0\),
\[
\frac{r_{en}}{\sqrt nL^{3/2}}
\leq\frac{L^{(\kappa-1)/2}}{\sqrt n}
\leq Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n}),\qquad
\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}L\leq S_e. \tag{3}
\]
For \(0<\kappa<1\), the second inequality in the first part of (3) follows from \(L\geq b_g\):
\[
n^{-1/2}L^{-(1-\kappa)/2}
\leq n^{-\kappa/2}r_{1n}^{1-\kappa}.
\]
For \(\kappa\geq1\), it follows from \(L<\lambda_0<1\) and the parametric term in \(Q_\kappa\).  The same comparison with \(b_g\) gives
\[
\frac{v_\kappa(L)}{\sqrt n}\leq Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n}) \tag{4}
\]
for \(0<\kappa\leq1\): at \(\kappa=1\),
\(\log(\lambda_0/L)\leq\log_+(nr_{1n}^2)\).  For \(\kappa>1\), (4) is equality.  Finally, taking the positive \(\kappa/2\)-power of the active maximum and bounding the maximum by the sum gives
\[
r_{1n}L^{\kappa/2}
\leq S_o+S_e+Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n}). \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the \(b_g\) contribution is
\(n^{-\kappa/2}r_{1n}^{1-\kappa}\) for \(0<\kappa<1\) and \(n^{-1/2}\) for \(\kappa=1\); \(b_g\) is inactive for \(\kappa>1\).  Substitution of (2)--(5) into the six terms of \(U_n\) yields
\[
U_n(L)\leq4Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})+2S_o+3S_e+S_0
\leq4\rho_n. \tag{6}
\]

Now suppose \(L\geq\lambda_0\), so \(\lambda_n^\star=\lambda_0\).  At least one active component is at least \(\lambda_0\).  If \(b_o\geq\lambda_0\), then \(r_{1n}\lambda_0^{\kappa/2}\leq S_o\).  If \(b_e\geq\lambda_0\), then \(r_{1n}\lambda_0^{\kappa/2}\leq S_e\).  In either case, \(Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})\geq n^{-1/2}\), the radius caps, and
\[
\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}{\lambda_0}
\leq\lambda_0^{-1}\bar r^{2/(\kappa+2)}S_e
\]
give, term by term,
\[
U_n(\lambda_0)\leq C_{\mathrm{oe}}\rho_n. \tag{7}
\]
If \(0<\kappa\leq1\) and \(b_g\geq\lambda_0\), then
\(r_{1n}\leq(n\lambda_0)^{-1/2}\), also when \(r_{1n}=0\).  Hence
\[
\frac{r_{1n}}{\sqrt{n\lambda_0}}\leq\lambda_0^{-1}n^{-1/2},\quad
r_{1n}\lambda_0^{\kappa/2}\leq
\lambda_0^{-(1-\kappa)/2}n^{-1/2},\quad
\frac{r_{1n}r_{en}}{\lambda_0}\leq
\bar r\lambda_0^{-3/2}n^{-1/2}.
\]
Together with
\(v_\kappa(\lambda_0)n^{-1/2}\),
\(r_{en}n^{-1/2}\lambda_0^{-3/2}\), and \(S_0\), the displayed definition of \(C_{\mathrm g}\) gives
\[
U_n(\lambda_0)\leq C_{\mathrm g}\rho_n. \tag{8}
\]
The cases (7)--(8) exhaust the active maximum.  By the piecewise definition
\(C_{\mathrm{opt}}=4+C_{\mathrm{oe}}+C_{\mathrm g}\) for \(0<\kappa\leq1\) and \(C_{\mathrm{opt}}=4+C_{\mathrm{oe}}\) for \(\kappa>1\), equations (6)--(8) prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The regular-benchmark reduction is only an algebraic check: as \(\kappa\to\infty\), the treated side has the same parametric-plus-product form as the verified published lower-bound templates after their explicit radius identifications. Bonvini's attested one-functional statement is used only for the treated-side mapping \(\varepsilon_n=r_{1n}\), \(\delta_n=r_{en}\); no control-arm term is attributed to that source. The shell class and the regular comparator class are disjoint, so the calculation establishes neither minimax equivalence nor inclusion transfer. The accepted-bank runs \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match} and \texttt{stat\_discrete\_ate\_heterogeneity\_frontier\_v1} study the same full-population binary ATE only within fixed-overlap finite-discrete-occupancy experiments. The first isolates unknown rare-cell occupancy for binary outcomes, and the second isolates approximate cell-effect heterogeneity for real outcomes with bounded conditional means. Those experiments and this infinite-support, dyadic weak-overlap, public-global-\(L^2(P_X)\) experiment are non-nested; no lower bound, rate, or honest-length conclusion is transferred between them. The accepted policy-regret and continuous-exposure clamp frontiers answer non-nested causal decision problems under non-nested law classes.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The converse uses reservoir-rich fixed shell constants satisfying \(\texttt{ass:lower-shell-richness}\) and \(\texttt{ass:upper-shell-richness}\). Its probability law is complete: the displayed background mass is less than one half, active signal and balancing masses total \(2As_0^\kappa\), explicit completion masses sum to the remaining mass, and all three pieces sum exactly to one. Every active mass is relocated from a virtual dyadic endpoint into a fixed interior fraction of its shell; the relocation cap proves the upper shell inequality even when nonemptiness leaves no unused global capacity. Treated fuzzy propensities have ratios in \([4/5,4/3]\), control fuzzy propensities in \([3/4,5/4]\), and opposite-tilt companions make each fixed-sign propensity multiset invariant. At \(\kappa=1\), the proof uses exact dyadic sums for \(\lVert f\rVert_2^2\), \(E[e_0f^2]\), and \(E[f]\), not density or integral heuristics. The upper bound needs no reservoir richness, so shell classes outside the stated richness regime may have different converse geometry.

\section{Honest scope}

The theorem concerns supplied public functions, bounded certified radii, fixed shell constants, and \(E_P[(g_1(X)-g_0(X))^2]\leq M\); it imposes no pointwise potential-outcome envelope and makes no Wald or Gaussian-limit claim.  The confidence interval is bias-aware and is evaluable when the oracle certificates and fixed class constants are supplied: its multiplier is the displayed algebraic \(K_\alpha\).  The upper interval needs no reservoir richness, whereas matching length optimality uses both richness assumptions.  Adaptation to unknown shell constants, tail exponent, and nuisance radii, bounded-outcome variants, shell constants outside the richness regime, and exact studentized validity remain open.
