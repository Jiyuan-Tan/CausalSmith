Choose \(L=1-\eta_+\), \(a=\min\lbrace L,\lambda_0\rbrace\), \(q_\ell=a2^{-\ell}\), \(\ell\geq0\), an integer \(J\geq3\), \(\zeta=7/10\), \(\gamma=21/40\), and \(s_0=\zeta q_J\). Put background mass \(b_0=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa\) at propensity \(q_0\) and, for \(\ell\geq1\), mass \(b_\ell=c_-q_{\ell-1}^\kappa\) at propensity \(q_\ell\). Let \(B_{\mathrm{bg}}=\sum_{\ell\geq0}b_\ell\). Choose
\[
0<A<\min\left\lbrace
{c_+-2^\kappa c_-\over2^{\kappa+1}},\quad
{1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}\over2s_0^\kappa},\quad
{c_+{(2\gamma)^\kappa-1}\over2^{\kappa+1}(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa}
\right\rbrace,
\]
and set
\[
t_j=s_0 2^{-j},\qquad
w_j=A(1-2^{-\kappa})t_j^\kappa,\qquad j\geq0.
\]
For each finite \(N\), use distinct signal points \(x_{j,k}\) and balancing points \(\widetilde x_{j,k}\), \(1\leq k\leq N\), each of mass \(w_j/N\), with public baseline propensity \(e_0=t_j\).  Reserve their combined mass \(R_{J+j}=2w_j\) from the virtual endpoint \(q_{J+j}\), with \(R_\ell=0\) otherwise.  Define
\[
d_\ell=c_+q_\ell^\kappa-b_\ell-R_\ell,\quad
H=1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}-2As_0^\kappa,\quad
D_{\mathrm{cap}}=\sum_{\ell\geq0}d_\ell,\quad
\pi_\ell={H\over D_{\mathrm{cap}}}d_\ell,
\]
and put completion mass \(\pi_\ell\) at a distinct point of propensity \(q_\ell\).  In unchanged families both members of an active pair have propensity \(t_j\).  In a treated fuzzy cell, with \(a_{\theta,k}=\theta\delta Z_k\), use propensities \(t_j/(1+a_{\theta,k})\) and \(t_j/(1-a_{\theta,k})\) on the signal and balancing points, respectively, and put the treated-regression perturbation only on the signal point.  In the control fuzzy cell, with \(a_{\theta,k}=\theta tZ_k\), use propensities \((t_j+a_{\theta,k})/(1+a_{\theta,k})\) and \((t_j-a_{\theta,k})/(1-a_{\theta,k})\), and put the control-regression perturbation only on the signal point.  Thus each fixed-sign signal--balancing pair has a sign-invariant propensity multiset.

On this completed support build four subexperiments in \(\mathcal M_n\): shell-localized treated Gaussian mean shifts for \(Q_\kappa\); an untreated-observation coupling at scale \(n^{-1/(\kappa+1)}\); a treated signed-cell fuzzy mixture on the unique signal level in \(B_s=\{s<e_0\leq2s\}\), with \(s\asymp r_{en}^{2/(\kappa+2)}\); and a control signed-cell fuzzy mixture on \(B=\{s_c<e_0\leq2s_c\}=\{x_{j_c,k}:1\leq k\leq N\}\), where \(j_c\geq1\), \(s_c=t_{j_c+1}\), and \(P_X(B)=w_{j_c}\).  In the control family set \(g_0=h_0Z_k\), \(h_0=c_A r_{0n}/\sqrt{P_X(B)}\), \(m_0=0\), \(p=e_0\), and
\[
1-e_{\theta,Z}={1-e_0\over1+\theta tZ_k},\qquad
t=c_A\min\left\{{r_{en}\over\sqrt{P_X(B)}},{1\over4}\right\}
\]
on signal cells, using the opposite tilt on their balancing companions.  Keep the public functions fixed within each subexperiment, take \(N\) large enough to make cell collisions and prior-target fluctuations negligible, and combine the four component separations into \(\rho_n\) by the maximum-to-sum comparison.

Write \(L=1-\eta_+\).  Nonemptiness of \(\mathcal M_n\), \(\texttt{ass:uniform-shell}\) at \(u=\lambda_0\), and \(\texttt{ass:right-overlap}\) imply
\[
L>{\lambda_0\over2}.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if \(L\leq\lambda_0/2\), then \(P\{\lambda_0/2<e(X)\leq\lambda_0\}=0\), contrary to \(c_-\lambda_0^\kappa>0\).  Positivity and the lower shell bound also imply that \(\mathcal X\) is infinite: finitely many positive propensity values would have a positive minimum and would leave all sufficiently small shells empty.  Choose recursively a countable set of distinct points of \(\mathcal X\).  Singletons in a standard Borel space are measurable, so every atomic construction below is measurable.  For each finite \(N\), the required background, completion, signal, and balancing points form only a countable subset of this set.

We first complete the probability law specified by \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{lb}}\).  Put
\[
a=\min\{L,\lambda_0\},\qquad q_\ell=a2^{-\ell},\quad \ell\geq0.
\]
By (1), \(\lambda_0/2<a\leq\lambda_0\).  At \(q_0\) put background mass \(b_0=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa\), and at \(q_\ell\), \(\ell\geq1\), put background mass
\[
b_\ell=c_-q_{\ell-1}^\kappa=2^\kappa c_-q_\ell^\kappa.                  \tag{2}
\]
For \(0<u<a\), there is a unique \(\ell\geq1\) with \(q_\ell\leq u<q_{\ell-1}\), and exactly \(q_\ell\) lies in \((u/2,u]\).  Hence
\[
c_-u^\kappa<b_\ell\leq2^\kappa c_-u^\kappa.                            \tag{3}
\]
At a dyadic endpoint the same assertion holds with the adjacent index.  For \(a\leq u\leq\lambda_0\), only \(q_0\) from the background lies in the shell and
\[
c_-u^\kappa\leq b_0=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa
 <2^\kappa c_-a^\kappa\leq2^\kappa c_-u^\kappa.                         \tag{4}
\]
The strict inequality uses \(\lambda_0<2a\).  Thus the background alone supplies the required lower shell bound and has upper shell coefficient \(2^\kappa c_-\).  Its displayed total mass is
\[
B_{\mathrm{bg}}
=c_-\lambda_0^\kappa+{c_-a^\kappa\over1-2^{-\kappa}}
\leq {1-2^{-\kappa}\over4}+{1\over4}< {1\over2},                       \tag{5}
\]
by \(\texttt{ass:lower-shell-richness}\).

Let \(\Delta=c_+-2^\kappa c_-\).  The two richness inequalities give \(\Delta>0\): multiplying by \(4\lambda_0^\kappa\), the asserted lower bound on \(c_+\) is \(2^{\kappa+1}\), whereas the asserted upper bound on \(2^\kappa c_-\) is \(2^\kappa-1\).  Fix \(J\geq3\), \(\zeta=7/10\), and
\[
s_0=\zeta q_J< {1\over4}\min\{L,\lambda_0\},\qquad
t_j=s_02^{-j}=\zeta q_{J+j}.                                              \tag{6}
\]
Let \(\gamma=21/40\).  Choose \(A>0\) strictly below
\[
{\Delta\over2^{\kappa+1}},\qquad
{1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}\over2s_0^\kappa},\qquad
{c_+\{(2\gamma)^\kappa-1\}
 \over2^{\kappa+1}(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa}.                         \tag{7}
\]
All three caps are positive.  Put
\[
w_j=A(1-2^{-\kappa})t_j^\kappa,\qquad j\geq0.                            \tag{8}
\]
For each \(j\) and \(k=1,\ldots,N\), give both \(x_{j,k}\) and its balancing companion \(\widetilde x_{j,k}\) mass \(w_j/N\).  The total signal-reservoir mass is \(As_0^\kappa\), because
\[
\sum_{j\geq0}w_j
=A(1-2^{-\kappa})s_0^\kappa\sum_{j\geq0}2^{-j\kappa}
=As_0^\kappa,                                                            \tag{9}
\]
and the companions have the same total mass.

We now specify every completion atom.  Reserve the two active copies from endpoint \(q_{J+j}\) by setting \(R_{J+j}=2w_j\), and set \(R_\ell=0\) otherwise.  Define
\[
d_\ell=c_+q_\ell^\kappa-b_\ell-R_\ell.                                \tag{10}
\]
For \(\ell<J\), (3)--(4) and \(\Delta>0\) give \(d_\ell>0\).  For \(\ell\geq J\), (7)--(8) give
\[
b_\ell+R_\ell
=\{2^\kappa c_-+2A(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa\}q_\ell^\kappa
\leq c_+q_\ell^\kappa,                                                  \tag{11}
\]
so \(d_\ell\geq0\).

Set \(S=c_+a^\kappa/(1-2^{-\kappa})\).  We claim \(S\geq1\).  If \(a=L\leq\lambda_0\), take any member of the nonempty class.  Positivity and right overlap make the disjoint events \(\{q_{\ell+1}<e(X)\leq q_\ell\}\), \(\ell\geq0\), a partition up to null sets; the upper shell bound at \(u=q_\ell\) gives
\[
1\leq\sum_{\ell\geq0}c_+q_\ell^\kappa=S.                               \tag{12}
\]
If \(a=\lambda_0<L\), \(\texttt{ass:upper-shell-richness}\) gives
\[
S\geq {2^{\kappa-1}\over1-2^{-\kappa}}\geq1,                            \tag{13}
\]
because, for \(x=2^\kappa>1\), \(x^2\geq2(x-1)\).  Therefore
\[
D_{\mathrm{cap}}:=\sum_{\ell\geq0}d_\ell
=S-B_{\mathrm{bg}}-2As_0^\kappa
\geq1-B_{\mathrm{bg}}-2As_0^\kappa=:H>0.                                \tag{14}
\]
At a distinct completion point of propensity \(q_\ell\), put the explicit mass
\[
\pi_\ell={H\over D_{\mathrm{cap}}}d_\ell.                                                \tag{15}
\]
Then \(0\leq\pi_\ell\leq d_\ell\), \(\sum_\ell\pi_\ell=H\), and the exact total-mass identity is
\[
\sum_{\ell\geq0}b_\ell+2\sum_{j\geq0}w_j+
\sum_{\ell\geq0}\pi_\ell
=B_{\mathrm{bg}}+2As_0^\kappa+H=1.                                      \tag{16}
\]
This is the required complete probability law; no capacity is left implicit.

For shell membership, first form the virtual endpoint law having mass
\[
M_\ell=b_\ell+\pi_\ell+R_\ell\leq c_+q_\ell^\kappa                     \tag{17}
\]
at \(q_\ell\).  The actual law relocates the mass \(R_\ell\) into the interval \([\gamma q_\ell,q_\ell)\).  This includes unchanged active propensities because \(t_j=\zeta q_{J+j}\); treated fuzzy ratios \([4/5,4/3]\) because
\[
{4\over5}\zeta>{21\over40},\qquad {4\over3}\zeta<1;                   \tag{18}
\]
and control fuzzy ratios \([3/4,5/4]\) because
\[
{3\over4}\zeta={21\over40},\qquad {5\over4}\zeta<1.                   \tag{19}
\]
The signal point receives the stated tilt and the companion the opposite tilt.  Thus, for every fixed sign, their unordered propensity pair is independent of the sign and of \(\theta\), and both relocated values satisfy (18) or (19).

Fix \(0<u<a\) and the unique \(\ell\geq1\) with \(q_\ell\leq u<q_{\ell-1}\).  Relocation from \(q_\ell\) can add at most the mass removed from \(q_\ell\); relocation from lower endpoints remains below \(u/2\).  The only possible extra mass comes from \(q_{\ell-1}\), and it can enter only if \(u\geq\gamma q_{\ell-1}=2\gamma q_\ell\).  Consequently, using (17),
\[
P\{u/2<e(X)\leq u\}
\leq M_\ell+
R_{\ell-1}\mathbf 1\{u\geq2\gamma q_\ell\}.                            \tag{20}
\]
When the indicator is one, (7)--(8) imply
\[
R_{\ell-1}
\leq2^{\kappa+1}A(1-2^{-\kappa})\zeta^\kappa q_\ell^\kappa
\leq c_+\{(2\gamma)^\kappa-1\}q_\ell^\kappa
\leq c_+(u^\kappa-q_\ell^\kappa).                                      \tag{21}
\]
Equations (17), (20), and (21) prove the upper cap \(c_+u^\kappa\).  For \(a\leq u\leq\lambda_0\), active propensities are below \(q_J<a/2\), and only the unrelocated endpoint \(q_0\) can enter; (17) again gives the upper cap.  The background atoms were not relocated, so (3)--(4) give the lower cap for every \(u\).  Hence every fixed-sign law satisfies
\[
c_-u^\kappa\leq P\{u/2<e(X)\leq u\}\leq c_+u^\kappa,\qquad
0<u\leq\lambda_0.                                                       \tag{22}
\]
This also proves the cruder directive check: either active copy has tail mass at most \(Av^\kappa\), hence a factor-two perturbation contributes at most \(A(2u)^\kappa\leq\Delta u^\kappa/2\).  Equations (20)--(21) additionally certify the completion mass even when the global capacity in (12) is tight.

The signal reservoir has exact tail calculus.  For \(0<v\leq s_0\), let \(j(v)=\min\{j:t_j\leq v\}\).  Then \(v/2<t_{j(v)}\leq v\), and (8) gives
\[
P_X\{x_{j,k}:e_0\leq v\}
=\sum_{j\geq j(v)}w_j=A t_{j(v)}^\kappa,\qquad
A2^{-\kappa}v^\kappa<P_X\{e_0\leq v,\mathrm{signal}\}\leq Av^\kappa, \tag{23}
\]
and
\[
E[e_0\mathbf 1\{e_0\leq v,\mathrm{signal}\}]
={A(1-2^{-\kappa})\over1-2^{-(\kappa+1)}}t_{j(v)}^{\kappa+1}
\asymp v^{\kappa+1}.                                                     \tag{24}
\]
For arbitrary \(0<s\leq s_0/2\), \((s,2s]\) contains exactly one dyadic level: if \(s=t_j\), it contains \(t_{j-1}=2s\) and excludes \(t_j=s\); otherwise choose the unique \(j\) with \(t_j<2s<t_{j-1}\), and then \(t_j\in(s,2s)\).  Thus
\[
B_s=\{x_{j,k}:1\leq k\leq N\},\qquad
P_X(B_s)=w_j\asymp s^\kappa.                                             \tag{25}
\]
Choose \(j_c\geq1\), set \(s_c=t_{j_c+1}\), and obtain exactly
\[
B=(s_c,2s_c]=\{x_{j_c,k}:1\leq k\leq N\},\qquad
m_*:=P_X(B)=w_{j_c}>0,                                                    \tag{26}
\]
with equal cell masses \(m_*/N\).

We next state the testing reduction.  If sample laws \(Q_+\) and \(Q_-\) have targets separated by at least \(2d\), the events \(\{|T-\tau_+|<d\}\) and \(\{|T-\tau_-|<d\}\) are disjoint.  The variational definition of total variation therefore yields
\[
Q_+\{|T-\tau_+|\geq d\}+Q_-\{|T-\tau_-|\geq d\}
\geq1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_+,Q_-).                                       \tag{27}
\]
The same argument applies to priors: if mixture sample laws have total variation at most \(\Gamma\), and each prior target is within \(d/4\) of centers separated by \(2d\) except with prior probability \(\varepsilon\), then one mixture has error at least \(d/2\) with probability at least \((1-\Gamma-2\varepsilon)/2\).  This follows by testing the nearer center.  A mixture probability is an average of fixed-law probabilities, so a fixed law in its support inherits the lower bound.

For every family, take \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) to be independent copies, generate \(D\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e(X))\), generate \((Y_0,Y_1)\) conditionally independently of \(D\) given \(X\), and set \(Y=DY_1+(1-D)Y_0\).  This verifies \(\texttt{ass:iid}\), \(\texttt{ass:ignorability}\), and \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\).  Use centered Gaussian treated noise of variance
\[
0<\sigma^2\leq \min\{\sigma_1^2,M_Y/4\}                                \tag{28}
\]
only for Gaussian mean-shift families, and zero residual noise otherwise.  Choose one constant \(c_A>0\), depending only on the fixed class constants, small enough that every regression perturbation below has squared \(L^2(P_X)\) norm at most \(M\wedge(M_Y/4)\), that the public nuisance radii are respected, and that \(c_A\leq\min\{1/4,s_c\}\).  This is possible because every such squared norm is at most a fixed multiple of \(c_A^2\bar r^2\) or \(c_A^2\).  Thus the treatment-effect signal bound, the outcome second moment, and both residual-variance bounds are verified for every fixed law.  Positivity and right overlap follow from (17)--(22): completion and background propensities are at most \(a\leq L\), while active propensities are positive and below \(q_J<L\).

For the parametric obstruction, split the positive top background mass \(b_0\) between two points of the same propensity.  Give them fixed treatment effects \(+c_A\) and \(-c_A\), take the public nuisances equal to the true nuisances, and under hypothesis \(\theta\in\{-1,1\}\) give their covariate masses \(b_0/2+\theta\epsilon_n\) and \(b_0/2-\theta\epsilon_n\), where \(\epsilon_n=c_pn^{-1/2}\) and \(c_p<b_0/4\).  The propensity marginal, hence (22), is unchanged.  The one-row categorical divergence is
\[
2\epsilon_n\log {b_0/2+\epsilon_n\over b_0/2-\epsilon_n}
\leq {16\epsilon_n^2\over b_0},                                           \tag{29}
\]
so the product divergence is at most \(16c_p^2/b_0\).  To convert it to total variation without an undeclared theorem, write the likelihood ratio as \(f\); the pointwise inequality \(f\log f-f+1\geq(\sqrt f-1)^2\), integration, and Cauchy--Schwarz give \(\operatorname{TV}^2\leq\int(\sqrt f-1)^2\,dQ_-\leq\operatorname{KL}(Q_+,Q_-)\).  Decreasing \(c_p\) makes product total variation at most \(1/4\).  The two ATEs differ by \(4c_Ac_pn^{-1/2}\), so (27) yields the \(n^{-1/2}\) obstruction.

For \(0<\kappa<1\) and \(r_{1n}>0\), first suppose \(s=(nr_{1n}^2)^{-1}\leq s_0\).  On the signal set \(A_s=\{x_{j,k}:e_0\leq s\}\), write \(m_s=P_X(A_s)\asymp s^\kappa\), and set
\[
m_1=0,\quad p=e_0,\quad g_{1,\pm}=\pm h\mathbf 1_{A_s},\quad
h={c_A r_{1n}\over\sqrt{m_s}},\qquad g_0=m_0=0.                         \tag{30}
\]
Use the unchanged active propensities.  Then \(\|m_1-g_{1,\pm}\|_2=c_A r_{1n}\leq r_{1n}\).  By (24), \(E[e_0\mathbf 1_{A_s}]\leq Csm_s\).  Direct integration of the two Gaussian densities gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_+^n,P_-^n)
={2nh^2\over\sigma^2}E[e_0\mathbf 1_{A_s}]
\leq Cc_A^2,                                                            \tag{31}
\]
whereas
\[
|\tau(P_+)-\tau(P_-)|=2hm_s
\asymp n^{-\kappa/2}r_{1n}^{1-\kappa}.                                  \tag{32}
\]
If \(s>s_0\), then \(r_{1n}<s_0^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}\), so the right side of (32) is at most \(s_0^{-(1-\kappa)/2}n^{-1/2}\), already covered by (29).  If \(r_{1n}=0\), only that parametric part of \(Q_\kappa\) remains.

At \(\kappa=1\), first assume \(r_{1n}>0\) and put \(s=(nr_{1n}^2)^{-1}\).  If \(s\leq s_0\), define
\[
J_s=\max\{j:t_j\geq s\},\quad K_s=J_s+1,\quad
a_s={c_A\over\sqrt{nK_s}},\quad
f={a_s\over e_0}\mathbf 1\{s\leq e_0\leq s_0,\mathrm{signal}\}.       \tag{33}
\]
Here \(w_j=(A/2)t_j\).  Exact sums, rather than a density approximation, give
\[
\|f\|_2^2={Aa_s^2(2^{K_s}-1)\over2s_0}\asymp {a_s^2\over s},\qquad
E[e_0f^2]={A\over2}a_s^2K_s,\qquad
E[f]={A\over2}a_sK_s.                                                    \tag{34}
\]
Indeed, \(s_02^{-J_s}\geq s>s_02^{-(J_s+1)}\), which also proves the comparison in the first display.  Taking \(g_{1,\pm}=\pm f\), the first identity and \(s^{-1}=nr_{1n}^2\) give \(\|f\|_2\leq r_{1n}\) after the fixed calibration of \(c_A\).  The second identity gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_+^n,P_-^n)
={2n\over\sigma^2}E[e_0f^2]\leq Cc_A^2,                                  \tag{35}
\]
and the third gives target separation
\[
2E[f]=Aa_sK_s\asymp n^{-1/2}\sqrt{1+\log_+(nr_{1n}^2)}.                  \tag{36}
\]
If \(s>s_0\), the logarithm in (36) is bounded by a class constant and the parametric family applies.  If \(r_{1n}=0\), define no \(s\) and again use the parametric family.  For \(\kappa>1\), \(Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n})=n^{-1/2}\) by \(\texttt{def:tail-noise-rate}\).  Equations (29)--(36) prove the entire \(Q_\kappa\) channel.

For the empty-treated-shell channel, choose a fixed \(c_s\leq s_0\) sufficiently small and put \(s=c_sn^{-1/(\kappa+1)}\).  Use (30) on \(A_s\).  Couple the two observed samples identically unless an observation is both in \(A_s\) and treated.  Equations (23)--(24) and a union bound yield
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_+^n,P_-^n)
\leq nP\{X\in A_s,D=1\}
\leq Cns^{\kappa+1}=Cc_s^{\kappa+1}.                                    \tag{37}
\]
Choose \(c_s\) so the last quantity is at most \(1/4\).  The target separation is
\[
2hm_s\asymp r_{1n}n^{-\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]}.                             \tag{38}
\]
The channel is vacuous when \(r_{1n}=0\).

For the treated nuisance product, suppose \(r_{en}>0\).  Because \(r_{en}\leq\bar r\), choose a fixed \(c_s>0\), depending only on \(\bar r\) and the class constants, such that
\[
s=c_sr_{en}^{2/(\kappa+2)}\leq s_0/2                                      \tag{39}
\]
and the propensity-radius inequality below holds.  By (25), the signal band \(B_s\) is exactly one level and has mass \(m\asymp s^\kappa\).  Let \(\delta=1/4\), draw independent signs with
\[
\Pr_\theta(Z_k=z)={1+\theta\delta z\over2},                             \tag{40}
\]
put \(g_1=hZ_k\), \(h=c_A r_{1n}/\sqrt m\), on the signal cells, and put all regressions equal to zero on their companions.  On a signal--companion pair set, with \(a_{\theta,k}=\theta\delta Z_k\),
\[
e^S_{\theta,Z}={e_0\over1+a_{\theta,k}},\qquad
e^C_{\theta,Z}={e_0\over1-a_{\theta,k}},\qquad p=e_0.                   \tag{41}
\]
The ratio interval is \([4/5,4/3]\), so (18), (22), positivity, and right overlap hold for every fixed sign.  Both members of the pair have public propensity error at most \(e_0/3\); hence
\[
\|p-e_{\theta,Z}\|_2\leq Cs\sqrt m
\leq Cc_s^{1+\kappa/2}r_{en}\leq r_{en},                                \tag{42}
\]
by the choice of \(c_s\).  Also \(\|m_1-g_1\|_2=c_A r_{1n}\leq r_{1n}\).

For the signal row, including the treated outcome with mean \(hz\),
\[
\Pr_\theta(Z_k=z)e^S_{\theta,z}
={1+\theta\delta z\over2}{e_0\over1+\theta\delta z}={e_0\over2},        \tag{43}
\]
and summing the control weights gives \(1-e_0\).  On the companion, the law of \(a_{\theta,k}=\theta\delta Z_k\) under (40) is independent of \(\theta\), so its entire one-row mixture is independent of \(\theta\).  Thus the two sample mixtures can differ only if two observations hit the same signal--companion index.  Since index \(k\) has total mass \(2m/N\),
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\overline P_+^n,\overline P_-^n)
\leq {2n(n-1)m^2\over N}.                                                \tag{44}
\]
The target depends only on signal cells:
\[
\tau(P_{\theta,Z})={hm\over N}\sum_{k=1}^NZ_k,\quad
E_\theta\tau=\theta\delta hm,\quad
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\tau)\leq {h^2m^2\over N}.                   \tag{45}
\]
Choose \(N\) larger than fixed multiples of \(n^2\) and \(\delta^{-2}\).  Then (44) and the Chebyshev failure probability for a \(\delta hm/4\) target deviation are each at most \(1/16\).  The prior version of (27) yields an obstruction of order
\[
hm\asymp r_{1n}s^{\kappa/2}\asymp
r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}.                                        \tag{46}
\]
The bounded-radius choice in (39) handles the entire large-radius branch without leaving the active reservoir.  If \(r_{en}=0\) or \(r_{1n}=0\), the desired component is zero.

For the control product, use the fixed signal band (26), its equal-mass companions, and write \(e_0=t_{j_c}=2s_c\).  Put
\[
g_0=h_0Z_k,\quad h_0={c_A r_{0n}\over\sqrt{m_*}},\quad m_0=0,\quad
\xi=c_A\min\left\{{r_{en}\over\sqrt{m_*}},{1\over4}\right\},         \tag{47}
\]
on signal cells and put all regressions equal to zero on companions.  Draw signs according to \(\Pr_\theta(Z_k=z)=(1+\theta\xi z)/2\).  With \(a_{\theta,k}=\theta\xi Z_k\), set
\[
e^S_{\theta,Z}={e_0+a_{\theta,k}\over1+a_{\theta,k}},\qquad
e^C_{\theta,Z}={e_0-a_{\theta,k}\over1-a_{\theta,k}},\qquad p=e_0.     \tag{48}
\]
Because \(c_A\leq s_c\), \(\xi\leq s_c/4\).  Direct algebra gives
\[
{e^S_{\theta,Z}\over e_0}
=1+{a_{\theta,k}(1-e_0)\over e_0(1+a_{\theta,k})},                      \tag{49}
\]
and the same formula with \(-a_{\theta,k}\) for the companion.  Since \(e_0=2s_c\), both ratios lie in \([3/4,5/4]\).  Thus (19), (22), positivity, and right overlap hold fixed-sign by fixed-sign.  Moreover,
\[
\|p-e_{\theta,Z}\|_2
\leq{\xi\sqrt{2m_*}\over1-\xi}\leq r_{en},\qquad
\|m_0-g_0\|_2=c_A r_{0n}\leq r_{0n},                                    \tag{50}
\]
after the fixed choice of \(c_A\).  For the first inequality, use the first branch of (47) when \(r_{en}\leq\sqrt{m_*}/4\), and use \(r_{en}>\sqrt{m_*}/4\) in the other branch.

The signal control row cancels exactly:
\[
\Pr_\theta(Z_k=z)(1-e^S_{\theta,z})
={1+\theta\xi z\over2}{1-e_0\over1+\theta\xi z}
={1-e_0\over2},                                                          \tag{51}
\]
including its outcome \(h_0z\), while the summed treated row has probability \(e_0\) and outcome zero.  The companion one-row mixture is independent of \(\theta\) because the distribution of \(a_{\theta,k}\) is.  The collision bound is \(2n(n-1)m_*^2/N\).  Furthermore,
\[
\tau(P_{\theta,Z})=-{h_0m_*\over N}\sum_{k=1}^NZ_k,\quad
E_\theta\tau=-\theta\xi h_0m_*,\quad
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\tau)\leq {h_0^2m_*^2\over N}.                \tag{52}
\]
Taking \(N\) larger than fixed multiples of \(n^2\) and \(\xi^{-2}\) when \(\xi>0\), the fuzzy testing reduction yields half-separation
\[
d_0=\xi h_0m_*
=c_A^2r_{0n}\min\{r_{en},\sqrt{m_*}/4\}.                                \tag{53}
\]
In the small branch this is \(c_A^2r_{0n}r_{en}\).  In the large branch, \(r_{en}>\sqrt{m_*}/4\) and \(r_{en}\leq\bar r\), so
\[
d_0={c_A^2\sqrt{m_*}\over4}r_{0n}
\geq {c_A^2\sqrt{m_*}\over4\bar r}r_{0n}r_{en}.                         \tag{54}
\]
If \(\xi=0\), the component is zero.  Equations (47)--(54) prove the control obstruction and every fixed-sign nuisance, support, and moment condition.

We have therefore constructed fixed laws in \(\mathcal M_n\), with identical public functions and certified radii within each subexperiment, yielding constant testing error at the four scales
\[
Q_\kappa(n,r_{1n}),\qquad
r_{1n}n^{-\kappa/[2(\kappa+1)]},\qquad
r_{1n}r_{en}^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)},\qquad
r_{0n}r_{en}.                                                            \tag{55}
\]
The first includes the parametric floor.  The sum of four nonnegative numbers is at most four times their maximum.  Decreasing one class-constant separation and taking the minimum of the positive testing probabilities gives constants \(c>0\) and \(\delta_0\in(0,1/4)\), independent of \(n\) and the admissible radius triple, such that every \(T\in\mathcal T_n\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{(P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\mathcal M_n}
P\{|T-\tau(P)|\geq c\rho_n\}\geq\delta_0.                             \tag{56}
\]
Public inputs cannot distinguish a subexperiment because they are identical throughout that subexperiment.  On the event in (56), absolute loss is at least \(c\rho_n\) and squared loss at least \(c^2\rho_n^2\).  Taking expectations, then the supremum over \(\mathcal M_n\), and finally the infima in \(\texttt{def:absolute-risk}\) and \(\texttt{def:root-mse-risk}\), yields
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{(1)}\geq c\delta_0\rho_n,\qquad
\mathfrak R_n^{(2)}\geq c\sqrt{\delta_0}\rho_n.
\]
This proves all assertions of the theorem, including the complete probability identity, fixed-sign class membership, and treatment-effect-signal calibration.

%%% FIELD proof-sharp-four-risk
The upper and lower bounds use the same law--function class \(\mathcal M_n\) and the same public-information decision class \(\mathcal T_n\).  By \(\texttt{thm:clipped-absolute-upper}\) and \(\texttt{def:absolute-risk}\),
\[
\mathfrak R_n^{(1)}
\leq\sup_{\mathcal M_n}E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^\star}-\tau(P)|
\leq C\rho_n.                                                           \tag{57}
\]
For \(n\geq5\), every block size \(m_j\) in \(\texttt{def:median-estimator}\) satisfies \(n/9\leq m_j\leq n\).  The conditional-expectation decomposition used in \(\texttt{thm:clipped-absolute-upper}\), applied to a block at the common threshold \(\lambda_n^\star\), has the same six terms, with each occurrence of \(n\) in a sampling factor replaced by \(m_j\).  The deterministic nuisance terms are unchanged.  Moreover,
\[
m_j^{-1/2}\leq3n^{-1/2},\qquad
m_j^{-1/(\kappa+1)}\leq9^{1/(\kappa+1)}n^{-1/(\kappa+1)},\qquad
(m_jr_{1n}^2)^{-1}\leq9(nr_{1n}^2)^{-1}.                                 \tag{58}
\]
The propensity-error scale is unchanged.  Thus the same bias--variance substitutions as in that theorem give
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}E_P|\widehat\tau_{\lambda_n^\star}^{(j)}-\tau(P)|
\leq K\rho_n                                                            \tag{59}
\]
with a class-constant \(K\).  Independence of the blocks follows from \(\texttt{ass:iid}\), so \(\texttt{lem:median-risk-upgrade}\) gives
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}
\{E_P(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P))^2\}^{1/2}
\leq\sqrt{21}\,K\rho_n.                                                \tag{60}
\]

For \(1\leq n<5\), \(\lambda_n^\star\) is bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(\lambda_0\) and \(\kappa\), and \(1-p^\circ\geq\eta_+/2\).  Jensen's inequality, \(\texttt{ass:outcome-second-moment}\), and \(1-e\geq\eta_+\) give
\[
E_P[g_0(X)^2]\leq {M_Y\over\eta_+}.                                     \tag{61}
\]
The treatment-effect signal condition then gives
\[
E_P[g_1(X)^2]\leq2M+{2M_Y\over\eta_+}.                                  \tag{62}
\]
The three public-radius bounds and \(r_{an}\leq\bar r\) therefore bound the second moment of the full-sample clipped score uniformly.  Since \(\rho_n\geq n^{-1/2}\geq1/2\) on this finite set of sample sizes, (60) remains valid after increasing \(K\).  The median rule belongs to \(\mathcal T_n\) by \(\texttt{lem:median-risk-upgrade}\), so \(\texttt{def:root-mse-risk}\) yields \(\mathfrak R_n^{(2)}\leq C\rho_n\).

For fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), the probability conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:clipped-absolute-upper}\) and \(\texttt{def:probability-risk}\) give
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,\delta}^{(\mathrm{prob})}\leq C_\delta\rho_n.           \tag{63}
\]
Conversely, \(\texttt{thm:four-channel-converse}\) gives both loss lower bounds and, for every \(T\in\mathcal T_n\),
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}P\{|T-\tau(P)|\geq c\rho_n\}\geq\delta_0.         \tag{64}
\]
If \(0<\delta\leq\delta_0\) and \(u<c\rho_n\), then
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}P\{|T-\tau(P)|>u\}
\geq\sup_{\mathcal M_n}P\{|T-\tau(P)|\geq c\rho_n\}
\geq\delta_0\geq\delta.                                                 \tag{65}
\]
Taking the two infima in \(\texttt{def:probability-risk}\) proves \(\mathfrak R_{n,\delta}^{(\mathrm{prob})}\geq c\rho_n\).  Combining (57)--(65) with the two loss lower bounds in \(\texttt{thm:four-channel-converse}\) proves all three equivalences uniformly over the stated radius regime.

%%% FIELD proof-honest-interval
For \(n\geq5\), equation (59) in the proof of \(\texttt{thm:sharp-four-risk-frontier}\) verifies the premise of \(\texttt{lem:median-risk-upgrade}\) with a uniform constant \(K\).  Hence
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}P\{|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)|>u\}
\leq\min\{1,10(K\rho_n/u)^3\}.                                         \tag{66}
\]
For \(1\leq n<5\), \(\texttt{thm:clipped-absolute-upper}\) and Markov's inequality give
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}P\{|\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{med}}-\tau(P)|
>C\rho_n/\alpha\}\leq\alpha.                                          \tag{67}
\]
Choose \(K_\alpha\) at least \(K(10/\alpha)^{1/3}\) and at least the finite-sample multiplier in (67).  By \(\texttt{def:bias-aware-interval}\),
\[
\inf_{\mathcal M_n}P\{\tau(P)\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\}\geq1-\alpha,
\qquad
\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha})=2K_\alpha\rho_n.          \tag{68}
\]
The center is measurable in \(O^{(n)}\), and the deterministic radius uses only the supplied constants and certified radii.  Therefore \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\in\mathcal I_n\), and (68) proves the honest expected-length upper bound.

For the converse, consider first a two-point subexperiment from \(\texttt{thm:four-channel-converse}\), with targets separated by \(2d\) and sample total variation at most \(\Gamma\).  Honesty under both fixed laws, transfer of an event by the variational definition of total variation, and the union bound imply under one law
\[
P\{\operatorname{length}(C_n)\geq2d\}\geq1-2\alpha-\Gamma.             \tag{69}
\]
Indeed, with probability at least the right side the interval contains both targets.

For a fuzzy subexperiment, let its prior target be within \(d/4\) of \(\mu_\theta\) except with probability \(\varepsilon\), let \(|\mu_+-\mu_-|=2d\), and let the mixture total variation be at most \(\Gamma\).  Fixed-law honesty averaged over either prior shows that the interval intersects the appropriate \(d/4\)-neighborhood with probability at least \(1-\alpha-\varepsilon\).  Transfer one intersection event between mixtures and intersect the two events.  An interval meeting both neighborhoods has length at least \(3d/2\), so
\[
\overline P\{\operatorname{length}(C_n)\geq3d/2\}
\geq1-2\alpha-\Gamma-2\varepsilon.                                     \tag{70}
\]
In the two-point channels, the fixed amplitude can be chosen so \(\Gamma\leq1/4\).  In the fuzzy channels, the cell count \(N\) can be enlarged so \(\Gamma+2\varepsilon\leq1/4\), without changing membership or separation.  Because \(\alpha<1/4\), the right sides of (69)--(70) are bounded below by a positive \(\alpha\)-dependent constant.  A mixture expectation is an average over fixed-sign laws, all of which belong to \(\mathcal M_n\) by \(\texttt{thm:four-channel-converse}\); hence at least one fixed law has expected length at least a positive constant times \(d\).

Choose the largest of the four component separations in (55).  Since the sum is at most four times the maximum, \(d\geq c\rho_n\) after a class-constant rescaling.  Thus every uniformly honest \(C_n\in\mathcal I_n\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{\mathcal M_n}E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_n)]
\geq c_\alpha\rho_n.                                                    \tag{71}
\]
Taking the infimum in \(\texttt{def:bias-aware-interval}\) and combining (68) with (71) proves \(\mathfrak L_{n,\alpha}\asymp\rho_n\).
