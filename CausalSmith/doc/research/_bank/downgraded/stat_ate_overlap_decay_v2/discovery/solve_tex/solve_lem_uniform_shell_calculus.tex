Fix \((P,m_0,m_1,p)\in\mathcal M_n\) and \(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\).  By \(\texttt{def:public-weak-overlap-class}\), the law \(P\) satisfies \(\texttt{ass:positivity}\) and \(\texttt{ass:uniform-shell}\).  Positivity and disjointness give
\[
 \{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 =\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \{2^{-j-1}\lambda<e(X)\leq 2^{-j}\lambda\}
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Consequently, the upper shell inequality and countable additivity imply
\[
 P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \leq c_+\lambda^\kappa\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\kappa}
 =\frac{c_+}{1-2^{-\kappa}}\lambda^\kappa.
\]
The shell with upper endpoint \(\lambda\) and the lower shell inequality give
\[
 P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \geq P\{\lambda/2<e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \geq c_-\lambda^\kappa.
\]
Thus \(P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}\asymp\lambda^\kappa\), uniformly in the chosen class member and in \(\lambda\).

It remains to control
\[
 H_\lambda:=E_P\!\left[(e(X)\vee\lambda)^{-1}\right].
\]
Write \(C_F=c_+/(1-2^{-\kappa})\).  The preceding tail bounds first show
\[
 c_-\lambda^{\kappa-1}
 \leq \lambda^{-1}P\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}
 \leq C_F\lambda^{\kappa-1}.
\]
For a uniform upper bound on the remaining part, set
\[
 J=\left\lfloor\log_2(\lambda_0/\lambda)\right\rfloor.
\]
Then \(J\geq0\), \(2^J\lambda\leq\lambda_0<2^{J+1}\lambda\), and the events
\[
 \{e(X)\leq\lambda\},\qquad
 A_j:=\{2^j\lambda<e(X)\leq2^{j+1}\lambda\}
 \quad(0\leq j<J),
\]
together with \(A_{\mathrm{top}}:=\{2^J\lambda<e(X)\leq\lambda_0\}\) and \(A_+:=\{e(X)>\lambda_0\}\), form a partition up to null sets.  For every \(0\leq j<J\), the upper endpoint \(2^{j+1}\lambda\) is at most \(\lambda_0\), so the upper shell inequality yields
\[
 E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_j}}{e(X)}\right]
 \leq \frac{P(A_j)}{2^j\lambda}
 \leq c_+2^\kappa\lambda^{\kappa-1}2^{j(\kappa-1)}.
\]
Moreover, the already proved tail upper bound and \(2^J\lambda>\lambda_0/2\) give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_{\mathrm{top}}}}{e(X)}\right]
 \leq \frac{P\{e(X)\leq\lambda_0\}}{2^J\lambda}
 \leq 2C_F\lambda_0^{\kappa-1},
\]
where the inequality is also valid when \(A_{\mathrm{top}}\) is empty.  On \(A_+\), one has \(e(X)^{-1}<\lambda_0^{-1}\).  Combining these bounds with the contribution below \(\lambda\) proves
\[
 H_\lambda
 \leq C\left{1+\lambda^{\kappa-1}
 \left(1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^{j(\kappa-1)}\right)\right},
\]
where an empty sum is zero and \(C\) depends only on \((c_+,\kappa,\lambda_0)\).

Suppose first that \(0<\kappa<1\).  The geometric sum in the last display is bounded by \((1-2^{\kappa-1})^{-1}\).  Since \(0<\lambda<1\), \(\lambda^{\kappa-1}\geq1\), and hence
\[
 H_\lambda\leq C\lambda^{\kappa-1}.
\]
The contribution from \(\{e(X)\leq\lambda\}\) in the earlier lower bound gives \(H_\lambda\geq c_-\lambda^{\kappa-1}\).  Therefore \(H_\lambda\asymp\lambda^{\kappa-1}=v_\kappa(\lambda)^2\).

Suppose next that \(\kappa=1\).  The upper bound becomes \(H_\lambda\leq C(J+1)\).  For each \(0\leq j<J\), the lower shell inequality gives
\[
 E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_j}}{e(X)}\right]
 \geq \frac{P(A_j)}{2^{j+1}\lambda}
 \geq c_-.
\]
Together with the below-threshold contribution, which is at least \(c_-\), this yields \(H_\lambda\geq c_-(J+1)\).  Because
\[
 J\leq\log_2(\lambda_0/\lambda)<J+1,
\]
the quantity \(J+1\) is comparable, by absolute constants, to \(1+\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)\).  Hence
\[
 H_\lambda\asymp 1+\log(\lambda_0/\lambda)=v_1(\lambda)^2.
\]

Finally, suppose that \(\kappa>1\).  The finite geometric sum satisfies
\[
 \lambda^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^{j(\kappa-1)}
 \leq \frac{(2^J\lambda)^{\kappa-1}}{2^{\kappa-1}-1}
 \leq \frac{\lambda_0^{\kappa-1}}{2^{\kappa-1}-1},
\]
so the displayed upper bound for \(H_\lambda\) is a constant.  Since the declared range of the propensity score and \(\lambda_0<1\) imply \(0<e(X)\vee\lambda\leq1\) almost surely, \(H_\lambda\geq1\).  Thus \(H_\lambda\asymp1=v_\kappa(\lambda)^2\).

All lower and upper constants obtained above depend only on \((c_-,c_+,\kappa,\lambda_0)\), and not on \(P\), \(n\), or \(\lambda\).  This proves both asserted uniform equivalences.
