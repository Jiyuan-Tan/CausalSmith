%%% FIELD regular-tipping-statement
Write the observed summary in the nonredundant chart
\[
b=\bigl(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{J-1},(p_x,r_{0,x},r_{1,x})_{x\in\mathcal X}\bigr)\in\mathbb R^{4J-1},
\qquad
\lambda_J=1-\sum_{x<J}\lambda_x,
\]
and write \(\gamma=(\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\).  Let \((b_0,\gamma_0)\) have strictly positive observed cells and finite budgets, let \(\gamma_0\) be an interior point of \(\mathcal B\cap(1,\infty)^3\), and suppose \(L(b_0,\gamma_0)=0\).  Assume that, on a neighborhood of \((b_0,\gamma_0)\), the legal-candidate system used by \(\mathsf{CERT}\) and stratified by \(\mathsf{ATLAS}\) has one unique active branch for \(L\), represented by an analytic objective \(\phi(z,b,\gamma)\) and analytic equations \(F(z,b,\gamma)=0\), where \(z\in\mathbb R^d\); at its solution \(z_0\), assume that \(F_z(z_0,b_0,\gamma_0)\) is invertible, every denominator is nonzero, every inactive inequality is strict, and the branch is strictly separated in objective value from all inactive candidates.  Define
\[
\dot L_b=\phi_b-\phi_zF_z^{-1}F_b,
\qquad
\dot L_\gamma=\phi_\gamma-\phi_zF_z^{-1}F_\gamma,
\]
with all derivatives evaluated at \((z_0,b_0,\gamma_0)\), and assume \(\dot L_\gamma\neq0\).  For independent observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\sim P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), suppose an estimator \(\widehat b_n\) obeys
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat b_n-b_0)
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi(O_i)+o_P(1)
\ \Longrightarrow\ Z\sim N(0,\Sigma),
\qquad
E\{\psi(O_i)\}=0,
\qquad
E\|\psi(O_i)\|^2<\infty.
\]
Then
\[
\sqrt n\{L(\widehat b_n,\gamma_0)-L(b_0,\gamma_0)\}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \dot L_bZ
\sim N(0,\sigma_L^2),
\qquad
\sigma_L^2=\dot L_b\Sigma\dot L_b^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Let \(\nu_0=\dot L_\gamma^{\mathsf T}/\|\dot L_\gamma\|\), and let \(t_n\) be the unique local solution of
\[
L(\widehat b_n,\gamma_0+t_n\nu_0)=0.
\]
Then \(t_n=O_P(n^{-1/2})\) and
\[
\sqrt n\,t_n
\ \Longrightarrow\
-\frac{\dot L_bZ}{\|\dot L_\gamma\|}
\sim N\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\|\nabla_\gamma L(b_0,\gamma_0)\|^2}\right).
\]
Thus the root-\(n\) standard-error constant for normal displacement of the regular zero-tipping frontier \(\mathfrak T_0(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is exactly \(\sigma_L/\|\nabla_\gamma L\|\).  If \(\sigma_L>0\), consistent plug-in estimators of \(\Sigma\), \(\dot L_b\), and \(\dot L_\gamma\) give the usual pointwise normal confidence interval for this normal coordinate.  The same conclusions hold for \(R\) after replacing \(L\), its active minimizing branch, and its derivatives by \(R\), its active maximizing branch, and its derivatives.
%%% FIELD regular-tipping-proof
The strict observed-cell conditions put \(b_0\) in the relative interior of its finite-dimensional probability chart, while the finite interior budget condition permits two-sided perturbations of \(\gamma_0\).  The hypotheses concerning denominators and inactive inequalities ensure that every displayed analytic expression is defined on a neighborhood of \((z_0,b_0,\gamma_0)\).  The unique active branch and its strict objective separation imply on a possibly smaller neighborhood that
\[
F\{z(b,\gamma),b,\gamma\}=0,
\qquad
L(b,\gamma)=\phi\{z(b,\gamma),b,\gamma\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is precisely the regular legal-candidate regime singled out by \(\mathsf{CERT}\) and the semialgebraic stratification of \(\mathsf{ATLAS}\).

We first derive, rather than assume, the derivative formula.  Put \(h=(h_b,h_\gamma)\) and \(\Delta z=z(b_0+h_b,\gamma_0+h_\gamma)-z_0\).  Analyticity and the first identity in (1) give
\[
0=F_z\Delta z+F_bh_b+F_\gamma h_\gamma
+o\bigl(\|\Delta z\|+\|h\|\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]
Because \(F_z\) is invertible, there is \(c>0\) such that \(\|F_zv\|\geq c\|v\|\) for every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\).  Applying this inequality to (2) and shrinking the neighborhood so that the remainder coefficient multiplying \(\|\Delta z\|\) is at most \(c/2\) yields \(\|\Delta z\|=O(\|h\|)\).  Substitution back into (2), followed by multiplication by \(F_z^{-1}\), gives
\[
\Delta z=-F_z^{-1}F_bh_b-F_z^{-1}F_\gamma h_\gamma+o(\|h\|).
\tag{3}
\]
Expanding the second identity in (1) and using (3) gives, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(b_0+h_b,\gamma_0+h_\gamma)-L(b_0,\gamma_0)
&=\phi_z\Delta z+\phi_bh_b+\phi_\gamma h_\gamma+o(\|h\|)\\
&=\bigl(\phi_b-\phi_zF_z^{-1}F_b\bigr)h_b
+\bigl(\phi_\gamma-\phi_zF_z^{-1}F_\gamma\bigr)h_\gamma
+o(\|h\|)\\
&=\dot L_bh_b+\dot L_\gamma h_\gamma+o(\|h\|).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(L\) is differentiable on this branch with the derivatives claimed in the statement.

The assumed asymptotically linear representation implies \(\widehat b_n-b_0=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Taking \(h_b=\widehat b_n-b_0\) and \(h_\gamma=0\) in (4), and recalling that \(L(b_0,\gamma_0)=0\), gives
\[
\sqrt nL(\widehat b_n,\gamma_0)
=\dot L_b\sqrt n(\widehat b_n-b_0)+o_P(1)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \dot L_bZ.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)\), the scalar \(\dot L_bZ\) is centered normal with variance
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\dot L_bZ)=\dot L_b\Sigma\dot L_b^{\mathsf T}=\sigma_L^2,
\tag{6}
\]
which proves the endpoint limit.

It remains to derive the frontier displacement.  By definition of \(\nu_0\),
\[
\dot L_\gamma\nu_0=\|\dot L_\gamma\|>0.
\tag{7}
\]
Continuity of the branch derivative therefore makes the scalar map \(t\mapsto L(b,\gamma_0+t\nu_0)\) strictly increasing for \((b,t)\) sufficiently close to \((b_0,0)\).  Equation (5) shows that its value at \(t=0\) is \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Applying (4) at \(t=\pm M_n n^{-1/2}\), where \(M_n\to\infty\) and \(M_n=o(\sqrt n)\), shows with probability tending to one that the two values have opposite signs.  Continuity and strict monotonicity consequently give a unique root \(t_n\) between them.  The mean-value bound following from (7) first gives \(t_n=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Using \(h_b=\widehat b_n-b_0\) and \(h_\gamma=t_n\nu_0\) in (4) then gives
\[
0=\dot L_b(\widehat b_n-b_0)+\|\dot L_\gamma\|t_n+o_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{8}
\]
Multiplying (8) by \(\sqrt n\), solving for \(\sqrt n t_n\), and applying (5) yields
\[
\sqrt n\,t_n
=-\frac{\dot L_b\sqrt n(\widehat b_n-b_0)}{\|\dot L_\gamma\|}+o_P(1)
\ \Longrightarrow\
-\frac{\dot L_bZ}{\|\dot L_\gamma\|}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (6) and (9) give variance \(\sigma_L^2/\|\dot L_\gamma\|^2\) and hence standard-error constant \(\sigma_L/\|\nabla_\gamma L\|\).  When \(\sigma_L>0\), consistency of the three plug-in quantities and the continuous mapping theorem give the stated pointwise normal interval.  Replacing the active minimum by the active maximum changes none of (1)--(9), proving the assertion for \(R\).

Let \(b=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{J-1},(p_x,r_{0,x},r_{1,x})_{x\in\mathcal X})\) be a nonredundant observed-summary chart and let \(\gamma=(\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\).  The handle \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{inf}}\) takes an asymptotically linear estimator \(\widehat b_n\), the legal active equations \(F(z,b,\gamma)=0\) and objective \(\phi(z,b,\gamma)\) used by \(\mathsf{CERT}\) or selected on a stratum of \(\mathsf{ATLAS}\), and a covariance estimator.  On a unique legal branch with invertible \(F_z\), nonzero denominators, strict inactive inequalities, strict objective separation, and \(\nabla_\gamma L\neq0\), it computes
\[
\dot L_b=\phi_b-\phi_zF_z^{-1}F_b,
\qquad
\dot L_\gamma=\phi_\gamma-\phi_zF_z^{-1}F_\gamma,
\qquad
\sigma_L^2=\dot L_b\Sigma\dot L_b^{\mathsf T},
\]
and reports the pointwise normal-displacement standard-error constant \(\sigma_L/\|\dot L_\gamma\|\); it applies the analogous formulas to \(R\).  At a finite tie it records the minimum, or maximum, of the tied branch derivatives as the pointwise directional derivative.  It makes no claim of uniform coverage at optimizer ties, legal-root collisions, positive-dimensional stationary continua, or higher-order algebraic contacts; constructing such a procedure and determining its local Hausdorff law remain the singular-frontier open problem.

Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}regular\text{-}tipping\text{-}inference}\) proves the \(k=1\) unique-branch law
\[
\sqrt n\,t_n\Longrightarrow
-\frac{\dot L_bZ}{\|\nabla_\gamma L\|},
\qquad
\operatorname{se}_{\sqrt n}(t_n)=\frac{\sigma_L}{\|\nabla_\gamma L\|}.
\]
For finitely many analytic branches \(L_j\) tied at a fixed point, the elementary finite-minimum expansion gives \(L'(h)=\min_{j\in I}\nabla L_j^{\mathsf T}h\), with a maximum in place of a minimum for an upper endpoint.  For one fixed scalar germ \(L(b_0,\gamma_0+t\nu)=ct^k+o(|t|^k)\), \(c\neq0\), balanced against an \(n^{-1/2}\) stochastic perturbation, the only possible nondegenerate displacement scale is \(n^{-1/(2k)}\), and any pointwise candidate limit must solve \(cs^k+G=0\).  These pointwise calculations do not establish uniform Hausdorff coverage, select among several moving tied germs, or handle disappearance of real roots when \(k\) is even.
