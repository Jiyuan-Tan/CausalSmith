Throughout, \(1\leq\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1<\infty\), as required by the declared parameter spaces.  Write \(V=(Y(0),Y(1))\), and define \(\operatorname{logit}(s)=\log\{s/(1-s)\}\) for \(0<s<1\).

First let \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal M^{\circ}(P_{\mathrm{obs}},\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\).  Strict positivity in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}positive\text{-}common\text{-}u\text{-}class}\) makes every conditional probability below an element of \((0,1)\).  Define
\[
e_{u,x}=\widetilde P(A=1\mid U=u,X=x),
\qquad
b_x=\operatorname{logit}(e_{0,x}),
\]
and
\[
\alpha_x=\operatorname{logit}(e_{1,x})-
          \operatorname{logit}(e_{0,x}).
\]
Because \(u\in\{0,1\}\), these definitions give the binary-logistic coordinate identity
\[
\operatorname{logit}\widetilde P(A=1\mid U=u,X=x)
=b_x+\alpha_xu.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Put \(s_{u,x}=\widetilde P(U=u,X=x)>0\).  Then the definition of \(\zeta\) gives
\[
\zeta_{1,u,x}=s_{u,x}e_{u,x},
\qquad
\zeta_{0,u,x}=s_{u,x}(1-e_{u,x}).                         \tag{2}
\]
Cancelling the positive factor \(s_{0,x}s_{1,x}\) in (2) yields
\[
\frac{\zeta_{1,1,x}\zeta_{0,0,x}}
     {\zeta_{1,0,x}\zeta_{0,1,x}}
=\frac{e_{1,x}(1-e_{0,x})}{e_{0,x}(1-e_{1,x})}
=\exp(\alpha_x).                                          \tag{3}
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}treatment\text{-}odds\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}treatment\text{-}odds\text{-}lower}\), together with positivity of the denominator in (3), are therefore equivalent to
\[
\Gamma_A^{-1}\leq\exp(\alpha_x)\leq\Gamma_A,
\]
and monotonicity of the logarithm makes this equivalent to
\[
|\alpha_x|\leq\log\Gamma_A.                              \tag{4}
\]

For the outcome coordinates, use the declared risks
\(\mu_{a,u,x}=\widetilde P\{Y(a)=1\mid U=u,X=x\}\) and define
\[
c_{a,x}=\operatorname{logit}(\mu_{a,0,x}),
\qquad
\delta_{a,x}=\operatorname{logit}(\mu_{a,1,x})-
              \operatorname{logit}(\mu_{a,0,x}).
\]
Again because \(u\) is binary,
\[
\operatorname{logit}\widetilde P\{Y(a)=1\mid U=u,X=x\}
=c_{a,x}+\delta_{a,x}u.                                   \tag{5}
\]
The definition of \(\eta\) gives
\[
\eta_{a,1,u,x}=s_{u,x}\mu_{a,u,x},
\qquad
\eta_{a,0,u,x}=s_{u,x}(1-\mu_{a,u,x}).                   \tag{6}
\]
Cancelling \(s_{0,x}s_{1,x}>0\) in (6) gives
\[
\frac{\eta_{a,1,1,x}\eta_{a,0,0,x}}
     {\eta_{a,1,0,x}\eta_{a,0,1,x}}
=\frac{\mu_{a,1,x}(1-\mu_{a,0,x})}
       {\mu_{a,0,x}(1-\mu_{a,1,x})}
=\exp(\delta_{a,x}).                                     \tag{7}
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}outcome\text{-}odds\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}outcome\text{-}odds\text{-}lower}\) are consequently equivalent, on the positive chart, to
\[
|\delta_{a,x}|\leq\log\Gamma_a.                          \tag{8}
\]

Conversely, suppose a strictly positive full-data law satisfies the observed-margin, consistency, and latent-ignorability conditions in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}positive\text{-}common\text{-}u\text{-}class}\), and admits (1) and (5) with (4) and (8).  Reversing (3) and (7) proves all four weak cross-product inequalities in that definition.  The law therefore belongs to \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\).  The two implications prove the asserted union equality.  The intercepts \(b_x\) and \(c_{a,x}\) may range over \(\mathbb R\), subject only to the requirement that, together with the law of \((X,U)\) and a strictly positive conditional coupling of \(V\), the resulting law obey the fixed observed margin.  Thus this is an internal reparameterization of the maintained class.

We next prove the closure assertion without assigning an odds ratio at a null cell.  Let \(C\subseteq\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}\) be the set of nonnegative full-data tables satisfying the observed-margin and consistency restrictions, the following division-free form of latent ignorability,
\[
\widetilde P(v,a,u,x)\widetilde P(u,x)
=\widetilde P(v,u,x)\widetilde P(a,u,x)                  \tag{9}
\]
for every \(v\in\{0,1\}^2\), \(a,u\in\{0,1\}\), and \(x\in\mathcal X\), and the four declared weak joint-cell inequalities in \(\zeta\) and \(\eta\).  Here the observed-margin and consistency restrictions state that the pushforward under
\[
(x,a,u,y_0,y_1)\longmapsto(x,a,ay_1+(1-a)y_0)
\]
equals \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  These pushforward restrictions are linear equalities in the full-table cells, (9) is a finite family of polynomial equalities, and the inequalities in \(\zeta\) and \(\eta\) are polynomial weak inequalities because \(\zeta\) and \(\eta\) are linear marginalizations of the full table.  Hence \(C\) is closed in the finite-dimensional simplex.  Every law in \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\) lies in \(C\), so Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}common\text{-}u\text{-}class}\) gives
\[
\mathcal M
=\operatorname{cl}_{\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}}\mathcal M^{\circ}
\subseteq C.                                              \tag{10}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, fix \(\widetilde P\in C\).  We construct strictly positive laws in the maintained class converging cellwise to \(\widetilde P\).  Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  By the declared support conditions, \(\lambda_x>0\), \(0<p_x<1\), and \(0<r_{a,x}<1\).  Thus
\[
w_{a,x}=\widetilde P(U=1\mid A=a,X=x)
\]
is well defined, and the treatment cells are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\zeta_{1,1,x}&=\lambda_xp_xw_{1,x},
&\zeta_{1,0,x}&=\lambda_xp_x(1-w_{1,x}),\\
\zeta_{0,1,x}&=\lambda_x(1-p_x)w_{0,x},
&\zeta_{0,0,x}&=\lambda_x(1-p_x)(1-w_{0,x}).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{11}
\]
Cancelling \(\lambda_x^2p_x(1-p_x)>0\) in the two treatment inequalities gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{1,x}(1-w_{0,x})
&\leq\Gamma_Aw_{0,x}(1-w_{1,x}),\\
w_{0,x}(1-w_{1,x})
&\leq\Gamma_Aw_{1,x}(1-w_{0,x}).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{12}
\]
Because \(\Gamma_A<\infty\), (12) implies the exhaustive alternative
\[
0<w_{0,x},w_{1,x}<1,
\qquad (w_{0,x},w_{1,x})=(0,0),
\qquad\text{or}\qquad(w_{0,x},w_{1,x})=(1,1).            \tag{13}
\]
Indeed, setting either coordinate equal to zero in the inequality having the other coordinate on its left forces both to be zero; setting either coordinate equal to one in the other inequality forces both to be one.  All remaining pairs are interior.

Consider first the interior case in (13).  Both values of \(U\) then have positive probability given \(X=x\), and every \((A,U,X=x)\)-cell is positive.  Equation (9) implies that the conditional table
\[
g_{u,x}(y_0,y_1)
=\widetilde P\{V=(y_0,y_1)\mid U=u,X=x\}
\]
is also the conditional table of \(V\) given \((A=a,U=u,X=x)\), for either \(a\).  Define its armwise marginals by
\[
\mu_{a,u,x}=\sum_{y_0,y_1}y_a g_{u,x}(y_0,y_1).
\]
Consistency, the observed-margin restriction, and (9) imply
\[
r_{a,x}=(1-w_{a,x})\mu_{a,0,x}+w_{a,x}\mu_{a,1,x}.       \tag{14}
\]
Writing \(\rho_{u,x}=\widetilde P(U=u\mid X=x)>0\), the outcome cells are
\[
\eta_{a,1,u,x}=\lambda_x\rho_{u,x}\mu_{a,u,x},
\qquad
\eta_{a,0,u,x}=\lambda_x\rho_{u,x}(1-\mu_{a,u,x}).      \tag{15}
\]
After cancelling \(\lambda_x^2\rho_{0,x}\rho_{1,x}>0\), the two outcome inequalities become
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{a,1,x}(1-\mu_{a,0,x})
&\leq\Gamma_a\mu_{a,0,x}(1-\mu_{a,1,x}),\\
\mu_{a,0,x}(1-\mu_{a,1,x})
&\leq\Gamma_a\mu_{a,1,x}(1-\mu_{a,0,x}).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) and \(0<r_{a,x}<1\) imply
\[
0<\mu_{a,0,x},\mu_{a,1,x}<1.                             \tag{17}
\]
To verify the lower inequalities in (17), if either risk in (16) were zero, the inequality with the other risk on its left would force that other risk to be zero, contradicting (14).  For the upper inequalities, if either risk were one, the inequality with the complementary factor on its right would force the other risk to be one, again contradicting (14).

Let \(h_{u,x}\) be the product-Bernoulli table having the same two marginals as \(g_{u,x}\):
\[
h_{u,x}(y_0,y_1)
=\prod_{a=0}^1
 \mu_{a,u,x}^{y_a}(1-\mu_{a,u,x})^{1-y_a}.               \tag{18}
\]
By (17), every cell of \(h_{u,x}\) is positive.  For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), set
\[
g_{u,x}^{(\varepsilon)}
=(1-\varepsilon)g_{u,x}+\varepsilon h_{u,x}.             \tag{19}
\]
This table is strictly positive and has the same two Bernoulli marginals as \(g_{u,x}\).  Keep the \((A,U,X)\)-table fixed and use \(g_{u,x}^{(\varepsilon)}\) as the conditional law of \(V\) for both treatment values.  This preserves (9), the observed binary outcome margins through (14), and every \(\zeta\)- and \(\eta\)-inequality through (11) and (15), while making every full-data cell in this stratum positive.

It remains to treat a constant-confounder case in (13); write its constant value as \(c\in\{0,1\}\).  Since both treatment arms have positive probability and (9) holds, there is a common conditional table
\[
g_{c,x}(y_0,y_1)
=\widetilde P\{V=(y_0,y_1)\mid U=c,X=x\},
\]
whose arm-\(a\) marginal equals \(r_{a,x}\), by consistency and the observed-margin restriction.  Let
\[
h_x(y_0,y_1)
=\prod_{a=0}^1 r_{a,x}^{y_a}(1-r_{a,x})^{1-y_a}.          \tag{20}
\]
Every cell of \(h_x\) is positive.  For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), define
\[
\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}(U=c\mid A=a,X=x)=1-\varepsilon,
\qquad
\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}(U=1-c\mid A=a,X=x)=\varepsilon, \tag{21}
\]
and set
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}}(V\mid U=c,X=x)
&=(1-\varepsilon)g_{c,x}+\varepsilon h_x,\\
\mathcal L_{\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}}(V\mid U=1-c,X=x)
&=h_x,
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{22}
\]
using each conditional law in (22) for both values of \(A\).  Equations (20)--(22) make every full-data cell positive and impose (9).  Both conditional tables in (22) have arm-\(a\) risk \(r_{a,x}\), so the observed margin is unchanged.  Equation (21) makes the treatment cross-product ratio equal to one, and equality of the armwise risks across the two values of \(U\) makes every outcome cross-product ratio equal to one.  Since each budget is at least one, all four weak inequalities hold.  Moreover, (21)--(22) converge cellwise to the original constant-\(U\) table as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

Apply (19) in every interior stratum and (21)--(22) in every constant-\(U\) stratum.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal X\) lets these constructions be combined, with the masses \(\lambda_x\) and the \((A,X)\)-margin fixed, into one law \(\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}\).  The binary outcome risks \(r_{a,x}\), propensities \(p_x\), and stratum masses \(\lambda_x\) characterize the observed law, so the observed-margin restriction is exact.  Every full-data cell is positive, and the law satisfies consistency, latent ignorability, and the four declared odds inequalities.  Therefore
\[
\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}
\in\mathcal M^{\circ}(P_{\mathrm{obs}},\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1),
\qquad
\widetilde P^{(\varepsilon)}\longrightarrow\widetilde P
\quad\text{cellwise}.                                    \tag{23}
\]
Thus \(C\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}}\mathcal M^{\circ}=\mathcal M\).  Together with (10), this proves \(C=\mathcal M\).  Every boundary step used only polynomial equalities and weak joint-cell inequalities, so no conditional odds ratio was evaluated at a null cell.  Finally, because the argument compares no distinct historical model class, it establishes neither equality nor inclusion with any class considered in Rosenbaum--Rubin Sections 2--4, exactly as claimed.
