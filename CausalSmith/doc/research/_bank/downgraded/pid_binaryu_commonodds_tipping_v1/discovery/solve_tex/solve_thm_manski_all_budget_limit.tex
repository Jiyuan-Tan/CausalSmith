%%% FIELD proof-manski-all-budget-limit
Let \(n\mapsto(\Gamma_{A,n},\Gamma_{0,n},\Gamma_{1,n})\) be a sequence in \([1,\infty)^3\) such that every coordinate tends to infinity, and let \(L_n,R_n\) denote the functionals in \(\mathrm{def{:}common\text{-}u\text{-}endpoints}\) evaluated at these budgets.  We prove convergence of the two endpoints.

First we derive a budget-free outer bound by worst-case completion of the two unobserved treatment-response means.  Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  For a compatible full-data law put
\[
 s_{1,x}=\widetilde E\{Y(1)\mid A=0,X=x\},
 \qquad
 s_{0,x}=\widetilde E\{Y(0)\mid A=1,X=x\}.
\]
The conditions \(0<p_x<1\) and \(\lambda_x>0\) make these conditional expectations well defined, and binary outcomes imply \(0\leq s_{a,x}\leq1\).  Observed-law compatibility and consistency give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde E\{Y(1)\mid X=x\}
   &=p_xr_{1,x}+(1-p_x)s_{1,x},\\
 \widetilde E\{Y(0)\mid X=x\}
   &=(1-p_x)r_{0,x}+p_xs_{0,x}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
 b_x^-:=p_xr_{1,x}-(1-p_x)r_{0,x}-p_x
 \leq \widetilde E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x\}
 \leq p_xr_{1,x}-(1-p_x)r_{0,x}+1-p_x=:b_x^+.
 \tag{1}
\]
The observed-margin and consistency restrictions are closed equalities in the finite-dimensional cell probabilities.  Thus (1) also holds for every law in the closure \(\mathcal M\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}common\text{-}u\text{-}class}\).  Since \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}common\text{-}u\text{-}envelope}\) identifies \([L_n,R_n]\) with the exact image of \(\tau\) over that closed class, weighting (1) by \(\lambda_x\) and summing over the finite set \(\mathcal X\) yields
\[
 B^-:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\lambda_xb_x^-
 \leq L_n\leq R_n\leq
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\lambda_xb_x^+=:B^+.
 \tag{2}
\]

We next give feasible common-confounder sequences approaching both inequalities in (2).  Define
\[
 \varepsilon_n=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\Gamma_{A,n}}},
 \qquad
 w_{1,x}^{(n)}=1-\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad
 w_{0,x}^{(n)}=\varepsilon_n
 \tag{3}
\]
for every \(x\).  These prevalences lie in \((0,1)\), converge respectively to one and zero, and satisfy
\[
 \frac{w_{1,x}^{(n)}(1-w_{0,x}^{(n)})}
      {w_{0,x}^{(n)}(1-w_{1,x}^{(n)})}
 =\left(\frac{1-\varepsilon_n}{\varepsilon_n}\right)^2
 =\Gamma_{A,n}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Hence (3) belongs to \(D_{A,x}\): the upper treatment-odds inequality is an equality and the lower one follows from \(\Gamma_{A,n}^{-1}\leq\Gamma_{A,n}\).  Its marginal confounder prevalence is
\[
 \pi_x^{(n)}=p_x(1-\varepsilon_n)+(1-p_x)\varepsilon_n\longrightarrow p_x.
 \tag{5}
\]
Bayes' formula, whose denominators are positive by (3) and \(0<p_x<1\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde P_n(A=1\mid U=1,X=x)
 &=\frac{p_x(1-\varepsilon_n)}
         {p_x(1-\varepsilon_n)+(1-p_x)\varepsilon_n}
 \longrightarrow1,\\
 \widetilde P_n(A=1\mid U=0,X=x)
 &=\frac{p_x\varepsilon_n}
         {p_x\varepsilon_n+(1-p_x)(1-\varepsilon_n)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This verifies explicitly the asserted loss of latent treatment positivity.

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), a positive number \(\vartheta\), and the posterior prevalence \(w_{a,x}^{(n)}\), set
\[
 m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)
   =\kappa(r_{a,x},w_{a,x}^{(n)},\vartheta),
 \qquad
 m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)
   =\frac{\vartheta m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)}
          {1+(\vartheta-1)m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Binary-mixture inversion, as incorporated in the sharp-envelope result, shows that both risks are in \((0,1)\) and that
\[
 r_{a,x}=(1-w_{a,x}^{(n)})m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)
          +w_{a,x}^{(n)}m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta),
 \qquad
 \frac{m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)}
      {1-m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)}
 =\vartheta
  \frac{m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)}
       {1-m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta)}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The positivity of the four denominators in (8) follows from the interior-risk conclusion.

We now establish the needed limits directly from (8).  If \(w_{a,x}^{(n)}\to1\), then
\[
 \left|m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta_n)-r_{a,x}\right|
 =(1-w_{a,x}^{(n)})
  \left|m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta_n)
        -m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta_n)\right|
 \leq1-w_{a,x}^{(n)}.
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(w_{a,x}^{(n)}\to0\), the analogous identity gives
\[
 \left|m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}(\vartheta_n)-r_{a,x}\right|
 \leq w_{a,x}^{(n)}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Because \(r_{a,x}\in(0,1)\), equations (8)--(10), continuity of the odds map on \((0,1)\), and divergence of the outcome budgets imply that on the lower branch \(\vartheta_{a,n}=\Gamma_{a,n}\),
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 m_{1,1,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{1,n})\longrightarrow r_{1,x},
 &m_{1,0,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{1,n})\longrightarrow0,\\
 m_{0,0,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{0,n})\longrightarrow r_{0,x},
 &m_{0,1,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{0,n})\longrightarrow1.
\end{array}
\tag{11}
\]
For example, the first limit is (9), and then the second odds in (8) equal the first odds divided by \(\Gamma_{1,n}\), which tends to zero.  The control-arm limits follow from (10), after which multiplication of the limiting positive finite odds by \(\Gamma_{0,n}\) makes the other odds diverge.  On the upper branch \(\vartheta_{a,n}=\Gamma_{a,n}^{-1}\), the same calculation gives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 m_{1,1,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{1,n}^{-1})\longrightarrow r_{1,x},
 &m_{1,0,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{1,n}^{-1})\longrightarrow1,\\
 m_{0,0,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{0,n}^{-1})\longrightarrow r_{0,x},
 &m_{0,1,x}^{(n)}(\Gamma_{0,n}^{-1})\longrightarrow0.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to verify that these algebraic branches are attained by compatible laws.  For each \(n\), each of the two branches, and every stratum, define the \((X,A,U)\) margin by
\[
 \widetilde P_n(X=x,A=a,U=u)
 =\lambda_xp_x^a(1-p_x)^{1-a}
  \{w_{a,x}^{(n)}\}^{u}\{1-w_{a,x}^{(n)}\}^{1-u}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Conditionally on \((X=x,U=u)\), let \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) be independent Bernoulli variables with the respective success probabilities in (7), use the same conditional law for both values of \(A\), and set \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\).  Every cell of this full-data law is strictly positive because \(\lambda_x>0\), \(0<p_x<1\), (3) is interior, and all risks in (7) lie in \((0,1)\).  The construction makes \((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid(X,U)\) and enforces consistency.  The mixture equality in (8) reproduces each observed risk \(r_{a,x}\), so the binary observed margin is \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Equation (4) verifies both treatment-odds restrictions.  In the outcome cross-product ratio, the factors \(\lambda_x\pi_x^{(n)}\) and \(\lambda_x(1-\pi_x^{(n)})\) cancel, leaving exactly the conditional-risk odds ratio in (8), which is either \(\Gamma_{a,n}\) or \(\Gamma_{a,n}^{-1}\); thus both outcome-odds restrictions hold.  Consequently each constructed law belongs to \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\).

Under either branch its adjusted arm mean satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde E_n\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}-r_{a,x}
 &=\bigl[(1-\pi_x^{(n)})m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}
          +\pi_x^{(n)}m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}\bigr]\\
 &\quad-\bigl[(1-w_{a,x}^{(n)})m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}
          +w_{a,x}^{(n)}m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}\bigr]\\
 &=(\pi_x^{(n)}-w_{a,x}^{(n)})
   (m_{a,1,x}^{(n)}-m_{a,0,x}^{(n)}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
which is precisely the adjusted-arm map used by \(\mathrm{def{:}common\text{-}u\text{-}endpoints}\).  Combining (5), (11), and the first line of (14), the lower-branch stratum contrast tends to
\[
 p_xr_{1,x}-\{(1-p_x)r_{0,x}+p_x\}=b_x^-.
 \tag{15}
\]
Combining (5), (12), and (14), the upper-branch stratum contrast tends to
\[
 \{p_xr_{1,x}+1-p_x\}-(1-p_x)r_{0,x}=b_x^+.
 \tag{16}
\]
The construction is simultaneous over the finite support \(\mathcal X\), so its stratum limits may be weighted by \(\lambda_x\) and summed.  Let \(\widetilde P_n^-\) and \(\widetilde P_n^+\) denote the resulting lower- and upper-branch laws.  Their feasibility and exact sharpness give
\[
 B^-\leq L_n\leq\tau(\widetilde P_n^- )\longrightarrow B^-,
 \qquad
 B^+\geq R_n\geq\tau(\widetilde P_n^+ )\longrightarrow B^+.
\]
Thus \(L_n\to B^-\) and \(R_n\to B^+\).  Endpoint convergence is equivalent to Hausdorff convergence of these compact intervals, proving
\[
 [L_n,R_n]\longrightarrow
 \left[
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\lambda_x\{p_xr_{1,x}-(1-p_x)r_{0,x}-p_x\},
 \ \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\lambda_x\{p_xr_{1,x}-(1-p_x)r_{0,x}+1-p_x\}
 \right].
\]
The proof uses divergence of both outcome budgets in (11)--(12), divergence of the treatment budget in (3)--(4), and the vanishing latent propensities in (6).  It therefore establishes only the stated all-budget, vanishing-positivity limit and makes no finite-budget recovery claim.
