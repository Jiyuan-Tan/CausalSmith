%%% FIELD one-arc-statement
Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\) and finite budgets \(1\leq\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1<\infty\).  When \(\Gamma_A>1\), define
\[
 W_{\Gamma_A}(t)=\frac{\Gamma_A t}{1+(\Gamma_A-1)t},\; 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(1<\Gamma_a<\infty\), let
\[
 z_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)=\kappa(r_{a,x},w,\Gamma_a),\;
 \Delta_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)=
 \frac{\Gamma_a z_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)}
      {1+(\Gamma_a-1)z_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)}-z_{a,\Gamma_a}(w),
\]
and set \(\Delta_{a,1}(w)=0\).  For \(0<w<1\) and \(\Gamma_a>1\),
\[
 -\frac{\Delta_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)}{w}
 <\frac{d}{dw}\Delta_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)
 <\frac{\Delta_{a,\Gamma_a}(w)}{1-w}.
\]
If \(\Gamma_A>1\) and \((\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\neq(1,1)\), then on \(0<w_1<w_0<1\), with \(e=w_0-w_1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_x^+(w_0,w_1)
 &=r_{1,x}-r_{0,x}
 +e\{(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(w_1)
       +p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(w_0)\},\\
H_x^-(w_0,w_1)
 &=r_{1,x}-r_{0,x}
 -e\{(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(1-w_1)
       +p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(1-w_0)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently \(H_x^+\) is strictly increasing in \(w_0\) and strictly decreasing in \(w_1\), whereas \(H_x^-\) is strictly decreasing in \(w_0\) and strictly increasing in \(w_1\).  Neither relevant extremum over \(D_{A,x}\) has a two-dimensional interior optimizer, every non-point-identified optimizer saturates a treatment-odds inequality, complementing \(U\) maps every forward-arc optimizer to a reverse-arc optimizer with the same objective value, and
\[
 \ell_x=\min_{0\leq t\leq1}H_x^-(W_{\Gamma_A}(t),t),
 \;
 \upsilon_x=\max_{0\leq t\leq1}H_x^+(W_{\Gamma_A}(t),t).
\]
If \(\Gamma_A=1\) or \(\Gamma_0=\Gamma_1=1\), then instead
\(\ell_x=\upsilon_x=r_{1,x}-r_{0,x}\).

%%% FIELD one-arc-proof
Fix the stratum and suppress its index.  The proof uses implicit differentiation, strict coordinate monotonicity, and the involution that relabels the two states of \(U\).

First take \(r\in(0,1)\), \(\vartheta>1\), and \(0<w<1\).  Put
\[
 f(z)=\frac{\vartheta z}{1+(\vartheta-1)z},
 \; z(w)=\kappa(r,w,\vartheta),
 \; \Delta(w)=f\{z(w)\}-z(w).
 \tag{16}
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}mixture\text{-}inversion}\) gives \(0<z(w)<f\{z(w)\}<1\) and
\[
 r=(1-w)z(w)+wf\{z(w)\}=z(w)+w\Delta(w).
 \tag{17}
\]
Because
\[
 f'(z)=\frac{\vartheta}{\{1+(\vartheta-1)z\}^2}>0,
 \;
 D(w)=1-w+wf'\{z(w)\}>0,
 \tag{18}
\]
the derivative of the left side of the mixture equation with respect to \(z\) is nonzero.  Implicit differentiation is therefore legal and yields
\[
 z'(w)=-\frac{\Delta(w)}{D(w)},
 \;
 \Delta'(w)=\{f'\{z(w)\}-1\}z'(w).
 \tag{19}
\]
Combining (18)--(19), equation by equation, gives
\[
 \Delta(w)+w\Delta'(w)=\frac{\Delta(w)}{D(w)}>0,
 \;
 \Delta(w)-(1-w)\Delta'(w)
 =\frac{\Delta(w)f'\{z(w)\}}{D(w)}>0.
 \tag{20}
\]
Division in (20) is valid because \(0<w<1\), and it proves
\[
 -\frac{\Delta(w)}{w}<\Delta'(w)
 <\frac{\Delta(w)}{1-w}.
 \tag{21}
\]
This proves the asserted derivative inequalities for every nontrivial arm budget.  The convention \(\Delta_{a,1}=0\) covers a trivial arm without applying the strict inequalities to it.

We next record the relabeling identity.  For a posterior pair \(d=(w_0,w_1)\), define
\[
 d^c=(1-w_0,1-w_1),\quad \pi^c=1-\pi.
 \tag{22}
\]
If \((m_0,m_1)\) is the unique feasible risk pair at mixture weight \(w_a\) and odds ratio \(\vartheta\), then \((m_1,m_0)\) is feasible at weight \(1-w_a\) and odds ratio \(\vartheta^{-1}\).  Indeed,
\[
 w_am_1+(1-w_a)m_0=r_{a,x},
 \;
 \operatorname{OR}(m_0,m_1)=\vartheta^{-1}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Uniqueness in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}mixture\text{-}inversion}\) therefore identifies this as the pair used by the adjusted-arm map.  Since
\[
 \pi^c-(1-w_a)=-(\pi-w_a),
 \; m_0-m_1=-(m_1-m_0),
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}adjusted\text{-}arm\text{-}mean}\) gives
\[
 q_{a,x}(d^c;\vartheta^{-1})=q_{a,x}(d;\vartheta).
 \tag{24}
\]
The endpoint set \(\{\Gamma_a^{-1},\Gamma_a\}\) is invariant under reciprocation.  Hence
\[
 q_{a,x}^{\pm}(d^c)=q_{a,x}^{\pm}(d),
 \; H_x^{\pm}(d^c)=H_x^{\pm}(d).
 \tag{25}
\]
The two cross-product inequalities defining \(D_{A,x}\) are also exchanged by \(d\mapsto d^c\), so this involution preserves the exposure region and maps its forward half to its reverse half.

Now suppose \(0<w_1<w_0<1\) and put \(e=w_0-w_1\).  The definition of \(\pi\) gives
\[
 \pi-w_1=(1-p_x)e>0,
 \; \pi-w_0=-p_xe<0,
 \tag{26}
\]
where strictness uses the observed overlap condition \(0<p_x<1\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}outcome\text{-}budget\text{-}extrema}\), followed by (24) for the reciprocal tilt, gives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
q^+_{1,x}=r_{1,x}+(1-p_x)e\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(w_1),
&q^-_{1,x}=r_{1,x}-(1-p_x)e\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(1-w_1),\\
q^-_{0,x}=r_{0,x}-p_xe\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(w_0),
&q^+_{0,x}=r_{0,x}+p_xe\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(1-w_0).
\end{array}
\tag{27}
\]
Subtracting the appropriate two entries proves the two displayed formulas in the statement.

Write
\[
 B=(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(w_1)
   +p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(w_0).
\]
At least one summand is strictly positive because \((\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\neq(1,1)\).  Since \(e<w_0\), the lower inequality in (21) gives
\[
 \frac{\partial H_x^+}{\partial w_0}
 =B+ep_x\Delta'_{0,\Gamma_0}(w_0)
 >(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(w_1)
   +p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(w_0)\left(1-\frac e{w_0}\right)>0.
 \tag{28}
\]
Terms belonging to an arm with \(\Gamma_a=1\) are simply zero.  Since \(e<1-w_1\), the upper inequality in (21) similarly gives
\[
 \frac{\partial H_x^+}{\partial w_1}
 =-B+e(1-p_x)\Delta'_{1,\Gamma_1}(w_1)
 <-p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(w_0)
  -(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(w_1)
       \left(1-\frac e{1-w_1}\right)<0.
 \tag{29}
\]

For the lower objective put
\[
 C=(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(1-w_1)
   +p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(1-w_0).
\]
Using respectively \(e<w_0\), the upper inequality in (21) at \(1-w_0\), \(e<1-w_1\), and the lower inequality in (21) at \(1-w_1\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{\partial H_x^-}{\partial w_0}
 &=-C+ep_x\Delta'_{0,\Gamma_0}(1-w_0)\\
 &<-(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(1-w_1)
   -p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(1-w_0)\left(1-\frac e{w_0}\right)<0,\\
 \frac{\partial H_x^-}{\partial w_1}
 &=C+e(1-p_x)\Delta'_{1,\Gamma_1}(1-w_1)\\
 &>p_x\Delta_{0,\Gamma_0}(1-w_0)
   +(1-p_x)\Delta_{1,\Gamma_1}(1-w_1)
       \left(1-\frac e{1-w_1}\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus no relevant optimizer lies in the two-dimensional interior of either half of \(D_{A,x}\).

For an interior reverse-half point, the exposure inequalities are equivalent to
\[
 w_1\leq w_0\leq W_{\Gamma_A}(w_1).
 \tag{31}
\]
If the upper inequality is strict, increasing \(w_0\) to \(W_{\Gamma_A}(w_1)\) remains feasible; by (28) it strictly raises \(H_x^+\), and by (30) it strictly lowers \(H_x^-\).  Hence the upper maximizer and lower minimizer in the reverse half lie on
\[
 (w_0,w_1)=(W_{\Gamma_A}(t),t),\; 0<t<1.
 \tag{32}
\]
Equation (25) carries every forward-half candidate to the reverse half without changing either objective.  On the diagonal and at the constant-confounder corners, \(\pi=w_0=w_1\), so \(H_x^-=H_x^+=r_{1,x}-r_{0,x}\).  For every \(0<t<1\), however, \(W_{\Gamma_A}(t)>t\), and (27) makes \(H_x^+\) strictly larger and \(H_x^-\) strictly smaller than this diagonal value.  Therefore no diagonal point or constant-\(U\) corner is optimal in the non-point-identified case.  Continuity at \(t=0,1\), supplied by Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}closed\text{-}chart\text{-}extension}\), now proves the two one-dimensional endpoint formulas and shows that their optimizers actually have \(0<t<1\).

Finally, if \(\Gamma_A=1\), the two exposure inequalities imply \(w_0=w_1\), whence \(\pi=w_a\) and every adjusted arm mean equals \(r_{a,x}\).  If \(\Gamma_0=\Gamma_1=1\), both outcome tilts equal one, so the two latent risks in each arm are equal and again every adjusted arm mean equals its observed arm risk.  In either case \(\ell_x=\upsilon_x=r_{1,x}-r_{0,x}\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD target-statement
The ATE identified set over the closed class \(\mathcal M(P_{\mathrm{obs}},\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\) is exactly \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{com}}=[L,R]\), with \(L\) and \(R\) defined by one common \((w_{0,x},w_{1,x})\in D_{A,x}\) per stratum.  Every endpoint is attained by a law in \(\mathcal M\): at an interior selected pair the law uses the selected feasible risks as conditional Bernoulli marginals and any coupling of \((Y(0),Y(1))\) given \((X,U)\), while a constant-\(U\) boundary pair is attained as the finite-simplex limit of such strictly positive laws.  Over \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\), \(\inf\tau=L\), \(\sup\tau=R\), and the closure of the identified set is \([L,R]\); endpoint attainment is asserted there only when an attaining law is itself strictly positive.  The result is a theorem about the maintained common-binary-confounder class and does not assert that this bounded class, its arbitrary conditional potential-outcome coupling, or its finite-simplex closure is the class defined in Rosenbaum--Rubin Sections 2--4.

%%% FIELD target-proof
Define the scalar identified set
\[
 \Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\{\tau(\widetilde P):
       \widetilde P\in
       \mathcal M(P_{\mathrm{obs}},\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)\}.
 \tag{33}
\]
Here \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}=\mathcal L(X,A,Y)\) is a law on the finite set \(\mathcal X\times\{0,1\}^2\), \(\widetilde P\) is a law on \(\mathcal X\times\{0,1\}^4\), and \(\tau(\widetilde P)=\widetilde E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}\).  We use the declared support and overlap conditions \(\lambda_x>0\), \(0<p_x<1\), and \(0<r_{a,x}<1\), and finite budgets \(1\leq\Gamma_A,\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1<\infty\).  These conditions justify every conditioning operation below and ensure that every odds tilt and denominator is finite and positive.  No matrix inversion or rank condition is used.

Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\) and first let \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal M^{\circ}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}observed\text{-}margin}\) identifies \(\lambda_x\), \(p_x\), and \(r_{a,x}\) with the corresponding full-law marginals and conditionals.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) then gives
\[
 r_{a,x}=\widetilde P\{Y(a)=1\mid A=a,X=x\}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}latent\text{-}ignorability}\) then removes \(A\) from the conditional potential-outcome risk.  With the definition of \(w_{a,x}\), this yields
\[
 r_{a,x}=(1-w_{a,x})\mu_{a,0,x}+w_{a,x}\mu_{a,1,x}.
 \tag{34}
\]
Bayes' rule is legal because all full-data cells are positive, and gives
\[
 \pi_x=\widetilde P(U=1\mid X=x)
      =p_xw_{1,x}+(1-p_x)w_{0,x}.
 \tag{35}
\]
The four positive \((A,U,X=x)\)-cell probabilities also give
\[
 \frac{\zeta_{1,1,x}\zeta_{0,0,x}}
      {\zeta_{1,0,x}\zeta_{0,1,x}}
 =\frac{w_{1,x}(1-w_{0,x})}
       {w_{0,x}(1-w_{1,x})}.
 \tag{36}
\]
After cancellation of the positive factor \(\lambda_x^2p_x(1-p_x)\), Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}treatment\text{-}odds\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}treatment\text{-}odds\text{-}lower}\) are therefore exactly the condition \((w_{0,x},w_{1,x})\in D_{A,x}\).

On the strictly positive chart, let
\(\theta_{a,x}=\operatorname{OR}(\mu_{a,1,x},\mu_{a,0,x})\).  In each outcome cross product, the positive \((U,X=x)\)-margins cancel.  Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}outcome\text{-}odds\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}outcome\text{-}odds\text{-}lower}\) thus imply
\[
 \Gamma_a^{-1}\leq\theta_{a,x}\leq\Gamma_a.
 \tag{37}
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}mixture\text{-}inversion}\), applied to (34), uniquely identifies
\[
 \mu_{a,0,x}=\kappa(r_{a,x},w_{a,x},\theta_{a,x}),
 \;
 \mu_{a,1,x}
 =\frac{\theta_{a,x}\mu_{a,0,x}}
        {1+(\theta_{a,x}-1)\mu_{a,0,x}}.
 \tag{38}
\]
All quantities in (38) lie strictly between zero and one.  Subtracting (34) from the marginal potential-outcome mean and using (35) gives the exact bias decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde E\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}
 &=(1-\pi_x)\mu_{a,0,x}+\pi_x\mu_{a,1,x}\\
 &=r_{a,x}+(\pi_x-w_{a,x})
      (\mu_{a,1,x}-\mu_{a,0,x})\\
 &=q_{a,x}(w_{0,x},w_{1,x};\theta_{a,x}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
This is a direct algebraic identity, not a definition of the causal mean.

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}outcome\text{-}budget\text{-}extrema}\) optimizes (39) over (37), separately for the two outcome arms but at the same posterior pair.  Hence
\[
 H_x^-(w_{0,x},w_{1,x})
 \leq \widetilde E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x\}
 \leq H_x^+(w_{0,x},w_{1,x}).
 \tag{40}
\]
Minimizing and maximizing over the common feasible pair gives
\[
 \ell_x\leq
 \widetilde E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x\}
 \leq\upsilon_x.
 \tag{41}
\]
Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}one\text{-}arc\text{-}endpoint\text{-}reduction}\) strengthens this computable characterization: outside the two point-identified cases, both extrema in (41) are obtained on the single canonical arc
\[
 (w_{0,x},w_{1,x})=(W_{\Gamma_A}(t),t),\; 0<t<1,
 \tag{42}
\]
and the forward-arc candidates are exactly its images under relabeling \(U\).  If \(\Gamma_A=1\) or \(\Gamma_0=\Gamma_1=1\), it instead gives
\(\ell_x=\upsilon_x=r_{1,x}-r_{0,x}\).

Multiplying (41) by \(\lambda_x>0\), summing over the finite support, and using \(\sum_x\lambda_x=1\) proves
\[
 L\leq\tau(\widetilde P)\leq R,
 \; \widetilde P\in\mathcal M^{\circ}.
 \tag{43}
\]
If \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal M\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}common\text{-}u\text{-}class}\) supplies a sequence \(\widetilde P_k\in\mathcal M^{\circ}\) converging to \(\widetilde P\) in the finite simplex.  The functional \(\tau\) is linear, hence continuous, so (43) passes to the limit.  This proves the outer bound
\[
 \Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq[L,R].
 \tag{44}
\]

We next construct compatible laws and thereby prove sharpness.  In every stratum choose an interior posterior pair \(d_x=(w_{0,x},w_{1,x})\in D_{A,x}\cap(0,1)^2\) and tilts \(\theta_{a,x}\in[\Gamma_a^{-1},\Gamma_a]\).  Define
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \widetilde P(A=1,U=1\mid X=x)=p_xw_{1,x},
 &\widetilde P(A=1,U=0\mid X=x)=p_x(1-w_{1,x}),\\
 \widetilde P(A=0,U=1\mid X=x)=(1-p_x)w_{0,x},
 &\widetilde P(A=0,U=0\mid X=x)=(1-p_x)(1-w_{0,x}).
\end{array}
\tag{45}
\]
Observed overlap and interiority of \(d_x\) make all four entries positive.  Define \(\mu_{a,0,x}\) and \(\mu_{a,1,x}\) by (38).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}mixture\text{-}inversion}\) puts them in \((0,1)\) and makes (34) hold exactly.  Conditional on \((X=x,U=u)\), draw \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) independently as Bernoulli variables with success probabilities \(\mu_{0,u,x}\) and \(\mu_{1,u,x}\), use the same conditional law at both treatment values, and set
\[
 Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0).
 \tag{46}
\]
The conditional construction proves \((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid(X,U)\); it does not assume this relation after the fact.  Equations (34), (45), and (46) reproduce the observed conditional law of \((A,Y)\) in stratum \(x\).  Equation (36), membership in \(D_{A,x}\), and (37) verify the four odds inequalities.  Assigning \(\widetilde P(X=x)=\lambda_x\) therefore gives a law in \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\), and (39) gives its causal arm means.

The parameter set
\[
 \mathcal D_x^{\circ}
 =\{D_{A,x}\cap(0,1)^2\}
   \times[\Gamma_0^{-1},\Gamma_0]
   \times[\Gamma_1^{-1},\Gamma_1]
 \tag{47}
\]
is connected.  Indeed, the coordinatewise logit map sends its posterior-pair factor to the convex strip
\[
 \{(s_0,s_1)\in\mathbb R^2:
      |s_1-s_0|\leq\log\Gamma_A\}.
 \tag{48}
\]
The remaining two factors are intervals.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}closed\text{-}chart\text{-}extension}\) and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}binary\text{-}mixture\text{-}inversion}\) make the contrast map continuous on (47).

The one-arc theorem also shows that the minimum and maximum \(\ell_x,\upsilon_x\) are attained inside (47): in the non-point-identified case its optimizer satisfies \(0<t<1\) in (42), while in either point-identified case one may take \(w_0=w_1=1/2\) and \(\theta_{0,x}=\theta_{1,x}=1\).  A continuous image of a connected set is an interval.  Its image lies in \([\ell_x,\upsilon_x]\) by (40) and contains both endpoints, hence it equals that interval.  The finite product \(\prod_x\mathcal D_x^{\circ}\) is connected, and the continuous weighted-sum map has image
\[
 \left[\sum_x\lambda_x\ell_x,
       \sum_x\lambda_x\upsilon_x\right]=[L,R].
 \tag{49}
\]
Applying the construction (45)--(46) to every point in this product proves the stronger fact
\[
 [L,R]\subseteq\{\tau(\widetilde P):\widetilde P\in\mathcal M^{\circ}\}
 \subseteq\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
 \tag{50}
\]
Together, (44) and (50) establish \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=[L,R]\), and in particular imply the stated infimum, supremum, and closure conclusions for \(\mathcal M^{\circ}\).

For completeness, the boundary assertions follow without assigning an odds ratio at a null cell.  For finite \(\Gamma_A\), substitution in the two exposure inequalities shows that a feasible pair with a zero coordinate is \((0,0)\), and one with a unit coordinate is \((1,1)\).  Approximate these pairs by \((\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\) and \((1-\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\).  They remain in \(D_{A,x}\cap(0,1)^2\), and Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}closed\text{-}chart\text{-}extension}\) gives convergence of all adjusted means.  The corresponding laws from (45)--(46) converge in the finite simplex, so their limits belong to \(\mathcal M\) and attain the corner contrast.  At an interior posterior pair, any coupling of the two Bernoulli marginals may replace conditional independence.  If that coupling has zero cells, mix it with an arbitrarily small amount of the independent coupling; this preserves both Bernoulli marginals, makes every joint outcome cell positive, preserves conditional independence from \(A\), and converges back to the selected coupling.  Thus every such coupling defines a law in the closed class.

All identifying equalities above came from the maintained assumptions and the explicit construction.  Rosenbaum--Rubin is used only as a historical comparator for fixed binary-confounder sensitivity tuples; no equality or inclusion involving its Sections 2--4 class is used or proved.
