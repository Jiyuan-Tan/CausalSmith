%%% FIELD proof-quotient-sharpness
Write a full response type as \(f=(x_{\mathcal Z},y_{\mathcal T},r_{\mathcal C,\mathcal Z})\).  First let \(\pi\) be feasible for \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm F}(p)\), and put \(q=\kappa_{\#}\pi\).  For \(t\in\mathcal T\), \(y\in\mathcal Y\), and \(z\in\mathcal Z\), the definitions of \(A\), \(A_{\mathrm F}\), and \(\kappa\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
(Aq)_{z,(t,y)}
 &=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}q_r\mathbf 1\{x_z(r)=t,\ y_t(r)=y\}\\
 &=\sum_{f\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm F}}\pi_f\mathbf 1\{x_z(f)=t,\ y_t(f)=y\}\\
 &=(A_{\mathrm F}\pi)_{z,(t,y)}=p_z(t,y)=s_z(t,y).
\end{aligned}
\]
The third equality uses target-outcome consistency, while the interpretation of the conditional cell probability uses randomization, exposure consistency, and positive arm probability.  Likewise, because the outcome is binary and exactly one outcome coordinate occurs in each observed off-target cell,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(Aq)_{z,\star}
 &=\sum_f\pi_f\mathbf 1\{x_z(f)\in\mathcal C\}\\
 &=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}(A_{\mathrm F}\pi)_{z,(c,y)}
 =\sum_{c,y}p_z(c,y)=s_z(\star).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(q\in F(s)\).  Since \(\kappa\) does not change \(y_{\mathcal T}\),
\[
(Bq)_y=\sum_f\pi_f\mathbf 1\{y_{\mathcal T}(f)=y\}=(B_{\mathrm F}\pi)_y
\quad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal Y^{\mathcal T}.
\]
This proves \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm F}(p)\subseteq\Theta(s)\).

For the converse, fix \(q\in F(s)\).  Conditional on a reduced atom \(r=((x_z(r))_z,y_{\mathcal T}(r))\), apply the armwise refinement in the right-inverse definition: independently at every coordinate with \(x_z(r)=\star\), draw \((C_z,V_z)\) with probability \(\eta_z(C_z,V_z)\), set \(X_z=C_z\) and \(R_{C_z,z}=V_z\), and fill unused response coordinates by zero.  At a coordinate with \(x_z(r)=t\in\mathcal T\), set \(X_z=t\) and again fill unused off-target responses by zero.  Denote the resulting full-type law by \(\pi=\mathfrak R_p(q)\).  Every refined atom maps back to the atom from which it was drawn, so, coordinate by coordinate,
\[
\kappa_{\#}\mathfrak R_p(q)=q,
\qquad
B_{\mathrm F}\mathfrak R_p(q)=Bq.
\]
For a target cell, no off-target randomization enters, and hence
\[
(A_{\mathrm F}\pi)_{z,(t,y)}
=\sum_rq_r\mathbf 1\{x_z(r)=t,\ y_t(r)=y\}
=(Aq)_{z,(t,y)}=s_z(t,y)=p_z(t,y).
\]
For an off-target cell, the independent refinement instead yields
\[
(A_{\mathrm F}\pi)_{z,(c,y)}
=\left(\sum_rq_r\mathbf 1\{x_z(r)=\star\}\right)\eta_z(c,y)
=s_z(\star)\eta_z(c,y).
\]
If \(s_z(\star)>0\), the definition of \(\eta_z\) makes the last expression equal to \(p_z(c,y)\).  If \(s_z(\star)=0\), nonnegativity and \(Aq=s\) imply that every reduced atom with \(x_z(r)=\star\) has zero \(q\)-mass; also every \(p_z(c,y)=0\).  Thus the same equality holds for every arbitrary boundary allocation.  If \(\mathcal C=\varnothing\), this zero-mass case is automatic and the allocation space is a singleton.

To see attainability as a causal law rather than merely as a table calculation, draw the full response type with law \(\pi\), draw \(Z\) independently with the given strictly positive probabilities \(\lambda_z\), put \(X=X_Z\), and assign \(Y=Y_t\) when \(X=t\in\mathcal T\) and \(Y=R_{c,Z}\) when \(X=c\in\mathcal C\).  This construction satisfies randomization, target exclusion, exposure consistency, target-outcome consistency, and saturated off-target-outcome consistency.  Consequently \(A_{\mathrm F}\pi=p\), and every \(Bq\in\Theta(s)\) is attained.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal I_{\mathrm F}(p)=\Theta(s),
\]
and the equality is sharp, not merely an outer bound.

Finally, a reduced atom has \((|\mathcal T|+1)^m\) possible exposure vectors and \(2^{|\mathcal T|}\) possible target-response vectors.  Hence
\[
|\mathcal R|=(|\mathcal T|+1)^m2^{|\mathcal T|},
\qquad
\dim(\operatorname{codom}A)=md,
\qquad d=2|\mathcal T|+1,
\]
none of which involves \(|\mathcal C|\).  Results 1 and 3 of Gabriel, Sachs, and Sj\"olander \cite{GabrielSachsSjolander2025} concern their specified low-cardinality target contrasts.  The equality above is for every coordinate of \(\operatorname{law}(Y_{\mathcal T})\) and arbitrary finite \(m,\mathcal T,\mathcal C\), so it extends those results along precisely the stated cardinality and target-law axes.  Since the cited statements omit finite configurations allowed here and a complete target law determines all of their displayed contrasts, this extension of scope is strict.

%%% FIELD proof-enumeration-witness
The inclusion \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{common}}\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{sat}}\) follows from the definitions.  It is strict whenever \(m\ge2\) and \(\mathcal C\ne\varnothing\): choose \(c\in\mathcal C\) and distinct arms \(z,z'\), put \(R_{c,z}=0\) and \(R_{c,z'}=1\) almost surely, and take any fixed exposure and target-response type.  After drawing \(Z\) independently with positive probabilities and imposing the declared consistency rules, the resulting law is saturated but cannot have a common \(R_c\).

For the witness, order the saturated coordinates as \((x_1,x_2,y_a,y_b,r_{c,1},r_{c,2})\).  Exhaustive Cartesian-product enumeration gives
\[
3^2\,2^2=36,
\qquad
3^2\,2^2\,2^2=144,
\qquad
3^2\,2^2\,2=72
\]
reduced, saturated, and common-response columns, respectively.  These are exactly the three lists in the response-type certificate; no type is omitted because each factor independently ranges over its complete finite support.

More generally, quotient sharpness maps every saturated full column mixture with any finite off-target alphabet into the same reduced feasible fiber, and its explicit right inverse lifts every point of that fiber back to each requested refinement.  Therefore every target-law support function of the saturated program is exactly the corresponding support function of the 36-column reduced program in this two-arm, two-target case.  This proves equality for every support direction and every finite \(|\mathcal C|\), which is stronger than agreement on a finite collection of randomly selected directions.

In the saturated program, the mixture assigning mass \(1/2\) to each of
\[
(a,a,0,0,0,0),\qquad(c,c,1,0,1,0)
\]
generates \((a,0)/2+(c,1)/2\) in arm 1 and \((a,0)/2+(c,0)/2\) in arm 2, and has
\[
E(Y_b-Y_a)=\tfrac12(0-0)+\tfrac12(0-1)=-\tfrac12.
\]
The mixture assigning mass \(1/2\) to each of
\[
(a,a,0,1,0,0),\qquad(c,c,0,1,1,0)
\]
generates the same observed law and has \(E(Y_b-Y_a)=1\).

These feasible values are the endpoints.  Indeed, binary support gives
\[
\tau=E(Y_b)-E(Y_a)\le1.
\]
The witness cells imply \(P(X_1=a)=P(X_2=a)=1/2\), and target consistency implies \(Y_a=0\) on \(\{X_1=a\}\cup\{X_2=a\}\).  Therefore
\[
P(Y_a=0)\ge P(X_1=a\text{ or }X_2=a)\ge\tfrac12,
\qquad E(Y_a)\le\tfrac12,
\]
and hence
\[
\tau\ge-E(Y_a)\ge-\tfrac12.
\]
Thus the saturated interval is exactly \([-1/2,1]\).

Under the common-response restriction, write a type as \((x_1,x_2,y_a,y_b,r_c)\).  The mixture assigning mass \(1/2\) to each of
\[
(c,a,0,0,1),\qquad(a,c,0,0,0)
\]
generates \(p^{\mathrm w}\) and has \(\tau=0\); changing both displayed \(y_b\)-coordinates to one generates the same observed law and has \(\tau=1\).  To prove optimality, define
\[
E_1=\{X_1=c,R_c=1\},\qquad E_2=\{X_2=c,R_c=0\}.
\]
The two observed off-target cells give \(P(E_1)=P(E_2)=1/2\).  They are disjoint because a common binary \(R_c\) cannot equal both zero and one, so they partition the population up to a null set.  On \(E_1\), the zero probabilities of the arm-2 cells \((b,0),(b,1),(c,1)\) force \(X_2=a\), and the observed arm-2 target cell then forces \(Y_a=0\).  Symmetrically, on \(E_2\) the zero arm-1 cells force \(X_1=a\) and then \(Y_a=0\).  Consequently \(Y_a=0\) almost surely, and binary support gives
\[
0\le\tau=E(Y_b)\le1.
\]
The two mixtures attain both inequalities.  The enumerated linear programs therefore certify exactly \([-1/2,1]\) and \([0,1]\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-experiment-factorization
Fix the arm totals as required by the fixed-arm-count assumption.  Given \((q,\eta)\), let \(s=Aq\).  The quotient refinement and the two consistency assumptions imply that one observation in arm \(z\) has full-cell probabilities
\[
g_z(t,y)=s_z(t,y),\quad t\in\mathcal T,
\qquad
g_z(c,y)=s_z(\star)\eta_z(c,y),\quad c\in\mathcal C.
\]
Their sum is one because \(s_z\in\Delta^{d-1}\) and \(\sum_{c,y}\eta_z(c,y)=1\) when \(\mathcal C\ne\varnothing\).  When \(\mathcal C=\varnothing\), compatibility forces \(s_z(\star)=0\), and the same assertion uses the singleton allocation convention.

Conditional on \(n_z\), arm \(z\) is multinomial.  Its likelihood is therefore
\[
\frac{n_z!}{\prod_{t,y}N_{z,t,y}!\prod_{c,y}N_{z,c,y}!}
\prod_{t,y}s_z(t,y)^{N_{z,t,y}}
\prod_{c,y}\{s_z(\star)\eta_z(c,y)\}^{N_{z,c,y}}.
\]
Using \(S_{z,t,y}=N_{z,t,y}\) and \(S_{z,\star}=\sum_{c,y}N_{z,c,y}\), multiply and divide by \(S_{z,\star}!\).  With \(0^0=1\), the result is exactly
\[
\left[
\frac{n_z!}{\prod_{t,y}S_{z,t,y}!S_{z,\star}!}
\prod_{t,y}s_z(t,y)^{S_{z,t,y}}s_z(\star)^{S_{z,\star}}
\right]
\left[
\frac{S_{z,\star}!}{\prod_{c,y}N_{z,c,y}!}
\prod_{c,y}\eta_z(c,y)^{N_{z,c,y}}
\right]
=L_z(N_z;s_z,\eta_z).
\]
Randomization and conditional sampling make the arm likelihoods multiply, which proves the asserted product factorization.

Variation independence is constructive.  For every \(q\in\Delta^{|\mathcal R|-1}\) and every \(\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathcal C}^m\), apply the armwise refinement with target probabilities \((Aq)_{z,(t,y)}\) and off-target probabilities \((Aq)_{z,\star}\eta_z(c,y)\).  This produces a full experiment with those parameters, including zero-star boundaries.  Hence the parameter space is the stated Cartesian product.

Finally, conditional on a skeleton table \(S=\sigma\), all target counts are fixed and the off-target counts in arm \(z\) have probability
\[
\frac{\sigma_{z,\star}!}{\prod_{c,y}N_{z,c,y}!}
\prod_{c,y}\eta_z(c,y)^{N_{z,c,y}},
\qquad \sum_{c,y}N_{z,c,y}=\sigma_{z,\star}.
\]
The multinomial theorem shows that these probabilities sum to one.  This conditional law involves \(\eta\) but not \(q\).  At \(\sigma_{z,\star}=0\) it is the point mass at the all-zero allocation, so the conclusion also holds on the boundary.

%%% FIELD proof-nuisance-minimax
For fixed finite arm totals and any skeleton table \(\sigma\), each coordinate of a member of \(\Omega(\sigma)\) is an integer between zero and the corresponding \(n_z\).  Hence \(\Omega(\sigma)\) is finite.  By the multinomial theorem applied separately in every arm,
\[
\sum_{\nu\in\Omega(\sigma)}K_{\eta^0}(\nu\mid\sigma)=1,
\qquad K_{\eta^0}(\nu\mid\sigma)\ge0.
\]
Thus \(\delta_0(\sigma)\) is a finite convex combination of points \(\delta(\nu)\in\mathcal A\).  Convexity of \(\mathcal A\) implies \(\delta_0(\sigma)\in\mathcal A\); no closure or limiting argument is needed.

The experiment factorization shows that under \((q,\eta^0)\), the conditional distribution of \(N\) given \(S=\sigma\) is precisely \(K_{\eta^0}(\cdot\mid\sigma)\), independently of \(q\).  Convexity of \(a\mapsto\ell(a,Bq)\) and finite Jensen therefore give, for every \(\sigma\),
\[
\ell(\delta_0(\sigma),Bq)
\le
\sum_{\nu\in\Omega(\sigma)}K_{\eta^0}(\nu\mid\sigma)
\ell(\delta(\nu),Bq).
\]
The marginal distribution of \(S\) depends on \(q\) through \(Aq\) and does not depend on \(\eta\).  Multiplying by this marginal probability and summing over \(\sigma\) gives
\[
R_q(\delta_0)
\le R_{q,\eta^0}(\delta)
\le\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal E_{\mathcal C}^m}R_{q,\eta}(\delta).
\]
The first inequality uses only the conditional law at the fixed point \(\eta^0\); it does not compare the two risks pointwise at an arbitrary \(\eta\).

Conversely, for a skeleton rule \(\phi\), define \(\delta^\phi(\nu)=\phi(S(\nu))\).  Since both its action and its target \(Bq\) are functions of the skeleton parameter and skeleton data alone,
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{q,\eta}(\delta^\phi)
 &=\sum_{\sigma}P_q(S=\sigma)\ell(\phi(\sigma),Bq)
   \sum_{\nu\in\Omega(\sigma)}K_\eta(\nu\mid\sigma)\\
 &=\sum_{\sigma}P_q(S=\sigma)\ell(\phi(\sigma),Bq)
 =R_q(\phi)
\end{aligned}
\]
for every \(q,\eta\).  Hence
\[
\inf_\phi\sup_qR_q(\phi)
\le\inf_\delta\sup_{q,\eta}R_{q,\eta}(\delta)
\]
by the first construction, while
\[
\inf_\delta\sup_{q,\eta}R_{q,\eta}(\delta)
\le\inf_\phi\sup_qR_q(\phi)
\]
by the lift.  The two minimax values are equal, with the inequalities valid as extended nonnegative real inequalities if a supremum is infinite.

%%% FIELD proof-finite-set-coverage
Use the conventions \(0\log(0/a)=0\) and \(b\log(b/0)=+\infty\) for \(b>0\).  Fix an arm \(z\), abbreviate \(n_z\) by \(n_0\), and let \(r\) be an empirical type on the \(d\)-point skeleton alphabet.  Its exact multinomial probability under \(s_z\) is
\[
P_{s_z}(\widehat s_z=r)
=\frac{n_0!}{\prod_j(n_0r_j)!}\prod_js_{z,j}^{n_0r_j}.
\]
The multinomial expansion of \((\sum_jr_j)^{n_0}=1\) implies
\[
\frac{n_0!}{\prod_j(n_0r_j)!}\prod_jr_j^{n_0r_j}\le1,
\]
so
\[
P_{s_z}(\widehat s_z=r)
\le \exp\{-n_0D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|s_z)\}.
\]
There are at most \((n_0+1)^{d-1}\) types, because their first \(d-1\) integer counts determine the last count.  This method-of-types count yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{s_z}\{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat s_z\|s_z)>\rho_z(\alpha)\}
&\le(n_z+1)^{d-1}e^{-n_z\rho_z(\alpha)}\\
&=(n_z+1)^{d-1}
\exp\{-\log(m/\alpha)-(d-1)\log(n_z+1)\}\\
&=\alpha/m.
\end{aligned}
\]
Positive arm counts justify every division by \(n_z\).  Zero coordinates of \(s_z\) cause no problem: an empirical type charging such a coordinate has probability zero.

Define the simultaneous event
\[
\mathcal E_\alpha=
\bigcap_{z\in\mathcal Z}
\{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat s_z\|s_z)\le\rho_z(\alpha)\}.
\]
The union bound gives \(P(\mathcal E_\alpha\mid(n_z)_z)\ge1-\alpha\).  Quotient sharpness implies that the true skeleton belongs to \(\mathcal S\).  Therefore, on \(\mathcal E_\alpha\), \(s\in\mathcal K_\alpha(\widehat s)\), and the union defining the reported region contains its corresponding complete sharp set:
\[
\Theta(s)\subseteq
\bigcup_{s'\in\mathcal K_\alpha(\widehat s)}\Theta(s')
=\widehat\Theta_\alpha.
\]
The skeleton multinomial law does not involve \(\eta\), so the probability bound is uniform over the off-target allocation nuisance.  The argument uses set inclusion only, not differentiability of an endpoint; it therefore remains valid at facet switches and simultaneously covers every linear functional of every \(\mu\in\Theta(s)\).

For computation, eliminate \(s'\) to obtain the explicit projection
\[
\widehat\Theta_\alpha
=\left\{Bq:q\in\Delta^{|\mathcal R|-1},\ 
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat s_z\|(Aq)_z)\le\rho_z(\alpha)
\text{ for all }z\in\mathcal Z\right\}.
\]
It has \((|\mathcal T|+1)^m2^{|\mathcal T|}\) response-type columns and \(md\) skeleton coordinates.  The radius contains only \(m,d,n_z,\alpha\).  Thus calibration and optimization are independent of \(|\mathcal C|\), while direct column enumeration is exponential in \(m\) and \(|\mathcal T|\).

%%% FIELD cited-polyhedral-fiber-statement
Let \(C\) be a fixed full-row-rank real matrix whose first row is \(\mathbf 1^\top\), and let \(G(b)=\{x\in\mathbb R^k:Cx=b,\ x\ge0\}\).  Over all right-hand sides whose first coordinate is one and whose fiber is nonempty, there is a finite constant \(H(C)\) such that \(e_1(G(b'),G(b))\le H(C)\|b'-b\|_1\).  A valid upper constant is computable from the finitely many full-row-rank active submatrices of the equality-plus-nonnegativity system (equivalently, after replacing each equality by two inequalities).

%%% FIELD statement-finite-alphabet-pinsker
For probability vectors \(a,b\) on the same finite alphabet, with relative entropy defined by \(0\log(0/b_j)=0\) and \(a_j\log(a_j/0)=+\infty\) when \(a_j>0\),
\[
\|a-b\|_1\le\sqrt{2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a\|b)}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-finite-alphabet-pinsker
Let \(E=\{j:a_j\ge b_j\}\) and \(t=a(E)-b(E)=\|a-b\|_1/2\).  Convexity of \(x\mapsto x\log x\), applied separately on \(E\) and its complement, gives the log-sum reduction
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a\|b)\ge d_{\mathrm{bin}}(a(E)\|b(E)).
\]
For fixed \(r\in(0,1)\), the second derivative in \(x\) of \(d_{\mathrm{bin}}(x\|r)\) is \(1/[x(1-x)]\ge4\), while its value and first derivative vanish at \(x=r\).  Taylor's integral formula gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{bin}}(x\|r)\ge2(x-r)^2.
\]
Boundary cases follow by taking limits; if the relative entropy is infinite the desired inequality is immediate.  Consequently
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a\|b)\ge2t^2=\tfrac12\|a-b\|_1^2,
\]
which is equivalent to the claim.

%%% FIELD statement-hoffman-target
The compatible-fiber modulus \(H_A\) is finite and admits a computable upper certificate obtained by enumerating the full-row-rank active submatrices of the equality-plus-nonnegativity system for \(F(s)\). For all \(s,s'\in\mathcal S\), \(e_1(\Theta(s'),\Theta(s))\le H_A\|s'-s\|_1\) and \(|h_{s'}(u)-h_s(u)|\le H_A\|u\|_\infty\|s'-s\|_1\). On the coverage event in Theorem 5, \(e_1(\widehat\Theta_\alpha,\Theta(s))\le2H_A\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sqrt{2\rho_z(\alpha)}\). Every constant depends only on \(A\) and \(B\), hence on \(m\) and \(|\mathcal T|\), not on \(|\mathcal C|\).

%%% FIELD statement-regular-target
Under the interior, feasible-direction, regular-face, nonzero-derivative, and arm-proportion conditions, there exist constants \(c>0\) and \(c_0>0\) such that \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt n\inf_{\widehat h}\sup_{e\in\{-c,c\}}E_{s_0+ev/\sqrt n,\eta}|\widehat h-h_{s_0+ev/\sqrt n}(u_0)|\ge c_0\), even when \(\widehat h\) uses the full counts \(N\). The constants and the lower bound are uniform over the labels and cardinality of \(\mathcal C\).

%%% FIELD proof-hoffman-support
Let \(C\) be a full-row-rank submatrix whose rows are a linearly independent subcollection spanning the row space of the augmented equality matrix, choosing \(\mathbf 1^\top\) as its first row,
\[
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf 1^\top\\ A\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For \(s\in\mathcal S\), let \(b(s)\) be the corresponding subvector of \((1,s)\).  Since \((1,s)\) is compatible with all row relations of the augmented matrix,
\[
F(s)=\{q\ge0:Cq=b(s)\}.
\]
The cited fixed-matrix polyhedral feasible-fiber bound applies because every such fiber is a nonempty closed subset of the simplex and hence is bounded.  It supplies a finite, computable \(\overline H_A\), obtained from the finitely many full-row-rank active submatrices, such that
\[
e_1(F(s'),F(s))
\le\overline H_A\|b(s')-b(s)\|_1
\le\overline H_A\|s'-s\|_1.
\]
Taking the supremum in the definition of \(H_A\) proves \(H_A\le\overline H_A<\infty\) and gives the asserted computable upper certificate.

Every column of \(B\) contains exactly one entry equal to one.  Therefore, for every signed vector \(w\),
\[
\|Bw\|_1
\le\sum_y\sum_{r:y_{\mathcal T}(r)=y}|w_r|
=\|w\|_1.
\]
Fix \(\mu'=Bq'\in\Theta(s')\).  From the definition of directed excess, for every \(\epsilon>0\) there is \(q\in F(s)\) satisfying
\[
\|q'-q\|_1
\le e_1(F(s'),F(s))+\epsilon.
\]
It follows that
\[
\inf_{\mu\in\Theta(s)}\|\mu'-\mu\|_1
\le\|Bq'-Bq\|_1
\le H_A\|s'-s\|_1+\epsilon.
\]
Take the supremum over \(\mu'\) and then let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) to obtain
\[
e_1(\Theta(s'),\Theta(s))\le H_A\|s'-s\|_1.
\]

Compactness of \(F(s)\) ensures that the support maximum is attained.  If \(q'\) maximizes \(u^\top Bq'\) over \(F(s')\), choose the preceding approximating \(q\in F(s)\).  Then
\[
h_{s'}(u)-h_s(u)
\le u^\top B(q'-q)
\le\|u\|_\infty\|B(q'-q)\|_1
\le H_A\|u\|_\infty\|s'-s\|_1+\|u\|_\infty\epsilon.
\]
Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and interchanging \(s,s'\) proves the absolute-value inequality.

It remains to establish the confidence-width statement.  By the finite-alphabet Pinsker lemma, on the simultaneous event \(\mathcal E_\alpha\) used in the finite-set coverage proof, every \(s'\in\mathcal K_\alpha(\widehat s)\) obeys, arm by arm,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|s'_z-s_z\|_1
&\le\|s'_z-\widehat s_z\|_1+\|\widehat s_z-s_z\|_1\\
&\le\sqrt{2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat s_z\|s'_z)}
 +\sqrt{2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat s_z\|s_z)}\\
&\le2\sqrt{2\rho_z(\alpha)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\|s'-s\|_1\le2\sum_z\sqrt{2\rho_z(\alpha)}\).  Apply the target-set excess inequality uniformly to every set in the union defining \(\widehat\Theta_\alpha\) to conclude
\[
e_1(\widehat\Theta_\alpha,\Theta(s))
\le2H_A\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sqrt{2\rho_z(\alpha)}.
\]
The matrices \(A,B\), the active-submatrix list, and all displayed dimensions depend only on \(m\) and \(|\mathcal T|\).  No constant depends on the labels, allocation probabilities, or cardinality of \(\mathcal C\).

%%% FIELD proof-regular-face-lower-bound
Put
\[
a=\min_{z\in\mathcal Z,j\in\mathcal D}(s_0)_{z,j}>0,
\qquad
V=\max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sum_{j\in\mathcal D}v_{z,j}^2.
\]
The two-sided feasible-direction assumption implies \(\sum_jv_{z,j}=0\) for every arm.  Moreover \(V>0\), because \(V=0\) would imply \(v=0\) and hence \(\gamma=0\), contrary to the nonzero-derivative assumption.

The stable unique nondegenerate optimal basis in the regular-face assumption remains primal and dual optimal in a neighborhood of \(s_0\).  The corresponding basic solution is affine in the equality right-hand side, so its objective value is affine.  Consequently there is \(\epsilon_1>0\) such that
\[
h_{s_0+\epsilon v}(u_0)=h_{s_0}(u_0)+\gamma\epsilon
\quad\text{whenever }|\epsilon|\le\epsilon_1.
\]
Choose a fixed \(c>0\) small enough that
\[
\frac{8c^2V}{a}\le\frac12.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), feasibility of the two-sided direction gives \(s_{\pm,n}=s_0\pm cv/\sqrt n\in\mathcal S\), every coordinate of both laws is at least \(a/2\), and the local affine formula gives
\[
\Delta_n:=|h_{s_{+,n}}(u_0)-h_{s_{-,n}}(u_0)|
=\frac{2|\gamma|c}{\sqrt n}.
\]

Let \(P_{+,n}^{N,\eta}\) and \(P_{-,n}^{N,\eta}\) denote the conditional laws of the full counts at these two skeleton laws and the same arbitrary allocation \(\eta\).  Experiment factorization gives the same conditional kernel \(K_\eta(N\mid S)\) under both hypotheses.  The chain rule for the finite sum defining relative entropy therefore cancels that kernel and yields
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{+,n}^{N,\eta}\|P_{-,n}^{N,\eta})
=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}n_zD_{\mathrm{KL}}((s_{+,n})_z\|(s_{-,n})_z).
\]
For positive probability vectors \(p,q\), \(\log x\le x-1\) implies
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)
\le\sum_j\frac{(p_j-q_j)^2}{q_j}.
\]
Using \((s_{+,n})_z-(s_{-,n})_z=2cv_z/\sqrt n\), the lower bound \((s_{-,n})_{z,j}\ge a/2\), and \(\sum_zn_z/n=1\), we obtain
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{+,n}^{N,\eta}\|P_{-,n}^{N,\eta})
\le\frac{8c^2}{a}\sum_z\frac{n_z}{n}\sum_jv_{z,j}^2
\le\frac{8c^2V}{a}\le\frac12.
\]
This bound is uniform in \(\eta\) and \(\mathcal C\).  The arm-proportion assumption ensures the stipulated conditional asymptotic sequence has all arms diverging with positive limiting fractions; the displayed information bound itself only needs their fixed totals to sum to \(n\).

The finite-alphabet Pinsker lemma gives
\[
\|P_{+,n}^{N,\eta}-P_{-,n}^{N,\eta}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\sqrt{\tfrac12D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{+,n}^{N,\eta}\|P_{-,n}^{N,\eta})}
\le\frac12.
\]
For completeness, the two-point risk inequality follows directly.  With densities \(p_+,p_-\) relative to their sum and any full-count estimator \(\widehat h\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_+|\widehat h-h_{s_{+,n}}(u_0)|+E_-|\widehat h-h_{s_{-,n}}(u_0)|\\
&\quad\ge
\int\min(p_+,p_-)
\left(|\widehat h-h_{s_{+,n}}(u_0)|+|\widehat h-h_{s_{-,n}}(u_0)|\right)\\
&\quad\ge \Delta_n\int\min(p_+,p_-)
=\Delta_n\{1-\|P_{+,n}^{N,\eta}-P_{-,n}^{N,\eta}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
At least one of the two risks is therefore at least \(\Delta_n/4\).  Taking the infimum over all estimators, multiplying by \(\sqrt n\), and taking the lower limit yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt n\inf_{\widehat h}
\sup_{e\in\{-c,c\}}E_{s_0+ev/\sqrt n,\eta}
|\widehat h-h_{s_0+ev/\sqrt n}(u_0)|
\ge\frac{|\gamma|c}{2}=:c_0>0.
\]
Both \(c\) and \(c_0\) use only \(s_0,v,\gamma,A,B\) and the reduced alphabet.  The cancellation of \(K_\eta\) proves uniformity over every off-target allocation, label set, and finite cardinality \(|\mathcal C|\).

After redundant equality rows are removed, write the reduced primal program as
\[
h_s(u)=\max_{q\ge0}\{(B^\top u)^\top q:Cq=b(s)\}.
\]
Its dual is the finite-dimensional polyhedral program
\[
h_s(u)=\min_{\beta}\{\beta^\top b(s):C^\top\beta\ge B^\top u\}.
\]
On any neighborhood on which the value is finite, this is the lower envelope of the affine functions associated with the finitely many relevant dual vertices.  If \(\Lambda_0(u)\) is the set of vertices optimal at \(s_0\), then for every feasible direction \(v\), polyhedral envelope algebra gives the one-sided directional derivative
\[
h'_{s_0}(u;v)=\min_{\beta\in\Lambda_0(u)}\beta^\top \dot b(v).
\]
Indeed, vertices not optimal at \(s_0\) have a strictly positive objective gap and cannot minimize for sufficiently small positive perturbations, while taking the minimum over the vertices in \(\Lambda_0(u)\) gives the displayed first-order coefficient.  Thus a multiple-vertex point has a computable piecewise-linear first-order map, and unequal active slopes are exactly the first-order kink.  This establishes the deterministic local envelope, but it does not by itself determine a minimax risk or an adaptive confidence law.
