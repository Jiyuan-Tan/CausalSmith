%%% FIELD target-statement
If \(\mathcal C=\varnothing\), \(m=2\), and \(\mathcal T=\{a,b\}\), then \(\Theta(s)\) is the classical binary response-type polytope and the extrema of \(\tau=E[Y_b-Y_a]\) are the Balke--Pearl sharp bounds.
%%% FIELD balke-pearl-statement
Let \(\pi_{yd.z}\ge 0\), indexed by \(y,d,z\in\{0,1\}\), satisfy \(\sum_{y,d}\pi_{yd.z}=1\) for each \(z\). Suppose that the binary instrumental-variable response-type fiber
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{IV}}(\pi)
 =\left\{\widetilde q\in\Delta^{15}:
 \pi_{yd.z}=\sum_{d_0,d_1,u_0,u_1}\widetilde q_{d_0d_1u_0u_1}
 \mathbf 1\{d_z=d,\ u_d=y\}\ \text{for all }y,d,z\right\}
\]
is nonempty. For
\[
 \operatorname{ACE}(\widetilde q)
 =\sum_{d_0,d_1,u_0,u_1}\widetilde q_{d_0d_1u_0u_1}(u_1-u_0),
\]
the minimum over \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{IV}}(\pi)\) equals
\[
L_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)=\max\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{11.1}-1,\\
&\pi_{00.1}+\pi_{11.1}-1,\\
&\pi_{11.0}+\pi_{00.1}-1,\\
&\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{11.0}-1,\\
&2\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{11.0}+\pi_{10.1}+\pi_{11.1}-2,\\
&\pi_{00.0}+2\pi_{11.0}+\pi_{00.1}+\pi_{01.1}-2,\\
&\pi_{10.0}+\pi_{11.0}+2\pi_{00.1}+\pi_{11.1}-2,\\
&\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{01.0}+\pi_{00.1}+2\pi_{11.1}-2
\end{aligned}\right\},
\]
and the maximum equals
\[
U_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)=\min\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&1-\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{01.1},\\
&1-\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{10.1},\\
&1-\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{10.0},\\
&1-\pi_{01.1}-\pi_{10.1},\\
&2-2\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{10.1}-\pi_{11.1},\\
&2-\pi_{01.0}-2\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{00.1}-\pi_{01.1},\\
&2-\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{11.0}-2\pi_{01.1}-\pi_{10.1},\\
&2-\pi_{00.0}-\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{01.1}-2\pi_{10.1}
\end{aligned}\right\}.
\]
Both extrema are attainable by response-type distributions in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{IV}}(\pi)\).
%%% FIELD target-proof
Relabel the two elements of \(\mathcal Z\) as \(0\) and \(1\); this is only a bijective change of indices. Put \(x_0=a\) and \(x_1=b\). The positive-arm-probability assumption makes both conditional arm laws well defined. We first verify feasibility from the maintained causal model rather than assuming it. Because \(\mathcal C=\varnothing\), each \(X_z\) takes values in \(\mathcal T\). Let \(q^P\) be the joint law of \((X_0,X_1,Y_a,Y_b)\), embedded in \(\Delta^{|\mathcal R|-1}\) by assigning zero mass to every reduced type with a \(\star\) coordinate. Target exclusion makes \((Y_a,Y_b)\) a single response vector not indexed by the assigned arm. For every \(t\in\mathcal T\), \(y\in\{0,1\}\), and \(z\in\{0,1\}\), randomization, exposure consistency, and target-outcome consistency give
\[
\begin{aligned}
(Aq^P)_{z,(t,y)}
&=P(X_z=t,Y_t=y)\\
&=P(X_z=t,Y_t=y\mid Z=z)\\
&=P(X=t,Y=y\mid Z=z)\\
&=s_z(t,y).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses \(Z\perp\!\!\!\perp(X_{\mathcal Z},Y_{\mathcal T})\); the third holds almost surely on \(\{Z=z,X_z=t\}\). Also, by the definition of the skeleton law, for each arm \(z\),
\[
s_z(\star)=\sum_{c\in\varnothing}\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}p_z(c,y)=0=(Aq^P)_{z,\star}.
\]
Thus \(Aq^P=s\), so \(q^P\in F(s)\) and the fiber is nonempty.

We next identify the support of every feasible reduced law. 
If \(q\in F(s)\), then the definition of \(F(s)\), followed by the \(\star\)-row in the definition of \(A\), yields
\[
0=s_z(\star)=(Aq)_{z,\star}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}q_r\mathbf 1\{x_z(r)=\star\}.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative because \(q\) belongs to a probability simplex. Hence \(q_r=0\) whenever \(x_z(r)=\star\). Applying this argument for both arms proves that every \(q\in F(s)\) is supported on
\[
\mathcal R_0=\mathcal T^{\{0,1\}}\times\{0,1\}^{\mathcal T},
\qquad |\mathcal R_0|=2^2\,2^2=16.
\]

Define a bijection \(\iota:\mathcal R_0\to\{0,1\}^4\) by
\[
\iota(r)=(d_0,d_1,u_0,u_1),\qquad
d_z=\mathbf 1\{x_z(r)=b\},\quad u_0=y_a(r),\quad u_1=y_b(r).
\]
Thus \((d_0,d_1)\) is the binary response function from assignment to received exposure and \((u_0,u_1)\) is the binary response function from exposure to outcome. Push \(q\) forward through \(\iota\), writing the resulting mass as \(\widetilde q_{d_0d_1u_0u_1}\). For \(y,d,z\in\{0,1\}\), define the observed-law coordinate
\[
\pi_{yd.z}=s_z(x_d,y).
\]
The target-cell row of \(Aq=s\) then becomes, term by term,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_{yd.z}
&=s_z(x_d,y)\\
&=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_0}q_r
  \mathbf 1\{x_z(r)=x_d,\ y_{x_d}(r)=y\}\\
&=\sum_{d_0,d_1,u_0,u_1}
  \widetilde q_{d_0d_1u_0u_1}
  \mathbf 1\{d_z=d,\ u_d=y\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are exactly the classical binary instrumental-variable response-type equations. Conversely, any \(\widetilde q\in\Delta^{15}\) satisfying these equations defines, through \(\iota^{-1}\), a distribution on \(\mathcal R_0\); extending it by zero to \(\mathcal R\) satisfies every target-cell equation and also every \(\star\)-equation of \(Aq=s\). Therefore pushforward by \(\iota\) is an affine bijection
\[
F(s)\longleftrightarrow\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{IV}}(\pi).
\]
This establishes equality of the feasible response-type polytopes, rather than merely an outer relaxation.

It remains to check that the target maps agree. For \((u_0,u_1)\in\{0,1\}^2\), the definition of \(B\) and the bijection above give
\[
(Bq)_{(u_0,u_1)}
=\sum_{d_0,d_1}\widetilde q_{d_0d_1u_0u_1}.
\]
Consequently \(\Theta(s)=BF(s)\) is exactly the outcome-response-law image of the classical fiber. The causal contrast associated with a feasible response law is
\[
\tau(q)=E_q[Y_b-Y_a]
=\sum_{d_0,d_1,u_0,u_1}
 \widetilde q_{d_0d_1u_0u_1}(u_1-u_0).
\]
This causal quantity is distinct from the observed-law functionals \(L_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)\) and \(U_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)\), which depend only on the eight coordinates \(\pi_{yd.z}=s_z(x_d,y)\).

By the cited Balke--Pearl binary instrumental-variable linear-programming result, the minimum and maximum of the last display over \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{IV}}(\pi)\) are, respectively,
\[
L_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)=\max\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{11.1}-1,\\
&\pi_{00.1}+\pi_{11.1}-1,\\
&\pi_{11.0}+\pi_{00.1}-1,\\
&\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{11.0}-1,\\
&2\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{11.0}+\pi_{10.1}+\pi_{11.1}-2,\\
&\pi_{00.0}+2\pi_{11.0}+\pi_{00.1}+\pi_{01.1}-2,\\
&\pi_{10.0}+\pi_{11.0}+2\pi_{00.1}+\pi_{11.1}-2,\\
&\pi_{00.0}+\pi_{01.0}+\pi_{00.1}+2\pi_{11.1}-2
\end{aligned}\right\},
\]
and
\[
U_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)=\min\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&1-\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{01.1},\\
&1-\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{10.1},\\
&1-\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{10.0},\\
&1-\pi_{01.1}-\pi_{10.1},\\
&2-2\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{10.1}-\pi_{11.1},\\
&2-\pi_{01.0}-2\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{00.1}-\pi_{01.1},\\
&2-\pi_{10.0}-\pi_{11.0}-2\pi_{01.1}-\pi_{10.1},\\
&2-\pi_{00.0}-\pi_{01.0}-\pi_{01.1}-2\pi_{10.1}
\end{aligned}\right\}.
\]
The construction of \(q^P\) above proves that \(F(s)\), and hence \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{IV}}(\pi)\), is nonempty. Since the latter is the intersection of a finite-dimensional probability simplex with finitely many closed affine equalities, it is compact. Thus both extrema are attained. Convexity of the fiber and linearity of \(\tau\) further show that every value between the two endpoints is attained by a convex combination of an endpoint minimizer and an endpoint maximizer. Hence the sharp identified interval is
\[
\{\tau(q):q\in F(s)\}
=[L_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi),U_{\mathrm{BP}}(\pi)],
\]
To verify causal sharpness rather than only linear-program sharpness, take any \(q\in F(s)\), draw its response type independently of \(Z\), give \(Z\) its observed probabilities \((\lambda_0,\lambda_1)\), set \(Y_{t,z}=Y_t\), \(X=X_Z\), and \(Y=Y_X\). This full law satisfies randomization, target exclusion, exposure consistency, and target-outcome consistency, and the displayed incidence equations show that its observed skeleton law is \(s\). It realizes target law \(Bq\). Conversely, the construction of \(q^P\) shows that every compatible full law induces a point of \(F(s)\). Therefore the interval in the last display is sharp for the causal contrast, not merely an outer bound, which proves the theorem. The only positivity used is \(\lambda_z>0\) to define each conditional arm law; there is no division by a response-cell probability and no limiting or boundary argument.
