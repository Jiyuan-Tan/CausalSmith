%%% FIELD proof-posterior-fiber-change
For \(k\in\{2,4,6\}\), let \(\pi_k\) denote the certificate prior and, for every full-count cell with positive predictive mass, write
\[
 A_{xy}^{(k)}=\sum_{N\in\mathcal N_k}\pi_{k,N}K_N(x,y),
 \qquad
 B_{xy}^{(k)}=\sum_{N\in\mathcal N_k}\pi_{k,N}K_N(x,y)\tau_N,
 \qquad
 \mu_{xy}^{(k)}=\frac{B_{xy}^{(k)}}{A_{xy}^{(k)}}.
\]
The completed-square identity in Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm{:}response\mbox{-}type\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}games}\)
shows that \(\mu_{xy}^{(k)}\) is the unique squared-error Bayes action on such a cell.  For a difference \(j\), the corresponding \(W\)-fiber is
\[
 \mathcal F_j=\{(x,y):x-y=j\},\qquad W=2j.
\]
If \(\mu_{xy}^{(k)}=c_j\) at every positive-mass cell in \(\mathcal F_j\), then aggregation over the fiber gives
\[
 \frac{\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal F_j}B_{xy}^{(k)}}
 {\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal F_j}A_{xy}^{(k)}}
 =
 \frac{\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal F_j}A_{xy}^{(k)}c_j}
 {\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal F_j}A_{xy}^{(k)}}
 =c_j.
\]
Thus constancy of the full-count posterior means is exactly the posterior equation required for one scalar action on that positive-mass fiber.

At \(n=2\), the hypergeometric kernel for the three support states of \(\pi_2\) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
(x,y)&A_{xy}^{(2)}&B_{xy}^{(2)}&\mu_{xy}^{(2)}\\ \hline
(1,0)&\frac14+\frac12\frac12=\frac12&\frac14&\frac12\\[2pt]
(0,1)&\frac14+\frac12\frac12=\frac12&-\frac14&-\frac12 .
\end{array}
\]
These are the only positive-predictive-mass cells, and they lie in the distinct fibers \(W=2\) and \(W=-2\).  Their posterior means equal \(g_2(2)\) and \(g_2(-2)\), respectively.

At \(n=4\), put
\[
 h=\frac{\sqrt3-1}{4},
 \qquad
 z=z_4=\frac{3-\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
The two pure-effect states have prior mass \(h\), and the zero-effect state has prior mass \(z\).  The latter state assigns probabilities \(1/6,2/3,1/6\) to \((2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\), respectively.  Hence
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
(x,y)&A_{xy}^{(4)}&B_{xy}^{(4)}&\mu_{xy}^{(4)}\\ \hline
(2,0)&h+\frac z6&h&z\\[2pt]
(1,1)&\frac{2z}{3}&0&0\\[2pt]
(0,2)&h+\frac z6&-h&-z .
\end{array}
\]
For the first row, the displayed posterior identity follows from
\[
 h(1-z)=\frac{(\sqrt3-1)^2}{8}
 =\frac{2-\sqrt3}{4}
 =\frac{(3-\sqrt3)^2}{24}
 =\frac{z^2}{6},
\]
which is equivalent to \(h/(h+z/6)=z\); the third row follows by arm exchange.  The three positive cells belong to \(W=4,0,-4\), respectively, and their posterior means equal the corresponding values of \(g_4\).

At \(n=6\), cancellation of the common positive factor \(B_6\) in the seven prior weights gives the positive-difference posterior equations
\[
 \mu_{(2,0)}^{(6)}=\mu_{(3,1)}^{(6)}
 =\frac{2}{3(1+C_6)}=v_6,
\]
\[
 \mu_{(2,1)}^{(6)}
 =\frac{1/10}{3/20+9D_6/20}
 =\frac{2}{3+9D_6}=u_6,
\]
and
\[
 \mu_{(3,0)}^{(6)}
 =\frac{A_6/2+1/10}{A_6/2+3/20+D_6/20}=z_6.
\]
The first equality uses \(1+C_6=2/(3v_6)\).  The second uses
\[
 3+9D_6
 =3+\frac{2-3u_6}{u_6}
 =\frac2{u_6}.
\]
For the third, cross multiplication reduces the assertion to
\[
 10A_6(1-z_6)=3z_6+z_6D_6-2,
\]
which is precisely the defining equation for \(A_6\).  The positive-mass zero-difference cells \((1,1)\) and \((2,2)\) receive predictive mass only from zero-effect support states, so their first target moments vanish and their posterior means are zero.  Arm exchange changes \((x,y,\tau_N)\) to \((y,x,-\tau_N)\), and therefore supplies the negative-difference equations.  It follows that the posterior mean is \(z_6\) on the positive part of the \(W=6\) fiber, \(v_6\) at both \((2,0)\) and \((3,1)\) in the \(W=4\) fiber, \(u_6\) on the positive part of the \(W=2\) fiber, zero on the \(W=0\) fiber, and the corresponding negatives on the reflected fibers.  These values are exactly \(g_6(W)\).

The preceding posterior calculations fix the Bayes actions only on positive-predictive-mass cells.  The scalar rules in Definitions
\(\mathrm{def{:}n2\mbox{-}certificate}\),
\(\mathrm{def{:}n4\mbox{-}certificate}\), and
\(\mathrm{def{:}n6\mbox{-}algebraic\mbox{-}certificate}\)
also specify the prior-null cells.  The exhaustive risk calculations certified by Theorems
\(\mathrm{thm{:}n24\mbox{-}equality}\) and
\(\mathrm{thm{:}n6\mbox{-}seven\mbox{-}state\mbox{-}saddle}\)
then give
\[
 \max_{N\in\mathcal N_2}R_N^W(g_2)=\frac14,\qquad
 \max_{N\in\mathcal N_4}R_N^W(g_4)=1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\qquad
 \max_{N\in\mathcal N_6}R_N^W(g_6)=s_6^2,
\]
with every state outside the respective prior support strictly below the corresponding value.  Thus a scalar completion controls every remaining state at each of the three equality sizes, and those same two theorems yield
\[
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}=\rho_n^W\qquad(n=2,4,6).
\]

At \(n=8\), both cells \((1,0)\) and \((2,1)\) lie in \(\mathcal F_1\), because
\[
 2(1-0)=2=2(2-1).
\]
Nevertheless, Definition
\(\mathrm{def{:}n8\mbox{-}full\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}certificate}\)
gives
\[
 t_8(1,0)=\frac{73084}{10^6}
 \neq
 \frac{152579}{10^6}=t_8(2,1).
\]
Consequently \(t_8\) is not representable as \(g(W)\).  This exhibited within-fiber variation alone would not prove minimax loss.  The decisive certificate is the strict primal-dual comparison in Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm{:}n8\mbox{-}strict\mbox{-}separation}\):
\[
 \max_{N\in\mathcal N_8}R_N^{\mathrm{CR}}(t_8)
 =U_8<L_8\leq\rho_8^W.
\]
Indeed, for every scalar rule \(g\), the definition of \(\rho_8^W\) implies
\[
 \max_{N\in\mathcal N_8}R_N^W(g)
 \geq\rho_8^W
 \geq L_8
 >U_8.
\]
Hence no scalar completion can reproduce the certified full-count risk envelope.  Since the positive even sizes not exceeding eight are \(2,4,6,8\), equality at the first three sizes and strict separation at the fourth show that the second count first changes the minimax envelope at eight.  The posterior-fiber equations explain why scalar saddles close at \(2,4,6\), while the risk sandwich, rather than within-fiber variation by itself, certifies their failure at \(8\).  This argument makes no assertion for any later even size.
