\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}complete\_\allowbreak{}balance\_\allowbreak{}secondorder}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Exact balance removes treatment-share variation but leaves two observed arm-success counts. For fixed binary potential outcomes, the full pair and its difference have equal worst-schedule ATE squared-error minimax risk at \(n=2,4,6\), but a rational two-sided full-count bracket and a difference-only lower certificate prove strict loss at \(n=8\). A tensor-product endpoint extension separately proves that, for any fixed assignment law, bounded-cube minimax ATE risk equals \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\) times the corresponding labeled binary-endpoint minimax value. Thus balanced complete randomization has exact bounded-cube risks \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2/4\), \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2(1-\sqrt3/2)\), and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2s_6^2\) at two, four, and six, and a certified \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2[C_8,U_8]\) bracket at eight; it is strictly worse than Hull's globally optimized independent design at all four sizes. Later-even compression persistence, the exact eight-unit value, Hull's uniform strictness conjecture, and all asymptotic laws remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published minimax analyses establish the all-design shifted-binomial reduction and report numerical evidence that complete randomization remains strictly suboptimal with general estimators, but do not give exact endpoint certificates for that strict comparison. They also do not locate the first finite size at which compressing the two exact-balance success counts to their difference loses minimax information.

\paragraph{Niche.} The fixed-half binary response-type hypergeometric experiment supplies exact primal-dual certificates, while its binary endpoint embedding supplies rigorous lower bounds for the full bounded schedule class without claiming a nonbinary two-count reduction.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project gives analytic equality saddles at \(n=2,4,6\), rational lower and upper full-count certificates and strict count-compression separation at eight, and a fixed-design tensor-product endpoint theorem equating bounded-cube minimax risk with the scaled labeled binary-endpoint value. The latter yields exact balanced-design bounded risks at two, four, and six, a certified bracket at eight, and strict comparison with Hull's globally optimized independent design at those four sizes.

\section{Related work}

Hull (2026, Theorem 1 and Appendix A.4) optimizes worst-case ATE squared error jointly over all bounded schedules, assignment designs, and measurable estimators, proves that independent half assignment attains the shifted-binomial minimax program, and uses Bernstein extension plus separate convexity to lift endpoint risks to the full bounded cube. Corollary 1 gives the affine independent-design risk \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2/(\sqrt n+1)^2\); footnote 12 records uniform strict suboptimality of complete randomization with general estimators as a conjecture supported numerically. Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}fixed\mbox{-}design\mbox{-}endpoint\mbox{-}reduction}\) gives a distinct fixed-design minimax-value equality by extending the complete labeled binary rule, without asserting a nonbinary orbit statistic. Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}hull\mbox{-}class\mbox{-}complete\mbox{-}randomization\mbox{-}strict\mbox{-}small\mbox{-}n}\) combines that equality with finite certificates to prove Hull's strict comparison only for \(n=2,4,6,8\), leaving the uniform conjecture open. Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2026) study the unrestricted independent-design scalar program and its Airy expansion, not the exact balanced hypergeometric count-compression threshold. Ding and Dasgupta (2016) supply the binary response types and observed table used in the finite kernel. Blackwell (1953) gives only weak dominance of the full count pair over its difference. Bai (2023) treats permutation-invariant linear procedures. Rigdon and Hudgens (2015), Li and Ding (2016), and Li and Ding (2017) concern exact confidence sets or first-order finite-population limits and do not imply the point-estimation or bounded-design claims here.

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:hypergeometric-kernel]\label{def:hypergeometric-kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Exact response-\allowbreak{}type hypergeometric kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[K_N(x,y)=\binom{n}{m}^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\substack{0\le t_a\le a,\,0\le t_p\le p,\,0\le t_q\le q,\,0\le t_b\le b\\t_a+t_p+t_q+t_b=m,\,t_p+t_b=x\\q+b-t_q-t_b=y}}\binom a{t_a}\binom p{t_p}\binom q{t_q}\binom b{t_b}.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:full-count-game]\label{def:full-count-game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}count minimax game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}=\min_{t:\{0,\ldots,m\}^2\to[-1,1]}\max_{N\in\mathcal N_n}\sum_{x=0}^m\sum_{y=0}^mK_N(x,y)\{t(x,y)-\tau_N\}^2.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}hypergeometric-\allowbreak{}kernel}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:difference-game]\label{def:difference-game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Difference-\allowbreak{}only minimax game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\rho_n^W=\min_{g:\{-n,-n+2,\ldots,n\}\to[-1,1]}\max_{N\in\mathcal N_n}\sum_{x=0}^m\sum_{y=0}^mK_N(x,y)\{g(2x-2y)-\tau_N\}^2.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}hypergeometric-\allowbreak{}kernel}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-bayes-dual]\label{def:finite-bayes-dual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite squared-\allowbreak{}loss prior dual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal B_{\mathsf D}(\pi)=\sum_{N\in\mathcal N_n}\pi_N\tau_N^2-\sum_{d:A_d^{\pi}>0}\frac{(B_d^{\pi})^2}{A_d^{\pi}},\qquad A_d^{\pi}=\sum_N\pi_N\Pr_N(\mathsf D=d),\quad B_d^{\pi}=\sum_N\pi_N\Pr_N(\mathsf D=d)\tau_N.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[thm:response-type-minimax-games]\label{thm:response-type-minimax-games}
Under balanced complete randomization, averaging over unit permutations loses no minimax value, the observed-data orbit is exactly \((X_1,X_0)\), and its law at state \(N\) is \(K_N(x,y)\). Clipping and conditional expectation reduce attention to deterministic actions in \([-1,1]\). Consequently the two minimax values are exactly the games in Definitions 2 and 3, both minima are attained, \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}\le\rho_n^W\), and finite minimax duality gives \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}=\max_{\pi}\mathcal B_{(X_1,X_0)}(\pi)\) and \(\rho_n^W=\max_{\pi}\mathcal B_W(\pi)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a labeled potential-outcome schedule and write its observed record as the assignment labels together with the observed binary outcomes.  The only randomness in what follows is \(Z\), whose support has size \(\binom n m>0\) by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\mbox{-}exact\mbox{-}balance}\).

First reduce the labeled experiment to response-type counts.  Let \(S_n\) act by simultaneously permuting the unit labels in the schedule, assignment, and observed record.  Given any possibly randomized rule \(T\), replace its random output, conditional on the observed record, by its conditional mean and then project that mean onto \([-1,1]\).  For every \(\tau_N\in[-1,1]\), conditional variance decomposition and the metric-projection inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E_N\{(T-\tau_N)^2\mid O\}
 =\operatorname{Var}_N(T\mid O)+\{\mathbb E_N(T\mid O)-\tau_N\}^2
 \geq \{\operatorname{proj}_{[-1,1]}\mathbb E_N(T\mid O)-\tau_N\}^2.
\]
Thus randomization of the action and actions outside \([-1,1]\) cannot lower risk.  For a deterministic rule \(T\), define its permutation average
\[
 \overline T(O)=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}T(\sigma O).
\]
Squared-loss convexity and invariance of the uniform exact-balance law imply, for every schedule \(Y\),
\[
 R_Y(\overline T)
 \leq \frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}R_{\sigma Y}(T)
 \leq \sup_{Y'}R_{Y'}(T).
\]
Hence permutation averaging loses no minimax value.  Since the treated size is fixed at \(m\), two observed records are in the same permutation orbit exactly when their treated-success and control-success counts agree.  Therefore the observed-data orbit is exactly \((X_1,X_0)=(x,y)\).  Similarly, two schedules are in the same orbit exactly when their four response-type counts agree, so nature's orbit is \(N=(a,p,q,b)\in\mathcal N_n\).  This proves rather than assumes the reduction from the fixed causal schedule to the count experiment.

It remains to calculate the count law.  If \(t_a,t_p,t_q,t_b\) units of the four response types are treated, respectively, then exact balance, the treated-success equation, and the control-success equation are
\[
 t_a+t_p+t_q+t_b=m,\qquad t_p+t_b=x,\qquad q+b-t_q-t_b=y.
\]
There are
\(
 \binom a{t_a}\binom p{t_p}\binom q{t_q}\binom b{t_b}
\)
assignments realizing such a quadruple, out of \(\binom n m\) equiprobable assignments.  Summing over all legal quadruples gives exactly \(K_N(x,y)\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}hypergeometric\mbox{-}kernel}\).  In particular, its terms are nonnegative and
\[
 \sum_{x=0}^m\sum_{y=0}^mK_N(x,y)
 =\binom n m^{-1}\sum_{t_a+t_p+t_q+t_b=m}
   \binom a{t_a}\binom p{t_p}\binom q{t_q}\binom b{t_b}=1,
\]
where the final equality is the coefficient-of-\(z^m\) identity in
\((1+z)^{a+p+q+b}=(1+z)^n\).

Consequently the risk of a full-count action \(t\) is the expression in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}full\mbox{-}count\mbox{-}game}\).  A rule measurable with respect to \(W\) has the form \(t(x,y)=g(2x-2y)\), giving Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}difference\mbox{-}game}\).  Every such \(g\) is also a full-count rule, and therefore
\[
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}\leq \rho_n^W.
\]
Both action spaces are finite products of the compact interval \([-1,1]\), and each maximum risk is the maximum of finitely many continuous quadratic functions.  Thus both displayed minima are attained.

For completeness, finite minimax duality is derived directly.  Fix either finite observation map \(\mathsf D\) and a prior \(\pi\).  For cell actions \(h_d\in[-1,1]\), expansion of the prior-average risk gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_N\pi_NR_N(h)
 &=\sum_N\pi_N\tau_N^2+
   \sum_d\{A_d^\pi h_d^2-2B_d^\pi h_d\}\\
 &=\sum_N\pi_N\tau_N^2-
   \sum_{d:A_d^\pi>0}\frac{(B_d^\pi)^2}{A_d^\pi}
   +\sum_{d:A_d^\pi>0}A_d^\pi
      \left(h_d-\frac{B_d^\pi}{A_d^\pi}\right)^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(B_d^\pi/A_d^\pi\) is a convex combination of numbers \(\tau_N\in[-1,1]\), it is feasible.  Hence the minimum prior-average risk is exactly \(\mathcal B_{\mathsf D}(\pi)\).

To justify exchange of the finite minimum and maximum without assuming a minimax theorem, let
\[
 \mathcal C=\{c\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal N_n}:\text{there is an action }h
 \text{ such that }R_N(h)\leq c_N\text{ for every }N\}.
\]
This upper set is convex: if \(h_0,h_1\) witness \(c^0,c^1\), convexity of squared loss shows that \(\lambda h_0+(1-\lambda)h_1\) witnesses \(\lambda c^0+(1-\lambda)c^1\).  It is closed on every coordinatewise bounded box by compactness of the action cube and continuity of the risks, which is all that is needed below.  If \(\rho_{n,\mathsf D}=\min_h\max_NR_N(h)\), then \(\rho_{n,\mathsf D}{\bf1}\in\mathcal C\), whereas the open lower orthant
\[
 \mathcal O=\{c:c_N<\rho_{n,\mathsf D}\text{ for every }N\}
\]
is disjoint from \(\mathcal C\).  Here is a direct finite-dimensional separation argument.  For each positive integer \(k\), let \(q^k=(\rho_{n,\mathsf D}-1/k){\bf1}\notin\mathcal C\), and choose a closest point \(c^k\in\mathcal C\).  Such a point exists because \(\mathcal C\) is closed and the distance minimization may be restricted to a bounded ball containing \(\rho_{n,\mathsf D}{\bf1}\).  Convexity and differentiation of squared distance along the segment from \(c^k\) to any \(c\in\mathcal C\) give
\[
 (c^k-q^k)\mathbin{\cdot}(c-c^k)\geq0.
\]
Set \(\lambda^k=(c^k-q^k)/\lVert c^k-q^k\rVert_2\).  Since \(\mathcal C\) is an upper set, \(c^k+t e_N\in\mathcal C\) for every \(t\geq0\); the preceding inequality therefore gives \(\lambda_N^k\geq0\).  Also
\[
 \lVert c^k-q^k\rVert_2
 \leq\lVert\rho_{n,\mathsf D}{\bf1}-q^k\rVert_2
 =\frac{\sqrt{|\mathcal N_n|}}{k},
\]
so \(c^k\to\rho_{n,\mathsf D}{\bf1}\).  A convergent subsequence of the Euclidean unit vectors \(\lambda^k\) has a nonzero nonnegative limit \(\lambda\), and passage to the limit gives
\[
 \lambda\mathbin{\cdot}c\geq
 \rho_{n,\mathsf D}\sum_N\lambda_N\qquad(c\in\mathcal C).
\]
Normalize so that \(\sum_N\lambda_N=1\) and write \(\pi_N=\lambda_N\).  Membership of \(\rho_{n,\mathsf D}{\bf1}\) in \(\mathcal C\) makes the preceding lower bound an equality.  Since minimizing \(\lambda\mathbin{\cdot}c\) over the upper set is the same as minimizing \(\sum_N\pi_NR_N(h)\) over \(h\),
\[
 \rho_{n,\mathsf D}=\min_h\sum_N\pi_NR_N(h)
 \leq \max_{\pi'}\min_h\sum_N\pi'_NR_N(h).
\]
The reverse inequality follows for every \(h,\pi'\) from
\(
 \sum_N\pi'_NR_N(h)\leq\max_NR_N(h)
\).
Thus equality holds.  Substitution of the completed-square calculation yields
\[
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}=\max_{\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal N_n)}
 \mathcal B_{(X_1,X_0)}(\pi),\qquad
 \rho_n^W=\max_{\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal N_n)}\mathcal B_W(\pi),
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction makes every finite certificate a transparent randomization-game calculation from the four response types. \textit{Gap.} Ding and Dasgupta give the response-type table, while Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen give finite minimax duality for a different shifted-binomial experiment; neither states these two fixed-half games. \textit{Consumer.} Exact-balance software can evaluate an arbitrary full-count rule and a difference-only rule against the same finite schedule space.

\begin{theorem}[thm:n24-equality]\label{thm:n24-equality}
The certificates \((g_2,\pi_2)\) and \((g_4,\pi_4)\) are primal-dual saddles for both observation maps. \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All calculations in this proof use the kernel derived in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}response\mbox{-}type\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}games}\), rather than a labeled-assignment enumeration.

For \(n=2\), insertion of \(g_2(0)=0\) and \(g_2(\pm2)=\pm1/2\) into the hypergeometric risk gives the exhaustive table

\begin{array}{c|c|c}

\end{array}

Its only positive-predictive-mass full-count cells are \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\).  At the first cell, the target numerator and predictive denominator are, respectively,
\[
 \frac14\cdot1\cdot1=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \frac14\cdot1+\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac12,
\]
and hence the posterior mean is \(1/2=g_2(2)\); symmetry gives \(-1/2=g_2(-2)\) at the second cell.  Thus \(g_2\) is the full-count Bayes action under \(\pi_2\).  Every support-state risk is \(1/4\), so its full-count Bayes risk is \(1/4\).  The primal and dual inequalities from the preceding theorem give

 \frac14\leq\rho_2^{\mathrm{CR}}\leq\rho_2^W\leq\frac14.

The seven states outside the prior support have risk \(1/8\) or zero, strictly below \(1/4\).

 (1-z)^2=r,\qquad \frac{z^2}{3}=r.
\]
The first is the risk at each pure-effect state \((0,4,0,0)\) and \((0,0,4,0)\).  At \((2,0,0,2)\), the kernel puts masses \(1/6,2/3,1/6\) on \((2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\), respectively, so its risk is \(z^2/3=r\).

\end{array}

The multiplicities sum to \(35\).  The greatest inactive entry is \(25/32-3S/8\), and its exact slack is

\begin{proposition}[prop:posterior-fiber-change]\label{prop:posterior-fiber-change}

 =\{2,4,6\}.
\]
Because \(W=2(X_1-X_0)\) is precisely the stipulated compression of the full observation \((X_1,X_0)\), the strict inequality at \(n=8\) says exactly that this compression is minimax-lossy there.  Since no other admissible positive even size lies between six and eight, \(n=8\) is the first such size.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the finite threshold result that turns four isolated calculations into one interpretable theorem. \textit{Gap.} The closest papers leave unrestricted complete-randomization behavior unresolved and do not identify a first sample-size cutoff for count compression. \textit{Consumer.} DeclareDesign users receive the first exact sample size at which the conventional count difference ceases to be a minimax-preserving data reduction.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hull-bounded-global-reduction]\label{lem:hull-bounded-global-reduction}
Let \(n\geq1\) and \(L_{\mathrm{bd}}<U_{\mathrm{bd}}\).  In the bounded finite-population ATE problem of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}hull\mbox{-}bounded\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}risks}\), the minimax risk over all assignment designs and all measurable estimators is
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\star[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2
 \min_{d\in[0,2]^{n+1}}
 \max_{\substack{k,\ell\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\k+\ell\leq n}}
 \sum_{x=0}^n2^{-\ell}\binom{\ell}{x-k}
 \left(d_x-\frac{2k+\ell}{n}\right)^2,
\]
where \(\binom\ell r=0\) off \(r\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}\).  It is attained under independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) assignment.  More precisely, writing
\[
 A_i=\frac{Y_i(1)-L_{\mathrm{bd}}}{\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}},\qquad
 C_i=\frac{U_{\mathrm{bd}}-Y_i(0)}{\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}},\qquad
 Z_i^\circ=D_iA_i+(1-D_i)C_i\in[0,1],
\]
and \(p_x(z)=[u^x]\prod_{i=1}^n(1-z_i+z_i u)\), any endpoint action \(d\in[0,2]^{n+1}\) extends to the estimator
\(
 \Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}\{\sum_xd_xp_x(Z^\circ)-1\}.
\)
For this extension, the risk is separately convex in every coordinate of \((A,C)\in[0,1]^{2n}\), so its maximum over the full bounded cube is attained at a binary endpoint vertex and equals the maximum of the displayed shifted-binomial risks indexed by \((k,\ell)\).
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the published bridge that turns a shifted-binomial endpoint certificate into a risk bound over the entire bounded potential-outcome cube. \textit{Gap.} The present note does not reprove Hull's global all-design reduction; it supplies new exact finite certificates inside that reduction. \textit{Consumer.} The finite comparison theorem uses this result to compare balanced complete randomization against the globally optimized independent design.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hull-independent-affine-upper]\label{lem:hull-independent-affine-upper}
Let \(n\geq1\) and \(L_{\mathrm{bd}}<U_{\mathrm{bd}}\).  Under independent \(D_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), Hull's affine estimator
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{AF}}
 =\frac{\sqrt n}{\sqrt n+1}\widehat\tau^*,\qquad
 \widehat\tau^*=\frac2n\sum_{i=1}^n(2D_i-1)
 \left(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-\frac{U_{\mathrm{bd}}+L_{\mathrm{bd}}}{2}\right),
\]
has maximum squared-error risk over all schedules in \([L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]^{2n}\) equal to
\(
 \Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2/(\sqrt n+1)^2.
\)
Consequently \(\mathfrak R_n^\star[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]\leq\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2/(\sqrt n+1)^2\).
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Hull's closed-form affine rule supplies a simple certified independent-design upper bound at the three smaller even sizes. \textit{Gap.} The cited result is a global bounded-class upper certificate, while the new work is the strict finite comparison obtained from exact balanced-design binary saddles. \textit{Consumer.} The bounded-class theorem combines this upper bound with the endpoint-subclass lower bound for balanced complete randomization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:hull-class-complete-randomization-strict-small-n]\label{thm:hull-class-complete-randomization-strict-small-n}
For every \(L_{\mathrm{bd}}<U_{\mathrm{bd}}\) and every positive even \(n\), balanced complete randomization has the exact bounded-cube minimax value
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{bal}}[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}.
\]
Consequently, at the first three positive even sizes,
\[
 \mathfrak R_2^{\mathrm{bal}}=\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2}{4},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_4^{\mathrm{bal}}
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\left(1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_6^{\mathrm{bal}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2s_6^2,
\]
where all risks have arguments \([L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]\).  At \(n=8\),
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2C_8
 \leq\mathfrak R_8^{\mathrm{bal}}[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\rho_8^{\mathrm{CR}}
 \leq\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2U_8.
\]
For every \(n\in\{2,4,6,8\}\), balanced complete randomization is strictly suboptimal in Hull's class of all bounded schedules and all measurable estimators:
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\star[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 <\mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{bal}}[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}].
\]
For \(n\in\{2,4,6\}\), the certificate is
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{bal}}
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}
 >\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2}{(\sqrt n+1)^2}
 \geq\mathfrak R_n^\star.
\]
At \(n=8\), the certificate is
\[
 \mathfrak R_8^{\mathrm{bal}}
 \geq\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2C_8
 >\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2I_8
 \geq\mathfrak R_8^\star.
\]
The independent-design endpoint action \(d_8\) has normalized worst-case risk exactly \(I_8\), attained only at Hull states \((k,\ell)=(0,3)\) and \((5,3)\).  The exact value of \(\rho_8^{\mathrm{CR}}\), every comparison for even \(n\geq10\), Hull's uniform-in-\(n\) strict-suboptimality conjecture, and every asymptotic rate or constant remain open.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{bal}}[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}. \tag{11}
\]
This is a minimax-value equality obtained by a labeled-data multilinear extension, not a claim that the pair of success counts is sufficient for nonbinary schedules.

Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}n24\mbox{-}equality}\) gives
\[
 \rho_2^{\mathrm{CR}}=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \rho_4^{\mathrm{CR}}=1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}, \tag{12}
\]
and Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}n6\mbox{-}seven\mbox{-}state\mbox{-}saddle}\) gives

 \rho_6^{\mathrm{CR}}=s_6^2. \tag{13}
\]
Substitution of (12)--(13) into (11) proves the three exact bounded-class formulas in the statement.

We next compare these values with Hull's independent-design affine upper bound from Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}hull\mbox{-}independent\mbox{-}affine\mbox{-}upper}\).  At \(n=2\), \(\sqrt2>1\), and hence
\[
 \rho_2^{\mathrm{CR}}=\frac14>\frac1{(\sqrt2+1)^2}. \tag{14}
\]
At \(n=4\),

 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad

Both sides of the last inequality are positive, and it follows by squaring because

Also \(\sqrt6>2449/1000\), since both sides are positive and
\[

Equations (16)--(17) give

from Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}hull\mbox{-}independent\mbox{-}affine\mbox{-}upper}\) proves, for \(n\in\{2,4,6\}\),

 =\Delta^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}
 >\frac{\Delta^2}{(\sqrt n+1)^2}
 \geq\mathfrak R_n^\star. \tag{19}

\begin{lemma}[lem:fixed-design-endpoint-reduction]\label{lem:fixed-design-endpoint-reduction}
Let \(n\geq 1\), let \(L_{\mathrm{bd}}<U_{\mathrm{bd}}\), and let \(\delta\) be any fixed probability law on \(\{0,1\}^n\).  Put \(\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}=U_{\mathrm{bd}}-L_{\mathrm{bd}}>0\).  For \(A,C\in[0,1]^n\), define
\[
 \tau(A,C)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(A_i-C_i),
 \qquad O_D(A,C)=\bigl(D_iA_i+(1-D_i)C_i\bigr)_{i=1}^n.
\]
Define the bounded-cube fixed-design minimax risk
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\delta[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 :=\inf_{\widehat\tau\ {\rm measurable}}
 \sup_{\mathbf y\in[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]^{2n}}
 \sum_{D\in\{0,1\}^n}\delta(D)
 \left[\widehat\tau\bigl(D,(y_{iD_i})_{i=1}^n\bigr)
 -\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(y_{i1}-y_{i0})\right]^2,
\]
and define the labeled binary-endpoint minimax value
\[
 r_n^\delta
 :=\inf_{\{t_D:\{0,1\}^n\to[-1,1]\}_{D\in\{0,1\}^n}}
 \max_{(A,C)\in\{0,1\}^{2n}}
 \sum_{D\in\{0,1\}^n}\delta(D)
 \{t_D(O_D(A,C))-\tau(A,C)\}^2.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\delta[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2 r_n^\delta.
\]
In particular, if \(n\) is positive and even and \(\delta\) is uniform on \(\mathcal Z_n\), then
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{bal}}[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}.
\]
This is an equality of minimax values for a fixed design; it does not assert that two success counts, or any other binary orbit statistic, are sufficient for nonbinary schedules.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\), \(L_{\mathrm{bd}}<U_{\mathrm{bd}}\), and \(\delta\) as in the statement, and abbreviate \(\Delta=\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}>0\).  Write every bounded schedule uniquely as
\[
 y_{i1}=L_{\mathrm{bd}}+\Delta A_i,
 \qquad y_{i0}=L_{\mathrm{bd}}+\Delta C_i,
 \qquad (A,C)\in[0,1]^{2n}.
\]
Its causal average treatment effect is
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(y_{i1}-y_{i0})
 =\frac{\Delta}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(A_i-C_i)
 =\Delta\tau(A,C). \tag{1}
\]
The positivity of \(\Delta\) justifies every normalization below.

We first prove the lower bound.  The target in (1) lies in \([-\Delta,\Delta]\), so projecting any estimator onto that closed interval cannot increase its squared error at any schedule.  Hence it is enough to consider bounded estimators.  Given such an estimator \(\widehat\tau\), restrict it to endpoint observations and define
\[
 t_D(b)=\Delta^{-1}\widehat\tau
 \bigl(D,(L_{\mathrm{bd}}+\Delta b_i)_{i=1}^n\bigr),
 \qquad b\in\{0,1\}^n.
\]
Then \(t_D(b)\in[-1,1]\).  At every endpoint schedule \((A,C)\in\{0,1\}^{2n}\), the observed normalized outcome vector is exactly \(O_D(A,C)\), and (1) gives the risk identity
\[
 \sum_D\delta(D)
 \left[\widehat\tau\bigl(D,(y_{iD_i})_i\bigr)
       -\frac1n\sum_i(y_{i1}-y_{i0})\right]^2
 =\Delta^2\sum_D\delta(D)
 \{t_D(O_D(A,C))-\tau(A,C)\}^2. \tag{2}
\]
The endpoint schedules are contained in the bounded cube.  Taking the maximum over endpoints in (2), then using the definition of \(r_n^\delta\), gives for every \(\widehat\tau\)
\[
 \sup_{(A,C)\in[0,1]^{2n}}R_{\widehat\tau}(A,C)
 \geq \Delta^2 r_n^\delta.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\widehat\tau\) proves
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\delta[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 \geq\Delta^2 r_n^\delta. \tag{3}
\]

For the reverse inequality, let \(\varepsilon>0\).  By the definition of the infimum \(r_n^\delta\), there is a deterministic family \(\{t_D^\varepsilon\}\), with every action in \([-1,1]\), whose maximum binary risk is at most \(r_n^\delta+\varepsilon\).  No attainment or compactness assertion is needed.  For \(z\in[0,1]^n\), form its tensor-product multilinear extension
\[
 \overline t_D^\varepsilon(z)
 =\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^n}t_D^\varepsilon(b)
   \prod_{i=1}^n z_i^{b_i}(1-z_i)^{1-b_i}. \tag{4}
\]
The coefficients in (4) are nonnegative and sum to
\(
 \prod_i\{z_i+(1-z_i)\}=1
\), so \(\overline t_D^\varepsilon(z)\in[-1,1]\).  Moreover, if \(z=b\in\{0,1\}^n\), all summands except the one indexed by \(b\) vanish, whence
\[
 \overline t_D^\varepsilon(b)=t_D^\varepsilon(b). \tag{5}
\]
Define the bounded-outcome estimator
\[
 \widehat\tau_\varepsilon
 \bigl(D,(y_{iD_i})_{i=1}^n\bigr)
 =\Delta\,\overline t_D^\varepsilon
 \left(\left(\frac{y_{iD_i}-L_{\mathrm{bd}}}{\Delta}\right)_{i=1}^n\right)
 =\Delta\,\overline t_D^\varepsilon(O_D(A,C)). \tag{6}
\]
For each fixed \(D\), (4) is a polynomial, and the assignment space is finite; hence (6) is measurable.

We now locate the maximum risk of (6).  Define its normalized error at assignment \(D\) by
\[
 e_D(A,C)=\overline t_D^\varepsilon(O_D(A,C))-\tau(A,C).
\]
Fix all coordinates except \(A_j\).  If \(D_j=1\), the multilinearity in (4) and the identity \(O_{D,j}=A_j\) show that the first term in \(e_D\) is affine in \(A_j\); if \(D_j=0\), that term is constant in \(A_j\).  In either case \(\tau(A,C)\) is affine in \(A_j\), so \(e_D(A,C)\) is affine in \(A_j\).  The same argument applies to \(C_j\): the first term is affine when \(D_j=0\) and constant when \(D_j=1\), while the target is affine.  Thus, for every assignment and every one of the \(2n\) potential-outcome coordinates, \(e_D\) is affine in that coordinate.  Its square is therefore convex in that coordinate, because if \(e_D(r)=\alpha r+\beta\), then
\[
 \frac{d^2}{dr^2}e_D(r)^2=2\alpha^2\geq0. \tag{7}
\]
The normalized assignment-averaged risk
\[
 Q_\varepsilon(A,C)=\sum_D\delta(D)e_D(A,C)^2
\]
is separately convex as a nonnegative weighted sum of the functions in (7); here \(\delta(D)\geq0\) and \(\sum_D\delta(D)=1\) because \(\delta\) is a probability law.

For completeness, separate convexity alone gives the required endpoint reduction.  If \(f\) is convex in one coordinate and that coordinate equals \(r\in[0,1]\), then
\[
 f(\ldots,r,\ldots)
 \leq(1-r)f(\ldots,0,\ldots)+r f(\ldots,1,\ldots)
 \leq\max_{c\in\{0,1\}}f(\ldots,c,\ldots). \tag{8}
\]
Applying (8) successively to all \(2n\) coordinates yields
\[
 \sup_{(A,C)\in[0,1]^{2n}}Q_\varepsilon(A,C)
 =\max_{(A,C)\in\{0,1\}^{2n}}Q_\varepsilon(A,C). \tag{9}
\]
Equality follows because the endpoint set on the right is a subset of the cube.  On that endpoint set, (5) identifies \(Q_\varepsilon\) with the binary risk of \(t^\varepsilon\).  Equations (6) and (9) therefore imply
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\delta[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 \leq\Delta^2(r_n^\delta+\varepsilon).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and combining with (3) proves
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^\delta[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta^2r_n^\delta. \tag{10}
\]

Finally suppose that \(n\) is positive and even and \(\delta\) is uniform on \(\mathcal Z_n\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}response\mbox{-}type\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}games}\) proves, on the binary endpoint experiment, that permutation averaging loses no minimax value and that the resulting full-count game has value \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}\).  Hence \(r_n^\delta=\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}\), and (10) becomes
\[
 \mathfrak R_n^{\mathrm{bal}}[L_{\mathrm{bd}},U_{\mathrm{bd}}]
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{bd}}^2\rho_n^{\mathrm{CR}}.
\]
Only this last binary identification uses the response-type orbit theorem; the extension (4)--(10) uses the full labeled observation and makes no nonbinary sufficiency claim.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The rational full-count certificates at \(n=8\) prove only \(C_8\leq\rho_8^{\mathrm{CR}}\leq U_8\), with \(C_8<U_8\), so the exact binary and bounded-cube balanced minimax values remain unknown. An arbitrary prior can make the second count Bayes-informative even where the minimax values agree. The fixed-design endpoint result is an equality of minimax values obtained by tensor-product extension of a full labeled binary rule; it does not prove that two counts, or any other binary orbit statistic, suffice for nonbinary schedules.

\section{Honest scope}

The theorem-level scope consists of the exact binary response-type games, equality of full-count and difference-only risks at \(n=2,4,6\), strict count-compression separation and a two-sided full-count bracket at \(n=8\), the resulting first-lossy-size conclusion, the fixed-design endpoint minimax-value reduction, exact bounded-cube balanced risks at two, four, and six, a bounded-cube bracket at eight, and strict suboptimality of balanced complete randomization relative to the globally optimized design at those four sizes. The paper makes no confidence-set claim, pointwise-dominance claim, exact \(\rho_8^{\mathrm{CR}}\) claim, or nonbinary two-count sufficiency claim. Strict count-compression persistence for even \(n\geq10\), Hull's uniform complete-randomization conjecture, and every asymptotic rate, exponent, parity law, or constant for the balance or compression tax remain open.
