%%% FIELD finite-information-transfer-proof
For a legal deterministic array \(\theta=(\theta_{dj})\), let \(P^{\mathcal F}_{K,\theta}\) be the observable law of \(\mathcal O_K=((Z_{di},Y_{di}))_{d,i}\) specified by the uniform-label, independent-arm, and conditional-Bernoulli assumptions.  The array \(\theta\) is unobserved.  By Proposition \texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}, the causal target attached to this law is the sharp coupling endpoint
\[
 t_K(\theta):=L_K(\theta)=a-b\rho_K(\theta),
 \qquad
 \rho_K(\theta)=K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^K\theta_{0j}\theta_{1j}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The marked-Poisson target is the distinct decision functional \(t(\rho):=a-b\rho\).  The supported mixtures below are used only to compare experiments; equation (1) and an explicit target-discrepancy bound relate their proxy target \(t(q)\) to the realized causal target.

The proof uses bounded-loss total-variation comparison, a supported-mixture reduction for the lower bound, and explicit couplings for changes in the marked-Poisson parameter and intensity.  First note the following elementary bounded-loss fact.  If probability measures \(P,Q\) are on the same measurable space and \(0\le g\le B\), then the layer-cake representation gives
\[
 \left|\int g\,dP-\int g\,dQ\right|
 \le \int_0^B\left|P\{g>u\}-Q\{g>u\}\right|\,du
 \le B\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The same argument applied to the functions supplied by a Markov kernel proves contraction of total variation under randomization.  Because every target in this proof belongs to \(\mathcal A=[a-br,a+br]\), projection of an endpoint action onto \(\mathcal A\) cannot increase absolute loss.  Thus all rules may be taken to have values in \(\mathcal A\), and then their absolute loss is bounded by \(2br\).

Define the deterministic-statistic and supported-mixture errors
\[
 d_K:=\min\left\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\frac{\lambda_K^2}{2K}\right\},
 \qquad
 e_K:=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\},
 \tag{3}
\]
and put
\[
 \delta_K:=2s_K+\frac2K,
 \qquad
 \omega_K:=\min\left\{1,\frac{\lambda_Kc\delta_K}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

We first obtain an upper comparison over all legal deterministic arrays.  Let \(D_\lambda\in\mathscr D_{\lambda_K}\) be any marked-Poisson rule, projected onto \(\mathcal A\), and apply it to the positive- and negative-mark counts extracted from \((M_K,S_1,\ldots,S_{M_K})\).  This is a single measurable finite-array rule and does not depend on \(\theta\).  For every \(\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)\), the deterministic part of Theorem \texttt{thm:collision-comparison}, (1), (2), and (3) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\left|D_\lambda-L_K(\theta)\right|
 \le
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_K,\rho_K(\theta)}
 \left|D_\lambda-t(\rho_K(\theta))\right|+2brd_K.
 \tag{5}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\theta\), then the infimum over \(D_\lambda\), gives the finite-sample upper comparison
\[
 R_K\le R(\lambda_K)+2brd_K.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus the upper bound uses only the deterministic collision statistic and targets the realized endpoint in (1).

For the reverse comparison, fix an arbitrary finite-array rule \(D_K\in\mathscr D_K\), again projected onto \(\mathcal A\), and denote its maximal deterministic-array risk by
\[
 \mathcal R_K(D_K):=sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \mathbb E_\theta|D_K-L_K(\theta)|.
 \tag{7}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(K\), Proposition \texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality} makes \(\mathcal R_K^{\circ}(r)\) nonempty.  For each \(q\) in that lattice, the mixture is supported on \(\mathcal T_K(r)\) and satisfies \(|L_K(\theta)-t(q)|\le bs_K\) throughout its support.  The definition of expectation under the mixture law and the triangle inequality therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm{mix}}_{K,q}}|D_K-t(q)|
 &=\int \mathbb E_\theta|D_K-t(q)|\,
      d\Pi^{\mathrm{shell}}_{K,q}(\theta)\\
 &\le \int\!\left\{
       \mathbb E_\theta|D_K-L_K(\theta)|
       +|L_K(\theta)-t(q)|\right\}
       d\Pi^{\mathrm{shell}}_{K,q}(\theta)\\
 &\le \mathcal R_K(D_K)+bs_K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Compose \(D_K\) with the parameter-free reverse kernel \(\mathsf K_K^{\leftarrow}\) supplied by Theorem \texttt{thm:collision-comparison}, and call the resulting single marked-Poisson rule \(\widetilde D_K\).  The supported-mixture part of that theorem, (2), (3), and (8) imply, uniformly over \(q\in\mathcal R_K^{\circ}(r)\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_K,q}|\widetilde D_K-t(q)|
 \le \mathcal R_K(D_K)+bs_K+2bre_K.
 \tag{9}
\]

It remains to pass from the interior lattice to all \(\rho\in[-r,r]\).  Set \(q=q_K(\rho)\), so that Proposition \texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality} gives \(|q-\rho|\le\delta_K\).  Couple \(\mathcal E_{\lambda_K,\rho}\) and \(\mathcal E_{\lambda_K,q}\) by using the same \(N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K)\) and the same independent uniform variables for their marks.  A single mark differs with probability
\[
 \left|\frac{1+c\rho}{2}-\frac{1+cq}{2}\right|
 =\frac c2|\rho-q|.
\]
Conditional on \(N\), a union bound, followed by expectation, therefore shows that the entire marked observations disagree with probability at most
\[
 \min\left\{1,\mathbb E[N]\frac c2|\rho-q|\right\}
 \le \omega_K.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently their total-variation distance is at most \(\omega_K\).  Since \(|t(\rho)-t(q)|=b|\rho-q|\le b\delta_K\), equations (2), (9), and (10) give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_K,\rho}|\widetilde D_K-t(\rho)|
 \le \mathcal R_K(D_K)+b(s_K+\delta_K)
       +2br(e_K+\omega_K).
 \tag{11}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\rho\in[-r,r]\), using \(\widetilde D_K\) as an admissible rule in the definition of \(R(\lambda_K)\), and then taking the infimum over \(D_K\), proves
\[
 R(\lambda_K)
 \le R_K+b(s_K+\delta_K)+2br(e_K+\omega_K).
 \tag{12}
\]
This establishes the lower comparison using only mixtures supported on legal deterministic arrays.  In particular, no mixture target has been substituted for \(L_K\): the two discrepancies are paid explicitly in (8) and (11).

We next prove continuity of the limit decision frontier.  For \(\lambda,\lambda'\ge0\) and fixed \(\rho\), couple the two marked Poisson processes by retaining a common process of intensity \(\min\{\lambda,\lambda'\}\) and adding an independent process of intensity \(|\lambda-\lambda'|\) to the larger one, with all points marked according to \((1+c\rho)/2\).  The observations agree whenever the added process is empty, so, uniformly in \(\rho\),
\[
 \left\|P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda,\rho}
       -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda',\rho}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le 1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}
 \le |\lambda-\lambda'|.
 \tag{13}
\]
Both experiments have the same count space and the same target \(t(\rho)\).  Applying (2) to any projected rule, then taking the parameter supremum and the rule infimum in both directions, yields
\[
 |R(\lambda)-R(\lambda')|
 \le 2br\bigl(1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}\bigr).
 \tag{14}
\]
Hence \(R\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)\), including at zero.

Finally suppose \(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\).  By Lemma \texttt{lem:vanishing-triples-implies-growing-cells}, \(K\to\infty\).  Since \((\lambda_K)\) is bounded, the definition of \(s_K\) gives, for all sufficiently large \(K\),
\[
 s_K=\{K(1\vee\lambda_K)\}^{-1/4}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 Ks_K^2=\sqrt{\frac{K}{1\vee\lambda_K}}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus \(\eta_K=2e^{-Ks_K^2/2}\to0\).  The vanishing-triples assumption, boundedness of \(\lambda_K\), and (3)--(4) now imply
\[
 e_K\to0,
 \qquad d_K\to0,
 \qquad \delta_K\to0,
 \qquad \omega_K\to0.
 \tag{16}
\]
By (14), \(R(\lambda_K)\to R(\lambda)\).  The upper and lower comparisons (6) and (12), together with (16), squeeze \(R_K\) to \(R(\lambda)\).  Therefore
\[
 R_K\longrightarrow R(\lambda).
\]

%%% FIELD finite-information-transfer-statement
If \(\lambda_K\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)\), then \(R_K\to R(\lambda)\). The lower bound ranges over supported mixtures on legal deterministic arrays, and the upper bound uses only the deterministic collision statistic; both sides target the realized \(L_K\).
