%%% FIELD proof-honest-interval-transfer
We use total variation in the convention
\(\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|\).
Two elementary consequences used below are
\[
 \lVert P\mathsf K-Q\mathsf K\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 |\mathbb E_Pg-\mathbb E_Qg|
 \le B\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \tag{1}
\]
for every Markov kernel \(\mathsf K\) and every measurable
\(g\) with \(0\le g\le B\).  The first inequality follows by
integrating the test function \(\mathsf K(\,cdot\,,B)\in[0,1]\);
the second follows from the layer-cake representation
\(g=\int_0^B\mathbf 1\{g>t\}\,dt\).

Define
\[
 e_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\},\qquad
 \delta_K=2s_K+2/K,
\]
\[
 u_K=e_K+\min\{1,\lambda_Kc\delta_K/2\},
 \qquad
 d_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\lambda_K^2/(2K)\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Fix \(\zeta>0\), and choose
\(\mathcal C_K\in\mathscr C_K\) that is honest over
\(\mathcal T_K(r)\) and whose worst expected length is at most
\(C_{K,\alpha}+\zeta\).  For \(\rho\in[-r,r]\), put
\(q=q_K(\rho)\).  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) and Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) give
\[
 |q-\rho|\le\delta_K,
 \qquad
 |L_K-(a-bq)|\le bs_K
 \tag{3}
\]
on the support of \(\Pi_{K,q}^{\mathrm{shell}}\).  Integrating the
finite-array coverage inequality over this supported mixture and
expanding each endpoint of the reported interval by \(bs_K\), with
clipping to \(\mathcal A\), therefore gives coverage of \(a-bq\) at
least \(1-\alpha\).

For later use, couple two marked-Poisson observations at parameters
\(\rho,\rho'\) by using the same Poisson count and the same independent
uniform variable for each mark.  A mark differs with probability
\(c|\rho-\rho'|/2\), so a union bound conditional on the count and then
averaging over its Poisson law give
\[
 \left\lVert P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho}
 -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho'}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\min\{1,\lambda_Kc|\rho-\rho'|/2\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Apply \(\mathcal C_K\) after the parameter-free reverse kernel of
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\), and expand each endpoint
once more by \(b\delta_K\).  Equations (1)--(4) show that, under
\(P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho}\), the resulting interval covers
\(a-b\rho\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha-u_K\).  Since every
interval lies in \(\mathcal A\), its length is bounded by \(2br\), and
its worst expected length is at most
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}+\zeta+2b(s_K+\delta_K)+2bru_K.
 \tag{5}
\]

We now record an explicit coverage repair.  If a rule has coverage at
least \(1-\alpha-u\), independently replace its output by
\(\mathcal A\) with probability
\[
 \gamma(u)=\frac{u}{\alpha+u}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The assumptions \(\alpha>0\) and \(u\ge0\) make the division valid and
give \(0\le\gamma(u)\le1\).  The repaired coverage is at least
\[
 \gamma(u)+(1-\gamma(u))(1-\alpha-u)=1-\alpha,
 \tag{7}
\]
and the expected length rises by at most \(2br\gamma(u)\).  Applying
(6)--(7) to (5) with \(u=u_K\), and then letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\),
yields
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda_K)
 \le C_{K,\alpha}+2b(s_K+\delta_K)+2bru_K
 +\frac{2bru_K}{\alpha+u_K}.
 \tag{8}
\]

Conversely, take an honest marked-Poisson rule at intensity
\(\lambda_K\) with worst expected length at most
\(C_\alpha(\lambda_K)+\zeta\), and apply it to the deterministic
collision statistic.  The deterministic assertion of Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\), the identity
\(L_K=a-b\rho_K\) from Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\), and (1)--(2) show that coverage
falls by at most \(d_K\) and expected length rises by at most
\(2brd_K\).  Repairing by (6) with \(u=d_K\) and letting
\(\zeta\downarrow0\) gives
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}
 \le C_\alpha(\lambda_K)+2brd_K
 +\frac{2brd_K}{\alpha+d_K}.
 \tag{9}
\]

The marked-Poisson frontier is continuous in its intensity.  A common
Poisson-process construction at intensities \(\lambda\) and
\(\lambda'\), with identical marks on the common points, gives,
uniformly in \(\rho\),
\[
 v(\lambda,\lambda')
 :=\left\lVert P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda,\rho}
 -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda',\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}.
\]
Transporting an honest rule in either direction loses at most \(v\) in
coverage and changes expected length by at most \(2brv\).  Applying
(6) in both directions proves
\[
 |C_\alpha(\lambda)-C_\alpha(\lambda')|
 \le2brv+\frac{2brv}{\alpha+v}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Suppose now that \(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\).  Lemma
\(\texttt{lem:vanishing-triples-implies-growing-cells}\) gives
\(K\to\infty\).  From the definition of \(s_K\), boundedness of
\(\lambda_K\) implies
\(s_K\to0\), \(Ks_K^2\to\infty\), and hence \(\eta_K\to0\).
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:vanishing-triples}\) then gives
\(e_K\to0\), while boundedness of \(\lambda_K\) gives
\(\lambda_K^2/K\to0\).  Consequently \(u_K,d_K\to0\).
Equations (8)--(10) prove
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}\longrightarrow C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{11}
\]

We next verify the programs in
\(\texttt{def:certified-interval-lp}\) on their corrected admissible
domain.  Fix a computable \(\lambda\ge0\), a grid of mesh at most
\(h\), and an integer \(m\) with \(m+1>\lambda\).  For every
sufficiently fine directed enclosure,
\(\lambda_s^+<m+1\); equivalently, a certification tuple is admissible
only when \(m+1>\lambda_s^+\).  The coupling in (4), now at fixed
\(\lambda\), proves
\[
 \lVert f_{\lambda,\rho}-f_{\lambda,\rho'}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le
 \omega_\lambda(|\rho-\rho'|)
 :=\min\{1,\lambda c|\rho-\rho'|/2\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
By (1), a fixed event has this probability modulus and a
\([0,2br]\)-valued length has expectation modulus
\(2br\omega_\lambda\).

For the tail certificate, \(\tau_m(0)=0\).  If \(0<\lambda<m+1\),
the exponential Markov inequality gives, for every \(t>0\),
\[
 \Pr_\lambda(N\ge m+1)
 \le\exp\{\lambda(e^t-1)-t(m+1)\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The exponent is minimized at
\(t=\log((m+1)/\lambda)>0\), and substitution gives
\[
 \tau_m(\lambda)
 \le \overline\tau_m(\lambda)
 :=e^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{e\lambda}{m+1}\right)^{m+1}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Moreover,
\(d\log\overline\tau_m(x)/dx=(m+1)/x-1>0\) for
\(0<x<m+1\).  Thus admissibility
\(\lambda\le\lambda_s^+<m+1\) justifies the directed tail enclosure
\[
 \tau_m(\lambda)
 \le\overline\tau_m(\lambda)
 \le\overline\tau_m(\lambda_s^+).
 \tag{15}
\]
This is the only domain on which the displayed Chernoff quantity is
used as a certificate.

For the lower certificate, begin with any continuum-honest randomized
interval rule.  On each retained state, replace each realized interval
by the shortest grid action spanning exactly the grid targets it
contains, or by \(\varnothing_G\) if it contains none.  This preserves
grid-target membership and cannot increase length.  Deleting overflow
loses at most \(\tau_m(\lambda)\) in coverage.  The induced weights are
therefore feasible for the exact lower program and have objective no
larger than the original worst expected length.  Replacing coverage
coefficients upward and coverage right sides downward enlarges its
feasible set, while replacing nonnegative length contributions
downward cannot increase its recorded objective.  Hence the exactly
rounded rational optimum satisfies
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\le C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{16}
\]

For the upper certificate, take an exactly feasible solution of the
directed rational upper program, whose feasibility holds for all
sufficiently fine admissible \(s\).  Its rounding directions imply
actual coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) and actual expected length at
most \(P_{m,h,s}^+\) at every grid node; on overflow it reports
\(\mathcal A\).  For arbitrary \(\rho\), choose a nearest grid point
\(\rho_j\), so \(|\rho-\rho_j|\le h/2\).  If an action covers
\(a-b\rho_j\), its expansion \(E_h\) covers \(a-b\rho\).  Equation
(12) shows that the expanded rule has coverage at least
\(1-\alpha-\omega_h\), where
\[
 \omega_h=\min\{1,\lambda ch/4\}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Repair by (6) with
\(\gamma_h=\omega_h/(\alpha+\omega_h)\).  Expansion adds at most
\(bh\), parameter transfer adds at most \(2br\omega_h\) to expected
length, and repair adds at most \(2br\gamma_h\).  Directed upper
enclosure of every term proves
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \le P_{m,h,s}^++bh+2br\omega_h+2br\gamma_h
 =U_{m,h,s}^+.
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus (16)--(18) certify coverage on every parameter segment, not only
at grid nodes.

After directed enclosure, both programs have finitely many variables
and rational coefficients.  The row-sum and nonnegativity constraints
place their weights in a finite product of simplices, and the
always-full interval makes the upper program feasible once the
aggregate rounding error is smaller than the coverage slack
\(\alpha\).  A linear objective attains its minimum on a face
containing a vertex.  Every vertex is obtained by solving one of
finitely many square rational systems selected from active constraints;
hence it is rational, and finite basis enumeration finds the optimum
exactly.  The same enumeration applied to the algebraic dual gives
rational dual multipliers.  Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and
equality of their rational objectives certify optimality because their
objective gap is a sum of products of nonnegative primal slacks and
nonnegative dual multipliers.  Floating-point calculations may propose
a basis but are not part of this certificate.

It remains to prove contraction.  Let \(\xi_s\) dominate, uniformly
over the finitely many constraint rows, the sum of all directed widths
of the likelihood, tail, right-side, action-length, and correction
quantities entering that row.  The aggregate budget in the definition
gives a finite constant \(C_0\), depending only on the bounded fixed
inputs, such that
\[
 0\le\xi_s\le C_0 2^{-s}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{19}
\]
Fix \(\zeta>0\), and take a continuum-honest rule with worst expected
length at most \(C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta\).  Replace its overflow
action by \(\mathcal A\), at a cost of at most
\(2br\tau_m(\lambda)\).  On retained states, round both endpoints
outward to adjacent target-grid endpoints.  Coverage is preserved, the
resulting action belongs to \(\mathsf A_h\), and its length rises by at
most \(2bh\).  For sufficiently large \(s\), mix this rule with
\(\mathcal A\) with probability
\[
 \beta_s=\frac{2\xi_s}{\alpha}\le1.
 \tag{20}
\]
Before coefficient rounding this creates coverage slack
\(\alpha\beta_s=2\xi_s\), which absorbs every aggregate directed error.
The resulting weights are feasible for the rational upper program and
have directed objective at most
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta+2br\tau_m(\lambda)+2bh
 +2br\beta_s+\xi_s.
 \tag{21}
\]
After adding the expansion, modulus, repair, and enclosure corrections
in (18), take any refinement with \(m\to\infty\), \(h\downarrow0\),
\(s\to\infty\), and \(m+1>\lambda_s^+\).  Equation (14) gives
\(\tau_m(\lambda)\to0\), while (17) and (19)--(21) give, after
\(\zeta\downarrow0\),
\[
 \limsup U_{m,h,s}^+\le C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{22}
\]

For the reverse contraction, take an optimal rational lower-program
rule.  Interpret \(\varnothing_G\) as a degenerate interval at any
point of \(\mathcal A\) that is not one of the finitely many grid
targets; such a point exists because \(br>0\).  This interval has zero
length and preserves every grid-coverage pattern.  By the definition
of \(\xi_s\), directed rounding implies actual retained gridwise
coverage at least \(1-\alpha-\tau_m(\lambda)-\xi_s\) and actual retained
expected length at most \(D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s\).  Report
\(\mathcal A\) on overflow, expand every retained endpoint by
\(bh/2\), and use (12) from a nearest grid point.  The resulting rule
has continuum coverage at least
\[
 1-\alpha-(\xi_s+\omega_h)
 \tag{23}
\]
and worst expected length at most
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s+2br\tau_m(\lambda)+bh+2br\omega_h.
 \tag{24}
\]
One application of (6) with \(u=\xi_s+\omega_h\) makes (23) honest.
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \le{}&D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s+2br\tau_m(\lambda)+bh+2br\omega_h\\
 &+\frac{2br(\xi_s+\omega_h)}
 {\alpha+\xi_s+\omega_h}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{25}
\]
Every term following \(D_{m,h,s}^-\) vanishes along the admissible
refinement.  Combining (16), (22), and (25) proves
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\to C_\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
 U_{m,h,s}^+\to C_\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
 U_{m,h,s}^+-D_{m,h,s}^-\to0.
 \tag{26}
\]
In particular, at \(\lambda=1\) and \(\alpha=0.05\), every
sufficiently fine admissible certified upper rule has coverage at least
\(0.95\) on every parameter segment, and any admissible sequence that
doubles the interval-action resolution while sending \(m,s\) to
infinity has certified bracket width tending to zero.

Finally, the deterministic implementation is exactly the construction
behind (9): transport a marked upper rule through the deterministic
collision statistic, pay \(d_K\) in coverage and \(2brd_K\) in
expected length, and mix once with \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\(d_K/(\alpha+d_K)\).  The reverse inequality is (8), based on the
parameter-free supported-mixture reverse kernel.  Hence both the exact
interval-action certification and the two-sided honest-minimax
transfer concern the realized sharp endpoint \(L_K\), uniformly over
all legal deterministic arrays.

%%% FIELD proof-interpolation-transfer
For arm \(d\), put \(a_0=a\), \(a_1=b\), and define the step function
\[
 g^{\mathrm{step}}_{d,K,\theta}(u)
 =p_{d,\kappa_K(u)}.
\]
Fix a boundary \(j/K\), and write \(\delta=K^{-3}\) and
\(\Delta p=p_{d,j+1}-p_{dj}\).  On the left half
\([j/K-\delta,j/K]\), the affine connector differs from the left step
value \(p_{dj}\) by a triangle of base \(\delta\) and height
\(|\Delta p|/2\), so its integral is
\[
 \frac12\delta\frac{|\Delta p|}{2}
 =\frac{\delta|\Delta p|}{4}.
 \tag{27}
\]
On the right half \([j/K,j/K+\delta]\), its difference from the right
step value \(p_{d,j+1}\) is a second triangle with the same base and
height, and hence contributes another
\(\delta|\Delta p|/4\).  Thus the full strip contributes exactly
\[
 \frac{\delta}{2}|p_{d,j+1}-p_{dj}|.
 \tag{28}
\]
The strips are disjoint for \(K\ge2\).  By
\(\texttt{ass:sign-array}\) and the definitions of the cell
probabilities,
\(|p_{d,j+1}-p_{dj}|\le2a_d\).  Summing (28) over the
\(K-1\) boundaries gives
\[
 \int_0^1
 |g_{d,K,\theta}(u)-g^{\mathrm{step}}_{d,K,\theta}(u)|\,du
 \le (K-1)K^{-3}a_d
 \le\frac{a_d}{K^2}.
 \tag{29}
\]
This calculation validates the factor \(1/2\) because the construction
in \(\texttt{def:interpolation-image}\) joins the two cell values
across a strip having two halves of width \(K^{-3}\).

Couple a continuous observation and its step counterpart by using the
same uniform \(U_{di}\), the same label \(\kappa_K(U_{di})\), and the
same independent uniform variable to generate the Bernoulli outcome.
For one observation the mismatch probability is the integral in (29).
A union bound over all observations, followed by data processing
through the measurable pushforward \(\mathsf Q_K\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \left\lVert P^{\mathrm c}_{K,\theta}\mathsf Q_K
       -P^{\mathcal F}_{K,\theta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\le\frac{n_0a+n_1b}{K^2}\\
 &\le\frac{2(n_0+n_1)}{K^2},
\end{aligned}
 \tag{30}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(0<a,b<1/2\).  Conversely, the
parameter-free kernel that adds an independent uniform position within
each observed cell converts the discrete observation into the step
experiment.  The same coupling proves a reverse deficiency bound with
the sharper first right side of (30).  This is a comparison through
explicit kernels, not a total-variation comparison of raw observations
on different sample spaces.

For Bernoulli laws with success probabilities \(p_0,p_1\), the sharp
absolute-cost coupling value is \(|p_1-p_0|\), by the pointwise
coupling argument in Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\).  Therefore
\[
 L_K^{\mathrm c}
 =\int_0^1|g_{1,K,\theta}(u)-g_{0,K,\theta}(u)|\,du.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality and (29) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |L_K^{\mathrm c}-L_K|
 &\le
 \sum_{d=0}^1\int_0^1
 |g_{d,K,\theta}-g^{\mathrm{step}}_{d,K,\theta}|\,du\\
 &\le\frac{a+b}{K^2}
 \le\frac{2(a+b)}{K^2}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{31}
\]
Both (30) and (31) hold uniformly over the deterministic signs.

Composing a discrete rule with \(\mathsf Q_K\), or a continuous rule
with the uniform-within-cell reverse kernel, changes every bounded risk
or coverage probability by no more than (30).  Expanding an interval
by the target discrepancy in (31), followed when necessary by the
full-interval repair proved in Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:honest-interval-transfer}\), preserves honesty.  Because
\(n_0n_1\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{n_0+n_1}{K^2}
 \le\frac{n_0n_1(n_0+n_1)}{K^2}
 =\varepsilon_K\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{32}
\]
Thus the experiment-comparison error vanishes under
\(\texttt{ass:vanishing-triples}\), and the target error in (31)
vanishes because Lemma
\(\texttt{lem:vanishing-triples-implies-growing-cells}\) gives
\(K\to\infty\).  This transfers the finite-collision decision and
honest-interval conclusions at the actual endpoint
\(L_K^{\mathrm c}\).

In the sparse regime \(\lambda_K\to\infty\), the identity
\[
 \varepsilon_K
 =\lambda_K\frac{n_0+n_1}{K}
\]
and \(\varepsilon_K\to0\) imply
\((n_0+n_1)/K\to0\).  Hence
\[
 \frac{\lambda_K}{K}
 =\frac{n_0n_1}{K^2}
 \le\frac14\left(\frac{n_0+n_1}{K}\right)^2
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{33}
\]
Using (31) and \(K\to\infty\),
\[
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}|L_K^{\mathrm c}-L_K|
 \le\frac{2(a+b)\sqrt{\lambda_K}}{K^2}
 =\frac{2(a+b)}{K^{3/2}}
   \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_K}{K}}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{34}
\]
Also, \(0<c<1\) and \(|\rho_K|\le r<1\) imply the uniform positivity
bound
\[
 V(\rho_K)\ge\frac{1-c^2r^2}{16a^2}>0.
\]
Let \(T_K\) be the studentized statistic centered at \(L_K\), and let
\(T_K^{\mathrm c}\) be the same collision statistic centered at
\(L_K^{\mathrm c}\).  Uniform consistency of \(\widehat V_K\), supplied
by Theorem \(\texttt{thm:sparse-gaussian}\), and the displayed positive
lower bound imply
\[
 T_K^{\mathrm c}-T_K
 =\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_K}(L_K-L_K^{\mathrm c})}
 {\sqrt{\widehat V_K}}
 \longrightarrow0
\]
in probability uniformly over the array class.  The law of the
collision statistic after coarsening differs from its discrete law by
at most (30).  For every real \(x\) and every \(\epsilon>0\), the event
inclusions on \(\{|T_K^{\mathrm c}-T_K|\le\epsilon\}\) sandwich its
distribution function between those of \(T_K\) at
\(x-\epsilon\) and \(x+\epsilon\), up to the probability of the
complement and the total-variation error.  First letting \(K\to\infty\)
and then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), continuity of the standard normal
distribution proves the uniform Gaussian limit and the corresponding
two-sided quantile coverage directly.  Thus Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:sparse-gaussian}\), including its unprojected Wald
coverage, transfers from \(L_K\) to \(L_K^{\mathrm c}\).  The
construction, coarsening kernel, and reverse kernel are defined only on
\(\mathcal I_K(r)\); therefore none of these conclusions extends to a
larger continuous smoothness class without a separate approximation
and endpoint-bias theorem.

%%% FIELD proposed-honest-interval-statement
If \(\lambda_K\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)\), then
\(C_{K,\alpha}\to C_\alpha(\lambda)\).  For every computable
\(\lambda\), every grid mesh \(h\), every integer \(m\) satisfying
\(m+1>\lambda\), and all sufficiently large certification precisions
\(s\) for which the directed enclosure satisfies
\(m+1>\lambda_s^+\), the procedure
\(\operatorname{LP}_{m,h,s}^{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)\) returns exactly
verified rational values satisfying
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\le C_\alpha(\lambda)\le U_{m,h,s}^+.
\]
The upper rule uses the overflow action \(\mathcal A\), endpoint
expansion \(E_h\), and full-interval repair probability \(\gamma_h\),
and therefore has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) for every
\(\rho\in[-r,r]\), not only at grid points.  Along any refinement with
\(m\to\infty\), \(h\downarrow0\), \(s\to\infty\), and
\(m+1>\lambda_s^+\), one has
\(U_{m,h,s}^+-D_{m,h,s}^-\to0\), and both endpoints converge to
\(C_\alpha(\lambda)\).  Write
\(d_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\lambda_K^2/(2K)\}\).  Applying the
corrected upper rule through the deterministic collision statistic
loses at most \(d_K\) in coverage; mixing once more with
\(\mathcal A\) with probability \(d_K/(\alpha+d_K)\) restores coverage
at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal T_K(r)\) and adds at
most \(2brd_K/(\alpha+d_K)\) in expected length.  The matching lower
certificate transfers through the supported-mixture reverse kernel,
so the finite-array worst-case expected length converges to
\(C_\alpha(\lambda)\).

%%% FIELD corrected-certified-interval-lp
Fix a computable \(\lambda\ge0\), integers \(m,s\ge1\), and a grid
\(G_h=\{-r=\rho_0<\cdots<\rho_J=r\}\) of rational or computable-real
nodes with maximum adjacent gap at most \(h\).  The certification tuple
is admissible only after directed evaluation has produced a rational
upper enclosure \(\lambda_s^+\) satisfying
\[
 \lambda\le\lambda_s^+<m+1.
 \tag{A0}
\]
Equivalently, for every fixed \(m\) with \(m+1>\lambda\), all
sufficiently fine directed enclosures are admissible.  Let
\(\mathsf X_m=\{(k,l)\in\mathbb N_0^2:k+l\le m\}\), append an overflow
state \(\star\), and put
\(\tau_m(\lambda)=\Pr\{\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)>m\}\).  On the
admissible domain, the Chernoff calculation gives
\[
 \tau_m(\lambda)\le\overline\tau_m(\lambda)
 \le\overline\tau_m(\lambda_s^+),\qquad
 \overline\tau_m(x):=
 e^{-x}\left(\frac{ex}{m+1}\right)^{m+1}.
 \tag{A1}
\]
Its worst effects are at most
\(\overline\tau_m(\lambda_s^+)\) on coverage and
\(2br\overline\tau_m(\lambda_s^+)\) on expected length.  No use of
this Chernoff enclosure is made when (A0) fails.

The finite action grid is
\[
 \mathsf A_h=\{[a-b\rho_v,a-b\rho_u]:0\le u\le v\le J\}.
\]
The lower relaxation additionally admits a null grid-coverage pattern
of length zero.  For any \(\rho,\rho'\in[-r,r]\), common Poisson counts
and common-uniform-coupled Bernoulli marks give
\[
 \lVert f_{\lambda,\rho}-f_{\lambda,\rho'}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\omega_\lambda(|\rho-\rho'|)
 :=\min\{1,\lambda c|\rho-\rho'|/2\}.
\]
Thus a fixed event has this probability modulus and a
\([0,2br]\)-valued length has expectation modulus
\(2br\omega_\lambda\).

For the lower program let
\(\mathsf A_h^-=\mathsf A_h\cup\{\varnothing_G\}\), where
\(\varnothing_G\) has zero length and covers no grid target.  With
\(t_j=a-b\rho_j\), its exact-coefficient form is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname*{minimize}_{t,(w_{x,A})}\quad &t,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&
 w_{x,A}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{A\in\mathsf A_h^-}w_{x,A}=1
 &&(x\in\mathsf X_m),\\
 &\sum_{x\in\mathsf X_m} f_{\lambda,\rho_j}(x)
   \sum_{\substack{A\in\mathsf A_h^-\\t_j\in A}}w_{x,A}
   \ge1-\alpha-\tau_m(\lambda)
 &&(0\le j\le J),\\
 &\sum_{x\in\mathsf X_m} f_{\lambda,\rho_j}(x)
   \sum_{A\in\mathsf A_h^-}\operatorname{len}(A)w_{x,A}
   \le t
 &&(0\le j\le J),\\
 &t\ge0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{L}
\]
It is a relaxation: an arbitrary interval is replaced by the shortest
action spanning exactly the grid targets it contains, or by the null
pattern if it contains none.  For the upper program set
\(f_{\lambda,\rho_j}(\star)=\tau_m(\lambda)\), fix
\(A_\star=\mathcal A\), and solve
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname*{minimize}_{t,(w_{x,A})}\quad &t,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&
 w_{x,A}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{A\in\mathsf A_h}w_{x,A}=1
 &&(x\in\mathsf X_m),\\
 &\tau_m(\lambda)+\sum_{x\in\mathsf X_m} f_{\lambda,\rho_j}(x)
   \sum_{\substack{A\in\mathsf A_h\\t_j\in A}}w_{x,A}
   \ge1-\alpha
 &&(0\le j\le J),\\
 &2br\tau_m(\lambda)+\sum_{x\in\mathsf X_m} f_{\lambda,\rho_j}(x)
   \sum_{A\in\mathsf A_h}\operatorname{len}(A)w_{x,A}
   \le t
 &&(0\le j\le J),\\
 &t\ge0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{U}
\]

The certification index \(s\) specifies an aggregate outward-rounding
budget, not merely a pointwise precision.  The procedure requests
successively finer rational oracle brackets
\(\lambda_s^-\le\lambda\le\lambda_s^+\), with
\(\lambda_s^+-\lambda_s^-\le2^{-s}\), and corresponding brackets for
every nonrational grid node and every other fixed real input, until
(A0) holds and directed interval evaluation returns rational bounds
\[
 f_{s,j,x}^-\le f_{\lambda,\rho_j}(x)\le f_{s,j,x}^+,
 \qquad
 \tau_{m,s}^-\le\tau_m(\lambda)\le\tau_{m,s}^+
 \le\overline\tau_m(\lambda_s^+),
\]
with
\[
 \max_j\left\{
 \sum_{x\in\mathsf X_m}(f_{s,j,x}^+-f_{s,j,x}^-)
 +(\tau_{m,s}^+-\tau_{m,s}^-)
 \right\}\le2^{-s}.
 \tag{A2}
\]
The same aggregate budget is imposed on directed enclosures of the
finitely many action lengths and correction terms.  If
\(m+1>\lambda\), termination of the admissibility check follows from
computability and convergence of the brackets; termination of all
remaining enclosures follows from computability and finiteness of the
LP.  All right sides involving \(\alpha\) are rounded outward: the
lower program uses the smaller right side and the upper program the
larger right side.

In the rational lower relaxation, replace each coverage coefficient by
\(f^+\), replace \(1-\alpha-\tau_m(\lambda)\) by a rational lower
enclosure, and replace each length coefficient by a rational lower
enclosure formed from \(f^-\) and a downward-rounded action length.
These directions enlarge the feasible set and decrease its recorded
objective.  Let \(D_{m,h,s}^-\) be its exact rational optimum, certified
by an exact rational dual optimum.  In the rational upper restriction,
replace retained coverage coefficients by \(f^-\), overflow coverage
by \(\tau^-\), and \(1-\alpha\) by a rational upper enclosure; replace
all retained and overflow length contributions by rational upper
enclosures formed from \(f^+\), \(\tau^+\), and upward-rounded
lengths.  For every fixed \(m\) with \(m+1>\lambda\), this restriction
is feasible for all sufficiently fine admissible \(s\); let
\(P_{m,h,s}^+\) be its exact rational optimum, certified by an exact
rational primal optimum.  Exact rational LP algorithms exist by finite
pivoting or finite basis enumeration, so floating-point output is used
only to propose a basis and never as a certificate.

Expand each retained upper action \(A=[L,U]\) to
\[
 E_h(A)=[\max\{a-br,L-bh/2\},\min\{a+br,U+bh/2\}].
\]
Put \(\omega_h=\min\{1,\lambda ch/4\}\) and
\(\gamma_h=\omega_h/(\alpha+\omega_h)\).  Nearest-grid coverage
implies coverage after expansion up to an off-grid deficit
\(\omega_h\); mixing with \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\(\gamma_h\) repairs this deficit.  With every term replaced by its
directed rational upper enclosure, report
\[
 U_{m,h,s}^+=P_{m,h,s}^++bh+2br\omega_h+2br\gamma_h,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{LP}_{m,h,s}^{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)
 =[D_{m,h,s}^-,U_{m,h,s}^+].
\]
For every fixed \(m\) with \(m+1>\lambda\), every \(h\), and all
sufficiently fine admissible \(s\), one has
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\le C_\alpha(\lambda)\le U_{m,h,s}^+.
\]
Along every refinement with \(m\to\infty\), \(h\downarrow0\),
\(s\to\infty\), and \(m+1>\lambda_s^+\), the tail, mesh,
coverage-repair, and aggregate enclosure errors vanish.

If \(\lambda_K\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)\), then
\(C_{K,\alpha}\to C_\alpha(\lambda)\).  For every computable
\(\lambda\), every grid mesh \(h\), every integer \(m\) satisfying
\(m+1>\lambda\), and all sufficiently large certification precisions
\(s\) for which the directed enclosure satisfies
\(m+1>\lambda_s^+\), the procedure
\(\operatorname{LP}_{m,h,s}^{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)\) returns exactly
verified rational values satisfying
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\le C_\alpha(\lambda)\le U_{m,h,s}^+.
\]
The upper rule uses the overflow action \(\mathcal A\), endpoint
expansion \(E_h\), and full-interval repair probability \(\gamma_h\),
and therefore has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) for every
\(\rho\in[-r,r]\), not only at grid points.  Along any refinement with
\(m\to\infty\), \(h\downarrow0\), \(s\to\infty\), and
\(m+1>\lambda_s^+\), one has
\(U_{m,h,s}^+-D_{m,h,s}^-\to0\), and both endpoints converge to
\(C_\alpha(\lambda)\).  Write
\(d_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\lambda_K^2/(2K)\}\).  Applying the
corrected upper rule through the deterministic collision statistic
loses at most \(d_K\) in coverage; mixing once more with
\(\mathcal A\) with probability \(d_K/(\alpha+d_K)\) restores coverage
at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal T_K(r)\) and adds at
most \(2brd_K/(\alpha+d_K)\) in expected length.  The matching lower
certificate transfers through the supported-mixture reverse kernel,
so the finite-array worst-case expected length converges to
\(C_\alpha(\lambda)\).
