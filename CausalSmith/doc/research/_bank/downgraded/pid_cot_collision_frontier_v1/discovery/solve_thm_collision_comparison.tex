%%% FIELD proof-collision-comparison
Put
\[
 I_{ik}=\mathbf 1\{Z_{0i}=Z_{1k}\},
 \qquad
 W=\sum_{i=1}^{n_0}\sum_{k=1}^{n_1}I_{ik}.
\]
By Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:uniform-cells}\) and
\(\texttt{ass:arm-independence}\),
\[
 \mathbb E I_{ik}=K^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Give \((i,k)\) the dependency neighborhood consisting of the pairs
\((i',k')\) for which \(i'=i\) or \(k'=k\).  Outside that neighborhood
the indicators are functions of disjoint independent labels.  For a
distinct neighbor, exactly three independent uniform labels must agree,
so
\[
 \mathbb E(I_{ik}I_{i'k'})=K^{-2}.
\]
Consequently the quantities in
Lemma \(\texttt{lem:agg-poisson-approximation}\) are
\[
 b_1=\frac{n_0n_1(n_0+n_1-1)}{K^2},
 \qquad
 b_2=\frac{n_0n_1(n_0+n_1-2)}{K^2}.
\]
Since \(\mathbb EW=n_0n_1/K=\lambda_K\), that lemma and the definition of
\(\varepsilon_K\) give
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\mathcal L(W),
       \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K)\right)
 \le 2(b_1+b_2)
 \le 4\varepsilon_K.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here and below total variation is the supremum over measurable events.

Let \(B\) be the label-measurable event that some cell hit by both arms
contains at least three observations in total.  If \(B\) occurs, either
two specified arm-zero observations and one specified arm-one
observation have a common label, or the analogous event with the arms
interchanged occurs.  Each specified triple agrees with probability
\(K^{-2}\).  Hence the union bound yields
\[
 \Pr(B)
 \le
 \frac{\binom{n_0}{2}n_1+n_0\binom{n_1}{2}}{K^2}
 =
 \frac{n_0n_1(n_0+n_1-2)}{2K^2}
 \le \frac{\varepsilon_K}{2}.
 \tag{3}
\]
On \(B^{\mathrm c}\), every cross-arm matching pair is the unique pair in
a cell containing exactly one observation from each arm, and conversely.
Thus \(M_K=W\) on \(B^{\mathrm c}\), and the coupling inequality together
with (2)--(3) gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\mathcal L(M_K),
       \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K)\right)
 \le 4\varepsilon_K+\Pr(B).
 \tag{4}
\]
The distribution of \((M_K,B)\) depends only on the known label design,
not on the signs or on \(q\).

We next identify the parameter-bearing part of the unconditioned product
mixture.  Let \(Q_{K,q}\) be the observation law obtained by mixing
\(\mathcal F_K\) with \(\Gamma_{K,q}\), before conditioning on
\(A_{K,q}\).  In a cell containing exactly one observation from each
arm, Assumption \(\texttt{ass:conditional-bernoulli}\) and the
construction in \(\texttt{def:supported-mixture-handle}\) give, for
\(x,y\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr_q(X_0=x,X_1=y)
 &=\frac14\,
   \mathbb E_{\Gamma_{K,q}}
   \left[(1+2ax\theta_0)(1+2by\theta_1)\right]\\
 &=\frac{1+4abqxy}{4}
 =\frac{1+cqxy}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the product mixing law has
\(\mathbb E\theta_0=\mathbb E\theta_1=0\) and
\(\mathbb E(\theta_0\theta_1)=q\), and \(c=4ab\).  Hence the collision
mark \(S=X_0X_1\) satisfies
\[
 \Pr_q(S=s)=\frac{1+cqs}{2},
 \qquad s\in\{-1,1\},
 \tag{6}
\]
while, conditional on \(S\), the orientation \(X_0\) is fair and
independent of \(q\).  Product mixing across cells makes the marks in
distinct one-one cells independent.  Conditional on the occupancy
pattern, every cell occupied by only one arm also has a \(q\)-free
outcome-block law: the relevant latent sign has the fair marginal law
under \(\Gamma_{K,q}\).  The occupancy pattern itself is parameter free.

Define \(\mathsf K_K^{\to}\) to retain from the full observation only the
numbers of positive and negative marks among the \(M_K\) one-one cells.
This deterministic kernel is independent of \(q\).  Equations (5)--(6)
show that, under \(Q_{K,q}\), conditional on the parameter-free count
\(M_K\), the retained marks are independent with positive-mark
probability \((1+cq)/2\).  Applying the same marking kernel to a
\(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K)\) count gives exactly
\(P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,q}\).  Total variation contracts under a
Markov kernel, because the preimage probability of every output event is
a measurable function taking values in \([0,1]\).  Therefore (4) gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
 Q_{K,q}\mathsf K_K^{\to},P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,q}\right)
 \le 4\varepsilon_K+\Pr(B).
 \tag{7}
\]
By Proposition \(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\),
\(\Gamma_{K,q}(A_{K,q}^{\mathrm c})\le\eta_K\).  Conditioning a
probability law on an event \(A\) changes it in total variation by
\(\Pr(A^{\mathrm c})\): this follows by integrating the density
\(\mathbf 1_A/\Pr(A)\) relative to the original law.  Data processing
and (3), uniformly over \(q\in\mathcal R_K^\circ(r)\), now yield
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
 P^{\mathrm{mix}}_{K,q}\mathsf K_K^{\to},
 P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,q}\right)
 \le 4\varepsilon_K+\Pr(B)+\eta_K
 \le 5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K.
 \tag{8}
\]

We construct the reverse kernel explicitly.  The law of \(M_K\) is
known and parameter free.  Couple an input
\(N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K)\) maximally to a variable having
the law of \(M_K\); the maximal coupling is fixed by the two known count
laws.  Conditional on the resulting \(M_K=m\), sample a label-occupancy
configuration from its known conditional law.  On
\(\{N=M_K\}\cap B^{\mathrm c}\), randomly order the input's \(N_+\)
positive and \(N_-\) negative marks among the one-one cells, draw their
fair orientations, and draw every one-arm outcome block from its
fair-sign mixture.  Equations (5)--(6) show that this is exactly the
conditional law under \(Q_{K,q}\), and every operation is independent
of \(q\).  On \(\{N\ne M_K\}\cup B\), use any fixed, parameter-free
completion.  This defines a Markov kernel
\(\mathsf K_K^{\leftarrow}\) depending only on
\(K,n_0,n_1,a,b,r\).  Under the displayed coupling its output can
disagree with a \(Q_{K,q}\)-observation only if the counts disagree or
\(B\) occurs.  Hence (3)--(4) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
 P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,q}\mathsf K_K^{\leftarrow},Q_{K,q}\right)
 &\le
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K),\mathcal L(M_K)\right)+\Pr(B)\\
 &\le4\varepsilon_K+2\Pr(B)
 \le5\varepsilon_K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Shell conditioning adds at most \(\eta_K\), uniformly in \(q\).  Since
total variation is at most one, (8)--(9) prove both comparison
inequalities with
\[
 e_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The index on both sides is the same lattice point \(q\), and the
decision target attached to that index is \(a-bq\).

It remains to establish the deterministic-array assertion.  Fix
\(\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)\) and put
\(t_j=\theta_{0j}\theta_{1j}\).  Enumerate the one-one cells using an
independent uniform ordering.  Permutation symmetry of the uniform
labels implies that, conditional on \(M_K=m\), their ordered labels form
a uniform ordered sample without replacement from
\(\{1,\ldots,K\}\).  Given label \(j\), conditional outcome independence
gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta(S\mid j)
 =
 \mathbb E_\theta(X_0\mid j)\mathbb E_\theta(X_1\mid j)
 =(2a\theta_{0j})(2b\theta_{1j})
 =ct_j.
 \tag{11}
\]
As \(S\in\{-1,1\}\), its conditional distribution is therefore
\(\Pr_\theta(S=s\mid j)=(1+ct_js)/2\).

Couple the ordered sample without replacement to \(m\) independent
uniform cell labels.  The samples agree whenever the independent labels
are distinct, because their conditional law on that event is the
uniform law without replacement.  A union bound gives mismatch
probability at most
\[
 \frac{m(m-1)}{2K}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Under independent uniform labels, (11) and
\(K^{-1}\sum_jt_j=\rho_K\), followed by independent outcome draws,
produce independent marks with mean \(c\rho_K\), which is exactly the
conditional mark law in \(\mathcal E_{\lambda_K,\rho_K}\).

Pointwise \(M_K\le W\).  Every two distinct matching indicators have
joint expectation \(K^{-2}\), whether or not they share one observation.
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{M_K(M_K-1)\}
 &\le\mathbb E\{W(W-1)\}\\
 &=\frac{n_0n_1(n_0n_1-1)}{K^2}
 \le\lambda_K^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Average (12) over \(M_K\), and compare its count law with
\(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_K)\) using (4).  Equations
(3)--(4) and (13) then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_\theta(M_K,S_1,\ldots,S_{M_K}),
 \mathcal E_{\lambda_K,\rho_K}\right)\\
 &\quad\le
 4\varepsilon_K+\Pr(B)
 +\frac{\mathbb E\{M_K(M_K-1)\}}{2K}\\
 &\quad\le
 5\varepsilon_K+\frac{\lambda_K^2}{2K}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
All bounds in (14) are independent of the deterministic signs.  Capping
at one proves the asserted uniform error
\(e_K^{\mathrm{det}}=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\lambda_K^2/(2K)\}\).

%%% FIELD proof-sparse-gaussian
First record the consequences of the sparse assumptions used in the
limits.  Since \(n_0,n_1\) are positive integers, for \(d=0,1\),
\[
 \varepsilon_K
 =\frac{n_0n_1(n_0+n_1)}{K^2}
 \ge \frac{n_d^2}{K^2}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:vanishing-triples}\) therefore implies
\(n_d/K\to0\).  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\lambda_K^2/K}{\varepsilon_K}
 =
 \frac{n_0n_1}{K(n_0+n_1)}
 \le\frac{\min(n_0,n_1)}K
 \longrightarrow0,
 \tag{16}
\]
so \(\lambda_K^2/K\to0\).  Hence the deterministic comparison error in
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\),
\[
 d_K=\min\left\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\frac{\lambda_K^2}{2K}\right\},
 \tag{17}
\]
tends to zero.

Consider first \(\mathcal E_{\lambda_K,\rho}\).  Conditional on \(N=m\),
the marks are independent with
\[
 \mu_\rho=\mathbb E_\rho S_\ell=c\rho,
 \qquad
 \sigma_\rho^2=1-c^2\rho^2
 \ge1-c^2r^2>0.
 \tag{18}
\]
The final strict inequality follows from \(a,b<1/2\), whence
\(0<c=4ab<1\), and \(0<r<1\).  In particular all divisions by
\(\sigma_\rho\) below are uniform over \([-r,r]\).  Since centered marks
are uniformly bounded, Taylor's formula for their characteristic
functions gives, for fixed \(t\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\rho\exp\left\{
 it\frac{S-\mu_\rho}{\sigma_\rho\sqrt m}
 \right\}
 =1-\frac{t^2}{2m}+O(m^{-3/2}),
 \tag{19}
\]
where the remainder is uniform in \(\rho\).  Raising (19) to the
\(m\)-th power proves the conditional central limit theorem uniformly
over \(\rho\).  Also,
\[
 \Pr\{|N/\lambda_K-1|>\epsilon\}
 \le\frac{1}{\epsilon^2\lambda_K},
 \qquad
 \Pr(N=0)=e^{-\lambda_K},
 \tag{20}
\]
so \(N/\lambda_K\to1\) uniformly in probability and the zero-count
event vanishes.

On \(N>0\), factor the marked-experiment studentized statistic as
\[
 \frac{\sqrt N(\overline S-c\rho)}{\sigma_\rho}
 \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_K}{N}}\,
 \frac{\sigma_\rho}
 {\sqrt{1-\overline S^2+N^{-1}}}.
 \tag{21}
\]
The first factor has the uniform conditional Gaussian limit by (19);
the second converges uniformly in probability to one by (20); and the
third does so because the conditional variance bound and Chebyshev's
inequality give
\(\overline S-c\rho\to0\) uniformly in probability, while (18) is
bounded away from zero.  Thus
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\lambda_K}(\overline S-c\rho)}
 {\sqrt{1-\overline S^2+N^{-1}}}
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
 \tag{22}
\]
uniformly for \(\rho\in[-r,r]\).  One precise meaning of uniformity in
(22) is obtained by contradiction: along every sequence
\(\rho_K\in[-r,r]\), equations (18)--(21) give the same standard-normal
limit; compactness of \([-r,r]\) then rules out a sequence on which the
distributional error stays bounded away from zero.  The declared
defaults on \(N=0\) are harmless by (20).

Because \(c=4ab\), the estimator and variance identities in the marked
experiment are
\[
 \widehat L_K-(a-b\rho)
 =-\frac{\overline S-c\rho}{4a},
 \qquad
 \widehat V_K-V(\rho)
 =\frac{-\overline S^2+c^2\rho^2+N^{-1}}{16a^2}.
 \tag{23}
\]
The positivity \(a>0\) validates the divisions.  Equations (20)--(23)
prove both the asserted studentized limit and uniform variance
consistency in the marked experiment.

For each deterministic array, Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\) bounds the total-variation
distance between its collision statistic and the marked experiment at
\(\rho=\rho_K\) by \(d_K\to0\), uniformly over
\(\mathcal T_K(r)\).  Total variation controls every event and contracts
under the statistic mapping, so (22)--(23) transfer uniformly to the
finite-array collision estimator.  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) supplies the distinct causal-target
identity \(L_K=a-b\rho_K\).  Since the standard normal distribution has
no atom at \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\), the transferred convergence gives
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \Pr_\theta\!\left\{
 L_K\in
 \left[\widehat L_K\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \sqrt{\widehat V_K/\lambda_K}\right]\right\}
 =1-\alpha+o(1).
 \tag{24}
\]
No projection is used, so boundary clipping creates no atom.

%%% FIELD proof-interpolation-transfer
Let
\[
 g^{\mathrm{step}}_{d,K,\theta}(u)
 =p_{d,\kappa_K(u)}.
\]
The interpolation strips in
\(\texttt{def:interpolation-image}\) have half-width \(K^{-3}\).
On the strip about \(j/K\), the integral of the absolute difference
between the affine connector and the step function is
\[
 \frac12K^{-3}|p_{d,j+1}-p_{dj}|.
\]
Writing \(a_0=a\) and \(a_1=b\), the cell probabilities satisfy
\(|p_{d,j+1}-p_{dj}|\le2a_d\).  Summing over the \(K-1\) boundaries
therefore yields
\[
 \int_0^1
 |g_{d,K,\theta}(u)-g^{\mathrm{step}}_{d,K,\theta}(u)|\,du
 \le\frac{a_d}{K^2}.
 \tag{25}
\]

Couple a continuous observation and its step version using the same
uniform \(U_{di}\), the same label \(\kappa_K(U_{di})\), and the same
independent uniform random variable to generate the Bernoulli outcome.
The mismatch probability for one observation equals the integral in
(25).  A union bound over all observations, followed by data processing
through the measurable pushforward \(\mathsf Q_K\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \left\|P^{\mathrm c}_{K,\theta}\mathsf Q_K
       -P^{\mathcal F}_{K,\theta}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\le\frac{n_0a+n_1b}{K^2}\\
 &\le\frac{2(n_0+n_1)}{K^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Conversely, a parameter-free kernel that adds an independent uniform
position within every observed cell turns a discrete observation into
the step experiment.  The same coupling proves the reverse deficiency
bound with the right side of (26).  This is a comparison through
explicit kernels; it is not a total-variation comparison between raw
observations on different sample spaces.

For Bernoulli laws with success probabilities \(p_0,p_1\), the sharp
absolute-cost coupling value is \(|p_1-p_0|\), by Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\)'s pointwise coupling argument.
Consequently
\[
 L_K^{\mathrm c}
 =\int_0^1|g_{1,K,\theta}(u)-g_{0,K,\theta}(u)|\,du.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality and (25) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |L_K^{\mathrm c}-L_K|
 &\le
 \sum_{d=0}^1\int_0^1
 |g_{d,K,\theta}-g^{\mathrm{step}}_{d,K,\theta}|\,du\\
 &\le\frac{a+b}{K^2}
 \le\frac{2(a+b)}{K^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Both (26) and (27) are uniform over the deterministic signs.

Composing a discrete rule with \(\mathsf Q_K\), or a continuous rule
with the uniform-within-cell reverse kernel, changes any bounded risk or
coverage probability by at most the error in (26).  Expanding a
reported interval by the target discrepancy in (27), followed when
needed by the full-interval coverage repair used in Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:honest-interval-transfer}\), preserves honesty.  The
comparison error vanishes because
\[
 \frac{n_0+n_1}{K^2}
 \le\varepsilon_K
 \longrightarrow0,
 \tag{28}
\]
where \(n_0n_1\ge1\).  At finite collision intensity the target error in
(27) also vanishes.  In the sparse Gaussian regime, (15) implies
\(\lambda_K/K\to0\), and hence
\[
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}|L_K^{\mathrm c}-L_K|
 \le\frac{2(a+b)\sqrt{\lambda_K}}{K^2}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{29}
\]
Together with the variance lower bound in (18), (26)--(29) transfer the
finite-collision decision conclusions and the sparse Gaussian
conclusion at the actual endpoint \(L_K^{\mathrm c}\) whenever the
corresponding displayed error is negligible.  The construction and both
kernels are defined only on \(\mathcal I_K(r)\), so no conclusion
extends beyond that interpolation image.

%%% FIELD proof-finite-information-transfer
For a legal deterministic array \(\theta\), write
\[
 t_K(\theta)=L_K(\theta)=a-b\rho_K(\theta),
 \qquad
 t(\rho)=a-b\rho.
 \tag{30}
\]
The first equality is the causal endpoint identity in Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\); the second object is the target in
the marked decision experiment.  We relate them rather than identify
them by definition.

We use the following elementary bounded-loss consequence of total
variation.  If \(0\le g\le B\), the layer-cake formula gives
\[
 \left|\int g\,dP-\int g\,dQ\right|
 \le\int_0^B|P\{g>u\}-Q\{g>u\}|\,du
 \le B\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \tag{31}
\]
The same argument applied to transition probabilities proves contraction
under Markov kernels.  Since every target lies in
\(\mathcal A=[a-br,a+br]\), projection of an endpoint action onto
\(\mathcal A\) cannot increase absolute loss.  We may thus use actions
in \(\mathcal A\), for which the loss is bounded by \(2br\).

Define
\[
 d_K=\min\left\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\frac{\lambda_K^2}{2K}\right\},
 \qquad
 e_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\},
 \tag{32}
\]
and
\[
 \delta_K=2s_K+\frac2K,
 \qquad
 \omega_K=\min\left\{1,\frac{\lambda_Kc\delta_K}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{33}
\]
For the upper comparison, let
\(D_\lambda\in\mathscr D_{\lambda_K}\) be any marked-Poisson rule,
projected onto \(\mathcal A\), and apply it to the positive- and
negative-mark counts extracted from the deterministic collision
statistic.  This is one measurable finite-array rule, independent of
\(\theta\).  The deterministic part of Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\), (30)--(32), and (31) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta|D_\lambda-L_K(\theta)|
 \le
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_K,\rho_K(\theta)}
 |D_\lambda-t(\rho_K(\theta))|+2brd_K.
 \tag{34}
\]
Taking the supremum over arrays and the infimum over marked rules gives
\[
 R_K\le R(\lambda_K)+2brd_K.
 \tag{35}
\]

For the reverse comparison, fix a finite-array rule
\(D_K\in\mathscr D_K\), projected onto \(\mathcal A\), and set
\[
 \mathcal R_K(D_K)=
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \mathbb E_\theta|D_K-L_K(\theta)|.
\]
For sufficiently large \(K\), Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) makes
\(\mathcal R_K^\circ(r)\) nonempty.  For
\(q\in\mathcal R_K^\circ(r)\), its mixture is supported on legal arrays
and satisfies \(|L_K(\theta)-t(q)|\le bs_K\).  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm{mix}}_{K,q}}|D_K-t(q)|
 &=\int\mathbb E_\theta|D_K-t(q)|
      \,d\Pi_{K,q}^{\mathrm{shell}}(\theta)\\
 &\le\mathcal R_K(D_K)+bs_K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
Compose \(D_K\) with the parameter-free reverse kernel from Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\), and call the resulting single
marked rule \(\widetilde D_K\).  Equations (31)--(32) and (36) give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_K,q}|\widetilde D_K-t(q)|
 \le\mathcal R_K(D_K)+bs_K+2bre_K.
 \tag{37}
\]

For an arbitrary \(\rho\in[-r,r]\), set \(q=q_K(\rho)\).
Proposition \(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) gives
\(|q-\rho|\le\delta_K\).  Couple the two marked experiments using the
same Poisson count and common uniforms for the marks.  A single mark
differs with probability \(c|\rho-q|/2\), so a conditional union bound
and expectation over the count show
\[
 \|P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho}
   -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,q}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\omega_K.
 \tag{38}
\]
Since \(|t(\rho)-t(q)|\le b\delta_K\), equations (31), (37), and (38)
imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\lambda_K,\rho}|\widetilde D_K-t(\rho)|
 \le
 \mathcal R_K(D_K)+b(s_K+\delta_K)
 +2br(e_K+\omega_K).
 \tag{39}
\]
Taking the parameter supremum and then the infimum over \(D_K\) proves
\[
 R(\lambda_K)
 \le R_K+b(s_K+\delta_K)+2br(e_K+\omega_K).
 \tag{40}
\]
Thus the mixture target discrepancy is explicitly paid; no random
mixture endpoint is substituted for the realized \(L_K\).

For continuity in intensity, couple marked Poisson processes at
\(\lambda,\lambda'\ge0\) by retaining a common process of intensity
\(\min\{\lambda,\lambda'\}\) and adding an independent process of
intensity \(|\lambda-\lambda'|\) to the larger experiment.  Uniformly in
\(\rho\), the observations agree unless the added process is nonempty.
Therefore
\[
 \|P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda,\rho}
   -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda',\rho}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}.
\]
The two experiments have the same target \(t(\rho)\).  Applying (31) in
both directions and then taking minimax infima gives
\[
 |R(\lambda)-R(\lambda')|
 \le2br\{1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}\}.
 \tag{41}
\]

Suppose now that \(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\).
Lemma \(\texttt{lem:vanishing-triples-implies-growing-cells}\) gives
\(K\to\infty\).  Boundedness of \(\lambda_K\) and the definition of
\(s_K\) imply, eventually,
\[
 s_K=\{K(1\vee\lambda_K)\}^{-1/4}\to0,
 \qquad
 Ks_K^2=\sqrt{\frac{K}{1\vee\lambda_K}}\to\infty.
 \tag{42}
\]
Hence \(\eta_K=2e^{-Ks_K^2/2}\to0\).  Assumption
\(\texttt{ass:vanishing-triples}\), boundedness of \(\lambda_K\), and
(32)--(33) yield
\[
 e_K,d_K,\delta_K,\omega_K\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{43}
\]
Equation (41) gives \(R(\lambda_K)\to R(\lambda)\), and (35), (40), and
(43) squeeze \(R_K\) to \(R(\lambda)\).

%%% FIELD proof-honest-interval-transfer
We retain the convention that total variation is the supremum over
events.  The layer-cake and data-processing inequalities proved in
(31) imply, for \(0\le g\le B\),
\[
 |\mathbb E_Pg-\mathbb E_Qg|
 \le B\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
 \qquad
 \|P\mathsf K-Q\mathsf K\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \tag{44}
\]
Define
\[
 e_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\},\quad
 \delta_K=2s_K+2/K,
\]
\[
 u_K=e_K+\min\{1,\lambda_Kc\delta_K/2\},
 \qquad
 d_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\lambda_K^2/(2K)\}.
 \tag{45}
\]

Fix \(\zeta>0\), and choose
\(\mathcal C_K\in\mathscr C_K\) that is honest over
\(\mathcal T_K(r)\) and has worst expected length at most
\(C_{K,\alpha}+\zeta\).  For \(\rho\in[-r,r]\), put
\(q=q_K(\rho)\).  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) and Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) give
\[
 |q-\rho|\le\delta_K,
 \qquad
 |L_K-(a-bq)|\le bs_K
 \tag{46}
\]
on the supported mixture.  Integrating finite-array coverage over that
mixture and expanding both endpoints of the reported interval by
\(bs_K\), clipped to \(\mathcal A\), gives coverage of \(a-bq\) at
least \(1-\alpha\).

The common-count, common-uniform mark coupling used in (38) gives
\[
 \|P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho}
 -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\min\{1,\lambda_Kc|\rho-\rho'|/2\}.
 \tag{47}
\]
Apply \(\mathcal C_K\) after the reverse kernel of Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\), then expand once more by
\(b\delta_K\).  Equations (44), (46), and (47) show that under
\(P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho}\) the resulting interval covers
\(a-b\rho\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha-u_K\).  Because every
interval is contained in \(\mathcal A\), its length is at most \(2br\);
its worst expected length is at most
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}+\zeta+2b(s_K+\delta_K)+2bru_K.
 \tag{48}
\]

We use an explicit coverage repair.  If a rule has coverage at least
\(1-\alpha-u\), independently replace its output by \(\mathcal A\)
with probability
\[
 \gamma(u)=\frac{u}{\alpha+u}.
 \tag{49}
\]
Here \(\alpha>0\) and \(u\ge0\), so the division is valid and
\(0\le\gamma(u)\le1\).  The repaired coverage is at least
\[
 \gamma(u)+(1-\gamma(u))(1-\alpha-u)=1-\alpha,
 \tag{50}
\]
and expected length rises by at most \(2br\gamma(u)\).  Apply
(49)--(50) with \(u=u_K\) to (48), and let \(\zeta\downarrow0\), to get
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda_K)
 \le C_{K,\alpha}+2b(s_K+\delta_K)+2bru_K
 +\frac{2bru_K}{\alpha+u_K}.
 \tag{51}
\]

Conversely, take an honest marked-Poisson rule at intensity
\(\lambda_K\) with worst expected length at most
\(C_\alpha(\lambda_K)+\zeta\), and apply it to the deterministic
collision statistic.  The deterministic part of Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\), Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\), and (44)--(45) show that coverage
falls by at most \(d_K\) and expected length rises by at most
\(2brd_K\).  Repairing with (49) at \(u=d_K\) and letting
\(\zeta\downarrow0\) gives
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}
 \le C_\alpha(\lambda_K)+2brd_K
 +\frac{2brd_K}{\alpha+d_K}.
 \tag{52}
\]

The frontier is continuous in intensity.  The common-process coupling
used for (41) gives, uniformly in \(\rho\),
\[
 v(\lambda,\lambda')
 :=\|P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda,\rho}
 -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda',\rho}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}.
\]
Transporting an honest rule in either direction loses at most \(v\) in
coverage and changes expected length by at most \(2brv\).  The repair
(49) in both directions proves
\[
 |C_\alpha(\lambda)-C_\alpha(\lambda')|
 \le2brv+\frac{2brv}{\alpha+v}.
 \tag{53}
\]
When \(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\), equations (42)--(43) also imply
\(u_K,d_K\to0\).  Combining (51)--(53) yields
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}\longrightarrow C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{54}
\]

We next verify the programs in
\(\texttt{def:certified-interval-lp}\).  Their input domain is the
declared computable-real domain.  The coupling in (47), now at fixed
\(\lambda\), proves
\[
 \|f_{\lambda,\rho}-f_{\lambda,\rho'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le
 \omega_\lambda(|\rho-\rho'|)
 :=\min\{1,\lambda c|\rho-\rho'|/2\}.
 \tag{55}
\]
By (44), an event has this probability modulus and a
\([0,2br]\)-valued length has expectation modulus
\(2br\omega_\lambda\).

For the tail certificate, \(\tau_m(0)=0\).  If
\(0<\lambda<m+1\), exponential Markov inequality gives, for \(t>0\),
\[
 \Pr_\lambda(N\ge m+1)
 \le\exp\{\lambda(e^t-1)-t(m+1)\}.
 \tag{56}
\]
The exponent is minimized at
\(t=\log((m+1)/\lambda)>0\), and substitution yields
\[
 \tau_m(\lambda)
 \le e^{-\lambda}
 \left(\frac{e\lambda}{m+1}\right)^{m+1}.
 \tag{57}
\]

For the lower certificate, begin with an arbitrary continuum-honest
randomized interval rule.  On each retained state, replace every
realized interval by the shortest grid action spanning exactly the grid
targets that it contains, or by \(\varnothing_G\) if it contains none.
This preserves grid-target membership and cannot increase length.
Deleting overflow loses at most \(\tau_m(\lambda)\) in coverage.
Therefore the induced weights are feasible for the exact lower program
and have objective no larger than the original worst expected length.
Replacing coverage coefficients upward and their right sides downward
enlarges the feasible set; replacing length contributions downward
cannot increase the recorded objective.  Hence
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\le C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{58}
\]

For the upper certificate, take an exactly feasible rational solution
of the directed upper program.  Its rounding directions imply actual
coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) and actual expected length at most
\(P_{m,h,s}^+\) at each grid node.  Use \(\mathcal A\) at overflow.
For arbitrary \(\rho\), select a nearest grid point \(\rho_j\), so
\(|\rho-\rho_j|\le h/2\).  If an action covers \(a-b\rho_j\), its
expansion \(E_h\) covers \(a-b\rho\).  Equation (55) shows that the
expanded rule has coverage at least \(1-\alpha-\omega_h\), where
\[
 \omega_h=\min\{1,\lambda ch/4\}.
 \tag{59}
\]
Repair with
\(\gamma_h=\omega_h/(\alpha+\omega_h)\).  Expansion adds at most \(bh\),
parameter transfer adds at most \(2br\omega_h\) in expected length, and
repair adds at most \(2br\gamma_h\).  Directed upper enclosure of every
term proves
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \le P_{m,h,s}^++bh+2br\omega_h+2br\gamma_h
 =U_{m,h,s}^+.
 \tag{60}
\]
Equations (58)--(60) certify continuum, rather than merely gridwise,
coverage.

After directed enclosure, both programs have finitely many variables
and rational coefficients.  Row-sum and nonnegativity constraints put
the weights in a finite product of simplices, and the always-full
interval gives finite upper feasibility for sufficiently fine
rounding.  A linear objective attains its minimum at a face containing
a vertex.  Each vertex is obtained by solving one of finitely many
square rational systems selected from active constraints; hence it is
rational, and finite basis enumeration finds the optimum exactly.
Applying the same enumeration to the algebraic dual yields rational
multipliers.  Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and equality of
their rational objectives certify optimality through the identity
\[
 c^{\mathsf T}x-y^{\mathsf T}b
 =
 \text{a sum of products of nonnegative primal slacks and
 nonnegative dual multipliers}.
 \tag{61}
\]
Thus floating-point calculations can propose a basis but do not enter a
certificate.

It remains to prove contraction.  Let \(\xi_s\) be the largest, over the
finitely many constraint rows, of the aggregate widths of all directed
likelihood, tail, right-side, action-length, and correction-term
enclosures entering that row.  The aggregate budget in the definition
gives a constant \(C_0<\infty\), depending only on the bounded fixed
inputs, such that
\[
 0\le\xi_s\le C_0 2^{-s}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{62}
\]

Fix \(\zeta>0\) and take a continuum-honest rule with worst expected
length at most \(C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta\).  Replace its overflow action
by \(\mathcal A\), at a cost of at most \(2br\tau_m\).  On retained
states, round each endpoint outward to adjacent target-grid endpoints;
coverage is preserved, the resulting action belongs to
\(\mathsf A_h\), and length rises by at most \(2bh\).  For large \(s\),
mix with \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\[
 \beta_s=\frac{2\xi_s}{\alpha}\le1.
 \tag{63}
\]
Before directed rounding this creates coverage slack
\(\alpha\beta_s=2\xi_s\), enough to absorb all aggregate coefficient
and right-side errors.  The weights are feasible for the rational upper
program and have directed objective at most
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta+2br\tau_m+2bh
 +2br\beta_s+\xi_s.
 \tag{64}
\]
After adding the expansion, modulus, repair, and enclosure corrections
in (60), and then taking \(m\to\infty\), \(h\downarrow0\), and
\(s\to\infty\) along a declared refinement, (57), (59), and
(62)--(64) give
\[
 \limsup U_{m,h,s}^+\le C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \tag{65}
\]
after \(\zeta\downarrow0\).

For the reverse contraction, take an optimal rational lower-program
rule.  Interpret \(\varnothing_G\) as a degenerate interval at a point
of \(\mathcal A\) that is not one of the finitely many grid targets.
This has zero length and preserves every grid-coverage pattern.
Directed rounding implies actual retained gridwise coverage at least
\(1-\alpha-\tau_m-\xi_s\) and actual retained expected length at most
\(D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s\).  Report \(\mathcal A\) on overflow, expand by
\(bh/2\) at each retained endpoint, and use (55) from a nearest grid
point.  The resulting rule has continuum coverage at least
\[
 1-\alpha-(\xi_s+\omega_h)
 \tag{66}
\]
and worst expected length at most
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s+2br\tau_m+bh+2br\omega_h.
 \tag{67}
\]
One repair with \(u=\xi_s+\omega_h\) makes (66) honest.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \le{}&D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s+2br\tau_m+bh+2br\omega_h\\
 &+\frac{2br(\xi_s+\omega_h)}
 {\alpha+\xi_s+\omega_h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{68}
\]
Every term after \(D_{m,h,s}^-\) vanishes along the stated refinement,
by (57), (59), and (62).  Combining (58), (65), and (68) proves
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\to C_\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
 U_{m,h,s}^+\to C_\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
 U_{m,h,s}^+-D_{m,h,s}^-\to0.
 \tag{69}
\]
In particular, at \(\lambda=1\) and \(\alpha=0.05\), every sufficiently
fine certified upper rule covers on every parameter segment with
probability at least \(0.95\); successive valid bracket intersections
have widths tending to zero.

Finally, the deterministic implementation is exactly the construction
behind (52): transport a marked upper rule through the deterministic
collision statistic, pay \(d_K\) in coverage and \(2brd_K\) in expected
length, and mix once with \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\(d_K/(\alpha+d_K)\).  The lower comparison is (51), using the
parameter-free supported-mixture reverse kernel.  Hence both the exact
interval-action certification and the two-sided honest-minimax transfer
concern the realized sharp endpoint \(L_K\), uniformly over all legal
deterministic arrays.

%%% FIELD proof-deterministic-shell-local-minimax
Fix \(\rho_0\in(-r,r)\), and put
\[
 u_+=1+c\rho_0,\qquad
 u_-=1-c\rho_0,\qquad
 I_0=\frac{c^2}{1-c^2\rho_0^2}.
 \tag{70}
\]
The amplitude restrictions give \(0<c<1\), and
\(|\rho_0|<r<1\), so \(u_+\), \(u_-\), and \(I_0\) are strictly
positive.  Under \(\mathcal E_{\lambda_K,\rho_0}\), \(N_+\) and \(N_-\)
are independent Poisson variables with means
\(\lambda_Ku_+/2\) and \(\lambda_Ku_-/2\).  For
\(\rho=\rho_0+h/\sqrt{\lambda_K}\), Taylor expansion of the exact mass
\(f_{\lambda_K,\rho}\), uniformly for \(h\) in a fixed compact set,
gives
\[
 \log\frac{f_{\lambda_K,\rho}}
 {f_{\lambda_K,\rho_0}}
 =
 h\Delta_K-\frac12h^2I_0+o_{P_{\rho_0}}(1),
 \qquad
 \Delta_K=
 \frac c{\sqrt{\lambda_K}}
 \left(\frac{N_+}{u_+}-\frac{N_-}{u_-}\right).
 \tag{71}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\rho_0}\Delta_K=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{\rho_0}(\Delta_K)
 =
 \frac{c^2}{2}\left(\frac1{u_+}+\frac1{u_-}\right)
 =I_0.
 \tag{72}
\]
The centered-Poisson characteristic functions yield
\(\Delta_K\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,I_0)\), and the third-order
remainder in (71) is \(O_{P_{\rho_0}}(\lambda_K^{-1/2})\) on compact
\(h\)-sets because \(u_+\wedge u_->0\).  If \(\Lambda_{K,h}\) is the
likelihood ratio in (71), the Poisson exponential-moment formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\rho_0}\Lambda_{K,h}^2
 =
 \exp\left\{
 \frac{c^2h^2}{2}
 \left(\frac1{u_+}+\frac1{u_-}\right)\right\}
 =e^{h^2I_0}.
 \tag{73}
\]
Thus likelihood ratios are uniformly integrable on every compact
\(h\)-set.

For each finite collection of local parameters, (71)--(73) imply
convergence of every bounded finite-prior Bayes risk to that in the
Gaussian location experiment
\[
 X=-bh+\sqrt{V(\rho_0)}Z,
 \qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
 \tag{74}
\]
whose target is \(-bh\).  To verify this implication directly, dominate
the finite-prior experiment by the central law.  Its Bayes risk is the
expectation of the pointwise infimum, over the action, of a finite
weighted sum of bounded losses times likelihood ratios.  This infimum
is Lipschitz in the likelihood-ratio vector with constant at most \(T\).
Joint convergence from (71), together with uniform integrability from
(73), therefore gives convergence of its expectation.  The variance in
(74) is
\[
 \frac{b^2}{I_0}
 =
 \frac{b^2(1-c^2\rho_0^2)}{c^2}
 =
 \frac{1-c^2\rho_0^2}{16a^2}
 =V(\rho_0),
 \tag{75}
\]
where the middle equality uses \(c=4ab\).  Also, because \(\rho_0\) is
interior and \(\mathcal A\subset\mathcal D\), the scaled finite-sample
action image
\(\sqrt{\lambda_K}\{\mathcal D-(a-b\rho_0)\}\) exhausts
\(\mathbb R\).  Restricting actions to that image can only increase a
lower Bayes bound.

We calculate the Gaussian minimax value under \(\ell_T\).  Let
\(Z_V\sim\mathcal N(0,V(\rho_0))\), with density \(\varphi_V\), and set
\[
 m_T=\mathbb E\min\{Z_V^2,T\}.
\]
The translation-equivariant rule \(X\) has constant risk \(m_T\).
Conversely, average the risk of any rule against the uniform prior on
the target interval \([-A,A]\).  For \(0<B<A\), restrict the
observation integral to \([-A+B,A-B]\) and change variables.  The Bayes
risk is bounded below by
\[
 \left(1-\frac BA\right)
 \inf_{v\in\mathbb R}
 \int_{-B}^{B}\ell_T(v+z)\varphi_V(z)\,dz.
 \tag{76}
\]
The layer-cake identity
\[
 \ell_T(x)=\int_0^T\mathbf 1\{|x|>\sqrt t\}\,dt
\]
shows that the integral in (76) is minimized at \(v=0\): for every
\(t\), a centered interval of prescribed length has largest mass under
the symmetric unimodal density
\(\varphi_V(z)\mathbf 1\{|z|\le B\}\).  Letting first \(A\to\infty\)
and then \(B\to\infty\), monotone convergence makes (76) tend to
\(m_T\).  Thus the Gaussian minimax value is \(m_T\).  Gaussian shifts
are continuous in total variation and \(\ell_T\) is bounded and
continuous.  Hence compact uniform priors can be approximated by finite
grids: for each \(\delta>0\), some finite prior supported on target
values in \([-bG,bG]\) has Gaussian Bayes risk at least
\(m_T-\delta\), equivalently its \(h\)-support lies in \([-G,G]\).

We transfer this finite prior to supported mixtures of legal arrays.
Equation (15) gives \(n_d/K\to0\), and therefore
\[
 \frac{\lambda_K}{K}
 =\frac{n_0}{K}\frac{n_1}{K}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(\lambda_K\to\infty\), the definition of \(s_K\) implies,
eventually,
\[
 s_K=(K\lambda_K)^{-1/4},\qquad
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}s_K
 =\left(\frac{\lambda_K}{K}\right)^{1/4}\to0,
 \qquad
 Ks_K^2=\sqrt{\frac K{\lambda_K}}\to\infty.
 \tag{77}
\]
Thus \(\eta_K=2e^{-Ks_K^2/2}\to0\), and the supported-mixture
comparison error
\(e_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\}\) tends to zero.  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) also gives, uniformly for
\(|h|\le G\),
\[
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\left|
 q_K\left(\rho_0+\frac h{\sqrt{\lambda_K}}\right)
 -\rho_0-\frac h{\sqrt{\lambda_K}}
 \right|
 \le2\sqrt{\lambda_K}s_K+\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_K}}K
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{78}
\]
The arguments of \(q_K\) lie in \([-r,r]\) eventually because
\(\rho_0\) is interior and \(G\) is fixed.

For each support point \(h\), use
\[
 \Pi_{K,q_K(\rho_0+h/\sqrt{\lambda_K})}^{\mathrm{shell}}.
\]
By its shell support and (77)--(78), for every fixed \(H>G+1\) these
mixtures are supported on
\(\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)\) for all sufficiently large \(K\).
Propositions \(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) and
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) give the realized-target bound
\[
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\left|
 L_K-
 \left\{a-bq_K\left(\rho_0+
 \frac h{\sqrt{\lambda_K}}\right)\right\}\right|
 \le b\sqrt{\lambda_K}s_K\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{79}
\]
The function \(\ell_T\) is \(2\sqrt T\)-Lipschitz, so the replacement
in (79) changes risk by \(o(1)\), uniformly over the finite prior.

For arbitrary \(D_K\in\mathscr D_K\), compose it with the parameter-free
reverse kernel in Theorem \(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\).  The
marked rule's finite-prior Bayes risk is no greater than the full-data
mixture Bayes risk plus \(Te_K+o(1)\).  The latter is at most the
supremum of deterministic-array risks over
\(\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)\), because all mixtures are supported there.
Equations (71), (78), and (79), finite-prior Bayes-risk convergence, and
the prior choice imply
\[
 \liminf_{K\to\infty}
 \inf_{D_K\in\mathscr D_K}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}(D_K-L_K)\right)
 \ge m_T-\delta
 \tag{80}
\]
for every fixed \(T\) and every sufficiently large fixed \(H>G+1\).
Letting \(H\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves the ordered
lower bound \(m_T\).

For the upper bound, Theorem \(\texttt{thm:sparse-gaussian}\) and
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)}
 |\rho_K-\rho_0|\le H/\sqrt{\lambda_K}
\]
give, for fixed \(H,T\),
\[
 \limsup_{K\to\infty}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}(\widehat L_K-L_K)\right)
 \le
 \mathbb E\ell_T(\sqrt{V(\rho_0)}Z)
 =m_T.
 \tag{81}
\]
Indeed, studentization and uniform consistency of \(\widehat V_K\)
give uniform weak convergence of the unstudentized statistic to
\(\mathcal N(0,V(\rho_0))\); bounded continuity of \(\ell_T\) gives
(81).  The distance from \(a-b\rho_0\) to the boundary of
\(\mathcal D=[a-b,a+b]\) is positive.  On each fixed shell, (81)
implies uniform \(\lambda_K^{-1/2}\)-consistency, so clipping is active
with probability tending uniformly to zero.  Since the loss is bounded
by \(T\), (81) also holds for
\(\operatorname{clip}_{\mathcal D}(\widehat L_K)\).

Equations (80)--(81) give the inner ordered value \(m_T\).  Only after
the \(K\)-limit and then the \(H\)-limit do we use monotone convergence:
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}m_T
 =\mathbb EZ_V^2
 =V(\rho_0).
 \tag{82}
\]
This proves the displayed ordered identity and both estimator
assertions.  At no point is an unbounded scaled loss transported through
total variation.

%%% FIELD construction-anchor-containing-class
An anchor observable law \(P\) is generated by independent and
identically distributed units carrying a covariate \(Z\in[0,1]\) and
potential outcomes \((Y(0),Y(1))\in[0,1]^2\).  A Bernoulli treatment
indicator, with success probability strictly between zero and one, is
independent of \((Z,Y(0),Y(1))\), and the observed outcome obeys
consistency, \(Y=Y(d)\) in arm \(d\).  Conditional on the realized arm
sizes \(n_0,n_1\), its observation \(\mathcal O_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\) is
the corresponding pair of independent within-arm samples.  Write
\(\mu_{d,z}^P\) for the conditional observed-outcome law in arm \(d\)
at covariate value \(z\), and define the observed-law
conditional-transport functional
\[
 L(P)=\int_{[0,1]}
 \inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{0,z}^P,\mu_{1,z}^P)}
 \int_{[0,1]^2}|y_1-y_0|\,d\gamma(y_0,y_1)\,dP_Z(z).
 \tag{41}
\]
The covariate domain \([0,1]\) is compact, the ambient outcome domain
\([0,1]\) is compact and convex, and the cost
\(h(y_0,y_1)=|y_1-y_0|\) is continuous.

For \(\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)\), let
\(P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}}\) be the law obtained from
\(\mathcal F_K\) by the injective relabeling \(j\mapsto z_j=j/K\),
regarding \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) as taking values in \([0,1]\).  An
anchor-containing class \(\mathfrak P_K\) is any class of anchor
observable laws containing \(P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}}\) for every
\(\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)\).  Let
\(\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\) be all randomized measurable kernels
from \(\mathcal O_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\) to \([0,1]\), and let
\(\mathscr C_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\) be all randomized measurable kernels
from \(\mathcal O_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\) to the closed subintervals of
\([0,1]\).  Define
\[
 R_K^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 =
 \inf_{D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}\mathbb E_P|D-L(P)|,
 \tag{42}
\]
and
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 =
 \inf_{\mathcal C\in\mathscr C_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}
 \mathbb E_P\operatorname{len}(\mathcal C),
 \tag{43}
\]
where the infimum in (43) is subject to
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}
 P\{L(P)\in\mathcal C\}\ge1-\alpha.
 \tag{44}
\]
For \(\rho_0\in(-r,r)\) and \(H>0\), a broader local class
\(\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)\) is any class of anchor observable laws
satisfying
\[
 \{P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}}:
   \theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)\}
 \subseteq\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H).
 \tag{45}
\]
The families \(\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)\) need not be nested in \(H\).
No smoothness or rate condition beyond the displayed anchor conditions
is included in this definition.

%%% FIELD statement-anchor-class-collision-converse
Suppose that the covariate domain is compact, the ambient outcome
domain is compact and convex, and the absolute cost
\((y_0,y_1)\mapsto|y_1-y_0|\) is continuous, as in
\(\texttt{def:anchor-containing-class}\).  For every anchor-admissible
class \(\mathfrak P_K\) containing all embedded legal arrays, if
\(\lambda_K\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)\), then
\[
 \liminf_{K\to\infty}
 R_K^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)\ge R(\lambda),
 \qquad
 \liminf_{K\to\infty}
 C_{K,\alpha}^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 \ge C_\alpha(\lambda).
\]
If instead \(\lambda_K\to\infty\), then for every
\(\rho_0\in(-r,r)\) and every collection of classes
\(\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)\) satisfying
\[
 \{P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}}:
 \theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)\}
 \subseteq\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)
\]
for each \(K\) and \(H>0\), without any nesting requirement in \(H\),
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\liminf_{H\to\infty}\liminf_{K\to\infty}
 \inf_{D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\{D-L(P)\}\right)
 \ge V(\rho_0).
\]
These are converse lower bounds only; no broader-class upper bound or
equality is asserted.

For every legal array \(\theta\), injective relabeling of \(j\) as
\(j/K\) is a measurable isomorphism between the conditional
fixed-count observation under \(P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}}\) and
\(\mathcal O_K\).  The relabeling does not change any outcome law.
Lemma \(\texttt{lem:anchor-sharp-cot-identification}\) applies because
the covariate domain is compact, the ambient outcome domain \([0,1]\)
is compact and convex, treatment has overlap and is independent, and
\(h(y_0,y_1)=|y_1-y_0|\) is continuous.  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) then identifies the value on the
embedded submodel:
\[
 L(P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}})
 =L_K(\theta)=a-b\rho_K(\theta).
 \tag{88}
\]
Thus the causal target and the observed-law transport functional agree
on the embedded submodel by the cited sharp-identification result and
the separately proved binary specialization, not by definition.

Fix \(K\).  Any anchor estimator \(D\) restricts, through the measurable
relabeling, to a finite-array estimator.  Since
\(\mathcal D=[a-b,a+b]\subset[0,1]\), clipping that restriction to
\(\mathcal D\) cannot increase absolute loss at any target in
\(\mathcal A\).  Submodel inclusion and (88) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}\mathbb E_P|D-L(P)|
 &\ge
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \mathbb E_\theta|D-L_K(\theta)|\\
 &\ge R_K.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over anchor rules proves
\[
 R_K^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)\ge R_K.
 \tag{89}
\]
If \(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\), Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:finite-information-transfer}\) yields
\(R_K\to R(\lambda)\).  Taking lower limits in (89) proves the first
global inequality.

Now let \(\mathcal C\) be any anchor interval rule satisfying (44).
On every embedded law it covers \(L_K\) by (88).  Intersect its output
with \(\mathcal A\).  If the intersection is empty, replace it by any
fixed degenerate interval in \(\mathcal A\).  This operation produces a
closed subinterval of \(\mathcal A\), does not increase length, and
does not remove coverage of any target belonging to \(\mathcal A\).
Hence it is a feasible finite-array interval rule and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}
 \mathbb E_P\operatorname{len}(\mathcal C)
 \ge C_{K,\alpha}.
\]
Taking the infimum gives
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 \ge C_{K,\alpha}.
 \tag{90}
\]
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:honest-interval-transfer}\) gives
\(C_{K,\alpha}\to C_\alpha(\lambda)\), so (90) proves the second global
lower bound.

For the sparse local assertion, fix \(T>0\), \(H>0\), and \(K\).  Every
anchor estimator restricts to an estimator on the embedded shell.
Clipping to \(\mathcal D\) cannot increase the loss because
\(\ell_T(u)=\min\{u^2,T\}\) is nondecreasing in \(|u|\) and all shell
targets lie in \(\mathcal A\subset\mathcal D\).  Inclusion (45) and
(88) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\inf_{D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\{D-L(P)\}\right)\\
 &\quad\ge
 \inf_{D_K\in\mathscr D_K}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}(D_K-L_K)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{91}
\]
This inequality is pointwise in \(H\); it does not require the broader
families to be nested.  Take first \(\liminf_{K\to\infty}\), then
\(\liminf_{H\to\infty}\), and finally
\(\lim_{T\to\infty}\).  The deterministic shells themselves are nested
in \(H\), so their minimax values are nondecreasing in \(H\), and their
\(\liminf_H\) equals their \(H\)-limit.  Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:deterministic-shell-local-minimax}\), with its established
order of bounded-loss limits, turns the right side of (91) into
\(V(\rho_0)\).  This proves
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\liminf_{H\to\infty}\liminf_{K\to\infty}
 \inf_{D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\{D-L(P)\}\right)
 \ge V(\rho_0).
\]
Only submodel restriction was used, so the argument supplies no
broader-class achievability or equality.
