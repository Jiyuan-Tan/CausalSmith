%%% FIELD proof-honest-interval-transfer
We use the convention
\[
 \lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|.
\]
Two elementary consequences will be used repeatedly.  If \(\mathsf K\) is a
Markov kernel, then
\[
 \lVert P\mathsf K-Q\mathsf K\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq \lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}},
 \tag{1}
\]
because \(x\mapsto\mathsf K(x,B)\) takes values in \([0,1]\).  If
\(0\leq g\leq B_0\), then the layer-cake identity gives
\[
 |\mathbb E_Pg-\mathbb E_Qg|
 \leq B_0\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, integrate the total-variation bound for the events \(\{g>t\}\)
over \(0\leq t\leq B_0\).  These are the total-variation contraction and
bounded-loss transfer steps.  Every reported interval is contained in
\(\mathcal A=[a-br,a+br]\), so its length belongs to \([0,2br]\); here
\(b>0\) and \(r>0\) follow from the declared symbol ranges.

Define
\[
 e_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\eta_K\},\qquad
 \delta_K=2s_K+2/K,
\]
\[
 u_K=e_K+\min\{1,\lambda_Kc\delta_K/2\},
 \qquad
 d_K=\min\{1,5\varepsilon_K+\lambda_K^2/(2K)\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For \(t\geq0\), let the expansion of \([A_-,A_+]\subseteq\mathcal A\)
by \(t\) mean
\[
 \operatorname{Exp}_t([A_-,A_+])
 =\bigl[\max\{a-br,A_--t\},\min\{a+br,A_++t\}\bigr].
 \tag{4}
\]
It increases length by at most \(2t\).

We first use the parameter-free reverse comparison kernel to transfer a
finite-array rule to the marked-Poisson experiment.  Fix \(\zeta>0\) and
choose \(\mathcal C_K\in\mathscr C_K\) that is honest over
\(\mathcal T_K(r)\) and has worst expected length at most
\(C_{K,\alpha}+\zeta\).  For all sufficiently large \(K\), Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:supported-mixture-legality}\) makes \(q_K(\rho)\) available.
For \(\rho\in[-r,r]\), put \(q=q_K(\rho)\).  That proposition and
Proposition \(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) give
\[
 |q-\rho|\leq\delta_K,
 \qquad
 |L_K-(a-bq)|\leq bs_K
 \quad\Pi_{K,q}^{\mathrm{shell}}\text{-almost surely}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Honesty holds for every deterministic array in the support of this mixing
law.  Integrating the coverage inequality and then using (5) pointwise
therefore shows that
\(\operatorname{Exp}_{bs_K}(\mathcal C_K)\) covers \(a-bq\) with
probability at least \(1-\alpha\) under \(P^{\mathrm{mix}}_{K,q}\).

Apply this expanded rule after
\(\mathsf K_K^{\leftarrow}:\mathbb N_0^2\rightsquigarrow\mathcal O_K\)
from Theorem \(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\).  Equations (1)--(2)
and that theorem show that, at marked-Poisson parameter \(q\), coverage
can fall by at most \(e_K\), while expected length can rise by at most
\(2br e_K\).  To compare \(q\) with \(\rho\), couple their
marked-Poisson observations by using the same Poisson count and the same
independent uniform random variable for each mark.  A single mark differs
with probability \(c|q-\rho|/2\).  A union bound conditional on the count,
followed by \(\mathbb EN=\lambda_K\), yields
\[
 \left\lVert P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,q}
 -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\min\{1,\lambda_Kc|q-\rho|/2\}
 \leq\min\{1,\lambda_Kc\delta_K/2\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Expanding once more by \(b\delta_K\) converts coverage of \(a-bq\)
to coverage of \(a-b\rho\).  Thus the resulting marked-Poisson rule has
coverage at least \(1-\alpha-u_K\), and its worst expected length is at
most
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}+\zeta+2b(s_K+\delta_K)+2br u_K.
 \tag{7}
\]

We next derive the coverage repair used throughout the proof.  If a rule
has coverage at least \(1-\alpha-u\), where \(u\geq0\), independently
replace its output by \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\[
 \gamma(u)=\frac{u}{\alpha+u}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The declared range \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) makes the division valid and gives
\(0\leq\gamma(u)\leq1\).  The repaired coverage is at least
\[
 \gamma(u)+(1-\gamma(u))(1-\alpha-u)=1-\alpha,
 \tag{9}
\]
and expected length increases by at most \(2br\gamma(u)\).  Apply
(8)--(9) to (7), then let \(\zeta\downarrow0\), to obtain
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda_K)
 \leq C_{K,\alpha}+2b(s_K+\delta_K)+2br u_K
 +\frac{2br u_K}{\alpha+u_K}.
 \tag{10}
\]

For the other direction, choose an honest marked-Poisson rule at intensity
\(\lambda_K\) with worst expected length at most
\(C_\alpha(\lambda_K)+\zeta\), and apply it to the positive- and
negative-mark counts computed from the deterministic collision statistic.
The deterministic part of Theorem \(\texttt{thm:collision-comparison}\)
places the law of that statistic within \(d_K\) of
\(P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda_K,\rho_K}\), uniformly over
\(\mathcal T_K(r)\).  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\) gives the exact target identity
\(L_K=a-b\rho_K\).  Hence (1)--(2) imply that coverage falls by at most
\(d_K\) and expected length rises by at most \(2brd_K\).  Repairing once
by (8) with \(u=d_K\), and then letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\), gives
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}
 \leq C_\alpha(\lambda_K)+2brd_K
 +\frac{2brd_K}{\alpha+d_K}.
 \tag{11}
\]
This is the deterministic-statistic upper transfer; (10) is the
supported-mixture reverse-kernel lower transfer.

It remains to replace \(\lambda_K\) by its limit.  Couple Poisson point
processes of intensities \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda'\) by retaining their
common process and adding an independent process of intensity
\(|\lambda-\lambda'|\) to the larger experiment, with identically
distributed marks.  Uniformly in \(\rho\), the observations agree unless
the added process is nonempty, and therefore
\[
 v(\lambda,\lambda')
 :=\sup_{\rho\in[-r,r]}
 \left\lVert P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda,\rho}
 -P^{\mathcal E}_{\lambda',\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq1-e^{-|\lambda-\lambda'|}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Transporting an honest rule in either direction, using (2), and repairing
by (8) proves the explicit continuity modulus
\[
 |C_\alpha(\lambda)-C_\alpha(\lambda')|
 \leq2brv(\lambda,\lambda')
 +\frac{2brv(\lambda,\lambda')}{\alpha+v(\lambda,\lambda')}.
 \tag{13}
\]

Suppose \(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\).  Lemma
\(\texttt{lem:vanishing-triples-implies-growing-cells}\) gives
\(K\to\infty\).  Since \(\lambda_K\) is bounded, the definition
\[
 s_K=\min\{r/8,[K(1\vee\lambda_K)]^{-1/4}\}
\]
implies \(s_K\to0\) and \(Ks_K^2\to\infty\), whence
\(\eta_K=2e^{-Ks_K^2/2}\to0\).  Assumption
\(\texttt{ass:vanishing-triples}\) gives \(\varepsilon_K\to0\), and
boundedness of \(\lambda_K\) together with \(K\to\infty\) gives
\(\lambda_K^2/K\to0\).  Thus \(u_K\to0\) and \(d_K\to0\).  Equations
(10)--(13) prove
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}\longrightarrow C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{14}
\]

We now verify the interval-action programs in
\(\texttt{def:certified-interval-lp}\), including their corrected
admissibility domain.  Fix a computable \(\lambda\geq0\), a grid with
maximum parameter gap at most \(h\), and an integer \(m\) satisfying
\(m+1>\lambda\).  Because the directed enclosures decrease to
\(\lambda\), every sufficiently fine one satisfies
\[
 \lambda\leq\lambda_s^+<m+1.
 \tag{15}
\]
Conversely, the certificate is invoked only when (15) has actually been
verified.  No tail assertion below is used outside this domain.

The mark coupling used for (6), now at fixed \(\lambda\), gives for all
\(\rho,\rho'\in[-r,r]\)
\[
 \lVert f_{\lambda,\rho}-f_{\lambda,\rho'}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\omega_\lambda(|\rho-\rho'|)
 :=\min\{1,\lambda c|\rho-\rho'|/2\}.
 \tag{16}
\]
By (2), a fixed event has probability modulus \(\omega_\lambda\), and a
length functional taking values in \([0,2br]\) has expectation modulus
\(2br\omega_\lambda\).

For the tail calculation, \(\tau_m(0)=0\).  If
\(0<\lambda<m+1\), exponential Markov inequality, derived by applying
ordinary Markov inequality to \(e^{tN}\), gives for \(t>0\)
\[
 \Pr_\lambda(N\geq m+1)
 \leq\exp\{\lambda(e^t-1)-t(m+1)\}.
 \tag{17}
\]
The exponent is minimized at
\(t=\log((m+1)/\lambda)>0\).  Substitution yields
\[
 \tau_m(\lambda)
 \leq\overline\tau_m(\lambda)
 :=e^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{e\lambda}{m+1}\right)^{m+1}.
 \tag{18}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{d}{dx}\log\overline\tau_m(x)=\frac{m+1}{x}-1>0
 \quad(0<x<m+1).
 \tag{19}
\]
Consequently the verified inequalities in (15), and only those
inequalities, justify
\[
 \tau_m(\lambda)
 \leq\overline\tau_m(\lambda)
 \leq\overline\tau_m(\lambda_s^+).
 \tag{20}
\]

We next use finite-action relaxation for the lower certificate.  Start
with an arbitrary continuum-honest randomized interval rule.  On every
retained state, replace each realized interval by the shortest action in
\(\mathsf A_h\) spanning exactly the grid targets it contains, or by
\(\varnothing_G\) if it contains no grid target.  This preserves every
grid-target coverage indicator and cannot increase length.  Removing the
overflow states loses at most \(\tau_m(\lambda)\) in coverage.  Thus the
induced weights satisfy the exact program (L), and their objective is no
larger than the original worst expected length.  In the directed rational
lower program, replacing coverage coefficients upward and its coverage
right side downward enlarges the feasible set.  Replacing each
nonnegative length contribution downward also weakens its length
constraint.  It follows that its exact rational optimum obeys
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\leq C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{21}
\]

For the upper certificate, take an exactly feasible solution of the
directed rational upper program.  Such a solution exists for all
sufficiently fine \(s\) satisfying (15): the rule that always reports
\(\mathcal A\) has exact coverage one, leaving the positive slack
\(\alpha\), and the aggregate outward-rounding error eventually becomes
smaller than this slack.  The specified rounding directions imply that
the resulting actual rule has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) and expected
length at most \(P_{m,h,s}^+\) at every grid node; on overflow it reports
\(\mathcal A\).

For an arbitrary \(\rho\), choose a nearest grid point \(\rho_j\), so
\(|\rho-\rho_j|\leq h/2\).  If an action contains
\(a-b\rho_j\), its expansion \(E_h\) contains \(a-b\rho\).  Equation
(16) therefore gives coverage at least \(1-\alpha-\omega_h\), where
\[
 \omega_h=\min\{1,\lambda ch/4\}.
 \tag{22}
\]
The same modulus bounds the distributional change in expected length by
\(2br\omega_h\), while endpoint expansion adds at most \(bh\).  A repair
with
\[
 \gamma_h=\frac{\omega_h}{\alpha+\omega_h}
 \tag{23}
\]
restores coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) on the entire segment
\([-r,r]\).  Directed upper evaluation of every displayed term proves
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \leq P_{m,h,s}^++bh+2br\omega_h+2br\gamma_h
 =U_{m,h,s}^+.
 \tag{24}
\]
Equations (21) and (24) are the claimed certified enclosure, and (22)--(23)
show explicitly that coverage holds between grid nodes rather than only
at them.

For completeness, the claimed certificates can be obtained using exact
rational arithmetic.  After adjoining slack variables and deleting
rationally dependent equality rows, either rounded program is a finite
rational linear program in standard form.  Phase-I basis enumeration
produces a rational feasible basis; feasibility was established above.
To remove degeneracy, perturb the rational right side lexicographically by
formal ordered infinitesimals and apply the ratio test by exact comparison
of their coefficient vectors.  A pivot with a negative reduced cost then
strictly decreases the lexicographically perturbed objective.  It cannot
revisit a basis, and there are only finitely many bases, so it terminates.
The terminal reduced costs do not depend on the right-side perturbation.
Letting the infinitesimals decrease successively to zero leaves a feasible
unperturbed basic solution and preserves nonnegativity of all terminal
reduced costs.  If \(B\) is its basis matrix, then
\(y=B^{-\mathsf T}c_B\) is rational.  The nonnegative reduced costs say
that \(y\) satisfies every dual inequality, and
\[
 c^{\mathsf T}x=c_B^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}b=b^{\mathsf T}y.
 \tag{25}
\]
Weak duality follows directly by multiplying the primal and dual
inequalities by their nonnegative variables and summing.  Hence (25)
certifies simultaneous primal and dual optimality.  Equivalently, finite
basis enumeration followed by the same rational feasibility and equality
checks produces the certificate.  Floating-point arithmetic may suggest a
basis but is not used in any inequality or certificate.

We finally prove contraction of the certified bracket.  Let \(\xi_s\)
dominate, for each constraint row, the aggregate directed widths of all
likelihood, tail, right-side, action-length, and correction quantities in
that row.  The aggregate budget in
\(\texttt{def:certified-interval-lp}\), rather than a merely pointwise
precision requirement, gives a finite constant \(C_0\), independent of
the row, such that
\[
 0\leq\xi_s\leq C_0 2^{-s}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{26}
\]
Fix \(\zeta>0\) and take a continuum-honest rule with worst expected
length at most \(C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta\).  Replace its overflow output
by \(\mathcal A\), at an expected-length cost at most
\(2br\tau_m(\lambda)\).  On retained states, round both endpoints outward
to adjacent target-grid endpoints.  Coverage is preserved, the result
belongs to \(\mathsf A_h\), and length rises by at most \(2bh\).  For all
large \(s\), mix this rule with \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\[
 \beta_s=\frac{2\xi_s}{\alpha}\leq1.
 \tag{27}
\]
Before coefficient rounding, this creates coverage slack
\(\alpha\beta_s=2\xi_s\), which absorbs the aggregate directed error.
The resulting weights are feasible for the rational upper program and
have directed objective at most
\[
 C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta+2br\tau_m(\lambda)+2bh
 +2br\beta_s+\xi_s.
 \tag{28}
\]
After adding the expansion, modulus, repair, and enclosure corrections in
(24), every refinement satisfying
\(m\to\infty\), \(h\downarrow0\), \(s\to\infty\), and the verified
admissibility condition \(m+1>\lambda_s^+\) obeys, by (18), (22), and
(26)--(28),
\[
 \limsup U_{m,h,s}^+\leq C_\alpha(\lambda)+\zeta.
\]
Letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\) gives
\[
 \limsup U_{m,h,s}^+\leq C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{29}
\]

For the reverse contraction, take an optimal rational lower-program rule.
Interpret \(\varnothing_G\) as a degenerate interval at a point of
\(\mathcal A\) distinct from all finitely many grid targets.  Such a point
exists because \(br>0\); this realization has length zero and preserves
all grid-coverage patterns.  Directed rounding and (26) imply actual
retained gridwise coverage at least
\(1-\alpha-\tau_m(\lambda)-\xi_s\) and actual retained expected length at
most \(D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s\).  Report \(\mathcal A\) on overflow, expand
retained endpoints by \(bh/2\), and transfer from a nearest grid point by
(16).  The resulting rule has continuum coverage at least
\[
 1-\alpha-(\xi_s+\omega_h)
 \tag{30}
\]
and worst expected length at most
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s+2br\tau_m(\lambda)+bh+2br\omega_h.
 \tag{31}
\]
One application of (8) with \(u=\xi_s+\omega_h\) makes (30) honest, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_\alpha(\lambda)
 \leq{}&D_{m,h,s}^-+\xi_s+2br\tau_m(\lambda)+bh+2br\omega_h\\
 &+\frac{2br(\xi_s+\omega_h)}
 {\alpha+\xi_s+\omega_h}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{32}
\]
Every term following \(D_{m,h,s}^-\) vanishes along an admissible
refinement.  Combining (21), (24), (29), and (32) proves
\[
 D_{m,h,s}^-\longrightarrow C_\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
 U_{m,h,s}^+\longrightarrow C_\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
 U_{m,h,s}^+-D_{m,h,s}^-\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{33}
\]
In particular, at \(\lambda=1\) and \(\alpha=0.05\), every sufficiently
fine admissible upper certificate has coverage at least \(0.95\) on every
parameter segment, and any admissible refinement that doubles the
interval-action resolution while sending \(m,s\) to infinity has bracket
width tending to zero.

Lastly, the finite deterministic-array implementation is precisely the
construction leading to (11): after transport through the collision
statistic, pay at most \(d_K\) in coverage and \(2brd_K\) in expected
length, then mix once with \(\mathcal A\) with probability
\(d_K/(\alpha+d_K)\).  Its reverse lower comparison is (10), obtained from
the parameter-free supported-mixture kernel.  Thus both the exact
interval-action certificates and the two-sided honest-minimax transfer
concern the realized sharp endpoint \(L_K\), uniformly over every legal
deterministic array.

%%% FIELD proof-anchor-class-collision-converse
The proof uses submodel monotonicity, after first verifying that the causal
target and the observed-law transport functional agree on the embedded
submodel.  Fix a legal array \(\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)\).  The injective
map \(j\mapsto j/K\) is a measurable bijection between the finite set of
cell labels and its image in \([0,1]\), with measurable inverse on that
image.  Conditional on the fixed arm counts, applying this relabeling to
every observed cell therefore gives a measurable isomorphism between the
observation under \(P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}}\) and \(\mathcal O_K\).
It does not alter either conditional outcome law.

The hypotheses of Lemma
\(\texttt{lem:anchor-sharp-cot-identification}\) hold on the embedded
law: the covariate domain \([0,1]\) is compact, the ambient outcome domain
\([0,1]\) is compact and convex, treatment has the overlap and
independence specified in \(\texttt{def:anchor-containing-class}\), and
\(h(y_0,y_1)=|y_1-y_0|\) is continuous.  That lemma identifies the sharp
causal lower endpoint with the observed-law conditional-transport
functional \(L(P)\).  Proposition
\(\texttt{prop:sharp-cot-endpoint}\), which separately evaluates the
binary conditional transport problem cell by cell under the amplitude,
support, and conditional-Bernoulli restrictions, then gives
\[
 L(P_{K,\theta}^{\mathrm{emb}})
 =L_K(\theta)=a-b\rho_K(\theta).
 \tag{34}
\]
Thus (34) is an identification conclusion from the declared restrictions,
not part of the definition of the broader anchor functional.

We first prove the global absolute-risk comparison.  Fix an arbitrary
anchor rule \(D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\).  Restrict it through the
measurable relabeling to the embedded finite-array experiment.  Because
\(0<b<a<1/2\), the finite decision range
\(\mathcal D=[a-b,a+b]\) is contained in \([0,1]\); moreover every target
\(L_K\) belongs to \(\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal D\).  Projection onto
the closed interval \(\mathcal D\) therefore decreases
\(|D-L_K|\) pointwise.  Using the inclusion of every embedded legal array
in \(\mathfrak P_K\), followed by (34), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}\mathbb E_P|D-L(P)|
 &\geq
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \mathbb E_\theta|D-L_K(\theta)|\\
 &\geq
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal T_K(r)}
 \mathbb E_\theta
 \bigl|\operatorname{clip}_{\mathcal D}(D)-L_K(\theta)\bigr|\\
 &\geq R_K.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{35}
\]
The last inequality holds because the clipped restriction is one member of
\(\mathscr D_K\), whereas \(R_K\) is the infimum over all such rules.
Taking the infimum in (35) over anchor rules yields the finite-sample
submodel inequality
\[
 R_K^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)\geq R_K.
 \tag{36}
\]
If \(\lambda_K\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)\), Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:finite-information-transfer}\) gives
\(R_K\to R(\lambda)\).  Taking lower limits in (36) proves
\[
 \liminf_{K\to\infty}R_K^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 \geq R(\lambda).
 \tag{37}
\]

For the honest-length comparison, take any anchor interval rule
\(\mathcal C\in\mathscr C_K^{\mathrm{anc}}\) satisfying the uniform
coverage restriction (44).  On every embedded law it covers \(L_K\) by
(34).  Intersect its reported interval with \(\mathcal A\).  When that
intersection is empty, replace it by an arbitrary fixed degenerate interval
in \(\mathcal A\).  The result is a measurable closed-subinterval-valued
rule in \(\mathscr C_K\).  It never has greater length.  It also cannot
remove coverage of a target in \(\mathcal A\): if the original interval
contains that target, its intersection with \(\mathcal A\) is nonempty and
contains it.  Hence the restricted rule is feasible for the finite-array
honesty problem, and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_K}
 \mathbb E_P\operatorname{len}(\mathcal C)
 \geq C_{K,\alpha}.
 \tag{38}
\]
Taking the infimum over anchor rules in (38) gives
\[
 C_{K,\alpha}^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 \geq C_{K,\alpha}.
 \tag{39}
\]
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:honest-interval-transfer}\) gives
\(C_{K,\alpha}\to C_\alpha(\lambda)\) whenever
\(\lambda_K\to\lambda<\infty\).  Therefore
\[
 \liminf_{K\to\infty}
 C_{K,\alpha}^{\mathrm{anc}}(\mathfrak P_K)
 \geq C_\alpha(\lambda).
 \tag{40}
\]

It remains to establish the sparse local converse with exactly the stated
order of limits.  Assume \(\lambda_K\to\infty\), and fix
\(\rho_0\in(-r,r)\), \(T>0\), and \(H>0\).  An anchor estimator restricts
through the same relabeling to an estimator on the embedded deterministic
shell.  Since
\(\ell_T(u)=\min\{u^2,T\}\) is nondecreasing in \(|u|\), and every shell
target lies in \(\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal D\), clipping to
\(\mathcal D\) cannot increase the displayed truncated quadratic loss.
The assumed inclusion of the embedded shell in
\(\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)\), together with (34), therefore gives the
pointwise-in-\(H\) inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\inf_{D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\{D-L(P)\}\right)\\
 &\quad\geq
 \inf_{D_K\in\mathscr D_K}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal H_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}(D_K-L_K)\right).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{41}
\]
No nesting of the broader classes was used in (41).  For the right-hand
side, however, the deterministic shells are nested as \(H\) increases.
Consequently their minimax values, and then their \(K\)-lower limits, are
nondecreasing in \(H\); their \(H\)-limit therefore equals their
\(H\)-lower limit.  Apply successively
\(\liminf_{K\to\infty}\), \(\liminf_{H\to\infty}\), and finally
\(\lim_{T\to\infty}\) to (41).  Theorem
\(\texttt{thm:deterministic-shell-local-minimax}\), with precisely this
ordering of the bounded-loss, local-radius, and sample-size limits, turns
the resulting right-hand side into \(V(\rho_0)\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\lim_{T\to\infty}\liminf_{H\to\infty}\liminf_{K\to\infty}
 \inf_{D\in\mathscr D_K^{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak B_K(\rho_0,H)}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_T\!\left(
 \sqrt{\lambda_K}\{D-L(P)\}\right)\\
 &\qquad\geq V(\rho_0).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{42}
\]
Only restriction to an included submodel was used in (35), (38), and
(41).  The proof therefore establishes converse lower bounds and does not
assert an upper bound or equality over either broader anchor class; in
particular, no later anchor smoothness or rate hypothesis has been used.
