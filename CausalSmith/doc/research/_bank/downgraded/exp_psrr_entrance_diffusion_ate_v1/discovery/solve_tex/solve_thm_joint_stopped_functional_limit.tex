%%% FIELD def-psrr-class-construction
Let \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) be the class of deterministic triangular arrays \((x_{ni})_{i=1}^n\), indexed by even \(n\), with fixed \(k\), such that \(n^{-1}\sum_i x_{ni}=0\), \(\sup_{n,i}\|x_{ni}\|\le K_0\), \(S_{xx,n}\) converges entrywise to a finite matrix, and \(\liminf_n\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x>0\).  The design attached to every member is equal allocation on \(\mathcal A_n\), uniform initialization, fixed \(a>0\), fixed finite \(\gamma\ge0\), and the original first-entry PSRR rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}original\mbox{-}psrr}\).  Let \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\) be the outcome-inference subclass consisting of arrays \((x_{ni},Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1))_{i=1}^n\) whose covariate projection belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), whose potential outcomes are fixed under assignment with no interference, for which \(\sup_{n,i}\{|Y_{ni}(0)|+|Y_{ni}(1)|\}\le K_0\), the finite-population covariance matrix of \((x_{ni}^{\mathsf T},Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1))^{\mathsf T}\) converges entrywise to a finite matrix, and \(\liminf_n v_n\ge c_s>0\).  Thus \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) is the outcome-free covariate-design class, while \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\) is the subclass on which the joint residual and causal-inference conclusions are asserted.
%%% FIELD lem-covariate-characteristics-statement
For a sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), define
\[
 N_x=\min\left\{N\in2\mathbb N:\ \lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x/2\ \text{and}\ \mathcal A_n(a)\ne\varnothing\ \text{for every even }n\ge N\right\}.
\]
Then \(N_x<\infty\).  For even \(n\ge N_x\), let \((W^x_{n,m})_{m\ge0}\), \(T_n^x\), and \(\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\) denote the original PSRR chain, its first-entry time, and its actual filtration, and write \(D^x_{n,m}=D_n(W^x_{n,m})\) and \(\Delta D^x_{n,m}=D^x_{n,m+1}-D^x_{n,m}\).  For every fixed \(L,R<\infty\),
\[
 \max_{\substack{0\le m<\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\\ \|D^x_{n,m}\|\le R}}
 \left\|n\mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})+4D^x_{n,m}
 +\frac{8\gamma D^x_{n,m}}{\|D^x_{n,m}\|^2}\right\|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0,
\]
\[
 \max_{\substack{0\le m<\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\\ \|D^x_{n,m}\|\le R}}
 \left\|n\mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\Delta D^{x\mathsf T}_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})-8I_k\right\|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
The conditional Lindeberg terms vanish for every fixed positive cutoff.  For a smooth extension \(\phi\) that is constant near zero and equals \(\log r\) for \(r\ge a/2\), the same conclusions hold globally on compacts with \(D/\|D\|^2\) replaced by \(\phi'(\|D\|^2)D\); the extension agrees with original PSRR through first entry.  Its compactly stopped second moment obeys a bound of the form
\[
 n\mathbb E\!\left(\|D^x_{n,m+1}\|^2-\|D^x_{n,m}\|^2\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\right)
 \le C-c\|D^x_{n,m}\|^2.
\]
Moreover, the number of rejected proposals among the first \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\) proposals is \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), and, on \(\{T_n^x>0\}\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}\!\left(D^x_{n,T_n^x},\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\right)\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
All conclusions use only the covariate-design conditions and the actual uniformly initialized path; they are not uniform over arbitrary balanced states.
%%% FIELD lem-covariate-characteristics-proof
Put \(d_{ni}=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}x_{ni}\).  Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) and \(\liminf_n\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x\) imply that, eventually,
\[
 \max_i\|d_{ni}\|\le (2/c_x)^{1/2}K_0,
 \qquad \sum_i d_{ni}=0,
 \qquad \frac1n\sum_i d_{ni}d_{ni}^{\mathsf T}=\frac{n-1}{n}I_k. \tag{1}
\]
The finite-population vector central limit lemma applied to \(q_{ni}=d_{ni}\) is applicable because \(k\) is fixed, (1) gives covariance convergence to \(I_k\), and \(n^{-1}\max_i\|d_{ni}\|^2\to0\).  Hence
\[
 D_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow G\sim\mathcal N_k(0,I_k). \tag{2}
\]
Since the sphere \(\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\) has Gaussian probability zero and \(a>0\), the portmanteau theorem applied to the closed ball gives
\[
 \Pr\{M_n(W_{n,0})\le a\}\longrightarrow\Pr\{\|G\|^2\le a\}>0. \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal A_n(a)\ne\varnothing\) eventually, proving \(N_x<\infty\).  Positive definiteness on that tail makes every score and transition well defined.

Fix a balanced state and abbreviate \(D=D_n(W)\).  If \(i\) is uniform in the treated arm and \(j\) is independently uniform in the control arm, the proposed score increment is
\[
 \delta=\frac{2}{\sqrt n}(d_{nj}-d_{ni}).
\]
The treated and control means of \(d_{ni}\) are respectively \(D/\sqrt n\) and \(-D/\sqrt n\).  Expanding the two product averages and using (1) therefore gives the exact identities
\[
 \mathbb E(\delta\mid W)=-\frac{4D}{n},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W)
 =\frac{8}{n}\frac1n\sum_i d_{ni}d_{ni}^{\mathsf T}+\frac{8DD^{\mathsf T}}{n^2}. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, uniformly for bounded \(D\), the second expression multiplied by \(n\) tends to \(8I_k\).

Choose \(\phi\in C^3([0,\infty))\) constant near zero and equal to \(\log r\) for \(r\ge a/2\), with \(U(d)=\phi(\|d\|^2)\) having bounded first three derivatives.  The bound in (1) gives \(\|\delta\|\le Cn^{-1/2}\).  Taylor expansion, uniformly on compact score sets, yields
\[
 U(D+\delta)-U(D)=2\phi'(\|D\|^2)D^{\mathsf T}\delta+O(n^{-1}), \tag{5}
\]
and \(1\wedge e^{-\gamma y}=1-\gamma y_++O(y^2)\) uniformly for \(|y|=O(n^{-1/2})\).  Thus the acceptance probability \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
 \alpha=1-2\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)(D^{\mathsf T}\delta)_++O(n^{-1}). \tag{6}
\]
Since \(z_+=(z+|z|)/2\), (4)--(6) imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 n\mathbb E(\alpha\delta\mid W)
 &=-4D-\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)n\mathbb E(\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W)D\\
 &\quad-\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)n\mathbb E\{\delta|D^{\mathsf T}\delta|\mid W\}+O(n^{-1/2}).
 \end{split} \tag{7}
\]
It remains to control the last, nonsmooth average along the actual path; orthogonality at a single state would not suffice.

To do so, extend the process to an all-pairs clock.  At each tick choose a uniform unordered pair, act as the identity for equal labels, and otherwise use the acceptance rule (6).  Couple this process to a chain that starts at the same uniform balanced allocation and always performs the chosen transposition.  The latter chain is uniform on \(\mathcal A_n\) at every tick by permutation invariance.  Boundedness of \(\nabla U\), (1), and \(1-e^{-z}\le z\) for \(z\ge0\) give
\[
 \Pr(\text{rejection at a tick}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})\le Cn^{-1/2}. \tag{8}
\]
Consequently the number of discrepancies through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\) all-pairs ticks has expectation at most \(CL\sqrt n\), and Markov's inequality shows that the two allocation vectors have Hamming distance \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), uniformly through that horizon.  For any uniformly bounded deterministic score \(g_{ni}\), changing \(h\) labels changes its arm mean by at most \(2\|g_n\|_\infty h/n\).  To obtain the needed sampling-without-replacement bound, expose a uniform treated set one draw at a time and take the Doob martingale of its final centered mean.  If \(|g_{ni}|\le K\), replacing one revealed draw changes that conditional mean by at most \(4K/n\).  Conditional exponential-moment bounds followed by Chernoff optimization therefore give, directly,
\[
 \Pr\left(\left|\frac2n\sum_{i:V_i=1}g_{ni}-\frac1n\sum_i g_{ni}\right|>\eta\right)
 \le 2\exp\left(-\frac{n\eta^2}{8K^2}\right). \tag{9a}
\]
A union bound over \(O(n)\) ticks proves uniform convergence of the shadow arm means; the coupling transfers it to the actual path.  For a bounded Lipschitz family indexed by the unit sphere, a polynomial-size \(n^{-1}\)-net followed by Lipschitz interpolation makes the convergence uniform in the index.

For a unit vector \(u\), define
\[
 F_{u,\ell}(q,q')=(q'_{\ell}-q_{\ell})|u^{\mathsf T}(q'-q)|,
 \qquad g_{u,\ell,n}(q)=\int F_{u,\ell}(q,q')\,\mu_n(dq'), \tag{9}
\]
where \(\mu_n\) is the deterministic full-population empirical measure.  The kernel \(F_{u,\ell}\) is antisymmetric, so \(\int g_{u,\ell,n}\,d\mu_n=0\).  If \(\nu_T=\mu_n+\xi\) and \(\nu_C=\mu_n-\xi\) are the two arm empirical measures, antisymmetry cancels the \(\mu_n\otimes\mu_n\) and \(\xi\otimes\xi\) terms and makes the mixed terms equal.  Hence exactly
\[
 \iint F_{u,\ell}(q,q')\,\nu_T(dq)\nu_C(dq')
 =2\int g_{u,\ell,n}(q)\,\nu_T(dq). \tag{10}
\]
The family on the right is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz in \(u\).  The preceding uniform arm-mean result and its zero population mean show that (10) is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\), uniformly through every fixed scaled horizon.  Taking \(u=D/\|D\|\) in (10) proves that the last expectation in (7), after multiplication by \(n\), is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) uniformly on localized paths.  Equations (4) and (7) now give the asserted drift.  Because \(1-\alpha=O(n^{-1/2})\) and \(\|\delta\|^2=O(n^{-1})\),
\[
 n\left\|\mathbb E\{(1-\alpha)\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W\}\right\|=O(n^{-1/2}), \tag{11}
\]
so (4) gives the asserted covariance.  The jump bound makes the conditional Lindeberg expression identically zero for all sufficiently large \(n\).

An all-pairs tick selects opposite labels with the state-independent probability \(p_n=n/[2(n-1)]\).  The corresponding indicators are independent Bernoulli variables.  Their centered partial sums are martingales with variance at most the number of ticks; applying the squared-submartingale maximal inequality and then inverting the monotone counting process gives, for the inverse clock \(A_n(m)\),
\[
 \max_{m\le nL}\left|\frac{A_n(m)}n-\frac{m}{np_n}\right|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{12}
\]
Deleting identity ticks recovers the original treated--control proposal clock.  Thus the all-pairs characteristic rates are multiplied by \(p_n^{-1}\to2\), producing exactly the constants in (7) and (11), rather than a stationary-chain clock.

For the smooth extension, (4), (8), and \(2D^{\mathsf T}\mathbb E(\delta\mid W)=-8\|D\|^2/n\) yield constants \(C,c>0\) such that
\[
 n\mathbb E\{\|D+J\delta\|^2-\|D\|^2\mid W\}
 \le C-c\|D\|^2, \tag{13}
\]
where, conditionally on the proposal, \(J\) is its acceptance indicator with mean \(\alpha\), so \(J\delta\) is zero after rejection.  Indeed, the rejection correction to the scaled drift is bounded, its scalar product with \(D\) is at most \(C\|D\|\), the quadratic term is bounded, and \(C\|D\|\le(c/2)\|D\|^2+C^2/(2c)\).

The same bound (8), now on the original proposal clock, gives expected rejections at most \(CL\sqrt n\) through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\), hence the claimed \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\) bound.  Finally, on \(\{T_n^x>0\}\), the preterminal radius is larger than \(a\), the terminal radius is at most \(a\), and (1) gives \(\|D^x_{n,T_n^x}-D^x_{n,T_n^x-1}\|\le Cn^{-1/2}\).  The reverse triangle inequality therefore gives the asserted distance from the terminal score to the sphere.  This completes every claimed characteristic, clock, and crossing bound using covariates only.
%%% FIELD thm-two-class-functional-statement
The following two conclusions hold.

First, for every sequence in the covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), use \(N_x\), \((W^x_{n,m})\), and \(T_n^x\) from \(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\), and set
\[
 \bar D_n^x(t)=D_n\!\left(W^x_{n,\min\{\lfloor nt\rfloor,T_n^x\}}\right),
 \qquad
 A_n^x=\sum_{m=0}^{T_n^x-1}\mathbf 1\{W^x_{n,m+1}\ne W^x_{n,m}\}.
\]
Along even \(n\ge N_x\), jointly in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^k)\times[0,\infty)\),
\[
 (\bar D_n^x,T_n^x/n)\Rightarrow(D_{\cdot\wedge H},H),
 \qquad \frac{T_n^x-A_n^x}{n}\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{14}
\]
Here, before \(H=\inf\{t\ge0:\|D_t\|^2\le a\}\),
\[
 dD_t=\left(-4D_t-\frac{8\gamma D_t}{\|D_t\|^2}\right)dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t,
 \qquad D_0=G\sim\mathcal N_k(0,I_k), \tag{15}
\]
and \(H<\infty\) almost surely.  If \(R_0=\|D_0\|^2=r\), \(b=k/2-\gamma\), and \(\lambda>0\), then the proposal-time first-entry law has the computable transform
\[
 \mathbb E_r(e^{-\lambda H})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le a,\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\frac{U(\lambda/8,b,r/2)}{U(\lambda/8,b,a/2)},&r>a,
 \end{cases} \tag{16}
\]
where, for \(A>0\) and \(x>0\),
\[
 U(A,b,x)=\frac1{\Gamma(A)}\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t^{A-1}(1+t)^{b-A-1}\,dt. \tag{17}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\), (16) is understood as one.  Under the Gaussian initialization, with \(F_{\chi_k^2}\) the chi-square distribution function,
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{-\lambda H})=F_{\chi_k^2}(a)+
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_a^\infty U(\lambda/8,b,r/2)r^{k/2-1}e^{-r/2}\,dr}
 {\displaystyle 2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2)U(\lambda/8,b,a/2)}. \tag{18}
\]
For every \(r>a\) and \(\lambda>0\), the transform in (16) is strictly increasing in \(\gamma\); thus finite tuning changes the proposal-time search law.  Equations (14) and the rejection estimate show that the accepted-switch clock used in the published presentation and the all-proposal clock differ by \(o_{\Pr}(n)\).

Second, for every sequence in the outcome-inference subclass \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\), along the retained even outcome tail \(n\ge N_0\), jointly in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^{k+1})\times[0,\infty)\),
\[
 \bigl(\bar Z_n,T_n/n\bigr)\Rightarrow
 \left(\bigl((D_{t\wedge H}^{\mathsf T},E_{t\wedge H})^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{t\ge0},H\right), \tag{19}
\]
where (15) holds and \(E\) is the independent stationary solution of \(dE_t=-4E_tdt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t\), \(E_0\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).  This is the unique weak solution of the stopped system in \(\mathrm{def{:}limit\mbox{-}system}\).  No projected Markov property is assumed in either clause.
%%% FIELD thm-two-class-functional-proof
We prove the covariate and joint clauses in turn.

For the covariate class, use the smooth extension from the covariate characteristic lemma and write \(X_n(t)=D_n(W^x_{n,\lfloor nt\rfloor})\) for its unabsorbed proposal-clock interpolation.  The extension agrees with the original process until \(T_n^x\).  Its limiting drift and covariance are
\[
 \beta_\phi(d)=-4d-8\gamma\phi'(\|d\|^2)d,
 \qquad c_\phi=8I_k. \tag{20}
\]
The clock conversion (12) in that lemma is already on the original treated--control proposal scale, so no stationary or accepted-move time change is hidden in (20).

We first establish compact containment.  Stop the extension at the first exit \(\sigma_{n,R}\) from the ball of radius \(R\).  Summing (13) through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge\sigma_{n,R}\), using \(\mathbb E\|X_n(0)\|^2=k\), and noting that the overshoot is at most \(Cn^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \Pr\left\{\sup_{t\le L}\|X_n(t)\|>R\right\}
 \le\frac{k+CL+o(1)}{(R-Cn^{-1/2})^2}. \tag{21}
\]
Thus the left side can be made uniformly small by first taking \(R\to\infty\).  On a fixed ball, the predictable drift rate is bounded, the predictable quadratic-variation rate is bounded, and all jumps are at most \(Cn^{-1/2}\).  If \(\sigma_n\le L\) is a stopping time and \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the finite-variation increment over \([\sigma_n,\sigma_n+\delta_n]\) is \(O(\delta_n+n^{-1})\), while conditional Chebyshev applied to the martingale increment gives
\[
 \Pr\{\|M_n(\sigma_n+\delta_n)-M_n(\sigma_n)\|>\eta\}
 \le C\eta^{-2}(\delta_n+n^{-1}). \tag{22}
\]
Together with (21), this proves tightness, and the vanishing jump bound makes every subsequential limit continuous.

For \(f\in C_c^3(\mathbb R^k)\), Taylor's theorem and \(\|\Delta X_n\|\le Cn^{-1/2}\) give, before compact exit,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\{f(X_{n,(m+1)/n})-f(X_{n,m/n})\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\}
 &=\nabla f(X_{n,m/n})^{\mathsf T}\mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})\\
 &\quad+\frac12\nabla^2f(X_{n,m/n}):
 \mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\Delta D^{x\mathsf T}_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})+O(n^{-3/2}).
 \end{split} \tag{23}
\]
The trajectory-uniform characteristic bounds make the sum, through \(nL\) steps, of the difference between (23) and \(n^{-1}\mathcal L_\phi f(X_{n,m/n})\) converge to zero in probability, where
\[
 \mathcal L_\phi f(d)=\beta_\phi(d)^{\mathsf T}\nabla f(d)+4\operatorname{tr}\{\nabla^2f(d)\}. \tag{24}
\]
On compact stopping the errors are uniformly bounded, so truncation upgrades this convergence to \(L^1\).  Passing the conditional martingale identity to a subsequential limit shows directly that, for \(0\le s<t\) and every bounded continuous functional \(Q\) of the path through time \(s\),
\[
 \mathbb E\left[Q\left\{f(X_t)-f(X_s)-\int_s^t\mathcal L_\phi f(X_u)\,du\right\}\right]=0. \tag{25}
\]
Thus every limit solves the martingale problem for (24).  Because \(\phi\) is constant near zero, smooth on the transition annulus, and logarithmic away from zero, the map \(d\mapsto\phi'(\|d\|^2)d\) is globally Lipschitz.  Two solutions driven by the same Brownian motion therefore satisfy, by the Lipschitz bound and Gronwall's inequality,
\[
 \mathbb E\sup_{u\le t}\|X_u-X'_u\|^2\le C\int_0^t\mathbb E\sup_{v\le u}\|X_v-X'_v\|^2du=0. \tag{26}
\]
Picard iteration constructs the solution, and (26) gives pathwise uniqueness and hence uniqueness of the law solving (25).  The initialization (2) identifies \(X_0=G\).  Consequently the whole sequence converges to the unique solution of (20).

Before entry, \(\phi'(\|D_t\|^2)=1/\|D_t\|^2\).  It\^o's formula applied to \(R_t=\|D_t\|^2\) therefore gives
\[
 dR_t=8(k-2\gamma-R_t)dt+\sqrt{32R_t}\,d\beta_t,
 \qquad R_t>a. \tag{27}
\]
A scale derivative is \(s'(r)=e^{r/2}r^{\gamma-k/2}\), because direct substitution into
\[
 \mathcal L_Rg(r)=16r g''(r)+8(k-2\gamma-r)g'(r) \tag{28}
\]
gives \(\mathcal L_Rs=0\).  Define \(H_a=\inf\{t:R_t\le a\}\) and \(H_M=\inf\{t:R_t\ge M\}\).  For \(a<r<M\), stopping the bounded martingale \(s(R_{t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M})\) and solving for the upper-exit probability gives
\[
 \Pr_r(H_M<H_a)=\frac{s(r)-s(a)}{s(M)-s(a)}. \tag{29}
\]
The denominator diverges as \(M\to\infty\).  On each compact interval \([a,M]\), the diffusion coefficient in (27) is bounded away from zero; integrating the linear equation \(\mathcal L_Rg=-1\), \(g(a)=g(M)=0\), produces a bounded nonnegative \(C^2\) function, and Dynkin's identity gives finite expected exit time.  Therefore (29), followed by \(M\to\infty\), proves \(H<\infty\) almost surely from every exterior point.  Interior points have \(H=0\).

The Gaussian initialization assigns zero mass to \(R_0=a\).  At a hit from outside the martingale coefficient in (27) is \(\sqrt{32a}>0\).  On a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(a\), divide the equation by this coefficient and apply the time change given by its quadratic variation.  The drift is bounded, whereas the reflection-principle calculation for Brownian motion started at zero gives probability zero of remaining on one side on any positive interval.  Hence the extended radial path enters \((0,a)\) immediately after its first hit, almost surely.  Initial strict-interior paths have hit time zero stably, and exterior paths have a finite boundary hit followed immediately by a strict-interior crossing.  These facts show directly that the closed-ball hitting functional is continuous at almost every limiting path.  The terminal jump estimate in the covariate characteristic lemma supplies the corresponding discrete crossing condition.  Applying this continuity on \([0,L]\), then letting \(L\to\infty\) using \(\Pr(H>L)\to0\), proves (14) and also \(T_n^x/n=O_{\Pr}(1)\).

Let \(A_n^x\) be the accepted-switch count in the statement, and write \(R_n(L)\) for the number of rejections through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\).  For fixed \(L\), the characteristic lemma gives \(R_n(L)=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\).  Therefore, for \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{(T_n^x-A_n^x)/n>\eta\}
 \le \Pr\{T_n^x>nL\}+\Pr\{R_n(L)>\eta n\}. \tag{30}
\]
The second term tends to zero for fixed \(L\), and the first is made arbitrarily small by taking \(L\to\infty\).  This proves the second assertion in (14) and precisely identifies the accepted-switch/proposal-clock equivalence at first order.

We next derive the runtime transform rather than assume it.  For \(A=\lambda/8>0\), \(b=k/2-\gamma\), and \(x>0\), the integral (17) is finite and positive.  Differentiation under the integral, justified by its exponential tail and integrability at zero, followed by one integration by parts, gives Kummer's equation
\[
 xU_{xx}+(b-x)U_x-AU=0. \tag{31}
\]
With \(u_\lambda(r)=U(A,b,r/2)/U(A,b,a/2)\), (28) and (31) imply
\[
 \mathcal L_Ru_\lambda=\lambda u_\lambda,
 \qquad u_\lambda(a)=1. \tag{32}
\]
Changing variables \(z=xt\) in (17) and applying dominated convergence gives \(U(A,b,x)\sim x^{-A}\) as \(x\to\infty\), hence \(u_\lambda(r)\to0\).  It\^o's formula shows that \(e^{-\lambda(t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M)}u_\lambda(R_{t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M})\) is a bounded martingale.  Letting first \(t\to\infty\) and then \(M\to\infty\), using (29), the almost-sure finiteness of \(H_a\), and \(u_\lambda(M)\to0\), yields (16).  Integrating (16) against the \(\chi_k^2\) density and adding the mass \(R_0\le a\), where \(H=0\), gives (18).  Thus (18) is a one-dimensional positive integral and is computable without simulation of the allocation chain.

The tuning dependence is strict.  For \(\gamma_2>\gamma_1\), let \(u_j\) solve (32) for \(\gamma_j\).  Formula (17) gives \(u'_2(r)<0\).  Since
\[
 (\mathcal L_{R,\gamma_1}-\lambda)u_2
 =16(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)u'_2<0, \tag{33}
\]
the one-dimensional maximum principle on \([a,\infty)\), using \(u_2-u_1=0\) at \(a\) and \(u_2-u_1\to0\) at infinity, gives \(u_2(r)>u_1(r)\) for \(r>a\); equivalently one may apply the principle on \([a,M]\) and then let \(M\to\infty\).  The exterior Gaussian branch has positive probability, so the unconditional transforms are strictly ordered as well.  Tuning therefore changes the search-time law even though, as proved below, it does not change the endpoint law.

Now take a sequence in the outcome-inference subclass.  The initial-score lemma gives
\[
 Z_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow\mathcal N_{k+1}(0,I_{k+1}). \tag{34}
\]
The established actual-path characteristic theorem gives, on the outcome tail, drift
\[
 \beta_\phi(d,e)=\bigl(-4d-8\gamma\phi'(\|d\|^2)d,-4e\bigr)
\]
and covariance \(8I_{k+1}\), with vanishing conditional Lindeberg terms and the same terminal-crossing control.  Repeating (21)--(26) in dimension \(k+1\) identifies the unique solution
\[
 \begin{split}
 dD_t&=\{-4D_t-8\gamma\phi'(\|D_t\|^2)D_t\}dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t,\\
 dE_t&=-4E_tdt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t,
 \end{split} \tag{35}
\]
with independent standard-Gaussian initial coordinates and independent Brownian noises; the block-diagonal covariance and uniqueness justify this independence, rather than any finite-population independence assumption.  The hitting argument depends only on \(D\), so the same stopping transfer proves (19).  Before \(H\), (35) equals the stated limit system, and absorption at \(H\) completes the proof.
%%% FIELD thm-radial-design-class-statement
Over the covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), along even \(n\ge N_x\),
\[
 D_n(W^x_{n,T_n^x})\Rightarrow C_{k,a}.
\]
This conclusion is outcome-free.  Over the outcome-inference subclass \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\), along even \(n\ge N_0\),
\[
 \bigl(D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\bigr)
 \Rightarrow(C_{k,a},\varepsilon),
\]
where \(C_{k,a}\) and \(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) are independent.  In either scope,
\[
 \|C_{k,a}\|^2=\min\{\chi_k^2,a\},
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0.
\]
For every fixed finite \(\gamma\), the endpoint law is the same; tuning changes only the search-time transform in (16)--(18).  For finite \(a>0\), the covariate endpoint is not the classical uniform-rerandomization law \(L_{k,a}\), which assigns the boundary sphere probability zero.
%%% FIELD thm-radial-design-class-proof
The two-class functional theorem gives convergence of the stopped covariate path and its finite hitting time.  On \(\{\|G\|^2<a\}\), one has \(H=0\) and \(D_H=G\).  This event has no boundary ambiguity because \(\Pr\{\|G\|^2=a\}=0\).  On \(\{\|G\|^2>a\}\), continuity of \(D\) gives
\[
 \|D_H\|=\sqrt a. \tag{36}
\]
For every orthogonal matrix \(Q\), both the drift and diffusion coefficient in (15) satisfy the equivariance relations \(b(Qd)=Qb(d)\) and \(QI_kQ^{\mathsf T}=I_k\).  The initial Gaussian law and the stopping set are also invariant under \(Q\).  Uniqueness of the weak solution therefore implies
\[
 QD_H\overset d=D_H. \tag{37}
\]
The only rotation-invariant probability law on the sphere of radius \(\sqrt a\) is normalized surface measure.  Hence the exterior branch in (36) is uniform in direction and has mass \(\Pr(\|G\|^2>a)\).  The interior branch retains the original Gaussian measure.  A spherically symmetric Gaussian has direction uniform and independent of radius, so these two pieces are exactly the distribution of
\[
 G\min\{1,\sqrt a/\|G\|\}=C_{k,a}. \tag{38}
\]
This proves the outcome-free covariate assertion and shows directly that \(\gamma\) does not enter its law.

On the outcome-inference subclass, the residual coordinate in (35) has the explicit representation
\[
 E_t=e^{-4t}\varepsilon+\sqrt8\int_0^t e^{-4(t-u)}\,dB'_u. \tag{39}
\]
Its variance is
\[
 e^{-8t}+8\int_0^t e^{-8(t-u)}du=1, \tag{40}
\]
so \(E\) is stationary standard Gaussian.  The entire residual process is independent of the covariate process, and \(H\) is measurable with respect to the latter.  Thus, for bounded measurable \(f\) and any bounded covariate-path functional \(Q\), conditioning on the covariate path gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{f(E_H)Q(D)\}
 =\mathbb E\{f(\varepsilon)\}\mathbb E\{Q(D)\}. \tag{41}
\]
Consequently \(E_H\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and \(E_H\) is independent of \(D_H\).  This is a consequence of the block-diagonal limiting characteristics and stationarity, not an outcome--residual independence assumption on the finite populations.

Finally, (38) gives
\[
 \|C_{k,a}\|^2=\min\{\|G\|^2,a\}=\min\{\chi_k^2,a\}. \tag{42}
\]
Since \(k\ge1\) and \(0<a<\infty\), the chi-square distribution is continuous with a strictly positive upper tail, so the boundary mass is \(1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0\).  The final distinction from \(L_{k,a}\) follows from the next proposition.
%%% FIELD prop-nonuniform-design-class-statement
For every finite \(a>0\), over the outcome-free covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), the original first-entry PSRR covariate limit \(C_{k,a}\) is not equal in distribution to the uniform-rerandomization limit \(L_{k,a}\).  Specifically,
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0,
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\|L_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=0.
\]
The class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) is a regular equal-allocation subclass of the arrays admitted by the original Zhu--Liu first-entry algorithm.  Therefore this subclass certificate also refutes, on the broader published class, any universal representation of the original algorithm's output as a uniform draw from the acceptable set.  The positive diffusion assertion itself remains restricted to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\).
%%% FIELD prop-nonuniform-design-class-proof
By definition of the clipped Gaussian and continuity of the chi-square distribution,
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}
 =\Pr\{\|G\|^2\ge a\}
 =1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0. \tag{43}
\]
In contrast,
\[
 L_{k,a}\overset d=G\mid\{\|G\|^2\le a\}.
\]
Its conditional squared radius is a continuous chi-square law restricted to \([0,a]\).  Hence
\[
 \Pr\{\|L_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=0. \tag{44}
\]
The measurable sphere \(\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\) therefore separates the two probability measures, proving non-equality.

It remains to justify the scope statement.  Every design in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) has exactly equal arms, uniform balanced initialization, treated--control transposition proposals, the original Metropolis acceptance rule with fixed finite tuning, and immediate stopping at first entry into \(M_n\le a\).  These are the defining operations of the published original PSRR algorithm; boundedness, fixed dimension, second-moment convergence, and nondegeneracy only restrict the arrays on which it is run.  Thus \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) is a subclass of the broader algorithmic domain.  If a universal uniform-acceptable-set representation held on that broader domain, it would hold on every subclass, contradicting (43)--(44).  This logical transfer does not enlarge the scope of the positive functional-limit theorem.
