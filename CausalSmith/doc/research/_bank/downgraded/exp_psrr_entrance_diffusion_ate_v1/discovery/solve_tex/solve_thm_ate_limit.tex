%%% FIELD ate-proof
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}initial\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}clt}\), the following identity holds exactly on every retained row:
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)
 =b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
  +s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}). \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}radial\mbox{-}entrance\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}design\mbox{-}class}\),
\[
 \bigl(D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\bigr)
 \Rightarrow(C_{k,a},\varepsilon),
 \qquad C_{k,a}\perp\varepsilon. \tag{2}
\]
The hypotheses \(b_n\to b\) and \(s_n\to s\) are deterministic.  Apply the continuous mapping theorem to (2), together with deterministic Slutsky convergence, under the continuous map
\[
 (d,e,\widetilde b,\widetilde s)
 \longmapsto \widetilde b^{\mathsf T}d+\widetilde s e.
\]
Equation (1) then gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)
 \Rightarrow b^{\mathsf T}C_{k,a}+s\varepsilon. \tag{3}
\]
The independence asserted in the conclusion is exactly the independence in (2).  Throughout, \(\tau_n\) is the fixed finite-population causal contrast in \(\mathrm{def{:}ate\mbox{-}estimator}\); (3) is its randomization limit and does not redefine the estimand.

%%% FIELD stopped-moment-proof
The joint functional-limit theorem gives \(T_n/n=O_{\Pr}(1)\).  Thus, for every \(\eta>0\), there is \(L<\infty\) such that
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr(T_n>nL)<\eta. \tag{4}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}actual\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}arm\mbox{-}uniformity}\) to the finite collection of deterministic arrays
\[
 1,\quad x_{nij},\quad x_{nij}x_{ni\ell},\quad
 Y_{ni}(z),\quad x_{nij}Y_{ni}(z),\quad Y_{ni}(z)^2,
 \qquad z\in\{0,1\},\quad 1\le j,\ell\le k. \tag{5}
\]
The uniform-boundedness assumption gives this collection a common finite envelope.  Its parameter set is finite, hence compact, and the Lipschitz requirement is vacuous.  On \(\{T_n\le nL\}\), that lemma evaluated at \(m=T_n\) says that every treated- and control-arm raw empirical moment in (5) differs from its finite-population counterpart by \(o_{\Pr}(1)\).  By (4), the complementary event has limiting upper probability at most \(\eta\).  Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the asserted convergence at the random stopping time.

Centered covariances are polynomial functions of the raw moments in (5).  Consequently, for \(z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \widehat S_{xx,z,n}-S_{xx,n}=o_{\Pr}(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat c_{z,n}-S_{x,Y(z),n}=o_{\Pr}(1). \tag{6}
\]
Changing a divisor \(n/2\) to the corresponding degrees-of-freedom divisor changes these bounded quantities by \(o(1)\).  On the regular tail, \(\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x/2\).  Weyl's inequality and (6) give
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\min_{z\in\{0,1\}}
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat S_{xx,z,n})\ge c_x/4\right\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{7}
\]
On the event in (7), inversion is Lipschitz on the set of symmetric matrices with least eigenvalue at least \(c_x/4\), and hence
\[
 \widehat S_{xx,z,n}^{-1}-S_{xx,n}^{-1}=o_{\Pr}(1).
\]
Therefore the Moore--Penrose convention on the complement of (7) has no first-order effect.

On the event in (7), the intercept-including OLS residual mean square is
\[
 \widehat S_{e_z,n}^2
 =\widehat S_{Y(z)Y(z),z,n}
  -\widehat c_{z,n}^{\mathsf T}
   \widehat S_{xx,z,n}^{-1}\widehat c_{z,n}.
\]
The raw-moment convergence, (6), and inverse continuity imply
\[
 \widehat S_{e_z,n}^2-S_{e_ze_z,n}=o_{\Pr}(1),
 \qquad z\in\{0,1\}. \tag{8}
\]
Moreover, \(\|S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt{2/c_x}\) on the retained tail.  The definitions and (6)--(8) now give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat b_n-b_n
 &=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}\bigl\{(\widehat c_{1,n}-S_{x,Y(1),n})
 +(\widehat c_{0,n}-S_{x,Y(0),n})\bigr\}
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0,\\
 \widehat u_n-u_n
 &=2(\widehat S_{e_1,n}^2-S_{e_1e_1,n})
  +2(\widehat S_{e_0,n}^2-S_{e_0e_0,n})
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves every asserted stopped-design moment conclusion using the actual pre-entry path; no uniform draw from the acceptable set is introduced.

%%% FIELD coverage-proof
Fix an arbitrary sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\).  Every probability below is over assignment alone, because the potential outcomes are fixed.  The second-moment-limit and covariate-nondegeneracy conditions imply convergence of \(S_{xx,n}^{-1}\), the arm slopes, and all projection-residual second moments.  Hence
\[
 b_n\to b,\qquad s_n^2\to s^2,\qquad u_n\to u. \tag{10}
\]
Residual nondegeneracy gives \(s^2>0\).  Writing the residual of the projection of \(Y(z)\) on \(x\) as \(e_z\), linearity of projection and \(h=Y(1)+Y(0)\) give the exact identities
\[
 s_n^2=S_{e_1+e_0,e_1+e_0,n},\qquad
 u_n-s_n^2=S_{e_1-e_0,e_1-e_0,n}\ge0. \tag{11}
\]
The inequality is positivity of a finite-population variance.  Thus \(0<s^2\le u\).  Equation (11) is an upper envelope and does not identify the unobserved residual treatment-effect variance.

We next establish existence and computability of the critical value.  Put \(R=\|G\|\) and \(U=G/\|G\|\), assigning any value to \(U\) on the null event \(G=0\).  Spherical symmetry gives \(R\sim\chi_k\), \(U\) uniform on the unit sphere, and \(R\perp U\).  Therefore
\[
 (C_{k,a})_1=\min(R,\sqrt a)U_1. \tag{12}
\]
For \(v>0\), the distribution function of
\(X_{v,B}=|\sqrt v\,\varepsilon+B(C_{k,a})_1|\) is
\[
 F_{v,B}(t)=\mathbb E_{R,U}\!\left[
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{t-B\min(R,\sqrt a)U_1}{\sqrt v}\right)
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac{-t-B\min(R,\sqrt a)U_1}{\sqrt v}\right)
 \right]. \tag{13}
\]
For \(k\ge2\), \(U_1\) has density
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(k/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((k-1)/2)}
 (1-u^2)^{(k-3)/2}\mathbf 1_{\{-1<u<1\}},
\]
whereas for \(k=1\) it is uniform on \(\{-1,1\}\).  Combining these laws with the chi density of \(R\) turns (13) into the stated Gaussian--spherical integral.  At \(v=0\), the same formula is read as the indicator integral induced by (12).  It can be evaluated by numerical quadrature followed by scalar quantile search.

It remains to show that the outer supremum is attained.  Couple all \(X_{v,B}\) with the same \((\varepsilon,C_{k,a})\).  If \((v_j,B_j)\to(v,B)\), then \(X_{v_j,B_j}\to X_{v,B}\) almost surely.  When \(v>0\), Gaussian convolution gives a continuous positive density.  When \(v=0<B\), (12) puts positive probability in every nonempty interval in the interior of its support; for \(k=1\) endpoint atoms may occur, but there is no gap immediately below an endpoint.  Thus, if \(q=q_\alpha(v,B)\), then for every \(\delta>0\),
\[
 F_{v,B}((q-\delta)_+)<1-\alpha<F_{v,B}(q+\delta), \tag{14}
\]
with the left inequality omitted if \(q=0\).  Almost-sure convergence, bounded convergence at continuity points, and (14) show
\[
 q_\alpha(v_j,B_j)\longrightarrow q_\alpha(v,B). \tag{15}
\]
At \((v,B)=(0,0)\), this follows directly from
\[
 q_\alpha(v,B)\le B\sqrt a+\sqrt v\,q_\alpha(1,0).
\]
Hence \(q_\alpha\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)^2\).  Writing \(v=ut\), compactness of \([0,1]\) gives
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(u,B)=\max_{t\in[0,1]}q_\alpha(ut,B), \tag{16}
\]
and the maximum exists.  The same compactness argument, applied to maximizing subsequences, proves continuity of \(q_{*,\alpha}\) in \((u,B)\).  Equations (12)--(16) also give the advertised finite-dimensional numerical procedure: Gaussian--spherical quadrature, one scalar quantile search, and maximization over the compact interval \([0,u]\).

By stopped-moment consistency and (10),
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(\widehat u_n,\|\widehat b_n\|)
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}q_{*,\alpha}(u,\|b\|). \tag{17}
\]
Rotational invariance gives
\(b^{\mathsf T}C_{k,a}\overset d=\|b\|(C_{k,a})_1\).  By \(s^2\le u\) and the defining maximum,
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(u,\|b\|)\ge q_\alpha(s^2,\|b\|). \tag{18}
\]
The ATE limit theorem and \(s>0\) imply that the limiting absolute error has a continuous distribution.  Slutsky's theorem, (17), and (18) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr(\tau_n\in I_n)
 &=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left\{
 |\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)|
 \le q_{*,\alpha}(\widehat u_n,\|\widehat b_n\|)\right\}\\
 &\ge \Pr\!\left\{|s\varepsilon+\|b\|(C_{k,a})_1|
 \le q_\alpha(s^2,\|b\|)\right\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{19}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the sequencewise design-based guarantee.

For the required smallest-instance stress test, take \(n=4\), \(k=1\), \(a=1\),
\[
 x=(-3,-1,1,3),\qquad Y(0)=(0,0,0,0),\qquad
 Y(1)=(1,1,1,-3).
\]
Here \(S_{xx}=20/3\).  The six treated-pair covariate sums are \(-4,-2,0,0,2,4\), so exactly the four with sums \(-2,0,0,2\) are acceptable.  From either unacceptable allocation, all four treated--control transpositions are improving and reach an acceptable allocation.  Uniform initialization therefore assigns each terminal acceptable allocation probability
\[
 \frac16+2\left(\frac16\right)\left(\frac14\right)=\frac14,
\]
independently of finite \(\gamma\).  The finite-population ATE is \(\tau=0\).  For each of the two acceptable allocations selecting two units having treated potential outcomes \((1,1)\), both observed-arm intercept-plus-slope residual fits are exact; hence \(\widehat u=0\), \(\widehat b=0\), and \(I_n=\{1\}\), which misses zero.  Their total probability is \(1/2\).  Thus at, for example, \(\alpha=0.05\), finite-sample coverage is at most \(1/2\).  This exact enumeration delimits (19): the theorem is asymptotic and makes no finite-sample exactness claim.

%%% FIELD lin-proof
Let \(\widehat\beta_{z,n}\) denote the within-arm OLS slope.  Centering of the finite-population covariates and equal allocation imply \(\bar x_{1,n}+\bar x_{0,n}=0\).  The treatment coefficient in the fully interacted regression is the difference of its two fitted arm intercepts.  Whenever both arm Gram matrices are nonsingular,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}
 =\widehat\tau_n-\frac12(\widehat\beta_{1,n}+\widehat\beta_{0,n})^{\mathsf T}
 (\bar x_{1,n}-\bar x_{0,n}). \tag{20}
\]
The stopped-moment lemma gives \(\widehat\beta_{z,n}-\beta_{z,n}=o_{\Pr}(1)\), and its eigenvalue argument shows that singularity has probability tending to zero.  Thus the Moore--Penrose convention agrees with (20) with probability tending to one.

The score definition and finite-population centering give exactly
\[
 \bar x_{1,n}-\bar x_{0,n}
 =\frac2{\sqrt n}S_{xx,n}^{1/2}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),
 \qquad
 S_{xx,n}^{1/2}(\beta_{1,n}+\beta_{0,n})=b_n. \tag{21}
\]
The terminal rule gives \(\|D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\|\le\sqrt a\); convergence of \(S_{xx,n}\) bounds \(\|S_{xx,n}^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Hence the product of the \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) slope error with the scaled covariate difference in (21) is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\).  Combining (20)--(21) with the exact decomposition in \(\mathrm{lem{:}initial\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}clt}\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)
 &=b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
   +s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
   -b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})+o_{\Pr}(1)\\
 &=s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})+o_{\Pr}(1). \tag{22}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The radial-entrance theorem gives \(E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\Rightarrow\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), and the class moment conditions give \(s_n\to s>0\).  Slutsky's theorem proves
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,s^2). \tag{23}
\]

Let \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) be the standard-normal two-sided critical value.  The residual-Neyman interval has half-width \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat u_n/n}\).  Stopped-moment consistency gives \(\widehat u_n\to_{\Pr}u\), while (11) gives \(u\ge s^2\).  Since \(s>0\), (22)--(23) and continuity of the Gaussian law imply
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left\{
 |\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)|
 \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat u_n}\right\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{24}
\]
If \(u_n-s_n^2\to0\), then \(u=s^2\); the limiting studentized statistic is standard normal, so the limit in (24) equals \(1-\alpha\).

%%% FIELD orthogonal-proof
If \(b=0\), the ATE limit theorem immediately reduces to
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)\Rightarrow s\varepsilon
 \sim\mathcal N(0,s^2). \tag{25}
\]
When \(B_0=0\), the clipped-Gaussian summand in the critical-value definition vanishes.  Hence, for every \(v\ge0\),
\[
 q_\alpha(v,0)=\sqrt v\,c_\alpha,
 \qquad
 c_\alpha=\inf\{t\ge0:\Pr(|\varepsilon|\le t)\ge1-\alpha\}, \tag{26}
\]
including \(v=0\), where both sides vanish.  The right-hand side is nondecreasing in \(v\), and therefore
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(u,0)
 =\max_{0\le v\le u}q_\alpha(v,0)
 =\sqrt u\,c_\alpha. \tag{27}
\]
Substitution into the variance-envelope interval gives the standard conservative Neyman Gaussian half-width \(c_\alpha\sqrt{u/n}\), with \(u\) replaced by its stopped-design consistent estimator \(\widehat u_n\) in the observable interval.  The reduction is therefore obtained from the stopped-PSRR law and does not assume complete randomization or uniform sampling from the acceptable set.

%%% FIELD radial-proof
The two-class functional theorem yields joint convergence of the absorbed path and its finite stopping time.  Since the limiting path is continuous and absorbed after \(H\), the evaluation map \((z,t)\mapsto z(t)\) is continuous at the limit almost surely.  Thus the continuous mapping theorem applies to the terminal score.

On \(\{\|G\|^2<a\}\), \(H=0\) and \(D_H=G\).  The chi-square law is continuous, so \(\Pr(\|G\|^2=a)=0\).  On \(\{\|G\|^2>a\}\), continuity of \(D\), the definition of first entry, and \(H<\infty\) imply
\[
 \|D_H\|=\sqrt a. \tag{28}
\]
For every orthogonal \(k\times k\) matrix \(Q\), the exterior drift \(\mu(d)=-4d-8\gamma d/\|d\|^2\) and covariance obey
\[
 \mu(Qd)=Q\mu(d),\qquad Q(8I_k)Q^{\mathsf T}=8I_k. \tag{29}
\]
The Gaussian initial law and the stopping ball are also invariant under \(Q\).  Uniqueness in law of the stopped diffusion, supplied by the two-class functional theorem, therefore gives
\[
 QD_H\overset d=D_H. \tag{30}
\]
The unique rotation-invariant probability measure on the sphere of radius \(\sqrt a\) is normalized surface measure.  Hence the exterior branch in (28) is uniform in direction and has mass \(\Pr(\|G\|^2>a)\).  The interior branch retains the original Gaussian law.  A spherical Gaussian has uniform direction independent of its radius, so these two branches together give
\[
 D_H\overset d=G\min\{1,\sqrt a/\|G\|\}=C_{k,a}. \tag{31}
\]
The terminal evaluation described above now yields, on the outcome-free covariate-design class,
\[
 D_n(W^x_{n,T_n^x})\Rightarrow C_{k,a}. \tag{32}
\]
The endpoint argument uses only rotational equivariance; although \(\gamma\) changes the radial drift and the hitting-time transform, every fixed finite \(\gamma\) has the same endpoint law.

On the outcome-inference subclass, the residual coordinate has the explicit stationary Ornstein--Uhlenbeck representation
\[
 E_t=e^{-4t}\varepsilon+\sqrt8\int_0^t e^{-4(t-u)}\,dB'_u. \tag{33}
\]
At every deterministic \(t\), its variance is
\[
 e^{-8t}+8\int_0^t e^{-8(t-u)}\,du=1, \tag{34}
\]
and its mean is zero.  Thus \(E_t\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).  The entire residual process is independent of the covariate process, while \(H\) is measurable with respect to the latter.  Conditioning on the covariate path, for bounded measurable \(f\) and bounded covariate-path functional \(Q_0\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{f(E_H)Q_0(D)\}
 =\mathbb E\{f(\varepsilon)\}\mathbb E\{Q_0(D)\}. \tag{35}
\]
Therefore \(E_H\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and \(E_H\perp D_H\).  Terminal evaluation of the joint functional convergence gives
\[
 \bigl(D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\bigr)
 \Rightarrow(C_{k,a},\varepsilon),
 \qquad C_{k,a}\perp\varepsilon. \tag{36}
\]
This independence is derived from the block-diagonal limiting system and stationarity, not imposed on the finite-population outcomes.

Finally, (31) gives
\[
 \|C_{k,a}\|^2=\min\{\|G\|^2,a\}=\min\{\chi_k^2,a\}, \tag{37}
\]
so, because \(k\ge1\) and \(0<a<\infty\),
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0. \tag{38}
\]
The hitting-time transform in the two-class theorem exhibits the tuning dependence of runtime.  The distinct comparator law is proved in \(\mathrm{prop{:}not\mbox{-}uniform\mbox{-}rerandomization\mbox{-}design\mbox{-}class}\).

%%% FIELD nonuniform-proof
The clipped-Gaussian definition and continuity of the chi-square distribution imply
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}
 =\Pr\{\|G\|^2\ge a\}
 =1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0. \tag{39}
\]
By contrast, \(L_{k,a}\overset d=G\mid\{\|G\|^2\le a\}\).  Its conditional squared radius is a continuous chi-square law restricted to \([0,a]\), and hence
\[
 \Pr\{\|L_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=0. \tag{40}
\]
The measurable sphere \(\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\) therefore separates the two probability measures.

For completeness, uniformity of the terminal allocation on \(\mathcal A_n(a)\) would indeed have the comparator limit.  The complete-randomization vector CLT gives \(D_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow G\).  Since \(\Pr(\|G\|^2=a)=0\) and \(\Pr(\|G\|^2\le a)>0\), bounded-continuous test functions and the Portmanteau theorem give
\[
 \mathcal L\bigl(D_n(W_{n,0})\mid M_n(W_{n,0})\le a\bigr)
 \Rightarrow \mathcal L\bigl(G\mid\|G\|^2\le a\bigr)
 =\mathcal L(L_{k,a}). \tag{41}
\]
Every design in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\) uses equal arms, uniform balanced initialization, treated--control transposition proposals, the original Metropolis acceptance rule with fixed finite tuning, and immediate stopping at first entry into \(M_n\le a\).  The boundedness, fixed-dimension, moment, and nondegeneracy requirements restrict only the deterministic covariate arrays, so \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\) is a subclass of the broader domain of the published original algorithm.  A universal assertion that the original terminal allocation is uniform on its acceptable set would, by (41), force the limiting law \(L_{k,a}\) on every sequence in this subclass, contradicting (39)--(40) and the clipped-Gaussian limit.  Thus the subclass is a valid negative certificate for the broader universal representation, while the positive diffusion conclusion remains restricted to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\).

%%% FIELD stopped-moment-statement
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), every treated- and control-arm empirical mean entering \(\mathrm{def{:}observable\mbox{-}envelope\mbox{-}inputs}\) differs from its finite-population counterpart by \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) at \(T_n\). Consequently, \(\widehat b_n-b_n\to_{\Pr}0\) and \(\widehat u_n-u_n\to_{\Pr}0\).

%%% FIELD nonuniform-statement
For every finite \(a>0\), over the outcome-free covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), the original first-entry PSRR covariate limit \(C_{k,a}\) is not equal in distribution to the uniform-rerandomization limit \(L_{k,a}\). Specifically,
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0,
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\|L_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=0.
\]
The class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) is a regular equal-allocation subclass of the arrays admitted by the original Zhu--Liu first-entry algorithm. Therefore this subclass certificate also refutes, on the broader published class, any universal representation of the original algorithm's output as a uniform draw from the acceptable set. The positive diffusion assertion itself remains restricted to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\).
