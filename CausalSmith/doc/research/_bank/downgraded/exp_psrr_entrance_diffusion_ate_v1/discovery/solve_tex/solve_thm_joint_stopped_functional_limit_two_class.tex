%%% FIELD theorem-statement
The following two conclusions hold.

First, for every sequence in the covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), use \(N_x\), \((W^x_{n,m})\), and \(T_n^x\) from \(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\), and set
\[
 \bar D_n^x(t)=D_n\!\left(W^x_{n,\min\{\lfloor nt\rfloor,T_n^x\}}\right),
 \qquad
 A_n^x=\sum_{m=0}^{T_n^x-1}\mathbf 1\{W^x_{n,m+1}\ne W^x_{n,m}\}.
\]
Along even \(n\ge N_x\), jointly in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^k)\times[0,\infty)\),
\[
 (\bar D_n^x,T_n^x/n)\Rightarrow(D_{\cdot\wedge H},H),
 \qquad T_n^x-A_n^x=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n),
 \qquad \frac{T_n^x-A_n^x}{n}\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{14}
\]
Here, before \(H=\inf\{t\ge0:\|D_t\|^2\le a\}\),
\[
 dD_t=\left(-4D_t-\frac{8\gamma D_t}{\|D_t\|^2}\right)dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t,
 \qquad D_0=G\sim\mathcal N_k(0,I_k), \tag{15}
\]
and \(H<\infty\) almost surely. If \(R_0=\|D_0\|^2=r\), \(b=k/2-\gamma\), and \(\lambda>0\), then the proposal-time first-entry law has the computable transform
\[
 \mathbb E_r(e^{-\lambda H})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le a,\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\frac{U(\lambda/8,b,r/2)}{U(\lambda/8,b,a/2)},&r>a,
 \end{cases} \tag{16}
\]
where, for \(A>0\) and \(x>0\),
\[
 U(A,b,x)=\frac1{\Gamma(A)}\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t^{A-1}(1+t)^{b-A-1}\,dt. \tag{17}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\), equation (16) is understood as one. Under the Gaussian initialization, with \(F_{\chi_k^2}\) the chi-square distribution function,
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{-\lambda H})=F_{\chi_k^2}(a)+
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_a^\infty U(\lambda/8,b,r/2)r^{k/2-1}e^{-r/2}\,dr}
 {\displaystyle 2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2)U(\lambda/8,b,a/2)}. \tag{18}
\]
For every \(r>a\) and \(\lambda>0\), the transform in equation (16) is strictly increasing in \(\gamma\); thus finite tuning changes the proposal-time search law. Equation (14) and the rejection estimate show that the published accepted-switch clock and the all-proposal clock differ by \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), and therefore by \(o_{\Pr}(n)\).

Second, for every sequence in the outcome-inference subclass \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\), along the retained even outcome tail \(n\ge N_0\), jointly in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^{k+1})\times[0,\infty)\),
\[
 \bigl(\bar Z_n,T_n/n\bigr)\Rightarrow
 \left(\bigl((D_{t\wedge H}^{\mathsf T},E_{t\wedge H})^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{t\ge0},H\right), \tag{19}
\]
where equation (15) holds and \(E\) is the independent stationary solution of \(dE_t=-4E_tdt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t\), \(E_0\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\). This is the unique weak solution of the stopped system in \(\mathrm{def{:}limit\mbox{-}system}\). No projected Markov property is assumed in either clause.

%%% FIELD theorem-proof
We prove the covariate and joint clauses, including the strengthened proposal-versus-accepted clock rate.

Fix a sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\). Use the smooth extension supplied by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\), and write
\(X_n(t)=D_n(W^x_{n,\lfloor nt\rfloor})\) for its proposal-clock interpolation before absorption. By that lemma, the extension agrees with original PSRR through \(T_n^x\), and, on every compact set, its limiting drift and covariance are
\[
 \beta_\phi(d)=-4d-8\gamma\phi'(\|d\|^2)d,
 \qquad c_\phi=8I_k. \tag{28}
\]
The clock conversion in the same lemma puts these characteristics on the original treated--control proposal scale.

We first establish compact containment. Stop at the first exit \(\sigma_{n,R}\) from the radius-\(R\) ball. Summing the compactly stopped second-moment drift inequality in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\) through
\(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge\sigma_{n,R}\), using the complete-randomization covariance identity
\(\mathbb E\|X_n(0)\|^2=k\), and using the uniform boundedness and covariance nondegeneracy conditions to bound every score jump by \(C_0n^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\sup_{t\le L}\|X_n(t)\|>R\right\}
 \le \frac{k+CL+o(1)}{(R-C_0n^{-1/2})^2}. \tag{29}
\]
Thus the left side is uniformly small after first taking \(n\to\infty\) and then \(R\to\infty\).

On a fixed ball the predictable drift and quadratic-variation rates are bounded by the localized characteristic lemma. If \(\sigma_n\le L\) is a stopping time and \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the predictable finite-variation increment over \([\sigma_n,\sigma_n+\delta_n]\) is \(O(\delta_n+n^{-1})\), while conditional Chebyshev applied to the martingale increment gives, for each \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{\|M_n(\sigma_n+\delta_n)-M_n(\sigma_n)\|>\eta\}
 \le C\eta^{-2}(\delta_n+n^{-1})+o(1). \tag{30}
\]
Equations (29)--(30), localization, and the vanishing maximal jump yield tightness in
\(D([0,L],\mathbb R^k)\); every subsequential limit is continuous.

For \(f\in C_c^3(\mathbb R^k)\), Taylor's theorem and the \(O(n^{-1/2})\) jump bound give, before compact exit,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\{f(X_{n,(m+1)/n})-f(X_{n,m/n})\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\}
 &=\nabla f(X_{n,m/n})^{\mathsf T}
   \mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})\\
 &\quad+\frac12\nabla^2f(X_{n,m/n}):
 \mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\Delta D^{x\mathsf T}_{n,m}
 \mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})+O(n^{-3/2}). \tag{31}
 \end{split}
\]
The trajectory-uniform characteristic bounds make the sum through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\) of the difference between equation (31) and
\(n^{-1}\mathcal L_\phi f(X_{n,m/n})\) converge to zero in probability, where
\[
 \mathcal L_\phi f(d)
 =\beta_\phi(d)^{\mathsf T}\nabla f(d)
  +4\operatorname{tr}\{\nabla^2f(d)\}. \tag{32}
\]
Compact stopping bounds the errors, so truncation upgrades this convergence to \(L^1\). Passing the conditional martingale identity to a subsequential limit shows that, for \(0\le s<t\) and every bounded continuous path functional \(Q\) measurable through time \(s\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 Q\left\{f(X_t)-f(X_s)-\int_s^t\mathcal L_\phi f(X_u)\,du\right\}
 \right]=0. \tag{33}
\]
Thus every limit solves the martingale problem for equation (32).

Because \(\phi\) is constant near zero, smooth on the transition annulus, and equal to the logarithm away from zero, the map
\(d\mapsto\phi'(\|d\|^2)d\) is globally Lipschitz. Picard iteration constructs a strong solution with coefficients in equation (28). If two such solutions use the same Brownian motion, the Lipschitz bound and Gronwall's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E\sup_{u\le t}\|X_u-X'_u\|^2
 \le C\int_0^t\mathbb E\sup_{v\le u}\|X_v-X'_v\|^2\,du=0. \tag{34}
\]
Consequently the martingale problem has a unique law. The initial-score central limit theorem identifies \(X_0=G\), so tightness and uniqueness identify convergence of the whole extended process.

Before first entry, \(\phi'(\|D_t\|^2)=1/\|D_t\|^2\). It\^o's formula applied to \(R_t=\|D_t\|^2\) gives
\[
 dR_t=8(k-2\gamma-R_t)\,dt+\sqrt{32R_t}\,d\beta_t,
 \qquad R_t>a, \tag{35}
\]
with generator
\[
 \mathcal L_Rg(r)=16r g''(r)+8(k-2\gamma-r)g'(r). \tag{36}
\]
A scale derivative is \(s'(r)=e^{r/2}r^{\gamma-k/2}\), as direct differentiation gives \(\mathcal L_Rs=0\). Let
\(H_a=\inf\{t:R_t\le a\}\) and \(H_M=\inf\{t:R_t\ge M\}\). Optional stopping of the bounded martingale
\(s(R_{t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M})\) gives, for \(a<r<M\),
\[
 \Pr_r(H_M<H_a)=\frac{s(r)-s(a)}{s(M)-s(a)}. \tag{37}
\]
The denominator tends to infinity as \(M\to\infty\). On every compact interval \([a,M]\), the diffusion coefficient in equation (35) is bounded away from zero. Solving the linear boundary-value equation
\(\mathcal L_Rg=-1\), with \(g(a)=g(M)=0\), and applying It\^o's formula shows that the exit time from \((a,M)\) has finite expectation. Letting \(M\to\infty\) in equation (37) proves \(H_a<\infty\) almost surely from every \(r>a\); for \(r\le a\), \(H=0\).

The Gaussian initialization has no mass on \(R_0=a\). At a hit from outside, the martingale coefficient in equation (35) equals \(\sqrt{32a}>0\). In a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(a\), its quadratic variation is strictly increasing and its drift is bounded. A Brownian time change and the reflection principle therefore show that the path cannot remain on the exterior side for a positive interval after hitting \(a\). Hence it immediately crosses into \((0,a)\) almost surely. Initial strict-interior paths have hitting time zero stably, whereas exterior paths have a finite boundary hit followed immediately by a strict-interior crossing. The closed-ball first-hit functional is consequently continuous at almost every limiting path. Combining the continuous mapping theorem on \([0,L]\), the vanishing terminal jump from the characteristic lemma, and then \(L\to\infty\), yields
\[
 (\bar D_n^x,T_n^x/n)\Rightarrow(D_{\cdot\wedge H},H). \tag{38}
\]

We now sharpen the clock comparison. Let
\[
 R_n(L)=\sum_{m=0}^{(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x)-1}
 \mathbf 1\{W^x_{n,m+1}=W^x_{n,m}\}
\]
be the number of rejected proposals through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\). For each fixed \(L<\infty\),
\(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\) gives
\(R_n(L)=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\). Convergence in equation (38) implies tightness of \(T_n^x/n\). Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose \(L<\infty\) so that
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr(T_n^x>nL)<\epsilon/2. \tag{39}
\]
For this fixed \(L\), choose \(C_\epsilon<\infty\) and \(n_\epsilon\) so that
\(\Pr\{R_n(L)>C_\epsilon\sqrt n\}<\epsilon/2\) for all \(n\ge n_\epsilon\). Since \(T_n^x\) is integer, on \(\{T_n^x\le nL\}\) all proposals through absorption occur by \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\), and therefore
\[
 T_n^x-A_n^x=R_n(L).
\]
It follows that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \Pr\{T_n^x-A_n^x>C_\epsilon\sqrt n\}
 \le \Pr(T_n^x>nL)+\Pr\{R_n(L)>C_\epsilon\sqrt n\}<\epsilon+o(1). \tag{40}
\]
This is exactly
\[
 T_n^x-A_n^x=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n), \tag{41}
\]
and in particular \((T_n^x-A_n^x)/n\to_{\Pr}0\). Thus the sharper global rate follows from the fixed-horizon rejection estimate and hitting-time tightness; no limiting constant, rejection-count distribution, or population empirical-law limit is asserted.

We next derive the proposal-time entrance transform. Put
\[
 A=\lambda/8>0,\qquad b=k/2-\gamma,\qquad
 U(A,b,x)=\frac1{\Gamma(A)}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t^{A-1}(1+t)^{b-A-1}\,dt. \tag{42}
\]
The integral is finite and positive. Differentiation under the integral is justified by integrability at zero and the exponential tail; one integration by parts gives Kummer's equation
\[
 xU_{xx}+(b-x)U_x-AU=0. \tag{43}
\]
Define \(u_\lambda(r)=U(A,b,r/2)/U(A,b,a/2)\). Substitution of equation (43) into equation (36) gives
\[
 \mathcal L_Ru_\lambda=\lambda u_\lambda,\qquad u_\lambda(a)=1. \tag{44}
\]
Changing variables \(z=xt\) in equation (42), followed by dominated convergence, gives
\(U(A,b,x)\sim x^{-A}\) as \(x\to\infty\), hence \(u_\lambda(r)\to0\). It\^o's formula makes
\[
 e^{-\lambda(t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M)}
 u_\lambda(R_{t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M})
\]
a bounded martingale. Let first \(t\to\infty\) and then \(M\to\infty\). Equation (37), almost-sure finiteness of \(H_a\), and \(u_\lambda(M)\to0\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_r(e^{-\lambda H})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le a,\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle
 \frac{U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,r/2)}
 {U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,a/2)},&r>a.
 \end{cases} \tag{45}
\]
Integrating equation (45) against the \(\chi_k^2\) density and separating \(\{R_0\le a\}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{-\lambda H})
 =F_{\chi_k^2}(a)+
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_a^\infty
 U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,r/2)r^{k/2-1}e^{-r/2}\,dr}
 {\displaystyle2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2)
 U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,a/2)}. \tag{46}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\), the transform is one.

The tuning dependence is strict. Let \(\gamma_2>\gamma_1\) and let \(u_j\) be equation (45) for \(\gamma_j\). The integral representation gives \(u'_2(r)<0\). Consequently
\[
 (\mathcal L_{R,\gamma_1}-\lambda)u_2
 =16(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)u'_2<0. \tag{47}
\]
The one-dimensional maximum principle on \([a,M]\), followed by \(M\to\infty\), uses equality at \(a\) and convergence to zero at infinity to give
\(u_2(r)>u_1(r)\) for every \(r>a\). Hence the conditional transform in equation (45), and therefore its nondegenerate Gaussian-initialization mixture in equation (46), is strictly increasing in finite \(\gamma\).

Finally, fix a sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\). The initial-score lemma gives
\[
 Z_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow\mathcal N_{k+1}(0,I_{k+1}). \tag{48}
\]
The actual-filtration characteristic theorem gives, on the retained outcome tail, limiting drift and covariance
\[
 \beta_\phi(d,e)=
 \bigl(-4d-8\gamma\phi'(\|d\|^2)d,-4e\bigr),
 \qquad c_\phi=8I_{k+1}, \tag{49}
\]
with vanishing conditional Lindeberg terms and the same terminal-crossing control. The compact-containment, tightness, Taylor-expansion, and martingale-problem arguments in equations (29)--(34), now applied in dimension \(k+1\), identify the unique solution
\[
 \begin{split}
 dD_t&=\{-4D_t-8\gamma\phi'(\|D_t\|^2)D_t\}\,dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t,\\
 dE_t&=-4E_t\,dt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t.
 \end{split} \tag{50}
\]
The identity covariance in equation (49) and the independent Gaussian coordinates in equation (48) imply, by uniqueness of this product martingale problem, that \(B,B'\) and \(G,\varepsilon\) may be taken mutually independent. Thus independence is a conclusion of the limiting characteristics and Gaussian initialization, not a finite-population outcome--residual independence assumption. Since the hitting and stopping functional depends only on \(D\), the transfer used for equation (38) yields
\[
 (\bar Z_n,T_n/n)\Rightarrow
 \left(\bigl((D_{t\wedge H}^{\mathsf T},E_{t\wedge H})^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{t\ge0},H\right). \tag{51}
\]
Before \(H\), equation (50) is the system in \(\mathrm{def{:}limit\mbox{-}system}\), and absorption at \(H\) proves the joint clause and its uniqueness. At no point was a projected Markov property assumed.
