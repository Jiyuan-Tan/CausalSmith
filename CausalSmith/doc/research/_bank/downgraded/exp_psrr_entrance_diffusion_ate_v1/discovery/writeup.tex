\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}psrr\_\allowbreak{}entrance\_\allowbreak{}diffusion\_\allowbreak{}ate}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Original equal-allocation first-entry PSRR has an outcome-free allocation limit on a regular bounded covariate-design class: the proposal-time chain converges to an exterior radial diffusion, its first-entry time has an explicit Tricomi-\(U\) Laplace transform, and its all-proposal count exceeds its accepted-switch count by only \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\). The terminal imbalance is a radially clipped Gaussian, independent of a stationary Gaussian outcome-residual coordinate on the smaller outcome-inference subclass. Fixed finite tuning changes the search-time law but not the first-order endpoint law. On the outcome subclass, stopped-arm moments consistently estimate the inputs to a computable variance-envelope interval for the finite-population ATE, with the stated fully interacted corollary.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Zhu and Liu leave asymptotic distributions of common statistics under original first-entry PSRR open. Uniform rerandomization instead conditions a Gaussian inside the balance region, while Lu, Zhang, and Zhu analyze a stationary chain and add a correction; neither supplies the proposal-time first-entry law, its runtime transform, or weak-null ATE inference for the original output.

\paragraph{Niche.} The paper isolates two nested scopes: an outcome-free regular covariate-design class for the allocation path, runtime, clipped endpoint, and boundary-atom converse, and an outcome-inference subclass for the residual process, stopped moments, and finite-population causal intervals.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper derives actual-filtration predictable characteristics by a uniform-shadow coupling and antisymmetric-score cancellation, proves compact containment and martingale-problem convergence, transfers the first hit, and globalizes the fixed-horizon rejection bound to show that the all-proposal clock exceeds the accepted-switch clock by \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\). It solves the radial hitting ODE in Tricomi-\(U\) form and then obtains the independent residual limit and downstream variance-envelope inference without assuming projected Markovianity, stabilization, or finite-population outcome--residual independence.

\section{Related work}

Zhu and Liu define the original equal-allocation, immediate-stop PSRR algorithm and leave asymptotic distributions of commonly used statistics open; the two-class functional theorem closes that gap on a bounded regular covariate subclass and shows more sharply that their accepted-switch state index differs from the present all-proposal count by \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), hence by \(o_{\Pr}(n)\). Lu, Zhang, and Zhu derive a negative-power stationary law and restore uniformity through an additional correction, whereas the present result keeps the original first-entry output and obtains a tuning-indexed Tricomi-\(U\) runtime transform. Li, Ding, and Rubin derive the Gaussian-plus-truncated-Gaussian law for uniform Mahalanobis rerandomization; the outcome-free boundary atom here proves that original first-entry PSRR has a different terminal law, and this non-representation certificate transfers to the broader published algorithmic class by subclass inclusion even though the positive diffusion theorem remains on the regular subclass. Roberts, Gelman, and Gilks motivate the diffusion scaling, and Li and Ding supply the finite-population Gaussian initialization substrate. Li and Ding's regression-adjustment theory, Lin's fully interacted linearization, Zhao and Ding's cancellation result, Cohen and Fogarty's Gaussian push-forward, and Fiksel's Fisher-exact inversion remain distinct inference comparators to the outcome-subclass variance-envelope analysis.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:centered-covariates]\label{ass:centered-covariates}
For every even \(n\), \(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n x_{ni}=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Centered design covariates, \cite{LiDingRubin2018ReM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}
The arrays \(\{Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1):i\in\mathcal I_n\}\) are fixed under assignment, with one version of each treatment and no interference.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population SUTVA, \cite{ZhuLiu2023PSRR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-boundedness]\label{ass:uniform-boundedness}
There is a constant \(K_0<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that \(\max_{i\in\mathcal I_n}\{\|x_{ni}\|+|Y_{ni}(0)|+|Y_{ni}(1)|\}\le K_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded finite-population maximal-term condition, \cite{LiDing2017FPCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:second-moment-limit]\label{ass:second-moment-limit}
The finite-population covariance matrix of \(\bigl(x_{ni}^{\mathsf T},Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1)\bigr)^{\mathsf T}\) converges entrywise to a finite matrix.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population second-moment convergence, \cite{LiDing2017FPCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:covariate-nondegeneracy}
There is a constant \(c_x>0\) such that \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform positive definiteness of the covariate covariance, \cite{LiDingRubin2018ReM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:residual-nondegeneracy}
The every-row outcome-sum projection-residual variance satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}v_n\ge c_s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is the explicitly scoped design-relevance condition that keeps the stationary outcome-residual coordinate nondegenerate and makes its standardization meaningful. It is satisfied by bounded triangular arrays whose outcome-sum component retains variation orthogonal to the fixed-dimensional covariate span; degenerate residual sequences are outside this paper's declared scope and are not covered by the Li--Ding regression-adjustment citation.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:psrr-class]\label{def:psrr-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Covariate-\allowbreak{}design class and outcome-\allowbreak{}inference subclass}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) be the class of deterministic triangular arrays \((x_{ni})_{i=1}^n\), indexed by even \(n\), with fixed \(k\), such that \(n^{-1}\sum_i x_{ni}=0\), \(\sup_{n,i}\|x_{ni}\|\le K_0\), \(S_{xx,n}\) converges entrywise to a finite matrix, and \(\liminf_n\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x>0\).  The design attached to every member is equal allocation on \(\mathcal A_n\), uniform initialization, fixed \(a>0\), fixed finite \(\gamma\ge0\), and the original first-entry PSRR rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}original\mbox{-}psrr}\).  Let \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\) be the outcome-inference subclass consisting of arrays \((x_{ni},Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1))_{i=1}^n\) whose covariate projection belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), whose potential outcomes are fixed under assignment with no interference, for which \(\sup_{n,i}\{\|x_{ni}\|+|Y_{ni}(0)|+|Y_{ni}(1)|\}\le K_0\), the finite-population covariance matrix of \((x_{ni}^{\mathsf T},Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1))^{\mathsf T}\) converges entrywise to a finite matrix, and \(\liminf_n v_n\ge c_s>0\).  Thus \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) is the outcome-free covariate-design class, while \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\) is the subclass on which the joint residual and causal-inference conclusions are asserted.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}centered-\allowbreak{}covariates}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}covariate-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}potential-\allowbreak{}outcomes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}uniform-\allowbreak{}boundedness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}second-\allowbreak{}moment-\allowbreak{}limit}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:regular-tail]\label{def:regular-tail}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Deterministic regular tail}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(N_0=\min\{N\in2\mathbb N:\text{ for every }n\in2\mathbb N\text{ with }n\ge N,\ \lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x/2,\ v_n\ge c_s/2,\text{ and }\#\{w\in\mathcal A_n:\|n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(2w_i-1)S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}x_{ni}\|^2\le a\}\ge1\}\). Every inverse, whitened score, standardized score, algorithm, and asymptotic claim is indexed only by even \(n\ge N_0\); the finitely many rows \(n<N_0\) are discarded and have no asymptotic role.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}covariate-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}uniform-\allowbreak{}boundedness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}second-\allowbreak{}moment-\allowbreak{}limit}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:balanced-space]\label{def:balanced-space}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Equal-\allowbreak{}allocation assignment space}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal A_n=\{w\in\{0,1\}^n:\sum_{i=1}^n w_i=n/2\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:mahalanobis-score]\label{def:mahalanobis-score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Mahalanobis imbalance}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), \(M_n(w)=\|D_n(w)\|^2\), where \(D_n(w)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(2w_i-1)S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}x_{ni}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(w\in\mathcal A_n\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:acceptable-set]\label{def:acceptable-set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Acceptable allocation set and initialization probability}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), \(\mathcal A_n(a)=\{w\in\mathcal A_n:M_n(w)\le a\}\) and \(p_n=\#\mathcal A_n(a)/\#\mathcal A_n=\Pr\{M_n(W_{n,0})\le a\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:original-psrr]\label{def:original-psrr}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Original first-\allowbreak{}entry PSRR}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each even \(n\ge N_0\), for which \(\mathcal A_n(a)\neq\varnothing\), draw \(W_{n,0}\) uniformly from \(\mathcal A_n\). While \(M_n(W_{n,m})>a\), draw one treated index and one control index uniformly, propose their transposition \(W^*\), accept it with probability \(\min\{1,(M_n(W_{n,m})/M_n(W^*))^{\gamma}\}\) with every improving move and every move to \(M_n(W^*)=0\) accepted, count every proposal including rejections, and stop at \(T_n=\inf\{m\ge0:M_n(W_{n,m})\le a\}\). The chain is evaluated only through this first entry.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{covariates}; \texttt{threshold}; \texttt{tuning}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ate-estimator]\label{def:ate-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite-\allowbreak{}population ATE and terminal difference in means}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), \(\tau_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\{Y_{ni}(1)-Y_{ni}(0)\}\) and \(\widehat\tau_n=2n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\{W_{n,\mathrm{stop},i}Y_{ni}(1)-(1-W_{n,\mathrm{stop},i})Y_{ni}(0)\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:score-system]\label{def:score-system}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Whitened joint covariate-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}residual score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), let \(d_{ni}=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}x_{ni}\), \(b_n=S_{dh,n}=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}S_{xh,n}\), \(s_n=\sqrt{v_n}\), \(r_{ni}=\{h_{ni}-\bar h_n-b_n^{\mathsf T}d_{ni}\}/s_n\), and \(q_{ni}=(d_{ni}^{\mathsf T},r_{ni})^{\mathsf T}\). Define \(Z_n(w)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(2w_i-1)q_{ni}=(D_n(w)^{\mathsf T},E_n(w))^{\mathsf T}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:absorbed-path]\label{def:absorbed-path}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Proposal-\allowbreak{}time absorbed score path}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), \(\bar Z_n(t)=Z_n(W_{n,\min\{\lfloor nt\rfloor,T_n\}})\) for \(t\ge0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:characteristic-handle]\label{def:characteristic-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Actual-\allowbreak{}filtration characteristic handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Couple the all-pairs extension of original PSRR by common transpositions to a stationary uniform-allocation shadow; before first entry, use exact centering of the antisymmetric Metropolis-kink score against the deterministic population empirical measure to control drift and cross-characteristics uniformly along localized proposal-time paths. Analyze the single terminal crossing directly from its exact transposition increment and the vanishing jump size, without invoking the singular drift expansion at or after \(T_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}original-\allowbreak{}psrr}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}score-\allowbreak{}system}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:limit-system]\label{def:limit-system}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Candidate stopped diffusion system}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Before \(H=\inf\{t\ge0:\|D_t\|^2\le a\}\), \(dD_t=(-4D_t-8\gamma D_t/\|D_t\|^2)dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t\) and \(dE_t=-4E_tdt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t\), with \(D_0=G\), \(E_0=\varepsilon\), and \(G,\varepsilon,B,B'\) mutually independent; absorb both coordinates at \(H\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:clipped-gaussian]\label{def:clipped-gaussian}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Radially clipped Gaussian}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(C_{k,a}=G\min\{1,\sqrt a/\|G\|\}\), with the multiplier defined as \(1\) at \(G=0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:variance-envelope]\label{def:variance-envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Residual variance envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}\), let \(\beta_{z,n}=S_{xx,n}^{-1}S_{x,Y(z),n}\) and \(e_{z,ni}=Y_{ni}(z)-\bar Y_n(z)-\beta_{z,n}^{\mathsf T}x_{ni}\). Set \(u_n=2S_{e_1e_1,n}+2S_{e_0e_0,n}\), so algebraically \(u_n-s_n^2=S_{e_1-e_0,e_1-e_0,n}\ge0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:observable-envelope-inputs]\label{def:observable-envelope-inputs}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Stopped-\allowbreak{}design estimators of envelope inputs}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), within each observed arm compute the centered outcome--covariate covariance \(\widehat c_{z,n}\) and the intercept-including OLS residual mean square \(\widehat S_{e_z,n}^2\), using the Moore--Penrose fit on singular samples. Set \(\widehat b_n=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}(\widehat c_{1,n}+\widehat c_{0,n})\) and \(\widehat u_n=2\widehat S_{e_1,n}^2+2\widehat S_{e_0,n}^2\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:critical-value]\label{def:critical-value}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Computable variance-\allowbreak{}envelope critical value}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(v,B_0\ge0\), define \(q_{\alpha}(v,B_0)=\inf\{t\ge0:\Pr(|\sqrt v\,\varepsilon+B_0(C_{k,a})_1|\le t)\ge1-\alpha\}\) and \(q_{*,\alpha}(u,B_0)=\max_{0\le v\le u}q_{\alpha}(v,B_0)\). Compute it by Gaussian--spherical integration, scalar quantile search, and compact one-dimensional maximization.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ate-interval]\label{def:ate-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Variance-\allowbreak{}envelope ATE interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), \(I_n=[\widehat\tau_n-q_{*,\alpha}(\widehat u_n,\|\widehat b_n\|)/\sqrt n,\ \widehat\tau_n+q_{*,\alpha}(\widehat u_n,\|\widehat b_n\|)/\sqrt n]\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:lin-estimator]\label{def:lin-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Fully interacted terminal estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For even \(n\ge N_0\), \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}\) is the coefficient on treatment in the OLS regression of the observed outcome on an intercept, treatment, centered covariates, and all treatment--covariate interactions under \(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}\).
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:regular-tail-wellposedness]\label{lem:regular-tail-wellposedness}
The index \(N_0\) in def:regular-tail is finite. More precisely, on the eventual positive-definite covariance tail, the finite-population Gaussian initialization gives \(D_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow G\) and hence \(p_n=\Pr\{M_n(W_{n,0})\le a\}\to\Pr(\|G\|^2\le a)>0\). Therefore \(\mathcal A_n(a)\neq\varnothing\), \(S_{xx,n}\) is positive definite, and \(s_n=\sqrt{v_n}>0\) for every even \(n\ge N_0\). Because the balanced pair-swap graph is finite and connected and every required transition has positive probability for finite \(\gamma\), \(T_n<\infty\) almost surely on this retained tail; the discarded rows \(n<N_0\) have no asymptotic role.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the covariate nondegeneracy assumption, there is an even deterministic index \(N_x\) such that \(\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x/2\) for every even \(n\ge N_x\).  Likewise, residual nondegeneracy gives an even \(N_s\) such that \(v_n\ge c_s/2\) for every even \(n\ge N_s\).  On this tail \(S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}\) exists and has operator norm at most \(\sqrt{2/c_x}\), while \(s_n=\sqrt{v_n}\ge\sqrt{c_s/2}>0\).

Apply the complete-randomization vector central limit lemma to the first \(k\) coordinates of the score.  Its maximal-term condition follows from uniform boundedness and the preceding inverse bound:
\[
 \max_{i\le n}\frac{\|S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}x_{ni}\|^2}{n}
 \le \frac{2K_0^2}{c_x n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(D_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow G\).  Since the boundary of \(\{d:\|d\|^2\le a\}\) has \(G\)-probability zero,
\[
 p_n=\Pr\{\|D_n(W_{n,0})\|^2\le a\}
 \longrightarrow \Pr\{\|G\|^2\le a\}>0.
\]
Consequently \(p_n>0\), and hence \(\mathcal A_n(a)\ne\varnothing\), on a further deterministic even tail.  The three eventual properties in the definition of \(N_0\) therefore hold simultaneously, so the set over which its minimum is taken is nonempty and \(N_0<\infty\).

It remains to prove absorption rather than assume it.  The balanced pair-transposition graph is finite and connected: if \(w,w'\in\mathcal A_n\), successively interchange an index in \(\{i:w_i=1,w'_i=0\}\) with one in \(\{j:w_j=0,w'_j=1\}\).  At an exterior state \(M_n(w)>a>0\), every proposed neighboring transposition has strictly positive transition probability.  Indeed, an improving move is accepted, and a worsening move with positive finite score is accepted with probability \((M_n(w)/M_n(w^*))^\gamma>0\); a proposal with \(M_n(w^*)=0\) is accepted by definition.  Because the graph is finite and \(\mathcal A_n(a)\ne\varnothing\), from every exterior state there is a path of at most \(\#\mathcal A_n-1\) positive-probability transitions into \(\mathcal A_n(a)\).  Taking the minimum of the finitely many corresponding path probabilities gives \(\rho_n>0\).  By the Markov property, the probability of avoiding the target for \(r(\#\mathcal A_n-1)\) proposals is at most \((1-\rho_n)^r\), which tends to zero.  Hence \(T_n<\infty\) almost surely for each retained row.  This proves every assertion, and the finitely many discarded rows have no asymptotic role.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This derives, rather than assumes, that every retained experiment has a reachable first-entry target and valid score normalization. \textit{Gap.} Finite-population central limit theory supplies Gaussian initialization, but original PSRR asymptotics require the additional deterministic-tail consequence that the fixed-radius acceptable set is eventually nonempty. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma makes the stopping time, pre-entry characteristics, terminal allocation, and all subsequent causal limits well posed on one deterministic asymptotic tail.

\begin{lemma}[lem:initial-score-clt]\label{lem:initial-score-clt}
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), uniform equal allocation gives \(Z_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow\mathcal N_{k+1}(0,I_{k+1})\), and the score construction satisfies \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)=b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})+s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\) exactly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The score definition and covariate centering give
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n d_{ni}=0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n r_{ni}=0.
\]
The projection normal equations give \(S_{dr,n}=0\).  Moreover,
\[
 S_{dd,n}=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}S_{xx,n}S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}=I_k,
 \qquad S_{rr,n}=s_n^{-2}v_n=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac1{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n q_{ni}q_{ni}^{\mathsf T}=I_{k+1}. \tag{1}
\]
Uniform boundedness, the lower eigenvalue bound, and \(s_n\ge\sqrt{c_s/2}\) imply \(\max_i\|q_{ni}\|\le C\) on the retained tail for a deterministic \(C<\infty\).  In particular \(n^{-1}\max_i\|q_{ni}\|^2\to0\).  The complete-randomization vector central limit lemma applied to (1) now yields
\[
 Z_n(W_{n,0})=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(2W_{n,0,i}-1)q_{ni}
 \Rightarrow \mathcal N_{k+1}(0,I_{k+1}).
\]
For reference, the covariance is exactly the identity: balanced sampling gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(2W_i-1,2W_j-1)=-1/(n-1)\) for \(i\ne j\), and centering plus (1) gives \(\operatorname{Var}\{Z_n(W_{n,0})\}=I_{k+1}\).

For the causal identity, fixed potential outcomes and equal allocation imply, for every \(w\in\mathcal A_n\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_n(w)-\tau_n
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2w_i-1)\{Y_{ni}(1)+Y_{ni}(0)\}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2w_i-1)(h_{ni}-\bar h_n),
\]
where the last equality uses \(\sum_i(2w_i-1)=0\).  The score construction gives the exact projection identity
\[
 h_{ni}-\bar h_n=b_n^{\mathsf T}d_{ni}+s_nr_{ni}.
\]
Substitution, followed by taking \(w=W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}\), proves
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)
 =b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})+s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
\]
without an approximation or a superpopulation argument.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This identifies the complete-randomization initialization and the exact causal decomposition that the stopped process must transport. \textit{Gap.} Li and Ding give the finite-population vector CLT at a fixed complete-randomization draw, not along the Zhu--Liu first-entry path. \textit{Consumer.} The joint normalization supplies the initial law for the stopped martingale problem and the ATE scaling used by every later interval result.

\begin{theorem}[thm:ate-limit]\label{thm:ate-limit}
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), if \(b_n\to b\) and \(s_n\to s\), then \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)\Rightarrow b^{\mathsf T}C_{k,a}+s\varepsilon\), with \(C_{k,a}\perp\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}initial\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}clt}\), the following identity holds exactly on every retained row:
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)
 =b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
  +s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}). \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}radial\mbox{-}entrance\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}design\mbox{-}class}\),
\[
 \bigl(D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\bigr)
 \Rightarrow(C_{k,a},\varepsilon),
 \qquad C_{k,a}\perp\varepsilon. \tag{2}
\]
The hypotheses \(b_n\to b\) and \(s_n\to s\) are deterministic.  Apply the continuous mapping theorem to (2), together with deterministic Slutsky convergence, under the continuous map
\[
 (d,e,\widetilde b,\widetilde s)
 \longmapsto \widetilde b^{\mathsf T}d+\widetilde s e.
\]
Equation (1) then gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)
 \Rightarrow b^{\mathsf T}C_{k,a}+s\varepsilon. \tag{3}
\]
The independence asserted in the conclusion is exactly the independence in (2).  Throughout, \(\tau_n\) is the fixed finite-population causal contrast in \(\mathrm{def{:}ate\mbox{-}estimator}\); (3) is its randomization limit and does not redefine the estimand.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the requested common-statistic limit for the finite-population ATE under the actual terminal assignment. \textit{Gap.} Zhu and Liu prove unbiasedness and a variance-reduction lower bound but explicitly leave this asymptotic distribution to future work. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem turns the original PSRR design into an asymptotically analyzable ATE procedure under heterogeneous effects.

\begin{lemma}[lem:stopped-moment-consistency]\label{lem:stopped-moment-consistency}
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), every treated- and control-arm empirical mean entering def:observable-envelope-inputs differs from its finite-population counterpart by \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) at \(T_n\). Consequently, \(\widehat b_n-b_n\to_{\Pr}0\) and \(\widehat u_n-u_n\to_{\Pr}0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The joint functional-limit theorem gives \(T_n/n=O_{\Pr}(1)\).  Thus, for every \(\eta>0\), there is \(L<\infty\) such that
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr(T_n>nL)<\eta. \tag{4}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}actual\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}arm\mbox{-}uniformity}\) to the finite collection of deterministic arrays
\[
 1,\quad x_{nij},\quad x_{nij}x_{ni\ell},\quad
 Y_{ni}(z),\quad x_{nij}Y_{ni}(z),\quad Y_{ni}(z)^2,
 \qquad z\in\{0,1\},\quad 1\le j,\ell\le k. \tag{5}
\]
The uniform-boundedness assumption gives this collection a common finite envelope.  Its parameter set is finite, hence compact, and the Lipschitz requirement is vacuous.  On \(\{T_n\le nL\}\), that lemma evaluated at \(m=T_n\) says that every treated- and control-arm raw empirical moment in (5) differs from its finite-population counterpart by \(o_{\Pr}(1)\).  By (4), the complementary event has limiting upper probability at most \(\eta\).  Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the asserted convergence at the random stopping time.

Centered covariances are polynomial functions of the raw moments in (5).  Consequently, for \(z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \widehat S_{xx,z,n}-S_{xx,n}=o_{\Pr}(1),
 \qquad
 \widehat c_{z,n}-S_{x,Y(z),n}=o_{\Pr}(1). \tag{6}
\]
Changing a divisor \(n/2\) to the corresponding degrees-of-freedom divisor changes these bounded quantities by \(o(1)\).  On the regular tail, \(\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x/2\).  Weyl's inequality and (6) give
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\min_{z\in\{0,1\}}
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat S_{xx,z,n})\ge c_x/4\right\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{7}
\]
On the event in (7), inversion is Lipschitz on the set of symmetric matrices with least eigenvalue at least \(c_x/4\), and hence
\[
 \widehat S_{xx,z,n}^{-1}-S_{xx,n}^{-1}=o_{\Pr}(1).
\]
Therefore the Moore--Penrose convention on the complement of (7) has no first-order effect.

On the event in (7), the intercept-including OLS residual mean square is
\[
 \widehat S_{e_z,n}^2
 =\widehat S_{Y(z)Y(z),z,n}
  -\widehat c_{z,n}^{\mathsf T}
   \widehat S_{xx,z,n}^{-1}\widehat c_{z,n}.
\]
The raw-moment convergence, (6), and inverse continuity imply
\[
 \widehat S_{e_z,n}^2-S_{e_ze_z,n}=o_{\Pr}(1),
 \qquad z\in\{0,1\}. \tag{8}
\]
Moreover, \(\|S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt{2/c_x}\) on the retained tail.  The definitions and (6)--(8) now give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat b_n-b_n
 &=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}\bigl\{(\widehat c_{1,n}-S_{x,Y(1),n})
 +(\widehat c_{0,n}-S_{x,Y(0),n})\bigr\}
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0,\\
 \widehat u_n-u_n
 &=2(\widehat S_{e_1,n}^2-S_{e_1e_1,n})
  +2(\widehat S_{e_0,n}^2-S_{e_0e_0,n})
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves every asserted stopped-design moment conclusion using the actual pre-entry path; no uniform draw from the acceptable set is introduced.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Adaptive first entry selects the terminal arms through the whole covariate path, so fixed-draw laws of large numbers cannot simply be conditioned on acceptance. \textit{Gap.} Li and Ding establish armwise regression quantities under uniform rerandomization; the stopped nonuniform PSRR design has no published uniform path law. \textit{Consumer.} The result makes all projection and variance inputs in the proposed interval computable from the realized experiment.

\begin{theorem}[thm:variance-envelope-coverage]\label{thm:variance-envelope-coverage}
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), the maximum in \(q_{*,\alpha}(u,B_0)\) exists, its defining integral is computable, and the interval \(I_n\) obeys the sequencewise design-based guarantee \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr(\tau_n\in I_n)\ge1-\alpha\) for every sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an arbitrary sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\).  Every probability below is over assignment alone, because the potential outcomes are fixed.  The second-moment-limit and covariate-nondegeneracy conditions imply convergence of \(S_{xx,n}^{-1}\), the arm slopes, and all projection-residual second moments.  Hence
\[
 b_n\to b,\qquad s_n^2\to s^2,\qquad u_n\to u. \tag{10}
\]
Residual nondegeneracy gives \(s^2>0\).  Writing the residual of the projection of \(Y(z)\) on \(x\) as \(e_z\), linearity of projection and \(h=Y(1)+Y(0)\) give the exact identities
\[
 s_n^2=S_{e_1+e_0,e_1+e_0,n},\qquad
 u_n-s_n^2=S_{e_1-e_0,e_1-e_0,n}\ge0. \tag{11}
\]
The inequality is positivity of a finite-population variance.  Thus \(0<s^2\le u\).  Equation (11) is an upper envelope and does not identify the unobserved residual treatment-effect variance.

We next establish existence and computability of the critical value.  Put \(R=\|G\|\) and \(U=G/\|G\|\), assigning any value to \(U\) on the null event \(G=0\).  Spherical symmetry gives \(R\sim\chi_k\), \(U\) uniform on the unit sphere, and \(R\perp U\).  Therefore
\[
 (C_{k,a})_1=\min(R,\sqrt a)U_1. \tag{12}
\]
For \(v>0\), the distribution function of
\(X_{v,B}=|\sqrt v\,\varepsilon+B(C_{k,a})_1|\) is
\[
 F_{v,B}(t)=\mathbb E_{R,U}\!\left[
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{t-B\min(R,\sqrt a)U_1}{\sqrt v}\right)
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac{-t-B\min(R,\sqrt a)U_1}{\sqrt v}\right)
 \right]. \tag{13}
\]
For \(k\ge2\), \(U_1\) has density
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(k/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((k-1)/2)}
 (1-u^2)^{(k-3)/2}\mathbf 1_{\{-1<u<1\}},
\]
whereas for \(k=1\) it is uniform on \(\{-1,1\}\).  Combining these laws with the chi density of \(R\) turns (13) into the stated Gaussian--spherical integral.  At \(v=0\), the same formula is read as the indicator integral induced by (12).  It can be evaluated by numerical quadrature followed by scalar quantile search.

It remains to show that the outer supremum is attained.  Couple all \(X_{v,B}\) with the same \((\varepsilon,C_{k,a})\).  If \((v_j,B_j)\to(v,B)\), then \(X_{v_j,B_j}\to X_{v,B}\) almost surely.  When \(v>0\), Gaussian convolution gives a continuous positive density.  When \(v=0<B\), (12) puts positive probability in every nonempty interval in the interior of its support; for \(k=1\) endpoint atoms may occur, but there is no gap immediately below an endpoint.  Thus, if \(q=q_\alpha(v,B)\), then for every \(\delta>0\),
\[
 F_{v,B}((q-\delta)_+)<1-\alpha<F_{v,B}(q+\delta), \tag{14}
\]
with the left inequality omitted if \(q=0\).  Almost-sure convergence, bounded convergence at continuity points, and (14) show
\[
 q_\alpha(v_j,B_j)\longrightarrow q_\alpha(v,B). \tag{15}
\]
At \((v,B)=(0,0)\), this follows directly from
\[
 q_\alpha(v,B)\le B\sqrt a+\sqrt v\,q_\alpha(1,0).
\]
Hence \(q_\alpha\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)^2\).  Writing \(v=ut\), compactness of \([0,1]\) gives
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(u,B)=\max_{t\in[0,1]}q_\alpha(ut,B), \tag{16}
\]
and the maximum exists.  The same compactness argument, applied to maximizing subsequences, proves continuity of \(q_{*,\alpha}\) in \((u,B)\).  Equations (12)--(16) also give the advertised finite-dimensional numerical procedure: Gaussian--spherical quadrature, one scalar quantile search, and maximization over the compact interval \([0,u]\).

By stopped-moment consistency and (10),
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(\widehat u_n,\|\widehat b_n\|)
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}q_{*,\alpha}(u,\|b\|). \tag{17}
\]
Rotational invariance gives
\(b^{\mathsf T}C_{k,a}\overset d=\|b\|(C_{k,a})_1\).  By \(s^2\le u\) and the defining maximum,
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(u,\|b\|)\ge q_\alpha(s^2,\|b\|). \tag{18}
\]
The ATE limit theorem and \(s>0\) imply that the limiting absolute error has a continuous distribution.  Slutsky's theorem, (17), and (18) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr(\tau_n\in I_n)
 &=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left\{
 |\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)|
 \le q_{*,\alpha}(\widehat u_n,\|\widehat b_n\|)\right\}\\
 &\ge \Pr\!\left\{|s\varepsilon+\|b\|(C_{k,a})_1|
 \le q_\alpha(s^2,\|b\|)\right\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{19}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the sequencewise design-based guarantee.

For the required smallest-instance stress test, take \(n=4\), \(k=1\), \(a=1\),
\[
 x=(-3,-1,1,3),\qquad Y(0)=(0,0,0,0),\qquad
 Y(1)=(1,1,1,-3).
\]
Here \(S_{xx}=20/3\).  The six treated-pair covariate sums are \(-4,-2,0,0,2,4\), so exactly the four with sums \(-2,0,0,2\) are acceptable.  From either unacceptable allocation, all four treated--control transpositions are improving and reach an acceptable allocation.  Uniform initialization therefore assigns each terminal acceptable allocation probability
\[
 \frac16+2\left(\frac16\right)\left(\frac14\right)=\frac14,
\]
independently of finite \(\gamma\).  The finite-population ATE is \(\tau=0\).  For each of the two acceptable allocations selecting two units having treated potential outcomes \((1,1)\), both observed-arm intercept-plus-slope residual fits are exact; hence \(\widehat u=0\), \(\widehat b=0\), and \(I_n=\{1\}\), which misses zero.  Their total probability is \(1/2\).  Thus at, for example, \(\alpha=0.05\), finite-sample coverage is at most \(1/2\).  This exact enumeration delimits (19): the theorem is asymptotic and makes no finite-sample exactness claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Heterogeneous effects leave the cross-potential-outcome residual covariance unidentified, and the envelope converts that obstacle into an observable conservative reference law. \textit{Gap.} Fiksel's covariate-adjusted interval is a Fisher-exact randomization-test inversion under an observed-data sufficient condition, while Li and Ding's conservative law and Cohen and Fogarty's Gaussian push-forward concern uniform constrained designs; none supplies the clipped first-entry PSRR weak-null envelope. \textit{Consumer.} Investigators using the original PSRR workflow obtain a single-run, observed-moment ATE confidence interval under heterogeneous effects without Fisher sharp-null inversion or regenerating assignments over hypothesized effects.

\begin{proposition}[prop:fully-interacted-corollary]\label{prop:fully-interacted-corollary}
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), the fully interacted estimator satisfies \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,s^2)\). Its residual-Neyman variance based on \(\widehat u_n\) yields an asymptotically conservative Wald interval; coverage is asymptotically exact when \(u_n-s_n^2\to0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\widehat\beta_{z,n}\) denote the within-arm OLS slope.  Centering of the finite-population covariates and equal allocation imply \(\bar x_{1,n}+\bar x_{0,n}=0\).  The treatment coefficient in the fully interacted regression is the difference of its two fitted arm intercepts.  Whenever both arm Gram matrices are nonsingular,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}
 =\widehat\tau_n-\frac12(\widehat\beta_{1,n}+\widehat\beta_{0,n})^{\mathsf T}
 (\bar x_{1,n}-\bar x_{0,n}). \tag{20}
\]
The stopped-moment lemma gives \(\widehat\beta_{z,n}-\beta_{z,n}=o_{\Pr}(1)\), and its eigenvalue argument shows that singularity has probability tending to zero.  Thus the Moore--Penrose convention agrees with (20) with probability tending to one.

The score definition and finite-population centering give exactly
\[
 \bar x_{1,n}-\bar x_{0,n}
 =\frac2{\sqrt n}S_{xx,n}^{1/2}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),
 \qquad
 S_{xx,n}^{1/2}(\beta_{1,n}+\beta_{0,n})=b_n. \tag{21}
\]
The terminal rule gives \(\|D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\|\le\sqrt a\); convergence of \(S_{xx,n}\) bounds \(\|S_{xx,n}^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Hence the product of the \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) slope error with the scaled covariate difference in (21) is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\).  Combining (20)--(21) with the exact decomposition in \(\mathrm{lem{:}initial\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}clt}\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)
 &=b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
   +s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})
   -b_n^{\mathsf T}D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})+o_{\Pr}(1)\\
 &=s_nE_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})+o_{\Pr}(1). \tag{22}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The radial-entrance theorem gives \(E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\Rightarrow\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), and the class moment conditions give \(s_n\to s>0\).  Slutsky's theorem proves
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,s^2). \tag{23}
\]

Let \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) be the standard-normal two-sided critical value.  The residual-Neyman interval has half-width \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat u_n/n}\).  Stopped-moment consistency gives \(\widehat u_n\to_{\Pr}u\), while (11) gives \(u\ge s^2\).  Since \(s>0\), (22)--(23) and continuity of the Gaussian law imply
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left\{
 |\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{Lin},n}-\tau_n)|
 \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat u_n}\right\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{24}
\]
If \(u_n-s_n^2\to0\), then \(u=s^2\); the limiting studentized statistic is standard normal, so the limit in (24) equals \(1-\alpha\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Full interactions remove the clipped covariate component only after terminal slope consistency and limiting residual independence have been established under the stopped design. \textit{Gap.} Lin proves this Gaussian adjustment under complete randomization, and Li--Ding and Zhao--Ding prove related cancellation under uniform constrained designs, not path-selected PSRR. \textit{Consumer.} Applied analysts can use standard fully interacted software and robust residual variance after an original PSRR allocation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:orthogonal-outcome-reduction]\label{prop:orthogonal-outcome-reduction}
Along the retained even tail \(n\ge N_0\), if \(b=0\), then \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,s^2)\); for every \(u\ge0\), \(q_{*,\alpha}(u,0)=\sqrt u\inf\{t\ge0:\Pr(|\varepsilon|\le t)\ge1-\alpha\}\), so the envelope interval reduces to the standard conservative Neyman Gaussian form.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(b=0\), the ATE limit theorem immediately reduces to
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)\Rightarrow s\varepsilon
 \sim\mathcal N(0,s^2). \tag{25}
\]
When \(B_0=0\), the clipped-Gaussian summand in the critical-value definition vanishes.  Hence, for every \(v\ge0\),
\[
 q_\alpha(v,0)=\sqrt v\,c_\alpha,
 \qquad
 c_\alpha=\inf\{t\ge0:\Pr(|\varepsilon|\le t)\ge1-\alpha\}, \tag{26}
\]
including \(v=0\), where both sides vanish.  The right-hand side is nondecreasing in \(v\), and therefore
\[
 q_{*,\alpha}(u,0)
 =\max_{0\le v\le u}q_\alpha(v,0)
 =\sqrt u\,c_\alpha. \tag{27}
\]
Substitution into the variance-envelope interval gives the standard conservative Neyman Gaussian half-width \(c_\alpha\sqrt{u/n}\), with \(u\) replaced by its stopped-design consistent estimator \(\widehat u_n\) in the observable interval.  The reduction is therefore obtained from the stopped-PSRR law and does not assume complete randomization or uniform sampling from the acceptable set.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This sanity reduction verifies that the new design-specific law disappears when outcomes have no linear covariate loading. \textit{Gap.} The reduction is known under complete and uniform rerandomization, but it must be recovered from the stopped PSRR law rather than assumed. \textit{Consumer.} The result provides a diagnostic special case and connects the proposed critical value to familiar design-based Wald inference.

\begin{theorem}[thm:pathwise-characteristics-resolved]\label{thm:pathwise-characteristics-resolved}
The answer is affirmative along the actual uniformly initialized path, but not uniformly over arbitrary balanced states.  All displayed maxima over empty pre-entry index sets are defined as \(0\).  For every fixed \(L,R<\infty\), with \(Z_{n,m}=Z_n(W_{n,m})=(D_{n,m}^{\mathsf T},E_{n,m})^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
 \max_{\substack{0\le m<\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n\\ \|Z_{n,m}\|\le R}}
 \left\|n\mathbb E(\Delta Z_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F_{n,m})+4Z_{n,m}
 +\frac{8\gamma}{\|D_{n,m}\|^2}(D_{n,m}^{\mathsf T},0)^{\mathsf T}\right\|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0,
\]
\[
 \max_{\substack{0\le m<\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n\\ \|Z_{n,m}\|\le R}}
 \left\|n\mathbb E(\Delta Z_{n,m}\Delta Z_{n,m}^{\mathsf T}\mid\mathcal F_{n,m})-8I_{k+1}\right\|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), the analogous maximum of
\(n\mathbb E[\|\Delta Z_{n,m}\|^2\mathbf 1\{\|\Delta Z_{n,m}\|>\eta\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,m}]\) is eventually zero.  If \(\phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) is smooth, constant near zero, and equals \(\log m\) for \(m\ge a/2\), the corresponding smooth extension has localized characteristics with the singular factor \(D/\|D\|^2\) replaced by the globally smooth factor \(\phi'(\|D\|^2)D\).  It agrees with original PSRR before first entry.  On \(\{T_n>0\}\), the exact terminal transposition and the maximal score bound give
\[
 \operatorname{dist}\bigl(D_n(W_{n,T_n}),\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\bigr)\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
Thus the terminal crossing can be transferred separately, without evaluating the singular drift at or after \(T_n\).  No projected Markov property, stabilization, outcome--residual independence assumption, or empirical-law limit is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We interpret a maximum over an empty pre-entry set as \(0\); on \{T\_n=0\} the displayed characteristic assertions are therefore immediate.  Write \(q_i=q_{ni}\), \(Z=Z_n(W_{n,m})=(D^{\mathsf T},E)^{\mathsf T}\), and let \(i\) and \(j\) be respectively the uniformly selected treated and control indices.  Before the acceptance decision the proposed score increment is
\[
 \delta=\frac2{\sqrt n}(q_j-q_i).
\]
Since \(\sum_iq_i=0\), the treated and control score means are \(Z/\sqrt n\) and \(-Z/\sqrt n\).  Direct expansion therefore gives, at every balanced state,
\[
 \mathbb E(\delta\mid W)=-\frac4nZ,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W)
 =\frac{8(n-1)}{n^2}I_{k+1}+\frac8{n^2}ZZ^{\mathsf T}. \tag{5}
\]

On the localized pre-entry set, \(\|D\|^2>a\) and \(\|Z\|\le R\).  Taylor expansion of the exact Metropolis probability, uniformly on this set, yields
\[
 A=1-\frac{2\gamma}{\|D\|^2}(D^{\mathsf T}\delta_D)_++O(n^{-1}). \tag{6}
\]
Acceptance randomness is conditionally independent after the proposal, so the conditional drift is \(\mathbb E(A\delta\mid W)\).  Using \(x_+=(x+|x|)/2\), (5), and (6),
\[
 \begin{split}
 n\mathbb E(A\delta\mid W)
 ={}&-4Z-\frac{\gamma n}{\|D\|^2}\mathbb E(\delta\delta_D^{\mathsf T}\mid W)D\\
 &-\frac{\gamma n}{\|D\|^2}\mathbb E\{\delta|D^{\mathsf T}\delta_D|\mid W\}+O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{7}
 \end{split}
\]
The middle term in (7) is \(-8\gamma(D^{\mathsf T},0)^{\mathsf T}/\|D\|^2+o(1)\), uniformly on the localized path, by (5).  It remains to control the kink term; projection orthogonality alone is not enough.

Let \(\nu_T,\nu_C\) be the two arm empirical probability measures on the \(q_i\)'s, let \(\mu=(\nu_T+\nu_C)/2=n^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{q_i}\), and put \(\xi=(\nu_T-\nu_C)/2\).  For a unit vector \(u\in\mathbb R^k\) and coordinate \(\ell\le k+1\), define
\[
 F_{u,\ell}(q,q')=(q'_\ell-q_\ell)|u^{\mathsf T}(q'_D-q_D)|,
 \qquad g_{n,u,\ell}(q)=\int F_{u,\ell}(q,q')\,\mu(dq').
\]
The kernel is antisymmetric.  Hence its integrals against \(\mu\otimes\mu\) and \(\xi\otimes\xi\) vanish, the two mixed terms agree, and \(\int g_{n,u,\ell}\,d\mu=0\).  Thus the exact identity
\[
 \iint F_{u,\ell}(q,q')\,\nu_T(dq)\nu_C(dq')
 =2\int g_{n,u,\ell}(q)\,\nu_T(dq) \tag{8}
\]
holds for every row and state.  The functions on the right are deterministic, uniformly bounded, and uniformly Lipschitz in \(u\), because \(q_i\) are uniformly bounded.  Actual-path arm uniformity, with the unit sphere as parameter set, makes (8) \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) uniformly through \(nL\) proposals.  Taking \(u=D/\|D\|\) shows that the last expectation in (7), after multiplication by \(n\), is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\).  This proves the drift assertion.

The same expansion gives \(1-A=O(n^{-1/2})\) locally.  Since \(\|\delta\|=O(n^{-1/2})\),
\[
 n\left\|\mathbb E\{(1-A)\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W\}\right\|=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Combining this with (5) proves the second-characteristic assertion, including the vanishing covariate--residual cross-characteristics.  The deterministic jump bound makes the displayed conditional Lindeberg term zero for all sufficiently large \(n\).

The necessity of the actual-path qualification is witnessed exactly at \(a=1\).  Take six score pairs with covariate coordinates
\[
 (-2,0,2,-1,-1,2)
\]
and residual coordinates \((1,-2,1,0,0,0)\), treating the first three.  Both arm means and the population covariate--residual covariance are zero, but direct summation over the nine ordered treated--control pairs gives
\[
 \frac19\sum_{i\in T,j\in C}(r_j-r_i)|x_j-x_i|=\frac{-6+8-6}{9}=-\frac49. \tag{9}
\]
Repeating the six types and changing only \(O(\sqrt n)\) labels produces bounded exterior projected scores while preserving the limit in (9).  Thus an order-one erroneous kink drift would remain at both \(\gamma=1\) and \(\gamma=10\) if one asserted uniformity over all balanced states.  The coupling and (8) show that these states are not representative of the uniformly initialized actual path; no additional initialization or empirical-distribution assumption is needed.

For the smooth extension in the coupling lemma the identical computation uses
\(A=1-2\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)(D^{\mathsf T}\delta_D)_++O(n^{-1})\).  It gives drift \(-4Z-8\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)(D^{\mathsf T},0)^{\mathsf T}\) and covariance \(8I_{k+1}\).  Before entry, \(\phi'(\|D\|^2)=1/\|D\|^2\).

Finally, on \(\{T_n>0\}\), \(\|D_{n,T_n-1}\|>\sqrt a\), \(\|D_{n,T_n}\|\le\sqrt a\), and a transposition changes \(D\) by at most \(4C/\sqrt n\).  Hence
\[
 0\le\sqrt a-\|D_{n,T_n}\|\le4C/\sqrt n.
\]
This is the promised separate crossing control and never evaluates \(D/\|D\|^2\) at the stopped state.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:complete-randomization-vector-clt]\label{lem:complete-randomization-vector-clt}
Let \(q_{ni}\in\mathbb R^p\), where \(p\) is fixed, be a deterministic triangular array satisfying \(\sum_{i=1}^n q_{ni}=0\), \((n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nq_{ni}q_{ni}^{\mathsf T}\to\Sigma\), and \(n^{-1}\max_{i\le n}\|q_{ni}\|^2\to0\).  If \(W_{n,0}\) is uniform on the balanced allocation space, then
\[
 n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(2W_{n,0,i}-1)q_{ni}\Rightarrow\mathcal N_p(0,\Sigma).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:all-pairs-coupling-clock]\label{lem:all-pairs-coupling-clock}
Let \(\phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) be smooth, constant near zero, and equal to \(\log m\) for \(m\ge a/2\).  Extend each transition by using acceptance \(1\wedge\exp[-\gamma\{\phi(\|D^*\|^2)-\phi(\|D\|^2)\}]\), and implement this extension at an all-pairs clock, with same-label pairs acting as identity moves.  There is a coupling of this extension with a stationary uniform balanced-allocation shadow \((V_{n,\ell})_{\ell\ge0}\) such that, for every fixed \(L<\infty\),
\[
 \max_{0\le\ell\le\lfloor nL\rfloor}d_H(\widetilde W_{n,\ell},V_{n,\ell})=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n).
\]
The indicator that an all-pairs tick selects opposite labels is an independent Bernoulli variable with success probability \(p_n^{\mathrm{op}}=n/[2(n-1)]\).  If \(A_n(m)\) is the number of all-pairs ticks needed to obtain \(m\) opposite-label proposals, then, for every fixed \(L\),
\[
 \max_{0\le m\le\lfloor nL\rfloor}\left|\frac{A_n(m)}n-\frac{m}{np_n^{\mathrm{op}}}\right|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
Deleting same-label ticks therefore recovers the original proposal clock, and the factor \((p_n^{\mathrm{op}})^{-1}\to2\) converts the all-pairs characteristics to the original proposal-time characteristics.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the retained tail the score bound established in the initialization proof gives \(\max_i\|q_{ni}\|\le C\).  Hence every transposition changes \(Z_n\) by at most \(4C/\sqrt n\).  Choose a smooth \(\phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) which is constant near zero, equals \(\log m\) for \(m\ge a/2\), and has bounded first two derivatives after composition with \(d\mapsto\|d\|^2\).  Put \(U(d)=\phi(\|d\|^2)\), and extend the transition rule by accepting a proposed transposition with probability
\[
 A_n(d,d^*)=1\wedge\exp\{-\gamma(U(d^*)-U(d))\}. \tag{2}
\]
The radial change in one proposal is at most \(4C/\sqrt n\).  Thus, for all sufficiently large \(n\), a proposal from \(\|d\|^2>a\) cannot land in \(\|d^*\|^2<a/2\), so (2) agrees exactly with original PSRR up to first entry.

At each auxiliary tick choose an unordered pair uniformly from the \(n(n-1)/2\) pairs.  The process \(\widetilde W\) applies (2) when the labels are opposite and regards a same-label transposition as an identity move.  Start \(V_{n,0}=\widetilde W_{n,0}\), and at every tick transpose the same two coordinates of \(V\), without rejection.  A common transposition preserves Hamming distance, including when the two labels of \(\widetilde W\) agree, because then that coordinate permutation fixes \(\widetilde W\).  A rejection can increase Hamming distance by at most two.  Since \(U\) is globally Lipschitz and a score jump is \(O(n^{-1/2})\),
\[
 1-A_n(d,d^*)\le\gamma|U(d^*)-U(d)|\le C_1n^{-1/2}. \tag{3}
\]
If \(R_n(L)\) counts rejections through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\) ticks, then (3) gives \(\mathbb E R_n(L)\le C_1L\sqrt n\).  Markov's inequality and
\[
 \max_{\ell\le nL}d_H(\widetilde W_{n,\ell},V_{n,\ell})\le2R_n(L)
\]
prove the first assertion.  The shadow starts uniformly and each tick applies a coordinate permutation independent of its current value, so its uniform balanced law is stationary at every tick; no mixing assertion is being used.

Given the past, exactly \((n/2)^2\) unordered pairs have opposite labels.  Hence the opposite-label indicator \(J_{n,\ell}\) has the state-independent conditional probability
\[
 p_n^{\mathrm{op}}=\frac{n^2/4}{n(n-1)/2}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}.
\]
The constancy of this conditional law shows inductively that the indicators are independent Bernoulli variables.  For \(S_n(r)=\sum_{\ell<r}(J_{n,\ell}-p_n^{\mathrm{op}})\), the elementary maximal second-moment inequality gives, for fixed \(C_2\),
\[
 \Pr\left\{\max_{r\le C_2n}|S_n(r)|>\eta n\right\}
 \le \frac{C_2n p_n^{\mathrm{op}}(1-p_n^{\mathrm{op}})}{\eta^2n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Monotone inversion of the counting process yields the displayed uniform inverse-clock relation.  Conditional on \(J_{n,\ell}=1\), the chosen pair is uniform among the treated--control pairs, so deleting the zeros produces exactly the original proposal chain, including its Metropolis rejection self-loops.  This proves the clock conversion and not a modified algorithm.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:actual-path-arm-uniformity]\label{lem:actual-path-arm-uniformity}
Let \(A_{ni}=(x_{ni}^{\mathsf T},Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1))^{\mathsf T}\), and let \(\{f_{n,\vartheta}:\vartheta\in\Theta\}\) be any deterministic family evaluated on these records (or on the derived scores \(q_{ni}\)), where \(\Theta\) is a compact subset of a fixed-dimensional Euclidean space, \(\sup_{n,i,\vartheta}|f_{n,\vartheta}(A_{ni})|<\infty\), and
\[
 \sup_{n,i}|f_{n,\vartheta}(A_{ni})-f_{n,\vartheta'}(A_{ni})|\le C\|\vartheta-\vartheta'\|.
\]
Then, for every fixed \(L<\infty\), along the actual original-PSRR path,
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\max_{0\le m\le\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n}
 \left|\frac2n\sum_{i:W_{n,m,i}=1}f_{n,\vartheta}(A_{ni})-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf_{n,\vartheta}(A_{ni})\right|
 \longrightarrow_{\Pr}0,
\]
and the analogous control-arm assertion holds.  The conclusion requires no convergence of the population empirical distribution.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First work at the auxiliary all-pairs clock.  At every fixed tick \(\ell\), stationarity of the shadow makes \(\{i:V_{n,\ell,i}=1\}\) a uniform subset of size \(n/2\).  If \(|f_i|\le C_0\), exposing a uniform random permutation one coordinate at a time and applying the exponential-moment argument for its bounded martingale differences gives
\[
 \Pr\left(\left|\frac2n\sum_{i:V_{n,\ell,i}=1}f_i-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf_i\right|>\eta\right)
 \le2\exp(-c n\eta^2/C_0^2) \tag{4}
\]
for a universal \(c>0\).  This derivation uses only sampling without replacement and does not require independence across ticks.

For a mesh of size \(n^{-2}\), compactness in fixed dimension supplies a net \(\Theta_n\) of polynomial cardinality.  Take \(\eta_n=A\sqrt{\log n/n}\) with \(A\) large enough.  A union bound in (4) over \(\ell\le nL\) and \(\vartheta\in\Theta_n\) tends to zero.  Lipschitz interpolation from the net then shows that the displayed deviation for the shadow is \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt{\log n/n})\), uniformly in both arguments.

Changing one treatment label changes a normalized arm sum by at most \(2C_0/n\).  The coupling lemma consequently gives
\[
 \max_{\ell\le nL}\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}
 \left|\frac2n\sum_{i:\widetilde W_{n,\ell,i}=1}f_{n,\vartheta}(A_{ni})
 -\frac2n\sum_{i:V_{n,\ell,i}=1}f_{n,\vartheta}(A_{ni})\right|
 \le \frac{2C_0}{n}\max_{\ell\le nL}d_H(\widetilde W_{n,\ell},V_{n,\ell})
 =O_{\Pr}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Thus the all-pairs extension has the asserted uniformity.  The inverse-clock relation transfers it to every original proposal time up to \(nL\); before \(T_n\) the smooth extension and original PSRR coincide.  Finally, equal allocation and the identity that the two arm empirical measures average to the population empirical measure give the control-arm assertion.  The proof is rowwise and nowhere takes a limit of the empirical distribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:covariate-path-characteristics-and-clock]\label{lem:covariate-path-characteristics-and-clock}
For a sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), define
\[
 N_x=\min\left\{N\in2\mathbb N:\ \lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x/2\ \text{and}\ \mathcal A_n(a)\ne\varnothing\ \text{for every even }n\ge N\right\}.
\]
Then \(N_x<\infty\).  For even \(n\ge N_x\), let \((W^x_{n,m})_{m\ge0}\), \(T_n^x\), and \(\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\) denote the original PSRR chain, its first-entry time, and its actual filtration, and write \(D^x_{n,m}=D_n(W^x_{n,m})\) and \(\Delta D^x_{n,m}=D^x_{n,m+1}-D^x_{n,m}\).  All displayed maxima over empty pre-entry index sets are defined as \(0\).  For every fixed \(L,R<\infty\),
\[
 \max_{\substack{0\le m<\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\\ \|D^x_{n,m}\|\le R}}
 \left\|n\mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})+4D^x_{n,m}
 +\frac{8\gamma D^x_{n,m}}{\|D^x_{n,m}\|^2}\right\|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0,
\]
\[
 \max_{\substack{0\le m<\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\\ \|D^x_{n,m}\|\le R}}
 \left\|n\mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\Delta D^{x\mathsf T}_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})-8I_k\right\|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
The conditional Lindeberg terms vanish for every fixed positive cutoff.  For a smooth extension \(\phi\) that is constant near zero and equals \(\log r\) for \(r\ge a/2\), the same conclusions hold globally on compacts with \(D/\|D\|^2\) replaced by \(\phi'(\|D\|^2)D\); the extension agrees with original PSRR through first entry.  Its compactly stopped second moment obeys a bound of the form
\[
 n\mathbb E\!\left(\|D^x_{n,m+1}\|^2-\|D^x_{n,m}\|^2\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\right)
 \le C-c\|D^x_{n,m}\|^2.
\]
Moreover, the number of rejected proposals among the first \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\) proposals is \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), and, on \(\{T_n^x>0\}\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}\!\left(D^x_{n,T_n^x},\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\right)\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0.
\]
All conclusions use only the covariate-design conditions and the actual uniformly initialized path; they are not uniform over arbitrary balanced states.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(d_{ni}=S_{xx,n}^{-1/2}x_{ni}\).  Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) and \(\liminf_n\lambda_{\min}(S_{xx,n})\ge c_x\) imply that, eventually,
\[
 \max_i\|d_{ni}\|\le (2/c_x)^{1/2}K_0,
 \qquad \sum_i d_{ni}=0,
 \qquad \frac1n\sum_i d_{ni}d_{ni}^{\mathsf T}=\frac{n-1}{n}I_k. \tag{1}
\]
The finite-population vector central limit lemma applied to \(q_{ni}=d_{ni}\) is applicable because \(k\) is fixed, (1) gives covariance convergence to \(I_k\), and \(n^{-1}\max_i\|d_{ni}\|^2\to0\).  Hence
\[
 D_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow G\sim\mathcal N_k(0,I_k). \tag{2}
\]
Since the sphere \(\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\) has Gaussian probability zero and \(a>0\), the ball is a Gaussian continuity set, so convergence of its probabilities gives
\[
 \Pr\{M_n(W_{n,0})\le a\}\longrightarrow\Pr\{\|G\|^2\le a\}>0. \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal A_n(a)\ne\varnothing\) eventually, proving \(N_x<\infty\).  Positive definiteness on that tail makes every score and transition well defined.

Fix a balanced state and abbreviate \(D=D_n(W)\).  If \(i\) is uniform in the treated arm and \(j\) is independently uniform in the control arm, the proposed score increment is
\[
 \delta=\frac{2}{\sqrt n}(d_{nj}-d_{ni}).
\]
The treated and control means of \(d_{ni}\) are respectively \(D/\sqrt n\) and \(-D/\sqrt n\).  Expanding the two product averages and using (1) therefore gives the exact identities
\[
 \mathbb E(\delta\mid W)=-\frac{4D}{n},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W)
 =\frac{8}{n}\frac1n\sum_i d_{ni}d_{ni}^{\mathsf T}+\frac{8DD^{\mathsf T}}{n^2}. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, uniformly for bounded \(D\), the second expression multiplied by \(n\) tends to \(8I_k\).

Choose \(\phi\in C^3([0,\infty))\) constant near zero and equal to \(\log r\) for \(r\ge a/2\), with \(U(d)=\phi(\|d\|^2)\) having bounded first three derivatives.  The bound in (1) gives \(\|\delta\|\le Cn^{-1/2}\).  Taylor expansion, uniformly on compact score sets, yields
\[
 U(D+\delta)-U(D)=2\phi'(\|D\|^2)D^{\mathsf T}\delta+O(n^{-1}), \tag{5}
\]
and \(1\wedge e^{-\gamma y}=1-\gamma y_++O(y^2)\) uniformly for \(|y|=O(n^{-1/2})\).  Thus the acceptance probability \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
 \alpha=1-2\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)(D^{\mathsf T}\delta)_++O(n^{-1}). \tag{6}
\]
Since \(z_+=(z+|z|)/2\), (4)--(6) imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 n\mathbb E(\alpha\delta\mid W)
 &=-4D-\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)n\mathbb E(\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W)D\\
 &\quad-\gamma\phi'(\|D\|^2)n\mathbb E\{\delta|D^{\mathsf T}\delta|\mid W\}+O(n^{-1/2}).
 \end{split} \tag{7}
\]
It remains to control the last, nonsmooth average along the actual path; orthogonality at a single state would not suffice.

To do so, extend the process to an all-pairs clock.  At each tick choose a uniform unordered pair, act as the identity for equal labels, and otherwise use the exact acceptance probability \(1\wedge\exp[-\gamma\{U(D^*)-U(D)\}]\); equation (6) is its uniform local expansion.  Couple this process to a chain that starts at the same uniform balanced allocation and always performs the chosen transposition.  The latter chain is uniform on \(\mathcal A_n\) at every tick by permutation invariance.  Boundedness of \(\nabla U\), (1), and \(1-e^{-z}\le z\) for \(z\ge0\) give
\[
 \Pr(\text{rejection at a tick}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})\le Cn^{-1/2}. \tag{8}
\]
Consequently the number of discrepancies through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\) all-pairs ticks has expectation at most \(CL\sqrt n\), and Markov's inequality shows that the two allocation vectors have Hamming distance \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), uniformly through that horizon.  For any uniformly bounded deterministic score \(g_{ni}\), changing \(h\) labels changes its arm mean by at most \(2\|g_n\|_\infty h/n\).  To obtain the needed sampling-without-replacement bound, expose a uniform treated set one draw at a time and take the Doob martingale of its final centered mean.  If \(|g_{ni}|\le K\), replacing one revealed draw changes that conditional mean by at most \(4K/n\).  Conditional exponential-moment bounds followed by Chernoff optimization therefore give, directly,
\[
 \Pr\left(\left|\frac2n\sum_{i:V_i=1}g_{ni}-\frac1n\sum_i g_{ni}\right|>\eta\right)
 \le 2\exp\left(-\frac{n\eta^2}{8K^2}\right). \tag{9a}
\]
A union bound over \(O(n)\) ticks proves uniform convergence of the shadow arm means; the coupling transfers it to the actual path.  For a bounded Lipschitz family indexed by the unit sphere, a polynomial-size \(n^{-1}\)-net followed by Lipschitz interpolation makes the convergence uniform in the index.

For a unit vector \(u\), define
\[
 F_{u,\ell}(q,q')=(q'_{\ell}-q_{\ell})|u^{\mathsf T}(q'-q)|,
 \qquad g_{u,\ell,n}(q)=\int F_{u,\ell}(q,q')\,\mu_n(dq'), \tag{9}
\]
where \(\mu_n\) is the deterministic full-population empirical measure.  The kernel \(F_{u,\ell}\) is antisymmetric, so \(\int g_{u,\ell,n}\,d\mu_n=0\).  If \(\nu_T=\mu_n+\xi\) and \(\nu_C=\mu_n-\xi\) are the two arm empirical measures, antisymmetry cancels the \(\mu_n\otimes\mu_n\) and \(\xi\otimes\xi\) terms and makes the mixed terms equal.  Hence exactly
\[
 \iint F_{u,\ell}(q,q')\,\nu_T(dq)\nu_C(dq')
 =2\int g_{u,\ell,n}(q)\,\nu_T(dq). \tag{10}
\]
The family on the right is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz in \(u\).  The preceding uniform arm-mean result and its zero population mean show that (10) is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\), uniformly through every fixed scaled horizon.  Taking \(u=D/\|D\|\) in (10) proves that the last expectation in (7), after multiplication by \(n\), is \(o_{\Pr}(1)\) uniformly on localized paths.  Equations (4) and (7) now give the asserted drift.  Because \(1-\alpha=O(n^{-1/2})\) and \(\|\delta\|^2=O(n^{-1})\),
\[
 n\left\|\mathbb E\{(1-\alpha)\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}\mid W\}\right\|=O(n^{-1/2}), \tag{11}
\]
so (4) gives the asserted covariance.  The jump bound makes the conditional Lindeberg expression identically zero for all sufficiently large \(n\).

An all-pairs tick selects opposite labels with the state-independent probability \(p_n=n/[2(n-1)]\).  The corresponding indicators are independent Bernoulli variables.  Their centered partial sums are martingales with variance at most the number of ticks; applying the squared-submartingale maximal inequality and then inverting the monotone counting process gives, for the inverse clock \(A_n(m)\),
\[
 \max_{m\le nL}\left|\frac{A_n(m)}n-\frac{m}{np_n}\right|\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{12}
\]
Deleting identity ticks recovers the original treated--control proposal clock.  Thus the all-pairs characteristic rates are multiplied by \(p_n^{-1}\to2\), producing exactly the constants in (7) and (11), rather than a stationary-chain clock.

For the smooth extension, (4), (8), and \(2D^{\mathsf T}\mathbb E(\delta\mid W)=-8\|D\|^2/n\) yield constants \(C,c>0\) such that
\[
 n\mathbb E\{\|D+J\delta\|^2-\|D\|^2\mid W\}
 \le C-c\|D\|^2, \tag{13}
\]
where, conditionally on the proposal, \(J\) is its acceptance indicator with mean \(\alpha\), so \(J\delta\) is zero after rejection.  Indeed, the rejection correction to the scaled drift is bounded, its scalar product with \(D\) is at most \(C\|D\|\), the quadratic term is bounded, and \(C\|D\|\le(c/2)\|D\|^2+C^2/(2c)\).

The same bound (8), now on the original proposal clock, gives expected rejections at most \(CL\sqrt n\) through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\), hence the claimed \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\) bound.  Finally, on \(\{T_n^x>0\}\), the preterminal radius is larger than \(a\), the terminal radius is at most \(a\), and (1) gives \(\|D^x_{n,T_n^x}-D^x_{n,T_n^x-1}\|\le Cn^{-1/2}\).  The reverse triangle inequality therefore gives the asserted distance from the terminal score to the sphere.  This completes every claimed characteristic, clock, and crossing bound using covariates only.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:joint-stopped-functional-limit-two-class]\label{thm:joint-stopped-functional-limit-two-class}
The following two conclusions hold.

First, for every sequence in the covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), use \(N_x\), \((W^x_{n,m})\), and \(T_n^x\) from \(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\), and set
\[
 \bar D_n^x(t)=D_n\!\left(W^x_{n,\min\{\lfloor nt\rfloor,T_n^x\}}\right),
 \qquad
 A_n^x=\sum_{m=0}^{T_n^x-1}\mathbf 1\{W^x_{n,m+1}\ne W^x_{n,m}\}.
\]
Along even \(n\ge N_x\), jointly in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^k)\times[0,\infty)\),
\[
 (\bar D_n^x,T_n^x/n)\Rightarrow(D_{\cdot\wedge H},H),
 \qquad T_n^x-A_n^x=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n),
 \qquad \frac{T_n^x-A_n^x}{n}\longrightarrow_{\Pr}0. \tag{14}
\]
Here, before \(H=\inf\{t\ge0:\|D_t\|^2\le a\}\),
\[
 dD_t=\left(-4D_t-\frac{8\gamma D_t}{\|D_t\|^2}\right)dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t,
 \qquad D_0=G\sim\mathcal N_k(0,I_k), \tag{15}
\]
and \(H<\infty\) almost surely.  If \(R_0=\|D_0\|^2=r\), \(b=k/2-\gamma\), and \(\lambda>0\), then the proposal-time first-entry law has the computable transform
\[
 \mathbb E_r(e^{-\lambda H})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le a,\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\frac{U(\lambda/8,b,r/2)}{U(\lambda/8,b,a/2)},&r>a,
 \end{cases} \tag{16}
\]
where, for \(A>0\) and \(x>0\),
\[
 U(A,b,x)=\frac1{\Gamma(A)}\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t^{A-1}(1+t)^{b-A-1}\,dt. \tag{17}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\), (16) is understood as one.  Under the Gaussian initialization, with \(F_{\chi_k^2}\) the chi-square distribution function,
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{-\lambda H})=F_{\chi_k^2}(a)+
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_a^\infty U(\lambda/8,b,r/2)r^{k/2-1}e^{-r/2}\,dr}
 {\displaystyle 2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2)U(\lambda/8,b,a/2)}. \tag{18}
\]
For every \(r>a\) and \(\lambda>0\), the transform in (16) is strictly increasing in \(\gamma\); thus finite tuning changes the proposal-time search law.  Equations (14) and the rejection estimate show that the accepted-switch clock used in the published presentation and the all-proposal clock differ by \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), and therefore by \(o_{\Pr}(n)\).

Second, for every sequence in the outcome-inference subclass \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\), along the retained even outcome tail \(n\ge N_0\), jointly in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^{k+1})\times[0,\infty)\),
\[
 \bigl(\bar Z_n,T_n/n\bigr)\Rightarrow
 \left(\bigl((D_{t\wedge H}^{\mathsf T},E_{t\wedge H})^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{t\ge0},H\right), \tag{19}
\]
where (15) holds and \(E\) is the independent stationary solution of \(dE_t=-4E_tdt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t\), \(E_0\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).  This is the unique weak solution of the stopped system in \(\mathrm{def{:}limit\mbox{-}system}\).  No projected Markov property is assumed in either clause.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the covariate and joint clauses, including the strengthened proposal-versus-accepted clock rate.

Fix a sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\). Use the smooth extension supplied by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\), and write
\(X_n(t)=D_n(W^x_{n,\lfloor nt\rfloor})\) for its proposal-clock interpolation before absorption. By that lemma, the extension agrees with original PSRR through \(T_n^x\), and, on every compact set, its limiting drift and covariance are
\[
 \beta_\phi(d)=-4d-8\gamma\phi'(\|d\|^2)d,
 \qquad c_\phi=8I_k. \tag{28}
\]
The clock conversion in the same lemma puts these characteristics on the original treated--control proposal scale.

We first establish compact containment. Stop at the first exit \(\sigma_{n,R}\) from the radius-\(R\) ball. Summing the compactly stopped second-moment drift inequality in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\) through
\(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge\sigma_{n,R}\), using the complete-randomization covariance identity
\(\mathbb E\|X_n(0)\|^2=k\), and using the uniform boundedness and covariance nondegeneracy conditions to bound every score jump by \(C_0n^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\sup_{t\le L}\|X_n(t)\|>R\right\}
 \le \frac{k+CL+o(1)}{(R-C_0n^{-1/2})^2}. \tag{29}
\]
Thus the left side is uniformly small after first taking \(n\to\infty\) and then \(R\to\infty\).

On a fixed ball the predictable drift and quadratic-variation rates are bounded by the localized characteristic lemma. If \(\sigma_n\le L\) is a stopping time and \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the predictable finite-variation increment over \([\sigma_n,\sigma_n+\delta_n]\) is \(O(\delta_n+n^{-1})\), while conditional Chebyshev applied to the martingale increment gives, for each \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{\|M_n(\sigma_n+\delta_n)-M_n(\sigma_n)\|>\eta\}
 \le C\eta^{-2}(\delta_n+n^{-1})+o(1). \tag{30}
\]
Equations (29)--(30), localization, and the vanishing maximal jump yield tightness in
\(D([0,L],\mathbb R^k)\); every subsequential limit is continuous.

For \(f\in C_c^3(\mathbb R^k)\), Taylor's theorem and the \(O(n^{-1/2})\) jump bound give, before compact exit,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb E\{f(X_{n,(m+1)/n})-f(X_{n,m/n})\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m}\}
 &=\nabla f(X_{n,m/n})^{\mathsf T}
   \mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})\\
 &\quad+\frac12\nabla^2f(X_{n,m/n}):
 \mathbb E(\Delta D^x_{n,m}\Delta D^{x\mathsf T}_{n,m}
 \mid\mathcal F^x_{n,m})+O(n^{-3/2}). \tag{31}
 \end{split}
\]
The trajectory-uniform characteristic bounds make the sum through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\) of the difference between equation (31) and
\(n^{-1}\mathcal L_\phi f(X_{n,m/n})\) converge to zero in probability, where
\[
 \mathcal L_\phi f(d)
 =\beta_\phi(d)^{\mathsf T}\nabla f(d)
  +4\operatorname{tr}\{\nabla^2f(d)\}. \tag{32}
\]
Compact stopping bounds the errors, so truncation upgrades this convergence to \(L^1\). Passing the conditional martingale identity to a subsequential limit shows that, for \(0\le s<t\) and every bounded continuous path functional \(Q\) measurable through time \(s\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 Q\left\{f(X_t)-f(X_s)-\int_s^t\mathcal L_\phi f(X_u)\,du\right\}
 \right]=0. \tag{33}
\]
Thus every limit solves the martingale problem for equation (32).

Because \(\phi\) is constant near zero, smooth on the transition annulus, and equal to the logarithm away from zero, the map
\(d\mapsto\phi'(\|d\|^2)d\) is globally Lipschitz. Picard iteration constructs a strong solution with coefficients in equation (28). If two such solutions use the same Brownian motion, the Lipschitz bound and Gronwall's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E\sup_{u\le t}\|X_u-X'_u\|^2
 \le C\int_0^t\mathbb E\sup_{v\le u}\|X_v-X'_v\|^2\,du=0. \tag{34}
\]
Consequently the martingale problem has a unique law. The initial-score central limit theorem identifies \(X_0=G\), so tightness and uniqueness identify convergence of the whole extended process.

Before first entry, \(\phi'(\|D_t\|^2)=1/\|D_t\|^2\). It\^o's formula applied to \(R_t=\|D_t\|^2\) gives
\[
 dR_t=8(k-2\gamma-R_t)\,dt+\sqrt{32R_t}\,d\beta_t,
 \qquad R_t>a, \tag{35}
\]
with generator
\[
 \mathcal L_Rg(r)=16r g''(r)+8(k-2\gamma-r)g'(r). \tag{36}
\]
A scale derivative is \(s'(r)=e^{r/2}r^{\gamma-k/2}\), as direct differentiation gives \(\mathcal L_Rs=0\). Let
\(H_a=\inf\{t:R_t\le a\}\) and \(H_M=\inf\{t:R_t\ge M\}\). Optional stopping of the bounded martingale
\(s(R_{t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M})\) gives, for \(a<r<M\),
\[
 \Pr_r(H_M<H_a)=\frac{s(r)-s(a)}{s(M)-s(a)}. \tag{37}
\]
The denominator tends to infinity as \(M\to\infty\). On every compact interval \([a,M]\), the diffusion coefficient in equation (35) is bounded away from zero. Solving the linear boundary-value equation
\(\mathcal L_Rg=-1\), with \(g(a)=g(M)=0\), and applying It\^o's formula shows that the exit time from \((a,M)\) has finite expectation. Letting \(M\to\infty\) in equation (37) proves \(H_a<\infty\) almost surely from every \(r>a\); for \(r\le a\), \(H=0\).

The Gaussian initialization has no mass on \(R_0=a\). At a hit from outside, the martingale coefficient in equation (35) equals \(\sqrt{32a}>0\). In a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(a\), its quadratic variation is strictly increasing and its drift is bounded. A Brownian time change and the reflection principle therefore show that the path cannot remain on the exterior side for a positive interval after hitting \(a\). Hence it immediately crosses into \((0,a)\) almost surely. Initial strict-interior paths have hitting time zero stably, whereas exterior paths have a finite boundary hit followed immediately by a strict-interior crossing. The closed-ball first-hit functional is consequently continuous at almost every limiting path. Combining the continuous mapping theorem on \([0,L]\), the vanishing terminal jump from the characteristic lemma, and then \(L\to\infty\), yields
\[
 (\bar D_n^x,T_n^x/n)\Rightarrow(D_{\cdot\wedge H},H). \tag{38}
\]

We now sharpen the clock comparison. Let
\[
 R_n(L)=\sum_{m=0}^{(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x)-1}
 \mathbf 1\{W^x_{n,m+1}=W^x_{n,m}\}
\]
be the number of rejected proposals through \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\wedge T_n^x\). For each fixed \(L<\infty\),
\(\mathrm{lem{:}covariate\mbox{-}path\mbox{-}characteristics\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}clock}\) gives
\(R_n(L)=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\). Convergence in equation (38) implies tightness of \(T_n^x/n\). Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose \(L<\infty\) so that
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr(T_n^x>nL)<\epsilon/2. \tag{39}
\]
For this fixed \(L\), choose \(C_\epsilon<\infty\) and \(n_\epsilon\) so that
\(\Pr\{R_n(L)>C_\epsilon\sqrt n\}<\epsilon/2\) for all \(n\ge n_\epsilon\). Since \(T_n^x\) is integer, on \(\{T_n^x\le nL\}\) all proposals through absorption occur by \(\lfloor nL\rfloor\), and therefore
\[
 T_n^x-A_n^x=R_n(L).
\]
It follows that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \Pr\{T_n^x-A_n^x>C_\epsilon\sqrt n\}
 \le \Pr(T_n^x>nL)+\Pr\{R_n(L)>C_\epsilon\sqrt n\}<\epsilon+o(1). \tag{40}
\]
This is exactly
\[
 T_n^x-A_n^x=O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n), \tag{41}
\]
and in particular \((T_n^x-A_n^x)/n\to_{\Pr}0\). Thus the sharper global rate follows from the fixed-horizon rejection estimate and hitting-time tightness; no limiting constant, rejection-count distribution, or population empirical-law limit is asserted.

We next derive the proposal-time entrance transform. Put
\[
 A=\lambda/8>0,\qquad b=k/2-\gamma,\qquad
 U(A,b,x)=\frac1{\Gamma(A)}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t^{A-1}(1+t)^{b-A-1}\,dt. \tag{42}
\]
The integral is finite and positive. Differentiation under the integral is justified by integrability at zero and the exponential tail; one integration by parts gives Kummer's equation
\[
 xU_{xx}+(b-x)U_x-AU=0. \tag{43}
\]
Define \(u_\lambda(r)=U(A,b,r/2)/U(A,b,a/2)\). Substitution of equation (43) into equation (36) gives
\[
 \mathcal L_Ru_\lambda=\lambda u_\lambda,\qquad u_\lambda(a)=1. \tag{44}
\]
Changing variables \(z=xt\) in equation (42), followed by dominated convergence, gives
\(U(A,b,x)\sim x^{-A}\) as \(x\to\infty\), hence \(u_\lambda(r)\to0\). It\^o's formula makes
\[
 e^{-\lambda(t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M)}
 u_\lambda(R_{t\wedge H_a\wedge H_M})
\]
a bounded martingale. Let first \(t\to\infty\) and then \(M\to\infty\). Equation (37), almost-sure finiteness of \(H_a\), and \(u_\lambda(M)\to0\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_r(e^{-\lambda H})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le a,\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle
 \frac{U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,r/2)}
 {U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,a/2)},&r>a.
 \end{cases} \tag{45}
\]
Integrating equation (45) against the \(\chi_k^2\) density and separating \(\{R_0\le a\}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(e^{-\lambda H})
 =F_{\chi_k^2}(a)+
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_a^\infty
 U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,r/2)r^{k/2-1}e^{-r/2}\,dr}
 {\displaystyle2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2)
 U(\lambda/8,k/2-\gamma,a/2)}. \tag{46}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\), the transform is one.

The tuning dependence is strict. Let \(\gamma_2>\gamma_1\) and let \(u_j\) be equation (45) for \(\gamma_j\). The integral representation gives \(u'_2(r)<0\). Consequently
\[
 (\mathcal L_{R,\gamma_1}-\lambda)u_2
 =16(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)u'_2<0. \tag{47}
\]
The one-dimensional maximum principle on \([a,M]\), followed by \(M\to\infty\), uses equality at \(a\) and convergence to zero at infinity to give
\(u_2(r)>u_1(r)\) for every \(r>a\). Hence the conditional transform in equation (45), and therefore its nondegenerate Gaussian-initialization mixture in equation (46), is strictly increasing in finite \(\gamma\).

Finally, fix a sequence in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\). The initial-score lemma gives
\[
 Z_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow\mathcal N_{k+1}(0,I_{k+1}). \tag{48}
\]
The actual-filtration characteristic theorem gives, on the retained outcome tail, limiting drift and covariance
\[
 \beta_\phi(d,e)=
 \bigl(-4d-8\gamma\phi'(\|d\|^2)d,-4e\bigr),
 \qquad c_\phi=8I_{k+1}, \tag{49}
\]
with vanishing conditional Lindeberg terms and the same terminal-crossing control. The compact-containment, tightness, Taylor-expansion, and martingale-problem arguments in equations (29)--(34), now applied in dimension \(k+1\), identify the unique solution
\[
 \begin{split}
 dD_t&=\{-4D_t-8\gamma\phi'(\|D_t\|^2)D_t\}\,dt+\sqrt8\,dB_t,\\
 dE_t&=-4E_t\,dt+\sqrt8\,dB'_t.
 \end{split} \tag{50}
\]
The identity covariance in equation (49) and the independent Gaussian coordinates in equation (48) imply, by uniqueness of this product martingale problem, that \(B,B'\) and \(G,\varepsilon\) may be taken mutually independent. Thus independence is a conclusion of the limiting characteristics and Gaussian initialization, not a finite-population outcome--residual independence assumption. Since the hitting and stopping functional depends only on \(D\), the transfer used for equation (38) yields
\[
 (\bar Z_n,T_n/n)\Rightarrow
 \left(\bigl((D_{t\wedge H}^{\mathsf T},E_{t\wedge H})^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{t\ge0},H\right). \tag{51}
\]
Before \(H\), equation (50) is the system in \(\mathrm{def{:}limit\mbox{-}system}\), and absorption at \(H\) proves the joint clause and its uniqueness. At no point was a projected Markov property assumed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives the outcome-free covariate allocation limit and computable proposal-time first-entry law, then adds the joint outcome-residual path only on the smaller inference subclass. \textit{Gap.} It closes the common-statistic asymptotics gap for a regular subclass of original first-entry PSRR and distinguishes its proposal and accepted clocks from stationary-chain analyses. \textit{Consumer.} Design users can compute the asymptotic runtime transform and transport stopped statistics on the proposal or accepted-switch clock; inference users additionally obtain the joint residual process under the outcome conditions.

\begin{theorem}[thm:radial-entrance-law-design-class]\label{thm:radial-entrance-law-design-class}
Over the covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), along even \(n\ge N_x\),
\[
 D_n(W^x_{n,T_n^x})\Rightarrow C_{k,a}.
\]
This conclusion is outcome-free.  Over the outcome-inference subclass \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}\), along even \(n\ge N_0\),
\[
 \bigl(D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\bigr)
 \Rightarrow(C_{k,a},\varepsilon),
\]
where \(C_{k,a}\) and \(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) are independent.  In either scope,
\[
 \|C_{k,a}\|^2=\min\{\chi_k^2,a\},
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0.
\]
For every fixed finite \(\gamma\), the endpoint law is the same; tuning changes only the search-time transform in (16)--(18).  For finite \(a>0\), the covariate endpoint is not the classical uniform-rerandomization law \(L_{k,a}\), which assigns the boundary sphere probability zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two-class functional theorem yields joint convergence of the absorbed path and its finite stopping time.  Since the limiting path is continuous and absorbed after \(H\), the evaluation map \((z,t)\mapsto z(t)\) is continuous at the limit almost surely.  Thus the continuous mapping theorem applies to the terminal score.

On \(\{\|G\|^2<a\}\), \(H=0\) and \(D_H=G\).  The chi-square law is continuous, so \(\Pr(\|G\|^2=a)=0\).  On \(\{\|G\|^2>a\}\), continuity of \(D\), the definition of first entry, and \(H<\infty\) imply
\[
 \|D_H\|=\sqrt a. \tag{28}
\]
For every orthogonal \(k\times k\) matrix \(Q\), the exterior drift \(\mu(d)=-4d-8\gamma d/\|d\|^2\) and covariance obey
\[
 \mu(Qd)=Q\mu(d),\qquad Q(8I_k)Q^{\mathsf T}=8I_k. \tag{29}
\]
The Gaussian initial law and the stopping ball are also invariant under \(Q\).  Uniqueness in law of the stopped diffusion, supplied by the two-class functional theorem, therefore gives
\[
 QD_H\overset d=D_H. \tag{30}
\]
The unique rotation-invariant probability measure on the sphere of radius \(\sqrt a\) is normalized surface measure.  Hence the exterior branch in (28) is uniform in direction and has mass \(\Pr(\|G\|^2>a)\).  The interior branch retains the original Gaussian law.  A spherical Gaussian has uniform direction independent of its radius, so these two branches together give
\[
 D_H\overset d=G\min\{1,\sqrt a/\|G\|\}=C_{k,a}. \tag{31}
\]
The terminal evaluation described above now yields, on the outcome-free covariate-design class,
\[
 D_n(W^x_{n,T_n^x})\Rightarrow C_{k,a}. \tag{32}
\]
The endpoint argument uses only rotational equivariance; although \(\gamma\) changes the radial drift and the hitting-time transform, every fixed finite \(\gamma\) has the same endpoint law.

On the outcome-inference subclass, the residual coordinate has the explicit stationary Ornstein--Uhlenbeck representation
\[
 E_t=e^{-4t}\varepsilon+\sqrt8\int_0^t e^{-4(t-u)}\,dB'_u. \tag{33}
\]
At every deterministic \(t\), its variance is
\[
 e^{-8t}+8\int_0^t e^{-8(t-u)}\,du=1, \tag{34}
\]
and its mean is zero.  Thus \(E_t\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\).  The entire residual process is independent of the covariate process, while \(H\) is measurable with respect to the latter.  Conditioning on the covariate path, for bounded measurable \(f\) and bounded covariate-path functional \(Q_0\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{f(E_H)Q_0(D)\}
 =\mathbb E\{f(\varepsilon)\}\mathbb E\{Q_0(D)\}. \tag{35}
\]
Therefore \(E_H\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and \(E_H\perp D_H\).  Terminal evaluation of the joint functional convergence gives
\[
 \bigl(D_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}}),E_n(W_{n,\mathrm{stop}})\bigr)
 \Rightarrow(C_{k,a},\varepsilon),
 \qquad C_{k,a}\perp\varepsilon. \tag{36}
\]
This independence is derived from the block-diagonal limiting system and stationarity, not imposed on the finite-population outcomes.

Finally, (31) gives
\[
 \|C_{k,a}\|^2=\min\{\|G\|^2,a\}=\min\{\chi_k^2,a\}, \tag{37}
\]
so, because \(k\ge1\) and \(0<a<\infty\),
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0. \tag{38}
\]
The hitting-time transform in the two-class theorem exhibits the tuning dependence of runtime.  The distinct comparator law is proved in \(\mathrm{prop{:}not\mbox{-}uniform\mbox{-}rerandomization\mbox{-}design\mbox{-}class}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The clipped-Gaussian endpoint is a property of the covariate design alone, while residual independence is derived from the block-diagonal joint diffusion on the outcome subclass. \textit{Gap.} Classical uniform rerandomization gives a Gaussian conditioned inside the ball and does not yield the radial first-entry clipping law of original PSRR. \textit{Consumer.} The explicit endpoint law supports direct design calculations without outcomes and supplies the non-Gaussian component used by downstream ATE inference when outcomes are present.

\begin{proposition}[prop:not-uniform-rerandomization-design-class]\label{prop:not-uniform-rerandomization-design-class}
For every finite \(a>0\), over the outcome-free covariate-design class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\), the original first-entry PSRR covariate limit \(C_{k,a}\) is not equal in distribution to the uniform-rerandomization limit \(L_{k,a}\).  Specifically,
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0,
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\|L_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=0.
\]
The class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\) is a regular equal-allocation subclass of the arrays admitted by the original Zhu--Liu first-entry algorithm.  Therefore this subclass certificate also refutes, on the broader published class, any universal representation of the original algorithm's output as a uniform draw from the acceptable set.  The positive diffusion assertion itself remains restricted to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^{x}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The clipped-Gaussian definition and continuity of the chi-square distribution imply
\[
 \Pr\{\|C_{k,a}\|^2=a\}
 =\Pr\{\|G\|^2\ge a\}
 =1-F_{\chi_k^2}(a)>0. \tag{39}
\]
By contrast, \(L_{k,a}\overset d=G\mid\{\|G\|^2\le a\}\).  Its conditional squared radius is a continuous chi-square law restricted to \([0,a]\), and hence
\[
 \Pr\{\|L_{k,a}\|^2=a\}=0. \tag{40}
\]
The measurable sphere \(\{d:\|d\|^2=a\}\) therefore separates the two probability measures.

For completeness, uniformity of the terminal allocation on \(\mathcal A_n(a)\) would indeed have the comparator limit.  The complete-randomization vector CLT gives \(D_n(W_{n,0})\Rightarrow G\).  Since \(\Pr(\|G\|^2=a)=0\) and \(\Pr(\|G\|^2\le a)>0\), bounded-continuous test functions and the Portmanteau theorem give
\[
 \mathcal L\bigl(D_n(W_{n,0})\mid M_n(W_{n,0})\le a\bigr)
 \Rightarrow \mathcal L\bigl(G\mid\|G\|^2\le a\bigr)
 =\mathcal L(L_{k,a}). \tag{41}
\]
Every design in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\) uses equal arms, uniform balanced initialization, treated--control transposition proposals, the original Metropolis acceptance rule with fixed finite tuning, and immediate stopping at first entry into \(M_n\le a\).  The boundedness, fixed-dimension, moment, and nondegeneracy requirements restrict only the deterministic covariate arrays, so \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\) is a subclass of the broader domain of the published original algorithm.  A universal assertion that the original terminal allocation is uniform on its acceptable set would, by (41), force the limiting law \(L_{k,a}\) on every sequence in this subclass, contradicting (39)--(40) and the clipped-Gaussian limit.  Thus the subclass is a valid negative certificate for the broader universal representation, while the positive diffusion conclusion remains restricted to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PSRR}}^x\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A positive boundary atom is a computable measure-separating certificate and does not rely on outcome assumptions. \textit{Gap.} Stationary or correction-based uniformity results concern changed output mechanisms and cannot represent the original first-entry terminal allocation. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can rule out truncated-Gaussian calibration for original first-entry PSRR; by subclass inclusion, the certificate refutes a universal uniform representation over the broader published algorithmic class.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The six-row zero-correlation witness proves that the Metropolis-kink drift cannot be controlled uniformly over arbitrary balanced states: zero projected scores and zero covariate--residual covariance do not force the antisymmetric pair average to vanish. The proved characteristic result is therefore deliberately localized along the actual uniformly initialized path, where the all-pairs coupling and arm empirical concentration give the needed cancellation. Characteristics are used only before entry, where \(M_n>a>0\); the terminal crossing is handled by its exact vanishing transposition increment. This resolves the earlier kill test without adding an empirical-distribution limit or initialization assumption, but it does not create a uniform-over-states generator theorem.

\section{Honest scope}

The allocation-path, proposal-clock, runtime-transform, clipped-endpoint, and boundary-atom results hold on a regular outcome-free covariate-design subclass of original Zhu--Liu equal-allocation PSRR. The joint stationary residual coordinate and all ATE, stopped-moment, variance-envelope, and fully interacted conclusions hold only on the nested outcome-inference subclass with fixed bounded potential outcomes and nondegenerate projection-residual variance. Zhu--Liu's published state index counts accepted switches, whereas \(T_n^x\) here counts every proposal; the difference is \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\), so the clocks are first-order equivalent, but no limiting rejection-count constant or distribution is claimed. The limiting hitting-time law is computable through its Tricomi-\(U\) Laplace transform; no convergence of finite-chain expected runtime is claimed. The proof is complete for the actual-path characteristics, compact containment, martingale problem, random-clock conversion, and continuous first-hit transfer. It assumes fixed \(k\), fixed \(a>0\), fixed finite \(\gamma\), equal arms, bounded leverage, and sequencewise asymptotics. It excludes shrinking thresholds, growing dimension, divergent tuning, unequal arms, sequential enrollment, stationary or rejection-corrected variants, uniform approximation rates, superpopulation claims, and finite-sample exactness. The positive diffusion theorem does not extend beyond the regular subclass; only the logical refutation of a universal uniform-acceptable-set representation transfers to the broader published class by subclass inclusion.
