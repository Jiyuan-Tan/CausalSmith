\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}selfnormal\_\allowbreak{}mixedkernel\_\allowbreak{}complete}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The field-tier contribution remains a complete denominator audit for primitive coprime reduced rational causal certificates supported on three covariance variables. The rigidity mechanism is now proved in every matrix dimension on the corank-one branch: adjugate translation needs no homogeneity, and homogeneity plus positive-gradient cone order forces a fixed global kernel line and compression whenever the gradient has rank \(d-1\) somewhere in the positive-definite cone. This closes Tamano's mixed \(\operatorname{Sym}_3\) branch without degree or nonresonance restrictions and isolates the higher-dimensional open problem to generic rank at most \(d-2\), where the adjugate vanishes. The resulting \(\operatorname{Sym}_3\) classification and terminating rational recognizer decide exactly when a reduced half-trek or related certificate has one nested flag denominator; no full higher-dimensional converse or new coverage theorem is claimed.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Tamano leaves the arbitrary-degree mixed rank-two kernel line unresolved in \(\operatorname{Sym}_3\), and the all-dimensional converse assumes rather than derives a common invariant flag. The corank-one kernel obstruction can in fact be removed in every dimension, while the higher-corank branch remains open.

\paragraph{Niche.} The operative algebraic condition is determinant singularity of the trace-pairing symmetric-gradient matrix. Hessian symmetry turns its adjugate into an exact polynomial translation direction in every dimension; positive-cone order then globalizes a corank-one null direction. Coordinate-Hessian geometry and homogeneous-cone automorphisms concern different matrices or stronger supplied structure.

\paragraph{Fill.} The dimension-free rigidity theorem proves a fixed kernel line and compression for every positive-gradient corank-one branch. Its \(d=3\) specialization closes Tamano's remaining mixed branch, completes the nested-flag classification, and makes exact rational recognition and denominator-only Wishart pivot auditing exhaustive for qualifying three-direction primitive reduced rational certificates.

\section{Related work}

Tamano's Theorem 1 \cite{Tamano2026SelfNormalizing} proves all-dimensional flag-power sufficiency and the exact flag-power Wishart pivot. Theorem 3 proves the three-variable reduction, including determinant stripping, spectral reduction, and the pure branches, while Remark 4 isolates constancy of the mixed kernel line as the missing three-dimensional step. The present \(\operatorname{Sym}_3\) theorem closes that branch, and the dimension-free extension additionally proves the fixed-kernel conclusion for every positive-gradient corank-one branch; it does not settle higher-corank branches or furnish a full all-dimensional converse. Tamano's Example 2 gives the background three-node front-door benchmark, with the reduced form in equation (22), the covariance functional \[\tau(\Sigma)=\frac{\sigma_{AM}(\sigma_{AA}\sigma_{MY}-\sigma_{AM}\sigma_{AY})}{\sigma_{AA}(\sigma_{AA}\sigma_{MM}-\sigma_{AM}^{2})}\] in equation (23), and the denominator identity \(D=\Delta_1\Delta_2\) and \(c=10\) in equation (24); Theorem 1 yields \(m=(2,1,0)\) and the denominator pivot \(D(\widehat\Sigma)/D(\Sigma)\stackrel{d}{=}(\chi_n^2/n)^2(\chi_{n-1}^2/n)\). In that benchmark, \(\gamma\) is the reduced-form association generated by unobserved treatment--outcome confounding rather than a direct causal effect, and \(\tau=\alpha\beta\) is the total effect. These benchmark identities are cited background, not solve targets or new claims. Foygel--Draisma--Drton \cite{FoygelDraismaDrton2012HalfTrek} supplies the named family of rational linear-Gaussian causal certificates to which the denominator audit applies after primitive cancellation. García-Puente--Spielvogel--Sullivant \cite{GarciaPuenteSpielvogelSullivant2010ComputerAlgebra} motivates exact certificate-producing causal algebra, and Bareiss \cite{Bareiss1968SylvesterIdentity} supplies exact elimination operations. De Bondt \cite{DeBondt2017QuasiTranslations} informs only the finite translation mechanism and does not derive the adjugate direction from a symmetric gradient. Ciliberto--Russo--Simis \cite{CilibertoRussoSimis2008Homaloidal} and Gondim--Russo \cite{GondimRusso2015CubicHessian} study coordinate Hessians, not the matrix-valued trace-pairing gradient used here. Faraut--Korányi \cite{FarautKoranyi1994SymmetricCones} contextualize nested determinant powers. Tunçel--Vandenberghe \cite{TuncelVandenberghe2023HomogeneousCones} derive recursive structures from homogeneous-cone automorphisms; this adjacent mechanism does not settle whether self-normalization alone forces a flag in the remaining higher-corank branch.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:d-nonzero]\label{ass:d-nonzero}
\(D\not\equiv0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonzero polynomial condition, \cite{Tamano2026SelfNormalizing}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:d-nonconstant]\label{ass:d-nonconstant}
\(D\notin\mathbb R\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonconstant denominator condition, \cite{Tamano2026SelfNormalizing}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:d-homogeneous]\label{ass:d-homogeneous}
\(D(tX)=t^K D(X)\) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\) and \(X\in S3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Polynomial homogeneity of degree \(K\), \cite{Tamano2026SelfNormalizing}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:d-selfnormal]\label{ass:d-selfnormal}
\(2\operatorname{tr}\{(G_D(X)X)^2\}=cD(X)^2\) for every \(X\in S3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact Gaussian self-normalization, \cite{Tamano2026SelfNormalizing}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-sampling]\label{ass:gaussian-sampling}
\(X_1,\ldots,X_n\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N_3(0,\Sigma)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known-zero-mean Gaussian covariance sampling, \cite{Muirhead1982Multivariate}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:positive-gradient-kernel-rigidity]\label{thm:positive-gradient-kernel-rigidity}
Let \(P\) be homogeneous of degree \(r\geq1\). If \(G_P(X)\succeq0\) for every \(X\in C3\), \(\det G_P\equiv0\) in \(\mathbb R[S3]\), and \(\operatorname{rank}G_P(X_0)=2\) for some \(X_0\in C3\), then there exists \(u\in\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}\) such that \(G_P(X)u=0\) identically on \(S3\). Consequently, after a linear change of coordinates, \(P\) is a polynomial in one \(2\times2\) symmetric compression of \(X\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the all-dimensional positive-gradient kernel-rigidity theorem with \(d=3\), \(S_d=S3\), and \(C_d=C3\).  The present hypotheses say respectively that \(P\) is homogeneous of degree \(r\geq1\), that \(G_P\) is positive semidefinite on \(C3\), that \(\det G_P\equiv0\), and that \(G_P\) has rank \(3-1=2\) at \(X_0\in C3\).  The all-dimensional theorem consequently supplies \(u\in\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}\) with
\[
 G_P(X)u=0
\]
identically on \(S3\).  Its compression conclusion says, after an orthogonal change of coordinates sending the last coordinate vector to a multiple of \(u\), that \(P\) depends only on the symmetric compression to \(u^\perp\).  Since \(\dim u^\perp=2\), this is exactly one \(2\times2\) symmetric compression, as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The three-dimensional theorem is retained as the direct specialization used by the complete denominator classification. \textit{Gap.} The operative determinant is that of the trace-pairing symmetric-gradient matrix, not a coordinate Hessian; the new all-dimensional theorem supplies the applicable rigidity result. \textit{Consumer.} The complete \(\operatorname{Sym}_3\) denominator classification applies this specialization to the signed mixed residue.

\begin{lemma}[lem:tamano-mixed-residue-bridge]\label{lem:tamano-mixed-residue-bridge}
Let \(D\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{SN},3}\), construct \(E,s,F\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}signed\text{-}mixed\text{-}residue\), and suppose Tamano's determinant-stripped reduction places \(E\) in the mixed branch: \(E(X)\neq0\) on \(C3\), and for some integers \(a,b\geq1\), \(\operatorname{spec}\{G_E(X)X/E(X)\}=\{a+b,b,0\}\) for every \(X\in C3\). Then \(F(tX)=t^LF(X)\) with \(L=K-3e\geq1\), \(F(X)>0\) and \(G_F(X)\succeq0\) for every \(X\in C3\), \(\det G_F\equiv0\) in \(\mathbb R[S3]\), and \(\operatorname{rank}G_F(X)=2\) for every \(X\in C3\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The cited three-dimensional reduction supplies the mixed-branch hypotheses appearing in the statement; the remaining assertions are derived here.  Maximal divisibility \(D=\delta^eE\), homogeneity of \(D\) of degree \(K\), and homogeneity of \(\delta\) of degree three imply that \(E\), and hence \(F=sE\), is homogeneous of degree
\[
 L=K-3e.
\]
The cone \(C3\) is connected, \(E\) is continuous, and the mixed-branch premise says that \(E\) has no zero on \(C3\).  Therefore \(E\) has constant sign there.  Since \(s=\operatorname{sign}E(I3)\),
\[
 F(X)=sE(X)>0 \qquad(X\in C3). \tag{7}
\]
Euler's identity under the trace-pairing gradient convention is
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(G_E(X)X)=L E(X).
\]
Taking the trace of the assumed relative spectrum \(\{a+b,b,0\}\) gives \(L=a+2b\), so in particular \(L\geq1\).

For \(X\in C3\), define the symmetric matrix
\[
 B(X)=\frac{X^{1/2}G_E(X)X^{1/2}}{E(X)}.
\]
It is similar to \(G_E(X)X/E(X)\), because
\[
 B(X)=X^{1/2}\frac{G_E(X)X}{E(X)}X^{-1/2}.
\]
Hence \(B(X)\) has eigenvalues \(a+b,b,0\).  The conditions \(a,b\geq1\) show that \(B(X)\succeq0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}B(X)=2\).  Since \(G_F=sG_E\) and \(F=sE\), equation (7) yields the congruence
\[
 G_F(X)=F(X)X^{-1/2}B(X)X^{-1/2}\succeq0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}G_F(X)=2. \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(\det G_F(X)=0\) on the nonempty open set \(C3\).  Because \(\det G_F\) is a polynomial on \(S3\), polynomial continuation gives \(\det G_F\equiv0\) on \(S3\).  Equations (7)--(8), together with the homogeneity calculation, prove every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Sign normalization and the congruence \(X^{1/2}G_E(X)X^{1/2}/E(X)\) derive exactly the positivity, determinant-singularity, and rank hypotheses used by Theorem 2. \textit{Gap.} Tamano's Theorem 3 and its determinant-stripping and spectral-reduction arguments establish interior nonvanishing and the mixed relative spectrum; this bridge is needed to transfer those source conclusions to the half-weighted symmetric gradient \(G_F\). \textit{Consumer.} Theorem 4 can invoke kernel rigidity for the actual mixed residue rather than for an auxiliary polynomial supplied by assumption.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sym3-complete-classification]\label{thm:sym3-complete-classification}
For a polynomial \(D\) satisfying Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}d\text{-}nonzero\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}d\text{-}nonconstant\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}d\text{-}homogeneous\), the identity \(2\operatorname{tr}\{(G_D(X)X)^2\}=cD(X)^2\) holds on \(S3\) if and only if there exist \(V1\subset V2\), \(a,b,e\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) with \(a+b+e>0\), and \(\lambda_0\neq0\) such that \(D=D_{\mathrm{flag}}\). In that representation, \(c=2\{(a+b+e)^2+(b+e)^2+e^2\}\), and every active flag subspace is unique.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume first the self-normalization identity with \(c>0\).  Assumptions
\(\mathrm{ass{:}d\text{-}nonzero}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}d\text{-}nonconstant}\),
and \(\mathrm{ass{:}d\text{-}homogeneous}\), together with the displayed
identity, place \(D\) in \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{SN},3}\).  Write
\(D=\delta^eE\), where \(e\) is maximal.  The cited three-dimensional
reduction \(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}sym3\text{-}reduction}\) classifies
the constant, line, and pure-plane residue branches as, respectively,
\(\lambda_0\delta^e\), \(\lambda_0\Delta_1^a\delta^e\), and
\(\lambda_0\Delta_2^b\delta^e\).  In each pure branch an unused flag
space may be chosen so that \(V1\subset V2\).

It remains to treat the mixed branch.  The cited reduction supplies
integers \(a,b\geq1\), interior nonvanishing of \(E\), and
\[
 \operatorname{spec}\!\left(\frac{G_E(X)X}{E(X)}\right)
 =\{a+b,b,0\}
 \qquad (X\in C3).                                                    \tag{9}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}mixed\text{-}residue\text{-}bridge}\),
the signed residue \(F=sE\) is homogeneous of degree \(L\geq1\),
\(G_F(X)\succeq0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}G_F(X)=2\) for every
\(X\in C3\), and \(\det G_F\equiv0\).  Therefore
\(\mathrm{thm{:}positive\text{-}gradient\text{-}kernel\text{-}rigidity}\)
gives a fixed \(u\neq0\) such that \(G_F(X)u=0\) identically on \(S3\).
After an orthogonal change of coordinates carrying \(u\) to the third
coordinate direction, the compression conclusion of that theorem says
that \(F(X)=f(Y)\), where \(Y\) is the leading \(2\times2\) symmetric
block of \(X\).

Let \(Y\succ0\) and insert \(X=\operatorname{diag}(Y,z)\), with
\(z>0\), into (9).  Since \(F=sE\), the relative-gradient matrices for
\(E\) and \(F\) coincide.  Hence
\[
 2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(G_F(X)X)^2\right\}
 =2\bigl((a+b)^2+b^2\bigr)F(X)^2.                                   \tag{10}
\]
Both sides are polynomials in \(X\).  The cone \(C3\) is nonempty and
open in \(S3\), so equality on \(C3\) implies polynomial equality on
all of \(S3\).  In the chosen coordinates the last row and column of
\(G_F\) vanish.  Thus (10), restricted to block-diagonal \(X\), is
exactly
\[
 2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(G_f(Y)Y)^2\right\}
 =2\bigl((a+b)^2+b^2\bigr)f(Y)^2
\]
on \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\).  The polynomial \(f\) is
nonzero, nonconstant, and homogeneous: nonzero follows from positivity
of \(F\) on \(C3\), nonconstancy follows from \(L\geq1\), and
homogeneity is inherited from \(F\).  The cited converse
\(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}sym2\text{-}converse}\) therefore yields
\[
 f(Y)=\lambda_0\det(CYC^{\top})^{a'}\det(Y)^{b'}
\]
for a nonzero row vector \(C\), integers \(a',b'\geq0\), and
\(a'+b'>0\).  Direct differentiation of this two-dimensional flag
power gives ordered relative eigenvalues \(a'+b',b'\).  Comparison with
(9) gives \(a'=a\) and \(b'=b\).  Returning to the original coordinates
identifies \(V2=u^{\perp}\) and the line \(V1\subset V2\) represented by
\(C\).  Restoring the stripped determinant proves
\[
 D(X)=\lambda_0\Delta_1(X)^a\Delta_2(X)^b\delta(X)^e.                \tag{11}
\]

Conversely, \(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}flag\text{-}sufficiency}\)
applied to (11) gives
\[
 2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(G_D(X)X)^2\right\}
 =2\bigl((a+b+e)^2+(b+e)^2+e^2\bigr)D(X)^2.                         \tag{12}
\]
The condition \(a+b+e>0\) makes the displayed constant positive and
makes \(D\) nonconstant.  Since \(D\not\equiv0\), the polynomial
\(D^2\) is nonzero; consequently comparison of (12) with an identity
having constant \(c\) forces
\[
 c=2\bigl((a+b+e)^2+(b+e)^2+e^2\bigr).
\]

It remains to verify active-space uniqueness.  The polynomial ring in
the six independent entries of \(X\) is a unique-factorization domain.
After an invertible congruence change of variables, a line minor is a
nonzero scalar multiple of \(x_{11}\), a plane minor is a nonzero
scalar multiple of \(x_{11}x_{22}-x_{12}^2\), and the full determinant
is \(\delta\).  The first two polynomials are irreducible.  For the last,
view \(\delta\) as a polynomial of degree one in \(x_{33}\).  Its
coefficient is \(x_{11}x_{22}-x_{12}^2\).  A proper factor independent
of \(x_{33}\) would divide both this coefficient and the constant term;
the coefficient does not divide the constant term, as the specialization
\(x_{11}=1\), \(x_{12}=x_{22}=0\) shows.  Thus \(\delta\) is
irreducible.  Unique factorization now identifies each factor having a
positive exponent up to a nonzero scalar.  Associate line minors have
proportional rank-one coefficient matrices and hence determine the same
line.  A plane minor is, up to nonzero scale,
\(v^{\top}\operatorname{adj}(X)v\), where \(v\) spans the orthogonal
complement of the plane.  Equality up to scale of two such polynomials
forces the corresponding rank-one matrices \(vv^{\top}\) to be
proportional and therefore determines the same plane.  Hence every
active flag space is unique; no uniqueness is claimed for an inactive
space.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This degree-unrestricted converse is the algebraic engine for a complete audit of reduced three-variable rational causal denominators. \textit{Gap.} Tamano's Theorem 1 proves flag sufficiency, while Theorem 3 supplies determinant stripping, spectral reduction, and the pure branches; Theorem 3 and Remark 4 leave the mixed kernel line open, and Lemma 3 together with Theorem 2 closes exactly that branch. \textit{Consumer.} The reduced-denominator audit applies this iff classification directly on three compressed directions and, through Tamano's ambient-compression result, to arbitrary-dimensional rational covariance certificates supported on at most three fixed directions.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rational-recognition]\label{thm:rational-recognition}
For a finite rational coefficient encoding \(D\in\mathbb Q[S3]\), the procedure \(\mathcal A_D\) terminates and accepts exactly when \(D\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{SN},3}\). It rejects before forming \(J_D\) whenever \(D(I3)=0\). On acceptance, the ordered eigenvalues of \(J_D\) are \(a+b+e,b+e,e\), and \(CertD\) contains rational bases for the active spaces, the exponents, \(\lambda_0\), \(c\), and a zero coefficient residual. On rejection, \(CertD\) contains an exact failed guard, a forced nonpositive constant, or a nonzero residual coefficient. No bit-complexity bound is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All initial operations in \(\mathcal A_D\) are finite exact operations
on rational coefficient arrays.  After the zero, constant, and
homogeneity guards, set
\[
 T=2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(G_D(X)X)^2\right\},
 \qquad R=D^2.
\]
Because \(D\not\equiv0\), some monomial has a nonzero coefficient
\(p\) in \(R\).  Let \(t\) be its coefficient in \(T\).  If
\(T=cR\), coefficient comparison forces the unique candidate
\(c_0=t/p\).  Thus \(c_0\leq0\) is an exact rejection witness.  If
\(c_0>0\), any nonzero coefficient of \(T-c_0R\) is an exact rejection
witness.  If this residual vanishes, the guarded polynomial belongs to
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{SN},3}\), so
\(\mathrm{thm{:}sym3\text{-}complete\text{-}classification}\) applies.

Every compression determinant in a classified flag form is positive at
\(I3\), and \(\lambda_0\neq0\); hence \(D(I3)\neq0\).  It follows that
a true member never fails the next guard.  Conversely, if the exact
guard \(D(I3)=0\) fires after the identity test, the classification
shows that the input is not a member.  It is therefore legitimate to
form the rational symmetric matrix
\[
 J_D=\frac{G_D(I3)}{D(I3)}.
\]
For a full-row-rank matrix \(C\), differentiation gives
\[
 G_{\log\det(CXC^{\top})}(I3)
 =C^{\top}(CC^{\top})^{-1}C
 =\Pi_{\operatorname{row}(C)},                                      \tag{13}
\]
the orthogonal projection onto its row space.  Therefore a classified
flag form satisfies
\[
 J_D=a\Pi_{V1}+b\Pi_{V2}+eI_3.                                      \tag{14}
\]
The nested projections commute.  On the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 \mathbb R^3
 =V1\oplus(V2\cap V1^{\perp})\oplus V2^{\perp},
\]
the ordered eigenvalues of (14) are
\[
 \lambda_1=a+b+e,
 \qquad \lambda_2=b+e,
 \qquad \lambda_3=e.                                                \tag{15}
\]
Consequently exact subtraction recovers
\[
 a=\lambda_1-\lambda_2,
 \qquad b=\lambda_2-\lambda_3,
 \qquad e=\lambda_3.                                                \tag{16}
\]
These are nonnegative integers for every member.  Since \(J_D\) is
rational and the eigenvalues in (15) are integers, every active
eigenspace is the rational nullspace of a rational matrix.  Exact
Gaussian elimination therefore returns rational bases.  If \(a=0\), a
rational line may be chosen inside the recovered rational plane; if
\(b=0\), a rational plane may be chosen containing the recovered
rational line; if \(a=b=0\), any rational complete flag may be chosen.
Only inactive entries are affected by these choices.

For the recovered bases define
\[
 \lambda_0
 =\frac{D(I3)}{\Delta_1(I3)^a\Delta_2(I3)^b},
\]
using \(\delta(I3)^e=1\).  Full row rank makes both denominators in
this expression positive, so \(\lambda_0\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}\).
Exact expansion of \(D-D_{\mathrm{flag}}\) checks finitely many
coefficients.  Classification and active-space uniqueness imply that
this residual is zero for every member.  Conversely, a zero residual
directly verifies the flag form, and
\(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}flag\text{-}sufficiency}\) verifies
membership, so false acceptance is impossible.  Equation (15) also
gives the exact constant
\[
 c=2\bigl(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2+\lambda_3^2\bigr).
\]
Each branch uses only differentiation and arithmetic on finitely many
rational coefficients, factorization of a cubic characteristic
polynomial, rational nullspace computation, and finite coefficient
comparison.  Hence the procedure terminates.  Its failed guard or
coefficient, or on acceptance the data (15)--(16), rational active
bases, \(\lambda_0\), \(c\), and the zero residual, is exactly the
claimed certificate.  No bit-complexity assertion has been used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite exact-input contract turns the classification into an auditable recognition and flag-recovery certificate. \textit{Gap.} Tamano's factor-and-rank diagnostic is inconclusive for determinant-free irreducible factors of degree at least three; exact-algebra sources supply operations but no self-normalization classifier. \textit{Consumer.} A symbolic half-trek pipeline can certify the denominator class without altering the primitive reduced fraction.

\begin{proposition}[prop:reduced-denominator-audit]\label{prop:reduced-denominator-audit}
Let \(q\geq1\), let \(1\leq k\leq\min(q,3)\), let
\(B\in\mathbb R^{k\times q}\) have full row rank, and let \(\bar D\)
be a nonzero, nonconstant homogeneous polynomial on
\(\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R)\).  Put
\(D_B(A)=\bar D(BAB^{\top})\), and suppose that
\(\tau_B(\Sigma_q)=N_B(\Sigma_q)/D_B(\Sigma_q)\) is an upstream-valid
primitive coprime reduced rational causal certificate.  Then \(D_B\) is
exactly self-normalizing on \(\operatorname{Sym}_q(\mathbb R)\) if and
only if \(\bar D\) is a product of nonnegative integer powers of
compression determinants along one nested flag in \(\mathbb R^k\),
with positive total exponent.  Equivalently, its canonical padding
\(\widetilde D(X)=\bar D(R_kXR_k^{\top})\) has the three-level flag form
classified by \(\mathrm{thm{:}sym3\text{-}complete\text{-}classification}\).
Pulling each compressed flag basis \(C_j\) back to \(C_jB\) gives the
corresponding ambient denominator factors.  If \(B\) and \(\bar D\)
are rational, applying \(\mathcal A_{\widetilde D}\) as certified by
\(\mathrm{thm{:}rational\text{-}recognition}\) decides this property and
returns rational compressed and ambient active-flag bases, the
exponents, scale, constant, and coefficient residual.

If, in addition, \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) are independent
\(N_q(0,\Sigma_q)\) observations with \(\Sigma_q\succ0\), and
\(\widehat\Sigma_q=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_iZ_i^{\top}\), then acceptance gives
\[
 \frac{D_B(\widehat\Sigma_q)}{D_B(\Sigma_q)}
 \stackrel{d}{=}
 \prod_{j=1}^{k}
 \left(\frac{\chi^2_{n-j+1}}{n}\right)^{m_j}
 =W_m,
\]
where the chi-squared factors are independent and \(m_j=0\) for
\(j>k\).  Consequently the ambient denominator confidence set satisfies
\[
 \Pr_{\Sigma_q}\!\left\{D_B(\Sigma_q)
 \in\mathcal I_{D_B}^{(q)}(\eta)\right\}=1-\eta.
\]
The classification, recognition certificate, pivot, and confidence set
concern only the primitive denominator.  They neither modify the reduced
fraction nor give an inference statement for the distinct causal ratio
\(\tau_B\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the full-row-rank hypothesis on \(B\), the compression map
\(A\mapsto BAB^{\top}\) from
\(\operatorname{Sym}_q(\mathbb R)\) onto
\(\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R)\) is surjective.  Indeed, if
\(R=B^{\top}(BB^{\top})^{-1}\), then \(BR=I_k\), and
\(A=RYR^{\top}\) maps to any prescribed symmetric \(Y\).  Hence
pullback is injective on polynomials: the stated nonzero, nonconstant,
and homogeneity properties of \(\bar D\) pass to \(D_B\).
Primitivity and coprimality are premises of the upstream certificate and
fix its denominator up to a nonzero common scalar.  None of the following
operations multiplies an additional factor into both \(N_B\) and
\(D_B\).

The first assertion of
\(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}ambient\text{-}compression}\) gives
\[
 D_B\text{ is exactly self-normalizing with constant }c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bar D\text{ is exactly self-normalizing with constant }c.          \tag{17}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}canonical\text{-}three\text{-}padding}\), the
right-hand side of (17) is equivalent to the canonical padding
\(\widetilde D\) having the three-level flag form supplied by
\(\mathrm{thm{:}sym3\text{-}complete\text{-}classification}\), and it
is also equivalent to a nested flag factorization of \(\bar D\) on
\(\mathbb R^k\).  If \(C_j\) is a full-row-rank basis of the
\(j\)-dimensional compressed flag space, then \(C_jB\) has row rank
\(j\): multiplication on the right by the right inverse \(R\) gives
\((C_jB)R=C_j\).  Substitution therefore yields the ambient factors
\[
 \det\!\left(C_jB A B^{\top}C_j^{\top}\right),
\]
and nesting is preserved because all row spaces are pulled back by the
same injective map \(v\mapsto vB\).

If \(B\) and \(\bar D\) are rational, then \(R_k\),
\(\widetilde D\), and every pulled-back accepted basis are rational.
The canonical-padding lemma and
\(\mathrm{thm{:}rational\text{-}recognition}\) show that
\(\mathcal A_{\widetilde D}\) terminates, accepts exactly when (17)
holds, and returns the claimed exact data.  The exact coefficient
residual for \(\widetilde D\), followed by substitution through \(B\),
is also an exact residual certificate for \(D_B\).  This decision is
made from the supplied primitive denominator representation and does
not alter the numerator or denominator.

Now impose the stated Gaussian sampling premise.  Full row rank of
\(B\) and \(\Sigma_q\succ0\) imply
\(\Omega=B\Sigma_qB^{\top}\succ0\), because for \(v\neq0\),
\[
 v^{\top}\Omega v=(B^{\top}v)^{\top}\Sigma_q(B^{\top}v)>0.
\]
The vectors \(BZ_i\) are independent \(N_k(0,\Omega)\), and their
sample covariance is
\(\widehat\Omega=B\widehat\Sigma_qB^{\top}\).  The sampling part of
\(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}ambient\text{-}compression}\) and the
accepted nested flag form therefore give
\[
 \frac{D_B(\widehat\Sigma_q)}{D_B(\Sigma_q)}
 =\frac{\bar D(\widehat\Omega)}{\bar D(\Omega)}
 \stackrel{d}{=}
 \prod_{j=1}^{k}
 \left(\frac{\chi^2_{n-j+1}}{n}\right)^{m_j}.                       \tag{18}
\]
Because \(k\leq3\) and \(n\geq4\), every displayed chi-squared degree
of freedom is positive.  The padding lemma gives \(m_j=0\) for
\(j>k\), so the last product equals \(W_m\).  The positive total flag
exponent gives \(m_1>0\).  Consequently \(W_m\) is positive and has a
continuous distribution, and its lower and upper quantiles have tail
probabilities \(\eta/2\).  Also every compression determinant is
positive at \(\Omega\), so \(D_B(\Sigma_q)=\bar D(\Omega)\neq0\).
Substituting \(d_0=D_B(\Sigma_q)\) into the definition of
\(\mathcal I_{D_B}^{(q)}(\eta)\) and using (18) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\Pr_{\Sigma_q}\!\left\{D_B(\Sigma_q)
       \in\mathcal I_{D_B}^{(q)}(\eta)\right\} \\
 &\quad=\Pr\!\left\{q_m(\eta/2)\leq W_m
       \leq q_m(1-\eta/2)\right\}=1-\eta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The random ratio in (18) and the confidence set target only the
denominator value \(D_B(\Sigma_q)\).  No step establishes a sampling
law or confidence statement for the distinct causal functional
\(\tau_B(\Sigma_q)=N_B(\Sigma_q)/D_B(\Sigma_q)\), which proves the final
disclaimer and completes the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:all-dimensional-adjugate-translation]\label{lem:all-dimensional-adjugate-translation}
Let \(d\geq2\), let \(S_d=\operatorname{Sym}_{d}(\mathbb R)\), and let \(P:S_d\to\mathbb R\) be a polynomial, with no homogeneity assumption.  Define its trace-pairing gradient by \(\mathrm dP_X(H)=\operatorname{tr}\{G_P(X)H\}\), and put \(Q_P(X)=\operatorname{adj}G_P(X)\).  If \(\det G_P\equiv0\), then
\[
 \mathrm dG_{P,X}\bigl(Q_P(X)\bigr)=0.
\]
Moreover, for every \(X\in S_d\) and \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 G_P\bigl(X+tQ_P(X)\bigr)=G_P(X),\qquad
 Q_P\bigl(X+tQ_P(X)\bigr)=Q_P(X),\qquad
 P\bigl(X+tQ_P(X)\bigr)=P(X).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(G=G_P\) and \(Q=Q_P\).  The trace pairing \((A,B)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(AB)\) is nondegenerate on \(S_d\).  Since \(G\) is the trace-pairing gradient of the polynomial \(P\), symmetry of the second derivative gives, for every \(H,J\in S_d\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathrm dG_X(H)J\bigr)
 =\mathrm d^2P_X(H,J)
 =\mathrm d^2P_X(J,H)
 =\operatorname{tr}\bigl(H\,\mathrm dG_X(J)\bigr). \tag{1}
\]
In coordinates, (1) includes the half-weight convention for off-diagonal entries: differentiating with respect to the coordinate \(x_{ij}\), \(i<j\), gives twice the \((i,j)\) entry of the trace-pairing gradient.

For arbitrary square matrices \(A,B\), differentiation of the Leibniz formula for the determinant gives the polynomial identity
\[
 \mathrm d(\det)_A(B)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(A)B\bigr), \tag{2}
\]
including when \(A\) is singular.  Apply (2) to \(A=G(X)\), \(B=\mathrm dG_X(H)\).  The hypothesis \(\det G\equiv0\), followed by (1) with \(J=Q(X)\), yields
\[
 0=\mathrm d(\det G)_X(H)
 =\operatorname{tr}\bigl(Q(X)\,\mathrm dG_X(H)\bigr)
 =\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathrm dG_X(Q(X))H\bigr).
\]
Since this holds for every \(H\in S_d\), nondegeneracy of the trace pairing implies
\[
 \mathrm dG_X(Q(X))=0. \tag{3}
\]

Define the polynomial derivation \(\mathcal L\) by \((\mathcal Lh)(X)=\mathrm dh_X(Q(X))\), acting entrywise on matrix-valued polynomials.  Equation (3), the polynomial chain rule for the adjugate, and the identity \(GQ=(\det G)I_d\) give
\[
 \mathcal LG=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal LQ=\mathrm d(\operatorname{adj})_{G}(\mathcal LG)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal LP=\operatorname{tr}(GQ)=d\det G=0. \tag{4}
\]
For every scalar polynomial \(h\) and integer \(k\geq0\), induction using \(\mathcal LQ=0\) shows that
\[
 \mathcal L^kh(X)=\mathrm d^kh_X\bigl(Q(X),\ldots,Q(X)\bigr). \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, applying \(\mathcal L\) to the right side of (5) gives the next directional derivative, together with \(k\) terms each containing \(\mathrm dQ_X(Q(X))=\mathcal LQ(X)=0\).  Thus (4)--(5) imply that every positive-order directional derivative in the direction \(Q(X)\) vanishes for each entry of \(G\), each entry of \(Q\), and for \(P\).  Because these are polynomials, their Taylor series in that direction are finite.  Hence, for every real \(t\),
\[
 G(X+tQ(X))=G(X),
 \qquad
 Q(X+tQ(X))=Q(X),
 \qquad
 P(X+tQ(X))=P(X).
\]
This proves all assertions without a homogeneity hypothesis.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This dimension-free polynomial identity converts determinant singularity and symmetric-gradient integrability into exact finite adjugate-ray invariance without homogeneity. \textit{Gap.} De Bondt gives a general quasi-translation criterion, but does not derive this adjugate direction from the trace-pairing gradient of a scalar polynomial. \textit{Consumer.} The all-dimensional positive-gradient rigidity theorem uses the invariant ray through a corank-one positive-semidefinite gradient.

\begin{theorem}[thm:all-dimensional-positive-gradient-kernel-rigidity]\label{thm:all-dimensional-positive-gradient-kernel-rigidity}
Let \(d\geq2\), let \(S_d=\operatorname{Sym}_{d}(\mathbb R)\), and let \(C_d=\{X\in S_d:X\succ0\}\).  Suppose that \(P:S_d\to\mathbb R\) is a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(r\geq1\), with trace-pairing gradient \(G_P\).  If \(G_P(X)\succeq0\) for every \(X\in C_d\), \(\det G_P\equiv0\), and \(\operatorname{rank}G_P(X_0)=d-1\) for some \(X_0\in C_d\), then there is \(u\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\) such that \(G_P(X)u=0\) identically on \(S_d\).  After an orthogonal change of coordinates, \(P\) is a polynomial in the \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) symmetric compression to \(u^\perp\).  Consequently, under the first two displayed hypotheses, either this fixed-kernel conclusion holds or \(G_P\) has generic rank at most \(d-2\), in which case \(\operatorname{adj}G_P\equiv0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(G=G_P\).  First we derive cone monotonicity.  If \(A,B\in C_d\) and \(B-A\succeq0\), convexity of \(C_d\), the fundamental theorem of calculus, and the gradient hypothesis give
\[
 P(B)-P(A)
 =\int_0^1\operatorname{tr}\bigl(G(A+s(B-A))(B-A)\bigr)\,\mathrm ds
 \geq0. \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, both matrices in the trace integrand are positive semidefinite, so the integrand equals the trace of a positive-semidefinite congruence and is nonnegative.

Choose \(X_0\in C_d\) with \(\operatorname{rank}G(X_0)=d-1\).  Positive semidefiniteness implies that the \(d-1\) nonzero eigenvalues of \(G(X_0)\) are positive.  Therefore, for a unit vector \(u\) spanning \(\ker G(X_0)\),
\[
 Q_P(X_0)=\operatorname{adj}G(X_0)=\kappa uu^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \kappa>0, \tag{7}
\]
where \(\kappa\) is the product of those nonzero eigenvalues.  The all-dimensional adjugate-translation lemma and the substitution of its scalar parameter by \(t/\kappa\) give
\[
 P(X_0+tuu^{\mathsf T})=P(X_0)
 \qquad(t\in\mathbb R). \tag{8}
\]

Fix an arbitrary \(X\in C_d\).  Since \(X_0\succ0\), the finite number
\[
 \rho=\lambda_{\max}\bigl(X_0^{-1/2}XX_0^{-1/2}\bigr)
\]
satisfies \(X\preceq\rho X_0\).  Choose \(R\geq\max\{1,\rho\}\).  For \(t\geq0\), the matrices \(X\), \(X+tuu^{\mathsf T}\), and \(RX_0+tuu^{\mathsf T}\) are positive definite, and
\[
 X\preceq X+tuu^{\mathsf T}\preceq RX_0+tuu^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Apply (6), then homogeneity of degree \(r\), and finally (8), to obtain the two-sided cone-order bound
\[
 P(X)
 \leq P(X+tuu^{\mathsf T})
 \leq P(RX_0+tuu^{\mathsf T})
 =R^rP\left(X_0+\frac{t}{R}uu^{\mathsf T}\right)
 =R^rP(X_0). \tag{9}
\]
Thus the real polynomial \(t\mapsto P(X+tuu^{\mathsf T})\) is bounded above and below on \([0,\infty)\).  A nonconstant real polynomial diverges to one of \(+\infty\) or \(-\infty\) according to its leading term, so it cannot have both bounds.  The polynomial is therefore constant.  Differentiating at \(t=0\) and using the trace-pairing gradient gives
\[
 0=\mathrm dP_X(uu^{\mathsf T})
 =\operatorname{tr}\bigl(G(X)uu^{\mathsf T}\bigr)
 =u^{\mathsf T}G(X)u. \tag{10}
\]
Because \(G(X)\succeq0\), its positive-semidefinite square root exists, and (10) is \(\lVert G(X)^{1/2}u\rVert^2=0\).  Hence \(G(X)u=0\) for every \(X\in C_d\).  Each coordinate of the polynomial vector \(G(X)u\) vanishes on the nonempty open set \(C_d\), so the polynomial identity principle yields
\[
 G(X)u=0\qquad(X\in S_d). \tag{11}
\]

Choose an orthogonal matrix \(U\) with \(Ue_d=u\), replacing the unit representative of the kernel line if necessary, and define \(\widetilde P(Y)=P(UYU^{\mathsf T})\).  Its trace-pairing gradient is
\[
 G_{\widetilde P}(Y)=U^{\mathsf T}G(UYU^{\mathsf T})U.
\]
Equation (11) implies \(G_{\widetilde P}(Y)e_d=0\) identically.  Hence
\[
 \frac{\partial\widetilde P}{\partial y_{dd}}=0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\partial\widetilde P}{\partial y_{id}}=2\bigl(G_{\widetilde P}\bigr)_{id}=0
 \quad(1\leq i<d), \tag{12}
\]
where the factor two is exactly the trace-pairing convention for an off-diagonal symmetric coordinate.  A polynomial whose partial derivatives in these variables vanish is independent of them.  Thus \(\widetilde P\), and hence \(P\) after the orthogonal change of coordinates, depends only on the symmetric compression to \(u^\perp\).

It remains to justify the stated dichotomy.  Retain gradient positivity and \(\det G\equiv0\), but suppose no point of \(C_d\) has rank \(d-1\).  If some \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) minor of \(G\) were a nonzero polynomial, its nonvanishing set would be dense and open in \(S_d\), and would therefore meet the nonempty open cone \(C_d\).  At such a point the rank would be at least \(d-1\), while \(\det G=0\) would make it exactly \(d-1\), a contradiction.  All \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) minors consequently vanish identically.  Hence the generic rank is at most \(d-2\), and every entry of \(\operatorname{adj}G\), being a signed \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) minor, vanishes identically.  This proves the dichotomy and the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Adjugate-ray invariance and a two-sided Loewner comparison force one common kernel line in every dimension on the corank-one branch. \textit{Gap.} Existing common-flag converses assume invariant flag data; they do not derive a fixed kernel line from polynomiality, gradient positivity, and determinant singularity. \textit{Consumer.} The three-dimensional rigidity theorem is its direct specialization, and the higher-dimensional open question now begins only at generic rank at most \(d-2\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:tamano-ambient-compression]\label{lem:tamano-ambient-compression}
Let \(q\geq1\), \(1\leq k\leq\min(q,3)\), and let
\(B\in\mathbb R^{k\times q}\) have full row rank.  For a polynomial
\(\bar D\) on \(\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R)\), define
\(D_B(A)=\bar D(BAB^{\top})\) on
\(\operatorname{Sym}_q(\mathbb R)\).  Tamano's ambient-compression
invariance states that, for any \(c>0\),
\[
 2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(G_{D_B}(A)A)^2\right\}=cD_B(A)^2
 \quad\text{for every }A\in\operatorname{Sym}_q(\mathbb R)
\]
if and only if
\[
 2\operatorname{tr}\!\left\{(G_{\bar D}(Y)Y)^2\right\}=c\bar D(Y)^2
 \quad\text{for every }Y\in\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R),
\]
with the same constant \(c\).  Moreover, if
\(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) are independent \(N_q(0,\Sigma_q)\) vectors,
\(\widehat\Sigma_q=n^{-1}\sum_i Z_iZ_i^{\top}\),
\(\Omega=B\Sigma_qB^{\top}\), and
\(\widehat\Omega=B\widehat\Sigma_qB^{\top}\), then
\[
 \frac{D_B(\widehat\Sigma_q)}{D_B(\Sigma_q)}
 =\frac{\bar D(\widehat\Omega)}{\bar D(\Omega)}.
\]
If \(\bar D\) is a nested flag power on
\(\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R)\), with cumulative exponents
\(m_1\geq\cdots\geq m_k\geq0\), then the common ratio has law
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^{k}
 \left(\frac{\chi^2_{n-j+1}}{n}\right)^{m_j},
\]
with independent chi-squared factors.  Equivalently, after setting
\(m_j=0\) for \(k<j\leq3\), this law is \(W_m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:canonical-three-padding]\label{lem:canonical-three-padding}
Let \(1\leq k\leq3\), let
\(R_k=(I_k\;0)\in\mathbb Q^{k\times3}\), and let \(\bar D\) be a
nonzero, nonconstant homogeneous polynomial on
\(\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R)\).  Define
\(\widetilde D(X)=\bar D(R_kXR_k^{\top})\) on \(S3\).  Then
\(\widetilde D\) is nonzero, nonconstant, and homogeneous of the same
degree, and it is exactly self-normalizing if and only if \(\bar D\) is,
with the same constant.  It is exactly self-normalizing if and only if
it has the three-level flag form of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}sym3\text{-}complete\text{-}classification}\); in that
case every active flag space lies in
\(R_k^{\top}\mathbb R^k\), and the cumulative exponent vector
\(m=(m_1,m_2,m_3)\) satisfies \(m_j=0\) for \(j>k\).  If \(\bar D\)
has rational coefficients, then \(\widetilde D\) has rational
coefficients and \(\mathcal A_{\widetilde D}\) terminates and accepts
exactly in this case, returning rational bases for all active compressed
flag spaces and an exact certificate for \(\bar D\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The linear map
\[
 \pi_k:S3\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_k(\mathbb R),
 \qquad \pi_k(X)=R_kXR_k^{\top},
\]
is surjective: a right inverse sends \(Y\) to
\(\operatorname{diag}(Y,0_{3-k})\).  Therefore pullback by \(\pi_k\)
is injective on polynomial rings.  It follows immediately that
\(\widetilde D=\bar D\circ\pi_k\) is nonzero and nonconstant whenever
\(\bar D\) is, and
\[
 \widetilde D(tX)
 =\bar D(t\pi_k(X))
 =t^{\deg\bar D}\widetilde D(X),
\]
so its degree is unchanged.  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem{:}tamano\text{-}ambient\text{-}compression}\) with
compression matrix \(R_k\).  Its first equivalence proves exact
self-normalization of \(\widetilde D\) if and only if that of \(\bar D\),
with the same \(c\).  The three-dimensional classification then proves
the asserted flag-form equivalence.

Suppose the equivalent conditions hold.  At \(I3\), the trace-pairing
chain rule gives
\[
 G_{\widetilde D}(I3)
 =R_k^{\top}G_{\bar D}(I_k)R_k,
 \qquad
 \widetilde D(I3)=\bar D(I_k)\neq0.
\]
Thus the range of
\(J_{\widetilde D}=G_{\widetilde D}(I3)/\widetilde D(I3)\) is contained
in the coordinate subspace \(U_k=R_k^{\top}\mathbb R^k\), so
\(\operatorname{rank}J_{\widetilde D}\leq k\).  By
\(\mathrm{thm{:}rational\text{-}recognition}\), or directly by the
projection formula for a classified flag power, the ordered eigenvalues
of this matrix are \(m_1,m_2,m_3\), where
\(m_1\geq m_2\geq m_3\geq0\).  The rank bound forces
\(m_j=0\) for every \(j>k\).

Write the classified projection formula as
\(J_{\widetilde D}=a\Pi_{V1}+b\Pi_{V2}+eI_3\).  If \(e>0\), its range
is all of \(\mathbb R^3\); if \(b>0\), its range contains \(V2\); and
if \(a>0\), its range contains \(V1\).  Since the range is contained in
\(U_k\), every flag space with a positive exponent lies in \(U_k\).
Restricting its row basis to the first \(k\) coordinates therefore gives
a full-row-rank basis of a compressed flag in \(\mathbb R^k\).  Applying
the equality \(\widetilde D=D_{\mathrm{flag}}\) to
\(\operatorname{diag}(Y,0_{3-k})\) recovers the corresponding nested
flag factorization of \(\bar D(Y)\).  Only active compressed flag spaces lie in \(\mathbb R^k\); inactive ambient flag entries can be completed arbitrarily without changing the polynomial.

Finally, if \(\bar D\) is rational then composition with the rational
matrix \(R_k\) makes \(\widetilde D\) rational.  The exact recognizer
therefore terminates and decides its membership.  On acceptance its
active eigenspaces are rational nullspaces.  Because they lie in the
rational coordinate subspace \(U_k\), restriction to the first \(k\)
coordinates gives rational compressed bases.  The recovered exponents,
scale, constant, and exact coefficient equality then form an exact
certificate for \(\bar D\).  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

For \(S_d\), the trace-pairing gradient uses \((G_P)_{ij}=\tfrac12\partial P/\partial x_{ij}\) for \(i<j\). Polynomiality is essential: the smooth homogeneous Schur-complement function \(y+z-(u^2+v^2)/x\) has a positive-semidefinite rank-two gradient with a moving kernel. Positivity is also essential: \(xu+v^2\) has a singular polynomial gradient with a moving kernel but fails self-normalization. In generic corank at least two, every \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) minor and hence the adjugate vanish identically, so the present translation proof supplies no direction; no lower-cofactor replacement is proved.
