%%% FIELD proof-thm-1
Fix measurable versions of all conditional laws and fix \(x\) outside the common \(P_X\)-null set on which the assumptions may fail.  Abbreviate
\[
A_0=M_A(x),\qquad Z_0=M_Z(x),\qquad \Pi=\operatorname{diag}\{\pi(x)\},
\qquad \gamma_j=\Gamma_Y(x)_{\cdot j}.
\]
Conditional three-view independence in \(\mathcal M_{K,\varepsilon}\) gives, for the observable matrices in the statement,
\[
S=A_0\Pi Z_0^{\mathsf T},\qquad
T_u=A_0\Pi\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{u1},\ldots,\gamma_{uK})Z_0^{\mathsf T},
\quad u=1,\ldots,K. \tag{1}
\]
The latent-positivity, surrogate-rank, proxy-rank, and outcome-feature-rank assumptions make \(\Pi,A_0,Z_0,\Gamma_Y(x)\) nonsingular.  Thus \(S\) is nonsingular, and (1) implies
\[
T_uS^{-1}=A_0\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{u1},\ldots,\gamma_{uK})A_0^{-1}. \tag{2}
\]
Because \(\Gamma_Y(x)\) is nonsingular, its rows span \(\mathbb R^K\).  Hence the diagonal matrices on the right of (2) span the entire diagonal algebra.  The common one-dimensional invariant subspaces of that algebra are precisely the coordinate axes, so the common one-dimensional invariant subspaces of the observable family in (2) are
\(\operatorname{span}(A_{0,\cdot j})\), \(j=1,\ldots,K\).  This recovers the columns of \(A_0\) up to separate nonzero scales and one common permutation.  Each column is a probability vector and satisfies \(\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}A_{0,\cdot j}=1\), which fixes its scale (including its sign).

Set \(H=A_0^{-1}S\).  Since the columns of \(Z_0\) are probability vectors,
\[
H=\Pi Z_0^{\mathsf T},\qquad
H\mathbf 1_K=\Pi Z_0^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_K=\pi(x),\qquad
Z_0^{\mathsf T}=\Pi^{-1}H. \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\pi(x)\) and \(Z_0\) are recovered with the same permutation.  Returning to (1) then gives
\[
\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{u1},\ldots,\gamma_{uK})
=\Pi^{-1}A_0^{-1}T_u(Z_0^{\mathsf T})^{-1}, \tag{4}
\]
so \(\Gamma_Y(x)\) is recovered as well.

This recovery is measurable.  Indeed, choose from a fixed countable dense subset of \(\mathbb R^K\) the first vector \(r\) for which \(\sum_u r_uT_uS^{-1}\) has distinct eigenvalues.  Such an \(r\) exists because the columns of \(\Gamma_Y(x)\) are distinct, and the event that a candidate has distinct eigenvalues is Borel.  Eigenlines, inversion on the nonsingular matrices, and column normalization have Borel versions on this set.  The modal-label margin assigns each recovered column to the unique row where its largest entry occurs; the positive gap makes this permutation unique and measurable.  Hence the preceding display is an observed-law recovery map, not a definition of the latent factors.

Let \(p_A(y\mid x)\) be the observable \(K\)-vector whose \(a\)-th entry is the conditional joint density of \((Y,A=a)\) given \(X=x\).  Domination and conditional independence give, for \(\nu\)-almost every \(y\),
\[
p_A(y\mid x)=A_0\Pi f(y\mid x),\qquad
f(y\mid x)=\Pi^{-1}A_0^{-1}p_A(y\mid x). \tag{5}
\]
Every division and inversion in (2)--(5) is justified by the four stated positivity and rank conditions.  Equations (1)--(5), followed by modal alignment, therefore identify all objects asserted in the theorem.

It remains to relate the observed-law functional to the causal target.  Equation (5) implies
\(m_j(x)=E(Y\mid J=j,X=x)\).  Consistency and latent ignorability give, on the positive-propensity stratum,
\[
E\{Y(j)\mid J=j,X=x\}=E(Y\mid J=j,X=x)=m_j(x),
\qquad
E\{Y(j)\mid J=j,X=x\}=E\{Y(j)\mid X=x\}. \tag{6}
\]
The outcome is bounded, so these conditional means are integrable.  Taking \(P_X\)-expectations in (6) proves
\[
E\{Y(j)\}=E_P\{m_j(X)\}=\psi_j,
\]
for every \(j\in\mathcal J\), as required.

%%% FIELD proof-prop-2
Fix \(j\in\mathcal J\).  From the definitions of the inverse-feature bridges and the conditional measurement laws,
\[
E\{a_j(A,X)\mid J=\ell,X\}
=e_j^{\mathsf T}M_A(X)^{-1}M_A(X)e_\ell
=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}, \tag{7}
\]
and the same calculation gives
\(E\{z_j(Z,X)\mid J=\ell,X\}=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}\).  The inverses exist by the two measurement-rank assumptions.  Three-view conditional independence and (7) imply
\[
E(U_j\mid J=\ell,X)=m_j(X),\qquad E(U_j)=E\{m_j(X)\}=\psi_j, \tag{8}
\]
so \(\phi_j=U_j-\psi_j\) is centered.

Let \(s_X,s_\pi,s_Y,s_A,s_Z\) be factorwise centered latent scores as in the differential-score-lifting theorem.  Repeated conditioning, (7), and the centering of each factor score yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
E(\phi_js_X)&=E[\{m_j(X)-\psi_j\}s_X(X)],\\
E(\phi_js_\pi)&=0,\\
E(\phi_js_Y)&=E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{J=j\}}{\pi_j(X)}
       \{Y-m_j(X)\}s_Y(Y,J,X)\right],\\
E(\phi_js_A)&=E(\phi_js_Z)=0.
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The first line of (9) is the derivative of the outer expectation with respect to the \(X\)-marginal.  The third is the derivative of the class-\(j\) conditional outcome mean, since
\[
E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{J=j\}}{\pi_j(X)}
 \{Y-m_j(X)\}s_Y\,\middle|\,X\right]
=E[\{Y-m_j(X)\}s_Y\mid J=j,X]. \tag{10}
\]
The target does not vary with the propensity or either measurement law, which agrees with the remaining zero lines of (9).  By Theorem \(\mathrm{differential\text{-}score\text{-}lifting}\), every observed DQM score is the conditional expectation of such an additive latent score.  Since \(\phi_j\) is observed-measurable, conditional-expectation duality and (9)--(10) establish its influence-function equation for every observed path.

Furthermore, \(|Y|\leq L\), \(\pi_j\geq\varepsilon\), and \(\|M_A^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|M_Z^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\).  Hence \(\phi_j\) is bounded by a constant depending only on the regular stratum and belongs to \(L^2_0(P)\).

Finally, the cited categorical construction defines inverse features \(q_j^X=e_j^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_X^{-1}h_X\), proves their conditional means equal the latent-class indicator, takes products of two conditionally independent inverse features, and inserts them in its categorical-action influence function.  In its displayed Appendix A.8 formula, set the discount to zero, take the state to be \(X\), the initial and observed state marginal both to be \(P_X\), the target action policy to put mass one on class \(j\), the behavior propensity to be \(\pi_j(X)\), the reward to be \(Y\), and the two inverse features to be \(a_j\) and \(z_j\).  The density ratio is then \(1/\pi_j(X)\), the baseline is \(E\{m_j(X)\}\), and the cited formula reduces exactly to \(U_j-\psi_j\).  This proves the claimed specialization as well as validity.

%%% FIELD proof-lem-2a
Use the notation in (1) and consider a normalized derivative
\((\dot\pi,\dot A_0,\dot Z_0,\dot\Gamma)\) satisfying
\[
\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}\dot\pi=0,\qquad
\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}\dot A_0=0,\qquad
\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}\dot Z_0=0. \tag{11}
\]
We first prove injectivity.  Suppose \(\dot S=0\) and \(\dot T_u=0\) for every \(u\).  Put
\[
E_A=A_0^{-1}\dot A_0,\qquad E_Z=Z_0^{-1}\dot Z_0,
\qquad p_j=\dot\pi_j/\pi_j.
\]
After left multiplication of \(\dot S=0\) by \(A_0^{-1}\) and right multiplication by \(Z_0^{-\mathsf T}\), its \((r,s)\) entry for \(r\ne s\) is
\[
(E_A)_{rs}\pi_s+\pi_r(E_Z)_{sr}=0. \tag{12}
\]
Applying the same transformations to \(\dot T_u=0\), and subtracting \(\gamma_{ur}\) times (12), gives
\[
(E_A)_{rs}\pi_s(\gamma_{us}-\gamma_{ur})=0,
\qquad u=1,\ldots,K. \tag{13}
\]
The feature-rank assumption implies
\[
\|\gamma_s-\gamma_r\|_2
=\|\Gamma_Y(x)(e_s-e_r)\|_2\geq\sqrt 2\,\varepsilon.
\]
Thus (13), together with \(\pi_s\geq\varepsilon\), gives \((E_A)_{rs}=0\); (12) then gives \((E_Z)_{sr}=0\).  Hence both \(E_A\) and \(E_Z\) are diagonal.  Since \(\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}A_0=\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}\), (11) implies \(\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}E_A=0\), so \(E_A=0\); identically \(E_Z=0\).  The diagonal part of the transformed equation \(\dot S=0\) now gives \(p=0\), and the diagonal part of every transformed equation \(\dot T_u=0\) gives \(\dot\gamma_{ur}=0\).  The derivative is injective on the normalized tangent space.

The admissible tuples \((A_0,Z_0,\pi,\Gamma_Y)\) lie in a compact finite-dimensional set: the stochastic factors and \(\pi\) lie in compact simplices, their relevant least singular values and propensity entries are bounded below by \(\varepsilon\), and every feature moment is bounded by \(\|h_0\|_\infty\).  The derivative matrix is continuous on this set.  The injectivity just proved makes its least singular value positive at every point; compactness makes the infimum positive.  Therefore its Moore--Penrose inverse on its image has norm at most \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\), after enlarging that declared constant by a function only of \(K,L,\varepsilon\), and \(\|h_0\|_\infty\).

The Moore--Penrose inverse is Borel on this fixed-rank set.  The measurable recovery in Theorem 1 supplies a Borel choice of the common permutation, and the modal gap makes that choice locally constant.  Applying the inverse to a differentiated observed moment vector therefore recovers the normalized derivatives under the ordered labeling.  For any positive categorical factor probability its score is derivative divided by that probability; at a zero atom a two-sided probability path has derivative zero, and its score can be set to zero.  The normalization equations (11) then give the required factorwise centering.  This is the asserted bounded right inverse on the observed finite-feature score image.

%%% FIELD proof-differential-lifting
Let \(s\in L^2_0(P)\) be the score of an observed DQM path.  Define
\[
s_X(X)=E(s\mid X),\qquad s_c=s-s_X. \tag{14}
\]
Then \(E(s_c\mid X)=0\) and \(\|s_X\|_2,\|s_c\|_2\leq\|s\|_2\) up to an inessential factor of two.  Differentiating bounded conditional moments along the DQM path gives their derivatives as conditional expectations against \(s_c\).  Conditional Jensen's inequality and Lemma 2A therefore produce measurable derivatives of \(\pi,M_A,M_Z,\Gamma_Y\) whose conditional Euclidean norm is bounded by
\[
C_{\mathrm{reg}}\{E(s_c^2\mid X)\}^{1/2}. \tag{15}
\]
The modal permutation is locally constant, so no label derivative appears.  More explicitly, Theorem 1 makes the conditional factor tuple a single-valued inverse of the finite moment map on the normalized, label-aligned stratum.  Lemma 2A gives the derivative of this inverse on the moment-map image and a uniform operator bound.  Applying that inverse pointwise to the conditional moment derivative, and then using its uniform bound, gives the asserted derivative in conditional \(L^2(P_X)\); this rules out an unidentified factor derivative.

To obtain a preliminary full-density derivative, differentiate the recovery identity \(p_A=A_0\Pi f\) from (5):
\[
\dot f=\Pi^{-1}A_0^{-1}
 \{\dot p_A-\dot A_0\Pi f-A_0\dot\Pi f\}. \tag{15a}
\]
Domination makes (15a) an equality in conditional \(L^1(\nu)\), its columns integrate to zero by the probability normalizations, and common support permits the preliminary definition \(u_{Y,j}=\dot f_j/f_j\) \(f_jd\nu\)-almost everywhere.  Define \(u_{\pi,j}=\dot\pi_j/\pi_j\), and define the categorical factor scores by derivative divided by the corresponding positive atom probability.  At a zero categorical atom, a two-sided differentiable probability path has derivative zero, and we assign score zero.  Differentiating the product factorization now gives the conditional observed-score identity
\[
s_c(y,a,z,x)=\sum_{j=1}^KR_{(a,z),j}(y,x)
 \{u_{\pi,j}(x)+u_{Y,j}(y,x)+u_{A,aj}(x)+u_{Z,zj}(x)\}. \tag{16}
\]
This identity is used only to locate \(N_Bs_c\) in a finite span; the construction below replaces the possibly ill-conditioned scores in (16) by an equivalent bounded-norm representation.

Let \(u\in\mathbb R^{2K^2}\) collect the measurement-score coordinates.  The terms in (16) depending only on \((Y,J,X)\) have cell vector in \(\operatorname{range}(R)=\ker(N_B)\), while the measurement contribution equals \(c^{\mathsf T}u\).  Consequently
\[
N_Bs_c=h^{\mathsf T}u. \tag{17}
\]
Taking conditional inner products with \(h\) gives
\[
b_s(X):=E\{hN_Bs_c\mid X\}=G(X)u. \tag{18}
\]
If \(v\in\ker G(X)\), then \(E\{(v^{\mathsf T}h)^2\mid X\}=0\); hence \(v^{\mathsf T}h=0\) conditionally and \(v^{\mathsf T}b_s=0\).  Thus \(b_s\in\operatorname{range}G\).  Put \(u_0=G^+b_s\).  Because (17) already has a solution, \(G(u_0-u)=0\), whence \(h^{\mathsf T}(u_0-u)=0\) and \(h^{\mathsf T}u_0=N_Bs_c\).  Moreover the projection identity and the nonzero-spectrum gap give
\[
\|u_0(X)\|_2^2
=b_s^{\mathsf T}G^{+2}b_s
\leq\varepsilon^{-1}b_s^{\mathsf T}G^+b_s
\leq\varepsilon^{-1}E\{(N_Bs_c)^2\mid X\}. \tag{19}
\]

For each latent-class block, subtract from the \(A\)-coordinates of \(u_0\) their \(M_A(\cdot\mid j,X)\)-mean, and do the analogous operation for \(Z\).  A blockwise constant has cell image \(R_{\cdot j}\) and is annihilated by \(N_B\).  Thus this recentering preserves (17).  Since the factors are probabilities and \(K\) is fixed, it multiplies the bound in (19) by at most a constant depending on \(K\).  Denote the centered result by \((s_A,s_Z)\).

Let \(s_0=s_c-c^{\mathsf T}u\), where now \(u=(s_A,s_Z)\).  Equation (17) implies \(N_Bs_0=0\).  By Lemma 3A, \(\ker N_B=\operatorname{range}R\), so pointwise
\[
t(y,x)=\{R^{\mathsf T}DR\}^{-1}R^{\mathsf T}D
s_0(y,\cdot,x),\qquad s_0=Rt. \tag{20}
\]
The inverse exists by the posterior-Gram gap.  Since \(s_0^{\mathsf T}Ds_0=t^{\mathsf T}R^{\mathsf T}DRt\) and \(w_j\leq1\),
\[
\sum_{j=1}^Kw_j(y,x)t_j(y,x)^2
\leq\|t(y,x)\|_2^2
\leq\varepsilon^{-1}s_0(y,\cdot,x)^{\mathsf T}D(y,x)s_0(y,\cdot,x). \tag{21}
\]
Define
\[
s_{\pi,j}(x)=\int f_j(y\mid x)t_j(y,x)d\nu(y),\qquad
s_{Y,j}(y,x)=t_j(y,x)-s_{\pi,j}(x). \tag{22}
\]
Then \(E(s_Y\mid J,X)=0\).  The recentered measurement contribution has mean zero given \(X\), and \(E(s_c\mid X)=0\); integrating \(s_0=Rt\) therefore gives
\[
0=E(s_0\mid X)=\sum_j\pi_j(X)s_{\pi,j}(X), \tag{23}
\]
which is the propensity-score centering condition.  Equations (20)--(23) also show that (16), with the newly constructed scores, holds exactly.

For the norm bound, integrate (21) against \(g(y\mid x)d\nu(y)\), use \(g w_j=\pi_jf_j\), and apply Jensen's inequality to (22).  This bounds the combined latent \(L^2\)-norms of \(s_\pi\) and \(s_Y\) by a constant times \(\|s_0\|_2\).  The pointwise \(D\)-orthogonal projector is a contraction in \(D\)-norm, and \(d_{az}\geq\varepsilon\) converts this to a uniform Euclidean bound for \(N_B\).  Together with (19), this bounds \(s_A,s_Z,s_0,s_\pi,s_Y\) by \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\|s\|_2\).  All inverses are Borel on the declared fixed-rank strata, conditional expectations admit measurable versions because \(\mathcal X\) is standard Borel, and so the entire construction is measurable in \(X\).

Finally, common support makes (22) an \(f_j\)-almost-everywhere class-density score on the same support.  Its bounded truncations, recentered under \(f_j\), converge in the class-factor \(L^2\) norm and generate strictly positive normalized bounded exponential-tilt paths for both signs of the path parameter.  Thus the recovered class-density direction is support preserving in the tangent-closure sense.  We have constructed the asserted uniformly bounded right inverse and the displayed conditional-expectation representation.

%%% FIELD proof-thm-2
The affirmative differential-inverse resolution places every observed DQM score in \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lat}}\).  Taking closures gives
\[
\mathcal T\subseteq\overline{\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lat}}}^{\,L^2(P)}. \tag{24}
\]
Conversely, at the stated two-sided pathwise-interior point, every bounded factorwise centered additive score generates an admissible product-factor DQM path.  Differentiating its product likelihood shows that its observed score is its conditional expectation given \(O\), and hence belongs to \(\mathcal T\).  Truncation followed by factorwise recentering makes bounded scores dense in each factor \(L^2\) space; conditional expectation is an \(L^2\) contraction.  Since \(\mathcal T\) is closed, this proves the reverse inclusion in (24), and therefore equality.

For the orthocomplement, take \(k\in L^2_0(P)\).  Conditional-expectation duality says that orthogonality to every centered outcome-factor score is equivalent to
\[
E(k\mid Y,J,X)=E(k\mid J,X). \tag{25}
\]
The analogous statements with \(A\) and \(Z\) replace \(Y\) in (25). Thus orthogonality to the additive score image implies all three conditional expectations in the theorem are zero.

Conversely, if the three displayed conditional expectations are zero, the first already implies \(E(k\mid J,X)=E(k\mid X)=0\), and each factorwise inner product vanishes by iterated expectation.  Hence \(k\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lat}}\), and therefore to its closure \(\mathcal T\).  This proves the asserted characterization.

%%% FIELD proof-lem-6
Write \(H=B^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}B\) and \(\widehat H=\widehat B^{\mathsf T}\widehat D^{-1}\widehat B\).  The cell and half-margin bounds control \(D^{-1}\) and \(\widehat D^{-1}\).  The two measurement singular-value bounds imply, by matricizing \(Bv\) as \(M_A\operatorname{diag}(v)M_Z^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
\|Bv\|_2\geq\sigma_{\min}(M_A)\sigma_{\min}(M_Z)\|v\|_2,
\]
and identically for \(\widehat B\).  Thus \(H^{-1}\) and \(\widehat H^{-1}\) are uniformly bounded.  The exact inverse identities
\[
\widehat D^{-1}-D^{-1}=\widehat D^{-1}(D-\widehat D)D^{-1},
\qquad
\widehat H^{-1}-H^{-1}=\widehat H^{-1}(H-\widehat H)H^{-1} \tag{35}
\]
and product telescoping in the formula for \(N_B\) give pointwise
\[
\|\widehat N-N_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}
 \{\|\widehat D-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|\widehat B-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}. \tag{36}
\]
Taking the declared conditional \(L^2(P)\) norm proves the first assertion.

For symmetric matrices of the same fixed rank, direct multiplication of the four Penrose identities verifies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat G^+-G^+
={}&-\widehat G^+(\widehat G-G)G^+
+\widehat G^{+2}(\widehat G-G)(I-GG^+)\\
&+(I-\widehat G^+\widehat G)(\widehat G-G)G^{+2}.
\end{aligned} \tag{37}
\]
The true and fitted nonzero spectral gaps bound every pseudoinverse in (37), so
\[
\|\widehat G^+-G^+\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\|\widehat G-G\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{38}
\]
The projection-Gram rank assumption makes the true rank constant over the relevant covariate strata, and the corrected coherent-fit construction requires \(\widehat G\) to have that same rank.  These are precisely the equal-rank premises used in (37); no full-support or maximal-rank conclusion is inferred.

Apply (35)--(38), the bridge identity
\(\widehat M^{-1}-M^{-1}=\widehat M^{-1}(M-\widehat M)M^{-1}\), and product telescoping successively to \(R,N,c,h,G,b_U\).  Every untouched factor is uniformly bounded by bounded \(Y\), fixed \(K\), and the true and fitted half-margin constraints.  Conditional Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz then give
\[
\|\widehat h^{\mathsf T}\widehat G^+\widehat b_{\widehat U}
-h^{\mathsf T}G^+b_U\|_2\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma,
\qquad
\|\widehat N\widehat U-N_BU\|_2\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma. \tag{39}
\]
Subtracting the two terms in (39) proves
\(\|\widehat Q\widehat U-QU\|_2\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma\).  The same inverse and product identities applied directly to the product bridge give \(\|\widehat U-U\|_2\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma\), and the triangle inequality proves the claimed stability of the full efficient score.  Finally, each of \(r_R,r_N,r_h,r_G,r_b\) is either controlled by the corresponding telescoping bound or is itself a nonnegative summand of \(R_\Sigma\); their displayed sum is therefore \(O_P(R_\Sigma)\).

%%% FIELD proof-lem-7
Work under the support-preserving coherent-fit construction stated in this bundle.  Fix a target coordinate and put \(k=QU\) and \(\widehat k=\widehat Q\widehat U\).  Both terms defining \(\widehat k\) lie in \(\operatorname{range}(\widehat N)=\ker(\widehat B^{\mathsf T})\), so \(\widehat B^{\mathsf T}\widehat k=0\) pointwise.  Since \(d=Bw\), conditioning first on \((Y,X)\) gives the exact identity
\[
E_P(\widehat k\mid X)
=\int g(y\mid X)\sum_{j=1}^Kw_j(y,X)
 (B_{\cdot j}-\widehat B_{\cdot j})^{\mathsf T}\widehat k(y,X)d\nu(y). \tag{40}
\]
Let \(\Delta_A=M_A-\widehat M_A\) and \(\Delta_Z=M_Z-\widehat M_Z\).  Columnwise,
\[
B_{\cdot j}-\widehat B_{\cdot j}
=\Delta_{A,\cdot j}\otimes M_{Z,\cdot j}
+M_{A,\cdot j}\otimes\Delta_{Z,\cdot j}
-\Delta_{A,\cdot j}\otimes\Delta_{Z,\cdot j}. \tag{41}
\]
The first term in (41), paired with the true \(k\), integrates to
\[
\pi_j\sum_a\Delta_{A,aj}
 \int f_j(y\mid X)\sum_zM_{Z,zj}k_{az}(y,X)d\nu(y)=0, \tag{42}
\]
because Theorems 2--3 give \(E(k\mid A,J,X)=0\).  If \(M_{A,aj}=0\), support preservation gives \(\Delta_{A,aj}=0\), so (42) never conditions on a null atom.  The second linear term vanishes analogously by \(E(k\mid Z,J,X)=0\) and support preservation for \(M_Z\).  Replace \(k\) by \(\widehat k-k\) in the two linear terms and retain \(\widehat k\) in the product term.  Uniform bounds, conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, and Lemma 6 yield
\[
\|E_P\widehat k\|
\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\{(r_A+r_Z)\|\widehat k-k\|_2+r_Ar_Z\}
\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\{(r_A+r_Z)R_\Sigma+r_Ar_Z\}. \tag{43}
\]

For the raw bridge define
\[
\delta^A_{j\ell}=E\{\widehat a_j(A,X)\mid J=\ell,X\}-\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\},
\quad
\delta^Z_{j\ell}=E\{\widehat z_j(Z,X)\mid J=\ell,X\}-\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}. \tag{44}
\]
Direct conditioning under three-view independence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E(\widehat U_j\mid X)-m_j
={}&(\widehat m_j-m_j)
\left[1-\frac{\pi_j}{\widehat\pi_j}
(1+\delta^A_{jj})(1+\delta^Z_{jj})\right]\\
&+\widehat\pi_j^{-1}\sum_{\ell\ne j}\pi_\ell
 \delta^A_{j\ell}\delta^Z_{j\ell}(m_\ell-\widehat m_j).
\end{aligned} \tag{45}
\]
The inverse identity for the two measurement matrices and the half-margin bounds imply
\(\|\delta^A\|_2\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_A\) and
\(\|\delta^Z\|_2\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_Z\).  Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (45) and using bounded outcomes gives
\[
\|E_P\widehat U-\psi\|
\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\{r_m(r_\pi+r_A+r_Z)+r_Ar_Z\}. \tag{46}
\]
Since the estimated efficient score is \(\widehat U-\widehat k\), (43)--(46) prove the claimed \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\Delta_n\) bound.

If only \(M_A\) is perturbed, every displayed bias term is at least quadratic: (46) has no term linear in \(r_A\), while (43) is \(O(r_A^2)\) by score stability.  If only \(f\) is perturbed coherently, \(B=\widehat B\), so (40) is exactly zero, and \(\delta^A=\delta^Z=0\) makes (45) exactly zero.  This proves the two stated first-order cancellations.

%%% FIELD proof-thm-8
Let
\[
S(O)=U(O)-QU(O)=\psi+\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(O),
\qquad \widehat S_f=\widehat U_f-\widehat Q_f\widehat U_f
\]
for training fold \(f\).  Adding and subtracting foldwise conditional means yields the exact decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n(\widehat\psi-\psi)
={}&n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(O_i)\\
&+n^{-1/2}\sum_{f=1}^{F_{\mathrm{fold}}}
 \sum_{i:\,\mathrm{fold}(i)=f}
 \bigl[(\widehat S_f-S)(O_i)-P(\widehat S_f-S)\bigr]\\
&+n^{-1/2}\sum_{f=1}^{F_{\mathrm{fold}}}n_fP(\widehat S_f-\psi).
\end{aligned} \tag{47}
\]
Conditional on the training observations for fold \(f\), its held-out summands are independent, centered, and have variance at most \(\|\widehat S_f-S\|_2^2\).  Therefore the conditional variance of the corresponding normalized fold sum is at most \((n_f/n)\|\widehat S_f-S\|_2^2\).  Lemma 6 and \(R_\Sigma=o_P(1)\), followed by the elementary conditional second-moment inequality, make the middle line of (47) \(o_P(1)\) after summing the fixed number of folds.  Lemma 7 bounds the last line by
\[
n^{-1/2}\sum_fn_f C_{\mathrm{reg}}\Delta_n
\leq \sqrt n\,C_{\mathrm{reg}}\max_f\Delta_{n,f}=o_P(1). \tag{48}
\]
Equations (47)--(48) prove the asymptotic linear representation.

The regularity margins, bounded outcome, and fixed finite matrices make \(\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}\) bounded.  For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^K\), its centered characteristic function has the expansion
\[
E\exp\{it^{\mathsf T}\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}/\sqrt n\}
=1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}V_{\mathrm{eff}}t}{2n}+o(n^{-1}), \tag{49}
\]
where boundedness controls the Taylor remainder.  Independence raises (49) to the \(n\)-th power, whose limit is
\(\exp\{-t^{\mathsf T}V_{\mathrm{eff}}t/2\}\).  Thus the leading vector in (47), and hence the estimator, converges to \(N(0,V_{\mathrm{eff}})\).

For covariance consistency, Lemma 6 gives foldwise \(L^2(P)\) convergence \(\widehat S_f\to S\).  Conditional on each training sample, the second moment of the held-out empirical difference from its conditional expectation has variance tending to zero, by uniform boundedness; its conditional expectation converges to \(E(SS^{\mathsf T})\) by Cauchy--Schwarz and \(L^2\) convergence.  Summing the fixed folds and using \(\widehat\psi\to_P\psi\) shows that the centered empirical covariance converges to
\[
E\{(S-\psi)(S-\psi)^{\mathsf T}\}=V_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\]
This is exactly \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{eff}}\to_PV_{\mathrm{eff}}\).

Multiplying the expansion in Theorem 8 by the fixed matrix \(C\) gives
\[
\sqrt n(C\widehat\psi-C\psi)
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nC\phi^{\mathrm{eff}}(O_i)+o_P(1)
\rightsquigarrow N(0,CV_{\mathrm{eff}}C^{\mathsf T})
=N(0,\Sigma_C). \tag{50}
\]
Consistency of \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{eff}}\) and continuity of finite matrix multiplication give \(\widehat\Sigma_C\to_P\Sigma_C\).  Positive definiteness of \(\Sigma_C\) makes every diagonal entry positive, so conversion to the correlation matrices \(R_C\) and \(\widehat R_C\) is continuous and \(\widehat R_C\to_PR_C\).

For fixed \(q\), a Gaussian vector with positive-definite correlation matrix has a density.  The boundary of every rectangle \([-t,t]^q\) has probability zero, and every nonempty shell between two such rectangles has positive probability.  Therefore the distribution function of \(\max_{r\leq q}|Z_r|\), for \(Z\sim N(0,R_C)\), is continuous and strictly increasing on its nontrivial range.  Convergence of \(\widehat R_C\) implies convergence of the corresponding Gaussian probabilities by dominated convergence of their finite-dimensional densities, and hence convergence of the estimated \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile.  Combining this quantile convergence with (50) and the consistent coordinate standard errors proves simultaneous coverage tending to \(1-\alpha\).  The condition \(C\mathbf 1_K=0\) makes every row a treatment contrast, while full row rank and positive information exclude redundant or zero-variance reported coordinates.
