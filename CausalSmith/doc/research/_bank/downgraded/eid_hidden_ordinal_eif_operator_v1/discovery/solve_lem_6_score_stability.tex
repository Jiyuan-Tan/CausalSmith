%%% FIELD proof-lem-6-score-stability
Write \(\|V\|_2=\{E_P\|V\|^2\}^{1/2}\) for a random vector or matrix, using the Euclidean, Frobenius, or operator norm appropriate to its type.  All constants below may change from line to line but depend only on the quantities allowed in \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

First consider the pointwise cell projector.  Put
\[
 H=B^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}B,
 \qquad
 \widehat H=\widehat B^{\mathsf T}\widehat D^{-1}\widehat B.
 \tag{41}
\]
Observed-cell positivity, namely \(d_{az}(Y,X)\geq\varepsilon\), and the fitted half-margin in the coherent-fit definition imply
\[
 \|D^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\varepsilon^{-1},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat D^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2\varepsilon^{-1}.
 \tag{42}
\]
Moreover, if \(v\in\mathbb R^K\), matricizing \(Bv\) and using the Khatri--Rao definition of \(B\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{mat}(Bv)=M_A\operatorname{diag}(v)M_Z^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The surrogate- and proxy-rank assumptions therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|Bv\|_2
 &=\|M_A\operatorname{diag}(v)M_Z^{\mathsf T}\|_{\mathrm F}\\
 &\geq \sigma_{\min}(M_A)\sigma_{\min}(M_Z)
       \|\operatorname{diag}(v)\|_{\mathrm F}
 \geq\varepsilon^2\|v\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
The same argument, with the fitted half-margins, yields
\(\|\widehat Bv\|_2\geq(\varepsilon^2/4)\|v\|_2\).  Because every diagonal entry of \(D\) and \(\widehat D\) is a probability and hence at most one,
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}Hv=\|D^{-1/2}Bv\|_2^2\geq\|Bv\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 v^{\mathsf T}\widehat Hv\geq\|\widehat Bv\|_2^2.
\]
Consequently \(H\) and \(\widehat H\) are invertible and
\[
 \|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\varepsilon^{-4},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq16\varepsilon^{-4}.
 \tag{44}
\]
Thus the Moore--Penrose inverses in the two cell-projector formulas are ordinary inverses.

The exact resolvent identities
\[
 \widehat D^{-1}-D^{-1}
 =\widehat D^{-1}(D-\widehat D)D^{-1},
 \qquad
 \widehat H^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\widehat H^{-1}(H-\widehat H)H^{-1}
 \tag{45}
\]
and a four-factor telescoping of \(B^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}B\) imply, using the uniform bounds in (42)--(44),
\[
 \|\widehat H-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}
 \bigl(\|\widehat B-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
       +\|\widehat D-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr).
 \tag{46}
\]
Apply (45)--(46) to
\[
 N_B=I_{K^2}-D^{-1}BH^{-1}B^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \widehat N=I_{K^2}-\widehat D^{-1}\widehat B
                    \widehat H^{-1}\widehat B^{\mathsf T}.
\]
A second product telescoping gives pointwise
\[
 \|\widehat N-N_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}
 \bigl(\|\widehat D-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}
       +\|\widehat B-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr).
 \tag{47}
\]
Taking the declared conditional \(L^2(P)\) norm proves
\[
 \{E_P\|\widehat N-N_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\}^{1/2}
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(r_D+r_B).
 \tag{48}
\]

We next control the conditional Gram step.  The projection-Gram gap says that every nonzero eigenvalue of \(G(X)\) is at least \(\varepsilon\), and the coherent fit uses the observable threshold \(\tau_G=\varepsilon/2\).  The already established data-driven Gram-threshold lemma therefore gives
\[
 \|\widehat G_{\tau_G}^{\dagger}-G^+\|_{L^2(P_X;\mathrm{op})}
 \leq26\varepsilon^{-2}r_G,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat G_{\tau_G}^{\dagger}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq2\varepsilon^{-1}.
 \tag{49}
\]
It also gives, on the event \(r_{G,\infty}<\varepsilon/4\), equality of the threshold-selected rank and \(\operatorname{rank}(G(X))\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\).  Since \(r_{G,\infty}=o_P(1)\), this event has probability tending to one.  Hence rank recovery is a conclusion from the fitted spectrum and not an oracle input.

All remaining plug-in maps are uniformly bounded on the true and fitted regular strata.  Indeed, bounded outcome support gives \(|m_j|\vee|\widehat m_j|\leq L\); latent positivity and its fitted half-margin bound the inverse propensities; the true and fitted measurement singular-value margins bound the inverse bridges; \(R\) and \(\widehat R\) are posterior matrices from their respective coherent product laws; and (42), (44), and (49) bound the projector and generalized-inverse factors.  Conditional expectation is an \(L^2\)-contraction.  It follows by applying (45), the analogous identity
\[
 \widehat M^{-1}-M^{-1}
 =\widehat M^{-1}(M-\widehat M)M^{-1},
 \tag{50}
\]
and product telescoping successively to \(R\), \(N\), \(c\), \(h\), \(G\), and \(b_{(\cdot)}\) that one differentiated factor is measured by its declared coordinate of \(r_\eta\), while all undifferentiated factors are bounded by \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).  In particular,
\[
 \|\widehat U-U\|_2
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(r_m+r_\pi+r_A+r_Z)
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma.
 \tag{51}
\]
Here the bridge part follows directly from (50): for example,
\(\widehat M_A^{-1}M_A-I=widehat M_A^{-1}(M_A-\widehat M_A)\), and similarly for \(M_Z\).  Since \(R_\Sigma\) is the sum of nonnegative declared errors and contains \(r_R,r_N,r_h,r_G,r_b\), it also gives directly
\[
 r_R+r_N+r_h+r_G+r_b\leq R_\Sigma,
 \tag{52}
\]
which is the asserted \(O_P(R_\Sigma)\) bound.

For completeness, let \(\widehat b_{\widehat U}\) denote the fitted-law right-hand side formed coherently from \(\widehat U\).  Add and subtract one factor at a time in the Gram correction:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\widehat h^{\mathsf T}\widehat G_{\tau_G}^{\dagger}
       \widehat b_{\widehat U}-h^{\mathsf T}G^+b_U\\
 &\quad=(\widehat h-h)^{\mathsf T}\widehat G_{\tau_G}^{\dagger}
              \widehat b_{\widehat U}
 +h^{\mathsf T}(\widehat G_{\tau_G}^{\dagger}-G^+)
              \widehat b_{\widehat U}
 +h^{\mathsf T}G^+(\widehat b_{\widehat U}-b_U).
\end{aligned}
\tag{53}
\]
The uniform bounds just established, conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, (49), and the definitions of \(r_h,r_G,r_b\) yield
\[
 \|\widehat h^{\mathsf T}\widehat G_{\tau_G}^{\dagger}
       \widehat b_{\widehat U}-h^{\mathsf T}G^+b_U\|_2
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(r_h+r_G+r_b)
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma.
 \tag{54}
\]
Likewise, (47), (51), and the boundedness of \(N_B,\widehat N,U,\widehat U\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat N\widehat U-N_BU\|_2
 &\leq\|(\widehat N-N_B)\widehat U\|_2
       +\|N_B(\widehat U-U)\|_2\\
 &\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma.
\end{aligned}
\tag{55}
\]
Subtracting (54) from (55) according to the definitions of \(\widehat Q\) and \(Q\), and then using the triangle inequality, proves
\[
 \|\widehat Q\widehat U-QU\|_{L^2(P)}
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma.
 \tag{56}
\]
Finally,
\[
 (\widehat U-\widehat Q\widehat U)-(U-QU)
 =(\widehat U-U)-(\widehat Q\widehat U-QU),
\]
so (51), (56), and the triangle inequality establish the same linear \(L^2(P)\) bound for the full estimated efficient score.

%%% FIELD proof-lem-7-quadratic-bias
Work coordinatewise and put \(k=QU\) and \(\widehat k=\widehat Q\widehat U\).  The already established support-leakage conditional-zero lemma applies because both objects are formed from the same coherent fitted product law and the same data-driven spectral inverse.  If \(\kappa_{\mathrm{supp},n}=o_P(1)\), its event \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{supp},n}\) has probability tending to one, and on that event
\[
 \|E_P\widehat k\|
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\left[
 (r_A+r_Z)\|\widehat k-k\|_{L^2(P)}+r_Ar_Z
 +\ell_{A,n}+\ell_{Z,n}\right].
 \tag{57}
\]
The score-stability lemma just proved gives
\(\|\widehat k-k\|_{L^2(P)}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}R_\Sigma\).  Hence
\[
 \|E_P\widehat k\|
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}
 \{(r_A+r_Z)R_\Sigma+r_Ar_Z
   +\ell_{A,n}+\ell_{Z,n}\}.
 \tag{58}
\]

It remains to evaluate the raw bridge mean.  For \(j,\ell\in\mathcal J\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta^A_{j\ell}(X)
 &=E_P\{\widehat a_j(A,X)\mid J=\ell,X\}
   -\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\},\\
 \delta^Z_{j\ell}(X)
 &=E_P\{\widehat z_j(Z,X)\mid J=\ell,X\}
   -\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}
\]
By the bridge definitions and the true conditional laws of \(A\) and \(Z\), these matrices satisfy the exact identities
\[
 \delta^A=\widehat M_A^{-1}M_A-I_K
 =\widehat M_A^{-1}(M_A-\widehat M_A),
 \qquad
 \delta^Z=\widehat M_Z^{-1}M_Z-I_K
 =\widehat M_Z^{-1}(M_Z-\widehat M_Z).
 \tag{60}
\]
The fitted singular-value half-margins and the definition of \(r_A,r_Z\) therefore imply
\[
 \|\delta^A\|_{L^2(P_X)}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_A,
 \qquad
 \|\delta^Z\|_{L^2(P_X)}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_Z,
 \tag{61}
\]
and both matrices are uniformly bounded.

Conditional three-view independence implies that, given \((J=\ell,X)\), the three factors \(Y\), \(A\), and \(Z\) separate.  Consistently with the definition of \(m_\ell\),
\(E_P(Y-\widehat m_j\mid J=\ell,X)=m_\ell-\widehat m_j\).  Expanding the definition of \(\widehat U_j\), conditioning first on \((J,X)\), and then summing over \(J\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P(\widehat U_j\mid X)-m_j
={}&(\widehat m_j-m_j)
 \left[1-\frac{\pi_j}{\widehat\pi_j}
 (1+\delta^A_{jj})(1+\delta^Z_{jj})\right]\\
&+\widehat\pi_j^{-1}\sum_{\ell\ne j}\pi_\ell
 \delta^A_{j\ell}\delta^Z_{j\ell}
 (m_\ell-\widehat m_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{62}
\]
This identity is exact: for \(\ell=j\) both conditional bridge means are \(1+\delta_{jj}\), whereas for \(\ell\ne j\) both indicator terms vanish and only the product of the two errors remains.

The true latent-positivity assumption and the fitted half-margin give \(\pi_j^{-1}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\) and \(\widehat\pi_j^{-1}\leq2\varepsilon^{-1}\).  Also \(|m_\ell|\vee|\widehat m_j|\leq L\) because both regressions arise from laws supported on the declared bounded outcome support.  From
\[
 \left|1-\frac{\pi_j}{\widehat\pi_j}ight|
 \leq2\varepsilon^{-1}|\widehat\pi_j-\pi_j|
\]
and the uniform bounds on \(\delta^A,\delta^Z\), the absolute value of the first line of (62), integrated over \(X\), is at most
\[
 C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_m(r_\pi+r_A+r_Z),
\]
by Cauchy--Schwarz.  The second line is at most \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_Ar_Z\), again by Cauchy--Schwarz applied to (61), using that \(K\) is fixed.  Since \(E_Pm_j(X)=\psi_j\), summing the finitely many coordinates yields
\[
 \|E_P\widehat U-\psi\|
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}
 \{r_m(r_\pi+r_A+r_Z)+r_Ar_Z\}.
 \tag{63}
\]
The mean of the estimated efficient score obeys
\[
 E_P(\widehat U-\widehat Q\widehat U)-\psi
 =(E_P\widehat U-\psi)-E_P\widehat k.
\]
Combining (58), (63), and the definition of \(\Delta_n\) proves, on \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{supp},n}\),
\[
 \|E_P(\widehat U-\widehat Q\widehat U)-\psi\|
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\Delta_n.
 \tag{64}
\]
The probability of that event tends to one, as required.

We finally verify the two derivative assertions without using a rate bound as a substitute for the cancellation.  Let \(t\mapsto\eta_t\) be a support-preserving two-sided regular perturbation with \(\eta_0=\eta\), and evaluate its score mean under \(P=P_{\eta_0}\).  If only \(M_A\) varies, then \(m_t=m\), \(\pi_t=\pi\), and \(\delta^Z_t=0\).  Equation (62) consequently gives
\[
 E_PU(\eta_t)-\psi=0
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently small }t.
 \tag{65}
\]
Support preservation makes the off-support leakage exactly zero.  The conditional-zero inequality with \(r_Z=0\), together with the locally Lipschitz plug-in bounds (45)--(56), gives
\[
 \|E_P Q(\eta_t)U(\eta_t)\|
 \leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}r_A(t)
 \|Q(\eta_t)U(\eta_t)-QU\|_2
 =O(t^2),
 \tag{66}
\]
because differentiability of the path gives \(r_A(t)=O(|t|)\), and the separated inverse and Gram-gap bounds give score displacement \(O(|t|)\).  Equations (65)--(66) show that the derivative at zero of the mean bias is zero.

If only \(f\) varies coherently, then \(M_A,M_Z,\pi\), and hence \(B\), do not vary, so \(\delta^A_t=\delta^Z_t=0\).  Equation (62) again gives the exact equality \(E_PU(\eta_t)=\psi\), even though \(m_t\) may vary.  Furthermore, the projector algebra gives \(B^{\mathsf T}N_t=0\).  Since every column of \(h_t=N_tc_t\) also lies in \(\ker(B^{\mathsf T})\), the definition of \(Q_t\) implies, cellwise,
\[
 B^{\mathsf T}\{Q_tU(\eta_t)\}=0.
 \tag{67}
\]
Conditional three-view independence identifies the \(j\)-th coordinate of the left side with
\[
 E_P\{Q_tU(\eta_t)\mid Y,J=j,X\}
 =\sum_{a,z}B_{(a,z),j}
   \{Q_tU(\eta_t)\}_{a,z}(Y,X)=0.
\]
Iterated expectation therefore gives \(E_PQ_tU(\eta_t)=0\) exactly.  Together with the raw equality, this shows that the mean bias is identically zero along the coherent \(f\)-only perturbation, and in particular its pathwise derivative at zero vanishes.
