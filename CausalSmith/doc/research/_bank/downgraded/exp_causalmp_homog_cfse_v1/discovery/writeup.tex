\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}causalmp\_\allowbreak{}homog\_\allowbreak{}cfse}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A fixed-horizon randomized experiment under homogeneous Causal-MP has a root-\(N\) fluctuation state evolution even though one unobserved Gaussian interaction matrix is reused at every date. An exact finite-\(N\) bridge conditions on the full observed past and uses empirical Gram projections to couple the reused-matrix fields to the auxiliary Gaussian-residual recursion. The theorem is uniform over an explicitly typed compact family \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal Q_T\) under named common moment, identification, rank, overlap, Gram, and Schur-residual margins. A single joint Gaussian experiment combines the differentiated mean, energy, Gram, and score moments with the initial-row empirical process. Its structural loading gives a direct asymptotic-linear representation for the all-treated-minus-none-treated path, and observable row contributions estimate the same covariance used by simultaneous Gaussian-max bands.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Causal-MP supplies first-order state evolution, consistent total-treatment-effect estimation, and heuristic intervals, but no root-\(N\) law for empirical means, second moments, or scores under reuse of one latent matrix.

\paragraph{Niche.} Order-\(N^{-1/2}\) common-disorder terms are discarded by first-order laws and cannot be recovered by treating panel rows as independent.

\paragraph{Fill.} Establish the finite-\(N\) conditional bridge with empirical Gram coefficients, construct the ordered auxiliary recursion and its ordinary derivative on the all-active interior, prove the expansion uniformly on compact \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal Q_T\), derive one joint moment--initial-law Gaussian experiment, map it through the structural and counterfactual derivatives, and calibrate an observable simultaneous path band from plug-in estimates of the same row influence.

\section{Related work}

Shirani and Bayati (2024, published Section 3, Theorems 1--2) establish first-order state evolution and consistency; their published Section 2.5 labels confidence-interval analysis heuristic. Bayati, Luo, Overman, Shirani, and Xiong (2024, Sections 2--4 and Algorithm 1) learn higher-order state-evolution update functions from observed mean, variance, treatment, and nonlinear features and discuss empirical intervals or replicated error bars, but they do not prove the proposed analytic root-\(N\) joint covariance, feasible influence covariance, or simultaneous coverage law. Bayati and Montanari (2011) supply adaptive Gaussian conditioning for AMP with an Onsager correction; the relevant distinction is the exact finite-\(N\) full-past bridge with empirical Gram coefficients for a reused nonsymmetric matrix and no Onsager correction. Rush and Venkataramanan (2018) establish finite-sample concentration, and Bao, Han, and Xu (2025) establish leave-one-out representations; these papers inform the projection and remainder arguments without claiming the aggregate causal fluctuation law. Qiu, Sun, and Zhou (2024) study multiple derived causal outcomes, while Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2013) supply Gaussian-max calibration after asymptotic linearity. Wang, Zhong, and Fan (2024) and Gueddari, Hachem, and Najim (2025) concern broader first-order AMP laws rather than centered Causal-MP covariance.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat q_t=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Ng_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{it},W_{i,t+1},X_i)^2\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat v} --- \texttt{observed outcome-\allowbreak{}variance vector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat v_{t+1}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N(Y_{i,t+1}-N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^NY_{j,t+1})^2\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat X\_\allowbreak{}v} --- \texttt{observable variance-\allowbreak{}design matrix}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the \(T\)-by-two matrix with row \((\widehat q_t,1)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}sigma\textasciicircum{}2} --- \texttt{estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the first coordinate of \((\widehat X_v^{\top}W_v\widehat X_v)^{-1}\widehat X_v^{\top}W_v\widehat v\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau\textasciicircum{}2} --- \texttt{estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the second coordinate of \((\widehat X_v^{\top}W_v\widehat X_v)^{-1}\widehat X_v^{\top}W_v\widehat v\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat K} --- \texttt{Gram estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat K_{rs}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Ng_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{ir},W_{i,r+1},X_i)g_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{is},W_{i,s+1},X_i)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat P\_\allowbreak{}\{0X\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{empirical initial-\allowbreak{}row law}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat P_{0X}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{(Y_{i0},X_i)}\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat C} --- \texttt{observable residual covariance}; \(\mathbb S_+^T\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat C=\widehat\sigma^2\widehat K+\widehat\tau^2I_T\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat e\_\allowbreak{}i} --- \texttt{observable residual vector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat e_{i,t}=Y_{i,t+1}-N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^Ng_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{jt},W_{j,t+1},X_j)\) for \(0\le t<T\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat J} --- \texttt{observable mean-\allowbreak{}moment Jacobian}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat J_{tk}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\partial_{\beta_k}g_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{it},W_{i,t+1},X_i)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat Q\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}beta} --- \texttt{observable variance-\allowbreak{}score Jacobian}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \((\widehat Q_\beta)_{t\cdot}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N2g_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{it},W_{i,t+1},X_i)\partial_\beta g_{\widehat\beta}(Y_{it},W_{i,t+1},X_i)^{\top}\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat L} --- \texttt{observable mean influence loading}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat L=(\widehat J^{\top}W_m\widehat J)^{-1}\widehat J^{\top}W_m\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat A\_\allowbreak{}v} --- \texttt{observable variance influence loading}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat A_v=(\widehat X_v^{\top}W_v\widehat X_v)^{-1}\widehat X_v^{\top}W_v\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}zeta\_\allowbreak{}i} --- \texttt{observable structural-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}initial-\allowbreak{}law influence direction}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} write \(\bar e=N^{-1}\sum_j\widehat e_j\), \(\widehat z_{m,i}=\widehat e_i-\bar e\), and \(\widehat z_{v,i}=\widehat e_i\circ\widehat e_i-N^{-1}\sum_j\widehat e_j\circ\widehat e_j\); then \(\widehat\zeta_i=(\widehat L\widehat z_{m,i},\widehat A_v\{\widehat z_{v,i}-\widehat\sigma^2\widehat Q_\beta\widehat L\widehat z_{m,i}\},\delta_{(Y_{i0},X_i)}-\widehat P_{0X})\)
  \item \texttt{m\_\allowbreak{}t\textasciicircum{}\{(a)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{counterfactual mean state}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \item \texttt{v\_\allowbreak{}t\textasciicircum{}\{(a)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{counterfactual variance state}; \([0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mu\_\allowbreak{}t\textasciicircum{}\{(a)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{counterfactual message mean}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}kappa\_\allowbreak{}t\textasciicircum{}\{(a)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{counterfactual message energy}; \([0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Psi\_\allowbreak{}T} --- \texttt{counterfactual path functional}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} set \(\mu_0^{(a)}=\mathbb E_{P_{0X}}[g_{\beta_0}(Y_0,a,X)]\), \(\kappa_0^{(a)}=\mathbb E_{P_{0X}}[g_{\beta_0}(Y_0,a,X)^2]\), \(m_1^{(a)}=\mu_0^{(a)}\), and \(v_1^{(a)}=\sigma^2\kappa_0^{(a)}+\tau^2\); for \(1\le t<T\), set \(\mu_t^{(a)}=\mathbb E_{X\sim P_X,Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)}[g_{\beta_0}(m_t^{(a)}+\sqrt{v_t^{(a)}}Z,a,X)]\), \(\kappa_t^{(a)}=\mathbb E_{X,Z}[g_{\beta_0}(m_t^{(a)}+\sqrt{v_t^{(a)}}Z,a,X)^2]\), \(m_{t+1}^{(a)}=\mu_t^{(a)}\), and \(v_{t+1}^{(a)}=\sigma^2\kappa_t^{(a)}+\tau^2\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta\_\allowbreak{}T} --- \texttt{causal path}; \(\mathbb R^T\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\theta_T=\Psi_T(\beta_0,\sigma^2,\tau^2,P_{0X})=(m_t^{(1)}-m_t^{(0)})_{t=1}^T\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta\_\allowbreak{}T} --- \texttt{path estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\theta_T=\Psi_T(\widehat\beta,\widehat\sigma^2,\widehat\tau^2,\widehat P_{0X})\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal A\_\allowbreak{}T} --- \texttt{joint structural-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}initial-\allowbreak{}law loading}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal A_T\mathbb Z_T^{\mathrm{full}}=(LZ_m,A_v\{Z_v-\sigma^2Q_\beta LZ_m\},\mathbb G_{0X})\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}zeta\_\allowbreak{}T(\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}xi)} --- \texttt{row-\allowbreak{}level structural-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}initial-\allowbreak{}law influence direction}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\zeta_T(\xi)=(LS_mI_T(\xi),A_v\{S_vI_T(\xi)-\sigma^2Q_\beta LS_mI_T(\xi)\},\delta_{(Y_0,X)}-P_{0X})\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}psi\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta,\allowbreak{}T\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{counterfactual-\allowbreak{}path influence vector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\psi_{\theta,T}(\xi)=D\Psi_T(\beta_0,\sigma^2,\tau^2,P_{0X})[\zeta_T(\xi)]\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Omega\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta} --- \texttt{path covariance}; \(\mathbb S_+^T\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\Omega_\theta=P_\xi[\psi_{\theta,T}(\xi)\psi_{\theta,T}(\xi)^{\top}]\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}psi\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta,\allowbreak{}T,\allowbreak{}i\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{estimated path influence vector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\psi_{\theta,T,i}=D\Psi_T(\widehat\beta,\widehat\sigma^2,\widehat\tau^2,\widehat P_{0X})[\widehat\zeta_i]\), where the derivative is evaluated by forward differentiation of every displayed scalar update in \(\Psi_T\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Omega\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta} --- \texttt{observable covariance estimator}; \(\mathbb S_+^T\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Omega_\theta=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\widehat\psi_{\theta,T,i}\widehat\psi_{\theta,T,i}^{\top}\)
  \item \texttt{Z\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta} --- \texttt{conditional Gaussian path vector}; \(\mathbb R^T\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(Z^\theta\mid\text{data}\sim\mathcal N(0,\widehat\Omega_\theta)\)
  \item \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}\{1-\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{unstudentized Gaussian-\allowbreak{}max critical value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the conditional \(1-\alpha\) quantile of \(\max_{1\le t\le T}|Z_t^\theta|\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal C\_\allowbreak{}N} --- \texttt{simultaneous band}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal C_{N,t}=[\widehat\theta_{T,t}-c_{1-\alpha}/\sqrt N,\widehat\theta_{T,t}+c_{1-\alpha}/\sqrt N]\) for every \(1\le t\le T\)
  \item \texttt{Q} --- \texttt{regular model index}; \texttt{index collecting a primitive-\allowbreak{}row law and the finite-\allowbreak{}dimensional homogeneous Causal-\allowbreak{}MP parameters for the fixed horizon}
  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\{0X,\allowbreak{}Q\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{initial-\allowbreak{}row law induced by a model index}; \texttt{the initial-\allowbreak{}outcome and covariate component of Q}
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}vartheta\_\allowbreak{}Q} --- \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}dimensional parameter coordinate}

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}beta\_\allowbreak{}\{0,\allowbreak{}Q\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{structural parameter at model index Q}; \(\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\)

  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}xi,\allowbreak{}Q\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{primitive-\allowbreak{}row law induced by Q}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the product law of \(\xi_i\) obtained from \(P_{0X,Q}\), the Bernoulli probabilities \(p_{0:T-1,Q}\), Gaussian innovations with variance \(\tau_Q^2\), and the auxiliary standard Gaussian coordinates
  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\{N,\allowbreak{}Q\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}array law induced by Q}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the law of the size-\(N\) observed array obtained by drawing primitive rows from \(P_{\xi,Q}\), one independent Gaussian interaction matrix, and applying the homogeneous structural recursion with \((\beta_{0,Q},\sigma_Q^2)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathbb P\_\allowbreak{}\{N,\allowbreak{}Q\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{empirical primitive-\allowbreak{}row law under Q}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathbb P_{N,Q}=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{\xi_i}\) when the array is distributed according to \(P_{N,Q}\)
  \item \texttt{[\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}cdot]\_\allowbreak{}Q} --- \texttt{model-\allowbreak{}evaluation operator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \([A]_Q\) denotes the displayed generic object \(A\) after replacing \((P_{0X},P_\xi,\beta_0,\sigma^2,\tau^2,p_t,\mathbb P_N)\) by \((P_{0X,Q},P_{\xi,Q},\beta_{0,Q},\sigma_Q^2,\tau_Q^2,p_{t,Q},\mathbb P_{N,Q})\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon} --- \texttt{positive tolerance}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{f} --- \texttt{bounded continuous primitive-\allowbreak{}row test function}; \texttt{generic generator of the weak component of the envelope-\allowbreak{}weighted topology}

  \item \texttt{B\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal E\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{common envelope-\allowbreak{}moment bound}; \((0,\infty)\)

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{id\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{common identification radius}; \((0,\infty)\)

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}underline j} --- \texttt{common mean-\allowbreak{}Jacobian singular-\allowbreak{}value bound}; \((0,\infty)\)

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak r\_\allowbreak{}Q(\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}beta)} --- \texttt{population mean-\allowbreak{}residual map at Q}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-horizon]\label{ass:fixed-horizon}
\(T\) is fixed as \(N\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed-iteration state-evolution regime, \cite{BayatiMontanari2011DenseGraphs}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-interactions]\label{ass:gaussian-interactions}
The entries \(G_{ij}\) are independent \(\mathcal N(0,1)\) variables, and the same matrix is reused for every \(t\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian dense interactions with time reuse, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bernoulli-design]\label{ass:bernoulli-design}
The variables \(W_{i,t+1}\) are independent Bernoulli variables with known probabilities satisfying \(\eta\le p_t\le1-\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known Bernoulli design with overlap, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-innovations]\label{ass:gaussian-innovations}
The variables \(\epsilon_{it}\) are independent \(\mathcal N(0,\tau^2)\) variables.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian outcome innovations, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:primitive-independence]\label{ass:primitive-independence}
The arrays \(G\), \(W\), \(\epsilon\), and the initial rows \((Y_{i0},X_i)\) are mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Primitive independence, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:initial-iid]\label{ass:initial-iid}
The rows \((Y_{i0},X_i)\) are independent and identically distributed with law \(P_{0X}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed initial rows, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:initial-moments]\label{ass:initial-moments}
The law \(P_{0X}\) has every finite moment required by the derivative envelopes.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Polynomial-envelope moment condition, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:smooth-messages]\label{ass:smooth-messages}
Near \(\beta_0\), the map \(g_\beta\) is three times continuously differentiable in its continuous arguments and parameter, with derivatives dominated by integrable polynomial envelopes under the fixed-\(T\) law.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Smooth polynomial-envelope state-evolution condition, \cite{RushVenkataramanan2018FiniteSampleAMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:identified-branch]\label{ass:identified-branch}
On \(\mathcal B\), the population mean-moment criterion has the unique zero \(\beta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Identified minimum-distance branch, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mean-rank]\label{ass:mean-rank}
The Jacobian \(J\) has full column rank.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Full-rank minimum-distance Jacobian, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-excitation]\label{ass:variance-excitation}
The matrix \(X_v\) has rank two.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Distinct message-energy design, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:path-smoothness]\label{ass:path-smoothness}
The finite recursion \(\Psi_T\) is Hadamard differentiable at \((\beta_0,\sigma^2,\tau^2,P_{0X})\) tangentially to the initial-row empirical-process directions.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Functional delta-method differentiability, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996WeakConvergence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:retained-gram-margin]\label{ass:retained-gram-margin}
For every \(1\le t<T\), \(\lambda_{\min}\{K_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}(P)\}>\underline\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform nonsingularity of finite Gram blocks, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:active-residual-margin]\label{ass:active-residual-margin}
For every \(0\le t<T\), \(\rho_t(P)>\underline\rho\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform interior-pivot condition, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rank-regular-law]\label{ass:rank-regular-law}
The primitive row law satisfies \(P_\xi\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rr}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior regular-point restriction, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996WeakConvergence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:family-compactness]\label{ass:family-compactness}
The set \(\mathcal K\) is compact in \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal E}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Compact uniformity set in a weighted weak topology, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996WeakConvergence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-envelope]\label{ass:uniform-envelope}
For every \(Q\in\mathcal K\), one common \(\mathcal E_T\) dominates in absolute value every coordinate of \([\phi_T(\xi;P_\xi)]_Q\), \([I_T(\xi)]_Q\), \([\zeta_T(\xi)]_Q\), and \([\psi_{\theta,T}(\xi)]_Q\), every test function in \(\mathcal F_{0X,T}\), and every message derivative through total order three appearing in \(\mathfrak R_T\), \(\dot{\mathfrak R}_T\), \(\Psi_T\), or \(D\Psi_T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform integrable-envelope condition, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996WeakConvergence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-envelope-moment]\label{ass:uniform-envelope-moment}
\(\sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}P_{\xi,Q}[\mathcal E_T^{2+\delta_{\mathrm{mom}}}]\le B_{\mathcal E}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform integrable-envelope moment condition, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996WeakConvergence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-chart-interior]\label{ass:uniform-chart-interior}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}\operatorname{dist}(\beta_{0,Q},\partial\mathcal B)\ge\varepsilon_{\mathcal B}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform interiority of a finite-dimensional parameter, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-identification]\label{ass:uniform-identification}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}\inf_{\beta\in\mathcal B:\|\beta-\beta_{0,Q}\|\ge\varepsilon_{\mathrm{id}}}\|\mathfrak r_Q(\beta)\|\ge c_{\mathrm{id}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly separated minimum-distance zero, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-mean-rank]\label{ass:uniform-mean-rank}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}s_{\min}([J]_Q)\ge\underline j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform minimum-distance Jacobian rank, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-variance-rank]\label{ass:uniform-variance-rank}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}s_{\min}([X_v]_Q)\ge\underline x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform variance-design excitation, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-overlap]\label{ass:uniform-overlap}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}\min_{0\le t<T}\min\{p_{t,Q},1-p_{t,Q}\}\ge\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform Bernoulli-design overlap, \cite{ShiraniBayati2024CausalMP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-gram-margin]\label{ass:uniform-gram-margin}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}\min_{1\le t<T}\lambda_{\min}([K_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}]_Q)\ge2\underline\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform nonsingularity of finite Gram blocks, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-residual-margin]\label{ass:uniform-residual-margin}
\(\inf_{Q\in\mathcal K}\min_{0\le t<T}[\rho_t]_Q\ge2\underline\rho\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform interior-pivot separation, \cite{NeweyMcFadden1994LargeSample}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:rank-regular-domain]\label{def:rank-regular-domain}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} All-active full-rank row-law interior \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rr}}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rr}}=\{P:\lambda_{\min}\{K_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}(P)\}>\underline\kappa\text{ for every }1\le t<T,\text{ and }\rho_t(P)>\underline\rho\text{ for every }0\le t<T\}\), where \(D_t^{\star}=\{0,\ldots,t-1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}retained-\allowbreak{}gram-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}active-\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}margin}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:regular-index-topology]\label{def:regular-index-topology}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Envelope-weighted weak model-index topology \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal E}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} On primitive-row laws, take the initial topology generated by the maps \(P\mapsto P[f]\) for every bounded continuous test function and the moment map \(P\mapsto P[\mathcal E_T^{2+\delta_{\mathrm{mom}}}]\). Thus convergence is ordinary weak convergence plus convergence of the common envelope moment. Equip \(\vartheta_Q=(\beta_{0,Q},\sigma_Q^2,\tau_Q^2,p_{0:T-1,Q})\) with its Euclidean topology. Their product is \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal E}\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-n-gaussian-bridge]\label{lem:finite-n-gaussian-bridge}
Fix \(N\), \(T\), and \(0\le t<T\). On \(\mathcal A_{N,t}^{\mathrm{act}}\), Gaussian row conditioning gives the exact regular conditional law \[\mathcal L\!\left((H_{it}^{G,N})_{i=1}^N\mid\mathscr F_{N,t}\right)=\mathcal L\!\left(\left((\lambda_t^{N})^{\top}H_{i,D_t^{\star}}^{G,N}+\sqrt{\rho_t^{N}}Z_{it}^{\circ}\right)_{i=1}^N\mid\mathscr F_{N,t}\right).\] Conditionally on \(\mathscr F_{N,t}\), the \(Z_{it}^{\circ}\) are independent standard Gaussians, \(\lambda_t^{N}=(K_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}^{N})^{-1}K_{D_t^{\star},t}^{N}\), and \(\rho_t^{N}=K_{tt}^{N}-K_{t,D_t^{\star}}^{N}(K_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}^{N})^{-1}K_{D_t^{\star},t}^{N}\). Iterating these kernels through \(t=0,\ldots,T-1\), stopping at the first exit from the all-active domain, yields conditional equality in law between the stopped observed reused-\(G\) field--outcome--message path and the stopped auxiliary path in \(\text{def:finite-n-bridge-recursion}\). On the event of no exit, the equality holds jointly for the complete paths and every displayed measurable statistic, including \(M_N\), \(r_N\), \(\widehat v\), message Gram products, and mean- and variance-score moments.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(U_{<t}=(\mathbf U_0,\ldots,\mathbf U_{t-1})\).  On \(\mathcal A_{N,t}^{\mathrm{act}}\), this matrix and \(\mathbf U_t\) are \(\mathscr F_{N,t}\)-measurable and \(U_{<t}^{\top}U_{<t}/N=K^N_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}\) is invertible.  For row \(i\), write \(G_i=(G_{i1},\ldots,G_{iN})^{\top}\).  Conditional on the primitives and the previously revealed products, the only restrictions on \(G_i\) are
\[
 N^{-1/2}U_{<t}^{\top}G_i=H^{G,N}_{i,D_t^{\star}}.
\]
Indeed, by the structural recursion, every message through date \(t\) is a measurable function of the primitives and the fields before date \(t\); hence conditioning additionally on the message columns introduces no further restriction.  Orthogonally decompose
\[
 \mathbf U_t=U_{<t}\lambda_t^N+v_t^N,
 \qquad U_{<t}^{\top}v_t^N=0.
\]
The normal equations and the definitions in the statement give
\[
 \lambda_t^N=(K^N_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}})^{-1}K^N_{D_t^{\star},t},
 \qquad N^{-1}\|v_t^N\|^2=\rho_t^N>0.
\]
The standard normal density is invariant under orthogonal transformations.  Completing the square in coordinates along \(\operatorname{span}(U_{<t})\) and its orthogonal complement therefore shows directly that, conditional on the displayed linear restrictions, the orthogonal component of \(G_i\) remains standard Gaussian and is independent of its fixed projected component.  Consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_{it}^{G,N}
 &=N^{-1/2}G_i^{\top}U_{<t}\lambda_t^N+N^{-1/2}G_i^{\top}v_t^N\\
 &=(\lambda_t^N)^{\top}H^{G,N}_{i,D_t^{\star}}+\sqrt{\rho_t^N}\,Z_{it}^{\circ}
 \end{aligned}
\]
in regular conditional law.  The rows of \(G\) are independent by \(\text{ass:gaussian-interactions}\), so the last coordinates are conditionally independent over \(i\).  By \(\text{ass:primitive-independence}\), they are also independent of the conditioned primitives.  At \(t=0\) the same calculation uses the empty projection and gives \(H_{i0}^{G,N}=\sqrt{K_{00}^N}Z_{i0}^{\circ}\).

It remains to justify the joint, adaptive assertion.  Suppose the two stopped paths have the same joint law through date \(t-1\).  Their current messages and hence \(K^N\), \(\lambda_t^N\), \(\rho_t^N\), and the active-event indicator are the same measurable functions of that history.  The conditional kernel just computed is therefore the kernel used in \(\text{def:finite-n-bridge-recursion}\).  Integrating the common past law against this common kernel proves equality through date \(t\).  Induction over the fixed set \(t=0,\ldots,T-1\), permitted by \(\text{ass:fixed-horizon}\), proves equality of the stopped paths.  On no exit, every statistic listed in the claim is a measurable function of the complete path, so equality in law is preserved under each such map.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This fixed-sample bridge is the exact probabilistic link from the observed reused matrix to the auxiliary recursion differentiated in the CFSE. \textit{Gap.} Bayati and Montanari provide adaptive Gaussian conditioning for AMP with an Onsager correction, whereas this finite-\(N\), full-past equality in law with empirical Gram coefficients is not stated for the non-Onsager Causal-MP recursion. \textit{Consumer.} The public CausalMP implementation can use the recursion as an exact conditional simulator and covariance-engine primitive.

\begin{lemma}[lem:empirical-interior]\label{lem:empirical-interior}
For every admissible compact family \(\mathcal K\), \(\sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}P_{N,Q}\{\mathbb P_{N,Q}\notin\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rr}}\}\to0\). Consequently, uniformly over \(Q\in\mathcal K\), the empirical moment vector is computed by the same all-active full-rank recursion with probability tending to one; every \(K_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}}^{N}\) has smallest eigenvalue exceeding \(\underline\kappa\), and every \(\rho_t^{N}\) exceeds \(\underline\rho\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an admissible \(\mathcal K\).  In the auxiliary representation, all coordinates at date zero are functions of independent primitive rows.  We prove by induction on \(t\) that every empirical mean and Gram entry generated through date \(t\) converges to its population counterpart uniformly over \(Q\in\mathcal K\).  At the initial step, Chebyshev's inequality and \(\text{ass:uniform-envelope-moment}\) give, for each of the finitely many required integrands \(f_Q\),
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}P_{N,Q}\!left(\left|\mathbb P_{N,Q}f_Q-P_{\xi,Q}f_Q\right|>u\right)
 \le {\sup_QP_{\xi,Q}f_Q^2\over Nu^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Suppose the assertion holds through date \(t-1\).  The inverse identity
\[
 A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}
\]
shows that inversion is uniformly continuous when the two smallest eigenvalues are at least \(\underline\kappa\).  Likewise, for \(x,y\ge\underline\rho\),
\[
 |\sqrt x-\sqrt y|={|x-y|\over \sqrt x+\sqrt y}
 \le { |x-y|\over2\sqrt{\underline\rho}}.
\]
Thus the empirical projection coefficients and residual energies converge uniformly.  The smooth-message condition and the common envelope then give convergence of the newly generated messages in empirical \(L^2\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives convergence of all new Gram products.  Applying the preceding Chebyshev bound to the finitely many population-row integrands completes the induction.

The population margins in \(\text{ass:uniform-gram-margin}\) and \(\text{ass:uniform-residual-margin}\) are \(2\underline\kappa\) and \(2\underline\rho\).  Since \(T\) is fixed, the union bound over all retained blocks and pivots and the just-proved uniform convergence imply
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}P_{N,Q}\!left(min_{1\le t<T}\lambda_{\min}(K^N_{D_t^{\star}D_t^{\star}})\le\underline\kappa
 \text{ or }\min_{0\le t<T}\rho_t^N\le\underline\rho\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This event is precisely the possible failure of membership in the open set \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rr}}\) for the recursively generated empirical row law.  Finally, \(\text{def:moment-vector}\) agrees with the all-active expression on this event, proving every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This interior-stability result makes the observable empirical statistic use the same full-rank recursion as its population limit. \textit{Gap.} Standard laws of large numbers do not by themselves record stability of every recursively generated Gram inverse and Schur residual in this adaptive reused-matrix system. \textit{Consumer.} The covariance implementation may run the ordinary full-rank derivative recursion without data-dependent deletion on events whose probability tends to one.

\begin{theorem}[thm:joint-cfse]\label{thm:joint-cfse}
For every admissible compact family \(\mathcal K\), \(\sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}P_{N,Q}\{\mathbb P_{N,Q}\notin\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rr}}\}\to0\), and \[\sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L_{P_{N,Q}}\!\left(\sqrt N\{[M_N]_Q-[M_T]_Q\},\sqrt N\{[\widehat P_{0X}]_Q-P_{0X,Q}\}\right),\mathcal L\{[\mathbb Z_T^{\mathrm{full}}]_Q\}\right)\to0\] in \(\mathbb R^{\dim(M_T)}\times\ell^\infty(\mathcal F_{0X,T})\). At each \(Q\), the covariance is the single bilinear formula defining \([\mathbb Z_T^{\mathrm{full}}]_Q\); hence \(\operatorname{Var}([Z_M]_Q)=[\Gamma_T]_Q\), including every cross-covariance between the initial empirical-process block and the reused-matrix mean, energy, Gram, and score blocks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is \(\text{lem:empirical-interior}\).  Enumerate the finite class \(\mathcal F_{0X,T}=\{f_1,\ldots,f_m\}\), and define the centered augmented row vector
\[
 V_Q(\xi)=\left([I_T(\xi)]_Q,
 (f_\ell(Y_0,X)-P_{0X,Q}f_\ell)_{\ell=1}^m\right).
\]
The resolved adaptive-linearization theorem gives, uniformly over \(Q\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \left(\sqrt N\{[M_N]_Q-[M_T]_Q\},
 \sqrt N\{[\widehat P_{0X}]_Q-P_{0X,Q}\}\right)
 ={1\over\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^NV_Q(\xi_i)+o_{P_{N,Q}}(1).
\]
The rows in the auxiliary representation are independent with mean zero.  Moreover, \(\text{ass:uniform-envelope}\) and \(\text{ass:uniform-envelope-moment}\) imply the uniform Lindeberg bound
\[
 \sup_{Q\in\mathcal K}P_{\xi,Q}!left[|V_Q\|^2
 \mathbf 1\{\|V_Q\|>u\sqrt N\}\right]
 \le {C B_{\mathcal E}\over u^{\delta_{\mathrm{mom}}}N^{\delta_{\mathrm{mom}}/2}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
For completeness, this bound yields the required triangular-array Gaussian limit directly.  For a fixed vector \(b\), truncate \(b^{\top}V_Q\) at \(u\sqrt N\), expand \(e^{ix}=1+ix-x^2/2+o(x^2)\) uniformly on the truncated set, and use the last display on its complement.  Thus
\[
 \left(P_{\xi,Q}e^{i b^{\top}V_Q/\sqrt N}\right)^N
 -\exp\{-\tfrac12b^{\top}\Sigma_Qb\}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \Sigma_Q=P_{\xi,Q}[V_QV_Q^{\top}],
\]
along every sequence \(Q=Q_N\) for which \(\Sigma_{Q_N}\) converges.

To make the convergence uniform, argue by contradiction.  Any offending sequence \(Q_N\in\mathcal K\) has a subsequence converging in \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal E}\), by compactness.  The finite recursion, the uniform inverse and pivot margins, and ordinary dominated continuity show that \(V_{Q_N}\) and its covariance converge along this subsequence; the \((2+\delta_{\mathrm{mom}})\)-moment bound supplies uniform integrability.  The characteristic-function calculation therefore gives convergence to the same Gaussian law as \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{Q_N})\).  In fixed dimension, truncating to a large compact ball and using a finite Lipschitz net converts this convergence into bounded-Lipschitz convergence, contradicting the offending sequence.  Hence the asserted supremum tends to zero.

By the definition of \(V_Q\), its covariance is exactly the single bilinear covariance displayed for \([\mathbb Z_T^{\mathrm{full}}]_Q\).  Taking \(f=g=0\) gives \(\operatorname{Var}([Z_M]_Q)=[\Gamma_T]_Q\), and taking one or both of \(f,g\) nonzero gives all claimed cross-blocks.  The exact finite-\(N\) bridge transfers this auxiliary-row conclusion to \(P_{N,Q}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The joint fluctuation law is the missing inferential layer between state evolution and causal-path uncertainty. \textit{Gap.} Shirani and Bayati establish first-order state evolution and consistency, while available AMP results do not give this aggregate covariance recursion. \textit{Consumer.} Configured Causal-MP regressions can replace an independence-based sandwich matrix by \(\Gamma_T\).

\begin{proposition}[prop:residual-cancellation]\label{prop:residual-cancellation}
For every admissible compact family \(\mathcal K\), uniformly over \(Q\in\mathcal K\), the \(Q\)-evaluations of \(r_N(\beta_0)\) and \(\widehat v-\sigma^2(N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^NU_{it}^2)_{t=0}^{T-1}-\tau^2\mathbf 1_T\) have a joint centered Gaussian root-\(N\) limit with covariance \([\operatorname{blockdiag}\{C,2(C\circ C)\}]_Q\) and zero mixed block.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the auxiliary realization and write \(s=\sigma^2\), \(e=\tau^2\).  On the all-active event the sequential Gaussian projection recursion has the pathwise triangular form
\[
 H_i^N=B_NZ_i,
 \qquad B_NB_N^{\top}=K^N,
\]
where \(Z_i=(Z_{i0}^{\circ},\ldots,Z_{i,T-1}^{\circ})^{\top}\) are independent standard Gaussian vectors.  Adaptive linearization and the common margins imply \(K^N\to K\) uniformly in probability.  Because every pivot is bounded below, the triangular square-root map is continuous there; hence \(B_N\to B\) uniformly in probability, where \(BB^{\top}=K\).  Put
\[
 R_i^N=\sqrt s\,B_NZ_i+\epsilon_i,
 \qquad R_i=\sqrt s\,BZ_i+\epsilon_i.
\]
No independence between \(B_N\) and the rows is used below.

The structural equation gives the exact identities
\[
 r_N(\beta_0)=\overline R^{,N},
 \qquad
 \widehat v- s(q_t^N)_{t=0}^{T-1}-e\mathbf 1_T
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left\{N^{-1}\sum_iR_i^N(R_i^N)^{\top}-(sK^N+eI_T)\right\}
 -\overline R^{,N}\circ\overline R^{,N}.
\]
Since \(\sqrt N\,\overline Z=O_P(1)\), \(\sqrt N\,\overline\epsilon=O_P(1)\), and \(B_N-B=o_P(1)\),
\[
 \sqrt N\,\overline R^{,N}={1\over\sqrt N}\sum_iR_i+o_P(1).
\]
For second moments, direct multiplication yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &N^{-1}\sum_iR_i^N(R_i^N)^{\top}-(sK^N+eI_T)\\
 &=sB_N\left(N^{-1}\sum_iZ_iZ_i^{\top}-I_T\right)B_N^{\top}
 +\sqrt s B_NN^{-1}\sum_iZ_i\epsilon_i^{\top}
 +\sqrt s N^{-1}\sum_i\epsilon_iZ_i^{\top}B_N^{\top}
 +N^{-1}\sum_i\epsilon_i\epsilon_i^{\top}-eI_T.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each centered bracket is \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\) by its finite second moment.  Therefore replacing \(B_N\) by \(B\) changes the root-\(N\) expression by \(o_P(1)\), even though \(B_N\) is data dependent.  Also \(\sqrt N\,\overline R^{,N}\circ\overline R^{,N}=o_P(1)\).  We have proved the joint representation
\[
 \sqrt N\binom{r_N(\beta_0)}{\widehat v-sq^N-e\mathbf1_T}
 ={1\over\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^N
 \binom{R_i}{R_i\circ R_i-\operatorname{diag}(C)}+o_P(1).
\]
Here the \(R_i\) are independent centered Gaussian vectors with covariance \(C=sK+eI_T\), by \(\text{ass:gaussian-innovations}\) and primitive independence.

This representation also identifies the row influence, not merely its covariance.  Adaptive linearization applied to the same two linear coordinate maps gives the alternative expansion with summands \((S_mI_T,S_vI_T)\).  Subtracting the two expansions produces a normalized sum of independent, centered copies of
\[
 D(\xi)=\binom{S_mI_T(\xi)-R}{S_vI_T(\xi)-\{R\circ R-\operatorname{diag}(C)\}}
\]
that converges to zero in probability.  Truncating \(D\) at level \(o(\sqrt N)\) and expanding \(e^{iu^{\top}D/\sqrt N}\) to second order, with the common \((2+\delta_{\mathrm{mom}})\)-moment controlling the tail, also makes this sum converge to \(\mathcal N(0,P_\xi[DD^{\top}])\).  Uniqueness of the probability limit forces \(P_\xi[DD^{\top}]=0\).  Thus
\[
 S_mI_T(\xi)=R,
 \qquad S_vI_T(\xi)=R\circ R-\operatorname{diag}(C)
\]
almost surely.  This is the influence-level cancellation used by the feasible covariance construction.

The same second-order characteristic-function expansion, with the common envelope providing uniform tail control, gives the uniform joint Gaussian limit.  Differentiating the centered Gaussian moment-generating function, or equivalently pairing the four factors in its fourth moment, gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(R_r,R_s)=C_{rs},\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(R_r^2,R_s^2)=2C_{rs}^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(R_r,R_s^2)=0.
\]
Thus the covariance is \(\operatorname{blockdiag}\{C,2(C\circ C)\}\).  All convergence and replacement bounds are uniform because the family has common envelope and interior constants.  Finally, the bridge lemma transfers the conclusion to the observed recursion.
\end{proof}

 =h_N\phi_T(\,cdot\,;P)+P\dot\phi_T(\,cdot\,;P)[h_N]+o_P(N^{-1/2}).
\]
The potentially additional term \(h_N\dot\phi_T[h_N]\) is a finite sum of products of two coordinates bounded by \(D_{N,T}^2\), and hence is \(O_P(N^{-1})\).  Linearity of the derivative in its direction gives
\[
 P\dot\phi_T[h_N]
 ={1\over N}\sum_{i=1}^NP\dot\phi_T[\delta_{\xi_i}-P].
\]
Multiplication by \(\sqrt N\) and the definition of \(I_T\) prove the desired von Mises expansion.  For every \(f\in\mathcal F_{0X,T}\),
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat P_{0X}-P_{0X})f
 =N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N\{f(Y_{i0},X_i)-P_{0X}f\}
\]
exactly, so adjoining the finite list preserves the expansion.  Uniformity follows from the common constants in \(\text{def:compact-regular-family}\) at every displayed bound.  The empirical-interior lemma makes the complement negligible uniformly, and the bridge lemma transfers the conclusion from the auxiliary recursion to the observed reused-matrix array.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The target is the deterministic population-limit path, not a realized finite-network counterfactual path. The theory is fixed-\(T\), Gaussian-interaction, and confined to compact regular families \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal Q_T\) with the named common envelope, chart, identification, rank, overlap, Gram, and Schur-residual constants in \(\text{def:compact-regular-family}\). Rank-changing laws and exact zero-pivot laws are excluded boundary cases; no continuity or differentiability is claimed there. In \(\text{def:rank-boundary-perturbation}\), \(U_{i1}^{(\delta)}=U_{i0}+\delta V_i\) gives residual energy \(\delta^2\) and field \(H_{i0}+|\delta|Z_{i1}^{\circ}\), so the two-sided derivative fails at the redundant-message boundary. Weak energy designs, variance boundaries, fixed positive ridge, model selection, and growing horizons require separate limits.
