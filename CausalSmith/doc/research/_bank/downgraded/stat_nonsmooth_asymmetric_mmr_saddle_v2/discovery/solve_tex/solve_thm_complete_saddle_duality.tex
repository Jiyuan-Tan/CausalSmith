%%% FIELD proof-endpoint-reduction
Fix \(h\in H\) and write \(q=q_{\delta}(h)\in[0,1]\), where the range follows from \(0\leq\delta\leq1\) in \(\mathrm{def:rule-class}\). If the welfare gain is \(x\), the oracle treats when \(x>0\) and does not treat when \(x<0\). Hence the expected regret of a rule whose treatment probability is \(q\) is
\[
 \rho(x,q)=x_{+}(1-q)+(-x)_{+}q.
\]
This is the definition of regret relative to the better of the two constant actions. On \([0,\infty)\), \(x\mapsto\rho(x,q)=x(1-q)\) is nondecreasing, whereas on \((-\infty,0]\), \(x\mapsto\rho(x,q)=(-x)q\) is nonincreasing as \(x\) increases. Because \(\ell(h)\leq u(h)\) by \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty-identified-interval}\), splitting the interval at zero, with an empty side omitted, gives
\[
 \sup_{x\in[\ell(h),u(h)]}\rho(x,q)
 =\max\bigl\{[u(h)]_{+}(1-q),[-\ell(h)]_{+}q\bigr\}
 =\max\bigl\{c_{+}(h)[1-q_{\delta}(h)],c_{-}(h)q_{\delta}(h)\bigr\}.
\]
The two entries in the last maximum are precisely \(r_{(h,+)}(\delta)\) and \(r_{(h,-)}(\delta)\) by \(\mathrm{def:endpoint-game}\). Taking the supremum over \(h\in H\) therefore gives the same value as taking the supremum over \(S=H\times\{+,-\}\), which proves the reduction without imposing any restriction on \(\delta\).

%%% FIELD proof-complete-saddle-duality
Fix \(\mathsf G\in\mathcal G_p\). All endpoint functions are continuous because each is a finite maximum or minimum of affine functions. Thus \(c_{+}\) and \(c_{-}\) are continuous and bounded on the compact set \(H\), by \(\mathrm{ass:compact-polyhedral-domain}\). Positive definiteness in \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate-gaussian-shift}\) implies that every \(N(h,\Sigma)\) has a strictly positive Lebesgue density.

We first establish the compactness and continuity facts used below. Put \(\mu=N(0,\Sigma)\) and
\[
 L_h(z)=\frac{dN(h,\Sigma)}{d\mu}(z)
 =\exp\!\left(h^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}z-\frac12h^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}h\right).
\]
Then \(L_h\in L^2(\mu)\), and \(h\mapsto L_h\) is continuous in \(L^2(\mu)\); both assertions follow by evaluating Gaussian exponential moments. In particular,
\[
 q_{\delta}(h)=\int\delta(z)L_h(z)\,d\mu(z).
\]
Every sequence in \(\mathcal D\) is bounded in the Hilbert space \(L^2(\mu)\). A diagonal extraction against a countable orthonormal basis produces a weakly convergent subsequence. If its weak limit is \(d\), testing against indicators of the sets \(\{d<0\}\) and \(\{d>1\}\), and using weak closedness of closed convex sets, gives \(0\leq d\leq1\) almost everywhere. Since \(\mu\) and \(\lambda^p\) are equivalent, \(d\) represents an element of \(\mathcal D\). Consequently, for every finite collection of states, the corresponding risk-vector set
\[
 \bigl\{(r_{s_1}(\delta),\ldots,r_{s_N}(\delta)):\delta\in\mathcal D\bigr\}\subset\mathbb R^N
\]
is compact and convex. Moreover, the \(L^1\)-continuity of Gaussian translations gives
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}|q_{\delta}(h)-q_{\delta}(h')|
 \leq\int|\varphi_{\Sigma}(z-h)-\varphi_{\Sigma}(z-h')|\,dz\longrightarrow0
\]
as \(h'\to h\). Together with uniform continuity of \(c_{+}\) and \(c_{-}\), this proves that \(s\mapsto r_s(\delta)\) is equicontinuous over \(\delta\in\mathcal D\).

For completeness, consider first a finite state set \(T=\{s_1,\ldots,s_N\}\). Let \(C\) be the compact convex risk-vector set just displayed and put
\[
 t_T=\min_{x\in C}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}x_i.
\]
The following elementary finite-dimensional separation argument yields
\[
 t_T=\max_{a\in\Delta_N}\min_{x\in C}\sum_{i=1}^Na_ix_i. \tag{1}
\]
For \(\epsilon>0\), the compact set \(C\) and the closed lower orthant \(O_{\epsilon}=\{y:y_i\leq t_T-\epsilon\ \forall i\}\) are disjoint. Choose a closest pair. The vector joining the pair separates the two convex sets; because \(O_{\epsilon}\) is unbounded in every negative coordinate direction, every coordinate of the separating normal is nonnegative. Normalize its coordinates to sum to one and let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Compactness of \(\Delta_N\) gives \(a\in\Delta_N\) with \(\min_{x\in C}a^{\top}x\geq t_T\). The reverse inequality follows from \(a^{\top}x\leq\max_i x_i\). This proves (1).

Choose nested finite sets \(T_m\subset S\) with dense union and let \(t_m=t_{T_m}\). Compactness of \(S\) and the equicontinuity proved above imply that there is \(e_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 0\leq R(\delta)-\max_{s\in T_m}r_s(\delta)\leq e_m
 \qquad\text{for every }\delta\in\mathcal D. \tag{2}
\]
It follows that \(t_m\uparrow v\). Let \(\delta_m\) minimize the finite problem. A weak \(L^2(\mu)\) subsequential limit \(\delta^{\star}\in\mathcal D\) satisfies \(r_s(\delta^{\star})\leq v\) first for every \(s\) in the dense union and then, by continuity in \(s\), for every \(s\in S\). Hence \(R(\delta^{\star})=v\), so the primal minimum is attained.

Define the bilinear payoff
\[
 \mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=\int_S r_s(\delta)\,d\pi(s).
\]
For any \(\pi\in\Pi\), boundedness permits Tonelli's theorem and gives
\[
 \mathcal L(\delta,\pi)
 =\int_Hc_{+}(h)\,d\pi(h,+)
   +\int_{\mathbb R^p}\delta(z)\{A_{\pi,-}(z)-A_{\pi,+}(z)\}\,dz. \tag{3}
\]
Pointwise minimization over \(0\leq\delta\leq1\) in (3) yields
\[
 \min_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)
 =\int_{\mathbb R^p}\min\{A_{\pi,+}(z),A_{\pi,-}(z)\}\,dz=b(\pi). \tag{4}
\]
The minimum is attained, and the nonnegative pointwise excess in (3) vanishes exactly when
\[
 \delta=1\ \text{on }\{B_{\pi}>0\},\qquad
 \delta=0\ \text{on }\{B_{\pi}<0\}, \tag{5}
\]
with arbitrary values on \(\{B_{\pi}=0\}\). Thus (5) proves, rather than assumes, the asserted Bayes-response characterization.

For each finite \(T_m\), equations (1) and (4) give a prior \(\pi_m\) supported on \(T_m\) with \(b(\pi_m)=t_m\). Probability measures on the compact metric space \(S\) are sequentially compact for weak convergence: one direct proof takes successively finer finite Borel partitions with null boundaries, extracts a diagonal subsequence of all cell masses, and extends the consistent limiting masses to a probability measure. Let \(\pi_m\) converge along such a subsequence to \(\pi^{\star}\). For fixed \(\delta\), continuity of \(s\mapsto r_s(\delta)\) gives continuity of \(\pi\mapsto\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)\). Therefore \(b=\inf_{\delta}\mathcal L(\delta,\cdot)\) is upper semicontinuous, so
\[
 b(\pi^{\star})\geq\limsup_m b(\pi_m)=v. \tag{6}
\]
Weak duality, obtained from \(b(\pi)\leq\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)\leq R(\delta)\), gives \(b(\pi)\leq v\) for every \(\pi\). Thus equality holds in (6), the dual maximum is attained, and
\[
 v=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\max_{s\in S}r_s(\delta)
  =\max_{\pi\in\Pi}b(\pi). \tag{7}
\]

We next characterize every saddle. If \((\delta,\pi)\) is a saddle, its payoff equals the common value in (7). Minimization against \(\pi\) and (4) imply the sign rule (5). Maximization against \(\delta\) implies \(r_s(\delta)\leq v\) for every \(s\). Finally,
\[
 0=v-\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=\int_S\{v-r_s(\delta)\}\,d\pi(s),
\]
whose integrand is nonnegative; hence \(r_s(\delta)=v\) for \(\pi\)-almost every \(s\). Conversely, the three displayed conditions imply by (4) that \(b(\pi)=\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=v\), while the global inequalities imply \(R(\delta)=v\). For arbitrary \(\delta'\) and \(\pi'\),
\[
 \mathcal L(\delta,\pi')\leq R(\delta)=v
 =b(\pi)\leq\mathcal L(\delta',\pi),
\]
so the pair is a saddle.

The set \(\mathcal D^{\star}\) is nonempty by primal attainment and convex because \(R\), a supremum of affine maps, is convex. The set \(\Pi^{\star}\) is nonempty by dual attainment and convex because \(b\), an infimum of affine maps of \(\pi\), is concave. If \(\delta\in\mathcal D^{\star}\) and \(\pi\in\Pi^{\star}\), then
\[
 v=b(\pi)\leq\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)\leq R(\delta)=v,
\]
so every such pair is a saddle.

Finally, \(\mathfrak F^{\star}\) is finite because the two endpoints have finitely many affine pieces. If it is nonempty, choose \(\pi_F\) as in \(\mathrm{def:face-complete-prior}\) for every \(F\in\mathfrak F^{\star}\), and set
\[
 \bar\pi=|\mathfrak F^{\star}|^{-1}\sum_{F\in\mathfrak F^{\star}}\pi_F.
\]
Convexity of \(\Pi^{\star}\) puts \(\bar\pi\) in \(\Pi^{\star}\), and every required relative interior receives positive \(\bar\pi\)-mass. Thus \(\bar\pi\in\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\). If \(\mathfrak F^{\star}=\varnothing\), the defining face-completeness requirement is vacuous and every member of the nonempty set \(\Pi^{\star}\) belongs to \(\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\).

Let
\[
 v_m=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\max_{s\in S_m}r_s(\delta).
\]
The restricted saddle chosen in \(\mathrm{def:exchange-certificate}\) and the finite-state separation argument in \(\mathrm{thm:complete-saddle-duality}\) give
\[
 L_m=b(\pi_m)=v_m=\max_{s\in S_m}r_s(\delta_m). \tag{1}
\]
Weak duality and the definition of \(U_m\) therefore yield
\[
 L_m\leq v\leq U_m. \tag{2}
\]
Because \(S_m\subseteq S_{m+1}\), the values \(v_m=L_m\) are nondecreasing.

Equip the compact set \(S\) with any compatible metric that separates its two sign components, and put
\[
 d_m=\sup_{s\in S}\inf_{t\in S_m}d_S(s,t).
\]
The nested meshes contain successive points of a dense sequence, so compactness of \(S\) gives \(d_m\to0\). As established equation by equation in \(\mathrm{thm:complete-saddle-duality}\), continuity of the endpoint weights and \(L^1\)-continuity of Gaussian translations imply a deterministic modulus \(\zeta(t)\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 |r_s(\delta)-r_t(\delta)|\leq\zeta(d_S(s,t))
 \quad\text{for all }s,t\text{ with the same sign and every }\delta\in\mathcal D.
\]
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(m\) (and this is all that is needed for the limit),
\[
 0\leq U_m-L_m
 =R(\delta_m)-\max_{s\in S_m}r_s(\delta_m)
 \leq\zeta(d_m). \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) prove \(G_m\to0\) and \(L_m\uparrow v\).

Suppose \(\pi_{m_j}\Rightarrow\bar\pi\). For fixed \(\delta\), the map \(\pi\mapsto\int r_s(\delta)\,d\pi(s)\) is weakly continuous. Hence \(b\), its infimum over \(\delta\), is upper semicontinuous. Using \(b(\pi_{m_j})=L_{m_j}\to v\) and weak duality,
\[
 v\geq b(\bar\pi)\geq\limsup_j b(\pi_{m_j})=v,
\]
so \(\bar\pi\in\Pi^{\star}\).

Now suppose that \(\delta_{m_j}\to\bar\delta\) in \(\sigma(L^{\infty}(\lambda^p),L^1(\lambda^p))\) and \(G_{m_j}\to0\). Each \(r_s\) is weak-star continuous because its only rule-dependent term is integration against the \(L^1\) density \(\varphi_{\Sigma}(\,\cdot-h)\). Therefore \(R=\sup_s r_s\) is weak-star lower semicontinuous, and
\[
 R(\bar\delta)\leq\liminf_jR(\delta_{m_j})
 =\liminf_jU_{m_j}=v.
\]
The definition of \(v\) gives the reverse inequality. Thus \(R(\bar\delta)=v\), so \(\bar\delta\in\mathcal D^{\star}\). No step bounds \(d_m\) as a function of \(m\), so the argument asserts neither an iteration complexity nor a convergence rate.

 L\leq v_{\nu}\leq U=R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu}).

%%% FIELD proof-coexistence-witness
The triangle \(H_0\) is compact, convex, and has nonempty interior, and \(I_2\) is positive definite. Convexity of the unit \(\ell_{\infty}\)-ball and the fact that all three vertices have \(\ell_{\infty}\)-norm one imply
\[
 \|h\|_{\infty}\leq1\qquad(h\in H_0). \tag{1}
\]
The functions \(\ell_0\) and \(u_0\) are respectively a finite maximum and a finite minimum of affine functions, since
\[
 \ell_0(h)=\max_{\substack{i=1,2\\ \sigma\in\{-1,1\}}}\{-4/3+\sigma h_i\},
 \qquad
 u_0(h)=\min_{\substack{i=1,2\\ \sigma\in\{-1,1\}}}\{4/3-\sigma h_i\}.
\]
By (1), \(\ell_0(h)\leq-1/3<1/3\leq u_0(h)\). Also
\(0=\tfrac14(-1,-1)+\tfrac14(1,-1)+\tfrac12(0,1)\), so at \(h=0\) the interval strictly straddles zero. Thus all member conditions in \(\mathrm{def:game-class}\) hold. The point \((-1,1)=-(1,-1)\) is not in \(H_0\), because the only point of \(H_0\) with second coordinate one is \((0,1)\). Hence \(H_0\neq-H_0\), so the game is not in \(\mathcal G_2^{\mathrm{cs}}\).

By (1), both loss weights equal
\[
 c_{+}(h)=c_{-}(h)=c(h):=4/3-\|h\|_{\infty}. \tag{2}
\]
At \(h=0\), for every rule \(\delta\),
\[
 \max\{r_{(0,+)}(\delta),r_{(0,-)}(\delta)\}
 =\frac43\max\{1-q_{\delta}(0),q_{\delta}(0)\}\geq\frac23. \tag{3}
\]
The constant rule has \(q_{\delta_0}(h)=1/2\), so (2) gives \(R(\delta_0)\leq2/3\). Equation (3) proves \(v=2/3\) and \(\delta_0\in\mathcal D^{\star}\). It belongs to \(\mathcal D_1\): take any nonzero \(w\) and the constant Borel map \(g\equiv1/2\).

Let \(X\sim N(0,1)\) and define \(f(t)=E[\tanh(t+X)]\). Symmetry of \(X\) and oddness of \(\tanh\) make \(f\) odd. Dominated differentiation gives
\[
 f'(t)=E[\operatorname{sech}^2(t+X)]\in(0,1],
\]
so \(f\) has the sign of \(t\) and \(|f(t)|\leq|t|\). Independence of the coordinates under \(N(h,I_2)\) yields
\[
 q_{\delta_{\eta}}(h)=\frac12+\eta f(h_1)f(h_2). \tag{4}
\]
Put \(r=\|h\|_{\infty}\leq1\). From (2)--(4), \(0<\eta\leq1/2\), and \(|h_1h_2|\leq r^2\), either signed risk is at most
\[
 (4/3-r)\left(\frac12+\frac12r^2\right)\leq\frac23. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, after multiplying by two, the difference between the left side and \(4/3\) equals
\[
 r\left(\frac43r-1-r^2\right)<0\quad(0<r\leq1),
\]
because \(r^2-4r/3+1>0\); equality holds at \(r=0\). Thus \(R(\delta_{\eta})=2/3\), so \(\delta_{\eta}\in\mathcal D^{\star}\).

It remains to exclude a one-index representation. Suppose
\(\delta_{\eta}(z)=g(w^{\top}z)\) almost everywhere for \(w=(w_1,w_2)\neq0\), and put \(e=(-w_2,w_1)\). For every fixed \(t\), translation invariance of Lebesgue null sets gives
\(\delta_{\eta}(z+te)=\delta_{\eta}(z)\) almost everywhere. Both sides are continuous in \(z\), hence the equality holds everywhere. Differentiating at \(t=0\) gives
\[
 -w_2\operatorname{sech}^2(z_1)\tanh(z_2)
 +w_1\tanh(z_1)\operatorname{sech}^2(z_2)=0
 \qquad(z\in\mathbb R^2). \tag{6}
\]
Setting \(z_1=0\) in (6) gives \(w_2=0\), and setting \(z_2=0\) gives \(w_1=0\), a contradiction. Therefore \(\delta_{\eta}\notin\mathcal D_1\). The two optimal rules establish \(\mathsf C_{\mathrm{co}}\), and the exhibited member outside \(\mathcal G_2^{\mathrm{cs}}\) proves \(\mathcal G_2^{\mathrm{cs}}\subsetneq\mathcal G_2\).

Write \(\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=\int r_s(\delta)\,d\pi(s)\). Weak duality from \(\mathrm{thm:complete-saddle-duality}\) gives, for the certified pair,
\[
 b(\pi)\leq v\leq R(\delta_{\pi}). \tag{1}
\]
The certificate in \(\mathrm{def:curved-score-certificate}\) gives
\(R(\delta_{\pi})\leq q\leq b(\pi)\). Combining with (1) forces
\[
 R(\delta_{\pi})=q=b(\pi)=v. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\delta_{\pi}\in\mathcal D^{\star}\), \(\pi\in\Pi^{\star}\), and the pair is a saddle.

We next show that its Bayes response is unique almost everywhere. After combining atoms having the same mean, the finite score can be written
\[
 B_{\pi}(z)=\varphi_{\Sigma}(z)
 \sum_{i=1}^N d_i
 \exp\!\left(h_i^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}z-\frac12h_i^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}h_i\right), \tag{3}
\]
where the \(h_i\)'s are distinct and every remaining \(d_i\neq0\). The certificate says that (3) is not identically zero. Choose a vector \(e\) outside the finitely many hyperplanes
\(\{e:e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(h_i-h_j)=0\}\). On every affine line \(x+te\), the second factor in (3) is a linear combination of exponentials with distinct real exponents and nonzero coefficients. Such a combination has at most \(N-1\) zeros, counting multiplicities: divide by the smallest exponential, differentiate, and apply Rolle's theorem inductively. Fubini's theorem therefore gives
\[
 \lambda^2\{z:B_{\pi}(z)=0\}=0. \tag{4}
\]
Every \(\delta\in\mathcal D^{\star}\) forms a saddle with this \(\pi\) by \(\mathrm{thm:complete-saddle-duality}\). Its Bayes-response characterization then fixes \(\delta=1\) on \(\{B_{\pi}>0\}\) and \(\delta=0\) on \(\{B_{\pi}<0\}\). Equation (4) proves \(\delta=\delta_{\pi}\) almost everywhere. Hence \(\mathcal D^{\star}=\{\delta_{\pi}\}\) modulo \(\lambda^2\)-null sets.

It remains to rule out every measurable one-index representation. Let \(\Omega=\{B_{\pi}>0\}\), so \(\delta_{\pi}=\mathbf1_{\Omega}\). Suppose, to the contrary, that \(\mathbf1_{\Omega}(z)=g(w^{\top}z)\) almost everywhere for some \(w\neq0\), and choose a nonzero \(e\perp w\). For every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \mathbf1_{\Omega}(z+te)=\mathbf1_{\Omega}(z)\quad\text{for almost every }z. \tag{5}
\]
Equation (5) says that the distributional derivative of \(\mathbf1_{\Omega}\) in direction \(e\) is zero: translate a compactly supported smooth test function, integrate, and differentiate at zero. On a neighborhood of any point of the regular arc \(\Gamma\), the implicit-function theorem and \(\nabla B_{\pi}\neq0\) identify the distributional boundary measure of \(\Omega\) as its ordinary \(C^1\) boundary measure with unit normal
\[
 n(z)=\frac{\nabla B_{\pi}(z)}{\|\nabla B_{\pi}(z)\|}.
\]
Integration by parts against test functions supported in that neighborhood turns the zero distributional derivative into \(e^{\top}n(z)=0\) for boundary-almost every \(z\in\Gamma\). Continuity of \(n\) upgrades this equality to every \(z\in\Gamma\). Hence the normal is everywhere parallel to the fixed vector \(w\), so the tangent is everywhere parallel to the fixed vector \(e\). The connected arc \(\Gamma\) is therefore contained in an affine line.

Let \(T(z)=(-\partial_2B_{\pi}(z),\partial_1B_{\pi}(z))\), a nonzero tangent to the regular level set. Along a straight level-set arc, twice differentiating \(B_{\pi}(z+te)=0\) gives \(e^{\top}\nabla^2B_{\pi}(z)e=0\). Since \(T\) is a nonzero multiple of \(e\),
\[
 \mathcal K_{\pi}(z)=T(z)^{\top}\nabla^2B_{\pi}(z)T(z)=0
 \qquad(z\in\Gamma),
\]
contradicting the certified strictly positive lower bound for \(|\mathcal K_{\pi}|\). Thus \(\delta_{\pi}\notin\mathcal D_1\). Uniqueness then gives \(\mathcal D^{\star}\cap\mathcal D_1=\varnothing\), which is \(\mathsf C_{\mathrm{none}}\) by \(\mathrm{def:indexability-categories}\).

We first prove the deterministic modulus. Write \(\mathsf G'=(H',\Sigma',\ell',u')\), suppose both \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\) and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G')\) hold, and put \(\rho=d_{\mathsf G}(\mathsf G',\mathsf G)\leq(2\kappa)^{-1}\). Every endpoint loss weight in either game is bounded by

 \kappa+\kappa^2=M_{\kappa}, \tag{1}

because every mean has norm at most \(\kappa\), and every affine intercept and normal has magnitude or norm at most \(\kappa\).

Fix a signed state \((h',\sigma)\) of \(\mathsf G'\). Hausdorff distance at most \(\rho\) between the corresponding endpoint graphs supplies a state \((h,\sigma)\) of \(\mathsf G\) such that

 \|h'-h\|\leq\rho,
 \qquad |c'_{\sigma}(h')-c_{\sigma}(h)|\leq\rho, \tag{2}

where the second inequality uses the fact that the positive-part map is one-Lipschitz. Put \(E=\Sigma^{-1/2}(\Sigma'-\Sigma)\Sigma^{-1/2}\). The eigenvalue bound and the definition of \(d_{\mathsf G}\) give
\(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\rho\leq1/2\). Direct integration of the two Gaussian densities gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\{N(h',\Sigma')\|N(h,\Sigma)\}
 =\frac12\left[\operatorname{tr}E-\log\det(I+E)
 +(h-h')^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(h-h')\right]. \tag{3}
\]
For \(|x|\leq1/2\), \(x-\log(1+x)\leq2x^2\), as follows by differentiating the two sides. Thus (3) is at most
\[
 \rho^2\left(p\kappa^2+\frac\kappa2\right). \tag{4}
\]
The total-variation inequality used here follows directly from scalar convexity: applying the log-sum inequality to the set where the first density exceeds the second reduces relative entropy to binary relative entropy, and two differentiations show that binary relative entropy is at least twice the squared difference of its success probabilities. Therefore
\[
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)/2}.
\]
Together with (4), this gives
\[
 \|N(h',\Sigma')-N(h,\Sigma)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\rho\sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}. \tag{5}
\]
For every \(0\leq\delta\leq1\), differences of expectations are bounded by total variation. Equations (1), (2), and (5) therefore imply, for the matched signed states,
\[
 |r'_{(h',\sigma)}(\delta)-r_{(h,\sigma)}(\delta)|
 \leq\rho+M_{\kappa}\rho\sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}
 =\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). \tag{6}
\]
Repeating the graph matching in the opposite direction and taking suprema proves
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}|R'(\delta)-R(\delta)|
 \leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). \tag{7}
\]
Taking infima in (7) gives \(|v'-v|\leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)\). For larger \(\rho\), both signed risks lie in \([0,M_{\kappa}]\), so the same two conclusions hold with the extended modulus \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)=M_{\kappa}\).

Apply (7) to \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\). The gap lemma gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v
 &\leq |R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})|
 +(R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v_{\nu})+|v_{\nu}-v|\\
 &\leq2\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu})+\varepsilon_{\nu}.
\end{aligned}\tag{8}
\]
The derivation uses neither uniqueness nor constancy of active faces, so it is uniform across every saddle, face, and indexability tie.

We turn to the original experiment. The almost-sure legality and boundedness assertions follow separately from \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-legality}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-kappa-bounds}\). They also hold under \(Q_{n,h}\), because the definition of that law gives
\[
 \mathcal L_{Q_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)
 =\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n). \tag{9}
\]
Condition on a realized legal game \(G'=\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\), and let \(d_{G'}\) be the certified rule chosen at tolerance \(\varepsilon_n>0\). Under \(Q_{n,h}\), its signal has law \(N(h,\widehat\Sigma_n)\). Match the true signed state \((h,\sigma)\) to the corresponding endpoint graph of \(G'\). The proof of (6), now with covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) on both sides of this single state comparison, bounds the conditional true signed loss by the matching risk in \(G'\) plus \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(d_{\mathsf G}(G',\mathsf G))\). The gap lemma and the value part of (7) then give, for either sign,
\[
 \operatorname{loss}_{Q_{n,h}}(h,\sigma\mid G')
 \leq v+\varepsilon_n
 +2\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(d_{\mathsf G}(G',\mathsf G)). \tag{10}
\]
The common marginal in (9) and the definition of \(\alpha_n\) imply, after averaging (10),
\[
 \sup_{h\in H}\max_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
 \operatorname{loss}_{Q_{n,h}}(h,\sigma)
 \leq v+\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n. \tag{11}
\]
Each loss integrand is between zero and \(M_{\kappa}\). The joint total-variation premise \(\mathrm{ass:joint-risk-transfer}\) therefore transfers (11) to the actual joint law at cost at most \(M_{\kappa}\gamma_n\), proving
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v
 \leq\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n+M_{\kappa}\gamma_n. \tag{12}
\]

For the lower bound, take any feasible original-experiment decision \(d:\mathcal X_n\to[0,1]\), including any auxiliary randomization through its conditional treatment probability, and compose it with the reverse kernel:
\[
 \delta_d(z)=\int_{\mathcal X_n}d(x)L_n(dx\mid z).
\]
This is a member of \(\mathcal D\). The reverse half of \(\mathrm{ass:uniform-two-sided-experiment-equivalence}\) and (1) imply, state by state,
\[
 r_s(\delta_d)\leq r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,s}(d)+M_{\kappa}\lambda_n.
\]
Here \(r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,(h,+)}(d)=c_{+}(h)\{1-E_{P_{n,h}}d\}\) and \(r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,(h,-)}(d)=c_{-}(h)E_{P_{n,h}}d\), so their supremum is exactly \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)\).
Taking the supremum over \(s\), then using \(v\leq R(\delta_d)\), yields
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)-v\geq-M_{\kappa}\lambda_n. \tag{13}
\]
Apply (13) to \(d=\widehat\delta_n\) and combine it with (12). If \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) through strictly positive tolerances and \(\alpha_n+\gamma_n+\lambda_n\to0\), the two bounds squeeze \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v\) to zero. No step supplies a rate for any of these externally controlled error sequences.
