Fix \(\kappa\geq 1\). Suppose \(\mathsf G\in\mathcal G_p\), \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\in\mathcal G_p\), \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\), and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G_{\nu})\) for every \(\nu\). Put \(\rho_{\nu}=d_{\mathsf G}(\mathsf G_{\nu},\mathsf G)\). Whenever \(\rho_{\nu}\leq(2\kappa)^{-1}\),
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\lvert R_{\nu}(\delta)-R(\delta)\rvert
 \leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu}),
 \qquad
 \lvert v_{\nu}-v\rvert\leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu}).
\]
Hence a certified \(\widetilde\delta_{\nu}\) computed at a strictly positive tolerance \(\varepsilon_{\nu}>0\) obeys
\[
 R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v
 \leq\varepsilon_{\nu}+2\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu}),
\]
including along sequences crossing endpoint-face ties and all three indexability categories.

For feasible transfer, suppose separately that the estimated game is legal and \(\kappa\)-bounded almost surely, that the joint total-variation condition \(\gamma_n\to0\) holds, and that the finite-tolerance certified selector is measurable. Then, for every \(n\) and \(h\in H\), \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\in\mathcal G_p\) and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\), \(P_{n,h}\)-almost surely. Moreover,
\[
 \mathcal L_{Q_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)
 =\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n),
\]
so both properties also hold \(Q_{n,h}\)-almost surely. On this common legal event, run \(\mathrm{def:stable-selector}\) at a strictly positive tolerance \(\varepsilon_n>0\), and put
\[
 \alpha_n=\sup_{h\in H}E_{P_{n,h}}\!\left[
 \overline\omega_{p,\kappa}\!\left(
 d_{\mathsf G}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n,\mathsf G)
 \right)\right].
\]
The resulting measurable feasible rule satisfies the upper bound
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v
 \leq\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n+M_{\kappa}\gamma_n.
\]
Separately, suppose there are \(h\)-independent reverse Markov kernels \(L_n\) such that
\[
 \lambda_n=sup_{h\in H}
 \left\lVert L_nN(h,\Sigma)-P_{n,h}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Then every measurable randomized original-experiment rule \(d:\mathcal X_n\to[0,1]\) satisfies
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)-v\geq-M_{\kappa}\lambda_n.
\]
In particular, when both sets of conditions hold,
\[
 -M_{\kappa}\lambda_n
 \leq\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v
 \leq\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n+M_{\kappa}\gamma_n.
\]
Consequently, if \(\varepsilon_n>0\) for every finite \(n\), \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), and \(\alpha_n+\gamma_n+\lambda_n\to0\), then \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)\to v\). No convergence rate is asserted.

The former feasible claim did not state the measurability needed for a data-dependent certificate selection to define a rule, while its two-sided experiment-equivalence premise contained an unused forward-deficiency condition. The proposed statement adds the separate measurable-selector premise, conditions the feasible upper bound only on legality, numerical boundedness, and the joint total-variation premise, and uses the corrected reverse deficiency only for the lower comparison. Thus the feasible-rule assertion is honestly narrowed where necessary, without assuming any regret conclusion.

%%% FIELD condition-measurable-selector
For every \(n\) and every strictly positive finite tolerance \(\varepsilon_n>0\), the fixed deterministic tie-breaking implementation of the finite-tolerance exchange certificate has a Borel-measurable evaluation map from
\[
 \{\mathsf G\in\mathcal G_p:\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\}
 \times\mathbb R^p
\]
to \([0,1]\), where the game collection carries the Borel structure induced by \(d_{\mathsf G}\).

%%% FIELD proof-transfer-repaired
Write a second \(p\)-dimensional game as \(\mathsf G'=(H',\Sigma',\ell',u')\), and decorate its objects with primes. For \(x\in[0,1]\), define \(g_+(x)=1-x\) and \(g_-(x)=x\). Thus, by \(\mathrm{def:endpoint-game}\),
\[
 r_{(h,\sigma)}(\delta)=c_{\sigma}(h)
 g_{\sigma}\!\left(E_h\delta(Z_h)\right),
 \qquad \sigma\in\{+,-\}. \tag{1}
\]

We first prove the deterministic perturbation inequality. Suppose \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\) and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G')\), and let \(\rho=d_{\mathsf G}(\mathsf G',\mathsf G)\leq(2\kappa)^{-1}\). The bounded-primitive predicate gives \(\lVert h\rVert\leq\kappa\), intercept magnitude at most \(\kappa\), and face-normal norm at most \(\kappa\). Hence every affine endpoint piece has magnitude at most \(\kappa+\kappa^2\) on its domain. A finite maximum or minimum of numbers in that interval remains in the interval, so
\[
 0\leq c_{\sigma}(h)\leq M_{\kappa}:=\kappa(1+\kappa) \tag{2}
\]
for both signs and both games.

Fix \((h',\sigma)\in H'\times\{+,-\}\). By the definition of \(d_{\mathsf G}\), Hausdorff distance between the corresponding endpoint graphs is at most \(\rho\). Therefore there is \(h\in H\) for which
\[
 \lVert h'-h\rVert\leq\rho,
 \qquad
 \lvert c'_{\sigma}(h')-c_{\sigma}(h)\rvert\leq\rho. \tag{3}
\]
For the second inequality, use the matched vertical graph coordinates and the elementary one-Lipschitz inequalities \(\lvert[x]_+-[y]_+\rvert\leq\lvert x-y\rvert\) and \(\lvert[-x]_+-[-y]_+\rvert\leq\lvert x-y\rvert\).

Set
\[
 E=\Sigma^{-1/2}(\Sigma'-\Sigma)\Sigma^{-1/2}.
\]
The covariance part of \(d_{\mathsf G}\) and the lower eigenvalue bound in \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\) imply
\[
 \lVert E\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\lVert\Sigma^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
       \lVert\Sigma'-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\kappa\rho\leq\frac12. \tag{4}
\]
Positive definiteness, supplied by membership in \(\mathcal G_p\), makes every inverse and logarithm below well defined. Direct substitution in the Gaussian densities gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(N(h',\Sigma')\,\middle\|\,N(h,\Sigma)\right)
 =\frac12\left\{
 \operatorname{tr}E-\log\det(I+E)
 +(h-h')^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(h-h')
 \right\}. \tag{5}
\]
If \(x\in[-1/2,1/2]\), then \(x-\log(1+x)\leq2x^2\): subtract the left side from \(2x^2\), differentiate, and check its minimum on each side of zero, with equality at zero. Applying this scalar inequality to the eigenvalues of \(E\), and using (3)--(4), yields
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(N(h',\Sigma')\,\middle\|\,N(h,\Sigma)\right)
 \leq \rho^2\left(p\kappa^2+\frac{\kappa}{2}\right). \tag{6}
\]

For completeness, the total-variation inequality needed here follows from convexity. For probability laws \(P\ll Q\), let \(f=dP/dQ\), \(A=\{f\geq1\}\), \(t=P(A)\), and \(s=Q(A)\). Then \(\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=t-s\). Jensen's inequality for \(x\mapsto x\log x\), first under normalized \(Q\) on \(A\) and then on \(A^c\), gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)
 \geq t\log\frac ts+(1-t)\log\frac{1-t}{1-s}. \tag{7}
\]
The right side, as a function of \(t\) with \(s\) fixed, vanishes with zero first derivative at \(t=s\) and has second derivative \(1/[t(1-t)]\geq4\). Taylor's integral formula therefore bounds it below by \(2(t-s)^2\); boundary cases follow by continuity. Consequently,
\[
 \lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)/2}. \tag{8}
\]
Combining (6) and (8),
\[
 \left\lVert N(h',\Sigma')-N(h,\Sigma)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\rho\sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}. \tag{9}
\]

Because \(0\leq\delta\leq1\), the difference of its expectations under two laws is at most their total-variation distance. Since each \(g_{\sigma}\) is one-Lipschitz and takes values in \([0,1]\), equations (1)--(3) and (9) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\lvert r'_{(h',\sigma)}(\delta)-r_{(h,\sigma)}(\delta)\right\rvert
 &\leq \left\lvert c'_{\sigma}(h')-c_{\sigma}(h)\right\rvert
   +M_{\kappa}
   \left\lvert E_{h'}'\delta-E_h\delta\right\rvert\\
 &\leq\rho+M_{\kappa}\rho
 \sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}
 =\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). 
 \end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(R'(\delta)\leq R(\delta)+\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)\). Repeating the graph matching from \(\mathsf G\) to \(\mathsf G'\) gives the reverse inequality. Hence
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}
 \lvert R'(\delta)-R(\delta)\rvert
 \leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). \tag{11}
\]
Taking infima over the same nonempty rule class \(\mathcal D\) in the two inequalities comprising (11) yields
\[
 \lvert v'-v\rvert\leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). \tag{12}
\]
If \(\rho>(2\kappa)^{-1}\), (2) places both worst-case risks and both values in \([0,M_{\kappa}]\). Therefore (11)--(12) continue to hold with \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)=M_{\kappa}\).

Apply (11)--(12) to \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\). By \(\mathrm{lem:gap-near-optimality}\), the strictly positive finite-tolerance certificate satisfies
\[
 0\leq R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v_{\nu}
 \leq\varepsilon_{\nu}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v
 &\leq \lvert R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})
              -R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})\rvert
 +(R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v_{\nu})
 +\lvert v_{\nu}-v\rvert\\
 &\leq2\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu})+\varepsilon_{\nu}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{13}
\]
No uniqueness, active-face constancy, or indexability property entered (10)--(13), which proves the asserted tie-uniformity.

We next prove the feasible upper bound without using reverse deficiency. The separate legality and boundedness assumptions give, for every \(n\) and \(h\),
\[
 P_{n,h}\!\left(
 \widehat{\mathsf G}_n\in\mathcal G_p,
 \ \mathsf B_{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)
 \right)=1. \tag{14}
\]
The definition of \(Q_{n,h}\) gives equality of the \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\)-marginals,
\[
 \mathcal L_{Q_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)
 =\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n), \tag{15}
\]
so (14) also holds under \(Q_{n,h}\). By the measurable-selector assumption, the fixed finite-tolerance evaluation map \((G',z)\mapsto d_{G'}(z)\) is Borel measurable on the realized legal games. Its composition with \((\widehat{\mathsf G}_n,\widehat Z_n)\) is therefore measurable under either joint law. Thus all conditional expectations below are defined and \(\widehat\delta_n\) is a feasible rule.

Condition on a realized \(G'=\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\) in (14), write its covariance as \(\Sigma'\), and let \(d_{G'}\) be its certified rule at \(\varepsilon_n>0\). Under \(Q_{n,h}\), conditionally on \(G'\), the signal law is \(N(h,\Sigma')\). Let \(c_{\sigma}\) denote the true-game weight at \(h\). Define the conditional true signed loss by
\[
 \mathcal L^Q_{n,h,\sigma}(G')
 :=c_{\sigma}(h)
 E_{N(h,\Sigma')}\!\left[g_{\sigma}(d_{G'}(Z))\right]. \tag{16}
\]
Let \(\rho'=d_{\mathsf G}(G',\mathsf G)\). Matching the true endpoint graph at \((h,c_{\sigma}(h))\) to the corresponding graph of \(G'\), and repeating (3) and (10) with the common covariance \(\Sigma'\), gives
\[
 \mathcal L^Q_{n,h,\sigma}(G')
 \leq R_{G'}(d_{G'})+overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho'). \tag{17}
\]
This use of the extended modulus also covers \(\rho'>(2\kappa)^{-1}\). The gap lemma and (12), again in its extended form, give
\[
 R_{G'}(d_{G'})
 \leq v(G')+\varepsilon_n
 \leq v+\varepsilon_n+overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho'). \tag{18}
\]
Combining (17)--(18), averaging under \(Q_{n,h}\), using (15), and then using the definition of \(\alpha_n\), yields
\[
 \sup_{h\in H}\max_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
 E_{Q_{n,h}}\!\left[\mathcal L^Q_{n,h,\sigma}
       (\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\right]
 \leq v+\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n. \tag{19}
\]
For a fixed \((h,\sigma)\), the realized loss as a function of \((\widehat Z_n,\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\) is measurable by the selector premise and lies in \([0,M_{\kappa}]\) by (2). The separate joint total-variation assumption and the elementary bounded-function inequality therefore transfer (19) at cost at most \(M_{\kappa}\gamma_n\). Taking the maximum over signs and supremum over \(h\) gives
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v
 \leq\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n+M_{\kappa}\gamma_n. \tag{20}
\]
In particular, neither \(L_n\) nor \(\lambda_n\) has been used in the upper bound.

It remains to prove the lower comparison. Let \(d:\mathcal X_n\to[0,1]\) be any measurable randomized rule, and use the reverse kernel to define
\[
 \delta_d(z)=\int_{\mathcal X_n}d(x)L_n(dx\mid z). \tag{21}
\]
The defining measurability of a Markov kernel makes \(\delta_d\) Borel measurable; its range is contained in \([0,1]\), so its Lebesgue-almost-everywhere class belongs to \(\mathcal D\). Moreover,
\[
 E_{N(h,\Sigma)}\delta_d
 =E_{L_nN(h,\Sigma)}d. \tag{22}
\]
The reverse-deficiency assumption and \(0\leq d\leq1\) imply, uniformly in \(h\),
\[
 \left\lvert E_{L_nN(h,\Sigma)}d-E_{P_{n,h}}d\right\rvert
 \leq\lambda_n. \tag{23}
\]
Define
\[
 r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,(h,+)}(d)
 =c_+(h)\{1-E_{P_{n,h}}d\},
 \qquad
 r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,(h,-)}(d)
 =c_-(h)E_{P_{n,h}}d.
\]
Equations (2), (22), and (23) show, for every \(s\in S\),
\[
 r_s(\delta_d)
 \leq r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,s}(d)+M_{\kappa}\lambda_n. \tag{24}
\]
Taking suprema over \(s\), using the definition of \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\), and then using \(v=\inf_{\delta\in\mathcal D}R(\delta)\leq R(\delta_d)\), we obtain
\[
 v\leq R(\delta_d)
 \leq\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)+M_{\kappa}\lambda_n,
\]
or equivalently
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)-v\geq-M_{\kappa}\lambda_n. \tag{25}
\]
Apply (25) to the measurable feasible rule \(d=\widehat\delta_n\) and combine it with (20). If the finite tolerances remain strictly positive, \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), and \(\alpha_n+\gamma_n+\lambda_n\to0\), the lower and upper bounds converge to zero, proving \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)\to v\). The argument only propagates the displayed approximation errors and makes no rate claim.
