\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The new angle asks when asymmetric nonsmooth partial identification makes every minimax treatment rule genuinely multivariate. The proposal preserves the unrestricted measurable-rule saddle theorem, face-complete least-favorable priors, convergent global exchange bounds, and the asymmetric triangular coexistence witness, but moves the theorem-level frontier to a finite curved-score certificate. In two dimensions, exact primal-dual closure for a finite endpoint prior, noncancellation of its analytic Gaussian-mixture score, and one regular zero-set arc with curvature bounded away from zero imply a unique minimax rule and exclude every measurable one-index representation. The answer-open task is to construct an unbalanced rational endpoint prior and outward-rounded interval certificate satisfying these conditions, or prove that no such two-dimensional game exists. The full all-one-index, coexistence, or no-one-index normal-fan classification remains open. Every finite exchange tolerance is strictly positive; limiting tolerances may converge to zero. Feasible transfer is retained only as a conditional secondary corollary under two-sided experiment equivalence, legal bounded game estimation, and joint total-variation approximation, with no named-model or rate claim.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Theorem 3 of MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Local is the feasible fully differentiable result. Its Assumption 5(i) imposes convexity and centrosymmetry for directionally differentiable bounds, Theorem 4 supplies selected rules for that limit game, Theorem 5 gives their feasible transfer, and the Conclusion leaves the joint directional-differentiability and non-centrosymmetry regime open. Yata2025Optimal relies on an antipodal modulus pair. MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Decision imposes open-set sign ambiguity and establishes multiplicity without classifying the full unrestricted correspondence; the present primitive class instead uses a weaker pointwise sign-ambiguity condition. AradillasFernandezMontielOleaQiuStoyeTinda2026RobustBayes solves ex-ante \(\Gamma\)-MMR and ex-post \(\Gamma\)-PER under its equation-(2.9) constrained prior class and derives uniqueness, randomization, and linear-index threshold conclusions, but does not solve the unrestricted endpoint-state minimax-regret correspondence or exclude arbitrary measurable one-index rules. The corrected-proof Abstract of ChristensenMoonSchorfheide2026OptimalDecisionRules derives asymptotically optimal discrete-choice rules from a hybrid objective: minimax ambiguity treatment for the partially identified payoff parameter conditional on a point-identified parameter, combined with average-risk minimization for the point-identified parameter. That objective is orthogonal to the exact unrestricted signed-endpoint minimax-regret saddle studied here, and neither objective implies the other.

\paragraph{Niche.} Finite endpoint priors produce analytic differences of Gaussian mixtures. Their global primal-dual gap and local boundary jets are finite objects that can certify properties of the entire unrestricted minimax correspondence, including failure of every scalar-index representation.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives unrestricted saddle duality and face-complete dual representatives, then proves that exact finite-prior saddle closure plus a nonvanishing curved Bayes-score boundary yields uniqueness and the no-one-index category. Convergent exchange bounds locate candidate priors, while outward-rounded interval arithmetic closes the global risk inequalities and the local curvature inequalities. The triangular value-\(2/3\) construction remains the coexistence regression test, and the complete normal-fan trichotomy remains answer-open. Conditional feasible transfer is secondary and makes no statistical frontier or rate claim.

\section{Related work}

Montiel Olea, Qiu, and Stoye, \cite{MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Decision}, establish multiplicity and qualified failure of linear threshold rules in partially identified treatment choice, but do not exclude every randomized measurable rule based on one linear projection. Montiel Olea, Qiu, and Stoye, \cite{MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Local}, solve the smooth asymmetric and nonsmooth centrosymmetric regimes separately and leave their joint relaxation open; the present compact asymmetric polyhedral class occupies that orthogonal regime, not a superclass of their full Assumption-5 class. Yata, \cite{Yata2025Optimal}, obtains an optimal one-index rule from an antipodal modulus pair under centrosymmetry, providing the positive benchmark that the curved-score certificate can falsify outside symmetry. Aradillas Fernández, Montiel Olea, Qiu, Stoye, and Tinda, \cite{AradillasFernandezMontielOleaQiuStoyeTinda2026RobustBayes}, use the constrained ambiguity specification in their equation (2.9), with a symmetric uniform two-point distribution over the reduced-form parameter and unrestricted compatible conditional distributions, and Theorems 1--2 analyze ex-ante \(\Gamma\)-MMR and ex-post \(\Gamma\)-PER rules with uniqueness, randomization, and linear-index threshold conclusions. Those results are orthogonal to the present unrestricted minimax-regret saddle: Nature here ranges over every signed endpoint state, and the curved-score theorem excludes every arbitrary measurable one-index rule \(g(w^{\top}z)\), not only linear threshold rules. Christensen, Moon, and Schorfheide, \cite{ChristensenMoonSchorfheide2026OptimalDecisionRules}, derive asymptotically optimal discrete-choice rules under partial identification by combining minimax ambiguity treatment for the partially identified payoff parameter conditional on a point-identified parameter with average-risk minimization for that point-identified parameter; this description is anchored to the Abstract of the corrected proof dated 13 March 2026. Their hybrid asymptotic objective and the present exact unrestricted signed-endpoint minimax-regret objective are orthogonal, neither objective implies the other, and the present curved-score result concerns exclusion of every arbitrary measurable one-index rule rather than asymptotic efficiency for learning the point-identified parameter. Tetenov, \cite{Tetenov2012Asymmetric}, derives threshold structure for point-identified asymmetric regret, showing that asymmetry alone does not generate the proposed non-index phenomenon. Teicher, \cite{Teicher1963Mixtures}, supplies finite-normal-mixture identifiability, while Zahm et al., \cite{ZahmEtAl2020Ridge}, formalize ridge functions as measurable functions of linear projections and study gradient diagnostics; neither source links analytic mixture-score geometry to uniqueness of an unrestricted minimax treatment rule. Aradillas Fernández et al., \cite{AradillasFernandezEtAl2025Epsilon}, optimize mixtures over a fixed finite rule menu, and Zhang, Wu, and López, \cite{ZhangWuLopez2010Exchange}, treat convex semi-infinite exchange with finite-dimensional primals. The proposed exchange construction instead preserves the exact measurable-rule Bayes envelope and requests only strictly positive finite tolerances. The deeper scan located no paper giving an exact unbalanced least-favorable endpoint distribution, a curved Gaussian-mixture Bayes boundary, and a global certificate excluding every one-index minimax rule. Hirano and Porter, \cite{HiranoPorter2009}, remain pointwise limit-experiment motivation only; the secondary feasible corollary is explicitly conditional on stronger two-sided and joint total-variation premises.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G')\) holds when the mean domain of \(\mathsf G'\) is contained in the Euclidean ball of radius \(\kappa\), every covariance eigenvalue lies in \([\kappa^{-1},\kappa]\), and every lower or upper endpoint has at most \(\kappa\) affine pieces whose intercept magnitudes and normal norms are at most \(\kappa\).
  \item \(\omega_{p,\kappa}\) --- \texttt{primitive-\allowbreak{}game continuity modulus}; \([0,(2\kappa)^{-1}]\to\mathbb R_{+}\); \texttt{stability modulus}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)=\rho\left[1+\kappa(1+\kappa)\sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}\right]\).
  \item \(M_{\kappa}\) --- \texttt{uniform loss bound}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{risk-\allowbreak{}transfer envelope}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(M_{\kappa}=\kappa(1+\kappa)\), an upper bound for both endpoint loss weights on the \(\kappa\)-bounded primitive collection.
  \item \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}\) --- \texttt{extended primitive-\allowbreak{}game modulus}; \([0,\infty)\to\mathbb R_{+}\); \texttt{global bounded-\allowbreak{}loss modulus}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)=\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)\) for \(0\le\rho\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\), and \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)=M_{\kappa}\) otherwise.
  \item \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\) --- \texttt{perturbed game sequence}; \texttt{polyhedral Gaussian endpoint games}; \texttt{perturbation sequence}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Generic sequence converging to \(\mathsf G\) in \(d_{\mathsf G}\).
  \item \(\rho_{\nu}\) --- \texttt{primitive-\allowbreak{}game perturbation radius}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{stability radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\rho_{\nu}=d_{\mathsf G}(\mathsf G_{\nu},\mathsf G)\).
  \item \(v_{\nu}\) --- \texttt{perturbed minimax value}; \(\mathbb R_{+}\); \texttt{perturbation target}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Minimax-regret value of \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\).
  \item \(R_{\nu}\) --- \texttt{perturbed-\allowbreak{}game worst-\allowbreak{}case regret}; measurable randomized rules \(\to\mathbb R_{+}\); \texttt{perturbation objective}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Worst-case regret functional induced by \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\).
  \item \(\varepsilon_{\nu}\) --- \texttt{certificate tolerance}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{approximation index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Strictly positive approximation tolerance for every finite \(\nu\), satisfying \(\varepsilon_{\nu}\to0\).
  \item \(\varepsilon_n\) --- \texttt{feasible certificate tolerance}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{feasible approximation index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Strictly positive stopping tolerance for every finite \(n\), with \(\varepsilon_n\to0\).
  \item \(\widetilde\delta_{\nu}\) --- \texttt{certified perturbed-\allowbreak{}game rule}; \texttt{measurable randomized rules}; \texttt{stable selector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Rule whose certified primal-dual gap for \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\) is at most \(\varepsilon_{\nu}\).
  \item \(\mathcal X_n\) --- \texttt{original sample space}; \texttt{standard Borel spaces}; \texttt{experiment space}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Standard Borel sample space supporting the local experiment at index \(n\).
  \item \(P_{n,h}\) --- \texttt{local original experiment}; probability laws on \(\mathcal X_n\) indexed by \(n\) and \(h\in H\); \texttt{local experiment}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Original sampling experiment along local parameter \(h\).
  \item \(T_n\) --- \texttt{Gaussian-\allowbreak{}pivot statistic}; \(\mathcal X_n\to\mathbb R^p\); \texttt{forward sufficient-\allowbreak{}construction map}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Measurable statistic used in the sufficient construction for two-\allowbreak{}sided experiment equivalence.}
  \item \(\mathsf R_n\) --- \texttt{reconstruction Markov kernel}; \(\mathbb R^p\rightsquigarrow\mathcal X_n\); \texttt{reverse sufficient-\allowbreak{}construction kernel}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} An \(h\)-independent Markov kernel used to reconstruct the original observation from the Gaussian-pivot statistic.
  \item \(a_n\) --- \texttt{uniform Gaussian-\allowbreak{}pivot error}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{sufficient-\allowbreak{}construction error}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Deterministic total-variation error bound for the Gaussian-pivot approximation, with \(a_n\to0\).
  \item \(b_n\) --- \texttt{uniform reconstruction error}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{sufficient-\allowbreak{}construction error}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Deterministic total-variation error bound for asymptotic reconstruction, with \(b_n\to0\).
  \item \(K_n\) --- \texttt{Le Cam randomization kernel}; original sample space \(\rightsquigarrow\mathbb R^p\); \texttt{experiment-\allowbreak{}transfer kernel}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Markov kernel that maps the original experiment into its Gaussian-\allowbreak{}shift approximation.}
  \item \(L_n\) --- \texttt{reverse Le Cam randomization kernel}; \(\mathbb R^p\rightsquigarrow\) original sample space; \texttt{reverse experiment-\allowbreak{}transfer kernel}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Markov kernel that maps the Gaussian-\allowbreak{}shift experiment back into an approximation of the original local experiment.}
  \item \(\lambda_n\) --- \texttt{two-\allowbreak{}sided Le Cam approximation error}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{experiment-\allowbreak{}equivalence error}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\lambda_n=\max\{\sup_{h\in H}\lVert K_nP_{n,h}-N(h,\Sigma)\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}},\sup_{h\in H}\lVert L_nN(h,\Sigma)-P_{n,h}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}\).
  \item \(\widehat Z_n\) --- \texttt{randomized reduced-\allowbreak{}form statistic}; \(\mathbb R^{p}\); \texttt{feasible data reduction}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Output drawn from \(K_n\) conditionally on the original data.
  \item \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\) --- \texttt{estimated endpoint-\allowbreak{}game primitive}; \texttt{polyhedral Gaussian endpoint games}; \texttt{nuisance estimate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Plug-\allowbreak{}in game built from estimated covariance and endpoint coefficients.}
  \item \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) --- \texttt{estimated Gaussian covariance matrix}; \(\mathbb S_{++}^{p}\); \texttt{joint risk-\allowbreak{}transfer nuisance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Covariance component of the estimated game \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\).
  \item \(Q_{n,h}\) --- \texttt{joint Gaussian reference law}; \texttt{probability laws on a Gaussian signal and an estimated game}; \texttt{joint risk-\allowbreak{}transfer reference}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The law obtained by first drawing \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\) from its \(P_{n,h}\)-marginal and then, conditionally on that game, drawing the signal from \(N(h,\widehat\Sigma_n)\); in particular, \(\mathcal L_{Q_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)=\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\).
  \item \(\gamma_n\) --- \texttt{joint total-\allowbreak{}variation transfer error}; \([0,1]\); \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}loss transfer error}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\gamma_n=\sup_{h\in H}\lVert\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat Z_n,\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)-Q_{n,h}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\).
  \item \(\alpha_n\) --- \texttt{uniform expected primitive-\allowbreak{}game error}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{plug-\allowbreak{}in risk error}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\alpha_n=\sup_{h\in H}E_{P_{n,h}}[\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(d_{\mathsf G}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n,\mathsf G))]\).
  \item \(\widehat\delta_n\) --- \texttt{feasible treatment rule}; \texttt{feasible decision}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Certified near-saddle rule for \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\) evaluated at \(\widehat Z_n\).
  \item \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\) --- \texttt{original-\allowbreak{}experiment maximal local regret}; feasible treatment rules \(\to\mathbb R_{+}\); \texttt{transfer objective}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)=\sup_{h\in H}\max\{c_+(h)[1-E_{P_{n,h}}d],c_-(h)E_{P_{n,h}}d\}\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compact-polyhedral-domain]\label{ass:compact-polyhedral-domain}
The set \(H\subset\mathbb R^p\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope with nonempty relative interior in its affine hull.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact polyhedral index-set condition, \cite{ZhangWuLopez2010Exchange}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nondegenerate-gaussian-shift]\label{ass:nondegenerate-gaussian-shift}
The covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) is positive definite and does not depend on \(h\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate Gaussian location experiment, \cite{Yata2025Optimal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonempty-identified-interval]\label{ass:nonempty-identified-interval}
For every \(h\in H\), the endpoints satisfy \(\ell(h)\le u(h)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nonempty interval-identified welfare condition, \cite{MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Local}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nontrivial-sign-ambiguity]\label{ass:nontrivial-sign-ambiguity}
There exists \(h_0\in H\) such that \(\ell(h_0)<0<u(h_0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a weaker pointwise sign-ambiguity condition, not the open-set condition in Definition 2 of \cite{MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Decision}. It is realistic when one economically plausible local reduced-form mean has strictly two-sided welfare bounds, including finite-support moment-inequality models with continuous polyhedral endpoint maps; no ambient open set of such means is required.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-two-sided-experiment-equivalence]\label{ass:uniform-two-sided-experiment-equivalence}
There exist Markov kernels \(K_n\) and \(L_n\) such that \(\lambda_n=\max\{\sup_{h\in H}\lVert K_nP_{n,h}-N(h,\Sigma)\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}},\sup_{h\in H}\lVert L_nN(h,\Sigma)-P_{n,h}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit nonstandard high-level premise. Differentiability in quadratic mean or pointwise local asymptotic normality does not imply the displayed compact-\(H\), two-sided total-variation deficiency bound, so feasible transfer is conditional on this stronger equivalence. A self-contained sufficient construction is available on the standard Borel spaces \(\mathcal X_n\): suppose there are a measurable statistic \(T_n\), an \(h\)-independent reconstruction Markov kernel \(\mathsf R_n(\cdot\mid z)\), and deterministic \(a_n,b_n\to0\) such that \[\sup_{h\in H}\left\lVert\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(T_n)-N(h,\Sigma)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\le a_n\] and \[\sup_{h\in H}\left\lVert P_{n,h}-\int \mathsf R_n(\cdot\mid z)\,\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(T_n)(dz)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\le b_n.\] Taking \(K_n(x,\cdot)=\delta_{T_n(x)}\) and \(L_n=\mathsf R_n\), contraction of total variation under \(\mathsf R_n\) and the triangle inequality give \(\lambda_n\le a_n+b_n\to0\). These conditions are uniform Gaussian-pivot approximation plus uniform asymptotic sufficiency and reconstruction; they are not claimed for every regular model and impose no convergence rate.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:estimated-game-legality]\label{ass:estimated-game-legality}
For every \(n\) and \(h\in H\), \(P_{n,h}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\in\mathcal G_p)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A training-sample construction can enforce legal membership by fitting the endpoint coefficients subject to the nonempty-interval and pointwise sign-ambiguity restrictions while maintaining a positive-definite covariance estimate. This is an independent legality requirement: numerical bounds on fitted coefficients do not imply membership in \(\mathcal G_p\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:estimated-game-kappa-bounds]\label{ass:estimated-game-kappa-bounds}
For every \(n\) and \(h\in H\), \(P_{n,h}(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n))=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded parameterizations of compact polyhedral endpoint models and covariance estimators with eigenvalues constrained to a fixed compact interval satisfy this condition. It controls the loss envelope and continuity modulus but is separate from legal membership in \(\mathcal G_p\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:joint-risk-transfer]\label{ass:joint-risk-transfer}
The joint approximation error satisfies \(\gamma_n=\sup_{h\in H}\lVert\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat Z_n,\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)-Q_{n,h}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A concrete sufficient construction uses a training split to form \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\), including \(\widehat\Sigma_n\), with legal membership and the numerical bounds imposed separately by \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-legality}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-kappa-bounds}\), and a conditionally independent evaluation split to form \(\widehat Z_n\). If the conditional Gaussian approximation obeys \[\sup_{h\in H}E_{P_{n,h}}\!\left[\left\lVert\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat Z_n\mid\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)-N(h,\widehat\Sigma_n)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]\to0,\] then the common \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\)-marginal and conditional disintegration give \(\gamma_n\) no larger than the displayed quantity, hence \(\gamma_n\to0\). This sample-splitting or stable-construction requirement is independent of the two-sided experiment equivalence in \(\mathrm{ass:uniform-two-sided-experiment-equivalence}\), and neither joint approximation nor numerical boundedness substitutes for legal membership in \(\mathcal G_p\).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Unrestricted randomized decision space \(\mathcal D\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal D=\{\delta\in L^{\infty}(\lambda^p):0\le\delta\le1\ \lambda^p\text{-almost everywhere}\}\), the positive order interval in the closed unit ball, equipped with \(\sigma(L^{\infty}(\lambda^p),L^1(\lambda^p))\). Its elements are \(\lambda^p\)-almost-everywhere equivalence classes. Whenever \(\delta(z)\) is written, an arbitrary Borel representative is used; because every \(N(h,\Sigma)\) is equivalent to \(\lambda^p\), \(q_{\delta}\), every regret, and every Bayes response are representative-invariant.

\begin{definition}[def:endpoint-game]\label{def:endpoint-game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Endpoint minimax game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(s=(h,+)\), set \(r_s(\delta)=c_+(h)\{1-q_{\delta}(h)\}\); for \(s=(h,-)\), set \(r_s(\delta)=c_-(h)q_{\delta}(h)\); then \(R(\delta)=\sup_{s\in S}r_s(\delta)\) and \(v=\inf_{\delta\in\mathcal D}R(\delta)\).

\begin{definition}[def:dual-envelope]\label{def:dual-envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Gaussian-\allowbreak{}mixture Bayes envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\pi\in\Pi\), form \(A_{\pi,+}\), \(A_{\pi,-}\), and \(B_{\pi}=A_{\pi,+}-A_{\pi,-}\); the dual payoff is \(b(\pi)=\int_{\mathbb R^p}\min\{A_{\pi,+}(z),A_{\pi,-}(z)\}\,dz\), and its Bayes responses are exactly the rules with \(\delta(z)=1\) on \(\{B_{\pi}>0\}\), \(\delta(z)=0\) on \(\{B_{\pi}<0\}\), and arbitrary values in \([0,1]\) on \(\{B_{\pi}=0\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:saddle-correspondence]\label{def:saddle-correspondence}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Primal and dual optimal correspondences}

\begin{definition}[def:no-index-witness-handle]\label{def:no-index-witness-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Finite rational and interval-arithmetic witness handle \(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{noidx}}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Search over games whose vertices of \(H\), entries of \(\Sigma\), endpoint intercepts, and endpoint normals are rational, and over finite endpoint priors \(\pi\) with rational support points, rational masses, and unequal total masses on the two endpoint signs. Use the Gaussian-mixture Bayes rule \(\delta_{\pi}\). Certify exact saddle closure by a rational number \(q\), a finite rational box partition of \(H\), a finite rational truncation box in \(\mathbb R^2\), outward-rounded interval enclosures, and analytic Gaussian-tail bounds proving \(R(\delta_{\pi})\le q\le b(\pi)\). Certify a genuinely curved boundary on a rational box meeting \(\mathcal Z_{\pi}\) by interval lower bounds away from zero for the score gradient and \( |\mathcal K_{\pi}|\).

\begin{definition}[def:curved-score-certificate]\label{def:curved-score-certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite curved-\allowbreak{}score no-\allowbreak{}index certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(p=2\), take a finite endpoint prior \(\pi\in\Pi\), form \(\delta_{\pi}=\mathbf 1\{B_{\pi}>0\}\), the regular boundary \(\mathcal Z_{\pi}\), and the curvature numerator \(\mathcal K_{\pi}\). The certificate consists of the exact inequalities \(R(\delta_{\pi})\le q\le b(\pi)\), a proof that \(B_{\pi}\not\equiv0\), and a nondegenerate arc \(\Gamma\subset\mathcal Z_{\pi}\) on which \(\inf_{z\in\Gamma}\lVert\nabla B_{\pi}(z)\rVert>0\) and \(\inf_{z\in\Gamma}|\mathcal K_{\pi}(z)|>0\).

\begin{definition}[def:exchange-certificate]\label{def:exchange-certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Unrestricted primal-\allowbreak{}dual exchange certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix a dense sequence in \(S\) and start from a finite \(S_1\subset S\). At iteration \(m\), solve the restricted game against \(S_m\) over all \(\delta\in\mathcal D\), choose a restricted saddle \((\delta_m,\pi_m)\), compute \(L_m=b(\pi_m)\) and \(U_m=R(\delta_m)\), and adjoin both the next point of the dense sequence and an endpoint state attaining \(U_m\). A finite run requests a strictly positive tolerance and stops when \(G_m=U_m-L_m\) is no larger than that tolerance; convergence to zero refers only to the infinite sequence, and no finite termination claim is made at tolerance zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:coexistence-witness]\label{def:coexistence-witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}dimensional coexistence witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Take \(H_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(1,-1),(0,1)\}\), \(\Sigma=I_2\), \(\ell_0(h)=-4/3+\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}\), \(u_0(h)=4/3-\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}\), \(\delta_0(z)=1/2\), and \(\delta_{\eta}(z)=1/2+\eta\tanh(z_1)\tanh(z_2)\) for \(0<\eta\le1/2\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:stable-selector]\label{def:stable-selector}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Tie-\allowbreak{}uniform certified selector}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each legal perturbed game \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\in\mathcal G_p\), choose a strictly positive \(\varepsilon_{\nu}>0\), run \(\mathrm{def:exchange-certificate}\) until its gap is at most \(\varepsilon_{\nu}\), and denote the resulting rule by \(\widetilde\delta_{\nu}\). In the original experiment, on the event \(\{\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\in\mathcal G_p,\ \mathsf B_{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\}\), choose a strictly positive \(\varepsilon_n>0\), run the same construction for \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\) to gap at most \(\varepsilon_n\), and evaluate the certified rule at \(\widehat Z_n\); on the complementary null event set the treatment probability to \(1/2\). The resulting decision is \(\widehat\delta_n\). The sequences may converge to zero, but this construction has no zero-tolerance finite branch.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}exchange-\allowbreak{}certificate}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}estimated-\allowbreak{}game-\allowbreak{}legality}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}estimated-\allowbreak{}game-\allowbreak{}kappa-\allowbreak{}bounds}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:endpoint-reduction]\label{lem:endpoint-reduction}
For every \(\delta\in\mathcal D\) and \(h\in H\), the supremum of expected regret over \(U^{\ast}\in[\ell(h),u(h)]\) equals \(\max\{c_+(h)[1-q_{\delta}(h)],c_-(h)q_{\delta}(h)\}\). Consequently, Nature loses no value by restricting its pure strategies to \(S=H\times\{+,-\}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(h\in H\) and write \(q=q_{\delta}(h)\in[0,1]\), where the range follows from \(0\leq\delta\leq1\) in \(\mathrm{def:rule-class}\). If the welfare gain is \(x\), the oracle treats when \(x>0\) and does not treat when \(x<0\). Hence the expected regret of a rule whose treatment probability is \(q\) is
\[
 \rho(x,q)=x_{+}(1-q)+(-x)_{+}q.
\]
This is the definition of regret relative to the better of the two constant actions. On \([0,\infty)\), \(x\mapsto\rho(x,q)=x(1-q)\) is nondecreasing, whereas on \((-\infty,0]\), \(x\mapsto\rho(x,q)=(-x)q\) is nonincreasing as \(x\) increases. Because \(\ell(h)\leq u(h)\) by \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty-identified-interval}\), splitting the interval at zero, with an empty side omitted, gives
\[
 \sup_{x\in[\ell(h),u(h)]}\rho(x,q)
 =\max\bigl\{[u(h)]_{+}(1-q),[-\ell(h)]_{+}q\bigr\}
 =\max\bigl\{c_{+}(h)[1-q_{\delta}(h)],c_{-}(h)q_{\delta}(h)\bigr\}.
\]
The two entries in the last maximum are precisely \(r_{(h,+)}(\delta)\) and \(r_{(h,-)}(\delta)\) by \(\mathrm{def:endpoint-game}\). Taking the supremum over \(h\in H\) therefore gives the same value as taking the supremum over \(S=H\times\{+,-\}\), which proves the reduction without imposing any restriction on \(\delta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:complete-saddle-duality]\label{thm:complete-saddle-duality}
For every \(\mathsf G\in\mathcal G_p\), minimax equality and attainment hold: \[v=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\max_{s\in S}r_s(\delta)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}b(\pi).\] The sets \(\mathcal D^{\star}\) and \(\Pi^{\star}\) are nonempty and convex. A pair \((\delta,\pi)\in\mathcal D\times\Pi\) is a saddle if and only if: first, \(\delta\) has the Bayes-response form determined by the sign of \(B_{\pi}\); second, \(r_s(\delta)\le v\) for every \(s\in S\); and third, \(r_s(\delta)=v\) for \(\pi\)-almost every \(s\). The set \(\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\) is nonempty. Indeed, if \(\mathfrak F^{\star}\ne\varnothing\), choose for each \(F\in\mathfrak F^{\star}\) a prior \(\pi_F\in\Pi^{\star}\) assigning positive mass to the relative interior of \(F\) with its endpoint sign; the finite uniform convex combination of the \(\pi_F\) belongs to the convex set \(\Pi^{\star}\) and lies in \(\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\). If \(\mathfrak F^{\star}=\varnothing\), every prior in the nonempty set \(\Pi^{\star}\) is face-complete. Finally, every \(\delta\in\mathcal D^{\star}\) forms a saddle with every \(\pi\in\Pi^{\star}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathsf G\in\mathcal G_p\). All endpoint functions are continuous because each is a finite maximum or minimum of affine functions. Thus \(c_{+}\) and \(c_{-}\) are continuous and bounded on the compact set \(H\), by \(\mathrm{ass:compact-polyhedral-domain}\). Positive definiteness in \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate-gaussian-shift}\) implies that every \(N(h,\Sigma)\) has a strictly positive Lebesgue density.

We first establish the compactness and continuity facts used below. Put \(\mu=N(0,\Sigma)\) and
\[
 L_h(z)=\frac{dN(h,\Sigma)}{d\mu}(z)
 =\exp\!\left(h^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}z-\frac12h^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}h\right).
\]
Then \(L_h\in L^2(\mu)\), and \(h\mapsto L_h\) is continuous in \(L^2(\mu)\); both assertions follow by evaluating Gaussian exponential moments. In particular,
\[
 q_{\delta}(h)=\int\delta(z)L_h(z)\,d\mu(z).
\]
Every sequence in \(\mathcal D\) is bounded in the Hilbert space \(L^2(\mu)\). A diagonal extraction against a countable orthonormal basis produces a weakly convergent subsequence. If its weak limit is \(d\), testing against indicators of the sets \(\{d<0\}\) and \(\{d>1\}\), and using weak closedness of closed convex sets, gives \(0\leq d\leq1\) almost everywhere. Since \(\mu\) and \(\lambda^p\) are equivalent, \(d\) represents an element of \(\mathcal D\). Consequently, for every finite collection of states, the corresponding risk-vector set
\[
 \bigl\{(r_{s_1}(\delta),\ldots,r_{s_N}(\delta)):\delta\in\mathcal D\bigr\}\subset\mathbb R^N
\]
is compact and convex. Moreover, the \(L^1\)-continuity of Gaussian translations gives
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}|q_{\delta}(h)-q_{\delta}(h')|
 \leq\int|\varphi_{\Sigma}(z-h)-\varphi_{\Sigma}(z-h')|\,dz\longrightarrow0
\]
as \(h'\to h\). Together with uniform continuity of \(c_{+}\) and \(c_{-}\), this proves that \(s\mapsto r_s(\delta)\) is equicontinuous over \(\delta\in\mathcal D\).

For completeness, consider first a finite state set \(T=\{s_1,\ldots,s_N\}\). Let \(C\) be the compact convex risk-vector set just displayed and put
\[
 t_T=\min_{x\in C}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}x_i.
\]
The following elementary finite-dimensional separation argument yields
\[
 t_T=\max_{a\in\Delta_N}\min_{x\in C}\sum_{i=1}^Na_ix_i. \tag{1}
\]
For \(\epsilon>0\), the compact set \(C\) and the closed lower orthant \(O_{\epsilon}=\{y:y_i\leq t_T-\epsilon\ \forall i\}\) are disjoint. Choose a closest pair. The vector joining the pair separates the two convex sets; because \(O_{\epsilon}\) is unbounded in every negative coordinate direction, every coordinate of the separating normal is nonnegative. Normalize its coordinates to sum to one and let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Compactness of \(\Delta_N\) gives \(a\in\Delta_N\) with \(\min_{x\in C}a^{\top}x\geq t_T\). The reverse inequality follows from \(a^{\top}x\leq\max_i x_i\). This proves (1).

Choose nested finite sets \(T_m\subset S\) with dense union and let \(t_m=t_{T_m}\). Compactness of \(S\) and the equicontinuity proved above imply that there is \(e_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 0\leq R(\delta)-\max_{s\in T_m}r_s(\delta)\leq e_m
 \qquad\text{for every }\delta\in\mathcal D. \tag{2}
\]
It follows that \(t_m\uparrow v\). Let \(\delta_m\) minimize the finite problem. A weak \(L^2(\mu)\) subsequential limit \(\delta^{\star}\in\mathcal D\) satisfies \(r_s(\delta^{\star})\leq v\) first for every \(s\) in the dense union and then, by continuity in \(s\), for every \(s\in S\). Hence \(R(\delta^{\star})=v\), so the primal minimum is attained.

Define the bilinear payoff
\[
 \mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=\int_S r_s(\delta)\,d\pi(s).
\]
For any \(\pi\in\Pi\), boundedness permits Tonelli's theorem and gives
\[
 \mathcal L(\delta,\pi)
 =\int_Hc_{+}(h)\,d\pi(h,+)
   +\int_{\mathbb R^p}\delta(z)\{A_{\pi,-}(z)-A_{\pi,+}(z)\}\,dz. \tag{3}
\]
Pointwise minimization over \(0\leq\delta\leq1\) in (3) yields
\[
 \min_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)
 =\int_{\mathbb R^p}\min\{A_{\pi,+}(z),A_{\pi,-}(z)\}\,dz=b(\pi). \tag{4}
\]
The minimum is attained, and the nonnegative pointwise excess in (3) vanishes exactly when
\[
 \delta=1\ \text{on }\{B_{\pi}>0\},\qquad
 \delta=0\ \text{on }\{B_{\pi}<0\}, \tag{5}
\]
with arbitrary values on \(\{B_{\pi}=0\}\). Thus (5) proves, rather than assumes, the asserted Bayes-response characterization.

For each finite \(T_m\), equations (1) and (4) give a prior \(\pi_m\) supported on \(T_m\) with \(b(\pi_m)=t_m\). Probability measures on the compact metric space \(S\) are sequentially compact for weak convergence: one direct proof takes successively finer finite Borel partitions with null boundaries, extracts a diagonal subsequence of all cell masses, and extends the consistent limiting masses to a probability measure. Let \(\pi_m\) converge along such a subsequence to \(\pi^{\star}\). For fixed \(\delta\), continuity of \(s\mapsto r_s(\delta)\) gives continuity of \(\pi\mapsto\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)\). Therefore \(b=\inf_{\delta}\mathcal L(\delta,\cdot)\) is upper semicontinuous, so
\[
 b(\pi^{\star})\geq\limsup_m b(\pi_m)=v. \tag{6}
\]
Weak duality, obtained from \(b(\pi)\leq\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)\leq R(\delta)\), gives \(b(\pi)\leq v\) for every \(\pi\). Thus equality holds in (6), the dual maximum is attained, and
\[
 v=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal D}\max_{s\in S}r_s(\delta)
  =\max_{\pi\in\Pi}b(\pi). \tag{7}
\]

We next characterize every saddle. If \((\delta,\pi)\) is a saddle, its payoff equals the common value in (7). Minimization against \(\pi\) and (4) imply the sign rule (5). Maximization against \(\delta\) implies \(r_s(\delta)\leq v\) for every \(s\). Finally,
\[
 0=v-\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=\int_S\{v-r_s(\delta)\}\,d\pi(s),
\]
whose integrand is nonnegative; hence \(r_s(\delta)=v\) for \(\pi\)-almost every \(s\). Conversely, the three displayed conditions imply by (4) that \(b(\pi)=\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=v\), while the global inequalities imply \(R(\delta)=v\). For arbitrary \(\delta'\) and \(\pi'\),
\[
 \mathcal L(\delta,\pi')\leq R(\delta)=v
 =b(\pi)\leq\mathcal L(\delta',\pi),
\]
so the pair is a saddle.

The set \(\mathcal D^{\star}\) is nonempty by primal attainment and convex because \(R\), a supremum of affine maps, is convex. The set \(\Pi^{\star}\) is nonempty by dual attainment and convex because \(b\), an infimum of affine maps of \(\pi\), is concave. If \(\delta\in\mathcal D^{\star}\) and \(\pi\in\Pi^{\star}\), then
\[
 v=b(\pi)\leq\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)\leq R(\delta)=v,
\]
so every such pair is a saddle.

Finally, \(\mathfrak F^{\star}\) is finite because the two endpoints have finitely many affine pieces. If it is nonempty, choose \(\pi_F\) as in \(\mathrm{def:face-complete-prior}\) for every \(F\in\mathfrak F^{\star}\), and set
\[
 \bar\pi=|\mathfrak F^{\star}|^{-1}\sum_{F\in\mathfrak F^{\star}}\pi_F.
\]
Convexity of \(\Pi^{\star}\) puts \(\bar\pi\) in \(\Pi^{\star}\), and every required relative interior receives positive \(\bar\pi\)-mass. Thus \(\bar\pi\in\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\). If \(\mathfrak F^{\star}=\varnothing\), the defining face-completeness requirement is vacuous and every member of the nonempty set \(\Pi^{\star}\) belongs to \(\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the theorem-level kernel: it gives the value, existence, every saddle, and a face-complete dual representative over all measurable randomized rules in the asymmetric polyhedral regime. \textit{Gap.} Yata2025Optimal uses an antipodal modulus pair under centrosymmetry. Assumption 5(i) and Theorem 4 of MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Local supply selected rules for a directionally differentiable limit game under convexity and centrosymmetry. \begin{proposition}[prop:certificate-convergence]\label{prop:certificate-convergence}

Moreover, \(\delta_{\pi}\notin\mathcal D_1\), so \(\mathsf G\in\mathsf C_{\mathrm{none}}\). Thus exact primal-dual closure, nonvanishing of the finite Gaussian-mixture score, and one regular boundary arc with curvature bounded away from zero form a finite sufficient certificate for the no-one-index branch.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathcal L(\delta,\pi)=\int r_s(\delta)\,d\pi(s)\). Weak duality from \(\mathrm{thm:complete-saddle-duality}\) gives, for the certified pair,
\[
 b(\pi)\leq v\leq R(\delta_{\pi}). \tag{1}
\]
The certificate in \(\mathrm{def:curved-score-certificate}\) gives
\(R(\delta_{\pi})\leq q\leq b(\pi)\). Combining with (1) forces
\[
 R(\delta_{\pi})=q=b(\pi)=v. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\delta_{\pi}\in\mathcal D^{\star}\), \(\pi\in\Pi^{\star}\), and the pair is a saddle.

We next show that its Bayes response is unique almost everywhere. After combining atoms having the same mean, the finite score can be written
\[
 B_{\pi}(z)=\varphi_{\Sigma}(z)
 \sum_{i=1}^N d_i
 \exp\!\left(h_i^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}z-\frac12h_i^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}h_i\right), \tag{3}
\]
where the \(h_i\)'s are distinct and every remaining \(d_i\neq0\). The certificate says that (3) is not identically zero. Choose a vector \(e\) outside the finitely many hyperplanes
\(\{e:e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(h_i-h_j)=0\}\). On every affine line \(x+te\), the second factor in (3) is a linear combination of exponentials with distinct real exponents and nonzero coefficients. Such a combination has at most \(N-1\) zeros, counting multiplicities: divide by the smallest exponential, differentiate, and apply Rolle's theorem inductively. Fubini's theorem therefore gives
\[
 \lambda^2\{z:B_{\pi}(z)=0\}=0. \tag{4}
\]
Every \(\delta\in\mathcal D^{\star}\) forms a saddle with this \(\pi\) by \(\mathrm{thm:complete-saddle-duality}\). Its Bayes-response characterization then fixes \(\delta=1\) on \(\{B_{\pi}>0\}\) and \(\delta=0\) on \(\{B_{\pi}<0\}\). Equation (4) proves \(\delta=\delta_{\pi}\) almost everywhere. Hence \(\mathcal D^{\star}=\{\delta_{\pi}\}\) modulo \(\lambda^2\)-null sets.

It remains to rule out every measurable one-index representation. Let \(\Omega=\{B_{\pi}>0\}\), so \(\delta_{\pi}=\mathbf1_{\Omega}\). Suppose, to the contrary, that \(\mathbf1_{\Omega}(z)=g(w^{\top}z)\) almost everywhere for some \(w\neq0\), and choose a nonzero \(e\perp w\). For every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \mathbf1_{\Omega}(z+te)=\mathbf1_{\Omega}(z)\quad\text{for almost every }z. \tag{5}
\]
Equation (5) says that the distributional derivative of \(\mathbf1_{\Omega}\) in direction \(e\) is zero: translate a compactly supported smooth test function, integrate, and differentiate at zero. On a neighborhood of any point of the regular arc \(\Gamma\), the implicit-function theorem and \(\nabla B_{\pi}\neq0\) identify the distributional boundary measure of \(\Omega\) as its ordinary \(C^1\) boundary measure with unit normal
\[
 n(z)=\frac{\nabla B_{\pi}(z)}{\|\nabla B_{\pi}(z)\|}.
\]
Integration by parts against test functions supported in that neighborhood turns the zero distributional derivative into \(e^{\top}n(z)=0\) for boundary-almost every \(z\in\Gamma\). Continuity of \(n\) upgrades this equality to every \(z\in\Gamma\). Hence the normal is everywhere parallel to the fixed vector \(w\), so the tangent is everywhere parallel to the fixed vector \(e\). The connected arc \(\Gamma\) is therefore contained in an affine line.

Let \(T(z)=(-\partial_2B_{\pi}(z),\partial_1B_{\pi}(z))\), a nonzero tangent to the regular level set. Along a straight level-set arc, twice differentiating \(B_{\pi}(z+te)=0\) gives \(e^{\top}\nabla^2B_{\pi}(z)e=0\). Since \(T\) is a nonzero multiple of \(e\),
\[
 \mathcal K_{\pi}(z)=T(z)^{\top}\nabla^2B_{\pi}(z)T(z)=0
 \qquad(z\in\Gamma),
\]
contradicting the certified strictly positive lower bound for \(|\mathcal K_{\pi}|\). Thus \(\delta_{\pi}\notin\mathcal D_1\). Uniqueness then gives \(\mathcal D^{\star}\cap\mathcal D_1=\varnothing\), which is \(\mathsf C_{\mathrm{none}}\) by \(\mathrm{def:indexability-categories}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the new angle's theorem-level kernel: it turns a finite-support prior, a closed global regret gap, and a local two-jet calculation into uniqueness over all measurable randomized rules and impossibility of every scalar-index representation. \textit{Gap.} MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Decision excludes linear threshold rules only under special conditions and does not exclude arbitrary randomized one-index rules. Theorems 1--2 of AradillasFernandezMontielOleaQiuStoyeTinda2026RobustBayes characterize uniqueness, randomization, and linear-index threshold behavior for ex-ante and ex-post robust-Bayes criteria under the constrained prior class in their equation (2.9); they do not characterize the unrestricted minimax-regret saddle over all endpoint states or exclude every measurable rule of the form \(g(w^{\top}z)\). The Abstract of the corrected proof of ChristensenMoonSchorfheide2026OptimalDecisionRules states an asymptotic discrete-choice criterion that combines minimax treatment of ambiguity in the partially identified payoff parameter conditional on a point-identified parameter with average-risk minimization for that point-identified parameter. That hybrid asymptotic criterion neither implies nor is implied by exact minimax regret over every signed endpoint state, and the cited abstract makes no claim excluding every arbitrary measurable one-index rule. ZahmEtAl2020Ridge characterizes ridge approximation through projections, but not exact minimax decision correspondences or curved Gaussian-mixture Bayes boundaries. \textit{Consumer.} A researcher can validate one finite certificate and conclude that every minimax treatment rule must use genuinely multivariate data, without searching the unrestricted rule space for alternative index representations.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:unbalanced-no-index-witness]\label{oeq:unbalanced-no-index-witness}
Does there exist a game in \(\mathcal G_p\) with \(p=2\), rational vertices of \(H\), rational entries of \(\Sigma\), rational endpoint intercepts and normals, and a finite prior \(\pi\in\Pi^{\mathrm{fc}}\) having rational support points, rational masses, and unequal total masses on the two endpoint signs, such that the finite curved-score certificate holds for \(\delta_{\pi}\); or are these requirements impossible in two dimensions? An affirmative certificate must instantiate \(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{noidx}}\): give a rational \(q\), finite rational box partitions, outward-rounded interval enclosures, and analytic Gaussian-tail bounds proving \(R(\delta_{\pi})\le q\le b(\pi)\). It must also enclose \(B_{\pi}\), its first two derivatives, and \(\mathcal K_{\pi}\) on a rational box meeting a nondegenerate arc \(\Gamma\subset\mathcal Z_{\pi}\), with strictly positive rational lower bounds on the score-gradient norm and on \( |\mathcal K_{\pi}|\), and certify \(B_{\pi}\not\equiv0\). The conclusion \(R(\delta_{\pi})=b(\pi)=v=q\), uniqueness almost everywhere, and \(\mathcal D^{\star}\cap\mathcal D_1=\varnothing\) must then follow through \(\mathrm{thm:curved-score-no-index-certificate}\), rather than being assumed.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the early kill test for the no-one-index branch and demands finite rational data, exact primal-dual closure, and a reproducible interval-arithmetic curvature certificate rather than a numerical plot. \textit{Gap.} Tetenov2012Asymmetric retains threshold structure in point-identified asymmetric problems, and the known partial-identification examples do not certify an unbalanced globally minimax non-halfspace rule. \textit{Consumer.} A certified example would establish that multivariate reporting is sometimes intrinsically necessary; an impossibility theorem would sharply simplify the classification program.

\begin{proposition}[prop:conditional-tie-uniform-transfer]\label{prop:conditional-tie-uniform-transfer}
Fix \(\kappa\ge1\). Suppose \(\mathsf G\in\mathcal G_p\), \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\in\mathcal G_p\), \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\), and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G_{\nu})\) for every \(\nu\). Put \(\rho_{\nu}=d_{\mathsf G}(\mathsf G_{\nu},\mathsf G)\). Whenever \(\rho_{\nu}\le(2\kappa)^{-1}\), \[\sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}|R_{\nu}(\delta)-R(\delta)|\le\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu}),\qquad |v_{\nu}-v|\le\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu}).\] Hence a certified \(\widetilde\delta_{\nu}\) computed at a strictly positive tolerance \(\varepsilon_{\nu}>0\) obeys \(R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v\le\varepsilon_{\nu}+2\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu})\), including along sequences crossing endpoint-face ties and all three indexability categories. Under \(\mathrm{ass:uniform-two-sided-experiment-equivalence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-legality}\), \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-kappa-bounds}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:joint-risk-transfer}\), for every \(n\) and \(h\in H\) the training-sample estimate satisfies \(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\in\mathcal G_p\) and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\), \(P_{n,h}\)-almost surely. Moreover, \(\mathcal L_{Q_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)=\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)\), so both properties also hold \(Q_{n,h}\)-almost surely. On this common legal event, run \(\mathrm{def:stable-selector}\) at a strictly positive tolerance \(\varepsilon_n>0\), and define \(\alpha_n=\sup_{h\in H}E_{P_{n,h}}[\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(d_{\mathsf G}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n,\mathsf G))]\). The resulting feasible certified rule satisfies \[-M_{\kappa}\lambda_n\le\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v\le\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n+M_{\kappa}\gamma_n.\] Consequently, if the strictly positive sequence \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) and \(\alpha_n+\gamma_n+\lambda_n\to0\), then \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)\to v\). The sequence \(\lambda_n\) is an assumed or independently verified approximation error; no rate is asserted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the deterministic modulus. Write \(\mathsf G'=(H',\Sigma',\ell',u')\), suppose both \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G)\) and \(\mathsf B_{\kappa}(\mathsf G')\) hold, and put \(\rho=d_{\mathsf G}(\mathsf G',\mathsf G)\leq(2\kappa)^{-1}\). Every endpoint loss weight in either game is bounded by
\[
 \kappa+\kappa^2=M_{\kappa}, \tag{1}
\]
because every mean has norm at most \(\kappa\), and every affine intercept and normal has magnitude or norm at most \(\kappa\).

Fix a signed state \((h',\sigma)\) of \(\mathsf G'\). Hausdorff distance at most \(\rho\) between the corresponding endpoint graphs supplies a state \((h,\sigma)\) of \(\mathsf G\) such that
\[
 \|h'-h\|\leq\rho,
 \qquad |c'_{\sigma}(h')-c_{\sigma}(h)|\leq\rho, \tag{2}
\]
where the second inequality uses the fact that the positive-part map is one-Lipschitz. Put \(E=\Sigma^{-1/2}(\Sigma'-\Sigma)\Sigma^{-1/2}\). The eigenvalue bound and the definition of \(d_{\mathsf G}\) give
\(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa\rho\leq1/2\). Direct integration of the two Gaussian densities gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\{N(h',\Sigma')\|N(h,\Sigma)\}
 =\frac12\left[\operatorname{tr}E-\log\det(I+E)
 +(h-h')^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(h-h')\right]. \tag{3}
\]
For \(|x|\leq1/2\), \(x-\log(1+x)\leq2x^2\), as follows by differentiating the two sides. Thus (3) is at most
\[
 \rho^2\left(p\kappa^2+\frac\kappa2\right). \tag{4}
\]
The total-variation inequality used here follows directly from scalar convexity: applying the log-sum inequality to the set where the first density exceeds the second reduces relative entropy to binary relative entropy, and two differentiations show that binary relative entropy is at least twice the squared difference of its success probabilities. Therefore
\[
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)/2}.
\]
Together with (4), this gives
\[
 \|N(h',\Sigma')-N(h,\Sigma)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\rho\sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}. \tag{5}
\]
For every \(0\leq\delta\leq1\), differences of expectations are bounded by total variation. Equations (1), (2), and (5) therefore imply, for the matched signed states,
\[
 |r'_{(h',\sigma)}(\delta)-r_{(h,\sigma)}(\delta)|
 \leq\rho+M_{\kappa}\rho\sqrt{p\kappa^2/2+\kappa/4}
 =\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). \tag{6}
\]
Repeating the graph matching in the opposite direction and taking suprema proves
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\mathcal D}|R'(\delta)-R(\delta)|
 \leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho). \tag{7}
\]
Taking infima in (7) gives \(|v'-v|\leq\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)\). For larger \(\rho\), both signed risks lie in \([0,M_{\kappa}]\), so the same two conclusions hold with the extended modulus \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho)=M_{\kappa}\).

Apply (7) to \(\mathsf G_{\nu}\). The gap lemma gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v
 &\leq |R(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})|
 +(R_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta_{\nu})-v_{\nu})+|v_{\nu}-v|\\
 &\leq2\omega_{p,\kappa}(\rho_{\nu})+\varepsilon_{\nu}.
\end{aligned}\tag{8}
\]
The derivation uses neither uniqueness nor constancy of active faces, so it is uniform across every saddle, face, and indexability tie.

We turn to the original experiment. The almost-sure legality and boundedness assertions follow separately from \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-legality}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:estimated-game-kappa-bounds}\). They also hold under \(Q_{n,h}\), because the definition of that law gives
\[
 \mathcal L_{Q_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n)
 =\mathcal L_{P_{n,h}}(\widehat{\mathsf G}_n). \tag{9}
\]
Condition on a realized legal game \(G'=\widehat{\mathsf G}_n\), and let \(d_{G'}\) be the certified rule chosen at tolerance \(\varepsilon_n>0\). Under \(Q_{n,h}\), its signal has law \(N(h,\widehat\Sigma_n)\). Match the true signed state \((h,\sigma)\) to the corresponding endpoint graph of \(G'\). The proof of (6), now with covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) on both sides of this single state comparison, bounds the conditional true signed loss by the matching risk in \(G'\) plus \(\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(d_{\mathsf G}(G',\mathsf G))\). The gap lemma and the value part of (7) then give, for either sign,
\[
 \operatorname{loss}_{Q_{n,h}}(h,\sigma\mid G')
 \leq v+\varepsilon_n
 +2\overline\omega_{p,\kappa}(d_{\mathsf G}(G',\mathsf G)). \tag{10}
\]
The common marginal in (9) and the definition of \(\alpha_n\) imply, after averaging (10),
\[
 \sup_{h\in H}\max_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
 \operatorname{loss}_{Q_{n,h}}(h,\sigma)
 \leq v+\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n. \tag{11}
\]
Each loss integrand is between zero and \(M_{\kappa}\). The joint total-variation premise \(\mathrm{ass:joint-risk-transfer}\) therefore transfers (11) to the actual joint law at cost at most \(M_{\kappa}\gamma_n\), proving
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v
 \leq\varepsilon_n+2\alpha_n+M_{\kappa}\gamma_n. \tag{12}
\]

For the lower bound, take any feasible original-experiment decision \(d:\mathcal X_n\to[0,1]\), including any auxiliary randomization through its conditional treatment probability, and compose it with the reverse kernel:
\[
 \delta_d(z)=\int_{\mathcal X_n}d(x)L_n(dx\mid z).
\]
This is a member of \(\mathcal D\). The reverse half of \(\mathrm{ass:uniform-two-sided-experiment-equivalence}\) and (1) imply, state by state,
\[
 r_s(\delta_d)\leq r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,s}(d)+M_{\kappa}\lambda_n.
\]
Here \(r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,(h,+)}(d)=c_{+}(h)\{1-E_{P_{n,h}}d\}\) and \(r^{\mathrm{orig}}_{n,(h,-)}(d)=c_{-}(h)E_{P_{n,h}}d\), so their supremum is exactly \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)\).
Taking the supremum over \(s\), then using \(v\leq R(\delta_d)\), yields
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(d)-v\geq-M_{\kappa}\lambda_n. \tag{13}
\]
Apply (13) to \(d=\widehat\delta_n\) and combine it with (12). If \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) through strictly positive tolerances and \(\alpha_n+\gamma_n+\lambda_n\to0\), the two bounds squeeze \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{loc}}(\widehat\delta_n)-v\) to zero. No step supplies a rate for any of these externally controlled error sequences.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit primitive-game modulus controls values and certified rules, while the nonstandard two-sided experiment-equivalence premise, separate almost-sure legality and numerical boundedness, and joint total-variation premise control every bounded endpoint loss; multiplicity and active-face changes therefore do not invalidate the conditional feasible transfer. \textit{Gap.} Theorem 3 of MontielOleaQiuStoye2026Local gives the feasible fully differentiable rule, while Assumption 5 and Theorem 5 give feasible transfer for its directionally differentiable centrosymmetric rule. HiranoPorter2009 motivates pointwise limit-experiment treatment-rule analysis but does not supply the compact-\(H\), two-sided total-variation equivalence or the joint approximation with an estimated game assumed here. The present theorem conditionally treats the non-centrosymmetric polyhedral class and quantifies perturbation error uniformly over saddle and indexability ties; FangSantos2019 and FirpoGalvaoParker2023 explain why pointwise plug-in reasoning alone is insufficient at such nonsmooth boundaries. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers estimating covariance and bound derivatives obtain a feasible treatment rule whose regret guarantee remains valid near face and indexability boundaries.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The curved-score theorem is a sufficient certificate, not the requested necessary-and-sufficient normal-fan classification. Its global premise must be closed by exact inequalities; a plot or floating-point near-zero gap is insufficient. The value-\(2/3\) triangular witness establishes coexistence, not the no-one-index branch. Face completeness concerns only faces charged by some dual optimum. Exchange iterates use exact restricted-game and worst-state oracles, make no complexity-rate claim, and request a strictly positive tolerance at every finite run; no finite termination at tolerance zero is asserted. The conditional transfer corollary requires two-sided experiment equivalence, legal \(\kappa\)-bounded estimated games, and joint total-variation approximation, none of which follows from ordinary DQM or pointwise LAN.

\section{Honest scope}

The theorem-level frontier is exact decision geometry in a two-dimensional compact polyhedral Gaussian endpoint game. The all-saddle theorem and face-complete priors hold in general dimension, the triangular construction certifies coexistence, and the curved-score theorem gives a finite sufficient route to the no-one-index category. The existence of a rational unbalanced witness satisfying that route and the full finite primitive trichotomy are open-ended questions. As a sanity check, the centered antipodal specialization recovers Yata's selected one-index rule, while the compact-polyhedral overlap with Theorem 4 of Montiel Olea--Qiu--Stoye has the identical objective by construction; no containment of their full Assumption-5 class is asserted. Conditional feasible transfer is secondary, has no named-model instantiation or rate claim, and uses only strictly positive finite certificate tolerances tending to zero along a sequence.
