%%% FIELD proof-endpoint-exclusion
Fix a member of \(\mathfrak M\), and condition throughout on its deterministic complete baseline array.  Write
\[
T=T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}},
\qquad
a_- = \inf_{\beta\in\mathcal K}a(\beta)>0.
\tag{1}
\]
The strict inequality in (1) is the uniform positivity conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm{:}oracle\text{-}deletion\text{-}loss}\).  We first derive the strengthened deletion consequence needed in both branches.  Define
\[
u_n=sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\left|n d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta)-a(\beta)\right|.
\tag{2}
\]
For every balanced sequence for which \(N\to\infty\), also define
\[
v_n=E_{S_m}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\left|N d_T(\beta)-a(\beta)\right|.
\tag{3}
\]
The first two conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm{:}oracle\text{-}deletion\text{-}loss}\) give \(u_n\to0\) and \(v_n\to0\).  By \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}risk\text{-}components}\), and using \(N=n-m\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
nD_{n,m}(\beta)-\frac mn a(\beta)
&=n d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta)-a(\beta)\\
&\quad-\frac Nn E_{S_m}
\left\{N d_T(\beta)-a(\beta)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\beta\) in (4) yields
\[
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\left|nD_{n,m}(\beta)-\frac mn a(\beta)\right|
\leq u_n+\frac Nn v_n
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus (5) holds for every balanced sequence with \(N\to\infty\), without requiring convergence of \(m/n\).  In particular, if \(m/n\to c\), then \(nD_{n,m}(\beta)\to c a(\beta)\) uniformly in \(\beta\), so the earlier fixed-fraction display is a corollary of (5).

We next obtain a lower bound for every pilot-measurable action.  Put
\[
q_n(\beta)=E_{S_m}\inf_{H\in\mathcal H_T}
N\left\{V_T(H,\beta)-V_T(G_T(\beta),\beta)\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
By \(\mathrm{prop{:}first\text{-}order\text{-}arbitrary\text{-}action\text{-}optimality}\),
\[
\epsilon_n:=\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}|q_n(\beta)|\longrightarrow0
\qquad (N\to\infty).
\tag{7}
\]
Conditional on \(S_m\) and the pilot information \(\mathcal O_m\), the realized action \(\delta_m\) is an element of \(\mathcal H_T\).  The main-wave outcomes are conditionally independent of the pilot outcomes by \(\mathrm{ass{:}stable\text{-}product\text{-}law}\).  Hence conditioning first on \((S_m,\mathcal O_m)\), then bounding the realized action by the infimum over \(H\in\mathcal H_T\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
nL_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)
&=\frac Nn E\!\left[
N\left\{V_T(\delta_m,\beta)
-V_T(G_T(\beta),\beta)\right\}
\right]\\
&\geq \frac Nn q_n(\beta)
\geq-\frac Nn\epsilon_n .
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
This is uniform over \(\beta\) and over all pilot rules.  Combining (5), (8), and the exact identity in \(\mathrm{prop{:}second\text{-}order\text{-}risk\text{-}reduction}\), we obtain, for every \(N\to\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
n\left\{R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta)\right\}
&=nD_{n,m}(\beta)+nL_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)\\
&\geq \frac mn a(\beta)-u_n-\frac Nn(v_n+\epsilon_n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Suppose first that
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac mn>0,
\qquad
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac Nn>0.
\tag{10}
\]
The second condition in (10) implies \(N\to\infty\).  If \(c_m=\liminf_n m/n\), then (1), (7), and (9) show that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), all \(\beta\in\mathcal K\), and every \(\delta_m\),
\[
R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta)
\geq \frac{c_m a_-}{2n}>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Taking the positive part, the supremum over \(\beta\), and then the infimum over \(\delta_m\) in (11) gives
\[
\mathcal E_n(m)\geq \frac{c_m a_-}{2n}.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently \(n^{3/2}\mathcal E_n(m)\to\infty\).  Notice that no limit of \(m/n\) is needed; the two lower limits in (10) are sufficient.

It remains to treat \(N=o(n)\).  Along any subsequence on which \(N\to\infty\), one has \(m/n\to1\), so (9) implies, eventually and uniformly over \(\beta\) and \(\delta_m\),
\[
R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta)
\geq \frac{a_-}{2n}.
\tag{13}
\]
For a subsequence on which \(N\) is bounded, fix any \(\beta_0\in\mathcal K\).  Combining the two main-wave oracle terms in \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}ht\text{-}accounting}\) gives the exact equality
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta_0)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta_0)
&=d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta_0)\\
&\quad+\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E\!\left[
V_T(\delta_m,\beta_0)-V_T(0,\beta_0)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Every design risk is nonnegative.  Because the model member is fixed, \(\mathrm{ass{:}arraywise\text{-}moment\text{-}regularity}\) supplies a common finite second-moment envelope.  Applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\text{-}single\text{-}wave\text{-}risk}\) with \(H=0\) therefore gives a constant \(C<\infty\), independent of the balanced set \(T\), such that
\[
V_T(0,\beta_0)\leq \frac CN.
\tag{15}
\]
Since admissibility forces \(N\geq Q\), division by \(N\) in (15) is valid.  Equations (14)--(15) imply
\[
n\left\{R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta_0)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta_0)\right\}
\geq n d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta_0)-C\frac Nn.
\tag{16}
\]
Along the bounded-\(N\) subsequence, the right side of (16) converges to \(a(\beta_0)>0\) by \(\mathrm{thm{:}oracle\text{-}deletion\text{-}loss}\).  Thus, eventually along that subsequence and for every \(\delta_m\),
\[
\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\left[R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta)\right]_+
\geq \frac{a(\beta_0)}{2n}.
\tag{17}
\]

To combine (13) and (17), suppose for contradiction that a sequence with \(N=o(n)\) had \(\liminf_n n\mathcal E_n(m)=0\).  Choose a subsequence along which \(n\mathcal E_n(m)\to0\).  Its integer sequence \(N\geq Q\) has a further subsequence on which either \(N\) is bounded or \(N\to\infty\).  The first alternative contradicts (17) after taking the infimum over \(\delta_m\), and the second contradicts (13).  Therefore
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n\mathcal E_n(m)>0
\qquad\text{whenever }N=o(n).
\tag{18}
\]
Multiplying (18), or the bound (12), by \(\sqrt n\) proves
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{3/2}\mathcal E_n(m)=\infty
\tag{19}
\]
under either of the two asserted endpoint conditions.  All finite-lattice ties and unequal inclusion probabilities are already retained in the exact identities (8), (14), and (16); positivity of the exact mixture ensures the Horvitz--Thompson risks used there are well defined.  The argument makes no claim for bounded pilots, diverging pilots with \(m=o(\sqrt n)\), or the large interior window \(\sqrt n=o(m)=o(n)\).
