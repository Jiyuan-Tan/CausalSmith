Because \(\mathcal K\) is compact, because \(A\) and \(B\) are continuous, and because their values are strictly positive, the extreme-value theorem gives finite constants
\[
 a_-:=\min_{\beta\in\mathcal K}A(\beta)>0,
 \qquad
 b_-:=\min_{\beta\in\mathcal K}B(\beta)>0.
\]
Put \(t=e^s\) and, for \(\beta\in\mathcal K\), define
\[
 f_\beta(s):=A(\beta)e^s+B(\beta)e^{-s},
 \qquad
 h(s):=\Phi(e^s)=\max_{\beta\in\mathcal K}f_\beta(s).
\]
The map \((\beta,s)\mapsto f_\beta(s)\) is continuous.  Compactness of \(\mathcal K\) therefore implies that the displayed maximum is attained and that \(h\) is continuous.  Moreover,
\[
 f_\beta''(s)=f_\beta(s)
 \ge 2\sqrt{A(\beta)B(\beta)}
 \ge 2\sqrt{a_-b_-}=:\mu>0,
\]
where the first inequality is the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality.  Consequently
\[
 s\longmapsto f_\beta(s)-\frac{\mu}{2}s^2
\]
is convex for every \(\beta\).  Taking pointwise maxima preserves convexity, so
\[
 h(s)-\frac{\mu}{2}s^2
 =\max_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
 \left\{f_\beta(s)-\frac{\mu}{2}s^2\right\}
\]
is convex.  Thus \(h\) is strongly convex and in particular has at most one minimizer.  Also, for every \(s\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 h(s)\ge a_-e^s+b_-e^{-s},
\]
whose right-hand side tends to infinity as \(s\to\infty\) or \(s\to-\infty\).  Hence continuity and coercivity give a minimizer \(s^\star\), and strong convexity makes it unique.  Since \(s\mapsto e^s\) is a bijection from \(\mathbb R\) to \((0,\infty)\), \(t^\star=e^{s^\star}\) is the unique minimizer of \(\Phi\).

Define the nonempty compact active set
\[
 \mathcal A^\star
 :=\left\{\beta\in\mathcal K:
 A(\beta)t^\star+\frac{B(\beta)}{t^\star}
 =\Phi(t^\star)\right\}
\]
and the derivative
\[
 d_\beta:=A(\beta)-\frac{B(\beta)}{(t^\star)^2}.
\]
We next establish the needed first-order condition directly.  For \(u>0\), comparison with each active \(\beta\), followed by Taylor expansion uniform over the compact parameter set, gives
\[
 \liminf_{u\downarrow0}
 \frac{\Phi(t^\star+u)-\Phi(t^\star)}{u}
 \ge \max_{\beta\in\mathcal A^\star}d_\beta.
\]
Conversely, choose a maximizer \(\beta_u\) at \(t^\star+u\).  Along every sequence \(u\downarrow0\), compactness supplies a convergent subsequence.  Continuity of the objective and of \(\Phi\) makes every such subsequential limit an element of \(\mathcal A^\star\).  Since
\[
 \frac{\Phi(t^\star+u)-\Phi(t^\star)}{u}
 \le
 \frac{A(\beta_u)(t^\star+u)+B(\beta_u)/(t^\star+u)
       -A(\beta_u)t^\star-B(\beta_u)/t^\star}{u},
\]
uniform Taylor expansion yields the reverse upper bound.  Therefore
\[
 \Phi'_+(t^\star)=\max_{\beta\in\mathcal A^\star}d_\beta.
\]
The same argument with \(u<0\), noting the reversal produced by division by a negative number, gives
\[
 \Phi'_-(t^\star)=\min_{\beta\in\mathcal A^\star}d_\beta.
\]
Minimality of \(t^\star\) implies \(\Phi'_-(t^\star)\le0\le\Phi'_+(t^\star)\), and hence
\[
 0\in\operatorname{conv}
 \{d_\beta:\beta\in\mathcal A^\star\}.
\]
Because this convex hull lies in one dimension, either some active derivative is zero or two active derivatives bracket zero.  Thus there are \(J\le2\), active points \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_J\), and weights \(\lambda_j\ge0\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_j=1\), such that
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_jd_{\beta_j}=0.
\]
Set
\[
 a^\star:=\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_jA(\beta_j),
 \qquad
 b^\star:=\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_jB(\beta_j).
\]
Both numbers are positive, and the preceding derivative identity is exactly
\[
 a^\star=\frac{b^\star}{(t^\star)^2},
 \qquad\text{so}\qquad
 t^\star=\sqrt{\frac{b^\star}{a^\star}}.
\]
Since every selected point is active, averaging its active equality gives
\[
 \Phi(t^\star)
 =a^\star t^\star+\frac{b^\star}{t^\star}
 =2\sqrt{a^\star b^\star}.
\tag{1}
\]

It remains to certify the global convex-hull formula.  Let
\[
 \mathcal C:=\operatorname{conv}
 \{(A(\beta),B(\beta)):\beta\in\mathcal K\}.
\]
This set is compact: any finite convex combination in \(\mathbb R^2\) involving more than three points has an affine dependence, and its weights can be varied along that dependence until one weight vanishes; iteration reduces the representation to at most three points.  Hence \(\mathcal C\) is the continuous image of the compact set \(\mathcal K^3\times\{(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3):\lambda_j\ge0,\ \sum_j\lambda_j=1\}\).  For every \((a,b)\in\mathcal C\), linearity in \((a,b)\) and the definition of \(\Phi\) imply
\[
 at^\star+\frac{b}{t^\star}\le\Phi(t^\star).
\]
The arithmetic--geometric mean inequality then gives
\[
 2\sqrt{ab}
 \le at^\star+\frac{b}{t^\star}
 \le\Phi(t^\star).
\tag{2}
\]
The point \((a^\star,b^\star)\) belongs to \(\mathcal C\), and equation \((1)\) shows that both inequalities in \((2)\) are equalities there.  Therefore
\[
 \Phi(t^\star)
 =\max_{(a,b)\in\mathcal C}2\sqrt{ab}.
\]
The construction of \((a^\star,b^\star)\) simultaneously supplies the asserted certificate using at most two active parameter values and completes the proof.
