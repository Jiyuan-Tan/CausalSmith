%%% FIELD proof-exact-ht-accounting
Put \(w_q=2^{1-K_f}c_q\).  Fix a nonempty balanced set \(T\), a legal action \(H\), and a realized bounded potential-outcome table.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}mixture}\),
\[
p_{T,H}(z)
=|\Omega_T|^{-1}\left\{\eta+\frac{1-\eta}{\alpha}a_{T,H}(z)\right\}
\geq \frac{\eta}{|\Omega_T|}.
\]
Exactly \(|\Omega_T|/Q\) members of \(\Omega_T\) assign a fixed unit \(i\) to a fixed \(q\), so the definition of \(\pi_{iq}(T,H)\) gives
\[
\pi_{iq}(T,H)
=\sum_{z\in\Omega_T}p_{T,H}(z)\mathbf 1\{z_i=q\}
\geq \frac{\eta}{Q}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus every inverse inclusion probability in \(\mathrm{def{:}pooled\text{-}ht}\) is well defined.  Moreover, directly from that same inclusion-probability definition,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{p_{T,H}}\!\left\{
\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(H)
\mid\mathbf Y_n,\mathbf X_n\right\}
&=\frac1{|T|}\sum_{i\in T}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}
w_qY_{in}(q)
\frac{P_{p_{T,H}}(Z_i=q)}{\pi_{iq}(T,H)}\\
&=\frac1{|T|}\sum_{i\in T}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}w_qY_{in}(q)
=\boldsymbol\tau_T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
No label symmetry and no equality among the \(\pi_{iq}(T,H)\) was used.

For a realized split \(S=S_m\), let \(T=S^c\) and define
\[
e_S=\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_S(0)-\boldsymbol\tau_S,
\qquad
e_T=\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(\delta_m)-\boldsymbol\tau_T.
\]
Partitioning the finite-population sum in the definition of the causal estimand gives the pointwise identity
\[
\boldsymbol\tau_n=\frac mn\boldsymbol\tau_S+\frac Nn\boldsymbol\tau_T.
\tag{3}
\]
Conditional on \(S,\mathbf Y_n,\mathbf X_n,\mathcal O_m\), the pilot-measurable action \(\delta_m\) is fixed.  Applying (2) to the subsequent main-wave randomization yields
\[
E(e_T\mid S,\mathbf Y_n,\mathbf X_n,\mathcal O_m)=0.
\]
Since \(e_S\) is measurable under this conditioning,
\[
E(e_S^{\mathsf T}We_T\mid S,\mathbf Y_n,\mathbf X_n,\mathcal O_m)=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Equation (2), first for the pilot and then conditionally for the main wave, together with (3), also proves
\[
E\{\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{n,m}(\delta_m)
\mid\mathbf Y_n,\mathbf X_n\}=\boldsymbol\tau_n.
\tag{5}
\]

Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}stable\text{-}product\text{-}law}\), conditional on the split, the unobserved main-wave outcome vectors have their product conditional law independently of the pilot outcomes on which \(\delta_m\) depends.  Therefore averaging (4) first over the two randomizations and then over the conditional outcome law gives
\[
R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)
=\left(\frac mn\right)^2E_{S_m}V_{S_m}(0,\beta)
+\left(\frac Nn\right)^2
E V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\delta_m,\beta).
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to rewrite the complete-randomization component.  If \(H=0\), every score is zero.  The common boundary randomization and exact mass condition force
\[
a_{T,0}(z)=\alpha\quad(z\in\Omega_T),
\]
and hence \(p_{T,0}(z)=|\Omega_T|^{-1}\) and \(\pi_{iq}(T,0)=1/Q\).  Generate a uniform \(m\)-subset, assign it uniformly on its balanced support, and independently assign its complement uniformly on its balanced support.  For any \(z\in\Omega_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}\), the number of compatible pilot subsets is
\[
\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}{n/Q\choose m/Q},
\]
which is independent of \(z\).  All split and conditional assignment probabilities also depend only on \(n,m,Q\).  Thus the combined assignment is uniform on \(\Omega_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}\).  Pointwise in the split and combined assignment,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac mn\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}\sum_q
\frac{w_q\mathbf 1\{Z_i=q\}Y_{in}(q)}{1/Q}
+\frac Nn\frac1N\sum_{i\in T}\sum_q
\frac{w_q\mathbf 1\{Z_i=q\}Y_{in}(q)}{1/Q}\\
&\hspace{3em}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_q
\frac{w_q\mathbf 1\{Z_i=q\}Y_{in}(q)}{1/Q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Applying the cross-term cancellation (4) with both actions equal to zero, and using the just-proved equality of assignment laws, gives
\[
V_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(0,\beta)
=\left(\frac mn\right)^2E_{S_m}V_{S_m}(0,\beta)
+\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E_{S_m}
V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(0,\beta).
\tag{8}
\]

Subtract \(\mathrm{def{:}empirical\text{-}oracle\text{-}comparator}\),
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta)
=V_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}
\!\left(G_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta),\beta\right),
\]
from (6), substitute (8), and add and subtract
\[
\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E
V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}
\!\left(G_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\beta),\beta\right).
\]
The definition of \(d_T\) then yields, without approximation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{n,m}(\delta_m,\beta)-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}(\beta)
&=d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta)
-\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E_{S_m}
d_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\beta)\\
&\quad+\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E\!\left[
V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\delta_m,\beta)
-V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}
\!\left(G_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\beta),\beta\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Randomized ties only change the numerical values in \(p_{T,H}\), and unequal inclusions remain explicitly in (1)--(2); neither affects an equality above.

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-single-wave-risk
Let \(U_T\) be the uniform law on \(\Omega_T\), put \(k=|T|\), and set
\[
L=\eta+\frac{1-\eta}{\alpha}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}mixture}\) and the counting argument in (1) of \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}ht\text{-}accounting}\),
\[
\frac{p_{T,H}(z)}{U_T(z)}
=\eta+\frac{1-\eta}{\alpha}a_{T,H}(z)\leq L,
\qquad
\pi_{iq}(T,H)\geq\frac{\eta}{Q}.
\tag{9}
\]

Fix a Euclidean coordinate vector \(e\) after applying \(W^{1/2}\), and define
\[
f_i(q)=
\frac{e^{\mathsf T}W^{1/2}w_qY_{in}(q)}
{\pi_{iq}(T,H)}.
\]
Under \(U_T\), \(Z_i\) is uniform on \(\mathcal Q\).  Direct enumeration of the ordered labels assigned to two distinct units gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{U_T}\{f_i(Z_i),f_j(Z_j)\}
=-\frac1{k-1}
\operatorname{Cov}_{q\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal Q)}
\{f_i(q),f_j(q)\},
\qquad i\ne j.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, for \(q,r\in\mathcal Q\), the joint probability is
\[
P_{U_T}(Z_i=q,Z_j=r)
=\frac{(k/Q)(k/Q-\mathbf 1\{q=r\})}{k(k-1)},
\]
and substitution proves (10).  Expanding the variance and collecting diagonal and off-diagonal terms therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_{U_T}\!\left\{\sum_{i\in T}f_i(Z_i)\right\}
&=\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_{i\in T}\operatorname{Var}_{q}\{f_i(q)\}\\
&\quad-\frac1{k-1}
\operatorname{Var}_{q}\!\left\{\sum_{i\in T}f_i(q)\right\}\\
&\leq\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_{i\in T}E_q f_i(q)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Let \(X_e=k^{-1}\sum_i f_i(Z_i)\) and \(c_e=E_{U_T}X_e\).  Exact Horvitz--Thompson centering from \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}ht\text{-}accounting}\), followed by (9), yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{p_{T,H}}(X_e)
\leq E_{p_{T,H}}(X_e-c_e)^2
\leq L E_{U_T}(X_e-c_e)^2
=L\operatorname{Var}_{U_T}(X_e).
\tag{12}
\]
Sum (12) over an orthonormal basis, apply (11), and use
\[
\pi_{iq}^{-2}\leq\frac{Q^2}{\eta^2},
\qquad
\sum_e\{e^{\mathsf T}W^{1/2}w_qY_{in}(q)\}^2
=Y_{in}(q)^2w_q^{\mathsf T}Ww_q.
\]
Because \(\mathrm{ass{:}factor\text{-}count}\) inside \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) gives \(Q\geq4\), every balanced \(k\geq Q\) satisfies \(k/(k-1)\leq4/3\).  Conditional on the table, the result is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_{p_{T,H}}\!\left[
(\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(H)-\boldsymbol\tau_T)^{\mathsf T}
W(\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(H)-\boldsymbol\tau_T)
\mid\mathbf Y_n,\mathbf X_n\right]\\
&\qquad\leq
\frac{4LQ}{3\eta^2k^2}
\left\{\max_{q\in\mathcal Q}w_q^{\mathsf T}Ww_q\right\}
\sum_{i\in T}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}Y_{in}(q)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Averaging (13) under \(P_{\beta,n}(d\mathbf Y_n\mid\mathbf X_n)\) proves the displayed inequality in the lemma.  If the displayed average conditional second moment has a common finite envelope on a subclass, its double sum is \(O(k)\), so (13) is uniformly \(O(k^{-1})\) there.

Finally, the qualification concerning all of \(\mathfrak M\) is necessary.  Starting with any admissible nonzero bounded regular conditional family, push it forward by \(Y(q)\mapsto M Y(q)\) for an arbitrary finite \(M>0\).  Each rescaled member still has bounded outcomes and the same score information after the one-to-one change of sample coordinate; trace normalization leaves the oracle action unchanged, whereas every quadratic design risk is multiplied by \(M^2\).  Hence memberwise boundedness alone cannot furnish one numerical risk constant uniform over all members of \(\mathfrak M\).

%%% FIELD proof-experiment-indexed-oracle-convergence
Fix one member of \(\mathfrak M\), write \(k=|T|\), and define
\[
\bar x_T=\frac1k\sum_{i\in T}x_{in},
\qquad
S_T=\frac1k\sum_{i\in T}
(x_{in}-\bar x_T)(x_{in}-\bar x_T)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Let
\[
\mu_q(x,\beta)=E_\beta\{Y(q)\mid X=x\},
\qquad w_q=2^{1-K_f}c_q,
\]
and
\[
D_T(\beta)
=\frac1k\sum_{i\in T}\frac1Q\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}
\mu_q(x_{in},\beta)w_q
\{w_q\otimes(x_{in}-\bar x_T)\}^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{14}
\]
We first express the unnormalized oracle entirely through these empirical moments.

For vector arrays \(a_i(q)\) and \(b_i(q)\), direct use of the balanced two-unit probabilities displayed in (10) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}_{U_T}\!\left\{\sum_i a_i(Z_i),\sum_i b_i(Z_i)\right\}
&=\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_i\operatorname{Cov}_q\{a_i(q),b_i(q)\}\\
&\quad-\frac1{k-1}
\operatorname{Cov}_q\!\left\{\sum_i a_i(q),\sum_i b_i(q)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The nonconstant Walsh characters satisfy
\[
\frac1Q\sum_qw_q=0,
\qquad
\frac1Q\sum_qw_qw_q^{\mathsf T}=\frac4{Q^2}I_F.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(b_i(q)=w_q\otimes(x_{in}-\bar x_T)\), one has \(\sum_i b_i(q)=0\).  Since
\[
\Delta_T=\frac Qk\sum_{i\in T}b_i(Z_i),
\]
equations (15)--(16) imply the exact identity
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_T)
=\frac4{k-1}(I_F\otimes S_T).
\tag{17}
\]
Likewise, conditional outcome averaging and \(\mathrm{ass{:}stable\text{-}product\text{-}law}\) show that only \(\mu_q\) enters the covariance with \(\Delta_T\).  Taking \(a_i(q)=w_q\mu_q(x_{in},\beta)\) in (15) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\beta\{
\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(0),\Delta_T\}
=\frac{Q^2}{k-1}D_T(\beta).
\tag{18}
\]
Whenever \(S_T\) is nonsingular, (17)--(18) yield
\[
\mathcal B_T(\beta)
=\frac{Q^2}{4}D_T(\beta)(I_F\otimes S_T^{-1}).
\tag{19}
\]

We now pass to the limit.  The compact set \(\mathcal X\) in
\(\mathrm{ass{:}arraywise\text{-}moment\text{-}regularity}\) contains every baseline point and \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\).  For \(0\leq j\leq3\), every coordinate of
\(D_\rho^j\mu_q(x,\beta)\), and every product of it with a coordinate polynomial of degree at most two in \(x\), is continuous on the compact set
\(\mathcal X\times\mathcal K\), uniformly bounded, and uniformly continuous.  A finite-net argument in \(\beta\), followed by
\(\mathrm{ass{:}baseline\text{-}limit}\) at the net points, therefore gives uniform convergence of all empirical moments in (14) and their first three \(\rho\)-derivatives.  In particular, with
\[
\bar x_\nu=\int x\,\nu(dx),
\qquad
S_\nu=\int(x-\bar x_\nu)(x-\bar x_\nu)^{\mathsf T}\nu(dx),
\]
there is a \(C^3\) matrix function \(D_\infty\) such that
\[
S_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}\to S_\nu,
\qquad
\max_{0\leq j\leq3}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\|D_\rho^jD_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta)
-D_\rho^jD_\infty(\beta)\|\to0.
\tag{20}
\]

The Gram lower bound in \(\mathrm{ass{:}baseline\text{-}gram}\) and (20) give
\(\lambda_{\min}(S_\nu)>0\).  Hence matrix inversion is smooth on a fixed neighborhood of \(S_\nu\), and (19)--(20), with the ordinary product and inverse differentiation rules, produce a \(C^3\) limit
\[
\mathcal B_\infty(\beta)
=\frac{Q^2}{4}D_\infty(\beta)(I_F\otimes S_\nu^{-1})
\tag{21}
\]
such that
\[
\max_{0\leq j\leq3}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\|D_\rho^j\mathcal B_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta)
-D_\rho^j\mathcal B_\infty(\beta)\|\to0.
\tag{22}
\]

The same limit holds along the complements.  Indeed, for every uniformly bounded scalar function \(f\) appearing in the preceding finite list of moment coordinates,
\[
\frac1N\sum_{i\notin S_m}f(x_{in},\beta)
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf(x_{in},\beta)
=\frac mN\left\{
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf(x_{in},\beta)
-\frac1m\sum_{i\in S_m}f(x_{in},\beta)
\right\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus the supremum of the absolute value in (23), also for each required \(\rho\)-derivative, is \(O(m/N)=o(1)\), uniformly over the split.  The same calculation for first and second baseline moments shows
\[
\sup_{S_m}\|S_{S_m^c}-S_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}\|=O(m/N)=o(1).
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently (19)--(22) also imply
\[
\max_{0\leq j\leq3}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\|D_\rho^j\mathcal B_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\beta)
-D_\rho^j\mathcal B_\infty(\beta)\|
\xrightarrow{P_{S_m}}0.
\tag{25}
\]

Define the raw matrices
\[
M_T(\beta)=\mathcal B_T(\beta)^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal B_T(\beta),
\qquad
M_\infty(\beta)=\mathcal B_\infty(\beta)^{\mathsf T}
W\mathcal B_\infty(\beta).
\]
The product rule applied to (22) and (25) gives uniform convergence of \(M_T\) and all its \(\rho\)-derivatives through order three to the corresponding derivatives of \(M_\infty\).  Crucially, positivity of the normalization denominator now comes from the independent raw-signal assumptions, not from the rank of a normalized limit.  Specifically,
\(\mathrm{ass{:}full\text{-}oracle\text{-}signal\text{-}floor}\) and the uniform raw convergence imply
\[
\inf_{\beta\in\mathcal K}\operatorname{tr}M_\infty(\beta)>0,
\tag{26}
\]
while \(\mathrm{ass{:}complement\text{-}oracle\text{-}signal\text{-}floor}\) bounds the complement denominators away from zero on events whose split probability tends to one.

The map \(M\mapsto M/\operatorname{tr}(M)\) is \(C^\infty\) wherever
\(\operatorname{tr}(M)>0\).  Define
\[
G_\infty(\beta)=
\frac{M_\infty(\beta)}{\operatorname{tr}M_\infty(\beta)}.
\]
Equation (26) makes \(G_\infty\) a \(C^3\) map on \(\mathcal K\).  Repeated quotient differentiation, using the uniform raw derivative convergence and the denominator bounds just established, proves
\[
\max_{0\leq j\leq3}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\|D_\rho^jG_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta)
-D_\rho^jG_\infty(\beta)\|\to0
\]
and
\[
\max_{0\leq j\leq3}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}
\|D_\rho^jG_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\beta)
-D_\rho^jG_\infty(\beta)\|
\xrightarrow{P_{S_m}}0.
\]
This constructs the same limit for the complete array and every stated vanishing-complement sequence without any circular appeal to \(\operatorname{rank}(G_\infty)\).

%%% FIELD proof-gaussian-boundary-regularity
Set \(d=Fp\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}experiment\text{-}indexed\text{-}oracle\text{-}convergence}\), \(G_\infty(\beta)\) is positive semidefinite, has trace one, and is \(C^3\) in \(\beta\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}oracle\text{-}rank}\), its rank is the fixed positive integer \(r_G\).  The max--min variational formulas for ordered eigenvalues imply their continuity in the operator norm.  Hence compactness of \(\mathcal K\), constant rank, and unit trace give a constant \(\lambda_->0\) such that every positive eigenvalue \(\lambda_j(\beta)\) of \(G_\infty(\beta)\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_-\leq\lambda_j(\beta)\leq1,
\qquad 1\leq j\leq r_G,\quad\beta\in\mathcal K.
\tag{27}
\]

Rotational invariance of \(Z\) gives
\[
Z^{\mathsf T}G_\infty(\beta)Z
\ \stackrel{d}{=}\
\sum_{j=1}^{r_G}\lambda_j(\beta)\xi_j^2,
\tag{28}
\]
where the \(\xi_j\) are independent standard normal variables.  Each scaled chi-square summand has a continuous strictly positive density on \((0,\infty)\); convolution, with the one-summand formula when \(r_G=1\), proves the same for the sum.  On compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\), the bounds (27) give one integrable dominating convolution kernel.  Consequently both the distribution function and its density \(f_\beta(u)\) are jointly continuous in \((\beta,u)\).

Under the coupling in (28),
\[
\lambda_-\chi^2_{r_G}
\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r_G}\lambda_j(\beta)\xi_j^2
\leq\chi^2_{r_G}.
\tag{29}
\]
Because \(0<\alpha<1\), (29) places every \(q_{\alpha,\beta}\) in the compact interval
\[
\left[\lambda_-q_\alpha(\chi^2_{r_G}),
q_\alpha(\chi^2_{r_G})\right]\subset(0,\infty).
\tag{30}
\]
Joint continuity and strict increase of the distribution functions on \((0,\infty)\) show, by the defining quantile inequalities, that
\(\beta\mapsto q_{\alpha,\beta}\) is continuous.  Joint continuity and strict positivity of \(f_\beta\) on the compact quantile graph then give
\[
0<\inf_{\beta\in\mathcal K}f_\beta(q_{\alpha,\beta})
\leq\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal K}f_\beta(q_{\alpha,\beta})<\infty.
\tag{31}
\]

It remains to verify the coarea moments.  The spectral projection onto
\(\operatorname{range}G_\infty(\beta)\) is continuous because (27) separates the positive spectrum uniformly from zero.  Near any fixed \(\beta\), projecting a fixed basis and applying Gram--Schmidt supplies continuous orthonormal frames of the range and null space.  In these coordinates the positive range block \(A_\beta\) has spectrum in \([\lambda_-,1]\), and the level constraint is \(r^{\mathsf T}A_\beta r=u\).  Parameterize it by
\[
r=\sqrt u\,A_\beta^{-1/2}\omega,
\qquad \omega\in\mathbb S^{r_G-1}.
\tag{32}
\]
The null coordinate is unconstrained and retains its Gaussian density.  After inserting (32), each denominator, first moment, and second moment in the definition of \(\mathcal C\) is an integral over the fixed sphere and the null Euclidean space.  Its integrand is a polynomial of degree at most four in those coordinates, with coefficients continuous in
\[
u,\quad A_\beta,\quad
D_{\rho_a}G_\infty(\beta),\quad
D_{\rho_b}G_\infty(\beta),
\]
times a Gaussian factor and continuous determinant powers of \(A_\beta\).  On a compact neighborhood of the graph in (30), (27) supplies a common polynomial-times-Gaussian integrable bound.  Dominated convergence therefore proves continuity of all the raw coarea integrals.  The denominator is the strictly positive density in (31), so taking ratios and subtracting the product of the first moments proves continuity of
\(\mathcal C(\beta,u)\) on such a neighborhood.

The defining equality
\[
\Gamma_{ab}(\beta)
=\frac{1-\eta}{2\alpha}
f_\beta(q_{\alpha,\beta})
\mathcal C_{ab}(\beta,q_{\alpha,\beta})
\]
and the continuity already proved show that \(\Gamma\) is continuous on \(\mathcal K\).  Finally, if \(I_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is continuous with values in \(\mathbb S_{++}^r\), compactness bounds its least eigenvalue away from zero.  Matrix inversion, multiplication, and trace are continuous there, so
\[
B(\beta)=\operatorname{tr}\{\Gamma(\beta)I_{\mathrm{eff}}(\beta)^{-1}\}
\]
is continuous on \(\mathcal K\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-symmetry-reduction
Fix \(i\in T\).  The treatment-label flip group acts transitively on \(\mathcal Q\).  If \(p_{T,H}\) is invariant under that group, then for any \(q,r\in\mathcal Q\) a flip carrying \(q\) to \(r\) is a probability-preserving bijection between \(\{z:z_i=q\}\) and \(\{z:z_i=r\}\).  Hence \(\pi_{iq}(T,H)\) is constant in \(q\).  Since these \(Q\) probabilities sum to one,
\[
\pi_{iq}(T,H)=\frac1Q.
\]
Substitution in the Horvitz--Thompson formula gives
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(H)
=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}w_q
\left\{\frac Q{|T|}\sum_{i\in T:Z_i=q}Y_{in}(q)\right\},
\]
which is the ordinary balanced factorial contrast of the observed treatment-cell means.

When \(H=0\), every quadratic score equals zero.  The common randomized boundary rule with mean \(\alpha\) therefore has
\(a_{T,0}(z)=\alpha\) for every \(z\in\Omega_T\), and
\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}mixture}\) gives the uniform law on \(\Omega_T\).  For a fixed full balanced assignment, the number of \(m\)-subsets containing exactly \(m/Q\) units from each treatment cell is
\[
\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}{n/Q\choose m/Q},
\]
independent of the assignment.  Thus a uniform split followed by independent balanced randomizations of both waves induces the uniform law on the full-sample balanced support.  The pointwise calculation (7) in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}ht\text{-}accounting}\) then proves that the pooled estimator equals the full-sample balanced complete-randomization estimator.  These arguments are finite and exact; none supplies or invokes an asymptotic limit law.

%%% FIELD partial-oracle-deletion-loss
The exact accounting theorem reduces this node to a one-wave expansion.  If one establishes, uniformly on regular subsets,
\[
d_T(\beta)=\frac{A(\beta,M_T)}{|T|}+O(|T|^{-2}),
\]
where \(M_T\) is the finite vector of empirical moments and \(A(\beta,\cdot)\) has two bounded derivatives, then uniform-subset identities give
\[
E(M_T-M_n)=0,
\qquad
E\|M_T-M_n\|^2=O\{m/(nN)\}.
\]
Taylor expansion then yields
\[
d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}
-\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E d_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}
=A(\beta,M_n)\frac m{n^2}
+O\!\left(\frac m{n^3}+\frac1{n^2}\right),
\]
which has the required remainder on the interior pilot scale.  The missing part is the uniform finite-randomization expansion for \(d_T\).

%%% FIELD partial-local-action-regret
The fixed-baseline and product-law assumptions imply that the pilot likelihood ratio contains only the \(m\) revealed conditional outcome likelihood ratios.  Under the stated quadratic-mean differentiability conditions its formal central sequence is
\[
\Delta_m=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in S_m}
s_{Z_i,\beta}(Y_{in}(Z_i)\mid x_{in}),
\]
with nuisance-efficient information \(I_{\mathrm{eff}}\).  The Gaussian boundary calculation makes the candidate quadratic action loss equal to

\]
What is not established is the uniform triangular-array local-asymptotic-normality-to-decision transfer together with the exact finite Horvitz--Thompson boundary comparison for unrestricted measurable actions.

The exact accounting theorem proves the algebraic reduction
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{n,m}-R_n^{\mathrm{or}}
&=\left\{d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}
-\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E d_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}\right\}\\
&\quad+\left(\frac Nn\right)^2E\left\{
V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(\widehat G_m)
-V_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}}(G_{T_{n,m}^{\mathrm{main}}})
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the advertised expansion follows immediately once the first bracket is shown to equal
\[
A(\beta)m/n^2+o\{m/n^2+(nm)^{-1}\}
\]
uniformly and the second is shown to equal
\[
B(\beta)/(nm)+o\{m/n^2+(nm)^{-1}\}
\]
uniformly.  Those are precisely the two unresolved finite comparison statements; the exact identity introduces no third term.

%%% FIELD partial-pilot-endpoint-exclusion
For a fixed model member with a common conditional second-moment envelope, the corrected one-wave bound implies that the greatest possible contribution of a main wave of size \(N\) to the pooled risk is \(O(N/n^2)\).  Therefore, if
\[
n\,d_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(\beta_0)\to A(\beta_0)>0,
\]
every sequence with \(N=o(n)\) has excess at least \(A(\beta_0)/(2n)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and its \(n^{3/2}\)-scaled excess diverges.  Bounded-pilot and positive-fraction windows still require a first-order arbitrary-action lower comparison that is not supplied by the frozen assumptions.

%%% FIELD partial-global-sharp-pilot
The deterministic part is settled by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}deterministic\text{-}envelope\text{-}certificate}\): for continuous positive \(A,B\), the envelope has a unique minimizer and the convex-hull value has a certificate supported on at most two active parameter values.  Conditional on the uniform interior risk expansion and endpoint exclusion, balanced rounding changes \(m/\sqrt n\) by \(O(n^{-1/2})\); coercivity makes every asymptotically minimax pilot ratio precompact, and continuity plus uniqueness forces every subsequential limit to equal \(t^\star\).  The statistical premises of that conditional transfer remain open.
