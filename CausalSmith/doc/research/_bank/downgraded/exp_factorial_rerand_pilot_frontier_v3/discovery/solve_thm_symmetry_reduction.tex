Put (k=|T|).  The balanced support (Omega_T) is nonempty only for (Qmid k), and every (zinOmega_T) assigns exactly (k/Q) units to each treatment combination.  Let (mathcal G={-1,+1}^{K_f}) act on (mathcal Q) by componentwise multiplication and on (Omega_T) by
\[
(g\cdot z)_j=g z_j,qquad j\in T.
\]
This action preserves (Omega_T), because it merely permutes its (Q) treatment-cell labels.  It is transitive on (mathcal Q): for (q,rinmathcal Q), the componentwise product (g=rq) satisfies (gq=r).

Fix (iin T) and (q,rinmathcal Q), and choose (ginmathcal G) with (gq=r).  The map (zmapsto g\cdot z) is a bijection from ({zinOmega_T:z_i=q}) to ({zinOmega_T:z_i=r}).  By the asserted invariance of (p_{T,H}),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_{iq}(T,H)
&=\sum_{z\in\Omega_T}p_{T,H}(z)\mathbf 1\{z_i=q\}\\
&=\sum_{z\in\Omega_T}p_{T,H}(g\cdot z)\mathbf 1\{(g\cdot z)_i=r\}\\
&=\pi_{ir}(T,H),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the first and last equalities are the definition of (pi_{iq}(T,H)), the second uses the probability-preserving bijection, and invariance gives (p_{T,H}(g\cdot z)=p_{T,H}(z)).  The events ({Z_i=q}), (qinmathcal Q), partition (Omega_T); hence (sum_{qinmathcal Q}pi_{iq}(T,H)=1).  Since the (Q) summands are equal, (pi_{iq}(T,H)=1/Q>0) for every (i,q).

Define the observed treatment-cell mean
\[
\overline Y_T^{\mathrm{obs}}(q)=\frac{Q}{k}\sum_{i\in T:Z_i=q}Y_{in}(q).
\]
Substitution of (pi_{iq}(T,H)=1/Q) into the definition of (widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(H)) yields, pointwise in (Z_T),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_T(H)
&=\frac1k\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}2^{1-K_f}c_q
       \sum_{i\in T:Z_i=q}\frac{Y_{in}(q)}{1/Q}\\
&=2^{1-K_f}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}c_q\,
       \overline Y_T^{\mathrm{obs}}(q),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is the ordinary balanced factorial difference-in-means vector.

It remains to establish the two-wave assertion.  If (H=0), then (Delta_T(z)^{\mathsf T}HDelta_T(z)=0) for every (zinOmega_T).  Thus all assignments are on the single boundary score.  The definition of the exact lower-tail rule prescribes one common randomized acceptance probability on that boundary; because its uniform average is (alpha), this common value is (a_{T,0}(z)=alpha).  The exact-mixture definition therefore gives
\[
p_{T,0}(z)=\frac1{|\Omega_T|}
\left\{\eta+\frac{1-\eta}{\alpha}\alpha\right\}
=\frac1{|\Omega_T|}.
\]
In particular, both wave assignments are conditionally uniform on their respective balanced supports.

By (minmathcal M_n) and (Qmid n), both (m/Q) and (N/Q=(n-m)/Q) are integers.  Fix a full-sample balanced assignment (zinOmega_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}).  A pilot set (S) is compatible with (z) precisely when it contains (m/Q) of the (n/Q) units in each treatment cell.  Consequently the number of compatible pilot sets is
\[
L=\prod_{qinmathcal Q}{n/Q\choose m/Q},
\]
which does not depend on (z).  For each fixed (S), the cardinalities (|\Omega_S|=m!/(m/Q)!^Q) and (|\Omega_{S^c}|=N!/(N/Q)!^Q) also do not depend on (S).  Since (S_m) is uniform and, when both actions are zero, the conditional wave laws just proved are uniform, the chain rule gives
\[
\Pr(Z_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}=z)
=\frac{L}{{n\choose m}|\Omega_S||\Omega_{S^c}|},
\]
the same value for every (zinOmega_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}).  Assignments outside that support have probability zero, and the displayed probabilities sum to one.  Hence the induced full assignment is exactly uniform on (Omega_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}).

Finally, the already proved exact Horvitz--Thompson accounting theorem permits the two wave contributions to be combined without an omitted cross-wave bias term.  More directly, applying the first part with (H=0) in each wave and using the pooled-HT definition gives the pointwise identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}_{n,m}(0)
&=\frac mn\frac1m\sum_{i\in S_m}2^{1-K_f}c_{Z_i}\,QY_{in}(Z_i)
 +\frac Nn\frac1N\sum_{i\in S_m^c}2^{1-K_f}c_{Z_i}\,QY_{in}(Z_i)\\
&=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n2^{1-K_f}c_{Z_i}\,QY_{in}(Z_i)
=\widehat{\boldsymbol\tau}^{\mathrm{HT}}_{T_n^{\mathrm{full}}}(0).
\end{aligned}
\]
The last expression is exactly the full-sample balanced complete-randomization difference-in-means estimator.  Every argument above is a finite counting or algebraic identity.  Therefore it neither assumes nor implies an asymptotic limit law, as claimed.
