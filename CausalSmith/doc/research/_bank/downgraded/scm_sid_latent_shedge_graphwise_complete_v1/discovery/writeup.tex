\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}sid\_\allowbreak{}latent\_\allowbreak{}shedge\_\allowbreak{}graphwise\_\allowbreak{}complete}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For graphs satisfying \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\), published latent S-ID Algorithm 1 succeeds if and only if the selected causal query is graphwise identifiable over the full positive exact-graph augmented-SCM class, with models compared only within one finite signature. Success returns Abouei et al.'s Eq. 23 functional. A terminal recursive failure contains an ordinary hedge whose edges are actual edges of \(G^S[N_S]\); Shpitser--Pearl's hedge criterion and KivvaEtAl2022's positive finite-state construction give a full-support multistate separator, and a common randomized-response routing plus finite exactification yields two exact-graph models with the same strictly positive selected law and unequal intervention-before-conditioning targets. Separate finite rank bounds certify graph recursion, and the rational checker is sound for any supplied certificate. The result does not construct binary or rational failure witnesses. Universal virtual-edge completeness, the positive-binary two-virtual-edge separator, initial selected-d-separation failure construction, exact-rational inverse lifting, and total success-or-certificate compilation remain open. The UK Biobank component remains only a conditional two-selector feasibility audit.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID proves Algorithm 1 sound and leaves completeness open. Ordinary hedge completeness alone does not solve terminal failures whose projected bidirected edges must be realized through shared or branching ancestors of \(S\), because such realizations can change every selected atom and its normalizer. KivvaEtAl2022 supplies a positive finite-state construction for ordinary identification failures, but not a selected-law inverse lift through those virtual edges.

\paragraph{Niche.} The predicate \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\) isolates the terminal traces for which the nested common-root forests can be chosen using actual directed and bidirected edges of \(G^S[N_S]\). On this class the ordinary positive-forest construction can be used before adding a common selection mechanism and actual-edge root-to-outcome channels.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves the missing converse on \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\). It verifies the Shpitser--Pearl hedge clauses for each terminal native forest, invokes KivvaEtAl2022 for finite full-support ordinary countermodels, extracts a nonzero root-atom contrast, routes it to \(Y_N\) through common full-support randomized-response channels on treatment-avoiding actual paths, and exactifies inert interfaces without changing the equal selected law or target contrast. Combined with published soundness, this gives the success-if-and-only-if theorem and Eq. 23 on success. Independent results bound the graph recursion and verify any supplied rational certificate, without claiming a binary, rational, or executable failure constructor.

\section{Related work}

AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID defines latent subpopulation S-ID, Algorithm 1, S-components, S-Hedges, and Eq. 23, proves soundness, and leaves completeness open. This paper advances that frontier only on \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\): it does not settle unrestricted terminal traces or initial selected-d-separation failure. ShpitserPearl2006 provides Definition 7's ordinary hedge criterion and Theorem 4's nonidentifiability implication. KivvaEtAl2022, not KivvaEtAl2023 Appendix A.1.1, provides the positive finite-state ordinary-ID substrate used here; the new work is the reduction from an actual-edge terminal S-Hedge to that substrate, followed by selected-law extension, actual-edge root-to-outcome routing, and inert exactification. KivvaEtAl2023 concerns conditional generalized identification and does not directly state this ordinary-forest specialization. HuangValtorta2008 and TianPearl2002 provide the ordinary recursive-identification and district-factorization background. AboueiEtAl2024SID is used only for the known no-latent DAG regression check. ChenZoeterMooij2024 explains selection conditioning as an SCM transformation but does not supply a common-selected-law inverse countermodel lift. RichardsonEtAl2017 supplies nested Markov and district-kernel language useful for describing recursive kernels and full-graph extension; it does not prove the selected-law converse. BhattacharyaNabiShpitser2022 studies semiparametric inference after hidden-variable identification and is relevant only to separating this population identification result from downstream estimation; it supplies no completeness claim. LeeGhassamiShpitser2024 uses multiple systematic-selection inputs rather than one full selected law. DuarteEtAl2024AutoBounds addresses optimization-based discrete partial identification, not singleton collapse at S-ID success. The UK Biobank citations motivate a conditional two-selector feasibility audit only; no empirical effect or unconditional fusion claim is made.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:augmented-scm-compatibility]\label{ass:augmented-scm-compatibility}
For the fixed finite signature \(\sigma\), the generic model \(M\) is a recursive augmented SCM whose exogenous variables are mutually independent and whose induced augmented graph is exactly \(G^S\): for every endogenous vertex \(v\), its declared observed-parent set equals \(\operatorname{Pa}_{G^S}(v)\), and for distinct \(v,w\), \(v\leftrightarrow w\) belongs to \(G^S\) if and only if their mechanisms share an exogenous input.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (recursive augmented-SCM compatibility with exact induced graph and shared exogenous variables, \cite{AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selected-cell-positivity]\label{ass:selected-cell-positivity}
Within the fixed signature \(\sigma\), the generic model \(M\) satisfies \(P_M(V=v,S=1)>0\) for every \(v\in\prod_{j\in V}\mathcal X_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (strict selected observational-law positivity, \cite{AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:two-selector-exact-compatibility]\label{ass:two-selector-exact-compatibility}
The generic two-selector model \(M_{EI}\) is recursive, its exogenous variables are mutually independent, both \(S_E\) and \(S_I\) are childless, every declared observed-parent interface equals the corresponding parent set in \(G^{EI}\), and two distinct mechanisms share an exogenous input if and only if their vertices are joined by a bidirected edge in \(G^{EI}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an analyst-imposed two-selector exact-graph feasibility regime for combining exposure and imaging margins. AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID defines the exact induced-graph convention only for one selector, while two childless selectors with a common substantive system are realistic for nonoverlapping exposure and imaging cohorts.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exposure-selector-positivity]\label{ass:exposure-selector-positivity}
The generic two-selector model \(M_{EI}\) satisfies \(P_{M_{EI}}(V_{EI}=v,S_E=1)>0\) for every \(v\in\prod_{j\in V_{EI}}\mathcal X_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an analyst-imposed full-support feasibility condition for the exposure-selected cohort, appropriate after finite coarsening to cells retained in a positivity audit. AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID supplies only the one-selector analogue.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:imaging-selector-positivity]\label{ass:imaging-selector-positivity}
The generic two-selector model \(M_{EI}\) satisfies \(P_{M_{EI}}(V_{EI}=v,S_I=1)>0\) for every \(v\in\prod_{j\in V_{EI}}\mathcal X_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an analyst-imposed full-support feasibility condition for the imaging-selected cohort, appropriate after finite coarsening to cells retained in a positivity audit. AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID supplies only the one-selector analogue.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[lem:target-support]\label{lem:target-support}
For every finite signature \(\sigma\), every \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\), and every \(x\in\prod_{v\in X}\mathcal X_v\), strict selected-cell positivity on \(\prod_{v\in V}\mathcal X_v\) and SCM consistency imply \(P_M(S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))>0\). Hence \(\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M)\) is defined without an additional postintervention support assumption.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\sigma\), \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\), and \(x\in\mathcal X_X\).  Because every substantive state space is nonempty, choose \(z\in\mathcal X_{V\setminus X}\), and put \(v=(x,z)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:selected\text{-}cell\text{-}positivity}\),
\[
  P_M(V=v,S=1)>0. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(U\) denote the complete exogenous input and let
\[
  A_v=\{u:V_M(u)=v,\ S_M(u)=1\}.
\]
Equation (1) says \(P_U(A_v)>0\).  We claim that every \(u\in A_v\) also gives \(S=1\) after \(\operatorname{do}(X=x)\).  Indeed, order the endogenous vertices topologically, which is possible by the recursiveness clause of \(\mathrm{ass:augmented\text{-}scm\text{-}compatibility}\).  At a vertex in \(X\), the intervention assigns the value \(x\), which is its already realized value on \(A_v\).  At a vertex outside \(X\), if all preceding parents retain their observational values, its unchanged structural function, evaluated at the same exogenous input, retains its observational value.  Induction over the topological order therefore leaves every nonintervened endogenous variable, in particular \(S\), unchanged.  Thus
\[
  A_v\subseteq\{u:S_{M,x}(u)=1\}.
\]
The intervention does not alter the law of \(U\), so
\[
  P_M(S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
  =P_U\{S_{M,x}(U)=1\}
  \ge P_U(A_v)>0. \tag{2}
\]
The denominator in the intervention-before-conditioning definition of \(\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M)\) is therefore positive by (2), proving the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This removes a redundant support premise while keeping intervention-before-conditioning mathematically defined. \textit{Gap.} AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID assumes a strictly positive selected observational law but does not isolate this consistency consequence as a separate lemma. \textit{Consumer.} Every graphwise comparison and every finite-binary failure certificate can use the selected intervention target without strengthening the published model class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:exactification-preserves-laws]\label{lem:exactification-preserves-laws}
For every finite binary table encoding \(E\in\operatorname{RawCertSCM}(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\), \(\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(E)\in\operatorname{CertSCM}(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\). Its realized SCM has declared observed-parent set exactly \(\operatorname{Pa}_{G^S}(v)\) at every \(v\), and two distinct mechanisms share an exogenous input if and only if their vertices are joined by a bidirected edge in \(G^S\). Moreover, \(\llbracket E\rrbracket\) and \(\llbracket\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(E)\rrbracket\) induce the same observational law on \(V\cup\{S\}\), the same postintervention law under \(\operatorname{do}(X=x_{\mathrm{bin}})\), and \(\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket E\rrbracket)=\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(E)\rrbracket)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(E\in\operatorname{RawCertSCM}(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\).  Write its original mutually independent latent factors as \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\).  The map \(\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}\) makes two kinds of additions, as specified in \(\mathrm{def:exactification\text{-}map}\).

First, every missing observed parent \(u\in\operatorname{Pa}_{G^S}(v)\) is added to the declared interface of \(v\), but the extended table is constant in that new coordinate.  Second, for every still-unwitnessed edge \(v\leftrightarrow w\), a fresh fair factor \(F_{vw}\) with scope \(\{v,w\}\) is added, and both incident tables are constant in \(F_{vw}\).  No other interface or scope is added.  Since the raw incidence graph is contained in \(G^S\), every old parent and every pair in an old latent scope is allowed by \(G^S\).  Consequently the completed parent interfaces are exactly \(\operatorname{Pa}_{G^S}(v)\), every listed bidirected edge is witnessed, and no unlisted pair shares a factor.  All new factors are independent of one another and of \((U_1,\ldots,U_m)\).  The rational normalization, deterministic-table completeness, and strict selected-cell positivity requirements of \(\mathrm{def:certscm\text{-}encoding}\) are therefore satisfied once law preservation below is established.  Hence the completed string belongs to \(\operatorname{CertSCM}(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\).

For law preservation, fix an old joint latent assignment \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_m)\), a joint assignment \(f\) of all fresh factors, and a topological order of the directed part of \(G^S\).  Induction in that order shows
\[
  (V,S)_{\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(E)}(u,f)=(V,S)_E(u), \tag{3}
\]
because each added coordinate is ignored by the extended structural table.  Therefore, for every event \(A\) in the finite state space,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\llbracket\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(E)\rrbracket}(A)
 &=\sum_u\sum_f
   \left\{\prod_{j=1}^m p_j(u_j)\right\}
   \left\{\prod_{e}2^{-1}\right\}
   \mathbf 1\{(V,S)_E(u)\in A\}\\
 &=\sum_u\left\{\prod_{j=1}^m p_j(u_j)\right\}
   \mathbf 1\{(V,S)_E(u)\in A\}
 =P_{\llbracket E\rrbracket}(A), \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the middle equality uses \(\sum_f\prod_e2^{-1}=1\).  In particular, (4) preserves every selected atom and hence its strict positivity.

Replace the structural tables at vertices in \(X\) by the constants \(x_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  The same topological induction gives the analogue of (3) under this intervention, because the only additional coordinates at nonintervened vertices are still ignored and both models use the same intervention at vertices in \(X\).  Repeating (4) yields equality of the complete postintervention laws.  The raw type has strictly positive selected observational cells.  Applying the same topological consistency argument to any observational cell with \(X=x_{\mathrm{bin}}\) shows directly that its postintervention probability of \(S=1\) is positive; equality of the postintervention laws gives the same conclusion for the exactified encoding.  Division of the equal joint masses by the equal positive normalizer gives
\[
 \Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket E\rrbracket)
 =\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}
   (\llbracket\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(E)\rrbracket).
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The published graph is induced syntactically by declared parent sets and shared exogenous inputs, so every certificate must meet graph equality rather than adjacency containment. \textit{Gap.} AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID defines exact graph induction but does not give a finite-certificate exactification lemma preserving selected observational and interventional laws. \textit{Consumer.} Ordinary hedge and fully observed necessity witnesses can be embedded into the exact published augmented graph without changing their observable equality or target separation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:certscm-ambient-inclusion]\label{lem:certscm-ambient-inclusion}

\]

\begin{lemma}[lem:certificate-checker-soundness]\label{lem:certificate-checker-soundness}
For \(E_0,E_1\in\operatorname{CertSCM}(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\), if \(\mathsf{Check}_{\mathrm{NONID}}(G^S,X,Y,x_{\mathrm{bin}},E_0,E_1)=\mathrm{accept}\), then the realized models lie in \(\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\), satisfy \(P_{\llbracket E_0\rrbracket,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}^S=P_{\llbracket E_1\rrbracket,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}^S>0\) on every binary cell, and satisfy \(\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket E_0\rrbracket)\ne\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket E_1\rrbracket)\). The checker terminates after at most \(c\{|V|^2+L(E_0)+L(E_1)+2^{|V|}(R(E_0)+R(E_1))(|V|+|Y|+1)\}\) exact rational arithmetic and table-lookup operations for a universal implementation constant \(c\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume that the checker accepts \((E_0,E_1)\).  Its initial validation, by \(\mathrm{def:certificate\text{-}checker}\), verifies the complete rational latent tables, their product-factor interpretation, all structural tables, and exact equality of the induced graph with \(G^S\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{lem:certscm\text{-}ambient\text{-}inclusion}\) gives
\[
  \llbracket E_i\rrbracket\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}),
  \qquad i=0,1. \tag{8}
\]
For a binary substantive atom \(v\), let
\[
 a_i(v)=P_{\llbracket E_i\rrbracket}(V=v,S=1),
 \qquad z_i=\sum_v a_i(v).
\]
Acceptance requires \(a_i(v)>0\) for every \(v\).  Hence \(z_i>0\), and it also requires
\[
 a_0(v)z_1=a_1(v)z_0\qquad\text{for every }v. \tag{9}
\]
Dividing (9) by the positive number \(z_0z_1\) yields
\[
 P^S_{\llbracket E_0\rrbracket,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(v)
 =\frac{a_0(v)}{z_0}
 =\frac{a_1(v)}{z_1}
 =P^S_{\llbracket E_1\rrbracket,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(v)>0. \tag{10}
\]
After replacing the tables at \(X\) by \(x_{\mathrm{bin}}\), the checker computes
\[
 b_i(y)=P_{\llbracket E_i\rrbracket}
   (Y=y,S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x_{\mathrm{bin}})),
 \qquad q_i=\sum_y b_i(y).
\]
Acceptance requires \(q_0q_1>0\) and, for some \(y\),
\[
 b_0(y)q_1\ne b_1(y)q_0. \tag{11}
\]
Thus both intervention-before-conditioning distributions are defined, and division of (11) by \(q_0q_1\) gives
\[
 \Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket E_0\rrbracket)(y)
 =\frac{b_0(y)}{q_0}
 \ne\frac{b_1(y)}{q_1}
 =\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}(\llbracket E_1\rrbracket)(y). \tag{12}
\]

It remains to justify the stated operation bound.  Table and normalization validation costs at most a constant multiple of \(L(E_0)+L(E_1)\); checking all vertex pairs costs \(O(|V|^2)\).  For model \(i\), each observational or postintervention pass enumerates \(R(E_i)\) latent assignments, evaluates at most \(|V|+1\) endogenous tables in topological order, and updates an atom table.  Inspecting all substantive atoms costs \(O(2^{|V|})\), and inspecting all outcome atoms costs \(O(2^{|Y|})\le O(2^{|V|})\).  Since \(R(E_i)\ge1\), all these terms are bounded, after increasing one universal implementation constant \(c\), by
\[
 c\left\{|V|^2+L(E_0)+L(E_1)
 +2^{|V|}\bigl(R(E_0)+R(E_1)\bigr)(|V|+|Y|+1)\right\}.
\]
Every loop has a finite explicitly encoded range, so the checker terminates.  Equations (8), (10), and (12) prove its semantic soundness.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The failure branch is reproducible only when finite syntax, exact equality, target separation, and an explicit enumeration bound are certified together. \textit{Gap.} Neither latent S-ID nor ordinary hedge completeness supplies an exact certificate checker for normalized selected-law equality under shared exogenous incidence. \textit{Consumer.} Independent software can validate a returned failure witness without trusting the inverse-lift implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:projection-components]\label{lem:projection-components}
Let \(G^S\) be the exact augmented graph induced by any \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\). For every \(H\subseteq N_S\), the districts of \(\Pi_S(G^S)[H]\) are exactly \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(H)\). More precisely, two vertices of \(H\) are bidirected-connected in \(G^S[H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}]\) if and only if they are bidirected-connected in \(\Pi_S(G^S)[H]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(H\subseteq N_S\).  Suppose first that \(u,v\in H\) are joined by a bidirected path \(\gamma\) in \(G^S[H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}]\).  List, in their order along \(\gamma\), the vertices of \(\gamma\) that lie in \(H\):
\[
  h_0=u,h_1,\ldots,h_r=v.
\]
The internal vertices of the subpath from \(h_{j-1}\) to \(h_j\) lie outside \(H\), and every vertex of \(\gamma\) lies in \(H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}\).  Since \(H\subseteq N_S\) and \(A_S\cap N_S=\varnothing\), those internal vertices lie in \(A_S\cup\{S\}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:suppression\text{-}projection}\), \(h_{j-1}\leftrightarrow h_j\) is therefore an edge of \(\Pi_S(G^S)[H]\).  Hence \(u\) and \(v\) are bidirected-connected in the projection.

Conversely, suppose \(u=h_0,\ldots,h_r=v\) is a bidirected path in \(\Pi_S(G^S)[H]\).  For each projected edge \(h_{j-1}\leftrightarrow h_j\), \(\mathrm{def:suppression\text{-}projection}\) supplies a bidirected path in \(G^S\) whose internal vertices lie in \(A_S\cup\{S\}\).  Concatenating these finitely many paths gives a bidirected walk from \(u\) to \(v\) contained in \(H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}\).  Deleting closed subwalks gives a bidirected path in the same induced graph.  Thus the two bidirected-connectivity relations on \(H\) coincide.  Districts are the equivalence classes of bidirected connectivity, while \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(H)\) is defined in \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}component}\) by intersecting the original districts with \(H\).  The asserted equality of partitions follows.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This establishes that S-components are ordinary districts after the precise suppression operation. \textit{Gap.} AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID defines S-components through ancestors of selection but does not state this projection equivalence. \textit{Consumer.} The ordinary hedge countermodel theorem of ShpitserPearl2006 can be invoked only after this graph-level correspondence is certified.

\begin{lemma}[lem:shedge-projects-to-hedge]\label{lem:shedge-projects-to-hedge}
For every finite signature \(\sigma\), fix \(D_i\in\mathcal D_S(D)\), let \(T_i^{(0)}\) be the unique member of \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(N_S)\) containing \(D_i\), and consider the call chain of \(\operatorname{sID\text{-}Single}(D_i,T_i^{(0)},Q_{P_\sigma}^S[T_i^{(0)}])\). If this chain fails at \((C,T)\), then \(C=D_i\), \(C\subsetneq T\subseteq T_i^{(0)}\), \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(C)=\{C\}\), \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)=\{T\}\), and \(\operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T\), so \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\). Moreover, \(\Pi_S(G^S)[C]\) and \(\Pi_S(G^S)[T]\) are single districts, \(\operatorname{An}_{\Pi_S(G^S)[T]}(C)=T\), and there are nested common-\(R\)-rooted \(C\)-forests \(F_0\subset F_1\) in the projection, with \(R=C\), vertex sets \(C\) and \(T\), and \(C\cap X_N=\varnothing\), \(T\cap X_N\ne\varnothing\), and \(R\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{\Pi_S(G^S)[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N)\). If \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\) also holds, the forests can instead be chosen as subgraphs of \(G^S[N_S]\), so every directed and bidirected forest edge is an actual edge of the original nonancestor graph and \(R\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{G^S[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N)\). These are graph-theoretic conclusions only; they assert no binary, rational, or positive countermodel construction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The target of every call on the chain is fixed at \(C=D_i\) by \(\mathrm{def:success\text{-}or\text{-}certificate\text{-}algorithm}\). At a terminal failure,
\[
 C\subsetneq T\subseteq T_i^{(0)},\qquad
 \operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(C)=\{C\},\qquad
 \operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)=\{T\},\qquad
 \operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T.
\]
These are exactly the clauses of \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\) in \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\).

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:projection\text{-}components}\) with \(H=C\) and \(H=T\). The two singleton S-component identities imply that \(\Pi_S(G^S)[C]\) and \(\Pi_S(G^S)[T]\) each have one district. Projection preserves all and only directed edges with endpoints in \(N_S\), so
\[
 \operatorname{An}_{\Pi_S(G^S)[T]}(C)
 =\operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T.
\]
Choose a bidirected spanning tree \(K_0\) of \(\Pi_S(G^S)[C]\). Starting from \(K_0\), repeatedly attach a vertex outside the current set by the first edge on a bidirected path to that set; this gives a spanning tree \(K_1\) of \(\Pi_S(G^S)[T]\) with \(K_1[C]=K_0\). Put \(R=C\). Give \(F_0\) vertex set \(C\), bidirected tree \(K_0\), and no directed edges. For \(t\in T\setminus C\), define
\[
 d(t)=\min\{\ell:\text{a directed path of length }\ell
 \text{ runs from }t\text{ to }C\text{ in }G^S[T]\}.
\]
The ancestral equality makes \(d(t)\) finite. Choose a next vertex \(c(t)\) on a shortest path and put \(t\to c(t)\) in \(F_1\). Since \(d(c(t))=d(t)-1\), there is no directed cycle; vertices outside \(C\) have one child and vertices in \(C\) have none. With bidirected tree \(K_1\), \(F_1\) is an \(R\)-rooted \(C\)-forest and \(F_1[C]=F_0\).

From \(D=\operatorname{An}_{G^S[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N)\) and \(C=D_i\subseteq D\),
\[
 C\cap X_N=\varnothing,\qquad
 R=C\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{G^S[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N)
 =\operatorname{An}_{\Pi_S(G^S)[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N).
\]
If \(T\cap X_N=\varnothing\), every \(t\in T\) reaches \(C\), and every vertex of \(C\) reaches \(Y_N\), inside \(N_S\setminus X_N\). Thus \(T\subseteq D\). Since \(T\) is one S-component containing the S-component \(D_i=C\) of \(D\), maximality of \(D_i\) gives \(T\subseteq C\), contrary to \(C\subsetneq T\). Hence \(T\cap X_N\ne\varnothing\).

If \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\) holds, its terminal-trace clause supplies forests with the same vertex, root, and incidence properties whose edges all belong to \(G^S[N_S]\). This proves the native conclusion. No step constructs a causal model.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proves terminal S-Hedge geometry and projected/native forest extraction only. \textit{Gap.} Countermodels require separate positive finite constructions. \textit{Consumer.} Both the native separator and universal inverse-lift OEQ use the forest skeleton.

\begin{lemma}[lem:local-contrast-survives-eq23]\label{lem:local-contrast-survives-eq23}
Fix an actual terminal call for \(D_i=C\) and two candidate full-graph extensions that use kernels \(q_C^{(0)}\) and \(q_C^{(1)}\) in the \(C\)-position, the same kernels \(q_j\) for every \(D_j\ne C\), and the same outer selected conditional \(p^S\). Their pointwise Eq. 23 product contrast is
\[
 \Delta(y)=\sum_{w,d_{D\setminus Y_N}}
 p^S(y_S,w\mid x_S)
 \bigl(q_C^{(0)}-q_C^{(1)}\bigr)
 \prod_{D_j\ne C}q_j.
\]
The two constructed full targets differ if and only if \(\Delta(y)\ne0\) for at least one outcome value \(y\). Strict positivity of \(p^S\) and of the common factors, together with \(q_C^{(0)}\ne q_C^{(1)}\), does not by itself imply this nonvanishing condition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(r\in\{0,1\}\), substitute \(q_C^{(r)}\), the common \(q_j\) for \(D_j\ne C\), and the common \(p^S\) into \(\mathrm{def:eq23\text{-}functional}\). Finiteness permits termwise subtraction, and distributivity gives
\[
 \Phi^{(0)}(y)-\Phi^{(1)}(y)
 =\sum_{w,d_{D\setminus Y_N}}
 p^S(y_S,w\mid x_S)
 \bigl(q_C^{(0)}-q_C^{(1)}\bigr)
 \prod_{D_j\ne C}q_j
 =\Delta(y).
\]
Two measures on finite \(Y\) are unequal exactly when one point mass is unequal, proving the equivalence.

Positivity does not make this contraction injective. For binary \((A,R)\), take
\[
 q_C^{(0)}(0,0)=q_C^{(0)}(1,1)=\frac38,\quad
 q_C^{(0)}(1,0)=q_C^{(0)}(0,1)=\frac18,\quad
 q_C^{(1)}(a,r)=\frac14.
\]
Both kernels are positive and unequal, but for each \(r\),
\[
 \sum_a\bigl(q_C^{(0)}(a,r)-q_C^{(1)}(a,r)\bigr)=0.
\]
If \(A\subseteq D\setminus Y_N\) is marginalized and all common factors are independent of \(A\), the contraction contains this zero sum, so \(\Delta(y)=0\) for every \(y\). This is the claimed obstruction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gives the exact contraction test and an explicit positive cancellation witness. \textit{Gap.} Local inequality alone does not imply target inequality. \textit{Consumer.} Any future universal extension must certify a nonzero outcome contrast.

\begin{proposition}[prop:latent-free-regression]\label{prop:latent-free-regression}
Known-case regression corollary. If \(G^S\) has no bidirected edges and \(S\) has no children, then for every \(H\subseteq N_S\), \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(H)\) is the singleton partition of \(H\), and no recursive S-Hedge exists. Consequently the recursive S-Hedge failure branch of \(\mathsf{sID}^{\pm}\) cannot occur. The latent-paper criterion reduces to its initial selected d-separation condition, which the published paper states is consistent with the fully observed S-ID criterion. Whenever the latent Algorithm 1 succeeds, its returned \(\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}\) identifies \(\Theta_{x,\sigma}\) by published soundness. This corollary does not assert byte-for-byte equality of the two papers' displayed formulas.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume that \(G^S\) has no bidirected edges and that \(S\) has no children.  Fix \(H\subseteq N_S\), and write
\[
  J_H=G^S[H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}].
\]
Taking an induced subgraph cannot create an edge, so \(J_H\) has no bidirected edges.  A district is a maximal set joined by bidirected paths.  A bidirected path between two distinct vertices contains a bidirected edge; hence every district of \(J_H\) is a singleton.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}component}\) therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(H)
 &=\bigl\{K\cap H:K\in\mathcal D(J_H),\ K\cap H\ne\varnothing\bigr\}\\
 &=\bigl\{\{h\}:h\in H\bigr\}. \tag{1}
\end{aligned}
\]
When \(H=\varnothing\), both sides of (1) are the empty partition, so (1) covers every \(H\subseteq N_S\).

We next exclude an S-Hedge directly from the definitions.  Suppose for contradiction that \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\) holds.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\),
\[
 C\subsetneq T\subseteq N_S,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)=\{T\}. \tag{2}
\]
The declared type of \(C\) makes \(C\) nonempty, so choose \(t\in C\).  Since \(t\in T\), (1) applied with \(H=T\) says that \(\{t\}\) is a block of \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)\).  Combining this fact with (2) yields \(\{t\}=T\).  The inclusions \(\varnothing\ne C\subseteq T=\{t\}\) then force \(C=T\), contradicting \(C\subsetneq T\).  Thus
\[
  \neg\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)
  \quad\text{for every admissible pair }(C,T). \tag{3}
\]

Definition \(\mathrm{def:success\text{-}or\text{-}certificate\text{-}algorithm}\) states that a terminal recursive failure supplies sets \(C\subsetneq T\subseteq N_S\) such that both are single S-components and \(\operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\), those conditions are exactly \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\).  This contradicts (3), so the recursive S-Hedge failure branch of \(\mathsf{sID}^{\pm}\) cannot occur.  This argument proves the singleton-component and no-failure conclusions internally; it does not borrow them from the citation.

For the remaining graphical comparison, \(\mathrm{lem:published\text{-}dag\text{-}sid\text{-}consistency}\) records the precise DAG specialization of AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID Theorem 5.1 and the paragraph immediately following Remark 5.2: the auxiliary effect is always s-ID, while the original selected causal effect is s-ID exactly when the paper's Equation (5), its initial selected d-separation condition, holds; the authors call this consistent with AboueiEtAl2024SID Theorem 2.  This is a comparison of graphical criteria and does not assert literal equality of the two papers' displayed formulas.

It remains to verify the identifying claim as an equality between the observed-law functional and the causal target.  Fix a finite signature \(\sigma\), an intervention value \(x\in\mathcal X_X\), and a model \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:target\text{-}support}\),
\[
  P_M\bigl(S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x)\bigr)>0, \tag{4}
\]
so the intervention-before-conditioning law \(\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M)\) is defined.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\),
\[
  P_M(V=v,S=1)>0
  \quad\text{for every }v\in\prod_{j\in V}\mathcal X_j. \tag{5}
\]
Summing (5) over the nonempty finite state space of \(V\) gives \(P_M(S=1)>0\), and hence
\[
  P_{M,\sigma}^S(v)
  =\frac{P_M(V=v,S=1)}{P_M(S=1)}>0. \tag{6}
\]
More generally, for any \(Z\subseteq V\) and \(z\in\mathcal X_Z\), finiteness of the signature and (6) give
\[
  P_{M,\sigma}^S(Z=z)
  =\sum_{v\in\mathcal X_V:\,v|_Z=z}P_{M,\sigma}^S(v)>0, \tag{7}
\]

\begin{lemma}[lem:independent-smoothing-kills-early-contrast]\label{lem:independent-smoothing-kills-early-contrast}
In the following legal raw response family for the early-kill graph, independent smoothing cannot preserve a target contrast. Both models set \(B=U\) and \(D=B\oplus E\). Model zero sets \(Z_2=U\oplus W\), model one sets \(Z_2=W\), and model \(j\) uses a Bernoulli outcome kernel \(P_j(Y=1\mid D=d,W=w)=\kappa_j(d,w)\). If their selected observational laws agree, then for each \(d\), \(\kappa_0(d,0)=\kappa_0(d,1)=\kappa_1(d,0)=\kappa_1(d,1)\); consequently their selected targets under \(\operatorname{do}(B=0,D=d)\) are equal. Coordinate-constant exactification cannot change this conclusion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because \(U\) and \(W\) are fair and independent, \((B,Z_2)\) is uniform in both models. The variable \(Z_1\), the conditional law of \(D\) given \(B\), and the positive selection kernel are also common. In particular every stratum \((b,d,z_1,z_2,S=1)\) has positive probability because \(0<P(E=1)<1\) and the selection probabilities are positive. Equality of the selected laws therefore implies equality of the conditional probabilities of \(Y=1\) in each such stratum.

In model zero, \(B=b\) and \(Z_2=z\) determine \(W=b\oplus z\); in model one, \(Z_2=z\) determines \(W=z\). Thus, for every \(b,d,z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \kappa_0(d,b\oplus z)=\kappa_1(d,z).
\]
Taking \(b=0\) gives \(\kappa_0(d,z)=\kappa_1(d,z)\), while taking \(b=1\) gives \(\kappa_0(d,1\oplus z)=\kappa_1(d,z)\). Hence all four values at fixed \(d\) are one number, say \(\kappa_d\).

After \(\operatorname{do}(B=0,D=d)\), selection may change the posterior law of \(W\), but the outcome probability in either model is the constant \(\kappa_d\) for both values of \(W\). Since the selection denominator is positive,
\[
 P_j(Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(B=0,D=d),S=1)=\kappa_d,
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The targets are equal. The law-preservation conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:exactification\text{-}preserves\text{-}laws}\) shows that adding inactive declared coordinates cannot restore a contrast.
\end{proof}

\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This revalidated lemma extracts a named root-atom contrast from the repaired ordinary positive-forest carrier on an actual terminal trace. \textit{Gap.} Its state spaces are finite but multistate, and it makes no rational or effective-encoding claim. \textit{Consumer.} The selected extension carries this root contrast to the original outcome along actual treatment-avoiding paths.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-exactification-preserves-laws]\label{lem:finite-exactification-preserves-laws}
For every finite-signature recursive augmented SCM \(\widetilde M\) whose declared parent interfaces and shared-exogenous incidences induce a subgraph of \(G^S\), \(\operatorname{Exactify}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{G^S}(\widetilde M)\) induces exactly \(G^S\). It has the same observational law and the same postintervention law under every intervention on observed vertices. In particular, strict selected-cell positivity and every intervention-before-conditioning target with positive selection denominator are preserved.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order the endogenous vertices topologically. Fix every original and fresh exogenous assignment. At the first vertex, the completed function ignores all new parent and latent coordinates, so its output equals the old output. If equality holds at all earlier vertices, coordinate-constancy gives equality at the next vertex. Induction proves pointwise equality of the full endogenous vector.

The fresh factors are independent and normalized, so summing them multiplies every original atom by
\[
 \prod_{U\ \mathrm{fresh}}\sum_uP(U=u)=1.
\]
The observational laws coincide. Under an intervention, the same equations are replaced by the same constants, and the induction applies to the remaining equations; all postintervention laws coincide. Thus selected atoms, selection denominators, and defined conditional targets are preserved.

Every missing directed edge is now in a declared parent interface and every missing bidirected edge has a fresh shared factor. No unlisted incidence was added. The completed induced graph is exactly \(G^S\), and equality of laws preserves positivity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This restores unused exact-graph incidences for finite multistate models without changing laws. \textit{Gap.} It is a representation lemma, not a separator. \textit{Consumer.} The native extension applies it after constructing the contrast.

\begin{lemma}[lem:native-forest-trace-subclass]\label{lem:native-forest-trace-subclass}
If \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\) holds, then \(G^S\) is an admissible finite childless-selector augmented ADMG for the published latent S-ID problem. For every finite signature \(\sigma\), the class \(\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\) on this restricted trace is exactly the exact-induced-graph positive compatibility class of \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\). Hence native-forest traces form a subclass of the published graph instances, without restricting the SCM class on a fixed member graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Clause (i) of \(\mathrm{def:native\text{-}forest\text{-}trace}\) gives finiteness, acyclicity, childlessness of \(S\), and the query's disjointness and nonemptiness. These are the published graph and query premises. The definition's final sentence leaves compatibility unchanged. Unfolding \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), every \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\) has exactly the graph parent interfaces, exactly the graph shared-exogenous incidences, mutually independent exogenous variables, and positive selected cells. Thus the native condition restricts only graph traces and selects a subclass of published instances. No strict-inclusion claim is made.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proves that native traces remain inside the published graph setting. \textit{Gap.} No strict-inclusion claim is made. \textit{Consumer.} The native headline can reuse published soundness without changing the model class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:native-forest-selected-extension]\label{lem:native-forest-selected-extension}
Assume \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\), and let \((C,T)\) be an actual terminal Algorithm 1 failure. Then there exist a finite multistate signature \(\widetilde\sigma\), an intervention value \(x^\dagger\in\mathcal X_X\), two models \(M_0,M_1\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\widetilde\sigma)\), and an event \(A_Y\subseteq\mathcal X_Y\) such that
\[
 P_{M_0,\widetilde\sigma}^S(v)=P_{M_1,\widetilde\sigma}^S(v)>0
 \quad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal X_V,
\]
but
\[
 P_{M_0}(A_Y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x^\dagger),S=1)
 \ne P_{M_1}(A_Y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x^\dagger),S=1).
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{Ident}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S(G^S;X,Y)\) is false. The construction is finite and positive but is not claimed binary, rational, or executable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use \(\mathrm{lem:positive\text{-}multistate\text{-}native\text{-}forest\text{-}separator}\) and put
\[
 h(a)=P_{\overline M_0}(R=a\mid\operatorname{do}(X_N\cap T=0))
 -P_{\overline M_1}(R=a\mid\operatorname{do}(X_N\cap T=0)),
\]
with \(\delta=h(r^\dagger)\ne0\). For each \(r\in R\), native incidence supplies a path
\[
 \gamma_r:r=v_{r,0}\to\cdots\to v_{r,\ell_r}=y(r),
 \qquad y(r)\in Y_N,
\]
inside \(G^S[N_S\setminus X_N]\). Paths may share vertices or branches.

Augment each path vertex by one signal coordinate for every path through it. The \(r\)-signal has alphabet \(\mathcal X_r\). At its source and each later path vertex use the same randomized-response channel
\[
 K_{\epsilon,r}(z\mid u)
 =(1-\epsilon)\mathbf 1\{z=u\}+\frac{\epsilon}{|\mathcal X_r|},
 \qquad 0<\epsilon<1.
\]
Use independent finite private noises, and product channels at shared vertices. Retain the Kivva base coordinates on \(T\), and give all other base coordinates independent full-support laws. Conditional on the base vector on \(T\), all new coordinates have one common strictly positive conditional kernel. Equality and positivity of the base laws therefore imply
\[
 P_0(V=v)=P_1(V=v)>0
\]
for every augmented state \(v\).

Give all vertices in \(A_S\) identical independent full-support mechanisms, and make \(S\) an independent fair bit while ignoring its declared parents. Then
\[
 P_j(V=v,S=1)=\frac12P_j(V=v)>0,\qquad
 P_j(V=v\mid S=1)=P_j(V=v),
\]
so the selected laws agree.

Let \(L=\sum_{r\in R}(\ell_r+1)\). Couple every channel as exact copying with probability \(1-\epsilon\) and uniform redrawing with probability \(\epsilon\). All terminal signals equal their root inputs with probability at least \((1-\epsilon)^L\). If \(A_Y\) is the event that the terminal signal tuple equals \(r^\dagger\), then for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 \left|P_j(A_Y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x^\dagger))
 -P_{\overline M_j}(R=r^\dagger\mid\operatorname{do}(X_N\cap T=0))\right|
 \le1-(1-\epsilon)^L.
\]
Here \(x^\dagger\) uses Kivva's zero levels on \(X_N\cap T\) and fixed values elsewhere. The paths avoid \(X_N\), so intervention cuts no signal channel. Choose \(\epsilon\) with
\[
 2\{1-(1-\epsilon)^L\}<|\delta|.
\]
The triangle inequality yields
\[
 |P_0(A_Y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x^\dagger))
 -P_1(A_Y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x^\dagger))|
 \ge|\delta|-2\{1-(1-\epsilon)^L\}>0.
\]
Independence of \(S\) persists after intervention, so conditioning on \(S=1\) changes neither target.

All used forest and routing edges are actual edges of \(G^S\); the premodels induce subgraphs of \(G^S\). Apply \(\operatorname{Exactify}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{G^S}\). By \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}exactification\text{-}preserves\text{-}laws}\), the completed models induce exactly \(G^S\) and preserve the equal positive selected law and target contrast. They lie in \(\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\widetilde\sigma)\). This one finite-signature pair falsifies \(\mathrm{def:graphwise\text{-}sid}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This constructs equal positive selected laws and a nonzero original-outcome event contrast. \textit{Gap.} Its channel construction requires native actual edges. \textit{Consumer.} It proves the failure direction of the native headline.

\begin{theorem}[thm:native-forest-graphwise-sid-completeness]\label{thm:native-forest-graphwise-sid-completeness}
Let \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\) hold. Then published Algorithm 1 succeeds on \((G^S,X,Y)\) if and only if \(\operatorname{Ident}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S(G^S;X,Y)\) holds. On success, for every finite signature \(\sigma\), every \(x\in\prod_{v\in X}\mathcal X_v\), and every \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\),
\[
 \Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(P_{M,\sigma}^S)=\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M),
\]
with the outer conditional evaluated at \(x_S\) and every recursive kernel at \(x_N\). On terminal failure, \(\mathrm{lem:native\text{-}forest\text{-}selected\text{-}extension}\) supplies one finite multistate signature and two exact-graph models with one common strictly positive selected law and unequal intervention-before-conditioning targets. No binary, rational, or executable failure certificate is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume throughout that \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\) holds.  By \(\mathrm{lem:native\text{-}forest\text{-}trace\text{-}subclass}\), \(G^S\) is an admissible finite childless-selector augmented ADMG for published Algorithm~1, and, for each finite signature \(\sigma\), its compatible models are exactly the members of \(\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\).

First suppose that Algorithm~1 succeeds.  Fix a finite signature \(\sigma\), an intervention value \(x\in\prod_{v\in X}\mathcal X_v\), and a model \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:selected\text{-}cell\text{-}positivity}\),
\[
 P_M(V=v,S=1)>0
 \qquad\text{for every }v\in\prod_{j\in V}\mathcal X_j.
\]
Consequently, every marginal cell of the selected law that occurs as a denominator in the outer conditional or in a recursively extracted selected kernel is a finite sum of positive cells and is therefore positive.  In particular,
\[
 P_M(X_S=x_S,S=1)
 =\sum_{v:\,v|_{X_S}=x_S}P_M(V=v,S=1)>0,
\]
so the outer conditional at \(X_S=x_S\) is defined.  The same summation argument applies to every conditioning configuration used by the successful recursive factors evaluated at \(X_N=x_N\).  Moreover, \(\mathrm{lem:target\text{-}support}\) gives
\[
 P_M(S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))>0,
\]
so the intervention-before-conditioning target is defined.  The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:published\text{-}latent\text{-}sid\text{-}soundness}\) therefore apply, and, pointwise for every \(y\in\prod_{v\in Y}\mathcal X_v\),
\[
 \Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(P_{M,\sigma}^S)(y)
 =\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M)(y).
\]
This is precisely the displayed success conclusion, with the evaluation conventions fixed by \(\mathrm{def:eq23\text{-}functional}\).

To deduce graphwise identification, take arbitrary \(M_0,M_1\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\) satisfying
\[
 P_{M_0,\sigma}^S=P_{M_1,\sigma}^S.
\]
Applying the preceding equality first to \(M_0\) and then to \(M_1\) yields
\[
 \Theta_{x,\sigma}(M_0)
 =\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(P_{M_0,\sigma}^S)
 =\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(P_{M_1,\sigma}^S)
 =\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M_1).
\]
Because \(\sigma\), \(x\), \(M_0\), and \(M_1\) were arbitrary within one common signature, \(\mathrm{def:graphwise\text{-}sid}\) implies \(\operatorname{Ident}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S(G^S;X,Y)\).

Conversely, suppose that Algorithm~1 does not succeed.  Clause~(ii) of \(\mathrm{def:native\text{-}forest\text{-}trace}\) says that its initial selected d-separation check passes.  Hence nonsuccess occurs, if at all, in a single-component recursive chain.  By \(\mathrm{lem:sid\text{-}graph\text{-}recursion\text{-}termination}\), the chain rooted at each \(T_i^{(0)}\) has at most \(|T_i^{(0)}|\) invocations, every chain has depth at most \(|N_S|\), and the total number of single-component invocations is at most
\[
 B_0=\sum_{(D_i,T_i^{(0)})\in\mathcal I_0}|T_i^{(0)}|<\infty.
\]
Therefore a finite execution that does not return the success branch reaches an actual terminal failed call \((C,T)\).  The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:native\text{-}forest\text{-}selected\text{-}extension}\) now hold.  That lemma supplies one finite multistate signature \(\widetilde\sigma\), one value \(x^\dagger\in\mathcal X_X\), models
\[
 M_0,M_1\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\widetilde\sigma),
\]
and an event \(A_Y\subseteq\mathcal X_Y\) such that, pointwise on \(\mathcal X_V\),
\[
 P_{M_0,\widetilde\sigma}^S(v)
 =P_{M_1,\widetilde\sigma}^S(v)>0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \Theta_{x^\dagger,\widetilde\sigma}(M_0)(A_Y)
 \ne
 \Theta_{x^\dagger,\widetilde\sigma}(M_1)(A_Y).
\]
The latter inequality implies that the two finite probability distributions \(\Theta_{x^\dagger,\widetilde\sigma}(M_0)\) and \(\Theta_{x^\dagger,\widetilde\sigma}(M_1)\) are unequal, while the preceding pointwise equality says that their selected observational laws coincide.  Thus this single within-\(\widetilde\sigma\) pair violates the implication quantified in \(\mathrm{def:graphwise\text{-}sid}\), and
\[
 \neg\operatorname{Ident}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S(G^S;X,Y).
\]
We have proved that success implies identification and that failure implies nonidentification; equivalently, Algorithm~1 succeeds if and only if \(\operatorname{Ident}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S(G^S;X,Y)\) holds.  The converse used the terminal selected extension only under \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\); it makes no assertion about virtual-edge traces, initial-check failures, binary or rational witnesses, or an executable failure compiler.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The advance over AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID is a converse only on \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\); published soundness supplies the success direction, while the note's selected-law extension supplies the failure direction. \textit{Gap.} KivvaEtAl2022 supplies only the ordinary positive-forest substrate. Virtual-edge lifting, initial-check failure, binary and rational construction, and total certificate compilation remain open. \textit{Consumer.} On native traces, graphical analysts obtain the exact success criterion and Eq. 23 on success, together with an existential finite multistate exact-graph counterpair on terminal failure.

\section{Interpretation}

The native theorem marks the boundary at which terminal latent S-ID failure already contains an ordinary hedge inside the selection-nonancestor graph. At that boundary, an ordinary positive separator can be passed through a common independent selector and sufficiently accurate full-support channels without canceling its root contrast. When a terminal forest uses virtual edges through ancestors of \(S\), this argument no longer controls the selected-law normalizer, so the universal converse remains unresolved.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The proof requires actual-edge nested forests on every reachable terminal failure. It does not realize projected virtual bidirected edges through shared or branching ancestors of \(S\). Accordingly, unrestricted graphwise completeness, the positive-binary two-virtual-edge separation problem, the initial selected-d-separation failure constructor, exact-rational inverse lifting, and total success-or-certificate compilation all remain open. The proved Kivva reduction is finite and full-support but multistate; no binary reduction, rational parameter choice, or executable constructor follows from it. The rational checker theorem is only a sound verification result conditional on a certificate being supplied.

\section{Honest scope}

The headline theorem is an if-and-only-if result only for finite childless-selector queries satisfying \(\operatorname{NativeForestTrace}(G^S;X,Y)\). The compatible class is the full positive recursive augmented-SCM class whose declared observed-parent interfaces and shared-exogenous incidences induce exactly \(G^S\), and observational equivalence compares models only within one finite signature. On success, Eq. 23 is evaluated at \(X_S=x_S\) in the outer conditional and at \(X_N=x_N\) in every recursive selected kernel. On a terminal native failure, the proof supplies one finite multistate exact-graph pair with a common strictly positive selected law and unequal intervention-before-conditioning targets. It does not supply a binary or rational pair. Coordinate-constant exactification preserves the relevant laws under the declared-interface graph convention. Graph-recursion bounds and sound finite checking of a supplied rational certificate are separate proved properties; they do not make the failure branch executable. Universal virtual-edge completeness, the positive-binary two-virtual-edge separator, initial-check failure construction, exact-rational inverse lifting, and total compilation remain open. The no-bidirected-edge result is only a published-case regression check. The UK Biobank component is conditional on a specified two-selector graph and a nonempty feasibility fiber; absent two exact-graph models in that same fiber with unequal targets, it reports only unmet assumptions or an inconclusive audit, not data-compatible nonidentification.
