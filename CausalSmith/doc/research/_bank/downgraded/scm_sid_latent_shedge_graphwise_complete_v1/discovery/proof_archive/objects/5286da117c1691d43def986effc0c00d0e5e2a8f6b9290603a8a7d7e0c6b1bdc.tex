The first equivalence is exactly \(\mathrm{lem:local\text{-}contrast\text{-}survives\text{-}eq23}\), applied to the two stipulated target factorizations: pointwise subtraction yields
\[
 \Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}
   (\llbracket\widetilde E_0\rrbracket)(y)
 -\Theta_{x_{\mathrm{bin}},\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}
   (\llbracket\widetilde E_1\rrbracket)(y)
 =\Delta(y).
\]
Because \(Y\) has a finite state space, the target distributions are unequal if and only if the right-hand side is nonzero for some \(y\).

Now assume that \(\Delta(y_0)\ne0\) for some \(y_0\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:exactification\text{-}preserves\text{-}laws}\), for each \(r\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 E_r=\operatorname{Exactify}_{G^S}(\widetilde E_r)
 \in\operatorname{CertSCM}(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}),
\]
and exactification changes neither the observational law nor the postintervention law.  Hence the exactified models still have the stipulated equal strictly positive selected law, and their target masses at \(y_0\) still differ by \(\Delta(y_0)\).  Membership in \(\operatorname{CertSCM}\) supplies complete normalized rational tables and exact parent and latent-incidence validation by \(\mathrm{def:certscm\text{-}encoding}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:certscm\text{-}ambient\text{-}inclusion}\), the two realizations belong to \(\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}})\); hence \(\mathrm{lem:target\text{-}support}\) implies that their postintervention selection probabilities are positive.  Equality of the normalized selected laws is equivalent to the cross-multiplication identities \(a_0(v)z_1=a_1(v)z_0\) in \(\mathrm{def:certificate\text{-}checker}\), while inequality of the target mass at \(y_0\), together with positive postintervention normalizers, is equivalent to
\[
 b_0(y_0)q_1\ne b_1(y_0)q_0.
\]
Every acceptance condition in \(\mathrm{def:certificate\text{-}checker}\) is therefore satisfied, so the checker accepts.

Conversely, if \(\Delta(y)=0\) for every \(y\), the first equivalence gives equality of the two target distributions.  Exactification preserves those distributions, so for every \(y\) the checker has \(b_0(y)q_1=b_1(y)q_0\).  Its final target-separation test therefore rejects.  The strictly positive unequal-kernel example proved in \(\mathrm{lem:local\text{-}contrast\text{-}survives\text{-}eq23}\) shows that this zero-contraction case can occur despite \(q_C^{(0)}\ne q_C^{(1)}\), establishing the final assertion.
