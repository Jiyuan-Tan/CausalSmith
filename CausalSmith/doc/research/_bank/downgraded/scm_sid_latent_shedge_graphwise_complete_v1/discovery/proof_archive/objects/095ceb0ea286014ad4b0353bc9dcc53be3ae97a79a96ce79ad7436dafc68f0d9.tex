Let \(\oplus\) denote addition in \(\mathbb F_2\).  The strongest exact construction currently available is a finite rational boundary pair.  Let \(U,W,A,C,K\) be mutually independent fair bits.  Give \(U,W,A,C\) the respective latent scopes \(\{B,Z_2\}\), \(\{Z_2,Y\}\), \(\{D,Z_1\}\), and \(\{Z_1,B\}\), and set \(Z_1=K\), with the equations at \(B,D,Z_1\) constant in any displayed scoped input they do not otherwise mention.  Let \(R\) be independent and uniform on \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), and define the childless selector by
\[
 S=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(Z_2=1\bigr)\ \text{or}\ \bigl(Z_2=0\text{ and }R\in\{0,1,2\}\bigr).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 P(S=1\mid Z_1=z_1,Z_2=0)=\frac35,
 \qquad
 P(S=1\mid Z_1=z_1,Z_2=1)=1.
\]
In model zero set
\[
 B=U,\qquad D=B,\qquad Z_2=U\oplus W,\qquad Y=W,
\]
and in model one set
\[
 B=U,\qquad D=B,\qquad Z_2=W,\qquad Y=D\oplus W.
\]
The variables \((B,Z_2)\) are uniform on \(\{0,1\}^2\) in both models: in model zero the map \((U,W)\mapsto(U,U\oplus W)\) is bijective, and in model one it is the identity.  In both models every realized observational cell satisfies
\[
 D=B,\qquad Y=B\oplus Z_2.
\]
Conversely, each triple \((b,z_1,z_2)\in\{0,1\}^3\) determines one such realized cell and has probability \(1/8\) before selection.  Writing \(s(0)=3/5\) and \(s(1)=1\), the common unnormalized selected law is therefore
\[
 P(B=b,D=d,Y=y,Z_1=z_1,Z_2=z_2,S=1)
 =\frac18s(z_2)\,\mathbf 1\{d=b,\ y=b\oplus z_2\}.
\]
Its normalizing constant is
\[
 P(S=1)=\frac12\left(\frac35+1\right)=\frac45.
\]
Thus the two selected observational laws agree exactly; their positive atoms on the common support equal \(3/32\) when \(z_2=0\) and \(5/32\) when \(z_2=1\).

Take \(x_\star=(0,0)\).  Intervention is applied before conditioning.  In model zero, after \(\operatorname{do}(B=0,D=0)\), the bit \(Z_2=U\oplus W\) is fair and independent of \(Y=W\).  Hence \(Y\) is independent of \(S\), so
\[
 Q_{\star,x_\star}(M_{\star,0})(Y=1)=\frac12.
\]
In model one, after the same intervention, \(Z_2=W=Y\).  The selection denominator is positive and direct normalization gives
\[
 Q_{\star,x_\star}(M_{\star,1})(Y=1)
 =\frac{P(W=1)s(1)}{P(W=0)s(0)+P(W=1)s(1)}
 =\frac{(1/2)\cdot1}{(1/2)\cdot(3/5)+(1/2)\cdot1}
 =\frac58.
\]
Declare the observed-parent interfaces of \(D,Y,S\) to be \(\{B\},\{D\},\{Z_1,Z_2\}\), respectively, and the other observed-parent interfaces to be empty.  The four displayed pair scopes then induce exactly the bidirected chain of \(G_\star^S\), while the interfaces induce exactly its directed edges.  All unused coordinates are ignored by deterministic tables, so the preceding law calculations apply without change.  Nevertheless, the displayed indicators show that cells with \(d\ne b\) or \(y\ne b\oplus z_2\) have zero selected mass.  Thus the pair satisfies exact graph compatibility and has rational finite tables, but it is not in \(\operatorname{CertSCM}(G_\star^S,\sigma_{\mathrm{bin},\star})\) because that type includes strict selected-cell positivity; correspondingly, \(\mathsf{Check}_{\mathrm{NONID}}\) rejects it at the positivity check.  The unresolved step is therefore exactly a positivity-preserving deformation or a different finite rational construction, not the intervention algebra or exact-graph realization.
