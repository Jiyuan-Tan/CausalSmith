Assume that \(G^S\) has no bidirected edges and that \(S\) has no children, as in the proposition.  Fix \(H\subseteq N_S\).  The induced graph \(G^S[H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}]\) also has no bidirected edges.  A district is a maximal set connected by bidirected paths; hence every district of this induced graph is a singleton.  Substitution into \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}component}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(H)
&=\bigl\{K\cap H:K\in\mathcal D(G^S[H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}]),\ K\cap H\ne\varnothing\bigr\}\\
&=\bigl\{\{h\}:h\in H\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the S-component partition of every \(H\subseteq N_S\) is the singleton partition (with the displayed family empty when \(H=\varnothing\)).

We next rule out an S-Hedge.  Suppose, for contradiction, that \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\) holds.  By \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\),
\[
C\subsetneq T\subseteq N_S,
\qquad
\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)=\{T\}.
\]
The first relation and the stipulated nonemptiness of \(C\) imply \(T\ne\varnothing\).  Applying the preceding singleton-partition identity with \(H=T\) yields
\[
\{T\}=\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)
=\bigl\{\{t\}:t\in T\bigr\}.
\]
Choose \(t\in T\).  Equality of these two families implies \(T=\{t\}\).  A singleton has no nonempty proper subset, contradicting \(\varnothing\ne C\subsetneq T\).  Therefore no recursive S-Hedge exists.  Since the recursive S-Hedge failure branch is precisely the branch certified by such a pair \((C,T)\), that branch of \(\mathsf{sID}^{\pm}\) cannot occur.
