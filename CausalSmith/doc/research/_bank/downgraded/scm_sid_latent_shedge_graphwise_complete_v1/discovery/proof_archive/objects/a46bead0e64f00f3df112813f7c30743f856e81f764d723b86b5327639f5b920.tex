For \(r\in\{0,1\}\), substitute \(q_C^{(r)}\), the common \(q_j\) for \(D_j\ne C\), and the common \(p^S\) into \(\mathrm{def:eq23\text{-}functional}\). Finiteness permits termwise subtraction, and distributivity gives
\[
 \Phi^{(0)}(y)-\Phi^{(1)}(y)
 =\sum_{w,d_{D\setminus Y_N}}
 p^S(y_S,w\mid x_S)
 \bigl(q_C^{(0)}-q_C^{(1)}\bigr)
 \prod_{D_j\ne C}q_j
 =\Delta(y).
\]
Two measures on finite \(Y\) are unequal exactly when one point mass is unequal, proving the equivalence.

Positivity does not make this contraction injective. For binary \((A,R)\), take
\[
 q_C^{(0)}(0,0)=q_C^{(0)}(1,1)=\frac38,\quad
 q_C^{(0)}(1,0)=q_C^{(0)}(0,1)=\frac18,\quad
 q_C^{(1)}(a,r)=\frac14.
\]
Both kernels are positive and unequal, but for each \(r\),
\[
 \sum_a\bigl(q_C^{(0)}(a,r)-q_C^{(1)}(a,r)\bigr)=0.
\]
If \(A\subseteq D\setminus Y_N\) is marginalized and all common factors are independent of \(A\), the contraction contains this zero sum, so \(\Delta(y)=0\) for every \(y\). This is the claimed obstruction.
