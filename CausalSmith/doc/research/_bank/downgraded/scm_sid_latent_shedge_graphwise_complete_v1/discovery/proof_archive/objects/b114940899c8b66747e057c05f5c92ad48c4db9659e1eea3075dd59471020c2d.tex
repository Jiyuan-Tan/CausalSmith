The graph recursion has the advertised finite bound independently of the open compiler.  On a chain rooted at \(T_i^{(0)}\), every recursive invocation replaces an enclosure \(T\) by \(T'\subsetneq T\) containing the fixed nonempty target \(D_i\).  Hence \(|T'|\le |T|-1\); a chain starting at size \(m\) has at most \(m\) invocations.  Summing over the finite initial family gives at most \(B_0\) invocations, and the maximum depth is at most \(\max_i|T_i^{(0)}|\le|N_S|\).  The checker bound is proved in \(\mathrm{lem:certificate\text{-}checker\text{-}soundness}\).  Total certified output is not yet established because effective accepted-certificate constructors for both the terminal inverse-lift branch and the initial d-separation-failure branch have not been proved as statements; their construction definitions alone cannot supply those guarantees.
