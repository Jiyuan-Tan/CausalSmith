Assume that \(G^S\) has no bidirected edges and that \(S\) has no children.  Fix \(H\subseteq N_S\), and write
\[
  J_H=G^S[H\cup A_S\cup\{S\}].
\]
Taking an induced subgraph cannot create an edge, so \(J_H\) has no bidirected edges.  A district is a maximal set joined by bidirected paths.  A bidirected path between two distinct vertices contains a bidirected edge; hence every district of \(J_H\) is a singleton.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}component}\) therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(H)
 &=\bigl\{K\cap H:K\in\mathcal D(J_H),\ K\cap H\ne\varnothing\bigr\}\\
 &=\bigl\{\{h\}:h\in H\bigr\}. \tag{1}
\end{aligned}
\]
When \(H=\varnothing\), both sides of (1) are the empty partition, so (1) covers every \(H\subseteq N_S\).

We next exclude an S-Hedge directly from the definitions.  Suppose for contradiction that \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\) holds.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\),
\[
 C\subsetneq T\subseteq N_S,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)=\{T\}. \tag{2}
\]
The declared type of \(C\) makes \(C\) nonempty, so choose \(t\in C\).  Since \(t\in T\), (1) applied with \(H=T\) says that \(\{t\}\) is a block of \(\operatorname{SComp}_{G^S}(T)\).  Combining this fact with (2) yields \(\{t\}=T\).  The inclusions \(\varnothing\ne C\subseteq T=\{t\}\) then force \(C=T\), contradicting \(C\subsetneq T\).  Thus
\[
  \neg\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)
  \quad\text{for every admissible pair }(C,T). \tag{3}
\]

Definition \(\mathrm{def:success\text{-}or\text{-}certificate\text{-}algorithm}\) states that a terminal recursive failure supplies sets \(C\subsetneq T\subseteq N_S\) such that both are single S-components and \(\operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\), those conditions are exactly \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\).  This contradicts (3), so the recursive S-Hedge failure branch of \(\mathsf{sID}^{\pm}\) cannot occur.  This argument proves the singleton-component and no-failure conclusions internally; it does not borrow them from the citation.

For the remaining graphical comparison, \(\mathrm{lem:published\text{-}dag\text{-}sid\text{-}consistency}\) records the precise DAG specialization of AboueiEtAl2024LatentSID Theorem 5.1 and the paragraph immediately following Remark 5.2: the auxiliary effect is always s-ID, while the original selected causal effect is s-ID exactly when the paper's Equation (5), its initial selected d-separation condition, holds; the authors call this consistent with AboueiEtAl2024SID Theorem 2.  This is a comparison of graphical criteria and does not assert literal equality of the two papers' displayed formulas.

It remains to verify the identifying claim as an equality between the observed-law functional and the causal target.  Fix a finite signature \(\sigma\), an intervention value \(x\in\mathcal X_X\), and a model \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:target\text{-}support}\),
\[
  P_M\bigl(S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x)\bigr)>0, \tag{4}
\]
so the intervention-before-conditioning law \(\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M)\) is defined.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\),
\[
  P_M(V=v,S=1)>0
  \quad\text{for every }v\in\prod_{j\in V}\mathcal X_j. \tag{5}
\]
Summing (5) over the nonempty finite state space of \(V\) gives \(P_M(S=1)>0\), and hence
\[
  P_{M,\sigma}^S(v)
  =\frac{P_M(V=v,S=1)}{P_M(S=1)}>0. \tag{6}
\]
More generally, for any \(Z\subseteq V\) and \(z\in\mathcal X_Z\), finiteness of the signature and (6) give
\[
  P_{M,\sigma}^S(Z=z)
  =\sum_{v\in\mathcal X_V:\,v|_Z=z}P_{M,\sigma}^S(v)>0, \tag{7}
\]
because the set of completions of \(z\) is nonempty and every summand is positive.  Thus every observed-coordinate conditioning denominator used in the outer conditional and in the successful finite recursive selected-kernel construction is positive.

Now suppose published Algorithm 1 succeeds.  Its soundness theorem, recorded as \(\mathrm{lem:published\text{-}latent\text{-}sid\text{-}soundness}\), applies because of (4)--(7).  Using Definition \(\mathrm{def:eq23\text{-}functional}\), it yields, for every \(y\in\mathcal X_Y\),
\[
 \Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(P_{M,\sigma}^S)(y)
 =P_M\bigl(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x),S=1\bigr)
 =\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M)(y). \tag{8}
\]
Since \(\mathcal X_Y\) is finite, the pointwise equalities in (8) imply equality of probability laws.

Finally, let \(M_0,M_1\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\) have the same selected observational law \(P_{M_0,\sigma}^S=P_{M_1,\sigma}^S=p\).  Applying (8) to each model gives
\[
  \Theta_{x,\sigma}(M_0)
  =\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(p)
  =\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M_1). \tag{9}
\]
Therefore the explicit observed-law map \(p\mapsto\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(p)\) identifies \(\Theta_{x,\sigma}\) whenever Algorithm 1 succeeds, as claimed.
