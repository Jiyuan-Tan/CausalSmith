The forward implication is complete.  Suppose published Algorithm 1 succeeds.  By the cited soundness result \(\mathrm{lem:published\text{-}latent\text{-}sid\text{-}soundness}\), for every finite signature \(\sigma\), every \(x\in\mathcal X_X\), and every \(M\in\mathfrak M_+(G^S,\sigma)\),

 \Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}(P_{M,\sigma}^S)
 =\Theta_{x,\sigma}(M).
\]
The selected conditionals in \(\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}\) are defined because every selected cell is positive by \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), and the target is defined by \(\mathrm{lem:target\text{-}support}\).  Thus two models in the same signature having the same selected law have the same value of \(\Phi_{23,x}^{\sigma}\), hence the same target.  Quantifying over \(\sigma\), \(x\), and the two models gives \(\operatorname{Ident}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S(G^S;X,Y)\) by \(\mathrm{def:graphwise\text{-}sid}\).

For the converse, an actual terminal single-component failure supplies the graph-theoretic S-Hedge and projected ordinary-hedge geometry through \(\mathrm{lem:shedge\text{-}projects\text{-}to\text{-}hedge}\). Exactification cannot create separation because \(\mathrm{lem:exactification\text{-}preserves\text{-}laws}\) preserves both equality and inequality of the targets already present.  Hence the converse is reduced to, but not proved beyond, this explicit certificate-construction problem.
