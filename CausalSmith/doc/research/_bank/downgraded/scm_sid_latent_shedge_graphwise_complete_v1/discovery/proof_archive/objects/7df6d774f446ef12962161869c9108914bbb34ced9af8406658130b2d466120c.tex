Put \(H=F_1\), regarded as the semi-Markovian graph whose observed vertex set is \(V(F_1)\), and put
\[
 Z=X_N\cap V(F_1),\qquad O=R.
\]
The sets \(Z\) and \(O\) are disjoint: indeed, \(R\subseteq C=V(F_0)\) because \(F_0\) is \(R\)-rooted, while the hypothesis \(C\cap X_N=\varnothing\) gives \(R\cap Z=\varnothing\). The hypothesis \(V(F_1)\cap X_N\ne\varnothing\) gives \(V(F_1)\cap Z=Z\ne\varnothing\), and
\[
 V(F_0)\cap Z=C\cap X_N\cap V(F_1)=\varnothing.
\]
By hypothesis, \(F_1\) and \(F_0\) are both \(R\)-rooted C-forests, and \(F_0\subset F_1\). Every vertex is its own ancestor via the length-zero directed path. Since \(R\cap Z=\varnothing\), those paths remain in \(H_{\overline Z}\), whence
\[
 R\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{H_{\overline Z}}(R).
\]
This inclusion is proper if the source's symbol \(\subset\) is read strictly. Indeed, \(Z\ne\varnothing\), and every nonroot vertex of the finite directed forest has a unique directed path ending in \(R\). On such a path starting from any element of \(Z\), choose the last vertex \(z_*\) that belongs to \(Z\). The remaining path from \(z_*\) to \(R\) has no arrow whose head lies in \(Z\), so it survives removal of arrows into \(Z\). Thus \(z_*\in\operatorname{An}_{H_{\overline Z}}(R)\setminus R\).
Thus \(\langle F_1,F_0\rangle\) satisfies every clause of Shpitser--Pearl's Definition 7 for a hedge for \(P_z(R)\): the outer forest meets \(Z\), the inner forest avoids \(Z\), the forests are nested and have the same root set, and the root-ancestor condition holds. By \(\mathrm{lem:shpitser\text{-}pearl\text{-}ordinary\text{-}hedge\text{-}criterion}\), the ordinary causal effect \(P_z(R)\) is not identifiable in \(H\).

To instantiate the positive construction without changing its information regime, take Kivva et al.'s collection of known distributions to be the singleton \(\mathcal A=\{V(F_1)\}\). Its only known kernel is then
\[
 Q[V(F_1)]=P(V(F_1)).
\]
Kivva et al.'s Definition 3 quantifies identifiability over models having full-support observational laws. Their complete ordinary Algorithm 1 therefore reports failure for the hedge above. Equivalently, with
\[
 W=\operatorname{An}_{H[V(F_1)\setminus Z]}(R),
\]
the reduction used by that algorithm contains a single-C-component kernel \(Q[B]\), for some district \(B\subseteq W\), on which its \(\operatorname{ID\text{-}Single}\) call fails. In the proof of Kivva et al.'s Theorem 1, the choice \(\mathcal A=\{V(F_1)\}\) makes equation (4) precisely equality of the full observational law, whereas equation (5) is disagreement on this failed kernel. That proof chooses an arbitrary odd \(\kappa>4\), uses only finite alphabets, and solves its linear system with strictly positive latent weights. Kivva et al.'s Proposition 4 combines the component kernels into \(Q[W]\), and the supplementary proof of Proposition 3 transports the resulting nonidentifiability back to the causal target \(P_z(R)\) while preserving equality of the known distribution. These cited facts are recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:kivva\text{-}positive\text{-}finite\text{-}id\text{-}witness}\).

Consequently there are common finite state spaces and two semi-Markovian models \(\overline M_0,\overline M_1\) inducing \(H\), together with a common \(z^\dagger\in\mathcal X_Z\), such that
\[
 P_{\overline M_0}(V(F_1)=h)
 =P_{\overline M_1}(V(F_1)=h)>0
 \quad\text{for every }h\in\mathcal X_{V(F_1)},
\]
but
\[
 P_{\overline M_0}(R\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=z^\dagger))
 \ne
 P_{\overline M_1}(R\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=z^\dagger)).
\]
Because \(H=F_1\), these models use precisely the forest incidence. The cited construction uses \(\{0,\ldots,\kappa\}\)-valued forest coordinates and finite product alphabets for the remaining coordinates. This proves the claim. The additional hypothesis \(R\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{G[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N)\) is not needed to separate the local root distribution; it is the load-bearing condition for routing that contrast to \(Y_N\) in the selected-law extension.

For an exact smallest-instance check, take the two-observed-node bow graph \(Z\to R\) and \(Z\leftrightarrow R\), with \(F_1=H\), \(F_0=\{R\}\), and a fair binary latent variable \(U\) shared by the two mechanisms. In both models let
\[
 P(Z=1\mid U=0)=\frac15,
 \qquad
 P(Z=1\mid U=1)=\frac45.
\]
For \(j=0\), set the four probabilities \(P_j(R=1\mid Z=z,U=u)\), ordered as \((z,u)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), equal to
\[
 \left(\frac25,\frac9{10},\frac9{10},\frac25\right),
\]
and for \(j=1\) set them equal to
\[
 \left(\frac35,\frac1{10},\frac1{10},\frac35\right).
\]
Independent finite private randomizers realize these kernels as recursive SCMs. Every displayed probability lies strictly between zero and one; both \(U\to Z\) and \(U\to R\) are active and the response of \(R\) changes with \(Z\), so both models induce the bow graph. Bayes' rule gives
\[
 P(U=0\mid Z=1)=\frac15,
 \qquad
 P(U=0\mid Z=0)=\frac45.
\]
Substitution in the two response tables yields
\[
 P_0(R=1\mid Z=z)=P_1(R=1\mid Z=z)=\frac12
 \quad (z=0,1),
\]
and \(P_j(Z=z)=1/2\); hence both observational laws assign probability \(1/4\) to every \((z,r)\). On the other hand,
\[
 P_0(R=1\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=1))=\frac{13}{20},
 \qquad
 P_1(R=1\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=1))=\frac7{20}.
\]
This verifies the mechanism on the smallest hedge. It is not a binary construction for arbitrary forests. The outer-treatment condition is load-bearing: if \(V(F_1)\cap X_N=\varnothing\), then \(Z=\varnothing\) in the specialization above and the target is the observed marginal \(P(R)\), which cannot differ between models sharing the full observational law.
