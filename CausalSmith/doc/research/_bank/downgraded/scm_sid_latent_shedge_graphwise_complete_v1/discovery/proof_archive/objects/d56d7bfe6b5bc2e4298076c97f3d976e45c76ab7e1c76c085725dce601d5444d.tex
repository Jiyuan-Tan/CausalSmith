Writing \(q_j\) for the common factors outside \(C\), the claimed target difference is the finite linear contraction
\[
 \Delta(y)=\sum_{w,d_{D\setminus Y_N}}
 p^S(y_S,w\mid x_S)
 \bigl(q_C^{(0)}-q_C^{(1)}\bigr)
 \prod_{D_j\ne C}q_j.
\]
Thus the desired conclusion is exactly \(\Delta(y)\ne0\) for some \(y\).  Positivity of the common factors does not by itself prevent this linear map from having a nontrivial kernel; a proof must construct factors that transmit a particular signed cell of the ordinary-hedge contrast to an outcome along the stated root-to-outcome paths.
