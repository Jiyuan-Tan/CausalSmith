For \(r\in\{0,1\}\), substitute \(q_C^{(r)}\) and the common factors into the product in \(\mathrm{def:eq23\text{-}functional}\). All sums are finite. Subtracting the expression for \(r=1\) from the expression for \(r=0\), and factoring the terms that are common to the two constructions, gives pointwise
\[
 \Phi^{(0)}(y)-\Phi^{(1)}(y)
 =\sum_{w,d_{D\setminus Y_N}}
 p^S(y_S,w\mid x_S)
 \bigl(q_C^{(0)}-q_C^{(1)}\bigr)
 \prod_{D_j\ne C}q_j
 =\Delta(y).
\]
Two distributions on the finite state space of \(Y\) are unequal exactly when one of their point masses is unequal. Hence the two constructed targets differ if and only if \(\Delta(y)\ne0\) for some \(y\).

The last assertion is an algebraic obstruction, not an appeal to absence of positivity. Consider a binary local pair on variables \((A,R)\) with
\[
 q_C^{(0)}(0,0)=q_C^{(0)}(1,1)=\frac38,
 \qquad
 q_C^{(0)}(1,0)=q_C^{(0)}(0,1)=\frac18,
\]
and \(q_C^{(1)}(a,r)=1/4\) for all \((a,r)\). Both kernels are strictly positive and unequal, but for each \(r\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}
 \bigl(q_C^{(0)}(a,r)-q_C^{(1)}(a,r)\bigr)=0.
\]
If \(A\subseteq D\setminus Y_N\) is marginalized and all common factors are chosen independent of \(A\), the displayed contraction sums this zero quantity and therefore satisfies \(\Delta(y)=0\) for every \(y\). Thus positivity does not make the contraction injective; an additional contrast-transmission construction is necessary.
