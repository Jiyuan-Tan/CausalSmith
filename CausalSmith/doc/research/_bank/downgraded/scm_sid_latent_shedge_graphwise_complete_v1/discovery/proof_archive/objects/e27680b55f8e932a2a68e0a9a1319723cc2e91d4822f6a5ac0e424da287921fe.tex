The target of every call in the chain is fixed at \(C=D_i\). By the definition of a terminal failure of \(\operatorname{sID\text{-}Single}\), the current enclosure is a single S-component \(T\), the target is a single S-component \(C\), \(C\subsetneq T\subseteq T_i^{(0)}\), and the failure test is
\[
 A:=\operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T.
\]
These are exactly the four clauses of \(\operatorname{SHedge}_{G^S}(C,T)\) in \(\mathrm{def:s\text{-}hedge}\).

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:projection\text{-}components}\) first with \(H=C\) and then with \(H=T\). The singleton S-component identities imply that \(\Pi_S(G^S)[C]\) and \(\Pi_S(G^S)[T]\) each have one district. The suppression projection inherits every directed edge whose endpoints lie in \(N_S\). Since \(T\subseteq N_S\), every directed path in \(G^S[T]\) is present in \(\Pi_S(G^S)[T]\), and the projection adds no directed edge. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{An}_{\Pi_S(G^S)[T]}(C)
 =\operatorname{An}_{G^S[T]}(C)=T.
\]

We now construct the two forests. Choose a bidirected spanning tree \(K_0\) of the connected bidirected graph \(\Pi_S(G^S)[C]\). Because \(\Pi_S(G^S)[T]\) is bidirected-connected, the finite forest \(K_0\) extends to a bidirected spanning tree \(K_1\) on \(T\). No additional edge of \(K_1\) can have both endpoints in \(C\), since \(K_0\) already connects \(C\) and such an edge would create a cycle. Thus \(K_1[C]=K_0\).

Put \(R=C\). Give \(F_0\) the vertex set \(C\), bidirected tree \(K_0\), and no directed edges. For every \(t\in T\setminus C\), the ancestral equality supplies a directed path from \(t\) to \(C\) in \(G^S[T]\). Let \(d(t)\) be the minimum length of such a path and choose as the unique child of \(t\) a next vertex \(c(t)\) on a shortest path. Then \(d(c(t))=d(t)-1\). Give \(F_1\) the vertex set \(T\), bidirected tree \(K_1\), and directed edges \(t\to c(t)\) for \(t\in T\setminus C\). Strict decrease of \(d\) excludes a directed cycle, every vertex outside \(C\) has exactly one child, and every vertex of \(C\) has no child. Consequently the roots of both forests are exactly \(R=C\), both have one bidirected district, and \(F_1[C]=F_0\).

It remains to verify the incidence conditions. From
\[
 D=\operatorname{An}_{G^S[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N)
\]
and \(C=D_i\subseteq D\), one obtains \(C\cap X_N=\varnothing\) and, because directed edges on \(N_S\) are unchanged by projection,
\[
 R=C\subseteq
 \operatorname{An}_{\Pi_S(G^S)[N_S\setminus X_N]}(Y_N).
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(T\cap X_N=\varnothing\). Then \(T\subseteq N_S\setminus X_N\). Every \(t\in T\) is an ancestor in \(T\) of some \(c\in C\), while every \(c\in C\subseteq D\) is an ancestor of \(Y_N\) in \(G^S[N_S\setminus X_N]\). Concatenating the directed paths shows \(t\in D\), hence \(T\subseteq D\). But \(T\) is one S-component, contains \(C=D_i\), and \(D_i\) is the maximal S-component of \(D\) containing \(C\). Therefore \(T\subseteq D_i=C\), contradicting \(C\subsetneq T\). Thus \(T\cap X_N\ne\varnothing\), completing the construction.
