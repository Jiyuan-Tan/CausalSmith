\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}incremental\_\allowbreak{}hardening\_\allowbreak{}limit}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Binary incremental propensity interventions avoid a fixed positivity condition only while their odds multiplier remains moderate. On a second-order regularly varying lower-propensity class, the cross-fitted incremental-mean score instead has a hardening frontier indexed by \(h_n=\delta_n/a_n\): a Gaussian law below the frontier, a compensated marked infinitely-divisible law at it, and a residual stable law above it. The critical jump retains both the treated outcome residual and the propensity-path treatment contrast. The project derives these laws, proves oracle replacement and relative consistency of an explicit cross-fitted score-variance estimator on a nonparametric \(C^2\) class, estimates the tail normalizer and joint boundary marks, certifies numerical Lévy inversion, and establishes branchwise uniform coverage. The Gaussian branch carries an oracle ambient pointwise convolution bound over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\), with feasible attainment asserted only on \(\mathcal P_{C^2}\).

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published IPSI inference holds over fixed compact multiplier sets, while published weak-overlap stable and infinitely-divisible results concern fixed inverse-weight targets or externally trimmed scores. Neither literature handles a moving binary incremental target whose score itself changes at the rare-treatment scale.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is a feasible marked-law calibration theorem: the finite-positive branch depends jointly on a residual mark and a policy-path contrast, and both its normalizer and its asymmetric quantiles must be estimated near an unknown propensity boundary.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper constructs the three-regime law, a concrete cross-fitted boundary learner, an explicit empirical variance of held-out fitted scores, empirical tail and joint-mark estimates, and confidence intervals whose uniform coverage includes nuisance and variance replacement, logarithmic normalizer sensitivity, anti-concentration, and deterministic inversion error.

\section{Related work}

Kennedy2019Incremental supplies the exact incremental estimand, its propensity-dependent efficient influence function, and Gaussian-process inference on bounded multiplier sets; Theorem 3 strictly enlarges that coverage domain to diverging multipliers in separated regimes, while Theorem 4 gives the distinct ambient pointwise efficiency statement. MaWang2020RobustIPW supplies the regular-variation and compensation template for trimmed fixed-target inverse weighting, but its scalar boundary mark cannot represent the IPSI propensity-path contrast. HeilerKazak2021Irregular establishes stable causal-score limits and warns that the ordinary bootstrap fails, informing the direct non-Gaussian calibration in Theorem 3. ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML provides the foldwise orthogonal expansion rebuilt in tail-weighted and truncated norms in Theorem 2. HiranoImbensRidder2003 motivates the concrete growing-local-basis propensity fit, while the boundary information calculation targets relative error. SchindlShenKennedy2026Continuous shows that unbounded tilts matter for continuous exposures, but its effective-sample-size and edge-dose analysis has a different localization mechanism. NaimiEtAl2021TimeFixed provides the writing and application exemplar: its nuMoM2b analysis uses odds multipliers from \(0.20\) to \(5.0\), and Theorem 3 governs extensions toward increasingly forceful odds shifts rather than revising that bounded-range analysis.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid]\label{ass:iid}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent identical sampling, \cite{Kennedy2019Incremental}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency, \cite{Kennedy2019Incremental}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exchangeability]\label{ass:exchangeability}
\(A\perp\!\!\!\perp\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\mid X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability, \cite{Kennedy2019Incremental}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes}
\(\max\{|Y(0)|,|Y(1)|\}\leq B\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded outcomes, \cite{NaimiEtAl2021TimeFixed}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-treated-support]\label{ass:positive-treated-support}
\(P\{e(X)>0\}=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (One-sided weak positivity, \cite{HeilerKazak2021Irregular}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compact-tail-index]\label{ass:compact-tail-index}
\(\kappa\in[\kappa_-,\kappa_+]\subset(0,1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior regular-variation index, \cite{MaWang2020RobustIPW}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compact-tail-scale]\label{ass:compact-tail-scale}
\(c\in[c_-,c_+]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform regular-variation scale separation, \cite{deHaanFerreira2006}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compact-second-order-index]\label{ass:compact-second-order-index}
\(\rho\in[\rho_-,\rho_+]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform second-order index separation, \cite{deHaanFerreira2006}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-tail-neighborhood]\label{ass:common-tail-neighborhood}
\(t_0\geq\underline t\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Common regular-variation neighborhood, \cite{deHaanFerreira2006}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:second-order-lower-tail]\label{ass:second-order-lower-tail}
\(\left|P\{e(X)\leq t\}/(ct^{\kappa})-1\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{tail}}t^{\rho}\) for every \(0<t\leq t_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Second-order regular variation at zero, \cite{deHaanFerreira2006}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:joint-boundary-mark]\label{ass:joint-boundary-mark}
\(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L_P\{(R,\tau(X))\mid A=1,e(X)\leq t\},H\right)\leq C_{\mathrm{mark}}t^{\rho}\) for every \(0<t\leq t_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Joint convergence is realistic when the outcome regressions and treated residual law vary continuously at a smooth propensity boundary. The explicit \(C^2\) Bernoulli subclass below satisfies it with a deterministic boundary law and order \(t\) error.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-separation]\label{ass:residual-separation}
\(E_H(R^2)\geq v_*\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Boundary outcome nondegeneracy, residual form, \cite{MaWang2020RobustIPW}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hardening]\label{ass:hardening}
\(\delta_n\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Diverging odds multipliers formalize increasingly forceful encouragement policies and the approach from support-respecting IPSI toward near-universal treatment; this sequence is the scientific intervention regime rather than a proof device.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:c2-density-form]\label{ass:c2-density-form}
\(f_X(x)=x^{\kappa-1}\ell(x)\) for \(x\in(0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Polynomial boundary density is a concrete covariate model generating the stipulated weak-overlap tail; it includes beta-type densities and smooth perturbations used in applied propensity diagnostics.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:c2-propensity-factor]\label{ass:c2-propensity-factor}
\(e(x)=xg(x)\) for \(x\in[0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A linearly vanishing propensity arises in threshold-crossing assignment models after a smooth boundary coordinate transformation and allows direct estimation of relative, rather than merely absolute, propensity error.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:c2-smoothness]\label{ass:c2-smoothness}
\(\max\{\|\ell\|_{C^2},\|g\|_{C^2},\|\mu_0\|_{C^2},\|\mu_1\|_{C^2}\}\leq M_2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Common second-order Hölder ball, \cite{HiranoImbensRidder2003}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:c2-lower-bounds]\label{ass:c2-lower-bounds}
\(\inf_{x\in[0,1]}\min\{\ell(x),g(x),\mu_0(x),1-\mu_0(x),\mu_1(x),1-\mu_1(x)\}\geq\varepsilon\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The restriction admits beta-like covariate densities, a broad open ball of smooth propensity factors, and nondegenerate Bernoulli regressions; it excludes only outcome and design degeneracy at the boundary.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:c2-upper-propensity]\label{ass:c2-upper-propensity}
\(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}xg(x)\leq1-\varepsilon\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Control-arm separation, \cite{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:c2-bernoulli-outcomes]\label{ass:c2-bernoulli-outcomes}
\(\mathcal L_P(Y\mid A=a,X=x)=\operatorname{Bernoulli}[(1-a)\mu_0(x)+a\mu_1(x)]\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary conditional outcome model, \cite{NaimiEtAl2021TimeFixed}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:rv-class]\label{def:rv-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Second-\allowbreak{}order marked weak-\allowbreak{}overlap class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}=\{P:(O_i)_{i=1}^n\text{ obey independent identical sampling, causal consistency, conditional exchangeability, bounded outcomes, one-sided support, }\kappa\in[\kappa_-,\kappa_+],\text{ the second-order lower-tail inequality, joint boundary-mark convergence, and residual separation}\}\) for every triangular index \(n\geq1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}exchangeability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}potential-\allowbreak{}outcomes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}positive-\allowbreak{}treated-\allowbreak{}support}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}compact-\allowbreak{}tail-\allowbreak{}index}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}compact-\allowbreak{}tail-\allowbreak{}scale}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}compact-\allowbreak{}second-\allowbreak{}order-\allowbreak{}index}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}common-\allowbreak{}tail-\allowbreak{}neighborhood}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}second-\allowbreak{}order-\allowbreak{}lower-\allowbreak{}tail}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}joint-\allowbreak{}boundary-\allowbreak{}mark}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}separation}.
\end{definition}

\end{split}
\]
where every fitted value is trained off the fold containing \(i\).  Set \(\widehat\psi_{\delta_n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat\varphi_{\delta_n,i}\).  Thus the construction is a measurable total map even on singular training samples; the fallback is used only on the vanishing Gram-failure event.

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-hardening-frontier]\label{thm:oracle-hardening-frontier}
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\): if \(h_n\to0\), then \(\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\delta_n}(O_i)/(\sqrt n\sigma_n)\Rightarrow N(0,1)\); if \(h_n\to h_0\in(0,\infty)\), then \(a_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\delta_n}(O_i)\Rightarrow L_{\kappa,H,h_0}\); and if \(h_n\to\infty\), then \(a_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\delta_n}(O_i)\Rightarrow S_{\alpha,H}\). Uniformity is branchwise over compact parameter sets separated from the two transition boundaries. This is a strict multiplier-domain extension of Kennedy's fixed-compact Gaussian result because it includes diverging \(\delta_n\) and two non-Gaussian branches.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(p=e(X)\), \(D=1+(\delta_n-1)p\), \(R=Y-\mu_1(X)\) on \(\{A=1\}\), and \(U=\tau(X)\).  All constants below are uniform over the compact constants in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\).  First, Stieltjes integration and the second-order lower-tail assumption give, uniformly as \(t\downarrow0\),
\[
\begin{split}
 T(t)
 &=\int_{(0,t]}x\,dP\{p\leq x\}
 =tP(p\leq t)-\int_0^tP(p\leq x)\,dx\\
 &=c t^{1+\kappa}-\frac{c}{1+\kappa}t^{1+\kappa}
   +O(t^{1+\kappa+\rho})
 =K t^\alpha\{1+O(t^\rho)\},
\end{split}
\]
where \(K=c\kappa/(1+\kappa)\) and \(\alpha=1+\kappa\).  Hence \(a_n^\alpha=nK\), \(nT(1/a_n)=1+o(1)\), and \(a_n\asymp n^{1/\alpha}\).  The same conclusions hold for the admitted asymptotically equivalent generalized inverse.

We next record the marked rare-treatment measure.  For \(0<x<\infty\) and bounded Lipschitz \(f\) on \([-2B,2B]^2\), the joint-boundary-mark assumption and the preceding expansion imply
\[
 nE\!\left[A\mathbf1\{a_np\leq x\}f(R,U)\right]
 =nT(x/a_n)\{E_Hf+O((x/a_n)^\rho)\}
 \longrightarrow x^\alpha E_Hf.
\]
Subtracting this identity at two continuity points \(x<y\) proves vague convergence on compact \(s\)-annuli of the marked measures
\[
 n\mathcal L_P(a_np,R,U;A=1)
 \ Longrightarrow\ 
 \Lambda(ds,dr,du)=\alpha s^\kappa ds\,H(dr,du).
\]
The asserted uniformity follows because every remainder above is bounded by the displayed common envelopes.  Moreover, conditional residual centering gives
\[
 E\{A R f(U)\mathbf1(p\leq t)\}=0
\]
for every bounded continuous \(f\).  Dividing by \(T(t)>0\), taking \(t\downarrow0\), and using bounded-Lipschitz convergence yield
\[
 E_HR=0,\qquad E_H(RU)=0. \tag{1}
\]

Let \(s(X)=m_{\delta_n}(X)-\psi_{\delta_n}(P)\).  Since the three residual terms in the oracle score are conditionally centered, direct conditioning gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \sigma_n^2={}&E\!\left[
 \frac{\delta_n^2p\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=1,X)}{D^2}
 +\frac{(1-p)\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid A=0,X)}{D^2}
 +\frac{\delta_n^2p(1-p)U^2}{D^4}
 \right]+\operatorname{Var}(m_{\delta_n}(X)).
\end{split} \tag{2}
\]
The last two bounded terms not containing a diverging treated weight are \(O(1)\).  Applying the marked-measure convergence after the substitution \(q=\delta_np\), truncating first to \(q\in[\epsilon,M]\), and then using boundedness plus the tail envelope to let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), \(M\uparrow\infty\), gives
\[
 \frac{\sigma_n^2}{\delta_n^{1-\kappa}}
 \longrightarrow V_{\kappa,H}:=c\kappa\!\left[
 E_H(R^2)\operatorname{Beta}(1+\kappa,1-\kappa)
 +E_H(U^2)\operatorname{Beta}(1+\kappa,3-\kappa)
 \right]. \tag{3}
\]
Here (1) eliminates the possible residual--contrast cross term, although (2) already separates the conditional variance terms.  Compactness, bounded marks, and \(E_HR^2\geq v_*>0\) imply
\[
 0<C_1\leq V_{\kappa,H}\leq C_2<\infty. \tag{4}
\]

The bounded-outcome and support assumptions imply \(\|\phi_{\delta_n}\|_\infty\leq C\delta_n\).  Combining this with (3) and \(a_n^\alpha=nK\) yields
\[
 \frac{\|\phi_{\delta_n}\|_\infty}{\sqrt n\,\sigma_n}
 \leq C\left(\frac{\delta_n^\alpha}{n}\right)^{1/2}
 \leq C h_n^{\alpha/2}. \tag{5}
\]
If \(h_n\to0\), (5) makes the standardized summands uniformly infinitesimal.  Expanding their characteristic functions to second order, with the remainder bounded by the left side of (5) times the standardized second moment, proves
\[
 \frac{1}{\sqrt n\sigma_n}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\delta_n}(O_i)\Rightarrow N(0,1). \tag{6}
\]

Suppose next that \(h_n\to h_0\in(0,\infty)\).  On a treated observation with \(a_np=s\), its two possibly non-negligible score components satisfy, uniformly on compact \(s\)-annuli,
\[
 \frac1{a_n}\left\{\frac{A\delta_nR}{D}+\frac{\delta_nU(A-p)}{D^2}\right\}
 \longrightarrow \frac{h_0R}{1+h_0s}+\frac{h_0U}{(1+h_0s)^2}=g_{h_0}(s,R,U). \tag{7}
\]
For \(h\) in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\),
\[
 \int_{s\leq\epsilon}g_h^2\,d\Lambda\leq C\epsilon^\alpha,
 \qquad
 \int_{s\geq M}g_h^2\,d\Lambda\leq C M^{\kappa-1}. \tag{8}
\]
The control residual, the \(A=0\) part of the propensity term, and \(s(X)\) are bounded, so their centered sums divided by \(a_n\) have variance \(O(n/a_n^2)=o(1)\).  For the treated part, vague convergence, (7), and (8) show for every fixed \(t\),
\[
 nE\!\left[
 e^{it\phi_{\delta_n}/a_n}-1-it\phi_{\delta_n}/a_n
 \right]
 \longrightarrow
 \int\{e^{it g_{h_0}}-1-itg_{h_0}\}\,d\Lambda. \tag{9}
\]
The integrand on the right is integrable: the intensity has finite mass near zero, while it is \(O(s^{-2})s^\kappa\) at infinity.  The expectation inside (9) is \(O(n^{-1})\); hence \(n\log E e^{it\phi_{\delta_n}/a_n}\) has the same limit.  The limiting exponent is continuous at zero and is the exponent in the definition of \(L_{\kappa,H,h_0}\).  The cited continuity theorem therefore proves
\[
 a_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\delta_n}(O_i)\Rightarrow L_{\kappa,H,h_0}. \tag{10}
\]

For the hard branch introduce the oracle residual-IPW score
\[
 \phi_\infty(O)=\frac{AR}{p}+\mu_1(X)-E\mu_1(X).
\]
It is well defined almost surely by positive treated support and has mean zero by conditional residual centering.  Elementary denominator identities and the tail bound give
\[
\begin{split}
 E\left[A|R|\left|\frac{\delta_n}{D}-\frac1p\right|\right]
 &\leq 2B E\frac{1-p}{D}=O(\delta_n^{-\kappa}),\\
 E\left|\frac{(1-A)(Y-\mu_0)}D\right|&=O(\delta_n^{-\kappa}),\\
 E\left|\frac{\delta_nU(A-p)}{D^2}\right|&\leq4B E\frac{\delta_np(1-p)}{D^2}=O(\delta_n^{-\kappa}),\\
 E|m_{\delta_n}(X)-\mu_1(X)|&\leq2B E\frac{1-p}{D}=O(\delta_n^{-\kappa}).
\end{split} \tag{11}
\]
For example, each expectation in (11) is split at \(p=1/\delta_n\) and the second-order tail inequality is integrated on the two pieces.  Centering costs at most another factor two, so
\[
 \frac1{a_n}E\left|\sum_{i=1}^n(\phi_{\delta_n}-\phi_\infty)(O_i)\right|
 \leq C\frac n{a_n}\delta_n^{-\kappa}\leq C h_n^{-\kappa}. \tag{12}
\]
The same marked-measure argument as in (9), now with jump \(r/s\), gives
\[
 a_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_\infty(O_i)\Rightarrow S_{\alpha,H}. \tag{13}
\]
Indeed the bounded \(\mu_1-E\mu_1\) part has variance \(O(n/a_n^2)=o(1)\), while the exponent of the treated part converges to
\[
 \int\{e^{itr/s}-1-itr/s\}\,\alpha s^\kappa ds\,H(dr,du).
\]
This is integrable because \(\kappa>0\) at zero and \(\kappa<1\) at infinity.  Combining (12)--(13) proves the stable assertion whenever \(h_n\to\infty\).  All truncation errors in (5), (8), and (11) are uniform on the branchwise separated compact sets, which proves the stated branchwise uniformity.  The cited Kennedy scope result is restricted to a fixed compact multiplier set, whereas the present conclusions cover \(\delta_n\to\infty\), including the two non-Gaussian branches; this proves the claimed strict multiplier-domain extension.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem locates the exact stochastic frontier at which a positivity-robust incremental mean acquires weak-overlap non-Gaussian uncertainty. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy2019Incremental stops at fixed compact multipliers, while MaWang2020RobustIPW varies trimming for a fixed target and therefore has neither the moving centering nor the IPSI contrast mark. \textit{Consumer.} It determines when the Gaussian approximation in \(\mathtt{npcausal::ipsi}\) must be replaced as analysts extend odds shifts beyond a bounded range.

\begin{proposition}[prop:contrast-extinction]\label{prop:contrast-extinction}
For every \(h_0\in(0,\infty)\), the absolute \(\Lambda\)-mass of the propensity-path component is \(\int |h_0u/(1+h_0s)^2|\,d\Lambda=\alpha\operatorname{Beta}(1+\kappa,1-\kappa)E_H|u|\,h_0^{-\kappa}\). Hence this component is absent at a finite critical coordinate if and only if \(u=0\) \(H\)-almost surely; if \(E_H|u|>0\), it remains strictly positive for every finite \(h_0\) and vanishes only at the stable endpoint \(h_0\to\infty\). Moreover \(\int|g_{h_0}(s,r,u)-r/s|\,d\Lambda=O(h_0^{-\kappa})\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The propensity-path part of \(g_{h_0}\) is \(h_0u/(1+h_0s)^2\).  Tonelli's theorem applies because its absolute value is nonnegative, and the bounded support of \(H\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \int\left|\frac{h_0u}{(1+h_0s)^2}\right|d\Lambda
 &=\alpha E_H|u|\int_0^\infty\frac{h_0s^\kappa}{(1+h_0s)^2}\,ds\\
 &=\alpha E_H|u|h_0^{-\kappa}\int_0^\infty\frac{v^\kappa}{(1+v)^2}\,dv\\
 &=\alpha\operatorname{Beta}(1+\kappa,1-\kappa)E_H|u|h_0^{-\kappa}.
\end{split} \tag{1}
\]
The beta integral is finite and strictly positive because \(0<\kappa<1\).  Thus (1) is zero exactly when \(E_H|u|=0\), equivalently when \(u=0\) \(H\)-almost surely.  If \(E_H|u|>0\), it is positive at every finite \(h_0\), and it tends to zero exactly along \(h_0\to\infty\).

For the complete jump map,
\[
 g_{h_0}(s,r,u)-\frac rs
 =-\frac{r}{s(1+h_0s)}+\frac{h_0u}{(1+h_0s)^2}. \tag{2}
\]
Using (2), Tonelli, and \(|r|,|u|\leq2B\),
\[
\begin{split}
 \int\left|g_{h_0}-\frac rs\right|d\Lambda
 &\leq \alpha E_H|r|\int_0^\infty\frac{s^{\kappa-1}}{1+h_0s}\,ds
 +\alpha E_H|u|\int_0^\infty\frac{h_0s^\kappa}{(1+h_0s)^2}\,ds\\
 &=h_0^{-\kappa}\alpha\!\left[
 E_H|r|\operatorname{Beta}(\kappa,1-\kappa)
 +E_H|u|\operatorname{Beta}(1+\kappa,1-\kappa)
 \right]\\
 &=O(h_0^{-\kappa}).
\end{split}
\]
This proves both the exact mass formula and the hard-endpoint extinction rate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This prevents invalid residual-only critical calibration and identifies precisely when the extra causal-policy mark can be ignored. \textit{Gap.} Neither Kennedy2019Incremental nor the scalar boundary laws in MaWang2020RobustIPW characterize equality of these marked Lévy pushforwards. \textit{Consumer.} Software can use the criterion to decide whether a simpler stable-mark approximation is legitimate at a finite hardening coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:c2-inclusion]\label{prop:c2-inclusion}
Under the declared primitive envelope \(B\geq1\), the conditional Bernoulli support \(\{0,1\}\) lies in \([-B,B]\). There exist common \(c_-\), \(c_+\), \(\underline t\), \(C_{\mathrm{tail}}\), \(C_{\mathrm{mark}}\), and \(v_*\), depending only on the primitive class constants, such that every \(P\in\mathcal P_{C^2}\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\), with \(c=\ell(0)/\{\kappa g(0)^\kappa\}\), second-order exponent \(\rho=1\), and \(H\) equal to the boundary law of the Bernoulli treated residual paired with \(\mu_1(0)-\mu_0(0)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{C^2}\).  The Bernoulli conditional law has support \(\{0,1\}\subset[-B,B]\) because \(B\geq1\), so the bounded-outcome condition holds.  Also \(e(x)=xg(x)>0\) for every \(x>0\), and \(P(X=0)=0\) because \(X\) has a density; hence positive treated support holds almost surely.

Put \(g_0=g(0)\) and \(\ell_0=\ell(0)\).  The lower bound on \(g\), the common \(C^2\) radius, and compactness of \([\kappa_-,\kappa_+]\) provide a common \(t_*>0\) such that \(x\mapsto xg(x)\) is strictly increasing on \([0,2t_*/\varepsilon]\).  If \(e(x)\leq t\), then \(x\leq t/\varepsilon\); thus for \(t\leq t_*\) the event \(e(X)\leq t\) equals \(X\leq x(t)\), where the inverse-function expansion is
\[
 x(t)=\frac{t}{g_0}+O(t^2) \tag{1}
\]
uniformly over the primitive class.  Taylor expansion of \(\ell\) and (1) give
\[
\begin{split}
 P\{e(X)\leq t\}
 &=\int_0^{x(t)}x^{\kappa-1}\ell(x)\,dx\\
 &=\frac{\ell_0}{\kappa}x(t)^\kappa+O(x(t)^{\kappa+1})
 =\frac{\ell_0}{\kappa g_0^\kappa}t^\kappa\{1+O(t)\}.
\end{split} \tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(c=\ell(0)/\{\kappa g(0)^\kappa\}\) and \(\rho=1\).  Since \(\ell_0,g_0\in[\varepsilon,M_2]\) and \(\kappa\in[\kappa_-,\kappa_+]\), this \(c\) lies between common positive finite constants.  The remainders in (1)--(2), the common neighborhood \(t_*\), and their constants depend only on \(\varepsilon,M_2,\kappa_-,\kappa_+\).  Taking \(\underline t\leq t_*\), and enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{tail}}\) if necessary, proves the required second-order tail inequality.  The value \(\rho=1\) lies in \([\rho_-,\rho_+]\) by the declared bounds \(\rho_-\leq1\leq\rho_+\).

It remains to derive, rather than assume, the boundary mark.  Let \(U_0\) be uniform on \([0,1]\), and couple the treated Bernoulli outcome at covariate \(x\) as \(Y_x=\mathbf1\{U_0\leq\mu_1(x)\}\).  Set
\[
 R_x=Y_x-\mu_1(x),\qquad u_x=\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x),
\]
and let \(H\) be the law of \((R_0,u_0)\).  Smoothness gives
\[
 E|R_x-R_0|+|u_x-u_0|
 \leq 2|\mu_1(x)-\mu_1(0)|+|u_x-u_0|\leq Cx. \tag{3}
\]
Under \(A=1,e(X)\leq t\), one has \(X\leq t/\varepsilon\).  Applying the coupling (3) conditionally therefore yields
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L_P\{(R,\tau(X))\mid A=1,e(X)\leq t\},H\right)
 \leq Ct. \tag{4}
\]
This supplies a common \(C_{\mathrm{mark}}\).  Finally,
\[
 E_HR^2=\mu_1(0)\{1-\mu_1(0)\}\geq\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon). \tag{5}
\]
Thus a common choice \(v_*\leq\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\) gives residual separation.  Equations (2), (4), and (5), together with the sampling and causal assumptions already included in \(\mathcal P_{C^2}\), verify every member property of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\), proving the inclusion and the asserted form of \(H\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The inclusion proves that the explicit feasible model is nonempty and genuinely instantiates the marked regular-variation class rather than assuming membership. \textit{Gap.} The published weak-overlap papers impose tail conditions directly; they do not derive the required joint IPSI boundary mark from a primitive nonparametric covariate model. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition makes the early learner and calibration tests executable on a transparent beta-type design with binary outcomes.

\begin{theorem}[thm:crossfit-oracle-equivalence]\label{thm:crossfit-oracle-equivalence}
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{C^2}\), the local-linear construction satisfies \(\|\widehat e/e-1\|_\infty+\|\widehat\mu_0-\mu_0\|_\infty+\|\widehat\mu_1-\mu_1\|_\infty=O_P(r_n)\), with \(r_n=(\log n/n)^{2/(5+\kappa_+)}\), and its centered fitted score differs from the centered oracle score by \(o_P(\sigma_n/\sqrt n)\) when \(h_n\to0\) and by \(o_P(a_n/n)\) when \(h_n\) is bounded away from zero, including \(h_n\to\infty\). On every separated Gaussian sequence \(h_n\leq\eta_n\to0\), the explicit empirical score variance satisfies \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to_P1\). Consequently \(\widehat\psi_{\delta_n}\) inherits every branch of Theorem 1 without an assumed final remainder.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the corrected total-map convention for the local fits; its fallback is irrelevant on the high-probability Gram event proved below.  Let \(I_j\) be a bin and use the scaled basis \(B_j(x)=(1,(x-x_j)/b_n)^{\mathsf T}\), whose norm is uniformly bounded on \(I_j\).  For every vector \(v\in\mathbb R^2\), the propensity-fit population Gram matrix satisfies
\[
\begin{split}
 v^{\mathsf T}G_{g,j}v
 &=\int_{I_j}x\{v^{\mathsf T}B_j(x)\}^2x^{\kappa-1}\ell(x)\,dx
 \geq C b_n^{\kappa_++1}\|v\|^2.
\end{split} \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, after rescaling a boundary bin by \(x=b_nz\), the integral is \(b_n^{\kappa+1}\) times the integral of a nonzero linear polynomial squared against \(z^\kappa\); on every other bin it is larger.  Compactness of the exponent interval and the lower bound on \(\ell\) make the smallest eigenvalue uniform.  The treated-regression Gram has the additional factor \(g(x)\geq\varepsilon\) and obeys the same bound.  The control Gram has factor \(1-e(x)\geq\varepsilon\) and is no smaller than order \(b_n^{\kappa_+}\), hence also obeys (1).

For the propensity equation the centered summand is \(\mathbf1_{I_j}B_j(X)\{A-Xg(X)\}\), whose conditional variance is bounded by \(C\mathbf1_{I_j}X\); the treated- and control-regression score summands have variances bounded by their corresponding Gram weights.  Let \(q_j\) denote the appropriate population information mass in bin \(j\).  The rescaling argument used in (1) gives \(q_j\geq Cb_n^{\kappa_++1}\), while both the smallest and largest eigenvalues of the binwise population Gram are constant multiples of \(q_j\).  Applying the cited Bernstein inequality to the two coordinates, and taking a union bound over \(O(b_n^{-1})\) bins and the fixed folds, gives simultaneously for every bin
\[
 \left\|\frac1n\sum_i(Z_{ij}-EZ_{ij})\right\|
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q_j\log n}{n}}+\frac{\log n}{n}\right). \tag{2}
\]
The same bin-specific inequality entrywise for the two-dimensional Gram matrices and Weyl's elementary eigenvalue bound show that the empirical inverse in bin \(j\) has norm \(O_P(q_j^{-1})\), uniformly.  Taylor's theorem gives a local-linear approximation remainder at most \(C b_n^2\); multiplication by the inverse Gram does not enlarge its order because the weighted remainder is at most \(Cb_n^2q_j\).  Combining these observations with \(q_j\geq Cb_n^{\kappa_++1}\) yields
\[
 \|\widehat g-g\|_\infty+\sum_{a=0}^1\|\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a\|_\infty
 =O_P\!\left(b_n^2+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n b_n^{\kappa_++1}}}
 +\frac{\log n}{n b_n^{\kappa_++1}}\right)=O_P(r_n). \tag{3}
\]
Clipping cannot increase the error because the true functions lie in the clipping intervals.  Since \(e(x)=xg(x)\) and \(g(x)\geq\varepsilon\), the quotient is continuously extended at zero and
\[
 \left\|\frac{\widehat e}{e}-1\right\|_\infty
 =\left\|\frac{\widehat g}{g}-1\right\|_\infty=O_P(r_n). \tag{4}
\]

We now prove score replacement.  On a training event on which \(v=\widehat e\) obeys \(\|v/e-1\|_\infty=b<1/2\) and \(u_a=\widehat\mu_a-\mu_a\) obeys \(\|u_a\|_\infty=r_a\), condition the held-out fitted score on \(X\).  Direct collection of the coefficients of \(\tau,u_1,u_0\) gives the exact conditional bias
\[
 \mathcal B_\delta(v,u)
 =E\!\left[
 \frac{(\delta-1)(v-p)}{D_\delta(v)^2}
 \left\{\delta vu_1+(1-v)u_0+\frac{\delta(v-p)\tau}{D_\delta(p)}\right\}
 \right]. \tag{5}
\]
Because \((1-b)D_\delta(p)\leq D_\delta(v)\leq(1+b)D_\delta(p)\), splitting at \(p=1/\delta\) and integrating the tail bound proves
\[
 |\mathcal B_\delta(v,u)|
 \leq C\{br_1+\delta^{-\kappa}(br_0+b^2)\}. \tag{6}
\]
For example, the treated product is bounded by \(Cbr_1E\{\delta^2p^2/D_\delta(p)^2\}\leq Cbr_1\), while the two remaining kernels are bounded by constants times \(E\{\delta p/D_\delta(p)^2\}=O(\delta^{-\kappa})\).

Let \(\Delta_\delta\) be fitted minus oracle uncentered score and put \(r=b+r_0+r_1\).  Termwise denominator comparison gives
\[
 |\Delta_\delta|\leq Cr\{1+A\min(\delta,1/p)\},\qquad
 E(\Delta_\delta^2\mid\text{training})\leq Cr^2\{1+\delta^{1-\kappa}\}. \tag{7}
\]
The only unbounded term in the first inequality is a treated inverse-propensity term; integrating
\(E[A\min(\delta,1/p)^2]\) at \(p=1/\delta\) gives the second inequality.

In the Gaussian branch, (7), the variance equivalence in the oracle-frontier proof, and \(r_n=o(1)\) show that the conditionally centered average of \(\Delta_\delta\) is \(o_P(\sigma_n/\sqrt n)\).  Its bias is negligible because
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n r_n^2}{\sigma_n}\leq\sqrt n r_n^2=o(1), \tag{8}
\]
where \(4/(5+\kappa_+)>1/2\).  If \(h_n\) is bounded above and away from zero, the standard deviation from (7), divided by \(a_n/n\), is \(O_P(r_n)\), and the bias is negligible since
\[
 r_n^2=o(a_n/n),\qquad
 \frac4{5+\kappa_+}-\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}
 \geq\frac{4-\kappa_+-\kappa_+^2}{(5+\kappa_+)(1+\kappa_+)}>0. \tag{9}
\]

For the unrestricted hard branch put \(Z=A/p\).  The truncated-moment expansion gives
\[
 P(Z>z)=E[p\mathbf1(p<z^{-1})]=T(z^{-1})\leq Cz^{-\alpha}. \tag{10}
\]
Using (7), (10), and integration of the tail probability, for fixed \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\begin{split}
 nP(|\Delta_\delta|>\epsilon a_n)&\leq C_\epsilon r^\alpha,\\
 \frac n{a_n^2}E\min(\Delta_\delta^2,\epsilon^2a_n^2)&\leq C_\epsilon r^\alpha,\\
 \frac n{a_n}E\{|\Delta_\delta|\mathbf1(|\Delta_\delta|>\epsilon a_n)\}&\leq C_\epsilon r^\alpha.
\end{split} \tag{11}
\]
Large jumps, the centered truncated sum, and its truncation mean are therefore all \(o_P(1)\) after division by \(a_n\).  Equations (6), (9), and (11) prove score replacement at rate \(o_P(a_n/n)\) for every branch bounded away from zero, including \(h_n\to\infty\).  Conditioning separately on each fixed training fold and taking a finite union bound proves the cross-fitted assertion.

Finally, the oracle empirical second moment has relative variance at most
\[
 \frac{E\phi_{\delta_n}^4}{n\sigma_n^4}
 \leq\frac{\|\phi_{\delta_n}\|_\infty^2}{n\sigma_n^2}
 =O(h_n^\alpha)=o(1) \tag{12}
\]
on a separated Gaussian sequence.  Equations (7)--(8) transfer (12) to the centered fitted scores, so \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to_P1\).  Combining replacement with the three oracle limits proves every asserted inherited branch.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result turns the point-process law into a feasible nonparametric estimator and supplies the early kill test for the proposal. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary DML controls fixed finite-variance scores in unweighted \(L_2\) norms; HiranoImbensRidder2003 and MaWang2020RobustIPW use regular or parametric first steps rather than relative-error boundary learning. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a checkable learner implementation for the binary nuMoM2b outcome and a reference path for extending \(\mathtt{npcausal::ipsi}\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:feasible-separated-coverage]\label{thm:feasible-separated-coverage}
Choose \(t_n=n^{-\beta}\) so that \(nT(t_n)\to\infty\) and \(\log n\{r_n+t_n^\rho+[nT(t_n)]^{-1/2}\}\to0\). Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_{C^2}\), the empirical calibration obeys \(\widehat a_n/a_n\to_P1\) and \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\widehat H,H)\to_P0\). If \(h_n\leq\eta_n\to0\), then the cross-fitted empirical variance obeys \(\widehat\sigma_n/\sigma_n\to_P1\) and \(P\{\psi_{\delta_n}\in C_n^{\mathrm G}(\gamma)\}=1-\gamma+o(1)\); if \(h_n\in[h_-,h_+]\subset(0,\infty)\), then \(P\{\psi_{\delta_n}\in C_n^{\mathrm C}(\gamma)\}=1-\gamma+o(1)\); and if \(h_n\geq M_n\to\infty\), then \(P\{\psi_{\delta_n}\in C_n^{\mathrm S}(\gamma)\}=1-\gamma+o(1)\). Each remainder is uniform within its stated separated branch and includes score replacement, variance estimation in the Gaussian branch, tail and mark estimation, normalizer error, anti-concentration, and certified inversion error. The critical branch strictly extends Kennedy's multiplier-bootstrap coverage beyond a fixed compact multiplier set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(N_n=nT(t_n)\) and
\[
 \xi_n=r_n+t_n^\rho+N_n^{-1/2}.
\]
The premise gives \(N_n\to\infty\) and \((\log n)\xi_n\to0\).  The total-map truncations in the corrected calibration are inactive on the events established below.

On the relative-propensity event from the cross-fit theorem, selection disagreements at a deterministic threshold \(t\) are contained in
\[
 \left\{\frac{t}{1+Cr_n}<e(X)\leq\frac{t}{1-Cr_n}\right\}.
\]
The expansion \(T(t)=Kt^\alpha\{1+O(t^\rho)\}\) therefore gives
\[
 T\!\left(\frac{t}{1-Cr_n}\right)-T\!\left(\frac{t}{1+Cr_n}\right)
 \leq CT(t)(r_n+t^\rho). \tag{1}
\]
This argument does not assume a density for \(e(X)\); in particular, the second-order inequality itself bounds any jump of \(T\) at \(t\) by \(Ct^{\alpha+\rho}\).

Conditional on each training fold, the validation indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with total variance at most a constant times \(nT(t)\).  The cited Bernstein inequality, (1), and a finite fold union bound imply, at \(t=t_n,2t_n\),
\[
 \frac{\widehat T(t)}{T(t)}=1+O_P(r_n+t_n^\rho+N_n^{-1/2})=1+O_P(\xi_n). \tag{2}
\]
Since \(T(2t_n)/T(t_n)=2^\alpha\{1+O(t_n^\rho)\}\), (2) yields
\[
 \widehat\alpha-\alpha=O_P(\xi_n). \tag{3}
\]
The clipping interval contains \([1+\kappa_-,1+\kappa_+]\) in its interior, so clipping is inactive with probability tending to one.  From the definitions and \(|\log t_n|=\beta\log n\),
\[
 \log(\widehat K/K)=O_P((\log n)\xi_n),\qquad
 \log(\widehat a_n/a_n)=O_P((\log n)\xi_n)=o_P(1). \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\widehat a_n/a_n\to_P1\), and \(\widehat h_n/h_n\to_P1\).

For the mark law, first replace fitted marks by true marks; bounded-Lipschitz distance changes by at most \(Cr_n\).  The population law selected by the fitted threshold differs from the true law conditional on \(A=1,e\leq t_n\) by at most the relative shell probability in (1), hence by \(O_P(r_n+t_n^\rho)\).  The latter true conditional law is within \(C t_n^\rho\) of \(H\) by the joint-boundary-mark assumption.  To control empirical fluctuation without any mark density, partition the bounded mark square into a grid of mesh \(N_n^{-1/4}\).  There are \(O(N_n^{1/2})\) cells.  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the multinomial variance identity bound the expected total cell-frequency error by
\[
 \sqrt{O(N_n^{1/2})/N_n}=O(N_n^{-1/4}),
\]
and moving each mark to its grid representative costs another \(O(N_n^{-1/4})\) in bounded-Lipschitz distance.  The random denominator is equivalent to \(N_n\) by (2).  Therefore
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\widehat H,H)
 =O_P(r_n+t_n^\rho+N_n^{-1/4})=o_P(1). \tag{5}
\]

We next justify quantile inversion.  The compatibility identity proved in the oracle-frontier argument gives \(E_H(RU)=0\).  For \(h\in[h_-,h_+]\), the real part of the critical exponent obeys, for sufficiently large \(|t|\),
\[
\begin{split}
 -\Re\Psi_{\kappa,H,h}(t)
 &=\int\{1-\cos(tg_h)\}\,d\Lambda\\
 &\geq c t^2\int_{C|t|}^\infty E_H\{g_h(s,R,U)^2\}s^\kappa ds
 \geq c'|t|^{1+\kappa}.
\end{split} \tag{6}
\]
For the last inequality, \(|tg_h|\leq1\) on the integration range, \(1-\cos x\geq x^2/4\) there, and
\[
 E_Hg_h^2=\frac{h^2E_HR^2}{(1+hs)^2}
 +\frac{h^2E_HU^2}{(1+hs)^4}
 \geq \frac{c v_*}{s^2}.
\]
For \(\widehat H\), (5) makes its empirical residual second moment at least \(v_*/2\) with probability tending to one; the inequality \((a+b)^2\geq a^2/2-b^2\) absorbs the empirical cross term for large \(s\).  The stable exponent satisfies the same bound directly with jump \(R/s\).  Consequently the true and plug-in characteristic functions satisfy uniformly
\[
 |\chi(t)|\leq e^{-c|t|^{\alpha_-}} \tag{7}
\]
outside a common compact frequency interval, with probability tending to one for the plug-in laws.

On every bounded frequency interval, truncate the \(s\)-integral to \([\epsilon,M]\).  The omitted critical-exponent errors are bounded by \(C t^2\epsilon^\alpha+Ct^2M^{\kappa-1}\); for the stable exponent the small-\(s\) error is bounded by \(C(\epsilon^\alpha+|t|\epsilon^\kappa)\), and the large-\(s\) error is again \(Ct^2M^{\kappa-1}\).  On \([\epsilon,M]\), bounded-Lipschitz convergence of the marks, (3), and \(\widehat h_n/h_n\to_P1\) imply uniform convergence of the plug-in exponents.  Sending first \(n\to\infty\), then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and \(M\uparrow\infty\), proves convergence of characteristic functions, uniformly on compact frequencies.  The cited continuity theorem and (7) give uniform convergence of the corresponding continuous distribution functions.

By (7) and the cited Fourier inversion result, all these laws have bounded continuous densities.  No density can vanish on an interval: its Fourier transform decays faster than \(e^{-L|t|}\) for every fixed \(L\), so the density is real analytic and a zero interval would force it to be identically zero.  Hence every distribution function is strictly increasing.  The parameter sets for \(\kappa\), \(h\), and the weakly compact bounded-support mark laws are compact; residual separation is closed.  A subsequence contradiction therefore upgrades pointwise inverse continuity to uniform continuity of the fixed interior quantiles.  Thus, for \(u\in\{\gamma/2,1-\gamma/2\}\),
\[
 \widehat q_n(u)-q_{\kappa,H,h_n}(u)=o_P(1) \tag{8}
\]
in the critical branch, and the analogous relation holds for the stable endpoint.

For completeness, the certified inversion required in the interval definition is constructive.  Truncate the \(s\)-integral using the explicit errors above, integrate its smooth one-dimensional integrand on the remaining compact interval by interval quadrature, truncate Fourier inversion using (7), and enclose the fixed interior quantile by bisection of certified distribution-function brackets.  Each bound is computable from \(B,\kappa_-,\kappa_+,h_-,h_+\) and the empirical marks.  Refining the three truncations and the bisection until their sum is at most \(\epsilon_n^{\mathrm{inv}}\) produces the declared \(\widehat q_n\); because \(\epsilon_n^{\mathrm{inv}}\to0\), it does not alter (8).

The cross-fit theorem and the oracle frontier now finish coverage.  In the Gaussian branch,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n(\widehat\psi_{\delta_n}-\psi_{\delta_n})}{\widehat\sigma_n}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1),
\]
uniformly for \(h_n\leq\eta_n\to0\), so the Gaussian interval has coverage \(1-\gamma+o(1)\).  In the critical branch,
\[
 \frac n{a_n}(\widehat\psi_{\delta_n}-\psi_{\delta_n})
 \Rightarrow L_{\kappa,H,h_n},
\]
in the uniform current-parameter sense.  Membership of \(\psi_{\delta_n}\) in the critical interval is equivalent to this statistic lying between
\[
 \frac{\widehat a_n}{a_n}\widehat q_n(\gamma/2)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{\widehat a_n}{a_n}\widehat q_n(1-\gamma/2).
\]
Equations (4) and (8), strict quantile continuity, and the uniform distributional approximation give coverage \(1-\gamma+o(1)\).  The identical argument with the stable quantiles, together with the oracle reduction error \(O(M_n^{-\kappa_-})\), proves stable-branch coverage for \(h_n\geq M_n\to\infty\).  Every displayed bound uses common class constants, establishing the stated branchwise uniformity and accounting separately for score replacement, calibration, normalizer error, anti-concentration, and numerical inversion.  Because the cited Kennedy multiplier-bootstrap guarantee indexes only a fixed compact multiplier set, the critical interval for \(\delta_n\to\infty\) is a strict multiplier-domain extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:ambient-gaussian-efficiency]\label{thm:ambient-gaussian-efficiency}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\) and a sequence with \(h_n\to0\). Along the dominated local paths \(dP_{n,u}/dP=1+u\phi_{\delta_n}/(\sqrt n\sigma_n)\), uniformly for \(u\) in compact sets, the log-likelihood ratio is \(u\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\delta_n}(O_i)/(\sqrt n\sigma_n)-u^2/2+o_P(1)\) and \(\sqrt n\{\psi_{\delta_n}(P_{n,u})-\psi_{\delta_n}(P)\}/\sigma_n=u+o(1)\). Oracle pointwise bound over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\): every estimator regular in this ambient triangular experiment has a convolution factor \(N(0,1)\), and the oracle one-step estimator \(\widetilde\psi_{\delta_n}\) attains the bound. Feasible attainment on \(\mathcal P_{C^2}\): if in addition \(P\in\mathcal P_{C^2}\), then the cross-fitted estimator \(\widehat\psi_{\delta_n}\) is regular in the same pointwise experiment and attains the bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the base law \(P\) and abbreviate \(\delta=\delta_n\), \(\phi=\phi_{\delta_n}\), \(\sigma=\sigma_n\), and \(\lambda=u/(\sqrt n\sigma)\).  The oracle-frontier variance calculation gives \(\sigma^2\asymp\delta^{1-\kappa}\), while \(h_n\to0\) gives
\[
 \|\lambda\phi\|_\infty\leq C|u|\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\sigma}
 =O(h_n^{\alpha/2})=o(1) \tag{1}
\]
uniformly for \(u\) in compact sets.  Thus \(1+\lambda\phi\) is eventually positive.  Since \(E_P\phi=0\), it integrates to one and defines the dominated probability law \(P_{n,u}\).

Taylor expansion, (1), and \(E\phi^2=\sigma^2\) give
\[
\begin{split}
 \log\frac{dP_{n,u}^{\,n}}{dP^n}
 &=\lambda\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(O_i)-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(O_i)^2+o_P(1).
\end{split} \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the absolute cubic remainder is at most \(C\|\lambda\phi\|_\infty\lambda^2\sum_i\phi_i^2=o_P(1)\).  Moreover
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{n\sigma^2}\sum_i\phi_i^2\right)
 \leq\frac{E\phi^4}{n\sigma^4}
 \leq\frac{\|\phi\|_\infty^2}{n\sigma^2}=o(1), \tag{3}
\]
and the Gaussian branch of the oracle frontier gives
\(Z_n=\sum_i\phi_i/(\sqrt n\sigma)\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).  Substitution in (2) proves the uniform LAN expansion
\[
 \log\frac{dP_{n,u}^{\,n}}{dP^n}=uZ_n-u^2/2+o_P(1). \tag{4}
\]

It remains to verify the derivative of the moving functional rather than import a fixed-\(\delta\) calculation.  Put
\[
 w=\delta A+1-A,\quad D=E(w\mid X),\quad m=\frac{E(wY\mid X)}D,
\]
\[
 s_X=E(\phi\mid X)=m-\psi_\delta(P),\quad
 b_X=\frac{E(w\phi\mid X)}D,\quad W_X=\operatorname{Var}(\phi\mid X).
\]
The pointwise identity
\[
 \frac{w(Y-m)}D=\phi-s_X \tag{5}
\]
follows by checking \(A=1\) and \(A=0\) in the displayed oracle score.  Hence
\[
 \frac{E\{w(Y-m)\phi\mid X\}}D=W_X. \tag{6}
\]
Also, direct conditioning on the binary treatment gives
\[
 b_X-s_X=\frac{\delta(\delta-1)\tau(X)p(1-p)}{D^3}. \tag{7}
\]
Under the tilted law, the marginal density of \(X\) is multiplied by \(1+\lambda s_X\), while its incremental weighted conditional mean is
\[
 \frac{m+\lambda E(wY\phi\mid X)/D}{1+\lambda b_X}.
\]
Using (6) to write \(E(wY\phi\mid X)/D=mb_X+W_X\), exact algebra, not merely differentiation, yields
\[
 \psi_\delta(P_{n,u})-\psi_\delta(P)
 =\lambda\sigma^2+\lambda^2E\!\left[\frac{W_X(s_X-b_X)}{1+\lambda b_X}\right]. \tag{8}
\]
Bounded outcomes imply \(|b_X|+|s_X-b_X|\leq C\delta\), and \(EW_X\leq\sigma^2\).  By (1), the denominator in (8) is bounded away from zero, so the remainder divided by \(\sigma/\sqrt n\) is bounded by
\[
 C\frac{\delta}{\sqrt n\sigma}=o(1). \tag{9}
\]
Consequently, uniformly for compact \(u\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\psi_{\delta_n}(P_{n,u})-\psi_{\delta_n}(P)\}}{\sigma_n}=u+o(1). \tag{10}
\]

Equations (4) and (10) form a scalar LAN experiment with unit information and local parameter equal to the normalized target displacement.  The cited scalar LAN convolution theorem implies that every estimator regular in this experiment has a limiting law \(N(0,1)*M\) for some probability law \(M\).  For the oracle one-step estimator,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n(\widetilde\psi_{\delta_n}-\psi_{\delta_n}(P))}{\sigma_n}=Z_n.
\]
Under \(P_{n,u}\), (4) and direct exponential tilting give \(Z_n\Rightarrow N(u,1)\); subtracting the target shift (10) gives \(N(0,1)\).  Hence the oracle estimator is regular and attains the convolution bound.

If the base law also belongs to \(\mathcal P_{C^2}\), cross-fit oracle equivalence gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n(\widehat\psi_{\delta_n}-\widetilde\psi_{\delta_n})}{\sigma_n}=o_P(1). \tag{11}
\]
LAN implies contiguity of \(P_{n,u}^n\) and \(P^n\) for fixed \(u\), so (11) remains true under every local alternative.  Combining (10)--(11) with the tilted limit of \(Z_n\) proves that the cross-fitted estimator is regular with centered limit \(N(0,1)\), and therefore attains the same pointwise ambient bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A moving functional requires its own local experiment; the result certifies that the Gaussian normalization is an efficiency frontier rather than a convenient CLT scale. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy2019Incremental gives a fixed-functional efficiency bound, and KhanTamer2010Irregular diagnoses inverse-weight irregularity without a drifting IPSI tangent experiment. \textit{Consumer.} It provides the benchmark against which alternative regular estimators for moderate but increasing odds shifts can be compared.

\begin{proposition}[prop:fixed-multiplier-reduction]\label{prop:fixed-multiplier-reduction}
If \(\delta_n\equiv\delta_0\in(0,\infty)\) and \(E_P[\phi_{\delta_0}(O)^2]\in(0,\infty)\), then the target and score reduce to Kennedy's point-treatment incremental mean and efficient influence function, and the Gaussian interval reduces to the pointwise version of Kennedy's fixed-compact multiplier procedure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
D_0(x):=1+(\delta_0-1)e(x)=\delta_0e(x)+1-e(x).
\]
The declared ranges \(e(x)\in(0,1]\) and \(\delta_0\in(0,\infty)\) imply
\[
D_0(x)=e(x)\delta_0+\{1-e(x)\}\cdot 1
\geq \min\{\delta_0,1\}>0.
\]
Thus all divisions by \(D_0\) below are well defined.  By
\(\texttt{def:incremental-intervention}\), the identity
\(\tau=\mu_1-\mu_0\), and the restriction \(\delta_n\equiv\delta_0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\delta_0}(x)
&=\mu_0(x)+
  \frac{\delta_0e(x)\{\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)\}}{D_0(x)}\\
&=\frac{D_0(x)\mu_0(x)
  +\delta_0e(x)\{\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)\}}{D_0(x)}\\
&=\frac{\delta_0e(x)\mu_1(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\mu_0(x)}
        {\delta_0e(x)+1-e(x)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bounded ranges of \(\mu_0\) and \(\mu_1\) make the last display
integrable.  Taking \(P\)-expectations and using the definition of
\(\psi_{\delta_0}(P)\) therefore gives
\[
\psi_{\delta_0}(P)
=E_P\!\left[
\frac{\delta_0e(X)\mu_1(X)+\{1-e(X)\}\mu_0(X)}
     {\delta_0e(X)+1-e(X)}
\right].
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:kennedy-point-treatment-mean}\), this is Kennedy's
point-treatment incremental-mean functional after the substitutions
\(\pi=e\) and \(\mu(\cdot,a)=\mu_a\).  This argument proves equality of
the two observed-law identifying functionals; it does not redefine the
causal target.

We next compare the scores.  Let \(\varphi_a^{\mathrm K}\) be the two
treatment-specific uncentered terms appearing in
\(\texttt{lem:kennedy-point-treatment-eif}\).  Since \(e(X)>0\),
\[
e(X)\varphi_1^{\mathrm K}(O)
=A\{Y-\mu_1(X)\}+e(X)\mu_1(X).
\]
On the event \(\{e(X)<1\}\), direct cancellation similarly gives
\[
\{1-e(X)\}\varphi_0^{\mathrm K}(O)
=(1-A)\{Y-\mu_0(X)\}+\{1-e(X)\}\mu_0(X).
\]
On \(\{e(X)=1\}\), the defining equality
\(e(X)=P(A=1\mid X)\) implies \(A=1\) almost surely, conditional on
\(X\), because
\[
P\{A=0,e(X)=1\}
=E_P\!\left[\mathbf 1\{e(X)=1\}P(A=0\mid X)\right]=0.
\]
Hence the right-hand side of the preceding product identity is zero
almost surely on \(\{e(X)=1\}\), which agrees with the boundary-product
interpretation stipulated in
\(\texttt{lem:kennedy-point-treatment-eif}\).  The two product identities
therefore hold \(P\)-almost surely on the whole support.

Substitution into the cited Kennedy influence function yields, almost
surely,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_{\delta_0}^{\mathrm K}(O)
&=\frac{\delta_0e(X)\varphi_1^{\mathrm K}(O)
       +\{1-e(X)\}\varphi_0^{\mathrm K}(O)}{D_0(X)}
  +\frac{\delta_0\tau(X)\{A-e(X)\}}{D_0(X)^2}
  -\psi_{\delta_0}(P)\\
&=\frac{A\delta_0\{Y-\mu_1(X)\}}{D_0(X)}
  +\frac{(1-A)\{Y-\mu_0(X)\}}{D_0(X)}\\
&\quad
  +\frac{\delta_0e(X)\mu_1(X)+\{1-e(X)\}\mu_0(X)}{D_0(X)}
  +\frac{\delta_0\tau(X)\{A-e(X)\}}{D_0(X)^2}
  -\psi_{\delta_0}(P)\\
&=\frac{A\delta_0\{Y-\mu_1(X)\}}{D_0(X)}
  +\frac{(1-A)\{Y-\mu_0(X)\}}{D_0(X)}
  +\frac{\delta_0\tau(X)\{A-e(X)\}}{D_0(X)^2}\\
&\quad +m_{\delta_0}(X)-\psi_{\delta_0}(P)\\
&=\phi_{\delta_0}(O).
\end{aligned}
\]
The third equality uses the proved formula for \(m_{\delta_0}\), and the
last equality is exactly \(\texttt{def:oracle-score}\).  Thus the oracle
score, including its propensity-path term, equals Kennedy's efficient
influence function.  The variance premise consequently gives the common,
nondegenerate scale
\[
0<E_P\!\left[\phi_{\delta_0}(O)^2\right]
=E_P\!\left[\{\phi_{\delta_0}^{\mathrm K}(O)\}^2\right]<\infty.
\]

It remains to compare the feasible maps.  The preceding calculation is
an algebraic identity for any triple of fitted functions, provided
weighted propensity products at zero or one are evaluated in their
cancelled forms.  For every held-out observation, put
\[
\widehat D_i=1+(\delta_0-1)\widehat e_i.
\]
The clipping in \(\texttt{def:local-linear-one-step}\) gives
\(\widehat e_i\in[0,1]\), and hence
\(\widehat D_i\geq\min\{1,\delta_0\}>0\).  Applying the same cancellation
with \((e,\mu_0,\mu_1)\) replaced by
\((\widehat e_i,\widehat\mu_{0,i},\widehat\mu_{1,i})\) shows that the
displayed \(\widehat\varphi_{\delta_0,i}\) in
\(\texttt{def:local-linear-one-step}\) is precisely the corresponding
held-out fitted uncentered Kennedy score.  Therefore
\[
\widehat\psi_{\delta_0}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat\varphi_{\delta_0,i}
\]
is the sample-split estimator described in
\(\texttt{lem:kennedy-pointwise-gaussian-procedure}\).  By
\(\texttt{def:crossfit-score-variance}\), its variance estimate is
\[
\widehat\sigma_n^2
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \{\widehat\varphi_{\delta_0,i}-\widehat\psi_{\delta_0}\}^2,
\]
which is exactly the empirical centered second moment in that procedure.

Finally, \(\texttt{def:branchwise-intervals}\) specializes to
\[
C_n^{\mathrm G}(\gamma)
=\left[
\widehat\psi_{\delta_0}
-z_{1-\gamma/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_n}{\sqrt n},\;
\widehat\psi_{\delta_0}
+z_{1-\gamma/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_n}{\sqrt n}
\right].
\]
This is the pointwise Gaussian interval obtained by evaluating the
fixed-compact-multiplier construction at \(\delta_0\).  At
\(\gamma=0.05\), \(z_{1-\gamma/2}=z_{0.975}\), conventionally rounded to
\(1.96\), so the display is exactly the interval recorded in
\(\texttt{lem:kennedy-pointwise-gaussian-procedure}\).  The conclusion is
an equality of target, score, estimator, variance, and interval formulas;
it makes no additional coverage assertion without the weak-convergence
and nuisance-rate conditions invoked by Kennedy's Gaussian theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction is the required sanity check and prevents the hardening theory from changing the established estimand at ordinary multipliers. \textit{Gap.} This is a compatibility corollary rather than a novelty claim; it pins the new notation to Kennedy2019Incremental exactly. \textit{Consumer.} Existing bounded-multiplier analyses retain their familiar pointwise Gaussian interpretation.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:transition-adaptation]\label{oeq:transition-adaptation}
Can a single data-driven confidence set based on the transition-experiment and ordered-modulus handle cover \(\psi_{\delta_n}\) uniformly over local sequences approaching both hardening boundaries, with oracle-order expected length on each separated branch; or must every honest procedure pay a logarithmic length factor determined by simultaneous uncertainty in \(\kappa\), \(\rho\), and \(H\)?
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Separated-regime validity does not answer what an analyst should do when the estimated normalizer places the multiplier near a transition. \textit{Gap.} HeilerKazak2021Irregular adapts between regular and stable laws for a fixed target, whereas this problem has two transitions, a moving target, and an intermediate marked family. \textit{Consumer.} A resolution would permit one automatic hardening diagnostic and interval in software without user-supplied regime labels.

\begin{lemma}[lem:levy-continuity]\label{lem:levy-continuity}
If probability measures on \(\mathbb R\) have characteristic functions converging pointwise to a characteristic function that is continuous at zero, then the probability measures converge weakly to the law having that limiting characteristic function.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:bernstein-bounded-sums]\label{lem:bernstein-bounded-sums}
Let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) be independent, centered random variables satisfying \(|Z_j|\leq M\), and put \(v=\sum_{j=1}^m E Z_j^2\).  For every \(x>0\),
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^m Z_j\right|\geq \sqrt{2vx}+\frac{Mx}{3}\right)\leq 2e^{-x}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:integrable-characteristic-inversion]\label{lem:integrable-characteristic-inversion}
If the characteristic function \(\chi\) of a probability law on \(\mathbb R\) is integrable, then that law has a bounded continuous density
\[
 f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-itx}\chi(t)\,dt.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:scalar-lan-convolution]\label{lem:scalar-lan-convolution}
In a scalar locally asymptotically normal experiment with unit information, the weak limit of every regular estimator of the local parameter is the convolution of \(N(0,1)\) with a probability law; an estimator whose limit is \(N(0,1)\) attains the convolution bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:kennedy-fixed-compact-scope]\label{lem:kennedy-fixed-compact-scope}
Kennedy's Gaussian-process and multiplier-bootstrap results for incremental propensity-score effects index the odds multiplier by a fixed compact set; those results do not cover a sequence of multipliers \(\delta_n\to\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:kennedy-point-treatment-mean]\label{lem:kennedy-point-treatment-mean}
For a single binary treatment and a fixed multiplier \(\delta_0>0\), Kennedy's point-treatment incremental mean, after the notational substitutions \(\pi=e\) and \(\mu(\cdot,a)=\mu_a\), is
\[
E_P\!\left[
\frac{\delta_0e(X)\mu_1(X)+\{1-e(X)\}\mu_0(X)}
{\delta_0e(X)+1-e(X)}
\right].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:kennedy-point-treatment-eif]\label{lem:kennedy-point-treatment-eif}
For a single binary treatment and a fixed multiplier \(\delta_0>0\), define \(D_0(X)=\delta_0e(X)+1-e(X)\), \(\varphi_1^{\mathrm K}(O)=A\{Y-\mu_1(X)\}/e(X)+\mu_1(X)\), and \(\varphi_0^{\mathrm K}(O)=(1-A)\{Y-\mu_0(X)\}/\{1-e(X)\}+\mu_0(X)\), with weighted products interpreted at propensity boundaries.  Kennedy's point-treatment efficient influence function is
\[
\frac{\delta_0e(X)\varphi_1^{\mathrm K}(O)+\{1-e(X)\}\varphi_0^{\mathrm K}(O)}{D_0(X)}
+\frac{\delta_0\tau(X)\{A-e(X)\}}{D_0(X)^2}-\psi_{\delta_0}(P).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:kennedy-pointwise-gaussian-procedure]\label{lem:kennedy-pointwise-gaussian-procedure}
For a multiplier in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), Kennedy's sample-split estimator averages held-out fitted uncentered influence-function values and estimates variance by their empirical second moment centered at the estimator.  Denote the resulting estimator and standard-error scale at a fixed multiplier \(\delta_0\) by \(\widehat\psi^{\mathrm K}(\delta_0)\) and \(\widehat\sigma^{\mathrm K}(\delta_0)\), respectively.  Under the conditions of his weak-convergence theorem, the pointwise ninety-five percent interval is
\[
\left[
\widehat\psi^{\mathrm K}(\delta_0)-1.96\frac{\widehat\sigma^{\mathrm K}(\delta_0)}{\sqrt n},\;
\widehat\psi^{\mathrm K}(\delta_0)+1.96\frac{\widehat\sigma^{\mathrm K}(\delta_0)}{\sqrt n}
\right].
\]
\end{lemma}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The branchwise theorem deliberately excludes automatic classification on local sequences at the two transition boundaries. The critical Lévy inversion must carry deterministic quadrature, frequency-truncation, spatial-tail, and aliasing certificates; numerical grid stability alone is not counted as proof. Theorem 4 is pointwise in the full dominated observed-law experiment: its oracle convolution bound applies over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\), its feasible-attainment clause applies only at base laws in \(\mathcal P_{C^2}\), and neither clause is a minimax claim within the smaller \(C^2\) tail class.

\section{Honest scope}

The oracle jump map, score-bias algebra, hard-endpoint reduction, local-linear rate route, empirical score-variance route, and smoothing inequality are verified presolve evidence, not established results. The main open proof is Theorem 3: uniform plug-in quantile coverage after score and variance replacement, random tail-shell estimation, logarithmically sensitive normalizer calibration, joint-mark estimation, and certified inversion. Theorem 4 gives an oracle pointwise ambient convolution bound over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RV}}\); feasible attainment is limited to \(\mathcal P_{C^2}\). The fully adaptive transition problem remains Open-ended Question 1. Fixed multipliers reduce to Kennedy's result, and neither endpoint \(\kappa=0\) nor \(\kappa=1\), unbounded outcomes, longitudinal treatment, or simultaneous bands over an unbounded multiplier curve are claimed.
