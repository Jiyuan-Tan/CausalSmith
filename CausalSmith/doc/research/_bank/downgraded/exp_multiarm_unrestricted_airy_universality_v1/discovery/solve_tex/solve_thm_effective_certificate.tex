%%% FIELD def-risk-game-construction
For binary schedules,
\[
R_n(c)=R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c)
=\inf_{\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau}
\max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}
\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;y).
\]
For finite real numbers \(L<U\), a fixed bounded schedule \(x=(x_{i,a})\in[L,U]^{n\times K}\), and an outcome-independent assignment law \(\mathcal D_n\), define
\[
R_n^{[L,U]}(c)
=\inf_{\mathcal D_n,\delta}
\sup_{x\in[L,U]^{n\times K}}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,\delta}
\left[
\left\{
\delta\bigl(Z,(x_{i,Z_i})_{i=1}^n\bigr)
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_ax_{i,a}
\right\}^{2}
\right],
\]
where \(\delta\) ranges over all Markov kernels from the full labeled observed data \((Z_i,x_{i,Z_i})_{i=1}^n\) to \(\mathbb R\).  Thus the schedule is fixed, the design is chosen without observing it, and the expectation is only over assignment and any estimator randomization.

%%% FIELD prop-bounded-statement
For every fixed \(K\ge2\), every \(c\in\mathcal C_K\), and every finite pair \(L<U\),
\[
R_n^{[L,U]}(c)=(U-L)^2R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c).
\]
The equality holds at every \(n\), with the same unrestricted class of outcome-independent possibly dependent designs and possibly randomized full-label estimators.  Its upper inequality is attained, pair by pair, by normalized conditional Bernoulliization followed by Rao--Blackwellization; its lower inequality uses the binary endpoint schedules.  Hence inactive-arm allocations and every zero-count face are retained rather than removed by the reduction.

%%% FIELD prop-bounded-proof
Put \(D=U-L>0\).  We prove the two inequalities without changing the design class.

First fix an admissible binary design-estimator pair \((\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau)\).  Randomization internal to \(\widehat\tau\) is included in the expectation below.  For a labeled bounded observation \((z,o)\), where \(o_i\in[L,U]\), let \(W_1,\ldots,W_n\), conditionally on \((z,o)\), be independent with
\[
\Pr(W_i=1\mid z,o)=\frac{o_i-L}{D}.
\]
Define the total full-label bounded rule
\[
\delta^{[L,U]}(z,o)
=D\,\mathbb E\!\left[\widehat\tau(z,W)\mid z,o\right].
\tag{1}
\]
The right side is a finite sum over \(\{0,1\}^n\), including integration over any randomization of \(\widehat\tau\), so (1) is a measurable function of the actually observed labels and bounded outcomes.  The auxiliary Bernoulli variables are integrated out; they are not an additional source of randomness in the implemented experiment.

Fix a bounded schedule \(x\in[L,U]^{n\times K}\) and set
\[
p_{i,a}=\frac{x_{i,a}-L}{D}\in[0,1].
\]
On an auxiliary probability space generate all \(W_{i,a}\) independently with \(W_{i,a}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{i,a})\), independently of \(Z\sim\mathcal D_n\).  Because \(\sum_a c_a=0\),
\[
\mathbb E_W\!\left[\tau_c(W)\right]
=\frac1n\sum_{i,a}c_ap_{i,a}
=\frac{1}{D n}\sum_{i,a}c_a(x_{i,a}-L)
=\frac{1}{D n}\sum_{i,a}c_ax_{i,a}.
\tag{2}
\]
Outcome independence of the design gives \(\mathbb E_W[\tau_c(W)\mid Z]=\mathbb E_W[\tau_c(W)]\).  Moreover, conditional on \(Z=z\), the law of \((W_{i,z_i})_i\) is exactly the product law used in (1) with \(o_i=x_{i,z_i}\).  Therefore conditional Jensen's inequality, applied to
\(\widehat\tau(Z,(W_{i,Z_i})_i)-\tau_c(W)\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n}
\left[
\left\{
D^{-1}\delta^{[L,U]}\bigl(Z,(x_{i,Z_i})_i\bigr)
-D^{-1}n^{-1}\sum_{i,a}c_ax_{i,a}
\right\}^{2}
\right]\\
&\quad\le
\mathbb E_{W,\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau}
\left[
\left\{
\widehat\tau\bigl(Z,(W_{i,Z_i})_i\bigr)-\tau_c(W)
\right\}^{2}
\right]\\
&\quad=
\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}
\Pr(W=w)\,
\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;w)\\
&\quad\le
\max_{w\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}
\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Multiplying by \(D^2\), taking the supremum over \(x\), and then the infimum over binary pairs proves
\[
R_n^{[L,U]}(c)\le D^2R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c).
\tag{4}
\]

For the reverse inequality, fix any admissible bounded pair \((\mathcal D_n,\delta)\).  Given binary observed outcomes \(b_i\), define
\[
\widetilde\delta(z,b)=D^{-1}\delta\bigl(z,(L+Db_i)_{i=1}^n\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
For every binary schedule \(w\), form the endpoint schedule \(x_{i,a}=L+Dw_{i,a}\).  Again using \(\sum_a c_a=0\),
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i,a}c_ax_{i,a}
=\frac{L}{n}\sum_{i,a}c_a+D\tau_c(w)
=D\tau_c(w).
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently the binary risk of \((\mathcal D_n,\widetilde\delta)\) at \(w\) is exactly \(D^{-2}\) times the bounded risk of \((\mathcal D_n,\delta)\) at the associated endpoint schedule.  Endpoint schedules form a subset of \([L,U]^{n\times K}\), whence
\[
R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c)
\le D^{-2}
\sup_{x\in[L,U]^{n\times K}}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,\delta}
\left[
\left\{
\delta\bigl(Z,(x_{i,Z_i})_i\bigr)
-n^{-1}\sum_{i,a}c_ax_{i,a}
\right\}^{2}
\right].
\tag{7}
\]
Taking the infimum over bounded pairs proves the inequality opposite to (4), hence the equality.  Neither construction changes \(\mathcal D_n\), so arbitrary assignment dependence, inactive-arm support, and missing-active-arm faces remain in the quantified design class.  Equations (1) and (5) also integrate or transport arbitrary estimator randomization, completing the pairwise claim.

%%% FIELD thm-universal-proof
Fix \(K\ge2\) and \(c\in\mathcal C_K\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}upper}\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)\ge\Lambda_c,
\]
whereas \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}lower}\) gives
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)\le\Lambda_c.
\]
The two limits therefore coincide.  Substituting the definitions of \(d_n(c)\), \(\Lambda_c\), and \(\kappa(c)\) yields
\[
R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c)=R_n(c)
=n^{-1}-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
=n^{-1}-C_AV_c(s_c/2)^{2/3}n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
The upper theorem proves attainment by the product allocation \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/2\) on active arms and the total nonlinear estimator \(\delta_n^\star\).  If \(|S_c|=2\), \(\mathrm{prop:two\text{-}arm\text{-}reduction}\) proves that the two active coefficients are \(+1\) and \(-1\), that \(V_c(s_c/2)=1\), and that inactive labels do not affect either the active-only upper construction or the all-count lower bound.  If \(|S_c|\ge3\), the established response-alphabet upper argument and full-dimensional all-count lower argument give the new closure.  Finally, zero sum and nonzero \(\ell^1\)-norm imply \(|S_c|\ge2\), so these cases exhaust \(\mathcal C_K\).

%%% FIELD thm-bounded-universal-statement
For every fixed \(K\ge2\), every \(c\in\mathcal C_K\), and every finite real pair \(L<U\),
\[
R_n^{[L,U]}(c)
=(U-L)^2\left\{
n^{-1}-C_AV_c(s_c/2)^{2/3}n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
\right\}.
\]
The same independent allocation \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/2\) on active arms attains this expansion when paired with the total bounded full-label rule obtained by applying normalized conditional Bernoulliization and Rao--Blackwellization to \(\delta_n^\star\).  The conclusion retains arbitrary outcome-independent dependent competitors, randomized full-label estimators, inactive arms, and all zero-count faces.  The binary kernel is the special case \([L,U]=[0,1]\).

%%% FIELD thm-bounded-universal-proof
Let \(D=U-L>0\).  The exact endpoint reduction \(\mathrm{prop:bounded\text{-}outcome\text{-}reduction}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}airy\text{-}law}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_n^{[L,U]}(c)
&=D^2R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c)\\
&=D^2\left\{
n^{-1}-C_AV_c(s_c/2)^{2/3}n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(D\) is fixed, multiplication by \(D^2\) preserves the little-\(o(n^{-4/3})\) remainder.  The binary upper theorem supplies the design \(\mathcal D_n^\star\) and total estimator \(\delta_n^\star\).  Applying formula (1) in the reduction proof to this pair leaves \(\mathcal D_n^\star\), hence \(q^\star\), unchanged and produces a total bounded observed-data rule.  Its worst bounded-schedule risk is at most \(D^2\) times the binary pair's risk.  Conversely, the exact reduction says that no bounded design-estimator pair can have risk below \(D^2R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c)\).  Thus the transformed pair attains the displayed expansion.  Since the reduction never conditions on positive counts and never restricts the support of a competing design, every stated face and estimator quantifier is retained.

%%% FIELD thm-effective-proof
Fix rational \(c\in\mathcal C_K\cap\mathbb Q^K\) and rational \(\eta>0\).  We describe a finite search, prove that it terminates, and identify the exact witnesses it returns.

By \(\mathrm{lem:response\text{-}gauge\text{-}geometry}\), finite rational linear programming returns exact rational primal and dual optimizers, \(\kappa(c)>0\), \(\ell^\star\), and \(g^\star\).  A rational basis of \(\ker(c^\top)\), nonnegative response-alphabet representations of its signed columns and of \(\pm v\), and every finite maximum used in the tube construction are likewise obtained by rational row reduction and finite linear programming.  When \(|S_c|=2\), \(\mathrm{prop:two\text{-}arm\text{-}reduction}\) additionally certifies \(\kappa(c)=1\); the same enumeration then has the single transverse coordinate required by the frozen construction.

The one-dimensional Airy data admit certified rational enclosures of arbitrary prescribed width.  Concretely, solve the Airy differential equation by its power-series recurrence on successive rational compact intervals, use interval quadrature for the initial integral constants, and bound each Taylor tail by the first omitted majorant series.  The differential equation and interval signs isolate the rightmost negative zero of the derivative of the decaying solution; this zero is simple because at such a zero the equation gives \(A''(x)=xA(x)\ne0\).  Rational bisection therefore terminates at every requested width.  Scaling by the positive rational number \(\kappa(c)\) then gives certified rational enclosures for \(C_A\), \(\Lambda_c\), the feedback \(h_\kappa\), its derivative, and the tail and Lipschitz majorants used in \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}upper}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:all\text{-}count\text{-}bayes\text{-}inequality}\).  Every verification at a fixed requested width is a finite list of rational interval inequalities, so this enclosure procedure terminates.

Choose a rational \(\epsilon_0>0\) with \(8\epsilon_0<\eta\).  Enumerate compact rational piecewise-polynomial functions \(f\) until exact integration together with the Airy enclosure verifies
\[
4\frac{\int_{\mathbb R}|f'(x)|^2\,dx}{\int_{\mathbb R}|f(x)|^2\,dx}
+\kappa(c)\frac{\int_{\mathbb R}|x|f(x)^2\,dx}{\int_{\mathbb R}|f(x)|^2\,dx}
\le \Lambda_c+\epsilon_0.
\tag{8}
\]
Such functions are dense in \(H^1(\mathbb R)\) with finite first absolute moment, and truncation followed by rational spline approximation converges in all three integrals in (8).  The definition of \(C_A\) and the preceding certified enclosure therefore prove termination of the enumeration.  Next choose a positive rational transverse width small enough that its finitely many rational boundary-error coefficients sum to at most \(\epsilon_0\).  The resulting tube probabilities are piecewise polynomial on finitely many rational sign cells, with coefficients in the ordered algebraic field generated by rational numbers and \(n^{-1/3}\).  Exact cell integration therefore makes every mass of the finite schedule prior an element of \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm R}\).

For the upper rule, refine interval evaluations of \(h_\kappa\) at every algebraic score until a rational value within \(n^{-2}\) of it is fixed, and project exactly onto \([-1,1]\).  This gives a finite rational table \(\widetilde\delta_n\).  Since both the target and the reported values lie in \([-1,1]\), changing an output by at most \(n^{-2}\) changes squared loss by at most \(4n^{-2}\).  The explicit finite Taylor remainders in the established upper proof consequently yield a computable monotone threshold above which
\[
\max_y\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n^\star,\widetilde\delta_n;y)
\le n^{-1}-(\Lambda_c-4\epsilon_0)n^{-4/3}.
\tag{9}
\]
Similarly, weak integration by parts on the finite sign-cell partition, the endpoint-vanishing transverse densities, the local/exterior/zero-count decomposition in the established all-count proof, and (8) give a computable monotone threshold above which the selected finite prior \(\nu_n\) obeys
\[
\inf_{r:\,\sum_ar_a=n}\operatorname{BR}_{\nu_n}(r)
\ge n^{-1}-(\Lambda_c+4\epsilon_0)n^{-4/3}.
\tag{10}
\]
All constants in these remainder inequalities are rational or represented real-algebraic numbers already enclosed by rational intervals.  Each sufficient condition is an inequality between an explicit constant and a negative rational power of \(n\).  Sequential integer search thus finds \(N\) after which every condition in (9)--(10) holds.  Increasing \(N\) absorbs the \(4n^{-2}\) rounding term.

It remains to define exact endpoints, rather than merely enclosed ones.  Let \(\nu_n(y)\in\mathbb A_{\mathrm R}\) be the exact mass of schedule \(y\) under the selected tube prior.  For a fixed assignment \(z\), let \(O_z(y)\) denote its observed binary string and put
\[
D_{z,o}=\sum_{y:O_z(y)=o}\nu_n(y).
\]
The exact posterior Bayes risk is
\[
\operatorname{BR}_{\nu_n}(z)
=\sum_y\nu_n(y)\tau_c(y)^2
-\sum_{o:D_{z,o}>0}
\frac{\left\{\sum_{y:O_z(y)=o}\nu_n(y)\tau_c(y)\right\}^2}{D_{z,o}}.
\tag{11}
\]
Define
\[
B_n=\min_{z\in\mathcal A_K^n}\operatorname{BR}_{\nu_n}(z).
\tag{12}
\]
For any outcome-independent assignment law, the posterior risk is the assignment-law average of (11), hence is at least (12).  Thus \(B_n\le R_n(c)\), uniformly over dependent laws and allocation faces.  Define
\[
U_n=\max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal A_K^n}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^nq_{z_i}^\star\right)
\left\{\widetilde\delta_n(z,O_z(y))-\tau_c(y)\right\}^2.
\tag{13}
\]
This is the exact worst-schedule risk of one admissible design-estimator pair, so \(R_n(c)\le U_n\).  The sets in (11)--(13) are finite.  Arithmetic and order comparisons in \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm R}\) terminate by the specified square-free-polynomial, isolating-interval, Thom-sign, resultant, and Sturm-sequence representation.  Hence \(B_n,U_n\) are exact represented algebraic numbers.  From (9)--(10), after the stated enlargement of \(N\),
\[
B_n\le R_n(c)\le U_n,
\qquad
n^{4/3}(U_n-B_n)\le8\epsilon_0<\eta
\quad(n\ge N).
\tag{14}
\]
The finite gauge data, Airy interval certificates, tube and estimator schemas, monotone inequalities, and finite algorithms (11)--(13) form \(\Gamma\).  For each supplied integer \(n\ge N\), every loop ranges over a displayed finite set and every algebraic operation terminates, so the encoded evaluator is total.  This proves the asserted binary certificate without a complexity claim.

We finally prove the real-contrast transfer.  Let \(c,c'\) lie in the same sign chamber and satisfy both normalizations, and put \(u=c-c'\).  Then \(\sum_a u_a=0\).  For any binary vector \(t\), if \(P=\{a:u_a>0\}\),
\[
u^\top t\le\sum_{a\in P}u_a=\frac12\lVert u\rVert_1,
\]
and the same inequality applied to \(-u\) proves
\[
|u^\top t|\le\frac12\lVert u\rVert_1=\omega(c,c').
\tag{15}
\]
For any fixed design-estimator pair, Minkowski's inequality in \(L^2\), (15), and the supremum over schedules bound the change in root worst-case risk by \(\omega(c,c')\).  Apply this to arbitrarily near-optimal pairs in both directions and let their optimization errors decrease to zero.  This yields
\[
\left|\sqrt{R_n(c)}-\sqrt{R_n(c')}\right|
\le\omega(c,c').
\tag{16}
\]
Normalization-preserving rational contrasts with fixed support and signs are dense because the positive magnitudes and negative magnitudes lie in relative interiors of rational simplices, each of mass one.  In the support-two case those simplices are singletons, so the active contrast is already rational.

Within one fixed sign chamber the response columns and target direction are fixed, and the gauge value is the minimum over finitely many basic feasible solutions of quadratic polynomials in \(c\).  Hence \(\kappa(c)\), and therefore \(\Lambda_c\), is locally Lipschitz.  On a sufficiently small compact neighborhood of a fixed \(c\), all active magnitudes and \(\kappa(c)\) are bounded away from zero; one may choose a continuous local basis of \(\ker(c^\top)\), and the finitely many primal masses, response representations, inverse-basis entries, and upper and lower remainder constants are uniformly bounded.  Thus the established expansion is uniform on that neighborhood.

Choose rational \(c^{(n)}\) in the same chamber with \(e_n=\omega(c,c^{(n)})=o(n^{-5/6})\).  Under \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/2\), the unshrunk score contribution has absolute value at most one, is unbiased by zero sum, and is independent across units; its average therefore has risk at most \(n^{-1}\).  Hence \(R_n(c)\le n^{-1}\) for every \(c\in\mathcal C_K\).  Combining this bound with (16) gives
\[
|R_n(c)-R_n(c^{(n)})|
\le e_n\{2n^{-1/2}+e_n\}
=o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{17}
\]
The locally uniform rational-contrast expansion and \(\Lambda_{c^{(n)}}\to\Lambda_c\) transfer the coefficient and risk expansion to the real contrast \(c\).  This is a mathematical density argument; it does not supply a Turing evaluator for an unrepresented real input.

%%% FIELD thm-bounded-certificate-statement
Let \(c\in\mathcal C_K\cap\mathbb Q^K\), \(\eta\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), and \(L,U\in\mathbb Q\) with \(L<U\).  There is a terminating finite augmentation of \(\mathsf{Cert}\) that calls the binary certificate at tolerance \(\eta/(U-L)^2\), stores \(L,U\), and returns a total evaluator whose exact output at every integer \(n\ge N\) is
\[
\bigl((U-L)^2B_n,(U-L)^2U_n\bigr)\in\mathbb A_{\mathrm R}^2.
\]
These values satisfy
\[
(U-L)^2B_n\le R_n^{[L,U]}(c)\le (U-L)^2U_n,
\qquad
n^{4/3}(U-L)^2(U_n-B_n)\le\eta.
\]
The lower endpoint is the exact Bayes risk of the endpoint-scaled finite-schedule prior, and the upper endpoint is the exact scaled binary risk witness for the Bernoulliized, Rao--Blackwellized total full-label rule under the same allocation \(q^\star\).  The mathematical expansion and coefficient extend to every real \(c\in\mathcal C_K\) and every finite real \(L<U\), but no Turing evaluator is asserted for an unrepresented real contrast or endpoint.

%%% FIELD thm-bounded-certificate-proof
Put \(D=U-L\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) and run \(\mathsf{Cert}(c,\eta/D^2)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:effective\text{-}certificate}\), this terminates and returns \(N\), a finite record, and exact values \((B_n,U_n)\in\mathbb A_{\mathrm R}^2\) for every \(n\ge N\), with
\[
B_n\le R_n^{\{0,1\}}(c)\le U_n,
\qquad
n^{4/3}(U_n-B_n)\le\eta/D^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(D^2\) is a positive rational number, multiplication by \(D^2\) is an exact field operation in \(\mathbb A_{\mathrm R}\).  The endpoint map \(w_{i,a}\mapsto L+Dw_{i,a}\) sends the binary finite-schedule prior witnessing \(B_n\) to a prior supported on \(\{L,U\}^{n\times K}\subset[L,U]^{n\times K}\).  Equation (6) in the endpoint reduction proves that its target is exactly \(D\tau_c(w)\); posterior conditional means and posterior squared errors therefore scale by \(D\) and \(D^2\), respectively.  Its exact Bayes risk is consequently \(D^2B_n\), which is a valid lower witness for the bounded game.

For the upper witness, apply formula (1) in the reduction proof to the exact binary rule whose worst-schedule risk is \(U_n\).  Inequality (3) proves, for every bounded schedule, that the resulting total observed-data rule under the unchanged design has risk at most \(D^2U_n\).  Thus the exact algebraic number \(D^2U_n\) is an upper risk witness.  Multiplying (18) by \(D^2\) and invoking the exact identity in \(\mathrm{prop:bounded\text{-}outcome\text{-}reduction}\) gives
\[
D^2B_n\le R_n^{[L,U]}(c)\le D^2U_n,
\qquad
n^{4/3}D^2(U_n-B_n)\le\eta.
\]
Appending the rational pair \((L,U)\) and the instruction to multiply both evaluator outputs by \(D^2\) is a finite operation, and the resulting evaluator is total because the binary evaluator is total.  The real-contrast statement follows either by multiplying the binary expansion from \(\mathrm{thm:universal\text{-}airy\text{-}law}\) by \(D^2\), or directly from \(\mathrm{thm:bounded\text{-}universal\text{-}airy\text{-}law}\).  The root-risk modulus scales to \(D\omega(c,c')\) by the exact reduction, so the same \(o(n^{-5/6})\) rational bracketing transfers the coefficient for each fixed real \(D>0\).  This last argument is mathematical and does not encode an unrepresented real input.
