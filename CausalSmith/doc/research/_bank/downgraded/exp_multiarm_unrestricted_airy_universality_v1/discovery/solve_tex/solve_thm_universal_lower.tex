%%% FIELD proof-universal-upper
Fix a schedule \(y\), and put
\[
t_i=(Y_i(a))_{a\in S_c},\qquad
\theta_i=c^\top t_i,\qquad
u_i=(g^\star)^\top b_{t_i}.
\]
The zero-sum assumption gives \(c^\top t_i=c^\top b_{t_i}\), and hence
\(\tau_c(y)=n^{-1}\sum_i\theta_i\).  The normalization gives
\[
\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a^\star=\frac12\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|=1.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal D_n^\star\) is a probability law and puts probability zero on every inactive arm.  Moreover \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/2>0\) for each active arm, so every division below is justified.

Under this product design, the score definitions give
\[
\mathbb ET_i
=\sum_{a\in S_c}q_a^\star
\frac{c_a(t_{i,a}-1/2)}{q_a^\star}
=c^\top b_{t_i}=\theta_i,
\qquad
\mathbb EU_i
=\sum_{a\in S_c}(g_a^\star)(t_{i,a}-1/2)=u_i.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \((Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1/2)^2=1/4\),
\[
\mathbb ET_i^2
=\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_a^2}{4q_a^\star}
=\frac12\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E(T_iU_i)
=\sum_{a\in S_c}\frac{c_ag_a^\star}{4q_a^\star}
=(g^\star)^\top v=1.
\tag{2}
\]
For the last equality, primal--dual equality from
\(\mathrm{lem:response\text{-}gauge\text{-}geometry}\) gives
\((\ell^\star)^\top v=\kappa(c)\), and
\(g^\star=\ell^\star/\kappa(c)\) by
\(\mathrm{def:dual\text{-}score}\).  The same dual constraints imply
\[
|u_i|
=\frac{|(\ell^\star)^\top b_{t_i}|}{\kappa(c)}
\le\frac{\theta_i^2}{\kappa(c)}.
\tag{3}
\]
All variables in (1)--(3) are bounded by constants depending only on the fixed contrast.  This uses fixed \(K\), the finiteness of the response alphabet, and positivity of every active \(q_a^\star\).

Let \(\psi\) and \(h=-2\psi'/\psi\) be supplied by
\(\mathrm{lem:unit\text{-}airy\text{-}feedback\text{-}properties}\).
Its Riccati identity is equivalent in the weak sense to
\[
-4\psi''+|x|\psi=C_A\psi.
\]
For compactly supported smooth \(f\), weak integration by parts gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\{4|f'|^2+(|x|-C_A)f^2\}\,dx
=4\int_{\mathbb R}\psi^2
\left|\left(\frac f\psi\right)'\right|^2dx\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
Approximation extends (4) to the variational domain, while equality holds for \(f=\psi\).  Thus \(\psi\) is the unit-scale minimizer in the definition of \(C_A\).  Writing \(\kappa=\kappa(c)>0\), scaling the variational problem yields
\[
\phi_\kappa(x)=\kappa^{1/6}\psi(\kappa^{1/3}x),
\qquad
h_\kappa(x)=\kappa^{1/3}h(\kappa^{1/3}x).
\]
Therefore
\[
h_\kappa(x)^2-2h_\kappa'(x)
=\kappa|x|-C_A\kappa^{2/3}
=\kappa|x|-\Lambda_c.
\tag{5}
\]
The cited unit-scale bounds and this scaling also give, for a finite constant \(M_c\),
\[
|h_\kappa'(x)|\le\frac{M_c}{1+\sqrt{|x|}},
\tag{6}
\]
and show that \(h_\kappa^2\) and \(h_\kappa'\) are globally Lipschitz.

Set
\[
X_i=T_i-\theta_i,\quad W_i=U_i-u_i,\quad
A_n=\sum_iX_i,\quad x_n=n^{-2/3}\sum_i u_i,\quad
S_n=n^{-2/3}\sum_iU_i.
\]
Independence across units is part of
\(\mathrm{def:independent\text{-}qstar\text{-}design}\), so (1)--(2) imply
\[
\mathbb EA_n^2=\sum_i(1-\theta_i^2).
\tag{7}
\]
For
\[
S_{-i}=x_n+n^{-2/3}\sum_{j\ne i}W_j,
\]
the pair \((X_i,W_i)\) is independent of \(S_{-i}\).  Taylor's formula with Lipschitz derivative, boundedness of the summands, and (2) give
\[
\mathbb E\{X_ih_\kappa(S_n)\}
=n^{-2/3}(1-\theta_iu_i)\mathbb Eh_\kappa'(S_{-i})
+O_c(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{8}
\]
The bounded independent centered variables satisfy
\[
\mathbb E|S_{-i}-x_n|
\le\{\mathbb E(S_{-i}-x_n)^2\}^{1/2}=O_c(n^{-1/6}).
\]
The Lipschitz property of \(h_\kappa'\), followed by summation of (8), yields uniformly in \(y\)
\[
\mathbb E\{A_nh_\kappa(S_n)\}
=n^{1/3}h_\kappa'(x_n)
-n^{-2/3}h_\kappa'(x_n)\sum_i\theta_iu_i
+O_c(n^{1/6}).
\tag{9}
\]
Similarly, the global Lipschitz property of \(h_\kappa^2\) gives
\[
\mathbb Eh_\kappa(S_n)^2
=h_\kappa(x_n)^2+O_c(n^{-1/6}).
\tag{10}
\]

Before projection, expanding squared error and applying (7)--(10) gives
\[
\begin{split}
&\mathbb E\left[
n^{-1}\sum_iT_i-n^{-2/3}h_\kappa(S_n)-\tau_c(y)
\right]^2\\
={}&\frac1n-\frac1{n^2}\sum_i\theta_i^2
+n^{-4/3}\{h_\kappa(x_n)^2-2h_\kappa'(x_n)\}\\
&\quad
+2n^{-7/3}h_\kappa'(x_n)\sum_i\theta_iu_i
+O_c(n^{-3/2}).
\end{split}
\tag{11}
\]
By (3),
\[
\sum_i\theta_i^2
\ge\kappa\left|\sum_i u_i\right|
=\kappa n^{2/3}|x_n|.
\tag{12}
\]
Using (3), (6), (12), and the deterministic bound
\(|x_n|=O_c(n^{1/3})\), one obtains
\[
-n^{-2}\sum_i\theta_i^2
+2n^{-7/3}h_\kappa'(x_n)\sum_i\theta_iu_i
\le-\kappa|x_n|n^{-4/3}+O_c(n^{-3/2}).
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, after the leading negative term is bounded with (12), the possible loss is at most
\[
C_cn^{-5/3}\frac{|x_n|}{1+\sqrt{|x_n|}}
=O_c(n^{-3/2}).
\]

Substituting (5) and (13) into (11) proves
\[
\sup_y\mathbb E\left[
n^{-1}\sum_iT_i-n^{-2/3}h_\kappa(S_n)-\tau_c(y)
\right]^2
\le n^{-1}-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+O_c(n^{-3/2}).
\]
The target lies in \([-1,1]\): zero sum and \(\ell^1\)-normalization imply that the positive coefficients sum to \(1\) and the negative coefficients sum to \(-1\), so every binary unit-level contrast, and hence its average, lies in that interval.  Metric projection onto \([-1,1]\) cannot increase squared error.  Therefore
\[
\sup_y\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n^\star,\delta_n^\star;y)
\le n^{-1}-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
The minimax definition gives
\[
R_n(c)\le
\sup_y\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n^\star,\delta_n^\star;y).
\]
Since \(d_n(c)=n^{-1}-R_n(c)\), rearrangement yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)\ge\Lambda_c.
\]
The argument uses only active labels and therefore remains valid when \(|S_c|=2\) and when \(K-|S_c|\) inactive labels are present.
%%% FIELD proof-universal-lower
Let \(\Pi_n\) be the finite-schedule prior supplied by
\(\mathrm{thm:all\text{-}count\text{-}bayes\text{-}inequality}\).
For any outcome-independent design \(\mathcal D_n\) and randomized estimator
\(\widehat\tau\), the maximum risk dominates its prior average:
\[
\max_y\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;y)
\ge\sum_y\Pi_n(y)\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;y).
\tag{14}
\]
Outcome independence makes the assignment law independent of \(y\).  Condition on the entire assignment vector \(Z=z\).  Conditional Jensen's inequality under squared loss removes auxiliary estimator randomization.  Then posterior optimality of the conditional mean follows from the identity
\[
\mathbb E_{\Pi_n}\!\left[(d-\tau_c(y))^2\mid\mathscr O_z\right]
=\left\{d-\mathbb E_{\Pi_n}[\tau_c(y)\mid\mathscr O_z]\right\}^2
+\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_n}(\tau_c(y)\mid\mathscr O_z)
\]
for every report \(d\) measurable with respect to the labeled observation sigma-field \(\mathscr O_z\).  Consequently,
\[
\sum_y\Pi_n(y)\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;y)
\ge\sum_z\mathcal D_n(z)\operatorname{BR}_{\Pi_n}(r(z))
\ge\inf_{r:\,\sum_ar_a=n}\operatorname{BR}_{\Pi_n}(r).
\tag{15}
\]
The countwise theorem covers every \(r(z)\), including inactive-only and missing-active-arm faces, and its remainder is independent of the design and estimator.  Combining (14)--(15) with
\(\mathrm{thm:all\text{-}count\text{-}bayes\text{-}inequality}\) gives
\[
\max_y\mathcal R_n(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau;y)
\ge n^{-1}-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}-o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
This is the displayed form because the negative of an \(o(n^{-4/3})\) sequence is again \(o(n^{-4/3})\).  Taking the infimum over the design-estimator pair in
\(\mathrm{def:risk\text{-}game}\) proves the lower bound for \(R_n(c)\).  Since
\(d_n(c)=n^{-1}-R_n(c)\), rearrangement yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}d_n(c)\le\Lambda_c.
\]
Nothing in this argument uses \(|S_c|\ge3\).
%%% FIELD proof-two-arm-reduction
Let \(S_c=\{a_1,a_2\}\).  The zero-sum condition gives
\(c_{a_2}=-c_{a_1}\), and the \(\ell^1\)-normalization gives
\[
2|c_{a_1}|=2.
\]
Thus \(|c_{a_1}|=|c_{a_2}|=1\); exchanging labels and, if necessary, changing the common sign identifies the active vector with \((1,-1)\).  The definition of \(q^\star\) therefore assigns probability \(1/2\) to each active label and zero to every inactive label.

Suppose first that the active vector is \((1,-1)\).  Then
\(v=(1/2,-1/2)\).  The response type \(t=(1,0)\) has
\(b_t=v\) and \(\theta_t=1\), so the one-atom representation gives
\[
V_c(v)\le1.
\tag{16}
\]
Conversely, every feasible representation obeys
\[
1=c^\top v=\sum_t\lambda_t\theta_t.
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(\theta_t\in\{-1,0,1\}\).  Therefore
\[
\sum_t\lambda_t\theta_t^2
=\sum_t\lambda_t|\theta_t|
\ge\left|\sum_t\lambda_t\theta_t\right|=1.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(V_c(v)=1\).  Exchanging the active labels or negating \(c\) maps response types bijectively, preserves the squared costs \(\theta_t^2\), and maps \(v\) accordingly, so the same conclusion holds for every support-two contrast.

The upper theorem applies because the product design uses only the two active labels; inactive potential outcomes are irrelevant both to its observations and to the target.  The all-count theorem applies with \(J=2\), hence one transverse vector, and its uniform countwise assertion includes positive exterior allocations, allocations to inactive labels, and a zero count on either active label.  The universal lower theorem therefore applies to every dependent outcome-independent design and every randomized estimator.  Since
\[
\Lambda_c=C_AV_c(v)^{2/3}=C_A,
\]
the upper and lower limits coincide and give
\[
R_n(c)=n^{-1}-C_An^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\]
For \(K=2\), the cited node
\(\mathrm{lem:published\text{-}two\text{-}arm\text{-}airy\text{-}law}\)
gives the identical formula.  Conversely, adding inactive labels cannot improve the lower bound just proved and cannot worsen the explicit upper design, so it leaves the minimax value unchanged to the asserted order.
