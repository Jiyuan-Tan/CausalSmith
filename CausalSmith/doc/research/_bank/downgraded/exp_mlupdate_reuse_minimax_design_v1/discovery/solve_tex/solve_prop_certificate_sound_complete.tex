%%% FIELD proof-milp-equivalence
Fix an integral selection vector \(z\), and put
\[
S_z=\{c\in\mathcal C:z_c=1\}.
\]
The budget constraint in \(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}}\) gives
\(|S_z|=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}z_c\leq k\), hence
\(S_z\in\mathcal P_k\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}feasible\text{-}panels}\).
For fixed \((\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X\), nonnegativity,
normalization, and the inequalities \(b_{\pi xc}\leq z_c\) imply that
\((b_{\pi xc})_{c\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}}\) is a convex
combination supported on \(S_z\cup\{\varnothing\}\).  Because
\(d_{\pi x\varnothing}=1\), every such combination satisfies
\[
\sum_{c\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}}
d_{\pi xc}b_{\pi xc}
\geq
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S_z\}\bigr)
=r_{\pi x}(S_z),                                                    \tag{1}
\]
where the equality is \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\).  Multiplying
(1) by the positive weights \(q_x\), summing over \(x\), and applying the
scenario constraint of \(\mathrm{def{:}panel\text{-}milp}\) yields
\[
v\geq R_\pi(S_z)\qquad(\pi\in\mathcal F),
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
v\geq\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S_z).                              \tag{2}
\]
All sets involved are finite.  Conversely, for each \((\pi,x)\), choose
one cheapest element of \(S_z\cup\{\varnothing\}\), set its
\(b_{\pi xc}\)-coordinate to one, and set all other coordinates to zero.
These choices satisfy normalization and \(b_{\pi xc}\leq z_c\), and they
make (1) an equality for every \((\pi,x)\).  Taking
\[
v=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S_z)
\]
then satisfies every scenario constraint.  Thus the conditional optimum
over \(b\) and \(v\), for the fixed integral vector \(z\), is exactly the
right side of (2).  No integrality constraint on \(b\) is needed.

Integral selection vectors satisfying the budget constraint are in
bijection with \(\mathcal P_k\).  Minimizing the conditional optimum and
using \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}prospective\text{-}loss}\) therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}})
=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S)
=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\overline W(S)
=D^\star.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-certificate-sound-complete
We first establish the monotonicity used by every leaf bound.  The formula
in \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) is independent of the budget.
Thus, for arbitrary \(S\subseteq S'\subseteq\mathcal C\), apply that theorem
with the auxiliary admissible budget \(|\mathcal C|\).  Enlarging the set
over which a minimum is taken gives, for every \((\pi,x)\),
\[
r_{\pi x}(S')\leq r_{\pi x}(S).
\]
Since \(q_x>0\), summation and then maximization preserve the inequality, so
\[
R_\pi(S')\leq R_\pi(S),
\qquad
\overline W(S')\leq\overline W(S).                                    \tag{3}
\]

Suppose \(\mathsf{Check}(S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)=\mathrm{true}\).
By the explicit tests in \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}grammar}\),
the checker has established
\[
S^\star\in\mathcal P_k,
\qquad
\overline W(S^\star)=v^\star                                      \tag{4}
\]
by exact rational recomputation.  For a node \((I,E,O)\), call a subset
\(S\subseteq\mathcal C\) a descendant when
\(I\subseteq S\subseteq I\cup E\).  The checked root has every subset as a
descendant.  If an internal node branches on \(c\in E\), its two checked
children partition its descendants according as \(c\in S\) or
\(c\notin S\).  Induction on depth, together with the check that both
children occur, shows that every subset of \(\mathcal C\), and hence every
feasible panel, reaches a checked leaf.

A leaf satisfying \(|I|>k\) has no feasible descendant because each
descendant contains \(I\).  At every other leaf, the checker has recomputed
\[
\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^\star.                                     \tag{5}
\]
If \(S\) is a feasible descendant of that leaf, then
\(S\subseteq I\cup E\); applying (3) and (5) gives
\[
\overline W(S)\geq\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^\star.                   \tag{6}
\]
Every feasible panel is covered by one of these cases, so (6) holds for
all \(S\in\mathcal P_k\).  Combining it with (4) and the definition of
\(D^\star\) yields
\[
D^\star=v^\star=\overline W(S^\star).
\]
Thus global optimality is derived from the local Boolean checks; it is not
an unverified field of the certificate.

For completeness, fix any rational instance.  The family
\(\mathcal P_k\) is finite and nonempty because it contains
\(\varnothing\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\), every
\(\overline W(S)\) is a maximum of finite rational weighted sums of finite
minima of rational costs.  Hence exact enumeration produces a minimizer
\(S^\star\in\mathcal P_k\) and the rational value
\[
v^\star=\overline W(S^\star)=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\overline W(S).   \tag{7}
\]
Start with the required root and branch on the first element of \(E\) in a
fixed order.  Close a node if \(|I|>k\).  Otherwise continue until
\(E=\varnothing\), and then close it using the optimistic-bound rule: at
such a node, \(I\in\mathcal P_k\), so (7) gives
\[
\overline W(I\cup E)=\overline W(I)\geq v^\star.                      \tag{8}
\]
Each branch reduces \(|E|\) by one.  Therefore every path has length at
most \(|\mathcal C|\), the resulting binary tree is finite, and the checked
branch relations cover every subset.  Equations (7)--(8), together with
the exact root, partition, and child relations, make every checker test
succeed.  All minima, maxima, additions, and comparisons are rational, so
the construction and verification use exact rational arithmetic.  This
proves existence of an accepted finite certificate for every rational
instance, without asserting a polynomial bound on its size.

%%% FIELD proof-np-hardness
Take an instance of maximum \(k\)-coverage with nonempty universe
\(\mathcal X\), \(|\mathcal X|=M\), and subset family
\((B_c)_{c\in\mathcal C}\).  Repeated subsets may be deleted without
changing any attainable union.  Construct a finite population with
\(N=M\) and one unit in every cell, so \(N_x=1\) and \(q_x=1/M\).  Let
\(\mathcal A=\{0,1\}\), let \(a_0=0\), set \(\Gamma=0\), and use one
future policy \(\pi\) satisfying \(\pi(x)=1\) for all \(x\).  For each
subset define the candidate policy
\[
c(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&x\in B_c,\\
0,&x\notin B_c.
\end{cases}
\]
Set every policy score to zero.  This construction writes one action for
each incidence entry and therefore has size polynomial in the input size.

By \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\), action matching and
\(\Gamma=0\) give
\[
d_{\pi xc}=
\begin{cases}
0,&x\in B_c,\\
1,&x\notin B_c.
\end{cases}                                                           \tag{9}
\]
The frozen maximum-coverage lemma states that maximizing the covered
cardinality over panels of size at most \(k\) is NP-hard.  Substituting (9)
into \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) gives, for every feasible
panel \(S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline W(S)
&=\frac1M\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
  \min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr)\\
&=\frac1M\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
  \mathbf 1\!\left\{x\notin\bigcup_{c\in S}B_c\right\}\\
&=1-\frac1M\left|\bigcup_{c\in S}B_c\right|.                         \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently a panel covers at least \(B\) elements if and only if its
width is at most \(1-B/M\).  Thus an algorithm computing \(D^\star\) on
the stated restricted instances would solve maximum \(k\)-coverage through
this polynomial reduction.  This proves the claimed NP-hardness even with
one future policy, equal weights, two actions, and \(\Gamma=0\).

%%% FIELD proof-exact-rational-generator
For every \(S\subseteq\mathcal C\), define the computable rational map
\[
g(S)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr).                     \tag{17}
\]
The indexing sets are finite, \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty, and all weights
and costs are rational.  Hence all minima and maxima in (17) are attained,
and \(g(S)\) is exactly computable by rational arithmetic.  The formula in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) does not depend on the budget.
Applying it with the auxiliary budget \(|\mathcal C|\) gives
\[
g(S)=\overline W(S)\qquad(S\subseteq\mathcal C).                       \tag{18}
\]

Fix total orders on all encoded finite sets.  Enumerate the finite,
nonempty family \(\mathcal P_k\), let \(S^\star\) be its first minimizer,
and set
\[
v^\star=g(S^\star)=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}g(S)=D^\star,               \tag{19}
\]
where the final equality follows from (18) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}prospective\text{-}loss}\).  Construct the branch tree
from \((\varnothing,\mathcal C,\varnothing)\).  At a node \((I,E,O)\),
close the node precisely when
\[
|I|>k
\quad\text{or}\quad
g(I\cup E)\geq v^\star.                                                \tag{20}
\]
If neither condition holds, then \(E\neq\varnothing\).  Indeed, if
\(E=\varnothing\), failure of the first condition makes
\(I\in\mathcal P_k\), whereas failure of the second contradicts (19):
\[
g(I)<v^\star=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}g(S)\leq g(I).                    \tag{21}
\]
Thus the least \(c\in E\) exists.  Attach exactly the two children
\[
(I\cup\{c\},E\setminus\{c\},O),
\qquad
(I,E\setminus\{c\},O\cup\{c\}).                                     \tag{22}
\]
The cardinality of \(E\) decreases by one at every edge, so every path has
length at most \(|\mathcal C|\).  Equation (21) ensures closure by the time
\(E\) is empty.  Hence the construction terminates in a finite binary tree
with at most \(2^{|\mathcal C|+1}-1\) nodes.

Equations (18)--(22) match the root, branching, and leaf grammar in
\(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}grammar}\).  The checker receives only
the rational instance and \((S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)\); it recomputes
\(g(S^\star)\), every set relation in (22), and every leaf value in (20),
so it does not trust the enumeration log or a supplied optimality claim.
All its tests succeed.  The proved certificate soundness theorem then gives
\[
\mathsf{Check}(S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)=\mathrm{true}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline W(S^\star)=v^\star=D^\star.                                 \tag{23}
\]
This constructs the required exact-rational implementation of
\(\mathsf{Handle}\).  It is finite on every rational instance but may be
exponential.

For the bounded audit, if \(|\mathcal C|\leq5\), then
\[
|\mathcal P_k|=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{|\mathcal C|\choose j}\leq2^5=32.       \tag{24}
\]
The generator and exhaustive enumeration both evaluate this same finite
list using (17) without rounding.  By the proved mixed-integer equivalence,
for each fixed integral selection vector the optimum over the assignment
variables is also exactly \(g(S)\).  Thus the three computations agree
panel by panel, and consequently agree on the optimum and the complete set
of minimizing panels.  A common deterministic tie rule selects the same
panel.  This proves the assertion for every three-cell instance with at
most five candidates; the equality itself does not require three cells,
but that restriction is the requested audit class.

For \(\mathsf E_{3,5}\), the exact audit proposition gives, in the order
consisting of the empty panel, five singletons, and ten lexicographically
ordered pairs, the worst-width list
\[
1;\quad
\frac78,\frac58,\frac78,\frac58,1;\quad
\frac12,\frac12,\frac18,\frac78,\frac18,
\frac16,\frac58,\frac12,\frac58,\frac38.                              \tag{25}
\]
Its minimum is \(1/8\), attained exactly by
\(\{c_1,c_4\}\) and \(\{c_2,c_3\}\).  That proposition also verifies
the fixed-selection mixed-integer values and every leaf of
\(\mathcal T_{3,5}\), so
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}})=\frac18,
\qquad
\mathsf{Check}(\{c_1,c_4\},1/8,\mathcal T_{3,5})=\mathrm{true}.        \tag{26}
\]
Thus (25)--(26) are an exact exhaustive early-kill audit.

Now suppose the structural condition \(\mathrm{(SC)}\) holds for some
\(S_0\in\mathcal P_k\).  Multiplying its coordinatewise equalities by
\(q_x>0\), summing over \(x\), and maximizing over \(\pi\) gives
\[
g(S_0)=g(\mathcal C).                                                   \tag{27}
\]
For every \(S\subseteq\mathcal C\), the inclusion
\(S\subseteq\mathcal C\) and monotonicity of a minimum imply
\[
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr)
\geq
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr).
\]
Nonnegative weighted summation and maximization preserve this inequality,
so \(g(S)\geq g(\mathcal C)\).  Since \(S_0\) is feasible, (19) and (27)
therefore give
\[
g(\mathcal C)\leq v^\star\leq g(S_0)=g(\mathcal C),
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad v^\star=g(\mathcal C).                      \tag{28}
\]
At the root, \(I\cup E=\mathcal C\), so (28) makes the second test in
(20) hold with equality.  The generated tree consequently consists only
of the root.  Its incumbent characteristic vector, one rational value, and
the root label have encoding length polynomial in the rational input
length.  The arithmetic values also have polynomial encoding length:
minima and maxima do not enlarge rational representations, and each of the
finitely many products and sums in (17) increases numerator and denominator
bit lengths by at most the sum of the corresponding input bit lengths.

Finally, define
\[
\delta_{\pi x}=
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr).              \tag{29}
\]
Whenever \(\delta_{\pi x}<1\), finiteness of \(\mathcal C\) guarantees an
attaining candidate; choose the first such candidate and let \(H\) be the
union of all choices.  If \(\delta_{\pi x}<1\), the chosen candidate shows
that the minimum over \(H\) is at most \(\delta_{\pi x}\), while
\(H\subseteq\mathcal C\) gives the reverse inequality.  If
\(\delta_{\pi x}=1\), the dummy cost makes both minima equal to one.  Hence
\[
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in H\}\bigr)=\delta_{\pi x}
\qquad\text{for every }(\pi,x).                                       \tag{30}
\]
If \(|H|\leq k\), then \(H\in\mathcal P_k\), and (30) proves
\(\mathrm{(SC)}\) with \(S_0=H\).  Computing (29), its tie-broken
minimizers, and their union takes
\(O(|\mathcal F||\mathcal X||\mathcal C|)\) exact rational comparisons.
The condition can hold with \(k<|\mathcal C|\), for example when some
candidates never attain a coordinatewise minimum, so it is a genuine
restriction on the cost tensor.

The generator above makes no unrestricted polynomial-time claim.  If it
were polynomial-time on all rational inputs, its returned incumbent value
would compute \(D^\star\) in polynomial time.  The proved maximum-coverage
reduction makes that task NP-hard on the stated boundary subclass.  Thus an
unrestricted polynomial-time exact generator would imply
\(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), while this obstruction neither affects finite
termination nor the one-node certificate under \(\mathrm{(SC)}\).

%%% FIELD theorem-certificate-compression-resolution
The answer is affirmative.  After fixing total orders, the
dominance-and-symmetry handle \(\mathsf{Handle}\) admits a deterministic
exact-rational generator that enumerates an incumbent and branches on the
least undecided candidate; it returns a finite certificate
\(\mathsf{Cert}=(S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)\) accepted by the independently
executed checker \(\mathsf{Check}\), and acceptance proves
\(\overline W(S^\star)=v^\star=D^\star\).  On every rational instance with
\(|\mathcal X|=3\) and \(|\mathcal C|\leq5\), this generator, exhaustive
panel enumeration, and all fixed-selection values of
\(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}}\) agree exactly on the optimum and on the full
set of minimizing panels; a common tie rule returns the same panel.  If
there is \(S_0\in\mathcal P_k\) such that
\[
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S_0\}\bigr)
=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr)
\quad\text{for every }(\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X,
\]
then the generated certificate tree consists only of the root and has
encoding length polynomial in the rational input length.  Without this
structural condition the construction still terminates, but its tree can
be exponential.
