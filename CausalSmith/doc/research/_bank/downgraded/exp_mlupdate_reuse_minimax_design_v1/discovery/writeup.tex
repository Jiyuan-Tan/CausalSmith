\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}mlupdate\_\allowbreak{}reuse\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}design}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} One randomized panel of deterministic machine-learning policies must support sharp causal validation of a prespecified update library. The exact finite-\(\Gamma\) nearest-matching-score width is attained by common homogeneous endpoint schedules both in the aggregate mean class and in its source-faithful individual-level Lipschitz realization. Over the actual no-Lipschitz Chen--Oberst class the exact prospective cost is binary, and the two design objectives meet at the explicit finite-library threshold \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\): panelwise widths, optimal values, all optimal panels, the mixed-integer program, and independently checked certificates then coincide. The optimal width is nondecreasing and finitely piecewise linear in \(\Gamma\), and is constant beyond the threshold. This objective-level saturation does not equate the underlying response classes. Simultaneous assignment-only coverage remains the supporting inference result.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} ChenOberst2025 supplies tight monotonicity-only reusable-validation bounds under unit-level performance monotonicity and neutral-action equality, but not the finite-\(\Gamma\) prospective nearest-score identity or the finite-score saturation elbow. AnthonyGoyalGuptaNagarajan2010 supplies the robust scenario nearest-facility skeleton, while generic exact-proof-log work motivates independently checkable optimization records but does not provide the causal cost derivation, the saturation equivalence, or the specialized deterministic rational workflow proved here.

\paragraph{Niche.} The field niche is an exact causal design path over fixed finite policy libraries. A faithful bridge validates the finite-\(\Gamma\) common-schedule width on an individual-level Chen--Oberst Lipschitz subclass; a separate theorem gives the genuine no-Lipschitz binary geometry; and the finite-score saturation theorem proves where the two panel objectives, full minimizer sets, and certificates become identical without asserting equality of response classes.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves common sharp endpoint schedules, attained prospective causal-width identities in the finite-Lipschitz and no-Lipschitz regimes, a nondecreasing finite piecewise-linear optimal-width path with an explicit constant tail, a rational budget-optimal mixed-integer panel formulation, simultaneous randomization-only outer coverage, and an exact deterministic certificate workflow. At and above the computable saturation threshold, literal binary-cost substitution preserves the mixed-integer problem and every exact checker comparison.

\section{Related work}

Chen and Oberst (2025, Introduction, Section 4, Remark 3.1, and Appendix D.1) study reusable validation under bounded unit-level performance monotonicity and unit-level neutral-action equality, without a Lipschitz restriction, and prove tight bounds. The present conditional-mean monotonicity and neutral invariance are relaxations in aggregation, whereas finite \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz smoothness is a refinement; the two unrestricted classes are incomparable. A homogenization bridge proves that the individual-level \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz subclass and aggregate class generate the same mean surfaces. A separate no-Lipschitz theorem recovers the exact Chen--Oberst binary exact-score or neutral matching geometry, and the new finite-score theorem shows that the finite-\(\Gamma\) panel objective reaches that binary objective at a computable threshold over the fixed libraries. This is equality of design objectives and optimal panels, not equality of the underlying classes. Anthony, Goyal, Gupta, and Nagarajan (2010, Section 2 and Theorem 2.2) supply the robust min--max \(k\)-median skeleton; the metric lift is informed by that result and does not claim its approximation theory. Cheung, Gleixner, and Steffy (2017) and Szeider (2026) motivate independently checkable rational proof logs; the present result specializes that goal to the causal action-score tensor through a finite branch generator, exact bounded audit, computable compression condition, and now a binary-cost saturation transfer. The NP-hardness boundary rules out an implied unrestricted polynomial-time guarantee unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\). Causal finite-sample randomization inference and adaptive policy evaluation remain distinct from the fixed-panel optimization question.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mean-monotonicity]\label{ass:mean-monotonicity}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(a\in\mathcal A\setminus\{a_0\}\), and \(0\leq s\leq t\leq1\), \(\mu_{xa}(s)\leq\mu_{xa}(t)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (aggregate cell-mean monotonicity implied by, but not equivalent to, unit-level performance monotonicity, \cite{ChenOberst2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mean-lipschitz]\label{ass:mean-lipschitz}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(a\in\mathcal A\setminus\{a_0\}\), and \(s,t\in[0,1]\), \( |\mu_{xa}(t)-\mu_{xa}(s)|\leq\Gamma|t-s|\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known Lipschitz conditional-mean class adapted to model score, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:neutral-invariance]\label{ass:neutral-invariance}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(s,t\in[0,1]\), \(\mu_{x a_0}(s)=\mu_{x a_0}(t)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (aggregate neutral-action invariance implied by, but not equivalent to, unit-level neutral-action equality, \cite{ChenOberst2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:complete-randomization]\label{ass:complete-randomization}
For a sampled design \(S\neq\varnothing\) and an allocation \((n_c)_{c\in S}\in\mathbb N_{>0}^{S}\) satisfying \(\sum_{c\in S}n_c=N\), conditional on \(S\) and \((n_c)_{c\in S}\), \(Z\) is uniform over assignments satisfying \(\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mathbf1\{Z_i=c\}=n_c\) for every \(c\in S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (multi-arm complete randomization, \cite{CaugheyDafoeLiMiratrix2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:certificate-grammar]\label{def:certificate-grammar}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Specialized rational branch-\allowbreak{}tree certificate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathsf{Cert}=(S^{\star},v^{\star},\mathcal T)\) contains an incumbent \(S^{\star}\in\mathcal P_k\), only a claimed rational incumbent value \(v^{\star}\), and a rooted binary tree \(\mathcal T\). The root is \((\varnothing,\mathcal C,\varnothing)\); every node label \((I,E,O)\) partitions \(\mathcal C\); and every internal node chooses \(c\in E\) and has exactly the two children \((I\cup\{c\},E\setminus\{c\},O)\) and \((I,E\setminus\{c\},O\cup\{c\})\). A leaf is justified either by \(|I|>k\) or by the exact optimistic-descendant bound \(\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^{\star}\). The map \(\mathsf{Check}\) independently recomputes \(\overline W(S^{\star})\) and requires equality to \(v^{\star}\); it verifies incumbent feasibility, the root, every partition, both branch children of every internal node, every leaf justification, and exhaustive branch coverage in exact rational arithmetic. Neither \(D^{\star}\) nor a global-optimality assertion occurs in \(\mathsf{Cert}\).
\end{definition}

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:envelope-feasibility]\label{lem:envelope-feasibility}
For every compatible selected mean table \(y\), each map \(t\mapsto\ell_{xa}(t;y)\) and \(t\mapsto u_{xa}(t;y)\) belongs to the bounded monotone \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz mean class, agrees with every matching anchor, and satisfies \(\ell_{xa}(t;y)\leq u_{xa}(t;y)\) for all \(x,a,t\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  First let \(a\ne a_0\).  For an anchor \(c\in T_{xa}(S)\), put
\[
p_c(t)=y_{cx}-\Gamma(m_c-t)_+,
\qquad q_c(t)=y_{cx}+\Gamma(t-m_c)_+.
\]
The maps \(t\mapsto-(m_c-t)_+\) and \(t\mapsto(t-m_c)_+\) are nondecreasing and one-Lipschitz.  Hence \(p_c\) and \(q_c\) are nondecreasing and \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz.  A finite maximum or minimum preserves these two properties: if all \(f_j\) have the properties, then for \(s\le t\),
\[
\max_j f_j(s)\leq\max_j f_j(t),
\qquad
\left|\max_j f_j(t)-\max_j f_j(s)\right|
\leq\max_j|f_j(t)-f_j(s)|\leq\Gamma|t-s|,
\]
and the same inequalities follow for a minimum by applying the maximum statement to \(-f_j\).  Constants zero and one have Lipschitz constant zero, so the definitions in \(\mathrm{def{:}envelopes}\) show that both envelopes are nondecreasing and \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz.

Compatibility means that there is a response schedule \(\widetilde\mu\in\mathcal M_N\) satisfying \(y_{cx}=\widetilde\mu_{xa}(m_c)\) at every matching anchor.  If \(m_c\geq t\), Lipschitz continuity gives
\[
y_{cx}-\Gamma(m_c-t)\leq\widetilde\mu_{xa}(t)\leq y_{cx},
\]
whereas if \(m_c\leq t\), monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity give
\[
y_{cx}\leq\widetilde\mu_{xa}(t)
\leq y_{cx}+\Gamma(t-m_c).
\]
Thus every term in the lower maximum is at most \(\widetilde\mu_{xa}(t)\), and every term in the upper minimum is at least \(\widetilde\mu_{xa}(t)\).  Since \(0\leq\widetilde\mu_{xa}(t)\leq1\),
\[
0\leq\ell_{xa}(t;y)\leq\widetilde\mu_{xa}(t)
\leq u_{xa}(t;y)\leq1.
\]
At an anchor \(d\in T_{xa}(S)\), the \(d\)-term gives \(\ell_{xa}(m_d;y)\geq y_{dx}\) and \(u_{xa}(m_d;y)\leq y_{dx}\); the preceding sandwich gives the reverse inequalities, so both envelopes equal \(y_{dx}\).

For \(a=a_0\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}neutral\text{-}invariance}\) implies that all matching anchor values are the same.  With the prescribed replacement \(\Gamma=0\), the two envelopes equal this common constant when an anchor exists, and equal the constants zero and one when none exists.  They are therefore bounded, constant, agree with every neutral anchor, and satisfy the claimed order.  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The common-envelope construction is the compatibility step that prevents separately optimized marginal bounds from using different latent responses. \textit{Gap.} ChenOberst2025 proves tight monotonicity bounds; the Lipschitz envelope and its simultaneous common-response closure are not stated there. \textit{Consumer.} It gives trial analysts one coherent response surface for all future model queries rather than unrelated endpoint witnesses.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-common-endpoints]\label{thm:sharp-common-endpoints}
For every \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), every compatible \(y\), and every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), the identified set for \(V_{\mu}(\pi)\) over \(\mathcal M_N\) conditional on \(y\) is exactly \([L_S(\pi;y),U_S(\pi;y)]\). One response schedule \(\mu^{-}\) attains all lower endpoints and one response schedule \(\mu^{+}\) attains all upper endpoints simultaneously over \(\mathcal F\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), a selected table \(y\) compatible with \(\mathcal M_N\), and an array \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\) that generates \(y\).  Let \(\mu\) be its cell-mean surface.  For a nonneutral action and a matching anchor \(c\in T_{xa}(S)\), monotonicity and the Lipschitz inequality in \(\mathrm{def{:}response\text{-}class}\) imply
\[
\mu_{xa}(t)
\geq y_{cx}-\Gamma(m_c-t)_+,
\qquad
\mu_{xa}(t)
\leq y_{cx}+\Gamma(t-m_c)_+.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if \(m_c\geq t\), the first inequality is the Lipschitz bound and the second follows from monotonicity; if \(m_c\leq t\), the roles reverse.  For the neutral action, invariance gives \(\mu_{x a_0}(t)=y_{cx}\) at every matching anchor.  Combining (1) with \(0\leq\mu_{xa}(t)\leq1\), and then taking the maximum of the lower bounds and the minimum of the upper bounds in \(\mathrm{def{:}envelopes}\), gives
\[
\ell_{xa}(t;y)\leq\mu_{xa}(t)\leq u_{xa}(t;y).
\tag{2}
\]
The weights satisfy \(q_x=N_x/N>0\).  Multiplying (2) at \(a=\pi(x)\), \(t=m_\pi\) by \(q_x\), summing over the finite set \(\mathcal X\), and using the definitions of \(L_S\), \(U_S\), and \(V_\mu\), yields
\[
L_S(\pi;y)
\leq V_\mu(\pi)
\leq U_S(\pi;y).
\tag{3}
\]

Define two complete surfaces simultaneously for every cell and action by
\[
\mu^-_{xa}(t)=\ell_{xa}(t;y),
\qquad
\mu^+_{xa}(t)=u_{xa}(t;y).
\tag{4}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}envelope\text{-}feasibility}\), the surfaces in (4) are bounded, monotone and \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz for nonneutral actions, constant for the neutral action, ordered, and equal to every selected anchor.  Homogenize them within cells:
\[
Y_i^-(a,t)=\mu^-_{x_i a}(t),
\qquad
Y_i^+(a,t)=\mu^+_{x_i a}(t).
\tag{5}
\]
The properties just listed hold unit by unit in (5), so both arrays belong not only to \(\mathcal M_N\) but to the individual-level subclass \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\).  Since \(N_x>0\), averaging (5) within cell \(x\) recovers (4), and anchor agreement implies that both arrays generate the same table \(y\).  Therefore, for every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\),
\[
V_{\mu^-}(\pi)
=\sum_xq_x\ell_{x,\pi(x)}(m_\pi;y)
=L_S(\pi;y),
\qquad
V_{\mu^+}(\pi)
=U_S(\pi;y).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the two arrays in (5) are common lower and upper schedules for the entire library.

For \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), set
\[
Y_i^\lambda(a,t)=(1-\lambda)Y_i^-(a,t)+\lambda Y_i^+(a,t).
\]
Convex combinations preserve the range \([0,1]\), monotonicity, the \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz inequality, neutral invariance, and all anchor equalities.  Hence \(Y^\lambda\in\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\subseteq\mathcal M_N\), and linearity of cell averages and policy values gives
\[
V_{\mu^\lambda}(\pi)
=(1-\lambda)L_S(\pi;y)+\lambda U_S(\pi;y).
\tag{7}
\]
As \(\lambda\) ranges over \([0,1]\), (7) fills the whole interval in (3).  This proves the claimed identified set.  Moreover, the bridge lemma shows that the attainable induced mean surfaces under \(\mathcal M_N\) and \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\) coincide.  Hence the same exact interval and the same homogeneous common endpoint schedules hold when the identifying class is restricted to the source-faithful individual-level Lipschitz subclass.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The homogeneous endpoint arrays lie in the source-faithful individual-level \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz subclass, so the same common endpoints are sharp both there and in the aggregate mean class. \textit{Gap.} ChenOberst2025 proves tight monotonicity-only reusable-validation bounds; the finite-\(\Gamma\) common schedules and score-sensitive geometry are additional, while the class bridge prevents attributing aggregate assumptions to that paper. \textit{Consumer.} A hospital can report auditable sharp value intervals for every prespecified deterministic model update from one panel under either equivalent induced-mean formulation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:width-identity]\label{thm:width-identity}
For every \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), \(\overline W(S)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_{\pi}(S)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\min(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\})\), and the supremum defining \(\overline W(S)\) is attained by two observationally indistinguishable fixed finite-population response schedules. Consequently \(D^{\star}\) is rational and attained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), a table \(y\) compatible with the selected arm--cell means, a future policy \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), and a cell \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Write
\[
a=\pi(x),\qquad t=m_\pi.
\]
We first bound the conditional interval diameter in this cell.  If
\(T_{xa}(S)=\varnothing\), the range restriction \([0,1]\) in the envelope
definition gives
\[
u_{xa}(t;y)-\ell_{xa}(t;y)\leq 1=r_{\pi x}(S).                    \tag{1}
\]
Suppose next that \(a=a_0\) and \(T_{x a_0}(S)\neq\varnothing\).  By
neutral invariance and compatibility, all matching neutral anchors have one
common value \(y_0\in[0,1]\).  The neutral case of the envelope definition
uses Lipschitz constant zero, and therefore
\[
\ell_{x a_0}(t;y)=\max\{0,y_0\}=y_0,
\qquad
u_{x a_0}(t;y)=\min\{1,y_0\}=y_0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
u_{x a_0}(t;y)-\ell_{x a_0}(t;y)=0=r_{\pi x}(S).                 \tag{2}
\]

It remains to consider \(a\neq a_0\).  For any
\(c\in T_{xa}(S)\), if \(m_c\leq t\), the two envelope formulas imply
\[
\ell_{xa}(t;y)\geq y_{cx},
\qquad
u_{xa}(t;y)\leq y_{cx}+\Gamma(t-m_c).
\]
If \(m_c\geq t\), the same formulas imply
\[
\ell_{xa}(t;y)\geq y_{cx}-\Gamma(m_c-t),
\qquad
u_{xa}(t;y)\leq y_{cx}.
\]
In either case, also using that both endpoints lie in \([0,1]\),
\[
u_{xa}(t;y)-\ell_{xa}(t;y)
\leq \min\{1,\Gamma|t-m_c|\}=d_{\pi xc}.                         \tag{3}
\]
Action-mismatching candidates have cost one.  Taking the minimum of (3)
over matching anchors and including the dummy bound one, then combining
with (1)--(2), proves
\[
u_{x,\pi(x)}(m_\pi;y)-\ell_{x,\pi(x)}(m_\pi;y)
\leq r_{\pi x}(S).                                                \tag{4}
\]
The weights satisfy \(q_x=N_x/N>0\).  Summing (4), using the definitions of
\(L_S\), \(U_S\), and \(R_\pi\), and maximizing over the finite nonempty
set \(\mathcal F\) yields, for every compatible \(y\),
\[
\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}
\{U_S(\pi;y)-L_S(\pi;y)\}
\leq \max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S).
\]
Taking the supremum over \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\) in the definition of
\(\overline W(S)\) gives
\[
\overline W(S)\leq\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S).              \tag{5}
\]

We prove the reverse inequality by an explicit pair of fixed arrays.
Finiteness and nonemptiness of \(\mathcal F\) give a maximizer
\(\pi^\star\) of \(R_\pi(S)\).  For each cell \(x\), put
\[
a_x=\pi^\star(x),\qquad t=m_{\pi^\star},
\qquad w_x=r_{\pi^\star x}(S).
\]
If \(a_x\neq a_0\) and \(T_{x a_x}(S)=\varnothing\), let
\(g_x^-(s)=0\) and \(g_x^+(s)=1\).  If
\(a_x\neq a_0\), \(T_{x a_x}(S)\neq\varnothing\), and \(w_x=0\),
let both functions be identically zero.  In the remaining nonneutral case,
\(T_{x a_x}(S)\neq\varnothing\) and \(w_x>0\).  This forces
\(\Gamma>0\), and we define
\[
g_x^-(s)=\min\{w_x,\max\{0,\Gamma(s-t)\}\},
\qquad
g_x^+(s)=\min\{w_x,\max\{0,\Gamma(s-t)+w_x\}\}.                 \tag{6}
\]
The maps in (6) are compositions of a nondecreasing affine map with
clipping to \([0,w_x]\).  Hence they are bounded, nondecreasing, and
\(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz.  By the definition of \(w_x\), every matching
anchor \(c\in T_{x a_x}(S)\) satisfies
\[
w_x\leq \min\{1,\Gamma|m_c-t|\},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad \Gamma|m_c-t|\geq w_x.                    \tag{7}
\]
If \(m_c<t\), equations (6)--(7) give
\(g_x^-(m_c)=g_x^+(m_c)=0\); if \(m_c>t\), they give
\(g_x^-(m_c)=g_x^+(m_c)=w_x\).  The equality \(m_c=t\) would imply
\(w_x=0\), contrary to this case.  Thus the two functions agree at every
matching selected anchor, while
\[
g_x^-(t)=0,
\qquad
g_x^+(t)=w_x.                                                       \tag{8}
\]
For \(a_x=a_0\), take the identical zero constants if a matching neutral
anchor exists and take the two constants zero and one otherwise.  Neutral
invariance holds in both cases, the functions agree at all matching selected
anchors, and their target difference is again \(w_x\).

For every cell use the preceding pair for action \(a_x\), and use the
identical zero function for every other action.  Define homogeneous
fixed-population arrays by assigning these cell functions to every unit in
the cell.  Every nonneutral trajectory is bounded, nondecreasing, and
\(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz, and every neutral trajectory is constant.  Therefore
the two arrays lie in \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\), and the bridge
lemma also places them in \(\mathcal M_N\).  For a selected candidate and
cell, either its action is \(a_x\), in which case the two schedules agree by
(7)--(8), or its action is different, in which case both outcomes are zero.
Thus the arrays produce exactly the same selected table \(y^\star\); in
fact, they produce the same realized outcome for every unit under every
assignment to the selected arms.  Their causal values at \(\pi^\star\)
satisfy
\[
V_{\mu^+}(\pi^\star)-V_{\mu^-}(\pi^\star)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_xw_x
=R_{\pi^\star}(S).                                                   \tag{9}
\]
The sharp-common-endpoints theorem places both feasible values in the same
identified interval conditional on \(y^\star\).  Hence its width is at least
the separation in (9).  Since \(\pi^\star\) maximizes \(R_\pi(S)\), this
and (5) give
\[
\overline W(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr).                \tag{10}
\]
The common table \(y^\star\) is generated by each member of the constructed
pair, so the supremum in \(\overline W(S)\) is attained by two
observationally indistinguishable fixed arrays.  Because those arrays lie in
\(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\), while the bridge lemma says that
this subclass and \(\mathcal M_N\) generate the same mean surfaces, the
same exact width is obtained on the individual-level Lipschitz subclass.

Finally, \(q_x=N_x/N\), every score, and \(\Gamma\) are rational.  The
costs \(d_{\pi xc}\) are therefore rational, and (10) uses only minima,
sums, and maxima over finite sets.  Thus \(\overline W(S)\) is rational for
each \(S\).  The feasible family \(\mathcal P_k\) is finite and nonempty
because it contains \(\varnothing\).  Its minimum \(D^\star\) is therefore
rational and attained.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Two observationally indistinguishable homogeneous arrays in the individual-level \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz subclass attain the aggregate-class adversarial width, yielding the exact finite-\(\Gamma\) side of the saturation theorem. \textit{Gap.} AnthonyGoyalGuptaNagarajan2010 supplies the robust nearest-facility skeleton and MontielOleaPrallonQiuStoyeSun2025 supplies a causal \(k\)-median analogy; the new result derives these action- and score-specific costs as exact causal diameters. \textit{Consumer.} Trial designers can evaluate every candidate panel without simulating outcomes or solving an inner shape-constrained response program, then assess its position on the exact sensitivity path.

\begin{theorem}[prop:milp-equivalence]\label{prop:milp-equivalence}
The optimal value of \(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}}\) equals \(D^{\star}\). For every integral selection vector \(z\), minimizing over \(b\) assigns each pair \((\pi,x)\) to a cheapest selected matching column or the dummy column and yields \(v=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_{\pi}(S_z)\), where \(S_z=\{c\in\mathcal C:z_c=1\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an integral selection vector \(z\), and put
\[
S_z=\{c\in\mathcal C:z_c=1\}.
\]
The budget constraint in \(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}}\) gives
\(|S_z|=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}z_c\leq k\), hence
\(S_z\in\mathcal P_k\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}feasible\text{-}panels}\).
For fixed \((\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X\), nonnegativity,
normalization, and the inequalities \(b_{\pi xc}\leq z_c\) imply that
\((b_{\pi xc})_{c\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}}\) is a convex
combination supported on \(S_z\cup\{\varnothing\}\).  Because
\(d_{\pi x\varnothing}=1\), every such combination satisfies
\[
\sum_{c\in\mathcal C\cup\{\varnothing\}}
d_{\pi xc}b_{\pi xc}
\geq
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S_z\}\bigr)
=r_{\pi x}(S_z),                                                    \tag{1}
\]
where the equality is \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\).  Multiplying
(1) by the positive weights \(q_x\), summing over \(x\), and applying the
scenario constraint of \(\mathrm{def{:}panel\text{-}milp}\) yields
\[
v\geq R_\pi(S_z)\qquad(\pi\in\mathcal F),
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
v\geq\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S_z).                              \tag{2}
\]
All sets involved are finite.  Conversely, for each \((\pi,x)\), choose
one cheapest element of \(S_z\cup\{\varnothing\}\), set its
\(b_{\pi xc}\)-coordinate to one, and set all other coordinates to zero.
These choices satisfy normalization and \(b_{\pi xc}\leq z_c\), and they
make (1) an equality for every \((\pi,x)\).  Taking
\[
v=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S_z)
\]
then satisfies every scenario constraint.  Thus the conditional optimum
over \(b\) and \(v\), for the fixed integral vector \(z\), is exactly the
right side of (2).  No integrality constraint on \(b\) is needed.

Integral selection vectors satisfying the budget constraint are in
bijection with \(\mathcal P_k\).  Minimizing the conditional optimum and
using \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}prospective\text{-}loss}\) therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}})
=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_\pi(S)
=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\overline W(S)
=D^\star.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The mixed-integer formulation is proof-carrying implementation infrastructure for the causal width identity, not a new facility-location algorithm. \textit{Gap.} AnthonyGoyalGuptaNagarajan2010 already studies min-max nearest-facility optimization. This corollary records an exact rational formulation for the causal cost tensor after the causal theorem has derived that tensor. \textit{Consumer.} A trial team can optimize over thousands of candidate model versions using standard mixed-integer solvers while retaining an exact target objective.

\begin{theorem}[prop:certificate-sound-complete]\label{prop:certificate-sound-complete}
If \(\mathsf{Check}(\mathsf{Cert})=\mathrm{true}\) for \(\mathsf{Cert}=(S^{\star},v^{\star},\mathcal T)\), then \(S^{\star}\in\mathcal P_k\), the checker has independently recomputed \(\overline W(S^{\star})=v^{\star}\), every feasible panel is covered by a verified root-to-leaf branch, and every leaf with a feasible descendant has verified \(\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^{\star}\). Monotonicity under adding candidates gives \(\overline W(S)\geq\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^{\star}\) for every feasible descendant \(S\), so \(\overline W(S^{\star})=v^{\star}=D^{\star}\). Conversely, every rational instance admits such an accepted finite certificate by branching until every path is closed. The certificate supplies neither \(D^{\star}\) nor global optimality; both are derived by the checker.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first establish the monotonicity used by every leaf bound.  The formula
in \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) is independent of the budget.
Thus, for arbitrary \(S\subseteq S'\subseteq\mathcal C\), apply that theorem
with the auxiliary admissible budget \(|\mathcal C|\).  Enlarging the set
over which a minimum is taken gives, for every \((\pi,x)\),
\[
r_{\pi x}(S')\leq r_{\pi x}(S).
\]
Since \(q_x>0\), summation and then maximization preserve the inequality, so
\[
R_\pi(S')\leq R_\pi(S),
\qquad
\overline W(S')\leq\overline W(S).                                    \tag{3}
\]

Suppose \(\mathsf{Check}(S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)=\mathrm{true}\).
By the explicit tests in \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}grammar}\),
the checker has established
\[
S^\star\in\mathcal P_k,
\qquad
\overline W(S^\star)=v^\star                                      \tag{4}
\]
by exact rational recomputation.  For a node \((I,E,O)\), call a subset
\(S\subseteq\mathcal C\) a descendant when
\(I\subseteq S\subseteq I\cup E\).  The checked root has every subset as a
descendant.  If an internal node branches on \(c\in E\), its two checked
children partition its descendants according as \(c\in S\) or
\(c\notin S\).  Induction on depth, together with the check that both
children occur, shows that every subset of \(\mathcal C\), and hence every
feasible panel, reaches a checked leaf.

A leaf satisfying \(|I|>k\) has no feasible descendant because each
descendant contains \(I\).  At every other leaf, the checker has recomputed
\[
\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^\star.                                     \tag{5}
\]
If \(S\) is a feasible descendant of that leaf, then
\(S\subseteq I\cup E\); applying (3) and (5) gives
\[
\overline W(S)\geq\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^\star.                   \tag{6}
\]
Every feasible panel is covered by one of these cases, so (6) holds for
all \(S\in\mathcal P_k\).  Combining it with (4) and the definition of
\(D^\star\) yields
\[
D^\star=v^\star=\overline W(S^\star).
\]
Thus global optimality is derived from the local Boolean checks; it is not
an unverified field of the certificate.

For completeness, fix any rational instance.  The family
\(\mathcal P_k\) is finite and nonempty because it contains
\(\varnothing\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\), every
\(\overline W(S)\) is a maximum of finite rational weighted sums of finite
minima of rational costs.  Hence exact enumeration produces a minimizer
\(S^\star\in\mathcal P_k\) and the rational value
\[
v^\star=\overline W(S^\star)=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\overline W(S).   \tag{7}
\]
Start with the required root and branch on the first element of \(E\) in a
fixed order.  Close a node if \(|I|>k\).  Otherwise continue until
\(E=\varnothing\), and then close it using the optimistic-bound rule: at
such a node, \(I\in\mathcal P_k\), so (7) gives
\[
\overline W(I\cup E)=\overline W(I)\geq v^\star.                      \tag{8}
\]
Each branch reduces \(|E|\) by one.  Therefore every path has length at
most \(|\mathcal C|\), the resulting binary tree is finite, and the checked
branch relations cover every subset.  Equations (7)--(8), together with
the exact root, partition, and child relations, make every checker test
succeed.  All minima, maxima, additions, and comparisons are rational, so
the construction and verification use exact rational arithmetic.  This
proves existence of an accepted finite certificate for every rational
instance, without asserting a polynomial bound on its size.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The checker makes a reported causal panel auditable without treating generic exact branch certification as the scientific contribution. \textit{Gap.} CheungGleixnerSteffy2017 and Szeider2026 establish general exact proof-log methodology. This corollary only specializes exhaustive checking to the derived causal width and claims no new generic certification framework. \textit{Consumer.} Regulators or independent statisticians can verify the claimed trial panel with a small exact checker without rerunning the original solver.

\begin{proposition}[prop:robust-kmedian-lift]\label{prop:robust-kmedian-lift}
For every nonempty panel \(S\), introduce one client \(j_{\pi x}\) of weight \(q_x\) for each \((\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X\), one eligible facility \(f_c\) for each \(c\in\mathcal C\), cross-distance \(\delta(j_{\pi x},f_c)=1+d_{\pi xc}\), and distance \(2\) between distinct clients or distinct facilities. Then \(\delta\) is a metric and the weighted cost of scenario \(\pi\) is \(1+R_{\pi}(S)\). Thus the action-match exclusions, neutral-action convention, and clipping embed exactly into a weighted candidate-restricted scenario nearest-facility objective after an additive constant. Candidate-only facility eligibility, rational client weights, the dummy option, and the at-most-\(k\) convention depart from the literal unweighted \(F\subseteq V\), \(|F|=k\) statement in AnthonyGoyalGuptaNagarajan2010.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
\mathcal J=\{j_{\pi x}:(\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X\},
\qquad
\mathcal K=\{f_c:c\in\mathcal C\},
\]
where the two displayed sets are taken to be disjoint, and let \(\mathcal V=\mathcal J\mathbin{\dot\cup}\mathcal K\).  Complete the displayed cross-distance in the statement symmetrically, put \(\delta(v,v)=0\), and put \(\delta(v,w)=2\) when \(v\neq w\) belong to the same one of \(\mathcal J\) and \(\mathcal K\).  By \(\textnormal{def:score-cost}\),
\[
0\leq d_{\pi xc}\leq 1,
\]
including each of its action-mismatch, neutral-action, and matching-nonneutral cases.  Consequently every pair of distinct points \(v,w\in\mathcal V\) satisfies
\[
1\leq\delta(v,w)\leq2.
\]
Thus \(\delta\) is nonnegative and symmetric, and \(\delta(v,w)=0\) if and only if \(v=w\).  It remains only to verify the triangle inequality.  If two of \(v,w,z\) coincide, the nontrivial triangle inequality either reduces to an equality or follows from nonnegativity.  If they are pairwise distinct, the preceding bounds give
\[
\delta(v,w)\leq2\leq1+1\leq\delta(v,z)+\delta(z,w).
\]
Hence \(\delta\) is a metric on \(\mathcal V\).

Fix a nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal C\).  Since every \(d_{\pi xc}\leq1\), the dummy value \(1\) in \(\textnormal{def:score-cost}\) cannot decrease the minimum, and therefore
\[
r_{\pi x}(S)
=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr)
=\min_{c\in S}d_{\pi xc}.
\]
The weighted nearest-facility cost for scenario \(\pi\) is consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\min_{c\in S}
 \delta(j_{\pi x},f_c)
&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\min_{c\in S}(1+d_{\pi xc})\\
&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
  +\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\min_{c\in S}d_{\pi xc}\\
&=1+\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_xr_{\pi x}(S)\\
&=1+R_{\pi}(S).
\end{aligned}
\]
The third equality uses
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}N_x
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}1
=1,
\]
which follows from the definitions of \(q_x\) and \(N_x\) and from the fact that the cell labels partition the \(N\) fixed units.  Maximizing the scenario cost over \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), and then invoking \(\textnormal{thm:width-identity}\), yields
\[
\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\min_{c\in S}\delta(j_{\pi x},f_c)
=1+\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_{\pi}(S)
=1+\overline W(S).
\]
Thus minimizing the causal prospective loss over nonempty feasible panels is exactly the displayed weighted candidate-restricted robust nearest-facility problem, up to the panel-independent additive constant \(1\).

Finally, the construction has eligible facilities only at the candidate points \(f_c\), and its client weights are the rational numbers \(q_x=N_x/N\).  The definition of \(r_{\pi x}(S)\) supplies the dummy cost \(1\) when \(S=\varnothing\), whereas a nearest-facility cost with no open facility is not defined by the metric construction.  Likewise, \(\mathcal P_k\) permits \(|S|\leq k\), rather than requiring equality.  These are precisely the candidate-only eligibility, rational weighting, dummy-option, and at-most-budget qualifications stated in the proposition, so the representation does not identify them with the literal unweighted, unrestricted-vertex, exactly-\(k\) comparator formulation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lift makes the prior-art boundary explicit: the optimization skeleton is robust nearest-facility selection, while the causal contribution is the sharp derivation of its costs. \textit{Gap.} AnthonyGoyalGuptaNagarajan2010 proves approximation guarantees for the general metric min-max location problem; this proposition is an exact representation check, not a stronger optimization theorem. \textit{Consumer.} Optimization researchers can apply robust facility-location tools without confusing those tools with the causal identification result.

\begin{proposition}[prop:np-hardness]\label{prop:np-hardness}
Computing \(D^{\star}\) is NP-hard even when \(\Gamma=0\), there is one future policy, all \(q_x\) are equal, and there are two actions, by a polynomial reduction from maximum coverage in which candidate policies encode subsets of \(\mathcal X\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take an instance of maximum \(k\)-coverage with nonempty universe
\(\mathcal X\), \(|\mathcal X|=M\), and subset family
\((B_c)_{c\in\mathcal C}\).  Repeated subsets may be deleted without
changing any attainable union.  Construct a finite population with
\(N=M\) and one unit in every cell, so \(N_x=1\) and \(q_x=1/M\).  Let
\(\mathcal A=\{0,1\}\), let \(a_0=0\), set \(\Gamma=0\), and use one
future policy \(\pi\) satisfying \(\pi(x)=1\) for all \(x\).  For each
subset define the candidate policy
\[
c(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&x\in B_c,\\
0,&x\notin B_c.
\end{cases}
\]
Set every policy score to zero.  This construction writes one action for
each incidence entry and therefore has size polynomial in the input size.

By \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\), action matching and
\(\Gamma=0\) give
\[
d_{\pi xc}=
\begin{cases}
0,&x\in B_c,\\
1,&x\notin B_c.
\end{cases}                                                           \tag{9}
\]
The frozen maximum-coverage lemma states that maximizing the covered
cardinality over panels of size at most \(k\) is NP-hard.  Substituting (9)
into \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) gives, for every feasible
panel \(S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline W(S)
&=\frac1M\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
  \min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr)\\
&=\frac1M\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
  \mathbf 1\!\left\{x\notin\bigcup_{c\in S}B_c\right\}\\
&=1-\frac1M\left|\bigcup_{c\in S}B_c\right|.                         \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently a panel covers at least \(B\) elements if and only if its
width is at most \(1-B/M\).  Thus an algorithm computing \(D^\star\) on
the stated restricted instances would solve maximum \(k\)-coverage through
this polynomial reduction.  This proves the claimed NP-hardness even with
one future policy, equal weights, two actions, and \(\Gamma=0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition imports the standard maximum-coverage complexity boundary and explains why exact search may require exponential certificates. \textit{Gap.} HochbaumPathria1998 already studies maximum \(k\)-coverage and its greedy approximation. The proposition is a transparent reduction showing that the causal design contains that established problem at \(\Gamma=0\), not an independent hardness novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} Software users receive a principled boundary between exact certified search and scalable approximation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:empty-panel-width]\label{prop:empty-panel-width}
For the separate case \(S=\varnothing\), the selected mean table is empty, \(\mathcal I_{\varnothing}(\pi;\varnothing)=[0,1]\) for every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), and \(\overline W(\varnothing)=1\). This is a deterministic population-width statement with no assignment or randomization distribution.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(S=\varnothing\) and \(\pi\in\mathcal F\).  Since the selected mean table has domain \(S\times\mathcal X=\varnothing\), there is exactly one such table, namely the empty table \(y=\varnothing\).  By \(\mathrm{def{:}anchors}\),
\[
T_{xa}(\varnothing)=\{c\in\varnothing:c(x)=a\}=\varnothing
\qquad (x\in\mathcal X, a\in\mathcal A).
\]
Therefore \(\mathrm{def{:}envelopes}\) gives, for every \(t\in[0,1]\),
\[
\ell_{xa}(t;\varnothing)
=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\varnothing\bigr)=0,
\qquad
u_{xa}(t;\varnothing)
=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\varnothing\bigr)=1.
\]
This calculation also covers \(a=a_0\): replacing \(\Gamma\) by zero in the neutral-action convention does not change either empty collection of anchor terms.

The cells form a partition of \(\mathcal I\), so the definitions of \(N_x\) and \(q_x\), together with \(N>0\), imply
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}N_x
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}1
=1.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{def{:}sharp\text{-}interval}\) yields
\[
L_{\varnothing}(\pi;\varnothing)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\,0=0,
\qquad
U_{\varnothing}(\pi;\varnothing)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\,1=1,
\]
and hence
\[
\mathcal I_{\varnothing}(\pi;\varnothing)
=[L_{\varnothing}(\pi;\varnothing),U_{\varnothing}(\pi;\varnothing)]
=[0,1].
\]

For the prospective width, \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\) gives, separately for every \(x\in\mathcal X\),
\[
r_{\pi x}(\varnothing)
=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\varnothing\}\bigr)
=\min\{1\}=1,
\]
so
\[
R_{\pi}(\varnothing)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_xr_{\pi x}(\varnothing)
=1.
\]
Because \(k\geq0\), \(\varnothing\in\mathcal P_k\).  Applying \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\), and using that the finite set \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty, gives
\[
\overline W(\varnothing)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_{\pi}(\varnothing)
=1.
\]
Every object in this argument is a fixed finite-population quantity.  In particular, no allocation, realized assignment, or randomization distribution is invoked, which proves the final design-domain assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Separating the empty panel prevents a degenerate zero-arm object from being treated as a randomized experiment. \textit{Gap.} The empty-panel boundary is a design-domain clarification rather than a novelty claim; it records the population width implied by the sharp-width identity. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can return the deterministic no-trial benchmark without constructing an invalid allocation or assignment law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:design-based-coverage]\label{thm:design-based-coverage}
For every sampled design with \(\varnothing\neq S\in\mathcal P_k\), allocation \((n_c)_{c\in S}\in\mathbb N_{>0}^{S}\), and \(\sum_{c\in S}n_c=N\), under complete randomization and fixed \(Y\), with probability at least \(1-\alpha\) over \(Z\), simultaneously for every \(c\in S\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(l_{cx}\leq y_{cx}\leq h_{cx}\), and simultaneously for every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), \([L_S(\pi;y),U_S(\pi;y)]\subseteq\mathcal O_S(\pi)\). The probability statement is uniform over \(\mathcal M_N\), carries no additional multiplicity factor depending on \(|\mathcal F|\), and has no domain at \(S=\varnothing\); that case is the separate deterministic population-width proposition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\varnothing\neq S\in\mathcal P_k\), positive integers \((n_c)_{c\in S}\) with \(\sum_{c\in S}n_c=N\), and a fixed array \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\).  All probabilities below are conditional on this panel and allocation and are taken only over \(Z\).  Because \(S\) and \(\mathcal X\) are finite and nonempty,
\[
J=|S||\mathcal X|\geq 1.
\]

Fix \(c\in S\) and put \(A_c=\{i\in\mathcal I:Z_i=c\}\).  Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}complete\text{-}randomization}\), \(|A_c|=n_c\).  Moreover, \(A_c\) is uniform over the \(n_c\)-subsets of \(\mathcal I\): for any fixed such subset \(B\), the number of admissible assignments satisfying \(A_c=B\) is
\[
\frac{(N-n_c)!}{\prod_{d\in S\setminus\{c\}}n_d!},
\]
which is independent of \(B\).  Thus the marginal random set \(A_c\) satisfies the sampling law in \(\mathrm{lem{:}hoeffding\text{-}sampling\text{-}without\text{-}replacement}\).

For a fixed \(x\in\mathcal X\), define the fixed numbers
\[
w_i^{(c,x)}=\mathbf 1\{x_i=x\}Y_i(c(x_i),m_c),\qquad i\in\mathcal I.
\]
The ambient range in \(\mathrm{def{:}response\text{-}class}\) gives \(w_i^{(c,x)}\in[0,1]\).  By the definitions of \(N_x\), \(q_x\), \(y_{cx}\), and \(\mu\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1N\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}w_i^{(c,x)}
&=\frac1N\sum_{i:x_i=x}Y_i(c(x),m_c)\\
&=\frac{N_x}{N}\,\mu_{x,c(x)}(m_c)
=q_xy_{cx}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(i\in A_c\), the observed-outcome definition gives
\[
Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
=Y_i(Z_i(x_i),m_{Z_i})
=Y_i(c(x_i),m_c).
\]
Consequently, the Horvitz--Thompson formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}design\text{-}bands}\), together with \(q_x=N_x/N>0\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat y_{cx}^{\mathrm{HT}}
&=\frac1{N_x}\frac N{n_c}
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\mathbf1\{Z_i=c,x_i=x\}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\\
&=\frac1{q_x}\frac1{n_c}\sum_{i\in A_c}w_i^{(c,x)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Set
\[
t_{cx}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2J/\alpha)}{2n_c}}.
\]
Here \(t_{cx}>0\), since \(J\geq1\), \(0<\alpha<1\), and \(n_c\geq1\).  Applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}hoeffding\text{-}sampling\text{-}without\text{-}replacement}\) to the displayed finite population and using the preceding two identities gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_Z\!\left(
 \left|\widehat y_{cx}^{\mathrm{HT}}-y_{cx}\right|
 \geq q_x^{-1}t_{cx}
\right)
&\leq 2\exp(-2n_ct_{cx}^{2})\\
&=\frac{\alpha}{J}.
\end{aligned}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}design\text{-}bands}\), \(q_x^{-1}t_{cx}=\varepsilon_{cx}\).  A union bound over the exactly \(J\) pairs in \(S\times\mathcal X\) therefore shows that the event
\[
\mathcal E=
\bigcap_{(c,x)\in S\times\mathcal X}
\left\{\left|\widehat y_{cx}^{\mathrm{HT}}-y_{cx}\right|<\varepsilon_{cx}\right\}
\]
satisfies
\[
\Pr_Z(\mathcal E)
\geq 1-\sum_{(c,x)\in S\times\mathcal X}\frac{\alpha}{J}
=1-\alpha.
\]
For every \((c,x)\), the response-class range and the definition of \(y_{cx}\) imply \(0\leq y_{cx}\leq1\).  On \(\mathcal E\), therefore,
\[
\max\{0,\widehat y_{cx}^{\mathrm{HT}}-\varepsilon_{cx}\}
\leq y_{cx}
\leq
\min\{1,\widehat y_{cx}^{\mathrm{HT}}+\varepsilon_{cx}\}.
\]
The two extrema are respectively \(l_{cx}\) and \(h_{cx}\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}design\text{-}bands}\).  This proves the claimed simultaneous primitive coverage.

It remains to propagate that event to every future policy.  For fixed \((x,a,t)\), the formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}envelopes}\) expresses \(\ell_{xa}(t;\cdot)\) as the maximum of a constant and terms that are increasing in one anchor coordinate, and expresses \(u_{xa}(t;\cdot)\) as the minimum of a constant and terms that are increasing in one anchor coordinate.  These observations also apply to the neutral-action formula, where \(\Gamma=0\).  Hence both envelope maps are coordinatewise nondecreasing.  On \(\mathcal E\), where \(l\leq y\leq h\) coordinatewise, the positive-weighted sums in \(\mathrm{def{:}sharp\text{-}interval}\) give, simultaneously for every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\),
\[
L_S(\pi;l)\leq L_S(\pi;y)
\quad\text{and}\quad
U_S(\pi;y)\leq U_S(\pi;h).
\]
Because \(y\) is induced by the fixed \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\), it is compatible.  Thus \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\text{-}common\text{-}endpoints}\) gives
\[
L_S(\pi;y)\leq U_S(\pi;y).
\]
Combining the last two displays and using the definition of \(\mathcal O_S(\pi)\),
\[
[L_S(\pi;y),U_S(\pi;y)]
\subseteq
[L_S(\pi;l),U_S(\pi;h)]
=\mathcal O_S(\pi)
\qquad(\pi\in\mathcal F).
\]
No union bound over \(\mathcal F\) was taken: on the single event \(\mathcal E\), deterministic coordinatewise monotonicity supplies all of these inclusions.  The probability bound used only the common range \([0,1]\) and the assignment law, so the lower bound \(1-\alpha\) is uniform over \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\).  Finally, \(S\neq\varnothing\) was used to have positive allocations and \(J\geq1\); \(\mathrm{def{:}design\text{-}bands}\) consequently supplies no randomization-band assertion for \(S=\varnothing\), exactly as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies assignment-only finite-sample validity for reusing one experiment across every prespecified update. \textit{Gap.} CaugheyDafoeLiMiratrix2023 gives exact simultaneous randomization inference for effect quantiles, not for optimized monotone-Lipschitz policy-value endpoints; ChenOberst2025 uses asymptotic sampling inference. \textit{Consumer.} A fixed randomized trial can validate many later model updates without treating patient outcomes or covariate cells as independent superpopulation draws.

\begin{proposition}[prop:zero-gamma-reduction]\label{prop:zero-gamma-reduction}
When \(\Gamma=0\), \(d_{\pi xc}=0\) exactly when \(c(x)=\pi(x)\) and equals one otherwise, so \(\overline W(S)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\mathbf1\{c(x)\neq\pi(x)\text{ for every }c\in S\}\). In particular, exact matching coverage of every future cell gives zero width.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\Gamma=0\). Fix \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(c\in\mathcal C\). By \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\), if \(c(x)\neq\pi(x)\), then \(d_{\pi xc}=1\). If \(c(x)=\pi(x)=a_0\), the same definition gives \(d_{\pi xc}=0\). In the remaining case, \(c(x)=\pi(x)\neq a_0\), and therefore
\[
d_{\pi xc}=\min\{1,\Gamma|m_{\pi}-m_c|\}=\min\{1,0\}=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
d_{\pi xc}=
\begin{cases}
0,&c(x)=\pi(x),\\
1,&c(x)\neq\pi(x).
\end{cases}
\]
By the definition of \(r_{\pi x}(S)\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}score\text{-}cost}\),
\[
r_{\pi x}(S)=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr).
\]
If some \(c\in S\) satisfies \(c(x)=\pi(x)\), the set inside the minimum contains zero, so \(r_{\pi x}(S)=0\). If no such \(c\) exists, including when \(S=\varnothing\), every displayed cost contributed by \(S\) equals one and the adjoined element is one, so \(r_{\pi x}(S)=1\). Hence
\[
r_{\pi x}(S)=\mathbf 1\{c(x)\neq\pi(x)\text{ for every }c\in S\}.
\]
The already-established width identity \(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) now yields
\[
\overline W(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R_{\pi}(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x r_{\pi x}(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\mathbf 1\{c(x)\neq\pi(x)\text{ for every }c\in S\}.
\]
Finally, suppose that every future cell is exactly covered: for each \(\pi\in\mathcal F\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\), there exists \(c\in S\) with \(c(x)=\pi(x)\). Then every indicator in the last display is zero. Thus each weighted sum is zero, and because \(\mathcal F\) is finite and nonempty, its maximum is zero. Therefore \(\overline W(S)=0\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This boundary case verifies that the causal width collapses to the established weighted maximum-coverage objective when score sensitivity vanishes. \textit{Gap.} HochbaumPathria1998 supplies the maximum-coverage algorithmic benchmark; this proposition identifies it as the exact \(\Gamma=0\) specialization of the causal width rather than claiming a new coverage theorem. \textit{Consumer.} Practitioners can check the implementation against a transparent coverage instance before using nonzero score sensitivity.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-rational-generator-and-core-compression]\label{thm:exact-rational-generator-and-core-compression}
Fix deterministic total orders on the encoded finite sets.  The dominance-and-symmetry handle admits the following exact resolution.  There is a deterministic exact-rational generator which, on every rational instance, returns a finite certificate \(\mathsf{Cert}=(S^{\star},v^{\star},\mathcal T)\) accepted by the independently executed map \(\mathsf{Check}\); acceptance certifies \(v^{\star}=\overline W(S^{\star})=D^{\star}\).  The generator enumerates \(\mathcal P_k\) to obtain its incumbent and then branches on the least undecided candidate, closing a node \((I,E,O)\) exactly when \(|I|>k\) or \(\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^{\star}\).  Thus it always terminates, although its tree can be exponential.

For every rational instance with \(|\mathcal X|=3\) and \(|\mathcal C|\leq5\), the generator, exhaustive panel enumeration, and the fixed-selection values of \(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}}\) agree exactly on the optimum and on the complete set of minimizing panels; with the same deterministic tie rule they return the same panel.  In particular, on \(\mathsf E_{3,5}\) the exact value is \(1/8\), the minimizing panels are \(\{c_1,c_4\}\) and \(\{c_2,c_3\}\), and \(\mathsf{Check}(\{c_1,c_4\},1/8,\mathcal T_{3,5})=\mathrm{true}\).

Moreover, the following nontrivial, computable structural condition on the action-score cost tensor guarantees a polynomial certificate:
\[
 \text{there exists }S_0\in\mathcal P_k\text{ such that }
 \min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S_0\}\bigr)
 =\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr)
 \quad\text{for every }(\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X. \tag{SC}
\]
Under \(\mathrm{(SC)}\), the generated branch tree consists only of the root.  A sufficient condition checkable with \(O(|\mathcal F||\mathcal X||\mathcal C|)\) rational comparisons is that the union of one fixed tie-broken minimizing candidate for every pair \((\pi,x)\) whose full-library minimum is strictly below the dummy cost \(1\) has cardinality at most \(k\).  The one-node tree and its incumbent have encoding length polynomial in the rational input length.  This finite completeness and structural compression do not assert a polynomial-time exact generator on unrestricted instances; such a generator would, by the stated NP-hardness reduction, imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every \(S\subseteq\mathcal C\), define the computable rational map
\[
g(S)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr).                     \tag{17}
\]
The indexing sets are finite, \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty, and all weights
and costs are rational.  Hence all minima and maxima in (17) are attained,
and \(g(S)\) is exactly computable by rational arithmetic.  The formula in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}width\text{-}identity}\) does not depend on the budget.
Applying it with the auxiliary budget \(|\mathcal C|\) gives
\[
g(S)=\overline W(S)\qquad(S\subseteq\mathcal C).                       \tag{18}
\]

Fix total orders on all encoded finite sets.  Enumerate the finite,
nonempty family \(\mathcal P_k\), let \(S^\star\) be its first minimizer,
and set
\[
v^\star=g(S^\star)=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}g(S)=D^\star,               \tag{19}
\]
where the final equality follows from (18) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}prospective\text{-}loss}\).  Construct the branch tree
from \((\varnothing,\mathcal C,\varnothing)\).  At a node \((I,E,O)\),
close the node precisely when
\[
|I|>k
\quad\text{or}\quad
g(I\cup E)\geq v^\star.                                                \tag{20}
\]
If neither condition holds, then \(E\neq\varnothing\).  Indeed, if
\(E=\varnothing\), failure of the first condition makes
\(I\in\mathcal P_k\), whereas failure of the second contradicts (19):
\[
g(I)<v^\star=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}g(S)\leq g(I).                    \tag{21}
\]
Thus the least \(c\in E\) exists.  Attach exactly the two children
\[
(I\cup\{c\},E\setminus\{c\},O),
\qquad
(I,E\setminus\{c\},O\cup\{c\}).                                     \tag{22}
\]
The cardinality of \(E\) decreases by one at every edge, so every path has
length at most \(|\mathcal C|\).  Equation (21) ensures closure by the time
\(E\) is empty.  Hence the construction terminates in a finite binary tree
with at most \(2^{|\mathcal C|+1}-1\) nodes.

Equations (18)--(22) match the root, branching, and leaf grammar in
\(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}grammar}\).  The checker receives only
the rational instance and \((S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)\); it recomputes
\(g(S^\star)\), every set relation in (22), and every leaf value in (20),
so it does not trust the enumeration log or a supplied optimality claim.
All its tests succeed.  The proved certificate soundness theorem then gives
\[
\mathsf{Check}(S^\star,v^\star,\mathcal T)=\mathrm{true}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline W(S^\star)=v^\star=D^\star.                                 \tag{23}
\]
This constructs the required exact-rational implementation of
\(\mathsf{Handle}\).  It is finite on every rational instance but may be
exponential.

For the bounded audit, if \(|\mathcal C|\leq5\), then
\[
|\mathcal P_k|=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{|\mathcal C|\choose j}\leq2^5=32.       \tag{24}
\]
The generator and exhaustive enumeration both evaluate this same finite
list using (17) without rounding.  By the proved mixed-integer equivalence,
for each fixed integral selection vector the optimum over the assignment
variables is also exactly \(g(S)\).  Thus the three computations agree
panel by panel, and consequently agree on the optimum and the complete set
of minimizing panels.  A common deterministic tie rule selects the same
panel.  This proves the assertion for every three-cell instance with at
most five candidates; the equality itself does not require three cells,
but that restriction is the requested audit class.

For \(\mathsf E_{3,5}\), the exact audit proposition gives, in the order
consisting of the empty panel, five singletons, and ten lexicographically
ordered pairs, the worst-width list
\[
1;\quad
\frac78,\frac58,\frac78,\frac58,1;\quad
\frac12,\frac12,\frac18,\frac78,\frac18,
\frac16,\frac58,\frac12,\frac58,\frac38.                              \tag{25}
\]
Its minimum is \(1/8\), attained exactly by
\(\{c_1,c_4\}\) and \(\{c_2,c_3\}\).  That proposition also verifies
the fixed-selection mixed-integer values and every leaf of
\(\mathcal T_{3,5}\), so
\[
\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}})=\frac18,
\qquad
\mathsf{Check}(\{c_1,c_4\},1/8,\mathcal T_{3,5})=\mathrm{true}.        \tag{26}
\]
Thus (25)--(26) are an exact exhaustive early-kill audit.

Now suppose the structural condition \(\mathrm{(SC)}\) holds for some
\(S_0\in\mathcal P_k\).  Multiplying its coordinatewise equalities by
\(q_x>0\), summing over \(x\), and maximizing over \(\pi\) gives
\[
g(S_0)=g(\mathcal C).                                                   \tag{27}
\]
For every \(S\subseteq\mathcal C\), the inclusion
\(S\subseteq\mathcal C\) and monotonicity of a minimum imply
\[
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr)
\geq
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr).
\]
Nonnegative weighted summation and maximization preserve this inequality,
so \(g(S)\geq g(\mathcal C)\).  Since \(S_0\) is feasible, (19) and (27)
therefore give
\[
g(\mathcal C)\leq v^\star\leq g(S_0)=g(\mathcal C),
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad v^\star=g(\mathcal C).                      \tag{28}
\]
At the root, \(I\cup E=\mathcal C\), so (28) makes the second test in
(20) hold with equality.  The generated tree consequently consists only
of the root.  Its incumbent characteristic vector, one rational value, and
the root label have encoding length polynomial in the rational input
length.  The arithmetic values also have polynomial encoding length:
minima and maxima do not enlarge rational representations, and each of the
finitely many products and sums in (17) increases numerator and denominator
bit lengths by at most the sum of the corresponding input bit lengths.

Finally, define
\[
\delta_{\pi x}=
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr).              \tag{29}
\]
Whenever \(\delta_{\pi x}<1\), finiteness of \(\mathcal C\) guarantees an
attaining candidate; choose the first such candidate and let \(H\) be the
union of all choices.  If \(\delta_{\pi x}<1\), the chosen candidate shows
that the minimum over \(H\) is at most \(\delta_{\pi x}\), while
\(H\subseteq\mathcal C\) gives the reverse inequality.  If
\(\delta_{\pi x}=1\), the dummy cost makes both minima equal to one.  Hence
\[
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in H\}\bigr)=\delta_{\pi x}
\qquad\text{for every }(\pi,x).                                       \tag{30}
\]
If \(|H|\leq k\), then \(H\in\mathcal P_k\), and (30) proves
\(\mathrm{(SC)}\) with \(S_0=H\).  Computing (29), its tie-broken
minimizers, and their union takes
\(O(|\mathcal F||\mathcal X||\mathcal C|)\) exact rational comparisons.
The condition can hold with \(k<|\mathcal C|\), for example when some
candidates never attain a coordinatewise minimum, so it is a genuine
restriction on the cost tensor.

The generator above makes no unrestricted polynomial-time claim.  If it
were polynomial-time on all rational inputs, its returned incumbent value
would compute \(D^\star\) in polynomial time.  The proved maximum-coverage
reduction makes that task NP-hard on the stated boundary subclass.  Thus an
unrestricted polynomial-time exact generator would imply
\(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), while this obstruction neither affects finite
termination nor the one-node certificate under \(\mathrm{(SC)}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:chen-oberst-gamma-bridge]\label{lem:chen-oberst-gamma-bridge}
The individual-level Lipschitz class satisfies
\[
\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\subseteq\mathcal M_N.
\]
Conversely, for every \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\) there is a homogeneous-within-cell array \(\widetilde Y\in\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\) whose induced cell-mean surface equals that of \(Y\) at every \((x,a,t)\).  Consequently the sets of induced cell-mean surfaces, selected mean tables, and policy values generated by \(\mathcal M_N\) and by \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\) coincide.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(Y\in\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\).  Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(a\neq a_0\), and \(0\leq s\leq t\leq1\).  The individual monotonicity in \(\mathrm{def{:}chen\text{-}oberst\text{-}individual\text{-}class}\) and \(N_x>0\) give
\[
\mu_{xa}(t)-\mu_{xa}(s)
=\frac1{N_x}\sum_{i:x_i=x}\{Y_i(a,t)-Y_i(a,s)\}\geq0.
\]
The triangle inequality and the individual Lipschitz condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}chen\text{-}oberst\text{-}gamma\text{-}subclass}\) give
\[
|\mu_{xa}(t)-\mu_{xa}(s)|
\leq\frac1{N_x}\sum_{i:x_i=x}|Y_i(a,t)-Y_i(a,s)|
\leq\Gamma|t-s|.
\]
For the neutral action, individual invariance gives
\[
\mu_{x a_0}(t)-\mu_{x a_0}(s)
=\frac1{N_x}\sum_{i:x_i=x}\{Y_i(a_0,t)-Y_i(a_0,s)\}=0.
\]
Every mean lies in \([0,1]\) because it is an average of numbers in \([0,1]\).  These are exactly the membership conditions in \(\mathrm{def{:}response\text{-}class}\), proving the inclusion.

For the converse, let \(Y\in\mathcal M_N\), let \(\mu\) be its induced cell-mean surface, and define
\[
\widetilde Y_i(a,t)=\mu_{x_i a}(t)
\quad(i\in\mathcal I, a\in\mathcal A, t\in[0,1]).
\]
The range, monotonicity, Lipschitz, and neutral-invariance requirements for \(\widetilde Y\) follow componentwise from the membership of \(Y\) in \(\mathcal M_N\).  Hence \(\widetilde Y\in\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO},\Gamma}\).  Moreover, using the definition of \(N_x\) and its positivity,
\[
\frac1{N_x}\sum_{i:x_i=x}\widetilde Y_i(a,t)
=\frac1{N_x}\sum_{i:x_i=x}\mu_{xa}(t)
=\mu_{xa}(t).
\]
Thus homogenization preserves the entire cell-mean surface.  Selected mean tables and policy values are functions only of that surface by the definitions of \(y\) and \(V_\mu\), so their attainable sets coincide as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This bridge proves that the aggregate mean formulation and the source-faithful individual-level \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz subclass generate exactly the same observable mean surfaces and causal policy values. \textit{Gap.} ChenOberst2025 uses unit-level performance monotonicity and neutral-action equality; the bridge records precisely how the present finite-population mean model relates to that class after adding individual-level Lipschitz smoothness. \textit{Consumer.} A reviewer can audit the class comparison without treating an aggregate restriction as if it were the published individual-level assumption.

\begin{proposition}[prop:aggregate-individual-class-incomparability]\label{prop:aggregate-individual-class-incomparability}
Suppose \(\mathcal A\setminus\{a_0\}\neq\varnothing\) and some cell \(x_\star\) contains at least two units.  Then there is a bounded array in \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\setminus\mathcal M_N\), and there is a bounded array in \(\mathcal M_N\setminus\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\).  Thus, on such nondegenerate fixed populations, the unrestricted individual-level monotonicity class and the aggregate monotone \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz mean class are incomparable; the former strengthens aggregation while the latter strengthens smoothness.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(a_1\in\mathcal A\setminus\{a_0\}\).  For the first witness, set
\[
Y_i(a_1,t)=\mathbf 1\{t>1/2\}
\]
for every unit, and set all other nonneutral outcomes to zero and all neutral outcomes to zero.  This array is bounded, every nonneutral trajectory is nondecreasing, and every neutral trajectory is constant, so it belongs to \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\).  Its cell mean for action \(a_1\) is the same step function.  If \(\Gamma=0\), any two points straddling \(1/2\) violate the aggregate Lipschitz condition.  If \(\Gamma>0\), choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\{1/2,1/\Gamma\}.
\]
Between \(s=1/2\) and \(t=1/2+\varepsilon\), the mean changes by one whereas \(\Gamma|t-s|<1\).  Thus this array is not in \(\mathcal M_N\).

For the reverse witness, choose distinct \(i_1,i_2\) with \(x_{i_1}=x_{i_2}=x_\star\), and define for action \(a_1\)
\[
Y_{i_1}(a_1,t)=1-t,
\qquad
Y_{i_2}(a_1,t)=t,
\qquad
Y_i(a_1,t)=\frac12
\quad\text{for every other }i\text{ in cell }x_\star.
\]
Set the action-\(a_1\) outcomes in other cells to \(1/2\), all remaining nonneutral outcomes to zero, and all neutral outcomes to zero.  The cell mean at \((x_\star,a_1)\) is
\[
\frac{(1-t)+t+(N_{x_\star}-2)/2}{N_{x_\star}}=\frac12,
\]
and every other cell-mean trajectory is constant.  Hence all cell means are monotone and \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz for every \(\Gamma\geq0\), and neutral means are invariant, so the array belongs to \(\mathcal M_N\).  But \(t\mapsto Y_{i_1}(a_1,t)=1-t\) is strictly decreasing, so the array is not in \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\).  The two explicit witnesses prove incomparability.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The two explicit arrays prevent a false claim that the aggregate mean assumptions are simply the published assumptions in different notation. \textit{Gap.} The class comparison is bookkeeping for the bridge to ChenOberst2025, not a new causal estimand or an additional design result. \textit{Consumer.} Readers can see exactly which heterogeneity and discontinuity patterns distinguish the two unrestricted classes.

\begin{lemma}[lem:chen-oberst-monotone-envelope-feasibility]\label{lem:chen-oberst-monotone-envelope-feasibility}
For every selected mean table \(y\) compatible with \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\), the no-Lipschitz envelopes are bounded and nondecreasing for nonneutral actions, constant for the neutral action, agree with every matching anchor, and satisfy
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y)\leq\mu_{xa}(t)\leq u^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y)
\]
for every compatible induced mean surface \(\mu\).  In particular, \(\ell^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y)\leq u^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y)\) for all \((x,a,t)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compatible array and its induced mean surface \(\mu\).  Averaging the unit-level inequalities in \(\mathrm{def{:}chen\text{-}oberst\text{-}individual\text{-}class}\) shows that \(t\mapsto\mu_{xa}(t)\) is nondecreasing when \(a\neq a_0\), and that \(t\mapsto\mu_{x a_0}(t)\) is constant.  If \(a\neq a_0\), every matching anchor with \(m_c\leq t\) satisfies
\[
y_{cx}=\mu_{xa}(m_c)\leq\mu_{xa}(t),
\]
whereas every matching anchor with \(m_c\geq t\) satisfies
\[
\mu_{xa}(t)\leq\mu_{xa}(m_c)=y_{cx}.
\]
Together with \(0\leq\mu_{xa}(t)\leq1\), taking the maximum of all lower inequalities and the minimum of all upper inequalities yields
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y)\leq\mu_{xa}(t)\leq u^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y).
\tag{1}
\]
As \(t\) increases, the anchor set in the lower maximum can only grow, while the anchor set in the upper minimum can only shrink.  Therefore both envelopes are nondecreasing.  At an anchor score \(t=m_d\), the defining lower maximum contains \(y_{dx}\), the defining upper minimum contains \(y_{dx}\), and (1) is centered at \(\mu_{xa}(m_d)=y_{dx}\); hence both envelopes equal \(y_{dx}\).

For \(a=a_0\), compatibility and unit-level neutral invariance imply that all matching anchor values equal one common number.  If there is an anchor, both neutral formulas therefore equal that number; if there is none, they are the constants zero and one.  Thus the neutral envelopes are constant, bounded, agree with all anchors, and satisfy (1).  Finally (1) directly implies the asserted order between the two envelopes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the monotonicity-only interpolation fact needed to obtain the faithful no-Lipschitz corollary. \textit{Gap.} Unlike the \(\Gamma\)-Lipschitz envelopes, these endpoints use only order relative to the target score. \textit{Consumer.} The no-Lipschitz sharpness theorem uses the lemma to compute endpoints without importing smoothness.

\begin{theorem}[thm:chen-oberst-no-lipschitz-sharpness]\label{thm:chen-oberst-no-lipschitz-sharpness}
For every \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), every selected mean table \(y\) compatible with \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\), and every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), the identified set for \(V_\mu(\pi)\) over \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\) conditional on \(y\) is exactly \(\mathcal I^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y)\).  One homogeneous-within-cell array attains all lower endpoints and one such array attains all upper endpoints simultaneously over \(\mathcal F\).  Moreover,
\[
\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr),
\]
and the supremum is attained by two observationally indistinguishable homogeneous fixed arrays.  For every \((\pi,x)\), \(r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}(S)=0\) exactly when a selected candidate uses the matching neutral action, or uses the matching nonneutral action at exactly the target score; \(r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}(S)=1\) otherwise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(S\in\mathcal P_k\) and a selected mean table \(y\) compatible with \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\).  For any array \(Y\in\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\) inducing this table, Lemma \(\text{lem:chen-oberst-monotone-envelope-feasibility}\) gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(a\in\mathcal A\), and \(t\in[0,1]\),
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y)
\leq \mu_{xa}(t)
\leq u^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y).
\]
Taking \(a=\pi(x)\) and \(t=m_\pi\), multiplying by \(q_x>0\), and summing over the finite set \(\mathcal X\), the definitions of \(V_\mu\), \(L^{\mathrm{CO}}_S\), and \(U^{\mathrm{CO}}_S\) yield
\[
L^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y)
\leq V_\mu(\pi)
\leq U^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y)
\qquad(\pi\in\mathcal F).
\tag{1}
\]

Define two cellwise response surfaces by
\[
\mu^-_{xa}(t)=\ell^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y),
\qquad
\mu^+_{xa}(t)=u^{\mathrm{CO}}_{xa}(t;y),
\]
and define homogeneous-within-cell arrays by
\[
Y_i^-(a,t)=\mu^-_{x_i a}(t),
\qquad
Y_i^+(a,t)=\mu^+_{x_i a}(t).
\tag{2}
\]
The cited envelope-feasibility lemma states that both envelopes take values in \([0,1]\), are nondecreasing in \(t\) for every nonneutral action, are constant in \(t\) for the neutral action, and agree with every matching anchor.  Applying these properties unit by unit in (2) and then the definition of \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\) shows that \(Y^-\) and \(Y^+\) belong to \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\).  Since \(N_x>0\), averaging (2) over the units in cell \(x\) recovers \(\mu^-_{xa}(t)\) and \(\mu^+_{xa}(t)\), respectively.  Agreement of the envelopes with every anchor therefore implies
\[
\mu^-_{x,c(x)}(m_c)=y_{cx}
=\mu^+_{x,c(x)}(m_c)
\qquad(c\in S,\ x\in\mathcal X),
\tag{3}
\]
so both arrays induce the given table \(y\).  By the definitions of the policy endpoints, the same two arrays satisfy, simultaneously for every \(\pi\in\mathcal F\),
\[
V_{\mu^-}(\pi)=L^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y),
\qquad
V_{\mu^+}(\pi)=U^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y).
\tag{4}
\]
For any \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), the array \(Y^\lambda=(1-\lambda)Y^-+\lambda Y^+\) remains bounded, nondecreasing for every nonneutral action, and neutral-invariant, so it belongs to \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\).  Equation (3) shows that it also induces \(y\), while linearity of finite-population averaging and of \(V_\mu\) gives
\[
V_{\mu^\lambda}(\pi)
=(1-\lambda)L^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y)
+\lambda U^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y).
\tag{5}
\]
Every point between the two endpoints is represented by (5), including the degenerate case in which they coincide.  Together with (1), this proves that the conditional identified set is exactly \(\mathcal I^{\mathrm{CO}}_S(\pi;y)\), and (4) proves simultaneous attainment of all lower endpoints by one homogeneous array and of all upper endpoints by another.

We next calculate the worst prospective diameter.  By the definition of \(d^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi xc}\), every such cost is either zero or one.  Hence the definition of the residual gives
\[
r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}(S)=0
\]
if and only if some \(c\in S\) satisfies either \(c(x)=\pi(x)=a_0\), or \(c(x)=\pi(x)\neq a_0\) and \(m_c=m_\pi\); otherwise \(r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}(S)=1\).  In the zero case the envelope-feasibility lemma makes the lower and upper envelopes agree at the target response: neutral invariance transports a matching neutral anchor to every score, while a nonneutral exact-score anchor is already at the target score.  In the one case the boundedness of both envelopes gives a gap of at most one.  Thus, in both cases,
\[
0\leq
u^{\mathrm{CO}}_{x,\pi(x)}(m_\pi;y)
-\ell^{\mathrm{CO}}_{x,\pi(x)}(m_\pi;y)
\leq r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}(S).
\tag{6}
\]
Using the definitions of \(L^{\mathrm{CO}}_S\), \(U^{\mathrm{CO}}_S\), and \(R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi\), summing (6) with the positive weights \(q_x\), maximizing over the finite nonempty set \(\mathcal F\), and then taking the supremum over \(Y\in\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\) proves
\[
\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S)
\leq \max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi(S).
\tag{7}
\]

For the reverse inequality, finiteness and nonemptiness of \(\mathcal F\) provide a maximizer \(\pi^\star\) of \(R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi(S)\).  Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\), put \(a=\pi^\star(x)\), \(t=m_{\pi^\star}\), and \(w_x=r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi^\star x}(S)\).  Construct two response functions for action \(a\) in cell \(x\) as follows.  If \(w_x=0\), take both functions identically zero.  If \(w_x=1\) and \(a\neq a_0\), take
\[
g_x^-(s)=\mathbf 1\{s>t\},
\qquad
g_x^+(s)=\mathbf 1\{s\geq t\}.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(w_x=1\) and \(a=a_0\), take \(g_x^-\) identically zero and \(g_x^+\) identically one.  In (8) both functions are bounded and nondecreasing.  Moreover, \(w_x=1\) rules out any selected matching-action anchor at score \(t\); consequently the two functions in (8) agree at every selected matching-action score, being jointly zero below \(t\) and jointly one above \(t\).  In the neutral case \(w_x=1\) rules out every selected matching neutral anchor, so the two different constants create no conflict with observed anchors.

For each cell use this pair for action \(\pi^\star(x)\), use the identical zero function for every other action, and assign the resulting cellwise functions to every unit in that cell.  Call the resulting homogeneous arrays \(\widetilde Y^-\) and \(\widetilde Y^+\).  The preceding construction and the definition of \(\mathcal M_N^{\mathrm{CO}}\) show that both arrays lie in that class.  They induce one common selected mean table because their response functions agree at every selected arm-cell anchor.  At the target point their cellwise differences are exactly \(w_x\), so
\[
V_{\widetilde\mu^+}(\pi^\star)
-V_{\widetilde\mu^-}(\pi^\star)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x w_x
=R^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi^\star}(S).
\tag{9}
\]
The already proved conditional sharpness says that both values in (9) lie in the interval associated with their common table.  Therefore that interval has width at least the right-hand side of (9).  Taking either of the two common-table arrays in the supremum defining \(\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S)\) gives the reverse inequality to (7), and the supremum is attained by these two observationally indistinguishable homogeneous fixed arrays.  Combining (7) and (9), and substituting the definitions of \(R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi\) and \(r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}\), gives
\[
\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x
\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi xc}:c\in S\}\bigr).
\]
The asserted zero-residual characterization is exactly the dichotomy following the definition of \(d^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi xc}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies the exact monotonicity-only endpoints and binary prospective panel loss under the faithful individual-level class. \textit{Gap.} It matches the tight no-Lipschitz ChenOberst2025 regime while making explicit the prospective exact-score or neutral matching criterion needed for panel design. \textit{Consumer.} A designer unwilling to impose finite score smoothness can optimize a panel against the correct binary worst-case loss.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certificate-compression-resolution]\label{thm:certificate-compression-resolution}
Fix deterministic total orders on the encoded finite sets. The dominance-and-symmetry handle admits the following exact resolution. There is a deterministic exact-rational generator which, on every rational instance, returns a finite certificate \(\mathsf{Cert}=(S^{\star},v^{\star},\mathcal T)\) accepted by the independently executed map \(\mathsf{Check}\); acceptance certifies \(v^{\star}=\overline W(S^{\star})=D^{\star}\). The generator enumerates \(\mathcal P_k\) to obtain its incumbent and then branches on the least undecided candidate, closing a node \((I,E,O)\) exactly when \(|I|>k\) or \(\overline W(I\cup E)\geq v^{\star}\). Thus it always terminates, although its tree can be exponential.

For every rational instance with \(|\mathcal X|=3\) and \(|\mathcal C|\leq5\), the generator, exhaustive panel enumeration, and the fixed-selection values of \(\mathsf P_{\mathrm{MILP}}\) agree exactly on the optimum and on the complete set of minimizing panels; with the same deterministic tie rule they return the same panel. In particular, on \(\mathsf E_{3,5}\) the exact value is \(1/8\), the minimizing panels are \(\{c_1,c_4\}\) and \(\{c_2,c_3\}\), and \(\mathsf{Check}(\{c_1,c_4\},1/8,\mathcal T_{3,5})=\mathrm{true}\).

Moreover, the following nontrivial, computable structural condition on the action-score cost tensor guarantees a polynomial certificate:
\[
 \text{there exists }S_0\in\mathcal P_k\text{ such that }
 \min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in S_0\}\bigr)
 =\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{\pi xc}:c\in\mathcal C\}\bigr)
 \quad\text{for every }(\pi,x)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal X. \tag{SC}
\]
Under \(\mathrm{(SC)}\), the generated branch tree consists only of the root. A sufficient condition checkable with \(O(|\mathcal F||\mathcal X||\mathcal C|)\) rational comparisons is that the union of one fixed tie-broken minimizing candidate for every pair \((\pi,x)\) whose full-library minimum is strictly below the dummy cost \(1\) has cardinality at most \(k\). The one-node tree and its incumbent have encoding length polynomial in the rational input length. This finite completeness and structural compression do not assert a polynomial-time exact generator on unrestricted instances; such a generator would, by the stated NP-hardness reduction, imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This is the immediate corollary obtained by applying \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}rational\text{-}generator\text{-}and\text{-}core\text{-}compression}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-score-gamma-saturation]\label{thm:finite-score-gamma-saturation}
Let \(\Delta\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\) be defined by the finite
future-policy/candidate score gaps.  For every
\(\gamma\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) satisfying
\(\gamma\geq\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\),
\[
d^{(\gamma)}_{\pi xc}=d^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi xc}
\quad\text{for every }(\pi,x,c)\in
\mathcal F\times\mathcal X\times\mathcal C.                       \tag{12}
\]
Consequently, for every \(S\in\mathcal P_k\),
\[
\overline W_\gamma(S)=\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S),                \tag{13}
\]
and therefore
\[
D^\star(\gamma)=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S),
\qquad
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{S\in\mathcal P_k}\overline W_\gamma(S)
=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{S\in\mathcal P_k}
\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S).                                      \tag{14}
\]
Substitution of the binary tensor \(d^{\mathrm{CO}}\) for
\(d^{(\gamma)}\) therefore transfers the exact mixed-integer formulation,
the independently checked rational certificate workflow, and its accepted
certificates without changing their panel objective values.  The map
\(\gamma\mapsto D^\star(\gamma)\) on nonnegative rational \(\gamma\) is
the restriction of a continuous nondecreasing finite piecewise-linear
function on \(\mathbb R_{\geq0}\); its finite arrangement is induced by
the positive score-gap saturation knots and the crossings of the resulting
affine panel--policy pieces.  It is constant for
\(\gamma\geq\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\).  If there is no positive same-action
nonneutral score gap, then \(\Delta=\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}=0\), and
(12)--(14) hold for every \(\gamma\geq0\).  These equalities unify the
finite-library design objectives; they do not assert equality of the
underlying aggregate and individual-level response classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal G\), \(\Delta\), and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\) be as in
the finite-score saturation definition, and fix
\(\gamma\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) with
\(\gamma\geq\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\).  Fix a coordinate
\((\pi,x,c)\).  If \(c(x)\neq\pi(x)\), both
\(d^{(\gamma)}_{\pi xc}\) and \(d^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi xc}\) equal one
by their definitions.  If \(c(x)=\pi(x)=a_0\), both equal zero.  Suppose
therefore that
\[
c(x)=\pi(x)\neq a_0,
\qquad \delta=|m_\pi-m_c|.
\]
When \(\delta=0\), both costs again equal zero.  When \(\delta>0\), the
definition of \(\mathcal G\) gives \(\delta\in\mathcal G\); hence
\(\mathcal G\neq\varnothing\), \(\Delta>0\), and
\(\delta\geq\Delta\).  The threshold condition now gives
\[
\gamma\delta\geq \Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\Delta
=\Delta^{-1}\Delta=1.
\]
Thus \(d^{(\gamma)}_{\pi xc}=\min\{1,\gamma\delta\}=1\), which is also
the no-Lipschitz binary cost because the scores differ.  This exhausts the
cases and proves (12).  If \(\mathcal G=\varnothing\), the case
\(\delta>0\) cannot occur for a same-action nonneutral coordinate;
therefore the same case analysis proves (12) for every \(\gamma\geq0\)
while (11) sets \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}=0\) without division.

Taking minima over the same finite set of selected candidates in (12),
then multiplying by \(q_x>0\) and summing, gives
\[
r^{(\gamma)}_{\pi x}(S)=r^{\mathrm{CO}}_{\pi x}(S),
\qquad
R^{(\gamma)}_\pi(S)=R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi(S)                           \tag{15}
\]
for every \(S\in\mathcal P_k\), \(\pi\in\mathcal F\), and
\(x\in\mathcal X\).  The finite-\(\gamma\) width identity and the
no-Lipschitz Chen--Oberst sharpness theorem identify the two causal losses
with the respective computable maxima.  Equation (15) therefore yields
\[
\overline W_\gamma(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R^{(\gamma)}_\pi(S)
=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}R^{\mathrm{CO}}_\pi(S)
=\overline W^{\mathrm{CO}}(S),                                      \tag{16}
\]
which proves (13).  Minimizing the pointwise equality (16) over the same
finite nonempty feasible family \(\mathcal P_k\) proves both the value and
full minimizer-set equalities in (14).

The panel mixed-integer program uses the cost tensor only through its
rational objective coefficients, and its equivalence theorem identifies
its fixed-selection value with the corresponding maximum of weighted
nearest-candidate residuals.  At saturation, (12) makes the
finite-\(\gamma\) and binary Chen--Oberst programs identical after literal
substitution.  Likewise, the certificate checker recomputes the incumbent
width and each optimistic descendant width; by (16), every such exact
rational comparison has the same value after substitution.  Hence a branch
tree is accepted in one formulation exactly when the substituted tree is
accepted in the other.  The certificate soundness-and-completeness theorem
turns checker acceptance into exact global optimality and guarantees a
finite accepted branch tree.  The exact-rational
generator-and-compression theorem supplies a terminating deterministic
generator and independently executed checker for the finite-\(\gamma\)
tensor.  Since their evaluated costs, branch decisions, incumbent values,
and acceptance tests are unchanged under (12) and (16), the entire
workflow transfers to the binary tensor, with the same optimal panels and
certificates.

It remains to prove the shape of the sensitivity path.  Temporarily allow
\(\gamma\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}\) in the explicit cost formula and define
the panel functions and real extension
\[
G_S(\gamma)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}
\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x r^{(\gamma)}_{\pi x}(S),
\qquad
\widetilde D(\gamma)=\min_{S\in\mathcal P_k}G_S(\gamma).            \tag{17}
\]
For fixed \((S,\pi,x)\), let
\[
M_{\pi x}(S)=\{c\in S:c(x)=\pi(x)\}.
\]
The score-cost definition gives the following exhaustive formula:
\[
r^{(\gamma)}_{\pi x}(S)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\pi(x)=a_0\text{ and }M_{\pi x}(S)\neq\varnothing,\\
1,&M_{\pi x}(S)=\varnothing,\\
\min\{1,\gamma\delta_{\pi x}(S)\},
&\pi(x)\neq a_0\text{ and }M_{\pi x}(S)\neq\varnothing,
\end{cases}                                                         \tag{18}
\]
where, in the last line, the finite nonempty set permits the definition
\[
\delta_{\pi x}(S)=\min_{c\in M_{\pi x}(S)}|m_\pi-m_c|.
\]
Every function in (18) is continuous, nondecreasing, and piecewise affine,
with at most one positive saturation knot, namely
\(1/\delta_{\pi x}(S)\) when \(\delta_{\pi x}(S)>0\).  Every positive
\(\delta_{\pi x}(S)\) is one of the finitely many score gaps in
\(\mathcal G\).  On each interval cut out by these reciprocal gaps, every
weighted policy sum is affine.  A finite maximum over policies and a finite
minimum over panels remain continuous and piecewise affine after refining
those intervals at the finitely many crossings of the affine pieces.
Therefore (17) is a continuous finite piecewise-linear function.  Since
the scores and weights are rational, all nondegenerate knots and crossings
are rational.

For \(0\leq\gamma_1\leq\gamma_2\), equation (18) gives
\(r^{(\gamma_1)}_{\pi x}(S)\leq
r^{(\gamma_2)}_{\pi x}(S)\).  Positive weighted sums and maxima preserve
this inequality, so
\[
G_S(\gamma_1)\leq G_S(\gamma_2)
\quad\text{for every }S.                                            \tag{19}
\]
Let \(S_2\) minimize the right-hand criterion at \(\gamma_2\), which
exists because \(\mathcal P_k\) is finite and nonempty.  Then
\[
\widetilde D(\gamma_1)
\leq G_{S_2}(\gamma_1)
\leq G_{S_2}(\gamma_2)
=\widetilde D(\gamma_2).                                            \tag{20}
\]
Thus the path is nondecreasing.  For rational \(\gamma\), the width
identity makes the causal optimum \(D^\star(\gamma)\) equal to (17), so
the latter is the asserted real piecewise-linear extension.  Finally, the
coordinatewise case analysis proving (12) uses only real inequalities and
therefore also applies to the real extension.  Thus (15), with
\(G_S(\gamma)\) in place of the causal loss, makes (17) equal to the same
binary optimum for every \(\gamma\geq\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\); hence it is
constant from the threshold onward.  All these conclusions compare the exact design
functionals over the fixed finite libraries.  They use neither, and imply
neither, equality of the aggregate response class with the unrestricted
individual-level Chen--Oberst class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite score libraries force the finite-\(\Gamma\) design loss to reach the exact no-Lipschitz binary loss at an explicit computable threshold, while the full sensitivity path is monotone and finitely piecewise linear. \textit{Gap.} ChenOberst2025 gives tight monotonicity-only validation bounds, but does not derive the finite-library saturation threshold, equality of full optimal-panel sets, or transfer of the exact panel certificate workflow from a finite Lipschitz design objective. \textit{Consumer.} A trial designer can compute the exact sensitivity elbow, know when larger smoothness constants no longer change any panel ranking, and reuse the same mixed-integer and independently checked certificate machinery for the binary objective.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The common endpoint schedules establish simultaneous marginal lower and upper endpoints, not rectangularity of the full joint value set. The finite-score threshold compares panel loss functionals over fixed finite candidate and future libraries; it does not make the aggregate finite-Lipschitz class equal to the unrestricted individual-level Chen--Oberst class. If future libraries become denser, the smallest positive relevant score gap may shrink and the saturation threshold may increase. The exact certificate generator may enumerate every feasible panel and its branch tree may be exponential. The polynomial certificate conclusion remains conditional on a feasible panel attaining every full-library coordinatewise residual minimum. The outer confidence intervals are conservative rather than shortest, and scores, libraries, \(\Gamma\), and the budget are fixed before assignment.

\section{Honest scope}

The field contributions are the common causal endpoint schedules, exact prospective widths derived from observationally indistinguishable fixed arrays, the faithful bridge to the individual-level Chen--Oberst class, the finite-score saturation elbow, and simultaneous assignment-only coverage. The finite-\(\Gamma\) identity is exact on the individual-level Lipschitz subclass and on the aggregate mean class because their induced mean surfaces coincide. The published no-Lipschitz class instead has the proved binary prospective cost. At and above \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}\), those two finite-library design objectives have the same panelwise values, optimum, full minimizer set, mixed-integer coefficients, and certificate checks; no equality of the response classes is claimed. The certificate workflow is exact and terminating but not polynomial-time on unrestricted instances. The project does not claim a rectangular joint value set, optimal allocation, estimation of future scores, or a saturation threshold uniform over expanding or infinite policy libraries.
