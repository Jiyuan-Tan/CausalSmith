%%% FIELD proof-four-stratum-reduction
Fix \(M\in MC(A,B)\), and write \(\mathbb P_M\) for its induced law \(PM\).  Under \(\operatorname{do}(Z=0)\), the structural equation for \(X\) is unchanged, so \(X=Xn\).  Therefore \(\text{ass:regime-a-match}\), the definition of \(p\), and normalization of \(A\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_M(Xn=0)
&=\sum_{y\in D}\mathbb P_M(X=0,Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=0))\\
&=A(0,0)+A(0,1)=p.
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly, under \(\operatorname{do}(X=0)\), the equation for \(Z\) is unchanged, so \(Z=Zn\), and \(\text{ass:regime-b-match}\) yields
\[
\mathbb P_M(Zn=0)=B(0,0)+B(0,1)=r.
\]

By \(\text{ass:independent-roots}\), \(U0\) and \(V0\) are independent.  Since \(Xn=fX(U0)\) and \(Zn=fZ(V0)\), for every \((i,j)\in D^2\) the inverse-image definition of a pushforward gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_M(Xn=i,Zn=j)
&=\mathbb P_M\bigl(U0\in fX^{-1}(\{i\}),V0\in fZ^{-1}(\{j\})\bigr)\\
&=\mathbb P_M\bigl(U0\in fX^{-1}(\{i\})\bigr)
  \mathbb P_M\bigl(V0\in fZ^{-1}(\{j\})\bigr)\\
&=\mathbb P_M(Xn=i)\mathbb P_M(Zn=j).
\end{aligned}
\]
Substitution of the two marginal probabilities and their complements proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_M(Xn=0,Zn=0)&=pr=w00,\\
\mathbb P_M(Xn=0,Zn=1)&=p(1-r)=w01,\\
\mathbb P_M(Xn=1,Zn=0)&=(1-p)r=w10,\\
\mathbb P_M(Xn=1,Zn=1)&=(1-p)(1-r)=w11.
\end{aligned}
\]

On the event \(\{Xn=0\}\), surgical substitution under \(\operatorname{do}(Z=0)\) gives
\[
Y=fY(Xn,0,U0,V0,W0)=fY(0,0,U0,V0,W0)=Y00.
\]
Partitioning \(\{Xn=0\}\) by the two values of \(Zn\), and using \(\text{def:natural-response-masses}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
a=A(0,1)
&=\mathbb P_M(X=0,Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=0))\\
&=\mathbb P_M(Xn=0,Y00=1)\\
&=s(0,0)+s(0,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Symmetrically, on \(\{Zn=0\}\) under \(\operatorname{do}(X=0)\), one has \(Y=Y00\), and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
b=B(0,1)
&=\mathbb P_M(Z=0,Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=0))\\
&=\mathbb P_M(Zn=0,Y00=1)\\
&=s(0,0)+s(1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Writing \(c=s(0,0)\) and \(d=s(1,1)\), these two identities force
\[
\bigl(s(0,0),s(0,1),s(1,0),s(1,1)\bigr)
=\bigl(c,a-c,b-c,d\bigr).
\]
The four events \(\{Xn=i,Zn=j\}\), \((i,j)\in D^2\), partition the sample space.  Therefore the definition of \(q\) and finite additivity give
\[
\begin{aligned}
q
&=\mathbb P_M(Y00=1)\\
&=\sum_{i,j\in D}s(i,j)\\
&=c+(a-c)+(b-c)+d=a+b-c+d.
\end{aligned}
\]

For each stratum, its success event is contained in the stratum itself.  The four stratum-mass identities therefore imply
\[
0\le c\le w00,\qquad
0\le a-c\le w01,\qquad
0\le b-c\le w10,\qquad
0\le d\le w11.
\]
The first three pairs are equivalent, one endpoint at a time, to
\[
\max\{0,a-w01,b-w10\}=cL
\le c\le
\min\{w00,a,b\}=cU.
\]
Conversely, if \(cL\le c\le cU\) and \(0\le d\le w11\), then \(c\ge0\) and \(c\le w00\); moreover, \(c\le a\) and \(c\ge a-w01\) give \(0\le a-c\le w01\), while \(c\le b\) and \(c\ge b-w10\) give \(0\le b-c\le w10\).  Thus \((c,a-c,b-c,d)\) obeys exactly the four stratum-capacity constraints.  This proves the claimed exact parameterization at the mass-accounting level, including strata of zero mass.

%%% FIELD statement-source-preserving-lift
For every pair \((A,B)\) of normalized nonnegative \(2\times2\) probability tables for which \(F(A,B)\ne\varnothing\), and every \((c,d)\in F(A,B)\), the handle \(Hthr\) constructs a finite model \(M\in MC(A,B)\) with binary-valued roots \(U0,V0\), possibly degenerate, a private root \(W0\) with at most eight support points, mutually independent roots, and one deterministic outcome function \(fY\) shared by all regimes, such that \(q=a+b-c+d\).  The construction reproduces all four cells of \(A\) and all four cells of \(B\).

%%% FIELD proof-source-preserving-lift
Fix normalized nonnegative \(2\times2\) probability tables \(A,B\) and \((c,d)\in F(A,B)\).  Normalization and nonnegativity imply
\[
0\le p\le1,\qquad 0\le r\le1,\qquad 0\le a\le p,\qquad 0\le b\le r.
\]
Define the four proposed success masses by
\[
(n_{00},n_{01},n_{10},n_{11}):=(c,a-c,b-c,d).
\]
By \(\text{def:mass-fiber}\), membership of \((c,d)\) in \(F(A,B)\) is precisely
\[
\max\{0,a-w01,b-w10\}\le c\le\min\{w00,a,b\},
\qquad 0\le d\le w11.
\]
Consequently
\[
0\le n_{00}\le w00,
\qquad
0\le n_{01}\le w01,
\qquad
0\le n_{10}\le w10,
\qquad
0\le n_{11}\le w11.
\]
Indeed, the first and fourth inequalities are immediate.  For the second, \(c\le a\) gives \(a-c\ge0\), whereas \(c\ge a-w01\) gives \(a-c\le w01\); the third follows symmetrically from \(c\le b\) and \(c\ge b-w10\).  In particular,
\[
w_{ij}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n_{ij}=0.
\]
Thus the zero-denominator branches in \(\text{def:threshold-extension-handle}\) are valid.  Explicitly, set
\[
t_{ij}:=
\begin{cases}
n_{ij}/w_{ij},&w_{ij}>0,\\
0,&w_{ij}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
The preceding capacity inequalities prove \(0\le t_{ij}\le1\), and both branches satisfy
\[
w_{ij}t_{ij}=n_{ij}
\qquad\text{for every }(i,j)\in D^2.
\]

Define
\[
\gamma_A:=
\begin{cases}
A(1,1)/(1-p),&1-p>0,\\
0,&1-p=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\gamma_B:=
\begin{cases}
B(1,1)/(1-r),&1-r>0,\\
0,&1-r=0.
\end{cases}
\]
When \(1-p>0\), the row-sum identity
\[
A(1,0)+A(1,1)=1-p
\]
and nonnegativity yield \(0\le\gamma_A\le1\) and \((1-p)\gamma_A=A(1,1)\).  When \(1-p=0\), the same identity forces \(A(1,0)=A(1,1)=0\), so the selected value \(\gamma_A=0\) again lies in \([0,1]\) and obeys \((1-p)\gamma_A=A(1,1)\).  Hence, in both cases,
\[
0\le\gamma_A\le1,
\qquad
(1-p)\gamma_A=A(1,1).
\]
The identical argument using \(B(1,0)+B(1,1)=1-r\) gives
\[
0\le\gamma_B\le1,
\qquad
(1-r)\gamma_B=B(1,1).
\]

Let \(U0,V0\) be independent \(D\)-valued roots with
\[
\mathbb P(U0=0)=p,
\qquad
\mathbb P(V0=0)=r,
\]
and define \(fX(u)=u\) and \(fZ(v)=v\).  These laws are allowed to be degenerate when \(p\) or \(r\) is an endpoint of \([0,1]\).  For \((x,z,u,v)\in D^4\), define the prescribed response probability
\[
h(x,z,u,v):=
\begin{cases}
t_{uv},&(x,z)=(0,0),\\
\gamma_A,&(x,z)=(1,0),\\
\gamma_B,&(x,z)=(0,1),\\
1/2,&(x,z)=(1,1).
\end{cases}
\]
Every value of \(h\) is in \([0,1]\).  Let
\[
\mathcal H:=\{t_{00},t_{01},t_{10},t_{11},\gamma_A,\gamma_B,1/2\}.
\]
This set has at most seven distinct elements.  List the distinct elements of \(\mathcal H\cup\{0,1\}\) in increasing order as
\[
0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_m=1.
\]
There are at most nine listed values, and hence \(m\le8\).  Independently of \((U0,V0)\), let the private root \(W0\) take values in \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\) with
\[
\mathbb P(W0=k)=\lambda_k-\lambda_{k-1},
\qquad 1\le k\le m.
\]
The atom masses are positive, and their telescoping sum is one.  Define
\[
g(h,k):=\mathbf 1\{\lambda_k\le h\}.
\]
For any \(h=\lambda_j\in\mathcal H\), including \(j=0\) and \(j=m\), telescoping gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\{g(h,W0)=1\}
&=\sum_{k=1}^{j}(\lambda_k-\lambda_{k-1})\\
&=\lambda_j=h,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the sum is empty when \(j=0\).  Now set
\[
fY(x,z,u,v,k):=g(h(x,z,u,v),k).
\]
This is one deterministic structural function used under every regime.  By the product construction, \(U0,V0,W0\) are mutually independent, and \(W0\) is private to the outcome.

It remains to verify all eight supplied cells.  Under \(\operatorname{do}(Z=0)\), one has \(X=U0\).  Conditional threshold probabilities and \(w_{ij}t_{ij}=n_{ij}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_M(X=0,Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=0))
&=w00t_{00}+w01t_{01}\\
&=n_{00}+n_{01}=c+(a-c)=a=A(0,1),\\
\mathbb P_M(X=1,Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=0))
&=(1-p)\gamma_A=A(1,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The two \(X\)-row masses are \(p\) and \(1-p\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_M(X=0,Y=0\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=0))
&=p-a=A(0,0),\\
\mathbb P_M(X=1,Y=0\mid\operatorname{do}(Z=0))
&=1-p-A(1,1)=A(1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Under \(\operatorname{do}(X=0)\), one has \(Z=V0\), and the symmetric calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_M(Z=0,Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=0))
&=w00t_{00}+w10t_{10}\\
&=n_{00}+n_{10}=c+(b-c)=b=B(0,1),\\
\mathbb P_M(Z=1,Y=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X=0))
&=(1-r)\gamma_B=B(1,1),\\
\mathbb P_M(Z=0,Y=0\mid\operatorname{do}(X=0))
&=r-b=B(0,0),\\
\mathbb P_M(Z=1,Y=0\mid\operatorname{do}(X=0))
&=1-r-B(1,1)=B(1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the construction matches all four cells of each table, including null rows, so it belongs to \(MC(A,B)\).

Finally, under \(\operatorname{do}(X=0,Z=0)\), the first branch of \(h\) applies in every source stratum.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
q
&=\sum_{i,j\in D}\mathbb P(U0=i,V0=j)t_{ij}\\
&=\sum_{i,j\in D}w_{ij}t_{ij}\\
&=n_{00}+n_{01}+n_{10}+n_{11}\\
&=c+(a-c)+(b-c)+d=a+b-c+d.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the asserted finite source-preserving lift over the entire closed simplex.

For the required destructive check, take
\[
A=((1/8,3/8),(3/8,1/8)),
\qquad
B=((3/8,1/8),(3/8,1/8)).
\]
Then \(p=r=1/2\), \(a=3/8\), \(b=1/8\), \(w00=w01=w10=w11=1/4\), and \(cL=cU=1/8\).  At the lower and upper endpoints choose, respectively, \(d=0\) and \(d=1/4\).  The corresponding response probabilities are
\[
(t_{00},t_{01},t_{10},t_{11})=(1/2,1,0,0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1/2,1,0,1),
\qquad
\gamma_A=\gamma_B=1/4.
\]
In either model the threshold set is \(\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}\), so \(W0\) has the three atom masses \(1/4,1/4,1/2\), independently of fair binary \(U0,V0\).  Direct evaluation gives the four cells under \(\operatorname{do}(Z=0)\), in \((x,y)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\) order, as
\[
\left(\frac12-\left[\frac14\frac12+\frac14\right],
\frac14\frac12+\frac14,
\frac12-\frac12\frac14,
\frac12\frac14\right)
=\left(\frac18,\frac38,\frac38,\frac18\right)=A,
\]
and the four cells under \(\operatorname{do}(X=0)\), in \((z,y)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\) order, as
\[
\left(\frac12-\left[\frac14\frac12+\frac14\,0\right],
\frac14\frac12+\frac14\,0,
\frac12-\frac12\frac14,
\frac12\frac14\right)
=\left(\frac38,\frac18,\frac38,\frac18\right)=B.
\]
The target values are
\[
q=\frac14\left(\frac12+1+0+t_{11}\right),
\]
which equal \(3/8\) and \(5/8\).  Thus both endpoint SCMs have mutually independent roots, use one deterministic outcome function per SCM across all regimes, and reproduce all eight cells exactly.
