Fix \(P\in\mathcal P^{0,\mathrm{bin}}_{d,\epsilon,M}\).  By
\(\texttt{def:control-arm-zero-binary-subclass}\), this law belongs to
\(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}\), satisfies
\(Y(0)=0\) almost surely by
\(\texttt{ass:control-arm-zero-subclass}\), and satisfies
\(Y(1)\in\{0,M\}\) almost surely by
\(\texttt{ass:treated-binary-subclass}\).  If \(p_k>0\), then
\(\texttt{ass:fixed-overlap}\) gives
\[
 P(A=0,X=k)=p_k(1-\pi_k)\ge \epsilon p_k>0.
\tag{1}
\]
On \(\{A=0\}\), consistency
\(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) and \(Y(0)=0\) give \(Y=Y(0)=0\)
almost surely.  The conditional mean is therefore well defined on the event
in (1), and
\[
 \mu_{0k}=E_P[Y\mid A=0,X=k]=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Substituting (2) into the identifying equality in
\(\texttt{def:ate-functional}\), and then using
\(\texttt{def:one-arm-functional}\), yields
\[
 \tau(P)
 =\sum_{k=1}^d p_k(\mu_{1k}-\mu_{0k})
 =\sum_{k=1}^d p_k\mu_{1k}
 =\psi_1(P).
\tag{3}
\]

We next verify the claimed embedding and its risk scaling.  Consider any
unit-scale law in the binary one-arm hard family to which the cited result
\(\texttt{lem:zeng-one-arm-lower}\) applies.  Write its cell masses and
propensities as \(p_k\) and \(\pi_k\), and write
\(m_k\in[0,1]\) for the success probability of its binary treated outcome
\(B\in\{0,1\}\) in cell \(k\).  Construct a full-data law
\(P^{(M)}\) as follows.  Retain the law of \(X\), draw
\(A\mid X=k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\), draw
\(B\mid X=k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_k)\) independently of \(A\)
conditional on \(X\), and set
\[
 Y(0)=0,\qquad Y(1)=MB,
 \qquad Y=Y(A).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus (4) gives consistency, while the stipulated conditional independence
gives \((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X\), as required by
\(\texttt{ass:conditional-exchangeability}\).  Cell masses and propensities
are unchanged, so the source family's overlap bounds imply
\(\texttt{ass:fixed-overlap}\).  For every positive-mass cell,
\[
 \mu_{0k}=0,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1k}=Mm_k,
 \qquad
 |\mu_{ak}|\le M,
\tag{5}
\]
which verifies \(\texttt{ass:cell-mean-envelope}\).  For the control arm the
conditional fourth central moment is zero.  For the treated arm, conditional
independence in (4) and \(|B-m_k|\le1\) imply
\[
 E_{P^{(M)}}[(Y-\mu_{1k})^4\mid X=k,A=1]
 =M^4E[(B-m_k)^4\mid X=k]
 \le M^4.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence \(\texttt{ass:conditional-fourth-moment}\) also holds.  Equations
(4)--(6), together with \(\texttt{def:global-law-class}\) and
\(\texttt{def:control-arm-zero-binary-subclass}\), prove
\[
 P^{(M)}\in
 \mathcal P^{0,\mathrm{bin}}_{d,\epsilon,M}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}.
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover, (3) and (5) give the exact target scaling
\[
 \tau(P^{(M)})
 =M\sum_{k=1}^d p_km_k
 =M\psi_1(P^{(1)}),
\tag{8}
\]
where \(P^{(1)}\) denotes the corresponding unit-scale one-arm law.  Thus
every separation between two source values of \(\psi_1\) is multiplied by
\(M\) after embedding.

For completeness, let \(T_M\) be the deterministic sample transformation
that replaces each unit-scale observed binary outcome by its product with
\(M\), leaving \(X\) and \(A\) unchanged.  By (4), applying \(T_M\) to an
iid observed sample with law \(Q_{P^{(1)}}^{(n)}\) has exactly the observed
sample law
\(Q_{P^{(M)}}^{(n)}\).  Given any measurable ATE estimator
\(\widehat\tau\), define on the source experiment
\[
 \widehat\psi(D)=M^{-1}\widehat\tau(T_M D).
\tag{9}
\]
The division in (9) is valid because \(M\ge1\).  From (8)--(9), for every
source law,
\[
 E_{Q_{P^{(1)}}^{(n)}}[(\widehat\psi-\psi_1(P^{(1)}))^2]
 =M^{-2}E_{Q_{P^{(M)}}^{(n)}}
   [\{\widehat\tau-\tau(P^{(M)})\}^2].
\tag{10}
\]
The estimator in (9) is an admissible estimator in the source experiment.
Therefore \(\texttt{lem:zeng-one-arm-lower}\), followed by (7) and (10),
implies for every \(\widehat\tau\)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[\{\widehat\tau-\tau(P)\}^2]
 &\ge
 \sup_{P^{(1)}\ \mathrm{in\ the\ source\ hard\ family}}
 E_{Q_{P^{(M)}}^{(n)}}
 [\{\widehat\tau-\tau(P^{(M)})\}^2]\\
 &\ge c_{\epsilon}M^2
 \left\{n^{-1}+\frac{d^2}{n^2\log^2(en)}\right\}
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
throughout the dimension range stated by that cited lemma.  Taking the
infimum of (11) over all measurable \(\widehat\tau\) establishes the claimed
ATE point-estimation lower scale by subclass restriction.

Finally, (11) is only a minimax squared-error statement.  Its derivation does
not assert that the source lower-bound prior puts its ATE values in two
separated target sets after any conditioning, nor does it bound total
variation between the corresponding prior-predictive observed laws.  Those
are distinct hypotheses of the interval testing argument in
\(\texttt{lem:coverage-testing-inequality}\).  Accordingly, this proposition
does not by itself imply a confidence-length lower bound; the direct
target-set separation and prior-predictive total-variation calculation used
for that purpose are supplied separately in
\(\texttt{thm:same-class-testing-converse}\), exactly as the final sentence
of the proposition states.
