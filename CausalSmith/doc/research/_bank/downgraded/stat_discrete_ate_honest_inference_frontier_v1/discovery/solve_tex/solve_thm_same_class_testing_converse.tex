%%% FIELD proposed-target-statement
For every \(d\le c_{\epsilon}n\log(en)\),
\[
 \mathsf W^*_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\alpha}
 \gtrsim_{\epsilon,\alpha} r_{n,d,M}.
\]
The \(Mn^{-1/2}\) component is certified by two point-mass priors supported on the exact fixed-\(n\) class \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}\).  The rare-cell component is certified without asserting an unsupported exact fixed-\(n\) rare-prior pair: at Poisson intensity \(N=4n\), condition the published moment-matching priors on simultaneous mass and one-arm-functional concentration, use their directly computed count-mixture total-variation bound, and map every exact-\(n\) honest interval into that relaxed experiment by uniformly selecting \(n\) observations when the Poisson total is at least \(n\), applying the interval to the normalized law, and enlarging it by the observable-independent mass-defect allowance \(M\delta\).  This gives the rare-cell lower bound \(M d/[n\log(en)]\) when \(d\gtrsim_{\epsilon}\log^4(en)\); in the complementary regime that term is bounded by a constant multiple of \(Mn^{-1/2}\).  Thus the displayed full-width frontier is a direct consequence of uniform same-class coverage and a confidence-interval de-Poissonization argument, not of taking the square root of a point-estimation risk bound.
%%% FIELD cited-moment-matching-statement
There exist constants \(c,c'>0\) such that, for any \(c_1,c_2,c_3>0\) satisfying \(cc_1c_3/c_2^2\le 1\) and \(d\le c_3n\log n\), there are two probability distributions \(\nu_0,\nu_1\) on triples \((p,\pi,\mu)\) with the following properties.  Almost surely under either distribution,
\[
 0\le p\le \frac{c_1\log n}{n},\qquad
 \epsilon\le\pi\le1-\epsilon,\qquad 0\le\mu\le1.
\]
Writing \(K=c_2\log n\), for all nonnegative integers \(i,j,k\) with \(i+j+k\le3K\),
\[
 E_{\nu_0}\!\left[p^i(p\pi)^j(p\pi\mu)^k\right]
 =E_{\nu_1}\!\left[p^i(p\pi)^j(p\pi\mu)^k\right].
\]
Moreover,
\[
 a_0:=E_{\nu_0}[p]=E_{\nu_1}[p]\le\frac1d,
 \qquad
 \left|E_{\nu_0}[p\mu]-E_{\nu_1}[p\mu]\right|
 \ge \frac{c_4}{n\log n},
 \qquad c_4=\frac{cc'c_1}{c_2^2}.
\]
%%% FIELD proof-target
We prove the proposed statement.  Throughout, total variation is normalized to lie in \([0,1]\).

First let \(C_n\) be any interval admissible in \(\texttt{def:minimax-honest-length}\).  The exact-class point-mass priors from \(\texttt{lem:root-n-testing-priors}\), followed by \(\texttt{lem:coverage-testing-inequality}\), give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[|C_n|]
 \ge \kappa_\alpha M n^{-1/2},
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
 \kappa_\alpha
 =(1-2\alpha)
 \min\!\left\{\frac14,
 \sqrt{\frac{3\log\{1+(1-2\alpha)^2\}}{32}}\right\}>0.
\]
Indeed, the target separation in that lemma is twice the displayed minimum times \(Mn^{-1/2}\), while its total-variation upper bound is \((1-2\alpha)/2\).  Taking the infimum over admissible \(C_n\) proves
\[
 \mathsf W^*_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\alpha}
 \ge \kappa_\alpha M n^{-1/2}.
\tag{2}
\]

We next prove the rare-cell bound by a confidence-interval de-Poissonization.  Put
\[
 N=4n,\qquad D=d-1.
\tag{3}
\]
Only the regime \(D\ge A\log^4N\) and \(N\ge N_0\), for constants \(A,N_0\) fixed below, needs a rare-cell construction.  In particular \(D\ge1\).  Since \(d\le c_\epsilon n\log(en)\), \(c_\epsilon\le1\), and \(N=4n\),
\[
 D\le N\log N.
\tag{4}
\]

Apply \(\texttt{lem:zeng-moment-matching-triples}\) with sample-size argument \(N\), dimension argument \(D\), and \(c_3=1\).  Choose fixed \(c_2>0\) and then \(c_1>0\) sufficiently small that
\[
 \frac{cc_1}{c_2^2}\le1,
 \qquad
 3c_2\log\!\left(\frac{3c_2}{2ec_1}\right)\ge2.
\tag{5}
\]
This is possible because the logarithm in (5) tends to infinity as \(c_1\downarrow0\).  Let \(\nu_0,\nu_1\) be the resulting distributions, orient their labels so that
\[
 h_1-h_0\ge \frac{c_4}{N\log N},
 \qquad h_j=E_{\nu_j}[p\mu],
\tag{6}
\]
and write
\[
 b=\frac{c_1\log N}{N},\qquad
 q=\frac{c_4D}{N\log N},\qquad
 \delta=4b\sqrt D.
\tag{7}
\]
Under hypothesis \(j\), draw \((p_k,\pi_k,\mu_k)\), \(1\le k\le D\), independently from \(\nu_j\).  The common first moment
\[
 a_0=E_{\nu_0}[p]=E_{\nu_1}[p]\le D^{-1}
\tag{8}
\]
allows us to append a residual coordinate
\[
 p_d=1-Da_0\ge0,\qquad \pi_d=\epsilon,\qquad \mu_d=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Define the conditioning event
\[
 E_j=
 \left\{\left|\sum_{k=1}^D p_k-Da_0\right|\le\delta,
 \ \left|\sum_{k=1}^D p_k\mu_k-Dh_j\right|\le\frac q4\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Independence, \(0\le p\le b\), and \(0\le p\mu\le b\) give
\[
 E_{\nu_j^{\otimes D}}
 \left[\left\{\sum_{k=1}^D(p_k-a_0)\right\}^2\right]
 =D\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_j}(p)\le Db^2
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
 E_{\nu_j^{\otimes D}}
 \left[\left\{\sum_{k=1}^D(p_k\mu_k-h_j)\right\}^2\right]
 =D\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_j}(p\mu)\le Db^2.
\tag{12}
\]
For any centered random variable \(Z\), the pointwise inequality
\(t^2\mathbf 1\{|Z|>t\}\le Z^2\) yields
\(P(|Z|>t)\le E[Z^2]/t^2\).  Applying it to (11)--(12), then using (7), gives
\[
 P_j(E_j^c)
 \le \frac{Db^2}{\delta^2}
      +\frac{16Db^2}{q^2}
 \le \frac1{16}
      +\frac{16c_1^2\log^4N}{c_4^2D}.
\tag{13}
\]

We now derive the count-mixture distance rather than importing a squared-risk conclusion.  In one nonresidual cell, let
\[
 Z_k=(Z_{k11},Z_{k10},Z_{k0})
\tag{14}
\]
have conditionally independent coordinates with means
\[
 Np\pi\mu,\qquad Np\pi(1-\mu),\qquad Np(1-\pi),
\tag{15}
\]
respectively.  For nonnegative integers \(x,y,z\), its mixture probability under \(\nu_j\) is
\[
 E_{\nu_j}\!\left[
 e^{-Np}
 \frac{(Np\pi\mu)^x\{Np\pi(1-\mu)\}^y\{Np(1-\pi)\}^z}
 {x!y!z!}
 \right].
\tag{16}
\]
Expand \(e^{-Np}=\sum_{t\ge0}(-Np)^t/t!\).  The absolute series is summable because \(Np\le c_1\log N\).  Moreover,
\[
 p^t(p\pi\mu)^x\{p\pi(1-\mu)\}^y\{p(1-\pi)\}^z
\tag{17}
\]
is a polynomial of total degree \(t+x+y+z\) in the three coordinates
\[
 p,\qquad p\pi,\qquad p\pi\mu,
\tag{18}
\]
because \(p\pi(1-\mu)=p\pi-p\pi\mu\) and
\(p(1-\pi)=p-p\pi\).  Hence all terms of degree at most \(3K\), where \(K=c_2\log N\), cancel between the two mixture probabilities by the moment identities in \(\texttt{lem:zeng-moment-matching-triples}\).

For either prior, the sum of the absolute remaining terms over all \(x,y,z,t\) with \(x+y+z+t>3K\) is at most
\[
 \sum_{r>3K}\frac{(2c_1\log N)^r}{r!}
 =N^{2c_1}P\{W>3K\},
 \qquad W\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(2c_1\log N).
\tag{19}
\]
To verify the tail bound used in (19), if \(W\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\) and \(L>e\lambda\), exponential Markov with \(t=\log(L/\lambda)>1\) gives
\[
 P(W>L)
 \le \exp\{\lambda(e^t-1)-tL\}
 =e^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{e\lambda}{L}\right)^L.
\tag{20}
\]
Taking \(\lambda=2c_1\log N\), \(L=3c_2\log N\), and using (5), equations (19)--(20) give
\[
 \sum_{r>3K}\frac{(2c_1\log N)^r}{r!}
 \le
 \left(\frac{2ec_1}{3c_2}\right)^{3c_2\log N}
 \le N^{-2}.
\tag{21}
\]
The total-variation distance is one half the \(\ell^1\) distance of the mixture mass functions.  Equations (16)--(21), applied to both priors, therefore imply
\[
 \|\mathcal L_0(Z_k)-\mathcal L_1(Z_k)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le N^{-2}.
\tag{22}
\]
The \(D\) triples in (14) are independent before conditioning, and the residual-cell count has the same law under both hypotheses.  A telescoping expansion of the two product mass functions, followed by the triangle inequality, thus yields
\[
 \|\mathcal L_0(Z)-\mathcal L_1(Z)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \sum_{k=1}^D
 \|\mathcal L_0(Z_k)-\mathcal L_1(Z_k)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \frac{D}{N^2}
 \le \frac{\log N}{N},
\tag{23}
\]
where the last step uses (4).

Conditioning a probability measure \(\Gamma\) on an event \(E\) changes it in total variation by exactly \(\Gamma(E^c)\); applying a common observation kernel cannot increase total variation.  Consequently (13) and (23) imply that the two predictive count laws \(\widetilde Q_0,\widetilde Q_1\), after conditioning their parameter priors on \(E_0,E_1\), satisfy
\[
 \|\widetilde Q_0-\widetilde Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \frac18
 +\frac{32c_1^2\log^4N}{c_4^2D}
 +\frac{\log N}{N}.
\tag{24}
\]
Choose \(A\) and then \(N_0\) large enough that, whenever
\(D\ge A\log^4N\) and \(N\ge N_0\), the last two terms in (24) are at most \(1/8\).  Then
\[
 \|\widetilde Q_0-\widetilde Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac14.
\tag{25}
\]

The relaxed one-arm targets are
\[
 \vartheta=M\sum_{k=1}^D p_k\mu_k.
\tag{26}
\]
Under the prior conditioned on \(E_j\), they lie in
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_j
 =\left[M\left(Dh_j-\frac q4\right),
         M\left(Dh_j+\frac q4\right)\right].
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) give the direct separation
\[
 \Delta(\widetilde{\mathcal T}_0,\widetilde{\mathcal T}_1)
 \ge M\{D(h_1-h_0)-q/2\}
 \ge \frac{Mq}{2}.
\tag{28}
\]
Also, after (9), the total unnormalized mass \(S\) obeys on \(E_j\)
\[
 S=p_d+\sum_{k=1}^D p_k,
 \qquad |S-1|\le\delta.
\tag{29}
\]
Increasing \(N_0\) if necessary, (4) and (7) ensure
\[
 \delta\le4c_1\frac{\log^{3/2}N}{\sqrt N}\le\frac13.
\tag{30}
\]

It remains to connect this relaxed experiment to the exact class defining \(\mathsf W^*_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\alpha}\).  Fix a parameter vector in either conditioned prior and normalize its cell masses to
\[
 p_k^\circ=p_k/S,\qquad 1\le k\le d.
\tag{31}
\]
Construct a full-data law \(P^\circ\) as follows: given \(X=k\), draw
\(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\pi_k)\), set \(Y(0)=0\), draw
\(Y(1)=M B_k\) with \(B_k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\mu_k)\) independently of \(A\) conditional on \(X\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  Consistency and conditional exchangeability hold by construction.  The cited support gives fixed overlap.  Furthermore,
\[
 |E[Y\mid A=1,X=k]|=M\mu_k\le M,
 \qquad E[Y\mid A=0,X=k]=0,
\tag{32}
\]
and, for the treated arm,
\[
 E[(Y-M\mu_k)^4\mid A=1,X=k]
 =M^4E[(B_k-\mu_k)^4]
 \le M^4,
\tag{33}
\]
while the control-arm central fourth moment is zero.  Thus (31)--(33) and
\(\texttt{def:global-law-class}\) show
\[
 P^\circ\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}.
\tag{34}
\]
By \(\texttt{def:ate-functional}\),
\[
 \tau(P^\circ)=\frac{M\sum_{k=1}^Dp_k\mu_k}{S}
 =\frac{\vartheta}{S}.
\tag{35}
\]
Since \(0\le\sum p_k\mu_k\le S\), equations (29) and (35) yield
\[
 |\tau(P^\circ)-\vartheta|
 =M\left(\sum_{k=1}^Dp_k\mu_k\right)
   \frac{|1-S|}{S}
 \le M|1-S|le M\delta.
\tag{36}
\]

Under the Poisson experiment, let \(R\) be the total count over all cells and all three observable labels.  Conditional on the parameter,
\[
 R\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(NS),
\tag{37}
\]
and, conditional on \(R=r\), a parameter-free uniform ordering of the \(r\) observed labels is an iid sample from the observed margin of \(P^\circ\).  Attach such an ordering to the count statistic; because this is a common Markov kernel, (25) remains valid for the augmented experiment.

Let
\[
 L(C_n)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon,M}}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[|C_n|].
\tag{38}
\]
Define an interval in the augmented Poisson experiment by
\[
 \widetilde C=
 \begin{cases}
 C_n(O_1^*,\ldots,O_n^*)+[-M\delta,M\delta],&R\ge n,\\
 [-2M,2M],&R<n,
 \end{cases}
\tag{39}
\]
where \(O_1^*,\ldots,O_n^*\) are the first \(n\) observations in the attached uniform ordering and \(+\) is Minkowski addition.  Conditional on every \(R=r\ge n\), these \(n\) observations have law \(Q_{P^\circ}^{(n)}\).  Hence admissibility of \(C_n\), (34), and (36) show pointwise for every relaxed parameter satisfying (29) that
\[
 P\{\vartheta\in\widetilde C\mid R=r\}\ge1-\alpha,
 \qquad r\ge n.
\tag{40}
\]
When \(R<n\), equations (26), (29), and (30) give
\(0\le\vartheta\le M(1+\delta)<2M\), so the second branch of (39) covers surely.  Thus
\[
 P\{\vartheta\in\widetilde C\}\ge1-\alpha
\tag{41}
\]
for every parameter in either conditioned relaxed prior.

The lower tail in (37) is exponentially small.  Indeed, with
\(\lambda=NS\ge(2/3)N=8n/3\), one has \(n\le\lambda/2\); exponential Markov at \(t=\log2\) gives
\[
 P\{\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\le\lambda/2\}
 \le \exp\{\lambda((\log2)/2-1/2)\}
 \le e^{-\lambda/8}\le e^{-n/3}.
\tag{42}
\]
For every \(r\ge n\), (34), (38), and the conditional iid property imply that the conditional expected length of the first interval in (39), before enlargement, is at most \(L(C_n)\).  Therefore
\[
 E[|\widetilde C|]
 \le L(C_n)+2M\delta+4Me^{-n/3}
\tag{43}
\]
under either conditioned prior-predictive law.

For completeness, we apply coverage to length directly in this augmented experiment.  Let
\(G_j=\{\widetilde C\cap\widetilde{\mathcal T}_j\ne\varnothing\}\).  Equation (41) and prior support on (27) give
\[
 \widetilde Q_j(G_j)\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{44}
\]
By (25),
\[
 \widetilde Q_0(G_1)
 \ge\widetilde Q_1(G_1)
   -\|\widetilde Q_0-\widetilde Q_1\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-\alpha-\frac14.
\tag{45}
\]
The union bound, (44), and (45) yield
\[
 \widetilde Q_0(G_0\cap G_1)
 \ge1-2\alpha-\frac14>\frac14,
\tag{46}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\alpha<1/4\).  On \(G_0\cap G_1\), the interval has length at least the separation in (28).  Thus
\[
 E_{\widetilde Q_0}[|\widetilde C|]
 \ge \frac{Mq}{2}\left(1-2\alpha-\frac14\right)
 \ge\frac{Mq}{8}.
\tag{47}
\]
Combining (43) and (47) gives
\[
 L(C_n)\ge\frac{Mq}{8}-2M\delta-4Me^{-n/3}.
\tag{48}
\]

Finally choose \(A\) still larger, if necessary, so that
\[
 D\ge A\log^4N
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{2\delta}{q}
 =\frac{8c_1\log^2N}{c_4\sqrt D}\le\frac1{32},
\tag{49}
\]
and increase \(N_0\) so that \(4e^{-n/3}\le q/32\) throughout this regime.  The latter is possible because (7) and \(D\ge A\log^4N\) imply
\(q\ge c_4A\log^3N/N\), whereas \(N=4n\).  Equations (48)--(49) then prove
\[
 L(C_n)\ge\frac{Mq}{16}
 \ge c_\epsilon M\frac{d}{n\log(en)}.
\tag{50}
\]
For the second inequality, use \(D=d-1\ge d/2\) in the present regime,
\(N=4n\), and \(\log N\le(\log4)\log(en)\).

It remains only to check the complementary ranges.  If \(N<N_0\), then
\(d/[n\log(en)]\le1\le C_{N_0}n^{-1/2}\).  If
\(D<A\log^4N\), then
\[
 \frac{d}{n\log(en)}
 \le C_A\frac{1+\log^3(en)}{n}
 \le C_A' n^{-1/2}.
\tag{51}
\]
The last inequality is uniform because
\(\log(en)=1+\log n\le(1+6/e)n^{1/6}\), the latter following by maximizing
\((\log x)x^{-1/6}\) over \(x\ge1\).  Thus (2) also dominates the rare-cell term in every range not covered by (50).

Equations (2), (50), and (51) show, for all
\(d\le c_\epsilon n\log(en)\),
\[
 \mathsf W^*_{n,d,\epsilon,M,\alpha}
 \ge c_{\epsilon,\alpha}M
 \max\!\left\{n^{-1/2},\frac{d}{n\log(en)}\right\}
 \ge \frac{c_{\epsilon,\alpha}M}{2}
 \min\!\left\{1,n^{-1/2}+\frac{d}{n\log(en)}\right\}.
\tag{52}
\]
The final expression is a constant multiple of \(r_{n,d,M}\) by
\(\texttt{def:frontier-scale}\), which proves the claim.
