%%% FIELD proof-fixed-alphabet-degenerate-variance
All unqualified expectations and probabilities below are taken under the row
law \(P_n\) and its iid sample law \(Q_{P_n}^{(n)}\), respectively.  Write
\[
 p_{k,n}=P_n(X=k),\qquad
 \pi_{k,n}=P_n(A=1\mid X=k),\qquad
 \sigma_{ak,n}^2
 =E_{P_n}\!\left[(Y-\mu_{ak,n})^2\mid X=k,A=a\right].
\]
The lower mass condition gives \(p_{k,n}\ge c_0/d>0\), and fixed overlap
then gives positive probability to both arm--cell events, so every displayed
conditional quantity is defined.  By the conditional fourth-moment
assumption and
\(\{E(Z^2)\}^2\le E(Z^4)\), which follows from
\(E[(Z^2-EZ^2)^2]\ge0\),
\[
 \sigma_{ak,n}^2\le M^2.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
 h_{k,n}=\mu_{1k,n}-\mu_{0k,n}-\tau(P_n),\qquad
 V_n=V_{\mathrm{eff}}(P_n),\qquad \phi_n=\phi_{P_n}.
\]
The identifying equality in \(def:ate\text{-}functional\) gives
\(\sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}h_{k,n}=0\).  Conditional on \(X=k\), each outcome
residual in \(def:efficient\text{-}influence\text{-}function\) has mean zero,
and the treated and control residual terms have product zero because
\(A(1-A)=0\).  Expanding the square and then averaging over \(X\) therefore
gives
\[
 V_n=\sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}\left\{
 h_{k,n}^2+\frac{\sigma_{1k,n}^2}{\pi_{k,n}}
              +\frac{\sigma_{0k,n}^2}{1-\pi_{k,n}}
 \right\}.                                                         \tag{2}
\]
Every term in braces is nonnegative.  Since fixed overlap implies
\(0<\pi_{k,n}<1\), for \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}\sigma_{ak,n}^2\le V_n,
 \qquad
 \sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}h_{k,n}^2\le V_n.                             \tag{3}
\]

We first analyze the saturated one-step construction.  For validation fold
\(I_s\), define its training size \(m_{s,n}=n-|I_s|\) and the training
arm--cell count
\[
 \overline N_{ak}^{(-s)}
 =\sum_{j\notin I_s}\mathbf 1\{X_j=k,A_j=a\}.
\]
Also set
\[
 q_{1k,n}=p_{k,n}\pi_{k,n},\qquad
 q_{0k,n}=p_{k,n}(1-\pi_{k,n}).
\]
The mass lower bound and fixed overlap imply
\[
 q_{ak,n}\ge q_*:=\frac{c_0\epsilon}{d}>0.                          \tag{4}
\]
Because \(K_0\) is fixed and the fold sizes differ by at most one, there is
a constant \(c_{\mathrm{tr}}>0\), depending only on \(K_0\), such that
\(m_{s,n}\ge c_{\mathrm{tr}}n\) for all folds and all sufficiently large
\(n\).  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(m,q)\), then
\[
 P(N=0)=(1-q)^m\le e^{-mq}.                                        \tag{5}
\]
When \(mq\ge1\), splitting according to \(N\ge mq/2\) and applying
Markov's inequality to \((N-mq)^2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{N>0\}}{N}\right]
 &\le \frac{2}{mq}+P(0<N<mq/2)\\
 &\le \frac{2}{mq}+\frac{4\operatorname{Var}(N)}{m^2q^2}
 \le\frac{6}{mq}.
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{6}
\]

Let
\[
 e_{ak}^{(-s)}=\widehat\mu_{ak}^{(-s)}-\mu_{ak,n},\qquad
 \delta_k^{(-s)}=\widehat\pi_k^{(-s)}-\pi_{k,n}.
\]
Conditional on \(\overline N_{ak}^{(-s)}>0\), the outcome estimate in
\(def:saturated\text{-}one\text{-}step\) is a mean of that many independent
draws from the arm--cell law; it is unbiased and has conditional variance
\(\sigma_{ak,n}^2/\overline N_{ak}^{(-s)}\).  On the empty-cell event its
zero fallback has squared error at most \(M^2\) by the cell-mean envelope.
Equations \((4)\)--\((6)\) therefore imply, uniformly over the fixed set of
\((a,k,s)\),
\[
 E[(e_{ak}^{(-s)})^2]
 \le \frac{C\sigma_{ak,n}^2}{n}+CM^2e^{-cn}.                        \tag{7}
\]
For the propensity estimate, condition instead on the number of training
observations with \(X=k\).  On a nonempty cell the empirical treatment
fraction is unbiased with conditional variance at most the reciprocal of
that count; on an empty cell its squared error is at most one.  Clipping
cannot increase distance from
\(\pi_{k,n}\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), because this interval is contained
in the clipping interval \([\epsilon/2,1-\epsilon/2]\).  Applying
\((5)\)--\((6)\) with \(p_{k,n}\ge c_0/d\) gives
\[
 E[(\delta_k^{(-s)})^2]\le \frac Cn+Ce^{-cn}.                       \tag{8}
\]
Here and below \(c,C>0\) may depend on
\((d,c_0,\epsilon,K_0)\), but not on \(n\) or on the row law.

Define the oracle uncentered score
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \psi_n(O)
 &=\mu_{1X,n}-\mu_{0X,n}
 +\frac{A(Y-\mu_{1X,n})}{\pi_{X,n}}
 -\frac{(1-A)(Y-\mu_{0X,n})}{1-\pi_{X,n}}\\
 &=\tau(P_n)+\phi_n(O).
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{9}
\]
For \(i\in I_s\), put \(D_{i,s}=\widehat\psi_i-\psi_n(O_i)\).
Iid sampling and deterministic sample splitting make \(O_i\) independent
of the training sample \(\mathcal D_n^{(-s)}=(O_j:j\notin I_s)\).
Direct substitution in the two scores and conditional expectation over
\(O_i\) give
\[
 E[D_{i,s}\mid X_i=k,\mathcal D_n^{(-s)}]
 =\delta_k^{(-s)}\left\{
 \frac{e_{1k}^{(-s)}}{\widehat\pi_k^{(-s)}}
 +\frac{e_{0k}^{(-s)}}{1-\widehat\pi_k^{(-s)}}
 \right\}.                                                        \tag{10}
\]
Both denominators are at least \(\epsilon/2\).  If
\(B_{s,n}=E[D_{i,s}\mid\mathcal D_n^{(-s)}]\), then Cauchy--Schwarz,
\((7)\), and \((8)\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E|B_{s,n}|
 &\le \frac{2}{\epsilon}\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}
    \{E[(\delta_k^{(-s)})^2]E[(e_{ak}^{(-s)})^2]\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \frac Cn\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}\sigma_{ak,n}
       +CMe^{-cn}\\
 &\le C\left\{\frac{\sqrt{V_n}}n+Me^{-cn}\right\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{11}
\]
The last line uses \(\sum_kp_{k,n}=1\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \((3)\).
The mean envelope and the identifying formula give \(|h_{k,n}|\le4M\).
The inequality \((x_1+x_2+x_3)^4\le27(x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4)\), the
conditional fourth-moment bound, and overlap then give
\[
 E_{P_n}[\phi_n(O)^4]\le C_\epsilon M^4,
 \qquad V_n\le C_\epsilon M^2.                                    \tag{12}
\]
Consequently \((11)\) and Markov's inequality show
\(\sqrt n B_{s,n}=o_{Q_{P_n}^{(n)}}(1)\), uniformly over the fixed number
of folds.

Before taking conditional expectation in \((10)\), the same score
substitution, the clipping bounds, and
\((x_1+\cdots+x_r)^2\le r\sum_jx_j^2\) give
\[
 E[D_{i,s}^2\mid\mathcal D_n^{(-s)}]
 \le C\sum_{k=1}^d p_{k,n}\left[
 (e_{1k}^{(-s)})^2+(e_{0k}^{(-s)})^2
 +(\delta_k^{(-s)})^2
       (\sigma_{1k,n}^2+\sigma_{0k,n}^2)
 \right].                                                         \tag{13}
\]
Taking expectations and applying \((3)\), \((7)\), and \((8)\) yields
\[
 E[D_{i,s}^2]\le \frac{CV_n}{n}+CM^2e^{-cn}=o(M^2).                \tag{14}
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal D_n^{(-s)}\), the centered variables
\(D_{i,s}-B_{s,n}\), \(i\in I_s\), are independent.  For each fold,
conditional variance and \((14)\) give
\[
 E\!\left[\left\{\frac1{\sqrt n}
   \sum_{i\in I_s}(D_{i,s}-B_{s,n})\right\}^2\right]
 \le \frac{|I_s|}{n}E[D_{i,s}^2]=o(M^2).
\]
Together with \(\sqrt nB_{s,n}=o_P(1)\) and fixed \(K_0\), the exact
decomposition
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{s=0}^{K_0-1}\sum_{i\in I_s}D_{i,s}
 =\sum_{s=0}^{K_0-1}\left\{
   \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i\in I_s}(D_{i,s}-B_{s,n})
   +\frac{|I_s|}{\sqrt n}B_{s,n}\right\}
\]
therefore proves
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{s=0}^{K_0-1}\sum_{i\in I_s}D_{i,s}
 =o_{Q_{P_n}^{(n)}}(1).                                             \tag{15}
\]
Combining \((9)\), \((15)\), and the definition of
\(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{OS}}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{OS}}-\tau(P_n)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_n(O_i)
  +o_{Q_{P_n}^{(n)}}(1).                                            \tag{16}
\]
The leading sum has mean zero and variance \(V_n\) by \((2)\).  Since
\(M\) is fixed and the premise \(V_n/M^2\to0\) implies \(V_n\to0\),
Chebyshev's inequality applied to the leading sum and \((16)\) imply
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{OS}}-\tau(P_n)\}
 \longrightarrow0                                                  \tag{17}
\]
in \(Q_{P_n}^{(n)}\)-probability.

For the variance estimator, independence and \((12)\) give
\[
 E\!\left[\left\{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_n(O_i)^2-V_n\right\}^2\right]
 \le \frac{E[\phi_n(O)^4]}n
 \le \frac{C_\epsilon M^4}{n}.                                    \tag{18}
\]
Averaging \((14)\) over observations and applying Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nD_{i,s(i)}^2=o_{Q_{P_n}^{(n)}}(M^2),           \tag{19}
\]
where \(s(i)\) is the fold containing \(i\).  Centering at the empirical
mean minimizes the empirical sum of squares, so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\le\widehat V^{\mathrm{OS}}
 &\le\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{\widehat\psi_i-\tau(P_n)\}^2\\
 &\le\frac2n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_n(O_i)^2
     +\frac2n\sum_{i=1}^nD_{i,s(i)}^2.
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{20}
\]
Equations \((18)\)--\((20)\) and \(V_n/M^2\to0\) prove
\[
 \widehat V^{\mathrm{OS}}/M^2\longrightarrow0                     \tag{21}
\]
in probability.

It remains to transfer \((17)\) and \((21)\) to the heavy--light outputs.
Let
\[
 \mathcal H_n
 =\left\{\min_{a\in\{0,1\},\,1\le k\le d}N_{ak}^{(0)}
             >256\log(en)\right\}
 =\{\widehat{\mathcal L}=\varnothing\}.
\]
Because the pilot fold sizes differ from \(n/K_0\) by at most one, there is
\(c_{\mathrm{pil}}>0\), depending only on \(K_0\), such that
\(|I_0|\ge c_{\mathrm{pil}}n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Each
\(N_{ak}^{(0)}\) is binomial with mean
\(\nu_{ak,n}=|I_0|q_{ak,n}\ge c_{\mathrm{pil}}q_*n\), and hence
\(256\log(en)\le\nu_{ak,n}/2\) eventually.  For
\(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(m,q)\), exponential Markov with
\(t=\log2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(N\le mq/2)
 &\le e^{tmq/2}E[e^{-tN}]\\
 &=e^{tmq/2}(1-q+qe^{-t})^m\\
 &\le\exp\{mq[t/2-(1-e^{-t})]\}
 \le e^{-mq/8},
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{22}
\]
because \(1-u\le e^{-u}\) and \((1-\log2)/2>1/8\).  A union bound over
the \(2d\) pilot arm--cells and \((4)\) therefore gives
\[
 Q_{P_n}^{(n)}(\mathcal H_n^c)
 \le2d\exp\!\left(-\frac{c_{\mathrm{pil}}c_0\epsilon}{8d}n\right)
 =o(1).                                                            \tag{23}
\]
The all-heavy clause of
\(def:robust\text{-}heavy\text{-}light\text{-}interval\text{-}handle\)
is the exact sample identity
\[
 (\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{HL}},\widehat V^{\mathrm{HL}})
 =(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{OS}},\widehat V^{\mathrm{OS}})
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal H_n.                                     \tag{24}
\]
Thus, for every \(r>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{P_n}^{(n)}\!\left(\sqrt n
   |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{HL}}-\tau(P_n)|>r\right)
 &\le Q_{P_n}^{(n)}(\mathcal H_n^c)
  +Q_{P_n}^{(n)}\!\left(\sqrt n
   |\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{OS}}-\tau(P_n)|>r\right),\\
 Q_{P_n}^{(n)}\!\left(\widehat V^{\mathrm{HL}}/M^2>r\right)
 &\le Q_{P_n}^{(n)}(\mathcal H_n^c)
  +Q_{P_n}^{(n)}\!\left(\widehat V^{\mathrm{OS}}/M^2>r\right).
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{25}
\]
Equations \((17)\), \((21)\), and \((23)\) prove both heavy--light
conclusions.  Notice that this transfer uses no asserted property of either
proposed confidence radius.

Finally, the non-implication is witnessed inside the same class.  Let
\(P_n=P^0\) for every \(n\), where \(X\) is uniform on
\(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), \(A\) is independent of \(X\) with
\(P^0(A=1)=1/2\), and \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y=0\) almost surely.  Consistency and
conditional exchangeability hold by construction.  Fixed overlap holds
because \(\epsilon<1/2\); the cell-mean and conditional fourth-moment bounds
hold with value zero; and \(p_k=1/d\ge c_0/d\) because \(c_0\le1\).
The identifying functional and efficient-influence-function definition give
\(\tau(P^0)=0\) and \(\phi_{P^0}(O)=0\) almost surely, hence
\[
 V_{\mathrm{eff}}(P^0)=0.                                         \tag{26}
\]
Consequently a statistic divided by \(\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{eff}}(P^0)}\), a
variance ratio with denominator \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}(P^0)\), and a Wald
endpoint comparison normalized by
\(\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{eff}}(P^0)/n}\) are undefined.  Since this sequence
satisfies every premise, those standardized, variance-ratio, or relative
endpoint conclusions do not follow on the stated class.  This witness makes
no claim about an absolute \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\) endpoint comparison.
