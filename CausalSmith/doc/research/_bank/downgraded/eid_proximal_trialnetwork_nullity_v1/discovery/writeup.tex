\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}proximal\_\allowbreak{}trialnetwork\_\allowbreak{}nullity}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A finite randomized treatment multigraph can identify transported root contrasts even when every trial-specific bridge is nonunique and the common proxy kernel has structural zeros.  If each covariate stratum has a nonempty proxy carrier positive for every latent class and the required outcome tangents exist only on that carrier, every bridge-null direction lifts to an explicit two-sided interval of observationally equivalent full-data laws.  Thus the target is identified exactly when \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\).  In the rank-one design submodel the exact generic stratum rank is the graphic-union rank \(r_{q_x}(E)\), not merely its saturation value; simultaneous saturation is obtained by a minimum-weight \(q_{\max}\)-fold contracted-union augmentation, where \(q_{\max}=\max_xq_x\).  Separated-rank Wald inference and exact cell-region projection cover regular and rank-unstable regimes, respectively.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published bridge-fiber criteria characterize invariance of statistical solutions, but they do not prove that every target-relevant null direction is realized by two finite full-data causal laws preserving every trial cell and every shared latent node contrast.  Classical tree packing also reports only saturation unless it is connected to the full graphic-matroid-union rank attainable by common stochastic proxy rows.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is a support-feasible causal lift coupled to an exact graph-design rank.  It must tolerate proxy structural zeros, distinguish target identification from full bridge recovery, and coordinate heterogeneous covariate-stratum ranks through one trial menu.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper constructs an active-carrier signed-cell lift, proves exact causal nullity and local ambiguity dimension, represents the generic stratum rank as \(r_{q_x}(E)\), and turns \(q_{\max}\)-fold matroid contraction into an exact-cardinality minimum-cost augmentation.  It then supplies separated-rank Wald inference and rank-free finite-sample projection.

\section{Related work}

Proposition 2.3 of \cite{ZhangLiMiaoTchetgen2023} studies null-space invariance of a linear functional over a statistical bridge solution set.  The present result is a causal realizability refinement in a different finite treatment-network model, not a same-class theorem inclusion.  Proposition 2 of \cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026} assumes both observed bridge sets are nonempty and treats a pairwise indirect comparison through a common comparator; it does not give arbitrary-multigraph attainable rank.  The finite-proxy rank perspective of \cite{MiaoGengTchetgen2018} motivates the proxy ceiling, while \cite{DahabrehRobertsonPetitoHernanSteingrimsson2023} transports through observed rather than latent effect modifiers.  The graph certificates are the classical tree-packing theorem of \cite{NashWilliams1961} and matroid-union formula of \cite{Edmonds1965}; the new step is the exact noncancelling-minor representation on attainable stochastic proxy rows and its causal design interpretation.  \cite{Rosa2018} concerns precision design after rank is available.  \cite{BennettEtAl2023} informs regular inference for an identified functional with a nonunique nuisance, and exact Clopper--Pearson projection is used as a conservative finite-sample complement to \cite{KaidoMolinariStoye2019}.  The NETMOTION consumer is \cite{SotiropoulosEtAl2024}, where the proxy-rank ceiling can bind before graph connectivity.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For count \(K_{sj}\) out of \(n_s\), \(I_{sj}(\delta)=\{t\in[0,1]:F_{n_s,K_{sj}}(t)\ge\delta/2,\ H_{n_s,K_{sj}}(t)\ge\delta/2\}\).
  \item \texttt{rho\_\allowbreak{}x} --- \texttt{active-\allowbreak{}carrier auxiliary probability vector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For each \(x\), \(\rho_x(w)>0\) on \(J_x\), \(\rho_x(w)=0\) off \(J_x\), and \(\sum_{w\in J_x}\rho_x(w)=1\).
  \item \texttt{B\_\allowbreak{}n} --- \texttt{simultaneous primitive cell region}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(B_n=\prod_{s,j}I_{sj}(\delta)\).
  \item \texttt{d\_\allowbreak{}h} --- \texttt{active-\allowbreak{}carrier conditional mean perturbation array}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(d^h_{s,a}(x,u,w)=\rho_x(w)\{r^h_a(x,u)-r^h_{a_s}(x,u)\}/C_x(w\mid u)\) for \(w\in J_x\), and \(d^h_{s,a}(x,u,w)=0\) for \(w\notin J_x\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}n\_\allowbreak{}P} --- \texttt{restricted observed-\allowbreak{}law region}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal C_n^P\) is \(B_n\) intersected with normalized nonnegative tables, the \(\eta\)-cell restriction, and known trial randomization constraints.
  \item \texttt{Delta\_\allowbreak{}h} --- \texttt{active-\allowbreak{}carrier signed-\allowbreak{}cell perturbation}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(w\in J_x\), \(\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}=\Gamma_{s,x,u,w,a_s}((d^h_{s,a}(x,u,w))_{a=0}^{k})\), extended by zero off \(\mathcal Y^+_{s,x,u,w}\); for \(w\notin J_x\), \(\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}=0\).
  \item \texttt{epsilon\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{positive perturbation radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\varepsilon_*(h)=\min_{\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}(y)\ne0}K_{s,x,u,w}(y)/|\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}(y)|\), with the empty minimum equal to \(+\infty\); finiteness of the index set and support restriction of \(\Delta^h\) make every nonempty ratio strictly positive.
  \item \texttt{Q\_\allowbreak{}epsilon\_\allowbreak{}h} --- \texttt{perturbed full-\allowbreak{}data law}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(Q^{\varepsilon,h}\) keeps every component of \(Q\) fixed except that \(K_{s,x,u,w}\) is replaced by \(K_{s,x,u,w}+\varepsilon\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}n\_\allowbreak{}theta} --- \texttt{projected causal confidence set}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal C_n^\theta=\{R_p g:p\in\mathcal C_n^P,\ A_p g=b_p\}\cap[-1,1]^k\).
  \item \texttt{F\_\allowbreak{}P} --- \texttt{observable identifying multiplier}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} When it exists, \(F_P\) is any matrix satisfying \(F_PA_P=R_0\).
  \item \texttt{q\_\allowbreak{}max} --- \texttt{maximum stratum proxy rank}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(q_{\max}=\max_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\).
  \item \texttt{F\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{minimum-\allowbreak{}cost simultaneous rank-\allowbreak{}saturating augmentation}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(F_*\) is a minimum-cost subset of \(E^+\) satisfying \(r_{q_{\max}}(E\cup F_*)=q_{\max}(|V|-1)\), whenever such a subset exists; the contracted graphic-union representation is its computation rule.
  \item \texttt{N\_\allowbreak{}Q\_\allowbreak{}pm} --- \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}completion feasible bridge-\allowbreak{}tangent subspace}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\) is the projection onto bridge increments \(h\) of the finite homogeneous support-restricted observed-cell and shared-contrast system stated in Proposition \(\text{prop:support-face-local-characterization}\).
  \item \texttt{support\_\allowbreak{}face\_\allowbreak{}handle} --- \texttt{residual support-\allowbreak{}face extension handle}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} When some \(J_x\) is empty or carrierwise outcome tangents cannot solve the coupled restrictions, form the finite support-restricted observed-cell and shared-contrast system at each completion, compute \(\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\), and compare its annihilator with the global secants across compatible latent representations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-observed-support]\label{ass:finite-observed-support}
The alphabets \(\mathcal X\), \(\mathcal W\), and \(\mathcal Z_e\) are finite for every \(e\in E\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite categorical proxy model, \cite{MiaoGengTchetgen2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:binary-potential-outcomes}
\(Y(a)\in\{0,1\}\) for every \(a\in V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary-outcome potential-outcome model, \cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-latent-support]\label{ass:bounded-latent-support}
\(|\mathcal U|\le L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A public support bound is credible when the latent modifier is a finite clinical severity type or a prespecified coarsening of a finite baseline profile; it prevents a necessity witness from hiding support enlargement.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely in every trial population.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Causal consistency, \cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:balanced-randomization]\label{ass:balanced-randomization}
For each \(e\in E\), \(Q(A=l_e\mid X,U,W,Z_e,Y_a,S=e)=Q(A=r_e\mid X,U,W,Z_e,Y_a,S=e)=1/2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Within-trial randomization, \cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-population-overlap]\label{ass:latent-population-overlap}
\(\pi_{s,x}(u)>0\) for every \((s,x,u)\in\mathcal S\times\mathcal X\times\mathcal U\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition holds for finite latent-class mixtures in which each prespecified severity type occurs in every harmonized trial population; it excludes population-specific deletion of a latent type while allowing arbitrarily different mixing weights.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-proxy-kernel]\label{ass:common-proxy-kernel}
\(Q(W=w\mid X=x,U=u,S=s)=C_x(w\mid u)\) for every \((s,x,u,w)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Cross-population adjustment-proxy invariance, \cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-proxy-kernel]\label{ass:positive-proxy-kernel}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), the common active carrier \(J_x\) is nonempty.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A nonempty assay category detectable in every latent class suffices; structural zeros in all other categories are permitted.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:proxy-exclusion]\label{ass:proxy-exclusion}
For every trial \(e\), \(Z_e\perp (W,Y_a)\mid(X,U,S=e)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Proximal proxy exclusion, \cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-support-tangent]\label{ass:outcome-support-tangent}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(u\in\mathcal U\), and \(w\in J_x\), the anchor-zero subspace \(D_{a_s}\) is contained in the range of \(\mathscr L_{s,x,u,w}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition is imposed only on proxy categories actually used by the causal lift and continues to allow structural zeros in the joint potential-outcome support.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:shared-contrast-bridge]\label{ass:shared-contrast-bridge}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(a\in V\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(u\in\mathcal U\), \(\mathbb E_Q[Y(a)-Y(0)\mid X=x,U=u,S=s]=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}C_x(w\mid u)g_a(w,x)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The restriction allows arbitrary population-specific baseline risks and transports only treatment-node contrasts. It is natural for harmonized trials in which latent effect modification is shared while prognostic levels and latent mixtures differ.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-cell-positivity]\label{ass:observed-cell-positivity}
Every complete observed cell probability under \(P\) is at least \(\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-multinomial interiority, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:edge-incomplete]\label{ass:edge-incomplete}
\(\operatorname{rank}(T_{e,x})<d\) for every \((e,x)\in E\times\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This restriction isolates the empirically relevant regime in which no single randomized comparison has enough proxy variation for full bridge recovery, so identification must be collective across the network.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:connected-network]\label{ass:connected-network}
The underlying undirected multigraph of \(G\) is connected.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Connected treatment network, \cite{Rosa2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rank-one-rows]\label{ass:rank-one-rows}
For the design theorem, every row of \(T_{e,x}\) equals an edge-specific vector \(t_e\in O_C(\eta_0)\) in the fixed stratum \(x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The submodel describes trials whose treatment-side proxy does not refine the observed distribution of the adjustment proxy after conditioning on the covariate stratum; edge-to-edge latent mixtures still generate distinct attainable rows.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-rank]\label{ass:fixed-rank}
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_P)=r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed-rank matrix stratum, \cite{StewartSun1990}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:singular-separation]\label{ass:singular-separation}
\(\sigma_r(M_P)\ge\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonzero singular-value separation, \cite{StewartSun1990}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-sampling-fractions]\label{ass:positive-sampling-fractions}
\(\lambda_s>0\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Stable stratified sampling proportions, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:observable-reduction]\label{lem:observable-reduction}
For every \((Q,P)\in\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\), the shared bridge satisfies \(A_Pg=b_P\) and the causal target satisfies \(\theta=R_0g\). Consequently, \(H(P)\ne\varnothing\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(e\in E\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(z\in\mathcal Z_e\).  The relevant conditioning event has positive probability by observed-cell positivity.  Balanced randomization and consistency give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_Q[\widetilde Y_e\mid Z_e=z,X=x,S=e]
&=\mathbb E_Q[Y(r_e)-Y(l_e)\mid Z_e=z,X=x,S=e].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, conditional on \((X,U,W,Z_e,Y_a,S=e)\), each endpoint is assigned with probability \(1/2\), and the factor two in \(\widetilde Y_e\) cancels this probability.  Put \(\bar g_e(w,x)=g_{r_e}(w,x)-g_{l_e}(w,x)\).  By successive conditioning, proxy exclusion, and the shared-contrast bridge,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_Q[Y(r_e)-Y(l_e)\mid z,x,e]
&=\sum_uH^Q_{e,x}[z,u]\,
  \mathbb E_Q[Y(r_e)-Y(l_e)\mid x,u,e]\\
&=\sum_{u,w}H^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u)\bar g_e(w,x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The same two assumptions give, entrywise,
\[
T_{e,x}[z,w]=\sum_uH^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u).
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) imply
\[
\mathbb E_Q[\widetilde Y_e\mid z,x,e]
=\sum_wT_{e,x}[z,w]\bar g_e(w,x),
\]
which is the \((e,x,z)\)-coordinate of \(A_Pg=b_P\).  Stacking proves that identity.

For \(a=1,\ldots,k\), the tower property, shared-contrast bridge, and common proxy kernel give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta_a
&=\sum_{x,u}Q(x,u\mid S=0)
  \mathbb E_Q[Y(a)-Y(0)\mid x,u,S=0]\\
&=\sum_{x,u,w}Q(x,u\mid S=0)C_x(w\mid u)g_a(w,x)\\
&=\sum_{x,w}P(X=x,W=w\mid S=0)g_a(w,x)
=(R_0g)_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\theta=R_0g\).  The model supplies this \(g\), so \(g\in H(P)\) and \(H(P)\ne\varnothing\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This isolates the observable bridge equation and target integration map from the separate causal-lifting argument. \textit{Gap.} The pairwise proximal formula does not state the stacked multigraph operator, and its proposition assumes both observed bridge sets are nonempty. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts obtain the finite linear system whose solution fiber contains every causally admissible root contrast.

\begin{theorem}[thm:signed-cell-lift]\label{thm:signed-cell-lift}
Fix \((Q,P)\in\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\) and \(h\in\ker(A_P)\). The support-tangent right inverses produce \(\Delta^h\) with zero total mass and one-way marginal increment \(d^h\). Moreover \(\varepsilon_*(h)>0\), because every nonzero coordinate of \(\Delta^h\) lies on a positive atom of the finite kernel \(K\). For every \(|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_*(h)\), \(Q^{\varepsilon,h}\in\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\) has observed law \(P\), the same latent support, proxy kernels, and assignment mechanism as \(Q\), admits bridge \(g+\varepsilon h\), and has target \(\theta(Q^{\varepsilon,h})=\theta(Q)+\varepsilon R_0h\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(h\in\ker(A_P)\).  Because \(\mathcal W\) is finite and \(J_x\ne\varnothing\), a probability vector \(\rho_x\) positive on \(J_x\) and zero off it exists, for example the uniform law on \(J_x\).  For \(w\in J_x\), the definition of \(d^h\) gives \(d^h_{s,a_s}(x,u,w)=0\), so \(d^h_s(x,u,w)\in D_{a_s}\).  The carrierwise support-tangent assumption and the right-inverse identity therefore give
\[
\operatorname{supp}(\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w})\subseteq\mathcal Y^+_{s,x,u,w},\qquad
\sum_y\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}(y)=0,
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\sum_y\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}(y)y_a=d^h_{s,a}(x,u,w)
\quad(a\in V).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(w\notin J_x\), both sides of (1)--(2) are zero by construction.  All index sets are finite.  If \(\Delta^h\ne0\), every ratio in \(\varepsilon_*(h)\) has positive numerator because \(\Delta^h\) is supported on \(\mathcal Y^+\), so their finite minimum is positive.  If \(\Delta^h=0\), the stipulated empty minimum is \(+\infty\).  Hence \(\varepsilon_*(h)>0\), and for \(|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_*(h)\),
\[
K_{s,x,u,w}(y)+\varepsilon\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}(y)
\ge K_{s,x,u,w}(y)-|\varepsilon|\,|\Delta^h_{s,x,u,w}(y)|>0
\tag{3}
\]
on every changed atom.  Equations (1) and (3) show that the perturbed arrays are normalized probability kernels on the same support.

We next preserve every observed trial cell.  From \(A_Ph=0\), common proxy invariance, and proxy exclusion,
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=\sum_wT_{e,x}[z,w]\{h_{r_e}(w,x)-h_{l_e}(w,x)\}\\
&=\sum_uH^Q_{e,x}[z,u]\{r^h_{r_e}(x,u)-r^h_{l_e}(x,u)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
By (2), the conditional probability of \(Y(a)=1\) changes by \(\varepsilon d^h_{s,a}\), and binary support plus zero total mass makes the change for \(Y(a)=0\) its negative.  In trial \(e\), the left endpoint has \(d^h_{e,l_e}=0\).  For the right endpoint, \(w\in J_x\), and \(y\in\{0,1\}\), the change in the conditional \((W=w,Y=y)\) cell given \((Z_e=z,X=x,S=e,A=r_e)\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\varepsilon(2y-1)\sum_uH^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u)d^h_{e,r_e}(x,u,w)\\
&\quad=\varepsilon(2y-1)\rho_x(w)
\sum_uH^Q_{e,x}[z,u]\{r^h_{r_e}(x,u)-r^h_{l_e}(x,u)\}=0
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
by (4); division is valid because the definition of \(J_x\) gives \(C_x(w\mid u)>0\) for every \(u\).  When \(w\notin J_x\), \(\Delta^h=0\), so the cell is unchanged without division.  Randomization probabilities and all \((X,Z_e,W)\) margins are fixed, proving equality of every complete observed trial cell.  The target records only \((X,W)\), whose law is fixed.

The perturbed outcome kernel remains independent of \(Z_e\) conditional on \((X,U,W,S=e)\).  Thus proxy exclusion is retained.  The positive support of every conditional outcome kernel is unchanged by (3), so the carrierwise support-tangent condition and the same right inverses remain valid.  Assignment, latent mixtures, proxy kernels, latent support, common-carrier condition, and observed \(\eta\)-positivity are fixed; consistency is retained by continuing to set \(Y=Y(A)\), and the incomplete-rank restriction is retained because each \(T_{e,x}\) is unchanged.

It remains to verify the shared latent contrasts.  Since \(\rho_x\) is supported on \(J_x\) and sums to one there,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_wC_x(w\mid u)\{d^h_{s,a}(x,u,w)-d^h_{s,0}(x,u,w)\}
&=\sum_{w\in J_x}\rho_x(w)\{r^h_a(x,u)-r^h_0(x,u)\}\\
&=r^h_a(x,u)
=\sum_wC_x(w\mid u)h_a(w,x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The anchor term cancels in the first equality and \(r^h_0=0\).  Thus \(g+\varepsilon h\) is a bridge for the perturbed law in every population and at every node.  Applying (6) in the target population and then integrating over \((X,U)\) yields
\[
\theta(Q^{\varepsilon,h})-\theta(Q)=\varepsilon R_0h.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (1)--(7) verify every defining member property and prove the claim while allowing arbitrary zeros of \(C_x\) outside \(J_x\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-nullity]\label{thm:exact-nullity}
For every observed law \(P\) compatible with \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\), \(\theta\) is point identified over its compatible full-data laws if and only if \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\), equivalently \(\nu(P)=0\), equivalently there is a matrix \(F_P\) such that \(F_PA_P=R_0\).  In that event \(\theta=F_Pb_P\).  The sufficiency implication holds for any completion class in which every law admits the observable representation \(A_Pg=b_P\), \(\theta=R_0g\); causal necessity is proved on the tangent-rich class \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\), and its pair of witnesses remains a nonidentification witness in every supermodel containing that observed-law fiber.  Relative to Proposition 2.3 of \cite{ZhangLiMiaoTchetgen2023}, this is a causal realizability refinement in a different finite network model, not a theorem-class inclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(N=kd|\mathcal X|\), the number of columns of \(A_P\).  Rank--nullity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}([A_P;R_0])-\operatorname{rank}(A_P)
&=\{N-\dim(\ker A_P\cap\ker R_0)\}-\{N-\dim\ker A_P\}\\
&=\dim\ker A_P-\dim(\ker A_P\cap\ker R_0)\\
&=\dim(R_0\ker A_P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\nu(P)=0\) if and only if \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\).

Assume the inclusion.  For any two compatible full-data laws inducing \(P\), the observable-reduction lemma supplies bridges \(g_1,g_2\in H(P)\) and targets \(R_0g_1,R_0g_2\).  Since \(A_P(g_1-g_2)=0\), the inclusion gives \(R_0(g_1-g_2)=0\).  Their causal targets agree.  This argument uses only the two observable-representation identities, so it proves the stated broader sufficiency claim for any class in which those identities hold.

Conversely, if the inclusion fails, choose \(h\in\ker(A_P)\) with \(R_0h\ne0\) and choose a compatible completion \(Q\), which exists by compatibility.  The signed-cell theorem gives \(\varepsilon_*(h)>0\) and, for every \(0<|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_*(h)\), a second completion in \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\) with the same \(P\) and target
\[
\theta(Q^{\varepsilon,h})=\theta(Q)+\varepsilon R_0h\ne\theta(Q).
\tag{2}
\]
Hence point identification fails on the tangent-rich class.  The two laws in (2) also belong to every supermodel containing this observed-law fiber, so the same pair proves upward transfer of nonidentification.

Finite-dimensional row--nullspace duality gives
\[
\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{row}(R_0)\subseteq\operatorname{row}(A_P).
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the forward implication says that every row of \(R_0\) annihilates \(\ker(A_P)\), whose orthogonal complement is \(\operatorname{row}(A_P)\); the reverse implication is immediate.  The row inclusion holds exactly when every row of \(R_0\) is a linear combination of rows of \(A_P\), namely when some \(F_P\) obeys \(F_PA_P=R_0\).  Then observable reduction yields
\[
\theta=R_0g=F_PA_Pg=F_Pb_P.
\tag{4}
\]
The cited Zhang result concerns invariance of a functional over solutions of an operator equation.  Equation (2) supplies the additional causal realizability statement for this finite multigraph model.  Because the two works impose different model structures, this is a causal refinement of the algebraic issue rather than an inclusion comparison between theorem classes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The equivalence is the exact identification frontier and yields the observable formula \(F_Pb_P\) when it holds. \textit{Gap.} The published annihilator result is statistical and belongs to a different model; causal necessity here requires the signed-cell completion witness. \textit{Consumer.} NETMOTION investigators can identify oxygen-level contrasts without requiring uniqueness of the full bridge.

\begin{proposition}[prop:local-ambiguity]\label{prop:local-ambiguity}
For any tangent-rich completion \(Q\) of \(P\), the compatible target set contains a relatively open neighborhood of \(\theta(Q)\) in \(\theta(Q)+R_0\ker(A_P)\), and this affine space has dimension \(\nu(P)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\nu=\nu(P)=\dim(R_0\ker A_P)\), using the rank identity in the exact-nullity theorem.  For \(\nu=0\), the singleton \(\{\theta(Q)\}\) is relatively open in its zero-dimensional affine space.  Suppose \(\nu>0\).  Choose \(h_1,\ldots,h_\nu\in\ker(A_P)\) so that \(R_0h_1,\ldots,R_0h_\nu\) is a basis of \(R_0\ker(A_P)\), and let \(\Delta_j\) be the carrier-supported signed-cell array constructed from \(h_j\).  Each step \(h\mapsto r^h\mapsto d^h\mapsto\Delta^h\) is linear, so the array associated with \(\sum_jt_jh_j\) is \(\sum_jt_j\Delta_j\).

On the finite positive support set define
\[
M=\max_{s,x,u,w,y:\,K_{s,x,u,w}(y)>0}
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}|\Delta_{j,s,x,u,w}(y)|}{K_{s,x,u,w}(y)}.
\tag{1}
\]
Finiteness and positivity make \(M<\infty\).  Moreover \(M>0\): if every \(\Delta_j\) were zero, then the target-population part of the carrier construction would give \(r^{h_j}_a(x,u)=0\) for all \(a,x,u\), and hence \(R_0h_j=0\), contradicting the basis choice.  If \(\lVert t\rVert_\infty<M^{-1}\), then on each positive atom
\[
\left|\sum_jt_j\Delta_{j,s,x,u,w}(y)\right|
\le\lVert t\rVert_\infty\sum_j|\Delta_{j,s,x,u,w}(y)|
<K_{s,x,u,w}(y).
\tag{2}
\]
The linear preservation equations in the signed-cell proof therefore show that \(K+\sum_jt_j\Delta_j\) defines a compatible law with observed law \(P\) and target
\[
\theta(Q)+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}t_jR_0h_j.
\tag{3}
\]
As \(t\) ranges over the open cube in (2), (3) is a relatively open neighborhood of \(\theta(Q)\) in \(\theta(Q)+R_0\ker(A_P)\).  Its dimension is \(\nu(P)\) by (1) of the exact-nullity proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The dimension \(\nu(P)\) quantifies the locally attainable causal ambiguity rather than reporting only a binary identification verdict. \textit{Gap.} Existing bridge-fiber statements do not establish a neighborhood of support-feasible causal targets. \textit{Consumer.} Designers can measure how many target-relevant directions a proposed data addition removes.

\begin{theorem}[thm:proxy-rank-ceiling]\label{thm:proxy-rank-ceiling}
For every compatible completion \(Q\), trial \(e\), and covariate stratum \(x\), \(T_{e,x}=H^Q_{e,x}C_x\). Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\le q_x(|V|-1)\le\min\{L,d\}(|V|-1)\) for each \(x\), and \(\operatorname{rank}(A_P)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\le(|V|-1)\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compatible completion \(Q\), an edge \(e\), and a stratum \(x\).  Write \(m=|\mathcal U|\).  Observed-cell positivity in \(\text{def:model-class}\) makes the displayed conditional laws well defined on every declared observed row.  For every \(z\in\mathcal Z_e\) and \(w\in\mathcal W\), the law of total probability over the finite latent support gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{e,x}[z,w]
&=Q(W=w\mid Z_e=z,X=x,S=e)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}Q(U=u\mid Z_e=z,X=x,S=e)\,
 Q(W=w\mid Z_e=z,X=x,U=u,S=e).
\end{aligned}
\]
By proxy exclusion, \(Z_e\perp W\mid(X,U,S=e)\), and hence the second conditional probability in each summand equals \(Q(W=w\mid X=x,U=u,S=e)\).  By the common-proxy-kernel assumption, the latter probability is \(C_x(w\mid u)\).  Recalling the definition of \(H^Q_{e,x}\), we therefore have, entry by entry,
\[
T_{e,x}[z,w]=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}H^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u),
\qquad  ext{so that}\qquad
T_{e,x}=H^Q_{e,x}C_x.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(k=|V|-1\), and define the proof-intermediate matrix
\[
\widetilde A_x=\operatorname{stack}_{e\in E}(B_e\otimes H^Q_{e,x}).
\]
The mixed-product identity for Kronecker products and (1) imply, edge by edge,
\[
(B_e\otimes H^Q_{e,x})(I_k\otimes C_x)
=B_e\otimes(H^Q_{e,x}C_x)
=B_e\otimes T_{e,x}.
\]
Stacking these equalities and using the definition of \(A_{P,x}\) yields
\[
A_{P,x}=\widetilde A_x(I_k\otimes C_x).
\tag{2}
\]
Rank cannot increase under left multiplication, while the rank of a Kronecker product is the product of the ranks.  Thus (2) gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})
\leq \operatorname{rank}(I_k\otimes C_x)
=\operatorname{rank}(I_k)\operatorname{rank}(C_x)
=kq_x=q_x(|V|-1).
\tag{3}
\]
The matrix \(C_x\) has \(m\) rows and \(d\) columns, so
\[
q_x=\operatorname{rank}(C_x)\leq\min\{m,d\}.
\]
The bounded-latent-support condition in \(\text{def:model-class}\) gives \(m\leq L\); consequently
\[
q_x(|V|-1)\leq\min\{L,d\}(|V|-1).
\tag{4}
\]
Finally, \(A_P=\operatorname{blockdiag}_{x\in\mathcal X}A_{P,x}\).  Rank is additive across diagonal blocks, and hence (3) gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_P)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})
\leq (|V|-1)\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x.
\]
This proves every asserted factorization and rank bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The ceiling distinguishes information supplied by more treatment comparisons from information absent because the common proxy kernel is low rank. \textit{Gap.} Miao et al. make proxy rank central in one proximal problem but do not tensor that ceiling with a treatment-incidence network. \textit{Consumer.} Trial planners can rule out futile edge additions when proxy rank, rather than network connectivity, is binding.

\begin{proposition}[prop:classical-matroid-corollary]\label{prop:classical-matroid-corollary}
As a classical corollary of the graphic-matroid union theorem in \cite{Edmonds1965}, \(r_q(E)=\min_{F\subseteq E}\{|E\setminus F|+q(|V|-c(F))\}\). Applied after the attainable-row theorem and the tree-packing criterion of \cite{NashWilliams1961}, its disjoint-forest and minimizing-set outputs certify whether the causal operator can generically attain rank \(q(|V|-1)\); no new combinatorial min--max theorem is claimed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The cited matroid-union theorem gives, for the union of \(q\) copies of a matroid with rank function \(r_M\),
\[
r_{M^{\vee q}}(E)=\min_{F\subseteq E}
\{|E\setminus F|+q\,r_M(F)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For the graphic matroid, a maximal forest in the spanning subgraph \((V,F)\) contains one fewer edge than vertices in each component, so
\[
r_M(F)=\sum_{j=1}^{c(F)}(|V_j|-1)=|V|-c(F).
\tag{2}
\]
The independent sets of the union are exactly the edge sets partitionable into \(q\) pairwise disjoint forests; hence its rank is the defined \(r_q(E)\).  Substitution of (2) into (1) proves
\[
r_q(E)=\min_{F\subseteq E}
\{|E\setminus F|+q(|V|-c(F))\}.
\tag{3}
\]
A returned forest family certifies the lower bound in (3), and a minimizing \(F\) certifies the matching upper bound.  Moreover, \(r_q(E)=q(|V|-1)\) exactly when the family consists of \(q\) spanning trees, in agreement with the cited Nash--Williams partition criterion.  Combining (3) with the exact generic union-rank theorem makes these classical outputs certificates for the attainable causal-operator rank; no new combinatorial min--max statement is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The cited min--max formula makes the exact causal-rank result auditable with disjoint forests and a matching upper certificate. \textit{Gap.} The combinatorial identity is classical; only its connection to attainable proximal-network rank is project-specific. \textit{Consumer.} A proposed evidence graph can carry a short verifiable rank certificate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:full-bridge-recovery]\label{prop:full-bridge-recovery}
In any fixed stratum of the rank-one design submodel, generic recovery of the full \(W\)-indexed bridge holds if and only if \(q_x=d\) and \(\operatorname{TreePack}_d(G)\) holds. If \(q_x<d\), no addition of randomized comparison edges can recover the full bridge in that stratum, although selected target contrasts may still satisfy \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix one covariate stratum and write \(k=|V|-1\).  The unknown stratum-specific \(W\)-indexed bridge has \(kd\) coordinates.  Because compatibility supplies at least one solution of its moment equation, recovery of the entire bridge from that equation is equivalent to uniqueness of the solution, hence to
\[
\ker(A_{P,x})=\{0\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=kd.
\tag{15}
\]
The proxy-rank-ceiling theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\leq q_xk\leq dk.
\tag{16}
\]
If generic full recovery holds, (15)--(16) force \(q_x\geq d\).  Since \(C_x\) has exactly \(d\) columns, \(q_x\leq d\), so necessarily \(q_x=d\).  Under that equality, the generic matroid-union rank theorem states that
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=r_d(E)
\quad\text{for \(\mu_C\)-almost every edge-row collection},
\]
and its saturation corollary gives
\[
r_d(E)=d(|V|-1)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{TreePack}_d(G).
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (15) and (17) prove both directions of the asserted generic full-recovery equivalence.

If \(q_x<d\), then (16) is strict:
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\leq q_xk<dk.
\]
This argument applies after adjoining any number of randomized comparison edges, because such an augmentation changes the incidence stack but not the fixed common kernel \(C_x\) or its rank \(q_x\).  Thus edge addition cannot recover the full bridge when \(q_x<d\).

The causal functional has a weaker requirement.  The exact-nullity theorem requires only \(R_0\ker(A_P)=\{0\}\), not \(\ker(A_P)=\{0\}\).  To see concretely why this can hold under \(q_x<d\), suppose the graph contains \(q_x\) edge-disjoint spanning trees and take a generic edge-row collection.  The generic rank theorem then gives \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=q_xk\).  From \(T_{e,x}=H^Q_{e,x}C_x\), every row of \(A_{P,x}\) lies in the \(q_xk\)-dimensional space
\[
\operatorname{row}(I_k\otimes C_x).
\]
Equality of dimensions therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{row}(A_{P,x})=\operatorname{row}(I_k\otimes C_x).
\tag{18}
\]
By the common-proxy-kernel condition, the target proxy row in this stratum is
\[
t_{0,x}=P(W=\mathord\cdot\mid X=x,S=0)
=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\pi_{0,x}(u)C_x(\mathord\cdot\mid u)
\in\operatorname{row}(C_x).
\tag{19}
\]
Every row of the corresponding block of \(R_0\) is a scalar multiple of \(e_a^{\mathsf T}\otimes t_{0,x}\).  By (18)--(19), these rows belong to \(\operatorname{row}(A_{P,x})\), so they annihilate \(\ker(A_{P,x})\).  Applying this argument to the relevant strata gives \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\), even though (16) leaves a bridge nullspace of dimension at least \(k(d-q_x)>0\).  Hence selected, and under this saturation condition all, target root contrasts can be identified without full bridge recovery.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corollary separates full nuisance recovery from identification of the causal functional. \textit{Gap.} Finite-proxy rank results do not distinguish graph saturation of effective coordinates from full bridge recovery in a trial network. \textit{Consumer.} NETMOTION analysts can see why additional trials may identify clinically relevant contrasts without ever identifying every proxy-indexed bridge coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:separated-rank-wald]\label{thm:separated-rank-wald}
Uniformly over every compact subset of observed laws from \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\) satisfying \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\), \(\operatorname{rank}(A_P)=r\), and \(\sigma_r(M_P)\ge\kappa\), the vector \(\sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta)\) is asymptotically Gaussian with covariance \(V(P,\lambda)\), the plug-in covariance is uniformly consistent, and the resulting joint Wald ellipsoid has coverage converging uniformly to \(1-\alpha\).  The derivative is evaluated at the unique minimum Euclidean-norm solution \(g_*=M_P^+\Omega^{1/2}b_P\) of the weighted moment equation \(M_Pg=\Omega^{1/2}b_P\), so uniqueness of the unweighted bridge equation is not required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(p\) denote the finite vector of primitive observed-cell probabilities.  On the stated compact set, observed-cell positivity bounds every conditioning denominator away from zero, so \(p\mapsto(A_P,b_P,R_0)\) is twice continuously differentiable with uniformly bounded derivatives.  The same holds for \(\Omega^{1/2}\), since positive definiteness and compactness bound its least eigenvalue away from zero.

Put \(M=M_P=\Omega^{1/2}A_P\) and \(y=\Omega^{1/2}b_P\).  Observable reduction implies \(b_P\in\operatorname{col}(A_P)\), hence \(y\in\operatorname{col}(M)\).  Therefore
\[
g_*=M^+y
\tag{1}
\]
is the unique minimum Euclidean-norm solution of the weighted equation \(Mg=y\), and \(Mg_*=y\), equivalently \(A_Pg_*=b_P\).  Moreover,
\[
M^+M=\Pi_{\operatorname{row}(A_P)},
\qquad R_0M^+M=R_0,
\tag{2}
\]
where the second equality follows from \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\).

Uniform consistency of cell frequencies and the singular-value perturbation bound in the cited pseudoinverse lemma imply \(\sup_P\|\widehat M-M\|=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Since \(\sigma_r(M)\ge\kappa\) and \(\sigma_{r+1}(M)=0\), with probability tending to one uniformly exactly \(r\) sample singular values exceed \(\kappa/2\).  On this event the truncated inverse is the smooth fixed-rank extension in the cited lemma.

Differentiate \(\Phi(p)=R_0M^+y\).  For \(E=dM\), the cited formula is
\[
D(M^+)[E]=-M^+EM^++M^+M^{+\mathsf T}E^{\mathsf T}(I-MM^+)
+(I-M^+M)E^{\mathsf T}M^{+\mathsf T}M^+.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(y=Mg_*\), the middle term in (3), applied to \(y\), is zero.  The last term has range in \(\ker(M)=\ker(A_P)\), so \(R_0\) kills it.  Consequently
\[
d\Phi=(dR_0)g_*+R_0M^+\{dy-(dM)g_*\}.
\]
Using \(M=\Omega^{1/2}A_P\), \(y=\Omega^{1/2}b_P\), and \(b_P-A_Pg_*=0\), all derivatives of \(\Omega^{1/2}\) cancel, leaving
\[
d\Phi=(dR_0)g_*+R_0L_g\{db_P-(dA_P)g_*\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This uses the unique representative (1), not uniqueness of all solutions of \(A_Pg=b_P\).

The quotient rule for the conditional trial moments in (4) gives
\[
v_e(O_e)[x,z]=
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X=x,Z_e=z\}}{P(X=x,Z_e=z\mid S=e)}
\left[\widetilde Y_e-\{g_{*,r_e}(W,X)-g_{*,l_e}(W,X)\}\right].
\tag{5}
\]
The bracket has conditional mean zero given \((X=x,Z_e=z,S=e)\), by \(A_Pg_*=b_P\).  The target derivative is
\[
\psi_0(O_0)=
(g_{*,1}(W,X),\ldots,g_{*,k}(W,X))^{\mathsf T}-\theta.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (4), the finite-dimensional delta method, and independent sampling by population yield the uniform expansion
\[
\widehat\theta-\theta
=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\psi_s(O_{s,i})
+o_P(n^{-1/2}),
\tag{7}
\]
where \(\psi_e=R_0L_gJ_ev_e\).  The cited finite-multinomial central limit theorem, positive sampling fractions, and (7) give
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta)\Longrightarrow
N_k(0,V(P,\lambda)),
\qquad
V(P,\lambda)=\sum_s\lambda_s^{-1}\mathbb E_P[\psi_s\psi_s^{\mathsf T}].
\tag{8}
\]
Uniformity follows from fixed finite supports, uniformly bounded summands on the compact positive-cell set, and uniformly bounded derivatives and Taylor remainders.  Equivalently, any contrary sequence has a convergent subsequence on which the ordinary finite-multinomial delta method gives (8).

The covariance is nonsingular.  If \(c^{\mathsf T}V(P,\lambda)c=0\), positivity of every \(\lambda_s\) implies \(c^{\mathsf T}\psi_e=0\) almost surely for every trial.  Let \(q_{e,x,z}\) be the \((x,z)\)-coordinate of \(c^{\mathsf T}R_0L_gJ_e\).  If it were nonzero, complete observed-cell positivity would give positive probability to both \(Y=0\) and \(Y=1\) within a fixed arm and \((x,z,w)\) cell; the bracket in (5) then takes two values differing by two and cannot vanish almost surely.  Hence every \(q_{e,x,z}=0\), so \(c^{\mathsf T}R_0L_g=0\).  Equation (2) gives
\[
c^{\mathsf T}R_0=c^{\mathsf T}R_0L_gA_P=0.
\]
The rows of \(R_0\) are linearly independent because the nonzero target cell vector occupies disjoint node blocks.  Thus \(c=0\).  Continuity and compactness give a uniform positive lower bound for the least eigenvalue of \(V(P,\lambda)\).

Replacing probabilities, \(g_*\), and influence moments by their plug-in versions is uniformly consistent by the finite-support law of large numbers and separated smoothness.  Uniform Slutsky convergence sends the Wald quadratic form to \(\chi_k^2\), so the joint ellipsoid based on its \(1-\alpha\) quantile has coverage converging uniformly to \(1-\alpha\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives regular root-\(n\) inference without bridge uniqueness and correctly identifies the Moore--Penrose representative as minimum Euclidean norm for the weighted moment equation. \textit{Gap.} Existing regular-functional results do not state the finite multigraph derivative and its null-space cancellation on a fixed deficient-rank stratum. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts obtain joint Wald inference whenever the retained singular values are uniformly separated.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-cell-projection]\label{thm:exact-cell-projection}
For every finite vector of sample sizes and every \(P\) compatible with \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\), \(\Pr_P\{\Theta_I(P)\subseteq\mathcal C_n^\theta\}\ge1-\alpha\). The guarantee holds at every rank, including zero cell counts and nonidentified laws, uses no rank selection, and the set \(\mathcal C_n^\theta\) has a terminating semialgebraic description after denominator clearing.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each population \(s\in\mathcal S\) and each of its complete observed cells \(j\), let
\[
p_{sj}=P\{\text{cell }j\mid S=s\}.
\]
By the declared independent multinomial sampling design, the marginal distribution of the count \(K_{sj}\) is \(\operatorname{Binomial}(n_s,p_{sj})\), even though counts belonging to the same multinomial table are dependent.  We first establish the required cellwise coverage directly.

Fix \(n\geq 0\), \(p\in[0,1]\), and let \(K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)\).  If \(n\geq 1\), then, at the fixed true value \(p\),
\[
F_{n,k}(p)=\Pr_p(K\leq k)
\]
is nondecreasing in \(k\).  Therefore \(\{k:F_{n,k}(p)<\delta/2\}\) is empty or is an initial segment \(\{0,\ldots,k_-\}\).  In the latter case,
\[
\Pr_p\{F_{n,K}(p)<\delta/2\}
=\Pr_p(K\leq k_-)
=F_{n,k_-}(p)
<\delta/2.
\tag{1}
\]
Likewise,
\[
H_{n,k}(p)=\Pr_p(K\geq k)
\]
is nonincreasing in \(k\).  Thus \(\{k:H_{n,k}(p)<\delta/2\}\) is empty or is a terminal segment \(\{k_+,\ldots,n\}\), and in the latter case
\[
\Pr_p\{H_{n,K}(p)<\delta/2\}
=\Pr_p(K\geq k_+)
=H_{n,k_+}(p)
<\delta/2.
\tag{2}
\]
The definition of \(I_{sj}(\delta)\), followed by the union bound applied to (1) and (2), now gives
\[
\Pr_p\{p\notin I_{n,K}(\delta)\}
\leq
\Pr_p\{F_{n,K}(p)<\delta/2\}
+\Pr_p\{H_{n,K}(p)<\delta/2\}
\leq\delta.
\tag{3}
\]
This argument includes \(p=0\), \(p=1\), \(K=0\), and \(K=n\).  If \(n=0\), then \(K=0\) and \(F_{0,0}(p)=H_{0,0}(p)=1\), so (3) also holds in that boundary case.

There are exactly \(M_{\mathrm{total}}\) complete observed cells.  Since \(M_{\mathrm{total}}\) is positive and finite and \(\delta=\alpha/M_{\mathrm{total}}\), another union bound, which does not require independence among cell counts, yields
\[
\Pr_P\left\{p_{sj}\in I_{sj}(\delta)
\text{ for every }(s,j)\right\}
\geq 1-\sum_{s,j}\delta
=1-M_{\mathrm{total}}\delta
=1-\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
The event in (4) is precisely the event that the true primitive table lies in \(B_n\).  Because \(P\) is compatible with \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\), the assumption of observed-cell positivity gives every true complete cell probability at least \(\eta\), and the model-class restrictions give normalization, nonnegativity, and the known balanced-randomization equalities.  Hence, by the definition of \(\mathcal C_n^P\),
\[
\{P\in B_n\}\subseteq\{P\in\mathcal C_n^P\}.
\tag{5}
\]

We next prove the deterministic causal projection on the event in (5).  Let \(\vartheta\in\Theta_I(P)\).  By the definition of \(\Theta_I(P)\), there is a law \(Q\in\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\) such that \(\operatorname{obs}(Q)=P\) and \(\vartheta=\theta(Q)\).  The observable-reduction lemma applied to this \(Q\) supplies its shared bridge \(g_Q\) and states
\[
A_Pg_Q=b_P,
\qquad
\theta(Q)=R_0g_Q.
\tag{6}
\]
Evaluating the candidate-law maps at the candidate table \(p=P\) gives, by their definitions,
\[
A_p\big|_{p=P}=A_P,
\qquad
b_p\big|_{p=P}=b_P,
\qquad
R_p\big|_{p=P}=R_0.
\tag{7}
\]
On \(\{P\in\mathcal C_n^P\}\), equations (6) and (7) show that the feasible pair \((p,g)=(P,g_Q)\) is included in the projection defining \(\mathcal C_n^\theta\), and its projected value is \(R_0g_Q=\vartheta\).  Moreover, binary potential outcomes imply pointwise
\[
-1\leq Y(a)-Y(0)\leq 1,
\]
so conditional expectation gives \(\vartheta_a\in[-1,1]\) for every \(a=1,\ldots,k\).  The final intersection with \([-1,1]^k\) therefore does not remove \(\vartheta\).  Since \(Q\), and hence \(\vartheta\), was arbitrary, we have proved the deterministic implication
\[
P\in\mathcal C_n^P
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Theta_I(P)\subseteq\mathcal C_n^\theta.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (4), (5), and (8) proves
\[
\Pr_P\{\Theta_I(P)\subseteq\mathcal C_n^\theta\}
\geq 1-\alpha.
\tag{9}
\]
No step in (1)--(9) selects a rank, divides by a singular value, or assumes identification.  Thus (9) holds for every rank of \(A_P\), including rank-deficient and nonidentified laws, and it permits zero observed counts.

It remains to verify the asserted terminating semialgebraic description.  For a trial \(e\), write its candidate complete-cell probabilities as \(p_e(a,y,x,w,z)\), and define the polynomial expressions
\[
q_e(x,z,w)=\sum_{a\in\{l_e,r_e\}}\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}
p_e(a,y,x,w,z),
\qquad
q_e(x,z)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}q_e(x,z,w),
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
m_e(x,z)=2\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}
\{p_e(r_e,1,x,w,z)-p_e(l_e,1,x,w,z)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The \(\eta\)-cell restriction and \(\eta>0\) imply \(q_e(x,z)>0\).  By the definitions of \(T_{e,x}\), \(b_p\), and the signed incidence row \(B_e\), the \((e,x,z)\) row of \(A_pg=b_p\) is
\[
\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}
\frac{q_e(x,z,w)}{q_e(x,z)}
\{g_{r_e}(w,x)-g_{l_e}(w,x)\}
=\frac{m_e(x,z)}{q_e(x,z)}.
\tag{12}
\]
Because the common denominator in (12) is strictly positive, (12) is equivalent, not merely implied, to the polynomial equation
\[
\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}q_e(x,z,w)
\{g_{r_e}(w,x)-g_{l_e}(w,x)\}
=m_e(x,z).
\tag{13}
\]
Here \(g_0(w,x)=0\) by the bridge definition, so (13) also covers edges incident to the root.  The known balanced-randomization restrictions are the polynomial equalities
\[
2\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}p_e(a,y,x,w,z)=q_e(x,z,w),
\qquad a\in\{l_e,r_e\},
\tag{14}
\]
and, writing \(p_0(x,w)\) for a target-table cell, target integration is encoded by
\[
\vartheta_a=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}
p_0(x,w)g_a(w,x),
\qquad a=1,\ldots,k.
\tag{15}
\]

For fixed counts and sample sizes, each constraint
\[
F_{n_s,K_{sj}}(p_{sj})\geq\frac{\alpha}{2M_{\mathrm{total}}},
\qquad
H_{n_s,K_{sj}}(p_{sj})\geq\frac{\alpha}{2M_{\mathrm{total}}}
\tag{16}
\]
is a polynomial inequality, since \(F_{n,k}\) and \(H_{n,k}\) are finite binomial polynomials.  Multiplication by the positive integer \(2M_{\mathrm{total}}\) clears the only displayed rational denominator.  Normalization, nonnegativity, the bounds \(p_{sj}\geq\eta\), equations (13)--(15), and \(-1\leq\vartheta_a\leq1\) are also finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities.  Finiteness follows from the finite observed supports in the model class.  Consequently, membership of \(\vartheta\) in \(\mathcal C_n^\theta\) is expressed exactly by a finite formula of the form
\[
\exists p\,\exists g\;\Phi(p,g,\vartheta;\alpha,\eta),
\tag{17}
\]
where \(\Phi\) is a Boolean combination of polynomial equalities and inequalities.  Treating \(\alpha\) and \(\eta\) as additional free variables makes the coefficients of (17) rational; specialization to their declared values occurs after elimination.  The cited real-closed-field elimination lemma therefore supplies an algorithmically computable, equivalent quantifier-free formula in \((\vartheta,\alpha,\eta)\), and the elimination algorithm terminates.  Thus \(\mathcal C_n^\theta\) has the claimed terminating semialgebraic description.  The construction uses neither a bound on \(g\) nor a rank choice.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This provides finite-sample coverage of every causally compatible contrast through rank transitions where Wald theory is discontinuous. \textit{Gap.} Kaido et al. provide uniformly asymptotic calibrated projection, whereas the finite multinomial model permits an exact conservative primitive region whose deterministic projection remains valid without rank regularity. \textit{Consumer.} Small neonatal trial networks receive honest simultaneous regions even when proxy rank is weak, deficient, or empirically unstable.

\begin{theorem}[thm:generic-matroid-union-rank]\label{thm:generic-matroid-union-rank}
Fix a covariate stratum \(x\), a stochastic common proxy kernel \(C_x\) of rank \(q=q_x\), and nonempty \(O_C(\eta_0)\).  Under the rank-one row submodel, for every treatment multigraph \(G\),
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=r_q(E)
\]
for \(\mu_C\)-almost every edge-row collection.  The deficient collections are exactly the common zero set, in relative coordinates on \(H_C^E\), of all \(r_q(E)\)-minors of \(A_{P,x}\), with the \(0\)-minor equal to one.  Consequently the generic global rank is \(\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}r_{q_x}(E)\).  In particular, rank \(q_x(|V|-1)\) is attained generically in stratum \(x\) if and only if \(G\) contains \(q_x\) edge-disjoint spanning trees, and simultaneous stratumwise saturation holds if and only if \(G\) contains \(q_{\max}=\max_xq_x\) edge-disjoint spanning trees.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppress the fixed stratum \(x\), and put \(k=|V|-1\).  Every row \(c_u=C_x(u,\cdot)\) is stochastic.  If \(S_C=\operatorname{span}\{c_u:u\in\mathcal U\}\), then \(\dim S_C=q\), while the coordinate-sum functional \(\ell(t)=\sum_wt_w\) is nonzero on \(S_C\) and equals one on each \(c_u\).  Therefore
\[
H_C=\operatorname{aff}\{c_u:u\in\mathcal U\}
=\{t\in S_C:\ell(t)=1\},
\qquad \dim H_C=q-1.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose \(\pi_*\in D_C(\eta_0)\), possible because \(O_C(\eta_0)\ne\varnothing\).  All defining inequalities are strict, so \(\pi_*+v\in D_C(\eta_0)\) for every sufficiently small \(v\) with \(\sum_uv_u=0\).  The map \(v\mapsto v^{\mathsf T}C_x\) sends this zero-sum subspace onto \(H_C-H_C\), since row differences span the latter.  A linear right inverse on this finite-dimensional image maps a sufficiently small ball into the admissible neighborhood of \(\pi_*\).  Thus \(O_C(\eta_0)\) is relatively open in \(H_C\) and has affine hull \(H_C\).

Choose coordinates on \(S_C\) whose first coordinate is \(\ell\), and extend them to an invertible coordinate change on \(\mathbb R^d\).  Every attainable row then has the form
\[
t_e=(1,p_{e1},\ldots,p_{e,q-1},0,\ldots,0).
\]
After deleting duplicate rows of each rank-one \(T_{e,x}\), permuting columns, and applying the invertible column change, the nonzero part of \(A_{P,x}\) is
\[
\mathcal A(p)=
\bigl[B,\operatorname{diag}(p_{\cdot1})B,\ldots,
\operatorname{diag}(p_{\cdot,q-1})B\bigr],
\tag{2}
\]
where \(B\) is the reduced incidence matrix.  The remaining \(k(d-q)\) columns are zero.

We use the following elementary incidence fact.  A set \(F\subseteq E\) of incidence rows is linearly independent if and only if \(F\) is a forest.  If \(F\) contains a cycle, its signed cycle sum is zero.  If \(F\) is a forest, root each component, delete from the vertex columns one root per component, and order every other vertex after its parent; the corresponding \(|F|\)-square incidence submatrix is triangular up to signs and permutations, with diagonal entries \(\pm1\).  This proves both directions, including disconnected spanning subgraphs.

Consider any nonzero \(s\)-square minor of (2).  Group its selected columns by the \(q\) blocks, with block sizes \(s_0,\ldots,s_{q-1}\).  Laplace expansion by these column blocks partitions the selected row set into disjoint sets \(F_0,\ldots,F_{q-1}\), where \(|F_j|=s_j\).  A nonzero expansion term requires every incidence subminor \(B[F_j,C_j]\) to be nonzero; by the incidence fact, each \(F_j\) is a forest.  Hence
\[
s=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}|F_j|\le r_q(E).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\le r_q(E)\) for every attainable row collection.

Conversely, choose pairwise disjoint forests \(F_0,\ldots,F_{q-1}\) with total size \(r_q(E)\).  For each \(j\), the incidence fact supplies \(|F_j|\) vertex columns \(C_j\) for which \(\det B[F_j,C_j]\ne0\).  Select in (2) the row set \(F_0\cup\cdots\cup F_{q-1}\) and, from block \(j\), the columns \(C_j\).  In its determinant expansion, the ordered partition \((F_0,\ldots,F_{q-1})\) contributes
\[
\pm\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\det B[F_j,C_j]
\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}\prod_{e\in F_j}p_{ej}.
\tag{4}
\]
Distinct ordered partitions give distinct monomials: the variables in (4) determine \(F_1,\ldots,F_{q-1}\), and their complement in the selected rows determines \(F_0\).  Therefore the nonzero coefficient in (4) cannot cancel, and this \(r_q(E)\)-minor is a nonzero polynomial.

A nonzero real polynomial has a Lebesgue-null zero set.  This follows by induction on the number of variables: outside the common zero set of its coefficient polynomials, every one-dimensional section has finitely many roots, and the induction hypothesis and Fubini's theorem finish the argument.  Since \(O_C(\eta_0)^E\) is relatively open in \(H_C^E\), (3)--(4) imply
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=r_q(E)
\quad\text{for }\mu_C\text{-almost every collection}.
\tag{5}
\]
Rank is below \(r_q(E)\) exactly when all minors of that size vanish; for \(r_q(E)=0\), the sole \(0\)-minor is one and the deficient locus is empty.  This proves the stated algebraic description.

Block diagonality and the finiteness of \(\mathcal X\) give the generic global rank \(\sum_xr_{q_x}(E)\).  Finally, \(r_q(E)=qk\) exactly when the maximizing disjoint forests all have \(k\) edges, hence are spanning trees; conversely, \(q\) disjoint spanning trees attain \(qk\).  Because a packing of \(q_{\max}\) trees contains a packing of \(q_x\) trees for every \(x\), simultaneous saturation is equivalent to the \(q_{\max}\)-tree condition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives the exact generic rank \(r_{q_x}(E)\) for every graph and recovers tree packing only at saturation. \textit{Gap.} The arbitrary-size block-minor expansion and attainable-row noncancellation are the project-specific representation step beyond the classical combinatorial rank formula. \textit{Consumer.} Network curators receive both an exact rank forecast and a certificate of the remaining deficit.

\begin{proposition}[prop:qmax-network-augmentation]\label{prop:qmax-network-augmentation}
Let \(q_{\max}=\max_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x\), and suppose the candidate menu contains \(F\subseteq E^+\) with \(r_{q_{\max}}(E\cup F)=q_{\max}(|V|-1)\).  The minimum-weight contracted-union augmentation \(F_*\) exists, every minimum-cost saturating augmentation has exactly
\[
q_{\max}(|V|-1)-r_{q_{\max}}(E)
\]
new labeled edges, and \(G+F_*\) generically attains rank \(q_x(|V|-1)\) in every stratum.  Hence \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\) and all target root contrasts are generically identified for every compatible target latent mixture.  If the menu is infeasible at rank \(q_{\max}(|V|-1)\), no permitted augmentation simultaneously saturates all proxy-effective stratum spaces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(M\) be the union of \(q_{\max}\) copies of the graphic matroid on \(E\cup E^+\), and put
\[
K=q_{\max}(|V|-1),\qquad r_E=r_{q_{\max}}(E).
\]
Choose a basis \(I\subseteq E\) of the restriction \(M|E\), so \(|I|=r_E\).  The feasibility hypothesis says that \(M|(E\cup E^+)\) has rank \(K\).  By the matroid basis-extension axiom, \(I\) extends using candidate elements to a basis \(I\cup F\) of size \(K\).  Equivalently, the contraction by \(I\), after deletion of \(E\setminus I\), has a basis \(F\subseteq E^+\) of size
\[
|F|=K-r_E=q_{\max}(|V|-1)-r_{q_{\max}}(E).
\tag{1}
\]
The candidate ground set is finite, so a minimum-weight such basis exists.

Now let \(F'\subseteq E^+\) be any saturating augmentation.  Again by basis extension, \(I\) extends to a basis of \(M|(E\cup F')\) using a subset \(F''\subseteq F'\), and (1) gives \(|F''|=K-r_E\).  If \(F'\setminus F''\ne\varnothing\), strict positivity of every \(c^+(e)\) makes \(F''\) strictly cheaper.  Therefore every minimum-cost saturating augmentation contains no redundant element and has exactly the cardinality in (1).  This also proves the equivalence between minimum-cost saturation and the minimum-weight basis problem in the contracted union matroid.

The equality \(r_{q_{\max}}(E\cup F_*)=K\) means that \(G+F_*\) contains \(q_{\max}\) edge-disjoint spanning trees.  Retaining any \(q_x\) of them and applying the exact generic rank theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=q_x(|V|-1)
\tag{2}
\]
for almost every attainable row collection in every stratum.  For any compatible target population,
\[
P(X=x,W=\cdot\mid S=0)
=P(X=x\mid S=0)\,\pi_{0,x}^{\mathsf T}C_x
\in\operatorname{row}(C_x).
\tag{3}
\]
Common-proxy factorization gives
\[
\operatorname{row}(A_{P,x})
\subseteq\operatorname{row}(I_{|V|-1}\otimes C_x),
\]
and both sides have dimension \(q_x(|V|-1)\) under (2), so the row spaces are equal.  Equation (3), node block by node block, therefore implies \(\operatorname{row}(R_0)\subseteq\operatorname{row}(A_P)\), or \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\).  Exact nullity identifies all root contrasts for every compatible target mixture.  If feasibility fails at rank \(K\), no subset of the candidate menu can contain \(q_{\max}\) disjoint spanning trees, and the same exact generic rank theorem rules out simultaneous saturation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition gives one simultaneous multistratum design and proves that every positive-cost optimum uses exactly the union-rank deficit in new edges. \textit{Gap.} Classical weighted matroid algorithms do not supply the causal row-space argument or the \(q_{\max}\) heterogeneous-stratum interpretation. \textit{Consumer.} NETMOTION planners can distinguish a rank-completing trial menu from one that is provably unable to saturate all strata.

\begin{proposition}[prop:support-face-local-characterization]\label{prop:support-face-local-characterization}
Fix a finite compatible completion \(Q\), allowing zeros in \(C_x\) and arbitrary positive-support faces of \(K_{s,x,u,w}\), and freeze its latent mixtures, proxy kernels, covariate laws, and assignment mechanism.  Define \(\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\) as the set of arrays \(h\), with \(h_0=0\), for which signed arrays \(\xi_{s,x,u,w}\), supported on \(\mathcal Y^+_{s,x,u,w}\), satisfy
\[
\sum_y\xi_{s,x,u,w}(y)=0,
\qquad
d_{s,a}(x,u,w)=\sum_y\xi_{s,x,u,w}(y)y_a,
\]
\[
\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}H^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u)d_{e,a}(x,u,w)=0
\]
for every \(e,x,z,w\) and \(a\in\{l_e,r_e\}\), and
\[
\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}C_x(w\mid u)
\{d_{s,a}(x,u,w)-d_{s,0}(x,u,w)\}
=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}C_x(w\mid u)h_a(w,x)
\]
for every \(s,x,u,a\).  Then \(\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\) is the kernel projection of an explicit finite linear system and is computable by Gaussian elimination.  Its image \(R_0\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\) is exactly the space of target tangent directions generated by two-sided fixed-support perturbations that freeze the stated factors and admit bridge derivative \(h\).  Local point identification along this frozen-factor route holds if and only if \(R_0\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}=\{0\}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All displayed constraints in the statement are homogeneous linear equations in \((h,\xi)\); support restriction merely deletes coordinates.  Their solution set is therefore a finite-dimensional linear subspace, and its projection onto \(h\) is \(\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\).  Row reduction computes both this projection and \(R_0\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\).

Take \(h\in\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\) and a witnessing array \(\xi\).  Since the positive supports are finite, the number
\[
\delta(\xi)=
\min_{\xi_{s,x,u,w}(y)\ne0}
\frac{K_{s,x,u,w}(y)}{|\xi_{s,x,u,w}(y)|}
\tag{1}
\]
is positive, with the empty minimum equal to \(+\infty\).  For \(|\varepsilon|<\delta(\xi)\), the kernels \(K+\varepsilon\xi\) are nonnegative on the same support and normalized by \(\sum_y\xi=0\).  The marginal probability of \(Y(a)=1\) changes by \(\varepsilon d_{s,a}\); binary support makes the change for \(Y(a)=0\) its negative.

For a trial endpoint \(a\in\{l_e,r_e\}\), the change in the conditional observed cell \((W=w,Y=y)\), given \((Z_e=z,X=x,S=e,A=a)\), is
\[
\varepsilon(2y-1)
\sum_uH^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u)d_{e,a}(x,u,w),
\tag{2}
\]
which is zero by the first family of linear equations.  Balanced randomization and every frozen non-outcome factor then show that all complete observed trial cells are unchanged.  The target \((X,W)\) law is frozen.  Because the perturbed kernel depends on \((S,X,U,W)\) but not on \(Z_e\), proxy exclusion remains valid.

The second family of equations gives, for every population and node, that the perturbation of the latent treatment contrast equals
\[
\varepsilon\sum_wC_x(w\mid u)h_a(w,x).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, if \(g\) is the original bridge, \(g+\varepsilon h\) is a bridge for the perturbed law.  In the target population, integration of (3) over \((X,U)\) gives target increment \(\varepsilon R_0h\).  Hence every vector in \(R_0\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\) is attained as a two-sided frozen-factor target tangent direction.

Conversely, consider any differentiable two-sided fixed-support path with the stated frozen factors and bridge derivative \(h\).  Let \(\xi\) be the derivative of its outcome kernel at zero.  Normalization gives \(\sum_y\xi=0\).  If \(K(y)=0\), nonnegativity for both signs forces \(\xi(y)=0\), so \(\xi\) is supported on \(\mathcal Y^+\).  Differentiating each observed-cell equality yields (2) equal to zero, and differentiating the shared-contrast bridge equality yields (3).  Therefore \(h\in\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\).  The attainable tangent-direction space is exactly \(R_0\mathcal N_Q^{\pm}\), and it is zero exactly when the target is locally invariant along every path in this frozen-factor route.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact computable side of the residual sparse-support frontier. \textit{Gap.} It deliberately fixes a latent representation and does not assert that local tangents connect globally distinct support faces. \textit{Consumer.} Sparse-completion sensitivity can be audited by a finite linear system without artificial proxy smoothing.

\section{Interpretation}

The observable operator separates three distinct questions.  The equality \(A_Pg=b_P\) describes bridge representatives; annihilation \(R_0\ker(A_P)=\{0\}\) identifies the causal target without identifying the bridge; and the graphic-union rank \(r_{q_x}(E)\) measures how much of the proxy-effective bridge space a graph can generically learn.  Proxy categories outside \(J_x\) may have structural zeros and need not be smoothed.  Adding trials can increase the union rank only up to \(q_x(|V|-1)\), so it cannot defeat a low-rank common proxy kernel or recover all \(W\)-indexed bridge coordinates unless \(q_x=d\).

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The constructive causal converse uses finite binary potential outcomes, a public finite latent-support bound, a nonempty common active proxy carrier \(J_x\) in each stratum, and anchor-zero one-way tangent surjectivity only on that carrier.  It permits zeros of \(C_x(w\mid u)\) outside \(J_x\) and structural zeros in the joint potential-outcome kernel.  Empty common carriers and genuinely coupled deficient support faces remain a global secant problem: the fixed-completion tangent space is computable, but disconnected latent representations need not be joined by such tangents.  Exact generic rank and the augmentation certificate concern the rank-one observed-row submodel.  The exact confidence projection may be conservative, nonconvex, or nonshrinking near rank changes.

\section{Honest scope}

Broad sufficiency requires only that every compatible law have a bridge satisfying \(A_Pg=b_P\) and \(\theta=R_0g\).  Necessity is proved on the active-carrier tangent-rich class; its explicit pair of indistinguishable laws transfers nonidentification upward to any supermodel containing that observed-law fiber.  This is not a claim that the Zhang or Su theorem classes are nested in the present class.  The exact generic design rank is \(r_{q_x}(E)\); the \(q_x\)-tree condition is precisely its saturation corollary, and a single graph simultaneously saturates all strata at the \(q_{\max}\)-tree threshold.  Full bridge recovery additionally requires \(q_x=d=|\mathcal W|\).  The NETMOTION interpretation is conditional on valid harmonized proxies, shared latent node contrasts, and an independent target sample.
