%%% FIELD proof-proxy-rank-ceiling
Fix a compatible completion \(Q\), an edge \(e\), and a stratum \(x\).  Write \(m=|\mathcal U|\).  Observed-cell positivity in \(\text{def:model-class}\) makes the displayed conditional laws well defined on every declared observed row.  For every \(z\in\mathcal Z_e\) and \(w\in\mathcal W\), the law of total probability over the finite latent support gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{e,x}[z,w]
&=Q(W=w\mid Z_e=z,X=x,S=e)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}Q(U=u\mid Z_e=z,X=x,S=e)\,
 Q(W=w\mid Z_e=z,X=x,U=u,S=e).
\end{aligned}
\]
By proxy exclusion, \(Z_e\perp W\mid(X,U,S=e)\), and hence the second conditional probability in each summand equals \(Q(W=w\mid X=x,U=u,S=e)\).  By the common-proxy-kernel assumption, the latter probability is \(C_x(w\mid u)\).  Recalling the definition of \(H^Q_{e,x}\), we therefore have, entry by entry,
\[
T_{e,x}[z,w]=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}H^Q_{e,x}[z,u]C_x(w\mid u),
\qquad	ext{so that}\qquad
T_{e,x}=H^Q_{e,x}C_x.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(k=|V|-1\), and define the proof-intermediate matrix
\[
\widetilde A_x=\operatorname{stack}_{e\in E}(B_e\otimes H^Q_{e,x}).
\]
The mixed-product identity for Kronecker products and (1) imply, edge by edge,
\[
(B_e\otimes H^Q_{e,x})(I_k\otimes C_x)
=B_e\otimes(H^Q_{e,x}C_x)
=B_e\otimes T_{e,x}.
\]
Stacking these equalities and using the definition of \(A_{P,x}\) yields
\[
A_{P,x}=\widetilde A_x(I_k\otimes C_x).
\tag{2}
\]
Rank cannot increase under left multiplication, while the rank of a Kronecker product is the product of the ranks.  Thus (2) gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})
\leq \operatorname{rank}(I_k\otimes C_x)
=\operatorname{rank}(I_k)\operatorname{rank}(C_x)
=kq_x=q_x(|V|-1).
\tag{3}
\]
The matrix \(C_x\) has \(m\) rows and \(d\) columns, so
\[
q_x=\operatorname{rank}(C_x)\leq\min\{m,d\}.
\]
The bounded-latent-support condition in \(\text{def:model-class}\) gives \(m\leq L\); consequently
\[
q_x(|V|-1)\leq\min\{L,d\}(|V|-1).
\tag{4}
\]
Finally, \(A_P=\operatorname{blockdiag}_{x\in\mathcal X}A_{P,x}\).  Rank is additive across diagonal blocks, and hence (3) gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_P)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})
\leq (|V|-1)\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}q_x.
\]
This proves every asserted factorization and rank bound.

%%% FIELD proof-multistratum-example
We first verify the relevant model and positivity conditions for \(\mathfrak E\).  All declared alphabets are finite and \(|\mathcal U|=2=L\).  At \(x_1\), the two coordinates of every trial mixture and of the target mixture are at least \(1/8>\eta_0\); at \(x_2\), both coordinates equal \(1/2\).  Both columns of each \(C_x\) are positive, so \(J_{x_1}=J_{x_2}=\mathcal W\).  The singleton \(Z_e\) satisfies proxy exclusion, the stipulated kernel is common across populations, treatment is balanced, and the realized outcome is set equal to \(Y(A)\), giving consistency.  Each potential-outcome marginal under the five-atom kernel equals \(1/5\), independently of \((s,x,u,w)\).  Therefore every latent node contrast is zero and the stipulated bridge \(g_a(w,x)=0\) satisfies the shared-contrast equation.  Since every \(Z_e\) is a singleton, every \(T_{e,x}\) is a nonzero one-row stochastic matrix and has rank \(1<d=2\), as required by edge incompleteness.

For completeness, the smallest trial-table cell on the declared observed support has probability at least
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac5{16}\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac15=\frac1{64}>\frac1{1000}=\eta;
\]
the four factors are respectively the stratum, smallest proxy, treatment-arm, and smallest outcome probabilities, while the singleton \(Z_e\) contributes one.  A target \((X,W)\) cell has probability at least \((1/2)(5/16)=5/32\).  Thus observed-cell positivity also holds.

For any \(d=(d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3)\in D_{a_s}\), the displayed map \(\Gamma\) is supported on the five positive atoms and satisfies
\[
\sum_y\Gamma(d)(y)
=-\sum_{a=0}^3d_a+\sum_{a=0}^3d_a=0.
\]
Only the atom having a one in coordinate \(a\) contributes to its \(a\)-th one-way marginal, so
\[
\mathscr L_{s,x,u,w}\Gamma(d)=d.
\]
Thus the support-tangent assumption holds for every population anchor, even though the other eleven joint-outcome atoms have probability zero.  These checks place the relevant five-edge and six-edge restrictions of \(\mathfrak E\) in \(\mathcal M_{L,\eta}\).

Now order the edges as \(01,12,23,02,03,13\).  If edge \(j\) has latent mixture \((j/8,1-j/8)\) at \(x_1\), its observed proxy row is
\[
\begin{aligned}
t_j
&=\frac j8\left(\frac34,\frac14\right)
+\left(1-\frac j8\right)\left(\frac14,\frac34\right)\\
&=\left(\frac{4+j}{16},\frac{12-j}{16}\right),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Delete edge \(13\), orient every edge from its first endpoint to its second, and order columns by the two proxy coordinates within nodes \(1,2,3\).  Substitution of (5) into \(B_e\otimes t_e\) gives
\[
16A_{P,x_1}^{(5)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
5&11&0&0&0&0\\
-6&-10&6&10&0&0\\
0&0&-7&-9&7&9\\

0&0&0&0&9&7

\tag{6}
\]
The determinant of the five-by-five minor of the unscaled matrix obtained by deleting its first column is \(-11/1024\), which is nonzero.  Since (6) has only five rows,
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x_1}^{(5)})=5<6.
\tag{7}
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
16A_{P,x_1}^{(5)}
\begin{pmatrix}44\\-20\\-16\\16\\-7\\9\end{pmatrix}
=0.
\]
Extend this vector by zero on the \(x_2\) block and write its node rows as
\[
h_1=(44,-20),\qquad h_2=(-16,16),\qquad h_3=(-7,9),
\qquad h_0=(0,0).
\]
It then belongs to \(\ker(A_P)\).  The target proxy row at \(x_1\) is
\[
\frac78\left(\frac34,\frac14\right)
+\frac18\left(\frac14,\frac34\right)
=\left(\frac{11}{16},\frac5{16}\right).
\]
Since the target gives probability \(1/2\) to \(x_1\) and \(h\) vanishes at \(x_2\), the definition of \(R_0\) gives

R_0h
=\frac1{32}
\begin{pmatrix}

\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}12\\-3\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8}
\]

The positivity interval in the signed-cell construction can be made completely explicit here.  Choose \(\rho_{x_1}=\rho_{x_2}=(1/2,1/2)\).  At \(x_1\), multiplication by the two rows of \(C_{x_1}\) gives
\[
(r^h_0,r^h_1,r^h_2,r^h_3)
=\begin{cases}
(0,28,-8,-3),&u=1,\\
(0,-4,8,5),&u=2.

\tag{9}

The possible anchors are \(a_s\in\{0,1,2\}\).  Substituting (9) into the definition of \(d^h\), and then into the displayed right inverse \(\Gamma\), gives the following maxima of \(|\Delta^h_{s,x_1,u,w}(y)|\) over the five positive outcome atoms:

\begin{array}{c|ccc}

(u,w)=(1,2)&56&190&98\\

\end{array}

\]
At \(x_2\) the increment is zero.  Since every positive atom has mass \(1/5\), (10) and the definition of \(\varepsilon_*(h)\) yield the exact radius
\[
\varepsilon_*(h)=\frac{1/5}{190}=\frac1{950}.
\tag{11}
\]
The signed-cell-lift theorem now applies.  For every \(|\varepsilon|<1/950\), it preserves every observed trial and target cell, the latent support, the proxy kernels, randomization, all proxy restrictions, and all cross-population shared-latent-node-contrast equalities, with common bridge \(g+\varepsilon h\), while (8) gives

In particular, both signs at \(|\varepsilon|=1/1900\) are valid; indeed every originally positive outcome atom then retains mass at least

\]
Thus (12) is a nonzero, two-sided observational equivalence witness rather than merely a formal bridge ambiguity.

Add edge \(13\).  The sets
\[
\{01,12,23\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{02,03,13\}
\]
are edge-disjoint spanning trees.  Here \(q_{x_1}=2\), so the generic matroid-union rank theorem gives generic rank \(2(|V|-1)=6\) at \(x_1\).  In fact, adjoining the sixth row from \(t_6=(10/16,6/16)\) to (6) gives

At \(x_2\), \(C_{x_2}\) has rank \(q_{x_2}=1\) and every edge row is \(t=(1/2,1/2)\).  The graph is connected, so its reduced incidence matrix has rank \(|V|-1=3\), and

\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x_2})

Equivalently, this is the generic value \(r_1(E)=3\) supplied by the generic matroid-union rank theorem.  Block diagonality, the same theorem, and (13) give the global generic rank, and here also the actual rank,

\operatorname{rank}(A_P)=6+3=9.

\]

To verify causal identification rather than infer it from rank alone, take any \(\bar h\in\ker(A_P)\) for the augmented instance.  Full column rank at \(x_1\) implies \(\bar h_{x_1}=0\).  At \(x_2\), each edge equation is

\]
Connectivity and the root convention \(\bar h_{0,x_2}=0\) imply \(t\bar h_{a,x_2}=0\) for every node \(a\).  The target mixture at \(x_2\) induces the same proxy row \(t\), while \(P(X=x_2\mid S=0)=1/2\).  Therefore \(R_0\bar h=0\).  We have proved \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\), so the exact-nullity theorem identifies all root contrasts.

Nevertheless, (13) is strictly below the six columns in the \(x_2\) bridge block.  The proxy-rank ceiling, with \(q_{x_2}=1<d=2\), therefore rules out full recovery of that \(W\)-indexed bridge.  Finally, the three frozen comparison-scope lemmas establish respectively that the guarantee of \(\cite{MiaoGengTchetgen2018}\) uses full latent-rank categorical proxy matrices, that the main construction of \(\cite{SuRytgaardRavnEriksson2026}\) has the stated two-trial common-comparator scope and assumes nonempty observed bridge sets, and that \(\cite{DahabrehRobertsonPetitoHernanSteingrimsson2023}\) transports through observed modifiers.  The rank-one \(x_2\) kernel, six-edge treatment multigraph, and latent effect modifier in \(\mathfrak E\) place this proved instance outside those respective guarantees, exactly as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-full-bridge-recovery
Fix one covariate stratum and write \(k=|V|-1\).  The unknown stratum-specific \(W\)-indexed bridge has \(kd\) coordinates.  Because compatibility supplies at least one solution of its moment equation, recovery of the entire bridge from that equation is equivalent to uniqueness of the solution, hence to
\[
\ker(A_{P,x})=\{0\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=kd.
\tag{15}
\]
The proxy-rank-ceiling theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\leq q_xk\leq dk.
\tag{16}
\]
If generic full recovery holds, (15)--(16) force \(q_x\geq d\).  Since \(C_x\) has exactly \(d\) columns, \(q_x\leq d\), so necessarily \(q_x=d\).  Under that equality, the generic matroid-union rank theorem states that
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=r_d(E)
\quad\text{for \(\mu_C\)-almost every edge-row collection},
\]
and its saturation corollary gives
\[
r_d(E)=d(|V|-1)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{TreePack}_d(G).
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (15) and (17) prove both directions of the asserted generic full-recovery equivalence.

If \(q_x<d\), then (16) is strict:
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})\leq q_xk<dk.
\]
This argument applies after adjoining any number of randomized comparison edges, because such an augmentation changes the incidence stack but not the fixed common kernel \(C_x\) or its rank \(q_x\).  Thus edge addition cannot recover the full bridge when \(q_x<d\).

The causal functional has a weaker requirement.  The exact-nullity theorem requires only \(R_0\ker(A_P)=\{0\}\), not \(\ker(A_P)=\{0\}\).  To see concretely why this can hold under \(q_x<d\), suppose the graph contains \(q_x\) edge-disjoint spanning trees and take a generic edge-row collection.  The generic rank theorem then gives \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{P,x})=q_xk\).  From \(T_{e,x}=H^Q_{e,x}C_x\), every row of \(A_{P,x}\) lies in the \(q_xk\)-dimensional space
\[
\operatorname{row}(I_k\otimes C_x).
\]
Equality of dimensions therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{row}(A_{P,x})=\operatorname{row}(I_k\otimes C_x).
\tag{18}
\]
By the common-proxy-kernel condition, the target proxy row in this stratum is
\[
t_{0,x}=P(W=\mathord\cdot\mid X=x,S=0)
=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\pi_{0,x}(u)C_x(\mathord\cdot\mid u)
\in\operatorname{row}(C_x).
\tag{19}
\]
Every row of the corresponding block of \(R_0\) is a scalar multiple of \(e_a^{\mathsf T}\otimes t_{0,x}\).  By (18)--(19), these rows belong to \(\operatorname{row}(A_{P,x})\), so they annihilate \(\ker(A_{P,x})\).  Applying this argument to the relevant strata gives \(\ker(A_P)\subseteq\ker(R_0)\), even though (16) leaves a bridge nullspace of dimension at least \(k(d-q_x)>0\).  Hence selected, and under this saturation condition all, target root contrasts can be identified without full bridge recovery.
