\paragraph{TL;DR.} Taking Watson et al.'s unrestricted raw \(p=2\) interval as a published input, this note intersects it with \(K\) and studies tangent contact. The total positive-part plug-in endpoints have a fourth-root Gaussian law, while the fully specified feasibility-retained bootstrap converges to a random truncated kernel and is not contact-uniform. \section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Watson et al.'s Corollary 2.1 and Appendix A.7 provide the \(p=2\) sharp quadratic sublevel-set identity and its raw endpoint representation at nonnegative slack, and their Theorem 4 supplies a feasibility-retained bootstrap workflow. They do not derive the fourth-root contact law, the random retained-resample kernel for the fully specified finite Monte Carlo algorithm, contact-uniform validity, a whole-identified-set score hull, or a minimax loss frontier. Existing boundary and directional-bootstrap theory does not supply those leaky-IV results.

\paragraph{Niche.} The published scalar quadratic score remains regular at tangent contact even though the endpoint square root is only half-Hölder. Calibrating the score before endpoint transformation creates a route to uniform set inference and makes both contact and interior local minimax geometry explicit.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project keeps the causal target distinct from its observed-law score representation, takes Watson et al.'s unrestricted raw interval as a published input, and imposes the intersection with \(K\) itself. It derives the fourth-root contact law and the retained random kernel for an exact Efron algorithm, proves nonuniformity for that named workflow, and constructs an every-sample multiplier score hull using a common ridge convention. The uniform multiplier theorem is fully discharged and gives whole-set coverage throughout the strict uniformly regular fixed-relevance subclass \(\mathcal P_W\), while the exact score-set modulus gives a continuous loss elbow and matched contact and interior Gaussian LAN frontiers. The bootstrap counterexample and converse transfer to the broader published \(p=2\) model, but the upper guarantee does not.

\section{Related work}

Watson et al. (2024, Corollary 2.1 and Appendix A.7) supply the unrestricted raw \(p=2\) sharp interval; intersection with \(K\) is imposed by this note. Their Theorem 4 supplies the feasibility-retained endpoint-bootstrap workflow. The exact finite-\(B_n\) Efron algorithm, its negligible zero-retention fallback, the contact endpoint law, retained random kernel, nonuniformity result, score hull, continuous loss elbow, and matched frontiers are not attributed to that paper. Andrews (2000) supplies local boundary-sequence logic, and Fang and Santos (2019) distinguish a regular Gaussian primitive from a nonsmooth transform. Romano and Shaikh (2010) inform the whole-identified-set coverage criterion; Chernozhukov, Newey, and Santos (2023) inform constrained-score inversion; and Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2013) inform multiplier and anti-concentration arguments. The finite-dimensional quadratic-score proof adapted here fully discharged the paper's multiplier question, but only on the strict uniformly regular subclass \(\mathcal P_W\). Hirano and Porter (2012) rule out regular estimation of nondifferentiable functionals; the present Gaussian LAN calculations sharpen that diagnosis to matched fourth-root and root-\(n\) directed-Hausdorff frontiers. Hong and Li's numerical bootstrap regularizes inference for nondifferentiable maps through a tuning/perturbation scheme; it does not deliver the present tuning-free inversion of the regular quadratic score, whole-identified-set coverage, or its explicit contact-to-interior loss elbow. Takatsu and Kuchibhotla provide general local-minimax lower-bound tools for nonsmooth and irregular functionals; the Gaussian LAN converses here are problem-specific two-law-testing counterparts matched to the constructive score-hull upper rates. Staiger and Stock study weak-instrument sequences in which first-stage relevance can vanish, whereas the upper score-hull theorem here assumes relevance uniformly bounded away from zero; no weak-IV robustness is claimed. The bootstrap counterexample and converse witnesses belong to Watson et al.'s broader published \(p=2\) model, whereas the upper hull guarantee does not extend beyond \(\mathcal P_W\).

  \item \texttt{Z\_\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{bootstrap-\allowbreak{}limit slack increment}; \(N(0,4)\); \texttt{conditionally independent bootstrap shock}
  \item \texttt{K\_\allowbreak{}ret} --- \texttt{retained-\allowbreak{}resample endpoint kernel}; \texttt{random conditional limit law}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal K_h(Z)=\mathcal L(\sqrt{h+Z+Z^{*}}\mid Z^{*}>-(h+Z),Z)\)
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-centered]\label{ass:iid-centered}
The observations \(W_1,\ldots,W_n\) are independent draws from \(P\) and satisfy \(E_P[W_i]=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (centered i.i.d. sampling, \cite{watson2024bounding}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-equation]\label{ass:treatment-equation}
The treatment obeys \(X_i=\beta(P)^{\prime}Z_i+\epsilon_{x,i}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Watson linear treatment equation, \cite{watson2024bounding}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-equation]\label{ass:outcome-equation}
The outcome obeys \(Y_i=\gamma^{\prime}Z_i+\theta_0X_i+\epsilon_{y,i}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Watson linear outcome equation, \cite{watson2024bounding}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-orthogonality]\label{ass:outcome-orthogonality}
The outcome disturbance satisfies \(E_P[Z_i\epsilon_{y,i}]=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (instrument--outcome disturbance orthogonality, \cite{watson2024bounding}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:l2-leakage]\label{ass:l2-leakage}
The direct-effect vector satisfies \(\|\gamma\|_2\leq\tau\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (scalar \(L_2\) \(\tau\)-exclusion, \cite{watson2024bounding}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-conditioning]\label{ass:covariance-conditioning}
The covariance satisfies \(m_{\Sigma}\leq\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma(P))\leq\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma(P))\leq M_{\Sigma}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform covariance conditioning, \cite{chernozhukovNeweySantos2023constrained}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-relevance]\label{ass:fixed-relevance}
The reduced-form relevance satisfies \(\|\beta(P)\|_2\geq c_{\beta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (strong-instrument relevance bounded away from zero, \cite{staigerStock1997weak}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:eighth-moment]\label{ass:eighth-moment}
The observable vector satisfies \(E_P[\|W_i\|_2^8]\leq M_8\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform eighth-moment envelope, \cite{romanoShaikh2010identifiedSet}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:score-nondegeneracy}
The score variance satisfies \(c_{\sigma}\leq\inf_{\theta\in K}\sigma_P(\theta)\leq\sup_{\theta\in K}\sigma_P(\theta)\leq C_{\sigma}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform nondegenerate studentization, \cite{chernozhukovChetverikovKato2013gaussian}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:parameter-margin]\label{ass:parameter-margin}
The sharp set satisfies \(\operatorname{dist}(\Theta_I(P),\partial K)\geq m_K\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact parameter-space interiority, \cite{romanoShaikh2010identifiedSet}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:contact-slack]\label{ass:contact-slack}
A contact-local law at index \(n\) satisfies \(0\leq\delta(P)\leq C_{\delta}n^{-1/2}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (local-to-boundary sequence, \cite{andrews2000boundary}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:interior-slack]\label{ass:interior-slack}
A fixed-interior law satisfies \(\delta(P)\geq\delta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (strict interiority of the inequality constraint, \cite{chernozhukovNeweySantos2023constrained}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:contact-competitor-coverage]\label{ass:contact-competitor-coverage}
A competing sequence \(D_n\) satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{h\in[0,\bar H]}P_{n,h}\{\Theta_I(P_{n,h})\subseteq D_n\}\geq1-\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform whole-identified-set coverage requirement, \cite{romanoShaikh2010identifiedSet}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:interior-competitor-coverage]\label{ass:interior-competitor-coverage}
A competing sequence \(D_n\) satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{t\in[-\bar T,\bar T]}P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}\{\Theta_I(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})\subseteq D_n\}\geq1-\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform whole-identified-set coverage requirement, \cite{romanoShaikh2010identifiedSet}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:identified-set-endpoint-domain]\label{lem:identified-set-endpoint-domain}
For every law \(P\) for which the reduced-form primitives are defined, the sharp identified set is the always-defined sublevel set \(\Theta_I(P)=\{\theta\in K:q_P(\theta)\leq0\}\). If \(\delta(P)<0\), then \(\Theta_I(P)=\varnothing\). If \(\delta(P)\geq0\), the raw analytic endpoints \(\theta_-(P)\) and \(\theta_+(P)\) are real-valued and \(\Theta_I(P)=K\cap[\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)]\). For every \(P\in\mathcal P_W\), the structural equations and \(L_2\) leakage restriction give \(\alpha(P)-\theta_0\beta(P)=\gamma\) and hence \(\theta_0\in\Theta_I(P)\). Therefore the parameter-margin condition forces \(\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)\in\operatorname{int}(K)\), so \(\Theta_I(P)=[\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)]\), for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_{\delta})\cup\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\); no \(K\)-truncation occurs on either maintained regime.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(b=\|\beta(P)\|_2\), \(c=\check\theta(P)\), and
\[
 r=\alpha(P)-c\beta(P).
\]
The phrase ``the reduced-form primitives are defined'' includes \(b>0\), because the displayed definition of \(\check\theta(P)\) divides by \(b^2\). That definition gives
\[
 \beta(P)^{\prime}r
 =\beta(P)^{\prime}\alpha(P)-c\|\beta(P)\|_2^2=0.
\]
Consequently, the orthogonal-sum identity and the definitions of \(q_P\), \(\lambda(P)\), and \(\delta(P)\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_P(\theta)
 &=\|r+(c-\theta)\beta(P)\|_2^2-\tau^2\\
 &=\|r\|_2^2+b^2(\theta-c)^2-\tau^2\\
 &=b^2(\theta-c)^2-\delta(P). \tag{1}
 \end{aligned}
\]
By the definition of \(\Theta_I(P)\), the identified set is the always-defined \(K\)-restricted sublevel set of (1). The published raw-interval identification result recorded in \(\mathrm{def:watson\mbox{-}published\mbox{-}reduction}\) establishes the relationship between this observed-law score and the causal target on its stated validity and feasibility domain; the relationship is not manufactured by defining an endpoint. If \(\delta(P)<0\), (1) is strictly positive for every real \(\theta\), and hence \(\Theta_I(P)=\varnothing\). If \(\delta(P)\geq0\), division by \(b>0\) in (1) gives
\[
 q_P(\theta)\leq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 c-\frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)}}{b}\leq\theta\leq
 c+\frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)}}{b}.
\]
The two endpoints are exactly \(\theta_-(P)\) and \(\theta_+(P)\). Therefore
\[
 \Theta_I(P)=K\cap[\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)]. \tag{2}
\]

Now let \(P\in\mathcal P_W\). Premultiplying the outcome equation in \(\mathrm{ass:outcome\mbox{-}equation}\) by \(Z_i\), taking expectations, and using \(\mathrm{ass:outcome\mbox{-}orthogonality}\) gives
\[
 \Sigma_{zy}(P)=\Sigma_{zz}(P)\gamma+
 \theta_0\Sigma_{zx}(P).
\]
The covariance-conditioning assumption makes \(\Sigma_{zz}(P)\) invertible. Applying its inverse and the definitions of \(\alpha(P)\) and \(\beta(P)\) yields
\[
 \alpha(P)-\theta_0\beta(P)=\gamma. \tag{3}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:l2\mbox{-}leakage}\), equation (3) implies
\[
 q_P(\theta_0)=\|\gamma\|_2^2-\tau^2\leq0.
\]
Because \(\theta_0\in K\), the structural target belongs to \(\Theta_I(P)\). In particular, this set is nonempty and (1) implies \(\delta(P)\geq0\).

Write \(K=[k_-,k_+]\). If \(\theta_-(P)\leq k_-\), then (2) and nonemptiness imply \(k_-\in\Theta_I(P)\), so \(\operatorname{dist}\{\Theta_I(P),\partial K\}=0\), contrary to \(\mathrm{ass:parameter\mbox{-}margin}\), which has \(m_K>0\). Hence \(\theta_-(P)>k_-\). The same argument at the right boundary gives \(\theta_+(P)<k_+\). Thus (2) has no truncation and
\[
 \Theta_I(P)=[\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)].
\]
Both \(\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_\delta)\) and \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\) are subclasses of \(\mathcal P_W\), so the conclusion applies on their union.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma gives the raw square-root formulas their exact domain and shows that the maintained margin condition removes parameter-space truncation wherever endpoint asymptotics are used. \textit{Gap.} Watson et al. give the quadratic reduction, but the contact analysis requires an explicit distinction between the globally defined sublevel set and partial raw endpoints. \textit{Consumer.} All endpoint limits and lower-bound paths can invoke raw endpoints without evaluating a square root at negative slack or silently dropping the intersection with \(K\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:witness-membership-lan]\label{thm:witness-membership-lan}
Under the compatibility regime in the Gaussian-witness definition and whenever \(\sqrt n\geq2\bar H\), uniformly over \(h\in[0,\bar H]\), \[(3-\sqrt5)/2\leq\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma(a_{n,h}))\leq\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma(a_{n,h}))\leq(3+\sqrt5)/2,\] \(E_{P_{n,h}}[\|W_i\|_2^8]\leq1920\{(3+\sqrt5)/2\}^4\), and \(\sigma_{P_{n,h}}(\theta)^2=4(\theta^2+a_{n,h}^2)(1+\theta^2)\in[2,100]\) for every \(\theta\in[-2,2]\). Moreover, \(\beta=(1,0)^{\prime}\), \(\alpha=(0,a_{n,h})^{\prime}\), \(\theta_0=\check\theta=0\), \(\gamma=(0,a_{n,h})^{\prime}\), and \(\delta(P_{n,h})=h/\sqrt n\leq C_{\delta}/\sqrt n\). The raw endpoints are therefore defined and \(\Theta_I(P_{n,h})=[-\sqrt{h/\sqrt n},\sqrt{h/\sqrt n}]\subset(-2,2)\), so \(\operatorname{dist}(\Theta_I(P_{n,h}),\partial K)=2-\sqrt{h/\sqrt n}\geq2-1/\sqrt2>1\); no \(K\)-truncation occurs. Hence \(P_{n,h}\in\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_{\delta})\) along the full path. The experiment \(\{P_{n,h}:h\in[0,\bar H]\}\) is locally asymptotically normal and mutually contiguous on compact \(h\)-sets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(h\in[0,\bar H]\), put \(a=a_{n,h}\), and use the specialization \(d=2\), \(\tau=1\), and \(K=[-2,2]\) from \(\mathrm{def:gaussian\mbox{-}witness}\). Permuting coordinates from \((Z_1,Z_2,X,Y)\) to \((Z_1,X,Z_2,Y)\) turns \(\Sigma(a)\) into the block-diagonal matrix
\[
 B_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B_2(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&1+a^2\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1}
\]
Both determinants equal one. The trace of \(B_1\) is three, while the trace of \(B_2(a)\) is \(2+a^2\leq3\). Both blocks are positive definite because their leading principal minors and determinants are positive. For a positive \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix of determinant one, the larger eigenvalue \((t+\sqrt{t^2-4})/2\) is increasing in its trace \(t\geq2\), and the smaller eigenvalue is its reciprocal. The eigenvalues of \(B_1\) are \((3\pm\sqrt5)/2\). Thus (1) proves
\[
 \frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\leq\lambda_{\min}\{\Sigma(a)\}
 \leq\lambda_{\max}\{\Sigma(a)\}\leq\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}. \tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(\Sigma(a)\) is positive definite. If \(G_4\sim N(0,I_4)\) and \(L=(3+\sqrt5)/2\), then \(W\) has the same law as \(\Sigma(a)^{1/2}G_4\), whence \(\|W\|_2^8\leq L^4\|G_4\|_2^8\). Since \(\|G_4\|_2^2\) is chi-squared with four degrees of freedom,
\[
 E\|G_4\|_2^8=4\cdot6\cdot8\cdot10=1920,
\]
which gives the asserted eighth-moment bound.

The observable covariance blocks are \(\Sigma_{zz}=I_2\), \(\Sigma_{zx}=(1,0)^{\prime}\), and \(\Sigma_{zy}=(0,a)^{\prime}\). Therefore
\[
 \beta=(1,0)^{\prime},\qquad
 \alpha=(0,a)^{\prime},\qquad
 \check\theta=0,
\]
and the structural representation in \(P_{n,h}\) has \(\theta_0=0\) and \(\gamma=(0,a)^{\prime}\). Its leakage norm is \(a\leq1=\tau\), its relevance norm is one, and
\[
 \lambda(P_{n,h})=a^2,
 \qquad
 \delta(P_{n,h})=1-a^2=\frac{h}{\sqrt n}
 \leq\frac{C_\delta}{\sqrt n}. \tag{3}
\]

We next calculate the score variance. At this law, differentiating the two least-squares reduced forms in the direction of the covariance influence matrix gives
\[
 d\alpha=Z\{Y-\alpha^{\prime}Z\}=Z\epsilon_y,
 \qquad
 d\beta=Z\{X-\beta^{\prime}Z\}=Z\epsilon_x.
\]
For \(g_\theta=\alpha-\theta\beta=(-\theta,a)^{\prime}\), differentiation of \(q_P(\theta)=\|g_\theta\|_2^2-1\) consequently yields
\[
 \psi_{P_{n,h}}(W;\theta)
 =2(-\theta Z_1+aZ_2)(\epsilon_y-\theta\epsilon_x). \tag{4}
\]
The two factors in (4) are independent centered Gaussian variables, with variances \(\theta^2+a^2\) and \(1+\theta^2\), respectively. Hence
\[
 \sigma_{P_{n,h}}(\theta)^2
 =4(\theta^2+a^2)(1+\theta^2). \tag{5}
\]
The condition \(\sqrt n\geq2\bar H\) gives \(a^2=1-h/\sqrt n\geq1/2\). For \(\theta\in[-2,2]\), equation (5) is therefore between \(2\) and \(4(4+1)(1+4)=100\). Equations (2)--(5), together with the numerical compatibility inequalities in the witness definition, verify the covariance, moment, relevance, leakage, and score-nondegeneracy conditions in \(\mathcal P_W\).

By the endpoint-domain lemma and (3),
\[
 \Theta_I(P_{n,h})=
 \left[-\sqrt{h/\sqrt n},\sqrt{h/\sqrt n}\right]. \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, \(h/\sqrt n\leq1/2\), so its distance from the boundary of \([-2,2]\) is
\[
 2-\sqrt{h/\sqrt n}\geq2-1/\sqrt2>1\geq m_K. \tag{7}
\]
Thus the parameter margin holds, no \(K\)-truncation occurs, and (3) proves \(P_{n,h}\in\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_\delta)\), uniformly in \(h\).

It remains to verify the experiment claim. Under the reference \(P_{n,0}\), only the conditional regression of \(Y\) on \(Z_2\) changes. With \(u_{n,h}=\sqrt n(a_{n,h}-1)\), the log likelihood ratio is exactly
\[
 \log\frac{dP_{n,h}^{\,n}}{dP_{n,0}^{\,n}}
 =u_{n,h}\Delta_n-\frac{u_{n,h}^2}{2}\,
 n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{2i}^2,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{2i}\epsilon_{y,i}. \tag{8}
\]
Uniformly on the compact \(h\)-set, Taylor expansion of \(x\mapsto\sqrt{1-x}\) at zero gives \(u_{n,h}=-h/2+O(n^{-1/2})\). The summands \(Z_{2i}\epsilon_{y,i}\) are centered, have variance one, and have a finite third absolute moment. Expanding their characteristic function at the origin and taking its \(n\)-th power gives \(\Delta_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\); the same finite-moment calculation and Chebyshev's inequality give \(n^{-1}\sum_i Z_{2i}^2\to1\) in probability. Substitution in (8) yields
\[
 \log\frac{dP_{n,h}^{\,n}}{dP_{n,0}^{\,n}}
 =-\frac h2\Delta_n-\frac{h^2}{8}+o_{P_{n,0}}(1), \tag{9}
\]
uniformly for \(h\in[0,\bar H]\). This is the LAN expansion with local coordinate \(-h/2\) and information one.

For mutual contiguity, put \(\Delta a=a_{n,h}-1\). Conditioning on the \(Z_{2i}\)'s in (8) and integrating the squared Gaussian likelihood ratio gives
\[
 E_{P_{n,0}}\!\left[
 \left(\frac{dP_{n,h}^{\,n}}{dP_{n,0}^{\,n}}\right)^2\right]
 =(1-2\Delta a^2)^{-n/2}. \tag{10}
\]
This is uniformly bounded on compact \(h\)-sets because \(n\Delta a^2\to h^2/4\). The analogous reverse likelihood ratio has the same uniform bound. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to either likelihood ratio shows that an event whose probability tends to zero under one sequence has probability tending to zero under the other. This proves mutual contiguity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:contact-endpoint-and-retention-law]\label{thm:contact-endpoint-and-retention-law}
Let \(B_n\to\infty\) and use exactly the Efron candidate-resampling, strict-feasibility retention, empirical-law output, and zero-retention fallback in the retained-bootstrap definition. Uniformly for \(h\in[0,\bar H]\) under \(P_{n,h}\), \(\sqrt n(\widehat\delta_n-h/\sqrt n)\Rightarrow Z\) with \(Z\sim N(0,4)\), and the ordered endpoints of \(\widehat\Theta_n^{\mathrm{tot}}\) satisfy \(n^{1/4}(\widehat\theta_-^{\mathrm{tot}},\widehat\theta_+^{\mathrm{tot}})\Rightarrow(-\sqrt{(h+Z)_+},\sqrt{(h+Z)_+})\). Conditional on the contact-limit realization, the candidate-retention probability converges to \(\Phi\{(h+Z)/2\}\), whose infimum over \(h\in[0,\bar H]\) is \(\Phi(Z/2)>0\) almost surely. Thus \(B_n\to\infty\) makes the fallback negligible: \(\sup_{h\in[0,\bar H]}P_{n,h}(M_n=0)\to0\). Writing \(\widehat{\mathbb L}_{n,+}^{\mathrm{ret}}\) for the upper-coordinate marginal, the bounded-Lipschitz distance between its pushforward under \(u\mapsto n^{1/4}u\) and \(\mathcal K_h(Z)\) converges to zero in probability uniformly in \(h\); the lower-coordinate marginal converges to the reflected kernel. Centering the retained upper-coordinate law at \(n^{1/4}\widehat\theta_+^{\mathrm{tot}}\) subtracts \(\sqrt{(h+Z)_+}\) from the random kernel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Throughout the proof, \(h\in[0,\bar H]\), \(a=a_{n,h}\), and all remainders are uniform in \(h\). The witness-membership theorem gives \(a\to1\), \(\beta=e_1\), \(\alpha=ae_2\), \(\check\theta=0\), and \(\delta=h/\sqrt n\).

Let \(S_n=n^{-1}\sum_iW_iW_i^{\prime}\). Since the observations are centered Gaussian with uniformly bounded moments,
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_n-\Sigma(a)
 =n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\{W_iW_i^{\prime}-\Sigma(a)\}
 -\overline W_n\overline W_n^{\prime},
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\,\|\overline W_n\overline W_n^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =o_P(1). \tag{1}
\]
On the event \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n})\geq m_\Sigma/2\), the resolvent identity gives
\[
 \|R_n(\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n})-
 \widehat\Sigma_{zz,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq4m_\Sigma^{-2}n^{-2}. \tag{2}
\]
The uniform fourth-moment expansion for entries of the sample covariance makes the complement of this event \(O(n^{-2})\), as formalized by the ridge-totalization lemma. Thus (2) is \(o(n^{-1/2})\) for all first-order calculations below.

Differentiate \(\delta=1-\lambda\). At the witness, the residual in the projection defining \(\lambda\) is \(ae_2\), which is orthogonal to \(\beta=e_1\); hence the derivative of the estimated center drops out and
\[
 \operatorname{IF}_{\delta}(W)=-2aZ_2\epsilon_y. \tag{3}
\]
The smooth covariance expansion based on (1)--(3) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\delta_n-h/\sqrt n\}
 =-2a\,n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{2i}\epsilon_{y,i}+o_P(1). \tag{4}
\]
The summands before multiplication by \(-2a\) have mean zero, variance one, and uniformly bounded third absolute moments. The characteristic-function expansion used in the witness theorem, now uniformly over \(a\to1\), therefore gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\delta_n-h/\sqrt n\}\Rightarrow Z,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,4). \tag{5}
\]
The center has influence function
\[
 \operatorname{IF}_{\check\theta}(W)
 =Z_1\epsilon_y+aZ_2\epsilon_x,
\]
so \(\widehat{\check\theta}_n=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), while \(\|\widehat\beta_n\|_2=1+O_P(n^{-1/2})\). The lower truncation \(\|\widehat\beta_n\|_2\vee n^{-1}\) is consequently inactive with probability tending to one. By the continuous-mapping identity
\[
 n^{1/4}\sqrt{(\widehat\delta_n)_+}
 =\sqrt{(\sqrt n\widehat\delta_n)_+},
\]
equation (5) and the definition of the total interval yield
\[
 n^{1/4}(\widehat\theta_-^{\mathrm{tot}},
 \widehat\theta_+^{\mathrm{tot}})
 \Rightarrow
 \left(-\sqrt{(h+Z)_+},\sqrt{(h+Z)_+}\right). \tag{6}
\]

Conditional on the sample, the empirical-bootstrap analogue of (4) is
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\delta_{n,b}^*-\widehat\delta_n)
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^n
 \{\widehat\varphi_{I_{bj}^*}-\overline{\widehat\varphi}\}
 +o_{P^*}(1), \tag{7}
\]
where \(\widehat\varphi_i=-2\widehat a\,Z_{2i}\widehat\epsilon_{y,i}\) is the plug-in version of (3). The empirical second moment in (7) converges to four and its empirical Lindeberg remainder vanishes uniformly in \(h\), because the Gaussian witness has moments of every order uniformly on the compact path. Expanding the conditional characteristic function of the multinomial sum in (7) gives a conditional \(N(0,4)\) limit. Jointly with (5), it can be represented by \(Z^*\sim N(0,4)\) independent of \(Z\). Therefore
\[
 \sqrt n\widehat\delta_{n,b}^*\Rightarrow h+Z+Z^* \tag{8}
\]
conditionally on the contact-limit realization. The bootstrap center is again \(O_{P^*}(n^{-1/2})\), and its bootstrap denominator converges to one. Hence the scaled candidate upper endpoint converges conditionally to \(\sqrt{(h+Z+Z^*)_+}\), with the lower endpoint its negative.

Strict retention is the event \(\widehat\delta_{n,b}^*>0\). Because the limiting normal variable in (8) has no mass at zero, its conditional probability converges to
\[
 p_h(Z)=P\{Z^*>-(h+Z)\mid Z\}
 =\Phi\!\left(\frac{h+Z}{2}\right). \tag{9}
\]
For every realized finite \(Z\),
\[
 \inf_{0\leq h\leq\bar H}p_h(Z)=\Phi(Z/2)>0. \tag{10}
\]
Let \(p_{n,h}\) be the exact conditional candidate-retention probability. Conditional independence of the \(B_n\) candidates gives
\[
 P(M_n=0\mid W_1,\ldots,W_n)=(1-p_{n,h})^{B_n}. \tag{11}
\]
For any \(\varepsilon>0\), the right-hand side is at most \(e^{-B_n\varepsilon}+\mathbf 1\{p_{n,h}<\varepsilon\}\). Uniform conditional convergence in (9), followed by (10), implies
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\limsup_n
 \sup_{h\in[0,\bar H]}P_{n,h}(p_{n,h}<\varepsilon)=0.
\]
Since \(B_n\to\infty\), equation (11) proves \(\sup_hP_{n,h}(M_n=0)\to0\).

Finally, for any function \(f\) bounded by one, the conditional variance of the retained empirical average is bounded, on \(p_{n,h}>0\), by a constant times \((B_np_{n,h})^{-1}\). The preceding small-\(p_{n,h}\) argument shows \(B_np_{n,h}\to\infty\) in probability uniformly. A finite bounded-Lipschitz net on a sufficiently large compact set, followed by its Lipschitz extension and Gaussian tail control, therefore turns the conditional ratio law of large numbers into bounded-Lipschitz convergence of the empirical retained law. Combining this fact with (8)--(9) gives
\[
 \mathcal L\!\left(\sqrt{h+Z+Z^*}\mid
 Z^*>-(h+Z),Z\right)=\mathcal K_h(Z) \tag{12}
\]
for the scaled upper coordinate, and its reflection for the lower coordinate. Subtracting the original scaled upper endpoint in (6) changes (12) by exactly \(-\sqrt{(h+Z)_+}\). The zero-retention fallback is negligible by (11), which completes every asserted convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:bootstrap-nonuniformity]\label{thm:bootstrap-nonuniformity}
There exist constants \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\pi>0\) and a nonempty compact interval \(H_0\subseteq[0,\bar H]\) such that, uniformly for \(h\in H_0\), with probability at least \(\pi+o(1)\) the bounded-Lipschitz distance between the conditional ordinary Efron-bootstrap law of the centered, \(n^{1/4}\)-scaled endpoint pair and its sampling law exceeds \(\varepsilon\). For the exact \(B_n\)-candidate retained algorithm in the retained-bootstrap definition, the same conclusion holds for \(\widehat{\mathbb L}_n^{\mathrm{ret}}\) because its scaled coordinate marginals converge to the random kernel \(\mathcal K_h(Z)\) and its reflection, while \(\sup_{h\in H_0}P_{n,h}(M_n=0)\to0\). Consequently, neither the ordinary conditional endpoint-pair law nor the feasibility-retained conditional endpoint-pair law consistently estimates its sampling law uniformly over the contact-local sequences in \(H_0\). Thus the finite-\(B_n\) feasibility-retained workflow associated with Watson et al.'s Theorem 4 is not contact-uniform as a conditional distributional approximation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(g(x)=\sqrt{x_+}\). The contact endpoint theorem gives the following conditional limit for the ordinary centered upper endpoint:
\[
 \mathcal O_{h,z}
 =\mathcal L\{g(h+z+Z^*)-g(h+z)\}, \tag{1}
\]
whereas its sampling limit, centered at the population endpoint, is
\[
 \mathcal S_h
 =\mathcal L\{g(h+Z)-\sqrt h\}. \tag{2}
\]
For the retained algorithm, the corresponding conditional centered limit is
\[
 \mathcal R_{h,z}
 =\mathcal L\{g(h+z+Z^*)-g(h+z)\mid
 Z^*>-(h+z)\}. \tag{3}
\]
These kernels are not constant in the first-stage realization \(z\). Put
\[
 f(x)=\min\{(-x)_+,1\}.
\]
This function is bounded by one and has Lipschitz constant one. At \(h=0\), the law in (2) is supported on \([0,\infty)\), so \(\int f\,d\mathcal S_0=0\). At \(z=1\), the variable in (1) equals \(-1\) whenever \(Z^*\leq-1\); hence
\[
 \int f\,d\mathcal O_{0,1}
 \geq P(Z^*\leq-1)=\Phi(-1/2)>0. \tag{4}
\]
For (3), on the retained event \(-1<Z^*<-1/2\), the centered endpoint belongs to \((-1,-1+1/\sqrt2)\), and therefore
\[
 \int f\,d\mathcal R_{0,1}
 \geq (1-1/\sqrt2)
 \frac{P(-1<Z^*<-1/2)}{P(Z^*>-1)}>0. \tag{5}
\]
Normal probabilities and the integrals in (1)--(3) are continuous in \((h,z)\) near \((0,1)\); the conditioning probability in (3) is bounded away from zero there. Thus (4)--(5) imply that there are \(h_0>0\), \(\rho>0\), and \(\varepsilon>0\) such that, for
\[
 H_0=[0,h_0]\subseteq[0,\bar H],
\]
after reducing \(h_0\) if necessary, both bounded-Lipschitz distances from (2) exceed \(2\varepsilon\) whenever \(h\in H_0\) and \(z\in[1-\rho,1+\rho]\). The probability
\[
 \pi_0=P\{Z\in[1-\rho,1+\rho]\}
\]
is strictly positive. Uniform conditional convergence in the contact endpoint theorem and the triangle inequality now give, for any \(0<\pi<\pi_0\),
\[
 \inf_{h\in H_0}P_{n,h}\!\left\{
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{n,h},\mathcal S_h)
 >\varepsilon\right\}\geq\pi+o(1), \tag{6}
\]
and the identical assertion for the centered version of \(\widehat{\mathbb L}_n^{\mathrm{ret}}\).

Equation (6), coordinate projection, and the uniformly negligible zero-retention fallback establish the asserted positive-probability bounded-Lipschitz separation for both endpoint-pair laws. Hence uniform conditional weak consistency fails on \(H_0\). No conclusion about coverage at any preassigned percentile level is drawn, because bounded-Lipschitz separation does not by itself locate the distributional discrepancy at that level.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A random limiting kernel turns a qualitative boundary warning into a falsifiable nonuniformity theorem for a named implementation. \textit{Gap.} Andrews treats a projected boundary estimator and Fang--Santos treat directionally differentiable maps; neither covers the half-Hölder map \(x\mapsto\sqrt{x_+}\) or endogenous retention in leaky-IV bounds. \textit{Consumer.} The result shows that ordinary and feasibility-retained resampling are not contact-uniform conditional distributional approximations; coverage of any specified percentile construction requires a separate calibration argument.

\begin{lemma}[lem:score-error-set-modulus]\label{lem:score-error-set-modulus}
Let \(P\in\mathcal P_W\), let \(r\geq0\), and let \(\widetilde q:K\to\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\sup_{\theta\in K}|\widetilde q(\theta)-q_P(\theta)|\leq r\). For \(A_r=\{\theta\in K:\widetilde q(\theta)\leq r\}\),
\[
 d_{\to}\{A_r,\Theta_I(P)\}
 \leq \frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2r}-\sqrt{\delta(P)}}{c_\beta}. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, if \(0\leq\delta(P)\leq C_\delta r\), then \(d_{\to}\{A_r,\Theta_I(P)\}\leq \sqrt{2r}/c_\beta\), while if \(\delta(P)\geq\delta_0\), then \(d_{\to}\{A_r,\Theta_I(P)\}\leq r/(c_\beta\sqrt{\delta_0})\). Thus the same deterministic map has a square-root contact modulus and a linear fixed-interior modulus.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(b=\|\beta(P)\|_2\) and \(c=\check\theta(P)\). By fixed relevance, \(b\geq c_\beta>0\). From the definition of \(c\),
\[
 \beta(P)^{\prime}\{\alpha(P)-c\beta(P)\}
 =\beta(P)^{\prime}\alpha(P)-c\|\beta(P)\|_2^2=0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the orthogonal-sum identity, followed by the definitions of \(\lambda(P)\), \(\delta(P)\), and \(q_P\), gives for every \(\theta\in K\)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_P(\theta)
 &=\|\alpha(P)-c\beta(P)+(c-\theta)\beta(P)\|_2^2-\tau^2\\
 &=\lambda(P)+b^2(\theta-c)^2-\tau^2\\
 &=b^2(\theta-c)^2-\delta(P). \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The endpoint-domain lemma, using the structural restrictions defining \(\mathcal P_W\), shows that \(\Theta_I(P)\) is nonempty, that \(\delta(P)\geq0\), and that the parameter margin removes \(K\)-truncation. Hence
\[
 \Theta_I(P)=\left[c-\frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)}}b,
 c+\frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)}}b\right]. \tag{3}
\]

If \(A_r=\varnothing\), the definition of \(d_{\to}\) gives zero and the claim follows. Otherwise, every \(\theta\in A_r\) satisfies, by the uniform approximation hypothesis,
\[
 q_P(\theta)\leq\widetilde q(\theta)+r\leq2r. \tag{4}
\]
Combining (2) and (4) yields
\[
 |\theta-c|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2r}}b. \tag{5}
\]
The distance from this point to the interval (3) is therefore at most
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2r}-\sqrt{\delta(P)}}b
 \leq
 \frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2r}-\sqrt{\delta(P)}}{c_\beta}. \tag{6}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(A_r\) proves the exact displayed bound in the statement. The elementary inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt x\leq\sqrt y\), for \(x,y\geq0\), turns (6) into \(\sqrt{2r}/c_\beta\) in the contact regime. If \(\delta(P)\geq\delta_0\), rationalization instead gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2r}-\sqrt{\delta(P)}}{c_\beta}
 =\frac{2r}{c_\beta\{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2r}+\sqrt{\delta(P)}\}}
 \leq\frac{r}{c_\beta\sqrt{\delta_0}}. \tag{7}
\]
These are the asserted square-root and linear specializations.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit modulus is the deterministic mechanism by which one root-\(n\) score procedure adapts continuously without regime selection. \textit{Gap.} Generic criterion-set rate results do not isolate the exact square-root-to-linear modulus of this quadratic leakage score. \textit{Consumer.} The adaptive score-hull theorem transfers its integrable score radius through this formula to obtain the continuous loss elbow.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-score-hull]\label{thm:adaptive-score-hull}
The ridge-totalized single hull \(C_n\), defined on every sample and allowed to be empty, satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_W}
 P\{\Theta_I(P)\subseteq C_n\}\geq1-\eta. \tag{1}
\]
Under the stated empty-set convention for \(d_{\to}\), it also satisfies the continuous elbow bound
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}
 \{\sqrt{\delta(P)}+n^{-1/4}\}
 E_P[d_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}]=O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_\delta)}
 E_P[d_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}]=O(n^{-1/4}),
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)}
 E_P[d_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}]=O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{3}
\]
All three conclusions use the same score statistic and the same multiplier critical-value rule, without a regime selector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 A_{n,P}=\left\{
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_I(P)}
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat q_n(\theta)-q_P(\theta)\}}
 {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}}
 \leq\widehat c_{1-\eta}\right\}. \tag{1}
\]
For \(\theta\in\Theta_I(P)\), the sharp score characterization gives \(q_P(\theta)\leq0\). On \(A_{n,P}\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\widehat q_n(\theta)}
 {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}}
 \leq
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat q_n(\theta)-q_P(\theta)\}}
 {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}}
 \leq\widehat c_{1-\eta}. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\theta\in C_n\), and \(A_{n,P}\subseteq\{\Theta_I(P)\subseteq C_n\}\). Assertion (A) of the already-established uniform multiplier-coupling theorem now gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_W}
 P\{\Theta_I(P)\subseteq C_n\}\geq1-\eta. \tag{3}
\]

For loss, take \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\) and \(R_{n,P}\) from assertions (B)--(C) of that theorem. The endpoint-domain lemma shows that \(\Theta_I(P)\neq\varnothing\) for \(P\in\mathcal P_W\). Since both \(C_n\) and \(\Theta_I(P)\) are subsets of compact \(K\), the empty-set convention implies
\[
 0\leq d_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}\leq\operatorname{diam}(K) \tag{4}
\]
on every sample. On \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\), assertion (C) gives
\[
 C_n\subseteq B_{n,P}:=\{\theta\in K:q_P(\theta)\leq R_{n,P}\}. \tag{5}
\]
Apply the score-error-set modulus pathwise with \(\widetilde q=q_P\) and \(r=R_{n,P}\); its approximation premise holds because the error is zero. Monotonicity of directed loss in its first argument under the inclusion (5) then gives
\[
 d_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}
 \leq d_{\to}\{B_{n,P},\Theta_I(P)\}
 \leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta(P)+2R_{n,P}}-\sqrt{\delta(P)}}{c_\beta}. \tag{6}
\]
For \(x,u\geq0\),
\[
 \sqrt x\{\sqrt{x+2u}-\sqrt x\}\leq u,
 \qquad
 \sqrt{x+2u}-\sqrt x\leq\sqrt{2u}. \tag{7}
\]
The first inequality follows by rationalization and the second by squaring. Set \(a_n=n^{-1/4}\). Multiplying (6) by \(\sqrt{\delta(P)}+a_n\), applying (7), and then using (4) on \(\mathcal E_{n,P}^c\), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\{\sqrt{\delta(P)}+a_n\}
 E_Pd_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}\\
 &\quad\leq c_\beta^{-1}E_PR_{n,P}
 +\sqrt2c_\beta^{-1}a_nE_P\sqrt{R_{n,P}}\\
 &\qquad
 +\{\sqrt{\delta(P)}+a_n\}\operatorname{diam}(K)
 P(\mathcal E_{n,P}^c). \tag{8}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(P\in\mathcal P_W\), nonemptiness gives \(0\leq\delta(P)\leq\tau^2\), since \(\lambda(P)\geq0\). Uniformly in \(P\), the coupling theorem gives \(E_PR_{n,P}=O(n^{-1/2})\) and \(P(\mathcal E_{n,P}^c)=O(n^{-2})\); Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 E_P\sqrt{R_{n,P}}\leq\sqrt{E_PR_{n,P}}=O(n^{-1/4})
\]
Therefore the right-hand side of (8) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), proving the continuous elbow bound.

If \(P\in\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_\delta)\), the multiplier \(\sqrt{\delta(P)}+n^{-1/4}\) is at least \(n^{-1/4}\), so the elbow bound gives \(O(n^{-1/4})\) loss. If \(P\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\), that multiplier is at least \(\sqrt{\delta_0}\), so it gives \(O(n^{-1/2})\) loss. The definition of \(C_n\), including its score statistic and multiplier critical value, is unchanged in these deductions, which proves adaptation by one rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:interior-witness-membership-lan]\label{thm:interior-witness-membership-lan}
Under the compatibility regime in the fixed-interior Gaussian-witness definition, for every \(n\) satisfying \(\bar T/\sqrt n\leq\min\{a_{\mathrm{int}}/2,(1-a_{\mathrm{int}})/2\}\) and \(2a_{\mathrm{int}}\bar T/\sqrt n+\bar T^2/n\leq1-a_{\mathrm{int}}^2-\delta_0\), uniformly over \(t\in[-\bar T,\bar T]\), the coefficient \(b_{n,t}=a_{\mathrm{int}}+t/\sqrt n\) lies in \([a_{\mathrm{int}}/2,(1+a_{\mathrm{int}})/2]\subset(0,1)\), \[(3-\sqrt5)/2\leq\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma(b_{n,t}))\leq\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma(b_{n,t}))\leq(3+\sqrt5)/2,\] \(E_{P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}}[\|W_i\|_2^8]\leq1920\{(3+\sqrt5)/2\}^4\), and \(\sigma_{P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}}(\theta)^2=4(\theta^2+b_{n,t}^2)(1+\theta^2)\in[a_{\mathrm{int}}^2,100]\) for every \(\theta\in[-2,2]\). Moreover, \(\beta=(1,0)^{\prime}\), \(\alpha=(0,b_{n,t})^{\prime}\), \(\theta_0=\check\theta=0\), \(\gamma=(0,b_{n,t})^{\prime}\), and \(\delta(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})=1-b_{n,t}^2\geq\delta_0\). The raw endpoints are therefore defined and \(\Theta_I(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})=[-\sqrt{1-b_{n,t}^2},\sqrt{1-b_{n,t}^2}]\subset(-1,1)\), so \(\operatorname{dist}(\Theta_I(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}),\partial K)>1\); no \(K\)-truncation occurs. Hence \(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\) along the full compact path. The experiment \(\{P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}:t\in[-\bar T,\bar T]\}\) is locally asymptotically normal and mutually contiguous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(a=a_{\mathrm{int}}\), \(b=b_{n,t}=a+t/\sqrt n\), and use \(d=2\), \(\tau=1\), and \(K=[-2,2]\). The first displayed condition on \(n\) gives
\[
 b\geq a-\bar T/\sqrt n\geq a/2,
 \qquad
 b\leq a+\bar T/\sqrt n\leq(1+a)/2<1. \tag{1}
\]
After the same coordinate permutation used for the contact witness,
\[
 \Sigma(b)\sim
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}
 \oplus
 \begin{pmatrix}1&b\\b&1+b^2\end{pmatrix}. \tag{2}
\]
Both blocks in (2) have determinant one, and the second has trace \(2+b^2\leq3\). The eigenvalue calculation in the contact witness proof therefore gives
\[
 \frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\leq\lambda_{\min}\{\Sigma(b)\}
 \leq\lambda_{\max}\{\Sigma(b)\}\leq\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}. \tag{3}
\]
As in that calculation, if \(L=(3+\sqrt5)/2\), then
\[
 E\|W_i\|_2^8\leq L^4E\|N(0,I_4)\|_2^8=1920L^4. \tag{4}
\]
The reduced forms and structural quantities are
\[
 \beta=(1,0)^{\prime},\qquad
 \alpha=(0,b)^{\prime},\qquad
 \check\theta=\theta_0=0,\qquad
 \gamma=(0,b)^{\prime}. \tag{5}
\]
In particular, relevance equals one and the leakage restriction holds because \(0<b<1=\tau\). The score influence calculation is unchanged except that the contact coefficient is replaced by \(b\):
\[
 \psi(W;\theta)=2(-\theta Z_1+bZ_2)(\epsilon_y-\theta\epsilon_x),
 \qquad
 \sigma(\theta)^2=4(\theta^2+b^2)(1+\theta^2). \tag{6}
\]
By (1), the lower bound in (6) is \(4b^2\geq a^2\); on \([-2,2]\), the upper bound is at most \(100\). The compatibility restrictions on \(c_\sigma\) and \(C_\sigma\) therefore verify score nondegeneracy.

Moreover,
\[
 \delta(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})=1-b^2
 =1-a^2-\frac{2at}{\sqrt n}-\frac{t^2}{n}. \tag{7}
\]
Uniformly for \(|t|\leq\bar T\), the second displayed restriction on \(n\) and (7) imply \(\delta(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})\geq\delta_0\). The endpoint-domain lemma now yields
\[
 \Theta_I(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})
 =[-\sqrt{1-b^2},\sqrt{1-b^2}]\subset(-1,1), \tag{8}
\]
because \(b>0\). Its distance to the boundary of \([-2,2]\) is strictly greater than one and hence at least \(m_K\). Equations (1), (3)--(8), together with the structural Gaussian construction, verify all defining assumptions of \(\mathcal P_W\) and the interior-slack condition. Thus \(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\) uniformly over the full compact path.

For LAN, take \(P_{n,0}^{\mathrm{int}}\) as reference and put
\[
 \Delta_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{2i}\epsilon_{y,i}.
\]
The conditional normal-regression likelihood gives the exact identity
\[
 \log\frac{d(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})^n}
 {d(P_{n,0}^{\mathrm{int}})^n}
 =t\Delta_n-\frac{t^2}{2}\,n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{2i}^2. \tag{9}
\]
The characteristic-function calculation for the centered products gives \(\Delta_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), and Chebyshev's inequality makes the second term in (9) converge to \(-t^2/2\), uniformly on \([ -\bar T,\bar T]\). This is LAN with information one. Finally, direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
 E_{P_{n,0}^{\mathrm{int}}}\!\left[
 \left\{\frac{d(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})^n}
 {d(P_{n,0}^{\mathrm{int}})^n}\right\}^{\!2}\right]
 =(1-2t^2/n)^{-n/2}. \tag{10}
\]
This is bounded uniformly for \(|t|\leq\bar T\) and large \(n\); the reverse ratios obey the same bound. Cauchy--Schwarz proves mutual contiguity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-loss-frontier]\label{thm:minimax-loss-frontier}
Fix \(\eta<1/2\), \(\bar H>0\), \(\bar T>0\), and the contact and fixed-interior Gaussian-witness compatibility regimes. There are constants \(c_{\mathrm{ct}}>0\) and \(c_{\mathrm{int}}>0\), depending only on \(\eta,\bar H,\bar T,a_{\mathrm{int}}\) and the fixed class constants, with the following properties. For every \((D_n)\in\mathfrak D_{\eta}^{\mathrm{ct}}\), there exists \(N_{\mathrm{ct}}(D)<\infty\) such that, for every \(n\geq N_{\mathrm{ct}}(D)\) with \(\sqrt n\geq2\bar H\),
\[
 \sup_{h\in[0,\bar H]}E_{P_{n,h}}\!\left[d_{\to}\{D_n,\Theta_I(P_{n,h})\}\right]
 \geq c_{\mathrm{ct}}n^{-1/4}. \tag{1}
\]
For every \((D_n)\in\mathfrak D_{\eta}^{\mathrm{int}}(a_{\mathrm{int}},\bar T)\), there exists \(N_{\mathrm{int}}(D)<\infty\) such that, for every \(n\geq N_{\mathrm{int}}(D)\) satisfying \(\bar T/\sqrt n\leq\min\{a_{\mathrm{int}}/2,(1-a_{\mathrm{int}})/2\}\) and \(2a_{\mathrm{int}}\bar T/\sqrt n+\bar T^2/n\leq1-a_{\mathrm{int}}^2-\delta_0\),
\[
 \sup_{t\in[-\bar T,\bar T]}E_{P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}}\!\left[d_{\to}\{D_n,\Theta_I(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})\}\right]
 \geq c_{\mathrm{int}}n^{-1/2}. \tag{2}
\]
The interior witness-membership theorem gives \(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\) throughout this quantified regime. Together with the multiplier-hull upper theorem, these inequalities give matched \(n^{-1/4}\) contact and \(n^{-1/2}\) interior frontiers under the same outer directed-loss convention.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove both assertions by two-law testing. No regularity of the competing random closed set beyond the stated measurability and coverage is used.

\textbf{Contact experiment.} Choose \(h_*\in(0,\bar H]\) sufficiently small that
\[
 v_{\mathrm{ct}}:=\frac12\sqrt{\exp(h_*^2/4)-1}<1-\eta. \tag{1}
\]
This is possible because \(\bar H>0\) and \(\eta<1/2\). Let \(P_n=P_{n,0}\), \(Q_n=P_{n,h_*}\), and \(L_n=dQ_n^n/dP_n^n\). With \(\Delta a_n=a_{n,h_*}-1\), the Gaussian-regression likelihood calculation gives
\[
 E_{P_n}L_n^2=(1-2\Delta a_n^2)^{-n/2}
 \longrightarrow\exp(h_*^2/4). \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, conditionally on \(Z_2\), only the mean of \(Y\) changes, its error variance is one, and direct integration of the squared normal likelihood ratio gives the equality in (2). For every event \(A\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 |Q_n(A)-P_n(A)|
 \leq\frac12E_{P_n}|L_n-1|
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{E_{P_n}L_n^2-1}. \tag{3}
\]
Choose \(\epsilon>0\) with \(1-\eta-\epsilon-v_{\mathrm{ct}}>0\). If \((D_n)\in\mathfrak D_\eta^{\mathrm{ct}}\), uniform coverage and (2)--(3) imply, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 P_n\{\Theta_I(Q_n)\subseteq D_n\}
 \geq1-\eta-\epsilon-v_{\mathrm{ct}}+o(1)
 \geq\kappa_{\mathrm{ct}}>0. \tag{4}
\]
The contact witness theorem gives
\[
 \Theta_I(P_n)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \Theta_I(Q_n)=[-\sqrt{h_*}\,n^{-1/4},
 \sqrt{h_*}\,n^{-1/4}]. \tag{5}
\]
On the event in (4), \(D_n\) contains \(\sqrt{h_*}\,n^{-1/4}\); hence
\[
 d_{\to}\{D_n,\Theta_I(P_n)\}
 \geq\sqrt{h_*}\,n^{-1/4}. \tag{6}
\]
Taking expectations on this event yields, after decreasing the positive constant if necessary,
\[
 \sup_{h\in[0,\bar H]}E_{P_{n,h}}
 d_{\to}\{D_n,\Theta_I(P_{n,h})\}
 \geq c_{\mathrm{ct}}n^{-1/4}. \tag{7}
\]

\textbf{Interior experiment.} Choose \(s\in(0,\bar T]\) sufficiently small that
\[
 v_{\mathrm{int}}:=\frac12\sqrt{\exp(s^2)-1}<1-\eta. \tag{8}
\]
Let \(P_n=P_{n,0}^{\mathrm{int}}\), \(Q_n=P_{n,-s}^{\mathrm{int}}\), and \(L_n=dQ_n^n/dP_n^n\). The conditional Gaussian regression has coefficient difference \(-s/\sqrt n\), and the same direct integration gives
\[
 E_{P_n}L_n^2=(1-2s^2/n)^{-n/2}\longrightarrow e^{s^2}. \tag{9}
\]
Repeating (3)--(4) with interior-honest coverage gives \(\kappa_{\mathrm{int}}>0\) such that
\[
 P_n\{\Theta_I(Q_n)\subseteq D_n\}\geq\kappa_{\mathrm{int}} \tag{10}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). Put
\[
 r_0=\sqrt{1-a_{\mathrm{int}}^2},
 \qquad
 r_{n,-s}=\sqrt{1-(a_{\mathrm{int}}-s/\sqrt n)^2}. \tag{11}
\]
The interior witness-membership theorem makes both identified sets untruncated, and
\[
 r_{n,-s}-r_0
 =\frac{2a_{\mathrm{int}}s/\sqrt n-s^2/n}{r_{n,-s}+r_0}
 \geq\frac{a_{\mathrm{int}}s}{2\sqrt n} \tag{12}
\]
for sufficiently large \(n\), because the numerator is at least \(a_{\mathrm{int}}s/\sqrt n\) and the denominator is at most two. On the event in (10), \(D_n\) contains \(r_{n,-s}\), whereas the right endpoint of \(\Theta_I(P_n)\) is \(r_0\). Thus (10)--(12) imply
\[
 \sup_{t\in[-\bar T,\bar T]}E_{P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}}}
 d_{\to}\{D_n,\Theta_I(P_{n,t}^{\mathrm{int}})\}
 \geq\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{int}}a_{\mathrm{int}}s}{2}n^{-1/2}. \tag{13}
\]
The restrictions on \(n\) in the statement are exactly those under which the interior witness theorem certifies class membership; enlarging \(N_{\mathrm{int}}(D)\) enforces them. The adaptive score-hull theorem supplies upper risks of orders \(n^{-1/4}\) and \(n^{-1/2}\) under the same directed loss, so (7) and (13) establish the matched frontiers.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse shows that fourth-root contact loss is information-theoretic and matches the now-proved upper hull. \textit{Gap.} The result strengthens generic nonregularity into explicit leaky-instrument directed-Hausdorff lower bounds in both local experiments. \textit{Consumer.} The two lower bounds provide certified benchmarks for alternative endpoint and score procedures.

\begin{proposition}[prop:interior-gaussian-reduction]\label{prop:interior-gaussian-reduction}
For each fixed \(P\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\), the raw endpoints are well defined, the maintained parameter margin gives \(\Theta_I(P)=[\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)]\) without \(K\)-truncation, and the endpoint map is continuously differentiable at \(\Sigma(P)\). Hence \(\sqrt n\{(\widehat\theta_-^{\mathrm{tot}},\widehat\theta_+^{\mathrm{tot}})-(\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P))\}\) has the ordinary delta-method Gaussian limit, and the same multiplier hull \(C_n\) has root-\(n\) outer loss. This is the regular special-case reduction of Watson et al.'s smooth endpoint argument.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\). By definition, \(\delta(P)\geq\delta_0>0\), and fixed relevance within \(\mathcal P_W\) gives \(\|\beta(P)\|_2\geq c_\beta>0\). The endpoint-domain lemma and the parameter margin give
\[
 \Theta_I(P)=[\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P)] \tag{1}
\]
without \(K\)-truncation.

On the open neighborhood of \(\Sigma(P)\) on which \(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{zz})>m_\Sigma/2\), \(\|\beta\|_2>c_\beta/2\), and \(\delta>\delta_0/2\), the maps
\[
 \Sigma\longmapsto\alpha=\Sigma_{zz}^{-1}\Sigma_{zy},
 \qquad
 \Sigma\longmapsto\beta=\Sigma_{zz}^{-1}\Sigma_{zx},
 \qquad
 (\alpha,\beta)\longmapsto\check\theta
 =\frac{\beta^{\prime}\alpha}{\|\beta\|_2^2} \tag{2}
\]
are continuously differentiable. So are
\[
 \lambda=\|\alpha-\check\theta\beta\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \delta=\tau^2-\lambda,
 \qquad
 \theta_\pm=\check\theta\pm\frac{\sqrt\delta}{\|\beta\|_2}, \tag{3}
\]
because all inversions, divisions, and the square root in (2)--(3) occur on domains bounded away from zero. This proves the claimed differentiability.

The eighth-moment restriction supplies a finite second moment for the covariance influence vector. For every fixed vector \(v\), expansion of the characteristic function of the centered scalar summand
\[
 v^{\prime}\operatorname{vech}\{W_iW_i^{\prime}-\Sigma(P)\}
\]
at the origin proves its scalar central limit theorem; the Cram\'{e}r--Wold argument therefore gives the multivariate central limit theorem for
\[
 n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \operatorname{vech}\{W_iW_i^{\prime}-\Sigma(P)\}. \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, centered independent sampling and finite second moments give \(\sqrt n\,\overline W_n\overline W_n^{\prime}=o_P(1)\). Taylor expansion of (2)--(3) around \(\Sigma(P)\), combined with (4), yields
\[
 \sqrt n\left\{
 (\widehat\theta_-^{\mathrm{tot}},\widehat\theta_+^{\mathrm{tot}})
 -(\theta_-(P),\theta_+(P))\right\}
 \rightsquigarrow N(0,V_P), \tag{5}
\]
where \(V_P\) is obtained by applying the derivative in (2)--(3) to the covariance influence vector; the Gaussian law may be degenerate. Since \(\widehat\delta_n\to_P\delta(P)\geq\delta_0\), the positive-part convention is inactive with probability tending to one. The ridge-totalization lemma makes the resolvent correction \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\) on an event whose probability tends to one. Hence (5) applies to the totalized endpoints.

Finally, the fixed-interior specialization of the adaptive score-hull theorem gives
\[
 E_Pd_{\to}\{C_n,\Theta_I(P)\}=O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{6}
\]
Thus the contact-robust procedure retains the ordinary root-\(n\) endpoint limit and root-\(n\) outer set loss at every fixed interior law.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction verifies that contact-robust inversion sacrifices neither the smooth endpoint limit nor root-\(n\) loss away from tangency. \textit{Gap.} Watson et al.'s smooth argument applies after a fixed positive slack is imposed; this proposition states that domain and connects it to the proved hull. \textit{Consumer.} Practitioners far from feasibility contact recover conventional root-\(n\) precision from the same implementation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:ridge-totalization-negligible]\label{lem:ridge-totalization-negligible}
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_W\), \(P\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n})<m_{\Sigma}/2\}=O(n^{-2})\). On the complementary event, \(\|R_n(\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n})-\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq4n^{-2}/m_{\Sigma}^2\); consequently the ridge-totalized reduced forms, score, estimated influence process, and multiplier critical value differ from their inverse-defined versions by \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly. Thus the near-singular fallback event is asymptotically immaterial for every stated weak limit and, because confidence sets lie in compact \(K\), for the stated expected outer-loss rates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(A=\Sigma_{zz}(P)\), \(\widehat A_n=\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n}\), and \(\rho_n=n^{-2}\). All constants below may depend on the fixed dimension \(d\), the compact interval \(K\), and the constants defining \(\mathcal P_W\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_W\) or \(n\).

\textbf{1. Uniform control of the empirical instrument covariance.}
Because \(A\) is a principal submatrix of \(\Sigma(P)\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:covariance\mbox{-}conditioning}\) and the variational characterization of the smallest eigenvalue give
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(A)\geq m_{\Sigma}.
\]
Let \(A_n^0=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_iZ_i^{\prime}\) and \(\overline Z_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i\). The centering convention in the definition of \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) gives
\[
 \widehat A_n=A_n^0-\overline Z_n\overline Z_n^{\prime}.
\]
For fixed coordinates \(j,k\), put \(U_i^{jk}=Z_{ij}Z_{ik}-E_P[Z_{ij}Z_{ik}]\). By \((a-b)^4\leq 8(a^4+b^4)\), Jensen's inequality, and assumption \(\mathrm{ass:eighth\mbox{-}moment}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P|U_i^{jk}|^4<\infty.
\]
Independence and centering from assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}centered}\) imply, by expanding the fourth power and retaining only index patterns in which every index occurs at least twice,
\[
 E_P\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nU_i^{jk}\right|^4
 =n^{-4}\left\{nE_P[(U_1^{jk})^4]+3n(n-1)E_P[(U_1^{jk})^2]^2\right\}
 \leq Cn^{-2}.
\]
Since \(d\) is fixed, equivalence of matrix norms therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P\|A_n^0-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^4\leq Cn^{-2}. \tag{1}
\]
Similarly, expansion of the eighth power of each coordinate of \(\overline Z_n\) has at most four distinct indices in every nonzero term: any index occurring exactly once makes that term vanish by the centering and independence in \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}centered}\). H\"older's inequality and \(\mathrm{ass:eighth\mbox{-}moment}\) thus give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P\|\overline Z_n\|_2^8\leq Cn^{-4}. \tag{2}
\]
Using \(\|vv^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|v\|_2^2\), equations \((1)\)--\((2)\), and \((a+b)^4\leq8(a^4+b^4)\), we obtain
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P\|\widehat A_n-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^4\leq Cn^{-2}. \tag{3}
\]
For symmetric matrices the variational formula gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat A_n)
 \geq \lambda_{\min}(A)-\|\widehat A_n-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Consequently, Markov's inequality and \((3)\) imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}P\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat A_n)<m_{\Sigma}/2\}
 &\leq \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}P\{\|\widehat A_n-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}>m_{\Sigma}/2\}\\
 &\leq (2/m_{\Sigma})^4\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P\|\widehat A_n-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^4
 \leq Cn^{-2}. \tag{4}
 \end{split}
\]
This proves the asserted uniform probability bound.

\textbf{2. Resolvent, reduced-form, score, and derivative comparisons.}
Let \(E_n=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat A_n)\geq m_{\Sigma}/2\}\). On \(E_n\), both \(T_0=\widehat A_n^{-1}\) and \(T_{\rho}=(\widehat A_n+\rho_n I_d)^{-1}\) exist, and
\[
 \|T_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2/m_{\Sigma},
 \qquad
 \|T_{\rho}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2/m_{\Sigma}.
\]
The resolvent identity
\[
 T_{\rho}-T_0=-\rho_nT_{\rho}T_0
\]
follows by multiplying the two sides by \(\widehat A_n+\rho_nI_d\) and \(\widehat A_n\). Hence, on \(E_n\),
\[
 \|R_n(\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n})-\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq \frac{4n^{-2}}{m_{\Sigma}^2}. \tag{5}
\]

To compare all induced objects, for a generic covariance matrix \(S\) write \(A_S=S_{zz}\), \(b_{x,S}=S_{zx}\), \(b_{y,S}=S_{zy}\), and define
\[
 b_{\theta,S}=b_{y,S}-\theta b_{x,S},\qquad
 T_{r,S}=(A_S+rI_d)^{-1},\qquad
 u_{r,S}(\theta)=T_{r,S}b_{\theta,S},
\]
and
\[
 f_{r,S}(\theta)=\|u_{r,S}(\theta)\|_2^2-\tau^2.
\]
At \(S=\widehat\Sigma_n\), \(r=\rho_n\) gives the ridge-defined reduced forms and score, while \(r=0\) gives their inverse-defined versions on \(E_n\). Since \(K\) is compact, \(M_K=\sup_{\theta\in K}|\theta|<\infty\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:eighth\mbox{-}moment}\), Markov's inequality, and fixed dimensionality imply
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(1) \tag{6}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal P_W\): indeed, its squared operator norm is bounded, up to a dimension-dependent constant, by a sample average of \(\|W_i\|_2^4\) plus \(\|\overline W_n\|_2^4\), whose expectations are uniformly bounded.

Equations \((5)\)--\((6)\) give
\[
 \|\widehat\beta_n-\widetilde\beta_n\|_2
 +\|\widehat\alpha_n-\widetilde\alpha_n\|_2
 =O_P(n^{-2}) \tag{7}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal P_W\), where tildes denote inverse-defined versions on \(E_n\), with arbitrary measurable finite extensions on \(E_n^c\). The identity
\[
 |\|x\|_2^2-\|y\|_2^2|
 \leq (\|x\|_2+\|y\|_2)\|x-y\|_2
\]
and \((6)\)--\((7)\) then yield
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}|\widehat q_n(\theta)-\widetilde q_n(\theta)|=O_P(n^{-2}). \tag{8}
\]

The derivative comparison is also explicit. For a symmetric perturbation \(H\), set \(h_\theta=H_{zy}-\theta H_{zx}\). Differentiating \((A_S+rI_d)T_{r,S}=I_d\) gives \(D T_{r,S}[H]=-T_{r,S}H_{zz}T_{r,S}\), and the product rule gives
\[
 D_Sf_{r,S}(\theta)[H]
 =2u_{r,S}(\theta)^{\prime}T_{r,S}
   \{h_\theta-H_{zz}u_{r,S}(\theta)\}. \tag{9}
\]
On \(E_n\cap\{\|\widehat\Sigma_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq M\}\), formula \((9)\), the resolvent identity, compactness of \(K\), and the triangle inequality imply for a constant \(C_M<\infty\)
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}
 \|D_Sf_{\rho_n,\widehat\Sigma_n}(\theta)
      -D_Sf_{0,\widehat\Sigma_n}(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C_Mn^{-2}. \tag{10}
\]
Here the operator norm is for linear maps from symmetric matrices with Frobenius norm to \(\mathbb R\); no division occurs beyond the inverses controlled by \((5)\).

Put
\[
 H_{i,n}=(W_i-\overline W_n)(W_i-\overline W_n)^{\prime}-\widehat\Sigma_n.
\]
The fourth-moment consequence of \(\mathrm{ass:eighth\mbox{-}moment}\), the inequality \(\|uv^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm F}=\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\), and Markov's inequality give
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\|H_{i,n}\|_{\mathrm F}^2=O_P(1) \tag{11}
\]
uniformly. Applying \((10)\) to \(H_{i,n}\), then using \((6)\) and \((11)\), proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}
 \left[n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
   \{\widehat\psi_i(\theta)-\widetilde\psi_i(\theta)\}^2
 \right]^{1/2}
 =O_P(n^{-2})=o_P(n^{-1/2}). \tag{12}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality for Euclidean norms also gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}|\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)-\widetilde\sigma_n(\theta)|
 \leq
 \sup_{\theta\in K}
 \left[n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
   \{\widehat\psi_i(\theta)-\widetilde\psi_i(\theta)\}^2
 \right]^{1/2}
 =O_P(n^{-2}). \tag{13}
\]

Studentization is uniformly safe. Formula \((9)\) shows that, as a function of \(\theta\), every inverse-defined influence score is a polynomial of degree at most two. Its square is therefore a polynomial of degree at most four. The finitely many empirical coefficients of these polynomials are continuous functions of \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) and sample averages of products of at most four coordinates of \(W_i\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:eighth\mbox{-}moment}\) bounds their second moments uniformly. Entrywise Chebyshev bounds, equation \((3)\), and compactness of \(K\) consequently imply
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}|\widetilde\sigma_n(\theta)-\sigma_P(\theta)|=o_P(1) \tag{14}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_W\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:score\mbox{-}nondegeneracy}\), together with \((13)\)--\((14)\), implies that, with probability tending to one uniformly,
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in K}\{\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\wedge\widetilde\sigma_n(\theta)\}
 \geq c_{\sigma}/2. \tag{15}
\]
Thus truncation by \(n^{-1}\) in both critical-value formulas is inactive for all sufficiently large \(n\) on this event.

\textbf{3. Conditional Gaussian process and quantile stability.}
Let
\[
 v_r(\theta)=\bigl(D_Sf_{r,\widehat\Sigma_n}(\theta)[H_{1,n}],\ldots,
 D_Sf_{r,\widehat\Sigma_n}(\theta)[H_{n,n}]\bigr)^{\prime},
 \qquad
 a_r(\theta)=\frac{v_r(\theta)}{\|v_r(\theta)\|_2}.
\]
On the event in \((15)\), these vectors are well defined for \(r\in\{0,\rho_n\}\), and
\[
 \frac{n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_iD_Sf_{r,\widehat\Sigma_n}(\theta)[H_{i,n}]}
 {(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nD_Sf_{r,\widehat\Sigma_n}(\theta)[H_{i,n}]^2)^{1/2}}
 =\xi^{\prime}a_r(\theta). \tag{16}
\]
Equations \((12)\)--\((15)\) and the normalization inequality
\[
 \left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|_2}-\frac{y}{\|y\|_2}\right\|_2
 \leq \frac{2\|x-y\|_2}{\|y\|_2}
\]
give
\[
 e_n:=\sup_{\theta\in K}\|a_{\rho_n}(\theta)-a_0(\theta)\|_2=O_P(n^{-2}) \tag{17}
\]
uniformly. By \((9)\), \(v_r(\theta)\) is a vector-valued polynomial of degree at most two. Hence \(\{v_r(\theta):\theta\in K\}\), and therefore \(\{a_r(\theta):\theta\in K\}\), is contained in a subspace of dimension at most three. The union for \(r=0\) and \(r=\rho_n\) is contained in a subspace of dimension at most six. Conditional on the data, the two suprema defining the critical values are thus support functions of compact subsets of a unit sphere evaluated at one standard Gaussian vector \(G\in\mathbb R^s\), where \(1\leq s\leq6\).

We verify the needed quantile continuity directly. Fix \(p=1-\eta>1/2\). For a nonempty compact \(\mathcal A\subseteq\mathbb S^{s-1}\), let
\[
 X_{\mathcal A}=\sup_{a\in\mathcal A}a^{\prime}G.
\]
For any \(a_0\in\mathcal A\),
\[
 a_0^{\prime}G\leq X_{\mathcal A}\leq\|G\|_2.
\]
Therefore its \(p\)-quantile lies between \(z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)>0\) and the \(p\)-quantile of \(\|N(0,I_6)\|_2\). Write \(G=RU\), where \(R=\|G\|_2\), \(U\) is uniform on the unit sphere, and \(R\) and \(U\) are independent. On the spherical cap \(\{a_0^{\prime}U\geq1/2\}\), whose probability is bounded below uniformly over \(1\leq s\leq6\),
\[
 1/2\leq\sup_{a\in\mathcal A}a^{\prime}U\leq1.
\]
The radial density is continuous and bounded below on every compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\). Conditioning on this cap therefore gives constants \(c_p>0\) and \(s_p>0\), depending only on \(p\) and the bound \(s\leq6\), such that
\[
 P\{x<X_{\mathcal A}\leq x+t\}\geq c_pt \tag{18}
\]
whenever \(0<t\leq s_p\) and \(x\) lies in a fixed compact interval containing all the foregoing \(p\)-quantiles. The same polar representation shows that \(X_{\mathcal A}\) has no atom at any positive point.

If compact \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb S^{s-1}\) have Euclidean Hausdorff distance at most \(e\), then
\[
 |X_{\mathcal A}-X_{\mathcal B}|\leq e\|G\|_2. \tag{19}
\]
For sufficiently small \(e\), choose \(u_e=C e\sqrt{\log(1/e)}\), with \(C\) large enough that
\[
 P\{e\|G\|_2>u_e\}\leq e^2
\]
uniformly for \(s\leq6\). Since the distribution function is continuous at its positive \(p\)-quantile, equation \((18)\), applied with \(t=2e^2/c_p\), and the coupling inequality \((19)\) give
\[
 Q_p(X_{\mathcal A})\leq Q_p(X_{\mathcal B})+u_e+2e^2/c_p.
\]
Interchanging \(\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal B\) proves
\[
 |Q_p(X_{\mathcal A})-Q_p(X_{\mathcal B})|
 \leq C_p e\sqrt{\log(1/e)}. \tag{20}
\]
Thus \((16)\)--\((17)\) and \((20)\) yield
\[
 |\widehat c_{1-\eta}-\widetilde c_{1-\eta}|
 =O_P(n^{-2}\sqrt{\log n})=o_P(n^{-1/2}) \tag{21}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal P_W\). Under the same coupling of multipliers, \((19)\) also gives
\[
 E_{\xi}\left[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}|\xi^{\prime}a_{\rho_n}(\theta)-\xi^{\prime}a_0(\theta)|
 \mathrel{\big|}W_1,\ldots,W_n\right]
 \leq E\|N(0,I_6)\|_2\,e_n=O_P(n^{-2}),
\]
which proves first-order equivalence of the standardized multiplier influence processes.

The same covariance argument applies conditionally to an empirical-bootstrap resample. Conditional on the data, its eighth-moment envelope is \(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\|W_i\|_2^8=O_P(1)\). Its centered instrument covariance has conditional mean \((1-n^{-1})\widehat A_n\), so its bias relative to \(\widehat A_n\) is \(O_P(n^{-1})\). Repeating the fourth-moment expansion conditionally shows, on \(E_n\), that the conditional probability that the resample instrument covariance is more than \(m_{\Sigma}/4\) away from \(\widehat A_n\) is \(O_P(n^{-2})\). On the complementary conditional event its smallest eigenvalue is at least \(m_{\Sigma}/4\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and the preceding resolvent and derivative calculations apply with changed constants. Hence ridge totalization is asymptotically immaterial for the empirical-bootstrap weak limits as well.

Equations \((7)\), \((8)\), \((12)\), and \((21)\), together with \((4)\), establish the claimed uniform \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\) equivalence. For positive-part endpoint maps, the only additional nonsmooth operation obeys
\[
 |\sqrt{x_+}-\sqrt{y_+}|\leq\sqrt{|x-y|}.
\]
Thus the \(O_P(n^{-2})\) score perturbation contributes only \(O_P(n^{-1})\) at quadratic contact, which is negligible after \(n^{1/4}\) scaling; away from contact the ordinary local Lipschitz bound is sharper. Therefore all stated contact and interior weak limits are unchanged.

\textbf{4. Expected-loss contribution of the fallback event.}
The target set is nonempty under the maintained structural restrictions. Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:outcome\mbox{-}equation}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:outcome\mbox{-}orthogonality}\) give
\[
 \Sigma_{zy}(P)=\Sigma_{zz}(P)\gamma+\theta_0\Sigma_{zx}(P),
 \qquad
 \alpha(P)=\gamma+\theta_0\beta(P),
\]
where inversion is permitted because \(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{zz}(P))\geq m_{\Sigma}>0\). Because \(\theta_0\in K\) and assumption \(\mathrm{ass:l2\mbox{-}leakage}\) gives \(\|\gamma\|_2\leq\tau\),
\[
 q_P(\theta_0)=\|\gamma\|_2^2-\tau^2\leq0,
 \qquad
 \theta_0\in\Theta_I(P).
\]
Let \(D_K=\operatorname{diam}(K)<\infty\). For every closed \(B\subseteq K\), including \(B=\varnothing\), the definition of \(d_{\to}\) and nonemptiness of \(\Theta_I(P)\) imply
\[
 0\leq d_{\to}(B,\Theta_I(P))\leq D_K.
\]
Therefore the contribution of the near-singular event to any expected outer-loss comparison is at most
\[
 D_KP(E_n^c)=O(n^{-2})
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_W\). This is negligible relative to both \(n^{-1/4}\) and \(n^{-1/2}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma makes all reported procedures measurable on singular samples while preserving their first-order contact and interior behavior. \textit{Gap.} Watson et al.'s implementation removes undefined rows; this result replaces that implicit conditioning by an every-sample convention and quantifies its uniform asymptotic cost. \textit{Consumer.} Software can return an interval or confidence hull on every dataset without altering the endpoint laws, coverage claim, or adaptive loss frontier.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-multiplier-coupling]\label{thm:uniform-multiplier-coupling}
The answer is affirmative.  Define
\[
 T_{n,P}=\sup_{\theta\in K}
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat q_n(\theta)-q_P(\theta)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}},
 \qquad
 T_n^{\xi}=\sup_{\theta\in K}
 \frac{\widehat{\mathbb G}_n^{\xi}(\theta)}
      {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}}.
\]
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_W\), the bounded-Lipschitz distance between \(\mathcal L_P(T_{n,P})\) and the conditional law \(\mathcal L_\xi(T_n^{\xi}\mid W_1,\ldots,W_n)\) converges to zero in \(P\)-probability, and the conditional quantile is uniformly valid:
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_W}
 P\!\left\{
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_I(P)}
 \frac{\sqrt n\{\widehat q_n(\theta)-q_P(\theta)\}}
      {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}}
 \leq\widehat c_{1-\eta}\right\}\geq1-\eta. \tag{A}
\]
There are events \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\) and nonnegative random radii \(R_{n,P}\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}P(\mathcal E_{n,P}^c)=O(n^{-2}),
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P[R_{n,P}]=O(n^{-1/2}), \tag{B}
\]
the family \(\{\sqrt nR_{n,P}:n\geq1,P\in\mathcal P_W\}\) is uniformly integrable, and, on \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\),
\[
 C_n\subseteq\{\theta\in K:q_P(\theta)\leq R_{n,P}\},
 \qquad R_{n,P}\geq n^{-1/2}. \tag{C}
\]
Thus the same observable multiplier rule supplies simultaneous whole-identified-set calibration and an integrable root-\(n\) score-band radius on both \(\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{ct}}(C_\delta)\) and \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{int}}(\delta_0)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give a finite-dimensional proof tailored to the quadratic score. Let \(m=(d+2)(d+3)/2\), let \(\operatorname{vech}\) be a fixed isometry from symmetric \((d+2)\)-by-\((d+2)\) matrices into \(\mathbb R^m\), and define
\[
 V_{i,P}=\operatorname{vech}\{W_iW_i'-\Sigma(P)\},
 \qquad S_{n,P}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nV_{i,P}. \tag{1}
\]
Centered independent sampling gives \(E_PV_{i,P}=0\), and the eighth-moment restriction gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_P\|V_{i,P}\|_2^4<\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Covariance conditioning makes every \(\Sigma_{zz}(P)\) invertible with inverse norm at most \(m_\Sigma^{-1}\). On the neighborhood where the instrument block has smallest eigenvalue at least \(m_\Sigma/2\), explicit differentiation of the inverse shows that the first two derivatives of \(S\mapsto\mathfrak q_n(S;\theta)\) are bounded uniformly in \(\theta\in K\), \(P\in\mathcal P_W\), and all sufficiently large \(n\). In particular,
\[
 \psi_P(W_i;\theta)=a_P(\theta)'V_{i,P},
 \qquad
 a_P(\theta)=a_{0,P}+a_{1,P}\theta+a_{2,P}\theta^2, \tag{3}
\]
where the three coefficient vectors are uniformly bounded. This follows from
\[
 D\{A^{-1}b\}[H]=A^{-1}(h-H_AA^{-1}b),
 \qquad D\|u\|_2^2[du]=2u'du. \tag{4}
\]

The centered covariance obeys
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_n-\Sigma(P)
 =n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\{W_iW_i'-\Sigma(P)\}
 -\overline W_n\overline W_n'. \tag{5}
\]
Fourth-moment expansion using (2) gives
\[
 \sup_PE_P\|\widehat\Sigma_n-\Sigma(P)\|_{\mathrm F}^4=O(n^{-2}),
 \qquad
 \sup_PE_P\|\overline W_n\|_2^8=O(n^{-4}). \tag{6}
\]
Let \(\mathcal E_{n,P}=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Sigma_{zz,n})\geq m_\Sigma/2\}\). The eigenvalue variational inequality and Markov's inequality applied to (6), equivalently the established ridge-totalization lemma, imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}P(\mathcal E_{n,P}^c)=O(n^{-2}). \tag{7}
\]
Taylor's formula, (5)--(6), and the \(n^{-2}\) ridge resolvent bound give on \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\)
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}\left|
 \sqrt n\{\widehat q_n(\theta)-q_P(\theta)\}
 -a_P(\theta)'S_{n,P}\right|=\rho_{n,P},
 \qquad
 \sup_PE_P[\rho_{n,P}\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_{n,P}}]=O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{8}
\]

The sample counterpart of (3) is also a polynomial of degree at most two in \(\theta\). With \(\Omega_P=E_P[V_{i,P}V_{i,P}']\), expansion of its finitely many coefficients and (2) gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in K}\left|
 \widehat\sigma_n^2(\theta)-a_P(\theta)'\Omega_Pa_P(\theta)
 \right|=o_P(1) \tag{9}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_W\); on \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\), this difference is bounded in \(L^2\) by \(Cn^{-1/2}\). Score nondegeneracy therefore implies
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_W}P\left\{
 \inf_{\theta\in K}\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\geq c_\sigma/2
 \right\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{10}
\]
Thus denominator truncation is asymptotically inactive.

Conditionally on the sample, the multiplier process is a centered Gaussian polynomial of degree at most two. After studentization it has the representation
\[
 \frac{\widehat{\mathbb G}_n^\xi(\theta)}
      {\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}}
 =u_n(\theta)'G_n, \tag{11}
\]
where \(G_n\) is standard Gaussian in dimension at most three and \(\|u_n(\theta)\|_2\leq1\). Consequently,
\[
 \widehat c_{1-\eta}^+
 \leq Q_{1-\eta}\{\|N(0,I_3)\|_2\}=:c_\eta<\infty. \tag{12}
\]

We prove uniform conditional approximation by contradiction. If it failed, some sequence \(P_n\in\mathcal P_W\) would have failure probability bounded away from zero. The matrices \(\Omega_{P_n}\), the three bounded coefficient vectors in (3), and the finitely many coefficients of \(\sigma_{P_n}^2(\theta)\) lie in compact Euclidean sets, so pass to a convergent subsequence. For each fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^m\), (2) permits the uniform characteristic-function expansion
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp\{it'V_{i,P_n}/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{t'\Omega_{P_n}t}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}). \tag{13}
\]
Raising (13) to the \(n\)-th power proves that \(S_{n,P_n}\) converges to a centered Gaussian vector with the limiting covariance. Every entry of the empirical covariance driving (11) is an average of a polynomial of degree at most four in \(W_i\), whose variance is bounded by the eighth-moment restriction. Hence that empirical covariance converges in probability to the same limiting covariance, and (5)--(6) make the sample polynomial coefficients converge to their population counterparts.

The map from these finitely many coefficients to the supremum over compact \(K\) is continuous under uniform coefficient convergence, because \(c_\sigma>0\) prevents division by zero. Along the selected subsequence,
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_n}(T_{n,P_n}),
 \mathcal L_\xi(T_n^\xi\mid W_1,\ldots,W_n)
 \right)\longrightarrow0 \tag{14}
\]
in \(P_n\)-probability, contradicting the assumed failure. Thus (14) holds uniformly over \(\mathcal P_W\).

For quantile transfer, each subsequential Gaussian limit has the form
\[
 H(G)=\sup_{\theta\in K}v(\theta)'G,
 \qquad \|v(\theta)\|_2=1, \tag{15}
\]
on its effective Gaussian support. Its \((1-\eta)\)-quantile is at least \(\Phi^{-1}(1-\eta)>0\), because the supremum dominates any fixed \(v(\theta_0)'G\). Write \(G=RU\), with the Gaussian radius \(R\) independent of its direction \(U\). Conditional on a direction with \(H(U)>0\), \(RH(U)\) has a continuous radial density; directions with \(H(U)\leq0\) cannot create an atom at a positive value. Hence (15) has no atom at its positive \((1-\eta)\)-quantile. Compactness of the coefficient limits turns this subsequential fact into uniform upper-quantile continuity. Combining it with (14) yields
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_W}
 P\{T_{n,P}\leq\widehat c_{1-\eta}\}\geq1-\eta. \tag{16}
\]
Replacing the supremum over \(K\) in (16) by the smaller set \(\Theta_I(P)\) proves assertion (A) of the theorem.

On \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\), define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{n,P}={}&n^{-1/2}
 +\sup_{\theta\in K}|\widehat q_n(\theta)-q_P(\theta)|\\
 &+n^{-1/2}\widehat c_{1-\eta}^+
 \sup_{\theta\in K}\{\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}\};
 \end{aligned} \tag{17}
\]
on \(\mathcal E_{n,P}^c\), set \(R_{n,P}=n^{-1/2}\). Equations (2), (6), (8)--(9), and (12) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_W}E_PR_{n,P}=O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{18}
\]
The same calculations with squares give \(\sup_{n,P}E_P[(\sqrt nR_{n,P})^2]<\infty\), which proves uniform integrability of \(\{\sqrt nR_{n,P}\}\). If \(\theta\in C_n\), its defining inequality implies
\[
 \widehat q_n(\theta)
 \leq n^{-1/2}\widehat c_{1-\eta}^+
 \{\widehat\sigma_n(\theta)\vee n^{-1}\}. \tag{19}
\]
Adding the uniform score error in (17) gives \(q_P(\theta)\leq R_{n,P}\) on \(\mathcal E_{n,P}\), and the first term in (17) gives \(R_{n,P}\geq n^{-1/2}\). This proves assertions (B)--(C). Every bound is uniform over the uniformly regular fixed-relevance subclass \(\mathcal P_W\), and therefore over its contact and fixed-interior subclasses.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-dimensional quadratic-score argument fully discharged the former multiplier question and supplies both calibration and an integrable score-band radius. \textit{Gap.} Generic multiplier results do not directly combine the ridge-totalized polynomial score, uniform studentization, whole-set one-sided quantile transfer, and the expected-radius control used here. \textit{Consumer.} The adaptive hull uses the theorem for uniform coverage on \(\mathcal P_W\) and for its continuous expected-loss elbow.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The uniform upper theorem is deliberately limited to the strict subclass \(\mathcal P_W\): fixed dimension, known leakage radius, uniform covariance conditioning, a bounded eighth moment, score-variance bounds, parameter-space margin, and fixed relevance are load-bearing. It does not establish a multiplier approximation for Watson et al.'s unrestricted \(p=2\) class, weak instruments, growing dimension, unknown leakage radius, or contact with the boundary of \(K\). The expected-loss elbow also uses the theorem's integrable root-\(n\) score-band radius; distributional coupling alone would give coverage but not the displayed risk bound. The Gaussian bootstrap nonuniformity witness and the minimax converses require none of that upward extension of the upper theorem and remain valid in the broader published class.

\section{Honest scope}

Watson et al.'s Corollary 2.1 and Appendix A.7 supply the unrestricted raw \(p=2\) interval. This note separately restricts the target to \(K\), so the identified set is the \(K\)-restricted score sublevel set and the raw square-root endpoints are used only at nonnegative slack. The parameter margin removes \(K\)-truncation on the maintained contact-local and fixed-interior regimes. The uniform multiplier-coupling theorem is fully discharged and proves whole-set coverage plus the continuous expected-loss elbow on the strict uniformly regular fixed-relevance subclass \(\mathcal P_W\). That upper theorem uses fixed \(d\), known scalar leakage radius, uniformly conditioned covariance, bounded eighth moments, nondegenerate score variance, compact parameter margin, and relevance bounded away from zero; it is not asserted for Watson et al.'s unrestricted class. By contrast, the explicit Gaussian bootstrap counterexample and both minimax lower-bound paths lie within the published model, so those negative and converse conclusions transfer upward. All finite-sample covariance operations use the stated ridge convention, and the directed loss is defined when a random hull is empty.
