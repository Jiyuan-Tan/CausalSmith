Fix \(\epsilon>0\), an input \((q_S,q_0)\in\mathcal C_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0)\), a model \(M\in\mathfrak F_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0)\), and a cell \((x,y)\). By Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}data\text{-}map}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}observed\text{-}fiber}\),
\[
 q_S(v)=P_M(V=v\mid S=1),
 \qquad
 q_0(t)=P_M(T_0=t).
\]
Moreover, Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\text{-}input\text{-}image}\) give \(P_M(S=1)>0\) and \(q_S(v)\geq\epsilon>0\) for every \(v\in\mathcal X_V\). Hence
\[
 P_M(V=v)\geq P_M(V=v,S=1)
 =P_M(S=1)q_S(v)
 \geq P_M(S=1)\epsilon>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the full population law is strictly positive at every substantive cell, and every one of its marginal conditioning cells is positive.

Suppose first that \(T_0=V\). The population-initialization clause of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\) puts, for each \(v\in\mathcal X_V\), the key
\[
 (V,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop},v,\ast,\ast)
\]
in the initial state with annotation
\[
 q_0(v)=P_M(T_0=v)=P_M(V=v).
 \tag{2}
\]
Consider any finite, cellwise derivation of \(P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))\) from \(P_M(V)\) using ordinary finite probability manipulations and the three rules of do-calculus. We prove inductively over that derivation that the scalar key produced at each step becomes present in the saturation state. Every initial scalar is present by (2). For the induction step, all finitely many input keys are present. If the step is a probability manipulation, its marginalization, conditioning, or chain instance is one of the complete scalar moves in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\). A division is used only at a positive conditioning cell. Positivity for conditionals formed directly from the observational law follows from (1), and positivity of any other conditioning cell used is part of the legality of the stipulated cellwise derivation. If the step is do-calculus, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}rules}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}graphical\text{-}fixing\text{-}moves}\) give the corresponding population-tagged move and equality of its input and output kernels.

In either case, the move instance lies in the finite universe \(\mathscr U\), its complete input-key set is present, and its graphical and denominator premises hold. If its output is absent, it remains enabled because Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\) only adds keys. It cannot be postponed forever: each earlier selected move adds a previously absent key, while Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}extid\text{-}sound\text{-}termination}\) bounds the number of present keys by \(\lvert\mathscr K_{\mathcal G}\rvert\). A terminal state omitting that output would therefore contradict the rule that failure occurs only when no enabled instance has an absent output. This completes the induction.

The target key \((Y,X,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop},y,x,\ast)\) consequently becomes present and is checked before the next transition. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}extid\text{-}storage\text{-}invariant}\) identifies its annotation, throughout the exact fiber, with
\[
 k^M_{(Y,X,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop})}(y\mid x,\ast)
 =P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
 =\psi_{x,y}(M).
 \tag{3}
\]
The returned expression belongs to \(\mathcal A(q_S,q_0)\) by Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}arithmetic\text{-}language}\), \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\), and \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\). Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}ordinary\text{-}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}completeness}\) supplies such a finite ordinary derivation whenever the cell is identifiable from the full observational law, proving the first reduction.

Suppose next that \(\mathcal G\) entails \(S\perp\!\!\!\perp V\). Selected initialization provides
\[
 (V,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{sel},v,\ast,\ast)
 \quad\text{with annotation}\quad q_S(v)
\]
for every \(v\). With \(A=V\) and \(B=C=\varnothing\), the population-storage predicate in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}source\text{-}bridge\text{-}moves}\) is exactly \(V\perp\!\!\!\perp S\). Saturation therefore makes the corresponding population key present, unless it is already present, and preserves its annotation. The storage invariant then gives
\[
 q_S(v)=P_M(V=v\mid S=1)=P_M(V=v),
 \tag{4}
\]
where conditioning is defined because \(P_M(S=1)>0\). Thus the same full-population scalar keys are available with the claimed annotations.

Now let \(T_0=\varnothing\). Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\) says that the unique trivial margin contributes no scalar key. A margin-extension instance requires a nonempty population-margin input key, so Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}source\text{-}bridge\text{-}moves}\) makes no such instance admissible. Initially every present key is selected-tagged. Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}graphical\text{-}fixing\text{-}moves}\) preserve source tags. The only source-changing moves are margin extension and population storage; the former is unavailable, while the latter maps
\[
 (A,B,C,\mathrm{sel},a,b,c)
 \longmapsto
 (A,B,C,\mathrm{pop},a,b,c)
 \tag{5}
\]
with unchanged annotation exactly when
\[
 A\perp\!\!\!\perp S\mid B,C
 \quad\text{in }G_{\overline B}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Induction on the number of transitions shows that every selected key belongs to the closure of selected initialization under selected probability and graphical-fixing moves, and that every population key is either an output (5) satisfying (6) or follows from such outputs by population probability and graphical-fixing moves. Conversely, any absent output in these closures whose complete inputs are present remains enabled. Finiteness and the preceding no-permanent-postponement argument show that saturation adds it before termination. Hence the resulting closure has exactly the asserted keys. Setting \(B=C=\varnothing\) in (6) gives unconditional selection independence.

Finally, suppose margin extension reconstructs every cell of the full population law. Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}margin\text{-}extension}\) gives, at each reconstructed cell \((t,z)\),
\[
 P_M(T_0=t,Z=z)
 =P_M(Z=z\mid T_0=t,S=1)q_0(t),
 \tag{7}
\]
and Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}extid\text{-}storage\text{-}invariant}\) says that the transition annotation is the right-hand side of (7). Taking \(Z=V\setminus T_0\), or composing reconstructed population blocks by the probability moves, makes every key
\[
 (V,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop},v,\ast,\ast)
\]
present with annotation \(P_M(V=v)\). The replay argument leading to (3) now applies. In particular, under an adjustment criterion with adjustment block \(L\), it returns
\[
 P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
 =\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal X_L}
 P_M(Y=y\mid X=x,L=\ell)P_M(L=\ell),
 \tag{8}
\]
and under the corresponding front-door criterion with mediating block \(D\), it returns
\[
 P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
 =\sum_{d\in\mathcal X_D}P_M(D=d\mid X=x)
 \sum_{x'\in\mathcal X_X}
 P_M(Y=y\mid D=d,X=x')P_M(X=x').
 \tag{9}
\]
The named graphical criteria are precisely the premises making the usual do-calculus derivations of (8)--(9) legal; Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}rules}\) validates each graphical equality, and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\) realizes the finite sums, products, and positive divisions.

Apply (3) to every successful \((x,y)\in\mathcal I\). Each returned coordinate equals its coordinate of \(\boldsymbol\psi(M)\) throughout the exact fiber. If all runs succeed, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}full\text{-}kernel\text{-}collection}\) forms \(\mathbf F\) in the same order, so
\[
 \mathbf F(q_S,q_0)
 =\bigl(F_{x,y}(q_S,q_0):(x,y)\in\mathcal I\bigr)^{\mathsf T}
 =\bigl(\psi_{x,y}(M):(x,y)\in\mathcal I\bigr)^{\mathsf T}
 =\boldsymbol\psi(M).
\]
If some run fails, \(\mathbf F\) is not formed, while the proved coordinatewise equality remains valid for every returned cell. This proves all four reductions and the collection claim.
