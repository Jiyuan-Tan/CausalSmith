We prove the necessary direction by contraposition. Suppose \(\psi_{x,y}\) is not identifiable from \(P_N(V)\) over the positive finite substantive model class compatible with \(G_V\). Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}all\text{-}graph\text{-}positive\text{-}ordinary\text{-}selection\text{-}lift}\) supplies edge-silent models \(M_1,M_2\) compatible with the full arbitrary graph \(\mathcal G\) such that
\[
 \operatorname{Obs}_{T_0}(M_1)=\operatorname{Obs}_{T_0}(M_2)
 \quad\text{but}\quad
 \psi_{x,y}(M_1)\ne\psi_{x,y}(M_2).
 \tag{1}
\]
Their selected tables are strictly positive, so both belong to the full positive graph-compatible model class. If a single two-law identifying map existed on that full class, applying it to the common left-hand input in (1) would force the two right-hand query values to be equal. This contradiction proves the contrapositive. The reasoning uses a separating subclass to refute identification on its superclass; it makes no reverse claim from subclass identification.

Now suppose \(S\) is terminal and \(T_0=V\). The necessary implication just proved does not require terminality. For the converse, assume ordinary identification on \(G_V\), and let \(f\) be its identifying map, so
\[
 f(P_N(V))=\psi_{x,y}(N)
 \tag{2}
\]
for every positive finite substantive model \(N\) compatible with \(G_V\). Take any positive finite model \(M\) compatible with \(\mathcal G\). Delete the equation for \(S\) and retain all substantive equations and exogenous coordinates. Terminality means that \(S\) is not a directed parent of any substantive vertex, so no retained equation uses \(S\). A coordinate represented by a bidirected edge \(S\leftrightarrow W\) can remain as a private disturbance of \(W\) after \(S\) is deleted; it is independent of the other exogenous coordinates and affects at most that one retained equation. Thus the retained system, denoted \(N_M\), is recursive, semi-Markovian, and compatible with \(G_V\), even when bidirected edges are incident to \(S\). Deletion changes neither the factual law on \(V\) nor any intervention on \(V\), whence
\[
 P_{N_M}(V)=P_M(V),
 \qquad
 \psi_{x,y}(N_M)=\psi_{x,y}(M).
 \tag{3}
\]

The ordinary positivity premise required in (2) follows from selected positivity rather than being added silently. If \(P_M(S=1)>0\) and \(P_M(V=v\mid S=1)>0\), then
\[
 P_M(V=v)
 \geq P_M(V=v,S=1)
 =P_M(S=1)P_M(V=v\mid S=1)>0
 \tag{4}
\]
for every \(v\). Because \(T_0=V\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}data\text{-}map}\) gives \(q_0=P_M(V)\). Combining (2)--(4), the explicit two-law map
\[
 F(q_S,q_0)=f(q_0)
 \tag{5}
\]
satisfies \(F(q_S,q_0)=\psi_{x,y}(M)\) for every positive model compatible with \(\mathcal G\). This proves the converse and the exact terminal full-margin equivalence without excluding incident bidirected edges.

For the executable claim, fix \(\epsilon>0\) and \((q_S,q_0)\in\mathcal C_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,V)\). Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}ordinary\text{-}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}completeness}\) provides a finite ordinary do-calculus and probability derivation of every ordinarily identifiable cell from \(q_0=P(V)\) on \(G_V\). Its graphical separation predicates remain valid in \(\mathcal G\): every path between substantive vertices that is absent from \(G_V\) but present in \(\mathcal G\) must pass through \(S\); because \(S\) is terminal, both incident edges have arrowheads at \(S\), and \(S\) has no directed descendant. Thus \(S\) is an unconditioned collider with no conditioned descendant in every relevant mutilated graph, since the conditioning blocks are subsets of \(V\). Such a path is blocked. Hence the ordinary derivation is a legal population-tagged derivation in the transition system.

Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}boundary\text{-}reductions}\) now applies: full-margin initialization stores every cell of \(q_0=P(V)\), and saturation replays that finite derivation. It returns some \(F_{x,y}\in\mathcal A(q_S,q_0)\). Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}extid\text{-}sound\text{-}termination}\) then yields, for every \(M\in\mathfrak F_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,V;q_S,q_0)\),
\[
 F_{x,y}(q_S,q_0)=\psi_{x,y}(M).
 \tag{6}
\]
This argument proves success only on the terminal full-margin boundary for ordinarily identifiable cells and does not turn \(\operatorname{FAIL}\) into a general nonidentification certificate.
