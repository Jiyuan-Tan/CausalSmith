Set
\[
 A_0=u^{\mathsf T}a_+,
 \qquad N_v=u^{\mathsf T}a_v,
 \qquad \delta_+=\beta^{\mathsf T}a_+,
 \qquad \delta_v=\beta^{\mathsf T}a_v.
\]
The law of total probability over the active atoms \(j\in\mathcal J_0\), with every other independent exogenous coordinate integrated out as in the row definitions, gives
\[
 P_M(V=v,S=1)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_0}u_ja_v(j)=N_v,
 \qquad
 P_M(S=1)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_0}u_ja_+(j)=A_0>0.
\]
The last inequality follows from membership in the exact fiber and \(\mathrm{ass{:}positive\text{-}selection}\).  Therefore
\[
 q_S(v)=\frac{N_v}{A_0}.
\]
The same total-probability calculation, using Definitions of \(b_t\) and \(h_{x,y}\), gives
\[
 q_0(t)=P_M(T_0=t)=u^{\mathsf T}b_t,
 \qquad
 \psi_{x,y}(M)=u^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}.
\]

Because \(\Gamma_{(M,\mathcal R,U_0)}\) is a finite-dimensional linear subspace and \(h_{x,y}\notin\Gamma_{(M,\mathcal R,U_0)}\), let
\[
 \beta=h_{x,y}-\operatorname{proj}_{\Gamma_{(M,\mathcal R,U_0)}}h_{x,y}.
\]
Then \(\beta\in\Gamma_{(M,\mathcal R,U_0)}^{\perp}\), \(\beta\neq0\), and
\[
 \beta^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}=\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2>0.
\]
Orthogonality to the generators of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}projective\text{-}row\text{-}space}\) yields
\[
 \beta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1=0,
 \qquad
 \beta^{\mathsf T}b_t=0,
 \qquad
 A_0\delta_v-N_v\delta_+=0
 \quad\text{for every }t,v.
\]

The step-size bound is defined because \(\beta\neq0\).  For every active atom,
\[
 u_j^{\pm}
 \geq u_j-\lvert\tau\rvert\lVert\beta\rVert_\infty
 \geq\frac{\eta}{2}>0,
 \qquad
 \sum_j u_j^{\pm}=1\pm\tau\beta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1=1.
\]
Thus both \(u^+\) and \(u^-\) are probability vectors on exactly the unchanged support \(\mathcal J_0\).  Every other exogenous coordinate is unchanged and has all active atom masses at least \(\eta\); hence every atom of either perturbed representation has mass at least \(\eta/2\).

Because \(A_0>0\), at least one coordinate of the nonnegative row \(a_+\) is positive.  Every coordinate of \(u^\pm\) is positive, so
\[
 (u^\pm)^{\mathsf T}a_+=A_0\pm\tau\delta_+>0.
\]
For each \(v\), the cross-product equality above gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{(u^\pm)^{\mathsf T}a_v}{(u^\pm)^{\mathsf T}a_+}
 &=\frac{N_v\pm\tau\delta_v}{A_0\pm\tau\delta_+}\\
 &=\frac{N_v}{A_0}=q_S(v),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the second equality follows by cross multiplication from \(A_0\delta_v=N_v\delta_+\), and both denominators are positive.  Similarly,
\[
 (u^\pm)^{\mathsf T}b_t=u^{\mathsf T}b_t\pm\tau\beta^{\mathsf T}b_t=q_0(t).
\]
Hence the two perturbations reproduce both observed tables exactly and preserve every selected lower bound \(q_S(v)\geq\epsilon\).

Only the mass vector of the independent coordinate \(U_0\) changes.  All deterministic response maps, their parent arguments, and all other independent exogenous laws remain fixed.  Thus the induced models remain recursive, semi-Markovian, and compatible with \(\mathcal G\).  The preceding equalities and positivity place the perturbed triples in \(\mathfrak R_{\epsilon,\eta/2}^{\circ}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0)\).  Finally,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \psi_{x,y}(M_1)-\psi_{x,y}(M_2)
 &=(u+\tau\beta)^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}-(u-\tau\beta)^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}\\
 &=2\tau\beta^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}\neq0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Each scalar query is a convex combination of entries of \(h_{x,y}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal J_0}\), so it lies in \([0,1]\).  This proves every assertion.
