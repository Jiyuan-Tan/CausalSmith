First suppose \(\operatorname{RF}_{x,y}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0)\) is feasible.  For \(i=1,2\), use the deterministic response maps in the selected stratum and the independent coordinate laws \(w^{(i)}_{\ell j}\) to construct the recursive SCM \(M_i\).  The stratum contains only response maps allowed by \(\mathcal G\), so \(M_i\) is graph compatible and semi-Markovian.  Its exogenous law is the product law, and the law of total probability over \(\omega\) gives, directly from Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}canonical\text{-}response\text{-}fiber\text{-}formula}\),
\[
 P_{M_i}(V=v,S=1)=p_{\vartheta^{(i)}}(v,1),
 \quad
 P_{M_i}(T_0=t)=m_{\vartheta^{(i)}}(t),
 \quad
 P_{M_i}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))=g_{\vartheta^{(i)},x,y}.
\]
Also
\[
 P_{M_i}(S=1)=\sum_vp_{\vartheta^{(i)}}(v,1)
 =\rho_{\vartheta^{(i)}}>0.
\]
The selected-law equations in the formula therefore imply
\[
 P_{M_i}(V=v\mid S=1)
 =\frac{p_{\vartheta^{(i)}}(v,1)}{\rho_{\vartheta^{(i)}}}
 =q_S(v)
\]
for every \(v\); division is licensed by \(\rho_{\vartheta^{(i)}}>0\).  The margin equations similarly give \(P_{M_i}(T_0=t)=q_0(t)\) for every \(t\).  Since the fixed input satisfies \(q_S(v)\geq\epsilon\), each \(M_i\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G)\), and hence to \(\mathfrak F_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0)\).  Finally,
\[
 \psi_{x,y}(M_1)-\psi_{x,y}(M_2)
 =g_{\vartheta^{(1)},x,y}-g_{\vartheta^{(2)},x,y}\neq0.
\]
The inequalities \(w^{(i)}_{\ell j}\geq\eta>0\) give the claimed common interior slack on the exact active supports.

Conversely, suppose finite models \(M_1,M_2\) in the exact fiber separate the query.  Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}response\text{-}selection\text{-}admg\text{-}reduction}\) to each model, preserving its full factual and counterfactual law.  Record the resulting deterministic response map taken by every atom of each independent finite exogenous coordinate.  Merge atoms that induce the same response label, and delete every label of mass zero.  This produces a canonical active-support stratum and strictly positive coordinate weights \(w^{(i)}_{\ell j}\) without changing any structural response, observational probability, margin probability, or intervention probability.  Since the pair has finitely many active weights, the number
\[
 \eta=\min_{i,\ell,j}w^{(i)}_{\ell j}
\]
exists and is strictly positive.  Let \(\vartheta^{(i)}\) be the resulting response point and \(\rho_i=P_{M_i}(S=1)>0\).  Membership in the exact fiber gives, cell by cell,
\[
 p_{\vartheta^{(i)}}(v,1)
 =P_{M_i}(V=v,S=1)
 =q_S(v)P_{M_i}(S=1)
 =q_S(v)\rho_i,
\]
\[
 m_{\vartheta^{(i)}}(t)=P_{M_i}(T_0=t)=q_0(t),
 \qquad
 g_{\vartheta^{(i)},x,y}=\psi_{x,y}(M_i).
\]
The last two values differ by hypothesis, so every clause of \(\operatorname{RF}_{x,y}\) holds.

Finally, the graph admits only finitely many deterministic response maps and active-support strata.  Within each stratum, the displayed probabilities are finite sums of monomials in the coordinate weights; normalization, common-interior, positive-normalizer, exact-fiber, and query-separation requirements are polynomial equalities or strict or weak polynomial inequalities.  Thus the disjunction is a finite semialgebraic feasibility problem whose variables and constraints are written explicitly in the definition.  This establishes both directions and the computable witness characterization.
