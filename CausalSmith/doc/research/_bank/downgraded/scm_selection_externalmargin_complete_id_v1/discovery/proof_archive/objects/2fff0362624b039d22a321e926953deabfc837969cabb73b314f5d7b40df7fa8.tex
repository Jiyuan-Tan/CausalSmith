Fix \(\epsilon>0\), an input \((q_S,q_0)\in\mathcal C_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0)\), a model \(M\in\mathfrak F_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0)\), and a cell \((x,y)\).  By Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}data\text{-}map}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}observed\text{-}fiber}\),
\[
q_S(v)=P_M(V=v\mid S=1),\qquad q_0(t)=P_M(T_0=t).
\]
Moreover, Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\text{-}input\text{-}image}\) give \(P_M(S=1)>0\) and \(q_S(v)\geq\epsilon>0\) for every \(v\in\mathcal X_V\).  Hence
\[
P_M(V=v)\ \geq\ P_M(V=v,S=1)
=P_M(S=1)q_S(v)
\geq P_M(S=1)\epsilon>0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the full population law is strictly positive at every substantive cell.  In particular, every marginal of that law on a conditioning cell is positive.

Suppose first that \(T_0=V\).  The population-initialization clause of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\) puts, for each \(v\in\mathcal X_V\), the key
\[
 (V,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop},v,\ast,\ast)
\]
in the initial state with annotation
\[
q_0(v)=P_M(T_0=v)=P_M(V=v). \tag{2}
\]
Consider any finite, cellwise derivation of \(P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))\) from \(P_M(V)\) using ordinary finite probability manipulations and the three rules of do-calculus.  Every initial scalar in this derivation is present by (2).  We prove inductively over the derivation that the scalar key produced at each step becomes present in the saturation state.  For the induction step, all of the finitely many input keys are present.  If the step is a probability manipulation, the corresponding marginalization, conditioning, or chain instance is one of the complete scalar moves in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\); a division occurs only at a positive conditioning cell, as required there.  Positivity for conditionals formed directly from the observational law follows from (1), and positivity of any other conditioning cell used is part of the legality of the stipulated cellwise derivation.  If the step is do-calculus, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}rules}\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}graphical\text{-}fixing\text{-}moves}\) give the corresponding population-tagged move and the equality of its input and output kernels.

In either case, the move instance lies in the finite universe \(\mathscr U\), its complete input-key set is present, and its graphical and denominator premises hold.  If its output is not already present, it remains enabled because Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\) only adds keys.  It cannot be postponed forever: each move chosen before it adds a previously absent key, while Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}extid\text{-}sound\text{-}termination}\) bounds the total number of present keys by \(\lvert\mathscr K_{\mathcal G}\rvert\).  Equivalently, a terminal state omitting that output would contradict the rule that failure occurs only when no enabled instance has an absent output.  This proves the induction.

Therefore the target key \((Y,X,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop},y,x,\ast)\) becomes present.  It is checked before the next transition and its annotation is returned.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}extid\text{-}storage\text{-}invariant}\) identifies that annotation, simultaneously for every model in the exact fiber, with
\[
k^M_{(Y,X,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop})}(y\mid x,\ast)
=P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
=\psi_{x,y}(M). \tag{3}
\]
The stored expression belongs to \(\mathcal A(q_S,q_0)\) by Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}arithmetic\text{-}language}\), \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\), and \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}ordinary\text{-}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}completeness}\) supplies such a finite ordinary derivation whenever the target cell is identifiable from the full observational law, proving the first reduction.

Suppose next that \(\mathcal G\) entails \(S\perp\!\!\!\perp V\).  The selected initialization provides
\[
(V,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{sel},v,\ast,\ast)
\quad\text{with annotation}\quad q_S(v)
\]
for every \(v\).  With \(A=V\) and \(B=C=\varnothing\), the graphical predicate for population storage in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}source\text{-}bridge\text{-}moves}\) is exactly \(V\perp\!\!\!\perp S\).  Hence saturation makes the corresponding population key present, unless it is already present from population initialization, and preserves its annotation.  The storage invariant then yields
\[
q_S(v)=P_M(V=v\mid S=1)=P_M(V=v), \tag{4}
\]
where conditioning is defined because \(P_M(S=1)>0\).  Thus the same full-population scalar keys are available with the claimed annotations.

Now let \(T_0=\varnothing\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}extid\text{-}state\text{-}machine}\), the unique trivial margin contributes no scalar key.  A margin-extension instance in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}source\text{-}bridge\text{-}moves}\) requires a nonempty population-margin input key, so no such instance is admissible.  To prove the asserted exact closure, inspect a transition according to its source tag.  Initially every present key is selected-tagged.  Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}graphical\text{-}fixing\text{-}moves}\) preserve the source tag.  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}source\text{-}bridge\text{-}moves}\) says that the only source-changing moves are margin extension and population storage.  The first is unavailable, while the second maps
\[
(A,B,C,\mathrm{sel},a,b,c)
\longmapsto
(A,B,C,\mathrm{pop},a,b,c) \tag{5}
\]
with unchanged annotation exactly when
\[
A\perp\!\!\!\perp S\mid B,C
\quad\text{in }G_{\overline B}. \tag{6}
\]
Induction on the number of transitions therefore shows that every selected key belongs to the closure of the selected initialization under selected probability and graphical-fixing moves, and every population key is either an output (5) satisfying (6) or is obtained from such outputs by population probability and graphical-fixing moves.  Conversely, any absent output in these two closures whose complete inputs are present remains enabled; finiteness and the same no-permanent-postponement argument used above show that saturation adds it before terminating.  Hence there are no fewer and no additional keys than the stated closure.  Setting \(B=C=\varnothing\) in (6) gives precisely unconditional selection independence.

Finally, suppose margin extension reconstructs every cell of the full population law.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}margin\text{-}extension}\) gives, at every reconstructed cell \((t,z)\),
\[
P_M(T_0=t,Z=z)
=P_M(Z=z\mid T_0=t,S=1)q_0(t), \tag{7}
\]
and Proposition \(\mathrm{prop{:}extid\text{-}storage\text{-}invariant}\) says that the margin-transition annotation is exactly the right-hand side of (7).  Taking \(Z=V\setminus T_0\), or composing the reconstructed population blocks by the probability moves, therefore makes every key
\[
(V,\varnothing,\varnothing,\mathrm{pop},v,\ast,\ast)
\]
present with annotation \(P_M(V=v)\).  The finite-derivation replay leading to (3) now applies verbatim.  In particular, under an adjustment criterion with adjustment block \(L\), the ordinary derivation returns the standard functional
\[
P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal X_L}P_M(Y=y\mid X=x,L=\ell)P_M(L=\ell), \tag{8}
\]
and under the corresponding front-door criterion with mediating block \(D\), it returns
\[
P_M(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
=\sum_{d\in\mathcal X_D}P_M(D=d\mid X=x)
  \sum_{x'\in\mathcal X_X}P_M(Y=y\mid D=d,X=x')P_M(X=x'). \tag{9}
\]
The graphical criteria are exactly the premises under which the usual do-calculus derivations of (8) and (9) are legal; Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}do\text{-}calculus\text{-}rules}\) validates each graphical equality, and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}probability\text{-}moves}\) realizes their finite sums, products, and positive divisions.  Thus these are particular instances of the already proved replay argument, not additional identification assumptions.

Apply (3) separately to every successful \((x,y)\in\mathcal I\); each such returned coordinate equals the corresponding coordinate of \(\boldsymbol\psi(M)\) throughout the exact fiber.  If every cell run succeeds, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}full\text{-}kernel\text{-}collection}\) forms \(\mathbf F\) and places those coordinates in the same fixed order as \(\boldsymbol\psi(M)\).  Therefore, on the exact fiber,
\[
\mathbf F(q_S,q_0)
=\bigl(F_{x,y}(q_S,q_0):(x,y)\in\mathcal I\bigr)^{\mathsf T}
=\bigl(\psi_{x,y}(M):(x,y)\in\mathcal I\bigr)^{\mathsf T}
=\boldsymbol\psi(M).
\]
When some cell run fails, that definition does not form \(\mathbf F\), but the coordinatewise equality just proved remains valid for every returned cell.  This proves all four reductions and the final coordinatewise collection claim.
