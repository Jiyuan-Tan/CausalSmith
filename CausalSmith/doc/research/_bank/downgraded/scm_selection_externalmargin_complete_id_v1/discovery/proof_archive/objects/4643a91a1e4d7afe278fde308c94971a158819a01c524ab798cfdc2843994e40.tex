Put \(G_V=\mathcal G[V]\). Semantic nonidentifiability means that the query is not constant on at least one fiber of the full observational-law map. The positive finite separating pair is supplied by Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}kivva\text{-}positive\text{-}ordinary\text{-}id\text{-}specialization}\), which gives substantive models \(N_1,N_2\) compatible with \(G_V\) and a table \(p\) satisfying
\[
 P_{N_1}(V=v)=P_{N_2}(V=v)=p(v)>0
 \quad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal X_V,
 \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \psi_{x,y}(N_1)\ne\psi_{x,y}(N_2).
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus the cited positive ordinary-identification result is used only for (1)--(2). By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}response\text{-}selection\text{-}admg\text{-}reduction}\), we may choose finite independent response representations of \(N_1,N_2\) preserving their complete factual and counterfactual laws. Delete zero-mass response labels; hence every remaining coordinate atom has positive mass.

We next construct the extensions rather than assuming their existence. Let
\[
 \mathcal H_S
 =\bigl\{g:\mathcal X_{\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}\bigr\}.
\]
This is finite by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}finite\text{-}state}\). Introduce an exogenous coordinate \(U_S\), independent of all substantive coordinates, with
\[
 P(U_S=g)
 =\prod_{r\in\mathcal X_{\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)}}
 \pi_S(1\mid r)^{g(r)}
 \{1-\pi_S(1\mid r)\}^{1-g(r)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The empty-parent case uses the unique empty-block cell. Repeated finite distributivity gives
\[
 \sum_{g\in\mathcal H_S}P(U_S=g)
 =\prod_r\{\pi_S(1\mid r)+1-\pi_S(1\mid r)\}=1.
\]
Every factor in (3) is strictly positive, so every atom of \(U_S\) has positive mass. Define
\[
 S=U_S\bigl(\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)\bigr).
 \tag{4}
\]
For every bidirected edge incident to \(S\), take its displayed shared exogenous coordinate to have one atom of mass one and make both incident structural equations ignore that coordinate. For every \(W\in V\), retain the substantive equation from \(N_k\); if \(S\) is a displayed parent of \(W\), extend the equation constantly in its \(S\)-argument. Thus every substantive equation also ignores the coordinates displayed by bidirected edges incident to \(S\).

We verify compatibility explicitly. Under the nonparametric ADMG convention in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}semi\text{-}markovian}\), a structural function may depend only on displayed directed parents and its allowed exogenous coordinates; it is not required to depend nontrivially on every displayed argument. Indeed, if \(f(d,u)\) is an allowed function of a subset of displayed arguments, then \(\widetilde f(d,e,u)=f(d,u)\) is a function on the full displayed argument space, constant in the unused coordinate \(e\). Such constant extension introduces no unauthorized directed or bidirected dependence. The equations just constructed therefore use only arguments permitted by \(\mathcal G\). Their exogenous coordinates are independent, and each shared coordinate is incident to at most its displayed pair. Finally, the directed part of \(\mathcal G\) is acyclic, so evaluating these equations in a topological order is recursive even when \(S\) is not terminal. Hence each \(M_k\) is recursive, semi-Markovian, and compatible with the full arbitrary graph \(\mathcal G\).

Because every substantive equation ignores \(S\), \(U_S\), and every \(S\)-incident shared coordinate, the factual vector \(V\) is exactly the factual vector generated by \(N_k\). The same observation holds after any intervention on a subset of \(V\). Therefore (1)--(2) imply
\[
 P_{M_k}(V=v)=p(v),
 \qquad
 \psi_{x,y}(M_k)=\psi_{x,y}(N_k),
 \quad k\in\{1,2\},
 \tag{5}
\]
and in particular
\[
 \psi_{x,y}(M_1)\ne\psi_{x,y}(M_2).
 \tag{6}
\]
This is the step that removes the former terminality and selection-unconfoundedness restrictions.

Equation (3) also makes \(U_S\) independent of \(V\). For a fixed parent cell \(r\), summing (3) over all response maps with \(g(r)=1\) gives
\[
 P\{U_S(r)=1\}
 =\pi_S(1\mid r)
 \prod_{r'\ne r}\{\pi_S(1\mid r')+1-\pi_S(1\mid r')\}
 =\pi_S(1\mid r).
 \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, for every substantive cell \(v\), (4), (5), and (7) give
\[
 P_{M_k}(V=v,S=1)
 =\pi_S(1\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)})p(v).
 \tag{8}
\]
Let
\[
 Z_S=\sum_{v'}\pi_S(1\mid v'_{\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)})p(v').
\]
All summands are strictly positive, and there are finitely many cells, so \(0<Z_S<\infty\). Summing (8) yields \(P_{M_k}(S=1)=Z_S\). Division is therefore licensed, and
\[
 P_{M_k}(V=v\mid S=1)
 =\frac{\pi_S(1\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)})p(v)}{Z_S}
 =q_S(v).
 \tag{9}
\]
The right-hand side is common to the two models, sums to one, and is strictly positive at every cell. Marginalizing (5) gives, for every \(t\in\mathcal X_{T_0}\),
\[
 P_{M_k}(T_0=t)
 =\sum_{v:v_{T_0}=t}p(v)
 =q_0(t).
 \tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) and Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}data\text{-}map}\) show
\[
 \operatorname{Obs}_{T_0}(M_1)
 =\operatorname{Obs}_{T_0}(M_2)
 =(q_S,q_0).
 \tag{11}
\]

Finiteness and cellwise strict positivity imply \(\min_vq_S(v)>0\). Fix \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon\leq\min_vq_S(v)\). The compatibility proved above, \(P_{M_k}(S=1)=Z_S>0\), and (9) put \(M_k\) in \(\mathfrak M_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G)\) by Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\). Equation (11) and Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\text{-}input\text{-}image}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}observed\text{-}fiber}\) then give
\[
 (q_S,q_0)\in\mathcal C_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0),
 \qquad
 M_1,M_2\in\mathfrak F_{\epsilon}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0).
 \tag{12}
\]

It remains to check the response-interior conclusion. The reduced representations of \(N_1,N_2\) have finitely many active coordinate atoms of positive mass. The selection coordinate in (3) has finitely many positive atoms, and every ignored \(S\)-incident coordinate has its single atom of mass one. Hence the union of the active coordinate-atom masses across both extensions is a finite nonempty set of positive real numbers. Its minimum
\[
 \eta
 =\min\{\text{active coordinate-atom masses in either extension}\}
 >0
 \tag{13}
\]
is a common witness-specific slack. Equations (6), (11), and (12) exhibit two members of one exact two-law fiber with different query values. Because these edge-silent models are members of, hence a subclass of, the full positive \(\mathcal G\)-compatible class, a functional valid on that superclass would have to take one value on their common input, contradicting (6). This monotonic superclass argument proves two-law nonidentifiability. It does not assert the invalid reverse implication that identification on the edge-silent subclass would establish identification on the full superclass.
