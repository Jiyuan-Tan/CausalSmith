Write
\[
 r_v=\pi_S(1\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_{\mathcal G}(S)}),
 \qquad p_v=u^{\mathsf T}c_v,
 \qquad A_0=\sum_v r_vp_v.
\]
The hypotheses give \(r_v>0\) and \(p_v>0\) for every \(v\), whence \(A_0>0\) and every displayed cell \(q_S(v)=r_vp_v/A_0\) is positive.  Also
\[
 \sum_v q_S(v)=A_0^{-1}\sum_vr_vp_v=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_tq_0(t)=\sum_vp_v=u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1=1.
\]
Thus \(q_S\) and \(q_0\) are probability tables, and \(q_S(v)\geq\epsilon\) by the stated choice of \(\epsilon\).

Because \(S\) is terminal, adjoining its structural equation does not change any substantive structural equation or the intervention response \(h_{x,y}\).  Because no bidirected edge is incident to \(S\), an independent exogenous selection coordinate is compatible with \(\mathcal G\).  A finite implementation of the kernel is obtained by ordering its finitely many distinct probabilities, partitioning \((0,1)\) at those values, and recording on each positive-length interval the deterministic map \(p\mapsto\mathbf 1\{z\leq\pi_S(1\mid p)\}\).  Removing zero-length intervals leaves finitely many positive response-atom masses and implements every kernel cell exactly.  Since \(\mathcal R^{V}\) also has finitely many strictly positive active atom masses, their joint finite representation \(\mathcal R\) has
\[
 \eta:=\min\{\text{all active atom masses in }\mathcal R\}>0.
\]
This verifies the response-interior requirement rather than assuming it.

Conditional on \(U_0=j\), selection noise is independent and has success probability \(r_v\) on the substantive cell \(v\).  Therefore the rows of the extended representation satisfy
\[
 a_v=r_vc_v,\qquad
 a_+=\sum_vr_vc_v,\qquad
 b_t=\sum_{v:v_{T_0}=t}c_v.
\]
The positive-hedge equalities now imply, for every \(v,t\),
\[
 \beta^{\mathsf T}a_v=r_v\beta^{\mathsf T}c_v=0,\qquad
 \beta^{\mathsf T}a_+=\sum_vr_v\beta^{\mathsf T}c_v=0,\qquad
 \beta^{\mathsf T}b_t=\sum_{v:v_{T_0}=t}\beta^{\mathsf T}c_v=0.
\]
Together with \(\beta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1=0\), these identities show that \(\beta\) is orthogonal to every generator in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}projective\text{-}row\text{-}space}\): indeed,
\[
 \beta^{\mathsf T}\bigl\{(u^{\mathsf T}a_+)a_v-(u^{\mathsf T}a_v)a_+\bigr\}
 =(u^{\mathsf T}a_+)\beta^{\mathsf T}a_v-(u^{\mathsf T}a_v)\beta^{\mathsf T}a_+=0.
\]
Hence \(\beta\in\Gamma_{(M,\mathcal R,U_0)}^{\perp}\).  The hypothesis \(\beta^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}\neq0\) then implies \(h_{x,y}\notin\Gamma_{(M,\mathcal R,U_0)}\).

The extended model has population law \(p_v\), selected joint mass \(r_vp_v\), selection normalizer \(A_0>0\), and margin \(q_0\).  Thus its observed pair is exactly the displayed \((q_S,q_0)\), and the preceding positivity and compatibility checks put the baseline in \(\mathfrak R_{\epsilon,\eta}^{\circ}(\mathcal G,T_0;q_S,q_0)\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}projective\text{-}nullspace\text{-}certificate}\), applied with any nonzero \(\tau\) satisfying its step-size bound, now produces \(M_1,M_2\) in the same exact fiber, with active atom masses at least \(\eta/2\), and gives
\[
 \psi_{x,y}(M_1)-\psi_{x,y}(M_2)
 =2\tau\beta^{\mathsf T}h_{x,y}\neq0.
\]
This proves the selection lift.
