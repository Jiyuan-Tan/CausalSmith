Let \(C\), \(D\), and \(R\) denote, respectively, the covariate block, binary treatment, and outcome in the cited Qiu et al. result \texttt{lem:qiu-external-ate-characterization}. Reindex those variables in the present notation by
\[
T_0=C,\qquad X=\{D\},\qquad Y=\{R\},\qquad V=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{D,R\}.
\]
The unions are disjoint by the roles of covariate, treatment, and outcome in that result. By \texttt{def:observed-data-map}, the present two-source observation on this boundary is
\[
\operatorname{Obs}_{T_0}(M)
=\bigl(P_M(V\mid S=1),P_M(T_0)\bigr)
=\bigl(P_M(C,D,R\mid S=1),P_M(C)\bigr)
=\bigl(q_S^M,q_0^M\bigr).
\]
These are exactly the two kinds of input in \texttt{lem:qiu-external-ate-characterization}. Thus, when \(T_0\) is the covariate block, Qiu et al.'s external-data problem is a specialization of the present input signature.

The named unconfoundedness and overlap restrictions in that result, written after the same reindexing, are
\[
\bigl(R(0),R(1)\bigr)\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}D\mid C,
\qquad
0<P_M(D=1\mid C=c)<1
\quad\text{for every }c\in\operatorname{supp}(P_M(C)).
\]
The support qualification makes the displayed conditional probabilities well defined. These are source-side restrictions, not consequences of the input equality above.

There is also a genuine overlap in the causal targets. On the finite subcase in which \(R\) is numeric and \(D\) is binary, the definitions of \(\psi_{x,y}\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}\), followed by the finite expectation identity, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}(M)
&=E_M[R\mid\operatorname{do}(D=1)]-E_M[R\mid\operatorname{do}(D=0)]\\
&=\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,P_M(R=r\mid\operatorname{do}(D=1))
  -\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,P_M(R=r\mid\operatorname{do}(D=0))\\
&=\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,\psi_{1,r}(M)
  -\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,\psi_{0,r}(M).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence the scalar target of the cited theorem is a computable linear contrast of the present finite full table \(\boldsymbol\psi(M)\). This proves the claimed nonempty special-case intersection at the level of input and target signatures.

The first non-subsumption direction follows both from the cited theorem's hypotheses and from its scalar conclusion. By \texttt{lem:qiu-external-ate-characterization}, its conclusion assumes unconfoundedness and treatment overlap and identifies only \(\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}\). In contrast, \texttt{def:model-class} permits an arbitrary finite-state recursive semi-Markovian model compatible with \(\mathcal G\), including ADMGs with latent treatment--outcome confounding, and \texttt{def:full-kernel-collection} asks separately for every coordinate \(\psi_{x,y}\). Moreover, the linear contrast above is not injective, even on strictly interior binary kernels. To see this within finite recursive SCMs, let \(C\) be constant, let \(U_D,U_S\) be independent fair binary variables, and let \(U_R\) be independent and uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,12\}\). In both models set \(D=U_D\) and \(S=U_S\). Define
\[
R_1=\mathbf 1\{U_R\le 3+6D\},
\qquad
R_2=\mathbf 1\{U_R\le 4+6D\}.
\]
Each construction is a finite recursive Markovian, hence semi-Markovian, SCM on the DAG \(D\to R\) with \(C\) and \(S\) isolated. All four selected \((D,R_j)\)-cells are positive; their minima are \(1/8\) and \(1/12\), respectively. Direct evaluation gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
 & P(R_j=1\mid\operatorname{do}(D=0))
 & P(R_j=1\mid\operatorname{do}(D=1))
 & \theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}\\ \hline
j=1 & 3/12 & 9/12 & 6/12\\
j=2 & 4/12 & 10/12 & 6/12.
\end{array}
\]
Thus both ATEs equal \(1/2\), while, for example, the \((D=0,R=1)\) interventional cells are \(1/4\) and \(1/3\). Consequently, identification of the scalar ATE cannot by itself decide the coordinates of \(\boldsymbol\psi\); a fortiori the cited scalar theorem is not a completeness theorem for the present arbitrary-cell ADMG decision problem.

For the reverse direction, \texttt{def:model-class} and its finite-state support requirement imply that every substantive law in the present model class has finite support. The cited Qiu et al. setup, by contrast, includes covariate--outcome laws having densities on Euclidean spaces and continuous probability-family examples. Such laws are outside the present finite-state class. In addition, Qiu et al.'s Condition 2 is a condition on an explicitly chosen triple of probability classes and quantifies over pairs of compatible laws in those classes. Compatibility with a graph \(\mathcal G\) alone supplies no such choice: changing the positive exogenous mass functions or structural kernels while leaving \(\mathcal G\) fixed changes the propensity, outcome, and selection laws without changing the graph. Therefore the present graph-only premises do not imply Qiu et al.'s probability-class condition, and the present finite-state result cannot subsume their continuous-law cases.

The two directions together prove that the common selected-law-plus-margin, scalar-ATE boundary is nonempty, while neither result subsumes the other.
