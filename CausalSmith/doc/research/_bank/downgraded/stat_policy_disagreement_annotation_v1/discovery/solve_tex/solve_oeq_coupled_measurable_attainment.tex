%%% FIELD coupled-theorem-statement
There is a jointly measurable implementation of \(\mathrm{def:implementation\text{-}handle}\) with the stipulated coordinate order, parent-only recursion, lexicographic child order, and separate testing of every closed child containing a shared-boundary point. For each fixed depth \(k\), its selected ambient boxes and feasible cells equal \(\mathcal D_j^\dagger\) and \(\mathcal C_j^\dagger\), respectively, for all \(j\le k\) with probability tending to one locally uniformly on every bounded nuisance fiber. Define the population prefix gaps \(\gamma_j(L)\) as in the proof and let \(j_n(L)\) be the largest certified depth for which the approximation envelope is smaller than all preceding positive prefix gaps. Then \(a_n(L)=\sup_{m\ge n}2^{-j_m(L)}\downarrow0\) and \(\lVert\widehat r_n-r^\dagger\rVert=O_P(a_n(L))\) locally uniformly. This conclusion includes disconnected, flat, tied, and dyadic-boundary minimizing sets. A jointly refined rectangle-constant Gaussian rule has risk error \(o(1)\) uniformly over every fixed bounded nuisance fiber. The final rate limitation is only a generic continuous-objective statement: on \([0,1]\), continuity and uniform objective-approximation control alone imply no spatial recovery modulus tending to zero uniformly over all continuous objectives, because for every \(\epsilon>0\), \(f_+(z)=\epsilon z\) and \(f_-(z)=\epsilon(1-z)\) have canonical minimizers zero and one while \(g(z)=\epsilon/2\) is within \(\epsilon/2\) uniformly of both. No model-specific impossibility over the Gaussian-risk family \(r\mapsto L(r)\) is asserted.
%%% FIELD coupled-theorem-proof
We first construct a uniformly accurate pilot objective.  Put \(c_{ax}=p_xe_{ax}\).  Every \(c_{ax}\) is strictly positive because \(p_x>0\) and \(e_{ax}>0\), and every admissible main-wave rate is at least \(\rho>0\).  Choose two distinct support points \(y_u\ne y_w\).  The identity
\[
\sigma^2_{ax}(q)=\sum_{\ell<m}q_{ax\ell}q_{axm}(y_\ell-y_m)^2
\]
and \(q^0_{ax\ell}\ge\eta\) give the uniform variance floor
\[
\underline\sigma^2:=\eta^2(y_u-y_w)^2
\le \sigma^2_{ax}(q^0),
\]
while \(|Y|\le C_Y\) gives \(\sigma^2_{ax}(q^0)\le4C_Y^2\).  Thus \(I_r\) and \(\Sigma(r)\) are well defined.  Since \(\operatorname{rank}(F)=d\), the vectors \(d_x\) span \(\mathbb R^d\); the fixed positive cell weights, the annotation floor, and the displayed variance bounds consequently put all \(\Sigma(r)\), \(r\in\mathcal R_B\), in one compact subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^d\).

From the fully labelled pilot form the empirical cell probabilities, first moments, and second moments, and clip the resulting cell variances to \([\underline\sigma^2/2,4C_Y^2]\).  Define
\[
b_n=\left\{\frac{\log(n+1)}{m_n}\right\}^{1/2}+n^{-1/2},
\qquad t_n=b_n^{1/2}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:pilot\text{-}rate}\) implies \(m_n\to\infty\), \(b_n\to0\), \(t_n\to0\), and \(b_n=o(t_n)\).  Each raw pilot moment is bounded.  Chebyshev's inequality at threshold proportional to \(\sqrt{\log(n+1)/m_n}\), a union bound over the fixed \(2K\) cells, and the positive cell probabilities show that on events \(E_n\) with
\[
\inf_{h\in\mathcal H,\ \lVert v\rVert\le C_v}
P_{q_n(h,v)}(E_n)\longrightarrow1,
\]
the clipped estimates obey
\[
\max_{a,x}|\widehat\sigma^2_{ax}-\sigma^2_{ax}(q^0)|\le C_0b_n.
\]
The \(n^{-1/2}\) term covers the uniform difference between the pilot law \(q_n(h,v)\) and \(q^0\); this difference is bounded because \(s(h,v)\) ranges over a compact set.  The same argument applies to the pilot cell means.

Let \(\widehat\Sigma_n(r)\) be \(\Sigma(r)\) with these clipped variances, and let \(\widehat L_n(r)\) be the corresponding Gaussian minimax value.  The explicit covariance formula and \(r_{ax}\ge\rho\) imply
\[
\sup_{r\in\mathcal R_B}
\lVert\widehat\Sigma_n(r)-\Sigma(r)\rVert\le C_1b_n
\quad\text{on }E_n.
\]
For covariance matrices in the compact positive-definite set just identified, their centered Gaussian densities and their first covariance derivatives are dominated by one integrable Gaussian envelope.  Integrating the line segment between two covariance matrices therefore gives
\[
\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}
\operatorname{TV}\{N(h,S),N(h,S')\}
\le C_2\lVert S-S'\rVert.
\]
The regret loss is bounded by
\(R_H=2\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}\lVert h\rVert_\infty<\infty\).  Applying the last display to every rule and state, and then taking the supremum and infimum that define the game, proves
\[
\sup_{r\in\mathcal R_B}|\widehat L_n(r)-L(r)|
\le R_HC_2C_1b_n.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the plug-in objective is uniformly accurate locally uniformly on each bounded nuisance fiber.

Set \(k_n=\lfloor\log_2\log(n+2)\rfloor\).  At every recursion step through depth \(k_n\), impose the parent-only rule in \(\mathrm{def:implementation\text{-}handle}\).  For each nonempty feasible part of a closed child, approximate the minimum of \(\widehat L_n\) with deterministic error at most \(b_n\).  On represented instances, \(\mathrm{thm:certified\text{-}computation}\), with the child's rational box inequalities appended, supplies terminating outward lower and upper values of this accuracy.  For a fixed nonrepresented mathematical instance, the same theoretical map is obtained from deterministic refining state, observation, and feasible-design nets: compactness and the density bound above make the finite approximations converge uniformly on the compact covariance family.  Every finite approximation consists of Gaussian rectangle evaluations, finite sums, and finite linear programs; choosing the lexicographically first certified solution makes it Borel measurable.  Therefore the pilot moments, every approximate cell value, every finite comparison, and the final selection are jointly measurable.

The map \(P_B\) is continuous, equals the identity on \(\mathcal R_B\), and maps the full annotation box onto \(\mathcal R_B\).  Thus projections of successively finer full-box nets are dense in \(\mathcal R_B\) and supply the stipulated feasible design nets.  For each of the finitely many nonempty closed feasible cells that can be selected at sample size \(n\), choose its point by successively minimizing the ordered coordinates over that compact cell.  This fixed finite lookup is measurable in the selected cell index and defines the final feasible output \(\widehat r_n\).

Let \(\widehat v_{n,j,b}\) denote the approximate value of feasible child \(b\) of the currently selected depth-\((j-1)\) parent, and let \(v_{j,b}\) denote its population value when that parent is \(\mathcal D_{j-1}^\dagger\).  By (1) and the numerical tolerance, there is a deterministic \(\alpha_n=C_3b_n=o(t_n)\) such that, on \(E_n\),
\[
|\widehat v_{n,j,b}-v_{j,b}|\le\alpha_n
\tag{2}
\]
simultaneously for every child tested through depth \(k_n\).  Retain exactly the siblings satisfying
\[
\widehat v_{n,j,b}
\le\min_c\widehat v_{n,j,c}+t_n,
\tag{3}
\]
and select the lexicographically first retained child.  The sibling attaining the finite empirical minimum is retained, so the empirical cells are nonempty; parent recursion only inside the selected parent makes them nested.

For the feasible children of the population parent \(\mathcal D_{j-1}^\dagger\), define
\[
E_j=\{b:v_{j,b}=V^*\},
\qquad
\gamma_j(L)=
\begin{cases}
\min_{b\notin E_j}(v_{j,b}-V^*),&E_j\text{ is not the set of all feasible children},\\
+\infty,&E_j\text{ is the set of all feasible children}.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
At least one child is in \(E_j\), because the closed children cover the selected population cell and that cell contains a minimizer.  There are finitely many children, their feasible parts are compact, and an ineligible child's attained value is strictly larger than \(V^*\); hence \(\gamma_j(L)>0\).

Suppose the empirical parent at depth \(j-1\) is the population parent.  By (2), every child in \(E_j\) lies within \(2\alpha_n\) of the smallest empirical child value, and hence is retained whenever \(2\alpha_n<t_n\).  Conversely, an ineligible child lies at least \(\gamma_j(L)-2\alpha_n\) above an eligible child's empirical value, so it is rejected whenever
\[
t_n+2\alpha_n<\gamma_j(L).
\tag{5}
\]
Under these two inequalities, the retained set is exactly \(E_j\), and the lexicographically first retained child is precisely \(\mathcal D_j^\dagger\).  Induction proves stabilization of every fixed prefix.  If a minimizer lies on a shared boundary, every closed child containing it is separately feasible and belongs to \(E_j\); (3) retains all of them before applying the same lexicographic convention.  Flat, disconnected, or tied minimizing sets only enlarge \(E_j\), so they cause no change in the argument.  This proves the early-kill fixed-prefix property also at dyadic-boundary minima.

The outward cell bounds from \(\mathrm{thm:certified\text{-}computation}\) converge to each of the finitely many values in (4).  Consequently every positive prefix gap eventually has a positive certified lower bound.  Equivalently, define the deterministic analysis depth
\[
j_n(L)=\max\left\{j\le k_n:
t_n+2\alpha_n<\min_{1\le i\le j}\gamma_i(L)
\right\},
\tag{6}
\]
with the empty maximum equal to zero; replacing the gaps in (6) by their eventually valid outward lower certificates gives the announced certified version and the same eventual fixed prefixes.  Since every fixed finite minimum of the positive quantities \(\gamma_i(L)\) is positive, while \(k_n\to\infty\) and \(t_n+2\alpha_n\to0\), we have \(j_n(L)\to\infty\).  Hence
\[
a_n(L)=\sup_{m\ge n}2^{-j_m(L)}\downarrow0.
\]
On \(E_n\), the empirical and population paths agree through depth \(j_n(L)\).  The selected final feasible point \(\widehat r_n\) and \(r^\dagger\) both lie in the same depth-\(j_n(L)\) population cell, even if later empirical descendants choose a different branch within that cell.  The dyadic diameter bound therefore gives
\[
\lVert\widehat r_n-r^\dagger\rVert
\le\sqrt{2K}(1-\rho)2^{-j_n(L)}
\le\sqrt{2K}(1-\rho)a_n(L).
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(P(E_n)\to1\) locally uniformly, (7) is the asserted locally uniform \(O_P(a_n(L))\) result and, in particular, \(\widehat r_n\to r^\dagger\) in probability.

We now couple the design sieve to a measurable Gaussian rule.  At refinement level \(s\), use the finite state net, rational observation rectangles, tail truncation, and finite primal--dual game from \(\mathrm{thm:certified\text{-}computation}\), with total Gaussian-game error at most \(2^{-s}\).  Resolve every finite tie lexicographically.  Let \(J_s<\infty\) be the number of observation rectangles.  A deterministic diagonal choice \(s_n\uparrow\infty\) can be made so slowly that
\[
J_{s_n}/\sqrt n\longrightarrow0,
\]
while the state-mesh, Gaussian-tail, quadrature, rounding, covariance, and game errors all tend to zero.  This is a jointly measurable rectangle-constant rule because it uses only finitely many measurable pilot quantities and finite lexicographic comparisons at each \(n\).

For completeness, consider the conditional AIPW contrast summands on a fixed bounded nuisance fiber.  If \(\beta_n=\beta(q_n(h,v))\), \(b=\beta_n-\widehat\beta\), and
\[
H_{i,ax}=\frac{\mathbf1\{X_i=x,A_i=a,R_i=1\}}
{p_xe_{ax}\widehat r_{n,ax}}
(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-\widehat\beta_{ax}),
\]
then annotation ignorability gives \(E(H_i\mid\mathscr P_n)=b\).  The summands
\(A_{\mathrm{map}}(H_i-b)\) are conditionally centered and uniformly bounded because \(|Y|\le C_Y\) and all denominators have fixed positive floors.  Their covariance is
\[
S_n=A_{\mathrm{map}}\left[
\operatorname{diag}_{a,x}\left\{
\frac{\sigma^2_{n,ax}+b_{ax}^2}
{p_xe_{ax}\widehat r_{n,ax}}
\right\}-bb^\top\right]A_{\mathrm{map}}^\top.
\tag{8}
\]
The extra matrix
\(\operatorname{diag}\{b_{ax}^2/(p_xe_{ax}\widehat r_{n,ax})\}-bb^\top\)
is a covariance matrix and is positive semidefinite.  The pilot moment bounds, the affine local chart, and (7) imply
\[
\lVert S_n-\Sigma(r^\dagger)\rVert=o_P(1)
\tag{9}
\]
locally uniformly.  The variance floor and menu rank give a common positive lower eigenvalue for \(S_n\) with probability tending to one.  After normalization by \(S_n^{-1/2}\), the sum of the third absolute moments of the \(n\) scaled summands is \(O(n^{-1/2})\).  Thus \(\mathrm{lem:bentkus\text{-}convex\text{-}set}\) bounds the conditional probability error of each rectangle by \(O(n^{-1/2})\), uniformly on the bounded fiber.  Summing over the \(J_{s_n}\) rectangles, multiplying by the bounded regret, and adding the deterministic errors gives
\[
\sup_{h\in\mathcal H,\ \lVert v\rVert\le C_v}
|\operatorname{Risk}_{n}(h,v)-\operatorname{Risk}_{G,n}(h)|=o_P(1).
\]
Boundedness converts this to an \(o(1)\) unconditional risk error.  This proves the coupled rule assertion.

Finally, only a generic limitation follows from objective-approximation control.  On \([0,1]\), for any \(\epsilon>0\), let
\[
f_+(z)=\epsilon z,\qquad f_-(z)=\epsilon(1-z),
\qquad g(z)=\epsilon/2.
\]
The continuous objectives \(f_+\) and \(f_-\) have unique, hence canonical, minimizers zero and one, respectively, while
\[
\lVert g-f_+\rVert_\infty
=\lVert g-f_-\rVert_\infty=\epsilon/2.
\]
Any selector whose only information is the common approximation \(g\) returns the same point \(z_g\) in the two cases, and
\[
\max\{|z_g-0|,|z_g-1|\}\ge\frac12.
\]
Since \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) arbitrarily, no spatial recovery modulus tending to zero can hold uniformly over the class of all continuous objectives using continuity and uniform objective error alone.  This witness does not embed \(f_+\) and \(f_-\) into the present Gaussian-risk family, so it proves no model-specific impossibility for \(r\mapsto L(r)\), exactly as stated in the narrowed claim.
