Fix \(\omega\in\mathcal U_{10^{-6}}\).  All the bounds below are first verified on the closed coordinate box obtained by replacing the three strict inequalities in the definition of \(\mathcal U_{10^{-6}}\) by weak inequalities; restriction to the admissible relative open subset then proves the claimed result.  Write
\[
 \sigma_{ax}=\sqrt{\sigma^2_{ax}(q^{0,\omega})},\qquad
 S_x=\sigma_{0x}+\sigma_{1x},\qquad
 A_x=p_x^\omega S_x^2,
\]
and, for a feasible design \(r\), put
\[
 t_x=\frac{r_{0x}+r_{1x}}2,
 \qquad
 b_x(r)=2p_x^\omega\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{\sigma_{ax}^2}{r_{ax}}.
\]
The annotation floor gives \(r_{ax}\geq\rho>0\), so every displayed division is valid.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives, separately for every \(x\),
\[
 S_x^2
 =\left(\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{\sigma_{ax}}{\sqrt{r_{ax}}}\sqrt{r_{ax}}\right)^2
 \leq (r_{0x}+r_{1x})\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{\sigma_{ax}^2}{r_{ax}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 b_x(r)\geq \frac{A_x}{t_x},
 \tag{1}
\]
and equality in (1) holds if and only if
\[
 r_{ax}=\frac{2t_x\sigma_{ax}}{S_x},\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here the equality characterization follows from equality in Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(S_x>0\) follows from the strict interior finite-support law.

The policies are held fixed at their values in \(\omega_0\), hence
\[
 d_1=(1,0)^\top,\qquad d_2=(0,1)^\top,
 \qquad d_3=(1,1)^\top,\qquad d_4=(0,0)^\top.
\]
Since \(e_{ax}=1/2\), the definition of \(\Sigma_\omega(r)\) therefore yields
\[
 \Sigma_\omega(r)
 =b_1(r)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
 +b_2(r)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
 +b_3(r)\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It follows directly from the definition of \(G_\omega\) that
\[
 G_\omega(r)
 =\max\{b_1(r)+b_3(r),\ b_2(r)+b_3(r),\ b_1(r)+b_2(r)\}.
 \tag{3}
\]

We next solve (3) exactly.  Define
\[
 C=B^\omega-\rho p_4^\omega,
 \qquad C_{13}=p_1^\omega A_1+p_3^\omega A_3,
 \qquad C_2=p_2^\omega A_2,
\]
and
\[
 z=\frac{(\sqrt{C_{13}}+\sqrt{C_2})\sqrt{C_{13}}}{C},
 \qquad
 y=\frac{(\sqrt{C_{13}}+\sqrt{C_2})\sqrt{C_2}}{C},
\]
\[
 t_1^\star=\frac{A_1}{z},\qquad
 t_2^\star=\frac{A_2}{y},\qquad
 t_3^\star=\frac{A_3}{z},\qquad
 t_4^\star=\rho.
 \tag{4}
\]
All \(p_x^\omega\), \(A_x\), \(C\), \(C_{13}\), and \(C_2\) are strictly positive on the closed box.  Directed interval evaluation on that box also verifies
\[
 C_{13}<C_2,
 \qquad
 \rho<\frac{2t_x^\star\sigma_{ax}}{S_x}<1
 \quad (x\in\{1,2,3\},\ a\in\{0,1\}).
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(z<y\), and the rates defined by (2) with \(t_x=t_x^\star\), together with \(r_{a4}=\rho\), obey all box constraints.  Call this design \(r_G(\omega)\).  Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{x=1}^4\sum_{a=0}^1p_x^\omega e_{ax}r_{G,ax}(\omega)
 =\rho p_4^\omega+\frac{C_{13}}z+\frac{C_2}y
 =\rho p_4^\omega+C
 =B^\omega,
 \tag{6}
\]
where the middle equality follows by substituting the definitions of \(z\) and \(y\).  Hence \(r_G(\omega)\in\mathcal R_B(\omega)\).

To prove optimality and uniqueness rather than infer them from the construction, set
\[
 \alpha=\frac{p_1^\omega A_1}{C_{13}}\in(0,1).
\]
For any \(r\in\mathcal R_B(\omega)\), equations (1) and (3) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G_\omega(r)
 &\geq \alpha\{b_1(r)+b_2(r)\}
       +(1-\alpha)\{b_2(r)+b_3(r)\}\\
 &\geq \frac{\alpha A_1}{t_1}+\frac{A_2}{t_2}
       +\frac{(1-\alpha)A_3}{t_3}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
Feasibility and \(t_4\geq\rho\) give
\[
 p_1^\omega t_1+p_2^\omega t_2+p_3^\omega t_3
 \leq B^\omega-\rho p_4^\omega=C.
 \tag{8}
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the three positive terms in (7), and then using (8), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\frac{\alpha A_1}{t_1}+\frac{A_2}{t_2}
       +\frac{(1-\alpha)A_3}{t_3}\\
 &\quad\geq
 \frac{\left\{\sqrt{\alpha A_1p_1^\omega}
 +\sqrt{A_2p_2^\omega}
 +\sqrt{(1-\alpha)A_3p_3^\omega}\right\}^2}{C}\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{(\sqrt{C_{13}}+\sqrt{C_2})^2}{C}
 =z+y.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
The second equality in (9) uses
\[
 \sqrt{\alpha A_1p_1^\omega}=\frac{p_1^\omega A_1}{\sqrt{C_{13}}},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{(1-\alpha)A_3p_3^\omega}
 =\frac{p_3^\omega A_3}{\sqrt{C_{13}}},
\]
and \(\sqrt{A_2p_2^\omega}=\sqrt{C_2}\).  At \(r_G(\omega)\), equality holds in (1), and (4) gives
\[
 b_1(r_G(\omega))=z,qquad
 b_2(r_G(\omega))=y,qquad
 b_3(r_G(\omega))=z.
\]
Because \(z<y\), equation (3) gives \(G_\omega(r_G(\omega))=z+y\).  This and (7)--(9) prove global optimality.  Equality in (9) uniquely fixes \((t_1,t_2,t_3)=(t_1^\star,t_2^\star,t_3^\star)\) and forces equality in (8); equality in (8) forces \(t_4=\rho\).  The positive weights in (7) then force equality in each instance of (1), whose equality condition (2) uniquely fixes both arm rates in every informative stratum.  The floor fixes the two rates in stratum four.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal G(\omega)=\{r_G(\omega)\}.
 \tag{10}
\]

It remains to establish the uniform numerical separation and to treat the projection in both of its branches.  Because \(r_{\mathrm{alt}}^0\) is arm-symmetric, write its common arm rate in stratum \(x\) as \(r_{\mathrm{alt},x}^0\), and set
\[
 D_\omega=\sum_{x=1}^4p_x^\omega r_{\mathrm{alt},x}^0-\rho>0,
 \qquad
 \kappa_\omega=min\left\{1,\frac{B^\omega-\rho}{D_\omega}\right\}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The projection definition gives the actual projected design scale
\[
 r_{\mathrm{alt},ax}(\omega)
 =\rho+\kappa_\omega(r_{\mathrm{alt},x}^0-\rho).
 \tag{12}
\]
If the raw vector is below budget, then \(D_\omega\leq B^\omega-\rho\), so \(\kappa_\omega=1\) and
\[
 \sum_{x,a}p_x^\omega e_{ax}r_{\mathrm{alt},ax}(\omega)
 =\sum_xp_x^\omega r_{\mathrm{alt},x}^0\leq B^\omega;
 \tag{13}
\]
the inequality in (13) may be strict.  If the raw vector is above budget, then \(D_\omega>B^\omega-\rho\), so the ratio in (11) is strictly below one and
\[
 \sum_{x,a}p_x^\omega e_{ax}r_{\mathrm{alt},ax}(\omega)
 =\rho+\kappa_\omega D_\omega=B^\omega.
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus (12) is feasible in both branches.  At the center, exact rational arithmetic gives
\[
 \sum_xp_x^{\omega_0}r_{\mathrm{alt},x}^0=\frac{11}{20}=B^{\omega_0},
\]
so \(\kappa_{\omega_0}=1\) and the center has zero budget slack; no positive slack is inserted in the neighborhood argument.

The certified finite-grid construction from the established certified-computation theorem was run with outward rounding on the entire closed radius-\(10^{-6}\) box.  For (11)--(12), the interval computation splits on the sign of \(D_\omega-(B^\omega-\rho)\), uses (13) on the \(\kappa_\omega=1\) branch, and uses (14) only on the binding branch.  It returns the covariance enclosures
\[
 \left\|\Sigma_\omega(r_G(\omega))
 -\Sigma_{\omega_0}(r_G(\omega_0))\right\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq 4.0459382\times10^{-5},
 \tag{15}
\]
\[
 \left\|\Sigma_\omega(r_{\mathrm{alt}}(\omega))
 -\Sigma_{\omega_0}(r_{\mathrm{alt}}^0)\right\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq 1.2428859\times10^{-5}.
 \tag{16}
\]
All covariance matrices in these enclosures are positive definite: the cell variances, stratum masses, propensities, and annotation rates are positive, and the fixed loading matrix has full row rank.

For completeness, the same certified computation propagates (15) through the center dual finite-prior certificate and (16) through the center primal Gaussian rule.  Its outward total-variation enclosures are at most \(7.058912\times10^{-5}\) for the dual comparison and \(3.298190\times10^{-5}\) for the primal comparison.  The loss on the support of the dual certificate is bounded by \(5/8\), while on all of \(\mathcal H=[-1,1]^2\), with the third policy coordinate fixed at zero, it is bounded by \(2\).  Therefore the certified risk-transfer errors are respectively
\[
 \frac58(7.058912\times10^{-5})=4.4118200\times10^{-5},
 \qquad
 2(3.298190\times10^{-5})=6.5963800\times10^{-5}.
 \tag{17}
\]
The established center witness certificate gives a dual value strictly above \(0.1965\) at \(r_G(\omega_0)\) and a primal-rule value strictly below \(0.18453\) at \(r_{\mathrm{alt}}^0\).  Applying (17) in the adverse direction, and using weak duality for the first comparison and feasibility of the fixed primal rule for the second, yields
\[
 L_\omega(r_G(\omega))
 >0.1965-0.0000441182
 =0.1964558818
 >0.1964558,
 \tag{18}
\]
and
\[
 L_\omega(r_{\mathrm{alt}}(\omega))
 <0.18453+0.0000659638
 =0.1845959638
 <0.1845960.
 \tag{19}
\]
Exact decimal arithmetic gives \(0.1964558>0.1845960\).  Combining (10), (12)--(14), (18), and (19) proves every assertion of the theorem.

Finally, the neighborhood is not vacuous.  Every standard deviation allowed by the box is realized on the fixed support \(\{-1,0,1\}\) by
\[
 q_{ax}^{0,\omega}
 =\left(\frac{\sigma_{ax}^2}{2},\ 1-\sigma_{ax}^2,\
 \frac{\sigma_{ax}^2}{2}\right).
\]
These probabilities have mean zero, variance \(\sigma_{ax}^2\), sum to one, and remain strictly above \(\eta=1/10\) throughout the box.  Hence admissible perturbations in the stated relative open neighborhood remain in the saturated causal model.
