\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The note's exact-rank, row-heteroskedastic model is now explicitly an event-transfer class, not the published Choi--Yuan class.  The eligible blocks aggregate exactly into one event-by-cell score, and a generic remainder event \(\mathcal R_n(u)\) yields a finite whole-curve error contribution \(P_n\{\mathcal R_n(u)^c\}+Cu\sqrt{\log(2p)}\).  Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato--Koike's 2022 singular-covariance-safe theorem sharpens the Gaussian component to log power five and gives the balanced-loading elbow \(\log(Km)=o(m^{1/5})\).  Feasible growing-curve coverage remains conditional on the displayed remainder-event and score-stability probabilities.  The published approximate-rank, i.i.d. homoskedastic selected-functional class and the event-transfer class are incomparable; their explicit balanced rank-one common subclass is nonempty, but the attained nonlinear-remainder frontier remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published low-rank MNAR inference treats one block, entry, or selected scalar functional.  Choi--Yuan's staggered Theorem 3.2 attaches six conditions to one chosen group, time, and subgroup partition; it does not provide a growing event-path remainder probability or the rowwise feasible-score rate needed here.  Generic high-dimensional approximation does not supply those panel-specific controls.

\paragraph{Niche.} The useful bridge is a primitive-cell event score with exact staircase covariance and a generic finite-error transfer that exposes the nonlinear remainder event and score discrepancy.  Class comparison must be made after pairing the published law array with its one selected functional.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note fixes eligible-unit weights, proves exact block aggregation and shared-cell covariance, concentrates oracle unit variances, and bounds covariance, scale, and correlation errors deterministically by the feasible-score discrepancy.  The 2022 CCKK theorem gives Gaussian error \(C\{L_n(K)^2\log^5((p\vee3)m)\}^{1/4}\); anti-concentration adds \(Cu\sqrt{\log(2p)}\) on \(\mathcal R_n(u)\).  The Choi--Yuan bound enters only through \(\mathcal A_n(C)\subseteq\mathcal R_n(C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K))\) and its unsimplified source-submodel union bound.  An explicit proposition proves class incomparability and nonemptiness of a balanced rank-one common subclass.

\section{Related work}

Choi and Yuan, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}, allow \(M^{\mathrm{pub}}=M^\star+M^R\) with a globally incoherent low-rank signal and i.i.d. homoskedastic sub-Gaussian noise.  Their published staggered Theorem 3.2 has six hypotheses for one selected \(\mathcal G,t_0,\{\mathcal G_\ell\}\), while Proposition A.4 supplies the one-block expansion used here.  Aggregation over growing eligible blocks yields only the displayed finite union bound.  Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, Kato, and Koike (2022), Theorem 2.1, doi:10.1214/22-AOS2193, supplies the singular-covariance-safe fourth-root approximation with log power five.  The separate 2023 nearly-optimal theorem is not used because its covariance nondegeneracy condition is unavailable for staircase scores.  Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2013Maxima}, Lemma 3.1, supplies singular-safe Gaussian covariance comparison, and \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2014Bands} supplies anti-concentration.  Choi, Kwon, and Liao, \cite{ChoiKwonLiao2024Completion}, cover fixed scalar functionals under additional conditions, not the frozen growing rowwise score discrepancy.  Ben-Michael and Feller, \cite{BenMichaelFeller2026Discussion}, motivate the right-to-carry event curve; Callaway and Sant'Anna, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DID}, obtain simultaneous staggered paths under parallel trends rather than low-rank MNAR completion.  Yan and Wainwright, \cite{YanWainwright2024Entrywise}, and Xia, Yan, and Wainwright, \cite{XiaYanWainwright2024ACA}, provide entrywise or scalar inference rather than this conditional growing-path transfer.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-cells]\label{ass:independent-cells}
For every \(n\), \(\{e_{it}:(i,t)\in\mathcal Q_n^+\}\) are mutually independent under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent cell noise in structured matrix completion, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:centered-errors]\label{ass:centered-errors}
For every \(n\), \(E_{P_n}[e_{it}]=0\) for every \((i,t)\in\mathcal Q_n^+\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Centered idiosyncratic errors, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:heteroskedastic-subgaussian]\label{ass:heteroskedastic-subgaussian}
For every \(n\), \(\|e_{it}\|_{\psi_2}\le C_e\sigma_{it}\) for every \((i,t)\in\mathcal Q_n^+\), with the same \(C_e\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly sub-Gaussian heteroskedastic noise, \cite{ChoiKwonLiao2024Completion}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-bounds]\label{ass:variance-bounds}
For every \(n\), \(0<\underline\sigma\le\min_{(i,t)\in\mathcal Q_n^+}\sigma_{it}\le\max_{(i,t)\in\mathcal Q_n^+}\sigma_{it}\le\bar\sigma<\infty\), with the same \(\underline\sigma\) and \(\bar\sigma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nondegenerate bounded heteroskedastic variances, \cite{ChoiKwonLiao2024Completion}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:row-heteroskedasticity]\label{ass:row-heteroskedasticity}
For every \(n\), \(\sigma_{it}^2=\sigma_i^2\) for every \(i\le N\) and \(t\le T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unit-specific heteroskedasticity for a feasible low-rank group-average CLT, \cite{ChoiKwonLiao2024Completion}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-replication]\label{ass:variance-replication}
For every \(n\), \(\sqrt{\log(2N)/\min_{i\le N}|\{t:(i,t)\in\mathcal V_n\}|}\,\log^2(2K)\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{vrep}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The envelope is satisfied when every unit retains an untreated history that grows faster than \(\log(2N)\log^4(2K)\). Long administrative and policy panels with adoption bounded away from the first sample periods provide this replication while allowing the reported horizon set to grow.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-low-rank]\label{ass:exact-low-rank}
For every \(n\), \(\operatorname{rank}(M^0)=r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact low-rank untreated mean model, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:block-incoherence]\label{ass:block-incoherence}
For every \(n\), \(\max_{g\in E_k,k\in\mathcal K_n}\mu_{gk}\le\bar\mu\), with the same \(\bar\mu\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform Choi--Yuan block incoherence, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:signal-ratio]\label{ass:signal-ratio}
For every \(n\), \(\max_{g\in E_k,k\in\mathcal K_n}\frac{\bar\sigma\kappa_{gk}^2\mu_{gk}^{1/2}r^{1/2}\max\{N_{gk}\sqrt{\log N_{gk}},T_g\sqrt{\log T_g}\}}{\psi_{gk}\min\{\sqrt{N_{gk}},\sqrt{T_g}\}}\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{sig}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform version of the Choi--Yuan signal-to-noise ratio, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:spectral-size-ratio]\label{ass:spectral-size-ratio}
For every \(n\), \(\max_{g\in E_k,k\in\mathcal K_n}\frac{\kappa_{gk}^4\mu_{gk}^2r^2\max\{N_{gk}\log^3N_{gk},T_g\log^3T_g\}}{\min\{N_{gk}^2,T_g^2\}}\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{spec}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform version of the Choi--Yuan spectral dimension ratio, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:missing-block-ratio]\label{ass:missing-block-ratio}
For every \(n\), \(\max_{g\in E_k,k\in\mathcal K_n}\frac{n_g\kappa_{gk}^2\mu_{gk}r}{\min\{N_{gk},T_g\}}\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{block}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform version of the Choi--Yuan missing-block ratio, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-gram-balance]\label{ass:donor-gram-balance}
For every \(n\), \(c_A|J_{gk}|I_r\preceq A_{gk}\preceq C_A|J_{gk}|I_r\) for all \(g\in E_k\) and \(k\in\mathcal K_n\), with the same \(c_A,C_A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Choi--Yuan donor-submatrix Gram balance, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:preperiod-gram-balance]\label{ass:preperiod-gram-balance}
For every \(n\), \(c_B|S_g|I_r\preceq B_g\preceq C_B|S_g|I_r\) for all \(g\in E_k\) and \(k\in\mathcal K_n\), with the same \(c_B,C_B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Choi--Yuan pre-period Gram balance, \cite{ChoiYuan2024MNAR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}
For every \(n\), \(\min_{k\in\mathcal K_n}s_k>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Coordinatewise nondegeneracy for Gaussian maxima, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2013Maxima}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cell-leverage]\label{ass:cell-leverage}
For every \(n\), \(L_n(K)^2\log^5(((2K)\vee3)|\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}|)\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{lev}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sub-exponential high-dimensional maximum leverage condition, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKoike2023NearlyOptimal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-remainder-envelope]\label{ass:uniform-remainder-envelope}
For every \(n\), \(\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)\sqrt{\log(2K)}\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{rem}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a primitive uniformization of the published block ratios rather than a condition on an estimated remainder. It is satisfied by balanced strong-factor panels with fixed rank, bounded condition numbers, cohort blocks of vanishing panel share, and polynomially growing horizon sets, a configuration approximated by long staggered state-policy panels after restricting to eligible cohorts.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-influence]\label{thm:uniform-influence}
Fix \(n\).  For \(g\in E_k\), let \(b_{gk,q}\) denote the expression inside square brackets in the definition of \(c_{kq}\), let
\[
 \widehat\tau_{gk}=n_g^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_g}(Y_{i,g+k}-\widehat m^0_{i,g+k}),
 \qquad
 \tau_{gk}=n_g^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_g}\tau_{i,g+k},
\]
and define the exact block remainder \(r_{gk}\) by
\[
 \widehat\tau_{gk}-\tau_{gk}
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}b_{gk,q}e_q+r_{gk}.
\]
Then the eligible-unit aggregation obeys the exact eventwise identity
\[
 D_n^{-1}(\widehat\tau-\tau^{\mathrm{event}})
 =\left(\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}a_{kq}e_q/\sigma_q\right)_{k\in\mathcal K_n}+\rho_n,
 \qquad
 \rho_{n,k}=s_k^{-1}\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}r_{gk}.
\]
For \(C>0\), define
\[
 \mathcal A_n(C)=\bigcap_{k\in\mathcal K_n}\bigcap_{g\in E_k}
 \{|r_{gk}|\le C R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_n(C)\), the deterministic implication
\[
 \|\rho_n\|_\infty\le C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)
\]
holds.  In the i.i.d. homoskedastic source submodel in which every eligible block satisfies the hypotheses of Choi--Yuan Proposition A.4, there are constants \(C,C_0<\infty\), common to the eligible blocks for all sufficiently large \(n\), such that
\[
 P_n\{\mathcal A_n(C)^c\}
 \le C_0\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_n}\sum_{g\in E_k}
 \min\{N_{gk}^{-9},T_g^{-9}\}.
\]
No assertion that the last sum vanishes, and no uniform \(O_{P_n}(\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K))\) assertion over \(\mathfrak M^{\mathrm{CY}}\), is made.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each eligible pair \((g,k)\), the definition of \(r_{gk}\) is an
exact equality.  The estimator and the causal target obey
\[
 \widehat\tau_k=\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}\widehat\tau_{gk},
 \qquad
 \tau_k^{\mathrm{event}}
 =\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}\tau_{gk}.
\]
The second equality follows from \(N_k=\sum_{g\in E_k}n_g\) and the
definition of \(\tau_k^{\mathrm{event}}\).  Subtraction and substitution
of the block decompositions therefore give
\[
 \widehat\tau_k-\tau_k^{\mathrm{event}}
 =\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}
   \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}b_{gk,q}e_q
  +\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}r_{gk}.
\]
All sums are finite, so they may be interchanged.  By
\(\texttt{def:staircase-influence-map}\),
\[
 \sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}b_{gk,q}=c_{kq}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \widehat\tau_k-\tau_k^{\mathrm{event}}
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}e_q
  +\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}r_{gk}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}\) gives
\(s_k>0\).  Since \(a_{kq}=c_{kq}\sigma_q/s_k\), division by \(s_k\)
yields
\[
 s_k^{-1}(\widehat\tau_k-\tau_k^{\mathrm{event}})
 =\sum_q a_{kq}\frac{e_q}{\sigma_q}
  +s_k^{-1}\sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}r_{gk}.
\]
Comparison with the definition of \(\rho_{n,k}\) proves the coordinate
identity and the formula for \(\rho_{n,k}\); stacking over
\(k\in\mathcal K_n\) proves the vector identity.

On \(\mathcal A_n(C)\), positivity of the cohort shares, the triangle
inequality, and the definition of \(\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\rho_n\|_\infty
 &\le \max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}s_k^{-1}
       \sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}|r_{gk}|\\
 &\le C\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}s_k^{-1}
       \sum_{g\in E_k}\frac{n_g}{N_k}R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\\
 &=C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus, for the generic event
\(\mathcal R_n(u)=\{\|\rho_n\|_\infty\le u\}\),
\[
 \mathcal A_n(C)\subseteq
 \mathcal R_n\!\left(C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)\right),
 \qquad
 P_n\!\left\{\mathcal R_n\!\left(C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)\right)^c\right\}
 \le P_n\{\mathcal A_n(C)^c\}.
\]
This is precisely the Choi--Yuan specialization needed by the generic
remainder-event transfer in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-multiplier-band}\).

It remains to prove the finite source-submodel probability statement.
Apply \(\texttt{lem:choi-yuan-one-block-remainder}\) separately to each
eligible block.  Its notation maps as
\((N_o,T_o,\vartheta_o)=(N_{gk},T_g,n_g)\).  For \(i\in I_g\), the cited
proposition decomposes the imputation error into the donor-column term,
the treated-row pre-period term, and an entry remainder
\(\mathcal R^M_{gk,i}\).  Since
\[
 Y_{i,g+k}-m^0_{i,g+k}-\tau_{i,g+k}=e_{i,g+k},
\]
subtracting that expansion and averaging over \(I_g\) gives the stated
block expansion with
\[
 r_{gk}=-\frac1{n_g}\sum_{i\in I_g}\mathcal R^M_{gk,i}.
\]
Hence
\(\max_{i\in I_g}|\mathcal R^M_{gk,i}|\le
C R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\) implies
\(|r_{gk}|\le C R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\).  In the stated i.i.d.
homoskedastic source submodel, the uniform block ratios and common
constants make \(C\) and \(C_0\) common to all eligible blocks for all
sufficiently large \(n\).  Therefore
\[
 P_n\{|r_{gk}|>C R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\}
 \le C_0\min\{N_{gk}^{-9},T_g^{-9}\}.
\]
Boole's inequality over the finite collection of eligible pairs gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_n\{\mathcal A_n(C)^c\}
 &\le\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_n}\sum_{g\in E_k}
 P_n\{|r_{gk}|>C R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\}\\
 &\le C_0\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_n}\sum_{g\in E_k}
 \min\{N_{gk}^{-9},T_g^{-9}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is an unsimplified finite union bound.  Neither the source result nor
the event-transfer class makes its right-hand side converge to zero.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact Choi--Yuan specialization of the generic remainder-event transfer: algebra gives the event score and \(\mathcal A_n(C)\subseteq\mathcal R_n(C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K))\). \textit{Gap.} Proposition A.4 is one-block and i.i.d. homoskedastic; its honest growing-block consequence is the unsimplified finite union bound, not a class-uniform stochastic order. \textit{Consumer.} The generic headline substitutes the block event only when an analyst can make its displayed failure probability small.

\begin{lemma}[lem:staircase-covariance]\label{lem:staircase-covariance}
For all \(k,k'\in\mathcal K_n\), mutual independence and centeredness on \(\mathcal Q_n^+\) give the algebraic identity \[\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}\!\left(\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}e_q,\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{k'q}e_q\right)=\Omega_{kk'}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}}c_{kq}c_{k'q}\sigma_q^2.\] Terms outside \(\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}\) vanish by definition, and \(\Omega_{kk'}\ne0\) if and only if the displayed signed shared-cell sum is nonzero; individual products may cancel. Studentization is the identity \(\Sigma_n=D_n^{-1}\Omega_nD_n^{-1}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 U_k=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}e_q,
 \qquad k\in\mathcal K_n.
\]
All sums are finite.  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:centered-errors}\) gives
\(E_{P_n}[U_k]=0\).  Thus, for \(k,k'\in\mathcal K_n\), bilinearity of
covariance gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U_k,U_{k'})
 =\sum_{q,q'\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
 c_{kq}c_{k'q'}E_{P_n}[e_qe_{q'}].
\]
If \(q\ne q'\), assumption \(\texttt{ass:independent-cells}\) and
assumption \(\texttt{ass:centered-errors}\) imply
\[
 E_{P_n}[e_qe_{q'}]
 =E_{P_n}[e_q]E_{P_n}[e_{q'}]=0.
\]
For \(q=q'=(u,v)\), the definition of \(\sigma_{uv}^2\) gives
\(E_{P_n}[e_q^2]=\sigma_{uv}^2=\sigma_q^2\).  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U_k,U_{k'})
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}c_{k'q}\sigma_q^2
 =\Omega_{kk'},
\]
where the second equality is the definition of \(\Omega_n\).  By the
definition of \(\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}\), every term indexed by
\(q\notin\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}\) has
\(c_{kq}c_{k'q}=0\).  Hence
\[
 \Omega_{kk'}
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}}
 c_{kq}c_{k'q}\sigma_q^2.
\]
This equality proves the asserted characterization: \(\Omega_{kk'}\ne0\)
if and only if the displayed signed sum is nonzero.  In particular, the
existence of shared cells alone is insufficient because products with
different signs may cancel.  Finally,
\(\texttt{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}\) gives \(s_k>0\) for every
\(k\in\mathcal K_n\), so \(D_n\) is invertible.  The definition of
\(\Sigma_n\) then yields
\[
 \Sigma_n=D_n^{-1}\Omega_nD_n^{-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:two-horizon-shared-cell-witness]\label{prop:two-horizon-shared-cell-witness}
For \(\mathcal W^{(2)}\), the untreated mean has rank one, \(\mu_{2,0}=\mu_{2,1}=1\), \(A_{2,0}=A_{2,1}=4\), and \(B_2=1\). At each horizon, the four treated target cells have coefficient \(1/4\), the four donor target cells have coefficient \(-1/4\), and the four treated pre-period cells shared by both horizons have coefficient \(-1/4\). Therefore each marginal variance is \(12(1/4)^2=3/4\), each shared-cell covariance product is \((-1/4)(-1/4)=1/16\), the off-diagonal covariance is \(4/16=1/4\), and the correlation is \((1/4)/(3/4)=1/3\). This explicitly satisfies the noncancellation condition in \(\mathrm{lem:staircase\mbox{-}covariance}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In \(\mathcal W^{(2)}\), the definition of the witness gives
\[

\]
Both vectors are nonzero, so \(\operatorname{rank}(M^0)=1\).  For either
\(k\in\{0,1\}\), the eligible block has rows
\(I_2\cup J_{2,k}=\{1,\ldots,8\}\), columns
\(S_2\cup\{2+k\}=\{1,2+k\}\), and every entry equal to one.  A compact
singular value decomposition is therefore
\[
 U_{2,k}=\frac{\mathbf 1_8}{\sqrt 8},
 \qquad
 V_{2,k}=\frac{\mathbf 1_2}{\sqrt 2},
 \qquad
 \psi_{2,k}=\sqrt{8\cdot2}=4.
\]
Because \(r=1\), the definition of block incoherence gives
\[
 \mu_{2,k}
 =\max\!\left\{
 8\left\|U_{2,k}\right\|_{2,\infty}^2,
 2\left\|V_{2,k}\right\|_{2,\infty}^2
 \right\}
 =\max\{8(1/8),2(1/2)\}=1.
\]
Every loading and factor equals one.  Since \(|J_{2,k}|=4\) and
\(S_2=\{1\}\), the definitions of the Gram matrices yield, identifying
one-by-one matrices with their scalar entries,
\[
 A_{2,k}=\sum_{j=5}^{8}x_j^2=4,
 \qquad
 B_2=\sum_{s\in S_2}z_s^2=z_1^2=1.
\]

For both horizons, \(E_k=\{2\}\) and \(N_k=n_2=4\); hence the outer
cohort weight \(n_2/N_k\) in the definition of \(c_{kq}\) equals one.
Moreover, \(\bar x_2=1\).  Substituting
\(A_{2,k}^{-1}=1/4\), \(B_2^{-1}=1\), \(z_{2+k}=1\), and \(z_1=1\)
into the displayed coefficient formula gives, with \(t_k=2+k\),
\[
 c_{k,(u,v)}=
 \begin{cases}
  1/4,&u\in I_2,\ v=t_k,\\
 -1/4,&u\in J_{2,k},\ v=t_k,\\
 -1/4,&u\in I_2,\ v=1,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The three nonzero cases are disjoint, and each contains four cells.  Since
the witness sets every cell variance equal to one, the definition of
\(\Omega_n\) gives
\[
 \Omega_{kk}
 =4(1/4)^2+4(-1/4)^2+4(-1/4)^2
 =\frac34.
\]
The treated and donor target cells occur at calendar date \(2+k\), so they
are distinct between horizons zero and one.  The only cells having nonzero
coefficients at both horizons are the four pre-treatment cells \((u,1)\)
with \(u\in I_2\).  Each such cell contributes
\[
 c_{0,(u,1)}c_{1,(u,1)}=(-1/4)(-1/4)=\frac1{16}.
\]
Applying \(\texttt{lem:staircase-covariance}\), whose independence and
centering hypotheses hold for the witness noise, yields
\[
 \Omega_{01}=4\left(\frac1{16}\right)=\frac14.
\]
Finally, the definitions of \(s_k\), \(D_n\), and \(\Sigma_n\) give
\[
 \Sigma_{01}
 =\frac{\Omega_{01}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{00}\Omega_{11}}}
 =\frac{1/4}{3/4}=\frac13.
\]
Thus the shared-cell signed sum does not cancel.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:heteroskedastic-covariance]\label{thm:heteroskedastic-covariance}
Let \(h_{\min}=\min_{i\le N}h_i\), which is positive under \(\texttt{ass:variance-replication}\), and define the oracle residual variance
\[
 \widetilde\sigma_i^2=h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2.
\]
There is a constant \(C_1<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\) and \(\bar\sigma\), such that, for every \(x\ge0\),
\[
 \sup_{P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{CY}}}P_n\!\left\{
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|>
 C_1\left[\sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)+x}{h_{\min}}}
 +\frac{\log(2N)+x}{h_{\min}}\right]\right\}\le2e^{-x}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 =O_{P_n}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}}
 +\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}\right)
 =o_{P_n}((\log(2K))^{-2})
\]
uniformly over the finite slices.  For
\[
 d_{it}=\widehat m_{it}^{0,(-it)}-m^0_{it},
 \qquad
 d_i^2=h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}d_{it}^2,
\]
the feasible residual variance has the exact contamination bound
\[
 |\widehat\sigma_i^2-\widetilde\sigma_i^2|
 \le2\left(h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}d_i+d_i^2.
\]
Finally, set
\[
 \widehat b_{kq}=\widehat c_{kq}\widehat\sigma_u/s_k,
 \qquad
 r_n^{\mathrm{score}}=\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}
 \left\{\sum_{q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
 (\widehat b_{kq}-a_{kq})^2\right\}^{1/2},
\]
and \(\mathcal B_n(h)=\{r_n^{\mathrm{score}}\le h\}\).  On \(\mathcal B_n(h)\), with \(\delta_h=2h+h^2<1\),
\[
 \max_{k,k'\in\mathcal K_n}
 \frac{|\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}|}{s_ks_{k'}}\le\delta_h,
 \qquad
 \max_k\left|\frac{\widehat s_k}{s_k}-1\right|
 \le\frac{\delta_h}{1+\sqrt{1-\delta_h}},
\]
and
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_n-\Sigma_n\|_{\max}
 \le\frac{2\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}.
\]
These are deterministic consequences of the displayed score discrepancy.  No stochastic rate for \(r_n^{\mathrm{score}}\) is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(h_{\min}=\min_{i\le N}h_i\).  By the definitions of
\(\mathcal V_n\) and \(h_i\),
\[
 \min_{i\le N}|\{t:(i,t)\in\mathcal V_n\}|=h_{\min}.
\]
The real-valued inequality in \(\texttt{ass:variance-replication}\) has
this quantity in its denominator; hence that assumption entails
\(h_{\min}>0\).  Thus every oracle average in the statement is well
defined.

Fix \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{CY}}\).  By
\(\texttt{ass:row-heteroskedasticity}\), for every \(t\in H_i\),
\[
 E_{P_n}[e_{it}^2]=\sigma_{it}^2=\sigma_i^2.
\]
We first establish the claimed concentration uniformly over the finite
slices.  The definition of the sub-Gaussian norm and
\(\texttt{ass:heteroskedastic-subgaussian}\) imply that there is a
constant \(C_2<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\), such that
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp\!\left\{\frac{e_{it}^2}{C_2\sigma_i^2}\right\}\le2.
\]
For every integer \(m\ge1\), the elementary inequality
\(y^m\le m!e^y\), applied with
\(y=e_{it}^2/(C_2\sigma_i^2)\), gives
\[
 E_{P_n}|e_{it}|^{2m}
 \le 2m!(C_2\sigma_i^2)^m.
\]
Set \(U_{it}=e_{it}^2-\sigma_i^2\).  Then
\(E_{P_n}[U_{it}]=0\).  The preceding moment inequality,
\((x+y)^m\le2^{m-1}(x^m+y^m)\) for \(x,y\ge0\), and
\(\sigma_i\le\bar\sigma\) from \(\texttt{ass:variance-bounds}\) imply
that a constant \(C_3<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\), satisfies
\[
 E_{P_n}|U_{it}|^m\le m!(C_3\bar\sigma^2)^m,
 \qquad m\ge2.
\]
Let \(B=C_3\bar\sigma^2\).  Expanding the exponential, using
\(E_{P_n}[U_{it}]=0\), and bounding every remaining signed moment by its
absolute moment shows that, whenever \(|\lambda|B\le1/2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{P_n}e^{\lambda U_{it}}
 &\le 1+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}(|\lambda|B)^m\\
 &\le 1+2\lambda^2B^2
 \le \exp(2\lambda^2B^2).
 \end{aligned}
\]

For a fixed unit \(i\), assumption \(\texttt{ass:independent-cells}\)
makes \(\{U_{it}:t\in H_i\}\) mutually independent.  Multiplication of
the preceding moment-generating-function bounds therefore gives
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp\!\left\{\lambda\sum_{t\in H_i}U_{it}\right\}
 \le \exp(2h_i\lambda^2B^2),
 \qquad |\lambda|B\le1/2.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, first with positive \(\lambda\) and then
with negative \(\lambda\), and the choice of \(|\lambda|\) proportional
to
\[
 \min\!\left\{\frac{\sqrt y}{B\sqrt{h_i}},\frac1B\right\}
\]
show that there is a constant \(C_4<\infty\), depending only on
\(C_e\) and \(\bar\sigma\), for which, for every \(y\ge0\),
\[
 P_n\!\left\{
 \left|h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}U_{it}\right|
 >C_4\left(\sqrt{\frac y{h_i}}+\frac y{h_i}\right)
 \right\}\le2e^{-y}.
\]
Because
\(h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}U_{it}=\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2\),
we may take \(y=\log(2N)+x\), use \(h_i\ge h_{\min}\), and apply the
union bound over \(i\le N\).  This yields
\[
 P_n\!\left\{
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 >C_4\left[
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)+x}{h_{\min}}}
 +\frac{\log(2N)+x}{h_{\min}}
 \right]\right\}
 \le 2N e^{-\log(2N)-x}=e^{-x}\le2e^{-x}.
\]
The constants used above do not depend on \(P_n\), \(i\), or \(n\).
Taking the supremum over \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{CY}}\) proves the
first assertion with \(C_1=C_4\).

For completeness, taking \(x=\log(2/\delta)\) in the preceding display,
for arbitrary fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\), proves uniformly over the finite
slices that
\[
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 =O_{P_n}\!\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}}
 +\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}
 \right).
\]
Indeed, \(\log(2N)\ge\log2\), so the fixed additive term
\(\log(2/\delta)\) can be absorbed into a \(\delta\)-dependent
multiplicative constant.  Put
\[
 v_n=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:variance-replication}\) states
\[
 v_n\log^2(2K)\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{vrep}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
It also implies \(v_n\to0\), since \(\log(2K)\ge\log2\).  Therefore
\[
 (v_n+v_n^2)\log^2(2K)
 \le \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{vrep}}(1+v_n)longrightarrow0.
\]
Combining this deterministic relation with the uniform stochastic order
proves
\[
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 =o_{P_n}((\log(2K))^{-2})
\]
uniformly over the finite slices.

We next isolate imputation contamination.  For \(t\in H_i\), the cell is
untreated by definition of \(H_i\).  The observation equation consequently
reduces to \(Y_{it}=m^0_{it}+e_{it}\).  With
\(d_{it}=\widehat m_{it}^{0,(-it)}-m^0_{it}\), the definition of the
leave-one-cell-out residual gives
\[
 \widehat e_{it}=Y_{it}-\widehat m_{it}^{0,(-it)}=e_{it}-d_{it}.
\]
Expanding the square exactly yields
\[
 \widehat\sigma_i^2-\widetilde\sigma_i^2
 =-\frac2{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}d_{it}
 +\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}d_{it}^2.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz and the definition of \(d_i\),
\[
 \left|\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}d_{it}\right|
 \le
 \left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}d_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}
 =\left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}d_i.
\]
The triangle inequality proves
\[
 |\widehat\sigma_i^2-\widetilde\sigma_i^2|
 \le2\left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}d_i+d_i^2.
\]
No frozen assumption supplies a stochastic bound for \(d_i\), so the
argument makes no stronger feasible-variance assertion.

It remains to prove the deterministic score-stability conclusions.  By
\(\texttt{ass:row-heteroskedasticity}\), if \(q=(u,v)\), then
\(\sigma_{uv}=\sigma_u\).  The definitions of \(a_{kq}\), \(s_k\), and
\(\Omega_{kk}\), together with \(s_k>0\) from
\(\texttt{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}\), imply
\[
 \|a_k\|_2^2
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}a_{kq}^2
 =s_k^{-2}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}^2\sigma_u^2
 =s_k^{-2}\Omega_{kk}=1.
\]
By the definitions of \(\widehat b_{kq}\), \(\widehat\Omega_n\), and
\(\Omega_n\),
\[
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}}
 =\sum_q\widehat b_{kq}\widehat b_{k'q},
 \qquad
 \frac{\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}}
 =\sum_qa_{kq}a_{k'q}.
\]
Set \(u_k=\widehat b_k-a_k\).  Subtracting the two inner products gives
\[
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}}
 =\langle u_k,a_{k'}\rangle
 +\langle a_k,u_{k'}\rangle
 +\langle u_k,u_{k'}\rangle.
\]
On \(\mathcal B_n(h)\), one has \(\|u_j\|_2\le h\) for every
\(j\in\mathcal K_n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\|a_j\|_2=1\) therefore
give
\[
 \max_{k,k'\in\mathcal K_n}
 \frac{|\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}|}{s_ks_{k'}}
 \le h+h+h^2=\delta_h.
\]

Set \(t_k=\widehat s_k/s_k\).  The diagonal case of the last bound gives
\[
 |t_k^2-1|
 =\left|\frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk}-\Omega_{kk}}{s_k^2}\right|
 \le\delta_h.
\]
If \(\delta_h<1\), then
\(t_k\ge\sqrt{1-\delta_h}>0\), so every feasible standard error is
positive and \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) is well defined.  Moreover,
\[
 |t_k-1|
 =\frac{|t_k^2-1|}{t_k+1}
 \le\frac{\delta_h}{1+\sqrt{1-\delta_h}}.
\]
For any \(k,k'\), the bounds on \(t_k^2\) and \(t_{k'}^2\) imply
\[
 1-\delta_h\le t_kt_{k'}\le1+\delta_h.
\]
Also \(|\Sigma_{kk'}|\le1\) by Cauchy--Schwarz because \(\Sigma_n\) is
the Gram matrix of the unit vectors \(a_k\).  If
\[
 E_{kk'}=
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}},
\]
then \(|E_{kk'}|\le\delta_h\) and
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{kk'}
 =\frac{\Sigma_{kk'}+E_{kk'}}{t_kt_{k'}}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\Sigma_{kk'}-\Sigma_{kk'}|
 &\le \frac{|E_{kk'}|}{t_kt_{k'}}
 +|\Sigma_{kk'}|\frac{|1-t_kt_{k'}|}{t_kt_{k'}}\\
 &\le\frac{\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}
 +\frac{\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}
 =\frac{2\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(k,k'\in\mathcal K_n\) proves the final claim.
Every assertion after the definition of \(\mathcal B_n(h)\) is therefore
a deterministic implication of the score discrepancy, as required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem separates the variance component that replication identifies from the imputation and loading components that the frozen assumptions do not control. \textit{Gap.} Available feasible low-rank central limit theorems concern fixed functionals and do not establish the growing normalized rowwise \(\ell_2\) score rate required by this plug-in covariance. \textit{Consumer.} The deterministic map \(r_n^{\mathrm{score}}\mapsto(\widehat\Omega,\widehat s,\widehat\Sigma)\) is the feasible covariance input to the conditional multiplier comparison.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-multiplier-band]\label{thm:uniform-multiplier-band}
Let \(m=|\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}|\ge3\), \(p=2K\ge2\),
\(\epsilon_q=e_q/\sigma_q\), and
\[
 S_{n,k}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}a_{kq}\epsilon_q.
\]
After arbitrarily ordering the cells, define the sign-expanded vectors
\[
 X_q=\sqrt m\,(a_{0q}\epsilon_q,\ldots,
 a_{K-1,q}\epsilon_q,-a_{0q}\epsilon_q,\ldots,
 -a_{K-1,q}\epsilon_q)\in\mathbb R^p.
\]
Then \(m^{-1/2}\sum_qX_q\) has maximum
\(\max_k|S_{n,k}|\).  For every coordinate \(j\le p\),
\[
 \frac1m\sum_qE_{P_n}X_{qj}^2=1,
 \qquad
 \frac1m\sum_qE_{P_n}X_{qj}^4
 \le C mL_n(K)^2,
 \qquad
 \|X_{qj}\|_{\psi_1}\le C\sqrt mL_n(K).
\]
There is a constant \(C_1<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\), such
that, with
\[
 \mathfrak g_n=C_1\{L_n(K)^2\log^5((p\vee3)m)\}^{1/4},
 \qquad M_n^Z=\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}|Z_{n,k}|,
\]
one has
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P_n\{\max_k|S_{n,k}|\le z\}-P\{M_n^Z\le z\}\right|
 \le\mathfrak g_n.
\]
The \(p\)-dimensional Gaussian covariance in this comparison is the
sign expansion induced by \(\Sigma_n\), and may be singular.

For \(u\ge0\), define the exact remainder event
\[
 \mathcal R_n(u)=\{\|\rho_n\|_\infty\le u\}.
\]
For \(h\ge0\), let \(\mathcal B_n(h)\) and
\(\delta_h=2h+h^2\) be as in
\(\texttt{thm:heteroskedastic-covariance}\), set
\(\mathfrak c_n(0)=0\), and, when \(h>0\), set
\[
 \mathfrak c_n(h)=C_3\delta_h^{1/3}
 \{1\vee\log(p/\delta_h)\}^{2/3}.
\]
There are constants \(C_2,C_3<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\),
such that whenever \(0\le\delta_h\le1/2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|P_n\!\left\{
 \max_k\frac{|\widehat\tau_k-\tau_k^{\mathrm{event}}|}{\widehat s_k}
 \le z\right\}-P\{M_n^Z\le z\}\right|\\
 &\quad\le \mathfrak g_n+P_n\{\mathcal R_n(u)^c\}
 +P_n\{\mathcal B_n(h)^c\}
 +C_2\{u\sqrt{\log(2p)}+\delta_h\log(2p)\}
 +\mathfrak c_n(h).
 \end{aligned}
\]
On \(\mathcal B_n(h)\), conditionally on \(Y\), \(W_n^\xi\) is
exactly the maximum absolute coordinate of
\(N(0,\widehat\Sigma_n)\), and
\[
 \sup_z|P(W_n^\xi\le z\mid Y)-P(M_n^Z\le z)|
 \le\mathfrak c_n(h).
\]
If \(\mathfrak c_n(h)<\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\), then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left|P_n\{\tau_k^{\mathrm{event}}\in\mathcal C_{n,k}
 \text{ for every }k\in\mathcal K_n\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\\
 &\quad\le1\wedge\Big[\mathfrak g_n
 +P_n\{\mathcal R_n(u)^c\}+2P_n\{\mathcal B_n(h)^c\}\\
 &\hspace{35mm}+C_2\{u\sqrt{\log(2p)}+\delta_h\log(2p)\}
 +2\mathfrak c_n(h)\Big].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, along any complete array in the baseline event-transfer
class, asymptotic coverage follows if deterministic \(u_n,h_n\downarrow0\)
satisfy
\[
 P_n\{\mathcal R_n(u_n)^c\}\to0,
 \quad P_n\{\mathcal B_n(h_n)^c\}\to0,
 \quad u_n\sqrt{\log(2p)}\to0,
 \quad h_n\log^2(2p)\to0.
\]
For the Choi--Yuan specialization,
\(\texttt{thm:uniform-influence}\) permits
\(u_n=C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)\) and replaces
\(P_n\{\mathcal R_n(u_n)^c\}\) by the larger displayed probability
\(P_n\{\mathcal A_n(C)^c\}\).  Thus, along an array in the band
event-transfer class, coverage tends to \(1-\alpha\) only if this block
failure probability and the score failure probability also vanish at the
displayed rates.  Finally, under balanced loadings
\(L_n(K)\asymp m^{-1/2}\), the Gaussian-approximation term vanishes under
the elbow condition
\[
 \log(Km)=o(m^{1/5}).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}\) permits
division by every \(s_k\).  By the definitions of \(s_k\) and \(a_{kq}\),
\[
 \sum_q a_{kq}^2
 =s_k^{-2}\sum_qc_{kq}^2\sigma_q^2=1.
\]
Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:centered-errors}\) and
\(\texttt{ass:independent-cells}\) make the vectors \(X_q\) independent
and centered.  Since \(E_{P_n}\epsilon_q^2=1\), every sign-expanded
coordinate \(j\) associated with a horizon \(k\) satisfies
\[
 \frac1m\sum_qE_{P_n}X_{qj}^2
 =\sum_q a_{kq}^2=1.
\]
Uniform sub-Gaussianity gives
\(E_{P_n}\epsilon_q^4\le C\) and
\(\|\epsilon_q\|_{\psi_1}\le\|\epsilon_q\|_{\psi_2}\le C_e\).
Using \(|a_{kq}|\le L_n(K)\) and \(\sum_qa_{kq}^2=1\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1m\sum_qE_{P_n}X_{qj}^4
 &=m\sum_qa_{kq}^4E_{P_n}\epsilon_q^4\\
 &\le CmL_n(K)^2\sum_qa_{kq}^2
 =CmL_n(K)^2,
 \end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
 \|X_{qj}\|_{\psi_1}
 \le C\sqrt m\,|a_{kq}|
 \le C\sqrt mL_n(K).
\]
Because \(1=\sum_qa_{kq}^2\le mL_n(K)^2\), the envelope
\(B_m=C\sqrt mL_n(K)\) can be chosen at least one.  Thus the variance,
fourth-moment, and sub-exponential hypotheses of
\(\texttt{lem:cckk2022-gaussian-approximation}\) hold with constants
depending only on \(C_e\).  That cited theorem applies because
\(m\ge3\) and \(p\ge2\), and yields
\[
 C\left\{\frac{B_m^2\log^5(pm)}m\right\}^{1/4}
 \le C_1\{L_n(K)^2\log^5((p\vee3)m)\}^{1/4}
 =\mathfrak g_n.
\]
The normalized sum has coordinate list
\((S_{n,0},\ldots,S_{n,K-1},-S_{n,0},\ldots,-S_{n,K-1})\), so its
maximum is \(\max_k|S_{n,k}|\).  Its first \(K\) coordinates have
covariance
\[
 \sum_q a_{kq}a_{k'q}=\Sigma_{n,kk'}
\]
by \(\texttt{lem:staircase-covariance}\); hence its full Gaussian
covariance is the sign expansion induced by \(\Sigma_n\), not the
\(K\)-by-\(K\) matrix itself.  The cited result requires only the unit
coordinate variances established above and therefore permits singular
covariance.

We transfer this comparison to the feasible statistic.  Write
\(t_k=\widehat s_k/s_k\).  On \(\mathcal B_n(h)\),
\(\texttt{thm:heteroskedastic-covariance}\) gives
\(|t_k^2-1|\le\delta_h\).  If \(\delta_h\le1/2\), then
\(t_k\ge\sqrt{1-\delta_h}\), \(t_k^{-1}\le\sqrt2\), and
\[
 |t_k^{-1}-1|
 =\frac{|t_k-1|}{t_k}
 \le\frac{\delta_h}
 {(1+\sqrt{1-\delta_h})\sqrt{1-\delta_h}}
 \le\delta_h.
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:uniform-influence}\), on \(\mathcal R_n(u)\),
\[
 \max_k\left|t_k^{-1}(S_{n,k}+\rho_{n,k})-S_{n,k}\right|
 \le\delta_h\max_k|S_{n,k}|+\sqrt2u.
\]
Independence, the sub-Gaussian moment-generating-function bound, and
\(\sum_qa_{kq}^2=1\) imply
\[
 P_n\{\max_k|S_{n,k}|>C_4\sqrt{\log(2p/\delta_h)}\}
 \le\delta_h
\]
for \(0<\delta_h\le1/2\).  On the complementary event the last
perturbation is at most
\[
 v_n=C_5\{\delta_h\sqrt{\log(2p/\delta_h)}+u\}.
\]
The Gaussian vector has unit marginal variances.  Hence
\(\texttt{lem:cck-gaussian-anti-concentration}\) gives
\[
 \sup_zP\{|M_n^Z-z|\le v_n\}
 \le Cv_n\sqrt{\log(2p)}.
\]
Sandwich the feasible event between linear events at thresholds
\(z-v_n\) and \(z+v_n\), then use the Gaussian approximation.  Adding
the two failure probabilities and the tail probability \(\delta_h\)
gives the asserted distribution bound after using, for
\(0<\delta_h\le1/2\),
\[
 \delta_h+
 \delta_h\sqrt{\log(2p/\delta_h)\log(2p)}
 \le C\left[\delta_h\log(2p)+
 \delta_h^{1/3}\{1\vee\log(p/\delta_h)\}^{2/3}\right].
\]
This elementary inequality follows by splitting
\(\log(2p/\delta_h)=\log(2p)+\log(1/\delta_h)\), applying
\(2\sqrt{ab}\le a+b\), and using
\(x\log(1/x)=O(x^{1/3})\) on \((0,1/2]\).  When \(h=0\), scale error
is zero and the same bound follows directly with
\(\mathfrak c_n(0)=0\).

For the multiplier comparison, conditional on \(Y\), independence and
Gaussianity of \(\xi_q\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
 \sum_q\frac{\widehat c_{kq}\widehat\sigma_u}{\widehat s_k}\xi_q,
 \sum_q\frac{\widehat c_{k'q}\widehat\sigma_u}{\widehat s_{k'}}\xi_q
 \mathrel{\Big|}Y\right)=\widehat\Sigma_{kk'}.
\]
Thus \(W_n^\xi\) is conditionally the maximum absolute coordinate of
\(N(0,\widehat\Sigma_n)\).  On \(\mathcal B_n(h)\),
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_n-\Sigma_n\|_{\max}
 \le\frac{2\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}\le4\delta_h.
\]
Apply \(\texttt{lem:cck-gaussian-comparison-maxima}\) to the two
sign-expanded Gaussian vectors.  Its coordinate variances are bounded
and it permits singular covariance, so
\[
 \sup_z|P(W_n^\xi\le z\mid Y)-P(M_n^Z\le z)|
 \le\mathfrak c_n(h).
\]

The maximum of finitely many absolute Gaussian coordinates with positive
marginal variances has no atoms: for every \(z\), its atom is bounded by
the sum of the zero point-masses of the individual absolute Gaussian
coordinates.  Its distribution function is therefore continuous.  On
\(\mathcal B_n(h)\), Gaussian comparison brackets the conditional
\((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(W_n^\xi\) between the
\((1-\alpha-\mathfrak c_n(h))\)- and
\((1-\alpha+\mathfrak c_n(h))\)-quantiles of \(M_n^Z\).  Evaluating the
feasible distribution bound at those two deterministic quantiles and
paying \(P_n\{\mathcal B_n(h)^c\}\) once more proves the finite coverage
inequality.

Assumption \(\texttt{ass:cell-leverage}\) makes \(\mathfrak g_n\to0\).
If \(h_n\log^2(2p)\to0\), then
\(\delta_{h_n}\log(2p)\to0\) and
\(\delta_{h_n}\log^2(p/\delta_{h_n})\to0\), so
\(\mathfrak c_n(h_n)\to0\).  The generic asymptotic statement follows
by inserting the four displayed remainder and score conditions into the
finite bound.  For the Choi--Yuan specialization,
\(\mathcal A_n(C)\subseteq
\mathcal R_n(C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K))\) was proved in
\(\texttt{thm:uniform-influence}\), and
\(\texttt{ass:uniform-remainder-envelope}\) makes its deterministic
anti-concentration term vanish.  The two failure probabilities remain
additional premises.  Finally, if \(L_n(K)\asymp m^{-1/2}\), then
\(\mathfrak g_n\to0\) whenever
\(m^{-1}\log^5((p\vee3)m)\to0\), equivalently
\(\log(Km)=o(m^{1/5})\), proving the elbow statement.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The retained headline gives a generic finite-error transfer and uses the 2022 CCKK theorem to reduce the leverage logarithm from power seven to power five without a covariance eigenvalue assumption. \textit{Gap.} A growing-path theorem still needs high-probability control of the exact nonlinear remainder event and feasible score discrepancy; neither follows from the class label. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers obtain an explicit diagnostic coverage bound and the balanced-loading elbow \(\log(Km)=o(m^{1/5})\).

\begin{proposition}[prop:fixed-horizon-reduction]\label{prop:fixed-horizon-reduction}
When \(K=1\), \(\mathcal K_n=\{0\}\), the eligible set contains one target block \(E_0=\{g_0\}\), and \(\widehat\Omega_{00}>0\), \(c_{0q}\) and \(\Omega_{00}\) reduce to the Choi--Yuan Proposition A.4 influence coefficients and their heteroskedastic scalar influence variance, while \(\widehat\Omega_{00}\) and \(\mathcal C_{n,0}\) reduce to the Choi--Kwon--Liao-style unit-residual plug-in variance and the ordinary studentized scalar interval.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume the hypotheses in the proposition.  Because \(g_0\) is a realized
finite adoption cohort, \(I_{g_0}\neq\varnothing\), and hence
\(n_{g_0}=|I_{g_0}|\geq 1\).  Thus all cohort averages below are defined.
Since \(E_0=\{g_0\}\), the definition of \(N_k\) gives
\[
N_0=\sum_{g\in E_0}n_g=n_{g_0},
\qquad
\frac{n_{g_0}}{N_0}=1.
\]
Substituting \(k=0\), \(E_0=\{g_0\}\), and this last equality into the
coefficient array defined by \(\texttt{def:staircase-influence-map}\) yields,
for every \(q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{0,(u,v)}
={}&\frac{\mathbf 1\{u\in I_{g_0},\ v=g_0\}}{n_{g_0}}
-\mathbf 1\{u\in J_{g_0,0},\ v=g_0\}
  \bar x_{g_0}^{\top}A_{g_0,0}^{-1}x_u \\
&-\frac{\mathbf 1\{u\in I_{g_0},\ v\in S_{g_0}\}}{n_{g_0}}
  z_{g_0}^{\top}B_{g_0}^{-1}z_v .
\end{aligned}
\]
The three terms are respectively the observed treated-cell, target-column
donor-row, and treated-row preperiod coefficients of the single eligible
Choi--Yuan block.  The construction in
\(\texttt{def:staircase-influence-map}\) specifies these as the exact
Proposition A.4 coefficients.  There is no cohort aggregation left, so
\(c_{0q}\) is precisely that single-block coefficient.

Because \(\mathcal K_n=\{0\}\), the definition of \(\Omega_n\) has only one
entry and gives
\[
\Omega_{00}
=\sum_{q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
  c_{0q}c_{0q}\sigma_{uv}^{2}
=\sum_{q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
  c_{0q}^{2}\sigma_{uv}^{2}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:row-heteroskedasticity}\),
\(\sigma_{uv}^{2}=\sigma_u^{2}\).  Consequently
\[
\Omega_{00}
=\sum_{q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{0q}^{2}\sigma_u^{2},
\]
which is the heteroskedastic scalar influence variance of the one block.
Likewise, direct specialization of
\(\texttt{def:heteroskedastic-plugin}\) gives
\[
\widehat\Omega_{00}
=\sum_{q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
  \widehat c_{0q}^{\,2}\widehat\sigma_u^{2}.
\]
This is the corresponding unit-residual plug-in scalar variance: the unit
variance \(\widehat\sigma_u^2\) is the leave-one-cell-out residual-square
average specified in that definition.

It remains to identify the critical value.  The assumed positivity
\(\widehat\Omega_{00}>0\) implies
\[
\widehat s_0=\sqrt{\widehat\Omega_{00}}>0,
\]
so studentization is well defined.  Conditional on \(Y\), the quantities
\(\widehat c_{0q}\), \(\widehat\sigma_u\), and \(\widehat s_0\) are fixed,
whereas the multipliers are independent \(N(0,1)\) variables and are
independent of \(Y\) by \(\texttt{def:multiplier-band}\).  Hence
\[
Z_0^\xi
:=\sum_{q=(u,v)\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
  \frac{\widehat c_{0q}\widehat\sigma_u}{\widehat s_0}\xi_q
\ \bigg|\ Y
\sim N\!\left(
0,
\frac{\sum_q\widehat c_{0q}^{\,2}\widehat\sigma_u^2}
     {\widehat s_0^2}
\right)
=N(0,1),
\]
where the penultimate variance equals one because the numerator is
\(\widehat\Omega_{00}\) and
\(\widehat s_0^2=\widehat\Omega_{00}\).  Therefore
\[
W_n^\xi=|Z_0^\xi|,
\qquad
c_{1-\alpha,n}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2),
\]
with \(\Phi\) the standard-normal distribution function; the quantile is
finite and unique because \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  Finally,
\(\texttt{def:multiplier-band}\) reduces to
\[
\mathcal C_{n,0}
=\left[
\widehat\tau_0
\pm \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\widehat s_0
\right],
\]
the ordinary two-sided studentized scalar interval.  Every equality above is
a finite-sample specialization of the declared definitions; no
growing-dimensional approximation or limit argument is used.
\end{proof}

Working over the baseline event-transfer slices

\(\Delta_n^{\dagger}(K;\eta_n)\) and constants

 c\Delta_n^{\dagger}(K;\eta_n)
 \le\Delta_n^*(K;\eta_n)
 \le C\Delta_n^{\dagger}(K;\eta_n)
\]
for every vanishing \(\eta_n\).  The upper-band implication is expressly
conditional on the unresolved feasible-score premise: there must exist
\(h_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{CY},0}}
 P_n\{\mathcal B_n(h_n)^c\}\to0,
 \qquad h_n\log^2(4K)\to0.
\]
Under that premise, prove that
\(\Delta_n^{\dagger}(K;\eta_n)\sqrt{\log(2K)}\to0\) implies

Conversely, for every primitive candidate \(\beta\) with

\(\Delta_n^\dagger(K;\eta_n)\sqrt{\log(2K)}\) is bounded away from zero,

 \delta_n(P_n;\eta_n)\ge c\Delta_n^\dagger(K;\eta_n),

\]
and the witness itself must establish feasible-score stability for some

 P_n\{\mathcal B_n(h_n)^c\}\to0,

\]
The derivation must first compute whether the balanced strong-factor

\begin{lemma}[lem:choi-yuan-one-block-remainder]\label{lem:choi-yuan-one-block-remainder}
For one Choi--Yuan missing block with dimensions \(N_o\) and \(T_o\), missing-group size \(\vartheta_o\), i.i.d. centered homoskedastic sub-Gaussian noise, incoherence, signal, spectral-size, and missing-block conditions as stated in their Appendix A, Proposition A.4 gives, with probability at least \(1-O(\min\{N_o^{-9},T_o^{-9}\})\), a simultaneous entrywise linear expansion of every target imputation error into its donor-column and observed-row terms, with the maximum entry remainder bounded by a common constant times the three-term expression used here as \(R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\) after setting \((N_o,T_o,\vartheta_o)=(N_{gk},T_g,n_g)\).
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the published one-block remainder premise used only for the source-submodel finite union bound. \textit{Gap.} The cited proposition is not a growing-block or heterogeneous-noise result. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the blockwise failure probability in thm:uniform-influence.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cck-gaussian-comparison-maxima]\label{lem:cck-gaussian-comparison-maxima}
Let \(V\) and \(Y\) be centered Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^p\), assume the coordinate variances of \(Y\) are bounded above and away from zero, and put \(\Delta_0=\max_{j,\ell}|\operatorname{Cov}(V_j,V_\ell)-\operatorname{Cov}(Y_j,Y_\ell)|\).  Lemma 3.1 gives
\[
 \sup_z\left|P\{\max_jV_j\le z\}-P\{\max_jY_j\le z\}\right|
 \le C\Delta_0^{1/3}\{1\vee\log(p/\Delta_0)\}^{2/3},
\]
without a positive-minimum-eigenvalue requirement.
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This converts the deterministic maximum covariance error into a conditional Gaussian distribution error. \textit{Gap.} The application must still prove the covariance discrepancy for the frozen score construction. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the covariance-comparison term in thm:uniform-multiplier-band.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cck-gaussian-anti-concentration]\label{lem:cck-gaussian-anti-concentration}
For a separable centered Gaussian process with unit pointwise variances and almost surely finite supremum, Theorem 2.1 bounds the Levy concentration function of the supremum at radius \(\varepsilon\) by \(4\varepsilon\) times one plus its expected supremum.  Corollary 2.1 gives the same bound for the supremum of the absolute value.  For a finite \(p\)-coordinate Gaussian vector this implies
\[
 \sup_zP\left\{\left|\max_{j\le p}|Z_j|-z\right|\le\varepsilon\right\}
 \le C\varepsilon\sqrt{\log(2p)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This turns the event-score and studentization perturbations into Kolmogorov and coverage errors. \textit{Gap.} The event-specific perturbation radius is derived in this note. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the anti-concentration terms in thm:uniform-multiplier-band.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cckk2022-gaussian-approximation]\label{lem:cckk2022-gaussian-approximation}
Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\) be independent centered random vectors in
\(\mathbb R^p\), where \(m\ge3\) and \(p\ge2\), and let
\(G\) be centered Gaussian with covariance
\(m^{-1}\sum_{q=1}^mE[x_qx_q^\top]\).  Suppose that \(B_m\ge1\),
\(E\exp(|x_{qj}|/B_m)\le2\) for all \(q,j\), and constants
\(0<b_1\le b_2<\infty\) satisfy
\[
 b_1^2\le \frac1m\sum_qE[x_{qj}^2],
 \qquad
 \frac1m\sum_qE[x_{qj}^4]\le B_m^2b_2^2
 \quad(j\le p).
\]
Then Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato--Koike (2022), Theorem 2.1, gives
\[
 \sup_{A\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm{re}}}
 \left|P\!\left\{m^{-1/2}\sum_qx_q\in A\right\}-P\{G\in A\}\right|
 \le C\left\{\frac{B_m^2\log^5(pm)}m\right\}^{1/4},
\]
where \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{re}}\) is the class of axis-aligned
hyperrectangles and \(C\) depends only on \(b_1,b_2\).  The theorem
places no positive-minimum-eigenvalue condition on the covariance matrix.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:choi-yuan-published-selected-scope]\label{lem:choi-yuan-published-selected-scope}
In Choi--Yuan's published model the panel mean is
\(M^{\mathrm{pub}}=M^\star+M^R\), where \(M^\star\) has rank \(r\), global
incoherence and condition-number indices are attached to \(M^\star\),
and \(\max_{i,t}|m^R_{it}|\to0\); the errors are i.i.d., centered,
homoskedastic, and sub-Gaussian.  Their staggered-adoption Theorem 3.2
fixes one scalar group average, indexed by one chosen
\((\mathcal G,t_0,\{\mathcal G_\ell\}_{\ell=0}^L)\), and imposes its six
conditions only for the adoption dates and blocks used by that chosen
functional.  Those conditions are three signal, spectral-size, and
group-size ratios, a local rate for \(M^R\), two normalized Gram bounds,
and two nonvanishing loading conditions.  The theorem does not impose
these six conditions simultaneously for every possible scalar functional
or every event horizon.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[prop:published-class-comparison]\label{prop:published-class-comparison}
After projecting the published array-plus-selected-functional objects to
their law arrays on the common staggered-panel sample space, neither the
baseline event-transfer class \(\mathfrak M^{\mathrm{CY},0}\) nor its band
subclass \(\mathfrak M^{\mathrm{CY}}\) contains the published Choi--Yuan
class \(\mathfrak M^{\mathrm{CY,pub}}_{\mathrm{sel}}\), and the published
class contains neither event-transfer class.  The common subclass
\(\mathfrak M^\cap\) is nonempty.  Its event-block/published-block
correspondence is
\(N_0=|J_{gk}|\), \(T_d=|S_g|\),
\(N_{gk}=|I_g\cup J_{gk}|\), and \(T_g=|S_g|+1\); membership requires
both, not an identification of, the two families of rate inequalities.
A converse proved on \(\mathfrak M^\cap\) transfers upward to the
published approximate-rank class.  In contrast, the event-transfer
upper-band theorem does not transfer to the full published class without
adding the approximation bias generated by \(M^R\) to the remainder
event.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The comparison is well typed after applying the law projection in
\(\texttt{def:choi-yuan-published-selected-class}\): both sides are then
sets of complete laws on the same \(N\)-by-\(T\) staggered-panel sample
space.  The source scope encoded by
\(\texttt{lem:choi-yuan-published-selected-scope}\) verifies that the
published object carries only one selected scalar functional and permits
\(M^R\ne0\), while the definitions of the two event-transfer classes
require exact low rank, blockwise uniform geometry, rowwise variance
structure, variance replication, and a maximum-cell leverage envelope.

For the first strict noncontainment, start from a balanced rank-one
published signal \(M^\star=\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_d^\top\) with i.i.d.
\(N(0,1)\) errors and the selected functional used below.  Let
\(M^R=q_dI_d\), where \(q_d>0\) decreases sufficiently fast to satisfy
both the global approximation requirement and the selected local
condition (iv).  All six conditions concerning \(M^\star\) are unchanged,
but \(M^{\mathrm{pub}}=M^\star+q_dI_d\) has rank \(d\).  Since the
event-transfer classes require rank \(r<\min\{N,T\}=d\), this published
law is in neither event-transfer class.  This witness uses no fixed bound
on the published \(\kappa\), \(\mu\), or noise variance.

For the reverse strict noncontainment, use the exact balanced rank-one
array below but replace the i.i.d. errors by independent Gaussian errors
whose unit variances alternate between \(1\) and \(2\) and are constant
over time.  The fixed variance bounds, row heteroskedasticity, replication,
and all event-transfer rate conditions continue to hold.  No selected
functional can turn unequal Gaussian variances into i.i.d. homoskedastic
noise.  Hence this array belongs to the band event-transfer class, and
therefore also to its baseline class, but its law is outside the published
projection.  The two noncontainments prove incomparability.

It remains to prove that \(\mathfrak M^\cap\ne\varnothing\).  Let
\(d=d_n\to\infty\) be even, put \(N=T=d\),
\(b=\lfloor d^{1/3}\rfloor\), \(g=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\), and
\(K=\lfloor d^{1/10}\rfloor\).  Let one cohort of size \(b\) adopt at
\(g\), let the other \(d-b\) units never adopt, set \(x_i=z_t=1\),
\(M^R=0\), and take i.i.d. \(N(0,1)\) errors.  Select
\(\phi_n=(I_g,g,\{I_g\})\).  Every event block has
\[
 N_{gk}=d,\qquad T_g=g,\qquad
 \psi_{gk}=\sqrt{dg},\qquad \kappa_{gk}=\mu_{gk}=r=1,
 \qquad A_{gk}=d-b,\quad B_g=g-1.
\]
Thus both Gram bounds hold with constants bounded away from zero and
infinity.  Direct substitution gives the event-transfer ratios
\[
 O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right),\qquad
 O\!\left(\frac{\log^3d}{d}\right),\qquad
 O(d^{-2/3})
\]
for the signal, spectral-size, and missing-block conditions, respectively,
and the variance-replication ratio is
\(O(d^{-1/2}\log^{5/2}d)\).

For every horizon, the \(b\) treated target coefficients are \(1/b\),
the \(d-b\) donor target coefficients are \(-1/(d-b)\), and the
\(b(g-1)\) treated pre-period coefficients are
\(-1/[b(g-1)]\).  Therefore
\[
 s_k^2=\frac1b+\frac1{d-b}+\frac1{b(g-1)}\asymp b^{-1},
 \qquad L_n(K)\asymp b^{-1/2}=d^{-1/6}.
\]
Moreover \(m=|\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}|\le Kd+b(g-1)\le Cd^{4/3}\),
so
\[
 L_n(K)^2\log^5((2K\vee3)m)=O(d^{-1/3}\log^5d)\to0.
\]
The three terms in \(R_{gk}^{\mathrm{CY}}\) have orders
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right),\qquad
 O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}d\right),\qquad
 O\!\left(\frac{b\sqrt{\log d}}{d^{3/2}}\right).
\]
Since \(s_k^{-1}\asymp\sqrt b\), it follows that
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K)\sqrt{\log(2K)}
 =O(d^{-5/6}\log^{3/2}d)\to0.
\]
Because every fixed envelope in the class is positive, \(d_n\) can be
chosen recursively large enough that all five baseline ratios, and even
the band remainder ratio, are below their prescribed envelopes at index
\(n\).

For the selected published functional, \(N_0=d-b\asymp d\),
\(T_d=g-1\asymp d\), and
\(\psi^{\mathrm{pub}}_{\min,O_d}=\sqrt{N_0T_d}\asymp d\).  Substitution into its first
three conditions gives, respectively,
\[
 O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right),\qquad
 O\!\left(\frac{\log^{3/2}d}{\sqrt d}\right),\qquad
 O\!\left(d^{-2/3}\sqrt{\log d}\right),
\]
which vanish.  Condition (iv) is zero.  The normalized row and column
Gram matrices in condition (v) equal \(1\), because the global singular
vectors have entries \(d^{-1/2}\), and condition (vi) also equals one:
\(\sqrt d\,|\bar u^{\mathrm{pub}}_{I_g}|=\sqrt d\,|v_g^{\mathrm{pub}}|=1\).  Thus the array satisfies
all six selected-functional conditions and every uniform event-block
condition.  It belongs to \(\mathfrak M^\cap\), proving nonemptiness and
the stated actual block correspondence.

Finally, set inclusion gives the transfer direction.  Any lower-bound or
counterexample law in \(\mathfrak M^\cap\) is also a law admitted by the
published class.  An upper theorem on the exact-rank event-transfer class
does not cover a nonzero \(M^R\); its deterministic bias appears in the
target-centered statistic and must be included in \(\rho_n\) or in a
separate approximation-bias event.  No such term is present in the frozen
band, so upper validity cannot be transferred to the full published class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition prevents the event-transfer assumptions from being misattributed to Choi--Yuan and supplies a legal common subclass for any eventual converse. \textit{Gap.} The published model and the event-transfer model differ in rank approximation, noise structure, geometry, functional quantification, and growing-path envelopes. \textit{Consumer.} A counterarray in the common subclass transfers upward to the published model, while upper validity requires a separate approximation-bias argument.

\section{Interpretation}

For a right-to-carry event curve, the common critical value is justified only after the analyst controls the exact nonlinear remainder event and the realized score discrepancy.  Shared donor and pre-treatment cells remain in the full covariance.  Under balanced loadings, the Gaussian part allows \(\log(Km)=o(m^{1/5})\), but this does not certify either missing panel-specific probability.  A converse on the explicit common subclass would apply to the published model, whereas the event-transfer upper theorem does not cover published approximation bias from \(M^R\).

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The primitive Choi--Yuan expression controls a one-block upper perturbation bound, not an attained nonlinear remainder and not a simultaneous heterogeneous-block event.  Its direct union bound may fail to vanish.  Variance replication controls the oracle residual squares but not the leave-one-cell-out imputation errors or the normalized rowwise loading discrepancy.  Therefore neither \(\|\rho_n\|_\infty=O_{P_n}(\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K))\) nor \(r_n^{\mathrm{score}}=o_{P_n}(\log^{-2}(2K))\) is asserted over the event-transfer class.  The common rank-one array verifies both class condition families, but the estimator's exact signed second-order KKT expansion is absent from the frozen primitives; the early-kill comparison and attainable frontier remain open.  Approximate-rank upper validity would additionally need an explicit \(M^R\) bias remainder.

\section{Honest scope}

The causal target is the eligible-cohort unit-share event curve under deterministic staggered adoption.  Settled results are the exact eventwise influence identity, full staircase covariance, oracle variance concentration, deterministic feasible-score stability, and a generic finite-error Gaussian-multiplier transfer with the 2022 log-power-five approximation.  The Choi--Yuan specialization supplies only the deterministic inclusion \(\mathcal A_n(C)\subseteq\mathcal R_n(C\Delta_{\mathrm{CY},n}(K))\) and an unsimplified i.i.d. homoskedastic source union bound.  Coverage tending to \(1-\alpha\) is conditional on explicit remainder and score probability limits.  The inferential event-transfer class is incomparable with the published approximate-rank selected-functional class.  Serial dependence, tuning selection, uniform feasible-score stability, simultaneous block probability, approximation-bias transfer, and the attainable nonlinear-remainder frontier remain open.
