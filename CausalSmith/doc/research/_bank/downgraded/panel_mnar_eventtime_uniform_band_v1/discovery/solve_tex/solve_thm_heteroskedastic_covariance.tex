%%% FIELD staircase-covariance-proof
Put
\[
 U_k=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}e_q,
 \qquad k\in\mathcal K_n.
\]
All sums are finite.  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:centered-errors}\) gives
\(E_{P_n}[U_k]=0\).  Thus, for \(k,k'\in\mathcal K_n\), bilinearity of
covariance gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U_k,U_{k'})
 =\sum_{q,q'\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}
 c_{kq}c_{k'q'}E_{P_n}[e_qe_{q'}].
\]
If \(q\ne q'\), assumption \(\texttt{ass:independent-cells}\) and
assumption \(\texttt{ass:centered-errors}\) imply
\[
 E_{P_n}[e_qe_{q'}]
 =E_{P_n}[e_q]E_{P_n}[e_{q'}]=0.
\]
For \(q=q'=(u,v)\), the definition of \(\sigma_{uv}^2\) gives
\(E_{P_n}[e_q^2]=\sigma_{uv}^2=\sigma_q^2\).  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U_k,U_{k'})
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}c_{k'q}\sigma_q^2
 =\Omega_{kk'},
\]
where the second equality is the definition of \(\Omega_n\).  By the
definition of \(\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}\), every term indexed by
\(q\notin\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}\) has
\(c_{kq}c_{k'q}=0\).  Hence
\[
 \Omega_{kk'}
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_{n,kk'}^{\mathrm{sh}}}
 c_{kq}c_{k'q}\sigma_q^2.
\]
This equality proves the asserted characterization: \(\Omega_{kk'}\ne0\)
if and only if the displayed signed sum is nonzero.  In particular, the
existence of shared cells alone is insufficient because products with
different signs may cancel.  Finally,
\(\texttt{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}\) gives \(s_k>0\) for every
\(k\in\mathcal K_n\), so \(D_n\) is invertible.  The definition of
\(\Sigma_n\) then yields
\[
 \Sigma_n=D_n^{-1}\Omega_nD_n^{-1}.
\]

%%% FIELD two-horizon-witness-proof
In \(\mathcal W^{(2)}\), the definition of the witness gives
\[
 M^0=\mathbf 1_8\mathbf 1_3^{\top}.
\]
Both vectors are nonzero, so \(\operatorname{rank}(M^0)=1\).  For either
\(k\in\{0,1\}\), the eligible block has rows
\(I_2\cup J_{2,k}=\{1,\ldots,8\}\), columns
\(S_2\cup\{2+k\}=\{1,2+k\}\), and every entry equal to one.  A compact
singular value decomposition is therefore
\[
 U_{2,k}=\frac{\mathbf 1_8}{\sqrt 8},
 \qquad
 V_{2,k}=\frac{\mathbf 1_2}{\sqrt 2},
 \qquad
 \psi_{2,k}=\sqrt{8\cdot2}=4.
\]
Because \(r=1\), the definition of block incoherence gives
\[
 \mu_{2,k}
 =\max\!\left\{
 8\left\|U_{2,k}\right\|_{2,\infty}^2,
 2\left\|V_{2,k}\right\|_{2,\infty}^2
 \right\}
 =\max\{8(1/8),2(1/2)\}=1.
\]
Every loading and factor equals one.  Since \(|J_{2,k}|=4\) and
\(S_2=\{1\}\), the definitions of the Gram matrices yield, identifying
one-by-one matrices with their scalar entries,
\[
 A_{2,k}=\sum_{j=5}^{8}x_j^2=4,
 \qquad
 B_2=\sum_{s\in S_2}z_s^2=z_1^2=1.
\]

For both horizons, \(E_k=\{2\}\) and \(N_k=n_2=4\); hence the outer
cohort weight \(n_2/N_k\) in the definition of \(c_{kq}\) equals one.
Moreover, \(\bar x_2=1\).  Substituting
\(A_{2,k}^{-1}=1/4\), \(B_2^{-1}=1\), \(z_{2+k}=1\), and \(z_1=1\)
into the displayed coefficient formula gives, with \(t_k=2+k\),
\[
 c_{k,(u,v)}=
 \begin{cases}
  1/4,&u\in I_2,\ v=t_k,\\
 -1/4,&u\in J_{2,k},\ v=t_k,\\
 -1/4,&u\in I_2,\ v=1,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The three nonzero cases are disjoint, and each contains four cells.  Since
the witness sets every cell variance equal to one, the definition of
\(\Omega_n\) gives
\[
 \Omega_{kk}
 =4(1/4)^2+4(-1/4)^2+4(-1/4)^2
 =\frac34.
\]
The treated and donor target cells occur at calendar date \(2+k\), so they
are distinct between horizons zero and one.  The only cells having nonzero
coefficients at both horizons are the four pre-treatment cells \((u,1)\)
with \(u\in I_2\).  Each such cell contributes
\[
 c_{0,(u,1)}c_{1,(u,1)}=(-1/4)(-1/4)=\frac1{16}.
\]
Applying \(\texttt{lem:staircase-covariance}\), whose independence and
centering hypotheses hold for the witness noise, yields
\[
 \Omega_{01}=4\left(\frac1{16}\right)=\frac14.
\]
Finally, the definitions of \(s_k\), \(D_n\), and \(\Sigma_n\) give
\[
 \Sigma_{01}
 =\frac{\Omega_{01}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{00}\Omega_{11}}}
 =\frac{1/4}{3/4}=\frac13.
\]
Thus the shared-cell signed sum does not cancel.

%%% FIELD heteroskedastic-covariance-proof
Write \(h_{\min}=\min_{i\le N}h_i\).  By the definitions of
\(\mathcal V_n\) and \(h_i\),
\[
 \min_{i\le N}|\{t:(i,t)\in\mathcal V_n\}|=h_{\min}.
\]
The real-valued inequality in \(\texttt{ass:variance-replication}\) has
this quantity in its denominator; hence that assumption entails
\(h_{\min}>0\).  Thus every oracle average in the statement is well
defined.

Fix \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{CY}}\).  By
\(\texttt{ass:row-heteroskedasticity}\), for every \(t\in H_i\),
\[
 E_{P_n}[e_{it}^2]=\sigma_{it}^2=\sigma_i^2.
\]
We first establish the claimed concentration uniformly over the finite
slices.  The definition of the sub-Gaussian norm and
\(\texttt{ass:heteroskedastic-subgaussian}\) imply that there is a
constant \(C_2<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\), such that
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp\!\left\{\frac{e_{it}^2}{C_2\sigma_i^2}\right\}\le2.
\]
For every integer \(m\ge1\), the elementary inequality
\(y^m\le m!e^y\), applied with
\(y=e_{it}^2/(C_2\sigma_i^2)\), gives
\[
 E_{P_n}|e_{it}|^{2m}
 \le 2m!(C_2\sigma_i^2)^m.
\]
Set \(U_{it}=e_{it}^2-\sigma_i^2\).  Then
\(E_{P_n}[U_{it}]=0\).  The preceding moment inequality,
\((x+y)^m\le2^{m-1}(x^m+y^m)\) for \(x,y\ge0\), and
\(\sigma_i\le\bar\sigma\) from \(\texttt{ass:variance-bounds}\) imply
that a constant \(C_3<\infty\), depending only on \(C_e\), satisfies
\[
 E_{P_n}|U_{it}|^m\le m!(C_3\bar\sigma^2)^m,
 \qquad m\ge2.
\]
Let \(B=C_3\bar\sigma^2\).  Expanding the exponential, using
\(E_{P_n}[U_{it}]=0\), and bounding every remaining signed moment by its
absolute moment shows that, whenever \(|\lambda|B\le1/2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{P_n}e^{\lambda U_{it}}
 &\le 1+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}(|\lambda|B)^m\\
 &\le 1+2\lambda^2B^2
 \le \exp(2\lambda^2B^2).
 \end{aligned}
\]

For a fixed unit \(i\), assumption \(\texttt{ass:independent-cells}\)
makes \(\{U_{it}:t\in H_i\}\) mutually independent.  Multiplication of
the preceding moment-generating-function bounds therefore gives
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp\!\left\{\lambda\sum_{t\in H_i}U_{it}\right\}
 \le \exp(2h_i\lambda^2B^2),
 \qquad |\lambda|B\le1/2.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, first with positive \(\lambda\) and then
with negative \(\lambda\), and the choice of \(|\lambda|\) proportional
to
\[
 \min\!\left\{\frac{\sqrt y}{B\sqrt{h_i}},\frac1B\right\}
\]
show that there is a constant \(C_4<\infty\), depending only on
\(C_e\) and \(\bar\sigma\), for which, for every \(y\ge0\),
\[
 P_n\!\left\{
 \left|h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}U_{it}\right|
 >C_4\left(\sqrt{\frac y{h_i}}+\frac y{h_i}\right)
 \right\}\le2e^{-y}.
\]
Because
\(h_i^{-1}\sum_{t\in H_i}U_{it}=\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2\),
we may take \(y=\log(2N)+x\), use \(h_i\ge h_{\min}\), and apply the
union bound over \(i\le N\).  This yields
\[
 P_n\!\left\{
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 >C_4\left[
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)+x}{h_{\min}}}
 +\frac{\log(2N)+x}{h_{\min}}
 \right]\right\}
 \le 2N e^{-\log(2N)-x}=e^{-x}\le2e^{-x}.
\]
The constants used above do not depend on \(P_n\), \(i\), or \(n\).
Taking the supremum over \(P_n\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{CY}}\) proves the
first assertion with \(C_1=C_4\).

For completeness, taking \(x=\log(2/\delta)\) in the preceding display,
for arbitrary fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\), proves uniformly over the finite
slices that
\[
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 =O_{P_n}\!\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}}
 +\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}
 \right).
\]
Indeed, \(\log(2N)\ge\log2\), so the fixed additive term
\(\log(2/\delta)\) can be absorbed into a \(\delta\)-dependent
multiplicative constant.  Put
\[
 v_n=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2N)}{h_{\min}}}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:variance-replication}\) states
\[
 v_n\log^2(2K)\le\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{vrep}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
It also implies \(v_n\to0\), since \(\log(2K)\ge\log2\).  Therefore
\[
 (v_n+v_n^2)\log^2(2K)
 \le \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm{vrep}}(1+v_n)longrightarrow0.
\]
Combining this deterministic relation with the uniform stochastic order
proves
\[
 \max_{i\le N}|\widetilde\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i^2|
 =o_{P_n}((\log(2K))^{-2})
\]
uniformly over the finite slices.

We next isolate imputation contamination.  For \(t\in H_i\), the cell is
untreated by definition of \(H_i\).  The observation equation consequently
reduces to \(Y_{it}=m^0_{it}+e_{it}\).  With
\(d_{it}=\widehat m_{it}^{0,(-it)}-m^0_{it}\), the definition of the
leave-one-cell-out residual gives
\[
 \widehat e_{it}=Y_{it}-\widehat m_{it}^{0,(-it)}=e_{it}-d_{it}.
\]
Expanding the square exactly yields
\[
 \widehat\sigma_i^2-\widetilde\sigma_i^2
 =-\frac2{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}d_{it}
 +\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}d_{it}^2.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz and the definition of \(d_i\),
\[
 \left|\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}d_{it}\right|
 \le
 \left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}d_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}
 =\left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}d_i.
\]
The triangle inequality proves
\[
 |\widehat\sigma_i^2-\widetilde\sigma_i^2|
 \le2\left(\frac1{h_i}\sum_{t\in H_i}e_{it}^2\right)^{1/2}d_i+d_i^2.
\]
No frozen assumption supplies a stochastic bound for \(d_i\), so the
argument makes no stronger feasible-variance assertion.

It remains to prove the deterministic score-stability conclusions.  By
\(\texttt{ass:row-heteroskedasticity}\), if \(q=(u,v)\), then
\(\sigma_{uv}=\sigma_u\).  The definitions of \(a_{kq}\), \(s_k\), and
\(\Omega_{kk}\), together with \(s_k>0\) from
\(\texttt{ass:horizon-variance-nondegeneracy}\), imply
\[
 \|a_k\|_2^2
 =\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}a_{kq}^2
 =s_k^{-2}\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_n^{\mathrm{IF}}}c_{kq}^2\sigma_u^2
 =s_k^{-2}\Omega_{kk}=1.
\]
By the definitions of \(\widehat b_{kq}\), \(\widehat\Omega_n\), and
\(\Omega_n\),
\[
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}}
 =\sum_q\widehat b_{kq}\widehat b_{k'q},
 \qquad
 \frac{\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}}
 =\sum_qa_{kq}a_{k'q}.
\]
Set \(u_k=\widehat b_k-a_k\).  Subtracting the two inner products gives
\[
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}}
 =\langle u_k,a_{k'}\rangle
 +\langle a_k,u_{k'}\rangle
 +\langle u_k,u_{k'}\rangle.
\]
On \(\mathcal B_n(h)\), one has \(\|u_j\|_2\le h\) for every
\(j\in\mathcal K_n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\|a_j\|_2=1\) therefore
give
\[
 \max_{k,k'\in\mathcal K_n}
 \frac{|\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}|}{s_ks_{k'}}
 \le h+h+h^2=\delta_h.
\]

Set \(t_k=\widehat s_k/s_k\).  The diagonal case of the last bound gives
\[
 |t_k^2-1|
 =\left|\frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk}-\Omega_{kk}}{s_k^2}\right|
 \le\delta_h.
\]
If \(\delta_h<1\), then
\(t_k\ge\sqrt{1-\delta_h}>0\), so every feasible standard error is
positive and \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) is well defined.  Moreover,
\[
 |t_k-1|
 =\frac{|t_k^2-1|}{t_k+1}
 \le\frac{\delta_h}{1+\sqrt{1-\delta_h}}.
\]
For any \(k,k'\), the bounds on \(t_k^2\) and \(t_{k'}^2\) imply
\[
 1-\delta_h\le t_kt_{k'}\le1+\delta_h.
\]
Also \(|\Sigma_{kk'}|\le1\) by Cauchy--Schwarz because \(\Sigma_n\) is
the Gram matrix of the unit vectors \(a_k\).  If
\[
 E_{kk'}=
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{kk'}-\Omega_{kk'}}{s_ks_{k'}},
\]
then \(|E_{kk'}|\le\delta_h\) and
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{kk'}
 =\frac{\Sigma_{kk'}+E_{kk'}}{t_kt_{k'}}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\Sigma_{kk'}-\Sigma_{kk'}|
 &\le \frac{|E_{kk'}|}{t_kt_{k'}}
 +|\Sigma_{kk'}|\frac{|1-t_kt_{k'}|}{t_kt_{k'}}\\
 &\le\frac{\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}
 +\frac{\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}
 =\frac{2\delta_h}{1-\delta_h}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(k,k'\in\mathcal K_n\) proves the final claim.
Every assertion after the definition of \(\mathcal B_n(h)\) is therefore
a deterministic implication of the score discrepancy, as required.
