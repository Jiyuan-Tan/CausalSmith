A useful special case does close.  Let a coarse block \(B\in\pi_{R'}\) be a root in \(G_{R'}\), suppose it is a union of at least two finer blocks of \(\pi_R\), and suppose every parent in \(G_R\) of a finer block contained in \(B\) is itself contained in \(B\).  Choose a sink finer block \(D\subset B\) in the induced finer DAG and put \(C=B\setminus D\).  Acyclicity implies that \(X_C\) is a measurable function of the innovations of the finer blocks in \(C\), whereas
\[
 X_D=f_{R,D}(X_{\cup\operatorname{pa}_{G_R}(D)})+\varepsilon_{R,D}
\]
has all its displayed parents in \(C\).  Mutual block-noise independence gives \(\varepsilon_{R,D}\perp\!\!\!\perp X_C\), and its law belongs to \(\operatorname{SmoothPos}(|D|)\).  Since the coarse block is a root, \(\varepsilon_{R',B}=X_B\), so this is a forbidden directional split of the coarse innovation.  The general claim does not follow from this argument when external fine parents enter different coordinates of \(B\): subtracting the coarse mechanism can leave parent-dependent terms inside both sides of the proposed split, and independence of the resulting quotient residual has not been established by the two triangular factorizations.
