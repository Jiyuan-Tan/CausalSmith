For any two finite coordinate partitions \(\pi_R\) and \(\pi_{R'}\), the
refinement relation in Definition~\ref{def:refinement-order} is a partial
order.  If \(\pi_R\ne\pi_{R'}\), antisymmetry shows that exactly one of the
following alternatives holds:
\[
 \pi_R\text{ and }\pi_{R'}\text{ are incomparable},\qquad
 \pi_R\prec\pi_{R'},\qquad
 \pi_{R'}\prec\pi_R.
\]
If the conclusion requested in Open-ended
Question~\ref{oeq:crossing-rigidity} is available, the first alternative
produces, in one of the two representations, a nontrivial directional
additive-noise split with a \(C^3\) mechanism, an independent residual, and a
dimension-matched \(\operatorname{SmoothPos}\) residual law.  This contradicts
Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation-atomicity}.  If the conclusion requested in
Open-ended Question~\ref{oeq:strict-refinement-rigidity} is available, the
second alternative produces such a split in a coarse block of \(R'\), and
the third produces one in a coarse block of \(R\) after exchanging the two
representations.  Each again contradicts innovation atomicity.  Consequently
the finite-poset reduction is complete: the two stated rigidity conclusions
would imply \(\pi_R=\pi_{R'}\).  The frozen core, however, contains those two
conclusions only as open-ended questions, not as established premises.
