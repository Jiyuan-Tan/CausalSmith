Put \(r=p_{\varepsilon_{R,\{3\}}}\) and \(s=p_{\varepsilon_{R',\{2\}}}\), and abbreviate \(x=x_2\) and \(y=x_3\).  The assumptions \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\), together with \(\mathrm{ass:block-acyclicity}\), \(\mathrm{ass:mechanisms-c3}\), \(\mathrm{ass:block-noise-independence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:innovation-positive-density}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:innovation-c3-density}\), meet the hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:triangular-observed-density}\).  Applying that lemma to the two representations of the same law gives, pointwise on \(\mathbb R^3\),
\[
 r(y)p_\varepsilon\bigl(x_1-f_1(y),x-f_2(y)\bigr)
 =s(x)p_\eta\bigl(x_1-g_1(x),y-g_3(x)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
Every factor in \((1)\) is finite and strictly positive by \(\mathrm{ass:innovation-positive-density}\).  Set
\[
 v=x-f_2(y),\qquad z=y-g_3(x),\qquad
 \delta=f_1(y)-g_1(x).
\tag{2}
\]
Substitution of \(x_1=f_1(y)+t\) into \((1)\) gives
\[
 r(y)p_\varepsilon(t,v)=s(x)p_\eta(t+\delta,z)
 \quad\text{for every }(t,x,y)\in\mathbb R^3.
\tag{3}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:tonelli-nonnegative-product-integral}\) to each nonnegative bivariate innovation density.  Since each density has total integral one,
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}m_\varepsilon(v)\,dv=1,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}m_\eta(z)\,dz=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Strict positivity of the bivariate densities implies \(m_\varepsilon(v)>0\) and \(m_\eta(z)>0\), with the value \(+\infty\) allowed, for every slice value.  Equation \((4)\) implies that the sets on which these quantities are finite are conull.  This proves the asserted conullness of \(E_\varepsilon\) and \(E_\eta\).  If \((x,y)\in D=\Phi^{-1}(E_\varepsilon\times E_\eta)\), integration of \((3)\) in \(t\), justified by the same nonnegative-product integration result and translation invariance of Lebesgue measure, gives
\[
 r(y)m_\varepsilon(v)=s(x)m_\eta(z).
\tag{5}
\]
Both sides of \((5)\) are finite and positive.  Dividing \((3)\) by \((5)\) therefore proves
\[
 q_\varepsilon(t\mid v)=q_\eta(t+\delta\mid z),
 \qquad t\in\mathbb R,quad (x,y)\in D.
\tag{6}
\]

Fix \(t_0\in\mathbb R\).  Dividing \((3)\) by its value at \(t_0\) is legitimate at every \((x,y)\), not merely on \(D\).  Taking logarithms yields the global identity
\[
 \nu_\varepsilon(t,v)-\nu_\varepsilon(t_0,v)
 =\nu_\eta(t+\delta,z)-\nu_\eta(t_0+\delta,z).
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the continuity extension requested in the statement is immediate and does not use regularity of either slice normalizer.

We next derive the local translation conclusion using only \((7)\).  Define
\[
 S(t,v)=\partial_t\nu_\varepsilon(t,v),
 \qquad
 T(u,z)=\partial_u\nu_\eta(u,z),
 \qquad
 J(x,y)=1-f_2'(y)g_3'(x).
\tag{8}
\]
The two log-densities are \(C^3\), so \(S,T\in C^2\).  Differentiating \((7)\) first in \(t\), and then differentiating the resulting equality in \(x\) and in \(y\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S_v(t,v)&=-g_1'(x)T_u(t+\delta,z)-g_3'(x)T_z(t+\delta,z),\\
 -f_2'(y)S_v(t,v)&=f_1'(y)T_u(t+\delta,z)+T_z(t+\delta,z),\\
 S_t(t,v)&=T_u(t+\delta,z).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
All differentiations in \((9)\) are of the pointwise \(C^3\) log-density identity \((7)\); no marginal or conditional density is differentiated.  On \(\Omega=\{J\ne0\}\), elimination in \((9)\) gives
\[
 S_v(t,v)=a(x,y)S_t(t,v),
 \qquad
 a(x,y)=\frac{g_3'(x)f_1'(y)-g_1'(x)}{J(x,y)},
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 T_z(u,z)=b(x,y)T_u(u,z),
 \qquad
 b(x,y)=\frac{f_2'(y)g_1'(x)-f_1'(y)}{J(x,y)}.
\tag{11}
\]
The set \(\Omega\) is open because \(J\) is continuous.  Moreover,
\[
 D\Phi(x,y)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-f_2'(y)\\-g_3'(x)&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det D\Phi(x,y)=J(x,y).
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently every point of \(\Omega\) has a \(C^3\) inverse chart.  On any such chart, null sets are preserved in both directions locally; since \(E_\varepsilon\times E_\eta\) is conull, \(D_U=U\cap D\) is conull and therefore dense in \(U\).

Shrink a chart if necessary so that its image contains a rectangle in the \((v,z)\) coordinates.  If \(v\in E_\varepsilon\), then \(S_t(\mathord\cdot,v)\) cannot vanish identically.  Otherwise \(\nu_\varepsilon(\mathord\cdot,v)\) would be affine, and the exponential of an affine function has infinite integral on \(\mathbb R\), contradicting \(m_\varepsilon(v)<\infty\).  For such a regular \(v\), choose \(t\) with \(S_t(t,v)\ne0\).  Equation \((10)\) shows that \(a\), expressed in the \((v,z)\) coordinates, is independent of \(z\).  Regular \(v\)'s are dense by \((4)\), so continuity extends this independence to every \(v\) in the chart.  Thus \(a=A(v)\) there, with \(A\in C^2\).  Choose \(h_U\in C^3\) with \(h_U'=-A\).  Along the characteristics of \((10)\),
\[
 \frac{d}{dv}S\bigl(w+h_U(v),v\bigr)
 =h_U'(v)S_t+S_v=0,
\tag{13}
\]
so \(S(t,v)=S_0(t-h_U(v))\).  Integrating in \(t\) gives
\[
 \nu_\varepsilon(t,v)=\lambda_U(t-h_U(v))+c_U(v),
\tag{14}
\]
where \(\lambda_U,c_U\in C^3\).  Choose a regular base slice in the chart.  Its finite integral normalizes \(e^{\lambda_U}\) to a strictly positive unit-integral density \(\rho_U\) with \(\log\rho_U\in C^3\).  For every regular \(v\) in the chart, \((14)\) therefore yields
\[
 q_\varepsilon(t\mid v)=\rho_U(t-h_U(v)).
\tag{15}
\]
The identical argument applied to \((11)\) gives a \(C^3\) local shift \(k_U\) and a smooth positive unit-integral profile for the regular conditional laws of \(\eta_1\) given \(\eta_3=z\).

We record the uniqueness needed for gluing.  A strictly positive integrable density has no nonzero period: if \(\rho(t+c)=\rho(t)\) for \(c\ne0\), then a compact interval of length less than \(|c|\) has positive \(\rho\)-mass, while its translates by integer multiples of \(c\) are disjoint and have the same mass, contradicting \(\int\rho=1\).  Hence, on an overlap, two representations of the same regular conditional slice as translates of fixed unit densities determine their shift difference uniquely.  Around an overlap chain the accumulated shift must be a period of the initial profile and is therefore zero.  This proves the asserted overlap compatibility and path independence wherever the relevant chart cover exists.

It remains to show that the chart cover cannot lose a residual value.  Define the fully singular forward-fiber set
\[
 \Sigma_v=\left\{v_0:J\bigl(v_0+f_2(y),y\bigr)=0
 \text{ for every }y\in\mathbb R\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
We prove \(\Sigma_v=\varnothing\) directly from the two block-independence assumptions.  Suppose instead that \(v_0\in\Sigma_v\).  Then
\[
 f_2'(y)g_3'\bigl(v_0+f_2(y)\bigr)=1
 \quad\text{for every }y,
\tag{17}
\]
so \(f_2'\) never vanishes and has a constant sign.  Differentiating
\(g_3(v_0+f_2(y))-y\) and using \((17)\) shows that it is constant.  Thus there is \(z_0\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 g_3(v_0+f_2(y))=y-z_0.
\tag{18}
\]
The range of the strictly monotone map \(f_2\) is an interval.  If this interval had a finite endpoint, a sequence \(y_n\to+\infty\) or \(y_n\to-\infty\) would make \(v_0+f_2(y_n)\) converge to a finite number, whereas \((18)\) would make the continuous function \(g_3(v_0+f_2(y_n))=y_n-z_0\) diverge.  Hence \(f_2(\mathbb R)=\mathbb R\), and \((18)\) becomes the global identity
\[
 g_3(x)=f_2^{-1}(x-v_0)-z_0.
\tag{19}
\]

Let \(Y=X_3\), \(E=\varepsilon_2\), \(X=X_2=f_2(Y)+E\), and \(Z=\eta_3=Y-g_3(X)\).  Mutual block-noise independence in \(R\) gives \(Y\perp\!\!\!\perp E\), because a root mechanism is constant; mutual block-noise independence in \(R'\) gives \(X\perp\!\!\!\perp Z\).  Put \(u=f_2^{-1}(X-v_0)\), and let \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) be the constant sign of \(f_2'\).  By \((19)\),
\[
 E-v_0=f_2(u)-f_2(Y),
 \qquad
 Z-z_0=Y-u,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sgn}(E-v_0)=-\sigma\operatorname{sgn}(Z-z_0).
\tag{20}
\]
The marginal law of \(E\) is absolutely continuous.  Strict positivity of the bivariate density \(p_\varepsilon\) gives positive probability to rectangles on both sides of the line \(E=v_0\); hence
\[
 0<\mathbb P(E>v_0)<1,
 \qquad \mathbb P(E=v_0)=0.
\tag{21}
\]
By \((20)\), the event \(A=\{E>v_0\}\) agrees almost surely with either \(\{Z<z_0\}\) or \(\{Z>z_0\}\).  Since \(Z\perp\!\!\!\perp X\), this makes \(A\) independent of \(X\).

This is impossible.  Indeed, let \(W=f_2(Y)\).  Then \(W\perp\!\!\!\perp E\) and \(X=W+E\).  Let \(E_+\) and \(E_-\) have the conditional laws of \(E\) given \(A\) and \(A^c\), respectively, and couple them with one common independent copy of \(W\).  Independence of \(A\) and \(X\) would imply
\[
 \mathcal L(W+E_+)=\mathcal L(X\mid A)
 =\mathcal L(X\mid A^c)=\mathcal L(W+E_-).
\tag{22}
\]
But \(E_+>v_0>E_-\) almost surely, so \(W+E_+>W+E_-\) almost surely.  Applying the bounded strictly increasing function \(q\mapsto\arctan q\) gives a strict inequality between the expectations of the two sides, contradicting their equality in law in \((22)\).  Therefore \(\Sigma_v=\varnothing\).

The symmetric argument gives the corresponding conclusion for
\[
 \Sigma_z=\left\{z_0:J\bigl(x,z_0+g_3(x)\bigr)=0
 \text{ for every }x\in\mathbb R\right\}.
\tag{23}
\]
For completeness, if \(z_0\in\Sigma_z\), then
\(f_2(z_0+g_3(x))=x-v_0\) for a constant \(v_0\), the range of \(g_3\) must be all of \(\mathbb R\), and the sign of \(\eta_3-z_0\) becomes a deterministic threshold event of \(\varepsilon_2-v_0\).  The independence \(X_2\perp\!\!\!\perp\eta_3\) makes this event independent of \(X_2\), while \(X_3\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\) makes it independent of \(X_3=g_3(X_2)+\eta_3\).  Repeating the strictly ordered convolution argument with the reverse representation yields a contradiction.  Thus \(\Sigma_z=\varnothing\).

Assume now that \(\Omega\ne\varnothing\).  The conclusion \(\Sigma_v=\varnothing\) says that for every \(v\in\mathbb R\) there is a nonsingular point \((v+f_2(y),y)\).  The projections of inverse charts to their \(v\)-coordinates therefore form an open cover of \(\mathbb R\).  The local forms \((14)\)--\((15)\), overlap uniqueness, and the connectedness of \(\mathbb R\) glue to a global \(h\in C^3(\mathbb R)\), a global unit density \(\rho\) with \(\log\rho\in C^3(\mathbb R)\), and a positive \(C^3\) function \(c_\varepsilon\) such that
\[
 p_\varepsilon(t,v)=c_\varepsilon(v)\rho(t-h(v))
 \quad\text{for every }(t,v)\in\mathbb R^2.
\tag{24}
\]
To justify that the overlap graph is connected, declare two points equivalent when they can be joined by a finite chain of overlapping projected chart intervals.  Each equivalence class and its complement are open; connectedness of \(\mathbb R\) leaves only one class.  Path independence follows from the no-period argument.  Similarly, \(\Sigma_z=\varnothing\) yields \(k\in C^3(\mathbb R)\), a unit density \(\widetilde\rho\) with \(\log\widetilde\rho\in C^3\), and positive \(C^3\) \(c_\eta\) such that
\[
 p_\eta(u,z)=c_\eta(z)\widetilde\rho(u-k(z))
 \quad\text{for every }(u,z)\in\mathbb R^2.
\tag{25}
\]
In particular, integration of \((24)\)--\((25)\) shows that every slice is regular and
\(m_\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon\), \(m_\eta=c_\eta\).

Choose one point of \(\Omega\).  Substitution of \((24)\)--\((25)\) into the pointwise log-ratio identity \((7)\) shows that \(\widetilde\rho\) is a translate of \(\rho\); the possible proportionality constant is one because both integrate to one.  Absorb that single translation into \(k\).  At an arbitrary \((x,y)\in\mathbb R^2\), equation \((7)\) now says
\[
 \frac{\rho(t-h(v))}{\rho(t_0-h(v))}
 =\frac{\rho(t+\delta-k(z))}{\rho(t_0+\delta-k(z))}
 \quad\text{for every }t.
\tag{26}
\]
The two translated densities in \((26)\) are proportional; integration makes the proportionality constant one.  Their shift difference is therefore a period of \(\rho\), so the no-period argument gives
\[
 \delta=k(z)-h(v)
 \quad\text{for every }(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2.
\tag{27}
\]
This also proves the stated critical-set continuation.  Namely, along any nonsingular-chart sequence approaching a critical point, the shifts converge to the chart-independent values of the global \(C^3\) functions \(h\) and \(k\).  Since \(\log\rho\in C^3\), translation is continuous in the \(C^2\)-topology on every compact \(t\)-interval; hence the log-density ratios and their first two \(t\)-derivatives converge locally uniformly.  Equation \((27)\) makes the limiting shift difference independent of the overlap chain.

Define
\[
 \zeta=\varepsilon_1-h(\varepsilon_2).
\tag{28}
\]
The triangular change of variables \((t,v)\mapsto(t-h(v),v)\) has determinant one.  By \((24)\), the joint density of \((\zeta,\varepsilon_2)\) is
\[
 (u,v)\longmapsto\rho(u)m_\varepsilon(v).
\tag{29}
\]
Thus \(\zeta\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\), and its density \(\rho\) is strictly positive, has unit integral, and has a \(C^3\) log-density.  By \(\mathrm{def:smooth-positive-laws}\), \(\mathcal L(\zeta)\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1)\).  The symmetric change of variables applied to \((25)\) gives
\[
 \eta_1=k(\eta_3)+\zeta',
 \qquad
 \zeta'\perp\!\!\!\perp\eta_3,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(\zeta')\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1).
\tag{30}
\]
Equations \((28)\)--\((30)\) are forbidden by \(\mathrm{ass:innovation-atomicity}\), applied to the nontrivial coordinate splits of the two bivariate innovations.

It remains to verify separately the identically singular regime specified in the statement.  If \(\Omega=\varnothing\), then
\[
 f_2'(y)g_3'(x)=1\quad\text{for all }(x,y).
\tag{31}
\]
Fixing first \(x\) and then \(y\) in \((31)\) shows that both derivatives are constant and reciprocal.  Hence, for constants \(a\ne0,c,d_0\),
\[
 f_2(y)=ay+c,
 \qquad
 g_3(x)=a^{-1}x+d_0.
\tag{32}
\]
The two structural equations imply
\[
 X_2=aX_3+c+\varepsilon_2,
 \qquad
 \eta_3=-a^{-1}\varepsilon_2-a^{-1}c-d_0.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus \(X_2\perp\!\!\!\perp\eta_3\) makes \(E=\varepsilon_2\) independent of \(E+W\), where \(W=aX_3+c\), while the first representation gives \(E\perp\!\!\!\perp W\).  Let \(\varphi_E\) and \(\varphi_W\) be their characteristic functions.  Independence in both assertions yields
\[
 \varphi_E(q+t)\varphi_W(t)
 =\varphi_E(q)\varphi_E(t)\varphi_W(t).
\tag{34}
\]
Continuity and \(\varphi_W(0)=1\) allow cancellation for all sufficiently small \(|t|\).  Taking \(q=-t\) gives \(|\varphi_E(t)|=1\) near zero.  For arbitrary \(u\), choose an integer \(n\) with \(|u/n|\) in that neighborhood and iterate the multiplicative identity from \((34)\); then \(|\varphi_E(u)|=1\).  If \(E'\) is an independent copy, this implies
\(\mathbb E[e^{iu(E-E')}]=1\) for every rational \(u\).  Equality in the triangle inequality, followed on the countable set of rational \(u\)'s, gives \(E=E'\) almost surely.  Hence \(E\) is almost surely constant.  This contradicts the absolute continuity and full support supplied by the strictly positive density \(p_\varepsilon\).  Therefore \(\Omega=\varnothing\) is impossible.

In the nonsingular regime, innovation atomicity is contradicted by \((28)\) and \((30)\); in the identically singular regime, block-noise independence and positivity are already contradictory.  Hence the two crossed representations cannot both belong to \(\mathfrak A(P)\), which proves every assertion of the theorem.
