Fix an observational law \(P\).  We verify the three reductions separately.

First, let \(\pi_0\) be supplied and let \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\) satisfy
\(\pi_R=\pi_{R'}=\pi_0\).  By Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class}, each
representation is a grouped additive-noise model on the labelled blocks
\(\pi_0\), its graph is acyclic and causally minimal, its mechanisms and
innovation log-densities have the stated \(C^3\) regularity, its innovation
densities are strictly positive, its block noises are mutually independent,
and each parent--node--nondescendant conditional triple fails the displayed
Göbler--Windisch--Drton Equation~(3).  These are precisely the membership
conditions of Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class}; innovation atomicity
is an additional restriction.  Hence
\[
 R,R'\in\mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P).
\]
The cited fixed-partition identification result,
Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability}, now gives
\(G_R=G_{R'}\).  Therefore, whenever the restricted fiber is nonempty,
\[
 \bigl\{G_R:R\in\mathfrak A(P),\ \pi_R=\pi_0\bigr\}
\]
is a singleton.  This is exactly the graph-coordinate conclusion obtained
when the partition coordinate is supplied rather than recovered.

Second, suppose that \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P)\).  Membership
in \(\mathfrak S(P)\) fixes both partitions to
\(\pi_{\mathrm{sing}}\) by Definition~\ref{def:scalar-restricted-class}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability}, the cited form of
Theorem~28 of Peters et al., yields \(G_R=G_{R'}\).  Thus on the common
singleton subfiber the graph coordinate is the same unique labelled scalar
DAG as in the published scalar restricted-ANM result.

Third, suppose \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\) and both graphs are empty.  The
structural equations then read \(X_B=\varepsilon_{R,B}\) for
\(B\in\pi_R\), and Assumption~\ref{ass:block-noise-independence} makes the
coordinate subvectors indexed by distinct blocks mutually independent.
Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-dag-common-refinement} applies to the two empty-DAG
representations and gives
\[
 \pi_R=\pi_{R'}.
\]
It also gives the advertised ``finest'' interpretation.  Indeed, if a
strictly finer smooth-positive independent coordinate partition split some
\(B\in\pi_R\) as \(B=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}D\), then
\[
 \varepsilon_{R,D}=0(\varepsilon_{R,C})+\varepsilon_{R,D},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{R,D}\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_{R,C}.
\]
The factor densities of the proposed smooth-positive factorization have
everywhere positive \(C^3\) log-densities, so the residual law belongs to
\(\operatorname{SmoothPos}(|D|)\) by
Definition~\ref{def:smooth-positive-laws}.  The displayed zero-mechanism
representation contradicts Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation-atomicity} for the
block \(B\).  If two candidate independent partitions cross, the
common-refinement conclusion in
Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-dag-common-refinement} supplies the same forbidden split.
Thus no strictly finer or incomparable smooth-positive independent
factorization can replace the atomic empty-DAG partition, which proves the
third reduction.
