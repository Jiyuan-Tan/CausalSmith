Fix \(P\) and define
\[
 \mathcal D_P=\{\pi_R:R\in\mathfrak A(P)\}.
\]
For any \(\pi\in\mathcal D_P\), existence in this definition supplies a
representation \(R\in\mathfrak A(P)\) with \(\pi_R=\pi\).  If \(R'\) is a
second such representation, the first conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-uniqueness} gives \(G_R=G_{R'}\).  Therefore there
is exactly one labelled block DAG associated with \(\pi\).  Define
\(\Gamma_P(\pi)\) to be that DAG.  The preceding uniqueness makes this
definition independent of the representing \(R\), and it also shows that any
function satisfying \(G_R=\Gamma_P(\pi_R)\) for all admissible \(R\) must
coincide with \(\Gamma_P\) at every \(\pi\in\mathcal D_P\).  Thus
\(\Gamma_P\) exists and is unique.  If \(\mathfrak A(P)=\varnothing\), then
\(\mathcal D_P=\varnothing\) and the same assertion is the uniqueness of the
empty function.

Using Definition~\ref{def:recovery-map} and the defining equality for
\(\Gamma_P\), we obtain equation by equation
\[
 \mathcal T(P)
 =\{(\pi_R,G_R):R\in\mathfrak A(P)\}
 =\{(\pi_R,\Gamma_P(\pi_R)):R\in\mathfrak A(P)\}
 =\{(\pi,\Gamma_P(\pi)):\pi\in\mathcal D_P\}.
\]
This proves that the observable recovery relation is the graph of a partial
function; it does not prove that its domain has one element.

For the three additional exclusions, choose representations of any proposed
domain points.  If their graphs are both empty, the second conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:partition-uniqueness} makes their partitions equal.  The
third conclusion of that theorem rules out the relabelled three-coordinate
crossed single-edge pair.  Its fourth conclusion rules out a strict refinement
having a coarse root whose constituent fine blocks have no external fine
parents, as well as the symmetric configuration.  These are exactly the three
asserted restrictions on the graph of \(\Gamma_P\).
