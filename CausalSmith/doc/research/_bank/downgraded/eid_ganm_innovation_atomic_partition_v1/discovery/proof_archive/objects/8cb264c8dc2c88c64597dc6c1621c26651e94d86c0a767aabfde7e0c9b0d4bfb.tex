The following conditional closure is proved from the frozen primitives.  Add the proposed local slice-mass boundedness condition.  Then every assertion in the target theorem follows, including the global \(C^3\) innovation splits.  Without that condition, the proof below still establishes the pointwise factorization, the global log-ratio identity, all nonsingular-chart conclusions, discreteness of the only unresolved singular fibers, and the complete contradiction when \(\Omega=\varnothing\).

Write \(r=p_{\varepsilon_{R,\{3\}}}\), \(s=p_{\varepsilon_{R',\{2\}}}\), \(x=x_2\), and \(y=x_3\).  By the frozen triangular observed-density lemma, the two residual maps are global triangular diffeomorphisms with determinant one.  Mutual block-noise independence therefore gives, pointwise on the full support \(\mathbb R^3\),
\[
 r(y)p_\varepsilon\bigl(x_1-f_1(y),x-f_2(y)\bigr)
 =s(x)p_\eta\bigl(x_1-g_1(x),y-g_3(x)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
All four factors are finite and strictly positive by the innovation support assumption.  Put
\[
 v=x-f_2(y),\qquad z=y-g_3(x),\qquad
 \delta=f_1(y)-g_1(x).
\]
At \(x_1=f_1(y)+t\), equation \((1)\) becomes
\[
 r(y)p_\varepsilon(t,v)=s(x)p_\eta(t+\delta,z).
\tag{2}
\]
Dividing \((2)\) by the same equality with \(t=t_0\) is legitimate by strict positivity.  Consequently, without any exceptional set and without differentiating a marginal density,
\[
 \nu_\varepsilon(t,v)-\nu_\varepsilon(t_0,v)
 =\nu_\eta(t+\delta,z)-\nu_\eta(t_0+\delta,z)
\tag{3}
\]
for every \((t,x,y)\in\mathbb R^3\).  The previously established nonnegative-integration calculation gives \(\int m_\varepsilon(v)\,dv=\int m_\eta(z)\,dz=1\); hence the two regular slice sets are conull, and integrating \((2)\) in \(t\) gives the normalized identity on \(D\).

Set
\[
 S(t,v)=\partial_t\nu_\varepsilon(t,v),\qquad
 T(u,z)=\partial_u\nu_\eta(u,z),\qquad
 J(x,y)=1-f_2'(y)g_3'(x).
\]
The \(C^3\) hypotheses make \(S,T\in C^2\), and differentiating \((3)\), first in \(t\) and then in \(x\) and \(y\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  S_v(t,v)&=-g_1'(x)T_u(t+\delta,z)-g_3'(x)T_z(t+\delta,z),\\
  -f_2'(y)S_v(t,v)&=f_1'(y)T_u(t+\delta,z)+T_z(t+\delta,z).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(S_t(t,v)=T_u(t+\delta,z)\), elimination in \((4)\) gives on \(\Omega=\{J\ne0\}\)
\[
 S_v(t,v)=a(x,y)S_t(t,v),\qquad
 a(x,y)=\frac{g_3'(x)f_1'(y)-g_1'(x)}{J(x,y)}.
\tag{5}
\]
The symmetric elimination gives
\[
 T_z(u,z)=b(x,y)T_u(u,z),\qquad
 b(x,y)=\frac{f_2'(y)g_1'(x)-f_1'(y)}{J(x,y)}.
\tag{6}
\]
For every regular \(v\), the function \(S_t(\mathord\cdot,v)\) is not identically zero: otherwise \(\nu_\varepsilon(\mathord\cdot,v)\) would be affine and its positive exponential could not have a finite integral over \(\mathbb R\).  Thus, on an inverse chart, the quotient \(S_v/S_t\) at any point where its denominator is nonzero shows that \(a\) depends only on \(v\).  Regular \(v\)'s are dense, so continuity extends this conclusion throughout the chart.  The same argument makes \(b\) a function only of \(z\).  Because the displayed coefficients and the inverse chart are \(C^2\), these one-variable coefficients are \(C^2\).  Solving \(h'=-a\) and \(k'=-b\), equations \((5)\)--\((6)\) give
\[
 S(t,v)=S_0(t-h(v)),\qquad T(u,z)=T_0(u-k(z))
\tag{7}
\]
with \(h,k\in C^3\) locally.  Integrating \((7)\) in the first argument and normalizing at a regular slice gives a strictly positive unit-integral profile \(\rho\), with \(\log\rho\in C^3(\mathbb R)\), such that
\[
 q_\varepsilon(t\mid v)=\rho(t-h(v))
\tag{8}
\]
on every regular slice in the chart; the analogous assertion holds for \(q_\eta\).  Equation \((3)\) then gives \(\delta=k(z)-h(v)\) after one common additive normalization.

The normalization is unique.  Indeed, if a positive integrable density \(\rho\) had a nonzero period \(c\), then the translates of an interval of length less than \(|c|\) would have the same strictly positive mass and would be pairwise disjoint, contradicting \(\int\rho=1\).  Hence two chart shifts representing the same slice differ by one common constant.  This proves overlap gluing and path independence wherever a nonsingular chart exists.

It remains to locate the points not covered by any such chart.  Define the fully singular \(v\)-fiber set
\[
 \Sigma_v=\{v_0:J(v_0+f_2(y),y)=0\text{ for every }y\in\mathbb R\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This set is closed.  If \(v_0\in\Sigma_v\), then \(f_2'\) never vanishes and
\[
 g_3'\bigl(v_0+f_2(y)\bigr)=\frac{1}{f_2'(y)}.
\tag{10}
\]
If distinct \(v_n\in\Sigma_v\) converged to \(v_0\), let \(I=f_2(\mathbb R)\) and define \(H(u)=1/f_2'(f_2^{-1}(u))\) on the interval \(I\).  Equation \((10)\), applied at \(v_n\) and \(v_0\), gives
\[
 H(u+v_n-v_0)=H(u)
\tag{11}
\]
whenever both arguments belong to \(I\).  Taking difference quotients along the nonzero periods \(v_n-v_0\to0\) yields \(H'(u)=0\) for every \(u\in I\).  Hence \(f_2'\) is constant.  Its nonzero constant derivative makes \(I=\mathbb R\), and \((10)\) then makes \(g_3'\) the reciprocal constant on \(\mathbb R\), contrary to \(\Omega\ne\varnothing\).  Therefore, when \(\Omega\ne\varnothing\), \(\Sigma_v\) is closed and discrete.  The symmetric set \(\Sigma_z\) is also closed and discrete.  Thus \((7)\)--\((8)\) already define and glue the shifts away from isolated fibers; arbitrary critical points whose \(v\)- or \(z\)-fiber is not fully singular are covered by a different inverse chart on that same fiber.

Now use the proposed local slice-mass boundedness.  It makes \(m_\varepsilon\) and \(m_\eta\) finite everywhere and locally bounded.  Let \(v_n\to v_0\in\Sigma_v\) through one adjacent component and write \((8)\) there with one fixed profile \(\rho\).  On a compact interval \(K\), positivity and continuity of \(p_\varepsilon\) give \(p_\varepsilon(t,v_n)\ge c_K>0\) for all \(t\in K\) and all large \(n\), while local boundedness gives \(m_\varepsilon(v_n)\le M\).  Hence
\[
 \int_K\rho(t-h(v_n))\,dt
 =\int_K\frac{p_\varepsilon(t,v_n)}{m_\varepsilon(v_n)}\,dt
 \ge \frac{c_K|K|}{M}>0.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(|h(v_n)|\to\infty\), a subsequence makes the intervals \(K-h(v_n)\) pairwise disjoint, and summing \((12)\) contradicts \(\int\rho=1\).  Thus the shifts are bounded at \(v_0\).  If two subsequences had limits \(a\) and \(a'\), continuity of the global ratio \((3)\) would give
\[
 \frac{\rho(t-a)}{\rho(t_0-a)}
 =\frac{p_\varepsilon(t,v_0)}{p_\varepsilon(t_0,v_0)}
 =\frac{\rho(t-a')}{\rho(t_0-a')}
\tag{13}
\]
for every \(t\).  Integration shows that the proportionality constant in \((13)\) is one, so \(a-a'\) is a period of \(\rho\).  Uniqueness of translation periods gives \(a=a'\).  Therefore \(h\) has a unique finite continuous extension through \(v_0\), and the profiles on the two adjacent components align by a unique translation.

This extension is \(C^3\).  With \(\lambda=\log\rho\), choose \(t_*\) so that
\[
 -\lambda'(t_*-h(v_0))+\lambda'(t_0-h(v_0))\ne0.
\tag{14}
\]
Such a \(t_*\) exists, since failure for every \(t_*\) would make \(\lambda'\) constant and \(e^\lambda\) nonintegrable.  The \(C^3\) identity
\[
 \nu_\varepsilon(t_*,v)-\nu_\varepsilon(t_0,v)
 =\lambda(t_*-h(v))-\lambda(t_0-h(v))
\tag{15}
\]
and the nonzero derivative in \((14)\) uniquely solve for \(h(v)\) as a \(C^3\) function near \(v_0\).  The already obtained continuous extension must equal that local solution.  Repeating at the closed discrete set \(\Sigma_v\), and symmetrically for \(k\) at \(\Sigma_z\), produces global \(C^3\) shifts.

For almost every \(v\), equation \((8)\) says that the conditional density of
\[
 \zeta=\varepsilon_1-h(\varepsilon_2)
\]
given \(\varepsilon_2=v\) is the fixed density \(\rho\).  The change of variables \((\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)\mapsto(\zeta,\varepsilon_2)\), whose Jacobian is one, therefore factorizes its joint density as \(\rho(u)m_\varepsilon(v)\).  Hence \(\zeta\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\), and \(\mathcal L(\zeta)\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1)\).  The symmetric construction gives \(\eta_1=k(\eta_3)+\zeta'\), with \(\zeta'\perp\!\!\!\perp\eta_3\) and \(\mathcal L(\zeta')\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1)\).  Either split contradicts innovation atomicity.

Finally, the regime \(\Omega=\varnothing\) needs no added slice condition.  The identity \(f_2'(y)g_3'(x)=1\) for all \((x,y)\) implies, by fixing first \(x\) and then \(y\),
\[
 f_2(y)=ay+c,qquad g_3(x)=a^{-1}x+d_0,qquad a\ne0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus
\[
 \eta_3=-a^{-1}\varepsilon_2-a^{-1}c-d_0,qquad
 X_2=aX_3+c+\varepsilon_2.
\tag{17}
\]
Block-noise independence in the first representation gives \(X_3\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\), while block-noise independence in the second gives \(X_2\perp\!\!\!\perp\eta_3\).  With \(E=\varepsilon_2\) and \(Z=aX_3+c\), this says \(E\perp\!\!\!\perp Z\) and \(E\perp\!\!\!\perp E+Z\).  Their joint characteristic function satisfies
\[
 \varphi_E(s+t)\varphi_Z(t)
 =\varphi_E(s)\varphi_E(t)\varphi_Z(t).
\tag{18}
\]
Continuity and \(\varphi_Z(0)=1\) permit cancellation for all sufficiently small \(|t|\).  Taking \(s=-t\) gives \(|\varphi_E(t)|=1\) there, and subdividing an arbitrary argument into sufficiently small increments in the multiplicative relation gives \(|\varphi_E(u)|=1\) for every \(u\).  Equality in the triangle inequality then makes \(e^{iuE}\) almost surely constant for every rational \(u\), so \(E\) is almost surely constant.  This contradicts its everywhere positive Lebesgue density.  Hence the fully singular regime is impossible under the frozen assumptions alone.

The frozen assumptions do not imply the added local slice bound.  To see the precise analytic obstruction, fix \(0<\alpha<1\) and a positive \(C^\infty\) unit density \(\rho\) satisfying \(\rho(u)=Ce^{\alpha u}\) for \(u\le-1\).  For \(v\ne0\) near zero let \(H(v)=-\log|v|\), choose a positive smooth integrable weight \(w\) equal to one near zero, and set
\[
 p(t,v)=Z^{-1}w(v)e^{\alpha H(v)}\rho(t-H(v)),\qquad
 p(t,0)=Z^{-1}Cw(0)e^{\alpha t}.
\tag{19}
\]
Extend \(H\) smoothly away from zero and choose \(w\) so that \(Z=\int w(v)e^{\alpha H(v)}\,dv<\infty\).  For every bounded set of \(t\)'s and all sufficiently small \(|v|\), the exponential-tail identity makes the first formula in \((19)\) exactly \(Z^{-1}Cw(v)e^{\alpha t}\).  Thus \(p>0\) and \(\log p\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\), although
\[
 m_p(v)=Z^{-1}w(v)e^{\alpha H(v)}\asymp |v|^{-\alpha}
\tag{20}
\]
is unbounded and the conditional translates \(\rho(t-H(v))\) escape as \(v\to0\).  For every fixed \(t,t_0\), sufficiently small \(|v|\) put both \(t-H(v)\) and \(t_0-H(v)\) in the exponential tail, so
\[
 \log p(t,v)-\log p(t_0,v)=\alpha(t-t_0)
 =\log p(t,0)-\log p(t_0,0).
\tag{21}
\]
Thus even the log-ratios and all of their first two derivatives in \(t\) continue smoothly while the unique conditional-location shift diverges.  What remains open without the proposed condition is whether the additional global crossing functional equation rules out every such escape-tail configuration.
