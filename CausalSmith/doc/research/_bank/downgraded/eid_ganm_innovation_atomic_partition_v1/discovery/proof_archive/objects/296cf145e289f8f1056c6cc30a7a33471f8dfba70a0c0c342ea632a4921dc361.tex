Fix \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Part}(V)\). If \(R\in\mathfrak A(P)\) and \(\pi_R=\pi_0\), then the clauses of Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class}, except for the additional innovation-atomicity clause, are exactly the supplied-partition GANM, smoothness, positivity, independence, acyclicity, causal-minimality, and conditional Equation~(3)-failure clauses in Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class}. Hence
\[
\{R\in\mathfrak A(P):\pi_R=\pi_0\}
\subseteq \mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P). \tag{1}
\]
For any two members of the right-hand side, cited Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability} gives equality of their coordinate-labelled block DAGs. Consequently, after \(\pi_0\) is fixed, the graph-coordinate step in Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-partition-and-dag} is precisely the cited fixed-partition conclusion. The additional content of the atomic theorem is that its partition coordinate is recovered from \(P\).

The inclusion (1) is strict over law--representation pairs. Under Definition~\ref{def:merge-witness}, let \(B=\{1,2\}\), let \(\phi(s)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp(-s^2/2)\), and consider the empty graph with labelled blocks \(B\) and \(\{3\}\). The inverse of
\[
(n_1,n_2)\longmapsto (x_1,x_2)=(n_1,n_1+n_1^3+n_2)
\]
is \((n_1,n_2)=(x_1,x_2-x_1-x_1^3)\), and its Jacobian determinant is one. Therefore the block innovation has density
\[
p_{X_B}(x_1,x_2)=\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2-x_1-x_1^3), \tag{2}
\]
which is everywhere positive, integrates to one by the displayed change of variables, and has a \(C^\infty\) log-density. Since \((N_1,N_2)\perp\!\!\!\perp N_3\), this block innovation is independent of the \(\{3\}\)-innovation. The zero mechanisms are \(C^3\), the graph is acyclic, and both causal minimality and the edgewise Condition~1 requirement are vacuous because the graph has no edges. Thus this representation belongs to \(\mathfrak G_{\{B,\{3\}\}}(P_{\mathrm{merge}})\).

It does not belong to \(\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{merge}})\). Indeed, for the nontrivial split \(B=\{1\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{2\}\),
\[
\varepsilon_{B,2}=h(\varepsilon_{B,1})+N_2,
\qquad h(u)=u+u^3,
\qquad N_2\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_{B,1}. \tag{3}
\]
The map \(h\) is \(C^3\), and the standard Gaussian density is everywhere positive with a \(C^\infty\) log-density, so \(\mathcal L(N_2)\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1)\) by Definition~\ref{def:smooth-positive-laws}. Equation (3) violates innovation atomicity. This proves strictness of (1).

Now consider the common singleton subfiber and suppose it is nonempty. Choose
\[
R_0\in\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P).
\]
By (1) with \(\pi_0=\pi_{\mathrm{sing}}\), the singleton members of \(\mathfrak A(P)\) lie in \(\mathfrak G_{\pi_{\mathrm{sing}}}(P)\). The cited Göbler lemma makes their graph equal to \(G_{R_0}\), while cited Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability} makes every graph in \(\mathfrak S(P)\) equal to \(G_{R_0}\). Hence the two graph-identification conclusions agree on the common subfiber. This is an equality anchored by an actual common representation and does not equate the two papers' differential restrictions.

It remains to establish the strict non-scalar frontier. Fix \(\theta\in(0,\infty)^5\). The already established Lemma~\ref{lem:vector-family-atomicity} supplies an empty-DAG representation with blocks \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3\}\) in \(\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\), proves that \(U\) and \(V\) are dependent, and excludes both directional scalar additive-noise representations between \(U\) and \(V\). Thus
\[
\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\neq\varnothing. \tag{4}
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(R\in\mathfrak S(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\). We first show that no edge of its scalar DAG is incident to \(W\). We use the elementary translation fact that if an integrable, everywhere positive density \(q\) satisfies \(q(s)=q(s+c)\) for almost every \(s\), then \(c=0\): if \(c\neq0\), a bounded interval of length less than \(|c|\) has positive \(q\)-mass, and its disjoint translates would force the integral of \(q\) to be infinite.

If \(W\) is a parent of \(Y\in\{U,V\}\), write \(Z\) for the possibly empty set of other parents of \(Y\). Joint independence \(W\perp\!\!\!\perp(U,V)\) gives, at positive-density parent values,
\[
p_{Y\mid W,Z}(y\mid w,z)=p_{Y\mid Z}(y\mid z). \tag{5}
\]
The scalar ANM equation and independence of its noise from all parents write the left side as
\[
q_Y\bigl(y-f_Y(w,z)\bigr). \tag{6}
\]
Comparing (6) at two regular values \(w_1,w_2\) in (5), the translation fact yields \(f_Y(w_1,z)=f_Y(w_2,z)\). Strict positivity of the joint density makes regular parent values conull and dense, and the \(C^3\) continuity of \(f_Y\) extends this equality to all \((w_1,w_2,z)\). Hence \(f_Y\) is constant in its \(W\)-input, contradicting causal minimality of the edge \(W\to Y\).

If instead \(Y\in\{U,V\}\) is a parent of \(W\), with other-parent tuple \(Z\), joint independence gives
\[
p_{W\mid Y,Z}(w\mid y,z)=p_W(w). \tag{7}
\]
The ANM form is \(q_W(w-f_W(y,z))\); comparison at two regular \(y\)-values and the same translation argument makes \(f_W\) constant in its \(Y\)-input, again violating causal minimality. Thus \(W\) is isolated.

The induced acyclic graph on \(U,V\) cannot be empty, because an empty two-node scalar ANM has \(U\) and \(V\) equal to two mutually independent innovations, contrary to their dependence. It therefore has exactly one of the edges \(U\to V\) and \(V\to U\). Its child equation is respectively
\[
V=h(U)+\eta_V,\qquad \eta_V\perp\!\!\!\perp U,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
U=k(V)+\eta_U,\qquad \eta_U\perp\!\!\!\perp V. \tag{8}
\]
Both alternatives contradict Lemma~\ref{lem:vector-family-atomicity}. Hence
\[
\mathfrak S(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})=\varnothing. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (4) and (9) prove the strict representational frontier for every positive parameter vector, completing all parts of the theorem.
