Fix \(\pi_0\in\operatorname{Part}(V)\). If \(R\in\mathfrak A(P)\) and \(\pi_R=\pi_0\), then the clauses of Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class}, except for the additional innovation-atomicity clause, are exactly the supplied-partition grouped additive-noise, smoothness, positivity, independence, acyclicity, causal-minimality, and conditional Equation~(3)-failure clauses in Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class}. Hence
\[
\{R\in\mathfrak A(P):\pi_R=\pi_0\}
\subseteq \mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P). \tag{1}
\]
For any two members of the right-hand side, the cited Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability} gives equality of their coordinate-labelled block DAGs. Consequently, after \(\pi_0\) is fixed, the graph-coordinate step in Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-partition-and-dag} is precisely the cited fixed-partition conclusion. The additional content of the atomic theorem is that its partition coordinate is recovered from \(P\). In particular, no derivative-ratio or Hessian-rank condition has been inserted: the invoked hypotheses are exactly the conditional Equation~(3)-failure clauses built into Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class}.

The inclusion (1) is strict over law--representation pairs. Under Definition~\ref{def:merge-witness}, let \(B=\{1,2\}\), let \(\phi(s)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp(-s^2/2)\), and consider the empty graph with labelled blocks \(B\) and \(\{3\}\). The inverse of
\[
(n_1,n_2)\longmapsto (x_1,x_2)=(n_1,n_1+n_1^3+n_2)
\]
is \((n_1,n_2)=(x_1,x_2-x_1-x_1^3)\), and its Jacobian determinant is one. Therefore the \(B\)-block innovation has density
\[
p_{X_B}(x_1,x_2)=\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2-x_1-x_1^3). \tag{2}
\]
It is everywhere positive, integrates to one by the displayed change of variables, and has a \(C^\infty\) log-density. Since \((N_1,N_2)\perp\!\!\!\perp N_3\), this block innovation is independent of the \(\{3\}\)-innovation. The zero mechanisms are \(C^3\), the graph is acyclic, and both causal minimality and the edgewise Condition~1 requirement are vacuous because the graph has no edges. Thus this representation belongs to \(\mathfrak G_{\{B,\{3\}\}}(P_{\mathrm{merge}})\).

It does not belong to \(\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{merge}})\). Indeed, for the nontrivial split \(B=\{1\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{2\}\),
\[
\varepsilon_{B,2}=h(\varepsilon_{B,1})+N_2,
\qquad h(u)=u+u^3,
\qquad N_2\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_{B,1}. \tag{3}
\]
The map \(h\) is \(C^3\), and the standard Gaussian density is everywhere positive with a \(C^\infty\) log-density, so \(\mathcal L(N_2)\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1)\) by Definition~\ref{def:smooth-positive-laws}. Equation (3) violates innovation atomicity. This proves strictness of (1).

Now consider the common singleton subfiber. If it is empty, agreement on it is vacuous. Otherwise choose
\[
R_0\in\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P).
\]
By (1) with \(\pi_0=\pi_{\mathrm{sing}}\), the singleton members of \(\mathfrak A(P)\) lie in \(\mathfrak G_{\pi_{\mathrm{sing}}}(P)\). The cited Göbler lemma makes their graph equal to \(G_{R_0}\), while the cited Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability} makes every graph in \(\mathfrak S(P)\) equal to \(G_{R_0}\). Hence the two graph-identification conclusions agree on the common subfiber. This equality is anchored by an actual common representation and does not identify the vector Equation~(3) with the scalar Equation~(7).

It remains to establish the strict non-scalar frontier. Fix \(\theta\in(0,\infty)^5\). The already established Lemma~\ref{lem:vector-family-atomicity} supplies an empty-DAG representation with blocks \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3\}\) in \(\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\), proves that \(U\) and \(V\) are dependent, and excludes both directional scalar additive-noise representations between \(U\) and \(V\). Thus
\[
\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\neq\varnothing. \tag{4}
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(R\in\mathfrak S(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\). We first show that no edge of its scalar DAG is incident to \(W\). We use the following elementary translation fact. If an integrable, everywhere positive density \(q\) satisfies \(q(s)=q(s+c)\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(s\), then \(c=0\). Indeed, if \(c\neq0\), choose a bounded interval \(I\) of length less than \(|c|\). Positivity gives \(\int_I q(s)\,ds>0\), periodicity gives the same positive integral over every \(I+nc\), and those intervals are pairwise disjoint as \(n\) ranges over the integers, contradicting \(\int_{\mathbb R}q(s)\,ds=1\).

If \(W\) is a parent of \(Y\in\{U,V\}\), write \(Z\) for the possibly empty tuple of other parents of \(Y\). Joint independence \(W\perp\!\!\!\perp(U,V)\) gives, at marginal-almost every positive-density parent value,
\[
p_{Y\mid W,Z}(y\mid w,z)=p_{Y\mid Z}(y\mid z). \tag{5}
\]
The scalar additive-noise equation and independence of its innovation from its parents write the left-hand side as
\[
q_Y\bigl(y-f_Y(w,z)\bigr). \tag{6}
\]
Comparing (6) at two regular values \(w_1,w_2\) in (5), and translating the argument of \(q_Y\), the translation fact yields \(f_Y(w_1,z)=f_Y(w_2,z)\). Strict positivity of the parent density makes such regular values conull and therefore dense, and the \(C^3\) continuity of \(f_Y\) extends the equality to all \((w_1,w_2,z)\). Hence \(f_Y\) is constant in its \(W\)-input, contradicting causal minimality of the edge \(W\to Y\).

If instead \(Y\in\{U,V\}\) is a parent of \(W\), with possibly empty other-parent tuple \(Z\), joint independence gives
\[
p_{W\mid Y,Z}(w\mid y,z)=p_W(w). \tag{7}
\]
The additive-noise form writes the left-hand side as \(q_W(w-f_W(y,z))\). Comparison at two regular \(y\)-values and the same translation argument makes \(f_W\) constant in its \(Y\)-input, again violating causal minimality. Thus \(W\) is isolated.

The induced acyclic graph on \(U,V\) cannot be empty, because then the two root equations would make \(U\) and \(V\) mutually independent innovations, contrary to their dependence. An acyclic graph on two vertices therefore has exactly one of the edges \(U\to V\) and \(V\to U\). Its child equation is respectively
\[
V=h(U)+\eta_V,
\qquad \eta_V\perp\!\!\!\perp U,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
U=k(V)+\eta_U,
\qquad \eta_U\perp\!\!\!\perp V. \tag{8}
\]
Both alternatives contradict Lemma~\ref{lem:vector-family-atomicity}. Hence
\[
\mathfrak S(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})=\varnothing. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (4) and (9) prove the strict representational frontier for every positive parameter vector, completing all parts of the theorem.
