Fix a supplied partition \(\pi_0\).  Comparing the member properties in
Definitions~\ref{def:admissible-class} and
\ref{def:supplied-group-class} shows that every
\(R\in\mathfrak A(P)\) with \(\pi_R=\pi_0\) satisfies all requirements of
\(\mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P)\): both definitions impose the same \(C^3\)
mechanism regularity, positive \(C^3\) innovation log-densities, mutually
independent block noises, acyclicity, causal minimality, and the published
conditional Equation~(3)-failure condition.  Atomicity is an additional
condition in \(\mathfrak A(P)\).  Hence
\[
 \{R\in\mathfrak A(P):\pi_R=\pi_0\}
 \subseteq\mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P).
\]
If \(R,R'\) lie in the set on the left, then they lie in the same supplied-group
fiber, so Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability} yields
\(G_R=G_{R'}\).  This is only the published fixed-partition conclusion on a
subfiber.  Proposition~\ref{prop:atomicity-removal-converse} supplies a strict
witness: the merged empty-DAG representation of \(P_{\mathrm{merge}}\)
satisfies every supplied-group condition but its bivariate innovation obeys
the forbidden split
\[
 \varepsilon_{\{1,2\},2}
 =\varepsilon_{\{1,2\},1}+\varepsilon_{\{1,2\},1}^{3}+N_2,
 \qquad
 N_2\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_{\{1,2\},1},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(N_2)\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1).
\]
It therefore belongs to the supplied-group fiber and not to the atomic fiber.

On the common singleton subfiber, take
\(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P)\).  In particular
\(R,R'\in\mathfrak S(P)\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability} gives \(G_R=G_{R'}\).
This is exactly the asserted agreement with the Peters et al. fixed scalar
vertex conclusion; it makes no comparison between their scalar differential
condition and the vector Equation~(3).

Finally let \(\theta\in(0,\infty)^5\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:vector-family-atomicity}, the empty-DAG representation with
blocks \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3\}\) belongs to
\(\mathfrak A(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})\), so that fiber is nonempty.
Lemma~\ref{lem:vector-law-no-scalar-representation} gives the separate
conclusion \(\mathfrak S(P_{\mathrm{vec},\theta})=\varnothing\).  Combining
the two equalities proves the stated genuinely vector representational
frontier.  None of these three arguments asserts that the set of admissible
partitions is a singleton.
