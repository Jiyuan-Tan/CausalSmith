Fix \(B=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}D\) and suppose
\[
 Z_D=h(Z_C)+\eta_D,
 \qquad \eta_D\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_C.
\]
The assumed finite second moment of \(Z_D\) implies that the conditional second moment \(\mathbb E[\|Z_D\|^2\mid Z_C=c]\) is finite for \(P_{Z_C}\)-almost every \(c\).  Choose one such regular \(c\).  The conditional-law identity
\[
 \mathcal L(Z_D\mid Z_C=c)=\mathcal L(h(c)+\eta_D)
\]
then implies \(\mathbb E\|\eta_D\|^2<\infty\), because \(h(c)\) is a finite vector.  Consequently, for almost every regular \(c\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_D\mid Z_C=c)
 &=\operatorname{Cov}(h(c)+\eta_D\mid Z_C=c)\\
 &=\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_D),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the second equality uses independence.  The right-hand side does not depend on \(c\).  Hence a conditional covariance that is not essentially constant contradicts the proposed additive-noise representation.  The same calculation with \(C\) and \(D\) interchanged proves the reverse-direction assertion.  Applying both conclusions to every nontrivial split gives the stated sufficient criterion for atomicity.

For completeness, constant conditional covariance is not a converse.  Let \(r_0\) be the standard normal density and let
\[
 r_1(y)=\tfrac12\phi_{1/2}(y)+\tfrac12\phi_{3/2}(y),
\]
where \(\phi_{\sigma^2}\) is the centered normal density of variance \(\sigma^2\).  Both \(r_0\) and \(r_1\) have mean zero and variance one, but they are distinct.  With \(w(x)=(2+\tanh x)/4\), set
\[
 q_x(y)=w(x)r_0(y)+(1-w(x))r_1(y)
\]
and give \((Z_C,Z_D)\) the density \(\phi_1(x)q_x(y)\).  This density is everywhere positive with a smooth log-density, and \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_D\mid Z_C=x)=1\) for every \(x\).  If \(Z_D=h(Z_C)+\eta_D\) with an independent residual, finite first moments would give \(0=\mathbb E[Z_D\mid Z_C=x]=h(x)+\mathbb E\eta_D\), so \(h\) would be constant almost everywhere and, by continuity, everywhere.  The conditional densities would then be identical, contrary to the strict variation of \(w\) and \(r_0\neq r_1\).  Thus the covariance diagnostic can fail to vary even when the directional additive-noise model is false.
