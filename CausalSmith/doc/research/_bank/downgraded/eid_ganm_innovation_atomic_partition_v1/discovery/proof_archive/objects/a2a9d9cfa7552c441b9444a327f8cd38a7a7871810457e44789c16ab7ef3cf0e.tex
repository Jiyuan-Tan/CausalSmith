We verify the three reductions separately.

First fix a coordinate partition \(\pi_0\), and suppose it is supplied before graph recovery.  Let \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\) satisfy \(\pi_R=\pi_{R'}=\pi_0\).  By Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class}, each representation is an acyclic, causally minimal grouped additive-noise model with \(C^3\) mechanisms, mutually independent innovations having everywhere positive densities and \(C^3\) log-densities, and the exact parent--child--nondescendant Equation~(3)-failure requirement.  Comparing this list with Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class} gives
\[
 R,R'\in\mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P).
\]
The cited fixed-partition identifiability result, Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability}, therefore yields
\[
 G_R=G_{R'}.
\]
Thus, once the unique partition is supplied, the only remaining coordinate of the partition-and-DAG target is exactly the labelled block DAG identified by the published fixed-group theorem.  No innovation-atomicity argument is needed for this orientation step; atomicity is used only by the unknown-partition layer.

Second, suppose that the supplied partition is the singleton partition and that \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P)\).  Membership in \(\mathfrak S(P)\), as defined in Definition~\ref{def:scalar-restricted-class}, supplies the exact restricted scalar additive-noise hypotheses of Peters et al.  Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability} applies to the two representations and gives
\[
 G_R=G_{R'}.
\]
Hence the labelled graph recovered on this common singleton subfiber agrees with the scalar restricted-ANM graph; equality is literal because both graphs have the same coordinate-labelled singleton vertex set.

Third, let \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\) have empty block DAGs.  Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-dag-common-refinement} gives
\[
 \pi_R=\pi_{R'}.
\]
For an empty DAG the structural equations are \(X_B=\varepsilon_{R,B}\), and Assumption~\ref{ass:block-noise-independence}, incorporated into Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class}, says that these coordinate blocks are mutually independent.  Conversely, the same lemma proves that two admissible independent-block partitions factorize over their common refinement and that any unequal refinement would split at least one block innovation with the zero \(C^3\) mechanism and a residual law in the appropriate \(\operatorname{SmoothPos}\) class.  Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation-atomicity} excludes that split.  Therefore, among empty-DAG representations satisfying the displayed smoothness, positivity, and atomicity conditions, the independent coordinate partition is unique and admits no proper admissible independent refinement.  This is precisely the finest independent-coordinate-partition reduction asserted in the proposition.
