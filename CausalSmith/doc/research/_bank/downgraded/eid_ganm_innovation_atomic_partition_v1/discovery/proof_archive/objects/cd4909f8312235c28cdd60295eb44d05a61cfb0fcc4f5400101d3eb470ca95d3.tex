The graph-identification reduction is complete once partition uniqueness is available.  Assume temporarily that
\[
R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\pi_R=\pi_{R'}.
\]
Because \(\mathfrak A(P)\neq\varnothing\), choose \(R_0\in\mathfrak A(P)\) and set
\[
\pi^\star(P)=\pi_{R_0},\qquad G^\star(P)=G_{R_0}.
\]
For arbitrary \(R\in\mathfrak A(P)\), the temporary premise gives \(\pi_R=\pi^\star(P)\).  Comparing Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class} with Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class} then gives
\[
R,R_0\in\mathfrak G_{\pi^\star(P)}(P).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability} yields \(G_R=G_{R_0}=G^\star(P)\).  Finally Definition~\ref{def:recovery-map} gives
\[
\mathcal T(P)
=\{(\pi_R,G_R):R\in\mathfrak A(P)\}
=\{(\pi^\star(P),G^\star(P))\}.
\]
Thus the graph coordinate contains no further mathematical gap: the sole missing premise is the still-unproved general partition-uniqueness theorem.
