First suppose the unique partition \(\pi_0\) is supplied in advance. Unfolding Definitions~\ref{def:admissible-class} and~\ref{def:supplied-group-class} shows that every \(R\in\mathfrak A(P)\) with \(\pi_R=\pi_0\) belongs to \(\mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P)\): all supplied-group hypotheses are admissibility clauses, while innovation atomicity is an additional restriction. The cited Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability} therefore makes all such representations have the same coordinate-labelled block DAG. This is exactly the graph-coordinate conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-partition-and-dag} after its partition coordinate has been supplied.

Next specialize to the singleton partition and to laws admitting a representation in the common subfiber \(\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P)\). Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-partition-and-dag} identifies the labelled scalar DAG among the admissible representations, while the cited Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability} identifies the labelled scalar DAG among the restricted scalar representations. A representation in the intersection belongs to both fibers and supplies a common graph; uniqueness in each fiber therefore forces the two conclusions to agree.

Finally suppose both competing admissible block graphs are empty. Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-dag-common-refinement} shows that the positive observed density factorizes over all cells of the common-refinement partition. If two candidate partitions were unequal, some block innovation in one representation would split nontrivially into independent smooth positive subvectors. Taking the mechanism to be the zero \(C^3\) map turns that factorization into a directional additive-noise representation forbidden by innovation atomicity. Hence the empty-DAG admissible partition is unique. Equivalently, among independent coordinate partitions whose blocks are innovation-atomic, it is the unique finest partition. These arguments establish the three asserted special-case reductions.
