Let \(d=3\). Suppose \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\), \(\pi_R=\{\{1,2\},\{3\}\}\), \(\pi_{R'}=\{\{1,3\},\{2\}\}\), \(G_R\) has the single edge \(\{3\}\to\{1,2\}\), and \(G_{R'}\) has the single edge \(\{2\}\to\{1,3\}\). Write \(f=(f_1,f_2)\), \(g=(g_1,g_3)\), \(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)\), and \(\eta=(\eta_1,\eta_3)\) for the two nonroot mechanisms and innovations. The triangular density formula gives, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^3\), \[p_{\varepsilon_{R,\{3\}}}(x_3)p_{\varepsilon}(x_1-f_1(x_3),x_2-f_2(x_3))=p_{\varepsilon_{R',\{2\}}}(x_2)p_{\eta}(x_1-g_1(x_2),x_3-g_3(x_2)).\] Set \(v=x_2-f_2(x_3)\), \(z=x_3-g_3(x_2)\), and \(\delta(x_2,x_3)=f_1(x_3)-g_1(x_2)\). Define the extended slice normalizers and regular sets \[m_{\varepsilon}(v)=\int_{\mathbb R}p_{\varepsilon}(t,v)\,dt,\quad E_{\varepsilon}=\{v:0<m_{\varepsilon}(v)<\infty\},\qquad m_{\eta}(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}p_{\eta}(t,z)\,dt,\quad E_{\eta}=\{z:0<m_{\eta}(z)<\infty\}.\] Tonelli's theorem, \(\int p_{\varepsilon}=\int p_{\eta}=1\), and strict positivity imply that \(E_{\varepsilon}\) and \(E_{\eta}\) are conull. Define \(q_{\varepsilon}(t\mid v)=p_{\varepsilon}(t,v)/m_{\varepsilon}(v)\) only for \(v\in E_{\varepsilon}\), and define \(q_{\eta}(t\mid z)=p_{\eta}(t,z)/m_{\eta}(z)\) only for \(z\in E_{\eta}\). The normalized translation identity is \[q_{\varepsilon}(t\mid v)=q_{\eta}(t+\delta(x_2,x_3)\mid z),\qquad t\in\mathbb R,\quad (x_2,x_3)\in D:=\Phi^{-1}(E_{\varepsilon}\times E_{\eta}),\] where \(\Phi(x_2,x_3)=(v,z)\). Let \(\Omega=\{(x_2,x_3):1-f_2'(x_3)g_3'(x_2)\neq0\}\). It is open, and on every connected inverse chart \(U\subseteq\Omega\), \(\Phi|_U\) is a \(C^3\) diffeomorphism onto its open image and \(D_U=U\cap D\) is conull in \(U\), hence dense. Fix \(t_0\in\mathbb R\). Dividing the original positive factorization at \(t\) and \(t_0\), then taking logarithms, gives on \(D_U\) the identity \[\nu_{\varepsilon}(t,v)-\nu_{\varepsilon}(t_0,v)=\nu_{\eta}(t+\delta,z)-\nu_{\eta}(t_0+\delta,z).\] Continuity extends this log-ratio identity to every point of \(U\); only this extended identity, not \(m_{\varepsilon}\), \(m_{\eta}\), \(q_{\varepsilon}\), or \(q_{\eta}\), is differentiated. The extended identity implies the following chart and continuation conclusions. On each \(U\), there are \(C^3\) shift functions \(h_U\) and \(k_U\), normalized by \(h_U(v_U^0)=0\) at a regular base point, and a fixed smooth positive unit-integral profile chosen from one regular slice, such that the two regular conditional families are its translates and \(\delta=k_U(z)-h_U(v)\) on \(D_U\). On overlaps, the log-ratio identity forces any two normalized shift pairs to differ by one common additive constant; transport along every overlap chain is path independent. For any \(y\in\Omega^c\) approached by nonsingular charts, the same identity forces overlap-chain-independent limits of the shift differences and locally uniform limits of the translated log-density ratios and their first two \(t\)-derivatives, yielding a unique continuation through the critical set. These chart-gluing and critical-set statements are conclusions to be derived from the displayed log-ratio identity, not regularity assumptions on slice normalizers. They produce global \(C^3\) functions \(h,k\) and variables \(\zeta,\zeta'\) with \(\mathcal L(\zeta),\mathcal L(\zeta')\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(1)\) such that \(\varepsilon_1=h(\varepsilon_2)+\zeta\), \(\zeta\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\), and \(\eta_1=k(\eta_3)+\zeta'\), \(\zeta'\perp\!\!\!\perp\eta_3\). If \(\Omega=\varnothing\), then \(f_2(x_3)=ax_3+c\) and \(g_3(x_2)=a^{-1}x_2+d_0\) for some \(a\neq0\), so \(\eta_3=-a^{-1}\varepsilon_2-a^{-1}c-d_0\); independence of \(X_2=aX_3+c+\varepsilon_2\) from \(\eta_3\), with \(X_3\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\), would make \(\varepsilon_2\), whose strictly positive density gives full support, independent of its sum with an independent variable, which is impossible. Thus either regime forces a nontrivial directional innovation split, and the two crossed representations cannot both be innovation-atomic.
