Fix an observational law \(P\).  We verify the three reductions in the order stated.

First fix a supplied partition \(\pi_0\), and let \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\) satisfy
\[
\pi_R=\pi_{R'}=\pi_0.
\]
By Definition~\ref{def:admissible-class}, each representation has a coordinate-labelled acyclic block graph on \(\pi_0\), mutually independent block innovations with everywhere positive densities and \(C^3\) log-densities, \(C^3\) mechanisms, causal minimality, and the parent--node--nondescendant Equation~(3)-failure condition.  Comparing these conditions one by one with Definition~\ref{def:supplied-group-class} gives
\[
R,R'\in\mathfrak G_{\pi_0}(P).
\]
Innovation atomicity is an additional restriction and is not used in this inclusion.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gobler-fixed-partition-identifiability}, the cited fixed-partition identification result, therefore yields
\[
G_R=G_{R'}.
\]
Consequently, whenever \(\{R\in\mathfrak A(P):\pi_R=\pi_0\}\) is nonempty, its graph projection
\[
\{G_R:R\in\mathfrak A(P),\ \pi_R=\pi_0\}
\]
is a singleton.  Thus supplying the partition removes the partition-recovery question and leaves exactly the fixed-group labelled-DAG conclusion.

Second, let \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\cap\mathfrak S(P)\).  Definition~\ref{def:scalar-restricted-class} implies
\[
\pi_R=\pi_{R'}=\pi_{\mathrm{sing}}
\]
and supplies the restricted scalar additive-noise hypotheses, including nonvanishing innovation densities, \(C^3\) mechanisms, causal minimality, and the conditional restrictedness condition.  Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:peters-scalar-dag-identifiability}, the cited form of Theorem~28 of Peters et al., applies and gives
\[
G_R=G_{R'}.
\]
Therefore the graph coordinate on the common singleton subfiber is precisely the unique labelled scalar DAG delivered by the published scalar restricted-ANM theorem.

Third, let \(R,R'\in\mathfrak A(P)\) and suppose that both \(G_R\) and \(G_{R'}\) are empty.  Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-dag-common-refinement} applies directly and gives
\[
\pi_R=\pi_{R'}.
\]
This proves uniqueness among admissible empty-DAG partitions.  It remains to justify the stated ``finest independent partition'' interpretation.  In an empty-DAG representation, every root mechanism is a constant, so for distinct blocks \(B,B'\in\pi_R\), Assumption~\ref{ass:block-noise-independence} implies
\[
X_B\perp\!\!\!\perp X_{B'};
\]
adding the root constants does not affect independence.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:triangular-observed-density}, the common density \(p_P\) is everywhere positive and its log-density \(\ell_P\) belongs to \(C^3(\mathbb R^d)\).

Let \(\rho\) be any other smooth-positive independent coordinate partition of \(P\).  Thus there are normalized everywhere positive densities \(s_A\), with \(\log s_A\in C^3(\mathbb R^{|A|})\), such that
\[
 p_P(x)=\prod_{B\in\pi_R}r_B(x_B)=\prod_{A\in\rho}s_A(x_A),
\]
where the functions \(r_B\) are the translated innovation densities from \(R\).  Put \(\sigma=\pi_R\wedge\rho\).  If coordinates \(i\) and \(j\) lie in distinct cells of \(\sigma\), then they lie in distinct blocks of \(\pi_R\) or in distinct blocks of \(\rho\).  Differentiating the corresponding log-factorization therefore gives
\[
 \partial_i\partial_j\ell_P(x)=0
 \qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^d.
\]
For each \(E\in\sigma\), define
\[
 \gamma_E(x_E)=\ell_P(x_E,0_{V\setminus E})-\ell_P(0).
\]
The displayed mixed-derivative equality implies that \(\partial_i\ell_P(x)\), for \(i\in E\), is unchanged when any coordinate outside \(E\) varies: integrate each zero derivative along a coordinate line.  Hence the gradient of
\[
 H(x)=\ell_P(x)-\ell_P(0)-\sum_{E\in\sigma}\gamma_E(x_E)
\]
vanishes everywhere.  Since \(H(0)=0\) and \(\mathbb R^d\) is connected, integrating \(\nabla H\) along the line segment from \(0\) to \(x\) yields \(H(x)=0\).  Therefore
\[
 p_P(x)=e^{\ell_P(0)}\prod_{E\in\sigma}e^{\gamma_E(x_E)}.
\]
Every factor is continuous and strictly positive.  Iterated application of Lemma~\ref{lem:tonelli-nonnegative-product-integral} and \(\int p_P=1\) gives
\[
 1=e^{\ell_P(0)}\prod_{E\in\sigma}Z_E,
 \qquad
 Z_E:=\int_{\mathbb R^{|E|}}e^{\gamma_E(u)}\,du.
\]
Each \(Z_E\) is strictly positive; the finite product is finite, so every \(Z_E<\infty\).  Consequently \(r_E^sigma=Z_E^{-1}e^{\gamma_E}\) is a normalized everywhere positive density and \(\log r_E^sigma\in C^3\).  The preceding factorization, with its constant absorbed into the normalizers, proves that the coordinate subvectors indexed by the cells of \(\sigma\) are mutually independent.

If \(\sigma\) strictly refined \(\pi_R\), some block \(B\in\pi_R\) would be the disjoint union \(B=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}D\) of two nonempty unions of \(\sigma\)-cells.  The factorization over \(\sigma\), and invariance of independence under the deterministic root translation, would give
\[
 \varepsilon_{R,D}=0(\varepsilon_{R,C})+\varepsilon_{R,D},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_{R,D}\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_{R,C}.
\]
The density of \(\varepsilon_{R,D}\) is a translate of the product of the \(r_E^\sigma\) over \(E\subseteq D\); it is normalized, everywhere positive, and has a \(C^3\) log-density.  Definition~\ref{def:smooth-positive-laws} thus gives
\[
 \mathcal L(\varepsilon_{R,D})\in\operatorname{SmoothPos}(|D|).
\]
The zero map is \(C^3\), so this is exactly a directional split forbidden by Assumption~\ref{ass:innovation-atomicity}.  Hence \(\sigma=\pi_R\), which by Definition~\ref{def:common-refinement} is equivalent to \(\pi_R\preceq\rho\).  Since \(\rho\) was arbitrary, \(\pi_R\) refines every smooth-positive independent coordinate partition and is therefore the unique finest one.  This proves the empty-DAG reduction and completes all three claims.
