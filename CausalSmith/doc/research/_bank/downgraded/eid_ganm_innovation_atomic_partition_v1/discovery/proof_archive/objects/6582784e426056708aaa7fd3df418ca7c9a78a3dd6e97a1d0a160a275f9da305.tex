The triangular density lemma proves the displayed pointwise factorization.  Integrating it over \(x_1\) and applying Tonelli proves that the slice sets \(E_{\varepsilon}\) and \(E_{\eta}\) are conull and yields the normalized conditional identity on \(D\).  More strongly, positivity permits division of the original factorization at \(x_1=t+f_1(x_3)\) and \(x_1=t_0+f_1(x_3)\) without any slice normalizer.  Thus, for every \((x_2,x_3)\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
 \nu_{\varepsilon}(t,v)-\nu_{\varepsilon}(t_0,v)
 =\nu_{\eta}(t+\delta,z)-\nu_{\eta}(t_0+\delta,z).
\]
This identity is already global and \(C^3\).  Let \(s_{\varepsilon}=\partial_1\nu_{\varepsilon}\), \(s_{\eta}=\partial_1\nu_{\eta}\), and \(J=1-f_2'(x_3)g_3'(x_2)\).  Differentiating the score identity with respect to \(x_2\) and \(x_3\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \partial_v s_{\varepsilon}
 &=-g_1'(x_2)\partial_t s_{\eta}-g_3'(x_2)\partial_zs_{\eta},\\
 -f_2'(x_3)\partial_v s_{\varepsilon}
 &=f_1'(x_3)\partial_t s_{\eta}+\partial_zs_{\eta}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
On \(\Omega=\{J\neq0\}\), elimination yields
\[
 \partial_v s_{\varepsilon}
 =\frac{g_3'f_1'-g_1'}{J}\,\partial_t s_{\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 \partial_zs_{\eta}
 =\frac{f_2'g_1'-f_1'}{J}\,\partial_t s_{\eta}.
\]
The second \(t\)-derivative of the log-density cannot vanish identically on a whole regular slice, because then that positive slice would be the exponential of an affine function and could not be integrable on \(\mathbb R\).  Hence on each inverse chart the coefficients above depend only on \(v\), respectively only on \(z\).  Solving these first-order transport equations gives local location families.  Uniqueness follows because a strictly positive integrable density has no nonzero translation period.  The same uniqueness makes normalized local shifts differ by one common constant on chart overlaps.

There is also a complete treatment of the fully singular alternative.  If \(\Omega=\varnothing\), separation of variables in \(f_2'(x_3)g_3'(x_2)=1\) gives \(f_2(x_3)=ax_3+c\) and \(g_3(x_2)=a^{-1}x_2+d_0\) with \(a\neq0\).  Then
\[
 \eta_3=-a^{-1}\varepsilon_2-a^{-1}c-d_0,
 \qquad
 X_2=aX_3+c+\varepsilon_2.
\]
The two representations require \(X_3\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_2\) and \(\eta_3\perp\!\!\!\perp X_2\), so \(\varepsilon_2\) is independent of \(aX_3+c+\varepsilon_2\).  Writing \(E=\varepsilon_2\) and \(Z=aX_3+c\), independence gives, for all \(s\) and all sufficiently small \(t\) for which \(\varphi_Z(t)\neq0\),
\[
 \varphi_E(s+t)\varphi_Z(t)
 =\varphi_E(s)\varphi_E(t)\varphi_Z(t),
\]
and hence \(\varphi_E(s+t)=\varphi_E(s)\varphi_E(t)\).  Continuity and iteration reduce arbitrary increments to small ones, so \(\varphi_E(t)=e^{i\mu t}\), the characteristic function of a point mass.  This contradicts the everywhere positive density of \(E\).

If \(\Omega\neq\varnothing\), the set of \(v\)-fibers meeting \(\Omega\) is dense.  Indeed, if an open interval \(I\) of \(v\)'s were singular for every \(x_3\), then
\[
 g_3'(I+f_2(x_3))=1/f_2'(x_3).
\]
Overlapping intervals for nearby \(x_3\) force \(f_2'\) to be locally constant, hence constant on \(\mathbb R\); their union then forces \(g_3'\) to be its reciprocal everywhere, contradicting \(\Omega\neq\varnothing\).  The symmetric assertion holds for \(z\)-fibers.  What remains unproved is that the locally unique shift parameters have finite, path-independent \(C^3\) extensions across every closed nowhere-dense family of singular fibers.  Pointwise continuity of the unnormalized log-ratios gives compatibility when such finite limits exist, but it does not by itself supply their existence or the three derivatives required by innovation atomicity.
