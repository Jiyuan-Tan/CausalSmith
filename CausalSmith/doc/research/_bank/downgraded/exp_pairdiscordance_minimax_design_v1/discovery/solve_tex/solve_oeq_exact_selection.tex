Let \(\mathcal L_{(s_J)}\) denote the following second-order selection problem. Put
\[
 \Delta_J=s_J-\frac J2,
 \qquad
 \kappa_J=\frac{\Delta_J}{J^{2/3}},
 \qquad
 \overline m_J(q)=\int_{\Delta_2}m(x)\,d\mu_q(x),
\]
and define the finite design-profile risk
\[
 \mathsf r^{\mathrm{prof}}_{J,s_J}(q)
 =\min_{\eta\in[-1,1]^{\mathcal N_J\times\mathcal O_J}}
   \max_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s_J}}\mathcal R_h(q,\eta).
\]
On \(\mathcal P(\Delta_2)\), equipped with the weak topology, extend the grid profile by
\[
 \mathsf R_J(\mu)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf r^{\mathrm{prof}}_{J,s_J}(q),&\mu=\mu_q\text{ for some }q\in\Delta(\mathcal N_J),\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \Gamma_J(\mu)=J^{4/3}\left\{\mathsf R_J(\mu)-\frac1{2J}\right\}.
\]
The second-order pair-orbit selection problem is to derive, from the ten-type schedule experiment and the kernel \(K\), an unrestricted Airy/local-experiment functional \(\mathcal A\), and then to prove a matching liminf inequality, recovery bounds, convergence of minima, and exact-minimizer compactness for \(\Gamma_J\). At the critical boundary \(s_J/J\to1/2\) with \(\kappa_J\to\kappa\in\mathbb R\), the candidate upper-bound functional is
\[
 \Gamma^{\mathrm{up}}_\kappa(\mu)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \mathcal A(\mu)+\kappa\left(2\displaystyle\int m\,d\mu-1\right),
     &\mu\in\mathcal F_{1/2},\\
 +\infty,&\mu\notin\mathcal F_{1/2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The hard-budget term in this display is upper-bound evidence only until a uniform matching lower expansion is proved. If \(\kappa_J\to+\infty\), the residual problem must first restrict to the subface \(\int m\,d\mu=1/2\). If \(\kappa_J\to-\infty\), use the recentered functional
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma_J(\mu)
 =J^{4/3}\left\{\mathsf R_J(\mu)-\frac1{2J}-\frac{\Delta_J}{J^2}\right\}
\]
and analyze the subface \(\int m\,d\mu=1\). The regime \(\rho>1/2\) is a separate tangent problem on \(\mathcal F_\rho\), including transverse deviations of the finite-grid measures, and is not identified with the critical \(\kappa=+\infty\) problem. Only after the required lower and upper expansions are established may the contact sets of these limiting problems be compared with \(\mathcal Q^*_{\infty}[(s_J)]\); exact-minimizer recovery is an output to prove, not part of the definition.

The exact finite contact system in Theorem 1 computes every set \(\mathcal Q^*(J,s_J)\), and Theorem 5 gives the rigorous first-order statements
\[
 \mathcal Q^*_{\infty}[(s_J)]\subseteq\mathcal F_\rho
\]
and recovery of every \(\mu\in\mathcal F_\rho\) by invariant procedures whose excess worst risk is \(o(J^{-1})\). These conclusions do not replace those procedures by exact minimizers.

The critical scale follows algebraically from the candidate hard-budget correction. Indeed, with \(\overline m_J=\int m\,d\mu_{q_J}\),
\[
 J^{4/3}
 \frac{(2\overline m_J-1)\Delta_J}{J^2}
 =\frac{\Delta_J}{J^{2/3}}(2\overline m_J-1)
 =\kappa_J(2\overline m_J-1).
\]
Thus it competes with a \(J^{-4/3}\) unrestricted Airy correction exactly when \(\Delta_J=O(J^{2/3})\). On \(\mathcal F_{1/2}\), \(1/2\le\int m\,d\mu\le1\). Consequently the candidate hard-budget term with \(\kappa_J\to+\infty\) is finite only on \(\int m\,d\mu=1/2\). If \(\kappa_J\to-\infty\), subtracting \(\Delta_J/J^2\) changes that term to
\[
 2\kappa_J\left(\int m\,d\mu-1\right),
\]
which is nonnegative and diverges off \(\int m\,d\mu=1\). For \(\rho>1/2\), the first-order face already imposes \(\int m\,d\mu=1/2\); selection then depends on transverse finite-grid deviations and requires a separate tangent expansion. These are consequences of the candidate upper correction and identify the residual regimes, not a proved epi-limit.
