\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}pairdiscordance\_\allowbreak{}minimax\_\allowbreak{}design}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Known pairs are useful when their two units would respond similarly under each arm, but that two-arm similarity is latent. The integer \(s\) is therefore an analyst-selected sensitivity bound, and for every value the project jointly optimizes over all outcome-independent assignment laws and all measurable clipped SATE estimators. A pair-preserving symmetry reduction yields an exact finite conic game, a polynomial-size rational kernel, and dual certificates for the entire invariant optimal-design correspondence; an exact, nondegenerate \(J=2,s=1\) witness rules out the paper-defined paired--Bernoulli mixture workflow. The unrestricted all-design/all-estimator orbit-game layer and the \(J^{-4/3}\) second-order scale overlap with the accepted internal \texttt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier\_v1} artifact. At \(K=2\), the present hard-discordance class is a structured subclass of that artifact's unrestricted binary class, coinciding with it at \(s=J\), while the pair-preserving group is a subgroup of the full unit-permutation group. The present contribution adds the hard-budget pair geometry, its joint assignment--observation pair kernel, and the exact projected optimal-design correspondence; these are not obtained by direct specialization of the full-permutation quotient, and neither a global extension nor priority is claimed. The sensitivity frontier satisfies \(J R^*(J,s_J)\to\min\{\rho,1/2\}\), while exact finite-minimizer selection on the flat first-order face remains the open \(J^{2/3}\)-critical, \(J^{-4/3}\)-residual problem.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The accepted internal \texttt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier\_v1} artifact already supplies an unrestricted all-design/all-estimator binary response-type orbit game and establishes \(n^{-4/3}\) second-order scaling, so neither generic orbit minimax nor that scale alone is a gap. For \(K=2\) and \(n=2J\), the present schedule class is a structured subclass of its unrestricted binary class, with exact coincidence at \(s=J\), and the pair-preserving action is a subgroup of the full permutation action. What remains untreated is the hard armwise-discordance geometry for \(s<J\): full permutations do not preserve fixed pairing or the discordance cap, so the pair kernel and projected optimal-design correspondence do not follow by direct specialization of the unrestricted quotient. Published endpoint results also cover unrestricted binary schedules and a larger unrestricted bounded-interval class. The superpopulation half-treatment and fixed-design linear-estimator comparators remain non-nested with the present decision problem.

\paragraph{Niche.} The known-pair wreath-product action and hard discordance cap require a finer geometry than the neighboring unrestricted artifact's full unit-permutation quotient. Schedule states are ten-type unordered pair histograms, and the reduced data retain joint assignment-marked observed-pair histograms. Although the underlying \(K=2\) schedule class is a subclass of the unrestricted binary class and agrees with it at \(s=J\), this reduced kernel and its exact projected design correspondence are not direct specializations of the unrestricted quotient.

\paragraph{Fill.} For each selected \(s\), the project supplies the pair-orbit kernel, the primal game and matching least-favourable-prior dual, an if-and-only-if contact certificate for the projected invariant optimal-design correspondence, exact canonicality and uniqueness tests, and a strict rational \(J=2,s=1\) separation with full assignment support, positive active-prior mass, two explicit positive-risk observation cells, and nonzero posterior-estimator variance. It also proves the sharp first-order phase transition and exact inversion, while formulating but not solving the residual \(J^{4/3}\)-scaled epi-limit and exact-minimizer contact problem. The advance is the hard-budget pair geometry inside a structured subclass, not a global extension of or priority claim over the accepted unrestricted artifact.

\section{Related work}

The closest internal comparison is the accepted artifact \texttt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier\_v1}. Its \texttt{thm:exact-response-type-game} and the present Theorem 1 overlap at the abstract level where arbitrary outcome-independent designs and arbitrary measurable estimators are symmetrized into finite binary orbit games with least-favourable-prior saddles. With \(K=2\) and \(n=2J\), the present \(\Theta_{J,s}\) is a structured subclass of that artifact's unrestricted binary schedule class and coincides with it when \(s=J\); likewise \(G_J=\mathfrak S_J\ltimes(\mathfrak S_2)^J\) is a subgroup of the full unit-permutation group. Nevertheless, the present pair-orbit kernel is not obtained by directly specializing the artifact's full-permutation quotient, because a general full permutation does not preserve the fixed pairing or the armwise discordance cap. The present quotient therefore uses ten-type latent pair histograms and joint assignment-marked observed-pair histograms and, unlike the neighboring exact-response theorem, explicitly projects the complete contact system onto \(\mathcal Q^*(J,s)\). The artifact's \texttt{thm:first-order-saddle} corresponds to the unrestricted leading-risk layer, whereas the present phase-transition theorem changes that value to \(\min\{\rho,1/2\}\) under the hard budget. Its \texttt{thm:universal-second-order-rate} and \texttt{thm:second-order-rate-and-certificate-frontier} establish the unrestricted \(n^{-4/3}\) order; the present exact-selection question uses that scale only as neighboring evidence for a critical residual problem and does not claim a proved hard-budget Gamma limit. Its rational grid-certificate theorems certify unrestricted values to the second-order scale, whereas the present Theorem 1 supplies the exact finite pair kernel and projected optimal-design face for every \(J,s\). Thus the relationship is structured subclass plus different quotient geometry, not a global extension or a priority claim. Bai2023WhyRandomize supplies the general averaging template, and Kallus2021Optimality shows that structured fixed-estimator criteria may favor mixed designs; neither supplies this hard-budget all-estimator projected face. SudijonoDobribanTchetgen2026 treats exactly the unrestricted binary endpoint obtained at \(s=J\), and its Airy expansion motivates the residual scale without settling pair-budget selection. Hull2026BoundedOutcomes treats a larger bounded-interval class; the binary endpoint inherits its value and procedure only through the previously stated upper-bound and endpoint-prior lower-bound sandwich. The canonical segment used in Theorem 3 is solely this paper's workflow that convexly mixes paired assignment with the Bernoulli/nonlinear unrestricted prescription and then reoptimizes the estimator; no cited paper is said to advocate that mixture. Bai2022MatchedPairs and deChaisemartin2022Shrinkage remain different, non-nested problems, while HarshawSavjeSpielmanZhang2024, AronowLopatto2026, Wilson2026MatchedBinary, Zhang2026FastBinaryCI, and LiSpertusStark2025 provide design or inference context under different decision classes or assignment mechanisms.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-outcome-support]\label{ass:binary-outcome-support}
The schedule satisfies \(Y\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal P_J\times\{0,1\}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary potential-outcome support restriction for finite-population randomization inference, \cite{Wilson2026MatchedBinary}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:armwise-discordance-budget]\label{ass:armwise-discordance-budget}
The schedule satisfies \(D_a(Y)\le s\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition is a transparent sensitivity restriction on latent potential outcomes, analogous to varying an unobserved-confounding radius. Smaller values are substantively plausible for pairs formed from near-duplicate baseline profiles, but pretreatment records cannot verify both-arm discordance; conclusions are therefore reported across analyst-selected values of \(s\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-exogeneity]\label{ass:design-exogeneity}
The conditional assignment law satisfies \(Q_{\mathrm{design}}(A\mid Y)=Q_{\mathrm{design}}(A)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Outcome-independent randomized assignment, \cite{ImbensRubin2015}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:orbit-game-face]\label{thm:orbit-game-face}
Theorem 1. For every \(J\ge2\) and \(0\le s\le J\), averaging over \(G_J\) is lossless for the minimax problem. The full rectangular kernel has \(O(J^{20})\) rational entries of \(O(J)\) bit length and is exactly computable by coefficient recursion from \(F_h\). Moreover, \[R^*(J,s)=\min_{q,\eta}\max_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s}}\sum_{n,o}q_nK_{hno}(\eta_{no}-\tau_h)^2=\max_{\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal H_{J,s})}\min_{n\in\mathcal N_J}B_n(\pi).\] An orbit law \(q\) belongs to \(\mathcal Q^*(J,s)\) if and only if there exist \(\eta\), \(\pi\), and \(v\) such that \(\mathcal R_h(q,\eta)\le v\) for every \(h\) and \(B_n(\pi)\ge v\) for every \(n\). This certificate decides membership of \(q^{\mathrm{pair}}\), \(q^{\mathrm{Ber}}\), and \(\mathcal C_J\), essential noncanonicality, and singleton uniqueness by coordinate optimization over the projected optimal face.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(X_Y(a)=(a,Y_Y^{\mathrm{obs}}(a))\). By \(\texttt{def:pair-invariance-group}\), every \(g\in G_J\) permutes pairs and possibly exchanges the two labels inside each pair. Hence it preserves each armwise discordance count \(D_a\), the binary class \(\Theta_{J,s}\) from \(\texttt{ass:binary-outcome-support}\) and \(\texttt{ass:armwise-discordance-budget}\), and the unit-weighted contrast \(\tau\). It also satisfies the observation equivariance identity
\[
 g^{-1}X_Y(gb)=X_{g^{-1}Y}(b).
\]

Fix an admissible pair \((Q,\delta)\). Define
\[
 \overline Q(a)=\frac1{|G_J|}\sum_{g\in G_J}Q(g^{-1}a).
\]
The law \(\overline Q\) is chosen before outcomes and is outcome-independent by \(\texttt{ass:design-exogeneity}\), so it belongs to \(\mathfrak Q_{J,s}\). If \(\overline Q(a)>0\), set
\[
 w_g(a)=\frac{Q(g^{-1}a)}{\sum_{u\in G_J}Q(u^{-1}a)},
 \qquad
 \overline\delta(a,y)=\sum_{g\in G_J}w_g(a)\delta(g^{-1}a,g^{-1}y),
\]
and set \(\overline\delta(a,y)=0\) when \(\overline Q(a)=0\). The weights use only the observed assignment and the known design. Thus \(\overline\delta\) is measurable from \((A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}},\mathcal P_J)\), and it lies in \([-1,1]\) because it is a convex combination of clipped estimators.

Equivalently, draw \(g\) uniformly from \(G_J\), draw \(b\sim Q\), and report the relabelled assignment \(a=gb\). Conditional on \(a\), the distribution of \(g\) is \(w_g(a)\). Conditional Jensen inequality for squared loss, observation equivariance, and \(\tau(g^{-1}Y)=\tau(Y)\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_{J,s}(\overline Q,\overline\delta;Y)
 &\le \frac1{|G_J|}\sum_{g\in G_J}
 \mathcal R_{J,s}(Q,\delta;g^{-1}Y)\\
 &\le \sup_{Y'\in\Theta_{J,s}}\mathcal R_{J,s}(Q,\delta;Y').
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore restriction to invariant procedures does not change the minimax value.

The assignment orbits of this action are exactly the triples \(n=(n_{00},n_{11},n_{\mathrm m})\in\mathcal N_J\), and an invariant assignment law is uniform inside each orbit and is specified by \(q\). A schedule orbit is exactly a histogram \(h\in\mathcal H_{J,s}\) of the ten unordered pair types, and its target is \(\tau_h\). Because each local observed unit records both \(A_{jr}\) and \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{jr}\), an observation orbit is exactly a histogram \(o\in\mathcal O_J\); in particular it retains the joint assignment--outcome pair type. Hence an invariant estimator is specified by the values \(\eta_{no}\) on compatible orbit cells.

We next verify the kernel. Choose a list \(t_1,\ldots,t_J\) containing \(h_t\) copies of every \(t\in\mathcal T\). Let \(M_0(0,0)=1\), with every other value zero, and recursively define
\[
 M_{k+1}(n,o)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}}
 M_k\!\left(n-e_{\nu(a)},o-e_{\omega(t_{k+1},a)}\right),
\]
where a term with a negative coordinate is zero. Induction on \(k\) shows that \(M_k(n,o)\) is the number of local assignment strings for the first \(k\) pairs with assignment mark \(n\) and observed-pair mark \(o\). The induction starts from the empty string; appending \(a\) gives exactly the displayed recurrence. Thus
\[
 M_J(n,o)=
 [x_{00}^{n_{00}}x_{11}^{n_{11}}x_{\mathrm m}^{n_{\mathrm m}}z^o]F_h(x,z).
\]
There are
\[
 C_n=\frac{J!2^{n_{\mathrm m}}}{n_{00}!n_{11}!n_{\mathrm m}!}
\]
labelled assignments in orbit \(n\). Consequently
\[
 K_{hno}=\frac{M_J(n,o)}{C_n},
 \qquad
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal O_J}K_{hno}=1,
\]
which proves that \(K\) is the conditional observation law rather than merely defining it to be one.

The cardinalities satisfy
\[
 |\mathcal N_J|={J+2\choose2},\qquad
 |\mathcal H_{J,s}|\le {J+9\choose9},\qquad
 |\mathcal O_J|={J+9\choose9}.
\]
Therefore the full rectangular array has at most
\[
 {J+2\choose2}{J+9\choose9}^{\!2}=O(J^{20})
\]
entries. Since \(0\le M_J(n,o)\le4^J\) and \(1\le C_n\le4^J\), the numerator and denominator of every reduced rational \(K_{hno}\) have \(O(J)\) bits. The recurrence uses integer additions and computes all coefficients exactly.

It remains to derive the finite game and its dual. Put \(t_{no}=q_n\eta_{no}\) and define the compact convex perspective set
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{pr}}=
 \left\{(q,t):q_n\ge0,\ \sum_nq_n=1,\ -q_n\le t_{no}\le q_n
 \text{ for all }n,o\right\}.
\]
For \(q_n>0\), write
\[
 \psi_{hno}(q_n,t_{no})
 =K_{hno}\frac{(t_{no}-q_n\tau_h)^2}{q_n},
\]
and set \(\psi_{hno}(0,0)=0\). This is the closed perspective of a convex quadratic. Moreover, on the displayed wedge,
\[
 0\le \psi_{hno}(q_n,t_{no})
 =q_nK_{hno}(t_{no}/q_n-\tau_h)^2
 \le4K_{hno}q_n,
\]
so it is continuous also at \(q_n=0\). Thus
\[
 \Phi((q,t),\pi)=\sum_h\pi_h\sum_{n,o}\psi_{hno}(q_n,t_{no})
\]
is continuous and convex in \((q,t)\), and affine, hence concave, in \(\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal H_{J,s})\). Applying \(\texttt{lem:sion-minimax}\) on the two compact convex sets yields
\[
 \min_{(q,t)\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pr}}}\max_{\pi}\Phi((q,t),\pi)
 =\max_{\pi}\min_{(q,t)\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pr}}}\Phi((q,t),\pi),
\]
with both extrema attained. The left maximum over \(\pi\) is the maximum over \(h\), so it is exactly the invariant minimax game and hence, by symmetrization, \(R^*(J,s)\).

For fixed \(\pi\), define
\[
 d_{no}(\pi)=\sum_h\pi_hK_{hno}\tau_h^2.
\]
The contribution of cell \((n,o)\) to \(\Phi\) is
\[
 a_{no}(\pi)\frac{t_{no}^2}{q_n}-2c_{no}(\pi)t_{no}+q_nd_{no}(\pi).
\]
If \(a_{no}(\pi)>0\), completing the square gives the unique minimizing ratio
\[
 \frac{t_{no}}{q_n}=\frac{c_{no}(\pi)}{a_{no}(\pi)}.
\]
This ratio is a conditional average of \(\tau_h\in[-1,1]\), so it satisfies the clipping constraint. If \(a_{no}(\pi)=0\), nonnegativity of \(\pi_hK_{hno}\) implies \(c_{no}(\pi)=d_{no}(\pi)=0\), and the cell contributes zero at its minimum. Finally, row normalization gives
\[
 \sum_o d_{no}(\pi)=\sum_h\pi_h\tau_h^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \min_t\Phi((q,t),\pi)
 =\sum_nq_n\left\{\sum_h\pi_h\tau_h^2-
 \sum_{o:a_{no}(\pi)>0}\frac{c_{no}(\pi)^2}{a_{no}(\pi)}\right\}
 =\sum_nq_nB_n(\pi).
\]
Minimizing this linear expression over \(q\in\Delta(\mathcal N_J)\) gives \(\min_nB_n(\pi)\). We have proved
\[
 R^*(J,s)=\min_{q,\eta}\max_h\mathcal R_h(q,\eta)
 =\max_{\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal H_{J,s})}\min_{n\in\mathcal N_J}B_n(\pi).
\]

For the contact certificate, first suppose \(q\in\mathcal Q^*(J,s)\). Choose an attaining invariant estimator \(\eta\), choose an attaining dual prior \(\pi\), and put \(v=R^*(J,s)\). Primal and dual feasibility give
\[
 \mathcal R_h(q,\eta)\le v\quad\text{for every }h,
 \qquad
 B_n(\pi)\ge v\quad\text{for every }n.
\]
Conversely, if these inequalities hold for some \((q,\eta,\pi,v)\), weak duality and the equality just proved give
\[
 R^*(J,s)\le\max_h\mathcal R_h(q,\eta)\le v
 \le\min_nB_n(\pi)\le R^*(J,s).
\]
Every inequality is therefore an equality, and \(q\in\mathcal Q^*(J,s)\). Fixing \(q=q^{\mathrm{pair}}\) or \(q=q^{\mathrm{Ber}}\) yields the two membership tests. Adding the variable \(0\le\lambda\le1\) and the constraint \(q=\lambda q^{\mathrm{pair}}+(1-\lambda)q^{\mathrm{Ber}}\) tests intersection with \(\mathcal C_J\); infeasibility certifies essential noncanonicality. The optimal subset of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pr}}\) is compact and convex, and its projection onto the \(q\)-coordinates is exactly \(\mathcal Q^*(J,s)\). This projection is a singleton if and only if, for every \(n\), the minimum and maximum of \(q_n\) over it agree. These coordinate optimizations give the asserted uniqueness certificate.
\end{proof}

The accepted \texttt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier\_v1} artifact's \texttt{thm:exact-response-type-game} already contains the unrestricted all-design/all-estimator binary orbit-game architecture. For \(K=2\), the present schedule class is its structured subclass, equal at \(s=J\), and the pair group is a subgroup of the full permutation group. The present theorem's additional object is the hard-budget wreath-product quotient, joint assignment--observation pair kernel, and projected design correspondence, none of which follows by directly specializing the full-permutation quotient. No priority or global-extension claim is made. \textit{Gap.} The accepted neighboring theorem uses full unit permutations and unrestricted \(2^K\)-response-type histograms; full permutations do not preserve the known pairing or the armwise discordance cap. Bai2023WhyRandomize supplies a general averaging move, and Kallus2021Optimality studies a different fixed-estimator mixed-design geometry. \begin{proposition}[prop:endpoint-reductions]\label{prop:endpoint-reductions}
Proposition 2. If \(0\le s\le s'\le J\), then \(\Theta_{J,s}\subseteq\Theta_{J,s'}\) and \(R^*(J,s)\le R^*(J,s')\). At \(s=0\), \(R^*(J,0)=0\) and \(\mathcal Q^*(J,0)=\{q^{\mathrm{pair}}\}\). At \(s=J\), \(R^*(J,J)=r^{\mathrm U}_{2J}\), and \(q^{\mathrm{Ber}}\in\mathcal Q^*(J,J)\) with the nonlinear posterior-mean rule from the unrestricted binary experiment.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Throughout, the potential-outcome schedule is fixed and the only expectation is over the assignment law, as required by \(\texttt{ass:design-exogeneity}\) and \(\texttt{def:randomization-risk}\).

First let \(0\le s\le s'\le J\).  If \(Y\in\Theta_{J,s}\), then \(D_a(Y)\le s\le s'\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Hence, directly from \(\texttt{def:bounded-mismatch-class}\),
\[
 \Theta_{J,s}\subseteq\Theta_{J,s'}.
\]
The condition in \(\texttt{def:design-class}\) restricts a law only to be selected ex ante and independently of \(Y\); the numerical sensitivity label may inform which law is selected but does not remove any outcome-independent assignment law from the feasible simplex.  Thus \(\mathfrak Q_{J,s}=\mathfrak Q_{J,s'}\), while \(\mathfrak D_J\) is the same set at both sensitivity values by \(\texttt{def:estimator-class}\).  Consequently, for every feasible \((Q,\delta)\),
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\Theta_{J,s}}\mathcal R_{J,s}(Q,\delta;Y)
 \le
 \sup_{Y\in\Theta_{J,s'}}\mathcal R_{J,s'}(Q,\delta;Y),
\]
where the two risk formulas agree pointwise by \(\texttt{def:randomization-risk}\).  Taking the infimum over the common feasible procedure class and applying \(\texttt{def:minimax-risk}\) gives
\[
 R^*(J,s)\le R^*(J,s').
\]

Now set \(s=0\).  By \(\texttt{def:bounded-mismatch-class}\), every \(Y\in\Theta_{J,0}\) has constants \(y_j(0),y_j(1)\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 Y_{j1}(a)=Y_{j2}(a)=y_j(a),
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\},\quad 1\le j\le J.
\]
Under \(q^{\mathrm{pair}}\), exactly one member of each pair is treated.  Define the measurable estimator
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\sum_{r=1}^2
 \bigl\{A_{jr}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{jr}-(1-A_{jr})Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{jr}\bigr\}.
\]
Each pair contributes a number in \([-1,1]\), so \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}\in[-1,1]\) and therefore belongs to \(\mathfrak D_J\).  Pair homogeneity and consistency in the definition of \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\) imply, assignment by assignment,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}
 =\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\{y_j(1)-y_j(0)\}
 =\frac1{2J}\sum_{j=1}^J\sum_{r=1}^2
 \{Y_{jr}(1)-Y_{jr}(0)\}
 =\tau(Y),
\]
the last equality being \(\texttt{def:sate}\).  Thus the worst-case randomization risk of this procedure is zero.  Squared loss is nonnegative, so \(\texttt{def:minimax-risk}\) yields
\[
 R^*(J,0)=0.
\]

It remains to show that the optimal invariant orbit law is unique at this endpoint.  Let \(q\in\Delta(\mathcal N_J)\) place positive mass on some \(n\ne(0,0,J)\).  Then every assignment in orbit \(n\) contains an unmixed pair.  Choose one labelled assignment \(a\) in that orbit and one such pair \(j\).  By the orbit construction, \(Q(a)=q_n/C_n>0\).  Let \(Y^{(0)}\) be the all-zero schedule.  If pair \(j\) is all control under \(a\), obtain \(Y^{(1)}\) by setting \(Y^{(1)}_{j1}(1)=Y^{(1)}_{j2}(1)=1\) and leaving all other potential outcomes zero.  If pair \(j\) is all treated, instead set \(Y^{(1)}_{j1}(0)=Y^{(1)}_{j2}(0)=1\).  In either case, both schedules lie in \(\Theta_{J,0}\), and they produce the same observed data under \(a\).  Their SATEs are respectively zero and \(\varepsilon/J\), where \(\varepsilon=1\) in the all-control case and \(\varepsilon=-1\) in the all-treated case.  Therefore, if \(d\) is the common estimator value at that observed-data cell,
\[
 \max\left\{d^2,\left(d-\frac{\varepsilon}{J}\right)^2\right\}
 \ge \frac1{4J^2}.
\]
For at least one of \(Y^{(0)}\) and \(Y^{(1)}\), nonnegativity of all the other assignment contributions then gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{J,0}(Q,\delta;Y)
 \ge \frac{q_n}{4C_nJ^2}>0.
\]
Thus no such \(q\) can attain the already established value zero.  The only remaining orbit law is \(\delta_{(0,0,J)}=q^{\mathrm{pair}}\), so \(\texttt{def:finite-optimal-design-set}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal Q^*(J,0)=\{q^{\mathrm{pair}}\}.
\]

Finally set \(s=J\).  Every binary schedule has \(D_a(Y)\le J\), and hence
\[
 \Theta_{J,J}=\{0,1\}^{\mathcal P_J\times\{0,1\}}
\]
by \(\texttt{def:bounded-mismatch-class}\).  This is exactly the unrestricted binary fixed-population class studied by \(\cite{SudijonoDobribanTchetgen2026}\).  Since the design, estimator, target, and loss in \(\texttt{def:unrestricted-endpoint-risk}\) are the same as those in \(\texttt{def:minimax-risk}\) at \(s=J\), their defining infimum--supremum problems coincide, and therefore
\[
 R^*(J,J)=r^{\mathrm U}_{2J}.
\]

For clarity, this binary-class identity is distinct from the comparison with \(\cite{Hull2026BoundedOutcomes}\).  Let
\[
 \mathcal Y_{\mathrm{bin}}=\{0,1\}^{\mathcal P_J\times\{0,1\}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal Y_{[0,1]}=[0,1]^{\mathcal P_J\times\{0,1\}},
\]
and let \(V_{[0,1]}\) denote Hull's minimax value on \(\mathcal Y_{[0,1]}\).  Then \(\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{bin}}\subsetneq\mathcal Y_{[0,1]}\): the former is only the endpoint-valued subclass of the latter.  By \(\texttt{lem:unrestricted-binary-bernoulli-minimax}\), Hull's independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) design and nonlinear posterior-mean rule, denoted \((Q_H,\delta_H)\), obey the uniform bounded-interval upper bound
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{[0,1]}}
 \mathcal R(Q_H,\delta_H;Y)=V_{[0,1]}.
\]
Restriction of this one procedure to binary observations is feasible for the binary problem, so
\[
 r^{\mathrm U}_{2J}
 \le \sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{bin}}}
 \mathcal R(Q_H,\delta_H;Y)
 \le V_{[0,1]}.
\]
The same cited lemma supplies a least-favourable prior \(\Pi_H\) supported on endpoint schedules, hence supported on \(\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{bin}}\), whose optimal Bayes risk is \(V_{[0,1]}\).  For every binary procedure \((Q,\delta)\), the supremum dominates its \(\Pi_H\)-average risk.  Taking infima gives the reverse inequality
\[
 r^{\mathrm U}_{2J}
 =\inf_{Q,\delta}\sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{bin}}}
 \mathcal R(Q,\delta;Y)
 \ge
 \inf_{Q,\delta}\int_{\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{bin}}}
 \mathcal R(Q,\delta;Y)\,d\Pi_H(Y)
 =V_{[0,1]}.
\]
Thus the two values are equal and \((Q_H,\delta_H)\), restricted to binary data, attains \(r^{\mathrm U}_{2J}\).  This is a uniform-upper-bound plus endpoint-prior-lower-bound sandwich; it does not identify the binary and bounded-interval parameter classes or their experiments.

Under independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) assignment, every labelled assignment has probability \(4^{-J}\).  Orbit \(n\) contains \(C_n\) assignments, so its mass is
\[
 Q_H\{A\text{ lies in orbit }n\}=\frac{C_n}{4^J}=q_n^{\mathrm{Ber}}
\]
by \(\texttt{def:canonical-segment}\).  The binary schedule class and squared-error loss are invariant under \(G_J\); averaging the optimal procedure over this finite group leaves the already invariant Bernoulli design unchanged and, by convexity of squared loss, cannot increase its maximum risk.  The lossless invariant reduction in \(\texttt{thm:orbit-game-face}\) therefore supplies an invariant orbit estimator \(\eta\) achieving \(R^*(J,J)\) with orbit law \(q^{\mathrm{Ber}}\).  By \(\texttt{def:finite-optimal-design-set}\),
\[
 q^{\mathrm{Ber}}\in\mathcal Q^*(J,J),
\]
and the unrestricted attaining procedure is the asserted nonlinear posterior-mean rule.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The endpoints are mandatory reductions: perfect pair agreement makes paired assignment exact, while a vacuous budget recovers the published unrestricted problem. \textit{Gap.} Bai2022MatchedPairs and Hull2026BoundedOutcomes motivate opposite endpoint prescriptions under different information classes; this proposition embeds both endpoints in one fixed-population model. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts obtain a monotone worst-case-risk sensitivity curve in \(s\), with familiar paired and Bernoulli procedures anchoring its two endpoints; the optimal face itself is recomputed at each sensitivity value.

\begin{theorem}[thm:phase-transition]\label{thm:phase-transition}
Theorem 4. For every budget sequence with \(s_J/J\to\rho\in[0,1]\), \[\lim_{J\to\infty}J R^*(J,s_J)=\min\{\rho,1/2\}.\] If \(0\le\rho<1/2\), then every sequence \(q_J\in\mathcal Q^*(J,s_J)\) satisfies \(\mu_{q_J}\Rightarrow\delta_{(0,0,1)}\), equivalently convergence holds in \(W_1\) on \(\Delta_2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(x_0=(0,0,1)\).  We first establish two finite-sample upper bounds from explicit designs and estimators.  Under complete within-pair randomization, let
\[
 H_j=2A_{j1}-1,
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm P}
 =\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J
 H_j\bigl(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{j1}-Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{j2}\bigr).
\]
The definition of this design gives \(A_{j2}=1-A_{j1}\), and the variables \(H_1,\ldots,H_J\) are independent Rademacher variables.  Define
\(
 \mu_{jr}=\{Y_{jr}(1)+Y_{jr}(0)\}/2
\).
Direct substitution, using the definition of \(\tau\), gives the pointwise identity
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm P}-\tau(Y)
 =\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J H_j(\mu_{j1}-\mu_{j2}).
\]
The estimator is an average of numbers in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), so it belongs to the clipped estimator class.  Independence and \(\mathbb E H_j=0\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm P}-\tau)^2
 =\frac1{J^2}\sum_{j=1}^J(\mu_{j1}-\mu_{j2})^2.
\]
Writing \(\Delta_{ja}=Y_{j1}(a)-Y_{j2}(a)\), the elementary inequality \((u+v)^2\le 2(u^2+v^2)\) and binary support give
\[
 (\mu_{j1}-\mu_{j2})^2
 =\frac14(\Delta_{j0}+\Delta_{j1})^2
 \le \frac12\sum_{a=0}^1\Delta_{ja}^2
 =\frac12\sum_{a=0}^1
 \mathbf 1\{Y_{j1}(a)\ne Y_{j2}(a)\}.
\]
The two armwise discordance budgets therefore yield
\[
 \sup_{Y\in\Theta_{J,s}}
 \mathbb E(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm P}-\tau)^2
 \le \frac{s}{J^2}.
\]

Under independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) assignment, define the observable estimator
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm B}
 =\frac1{2J}\sum_{j=1}^J\sum_{r=1}^2
 (2A_{jr}-1)(2Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{jr}-1).
\]
It too lies in \([-1,1]\).  For
\(
 v_{jr}=Y_{jr}(1)+Y_{jr}(0)-1\in\{-1,0,1\}
\), substitution separately for \(A_{jr}=0\) and \(A_{jr}=1\) gives
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm B}-\tau(Y)
 =\frac1{2J}\sum_{j,r}(2A_{jr}-1)v_{jr}.
\]
The assignment signs are independent Rademacher variables, whence
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm B}-\tau)^2
 =\frac1{4J^2}\sum_{j,r}v_{jr}^2
 \le\frac1{2J}.
\]
Both assignment laws are outcome-independent and chosen ex ante, as required by the design class.  The definition of the joint minimax risk thus gives the finite bound
\[
 R^*(J,s)\le \min\left\{\frac{s}{J^2},\frac1{2J}\right\}.
 \tag{1}
\]

We next prove the matching asymptotic lower bound.  This uses a distribution over fixed schedules only as a Bayes lower-bound device; assignment remains the sole randomness after a schedule is fixed.  Suppose first that \(\rho>0\), and fix
\[
 0<r<\min\{\rho,1/2\}.
\]
Let \(b_J=J^{-1/4}\).  Choose a continuously differentiable density \(w\) on \((-1,1)\), positive in the interior, with zero trace at \(-1\) and \(1\), and with information
\(
 0<I_w=\int_{-1}^1\{w'(u)\}^2/w(u)\,du<\infty
\).
Give \(\theta\in(-b_J,b_J)\) density
\(
 w_J(\theta)=b_J^{-1}w(\theta/b_J)
\), whose Fisher information is \(I_w/b_J^2=o(J)\).
Conditional on \(\theta\), draw the \(J\) pairs independently from the following five schedule types:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{type}&H_0&H_1&D&E_+&E_-\\ \hline
\text{probability}
 &(1-r-2b_J)/2&(1-r-2b_J)/2&r
 &b_J+\theta/2&b_J-\theta/2.
\end{array}
\]
Here \(H_y\) consists of two copies of the no-effect type \((y,y)\); \(D\) consists of one \((0,0)\) unit and one \((1,1)\) unit, with the two labelled orientations equally likely; and \(E_+\) and \(E_-\) consist of two copies of \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\), respectively.  All five probabilities are strictly positive for all sufficiently large \(J\).  If \(T_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\) is the average treatment effect in pair \(j\), then
\[
 \tau_J:=\tau(Y)=\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J T_j,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(T_j\mid\theta)=\theta,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(T_j\mid\theta)=2b_J-\theta^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E(\tau_J\mid\theta)=\theta,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{(\tau_J-\theta)^2\mid\theta\}
 =\frac{2b_J-\theta^2}{J}\le\frac{2b_J}{J}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Condition on an arbitrary realized assignment.  Design exogeneity makes its probability independent of \(\theta\), and the induced observations from distinct pairs are conditionally independent.  On a mixed pair the two \(\theta\)-informative observation probabilities are
\(
 (r+2b_J+\theta)/2
\) and
\(
 (r+2b_J-\theta)/2
\), so direct summation of \((\partial_\theta p)^2/p\) gives
\[
 I_{\mathrm m}(\theta)
 =\frac{r+2b_J}{(r+2b_J)^2-\theta^2}.
\]
On either an all-treated or an all-control pair the two informative probabilities are
\(
 (1-r+\theta)/2
\) and
\(
 (1-r-\theta)/2
\), up to interchange, and hence
\[
 I_{\mathrm s}(\theta)
 =\frac{1-r}{(1-r)^2-\theta^2}.
\]
Uniformly for \(|\theta|\le b_J\),
\[
 I_{\mathrm m}(\theta)\le \frac1r+o(1),
 \qquad
 I_{\mathrm s}(\theta)\le\frac1{1-r}+o(1)
 \le\frac1r+o(1),
 \tag{3}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(r<1/2\).  Thus the Fisher information in \((A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\), averaged over any exogenous design, is at most \(J/r+o(J)\).

We verify the hypotheses of the tightened van Trees lemma rather than assuming a limit law.  The parameter domain \((-b_J,b_J)\) is bounded with piecewise continuously differentiable boundary; the scaled prior is continuously differentiable, has zero boundary trace, and has finite information.  The induced experiment has a finite alphabet and, on the support of any assignment, all its relevant cell probabilities are strictly positive and affine in \(\theta\).  Therefore differentiation under the finite sum is valid and gives the zero-mean likelihood-score identity.  The likelihood and prior scores are square-integrable by (3) and the definition of \(I_w\).  The boundary flux vanishes because the prior density vanishes at both endpoints.  Finally every clipped estimator is square-integrable.  Applying the positive scalar case of the cited van Trees lemma to \(\psi(\theta)=\theta\) therefore gives, uniformly over all designs and estimators,
\[
 \mathbb E(\delta-\theta)^2
 \ge \frac1{I_w/b_J^2+J/r+o(J)}
 =\frac{r+o(1)}J.
 \tag{4}
\]
By the \(L^2\) triangle inequality and (2),
\[
 \{\mathbb E(\delta-\tau_J)^2\}^{1/2}
 \ge
 \{\mathbb E(\delta-\theta)^2\}^{1/2}
 -\{\mathbb E(\tau_J-\theta)^2\}^{1/2},
\]
so (2)--(4) imply
\[
 \mathbb E(\delta-\tau_J)^2\ge\frac{r+o(1)}J.
 \tag{5}
\]

Only pairs of type \(D\) are armwise discordant, and their number \(N_D\) is \(\operatorname{Binomial}(J,r)\), independently of \(\theta\).  Put \(C_J=\{N_D\le s_J\}\).  Since \(s_J/J\to\rho>r\), one may choose \(\lambda>0\) so that, for some \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(J\), the exponential Markov calculation
\[
 \Pr(C_J^c)
 \le e^{-\lambda s_J}(1-r+re^\lambda)^J
 \le e^{-cJ}
 \tag{6}
\]
holds.  The event \(C_J\) has probability independent of \(\theta\), and the conditioned schedule prior is supported on \(\Theta_{J,s_J}\).  Since both \(\delta\) and \(\tau_J\) lie in \([-1,1]\), squared loss is at most four.  If \(L_J\) and \(L_{J,C}\) are the Bayes risks before and after conditioning, then
\[
 L_J
 \le \Pr(C_J)L_{J,C}+4\Pr(C_J^c).
\]
Equations (5)--(6) consequently give \(L_{J,C}\ge\{r+o(1)\}/J\).  A supremum over the fixed schedules in \(\Theta_{J,s_J}\) is at least this Bayes average, so
\[
 \liminf_{J\to\infty}J R^*(J,s_J)\ge r.
\]
Letting \(r\uparrow\min\{\rho,1/2\}\), and combining with (1), proves
\[
 \lim_{J\to\infty}J R^*(J,s_J)=\min\{\rho,1/2\}
\]
when \(\rho>0\).  When \(\rho=0\), nonnegativity and (1) give the same conclusion directly.

It remains to prove concentration for \(\rho<1/2\).  First take \(0<\rho<1/2\), fix \(0<r<\rho\), and condition the preceding scalar experiment on an assignment orbit \(n\).  Write \(m_n=n_{\mathrm m}/J\).  Equations (3)--(6), now retained at their orbit-specific values, the scalar van Trees inequality, and the same \(L^2\) transfer give, uniformly in \(n\),
\[
 J\,L_{J,C,n}\ge g_r(m_n)+o(1),
 \qquad
 g_r(m)=\left\{\frac m r+\frac{1-m}{1-r}\right\}^{-1},
 \tag{7}
\]
where \(L_{J,C,n}\) is the Bayes risk after hard-budget conditioning.  Indeed, conditional total likelihood information divided by \(J\) converges uniformly to \(m/r+(1-m)/(1-r)\), the prior information divided by \(J\) vanishes, (2) contributes only \(o(J^{-1})\) after the \(L^2\) transfer, and (6) contributes only \(O(e^{-cJ})\).

Let \(q_J\in\mathcal Q^*(J,s_J)\) and choose the invariant estimator supplied by the definition of \(\mathcal Q^*(J,s_J)\).  Averaging (7) over \(q_J\), the supremum over schedules dominates the conditioned Bayes average, and hence
\[
 J R^*(J,s_J)
 \ge \sum_nq_{J,n}g_r(m_n)+o(1).
 \tag{8}
\]
For \(r<1/2\), direct algebra gives
\[
 g_r(m)
 =\frac{r(1-r)}{r+m(1-2r)},
 \qquad
 g_r(m)-r
 =\frac{r(1-2r)(1-m)}{r+m(1-2r)}
 \ge \frac{r(1-2r)}{1-r}(1-m).
 \tag{9}
\]
Using the already proved value limit in (8)--(9) yields
\[
 \limsup_{J\to\infty}
 \sum_nq_{J,n}(1-m_n)
 \le
 \frac{\rho-r}{r(1-2r)/(1-r)}.
\]
Letting \(r\uparrow\rho\) shows that this limsup is zero.

For \(\rho=0\), use a prior already supported on \(\Theta_{J,0}\): independently over pairs and arms let \(Z_{ja}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and set \(Y_{j1}(a)=Y_{j2}(a)=Z_{ja}\).  Conditional on orbit \(n\), a mixed pair reveals both \(Z_{j0}\) and \(Z_{j1}\), whereas an unmixed pair reveals one and leaves the other as an independent fair Bernoulli variable.  Since
\(
 \tau=J^{-1}\sum_j(Z_{j1}-Z_{j0})
\), its conditional posterior variance is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\tau\mid A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}},n)
 =\frac{J-n_{\mathrm m}}{4J^2}.
\]
The posterior mean minimizes conditional squared loss, so every estimator has Bayes risk at least this quantity.  Therefore optimality and the value limit at \(\rho=0\) imply
\[
 J R^*(J,s_J)
 \ge\frac14\sum_nq_{J,n}(1-m_n),
 \qquad
 \sum_nq_{J,n}(1-m_n)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus the last convergence holds for every \(0\le\rho<1/2\).  For every \(x=(x_{00},x_{11},m)\in\Delta_2\),
\[
 \|x-x_0\|_2^2=x_{00}^2+x_{11}^2+(1-m)^2
 \le2(1-m)^2.
\]
Because a point mass admits only one coupling,
\[
 W_1(\mu_{q_J},\delta_{x_0})
 =\int\|x-x_0\|_2\,d\mu_{q_J}(x)
 \le\sqrt2\sum_nq_{J,n}(1-m_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This also gives weak convergence: for any continuous function on the compact simplex, uniform continuity and Markov's inequality applied to \(\|x-x_0\|_2\) show that its integral converges to its value at \(x_0\).  Hence \(\mu_{q_J}\Rightarrow\delta_{(0,0,1)}\), with the asserted equivalent \(W_1\) conclusion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The formula quantifies the exact robustness value of pairing and identifies the phase boundary where first-order risk stops selecting the design. \textit{Gap.} SudijonoDobribanTchetgen2026 and Hull2026BoundedOutcomes give the unrestricted endpoint constant, while Bai2022MatchedPairs gives a paired prescription under another criterion; no source derives this interpolation or concentration theorem. \textit{Consumer.} The curve maps an analyst-selected discordance fraction to the sharp first-order sample-size cost and identifies when the minimax design must concentrate asymptotically on mixed pairs.

\begin{theorem}[thm:first-order-face]\label{thm:first-order-face}
Theorem 5. Suppose \(s_J/J\to\rho\ge1/2\). Let \((q_J,\eta_J)_{J\ge2}\) be any sequence of invariant procedures satisfying \(\max_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s_J}}\mathcal R_h(q_J,\eta_J)=R^*(J,s_J)+o(J^{-1})\), and let \(\mu_{q_J}=q_J\circ\{n\mapsto(n_{00}/J,n_{11}/J,n_{\mathrm m}/J)\}^{-1}\). With \(\mathcal P(\Delta_2)\) endowed with the weak topology, every weak accumulation point \(\mu\) of the sequence \((\mu_{q_J})_{J\ge2}\) lies in \(\mathcal F_\rho\). Conversely, for every measure \(\mu\in\mathcal F_\rho\), there exists a sequence of invariant procedures \((q_J,\eta_J)_{J\ge2}\) such that \(\mu_{q_J}\Rightarrow\mu\) and \(\max_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s_J}}\mathcal R_h(q_J,\eta_J)=R^*(J,s_J)+o(J^{-1})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 v_J=\max_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s_J}}\mathcal R_h(q_J,\eta_J).
\]
The hypothesis and Theorem 4 give
\[
 Jv_J=J R^*(J,s_J)+o(1)\longrightarrow\frac12.
 \tag{11}
\]
We first derive the restrictions on an arbitrary weak accumulation point.  Pass to a subsequence, still indexed by \(J\), for which \(\mu_{q_J}\Rightarrow\mu\).  Fix \(r\in(0,\rho)\); when \(\rho=1/2\), take \(r<1/2\).  Put \(b_J=J^{-1/4}\), and give \(\theta\) and \(\phi\) independent priors with density \(b_J^{-1}w(\cdot/b_J)\), where \(w\) is the smooth zero-boundary density used in the proof of Theorem 4.  Conditional on \((\theta,\phi)\), draw pairs independently with probabilities
\[
 \frac{1-r-2b_J-\phi}{2},\quad
 \frac{1-r-2b_J+\phi}{2},\quad
 r,\quad
 b_J+\frac\theta2,\quad
 b_J-\frac\theta2
 \tag{12}
\]
for \(H_0,H_1,D,E_+,E_-\), respectively.  These probabilities are strictly positive for all sufficiently large \(J\).  As in (2),
\[
 \mathbb E(\tau_J\mid\theta,\phi)=\theta,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\{(\tau_J-\theta)^2\mid\theta,\phi\}
 =\frac{2b_J-\theta^2}{J}\le\frac{2b_J}{J}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The number of discordant pairs is again \(\operatorname{Binomial}(J,r)\) and is independent of \((\theta,\phi)\).

For parameter order \((\theta,\phi)\), summing \((\nabla p)(\nabla p)^{\!\top}/p\) over the finite local observation alphabet gives the exact information matrices for a mixed, all-treated, and all-control pair:
\[
 I_{\mathrm m,J}(\theta,\phi)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \dfrac{r+2b_J}{(r+2b_J)^2-\theta^2}&0\\[5pt]
 0&\dfrac{1-r-2b_J}{(1-r-2b_J)^2-\phi^2}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \tag{14}
\]
\[
 I_{11,J}(\theta,\phi)=
 \frac{1-r}{(1-r)^2-(\theta+\phi)^2}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 I_{00,J}(\theta,\phi)=
 \frac{1-r}{(1-r)^2-(\phi-\theta)^2}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{15}
\]
For example, on a mixed pair the four observed categories have probabilities
\[
 \frac{1-r-2b_J-\phi}{2},\quad
 \frac{1-r-2b_J+\phi}{2},\quad
 \frac{r+2b_J+\theta}{2},\quad
 \frac{r+2b_J-\theta}{2},
\]
which proves (14); combining \(H_0\) with \(E_-\) and \(H_1\) with \(E_+\) proves the first formula in (15), and the analogous control-arm combination proves the second.

Conditional on orbit \(n\), set
\[
 m_n=\frac{n_{\mathrm m}}J,
 \qquad
 d_n=\frac{n_{11}-n_{00}}J.
\]
The prior-averaged likelihood information is the corresponding sum of (14)--(15).  Since \(|\theta|,|\phi|\le b_J\), its value divided by \(J\), including the prior information \(I_w b_J^{-2}I_2/J=o(1)I_2\), converges uniformly over all orbits to
\[
 M_r(m,d)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \dfrac m r+\dfrac{1-m}{1-r}&\dfrac d{1-r}\\[6pt]
 \dfrac d{1-r}&\dfrac1{1-r}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{16}
\]

The tightened matrix van Trees lemma applies conditionally on every orbit.  Indeed, the parameter domain \((-b_J,b_J)^2\) is a bounded rectangle with piecewise continuously differentiable boundary; the product prior is continuously differentiable, has zero trace on every boundary face, and has finite information.  The experiment has a finite alphabet with strictly positive affine cell probabilities on the prior support.  Hence coordinatewise differentiation under the finite sum yields the zero-mean likelihood-score identity, all likelihood and prior scores are square-integrable by (14)--(15), and the zero boundary trace makes the integration-by-parts flux vanish.  The clipped estimator is square-integrable.  The total information matrix is positive definite because the prior-information matrix is positive definite, so the ordinary-inverse conclusion of the cited lemma applies to \(\psi(\theta,\phi)=\theta\), with \(a=e_1=(1,0)^{\!\top}\).

To pass uniformly through possibly singular limits, for \(\varepsilon>0\) define the continuous bounded function
\[
 g_{r,\varepsilon}(m,d)
 =e_1^{\!\top}\{M_r(m,d)+\varepsilon I_2\}^{-1}e_1.
\]
Uniform convergence in (16) implies that, for all sufficiently large \(J\), the total information divided by \(J\) is bounded above in Loewner order by \(M_r(m_n,d_n)+\varepsilon I_2\).  Since inversion reverses Loewner order on positive definite matrices, van Trees gives
\[
 J\,\mathbb E\{(\eta_J-\theta)^2\mid n\}
 \ge g_{r,\varepsilon}(m_n,d_n).
\]
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality and (13) replace \(\theta\) by \(\tau_J\), at a uniform error tending to zero because \(g_{r,\varepsilon}\le\varepsilon^{-1}\).  Conditioning on the event \(C_J=\{N_D\le s_J\}\) changes this bound by \(o(1)\) after multiplication by \(J\): the exponential estimate (6) applies because \(r<\rho\), the event is parameter-independent, and squared loss is at most four.  Thus, uniformly in \(n\),
\[
 J\,\mathbb E\{(\eta_J-\tau_J)^2\mid n,C_J\}
 \ge g_{r,\varepsilon}(m_n,d_n)-o(1).
 \tag{17}
\]
The conditioned prior is supported on \(\Theta_{J,s_J}\).  Averaging (17) over \(q_J\), its Bayes risk is bounded above by the worst fixed-schedule risk \(v_J\), and therefore
\[
 Jv_J\ge\int g_{r,\varepsilon}(m,d)\,d\mu_{q_J}-o(1).
 \tag{18}
\]
Weak convergence, bounded continuity of \(g_{r,\varepsilon}\), and (11) give
\[
 \int g_{r,\varepsilon}(m,d)\,d\mu\le\frac12.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), these functions increase pointwise to
\[
 g_r(m,d)=
 \left\{\frac m r+\frac{1-m-d^2}{1-r}\right\}^{-1},
 \tag{19}
\]
with value \(+\infty\) when the denominator is zero.  To see (19), take the Schur complement of the lower-right entry of (16).  Feasibility in \(\Delta_2\) gives \(|d|\le1-m\), hence \(1-m-d^2\ge0\); the denominator vanishes only at \((m,d)=(0,1)\) or \((0,-1)\).  Monotone convergence in (18) therefore proves
\[
 \int g_r(m,d)\,d\mu\le\frac12
 \qquad(0<r<\rho).
 \tag{20}
\]
This regularization also explains the singular case of the tightened van Trees lemma: at either singular endpoint \(e_1\) is not in the range of \(M_r\), and the regularized bound diverges, so no unjustified ordinary inverse is taken.

If \(\rho>1/2\), choose \(r=1/2\) in (20).  If \(\rho=1/2\), let \(r\uparrow1/2\) in (20) and use Fatou's lemma.  In either case,
\[
 \int\frac1{2(1-d^2)}\,d\mu\le\frac12.
 \tag{21}
\]
The integrand is at least \(1/2\), with equality exactly when \(d=0\); therefore
\[
 \mu\{d=0\}=1.
 \tag{22}
\]
On this line put
\[
 G_\mu(r)=\int
 \left\{\frac m r+\frac{1-m}{1-r}\right\}^{-1}d\mu.
\]
For \(r\) in a fixed neighborhood of \(1/2\), the integrand and its derivative are uniformly bounded on \(0\le m\le1\), so differentiation under the integral is justified.  Direct differentiation gives
\[
 G_\mu(1/2)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 G_\mu'(1/2)=\int(2m-1)\,d\mu
 =2\int m\,d\mu-1.
 \tag{23}
\]
At \(\rho=1/2\), (20) holds for \(r<1/2\), while \(G_\mu(1/2)=1/2\); its left derivative at \(1/2\) is therefore nonnegative, and (23) gives \(\int m\,d\mu\ge1/2\).  If \(\rho>1/2\), (20) holds on both sides of \(1/2\), so \(G_\mu\) has a local maximum there; (23) then gives \(\int m\,d\mu=1/2\).  Together with (22), these are precisely the conditions defining \(\mathcal F_\rho\).  This proves necessity.

For recovery, fix \(\mu\in\mathcal F_\rho\).  The balanced finite-orbit grid is
\[
 E_J=\left\{
 \left(\frac kJ,\frac kJ,1-\frac{2k}J\right):
 0\le k\le\lfloor J/2\rfloor
 \right\}.
 \tag{24}
\]
It has mixed-coordinate mesh at most \(2/J\) on the line \(d=0\).  Randomly rounding each point on that line to its adjacent grid points constructs a probability law \(\widetilde q_J\) on \(E_J\) coupled to \(\mu\) with mixed-coordinate error at most \(2/J\), and hence with Euclidean error at most \(\sqrt6/J\); in particular \(\mu_{\widetilde q_J}\Rightarrow\mu\), and its mean mixed fraction differs from \(\int m\,d\mu\) by at most \(2/J\).  If \(\int m\,d\mu=1/2\), mix \(\widetilde q_J\), as needed, with the grid endpoint having mixed fraction \(1\) or with the endpoint having mixed fraction \(0\) for even \(J\) and \(1/J\) for odd \(J\), choosing the mixing weight so that the resulting mean is exactly \(1/2\).  The required weight is \(O(J^{-1})\), so weak convergence is preserved.  If \(\rho=1/2\) and \(\int m\,d\mu>1/2\), no correction is needed for all sufficiently large \(J\).  We have therefore constructed laws \(q_J\) supported on (24), converging weakly to \(\mu\), such that
\[
 \bar m_J:=\sum_nq_{J,n}\frac{n_{\mathrm m}}J
 =\frac12\quad\text{if }\rho>1/2,
 \qquad
 \bar m_J\ge\frac12\quad\text{if }\rho=1/2.
 \tag{25}
\]

Let the assignment law be uniform within each orbit and have orbit masses \(q_J\), and use
\[
 \widehat\tau_J
 =\frac1{2J}\sum_{j,r}(2A_{jr}-1)(2Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{jr}-1).
 \tag{26}
\]
The law is ex ante and outcome-independent.  The estimator is clipped and invariant, hence it induces an array \(\eta_J\) indexed by the assignment and observation orbits.  Write \(S_{jr}=2A_{jr}-1\).  Every orbit in (24) has exactly \(J\) treated units, and invariance gives
\[
 \mathbb E S_{jr}=0,
 \qquad
 c_{\mathrm w}:=\mathbb E(S_{j1}S_{j2})=1-2\bar m_J.
 \tag{27}
\]
For units in distinct pairs, invariance makes \(c_{\mathrm b}:=\mathbb E(S_{jr}S_{k\ell})\) constant.  Squaring the deterministic identity \(\sum_{j,r}S_{jr}=0\), taking expectations, and counting ordered within-pair and between-pair terms gives
\[
 0=2J+2Jc_{\mathrm w}+4J(J-1)c_{\mathrm b},
 \qquad
 c_{\mathrm b}=-\frac{1-\bar m_J}{J-1}.
 \tag{28}
\]

Set \(v_{jr}=Y_{jr}(1)+Y_{jr}(0)-1\) and \(w_j=v_{j1}+v_{j2}\).  The same pointwise substitution as in the Bernoulli calculation gives
\(
 \widehat\tau_J-\tau=(2J)^{-1}\sum_{j,r}S_{jr}v_{jr}
\).  Equations (27)--(28) then yield the exact expansion
\[
\begin{split}
 4J^2\mathbb E(\widehat\tau_J-\tau)^2
 ={}&\sum_j\{v_{j1}^2+v_{j2}^2
       +2c_{\mathrm w}v_{j1}v_{j2}\}\\
 &+c_{\mathrm b}\left\{
       \left(\sum_jw_j\right)^2-\sum_jw_j^2
       \right\}.
\end{split}
 \tag{29}
\]
By (25), \(-1\le c_{\mathrm w}\le0\), and (28) gives \(c_{\mathrm b}\le0\).  Since \(|w_j|\le2\),
\[
 c_{\mathrm b}\left\{
 (\sum_jw_j)^2-\sum_jw_j^2\right\}
 \le -c_{\mathrm b}\sum_jw_j^2
 \le\frac{4J(1-\bar m_J)}{J-1}=O(1)
 \tag{30}
\]
uniformly over schedules.  Let
\(
 e_j=\sum_{a=0}^1\mathbf 1\{Y_{j1}(a)\ne Y_{j2}(a)\}
\).  The four binary unit types give, for every \(c\in[-1,0]\),
\[
 v_{j1}^2+v_{j2}^2+2cv_{j1}v_{j2}
 \le2(1+c)-2ce_j.
 \tag{31}
\]
For completeness, if both \(v\)'s are nonzero, the unit types are \((0,0)\) or \((1,1)\), and (31) is equality for equal as well as opposite signs; if exactly one is nonzero, then \(e_j=1\) and the two sides are \(1\) and \(2\); if both vanish, the right side is nonnegative whether the two effect types agree or disagree.  Thus (31) is exhaustive.

Summing (31), using the two armwise bounds \(D_0(Y)+D_1(Y)\le2s_J\), and then applying (29)--(30), gives
\[
 \sup_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s_J}}
 \mathcal R_h(q_J,\eta_J)
 \le
 \frac{1+c_{\mathrm w}(1-2s_J/J)}{2J}
 +O(J^{-2}).
 \tag{32}
\]
If \(\rho>1/2\), (25) makes \(c_{\mathrm w}=0\).  If \(\rho=1/2\), then \(-1\le c_{\mathrm w}\le0\) and \(1-2s_J/J=o(1)\).  In both cases the right side of (32) is \(1/(2J)+o(J^{-1})\).  Theorem 4 gives \(R^*(J,s_J)=1/(2J)+o(J^{-1})\), and the minimax value is no larger than the worst risk of this feasible procedure.  Hence
\[
 \max_{h\in\mathcal H_{J,s_J}}\mathcal R_h(q_J,\eta_J)
 =R^*(J,s_J)+o(J^{-1}),
\]
while construction gives \(\mu_{q_J}\Rightarrow\mu\).  This proves recovery and completes the characterization.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem identifies exactly why the leading minimax value cannot by itself determine limits of exact finite optimizers. \textit{Gap.} HarshawSavjeSpielmanZhang2024 gives covariance guarantees for one estimator and SudijonoDobribanTchetgen2026 separates first- and second-order risk in another model; neither characterizes this measure-valued first-order face. \textit{Consumer.} For each high-mismatch sensitivity sequence, the envelope identifies the macroscopic assignment mixtures that need an exact or smaller-order selection criterion.

\begin{proposition}[prop:exact-inversion]\label{prop:exact-inversion}
Proposition 7. For every analyst-selected \(s\), invariant law \(q\), orbit estimator \(\eta\), and \(Y\in\Theta_{J,s}\), the confidence set \(\mathcal C_{\alpha,s}(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfies \[\Pr_{A\sim q}\{\tau(Y)\in\mathcal C_{\alpha,s}(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\}\ge1-\alpha.\] The set and its interval hull are computable using the same \(O(J^{20})\)-entry rectangular rational orbit kernel as Theorem 1, including when \(q\) is correlated and essentially noncanonical. Varying \(s\) produces a coverage-guaranteed sensitivity family; neither its members nor their interval hulls are asserted to be nested when the design or statistic is reoptimized.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(Y\in\Theta_{J,s}\), let \(h_Y\in\mathcal H_{J,s}\) be its schedule orbit, and put \(t_0=\tau(Y)=\tau_{h_Y}\). By the coefficient-counting proof in Theorem 1, under the invariant design the probability of cell \((n,o)\) is \(q_nK_{h_Yno}\). For this simple schedule-orbit null define
\[
 p_{h_Y}(n,o)=\sum_{n',o'}q_{n'}K_{h_Yn'o'}
 \mathbf 1\{T_{t_0}(n',o')\ge T_{t_0}(n,o)\}.
\]
This tail value is superuniform. Indeed, order the finitely many distinct values of \(T_{t_0}\) from largest to smallest and let \(S_k\) be the cumulative probability through level \(k\). Every cell at level \(k\) has \(p_{h_Y}=S_k\), so the union of levels satisfying \(S_k\le\alpha\) has probability equal to its largest such cumulative probability and is at most \(\alpha\). Thus
\[
 \Pr_{h_Y,q}\{p_{h_Y}(n(A),o(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}))\le\alpha\}\le\alpha.
\]

Every realized cell has \(K_{h_Yno}>0\) almost surely. Therefore the maximization in \(p_{t_0}\) contains the true orbit \(h_Y\), and
\[
 p_{t_0}(n,o)\ge p_{h_Y}(n,o).
\]
Using the strict acceptance rule in \texttt{def:exact-confidence-set},
\[
 \Pr\{\tau(Y)\notin\mathcal C_{\alpha,s}\}
 =\Pr\{p_{t_0}(n(A),o(A,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}))\le\alpha\}
 \le\alpha.
\]
This proves the claimed coverage. The interval hull contains the confidence set, so it has at least the same coverage.

The target grid has \(4J+1\) points, all feasible schedule nulls are among the already enumerated histograms, and every tail probability is a finite sum of entries \(q_nK_{hno}\). Hence the set and its hull use the same \(O(J^{20})\)-entry rational kernel as Theorem 1. No factorization of \(q\) was used, so arbitrary correlated and essentially noncanonical invariant laws are covered. Repeating the argument separately for each analyst-selected \(s\) gives the sensitivity family. Coverage is pointwise in \(s\); the proof uses no inclusion relation between procedures or acceptance regions at different values of \(s\), so it makes no nesting assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact coverage makes a nonstandard minimax law usable in small binary experiments without an asymptotic approximation. \textit{Gap.} Wilson2026MatchedBinary and Zhang2026FastBinaryCI rely on within-pair coin flips or product Bernoulli structure, while LiSpertusStark2025 handles stratification but not arbitrary correlated orbit laws on an optimal face. \textit{Consumer.} Investigators can report a finite-sample SATE confidence-set sensitivity family indexed by \(s\), with each member uniformly valid over its corresponding latent mismatch class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:sion-minimax]\label{lem:sion-minimax}
Let \(X\) and \(Z\) be compact convex sets and let \(f:X\times Z\to\mathbb R\) be lower semicontinuous and convex in its first argument and upper semicontinuous and concave in its second argument. Then
\[
 \min_{x\in X}\max_{z\in Z}f(x,z)=\max_{z\in Z}\min_{x\in X}f(x,z),
\]
and both extrema are attained.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:multivariate-van-trees]\label{lem:multivariate-van-trees}
Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a bounded open parameter domain with piecewise continuously differentiable boundary and outward unit normal \(n\), and let \(\pi\) be a continuously differentiable prior density on \(\Omega\) whose trace vanishes on \(\partial\Omega\). Let \(P_\vartheta\) be dominated by a measure \(\lambda\), with density \(p_\vartheta\). On the sets where the relevant densities are positive, define the likelihood score \(\ell_\vartheta(x)=\nabla_\vartheta\log p_\vartheta(x)\), the prior score \(s_\pi(\vartheta)=\nabla_\vartheta\log\pi(\vartheta)\), and
\[
 I_{\mathrm{pr}}
 =\mathbb E_\pi\!\left[
 s_\pi(\vartheta)s_\pi(\vartheta)^{\!\top}
 \right],
\]
\[
 I(\vartheta)
 =\mathbb E_\vartheta\!\left[
 \ell_\vartheta(X)\ell_\vartheta(X)^{\!\top}
 \right].
\]
Assume these matrices are finite and that the joint score \(S(X,\vartheta)=\ell_\vartheta(X)+s_\pi(\vartheta)\) is square-integrable. Assume also that differentiation under the integral is valid coordinatewise and yields the score identity
\[
 \int \nabla_\vartheta p_\vartheta(x)\,d\lambda(x)
 =\nabla_\vartheta\int p_\vartheta(x)\,d\lambda(x)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\vartheta\{\ell_\vartheta(X)\}=0,
\]
and that, for the square-integrable estimator \(T=T(X)\) and differentiable scalar functional \(\psi\) under consideration, integration by parts is valid with zero boundary flux,
\[
 \int_{\partial\Omega}\int
 \{T(x)-\psi(\vartheta)\}p_\vartheta(x)\pi(\vartheta)
 n(\vartheta)\,d\lambda(x)\,dS(\vartheta)=0.
\]
Suppose \(a=\mathbb E_\pi\{\nabla\psi(\vartheta)\}\) is finite, and put
\[
 M=I_{\mathrm{pr}}+\mathbb E_\pi I(\vartheta).
\]
Then the score identity and integration by parts give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \{T(X)-\psi(\vartheta)\}S(X,\vartheta)
 \right]=a.
\]
If \(M\) is positive definite, then
\[
 \mathbb E\{T(X)-\psi(\vartheta)\}^{2}
 \ge a^{\!\top}M^{-1}a.
\]
If \(M\) is singular, the displayed score identity necessarily implies \(a\in\operatorname{Range}(M)\); under this necessary range condition the correct form is
\[
 \mathbb E\{T(X)-\psi(\vartheta)\}^{2}
 \ge a^{\!\top}M^{+}a,
\]
where \(M^{+}\) is the Moore--Penrose inverse. In one dimension the same assertions reduce to the corresponding scalar inequality, with ordinary division only when \(M>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:unrestricted-binary-bernoulli-minimax]\label{lem:unrestricted-binary-bernoulli-minimax}
In the unrestricted fixed-population problem on \(2J\) units with potential outcomes in a known bounded interval, minimization over all assignment laws and all measurable estimators is attained by independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) assignment together with the nonlinear Bayes rule generated by a least-favourable endpoint prior. In particular, for binary outcomes this procedure attains \(r^{\mathrm U}_{2J}\).
\end{lemma}
