Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\), \(T\ge2\), and the law \(P^{\dagger}\) in \(\mathrm{def:directional\text{-}matrix\text{-}witness}\). The context is constant, and the two nonzero potential outcomes are independent across occasions and arms with the displayed time-invariant laws. Hence the full-data vectors are independent and identically distributed. At occasion \(t\), \(\eta_{t-1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable and the action is drawn from \(\Pi(\cdot\mid X_t,\eta_{t-1})\) independently of current potential outcomes, verifying sequential randomization.

Writing \(u\in[0,1]\) for the state, linear interpolation gives
\[
 \Pi(1\mid x,u)=\frac25,\qquad
 \Pi(2\mid x,u)=\frac14-\frac{u}{12},\qquad
 \Pi(3\mid x,u)=\frac7{20}+\frac{u}{12}.
\tag{1}
\]
The probabilities sum to one and their coordinatewise minimum is \(1/6>\varepsilon\), proving overlap. Also
\[
 \sup_{a\in\{1,2,3\}}|\Pi(a\mid x,u)-\Pi(a\mid x,u')|
 \le\frac1{12}|u-u'|,
\tag{2}
\]
so \(L_\Pi=1/12\) is valid.

For every \(\theta\in[-1,1]^2\),
\[
 |Y_t(1)-\theta_1|\le1+\sqrt{2/5}<2,
 \qquad |Y_t(2)-\theta_2|\le1+\sqrt{1/2}<2.
\tag{3}
\]
Each nonzero score has only one nonzero coordinate and the third-arm score is zero, so \(B=2\) is a common envelope. The score is affine in \(\theta\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), with derivative matrices
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0.
\]
Their operator norms are at most one and all second derivatives vanish. Thus \(D_1=D_2=1\) are valid envelopes, with actual curvature zero.

Both nonzero outcomes have mean zero. Therefore
\[
 M_{P^{\dagger}}(\theta)=(-\theta_1,-\theta_2)^\top=-\theta,
 \qquad H=-I_2.
\tag{4}
\]
It follows that \(\theta^*=0\) is the unique root, \(\|M_{P^{\dagger}}(\theta)\|_2=\|\theta\|_2\), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)=1\). Hence \(\kappa(u)=u\) and \(h_-=1\) are valid, while
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(0,\partial[-1,1]^2)=1
\tag{5}
\]
gives \(s_\Theta=1\).

For completeness, the empirical moment has an exact zero for every assignment and outcome array. For \(a\in\{1,2\}\), put

 W_a=\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}}

 \theta_a^{(0)}=

\tag{6}

if \(W_a=0\), set \(\theta_a^{(0)}=0\). In the first case (6) is a convex combination of values in \([-1,1]\), and in the second case the corresponding empirical coordinate is identically zero. Hence \(\theta^{(0)}\in\Theta\), direct substitution gives \(G_T(\theta^{(0)})=0\), and

 \min_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)\|_2=0.
\tag{7}

At the first occasion \(\eta_0=1/2\), and (1) gives \(P^{\dagger}(A_1=1)=2/5\). Since \(J=1\) exactly when \(A_1=1\),

 P^{\dagger}(J=1)=\frac25,

\tag{8}

The label \(J\) is \(\mathcal F_1\)-measurable, \(\eta_\infty=\bar\eta_J\), and the two fixed attractors have distance one.  \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J|=\frac1{2T}

\tag{9}

Thus the attraction condition holds with \(r_T=1/(2T)\) and \(\delta_T=0\), and

 e_T=T^{-1/2}+\frac1{2T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}

 0<\rho=\frac\varepsilon8

Finally, (4) and the sandwich definition imply
\[
 V_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
 \qquad V_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,3).
\tag{15}
\]
For \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(c=(1,0)^\top\),
\[
 c^\top V_1c=c^\top V_2c=c^\top\Sigma c=1,
 \qquad V_1\ne V_2.
\tag{16}
\]
Along every horizon sequence the law of \(V_J\) is
\[
 \mu=\frac25\delta_{V_1}+\frac35\delta_{V_2}.
\tag{17}
\]
The projected equality (16) and \(\mathrm{thm:contrastwise\text{-}deterministic\text{-}sandwich\text{-}iff}\) give every-level validity for this contrast. But (8), (15), and (17) make \(V_J\) nonconstant; \(\mathrm{thm:matrixwise\text{-}deterministic\text{-}sandwich\text{-}iff}\) therefore rules out validity of one deterministic sandwich for all nonzero contrasts. This proves the separation. In particular, the claim has been checked at the smallest allowed horizon \(T=2\): equation (7) holds for every realized assignment, including assignments that omit either active arm, so no hidden root-existence exception is used.
