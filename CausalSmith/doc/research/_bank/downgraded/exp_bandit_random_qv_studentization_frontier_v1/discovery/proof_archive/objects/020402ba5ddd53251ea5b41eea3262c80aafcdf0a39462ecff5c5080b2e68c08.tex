All stochastic orders below are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\).  Fix the prespecified \(c\ne0\), and put
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]
Sequential randomization and independent full-data arrivals give
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
\tag{2}
\]
At \(\theta^*\), \(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\) is a martingale difference and \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\).  Bounded scores and overlap imply
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon,
 \qquad E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2\le M_0^2/T.
\tag{3}
\]

For root localization, the derivative envelope makes \(G_T\) and \(M_P\) Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with common moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\).  For fixed \(u>0\), take a finite net of the common compact set \(\Theta\) with mesh smaller than
\[
 \frac{\kappa(u)}{8(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
At each net point the centered terms in (2) are martingale differences of norm at most \(2M_0\), so
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le4M_0^2/T.
\tag{5}
\]
Chebyshev's inequality, a finite union bound, and the Lipschitz bounds yield
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>\kappa(u)/2\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The preliminary estimator inequality and (3) give
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1).
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) and root separation prove \(\widetilde\theta_T\to\theta^*\) uniformly in probability.

Let \(A_T^\partial=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\).  Differentiating (2) under the common derivative envelope makes every entry of \(A_T^\partial-H\) an average of bounded matrix martingale differences.  Since \(d\) is fixed,
\[
 A_T^\partial-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{8}
\]
The Jacobian lower bound and the singular-value perturbation inequality imply, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T^\partial)\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho\), curvature and overlap give
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T^\partial\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le(D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{10}
\]
On (9), \(F_T(\theta)=\theta-(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta)\) has Lipschitz modulus at most
\[
 q_0=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12
\tag{11}
\]
on the closed \(\rho\)-ball.  Root interiority puts this ball in \(\Theta\), and (3), (9) make \(\|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  Thus, with uniformly asymptotic probability one, \(F_T\) maps the ball into itself.  Its iterates are Cauchy by (11), so closedness and continuity give a unique fixed point \(\theta_T^0\), with
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\frac{\|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{12}
\]
Consistency of \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and (12) place this root within \(\rho/2\) of \(\widetilde\theta_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) lies in the \(\rho\)-ball.  Uniqueness therefore gives
\[
 \sup_P P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{13}
\]
On \(E_T\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=G_T(\theta^*)+\overline H_T
 (\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*),
 \quad
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 (\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*))\,ds.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (8), (10), and (13) imply \(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  The inverse identity
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1}
\tag{15}
\]
and (3), (14) yield
\[
 R_T:=\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 +H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{16}
\]
The fallback branch changes only the uniformly negligible event \(E_T^c\).

For the matrix comparisons, define
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\tag{17}
\]
Every entry is a martingale difference and \(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\).  Entrywise orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le C_dM_0^4/T.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  Smoothness, overlap, and (13) give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{19}
\]
Expanding the score outer-product difference and using boundedness gives
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{20}
\]

For the directional step put
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\qquad
 Q_T^c=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2=a^\top[S]_Ta.
\tag{21}
\]
Jacobian nonsingularity and (3) give
\[
 |U_t|\le L_c:=\frac{B\|c\|_2}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{22}
\]
The class definition supplies a positive \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable \(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}\) such that
\[
 T\omega_{c,T}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad \frac{k_T}{T\omega_{c,T}}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{23}
\]
and projected predictable-QV growth says
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1
\tag{24}
\]
uniformly in probability.  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}scale\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\) with \(N_n=T\), \(h_n=k_T\), \(\lambda_n=\Lambda_T\), and \(\nu_n=\omega_{c,T}\).  Equations (22)--(24) verify all its hypotheses, so
\[
 \frac{Q_T^c}{T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly.  Uniform weak convergence to the continuous standard-normal law is equivalent here to uniform Kolmogorov convergence: on a compact interval use a finite grid and monotonicity, and then use the normal tails outside that interval.

Equation (20), the Jacobian lower bound, and (15) imply \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) and, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{26}
\]
Let
\[
 q_T^0=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T(a^\top\xi_t)^2,\qquad
 \widehat u_t=c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\xi_t
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal H_T.
\tag{27}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality in Euclidean \(T\)-space, followed by (19), (26), \(\|\widehat\xi_t\|_2\le M_0\), and \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}-\sqrt{q_T^0}\right|
 &\le\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 |\widehat u_t-a^\top\xi_t|^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \|c\|_2\|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}M_0
 +\|a\|_2(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Division by \(\sqrt{\Lambda_T}\ge\sqrt{\omega_{c,T}}\) is legal, and (23) makes the resulting error \(o_P(1)\).  Combining (25) and (28) yields, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_T}}=1+o_P(1).
\tag{29}
\]
Therefore \(\widehat q_T(c)\ge\Lambda_T/2\ge\omega_{c,T}/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one, so
\[
 \sup_P P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{30}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), (16), (23), and (29) imply
\[
 \frac{|c^\top R_T|}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt{T\omega_{c,T}}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{31}
\]
Using (16), (21), (25), and (29) in the pivot definition gives
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}+o_P(1).
\tag{32}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\) is negligible by (30).  For any \(h>0\), the inclusions
\[
 \{Y_T\le z-h\}\setminus\{|R_T'|>h\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T+R_T'\le z\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T\le z+h\}\cup\{|R_T'|>h\}
\tag{33}
\]
together with (25), uniform continuity of \(\Phi\), and then \(h\downarrow0\), prove the uniform Kolmogorov limit.  Since \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\),
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
\tag{34}
\]
The normal limit and \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) prove coverage.

Finally take \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\).  Set
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\min\{T-1,\lceil T\beta_T\rceil\}.
\tag{35}
\]
This gives \(0\le k_T<T\) at every horizon.  Since \(r_T\to0\), it also gives \(k_T/T\to0\), and eventually \(k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil\ge1\) with \(Tr_T/k_T\le r_T/\beta_T\to0\).  Let \(J_T\) be the fixed-tie-broken nearest attractor to \(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\), using its declared value \(1\) when \(k_T=0\).  Then \(J_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  On the attraction event,
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le Tr_T/k_T<s_\eta/2
\tag{36}
\]
eventually.  Separation gives \(J_T=J\) there, hence \(\sup_PP(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\to0\).

With \(a=H^{-\top}c\), take
\[
 \Lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a,\qquad
 \omega_{c,T}=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}.
\tag{37}
\]
The scale is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  The basin-stable theorem gives \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\) and \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\), whence
\[
 \Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}>0.
\tag{38}
\]
The lower bound in (37) is constant in \(T\), so \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) and \(k_T/(T\omega_{c,T})\to0\).

Policy Lipschitzness, overlap, and bounded scores imply
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad \|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{39}
\]
On the attraction event, where \(J_T=J\),
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-
 \Omega_{J_T}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}r_T
 +\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_T}{T}.
\tag{40}
\]
Sequential randomization and independent full-data arrivals give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).
\tag{41}
\]
Since \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\) and the denominator in (37) is bounded below by (38), equations (39)--(41) yield on that event
\[
 \left|
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[(a^\top\xi_t)^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}-1\right|
 \le C_c\left(r_T+\frac{k_T}{T}\right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{42}
\]
The complementary probability is at most \(\delta_T\to0\) uniformly.  Thus (35)--(42) verify the relative suffix predictable-QV condition and all other early-scale premises.  The finite-attractor class inherits the pivot and coverage conclusions.
