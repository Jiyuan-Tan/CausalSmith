Use the notation and construction of Propositions~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\) and \(\mathrm{prop:saco\text{-}theorem\text{-}5\text{-}14\text{-}counterexample}\).  Thus \(T=2n\); during the first block the scores are independent with
\[
P(\xi_t=2)=P(\xi_t=-2)=\frac12,\qquad 1\le t\le n,
\tag{1}
\]
and the sign of \(S_n=\sum_{t=1}^n\xi_t\) selects conditional variance \(16/3\) for the second block when \(S_n>0\), and \(4\) otherwise.

Let \(k=k_{2n}=o(2n)\).  Then \(k<n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  We first show that the branch cannot be predicted from \(\mathcal F_k\).  Put
\[
p_n=P(S_n>0\mid\mathcal F_k).
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_k\), the remainder \(S_n-S_k\) is the sum of \(n-k\) independent variables having the law in (1).  The maximum point mass of a sum of \(r\) symmetric signs is at most \(C/\sqrt r\).  For completeness, at even \(r=2\ell\) the maximum is
\[
2^{-2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{2j-1}{2j}\le\frac1{\sqrt{\ell+1}},
\tag{2}
\]
where the inequality follows by induction from
\[
\frac{2\ell+1}{2\ell+2}\le\sqrt{\frac{\ell+1}{\ell+2}};
\]
the odd case is bounded by an adjacent even case.  Symmetry and (2) therefore imply
\[
\left|p_n-\frac12\right|\le\frac{C(1+|S_k|)}{\sqrt{n-k}}.
\tag{3}
\]
By martingale orthogonality in the first block, \(E S_k^2=4k\), whence \(E|S_k|\le2\sqrt k\).  Taking expectations in (3) and using \(k/n\to0\) gives
\[
E\left|p_n-\frac12\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable binary predictor \(B_n\), conditional error is at least \(\min(p_n,1-p_n)\).  Hence (4) yields
\[
P\{B_n\ne\mathbf1(S_n>0)\}\ge E\min(p_n,1-p_n)\longrightarrow\frac12.
\tag{5}
\]

Now compute the suffix predictable QV.  With \(J_n=\mathbf1\{S_n>0\}\), equations (8)--(10) of the first proposition give
\[
q_n:=\frac1{2n}\sum_{t=k+1}^{2n}E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{14}{3}-\frac{2k}{n},&J_n=1,\\[4pt]
\displaystyle4-\frac{2k}{n},&J_n=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The two possible values differ by \(2/3\).  Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a positive \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable \(\Lambda_{2n}\) satisfied \(q_n/\Lambda_{2n}\to1\) in probability.  Since \(q_n<5\), on \(\{|q_n/\Lambda_{2n}-1|<1/2\}\) one has \(\Lambda_{2n}<10\), and therefore
\[
|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|\le10|q_n/\Lambda_{2n}-1|.
\]
It follows that \(|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|\to0\) in probability.  Define the \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable predictor
\[
B_n=\mathbf1\left\{\Lambda_{2n}>\frac{13}{3}-\frac{2k}{n}\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
By (6), \(B_n=J_n\) whenever \(|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|<1/3\), and hence
\[
P(B_n\ne J_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]
This contradicts (5).  Thus no such early-measurable scale exists.  The argument did not use an upper bound on \(\Lambda_{2n}\), and so applies a fortiori when the scale is required to be uniformly bounded away from zero.  In the Saco specialization \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=T\), proving the final class-membership assertion.
