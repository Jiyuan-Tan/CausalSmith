All stochastic orders are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\) and, where present, unit contrasts. Bounded scores and overlap give
\[
 \|\xi_t\|_2\le M_\xi:=B/\varepsilon. \tag{1}
\]
Define \(D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\). Every entry of \(D_t\) is a martingale difference and \(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_\xi^2\). Orthogonality over the fixed \(d^2\) entries yields
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le\frac{4M_\xi^4}{T}. \tag{2}
\]
Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm, Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{3}
\]

Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) gives
\[
 \|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{4}
\]
For every \(t\), the derivative and overlap bounds imply
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le\frac{D_1}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2. \tag{5}
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(M_\xi\), so
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2. \tag{6}
\]
Averaging (6), then using (3)--(4), the predictable-QV expectation bound in Theorem 1, and Markov's inequality, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 (\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &\quad+\left\|T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}+r_T+\delta_T)=O_P(e_T).
\end{aligned}\tag{7}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball, curvature and overlap similarly imply, outside the negligible fallback event,
\[
 \|\widehat H_T-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\frac{D_2}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T). \tag{8}
\]
The fallback probability tends uniformly to zero, so (8) holds unconditionally in the uniform-in-probability sense.

The variational definitions give \(|\sigma_{\min}(A)-\sigma_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) and, for symmetric matrices, \(|\lambda_{\min}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). Thus (7)--(8), \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\), and \(\Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d\) imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(\mathcal E_T^c)\longrightarrow0. \tag{9}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T\), every inverse is legally evaluated and
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\widehat H_T^{-1}(H-\widehat H_T)H^{-1},
 \qquad \|\widehat H_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-. \tag{10}
\]
Theorem 1 also gives \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) and \(\|\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KB^2/\varepsilon\). Inserting and subtracting the three sandwich factors one at a time and using (7)--(10) proves on \(\mathcal E_T\) that
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\Omega_T\widehat H_T^{-\top}
 -H^{-1}\Omega_JH^{-\top}=O_P(e_T). \tag{11}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T^c\), \(\widehat V_T=I_d\), while Theorem 1 bounds \(V_J\) uniformly. Equation (9) makes this branch negligible, so
\[
\widehat V_T-V_J=O_P(e_T). \tag{12}
\]

Fix a unit vector \(c\), and define
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\quad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\quad
 A_T^2=\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}],\quad
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2. \tag{13}
\]
The Jacobian bounds from Theorem 1 give
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad |U_t|\le L:=\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-}. \tag{14}
\]
The predictable-QV approximation and basin nondegeneracy imply
\[
 \frac{A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)a
 =a^\top\Omega_Ja+o_P(1),
 \qquad a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0. \tag{15}
\]
Thus \(A_T^2\to\infty\) in probability uniformly. Equation (3) gives
\[
 \frac{Q_T-A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)a
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}), \tag{16}
\]
so \(Q_T/A_T^2\to_P1\) and \(P(Q_T>0)\to1\) uniformly. With \(\delta=2\), (14)--(15) imply
\[
 A_T^{-4}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4
 \le A_T^{-4}TL^4\longrightarrow_P0. \tag{17}
\]
Indeed, on \(A_T^2/T\ge v_0/2\), the right side is at most \(4L^4/(Tv_0^2)\), and that event has probability tending uniformly to one.

Every array formed by an arbitrary sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\) and unit contrasts \(c_T\) satisfies the hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\). Hence
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}
 =\frac{\sum_{t=1}^TU_t}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\Rightarrow N(0,1). \tag{18}
\]
This convergence is uniform: otherwise a sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) with Kolmogorov distance bounded away from zero would contradict (18) for that triangular array. Weak convergence to the continuous distribution function \(\Phi\) implies Kolmogorov convergence by a finite continuity-point partition and monotonicity.

Theorem 1 gives
\[
 \sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-T^{-1/2}a^\top S_T+O_P(e_T). \tag{19}
\]
Equations (3), (7), (12), and \(c^\top V_Jc=a^\top\Omega_Ja\) imply
\[
 c^\top\widehat V_Tc-T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta=O_P(e_T). \tag{20}
\]
By (15)--(16), both quantities in (20) are at least \(v_0/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one, so all divisions and square roots are legitimate on that event. Since \(e_T\to0\) and \(|\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=|x-y|/(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)\), equations (18)--(20) yield
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1) \tag{21}
\]
uniformly. Symmetry of the standard normal law and the two smoothing inclusions, together with uniform continuity of \(\Phi\), prove the asserted uniform standard-normal approximation.

Finally, \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\) and \(\mathrm{def:realized\text{-}qv\text{-}wald\text{-}interval}\) give
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}. \tag{22}
\]
The pivotal approximation and \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) give the stated uniform coverage for every fixed \(\alpha\). The cited self-normalized theorem supplies only the scalar martingale implication in (18); equations (1)--(17) verify its hypotheses from the finite-attractor IPW-Z primitives, including variance growth, realized-to-predictable QV, exact-root localization, feasible inversion, and truncation inactivity.
