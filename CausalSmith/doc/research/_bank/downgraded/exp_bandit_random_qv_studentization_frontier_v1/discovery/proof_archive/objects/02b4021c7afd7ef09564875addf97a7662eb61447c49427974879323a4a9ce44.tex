Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\), \(T\ge2\), and the law \(P^\dagger\) in \(\mathrm{def:directional\text{-}matrix\text{-}witness}\). The context is constant and the displayed potential outcomes are independent across occasions, so the full-data vectors are independent and identically distributed. At time \(t\), \(\eta_{t-1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable and \(A_t\) is drawn from the declared policy independently of current potential outcomes, verifying sequential randomization.

Writing \(u\in[0,1]\) for the state, linear interpolation gives
\[
 \Pi(1\mid x,u)=\frac25,\qquad
 \Pi(2\mid x,u)=\frac14-\frac{u}{12},\qquad
 \Pi(3\mid x,u)=\frac7{20}+\frac{u}{12}.
\tag{1}
\]
The probabilities sum to one and their coordinatewise minimum is \(1/6>\varepsilon\). Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{a\in\{1,2,3\}}|\Pi(a\mid x,u)-\Pi(a\mid x,u')|
 \le\frac1{12}|u-u'|,
\tag{2}
\]
so \(L_\Pi=1/12\) is valid.

For every \(\theta\in[-1,1]^2\),
\[
 |Y_t(1)-\theta_1|\le1+\sqrt{2/5}<2,
 \qquad |Y_t(2)-\theta_2|\le1+\sqrt{1/2}<2.
\tag{3}
\]
Each nonzero score has one nonzero coordinate and the third-arm score is zero, so \(B=2\) is a common envelope. The scores are affine in \(\theta\), their derivative matrices are
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0,
\]
and all second derivatives vanish. Hence \(D_1=D_2=1\) are valid envelopes.

Both nonzero outcomes have mean zero. Therefore
\[
 M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)=(-\theta_1,-\theta_2)^\top=-\theta,
 \qquad H=-I_2.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\theta^*=0\) is the unique root, \(\|M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)\|_2=\|\theta\|_2\), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)=1\). Hence \(\kappa(u)=u\), \(h_-=1\), and
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(0,\partial[-1,1]^2)=1
\tag{5}
\]
gives \(s_\Theta=1\).

Every realized empirical moment has an exact zero in \(\Theta\). For \(a\in\{1,2\}\), put
\[
 W_a=\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}.
\]
If \(W_a>0\), define
\[
 \theta_a^{(0)}=
 \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}Y_t(a)/
 \pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}{W_a};
\tag{6}
\]
if \(W_a=0\), set \(\theta_a^{(0)}=0\). In the first case (6) is a convex combination of outcomes in \([-1,1]\); in the second the corresponding empirical coordinate is identically zero. Hence \(\theta^{(0)}\in\Theta\), direct substitution gives \(G_T(\theta^{(0)})=0\), and
\[
 \min_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)\|_2=0.
\tag{7}
\]

At the first occasion \(\eta_0=1/2\), so (1) gives \(P^\dagger(A_1=1)=2/5\). By the definition of \(J\),
\[
 P^\dagger(J=1)=\frac25,
 \qquad P^\dagger(J=2)=\frac35.
\tag{8}
\]
The label is \(\mathcal F_1\)-measurable, \(\eta_\infty=\bar\eta_J\), and the fixed attractors are separated by one. Thus tail selection holds with \(m=2\), separation with \(s_\eta=1\), and the basin-mass floor with \(p_-=2/5\). Since \(\eta_s=\bar\eta_J\) for \(s\ge1\) and \(|\eta_0-\bar\eta_J|=1/2\),
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J|=\frac1{2T}
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{9}
\]
The attraction condition holds with \(r_T=1/(2T)\), \(\delta_T=0\), and
\[
 e_T=T^{-1/2}+\frac1{2T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
The radius restriction holds because
\[
 0<\rho=\frac\varepsilon8
 <\min\left\{1,\frac{\varepsilon h_-}{4D_2}\right\}
 =\min\left\{1,\frac\varepsilon4\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]

At \(\theta^*=0\), score outer products are diagonal, the third-arm term vanishes, and
\[
 E_{P^\dagger}[Y_t(1)^2]=\frac25,
 \qquad E_{P^\dagger}[Y_t(2)^2]=\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]
Using (1), (12), and the basin-covariance definition,
\[
 \Omega_1=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},
 \frac{1/2}{1/4}\right)=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
\tag{13}
\]
whereas
\[
 \Omega_2=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},
 \frac{1/2}{1/6}\right)=\operatorname{diag}(1,3).
\tag{14}
\]
Both dominate \(I_2\), so basin nondegeneracy holds with \(\omega_-=1\). Equations (1)--(14) verify every member condition in the finite-attractor class with constants common across horizons, hence \(P^\dagger\in\mathfrak P_T\) for every \(T\ge2\).

Since \(H=-I_2\),
\[
 V_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
 \qquad V_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,3).
\tag{15}
\]
For \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(c=(1,0)^\top\),
\[
 c^\top V_1c=c^\top V_2c=c^\top\Sigma c=1,
 \qquad V_1\ne V_2.
\tag{16}
\]
Along every horizon sequence the covariance law is
\[
 \mu=\frac25\delta_{V_1}+\frac35\delta_{V_2}.
\tag{17}
\]
The contrastwise theorem and (16) give every-level validity for this contrast. Equations (8), (15), and (17) make \(V_J\) nonconstant, so the matrixwise theorem rules out validity of one deterministic sandwich for all nonzero contrasts. At the smallest allowed horizon \(T=2\), equation (7) holds for every assignment, including assignments omitting either active arm; this is the exact finite-instance stress test. No finite-horizon coverage claim is made.
