Fix an even horizon \(T=2n\) and use the experiment constructed in Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\).  In Saco's binary notation define
\[
 A_t^{\mathrm S}:=\mathbf 1\{A_t=1\},\qquad
 \pi_t^{\mathrm S}:=p_t,
\]
and relabel the fixed potential outcomes as
\[
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1):=Y_t(1)=1,
 \qquad
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0):=Y_t(2)=1.
\]
We verify each source assumption recorded in Lemma~\(\text{lem:saco-pointwise-rqv-pivot}\).

For maintained Assumption~3.5, the potential outcomes above depend only on the current unit's own treatment and
\[
 Y_t=Y_t^{\mathrm S}(A_t^{\mathrm S})=1
\]
for every realization.  Thus consistency and no interference both hold.  For Assumption~3.6, the vectors \((X_t,Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))\) are identical deterministic vectors, hence are independent across \(t\) and identically distributed.  Their average treatment effect is
\[
 \theta_0=E\!\left[Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)-Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)\right]
 =1-1=0=\theta^*.
\tag{32}
\]

For Assumption~3.2, \(p_t\) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable and is the probability actually passed to the randomization device.  If
\[
 \mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}:=\sigma(\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t,p_t),
\]
then the construction gives
\[
 P(A_t^{\mathrm S}=1\mid\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S})
 =p_t=\pi_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{33}
\]
This proves logging integrity.  For Assumption~3.7, the potential outcomes are deterministic, so \(A_t^{\mathrm S}\) is conditionally independent of \((Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))\) given \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\).  For Assumption~3.8, the construction uses only \(p_t\in\{1/2,1/4\}\); consequently
\[
 \frac14\le p_t\le\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac12\le1-p_t\le\frac34,
\tag{34}
\]
so executed overlap holds with \(\varepsilon=1/4\).

For Assumption~4.8, take the scored set
\[
 \mathcal T_T:=\{1,\ldots,T\}.
\]
It is fixed before data collection and \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=|\mathcal T_T|=T\to\infty\).  For Assumption~4.5, choose
\[
 \widehat m_{t-1,0}(x)=\widehat m_{t-1,1}(x)=0
\tag{35}
\]
for every \(t,x\).  These fitted functions are deterministic and therefore \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable.  For Assumption~3.9, \(E|Y_t^{\mathrm S}(a)|^4=1<\infty\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  For Assumption~3.10, the fourth moments of both nuisance fits in (35) are zero uniformly in \(T\) and \(t\), so its bound holds with \(C=0\).

Substitution of (32)--(35) into Saco's AIPW pseudo-outcome gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\phi_t
 &=\frac{A_t^{\mathrm S}}{p_t}\{Y_t-0\}
   -\frac{1-A_t^{\mathrm S}}{1-p_t}\{Y_t-0\}+0-0\\
 &=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
   -\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}
 =\xi_t.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
Because \(\theta_0=0\), the centered AIPW increment \(\widehat\phi_t-\theta_0\) is exactly \(\xi_t\).  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\), sequential randomization and the definition of \(p_t\) give the intrinsic two-point law
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(\xi_t=\frac1{p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right)&=p_t,\\
 P\!\left(\xi_t=-\frac1{1-p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right)&=1-p_t,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]
and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]&=1-1=0,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 &=E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\\
 &=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}
 \in\left\{4,\frac{16}{3}\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]
Thus randomized treatment makes the observable centered-score law nondegenerate even though both potential outcomes equal one.  Also \(|\xi_t|\le4\), whence \(\sup_tE|\xi_t|^4\le256\).

By (36) and the definitions of realized and predictable quadratic variation,
\[
 S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t=S_T,
 \qquad
 Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t^2=[S]_T,
 \qquad
 (V_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^TE[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\langle S\rangle_T.
\tag{39}
\]
Equation~(38), or equivalently the pathwise bound in Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\), yields
\[
 (V_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2=\langle S\rangle_T\ge4T
 =4n_{\mathrm{eff}}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{40}
\]
Therefore Assumption~5.11 holds with \(v_-=4\) and failure probability zero.  This verifies the theorem's actual variance-growth premise; it does not invoke a deterministic variance limit.

The preceding proposition also proves
\[
 T^{-1}\bigl([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T\bigr)\to_P0.
\]
Combining this with (40) gives
\[
 \left|\frac{[S]_T}{\langle S\rangle_T}-1\right|
 \le\frac{|[S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T|}{4T}
 \to_P0.
\tag{41}
\]
Moreover, with Lyapunov exponent \(\delta=2\), boundedness and (40) imply
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=1}^TE|\xi_t|^4}{\langle S\rangle_T^2}
 \le\frac{256T}{16T^2}
 =\frac{16}{T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus the failure below is not attributable to missing logging integrity, sequential randomization, overlap, fourth moments, predictable nuisance construction, deterministic scoring, nuisance stability, quadratic-variation growth, realized-to-predictable quadratic-variation consistency, or the displayed Lyapunov bound.

Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\) nevertheless proves, along \(T=2n\), that
\[
 \frac{S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}{\sqrt{Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}}
 \Longrightarrow L,
 \qquad
 E[L]=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{43}
\]
The displayed formula for \(L\) in that proposition is a finite piecewise-linear function of two Gaussian variables, so \(E|L|<\infty\).  Since a standard normal variable has expectation zero, (43) implies that \(L\) is not \(N(0,1)\).  Full-sequence convergence to \(N(0,1)\) would force the same limit along the subsequence \(T=2n\), so (43) contradicts conclusion (5.5) of Saco's Theorem~5.14.

The feasible sample-variance statistic has the same failure.  In the source notation,
\[
 \widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}=T^{-1}S_T,
 \qquad
 (T-1)\widehat V^{\mathrm S}=Q_T-\frac{S_T^2}{T}.
\tag{44}
\]
Equation~(43) gives \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}=O_P(1)\), and therefore
\[
 \frac{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}{Q_T}
 =\frac{T}{T-1}
 \left\{1-\frac1T\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right)^2\right\}
 \to_P1.
\tag{45}
\]
Slutsky's lemma applied to (43) and (45) now yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt T(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0)}{\sqrt{\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}}
 =\frac{S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}}{\sqrt{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}/Q_T}}
 \Longrightarrow L,
\tag{46}
\]
which is not \(N(0,1)\).  Hence the construction satisfies every numbered hypothesis carried by Lemma~\(\text{lem:saco-pointwise-rqv-pivot}\), has a nondegenerate observable score law, and refutes both normal-limit conclusions of the source theorem exactly as claimed.
