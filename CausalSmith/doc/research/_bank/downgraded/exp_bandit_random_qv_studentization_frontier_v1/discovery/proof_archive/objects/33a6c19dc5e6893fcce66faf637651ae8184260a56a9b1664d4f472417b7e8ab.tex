Fix an even horizon \(T=2n\) and use the construction in \(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\). To compare it with Saco's binary notation, define \(A_t^{\mathrm S}:=\mathbf 1\{A_t=1\}\), write the executed propensity as \(\pi_t^{\mathrm S}:=p_t\), and relabel the fixed potential outcomes as \(Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1):=Y_t(1)=1\) and \(Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0):=Y_t(2)=1\). We verify every numbered premise in the cited source carrier.

For maintained Assumption~3.5, the two fixed potential outcomes are well defined and depend only on the current assignment, and the observed outcome obeys \(Y_t=Y_t^{\mathrm S}(A_t^{\mathrm S})=1\). Thus consistency and no interference hold. For Assumption~3.6, \((X_t,Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))\) is the same deterministic vector at every occasion, hence is independent across \(t\) and identically distributed. Its superpopulation average treatment effect is
\[
 \theta_0=E_P[Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)-Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)]=1-1=0=\theta^*.
\tag{32}
\]

For Assumption~3.2, \(p_t\) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable and is the probability actually passed to the randomization device. If \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}:=\sigma(\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t,p_t)\) is Saco's pre-treatment information, then
\[
 P(A_t^{\mathrm S}=1\mid\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S})=p_t=\pi_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{33}
\]
For Assumption~3.7, conditional on \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\), this randomized treatment is independent of the deterministic pair of potential outcomes. For Assumption~3.8, the construction uses only \(p_t\in\{1/2,1/4\}\), and therefore
\[
 \frac14\le p_t\le\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac12\le1-p_t\le\frac34.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus executed overlap holds with \(\varepsilon=1/4\).

For Assumption~4.8, choose the scored set \(\mathcal T_T:=\{1,\ldots,T\}\). It is deterministic and \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=T\to\infty\). For Assumption~4.5, choose
\[
 \widehat m_{t-1,0}(x)=\widehat m_{t-1,1}(x)=0
\tag{35}
\]
for every \(t,x\). These fits are deterministic and hence \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable. For Assumption~3.9, \(E_P[|Y_t^{\mathrm S}(a)|^4]=1<\infty\) for both \(a\in\{0,1\}\). For Assumption~3.10, both fitted regressions have fourth moment zero, so its double supremum is bounded with \(C=0\).

Substitution of (32)--(35) into the source's AIPW pseudo-outcome gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\phi_t
 &=\frac{A_t^{\mathrm S}}{p_t}\{Y_t-0\}
   -\frac{1-A_t^{\mathrm S}}{1-p_t}\{Y_t-0\}+0-0\\
 &=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
   -\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}
 =\xi_t.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
Because \(\theta_0=0\), the centered AIPW increment \(\widehat\phi_t-\theta_0\) is exactly \(\xi_t\). Its conditional observable score law is intrinsically nondegenerate:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(\xi_t=\frac1{p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right)&=p_t,\\
 P\!\left(\xi_t=-\frac1{1-p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right)&=1-p_t,\\
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 &=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}\in\left\{4,\frac{16}{3}\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]
Indeed, (33) supplies the first two equalities, their two support points are distinct, and (34) makes both conditional masses strictly positive. Thus randomized treatment makes the observable score law nondegenerate even though the potential outcomes are fixed. Moreover, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so \(\sup_tE_P|\xi_t|^4\le256\).

By (36) and the definitions of the two quadratic variations,
\[
 S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t=S_T,
 \qquad
 Q_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t^2=[S]_T,
 \qquad
 (V_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\langle S\rangle_T.
\tag{38}
\]
The preceding proposition proves pathwise that \(\langle S\rangle_T\ge4T\). Together with \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=T\to\infty\), this verifies the source's actual Assumption~5.11 with \(v_-=4\) and failure probability zero. It also proves \(T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)\to_P0\). Since \(\langle S\rangle_T\ge4T\),
\[
 \left|\frac{[S]_T}{\langle S\rangle_T}-1\right|
 \le\frac{|[S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T|}{4T}\to_P0.
\tag{39}
\]
For completeness, with Lyapunov exponent \(2\), boundedness of the score and the same variance floor give
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=1}^TE_P|\xi_t|^4}{\langle S\rangle_T^2}
 \le\frac{256T}{16T^2}=\frac{16}{T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{40}
\]
Hence the failure below is not caused by a failure of logging integrity, sequential randomization, overlap, predictability, deterministic scoring, fourth moments, nuisance stability, observable-law nondegeneracy, or the variance-growth premise.

The preceding proposition nevertheless proves, along \(T=2n\),
\[
 \frac{S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}{\sqrt{Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}}
 \Longrightarrow L,
 \qquad
 E[L]=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{41}
\]
A standard normal variable has mean zero, whereas the displayed integrable limit has nonzero mean; therefore \(L\) is not \(N(0,1)\). Convergence of the full sequence to \(N(0,1)\) would force every subsequence to have that limit, so (41) contradicts equation~(5.5) of the cited Theorem~5.14.

The source's feasible Studentized statistic fails as well. In its notation \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}=T^{-1}S_T\), and its sample variance \(\widehat V^{\mathrm S}\) satisfies the exact identity
\[
 (T-1)\widehat V^{\mathrm S}=Q_T-\frac{S_T^2}{T}.
\tag{42}
\]
Equation (41) gives \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}=O_P(1)\), and hence
\[
 \frac{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}{Q_T}
 =\frac{T}{T-1}\left\{1-
 \frac1T\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right)^2\right\}
 \to_P1.
\tag{43}
\]
Slutsky's lemma, (41), and (43) yield
\[
 \frac{\sqrt T(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0)}{\sqrt{\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}}
 =\frac{S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}}{\sqrt{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}/Q_T}}
 \Longrightarrow L,
\tag{44}
\]
not \(N(0,1)\). This proves the asserted in-class counterexample to both Studentized conclusions of the cited theorem while preserving its maintained consistency and no-interference condition.
