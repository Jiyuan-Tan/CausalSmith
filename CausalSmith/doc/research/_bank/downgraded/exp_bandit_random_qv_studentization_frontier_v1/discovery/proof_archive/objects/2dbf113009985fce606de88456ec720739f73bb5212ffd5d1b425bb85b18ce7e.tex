Identify a symmetric \(d\)-by-\(d\) matrix with its
\(s=d(d+1)/2\) upper-triangular entries.  The set
\[
 \mathcal K_{a,b}=\{u=u^\top:aI_d\preceq u\preceq bI_d\}
\]
is closed and bounded in \(\mathbb R^s\), hence compact.  For every integer
\(r\ge1\), choose a finite Borel partition \(\mathcal P_r\) of
\(\mathcal K_{a,b}\) into cells of diameter at most \(2^{-r}\), with
\(\mathcal P_{r+1}\) refining \(\mathcal P_r\).  Such partitions are
obtained recursively by intersecting each old cell with a finite ball cover
and disjointifying in a fixed order.

There are countably many cells in \(\bigcup_r\mathcal P_r\).  Successive
subsequence selection in \([0,1]\), followed by diagonal selection, gives a
subsequence, still denoted \((n_k)\), for which
\[
 \ell(C)=\lim_{k\to\infty}P\{U_{n_k}\in C\}
\tag{1}
\]
exists for every partition cell \(C\).  These limits are nonnegative, sum
to one on each level, and are consistent under refinement.

We construct the limiting measure directly.  Assign to the unique
level-zero cell the interval \([0,1)\).  Recursively partition the interval
assigned to a cell \(C\) into half-open subintervals assigned to its children
\(C'\), in a fixed order, with lengths \(\ell(C')\).  Consistency makes this
possible.  Let \(U_r(u)\) be a fixed representative of the level-\(r\) cell
whose assigned interval contains \(u\in[0,1)\).  Outside the countable set
of interval endpoints, the cells are nested; their diameters imply that
\((U_r(u))_r\) is Cauchy.  Compactness gives a limit
\(U(u)\in\mathcal K_{a,b}\).  Define \(U\) arbitrarily at the endpoints.
Each \(U_r\) is Borel measurable, so its pointwise limit off the countable
endpoint set is Borel measurable there; defining \(U\) arbitrarily on that
Borel null set preserves measurability.  Let \(\mu\)
be the pushforward of Lebesgue probability on \([0,1)\) by \(U\).

For continuous \(f:\mathcal K_{a,b}\to\mathbb R\), compactness makes
\(f\) uniformly continuous.  Write
\[
 \omega_f(h)=\sup\{|f(u)-f(v)|:u,v\in\mathcal K_{a,b},\
 \|u-v\|_2\le h\},
 \qquad \omega_f(h)\downarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
For one representative \(u_C\) per cell,
\[
 \left|E[f(U_{n_k})]-\sum_{C\in\mathcal P_r}
 f(u_C)P\{U_{n_k}\in C\}\right|\le\omega_f(2^{-r}).
\tag{3}
\]
By the interval construction, the same inequality holds with \(U\) and
the weights \(\ell(C)\): outside a null set, \(U(u)\) lies in the closure
of the level-\(r\) cell assigned to \(u\), hence within \(2^{-r}\) of its
representative.  First let \(k\to\infty\) in the finite sum using (1), and
then let \(r\to\infty\) using (2)--(3).  We obtain
\[
 E[f(U_{n_k})]\longrightarrow E[f(U)]=\int f(u)\,d\mu(u).
\]
The pushforward construction puts the support of \(\mu\) inside
\(\mathcal K_{a,b}\), as required.
