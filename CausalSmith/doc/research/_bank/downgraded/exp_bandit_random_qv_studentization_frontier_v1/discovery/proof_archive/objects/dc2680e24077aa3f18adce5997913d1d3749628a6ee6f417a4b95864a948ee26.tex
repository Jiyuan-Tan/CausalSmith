Put \(b_n^2=N_n\lambda_n\), and, for \(t>h_n\), define
\[
 Y_{n,t}=X_{n,t}/b_n,\qquad
 v_{n,t}=E[Y_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}],\qquad
 V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,t}.
\tag{1}
\]
Division is legal because \(\lambda_n\ge\nu_n>0\). Since \(b_n\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable, it is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable for every \(t>h_n\). Thus \((Y_{n,t},\mathcal G_{n,t})_{t>h_n}\) is a martingale-difference array. The bounded-increment and information hypotheses give
\[
 \max_{t>h_n}|Y_{n,t}|le
 \epsilon_n:=\frac{L}{\sqrt{N_n\nu_n}}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 V_n=\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{2}
\]

We derive the Gaussian limit for the suffix. Retain the increment at time \(t>h_n\) only when \(\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{t-1}v_{n,s}\le2\). This indicator is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable. Write the retained increment and its conditional variance as \(\widetilde Y_{n,t}\) and \(\widetilde v_{n,t}\). Their total predictable variance is at most \(2+\epsilon_n^2\). For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\), Taylor's formula, the martingale-difference property, and \(|\widetilde Y_{n,t}|\le\epsilon_n\) give
\[
 E[e^{iu\widetilde Y_{n,t}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 =1-\frac{u^2}{2}\widetilde v_{n,t}+r_{n,t},
 \qquad
 |r_{n,t}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,t}.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
 Z_{n,j}=\exp\left\{iu\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde Y_{n,t}
 +\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde v_{n,t}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Using (3) and the elementary remainder bound \(|e^x-1-x|\le x^2e^x/2\) for \(x\ge0\), conditional expectation yields
\[
 \left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
 \le C_u e^{u^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)/2}
 \left(\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,j}+\widetilde v_{n,j}^2\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(\widetilde v_{n,j}\le\epsilon_n^2\) and \(\sum_j\widetilde v_{n,j}\le2+\epsilon_n^2\), summing (5) proves \(E[Z_{n,N_n}]\to1\). By (2), stopping changes the suffix only on \(\{V_n>2\}\), whose probability tends to zero, and the stopped total variance \(\widetilde V_n=\sum_j\widetilde v_{n,j}\) converges in probability to one. Since \(0\le\widetilde V_n\le2+\epsilon_n^2\), an \(\epsilon\)-split around one gives
\[
 E\left|1-\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The identity
\[
 e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}
 =e^{iu\sum_t\widetilde Y_{n,t}}
 e^{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2}
\]
and (5)--(6), followed by removal of the event \(\{V_n>2\}\), show that the suffix characteristic function converges to \(e^{-u^2/2}\). The elementary characteristic-function continuity criterion therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}X_{n,t}/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{7}
\]

The prefix is negligible on the same scale. Martingale orthogonality and boundedness imply
\[
 E\left(\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\right)^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}E[X_{n,t}^2]\le h_nL^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Since \(b_n^2\ge N_n\nu_n\), Chebyshev's inequality and \(h_n/(N_n\nu_n)\to0\) give
\[
 b_n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) prove \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

For realized quadratic variation, let \(D_{n,t}=Y_{n,t}^2-v_{n,t}\) for \(t>h_n\). These are martingale differences and
\[
 E[D_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le E[Y_{n,t}^4\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le\epsilon_n^2v_{n,t}.
\tag{10}
\]
Under the same predictable stopping rule, martingale orthogonality bounds the second moment of the stopped sum of the \(D_{n,t}\)'s by \(\epsilon_n^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)\). Removing the event \(\{V_n>2\}\) therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{11}
\]
The prefix squares satisfy the deterministic bound
\[
 0\le b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2
 \le\frac{h_nL^2}{N_n\nu_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, by (1)--(2) and (10)--(12),
\[
 \frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
 =b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2+V_n
 +\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}
 \longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{13}
\]
In particular \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\). On the event \(Q_n/b_n^2\in[1/2,3/2]\), division is legal, and (7)--(9), (13), and the continuity of \(x\mapsto x^{-1/2}\) on this interval yield
\[
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}
 =\frac{S_n/b_n}{\sqrt{Q_n/b_n^2}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{14}
\]
Every bound above depends only on the common \(L\) and the three displayed premise errors. Hence the same argument applied to any contrary sequence of member arrays proves all claimed uniform conclusions.
