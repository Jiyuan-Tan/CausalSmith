Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). By sequential randomization, conditional independence of \(A_t\) and the current potential outcomes, the independent full-data arrival assumption, and overlap,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &=E_P\!\left[E_P\!\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}\right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
 &=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. The same conditioning calculation and \(\mathrm{def:basin\text{-}covariance}\) give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_J=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J).
\tag{2}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Gamma_P(\eta)u
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right]
 \le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). Because every event \(\{J=j\}\) has probability at least \(p_->0\), the basin-nondegeneracy assumption and \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) imply \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) for every \(j\). Differentiation through the finite sum and expectation is justified by the common derivative envelope, and hence
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le|p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). The policy Lipschitz condition and the definition of \(\Gamma_P\) therefore yield
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2},
\tag{5}
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equations (2) and (5) imply
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T.
\tag{6}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), and the uniform attraction assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T),
\tag{7}
\]
where \(C=\max\{L_\Gamma,2M_\Gamma\}\) is independent of \(T\) and \(P\).

We next establish the exact-root expansion. For fixed \(\theta\), sequential randomization and independent full-data arrivals make the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) mean-zero martingale differences. Their norms are uniformly bounded, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T.
\tag{8}
\]
The empirical and population maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). A finite mesh of the fixed compact set \(\Theta\), Chebyshev's inequality applied to (8), and a union bound therefore show, for every fixed \(x>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator satisfies its defining approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1)
\tag{10}
\]
uniformly. Root separation then implies, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\). Equations (1) and (3), martingale orthogonality, and fixed \(d\) give \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. The identical entrywise argument for the bounded derivative score gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\tag{12}
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap assumptions imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon.
\tag{13}
\]
The radius restriction, (12), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) make the right-hand side less than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On that event define \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Since \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), equation (13) makes \(\Psi_T\) a contraction with modulus at most \(1/2\), and
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2.
\tag{14}
\]
With probability tending uniformly to one, the last term is at most \(\rho/2\), and root interiority puts the entire closed ball in \(\Theta\). Starting from \(\theta^*\), iterate \(\theta^{(n+1)}=\Psi_T(\theta^{(n)})\). Equation (14) keeps every iterate in the closed ball, and the contraction bound gives \(\|\theta^{(n+1)}-\theta^{(n)}\|_2\le2^{-n}\|\theta^{(1)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_2\). The geometric-series bound makes the iterates Cauchy; completeness of \(\mathbb R^d\), closedness of the ball, and continuity of \(\Psi_T\) give a fixed point \(\theta_T^0\). If two fixed points existed, the contraction inequality would make their distance at most half itself, so the fixed point is unique. Since a fixed point of \(\Psi_T\) is a zero of \(G_T\),
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (11) and (15) put both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) and therefore equals \(\theta_T^0\). Hence \(\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\to0\), and the selected estimator equals \(\theta_T^0\) outside a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the exact-root event, Taylor's formula and the curvature envelope give
\[
 0=T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2.
\tag{16}
\]
Using (12), (15), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) in (16) yields
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\tag{17}
\]
The fallback event is uniformly negligible, so (17) holds unconditionally in the uniform-in-probability sense.

It remains to prove the basin-stable law. Let
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},
 \qquad k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil.
\tag{18}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(1\le k_T<T\), while the vanishing-modulus condition gives
\[
 \frac{k_T}{T}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\frac{r_T}{\beta_T}\le\sqrt{r_T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{19}
\]
Define the prefix average \(\bar\eta_T^{\mathrm{pre}}=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\), and let \(J_T\) be its nearest member of \(\{\bar\eta_1,\ldots,\bar\eta_m\}\), with deterministic tie-breaking. Then \(J_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable. On \(\mathcal A_T\),
\[
 \|\bar\eta_T^{\mathrm{pre}}-\bar\eta_J\|_2
 \le\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(m=1\), then \(J_T=J=1\) identically. If \(m\ge2\), (19) makes the right-hand side eventually less than \(s_\eta/2\), and the separation condition then makes \(\bar\eta_J\) the unique nearest attractor. In either case,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(P(J=j)\ge p_-\), equation (21) also gives \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\) for every \(j\) and all sufficiently large \(T\), uniformly in \(P\).

Fix a unit vector \(c\), put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), and define \(X_t=-a^\top\xi_t\). Equations (3)--(4) and Jacobian nonsingularity imply
\[
 \frac1{KD_1}\le\|a\|_2\le\frac1{h_-},
 \qquad |X_t|\le L:=\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-},
\tag{22}
\]
and, for \(\lambda_j=a^\top\Omega_ja=c^\top V_jc\),
\[
 v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\le\lambda_j
 \le v_1:=\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}.
\tag{23}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T\cap\{J=j\}\), equations (5) and (19) give
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-\Omega_j\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T.
\tag{24}
\]
The left-hand side is always at most \(2M_\Gamma\). Conditioning (24) on \(J_T=j\), using \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\), and charging \(\mathcal A_T^c\) and \(\{J_T\ne J\}\) to (21), yields
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left.\left|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T
 E_P[X_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]-\lambda_j\right|\ \right|J_T=j\right]
 \le C\{r_T+k_T/T+\delta_T\}\longrightarrow0
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c,j\).

Because \(\{J_T=j\}\in\mathcal F_{k_T}\), the suffix \((X_t,\mathcal F_t)_{t=k_T+1}^T\) remains a martingale-difference array under the conditional law \(P(\,\cdot\mid J_T=j)\): for \(t>k_T\), multiplication by the \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable indicator \(\mathbf1\{J_T=j\}\) preserves the zero conditional mean in (1). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) under that conditional law with early time \(k_T\), constant \(\lambda_T=\lambda_j\), increment bound \(L\), and the common lower and upper bounds in (23). Equation (25) verifies its predictable-variation premise. Hence
\[
 \sup_{P,c,j,z}\left|P\!\left\{T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TX_t\le z\mid J_T=j\right\}
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_j}}\right)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{26}
\]
Here and below the displayed supremum ranges over \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\), unit \(c\), \(1\le j\le m\), and \(z\in\mathbb R\), as applicable.

Martingale orthogonality, (22), and the basin-mass floor give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left.\left|T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}X_t\right|^2\right|J_T=j\right]
 \le \frac{2L^2k_T}{p_-T}=o(1).
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed the numerator restricted to \(\{J_T=j\}\) is at most the unconditional second moment, which equals the sum of the increment second moments by martingale orthogonality. The same argument applies conditionally on \(J=j\), with denominator at least \(p_-\). The remainder in (17) is uniformly \(o_P(1)\), and conditioning preserves that conclusion under either \(J_T=j\) or \(J=j\), because their probabilities are at least \(p_-/2\) and \(p_-\), respectively. To transfer (26), if \(Y\) denotes the suffix sum and \(R\) the sum of the prefix and linearization remainders, then for every \(h>0\),
\[
 P(Y\le z-h)-P(|R|>h)\le P(Y+R\le z)
 \le P(Y\le z+h)+P(|R|>h).
\]
The Gaussian laws in (26) have densities bounded uniformly by \((2\pi v_0)^{-1/2}\). First choose \(h\) small and then use (27) and the conditional \(o_P(1)\) remainder to make \(P(|R|>h)\) uniformly small. This proves the required transfer. Finally, for every event \(D\),
\[
 \left|P(D\mid J=j)-P(D\mid J_T=j)\right|
 \le \frac{2P(J_T\ne J)}{\min\{P(J=j),P(J_T=j)\}}
 \le\frac{4\delta_T}{p_-}.
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (21), (26)--(28), and \(\lambda_j=c^\top V_jc\) prove the displayed basin-conditional approximation.

For any \(\psi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to[-1,1]\), decompose by the disjoint events \(\{J=j\}\):
\[
 E_P[\psi(J)\mathbf1\{Z_T\le z\}]
 =\sum_{j=1}^mp_j\psi(j)P(Z_T\le z\mid J=j),
 \qquad Z_T=c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*).
\tag{29}
\]
The absolute difference between (29) and the asserted Gaussian-kernel sum is at most the maximum conditional Kolmogorov error, because \(\sum_jp_j|\psi(j)|\le1\). This proves the joint \(\sigma(J)\)-stable formula uniformly over \(\psi\). Equation (23) for every unit \(c\) is equivalent to the asserted Loewner bounds on every \(V_j\). Equation (2), the terminal-selection assumption, and the covariance definition give \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) and \(V_\infty=V_J\).

The compact covariance subsequence lemma now supplies a weakly convergent subsequence of the laws of \(V_J\). Along it, the marginal case \(\psi\equiv1\), continuity of \(v\mapsto\Phi(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc})\) on the compact covariance set, and the preceding uniform approximation give the limit law \(\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\). If \(c^\top Vc=q_0\) almost surely, this is \(N(0,q_0)\). Conversely, if it is \(N(0,\sigma^2)\), compact support permits comparison of second and fourth moments. For the standard normal density \(\phi\), integration by parts using \(\phi'(z)=-z\phi(z)\) gives \(E[Z_1^2]=1\) and \(E[Z_1^4]=3\). Independence therefore gives
\[
 E[c^\top Vc]=\sigma^2,
 \qquad 3E[(c^\top Vc)^2]=3\sigma^4,
\]
so \(\operatorname{Var}(c^\top Vc)=0\). Thus the mixture is Gaussian exactly when its projected scale is constant. The early proxy \(J_T\) was constructed solely from the common Cesaro attraction event; no pointwise convergence of the policy state was used.
