The early-scale condition is sufficient, not claimed necessary, and uniform behavior for states approaching a separatrix is not established. In the finite-attractor corollary, the full-horizon Cesaro attraction modulus is used to prove that an early nearest-attractor label equals the terminal basin with high probability; without a uniform modulus, that step can fail near the basin boundary. The no-intercept Guo--Xu recursion is not yet shown to have finite tail selection, the required uniform attraction modulus, or the separate causal-score structure. Correct coverage at one isolated nominal level does not identify a Gaussian scale mixture, so the deterministic-sandwich necessity theorem deliberately requires every nominal level.
