Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\).  Sequential randomization,
its conditional independence clause, independent full-data arrivals, and the
definition of the population moment give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &=E_P\!\left[
 E_P\!\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}
 \right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}
 \middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
 &=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array.  The same
conditioning calculation and \(\mathrm{def:basin\text{-}covariance}\) give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_J=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J).
\tag{2}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\).
For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Gamma_P(\eta)u
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right]
 \le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\).  Because
\(P(J=j)\ge p_->0\), terminal selection and basin nondegeneracy imply
\(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\): otherwise the deterministic matrix
\(\Omega_j\) would violate \(\Omega_\infty\succeq\omega_-I_d\) throughout
the positive-probability event \(\{J=j\}\).  The derivative envelope permits
differentiation under the finite sum and expectation, so
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\),
\(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le|p-q|/\varepsilon^2\).  Policy Lipschitzness and the
definition of \(\Gamma_P\) therefore yield
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2},
\tag{5}
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\).
On
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equations (2) and (5) imply
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T.
\tag{6}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), and the uniform
attraction assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J
 \right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T),
\tag{7}
\]
with \(C=\max\{L_\Gamma,2M_\Gamma\}\), independent of \(T\) and \(P\).

We next prove exact-root localization.  For fixed \(\theta\), equation (1)
with \(\theta^*\) replaced by \(\theta\) shows that the summands of
\(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences.  Their
norms are uniformly bounded, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T.
\tag{8}
\]
The empirical and population maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\),
with moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\).  A finite mesh of the fixed
compact set \(\Theta\), Chebyshev's inequality applied to (8), and a union
bound therefore show, for every fixed \(x>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
The defining approximate-minimum inequality, \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\), and (9)
give
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1)
\tag{10}
\]
uniformly.  Root separation then implies, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \ge\kappa(u)\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\).  Equations (1) and (3) and
martingale orthogonality give \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly.
The identical entrywise argument for the bounded derivative score gives
\[
 R_T^H:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{12}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), curvature and overlap imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T^H\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon.
\tag{13}
\]
The radius restriction, (12), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) make the
right side less than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one.
On that event define \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\).  Since
\(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), equation (13) makes \(\Psi_T\)
a contraction with modulus at most \(1/2\), and
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2
 +h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2.
\tag{14}
\]
With uniformly asymptotic probability one, the last term is at most
\(\rho/2\); root interiority puts the entire ball in \(\Theta\).  Iteration
from \(\theta^*\) remains in the ball by (14), and successive increments
shrink geometrically.  The iterates are therefore Cauchy and converge, by
closedness and continuity, to a fixed point \(\theta_T^0\).  The contraction
inequality makes this fixed point unique in the ball, and
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (11) and (15) put both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\)
within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one.
Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\)
is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) and hence equals \(\theta_T^0\).
Thus \(\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\to0\), and the selected estimator
equals \(\theta_T^0\) outside a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\).  On the exact-root event,
Taylor's formula and the curvature envelope give
\[
 0=T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T^H\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad
 \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2.
\tag{16}
\]
Using (12), (15), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) in (16)
yields
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\tag{17}
\]
The fallback event is uniformly negligible, so (17) holds unconditionally
in the uniform-in-probability sense.

It remains to derive the basin-stable law.  Set
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\min\{T-1,\lceil T\beta_T\rceil\}.
\tag{18}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(1\le k_T<T\), while vanishing of the
common modulus gives
\[
 \frac{k_T}{T}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\frac{r_T}{\beta_T}le\sqrt{r_T}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{19}
\]
Let \(J_T\) be the fixed-tie-broken nearest attractor to
\(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\).  It is
\(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  On \(\mathcal A_T\),
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(m=1\), \(J_T=J=1\).  If \(m\ge2\), (19) makes the right side
eventually less than \(s_\eta/2\), and attractor separation makes
\(\bar\eta_J\) the unique nearest attractor.  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\longrightarrow0,
\tag{21}
\]
and the basin-mass floor gives \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\) uniformly for large
\(T\).

Fix a unit vector \(c\), put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), and define
\(X_t=-a^\top\xi_t\).  Equations (3)--(4) and Jacobian nonsingularity imply
\[
 \frac1{KD_1}\le\|a\|_2\le\frac1{h_-},
 \qquad |X_t|\le L:=\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{22}
\]
For \(\lambda_j=a^\top\Omega_ja=c^\top V_jc\),
\[
 v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\le\lambda_j
 \le v_1:=\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}.
\tag{23}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T\cap\{J_T=j\}\), equations (5), (19), and (20) give
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-\Omega_j
 \right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T.
\tag{24}
\]
The left side is always at most \(2M_\Gamma\).  Divide the expectation of
this bound restricted to \(\{J_T=j\}\) by
\(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\), and use \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\).  Equations
(2) and (24) then imply
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left.\left|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T
 E_P[X_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]-\lambda_j\right|
 \ \right|J_T=j\right]
 \le C\{r_T+k_T/T+\delta_T\}\longrightarrow0
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c,j\).

Because \(\{J_T=j\}\in\mathcal F_{k_T}\), multiplication by its indicator
preserves the martingale-difference equality for every \(t>k_T\).  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\)
under \(P(\,\cdot\mid J_T=j)\), with early time \(k_T\), constant scale
\(\lambda_j\), increment bound (22), and scale bounds (23).  Equation (25)
verifies its predictable-variation premise and yields
\[
 \sup_{P,c,j,z}\left|P\!\left\{T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TX_t\le z
 \mid J_T=j\right\}
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_j}}\right)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{26}
\]
Here and below \(c\) ranges over the unit sphere.  Martingale orthogonality,
(22), and \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\) give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left.\left|T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}X_t\right|^2
 \ \right|J_T=j\right]
 \le\frac{2L^2k_T}{p_-T}=o(1).
\tag{27}
\]
The same argument holds conditional on \(J=j\).  The remainder in (17) is
uniformly \(o_P(1)\); conditioning preserves this conclusion because the
conditioning events have probabilities bounded below.  For arbitrary
random variables \(Y,R\) and \(h>0\),
\[
 P(Y\le z-h)-P(|R|>h)\le P(Y+R\le z)
 \le P(Y\le z+h)+P(|R|>h).
\tag{28}
\]
The Gaussian densities in (26) are bounded uniformly by
\((2\pi v_0)^{-1/2}\).  Applying (28), first letting \(T\to\infty\) and then
\(h\downarrow0\), transfers (26) across the prefix and the linearization
remainder.  Finally, for every event \(D\),
\[
 \left|P(D\mid J=j)-P(D\mid J_T=j)\right|
 \le\frac{2P(J_T\ne J)}{\min\{P(J=j),P(J_T=j)\}}
 \le\frac{4\delta_T}{p_-}.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (21) and (26)--(29) prove the displayed basin-conditional
approximation in the theorem.

For any \(\psi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to[-1,1]\), decompose over the disjoint basin
events:
\[
 E_P[\psi(J)\mathbf1\{Z_T\le z\}]
 =\sum_{j=1}^mp_j\psi(j)P(Z_T\le z\mid J=j),
 \quad Z_T=c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*).
\tag{30}
\]
The difference from the asserted Gaussian-kernel sum is at most the maximum
conditional Kolmogorov error because \(\sum_jp_j|\psi(j)|\le1\).  This proves
the joint \(\sigma(J)\)-stable formula.  Equation (23) for every unit \(c\)
is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d\preceq V_j
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d.
\tag{31}
\]
Terminal selection and the covariance definition also give
\(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) and \(V_\infty=V_J\) almost surely.

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:compact\text{-}covariance\text{-}law\text{-}subsequence}\)
to (31).  Along the resulting subsequence, take \(\psi\equiv1\) in (30).
The map \(v\mapsto\Phi(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc})\) is continuous on the compact
covariance set, so weak convergence of the laws of \(V_J\) gives the limit
law \(\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\), with \(V\sim\mu\) independent of
\(Z_1\sim N(0,1)\).  If \(c^\top Vc=q_0\) almost surely, the limit is
\(N(0,q_0)\).  Conversely, if it equals \(N(0,\sigma^2)\), compact support
permits comparison of second and fourth moments.  Integration by parts for
the standard normal density gives \(E[Z_1^2]=1\) and \(E[Z_1^4]=3\), whence
\[
 E[c^\top Vc]=\sigma^2,
 \qquad E[(c^\top Vc)^2]=\sigma^4.
\tag{32}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{Var}(c^\top Vc)=0\).  This proves the Gaussian
characterization.  The only state approximation used was (20), obtained
from the Cesaro attraction event; no pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\)
was used.
