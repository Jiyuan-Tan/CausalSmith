Suppose first that one deterministic positive-definite \(\Sigma\) gives the asserted coverage for every nonzero contrast and every nominal level. Apply \(\mathrm{thm:contrastwise\text{-}deterministic\text{-}sandwich\text{-}iff}\) to the finite set
\[
 \mathcal C_0=\{e_r:1\le r\le d\}\cup
 \{e_r+e_s:1\le r<s\le d\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For each \(c\in\mathcal C_0\),
\[
 c^\top(V-\Sigma)c=0\qquad\mu\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{2}
\]
The intersection of these finitely many probability-one events still has probability one. On it put \(A=V-\Sigma\), which is symmetric. Taking \(c=e_r\) gives \(A_{rr}=0\). For \(r<s\), taking \(c=e_r+e_s\) gives
\[
 0=(e_r+e_s)^\top A(e_r+e_s)=2A_{rs}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every entry of \(A\) vanishes and \(V=\Sigma\) almost surely.

Conversely, if \(V=\Sigma\) \(\mu\)-almost surely, then \(c^\top Vc=c^\top\Sigma c\) almost surely for every nonzero \(c\). The contrastwise theorem gives coverage \(1-\alpha\) for every such contrast and every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). This also proves the point-mass formulation. If the law of \(V_\infty\) is constant across horizons along a fixed horizon-asymptotic sequence, that law is its own weak limit; hence the point-mass condition is exactly \(V_\infty=\Sigma\) almost surely.
