We construct the experiment on the even horizons \(T=2n\); an arbitrary extension to odd horizons does not alter the asserted subsequential obstruction. Let \(K=2\), \(d=1\), \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), let the context be constant, and take the fixed potential outcomes
\[
Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=1\qquad(1\le t\le 2n).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the full-data law is degenerate, so \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) are independent and identically distributed and the only randomness is the randomized assignment, as required by \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}full\text{-}data}\). Define
\[
g(1,x,y;\theta)=y-\frac{\theta}{2},
\qquad
g(2,x,y;\theta)=-y-\frac{\theta}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then, by the definition of \(M_P\),
\[
M_P(\theta)=g(1,x,1;\theta)+g(2,x,1;\theta)=-\theta.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the unique causal moment root is
\[
\theta^*=0=E_P[Y_t(1)]-E_P[Y_t(2)],
\qquad H=M_P'(0)=-1.
\tag{4}
\]
This verifies the claimed causal target and \(\mathrm{ass:jacobian\text{-}nonsingular}\) with \(h_-=1\). On \(\Theta\), the score in (2) is bounded by \(3/2\), its first derivative is \(-1/2\), and its second derivative is zero. Hence \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}score}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\text{-}z\text{-}map}\) hold with, for example, \(B=3/2\), \(D_1=1/2\), and any fixed \(D_2>0\). Equation (3) verifies \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}root\text{-}separation}\) with \(\kappa(u)=\min\{u,1\}\): for \(0<u\le1\) the relevant infimum equals \(u\), whereas for \(u>1\) the constraint set is empty and its infimum is \(+\infty\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:root\text{-}interior}\) holds with \(s_\Theta=1\), and we choose
\[
0<\rho<\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{16D_2}\right\},
\tag{5}
\]
which is the required restriction because \(\varepsilon=1/4\) below. Set \(r_T=\delta_T=0\) in \(\mathrm{def:ipw\text{-}z\text{-}estimator}\).

We now define the adaptive design. Take the compact state space \(\mathsf H=\{-1,0,1\}\subset\mathbb R\). Set \(\eta_0=0\), set \(\eta_t=0\) for \(t<n\), set \(\eta_n=1\) when \(S_n>0\) and \(\eta_n=-1\) when \(S_n\le0\), and keep \(\eta_t=\eta_n\) for \(t>n\). Define the policy map by
\[
\Pi(1\mid x,0)=\Pi(1\mid x,-1)=\frac12,
\qquad
\Pi(1\mid x,1)=\frac14,
\tag{6}
\]
and let the arm-two probability be its complement. Equivalently, if
\[
p_t=P(A_t=1\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}),
\]
then
\[
p_t=\frac12\quad(t\le n),
\qquad
p_t=
\begin{cases}
1/4,&S_n>0,\\
1/2,&S_n\le0
\end{cases}
\quad(t>n).
\tag{7}
\]
The state \(\eta_{t-1}\), and hence (7), is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable. Conditional on this history, draw \(A_t\) with probabilities \((p_t,1-p_t)\), independently of the fixed potential outcomes. This proves \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\). Both arm probabilities are at least \(1/4\), so \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}overlap}\) holds with \(\varepsilon=1/4\).

At the root (4), the IPW score is
\[
\xi_t=
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
-\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}.
\tag{8}
\]
Sequential randomization gives
\[
E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=p_t\frac1{p_t}-(1-p_t)\frac1{1-p_t}=0,
\tag{9}
\]
so \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence. Its conditional second moment is
\[
v(p_t):=E_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular,
\[
v(1/2)=4,
\qquad
v(1/4)=\frac14(4)^2+\frac34\left(-\frac43\right)^2=\frac{16}{3}.
\tag{11}
\]
It follows directly from the definition of predictable quadratic variation that
\[
\frac1{2n}\langle S\rangle_{2n}
=
\begin{cases}
14/3,&S_n>0,\\
4,&S_n\le0.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \((2n)^{-1}\langle S\rangle_{2n}\ge4\) on every assignment path.

We next calculate the limiting score law. On a common product space, generate the first block and, independently of it and of each other, two candidate second blocks having respectively the score laws in (8) for \(p=1/4\) and \(p=1/2\). If \(U\) is any one of these bounded, centered score variables with variance \(\sigma^2\), the third-order Taylor formula gives, for each fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\),
\[
E\left[e^{iuU/\sqrt n}\right]
=1-\frac{u^2\sigma^2}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),
\tag{13}
\]
because the absolute remainder is bounded by a constant times \(E|U|^3n^{-3/2}\). Multiplying the characteristic functions of the independent summands in each block, and then those of the three independent blocks, gives the joint characteristic-function limit
\[
\exp\left[-\frac12\left(4u_1^2+\frac{16}{3}u_2^2+4u_3^2\right)\right].
\tag{14}
\]
This is the characteristic function of three independent centered normal variables with variances \(4,16/3,4\); hence
\[
\left(
\frac{S_n}{\sqrt n},
\frac{S^{(1/4)}_{2n}-S_n}{\sqrt n},
\frac{S^{(1/2)}_{2n}-S_n}{\sqrt n}
\right)
\Longrightarrow
\left(2Z,\frac4{\sqrt3}W_1,2W_2\right),
\tag{15}
\]
where \(Z,W_1,W_2\) are independent standard normal variables. The branch-selection map is continuous whenever its first coordinate is nonzero, and \(P(Z=0)=0\). Therefore (15) yields
\[
\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt n}
\Longrightarrow
\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\left(2Z+\frac4{\sqrt3}W_1\right)
+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}(2Z+2W_2).
\tag{16}
\]
If
\[
W=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}W_1+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}W_2,
\]
then \(W\) is standard normal and independent of \(Z\). Indeed, for bounded measurable \(f,h\), independence of \(Z,W_1,W_2\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E[f(Z)h(W)]
&=E[f(Z)\mathbf1\{Z>0\}]E[h(W_1)]\\
&\quad+E[f(Z)\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}]E[h(W_2)]
=E[f(Z)]E[h(W_1)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

To compare realized and predictable quadratic variation, put
\[
D_t=\xi_t^2-E_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
By (8) and the overlap floor, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so \((D_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence with \(|D_t|\le16\). Martingale orthogonality consequently gives
\[
E_P\left[\left\{\frac{[S]_{2n}-\langle S\rangle_{2n}}{n}\right\}^2\right]
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{t=1}^{2n}E_P[D_t^2]
\le\frac{512}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(n^{-1}([S]_{2n}-\langle S\rangle_{2n})\to_P0\). Combining (12), (16), and (18), and observing that both limiting denominators are strictly positive, proves
\[
\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt{[S]_{2n}}}
\Longrightarrow
L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8}.
\tag{19}
\]
The variable \(L\) is integrable. From (17), the terms containing \(W\) have conditional mean zero given \(Z\), while
\[
E[Z\mathbf1\{Z>0\}]=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}},
\qquad
E[Z\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}]=-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
E[L]
=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0,
\tag{20}
\]
where the inequality follows because \(3/28\ne1/8\). A standard normal variable has mean zero, so (20) proves that the law in (19) is not \(N(0,1)\); it is specifically noncentered.

It remains to verify that the feasible estimator and sandwich have the same limit. Define
\[
U_t=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
+\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t},
\qquad R_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t.
\]
Equations (2) and (8) give the exact affine identity
\[
G_T(\theta)=\frac1T\left(S_T-\frac\theta2R_T\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Moreover, \(E_P[U_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=2\) and \(0<U_t\le4\). The centered variables \(U_t-2\) are bounded martingale differences, so orthogonality and Chebyshev's inequality imply
\[
\frac{R_T}{T}\to_P2.
\tag{22}
\]
The same argument applied to the bounded martingale differences \(\xi_t\) gives \(S_T/T\to_P0\). More explicitly, for every \(\zeta>0\),
\[
P\left(\left|\frac{2S_T}{R_T}\right|>\zeta\right)
\le P(R_T/T<1)+P(2|S_T|/T>\zeta)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus the unique affine root
\[
\vartheta_T=\frac{2S_T}{R_T}
\tag{24}
\]
belongs to \(\Theta\), and indeed to the \(\rho/2\)-neighborhood of zero, with probability tending to one. On this event the global minimum of \(\|G_T(\theta)\|_2\) over \(\Theta\) is zero. Since \(r_T=\delta_T=0\), the preliminary estimator in \(\mathrm{def:ipw\text{-}z\text{-}estimator}\) must equal the unique root \(\vartheta_T\). Hence \(\mathcal Z_T=\{\vartheta_T\}\), \(E_T\) occurs, and the selected local exact root obeys
\[
\widehat\theta_T=\vartheta_T
\tag{25}
\]
with probability tending to one. Since \(E_P[S_T^2]=E_P[\langle S\rangle_T]\le(16/3)T\), we have \(S_T/\sqrt T=O_P(1)\). Equations (22), (24), and (25) then yield
\[
\sqrt T\,\widehat\theta_T-\frac{S_T}{\sqrt T}
=\left(\frac{2T}{R_T}-1\right)\frac{S_T}{\sqrt T}
\to_P0.
\tag{26}
\]

Differentiating (21) gives
\[
\widehat H_T=-\frac{R_T}{2T}\to_P-1=H.
\tag{27}
\]
On the event in (25), evaluation at the exact root gives
\[
\widehat\xi_t=\xi_t-\frac{\widehat\theta_T}{2}U_t.
\tag{28}
\]
Because \(|\xi_t|\le4\), \(U_t\le4\), and \(|\widehat\theta_T|\le1\), equation (28) implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\widehat\Omega_T-\frac{[S]_T}{T}\right|
&\le\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{|\widehat\theta_T|U_t}{2}
\left(2|\xi_t|+\frac{|\widehat\theta_T|U_t}{2}\right)\\
&\le20|\widehat\theta_T|\to_P0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Take \(c=1\) and choose the deterministic horizon-specific threshold \(\omega_{1,T}=4\) in \(\mathrm{def:event\text{-}truncated\text{-}rqv\text{-}sandwich}\). By (27), \(P(\mathcal H_T)\to1\). On \(\mathcal H_T\), equations (27) and (29), together with the deterministic bound \([S]_T/T\le16\), imply
\[
\widehat q_T(1)-\frac{[S]_T}{T}\to_P0.
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (12), (18), and (30) show
\[
P\{\widehat q_T(1)\ge2\}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{31}
\]
Hence \(P\{\mathcal E_T(1)\}\to1\), and on that event \(\widehat V_T(1)=\widehat q_T(1)\) in dimension one. The quantities \([S]_T/T\) are bounded away from zero on every path, since each score square is at least \(16/9\). Thus (26) and (30) give
\[
T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(1)-\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{[S]_T}}\to_P0.
\tag{32}
\]
Combining (19) and (32) proves that the feasible IPW--Z pivot has the same nonstandard limit \(L\).

For the required smallest-instance stress test, take \(n=1\). Enumerating all four assignment paths gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
(A_1,A_2)&P(A_1,A_2)&S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}&\langle S\rangle_2/2&2S_2/R_2\\ \hline
(1,1)&1/8&3/\sqrt5&14/3&2\\
(1,2)&3/8&1/\sqrt{13}&14/3&2/5\\
(2,1)&1/4&0&4&0\\
(2,2)&1/4&-\sqrt2&4&-2
\end{array}
\tag{33}
\]
The two unconstrained roots outside \(\Theta\) invoke the prescribed fallback. With zero tolerance, the four selected estimates are respectively \(1,2/5,0,-1\), whose expectation is \(1/40\ne\theta^*\); thus no finite-sample unbiasedness is being smuggled into the asymptotic claim. At the two-sided critical value \(1\), the exact score-pivot coverage is
\[
P\left\{\left|S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}\right|\le1\right\}=\frac58
<2\Phi(1)-1.
\tag{34}
\]
Finally, (12) and (18) simultaneously verify the predictable-QV floor and realized-to-predictable QV consistency. Therefore neither property prevents the endogenous selection mechanism in (7), which completes the claimed obstruction.
