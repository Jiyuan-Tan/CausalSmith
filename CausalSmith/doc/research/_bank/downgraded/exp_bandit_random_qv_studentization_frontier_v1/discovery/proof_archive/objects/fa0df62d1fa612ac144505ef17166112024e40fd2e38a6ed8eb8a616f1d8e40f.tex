All stochastic orders below are uniform over the displayed class.  Fix
\(c\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), and define
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]
By sequential randomization, conditioning first on
\((W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\) cancels the logged propensity.  Independent
full-data arrivals then imply
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
\tag{2}
\]
At \(\theta^*\), the defining equation \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) therefore makes
\(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\) a martingale difference and gives
\(G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T\).  Bounded scores and uniform overlap imply,
uniformly in \(\theta\),
\[
 \|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon.
\tag{3}
\]
Martingale orthogonality yields
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|\xi_t\|_2^2
 \le M_0^2/T.
\tag{4}
\]

We first establish exact-root localization and linearization.  The derivative
envelope and overlap make \(G_T\) \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz, while
differentiation under the bounded expectation makes \(M_P\)
\(KD_1\)-Lipschitz.  Fix \(u>0\), and choose a finite net of the common compact
set \(\Theta\) with mesh
\[
 \zeta_u=\frac{\kappa(u)}{8(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
\tag{5}
\]
At every net point \(\theta_\ell\), equation (2), martingale orthogonality,
and (3) give
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta_\ell)-M_P(\theta_\ell)\|_2^2
 \le \frac{4M_0^2}{T}.
\tag{6}
\]
The net has a fixed finite cardinality.  A union bound, Chebyshev's inequality,
and the two Lipschitz bounds therefore imply
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>\kappa(u)/2\right\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The preliminary estimator's defining inequality and (4) imply
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1).
\tag{8}
\]
On combining (7)--(8), the uniform root-separation assumption shows that
\[
 \widetilde\theta_T\longrightarrow\theta^*
 \quad\text{uniformly in probability}.
\tag{9}
\]

Let \(A_T^\partial=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\).  Differentiating (2)
under the common derivative envelope shows that every entry of
\(A_T^\partial-H\) is an average of bounded matrix martingale differences.
Since \(d\) is fixed,
\[
 A_T^\partial-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{10}
\]
The Jacobian singular-value bound and the singular-value perturbation
inequality imply, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T^\partial)\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho\), curvature and overlap give
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T^\partial\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le(D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{12}
\]
On the event in (11), the map
\(F_T(\theta)=\theta-(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta)\) has Lipschitz modulus at
most
\[
 q_0=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12
\tag{13}
\]
on the closed \(\rho\)-ball around \(\theta^*\).  Root interiority places
this ball in \(\Theta\), and (4), (11) imply
\(\|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  Hence, with probability
tending uniformly to one, \(F_T\) maps that ball into itself.  Its iterates
are Cauchy by (13), so completeness and continuity give a unique fixed point
\(\theta_T^0\) satisfying
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\frac{\|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (9) and (14) place this root within \(\rho/2\) of
\(\widetilde\theta_T\) with probability tending uniformly to one.  On the
same event, every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of
\(\theta^*\), so uniqueness on the \(\rho\)-ball gives
\[
 \sup_P P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{15}
\]
On \(E_T\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=G_T(\theta^*)+\overline H_T
 (\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*),
 \qquad
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 (\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*))\,ds.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (10), (12), and (15) imply
\(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  The inverse identity
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1}
\tag{17}
\]
and (4), (16) yield
\[
 R_T:=\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 +H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{18}
\]
The fallback branch is immaterial because its probability is bounded by
\(P(E_T^c)\).

For the quadratic-variation comparisons, set
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\tag{19}
\]
Every entry is a martingale difference and
\(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\).  Entrywise orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le\frac{4d^2M_0^4}{T},
\tag{20}
\]
and hence
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{21}
\]
Smoothness, overlap, and (15) give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{22}
\]
The score and derivative envelopes, (10), (12), (15), and (22) consequently
imply
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{23}
\]

The directional step is where early measurability is essential.  Put
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\qquad
 Q_T^c=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2=a^\top[S]_Ta.
\tag{24}
\]
Jacobian nonsingularity and (3) give
\[
 |U_t|\le L_c:=\frac{B\|c\|_2}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{25}
\]
The projected-class definition supplies an \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable
\(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}>0\) such that
\[
 T\omega_{c,T}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad \frac{k_T}{T\omega_{c,T}}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{26}
\]
and the projected predictable-QV assumption says
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{uniformly in probability}.
\tag{27}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\)
with \(N_n=T\), \(h_n=k_T\), \(\lambda_n=\Lambda_T\), and
\(\nu_n=\omega_{c,T}\).  Equations (25)--(27) verify all its hypotheses and
give
\[
 \frac{Q_T^c}{T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\tag{28}
\]
uniformly.  The normal distribution function is continuous.  Thus uniform
weak convergence in (28) implies uniform Kolmogorov convergence: a finite
grid on a compact interval and monotonicity bracket the distribution
functions between grid points, and the two normal tails control the
complement.

It remains to compare the oracle and feasible square roots at the rate needed
when \(\omega_{c,T}\) vanishes.  Equation (23), the Jacobian bound, and (17)
imply \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) and, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{29}
\]
Let
\[
 q_T^0=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T(a^\top\xi_t)^2,
 \qquad \widehat u_t=c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\xi_t
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal H_T.
\tag{30}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality in Euclidean \(T\)-space, followed by (22),
(29), \(\|\widehat\xi_t\|_2\le M_0\), and
\(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}-\sqrt{q_T^0}\right|
 &\le\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 |\widehat u_t-a^\top\xi_t|^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \|c\|_2\|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}M_0
 +\|a\|_2(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Division by \(\sqrt{\Lambda_T}\ge\sqrt{\omega_{c,T}}\) is legal, and (26)
makes the resulting error \(o_P(1)\).  Since (28) gives
\(q_T^0/\Lambda_T\to_P1\), equation (31) yields, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_T}}=1+o_P(1).
\tag{32}
\]
Thus, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\(\widehat q_T(c)\ge\Lambda_T/2\ge\omega_{c,T}/2\), and hence
\[
 \sup_P P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{33}
\]
This square-root comparison identifies the information elbow
\(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\).

On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), equations (18), (26), and (32) imply
\[
 \frac{|c^\top R_T|}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt{T\omega_{c,T}}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{34}
\]
Using (18), (24), (28), and (32), together with the definitions of the total
pivot and the event-truncated sandwich, gives
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}+o_P(1).
\tag{35}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\) is negligible by (33).  For every
\(h>0\), the inclusions
\[
 \{Y_T\le z-h\}\setminus\{|R_T'|>h\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T+R_T'\le z\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T\le z+h\}\cup\{|R_T'|>h\}
\tag{36}
\]
applied to (35), followed by the uniform Kolmogorov form of (28), uniform
continuity of \(\Phi\), and then \(h\downarrow0\), prove the asserted uniform
normal approximation.

By \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\), \(\widehat\tau_T=c^\top\widehat\theta_T\), and
the interval definition,
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
\tag{37}
\]
Since \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\), the normal approximation proves
the claimed coverage for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

Finally, take \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\), and put
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\min\{T-1,\lceil T\beta_T\rceil\}.
\tag{38}
\]
This gives \(0\le k_T<T\) at every horizon.  Nonnegativity and the vanishing
modulus imply \(r_T\to0\), so \(\beta_T\to0\).  Hence the minimum in (38) is
inactive for all sufficiently large \(T\), \(k_T/T\to0\), and
\[
 \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\frac{r_T}{\beta_T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{39}
\]
For \(k_T\ge1\), let \(J_T\) be the fixed-tie-broken nearest attractor to
\(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\); set \(J_T=1\) when \(k_T=0\).
Then \(J_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  On the attraction event
\[
 \mathcal A_T=
 \left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equation (39) gives
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le Tr_T/k_T<s_\eta/2
\tag{40}
\]
eventually.  Attractor separation then gives \(J_T=J\) on \(\mathcal A_T\),
while the uniform attraction assumption gives
\(\sup_PP(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\to0\).

Let \(a=H^{-\top}c\) and set
\[
 \Lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a.
\tag{41}
\]
The variable in (41) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  The established
basin-stable theorem gives \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\) and
\(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\).  Because
\(\|c\|_2=\|H^\top a\|_2\le\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|a\|_2\),
\[
 \Lambda_T\ge b_c:=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}>0.
\tag{42}
\]
Take \(\omega_{c,T}=b_c\).  Then \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) and
\(k_T/(T\omega_{c,T})\to0\).

Policy Lipschitzness, overlap, and bounded scores imply
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{43}
\]
Indeed, \(|1/p-1/q|\le|p-q|/\varepsilon^2\) for \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\),
and every score outer product has operator norm at most \(B^2\).  On
\(\mathcal A_T\), equations (40) and (43) give
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-\Omega_{J_T}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}r_T
 +\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_T}{T}.
\tag{44}
\]
Sequential randomization and independent full-data arrivals also give the
conditional covariance identity
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).
\tag{45}
\]
Since \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\) and \(\Lambda_T\ge b_c\), (44)--(45) imply
on \(\mathcal A_T\) that
\[
 \left|
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[(a^\top\xi_t)^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}-1
 \right|
 \le
 \frac{\|c\|_2^2}{h_-^2b_c}
 \left\{
 \frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}r_T
 +\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_T}{T}
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{46}
\]
The complementary event has probability at most \(\delta_T\to0\) uniformly.
Thus (38)--(46) verify the relative suffix predictable-QV condition and every
other early-scale requirement uniformly.  The finite-tail-attractor class
therefore inherits the pivot and coverage conclusions.  Only Cesaro
attraction was used; pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\) was not.
