The generic pivot theorem applies only to the early-stabilized projected class and not to every adaptive design with growing predictable QV. The former full floor-only class and its all-contrast Gaussian corollary are deleted. The finite-attractor theorem remains valid and supplies one explicit route into the repaired class through an early basin label; no pointwise convergence of the policy state is required. Saco, Shen and coauthors, Guo--Xu, and Leiner--Dunn--Ramdas are nonidentical neighboring regimes, and no wholesale theorem transfer or strict inclusion is claimed. The project uses independent full-data arrivals with known sequential randomization, rather than a pure fixed-finite-population design. The bounded two-basin covariance witness separates contrastwise from matrixwise invariance, while the Guo--Xu recursion remains an open dynamics problem.
