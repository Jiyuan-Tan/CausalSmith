Put
\[
 q_j=c^\top V_j^\infty c>0,
 \qquad s=c^\top\Sigma c>0. \tag{1}
\]
Positive definiteness and \(c\ne0\) justify both inequalities and all divisions below. By the deterministic interval definition,
\[
 \{\tau\in C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\left\{\left|\sqrt{T_\ell}\,c^\top
 (\widehat\theta_{T_\ell}-\theta^*)\right|
 \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt s\right\}. \tag{2}
\]
The finite-mixture approximation in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) applies to the triangular sequence \(P_\ell\). Its uniform CDF error, convergence of the weights and covariance matrices, and continuity of each centered normal CDF imply, with \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\),
\[
 \lim_{\ell\to\infty}P_\ell\{\tau\in C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\infty}p_j^\infty
 \left\{2\Phi\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q_j}}\right)-1\right\}. \tag{3}
\]
Atoms with zero limiting weight make no contribution. If \(q_j=s\) for every \(j\in\mathcal J_\infty\), the right side of (3) is \(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha\), which proves sufficiency.

Conversely, suppose the coverage limit equals \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). As \(\alpha\) ranges over \((0,1)\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) ranges over \((0,\infty)\); hence (3) gives
\[
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\infty}p_j^\infty
 \left\{2\Phi\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q_j}}\right)-1\right\}
 =2\Phi(z)-1
 \qquad(z>0). \tag{4}
\]
Let \(X\) have distribution \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\infty}p_j^\infty N(0,q_j)\). Equation (4) states that \(|X|/\sqrt s\) and \(|Z|\), for \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), have the same distribution. Both \(X/\sqrt s\) and \(Z\) are symmetric, so for every \(x\ge0\),
\[
 P(X/\sqrt s\le x)=\tfrac12\{1+P(|X|/\sqrt s\le x)\}
 =\tfrac12\{1+P(|Z|\le x)\}=P(Z\le x),
\]
and symmetry supplies the equality for \(x<0\). Thus \(X/\sqrt s\) is standard Gaussian.

The mixture is finite, so its second and fourth moments exist. Equality of laws gives
\[
 \sum_jp_j^\infty q_j=s,
 \qquad 3\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j^2=3s^2. \tag{5}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_jp_j^\infty(q_j-s)^2
 =\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j^2-2s\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j
 +s^2\sum_jp_j^\infty=0. \tag{6}
\]
Every weight indexed by \(\mathcal J_\infty\) is strictly positive and every summand in (6) is nonnegative. Therefore \(q_j=s\) for every \(j\in\mathcal J_\infty\), proving necessity. The use of every nominal level is essential: it is what yields (4) for every \(z>0\); no step infers mixture identification from coverage at a single level.
