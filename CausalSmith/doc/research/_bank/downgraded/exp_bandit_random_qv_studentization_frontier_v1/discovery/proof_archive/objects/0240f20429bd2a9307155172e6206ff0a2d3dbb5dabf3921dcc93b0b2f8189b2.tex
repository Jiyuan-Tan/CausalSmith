Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). By the i.i.d.-full-data and sequential-randomization assumptions, and using uniform overlap to justify every inverse probability,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=E_P\!\left[E_P\!\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}\right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
&=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
=M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. Define, only for this proof,
\[
 \Gamma_P(\eta)=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{g_{t,a}(\theta^*)g_{t,a}(\theta^*)^\top}
 {\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right]. \tag{2}
\]
The same conditioning calculation as in (1) yields
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_j=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_j). \tag{3}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Omega_ju
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\bar\eta_j)}\right]
 \le \frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}, \tag{4}
\]
which proves \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). The derivative envelope permits differentiation through the finite sum and expectation, and therefore
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1. \tag{5}
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le |p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). The policy-Lipschitz assumption and (2) consequently give
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}, \tag{6}
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equations (3) and (6) imply
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T. \tag{7}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm in (7) is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), while the uniform Cesaro-attraction assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T), \tag{8}
\]
for a constant depending only on the common envelopes.

We next derive localization. For fixed \(\theta\), sequential randomization and i.i.d. arrivals show that the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences. Their Euclidean norms are uniformly bounded, so orthogonality of distinct martingale increments gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T. \tag{9}
\]
The empirical and population maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). For fixed \(x>0\), choose a finite mesh of the compact set \(\Theta\) fine enough that the two Lipschitz approximation errors sum to at most \(x/2\). Chebyshev's inequality applied to (9), followed by the union bound over this fixed mesh, proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
Since \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator satisfies its defining approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1) \tag{11}
\]
uniformly. The uniform-root-separation assumption now yields, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\), so (1), martingale orthogonality, and bounded increments imply \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. Applying the same entrywise argument to the derivative score and using the derivative envelope gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}) \tag{13}
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap bounds imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon. \tag{14}
\]
The choice of \(\rho\), (13), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) make the right-hand side smaller than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On that event define \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Equations (14) and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) show that \(\Psi_T\) has Lipschitz constant at most \(1/2\) on the ball. Moreover,
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2. \tag{15}
\]
The last term is at most \(\rho/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one, and the root-interior assumption puts the closed ball inside \(\Theta\). Thus \(\Psi_T\) maps this complete closed ball into itself. Starting from any point in the ball, its iterates have successive distances bounded by a geometric sequence, hence are Cauchy; their limit \(\theta_T^0\) is a fixed point by continuity. If two fixed points existed, their distance would be at most half itself, so the fixed point is unique. Since fixed points are exactly roots,
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{16}
\]
Equations (12) and (16) imply that both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) are within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) and therefore equals the unique root \(\theta_T^0\) in the closed \(\rho\)-ball. Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\to0, \qquad
 \widehat\theta_T=\theta_T^0
\]
outside a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On this exact-root event, Taylor's formula and the curvature bound give
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)
 =T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad
 \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2. \tag{17}
\]
By (13) and (16), multiplication of (17) by \(\sqrt T H^{-1}\) proves
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T). \tag{18}
\]
The fallback branch has probability tending uniformly to zero, so restoring it preserves this uniform-in-probability order.

It remains to establish the label-free distributional conclusion. Let
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,\qquad
 \Lambda_T=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}). \tag{19}
\]
The vanishing-modulus assumption gives \(k_T/T\to0\) and \(Tr_T/k_T\le\sqrt{r_T}+o(1)\). Since \(\Lambda_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable, the calculation leading to (8) gives
\[
 E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1) \tag{20}
\]
uniformly. With
\[
 B_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
\]
the same calculation, accounting for the omitted prefix, yields
\[
 E_P\|B_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1). \tag{21}
\]
Thus \(E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)\) uniformly.

Fix a unit vector \(c\) and put \(a=H^{-\top}c\). Equations (5) and the Jacobian lower bound imply
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1}. \tag{22}
\]
Therefore \(|a^\top\xi_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), while regime nondegeneracy gives
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0. \tag{23}
\]
Also \(a^\top\Lambda_Ta\le v_+:=M_\Gamma/h_-^2\). The simultaneous bounds \(\omega_-I_d\preceq\Omega_j\preceq M_\Gamma I_d\) and \(h_-\le\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\) imply \(v_0\le v_+\). Define the \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable clipped variance
\[
 \lambda_{T,P,c}=\max\{v_0/2,a^\top\Lambda_Ta\}. \tag{24}
\]
Because (23) holds, (20)--(21) and \(\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1}\) give
\[
 E_P|a^\top B_Ta-\lambda_{T,P,c}|=o(1) \tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(P\) and unit \(c\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) to \(X_{T,t}=-a^\top\xi_t\), \(\mathcal G_{T,t}=\mathcal F_t\), and (24). Its boundedness, positive lower variance, measurability, and quadratic-variation approximation hypotheses are respectively (22), (23)--(24), the definition in (19), and (25). Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\left\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\xi_t\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_{T,P,c}}}\right)\right|\to0. \tag{26}
\]
For variances in a fixed interval bounded away from zero, differentiation shows
\[
 \sup_z\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\Phi(z/\sqrt v)\right|
 \le C/v.
\]
Equations (20) and (23)--(24) therefore permit replacement of the expectation in (26) by
\[
 E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_Ja}}\right)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_P}p_{P,j}\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{c^\top V_{P,j}c}}\right). \tag{27}
\]
Martingale orthogonality and bounded increments give
\[
 E_P\left|a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\xi_t\right|^2
 \le Ck_T/T=o(1), \tag{28}
\]
so the prefix is uniformly negligible. The exact-root remainder in (18) is also uniformly \(o_P(1)\). The mixture distributions in (27) have densities uniformly bounded by the positive variance lower bound (23). For any \(\eta>0\), the elementary inclusions \(\{X+R\le z\}\subseteq\{X\le z+\eta\}\cup\{|R|>\eta\}\) and \(\{X\le z-\eta\}\subseteq\{X+R\le z\}\cup\{|R|>\eta\}\), followed first by \(T\to\infty\) and then \(\eta\downarrow0\), transfer (26)--(28) through both remainders. This proves the asserted uniform finite-mixture approximation.

The regime covariance matrices obey the common bounds
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d\preceq V_{P,j}
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d. \tag{29}
\]
The upper bound uses (4) and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\); the lower bound uses \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) and (5). There are finitely many possible supports. Repeatedly select convergent subsequences of the finitely many bounded weights and matrix entries; (29) keeps every matrix limit positive definite. This produces the subsequence and limits stated in the theorem. The uniform CDF approximation then gives its centered Gaussian-mixture limit, with zero limiting weights omitted.

If \(q_j=c^\top V_j^\infty c\) and \(\bar q=\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j\), the second and fourth moments of the limiting finite mixture are \(\bar q\) and \(3\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j^2\). A centered Gaussian with variance \(\bar q\) has fourth moment \(3\bar q^2\), and the difference is
\[
 3\sum_jp_j^\infty(q_j-\bar q)^2. \tag{30}
\]
It is strictly positive when two positive limiting weights have different projected variances, so that mixture is not Gaussian.

Finally, stable convergence relative to the terminal label is not implied. For odd \(T\), take \(d=1\), two actions with constant probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), i.i.d. Rademacher variables \(R_t\), \(Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=R_t\), and \(g(a,x,y;\theta)=(y-\theta)/2\) on \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Let the state space be \(\{0\}\), let both regime representatives and every state equal zero, but set \(J=1\) when \(\sum_{t=1}^TR_t>0\) and \(J=2\) otherwise. The score is bounded and linear, \(M_P(\theta)=-\theta\), \(H=-1\), the root is interior and uniformly separated, the constant policy is Lipschitz and satisfies overlap, and both regime covariances equal one. Thus this triangular sequence satisfies every class primitive with \(r_T=\delta_T=0\), both labels have probability \(1/2\), and the exact empirical root is \(T^{-1}\sum_tR_t\).

Let \(h(1)=1\), \(h(2)=-1\), and \(f(x)=\tanh(x)\). The actual joint expectation is
\[
 E\left|\tanh\left(T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right)\right|. \tag{31}
\]
This is bounded away from zero without assuming a limit theorem. Indeed, for \(X_T=T^{-1}(\sum_tR_t)^2\), independence gives \(E[X_T]=1\) and \(E[X_T^2]=3-2/T\le3\). If \(A_T=\{X_T>1/2\}\), then
\[
 1=E[X_T]\le\tfrac12+E[X_T\mathbf 1_{A_T}]
 \le\tfrac12+\sqrt{3P(A_T)},
\]
so \(P(A_T)\ge1/12\), and (31) is at least \(\tanh(1/\sqrt2)/12>0\). Stable convergence to a Gaussian innovation independent of \(J\) would instead force this joint expectation to converge to \(E[h(J)]E[\tanh(Z)]=0\), by symmetry and oddness. This contradiction proves the final negative assertion while leaving the marginal mixture approximation intact.
