Let
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
Each matrix entry of \(D_t\) is a martingale difference. Since the bounded-score and overlap assumptions give \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\), martingale orthogonality and fixed \(d\) imply
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le C/T.
\]
The operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm, so
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly.

On the high-probability exact-root event proved in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\), \(\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). The derivative and overlap bounds give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le\frac{D_1}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(B/\varepsilon\); hence
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\]
After averaging and combining this inequality with the realized-minus-predictable-QV bound and the predictable-QV bound from Theorem 1, we obtain
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J=O_P(T^{-1/2}+r_T+\delta_T)=O_P(e_T)
\]
uniformly. Likewise, smoothness gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat H_T-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le\|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\frac{D_2}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\end{aligned}
\]
Restoring the fallback changes none of these uniform order statements because its probability tends uniformly to zero.

The last display and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) imply \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat H_T)\ge h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Basin nondegeneracy and \(\widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J=o_P(1)\) similarly imply \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Omega_T)\ge\omega_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(\mathcal E_T^c)\longrightarrow0.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T\),
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}=\widehat H_T^{-1}(H-\widehat H_T)H^{-1},
\]
and \(\|\widehat H_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-\). Expanding the two inverse factors and middle covariance factor in the sandwich gives
\[
 \widehat V_T-V_J=O_P(e_T)
\]
uniformly. The identity-matrix branch makes the statistic total at finite \(T\) and is entered with uniformly vanishing probability.

It remains to derive the pivot. Fix \(\|c\|_2=1\), put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), and set \(U_t=a^\top\xi_t\). Theorem 1 gives
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad |U_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-).
\]
Moreover, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 A_T^2:=\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =a^\top\langle S\rangle_Ta
 \ge\frac{\omega_-}{2(KD_1)^2}T.
\]
The first part of this proof also gives
\[
 a^\top([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)a=O_P(\sqrt T),
\]
so \(a^\top[S]_Ta/A_T^2\to_P1\) uniformly and \(P\{a^\top[S]_Ta>0\}\to1\) uniformly. The fourth-moment condition in the cited self-normalized martingale theorem holds with \(\delta=2\), because
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4\le T\left(\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-}\right)^4,
 \qquad A_T^4\ge C_-T^2
\]
outside an event of uniformly vanishing probability. Thus \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\) yields
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]
This convergence is uniform in \(P\) and unit \(c\): otherwise a violating sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) would itself be a triangular array satisfying the same common increment, variance-growth, QV-ratio, and fourth-moment bounds, contradicting the cited theorem.

The exact-root linearization and the feasible covariance expansions give
\[
 \sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-T^{-1/2}a^\top S_T+O_P(e_T)
\]
and
\[
 c^\top\widehat V_Tc=T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta+O_P(e_T)
\]
uniformly. The denominator is bounded away from zero with probability tending uniformly to one, so division is legitimate there and
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1)
\]
uniformly. Continuity of \(\Phi\) and the standard smoothing inequality for distribution functions now imply the asserted uniform Kolmogorov convergence. Finally,
\[
 P\{|N(0,1)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}=1-\alpha,
\]
which proves the Wald-coverage statement. The cited pointwise Saco pivot supplies the comparison claim; the uniform variance-growth, QV-ratio, feasible-inverse, and truncation controls used here were all derived from the frozen class primitives.
