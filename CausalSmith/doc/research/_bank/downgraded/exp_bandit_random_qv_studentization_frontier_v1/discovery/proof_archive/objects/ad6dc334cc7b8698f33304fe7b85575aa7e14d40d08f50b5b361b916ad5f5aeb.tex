Write \(\mu_1=1/2\), \(\mu_2=1/12\), and \(C_{t,a}=\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}\). The corrected recursion gives, for every \(t\ge1\),
\[
 D_{t,a}=1+\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_s^2,\qquad
 N_{t,a}=\eta_{0,a}+\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_s(\mu_a+\sigma_aR_{s,a}),\qquad
 \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=\frac{N_{t,a}}{D_{t,a}}. \tag{1}
\]
The clipping probability of either arm is at least \(q=1/100\). Since \(X_s\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_{s-1}\) and uniform on \(\{-4,1\}\),
\[
 E[C_{s,a}X_s^2\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}]
 \ge qE[X_s^2]=\frac{17}{200}. \tag{2}
\]
For any centered bounded martingale differences \((U_s)\), orthogonality gives \(E(\sum_{s\le n}U_s)^2\le Cn\). At the square indices \(n=k^2\), Chebyshev's inequality and \(\sum_k k^{-2}<\infty\) imply \(k^{-2}\sum_{s\le k^2}U_s\to0\) almost surely; the bounded increments between consecutive squares fill the gaps. Applying this argument to the centered version of (2) yields
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_{t,a}}t\ge\frac{17}{200},
 \qquad q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=D_{t,a}^{-1}=O(t^{-1})\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{3}
\]
The same martingale strong law applies to \(C_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\), because the action is randomized before the reward innovation is observed and the latter is conditionally centered. Hence
\[
 t^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]
Boundedness of \(X_s\) and of the Rademacher rewards, together with (1), also bounds every coordinate of \(\Psi_t\); by (3), both normalized Gram coordinates are eventually bounded away from zero.

Define
\[
 U_{t+1}=(C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}^2,C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}^2,
 C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(1),C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(2)).
\]
Dividing the four updates in (1) by \(t+1\) gives the exact recursion
\[
 \Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_t=\frac1{t+1}(U_{t+1}-\Psi_t). \tag{5}
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal F_t\), let \(u_t\) and \(v_t\) be the arm-\(1\) assignment probabilities at contexts \(1\) and \(-4\). Conditional centering of the rewards and direct averaging over the two contexts give
\[
 E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]=h(u_t,v_t), \tag{6}
\]
because the four coordinates on the right are, respectively,
\[
 \frac{u_t+16v_t}{2},\quad \frac{17-u_t-16v_t}{2},\quad
 \mu_1\frac{u_t-4v_t}{2},\quad
 \mu_2\frac{-3-u_t+4v_t}{2}.
\]
This is the displayed formula for \(h\).

Now put \(\psi=(d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)\), \(\Delta(\psi)=n_1/d_1-n_2/d_2\), and
\[
 b_t=\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}}-\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}}
 =t^{-1/2}(d_{t,1}^{-1/2}-d_{t,2}^{-1/2}). \tag{7}
\]
The difference between the two UCB indices has the sign of \(\Delta(\Psi_t)+b_t\) at context \(1\), and the sign of \(b_t-\Delta(\Psi_t)\) at context \(-4\). By (3), \(|b_t|=O(t^{-1/2})\). Away from the switching surface, the two greedy choices are therefore eventually opposite: \((u_t,v_t)=(p,q)\) when \(\Delta>0\) and \((u_t,v_t)=(q,p)\) when \(\Delta<0\). At \(\Delta=0\), however, (7) has the sign of \(d_2-d_1\). Thus both contexts make arm \(1\) greedy when \(d_1<d_2\), both make arm \(2\) greedy when \(d_1>d_2\), and the deterministic tie rule selects arm \(1\) when \(d_1=d_2\). These conditional means are \(h_1,h_2,h_1\), respectively.

This calculation proves the asserted closed convexified graph. More precisely, a same-arm mode can occur off the surface only when \(|\Delta(\Psi_t)|\le |b_t|\). On the eventual compact region from (3), changing one numerator coordinate by at most a fixed multiple of \(|\Delta(\Psi_t)|\) produces a point on the switching surface. Therefore there is a deterministic \(c_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]-\Psi_t\in F^{\delta_t}(\Psi_t),
 \qquad \delta_t=c_0t^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0, \tag{8}
\]
where \(F^{\delta}\) is the graph enlargement used in the cited Bena\"im--Hofbauer--Sorin result. The inclusion of \(h_+\) and \(h_-\) at every switching point makes the graph closed under approach from the two open half-spaces; the inclusion of all four atoms at \(d_1=d_2\) makes it closed under approach along the switching surface. Its values are nonempty, compact, and convex, and its velocities have linear growth because the four \(h\)'s are fixed. Extending the graph outside a compact convex region containing the eventual trajectory by composing its atom correspondence with metric projection and retaining the term \(-\psi\) preserves these properties.

Let \(M_{t+1}=U_{t+1}-E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\). Its coordinates are bounded martingale differences. With \(\gamma_t=1/t\), the stopped-square inequality used in the proof of the martingale strong law gives, for every scalar coordinate and every \(j\),
\[
 P\left\{\sup_{k\ge2^j}\left|\sum_{s=2^j}^k\gamma_{s+1}M_{s+1}\right|>\epsilon\right\}
 \le \frac{C}{\epsilon^2}\sum_{s=2^j}^{\infty}\gamma_{s+1}^2
 \le\frac{C'}{\epsilon^22^j}. \tag{9}
\]
The right side is summable in \(j\). Borel--Cantelli, followed by a countable intersection over rational \(\epsilon>0\), shows that the weighted martingale tails vanish almost surely, uniformly over every bounded interval of stochastic-approximation time. Equations (5), (8), and (9), and boundedness of \(\Psi_t\), verify the perturbed-solution conditions. The first cited Bena\"im--Hofbauer--Sorin lemma therefore makes the affine interpolation an asymptotic pseudotrajectory and its limit set internally chain transitive.

Let \(\mathcal C=h([q,p]^2)=\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1,h_2\}\). Every velocity in \(F(\psi)\) is \(z-\psi\) for some \(z\in\mathcal C\). If \(P_{\mathcal C}\psi\) is the Euclidean projection and \(V(\psi)=\operatorname{dist}(\psi,\mathcal C)^2\), the projection inequality gives, along every solution and almost every time outside \(\mathcal C\),
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}V(\psi(s))
 =2(\psi-P_{\mathcal C}\psi)^\top(z-\psi)
 \le-2\|\psi-P_{\mathcal C}\psi\|_2^2=-2V(\psi). \tag{10}
\]
The variation-of-constants formula shows that \(\mathcal C\) is positively invariant, so \(V\) is a Lyapunov function for \(\mathcal C\), and \(V(\mathcal C)=\{0\}\) has empty interior. The second cited Bena\"im--Hofbauer--Sorin lemma implies that every internally chain-transitive limit set lies in \(\mathcal C\). Since (3) keeps \(d_1,d_2\) positive on the limit set, slope projection is continuous there, proving the stated projected-limit characterization.

It is useful to identify exactly what the tempting two-mode inclusion would prove. Let \(\mathcal C_0=\operatorname{co}\{h_-,h_+\}\) and write its points uniquely as \(h_-+\lambda(h_+-h_-)\), \(0\le\lambda\le1\). Equivalently,
\[
 u=q+(p-q)\lambda,\qquad v=p-(p-q)\lambda=1-u. \tag{11}
\]
Cross-multiplication by the positive Gram denominators gives
\[
 \Delta(h(u,v))=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f(u,v):=u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0, \tag{12}
\]
and the numerator of the slope difference is \(-5f(u,v)\). Substituting \(v=1-u\) gives
\[
 f(u,1-u)=-75u^2+47u+8.
\]
Its only root in \([q,p]\) is \(u_0=(47+\sqrt{4609})/150\); direct substitution gives the common slope \(b_0=(\sqrt{4609}-91)/1224\). On the segment, the two-mode inclusion reduces to
\[
 \dot\lambda=-\lambda\quad(\lambda<\lambda_0),qquad
 \dot\lambda=1-\lambda\quad(\lambda>\lambda_0),qquad
 \dot\lambda\in[-\lambda_0,1-\lambda_0]\quad(\lambda=\lambda_0), \tag{13}
\]
where \(u_0=q+(p-q)\lambda_0\). Distance squared to \(\mathcal C_0\) first confines every internally chain-transitive set to the segment by the same argument as (10). On the segment, the continuous function
\[
 W(\lambda)=
 \begin{cases}\lambda/\lambda_0,&0\le\lambda\le\lambda_0,\\
 (1-\lambda)/(1-\lambda_0),&\lambda_0\le\lambda\le1,
 \end{cases}
\]
strictly decreases along every nonconstant solution outside \(\{0,\lambda_0,1\}\) and never increases from these three points. Since \(W(\{0,\lambda_0,1\})=\{0,1\}\) has empty interior, the cited Lyapunov proposition places every internally chain-transitive set inside \(\{0,\lambda_0,1\}\) with constant \(W\). The set \(\{0,1\}\) is not internally chain transitive: for \(\epsilon<1/3\), the solution from either endpoint is constant and no \((\epsilon,T)\)-chain inside that two-point set reaches the other endpoint. Hence only the three singleton sets are internally chain transitive for the smaller inclusion.

That smaller graph does not uniformly approximate the recursion at the switching surface. If \(\Delta=0\) and \(d_1<d_2\), equation (7) makes arm \(1\) greedy at both contexts and the conditional mean is \(h_1\). Yet \(h_1\notin\mathcal C_0\): the affine map \((u,v)\mapsto h(u,v)\) is injective, every parameter pair on \(\mathcal C_0\) satisfies \(u+v=1\), and \(h_1\) corresponds to \((p,p)\), whose sum is \(2p\ne1\). Compactness then gives \(\operatorname{dist}(h_1,\mathcal C_0)>0\). Thus the \(O(t^{-1/2})\) bonus cannot be discarded uniformly in the \(O(t^{-1/2})\) switching layer.

The resulting extra internally chain-transitive sets can be computed. Solving (12) for its smaller \(v\)-root yields
\[
 v(u)=\frac{3u+18-\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}{32}. \tag{14}
\]
For \(u\in[u_0,p]\), substitution verifies \(f(u,v(u))=0\) and \(v(u_0)=1-u_0\). Direct differentiation of (14) gives
\[
 v'(u)=\frac1{32}\left(3+\frac{228-50u}{2\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}\right)>0, \tag{15}
\]
because \(u\le p<1\). Using \(67<\sqrt{4609}<68\) and \(73<\sqrt{5457}<74\), its endpoints satisfy \(0.23<v(u_0)<0.24\) and \(0.308<v(p)<0.314\). Consequently \(q<u,v(u)<p\), \(u+v(u)\ge1\), and
\[
 u+16v(u)\le p+16v(p)<p+16(0.314)<6.014<\frac{17}{2}. \tag{16}
\]
The first two inequalities say that \((u,v(u))\) lies in the parameter triangle with vertices \((p,q),(q,p),(p,p)\), while (16) says that the first Gram coordinate of \(h(u,v(u))\) is smaller than the second. Hence
\[
 h(u,v(u))\in\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1\},qquad
 \Delta(h(u,v(u)))=0,qquad 0\in F(h(u,v(u))). \tag{17}
\]
Every singleton in (17) is therefore internally chain transitive. The affine map \(h\) is injective because the two linear forms \(u+16v\) and \(u-4v\) have nonzero determinant; the family is uncountable. At \(u=p\), (14) simplifies to the displayed \(v_*\), and the bounds above show that this equilibrium is distinct from the three two-mode candidates. This proves the advertised ICT obstruction.

It remains to record what the exact recursion itself, rather than its enlarged inclusion, already determines. Let \(\Delta_t=\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}-\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\). On \(\mathcal L_+\), (3) makes the bonus vanish while \(\Delta_t\) stays eventually positive, so the greedy arms are eventually arm \(1\) at context \(1\) and arm \(2\) at context \(-4\). Applying the bounded martingale strong law to the four context--action counts gives
\[
 \frac1t(D_{t,1},D_{t,2},N_{t,1},N_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow h_+=\left(\frac{23}{40},\frac{317}{40},\frac{19}{80},-\frac{79}{480}\right). \tag{18}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and (18) give the first displayed slope limit. On \(\mathcal L_-\), the greedy arms reverse and the same argument gives
\[
 \frac1t(D_{t,1},D_{t,2},N_{t,1},N_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow h_-=\left(\frac{317}{40},\frac{23}{40},-\frac{79}{80},\frac{19}{480}\right), \tag{19}
\]
which gives the second displayed slope limit. Their margins are
\[
 \frac{37955}{87492}>0,\qquad -\frac{16925}{87492}<0. \tag{20}
\]

Both separated regimes have positive probability from every permitted initialization. For the positive regime, fix a sufficiently large even \(n\), prescribe \(n\) observations with \(X=1,A=1\), then \(n\) with \(X=-4,A=2\), and use equally many positive and negative Rademacher signs for each selected arm. This finite history has probability at least \((q/4)^{2n}>0\), uniformly in \(\eta_0\), and leaves slopes
\[
 \frac{\eta_{0,1}+n/2}{1+n}\longrightarrow\frac12,qquad
 \frac{\eta_{0,2}-n/3}{1+16n}\longrightarrow-\frac1{48} \tag{21}
\]
uniformly over \([-1,1]^2\), with small inverse Gram coordinates. Under the fixed positive-regime policy, the deterministic ratio paths interpolate between (21) and the two coordinates of the positive limit in (18), so a fixed positive margin is preserved. For every future centered Gram or reward-moment sum \(M_k\), stopping at its first crossing of level \(x\) and using martingale orthogonality gives \(x^2P\{\max_{j\le k}|M_j|\ge x\}\le E[M_k^2]\le Ck\). Applying this on dyadic blocks after time \(n\), with levels proportional to the block length, bounds the probability of any margin-destroying relative deviation by \(C'/n\). For large \(n\) it is less than one half, so the actual recursion couples forever to the fixed policy with positive conditional probability. The analogous prefix with \(X=-4,A=1\) and then \(X=1,A=2\) leaves slopes tending to \((-1/8,1/12)\) and proves the negative regime. Thus
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_+)>0,
 \qquad
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_-)>0. \tag{22}
\]
Ordinary Cesaro convergence applied to the two slope limits proves the corresponding pathwise Cesaro statements.

Finally define
\[
 u_T=\frac2T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf 1\{X_t=1,A_t=1\},qquad
 v_T=\frac2T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf 1\{X_t=-4,A_t=1\}.
\]
Every subsequential limit lies in \([q,p]^2\). If both slopes converge to a common coordinate along the same subsequence, the context-count strong law, (1), and (4) make the normalized sufficient statistics converge to \(h(u,v)\). Equality of its two slope ratios is exactly (12), proving the occupation equation. If only the two opposite-context modes contribute asymptotically, then (11) forces \(v=1-u\) and gives \((u_0,b_0)\). Equations (7), (16), and (17) show why that premise is not automatic: same-arm behavior survives inside the shrinking layer and generates additional equilibria of the correct limiting graph. The graph is discontinuous there, so no smooth local drift with verified \(C^2\) regularity and omnidirectional one-sided noise has been obtained; an unstable-equilibrium avoidance theorem cannot be invoked. Nor do the pointwise limits and (22) supply the quantitative equicontinuity or hitting and escape bounds needed to take a supremum over a compact set of initial states. The final nonclaims in the theorem therefore follow without adding an assumption.
