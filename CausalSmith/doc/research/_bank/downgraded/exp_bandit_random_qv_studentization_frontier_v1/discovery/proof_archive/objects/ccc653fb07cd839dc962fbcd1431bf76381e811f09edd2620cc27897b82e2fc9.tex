Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). All probability orders and limits in this proof are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\), unless a pointwise counterexample is explicitly being constructed.

First, the i.i.d.-full-data and sequential-randomization assumptions, together with the overlap condition that makes every division below legal, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &=E_P\!\left[E_P\!\left\{\left.
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}
 \right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
 &=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. Define the deterministic covariance map
\[
 \Gamma_P(\eta)=E_P\!\left[
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{g_{t,a}(\theta^*)g_{t,a}(\theta^*)^\top}
 {\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right]. \tag{2}
\]
The same conditioning calculation as in (1) and the definition of the regime covariance yield
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_j=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_j). \tag{3}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply
\[
 \|\xi_t\|_2\le M_\xi:=B/\varepsilon. \tag{4}
\]
For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Omega_ju
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}
 {\Pi(a\mid X_t,\bar\eta_j)}\right]
 \le \frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}, \tag{5}
\]
so \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). The derivative envelope permits differentiation through the finite sum and expectation, and therefore
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1. \tag{6}
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le |p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). Hence the policy-Lipschitz assumption and (2) imply
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma:=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}, \tag{7}
\]
and (5) gives \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On the attraction event
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equations (3) and (7) give
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T. \tag{8}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm in (8) is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), while the uniform-attraction-modulus assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T), \tag{9}
\]
where \(C=\max\{L_\Gamma,2M_\Gamma\}\) is independent of \(P\) and \(T\).

We next derive root localization. For fixed \(\theta\), the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences by the calculation in (1). Their norms are bounded by a common constant, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T. \tag{10}
\]
The empirical and population maps are Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). Since the fixed parameter space \(\Theta\) is compact, for each \(x>0\) choose a finite mesh whose two Lipschitz interpolation errors sum to at most \(x/2\). Chebyshev's inequality applied to (10), followed by the union bound over this fixed mesh, proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{11}
\]
The defining approximate-minimum inequality for \(\widetilde\theta_T\), together with \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\), gives
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le 2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1). \tag{12}
\]
For every fixed \(u>0\), the uniform-root-separation assumption therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{13}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\). Equations (1) and (4), martingale orthogonality, and Chebyshev's inequality show that
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{14}
\]
Applying the same entrywise argument to the derivative score and using the derivative envelope gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{15}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap assumptions imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon. \tag{16}
\]
By the defining restriction \(D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/4\), (15) makes the right side of (16) smaller than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On that event define
\[
 \Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta).
\]
Since \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), the mean-value identity and (16) show that \(\Psi_T\) contracts distances by a factor at most \(1/2\) on the closed ball. Moreover,
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le \tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2
 +h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2. \tag{17}
\]
By (14), the last term is at most \(\rho/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one, so \(\Psi_T\) maps the ball into itself. To avoid importing an existence assertion, start with any point \(x_0\) in the ball and iterate \(x_{n+1}=\Psi_T(x_n)\). The contraction inequality gives \(\|x_{n+1}-x_n\|_2\le2^{-n}\|x_1-x_0\|_2\), hence the iterates are Cauchy. The closed Euclidean ball is complete, so they converge to a point \(\theta_T^0\) in the ball. Continuity gives \(\Psi_T(\theta_T^0)=\theta_T^0\), equivalently \(G_T(\theta_T^0)=0\), and the contraction inequality makes this fixed point unique. Taking \(\theta=\theta_T^0\) in (17) yields
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{18}
\]
The root-interior assumption ensures throughout that this closed \(\rho\)-ball lies in \(\Theta\).

By (13) and (18), both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) are within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Their mutual distance is then at most \(\rho/2\), so \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\). Conversely, any member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\), hence lies in the ball where the root is unique. Thus \(\mathcal Z_T=\{\theta_T^0\}\), \(E_T\) occurs, and the selected estimator equals \(\theta_T^0\), all with probability tending uniformly to one. In particular,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{19}
\]

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the event just constructed, Taylor's formula, (15), and the curvature bound give
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)
 =T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad
 \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2. \tag{20}
\]
Equations (15), (19), and (20), after multiplication by \(\sqrt T H^{-1}\), yield
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T). \tag{21}
\]
The fallback branch is contained in an event whose probability vanishes uniformly by (19), so it does not alter this uniform-in-probability conclusion.

It remains to prove the label-free distributional approximation. Set
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},
 \qquad k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,
 \qquad
 \Lambda_T=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}). \tag{22}
\]
The vanishing-modulus assumption implies \(k_T/T\to0\) and \(Tr_T/k_T\le\sqrt{r_T}\). The matrix \(\Lambda_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable. On \(\mathcal A_T\), (7) and the nonnegativity of all summands in the Cesaro error give
\[
 \|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma Tr_T/k_T.
\]
Using the common bound \(M_\Gamma\) on \(\mathcal A_T^c\),
\[
 E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma Tr_T/k_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1). \tag{23}
\]
For
\[
 B_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
\]
the same calculation, with the missing prefix bounded by \(M_\Gamma k_T/T\), gives
\[
 E_P\|B_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1). \tag{24}
\]
Thus \(E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)\).

Fix a unit vector \(c\) and put \(a=H^{-\top}c\). Equations (6) and the Jacobian lower bound imply
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1}. \tag{25}
\]
Hence \(|a^\top\xi_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\). Regime nondegeneracy gives
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0. \tag{26}
\]
Define the \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable clipped variance
\[
 \lambda_{T,P,c}=\max\{v_0/2,a^\top\Lambda_Ta\}. \tag{27}
\]
It is bounded below by \(v_0/2\) and above by the common constant \(v_+:=\max\{v_0/2,M_\Gamma/h_-^2\}\). Since \(a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0\), clipping can change \(a^\top\Lambda_Ta\) by no more than \(|a^\top(\Lambda_T-\Omega_J)a|\). Therefore (23)--(24) give
\[
 E_P|a^\top B_Ta-\lambda_{T,P,c}|
 \le \|a\|_2^2E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\|a\|_2^2E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =o(1), \tag{28}
\]
uniformly in \(P\) and unit \(c\).

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) to \(X_{T,t}=-a^\top\xi_t\), \(\mathcal G_{T,t}=\mathcal F_t\), the prefix length \(k_T\), and (27). Its bounded-increment premise follows from (4) and (25), its variance bounds follow from (26)--(27), and its predictable-variation premise is exactly (28). Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\left\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\xi_t\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_{T,P,c}}}\right)\right|
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{29}
\]
For variances in a compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\), differentiation shows
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|\Phi(z/\sqrt v)-\Phi(z/\sqrt w)\right|
 \le C_\Phi|v-w|, \tag{30}
\]
because \(\sup_u|u|\phi(u)<\infty\). Equations (23), (27), and (30) allow the expectation in (29) to be replaced by
\[
 E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_Ja}}\right)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_P}p_{P,j}
 \Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{c^\top V_{P,j}c}}\right). \tag{31}
\]
Martingale orthogonality and the common increment bound give
\[
 E_P\left|a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\xi_t\right|^2
 \le C k_T/T=o(1), \tag{32}
\]
so the omitted prefix is negligible uniformly. Finally, (21) has an \(o_P(1)\) remainder because \(e_T\to0\). By (26), every mixture density in (31) is bounded by \((2\pi v_0)^{-1/2}\). For any remainder \(R_T=o_P(1)\) and \(\eta>0\), the elementary inclusions
\[
 \{X_T\le z-\eta\}\setminus\{|R_T|>\eta\}
 \subseteq\{X_T+R_T\le z\}
 \subseteq\{X_T\le z+\eta\}\cup\{|R_T|>\eta\}
\]
and the density bound show, first letting \(T\to\infty\) and then \(\eta\downarrow0\), that (29)--(32) and (21) imply the theorem's uniform CDF approximation.

For subsequential compactness, (5)--(6), the Jacobian lower bound, and regime nondegeneracy give
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d
 \preceq V_{P,j}
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d. \tag{33}
\]
There are finitely many subsets of the fixed set \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Thus every sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\) has a subsequence with constant support; compactness of the probability simplex and the bounded finite-dimensional matrix set in (33) then gives convergent weights and covariance matrices. The uniform CDF approximation already proved gives the stated limiting mixture after atoms with zero limiting weight are omitted.

If \(q_j=c^\top V_j^\infty c\) and \(\bar q=\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j\), the limiting mixture has second moment \(\bar q\) and fourth moment \(3\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j^2\). Were it centered Gaussian, its variance would be \(\bar q\) and its fourth moment would be \(3\bar q^2\). The difference is
\[
 3\sum_jp_j^\infty(q_j-\bar q)^2, \tag{34}
\]
which is strictly positive when two positive limiting weights have unequal projected variances. This proves the asserted non-Gaussianity.

Finally, stable convergence relative to the terminal label is not implied. For each odd \(T\), take \(d=1\), two actions with constant probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), i.i.d. Rademacher variables \(R_t\), \(Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=R_t\), and \(g(a,x,y;\theta)=(y-\theta)/2\) on \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Let the state space be the singleton \(\{0\}\), let both regime representatives and every state equal zero, and set \(J=1\) if \(\sum_{t=1}^TR_t>0\) and \(J=2\) otherwise. This label is \(\mathcal F_T\)-measurable and hence terminally measurable. Fix any \(\varepsilon<1/2\), and take common envelopes \(B=D_1=D_2=h_-=L_\Pi=\omega_-=1\), \(\kappa(u)=u\), \(s_\Theta=1\), \(\rho=\varepsilon/8\), and \(r_T=\delta_T=0\). All class primitives then hold: the score is bounded and linear, \(M_P(\theta)=-\theta\), \(H=-1\), both regime covariances are one, the root is interior and separated, and the constant policy satisfies overlap and the Lipschitz condition. Since \(T\) is odd, both labels have probability \(1/2\), and the exact empirical root is \(T^{-1}\sum_tR_t\).

Let \(h(1)=1\), \(h(2)=-1\), and \(f(x)=\tanh(x)\). Then
\[
 E_P\!\left[h(J)f\!\left(\sqrt T\widehat\theta_T\right)\right]
 =E_P\left|\tanh\left(T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right)\right|. \tag{35}
\]
This is bounded away from zero without a limit theorem. Indeed, for \(X_T=T^{-1}(\sum_tR_t)^2\), independence gives \(E[X_T]=1\) and \(E[X_T^2]=3-2/T\le3\). If \(A_T=\{X_T>1/2\}\), then Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 1=E[X_T]\le\tfrac12+E[X_T\mathbf 1_{A_T}]
 \le\tfrac12+\sqrt{3P(A_T)},
\]
so \(P(A_T)\ge1/12\). The right side of (35) is therefore at least \(\tanh(1/\sqrt2)/12>0\). By the defining testing property of stable convergence relative to \(\sigma(J)\), convergence to a standard Gaussian innovation \(Z\) independent of \(J\) would instead force (35) to converge to \(E[h(J)]E[\tanh(Z)]=0\). The contradiction proves that the primitives do not imply label-stable convergence, even though the two regime covariance matrices coincide.
