All stochastic orders in the first part of the proof are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\), where \(c\ne0\) is fixed. Write
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\), conditional on \((X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\), inverse-probability weighting cancels the logged assignment probability. By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}full\text{-}data}\), \(W_t\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\). Hence, for every \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
\tag{2}
\]
At the causal root, \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\). Therefore \(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\), \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence, and
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T.
\tag{3}
\]
The score envelope and overlap give, for every \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
 \|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon.
\tag{4}
\]
Martingale orthogonality and (4) imply
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|\xi_t\|_2^2
 \le M_0^2/T,
\tag{5}
\]
so \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).

We next derive localization. By \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\text{-}z\text{-}map}\) and overlap, \(G_T\) is \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz on \(\Theta\). Dominated differentiation, justified by the derivative envelope, makes \(M_P\) \(KD_1\)-Lipschitz. Fix \(u>0\), put \(b_u=\kappa(u)>0\), and choose a finite Euclidean net of the fixed compact set \(\Theta\) with mesh
\[
 \zeta_u=\frac{b_u}{4(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
\tag{6}
\]
At every net point, (2), (4), martingale orthogonality, and Chebyshev's inequality give uniform convergence of \(G_T-M_P\) to zero in probability. The net is finite and common to the class, so a union bound followed by the two Lipschitz bounds yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P\!\left\{
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>b_u/2
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The definition of the preliminary estimator and (3) give
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T).
\tag{8}
\]
The second term is \(o(T^{-1/2})\) by \(\mathrm{ass:vanishing\text{-}modulus}\). Combining (7)--(8) with
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|M_P(\theta)-G_T(\theta)\|_2
 +\|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
\tag{9}
\]
and applying \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}root\text{-}separation}\) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2>u\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(A_T^{\partial}=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\). Differentiating (2) is legitimate by the common derivative envelope, and the summands of \(A_T^{\partial}-H\) are bounded matrix martingale differences. Entrywise martingale orthogonality in fixed dimension gives
\[
 A_T^{\partial}-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{11}
\]
The inequality \(\sigma_{\min}(C)\ge\sigma_{\min}(D)-\|C-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), (11), and \(\mathrm{ass:jacobian\text{-}nonsingular}\) imply, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T^{\partial})\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|(A_T^{\partial})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
\tag{12}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature envelope and overlap imply
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T^{\partial}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le(D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{13}
\]
Define \(F_T(\theta)=\theta-(A_T^{\partial})^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). On the event in (12), (13) and the declared restriction \(D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/4\) give
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta F_T(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le q_0:=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12.
\tag{14}
\]
Also, by (5) and (12),
\[
 \|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2
 =\|(A_T^{\partial})^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{15}
\]
The root-interior condition and \(\rho<s_\Theta\) put the closed ball inside \(\Theta\). On the event where the left side of (15) is at most \((1-q_0)\rho\), (14) makes \(F_T\) a contraction from the ball into itself. Its iterates from \(\theta^*\) are Cauchy because successive distances are bounded by a geometric series. Completeness of the closed ball and continuity of \(F_T\) therefore give a fixed point \(\theta_T^0\); (14) makes it unique. The same geometric bound yields
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\frac{\|(A_T^{\partial})^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{16}
\]
By (10), with \(u=\rho/4\), and (16), the root \(\theta_T^0\) lies within \(\rho/2\) of \(\widetilde\theta_T\) with probability tending uniformly to one. It is then a member of \(\mathcal Z_T\). Every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\), so uniqueness in the \(\rho\)-ball makes \(\theta_T^0\) the unique member. Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{17}
\]

On \(E_T\), let
\[
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 \bigl(\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\bigr)\,ds.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (11), (13), and (17) imply \(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), so \(\overline H_T\) is invertible with probability tending uniformly to one. The identities \(G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=0\) and (3) give
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-\overline H_T^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T.
\tag{19}
\]
Using
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1},
\tag{20}
\]
together with (5), (17)--(19), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), yields
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{21}
\]
The fallback branch changes none of these uniform probability statements because its probability is bounded by the first term in (17).

For realized quadratic variation, set
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\tag{22}
\]
Its entries are martingale differences and (4) gives \(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\). Entrywise martingale orthogonality therefore gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le4M_0^4/T.
\tag{23}
\]
Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm, Chebyshev's inequality proves
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{24}
\]
The derivative envelope and overlap give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{25}
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(M_0\), whence
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{26}
\]
Averaging (26) and using (17) gives
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (11), (13), and (17) similarly give
\[
 \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{28}
\]

Define the deterministic, law-dependent direction and its scalar quadratic variations by
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\qquad
 \mathcal A_T=\left(\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\right)^{1/2},\qquad
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2.
\tag{29}
\]
Dominated differentiation and the derivative envelope imply
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le KD_1.
\tag{30}
\]
Jacobian nonsingularity gives \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), and (4) then gives the common bound
\[
 |U_t|\le L_c:=\frac{B\|c\|_2}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{31}
\]
The projected-QV assumption is exactly
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\{\mathcal A_T^2/T<\omega_{c,T}\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{32}
\]
Because \(\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\), also \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\). Thus (32) implies \(\mathcal A_T^2\to\infty\) uniformly in probability. Equations (24), (29), and the common bound on \(a\) yield
\[
 T^{-1}(Q_T-\mathcal A_T^2)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{33}
\]
On the event in (32), division is legal and (33) gives
\[
 \frac{Q_T}{\mathcal A_T^2}-1
 =O_P\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{34}
\]
Here and in (35), each ratio is evaluated on the event \(\mathcal A_T^2/T\ge\omega_{c,T}>0\) and may be assigned the value zero on its complement, whose probability tends uniformly to zero. Together with \(\mathcal A_T^2\to\infty\), (34) also gives \(P(Q_T>0)\to1\) uniformly. With \(\delta=2\), on the event in (32), boundedness in (31) gives
\[
 \mathcal A_T^{-4}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4
 \le\frac{TL_c^4}{T^2\omega_{c,T}^2}
 =\frac{L_c^4}{(\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T})^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{35}
\]
The probability of the complementary event tends uniformly to zero. Equations (29), (31)--(35) verify every hypothesis of \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\), so
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}
 =\frac{\sum_{t=1}^TU_t}{\sqrt{Q_T}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{36}
\]
This convergence is uniform over the projected class. Otherwise there would be a sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\) whose Kolmogorov distance in (36) stayed bounded away from zero, while the common envelope in (31), the deterministic rate \(\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\), and the uniform probability bounds in (32)--(35) would verify the cited triangular-array theorem along that sequence, a contradiction. Since \(\Phi\) is continuous, weak convergence to \(\Phi\) implies convergence in Kolmogorov distance by a finite continuity-point partition and monotonicity.

It remains to connect (36) to the feasible denominator. On \(\mathcal H_T\), (28), the inverse identity in (20), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) give
\[
 \|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{37}
\]
Also \(\|\widehat\Omega_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le B^2/\varepsilon^2\) by (4). Define
\[
 q_T^0=T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta,
 \qquad
 \widehat q_T^0=
 \begin{cases}
 c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\Omega_T\widehat H_T^{-\top}c,
   &\text{on }\mathcal H_T,\\
 0,&\text{on }\mathcal H_T^c.
 \end{cases}
\tag{38}
\]
Thus \(\widehat q_T^0=\widehat q_T(c)\) is total, and its inverse factors are evaluated only on \(\mathcal H_T\). Because \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) uniformly and both quadratic forms are uniformly bounded, inserting and subtracting the two inverse factors on \(\mathcal H_T\), then applying (27) and (37), gives
\[
 \widehat q_T^0-q_T^0=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (32)--(33) and \(\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) show that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\{q_T^0<3\omega_{c,T}/4\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{40}
\]
Moreover, (39) is relative to the floor:
\[
 \frac{\widehat q_T^0-q_T^0}{\omega_{c,T}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{41}
\]
Equation (28) makes \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) uniformly; (40)--(41) then make \(\widehat q_T^0\ge\omega_{c,T}/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. By the definition of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus every inverse is used only where it exists, and the identity-matrix fallback makes the statistic total while being asymptotically inactive.

On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), (40)--(41) permit division and imply
\[
 \frac{\widehat q_T^0}{q_T^0}=1+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{q_T^0}{\widehat q_T^0}}=1+o_P(1),
\tag{43}
\]
where the second relation follows from \(|\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=|x-y|/(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)\) for \(x,y>0\). If \(R_T\) denotes the vector remainder in (21), then (40) and \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) give
\[
 \frac{|c^\top R_T|}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T^0}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T\omega_{c,T}}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{44}
\]
Combining (21), (36), and (43)--(44) yields
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1).
\tag{45}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\) is negligible by (42). For every \(h>0\), the elementary inequalities
\[
 P(Y_T\le z-h)-P(|R_T'|>h)
 \le P(Y_T+R_T'\le z)
 \le P(Y_T\le z+h)+P(|R_T'|>h)
\tag{46}
\]
applied to the remainder \(R_T'=o_P(1)\) in (45), followed by \(T\to\infty\) and \(h\downarrow0\), prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{47}
\]
By the definitions of \(\tau\), the pivot, and the interval,
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
\tag{48}
\]
Equation (47) and \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) prove uniform coverage for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

For the constant-floor corollary, suppose that a constant \(\omega_c>0\) satisfies the displayed constant projected-QV condition in the statement. Set \(\omega_{c,T}\equiv\omega_c\). Then \(\sqrt T\,\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\), the assumption in (32) holds, and the construction uses threshold \(\omega_c/2\); hence (1)--(48) apply without alteration.

We next derive the all-contrast corollary. For \(P\in\mathfrak Q_T\) and \(\|c\|_2=1\), (30) implies
\[
 \|H^{-\top}c\|_2\ge\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}
 \ge(KD_1)^{-1}.
\tag{49}
\]
On the event \(\lambda_{\min}(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)\ge\omega_-\),
\[
 c^\top H^{-1}(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)H^{-\top}c
 \ge\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}.
\tag{50}
\]
Therefore \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}predictable\text{-}qv\text{-}growth}\) implies the constant projected condition simultaneously over all unit \(c\) with \(\omega_c=\omega_-/(KD_1)^2\). The bounds in (1)--(46), including \(|U_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), are common over both \(P\) and unit \(c\). If uniformity in \((P,c)\) failed, a sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) witnessing failure would still satisfy the scalar cited theorem with these common bounds, contradicting it. Thus (47)--(48) hold with the additional supremum over \(\|c\|_2=1\). The full-matrix condition is used only for implication (50), not for the projected headline.

Finally let \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\). The established theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) gives a common \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T),
 \qquad \Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d.
\tag{51}
\]
For fixed \(c\ne0\), let \(a=H^{-\top}c\). Equations (30) and (51) give
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja
 \ge\omega_-\|a\|_2^2
 \ge b_c:=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}.
\tag{52}
\]
For any \(0<\omega_c<b_c\), the operator-norm inequality, Markov's inequality, \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), and (51) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}
 P\!\left\{a^\top(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)a<\omega_c\right\}\\
 &\quad\le
 \frac{C\|c\|_2^2(r_T+\delta_T)}
 {h_-^2(b_c-\omega_c)}\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{53}
\]
Thus every finite-attractor class satisfies the constant projected-QV primitive for that contrast and floor. Applying the constant-floor corollary gives its realized-QV pivot and Wald coverage. Equation (53), rather than pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\), is the required attraction-to-directional-growth map.
