We verify every class condition directly.  The three endpoint policy vectors sum to one, and linear interpolation preserves this property.  Their smallest coordinate is \(1/6\), so for any stated \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) uniform overlap holds.  The arm probabilities have slopes \(0,-1/12,1/12\); hence the policy Lipschitz modulus is \(L_\Pi=1/12\).

The full-data vectors are independent and identically distributed by construction, and the action at each time is drawn from the logged policy using the preceding state, independently of the current potential outcomes.  Thus independent arrivals and sequential randomization hold.  For \(\theta\in[-1,1]^2\),
\[
 \sup_{a,\theta}\|g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\|_2
 \le1+\sqrt{1/2}<2.
\]
The parameter derivative has operator norm at most one and the second derivative is zero, so the common choices \(B=2\), \(D_1=D_2=1\) are valid.  Since both active potential outcomes have mean zero,
\[
 M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)
 =(-\theta_1,-\theta_2)^\top=-\theta,
 \qquad H=-I_2.                                                           \tag{33}
\]
Thus \(\sigma_{\min}(H)=1\), \(\|M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)\|_2=\|\theta\|_2\), and the root is a Euclidean distance one from the boundary of \(\Theta\).  The choices \(h_-=1\), \(\kappa(u)=u\), and \(s_\Theta=1\) are valid.  Also \(\rho=\varepsilon/8<\min\{1,\varepsilon/4\}\), as required when the common curvature envelope is chosen as \(D_2=1\).

For any realized assignments, write
\[
 G_{T,1}(\theta)=u_1-v_1\theta_1,
 \qquad
 G_{T,2}(\theta)=u_2-v_2\theta_2,
\]
where \(v_a\ge0\), and, when \(v_a>0\), \(u_a/v_a\) is a positive-weight average of the observed values from arm \(a\).  If \(v_a=0\), that coordinate of the moment is identically zero; otherwise choose \(\theta_a=u_a/v_a\).  The choices lie respectively in \([ -\sqrt{2/5},\sqrt{2/5} ]\) and \([ -\sqrt{1/2},\sqrt{1/2} ]\), hence in \([-1,1]\).  Therefore an exact empirical root exists in \(\Theta\) for every assignment realization.

At the initial state \(1/2\), arm one has probability \(2/5\).  Consequently
\[
 P^\dagger(J=1)=2/5,
 \qquad P^\dagger(J=2)=3/5.                                               \tag{34}
\]
Take \(m=2\), \(s_\eta=1\), and \(p_-=2/5\).  The two attractors are separated by one, every basin has the required positive mass, and \(\eta_\infty=\bar\eta_J\).  Since \(\eta_0=1/2\) and \(\eta_t=\bar\eta_J\) for every \(t\ge1\),
\[
 T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2=\frac1{2T}              \tag{35}
\]
almost surely.  Thus \(r_T=1/(2T)\), \(\delta_T=0\), and the finite-attractor modulus vanishes.

At \(\theta^*=0\), the two active outcome variances are \(2/5\) and \(1/2\).  Dividing them by the basin policy probabilities gives
\[
 \Omega_1=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/4}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
\]
\[
 \Omega_2=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/6}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,3).                                               \tag{36}
\]
Both matrices dominate \(I_2\), so \(\omega_-=1\) is valid.  From (33), \(H^{-1}=-I_2\), and hence \(V_j=\Omega_j\).  Equations (33)--(36), together with the already verified envelopes and dynamics, prove membership in the stated common class at every \(T\ge2\).

Finally let \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(c=(1,0)^\top\).  Equation (36) gives
\[
 c^\top V_1c=c^\top V_2c=c^\top\Sigma c=1,
 \qquad V_1\ne V_2.
\]
Both basins have positive mass by (34).  The contrastwise deterministic-sandwich theorem therefore certifies validity for this contrast at every nominal level, whereas the matrixwise theorem rules out covariance invariance.  This is the required in-class separation.
