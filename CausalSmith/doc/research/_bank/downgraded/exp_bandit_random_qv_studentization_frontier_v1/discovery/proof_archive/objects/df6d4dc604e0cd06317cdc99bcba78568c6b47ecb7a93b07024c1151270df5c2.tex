Write \(\mu _1=1/2\), \(\mu _2=1/12\), \(p=99/100\), \(q=1/100\), and \(C_{t,a}=\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}\).  The recursion in \(\textnormal{def:guoxu-recursion}\) gives, for every \(t\geq 1\),
\[
 D_{t,a}=1+\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_s^2,\qquad
 N_{t,a}=\eta_{0,a}+\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_s(\mu_a+\sigma_aR_{s,a}),\qquad
 \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=\frac{N_{t,a}}{D_{t,a}}. \tag{1}
\]
The clipped randomization assigns either arm with conditional probability at least \(q\).  Because \(X_s\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_{s-1}\) and uniform on \(\{-4,1\}\),
\[
 E[C_{s,a}X_s^2\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}]
 \geq qE[X_s^2]=\frac{17}{200}. \tag{2}
\]
We first record the elementary bounded-martingale argument used repeatedly below.  Let \((U_s)\) be a scalar martingale-difference sequence with \(|U_s|\leq L\), and put \(S_n=\sum_{s=1}^nU_s\).  Expansion of the square and iterated conditional expectation make the cross terms zero, so \(E[S_n^2]\leq L^2n\).  For each integer \(m\geq1\), take \(\epsilon=1/m\) and define
\[
 A_m=\left\{\text{there exists }K<\infty\text{ such that }
 |S_{k^2}|\leq \frac{k^2}{m}\text{ for every }k\geq K\right\}. \tag{3}
\]
At \(n=k^2\), the pointwise inequality \(\mathbf 1\{|x|>a\}\leq x^2/a^2\) gives
\[
 P\left(|S_{k^2}|>\frac{k^2}{m}\right)
 \leq \frac{m^2E[S_{k^2}^2]}{k^4}
 \leq \frac{m^2L^2}{k^2}. \tag{4}
\]
Hence, for every \(K\),
\[
 P\left(\bigcup_{k\geq K}\left\{|S_{k^2}|>\frac{k^2}{m}\right\}\right)
 \leq m^2L^2\sum_{k\geq K}k^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Continuity from above of probability therefore yields \(P(A_m)=1\).  On the probability-one event \(A_0=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}A_m\), first let \(k\to\infty\) and then \(m\to\infty\) to get \(S_{k^2}/k^2\to0\).  If \(k^2\leq n<(k+1)^2\), boundedness of the increments gives
\[
 \frac{|S_n|}{n}
 \leq \frac{|S_{k^2}|}{k^2}+\frac{L(n-k^2)}{k^2}
 \leq \frac{|S_{k^2}|}{k^2}+\frac{L(2k+1)}{k^2}\longrightarrow0. \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^nU_s\to0\) almost surely.

Apply this result to \(C_{s,a}X_s^2-E[C_{s,a}X_s^2\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}]\).  Equations (1)--(2) yield
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_{t,a}}t\geq\frac{17}{200},
 \qquad q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=D_{t,a}^{-1}=O(t^{-1})
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{6}
\]
The same argument applies to \(C_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\): conditional on the history, context, and randomized action, the reward innovation is independent and centered.  Hence
\[
 t^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{7}
\]
Moreover, (1) expresses each slope as a convex combination of its initial value \(\eta_{0,a}\) and the observed ratios \(Y_s(a)/X_s\), with respective nonnegative weights \(1\) and \(C_{s,a}X_s^2\).  The definition of \(M_{\mathrm{GX}}\) therefore bounds both slopes.  Together with bounded contexts and bounded Rademacher rewards, this bounds every coordinate of \(\Psi_t\); by (6), both normalized Gram coordinates are eventually bounded away from zero.

Define
\[
 U_{t+1}=\bigl(C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}^2,C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}^2,
 C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(1),C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(2)\bigr).
\]
Dividing the four sufficient-statistic updates by \(t+1\) gives the exact stochastic-approximation recursion
\[
 \Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_t=\frac1{t+1}(U_{t+1}-\Psi_t). \tag{8}
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal F_t\), let \(u_t\) and \(v_t\) be the arm-one assignment probabilities at contexts \(1\) and \(-4\), respectively.  Conditional centering of the rewards and direct averaging over the two equally likely contexts give
\[
 E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]=h(u_t,v_t), \tag{9}
\]
because its four coordinates are
\[
 \frac{u_t+16v_t}{2},\qquad \frac{17-u_t-16v_t}{2},\qquad
 \mu_1\frac{u_t-4v_t}{2},\qquad
 \mu_2\frac{-3-u_t+4v_t}{2}.
\]
These are exactly the four coordinates in the displayed definition of \(h\).

For \(\psi=(d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)\), put \(\Delta(\psi)=n_1/d_1-n_2/d_2\).  At the current normalized state,
\[
 b_t:=\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}}-\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}}
 =t^{-1/2}\bigl(d_{t,1}^{-1/2}-d_{t,2}^{-1/2}\bigr). \tag{10}
\]
The arm-one minus arm-two UCB index has the sign of \(\Delta(\Psi_t)+b_t\) at context \(1\), and the sign of \(b_t-\Delta(\Psi_t)\) at context \(-4\).  Equation (6) gives \(|b_t|=O(t^{-1/2})\).  Consequently, away from \(\Delta=0\), the two greedy choices are eventually opposite: \((u_t,v_t)=(p,q)\) for \(\Delta>0\), and \((u_t,v_t)=(q,p)\) for \(\Delta<0\).  On \(\Delta=0\), equation (10) has the sign of \(d_2-d_1\).  Thus both contexts make arm one greedy when \(d_1<d_2\), both make arm two greedy when \(d_1>d_2\), and the specified deterministic tie rule makes arm one greedy when \(d_1=d_2\).  The corresponding conditional means are \(h_1,h_2,h_1\), respectively.

This calculation gives the asserted closed convexified graph.  To verify the perturbation condition explicitly, observe that a same-arm mode can occur off the switching surface only if \(|\Delta(\Psi_t)|\leq |b_t|\).  On the eventual compact region already obtained, replace \(n_1\) by \(n_1-d_1\Delta(\Psi_t)\), leaving the other coordinates fixed.  The resulting point lies on \(\Delta=0\) and is within a fixed multiple of \(|\Delta(\Psi_t)|\) of \(\Psi_t\).  Therefore, for a deterministic \(c_0<\infty\) and all sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
 E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]-\Psi_t\in F^{\delta_t}(\Psi_t),
 \qquad \delta_t=c_0t^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0, \tag{11}
\]
where \(F^\delta\) denotes the graph enlargement.  The atoms \(h_+\) and \(h_-\) at every switching point make the graph closed under approach from the two open half-spaces; the atom \(h_1\), respectively \(h_2\), records approach within the switching layer when \(d_1<d_2\), respectively \(d_1>d_2\); and all four atoms at \(d_1=d_2\) close the remaining approaches.  Hence the displayed graph has nonempty compact convex values and is closed.  Its velocities have linear growth because its four atoms are fixed.  Outside a compact convex region containing the eventual trajectory and having both Gram coordinates bounded away from zero, compose the atom correspondence with Euclidean projection onto that region and retain the term \(-\psi\).  This supplies a global extension with closed graph, nonempty compact convex values, and linear growth, without changing the eventual dynamics.

Let \(M_{t+1}=U_{t+1}-E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\) and \(\gamma_t=1/t\).  Each coordinate is a bounded martingale difference.  For a fixed coordinate and integers \(j\leq n\), stop the partial sum \(\sum_{s=j}^k\gamma_{s+1}M_{s+1}\) at its first crossing of \(\epsilon\), or at \(n\) if no crossing occurs.  Orthogonality of its stopped increments and the bounded-increment envelope imply
\[
 P\left\{\max_{j\leq k\leq n}\left|\sum_{s=j}^k\gamma_{s+1}M_{s+1}\right|>\epsilon\right\}
 \leq \frac{C}{\epsilon^2}\sum_{s=j}^n\gamma_{s+1}^2.
\]
Letting \(n\to\infty\), taking \(j=2^\ell\), and using a union bound over \(\ell\geq L\) give
\[
 P\left\{\text{some }\ell\geq L:\ 
 \sup_{k\geq2^\ell}\left|\sum_{s=2^\ell}^k\gamma_{s+1}M_{s+1}\right|>\epsilon\right\}
 \leq \frac{C'}{\epsilon^2}\sum_{\ell\geq L}2^{-\ell}\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the weighted martingale tails vanish almost surely, in particular uniformly over every bounded interval of stochastic-approximation time.  Equations (8), (11), and (12), together with boundedness of \(\Psi_t\), verify the bounded-perturbed-solution hypotheses of \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-perturbed-limit-set}\).  That cited lemma makes the affine interpolation an asymptotic pseudotrajectory and its limit set internally chain transitive.  Since boundedness of \(U_{t+1}\) and \(\Psi_t\) in (8) gives \(\|\Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_t\|_2=O(t^{-1})\), the interpolation and the discrete sequence have the same limit set.

Let
\[
 \mathcal C=h([q,p]^2)=\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1,h_2\}.
\]
Every velocity in \(F(\psi)\) is \(z-\psi\) for some \(z\in\mathcal C\).  If \(P_{\mathcal C}\psi\) is the Euclidean projection and \(V(\psi)=\operatorname{dist}(\psi,\mathcal C)^2\), the projection inequality gives, along every solution and for almost every time outside \(\mathcal C\),
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}V(\psi(s))
 =2(\psi-P_{\mathcal C}\psi)^\top(z-\psi)
 \leq-2\|\psi-P_{\mathcal C}\psi\|_2^2=-2V(\psi). \tag{13}
\]
If a solution starts in \(\mathcal C\), variation of constants writes it as a convex combination of its initial point and a weighted average of points \(z(s)\in\mathcal C\); hence \(\mathcal C\) is positively invariant.  Thus \(V\) is a continuous Lyapunov function for \(\mathcal C\), and \(V(\mathcal C)=\{0\}\) has empty interior.  By \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\), every internally chain-transitive normalized-state limit set lies in \(\mathcal C\).  Equation (6) keeps \(d_1,d_2\) positive on that limit set, so the slope projection \((d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)\mapsto(n_1/d_1,n_2/d_2)\) is continuous there.  Every normalized-state limit point therefore maps to a slope limit point.  Conversely, any convergent slope subsequence has, by boundedness of \(\Psi_t\), a further normalized-state subsequence converging to a point of the normalized limit set, and continuity identifies the original slope limit with the image of that point.  Hence the projected slope limit set is exactly the continuous image of the normalized-state limit set.  This proves the first paragraph of the claim.

We next identify exactly what the tempting two-mode inclusion would imply.  Let \(\mathcal C_0=\operatorname{co}\{h_-,h_+\}\) and write its points uniquely as \(h_-+\lambda(h_+-h_-)\), \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\).  Equivalently,
\[
 u=q+(p-q)\lambda,\qquad v=p-(p-q)\lambda=1-u. \tag{14}
\]
Cross-multiplication is legitimate because both Gram coordinates are positive, and direct expansion gives
\[
 \Delta(h(u,v))=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f(u,v):=u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0; \tag{15}
\]
indeed, after multiplication by the positive common denominator, the numerator of the slope difference is \(-5f(u,v)/48\).  Substitution of \(v=1-u\) yields \(f(u,1-u)=-75u^2+47u+8\).  Its unique root in \([q,p]\) is
\[
 u_0=\frac{47+\sqrt{4609}}{150},
\]
and substitution into either slope ratio gives \(b_0=(\sqrt{4609}-91)/1224\).

On \(\mathcal C_0\), the two-mode inclusion reduces to
\[
 \dot\lambda=-\lambda\quad(\lambda<\lambda_0),\qquad
 \dot\lambda=1-\lambda\quad(\lambda>\lambda_0),\qquad
 \dot\lambda\in[-\lambda_0,1-\lambda_0]\quad(\lambda=\lambda_0), \tag{16}
\]
where \(u_0=q+(p-q)\lambda_0\).  Distance squared to \(\mathcal C_0\) first confines every internally chain-transitive set to the segment by the argument of (13).  On the segment, the continuous function
\[
 W(\lambda)=
 \begin{cases}
 \lambda/\lambda_0,&0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_0,\\
 (1-\lambda)/(1-\lambda_0),&\lambda_0\leq\lambda\leq1
 \end{cases}
\]
strictly decreases along every nonconstant solution outside \(\{0,\lambda_0,1\}\) and never increases from those three points.  Since \(W(\{0,\lambda_0,1\})=\{0,1\}\) has empty interior, \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\) puts every internally chain-transitive set inside \(\{0,\lambda_0,1\}\) with \(W\) constant.  The set \(\{0,1\}\) is not internally chain transitive: for \(\epsilon<1/3\), the solutions from its endpoints are constant, so no \((\epsilon,S)\)-chain contained in that two-point set can travel from one endpoint to the other.  Thus only the three asserted singletons are internally chain transitive for the smaller inclusion.

That smaller graph cannot approximate the recursion uniformly near the switching surface.  If \(\Delta=0\) and \(d_1<d_2\), equation (10) makes arm one greedy at both contexts and the conditional mean is \(h_1\).  But \(h_1\notin\mathcal C_0\): the affine map \((u,v)\mapsto h(u,v)\) is injective, every parameter pair on \(\mathcal C_0\) satisfies \(u+v=1\), and \(h_1\) corresponds to \((p,p)\), whose sum is \(2p\neq1\).  Compactness therefore gives \(\operatorname{dist}(h_1,\mathcal C_0)>0\).  The \(O(t^{-1/2})\) bonus cannot be discarded uniformly in the \(O(t^{-1/2})\) switching layer.

The extra internally chain-transitive sets of the correct graph are computable.  Solving (15) for its smaller \(v\)-root gives
\[
 v(u)=\frac{3u+18-\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}{32}. \tag{17}
\]
For \(u\in[u_0,p]\), substitution verifies \(f(u,v(u))=0\) and \(v(u_0)=1-u_0\).  Direct differentiation gives
\[
 v'(u)=\frac1{32}\left(3+\frac{228-50u}{2\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}\right)>0, \tag{18}
\]
because \(u\leq p<1\).  The elementary bounds \(67<\sqrt{4609}<68\) and \(73<\sqrt{5457}<74\) imply
\[
 0.23<v(u_0)<0.24,\qquad 0.308<v(p)<0.314.
\]
Consequently \(q<u,v(u)<p\), \(u+v(u)\geq1\), and
\[
 u+16v(u)\leq p+16v(p)<p+16(0.314)<6.014<\frac{17}{2}. \tag{19}
\]
The first two inequalities put \((u,v(u))\) in the triangle with vertices \((p,q),(q,p),(p,p)\), while (19) says that the first Gram coordinate of \(h(u,v(u))\) is smaller than the second.  Therefore
\[
 h(u,v(u))\in\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1\},\qquad
 \Delta(h(u,v(u)))=0,\qquad 0\in F(h(u,v(u))). \tag{20}
\]
Each singleton in (20) is an equilibrium and hence internally chain transitive.  The map \(h\) is injective because the two linear forms \(u+16v\) and \(u-4v\) have nonzero determinant; the family is therefore uncountable.  At \(u=p\), equation (17) simplifies to
\[
 v(p)=\frac{2097}{3200}-\frac{3\sqrt{5457}}{640}=v_*,
\]
and the bounds above show that this equilibrium differs from the three two-mode candidates.  This proves the stated obstruction.

It remains to separate what the exact recursion itself establishes from what the enlarged inclusion merely permits.  Put \(\Delta_t=\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}-\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\).  On \(\mathcal L_+\), equation (6) makes the bonus vanish while \(\Delta_t\) is eventually bounded below by a positive constant.  Hence eventually arm one is greedy at context \(1\) and arm two at context \(-4\).  Applying the bounded martingale law proved above to the four context--action counts, and using (7) for the reward noise, gives
\[
 \frac1t(D_{t,1},D_{t,2},N_{t,1},N_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow h_+=\left(\frac{23}{40},\frac{317}{40},\frac{19}{80},-\frac{79}{480}\right). \tag{21}
\]
Equations (1) and (21) yield the first displayed slope limit.  The identical argument on \(\mathcal L_-\) gives
\[
 \frac1t(D_{t,1},D_{t,2},N_{t,1},N_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow h_-=\left(\frac{317}{40},\frac{23}{40},-\frac{79}{80},\frac{19}{480}\right), \tag{22}
\]
and hence the second displayed slope limit.  The limiting slope gaps are, respectively,
\[
 \frac{19}{46}+\frac{79}{3804}=
 \frac{37955}{87492}>0,
 \qquad
 -\frac{79}{634}-\frac{19}{276}=-\frac{16925}{87492}<0. \tag{23}
\]

We now prove the uniform positive-probability assertion directly.  Fix a sufficiently large even integer \(n\).  For the positive regime, prescribe a length-\(2n\) history consisting first of \(n\) observations with \(X=1,A=1\), then \(n\) observations with \(X=-4,A=2\), and prescribe equally many positive and negative Rademacher signs for each selected arm.  Each prescribed context has probability \(1/2\), each prescribed action has conditional probability at least \(q\), and each prescribed relevant sign has conditional probability \(1/2\).  Thus this history has probability at least \((q/4)^{2n}\), uniformly over \(\eta_0\in[-1,1]^2\).  At its end the slopes are exactly
\[
 \frac{\eta_{0,1}+n/2}{1+n},\qquad
 \frac{\eta_{0,2}-n/3}{1+16n}, \tag{24}
\]
which converge uniformly in \(\eta_0\) to \(1/2\) and \(-1/48\), and both inverse Gram coordinates are \(O(n^{-1})\).

Starting from the terminal sufficient statistics in (24), define the deterministic positive-regime mean ray by adding \(kh_+\) after \(k\) further occasions.  Each slope on this ray is a denominator-weighted average of its terminal slope and the corresponding slope ratio in \(h_+\).  By (23)--(24), compactness of the two pairs of endpoint ratios gives constants \(\gamma_0>0\) and \(n_0<\infty\), independent of \(\eta_0\), such that for \(n\geq n_0\) the two mean-ray slopes differ by at least \(3\gamma_0\) for every \(k\geq0\).  Its Gram coordinates are bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(n+k\), so the absolute difference of its UCB bonuses is at most \(C(n+k)^{-1/2}\).  Increase \(n_0\) so that this bound is at most \(\gamma_0\).

After the prefix, let \(\zeta\) be the first \(k\geq0\) for which the greedy-arm map computed from the actual state after \(k\) further observations is not arm one at context \(1\) and arm two at context \(-4\).  The prefix and the preceding gap bounds make \(\zeta>0\).  If \(U^{\circ}_\ell\) is the actual sufficient-statistic increment at the \(\ell\)-th occasion after the prefix, define
\[
 M_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k\mathbf 1\{\ell-1<\zeta\}\bigl(U^{\circ}_\ell-h_+\bigr).
\]
The indicator is known before the \(\ell\)-th post-prefix context is drawn.  On \(\{\ell-1<\zeta\}\), the clipped rule has conditional mean increment \(h_+\) by (9).  Thus \((M_k)\) is a four-dimensional stopped martingale with a common bounded-increment constant depending only on the fixed \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\).  The stopping argument used above, applied on the dyadic blocks \(2^jn\leq n+k<2^{j+1}n\), yields, for every fixed \(\gamma>0\),
\[
 P\left(\sup_{k\geq0}\frac{\|M_k\|_\infty}{n+k}>\gamma
 \mathrel{\Big|}\text{the prescribed prefix}\right)
 \leq \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{C}{\gamma^22^jn}
 =\frac{C_\gamma}{n}. \tag{25}
\]
Indeed, a crossing on the \(j\)-th block requires a stopped partial sum of size at least \(\gamma2^jn\), whereas orthogonality bounds its second moment by a constant times \(2^{j+1}n\).

All mean-ray denominators are bounded below by a constant times \(n+k\), so the ratio map is uniformly Lipschitz under the relative perturbation in (25).  Choose \(\gamma\) small enough both to preserve a positive constant lower bound for the perturbed denominators and to change the slope gap by at most \(\gamma_0\).  The same denominator bound makes the actual bonus difference \(O((n+k)^{-1/2})\); enlarge \(n_0\) once more so that it is at most \(\gamma_0\).  On the complementary event in (25), suppose that \(\zeta<\infty\).  All increments through time \(\zeta\) were generated in the positive regime, so the actual state at \(\zeta\) equals the mean-ray state plus \(M_\zeta\).  Its slope gap is therefore at least \(2\gamma_0\), while its bonus difference has absolute value at most \(\gamma_0\).  The UCB index difference is then positive at context \(1\) and negative at context \(-4\), so its greedy-arm map is still the positive-regime map, contradicting the definition of \(\zeta\).  Therefore \(\zeta=\infty\) on that event.  The bounded martingale law applied to the ensuing fixed clipped regime gives (21), and therefore \(\mathcal L_+\).  Taking \(n\) still larger so that \(C_\gamma/n\leq1/2\) proves
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_+)
 \geq \frac12(q/4)^{2n}>0. \tag{26}
\]

For the negative regime, prescribe first \(n\) observations with \(X=-4,A=1\), then \(n\) with \(X=1,A=2\), again balancing the selected Rademacher signs.  The terminal slopes converge uniformly to \(-1/8\) and \(1/12\); the deterministic continuation adds \(kh_-\), and the second strict gap in (23) supplies the same stopped-martingale bootstrap.  Consequently
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_-)>0. \tag{27}
\]
On either event the corresponding slope sequence converges.  If \(\beta^+\) and \(\beta^-\) denote the two displayed deterministic limits, split each Cesaro average at a fixed index after which every summand is at most an arbitrary \(\epsilon>0\); the fixed prefix divided by \(T\) vanishes.  Therefore, respectively,
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\left\|\bigl(\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\bigr)^\top-\beta^+\right\|_2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\left\|\bigl(\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\bigr)^\top-\beta^-\right\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is the asserted pathwise Cesaro-error conclusion.

Finally, define the empirical context-specific arm-one rates
\[
 u_T=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=1,A_t=1\}}
 {\max\{1,\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=1\}\}},\qquad
 v_T=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=-4,A_t=1\}}
 {\max\{1,\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=-4\}\}}.
\]
The conditional arm-one probability at either context is always in \([q,p]\).  Applying the bounded martingale law to each context--action indicator after subtracting its predictable mean, and applying it to the centered context indicators, shows that each context count divided by \(T\) tends to \(1/2\) and that every subsequential limit of \((u_T,v_T)\) lies in \([q,p]^2\).  If along a common subsequence both slopes tend to a common coordinate and \((u_T,v_T)\to(u,v)\), then (1), (7), and the context-count law give
\[
 \frac1T(D_{T,1},D_{T,2},N_{T,1},N_{T,2})\longrightarrow h(u,v).
\]
The denominators in this limit are positive because \((u,v)\in[q,p]^2\).  Equality of the two limiting slope ratios is therefore equivalent, by (15), to
\[
 u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0.
\]
If same-arm switching-layer occupation is excluded, only the two opposite-context modes remain and (14) forces \(v=1-u\), producing \((u_0,b_0)\).  But (10) and the continuum in (17)--(20) show that this exclusion is not a consequence of the exact recursion or of the differential-inclusion theorem: same-arm modes survive throughout the shrinking switching layer.  The graph is discontinuous there, and the preceding argument supplies neither an avoidance estimate for switching-surface convergence nor a quantitative hitting, escape, or equicontinuity estimate uniform over compact initial sets.  It therefore establishes neither finite almost-sure tail selection nor zero switching-surface probability, and it does not establish compact-uniform moduli \(r_T(K_0),\delta_T(K_0)\).  Accordingly, it provides no derivation of membership in \(\mathfrak P_T\) or of an inference theorem, exactly as the scope clause of the statement records.  This proves every part of the theorem.
