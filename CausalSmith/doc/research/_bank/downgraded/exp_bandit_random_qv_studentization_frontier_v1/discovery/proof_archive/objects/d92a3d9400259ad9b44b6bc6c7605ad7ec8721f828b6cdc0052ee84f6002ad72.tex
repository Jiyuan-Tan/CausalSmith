Set
\[
 q_j=c^\top V_jc>0,
 \qquad s=c^\top\Sigma c>0.
\]
Positivity follows from positive definiteness of \(V_j\) and \(\Sigma\) and from \(c\ne0\); it justifies every division below. By the deterministic interval definition,
\[
 \{\tau\in C_T^\Sigma(c,\alpha)\}
 =\left\{\left|\sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\right|
 \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{s}\right\}.
\]
The finite-mixture limit in Theorem 1 is continuous, so its CDF approximation at the two endpoints gives, with \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\),
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}P\{\tau\in C_T^\Sigma(c,\alpha)\}
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}p_j\left\{2\Phi\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q_j}}\right)-1\right\}.
\]
If \(q_j=s\) for every \(j\), the right-hand side is \(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha\), proving sufficiency.

Conversely, suppose the coverage equals \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). As \(\alpha\) ranges over \((0,1)\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) ranges over \((0,\infty)\). Hence
\[
 \sum_jp_j\left\{2\Phi\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q_j}}\right)-1\right\}
 =2\Phi(z)-1
 \qquad\text{for every }z>0.
\]
Let \(X\) have the centered finite Gaussian mixture \(\sum_jp_jN(0,q_j)\). Both \(X/\sqrt s\) and a standard Gaussian are symmetric. The last display equates their absolute-value distribution functions on \((0,\infty)\), and symmetry therefore equates their full laws. Equality of second and fourth moments is legitimate because the mixture is finite, and it gives
\[
 \sum_jp_jq_j=s,
 \qquad
 3\sum_jp_jq_j^2=3s^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_jp_j(q_j-s)^2
 =\sum_jp_jq_j^2-2s\sum_jp_jq_j+s^2\sum_jp_j=0.
\]
Every \(p_j\) is strictly positive and every summand is nonnegative, so \(q_j=s\) for each \(j\in\mathcal J\). This proves necessity as an equality-of-laws statement. The use of every nominal level is essential: it supplies the equality for all \(z>0\); no conclusion is drawn from a single accidental equality.
