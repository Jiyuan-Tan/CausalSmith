All stochastic orders below are uniform over the displayed class: for example, \(R_T=O_P(a_T)\) means that for every \(\zeta>0\) there are \(M<\infty\) and \(T_0\) such that \(\sup_P P\{\|R_T\|>Ma_T\}\le\zeta\) for all \(T\ge T_0\). Fix first \(c\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), \(\omega_c>0\), and the class \(\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\).

For \(\theta\in\Theta\), put
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t(\theta).
 \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\), conditional on \((X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\), inverse-probability weighting cancels the logged assignment probability. By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}full\text{-}data}\), \(W_t\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\). Iterated expectation therefore gives, for every \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
 \tag{2}
\]
At the defining causal root, \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\). Thus \(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\), the sequence \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence, and
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T.
 \tag{3}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply, simultaneously for all \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
 \|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon.
 \tag{4}
\]
Martingale orthogonality and (4) yield
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^T E_P\|\xi_t\|_2^2
 \le M_0^2/T,
 \tag{5}
\]
and hence \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).

We next localize the empirical root. The first-derivative envelope in \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\text{-}z\text{-}map}\) and overlap make \(G_T\) \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz on \(\Theta\). Dominated differentiation, justified by the same envelope, makes \(M_P\) \(KD_1\)-Lipschitz. Fix \(u>0\), let \(b_u=\kappa(u)>0\), and take a finite Euclidean net of the fixed compact set \(\Theta\) with mesh
\[
 \zeta_u=\frac{b_u}{8(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
At each net point, (2), (4), martingale orthogonality, and Chebyshev's inequality show that \(G_T-M_P\) converges uniformly in probability to zero. The net is finite and common to all member laws. A union bound and the two Lipschitz inequalities thus give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\!\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>b_u/2\right\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:ipw\text{-}z\text{-}estimator}\) and (3),
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T).
 \tag{8}
\]
The last term is \(o(T^{-1/2})\), since \(e_T\to0\) under \(\mathrm{ass:vanishing\text{-}modulus}\). Combining (7)--(8) with
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|M_P(\theta)-G_T(\theta)\|_2
 +\|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \tag{9}
\]
and then using \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}root\text{-}separation}\) proves, for each fixed \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2>u\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{10}
\]

Let \(A_T^\partial=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\). Differentiating (2) is legitimate by the common derivative envelope. The entries of \(A_T^\partial-H\) are averages of bounded matrix martingale differences, so entrywise martingale orthogonality in fixed dimension gives
\[
 A_T^\partial-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{11}
\]
The perturbation inequality \(\sigma_{\min}(C)\ge\sigma_{\min}(D)-\|C-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), (11), and \(\mathrm{ass:jacobian\text{-}nonsingular}\) imply, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T^\partial)\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
 \tag{12}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature envelope and overlap give
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T^\partial\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le (D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
 \tag{13}
\]
On the event in (12), define \(F_T(\theta)=\theta-(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Equations (12)--(13) and the declared restriction \(D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/4\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta F_T(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le q_0:=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12.
 \tag{14}
\]
Moreover, by (5) and (12),
\[
 \|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le (2/h_-)\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{15}
\]
The root-interior assumption and \(\rho<s_\Theta\) put this closed ball inside \(\Theta\). On the event where the left side of (15) is at most \((1-q_0)\rho\), (14) shows that \(F_T\) maps the ball into itself and is a contraction. Indeed, successive iterates from \(\theta^*\) have distances bounded by a geometric series, so completeness and continuity give a fixed point \(\theta_T^0\); (14) also makes it unique. The same geometric bound gives
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le \frac{\|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{16}
\]
By (10), with \(u=\rho/4\), and (16), this root is within \(\rho/2\) of \(\widetilde\theta_T\) with probability tending uniformly to one and hence belongs to \(\mathcal Z_T\). Every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\), so uniqueness in the \(\rho\)-ball makes \(\theta_T^0\) the unique member of \(\mathcal Z_T\). Consequently,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{17}
\]

On the asymptotically certain exact-root event, set
\[
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 \bigl(\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\bigr)\,ds.
 \tag{18}
\]
Equations (11), (13), and (17) give \(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). Thus \(\overline H_T\) is invertible with probability tending uniformly to one. The fundamental theorem of calculus, \(G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=0\), and (3) give
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-\overline H_T^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T.
 \tag{19}
\]
The inverse identity
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1},
 \tag{20}
\]
together with (5), (17)--(19), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), therefore yields
\[
 R_T:=\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 +H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{21}
\]
The fallback branch does not affect (17) or (21), because its probability is bounded by the first quantity in (17).

We next compare realized and predictable quadratic variation. Define
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
 \tag{22}
\]
Every entry of \(D_t\) is a martingale difference, and (4) gives \(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\). Entrywise martingale orthogonality implies
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T D_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^T E_P\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le4M_0^4/T.
 \tag{23}
\]
Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm, Chebyshev's inequality proves
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{24}
\]
The derivative envelope and overlap give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
 \tag{25}
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(M_0\), and hence
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
 \tag{26}
\]
Averaging (26) and invoking (17) yields
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{27}
\]
Equations (11), (13), and (17) likewise give
\[
 \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{28}
\]

For the scalar self-normalization step, define the deterministic law-dependent direction and quadratic variations
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\qquad
 A_T^2=\sum_{t=1}^T E_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}],\qquad
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^T U_t^2.
 \tag{29}
\]
Dominated differentiation and the derivative envelope imply
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le KD_1.
 \tag{30}
\]
Jacobian nonsingularity gives \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\); by (4),
\[
 |U_t|\le L_c:=\frac{B\|c\|_2}{\varepsilon h_-}.
 \tag{31}
\]
The projected predictable-QV assumption is exactly
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\{A_T^2/T<\omega_c\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{32}
\]
In particular, \(A_T^2\to\infty\) uniformly in probability. Equations (24) and (29), together with the common bound on \(a\), give
\[
 T^{-1}(Q_T-A_T^2)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{33}
\]
On the event in (32), division is legal, and (33) gives
\[
 Q_T/A_T^2-1=O_P(T^{-1/2})=o_P(1).
 \tag{34}
\]
The ratio may be assigned any value on the complementary event, whose probability tends uniformly to zero. Equations (32)--(34) also imply \(P(Q_T>0)\to1\) uniformly. With \(\delta=2\), (31)--(32) give
\[
 A_T^{-4}\sum_{t=1}^T E_P|U_t|^4
 \le \frac{TL_c^4}{T^2\omega_c^2}
 =\frac{L_c^4}{T\omega_c^2}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{35}
\]
on the event \(A_T^2/T\ge\omega_c\), while its complement is uniformly negligible. Thus (29), (31)--(35) verify every hypothesis of \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\), and
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}
 =\frac{\sum_{t=1}^T U_t}{\sqrt{Q_T}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1).
 \tag{36}
\]
This convergence is uniform over \(\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\). Indeed, otherwise a sequence \(P_T\) whose discrepancy stayed bounded away from zero would still satisfy (31)--(35), with the same envelope and floor, and the cited triangular-array theorem would give a contradiction. Since \(\Phi\) is continuous, a finite continuity-point partition followed by monotonicity upgrades convergence in distribution in (36) to convergence in Kolmogorov distance. Symmetry gives the same conclusion after multiplying the statistic by \(-1\).

It remains to connect (36) to the feasible denominator. Equation (28), the perturbation inequality used in (12), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) show that \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) uniformly and, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{37}
\]
Furthermore, \(\|\widehat\Omega_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_0^2\) by (4). Define the total scalar quantities
\[
 q_T^0=T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta,
 \qquad
 \widehat q_T^0=
 \begin{cases}
 c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\Omega_T\widehat H_T^{-\top}c,
 &\text{on }\mathcal H_T,\\
 0,&\text{on }\mathcal H_T^c.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{38}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:event\text{-}truncated\text{-}rqv\text{-}sandwich}\), \(\widehat q_T^0=\widehat q_T(c)\). On \(\mathcal H_T\), insert and subtract the two inverse factors and use (27), (37), and the uniform matrix bounds. Since \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\), this proves
\[
 \widehat q_T^0-q_T^0=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \tag{39}
\]
Equations (32)--(33) imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P\{q_T^0<3\omega_c/4\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{40}
\]
Because \(\omega_c\) is a fixed positive constant, (39) also gives
\[
 (\widehat q_T^0-q_T^0)/\omega_c=o_P(1).
 \tag{41}
\]
Equations (28), (40), and (41) imply that \(\mathcal H_T\) occurs and \(\widehat q_T(c)\ge\omega_c/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Hence the definition of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{42}
\]
Thus every inverse in the construction is evaluated only where it exists, and the identity-matrix fallback makes the statistic total while being asymptotically inactive. On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), (39)--(41) permit division and imply
\[
 \widehat q_T^0/q_T^0=1+o_P(1),
 \qquad \sqrt{q_T^0/\widehat q_T^0}=1+o_P(1).
 \tag{43}
\]
Also, (21) and \(\widehat q_T^0\ge\omega_c/2\) on this event imply
\[
 \frac{|c^\top R_T|}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T^0}}=O_P(T^{-1/2})=o_P(1).
 \tag{44}
\]
Using the definitions of the pivot and the sandwich, (21), and (38), we therefore obtain
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1).
 \tag{45}
\]
For any real random variables \(Y_T,R_T'\), every \(h>0\), and every \(z\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 P(Y_T\le z-h)-P(|R_T'|>h)
 \le P(Y_T+R_T'\le z)
 \le P(Y_T\le z+h)+P(|R_T'|>h).
 \tag{46}
\]
Apply (46) to the remainder in (45), use (36), (42), and the uniform continuity of \(\Phi\), and then let \(h\downarrow0\). This gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{47}
\]
By the definitions \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\), \(\widehat\tau_T=c^\top\widehat\theta_T\), \(\mathrm{def:realized\text{-}qv\text{-}pivot}\), and \(\mathrm{def:realized\text{-}qv\text{-}wald\text{-}interval}\),
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
 \tag{48}
\]
Equation (47) and \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) prove the stated uniform coverage for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

We now prove the simultaneous all-contrast corollary. For \(P\in\mathfrak Q_T\) and \(\|c\|_2=1\), (30) implies
\[
 \|H^{-\top}c\|_2\ge\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}\ge(KD_1)^{-1}.
 \tag{49}
\]
On the event \(\lambda_{\min}(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)\ge\omega_-\),
\[
 c^\top H^{-1}(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)H^{-\top}c
 \ge\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}.
 \tag{50}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}predictable\text{-}qv\text{-}growth}\) implies the projected condition simultaneously for all unit \(c\), with \(\omega_c=\omega_-/(KD_1)^2\). Every bound in (1)--(46), including \(|U_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), is common over \(P\) and the unit sphere. If either the pivot or coverage conclusion failed uniformly in \((P,c)\), a witnessing sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) would satisfy the scalar cited theorem and all comparison bounds with these common constants, contradicting (36)--(47). Therefore the conclusions hold with the additional supremum over \(\|c\|_2=1\).

Finally take \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\). The already established \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) gives a constant \(C<\infty\), common to \(T\) and \(P\), such that
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T),
 \qquad \Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d.
 \tag{51}
\]
For fixed \(c\ne0\), let again \(a=H^{-\top}c\). Equations (30) and (51) imply
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja
 \ge\omega_-\|a\|_2^2
 \ge b_c:=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}.
 \tag{52}
\]
For every fixed \(0<\omega_c<b_c\), the operator-norm inequality, Markov's inequality, \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), and (51) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}
 P\!\left\{a^\top(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)a<\omega_c\right\}\\
 &\quad\le
 \frac{C\|c\|_2^2(r_T+\delta_T)}
 {h_-^2(b_c-\omega_c)}\longrightarrow0,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{53}
\]
where the limit uses \(\mathrm{ass:vanishing\text{-}modulus}\). The definition of \(\mathfrak P_T\) includes all pointwise base conditions of \(\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\), and (53) supplies its only additional, directional class-level condition. Hence \((\mathfrak P_T)\) may be used as the chosen projected class for this fixed contrast and floor. Applying (17), (21), and (24)--(48) proves the finite-tail-attractor pivot and Wald-coverage corollaries. The implication (53) is precisely the required map from the uniform Cesaro attraction modulus to directional predictable-QV growth; no pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\) is used.
