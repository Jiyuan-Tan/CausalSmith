We construct the experiment on the even horizons \(T=2n\); this suffices for the asserted subsequential obstruction. Let \(K=2\), \(d=1\), \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), let the context be constant, and take the fixed potential outcomes
\[
 Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=1\qquad(1\le t\le 2n).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the full-data law is degenerate, so \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) are independent and identically distributed and the only randomness is the randomized assignment. This verifies the independent, identically distributed full-data property. Define
\[
 g(1,x,y;\theta)=y-\frac{\theta}{2},
 \qquad
 g(2,x,y;\theta)=-y-\frac{\theta}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then, by the definition of \(M_P\),
\[
 M_P(\theta)=g(1,x,1;\theta)+g(2,x,1;\theta)=-\theta.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the unique causal moment root is
\[
 \theta^*=0=E_P[Y_t(1)]-E_P[Y_t(2)],
 \qquad H=M_P'(0)=-1.
\tag{4}
\]
This verifies the claimed causal target and the nonsingular-Jacobian property with \(h_-=1\). Moreover, on \(\Theta\), the score in (2) is bounded by \(3/2\), its first derivative is \(-1/2\), and its second derivative is zero. Thus the bounded-score and smooth-\(Z\)-map properties hold, for example with \(B=3/2\), \(D_1=1/2\), and any positive \(D_2\). Equation (3) verifies uniform root separation with \(\kappa(u)=\min\{u,1\}\): for \(0<u\le1\) the relevant infimum equals \(u\), while for \(u>1\) the constraint set is empty and its infimum is \(+\infty\). The root-interiority property holds with \(s_\Theta=1\), and one may choose any
\[
 0<\rho<\min\{1,1/(16D_2)\},
\]
which obeys the prescribed restriction because \(\varepsilon=1/4\) below and \(h_-=1\). Set the numerical tolerances \(r_T\) and \(\delta_T\) in \(\mathrm{def:ipw\text{-}z\text{-}estimator}\) equal to zero.

Write
\[
 p_t=P(A_t=1\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}).
\]
For \(t\le n\), set \(p_t=1/2\). Once \(S_n\) has been observed, set, for every \(t>n\),
\[
 p_t=
 \begin{cases}
 1/4,&S_n>0,\\
 1/2,&S_n\le0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
The rule (5) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable. Conditional on that history, draw \(A_t\) using \(p_t\), independently of the fixed potential outcomes. Hence the sequential-randomization property holds. Both arm probabilities are at least \(1/4\), so the uniform-overlap property holds with \(\varepsilon=1/4\).

At the root (4), the IPW score is
\[
 \xi_t=
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
 -\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}.
\tag{6}
\]
Sequential randomization gives
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =p_t\frac1{p_t}-(1-p_t)\frac1{1-p_t}=0,
\tag{7}
\]
so \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence. Its conditional second moment is
\[
 v(p_t):=E_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular,
\[
 v(1/2)=4,
 \qquad
 v(1/4)=\frac14(4)^2+\frac34\left(-\frac43\right)^2=\frac{16}{3}.
\tag{9}
\]
It follows directly from the definition of predictable quadratic variation that
\[
 \frac1{2n}\langle S\rangle_{2n}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 14/3,&S_n>0,\\
 4,&S_n\le0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \((2n)^{-1}\langle S\rangle_{2n}\ge4\) on every assignment path.

We next calculate the limiting score law without imposing an exogenous variance regime. On a common product space, generate the first block and, independently of it and of each other, two candidate second blocks having respectively the score laws in (6) for \(p=1/4\) and \(p=1/2\). A bounded centered variable \(U\) with variance \(\sigma^2\) satisfies, for each fixed real \(u\),
\[
 E\!\left[e^{iuU/\sqrt n}\right]
 =1-\frac{u^2\sigma^2}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),
\tag{11}
\]
because the third-order Taylor remainder is bounded uniformly by a constant times \(E|U|^3n^{-3/2}\). Multiplying the characteristic functions of the independent summands in each block, and then those of the three independent blocks, gives
\[
 \left(
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt n},
 \frac{S^{(1/4)}_{2n}-S_n}{\sqrt n},
 \frac{S^{(1/2)}_{2n}-S_n}{\sqrt n}
 \right)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ 
 \left(2Z,\frac4{\sqrt3}W_1,2W_2\right),
\tag{12}
\]
where \(Z,W_1,W_2\) are independent standard normal variables. Indeed, (11) makes the joint characteristic function converge to
\[
 \exp\!\left[-\frac12\left(4u_1^2+\frac{16}{3}u_2^2+4u_3^2\right)\right],
\]
the characteristic function of the vector on the right side of (12).

The branch-selection map is continuous whenever its first coordinate is nonzero, and \(P(Z=0)=0\). Applying it to (12) therefore yields
\[
 \frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt n}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ 
 \mathbf 1\{Z>0\}\left(2Z+\frac4{\sqrt3}W_1\right)
 +\mathbf 1\{Z\le0\}(2Z+2W_2).
\tag{13}
\]
If
\[
 W=\mathbf 1\{Z>0\}W_1+\mathbf 1\{Z\le0\}W_2,
\]
then \(W\) is standard normal and independent of \(Z\): for bounded measurable \(f,h\), independence of \(Z,W_1,W_2\) gives
\[
 E[f(Z)h(W)]
 =E[f(Z)\mathbf 1\{Z>0\}]E[h(W_1)]
 +E[f(Z)\mathbf 1\{Z\le0\}]E[h(W_2)]
 =E[f(Z)]E[h(W_1)].
\tag{14}
\]

To compare realized and predictable quadratic variation, put
\[
 D_t=\xi_t^2-E_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
By (6) and the overlap floor, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so \((D_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a bounded martingale-difference sequence with \(|D_t|\le16\). Martingale orthogonality consequently gives
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left\{\frac{[S]_{2n}-\langle S\rangle_{2n}}{n}\right\}^2\right]
 =\frac1{n^2}\sum_{t=1}^{2n}E_P[D_t^2]
 \le\frac{512}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(n^{-1}([S]_{2n}-\langle S\rangle_{2n})\to_P0\). Combining (10), (13), and (15), and using that both limiting denominators are strictly positive, proves
\[
 \frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt{[S]_{2n}}}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ 
 L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
 +\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8}.
\tag{16}
\]
The limit is integrable. From (14), the terms containing \(W\) have mean zero conditionally on \(Z\), while
\[
 E[Z\mathbf1\{Z>0\}]=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}},
 \qquad
 E[Z\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}]=-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 E[L]
 =\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{17}
\]
Since a standard normal variable has mean zero, (17) proves that the limit in (16) is not \(N(0,1)\); in particular it is noncentered.

It remains to verify that the feasible estimator and sandwich in the proposition have the same limit. For
\[
 U_t=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
 +\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t},
 \qquad R_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t,
\]
equations (2) and (6) give the exact affine identity
\[
 G_T(\theta)=\frac1T\left(S_T-\frac\theta2R_T\right).
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover, \(E_P[U_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=2\) and \(0<U_t\le4\). Martingale orthogonality and Chebyshev's inequality therefore imply
\[
 \frac{R_T}{T}\to_P2.
\tag{19}
\]
The same argument applied to the bounded martingale differences \(\xi_t\) gives \(S_T/T\to_P0\). More explicitly, for every \(\zeta>0\),
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\frac{2S_T}{R_T}\right|>\zeta\right)
 \le P(R_T/T<1)+P(2|S_T|/T>\zeta)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus the unique affine root
\[
 \vartheta_T=\frac{2S_T}{R_T}
\tag{21}
\]
belongs to \(\Theta\), and indeed to the \(\rho/2\)-neighborhood of zero, with probability tending to one. On this event the global minimum of \(\|G_T(\theta)\|_2\) over \(\Theta\) is zero. Because the numerical tolerance was set to zero, the preliminary estimator in \(\mathrm{def:ipw\text{-}z\text{-}estimator}\) must equal the unique root \(\vartheta_T\); hence \(\mathcal Z_T=\{\vartheta_T\}\) and the selected local exact root satisfies
\[
 \widehat\theta_T=\vartheta_T
\tag{22}
\]
with probability tending to one. Since \(E_P[S_T^2]=E_P[\langle S\rangle_T]\le(16/3)T\), one has \(S_T/\sqrt T=O_P(1)\). Equations (19), (21), and (22) then yield the exact-root linearization
\[
 \sqrt T\,\widehat\theta_T-\frac{S_T}{\sqrt T}
 =\left(\frac{2T}{R_T}-1\right)\frac{S_T}{\sqrt T}
 \to_P0.
\tag{23}
\]

Differentiating (18) gives
\[
 \widehat H_T=-\frac{R_T}{2T}\to_P-1=H.
\tag{24}
\]
Also, on the event in (22), evaluation at the exact root gives
\[
 \widehat\xi_t=\xi_t-\frac{\widehat\theta_T}{2}U_t.
\tag{25}
\]
Because \(|\xi_t|\le4\), \(U_t\le4\), and \(|\widehat\theta_T|\le1\), (25) implies
\[
 \left|\widehat\Omega_T-\frac{[S]_T}{T}\right|
 \le\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
 \frac{|\widehat\theta_T|U_t}{2}
 \left(2|\xi_t|+\frac{|\widehat\theta_T|U_t}{2}\right)
 \le20|\widehat\theta_T|
 \to_P0.
\tag{26}
\]
Take \(c=1\), \(h_-=1\), and the constant horizon-specific event-truncation floor \(\omega_{1,T}=4\) in \(\mathrm{def:event\text{-}truncated\text{-}rqv\text{-}sandwich}\). By (24), \(P(\mathcal H_T)\to1\). On \(\mathcal H_T\), (24), (26), and the deterministic bound \([S]_T/T\le16\) imply
\[
 \widehat q_T(1)-\frac{[S]_T}{T}\to_P0.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (10), (15), and (27) show
\[
 P\{\widehat q_T(1)\ge2\}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{28}
\]
Hence \(P\{\mathcal E_T(1)\}\to1\), and on that event \(\widehat V_T(1)=\widehat q_T(1)\) in dimension one. The denominators in (27) are bounded away from zero with probability tending to one, so (23) and (27) give
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(1)
 -\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{[S]_T}}
 \to_P0.
\tag{29}
\]
Combining (16) and (29) proves that the feasible IPW--Z pivot has the same nonstandard limit \(L\).

As an exact smallest-instance stress test, take \(n=1\). Enumerating all four assignment paths gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
(A_1,A_2)&P(A_1,A_2)&S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}&\langle S\rangle_2/2&2S_2/R_2\\ \hline
(1,1)&1/8&3/\sqrt5&14/3&2\\
(1,2)&3/8&1/\sqrt{13}&14/3&2/5\\
(2,1)&1/4&0&4&0\\
(2,2)&1/4&-\sqrt2&4&-2
\end{array}
\tag{30}
\]
The two unconstrained roots outside \(\Theta\) invoke the prescribed fallback; with zero tolerance the four selected estimates are respectively \(1,2/5,0,-1\), whose expectation is \(1/40\ne\theta^*\). Thus no finite-sample unbiasedness is being smuggled into the asymptotic claim. Likewise, at the two-sided critical value \(1\), the exact score-pivot coverage is
\[
 P\!\left\{\left|S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}\right|\le1\right\}=\frac58
 <2\Phi(1)-1,
\tag{31}
\]
so the smallest instance already fails the corresponding nominal Gaussian coverage. Finally, (10) and (15) simultaneously verify the predictable-QV floor and realized-to-predictable QV consistency. Thus neither property prevents the endogenous selection mechanism in (5), completing the claimed obstruction.
