All stochastic orders in this proof are uniform over the displayed class. Fix \(c\ne0\), and write
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]
Sequential randomization gives cancellation of the logged propensity conditional on \((W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\), and independent full-data arrivals make \(W_t\) independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\). Therefore
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
\tag{2}
\]
At \(\theta^*\), the defining equation \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) makes \(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\) a martingale difference and gives \(G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T\). Bounded scores and overlap imply, uniformly in \(\theta\),
\[
 \|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon.
\tag{3}
\]
Martingale orthogonality then yields
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|\xi_t\|_2^2
 \le M_0^2/T.
\tag{4}
\]

We next verify the exact-root expansion. The derivative envelope and overlap make \(G_T\) \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz, while differentiation under the bounded expectation makes \(M_P\) \(KD_1\)-Lipschitz. For each fixed \(u>0\), take a finite net of the common compact set \(\Theta\) with mesh
\[
 \zeta_u=\frac{\kappa(u)}{8(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
\tag{5}
\]
At every net point, (2), (3), martingale orthogonality, and Chebyshev's inequality bound \(G_T-M_P\) uniformly; a union bound and the two Lipschitz inequalities give
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>\kappa(u)/2\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The preliminary estimator's defining inequality and (4) imply
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1).
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (6), (7), and the root-separation assumption proves
\[
 \widetilde\theta_T\longrightarrow\theta^*
 \quad\text{uniformly in probability}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(A_T=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\). Differentiating (2) under the common derivative envelope shows that the entries of \(A_T-H\) are averages of bounded matrix martingale differences. Thus, in fixed dimension,
\[
 A_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{9}
\]
The Jacobian lower bound and the singular-value perturbation inequality imply, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T)\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|A_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho\), the curvature and overlap assumptions give
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le(D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{11}
\]
On the event in (10), the map \(F_T(\theta)=\theta-A_T^{-1}G_T(\theta)\) has Lipschitz modulus at most
\[
 q_0=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12
\tag{12}
\]
on the closed \(\rho\)-ball. Root interiority puts this ball in \(\Theta\), and (4), (10) imply \(\|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). With uniformly asymptotic probability one, \(F_T\) consequently maps the ball into itself. Its iterates are Cauchy by (12), so completeness and continuity give a unique fixed point \(\theta_T^0\) in the ball, satisfying
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\frac{\|A_T^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (8) and (13) place this root within \(\rho/2\) of \(\widetilde\theta_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) lies within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\). Uniqueness on the \(\rho\)-ball therefore gives
\[
 \sup_P P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{14}
\]
On \(E_T\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=G_T(\theta^*)+overline H_T
 (\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*),
 \quad
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 (\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*))\,ds.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (9), (11), and (14) imply \(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). The inverse identity
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1}
\tag{16}
\]
and (4), (15) yield
\[
 R_T:=\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 +H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{17}
\]
The fallback branch is immaterial because its probability is bounded by \(P(E_T^c)\).

For the matrix comparisons, set
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\tag{18}
\]
Every entry is a martingale difference and \(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\). Entrywise orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le4M_0^4/T,
\tag{19}
\]
so \(T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). Smoothness, overlap, and (14) give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2,
\tag{20}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{21}
\]

The directional step is where early measurability is load-bearing. Put
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,qquad
 Q_T^c=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2=a^\top[S]_Ta.
\tag{22}
\]
The Jacobian lower bound and (3) give
\[
 |U_t|\le L_c:=\frac{B\|c\|_2}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{23}
\]
The class definition supplies \(\Lambda_T\in\mathcal F_{k_T}\), \(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}\),
\[
 T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty,qquad
 \frac{k_T}{T\omega_{c,T}}\to0,
\tag{24}
\]
and the replaced projected-QV assumption is exactly
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in probability. Apply \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\) with \(N_n=T\), \(h_n=k_T\), \(\lambda_n=\Lambda_T\), and \(\nu_n=\omega_{c,T}\). Equations (23)--(25) verify all its hypotheses and give
\[
 \frac{Q_T^c}{T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\tag{26}
\]
uniformly. Uniform weak convergence to the continuous normal law implies uniform Kolmogorov convergence: choose a finite grid on which the normal tails have mass below an arbitrary \(\epsilon\), use convergence at the grid points, and bracket every intermediate distribution-function value by monotonicity.

We now compare the oracle and feasible square roots at the rate needed when \(\omega_{c,T}\) vanishes. Equation (21), the Jacobian lower bound, and the inverse identity imply \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) and, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{27}
\]
Let
\[
 q_T^0=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T(a^\top\xi_t)^2,
 \qquad \widehat u_t=c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\xi_t
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal H_T.
\tag{28}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality in Euclidean \(T\)-space, followed by (20), (27), \(\|\widehat\xi_t\|_2\le M_0\), and \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}-\sqrt{q_T^0}\right|
 &\le\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 |\widehat u_t-a^\top\xi_t|^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \|c\|_2\|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}M_0
 +\|a\|_2(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Dividing (29) by \(\sqrt{\Lambda_T}\ge\sqrt{\omega_{c,T}}\) is legal, and (24) makes the result \(o_P(1)\). Equation (26) therefore yields, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_T}}
 =1+o_P(1).
\tag{30}
\]
In particular, uniformly with probability tending to one, \(\widehat q_T(c)\ge\Lambda_T/2\ge\omega_{c,T}/2\). Hence
\[
 \sup_P P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{31}
\]
This also shows why the information elbow is \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\): comparison of standard deviations in (29), rather than the coarser absolute comparison of variances, costs only \((T\omega_{c,T})^{-1/2}\).

On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), equations (17), (24), and (30) imply
\[
 \frac{|c^\top R_T|}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt{T\omega_{c,T}}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{32}
\]
Using (17), (22), (26), and (30), the definitions of the total pivot and event-truncated sandwich give
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}+o_P(1).
\tag{33}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\) is negligible by (31). For every \(h>0\), the inclusions
\[
 \{Y_T\le z-h\}\setminus\{|R_T'|>h\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T+R_T'\le z\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T\le z+h\}\cup\{|R_T'|>h\}
\tag{34}
\]
applied to (33), followed by (26), uniform continuity of \(\Phi\), and then \(h\downarrow0\), prove the asserted uniform Kolmogorov limit.

By \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\), \(\widehat\tau_T=c^\top\widehat\theta_T\), and the interval definition,
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
\tag{35}
\]
The normal limit and \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) prove coverage.

Finally let \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\). Put
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},
 \qquad k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,
\tag{36}
\]
and let \(J_T\) be the nearest attractor to \(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\), with fixed tie-breaking. Then \(J_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable, \(k_T/T\to0\), and \(Tr_T/k_T\to0\). On the attraction event,
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le Tr_T/k_T<s_\eta/2
\tag{37}
\]
eventually. Attractor separation therefore gives \(J_T=J\); hence \(\sup_PP(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\to0\).

Let \(\Lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a\). Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) gives \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\) and \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\), so
\[
 \Lambda_T\ge b_c:=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}>0.
\tag{38}
\]
Take \(\omega_{c,T}=b_c\). Then \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) and \(k_T/(T\omega_{c,T})\to0\). The covariance map satisfies
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad \|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon},
\tag{39}
\]
by policy Lipschitzness, overlap, and bounded scores. On the attraction event and \(J_T=J\), (39) gives
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-\Omega_{J_T}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}r_T
 +\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_T}{T}.
\tag{40}
\]
The left side is bounded by \(2KB^2/\varepsilon\) off this event. Equations (37)--(40), \(\delta_T\to0\), and the identity
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})
\tag{41}
\]
therefore verify the projected early-scale assumption after division by the positive constant \(b_c\). Thus \(\mathfrak P_T\) is an admissible early-stabilized projected class for this \(c\), and the preceding pivot and coverage proof applies uniformly. This construction uses only Cesaro attraction and never pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\).
