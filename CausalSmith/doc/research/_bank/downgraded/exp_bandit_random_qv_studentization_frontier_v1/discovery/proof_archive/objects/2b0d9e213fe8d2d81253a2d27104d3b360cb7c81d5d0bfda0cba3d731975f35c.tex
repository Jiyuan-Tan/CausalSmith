We verify every primitive defining the class. The full-data vectors are i.i.d. because the two Rademacher potential outcomes are independent across occasions and arms and the context is constant. At each occasion the displayed policy is a function of the past-measurable state, and the action is drawn from that logged policy independently of the current potential outcomes, so sequential randomization holds.

At \(\eta=0\) and \(\eta=1\), the arm-probability vectors are respectively
\[
 (2/5,1/4,7/20),\qquad (2/5,1/6,13/30).
\]
Linear interpolation preserves their coordinatewise minima, so every arm probability is at least \(1/6>\varepsilon\). The arm-one and arm-two policy coordinates have slopes \(0\) and \(-1/12\), while the arm-three coordinate has slope \(1/12\); hence the policy Lipschitz modulus in the required coordinatewise supremum norm is \(L_\Pi=1/12\).

For \(\theta\in[-1,1]^2\),
\[
 \sup|Y_t(1)-\theta_1|\le1+\sqrt{2/5}<2,
 \qquad
 \sup|Y_t(2)-\theta_2|\le1+\sqrt{1/2}<2.
\]
Each score has only one possibly nonzero coordinate, so \(B=2\) is a valid common envelope. The derivative matrices are \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,0)\), \(\operatorname{diag}(0,-1)\), and zero, respectively. Their operator norms are at most one and all second derivatives vanish; thus \(D_1=D_2=1\) are valid bounds. Direct expectation gives
\[
 M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)
 =(-\theta_1,-\theta_2)^\top=-\theta,
 \qquad H=-I_2.
\]
Therefore \(\theta^*=0\) is the unique root, \(\|M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)\|_2=\|\theta\|_2\), \(\kappa(u)=u\) is valid, \(\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial[-1,1]^2)=1\), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)=1\).

For completeness, every realized empirical moment has a zero in \(\Theta\). If arm \(a\in\{1,2\}\) is observed at least once, choose coordinate \(\theta_a\) as the positive-weighted average of its observed Rademacher outcomes with weights \(1/\pi_t(a)\); this lies in the convex hull of \(\{-\sqrt{2/5},\sqrt{2/5}\}\) for arm one and of \(\{-\sqrt{1/2},\sqrt{1/2}\}\) for arm two. If the arm is never observed, its empirical coordinate moment is identically zero and any coordinate in \([-1,1]\) works. The two coordinates separate, so these choices give \(G_T(\theta)=0\). Thus the empirical moment norm has exact minimum zero.

At the first occasion the state is \(1/2\), but the arm-one probability is still \(2/5\). Hence
\[
 P^\dagger(J=1)=P^\dagger(A_1=1)=2/5,
 \qquad P^\dagger(J=2)=3/5,
\]
so both basins have positive mass. For every \(t\ge1\), \(\eta_t=\bar\eta_J\), whereas \(|\eta_0-\bar\eta_J|=1/2\). It follows exactly, not asymptotically, that
\[
 T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J|=\frac1{2T}.
\]
Thus tail selection holds, the attraction condition holds with \(r_T=1/(2T)\) and \(\delta_T=0\), and \(e_T=T^{-1/2}+1/(2T)\to0\). The proposed radius \(\rho=\varepsilon/8\) satisfies
\[
 0<\rho<\min\{1,\varepsilon/(4D_2)\}
\]
because \(\varepsilon<1/6\) and \(D_2=1\).

At \(\theta^*=0\), only arms one and two contribute to the score covariance. Since \(E[Y_t(1)^2]=2/5\) and \(E[Y_t(2)^2]=1/2\), the basin formula gives
\[
 \Omega_1
 =\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/4}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
\]
and
\[
 \Omega_2
 =\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/6}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,3).
\]
Both matrices dominate \(I_2\), so \(\omega_-=1\) is valid. Since \(H=-I_2\), the sandwich definition gives \(V_j=H^{-1}\Omega_jH^{-\top}=\Omega_j\). All class conditions have now been checked with constants independent of \(T\), proving \(P^\dagger\in\mathfrak P_T\) for every \(T\ge2\).

Finally, take \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(c=(1,0)^\top\). Then
\[
 c^\top V_1c=c^\top V_2c=c^\top\Sigma c=1,
 \qquad V_1\ne V_2.
\]
The contrastwise theorem therefore validates the deterministic sandwich for this contrast at every nominal level, while the matrixwise theorem rules out validity for all contrasts. This supplies the claimed in-class separation witness.
