Consider the bounded-noise Guo--Xu recursion with arbitrary fixed \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2>0\) and deterministic tie arm \(1\). Put \(p=99/100\), \(q=1/100\), and, for \((u,v)\in[q,p]^2\), define
\[
 h(u,v)=\left(\frac{u+16v}{2},\frac{17-u-16v}{2},
                 \frac{u-4v}{4},\frac{-3-u+4v}{24}\right).
\]
For \(t\ge1\), put \(D_{t,a}=\Phi^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}\), \(N_{t,a}=b^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}\), and \(\Psi_t=(D_{t,1}/t,D_{t,2}/t,N_{t,1}/t,N_{t,2}/t)\). Then \(q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=O(t^{-1})\) almost surely, \(\Psi_t\) obeys an exact stochastic-approximation recursion with step \(1/(t+1)\), and its affine interpolation is a bounded perturbed solution, hence an asymptotic pseudotrajectory, of the closed convexified inclusion \(\dot\psi\in F(\psi)\) below. Writing \(\Delta(\psi)=n_1/d_1-n_2/d_2\), \(h_+=h(p,q)\), \(h_-=h(q,p)\), \(h_1=h(p,p)\), and \(h_2=h(q,q)\), its graph on \(d_1,d_2>0\) is
\[
 F(\psi)=
 \begin{cases}
  \{h_+-\psi\},&\Delta(\psi)>0,\\
  \{h_--\psi\},&\Delta(\psi)<0,\\
  \operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1\}-\psi,&\Delta(\psi)=0,\ d_1<d_2,\\
  \operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_2\}-\psi,&\Delta(\psi)=0,\ d_1>d_2,\\
  \operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1,h_2\}-\psi,&\Delta(\psi)=0,\ d_1=d_2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Every almost-sure normalized-state limit set is internally chain transitive for \(F\) and is contained in the computable parallelogram \(\mathcal C=\{h(u,v):(u,v)\in[q,p]^2\}\); the projected slope limit set is its continuous image under \((d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)\mapsto(n_1/d_1,n_2/d_2)\).

As a diagnostic internal to the present analysis, consider the smaller two-mode inclusion having only \(h_+-\psi\), \(h_--\psi\), and \(\operatorname{co}\{h_+-\psi,h_--\psi\}\) on the switching surface. It is not a valid unconditional reduction of this recursion. If it were the limiting inclusion, its only internally chain-transitive sets would be the three singletons \(\{h_-\}\), \(\{h(u_0,1-u_0)\}\), and \(\{h_+\}\), where
\[
 u_0=\frac{47+\sqrt{4609}}{150},\qquad
 \frac{N_1}{D_1}=\frac{N_2}{D_2}
 =b_0:=\frac{\sqrt{4609}-91}{1224}
 \quad\text{at }h(u_0,1-u_0).
\]
The correct graph instead has an uncountable family of additional internally chain-transitive singleton equilibria. Explicitly, for
\[
 u\in[u_0,p],\qquad
 v(u)=\frac{3u+18-\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}{32},
\]
one has \(h(u,v(u))\in\mathcal C\), equal slope coordinates, first normalized Gram coordinate smaller than the second, and \(0\in F(h(u,v(u)))\). In particular,
\[
 v_*:=v(p)=\frac{2097}{3200}-\frac{3\sqrt{5457}}{640}
\]
gives an equilibrium \(h(p,v_*)\) distinct from \(h_-\), \(h(u_0,1-u_0)\), and \(h_+\). This continuum is an explicit internally-chain-transitive obstruction to the three-candidate conclusion produced by that internally posited two-mode reduction; it is not a claim that a cited publication makes that reduction.

For the actual recursion, on \(\mathcal L_+=\{\liminf_t(\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}-\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2})>0\}\) and \(\mathcal L_-=\{\limsup_t(\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}-\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2})<0\}\), respectively,
\[
 (\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2})^\top
 \longrightarrow
 \left(\frac{19}{46},-\frac{79}{3804}\right)^\top
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \left(-\frac{79}{634},\frac{19}{276}\right)^\top.
\]
Both events have probability bounded away from zero uniformly over \(\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}\), and the corresponding pathwise Cesaro errors converge to zero. If both slopes have a common-coordinate subsequential limit and \((u,v)\) is a simultaneous subsequential limit of the empirical conditional arm-\(1\) rates, then necessarily
\[
 u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0.
\]
The relation \(v=1-u\), and hence the candidate \((u_0,b_0)\), is justified only if same-arm switching-layer occupation is separately excluded; the exact recursion does not provide that exclusion. Consequently, this argument proves neither finite almost-sure tail selection nor zero probability of switching-surface convergence. It also proves no compact-initial-set uniform attraction moduli \(r_T(K_0),\delta_T(K_0)\). These are dynamics-only conclusions: they assert neither membership in \(\mathfrak P_T\) nor applicability of any inference theorem.
