Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). By the i.i.d.-full-data and sequential-randomization assumptions, and using uniform overlap to justify division by every logged propensity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=E_P\!\left[E_P\!\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}\right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
&=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
=M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. Define, as a proof intermediate,
\[
 \Gamma_P(\eta)=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{g_{t,a}(\theta^*)g_{t,a}(\theta^*)^\top}
 {\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right].
\]
The same conditional-assignment calculation gives
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_j=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_j).
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Omega_ju
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\bar\eta_j)}\right]
 \le \frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon},
\]
which proves \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). The derivative envelope permits differentiation under the finite sum and expectation, and hence
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1.
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le |p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). The policy-Lipschitz assumption therefore yields
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2},
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). Let
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T\),
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), and the uniform-attraction-modulus assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T).
\tag{1}
\]
This is the only state-to-regime approximation used below. In particular, (1) used the Cesaro inequality directly and used neither pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\) nor tail measurability of \(J\).

We next prove localization. For fixed \(\theta\), sequential randomization and i.i.d. arrivals show that the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences. Their norms are uniformly bounded, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T.
\tag{2}
\]
The empirical and population moment maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). For fixed \(x>0\), take a finite mesh of the compact set \(\Theta\) fine enough that the two mesh-approximation errors sum to at most \(x/2\). Chebyshev's inequality applied to (2), followed by the union bound over this fixed mesh, gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator obeys its defining approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly. The uniform-root-separation assumption now implies, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\), so martingale orthogonality and bounded increments give \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. The identical entrywise calculation for the derivative score, using the derivative envelope, gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\tag{5}
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap bounds imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon.
\tag{6}
\]
The definition of \(\rho\), the Jacobian lower bound, and (5) make the right-hand side of (6) smaller than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On this event define \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Since \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), (6) makes \(\Psi_T\) a contraction with constant at most \(1/2\) on the closed \(\rho\)-ball. Moreover,
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2.
\]
The last term is at most \(\rho/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. The root-interior assumption makes the closed ball a subset of \(\Theta\). To derive existence directly, start at \(\theta^{(0)}=\theta^*\) and iterate \(\theta^{(n+1)}=\Psi_T(\theta^{(n)})\). The self-map inequality keeps every iterate in the closed ball, and contraction gives
\[
 \|\theta^{(n+1)}-\theta^{(n)}\|_2
 \le 2^{-n}\|\theta^{(1)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_2.
\]
The geometric bound makes the iterates Cauchy. The closed Euclidean ball is complete, so they converge to some \(\theta_T^0\) in the ball; continuity of \(\Psi_T\) makes this limit a fixed point. If \(x,y\) are two fixed points, then \(\|x-y\|_2\le\|x-y\|_2/2\), hence \(x=y\). Finally, applying the self-map inequality at the fixed point gives
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{7}
\]
By (4) and (7), both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) are within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\). Every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\), hence lies in the contraction ball and equals \(\theta_T^0\). Thus \(P(E_T^c)\to0\) uniformly and the selected estimator equals \(\theta_T^0\), except on a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the preceding event, Taylor's formula and the curvature bound give
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)
 =T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad
 \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2.
\]
Equations (5) and (7) imply \(\Delta_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) and \(R_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). Multiplication by \(\sqrt T H^{-1}\) proves
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T)
\tag{8}
\]
uniformly. Restoring the fallback changes no uniform order statement because its probability tends uniformly to zero.

It remains to obtain the distributional conclusion without conditioning on the regime label. Set
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,\qquad
 \Lambda_T=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).
\]
The vanishing-modulus assumption implies \(k_T/T\to0\). On \(\mathcal A_T\), the sum of the first \(k_T\) state discrepancies is no larger than \(Tr_T\); hence \(Tr_T/k_T\le r_T/\beta_T\le\sqrt{r_T}\). On \(\mathcal A_T^c\), both covariance averages are bounded by \(M_\Gamma\). Therefore
\[
 E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\sqrt{r_T}+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1)
\tag{9}
\]
uniformly. With
\[
 B_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
\]
the same calculation, now accounting for the omitted prefix, gives
\[
 E_P\|B_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1).
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently \(E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)\) uniformly. Notice that the early measurability of \(\Lambda_T\) comes from the predictable states; (9), which is itself a consequence of the Cesaro event, supplies its approximation to the possibly non-early-measurable label atom.

Fix a unit vector \(c\) and put \(a=H^{-\top}c\). The proved upper bound on \(H\) and the assumed lower singular-value bound give
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(|a^\top\xi_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), while regime nondegeneracy gives
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0.
\]
Also \(a^\top\Lambda_Ta\le v_+:=M_\Gamma/h_-^2\). Define the \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable clipped variance
\[
 \lambda_{T,P,c}=\max\{v_0/2,a^\top\Lambda_Ta\}.
\]
Since \(a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0\), (9)--(10) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P|a^\top B_Ta-\lambda_{T,P,c}|
 &\le \|a\|_2^2E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
   +\|a\|_2^2E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &=o(1)
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
uniformly in \(P\) and unit \(c\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) to \(X_{T,t}=-a^\top\xi_t\), \(\mathcal G_{T,t}=\mathcal F_t\), and \(\lambda_{T,P,c}\). Its bounded-increment, positive-lower-variance, finite-upper-variance, early-measurability, and tail predictable-QV conditions follow respectively from the preceding display, regime nondegeneracy, the covariance upper bound, the definition of \(\Lambda_T\), and (11). Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\left\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\xi_t\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_{T,P,c}}}\right)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
Let \(\phi\) denote the standard normal density. For variances in a compact interval bounded away from zero, differentiation shows
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\Phi(z/\sqrt q)\right|
 =\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}\frac{|x|\phi(x)}{2q}\le \frac{C}{q}.
\]
Thus (9) and the clipping definition permit replacing \(\lambda_{T,P,c}\) in (12) by \(a^\top\Omega_Ja\). The resulting expectation is
\[
 E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_Ja}}\right)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_P}p_{P,j}\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{c^\top V_{P,j}c}}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
Martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\left|a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\xi_t\right|^2
 \le Ck_T/T=o(1),
\tag{14}
\]
so the prefix is negligible uniformly. Equation (8) has a uniformly \(o_P(1)\) remainder because \(e_T\to0\). The normal-mixture densities in (13) are uniformly bounded by a constant \(C_f\) from the common positive variance lower bound. If \(R_T=o_P(1)\) is either negligible remainder, then for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
 P\{X_T\le z-\eta\}-P\{|R_T|>\eta\}
 \le P\{X_T+R_T\le z\}
 \le P\{X_T\le z+\eta\}+P\{|R_T|>\eta\}.
\]
The corresponding mixture distribution changes by at most \(C_f\eta\). First take the uniform limit in \(T\) and then let \(\eta\downarrow0\). Applied to (12)--(14) and (8), these inequalities prove the asserted uniform finite-mixture approximation.

For completeness, the regime covariance matrices satisfy the common bounds
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d\preceq V_{P,j}
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d.
\tag{15}
\]
The upper bound uses \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\); the lower bound uses \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) and \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\). Since there are finitely many possible supports, (15), compactness of the probability simplex, and finite-dimensional compactness give, from every sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\), a subsequence having constant support and convergent weights and covariance matrices. The uniform CDF approximation then gives the stated subsequential mixture law, with zero limiting weights omitted.

If \(q_j=c^\top V_j^\infty c\) and \(\bar q=\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j\), the second and fourth moments of the limiting mixture are \(\bar q\) and \(3\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j^2\). A centered Gaussian with variance \(\bar q\) has fourth moment \(3\bar q^2\), and the difference is
\[
 3\sum_jp_j^\infty(q_j-\bar q)^2.
\]
It is strictly positive when two positive limiting weights have different projected variances, proving non-Gaussianity.

Finally, stable convergence relative to the label is not implied by the broadened primitives, and the broadening beyond pointwise attraction is strict. For odd \(T\), take \(d=1\), two actions with constant probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), i.i.d. Rademacher variables \(R_t\), \(Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=R_t\), and \(g(a,x,y;\theta)=(y-\theta)/2\) on \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Let \(\mathsf H=[0,1]\), let both representative states equal zero, put \(\eta_0=0\), and deterministically set \(\eta_t=1\) when \(t\) is a power of two and \(\eta_t=0\) otherwise. Take the policy to be constant in \(\eta\), and set \(J=1\) if \(\sum_{t=1}^TR_t>0\) and \(J=2\) otherwise. The score is bounded and linear, \(M_P(\theta)=-\theta\), \(H=-1\), the root is interior and uniformly separated, the constant policy is Lipschitz and satisfies overlap, and both regime covariances equal one. The finite-label condition holds, and
\[
 T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J|
 \le \frac{1+\lfloor\log_2T\rfloor}{T}.
\]
Thus the Cesaro condition holds with \(r_T=(1+\lfloor\log_2T\rfloor)/T\) and \(\delta_T=0\), while \(\eta_t\) does not converge to zero because its power-of-two subsequence equals one. This law is admitted by the new class and excluded by the old pointwise-convergence premise. The exact empirical root is \(T^{-1}\sum_tR_t\).

Let \(h(1)=1\), \(h(2)=-1\), and \(f(x)=\tanh(x)\). The actual joint expectation is
\[
 E\left|\tanh\left(T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right)\right|.
\]
It is bounded away from zero without a limit theorem. Indeed, for \(X_T=T^{-1}(\sum_tR_t)^2\), independence gives \(E[X_T]=1\) and \(E[X_T^2]=3-2/T\le3\). If \(A_T=\{X_T>1/2\}\), then Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 1=E[X_T]\le\tfrac12+E[X_T\mathbf1_{A_T}]
 \le\tfrac12+\sqrt{3P(A_T)},
\]
so \(P(A_T)\ge1/12\). The joint expectation is therefore at least \(\tanh(1/\sqrt2)/12>0\). Stable convergence to a Gaussian innovation independent of \(J\) would instead give \(E[h(J)]E[f(Z)]=0\) by symmetry. Thus label-stable convergence fails inside the class even with equal covariance atoms. This counterexample also emphasizes why no pointwise state convergence or tail-label premise was used anywhere in the positive argument.
