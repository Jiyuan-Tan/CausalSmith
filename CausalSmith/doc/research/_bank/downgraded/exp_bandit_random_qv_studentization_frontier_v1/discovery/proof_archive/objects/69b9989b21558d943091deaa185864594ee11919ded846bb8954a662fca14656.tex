All stochastic orders below are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\) and, when a contrast is present, over \(\|c\|_2=1\). The bounded-score and overlap assumptions give
\[
 \|\xi_t\|_2\le M_\xi:=B/\varepsilon \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
Every entry of \(D_t\) is a martingale difference and
\[
 \|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_\xi^2. \tag{2}
\]
Martingale orthogonality, summed over the fixed \(d^2\) entries, yields
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le \frac{4M_\xi^4}{T}. \tag{3}
\]
Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm, Chebyshev's inequality proves
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{4}
\]

The exact-root localization and fallback conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) imply
\[
 \|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{5}
\]
For every \(t\), the derivative and overlap bounds give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le \frac{D_1}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2. \tag{6}
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(M_\xi\), and hence
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2. \tag{7}
\]
Averaging (7), then using (4)--(5) and the predictable-QV expectation bound in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\), with Markov's inequality for its last term, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 (\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &\quad+\left\|T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}+r_T+\delta_T)=O_P(e_T).
\end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball, the curvature and overlap bounds similarly imply, outside the uniformly negligible fallback event,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat H_T-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le\|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\frac{D_2}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The fallback branch has probability tending uniformly to zero, so (9) holds unconditionally as a uniform-in-probability statement.

For any matrices \(A,B\), the variational definitions of the least singular value and least eigenvalue give \(|\sigma_{\min}(A)-\sigma_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) and, for symmetric matrices, \(|\lambda_{\min}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). Thus (8)--(9), \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\), and \(\Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d\) imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(\mathcal E_T^c)\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T\), every inverse is legally evaluated and
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\widehat H_T^{-1}(H-\widehat H_T)H^{-1},
 \qquad \|\widehat H_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-. \tag{11}
\]
The first theorem also gives
\[
 \|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad \|\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Omega:=KB^2/\varepsilon. \tag{12}
\]
Insert and subtract the three sandwich factors one at a time. Equations (8)--(12) then imply, on \(\mathcal E_T\),
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\Omega_T\widehat H_T^{-\top}
 -H^{-1}\Omega_JH^{-\top}=O_P(e_T). \tag{13}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T^c\), \(\widehat V_T=I_d\), while (12) bounds \(V_J\) uniformly. Equation (10) makes this branch uniformly negligible, so (13) and the regime-indexed covariance definition prove
\[
 \widehat V_T-V_J=O_P(e_T). \tag{14}
\]

We now derive the pivotal limit. Fix a unit vector \(c\), and define
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\qquad
 A_T^2=\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}],\qquad
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2. \tag{15}
\]
The upper Jacobian bound from the first theorem and the assumed lower singular-value bound give
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad |U_t|\le L:=\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-}. \tag{16}
\]
The predictable-QV approximation and regime nondegeneracy imply
\[
 \frac{A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)a
 =a^\top\Omega_Ja+o_P(1),
 \qquad a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0. \tag{17}
\]
Thus \(A_T^2\to\infty\) in probability uniformly. Equation (4) gives
\[
 \frac{Q_T-A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)a
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}), \tag{18}
\]
so (17)--(18) imply \(Q_T/A_T^2\to_P1\) and \(P(Q_T>0)\to1\) uniformly. With \(\delta=2\), (16)--(17) also imply
\[
 A_T^{-4}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4
 \le A_T^{-4}TL^4\longrightarrow_P0. \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, on the event \(A_T^2/T\ge v_0/2\), the right-hand side is at most \(4L^4/(Tv_0^2)\), and that event has probability tending uniformly to one.

Every triangular array formed by an arbitrary sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\) and unit contrasts \(c_T\) therefore satisfies all hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\). It follows that
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}
 =\frac{\sum_{t=1}^TU_t}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\Rightarrow N(0,1). \tag{20}
\]
This convergence is uniform in \(P\) and unit \(c\): otherwise a sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) with Kolmogorov distance bounded away from zero would contradict (20) for that triangular array. Weak convergence to the continuous distribution \(\Phi\) implies Kolmogorov convergence directly: partition the real line at finitely many continuity points so that each intervening \(\Phi\)-increment is arbitrarily small, and use monotonicity of distribution functions between adjacent partition points.

The first theorem gives
\[
 \sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-T^{-1/2}a^\top S_T+O_P(e_T). \tag{21}
\]
Furthermore, (4), (8), (14), and \(c^\top V_Jc=a^\top\Omega_Ja\) imply
\[
 c^\top\widehat V_Tc-T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta=O_P(e_T). \tag{22}
\]
By (17)--(18), both quantities compared in (22) are at least \(v_0/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Division and square roots are therefore legitimate on an event whose probability tends uniformly to one. Since \(e_T\to0\), equations (20)--(22) and
\[
 |\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt x+\sqrt y}
\]
yield
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1) \tag{23}
\]
uniformly. Applying the same two-sided \(\eta\)-smoothing inclusions used in the first theorem to (20) and (23), together with uniform continuity of \(\Phi\), proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|\longrightarrow0. \tag{24}
\]

For the causal Wald consequence, define
\[
 I_T(c,\alpha)=\left[\widehat\tau_T-z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \sqrt{\frac{c^\top\widehat V_Tc}{T}},\ 
 \widehat\tau_T+z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \sqrt{\frac{c^\top\widehat V_Tc}{T}}\right].
\]
Because \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\), the event \(\tau\in I_T(c,\alpha)\) is exactly \(|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\). Hence (24) gives, for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}
 \left|P\{\tau\in I_T(c,\alpha)\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\longrightarrow0. \tag{25}
\]
No step uses pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\). The finite label enters only through \(\Omega_J\), and every path approximation is supplied by the uniform Cesaro inequality through the first theorem. Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}pointwise\text{-}rqv\text{-}pivot}\) concerns a distinct pointwise AIPW/DML setup; accordingly the present derivation verifies the claimed uniform controls but does not assert literal inclusion of that published class.
