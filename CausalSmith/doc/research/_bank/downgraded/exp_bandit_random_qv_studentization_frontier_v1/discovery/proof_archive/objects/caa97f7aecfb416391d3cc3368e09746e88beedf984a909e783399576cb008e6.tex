Set \(s=c^\top\Sigma c>0\) and \(q(v)=c^\top vc\).  Positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\), the compact covariance lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\), and \(c\ne0\) justify every square root and division below.  The interval definition gives
\[
 \{\tau\in C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\left\{\left|\sqrt{T_\ell}\,c^\top
 (\widehat\theta_{T_\ell}-\theta^*)\right|
 \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt s\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Apply the basin-stable theorem to \(c/\|c\|_2\) and rescale.  Its marginal case \(\psi\equiv1\), evaluated at the positive and negative endpoints, gives for each fixed \(z>0\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &P_\ell\!\left\{\left|\sqrt{T_\ell}\,c^\top
 (\widehat\theta_{T_\ell}-\theta^*)\right|\le z\sqrt s\right\}\\
 &\quad=E_{P_\ell}\!\left[
 2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{c^\top V_Jc}}\right)-1\right]+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The map \(v\mapsto2\Phi(z\sqrt{s/q(v)})-1\) is bounded and continuous on the compact covariance set.  Since \(V_J=V_\infty\) almost surely, weak convergence of the laws of \(V_\infty\) turns (2) into
\[
 \int\left\{2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q(v)}}\right)-1\right\}
 \,d\mu(v).
\tag{3}
\]
If \(q(V)=s\) \(\mu\)-almost surely, (3) equals \(2\Phi(z)-1\).  Taking \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}\) proves sufficiency for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

Conversely, suppose the coverage limit equals \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  As \(\alpha\) ranges over this interval, \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) ranges over \((0,\infty)\).  Equations (1)--(3) imply
\[
 \int\left\{2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q(v)}}\right)-1\right\}
 \,d\mu(v)=2\Phi(z)-1\qquad(z>0).
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(Q=q(V)\), let \(Z_1\sim N(0,1)\) be independent of \(V\), and set \(X=\sqrt QZ_1\).  Both \(X/\sqrt s\) and \(Z_1\) have continuous symmetric laws.  Equation (4) equates their absolute-value distribution functions at every positive argument, so symmetry equates their full laws.  The compact covariance bounds make \(Q\) bounded, and equality of laws permits comparison of second and fourth moments.  Direct Gaussian integration gives \(E[Z_1^2]=1\) and \(E[Z_1^4]=3\), whence
\[
 E[X^2]=E[Q]=s,
 \qquad E[X^4]=3E[Q^2]=3s^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently
\[
 E[(Q-s)^2]=E[Q^2]-2sE[Q]+s^2=0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(Q=s\) \(\mu\)-almost surely, proving necessity.  The argument uses all positive critical values through (4) and makes no inference from one isolated nominal level.
