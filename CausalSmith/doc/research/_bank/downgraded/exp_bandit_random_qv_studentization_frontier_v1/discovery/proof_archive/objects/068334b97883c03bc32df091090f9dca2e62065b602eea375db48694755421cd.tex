Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). By the i.i.d.-full-data and sequential-randomization assumptions, and using the overlap bound to justify division,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=E_P\left[E_P\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}\right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
&=E_P\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
=M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. Define for this proof
\[
 \Gamma_P(\eta)=E_P\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{g_{t,a}(\theta^*)g_{t,a}(\theta^*)^\top}
 {\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right].
\]
The same conditioning calculation gives
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_j=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_j).
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Omega_ju
 \le E_P\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\bar\eta_j)}\right]
 \le \frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon},
\]
which proves \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). The derivative envelope permits differentiation under the finite sum and expectation, and hence
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1.
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le|p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). The policy-Lipschitz assumption therefore yields
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2},
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
we have
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), and the uniform-attraction-modulus assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T).
\]

We next prove localization rather than assume it. For fixed \(\theta\), sequential randomization and i.i.d. arrivals show that the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences. Their norms are uniformly bounded, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T.
\]
The empirical and population maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). For fixed \(x>0\), take a finite mesh of the compact set \(\Theta\) fine enough that these two Lipschitz errors sum to at most \(x/2\). Chebyshev's inequality and the union bound over that fixed mesh then give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator obeys its defining approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly. The uniform-root-separation assumption now implies, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\), so martingale orthogonality and bounded increments give \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. The same entrywise calculation for the derivative score, using the derivative envelope, gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap bounds imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon.
\]
The choice of \(\rho\) and the Jacobian lower bound make the right side smaller than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On this event, define \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Since \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), \(\Psi_T\) is a contraction with constant at most \(1/2\) on the closed \(\rho\)-ball. Moreover,
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2.
\]
The last term is at most \(\rho/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. The root-interior condition ensures that this closed ball lies in \(\Theta\). Hence the contraction has exactly one fixed point \(\theta_T^0\) in the ball, and
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\]
Uniform consistency of \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and the last rate imply that both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) are within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) lies in the closed \(\rho\)-ball and therefore equals \(\theta_T^0\). Thus \(P(E_T^c)\to0\) uniformly and the selected estimator equals \(\theta_T^0\) except on a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the preceding event, Taylor's formula and the curvature bound give
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)
 =T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad
 \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2.
\]
Because \(\Delta_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) and \(R_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), multiplication by \(\sqrt T H^{-1}\) proves
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\]
The complement of the exact-root event has uniformly vanishing probability, so restoring the fallback does not change this uniform order statement.

It remains to obtain the distributional conclusion without conditioning on the label. Set
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,\qquad
 \Lambda_T=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).
\]
The vanishing-modulus assumption implies \(k_T/T\to0\), and \(Tr_T/k_T\le\sqrt{r_T}\). Since \(\Lambda_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable, the attraction-event calculation gives
\[
 E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1)
\]
uniformly. With
\[
 B_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
\]
the same calculation, now accounting for the omitted prefix, yields
\[
 E_P\|B_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1).
\]
Consequently \(E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)\) uniformly.

Fix a unit vector \(c\) and put \(a=H^{-\top}c\). The proved upper bound on \(H\) and the assumed lower singular-value bound give
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(|a^\top\xi_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), while basin nondegeneracy gives
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0.
\]
Also \(a^\top\Lambda_Ta\le v_+:=M_\Gamma/h_-^2\). Define the \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable clipped variance
\[
 \lambda_{T,P,c}=\max\{v_0/2,a^\top\Lambda_Ta\}.
\]
Because \(a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0\),
\[
 E_P|a^\top B_Ta-\lambda_{T,P,c}|
 \le \|a\|_2^2E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\|a\|_2^2E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)
\]
uniformly in \(P\) and unit \(c\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) to \(X_{T,t}=-a^\top\xi_t\), \(\mathcal G_{T,t}=\mathcal F_t\), and \(\lambda_{T,P,c}\). It follows that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_z
 \left|P\left\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\xi_t\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_{T,P,c}}}\right)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The normal CDF is uniformly Lipschitz in its variance on intervals bounded away from zero. Since \(\lambda_{T,P,c}-a^\top\Omega_Ja\to0\) in \(L^1\) uniformly, the expectation in the last display may be replaced by
\[
 E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_Ja}}\right)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_P}p_{P,j}\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{c^\top V_{P,j}c}}\right).
\]
Martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\left|a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\xi_t\right|^2
 \le Ck_T/T=o(1),
\]
so the prefix is negligible uniformly. Finally, the exact-root linearization has a uniformly \(o_P(1)\) remainder because \(e_T\to0\). The limiting mixture densities are uniformly bounded by the common positive variance lower bound, so the usual \(\eta\)-smoothing inequality transfers the preceding approximation through both negligible remainders and proves the asserted uniform CDF formula.

The matrices \(V_{P,j}\) satisfy the common bounds
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d\preceq V_{P,j}
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d.
\]
Indeed, the upper bound uses \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), while the lower bound uses \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) and \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\). There are finitely many possible supports. Hence every sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\) has a subsequence with a constant support and with all finitely many weights and covariance entries convergent. The uniform CDF approximation then gives the claimed subsequential mixture law.

If \(q_j=c^\top V_j^\infty c\) and \(\bar q=\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j\), the second and fourth moments of the limiting mixture are \(\bar q\) and \(3\sum_jp_j^\infty q_j^2\). A centered Gaussian with variance \(\bar q\) has fourth moment \(3\bar q^2\), and the difference is
\[
 3\sum_jp_j^\infty(q_j-\bar q)^2.
\]
It is strictly positive when two positive limiting weights have different projected variances, proving non-Gaussianity.

Finally, stable convergence relative to the label is not implied. For odd \(T\), take \(d=1\), two actions with constant probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), i.i.d. Rademacher variables \(R_t\), \(Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=R_t\), and \(g(a,x,y;\theta)=(y-\theta)/2\) on \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Let the compact state space be the singleton \(\{0\}\), let both candidate attractors and every state equal zero, but set \(J=1\) if \(\sum_{t=1}^TR_t>0\) and \(J=2\) otherwise. The score is bounded and linear, \(M_P(\theta)=-\theta\), \(H=-1\), the root is interior and uniformly separated, the constant policy is Lipschitz and satisfies overlap, and both basin covariances equal one. Thus this triangular sequence satisfies every defining primitive with \(r_T=\delta_T=0\); both labels have probability \(1/2\), and the exact empirical root is \(T^{-1}\sum_tR_t\). With \(h(1)=1\), \(h(2)=-1\), and \(f(x)=\tanh(x)\), the actual joint expectation is
\[
 E\left|\tanh\left(T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right)\right|.
\]
This expectation is bounded away from zero without invoking a limit theorem. Indeed, for \(X_T=T^{-1}(\sum_tR_t)^2\), independence gives \(E[X_T]=1\) and \(E[X_T^2]=3-2/T\le3\). If \(A_T=\{X_T>1/2\}\), then Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 1=E[X_T]\le\tfrac12+E[X_T\mathbf 1_{A_T}]
 \le\tfrac12+\sqrt{3P(A_T)},
\]
so \(P(A_T)\ge1/12\). The displayed joint expectation is therefore at least \(\tanh(1/\sqrt2)/12>0\). By symmetry and oddness, an innovation independent of \(J\) would instead give \(E[h(J)]E[\tanh(-Z)]=0\). Thus the stable-label claim fails inside the frozen class, whereas the marginal mixture approximation remains valid.
