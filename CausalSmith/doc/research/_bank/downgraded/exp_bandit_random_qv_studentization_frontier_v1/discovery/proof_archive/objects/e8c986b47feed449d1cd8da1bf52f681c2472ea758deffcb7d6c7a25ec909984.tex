Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). By sequential randomization, conditional independence of \(A_t\) and the current potential outcomes, independent full-data arrivals, and overlap,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &=E_P\!\left[E_P\!\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}\right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
 &=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. The same conditioning calculation and \(\mathrm{def:basin\text{-}covariance}\) give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_J=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J).
\tag{2}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Gamma_P(\eta)u
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right]
 \le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). Because \(P(J=j)\ge p_->0\) for every \(j\), the relations \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) and \(\Omega_\infty\succeq\omega_-I_d\) almost surely imply \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) for every \(j\). Differentiation through the finite sum and expectation is justified by the common derivative envelope, and hence
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le|p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). The policy Lipschitz condition and the definition of \(\Gamma_P\) therefore yield
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2},
\tag{5}
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equations (2) and (5) imply
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T.
\tag{6}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), and the uniform attraction assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T),
\tag{7}
\]
where \(C=\max\{L_\Gamma,2M_\Gamma\}\) is independent of \(T\) and \(P\).

We next establish the exact-root expansion. For fixed \(\theta\), equations (1)--(3) with \(\theta^*\) replaced by \(\theta\) show that the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences. Their norms are uniformly bounded, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T.
\tag{8}
\]
The empirical and population maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). A finite mesh of the fixed compact set \(\Theta\), Chebyshev's inequality applied to (8), and a union bound therefore show, for every fixed \(x>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator satisfies its defining approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1)
\tag{10}
\]
uniformly. Root separation then implies, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\). Equations (1) and (3), martingale orthogonality, and fixed \(d\) give \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. The identical entrywise argument for the bounded derivative score gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\tag{12}
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap assumptions imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon.
\tag{13}
\]
The radius restriction, (12), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) make the right-hand side less than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On that event define \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Since \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), equation (13) makes \(\Psi_T\) a contraction with modulus at most \(1/2\), and
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2.
\tag{14}
\]
With probability tending uniformly to one, the last term is at most \(\rho/2\), and root interiority puts the entire closed ball in \(\Theta\). Starting from \(\theta^*\), iterate \(\theta^{(n+1)}=\Psi_T(\theta^{(n)})\). Equation (14) keeps every iterate in the closed ball, and the contraction bound gives \(\|\theta^{(n+1)}-\theta^{(n)}\|_2\le2^{-n}\|\theta^{(1)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_2\). The geometric-series bound makes the iterates Cauchy; completeness of \(\mathbb R^d\), closedness of the ball, and continuity of \(\Psi_T\) give a fixed point \(\theta_T^0\). If two fixed points existed, the contraction inequality would make their distance at most half itself, so the fixed point is unique. Since a fixed point of \(\Psi_T\) is a zero of \(G_T\),
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (11) and (15) put both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) and therefore equals \(\theta_T^0\). Hence \(\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\to0\), and the selected estimator equals \(\theta_T^0\) outside a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the exact-root event, Taylor's formula and the curvature envelope give
\[
 0=T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2.
\tag{16}
\]
Using (12), (15), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) in (16) yields
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\tag{17}
\]
The fallback event is uniformly negligible, so (17) holds unconditionally in the uniform-in-probability sense.

It remains to prove the basin-stable law. Let
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},
 \qquad k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil.
\tag{18}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(1\le k_T<T\), while the vanishing-modulus condition gives
\[
 \frac{k_T}{T}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\frac{r_T}{\beta_T}\le\sqrt{r_T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{19}
\]
Define \(\bar\eta_T^{\mathrm{pre}}=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\), and let \(J_T\) be its nearest member of \(\{\bar\eta_1,\ldots,\bar\eta_m\}\), with deterministic tie-breaking. Then \(J_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable. On \(\mathcal A_T\),
\[
 \|\bar\eta_T^{\mathrm{pre}}-\bar\eta_J\|_2
 \le\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(m=1\), then \(J_T=J=1\) identically. If \(m\ge2\), (19) makes the right-hand side eventually less than \(s_\eta/2\), and finite-attractor separation then makes \(\bar\eta_J\) the unique nearest attractor. In either case,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(P(J=j)\ge p_-\), equation (21) also gives \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\) for every \(j\) and all sufficiently large \(T\), uniformly in \(P\).

Fix a unit vector \(c\), put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), and define \(X_t=-a^\top\xi_t\). Equations (3)--(4) and Jacobian nonsingularity imply
\[
 \frac1{KD_1}\le\|a\|_2\le\frac1{h_-},
 \qquad |X_t|\le L:=\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-},
\tag{22}
\]
and, for \(\lambda_j=a^\top\Omega_ja=c^\top V_jc\),
\[
 v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\le\lambda_j
 \le v_1:=\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}.
\tag{23}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T\cap\{J=j\}\), equations (5) and (19) give
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-\Omega_j\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T.
\tag{24}
\]
The left-hand side is always at most \(2M_\Gamma\). Conditioning (24) on \(J_T=j\), using \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\), and charging the exceptional event to (21), yields
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left.\left|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T
 E_P[X_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]-\lambda_j\right|\ \right|J_T=j\right]
 \le C_1\{r_T+k_T/T+\delta_T\}\longrightarrow0
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c,j\).

Because \(\{J_T=j\}\in\mathcal F_{k_T}\), the suffix \((X_t,\mathcal F_t)_{t=k_T+1}^T\) remains a martingale-difference array under \(P(\,\cdot\mid J_T=j)\): for \(t>k_T\), multiplication by the \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable indicator \(\mathbf1\{J_T=j\}\) preserves the zero conditional mean in (1). Its conditional predictable variances are the same as the original ones on \(\{J_T=j\}\). Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) under that conditional law, with early time \(k_T\), constant \(\lambda_T=\lambda_j\), increment bound \(L\), and the common bounds in (23). Equation (25) verifies its predictable-variation premise. Hence
\[
 \sup_{P,c,j,z}\left|P\!\left\{T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TX_t\le z\mid J_T=j\right\}
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_j}}\right)\right|\longrightarrow0,
\tag{26}
\]
where the supremum ranges over \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\), unit \(c\), \(j\in\mathcal J\), and \(z\in\mathbb R\).

Martingale orthogonality, (22), and the basin-mass floor give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left.\left|T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}X_t\right|^2\right|J_T=j\right]
 \le \frac{2L^2k_T}{p_-T}=o(1).
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, the numerator restricted to \(\{J_T=j\}\) is at most the unconditional second moment, which is the sum of the increment second moments by martingale orthogonality. The same argument applies conditionally on \(J=j\), with denominator at least \(p_-\). The remainder in (17) is uniformly \(o_P(1)\), and conditioning preserves that conclusion because \(P(J=j)\ge p_-\). To transfer (26), if \(Y\) denotes the suffix sum and \(R\) the sum of the prefix and linearization remainders, then for every \(h>0\),
\[
 P(Y\le z-h)-P(|R|>h)\le P(Y+R\le z)
 \le P(Y\le z+h)+P(|R|>h).
\tag{28}
\]
The Gaussian laws in (26) have densities bounded uniformly by \((2\pi v_0)^{-1/2}\). First choose \(h\) small and then use (27) and the conditional \(o_P(1)\) remainder to make \(P(|R|>h)\) uniformly small. Finally, for every event \(B\),
\[
 \left|P(B\mid J=j)-P(B\mid J_T=j)\right|
 \le \frac{2P(J_T\ne J)}{\min\{P(J=j),P(J_T=j)\}}
 \le\frac{4\delta_T}{p_-}.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (21), (26)--(29), and \(\lambda_j=c^\top V_jc\) prove the displayed basin-conditional approximation.

For any \(\psi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to[-1,1]\), decompose by the disjoint events \(\{J=j\}\):
\[
 E_P[\psi(J)\mathbf1\{Z_T\le z\}]
 =\sum_{j=1}^mp_j\psi(j)P(Z_T\le z\mid J=j),
 \qquad Z_T=c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*).
\tag{30}
\]
The absolute difference between (30) and the asserted Gaussian-kernel sum is at most the maximum conditional Kolmogorov error, because \(\sum_jp_j|\psi(j)|\le1\). This proves the joint \(\sigma(J)\)-stable formula uniformly over \(\psi\). Equation (23) for every unit \(c\) is equivalent to the asserted Loewner bounds on every \(V_j\). Equation (2), finite-tail selection, and the covariance definition give \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) and \(V_\infty=V_J\).

The compact covariance subsequence lemma now supplies a weakly convergent subsequence of the laws of \(V_J\). Along it, the marginal case \(\psi\equiv1\), continuity of \(v\mapsto\Phi(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc})\) on the compact covariance set, and the preceding uniform approximation give the limit law \(\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\). If \(c^\top Vc=q_0\) almost surely, this is \(N(0,q_0)\). Conversely, if it is \(N(0,\sigma^2)\), compact support permits comparison of second and fourth moments. For the standard normal density \(\phi\), integration by parts using \(\phi'(z)=-z\phi(z)\) gives \(E[Z_1^2]=1\) and \(E[Z_1^4]=3\). Independence therefore gives
\[
 E[c^\top Vc]=\sigma^2,
 \qquad 3E[(c^\top Vc)^2]=3\sigma^4,
\]
so \(\operatorname{Var}(c^\top Vc)=0\). Thus the mixture is Gaussian exactly when its projected scale is constant. The early proxy \(J_T\) was constructed solely from the common Cesaro attraction event; no pointwise convergence of the policy state was used.

All stochastic orders below are uniform over the indicated law class and, when a contrast is present, over \(\|c\|_2=1\). Write
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T Z_t(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]
By sequential randomization, conditional on \((X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\), inverse-probability weighting cancels the assignment probability. By independent full-data arrivals, \(W_t\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\). Therefore, for every \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
\tag{2}
\]
At \(\theta=\theta^*\), \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) by the definition of the causal root. Hence \(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\), \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence, and
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T.
\tag{3}
\]
The overlap and score assumptions give the common almost-sure bound
\[
 \|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon
 \quad\text{for every }\theta\in\Theta.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, martingale orthogonality and fixed \(d\) imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T}E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|\xi_t\|_2^2
 \le M_0^2/T,
\tag{5}
\]
so \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).

We next justify localization. From smoothness and overlap, \(G_T\) is \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz on \(\Theta\). Dominated differentiation, justified by the common derivative envelope, gives that \(M_P\) is \(KD_1\)-Lipschitz. Fix \(u>0\), and put \(b_u=\kappa(u)>0\), using uniform root separation. Compactness of \(\Theta\) supplies a finite Euclidean net with mesh
\[
 \zeta_u=\frac{b_u}{4(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
\tag{6}
\]
At each net point, (2), (4), martingale orthogonality, and Chebyshev's inequality show that \(G_T-M_P\) converges to zero in probability uniformly over \(P\); a union bound is legitimate because the net is finite and common to the class. Lipschitz extension from the net using (6) therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T}P\!\left\{
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>b_u/2
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The preliminary-estimator definition and (3) give
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T).
\tag{8}
\]
The numerical tolerance in (8) is \(o(1)\) by the vanishing-modulus assumption, while the first term is \(O_P(T^{-1/2})\) by (5). Combining (7)--(8) with
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|M_P(\theta)-G_T(\theta)\|_2
     +\|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
\tag{9}
\]
and then applying uniform root separation proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2>u\}
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every }u>0.
\tag{10}
\]

For exact-root existence, let \(A_T=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\). Conditional differentiation in (2), justified by the bounded derivative, shows that the summands of \(A_T-H\) are bounded matrix martingale differences. Entrywise martingale orthogonality in fixed dimension thus gives
\[
 A_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{11}
\]
For square matrices \(C,D\), the triangle inequality gives \(\sigma_{\min}(C)\ge\sigma_{\min}(D)-\|C-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). By Jacobian nonsingularity and (11), with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T)\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|A_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
\tag{12}
\]
On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap assumptions imply
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le (D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{13}
\]
Consider \(F_T(\theta)=\theta-A_T^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Equations (12)--(13) and \(D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/4\) give, on that ball,
\[
 \|\partial_\theta F_T(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le q_0:=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12.
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover, (5) and (12) give
\[
 \|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2
 =\|A_T^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{15}
\]
Root interiority and \(\rho<s_\Theta\) ensure that the closed ball is contained in \(\Theta\). On the event where (15) is at most \((1-q_0)\rho\), (14) shows that \(F_T\) maps the ball into itself and is a contraction. Starting at \(\theta^*\) and iterating \(F_T\), the geometric-series bound makes the iterates Cauchy. Completeness and continuity produce a fixed point \(\theta_T^0\); the contraction inequality proves uniqueness. The same bound gives
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\frac{\|A_T^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{16}
\]
By (10) with \(u=\rho/4\) and (16), with probability tending uniformly to one, \(\|\theta_T^0-\widetilde\theta_T\|_2<\rho/2\). Thus \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\). Conversely, every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is then within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\), so uniqueness in the \(\rho\)-ball makes it equal to \(\theta_T^0\). Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T}P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{17}
\]

On \(E_T\), the integral mean-value matrix
\[
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 \bigl(\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\bigr)\,ds
\tag{18}
\]
satisfies \(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) by (11), (13), and (17), and is invertible with probability tending uniformly to one. Equations (3) and \(G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=0\) yield
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-\overline H_T^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T.
\tag{19}
\]
The inverse identity
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1},
\tag{20}
\]
together with (5), (17)--(19), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), proves
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{21}
\]
The fallback branch does not change these uniform-in-probability conclusions because its probability tends uniformly to zero by (17).

We now compare realized and predictable quadratic variation directly. Set
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\tag{22}
\]
Every entry is a martingale difference, and (4) gives \(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\). Entrywise martingale orthogonality therefore gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le4M_0^4/T.
\tag{23}
\]
Since operator norm is at most Frobenius norm, Chebyshev's inequality proves
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{24}
\]
The derivative envelope and overlap give, for every \(t\),
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{25}
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(M_0\). Hence
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{26}
\]
Averaging (26) and applying (17) gives
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{27}
\]
Similarly, (11), (13), and (17) give
\[
 \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{28}
\]

By uniform predictable-QV growth, with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)\ge\omega_-.
\tag{29}
\]
For symmetric \(C,D\), the Rayleigh-quotient definition gives \(\lambda_{\min}(C)\ge\lambda_{\min}(D)-\|C-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). This inequality, (24), and (27) imply that \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Omega_T)\ge\omega_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. The singular-value inequality used in (12), (28), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) similarly imply \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat H_T)\ge h_-/2\). Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T}P(\mathcal E_T^c)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{30}
\]
Every inverse in the sandwich is therefore evaluated legally on \(\mathcal E_T\), and the truncation branch is asymptotically negligible.

Fix a unit vector \(c\), and define
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,\qquad
 A_T^2=\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}],\qquad
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2.
\tag{31}
\]
Dominated differentiation and the derivative envelope give
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\|\partial_\theta g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le KD_1.
\tag{32}
\]
Combining (32) with \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) yields
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad |U_t|\le L_0:=\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{33}
\]
On the event in (29),
\[
 \frac{A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)a
 \ge\omega_-\|a\|_2^2
 \ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus \(A_T^2\to\infty\) in probability uniformly. Equations (24), (33), and (34) give
\[
 \frac{Q_T}{A_T^2}-1
 =\frac{a^\top([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)a}{A_T^2}
 \longrightarrow_P0,
 \qquad P(Q_T>0)\longrightarrow1
\tag{35}
\]
uniformly. With \(\delta=2\), (33)--(34) verify the increment-moment condition in \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\): on the event in (34),
\[
 A_T^{-4}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4
 \le \frac{TL_0^4}{T^2v_0^2}
 =\frac{L_0^4}{Tv_0^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{36}
\]
That lemma and (31), (34)--(36) prove
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}
 =\frac{\sum_{t=1}^TU_t}{\sqrt{Q_T}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{37}
\]
This convergence is uniform over \((P,c)\). Indeed, failure of uniformity would supply a sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) with Kolmogorov distance bounded away from zero, while the common bounds (33)--(36) would make that triangular array satisfy the cited lemma, a contradiction. Since \(\Phi\) is continuous, weak convergence implies uniform convergence of distribution functions by a finite continuity-point partition and monotonicity.

To connect (37) to the feasible statistic, on \(\mathcal E_T\) use (27)--(28), the inverse identity (20), and the deterministic bounds (33). Since \(\|\widehat\Omega_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le B^2/\varepsilon^2\), inserting and subtracting sandwich factors one at a time yields
\[
 c^\top\widehat V_Tc
 -T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta=o_P(1).
\tag{38}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal E_T\) is negligible by (30), so (38) holds for the total event-truncated definition. Equations (24), (29), and (33) show that the second term in (38), and hence the first, is at least \(v_0/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. All square roots and divisions below are therefore legitimate on that event. Combining (21), (37), (38), and
\[
 |\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt x+\sqrt y}
 \quad(x,y>0)
\tag{39}
\]
gives
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1).
\tag{40}
\]
If \(Y_T\) is the leading term and \(R_T=o_P(1)\), then for every \(h>0\),
\[
 P(Y_T\le z-h)-P(|R_T|>h)
 \le P(Y_T+R_T\le z)
 \le P(Y_T\le z+h)+P(|R_T|>h).
\tag{41}
\]
Apply (41) uniformly, then let \(T\to\infty\) and \(h\downarrow0\). Uniform continuity and symmetry of \(\Phi\), together with (37), prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{42}
\]
Finally, the definitions \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\) and \(\mathrm{def:realized\text{-}qv\text{-}wald\text{-}interval}\) give
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
\tag{43}
\]
Equation (42) and \(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) prove the asserted uniform coverage for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

We finally verify the finite-attractor corollary without strengthening its boundary eigenvalue assumption. Let \(\mathfrak P_T\) have basin lower bound \(\omega_-\), and fix \(\underline\omega\in(0,\omega_-)\). The revalidated theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T),
 \qquad \Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d.
\tag{44}
\]
The Rayleigh-quotient inequality used after (29) and Markov's inequality therefore give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}
 P\!\left\{\lambda_{\min}(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)<\underline\omega\right\}
 \le\frac{C(r_T+\delta_T)}{\omega_--\underline\omega}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{45}
\]
Thus every finite-attractor class satisfies predictable-QV growth at every strictly smaller constant. Equations (24), (27), and (44) also give \(\widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J=o_P(1)\). Hence \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Omega_T)\ge\omega_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one, so the original finite-class truncation threshold is inactive as well. The untruncated sandwiches, pivots, and intervals under the smaller growth constant and the original threshold consequently agree with probability tending uniformly to one. This proves the stated finite-attractor corollary. The superclass proof (1)--(43) used no attractor, terminal representative, covariance limit, or variance stabilization. The cited self-normalized lemma supplies only the scalar implication in (37); the argument above verifies its variance-growth, realized-QV, exact-root, feasible-inverse, and truncation premises from the declared primitives. The separate cited pointwise Saco node records the narrower comparator scope asserted in the theorem's final sentence.

Set \(s=c^\top\Sigma c>0\) and \(q(v)=c^\top vc\). Positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\), the compact covariance lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\), and \(c\ne0\) justify every square root and division below. The deterministic interval definition gives

 \{\tau\in C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\left\{\left|\sqrt{T_\ell}\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_{T_\ell}-\theta^*)\right|
 \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt s\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For nonunit \(c\), apply the stable formula in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) to \(c/\|c\|_2\) and rescale. Put \(X_T=\sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\). Taking \(\psi\equiv1\) and applying the formula at both endpoints gives the probability of the half-open symmetric interval by subtraction. The distinction between half-open and closed intervals is \(o(1)\): for any boundary point \(x\) and \(h>0\), its atom is bounded by \(P(x-h<X_T\le x)\), and the stable approximation bounds the latter by its Kolmogorov error plus \(h\) times the common Gaussian-mixture density bound supplied by the positive covariance floor. Letting first \(T\to\infty\) and then \(h\downarrow0\) removes the atom. Therefore, for every fixed \(z>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &P_\ell\!\left\{\left|\sqrt{T_\ell}\,c^\top
 (\widehat\theta_{T_\ell}-\theta^*)\right|\le z\sqrt s\right\}\\
 &\quad=E_{P_\ell}\!\left[2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{c^\top V_Jc}}\right)-1\right]+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The map \(v\mapsto2\Phi(z\sqrt{s/q(v)})-1\) is bounded and continuous on the compact covariance set. Since \(V_\infty=V_J\) almost surely, weak convergence of the laws of \(V_\infty\) turns (2) into

 \int\left\{2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q(v)}}\right)-1\right\}\,d\mu(v).
\tag{3}
\]

Conversely, suppose the coverage limit is \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). As \(\alpha\) ranges over \((0,1)\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) ranges over \((0,\infty)\). Equations (1)--(3) imply
\[
 \int\left\{2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\frac{s}{q(v)}}\right)-1\right\}\,d\mu(v)
 =2\Phi(z)-1\qquad(z>0).
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(Q=q(V)\), let \(Z_1\sim N(0,1)\) be independent of \(V\), and put \(X=\sqrt QZ_1\). Both \(X/\sqrt s\) and \(Z_1\) have continuous symmetric laws. Equation (4) equates their absolute-value distribution functions at every positive argument, so symmetry gives equality of their full distributions. The compact covariance bounds make \(Q\) bounded, and equality of laws therefore permits comparison of second and fourth moments. Integration by parts for the standard normal density, using \(\phi'(z)=-z\phi(z)\), gives \(E[Z_1^2]=1\) and \(E[Z_1^4]=3\), so
\[
 E[X^2]=E[Q]=s,
 \qquad E[X^4]=3E[Q^2]=3s^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently
\[
 E[(Q-s)^2]=E[Q^2]-2sE[Q]+s^2=0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(Q=s\) \(\mu\)-almost surely, proving necessity. Every nominal level is essential because it supplies (4) for every \(z>0\); no conclusion was drawn from a single coverage equality.

 c^\top Vc=c^\top\Sigma c
 \qquad\mu\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{1}
