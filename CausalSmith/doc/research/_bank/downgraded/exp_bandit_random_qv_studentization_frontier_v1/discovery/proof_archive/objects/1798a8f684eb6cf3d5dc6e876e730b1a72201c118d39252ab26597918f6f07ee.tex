Put \(b_n^2=N_n\lambda_n\), and, for \(t>h_n\), define \(Y_{n,t}=X_{n,t}/b_n\) and
\[
 v_{n,t}=E[Y_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}],
 \qquad V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,t}.
\tag{1}
\]
The division is legal because \(\lambda_n\ge\nu_n>0\). Since \(b_n\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable, it is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable for every \(t>h_n\). Hence \((Y_{n,t},\mathcal G_{n,t})_{t>h_n}\) remains a martingale-difference array. Moreover,
\[
 \max_{t>h_n}|Y_{n,t}|\le
 \epsilon_n:=\frac{L}{\sqrt{N_n\nu_n}}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad V_n=\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
\tag{2}
\]
in probability.

We first derive, rather than assume, the Gaussian limit for the suffix. Fix \(u\in\mathbb R\). Stop the suffix immediately before its predictable normalized variance would exceed \(2+\epsilon_n^2\); explicitly, retain the increment at \(t\) only when \(\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{t-1}v_{n,s}\le2\). The retained indicator is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable. Taylor's formula with remainder and the martingale-difference property give for every retained increment
\[
 E[e^{iuY_{n,t}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 =1-\frac{u^2}{2}v_{n,t}+r_{n,t},
 \qquad |r_{n,t}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}\epsilon_n v_{n,t}.
\tag{3}
\]
A predictable exponential-compensator argument now gives the characteristic function. For the stopped increments write \(\widetilde Y_{n,t}\) and \(\widetilde v_{n,t}\), and define
\[
 Z_{n,j}=\exp\left\{iu\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde Y_{n,t}
 +\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde v_{n,t}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Using (3) and \(|e^x-1-x|\le x^2e^x/2\) for \(x\ge0\), conditional expectation gives
\[
 \left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
 \le C_u e^{u^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)/2}
 \left(\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,j}+\widetilde v_{n,j}^2\right).
 \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\widetilde v_{n,j}\le\epsilon_n^2\), so summing (5) and using \(\sum_j\widetilde v_{n,j}\le2+\epsilon_n^2\) proves \(E[Z_{n,N_n}]\to1\). By (2), the probability that stopping changes the suffix is at most \(P(V_n>2)\to0\), and the stopped predictable variance \(\widetilde V_n\) converges to one in probability. On the bounded range \([0,2+\epsilon_n^2]\),
\[
 \left|1-\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}\right|\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in }L^1.
 \tag{6}
\]
Since \(e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}=e^{iu\sum_t\widetilde Y_{n,t}}
e^{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2}\), equations (5)--(6), followed by removal of the negligible stopping event, yield
\[
 E\exp\left\{iu\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}Y_{n,t}\right\}
 \longrightarrow e^{-u^2/2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The elementary characteristic-function continuity criterion therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}X_{n,t}/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{8}
\]

The prefix is negligible at the same scale. Martingale orthogonality and boundedness imply
\[
 E\left(\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\right)^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}E[X_{n,t}^2]\le h_nL^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(b_n^2\ge N_n\nu_n\), Chebyshev's inequality and the second deterministic hypothesis give
\[
 \frac{1}{b_n}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (8)--(10) prove \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

It remains to establish realized quadratic variation. For \(t>h_n\), let
\[
 D_{n,t}=Y_{n,t}^2-v_{n,t}.
\tag{11}
\]
These are martingale differences, and
\[
 E[D_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le E[Y_{n,t}^4\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le\epsilon_n^2v_{n,t}.
\tag{12}
\]
Using the same predictable stopping rule, martingale orthogonality and (12) bound the stopped sum's second moment by \(\epsilon_n^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)\). The stopping probability tends to zero by (2); hence
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{13}
\]
The prefix squares obey the deterministic bound
\[
 0\le b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2
 \le\frac{h_nL^2}{N_n\nu_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (11)--(14) gives
\[
 \frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
 =\frac1{b_n^2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2+V_n+\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}
 \longrightarrow1.
\tag{15}
\]
In particular \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\). On that event,
\[
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}
 =\frac{S_n/b_n}{\sqrt{Q_n/b_n^2}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\tag{16}
\]
by (8)--(10), (15), and the elementary ratio argument obtained by restricting to \(Q_n/b_n^2\in[1/2,3/2]\). Every probability and moment bound above is uniform when the premises are uniform, which proves the final assertion.
