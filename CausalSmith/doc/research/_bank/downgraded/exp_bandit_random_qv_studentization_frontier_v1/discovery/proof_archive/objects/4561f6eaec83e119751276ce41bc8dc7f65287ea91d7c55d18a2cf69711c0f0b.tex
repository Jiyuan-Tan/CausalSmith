Put \(b_n=(N_n\lambda_n)^{1/2}\).  Division by \(b_n\) is legal because \(\lambda_n\ge\lambda_->0\).  For \(s>h_n\), \(b_n\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,s-1}\)-measurable, so
\[
Y_{n,s}:=\frac{X_{n,s}}{b_n},\qquad
v_{n,s}:=E[Y_{n,s}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}]
\tag{1}
\]
is again a martingale-difference array with conditional variances \(v_{n,s}\).  The predictable-QV premise is precisely
\[
V_n:=\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,s}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{2}
\]
The fourth-moment condition and the deterministic lower bound on \(\lambda_n\) give
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E|Y_{n,s}|^4
\le\frac{C_4}{N_n\lambda_-^2}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{3}
\]
and, by conditional Jensen followed by (3),
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E[v_{n,s}^2]
\le\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E|Y_{n,s}|^4\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]
Likewise, Lyapunov's inequality yields
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E|Y_{n,s}|^3
\le\frac{C_4^{3/4}}{\lambda_-^{3/2}\sqrt{N_n}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

We now derive the suffix central limit theorem.  Set
\[
I_{n,s}=\mathbf1\left\{\sum_{\ell=h_n+1}^{s-1}v_{n,\ell}\le2,\ v_{n,s}\le1\right\},\qquad
\widetilde Y_{n,s}=I_{n,s}Y_{n,s},\qquad
\widetilde v_{n,s}=I_{n,s}v_{n,s}.
\tag{6}
\]
The indicator is \(\mathcal G_{n,s-1}\)-measurable.  Therefore the retained variables remain martingale differences, and their total conditional variance \(\widetilde V_n=\sum_{s>h_n}\widetilde v_{n,s}\) is at most \(3\).  By (2), (4), and Markov's inequality,
\[
P\{\text{some suffix increment is deleted}\}
\le P(V_n>2)+P\!\left(\max_{s>h_n}v_{n,s}>1\right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix \(u\in\mathbb R\).  Taylor's formula, \(E[Y_{n,s}\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}]=0\), and (6) imply
\[
E[e^{iu\widetilde Y_{n,s}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}]
=1-\frac{u^2}{2}\widetilde v_{n,s}+r_{n,s},\qquad
|r_{n,s}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}I_{n,s}E[|Y_{n,s}|^3\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}].
\tag{8}
\]
Define the exponential compensator
\[
Z_{n,j}=\exp\left\{iu\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{j}\widetilde Y_{n,s}
+\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{j}\widetilde v_{n,s}\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\widetilde V_n\le3\), the modulus of every partial compensator is at most \(e^{3u^2/2}\).  Using (8) and \(|e^x(1-x)-1|\le C_u x^2\) for \(0\le x\le u^2/2\), conditional expectation in (9) gives
\[
\left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
\le C_u e^{3u^2/2}\left\{\widetilde v_{n,j}^2
+I_{n,j}E[|Y_{n,j}|^3\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Summing (10), then using (4)--(5), proves
\[
E Z_{n,N_n}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (2) and (7) imply \(\widetilde V_n\to1\) in probability.  Since \(0\le\widetilde V_n\le3\), bounded convergence in probability gives
\[
E\left|\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}-1\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
The identity
\[
e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}
=\exp\left\{iu\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde Y_{n,s}\right\}
\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}
\tag{13}
\]
and (11)--(12) show that the characteristic function of the retained suffix converges to \(e^{-u^2/2}\).  Removing the deletion event by (7) proves
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}\frac{X_{n,s}}{b_n}\ \Rightarrow\ N(0,1).
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to control the prefix and realized QV.  Martingale orthogonality and \(E X_{n,s}^2\le C_4^{1/2}\) give
\[
E\left(\frac1{b_n}\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}\right)^2
\le\frac{h_nC_4^{1/2}}{N_n\lambda_-}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{15}
\]
and Markov's inequality gives
\[
\frac1{b_n^2}\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}^2\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(s>h_n\), put \(D_{n,s}=Y_{n,s}^2-v_{n,s}\).  These are martingale differences and
\[
E D_{n,s}^2\le E Y_{n,s}^4.
\]
Hence martingale orthogonality and (3) imply
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,s}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in }L^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (2), (16), and (17) yields
\[
\frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
=\frac1{b_n^2}\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}^2+V_n
+\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,s}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\).  Equations (14)--(15) give \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\); division by the positive square root in (18) gives \(S_n/\sqrt{Q_n}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).  Because the standard normal distribution function is continuous, this weak convergence is equivalent to the displayed uniform-in-\(z\) convergence.

For uniformity, suppose one of the conclusions failed uniformly.  Choosing in row \(n\) a member law whose error stays a fixed positive amount away from zero would produce a single triangular array satisfying the same common bounds and convergence premises, contradicting the argument above.  This proves all uniform assertions.
