Put \(b_n^2=N_n\lambda_n\).  For \(t>h_n\), define
\[
 Y_{n,t}=X_{n,t}/b_n,\qquad
 v_{n,t}=E[Y_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}],\qquad
 V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,t}.
\tag{1}
\]
Division is legal because \(\lambda_n\ge\nu_n>0\).  Since \(b_n\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable, it is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable for \(t>h_n\); hence the suffix is again a martingale-difference array.  The increment bound and the first and third hypotheses give
\[
 \max_{t>h_n}|Y_{n,t}|
 \le\epsilon_n:=\frac{L}{\sqrt{N_n\nu_n}}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad V_n=\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
\tag{2}
\]
in probability.

Retain the increment at time \(t>h_n\) only when \(\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{t-1}v_{n,s}\le2\).  This indicator is predictable.  Denote the retained increment and variance by \(\widetilde Y_{n,t}\) and \(\widetilde v_{n,t}\).  Then
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde v_{n,t}\le2+\epsilon_n^2.
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\), Taylor's formula and the martingale-difference property imply
\[
 E[e^{iu\widetilde Y_{n,t}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 =1-\frac{u^2}{2}\widetilde v_{n,t}+r_{n,t},
 \qquad |r_{n,t}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,t}.
\tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
 Z_{n,j}=\exp\!\left\{iu\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde Y_{n,t}
 +\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde v_{n,t}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Using (4) and \(|e^x-1-x|\le x^2e^x/2\) for \(x\ge0\), conditional expectation gives
\[
 \left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
 \le C_u e^{u^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)/2}
 \left(\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,j}+\widetilde v_{n,j}^2\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(\widetilde v_{n,j}\le\epsilon_n^2\), summing (6) and using (3) gives \(E[Z_{n,N_n}]\to1\).  By (2), stopping changes the suffix only on \(\{V_n>2\}\), whose probability tends to zero.  The stopped total variance \(\widetilde V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde v_{n,t}\) converges in probability to one and is bounded by \(2+\epsilon_n^2\), so
\[
 E\left|1-\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The identity
\[
 e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}
 =e^{iu\sum_t\widetilde Y_{n,t}}
 e^{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2}
\]
together with (6)--(7), followed by removal of \(\{V_n>2\}\), shows that the suffix characteristic function converges to \(e^{-u^2/2}\) for every \(u\).  The characteristic-function continuity criterion gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}X_{n,t}/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{8}
\]

The prefix is negligible on this scale.  Martingale orthogonality and boundedness give
\[
 E\left(\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\right)^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}E[X_{n,t}^2]\le h_nL^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(b_n^2\ge N_n\nu_n\), Chebyshev's inequality and the second hypothesis yield
\[
 b_n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (8)--(10) prove \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

For realized quadratic variation put \(D_{n,t}=Y_{n,t}^2-v_{n,t}\) for \(t>h_n\).  These are martingale differences and
\[
 E[D_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le E[Y_{n,t}^4\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le\epsilon_n^2v_{n,t}.
\tag{11}
\]
Under the same predictable stopping rule, martingale orthogonality bounds the second moment of the stopped sum by \(\epsilon_n^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)\).  Removing \(\{V_n>2\}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{12}
\]
The prefix squares satisfy
\[
 0\le b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2
 \le\frac{h_nL^2}{N_n\nu_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
 =b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2+V_n
 +\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}
 \longrightarrow1
\tag{14}
\]
in probability.  Hence \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\).  On \(\{Q_n/b_n^2\in[1/2,3/2]\}\), division is legal, and (8)--(10), (14), and continuity of \(x\mapsto x^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}
 =\frac{S_n/b_n}{\sqrt{Q_n/b_n^2}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{15}
\]
Every bound depends only on the common \(L\) and the three displayed premise errors.  Applying the argument to any contrary sequence of member arrays proves all asserted uniform conclusions.
