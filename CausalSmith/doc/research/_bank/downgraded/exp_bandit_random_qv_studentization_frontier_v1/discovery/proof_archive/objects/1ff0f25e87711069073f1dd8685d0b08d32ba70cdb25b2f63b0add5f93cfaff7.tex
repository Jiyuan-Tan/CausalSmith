All stochastic orders below are uniform over
\(P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\).  Fix the prespecified
\(c\ne0\), and put
\[
 Z_t(\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=a\}g(a,X_t,Y_t;\theta)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})},
 \qquad G_T(\theta)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TZ_t(\theta).
\tag{1}
\]
Sequential randomization cancels the logged propensity conditional on
\((W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\), and independent full-data arrivals make \(W_t\)
independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\).  Hence
\[
 E_P[Z_t(\theta)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\right]
 =M_P(\theta).
\tag{2}
\]
At \(\theta^*\), \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\), so
\(Z_t(\theta^*)=\xi_t\) is a martingale difference and
\(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\).  Bounded scores and overlap imply
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|Z_t(\theta)\|_2\le M_0:=B/\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 E_P\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2^2\le M_0^2/T.
\tag{3}
\]

We first verify exact-root localization.  The derivative envelope makes
\(G_T\) and \(M_P\) Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective common moduli
\(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\).  For fixed \(u>0\), take a finite net of
the common compact set \(\Theta\) with mesh smaller than
\[
 \frac{\kappa(u)}{8(D_1/\varepsilon+KD_1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
For every fixed net point, the centered terms in (2) are martingale
differences of norm at most \(2M_0\), and therefore
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le4M_0^2/T.
\tag{5}
\]
Chebyshev's inequality, a union bound over the finite net, and the two
Lipschitz bounds give
\[
 \sup_P P\!\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>\kappa(u)/2\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The preliminary estimator's defining inequality and (3) imply
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1).
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) and uniform root separation prove
\(\widetilde\theta_T\to\theta^*\) uniformly in probability.

Let \(A_T^\partial=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\).  Differentiating (2)
under the common derivative envelope shows that every entry of
\(A_T^\partial-H\) is an average of bounded matrix martingale differences.
Since \(d\) is fixed,
\[
 A_T^\partial-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{8}
\]
The Jacobian lower bound and the singular-value perturbation inequality imply,
with probability tending uniformly to one,
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A_T^\partial)\ge h_-/2,
 \qquad \|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho\), curvature and overlap give
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-A_T^\partial\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le(D_2/\varepsilon)\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{10}
\]
On (9), the map
\(F_T(\theta)=\theta-(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta)\) has Lipschitz
modulus at most
\[
 q_0=\frac{2D_2\rho}{\varepsilon h_-}<\frac12
\tag{11}
\]
on the closed \(\rho\)-ball.  Root interiority puts this ball in \(\Theta\),
and (3), (9) make
\(\|F_T(\theta^*)-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  With uniformly asymptotic
probability one, \(F_T\) maps the ball into itself.  Its iterates are Cauchy
by (11), so closedness and continuity give a unique fixed point
\(\theta_T^0\) in the ball, with
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\frac{\|(A_T^\partial)^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2}{1-q_0}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{12}
\]
Consistency of \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and (12) place this root within
\(\rho/2\) of \(\widetilde\theta_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\)
lies inside the \(\rho\)-ball.  Uniqueness therefore gives
\[
 \sup_P P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{13}
\]
On \(E_T\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)=G_T(\theta^*)+\overline H_T
 (\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*),
 \qquad
 \overline H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T
 (\theta^*+s(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*))\,ds.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (8), (10), and (13) imply
\(\overline H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  The inverse identity
\[
 \overline H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\overline H_T^{-1}(H-\overline H_T)H^{-1}
\tag{15}
\]
and (3), (14) yield
\[
 R_T:=\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 +H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{16}
\]
The fallback branch changes only the uniformly negligible event \(E_T^c\).

For the matrix comparisons, set
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\tag{17}
\]
Every entry is a martingale difference and
\(\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\le2M_0^2\).  Entrywise orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le4M_0^4/T,
\tag{18}
\]
after enlarging the fixed constant if necessary for the fixed dimension.
Thus \(T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  Smoothness,
overlap, and (13) give
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\tag{19}
\]
Expanding the difference of the two score outer products and using their
common boundedness gives
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{20}
\]

The directional step uses early measurability.  Put
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,\qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,qquad
 Q_T^c=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2=a^\top[S]_Ta.
\tag{21}
\]
Jacobian nonsingularity and (3) give
\[
 |U_t|\le L_c:=\frac{B\|c\|_2}{\varepsilon h_-}.
\tag{22}
\]
The class supplies a positive \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable variable
\(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}\) such that
\[
 T\omega_{c,T}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad \frac{k_T}{T\omega_{c,T}}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{uniformly in probability}.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale
\text{-}clt}\) with \(N_n=T\), \(h_n=k_T\),
\(\lambda_n=\Lambda_T\), and \(\nu_n=\omega_{c,T}\).  Equations
(22)--(24) verify every hypothesis and yield
\[
 \frac{Q_T^c}{T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\tag{25}
\]
uniformly.  This weak convergence is uniform in Kolmogorov distance: choose a
finite grid on a compact interval, use convergence at its grid points and
monotonicity between them, then use the two normal tails outside the interval.

We compare the oracle and feasible standard deviations at the rate required
when \(\omega_{c,T}\) vanishes.  Equation (20), the Jacobian lower bound, and
(15) imply \(P(\mathcal H_T^c)\to0\) and, on \(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\tag{26}
\]
Let
\[
 q_T^0=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T(a^\top\xi_t)^2,
 \qquad \widehat u_t=c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\xi_t
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal H_T.
\tag{27}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality in Euclidean \(T\)-space, followed by
(19), (26), \(\|\widehat\xi_t\|_2\le M_0\), and
\(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}-\sqrt{q_T^0}\right|
 &\le\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 |\widehat u_t-a^\top\xi_t|^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \|c\|_2\|\widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}M_0
 +\|a\|_2(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Division by \(\sqrt{\Lambda_T}\ge\sqrt{\omega_{c,T}}\) is legal, and (23)
makes the resulting error \(o_P(1)\).  Combining (25) and (28) gives, on
\(\mathcal H_T\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}{\sqrt{\Lambda_T}}=1+o_P(1).
\tag{29}
\]
Thus \(\widehat q_T(c)\ge\Lambda_T/2\ge\omega_{c,T}/2\) with probability
tending uniformly to one, and
\[
 \sup_P P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{30}
\]
This is precisely where the information elbow \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\)
enters: (28) compares standard deviations, at relative cost
\((T\omega_{c,T})^{-1/2}\).

On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), equations (16), (23), and (29) imply
\[
 \frac{|c^\top R_T|}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt{T\omega_{c,T}}}\right)=o_P(1).
\tag{31}
\]
Using (16), (21), (25), and (29), the pivot and sandwich definitions give
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T^c}}+o_P(1).
\tag{32}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal E_T(c)\) is negligible by (30).  Applying the
two inclusions
\[
 \{Y_T\le z-h\}\setminus\{|R_T'|>h\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T+R_T'\le z\}
 \subseteq\{Y_T\le z+h\}\cup\{|R_T'|>h\}
\tag{33}
\]
to (32), then using (25), uniform continuity of \(\Phi\), and
\(h\downarrow0\), proves the asserted uniform Kolmogorov limit.  Since
\(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\) and \(\widehat\tau_T=c^\top\widehat\theta_T\),
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}.
\tag{34}
\]
The normal limit and
\(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\) prove coverage.

Finally take \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\).  Set
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\min\{T-1,\lceil T\beta_T\rceil\}.
\tag{35}
\]
This gives \(0\le k_T<T\) at every horizon.  Since \(r_T\to0\), it also
gives \(k_T/T\to0\); for all sufficiently large \(T\),
\(k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil\ge1\) and
\(Tr_T/k_T\le r_T/\beta_T\to0\).  Let \(J_T\) be the fixed-tie-broken
nearest attractor to \(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\), and use its
declared value \(1\) when \(k_T=0\).  Then \(J_T\) is
\(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  On the attraction event,
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le Tr_T/k_T<s_\eta/2
\tag{36}
\]
eventually.  Separation gives \(J_T=J\) there, so
\(\sup_PP(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\to0\).

With \(a=H^{-\top}c\), take
\[
 \Lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a,
 \qquad \omega_{c,T}=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}.
\tag{37}
\]
The scale is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) gives
\(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\) and
\(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\), whence
\[
 \Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}>0.
\tag{38}
\]
The number in (37) is constant in \(T\), so
\(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) and
\(k_T/(T\omega_{c,T})\to0\).

Policy Lipschitzness, overlap, and bounded scores imply
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad \|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{39}
\]
On the attraction event with \(J_T=J\),
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})-\Omega_{J_T}
 \right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}r_T
 +\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_T}{T}.
\tag{40}
\]
Sequential randomization and independent full-data arrivals give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).
\tag{41}
\]
Because \(\|a\|_2\le\|c\|_2/h_-\) and the denominator in (37) is bounded
below by the positive number in (38), equations (39)--(41) yield on that
event
\[
 \left|
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P[(a^\top\xi_t)^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}-1\right|
 \le C_c\left(r_T+\frac{k_T}{T}\right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{42}
\]
The complementary probability is at most
\(\delta_T+P(J_T\ne J)\to0\) uniformly.  Thus (35)--(42) verify the relative
suffix predictable-QV condition and every other early-scale premise.  The
finite-attractor class therefore inherits the pivot and coverage conclusions.
