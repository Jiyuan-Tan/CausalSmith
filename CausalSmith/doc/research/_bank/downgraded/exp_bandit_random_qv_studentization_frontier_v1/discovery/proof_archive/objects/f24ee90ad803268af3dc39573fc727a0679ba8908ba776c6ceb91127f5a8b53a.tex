Along any sequence for which the law of \(V_J\) converges weakly to \(\mu\), as in Theorem 3, one deterministic positive-definite matrix \(\Sigma\) gives asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and every nonzero contrast \(c\in\mathbb R^d\) if and only if
\[
 V=\Sigma\qquad\mu\text{-almost surely},
\]
where \(V\sim\mu\). Equivalently, the limiting covariance law is the point mass at \(\Sigma\). If \(p_{\ell j}\to p_j^0>0\) and \(V_{\ell j}\to W_j\) for every basin as in Theorem 3, this is equivalent to \(W_j=\Sigma\) for every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\). The implication from all projected equalities to matrix equality uses only the finitely many coordinate and pairwise-sum contrasts.
