\textbf{Proved partial classification.}
Put \(\mu _1=1/2\), \(\mu _2=1/12\), and write
\(C_{s,a}=\mathbf 1\{A_s=a\}\).  The augmented recursion in
\(\mathrm{def{:}guoxu\text{-}recursion}\) gives the exact identities
\[
 D_{t,a}:=\Phi^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}
 =1+\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_s^2,
 \qquad
 N_{t,a}:=b^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}
 =\eta_{0,a}+\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_s
       (\mu_a+\sigma_aR_{s,a}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=N_{t,a}/D_{t,a}.
 \tag{1}
\]
At every occasion and for either arm, the clipped randomization probability
is at least \(0.01\).  Since \(X_s\) is independent of the past and is
uniform on \(\{-4,1\}\),
\[
 E_{\eta_0}\!\left[C_{s,a}X_s^2\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}\right]
 \geq 0.01\,E[X_s^2]=\frac{17}{200}.
 \tag{2}
\]
For completeness, a strong law needed below follows directly in this bounded
setting.  If \((U_s)\) is any of the centered bounded increments used here
and \(|U_s|\leq L\), martingale orthogonality gives
\(E(\sum_{s\leq n}U_s)^2\leq L^2n\).  At \(n=k^2\), Chebyshev's inequality
therefore bounds the probability of an error larger than \(\epsilon k^2\)
by \(L^2/(\epsilon^2k^2)\).  Countable subadditivity makes only finitely many
of these events occur almost surely, and the deterministic bound of
\(L(2k+1)\) on the increments between \(k^2\) and \((k+1)^2\) fills the
gaps.  Hence \(n^{-1}\sum_{s\leq n}U_s\to0\) almost surely.  Applying this
argument to (2) yields
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_{t,a}}t\geq\frac{17}{200}
 \quad\text{and consequently}\quad
 q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=O(t^{-1})
 \qquad P_{\eta_0}\text{-almost surely}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The same argument applies to
\(C_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\), because the reward innovation is independent of the
action chosen before the reward is observed and has conditional mean zero.
Thus
\[
 \frac1t\sum_{s=1}^t C_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad P_{\eta_0}\text{-almost surely}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Let
\(\Delta_t=\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}-
\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\), and define the two separated events
\[
 \mathcal L_+=\{\liminf_t\Delta_t>0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_-=\{\limsup_t\Delta_t<0\}.
\]
The difference between the two LinUCB indices is
\[
 \Delta_{t-1}+\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t-1,1}}
             -\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t-1,2}}
 \quad\text{when }X_t=1,
\]
and is four times the negative of \(\Delta_{t-1}\) plus four times the same
square-root difference when \(X_t=-4\).  Equation (3) therefore implies
that, on \(\mathcal L_+\), the greedy arm is eventually arm (1) at context
\(1\) and arm (2) at context \(-4\).  The clipped probabilities are then
\(0.99\) and \(0.01\).  Centering each of the four context--action count
increments and applying the preceding bounded martingale strong law gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}D_{t,a}/t
 &\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_s\\[2mm]\hline
 a=1&23/40&19/40\\
 a=2&317/40&-79/40.
 \end{array}
 \tag{5}
\]
Combining (1), (4), and (5) proves
\[
 \left(\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},
       \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\right)^\top
 \longrightarrow
 \bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_+
 :=\left(\frac{19}{46},-\frac{79}{3804}\right)^\top
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal L_+.
 \tag{6}
\]
The identical calculation with the two greedy arms reversed gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}D_{t,a}/t
 &\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_s\\[2mm]\hline
 a=1&317/40&-79/40\\
 a=2&23/40&19/40,
 \end{array}
\]
and hence
\[
 \left(\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},
       \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\right)^\top
 \longrightarrow
 \bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_-
 :=\left(-\frac{79}{634},\frac{19}{276}\right)^\top
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal L_-.
 \tag{7}
\]
The two limiting margins are strictly separated:
\[
 (\bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_+)_1-(\bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_+)_2
 =\frac{37955}{87492}>0,
 \qquad
 (\bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_-)_1-(\bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_-)_2
 =-\frac{16925}{87492}<0.
 \tag{8}
\]
In particular, if the slope projection has an almost-sure limit with unequal
coordinates, (3) fixes the greedy decisions eventually and (6)--(7) show
that the limit must be exactly one of these two vectors.  Moreover, on either
\(\mathcal L_+\) or \(\mathcal L_-\), ordinary Cesaro convergence gives
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
 \left\|
 (\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t-1,1},
  \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t-1,2})^\top
 -\bar\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{\pm}
 \right\|_2\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{9}
\]

Both separated regimes are reachable with positive probability from every
allowed initialization.  Here is a direct lock-in argument.  For an even
integer \(n\), prescribe first \(n\) observations with \(X=1,A=1\), and then
\(n\) observations with \(X=-4,A=2\), taking equally many positive and
negative Rademacher signs for each selected arm.  Every such finite history
has probability at least \((0.5\cdot0.01\cdot0.5)^{2n}>0\), uniformly over
\(\eta_0\in[-1,1]^2\).  At its end,
\[
 \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{1}=\frac{\eta_{0,1}+n/2}{1+n}
 \longrightarrow\frac12,
 \qquad
 \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{2}=\frac{\eta_{0,2}-n/3}{1+16n}
 \longrightarrow-\frac1{48}
 \tag{10}
\]
uniformly over the initial square, and both inverse Gram coordinates are
arbitrarily small for large \(n\).  If the future greedy decisions are fixed
as in \(\mathcal L_+\), the deterministic mean paths of the two ratios are
weighted averages of the terminal ratios in (10) and the limits in (6), so
they remain a fixed positive distance from the switching surface.

To see that the fixed-policy coupling has positive probability of remaining
there, let \(M_k\) be any centered future partial sum entering a Gram or
reward-moment coordinate.  Its increments have a fixed finite bound, so
\(E[M_k^2]\leq Ck\).  Stopping at the first crossing of a level \(x\) gives
\(x^2P\{\max_{j\leq k}|M_j|\geq x\}\leq E[M_k^2]\).  Applying this inequality
on the dyadic blocks \([2^rn,2^{r+1}n]\) with levels proportional to
\(2^rn\), then summing over \(r\geq0\), bounds the probability of any future
relative deviation larger than a fixed \(\epsilon\) by \(C'/(\epsilon^2n)\).
Choose \(n\) so this is below one half and \(\epsilon\) so the margin in
(8) is preserved.  On the resulting event the coupling never stops, proving
\(P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_+)>0\).  Prescribing instead \(X=-4,A=1\) and then
\(X=1,A=2\) gives terminal slopes converging uniformly to
\((-1/8,1/12)^\top\); the same argument proves
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}
 P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_+)>0,
 \qquad
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}
 P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_-)>0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus, if switching-surface convergence is excluded, the finite family is
necessarily \(m_{\mathrm{GX}}=2\), with limits (6)--(7), and both
positive-probability basins equal the whole initial-slope square:
\(B^{\mathrm{GX}}_+=B^{\mathrm{GX}}_-=mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}\).

The remaining alternative is explicit rather than hidden.  Boundedness of
the outcomes, (3), and the one-step update in
\(\mathrm{def{:}guoxu\text{-}dynamics\text{-}handle}\) imply
\(|\Delta_t-\Delta_{t-1}|=O(t^{-1})\).  Hence every path outside
\(\mathcal L_+\cup\mathcal L_-\) satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}|\Delta_t|=0.
 \tag{12}
\]
There is also a computable necessary condition for a common-coordinate
limit.  Define the empirical conditional arm-(1) rates
\[
 u_T=\frac2T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=1,A_t=1\},
 \qquad
 v_T=\frac2T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=-4,A_t=1\}.
\]
Every subsequential limit \((u,v)\) lies in \([0.01,0.99]^2\).  If along the
same subsequence both slopes converge to a common value, (1) and (4) force
\[
 \frac12\frac{u-4v}{u+16v}
 =\frac1{12}\frac{-3-u+4v}{17-u-16v},
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad
 u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0.
 \tag{13}
\]
If occupation in the shrinking switching layer is asymptotically only a
mixture of the two opposite-context greedy regimes, then \(v=1-u\), and the
unique admissible solution of (13) is
\[
 u=\frac{47+\sqrt{4609}}{150},
 \qquad
 b=\frac{\sqrt{4609}-91}{1224}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Equations (12)--(14) isolate the unresolved separatrix: a complete answer
must prove that it has probability zero, or else characterize the additional
limit set supported there.  The same argument must be uniform in the initial
state before the requested common sequences \(r_T(K_0)\) and
\(\delta_T(K_0)\) can be asserted.
