The full-data vectors are i.i.d. because the two Rademacher potential outcomes are independent across occasions and arms and the context is constant. At each occasion the displayed policy is a function of the past-measurable state, and the action is drawn from that logged policy independently of current potential outcomes, so sequential randomization holds.

At states zero and one the arm-probability vectors are
\[
 (2/5,1/4,7/20),\qquad(2/5,1/6,13/30). \tag{1}
\]
Linear interpolation preserves their coordinatewise minima, so every arm probability is at least \(1/6>\varepsilon\). The coordinate slopes are \(0,-1/12,1/12\), proving the policy-Lipschitz condition with \(L_\Pi=1/12\). For \(\theta\in[-1,1]^2\),
\[
 \sup|Y_t(1)-\theta_1|\le1+\sqrt{2/5}<2,
 \qquad \sup|Y_t(2)-\theta_2|\le1+\sqrt{1/2}<2. \tag{2}
\]
Each score has only one nonzero coordinate, so \(B=2\) is valid. The derivative matrices are \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,0)\), \(\operatorname{diag}(0,-1)\), and zero; their norms are at most one and all second derivatives vanish. Thus \(D_1=D_2=1\) are valid envelopes. Direct expectation gives
\[
 M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)=(-\theta_1,-\theta_2)^\top=-\theta,
 \qquad H=-I_2. \tag{3}
\]
Therefore \(\theta^*=0\) is the unique root, \(\|M_{P^\dagger}(\theta)\|_2=\|\theta\|_2\), \(\kappa(u)=u\), \(\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial[-1,1]^2)=1\), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)=1\). Also \(0<\rho=\varepsilon/8<\min\{1,\varepsilon/(4D_2)\}\).

At the first occasion the state is \(1/2\), and the arm-one probability is \(2/5\). Hence
\[
 P^\dagger(\eta_\infty=0)=P^\dagger(A_1=1)=2/5,
 \qquad P^\dagger(\eta_\infty=1)=3/5. \tag{4}
\]
The representative is \(\mathcal F_1\)-measurable and hence terminally measurable. For every \(t\ge1\), \(\eta_t=\eta_\infty\), while \(|\eta_0-\eta_\infty|=1/2\). Therefore
\[
 T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T|\eta_{t-1}-\eta_\infty|=\frac1{2T} \tag{5}
\]
almost surely, so the attraction and vanishing-modulus conditions hold with the displayed sequences.

At \(\theta^*=0\), only arms one and two contribute to \(\Gamma_{P^\dagger}\). Since \(E[Y_t(1)^2]=2/5\) and \(E[Y_t(2)^2]=1/2\),
\[
 \Gamma_{P^\dagger}(0)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/4}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,2), \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 \Gamma_{P^\dagger}(1)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/6}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,3). \tag{7}
\]
Both dominate \(I_2\), so \(\omega_-=1\) is valid. Since \(H=-I_2\), the covariance definition gives the asserted two values of \(V_\infty\). Equations (1)--(7) verify every class primitive with constants independent of \(T\).

Finally, for \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(c=(1,0)^\top\),
\[
 c^\top V_\infty c=c^\top\Sigma c=1\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad P^\dagger\{V_\infty=\operatorname{diag}(1,3)\}=3/5. \tag{8}
\]
The contrastwise theorem and (8) give every-level validity for this contrast, while the matrixwise theorem rules out all-contrast validity with \(\Sigma\). This is a finite two-atom special case of the general covariance-law results, not a claim about the Guo--Xu recursion.
