Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\), \(T\ge2\), and the law \(P^{\dagger}\) of \(\mathrm{def:directional\text{-}matrix\text{-}witness}\). We first verify, one condition at a time, membership in the class of \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}attractor\text{-}class}\).

The context is constant, and the two nonzero potential outcomes are independent across occasions and arms with the displayed time-invariant laws. Consequently the full-data vectors \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) are independent and identically distributed, as required by \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}full\text{-}data}\). At occasion \(t\), the state \(\eta_{t-1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable and the action is drawn from \(\Pi(\cdot\mid X_t,\eta_{t-1})\) independently of the current potential outcomes. Thus \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\text{-}randomization}\) holds and the logged probability is the probability specified in the construction.

Writing \(u\in[0,1]\) for the state, linear interpolation gives
\[
 \Pi(1\mid x,u)=\frac25,\qquad
 \Pi(2\mid x,u)=\frac14-\frac{u}{12},\qquad
 \Pi(3\mid x,u)=\frac7{20}+\frac{u}{12}.
 \tag{1}
\]
These quantities sum to one. Their coordinatewise minimum over \(u\in[0,1]\) is at least \(1/6\), so (because \(\varepsilon<1/6\)) \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}overlap}\) holds with the prescribed \(\varepsilon\). Equation (1) also yields, for all \(u,u'\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \sup_{a\in\{1,2,3\}}\left|\Pi(a\mid x,u)-\Pi(a\mid x,u')\right|
 \le \frac1{12}|u-u'|.
 \tag{2}
\]
Hence \(\mathrm{ass:policy\text{-}lipschitz}\) holds with \(L_\Pi=1/12\).

For every \(\theta\in[-1,1]^2\),
\[
 |Y_t(1)-\theta_1|\le1+\sqrt{2/5}<2,
 \qquad
 |Y_t(2)-\theta_2|\le1+\sqrt{1/2}<2.
 \tag{3}
\]
Each nonzero score has only one nonzero coordinate, while the third-arm score is zero. Thus (3) proves \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}score}\) with the common envelope \(B=2\). The score is affine in \(\theta\) on all of \(\mathbb R^2\), with derivative matrices
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0.
\]
Their operator norms are at most one and every second derivative is zero. Therefore \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\text{-}z\text{-}map}\) holds with \(D_1=D_2=1\); the sharp curvature bound is zero, and hence is in particular bounded by the chosen positive envelope \(D_2=1\).

Both Rademacher outcomes have mean zero. By the definition of \(M_{P^{\dagger}}\), summing the three arm-specific mean scores therefore gives
\[
 M_{P^{\dagger}}(\theta)=(-\theta_1,-\theta_2)^\top=-\theta,
 \qquad H=\partial_\theta M_{P^{\dagger}}(0)=-I_2.
 \tag{4}
\]
It follows that \(\theta^*=0\) is the unique root, \(\|M_{P^{\dagger}}(\theta)\|_2=\|\theta\|_2\), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)=1\). Thus \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}root\text{-}separation}\) holds with \(\kappa(u)=u\) (with the usual value \(+\infty\) for the infimum over an empty set), and \(\mathrm{ass:jacobian\text{-}nonsingular}\) holds with \(h_-=1\). Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(0,\partial[-1,1]^2)=1,
 \tag{5}
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:root\text{-}interior}\) holds with \(s_\Theta=1\).

For completeness, the empirical moment has an exact zero for every realized assignment and outcome array. For \(a\in\{1,2\}\), put
\[
 W_a=\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}}{\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}.
\]
All denominators are positive by (1). If \(W_a>0\), define
\[
 \theta_a^{(0)}=
 \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^T
 \mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}Y_t(a)/\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}{W_a};
 \tag{6}
\]
if \(W_a=0\), set \(\theta_a^{(0)}=0\). In the first case (6) is a convex combination of values in \([-1,1]\), and in the second case the corresponding empirical coordinate is identically zero. Hence \(\theta^{(0)}=(\theta_1^{(0)},\theta_2^{(0)})^\top\in\Theta\), and direct substitution in the definition of \(G_T\) gives \(G_T(\theta^{(0)})=0\). Since a norm is nonnegative,
\[
 \min_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)\|_2=0.
 \tag{7}
\]

At the first occasion the state is \(\eta_0=1/2\). By (1), the probability of arm one at that state is \(2/5\). Since \(J=1\) exactly when \(A_1=1\),
\[
 P^{\dagger}(J=1)=\frac25,
 \qquad
 P^{\dagger}(J=2)=\frac35,
 \qquad \mathcal J=\{1,2\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The random variable \(J\), and hence \(\eta_\infty=\bar\eta_J\), is \(\mathcal F_1\)-measurable and therefore measurable with respect to \(\sigma(\bigcup_{t\ge1}\mathcal F_t)\). This verifies \(\mathrm{ass:finite\text{-}tail\text{-}selection}\). The construction sets \(\eta_s=\bar\eta_J\) for every \(s\ge1\), whereas \(|\eta_0-\bar\eta_J|=1/2\) for either value of \(J\). Consequently
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T|\eta_{t-1}-\eta_\infty|=\frac1{2T}
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}attraction\text{-}modulus}\) holds with \(r_T=1/(2T)\) and \(\delta_T=0\), and
\[
 e_T=T^{-1/2}+\frac1{2T}\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{10}
\]
which verifies \(\mathrm{ass:vanishing\text{-}modulus}\). The proposed radius also obeys the required strict bound because
\[
 0<\rho=\frac{\varepsilon}{8}
 <\min\left\{1,\frac{\varepsilon h_-}{4D_2}\right\}
 =\min\left\{1,\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right\}.
 \tag{11}
\]

It remains to verify terminal covariance nondegeneracy and compute the promised separation. At \(\theta^*=0\), the arm-specific score outer products are diagonal, the third-arm contribution vanishes, and
\[
 E_{P^{\dagger}}[Y_t(1)^2]=\frac25,
 \qquad
 E_{P^{\dagger}}[Y_t(2)^2]=\frac12.
 \tag{12}
\]
Using (1), (12), and \(\mathrm{def:basin\text{-}covariance}\), evaluation at \(\bar\eta_1=0\) gives
\[
 \Omega_1
 =\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/4}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
 \tag{13}
\]
whereas evaluation at \(\bar\eta_2=1\) gives
\[
 \Omega_2
 =\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2/5}{2/5},\frac{1/2}{1/6}\right)
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,3).
 \tag{14}
\]
By (8), \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) equals one of (13)--(14) almost surely, and both matrices dominate \(I_2\). Hence \(\mathrm{ass:basin\text{-}nondegeneracy}\) holds with \(\omega_-=1\). Equations (1)--(14) verify every member property in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}attractor\text{-}class}\), with constants and moduli common across horizons. Therefore \(P^{\dagger}\in\mathfrak P_T\) for every \(T\ge2\).

Finally, (4) and the sandwich definition imply
\[
 V_1=H^{-1}\Omega_1H^{-\top}=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),
 \qquad
 V_2=H^{-1}\Omega_2H^{-\top}=\operatorname{diag}(1,3).
 \tag{15}
\]
For \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(c=(1,0)^\top\),
\[
 c^\top V_1c=c^\top V_2c=c^\top\Sigma c=1,
 \qquad V_1\ne V_2.
 \tag{16}
\]
Along any horizon sequence the law of \(V_\infty=V_J\) is the fixed law
\[
 \mu=\frac25\delta_{V_1}+\frac35\delta_{V_2}.
 \tag{17}
\]
The projected equality in (16), together with \(\mathrm{thm:contrastwise\text{-}deterministic\text{-}sandwich\text{-}iff}\), proves asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\) for this \(c\), this \(\Sigma\), and every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). On the other hand, (8), (15), and (17) show that \(V\) is not almost surely constant under \(\mu\). By \(\mathrm{thm:matrixwise\text{-}deterministic\text{-}sandwich\text{-}iff}\), no single deterministic sandwich is valid at every nominal level for every nonzero contrast. This proves the claimed contrastwise--matrixwise separation.
