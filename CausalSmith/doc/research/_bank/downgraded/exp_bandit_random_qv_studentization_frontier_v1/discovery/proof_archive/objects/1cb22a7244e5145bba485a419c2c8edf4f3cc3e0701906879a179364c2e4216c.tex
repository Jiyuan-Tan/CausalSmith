Fix an even horizon \(T=2n\).  Retain the action process, its predictable arm-one probabilities \(p_t\in\{1/2,1/4\}\), and the numerical score process \((\xi_t)_{t=1}^T\) constructed in Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\).  Pass to the product extension carrying independent Rademacher variables \(U_1,\ldots,U_T\), independent of every action randomizer, so that
\[
 P(U_t=1)=P(U_t=-1)=\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
This extension does not change the joint law of \((p_t,A_t,\xi_t)_{t=1}^T\), and hence does not change any score or quadratic-variation conclusion of the preceding proposition.

In Saco's binary notation define
\[
 A_t^{\mathrm S}:=\mathbf1\{A_t=1\},
 \qquad
 \pi_t^{\mathrm S}:=p_t,
 \qquad
 X_t^{\mathrm S}:=(X_t,U_t),
\tag{2}
\]
and define the two potential outcomes by
\[
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1):=\frac32+U_t,
 \qquad
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0):=\frac12+U_t.
\tag{3}
\]
We verify in turn every source hypothesis recorded in Lemma~\(\text{lem:saco-pointwise-rqv-pivot}\).

First, (3) depends only on the current occasion's potential treatment and not on assignments at any other occasion.  Defining the observed response by consistency gives
\[
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}
 =A_t^{\mathrm S}Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)
  +(1-A_t^{\mathrm S})Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)
 =\frac12+U_t+A_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus maintained Assumption~3.5, including consistency and no interference, holds.  Equations (1)--(3), together with the deterministic base full-data coordinates in the preceding construction, show that
\[
 (X_t^{\mathrm S},Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))
 =\left((X_t,U_t),\frac12+U_t,\frac32+U_t\right)
\tag{5}
\]
are independent and identically distributed across \(t\), which verifies Assumption~3.6.  The causal target is distinct from the observed-law score functional and is identified directly from the two potential outcomes:
\[
 \theta_0
 :=E\!\left[Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)-Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)\right]
 =E[1]=1.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, conditional on \(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\), the variables \(U_t\) and \(A_t^{\mathrm S}\) are independent, with respective variances \(1\) and \(p_t(1-p_t)\).  Equation (4) therefore gives the required nondegenerate observable-outcome law,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Y_t^{\mathrm S}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S})
 =1+p_t(1-p_t)\ge1.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm S}\) be the history enlarged by \(U_1,\ldots,U_t\), and let the pre-randomization sigma-field be
\[
 \mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}
 :=\sigma(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S},X_t^{\mathrm S},p_t).
\tag{8}
\]
The rule from the preceding proposition makes \(p_t\) measurable with respect to the past action-score history and hence with respect to \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\).  Independence in (1) and use of the same randomization device yield
\[
 P(A_t^{\mathrm S}=1\mid\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S})
 =p_t=\pi_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is Assumption~3.2.  Conditional on \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\), both potential outcomes in (3) are already measurable, while the fresh action is drawn using (9).  Therefore \(A_t^{\mathrm S}\) is conditionally independent of \((Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))\) given \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\), which is Assumption~3.7.  Since the only probabilities used by the construction are \(1/2\) and \(1/4\),
\[
 \frac14\le p_t\le\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac12\le1-p_t\le\frac34.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus Assumption~3.8 holds with overlap constant \(1/4\).

For Assumption~4.8 take the deterministic scored set
\[
 \mathcal T_T:=\{1,\ldots,T\},
 \qquad
 n_{\mathrm{eff}}:=|\mathcal T_T|=T\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{11}
\]
Write a context value as \((x,u)\), where \(u\in\{-1,1\}\), and choose the fixed nuisance maps
\[
 \widehat m_{t-1,1}(x,u):=u+\frac12,
 \qquad
 \widehat m_{t-1,0}(x,u):=u-\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]
As a nonrandom function, (12) is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\)-measurable for every \(t\), proving Assumption~4.5.  The outcome and nuisance fourth-moment conditions, Assumptions~3.9 and 3.10, follow from the exact calculations
\[
 \max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E|Y_t^{\mathrm S}(a)|^4
 =\frac{313}{16}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E\!\left|\widehat m_{t-1,a}(X_t^{\mathrm S})\right|^4
 =\frac{41}{16}
\tag{13}
\]
uniformly in \(t\) and \(T\).  Indeed, the first maximum is attained by \(Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)\in\{1/2,5/2\}\), and the two nuisance maps have values \(\{-1/2,3/2\}\) and \(\{-3/2,1/2\}\), respectively.

It remains to show that this nondegenerate augmentation preserves the load-bearing score exactly.  Substitute (3), (4), and (12) into Saco's AIPW pseudo-outcome.  On every realization,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\phi_t
 &=\frac{A_t^{\mathrm S}}{p_t}
      \{Y_t^{\mathrm S}-(U_t+1/2)\}
   -\frac{1-A_t^{\mathrm S}}{1-p_t}
      \{Y_t^{\mathrm S}-(U_t-1/2)\}
   +(U_t+1/2)-(U_t-1/2)\\
 &=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
   -\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}
   +1
 =\xi_t+1.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Because \(\theta_0=1\) by (6), the centered AIPW increment \(\widehat\phi_t-\theta_0\) is exactly \(\xi_t\), not merely asymptotically equivalent to it.  Conditional on the enlarged past, (9) and (14) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(\xi_t=\frac1{p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\right)&=p_t,\\
 P\!\left(\xi_t=-\frac1{1-p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\right)&=1-p_t,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]&=1-1=0,\\
 E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]
 &=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}
 \in\left\{4,\frac{16}{3}\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so \(E|\xi_t|^4\le256\).

By the centered identity following (14) and the definitions in the cited source,
\[
 S_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t=S_T,
 \qquad
 Q_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t^2=[S]_T,
 \qquad
 (V_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^TE[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]
 =\langle S\rangle_T.
\tag{17}
\]
Equation (16) gives the pathwise information bound
\[
 (V_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2
 =\langle S\rangle_T
 \ge4T=4n_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence Assumption~5.11 holds with \(v_-=4\) and failure probability zero.  This verifies the source theorem's variance-growth premise without adding deterministic variance stabilization.  For completeness, the quadratic-variation consistency already proved in Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\), together with (18), also gives
\[
 \left|\frac{[S]_T}{\langle S\rangle_T}-1\right|
 \le\frac{|[S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T|}{4T}
 \longrightarrow_P0.
\tag{19}
\]
The bounded fourth moments are likewise negligible relative to the squared information:
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t=1}^TE|\xi_t|^4}{\langle S\rangle_T^2}
 \le\frac{256T}{16T^2}
 =\frac{16}{T}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]

All assumptions enumerated in Lemma~\(\text{lem:saco-pointwise-rqv-pivot}\) have now been verified for a law whose observed outcome has conditional variance at least one and whose causal effect equals one.  Nevertheless, since the augmentation left the complete centered-score process unchanged, Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\) applies verbatim and yields, along \(T=2n\),
\[
 \frac{S_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}{\sqrt{Q_{mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}}
 =\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{[S]_T}}
 \Longrightarrow
 L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
 +\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8},
\tag{21}
\]
where \(Z,W\) are independent standard normal variables and
\[
 E[L]=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{22}
\]
The variable \(L\) is integrable because it is bounded in absolute value by a fixed linear combination of \(|Z|\) and \(|W|\).  Since every standard normal variable has mean zero, (22) proves that \(L\) is not \(N(0,1)\).  Thus (21) contradicts the score-normality conclusion (5.5) asserted by Saco's Theorem~5.14.

We finally verify the failure of the feasible Studentized conclusion directly.  For this scored set, the source's sample mean and sample variance satisfy the exact identities
\[
 \widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0=\frac{S_T}{T},
 \qquad
 (T-1)\widehat V^{\mathrm S}
 =Q_T-\frac{S_T^2}{T}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equation (21) implies \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}=O_P(1)\).  Therefore
\[
 R_T^{\mathrm S}
 :=\frac{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}{Q_T}
 =\frac{T}{T-1}
 \left\{1-\frac1T
 \left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right)^2\right\}
 \longrightarrow_P1.
\tag{24}
\]
On the event \(R_T^{\mathrm S}>0\), which has probability tending to one by (24), (23) gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt T(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0)}
      {\sqrt{\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}}
 =\frac{S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}}{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}.
\tag{25}
\]
The difference between the right side of (25) and \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}\) converges to zero in probability: for arbitrary \(M>0\) and \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &P\!\left(
 \left|\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right|
 \left|\frac1{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}-1\right|>\epsilon
 \right)\\
 &\qquad\le
 P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right|>M\right)
 +P\!\left(\left|\frac1{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}-1\right|>\frac\epsilon M\right),
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
where the first probability can be made uniformly small for large \(T\) by tightness from (21), and the second tends to zero by (24).  To pass from (26) to the distributional limit without invoking an additional theorem, let \(F_L\) denote the distribution function of \(L\).  It is continuous because, conditional on each value of \(Z\) away from the null event \(\{Z=0\}\), each branch in (21) contains a nonzero Gaussian multiple of \(W\).  For every \(z\in\mathbb R\) and \(\epsilon>0\), the elementary event inclusions give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\le z-\epsilon\right)
 -P(|D_T|>\epsilon)
 &\le P\!\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}+D_T\le z\right)\\
 &\le P\!\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\le z+\epsilon\right)
 +P(|D_T|>\epsilon),
 \end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
where \(D_T\) is the difference in (26).  Taking limits in (27), using (21) and (26), and then sending \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves from continuity of \(F_L\) that
\[
 \frac{\sqrt T(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0)}
      {\sqrt{\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}}
 \Longrightarrow L.
\tag{28}
\]
By (22), this is not the standard normal limit asserted in conclusion (5.6).

As the required smallest-instance check, take \(n=1\), so \(T=2\).  The preceding proposition enumerates all four action paths and obtains
\[
 P\!\left\{\left|S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}\right|\le1\right\}=\frac58
 <2\Phi(1)-1.
\tag{29}
\]
The present augmentation adds the four equally likely values of \((U_1,U_2)\) independently of those action paths.  The centered identity following (14) holds for each of the resulting sixteen combinations, so the score-pivot enumeration and (29) are unchanged, while (6)--(7) certify a nonzero causal effect and positive conditional observed-outcome variance.  This completes the explicit nondegenerate within-class counterexample to both normal-limit conclusions.
