Put \(b_n^2=N_n\lambda_n\).  For \(t>h_n\), define
\[
 Y_{n,t}=X_{n,t}/b_n,\qquad
 v_{n,t}=E[Y_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}],\qquad
 V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,t}.
\tag{1}
\]
The division is legal because \(\lambda_n\ge\nu_n>0\).  Since \(b_n\) is
\(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable, it is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable for
every \(t>h_n\).  Hence \((Y_{n,t},\mathcal G_{n,t})_{t>h_n}\) is a
martingale-difference array.  The increment bound and the first and third
hypotheses give
\[
 \max_{t>h_n}|Y_{n,t}|\le
 \epsilon_n:=\frac{L}{\sqrt{N_n\nu_n}}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 V_n=\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{2}
\]

We first prove the Gaussian limit for the suffix.  Retain the increment at
time \(t>h_n\) only when
\(\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{t-1}v_{n,s}\le2\).  This indicator is
\(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable.  Denote the retained increment and its
conditional variance by \(\widetilde Y_{n,t}\) and \(\widetilde v_{n,t}\).
Then
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde v_{n,t}\le2+\epsilon_n^2.
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\), Taylor's formula, the martingale-difference
property, and \(|\widetilde Y_{n,t}|\le\epsilon_n\) imply
\[
 E[e^{iu\widetilde Y_{n,t}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 =1-\frac{u^2}{2}\widetilde v_{n,t}+r_{n,t},
 \qquad
 |r_{n,t}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,t}.
\tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
 Z_{n,j}=\exp\!\left\{iu\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde Y_{n,t}
 +\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde v_{n,t}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Using (4) and
\(|e^x-1-x|\le x^2e^x/2\) for \(x\ge0\), conditional expectation gives
\[
 \left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
 \le C_u e^{u^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)/2}
 \left(\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,j}+\widetilde v_{n,j}^2\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(\widetilde v_{n,j}\le\epsilon_n^2\), summing (6) and using (3)
shows that \(E[Z_{n,N_n}]\to1\).  By (2), the stopping changes the suffix
only on \(\{V_n>2\}\), whose probability tends to zero.  Moreover the stopped
total variance
\(\widetilde V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde v_{n,t}\) converges in
probability to one.  Since \(0\le\widetilde V_n\le2+\epsilon_n^2\), boundedness
and an \(\epsilon\)-split around one yield
\[
 E\left|1-\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The identity
\[
 e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}
 =e^{iu\sum_t\widetilde Y_{n,t}}
 e^{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2}
\]
together with (6)--(7), followed by removal of \(\{V_n>2\}\), shows that
the suffix characteristic function tends to \(e^{-u^2/2}\) for every
\(u\).  The characteristic-function continuity criterion therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}X_{n,t}/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{8}
\]

The prefix is negligible on this same scale.  Martingale orthogonality and
boundedness give
\[
 E\left(\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\right)^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}E[X_{n,t}^2]\le h_nL^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(b_n^2\ge N_n\nu_n\), Chebyshev's inequality and the second hypothesis
give
\[
 b_n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (8)--(10) prove \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

For realized quadratic variation, put
\(D_{n,t}=Y_{n,t}^2-v_{n,t}\) for \(t>h_n\).  These are martingale
differences and
\[
 E[D_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le E[Y_{n,t}^4\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le\epsilon_n^2v_{n,t}.
\tag{11}
\]
Under the same predictable stopping rule, martingale orthogonality bounds the
second moment of the stopped sum of the \(D_{n,t}\)'s by
\(\epsilon_n^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)\).  Removing \(\{V_n>2\}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{12}
\]
The prefix squares obey the deterministic bound
\[
 0\le b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2
 \le\frac{h_nL^2}{N_n\nu_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently, by (1)--(2) and (11)--(13),
\[
 \frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
 =b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2+V_n
 +\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}
 \longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{14}
\]
In particular \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\).  On
\(\{Q_n/b_n^2\in[1/2,3/2]\}\), division is legal, and (8)--(10), (14), and
continuity of \(x\mapsto x^{-1/2}\) on this interval yield
\[
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}
 =\frac{S_n/b_n}{\sqrt{Q_n/b_n^2}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{15}
\]
Every bound above depends only on the common \(L\) and the three displayed
premise errors.  Applying the argument to any contrary sequence of member
arrays proves the asserted uniform versions of all conclusions.
