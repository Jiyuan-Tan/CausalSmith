The project establishes a field-tier inference frontier conditional on either of two explicit early-information classes.  The finite-attractor and projected IPW-Z theorems concern bounded generic causal scores.  They do not by themselves repair Saco's published AIPW/DML theorem.  The direct Saco repair instead retains Assumptions~3.5, 3.6, 3.2, 3.7, 3.8, 4.5, 4.8, 3.9, 3.10, and 5.11 and adds uniform overlap/fourth-moment envelopes plus early-measurable suffix-QV stabilization; it permits unbounded scores with a common fourth moment.  The existing two-regime law proves that this added temporal condition is substantive, because no \(k_n=o(n_{\mathrm{eff}})\) early scale can predict its selected suffix variance.  This repairs the valid theorem on a strict intersection class; it does not validate the variance-growth-only source claim.

The project treats a growing-horizon superpopulation adaptive experiment with a known randomization rule, not a pure fixed-finite-population randomization theorem.  HeartSteps is a causal-excursion use case, not evidence that a deployed policy satisfies either early-information class.  The bounded directional-matrix construction is only an elementary logical-separation witness.  Neither it nor the bounded two-regime Saco counterexample proves that the published Gaussian no-intercept Guo--Xu recursion belongs to the finite-attractor class.  The open-ended Guo--Xu question concerns the slope projection of an augmented Markov chain and makes no inference claim.  The Gaussian moment extension remains optional and open.
