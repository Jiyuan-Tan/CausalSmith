Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). We first identify the martingale and its predictable covariance from the primitive design conditions. By independent full-data arrivals and sequential randomization,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
&=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
=M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equality uses \(E[\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}/\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1},W_t]=1\), which is legitimate because uniform overlap gives a denominator at least \(\varepsilon>0\). Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. Define, only for this proof,
\[
 \Gamma_P(\eta)=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{g_{t,a}(\theta^*)g_{t,a}(\theta^*)^\top}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right].
\]
The same conditioning calculation and the i.i.d. arrival assumption give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_j=\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_j).
\]
The bounded-score and overlap conditions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Omega_ju
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_a\frac{(u^\top g_{t,a}(\theta^*))^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\bar\eta_j)}\right]
 \le \frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon},
\]
so \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\). Differentiation under the finite sum and bounded expectation is justified by the common derivative envelope, and therefore
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_a\|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1.
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|=|p-q|/(pq)\le |p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). Hence the policy Lipschitz condition yields
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma:=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Also \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On the attraction event
\[
 \mathcal A_T:=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
the preceding Lipschitz inequality gives
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the same norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), while the uniform attraction modulus gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Consequently
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left[\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T).
\]
This proves the asserted predictable-QV bound directly from the design and attraction primitives.

We next localize the empirical root. For fixed \(\theta\), \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) is an average of mean-zero martingale differences bounded in norm by a common constant. Orthogonality of martingale differences therefore gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T.
\]
Both maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\): the empirical modulus is at most \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and the population modulus at most \(KD_1\). A finite mesh of the fixed compact set \(\Theta\), followed by the displayed second-moment bound and the union bound, gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(x>0).
\]
Because \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator satisfies the stated approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le 2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
     +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly. For each \(u>0\), uniform root separation now implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}\to0.
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\), so martingale orthogonality and \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\) show \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. Applying the identical calculation entrywise to the derivative score, whose norm is bounded by \(D_1/\varepsilon\), gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball, the curvature assumption and overlap imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon.
\]
Since \(D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/4\), with probability tending uniformly to one the right-hand side is smaller than \(h_-/2\). On that event define
\[
 \Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta).
\]
The Jacobian lower bound gives \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), so the last display makes \(\Psi_T\) a contraction with constant at most \(1/2\) on the closed \(\rho\)-ball. Moreover,
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le \tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2.
\]
The final term is at most \(\rho/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Thus \(\Psi_T\) maps the closed ball into itself and the contraction argument supplies exactly one fixed point \(\theta_T^0\), equivalently exactly one root of \(G_T\), in that ball. It also gives
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le 2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}).
\]
Uniform consistency of \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and this rate imply, with probability tending uniformly to one, that both points are within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\). Hence \(\|\theta_T^0-\widetilde\theta_T\|_2\le\rho/2\), so \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\). Conversely every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is then inside the closed \(\rho\)-ball and must equal \(\theta_T^0\). It follows that \(P(E_T^c)\to0\) uniformly and that the selected estimator equals this exact root except on a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the event just constructed, Taylor's formula gives
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)
   =T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad
 \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2.
\]
Since \(\Delta_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) and \(R_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), multiplication by \(\sqrt T H^{-1}\) yields
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\]
The complement of the root event is uniformly negligible, so it does not alter this uniform \(O_P\) statement.

It remains to establish the honest, label-free mixture approximation. Let
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,
 \qquad
 \Lambda_T=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).
\]
For large \(T\), \(k_T/T\to0\), while \(r_TT/k_T\le r_T/\beta_T\le\sqrt{r_T}\). The matrix \(\Lambda_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable. Repeating the attraction-event argument on the first \(k_T\) terms gives
\[
 E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1)
\]
uniformly. The prefix predictable variation has norm at most \(M_\Gamma k_T\); hence
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]-\Lambda_T\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1).
\]

Fix \(\|c\|_2=1\) and put \(a=H^{-\top}c\). The previously proved Jacobian upper bound and the assumed lower singular-value bound imply
\[
 \frac1{KD_1}\le\|a\|_2\le\frac1{h_-}.
\]
Thus \(|a^\top\xi_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), and basin nondegeneracy gives
\[
 a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0.
\]
Condition on \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\) and apply the cited bounded-martingale central-limit bound to the tail differences \(-a^\top\xi_t\), \(t>k_T\), with \(p=1\). The preceding \(L^1\) predictable-variation approximation implies, by conditional Jensen, that both the conditional total variance divided by \(T\) and the normalized conditional predictable variation approach \(a^\top\Lambda_Ta\); the last two displays keep these variances uniformly away from zero outside an event of probability \(o(1)\). The cited bound is therefore, after integration over \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\),
\[
 \sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\left\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\xi_t\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{a^\top\Lambda_Ta}}\right)\right|\to0
\]
uniformly in \(P\). Explicitly, the first term in the cited bound is \(O(\log T/\sqrt T)\), and its predictable-variation term tends to zero because the displayed matrix error tends to zero in \(L^1\). Martingale orthogonality also gives
\[
 E_P\left[\left|a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\xi_t\right|^2\right]
 \le Ck_T/T=o(1),
\]
so the omitted prefix is negligible. Since both \(a^\top\Lambda_Ta\) and \(a^\top\Omega_Ja\) are bounded away from zero with probability tending to one, the normal scale map is uniformly continuous there; the \(L^1\) convergence of \(\Lambda_T\) to \(\Omega_J\) permits replacement of the former by the latter. Finally,
\[
 E_P\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_Ja}}\right)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}p_j\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{c^\top V_jc}}\right),
\]
because \(a^\top\Omega_ja=c^\top H^{-1}\Omega_jH^{-\top}c=c^\top V_jc\). Combining this identity with the exact-root linearization proves the asserted uniform mixture approximation.

For the last assertion, let \(q_j=c^\top V_jc>0\). A centered finite Gaussian mixture with weights \(p_j\) has second moment \(\sum_jp_jq_j\) and fourth moment \(3\sum_jp_jq_j^2\). If it were a single centered Gaussian, its fourth moment would be \(3(\sum_jp_jq_j)^2\). Their difference is
\[
 3\left\{\sum_jp_jq_j^2-\left(\sum_jp_jq_j\right)^2\right\}
 =3\sum_jp_j(q_j-\bar q)^2,
 \qquad \bar q=\sum_jp_jq_j,
\]
which is strictly positive when two positive-mass basins have different \(q_j\).

The deletion of stability relative to the full label \(\sigma(J)\) is necessary. Indeed, take \(d=1\), two actions with the constant policy \((1/2,1/2)\), i.i.d. Rademacher variables \(R_t\), \(Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=R_t\), and \(g(a,x,y;\theta)=(y-\theta)/2\) on \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Let both candidate attractors equal zero, put \(\eta_t=0\), and, for odd \(T\), set \(J=1\) exactly when \(\sum_{t=1}^TR_t>0\). All attraction errors are zero and \(\Omega_1=\Omega_2=1\), but \(J\) is the sign of the normalized score. With \(h(1)=1\), \(h(2)=-1\), and \(f(x)=\tanh(x)\), the actual joint expectation tends to \(E|\tanh(Z)|>0\), whereas the proposed independent-innovation stable limit would give zero. Thus the frozen primitives imply the mixture approximation above but cannot imply stability against arbitrary bounded functions of the label.
