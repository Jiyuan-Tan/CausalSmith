Fix an even horizon \(T=2n\).  Retain the action process, its predictable arm-one probabilities \(p_t\in\{1/2,1/4\}\), and the numerical score process \((\xi_t)_{t=1}^T\) constructed in Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\).  Extend the probability space by independent Rademacher variables \(U_1,\ldots,U_T\), independent of every action randomizer, with
\[
P(U_t=1)=P(U_t=-1)=\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
This extension leaves the joint law of \((p_t,A_t,\xi_t)_{t=1}^T\) unchanged.

In Saco's binary notation define
\[
A_t^{\mathrm S}=\mathbf1\{A_t=1\},
\qquad \pi_t^{\mathrm S}=p_t,
\qquad X_t^{\mathrm S}=(X_t,U_t),
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)=\frac32+U_t,
\qquad
Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)=\frac12+U_t.
\tag{3}
\]
These potential outcomes depend only on the current treatment.  Consistency gives
\[
Y_t^{\mathrm S}
=A_t^{\mathrm S}Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)+(1-A_t^{\mathrm S})Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)
=\frac12+U_t+A_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus maintained Assumption~3.5 holds.  The triples
\[
(X_t^{\mathrm S},Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))
=\left((X_t,U_t),\frac12+U_t,\frac32+U_t\right)
\tag{5}
\]
are independent and identically distributed over \(t\), establishing Assumption~3.6.  The causal target is the fixed contrast of potential-outcome means
\[
\theta_0=E_P[Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)-Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)]=1.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(U_t\) and \(A_t^{\mathrm S}\) are conditionally independent given the past, (4) also gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y_t^{\mathrm S}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S})
=1+p_t(1-p_t)\ge1.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm S}\) be the history enlarged by \(U_1,\ldots,U_t\), and use the pre-randomization sigma-field
\[
\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}=\sigma(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S},X_t^{\mathrm S},p_t).
\tag{8}
\]
The adaptive rule makes \(p_t\) measurable with respect to this sigma-field, and the fresh action randomizer gives
\[
P(A_t^{\mathrm S}=1\mid\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S})=p_t=\pi_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is Assumption~3.2.  Conditional on \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\), both potential outcomes in (3) are measurable and the action is freshly randomized, proving Assumption~3.7.  Furthermore,
\[
\frac14\le p_t\le\frac12,
\qquad
\frac12\le1-p_t\le\frac34,
\tag{10}
\]
so Assumption~3.8 holds with overlap constant \(1/4\).

For Assumption~4.8 take the deterministic scored set
\[
\mathcal T_T=\{1,\ldots,T\},
\qquad n_{\mathrm{eff}}=|\mathcal T_T|=T\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{11}
\]
At a context \((x,u)\), choose the fixed nuisance maps
\[
\widehat m_{t-1,1}(x,u)=u+\frac12,
\qquad
\widehat m_{t-1,0}(x,u)=u-\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]
As nonrandom functions these are \(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\)-measurable, proving Assumption~4.5.  The two fourth-moment assumptions hold uniformly because
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E|Y_t^{\mathrm S}(a)|^4=\frac{313}{16},
\qquad
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E|\widehat m_{t-1,a}(X_t^{\mathrm S})|^4=\frac{41}{16}.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, the first maximum is attained by the values \(1/2,5/2\), while each nuisance has absolute values \(1/2,3/2\).

Substituting (3), (4), and (12) in the AIPW pseudo-outcome gives, pathwise,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\phi_t
&=\frac{A_t^{\mathrm S}}{p_t}\{Y_t^{\mathrm S}-(U_t+1/2)\}
-\frac{1-A_t^{\mathrm S}}{1-p_t}\{Y_t^{\mathrm S}-(U_t-1/2)\}\\
&\quad +(U_t+1/2)-(U_t-1/2)\\
&=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
-\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}+1
=\xi_t+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
By (6), the centered AIPW increment is therefore exactly \(\widehat\phi_t-\theta_0=\xi_t\).  Conditional on the enlarged past,
\[
P\!\left(\xi_t=\frac1{p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\right)=p_t,
\qquad
P\!\left(\xi_t=-\frac1{1-p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\right)=1-p_t.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently
\[
E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]=0,
\qquad
E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]
=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}\in\left\{4,\frac{16}{3}\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so all required fourth moments are finite.

The centered identity following (14) and Saco's definitions give
\[
S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t=S_T,
\qquad
Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t^2=[S]_T,
\qquad
(V_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2
=\sum_{t=1}^TE[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]
=\langle S\rangle_T.
\tag{17}
\]
Equation (16) gives the pathwise information bound
\[
(V_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2\ge4T=4n_{\mathrm{eff}},
\tag{18}
\]
so Assumption~5.11 holds with \(v_-=4\) and failure probability zero.  Moreover, the realized-to-predictable calculation in the preceding proposition gives
\[
\left|\frac{[S]_T}{\langle S\rangle_T}-1\right|
\le\frac{|[S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T|}{4T}\to_P0.
\tag{19}
\]

Thus every source condition recorded in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}pointwise\text{-}rqv\text{-}pivot}\) is satisfied by a law with a nonzero causal effect and conditionally nondegenerate observed outcomes.  Nevertheless, because the augmentation preserves the entire centered-score process, Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\) yields
\[
\frac{S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}{\sqrt{Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}}
=\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{[S]_T}
}\Longrightarrow
L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8},
\tag{20}
\]
where \(Z,W\) are independent standard normal variables and
\[
E[L]=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{21}
\]
Hence \(L\) is not standard normal, refuting the cited score-normality assertion on its stated class.

For the feasible statistic, the sample mean and sample variance satisfy the exact identities
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0=\frac{S_T}{T},
\qquad
(T-1)\widehat V^{\mathrm S}=Q_T-\frac{S_T^2}{T}.
\tag{22}
\]
The convergence in (20) implies \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}=O_P(1)\), and therefore
\[
R_T^{\mathrm S}:=\frac{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}{Q_T}
=\frac{T}{T-1}
\left\{1-\frac1T\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right)^2\right\}
\to_P1.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(P(R_T^{\mathrm S}>0)\to1\), and on this event
\[
\frac{\sqrt T(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0)}{\sqrt{\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}}
=\frac{S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}}{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(M,\epsilon>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\!\left(
\left|\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right|
\left|\frac1{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}-1\right|>\epsilon
\right)
&\le P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right|>M\right)\\
&\quad+P\!\left(\left|\frac1{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}-1\right|>\frac\epsilon M\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Tightness from (20) makes the first term arbitrarily small for large \(M\), and (23) sends the second term to zero.  Hence the difference between (24) and \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}\) converges to zero in probability, and (20) implies that the feasible statistic also converges to \(L\).

For the smallest-instance check, take \(T=2\).  The four action paths in the preceding proposition give
\[
P\!\left\{\left|S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}\right|\le1\right\}=\frac58<2\Phi(1)-1.
\tag{26}
\]
The sixteen combinations obtained by adjoining \((U_1,U_2)\) preserve the centered-score identity (14), so (26) is unchanged, while (6)--(7) certify a nonzero causal effect and positive conditional observed-outcome variance.  This completes the explicit within-class counterexample to both asserted normal limits.
