Let \(D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\). Each matrix entry of \(D_t\) is a martingale difference, and \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\) by bounded scores and overlap. Martingale orthogonality therefore gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le \frac{C}{T},
\]
with one common \(C\), since \(d\) is fixed. As the operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm,
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly.

On the high-probability exact-root event supplied by Theorem 1, \(\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\). The score derivative and overlap bounds imply, for every \(t\),
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le \frac{D_1}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\]
Both scores have norm at most \(B/\varepsilon\), and hence
\[
 \left\|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\]
After averaging and combining with the first display and the predictable-QV bound of Theorem 1, this yields
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J=O_P(T^{-1/2}+r_T+\delta_T)=O_P(e_T)
\]
uniformly. Likewise, smoothness gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat H_T-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\frac{D_2}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\end{aligned}
\]
All these statements remain valid after restoring the fallback event because its probability tends uniformly to zero.

The singular-value inequality and the last display imply \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat H_T)\ge h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Basin nondegeneracy and the covariance display similarly imply \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Omega_T)\ge\omega_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(\mathcal E_T^c)\to0.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T\), the inverse identity
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\widehat H_T^{-1}(H-\widehat H_T)H^{-1}
\]
and the bounds \(\|\widehat H_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-\), \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1/h_-\) show that inversion is uniformly Lipschitz. Expanding the two inverse factors and the middle covariance factor in the sandwich therefore gives
\[
 \widehat V_T-V_J=O_P(e_T).
\]
The event-truncated definition is essential only for finite-sample totality; its identity-matrix branch is entered with uniformly vanishing probability.

It remains to prove the pivot rather than assume it. Fix \(\|c\|_2=1\) and put \(a=H^{-\top}c\) and \(U_t=a^\top\xi_t\). Theorem 1 gives
\[
 \frac1{KD_1}\le\|a\|_2\le\frac1{h_-},\qquad
 |U_t|\le\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-},
\]
and
\[
 T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =a^\top T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_Ta
 \ge \frac{\omega_-}{2(KD_1)^2}
\]
with probability tending uniformly to one. Thus the predictable scalar variation diverges linearly. Moreover,
\[
 \frac{a^\top[S]_Ta}{a^\top\langle S\rangle_Ta}\to_P1
\]
uniformly, because the numerator minus denominator is \(O_P(\sqrt T)\) while the denominator is bounded below by a positive common multiple of \(T\). The fourth-moment condition in the cited self-normalized martingale theorem holds with \(\delta=2\), since
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4\le T\left(\frac{B}{\varepsilon h_-}\right)^4,
 \qquad
 \left(a^\top\langle S\rangle_Ta\right)^2\ge C_-T^2
\]
outside an event of uniformly vanishing probability. The cited theorem therefore gives
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]
This convergence is uniform in \(P\) and unit \(c\): otherwise a violating sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) would itself be a triangular array satisfying the same common increment, variance-growth, QV-ratio, and Lyapunov bounds, contradicting the cited theorem.

The exact-root linearization and the feasible covariance expansions give
\[
 \sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-T^{-1/2}a^\top S_T+O_P(e_T)
\]
and
\[
 c^\top\widehat V_Tc
 =T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta+O_P(e_T)
\]
uniformly. The denominator is bounded away from zero with probability tending uniformly to one. Division is therefore legitimate on that event, and
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1)
\]
uniformly. The normal distribution function is continuous; a standard \(\epsilon\)-smoothing inequality for distribution functions converts the preceding uniform weak convergence into
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|\to0.
\]
In particular, for the feasible interval
\[
 \left[c^\top\widehat\theta_T-z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{c^\top\widehat V_Tc/T},
 c^\top\widehat\theta_T+z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{c^\top\widehat V_Tc/T}\right],
\]
the coverage probability converges uniformly to \(P\{|N(0,1)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\}=1-\alpha\). Saco's cited theorem is pointwise and starts from abstract variance-growth and variance-ratio hypotheses. Here those conditions, the feasible inverse controls, and asymptotic inactivity of the truncation event were derived uniformly from the common finite-attractor primitives, which proves the final comparison in the claim.
