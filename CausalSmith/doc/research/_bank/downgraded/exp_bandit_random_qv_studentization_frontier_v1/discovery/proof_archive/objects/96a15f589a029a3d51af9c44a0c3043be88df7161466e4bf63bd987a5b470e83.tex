Use the witness from Propositions~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\) and \(\mathrm{prop:saco\text{-}theorem\text{-}5\text{-}14\text{-}counterexample}\), with \(T=2n\).  During the first block,
\[
P(\xi_t=2)=P(\xi_t=-2)=\frac12,
\qquad 1\le t\le n,
\tag{1}
\]
and the sign of \(S_n=\sum_{t=1}^n\xi_t\) selects second-block conditional variance \(16/3\) when \(S_n>0\) and \(4\) otherwise.

Let \(k=k_{2n}=o(2n)\).  Then \(k<n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Put
\[
p_n=P(S_n>0\mid\mathcal F_k).
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_k\), the remainder \(S_n-S_k\) is twice a sum of \(n-k\) independent symmetric signs.  The maximum point mass of a sum of \(r\) symmetric signs is at most \(C/\sqrt r\).  Indeed, for \(r=2\ell\) the maximum equals
\[
2^{-2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}
=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{2j-1}{2j}
\le\frac1{\sqrt{\ell+1}},
\tag{2}
\]
where the inequality follows by induction from
\[
\frac{2\ell+1}{2\ell+2}\le\sqrt{\frac{\ell+1}{\ell+2}};
\]
the odd case is bounded by an adjacent even case.  By symmetry, moving the threshold from zero to \(-S_k\) crosses at most \(1+|S_k|\) lattice atoms.  Hence
\[
\left|p_n-\frac12\right|
\le\frac{C(1+|S_k|)}{\sqrt{n-k}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Martingale orthogonality in the first block gives \(E[S_k^2]=4k\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(E|S_k|\le2\sqrt k\).  Since \(k/n\to0\), taking expectations in (3) yields
\[
E\left|p_n-\frac12\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]
For any \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable binary predictor \(B_n\), its conditional error for predicting \(J_n:=\mathbf1\{S_n>0\}\) is at least \(\min(p_n,1-p_n)\).  Therefore
\[
P(B_n\ne J_n)
\ge E\min(p_n,1-p_n)longrightarrow\frac12.
\tag{5}
\]

Now calculate the suffix predictable QV.  The first-block conditional variance is \(4\), while the second-block value is selected as above.  Consequently
\[
q_n:=\frac1{2n}\sum_{t=k+1}^{2n}E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{14}{3}-\frac{2k}{n},&J_n=1,\\[4pt]
\displaystyle4-\frac{2k}{n},&J_n=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The two values differ by \(2/3\).  Suppose, to obtain a contradiction, that a positive \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable variable \(\Lambda_{2n}\) satisfied \(q_n/\Lambda_{2n}\to_P1\).  Because \(q_n<5\), on \(\{|q_n/\Lambda_{2n}-1|<1/2\}\) one has \(\Lambda_{2n}<10\), and therefore
\[
|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|
=\Lambda_{2n}|q_n/\Lambda_{2n}-1|
\le10|q_n/\Lambda_{2n}-1|.
\tag{7}
\]
It follows that \(|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|\to_P0\).  Define the \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable predictor
\[
B_n=\mathbf1\left\{\Lambda_{2n}>\frac{13}{3}-\frac{2k}{n}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The threshold in (8) is the midpoint of the two values in (6).  Thus \(B_n=J_n\) whenever \(|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|<1/3\), so
\[
P(B_n\ne J_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
This contradicts (5).  Hence no positive early-measurable scale can satisfy the displayed suffix stabilization.  The argument used no upper bound on \(\Lambda_{2n}\), so it applies in particular when that scale is uniformly bounded away from zero.  In the Saco specialization \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=T\), which proves the final class-exclusion assertion.
