Write \(g_{t,a}(\theta)=g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\). By sequential randomization, conditional independence of the action and current potential outcomes, i.i.d. full-data arrivals, and the overlap bound that makes every denominator positive,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &=E_P\!\left[E_P\!\left\{\left.\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)}
 {\pi_t(a\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})}\right|W_t,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right\}\middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]\\
 &=E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\end{aligned}\tag{1}
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array. The same conditioning calculation and \(\mathrm{def:basin\text{-}covariance}\) give
\[
 E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad \Omega_\infty=\Gamma_P(\eta_\infty). \tag{2}
\]
The bounded-score and overlap assumptions imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\). For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
 u^\top\Gamma_P(\eta)u
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \frac{\|g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\Pi(a\mid X_t,\eta)}\right]
 \le\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon}. \tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(\Omega_\infty\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\), while \(\Omega_\infty\succeq\omega_-I_d\) is the basin-nondegeneracy assumption. The derivative envelope permits differentiation through the finite sum and expectation and yields
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \|\partial_\theta g_{t,a}(\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le KD_1. \tag{4}
\]

For \(p,q\ge\varepsilon\), \(|p^{-1}-q^{-1}|\le|p-q|/\varepsilon^2\). The policy-Lipschitz assumption and the definition of \(\Gamma_P\) therefore give
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\|\eta-\eta'\|_2,
 \qquad L_\Gamma=\frac{KB^2L_\Pi}{\varepsilon^2}, \tag{5}
\]
and \(\|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Gamma:=KB^2/\varepsilon\). On
\[
 \mathcal A_T=\left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 \|\eta_{t-1}-\eta_\infty\|_2\le r_T\right\},
\]
equations (2) and (5) imply
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_\infty\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T. \tag{6}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T^c\) the norm is at most \(2M_\Gamma\), and the uniform-attraction assumption gives \(P(\mathcal A_T^c)\le\delta_T\). Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_\infty\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T). \tag{7}
\]

We next derive localization. For fixed \(\theta\), sequential randomization and i.i.d. arrivals show that the summands of \(G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\) are mean-zero martingale differences. Their norms are uniformly bounded, so martingale orthogonality gives
\[
 E_P\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2^2\le C_0/T. \tag{8}
\]
The empirical and population maps are uniformly Lipschitz in \(\theta\), with respective moduli \(D_1/\varepsilon\) and \(KD_1\). For fixed \(x>0\), cover compact \(\Theta\) by a finite mesh fine enough that the two Lipschitz approximation errors total at most \(x/2\). Chebyshev's inequality applied to (8) and a union bound prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\left\{\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2>x\right\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{9}
\]
Since \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) and the preliminary estimator satisfies its defining approximate-minimum inequality,
\[
 \|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2
 +T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1) \tag{10}
\]
uniformly. Root separation therefore yields, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\ge u\}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\|M_P(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{11}
\]

At \(\theta^*\), \(G_T(\theta^*)=S_T/T\), so (1), martingale orthogonality, and bounded increments imply \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. Applying the same entrywise argument to the derivative score gives
\[
 R_T:=\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}) \tag{12}
\]
uniformly. On the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), the curvature and overlap bounds imply
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|R_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon. \tag{13}
\]
The radius restriction, (12), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) make the right side smaller than \(h_-/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. On that event set \(\Psi_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)\). Equation (13) and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) show that \(\Psi_T\) is a contraction with modulus at most \(1/2\) on the ball, and
\[
 \|\Psi_T(\theta)-\theta^*\|_2
 \le\tfrac12\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2+h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2. \tag{14}
\]
The last term is at most \(\rho/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one, and root interiority puts the closed ball inside \(\Theta\). Iteration from any point in the ball produces a Cauchy sequence whose limit \(\theta_T^0\) is the unique fixed point, hence the unique empirical root, in that ball. Moreover,
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le2h_-^{-1}\|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{15}
\]
Equations (11) and (15) put both \(\widetilde\theta_T\) and \(\theta_T^0\) within \(\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is within \(3\rho/4\) of \(\theta^*\) and must equal the unique root \(\theta_T^0\). Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\to0,
 \qquad \widehat\theta_T=\theta_T^0
\]
outside a uniformly negligible event.

Put \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\). On the exact-root event, Taylor's formula and the curvature bound give
\[
 0=G_T(\widehat\theta_T)
 =T^{-1}S_T+H\Delta_T+R_T\Delta_T+R_T^{(2)},
 \qquad \|R_T^{(2)}\|_2\le\frac{D_2}{2\varepsilon}\|\Delta_T\|_2^2. \tag{16}
\]
Using (12), (15), and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\) in (16) yields
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T). \tag{17}
\]
The fallback branch is uniformly negligible, so (17) holds unconditionally in the uniform-in-probability sense.

It remains to derive the terminal-representative-free mixture approximation. Let
\[
 \beta_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},\qquad
 k_T=\lceil T\beta_T\rceil,\qquad
 \Lambda_T=k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}). \tag{18}
\]
The vanishing-modulus assumption gives \(k_T/T\to0\) and \(Tr_T/k_T\le\sqrt{r_T}+o(1)\). The matrix \(\Lambda_T\) is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable. Repeating (6)--(7), first on the prefix and then on the suffix, gives
\[
 E_P\|\Lambda_T-\Omega_\infty\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma\frac{Tr_T}{k_T}+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1), \tag{19}
\]
uniformly, and, for \(B_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1})\),
\[
 E_P\|B_T-\Omega_\infty\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_\Gamma r_T+M_\Gamma k_T/T+2M_\Gamma\delta_T=o(1). \tag{20}
\]
Thus \(E_P\|B_T-\Lambda_T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)\) uniformly.

Fix a unit vector \(c\) and set \(a=H^{-\top}c\). Equations (4) and the Jacobian lower bound imply
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1}. \tag{21}
\]
Therefore \(|a^\top\xi_t|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-)\), while nondegeneracy gives
\[
 a^\top\Omega_\infty a\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0. \tag{22}
\]
Define the \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable clipped variance
\[
 \lambda_{T,P,c}=\max\{v_0/2,a^\top\Lambda_Ta\}. \tag{23}
\]
It lies between \(v_0/2\) and a common finite upper bound. Since clipping is one-Lipschitz and fixes every number at least \(v_0\), (19)--(22) give
\[
 E_P|a^\top B_Ta-\lambda_{T,P,c}|=o(1),
 \qquad E_P|a^\top\Omega_\infty a-\lambda_{T,P,c}|=o(1) \tag{24}
\]
uniformly in \(P\) and unit \(c\).

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}martingale\text{-}mixture}\) to \(X_{T,t}=-a^\top\xi_t\), \(\mathcal G_{T,t}=\mathcal F_t\), and (23). Its martingale-difference, bounded-increment, early-measurability, positive-variance, and quadratic-variation hypotheses are respectively (1), (21), (23), (22)--(23), and the first part of (24). Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\left\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T\xi_t\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_{T,P,c}}}\right)\right|\to0. \tag{25}
\]
Let \(\phi\) denote the standard normal density. For variances in a fixed compact interval bounded away from zero,
\[
 \sup_z\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\Phi(z/\sqrt v)\right|
 =\sup_x\frac{|x|\phi(x)}{2v}\le C/v_0. \tag{26}
\]
The second part of (24) and (26) replace the expectation in (25) by
\[
 E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_\infty a}}\right)
 =E_P\Phi\left(\frac z{\sqrt{c^\top V_\infty c}}\right). \tag{27}
\]
Martingale orthogonality and bounded increments give
\[
 E_P\left|a^\top T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\xi_t\right|^2
 \le Ck_T/T=o(1). \tag{28}
\]
The exact-root remainder in (17) is also uniformly negligible. The mixture distributions in (27) have densities uniformly bounded by (22). The inclusions
\[
 \{X+R\le z\}\subseteq\{X\le z+\eta\}\cup\{|R|>\eta\},
 \qquad
 \{X\le z-\eta\}\subseteq\{X+R\le z\}\cup\{|R|>\eta\}
\]
then transfer (25)--(28) through both remainders, first letting \(T\to\infty\) and then \(\eta\downarrow0\). This proves the uniform mixture approximation.

For every unit \(u\),
\[
 u^\top V_\infty u=(H^{-\top}u)^\top\Omega_\infty(H^{-\top}u).
\]
The upper bounds in (3) and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\), and the lower bounds in (4) and \(\Omega_\infty\succeq\omega_-I_d\), imply
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d\preceq V_\infty
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d. \tag{29}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:compact\text{-}covariance\text{-}law\text{-}subsequence}\) to the laws of \(V_\infty\) along any triangular sequence \(P_T\). It gives a subsequence and weak limit \(\mu\). For fixed nonzero \(c\) and every \(z\), the map
\[
 v\longmapsto\Phi\left(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc}\right)
\]
is bounded and continuous on the compact set in (29); hence the uniform mixture approximation converges to \(\int\Phi(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc})\,d\mu(v)\). This is the law of \(X=\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\) for independent \(V\sim\mu\) and \(Z_1\sim N(0,1)\).

Put \(Q=c^\top Vc\). The bounds in (29) make its first two moments finite and positive. Independence gives
\[
 E[X^2]=E[Q],\qquad E[X^4]=3E[Q^2]. \tag{30}
\]
If \(Q=q\) almost surely, then \(X\sim N(0,q)\). Conversely, if \(X\) is centered Gaussian, its variance must be \(E[Q]\), and its fourth moment is \(3(E[Q])^2\). Equation (30) then gives \(\operatorname{Var}(Q)=0\). Thus the limit is Gaussian exactly when its projected variance mixing law is degenerate.

Finally, stable convergence relative to the terminal representative is not implied. For odd \(T\), take \(d=1\), two actions with constant probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), i.i.d. Rademacher variables \(R_t\), \(Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=R_t\), and \(g(a,x,y;\theta)=(y-\theta)/2\) on \(\Theta=[-1,1]\). Let \(\mathsf H=[-1,1]\), set every predictable state \(\eta_t=0\), take \(r_T=T^{-1/4}\), \(\delta_T=0\), and define the terminally measurable representative
\[
 \eta_\infty=r_T\,\operatorname{sgn}\left(\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right). \tag{31}
\]
Then the attraction average is exactly \(r_T\). The policy is constant in its state, so \(\Omega_\infty=V_\infty=1\). All remaining primitives hold with fixed envelopes: \(M_P(\theta)=-\theta\), \(H=-1\), and the exact empirical root is \(T^{-1}\sum_tR_t\). Indeed, the full-data vectors are i.i.d.; constant half--half assignment is sequentially randomized and has any fixed overlap floor \(\varepsilon<1/2\); the score and its first derivative are bounded by one, while its second derivative is zero; \(\kappa(u)=u\), \(s_\Theta=1\), and \(h_-=1\) are valid; the constant policy has every positive Lipschitz envelope; and \(\Omega_\infty=1\) permits \(\omega_-=1\). Taking any fixed \(D_2>0\) and \(0<\rho<\min\{1,\varepsilon/(4D_2)\}\) satisfies the radius condition. Finally, \(e_T=T^{-1/2}+T^{-1/4}\to0\). Thus this is a genuine triangular sequence in one fixed-envelope class.

Let \(h_T=\operatorname{sgn}(\eta_\infty)\) and \(f(x)=\tanh(x)\). Symmetry gives \(E[h_T]=0\), whereas
\[
 E\left[h_Tf\left(T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right)\right]
 =E\left|\tanh\left(T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^TR_t\right)\right|. \tag{32}
\]
This is bounded away from zero. Indeed, for \(X_T=T^{-1}(\sum_tR_t)^2\), independence gives \(E[X_T]=1\) and \(E[X_T^2]=3-2/T\le3\). If \(A_T=\{X_T>1/2\}\), then
\[
 1=E[X_T]\le\tfrac12+E[X_T\mathbf1_{A_T}]
 \le\tfrac12+\sqrt{3P(A_T)},
\]
so \(P(A_T)\ge1/12\), and (32) is at least \(\tanh(1/\sqrt2)/12\). Uniform stable convergence to a Gaussian innovation independent of \(\sigma(\eta_\infty)\) would instead force (32) to converge to \(E[h_T]E[f(Z_1)]=0\). This contradiction proves the final assertion while leaving the marginal scale-mixture approximation intact.
