All stochastic orders below are uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\) and, when a contrast occurs, over \(\|c\|_2=1\). From the bounded-score and overlap assumptions,
\[
 \|\xi_t\|_2\le M_\xi:=B/\varepsilon. \tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
 D_t=\xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P[\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
Every entry of \(D_t\) is a martingale difference, and
\[
 \|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}
 \le \|\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm F}
 +E_P[\|\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm F}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 \le2M_\xi^2. \tag{2}
\]
Martingale orthogonality over the fixed \(d^2\) entries gives
\[
 E_P\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^TD_t\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P\|D_t\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le\frac{4M_\xi^4}{T}. \tag{3}
\]
Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm, Chebyshev's inequality proves
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{4}
\]

The exact-root localization and fallback conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) give
\[
 \|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}). \tag{5}
\]
The global first-derivative bound and overlap imply, pathwise,
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le\frac{D_1}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2. \tag{6}
\]
Both scores are bounded by \(M_\xi\), and therefore
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le(\|\widehat\xi_t\|_2+\|\xi_t\|_2)\|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le\frac{2BD_1}{\varepsilon^2}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2. \tag{7}
\]
Averaging (7), using (4)--(5), and applying Markov's inequality to the predictable-QV expectation bound in \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat\Omega_T-\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le\left\|T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
 (\widehat\xi_t\widehat\xi_t^\top-\xi_t\xi_t^\top)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &\quad+\left\|T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2}+r_T+\delta_T)=O_P(e_T). 
\end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
On the uniformly high-probability root-localization event, the curvature and overlap bounds similarly give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat H_T-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le\|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\frac{D_2}{\varepsilon}\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\\
 &=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
Its complement, including the fallback branch, has uniformly vanishing probability, so it does not change (9).

The elementary variational inequality \(|\sigma_{\min}(A)-\sigma_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), (9), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) show that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\sigma_{\min}(\widehat H_T)<h_-/2\}\to0. \tag{10}
\]
Likewise, the Rayleigh-quotient inequality \(|\lambda_{\min}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), (8), and \(\Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d\) give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Omega_T)<\omega_-/2\}\to0. \tag{11}
\]
The union bound proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(\mathcal E_T^c)\longrightarrow0. \tag{12}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T\), all inverses in the construction are legal and
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}-H^{-1}
 =\widehat H_T^{-1}(H-\widehat H_T)H^{-1},
 \qquad \|\widehat H_T^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/h_-. \tag{13}
\]
The basin-stable theorem also supplies
\[
 \|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad \|\Omega_J\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_\Omega:=KB^2/\varepsilon. \tag{14}
\]
Insert and subtract the three sandwich factors one at a time. Equations (8), (9), (13), and (14) imply on \(\mathcal E_T\) that
\[
 \widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\Omega_T\widehat H_T^{-\top}
 -H^{-1}\Omega_JH^{-\top}=O_P(e_T). \tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_T^c\), the construction sets \(\widehat V_T=I_d\), while (14) bounds \(V_J\) uniformly. Equation (12) therefore allows this branch to be discarded in uniform probability, and (15) plus the regime-indexed covariance definition prove
\[
 \widehat V_T-V_J=O_P(e_T). \tag{16}
\]

We now derive the pivotal limit. Fix a unit vector \(c\) and define
\[
 a=H^{-\top}c,
 \qquad U_t=a^\top\xi_t,
 \qquad A_T^2=\sum_{t=1}^TE_P[U_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}],
 \qquad Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t^2. \tag{17}
\]
The upper bound \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\) from the basin-stable theorem and the lower singular-value assumption give
\[
 (KD_1)^{-1}\le\|a\|_2\le h_-^{-1},
 \qquad |U_t|\le L:=B/(\varepsilon h_-). \tag{18}
\]
The predictable-QV approximation and regime nondegeneracy imply
\[
 \frac{A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T)a
 =a^\top\Omega_Ja+o_P(1),
 \qquad a^\top\Omega_Ja\ge v_0:=\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}>0. \tag{19}
\]
Thus \(A_T^2\to\infty\) in probability uniformly. Equation (4) yields
\[
 \frac{Q_T-A_T^2}{T}
 =a^\top T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)a
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}), \tag{20}
\]
so (19)--(20) imply \(Q_T/A_T^2\to_P1\) and \(P(Q_T>0)\to1\), uniformly. With \(\delta=2\), on the event \(A_T^2\ge Tv_0/2\),
\[
 A_T^{-4}\sum_{t=1}^TE_P|U_t|^4
 \le \frac{TL^4}{(Tv_0/2)^2}=O(T^{-1}), \tag{21}
\]
and that event has probability tending uniformly to one by (19). Thus every array formed from an arbitrary sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\) and arbitrary unit contrasts \(c_T\) satisfies every premise of \(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\). It follows that
\[
 \frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}
 =\frac{\sum_{t=1}^TU_t}{\sqrt{Q_T}}
 \Rightarrow N(0,1). \tag{22}
\]
This convergence is uniform in \(P\) and unit \(c\): otherwise a sequence \((P_T,c_T)\) with Kolmogorov distance bounded away from zero would contradict (22) for that triangular array. For completeness, weak convergence to the continuous CDF \(\Phi\) implies Kolmogorov convergence by partitioning the real line at finitely many continuity points with arbitrarily small intervening \(\Phi\)-increments and using monotonicity of distribution functions.

The exact-root linearization gives
\[
 \sqrt T\,c^\top(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)
 =-T^{-1/2}a^\top S_T+O_P(e_T). \tag{23}
\]
Furthermore, (4), (8), (16), and \(c^\top V_Jc=a^\top\Omega_Ja\) imply
\[
 c^\top\widehat V_Tc-T^{-1}a^\top[S]_Ta=O_P(e_T). \tag{24}
\]
By (19)--(20), both quantities compared in (24) exceed \(v_0/2\) with probability tending uniformly to one. Division and square roots are therefore legitimate on that event. Since \(e_T\to0\) and
\[
 |\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt x+\sqrt y}\qquad(x,y>0),
\]
equations (22)--(24) give
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{a^\top S_T}{\sqrt{a^\top[S]_Ta}}+o_P(1) \tag{25}
\]
uniformly. The same two-sided \(\eta\)-smoothing inclusions used in the basin-stable proof, now with the uniformly continuous limiting CDF \(\Phi\), prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{26}
\]

This pivotal statement has the required causal Wald consequence. For fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), define
\[
 I_T(c,\alpha)=\left[
 \widehat\tau_T-z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{c^\top\widehat V_Tc/T},
 \widehat\tau_T+z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{c^\top\widehat V_Tc/T}
 \right].
\]
Because \(\tau=c^\top\theta^*\), the event \(\tau\in I_T(c,\alpha)\) is exactly \(|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\). Therefore (26) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}
 \left|P\{\tau\in I_T(c,\alpha)\}-(1-\alpha)\right|
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{27}
\]
No step used pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\). The label enters only through \(\Omega_J\), and every approximation to this covariance atom came from the uniform Cesaro inequality through the basin-stable theorem. Thus the proof applies to the broadened finite-Cesaro-regime class. The cited Saco result is used only as a score-level self-normalized martingale theorem; no inclusion relation between its AIPW/DML class and the present IPW-Z class is asserted.
