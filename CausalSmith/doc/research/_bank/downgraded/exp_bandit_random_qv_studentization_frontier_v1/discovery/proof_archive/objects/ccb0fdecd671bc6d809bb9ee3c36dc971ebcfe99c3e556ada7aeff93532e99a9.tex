Write \(\mu_1=1/2\), \(\mu_2=1/12\), \(p=99/100\), \(q=1/100\), and \(C_{t,a}=\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}\). By \(\textnormal{def:guoxu-recursion}\), for every \(t\geq1\),
\[
 D_{t,a}=1+\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_s^2,\qquad
 N_{t,a}=\eta_{0,a}+\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_s(\mu_a+\sigma_aR_{s,a}),
 \qquad \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=\frac{N_{t,a}}{D_{t,a}}. \tag{1}
\]
The clipped design in that definition assigns each arm with conditional probability at least \(q\). Since \(X_s\) is independent of the past and uniform on \(\{-4,1\}\),
\[
 E[C_{s,a}X_s^2\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}]
 \ge qE[X_s^2]=\frac{17}{200}. \tag{2}
\]

We use the following elementary consequence of bounded martingale differences, deriving it here rather than adding an external premise. If \((U_s)\) is a scalar martingale-difference sequence and \(|U_s|\le L\), orthogonality of distinct increments gives \(E(\sum_{s=1}^nU_s)^2\le L^2n\). Hence
\[
 P\left\{\left|\sum_{s=1}^{k^2}U_s\right|>\epsilon k^2\right\}
 \le \frac{L^2}{\epsilon^2k^2}.
\]
The sum of the right-hand side over \(k\) is finite. Thus the tail probability that any such event occurs after index \(K\) tends to zero as \(K\to\infty\), and only finitely many occur almost surely. The at most \(2k+1\) bounded increments between \(k^2\) and \((k+1)^2\) then give \(n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^nU_s\to0\) almost surely.

Apply this result to \(C_{s,a}X_s^2-E[C_{s,a}X_s^2\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}]\). Equations (1)--(2) imply
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_{t,a}}t\ge\frac{17}{200},
 \qquad q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=D_{t,a}^{-1}=O(t^{-1})
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{3}
\]
The same argument applies to \(C_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\), since the action is randomized before the independent centered reward innovation is observed. Therefore
\[
 t^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^tC_{s,a}X_sR_{s,a}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]
Furthermore, (1) rewrites each slope as a convex combination of \(\eta_{0,a}\) and the observed ratios \(Y_s(a)/X_s\), with respective weights \(1\) and \(C_{s,a}X_s^2\). The definition of \(M_{\mathrm{GX}}\) therefore bounds both slopes. Bounded contexts and rewards then bound every coordinate of \(\Psi_t\), while (3) keeps both normalized Gram coordinates bounded away from zero eventually.

Define the sufficient-statistic increment
\[
 U_{t+1}=\bigl(C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}^2,C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}^2,
 C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(1),C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(2)\bigr).
\]
Dividing the four updates in \(\textnormal{def:guoxu-recursion}\) by \(t+1\) gives the exact recursion
\[
 \Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_t=\frac1{t+1}(U_{t+1}-\Psi_t). \tag{5}
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal F_t\), let \(u_t\) and \(v_t\) be the arm-one assignment probabilities when the next context equals \(1\) and \(-4\), respectively. Conditional centering of the rewards and direct averaging over the two contexts yield
\[
 E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]=h(u_t,v_t), \tag{6}
\]
because the four coordinates are
\[
 \frac{u_t+16v_t}{2},\qquad \frac{17-u_t-16v_t}{2},\qquad
 \mu_1\frac{u_t-4v_t}{2},\qquad
 \mu_2\frac{-3-u_t+4v_t}{2}.
\]

For \(\psi=(d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)\), set \(\Delta(\psi)=n_1/d_1-n_2/d_2\). At the current normalized state, the difference between the two square-root bonuses after removing the common factor \(|X|\) is
\[
 b_t:=\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}}-\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}}
 =t^{-1/2}\bigl(d_{t,1}^{-1/2}-d_{t,2}^{-1/2}\bigr). \tag{7}
\]
The arm-one minus arm-two index has the sign of \(\Delta(\Psi_t)+b_t\) at context \(1\), and the sign of \(b_t-\Delta(\Psi_t)\) at context \(-4\). By (3), \(|b_t|=O(t^{-1/2})\). Thus away from \(\Delta=0\), the limiting conditional-mean modes are \(h_+\) for \(\Delta>0\) and \(h_-\) for \(\Delta<0\). On \(\Delta=0\), equation (7) is positive when \(d_1<d_2\), negative when \(d_1>d_2\), and zero when \(d_1=d_2\). The design and deterministic tie rule then give the same-arm modes \(h_1,h_2,h_1\), respectively. Taking the closed convex hull together with the adjacent \(h_+\) and \(h_-\) modes gives exactly the displayed graph \(F\).

We next verify that this graph actually controls (5). A same-arm mode can occur off the switching surface only when \(|\Delta(\Psi_t)|\le |b_t|\). On the eventual compact region just obtained, replacing \(n_1\) by \(n_1-d_1\Delta(\Psi_t)\) and leaving the other coordinates fixed produces a point on \(\Delta=0\) at distance at most a fixed multiple of \(|\Delta(\Psi_t)|\). Hence, for a deterministic \(c_0<\infty\) and all sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
 E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]-\Psi_t\in F^{\delta_t}(\Psi_t),
 \qquad \delta_t=c_0t^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0, \tag{8}
\]
where \(F^\delta\) is the graph enlargement. The adjacent-mode calculation above shows that the displayed graph is closed; its values are nonempty, compact, and convex, and its velocities have linear growth because the four atoms are fixed. To meet the global-domain convention of \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-perturbed-limit-set}\), choose a compact convex set in \(\{d_1,d_2>0\}\) containing the eventual trajectory in its interior, project any exterior argument onto this set in the atom correspondence, and retain the term \(-\psi\). This extension has closed graph, compact convex values, and linear growth, and agrees with \(F\) along the eventual trajectory.

Let \(M_{t+1}=U_{t+1}-E[U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\) and \(\gamma_t=1/t\). Each coordinate of \(M_{t+1}\) is a bounded martingale difference. Stopping a weighted partial sum at its first crossing of \(\epsilon\), expanding its square, and using orthogonality of the stopped increments gives constants \(C,C'<\infty\) such that, for each coordinate and each integer \(j\),
\[
 P\left\{\sup_{k\ge2^j}\left|\sum_{s=2^j}^k\gamma_{s+1}M_{s+1}\right|>\epsilon\right\}
 \le \frac{C}{\epsilon^2}\sum_{s=2^j}^{\infty}\gamma_{s+1}^2
 \le\frac{C'}{\epsilon^22^j}. \tag{9}
\]
Summing the last bound over \(j\ge J\) and sending \(J\to\infty\) proves that the weighted martingale tails vanish almost surely. The increments in (5) are bounded on the eventual compact region, and the mesh tends to zero. Thus (5), (8), and (9) verify the bounded-perturbed-solution hypotheses of \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-perturbed-limit-set}\). That cited lemma makes the affine interpolation an asymptotic pseudotrajectory and its limit set internally chain transitive.

Put
\[
 \mathcal C=h([q,p]^2)=\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1,h_2\}.
\]
Every velocity in \(F(\psi)\) equals \(z-\psi\) for some \(z\in\mathcal C\). Let \(P_{\mathcal C}\psi\) be Euclidean projection and let \(V(\psi)=\operatorname{dist}(\psi,\mathcal C)^2\). The projection inequality gives, along every solution and almost everywhere outside \(\mathcal C\),
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}V(\psi(s))
 =2(\psi-P_{\mathcal C}\psi)^\top(z-\psi)
 \le-2\|\psi-P_{\mathcal C}\psi\|_2^2=-2V(\psi). \tag{10}
\]
Variation of constants shows that \(\mathcal C\) is positively invariant. Hence \(V\) is a continuous Lyapunov function for \(\mathcal C\), and \(V(\mathcal C)=\{0\}\) has empty interior. By \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\), every internally chain-transitive normalized-state limit set lies in \(\mathcal C\). Equation (3) keeps \(d_1,d_2\) positive on every such limit set, so the slope map \((d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)\mapsto(n_1/d_1,n_2/d_2)\) is continuous there. This proves the first part of the statement.

We now analyze the smaller two-mode inclusion solely as an internal diagnostic. Let \(\mathcal C_0=\operatorname{co}\{h_-,h_+\}\) and parameterize it as \(h_-+\lambda(h_+-h_-)\), \(0\le\lambda\le1\). Equivalently,
\[
 u=q+(p-q)\lambda,\qquad v=p-(p-q)\lambda=1-u. \tag{11}
\]
Both Gram coordinates are positive. Direct cross-multiplication therefore gives
\[
 \Delta(h(u,v))=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f(u,v):=u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0; \tag{12}
\]
indeed, after multiplication by the positive common denominator, the numerator of the slope difference is \(-5f(u,v)\). Substituting \(v=1-u\) gives \(f(u,1-u)=-75u^2+47u+8\), whose unique root in \([q,p]\) is
\[
 u_0=\frac{47+\sqrt{4609}}{150}.
\]
Substitution into either slope ratio yields \(b_0=(\sqrt{4609}-91)/1224\).

On \(\mathcal C_0\), the two-mode diagnostic inclusion reduces to
\[
 \dot\lambda=-\lambda\quad(\lambda<\lambda_0),\qquad
 \dot\lambda=1-\lambda\quad(\lambda>\lambda_0),\qquad
 \dot\lambda\in[-\lambda_0,1-\lambda_0]\quad(\lambda=\lambda_0), \tag{13}
\]
where \(u_0=q+(p-q)\lambda_0\). The distance-squared argument in (10) first confines every internally chain-transitive set to \(\mathcal C_0\). On the segment, define
\[
 W(\lambda)=
 \begin{cases}
 \lambda/\lambda_0,&0\le\lambda\le\lambda_0,\\
 (1-\lambda)/(1-\lambda_0),&\lambda_0\le\lambda\le1.
 \end{cases}
\]
This continuous function strictly decreases along every nonconstant solution outside \(\{0,\lambda_0,1\}\) and never increases from those three points. Since \(W(\{0,\lambda_0,1\})=\{0,1\}\) has empty interior, \(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\) confines every internally chain-transitive set to \(\{0,\lambda_0,1\}\) with \(W\) constant. The set \(\{0,1\}\) is not internally chain transitive: if \(\epsilon<1/3\), the solutions from both endpoints are constant and no \((\epsilon,S)\)-chain contained in that two-point set travels between them. Thus the only internally chain-transitive sets for the smaller diagnostic inclusion are the three asserted singletons.

That smaller graph does not approximate the exact recursion uniformly near the switching surface. If \(\Delta=0\) and \(d_1<d_2\), equation (7) makes arm one greedy at both contexts and the conditional mean is \(h_1\). Yet \(h_1\notin\mathcal C_0\): the affine map \((u,v)\mapsto h(u,v)\) is injective, every pair on \(\mathcal C_0\) satisfies \(u+v=1\), and \(h_1\) corresponds to \((p,p)\), whose sum is \(2p\ne1\). Compactness gives \(\operatorname{dist}(h_1,\mathcal C_0)>0\). Thus the \(O(t^{-1/2})\) bonus cannot be discarded uniformly throughout the \(O(t^{-1/2})\) switching layer.

The additional equilibria of the correct graph are explicit. Solving (12) for its smaller \(v\)-root gives
\[
 v(u)=\frac{3u+18-\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}{32}. \tag{14}
\]
For \(u\in[u_0,p]\), substitution gives \(f(u,v(u))=0\) and \(v(u_0)=1-u_0\). Moreover,
\[
 v'(u)=\frac1{32}\left(3+\frac{228-50u}{2\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}\right)>0. \tag{15}
\]
The elementary bounds \(67<\sqrt{4609}<68\) and \(73<\sqrt{5457}<74\) imply
\[
 0.23<v(u_0)<0.24,qquad 0.308<v(p)<0.314.
\]
Consequently \(q<u,v(u)<p\), \(u+v(u)\ge1\), and
\[
 u+16v(u)\le p+16v(p)<p+16(0.314)<6.014<\frac{17}{2}. \tag{16}
\]
The first inequalities put \((u,v(u))\) in the triangle with vertices \((p,q),(q,p),(p,p)\); the last inequality says that the first normalized Gram coordinate of \(h(u,v(u))\) is smaller than the second. Hence
\[
 h(u,v(u))\in\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1\},\qquad
 \Delta(h(u,v(u)))=0,qquad 0\in F(h(u,v(u))). \tag{17}
\]
Each singleton in (17) is an equilibrium and therefore internally chain transitive. The map \(h\) is injective because the two linear forms \(u+16v\) and \(u-4v\) have nonzero determinant, so this family is uncountable. At \(u=p\), (14) simplifies to
\[
 v(p)=\frac{2097}{3200}-\frac{3\sqrt{5457}}{640}=v_*.
\]
The preceding bounds show that \(h(p,v_*)\) differs from the three two-mode candidates. This establishes the stated internal diagnostic obstruction and makes no claim about a published method.

It remains to prove the assertions about the exact recursion. Put \(\Delta_t=\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}-\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\). On \(\mathcal L_+\), equation (3) makes the bonus vanish while \(\Delta_t\) is eventually bounded below by a positive constant. Eventually arm one is therefore greedy at context \(1\) and arm two at context \(-4\). Applying the bounded martingale law derived above to the four context--action counts, and using (4) for reward noise, gives
\[
 \frac1t(D_{t,1},D_{t,2},N_{t,1},N_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow h_+=\left(\frac{23}{40},\frac{317}{40},\frac{19}{80},-\frac{79}{480}\right). \tag{18}
\]
Equations (1) and (18) give the first displayed slope limit. The identical argument on \(\mathcal L_-\) gives
\[
 \frac1t(D_{t,1},D_{t,2},N_{t,1},N_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow h_-=\left(\frac{317}{40},\frac{23}{40},-\frac{79}{80},\frac{19}{480}\right), \tag{19}
\]
and hence the second displayed slope limit. Their limiting slope gaps are
\[
 \frac{19}{46}+\frac{79}{3804}=\frac{37955}{87492}>0,
 \qquad
 -\frac{79}{634}-\frac{19}{276}=-\frac{16925}{87492}<0. \tag{20}
\]

We prove the uniform positive-probability assertions constructively. Fix a sufficiently large even integer \(n\). For the positive regime, prescribe a length-\(2n\) history consisting first of \(n\) observations with \(X=1,A=1\), then \(n\) observations with \(X=-4,A=2\), with equally many positive and negative selected-arm Rademacher signs in each block. Each prescribed context has probability \(1/2\), each prescribed action has conditional probability at least \(q\), and each prescribed relevant sign has probability \(1/2\). Thus the history has probability at least \((q/4)^{2n}\), uniformly over \(\eta_0\in[-1,1]^2\). At its end the slopes are
\[
 \frac{\eta_{0,1}+n/2}{1+n},
 \qquad
 \frac{\eta_{0,2}-n/3}{1+16n}, \tag{21}
\]
which converge uniformly in \(\eta_0\) to \(1/2\) and \(-1/48\), and both inverse Gram coordinates are \(O(n^{-1})\).

After this prefix, consider the reference policy whose greedy arms are arm one at context \(1\) and arm two at context \(-4\). After \(k\) further steps, its conditional-mean sufficient statistics add \(kh_+\) to the prefix statistics. Each mean slope is a denominator-weighted average of its prefix slope and the corresponding slope ratio in \(h_+\). Equations (20)--(21) therefore give constants \(\gamma_0>0\) and \(n_0<\infty\), independent of \(\eta_0\), such that for \(n\ge n_0\) the two reference mean slopes differ by at least \(3\gamma_0\) for every \(k\ge0\). Their Gram coordinates are bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(n+k\), so their bonus difference is at most \(C(n+k)^{-1/2}\); enlarge \(n_0\) so this is at most \(\gamma_0\).

Couple the actual and reference policies using the same contexts, innovations, and randomization uniforms, and let \(\zeta\) be the first future occasion at which their pre-assignment greedy maps differ at either context. Let \(M_k^{(+)}\) be the sum of the first \(k\) reference-policy sufficient-statistic increments minus \(kh_+\). Conditional on the prescribed prefix, this is a four-dimensional martingale with bounded increments. The stopped-square argument used for (9), now over dyadic blocks \(2^jn\le n+k<2^{j+1}n\), gives, for every fixed \(\gamma>0\),
\[
 P\left\{\sup_{k\ge0}\frac{\|M_k^{(+)}\|_\infty}{n+k}>\gamma
 \mathrel{\Big|}\text{the prescribed prefix}\right\}
 \le\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{C}{\gamma^22^jn}
 =\frac{C_\gamma}{n}. \tag{22}
\]
Indeed, a crossing on block \(j\) requires a partial sum of order \(\gamma2^jn\), while orthogonality bounds its second moment through the block by a constant times \(2^{j+1}n\).

All reference denominators are bounded below by a constant times \(n+k\); consequently, the ratio map is uniformly Lipschitz under the relative perturbation in (22). Choose \(\gamma\) so that this perturbation changes the slope gap by at most \(\gamma_0\) and preserves the denominator lower bound. On the complement of the event in (22), the perturbed slope gap is at least \(2\gamma_0\), whereas the bonus difference is at most \(\gamma_0\). At a putative first exit time \(\zeta\), the actual and reference sufficient statistics agree through time \(\zeta-1\). The actual greedy map must therefore still equal the reference map, a contradiction. Hence \(\zeta=\infty\) on this event, and (18) implies \(\mathcal L_+\). Taking \(n\) large enough that \(C_\gamma/n\le1/2\) gives
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_+)
 \ge\frac12(q/4)^{2n}>0. \tag{23}
\]

For the negative regime, prescribe first \(n\) observations with \(X=-4,A=1\), then \(n\) with \(X=1,A=2\), again balancing the selected-arm Rademacher signs. The terminal slopes converge uniformly to \(-1/8\) and \(1/12\); the conditional-mean continuation adds \(kh_-\), and the second strict gap in (20) gives the identical stopped-martingale bootstrap. Therefore
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_-)>0. \tag{24}
\]
On either event the corresponding slope sequence converges. Splitting the Cesaro average at a fixed time after which every distance to the applicable displayed limit is below an arbitrary \(\epsilon>0\), and then letting \(T\to\infty\), proves that the corresponding pathwise Cesaro error converges to zero.

Finally, define empirical context-specific arm-one rates
\[
 u_T=\frac2T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=1,A_t=1\},
 \qquad
 v_T=\frac2T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1\{X_t=-4,A_t=1\}.
\]
The conditional arm-one probability at either context always lies in \([q,p]\). Applying the bounded martingale law to centered context--action indicators and to centered context indicators shows that every subsequential limit of \((u_T,v_T)\) lies in \([q,p]^2\). If, along a common subsequence, both slopes converge to a common coordinate and \((u_T,v_T)\to(u,v)\), then (1), (4), and the context-count law give
\[
 \frac1T(D_{T,1},D_{T,2},N_{T,1},N_{T,2})\longrightarrow h(u,v).
\]
The two limiting Gram coordinates are positive because \((u,v)\in[q,p]^2\). Equality of the two limiting slope ratios is therefore equivalent, by (12), to
\[
 u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0.
\]
The additional relation \(v=1-u\) follows only if same-arm switching-layer occupation is separately excluded. Equations (7) and (14)--(17) show why neither the exact recursion calculation nor the differential-inclusion certificate supplies that exclusion: same-arm modes persist throughout the shrinking switching layer. No avoidance, quantitative hitting or escape, or compact-initial-set equicontinuity estimate has been proved. Therefore the argument establishes neither finite almost-sure tail selection nor zero probability of switching-surface convergence, and it yields no compact-uniform moduli \(r_T(K_0),\delta_T(K_0)\). In particular, it proves no membership in \(\mathfrak P_T\) and no inference result. This proves every assertion, including every stated nonclaim.
