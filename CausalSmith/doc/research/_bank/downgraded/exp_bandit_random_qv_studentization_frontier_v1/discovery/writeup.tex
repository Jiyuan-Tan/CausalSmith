\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}bandit\_\allowbreak{}random\_\allowbreak{}qv\_\allowbreak{}studentization\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The field-tier contribution is a conditional finite-attractor theorem bundle for adaptive causal IPW-Z inference. Under common score, derivative, Jacobian-lower-bound, policy, covariance-lower-bound, root-separation, attraction, and local root-search envelopes, with attainable basins defined as the positive-mass support of the tail selector and with \(r_T+\delta_T\to0\), the bundle derives a basin-stable finite Gaussian mixture with predictable-QV error controlled by \(r_T+\delta_T\), proves that an always-defined same-path realized-QV sandwich is uniformly pivotal and asymptotically untruncated, and gives exact deterministic-sandwich boundaries: every-level validity for one contrast holds if and only if that contrast's variance is basin-invariant, while validity for all contrasts holds if and only if the full covariance is deterministic. The separate early-stabilized projected class uses only the scaled numerical-tolerance condition \(T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)\to0\). Theorem 2 has one direct strict-tightening comparator, Saco's pointwise realized-QV result; the remaining named papers provide motivation, ingredients, or orthogonal regimes rather than a second strict frontier. An elementary bounded two-basin law verifies that contrastwise invariance need not imply matrix invariance. The slope-projected limit set of the augmented bounded Guo--Xu Markov chain remains an open dynamics question with no class-membership or inference claim.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Saco supplies the one direct pointwise realized-QV comparator, but not uniform verification over an endogenous finite-attractor class. Guo--Xu and Shen and coauthors supply adjacent stable-policy and directional-stability results, Han studies continuous SDE-invariant Thompson-sampling pull proportions and raw-arm-mean inference, and Leiner, Dunn, and Ramdas study stationary-evaluation-policy off-policy M-estimation with flexible variance stabilization under unstable logging. Zhang, Janson, and Murphy instead study pooled longitudinal adaptive sampling with Z-estimation and an adaptive sandwich estimator in a fixed-within-user, growing-participant regime. Existing stochastic-approximation results do not by themselves supply the finite tail selector and compact-initial-set Cesaro modulus required here. For the bounded Guo--Xu analogue, the exact finite-law recursion and relative four-mode differential-inclusion reduction are now explicit, but finite tail selection and the named compact-attraction predicate remain unproved; no particular quantitative-hitting or quantitative-escape hypothesis is imposed.

\paragraph{Niche.} The unoccupied field-tier object is a conditional recursion-level uniform limit theory that preserves the random basin, validates a finite-sample-defined same-path studentizer under an explicit early-measurable suffix-QV condition, and states exact contrastwise and matrixwise boundaries for deterministic-sandwich inference. The nondegenerate Saco counterexample shows that this temporal stabilization is substantive rather than a reformulation of predictable-QV growth.

\paragraph{Fill.} The publishable contribution is the conditional finite-attractor theorem bundle: a basin-stable causal IPW-Z finite Gaussian mixture, a uniformly feasible same-path realized-QV pivot, and exact contrastwise and matrixwise deterministic-sandwich boundaries. A finite-attractor class indexed by common score, derivative, Jacobian lower, policy, covariance lower, root-separation, attraction, and local root-search envelopes imposes the class-specific vanishing attraction condition \(r_T+\delta_T\to0\) and converts the uniform Cesaro inequality into predictable-QV control; its attainable-basin index set is the positive-mass support of the tail selector. The distinct early-stabilized projected class retains only the scaled numerical-tolerance condition \(T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)\to0\), because it assumes directional predictable-QV growth directly and imposes no finite-attractor approximation. The score and derivative envelopes already imply \(\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d\) and \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1\), so no primitive upper matrix bounds are imposed. A preliminary moment-norm minimizer localizes a compact empirical-root set; martingale concentration and a quantitative inverse-function argument make the exact-root selector active with probability tending uniformly to one. Stable martingale convergence supplies the mixture, event-truncated realized QV supplies the feasible pivot, finite normal-mixture identification plus polarization supplies the two necessity results, and a bounded in-class law certifies strict logical separation of the directional and matrix frontiers.

\section{Related work}

Guo and Xu, Theorems 3.2--3.3, prove marginal and joint IPW-Z Gaussian limits after scaled inverse propensities stabilize; their Section 3.1 no-intercept clipped-LinUCB example supplies numerical evidence of two modes, not a proved finite-attractor theorem or certification of the present class. The bounded analogue studied here gives the exact finite one-step law, normalized stochastic-approximation recursion, coordinatewise martingale-tail control, and relatively closed four-mode graph on an eventual compact carrier; it also proves two separated positive-probability regimes, but it does not prove finite tail selection or the separately named compact-attraction predicate. Guo--Xu is an informed-by comparator. Saco, Theorem 5.14 and Design A in Section 6.6, establishes a pointwise realized-QV pivot under variance growth and illustrates random post-burn-in variance; Theorem 2 is the sole direct strict tightening, giving uniform validity over the conditional finite-attractor class and deriving truncation inactivity from common basin-covariance, attraction, and exact-root localization controls. The Abstract and Introduction of Shen and coauthors distinguish directional stability from full-matrix stability; that distinction informs Theorems 3--4, while the finite-basin every-level converses and bounded separation witness are not presented as a strict comparator chain. Han studies an orthogonal instability mechanism and target: for generalized Gaussian Thompson sampling, Proposition 2.6 and Theorem 2.7 on PDF pages 9--10, together with introduction equation (1.4) on page 4, identify the joint optimal-arm pull-proportion and noise limit with the unique invariant law of a continuous-state SDE. Han's Theorem 3.2 on PDF page 12 and introduction equation (1.5) on page 5 give Gaussian normalized-mean limits for stable arms and an SDE-defined semi-universal limit for multiple optimal arms; the body reports simulation evidence that the latter law is genuinely non-Gaussian, and Corollary 3.3 on PDF page 13 constructs confidence intervals from its quantiles. Han is close motivation, not a result anticipating Theorems 1--4 or the bounded Guo--Xu certification question. Leiner, Dunn, and Ramdas study off-policy M-estimation under working-model misspecification for adaptively collected bandit data. Their target is a projected solution under a stationary evaluation policy, and flexible variance stabilization permits unstable or nonconvergent logging policies; this is an informed-by comparator rather than a same-path-QV or finite-attractor result. Borkar supplies local lock-in bounds, and Pemantle supplies unstable-point avoidance, but neither certifies the bounded recursion. Aldous and Eagleson supply stable-convergence language. Mourrat supplies only the bounded-martingale central-limit and quadratic-variation ingredient. Teicher together with Andrews and Mallows supplies identification of centered normal scale mixtures. Qian and coauthors and Liao and coauthors define the HeartSteps causal-excursion consumer. Zhang, Janson, and Murphy study pooled longitudinal adaptive sampling with Z-estimation and an adaptive sandwich estimator in a fixed-within-user, growing-participant regime. Thus the comparison supports a field-tier conditional theorem bundle, with Saco as its only strict-tightening comparator and no second strict frontier.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-full-data]\label{ass:iid-full-data}
The vectors \(W_1,\ldots,W_T\) are independent and identically distributed under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sequential exogenous arrivals, \cite{GuoXu2026MisspecifiedBandits}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sequential-randomization]\label{ass:sequential-randomization}
Conditional on \(X_t\) and \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\), the action \(A_t\) is drawn from the logged distribution \(\pi_t(\cdot\mid X_t,\mathcal F_{t-1})\) independently of \((Y_t(a))_{a\in\mathcal A}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sequential randomization, \cite{QianEtAl2022MRT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-overlap]\label{ass:uniform-overlap}
For every \(a\in\mathcal A\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(\eta\in\mathsf H\), the policy satisfies \(\Pi(a\mid x,\eta)\ge\varepsilon\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Policy-wide clipped adaptive overlap, \cite{GuoXu2026MisspecifiedBandits}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-score]\label{ass:bounded-score}
The common envelope satisfies \(\sup_{a\in\mathcal A,\theta\in\Theta}\|g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta)\|_2\le B\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded martingale increments, \cite{Mourrat2013MartingaleCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:smooth-z-map]\label{ass:smooth-z-map}
The map \(\theta\mapsto g(a,x,y;\theta)\) is continuously differentiable on a common open neighborhood of \(\operatorname{conv}(\Theta)\), with \(\|\partial_\theta g(a,x,y;\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le D_1\) there, and is twice continuously differentiable on the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), with \(\|\partial_\theta^2 g(a,x,y;\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le D_2\) throughout that ball.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Smooth Z-estimation moments, \cite{GuoXu2026MisspecifiedBandits}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-root-separation]\label{ass:uniform-root-separation}
For every \(u>0\), \(\inf_{\theta\in\Theta:\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\ge u}\|M_P(\theta)\|_2\ge\kappa(u)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform separation of a Z-functional root, \cite{GuoXu2026MisspecifiedBandits}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:root-interior]\label{ass:root-interior}
The causal root satisfies \(\operatorname{dist}(\theta^*,\partial\Theta)\ge s_\Theta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior Z-functional root, \cite{GuoXu2026MisspecifiedBandits}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:jacobian-nonsingular]\label{ass:jacobian-nonsingular}
The population Jacobian satisfies \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Regular Z-estimation identification, \cite{GuoXu2026MisspecifiedBandits}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:policy-lipschitz]\label{ass:policy-lipschitz}
The common policy modulus satisfies \(\sup_{a\in\mathcal A,x\in\mathcal X}|\Pi(a\mid x,\eta)-\Pi(a\mid x,\eta')|\le L_\Pi\|\eta-\eta'\|_2\) for all \(\eta,\eta'\in\mathsf H\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Lipschitz stochastic-approximation observation map, \cite{Borkar2002LockIn}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-tail-selection]\label{ass:finite-tail-selection}
There exists a \(\sigma(\bigcup_{t\ge1}\mathcal F_t)\)-measurable \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\)-valued random variable \(J\) such that \(\eta_\infty=\bar\eta_J\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This atomic condition selects one of finitely many fixed terminal states without imposing pointwise convergence of the policy state.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-attraction-modulus]\label{ass:uniform-attraction-modulus}
The member law obeys \(P\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\|\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\|_2>r_T\}\le\delta_T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This single Cesaro relation is the only path-approximation restriction and supplies a falsifiable common triangular-array modulus without imposing pointwise state convergence.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:vanishing-modulus]\label{ass:vanishing-modulus}
The scaled numerical tolerance satisfies \(T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This proposal-specific condition controls only the scaled numerical approximation and root-localization tolerance in the early-stabilized projected IPW--Z class; it imposes no unscaled Cesaro-attraction rate.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:basin-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:basin-nondegeneracy}
The terminal covariance satisfies \(\Omega_\infty\succeq\omega_-I_d\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A common almost-sure lower eigenvalue bound for the terminal random covariance is the proposal-specific uniform variance-growth primitive. The matching upper bound is derived from policy-wide overlap and the bounded score, not assumed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-attractor-separation]\label{ass:finite-attractor-separation}
For all distinct \(j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), \(\|\bar\eta_j-\bar\eta_k\|_2\ge s_\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} novel: proposal-specific separated-attractor class primitive
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:basin-mass-floor]\label{ass:basin-mass-floor}
For every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), \(P(J=j)\ge p_-\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} novel: proposal-specific uniform basin-mass primitive
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-projected-predictable-qv-growth]\label{ass:uniform-projected-predictable-qv-growth}
For the prespecified nonzero contrast \(c\), deterministic integers \(0\le k_T<T\), deterministic \(\omega_{c,T}>0\), and positive \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable scales \(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}\) almost surely, the chosen sequence \((\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c))_{T\ge1}\) satisfies, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 P\!\left\{
 \left|
 \frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T E_P[(c^\top H^{-1}\xi_t)^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}
 {T\Lambda_T}-1
 \right|>u
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This proposal-specific condition requires relative suffix predictable-QV approximation to an information scale fixed by the early filtration; the explicit obstruction shows that lower growth without this measurability boundary is insufficient.

\begin{definition}[def:saco-early-stabilized-aipw-class]\label{def:saco-early-stabilized-aipw-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Saco enumerated AIPW class with early QV stabilization}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each row \(n\), let \(\mathcal T_n=\{t_{n,1}<\cdots<t_{n,N_n}\}\) be a deterministic scored set, fixed before data collection, and put \(N_n=n_{\mathrm{eff}}=|\mathcal T_n|\).  The class \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{Saco,es}}=(\mathfrak C_n^{\mathrm{Saco,es}})_{n\ge1}\) consists of triangular arrays of binary-treatment laws with the following properties, imposed row by row and uniformly over the displayed class.

First, the source conditions retained from Saco are written out explicitly.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(Y_{n,t}(a)\) is the potential outcome depending only on the current treatment, and \(Y_{n,t}=A_{n,t}Y_{n,t}(1)+(1-A_{n,t})Y_{n,t}(0)\) almost surely.  The triples \((X_{n,t},Y_{n,t}(0),Y_{n,t}(1))\) are independent and identically distributed over \(t\) within a row and are unaffected by the adaptive policy.  There is a pre-treatment sigma-field \(\mathcal B_{n,t}\), containing the past and \(X_{n,t}\), such that the logged propensity \(\pi_{n,t}\) is \(\mathcal B_{n,t}\)-measurable and
\[
0<\pi_{n,t}<1,\qquad
\mathbb P_n(A_{n,t}=1\mid\mathcal B_{n,t})=\pi_{n,t}\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal B_{n,t}\), \(A_{n,t}\) is independent of \((Y_{n,t}(0),Y_{n,t}(1))\).  There is one constant \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) such that
\[
\epsilon\le\pi_{n,t}\le1-\epsilon
\quad\text{almost surely for every }t\in\mathcal T_n.
\]
For each \(a\), the fitted regression \(\widehat m_{n,t-1,a}\), as a random function, is measurable with respect to the history \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\).  There are finite constants \(C_Y,C_M\), common to the class, such that
\[
\sup_{n,\mathbb P_n,t,a}E_{\mathbb P_n}|Y_{n,t}(a)|^4\le C_Y,\qquad
\sup_{n,\mathbb P_n,t\in\mathcal T_n,a}
E_{\mathbb P_n}|\widehat m_{n,t-1,a}(X_{n,t})|^4\le C_M.
\]
Thus these are uniform versions of source Assumptions \(3.5,3.6,3.2,3.7,3.8,4.5,4.8,3.9,\) and \(3.10\), in the order specified by the directive.

Define the causal target, AIPW pseudo-outcome, centered score, estimator, and its sample variance by
\[
\theta_{0,n}=E_{\mathbb P_n}\{Y_{n,t}(1)-Y_{n,t}(0)\},
\]
\[
\widehat\phi_{n,t}=\frac{A_{n,t}}{\pi_{n,t}}
\{Y_{n,t}-\widehat m_{n,t-1,1}(X_{n,t})\}
-\frac{1-A_{n,t}}{1-\pi_{n,t}}
\{Y_{n,t}-\widehat m_{n,t-1,0}(X_{n,t})\}
+\widehat m_{n,t-1,1}(X_{n,t})-\widehat m_{n,t-1,0}(X_{n,t}),
\]
\[
X_{n,s}=\widehat\phi_{n,t_{n,s}}-\theta_{0,n},\qquad
S_n=\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}X_{n,s},\qquad
Q_n=\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}X_{n,s}^2,
\]
\[
\widehat\theta_n=N_n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}\widehat\phi_{n,t_{n,s}},\qquad
\widehat V_n=(N_n-1)^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}
\{\widehat\phi_{n,t_{n,s}}-\widehat\theta_n\}^2
\quad(N_n\ge2).
\]
To order the deterministic scored set without discarding interstitial history, define
\[
\mathcal G_{n,0}=\mathcal F_{n,t_{n,1}-1},\qquad
\mathcal G_{n,s}=\mathcal F_{n,t_{n,s+1}-1}\ (1\le s<N_n),\qquad
\mathcal G_{n,N_n}=\mathcal F_{n,t_{n,N_n}}.
\]
Then \(X_{n,s}\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,s}\)-measurable and \(\mathcal G_{n,s-1}=\mathcal F_{n,t_{n,s}-1}\).  Put
\[
V_n^2=\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}E_{\mathbb P_n}[X_{n,s}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}].
\]
The source variance-growth Assumption \(5.11\) is retained: \(N_n\to\infty\), and for one \(v_->0\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb P_n\in\mathfrak C_n^{\mathrm{Saco,es}}}
\mathbb P_n(V_n^2<v_-N_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Finally, intersect those source conditions with early quadratic-variation stabilization.  There are deterministic integers \(0\le k_n<N_n\), a constant \(\lambda_->0\), and, for every member law, a positive \(\mathcal G_{n,k_n}\)-measurable random variable \(\Lambda_n\) such that
\[
\frac{k_n}{N_n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad \Lambda_n\ge\lambda_-\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
and, writing
\[
A_{n,+}^2=\sum_{s=k_n+1}^{N_n}
E_{\mathbb P_n}[X_{n,s}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}],
\]
one has, for every \(\zeta>0\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb P_n\in\mathfrak C_n^{\mathrm{Saco,es}}}
\mathbb P_n\left(\left|\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\Lambda_n}-1\right|>\zeta\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{Saco,es}}\) is exactly Saco's enumerated AIPW/DML class, with the overlap and fourth-moment constants made uniform across the triangular array, intersected with this early-measurable suffix-QV condition.  No almost-sure bound on \(X_{n,s}\) is imposed.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} .
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:basin-stable-ipwz]\label{thm:basin-stable-ipwz}
The common derivative and bounded-IPW envelopes imply
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le KD_1,
 \qquad
 \omega_-I_d\preceq\Omega_j\preceq(KB^2/\varepsilon)I_d
 \quad\text{for every }j\in\mathcal J=\{1,\ldots,m\}.
\]
The finite-tail-selection relation gives \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\) and \(V_\infty=V_J\) almost surely. There is a finite constant \(C\), independent of \(T\) and \(P\), such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\!\left[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right]
 \le C(r_T+\delta_T).
\]
The preliminary estimator is uniformly consistent, \(G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), and \(\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\) uniformly. Together with \(D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/4\), these bounds imply that, with probability tending uniformly to one, there is a unique empirical root in the closed \(\rho\)-ball about \(\theta^*\), it is \(O_P(T^{-1/2})\) from \(\theta^*\), and it is the unique member of \(\mathcal Z_T\). Hence \(\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(E_T^c)\to0\), and
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(e_T)
\]
uniformly.

For every basin \(j\in\mathcal J\), the basin-conditional Gaussian approximation is uniform:
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\!\left\{c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\le z\mid J=j\right\}
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{c^\top V_jc}}\right)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
More strongly, the corresponding joint \(\sigma(J)\)-stable Gaussian kernel is expressed by
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \sup_{\psi:\{1,\ldots,m\}\to[-1,1]}
 \left|E_P\!\left[\psi(J)\mathbf1\!\left\{
 c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\le z\right\}\right]
 -\sum_{j=1}^mp_j\psi(j)\Phi\!\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{c^\top V_jc}}\right)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}I_d\preceq V_j
 \preceq\frac{KB^2}{\varepsilon h_-^2}I_d
 \quad\text{for every }j\in\mathcal J.
\]
Thus, for every sequence \(P_T\in\mathfrak P_T\), the laws of \(V_J\) have a weakly convergent subsequence with a limiting law \(\mu\) supported on the displayed compact covariance set. Along that subsequence, every fixed nonzero contrast has the centered Gaussian scale-mixture limit \(\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\), where \(V\sim\mu\), \(Z_1\sim N(0,1)\), and \(V\) and \(Z_1\) are independent. This limit is Gaussian if and only if \(c^\top Vc\) is \(\mu\)-almost surely constant. No pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\) is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(M_0=KB^2/\varepsilon\) and \(L_0=KB^2L_\Pi/\varepsilon^2\).  All constants below depend only on the common constants defining \(\mathfrak P_T\), and not on \(T\) or on its member law \(P\).

First, sequential randomization and the independence of \(W_t\) from \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) give, at \(\theta^*\),
\[
 E_P(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array.  The same conditioning calculation gives
\[
 E_P(\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad
 \langle S\rangle_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).                                    \tag{1}
\]
Only one treatment indicator is nonzero, so uniform overlap and the bounded-score assumption imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\).  Moreover,
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{E_P\|g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\varepsilon}
 \le M_0.                                                                  \tag{2}
\]
For \(u,v\ge\varepsilon\), \(|u^{-1}-v^{-1}|\le |u-v|/\varepsilon^2\).  Hence the policy Lipschitz condition gives
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_0\|\eta-\eta'\|_2.                                                \tag{3}
\]
On the event in the uniform attraction assumption, (1)--(3) yield
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_0r_T.
\]
On its complement the left side is at most \(2M_0\).  Because that complement has probability at most \(\delta_T\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_0r_T+2M_0\delta_T\le C(r_T+\delta_T).                              \tag{4}
\]
Here finite-tail selection and the covariance definition give \(\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J)=\Omega_J\), \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\), and \(V_\infty=V_J\) almost surely.

Differentiation under the bounded derivative envelope gives
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\|\partial_\theta g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le KD_1.                                                                 \tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) evaluated at an attractor gives \(\Omega_j\preceq M_0I_d\).  Basin nondegeneracy says \(\Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d\) almost surely.  Since \(P(J=j)\ge p_->0\), failure of \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) for any \(j\) would make basin nondegeneracy fail on an event of positive probability.  Therefore
\[
 \omega_-I_d\preceq\Omega_j\preceq M_0I_d\quad(j\in\mathcal J).          \tag{6}
\]
For every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), (5), (6), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) imply
\[
 x^\top V_jx\ge\omega_-\|H^{-\top}x\|_2^2
 \ge\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\|x\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 x^\top V_jx\le M_0h_-^{-2}\|x\|_2^2.                                  \tag{7}
\]

We next establish the Z-estimation assertions.  Besides \(G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T\), the derivative counterpart of the first conditioning calculation is
\[
 E_P\{\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\}=H.
\]
The summands in the two centered averages are martingale differences bounded respectively by common multiples of \(B/\varepsilon\) and \(D_1/\varepsilon\).  Entrywise conditional-variance addition and Chebyshev's inequality, with fixed \(d\), therefore give uniformly
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).                            \tag{8}
\]
For completeness, a finite-net argument gives the uniform law of large numbers needed for the preliminary estimator.  Indeed, \(G_T\) is \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz and \(M_P\) is \(KD_1\)-Lipschitz on the fixed compact set \(\Theta\).  On every fixed finite net, the same martingale Chebyshev bound applies pointwise; taking the union bound and then sending the mesh to zero proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2=o_P(1)               \tag{9}
\]
uniformly.  The defining approximate-minimum inequality and \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) show
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1),                  \tag{10}
\]
where finite-attractor vanishing gives \(r_T+\delta_T\to0\).  Equations (9)--(10), followed by the common root-separation modulus \(\kappa\), imply \(\widetilde\theta_T\to_P\theta^*\) uniformly.

On the event where \(\|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-/4\), the curvature envelope gives, throughout the closed \(\rho\)-ball,
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le h_-/4+D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/2.                                    \tag{11}
\]
If also \(\|H^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2\le\rho/4\), the map
\[
 \mathcal T_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)
\]
maps that closed ball into itself and is a contraction with Lipschitz constant \(q_T<1/2\), by (11) and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\).  To make existence explicit, start at \(\theta^{(0)}=\theta^*\) and iterate \(\theta^{(n+1)}=\mathcal T_T(\theta^{(n)})\).  The self-map property keeps every iterate in the ball, while
\[
 \|\theta^{(n+1)}-\theta^{(n)}\|_2\le q_T^n\|\theta^{(1)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_2.
\]
The geometric-series bound makes the iterates Cauchy; the closed Euclidean ball is complete, and continuity of \(\mathcal T_T\) makes their limit \(\theta_T^0\) a fixed point, hence a root.  If two fixed points existed, their distance would be at most \(q_T\) times itself, so they would coincide.  At the fixed point,
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le \|H^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+ \frac12\|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2,
\]
so (8) yields \(\|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  With probability tending uniformly to one, both this distance and \(\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\) are below \(\rho/4\).  Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is in the closed \(\rho\)-ball and hence equals \(\theta_T^0\).  This proves uniform asymptotic certainty of \(E_T\), uniqueness of its selected root, and its stated rate.

On this event, Taylor's formula gives, for \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\),
\[
 0=G_T(\theta^*)+\bar H_T\Delta_T,
 \qquad
 \bar H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*+u\Delta_T)\,du.
\]
By (8), the curvature bound, and \(\Delta_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), \(\bar H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  Since \(T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(1)\), the inverse identity gives
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =(H^{-1}-\bar H_T^{-1})T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).               \tag{12}
\]
The exceptional event has probability tending uniformly to zero, so (12) remains a uniform \(O_P(e_T)\) assertion for the total estimator.

It remains to prove the stable kernel rather than assume it.  Put
\[
 b_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},
 \qquad
 k_T=\min\{T-1,\lceil Tb_T\rceil\}.
\]
Then \(k_T/T\to0\).  Let \(J_T\) be the fixed-tie-broken nearest attractor to \(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\).  It is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  On the attraction event,
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\frac{r_T}{b_T}\le\sqrt{r_T}.                 \tag{13}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), (13) and attractor separation make \(J\) the unique nearest attractor.  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\longrightarrow0.        \tag{14}
\]

Fix \(\|c\|_2=1\), put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), and define \(X_{T,t}=a^\top\xi_t\) and the early-measurable scale \(\lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a\).  From (5)--(7), uniformly in \(P,c\),
\[
 |X_{T,t}|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-),
 \quad
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\le\lambda_T\le\frac{M_0}{h_-^2}.            \tag{15}
\]
Using (4), deleting the bounded prefix, and then using (14), we obtain
\[
 E_P\left|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P(X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})-\lambda_T\right|
 \le h_-^{-2}\{C(r_T+\delta_T)+M_0k_T/T+2M_0P(J_T\ne J)\}=o(1)          \tag{16}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c\).

For each \(j\), (14) and the basin-mass floor give \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\) eventually.  Conditional on the event \(\{J_T=j\}\in\mathcal F_{k_T}\), the suffix remains a martingale-difference array, its predictable variances are unchanged, and its conditional version of the left side of (16) is at most \(2/p_-\) times the unconditional bound.  The already-established early-measurable martingale-mixture lemma, applied under this conditional law (equivalently with the now constant scale \(a^\top\Omega_ja\)), gives the uniform conditional Gaussian approximation for the suffix.  Its prefix is negligible because martingale orthogonality and (15) imply
\[
 E_P\left|T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}X_{T,t}\right|^2
 \le \frac{k_TB^2}{T\varepsilon^2h_-^2}=o(1).                            \tag{17}
\]
Combining the finitely many conditional approximations, (14), (17), and Gaussian anti-concentration under the lower bound in (15), for every \(\psi:\mathcal J\to[-1,1]\),
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|
 E_P\!\left[\psi(J)\mathbf1\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}S_T\le z\}\right]
 -\sum_{j=1}^mp_j\psi(j)\Phi\!\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_ja}}\right)
 \right|\longrightarrow0                                                \tag{18}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c,\psi\).  Since \(a^\top\Omega_ja=c^\top V_jc\), and since (12) has an \(o_P(1)\) remainder under finite-attractor vanishing, another application of the same uniform Gaussian anti-concentration transfers (18) to \(c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\).  Taking \(\psi=\mathbf1\{j=\cdot\}\) and dividing by \(p_j\ge p_-\) gives the stated basin-conditional approximation; leaving \(\psi\) arbitrary gives the stated \(\sigma(J)\)-stable kernel.

Finally, (7) places every \(V_J\) in the common compact covariance set.  The compact-covariance-law subsequence lemma supplies a weakly convergent subsequence with law \(\mu\).  Along it, (18) with \(\psi\equiv1\), continuity of \(v\mapsto\Phi(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc})\), and (12) give the scalar limit \(\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\), with the independent Gaussian representation stated in the theorem.  If \(c^\top Vc=q\) almost surely, this is \(N(0,q)\).  Conversely, if the mixture is \(N(0,q_0)\), boundedness of \(c^\top Vc\) permits comparison of second and fourth moments:
\[
 E(c^\top Vc)=q_0,
 \qquad
 3E\{(c^\top Vc)^2\}=3q_0^2.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{Var}(c^\top Vc)=0\).  This proves the claimed if-and-only-if statement and completes the proof without pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem derives predictable-QV control, exact-root localization, basin-conditional Gaussian limits, and the joint sigma(J)-stable Gaussian kernel from separated finite attractors and one common Cesaro modulus. \textit{Gap.} Classical Aldous--Eagleson stable martingale theory supplies the stable and mixed-normal limit apparatus. This theorem uniformly derives the random basin covariance from finite separated attractors and a common Cesaro modulus, constructs an early \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable decoder \(J_T\) to avoid conditioning the martingale on terminal \(J\), and carries the limit through uniform IPW--Z exact-root linearization. \textit{Consumer.} A HeartSteps-style online learner can retain the deployment-selected basin in the asymptotic law for a prespecified causal excursion contrast without assuming pointwise convergence of the adaptive state.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-realized-qv-pivot]\label{thm:uniform-realized-qv-pivot}
Fix a prespecified \(c\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\). For the early-stabilized projected class \(\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\), the exact-root event is uniformly asymptotically certain and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P(E_T^c)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =O_P(T^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly. Moreover,
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly over that class. Although no information growth is required outside the direction \(H^{-\top}c\), the early-scale condition with \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) makes the contrast-specific truncation inactive:
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)\le z\}-\Phi(z)\right|
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
and, for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)}
 \left|P\{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}-(1-\alpha)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Every finite-tail-attractor class \(\mathfrak P_T\) satisfies this early-stabilized condition for each fixed nonzero \(c\). At every horizon set
\[
 k_T=\min\!\left\{T-1,\left\lceil T\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\}\right\rceil\right\}.
\]
For \(k_T\ge1\), let \(J_T\) be the nearest attractor to \(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\), with fixed tie-breaking, and set \(J_T=1\) when \(k_T=0\). With \(a=H^{-\top}c\), take
\[
 \Lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a,\qquad
 \omega_{c,T}=\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}.
\]
Then \(J_T\) and \(\Lambda_T\) are \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable, all early-scale conditions hold uniformly, and the same pivot and coverage conclusions follow.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the prespecified \(c\ne0\) and a member law of \(\mathfrak Q_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(c)\).  The conditioning argument in the proof of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) uses only independent full-data arrivals, sequential randomization, and the causal moment equation, and hence again shows that \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array with \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\).  The corresponding derivative summands are bounded by \(D_1/\varepsilon\).  Therefore, uniformly over the projected class,
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).                            \tag{19}
\]
The finite-net proof of the uniform law of large numbers also remains valid.  Here the preliminary numerical error is \(T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o(1)\) by the projected-class rate assumption.  Root separation consequently makes \(\widetilde\theta_T\) uniformly consistent.  The contraction argument based on
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\rho}
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}+D_2\rho/\varepsilon
 <h_-/2
\]
then proves \(P(E_T^c)\to0\) uniformly, \(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), and, exactly as in the Taylor calculation leading to (12),
\[
 \sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).       \tag{20}
\]

For each matrix entry, the variables
\[
 \xi_t\xi_t^\top-E_P(\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
\]
are bounded martingale differences.  Conditional-variance addition, Chebyshev's inequality, and fixed \(d\) give
\[
 T^{-1}([S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T)=O_P(T^{-1/2}).                           \tag{21}
\]
On the root event, the derivative envelope gives
\[
 \|\widehat\xi_t-\xi_t\|_2
 \le(D_1/\varepsilon)\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2.
\]
Both scores are bounded by \(B/\varepsilon\), so averaging the outer-product difference and using (20) proves
\[
 \widehat\Omega_T-T^{-1}[S]_T=O_P(T^{-1/2}).                              \tag{22}
\]
Similarly, (19), the curvature bound, and (20) give
\[
 \widehat H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).                                            \tag{23}
\]
The complements of the root event have uniformly vanishing probability, so (21)--(23) hold for the total definitions.

Put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), \(X_{T,t}=a^\top\xi_t\),
\[
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^TX_{T,t}^2,
 \qquad
 A_{T,+}^2=\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P(X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}).
\]
The variables \(X_{T,t}\) are martingale differences bounded by \(\|c\|_2B/(\varepsilon h_-)\).  The defining projected-QV condition says
\[
 A_{T,+}^2/(T\Lambda_T)\to_P1
\]
uniformly, while the class conditions give \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\), \(k_T/(T\omega_{c,T})\to0\), and \(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}\).  The already-established early-measurable-scale self-normalized martingale lemma therefore yields
\[
 \frac{Q_T}{T\Lambda_T}\to_P1,
 \qquad
 \frac{\sum_{t=1}^TX_{T,t}}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)                \tag{24}
\]
uniformly.

It remains essential to compare square roots, since an absolute covariance error need not be small relative to a vanishing \(\Lambda_T\).  On \(\mathcal H_T\), define \(\widehat X_{T,t}=c^\top\widehat H_T^{-1}\widehat\xi_t\).  Then \(\widehat q_T(c)=T^{-1}\sum_t\widehat X_{T,t}^2\).  The inverse identity, (20), (23), and the score derivative bound imply
\[
 \left\{T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T(\widehat X_{T,t}-X_{T,t})^2\right\}^{1/2}
 =O_P(T^{-1/2}).                                                           \tag{25}
\]
Indeed, the two terms are bounded by common constants times \(\|\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\) and \(\|\widehat H_T-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), respectively.  The reverse triangle inequality in \(\mathbb R^T\) now gives
\[
 \left|\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}-\sqrt{Q_T/T}\right|=O_P(T^{-1/2}).         \tag{26}
\]
Dividing (26) by \(\sqrt{\Lambda_T}\), and using \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\), shows from (24) that
\[
 \widehat q_T(c)/\Lambda_T\to_P1.                                       \tag{27}
\]
Equations (23) and (27) imply
\[
 P\{\mathcal E_T(c)^c\}
 \le P(\mathcal H_T^c)+P\{\widehat q_T(c)<\Lambda_T/2\}\to0            \tag{28}
\]
uniformly, because \(\Lambda_T\ge\omega_{c,T}\).

On \(\mathcal E_T(c)\), combine (20) and (24)--(27) to obtain
\[
 T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)
 =-\frac{\sum_{t=1}^TX_{T,t}}{\sqrt{Q_T}}
   \frac{\sqrt{Q_T/T}}{\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}}+o_P(1).                  \tag{29}
\]
The remainder in (20), after division by \(\sqrt{\widehat q_T(c)}\), is \(O_P\{(T\omega_{c,T})^{-1/2}\}=o_P(1)\).  Thus the uniform version of Slutsky's argument in (24), together with (28) and symmetry of the standard normal law, proves the displayed uniform Kolmogorov convergence of the total statistic.  Since, for every realization, positivity of the constructed denominator gives the exact equivalence
\[
 \{\tau\in I_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c,\alpha)\}
 =\{|T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(c)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\},
\]
the two tail probabilities and the uniform Gaussian approximation yield the asserted coverage for each fixed \(\alpha\).

Finally consider \(P\in\mathfrak P_T\).  Use the cutoff and nearest-attractor label displayed in the theorem.  Since \(r_T\to0\),
\[
 k_T/T\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\sqrt{r_T},
\]
and the proof of (13)--(14) gives \(P(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\to0\) uniformly.  The label and
\(\Lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a\) are \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  Equations (5)--(7) from Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\) give
\[
 \Lambda_T\ge\omega_-\|H^{-\top}c\|_2^2
 \ge\frac{\omega_-\|c\|_2^2}{(KD_1)^2}=\omega_{c,T}>0.                 \tag{30}
\]
This lower bound is constant in \(T\), so \(T\omega_{c,T}\to\infty\) and \(k_T/(T\omega_{c,T})\to0\).  From the predictable-QV bound (4), the bounded prefix, and \(P(J_T\ne J)\to0\),
\[
 \left|\frac{A_{T,+}^2}{T}-\Lambda_T\right|\to_P0
\]
uniformly.  Division is legal by (30), and proves the required relative projected-QV condition.  Thus every finite-attractor member is in the displayed early-stabilized projected construction for this contrast, and all conclusions just proved apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:contrastwise-deterministic-sandwich-iff]\label{thm:contrastwise-deterministic-sandwich-iff}
Let \(T_\ell\to\infty\) and \(P_\ell\in\mathfrak P_{T_\ell}\) be a sequence along which the law of \(V_\infty\) under \(P_\ell\) converges weakly to a probability law \(\mu\) on the compact covariance set in Theorem 1. Let \(V\sim\mu\). Fix nonzero \(c\) and deterministic positive-definite \(\Sigma\). Then
\[
 \lim_{\ell\to\infty}P_\ell\{\tau\in C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\}=1-\alpha
 \quad\text{for every }\alpha\in(0,1)
\]
if and only if
\[
 c^\top Vc=c^\top\Sigma c\qquad\mu\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The necessity identifies the projected variance law of a general centered Gaussian scale mixture from coverage at every nominal level; it makes no claim from validity at only one level.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(s=c^\top\Sigma c>0\) and \(Q=c^\top Vc\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:basin\text{-}stable\text{-}ipwz}\), the assumed weak convergence of the covariance laws, and continuity on the common compact positive-definite set imply
\[
 c^\top\sqrt{T_\ell}(\widehat\theta_{T_\ell}-\theta^*)
 \Rightarrow \sqrt Q\,Z_1,                                                \tag{31}
\]
where \(Z_1\) is independent of \(Q\).  The boundary of every centered finite interval has zero probability under this mixture.  Therefore the definition of \(C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\) and (31) give, with \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\),
\[
 \lim_{\ell\to\infty}P_\ell\{\tau\in C_{T_\ell}^{\Sigma}(c,\alpha)\}
 =P\{|\sqrt QZ_1|\le z\sqrt s\}
 =E\!\left[2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{s/Q}\right)-1\right].                 \tag{32}
\]
If \(Q=s\) almost surely, (32) equals \(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\), proving sufficiency.

Conversely, suppose the left side of (32) equals \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  As \(\alpha\) ranges over \((0,1)\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) ranges over \((0,\infty)\).  Hence \(|\sqrt QZ_1|\) and \(|\sqrt sZ_1|\) have the same distribution.  Both underlying variables are symmetric, so \(\sqrt QZ_1\) and \(\sqrt sZ_1\) also have the same distribution.  The compact covariance support bounds \(Q\), and therefore all fourth moments exist.  Equality of the second and fourth moments gives
\[
 E(Q)=s,
 \qquad
 3E(Q^2)=3s^2.
\]
Consequently \(E\{(Q-s)^2\}=0\), or \(c^\top Vc=c^\top\Sigma c\) \(\mu\)-almost surely.  This proves necessity.  The use of every \(\alpha\), equivalently every positive \(z\), is exactly what identifies the absolute-value distribution; no conclusion here is drawn from a single nominal level.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem exactly characterizes every-level fixed-sandwich validity for one causal contrast and gives the computable basinwise certificate under labelwise limits. \textit{Gap.} Directional-stability work gives sufficient target-specific conditions and Saco highlights random predictable variance; neither gives the finite-basin centered-scale-mixture every-level converse. \textit{Consumer.} Trial analysts can decide whether a deterministic variance report remains valid for one prespecified HeartSteps excursion-effect contrast even when other covariance directions remain basin-dependent.

\begin{theorem}[thm:matrixwise-deterministic-sandwich-iff]\label{thm:matrixwise-deterministic-sandwich-iff}
Along any sequence for which the law of \(V_\infty\) converges weakly to \(\mu\), as in Theorem 3, one deterministic positive-definite matrix \(\Sigma\) gives asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and every nonzero contrast \(c\in\mathbb R^d\) if and only if
\[
 V=\Sigma\qquad\mu\text{-almost surely},
\]
where \(V\sim\mu\). Equivalently, the limiting covariance law is the point mass at \(\Sigma\). For a fixed law and horizon-asymptotic sequence, this specializes to \(V_\infty=\Sigma\) almost surely whenever the law of \(V_\infty\) itself is constant across horizons. The implication from all projected equalities to matrix equality uses only finitely many coordinate and pairwise-sum contrasts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(V=\Sigma\) \(\mu\)-almost surely, then \(c^\top Vc=c^\top\Sigma c\) almost surely for every nonzero \(c\), and Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:contrastwise\text{-}deterministic\text{-}sandwich\text{-}iff}\) gives every-level coverage for every such contrast.

Conversely, suppose the same deterministic positive-definite \(\Sigma\) gives every-level coverage for every nonzero contrast.  Apply the contrastwise theorem to the finite collection
\[
 \{e_i:1\le i\le d\}\cup\{e_i+e_j:1\le i<j\le d\},
\]
where \(e_i\) are the coordinate vectors.  Intersecting the finitely many probability-one events gives, simultaneously,
\[
 e_i^\top Ve_i=e_i^\top\Sigma e_i,
 \qquad
 (e_i+e_j)^\top V(e_i+e_j)=(e_i+e_j)^\top\Sigma(e_i+e_j).
\]
The first identities give \(V_{ii}=\Sigma_{ii}\).  Subtracting the two relevant diagonal identities from the second gives \(2V_{ij}=2\Sigma_{ij}\).  Thus every entry agrees and \(V=\Sigma\) almost surely.  This is equivalent to \(\mu=\delta_\Sigma\).

If, for a fixed horizon-asymptotic sequence, the law of \(V_\infty\) is the same at every horizon, that common law is its own weak limit \(\mu\).  The equivalence just proved then specializes to \(V_\infty=\Sigma\) almost surely exactly as stated.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem separates target-specific validity from target-agnostic covariance invariance and supplies a finite polarization certificate. \textit{Gap.} Shen and coauthors distinguish directional from matrix stability on the sufficiency side; the finite-basin every-level necessity and polarization certificate remain unstated. \textit{Consumer.} Software reporting many adaptive-experiment contrasts can determine whether one fixed covariance matrix is defensible.

\begin{lemma}[lem:early-measurable-martingale-mixture]\label{lem:early-measurable-martingale-mixture}
Let \((X_{T,t},\mathcal G_{T,t})_{t=1}^T\) be a scalar martingale-difference triangular array under \(P\), let \(0\le k_T<T\), and suppose \(|X_{T,t}|\le L<\infty\) almost surely. Let \(\lambda_T\) be \(\mathcal G_{T,k_T}\)-measurable and satisfy \(0<v_-\le\lambda_T\le v_+<\infty\) almost surely. If
\[
 \Delta_T:=E_P\left|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T
 E_P[X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{T,t-1}]-\lambda_T\right|\longrightarrow0,
\]
then
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|P\left\{T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T X_{T,t}\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\lambda_T}}\right)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The conclusion is uniform over any collection of arrays having common \(L,v_-,v_+\) and a common upper bound on \(\Delta_T\) that tends to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 Q_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^T
 E_P[X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{T,t-1}].
\tag{1}
\]
Fix \(\eta>0\) and partition \([v_-,v_+]\) into finitely many Borel intervals \(I_r\) of diameter at most \(\eta\). The events \(B_r=\{\lambda_T\in I_r\}\) belong to \(\mathcal G_{T,k_T}\); choose \(\lambda_r\in I_r\cap[v_-,v_+]\). For each cell of positive probability let \(P_r=P(\,\cdot\mid B_r)\). Since \(B_r\in\mathcal G_{T,k_T}\subseteq\mathcal G_{T,t-1}\) for \(t>k_T\), testing against bounded \(\mathcal G_{T,t-1}\)-measurable functions gives, on \(B_r\),
\[
 E_{P_r}[X_{T,t}\mid\mathcal G_{T,t-1}]=0,
 \qquad
 E_{P_r}[X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{T,t-1}]
 =E_P[X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{T,t-1}].
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the suffix remains a martingale-difference array under \(P_r\), with the same predictable second moments.

Define
\[
 d_r=E_{P_r}|Q_T-\lambda_T|+\eta,\qquad
 q_r=E_{P_r}Q_T,\qquad s_r^2=Tq_r.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(|\lambda_T-\lambda_r|\le\eta\) on \(B_r\),
\[
 |q_r-\lambda_r|\le d_r,\qquad
 \sum_rP(B_r)d_r\le\Delta_T+\eta.
\tag{4}
\]
Call a cell good when \(d_r\le v_-/2\). Markov's inequality for the weighted sum in (4) bounds the total mass of bad cells by \(2(\Delta_T+\eta)/v_-\). On a good cell, \(q_r\ge v_-/2\), and the normalized predictable quadratic variation in \(\mathrm{lem:mourrat\text{-}bounded\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\) is \(V_r^2=Q_T/q_r\). Moreover,
\[
 E_{P_r}|Q_T-q_r|
 \le2E_{P_r}|Q_T-\lambda_T|+2\eta\le2d_r,
 \qquad E_{P_r}|V_r^2-1|\le\frac{4d_r}{v_-}.
\tag{5}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), the suffix contains at least two increments. Otherwise boundedness would give \(Q_T\le L^2/T\), contradicting \(\Delta_T\to0\) and \(\lambda_T\ge v_-\). Its number of increments is at most \(T\), and \(s_r^2\ge Tv_-/2\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:mourrat\text{-}bounded\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\) with \(p=1\) under \(P_r\) yields a constant depending only on \(L,v_-,v_+\) such that
\[
 \sup_z\left|P_r\!\left\{T^{-1/2}\sum_{t>k_T}X_{T,t}\le z\right\}
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{q_r}}\right)\right|
 \le C\left\{\frac{\log T}{\sqrt T}+(d_r+T^{-1})^{1/3}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(\phi(u)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-u^2/2}\) denote the standard normal density. For \(q\ge v_-/2\), differentiation gives
\[
 \sup_z\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial q}
 \Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt q}\right)\right|
 =\sup_u\frac{|u|\phi(u)}{2q}\le C_{v_-}.
\tag{7}
\]
Therefore, on every good cell, (3)--(5) and (7) imply
\[
 \sup_z\left|\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{q_r}}\right)
 -E_{P_r}\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_T}}\right)\right|
 \le C_{v_-}d_r.
\tag{8}
\]
Multiply (6) and (8) by \(P(B_r)\), sum over cells, bound each bad-cell discrepancy by one, and use concavity of \(x^{1/3}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_z\left|P\!\left\{T^{-1/2}\sum_{t>k_T}X_{T,t}\le z\right\}
 -E_P\Phi\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt{\lambda_T}}\right)\right|\\
 &\quad\le C\left\{\frac{\log T}{\sqrt T}
 +(\Delta_T+\eta+T^{-1})^{1/3}+\Delta_T+\eta\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Taking \(\eta=T^{-1}\) proves the assertion. Every constant in (9) depends only on the common \(L,v_-,v_+\), proving uniformity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma turns an early-measurable variance proxy and bounded martingale increments into the uniform marginal mixture approximation used by Theorem 1. \textit{Gap.} Mourrat treats deterministic normalization; the finite-partition adaptation to a random early-measurable variance is supplied here. \textit{Consumer.} Theorem 1 uses it to avoid the invalid stable-label step.

\begin{lemma}[lem:compact-covariance-law-subsequence]\label{lem:compact-covariance-law-subsequence}
Let \(0<a\le b<\infty\), and let \((U_n)\) be random symmetric \(d\)-by-\(d\) matrices satisfying \(aI_d\preceq U_n\preceq bI_d\) almost surely. There are a subsequence \((n_k)\) and a Borel probability measure \(\mu\), supported on \(\mathcal K_{a,b}=\{u=u^\top:aI_d\preceq u\preceq bI_d\}\), such that \(E[f(U_{n_k})]\to\int f(u)\,d\mu(u)\) for every continuous \(f:\mathcal K_{a,b}\to\mathbb R\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Identify a symmetric \(d\)-by-\(d\) matrix with its \(s=d(d+1)/2\) upper-triangular entries. The set
\[
 \mathcal K_{a,b}=\{u=u^\top:aI_d\preceq u\preceq bI_d\}
\]
is closed and bounded in \(\mathbb R^s\), hence compact. For every integer \(r\ge1\), choose a finite Borel partition \(\mathcal P_r\) of \(\mathcal K_{a,b}\) into cells of diameter at most \(2^{-r}\), with \(\mathcal P_{r+1}\) refining \(\mathcal P_r\). Such partitions follow by intersecting each old cell with a finite ball cover and disjointifying in a fixed order.

There are countably many cells in \(\bigcup_r\mathcal P_r\). Successive subsequence selection in \([0,1]\), followed by diagonal selection, gives a subsequence, still denoted \((n_k)\), for which
\[
 \ell(C)=\lim_{k\to\infty}P\{U_{n_k}\in C\}
\tag{1}
\]
exists for every partition cell \(C\). These limits are nonnegative, sum to one on each level, and are consistent under refinement.

Construct the limiting measure directly. Assign to the unique level-zero cell the interval \([0,1)\). Recursively partition the interval assigned to a cell \(C\) into half-open subintervals assigned to its children \(C'\), in a fixed order, with lengths \(\ell(C')\). Consistency makes this possible. Let \(U_r(u)\) be a fixed representative of the level-\(r\) cell whose assigned interval contains \(u\in[0,1)\). Outside the countable set of interval endpoints the cells are nested. Since a level-\(r+1\) representative and its parent representative both lie in the parent cell,
\[
 \|U_{r+1}(u)-U_r(u)\|_2\le2^{-r}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \((U_r(u))_r\) is Cauchy, uniformly away from the irrelevant endpoint choices, and compactness gives a limit \(U(u)\in\mathcal K_{a,b}\). Define \(U\) arbitrarily at the endpoints. Each \(U_r\) is Borel measurable, so \(U\) is Borel measurable. Let \(\mu\) be the pushforward of Lebesgue probability on \([0,1)\) by \(U\).

For continuous \(f:\mathcal K_{a,b}\to\mathbb R\), compactness makes \(f\) uniformly continuous. Write
\[
 \omega_f(h)=\sup\{|f(u)-f(v)|:u,v\in\mathcal K_{a,b},\
 \|u-v\|_2\le h\},\qquad \omega_f(h)\downarrow0.
\tag{3}
\]
For one representative \(u_C\) per level-\(r\) cell,
\[
 \left|E[f(U_{n_k})]-\sum_{C\in\mathcal P_r}
 f(u_C)P\{U_{n_k}\in C\}\right|\le\omega_f(2^{-r}).
\tag{4}
\]
By construction,
\[
 \int_0^1 f(U_r(u))\,du=\sum_{C\in\mathcal P_r}f(u_C)\ell(C).
\tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) implies \(U_r\to U\) uniformly up to a null set, so uniform continuity and (5) give
\[
 \sum_{C\in\mathcal P_r}f(u_C)\ell(C)
 \longrightarrow\int_0^1f(U(u))\,du=\int f(v)\,d\mu(v).
\tag{6}
\]
First let \(k\to\infty\) in the finite sum in (4), using (1), and then let \(r\to\infty\), using (3) and (6). This gives
\[
 E[f(U_{n_k})]\longrightarrow\int f(v)\,d\mu(v).
\]
The pushforward construction supports \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal K_{a,b}\), as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction]\label{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}
There is a bounded two-arm, one-dimensional adaptive IPW-Z experiment targeting the causal arm-mean contrast \(\theta^*=E[Y_t(1)]-E[Y_t(2)]\), with uniform overlap and nonsingular Jacobian, for which \(T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T\ge4\) almost surely but the realized-QV score pivot, and hence its feasible IPW-Z counterpart, has a nonstandard noncentered limit. Specifically, at horizons \(T=2n\), the limit is
\[
 L=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
 +\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8},
\]
where \(Z,W\) are independent \(N(0,1)\) variables, and
\[
 E[L]=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\]
Thus a predictable-QV lower floor and realized-to-predictable QV consistency do not suffice for a Gaussian self-normalized pivot when an early score selects the later variance regime.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We construct the experiment on the even horizons \(T=2n\).  Let \(K=2\), \(d=1\), \(\Theta=[-1,1]\), let the context be constant, and take the fixed potential outcomes
\[
Y_t(1)=Y_t(2)=1\qquad(1\le t\le 2n).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the full-data array is fixed and the only randomness is assignment.  Define
\[
g(1,x,y;\theta)=y-\frac{\theta}{2},
\qquad
g(2,x,y;\theta)=-y-\frac{\theta}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
By the definition of \(M_P\),
\[
M_P(\theta)=g(1,x,1;\theta)+g(2,x,1;\theta)=-\theta.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the unique causal moment root and its Jacobian are
\[
\theta^*=0=E_P[Y_t(1)]-E_P[Y_t(2)],
\qquad H=M_P'(0)=-1.
\tag{4}
\]
This verifies the claimed causal target and nonsingular Jacobian, with \(h_-=1\).  On \(\Theta\), the score in (2) is bounded by \(3/2\), its first derivative is \(-1/2\), and its second derivative is zero.  Thus one may take \(B=3/2\), \(D_1=1/2\), and any positive \(D_2\).  Equation (3) gives the separation modulus \(\kappa(u)=\min\{u,1\}\), while the root has interior radius \(s_\Theta=1\).  With the overlap constant \(\varepsilon=1/4\) used below, choose
\[
0<\rho<\min\{1,1/(16D_2)\};
\tag{5}
\]
then \(\rho<\min\{s_\Theta,\varepsilon h_-/(4D_2)\}\).  Set the numerical tolerances \(r_T\) and \(\delta_T\) in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:ipw\text{-}z\text{-}estimator}\) equal to zero.

Write \(p_t=P(A_t=1\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})\).  For \(t\le n\), set \(p_t=1/2\).  After observing \(S_n\), set, for every \(t>n\),
\[
p_t=
\begin{cases}
1/4,&S_n>0,\\
1/2,&S_n\le0.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
This assignment law is predictable, and conditional on \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) the action is drawn independently of the fixed potential outcomes.  Both arm probabilities are at least \(1/4\), so overlap holds uniformly.

At the root (4), the IPW score is
\[
\xi_t=
\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
-\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}.
\tag{7}
\]
Sequential randomization gives
\[
E_P[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=p_t\frac1{p_t}-(1-p_t)\frac1{1-p_t}=0,
\tag{8}
\]
so \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence.  Its conditional second moment is
\[
v(p_t):=E_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t},
\qquad
v(1/2)=4,
\quad v(1/4)=\frac{16}{3}.
\tag{9}
\]
It follows from the definition of predictable quadratic variation that
\[
\frac1{2n}\langle S\rangle_{2n}
=
\begin{cases}
14/3,&S_n>0,\\
4,&S_n\le0.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
In particular, \((2n)^{-1}\langle S\rangle_{2n}\ge4\) on every assignment path.

We next derive the limiting score law directly.  On a product space generate the first block and, independently of it and of each other, two candidate second blocks with the score laws in (7) for \(p=1/4\) and \(p=1/2\).  If \(U\) is any of these centered, bounded one-step scores and \(\sigma^2=E[U^2]\), the third-order Taylor formula, with its remainder bounded by \(|u|^3E|U|^3/(6n^{3/2})\), gives for each fixed real \(u\)
\[
E[e^{iuU/\sqrt n}]
=1-\frac{u^2\sigma^2}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\]
Taking the \(n\)-th power gives \(\exp(-u^2\sigma^2/2)+o(1)\).  The three blocks are independent and have one-step variances \(4\), \(16/3\), and \(4\), so their joint characteristic function converges pointwise to
\[
\exp\!\left[-\frac12\left(4u_1^2+\frac{16}{3}u_2^2+4u_3^2\right)\right].
\]
This limiting characteristic function is continuous at the origin and is that of three independent centered Gaussian variables.  Hence
\[
\left(
\frac{S_n}{\sqrt n},
\frac{S^{(1/4)}_{2n}-S_n}{\sqrt n},
\frac{S^{(1/2)}_{2n}-S_n}{\sqrt n}
\right)
\Longrightarrow
\left(2Z,\frac4{\sqrt3}W_1,2W_2\right),
\tag{11}
\]
where \(Z,W_1,W_2\) are independent standard normal variables.

The branch-selection map is continuous whenever its first coordinate is nonzero, and \(P(Z=0)=0\).  Applying that map to (11) gives
\[
\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt n}
\Longrightarrow
\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\left(2Z+\frac4{\sqrt3}W_1\right)
+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}(2Z+2W_2).
\tag{12}
\]
If
\[
W=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}W_1+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}W_2,
\]
then \(W\) is standard normal and independent of \(Z\): for bounded measurable \(f,h\), independence of \(Z,W_1,W_2\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E[f(Z)h(W)]
&=E[f(Z)\mathbf1\{Z>0\}]E[h(W_1)]\\
&\quad+E[f(Z)\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}]E[h(W_2)]
=E[f(Z)]E[h(W_1)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

For realized quadratic variation, put
\[
D_t=\xi_t^2-E_P[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
By (7) and overlap, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so \((D_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference sequence with \(|D_t|\le16\).  Orthogonality of distinct martingale differences yields
\[
E_P\!\left[\left\{\frac{[S]_{2n}-\langle S\rangle_{2n}}{n}\right\}^2\right]
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{t=1}^{2n}E_P[D_t^2]
\le\frac{512}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(n^{-1}([S]_{2n}-\langle S\rangle_{2n})\to_P0\).  Combining (10), (12), and (14), with both limiting denominators positive, proves
\[
\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt{[S]_{2n}}}
\Longrightarrow
L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8}.
\tag{15}
\]
The variable \(L\) is integrable.  Equation (13) makes the terms containing \(W\) conditionally mean zero, whereas
\[
E[Z\mathbf1\{Z>0\}]=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}},
\qquad
E[Z\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}]=-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
E[L]=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{16}
\]
Since a standard normal variable is integrable and has mean zero, (16) proves that the law of \(L\) is not \(N(0,1)\).

It remains to compare the feasible IPW--\(Z\) statistic.  Define
\[
U_t=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
+\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t},
\qquad R_T=\sum_{t=1}^TU_t.
\]
Equations (2) and (7) give the exact identity
\[
G_T(\theta)=\frac1T\left(S_T-\frac\theta2R_T\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Moreover, \(E_P[U_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=2\) and \(0<U_t\le4\).  Martingale orthogonality and Chebyshev's inequality therefore give
\[
\frac{R_T}{T}\to_P2,
\qquad
\frac{S_T}{T}\to_P0.
\tag{18}
\]
For example, for every \(\zeta>0\),
\[
P\!\left(\left|\frac{2S_T}{R_T}\right|>\zeta\right)
\le P(R_T/T<1)+P(2|S_T|/T>\zeta)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{19}
\]
Hence the unique affine root \(\vartheta_T=2S_T/R_T\) belongs to \(\Theta\), and indeed to the \(\rho/2\)-ball about zero, with probability tending to one.  On that event the global minimum of \(\lVert G_T(\theta)\rVert_2\) is zero.  Since the numerical tolerance is zero, the preliminary estimator must equal the unique root, \(\mathcal Z_T=\{\vartheta_T\}\), and the selected exact root obeys
\[
\widehat\theta_T=\vartheta_T
\tag{20}
\]
with probability tending to one.  Since \(E_P[S_T^2]=E_P[\langle S\rangle_T]\le(16/3)T\), one has \(S_T/\sqrt T=O_P(1)\).  Equations (18)--(20) give
\[
\sqrt T\,\widehat\theta_T-\frac{S_T}{\sqrt T}
=\left(\frac{2T}{R_T}-1\right)\frac{S_T}{\sqrt T}
\to_P0.
\tag{21}
\]

Differentiating (17) gives
\[
\widehat H_T=-\frac{R_T}{2T}\to_P-1=H.
\tag{22}
\]
On the event in (20), evaluation at the exact root gives
\[
\widehat\xi_t=\xi_t-\frac{\widehat\theta_T}{2}U_t.
\tag{23}
\]
Because \(|\xi_t|\le4\), \(U_t\le4\), and \(|\widehat\theta_T|\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\widehat\Omega_T-\frac{[S]_T}{T}\right|
&\le\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{|\widehat\theta_T|U_t}{2}
\left(2|\xi_t|+\frac{|\widehat\theta_T|U_t}{2}\right)\\
&\le20|\widehat\theta_T|\to_P0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Take \(c=1\) and the constant truncation floor \(\omega_{1,T}=4\) in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:event\text{-}truncated\text{-}rqv\text{-}sandwich}\).  By (22), \(P(\mathcal H_T)\to1\).  On \(\mathcal H_T\), equations (22)--(24) and \([S]_T/T\le16\) imply
\[
\widehat q_T(1)-\frac{[S]_T}{T}\to_P0.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (10), (14), and (25) imply
\[
P\{\widehat q_T(1)\ge2\}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus \(P\{\mathcal E_T(1)\}\to1\), and on that event \(\widehat V_T(1)=\widehat q_T(1)\) in dimension one.  The denominators are bounded away from zero with probability tending to one, so (21) and (25) yield
\[
T_T^{\mathrm{rqv}}(1)-\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{[S]_T}}\to_P0.
\tag{27}
\]
Together with (15), this proves that the feasible IPW--\(Z\) pivot has the same nonstandard limit \(L\).

For the exact smallest-instance check, let \(n=1\).  Enumerating all four assignment paths gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
(A_1,A_2)&P(A_1,A_2)&S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}&\langle S\rangle_2/2&2S_2/R_2\\ \hline
(1,1)&1/8&3/\sqrt5&14/3&2\\
(1,2)&3/8&1/\sqrt{13}&14/3&2/5\\
(2,1)&1/4&0&4&0\\
(2,2)&1/4&-\sqrt2&4&-2
\end{array}
\tag{28}
\]
The two unconstrained roots outside \(\Theta\) invoke the prescribed fallback.  With zero tolerance the four selected estimates are \(1,2/5,0,-1\), whose expectation is \(1/40\ne\theta^*\).  Thus no finite-sample unbiasedness is used.  At two-sided critical value \(1\), exact score-pivot coverage is
\[
P\!\left\{\left|S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}\right|\le1\right\}
=\frac58<2\Phi(1)-1.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (10) and (14) simultaneously verify the predictable-QV floor and realized-to-predictable-QV consistency.  Hence neither property prevents the endogenous variance selection in (6), completing the obstruction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition is the explicit negative witness certifying that lower QV growth and realized-QV consistency are insufficient under endogenous variance selection. \textit{Gap.} It pinpoints the missing temporal separation by giving a bounded two-regime law with a noncentered self-normalized limit. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a concrete diagnostic failure mode for adaptive-trial variance procedures.

\begin{lemma}[lem:early-measurable-scale-self-normalized-martingale-clt]\label{lem:early-measurable-scale-self-normalized-martingale-clt}
Let \((X_{n,t},\mathcal G_{n,t})_{t=1}^{N_n}\) be a scalar martingale-difference triangular array, where \(N_n\) and \(h_n\) are deterministic integers satisfying \(0\le h_n<N_n\), and suppose \(|X_{n,t}|\le L<\infty\) almost surely. Let \(\lambda_n\) be a positive \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable random variable, and let \(\nu_n>0\) be deterministic with \(\lambda_n\ge\nu_n\) almost surely. Define
\[
 S_n=\sum_{t=1}^{N_n}X_{n,t},\qquad
 Q_n=\sum_{t=1}^{N_n}X_{n,t}^2,\qquad
 A_{n,+}^2=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}E[X_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}].
\]
If
\[
 N_n\nu_n\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \frac{h_n}{N_n\nu_n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{in probability},
\]
then
\[
 \frac{Q_n}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{in probability},\qquad
 P(Q_n>0)\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]
All conclusions are uniform over a collection of arrays if \(L\) is common and each displayed convergence premise is uniform over that collection.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(b_n^2=N_n\lambda_n\). For \(t>h_n\), define
\[
 Y_{n,t}=X_{n,t}/b_n,\qquad
 v_{n,t}=E[Y_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}],\qquad
 V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,t}.
\tag{1}
\]
Division is legal because \(\lambda_n\ge\nu_n>0\). Since \(b_n\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable, it is \(\mathcal G_{n,t-1}\)-measurable for \(t>h_n\); hence the suffix is again a martingale-difference array. The increment bound and the first and third hypotheses give
\[
 \max_{t>h_n}|Y_{n,t}|
 \le\epsilon_n:=\frac{L}{\sqrt{N_n\nu_n}}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad V_n=\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
\tag{2}
\]
in probability.

Retain the increment at time \(t>h_n\) only when \(\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{t-1}v_{n,s}\le2\). This indicator is predictable. Denote the retained increment and variance by \(\widetilde Y_{n,t}\) and \(\widetilde v_{n,t}\). Then
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde v_{n,t}\le2+\epsilon_n^2.
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\), Taylor's formula and the martingale-difference property imply
\[
 E[e^{iu\widetilde Y_{n,t}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 =1-\frac{u^2}{2}\widetilde v_{n,t}+r_{n,t},
 \qquad |r_{n,t}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,t}.
\tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
 Z_{n,j}=\exp\!\left\{iu\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde Y_{n,t}
 +\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{t=h_n+1}^j\widetilde v_{n,t}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Using (4) and \(|e^x-1-x|\le x^2e^x/2\) for \(x\ge0\), conditional expectation gives
\[
 \left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
 \le C_u e^{u^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)/2}
 \left(\epsilon_n\widetilde v_{n,j}+\widetilde v_{n,j}^2\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(\widetilde v_{n,j}\le\epsilon_n^2\), summing (6) and using (3) gives \(E[Z_{n,N_n}]\to1\). By (2), stopping changes the suffix only on \(\{V_n>2\}\), whose probability tends to zero. The stopped total variance \(\widetilde V_n=\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde v_{n,t}\) converges in probability to one and is bounded by \(2+\epsilon_n^2\), so
\[
 E\left|1-\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The identity
\[
 e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}
 =e^{iu\sum_t\widetilde Y_{n,t}}
 e^{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2}
\]
together with (6)--(7), followed by removal of \(\{V_n>2\}\), shows that the suffix characteristic function converges to \(e^{-u^2/2}\) for every \(u\).

For completeness, this characteristic-function conclusion determines the limit without an undeclared limit theorem. The stopping in (3) gives uniformly bounded second moments, hence tightness by Chebyshev's inequality. From any subsequence, diagonal selection of the nondecreasing distribution functions at rational arguments, followed by right-continuous extension, produces a further weakly convergent subsequence; tightness makes the limiting distribution have total mass one. If \(Y\) has such a subsequential limit, bounded continuity of \(x\mapsto e^{iux}\) gives \(E[e^{iuY}]=e^{-u^2/2}\). If \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) is independent of \(Y\), then for every \(\sigma>0\) the Gaussian Fourier formula and Fubini's theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E\!\left[\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
 e^{-(x-Y)^2/(2\sigma^2)}\right]
 &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iux}
 e^{-\sigma^2u^2/2}E[e^{iuY}]\,du\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi(1+\sigma^2)}}
 e^{-x^2/(2(1+\sigma^2))}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(Y+\sigma Z\sim N(0,1+\sigma^2)\). Letting \(\sigma\downarrow0\), Chebyshev's inequality gives \(\sigma Z\to0\) in probability, while the displayed normal laws converge pointwise; hence \(Y\sim N(0,1)\). Every subsequential limit is therefore standard normal, proving
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}X_{n,t}/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{9}
\]

The prefix is negligible on this scale. Martingale orthogonality and boundedness give
\[
 E\left(\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\right)^2
 =\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}E[X_{n,t}^2]\le h_nL^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(b_n^2\ge N_n\nu_n\), Chebyshev's inequality and the second hypothesis yield
\[
 b_n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (9)--(11) prove \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

For realized quadratic variation put \(D_{n,t}=Y_{n,t}^2-v_{n,t}\) for \(t>h_n\). These are martingale differences and
\[
 E[D_{n,t}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le E[Y_{n,t}^4\mid\mathcal G_{n,t-1}]
 \le\epsilon_n^2v_{n,t}.
\tag{12}
\]
Under the same predictable stopping rule, martingale orthogonality bounds the second moment of the stopped sum by \(\epsilon_n^2(2+\epsilon_n^2)\). Removing \(\{V_n>2\}\) gives
\[
 \sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{13}
\]
The prefix squares satisfy
\[
 0\le b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2
 \le\frac{h_nL^2}{N_n\nu_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
 =b_n^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{h_n}X_{n,t}^2+V_n
 +\sum_{t=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,t}
 \longrightarrow1
\tag{15}
\]
in probability. Hence \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\). On \(\{Q_n/b_n^2\in[1/2,3/2]\}\), division is legal, and (9)--(11), (15), and continuity of \(x\mapsto x^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}
 =\frac{S_n/b_n}{\sqrt{Q_n/b_n^2}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{16}
\]
Every bound depends only on the common \(L\) and the three displayed premise errors. Applying the argument to any contrary sequence of member arrays proves all asserted uniform conclusions.
\end{proof}

\texttt{Aldous and Eagleson show that a martingale central limit theorem can be obtained directly using stability.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[prop:saco-theorem-5-14-counterexample]\label{prop:saco-theorem-5-14-counterexample}
At every even horizon \(T=2n\), the bounded two-regime score and randomization process of Proposition~\(\text{prop:endogenous-qv-self-normalization-obstruction}\) has an explicit bounded augmentation with a nondegenerate observable-outcome law and a nonzero causal effect that satisfies every assumption explicitly listed in Saco's Theorem~5.14 and also its maintained consistency and no-interference condition. Namely, let \((U_t)_{t=1}^T\) be independent Rademacher variables, independent of the action randomizers, set \(A_t^{\mathrm S}:=\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}\) and \(X_t^{\mathrm S}:=(X_t,U_t)\), and set
\[
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)=\frac32+U_t,
 \qquad
 Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)=\frac12+U_t.
\]
At context value \((x,u)\), set the predictable nuisance regressions to \(\widehat m_{t-1,1}(x,u)=u+1/2\) and \(\widehat m_{t-1,0}(x,u)=u-1/2\). Then the causal target is \(\theta_0=E_P[Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)-Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)]=1\), while the observed outcome obeys \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_t^{\mathrm S}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=1+p_t(1-p_t)\ge1\), where \(p_t:=P(A_t=1\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})\). Saco's centered AIPW increment is nevertheless exactly \(\xi_t\). Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\),
\[
 P\!\left(\xi_t=\frac1{p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right)=p_t,
 \qquad
 P\!\left(\xi_t=-\frac1{1-p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right)=1-p_t,
\]
and hence \(E(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=0\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=1/p_t+1/(1-p_t)\in\{4,16/3\}\). Saco's realized and predictable quadratic variations are therefore \(Q_T=[S]_T\) and \(V_T^2=\langle S\rangle_T\), with \(V_T^2\ge4T\) almost surely. Nevertheless, along \(T=2n\),
\[
 \frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\Longrightarrow
 L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
 +\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8},
\]
where \(Z,W\) are independent \(N(0,1)\) variables and
\[
 E[L]=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\]
The feasible sample-variance Studentized AIPW statistic has the same limit \(L\). Thus the variance-growth-only normal-limit conclusions asserted in Saco's Theorem~5.14 are false as stated, even for bounded nondegenerate outcomes and a nonzero causal effect.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix an even horizon \(T=2n\).  Retain the action process, its predictable arm-one probabilities \(p_t\in\{1/2,1/4\}\), and the numerical score process \((\xi_t)_{t=1}^T\) constructed in Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\).  Extend the probability space by independent Rademacher variables \(U_1,\ldots,U_T\), independent of every action randomizer, with
\[
P(U_t=1)=P(U_t=-1)=\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
This extension leaves the joint law of \((p_t,A_t,\xi_t)_{t=1}^T\) unchanged.

In Saco's binary notation define
\[
A_t^{\mathrm S}=\mathbf1\{A_t=1\},
\qquad \pi_t^{\mathrm S}=p_t,
\qquad X_t^{\mathrm S}=(X_t,U_t),
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)=\frac32+U_t,
\qquad
Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)=\frac12+U_t.
\tag{3}
\]
These potential outcomes depend only on the current treatment.  Consistency gives
\[
Y_t^{\mathrm S}
=A_t^{\mathrm S}Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)+(1-A_t^{\mathrm S})Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)
=\frac12+U_t+A_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus maintained Assumption~3.5 holds.  The triples
\[
(X_t^{\mathrm S},Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0),Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1))
=\left((X_t,U_t),\frac12+U_t,\frac32+U_t\right)
\tag{5}
\]
are independent and identically distributed over \(t\), establishing Assumption~3.6.  The causal target is the fixed contrast of potential-outcome means
\[
\theta_0=E_P[Y_t^{\mathrm S}(1)-Y_t^{\mathrm S}(0)]=1.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(U_t\) and \(A_t^{\mathrm S}\) are conditionally independent given the past, (4) also gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y_t^{\mathrm S}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S})
=1+p_t(1-p_t)\ge1.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm S}\) be the history enlarged by \(U_1,\ldots,U_t\), and use the pre-randomization sigma-field
\[
\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}=\sigma(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S},X_t^{\mathrm S},p_t).
\tag{8}
\]
The adaptive rule makes \(p_t\) measurable with respect to this sigma-field, and the fresh action randomizer gives
\[
P(A_t^{\mathrm S}=1\mid\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S})=p_t=\pi_t^{\mathrm S}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is Assumption~3.2.  Conditional on \(\mathcal G_t^{\mathrm S}\), both potential outcomes in (3) are measurable and the action is freshly randomized, proving Assumption~3.7.  Furthermore,
\[
\frac14\le p_t\le\frac12,
\qquad
\frac12\le1-p_t\le\frac34,
\tag{10}
\]
so Assumption~3.8 holds with overlap constant \(1/4\).

For Assumption~4.8 take the deterministic scored set
\[
\mathcal T_T=\{1,\ldots,T\},
\qquad n_{\mathrm{eff}}=|\mathcal T_T|=T\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{11}
\]
At a context \((x,u)\), choose the fixed nuisance maps
\[
\widehat m_{t-1,1}(x,u)=u+\frac12,
\qquad
\widehat m_{t-1,0}(x,u)=u-\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]
As nonrandom functions these are \(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\)-measurable, proving Assumption~4.5.  The two fourth-moment assumptions hold uniformly because
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E|Y_t^{\mathrm S}(a)|^4=\frac{313}{16},
\qquad
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}E|\widehat m_{t-1,a}(X_t^{\mathrm S})|^4=\frac{41}{16}.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, the first maximum is attained by the values \(1/2,5/2\), while each nuisance has absolute values \(1/2,3/2\).

Substituting (3), (4), and (12) in the AIPW pseudo-outcome gives, pathwise,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\phi_t
&=\frac{A_t^{\mathrm S}}{p_t}\{Y_t^{\mathrm S}-(U_t+1/2)\}
-\frac{1-A_t^{\mathrm S}}{1-p_t}\{Y_t^{\mathrm S}-(U_t-1/2)\}\\
&\quad +(U_t+1/2)-(U_t-1/2)\\
&=\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=1\}}{p_t}
-\frac{\mathbf1\{A_t=2\}}{1-p_t}+1
=\xi_t+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
By (6), the centered AIPW increment is therefore exactly \(\widehat\phi_t-\theta_0=\xi_t\).  Conditional on the enlarged past,
\[
P\!\left(\xi_t=\frac1{p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\right)=p_t,
\qquad
P\!\left(\xi_t=-\frac1{1-p_t}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}\right)=1-p_t.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently
\[
E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]=0,
\qquad
E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]
=\frac1{p_t}+\frac1{1-p_t}\in\left\{4,\frac{16}{3}\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, \(|\xi_t|\le4\), so all required fourth moments are finite.

The centered identity following (14) and Saco's definitions give
\[
S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t=S_T,
\qquad
Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t^2=[S]_T,
\qquad
(V_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2
=\sum_{t=1}^TE[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm S}]
=\langle S\rangle_T.
\tag{17}
\]
Equation (16) gives the pathwise information bound
\[
(V_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S})^2\ge4T=4n_{\mathrm{eff}},
\tag{18}
\]
so Assumption~5.11 holds with \(v_-=4\) and failure probability zero.  Moreover, the realized-to-predictable calculation in the preceding proposition gives
\[
\left|\frac{[S]_T}{\langle S\rangle_T}-1\right|
\le\frac{|[S]_T-\langle S\rangle_T|}{4T}\to_P0.
\tag{19}
\]

Thus every source condition recorded in Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:saco\text{-}pointwise\text{-}rqv\text{-}pivot}\) is satisfied by a law with a nonzero causal effect and conditionally nondegenerate observed outcomes.  Nevertheless, because the augmentation preserves the entire centered-score process, Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\) yields
\[
\frac{S_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}{\sqrt{Q_{\mathcal T_T}^{\mathrm S}}}
=\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{[S]_T}
}\Longrightarrow
L:=\mathbf1\{Z>0\}\frac{2Z+4W/\sqrt3}{\sqrt{28/3}}
+\mathbf1\{Z\le0\}\frac{2Z+2W}{\sqrt8},
\tag{20}
\]
where \(Z,W\) are independent standard normal variables and
\[
E[L]=\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\left(\sqrt{\frac3{28}}-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\ne0.
\tag{21}
\]
Hence \(L\) is not standard normal, refuting the cited score-normality assertion on its stated class.

For the feasible statistic, the sample mean and sample variance satisfy the exact identities
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0=\frac{S_T}{T},
\qquad
(T-1)\widehat V^{\mathrm S}=Q_T-\frac{S_T^2}{T}.
\tag{22}
\]
The convergence in (20) implies \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}=O_P(1)\), and therefore
\[
R_T^{\mathrm S}:=\frac{T\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}{Q_T}
=\frac{T}{T-1}
\left\{1-\frac1T\left(\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right)^2\right\}
\to_P1.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(P(R_T^{\mathrm S}>0)\to1\), and on this event
\[
\frac{\sqrt T(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm S}-\theta_0)}{\sqrt{\widehat V^{\mathrm S}}}
=\frac{S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}}{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(M,\epsilon>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\!\left(
\left|\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right|
\left|\frac1{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}-1\right|>\epsilon
\right)
&\le P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_T}{\sqrt{Q_T}}\right|>M\right)\\
&\quad+P\!\left(\left|\frac1{\sqrt{R_T^{\mathrm S}}}-1\right|>\frac\epsilon M\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Tightness from (20) makes the first term arbitrarily small for large \(M\), and (23) sends the second term to zero.  Hence the difference between (24) and \(S_T/\sqrt{Q_T}\) converges to zero in probability, and (20) implies that the feasible statistic also converges to \(L\).

For the smallest-instance check, take \(T=2\).  The four action paths in the preceding proposition give
\[
P\!\left\{\left|S_2/\sqrt{[S]_2}\right|\le1\right\}=\frac58<2\Phi(1)-1.
\tag{26}
\]
The sixteen combinations obtained by adjoining \((U_1,U_2)\) preserve the centered-score identity (14), so (26) is unchanged, while (6)--(7) certify a nonzero causal effect and positive conditional observed-outcome variance.  This completes the explicit within-class counterexample to both asserted normal limits.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This specialization gives an explicit bounded within-class refutation while preserving the endogenous-QV score process pathwise. At the exact pre-treatment conditioning set it records the two observable-outcome support probabilities, their positive lower bound, and the exact conditional variance; the smallest instance also has a positive-mass failure region and an explicit nonzero coverage defect. \textit{Gap.} The published predictable-QV growth premise allows an early score to choose the later variance regime; neither realized-QV consistency nor a fully nondegenerate conditional observable law erases that selecting score component. \textit{Consumer.} It corrects the direct comparator record with an observable-law-certified nondegenerate counterexample and motivates the early-measurable suffix-QV stabilization imposed by the repaired theorem.

\begin{lemma}[lem:early-measurable-l4-self-normalized-martingale-clt]\label{lem:early-measurable-l4-self-normalized-martingale-clt}
Let \((X_{n,s},\mathcal G_{n,s})_{s=1}^{N_n}\) be a scalar martingale-difference triangular array, where \(N_n\to\infty\), and let \(0\le h_n<N_n\) be deterministic with \(h_n/N_n\to0\).  Suppose that for constants \(C_4<\infty\) and \(\lambda_->0\),
\[
\sup_{n,s}E|X_{n,s}|^4\le C_4,\qquad
\lambda_n\ge\lambda_-\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
where \(\lambda_n\) is positive and \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable.  Define
\[
S_n=\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}X_{n,s},\qquad
Q_n=\sum_{s=1}^{N_n}X_{n,s}^2,\qquad
A_{n,+}^2=\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E[X_{n,s}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}].
\]
If \(A_{n,+}^2/(N_n\lambda_n)\to1\) in probability, then
\[
\frac{Q_n}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1\quad\text{in probability},\qquad
P(Q_n>0)\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|P\left\{\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}\le z\right\}-\Phi(z)\right|\longrightarrow0,
\]
where the ratio is set to zero on \(\{Q_n=0\}\).  Every conclusion is uniform over any collection of arrays for which \(C_4,\lambda_-\), \(h_n/N_n\to0\), and the displayed predictable-QV convergence are uniform.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(b_n=(N_n\lambda_n)^{1/2}\).  This normalization is well defined because \(N_n\ge1\) and \(\lambda_n\ge\lambda_->0\).  For \(s>h_n\), the random variable \(b_n\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,h_n}\)-measurable and hence \(\mathcal G_{n,s-1}\)-measurable.  Consequently
\[
Y_{n,s}:=\frac{X_{n,s}}{b_n},\qquad
v_{n,s}:=E[Y_{n,s}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}]
\tag{1}
\]
defines a martingale-difference suffix array with conditional variances \(v_{n,s}\).  The assumed suffix predictable-QV convergence is exactly
\[
V_n:=\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}v_{n,s}
=\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{2}
\]
The fourth-moment bound and the deterministic lower bound for \(\lambda_n\) imply
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E|Y_{n,s}|^4
\le \frac{C_4}{N_n\lambda_-^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3}
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality and (3) give
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E[v_{n,s}^2]
\le \sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E|Y_{n,s}|^4\longrightarrow0,
\tag{4}
\]
whereas Lyapunov's inequality gives
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}E|Y_{n,s}|^3
\le \frac{C_4^{3/4}}{\lambda_-^{3/2}\sqrt{N_n}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

We next derive the suffix central limit theorem.  Define the predictable truncation
\[
I_{n,s}=\mathbf1\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=h_n+1}^{s-1}v_{n,\ell}\le2,
\ v_{n,s}\le1\right\},\qquad
\widetilde Y_{n,s}=I_{n,s}Y_{n,s},\qquad
\widetilde v_{n,s}=I_{n,s}v_{n,s}.
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(I_{n,s}\) is \(\mathcal G_{n,s-1}\)-measurable, the variables \(\widetilde Y_{n,s}\) are martingale differences.  Their total conditional variance \(\widetilde V_n=\sum_{s>h_n}\widetilde v_{n,s}\) is at most \(3\).  Moreover, if \(V_n\le2\) and \(\max_{s>h_n}v_{n,s}\le1\), no suffix increment is deleted.  Thus (2), (4), the union bound, and Markov's inequality yield
\[
P\{\text{some suffix increment is deleted}\}
\le P(V_n>2)+P\!\left(\max_{s>h_n}v_{n,s}>1\right)
\le P(V_n>2)+\sum_{s>h_n}E[v_{n,s}^2]
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix \(u\in\mathbb R\).  Taylor's formula with third-order remainder, the martingale-difference property, and (6) imply
\[
E[e^{iu\widetilde Y_{n,s}}\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}]
=1-\frac{u^2}{2}\widetilde v_{n,s}+r_{n,s},
\qquad
|r_{n,s}|\le\frac{|u|^3}{6}I_{n,s}
E[|Y_{n,s}|^3\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}].
\tag{8}
\]
For \(j=h_n,\ldots,N_n\), set
\[
Z_{n,j}=\exp\!\left\{iu\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{j}\widetilde Y_{n,s}
+\frac{u^2}{2}\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{j}\widetilde v_{n,s}\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The modulus of every partial compensator is at most \(e^{3u^2/2}\).  From (8), the elementary bound
\( |e^x(1-x)-1|\le C_u x^2 \) for \(0\le x\le u^2/2\), and \(\widetilde v_{n,s}\le1\), conditional expectation in (9) gives
\[
\left|E[Z_{n,j}\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]-Z_{n,j-1}\right|
\le C'_u\left\{\widetilde v_{n,j}^2+
I_{n,j}E[|Y_{n,j}|^3\mid\mathcal G_{n,j-1}]\right\},
\tag{10}
\]
where \(C'_u<\infty\) depends only on \(u\).  Summing expectations in (10), and then applying (4)--(5), proves
\[
E Z_{n,N_n}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (2) and (7) imply \(\widetilde V_n\to1\) in probability.  Since \(0\le\widetilde V_n\le3\), bounded convergence in probability gives
\[
E\left|\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\}-1\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
Using
\[
e^{-u^2/2}Z_{n,N_n}
=\exp\!\left\{iu\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}\widetilde Y_{n,s}\right\}
\exp\{u^2(\widetilde V_n-1)/2\},
\tag{13}
\]
equations (11)--(12) show that the characteristic function of the truncated suffix converges to \(e^{-u^2/2}\).  The characteristic-function criterion and (7) therefore yield
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}\frac{X_{n,s}}{b_n}\ \Rightarrow\ N(0,1).
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to control the prefix and realized quadratic variation.  Because \(b_n^2=N_n\lambda_n\ge N_n\lambda_-\), martingale orthogonality and \(E X_{n,s}^2\le C_4^{1/2}\) give
\[
E\left[\left(\frac1{b_n}\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}\right)^2\right]
\le\frac{1}{N_n\lambda_-}E\left(\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}\right)^2
\le\frac{h_nC_4^{1/2}}{N_n\lambda_-}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
Similarly, Markov's inequality gives
\[
\frac1{b_n^2}\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}^2\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(s>h_n\), let \(D_{n,s}=Y_{n,s}^2-v_{n,s}\).  These are martingale differences and
\[
E D_{n,s}^2
=E\!\left[\operatorname{Var}(Y_{n,s}^2\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1})\right]
\le E Y_{n,s}^4.
\]
Martingale orthogonality and (3) hence imply
\[
\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,s}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in }L^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (2), (16), and (17) yields
\[
\frac{Q_n}{b_n^2}
=\frac1{b_n^2}\sum_{s=1}^{h_n}X_{n,s}^2+V_n
+\sum_{s=h_n+1}^{N_n}D_{n,s}
\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\).  Equations (14)--(15) give \(S_n/b_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), and (18) with Slutsky's identity gives \(S_n/\sqrt{Q_n}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), with the stipulated value on \(\{Q_n=0\}\) asymptotically irrelevant.  Since \(\Phi\) is continuous, convergence in distribution is equivalent here to the asserted convergence uniformly over \(z\in\mathbb R\).

Finally, all estimates above use only the common constants \(C_4\) and \(\lambda_-\), the uniform relation \(h_n/N_n\to0\), and the uniform convergence in (2).  If any conclusion failed uniformly over a stated collection, selecting in each offending row a law whose error stays bounded away from zero would form a triangular array satisfying those same uniform premises and contradict the preceding argument.  This proves every uniform conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:saco-early-stabilized-aipw-pivot]\label{thm:saco-early-stabilized-aipw-pivot}

\begin{proof}
Fix a row and abbreviate \(t=t_{n,s}\), \(\pi=\pi_{n,t}\), and \(M_a=\widehat m_{n,t-1,a}(X_{n,t})\).  By the logging, consistency, conditional treatment-independence, and predictability conditions in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:saco\text{-}early\text{-}stabilized\text{-}aipw\text{-}class}\), conditional on \(\mathcal B_{n,t}\) and the two current potential outcomes,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E[\widehat\phi_{n,t}\mid\mathcal B_{n,t},Y_{n,t}(0),Y_{n,t}(1)]
&=(Y_{n,t}(1)-M_1)-(Y_{n,t}(0)-M_0)+M_1-M_0\\
&=Y_{n,t}(1)-Y_{n,t}(0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The current full-data triple is independent of the past and identically distributed within the row.  Taking conditional expectation of (1) given \(\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\) therefore yields
\[
E[\widehat\phi_{n,t}-\theta_{0,n}\mid\mathcal F_{n,t-1}]=0.
\tag{2}
\]
The scored-order filtration in the definition satisfies \(\mathcal G_{n,s-1}=\mathcal F_{n,t-1}\).  Hence
\[
E[X_{n,s}\mid\mathcal G_{n,s-1}]=0,
\tag{3}
\]
which proves the martingale-difference property in deterministic scored-set order without treating the scored observations as independent.

We next derive the fourth-moment envelope.  Uniform overlap gives the pathwise bound
\[
|\widehat\phi_{n,t}|
\le(1+\epsilon^{-1})
\{|Y_{n,t}(0)|+|Y_{n,t}(1)|+|M_0|+|M_1|\}.
\tag{4}
\]
For nonnegative \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\), convexity gives \((\sum_{j=1}^ru_j)^4\le r^3\sum_{j=1}^ru_j^4\).  Applying this inequality with \(r=4\) to (4) and using the common outcome and fitted-regression envelopes from the class definition gives
\[
E|\widehat\phi_{n,t}|^4
\le64(1+\epsilon^{-1})^4(2C_Y+2C_M).
\tag{5}
\]
Jensen's inequality and \(|x-y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) give
\[
|\theta_{0,n}|^4
\le E|Y_{n,t}(1)-Y_{n,t}(0)|^4
\le16C_Y.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus another application of \(|x-y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) yields
\[
E|X_{n,s}|^4
\le C_X:=512(1+\epsilon^{-1})^4(2C_Y+2C_M)+128C_Y<\infty.
\tag{7}
\]
Every constant in (7) is common to the triangular-array class, and no almost-sure increment bound has been introduced.

Apply Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem:early\text{-}measurable\text{-}l4\text{-}self\text{-}normalized\text{-}martingale\text{-}clt}\) with \(h_n=k_n\), \(\lambda_n=\Lambda_n\), and \(N_n=n_{\mathrm{eff}}\).  Equation (3) supplies its martingale premise, (7) supplies its fourth-moment premise, and Definition~\(\mathrm{def:saco\text{-}early\text{-}stabilized\text{-}aipw\text{-}class}\) supplies \(k_n/N_n\to0\), \(\Lambda_n\ge\lambda_->0\), and the uniform convergence
\[
\frac{A_{n,+}^2}{N_n\Lambda_n}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{8}
\]
The lemma therefore proves, uniformly over the class,
\[
\frac{Q_n}{N_n\Lambda_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
\left|P\left\{\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}\le z\right\}-\Phi(z)\right|
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{10}
\]
where the stipulated value on \(\{Q_n=0\}\) is asymptotically irrelevant because the lemma also gives \(P(Q_n>0)\to1\) uniformly.  Moreover, (8) and \(\Lambda_n\ge\lambda_-\) imply the retained source variance-growth condition with any fixed \(v_-<\lambda_-\) for all sufficiently large \(n\); that source condition is nevertheless recorded explicitly in the class definition.

It remains to pass from realized quadratic variation to the feasible sample variance.  The definitions give the exact identities
\[
\widehat\theta_n-\theta_{0,n}=\frac{S_n}{N_n},
\qquad
(N_n-1)\widehat V_n=Q_n-\frac{S_n^2}{N_n}.
\tag{11}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(0\le S_n^2\le N_nQ_n\).  The uniform limit (10) implies \(S_n/\sqrt{Q_n}=O_P(1)\) uniformly, and hence, on \(\{Q_n>0\}\),
\[
\frac{N_n\widehat V_n}{Q_n}
=\frac{N_n}{N_n-1}
\left\{1-\frac1{N_n}\left(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}\right)^2\right\}
\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability},
\tag{12}
\]
uniformly over the class.  Equations (9) and (12), together with \(\Lambda_n\ge\lambda_->0\), imply \(P(\widehat V_n>0)\to1\) uniformly.  On that event, (11)--(12) imply
\[
Z_n^{\mathrm{feas}}
=\frac{S_n/\sqrt{Q_n}}
{\sqrt{N_n\widehat V_n/Q_n}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{13}
\]
The exceptional event \(\{\widehat V_n=0\}\) has uniformly vanishing probability, so its stipulated value does not alter the limit.  Slutsky's argument applied uniformly to (10) and (12), followed by continuity of \(\Phi\), proves the displayed uniform Kolmogorov conclusion for \(Z_n^{\mathrm{feas}}\).

Finally, let \(z_\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\).  On \(\{\widehat V_n>0\}\), the event \(\{\theta_{0,n}\in\mathcal I_n(\alpha)\}\) is exactly \(\{|Z_n^{\mathrm{feas}}|\le z_\alpha\}\).  Its complement has uniformly vanishing probability, and
\[
\Phi(z_\alpha)-\Phi(-z_\alpha)=1-\alpha.
\tag{14}
\]
The uniform distributional convergence in (13) and (14) prove the asserted Wald coverage.  Thus the ten stated source conditions supply logging, causal centering, moments, and variance growth, while the explicitly intersected early-measurable suffix-QV condition supplies the additional stabilization used in (8); together they deliver the claimed realized-QV and feasible Studentized limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:saco-counterexample-violates-early-stabilization]\label{prop:saco-counterexample-violates-early-stabilization}
In the bounded two-regime AIPW witness of Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:saco\text{-}theorem\text{-}5\text{-}14\text{-}counterexample}\), at horizons \(T=2n\), no deterministic sequence \(k_T=o(T)\) and no positive \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable scale \(\Lambda_T\), even if bounded uniformly away from zero, can satisfy
\[
\frac{\sum_{t=k_T+1}^{T}E[\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]}{T\Lambda_T}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Consequently the witness belongs to Saco's variance-growth-only hypothesis class but cannot belong to the early-stabilized class in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:saco\text{-}early\text{-}stabilized\text{-}aipw\text{-}class}\) for any cutoff \(k_T=o(n_{\mathrm{eff}})\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work under the witness constructed in Propositions~\(\mathrm{prop:endogenous\text{-}qv\text{-}self\text{-}normalization\text{-}obstruction}\) and~\(\mathrm{prop:saco\text{-}theorem\text{-}5\text{-}14\text{-}counterexample}\), and take the even horizons \(T=2n\).  Write \(\mathcal F_t\) for the witness filtration.  During the first block the construction gives independent scores
\[
 P(\xi_t=2)=P(\xi_t=-2)=\frac12,
 \qquad 1\le t\le n,
\tag{1}
\]
and the sign of \(S_n=\sum_{t=1}^n\xi_t\) selects the second-block conditional variance: it is \(16/3\) when \(S_n>0\) and \(4\) when \(S_n\le0\).  The first-block conditional variance is \(4\).  These properties are unchanged by the bounded AIPW augmentation because Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:saco\text{-}theorem\text{-}5\text{-}14\text{-}counterexample}\) identifies its centered AIPW increment pathwise with \(\xi_t\).

Let \(k=k_{2n}\), where \(k_T=o(T)\) is deterministic.  Then \(k/n\to0\), so \(k<n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Define the terminal regime label and its conditional success probability by
\[
 J_n=\mathbf 1\{S_n>0\},
 \qquad
 p_n=P(J_n=1\mid\mathcal F_k).
\tag{2}
\]
We first show that no \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable binary variable can predict \(J_n\) consistently.  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_k\), equation~(1) gives
\[
 S_n=S_k+2R_{n-k},
 \qquad
 R_r=\sum_{i=1}^r\epsilon_i,
\tag{3}
\]
where the \(\epsilon_i\) are conditionally independent symmetric signs.  The maximum point mass of \(R_r\) is bounded by \(C r^{-1/2}\) for a universal finite \(C\).  To verify rather than assume this bound, if \(r=2\ell\), then
\[
 \max_xP(R_{2\ell}=x)
 =2^{-2\ell}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}
 =\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{2j-1}{2j}
 \le\frac1{\sqrt{\ell+1}}.
\tag{4}
\]
The last inequality follows by induction, because
\[
 \frac{2\ell+1}{2\ell+2}
 \le \sqrt{\frac{\ell+1}{\ell+2}}.
\]
For odd \(r=2\ell+1\), either modal probability is \((2\ell+1)/(2\ell+2)\) times the modal probability at \(2\ell\), so the same \(Cr^{-1/2}\) bound follows after enlarging \(C\).

By symmetry, \(|P(R_r>0)-1/2|\) is at most one modal mass.  Moving the threshold in \(P(2R_{n-k}>-S_k\mid\mathcal F_k)\) from zero to \(-S_k\) crosses at most \(1+|S_k|\) lattice atoms.  Hence (3)--(4) imply
\[
 \left|p_n-\frac12\right|
 \le \frac{C(1+|S_k|)}{\sqrt{n-k}}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(s<t\le k\), the martingale-difference property in the witness gives
\[
 E(\xi_s\xi_t)
 =E\{\xi_sE(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})\}=0.
\]
Together with (1), this yields
\[
 E(S_k^2)=\sum_{t=1}^kE(\xi_t^2)=4k,
 \qquad
 E|S_k|\le\sqrt{E(S_k^2)}=2\sqrt{k}.
\tag{6}
\]
Taking expectations in (5) and using \(k/n\to0\) therefore gives
\[
 E\left|p_n-\frac12\right|
 \le \frac{C(1+2\sqrt{k})}{\sqrt{n-k}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(B_n\) is any \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued predictor, conditioning on \(\mathcal F_k\) gives
\[
 P(B_n\ne J_n\mid\mathcal F_k)
 =\mathbf1\{B_n=0\}p_n+
   \mathbf1\{B_n=1\}(1-p_n)
 \ge \min\{p_n,1-p_n\}.
\]
Consequently, by (7),
\[
 P(B_n\ne J_n)
 \ge E\min\{p_n,1-p_n\}
 =\frac12-E\left|p_n-\frac12\right|
 \longrightarrow\frac12.
\tag{8}
\]

We now calculate the suffix predictable quadratic variation exactly.  There are \(n-k\) retained first-block increments, each with conditional variance \(4\), and \(n\) second-block increments, each with conditional variance \(16/3\) on \(\{J_n=1\}\) and \(4\) on \(\{J_n=0\}\).  Thus
\[
 q_n:=\frac1{2n}\sum_{t=k+1}^{2n}
 E(\xi_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{14}{3}-\frac{2k}{n},&J_n=1,\\[5pt]
 \displaystyle4-\frac{2k}{n},&J_n=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
The two possible values in (9) differ by \(2/3\), and \(0<q_n<5\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Suppose, for contradiction, that there were positive \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable random variables \(\Lambda_{2n}\) such that the stabilization displayed in the proposition held.  By the definition of \(q_n\), that stabilization is precisely
\[
 \frac{q_n}{\Lambda_{2n}}\longrightarrow_P1.
\tag{10}
\]
On the event \(\{|q_n/\Lambda_{2n}-1|<1/2\}\), positivity and \(q_n<5\) imply \(\Lambda_{2n}<2q_n<10\).  On that event,
\[
 |\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|
 =\Lambda_{2n}\left|\frac{q_n}{\Lambda_{2n}}-1\right|
 \le10\left|\frac{q_n}{\Lambda_{2n}}-1\right|.
\tag{11}
\]
Equation~(10) makes the event in (11) have probability tending to one, so (11) proves
\[
 |\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|\longrightarrow_P0.
\tag{12}
\]
Define the \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable binary predictor
\[
 B_n=\mathbf1\left\{\Lambda_{2n}>
 \frac{13}{3}-\frac{2k}{n}\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The threshold in (13) is the midpoint of the two values in (9).  Therefore
\[
 \{|\Lambda_{2n}-q_n|<1/3\}\subseteq\{B_n=J_n\}.
\]
Equation~(12) would then give
\[
 P(B_n\ne J_n)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{14}
\]
contradicting the lower limit \(1/2\) in (8).  Thus no positive \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable scale can satisfy the claimed suffix-QV stabilization for any deterministic \(k_T=o(T)\).  Since the argument imposed no upper or lower deterministic bound on \(\Lambda_T\), it applies in particular when \(\Lambda_T\) is uniformly bounded away from zero.

Finally, Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop:saco\text{-}theorem\text{-}5\text{-}14\text{-}counterexample}\) already verifies the variance-growth-only assumptions for this witness.  In its Saco specialization the scored set is \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\), so \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=T\), and the ordered scored-set filtration in Definition~\(\mathrm{def:saco\text{-}early\text{-}stabilized\text{-}aipw\text{-}class}\) satisfies \(\mathcal G_{T,k}=\mathcal F_k\).  Hence \(k_T=o(n_{\mathrm{eff}})\) is exactly the regime just ruled out, and the witness cannot belong to that early-stabilized intersection class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition certifies that early QV stabilization is a substantive temporal restriction, not a relabeling of Saco's predictable-QV growth condition. \textit{Gap.} The variance-regime selector depends on a first-block sign that no sublinear early history can predict consistently. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the matching obstruction that separates the repaired intersection class from the published variance-growth-only class.

\begin{theorem}[thm:guoxu-normalized-inclusion-obstruction]\label{thm:guoxu-normalized-inclusion-obstruction}

 \begin{cases}

\begin{proof}
Fix an initialized law \(P_{\eta _0}\).  Write \(C_{t,a}=\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}\), and retain \(p=99/100\) and \(q=1/100\).  We first establish the growth and noise facts needed below directly from Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-recursion}\).

For each arm, conditional on the pre-assignment information \(\sigma(\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t)\),
\[
 E(C_{t,a}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t)\ge q.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 E(C_{t,a}X_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 \ge qE(X_t^2)=\frac{17q}{2}=\frac{17}{200}.
\]
The centered summands in either display are bounded martingale differences.  We record an elementary argument for the strong law that will also be used below.  If \((Z_s)\) is a scalar martingale-difference sequence with \(|Z_s|\le L\), stopping the martingale \(M_k=\sum_{s=1}^kZ_s\) at its first exit from \([-x,x]\), expanding the square, and using conditional mean zero gives
\[
 P\!\left(\max_{k\le n}|M_k|>x\right)
 \le \frac{E(M_n^2)}{x^2}
 =\frac{\sum_{s=1}^nE(Z_s^2)}{x^2}
 \le \frac{nL^2}{x^2}.
\]
Taking \(n=2^{\ell+1}\) and \(x=\epsilon 2^\ell\), the resulting probabilities are summable in \(\ell\).  Since the probability of their limsup is at most the tail of this summable series, \(M_t/t\to0\) almost surely.  Applying this argument to \(C_{t,a}X_t^2-E(C_{t,a}X_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})\) yields, simultaneously for \(a=1,2\),
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_{t,a}}t\ge\frac{17}{200},
 \qquad
 D_{t,a}\ge\frac{17}{400}t\quad\hbox{eventually almost surely}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=D_{t,a}^{-1}\le\frac{400}{17t}
 \quad\hbox{eventually almost surely},
\]
which proves \(q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=O(t^{-1})\).

The sufficient-statistic updates in Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-recursion}\) give, coordinate by coordinate,
\[
 \Psi_{t+1}
 =\frac{t}{t+1}\Psi_t+\frac1{t+1}U_{t+1},
 \qquad
 \Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_t=\frac1{t+1}(U_{t+1}-\Psi_t).
\]
This is the asserted exact stochastic-approximation recursion.  To compute its drift, let
\[
 u_t=P(A_{t+1}=1\mid X_{t+1}=1,\mathcal F_t),
 \qquad
 v_t=P(A_{t+1}=1\mid X_{t+1}=-4,\mathcal F_t).
\]
Centeredness and independence of the Rademacher innovations, together with equiprobability of the two contexts, imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E(C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_t)&=\frac{u_t+16v_t}{2},\\
 E(C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_t)&=\frac{17-u_t-16v_t}{2},\\
 E(C_{t+1,1}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(1)\mid\mathcal F_t)&=\frac{u_t-4v_t}{4},\\
 E(C_{t+1,2}X_{t+1}Y_{t+1}(2)\mid\mathcal F_t)&=\frac{-3-u_t+4v_t}{24}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus, with \(M_{t+1}=U_{t+1}-E(U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t)\),
\[
 E(U_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t)=h(u_t,v_t),
 \qquad
 \Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_t
 =\frac1{t+1}\{h(u_t,v_t)-\Psi_t+M_{t+1}\}.
\]
This also verifies the finite conditional law displayed in Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-dynamics-handle}\), without assigning any special numerical value to \(\sigma_1\) or \(\sigma_2\).

The vector \(U_{t+1}\) is bounded by a deterministic constant depending only on the fixed \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\); hence so is \(M_{t+1}\).  Applying the preceding stopped-square argument to \(\sum_{s=j}^k(s+1)^{-1}M_{s+1}^{(i)}\), now with terminal variance bounded by \(C_i\sum_{s=j}^{\infty}(s+1)^{-2}\), gives
\[
 P_{\eta_0}\!\left(
 \sup_{k\ge 2^\ell}
 \left|\sum_{s=2^\ell}^{k}\frac{M_{s+1}^{(i)}}{s+1}\right|>\epsilon
 \right)
 \le \frac{C_i}{\epsilon^2}2^{-\ell}.
\]
The union bound over \(\ell\ge L\) proves exactly the dyadic tail limit in Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-dynamics-handle}\).  The same bounded-martingale argument, without weights, gives
\[
 \frac1t\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}M_{s+1}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{almost surely}.
\]

The lower Gram bound above and the deterministic bounds \(X_t^2\le16\) and
\[
 |X_tY_t(a)|\le4\max\left\{\frac12+\sigma_1,\frac1{12}+\sigma_2\right\}
\]
show that, eventually almost surely,
\[
 \Psi_t\in\mathsf K(C_{\mathrm{GX}},c_{\mathrm{GX}}),
 \quad
 c_{\mathrm{GX}}=\frac{17}{400},
 \quad
 C_{\mathrm{GX}}=\max\left\{17,
 1+4\max\left\{\frac12+\sigma_1,\frac1{12}+\sigma_2\right\}\right\}.
\]
In particular the path and all sufficiently late affine interpolation segments are in a fixed compact convex carrier.

We next derive the four-mode graph.  Put
\[
 \beta_{t,a}=\frac{N_{t,a}}{D_{t,a}},
 \quad
 \Delta_t=\beta_{t,1}-\beta_{t,2}=\Delta(\Psi_t),
 \quad
 a_t=\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}}-\sqrt{q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}}.
\]
The deterministic tie rule in Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-recursion}\) says that arm \(1\) maximizes the index at context \(1\) exactly when \(\Delta_t\ge-a_t\), and it maximizes at context \(-4\) exactly when \(\Delta_t\le a_t\).  Therefore \((u_t,v_t)\) is one of
\[
 (p,q),\qquad(q,p),\qquad(p,p),\qquad(q,q).
\]
Away from \(\Delta=0\), the first two modes are selected in the limit.  At \(\Delta=0\), if \(d_1<d_2\), then \(q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1}>q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2}\), so the same-arm mode is \((p,p)\); if \(d_1>d_2\), it is \((q,q)\).  At \(d_1=d_2\), all four modes occur in the local closed convexification.  Since on the carrier
\[
 q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=\frac1{td_a},
 \qquad
 |a_t|\le \frac1{\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{GX}}t}},
\]
all modes other than the appropriate separated one are confined to an \(O(t^{-1/2})\) neighborhood of the switching graph.  Hence the conditional drift belongs to
\[
 F_{C_{\mathrm{GX}},c_{\mathrm{GX}}}^{\,c_0t^{-1/2}}(\Psi_t)
\]
for a finite deterministic \(c_0\), where \(F_{C_{\mathrm{GX}},c_{\mathrm{GX}}}\) is precisely the five-case graph in the theorem.

Let \(s_t=\sum_{j=1}^t(j+1)^{-1}\), and linearly interpolate \(\Psi_t\) at the times \(s_t\).  On the interval \([s_t,s_{t+1})\), its derivative is \(h(u_t,v_t)-\Psi_t+M_{t+1}\).  The interpolation error is \(O((t+1)^{-1})\), the graph-enlargement error is \(O(t^{-1/2})\), and the integral of the noise over every late bounded time window vanishes by the displayed dyadic tail estimate.  Thus the interpolation is a bounded perturbed solution of the inclusion on the compact carrier.

For literal compatibility with the Euclidean-space hypotheses of Lemma~\(\textnormal{lem:bhs-perturbed-limit-set}\), let \(P_{\mathsf K}\) be metric projection onto the carrier and extend the relative graph by
\[
 \widetilde F(x)=
 \left\{f+P_{\mathsf K}x-x:f\in
 F_{C_{\mathrm{GX}},c_{\mathrm{GX}}}(P_{\mathsf K}x)\right\}.
\]
Continuity of \(P_{\mathsf K}\) and the relative closed-graph property in Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-dynamics-handle}\) show that \(\widetilde F\) has closed graph and nonempty compact convex values.  It has linear growth, agrees with \(F\) on the carrier, and the late interpolation is a perturbed solution for it.  Lemma~\(\textnormal{lem:bhs-perturbed-limit-set}\) therefore makes this interpolation an asymptotic pseudotrajectory and makes every one of its limit sets internally chain transitive.

Now
\[
 \mathcal C=h([q,p]^2)
 =\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1,h_2\}
\]
is a compact parallelogram, because \(h\) is affine and injective.  Every \(f\in\widetilde F(x)\) has the form \(y-x\) for some \(y\in\mathcal C\).  If \(z=P_{\mathcal C}x\), the metric-projection inequality gives
\[
 \left\langle x-z,y-x\right\rangle
 =\left\langle x-z,y-z\right\rangle-\|x-z\|_2^2
 \le-\|x-z\|_2^2.
\]
Thus \(x\mapsto\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal C)^2/2\) is a strict continuous Lyapunov function off \(\mathcal C\), and its value set on \(\mathcal C\) is \(\{0\}\).  Lemma~\(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\) implies that every internally chain-transitive limit set is contained in \(\mathcal C\).  The slope map
\[
 \varphi(d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2)=(n_1/d_1,n_2/d_2)
\]
is continuous on the carrier.  Compactness then shows in both directions, by taking convergent subsequences, that the slope limit set is exactly \(\varphi(L)\) when \(L\) is the normalized-state limit set.

We next analyze the counterfactual smaller graph \(F^0\) obtained by retaining only \(h_+-\psi\), \(h_--\psi\), and their convex hull on \(\Delta=0\).  The same distance Lyapunov argument first confines each internally chain-transitive set to \(\mathcal C\).  Because \(h\) is an affine injection, write a point of \(\mathcal C\) uniquely as \(h(u,v)\).  In these coordinates every velocity of the smaller graph is
\[
 (\dot u,\dot v)=(w-u,1-w-v),\qquad w\in[q,p],
\]
with \(w=p\) when \(\Delta>0\), \(w=q\) when \(\Delta<0\), and an interval of choices only on \(\Delta=0\).  Hence, for \(s=u+v\),
\[
 \dot s=1-s,
 \qquad
 \frac{d}{dt}\frac{(s-1)^2}{2}=-(s-1)^2.
\]
Lemma~\(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\) therefore confines an internally chain-transitive set further to \(v=1-u\).

Direct cross-multiplication, whose denominators are positive on \([q,p]^2\), gives
\[
 \Delta(h(u,v))
 =\frac{-5\{u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v\}}
 {12(u+16v)(17-u-16v)}.
\]
On \(v=1-u\), the numerator of this fraction is
\[
 5(75u^2-47u-8).
\]
The quadratic has one negative root and the single root
\[
 u_0=\frac{47+\sqrt{4609}}{150}
\]
in \([q,p]\).  Thus the induced scalar inclusion on \(v=1-u\) has
\[
 \dot u=q-u<0\quad(q<u<u_0),
 \qquad
 \dot u=p-u>0\quad(u_0<u<p),
\]
and at \(u_0\) it contains the zero velocity.  The continuous function \(-|u-u_0|\) is nonincreasing along every solution and strictly decreases outside \(\{q,u_0,p\}\).  A second application of Lemma~\(\textnormal{lem:bhs-lyapunov-ict}\), on this invariant line, puts every internally chain-transitive set inside those three equilibria.  A chain-transitive subset of a finite set of separated equilibria is a singleton.  The three normalized equilibria are consequently
\[
 h_-=h(q,p),\qquad h(u_0,1-u_0),\qquad h_+=h(p,q).
\]
At the middle one, substitution into either slope ratio gives
\[
 \frac{N_1}{D_1}=\frac{N_2}{D_2}
 =\frac{\sqrt{4609}-91}{1224}=b_0.
\]
This proves the conditional three-singleton assertion for the smaller graph.

The correct graph is different.  Solving the displayed equal-slope polynomial for its lower \(v\)-root gives
\[
 v(u)=\frac{3u+18-\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}{32}.
\]
For \(u\in[u_0,p]\), its radicand is positive and
\[
 v'(u)=\frac3{32}-
 \frac{50u-228}{64\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}>0.
\]
Moreover \(v(u_0)=1-u_0\), so \(u+v(u)\ge1\), while
\[
 q<v(u)\le v(p)<\frac{21}{32}<p.
\]
Finally,
\[
 \{25u^2-228u+324\}-(5u+1)^2=323-238u>0,
\]
and hence
\[
 u+16v(u)=\frac{5u+18-\sqrt{25u^2-228u+324}}2<\frac{17}{2}.
\]
The last inequality is exactly the first-Gram condition \(d_1<d_2\).  The inequalities \(u,v(u)\in[q,p]\) and \(u+v(u)\ge1\) say that
\[
 (u,v(u))\in\operatorname{co}\{(p,q),(q,p),(p,p)\}.
\]
Affineness of \(h\), the equal-slope equation, and the \(d_1<d_2\) branch of the correct graph now give
\[
 h(u,v(u))\in\operatorname{co}\{h_+,h_-,h_1\},
 \qquad
 0\in F(h(u,v(u))).
\]
Every such singleton is internally chain transitive.  The interval \([u_0,p]\) is nondegenerate and \(h\) is injective, so this family is uncountable.  At \(u=p\), direct simplification yields
\[
 v(p)=\frac{2097}{3200}-\frac{3\sqrt{5457}}{640}=v_*.
\]
Since \(v_*\notin\{q,p,1-u_0\}\), injectivity of \(h\) makes \(h(p,v_*)\) distinct from \(h_-\), \(h(u_0,1-u_0)\), and \(h_+\).  This proves the advertised obstruction.

We now return to the actual recursion.  On \(\mathcal L_+\), there are random \(g>0\) and \(t_0<\infty\) such that \(\Delta_t\ge g\) for \(t\ge t_0\).  The already proved bound \(q^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,a}=O(t^{-1})\) makes \(|a_t|<g\) eventually.  The two exact index inequalities above then give \((u_t,v_t)=(p,q)\) eventually.  The exact averaging identity and the martingale strong law give
\[
 \Psi_t-h_+
 =\frac{O(1)}t+\frac1t\sum_{s=t_0}^{t-1}M_{s+1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking ratios of the positive Gram coordinates yields
\[
 (\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},
   \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow
 \left(\frac{19}{46},-\frac{79}{3804}\right).
\]
The same argument on \(\mathcal L_-\) gives eventual mode \((q,p)\), convergence \(\Psi_t\to h_-\), and
\[
 (\widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,1},
   \widehat\beta^{\mathrm{GX}}_{t,2})
 \longrightarrow
 \left(-\frac{79}{634},\frac{19}{276}\right).
\]
In each case convergence of the slope vector implies convergence to zero of its pathwise Cesaro distance from the displayed limit.

It remains to prove that neither separated event is a null artifact.  We give a uniform finite-forcing and trapping argument.  By continuity of the slope map at \(h_+\), choose \(r_+>0\), \(g_+>0\), and \(d_+>0\) such that
\[
 \|\psi-h_+\|_2\le r_+
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_1,d_2\ge d_+,qquad \Delta(\psi)\ge g_+.
\]
Choose a multiple \(N=400\ell\) so large that
\[
 \frac2N\le\frac{r_+}{4},
 \qquad
 (Nd_+)^{-1/2}<g_+,
\]
and large enough for the martingale bound below.  Prescribe a length-\(N\) block with the following context and action counts:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &A=1&A=2\\ \hline
 X=1&198\ell&2\ell\\
 X=-4&2\ell&198\ell
 \end{array}
\]
and, within each cell, prescribe equally many \(+1\) and \(-1\) selected-arm Rademacher innovations.  The empirical average of the resulting \(U_t\)'s is exactly \(h_+\), for every fixed \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2>0\).  Since every context has probability \(1/2\), every prescribed action has conditional probability at least \(q\), and every prescribed selected-arm sign has conditional probability \(1/2\), this entry event \(E_N^+\) satisfies
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}
 P_{\eta_0}(E_N^+)\ge(q/4)^N>0.
\]
On it, using \(|\eta_{0,a}|\le1\),
\[
 \Psi_N=h_++\frac1N(1,1,\eta_{0,1},\eta_{0,2}),
 \qquad
 \|\Psi_N-h_+\|_2\le\frac2N\le\frac{r_+}{4}.
\]

Let \(\tau\) be the first \(n\ge N\) for which \(\|\Psi_n-h_+\|_2>r_+\), and stop the post-\(N\) martingale differences at \(\tau\).  Before \(\tau\), the lower Gram bound in the defining ball and the choice of \(N\) make the bonus difference smaller than \(g_+\), so the exact index inequalities force mode \((p,q)\) and conditional mean \(h_+\).  For a deterministic bound \(L_+<\infty\) on these stopped increments, the stopped-square inequality on successive dyadic blocks and a union bound over four coordinates give a constant \(C_+<\infty\) such that, conditionally on \(\mathcal F_N\),
\[
 P\!\left(
 \sup_{n\ge N}\left\|
 \frac1n\sum_{s=N}^{n-1}
 \mathbf1\{s<\tau\}(U_{s+1}-h_+)
 \right\|_2>\frac{r_+}{4}
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal F_N\right)
 \le\frac{C_+}{Nr_+^2}.
\]
Indeed, on \(2^jN\le n<2^{j+1}N\), the crossing threshold is at least \(2^jNr_+/4\), whereas the stopped second moment is at most a constant times \(2^{j+1}N\); summation over \(j\ge0\) gives the displayed order.  Enlarge \(N\), still keeping it a multiple of \(400\), so that the right side is at most \(1/2\).  On the complementary tail event, for every \(n\le\tau\),
\[
 \Psi_n-h_+
 =\frac{N}{n}(\Psi_N-h_+)
 +\frac1n\sum_{s=N}^{n-1}(U_{s+1}-h_+),
 \qquad
 \|\Psi_n-h_+\|_2\le\frac{r_+}{2}.
\]
This contradicts a finite exit, so \(\tau=\infty\).  The martingale strong law then gives \(\Psi_n\to h_+\), hence \(\mathcal L_+\).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}
 P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_+)
 \ge\frac12(q/4)^N>0.
\]
Repeating the construction at \(h_-\), with the counts \(198\ell\) and \(2\ell\) interchanged in each context row, gives a possibly different finite multiple \(N_-\) and
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}}
 P_{\eta_0}(\mathcal L_-)
 \ge\frac12(q/4)^{N_-}>0.
\]
Thus both separated events have probabilities bounded away from zero uniformly over the prescribed compact set of initial slopes.

Finally, define the empirical conditional arm-\(1\) rates
\[
 \bar u_t=\frac1t\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}u_s,
 \qquad
 \bar v_t=\frac1t\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}v_s.
\]
Because \(h\) is affine, the exact recursion and the martingale strong law imply
\[
 \Psi_t-h(\bar u_t,\bar v_t)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{almost surely}.
\]
Along any common subsequence on which \((\bar u_t,\bar v_t)\to(u,v)\) and both slope coordinates converge to the same value, positivity of the two limiting Gram coordinates permits cross-multiplication.  The earlier formula for \(\Delta(h(u,v))\) then yields the necessary relation
\[
 u^2+12uv-21u-64v^2+72v=0.
\]
To see exactly why \(v=1-u\) is an additional condition, let \(\lambda_{1,t}\) and \(\lambda_{2,t}\) be the empirical occupation proportions of the same-arm conditional modes \((p,p)\) and \((q,q)\).  Since the other two modes have coordinate sum one,
\[
 \bar u_t+\bar v_t-1=(p-q)(\lambda_{1,t}-\lambda_{2,t}).
\]
Separate exclusion of both same-arm switching-layer occupations implies \(\bar u_t+\bar v_t\to1\); the exact recursion and the martingale estimates above do not imply that exclusion.  Indeed, the continuum \((u,v(u))\) just constructed solves the equal-slope equation while satisfying \(u+v(u)>1\) for \(u>u_0\).

The proved inclusion statement therefore does not establish that the actual recursion selects a finite tail state, nor that switching-surface convergence has probability zero.  The trapping argument supplies positive mass for each separated event but does not show that their union has probability one.  It also contains no uniform hitting-or-escape estimate from which the compact-initial-set sequences \(r_T(K_0)\) and \(\delta_T(K_0)\) in Definition~\(\textnormal{def:guoxu-dynamics-handle}\) could be obtained.  Since finite tail selection and such a uniform attraction modulus are required separately for membership in \(\mathfrak P_T\), no such membership, and hence no inference-theorem applicability, follows from this dynamics result.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem resolves the standard differential-inclusion route exactly: it derives the normalized recursion, certifies the correct closed graph, proves the separated limits, and exhibits the continuum of extra switching equilibria that blocks a three-candidate ICT classification. \textit{Gap.} The differential-inclusion limit set need not be one of three candidates because the vanishing UCB bonus produces same-arm modes inside the shrinking switching layer. The exact recursion, relative four-mode graph, and coordinatewise martingale-tail limit do not establish actual selection within that layer, zero probability of switching-surface convergence, finite tail selection, or the separately named compact-initial-set attraction predicate. The latter remains unasserted, without imposing a particular quantitative-hitting or quantitative-escape method. \textit{Consumer.} The certificate tells later inference work precisely why the bounded numerical analogue cannot yet be placed in the finite-attractor class and which switching-layer estimate would be needed to do so.

\section{Interpretation}

A same-path variance estimate removes random basin scale only when the numerator is asymptotically Gaussian conditional on information that already fixes the relevant suffix scale. The early filtration boundary supplies exactly that separation. Realized QV can consistently estimate the variance ultimately selected by a path and still fail to produce a centered Gaussian statistic if the selecting early score remains a nonnegligible part of the numerator. In the finite-attractor subclass, the Cesaro attraction modulus makes an early basin proxy correct with high probability, so the scale is fixed early enough and studentization is valid. For a prespecified HeartSteps-style causal excursion contrast, weak information in orthogonal score directions is harmless; only the projected information condition is required.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The two slopes do not form a Markov state because clipped-UCB decisions also depend on the inverse ridge Gram coordinates. The dynamics question therefore concerns the slope projection of the augmented chain \(Z^{\mathrm{GX}}_t\) on \(\mathsf E_{\mathrm{GX}}\); \(\mathsf K_{\mathrm{GX}}\) indexes only its initial slopes and is not an invariant chain state space. The normalized sufficient-statistic recursion has an explicit one-step finite law, bounded martingale increment, coordinatewise dyadic tail limit, and a four-mode graph that is closed relative to an explicit eventual compact carrier. These reduction conditions still do not prove finite projected tail selection or the compact-initial-set finite-attractor/Cesaro predicate. That predicate now explicitly names its deterministic targets, terminal selector, selection event, uniform probability inequality, and sequences \(r_T(K_0),\delta_T(K_0)\to0\), but it is unasserted and is not part of the reduction conditions. The unresolved issue is actual occupation and selection in the shrinking switching layer; the note does not assume a quantitative-hitting mechanism, a quantitative-escape mechanism, or an arbitrary auxiliary vanishing sequence. The bounded recursion also has no separately specified causal-score structure, so it is not claimed to belong to \(\mathfrak P_T\) and Theorems 1--2 are not claimed to apply. Optional Gaussian moment-extension subquestion: after the bounded-model dynamics are classified, determine whether the published Gaussian-noise recursion admits analogous dynamics under suitable moment controls. This optional subquestion is not a premise, deliverable, theorem, or part of the bounded-model dynamics question. Correct coverage at one isolated nominal level also does not identify the scale mixture: two unequal basin variances can be paired with a deterministic scale whose symmetric interval has the desired probability at that one level. Theorem 3 deliberately requires every nominal level.

\section{Honest scope}

The project establishes a field-tier inference frontier conditional on membership in the finite-attractor class, whose uniform attraction moduli satisfy \(r_T+\delta_T\to0\). Its separate early-stabilized projected class requires only \(T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)\to0\) for the numerical root tolerance and directly assumes directional predictable-QV growth. Saco is the only direct strict-tightening comparator; the absence of a second direct strict frontier caps the contribution at field tier, which is the requested floor and achieved target. Guo--Xu, Shen and coauthors, Han, Leiner--Dunn--Ramdas, Borkar, and the remaining cited papers serve as informed-by comparators, ingredients, close motivation, or orthogonal regimes. The project treats a growing-horizon superpopulation adaptive experiment with a known randomization rule, not a pure fixed-finite-population randomization theorem. The bounded two-basin construction is solely an elementary, non-novel existence witness for strict logical separation between contrastwise and matrixwise invariance. It is not evidence that either the specified bounded Rademacher analogue or the published Gaussian no-intercept Guo--Xu recursion satisfies the finite-attractor, attraction, or common-envelope conditions. The open-ended question concerns only the slope-projected dynamics of the augmented bounded chain and does not assert membership in \(\mathfrak P_T\) or applicability of Theorems 1--2. Any future inference theorem must separately specify its causal score, parameter space, unique target root, nonzero target contrast, induced scalar target, and every class assumption. The Gaussian moment extension remains optional and open. When \(m=1\), \(J=1\), \(V_J=V_1\) is deterministic, Theorem 1 is ordinary asymptotic normality, Theorem 2 is ordinary realized-QV studentization, and Theorems 3--4 allow \(\Sigma=V_1\).
