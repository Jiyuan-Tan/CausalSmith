Write \(M_0=KB^2/\varepsilon\) and \(L_0=KB^2L_\Pi/\varepsilon^2\).  All constants below depend only on the common constants defining \(\mathfrak P_T\), and not on \(T\) or on its member law \(P\).

First, sequential randomization and the independence of \(W_t\) from \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) give, at \(\theta^*\),
\[
 E_P(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 =E_P\!\left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\right]
 =M_P(\theta^*)=0.
\]
Thus \((\xi_t,\mathcal F_t)\) is a martingale-difference array.  The same conditioning calculation gives
\[
 E_P(\xi_t\xi_t^\top\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 =\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}),
 \qquad
 \langle S\rangle_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\Gamma_P(\eta_{t-1}).                                    \tag{1}
\]
Only one treatment indicator is nonzero, so uniform overlap and the bounded-score assumption imply \(\|\xi_t\|_2\le B/\varepsilon\).  Moreover,
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{E_P\|g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_2^2}{\varepsilon}
 \le M_0.                                                                  \tag{2}
\]
For \(u,v\ge\varepsilon\), \(|u^{-1}-v^{-1}|\le |u-v|/\varepsilon^2\).  Hence the policy Lipschitz condition gives
\[
 \|\Gamma_P(\eta)-\Gamma_P(\eta')\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_0\|\eta-\eta'\|_2.                                                \tag{3}
\]
On the event in the uniform attraction assumption, (1)--(3) yield
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_0r_T.
\]
On its complement the left side is at most \(2M_0\).  Because that complement has probability at most \(\delta_T\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}E_P\left\|T^{-1}\langle S\rangle_T-\Omega_J\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le L_0r_T+2M_0\delta_T\le C(r_T+\delta_T).                              \tag{4}
\]
Here finite-tail selection and the covariance definition give \(\Gamma_P(\bar\eta_J)=\Omega_J\), \(\Omega_\infty=\Omega_J\), and \(V_\infty=V_J\) almost surely.

Differentiation under the bounded derivative envelope gives
\[
 \|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_P\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\|\partial_\theta g(a,X_t,Y_t(a);\theta^*)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le KD_1.                                                                 \tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) evaluated at an attractor gives \(\Omega_j\preceq M_0I_d\).  Basin nondegeneracy says \(\Omega_J\succeq\omega_-I_d\) almost surely.  Since \(P(J=j)\ge p_->0\), failure of \(\Omega_j\succeq\omega_-I_d\) for any \(j\) would make basin nondegeneracy fail on an event of positive probability.  Therefore
\[
 \omega_-I_d\preceq\Omega_j\preceq M_0I_d\quad(j\in\mathcal J).          \tag{6}
\]
For every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), (5), (6), and \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\ge h_-\) imply
\[
 x^\top V_jx\ge\omega_-\|H^{-\top}x\|_2^2
 \ge\frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\|x\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 x^\top V_jx\le M_0h_-^{-2}\|x\|_2^2.                                  \tag{7}
\]

We next establish the Z-estimation assertions.  Besides \(G_T(\theta^*)=T^{-1}S_T\), the derivative counterpart of the first conditioning calculation is
\[
 E_P\{\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)\}=H.
\]
The summands in the two centered averages are martingale differences bounded respectively by common multiples of \(B/\varepsilon\) and \(D_1/\varepsilon\).  Entrywise conditional-variance addition and Chebyshev's inequality, with fixed \(d\), therefore give uniformly
\[
 G_T(\theta^*)=O_P(T^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H=O_P(T^{-1/2}).                            \tag{8}
\]
For completeness, a finite-net argument gives the uniform law of large numbers needed for the preliminary estimator.  Indeed, \(G_T\) is \(D_1/\varepsilon\)-Lipschitz and \(M_P\) is \(KD_1\)-Lipschitz on the fixed compact set \(\Theta\).  On every fixed finite net, the same martingale Chebyshev bound applies pointwise; taking the union bound and then sending the mesh to zero proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|G_T(\theta)-M_P(\theta)\|_2=o_P(1)               \tag{9}
\]
uniformly.  The defining approximate-minimum inequality and \(M_P(\theta^*)=0\) show
\[
 \|G_T(\widetilde\theta_T)\|_2
 \le \|G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+T^{-1/2}(r_T+\delta_T)=o_P(1),                  \tag{10}
\]
where finite-attractor vanishing gives \(r_T+\delta_T\to0\).  Equations (9)--(10), followed by the common root-separation modulus \(\kappa\), imply \(\widetilde\theta_T\to_P\theta^*\) uniformly.

On the event where \(\|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-/4\), the curvature envelope gives, throughout the closed \(\rho\)-ball,
\[
 \|\partial_\theta G_T(\theta)-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le h_-/4+D_2\rho/\varepsilon<h_-/2.                                    \tag{11}
\]
If also \(\|H^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2\le\rho/4\), the map
\[
 \mathcal T_T(\theta)=\theta-H^{-1}G_T(\theta)
\]
maps that closed ball into itself and is a contraction with Lipschitz constant \(q_T<1/2\), by (11) and \(\|H^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le h_-^{-1}\).  To make existence explicit, start at \(\theta^{(0)}=\theta^*\) and iterate \(\theta^{(n+1)}=\mathcal T_T(\theta^{(n)})\).  The self-map property keeps every iterate in the ball, while
\[
 \|\theta^{(n+1)}-\theta^{(n)}\|_2\le q_T^n\|\theta^{(1)}-\theta^{(0)}\|_2.
\]
The geometric-series bound makes the iterates Cauchy; the closed Euclidean ball is complete, and continuity of \(\mathcal T_T\) makes their limit \(\theta_T^0\) a fixed point, hence a root.  If two fixed points existed, their distance would be at most \(q_T\) times itself, so they would coincide.  At the fixed point,
\[
 \|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2
 \le \|H^{-1}G_T(\theta^*)\|_2+ \frac12\|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2,
\]
so (8) yields \(\|\theta_T^0-\theta^*\|_2=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  With probability tending uniformly to one, both this distance and \(\|\widetilde\theta_T-\theta^*\|_2\) are below \(\rho/4\).  Then \(\theta_T^0\in\mathcal Z_T\), while every member of \(\mathcal Z_T\) is in the closed \(\rho\)-ball and hence equals \(\theta_T^0\).  This proves uniform asymptotic certainty of \(E_T\), uniqueness of its selected root, and its stated rate.

On this event, Taylor's formula gives, for \(\Delta_T=\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*\),
\[
 0=G_T(\theta^*)+\bar H_T\Delta_T,
 \qquad
 \bar H_T=\int_0^1\partial_\theta G_T(\theta^*+u\Delta_T)\,du.
\]
By (8), the curvature bound, and \(\Delta_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})\), \(\bar H_T-H=O_P(T^{-1/2})\).  Since \(T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(1)\), the inverse identity gives
\[
 \sqrt T\Delta_T+H^{-1}T^{-1/2}S_T
 =(H^{-1}-\bar H_T^{-1})T^{-1/2}S_T=O_P(T^{-1/2})=O_P(e_T).               \tag{12}
\]
The exceptional event has probability tending uniformly to zero, so (12) remains a uniform \(O_P(e_T)\) assertion for the total estimator.

It remains to prove the stable kernel rather than assume it.  Put
\[
 b_T=\max\{T^{-1/2},\sqrt{r_T}\},
 \qquad
 k_T=\min\{T-1,\lceil Tb_T\rceil\}.
\]
Then \(k_T/T\to0\).  Let \(J_T\) be the fixed-tie-broken nearest attractor to \(k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}\).  It is \(\mathcal F_{k_T}\)-measurable.  On the attraction event,
\[
 \left\|k_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}\eta_{t-1}-\bar\eta_J\right\|_2
 \le \frac{Tr_T}{k_T}\le\frac{r_T}{b_T}\le\sqrt{r_T}.                 \tag{13}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), (13) and attractor separation make \(J\) the unique nearest attractor.  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_T}P(J_T\ne J)\le\delta_T\longrightarrow0.        \tag{14}
\]

Fix \(\|c\|_2=1\), put \(a=H^{-\top}c\), and define \(X_{T,t}=a^\top\xi_t\) and the early-measurable scale \(\lambda_T=a^\top\Omega_{J_T}a\).  From (5)--(7), uniformly in \(P,c\),
\[
 |X_{T,t}|\le B/(\varepsilon h_-),
 \quad
 \frac{\omega_-}{(KD_1)^2}\le\lambda_T\le\frac{M_0}{h_-^2}.            \tag{15}
\]
Using (4), deleting the bounded prefix, and then using (14), we obtain
\[
 E_P\left|T^{-1}\sum_{t=k_T+1}^TE_P(X_{T,t}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})-\lambda_T\right|
 \le h_-^{-2}\{C(r_T+\delta_T)+M_0k_T/T+2M_0P(J_T\ne J)\}=o(1)          \tag{16}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c\).

For each \(j\), (14) and the basin-mass floor give \(P(J_T=j)\ge p_-/2\) eventually.  Conditional on the event \(\{J_T=j\}\in\mathcal F_{k_T}\), the suffix remains a martingale-difference array, its predictable variances are unchanged, and its conditional version of the left side of (16) is at most \(2/p_-\) times the unconditional bound.  The already-established early-measurable martingale-mixture lemma, applied under this conditional law (equivalently with the now constant scale \(a^\top\Omega_ja\)), gives the uniform conditional Gaussian approximation for the suffix.  Its prefix is negligible because martingale orthogonality and (15) imply
\[
 E_P\left|T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{k_T}X_{T,t}\right|^2
 \le \frac{k_TB^2}{T\varepsilon^2h_-^2}=o(1).                            \tag{17}
\]
Combining the finitely many conditional approximations, (14), (17), and Gaussian anti-concentration under the lower bound in (15), for every \(\psi:\mathcal J\to[-1,1]\),
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\left|
 E_P\!\left[\psi(J)\mathbf1\{-a^\top T^{-1/2}S_T\le z\}\right]
 -\sum_{j=1}^mp_j\psi(j)\Phi\!\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{a^\top\Omega_ja}}\right)
 \right|\longrightarrow0                                                \tag{18}
\]
uniformly in \(P,c,\psi\).  Since \(a^\top\Omega_ja=c^\top V_jc\), and since (12) has an \(o_P(1)\) remainder under finite-attractor vanishing, another application of the same uniform Gaussian anti-concentration transfers (18) to \(c^\top\sqrt T(\widehat\theta_T-\theta^*)\).  Taking \(\psi=\mathbf1\{j=\cdot\}\) and dividing by \(p_j\ge p_-\) gives the stated basin-conditional approximation; leaving \(\psi\) arbitrary gives the stated \(\sigma(J)\)-stable kernel.

Finally, (7) places every \(V_J\) in the common compact covariance set.  The compact-covariance-law subsequence lemma supplies a weakly convergent subsequence with law \(\mu\).  Along it, (18) with \(\psi\equiv1\), continuity of \(v\mapsto\Phi(z/\sqrt{c^\top vc})\), and (12) give the scalar limit \(\sqrt{c^\top Vc}\,Z_1\), with the independent Gaussian representation stated in the theorem.  If \(c^\top Vc=q\) almost surely, this is \(N(0,q)\).  Conversely, if the mixture is \(N(0,q_0)\), boundedness of \(c^\top Vc\) permits comparison of second and fourth moments:
\[
 E(c^\top Vc)=q_0,
 \qquad
 3E\{(c^\top Vc)^2\}=3q_0^2.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{Var}(c^\top Vc)=0\).  This proves the claimed if-and-only-if statement and completes the proof without pointwise convergence of \(\eta_t\).
