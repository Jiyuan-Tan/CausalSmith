%%% FIELD def-response-boundary-singleton-construction
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), choose constants \(0<a<\lambda<\min\{1,-\log\alpha\}\), and let \(\kappa_n\downarrow0\) satisfy \(\kappa_n\leq 1/4\) for every \(n\).  On \(\mathcal C=\mathcal X=\{1,2\}\), define \(\mathbb P^0=(P_n^{\circ,0})_{n\geq1}\) as follows.  Row \(n\) contains \(n\) independent response-study observations \((W_i,Y_i)\), where \(W_i\) is uniform on \(\mathcal C\times\mathcal X\) and
\[
Y_i\mid W_i=(c,x)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\lambda/n).
\]
The deployment margins are observed without error and satisfy \(p_n=r_n=(1/2,1/2)\), while \(\pi_{n,cx}=1/4\) and \(\mu_{n,cx}=\lambda/n\) for every \((c,x)\).  If \(\overline Y_{n,cx}\) is the within-cell response proportion, set it to zero when the cell is empty and use
\[
\widehat\mu_{n,cx}=\left(1-\frac{2a}{n}\right)\overline Y_{n,cx}+\frac{a}{n}.
\]
The multiplier draw is the empirical-influence multiplier for these four response estimators and has zero margin coordinates.  Define the singleton experiment class
\[
\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}=\{\mathbb P^0\}.
\]

%%% FIELD thm-response-boundary-failure-statement
Response-boundary failure under bounded-Lipschitz approximation.  For the singleton class \(\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}\) in \(\mathrm{def:response\text{-}boundary\text{-}singleton}\), one has \(\mathbb P^0\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}\subseteq\mathfrak P_{\kappa}^{0}\), and the two bounded-Lipschitz approximation conditions hold with their suprema restricted to \(\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}\):
\[
\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}}
\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BL}}\leq1}
\left|
E_{\mathbb P}f\!\left(\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_n-\beta_n)\right)
-E f(G_{\mathbb P,n})
\right|\longrightarrow0,
\]
and, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}}
\Pr_{\mathbb P}\!\left\{
\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BL}}\leq1}
\left|E_{\mathbb P}^{\star}f(G_n^\star)-E f(G_{\mathbb P,n})\right|>\epsilon
\right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Nevertheless, the same fixed experiment \(\mathbb P^0\), chosen independently of the numerical schedule, satisfies
\[
\exists\mathbb P^0\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}\quad
\forall t=(t_n)_{n\geq1}\ \text{with }t_n>0\text{ and }t_n\to0,qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}\!\left\{
[L(\beta_n),U(\beta_n)]\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n(t)
\right\}
\leq1-e^{-\lambda}<1-\alpha.
\]
Thus uniform bounded-Lipschitz Gaussian and multiplier approximation, even over a singleton class that can be embedded in the proposed sufficient margin-rate regime, does not by itself supply the response anti-concentration or relative variance control needed for coverage when the response variance collapses.  This is a response-boundary failure, not a sharp rare-margin threshold, and its conclusion has no condition on \(n\kappa_n^3\).

%%% FIELD thm-response-boundary-failure-proof
By \(\mathrm{def:response\text{-}boundary\text{-}singleton}\), the two deployment margins of \(\mathbb P^0\) equal \(1/2\), every response-study cell probability equals \(1/4\), and \(\kappa_n\leq1/4\).  Hence
\[
\min_c p_{n,c}\wedge\min_x r_{n,x}=\frac12\geq\kappa_n,
\qquad
\min_{c,x}\pi_{n,cx}=\frac14\geq\kappa_n.
\tag{1}
\]
The response means satisfy \(0<\lambda/n<1\), because \(0<\lambda<1\).  Thus (1), the fixed finite supports, and the independent row sampling specified in \(\mathrm{def:response\text{-}boundary\text{-}singleton}\) verify every membership condition in \(\mathrm{def:uniform\text{-}experiment}\), so
\[
\mathbb P^0\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}\subseteq\mathfrak P_{\kappa}^{0}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
D_n(\mathbb P)=
\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BL}}\leq1}
\left|
E_{\mathbb P}f\!\left(\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_n-\beta_n)\right)
-E f(G_{\mathbb P,n})
\right|
\]
and
\[
M_n(\mathbb P)=
\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BL}}\leq1}
\left|E_{\mathbb P}^{\star}f(G_n^\star)-E f(G_{\mathbb P,n})\right|.
\]
Since \(\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}=\{\mathbb P^0\}\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}}D_n(\mathbb P)=D_n(\mathbb P^0),
\qquad
\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathfrak Q_{\kappa}}
\Pr_{\mathbb P}\{M_n(\mathbb P)>\epsilon\}
=\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}\{M_n(\mathbb P^0)>\epsilon\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The data law, ridge estimator, and multiplier in \(\mathrm{def:response\text{-}boundary\text{-}singleton}\) are exactly those of \(\mathrm{lem:vanishing\text{-}variance\text{-}all\text{-}zero\text{-}certificate}\).  That lemma proves
\[
D_n(\mathbb P^0)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
M_n(\mathbb P^0)=o_{\mathbb P^0}(1).
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), the second relation in (4) gives
\(\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}\{M_n(\mathbb P^0)>\epsilon\}\to0\).  Substitution into (3) proves both displayed uniform approximation assertions.  Notice that the only uniformization step is equality (3); no rate for \(\kappa_n\) is used.

Now define the all-zero response event
\[
A_n=\{Y_1=\cdots=Y_n=0\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:vanishing\text{-}variance\text{-}all\text{-}zero\text{-}certificate}\),
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}(A_n)=\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^n
\longrightarrow e^{-\lambda}.
\tag{5}
\]
The same lemma proves, simultaneously for every deterministic schedule with \(t_n>0\), that on \(A_n\)
\[
L(\beta_n)=U(\beta_n)=\frac{\lambda}{n},
\qquad
F(\widehat\beta_n)=\left(\frac an,\frac an\right),
\qquad
q_{1-\alpha,n}^{\star}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:uniform\text{-}confidence\text{-}envelope}\), (6) implies
\[
\mathrm{CI}_n(t)=\left[\frac an,\frac an\right]
\quad\text{on }A_n.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(0<a<\lambda\), equations (6)--(7) show
\[
A_n\subseteq
\left\{[L(\beta_n),U(\beta_n)]\not\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n(t)\right\}
\quad\text{for every }t.
\tag{8}
\]
The law \(\mathbb P^0\) in (8) does not depend on \(t\).  Therefore, for every admissible deterministic schedule,
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}\!\left\{
[L(\beta_n),U(\beta_n)]\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n(t)
\right\}
\leq1-\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}(A_n).
\tag{9}
\]
Taking upper limits in (9) and using (5) yields
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}\!\left\{
[L(\beta_n),U(\beta_n)]\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n(t)
\right\}
\leq1-e^{-\lambda}.
\tag{10}
\]
The choice \(\lambda< -\log\alpha\) gives \(e^{-\lambda}>\alpha\), hence
\[
1-e^{-\lambda}<1-\alpha.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) establish the stated schedule-independent failure with the required quantifier order.

For completeness, this response-boundary failure can occur inside the proposed sufficient margin-rate regime without making that rate part of the theorem.  Choose
\[
\kappa_n=\frac{1}{4n^{1/4}}.
\]
Then (1) still holds and
\[
n\kappa_n^3=\frac{n^{1/4}}{64}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{12}
\]
The schedule \(t_n=n^{-5/16}\) also satisfies
\[
(n\kappa_n)^{-1/2}=2n^{-3/8}=o(n^{-5/16}),
\qquad
n^{-5/16}=o(\kappa_n).
\tag{13}
\]
Since (10) holds for every schedule, it holds in particular for the schedule in (13).  Thus the obstruction is collapsing response variance and the resulting failure of coverage at a degenerate limit, rather than failure of the proposed margin-rate inequalities.

%%% FIELD oeq-uniform-sufficient-revised-statement
The response-boundary theorem shows that \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}primitive\text{-}clt}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}multiplier\text{-}law}\) alone do not imply coverage, even over a singleton experiment class satisfying the proposed margin-rate window.  First determine a minimal, faithfully stated, independently checkable uniform response condition that excludes the collapsing-variance construction without assuming the desired critical-value or coverage conclusion.  Candidate ingredients include response-interiority, nondegeneracy of endpoint-relevant variances, relative consistency of the multiplier variance, or quantitative anti-concentration.  Then, over the subclass of \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa}^{0}\) satisfying the selected condition, determine whether
\[
n\kappa_n^3\to\infty,
\qquad
(n\kappa_n)^{-1/2}=o(t_n),
\qquad
t_n=o(\kappa_n)
\]
imply
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb P}
\Pr_{\mathbb P}\!\left\{
[L(\beta_n),U(\beta_n)]\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n(t)
\right\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
where the infimum is over that repaired subclass.  The additional condition must be stated in primitive response-law or multiplier-law terms and must not encode coverage itself.  This question concerns sufficiency only; whether \(n\kappa_n^3\) is sharp for rare-margin optimal-face switching remains a separate question after the response-boundary prerequisite is settled.

%%% FIELD oeq-uniform-sufficient-partial-result
The proved response-boundary theorem constructs one fixed \(2\times2\) experiment \(\mathbb P^0\) such that both bounded-Lipschitz approximation discrepancies converge to zero, but for every deterministic schedule \(t\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr_{\mathbb P^0}\!\left\{[L(\beta_n),U(\beta_n)]\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n(t)\right\}
\leq1-e^{-\lambda}<1-\alpha.
\]
The remaining problem is therefore not derivable from the frozen approximation assumptions: it first requires a noncircular uniform response-interiority, nondegenerate-variance, relative variance-consistency, or anti-concentration condition, followed by a new uniform face-localization and quantile argument under the displayed \(\kappa_n\) and \(t_n\) rates.
