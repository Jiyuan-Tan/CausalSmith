%%% FIELD proof-linear-risk
Fix a rule \(\ell\).  Because \(S_{\ell}\) is independent in \(C_G\), Definition \(\mathrm{def:conflict\mbox{-}graph}\) implies
\[
 i,j\in S_{\ell},\quad i\ne j
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D_i\cap D_j=\varnothing.
\]
Thus the treatment and control exposures required by Definition \(\mathrm{def:rdim\mbox{-}construction}\) are mutually compatible.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:full\mbox{-}neighborhood\mbox{-}interference}\) then implies that a treated focal \(i\) contributes the fixed endpoint \(Y_i(\mathbf 1)\), while a control focal \(i\) contributes \(Y_i(\mathbf 0)\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\mbox{-}isolation}\) gives \(K_{\ell}\ge2\), hence \(t(K_{\ell})\ge1\) and \(c(K_{\ell})\ge1\); every division in \(e_{\ell}\) is therefore legitimate.  Consequently Definition \(\mathrm{def:rdim\mbox{-}construction}\) gives
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\ell}=e_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}\theta.
\]
The causal estimand is not defined by this estimator: by the definitions of \(d\), \(\theta\), and \(\tau\), it is the fixed finite-population contrast
\[
 \tau=d^{\mathsf T}\theta
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{Y_i(\mathbf 1)-Y_i(\mathbf 0)\}.
\]
Since \(a_{\ell}=e_{\ell}-d\), subtraction yields the pointwise identity, for every realized assignment,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{\ell}-\tau
 =(e_{\ell}-d)^{\mathsf T}\theta
 =a_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}\theta.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:design\mbox{-}based\mbox{-}randomness}\) makes \(\theta\) fixed under \(P_{\ell}\).  Therefore bilinearity and the definition of \(Q_{\ell}\) give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\ell}\!\left[(\widehat\tau_{\ell}-\tau)^2\right]
 =\mathbb E_{\ell}\!\left[\theta^{\mathsf T}a_{\ell}a_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}\theta\right]
 =\theta^{\mathsf T}\mathbb E_{\ell}[a_{\ell}a_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}]\theta
 =\theta^{\mathsf T}Q_{\ell}\theta.
\]
This equality includes squared design bias as well as design variance; no unbiasedness assertion has been used.

%%% FIELD proof-greedy-certificate
Let the vertices be scanned from first to last in the strict order \(\pi\).  If \(S\) is the sequential greedy output, no selected vertex had a previously selected neighbor.  Hence no two vertices of \(S\) are adjacent, so \(S\) is independent.  If \(v\notin S\), the greedy rule rejected \(v\) precisely because, at the instant \(v\) was scanned, some already selected neighbor \(u\in S\) existed.  Such \(u\) precedes \(v\) in \(\pi\).  This proves necessity.

Conversely, suppose that \(S\) is independent and that every \(v\in V\setminus S\) has a neighbor in \(S\) preceding it.  Induct on the scan position.  Assume that among all earlier vertices the greedy algorithm has selected exactly the earlier members of \(S\).  If the current vertex \(v\) belongs to \(S\), independence says that it has no earlier neighbor in \(S\), hence no earlier selected neighbor, so the algorithm selects it.  If \(v\notin S\), the stated domination condition supplies an earlier neighbor \(u\in S\); by the induction hypothesis \(u\) has been selected, so the algorithm rejects \(v\).  The induction starts before the first vertex, where the assertion is vacuous, and ends with the greedy output equal to \(S\).  This proves sufficiency and the equivalence.

%%% FIELD proof-tagged-risk-matrix
Fix \(\ell\) and condition first on an isolated set \(S_{\ell}=S\) of size \(k\ge2\).  Write \(t=t(k)\) and \(c=c(k)=k-t\).  The conditional uniformity in Definition \(\mathrm{def:rdim\mbox{-}construction}\) gives, for \(i,j\in S\) with \(i\ne j\),
\[
 \Pr(i\in T_{\ell}\mid S)=\frac tk,
 \qquad
 \Pr(i\in U_{\ell}\mid S)=\frac ck,
\]
and
\[
 \Pr(i,j\in T_{\ell}\mid S)=\frac{t(t-1)}{k(k-1)},
 \quad
 \Pr(i,j\in U_{\ell}\mid S)=\frac{c(c-1)}{k(k-1)},
 \quad
 \Pr(i\in T_{\ell},j\in U_{\ell}\mid S)=\frac{tc}{k(k-1)}.
\]
Because \(e_{\ell}\) has treated coefficient \(1/t\) and control coefficient \(-1/c\), its conditional mean at coordinate \(i\) is
\[
 \mathbb E[e_{\ell,i}^{(1)}\mid S]
 =\mathbf 1\{i\in S\}\frac tk\frac1t
 =\frac{\mathbf 1\{i\in S\}}k,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[e_{\ell,i}^{(0)}\mid S]
 =-\frac{\mathbf 1\{i\in S\}}k.
\]
Taking expectations over \(S\) and grouping by \(K_{\ell}=k\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E[e_{\ell}]
 =(r_{\ell}^{\mathsf T},-r_{\ell}^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}
 =m_{\ell},
 \qquad
 r_{\ell i}=\sum_{k=2}^n\frac{p_{\ell i}(k)}k.
\]

For diagonal second moments, the same calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb E[(e_{ell,i}^{(1)})^2]
 =\sum_{k=2}^n p_{ell i}(k)\frac tk\frac1{t^2}
 =\sum_{k=2}^n\frac{p_{ell i}(k)}{k t(k)},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E[(e_{ell,i}^{(0)})^2]
 =\sum_{k=2}^n\frac{p_{ell i}(k)}{k c(k)}.
\]
For \(i\ne j\), division of the three joint-selection probabilities by their corresponding RDIM denominators gives
\[
 \mathbb E[e_{ell,i}^{(1)}e_{ell,j}^{(1)}]
 =\sum_{k=2}^n p_{ell ij}(k)
   \frac{t(k)-1}{t(k)k(k-1)},
\]
\[
 \mathbb E[e_{ell,i}^{(0)}e_{ell,j}^{(0)}]
 =\sum_{k=2}^n p_{ell ij}(k)
   \frac{c(k)-1}{c(k)k(k-1)},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E[e_{ell,i}^{(1)}e_{ell,j}^{(0)}]
 =-\sum_{k=2}^n\frac{p_{ell ij}(k)}{k(k-1)}.
\]
The same-unit cross-arm entry is zero because \(T_{ell}\cap U_{ell}=\varnothing\), and the other cross block is the transpose.  These are exactly the asserted entries of \(M_{ell}=\mathbb E_{ell}[e_{ell}e_{ell}^{\mathsf T}]\).

By Definition \(\mathrm{def:tagged\mbox{-}count\mbox{-}polynomial}\), coefficient extraction gives
\[
 [z^k]F_{ell,\{i\}}(z)
 =\Pr_{ell}\{i\in S_{ell},K_{ell}=k\}=p_{ell i}(k)
\]
and, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
 [z^k]F_{ell,\{i,j\}}(z)
 =\Pr_{ell}\{i,j\in S_{ell},K_{ell}=k\}=p_{ell ij}(k).
\]
Finally, expanding \(a_{ell}=e_{ell}-d\) and using that \(d\) is fixed gives
\[
 Q_{ell}
 =\mathbb E[(e_{ell}-d)(e_{ell}-d)^{\mathsf T}]
 =M_{ell}-m_{ell}d^{\mathsf T}-dm_{ell}^{\mathsf T}+dd^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus tags of size at most two determine every entry, including all odd-count and bias terms.

%%% FIELD proof-rational-output
Fix \(\ell\) and put \(W_{ell}=\sum_{i=1}^n h_{ell i}\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:wri\mbox{-}law}\), for every order \(\pi\),
\[
 P_{ell}(\pi)
 =\prod_{r=1}^n
 \frac{h_{ell,\pi_r}}{H_r},
 \qquad
 H_r=\sum_{s=r}^n h_{ell,\pi_s}.
\]
Positivity and integrality of the weights imply
\[
 W_{ell}=H_1>H_2>\cdots>H_n\ge1.
\]
Thus \(H_1,\ldots,H_n\) are distinct members of \(\{1,\ldots,W_{ell}\}\), so \(\prod_rH_r\) divides \(W_{ell}!\).  It follows that every order probability has denominator dividing \(W_{ell}!\).  For fixed \(A\) and \(k\), the coefficient \([z^k]F_{ell,A}\) is the sum of precisely those order probabilities for which \(A\subseteq S_{ell}(\pi)\) and \(|S_{ell}(\pi)|=k\).  A sum of rationals whose denominators divide \(W_{ell}!\) again has denominator dividing \(W_{ell}!\).  This proves the first assertion.

In the formulas of Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:tagged\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}matrix}\), every additional denominator is a product of at most three integers in \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\): these are \(k\), \(k-1\), \(t(k)\), \(c(k)\), and the factors \(n\) appearing in \(d\).  Each such integer divides \(n!\).  Consequently
\[
 D_{ell}=W_{ell}!(n!)^3
\]
is a common denominator of all entries of \(M_{ell}\), \(m_{ell}d^{\mathsf T}\), \(dm_{ell}^{\mathsf T}\), and \(dd^{\mathsf T}\), and hence of \(Q_{ell}\).

It remains to bound numerators.  On every design realization, \(|T_{ell}|=t(K_{ell})\), \(|U_{ell}|=c(K_{ell})\), and the two blocks of \(e_{ell}\) have disjoint support.  Hence
\[
 \|e_{ell}\|_2^2=\frac1{t(K_{ell})}+\frac1{c(K_{ell})},
 \qquad
 e_{ell}^{\mathsf T}d=\frac2n,
 \qquad
 \|d\|_2^2=\frac2n.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\mbox{-}isolation}\) gives \(t(K_{ell}),c(K_{ell})\ge1\), and therefore
\[
 \|a_{ell}\|_2^2
 =\|e_{ell}-d\|_2^2
 =\frac1{t(K_{ell})}+\frac1{c(K_{ell})}-\frac2n
 \le 2-\frac2n<2.
\]
It follows that \(\|Q_{ell}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\mathbb E\|a_{ell}\|_2^2<2\), and hence every entry of \(Q_{ell}\) has absolute value below \(2\).  When written over \(D_{ell}\), its numerator therefore has absolute value below \(2D_{ell}\).  Since \(W_{ell}\le nb\),
\[
 \log_2 D_{ell}
 \le \log_2(W_{ell}!)+3\log_2(n!)
 =O\!\left(nb\log(nb+1)+n\log(n+1)\right).
\]
The same bound holds for numerator and reduced-denominator lengths, proving the stated output-size result.

%%% FIELD proof-forest-oracle
Fix a rule \(\ell\) and abbreviate \(h_v=h_{ell v}\).  Introduce independent priorities \(X_v\) with density
\[
 f_v(x)=h_vx^{h_v-1},\qquad 0<x<1.
\]
If \(E_v=-\log X_v\), then \(\Pr(E_v>t)=e^{-h_vt}\).  Direct integration gives \(\Pr(E_v=\min_{j\in R}E_j)=h_v/\sum_{j\in R}h_j\); conditioning on the minimum and using
\[
 \Pr(E_j-t>s\mid E_j>t)=e^{-h_js}
\]
iterates this calculation.  Therefore the decreasing order of the \(X_v\)'s has probability
\[
 \prod_{r=1}^n\frac{h_{pi_r}}{\sum_{s=r}^nh_{pi_s}},
\]
which is exactly \(P_{ell}\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def:wri\mbox{-}law}\).  The priorities are distinct with probability one because they have densities.  We may therefore compute \(F_{ell,A}\) by integration over these independent priorities.

Root every component of the forest.  For a vertex \(v\), let \(T_v\) be its rooted subtree.  Conditional on \(X_v=x\), assign the following precise meanings to the messages in Definition \(\mathrm{def:forest\mbox{-}messages}\): \(A_v(x,z)\) is the joint density-generating polynomial for certificate configurations on \(T_v\) in which \(v\) is selected and every vertex of \(T_v\) is certified; \(B_v(x,z)\) is the corresponding polynomial in which \(v\) is unselected and is certified by a selected child of priority greater than \(x\); and \(C_v(x,z)\) is the polynomial in which \(v\) is unselected, is not certified inside \(T_v\), and must be certified by a selected parent of priority greater than \(x\).  Every selected vertex contributes one factor \(z\).  At a tagged vertex, only the \(A\)-state is allowed.

We prove the recurrence by induction from leaves toward the root.  Suppose first that \(v\) is selected.  Independence in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:greedy\mbox{-}certificate}\) forces each child \(u\) to be unselected.  Such a child is either internally certified, contributing \(\int_0^1B_u(y,z)\,dy=J_u(z)\), or requires its selected parent \(v\); in the latter case the parent's priority must be larger, so \(y<x\), and the contribution is \(\int_0^xC_u(y,z)\,dy\).  Conditional independence of child-subtree priorities therefore gives
\[
 A_v(x,z)=zf_v(x)\prod_u\left(J_u(z)+\int_0^xC_u(y,z)\,dy\right).
\]

Suppose next that \(v\) is unselected.  No child may be in state \(C_u\), because that state requires \(v\) to be selected.  Without imposing how \(v\) is certified, each child can consequently be selected or internally certified, giving
\[
 f_v(x)\prod_uD_u(z),
 \qquad
 D_u(z)=\int_0^1(A_u(y,z)+B_u(y,z))\,dy.
\]
The configurations in which no selected child has priority greater than \(x\) allow a child either to be internally certified at any priority or to be selected only below \(x\).  Their contribution is
\[
 f_v(x)\prod_uE_u(x,z),
 \qquad
 E_u(x,z)=J_u(z)+\int_0^xA_u(y,z)\,dy.
\]
These are exactly the configurations in which \(v\) still needs a higher-priority selected parent, so
\[
 C_v(x,z)=f_v(x)\prod_uE_u(x,z).
\]
Subtracting them from all admissible unselected configurations leaves exactly those with at least one higher-priority selected child, whence
\[
 B_v(x,z)=f_v(x)\left(\prod_uD_u(z)-\prod_uE_u(x,z)\right).
\]
This proves all three recurrences.  The empty-product convention gives the leaf values \(A_v=zf_v\), \(B_v=0\), and \(C_v=f_v\).  Setting \(B_v=C_v=0\) at \(v\in A\) imposes \(A\subseteq S_{ell}\) and changes no other case.

At a root, state \(C\) is impossible because there is no parent; hence integrating \(A_r+B_r\) over its priority sums the densities of exactly the certificate configurations in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:greedy\mbox{-}certificate}\).  That lemma identifies each such configuration with the unique greedy output.  Priority families belonging to different forest components are independent, and the greedy output in one component is unaffected by interleavings with another component.  Multiplication of the component polynomials therefore gives
\[
 \prod_{r\text{ root}}\int_0^1(A_r(x,z)+B_r(x,z))\,dx
 =\mathbb E_{ell}[z^{K_{ell}}\mathbf 1\{A\subseteq S_{ell}\}]
 =F_{ell,A}(z).
\]
This establishes exact correctness.

For complexity, let \(N_v=|T_v|\) and \(W_v=\sum_{u\in T_v}h_u\), and let \(W=\sum_{u\in V}h_u\le nb\).  Induction on the displayed recurrence shows that every message has \(z\)-degree at most \(N_v\) and \(x\)-degree at most \(W_v-1\): multiplication by \(f_v\) adds \(h_v-1\), while each child antiderivative has \(x\)-degree at most \(W_u\), and subtree weight sums add.  Thus dense bivariate storage uses \(O(n(W+1))\) coefficients per message.  A naive multiplication of two such arrays uses \(O(n^2(W+1)^2)\) rational operations.  Accumulating all child products over all vertices uses \(O(n^3(W+1)^2)\) rational operations for one tag, and there are
\[
 1+n+\binom n2=O(n^2)
\]
tags.  Root products, integrations, coefficient extraction, and the \(O(n^3)\) reconstruction in Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:tagged\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}matrix}\) fit within the coarse bound \(O(n^5(W+1)^2)\) rational operations per rule.

The arithmetic is exact and has polynomial height.  To see this without assuming the desired conclusion, fully expand any intermediate coefficient according to the three-state semantics above.  Each signed term contains at most one density factor per subtree vertex, hence an integer numerator factor at most \(b^n\), and at most one integration per vertex, each dividing by a positive integer no larger than \(W\).  A common denominator for all such terms is therefore \((W!)^n\).  Even the coarser expansion by all vertex states and priority orders has at most \(3^n n!=2^{O(n\log(n+1))}\) signed terms, so after collection the numerator has
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(b+1)+nW\log(W+1)+n\log(n+1)\right)
\]
bits; the denominator has the same polynomial order.  This bound applies to every intermediate message as well as to its output.  Standard integer addition and multiplication therefore turn the rational-operation bound into \(\operatorname{poly}(n,b)\) bit operations.  Repeating for \(L\) rules proves the asserted \(L\operatorname{poly}(n,b)\) bound, uniformly over all tags of size at most two, and Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:tagged\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}matrix}\) then returns every \(Q_{ell}\).

Every self-adjoint linear operator on a finite-dimensional real inner-product space has an orthonormal basis consisting of eigenvectors.

%%% FIELD statement-sdp-slater-duality
For a finite-dimensional convex optimization problem with affine equality constraints and an affine conic inequality in a closed convex cone, if the primal optimal value is finite and there is a feasible point whose conic slack lies in the relative interior of the cone and whose ordinary inequality coordinates are strict, then the Lagrange dual has the same optimal value and a dual optimizer is attained.

Real quantifier elimination and sample-point construction return a point whose coordinates are real algebraic numbers, each represented by a square-free integer polynomial and a rational isolating interval, within \((\Lambda_0sD)^{2^{O(k)}}\) bit operations.

 \Pr\!\left\{\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\sum_{s=1}^NX_s\right)\ge u\right\}

%%% FIELD proof-minimax-certificate
For \(q\in\Delta_L\), put \(R(q)=H^{1/2}Q(q)H^{1/2}\).  Positive definiteness of \(H\) and the cited real spectral theorem give its symmetric positive square root.  The substitution \(\vartheta=BH^{1/2}u\) maps \(\|u\|_2\le1\) onto \(\Theta_G(B)\).  Since \(Q(q)\succeq0\), also \(R(q)\succeq0\), and the cited spectral theorem yields
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta_G(B)}\vartheta^{\mathsf T}Q(q)\vartheta
 =B^2\max_{\|u\|_2\le1}u^{\mathsf T}R(q)u
 =B^2\lambda_{\max}(R(q)).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(R(q))\le t
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R(q)\preceq tI_{2n}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q(q)\preceq tH^{-1}.
\]
This proves that the primal program in Definition \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}sdp}\) has value \(V^{\star}\).

We next derive, rather than assume, its dual.  If \((q,t)\) is primal feasible and \((X,s)\) satisfies the displayed dual constraints, then
\[
 t
 =t\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}X)
 \ge\operatorname{tr}(Q(q)X)
 =\sum_{ell=1}^Lq_{ell}\operatorname{tr}(Q_{ell}X)
 \ge s.
\]
This is weak duality.  Conversely, using a multiplier \(X\succeq0\) for \(Q(q)\preceq tH^{-1}\), minimization of the Lagrangian over free \(t\) is finite exactly when \(\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}X)=1\), while minimization over \(q\in\Delta_L\) gives
\[
 \min_{q\in\Delta_L}\sum_{ell=1}^Lq_{ell}\operatorname{tr}(Q_{ell}X)
 =\min_{1\le\ell\le L}\operatorname{tr}(Q_{ell}X).
\]
Introducing \(s\) below each trace gives exactly the stated dual.

The primal satisfies the hypotheses of the cited Slater theorem.  Indeed, choose \(q_{ell}=1/L>0\) and then choose
\[
 t>\lambda_{\max}\!\left(H^{1/2}Q(q)H^{1/2}\right);
\]
the matrix slack is positive definite.  The optimal value is finite and nonnegative because \(Q(q)\succeq0\).  Hence strong duality holds and a dual optimizer exists.  A primal optimizer exists because \(q\mapsto\lambda_{\max}(R(q))\) is continuous on the compact simplex.  We have therefore obtained optimizers with
\[
 V^{\star}=B^2t^{\star}=B^2s^{\star}.
\]
No step requires \(q^{\star}\) to be interior.  Equality in the weak-duality chain gives
\[
 s^{\star}
 =\sum_{ell=1}^Lq_{ell}^{\star}\operatorname{tr}(Q_{ell}X^{\star}),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(Q_{ell}X^{\star})\ge s^{\star}.
\]
Thus every summand with \(q_{ell}^{\star}>0\) must equal \(s^{\star}\), proving the asserted complementary-slackness certificate.

Let
\[
 H^{-1/2}X^{\star}H^{-1/2}
 =\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_ju_ju_j^{\mathsf T}
\]
be its positive-eigenvalue spectral decomposition.  The cited spectral theorem and \(X^{\star}\succeq0\) give \(\lambda_j>0\), orthonormal \(u_j\), and \(r\le2n\).  Dual normalization gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j
 =\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1/2}X^{\star}H^{-1/2})
 =\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}X^{\star})=1.
\]
Each \(\vartheta_j=BH^{1/2}u_j\) lies on the boundary because
\[
 \vartheta_j^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}\vartheta_j=B^2\|u_j\|_2^2=B^2.
\]
For every library rule,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\nu^{\star}}[\vartheta^{\mathsf T}Q_{ell}\vartheta]
 =B^2\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_ju_j^{\mathsf T}H^{1/2}Q_{ell}H^{1/2}u_j
 =B^2\operatorname{tr}(Q_{ell}X^{\star})
 \ge B^2s^{\star}=V^{\star}.
\]
On the other hand, the primal certificate makes the loss of \(q^{\star}\) at every \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G(B)\) at most \(B^2t^{\star}=V^{\star}\).  Hence \((q^{\star},\nu^{\star})\) is a saddle pair and \(\nu^{\star}\) is least favourable.  A repeated eigenspace merely permits a different orthonormal basis within that space and does not change any displayed equality.

For the exact complexity statement, first compute the rational matrix \(H^{-1}\) by exact elimination; its encoding length is polynomial in \(\Lambda\) and \(n\).  Clear all rational denominators.  Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and equality \(t=s\) can be written as a nonempty semialgebraic system using factor variables
\[
 tH^{-1}-Q(q)=RR^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 X=ZZ^{\mathsf T},
\]
together with the simplex, trace, and rule inequalities.  It has \(O(L+n^2)\) real variables after all matrix entries and factor entries are counted, polynomially many quadratic equations and inequalities, and coefficient length polynomial in \(\Lambda,L,n\).  Strong duality proves that this system is nonempty.  To encode \(\nu^{\star}\), adjoin variables for the positive square root of \(H\), its inverse, an orthogonal eigenbasis, and the nonnegative eigenvalues; these add only \(O(n^2)\) variables and polynomially many bounded-degree equations.  The cited real-algebraic sample-point theorem therefore returns all coordinates in the encoding of Definition \(\mathrm{def:algebraic\mbox{-}certificate\mbox{-}encoding}\) within
\[
 (\Lambda Ln)^{2^{O(L+n^2)}}
\]
bit operations.  Zero mixture coordinates and repeated eigenspaces are semialgebraic boundary cases and are not excluded by this construction.

Finally, the weak-duality calculation applies to every feasible pair, rational or not.  For a rational feasible pair it is exactly checkable and gives
\[
 0\le V^{\star}-B^2s\le B^2(t-s),
 \qquad
 0\le B^2t-V^{\star}\le B^2(t-s).
\]
Thus \(B^2(t-s)\) is the asserted certified primal--dual gap.

%%% FIELD proof-simulation-certificate
Put \(A_n^2=2-2/n\).  The calculation in Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:rational\mbox{-}output}\), using \(K_{ell}\ge2\), gives the pointwise bound
\[
 \|a_{ell s}\|_2^2\le A_n^2.
\]
For a fixed rule \(\ell\), define
\[
 Y_s=a_{ell s}a_{ell s}^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad M=\mathbb E_{ell}Y_s=Q_{ell},
 \qquad Z_s=Y_s-M.
\]
The fixed-outcome design assumption and the independent offline draws give independent, centered, self-adjoint \(Z_s\).  Since \(0\preceq Y_s\preceq A_n^2I_{2n}\) and \(0\preceq M\preceq A_n^2I_{2n}\),
\[
 \|Z_s\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le A_n^2.
\]
Also \(Y_s^2=\|a_{ell s}\|_2^2Y_s\preceq A_n^4I_{2n}\), and direct expansion gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_s^2]=\mathbb E[Y_s^2]-M^2\preceq\mathbb E[Y_s^2]\preceq A_n^4I_{2n}.
\]
Apply the cited matrix Bernstein inequality to \(Z_s\) and to \(-Z_s\), with dimension \(2n\), bound \(R=A_n^2\), variance proxy \(NA_n^4\), and threshold \(N\varepsilon\).  A union bound over the two signs gives
\[
 \Pr\{\|\widehat Q_{ell}-Q_{ell}\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\varepsilon\}
 \le4n\exp\!\left\{-\frac{N\varepsilon^2}{2A_n^4+(2/3)A_n^2\varepsilon}\right\}.
\]
When \(0<\varepsilon\le A_n^2\), the denominator is at most \((8/3)A_n^4\).  Under the displayed lower bound on \(N\), the last probability is at most \(\delta/L\).  A union bound over \(\ell=1,ldots,L\) proves
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\max_{1\le\ell\le L}\|\widehat Q_{ell}-Q_{ell}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\varepsilon\right\}\ge1-\delta.
\]

Work on this simultaneous event and write
\[
 Q_{ell}^{-}=\widehat Q_{ell}-\varepsilon I_{2n},
 \qquad
 Q_{ell}^{+}=\widehat Q_{ell}+\varepsilon I_{2n}.
\]
Then \(Q_{ell}^{-}\preceq Q_{ell}\preceq Q_{ell}^{+}\) for every \(\ell\), and convex combination preserves these inequalities.  For any symmetric matrix family \(A_{ell}\), define
\[
 \phi_A(q)=B^2\lambda_{\max}\!\left(H^{1/2}\sum_{ell=1}^Lq_{ell}A_{ell}H^{1/2}\right).
\]
The epigraph SDP computes \(\min_q\phi_A(q)\) even when some \(A_{ell}\) are indefinite.  In particular the lower program is a valid numerical lower program, although \(Q_{ell}^{-}\) need not itself be a risk matrix.  Monotonicity of the maximum Rayleigh quotient gives
\[
 V_- =\min_q\phi_{Q^-}(q)
 \le\min_q\phi_Q(q)=V^{\star}
 \le\min_q\phi_{Q^+}(q)=V_+.
\]
For the upper-program minimizer \(q_+\), Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:minimax\mbox{-}certificate}\) and \(Q(q_+)\preceq Q^+(q_+)\) give
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta_G(B)}
 \vartheta^{\mathsf T}Q(q_+)\vartheta
 =\phi_Q(q_+)
 \le\phi_{Q^+}(q_+)=V_+.
\]

For every \(q\), the two robust transformed matrices differ by \(2\varepsilon H\).  The Rayleigh quotient directly implies
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(R+2\varepsilon H)
 \le\lambda_{\max}(R)+2\varepsilon\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\]
so minimization over \(q\) yields
\[
 V_+-V_-
 \le2B^2\varepsilon\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Finally, the combinatorial Laplacian obeys
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}L_Cx=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_C}(x_i-x_j)^2\ge0,
 \qquad L_C\mathbf 1_n=0.
\]
Because \(\gamma>0\), every eigenvalue of \((I_n+\gamma L_C)^{-1}\) is at most one, and the constant vector supplies eigenvalue one.  Thus \(\|H_G\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\), and block diagonality gives \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\).  Substitution proves the final bound \(V_+-V_-\le2B^2\varepsilon\).

%%% FIELD proof-empty-graph-reduction
If \(C_G\) has no edges, every scanned vertex has no previously selected neighbor.  Definition \(\mathrm{def:wri\mbox{-}law}\) therefore gives \(S_{ell}=V\) for every order and rule, so \(K_{ell}=n\).  Moreover, Definition \(\mathrm{def:conflict\mbox{-}graph}\) implies that the sets \(D_i\) are pairwise disjoint.  Since \(i\in D_i\) for all \(i\) and there are \(n\) such nonempty subsets of the \(n\)-element set \(V\), necessarily \(D_i=\{i\}\) for every \(i\).  Thus the construction chooses \(T_{ell}\) uniformly among all \(t(n)\)-subsets of \(V\), assigns precisely those units to treatment and the remaining \(c(n)\) units to control, and computes
\[
 \widehat\tau_{ell}
 =\frac1{t(n)}\sum_{i\in T_{ell}}Y_i(\mathbf 1)
  -\frac1{c(n)}\sum_{i\notin T_{ell}}Y_i(\mathbf 0).
\]
By full-neighborhood interference this is the ordinary balanced complete-randomization difference in means for the fixed finite-population total-effect endpoints.  Its distribution no longer depends on the priority order or on \(h_{ell i}\).  Hence the distribution of \(a_{ell}\), and therefore \(Q_{ell}=\mathbb E[a_{ell}a_{ell}^{\mathsf T}]\), is identical for all \(\ell\).  Consequently \(Q(q)=Q_1\) for every \(q\in\Delta_L\), so every mixture has the same risk.

For the smallest admissible check, take \(n=2\).  The two treatment allocations have
\[
 a_1=\tfrac12(1,-1,1,-1)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad a_2=-a_1,
\]
each with probability \(1/2\), and hence
\[
 Q_{ell}=\tfrac14(1,-1,1,-1)^{\mathsf T}(1,-1,1,-1)
\]
for every rule.  Direct enumeration therefore agrees simultaneously with the linear-risk identity, the tagged-count formulas \(F_{ell,\{i\}}(z)=F_{ell,\{1,2\}}(z)=z^2\), the rule-invariance conclusion, and the bound \(\|a_{ell}\|_2^2=2-2/n=1\).

The proved portion consists of the separator-local greedy certificate, the reduction of every dense risk matrix to \(O(n^2)\) one- and two-tagged count polynomials, polynomial output bit length, and an exact \(L\operatorname{poly}(n,b)\) integration algorithm when \(w=1\).  For a general bag with \(r\le w+1\) active priorities and total weight \(W\le nb\), the direct chamberwise polynomial representation has up to \(\binom{W+r}{r}\) monomial coefficients per count and discrete state.  Together with relative bag orders and selection/blocking labels, this yields only an \((n+nb)^{O(w+1)}2^{O(w\log(w+1))}\) dense-message architecture, whose polynomial exponent depends on \(w\).  No claim of fixed-parameter tractability or parameterized hardness follows from this representation-size calculation.
