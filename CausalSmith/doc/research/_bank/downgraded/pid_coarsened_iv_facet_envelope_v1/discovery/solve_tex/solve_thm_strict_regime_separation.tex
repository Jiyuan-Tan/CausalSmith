%%% FIELD proof-strict-regime-separation
For a trusted set \(S\) containing \(a\) and \(b\), define the observable-model image and the identified set, respectively, by
\[
 \mathcal P_S=\{p:\mathcal Q_S(p)\neq\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 \Theta_S(p)=\{\theta_{ab}(q):q\in\mathcal Q_S(p)\}.
\]
We first record the relevant nesting by an explicit restriction-map argument.  If \(S\subseteq S'\) and \(q'\in\mathcal Q_{S'}(p)\), push \(q'\) forward as follows: retain \(X_0,X_1\), retain \((V_x:x\in S)\), set
\[
 W_{xz}=V_x\quad(x\in S'\setminus S,\ z\in\{0,1\}),
\]
and retain the existing \(W_{xz}\) slots for \(x\in\mathcal E\setminus S'\).  For \(x\in S\), the defining probabilities \(\Pr(X_z=x,V_x=y)\) are unchanged.  For \(x\in S'\setminus S\),
\[
 \Pr(X_z=x,W_{xz}=y)=\Pr(X_z=x,V_x=y)=p^z_{xy},
\]
and the same equality is unchanged for \(x\in\mathcal E\setminus S'\).  Thus the definition of compatible response laws gives a law \(q\in\mathcal Q_S(p)\).  Because \(a,b\in S\), the map retains \(V_a,V_b\), whence
\[
 \theta_{ab}(q)=\mathbb E_q[V_b-V_a]
 =\mathbb E_{q'}[V_b-V_a]=\theta_{ab}(q').
\]
This finite pushforward construction proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{S'}\subseteq\mathcal P_S,
 \qquad
 \Theta_{S'}(p)\subseteq\Theta_S(p)\quad(p\in\mathcal P_{S'}).
 \tag{1}
\]

We next use the explicit response-law witness.  Its allocation probability is \(\rho=1/2\), so both instrument arms have positive probability.  Under \(q^{\rm sep}_{r,s,\tau,\lambda}\), the independence stipulated in the witness construction and the mixed-trust observation map give, for \(z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr(X_z=a,V_a=0)&=A_z(1-\tau_a),&
 \Pr(X_z=a,V_a=1)&=A_z\tau_a,\\
 \Pr(X_z=b,V_b=0)&=B_z(1-\tau_b),&
 \Pr(X_z=b,V_b=1)&=B_z\tau_b,\\
 \Pr(X_z=c_\star,W_{c_\star z}=0)&=r_z(1-\lambda_z),&
 \Pr(X_z=c_\star,W_{c_\star z}=1)&=r_z\lambda_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
These are exactly the displayed cells of \(p^{\rm sep}\).  Since \(c_\star\) is untrusted under \(T_0\), (2) satisfies every equality in the definition of \(\mathcal Q_{T_0}(p^{\rm sep})\).  Hence
\[
 q^{\rm sep}_{r,s,\tau,\lambda}\in\mathcal Q_{T_0}(p^{\rm sep}),
 \qquad p^{\rm sep}\in\mathcal P_{T_0}.
 \tag{3}
\]

The parameter ranges imply
\[
 1-r_z>\frac8{25},
 \qquad s_z,1-s_z,\tau_x,1-\tau_x>\frac25
 \quad(x\in\{a,b\}).
\]
Therefore each target-category cell in (2) is larger than
\[
 \frac8{25}\left(\frac25\right)^2
 =\frac{32}{625}>\frac{93}{5000}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For the two smaller nuisance cells,
\[
 r_0\lambda_0>\frac{31}{50}\frac3{100}=\frac{93}{5000},
 \qquad
 r_1(1-\lambda_1)>\frac{31}{50}\frac3{100}=\frac{93}{5000}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The other two nuisance cells exceed \((31/50)(93/100)\), because \(1-\lambda_0>93/100\) and \(\lambda_1>93/100\).  Equations (4)--(5) prove that every observed cell is strictly larger than \(93/5000\).

For \(x=a\), its exposure mass is \(A_z\), and for \(x=b\), its exposure mass is \(B_z\).  Since \(1-r_z<19/50\) and both \(s_z\) and \(1-s_z\) are smaller than \(3/5\),
\[
 A_z<\frac{57}{250},
 \qquad B_z<\frac{57}{250}.
\]
Every cell is bounded above by its category mass, so, for \(x\in\{a,b\}\) and \(y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 p^{{\rm sep},0}_{xy}+p^{{\rm sep},1}_{x,1-y}
 <2\frac{57}{250}=\frac{57}{125}.
 \tag{6}
\]

We now give a direct disjoint-event certificate for failure of full trust.  Under any law in \(\mathcal Q_{T_1}(p)\), the events
\[
 \mathcal A_0=\{X_0=c_\star,V_{c_\star}=0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_1=\{X_1=c_\star,V_{c_\star}=1\}
\]
are disjoint, because the same binary variable \(V_{c_\star}\) cannot equal both zero and one.  The compatibility equalities therefore imply the necessary inequality
\[
 p^0_{c_\star0}+p^1_{c_\star1}
 =\Pr(\mathcal A_0)+\Pr(\mathcal A_1)
 =\Pr(\mathcal A_0\cup\mathcal A_1)\leq1.
 \tag{7}
\]
For the separation table, however,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p^{{\rm sep},0}_{c_\star0}+p^{{\rm sep},1}_{c_\star1}
 &=r_0(1-\lambda_0)+r_1\lambda_1\\
 &>2\frac{31}{50}\frac{93}{100}
 =\frac{2883}{2500}>1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(p^{\rm sep}\notin\mathcal P_{T_1}\).  Together with (1) and (3), this proves strict observed-law inclusion \(\mathcal P_{T_1}\subsetneq\mathcal P_{T_0}\).

This separation is perturbation-stable.  Let \(p'\) be armwise normalized and satisfy
\(\lVert p'-p^{\rm sep}\rVert_\infty<1/200\).  By (4)--(5), every cell of \(p'\) is larger than
\[
 \frac{93}{5000}-\frac1{200}=\frac{68}{5000}>0.
 \tag{9}
\]
By (6), for every \(x\in\{a,b\}\) and \(y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 p'^0_{xy}+p'^1_{x,1-y}
 <\frac{57}{125}+\frac2{200}<1.
 \tag{10}
\]
Armwise normalization, nonnegativity from (9), and (10) satisfy the necessary and sufficient conditions in \(\mathrm{lem{:}compatibility}\), so \(p'\in\mathcal P_{T_0}\).  Conversely, (8) gives
\[
 p'^0_{c_\star0}+p'^1_{c_\star1}
 >\frac{2883}{2500}-\frac2{200}>1,
\]
which violates the direct necessary condition (7); hence \(p'\notin\mathcal P_{T_1}\).  This proves the asserted positive-radius obstruction in the affine space of armwise normalized tables.

It remains to prove a strict identified-set gain on the common domain.  The two arms of \(p^{\rm gain}\) are normalized and all twelve cells are positive.  For categories \(a\) and \(b\), every compatibility sum is \(1/5\).  For \(c_\star\), the two sums are
\[
 p^{{\rm gain},0}_{c_\star0}+p^{{\rm gain},1}_{c_\star1}=\frac9{10},
 \qquad
 p^{{\rm gain},0}_{c_\star1}+p^{{\rm gain},1}_{c_\star0}=\frac3{10}.
 \tag{11}
\]
All nonnegativity and compatibility inequalities are strict.  The compatibility lemma therefore gives \(p^{\rm gain}\in\mathcal P_{T_1}\), and the strict inequalities exhibit a relative neighborhood, within the arm-normalization affine space, contained in \(\mathcal P_{T_1}\).  Thus \(p^{\rm gain}\) is in the relative interior of the full-trust compatible set.  By (1), it also belongs to \(\mathcal P_{T_0}\).

At this table, the target cells give
\[
 \alpha_0=\alpha_1=\beta_0=\beta_1
 =\gamma_0=\gamma_1=\delta_0=\delta_1=\frac1{10},
 \qquad \kappa=-\frac35.
\]
The two individual diagonal caps are \(u=v=1/10\).  Hence the endpoint formula in \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp-envelope}\) yields
\[
 D_{T_0}(p^{\rm gain})=u+v=\frac15,
 \qquad
 L_{T_0}(p^{\rm gain})=-\frac35-\frac15=-\frac45.
 \tag{12}
\]
Outcome reversal leaves all target cells unchanged, so
\[
 U_{T_0}(p^{\rm gain})=-L_{T_0}(\bar p^{\rm gain})=\frac45.
 \tag{13}
\]
Under \(T_1\), the nuisance caps are
\[
 h_{c_\star0}=1-\frac5{10}-\frac4{10}=\frac1{10},
 \qquad
 h_{c_\star1}=1-\frac1{10}-\frac2{10}=\frac7{10}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 D_{T_1}(p^{\rm gain})
 =\min\left\{\frac15,\frac1{10},\frac7{10}\right\}=\frac1{10},
 \qquad
 L_{T_1}(p^{\rm gain})=-\frac7{10}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Outcome reversal interchanges the two nuisance caps and leaves the remaining quantities unchanged; hence \(U_{T_1}(p^{\rm gain})=7/10\).  Applying the sharp-envelope theorem to the compatible table proves
\[
 \Theta_{T_1}(p^{\rm gain})
 =\left[-\frac7{10},\frac7{10}\right]
 \subsetneq
 \left[-\frac45,\frac45\right]
 =\Theta_{T_0}(p^{\rm gain}).
 \tag{15}
\]

Finally, the gain itself is perturbation-stable.  Set \(\eta_{\rm gain}=1/100\), and let \(p'\) be armwise normalized with
\(\lVert p'-p^{\rm gain}\rVert_\infty<\eta_{\rm gain}\).  Every cell exceeds \(9/100\), and the largest compatibility sum in (11) can increase by less than \(2/100\); hence it remains smaller than one.  The compatibility lemma therefore gives \(p'\in\mathcal P_{T_1}\).

All eight target cells lie in \((9/100,11/100)\).  Each cross cap entering \(u\) or \(v\) is larger than \(1-2(11/100)=78/100\).  Thus the individual caps are determined by the corresponding target-cell minima and satisfy
\[
 \frac9{100}<u(p'),v(p')<\frac{11}{100},
 \qquad
 \frac{18}{100}<D_{T_0}(p')<\frac{22}{100}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The nuisance caps satisfy
\[
 \frac8{100}<h_{c_\star0}(p')<\frac{12}{100},
 \qquad
 \frac{68}{100}<h_{c_\star1}(p')<\frac{72}{100}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Equations (16)--(17) show that \(D_{T_1}(p')=h_{c_\star0}(p')\), and hence
\[
 L_{T_1}(p')-L_{T_0}(p')
 =D_{T_0}(p')-D_{T_1}(p')>\frac6{100}.
 \tag{18}
\]
For \(\bar p'\), all target cells obey the same bounds, while
\[
 h_{c_\star1}(\bar p')=h_{c_\star0}(p').
\]
Repeating (16)--(18) for \(\bar p'\), and using \(U_S(p')=-L_S(\bar p')\), gives
\[
 U_{T_0}(p')-U_{T_1}(p')>\frac6{100}.
 \tag{19}
\]
The sharp-envelope theorem and (18)--(19) therefore yield
\[
 [L_{T_1}(p'),U_{T_1}(p')]
 \subsetneq [L_{T_0}(p'),U_{T_0}(p')]
\]
throughout this relative open neighborhood.  The argument uses, respectively, a finite restriction pushforward, an explicit response-law witness, a disjoint-event capacity inequality, and direct evaluation of the sharp affine endpoint functional; all positivity, normalization, compatibility, and strict-slack conditions used by those steps were verified above.
