%%% FIELD proof-untrusted-refinement-invariance
Write \(\Delta(\mathcal I)\) for the probability simplex on \(\mathcal I=\mathcal E\times\{0,1\}\).  Thus the observable conditional table is \(p=(p^0,p^1)\in\Delta(\mathcal I)^2\), and, under the positive-allocation condition used by the sharp-envelope theorem, the observable law is determined by \((\rho,p)\) through
\[
 \Pr_P(Z=z,X=x,Y=y)=\rho^z(1-\rho)^{1-z}p^z_{xy}.
\]
The causal identified set is
\[
 \Theta_T(p):=\{\mathbb E_q[V_b-V_a]:q\in\mathcal Q_T(p)\};
\]
it is not defined to equal an endpoint interval.

Because \(g\) is a wholly untrusted refinement, no trusted category is split.  More precisely, the definition of a wholly untrusted refinement supplies a bijection
\[
 \iota:T\longrightarrow\widetilde T
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 g^{-1}(\{x\})=\{\iota(x)\}\quad(x\in T),
\]
whereas every element lying above \(U=\mathcal E\setminus T\) is untrusted.  The armwise push-forward condition therefore gives, for every \(x\in T\), \(y,z\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 p^z_{xy}
 =\sum_{\widetilde x:g(\widetilde x)=x}
       \widetilde p^z_{\widetilde x y}
 =\widetilde p^z_{\iota(x)y}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Aggregation preserves armwise normalization because
\[
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal E}\sum_{y=0}^1p^z_{xy}
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal E}\sum_{y=0}^1
   \sum_{\widetilde x:g(\widetilde x)=x}
       \widetilde p^z_{\widetilde x y}
 =\sum_{\widetilde x\in\widetilde{\mathcal E}}
   \sum_{y=0}^1\widetilde p^z_{\widetilde x y}=1.                 \tag{2}
\]
Nonnegativity is also preserved term by term.  For each trusted cell, equation \((1)\) gives
\[
 p^0_{xy}+p^1_{x,1-y}
 =\widetilde p^0_{\iota(x)y}
  +\widetilde p^1_{\iota(x),1-y}.                                \tag{3}
\]
The map \(x\mapsto\iota(x)\) bijects the entire family of trusted compatibility inequalities on the right of \((3)\) with the corresponding family on the left.  The compatibility lemma, applied once on \(\mathcal E\) and once on \(\widetilde{\mathcal E}\), together with normalization and nonnegativity in \((2)\), now yields
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal Q_T(p)\ne\varnothing.                                  \tag{4}
\]
This argument uses neither division nor strict positivity of any cell, so \((4)\) includes zero cells and equality in every compatibility inequality.

It remains to prove equality of the identifying functionals rather than stipulate it.  Put \(\widetilde a=\iota(a)\) and \(\widetilde b=\iota(b)\), and decorate endpoint components computed from \(\widetilde p\) with tildes.  Equation \((1)\), first for \(a\) and then for \(b\), implies for \(z\in\{0,1\}\)
\[
 \widetilde\alpha_z=\alpha_z,qquad
 \widetilde\gamma_z=\gamma_z,qquad
 \widetilde\beta_z=\beta_z,qquad
 \widetilde\delta_z=\delta_z.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Substitution in the definitions of \(\kappa\), \(u\), and \(v\) gives
\[
 \widetilde\kappa=\kappa,qquad
 \widetilde u=u,qquad
 \widetilde v=v.                                                   \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, \(c\mapsto\iota(c)\) is a bijection from \(T\setminus\{a,b\}\) to \(\widetilde T\setminus\{\widetilde a,\widetilde b\}\), and for each such \(c\) and each \(y\in\{0,1\}\), equation \((1)\) yields
\[
 \widetilde h_{\iota(c),y}
 =1-\widetilde p^0_{\iota(c)y}
    -\widetilde p^1_{\iota(c),1-y}
 =1-p^0_{cy}-p^1_{c,1-y}
 =h_{cy}.                                                          \tag{7}
\]
Consequently the two finite collections entering the minimum that defines the two-diagonal capacity are identical, including the case \(T=\{a,b\}\), when the collection consists only of \(u+v\).  Hence
\[
 D_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)
 =\min\!\left(\{\widetilde u+\widetilde v\}
       \cup\{\widetilde h_{\widetilde c y}:
               \widetilde c\in\widetilde T\setminus
                    \{\widetilde a,\widetilde b\},\ y\in\{0,1\}\}\right)
 =D_T(p).                                                          \tag{8}
\]
Equations \((6)\) and \((8)\), followed by the definition of the lower endpoint, prove
\[
 L_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)
 =\widetilde\kappa-D_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)
 =\kappa-D_T(p)
 =L_T(p).                                                          \tag{9}
\]

Outcome reversal commutes with push-forward: for every \(x,y,z\),
\[
 \sum_{\widetilde x:g(\widetilde x)=x}
      \overline{\widetilde p}^{,z}_{\widetilde x y}
 =\sum_{\widetilde x:g(\widetilde x)=x}
      \widetilde p^z_{\widetilde x,1-y}
 =p^z_{x,1-y}
 =\bar p^z_{xy}.                                                   \tag{10}
\]
Thus the already proved lower-endpoint argument applies to the pair \((\overline{\widetilde p},\bar p)\), and gives
\[
 L_{\widetilde T}(\overline{\widetilde p})=L_T(\bar p).
\]
Using the definition \(U_T(p)=-L_T(\bar p)\), we obtain
\[
 U_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)
 =-L_{\widetilde T}(\overline{\widetilde p})
 =-L_T(\bar p)
 =U_T(p).                                                          \tag{11}
\]

Finally suppose the equivalent compatibility conditions in \((4)\) hold.  The sharp-envelope theorem, applied to the coarse and refined models, identifies the causal sets as
\[
 \Theta_T(p)=[L_T(p),U_T(p)]
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \Theta_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)
   =[L_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p),U_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)].
\]
Equations \((9)\) and \((11)\) therefore prove
\[
 \Theta_{\widetilde T}(\widetilde p)=\Theta_T(p).
\]
This is sharp equality, not merely an outer bound, because the sharp-envelope theorem supplies endpoint-attaining response laws and mixtures attaining every interior point in each model.  Splitting a bin wholly inside \(U\) is exactly such a refinement, and merging those children is its push-forward comparison.  Hence either operation is target-ancillary, while no conclusion is asserted here for splitting a trusted category or declaring a refined child trusted.
