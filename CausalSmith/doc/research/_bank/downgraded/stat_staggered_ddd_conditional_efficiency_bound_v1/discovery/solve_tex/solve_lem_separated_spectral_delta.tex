Because the collection is finite, it suffices first to treat one of the maps, denoted by \(B\), and then intersect the resulting probability-one-asymptotic events.  Put \(B_0=B(z_0)\), and use \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) for the operator norm.  Continuous differentiability of \(B\) on a neighborhood of \(z_0\) implies local Lipschitz continuity there.  Since \(\widehat z-z_0=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), it follows that
\[
 \|B(\widehat z)-B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =O_P(n^{-1/2})=o_P(\tau_n),
 \tag{1}
\]
where the last equality uses \(n^{-1/2}/\tau_n\to0\).  In particular, \(\widehat z\to_P z_0\).

Order the eigenvalues of two real symmetric \(d\times d\) matrices increasingly.  The variational characterization of the \(k\)-th ordered eigenvalue gives
\[
 \bigl|\lambda_k(C)-\lambda_k(D)\bigr|
 \leq \sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigl|v'(C-D)v\bigr|
 \leq \|C-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Apply (2) with \(C=B(\widehat z)\) and \(D=B_0\).  By (1), by \(\tau_n\downarrow0\), and by the assumed lower bound \(\kappa_0\) on every nonzero eigenvalue of \(B_0\), with probability tending to one both
\[
 \|B(\widehat z)-B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\tau_n
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 2\tau_n<\kappa_0
 \tag{3}
\]
hold.  On this event, every eigenvalue paired with a zero eigenvalue of \(B_0\) has absolute value below \(\tau_n\), while every eigenvalue paired with a nonzero eigenvalue of \(B_0\) has absolute value at least
\[
 \kappa_0-\|B(\widehat z)-B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 >\kappa_0-\tau_n>\tau_n.
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus hard thresholding recovers \(\operatorname{rank}(B_0)\).  Applying this argument to the finitely many \(B_j\)'s and taking a finite intersection proves simultaneous rank recovery.

We next construct the differentiable selected-rank branch.  Choose a positively oriented circle \(\gamma\) centered at zero with radius strictly between zero and \(\kappa_0\).  Shrinking the neighborhood of \(z_0\) if necessary, spectral continuity as quantified by (2) ensures that \(\gamma\) encloses precisely the eigenvalue cluster emanating from the zero eigenvalues of \(B_0\).  Define its spectral projector by
\[
 P_0(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}
 \int_\gamma (wI-B(z))^{-1}\,dw.
 \tag{5}
\]
The resolvent identity
\[
 (wI-C)^{-1}-(wI-D)^{-1}
 =(wI-C)^{-1}(C-D)(wI-D)^{-1}
 \tag{6}
\]
and compactness of \(\gamma\) show that the integrand and its derivative are uniformly bounded on \(\gamma\) in a sufficiently small neighborhood.  Hence \(P_0(z)\) is continuously differentiable.  It is the orthogonal spectral projector onto the small-eigenvalue cluster and therefore commutes with \(B(z)\).  Set
\[
 B^{\sharp}(z)
 =(I-P_0(z))\{B(z)+P_0(z)\}^{-1}(I-P_0(z)).
 \tag{7}
\]
On the range of \(P_0(z)\), the eigenvalues of the matrix inside braces are \(1+\lambda\), with \(\lambda\) close to zero; on the orthogonal complement, they are the eigenvalues of \(B(z)\) separated from zero.  Thus the inverse in (7) exists locally.  The inverse rule together with (5)--(6) makes \(B^{\sharp}\) continuously differentiable.  At \(z_0\), it equals \(B_0^\dagger\).  On the rank-recovery event in (3)--(4), (7) inverts exactly the empirical eigenvalues whose absolute values exceed \(\tau_n\) and replaces the remaining eigenvalues by zero.  It consequently agrees there with the thresholded inverse.

For clarity, we derive the displayed derivative rather than assuming differentiability of the Moore--Penrose map across changing ranks.  Let \(D=B_0^\dagger\), \(P=B_0D=DB_0\), and \(Q=I-P\).  In an orthonormal basis adapted to \(\operatorname{ran}(B_0)\oplus\ker(B_0)\), write
\[
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda^{-1}&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{11}&H_{10}\\H_{01}&H_{00}\end{pmatrix},
 \tag{8}
\]
where \(\Lambda\) is invertible and \(H_{01}=H_{10}'\) because the matrix coordinate and its admissible directions are symmetric.  For the path \(B(t)=B_0+tH+o(t)\), the invariant subspace associated with the nonzero cluster is the graph
\[
 \left\{\binom{u}{tLu}+o(t):u\in\operatorname{ran}(B_0)\right\},
 \qquad
 L=H_{01}\Lambda^{-1},
 \tag{9}
\]
because the lower block of the eigen-subspace invariance equation at order \(t\) is \(H_{01}=L\Lambda\).  Restricting \(B(t)\) to this graph, inverting its range--range block, and embedding the inverse back into the ambient space yields
\[
 B^{\sharp}(t)
 =D+t
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\Lambda^{-1}H_{11}\Lambda^{-1}&\Lambda^{-2}H_{10}\\
 H_{01}\Lambda^{-2}&0
 \end{pmatrix}+o(t).
 \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the upper-left block in (10) follows from
\[
 (\Lambda+tH_{11})^{-1}
 =\Lambda^{-1}-t\Lambda^{-1}H_{11}\Lambda^{-1}+o(t),
\]
and the two off-diagonal blocks follow by multiplying this zeroth-order inverse by the graph and adjoint-graph terms in (9).  Translating (10) from blocks to the projectors \(P\) and \(Q\) gives, for every symmetric direction \(H\),
\[
 D B_0^{\sharp}[H]
 =-B_0^\dagger H B_0^\dagger
 +(B_0^\dagger)^2H(I-B_0B_0^\dagger)
 +(I-B_0^\dagger B_0)H(B_0^\dagger)^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
The null--null block is zero, as it must be because the selected branch discards the entire small-eigenvalue cluster.

Apply this construction to each of the finitely many matrix coordinates.  Addition and multiplication preserve continuous differentiability, and division does so on a neighborhood whenever its denominator is nonzero at \(z_0\).  Therefore the stated finite composition has a continuously differentiable selected-rank version, say \(F^\sharp\), near \(z_0\).  With \(d_0=DF(z_0)\), ordinary finite-dimensional differentiability gives
\[
 F^\sharp(z_0+h)-F(z_0)=d_0'h+o(\|h\|).
 \tag{12}
\]
Substitution of \(h=\widehat z-z_0=O_P(n^{-1/2})\) into (12) gives a remainder \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Finally, simultaneous rank recovery implies
\[
 F_{\tau_n}(\widehat z)=F^\sharp(\widehat z)
\]
with probability tending to one.  Combining this equality with (12) proves
\[
 F_{\tau_n}(\widehat z)-F(z_0)
 =d_0'(\widehat z-z_0)+o_P(n^{-1/2}),
\]
as claimed.
