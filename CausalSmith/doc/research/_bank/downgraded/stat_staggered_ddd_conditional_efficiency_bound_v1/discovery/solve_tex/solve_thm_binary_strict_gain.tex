Fix one of the two declared comparators and abbreviate \(b=b^{\mathrm{cmp}}\).  First note from Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) that the cell-residual terms have conditional mean zero.  Hence, for \((k,c)\in\mathcal I\), with \(k=(g,t)\),
\[
 E_P[\Psi_{k,c,P}(O)\mid X]
 =\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{\pi_{g,1}}\{q_{k,c}(X)-\theta_k(P)\}
 =h_k(X).
\tag{E7}
\]
The last equality uses the observable clean-comparison equality, under which \(q_{k,c}=q_{k,c_0(k)}\).  Both \(b\) and \(b^\star(X)\) are feasible, so \(A'b=A'b^\star(X)=\omega\).  It follows from (E7) that
\[
 E_P[b'\Psi_P\mid X]=E_P[b^\star(X)'\Psi_P\mid X]=\omega'h(X).
\tag{E8}
\]
The definition of \(\theta_k(P)\) also gives \(E_P[h_k(X)]=0\), so both scores in (E8) are mean zero.
Consequently the conditional-variance identity and the definition of \(\Gamma\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(b'\Psi_P)-\operatorname{Var}_P(b^{\star\prime}\Psi_P)
 =E_P[b'\Gamma_P(X)b-b^{\star\prime}\Gamma_P(X)b^\star].
\tag{E9}
\]

Because the columns of \(N\) are an orthonormal basis of \(\ker(A')\), there is a measurable vector \(z(X)\) such that
\[
 d(X):=b-b^\star(X)=Nz(X).
\tag{E10}
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:singular-operator}\) gives \(N'\Gamma_P(X)b^\star(X)=0\).  Expanding the integrand in (E9) therefore yields, pointwise,
\[
 b'\Gamma b-b^{\star\prime}\Gamma b^\star
 =2d'\Gamma b^\star+d'\Gamma d
 =z'H z
 =(b-b^\star)'\Gamma(b-b^\star).
\tag{E11}
\]
Since \(V_{\mathrm{agg}}=\operatorname{Var}_P(b^{\star\prime}\Psi_P)\), integration of (E11) proves (E1).  Adding the defining identities
\[
 V_{\mathrm{agg}}-V_{\mathrm{can}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{normal}}(P),
 \qquad
 V_{\mathrm{can}}-V_{\mathrm{OVS}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{model}}(P)
\tag{E12}
\]
proves (E2) and (E3).

For the first equality characterization, again use (E10) and Lemma \(\ref{lem:singular-operator}\) to obtain
\[
 N'\Gamma_P(X)b
 =N'\Gamma_P(X)\{b-b^\star(X)\}
 =H(X)z(X).
\tag{E13}
\]
If (E13) vanishes, the integrand \(z'Hz\) in (E11) vanishes.  Conversely, equality in (E1) implies \(E_P[z'Hz]=0\).  The integrand is nonnegative because \(H=N'\Gamma N\) is positive semidefinite, so \(z'H z=0\) almost surely.  For a positive semidefinite matrix, \(z'Hz=\|H^{1/2}z\|^2=0\) implies \(Hz=0\); (E13) then vanishes.  This proves (E4), including singular cases.

It remains to distinguish the two model-normal equations.  Let \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{per}}\) and \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{OVS}}\) denote the closed measurable spans generated by \(Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\) and \(Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\), respectively.  If \(b^{\mathrm{ref}}\) puts weight \(\omega_k\) on \((k,c_0(k))\) and zero on the other coordinates, then \(A'b^{\mathrm{ref}}=\omega\) and \(\psi_P^0=b^{\mathrm{ref}\prime}\Psi_P\).  (b-b^{\mathrm{ref}})'\Psi_P=(b-b^{\mathrm{ref}})'R_P\in\mathcal C_P.
\tag{E14}

 =-\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{\pi_{g,1}}

\tag{E15}

Every formal row on the right of (E15) is in the row span defining \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{per}}\).  Moreover, both \(c\) and \(c_0(k)\) exceed \(t\ge u\), so each such row is also an OVS row.  Linear decomposition within each target block, followed by closure, therefore proves
\[
 \mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathscr S_{\mathrm{per}}
 \subseteq\mathscr S_{\mathrm{OVS}}.
\tag{E16}

We next verify the projection formulas, including singular cases.  For either normal vector \(Z\), write
\[
 K(X)=E_P[ZZ'\mid X],\qquad r(X)=E_P[Z\psi_P^0\mid X].

If \(v\in\ker K(X)\), then

 E_P[(v'Z)^2\mid X]=v'K(X)v=0,
\]
and hence \(v'Z=0\) conditionally almost surely and \(v'r(X)=0\).  Thus \(r(X)\in\operatorname{range}K(X)\), and
\[
 E_P\!\left[Z\{\psi_P^0-r(X)'K(X)^\dagger Z\}\mid X\right]
 =r(X)-K(X)K(X)^\dagger r(X)=0.
\tag{E17}
\]
Moreover, with \(\alpha=K^\dagger r\), expansion of the nonnegative conditional square \(E_P[(\psi_P^0-\alpha'Z)^2\mid X]\) shows that \(r'K^\dagger r\le E_P[(\psi_P^0)^2\mid X]\); hence the displayed projection is square-integrable.  Equation (E17), first for bounded measurable multipliers and then for their \(L_2(P)\) closure, proves that \(r'K^\dagger Z\) is the orthogonal projection of \(\psi_P^0\) onto the corresponding normal span.  Therefore \(\phi_{\mathrm{can}}\) is orthogonal to \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{per}}\), and \(\phi_{\mathrm{OVS}}\) is orthogonal to \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{OVS}}\).  In the fixed-finite-support conditional-mean-interior regime, Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\) identifies these spans with the respective tangent orthocomplements, and Theorem \(\ref{thm:complete-normal-canonical-gradient}\) identifies the residuals with the corresponding canonical gradients.

 =\|g-\phi_{\mathrm{can}}\|_{L_2(P)}^2,
 \qquad

 =\|g-\phi_{\mathrm{OVS}}\|_{L_2(P)}^2.
\tag{E18}

The first difference in (E18) is zero exactly when \(g\perp\mathscr S_{\mathrm{per}}\), and the second is zero exactly when \(g\perp\mathscr S_{\mathrm{OVS}}\).  E_P[Zg\mid X]=0
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely};
\tag{E19}
\]
the forward implication follows by the defining property of conditional expectation, and the reverse implication follows by testing against bounded measurable multipliers and truncating.  Applying (E19) to the two normal vectors proves (E5) and (E6).  Together with (E4), these are precisely the three residuals reported in Definition \(\ref{def:projection-diagnostic}\).

Because the collection of matrix maps is finite, it suffices first to treat one map and then intersect the resulting probability-one-asymptotic events.  Write \(B_0=B(z_0)\).  Continuous differentiability on a neighborhood of \(z_0\) gives a finite local Lipschitz constant, and \(\widehat z-z_0=O_P(n^{-1/2})\) therefore implies
\[
 \|B(\widehat z)-B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(n^{-1/2})=o_P(\tau_n).
\tag{S1}
\]
For completeness, the eigenvalue perturbation bound needed here follows directly from the variational characterization.  For every unit vector \(v\),
\[
 |v'\{B(\widehat z)-B_0\}v|
 \le \|B(\widehat z)-B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\]
and applying this inequality inside the successive minimum--maximum formulas for the ordered eigenvalues gives
\[
 \max_k|\lambda_k\{B(\widehat z)\}-\lambda_k(B_0)|
 \le \|B(\widehat z)-B_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{S2}
\]
With probability tending to one, the right side of (S2) is smaller than \(\tau_n\), while \(\tau_n<\kappa_0/2\).  On that event, every eigenvalue descending from the zero cluster has absolute value below \(\tau_n\), and every eigenvalue descending from a nonzero population eigenvalue has absolute value at least \(\kappa_0/2>\tau_n\).  Thus absolute-value thresholding recovers the population rank.  Finiteness of the collection proves simultaneous recovery.

We next construct the local differentiable inverse branch rather than assuming it.  Choose a positively oriented circle \(\gamma\) centered at zero whose interior contains no nonzero eigenvalue of \(B_0\).  Shrinking the neighborhood if necessary, the resolvent exists on \(\gamma\) and the same circle separates the eigenvalue cluster emanating from zero from all remaining eigenvalues.  Define
\[
 P_0(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}\int_\gamma(wI-B(z))^{-1}\,dw.
\tag{S3}
\]
The resolvent identity
\[
 (wI-B_1)^{-1}-(wI-B_2)^{-1}
 =(wI-B_1)^{-1}(B_1-B_2)(wI-B_2)^{-1}
\tag{S4}
\]
and differentiation of the inverse show directly that \(P_0(z)\) is continuously differentiable.  Diagonalizing the real symmetric matrix \(B(z)\) inside the integrand shows coordinatewise that the integral is one on the separated small-eigenvalue cluster and zero on its complement.  Hence \(P_0(z)\) is the orthogonal spectral projector onto that cluster.  Set
\[
 B^\sharp(z)
 =(I-P_0(z))\{B(z)+P_0(z)\}^{-1}(I-P_0(z)).
\tag{S5}
\]
The middle matrix is nonsingular near \(z_0\): it acts as \(B(z)\) on the separated nonzero cluster and as \(B(z)+I\) on the small cluster.  Equations (S3)--(S5) therefore make \(B^\sharp\) continuously differentiable.  At \(z_0\) it equals \(B_0^\dagger\), and on the rank-recovery event it agrees with the thresholded inverse by zeroing the small cluster and inverting every retained eigenvalue.

To compute its derivative, decompose the Euclidean space orthogonally as \(\operatorname{range}(B_0)\oplus\ker(B_0)\).  In this basis write
\[
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B_0^\dagger=\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1}&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{11}&H_{12}\\H_{21}&H_{22}\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(D\) is nonsingular.  Differentiating (S3)--(S5), block by block, gives
\[
 DB_0^\sharp[H]
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 -D^{-1}H_{11}D^{-1}&D^{-2}H_{12}\\
 H_{21}D^{-2}&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{S6}
\]
Multiplying the displayed block matrices verifies that (S6) is exactly
\[
 DB_0^\sharp[H]
 =-B_0^\dagger HB_0^\dagger
 +(B_0^\dagger)^2H(I-B_0B_0^\dagger)
 +(I-B_0^\dagger B_0)H(B_0^\dagger)^2.
\tag{S7}
\]

Every ordinary division appearing in \(F\) is continuously differentiable on a sufficiently small neighborhood because its denominator is nonzero at \(z_0\).  Equations (S5)--(S7), ordinary matrix calculus, and the chain rule thus show that the selected-rank composition \(F^\sharp\) is continuously differentiable at \(z_0\).  Its first-order expansion is
\[
 F^\sharp(\widehat z)-F^\sharp(z_0)
 =DF(z_0)'(\widehat z-z_0)+o_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{S8}
\]
On the simultaneous rank-recovery event, \(F_{\tau_n}(\widehat z)=F^\sharp(\widehat z)\), while \(F^\sharp(z_0)=F(z_0)\).  Substitution into (S8) proves the claimed expansion and completes the proof.

\tag{W11}
\]
Since \(\widehat\mu-\mu(P)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n[U(O_i)-\mu(P)]\), equations (W8) and (W11) give
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp},z}(P)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_z(O_i)+o_P(1).
\tag{W12}
\]
The summands are independent and identically distributed by the sampling assumption, have mean zero by definition, and have variance \(V_{\Delta,z}\in(0,\infty)\) by (W10) and the stated positivity condition.  Lemma \(\ref{lem:iid-central-limit}\), followed by the elementary fact that adding an \(o_P(1)\) term preserves convergence at every continuity point, proves the normal limit in (W2).

It remains to prove variance consistency under the stated moments.  Rank recovery and continuity of the selected branch imply
\[
 \widehat d_z\longrightarrow_P d_z.
\tag{W13}
\]
For any entry \(W=U_jU_k\), \(E_P|W|<\infty\) by Cauchy--Schwarz and (W10).  Let \(W_M=W\mathbf 1\{|W|\le M\}\).  The sample average of bounded \(W_M\) converges in probability to its expectation by Chebyshev's inequality, while Markov's inequality gives, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 P\{\mathbb P_n|W-W_M|>\epsilon\}
 \le \epsilon^{-1}E_P[|W|\mathbf 1\{|W|>M\}].
\tag{W14}
\]
The right side tends to zero as \(M\to\infty\).  Finiteness of the matrix dimension and \(\widehat\mu\to_P\mu(P)\) consequently imply
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \{U(O_i)-\widehat\mu\}\{U(O_i)-\widehat\mu\}'
 \longrightarrow_P\Sigma_U.
\tag{W15}
\]
Premultiplying and postmultiplying (W15) by \(\widehat d_z\), and using (W13), gives
\[
 \widehat V_{\Delta,\mathrm{cmp}}
 \longrightarrow_P d_z'\Sigma_Ud_z=V_{\Delta,z}.
\tag{W16}
\]

Let \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) be the \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of the standard normal law.  Equations (W12) and (W16), with \(V_{\Delta,z}>0\), imply
\[
 T_{\Delta,\mathrm{cmp}}\Rightarrow N(0,1),
\]
so the interval
\[
 \left[\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp}}
 -z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat V_{\Delta,\mathrm{cmp}}/n},\ 
 \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp}}
 +z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat V_{\Delta,\mathrm{cmp}}/n}\right]
\]
has pointwise limiting coverage \(1-\alpha\).  Finally, (W12) gives \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp},z}(P)=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), and (W16) makes the half-width \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  If \(\Delta_{\mathrm{cmp},z}(P)\ge\delta_0>0\), their sum in absolute value is below \(\delta_0/2\) with probability tending to one.  The lower endpoint is then at least \(\delta_0/2\), as claimed.  The argument fixes the law and its selected ranks throughout, so it makes no uniform assertion at an equality or rank-changing boundary.
