%%% FIELD ovs-orthogonal-normal-signal-proof
Fix \(e=(u,c,c')\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{OVS}}\), and write
\[
\sigma_{e,c,1}=1,\qquad \sigma_{e,c,0}=-1,\qquad
\sigma_{e,c',1}=-1,\qquad \sigma_{e,c',0}=1.
\]
All conditional expectations below exist by the integrability in the nuisance and signal definitions and the displayed lower bound on the candidate propensities.  For one of the four cells \((a,s)\), Definition \(\ref{def:orthogonal-ovs-normal-signal}\), the definition of \(p_{a,s}\), and the definition of \(m_{a,s,u}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_P\!\left[\widetilde m_{a,s,u}(X)
+\frac{\mathbf 1\{G=a,S=s\}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}(X)}
 \{\Delta Y_u-\widetilde m_{a,s,u}(X)\}\,\middle|\,X\right]\\
&\qquad=\widetilde m_{a,s,u}(X)
+\frac{p_{a,s}(X)}{\widetilde p_{a,s}(X)}
 \{m_{a,s,u}(X)-\widetilde m_{a,s,u}(X)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35a}
\]
Subtracting \(m_{a,s,u}(X)\) from the last expression and collecting terms yields the exact identity
\[
\widetilde m_{a,s,u}-m_{a,s,u}
+\frac{p_{a,s}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}}(m_{a,s,u}-\widetilde m_{a,s,u})
=\frac{\widetilde p_{a,s}-p_{a,s}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}}
 (\widetilde m_{a,s,u}-m_{a,s,u}).
\tag{35b}
\]
The signal is the signed sum of these four augmented cell means.  Multiplying (35b) by the declared signs and summing proves
\[
E_P[S_e^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O;\widetilde\eta)\mid X]-g_e^{\mathrm{OVS}}(X)
=\sum_{(a,s)\in\{(c,1),(c,0),(c',1),(c',0)\}}
\sigma_{e,a,s}
\frac{\widetilde p_{a,s}(X)-p_{a,s}(X)}{\widetilde p_{a,s}(X)}
\{\widetilde m_{a,s,u}(X)-m_{a,s,u}(X)\},
\]
which is (35).  Along a one-dimensional nuisance path through the truth, the right side has no term linear in a propensity perturbation with the regression fixed at its truth, and no term linear in a regression perturbation with the propensity fixed at its truth.  More generally, along a joint differentiable path both factors are of order equal to the path parameter, so their product is second order.  This proves the two Gateaux derivatives are zero.

By Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\), a law induced by the published OVS model obeys \(d_{c,u}(X)=d_{c',u}(X)\) for every retained OVS row.  Hence \(g_e^{\mathrm{OVS}}(X)=0\) almost surely.  Substitution of \(\widetilde\eta=(p,m)\) into Definition \(\ref{def:orthogonal-ovs-normal-signal}\) then gives
\[
S_e^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O;\eta)=Z_{e,P}^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Finally, the assumed clipping gives \(\widetilde p_{a,s}\ge\varepsilon/2\), and therefore
\[
\left\|
\frac{\widetilde p_{a,s}-p_{a,s}}{\widetilde p_{a,s}}
(\widetilde m_{a,s,u}-m_{a,s,u})
\right\|_{P_X,2}
\le \frac{2}{\varepsilon}
\|\widetilde p_{a,s}-p_{a,s}\|_{P_X,4}
\|\widetilde m_{a,s,u}-m_{a,s,u}\|_{P_X,4}
\tag{35c}
\]
by H\"older's inequality.  There are four terms per row and a fixed finite number of rows because \(T\) and \(\mathcal G\) are fixed.  Applying the triangle inequality to (35c), and using the finite-dimensional norms declared for \(p\) and \(m\), gives (36) with a constant \(C_\varepsilon\) depending only on \(\varepsilon\) and the fixed number of coordinates.

%%% FIELD ovs-normal-finite-support-rates-proof
Write the fixed support as \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_D\}\), and put \(\nu_d=P(X=x_d)\).  The asserted lower support-mass bound and retained-cell overlap imply
\[
P(X=x_d,G=a,S=s)=\nu_d p_{a,s}(x_d)\ge \underline\nu\varepsilon>0
\tag{37a}
\]
for every retained \((d,a,s)\).  Under the stated fixed-fold construction, every training complement contains order \(n\) observations.  For any one of the finitely many cell variables \(W\) used below, let \(\widehat E_{-\ell}[W\mid a,s,x_d]\) denote its training-cell mean.  Conditional fourth moments of \(\Delta Y_u\), retained-cell overlap, and fixed \(T\) imply finite second moments for: indicators; indicator-weighted increments; products of two increments; and every fixed finite linear combination of these variables.  Chebyshev's inequality applied to their empirical numerators and denominators, followed by the elementary ratio identity
\[
\frac{A_n}{B_n}-\frac{A}{B}
=\frac{A_n-A}{B_n}+\frac{A(B-B_n)}{BB_n},
\tag{37b}
\]
on the event \(B_n\ge B/2\), proves, uniformly over the finite sets of folds, support points, cells, periods, and coordinates,
\[
\widehat p_{a,s}^{-\ell}(x_d)-p_{a,s}(x_d)=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
\qquad
\widehat m_{a,s,u}^{-\ell}(x_d)-m_{a,s,u}(x_d)=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{37c}
\]
The same argument gives the root-\(n\) rate for every training preliminary target ratio and every empirical conditional raw moment of degree at most two in the increments.  Since \(D\) and all vector dimensions are fixed,
\[
\|f\|_{P_X,4}\le \max_{1\le d\le D}|f(x_d)|
\tag{37d}
\]
coordinatewise, so the same rates hold in all displayed \(L_4(P_X)\) norms.

We next pass from the primitive cell moments to the two learned conditional moments.  On the event supplied by (37a)--(37c), all clipped reciprocals are bounded by \(2/\varepsilon\).  The explicit formulas in Definitions \(\ref{def:orthogonal-ovs-normal-signal}\) and \(\ref{def:crossfit-estimator}\) are then finite sums and products of the empirical quantities in (37c), bounded reciprocals, and conditional raw moments of degree at most two.  Repeated use of
\[
ab-a_0b_0=(a-a_0)b_0+a_0(b-b_0)+(a-a_0)(b-b_0)
\tag{37e}
\]
shows entry by entry that
\[
\widehat K_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}(x_d)-K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(x_d)
=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
\qquad
\widehat r_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}(x_d)-r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(x_d)
=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{37f}
\]
This calculation includes the off-diagonal products of score coordinates sharing an outcome cell; they are empirical cross-products in (37e), not omitted blockwise terms.  Taking the finite maximum, using equivalence of finite-dimensional matrix norms, and applying (37d) proves (37).

It remains to justify thresholding and the coefficient rate, including singular cases.  Fix a support point and abbreviate
\[
K=K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(x_d),\quad
\widehat K=\widehat K_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}(x_d),\quad
r=r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(x_d),\quad
\widehat r=\widehat r_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}(x_d).
\]
Both \(K\) and \(\widehat K\) are symmetric positive semidefinite second-moment matrices.  For ordered eigenvalues, the variational characterization of eigenvalues and the inequality \(|v'(\widehat K-K)v|\le\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) for \(\|v\|=1\) give
\[
|\lambda_j(\widehat K)-\lambda_j(K)|
\le\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{38a}
\]
At the fixed law, the minimum over all positive eigenvalues of all \(K(x_d)\) is a strictly positive number \(\delta_P\), with the zero-rank convention if no such eigenvalue exists.  Since \(n^{-1/2}/\tau_n\to0\) and \(\tau_n\to0\), (38a) implies simultaneously over the finite support and folds that every population zero eigenvalue is below \(\tau_n\) and every positive eigenvalue is above \(\tau_n\), with probability tending to one.  This proves rank recovery.

The population right-hand side is compatible with the singular system.  Indeed, if \(v\in\ker K\), then
\[
0=v'Kv=E_P[(v'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}})^2\mid X=x_d],
\]
so \(v'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}=0\) conditionally almost surely and consequently \(v'r=E_P[v'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\psi_P^0\mid X=x_d]=0\).  Thus \(r\in\operatorname{range}K\), and \(\alpha=K^\dagger r\) is the unique solution in \(\operatorname{range}K\).

At a support point where \(K\) has rank zero, rank recovery makes the retained matrix zero, so \(\widehat\alpha=\alpha=0\) and the coefficient-rate conclusion is immediate.  Consider a positive-rank support point.  On the rank-recovery event let \(\Pi\) and \(\widehat\Pi\) be the orthogonal projectors onto the ranges of \(K\) and the retained \(\widehat K\).  When \(\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\delta_P/4\), every retained eigenvalue is at least \(\delta_P/2\).  If \(\widehat u\) is a retained unit eigenvector and \(Q=I-\Pi\), then
\[
\lambda(\widehat K)Q\widehat u
=Q\widehat K\widehat u
=Q(\widehat K-K)\widehat u,
\]
because \(QK=0\).  Hence \(\|Q\widehat u\|\le 2\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\delta_P\).  Applying this to a retained orthonormal basis, and using equality of the two nonzero principal-angle spectra for equal-rank subspaces, gives
\[
\|\widehat\Pi-\Pi\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le C_P\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{38b}
\]
This is an elementary consequence of the displayed eigenvector equation, rather than an extra rank or spectral assumption.

Since \(\|\widehat K_\tau^\dagger\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\delta_P\) on this event and \(\widehat K_\tau^\dagger\widehat K=\widehat\Pi\), the identities \(r=K\alpha\) and \(\Pi\alpha=\alpha\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\alpha-\alpha
&=\widehat K_\tau^\dagger(\widehat r-r)
+\widehat K_\tau^\dagger(K-\widehat K)\alpha
+(\widehat\Pi-\Pi)\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38c}
\]
Equations (37f), (38b), and (38c) yield \(\widehat\alpha-\alpha=O_P(n^{-1/2})\) at each support point.  The finite maximum and (37d) give the stated sharper \(L_4(P_X)\) rate uniformly over folds.  Since \(\tau_n\le1\) eventually, this sharper fixed-law statement also implies the conservative bound
\[
\|\widehat\alpha_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}
-K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}^\dagger r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\|_{P_X,4}
=O_P(n^{-1/2}/\tau_n),
\]
which is (38).  If \(\tau_n=n^{-a}\), the right side is \(O_P(n^{-1/2+a})\); its square is \(O_P(n^{-1+2a})=o_P(n^{-1/2})\) for \(a<1/4\), and its product with either rate in (37c) is \(O_P(n^{-1+a})=o_P(n^{-1/2})\) for the same range.  This proves all assertions.

%%% FIELD crossfit-attainment-proof
Let \(\eta=(p,m)\), and define the population projection coefficient and uncentered reference score by
\[
\alpha_P(x)=K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(x)^\dagger r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(x),
\qquad
\mu_P(O)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\omega_k R_{k,c_0(k),P}(O).
\tag{41a}
\]
Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\) gives every published observable equality, and Lemma \(\ref{lem:ovs-orthogonal-normal-signal}\) therefore gives
\[
S^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O;\eta)=Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O),
\qquad E_P[Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\mid X]=0.
\tag{41b}
\]
The fixed support-mass lower bound, retained-cell overlap, independent fixed-\(T\) sampling, and the conditional fourth-moment envelope give the root-\(n\) finite-cell rates derived in Proposition \(\ref{prop:ovs-normal-finite-support-rates}\).  With \(\tau_n=n^{-a}\), \(0<a<1/4\), that proposition also gives, uniformly over the fixed number of folds,
\[
\|\widehat\alpha_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}-\alpha_P\|_{P_X,4}
=O_P(n^{-1/2+a})=o_P(1).
\tag{41c}
\]

We first expand the cross-fitted ratio part.  For \(k=(g,t)\), put
\[
M_{a,s,k}(x)=\sum_{u=g}^t m_{a,s,u}(x),
\qquad
\widehat M_{a,s,k}^{-\ell}(x)=\sum_{u=g}^t\widehat m_{a,s,u}^{-\ell}(x),
\]
and let \(B_{k,c,P}\) denote the bracketed numerator in Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\), so \(R_{k,c,P}=B_{k,c,P}/\pi_{g,1}\).  Conditional on the training complement, direct addition and subtraction of the true regressions in the estimated bracket gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[\widehat B_{k,c}^{-\ell}-B_{k,c,P}\mid X,\mathcal D_{-\ell}]
={}&\left(\frac{\widehat p_{g,1}^{-\ell}p_{g,0}}{\widehat p_{g,0}^{-\ell}}-p_{g,1}\right)
(\widehat M_{g,0,k}^{-\ell}-M_{g,0,k})\\
&+\left(\frac{\widehat p_{g,1}^{-\ell}p_{c,1}}{\widehat p_{c,1}^{-\ell}}-p_{g,1}\right)
(\widehat M_{c,1,k}^{-\ell}-M_{c,1,k})\\
&+\left(p_{g,1}-\frac{\widehat p_{g,1}^{-\ell}p_{c,0}}{\widehat p_{c,0}^{-\ell}}\right)
(\widehat M_{c,0,k}^{-\ell}-M_{c,0,k}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{41d}
\]
There is no \((g,1)\) regression-error term because the plug-in conditional contrast and the treated-cell residual in the first bracket cancel it algebraically.  Clipping and overlap make each coefficient in parentheses Lipschitz in \(\widehat p^{-\ell}-p\).  The finite-cell root-\(n\) rates and H\"older's inequality consequently imply
\[
\|E_P[\widehat B_{k,c}^{-\ell}-B_{k,c,P}\mid X,\mathcal D_{-\ell}]\|_{P_X,2}
=O_P(n^{-1}),
\qquad
\|\widehat B_{k,c}^{-\ell}-B_{k,c,P}\|_{P,2}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{41e}
\]
The second bound follows by expanding the displayed score formula: every coefficient error is root-\(n\), all inverse propensities are bounded, and every residual has a bounded conditional second moment by the conditional fourth-moment envelope.

By fixed-fold cross-fitting, conditional on \(\mathcal D_{-\ell}\) the validation observations in \(I_\ell\) are independent of the estimated functions used there.  Conditional Chebyshev inequality applied to the centered difference in (41e), followed by summation over the fixed number of folds, yields
\[
\frac1n\sum_{\ell=1}^L\sum_{i\in I_\ell}
\widehat B_{i,k,c}^{-\ell}
=P B_{k,c,P}+(\mathbb P_n-P)B_{k,c,P}+o_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{41f}
\]
Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) gives \(P B_{k,c,P}=\pi_{g,1}\theta_k(P)\).  Also, retained-cell overlap implies \(\pi_{g,1}>0\), while independent sampling and finite variance give \(\widehat\pi_{g,1}-\pi_{g,1}=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  The exact scalar expansion
\[
\widehat\pi_{g,1}^{-1}-\pi_{g,1}^{-1}
=-\pi_{g,1}^{-2}(\widehat\pi_{g,1}-\pi_{g,1})
+\frac{(\widehat\pi_{g,1}-\pi_{g,1})^2}
{\pi_{g,1}^2\widehat\pi_{g,1}}
\tag{41g}
\]
has an \(O_P(n^{-1})\) remainder.  Combining (41f)--(41g), and using
\(\widehat\pi_{g,1}-\pi_{g,1}=(\mathbb P_n-P)\mathbf 1\{G=g,S=1\}\), proves
\[
\frac{n^{-1}\sum_{\ell}\sum_{i\in I_\ell}\widehat B_{i,k,c}^{-\ell}}
{\widehat\pi_{g,1}}-\theta_k(P)
=(\mathbb P_n-P)\left\{
R_{k,c,P}-\frac{\theta_k(P)}{\pi_{g,1}}\mathbf 1\{G=g,S=1\}
\right\}+o_P(n^{-1/2})
=(\mathbb P_n-P)\Psi_{k,c,P}+o_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{41h}
\]
Thus the global empirical share contributes exactly the denominator-derivative term in the corrected score; it is not absorbed into an uncorrected centering.

We next expand the complete-normal correction.  Lemma \(\ref{lem:ovs-orthogonal-normal-signal}\), the root-\(n\) cell rates, and (41b) imply
\[
\|E_P[\widehat S^{\mathrm{OVS},-\ell}\mid X,\mathcal D_{-\ell}]\|_{P_X,2}
=O_P(n^{-1}),
\qquad
\|\widehat S^{\mathrm{OVS},-\ell}-Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\|_{P,2}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{41i}
\]
The finite-support population coefficient \(\alpha_P\) is finite at every support point under the Moore--Penrose convention.  Equations (41c), (41i), H\"older's inequality, and the conditional fourth-moment envelope therefore give
\[
\|\widehat\alpha_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}(X)'
\widehat S^{\mathrm{OVS},-\ell}
-\alpha_P(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\|_{P,2}=o_P(1).
\tag{41j}
\]
Moreover, (41i) and \(\widehat\alpha_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}=O_P(1)\) make the conditional expectation of the first product in (41j) \(O_P(n^{-1})\), whereas the expectation of the second product is zero by (41b).  Cross-fit independence and conditional Chebyshev inequality now show
\[
\frac1n\sum_{\ell=1}^L\sum_{i\in I_\ell}
\widehat\alpha_{\mathrm{OVS}}^{-\ell}(X_i)'
\widehat S_i^{\mathrm{OVS},-\ell}
=\mathbb P_n\{\alpha_P(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\}+o_P(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{41k}
\]

Summing (41h) at \(c=c_0(k)\) with the fixed weights \(\omega_k\), and subtracting (41k), proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}-\tau_\omega(P)\}
&=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\left\{\psi_P^0(O_i)-\alpha_P(X_i)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O_i)\right\}+o_P(1)\\
&=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(O_i)+o_P(1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{41l}
\]
where the second equality is Definition \(\ref{def:ovs-normal-projection}\).  The summands are independent and identically distributed by the sampling assumption, have mean zero by (41b) and the ambient-gradient centering, and are square integrable by overlap and the fourth-moment condition.  Their variance is \(V_{\mathrm{OVS}}>0\) by the theorem's positivity hypothesis.  Lemma \(\ref{lem:iid-central-limit}\) applied to (41l) proves (41).

For variance consistency, let \(\widehat\phi_i\) be the displayed quantity in Definition \(\ref{def:crossfit-estimator}\), and let \(\phi_i=\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(O_i)\).  Equations (37c), (41c), (41i), the ratio expansion (41g), and \(\widehat\theta_k-\theta_k=O_P(n^{-1/2})\), which follows from (41h), imply foldwise
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(\widehat\phi_i-\phi_i)^2=o_P(1).
\tag{42a}
\]
To see this without an empirical-process assumption, condition one fold at a time on its training complement, apply Markov's inequality to the nonnegative squared difference using its \(o_P(1)\) conditional mean, and sum over the fixed number of folds; the global shares and preliminary ratios are finite-dimensional root-\(n\) terms and hence do not alter the conclusion.  By Cauchy--Schwarz, (42a) gives
\[
\left|\mathbb P_n\widehat\phi^2-\mathbb P_n\phi^2\right|
\le \{\mathbb P_n(\widehat\phi-\phi)^2\}^{1/2}
\{2\mathbb P_n\phi^2+2\mathbb P_n(\widehat\phi-\phi)^2\}^{1/2}
=o_P(1).
\tag{42b}
\]
The ordinary law of large numbers applies to \(\phi_i^2\), which is integrable, and gives \(\mathbb P_n\phi^2\to_P V_{\mathrm{OVS}}\).  Also \(|\mathbb P_n\widehat\phi-\mathbb P_n\phi|\le\{\mathbb P_n(\widehat\phi-\phi)^2\}^{1/2}=o_P(1)\), while \(\mathbb P_n\phi=o_P(1)\).  Expanding the centered empirical second moment in Definition \(\ref{def:crossfit-estimator}\) therefore proves
\[
\widehat V_{\mathrm{eff}}\longrightarrow_P V_{\mathrm{OVS}}.
\tag{42c}
\]
Because \(V_{\mathrm{OVS}}>0\), (42c) makes the estimated standard deviation divided by \(\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{OVS}}}\) converge to one.  Combining this fact with (41), and sandwiching the studentized statistic between Gaussian continuity points, yields
\[
P\{\tau_\omega(P)\in\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}\}\longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\]
Finally, Theorem \(\ref{thm:complete-normal-canonical-gradient}\), whose finite-support and conditional-mean-interior hypotheses are imposed here, identifies \(\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\) as the canonical gradient in the published OVS observed-law tangent space and \(V_{\mathrm{OVS}}\) as its pointwise efficiency bound.  Hence the explicit cross-fitted estimator attains that bound at the fixed law.  The same theorem and Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\) give \(\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}\subseteq\mathcal T_P\), so the localized canonical gradient \(\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}\), which represents the derivative on \(\mathcal T_P\), also represents it on every published score and is therefore a valid regular gradient on the published subclass.  Its variance satisfies
\[
V_{\mathrm{can}}-V_{\mathrm{OVS}}
=E_P[(\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}-\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P})^2],
\]
again by Theorem \(\ref{thm:complete-normal-canonical-gradient}\).  Thus a regular estimator with the localized influence function attains the published bound exactly when the displayed squared distance vanishes, equivalently when the conditional published-normal equation in that theorem holds; otherwise its variance is strictly larger.  Every step used fixed support and a fixed-law positive spectrum after threshold recovery; none establishes continuous-covariate, rank-uniform, or equality-boundary inference.
