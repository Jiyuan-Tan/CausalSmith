Fix \(k=(g,t)\in\mathcal K\), \(c\in\mathcal J_k\), and \(u\in\{g,\ldots,t\}\).  Because \(P\) is induced by \(Q\), consistency implies
\[
m_{g,1,u}(X)
=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(g,1)\mid G=g,S=1,X].
\tag{C1}
\]
No effect on ineligible units, followed by consistency, implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{g,0,u}(X)
&=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,0)\mid G=g,S=0,X],\\
m_{c,0,u}(X)
&=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,0)\mid G=c,S=0,X].
\end{aligned}
\tag{C2}
\]
Since \(c\in\mathcal J_k\), one has \(c>t\ge u\).  If \(c\) is finite, mean no anticipation for cohort \(c\), applied at both \(u<c\) and \(u-1<c\), and consistency give
\[
m_{c,1,u}(X)
=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,1)\mid G=c,S=1,X].
\tag{C3}
\]
If \(c=\infty\), (C3) follows directly from consistency.  Define the untreated eligibility gap
\[
B_{a,u}(X)
=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,1)\mid G=a,S=1,X]
-E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,0)\mid G=a,S=0,X].
\]
The conditional parallel-gap assumption applies because \(u\ge g\) and \(c>u\), and hence gives \(B_{g,u}(X)=B_{c,u}(X)\) almost surely.  Using (C1)--(C3) and this equality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&m_{g,1,u}(X)-m_{g,0,u}(X)-m_{c,1,u}(X)+m_{c,0,u}(X)\\
&\quad=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(g,1)\mid G=g,S=1,X]
-E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,1)\mid G=g,S=1,X]\\
&\quad=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(g,1)-\Delta Y_u(\infty,1)\mid G=g,S=1,X].
\end{aligned}
\tag{C4}
\]
Summing (C4) over \(u=g,\ldots,t\), using the definition of \(q_{k,c}\), linearity of conditional expectation, and telescoping of the potential-outcome increments yields
\[
q_{k,c}(X)
=E_Q\!\left[
Y_t(g,1)-Y_t(\infty,1)
-\{Y_{g-1}(g,1)-Y_{g-1}(\infty,1)\}
\mathrel{\middle|}G=g,S=1,X\right].
\tag{C5}
\]
Mean no anticipation for the finite target cohort \(g\) at the pretreatment date \(g-1<g\) makes the conditional expectation of the braced term zero.  Therefore
\[
q_{k,c}(X)
=E_Q[Y_t(g,1)-Y_t(\infty,1)\mid G=g,S=1,X]
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{C6}
\]
This argument also shows that the right-hand side is independent of the chosen clean comparison \(c\).

Cell overlap and the definition of \(\pi_{g,1}\) give
\[
\pi_{g,1}=E_P[p_{g,1}(X)]\ge\varepsilon>0,
\tag{C7}
\]
so the ratio defining \(\theta_k(P)\) is well defined.  The conditional law of \(X\) given \(G=g,S=1\) has Radon--Nikodym derivative \(p_{g,1}(X)/\pi_{g,1}\) with respect to \(P_X\).  Multiplying (C6) by that derivative and integrating therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta_k(P)
&=\frac{E_P[p_{g,1}(X)q_{k,c}(X)]}{\pi_{g,1}}\\
&=E_Q[Y_t(g,1)-Y_t(\infty,1)\mid G=g,S=1].
\end{aligned}
\tag{C8}
\]
The asserted formula for \(\tau_\omega(P)\) follows by multiplying (C8) by the prespecified \(\omega_k\), summing over the finite set \(\mathcal K\), and using the definition of \(\tau_\omega\).

Finally, for every cell \((a,s)\), the definition of \(m_{a,s,u}\) gives
\[
E_P[\mathbf 1_{a,s}e_{a,s,k}(O)\mid X]=0.
\tag{C9}
\]
All inverse cell probabilities in Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) are finite by overlap.  Hence (C9), the displayed formula for \(R_{k,c,P}\), and (C6) imply
\[
E_P[\Psi_{k,c,P}(O)\mid X]
=\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{\pi_{g,1}}
\{q_{k,c}(X)-\theta_k(P)\}.
\tag{C10}
\]
More directly, subtracting the ratio-denominator derivative in that definition gives the pointwise identity
\[
\Psi_{k,c,P}(O)
=R_{k,c,P}(O)
-\frac{\theta_k(P)}{\pi_{g,1}}\mathbf 1\{G=g,S=1\},
\]
which is the claimed centered ratio-functional score.

Suppose first that every \(\mathcal J_k\) is a singleton.  By the definitions of \(M\) and \(K_0\),
\[
M=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}|\mathcal J_k|
=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}1
=|\mathcal K|=K_0.
\tag{K1}
\]
There is exactly one row of \(A\) for each \(k\), and that row has a one in the column indexed by \(k\) and zeros elsewhere.  Thus, after matching the fixed row order to the target order, \(A=I_{K_0}\); under another fixed order, \(A\) is a permutation matrix.  In either case \(A\) is square and nonsingular, so
\[
\ker(A')=\{0\},\qquad M-K_0=0.
\tag{K2}
\]
The definition of \(N\) therefore gives the zero-column matrix, and \(A'b=\omega\) has the unique solution \(b=(A')^{-1}\omega\).  Hence neither the conditional nor the constant program has a comparison-contrast direction.  Proposition \(\ref{prop:two-period-eif-reduction}\) supplies the source-transcribed two-period, one-target instance of this just-identified reduction, with canonicality only in the finite-support regime stated there.

Now suppose that \(X\) is degenerate.  Conditioning on \(X\) is then conditioning on the trivial sigma-field.  Because the corrected score vector belongs to \(L_2^0(P)^M\), the definitions of \(\Gamma\) and \(\Omega\) give
\[
\Gamma(X)
=\operatorname{Cov}_P(\Psi_P\mid X)
=\operatorname{Cov}_P(\Psi_P)
=E_P[\Psi_P\Psi_P']
=\Omega
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{K3}
\]
Thus the conditional and constant programs minimize the same positive-semidefinite quadratic form over the same affine set.  It remains to show that their singular-case selectors coincide.

Since \(A\) has full column rank, \(b^0=A(A'A)^{-1}\omega\) is feasible.  Every feasible vector has a unique representation
\[
b=b^0+Nz,
\qquad z\in\mathbb R^{M-K_0},
\tag{K4}
\]
because feasibility is equivalent to \(b-b^0\in\ker(A')\), and the columns of \(N\) are an orthonormal basis of that kernel.  Set
\[
H=N'\Gamma N,
\qquad d=N'\Gamma b^0.
\]
Expanding the common objective in (K4) yields
\[
(b^0+Nz)'\Gamma(b^0+Nz)
=(b^0)'\Gamma b^0+2z'd+z'Hz.
\tag{K5}
\]
The matrix \(H\) is symmetric positive semidefinite.  If \(v\in\ker H\), then
\[
0=v'Hv=(Nv)'\Gamma(Nv).
\]
For any \(w\in\mathbb R^M\) and any scalar \(t\), positive semidefiniteness gives
\[
0\le (Nv+tw)'\Gamma(Nv+tw)
=2t w'\Gamma Nv+t^2w'\Gamma w.
\]
If \(w'\Gamma Nv\ne0\), choosing \(t\) with the opposite sign and sufficiently small magnitude would make the right-hand side negative.  Therefore \(w'\Gamma Nv=0\) for every \(w\), so \(\Gamma Nv=0\).  Consequently
\[
v'd=(Nv)'\Gamma b^0=0.
\]
Thus \(d\perp\ker H\).  Since \(H\) is symmetric, \((\ker H)^\perp=\operatorname{range}H\), proving
\[
d\in\operatorname{range}H.
\tag{K6}
\]
The first-order equation for the convex quadratic (K5) is \(Hz=-d\).  By (K6), all and only its solutions are
\[
z=-H^\dagger d+v,
\qquad v\in\ker H.
\tag{K7}
\]
Necessity follows by differentiating (K5).  Conversely, if \(Hz=-d\), then replacing \(z\) by \(z+u\) changes (K5) by \(u'Hu\ge0\), proving sufficiency.

For the conditional selector, the requirement in Definition \(\ref{def:canonical-loading}\) is
\[
N'(b-b^0)=z\in\operatorname{range}H.
\tag{K8}
\]
In (K7), \(-H^\dagger d\in\operatorname{range}H\), while \(v\in\ker H\), and the two subspaces are orthogonal.  Hence (K8) holds if and only if \(v=0\).  Therefore
\[
b^\star=b^0-NH^\dagger N'\Gamma b^0.
\tag{K9}
\]
For the constant selector, \(b^0\in\operatorname{col}A\), whereas \(NH^\dagger d\) and \(Nv\) lie in \(\ker(A')=(\operatorname{col}A)^\perp\).  Orthonormality of the columns of \(N\), together with \(H^\dagger d\perp v\), gives
\[
\|b^0-NH^\dagger d+Nv\|_2^2
=\|b^0\|_2^2+\|H^\dagger d\|_2^2+\|v\|_2^2.
\tag{K10}
\]
The unique minimum-Euclidean-norm minimizer is therefore obtained at \(v=0\).  By the equivalent argmin characterization in the definition of \(b^{\mathrm{const}}\), (K9) is also \(b^{\mathrm{const}}\).  Together with (K3), this proves
\[
b^\star=b^{\mathrm{const}}
\quad\text{when \(X\) is degenerate},
\]
including singular cases.  Formula (K9) agrees with the singular operator representation in Lemma \(\ref{lem:singular-operator}\) whenever its fixed-rank regularity conditions are invoked.

Finally, Proposition \(\ref{prop:two-period-eif-reduction}\) establishes the only equality claimed here with externally published efficient influence functions from the theorem-level transcriptions of the two panel-DDD results.  At the fixed-finite-support laws specified in that proposition, canonicality in the present observed-law model follows from the tangent exhaustion in Theorem \(\ref{thm:complete-normal-canonical-gradient}\), rather than from algebraic resemblance.  The proposition makes no tangent-space nesting claim for the separate staggered-DiD result.  This proves the theorem's final qualification.

Write
\[
I_{a,s}=\mathbf 1\{G=a,S=s\},\qquad
p_{a,s}(x)=P(G=a,S=s\mid X=x),\qquad
\pi=P(G=g,S=1)=E_P[p_{g,1}(X)],
\]
and set \(\mu_{a,s}(x)=E_P[D\mid G=a,S=s,X=x]\).  In the one-period target, the definitions of \(q\) and \(\theta\) reduce to
\[
q(x)=\mu_{g,1}(x)-\mu_{g,0}(x)-\mu_{c,1}(x)+\mu_{c,0}(x),
\qquad
\theta=\frac{E_P[p_{g,1}(X)q(X)]}{\pi}.
\tag{R1}
\]
Retained-cell overlap gives \(p_{a,s}(X)\ge\varepsilon\) almost surely for the four retained cells and
\[
\pi=E_P[p_{g,1}(X)]\ge\varepsilon>0,
\tag{R2}
\]
so every ratio below is well defined.  Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\), with \(D=\Delta Y_g\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_{k,c,P}
=\frac1\pi\bigg[&I_{g,1}\{q(X)-\theta+D-\mu_{g,1}(X)\}
-I_{g,0}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{g,0}(X)}\{D-\mu_{g,0}(X)\}\\
&-I_{c,1}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{c,1}(X)}\{D-\mu_{c,1}(X)\}
+I_{c,0}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{c,0}(X)}\{D-\mu_{c,0}(X)\}\bigg].
\end{aligned}
\tag{R3}
\]

For the Ortiz--Villavicencio--Sant'Anna reduction, relabel their target cohort \(2\) as \(g\), their never-enabling cohort \(\infty\) as \(c\), and their eligibility indicator as \(S\).  Lemma \(\ref{lem:causal-identification}\) identifies their \(\operatorname{ATT}(2,2)\) with \(\theta\).  For every retained cell \((a,s)\), iterated expectation and (R2) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\!\left[I_{a,s}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{a,s}(X)}\right]
&=E_P\!\left[E_P[I_{a,s}\mid X]
\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{a,s}(X)}\right]\\
&=E_P[p_{g,1}(X)]=\pi.
\end{aligned}
\tag{R4}
\]
Thus the cited target weight is \(I_{g,1}/\pi\), and every cited comparison weight is
\[
\frac{I_{a,s}p_{g,1}(X)}{\pi p_{a,s}(X)}.
\tag{R5}
\]
The target pseudo-outcome in the cited formula satisfies, by (R1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&D-\mu_{g,0}(X)-\mu_{c,1}(X)+\mu_{c,0}(X)-\theta\\
&\qquad=D-\mu_{g,1}(X)+q(X)-\theta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{R6}
\]
Substitution of (R4)--(R6) into the formula transcribed in Lemma \(\ref{lem:ovs-two-period-panel-ddd-eif}\) gives exactly the four terms of (R3), in the cell order \((g,1),(g,0),(c,1),(c,0)\) and with signs \(+,-,-,+\).  Hence the OVS efficient influence function equals \(\Psi_{k,c,P}\) term by term.

For the Akbari--Kiyavash--Ghassami reduction, relabel \(G_A=1\) as \(G=g\), \(G_A=0\) as \(G=c\), and \(D_A\) as \(S\).  Their target becomes, by (R1) and conditional expectation,
\[
\psi_{\mathrm{pd}}
=E_P[q(X)\mid G=g,S=1]
=\frac{E_P[p_{g,1}(X)q(X)]}{\pi}
=\theta.
\tag{R7}
\]
Under the same relabeling, \(G_AD_A=I_{g,1}\) and
\(\pi_{r,a,s}(X)=p_{g,1}(X)/p_{a,s}(X)\).  The formula transcribed in Lemma \(\ref{lem:akg-panel-ddd-eif}\) is therefore
\[
\eta_A
=\frac1\pi\sum_{a,s\in\{0,1\}}\sigma_{a,s}
\left\{I_{g,1}-I_{a,s}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{a,s}(X)}\right\}
\{D-\mu_{a,s}(X)\}
-\frac{I_{g,1}}\pi\theta,
\tag{R8}
\]
where \(a=1\) denotes \(g\), \(a=0\) denotes \(c\), and \(\sigma_{a,s}=(-1)^{a+s+1}\).  In the order \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\),
\[
(\sigma_{0,0},\sigma_{0,1},\sigma_{1,0},\sigma_{1,1})=(-1,+1,+1,-1),
\qquad \sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}=0.
\tag{R9}
\]
The part of (R8) carrying the common indicator is consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{g,1}\sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}\{D-\mu_{a,s}(X)\}
&=-I_{g,1}\sum_{a,s}\sigma_{a,s}\mu_{a,s}(X)\\
&=I_{g,1}q(X),
\end{aligned}
\tag{R10}
\]
where the second equality is (R1).  The cell-specific part has coefficients
\[
-\sigma_{a,s}=(+1,-1,-1,+1)
\tag{R11}
\]
in the same order.  In particular, for \((a,s)=(1,1)\) its propensity ratio equals one.  Combining (R7)--(R11) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta_A=\frac1\pi\bigg[&I_{g,1}\{q(X)-\theta+D-\mu_{g,1}(X)\}
-I_{g,0}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{g,0}(X)}\{D-\mu_{g,0}(X)\}\\
&-I_{c,1}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{c,1}(X)}\{D-\mu_{c,1}(X)\}
+I_{c,0}\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{p_{c,0}(X)}\{D-\mu_{c,0}(X)\}\bigg]
=\Psi_{k,c,P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{R12}
\]
This proves the second term-by-term equality, including the denominator derivative \(-I_{g,1}\theta/\pi\).

In the stated two-period, two-cohort design, the clean comparison set at the sole post-treatment period contains only \(c\).  There are no two distinct clean cohorts and hence no nonzero row in \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{OVS}}\).  The zero-dimensional convention in Definition \(\ref{def:ovs-normal-projection}\) therefore gives
\[
Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}=0,
\qquad p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}=0.
\tag{R13}
\]
With one target and \(\omega_k=1\), the definition of the reference gradient gives \(\psi_P^0=\Psi_{k,c,P}\).  The definitions of \(\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\), together with (R13), imply for every such law
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}=\psi_P^0=\Psi_{k,c,P}.
\tag{R14}
\]
At the fixed-finite-support conditional-mean-interior laws covered by Theorem \(\ref{thm:complete-normal-canonical-gradient}\), that theorem proves tangent exhaustion and therefore proves that the common expression in (R14) is the canonical gradient in the present published observed-law model.  This finite-support qualification is essential: the theorem makes no continuous-covariate tangent-exhaustion claim.  If \(\omega_k\) is an arbitrary scalar, linearity of the target pathwise derivative and the definitions of \(\psi_P^0\) and \(\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\) multiply all three expressions by \(\omega_k\).

Finally, Lemma \(\ref{lem:csx-staggered-did-eif-scope}\) supplies a distinct conditional-efficiency formula for a staggered-DiD target under its stated assumptions.  None of (R1)--(R14) constructs an embedding or equality between that staggered-DiD tangent space and the present eligibility-gap DDD tangent space.  The result proved here is therefore exactly the two panel-DDD reductions above and entails no nesting claim concerning the Chen--Sant'Anna--Xie model.
