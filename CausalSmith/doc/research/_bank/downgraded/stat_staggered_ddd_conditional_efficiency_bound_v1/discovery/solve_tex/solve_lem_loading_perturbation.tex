Write the fixed support as \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_J\}\), where \(J<\infty\), and put \(\nu_j=P(X=x_j)\).  The proposed fold-balance condition and the support-mass hypothesis give constants \(c_F,\underline\nu>0\) such that
\[
 |I_\ell^c|\ge c_Fn,
 \qquad
 \min_{1\le j\le J}\nu_j\ge\underline\nu
 \tag{C1}
\]
for every fold and all \(n\).  Assumption \(\ref{ass:cell-overlap}\) then gives
\[
 P(X=x_j,G=a,S=s)=\nu_jp_{a,s}(x_j)
 \ge \underline\nu\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \pi_{g,1}=E\{p_{g,1}(X)\}\ge\varepsilon.
 \tag{C2}
\]
Thus every denominator entering Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) is separated from zero.

We first give an explicit description of the cell-frequency implementation.  For each training complement \(I_\ell^c\), form the empirical averages of the finite collection
\[
 \mathbf 1\{X=x_j,G=a,S=s\},\quad
 \mathbf 1\{X=x_j,G=a,S=s\}\Delta Y_u,quad
 \mathbf 1\{X=x_j,G=a,S=s\}\Delta Y_u\Delta Y_v,
 \tag{C3}
\]
where \(j,a,s,u,v\) range over their fixed sets, together with the target-cell indicators \(\mathbf 1\{G=g,S=1\}\).  Divide the first-moment averages by the corresponding empirical cell frequencies to obtain \(\widehat m^{-\ell}\), use the empirical conditional cell shares for \(\widehat p^{-\ell}\), and use the empirical target shares for \(\widehat\pi^{-\ell}\).  Substitute these quantities in Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\), including its ratio correction, and take within-\(X\)-cell empirical first and second moments of that score.  Their difference is \(\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x_j)\).  On the event that an empirical denominator is smaller than half its population lower bound in (C2), define the corresponding ratio arbitrarily; the probability of this event tends to zero at the rate established below.  This makes the estimator a total measurable map of the training sample.

For every retained cell and period, Assumption \(\ref{ass:conditional-fourth-envelope}\) and the elementary conditional inequality
\[
 |E(\Delta Y_u\mid G=a,S=s,X)|^4
 \le E(|\Delta Y_u|^4\mid G=a,S=s,X)
 \tag{C4}
\]
bound \(m_{a,s,u}(X)\) uniformly.  Because \(T\), \(\mathcal G\), and \(\mathcal I\) are fixed and (C2) bounds every inverse propensity and target share, Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) and \(|z_1+\cdots+z_d|^4\le d^3\sum_h|z_h|^4\) imply
\[
 \sup_{1\le j\le J}E\{\|\Psi_P(O)\|^4\mid X=x_j\}<\infty.
 \tag{C5}
\]
Moreover, for any two periods,
\[
 E\{(\Delta Y_u\Delta Y_v)^2\mid G=a,S=s,X=x_j\}
 \le
 E(|\Delta Y_u|^4\mid G=a,S=s,X=x_j)^{1/2}
 E(|\Delta Y_v|^4\mid G=a,S=s,X=x_j)^{1/2},
 \tag{C6}
\]
so every variable in (C3) has finite variance, uniformly over the fixed index set.

Let \(\mu\) be the finite vector of population moments in (C3) and the target shares, and let \(\widehat\mu^{-\ell}\) be its training-sample version.  Independence in Assumption \(\ref{ass:crossfit-independence}\) is not needed for this within-training calculation, but ensures that the same quantities may subsequently be evaluated on \(I_\ell\).  For every coordinate \(Z) of (C3), independence of the sampled units and (C1) give
\[
 E\!\left[\left\{|I_\ell^c|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_\ell^c}(Z_i-EZ)\right\}^2\right]
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Z)}{|I_\ell^c|}
 \le \frac{C}{n}.
 \tag{C7}
\]
Consequently, for every \(K>0\), applying \(P(U^2>K^2)\le E(U^2)/K^2\) coordinatewise and summing over the fixed numbers of coordinates and folds yields
\[
 \max_{1\le\ell\le L}\|\widehat\mu^{-\ell}-\mu\|
 =O_P(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{C8}
\]
In particular, (C8) and (C2) show that all empirical denominators exceed half their population lower bounds with probability tending to one.

For each \(x_j\), the substitutions following (C3) express \(\Gamma_P(x_j)\) as a finite rational map \(F_j(\mu)\), and express \(\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x_j)\) as the same map \(F_j(\widehat\mu^{-\ell})\).  This statement includes estimation of \(q\), \(\theta\), and the correction for \(\pi_{g,1}\): each is obtained from the same finite first-moment and share vector.  By (C2), all denominators of \(F_j\) are bounded away from zero.  On a fixed neighborhood of \(\mu\), its finitely many partial derivatives are therefore bounded by a constant \(C_F\).  The coordinatewise fundamental theorem of calculus and (C8) give
\[
 \max_{\ell,j}
 \|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x_j)-\Gamma(x_j)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C_F\max_\ell\|\widehat\mu^{-\ell}-\mu\|
 =O_P(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{C9}
\]
Since the support and matrix dimension are fixed,
\[
 \max_\ell\|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}-\Gamma\|_{P_X,4,\mathrm{op}}
 =\max_\ell\left\{\sum_{j=1}^J\nu_j
 \|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x_j)-\Gamma(x_j)\|_{\mathrm{op}}^4\right\}^{1/4}
 =O_P(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{C10}
\]
The rate sequences \(r_{p,n}\) and \(r_{m,n}\) are nonnegative, so (C10) implies the displayed \(O_P(n^{-1/2}+r_{p,n}+r_{m,n})\) assertion.  The construction in (C3) also proves the assertion that every conditional moment in the correctly specified one-step construction is estimated by its corresponding cell average.  Applying the same ratio argument to the first moments shows that the cell propensity and regression first stages are \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).

Set \(r_{\Gamma,n}=n^{-1/2}\) up to a fixed multiplicative constant.  Under Assumptions \(\ref{ass:fixed-contrast-rank}\) and \(\ref{ass:contrast-spectral-gap}\), if \(\tau_n=n^{-a}\) with \(0<a<1/4\), then Assumptions \(\ref{ass:rank-threshold-vanishing}\) and \(\ref{ass:rank-threshold-separation}\) hold because
\[
 \tau_n\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{r_{\Gamma,n}}{\tau_n}=O(n^{-1/2+a})\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{C11}
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:loading-perturbation}\), applied using (C10), therefore gives consistent rank recovery and
\[
 r_{b,n}=\frac{r_{\Gamma,n}}{\tau_n}=O(n^{-1/2+a}),
 \qquad
 r_{b,n}^2+r_{\Gamma,n}^2
 =O(n^{-1+2a}+n^{-1})=o(n^{-1/2}),
 \tag{C12}
\]
where the last equality uses \(a<1/4\).

For completeness, the final continuous-covariate qualification has a generic-class certificate.  Fix one coordinate \((k,c)\), with \(k=(g,t)\), take \(X\) uniform on \([0,1]\), and make \((G,S)\) independent of \(X\) with fixed probabilities \(p^0_{a,s}\ge\varepsilon\).  Put all conditional increment means equal to zero.  In cell \((g,1)\), set only \(\Delta Y_g=\{v_\beta(X)\}^{1/2}Z\), where \(Z\) is an independent Rademacher variable; set all other increments and cells equal to zero.  Here a known partition \((B_j)_{j=1}^{m}\) divides \([0,1]\) into equal intervals and
\[
 v_\beta(x)=1+\delta\beta_j\quad\text{for }x\in B_j,
 \qquad \beta_j\in\{0,1\},\qquad 0<\delta<1.
 \tag{C13}
\]
These laws satisfy overlap and the conditional fourth-moment envelope; their conditional means are zero, so any restrictions on those means are also satisfied.  Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) gives, for the chosen diagonal coordinate,
\[
 \Gamma_{(k,c),(k,c)}(x)=\frac{v_\beta(x)}{p^0_{g,1}}.
 \tag{C14}
\]
Give the bits \(\beta_j\) independent fair Bernoulli priors.  Whatever estimator is used, at most \(n\) of the \(m\) intervals contain a sampled \(X_i\).  Conditional on the data, every bit belonging to an unvisited interval remains fair.  If the two possible values in (C14) are denoted by \(u\) and \(u+d\), where \(d=\delta/p^0_{g,1}>0\), then for every real \(a\),
\[
 \frac12\{|a-u|^4+|a-u-d|^4\}\ge\frac{d^4}{16}.
 \tag{C15}
\]
Taking \(m=2n\), integrating over a fresh \(X\), and then averaging over the bits and the sample gives, for every estimator \(\widetilde\Gamma\),
\[
 \sup_{\beta\in\{0,1\}^{2n}}
 E_\beta\!\left[
 \|\widetilde\Gamma-\Gamma_\beta\|_{P_X,4,\mathrm{op}}^4
 \right]
 \ge \left(1-\frac{n}{2n}\right)\frac{d^4}{16}
 =\frac{d^4}{32}.
 \tag{C16}
\]
Indeed, operator-norm error dominates the absolute error of the diagonal entry (C14).  Truncating that entry to \([u,u+d]\) cannot increase its loss and makes the fourth-power loss bounded; hence (C16) also rules out uniform convergence in probability.  Thus a continuous-(X\) \(L_4(P_X)\) covariance rate cannot follow from overlap and a fourth-moment envelope alone: smoothness or other structure in \(x\) is genuinely necessary.

%%% FIELD proof-loading-perturbation
For a fold \(\ell\), write
\[
 \widetilde H_\ell(x)=N'\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x)N,
 \qquad
 E_\ell(x)=\|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x)-\Gamma(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
 \tag{B1}
\]
Because \(N'N=I\),
\[
 \|\widetilde H_\ell(x)-H(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le E_\ell(x).
 \tag{B2}
\]
For every unit vector \(v\), the absolute difference between the Rayleigh quotients of \(\widetilde H_\ell(x)\) and \(H(x)\) is at most \(E_\ell(x)\).  Taking the successive minima and maxima in the variational characterization of the ordered eigenvalues therefore gives
\[
 \max_j|\lambda_j\{\widetilde H_\ell(x)\}-\lambda_j\{H(x)\}|
 \le E_\ell(x).
 \tag{B3}
\]
By Assumptions \(\ref{ass:rank-threshold-vanishing}\) and \(\ref{ass:contrast-spectral-gap}\), \(\tau_n<\kappa/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Assumptions \(\ref{ass:fixed-contrast-rank}\) and \(\ref{ass:contrast-spectral-gap}\) say that \(H(x)\) has exactly \(r_0\) eigenvalues in \([\kappa,\kappa^{-1}]\) and all its other eigenvalues are zero, for almost every \(x\).  Hence (B3) shows that
\[
 \{\widehat r^{-\ell}(x)\ne r_0\}\subseteq\{E_\ell(x)>\tau_n\}.
 \tag{B4}
\]
Indeed, a retained eigenvalue from the zero cluster requires \(E_\ell(x)>\tau_n\), whereas failure to retain a positive population eigenvalue requires \(E_\ell(x)\ge\kappa-\tau_n>\tau_n\).  Conditional on the training sample, the elementary bound \(P_X(U>t)\le t^{-4}E_X(U^4)\), Assumption \(\ref{ass:covariance-rate}\), and fixed \(L\) now yield
\[
 \max_{1\le\ell\le L}P_X\{\widehat r^{-\ell}(X)\ne r_0\}
 \le \tau_n^{-4}\max_\ell\|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}-\Gamma\|_{P_X,4,\mathrm{op}}^4
 =O_P\!\left\{\left(\frac{r_{\Gamma,n}}{\tau_n}\right)^4\right\}.
 \tag{B5}
\]
This proves the rank statement, including its uniformity over the fixed folds.

It remains to control the loading.  By Lemma \(\ref{lem:singular-operator}\), with
\[
 g(x)=N'\Gamma(x)b^0,
 \qquad
 z^0(x)=H(x)^\dagger g(x),
 \tag{B6}
\]
one has \(g(x)\in\operatorname{range}H(x)\), \(H(x)z^0(x)=g(x)\), and
\[
 b^\star(x)=b^0-Nz^0(x).
 \tag{B7}
\]
The moment conditions invoked here also make the constants below uniform in \(x\).  In fact, Assumption \(\ref{ass:cell-overlap}\) implies \(\pi_{g,1}\ge\varepsilon\) and bounds every propensity ratio in Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\).  Assumption \(\ref{ass:conditional-fourth-envelope}\), the fixed horizon, and \(|z_1+\cdots+z_d|^4\le d^3\sum_h|z_h|^4\) then give
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x}E\{\|\Psi_P(O)\|^4\mid X=x\}<\infty.
 \tag{B8}
\]
Thus \(\sup_x\|\Gamma(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty\).  Since every positive eigenvalue of \(H(x)\) is at least \(\kappa\), (B6) implies
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x}\|z^0(x)\|
 \le \kappa^{-1}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x}\|N'\Gamma(x)b^0\|<\infty;
 \tag{B9}
\]
the inequality is also valid under the zero-rank convention, when \(z^0=0\).

Let \(\widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}(x)\) retain exactly the eigencomponents of \(\widetilde H_\ell(x)\) whose eigenvalues exceed \(\tau_n\), and put
\[
 \widehat P_{\ell,\tau}(x)
 =\widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}(x)^\dagger\widetilde H_\ell(x),
 \qquad
 P_H(x)=H(x)^\dagger H(x),
 \qquad
 \widehat g_\ell(x)=N'\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}(x)b^0.
 \tag{B10}
\]
These are the orthogonal projectors onto the retained empirical space and \(\operatorname{range}H(x)\), respectively.  Spectral truncation gives
\[
 \|\widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}(x)^\dagger\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\tau_n^{-1},
 \qquad
 \|\widehat g_\ell(x)-g(x)\|
 \le \|b^0\|E_\ell(x).
 \tag{B11}
\]
Using \(g=Hz^0\) and the fact that the truncated inverse annihilates the discarded empirical eigenvectors gives the exact identity
\[
 \widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}^\dagger\widehat g_\ell
 =\widehat P_{\ell,\tau}z^0
 +\widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}^\dagger
 \{(H-\widetilde H_\ell)z^0+(\widehat g_\ell-g)\}.
 \tag{B12}
\]

We next bound the projector term without assuming rank recovery.  If \(E_\ell(x)\le\tau_n\), (B3) and \(\tau_n<\kappa/2\) show that the retained empirical space has dimension \(r_0\).  If \(\widehat u\) is a unit retained empirical eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\widehat\lambda\), then \(\widehat\lambda\ge\kappa-E_\ell(x)\).  Multiplying
\(\widetilde H_\ell\widehat u=\widehat\lambda\widehat u\)
by \(I-P_H\), using \((I-P_H)H=0\), and applying (B2) yields
\[
 \|(I-P_H)\widehat u\|
 \le\frac{E_\ell(x)}{\kappa-E_\ell(x)}.
 \tag{B13}
\]
 Applying this bound to an orthonormal basis of the retained space, using equality of the two ranks, and bounding the operator norm by the Frobenius norm bounds both off-diagonal blocks of the two projectors; in particular,
\[
 \|\widehat P_{\ell,\tau}(x)-P_H(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{2\sqrt{r_0}\,E_\ell(x)}{\kappa-E_\ell(x)}.
\tag{B14}
\]
If \(E_\ell(x)>\tau_n\), the difference of two orthogonal projectors has norm at most two, and hence at most \(2E_\ell(x)/\tau_n\).  Combining the two cases, using \(P_Hz^0=z^0\), (B9), (B11), and (B12), gives a fixed constant \(C\) such that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\widehat b^{-\ell}(x)-b^\star(x)\|
 &=\|N\{\widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}(x)^\dagger\widehat g_\ell(x)-z^0(x)\}\|\\
 &\le C\frac{E_\ell(x)}{\tau_n}
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{B15}
\]
for almost every \(x\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).  Assumption \(\ref{ass:covariance-rate}\) therefore implies
\[
 \max_\ell\|\widehat b^{-\ell}-b^\star\|_{P_X,4}
 \le C\tau_n^{-1}max_\ell\|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}-\Gamma\|_{P_X,4,\mathrm{op}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac{r_{\Gamma,n}}{\tau_n}\right).
 \tag{B16}
\]
Because \(P_X\) is a probability measure, \(\|f\|_{P_X,2}\le\|f\|_{P_X,4}\), so the same rate holds in \(L_2(P_X)\).  By the definition of \(r_{b,n}\) and Assumption \(\ref{ass:rank-threshold-separation}\), (B16) is \(O_P(r_{b,n})=o_P(1)\).

Finally, the sharper assertion concerns the consistently selected spectral region
\[
 \mathcal A_{\ell n}=\{x:E_\ell(x)\le\tau_n\}.
 \tag{B17}
\]
On this region, (B3) selects exactly the population positive cluster, its retained eigenvalues are at least \(\kappa-\tau_n\ge\kappa/2\), and (B11) improves to \(\|\widetilde H_{\ell,\tau}^\dagger\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\kappa\).  Equations (B12)--(B14) then give
\[
 \max_\ell
 \|\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_{\ell n}}(\widehat b^{-\ell}-b^\star)\|_{P_X,4}
 \le C\max_\ell\|\widehat\Gamma^{-\ell}-\Gamma\|_{P_X,4,\mathrm{op}}
 =O_P(r_{\Gamma,n}),
 \tag{B18}
\]
which is the claimed sharper local bound on the consistently selected spectral range.
