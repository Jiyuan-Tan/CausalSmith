Let \(\mathcal C_u^{\mathrm{OVS}}=\{c\in\mathcal G:c>u\}\), where \(\infty>u\), and let \(d_{a,u}(x)=m_{a,1,u}(x)-m_{a,0,u}(x)\).  Under retained-cell overlap, an observed law \(P\), with otherwise arbitrary covariate support, admits a full-data extension \(Q\in\mathcal M\) if and only if
\[
d_{c,u}(X)=d_{c',u}(X)
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}
\tag{1}
\]
for every \(u\in\{2,\ldots,T\}\) for which some finite \(g\in\mathcal G\) satisfies \(g\le u\), and every \(c,c'\in\mathcal C_u^{\mathrm{OVS}}\).  Thus the published class uses every cohort clean at period \(u\), including a cohort with \(u<c\le t\) that is unavailable as a single clean-through-\(t\) comparison for a terminal target \((g,t)\).

Suppose in addition that the fourth-moment condition holds, that \(X\) has fixed finite support \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_D\}\) with \(P(X=x_d)>0\) for every \(d\), and that \(P\) is a conditional-mean interior law.  Then the published observed tangent orthocomplement is
\[
\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp
=\overline{\{a(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O):a\text{ measurable and }
E_P[(a(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O))^2]<\infty\}}^{L_2(P)}.
\tag{2}
\]
At the same law, the larger localized observed model \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{loc}}\) of Definition \(\ref{def:local-observed-model}\), which retains only cohorts clean through a target's terminal period, has
\[
\mathcal T_P^\perp
=\overline{\{a(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}(O):a\text{ measurable and }
E_P[(a(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}(O))^2]<\infty\}}^{L_2(P)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every local formal row is an OVS row.  Consequently
\[
\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal T_P^\perp
\subseteq\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp,
\qquad
\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}\subseteq\mathcal T_P.
\tag{4}
\]
The cumulative equalities \(q_{k,c}=q_{k,c_0(k)}\) are necessary in both models but, whenever a target spans more than one period, do not in general imply even the localized period-specific equalities.  No tangent-exhaustion assertion is made here for continuously distributed \(X\).

Write
\[
D^0_{a,u}(x)=E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,1)\mid G=a,S=1,X=x]
-E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,0)\mid G=a,S=0,X=x].
\]
We first prove necessity of (1).  Fix \(u\), a finite \(g\le u\), and \(c\in\mathcal C_u^{\mathrm{OVS}}\).  Because \(c>u\), Assumption \(\ref{ass:no-anticipation}\), applied at \(u-1\) and \(u\), and Assumption \(\ref{ass:consistency}\) give
\[
E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,1)\mid G=c,S=1,X]=m_{c,1,u}(X).
\tag{5}
\]
Assumptions \(\ref{ass:no-ineligible-effect}\) and \(\ref{ass:consistency}\) similarly give
\[
E_Q[\Delta Y_u(\infty,0)\mid G=c,S=0,X]=m_{c,0,u}(X).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(D^0_{c,u}(X)=d_{c,u}(X)\).  Assumption \(\ref{ass:conditional-parallel-gap}\), applied with comparison \(c\) and then with comparison \(c'\), yields
\[
d_{c,u}(X)=D^0_{g,u}(X)=d_{c',u}(X),
\]
which is (1).  This step uses the published condition \(c>u\), not the stronger condition \(c>t\).

Conversely, suppose (1) holds.  Since \(\infty\in\mathcal C_u^{\mathrm{OVS}}\), define the common observable gap
\[
\delta_u(x)=d_{\infty,u}(x).
\tag{7}
\]
Equation (1) gives \(d_{c,u}(x)=\delta_u(x)\) for every \(c>u\) at every period indexed by a published restriction.  We construct a full-data extension on the observed probability space, enlarged only by independent auxiliary variables if desired.  For every unit, set \(Y_v(a,0)=Y_v\) for all \(a\in\mathcal G\) and \(v\in\mathcal T\).  On units with \(S=0\), also set every eligible potential path equal to the observed path.  On a unit with \(S=1\) and finite factual cohort \(a\), set \(Y_v(a,1)=Y_v\), set \(Y_v(\infty,1)=Y_v\) for \(v<a\), and recursively set, for \(v\ge a\),
\[
\Delta Y_v(\infty,1)=m_{a,0,v}(X)+\delta_v(X)+\xi_{a,v},
\qquad
E_Q[\xi_{a,v}\mid G=a,S=1,X]=0.
\tag{8}
\]
The choice \(\xi_{a,v}=0\) is admissible.  On units with \((G,S)=(\infty,1)\), take the observed path as the never-enabled eligible path.  On eligible units, define every remaining nonfactual finite-cohort eligible path to equal the specified never-enabled eligible path.  The array is finite because \(T\) and \(\mathcal G\) are finite, so this prescription defines every potential-outcome coordinate, gives \(Y_v(G,S)=Y_v\), and preserves the observed marginal.

The construction on \(S=0\) proves no effect on ineligible units and consistency there.  The factual-path assignment proves consistency on \(S=1\).  For a finite factual cohort \(a\), the equality of its factual and never-enabled paths before \(a\) proves Assumption \(\ref{ass:no-anticipation}\).  For every finite \(g\le u\), (8) and the ineligible construction imply
\[
D^0_{g,u}(X)=m_{g,0,u}(X)+\delta_u(X)-m_{g,0,u}(X)=\delta_u(X).
\tag{9}
\]
For every \(c>u\), the constructed never-enabled path equals the observed path at \(u-1\) and \(u\); hence (5)--(7) give \(D^0_{c,u}(X)=\delta_u(X)\).  Equation (9) is therefore every restriction in Assumption \(\ref{ass:conditional-parallel-gap}\).  The constructed law belongs to \(\mathcal M\), proving sufficiency.  This construction did not restrict the support of \(X\), so the equivalence part of the theorem is general in the covariate law.

We now prove tangent exhaustion under the stated finite-support condition.  Put \(V=(\Delta Y_2,\ldots,\Delta Y_T)'\) and \(C=(G,S)\).  For every retained cell and period define
\[
U_{a,s,u}(O)=\frac{\mathbf 1_{a,s}}{p_{a,s}(X)}
\{\Delta Y_u-m_{a,s,u}(X)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Assumption \(\ref{ass:cell-overlap}\) and Assumption \(\ref{ass:fourth-moment}\) imply \(U_{a,s,u}\in L_2(P)\); Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) gives \(E_P[U_{a,s,u}\mid X]=0\).  If \(P_\epsilon\) is a regular dominated observed submodel with score \(s\in L_2^0(P)\), differentiating the conditional numerator and denominator gives
\[
\dot m_{a,s_0,u}(x)
=E_P[U_{a,s_0,u}(O)s(O)\mid X=x].
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, the denominator derivative subtracts \(m_{a,s_0,u}(x)E_P[s\mid G=a,S=s_0,X=x]\) from the numerator derivative, which is exactly the centered residual on the right.  Therefore, for an indexed row \(e=(u,c,c')\),
\[
\dot d_{c,u}(x)-\dot d_{c',u}(x)
=E_P[Z^{\mathrm{OVS}}_{e,P}(O)s(O)\mid X=x].
\tag{12}
\]

Because \(X\) has support \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_D\}\), define the finite-dimensional continuous operator
\[
\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}s
=\bigl(E_P[Z^{\mathrm{OVS}}_{e,P}s\mid X=x_d]
 :e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{OVS}},\ 1\le d\le D\bigr).
\tag{13}
\]
Differentiating (1) along any in-model regular submodel and using (12) proves
\(\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}\subseteq\ker\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}\).

We prove the reverse inclusion by an explicit finite-cell construction.  Fix a cell \(j=(a,s,x_d)\), write \(\mu_j=E_P[V\mid C=(a,s),X=x_d]\), and let
\[
\mathcal A_j=\operatorname{span}\{v-\mu_j:v\in
\operatorname{ess\,supp}(V\mid C=(a,s),X=x_d)\}.
\tag{14}
\]
This is the linear part of the affine support appearing in Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-mean-interior}\).  If its dimension is \(q_j>0\), choose essential-support points \(v_{j,0},\ldots,v_{j,q_j}\) whose differences span \(\mathcal A_j\).  Choose sufficiently small bounded neighborhoods \(B_{j,\ell}\) of these points.  Each has positive conditional probability by the definition of essential support, and the neighborhoods may be chosen so that their conditional means \(\bar v_{j,\ell}=E_P[V\mid V\in B_{j,\ell},C=(a,s),X=x_d]\) retain affine independence.  The bounded, cell-centered functions
\[
w_{j,\ell}(V)=
\frac{\mathbf 1\{V\in B_{j,\ell}\}}
{P(V\in B_{j,\ell}\mid C=(a,s),X=x_d)}
-
\frac{\mathbf 1\{V\in B_{j,0}\}}
{P(V\in B_{j,0}\mid C=(a,s),X=x_d)},
\quad 1\le\ell\le q_j,
\tag{15}
\]
satisfy
\[
E_P[w_{j,\ell}\mid C=(a,s),X=x_d]=0,
\qquad
E_P[(V-\mu_j)w_{j,\ell}mid C=(a,s),X=x_d]
=\bar v_{j,\ell}-\bar v_{j,0}.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus (15)--(16) give an explicit linear right inverse \(J_j\) from \(\mathcal A_j\) into bounded cell-centered likelihood scores.  If \(q_j=0\), take the unique zero map.  The relative-interior clause ensures that all admissible mean directions are interpreted in \(\mathcal A_j\); no direction orthogonal to the affine support is introduced.

Let \(\mathcal A_d\) be the direct sum of the finitely many \(\mathcal A_j\)'s at \(x_d\), let \(J_d\) be the direct sum of the maps \(J_j\), and let \(R_d:\mathcal A_d\to\mathbb R^{|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{OVS}}|}\) take a collection of cell-mean shifts to the signed eligibility-gap row shifts in (12).  In fixed bases, \(R_d\) is an ordinary finite matrix.  Hence its Moore--Penrose inverse is a right inverse on its range:
\[
R_dR_d^\dagger y=y,
\qquad y\in\operatorname{range}R_d.
\tag{17}
\]
Define \(B_d=J_dR_d^\dagger\).  Equations (16)--(17) show that \(B_dy\) is bounded and cell-centered and that its induced restriction derivative is exactly \(y\).  Since there are finitely many support points and cells, the operator norms of all \(B_d\)'s are bounded by one finite, law-dependent constant.

Take any \(s\in\ker\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}\), and decompose it orthogonally as
\[
s=s_X+s_C+s_Y,
\qquad
s_X=E_P[s\mid X],
\quad s_C=E_P[s\mid C,X]-E_P[s\mid X],
\quad s_Y=s-E_P[s\mid C,X].
\tag{18}
\]
Because both \(X\) and \(C\) have finite support and every retained cell has positive probability by overlap, \(s_X\) and \(s_C\) are bounded.  Only \(s_Y\) changes conditional outcome means, because residuals in (10) have conditional mean zero given \((C,X)\).  Let
\[
v_j=\max(-j,\min(s_Y,j))
-E_P[\max(-j,\min(s_Y,j))\mid C,X].
\tag{19}
\]
Then \(v_j\) is bounded and cell-centered and \(v_j\to s_Y\) in \(L_2(P)\).  At \(X=x_d\), let \(L_dv_j\) be the restriction derivative induced by the cell-mean shifts of \(v_j\), and define
\[
u_j=v_j-B_XL_Xv_j.
\tag{20}
\]
The vector \(L_dv_j\) belongs to \(\operatorname{range}R_d\), so (17) gives \(L_du_j=0\) exactly.  Continuity of (13), \(L_ds_Y=0\), and the finite bound on \(B_d\) imply
\[
\|u_j-s_Y\|_{P,2}
\le \|v_j-s_Y\|_{P,2}
+C\|\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}v_j\|
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently \(s^{(j)}=s_X+s_C+u_j\) is bounded, belongs to \(\ker\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}\), and converges to \(s\) in \(L_2(P)\).

For fixed \(j\) and sufficiently small \(|\epsilon|\), define the likelihood ratio
\[
\frac{dP_{\epsilon,j}}{dP}
=\{1+\epsilon s_X(X)\}
 \{1+\epsilon s_C(C,X)\}
 \{1+\epsilon u_j(O)\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Every factor is strictly positive for small enough \(|\epsilon|\).  The three centering identities in (18)--(20) show successively that the factors integrate to one for the marginal law of \(X\), the conditional law of \(C\) given \(X\), and the conditional law of \(Y\) given \((C,X)\).  Thus (22) is a dominated probability submodel.  Its score at zero is \(s_X+s_C+u_j=s^{(j)}\), and its square-root likelihood ratio has the uniform expansion
\[
\sqrt{dP_{\epsilon,j}/dP}
=1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}s^{(j)}+O(\epsilon^2),
\tag{23}
\]
so the submodel is differentiable in quadratic mean.

Under (22), a conditional outcome mean changes exactly, not merely to first order:
\[
E_{P_{\epsilon,j}}[V\mid C,X]
=E_P[V\mid C,X]
+\epsilon E_P[(V-E_P[V\mid C,X])u_j\mid C,X].
\tag{24}
\]
The other two factors change only the marginal law of \(X\) and the cell probabilities.  Since \(L_du_j=0\), substituting (24) leaves every basis-row equality exact for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\).  Definition \(\ref{def:ovs-normal-projection}\) selects a maximal linearly independent collection of the formal OVS rows, so every omitted row is a fixed linear combination of those basis rows and is also exactly zero.  The already proved sufficiency construction lifts every \(P_{\epsilon,j}\) to a full-data law in \(\mathcal M\).  Boundedness of (22) preserves the fourth moment, and overlap persists locally because all cell probabilities are multiplied by positive factors uniformly close to one.  Hence every \(s^{(j)}\) is the score of a regular in-model submodel.  Taking the \(L_2(P)\) limit in (21) proves
\[
\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}=ker\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}.
\tag{25}
\]

Let \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{OVS}}\) denote the closed space on the right of (2).  If \(s\perp\mathcal N_{\mathrm{OVS}}\), then for every coordinate \(e\) and every support indicator \(\mathbf 1\{X=x_d\}\),
\[
E_P[\mathbf 1\{X=x_d\}Z^{\mathrm{OVS}}_{e,P}s]=0.
\]
Since \(P(X=x_d)>0\), this is equivalent to every coordinate of \(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}s\) being zero.  Conversely, \(\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}s=0\) makes \(s\) orthogonal to every measurable multiple of \(Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\) and hence to its closure.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp
=\ker\mathscr D_{\mathrm{OVS}}
=\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}.
\]
Taking orthogonal complements of closed subspaces proves (2).  Repeating (10)--(25) with the finite local row matrix in Definition \(\ref{def:complete-normal-projection}\) proves (3).

If a local row is indexed through targets \(k=(g,t)\), \(k'=(g,t')\), period \(u\le\min(t,t')\), and comparisons \(c\in\mathcal J_k\), \(c'\in\mathcal J_{k'}\), then \(c>t\ge u\) and \(c'>t'\ge u\).  Hence every local row is a published OVS row.  This proves \(\mathcal T_P^\perp\subseteq\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp\), and orthogonal complementation gives the tangent inclusion in (4).

For two comparisons in one target block, direct subtraction of Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) gives
\[
R_{k,c,P}-R_{k,c',P}
=-\frac{p_{g,1}(X)}{\pi_{g,1}}
\sum_{u=g}^t Z_{u;c,c',P}.
\tag{26}
\]
Every summand is a local formal row, so its residual contrast is a fixed linear combination of the independent coordinates stacked in \(Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\).  Since each column of \(N\) has coefficients summing to zero within every target block, \(N'R_P\) is a finite linear combination of differences of the form (26).  Thus every square-integrable measurable aggregate contrast is in \(\mathcal T_P^\perp\); taking closure proves \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal T_P^\perp\) and completes (4).

Finally, let \(T=4\), \(\mathcal K=\{(2,3)\}\), \(\mathcal J_{(2,3)}=\{4,\infty\}\), and let \(X\) be degenerate.  Give every cohort--eligibility cell positive probability and choose bounded conditional outcome distributions with
\[
d_{4,2}=1,\qquad d_{4,3}=-1,
\qquad d_{\infty,2}=d_{\infty,3}=0.
\]
Then \(\sum_{u=2}^3d_{4,u}=\sum_{u=2}^3d_{\infty,u}=0\), so the cumulative comparison equality holds, while both period-specific equalities fail.  Equation (1) and the definition of the localized model therefore exclude this observed law from both models.  This supplies the asserted witness.

Let \(P\) be a conditional-mean interior observed law induced by a full-data law in the global OVS model \(\mathcal M\).  Suppose \(X\) has fixed finite support with positive mass at every support point.  Under global retained-cell overlap, the fourth-moment condition, fixed contrast rank, and the contrast spectral gap,
\[
\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp
=\overline{\{a(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O):a\text{ measurable and }
E_P[(a(X)'Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}(O))^2]<\infty\}}^{L_2(P)}.
\]
With the objects in Definition \(\ref{def:ovs-normal-projection}\),
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}
=\psi_P^0-r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(X)'K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(X)^\dagger
Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}
\tag{15}
\]
is the canonical gradient of \(\tau_\omega\) in the published class at \(P\), including singular and redundant fixed-law cases, and
\[
V_{\mathrm{OVS}}
=E_P[(\psi_P^0)^2]
-E_P[r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(X)'K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(X)^\dagger r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(X)].
\tag{16}
\]
The former complete-normal gradient \(\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}\) is the canonical gradient in the larger localized observed model \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{loc}}\) at this finite-support law.  It remains a valid gradient in the published subclass, but
\[
V_{\mathrm{OVS}}\le V_{\mathrm{can}},
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{can}}-V_{\mathrm{OVS}}
=E_P[(\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}-\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P})^2],
\tag{17}
\]
with equality if and only if
\[
E_P[Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}\mid X]=0
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{18}
\]

The conditional loading \(b^\star\) remains the singular-safe optimum inside the aggregate pairwise-score family.  In particular,
\[
V_{\mathrm{agg}}
=\operatorname{Var}_P\{\omega'h(X)\}
+E_P[b^\star(X)'\Gamma_P(X)b^\star(X)],
\tag{19}
\]
and every measurable feasible \(b\), with \(A'b(X)=\omega\) almost surely and \(b(X)'\Psi_P\in L_2(P)\), satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\{b(X)'\Psi_P\}-V_{\mathrm{agg}}
=E_P[(b(X)-b^\star(X))'\Gamma_P(X)(b(X)-b^\star(X))]\ge0.
\tag{20}
\]
The three nested frontiers obey
\[
V_{\mathrm{agg}}-V_{\mathrm{OVS}}
=E_P[(\phi_{\mathrm{agg},P}-\phi_{\mathrm{can},P})^2]
+E_P[(\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}-\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P})^2].
\tag{21}
\]
Thus \(b^\star(X)'\Psi_P\) reaches the localized bound exactly when \(E_P[Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\phi_{\mathrm{agg},P}\mid X]=0\), and it reaches the published OVS bound exactly when
\[
E_P[Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\phi_{\mathrm{agg},P}\mid X]=0
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{22}
\]
Accordingly, score-family projection is not called semiparametric efficiency unless (22) is verified.  No canonical-gradient claim is made here for continuously distributed \(X\).

The first tangent-normal identity is equation (2) of Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\), whose hypotheses include the finite-support, overlap, fourth-moment, and conditional-mean-interior conditions imposed here.  Lemma \(\ref{lem:reference-score-gradient}\) gives, for every \(s\in\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}\),
\[
\dot\tau_{\omega,P}[s]=E_P[\psi_P^0s].
\tag{T1}
\]
Abbreviate \(Z=Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\), \(K=K_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\), and \(r=r_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\).  If \(v\in\ker K(x)\), then
\[
0=v'K(x)v=E_P[(v'Z)^2\mid X=x].
\]
Thus \(v'Z=0\) conditionally almost surely and \(v'r(x)=E_P[v'Z\psi_P^0\mid X=x]=0\).  Since \(K(x)\) is symmetric,
\[
r(x)\in\operatorname{range}K(x),
\qquad K(x)K(x)^\dagger r(x)=r(x).
\tag{T2}
\]
The Moore--Penrose map is measurable at this fixed finite-support law; explicitly, for every symmetric matrix,
\[
K^\dagger=\lim_{j\to\infty}(K^2+j^{-1}I)^{-1}K.
\tag{T3}
\]
Put \(p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}=r'K^\dagger Z\).  Symmetry, (T2), and the Moore--Penrose identities imply
\[
E_P[p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}^2\mid X]
=r'K^\dagger KK^\dagger r
=r'K^\dagger r
=E_P[\psi_P^0p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\mid X].
\tag{T4}
\]
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
E_P[p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}^2\mid X]
\le E_P[(\psi_P^0)^2\mid X],
\]
where the assertion is immediate when the left side is zero and otherwise follows after division by its square root.  Since Lemma \(\ref{lem:reference-score-gradient}\) and the moment assumptions give \(\psi_P^0\in L_2(P)\), this proves \(p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\in L_2(P)\).  For every square-integrable measurable multiple \(a(X)'Z\),
\[
E_P[(\psi_P^0-p_{\mathrm{OVS},P})a(X)'Z]
=E_P[a(X)'\{r-KK^\dagger r\}]=0.
\tag{T5}
\]
Equations (T4)--(T5), first on the generating set and then on its \(L_2(P)\)-closure, and tangent exhaustion give
\[
p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}=\Pi_{\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp}\psi_P^0,
\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}=\Pi_{\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}}\psi_P^0.
\tag{T6}
\]
Combining (T1) and (T6) proves canonicality.  Taking expectations in (T4) and expanding \(E_P[(\psi_P^0-p_{\mathrm{OVS},P})^2]\) proves (16).  Coefficients that differ by an element of \(\ker K(X)\) have the same product with \(Z\), so singular or redundant coordinates create no ambiguity in the projected random variable.

The same calculation with \(Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\), using equation (3) of Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\), gives
\[
p_{\mathrm{per},P}=\Pi_{\mathcal T_P^\perp}\psi_P^0,
\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}=\Pi_{\mathcal T_P}\psi_P^0.
\tag{T7}
\]
Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\) gives \(\mathcal T_P^\perp\subseteq\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp\).  For nested closed subspaces, the difference between the two orthogonal projections lies in the larger normal space and is orthogonal to the smaller projection residual.  Applying this elementary fact here yields
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}
=\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}+(p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}-p_{\mathrm{per},P}),
\qquad
E_P[\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}(p_{\mathrm{OVS},P}-p_{\mathrm{per},P})]=0.
\]
Pythagoras proves (17).  Moreover, \(p_{\mathrm{per},P}\in\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp\), so subtracting it from the ambient gradient leaves a valid gradient on the published tangent space.  It is the published canonical gradient exactly when it is orthogonal to the published normal closure.  Because \(Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\) has finitely many coordinates, iterated expectation makes this equivalent to (18).

On the observable equality model, every residual in Definition \(\ref{def:pairwise-score}\) has conditional mean zero, and every clean comparison in a target block has the same conditional contrast.  Therefore
\[
E_P[\Psi_P\mid X]=Ah(X).
\tag{T8}
\]
Every feasible loading has conditional mean \(\omega'h(X)\), by \(A'b(X)=\omega\).  The conditional variance identity and Definition \(\ref{def:canonical-loading}\) yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}_P\{b(X)'\Psi_P\}
=\operatorname{Var}_P\{\omega'h(X)\}
+E_P[b(X)'\Gamma_P(X)b(X)].
\tag{T9}
\]
Lemma \(\ref{lem:singular-operator}\), using Assumptions \(\ref{ass:fixed-contrast-rank}\) and \(\ref{ass:contrast-spectral-gap}\), gives \(A'b^\star=\omega\) and \(N'\Gamma b^\star=0\).  If \(d=b-b^\star\), then \(A'd=0\).  Since the columns of \(N\) form a basis of \(\ker A'\), there is a measurable \(z(X)\) with \(d=Nz\), and hence \(d'\Gamma b^\star=z'N'\Gamma b^\star=0\).  Expanding the quadratic term in (T9) proves (19)--(20), including the singular fixed-rank case.

Let \(b^{\mathrm{ref}}\) put weight \(\omega_k\) on coordinate \((k,c_0(k))\) and zero on every other coordinate.  Then \(\psi_P^0=b^{\mathrm{ref}\prime}\Psi_P\).  Feasibility gives \(b^{\mathrm{ref}}-b^\star\in\operatorname{col}N\), and the target-share correction in \(\Psi\) is constant within each target block and is annihilated by \(N'\).  Therefore
\[
p_{\mathrm{agg},P}:=\psi_P^0-\phi_{\mathrm{agg},P}
=(b^{\mathrm{ref}}-b^\star(X))'R_P\in\mathcal C_P.
\tag{T10}
\]
Using \(N'R=N'\Psi\), (T8), \(N'A=0\), and \(N'\Gamma b^\star=0\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[N'R_P\phi_{\mathrm{agg},P}\mid X]
&=N'E_P[\Psi_P\Psi_P'\mid X]b^\star\\
&=N'\{\Gamma_P+Ahh'A'\}b^\star=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{T11}
\]
Thus \(p_{\mathrm{agg},P}=\Pi_{\mathcal C_P}\psi_P^0\).  The nested closed normal spaces from Theorem \(\ref{thm:observable-equivalence}\),
\[
\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal T_P^\perp
\subseteq\mathcal T_{P,\mathrm{OVS}}^\perp,
\]
have orthogonal successive projection increments.  Expanding the squared norms of their projection residuals proves (21).  Finally, a square-integrable variable is orthogonal to every square-integrable measurable multiple of a finite normal vector \(Z\) if and only if its conditional cross-moment with \(Z\) is zero: the forward implication follows by testing with bounded support-cell multiples, and the reverse implication follows by iterated expectation and closure.  Applying this equivalence to \(Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\) and \(Z_P^{\mathrm{OVS}}\) proves the two equality characterizations, including (22).  Hence \(\phi_{\mathrm{OVS},P}\), rather than \(b^\star(X)'\Psi_P\) absent (22), is the canonical gradient proved by the finite-support tangent exhaustion.

Let \(\mathscr E_{\mathrm{loc}}\) contain every nonzero triple \(e=(u,c,c')\) for which there exist \(k=(g,t)\), \(k'=(g,t')\in\mathcal K\) with the same \(g\), \(u\in\{g,\ldots,\min(t,t')\}\), \(c\in\mathcal J_k\), and \(c'\in\mathcal J_{k'}\).  Associate the formal cell-incidence row
\[
\rho_e=e_{(c,1,u)}-e_{(c,0,u)}-e_{(c',1,u)}+e_{(c',0,u)}.
\]
The deterministic set \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{per}}\) is the lexicographically first maximal linearly independent subcollection of these rows.  Define \(Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\) from these coordinates by the residual formula already displayed in this definition, and set
\[
\psi_P^0=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\omega_k\Psi_{k,c_0(k),P},\quad
K_P(X)=E_P[Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}Z_P^{\mathrm{per}\prime}\mid X],\quad
r_P(X)=E_P[Z_P^{\mathrm{per}}\psi_P^0\mid X],
\]
\[
p_{\mathrm{per},P}=r_P(X)'K_P(X)^\dagger Z_P^{\mathrm{per}},\quad
\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}=\psi_P^0-p_{\mathrm{per},P},\quad
V_{\mathrm{can}}=E_P[\phi_{\mathrm{can},P}^2].
\]

Outside that proved regime the same formulas denote the localized normal-span projection objects, without a tangent-exhaustion or canonicality assertion.
