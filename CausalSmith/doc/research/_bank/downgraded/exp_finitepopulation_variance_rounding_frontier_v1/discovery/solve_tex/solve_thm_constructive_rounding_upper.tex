For integers \(N\ge2\) and \(K\ge1\), a fixed vector \(\widetilde c=(\widetilde c_1,\ldots,\widetilde c_K)\in\mathbb R^K\), and \(K\) equal-weight empirical laws \(F^{(K)}=(F^{(K)}_1,\ldots,F^{(K)}_K)\) on \([0,1]\), let \(\Gamma(F^{(K)})\) be their Fr\'{e}chet class and let \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(K)})\) be the set of \(N\)-row arrays having those marginals.  Define
\[
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 =\min_{\pi\in\Gamma(F^{(K)})}
 \int\left(\sum_{j=1}^K\widetilde c_jy_j\right)^2d\pi(y),
\]
\[
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 =\min_{T\in\mathcal S_N(F^{(K)})}
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\left(\sum_{j=1}^K\widetilde c_jT_{ij}\right)^2,
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}
 =\sup_{F^{(K)}}\left\{
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})-
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})\right\},
\]
where the supremum ranges over all \(K\)-tuples of \(N\)-point equal-weight empirical laws, repetitions permitted.  Put \(J(\widetilde c)=\{j:\widetilde c_j\ne0\}\), \(r(\widetilde c)=|J(\widetilde c)|\), and, for \(j\in J(\widetilde c)\),
\[
 H_j(\widetilde c)=|\widetilde c_j|+2\sum_{k\in J(\widetilde c)\setminus\{j\}}|\widetilde c_k|
 =2\|\widetilde c\|_1-|\widetilde c_j|.
\]

Fix the objects in \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\), restrict to the active set \(J(\widetilde c)\), and put \(z_{js}=\widetilde c_js\).  Every optimal scaled coupling \(q^\star=N\pi^\star\), with positive support \(E\), admits dual potentials \(u_{js}\) satisfying \(\sum_ju_{je_j}\le(\sum_jz_{je_j})^2\) on every cell and equality on \(E\).  For any such potentials, put \(\phi_{js}=z_{js}^2-u_{js}\),
\[
 G_{js}=\left\{-2\sum_{k\in J(\widetilde c)\setminus\{j\}}z_{ke_k}:e\in E,\ e_j=s\right\},
 \qquad I_{js}=[z_{js}+\min G_{js},z_{js}+\max G_{js}].
\]
Then, for every \(g\in G_{js}\) and every atom \(t\) of the same marginal,
\[
 \phi_{jt}\ge\phi_{js}+g(z_{jt}-z_{js}).
\]
If \(z_{js}<z_{jt}\), every \(g\in G_{js}\) and \(h\in G_{jt}\) satisfy
\[
 g\le\frac{\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}}{z_{jt}-z_{js}}\le h.
\]
Consequently the closed intervals \(I_{js}\) are strictly ordered and disjoint within each active arm.  Writing \(L_k=\min(0,\widetilde c_k)\) and \(U_k=\max(0,\widetilde c_k)\), all of them lie in the root interval
\[
 \left[L_j-2\sum_{k\ne j}U_k,\ U_j-2\sum_{k\ne j}L_k\right],
\]
whose length is \(H_j(\widetilde c)\).  If two atom intervals lie in one interval of length \(h\), then \(|z_{jt}-z_{js}|\le h\) and the dual remainder, oriented from the source atom, lies in \([0,h^2]\).  These conclusions include repeated atoms after aggregation, ties, and contacts with half-open grid boundaries.

Project the coupling onto its active coordinates and aggregate cells with the same active tuple; this preserves the objective and all active marginals.  We first justify the asserted dual certificate without assuming it.  Enumerate these finite active cells, let \(A\) be their marginal-incidence matrix, let \(b\) be the scaled integer marginal vector, and let \(\kappa_e=(\sum_jz_{je_j})^2\).  Thus \(q^\star\ge0\), \(Aq^\star=b\), and \(q^\star\) minimizes \(\kappa^Tq\).  Consider the polyhedral cone
\[
 \mathcal C=\{A^Tu+s:u\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(A)},\ s_e=0\ (e\in E),\ s_e\ge0\ (e\notin E)\}.
\]
It is closed because it is the sum of a finite-dimensional subspace and a finitely generated cone.  If \(\kappa\notin\mathcal C\), let \(p\) be its Euclidean projection onto \(\mathcal C\) and put \(v=\kappa-p\).  The projection inequalities for a closed convex cone give \(v^Tp=0\) and \(v^Tw\le0\) for all \(w\in\mathcal C\).  Since \(\mathcal C\) contains \(A^Tu\) for both signs of every \(u\), \(Av=0\); since it contains every nonnegative coordinate ray off \(E\), \(v_e\le0\) for \(e\notin E\).  Consequently \(d=-v\) satisfies \(Ad=0\) and \(d_e\ge0\) off \(E\).  For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), \(q^\star+\varepsilon d\ge0\), while
\[
 \kappa^Td=-\kappa^Tv=-(p+v)^Tv=-\|v\|_2^2<0,
\]
contradicting optimality.  Hence \(\kappa=A^Tu+s\) for some \(u,s\) in the displayed cone.  Coordinatewise, \(A^Tu\le\kappa\), with equality on \(E\); moreover \(b^Tu=(q^\star)^TA^Tu=(q^\star)^T\kappa\), so these are optimal dual potentials.

Now fix an active arm \(j\), an atom \(s\), and a support cell \(e\in E\) with \(e_j=s\).  Write
\[
 w=\sum_{k\ne j}z_{ke_k},\qquad g=-2w.
\]
Dual tightness at \(e\) and dual feasibility after replacing only its \(j\)-th coordinate by an atom \(t\) give
\[
 u_{js}+\sum_{k\ne j}u_{ke_k}=(z_{js}+w)^2,
 \qquad
 u_{jt}+\sum_{k\ne j}u_{ke_k}\le(z_{jt}+w)^2.
\]
Subtracting and using \(u_{jr}=z_{jr}^2-\phi_{jr}\) yields
\[
 \phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}\ge-2w(z_{jt}-z_{js})=g(z_{jt}-z_{js}). \tag{1}
\]
Every atom has positive integer marginal multiplicity, so it is incident to the positive support of \(q^\star\); hence each \(G_{js}\) is nonempty.  If \(d=z_{jt}-z_{js}>0\), apply (1) from \(s\) to \(t\) and then from \(t\) to \(s\).  For \(g\in G_{js}\) and \(h\in G_{jt}\),
\[
 gd\le\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}\le hd. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(g\le(\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js})/d\le h\), so \(\max G_{js}\le\min G_{jt}\), and
\[
 z_{js}+\max G_{js}<z_{jt}+\min G_{jt}. \tag{3}
\]
This proves strict order and disjointness.

Because \(z_{k\cdot}\in[L_k,U_k]\), the sum of the other active coordinates belongs to \([\sum_{k\ne j}L_k,\sum_{k\ne j}U_k]\).  Hence
\[
 G_{js}\subset\left[-2\sum_{k\ne j}U_k,-2\sum_{k\ne j}L_k\right]
\]
and every \(I_{js}\) lies in the claimed root.  Its length is
\[
 (U_j-L_j)+2\sum_{k\ne j}(U_k-L_k)
 =|\widetilde c_j|+2\sum_{k\ne j}|\widetilde c_k|
 =H_j(\widetilde c). \tag{4}
\]

Finally suppose \(I_{js},I_{jt}\) lie in a common interval of length \(h\) and \(z_{js}<z_{jt}\).  Choose \(g\in G_{js}\), \(h'\in G_{jt}\), and set \(m=(\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js})/(z_{jt}-z_{js})\).  By (2), \(g\le m\le h'\), whereas the two points \(z_{js}+g,z_{jt}+h'\) in the common interval give
\[
 (z_{jt}-z_{js})+(h'-g)\le h. \tag{5}
\]
Both nonnegative summands are at most \(h\), and therefore
\[
 |z_{jt}-z_{js}|\le h,\qquad
 0\le\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}-g(z_{jt}-z_{js})
 =(m-g)(z_{jt}-z_{js})\le h^2. \tag{6}
\]
If the source is the larger atom, reverse the two roles; the same calculation uses the source slope and gives the same bounds.  Aggregation treats repeats as one atom with positive multiplicity.  Equations (1)--(6) are closed inequalities, so boundary contacts do not alter them.  This proves the lemma.

Under \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\), suppose \(r=r(\widetilde c)\ge3\).  Divide the arm-\(j\) root from \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\) at depth \(\ell\) into \(D_\ell=2^\ell\) consecutive half-open cells of width \(h_{j,\ell}=H_j(\widetilde c)2^{-\ell}\), with the last cell right-closed.  Group atom intervals contained in a common cell and make every other interval a singleton; let \(K_{j,\ell}\) be the resulting cluster count.  Apply basic-table support elimination, integer-part freezing, and armwise tree matching exactly as in the three-arm algorithm, now over the \(r\) active arms.  Then
\[
 K_{j,\ell}\le2D_\ell-1,
\]
the \(r\)-arm incidence matrix has \(r-1\) independent row dependencies, and the integer residuals obey
\[
 R_0=0,\qquad
 R_{\ell+1}\le\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}K_{j,\ell+1}-r
 \le2r(2^{\ell+1}-1).
\]
The number \(M_{j,\ell}\) of arm-\(j\) moves whose lowest common cluster has depth \(\ell\) satisfies \(M_{j,\ell}\le R_{\ell+1}\).  The algorithm produces exactly \(N\) rows with the prescribed active marginal multiplicities; inactive arms may be attached by arbitrary occurrence-wise bijections.  For fixed \(K\), rational coefficients, atoms, and input optimal rational coupling, the construction uses exact rational arithmetic and runs in time polynomial in \(N\) and the total input bit length.

At depth \(\ell\), at most one nonsingleton cluster lies in each of the \(D_\ell\) cells.  Every interval not contained in a cell contains an interior grid boundary; assign it its leftmost such boundary.  Strictly disjoint closed intervals cannot receive the same boundary.  Therefore
\[
 K_{j,\ell}\le D_\ell+(D_\ell-1)=2D_\ell-1. \tag{7}
\]
The half-open convention with a right-closed last cell includes the root endpoint and treats a boundary contact as a singleton already counted in (7).  Because every coarse boundary is also a finer dyadic boundary, the partitions are nested.  At depth zero every root is one cluster.  Distinct aggregated atoms have distinct \(z\)-coordinates because \(j\) is active.  By the final conclusion of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\), any two atoms in one nonsingleton depth-\(L\) cluster differ by at most \(h_{j,L}\).  A finite \(L\) for which every \(h_{j,L}\) is smaller than the least positive within-arm separation therefore makes every partition singleton.

Fix a depth and let \(A_\ell\) be the zero--one incidence matrix whose rows are all clusters of the \(r\) active arms and whose columns are the currently available cells of the original dual-tight support.  The residual table solves
\[
 A_\ell q=b_\ell, \tag{8}
\]
where \(b_\ell\) is integer because it is the original cluster-multiplicity vector minus already frozen integer counts.  If the positive columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational kernel vector \(d\) supported on them.  Summing all cluster rows of any one arm gives the all-ones row, so \(A_\ell d=0\) implies \(\sum_e d_e=0\).  Thus \(d\) has both signs.  Moving by the largest feasible multiple in one sign preserves (8), preserves nonnegativity, and deletes a positive cell.  Iteration gives a rational basic table.

For each active arm, the sum of that arm's cluster rows is the same all-ones row.  Relative to one reference arm, the \(r-1\) differences of these block sums are linearly independent: in a linear combination, inspection of any nonreference row block forces its coefficient to vanish.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_\ell)
 \le\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}K_{j,\ell}-(r-1). \tag{9}
\]
After freezing \(\lfloor q_e\rfloor\) copies of each positive cell, every residual entry is in \([0,1)\).  Its total \(R_\ell\) is an integer, since it equals the sum of the remaining integer cluster margins in any arm.  If positive, it is strictly smaller than the number of positive residual cells; (9) therefore gives
\[
 R_\ell\le\sum_jK_{j,\ell}-r. \tag{10}
\]
The same inequality is immediate when the residual is zero.  At the root, all \(K_{j,0}=1\), so (9) gives rank at most one; its integer total is entirely frozen and \(R_0=0\).  Applying (7) and (10) at depth \(\ell+1\) gives
\[
 R_{\ell+1}
 \le r(2D_{\ell+1}-1)-r
 =2r(D_{\ell+1}-1)
 =2r(2^{\ell+1}-1). \tag{11}
\]

For the tree repair, fix an active arm and a depth-\(\ell+1\) block \(B\).  Let \(f_B\) be the occurrences already frozen in \(B\), \(r_B\) its residual fractional mass, and \(l_B\) the occurrences selected there at subsequent coarser freezes.  Feasibility and conservation give
\[
 \operatorname{required}(B)=f_B+r_B,\qquad
 \operatorname{selected}(B)=f_B+l_B,\qquad
 \sum_Br_B=\sum_Bl_B=R_{\ell+1}. \tag{12}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac12\sum_B|\operatorname{selected}(B)-\operatorname{required}(B)|
 =\frac12\sum_B|l_B-r_B|\le R_{\ell+1}. \tag{13}
\]
Matching equal atoms at leaves and then opposite signed child imbalances at each parent consumes one positive and one negative discrepancy.  Thus every movement whose lowest common block has depth \(\ell\) is charged to (13), proving
\[
 M_{j,\ell}\le R_{\ell+1}. \tag{14}
\]
The root imbalance is zero, so these matchings are bijections and restore every active marginal exactly.  Inactive coordinates do not enter the cost and their \(N\) occurrences may be placed by arbitrary bijections.

For rational input of total bit length \(B\) and fixed \(K\), every nonzero scaled atom separation is at least \(2^{-p(B)}\) for a polynomial \(p\), so a singleton depth \(L=O(p(B))\) suffices.  Grid indices are computed by exact dyadic comparisons without materializing \(2^L\) cells.  There are at most the explicitly listed support cells in the input, and support elimination is rational Gaussian elimination on an incidence matrix with at most \(rN\) rows.  After denominators are cleared, a kernel vector may be chosen from minors of order at most \(rN\); the Leibniz formula bounds their magnitudes by \((rN)!\), hence their bit lengths by \(O(rN\log(rN))\).  Each maximal feasible update deletes a support cell and adds at most this many denominator bits.  Across at most \(L\) levels and the input support size, the number of eliminations and the accumulated bit length are polynomial in \(N+B\) for fixed \(K\).  Freezing creates at most \(N\) rows, and tree matching makes at most \(O(KNL)\) moves.  Hence all comparisons, vertex extractions, freezes, and matches use exact rational arithmetic in the stated polynomial time.

%%% FIELD general-quad-statement
In the setting and algorithm of \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\), denote the repaired schedule's average cost by \(C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})\).  A selected dual-tight row \(z\), its repaired row \(z'=z+\delta\), and \(g_j=-2\sum_{k\ne j}z_k\) satisfy the exact dual-slack identity
\[
 \left(\sum_jz'_j\right)^2-\sum_ju_j(z'_j)
 =\left(\sum_j\delta_j\right)^2-\sum_j\delta_j^2
 +\sum_j\{\phi_j(z'_j)-\phi_j(z_j)-g_j\delta_j\}.
\]
Every moved arm-\(j\) coordinate whose lowest common cluster has depth \(\ell\) contributes at most \(r h_{j,\ell}^2\) to this upper bound.  Summing over the output rows gives
\[
 N\left\{C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})-
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})\right\}
 \le r\sum_{\ell\ge0}\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}h_{j,\ell}^2R_{\ell+1}
 <\frac{16r^2}{3}\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}H_j(\widetilde c)^2.
\]

%%% FIELD general-quad-proof
Let \(S=\sum_jz_j\) and \(D=\sum_j\delta_j\).  Tightness at \(z\) gives \(S^2=\sum_ju_j(z_j)\), and \(u_j(v)=v^2-\phi_j(v)\).  Direct expansion therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left(\sum_jz'_j\right)^2-\sum_ju_j(z'_j)
 &= (S+D)^2-\sum_j(z_j+\delta_j)^2+\sum_j\phi_j(z'_j)\\
 &=D^2-\sum_j\delta_j^2
 +\sum_j\{\phi_j(z'_j)-\phi_j(z_j)-g_j\delta_j\},
\end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
because \(g_j=-2(S-z_j)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz in \(r\) active coordinates gives
\[
 D^2-\sum_j\delta_j^2\le(r-1)\sum_j\delta_j^2. \tag{16}
\]
If arm \(j\) moves at depth \(\ell\), the source and target intervals lie in a common cell of width \(h_{j,\ell}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\), with the actual source slope \(g_j\), gives
\[
 \delta_j^2\le h_{j,\ell}^2,\qquad
 0\le\phi_j(z'_j)-\phi_j(z_j)-g_j\delta_j\le h_{j,\ell}^2. \tag{17}
\]
Thus (16) and (17) charge at most \(r h_{j,\ell}^2\) to each moved coordinate.

The repair preserves each marginal multiplicity.  If \(b_{js}=NF_j^{(K)}(\{s\})\), dual optimality and tightness imply
\[
 \sum_{\mathrm{rows}}\sum_ju_j(z'_j)
 =\sum_{j,s}b_{js}u_{js}
 =N V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)}). \tag{18}
\]
Summing (15), using (14), and then (11), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N(C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}-V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c})
 &\le r\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\sum_jh_{j,\ell}^2M_{j,\ell}\\
 &\le r\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\sum_jH_j^2\,4^{-\ell}R_{\ell+1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{19}
\]
The finite sum is strictly below its infinite positive extension, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}4^{-\ell}\,2r(2^{\ell+1}-1)
 &=2r\left(2\sum_{\ell\ge0}2^{-\ell}-\sum_{\ell\ge0}4^{-\ell}\right)\\
 &=2r\left(4-\frac43\right)=\frac{16r}{3}.
\end{aligned} \tag{20}
\]
Substitution in (19) proves
\[
 N(C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}-V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c})
 <\frac{16r^2}{3}\sum_jH_j^2.
\]
If no repair is required, the left side is zero and the same conclusion holds.  This proves the lemma.

 \qquad

Thus the support-size dichotomy is exact: zero realization loss for at most two active arms and \(\Theta_{\widetilde c}(N^{-1})\) loss for every fixed contrast with at least three active arms, without an arithmetic exceptional subsequence.  The upper bound is attained as a guarantee by the deterministic multiscale algorithm of Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\); for fixed \(K\) and rational input it is exact-rational and polynomial in \(N\) and the input bit length. The cost depends only on coordinates in \(J\).  Every full coupling projects to a coupling of the active marginals.  Conversely, any active coupling extends to all \(K\) coordinates by adjoining the inactive marginal laws independently, so the fractional optimum equals the active-coordinate optimum.  Likewise, any active \(N\)-row schedule extends to the inactive empirical marginals by arbitrary occurrence-wise bijections.  It is therefore enough in every case to work on the \(r\) active coordinates.

First let \(r=0\).  The cost is identically zero, so both values in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\) vanish.  If \(r=1\), the cost depends on one marginal only, and its expectation is fixed by that marginal; again the two values coincide.

Let \(r=2\).  Aggregate repeated active-arm atoms and scale a fractional coupling by \(N\), so its row and column sums are integer multiplicities.  Choose an optimal table with the fewest positive cells.  Its bipartite positive-support graph is acyclic.  Indeed, on any support cycle the alternating perturbation preserves every margin.  If its objective directional derivative is nonzero, one of the two directions decreases cost until a cell vanishes; if it is zero, either direction preserves cost until a cell vanishes.  Either case contradicts minimal positive support.  Thus the support is a forest.  A leaf edge has weight equal to its incident integer margin.  Removing that edge and vertex leaves integer margins, so induction over each tree proves every positive weight is integer.  Expanding an integer weight into that many rows gives an \(N\)-row optimal schedule.  Inactive-arm occurrences can be attached arbitrarily, since they do not enter the cost.  Therefore
\[
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 =V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
\]
for every marginal tuple, proving \(\Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}=0\) when \(r\le2\).

Now suppose \(r\ge3\).  The finite coupling polytope is nonempty and compact, so a primal optimum exists.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\) derives a tight dual certificate directly from its optimality, and Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\) then construct a realizable schedule with
\[
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})-
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 <\frac{16r^2}{3N}\sum_{j\in J}H_j^2. \tag{24}
\]
Taking the supremum proves the upper bound.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}affine\mbox{-}obstruction\mbox{-}transfer}\) supplies, for every \(N\ge4\), a member of the same \(K\)-arm scalar class whose gap is exactly \(5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2/N\).  Since all three selected coefficients are nonzero, \(\lambda_{\widetilde c}>0\).  This proves both the lower bound and \(\Theta_{\widetilde c}(N^{-1})\) on every integer \(N\ge4\), so no arithmetic subsequence is excluded.

For the stated specialization, \(\|\widetilde c\|_1=1\), and hence
\[
 H_1=H_2=2-\frac14=\frac74,\qquad H_3=2-\frac12=\frac32.
\]
Therefore
\[
