\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}finitepopulation\_\allowbreak{}variance\_\allowbreak{}rounding\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For scalar rank-one squared-contrast transport with any fixed number \(K\) of empirical marginals and fixed coefficient vector \(\widetilde c\), the same-\(N\) realization loss has an exact support-size phase transition: it is zero when at most two coefficients are nonzero and is \(\Theta_{\widetilde c}(N^{-1})\) when at least three are nonzero.  Ordered dual-slope roots of lengths \(H_j=2\|\widetilde c\|_1-|\widetilde c_j|\), active-arm basic-table freezing, and hierarchical repair give the explicit upper constant \((16r^2/3)\sum_jH_j^2\), while affine placement of the padded four-row obstruction gives \(5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2/N\) on every \(N\ge4\).  For the causal contrast \(c=(1/4,1/4,-1/2)\), the upper constant is exactly \(402\) and the transferred lower constant improves incidentally to \(5/215296\).  The complete-randomization application remains the original three-arm finite-population variance certificate, whose deterministic realization width is \(O(N^{-2})\); sampling-without-replacement uncertainty remains separate and larger.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025) identify superpopulation causal variance bounds over fractional multi-marginal couplings, whereas a fixed population of \(N\) units requires one common \(N\)-row schedule; the distinction is operational and does not contradict their theorem.  Existing non-Monge, mixability, and integer-programming results do not determine how this same-denominator loss changes with the support size of a fixed scalar contrast.

\paragraph{Niche.} The mathematical object is the fractional-versus-realizable loss for \(K\) scalar \([0,1]\) empirical marginals under one fixed rank-one squared contrast.  The general theorem varies \(K\) only at the transport layer; the causal design, estimand, endpoint functional, and empirical application remain the original three-arm contrast.  Arbitrary vector-valued outcome dimension \(d\) and a general quadratic matrix \(A\) are outside the achievability theorem.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves integrality for zero, one, or two active coefficients; for \(r\ge3\) it gives the deterministic bound \(\Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}<(16r^2/3N)\sum_jH_j^2\), a polynomial exact-rational repair algorithm, and the matching all-\(N\ge4\) affine lower family \(5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2/N\).  It then specializes exactly to the \(402/N\) three-arm guarantee and retains the design-based variance and plug-in certificates.

\section{Related work}

Emami and Pass (2025, Introduction, Example 1, and Discussion) move from the empirical Kantorovich relaxation to Monge implementability, prove a minimal non-Monge quadratic example, and identify quantitative approximation error as future work; their barycenter cost has the opposite squared-sum orientation from the signed scalar contrast here.  Friesecke (2019, Theorem 2.2 and Section 3) analyzes non-Monge extreme points in a symmetric finite transport polytope but gives no all-denominator additive rate.  Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025, Sections 2--3 and Theorem 1) identify a superpopulation causal bound with fractional multi-marginal transport and prove plug-in rates; Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}variance\mbox{-}certificate}\) instead quantifies the operational error incurred when the same empirical marginals must be realized by one fixed-population schedule.  Haus (2015) studies exact matrix mixability and bottleneck approximation, while Altschuler and Boix-Adser\`a (2023) restore exact marginals inside the fractional transportation polytope.  Puccetti and Wang (2015) give rearrangement representations and higher-dimensional dependence geometry, but their discretization comparison changes marginals rather than imposing same-\(N\) realizability.  Brennan et al. (2025) compute broad finite causal completions by integer programming.  The new transport theorem identifies a support-size dichotomy for every fixed scalar coefficient vector: two active marginals are integral, while three or more have matching \(N^{-1}\) upper and lower rates.  This does not extend the achievability claim to arbitrary outcome dimension \(d\) or a general quadratic form \(A\).  Aronow, Green, and Lee (2014) remain the exact two-arm causal comparator; Li and Ding (2017) supply the fixed-potential-outcome randomization framework, and Hammer et al. (1996) provide the multi-arm trial consumer.

\begin{lemma}[lem:ordered-dual-slopes]\label{lem:ordered-dual-slopes}
For every optimal support cell and every replacement atom in the same arm, \(\phi_{at}\ge\phi_{as}+g(z_{at}-z_{as})\) for each \(g\in G_{as}\).  Hence, if \(z_{as}<z_{at}\), every \(g\in G_{as}\) and \(h\in G_{at}\) obey \(g\le[\phi_{at}-\phi_{as}]/[z_{at}-z_{as}]\le h\).  The intervals \(I_{as}\) are strictly ordered and disjoint within each arm, with \(I_{1s},I_{2s}\subset[-1/2,5/4]\) and \(I_{3s}\subset[-3/2,0]\).  If \(I_{as}\) and \(I_{at}\) lie in one interval of length \(H\), then \(|z_{at}-z_{as}|\le H\) and \(0\le\phi_{at}-\phi_{as}-g(z_{at}-z_{as})\le H^2\), including ties and grid-boundary contacts under the singleton convention.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\) with \(K=3\) and \(\widetilde c=c=(1/4,1/4,-1/2)\).  The generalized scaled atoms, dual ordinates, slope sets, and intervals are exactly \(z_{as},\phi_{as},G_{as},I_{as}\) in the frozen statement.  Here
\[
 z_1,z_2\in[0,1/4],\qquad z_3\in[-1/2,0].
\]
For arms \(1,2\), the other-coordinate sum lies in \([-1/2,1/4]\), so the slope lies in \([-1/2,1]\) and the root is \([-1/2,5/4]\).  For arm \(3\), the other-coordinate sum lies in \([0,1/2]\), so the slope lies in \([-1,0]\) and the root is \([-3/2,0]\).  All remaining inequalities, including their orientation when the source is the larger atom, are (1)--(6) of that lemma.  Aggregation and the half-open singleton convention give the asserted tie and boundary cases.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact arm-specific slope roots are the cost-specific mechanism that makes the multiscale repair quadratic and reduces the universal constant. \textit{Gap.} Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025, Lemma on finite-support optimal couplings) use coordinate exchanges to bound support sums; they do not derive ordered scalar dual intervals or an integer-repair modulus. \textit{Consumer.} The ranges of lengths seven-fourths, seven-fourths, and three-halves determine the summable per-scale charge in the deterministic rounding algorithm.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hierarchical-repair]\label{lem:hierarchical-repair}
Let \(D_\ell=2^\ell\) and \(K_{a,\ell}=|\mathcal P_{a,\ell}|\).  For every arm and depth, \(K_{a,\ell}\le2D_\ell-1\).  The post-freezing residual satisfies \(R_0=0\) and, for every \(\ell\ge0\), \(R_{\ell+1}\le\sum_aK_{a,\ell+1}-3\le12\,2^\ell-6\).  The tree repair produces exact marginal multiplicities and its cross-child movement counts satisfy \(M_{a,\ell}\le R_{\ell+1}\) for every arm and every nonterminal depth.  These conclusions hold with repeated atoms, arbitrarily small positive rational gaps, and slope intervals touching grid boundaries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Specialize Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\) to \(K=r=3\), \(\widetilde c=c\), and the roots computed in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\).  Its widths are exactly \(h_{1,\ell}=h_{2,\ell}=(7/4)2^{-\ell}\) and \(h_{3,\ell}=(3/2)2^{-\ell}\), so its partitions are \(\mathcal P_{a,\ell}\).  Equation (9) of the general proof becomes the rendered rank inequality
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_\ell)\le\sum_{a=1}^3K_{a,\ell}-2. \tag{27}
\]
Because the residual total is an integer strictly below the positive support count, (27) gives
\[
 R_{\ell+1}\le\sum_aK_{a,\ell+1}-3. \tag{28}
\]
Using \(K_{a,\ell+1}\le2D_{\ell+1}-1\) gives
\[
 R_{\ell+1}\le3(2D_{\ell+1}-1)-3
 =12\,2^\ell-6. \tag{29}
\]
The root, exact-marginal, movement, repetition, rational-gap, and boundary assertions are respectively the specialized conclusions (10)--(14) and the bit-depth argument of the general lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The sharper cluster count and integer residual bound turn sparse fractional residuals into exact same-size marginal counts without a false bounded-discrepancy premise. \textit{Gap.} Haus (2015) gives exact and bottleneck algorithms for special matrix classes, while standard tensor rounding returns another fractional plan; neither controls same-size row repair along an optimal-support hierarchy. \textit{Consumer.} The inequality \(R_{\ell+1}\le12\,2^\ell-6\) is the combinatorial input for the constant \(402\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:quadratic-slack-accounting]\label{lem:quadratic-slack-accounting}
For each selected dual-tight row \(z\), repaired row \(z'\), and displacements \(\delta_a=z'_a-z_a\), the dual slack equals \((\sum_a\delta_a)^2-\sum_a\delta_a^2+\sum_a[\phi_a(z'_a)-\phi_a(z_a)-g_a\delta_a]\), where \(g_a=-2\sum_{b\ne a}z_b\).  Summed over all rows produced by \(\mathsf A_{\mathrm{round}}\), this identity and the hierarchical movement bounds give \(N[C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)]\le3\sum_{\ell\ge0}\sum_a h_{a,\ell}^2R_{\ell+1}<402\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Specialize the identity (15) of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\) to the three active coordinates.  Then \((\sum_a\delta_a)^2-\sum_a\delta_a^2\le2\sum_a\delta_a^2\), while the ordered-slope remainder contributes at most one further \(h_{a,\ell}^2\); hence every moved coordinate costs at most \(3h_{a,\ell}^2\).  Marginal preservation makes the summed dual value \(NV_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\), so
\[
 N\{C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\}
 \le3\sum_{\ell\ge0}\sum_{a=1}^3h_{a,\ell}^2R_{\ell+1}. \tag{30}
\]
Here
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^3h_{a,\ell}^2
 =\left\{2\left(\frac74\right)^2+\left(\frac32\right)^2\right\}4^{-\ell}
 =\frac{67}{8}4^{-\ell}. \tag{31}
\]
Using (29) and extending the finite repair sum by positive terms,
\[
 \sum_{\ell\ge0}(12\,2^{-\ell}-6\,4^{-\ell})=24-8=16. \tag{32}
\]
Thus
\[
 N\{C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\}
 <3\cdot\frac{67}{8}\cdot16=402. \tag{33}
\]
This is also exactly the specialization (25) of the general bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact slack identity removes the logarithm that a generic bounded-cost or Lipschitz repair argument would accumulate across scales. \textit{Gap.} No cited Monge, mixability, or multi-marginal transport rounding result charges same-marginal integer repair by a dual Bregman remainder for this signed rank-one quadratic cost. \textit{Consumer.} The exact identity and arm-specific summation deliver the universal total-slack constant \(402\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:constructive-rounding-upper]\label{thm:constructive-rounding-upper}
For every \(N\ge4\) and every bounded empirical marginal triple \(F\), there exists \(T\in\mathcal S_N(F)\) with average cost at most \(V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)+402/N\); therefore \(V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\le\min\{1/4,402/N\}\).  The analytic term \(402/N\) is strictly smaller than the trivial bound \(1/4\) exactly when \(N\ge1609\).  If the atoms and an optimal coupling \(\pi^\star\) are rational, \(\mathsf A_{\mathrm{round}}\) returns such a schedule, equivalently two permutations relative to arm 1, in time polynomial in \(N\) and the rational input bit length.  The vertex extraction, comparisons, and output use exact rational arithmetic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}fixed\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}support\mbox{-}dichotomy}\), specialized as in (25), and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\) give the explicit schedule \(T_{\mathrm{alg}}\in\mathcal S_N(F)\) with
\[
 C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)<V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)+\frac{402}{N}. \tag{34}
\]
Every realizable schedule induces a coupling in \(\Gamma(F)\), whereas \(T_{\mathrm{alg}}\) is feasible for \(V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)\).  Hence
\[
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\le V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)\le C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)
 <V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)+\frac{402}{N}. \tag{35}
\]
On \([0,1]^3\), the fixed contrast lies in \([-1/2,1/2]\), so \(0\le C(y)\le1/4\).  Combining this range with (35) proves the stated minimum of the two bounds.  Since \(402/N<1/4\) exactly when \(N>1608\), the analytic term is nontrivial exactly for integer \(N\ge1609\).

The specialized algorithm is precisely \(\mathsf A_{\mathrm{round}}\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}rounding\mbox{-}algorithm}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\) proves exact marginals and polynomially many exact rational operations.  Expanding the output into \(N\) labeled arm-1 occurrences, the arm-2 and arm-3 occurrence labels are two permutations, even with repeated values.  This proves all algorithmic assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-rounding-frontier]\label{thm:sharp-rounding-frontier}

\begin{proof}

 \frac{5}{215296N}\le\Delta_N<\frac{402}{N}. \tag{40}
\]

The stronger lower bound holds for every \(N\ge4\), so in particular there is no arithmetic exceptional subsequence. For \(N=2\), write the two scaled values in arm \(a\) as \(m_a-d_a\) and \(m_a+d_a\), with \(d_a\ge0\).  Any fractional coupling is a law of \(S\in\{-1,1\}^3\) with \(\mathbb ES_a=0\); when \(d_a=0\), an auxiliary balanced sign label leaves the values unchanged.  With \(M=\sum_am_a\),

 =M^2+\mathbb E\left(\sum_ad_aS_a\right)^2. \tag{41}

Antipodal symmetrization preserves balanced marginals and (41).  Every antipodally symmetric law is a convex combination of the four laws uniform on a pair \(\{s,-s\}\). Thus \(V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)=V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\) for every two-row triple and \(\Delta_2=0\).

\begin{proposition}[prop:two-arm-reduction]\label{prop:two-arm-reduction}
Fix the two-arm schedule \(x^{(2)}\), its common-denominator marginals \(F^{(2)}\), positive counts \(m_0+m_1=N\), the zero-sum contrast \(d\), and the complete-randomization law for \(Z^{(2)}\). Every extreme point of \(\Gamma(F^{(2)})\) is representable by a table in \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\). Consequently \(\min_{\pi\in\Gamma(F^{(2)})}\int(d_0y_0+d_1y_1)^2\,d\pi=\min_{T\in\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})}N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N(d_0T_{0i}+d_1T_{1i})^2=C_{\mathrm{co}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)})\), while \(\max_{\pi\in\Gamma(F^{(2)})}\int(d_0y_0+d_1y_1)^2\,d\pi=\max_{T\in\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})}N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N(d_0T_{0i}+d_1T_{1i})^2=C_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)})\). The estimator \(\widehat\theta_N^{(2)}\) is design-unbiased for \(\theta_N^{(2)}\), and, as \(x^{(2)}\) ranges over \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\), the sharp interval hull of \(\operatorname{Var}_{Z^{(2)}}(\widehat\theta_N^{(2)})\) is exactly \([L_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)}),U_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)})]\). In particular, \(U_N^{(2)}=\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}/m_b-[\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1\overline S_{10}^{(2)}]/N\) and \(L_N^{(2)}=\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}/m_b-[\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1\underline S_{10}^{(2)}]/N\), which are the Aronow--Green--Lee (2014, Lemma 1 and equations (7)--(11)) sharp endpoints for the scaled difference in means.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the distinct atoms of \(F_0^{(2)}\) and \(F_1^{(2)}\) as \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_R\) and \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_S\), respectively, and put
\[
k_r=NF_0^{(2)}(\{\alpha_r\}),\qquad
\ell_s=NF_1^{(2)}(\{\beta_s\}).
\]
Because both laws are empirical laws of \(N\) fixed entries, every \(k_r\) and \(\ell_s\) is a positive integer and
\(\sum_r k_r=\sum_s\ell_s=N\).  Every coupling in \(\Gamma(F^{(2)})\) is supported on the finite Cartesian product of these atom sets.  Thus it is represented by a nonnegative matrix \(p=(p_{rs})\) satisfying
\[
\sum_s p_{rs}=\frac{k_r}{N},\qquad
\sum_r p_{rs}=\frac{\ell_s}{N}.
\]
After the invertible scaling \(q=Np\), the feasible set is the transportation polytope
\[
q_{rs}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_s q_{rs}=k_r,\qquad \sum_r q_{rs}=\ell_s. \tag{1}
\]

We first prove the required integrality rather than assume a transportation-polytope theorem.  Associate with a feasible \(q\) the bipartite graph having a row vertex \(r\), a column vertex \(s\), and an edge \((r,s)\) exactly when \(q_{rs}>0\).  If this positive-support graph contains a cycle, assign alternating signs \(+1,-1,+1,-1,\ldots\) to its edges and zero off the cycle.  The resulting matrix \(h\neq0\) has every row and column sum equal to zero.  Taking
\(0<\varepsilon<\min\{q_{rs}:(r,s)\text{ lies on the cycle}\}\) gives two distinct feasible matrices \(q+\varepsilon h\) and \(q-\varepsilon h\) whose midpoint is \(q\).  Hence the support of every extreme point is a forest.

Now let \(q\) be an extreme point.  In each nonempty component of its support forest, choose a leaf.  The weight on the unique edge incident to that leaf equals the leaf's residual margin in (1), which is an integer.  Delete that edge and leaf and subtract this integer weight from the neighboring residual margin.  The new residual margins remain nonnegative integers.  Iterating, while discarding any isolated zero-margin vertex, shows that every positive entry of \(q\) is an integer.  Expanding the cell \((r,s)\) into \(q_{rs}\) rows \((\alpha_r,\beta_s)\) produces exactly \(N\) rows and uses \(\alpha_r\) exactly \(k_r\) times and \(\beta_s\) exactly \(\ell_s\) times.  It therefore produces a member of \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\).  This proves that every extreme point of \(\Gamma(F^{(2)})\) is realizable.

The polytope (1) is nonempty, because \(q_{rs}=k_r\ell_s/N\) is feasible, and it is closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional space.  The linear cost therefore has a minimizer and a maximizer.  Starting from either optimizer, if its positive support contains a cycle, use the alternating perturbation above and increase \(\varepsilon\) until an edge first becomes zero.  Linearity says that the original objective is the average of the two perturbed objectives.  At a minimum both perturbed objectives are no smaller, so both equal the original objective; at a maximum the reversed inequalities give the same conclusion.  Retaining a perturbed optimizer with one fewer positive cell and iterating produces an acyclic, hence integral, optimizer.  Thus both extrema may be computed over \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\).  It remains to identify them.  Since \(d_0+d_1=0\), write \(d=(\lambda,-\lambda)\), where the case \(\lambda=0\) is included.  Expand the two empirical marginals into ordered lists
\[
A_1\leq\cdots\leq A_N,\qquad B_1\leq\cdots\leq B_N.
\]
After using the first list as row labels, every realizable table has cost
\[
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N(\lambda A_i-\lambda B_{\sigma(i)})^2
=\frac{\lambda^2}{N}\left(\sum_i A_i^2+\sum_iB_i^2-2\sum_iA_iB_{\sigma(i)}\right) \tag{2}
\]
for a permutation \(\sigma\).  If \(i<j\), \(A_i\leq A_j\), and the two paired values satisfy \(B_{\sigma(i)}>B_{\sigma(j)}\), swapping those two values changes the product sum by
\[
(A_iB_{\sigma(j)}+A_jB_{\sigma(i)})
 -(A_iB_{\sigma(i)}+A_jB_{\sigma(j)})
=(A_j-A_i)(B_{\sigma(i)}-B_{\sigma(j)})\geq0. \tag{3}
\]
Successively removing inversions proves that \(\sum_iA_iB_{\sigma(i)}\) is maximized by like ordering.  Applying the same exchange argument to \(-B_i\), or equivalently successively removing like-ordered pairs, proves that it is minimized by reverse ordering.  Equalities in (3) show that repeated atoms cause no problem.  Since each empirical quantile is constant on intervals of length \(1/N\), equation (2) now gives
\[
\min_{\pi\in\Gamma(F^{(2)})}\int(d_0y_0+d_1y_1)^2\,d\pi
=\int_0^1[d_0Q_0^{(2)}(u)+d_1Q_1^{(2)}(u)]^2\,du
=C_{\mathrm{co}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)}), \tag{4}
\]
and, with identities at the finitely many quantile boundaries irrelevant to the integral,
\[
\max_{\pi\in\Gamma(F^{(2)})}\int(d_0y_0+d_1y_1)^2\,d\pi
=\int_0^1[d_0Q_0^{(2)}(u)+d_1Q_1^{(2)}(1-u)]^2\,du
=C_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)}). \tag{5}
\]
The same extrema over \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\) follow from integrality.

We next derive the design variance.  Fix any table \(T\in\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\), and let
\[
I_{bi}=\mathbf 1\{Z_i^{(2)}=b\},\qquad
S_{10}^{(2)}(T)=\frac1{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N(T_{1i}-\mu_1^{(2)})(T_{0i}-\mu_0^{(2)}).
\]
The complete-randomization assumption and positivity of \(m_0,m_1\) give
\[
\mathbb E I_{bi}=\frac{m_b}{N},\qquad
\mathbb E(I_{bi}I_{bj})=\frac{m_b(m_b-1)}{N(N-1)}\quad(i\ne j), \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E(I_{0i}I_{1i})=0,\qquad
\mathbb E(I_{0i}I_{1j})=\frac{m_0m_1}{N(N-1)}\quad(i\ne j). \tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathbb E_{Z^{(2)}}\widehat\theta_N^{(2)}
=\sum_{b\in\mathbb B}\frac{d_b}{m_b}\sum_i\frac{m_b}{N}T_{bi}
=\sum_{b\in\mathbb B}d_b\mu_b^{(2)}
=\theta_N^{(2)}, \tag{8}
\]
so the estimator is design-unbiased for the fixed causal contrast.

For completeness, center \(T_{bi}\) at \(\mu_b^{(2)}\).  Using (6), the facts that the centered entries sum to zero and that their squared sum is \((N-1)S_b^{2,(2)}\) yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_b}\sum_i I_{bi}T_{bi}\right)
=\left(\frac1{m_b}-\frac1N\right)S_b^{2,(2)}. \tag{9}
\]
Likewise, (7) and
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}(T_{0i}-\mu_0^{(2)})(T_{1j}-\mu_1^{(2)})
=-\sum_i(T_{0i}-\mu_0^{(2)})(T_{1i}-\mu_1^{(2)})
\]
give
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\frac1{m_0}\sum_iI_{0i}T_{0i},
\frac1{m_1}\sum_iI_{1i}T_{1i}\right)
=-\frac1N S_{10}^{(2)}(T). \tag{10}
\]
Combining (9)--(10),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Z^{(2)}}(\widehat\theta_N^{(2)})
=\sum_{b\in\mathbb B}\frac{d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}}{m_b}
-\frac1N\left\{\sum_{b\in\mathbb B}d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}
+2d_0d_1S_{10}^{(2)}(T)\right\}. \tag{11}
\]
Define the fixed row contrast \(\tau_i=d_0T_{0i}+d_1T_{1i}\) and its average squared value
\(C_T=N^{-1}\sum_i\tau_i^2\).  Its mean is \(\theta_N^{(2)}\), because the marginal means do not depend on \(T\).  Therefore
\[
\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(\tau_i-\theta_N^{(2)})^2
=\frac{N}{N-1}\{C_T-(\theta_N^{(2)})^2\}
=\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1S_{10}^{(2)}(T). \tag{12}
\]
Since \(S_b^{2,(2)}=Nv_b^{(2)}/(N-1)\), substituting (12) into (11) gives the exact cost representation
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Z^{(2)}}(\widehat\theta_N^{(2)})
=\frac{1}{N-1}\left\{
\sum_{b\in\mathbb B}\frac{N}{m_b}d_b^2v_b^{(2)}
+(\theta_N^{(2)})^2-C_T\right\}
=\frac{B_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)})-C_T}{N-1}. \tag{13}
\]

The first term in the numerator of (13) depends only on the fixed marginals.  Thus (4)--(5) imply that the maximum design variance over \(\mathcal S_N(F^{(2)})\) is obtained by the comonotone table and the minimum by the countermonotone table.  The schedule class is finite, so both extrema are attained; hence the interval hull is exactly
\[
\left[
\frac{B_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)})-C_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)})}{N-1},
\frac{B_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)})-C_{\mathrm{co}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)})}{N-1}
\right]
=\bigl[L_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)}),U_N^{(2)}(F^{(2)})\bigr], \tag{14}
\]
where the last equality is exactly \(\mathrm{def:two-arm-endpoints}\).

Finally, expanding the comonotone cost around the two marginal means gives
\[
\frac{N}{N-1}\left\{C_{\mathrm{co}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)})-(\theta_N^{(2)})^2\right\}
=\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1\overline S_{10}^{(2)}, \tag{15}
\]
because
\(\overline S_{10}^{(2)}=\frac{N}{N-1}\{\int_0^1Q_1^{(2)}(u)Q_0^{(2)}(u)\,du-\mu_1^{(2)}\mu_0^{(2)}\}\).
The identical expansion with \(Q_1^{(2)}(1-u)\) gives
\[
\frac{N}{N-1}\left\{C_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{(2)}(F^{(2)})-(\theta_N^{(2)})^2\right\}
=\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1\underline S_{10}^{(2)}. \tag{16}
\]
Substitution of (15)--(16) into (13)--(14) proves
\[
U_N^{(2)}
=\sum_b\frac{d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}}{m_b}
-\frac{\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1\overline S_{10}^{(2)}}{N},
\]
and
\[
L_N^{(2)}
=\sum_b\frac{d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}}{m_b}
-\frac{\sum_b d_b^2S_b^{2,(2)}+2d_0d_1\underline S_{10}^{(2)}}{N}.
\]
These are the scaled-contrast versions of the sharp endpoints in Aronow, Green, and Lee (2014, Lemma~1 and equations~(7)--(11)); the argument above independently derives them from the stated complete-randomization law and the fixed empirical marginals.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The bivariate reduction is the required sanity check and isolates the loss of Birkhoff integrality as a genuinely three-arm phenomenon. \textit{Gap.} Aronow, Green, and Lee (2014) solve the two-arm endpoint exactly; the proposal preserves their result as the zero-gap boundary rather than replacing its estimand. \textit{Consumer.} Existing two-arm trial workflows remain unchanged, while software can activate the realization certificate only for three or more arms.

\begin{theorem}[thm:variance-certificate]\label{thm:variance-certificate}
Under complete randomization, every realizable completion \(T\in\mathcal S_N(F)\) with average squared contrast \(t\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_N)=[B_N(F)-t]/(N-1)\).  Consequently the sharp marginal-information upper endpoint is \(U_N(F)\), and \(U_{\mathrm{lo}}(F)\le U_N(F)\le U_{\mathrm{hi}}(F)\) with computable width \([C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)]/(N-1)\le402/[N(N-1)]\).  The fractional endpoint of Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025) is therefore an upper certificate for, but need not be representable by, an \(N\)-unit science table; this is a finite-population operational correction, not a contradiction of their superpopulation identified-set theorem.  The lower variance endpoint is exactly \(L_N(F)\), because the comonotone rank pairing attains \(C_{\max}(F)\) as both a fractional and realizable coupling.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under Assumption ass:complete-randomization, the potential-outcome array is fixed and \(Z\) is the sole source of randomness.  Put
\[
 I_{ai}=\mathbf 1\{Z_i=a\},\qquad Y_{ai}=x_{ai}-\mu_a.
\]
The fixed-count design gives \(\mathbb E I_{ai}=n_a/N\), so
\[
 \mathbb E_Z\widehat\theta_N
 =\sum_ac_an_a^{-1}\sum_i\frac{n_a}{N}x_{ai}
 =\sum_ac_a\mu_a=\theta_N. \tag{30}
\]
For distinct units, direct counting under the same design gives
\[
 \mathbb E(I_{ai}I_{aj})=\frac{n_a(n_a-1)}{N(N-1)},\qquad
 \mathbb E(I_{ai}I_{bj})=\frac{n_an_b}{N(N-1)}\quad(a\ne b), \tag{31}
\]
whereas \(I_{ai}I_{bi}=0\) when \(a\ne b\).  Define
\[
 S_{ab}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_iY_{ai}Y_{bi},\qquad S_a^2=S_{aa}.
\]
Substitution of (31) into the covariance sums, using \(\sum_iY_{ai}=0\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z\left(n_a^{-1}\sum_iI_{ai}x_{ai}\right)
 =\left(\frac1{n_a}-\frac1N\right)S_a^2, \tag{32}
\]
and, for \(a\ne b\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_Z\left(n_a^{-1}\sum_iI_{ai}x_{ai},
 n_b^{-1}\sum_iI_{bi}x_{bi}\right)=-\frac{S_{ab}}N. \tag{33}
\]
Let \(\tau_i=\sum_ac_ax_{ai}\) and
\[
 S_\tau^2=\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(\tau_i-\theta_N)^2.
\]
Expanding the variance of the contrast in (32)--(33) yields the finite-population identity
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_N)
 =\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_a^2}{n_a}-\frac{S_\tau^2}{N}. \tag{34}
\]
If the completion has \(t=N^{-1}\sum_i\tau_i^2\), its fixed marginal moments give
\[
 S_a^2=\frac{N}{N-1}v_a,\quad
 S_\tau^2=\frac{N}{N-1}(t-\theta_N^2). \tag{35}
\]
Using (35) in (34) and the definition of \(B_N(F)\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\theta_N)
 =\frac{\sum_a(N/n_a)c_a^2v_a+\theta_N^2-t}{N-1}
 =\frac{B_N(F)-t}{N-1}. \tag{36}
\]

Equation (36) is decreasing in \(t\).  Definition def:realizable-value says that the least realizable value is \(V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)\), so Definition def:variance-certificates makes the sharp upper endpoint \(U_N(F)\).  The constructive schedule and the fractional relaxation obey
\[
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\le V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)\le C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F),
\]
and hence
\[
 U_{\mathrm{lo}}(F)\le U_N(F)\le U_{\mathrm{hi}}(F). \tag{37}
\]
Their computable primal--dual width is
\[
 U_{\mathrm{hi}}(F)-U_{\mathrm{lo}}(F)
 =\frac{C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)}{N-1}
 \le\frac{402}{N(N-1)}. \tag{38}
\]
For \(N\ge4\), (38) follows from Theorem thm:constructive-rounding-upper.  Complete randomization into three positive arms also permits \(N=3\); in that case \(0\le C_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)\le1/4<402/3\), so (38) still holds.  The already-established Theorem thm:explicit-matching-lower supplies a schedule for which the fractional optimum is not realizable, proving the stated ``need not'' clause.  The conclusion only compares a finite-population \(N\)-row completion with the unrestricted coupling functional; it does not dispute an identified set defined over a superpopulation Fr\'{e}chet class.

For the lower endpoint, expand every empirical arm into \(N\) occurrences and sort the scaled values as \(r_1\le\cdots\le r_N\) and \(s_1\le\cdots\le s_N\).  Any fractional two-coordinate projection can be split among equal-valued occurrences to give a matrix \(P=(P_{ij})\) with nonnegative entries and every row and column sum one.  Write
\[
 s_j=s_1+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(s_{k+1}-s_k)\mathbf1\{j>k\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_{i,j}P_{ij}r_is_j
 =s_1\sum_ir_i+
 \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(s_{k+1}-s_k)\sum_i r_iw_{ik},
 \qquad w_{ik}=\sum_{j>k}P_{ij}. \tag{39}
\]
For each \(k\), \(0\le w_{ik}\le1\) and \(\sum_iw_{ik}=N-k\).  An exchange that moves weight from a smaller \(r_i\) to a larger \(r_j\) cannot decrease \(\sum_i r_iw_{ik}\); therefore
\[
 \sum_i r_iw_{ik}\le\sum_{i>k}r_i. \tag{40}
\]
Equations (39)--(40) prove that the equal-rank pairing maximizes the product sum for every fractional pair coupling, with repeats included.

For a three-arm completion, expansion of the total cost gives
\[
 \sum_i\left(\sum_az_{ai}\right)^2
 =\sum_{a,i}z_{ai}^2+2\sum_{a<b}\sum_i z_{ai}z_{bi}. \tag{41}
\]
The one-coordinate square sums in (41) are fixed by the marginals.  Applying (39)--(40) to each pairwise projection bounds every pair-product sum by its comonotone value.  Sorting all three scaled columns increasingly and pairing equal ranks attains all three bounds simultaneously, and is an \(N\)-row realizable schedule.  Hence it attains the maximum cost over both fractional and realizable couplings, namely \(C_{\max}(F)\).  Since (36) decreases with cost, the sharp lower variance endpoint is
\[
 L_N(F)=\frac{B_N(F)-C_{\max}(F)}{N-1}.
\]
This completes the certificate proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts the transport integrality gap into a two-sided numerical certificate around the sharp upper variance endpoint without mislabeling it as a bound on the unknown actual completion. \textit{Gap.} Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025) use fractional transport, and Brennan et al. (2025) use an integer program; neither supplies a universal analytic width connecting the two endpoints for the same empirical marginals. \textit{Consumer.} Three-arm contrasts can pair an attainable completion with a fractional dual bound and report an exact primal--dual width bounded universally by \(402/[N(N-1)]\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:plugin-endpoints]\label{thm:plugin-endpoints}
Under complete randomization, \(\mathbb E W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\rho_a}\) and \(W_1(G_a,\widehat F_a)\le N^{-1}\).  For the quantile-lifted triple \(G\), \(|L_N(G)-L_N(F)|\le D_N(F,G)/(N-1)\) and \(\max\{|U_{\mathrm{lo}}(G)-U_N(F)|,|U_{\mathrm{hi}}(G)-U_N(F)|\}\le[D_N(F,G)+402/N]/(N-1)\), with \(\mathbb ED_N(F,G)\le M_N\).  If \(n_a/N\to p_a\in(0,1)\), all three endpoint estimates have error \(O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-3/2})+O(N^{-2})\).  For every \(\eta\in(0,1)\), the computable interval \([U_{\mathrm{lo}}(G)-[M_N/\eta+402/N]/(N-1),U_{\mathrm{hi}}(G)+[M_N/\eta+402/N]/(N-1)]\) covers \(U_N(F)\) with probability at least \(1-\eta\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a probability law \(H\) on \([0,1]\), let \(Q_H\) be its nondecreasing quantile.  For finite laws, matching equal infinitesimal quantile masses and using
\[
 |r-s|=\int_0^1|\mathbf1\{r\le t\}-\mathbf1\{s\le t\}|\,dt
\]
gives the one-dimensional identity
\[
 W_1(H,K)=\int_0^1|Q_H(u)-Q_K(u)|\,du
 =\int_0^1|H(t)-K(t)|\,dt. \tag{42}
\]
On each interval \(((j-1)/N,j/N]\), monotonicity bounds the integral error from the midpoint quantile value by \(N^{-1}\) times the quantile variation across that interval.  The variations telescope to at most one.  Definition def:plugin-lift and (42) therefore give
\[
 W_1(G_a,\widehat F_a)\le N^{-1}. \tag{43}
\]

Under Assumption ass:complete-randomization, arm \(a\) reveals a simple random sample without replacement of size \(n_a\) from its fixed finite-population column.  For every \(t\in[0,1]\), direct indicator covariance calculation for this hypergeometric count gives
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat F_a(t)=F_a(t),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat F_a(t))
 =\rho_aF_a(t)\{1-F_a(t)\},\quad
 \rho_a=\frac{N-n_a}{n_a(N-1)}. \tag{44}
\]
Because complete randomization has finite assignment support, expectation is a finite weighted sum; distributing the integral in (42) across that sum, followed pointwise by Cauchy--Schwarz and then by \(p(1-p)\le1/4\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E W_1(\widehat F_a,F_a)
 &=\int_0^1\mathbb E|\widehat F_a(t)-F_a(t)|\,dt\\
 &\le\sqrt{\rho_a}\int_0^1\sqrt{F_a(t)\{1-F_a(t)\}}\,dt
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\rho_a}. \tag{45}
\end{aligned}
\]
The triangle inequality, (43), and (45) imply
\[
 \mathbb EW_1(G_a,F_a)\le\frac12\sqrt{\rho_a}+N^{-1}. \tag{46}
\]
This argument is armwise and does not assume independence between the observed arms.

We next prove the deterministic perturbation inequalities.  For \(X_a,Y_a\in[0,1]\), both contrasts lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\), so
\[
 \left|\left(\sum_ac_aX_a\right)^2-
 \left(\sum_ac_aY_a\right)^2\right|
 \le\sum_a|c_a|\,|X_a-Y_a|. \tag{47}
\]
Starting from any coupling of \(F\), glue to each coordinate an optimal one-dimensional \(W_1\) transport from \(F_a\) to \(G_a\), conditionally independently given that coordinate.  The resulting triple has marginals \(G\).  Applying (47), then reversing the roles of \(F\) and \(G\), proves
\[
 |V_{\mathrm{rel}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{rel}}(G)|
 \le\sum_a|c_a|W_1(F_a,G_a). \tag{48}
\]
The same argument with suprema proves (48) with \(C_{\max}\) in place of \(V_{\mathrm{rel}}\).  Since both \(F\) and \(G\) have \(N\) equal-weight occurrences, sorted coordinatewise matching attains every one-dimensional \(W_1\).  Applying those three bijections to an optimal schedule, and then reversing \(F,G\), gives
\[
 |V_{\mathrm{int}}(F)-V_{\mathrm{int}}(G)|
 \le\sum_a|c_a|W_1(F_a,G_a). \tag{49}
\]

For each arm,
\[
 |\mu_a(F)-\mu_a(G)|\le W_1(F_a,G_a). \tag{50}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 v_a(H)=\min_{m\in[0,1]}\int(x-m)^2\,dH(x),
\]
and \(x\mapsto(x-m)^2\) is uniformly \(2\)-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\).  Comparing at the minimizing mean for each law in turn gives
\[
 |v_a(F)-v_a(G)|\le2W_1(F_a,G_a). \tag{51}
\]
Since \(|\theta_N(F)|,|\theta_N(G)|\le1/2\), equation (50) gives
\[
 |\theta_N(F)^2-\theta_N(G)^2|
 \le|\theta_N(F)-\theta_N(G)|
 \le\sum_a|c_a|W_1(F_a,G_a). \tag{52}
\]
Combining (48)--(52) with the definitions of \(B_N\), \(D_N\), and the endpoints yields
\[
 |L_N(G)-L_N(F)|\le\frac{D_N(F,G)}{N-1},\qquad
 |U_N(G)-U_N(F)|\le\frac{D_N(F,G)}{N-1}. \tag{53}
\]
Taking expectations in the definition of \(D_N\) and using (46) proves
\[
 \mathbb ED_N(F,G)\le M_N. \tag{54}
\]

Apply Theorem thm:variance-certificate to \(G\).  Its deterministic width gives
\[
 0\le U_N(G)-U_{\mathrm{lo}}(G)\le\frac{402}{N(N-1)},
 \qquad
 0\le U_{\mathrm{hi}}(G)-U_N(G)\le\frac{402}{N(N-1)}. \tag{55}
\]
Equations (53) and (55) imply
\[
 \max\{|U_{\mathrm{lo}}(G)-U_N(F)|,
 |U_{\mathrm{hi}}(G)-U_N(F)|\}
 \le\frac{D_N(F,G)+402/N}{N-1}. \tag{56}
\]

If \(n_a/N\to p_a\in(0,1)\), then \(\rho_a=O(N^{-1})\), the coefficients in \(D_N\) are bounded, and (54) gives \(\mathbb ED_N=O(N^{-1/2})+O(N^{-1})\).  For any nonnegative random variable \(X\) and \(r>0\), the elementary inequality \(\mathbb EX\ge r\,\mathbb P\{X>r\}\) follows by restricting the expectation to \(\{X>r\}\).  Applied to \(D_N\), it gives \(D_N=O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1/2})+O(N^{-1})\).  Dividing (53) and (56) by \(N-1\), and retaining the deterministic term \(402/[N(N-1)]\), proves the asserted \(O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-3/2})+O(N^{-2})\) errors for \(L_N(G)\), \(U_{\mathrm{lo}}(G)\), and \(U_{\mathrm{hi}}(G)\).

Finally, for \(\eta\in(0,1)\), apply the preceding elementary inequality with \(X=D_N(F,G)\) and \(r=M_N/\eta\).  Equation (54) gives
\[
 \mathbb P\{D_N(F,G)\le M_N/\eta\}\ge1-\eta. \tag{57}
\]
On this event, (56) places \(U_N(F)\) inside
\[
 \left[
 U_{\mathrm{lo}}(G)-\frac{M_N/\eta+402/N}{N-1},
 U_{\mathrm{hi}}(G)+\frac{M_N/\eta+402/N}{N-1}
 \right].
\]
All quantities in this interval are computable from the observed arm samples, the known arm sizes, the fractional program, and the deterministic rounding output.  This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The sampling result separates armwise observation error from the smaller deterministic realization error and remains valid at ties and nonunique transport optima. \textit{Gap.} Aronow, Green, and Lee (2014) establish consistent two-arm sharp-bound estimators, and Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025) analyze superpopulation fractional plug-ins; neither treats a three-arm realizable endpoint under dependent sampling without replacement. \textit{Consumer.} The finite-sample uncertainty band retains sampling error separately while using \(402/[N(N-1)]\) for the deterministic realization component.

\begin{lemma}[lem:general-ordered-dual-slopes]\label{lem:general-ordered-dual-slopes}
Fix the objects in \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\), restrict to the active set \(J(\widetilde c)\), and put \(z_{js}=\widetilde c_js\).  Every optimal scaled coupling \(q^\star=N\pi^\star\), with positive support \(E\), admits dual potentials \(u_{js}\) satisfying \(\sum_ju_{je_j}\le(\sum_jz_{je_j})^2\) on every cell and equality on \(E\).  For any such potentials, put \(\phi_{js}=z_{js}^2-u_{js}\),
\[
 G_{js}=\left\{-2\sum_{k\in J(\widetilde c)\setminus\{j\}}z_{ke_k}:e\in E,\ e_j=s\right\},
 \qquad I_{js}=[z_{js}+\min G_{js},z_{js}+\max G_{js}].
\]
Then, for every \(g\in G_{js}\) and every atom \(t\) of the same marginal,
\[
 \phi_{jt}\ge\phi_{js}+g(z_{jt}-z_{js}).
\]
If \(z_{js}<z_{jt}\), every \(g\in G_{js}\) and \(h\in G_{jt}\) satisfy
\[
 g\le\frac{\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}}{z_{jt}-z_{js}}\le h.
\]
Consequently the closed intervals \(I_{js}\) are strictly ordered and disjoint within each active arm.  Writing \(L_k=\min(0,\widetilde c_k)\) and \(U_k=\max(0,\widetilde c_k)\), all of them lie in the root interval
\[
 \left[L_j-2\sum_{k\ne j}U_k,\ U_j-2\sum_{k\ne j}L_k\right],
\]
whose length is \(H_j(\widetilde c)\).  If two atom intervals lie in one interval of length \(h\), then \(|z_{jt}-z_{js}|\le h\) and the dual remainder, oriented from the source atom, lies in \([0,h^2]\).  These conclusions include repeated atoms after aggregation, ties, and contacts with half-open grid boundaries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Project the coupling onto its active coordinates and aggregate cells with the same active tuple; this preserves the objective and all active marginals.  We first justify the asserted dual certificate without assuming it.  Enumerate these finite active cells, let \(A\) be their marginal-incidence matrix, let \(b\) be the scaled integer marginal vector, and let \(\kappa_e=(\sum_jz_{je_j})^2\).  Thus \(q^\star\ge0\), \(Aq^\star=b\), and \(q^\star\) minimizes \(\kappa^Tq\).  Consider the polyhedral cone
\[
 \mathcal C=\{A^Tu+s:u\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(A)},\ s_e=0\ (e\in E),\ s_e\ge0\ (e\notin E)\}.
\]
It is closed because it is the sum of a finite-dimensional subspace and a finitely generated cone.  If \(\kappa\notin\mathcal C\), let \(p\) be its Euclidean projection onto \(\mathcal C\) and put \(v=\kappa-p\).  The projection inequalities for a closed convex cone give \(v^Tp=0\) and \(v^Tw\le0\) for all \(w\in\mathcal C\).  Since \(\mathcal C\) contains \(A^Tu\) for both signs of every \(u\), \(Av=0\); since it contains every nonnegative coordinate ray off \(E\), \(v_e\le0\) for \(e\notin E\).  Consequently \(d=-v\) satisfies \(Ad=0\) and \(d_e\ge0\) off \(E\).  For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), \(q^\star+\varepsilon d\ge0\), while
\[
 \kappa^Td=-\kappa^Tv=-(p+v)^Tv=-\|v\|_2^2<0,
\]
contradicting optimality.  Hence \(\kappa=A^Tu+s\) for some \(u,s\) in the displayed cone.  Coordinatewise, \(A^Tu\le\kappa\), with equality on \(E\); moreover \(b^Tu=(q^\star)^TA^Tu=(q^\star)^T\kappa\), so these are optimal dual potentials.

Now fix an active arm \(j\), an atom \(s\), and a support cell \(e\in E\) with \(e_j=s\).  Write
\[
 w=\sum_{k\ne j}z_{ke_k},\qquad g=-2w.
\]
Dual tightness at \(e\) and dual feasibility after replacing only its \(j\)-th coordinate by an atom \(t\) give
\[
 u_{js}+\sum_{k\ne j}u_{ke_k}=(z_{js}+w)^2,
 \qquad
 u_{jt}+\sum_{k\ne j}u_{ke_k}\le(z_{jt}+w)^2.
\]
Subtracting and using \(u_{jr}=z_{jr}^2-\phi_{jr}\) yields
\[
 \phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}\ge-2w(z_{jt}-z_{js})=g(z_{jt}-z_{js}). \tag{1}
\]
Every atom has positive integer marginal multiplicity, so it is incident to the positive support of \(q^\star\); hence each \(G_{js}\) is nonempty.  If \(d=z_{jt}-z_{js}>0\), apply (1) from \(s\) to \(t\) and then from \(t\) to \(s\).  For \(g\in G_{js}\) and \(h\in G_{jt}\),
\[
 gd\le\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}\le hd. \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(g\le(\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js})/d\le h\), so \(\max G_{js}\le\min G_{jt}\), and
\[
 z_{js}+\max G_{js}<z_{jt}+\min G_{jt}. \tag{3}
\]
This proves strict order and disjointness.

Because \(z_{k\cdot}\in[L_k,U_k]\), the sum of the other active coordinates belongs to \([\sum_{k\ne j}L_k,\sum_{k\ne j}U_k]\).  Hence
\[
 G_{js}\subset\left[-2\sum_{k\ne j}U_k,-2\sum_{k\ne j}L_k\right]
\]
and every \(I_{js}\) lies in the claimed root.  Its length is
\[
 (U_j-L_j)+2\sum_{k\ne j}(U_k-L_k)
 =|\widetilde c_j|+2\sum_{k\ne j}|\widetilde c_k|
 =H_j(\widetilde c). \tag{4}
\]

Finally suppose \(I_{js},I_{jt}\) lie in a common interval of length \(h\) and \(z_{js}<z_{jt}\).  Choose \(g\in G_{js}\), \(h'\in G_{jt}\), and set \(m=(\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js})/(z_{jt}-z_{js})\).  By (2), \(g\le m\le h'\), whereas the two points \(z_{js}+g,z_{jt}+h'\) in the common interval give
\[
 (z_{jt}-z_{js})+(h'-g)\le h. \tag{5}
\]
Both nonnegative summands are at most \(h\), and therefore
\[
 |z_{jt}-z_{js}|\le h,\qquad
 0\le\phi_{jt}-\phi_{js}-g(z_{jt}-z_{js})
 =(m-g)(z_{jt}-z_{js})\le h^2. \tag{6}
\]
If the source is the larger atom, reverse the two roles; the same calculation uses the source slope and gives the same bounds.  Aggregation treats repeats as one atom with positive multiplicity.  Equations (1)--(6) are closed inequalities, so boundary contacts do not alter them.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The support-order argument is coefficient-agnostic after scaling each active marginal by its fixed coefficient, and its exact root length is the quantity that controls rounding loss. \textit{Gap.} The existing three-arm lemma does not expose how the slope-root geometry depends on the support of an arbitrary fixed contrast. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the arm-specific lengths used by the general residual repair and quadratic charge.

\begin{lemma}[lem:general-hierarchical-repair]\label{lem:general-hierarchical-repair}
Under \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\), suppose \(r=r(\widetilde c)\ge3\).  Divide the arm-\(j\) root from \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\) at depth \(\ell\) into \(D_\ell=2^\ell\) consecutive half-open cells of width \(h_{j,\ell}=H_j(\widetilde c)2^{-\ell}\), with the last cell right-closed.  Group atom intervals contained in a common cell and make every other interval a singleton; let \(K_{j,\ell}\) be the resulting cluster count.  Apply basic-table support elimination, integer-part freezing, and armwise tree matching exactly as in the three-arm algorithm, now over the \(r\) active arms.  Then
\[
 K_{j,\ell}\le2D_\ell-1,
\]
the \(r\)-arm incidence matrix has \(r-1\) independent row dependencies, and the integer residuals obey
\[
 R_0=0,\qquad
 R_{\ell+1}\le\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}K_{j,\ell+1}-r
 \le2r(2^{\ell+1}-1).
\]
The number \(M_{j,\ell}\) of arm-\(j\) moves whose lowest common cluster has depth \(\ell\) satisfies \(M_{j,\ell}\le R_{\ell+1}\).  The algorithm produces exactly \(N\) rows with the prescribed active marginal multiplicities; inactive arms may be attached by arbitrary occurrence-wise bijections.  For fixed \(K\), rational coefficients, atoms, and input optimal rational coupling, the construction uses exact rational arithmetic and runs in time polynomial in \(N\) and the total input bit length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At depth \(\ell\), at most one nonsingleton cluster lies in each of the \(D_\ell\) cells.  Every interval not contained in a cell contains an interior grid boundary; assign it its leftmost such boundary.  Strictly disjoint closed intervals cannot receive the same boundary.  Therefore
\[
 K_{j,\ell}\le D_\ell+(D_\ell-1)=2D_\ell-1. \tag{7}
\]
The half-open convention with a right-closed last cell includes the root endpoint and treats a boundary contact as a singleton already counted in (7).  Because every coarse boundary is also a finer dyadic boundary, the partitions are nested.  At depth zero every root is one cluster.  Distinct aggregated atoms have distinct \(z\)-coordinates because \(j\) is active.  By the final conclusion of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\), any two atoms in one nonsingleton depth-\(L\) cluster differ by at most \(h_{j,L}\).  A finite \(L\) for which every \(h_{j,L}\) is smaller than the least positive within-arm separation therefore makes every partition singleton.

Fix a depth and let \(A_\ell\) be the zero--one incidence matrix whose rows are all clusters of the \(r\) active arms and whose columns are the currently available cells of the original dual-tight support.  The residual table solves
\[
 A_\ell q=b_\ell, \tag{8}
\]
where \(b_\ell\) is integer because it is the original cluster-multiplicity vector minus already frozen integer counts.  If the positive columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational kernel vector \(d\) supported on them.  Summing all cluster rows of any one arm gives the all-ones row, so \(A_\ell d=0\) implies \(\sum_e d_e=0\).  Thus \(d\) has both signs.  Moving by the largest feasible multiple in one sign preserves (8), preserves nonnegativity, and deletes a positive cell.  Iteration gives a rational basic table.

For each active arm, the sum of that arm's cluster rows is the same all-ones row.  Relative to one reference arm, the \(r-1\) differences of these block sums are linearly independent: in a linear combination, inspection of any nonreference row block forces its coefficient to vanish.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_\ell)
 \le\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}K_{j,\ell}-(r-1). \tag{9}
\]
After freezing \(\lfloor q_e\rfloor\) copies of each positive cell, every residual entry is in \([0,1)\).  Its total \(R_\ell\) is an integer, since it equals the sum of the remaining integer cluster margins in any arm.  If positive, it is strictly smaller than the number of positive residual cells; (9) therefore gives
\[
 R_\ell\le\sum_jK_{j,\ell}-r. \tag{10}
\]
The same inequality is immediate when the residual is zero.  At the root, all \(K_{j,0}=1\), so (9) gives rank at most one; its integer total is entirely frozen and \(R_0=0\).  Applying (7) and (10) at depth \(\ell+1\) gives
\[
 R_{\ell+1}
 \le r(2D_{\ell+1}-1)-r
 =2r(D_{\ell+1}-1)
 =2r(2^{\ell+1}-1). \tag{11}
\]

For the tree repair, fix an active arm and a depth-\(\ell+1\) block \(B\).  Let \(f_B\) be the occurrences already frozen in \(B\), \(r_B\) its residual fractional mass, and \(l_B\) the occurrences selected there at subsequent coarser freezes.  Feasibility and conservation give
\[
 \operatorname{required}(B)=f_B+r_B,\qquad
 \operatorname{selected}(B)=f_B+l_B,\qquad
 \sum_Br_B=\sum_Bl_B=R_{\ell+1}. \tag{12}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac12\sum_B|\operatorname{selected}(B)-\operatorname{required}(B)|
 =\frac12\sum_B|l_B-r_B|\le R_{\ell+1}. \tag{13}
\]
Matching equal atoms at leaves and then opposite signed child imbalances at each parent consumes one positive and one negative discrepancy.  Thus every movement whose lowest common block has depth \(\ell\) is charged to (13), proving
\[
 M_{j,\ell}\le R_{\ell+1}. \tag{14}
\]
The root imbalance is zero, so these matchings are bijections and restore every active marginal exactly.  Inactive coordinates do not enter the cost and their \(N\) occurrences may be placed by arbitrary bijections.

For rational input of total bit length \(B\) and fixed \(K\), every nonzero scaled atom separation is at least \(2^{-p(B)}\) for a polynomial \(p\), so a singleton depth \(L=O(p(B))\) suffices.  Grid indices are computed by exact dyadic comparisons without materializing \(2^L\) cells.  There are at most the explicitly listed support cells in the input, and support elimination is rational Gaussian elimination on an incidence matrix with at most \(rN\) rows.  After denominators are cleared, a kernel vector may be chosen from minors of order at most \(rN\); the Leibniz formula bounds their magnitudes by \((rN)!\), hence their bit lengths by \(O(rN\log(rN))\).  Each maximal feasible update deletes a support cell and adds at most this many denominator bits.  Across at most \(L\) levels and the input support size, the number of eliminations and the accumulated bit length are polynomial in \(N+B\) for fixed \(K\).  Freezing creates at most \(N\) rows, and tree matching makes at most \(O(KNL)\) moves.  Hence all comparisons, vertex extractions, freezes, and matches use exact rational arithmetic in the stated polynomial time.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact rank loss of the active-arm incidence matrix determines the number of fractional rows that survive each freezing level. \textit{Gap.} The three-arm count conceals the general fact that exactly \(r-1\) independent block-sum relations drive the support bound. \textit{Consumer.} Its residual and movement inequalities are the combinatorial input for the arbitrary-support-size upper rate.

\begin{lemma}[lem:general-quadratic-slack-accounting]\label{lem:general-quadratic-slack-accounting}
In the setting and algorithm of \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\), denote the repaired schedule's average cost by \(C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})\).  A selected dual-tight row \(z\), its repaired row \(z'=z+\delta\), and \(g_j=-2\sum_{k\ne j}z_k\) satisfy the exact dual-slack identity
\[
 \left(\sum_jz'_j\right)^2-\sum_ju_j(z'_j)
 =\left(\sum_j\delta_j\right)^2-\sum_j\delta_j^2
 +\sum_j\{\phi_j(z'_j)-\phi_j(z_j)-g_j\delta_j\}.
\]
Every moved arm-\(j\) coordinate whose lowest common cluster has depth \(\ell\) contributes at most \(r h_{j,\ell}^2\) to this upper bound.  Summing over the output rows gives
\[
 N\left\{C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})-
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})\right\}
 \le r\sum_{\ell\ge0}\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}h_{j,\ell}^2R_{\ell+1}
 <\frac{16r^2}{3}\sum_{j\in J(\widetilde c)}H_j(\widetilde c)^2.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(S=\sum_jz_j\) and \(D=\sum_j\delta_j\).  Tightness at \(z\) gives \(S^2=\sum_ju_j(z_j)\), and \(u_j(v)=v^2-\phi_j(v)\).  Direct expansion therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left(\sum_jz'_j\right)^2-\sum_ju_j(z'_j)
 &= (S+D)^2-\sum_j(z_j+\delta_j)^2+\sum_j\phi_j(z'_j)\\
 &=D^2-\sum_j\delta_j^2
 +\sum_j\{\phi_j(z'_j)-\phi_j(z_j)-g_j\delta_j\},
\end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
because \(g_j=-2(S-z_j)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz in \(r\) active coordinates gives
\[
 D^2-\sum_j\delta_j^2\le(r-1)\sum_j\delta_j^2. \tag{16}
\]
If arm \(j\) moves at depth \(\ell\), the source and target intervals lie in a common cell of width \(h_{j,\ell}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\), with the actual source slope \(g_j\), gives
\[
 \delta_j^2\le h_{j,\ell}^2,\qquad
 0\le\phi_j(z'_j)-\phi_j(z_j)-g_j\delta_j\le h_{j,\ell}^2. \tag{17}
\]
Thus (16) and (17) charge at most \(r h_{j,\ell}^2\) to each moved coordinate.

The repair preserves each marginal multiplicity.  If \(b_{js}=NF_j^{(K)}(\{s\})\), dual optimality and tightness imply
\[
 \sum_{\mathrm{rows}}\sum_ju_j(z'_j)
 =\sum_{j,s}b_{js}u_{js}
 =N V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)}). \tag{18}
\]
Summing (15), using (14), and then (11), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N(C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}-V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c})
 &\le r\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\sum_jh_{j,\ell}^2M_{j,\ell}\\
 &\le r\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\sum_jH_j^2\,4^{-\ell}R_{\ell+1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{19}
\]
The finite sum is strictly below its infinite positive extension, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}4^{-\ell}\,2r(2^{\ell+1}-1)
 &=2r\left(2\sum_{\ell\ge0}2^{-\ell}-\sum_{\ell\ge0}4^{-\ell}\right)\\
 &=2r\left(4-\frac43\right)=\frac{16r}{3}.
\end{aligned} \tag{20}
\]
Substitution in (19) proves
\[
 N(C_{\mathrm{alg}}^{K,\widetilde c}-V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c})
 <\frac{16r^2}{3}\sum_jH_j^2.
\]
If no repair is required, the left side is zero and the same conclusion holds.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rank-one quadratic identity turns each coordinate repair into a squared-scale charge and makes the multiscale sum finite for every fixed support size. \textit{Gap.} A generic Lipschitz repair would accumulate a logarithm and would not yield the fixed-contrast \(N^{-1}\) frontier. \textit{Consumer.} It proves the explicit upper constant \((16r^2/3)\sum_jH_j^2\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:affine-obstruction-transfer]\label{lem:affine-obstruction-transfer}
Suppose \(r(\widetilde c)\ge3\) and choose distinct active indices \(j_1,j_2,j_3\).  Let
\[
 \lambda_{\widetilde c}
 =\min\left\{\frac{|\widetilde c_{j_1}|}{109},
 \frac{|\widetilde c_{j_2}|}{116},
 \frac{|\widetilde c_{j_3}|}{207}\right\}>0.
\]
For every \(N\ge4\), affine placement of the three unshifted arrays in \(\mathrm{def{:}lower\mbox{-}family}\) into the coordinate segments between \(0\) and \(\widetilde c_{j_k}\), with every other arm constant, gives empirical laws in the class of \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\) satisfying
\[
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})-
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 =\frac{5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2}{N}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the three augmented unshifted arrays \(A_1,A_2,A_3\) from Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}lower\mbox{-}family}\), including their dummy values.  Their minima, maxima, and spans are
\[
 (m_1,M_1,M_1-m_1)=(-9,100,109),
\]
\[
 (m_2,M_2,M_2-m_2)=(-16,100,116),\qquad
 (m_3,M_3,M_3-m_3)=(-200,7,207). \tag{21}
\]
For \(k=1,2,3\), put \(L_k=\min(0,\widetilde c_{j_k})\) and define the scaled contrast-coordinate occurrences
\[
 z_{j_k,i}=L_k+\lambda_{\widetilde c}(A_{k,i}-m_k),\qquad
 x_{j_k,i}=z_{j_k,i}/\widetilde c_{j_k}. \tag{22}
\]
By (21) and the definition of \(\lambda_{\widetilde c}\), \(z_{j_k,i}\) belongs to the segment with endpoints \(0\) and \(\widetilde c_{j_k}\).  Division by the nonzero coefficient, with order reversed when it is negative, shows \(x_{j_k,i}\in[0,1]\).  Hold every other arm constant, so its scaled coordinate contributes a row-independent constant.

For any feasible coupling, the row contrast is
\[
 B+\lambda_{\widetilde c}(A_{1,i_1}+A_{2,i_2}+A_{3,i_3}), \tag{23}
\]
where \(B\) depends only on the affine shifts and the constant arms.  On summing the square of (23) over \(N\) rows, the term \(NB^2\) is constant, and the cross term is also constant because
\[
 \sum_{\mathrm{rows}}(A_{1,i_1}+A_{2,i_2}+A_{3,i_3})
 =\sum_iA_{1i}+\sum_iA_{2i}+\sum_iA_{3i}
\]
is fixed by the marginals.  Hence the difference between integral and fractional total costs is \(\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2\) times the corresponding unshifted difference.  The explicit dual and primal certificate proved in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}explicit\mbox{-}matching\mbox{-}lower}\) gives unshifted total optima \(55\) and \(60\).  Dividing their transformed difference \(5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2\) by \(N\) proves the claim.  All other active arms being constant cannot affect the difference.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Affine placement transfers the exact three-coordinate obstruction to any three nonzero entries of a fixed contrast without leaving the scalar bounded class. \textit{Gap.} Zero-coordinate embedding alone does not cover arbitrary coefficient magnitudes or signs. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a positive \(c\)-dependent lower constant for every contrast with at least three active arms.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-contrast-support-dichotomy]\label{thm:fixed-contrast-support-dichotomy}
For every fixed \(K\ge1\) and \(\widetilde c\in\mathbb R^K\), let \(r=r(\widetilde c)\), \(J=J(\widetilde c)\), and \(H_j=H_j(\widetilde c)\).  If \(r\le2\), then
\[
 \Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}=0
\]
for every \(N\ge2\).  If \(r\ge3\), then for every \(N\ge2\),
\[
 \Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}
 <\frac{16r^2}{3N}\sum_{j\in J}H_j^2,
\]
and, for every \(N\ge4\) and any distinct \(j_1,j_2,j_3\in J\),
\[
 \frac{5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2}{N}
 \le\Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}
 <\frac{16r^2}{3N}\sum_{j\in J}H_j^2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\widetilde c}=\min\left\{
 \frac{|\widetilde c_{j_1}|}{109},
 \frac{|\widetilde c_{j_2}|}{116},
 \frac{|\widetilde c_{j_3}|}{207}\right\}.
\]
Thus the support-size dichotomy is exact: zero realization loss for at most two active arms and \(\Theta_{\widetilde c}(N^{-1})\) loss for every fixed contrast with at least three active arms, without an arithmetic exceptional subsequence.  The upper bound is attained as a guarantee by the deterministic multiscale algorithm of Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\); for fixed \(K\) and rational input it is exact-rational and polynomial in \(N\) and the input bit length.  For \(K=3\) and \(\widetilde c=(1/4,1/4,-1/2)\), \((H_1,H_2,H_3)=(7/4,7/4,3/2)\), so the upper constant is exactly \(402\), while \(\lambda_{\widetilde c}=1/464\) gives the incidental lower improvement \(5/(215296N)\).  This theorem concerns scalar rank-one squared-contrast cost; it makes no achievability claim for vector-valued outcomes or general quadratic-form costs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The cost depends only on coordinates in \(J\).  Every full coupling projects to a coupling of the active marginals.  Conversely, any active coupling extends to all \(K\) coordinates by adjoining the inactive marginal laws independently, so the fractional optimum equals the active-coordinate optimum.  Likewise, any active \(N\)-row schedule extends to the inactive empirical marginals by arbitrary occurrence-wise bijections.  It is therefore enough in every case to work on the \(r\) active coordinates.

First let \(r=0\).  The cost is identically zero, so both values in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}general\mbox{-}contrast\mbox{-}frontier}\) vanish.  If \(r=1\), the cost depends on one marginal only, and its expectation is fixed by that marginal; again the two values coincide.

Let \(r=2\).  Aggregate repeated active-arm atoms and scale a fractional coupling by \(N\), so its row and column sums are integer multiplicities.  Choose an optimal table with the fewest positive cells.  Its bipartite positive-support graph is acyclic.  Indeed, on any support cycle the alternating perturbation preserves every margin.  If its objective directional derivative is nonzero, one of the two directions decreases cost until a cell vanishes; if it is zero, either direction preserves cost until a cell vanishes.  Either case contradicts minimal positive support.  Thus the support is a forest.  A leaf edge has weight equal to its incident integer margin.  Removing that edge and vertex leaves integer margins, so induction over each tree proves every positive weight is integer.  Expanding an integer weight into that many rows gives an \(N\)-row optimal schedule.  Inactive-arm occurrences can be attached arbitrarily, since they do not enter the cost.  Therefore
\[
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 =V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
\]
for every marginal tuple, proving \(\Delta_{N,K,\widetilde c}=0\) when \(r\le2\).

Now suppose \(r\ge3\).  The finite coupling polytope is nonempty and compact, so a primal optimum exists.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}ordered\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}slopes}\) derives a tight dual certificate directly from its optimality, and Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\) then construct a realizable schedule with
\[
 V_{\mathrm{int}}^{N,K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})-
 V_{\mathrm{rel}}^{K,\widetilde c}(F^{(K)})
 <\frac{16r^2}{3N}\sum_{j\in J}H_j^2. \tag{24}
\]
Parameterize the empirical marginals by the compact cube [0,1]\textasciicircum{}{K times N}.  The integral value is the minimum of finitely many continuous schedule costs.  Using labeled occurrences, the relaxed value is the minimum of a jointly continuous objective over the fixed compact multi-stochastic coupling polytope, hence is continuous.  Therefore the gap is continuous on a compact parameter space and attains its supremum.  Applying (24) at a maximizing tuple proves the displayed strict upper bound for the frontier.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}affine\mbox{-}obstruction\mbox{-}transfer}\) supplies, for every \(N\ge4\), a member of the same \(K\)-arm scalar class whose gap is exactly \(5\lambda_{\widetilde c}^2/N\).  Since all three selected coefficients are nonzero, \(\lambda_{\widetilde c}>0\).  This proves both the lower bound and \(\Theta_{\widetilde c}(N^{-1})\) on every integer \(N\ge4\), so no arithmetic subsequence is excluded.

For the stated specialization, \(\|\widetilde c\|_1=1\), and hence
\[
 H_1=H_2=2-\frac14=\frac74,\qquad H_3=2-\frac12=\frac32.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{16r^2}{3}\sum_{j=1}^3H_j^2
 =48\left\{2\left(\frac74\right)^2+\left(\frac32\right)^2\right\}
 =48\cdot\frac{67}{8}=402. \tag{25}
\]
The three span ratios are \(1/436,1/464,1/414\), so their minimum is \(1/464\) and the transferred gap is
\[
 \frac{5}{464^2N}=\frac{5}{215296N}. \tag{26}
\]
The algorithmic claim is exactly the complexity conclusion of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}hierarchical\mbox{-}repair}\), together with the exact arithmetic slack calculation of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}general\mbox{-}quadratic\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}accounting}\).  Nothing in those arguments supplies coordinatewise vector geometry or replaces the rank-one cost by a general quadratic form, proving the stated scope boundary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result identifies the exact support-size phase transition for same-denominator scalar rank-one transport: integrality through two active marginals and order \(N^{-1}\) thereafter. \textit{Gap.} Prior finite-support transport and mixability results do not give a uniform same-empirical-marginal rate simultaneously for every fixed number of arms and every fixed coefficient vector. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a reusable transport theorem while leaving the note's causal design and endpoint application at the original three-arm contrast.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The coefficient-dependent upper constant \((16r^2/3)\sum_jH_j^2\) is universal but deliberately conservative and can grow rapidly with the active support size.  Even at the original contrast, \(402/N\) improves on the trivial \(1/4\) cost-gap bound only at \(N=1609\), so ordinary-size trials should report the actual primal--dual width.  The exact normalized constant and extremal geometry remain open; the new affine transfer only improves the original lower localization to \(5/215296\) for the fixed three-arm contrast.  Plug-in sampling error dominates deterministic rounding error under proportional allocation.  The proof uses scalar ordering and rank-one squared-sum algebra and does not establish achievability for arbitrary vector dimension \(d\) or a general quadratic matrix \(A\).

\section{Honest scope}

The note proves the complete support-size dichotomy for every fixed scalar rank-one contrast: exact zero loss for at most two active arms and \(\Theta_{\widetilde c}(N^{-1})\) for at least three, with explicit coefficient-dependent constants, an all-\(N\ge4\) lower family, and an exact-rational polynomial construction for fixed \(K\).  Its causal claim remains narrower: the finite-population variance translation and plug-in analysis concern complete randomization with the original three arms and \(c=(1/4,1/4,-1/2)\).  The result is an operational same-science-table correction to a fractional functional, not a contradiction of a superpopulation identified-set theorem.  It does not claim that one experiment observes all full marginals, that every interior variance is attainable, that the unknown actual completion has variance at least \(U_{\mathrm{lo}}(F)\), or that the upper construction covers arbitrary \(d\) and general quadratic \(A\).  The exact value of \(\Delta_3\) and the limit of \(N\Delta_N\) remain outside the proved result; the two-row realization loss for the original contrast is exactly zero.
