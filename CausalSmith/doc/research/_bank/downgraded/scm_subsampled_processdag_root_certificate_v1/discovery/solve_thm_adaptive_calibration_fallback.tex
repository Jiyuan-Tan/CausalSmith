%%% FIELD proof-global-honest-inversion
Fix an arbitrary data-generating law \(P_\star\) in
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cmp}}(d,m,G;\gamma,L,d_{\min},d_{\max})\), let
\((A_\star,D_\star)\) be its fine-grid parameters, and let
\((M_{0\star},M_{1\star})\) be its stationary coarse lag-zero and lag-one
moments.  Define
\[
 E_n:=\{(M_{0\star},M_{1\star})\in\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha)\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}compact\mbox{-}class}\), \(A_\star\) is lower
triangular and
\(\max_{i\in V}|(A_\star)_{ii}|\leq 1-\gamma\).  Therefore
\[
 \det(\lambda I_d-A_\star)
 =\prod_{i\in V}\{\lambda-(A_\star)_{ii}\},
 \qquad
 \rho(A_\star)=\max_{i\in V}|(A_\star)_{ii}|
 \leq 1-\gamma<1.
\]
Thus the stability premise of
\(\mathrm{lem{:}coarse\mbox{-}law\mbox{-}reduction}\) is satisfied.
That lemma and the definition of the coarse innovation covariance give
\[
 M_{0\star}\succ0,
 \qquad
 M_{1\star}=A_\star^mM_{0\star},
\]
and
\[
 M_{0\star}
 =A_\star^mM_{0\star}(A_\star^m)^\top
  +\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A_\star^rD_\star(A_\star^r)^\top.
\]
The membership of \(P_\star\) in the compact class separately supplies
the lower-triangular and graph-support zero restrictions, diagonality of
\(D_\star\), and the inequalities
\[
 \max_{i\in V}|(A_\star)_{ii}|\leq1-\gamma,
 \qquad
 \max_{i,j\in V}|(A_\star)_{ij}|\leq L,
 \qquad
 d_{\min}I_d\preceq D_\star\preceq d_{\max}I_d.
\]
Consequently, on \(E_n\), every constraint in
\(\mathrm{def{:}inverse\mbox{-}region}\) is satisfied by
\((A_\star,D_\star)\), with existential witnesses
\((M_0,M_1)=(M_{0\star},M_{1\star})\).  Hence
\[
 E_n\subseteq\{(A_\star,D_\star)\in\mathcal R_n\}.
\]
For every \(z\in\mathbb T\), the stability margin yields
\[
 |1-(A_\star)_{vv}z|
 \geq 1-|(A_\star)_{vv}|\,|z|
 \geq\gamma>0.
\]
Thus the causal transfer
\(h_{uv,\star}(z)=(A_\star)_{vu}z/
\{1-(A_\star)_{vv}z\}\) is well defined.  By
\(\mathrm{def{:}transfer\mbox{-}confidence\mbox{-}set}\), the preceding
membership in \(\mathcal R_n\) implies
\(h_{uv,\star}\in\mathcal C_n^h\).  It follows that
\[
 P_\star\{h_{uv,\star}\in\mathcal C_n^h\}
 \geq P_\star(E_n)
 \geq1-\alpha,
\]
where the last inequality is
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}box}\).  Since
\(P_\star\) was arbitrary, the coverage statement is uniform over the
compact class.  This inclusion argument imposes all stationary moment
equations jointly and neither selects a matrix-root branch nor divides by a
divided difference.

For the final assertion, suppose that two compact-class members
\((A^{(1)},D^{(1)})\) and \((A^{(2)},D^{(2)})\) induce the same stationary
coarse moment pair \((M_0,M_1)\), while their queried transfers
\(h_{uv}^{(1)}\) and \(h_{uv}^{(2)}\) are unequal.  On the event
\[
 E_n^\circ:=\{(M_0,M_1)\in\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha)\},
\]
the coarse-law identities and the compact-class restrictions, applied
separately for \(k\in\{1,2\}\), show that
\[
 (A^{(k)},D^{(k)})\in\mathcal R_n.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \{h_{uv}^{(1)},h_{uv}^{(2)}\}\subseteq\mathcal C_n^h
 \quad\text{on }E_n^\circ
\]
by \(\mathrm{def{:}transfer\mbox{-}confidence\mbox{-}set}\).  Both
elements are attained by compatible compact-class models, rather than being
mere limiting values, which proves the asserted possible noncontraction.

%%% FIELD proof-adaptive-calibration-fallback
Fix \(n\geq3\) and a law \(P\) in
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cmp}}(d,m,G;\gamma,L,d_{\min},d_{\max})\).  On the
trajectory sample space \(\Omega_n=\mathbb R^{(n+1)d}\), equipped with its
Borel \(\sigma\)-field, define
\[
 \widehat K_{\Sigma,n}:\Omega_n\longrightarrow(0,\infty),
 \qquad
 \widehat K_{\Sigma,n}(w):=K_\Sigma.
\]
This function is measurable: the inverse image of a Borel set is
\(\Omega_n\) when that set contains \(K_\Sigma\), and is empty otherwise.

Write \(\Sigma_{n,P}=\operatorname{Cov}_P(W_n)\).  The explicit uniform
coarse spectral-density resolvent bound used in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}box}\) gives
\[
 0\preceq\Sigma_{n,P}\preceq K_\Sigma I_{(n+1)d},
 \qquad
 \|\Sigma_{n,P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq K_\Sigma=\widehat K_{\Sigma,n}(W_n)
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Thus the proposed constant function is a valid covariance envelope for
every member of the compact class.

It is compatible with the spectral split in
\(\mathrm{def{:}adaptive\mbox{-}calibration\mbox{-}handle}\).  Indeed,
for any symmetric lag-moment quadratic-form matrix, write
\(H_{\ell ij}=H_{\ell ij}^{+}-H_{\ell ij}^{-}\), where
\(H_{\ell ij}^{+},H_{\ell ij}^{-}\succeq0\) are its positive and negative
spectral parts.  For \(s\in\{+,-\}\), covariance domination and cyclicity
of trace imply
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(H_{\ell ij}^{s}\Sigma_{n,P})
 =\operatorname{tr}\!\left((H_{\ell ij}^{s})^{1/2}
 \Sigma_{n,P}(H_{\ell ij}^{s})^{1/2}\right)
 \leq K_\Sigma\operatorname{tr}(H_{\ell ij}^{s}),
\]
while submultiplicativity gives
\[
 \bigl\|\Sigma_{n,P}^{1/2}H_{\ell ij}^{s}
 \Sigma_{n,P}^{1/2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq K_\Sigma\|H_{\ell ij}^{s}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Hence both spectral parts are controlled by the constant envelope, and
their recombination recovers the deterministic two-sided calibration
certified in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}box}\).

For every realized trajectory, now set
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^M(\alpha):=\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n:=\mathcal R_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^h:=\mathcal C_n^h.
\]
For each \((\ell,i,j)\in\{0,1\}\times V\times V\), the associated
coordinate interval is therefore exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Out}_N\!\left((\widehat M_\ell)_{ij},
 2K_\Sigma\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(4d^2/\alpha)}{n}}
 +\frac{\log(4d^2/\alpha)}{n}\right\}\right),
\]
which is the deterministic-\(K_\Sigma\) interval of
\(\mathrm{def{:}moment\mbox{-}box}\).  Thus each fallback interval has
exactly, and therefore no more than, the width of its deterministic
counterpart at every sample point.  In particular,
\[
 P\!\left\{\text{all fallback coordinate widths are no larger than the
 deterministic-\(K_\Sigma\) widths}\right\}=1.
\]
Moreover, equality of the moment regions and
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}box}\) imply
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cmp}}}
 P\{(M_0,M_1)\in\widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^M(\alpha)\}
 =
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cmp}}}
 P\{(M_0,M_1)\in\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha)\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
\]
Every endpoint is rational by the defining property of
\(\operatorname{Out}_N\).  Finally,
\(\widetilde{\mathcal R}_n=\mathcal R_n\) leaves every equality and
inequality in \(\mathrm{def{:}inverse\mbox{-}region}\) unchanged, and
\(\widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^h=\mathcal C_n^h\) leaves its transfer
projection unchanged.  The rational encoding and exact branch-free
semialgebraic inverse are consequently preserved.  By
\(\mathrm{thm{:}global\mbox{-}honest\mbox{-}inversion}\), the same
equalities also preserve finite-sample transfer coverage.  Since all width
comparisons are equalities, the construction establishes no strict adaptive
improvement.

%%% FIELD statement-adaptive-calibration-resolution
The adaptive-calibration question has an affirmative answer in the literal
weak \emph{no wider} sense.  For every \(n\geq3\), choose in
\(\mathrm{CalHandle}\) the measurable constant covariance envelope
\(\widehat K_{\Sigma,n}\equiv K_\Sigma\), and set
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^M(\alpha)=\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n=\mathcal R_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^h=\mathcal C_n^h.
\]
Then, under every compact-class law, every coordinate interval has exactly
the deterministic-\(K_\Sigma\) width with probability one for every
\(n\).  Thus the coordinatewise no-wider probability tends to one on every
interior regular law.  The moment region retains uniform finite-sample
coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), rational outward rounding, and the exact
branch-free semialgebraic inverse.  This resolution does not assert a
strict adaptive improvement.

%%% FIELD proof-adaptive-calibration-resolution
For every \(n\geq3\), apply
\(\mathrm{thm{:}adaptive\mbox{-}calibration\mbox{-}fallback}\).  That
theorem supplies the measurable choice
\(\widehat K_{\Sigma,n}\equiv K_\Sigma\) and establishes, sample by
sample,
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^M(\alpha)=\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n=\mathcal R_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^h=\mathcal C_n^h.
\]
The first equality makes every coordinate width equal to its
deterministic-\(K_\Sigma\) counterpart.  Hence the coordinatewise no-wider
event is the entire trajectory sample space, so its probability is one
under every compact-class law for every \(n\).  A sequence identically
equal to one converges to one, in particular on each interior regular law.
The same fallback theorem supplies the uniform finite-sample coverage,
rational outward rounding, and preservation of exact branch-free
semialgebraic inversion.  Its equality-of-widths construction yields no
strict improvement, as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-adaptive-calibration-literal-answer
Apply \(\mathrm{thm{:}adaptive\mbox{-}calibration\mbox{-}fallback}\) for
each \(n\geq3\).  It supplies the measurable constant envelope
\(\widehat K_{\Sigma,n}\equiv K_\Sigma\) and proves, for every realized
trajectory,
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^M(\alpha)=\mathcal C_n^M(\alpha),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n=\mathcal R_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal C}_n^h=\mathcal C_n^h.
\]
The first equality makes every coordinate interval exactly as wide as its
deterministic-\(K_\Sigma\) counterpart.  Therefore the coordinatewise
no-wider event is the whole trajectory sample space and has probability one
under every compact-class law for every \(n\).  For each interior regular
law belonging to that class, this probability sequence is consequently
identically one and converges to one.  The fallback theorem also gives
uniform finite-sample coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), rational outward
rounding, and preservation of the exact branch-free semialgebraic inverse.
Because all width comparisons are equalities, no strict adaptive
improvement has been established.
