 E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m,(0)}=\{u\to v\},

 E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}=\bigcup_{r\ge0}E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m,(r)},

 \mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{ii}=B_{ii}^{1/m},

using the unique real odd root.  Process the edges \(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) in increasing order of \(i-j\), set graph-forbidden entries to zero, and put

 \mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{ij}
 =\frac{B_{ij}-\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}
 \bigl(\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)\bigr)}

 \mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{jj}\right)}.

The map is defined exactly when every displayed denominator is nonzero.  Its queried transfer is
\[
 \Phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)(z)
 =\frac{\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{vu}z}
 {1-\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{vv}z}.

Assume \(u\to v\in G\).  The increasing sequence defining \(E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) stabilizes after at most \(d(d-1)/2\) strict additions.  At its fixed point, every allowed off-diagonal variable occurring in a nonzero monomial of \(\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}\), for \(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\), belongs to \(E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) and has endpoint gap strictly smaller than \(i-j\). Write \(F_r=E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m,(r)}\).  By construction,
\[
 F_r\subseteq\mathfrak D_m(F_r)=F_{r+1}\subseteq E.
\tag{1}
\]
The supplied order makes every edge an ordered pair \(j\to i\) with \(j<i\), so \(E\) has at most \(d(d-1)/2\) elements.  Whenever \(F_{r+1}\ne F_r\), at least one new edge is added.  Thus there is an index \(r_*\le d(d-1)/2\) for which \(F_{r_*+1}=F_{r_*}\); monotonicity in \((1)\) then gives \(F_r=F_{r_*}\) for every \(r\ge r_*\).  This proves termination and
\[
 E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}=F_{r_*}=\mathfrak D_m(F_{r_*}).
\tag{2}
\]
The fixed-point equality \((2)\) is exactly the assertion that every allowed off-diagonal variable appearing in any retained remainder is retained.

We prove the corridor inclusion by induction on \(r\).  The seed \(u\to v\) lies on the one-edge directed path from \(u\) to \(v\), hence \(F_0\subseteq E_{uv}^{G}\).  Suppose \(F_r\subseteq E_{uv}^{G}\), fix \(j\to i\in F_r\), and let \(k\to\ell\) be newly added because \(A_{\ell k}\) occurs in a nonzero graph-supported monomial of \(\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}path\mbox{-}expansion}\), the strict edges of that monomial form a directed path from \(j\) to \(i\), the edge \(k\to\ell\) lies on that path, and its endpoint gap satisfies
\[
 \ell-k<i-j.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{G}\), there are directed paths from \(u\) to \(j\) and from \(i\) to \(v\).  Concatenating the former, the monomial path from \(j\) to \(i\), and the latter produces a directed \(u\)-to-\(v\) path containing \(k\to\ell\).  Hence \(k\to\ell\in E_{uv}^{G}\).  This proves \(F_{r+1}\subseteq E_{uv}^{G}\), and finite induction together with \((2)\) gives
\[
 E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\subseteq E_{uv}^{G}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every vertex in \(V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) is incident to an edge on the left of \((4)\), so it lies on the corresponding directed \(u\)-to-\(v\) path.  Thus \(V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\subseteq V_{uv}^{G}\).  Equation \((3)\), applied at the fixed point, proves the smaller-gap assertion.  It also shows that increasing endpoint gap is a valid topological order for the recursion.

It remains to verify strictness.  In the stated four-vertex graph, direct multiplication gives
\[
 (A^2)_{41}
 =A_{41}(A_{44}+A_{11})+A_{42}A_{21}+A_{43}A_{31}.
\tag{5}
\]
The edges \(2\to4\) and \(1\to3\) are absent, so graph support sets \(A_{42}=A_{31}=0\).  Consequently
\[
 \overline R^{G}_{41,2}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
Starting from \(F_0=\{1\to4\}\), equation \((6)\) adds no edge, and hence \(E_{14}^{\mathrm{alg},2}=\{1\to4\}\).  On the other hand, the direct edge and the chordless path \(1\to2\to3\to4\) are both directed paths from \(1\) to \(4\), so all four displayed edges belong to \(E_{14}^{G}\).  This proves the claimed strict inclusion without assigning any numerical coefficient a special value.

Fix arbitrary compatible pairs
\[
 (A,D),(\widetilde A,\widetilde D)\in\mathcal S(B,Q;G).
\tag{1}
\]
The triangular restrictions in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}fiber}\) imply that the diagonal entry of a matrix power is the corresponding power of the diagonal entry.  Since both pairs in \((1)\) satisfy the same equation \(A^m=B\), for every \(i\in V\),
\[
 B_{ii}=A_{ii}^m=\widetilde A_{ii}^m.
\tag{2}
\]
The map \(x\mapsto x^m\) is injective on \(\mathbb R\) when \(m\) is odd.  Therefore
\[
 A_{ii}=\widetilde A_{ii}=B_{ii}^{1/m}.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, all retained diagonal coordinates agree with the diagonal step of \(\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)\).

We now induct over the positive endpoint gap \(g=i-j\) among edges in \(E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\).  Assume that every retained edge of gap less than \(g\) has a common value under \(A\) and \(\widetilde A\), equal to the algebraic recursion.  For \(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) with \(i-j=g\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}path\mbox{-}expansion}\), after graph-forbidden variables are set to zero, gives
\[
 B_{ij}
 =A_{ij}C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})+\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}(A),
\tag{4}
\]
and the same equality with \(A\) replaced by \(\widetilde A\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}algebraic\mbox{-}dependency\mbox{-}closure}\), every off-diagonal variable in the remainder belongs to \(E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) and has gap less than \(g\); every diagonal variable is incident to one of those retained edges and hence belongs to \(V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\).  The induction hypothesis and \((3)\) therefore yield
\[
 \overline R^{G}_{ij,m}(A)
 =\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}(\widetilde A).
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, \((3)\) and the displayed premise of the theorem give
\[
 C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})
 =C_m(B_{ii}^{1/m},B_{jj}^{1/m})\ne0.
\tag{6}
\]
Subtracting the common remainder in \((5)\) from \((4)\) and dividing by the nonzero quantity in \((6)\) gives
\[
 A_{ij}=\widetilde A_{ij}
 =\frac{B_{ij}-\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}(A)}
 {C_m(B_{ii}^{1/m},B_{jj}^{1/m})}.
\tag{7}
\]
This is exactly the next algebraic-recursion update.  The closure is finite by \(\mathrm{lem{:}algebraic\mbox{-}dependency\mbox{-}closure}\), so finite induction proves all asserted coordinate equalities.

The seed belongs to the closure, so \(u\to v\in E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) and \(v\in V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\).  Equations \((3)\) and \((7)\) identify \(A_{vv}\) and \(A_{vu}\), respectively.  Thus every pair in the nonempty compatible fiber has transfer
\[
 h_{uv}(z)
 =\frac{A_{vu}z}{1-A_{vv}z}
 =\frac{\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{vu}z}
 {1-\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)_{vv}z}
 =\Phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)(z).
\tag{8}
\]
Compatibility supplies \(|A_{vv}|<1\).  Hence, for every \(|z|=1\),
\[
 |1-A_{vv}z|\ge1-|A_{vv}|>0,
\tag{9}
\]
so the rational function in \((8)\) is defined on \(\mathbb T\).  By \(\mathrm{def{:}transfer\mbox{-}fiber}\), equality \((8)\) for every compatible pair proves
\(\mathcal H_{uv}(B,Q;G)=\{\Phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)\}\).  Neither the induction nor the transfer calculation compares any coordinate outside the algebraic dependency closure or any entry of \(D\), so no broader uniqueness follows.

to the long-run Gaussian lag-moment covariance.  Let \(\widehat\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}\) be the positive-semidefinite plug-in estimate obtained from the Gaussian Isserlis covariance series and this algebraic-dependency Jacobian, and let \(\widehat c_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{alg}}\) be the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of the maximum complex modulus of the corresponding centered Gaussian vector.  Then

where the class in the supremum has the full argument list displayed above.  No root-derivative or divided-difference separation is imposed outside \(V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\) and \(E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\), and no full-\(A\) uniqueness is asserted.

Let

 \mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}
 =\mathcal M_{uv,\mathrm{alg}}^{\partial}
 (d,m,G;\gamma,L,d_{\min},d_{\max},\kappa).
\]
We first prove that this class is nonempty.  Because \(m\ge2\) is odd, \(m\ge3\).  Set
\[
 a=\left(\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{1/(m-1)}.
\tag{1}
\]
The feasibility condition gives \(0<a\le1-\gamma<1\).  Take \(A=aI_d\), take \(D=d_{\min}I_d\), and generate the stationary Gaussian fine and coarse processes from these parameters.  All off-diagonal coefficients vanish, so the lower-triangular and graph-support restrictions hold, and \(\rho(A)=a<1\).  Also \(|A_{ij}|\le1\le L\) and
\[
 d_{\min}I_d\preceq D=d_{\min}I_d\preceq d_{\max}I_d.
\tag{2}
\]
For every retained vertex and edge,
\[
 m|A_{ii}|^{m-1}=ma^{m-1}=\kappa,
 \qquad
 C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})=C_m(a,a)=ma^{m-1}=\kappa.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the model belongs to \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}\), proving nonemptiness.  Conversely, since the seed puts \(u\in V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\), membership in the class would imply
\[
 \kappa\le m|A_{uu}|^{m-1}\le m(1-\gamma)^{m-1},
\tag{4}
\]
so the stated feasibility range is also necessary.

The coefficient set underlying \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}\) is a closed subset of the finite-dimensional compact set in \(\mathrm{def{:}compact\mbox{-}class}\): graph zeros and the coefficient and innovation-eigenvalue bounds are closed, and the finitely many inequalities in \((3)\) are continuous polynomial inequalities.  Therefore it is compact.  For every member, triangularity and the stability margin give \(\rho(A)=\max_i|A_{ii}|\le1-\gamma<1\), so the stationary covariance is
\[
 M_0=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}A^rD(A^r)^\top.
\tag{5}
\]
The first term and the innovation lower bound imply \(M_0\succeq d_{\min}I_d\).  The series in \((5)\) is a continuous function of \((A,D)\) on this compact uniformly stable triangular set; hence there is a finite constant \(C_0=C_0(d,L,\gamma,d_{\max})\) such that
\[
 d_{\min}I_d\preceq M_0\preceq C_0I_d
\tag{6}
\]
uniformly.  Consequently the map
\[
 (M_0,M_1)\longmapsto B=M_1M_0^{-1}
\tag{7}
\]
is twice continuously differentiable with uniformly bounded first and second derivatives on a common neighborhood of the population moments.

For each \(i\in V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\), triangularity gives \(B_{ii}=A_{ii}^m\), and class membership gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\partial B_{ii}}{\partial A_{ii}}\right|
 =m|A_{ii}|^{m-1}\ge\kappa.
\tag{8}
\]
Oddness makes the real root unique.  On the class,
\[
 \frac{\partial A_{ii}}{\partial B_{ii}}
 =\frac{1}{mA_{ii}^{m-1}},
 \qquad
 \frac{\partial^2 A_{ii}}{\partial B_{ii}^2}
 =-\frac{m-1}{m^2A_{ii}^{2m-1}},
\tag{9}
\]
and \((8)\) implies \(|A_{ii}|\ge(\kappa/m)^{1/(m-1)}>0\), so both derivatives in \((9)\) are uniformly bounded.  For a retained edge, \(\mathrm{thm{:}algebraic\mbox{-}dependency\mbox{-}identification}\) gives
\[
 A_{ij}
 =\frac{B_{ij}-\overline R^{G}_{ij,m}(A)}{C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})},
 \qquad
 |C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})|\ge\kappa.
\tag{10}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}algebraic\mbox{-}dependency\mbox{-}closure}\), the polynomial remainder in \((10)\) uses only retained diagonals and retained edges of smaller gap.  Starting from \((9)\) and inducting over the finitely many gaps, each coordinate recovered by \(\mathscr R^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}\) is twice continuously differentiable in \(B\).  Compactness and continuity give a common neighborhood of the population \(B\)'s on which every divisor in \((8)\)--\((10)\) has absolute value at least \(\kappa/2\), and all first and second derivatives of the algebraic recursion are uniformly bounded.  No coordinate outside the algebraic closure enters this induction.

For every \(z\in\mathcal Z\subseteq\mathbb T\), the stability margin yields
\[
 |1-A_{vv}z|\ge1-|A_{vv}|\ge\gamma.
\tag{11}

Thus the transfer map is twice continuously differentiable on the same common neighborhood, and direct differentiation gives

 \mathrm dh_{uv}(z)
 =\frac{z\,\mathrm dA_{vu}}{1-A_{vv}z}

\[
 \vartheta=\operatorname{vec}_{s}(M_0,M_1),
 \qquad
 \widehat\vartheta=\operatorname{vec}_{s}(\widehat M_0,\widehat M_1),

and let \(F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)\) stack the real and imaginary parts of \((h_{uv}(z))_{z\in\mathcal Z}\).  Let \(J_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)\) be the Jacobian of the composite of \((7)\), the algebraic recursion, and \((12)\).  The common second-derivative bound gives

 F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\widehat\vartheta)-F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)
 =J_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)(\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta)+r_n,
 \qquad
 \|r_n\|\le C\|\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta\|^2
\tag{13}

whenever \(\widehat\vartheta\) lies in the common neighborhood.

The class \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}\) is contained in the compact class.  Therefore \(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\mbox{-}compact\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}lag\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}clt}\) supplies uniform tightness and

 \sqrt n(\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta)
 \Longrightarrow N(0,\Omega(P)),
 \qquad
 \Omega(P)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Cov}_P(g_0,g_k),

\]
uniformly over \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}\).  Uniform tightness makes the neighborhood event have probability tending to one uniformly.  For any \(\epsilon>0\), on
\(\sqrt n\|\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta\|\le K\), equation \((13)\) gives \(\sqrt n\|r_n\|\le CK^2/\sqrt n\), while uniform tightness makes the complementary probability uniformly small for large \(K\).  Hence

 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}}
 P\!\left(\sqrt n\|r_n\|>\epsilon\right)\longrightarrow0.

\[
 \sqrt n\{F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\widehat\vartheta)-F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)\}
 \Longrightarrow N(0,\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(P)),

 \Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(P)
 =J_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)\Omega(P)J_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(\vartheta)^\top.
\tag{16}

 \widehat J_{h,\mathrm{alg}}-J_{h,\mathrm{alg}}=o_{\mathbb P}(1),

 \widehat\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}

 =\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(P)+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
\tag{17}

uniformly.  The estimator is positive semidefinite because \(\widehat\Omega\succeq0\).

 \widetilde M_0=n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{n}Y_{s-1}Y_{s-1}^\top.

 \widehat M_1

uniformly.  The summands are martingale differences because \(\eta_s\) is independent of the preceding coarse history.  Their covariance, in a compatible vectorization order, is

 \Gamma_B=M_0^{-1}\otimes Q.

 \qquad c_B=d_{\min}/C_0>0.

 =\frac{1}{C_m(A_{vv},A_{uu})},
 \qquad
 \frac{\partial A_{vv}}{\partial B_{vu}}=0.

Since \(|A_{ii}|\le1-\gamma<1\) and \(|z|=1\),

 |C_m(A_{vv},A_{uu})|\le m,

 \left|\frac{\partial h_{uv}(z)}{\partial B_{vu}}\right|

 \mathbb E_P|Z_h(z)|^2\ge\frac{c_B}{4m^2}>0,

where \(Z_h\) is the Gaussian vector in \((16)\).  The covariance may change rank but is never the zero matrix, even when \(A_{vu}=0\).

For a real vector arranged in complex-coordinate pairs, define
\[
 \|x\|_{\mathcal Z,\infty}
 =\max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sqrt{x_{\Re,z}^2+x_{\Im,z}^2}.
\]
For \(\Sigma\succeq0\), let \(Z_\Sigma\sim N(0,\Sigma)\) and set
\[
 c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma)
 =\inf\{t:\Pr(\|Z_\Sigma\|_{\mathcal Z,\infty}\le t)\ge1-\alpha\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The confidence-level assumption gives \(0<1-\alpha<1\).  On the support subspace of a nonzero centered Gaussian vector, the restriction of \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal Z,\infty}\) is a norm and the Gaussian law has a positive density.  Norm spheres have Lebesgue measure zero on that subspace, and every nonempty norm annulus has positive measure.  Therefore the distribution function in \((26)\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), with a unique positive \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile.

If \(\Sigma_j\to\Sigma\ne0\), couple the Gaussian vectors with one \(\xi\sim N(0,I)\):
\[
 Z_{\Sigma_j}=\Sigma_j^{1/2}\xi
 \longrightarrow \Sigma^{1/2}\xi=Z_\Sigma
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Convergence of distribution functions at two continuity points bracketing the unique quantile shows
\[
 c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma_j)\longrightarrow c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma).
\tag{27}
\]
The covariance range in \((16)\) is compact by compactness of the parameter class and continuity of all ingredients, and \((25)\) keeps it away from zero.  Hence \((27)\) is uniform on a neighborhood of that range.  Defining
\[
 \widehat c_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{alg}}
 =c_{1-\alpha}(\widehat\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}),
\]
equations \((17)\) and \((27)\) yield
\[
 \widehat c_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{alg}}
 =c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(P))+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
\tag{28}
\]
uniformly.

Finally, suppose the asserted uniform coverage failed.  Then some sequence \(P_n\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{alg}}\) would keep the absolute coverage error bounded away from zero.  Compactness supplies a subsequence along which the fine parameters and hence \(\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{alg}}(P_n)\) converge to a limit \(\Sigma_*\ne0\).  Along that subsequence, \((16)\) converges to \(N(0,\Sigma_*)\), while \((28)\) converges in probability to \(c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma_*)\).  The boundary
\[
 \{x:\|x\|_{\mathcal Z,\infty}=c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma_*)\}
\]
has zero limiting probability by the norm-sphere argument.  Therefore
\[
 P_{P_n}\!\left\{
 \max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sqrt n\,
 |\widehat h_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg}}(z)-h_{uv}(z)|
 \le\widehat c_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{alg}}
 \right\}\longrightarrow1-\alpha,
\tag{29}
\]
contradicting the selected sequence.  Equation \((29)\) is exactly the simultaneous-disk event in the statement, proving uniform coverage on the exact algebraic-dependency class.

 E_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\subseteq E_{uv}^{G},
 \qquad
 V_{uv}^{\mathrm{alg},m}\subseteq V_{uv}^{G}.

 \mathcal H_{uv}(B,Q;G)
 =\{\Phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For completeness, we prove the stronger coordinate assertion made by this corridor corollary.  Fix any
\((A,D)\in\mathcal S(B,Q;G)\), which exists by hypothesis.  Lower triangularity gives
\[
 B_{ii}=(A^m)_{ii}=A_{ii}^m.
\]
Since \(m\) is odd,
\[
 A_{ii}=B_{ii}^{1/m}
 \qquad(i\in V_{uv}^{G}).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every compatible pair has the same corridor diagonals.  Induct over the positive endpoint gap \(g=i-j\) for corridor edges.  If all corridor edges of gap less than \(g\) have already been recovered, then \(\mathrm{lem{:}path\mbox{-}expansion}\) gives, for \(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{G}\) of gap \(g\),

 B_{ij}=A_{ij}C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})+R_{ij,m}(A).
\tag{4}

 C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})
 =C_m(B_{ii}^{1/m},B_{jj}^{1/m})\ne0.
\tag{5}
\]
Solving \((4)\) with the legitimate division in \((5)\) yields
\[
 A_{ij}
 =\frac{B_{ij}-R_{ij,m}(A)}
 {C_m(B_{ii}^{1/m},B_{jj}^{1/m})},
\tag{6}
\]
which is exactly the next update in \(\mathscr R_{uv,m,G}(B)\).  Finite induction proves that every compatible pair has all the common corridor coordinates asserted in the statement and that they equal \(\mathscr R_{uv,m,G}(B)\).

 \Phi_{uv,m,G}(B)(z)
 =\Phi^{\mathrm{alg}}_{uv,m,G}(B)(z).
\tag{7}
