%%% FIELD unrestricted-statement
Let \(m\) be even, let \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}\) satisfy \(\rho(A)<1\), and let \(D\in\mathbb S_{++}^{d}\) be arbitrary.  For each \(s\in\{-1,1\}\), let \((X_t^{(s)})_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) be the zero-mean stationary Gaussian VAR(1) model
\[
 X_t^{(s)}=sA X_{t-1}^{(s)}+\varepsilon_t^{(s)},
 \qquad
 (\varepsilon_t^{(s)})_{t\in\mathbb Z}\mathrel{\mathrm{iid}}\sim N(0,D).
\]
Then the subsampled processes \((X_{jm}^{(1)})_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) and \((X_{jm}^{(-1)})_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) have the same law.  This conclusion requires neither a supplied variable order, triangularity or graph-support restrictions on \(A\), nor diagonality of \(D\).  For any \(u\ne v\) such that \(A_{vu}\ne0\), the attained direct coordinate transfers
\[
 \frac{A_{vu}z}{1-A_{vv}z}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{-A_{vu}z}{1+A_{vv}z}
\]
are unequal as elements of \(\mathbb R(z)\).  Consequently the direct coordinate transfer is not point identified by the even-subsampled stationary Gaussian law on this unrestricted stable VAR(1) class.
%%% FIELD unrestricted-proof
Put \(A_s=sA\) for \(s\in\{-1,1\}\).  First we verify that the stationary models in the statement are well defined.  Choose \(\varrho\) with \(\rho(A)<\varrho<1\).  A finite-dimensional Jordan-form calculation gives a constant \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
 \lVert A^k\rVert\le C \varrho^k,
 \qquad k\ge0.
\]
Indeed, on a Jordan block with eigenvalue \(\lambda\), every entry of its \(k\)-th power is a constant multiple of \(\binom{k}{j}\lambda^{k-j}\) for some fixed \(j\); since \(|\lambda|<\varrho\), each such sequence is bounded by a constant times \(\varrho^k\).  Conjugating the finitely many blocks changes only the constant.  The same bound holds for \(A_s^k=s^kA^k\).  Taking \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) to be the spectral norm, independence and centering of the innovations therefore imply, for \(M>N\),
\[
 \mathbb E\left\lVert
   \sum_{k=N}^{M}A_s^k\varepsilon_{t-k}^{(s)}
 \right\rVert^2
 =\sum_{k=N}^{M}\operatorname{tr}
   \left(A_s^kD(A_s^k)^\top\right)
 \le \operatorname{tr}(D)C^2\sum_{k=N}^{M}\varrho^{2k}.
\]
The last expression tends to zero as \(N\to\infty\), uniformly in \(M>N\).  Thus
\[
 X_t^{(s)}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_s^k\varepsilon_{t-k}^{(s)}
\]
converges in second mean, is centered, stationary, and Gaussian, and shifting the series proves
\(X_t^{(s)}=A_sX_{t-1}^{(s)}+\varepsilon_t^{(s)}\).

Iterating this recursion through one block of length \(m\), and defining \(Y_j^{(s)}:=X_{jm}^{(s)}\), gives
\[
 Y_j^{(s)}=A_s^mY_{j-1}^{(s)}+\eta_j^{(s)},
 \qquad
 \eta_j^{(s)}:=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A_s^r\varepsilon_{jm-r}^{(s)}.
\]
Distinct indices \(j\) use disjoint innovation blocks.  Hence the \(\eta_j^{(s)}\) are independent centered Gaussian vectors, and \(\eta_j^{(s)}\) is independent of the sigma-field generated by \(Y_{j-1}^{(s)},Y_{j-2}^{(s)},\ldots\).  Because \(m\) is even,
\[
 A_s^m=s^mA^m=A^m=:\overline B.
\]
Moreover, symmetry of scalar multiplication with matrix transposition yields, term by term,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\eta_j^{(s)})
 =\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(sA)^rD\bigl((sA)^r\bigr)^\top
 =\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}s^{2r}A^rD(A^r)^\top
 =\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A^rD(A^r)^\top
 =:\overline Q.
\]
Notice that \(\overline Q\succ0\), because its \(r=0\) summand is \(D\succ0\) and every other summand is positive semidefinite.  Also \(\rho(\overline B)=\rho(A)^m<1\).  Iteration of the coarse recursion through \(L\) blocks gives
\[
 Y_0^{(s)}=\overline B^L Y_{-L}^{(s)}
 +\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\overline B^\ell\eta_{-\ell}^{(s)}.
\]
Stationarity gives \(\mathbb E\lVert Y_{-L}^{(s)}\rVert^2=\mathbb E\lVert Y_0^{(s)}\rVert^2<\infty\), while the same matrix-power bound applied to \(\overline B\) implies \(\overline B^L Y_{-L}^{(s)}\to0\) in second mean.  Taking \(L\to\infty\) shows that its stationary marginal covariance is uniquely
\[
 \overline\Gamma_0
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\overline B^\ell
      \overline Q(\overline B^\ell)^\top.
\]
Thus both sampled processes have initial distribution \(N(0,\overline\Gamma_0)\), conditional transition kernel
\[
 Y_j\mid Y_{j-1}=y\sim N(\overline B y,\overline Q),
\]
and independent block innovations.  Iterated conditioning proves equality of every finite-dimensional distribution, hence equality of the two sampled-process laws.  None of this calculation imposed an ordering, a zero restriction on \(A\), or diagonality of \(D\).

Finally, set \(p=A_{vu}\) and \(q=A_{vv}\).  The direct coordinate transfers under \(A\) and \(-A\) are respectively
\[
 h_+(z)=\frac{pz}{1-qz},
 \qquad
 h_-(z)=\frac{-pz}{1+qz}.
\]
Their denominator polynomials have constant term one and hence are nonzero polynomials.  If \(h_+=h_-\) in \(\mathbb R(z)\), cross multiplication would give
\[
 pz(1+qz)=-pz(1-qz),
\]
so cancellation of the equal quadratic terms would give \(2pz=0\) as a polynomial.  This contradicts \(p=A_{vu}\ne0\).  The same observed law therefore attains two unequal values of the causal query, which is precisely failure of point identification.

If \(m\) is even and \((A,D)\in\mathcal S(B,Q;G)\), then \((-A,D)\in\mathcal S(B,Q;G)\). If \(A_{vu}\ne0\), the two attained transfers \(A_{vu}z/(1-A_{vv}z)\) and \(-A_{vu}z/(1+A_{vv}z)\) are unequal as rational functions. Hence no nonzero queried edge transfer is point identified from the Gaussian coarse law under an even factor.
%%% FIELD target-proof
Assume that \(m\) is even and fix \((A,D)\in\mathcal S(B,Q;G)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}known\mbox{-}sampling\mbox{-}factor}\), \(m\ge2\) is the fixed sampling factor, so \(m=2k\) for an integer \(k\ge1\).  Put \(\widetilde A=-A\).  The first equality in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}fiber}\) gives \(A^m=B\), and therefore
\[
 \widetilde A^m=(-A)^{2k}=A^{2k}=B.
\]
For every \(r\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\),
\[
 \widetilde A^rD(\widetilde A^r)^\top
 =(-1)^rA^rD\bigl((-1)^rA^r\bigr)^\top
 =(-1)^{2r}A^rD(A^r)^\top
 =A^rD(A^r)^\top.
\]
Summing this equality and using the second equality in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}fiber}\) yields
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\widetilde A^rD(\widetilde A^r)^\top
 =\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A^rD(A^r)^\top
 =Q.
\]
Every required zero is preserved because \(\widetilde A_{ij}=-A_{ij}\): thus \(\widetilde A_{ij}=0\) when \(i<j\), and also when \(i>j\) and \(j\to i\notin G\).  Stability is preserved coordinatewise since
\[
 \max_i|\widetilde A_{ii}|=\max_i|A_{ii}|<1.
\]
The matrix \(D\) is unchanged, so it remains diagonal and positive definite.  These are all constraints in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}fiber}\), and hence
\[
 (-A,D)=(\widetilde A,D)\in\mathcal S(B,Q;G).
\]

The same membership conditions also show that \(A\) is lower triangular.  Its characteristic polynomial is therefore
\[
 \det(\lambda I-A)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda-A_{ii}),
\]
so \(\rho(A)=\max_i|A_{ii}|<1\).  The theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}unrestricted\mbox{-}even\mbox{-}sign\mbox{-}ambiguity}\), applied with this \(A\), the positive-definite matrix \(D\), and the even factor \(m\), now proves that the stationary Gaussian processes with fine transitions \(A\) and \(-A\) have exactly the same sampled law.  This application is valid a fortiori for diagonal \(D\); the unrestricted theorem itself does not use that restriction.

By \(\mathrm{ass{:}query\mbox{-}edge}\), \(u\to v\) is the queried arrow.  Write \(p=A_{vu}\) and \(q=A_{vv}\).  From the two established memberships and \(\mathrm{def{:}transfer\mbox{-}fiber}\), the common observed law attains both
\[
 h_+(z)=\frac{pz}{1-qz}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 h_-(z)=\frac{-pz}{1+qz}
\]
in \(\mathcal H_{uv}(B,Q;G)\).  Here \(|q|<1\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}fiber}\), so both denominators are nonzero for every \(|z|=1\).  If the two rational functions were equal, cross multiplication would imply
\[
 pz(1+qz)=-pz(1-qz),
\]
and cancellation of the quadratic terms would give \(2pz=0\).  This is impossible when \(p=A_{vu}\ne0\).  Hence the transfer fiber contains two attained unequal elements.  Since they arise from the same Gaussian coarse law, no functional of that law can equal the queried nonzero transfer in both compatible models; point identification fails.
