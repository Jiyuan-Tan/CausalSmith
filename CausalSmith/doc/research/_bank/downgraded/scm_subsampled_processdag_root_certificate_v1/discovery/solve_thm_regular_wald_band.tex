%%% FIELD proof-regular-wald-band
Put
\[
 \mathfrak P_{\partial}
 =\mathcal M_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\partial}
 (d,m,G;\gamma,L,d_{\min},d_{\max},\kappa)
\]
and let \(\mathfrak P_{uv,\partial}\) denote the target-corridor class in
\(\mathrm{def{:}corridor\mbox{-}derivative\mbox{-}separated\mbox{-}class}\),
with the same displayed arguments.  By
\(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}class}\) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}derivative\mbox{-}separated\mbox{-}regular\mbox{-}class}\),
every \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}\) belongs to the compact class, has odd
\(m\), and satisfies
\[
 m|A_{ii}|^{m-1}\geq\kappa\quad(i\in V),
 \qquad
 |C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})|\geq\kappa\quad(j\to i\in E).
 \tag{1}
\]
Since \(V_{uv}^{G}\subseteq V\) and \(E_{uv}^{G}\subseteq E\) by
\(\mathrm{def{:}target\mbox{-}corridor}\), restriction of the two minima
in \((1)\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak P_{\partial}\subseteq\mathfrak P_{uv,\partial}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The ordering and query-edge hypotheses, the assumed inequality
\(\kappa\leq m(1-\gamma)^{m-1}\), and
\(\mathrm{lem{:}derivative\mbox{-}separated\mbox{-}class\mbox{-}nonempty}\)
also give
\[
 \mathfrak P_{\partial}\neq\varnothing.
 \tag{3}
\]

We next prove that the full and corridor procedures agree with probability
tending to one uniformly on \(\mathfrak P_{\partial}\).  First we establish
the required uniform consistency of the coarse transition estimator.  Fix
any matrix norm.  A nonzero summand in an entry of \(A^r\) is indexed by a
nondecreasing walk in the supplied order.  Such a walk has at most
\(d-1\) strict increases.  If it has \(q\) strict increases, its absolute
value is at most \(L^q(1-\gamma)^{r-q}\), and, for fixed \(d\), the number
of such summands is bounded by a constant times \((r+1)^q\).  Hence, for
any fixed \(r_0\in(1-\gamma,1)\), the polynomial factor can be absorbed
into the larger geometric rate, yielding a constant \(C_0<\infty\),
depending only on \(d,\gamma,L\), such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}\|A^r\|
 \leq C_0r_0^r,
 \qquad r\geq0.
 \tag{4}
\]
Here the walk expansion uses the lower-triangular and graph-support
conditions contained in \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}class}\), while the
bounds on diagonal and strict off-diagonal factors use respectively the
stability-margin and entry-envelope conditions in that definition.

Iterating the structural equation and using \((4)\) shows in \(L^2\) that
the unique stationary solution is
\[
 X_t=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}A^r\varepsilon_{t-r}.
\]
Independence and centering of the Gaussian innovations therefore give
\[
 M_0=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}A^rD(A^r)^\top,
 \qquad
 d_{\min}I_d\preceq M_0\preceq C_1I_d,
 \tag{5}
\]
for a finite constant \(C_1\) uniform on \(\mathfrak P_{\partial}\).
Indeed, the lower bound is the \(r=0\) term and the upper bound follows
from \(D\preceq d_{\max}I_d\) and the summability of \(C_0^2r_0^{2r}\).
Writing \(B=A^m\), iteration over one coarse step gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y_{s+k}Y_s^\top]=B^kM_0\quad(k\geq0),
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}\|B^kM_0\|
 \leq C_2r_0^{mk}.
 \tag{6}
\]

For centered jointly Gaussian scalar random variables, direct
differentiation of their joint moment-generating function at zero gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(U_1U_2,U_3U_4)
 =\operatorname{Cov}(U_1,U_3)\operatorname{Cov}(U_2,U_4)
  +\operatorname{Cov}(U_1,U_4)\operatorname{Cov}(U_2,U_3).
 \tag{7}
\]
Apply \((7)\) to scalar entries of \(Y_sY_s^\top\) and
\(Y_sY_{s-1}^\top\).  Equation \((6)\), and its transpose for negative
lags, imply that for every scalar coordinate \(Z_s\) of these two product
arrays,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
 \bigl|\operatorname{Cov}_P(Z_0,Z_k)\bigr|\leq C_3
 \tag{8}
\]
with \(C_3<\infty\).  Expanding the variance of each sample average in
\(\mathrm{def{:}empirical\mbox{-}moments}\), summing \((8)\), and using
that \(d\) is fixed now yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \|\widehat M_0-M_0\|^2+
  \|\widehat M_1-M_1\|^2
 \right]\leq\frac{C_4}{n}.
 \tag{9}
\]
For every unit vector \(x\), \((5)\) gives
\(x^\top\widehat M_0x\geq d_{\min}-\|\widehat M_0-M_0\|\).
Moreover, \((9)\) implies directly that
\(P\{\|\widehat M_0-M_0\|\geq d_{\min}/2\}\leq
4C_4/(nd_{\min}^2)\).  Thus \(\widehat M_0\succ0\) with probability
tending to one uniformly.  On the event
\(\|\widehat M_0-M_0\|<d_{\min}/2\), the inverse
identity
\[
 \widehat M_0^{-1}-M_0^{-1}
 =\widehat M_0^{-1}(M_0-\widehat M_0)M_0^{-1}
\]
bounds the inverse difference by a uniform constant times
\(\|\widehat M_0-M_0\|\).  Since
\(M_1=BM_0\), \(\widehat B=\widehat M_1\widehat M_0^{-1}\), and \(B\) is
uniformly bounded by \((4)\), equation \((9)\) consequently implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}P(\widehat M_0\not\succ0)
 \longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}
 P\!\left(\|\widehat B-B\|>\delta\right)
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad(\delta>0).
 \tag{10}
\]

We now construct one neighborhood on which both recursions are regular.
By \((1)\),
\[
 |A_{ii}|\geq (\kappa/m)^{1/(m-1)}>0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus the odd-root inverse \(b\mapsto b^{1/m}\), evaluated at
\(b=A_{ii}^m\), has derivative
\[
 \frac{1}{mA_{ii}^{m-1}},
 \tag{12}
\]
whose absolute value is at most \(\kappa^{-1}\).  Every off-diagonal
division in the full recursion has denominator bounded in absolute value
below by \(\kappa\), by \((1)\).  The admissible \(A\)-parameters are a
closed subset of the finite-dimensional box imposed by the entry and
stability-margin bounds, and hence are compact.  Starting with the
diagonal updates and then inducting over the finitely many endpoint gaps,
continuity of polynomial evaluation and division, together with
\((11)\)--\((12)\), therefore supplies a common \(\delta_0>0\) such that,
whenever \(\|\widetilde B-B\|<\delta_0\), both the full recursion of
\(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}estimator}\) and the corridor recursion of
\(\mathrm{def{:}target\mbox{-}corridor\mbox{-}recursion}\) are defined and
all of their divided-difference denominators have absolute value at least
\(\kappa/2\).  Define
\[
 \mathcal E_n
 =\{\widehat M_0\succ0,\ \|\widehat B-B\|<\delta_0\}.
\]
Equation \((10)\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{13}
\]

On \(\mathcal E_n\), the full and corridor diagonal roots agree on
\(V_{uv}^{G}\), because each is the unique real odd root of the same
entry \(\widehat B_{ii}\).  Process the edges \(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{G}\)
by increasing endpoint gap.  By
\(\mathrm{lem{:}target\mbox{-}corridor\mbox{-}recursion\mbox{-}closure}\),
every diagonal and strict-edge coordinate in \(R_{ij,m}\) lies in the
corridor, and every such strict edge has smaller endpoint gap.  The
induction hypothesis therefore makes the numerator, denominator, and all
previously recovered inputs in the full and corridor updates identical.
It follows that
\[
 \widehat A_{ij}^{\mathrm{full}}
 =\widehat A_{ij}^{\mathrm{cor}}
 \quad(j\to i\in E_{uv}^{G}),
 \qquad
 \widehat A_{vv}^{\mathrm{full}}=
 \widehat A_{vv}^{\mathrm{cor}}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Because \(u\to v\in E_{uv}^{G}\), the estimator definitions and \((14)\)
give, simultaneously for all \(z\in\mathcal Z\),
\[
 \widehat h_{uv}(z)
 =\widehat h_{uv}^{\mathrm{cor}}(z)
 =\frac{\widehat A_{vu}^{\mathrm{cor}}z}
 {1-\widehat A_{vv}^{\mathrm{cor}}z}.
 \tag{15}
\]
The computation of \(\widehat Q\) and recovery of innovation variances in
the full procedure do not occur in the final expression in \((15)\).

The equality in \((15)\) is an identity between differentiable maps on
the common neighborhood just constructed.  Consequently the target rows
of the full and corridor recursive Jacobians agree there and vanish on
all non-corridor recursion inputs.  Differentiating the last expression
gives
\[
 \mathrm dh_{uv}(z)
 =\frac{z\,\mathrm dA_{vu}}{1-A_{vv}z}
  +\frac{A_{vu}z^2\,\mathrm dA_{vv}}{(1-A_{vv}z)^2}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Both denominators are nonzero because \(|A_{vv}z|\leq1-\gamma<1\) for
\(z\in\mathcal Z\subseteq\mathbb T\).  The two procedures use the same
Isserlis-series estimate of the coarse lag-moment covariance.  Equality
of their target Jacobians therefore implies on \(\mathcal E_n\) that
their positive-semidefinite plug-in covariance matrices and induced
Gaussian laws coincide.  With the quantile convention in the two theorem
statements,
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_h=\widehat\Sigma_{h,\mathrm{cor}},
 \qquad
 \widehat c_{1-\alpha}
 =\widehat c_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{cor}}.
 \tag{17}
\]

By \((2)\),
\(\mathrm{thm{:}target\mbox{-}corridor\mbox{-}wald\mbox{-}band}\)
applies to every \(P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}\).  Equations
\((13)\), \((15)\), and \((17)\) show that replacing the corridor
statistic, covariance, and critical value by their full-recursion
counterparts changes their joint law only on an event whose probability
tends to zero uniformly.  The asserted uniform centered Gaussian limit,
with derivative \((16)\), follows from the corridor theorem.

Finally, let \(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{full}}\) and
\(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{cor}}\) be the simultaneous coverage events in the
present theorem and in the corridor theorem.  Equations \((15)\) and
\((17)\) imply that they agree on \(\mathcal E_n\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{split}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}
 \left|P(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{full}})-(1-\alpha)\right|
 &\leq
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}
 \left|P(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{cor}})-(1-\alpha)\right|
 +\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)\\
 &\leq
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{uv,\partial}}
 \left|P(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{cor}})-(1-\alpha)\right|
 +\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_{\partial}}P(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{split}
 \tag{18}
\]
The first term tends to zero by
\(\mathrm{thm{:}target\mbox{-}corridor\mbox{-}wald\mbox{-}band}\), and
the second by \((13)\).  Together with \((3)\), this proves nonemptiness,
the stated uniform limit law, and the simultaneous coverage conclusion.
