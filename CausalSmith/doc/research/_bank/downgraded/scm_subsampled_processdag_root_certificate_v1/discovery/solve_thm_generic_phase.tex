Let \(\vartheta=\operatorname{vec}_{s}(M_0,M_1)\) and \(\widehat\vartheta=\operatorname{vec}_{s}(\widehat M_0,\widehat M_1)\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\mbox{-}gaussian\mbox{-}lag\mbox{-}moment\mbox{-}clt}\) gives the uniform Gaussian limit
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta)\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,\Omega)
\]
and a uniformly consistent parametric estimate \(\widehat\Omega\).

The population moment map satisfies
\[
 B=M_1M_0^{-1},\qquad Q=M_0-BM_0B^\top.
\]
Indeed \(M_1=\mathbb E[(BY_{s-1}+\eta_s)Y_{s-1}^\top]=BM_0\), because \(\eta_s\) is independent of \(Y_{s-1}\), and the stationary Lyapunov equation gives the formula for \(Q\).  Since \(M_0\succeq d_{\min}I_d\), matrix inversion and these two maps are continuously differentiable on a common neighborhood of all population moments.

On the odd branch, the diagonal root equation is \(B_{ii}=A_{ii}^m\).  The added separation condition gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\partial B_{ii}}{\partial A_{ii}}\right|
 =m|A_{ii}|^{m-1}\ge\kappa,
\]
so the inverse diagonal-root map is continuously differentiable with uniformly bounded derivative.  At every allowed off-diagonal coordinate, the regular-class condition gives
\[
 |C_m(A_{ii},A_{jj})|\ge\kappa.
\]
The path expansion shows that its remainder uses only earlier coordinates.  Induction over endpoint gap therefore makes every recursively recovered entry of \(A\) a continuously differentiable function of \(B\) on a common neighborhood, with uniformly bounded first derivative and uniformly vanishing Taylor remainder.  The innovation recursion has denominators
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A_{ii}^{2r}\ge1,
\]
so the recovered diagonal \(D\) is likewise a smooth function of \((B,Q)\), although the focal transfer uses only \(A_{vu}\) and \(A_{vv}\).

For \(|z|=1\), the stability margin yields
\[
 |1-A_{vv}z|\ge1-|A_{vv}|\ge\gamma.
\]
Thus the coefficient-pair-to-transfer map is continuously differentiable uniformly over the finite nonempty grid, and direct differentiation gives
\[
 \mathrm dh_{uv}(z)
 =\frac{z}{1-A_{vv}z}\,\mathrm dA_{vu}
 +\frac{A_{vu}z^2}{(1-A_{vv}z)^2}\,\mathrm dA_{vv}.
\]
Let \(J_h\) be the Jacobian of the composite map from \(\vartheta\) to the stacked real and imaginary parts of \((h_{uv}(z))_{z\in\mathcal Z}\).  The uniform Taylor expansion and the preceding moment CLT give
\[
 \sqrt n\,\operatorname{vec}_{\mathbb R}
 \big((\widehat h_{uv}(z)-h_{uv}(z))_{z\in\mathcal Z}\big)
 =J_h\sqrt n(\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta)+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \Longrightarrow N(0,\Sigma_h),
\]
uniformly, where
\[
 \Sigma_h=J_h\Omega J_h^\top.
\]
The recursively evaluated Jacobian \(\widehat J_h\) is uniformly consistent, so
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_h=\widehat J_h\widehat\Omega\widehat J_h^\top
 \longrightarrow\Sigma_h
\]
uniformly in probability.

It remains to justify exact nominal simultaneous coverage despite possible correlation or rank deficiency across frequencies.  The contemporaneous and conventional lagged denominator moment matrices differ exactly by
\[
 n^{-1}(Y_nY_n^\top-Y_0Y_0^\top)=O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1})
\]
uniformly.  Smoothness of inversion therefore makes their coefficient estimators differ by \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1})\), and the displayed estimator has the first-order expansion
\[
 \sqrt n\,\operatorname{vec}(\widehat B-B)
 =\operatorname{vec}\!\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\eta_sY_{s-1}^\top M_0^{-1}\right)+o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]
whose covariance is positive definite and uniformly bounded away from zero because \(Q\succeq d_{\min}I_d\) and \(M_0\) is uniformly bounded above.  Since \(u\to v\in G\), the recursive derivative of \(A_{vu}\) with respect to \(B_{vu}\) is \(1/C_m(A_{vv},A_{uu})\ne0\).  Moreover, \(|A_{ii}|\le1-\gamma<1\) gives \(|C_m(A_{vv},A_{uu})|\le m\), so the absolute value of this derivative is at least \(1/m\).  The diagonal coefficient \(A_{vv}\) depends on \(B_{vv}\), not on \(B_{vu}\).  Also, for every \(z\in\mathcal Z\),
\[
 \left|\frac{\partial h_{uv}(z)}{\partial A_{vu}}\right|
 =\frac{1}{|1-A_{vv}z|}\ge\frac{1}{1+|A_{vv}|}\ge\frac12.
\]
Hence the limiting Gaussian transfer vector is not identically zero, uniformly over the compact subfamily, even when \(A_{vu}=0\) or \(\Sigma_h\) is singular across coordinates.

For a real vector stacked in complex pairs, define
\[
 \|x\|_{\mathcal Z,\infty}
 =\max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sqrt{x_{\Re,z}^2+x_{\Im,z}^2}.
\]
If \(Z_{\Sigma}\sim N(0,\Sigma)\), let \(c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma)\) be the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\|Z_{\Sigma}\|_{\mathcal Z,\infty}\).  On every nonzero Gaussian support subspace this norm has a continuous distribution on \((0,\infty)\) and no atom at zero.  Compactness, the uniform lower nondegeneracy just proved, and a subsequence argument make this continuity uniform over the covariance family.  Consequently
\[
 \widehat c_{1-\alpha}=c_{1-\alpha}(\widehat\Sigma_h)
 \longrightarrow c_{1-\alpha}(\Sigma_h)
\]
uniformly in probability, and the uniform Gaussian approximation gives
\[
 \sup_{(A,D)}\left|
 \Pr\!\left\{
 \max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sqrt n\,|\widehat h_{uv}(z)-h_{uv}(z)|
 \le\widehat c_{1-\alpha}
 \right\}-(1-\alpha)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The event inside this probability is exactly simultaneous membership of every \(h_{uv}(z)\) in the disks stated in the theorem, which proves the claimed Wald coverage.
