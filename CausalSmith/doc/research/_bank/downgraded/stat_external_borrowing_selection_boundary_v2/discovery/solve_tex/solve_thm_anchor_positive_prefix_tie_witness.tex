%%% FIELD anchor-positive-prefix-proof
Choose \(0<\sigma _0,\sigma _1\leq 1/4\), take \(q>4\), choose \(\varepsilon\leq 1/10\), and take \(C\) larger than all of the bounded moment envelopes below.  Choose deterministic allocations with \(1/3\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq2/3\) and, on the infinite subsequence \(n=8m\), set \(n_{\mathcal R}=n_{\mathcal E}=n/2\).  As usual, any finitely many initial convention rows may be filled by bounded laws satisfying the same displayed conditions; they do not affect any limit below.

We construct the full-data laws.  Conditional on \(R=1\), let \(X\) have density \(1/2\) on each of \([-2,-1]\) and \([1,2]\), let \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and put
\[
 Y(a)=X+E_a,\qquad a\in\{0,1\},
\]
where \(E_0,E_1\) are independent of \((X,A)\), each \(E_a\) takes the values \(\pm\sigma_a\) with probability \(1/2\), and the observed outcome is \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\).  Conditional on \(R=0\), let \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), set \(X=1+U\) and \(A=0\), and define the potential outcomes on every row by
\[
 Y(0)=Y(1)=Y=
 \begin{cases}
  1+U+a_n/\sqrt n,&n=8m,\\
  1+U,&n\notin8\mathbb N,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(a_n=0\) off the displayed subsequence and, on it,
\[
 a_n^2=
 \frac{12\sigma_0^2/25}{3/100+404/(5625n)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Finally let \(V\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) be independent of every other variable.  Equation (1) makes external consistency literal, including off-subsequence rows.  The two strata are sampled independently according to \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}stratum\text{-}sampling}\).

Use the genuine squared intercept-regression loss
\[
 L((x,y);\theta)=(y-\theta)^2,
 \qquad \Theta=\mathbb R.
 \tag{3}
\]
Because \(E(X\mid R=1)=0\) and \(E(E_0)=0\), the trial-control risk has the unique minimizer and Hessian
\[
 \theta_n=E(Y\mid R=1,A=0)=0,
 \qquad H_n=2.
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus the population score of an external point \(z=(1+u,y,v_0)\) is
\[
 s_n(z)
 =2E\{|X+E_0|\mid R=1,A=0\}\,|y|.
 \tag{5}
\]
The external outcomes in (1) are positive and strictly increasing in \(u\), so (5) is strictly increasing in \(u\).  It follows directly from the definition of \(U_n\) that \(U_n(Z)=U\) under \(R=0\); hence a population prefix with \(k/n_{\mathcal E}=v\) retains exactly \(U\leq v\), and the auxiliary label \(V\) never resolves a population-score tie.

The empirical Hessian equals two whenever the empirical control fit is defined, while the empirical minimizer is the trial-control sample mean.  Consequently
\[
 \widehat s_n(z)=
 2\left\{n_{\mathcal R,0}^{-1}
 \sum_{i\leq n_{\mathcal R}:A_i=0}|Y_i-\widehat\theta_n|\right\}
 |y-\widehat\theta_n|.
 \tag{6}
\]
The boundedness and centering just imposed give, by Chebyshev's inequality applied to the trial sums,
\[
 \frac{n_{\mathcal R,0}}{n_{\mathcal R}}=\frac12+O_{\mathbb P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n=O_{\mathbb P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{7}
\]
On an event whose probability tends to one there are at least two trial controls, the positive multiplier in braces in (6) is nonzero, and \(|\widehat\theta_n|<1/2\).  Conditional on the trial sample, equality between the absolute values in (6) for two distinct external observations is contained in the union of the two zero-Lebesgue-measure sets \(U_j=U_{j'}\) and
\[
 U_j+U_{j'}=2\{\widehat\theta_n-1-a_n/\sqrt n\}.
\]
Therefore the generated scores are almost surely all distinct on that event.  Moreover, since every external outcome is at least one, (6) is then strictly increasing in \(U_j\), so the generated order agrees with the population order.  Thus, with probability tending to one, the generated prefixes are the same strict top-prefixes and neither ranking uses \(V\).

We next compute the two leading risks exactly on the subsequence.  Fix a prefix fraction \(v=k/n_{\mathcal E}\in[0,1]\) and write \(\Delta_n=a_n/\sqrt n\).  From the equal stratum sizes and the retention event \(U\leq v\),
\[
 p(v)=\frac{1+v}{2},
 \qquad q(v)=\frac1{1+v}.
 \tag{8}
\]
On the retained common covariate region \(I_v=[1,1+v]\), the trial density before normalization is \(1/4\), the external density before normalization is \(1/2\), and hence
\[
 \pi^+=P_{n,k}(R=1\mid X=x)=\frac13,
 \qquad e^+=P_{n,k}(A=1\mid X=x)=\frac16.
 \tag{9}
\]
Define
\[
 B=\frac{1-\pi^+}{1-\pi^+/2}=\frac45,
 \qquad c_0=\frac{\pi^+}{1-\pi^+/2}=\frac25,
 \qquad A(v)=\frac{vB}{2}=\frac{2v}{5}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The treated regression uses trial observations only, whereas among controls in \(I_v\) the external share is \(B\).  Therefore
\[
 m_{n,k,1}(x)=x,
 \qquad
 m_{n,k,0}(x)=x+B\Delta_n\quad(x\in I_v),
 \tag{11}
\]
and both regressions equal \(x\) off \(I_v\).  The residual terms in \(E_{P_{n,k}}\eta_{n,k}\) have conditional mean zero by the definitions of these regressions.  Since a trial observation lies in \(I_v\) with probability \(v/2\), (8)--(11) give
\[
 T_{n,k}
 =E\{m_{n,k,1}(X)-m_{n,k,0}(X)\mid R=1\}
 =-\frac{vB}{2}\Delta_n
 =-A(v)\Delta_n.
 \tag{12}
\]
The causal trial effect is
\[
 \tau_n=E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid R=1\}=E(E_1-E_0)=0,
\]
so (12) and the definition of \(\beta_{n,k}\) imply
\[
 b_{n,k}=-A(v)\frac{a_n}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad \beta_{n,k}=-A(v)a_n.
 \tag{13}
\]

For the variance coordinate, first set \(\Delta_n=0\).  Substitution of (8)--(11) into \(\psi_{n,k}=p(v)^{-1}W_{n,k}(\eta_{n,k}-T_{n,k})\) shows that the coefficient of \(E_1\) on a treated trial record is four.  The coefficient of \(E_0\) on a control trial record is \(-4/5\) on \(I_v\) and \(-4\) off \(I_v\), while the external residual is zero.  These noise terms are already conditionally centered.  Weighting the two conditional variances by \(P_n(R=1)=P_n(R=0)=1/2\) therefore gives
\[
 V(v)=4\sigma_1^2+4\sigma_0^2-\frac{48v}{25}\sigma_0^2.
 \tag{14}
\]

It remains to retain the order-\(n^{-1}\) variance caused by \(\Delta_n\).  Up to addition of a constant within the trial stratum, which does not change a conditional variance, the unit-\(\Delta_n\) trial coefficient is
\[
 G_{\mathcal R}
 =\mathbf 1\{X\in I_v\}
 \left[-2B\mathbf 1\{A=1\}
 +2B(c_0-1)\mathbf 1\{A=0\}\right].
 \tag{15}
\]
Under the trial law, \(P_n(X\in I_v\mid R=1)=v/2\) and \(A\) is an independent fair coin.  Hence
\[
 \frac12\operatorname{Var}(G_{\mathcal R}\mid R=1)
 =\frac{32}{25}\left(\frac{17v}{50}-\frac{4v^2}{25}\right).
 \tag{16}
\]
For example, (16) follows by noting that the two nonzero values in (15) are \(-8/5\) and \(-24/25\), so that
\[
 E(G_{\mathcal R}^2\mid R=1)=\frac{544v}{625},
 \qquad
 E(G_{\mathcal R}\mid R=1)=-\frac{16v}{25}.
\]
For an external record, direct substitution in the control-residual term yields the unit-shift coefficient
\[
 d(v)\mathbf 1\{U\leq v\},
 \qquad
 d(v)=-\frac4{25}+\frac{4v}{5(1+v)}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Its weighted conditional variance is \(v(1-v)d(v)^2/2\).  The noise component in (14) has zero covariance with (15) conditional on the trial stratum because \(E(E_a\mid X,A,R=1)=0\), and there is no external noise component.  It follows that
\[
 \Gamma_n(k,k)=V(v)+\frac{a_n^2}{n}K(v),
 \tag{18}
\]
where
\[
 K(v)=
 \frac{32}{25}\left(\frac{17v}{50}-\frac{4v^2}{25}\right)
 +\frac12v(1-v)
 \left\{-\frac4{25}+\frac{4v}{5(1+v)}\right\}^{2}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Combining (13), (18), and the definition \(\rho_{n,k}=\beta_{n,k}^2+\Gamma_n(k,k)\) proves the exact identity
\[
 \rho_n(v)=V(v)+a_n^2A(v)^2+\frac{a_n^2}{n}K(v).
 \tag{20}
\]

Now take \(v_1=1/4\) and \(v_2=1/2\), and put \(k_{n,1}=n_{\mathcal E}/4\) and \(k_{n,2}=n_{\mathcal E}/2\).  These are strictly positive integers for \(n=8m\).  Equations (10), (14), and (19) give
\[
 V(v_1)-V(v_2)=\frac{12}{25}\sigma_0^2,
 \qquad
 A(v_2)^2-A(v_1)^2=\frac3{100},
\]
and
\[
 K(v_1)=\frac{12}{125},
 \qquad
 K(v_2)=\frac{944}{5625},
 \qquad
 K(v_2)-K(v_1)=\frac{404}{5625}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Substitution of (2) and (21) into (20) yields
\[
 \rho_{n,k_{n,2}}-\rho_{n,k_{n,1}}
 =-\frac{12}{25}\sigma_0^2
 +a_n^2\left\{\frac3{100}+\frac{404}{5625n}\right\}=0.
 \tag{22}
\]
The denominator in (2) is larger than \(3/100\), so \(0<a_n<4\sigma_0\), and (2) also gives \(a_n\to4\sigma_0\).  Since a norm dominates the absolute difference of its scalar coordinates, (13) implies
\[
 d_n(k_{n,1},k_{n,2})
 \geq |\beta_{n,k_{n,2}}-\beta_{n,k_{n,1}}|
 =\frac{a_n}{10}
 \longrightarrow\frac{2\sigma_0}{5}>0.
 \tag{23}
\]

We finally verify class membership.  The construction gives fixed-stratum independence, full-data consistency, conditional trial randomization, treatment propensity \(1/2\), and the two independent-uniform tie-label conditions.  On the negative trial support the source propensity is one.  On \([1,2]\) it is
\[
 \frac{\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}/2}
 {\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}/2+\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}}
 \geq\frac15,
\]
using \(1/3\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq2/3\); thus source overlap holds for the chosen \(\varepsilon\).  The outcomes are uniformly bounded.  The loss (3) is convex and twice continuously differentiable, its Hessian is the constant two, and (4) proves the score equation and curvature.  Its score and Hessian moments are uniformly bounded.  Because the gradient increment is the nonrandom constant \(2(\theta-\theta_n)\) and the Hessian is constant, both stated equicontinuity conditions hold identically.

Furthermore, (5) maps the external uniform variable affinely with slope
\[
 c_s=2E\{|X+E_0|\mid R=1,A=0\}\geq2(1-\sigma_0)>0.
\]
Therefore, for every \(u>0\) and every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 P_n\{|s_n(Z)-t|\leq u\mid R=0\}
 \leq \min\{1,2u/c_s\}
 \leq C u
\]
after enlarging \(C\), which verifies the score-margin condition.  For every fold and every prefix, the true piecewise formulas (8)--(11), with the analogous deterministic allocation weights off the subsequence, are measurable constant learners based on the training sigma-field and have zero \(L_2(P_n)\) error.  Taking \(r_n=(n+1)^{-1/3}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,r_n^2\log(en)\longrightarrow0,
\]
so the nuisance-estimability and nuisance-rate conditions hold.  Finally, (13) applies to every prefix on the subsequence, while the bias is zero off it.  Since \(0\leq A(v)\leq2/5\) and \(a_n<4\sigma_0\),
\[
 \sup_n\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}|\sqrt n\,b_{n,k}|
 \leq\frac85\sigma_0<\infty,
\]
which is the local-misspecification condition.  Hence the constructed sequence belongs to \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\), and therefore also to \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\).

For this single legal sequence, (22) supplies an exact-risk pair in the maximization defining \(\omega^{\star}(0)\), while (23) supplies its distance.  Taking the limsup over all rows and retaining the subsequence gives
\[
 \omega^{\star}(0)
 \geq\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 d_{8m}(k_{8m,1},k_{8m,2})
 \geq\frac{2\sigma_0}{5}>0.
\]
This proves the asserted positive-prefix, anchor-loss counterexample to exact leading-risk tie invariance.
