%%% FIELD conditional-transfer-statement
Suppose \(\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq1-\varepsilon\) for every \(n\).  On a joint probability space whose observed-data marginal is \(\mathbb P_n\), let \(G_n\sim N(0,\Gamma_n)\), and write \(\mathbb P_n\) also for the coupled law.  Suppose there is a deterministic sequence \(a_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 \max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}
 \left|
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,k}-\tau_n)
 -(\beta_{n,k}+G_{n,k})
 \right|=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1), \tag{1}
\]
and define
\[
 R_n=\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}
       |\widehat C_{n,k}-Q_{n,k}(G_n)|,
 \qquad
 \mathcal M_n(a)=
 \left\{k\in\mathcal K_n:
 Q_{n,k}(G_n)\leq
 \min_{l\in\mathcal K_n}Q_{n,l}(G_n)+2a
 \right\},
\]
\[
 D_n(a)=\max_{k,l\in\mathcal M_n(a)}d_n(k,l).
\]
If
\[
 \mathbb P_n(R_n\leq a_n)\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 D_n(a_n)\sqrt{\log(e|\mathcal K_n|)}
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1), \tag{2}
\]
then the bounded-Lipschitz distance between the law of
\(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)\) and the law of
\(\beta_{n,J_n(G_n)}+G_{n,J_n(G_n)}\) tends to zero.  This is a conditional transfer result only: it does not assert that (1)--(2) hold for the generated fixed-cardinality prefix estimator.
%%% FIELD conditional-transfer-proof
Put \(J_n=J_n(G_n)\).  On the event \(\{R_n\leq a_n\}\), optimality of \(\widehat k_n\) for \(\widehat C_n\) and of \(J_n\) for \(Q_n(G_n)\) gives, equation by equation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{n,\widehat k_n}(G_n)
 &\leq \widehat C_{n,\widehat k_n}+a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(R_n\)},\\
 &\leq \widehat C_{n,J_n}+a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(\widehat k_n\)},\\
 &\leq Q_{n,J_n}(G_n)+2a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(R_n\)},\\
 &=\min_{l\in\mathcal K_n}Q_{n,l}(G_n)+2a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(J_n\)}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\widehat k_n\in\mathcal M_n(a_n)\); also \(J_n\in\mathcal M_n(a_n)\) directly from the definition of that set.  Consequently,
\[
 d_n(\widehat k_n,J_n)\leq D_n(a_n) \tag{4}
\]
on \(\{R_n\leq a_n\}\).

For deterministic \(k,l\in\mathcal K_n\), the definition of \(d_n\) gives
\[
 |\beta_{n,k}-\beta_{n,l}|\leq d_n(k,l), \tag{5}
\]
and the definition of \(G_n\), followed by the covariance identity in the definition of \(d_n\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l})
 &=\Gamma_n(k,k)+\Gamma_n(l,l)-2\Gamma_n(k,l)\\
 &\leq d_n(k,l)^2. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) remains valid when \(\Gamma_n\) is singular.  Let \(m_n=|\mathcal K_n|\).  If \(d_n(k,l)\leq r\) for \(r>0\), then \(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l}\) is a centered Gaussian variable with variance at most \(r^2\).  Its moment-generating function is at most \(\exp(t^2r^2/2)\).  On the event \(\{G_{n,k}-G_{n,l}>u\}\), the nonnegative variable \(\exp\{t(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l})\}\) is at least \(e^{tu}\); hence, for \(u>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P_n(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l}>u)
 \leq \exp\{-tu+t^2r^2/2\}.
\]
Minimizing at \(t=u/r^2\), applying the same argument to the negative tail, and then taking a union bound over at most \(m_n^2\) ordered pairs yields, for every \(x>0\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P_n\Bigg[
 \max_{\substack{k,l\in\mathcal K_n\\d_n(k,l)\leq r}}
 |(\beta_{n,k}+G_{n,k})-(\beta_{n,l}+G_{n,l})|\\
 >r+r\sqrt{2\{\log(2m_n^2)+x\}}
 \Bigg]\leq e^{-x}. 
 \end{split} \tag{7}
\]
Here (5) accounts for the first \(r\) on the threshold.  If \(r=0\), both differences in (5)--(6) vanish almost surely, so (7) is immediate by continuity.

Let \(L_n=\log(em_n)\).  The second condition in (2) implies that there is a deterministic sequence \(c_n>0\), \(c_n\downarrow0\), for which
\[
 \mathbb P_n\{D_n(a_n)\sqrt{L_n}>c_n\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, this is the standard diagonal characterization of convergence in probability: choose successive positive thresholds tending to zero after indices at which their exceedance probabilities are at most the thresholds.  Set \(r_n=c_n/\sqrt{L_n}\).  Then
\[
 \mathbb P_n\{D_n(a_n)>r_n\}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 r_n\sqrt{L_n}=c_n\longrightarrow0. \tag{9}
\]
The sampling-fraction condition and \(n_{\mathcal E}=n(1-\lambda_{n,\mathcal R})\) imply
\[
 m_n=n_{\mathcal E}+1\geq\varepsilon n+1\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so \(L_n\to\infty\).  Apply (7) with \(r=r_n\) and \(x=L_n\).  Since \(\log(2m_n^2)\leq2L_n+\log2\), the probability bound \(e^{-L_n}\) tends to zero and the threshold in (7) is \(O(r_n\sqrt{L_n})=O(c_n)=o(1)\).  Equations (4), (8)--(9), and \(\mathbb P_n(R_n\leq a_n)\to1\) therefore imply
\[
 (\beta_{n,\widehat k_n}+G_{n,\widehat k_n})
 -(\beta_{n,J_n}+G_{n,J_n})=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{10}
\]

Evaluating the maximum in (1) at the data-dependent index \(\widehat k_n\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)
 -(\beta_{n,\widehat k_n}+G_{n,\widehat k_n})
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{11}
\]
Combining (10) and (11),
\[
 \Delta_n:=
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)
 -(\beta_{n,J_n(G_n)}+G_{n,J_n(G_n)})
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{12}
\]
For every real function \(f\) bounded by one and with Lipschitz constant at most one, the coupling and (12) give
\[
 \left|E f(X_n)-E f(Y_n)\right|
 \leq E\min\{2,|\Delta_n|\}, \tag{13}
\]
where \(X_n\) and \(Y_n\) are the two variables in (12).  For every \(u>0\), the right side of (13) is at most
\[
 u+2\mathbb P_n(|\Delta_n|>u),
\]
which tends to at most \(u\) by (12), and then to zero as \(u\downarrow0\).  Taking the supremum over the bounded-Lipschitz class proves the claimed convergence of laws.
%%% FIELD boundary-obstruction-statement
Two distinct obstructions hold, on separate witnesses.  First, for an admissible choice of the fixed constants, there exist a sequence \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm B}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and deterministic interior prefixes \(k_n^{\mathrm B}/n_{\mathcal E}\to1/2\) such that
\[
 S_n^{\mathrm B}:=
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}-\tau_n-b_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}\}
\]
has a non-Gaussian subsequential limit.  Consequently the frozen all-prefix coupling to \(G_n\sim N(0,\Gamma_n)\) fails, and no repair that changes only the covariance of a centered Gaussian process can couple this coordinate with \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\) error.

Second, suppose \(\lambda\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), \(t\in(0,1]\), and \(\sigma_0^2,\sigma_1^2>0\).  Define
\[
 A_{\max}=\frac{2\sqrt2\,\sigma_0}{t\varepsilon^{3/2}},
\]
and suppose
\[
 \frac{\sigma_0^q+\sigma_1^q}{2}\leq\frac C2,
 \qquad
 A_{\max}\leq\min\{C,(C/2)^{1/q}\}.
\]
Then the separate finite-support construction gives a sequence \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm T}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\) for which, for every sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\rho_{n,0},
 \qquad
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)>0,
 \qquad
 \omega^{\star}(0)>0.
\]
Thus exact leading-risk tie invariance fails.  These two conclusions do not assert that the generated selector chooses the non-Gaussian coordinate, and they do not establish or refute any particular selected marginal limit law.
%%% FIELD boundary-obstruction-proof
The first witness is the one proved in \(\mathrm{thm:generated\text{-}prefix\text{-}coupling}\).  That theorem supplies \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm B}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\), a deterministic interior prefix \(k_n^{\mathrm B}\), and a subsequence along which
\[
 S_n^{\mathrm B}\ \Rightarrow\ S^{\mathrm B}, \tag{14}
\]
where \(S^{\mathrm B}\) is non-Gaussian.  If the frozen all-prefix coupling held, retaining its single \(k_n^{\mathrm B}\) coordinate would give
\[
 |S_n^{\mathrm B}-G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}|
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{15}
\]
For every real function \(f\) bounded by one and with Lipschitz constant at most one, (15) would imply
\[
 \left|E f(S_n^{\mathrm B})-E f(G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}})\right|
 \leq E\min\{2,|S_n^{\mathrm B}-G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}|\}
 \longrightarrow0, \tag{16}
\]
because for every \(u>0\) the expectation on the right is at most
\[
 u+2\mathbb P_n\{|S_n^{\mathrm B}-G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}|>u\}.
\]
The established theorem already proves that (15) is impossible for its frozen Gaussian coordinate; hence (16) contradicts its established non-Gaussian subsequential limit.  This is the asserted failure of the frozen coupling.

The same argument rules out a covariance-only Gaussian repair.  Suppose instead that, on the subsequence in (14), centered Gaussian variables \(Z_n\sim N(0,v_n)\), with arbitrary \(v_n\geq0\), satisfied
\[
 |S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n|=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{17}
\]
By (14), \(S_n^{\mathrm B}\) is tight.  Equation (17) then makes \(Z_n\) tight, because
\[
 \mathbb P_n(|Z_n|>M+1)
 \leq \mathbb P_n(|S_n^{\mathrm B}|>M)
 +\mathbb P_n(|S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n|>1).
\]
A family \(N(0,v_n)\) is tight only if \(\sup_n v_n<\infty\); otherwise, for every fixed \(M>0\), a subsequence with \(v_n\to\infty\) would satisfy \(\mathbb P(|Z_n|\leq M)\to0\).  Passing to a further subsequence, let \(v_n\to v\geq0\).  If \(v=0\), then, for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \eta^2\mathbb P_n(|Z_n|>\eta)\leq E[Z_n^2]=v_n\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(Z_n=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\), so (17) makes \(S_n^{\mathrm B}=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\), contradicting the non-Gaussian limit in (14).  If \(v>0\), then for every \(x\in\mathbb R\) and \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P_n(Z_n\leq x-\delta)-\mathbb P_n(|S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n|>\delta)
 \leq \mathbb P_n(S_n^{\mathrm B}\leq x)
 \leq \mathbb P_n(Z_n\leq x+\delta)+\mathbb P_n(|S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n|>\delta). \tag{18}
\]
At every continuity point \(x\) of the distribution function of \(S^{\mathrm B}\), first let \(n\to\infty\) in (18), using (14), (17), and
\[
 \mathbb P_n(Z_n\leq y)=\Phi(y/\sqrt{v_n})\longrightarrow\Phi(y/\sqrt v),
\]
and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Continuity of \(\Phi\) gives
\[
 \mathbb P(S^{\mathrm B}\leq x)=\Phi(x/\sqrt v).
\]
Distribution functions are right-continuous and their continuity points are dense, so this equality determines the law of \(S^{\mathrm B}\) as \(N(0,v)\), again contradicting (14).  Hence changing only the covariance of a Gaussian process cannot repair the boundary coordinate.  The established witness identifies the omitted term as the empirical fixed-cardinality quantile-boundary term and proves that it is non-Gaussian.

For the second conclusion, the stated positivity and moment inequalities are exactly the feasibility premises of \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}support\text{-}tie\text{-}witness}\).  That theorem proves that its different construction belongs to \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\) and that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\rho_{n,0},
 \qquad
 d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)^2
 \geq B_n^2(a_n^{\dagger})^2
 \longrightarrow D_\infty>0. \tag{19}
\]
All quantities in (19) are nonnegative, so taking square roots gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)
 \geq\sqrt{D_\infty}>0. \tag{20}
\]
The same theorem applies the definition of the selection modulus to the exact equalities in (19) and obtains
\[
 \omega^{\star}(0)\geq\sqrt{D_\infty}>0. \tag{21}
\]
Equations (19)--(21) prove failure of exact leading-risk tie invariance.  The first and second established theorems use different law sequences, and none of (14)--(21) analyzes the random event that \(\widehat k_n\) selects a particular coordinate.  Therefore no conclusion about the selected marginal law has been drawn.

For the actual generated fixed-cardinality prefix path, which primitive score-index regularity conditions and estimator-side entropy or near-minimizer conditions suffice to derive, uniformly over \(k\in\mathcal K_n\), the joint law of
\[
 \left(
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{n,k}-\tau_n-b_{n,k}\},
 \widehat C_{n,k}
 \right)_{k\in\mathcal K_n}
\]
from the displayed sample maps?  The corrected process must include both the external empirical-quantile boundary term created by fixed-cardinality ranking and the trial-sample derivative induced by replacing \(s_n(z)\) with \(\widehat s_n(z)\).  Derive from those primitive conditions a quantitative near-minimizer bound strong enough to invoke \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}quadratic\text{-}transfer}\), or prove the corresponding transfer directly, and state the resulting bounded-Lipschitz law of \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)\) across fixed, vanishing, and exact gaps.  On \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\) the corrected boundary process must be allowed to be non-Gaussian; a Gaussian correction may be asserted only under independently justified score-index smoothness.  Neither the desired coupling nor the near-minimizer conclusion may be imposed as a headline assumption.

The strongest established facts are a negative boundary and a conditional transfer.  The regular-class witness in \(\mathrm{thm:frozen\text{-}prefix\text{-}boundary\text{-}obstruction}\) gives an interior deterministic prefix for which
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{n,k_n}-\tau_n-b_{n,k_n}\}
 \Rightarrow Z+H(X),
\]
where \(Z\) is Gaussian and \(H(X)\) is a bounded, nonconstant, non-Gaussian empirical-quantile boundary contribution.  Thus the fixed-threshold \(\Gamma_n\)-Gaussian process and every covariance-only Gaussian repair are invalid for that coordinate.  A separate legal finite-support witness has
\[
 \rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\rho_{n,0},
 \qquad
 \liminf_n d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)>0,
\]
so exact risk ties are not target-coordinate ties.  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}quadratic\text{-}transfer}\) proves that any correctly coupled joint process yields a selected bounded-Lipschitz law once its random near-minimizer set has \(d_n\)-diameter \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1/\sqrt{\log(e|\mathcal K_n|)})\).  What remains is to derive the corrected process and this stability from primitive score-index and complexity conditions for the actual estimator.
