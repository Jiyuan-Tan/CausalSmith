\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Influence-ranked external-control borrowing has two verified necessity boundaries inside the displayed Yang--Li--Wu--Yang sample architecture.  A continuous generated ranking can create a quantitatively nondegenerate, non-Gaussian fixed-cardinality boundary term, so the frozen \(\Gamma_n\)-Gaussian all-prefix coupling and every covariance-only repair fail.  Separately, exact leading-risk ties can retain nonvanishing root-\(n\) ATE-coordinate distance, including between two positive prefixes; the repaired finite-support witness also defines the external potential outcomes on every row.  No Yang theorem asserts the refuted coupling.  A bounded feasibility attempt on a smooth-localizable generated-prefix subclass fails at the primitive fitted-score sublevel-set derivative, so the corrected selected law, sharp observable modulus, and computable tie-uniform interval remain unresolved and the present negative-plus-conditional package does not meet the positive Stat field-tier inference floor.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Yang, Li, Wu, and Yang's equations~(2)--(3) and Algorithm~1 define the squared outcome-regression fit, generated influence ranking, and positive top-\(k\) prefixes.  Their fixed-set Theorems~1--2 do not assert a generated all-prefix coupling, and their Theorem~3 assumes a fixed positive MSE gap and is silent at multiple minima.  The rank-boundary-corrected process, local-gap selected law, observable modulus, and post-selection ATE interval are therefore additional problems rather than consequences of those theorems.

\paragraph{Niche.} The verified boundary separates leading-risk proximity from proximity of the root-\(n\) ATE coordinates.  The positive-prefix theorem supplies separated-coordinate geometry at the exact zero-gap boundary excluded by Yang's Theorem~3, so any valid localization or confidence interval must pay an unavoidable width cost unless extra observable structure controls both bias and covariance.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves a non-Gaussian fixed-cardinality boundary witness with an explicit positive-probability nondegeneracy event, proves separate exact-tie coordinate separation witnesses, and retains a conditional quadratic transfer lemma.  It also performs one substantive smooth-localizable-subclass feasibility attempt from the actual sample maps.  That attempt derives the required decomposition but cannot justify the fitted-score sublevel-set derivative from the proposed primitive package; consequently it establishes no corrected all-prefix process, near-minimizer law, matching observable modulus, or uniformly valid multiplier interval.

\section{Related work}

Yang, Li, Wu, and Yang define the squared outcome-regression empirical risk minimizer in equation~(2), its generated influence score in equation~(3), and the nested positive top-\(k\) workflow in Algorithm~1.  Their Theorems~1--2 concern a given borrowed set, while Theorem~3 assumes a given ranking and fixed positive MSE separation.  No theorem there asserts the uniform local all-prefix coupling refuted here.  The positive-prefix tie theorem supplies separated-coordinate geometry precisely at the multiple-minimum boundary on which Theorem~3 is silent.  Mogstad, Romano, Shaikh, and Wilhelm treat inference for random ranks and provide a relevance precedent for tie handling, but not selection among generated external-control prefixes.  Valancius and coauthors provide hybrid-trial sampling and positivity structure, but not generated influence ranking or local-gap selection.  Claeskens and Hjort supply the focused local-misspecification architecture.  Patel, DiTraglia, Zuber, and Burgess study focused-selector inference for a fixed invalid-instrument problem, not estimated individual ranks.  Leeb and Poetscher give the generic local nonuniformity background.  Fang and Santos motivate derivative-aware resampling at nondifferentiable maps.  Chernozhukov, Fernandez-Val, and Luo establish sorting derivatives under stronger regular-value structure, but pointwise smooth score fitting, a scalar density, and Hölder score-indexed moments alone do not deliver the fitted-score sublevel-set derivative needed here.  Gaussian, multiplier, and localization results of Chernozhukov and coauthors, Zrnic and Fithian, and Andrews, Kitagawa, and McCloskey become applicable only after the corrected ranked functional and its anti-concentrated local set have been derived.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\psi_{n,k,i}=\frac{n}{n_{\mathcal R}+k}\widehat W_{n,k,i}\{\widehat\eta_{n,k}^{(-f(i))}(O_i)-\widehat\tau_{n,k}\}\).
  \item \(\widehat v_{n,k}\) --- \texttt{prefix variance-\allowbreak{}coordinate estimator}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{variance estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Let \(\overline\psi_{n,r,k}=n_r^{-1}\sum_{i:R_i=r}\widehat\psi_{n,k,i}\). Then \(\widehat v_{n,k}=n^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^1\sum_{i:R_i=r}(\widehat\psi_{n,k,i}-\overline\psi_{n,r,k})^2\).
  \item \(\widehat C_{n,k}\) --- \texttt{scaled generated MSE criterion}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{selection criterion}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat C_{n,k}=n(\widehat\tau_{n,k}-\widehat\tau_{n,0})^2+\widehat v_{n,k}\).
  \item \(\beta_{n,k}\) --- \texttt{local prefix bias coordinate}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{local parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\beta_{n,k}=\sqrt n\,b_{n,k}\).
  \item \(\Gamma_n\) --- \texttt{joint prefix influence covariance matrix}; positive semidefinite matrices indexed by \(\mathcal K_n\); \texttt{covariance functional}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\Gamma_n(k,l)=\sum_{r=0}^1P_n(R=r)\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}\{\psi_{n,k}(O),\psi_{n,l}(O)\mid R=r\}\).
  \item \(\widehat\Gamma_n\) --- \texttt{estimated joint prefix influence covariance matrix}; positive semidefinite matrices indexed by \(\mathcal K_n\); \texttt{covariance estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} With \(\overline\psi_{n,r,k}=n_r^{-1}\sum_{i:R_i=r}\widehat\psi_{n,k,i}\), define \(\widehat\Gamma_n(k,l)=n^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^1\sum_{i:R_i=r}(\widehat\psi_{n,k,i}-\overline\psi_{n,r,k})(\widehat\psi_{n,l,i}-\overline\psi_{n,r,l})\).
  \item \(G_n\) --- \texttt{joint Gaussian prefix process}; \(\mathbb R^{|\mathcal K_n|}\); \texttt{limit object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G_n\sim N(0,\Gamma_n)\), allowing singular and arbitrarily correlated coordinates.
  \item \(h_{n,k}(O)\) --- fixed-stratum root-\(n\) ATE coordinate; the direct sum \(\mathbb R\oplus L_2^0(P_n(\,\cdot\mid R=1))\oplus L_2^0(P_n(\,\cdot\mid R=0))\); \texttt{focal coordinate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(h_{n,k}=(\beta_{n,k},\psi_{n,k}-E_{P_n}[\psi_{n,k}\mid R=1],\psi_{n,k}-E_{P_n}[\psi_{n,k}\mid R=0])\), with the two function coordinates restricted to their respective strata and weighted by \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\) and \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}\).
  \item \(\rho_{n,k}\) --- \texttt{leading MSE coordinate}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{risk coordinate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\rho_{n,k}=\|h_{n,k}\|_n^2=\beta_{n,k}^2+\Gamma_n(k,k)\).
  \item \(\mathfrak r_{n,k}\) --- \texttt{unscaled leading MSE coordinate}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{global risk coordinate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathfrak r_{n,k}=b_{n,k}^2+\Gamma_n(k,k)/n=\rho_{n,k}/n\).
  \item \(k_n^{\star}\) --- \texttt{unique leading-\allowbreak{}risk minimizing prefix}; \(\mathcal K_n\); \texttt{oracle index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The unique minimizer of \(k\mapsto\rho_{n,k}\) when it exists.
  \item \(d_n(k,l)\) --- \texttt{ATE-\allowbreak{}coordinate distance}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{distance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(d_n(k,l)=\|h_{n,k}-h_{n,l}\|_n=\{(\beta_{n,k}-\beta_{n,l})^2+\Gamma_n(k,k)+\Gamma_n(l,l)-2\Gamma_n(k,l)\}^{1/2}\).
  \item \(\omega^{\star}(\delta)\) --- \texttt{sharp selection-\allowbreak{}relevance modulus}; functions from \([0,\infty)\) to \([0,\infty]\); \texttt{focal object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The class-wide near-tie modulus constructed in \(\mathrm{def:selection\text{-}modulus}\).
  \item \(Q_{n,k}(g)\) --- \texttt{quadratic local criterion map}; real-valued functions on \(\mathbb R^{|\mathcal K_n|}\); \texttt{limit criterion}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(Q_{n,k}(g)=(\beta_{n,k}+g_k-g_0)^2+\Gamma_n(k,k)\).
  \item \(J_n(g)\) --- \texttt{tie-\allowbreak{}broken quadratic argmin map}; maps from \(\mathbb R^{|\mathcal K_n|}\) to \(\mathcal K_n\); \texttt{limit selector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(J_n(g)=\min\arg\min_{k\in\mathcal K_n}Q_{n,k}(g)\).
  \item \(\widehat k_n\) --- \texttt{generated MSE-\allowbreak{}minimizing prefix}; \(\mathcal K_n\); \texttt{selector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat k_n=\min\arg\min_{k\in\mathcal K_n}\widehat C_{n,k}\).
  \item \(\mathcal I_n(\alpha)\) --- \texttt{tie-\allowbreak{}uniform confidence interval}; random closed intervals in \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{inferential target}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The selection-aware interval sought in \(\mathrm{oeq:multiplier\text{-}interval}\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-stratum-sampling]\label{ass:fixed-stratum-sampling}
For every \(n\), the first \(n_{\mathcal R}\) records are independent draws from \(P_n(\,\cdot\mid R=1)\), the remaining \(n_{\mathcal E}\) records are independent draws from \(P_n(\,\cdot\mid R=0)\), and the two strata are independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent fixed-stratum triangular-array sampling, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratum-fractions]\label{ass:stratum-fractions}
The deterministic sampling fractions satisfy \(\varepsilon\le\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\le1-\varepsilon\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Stable two-sample allocation, \cite{ValanciusEtAl2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
The observed outcome satisfies \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rct-randomization]\label{ass:rct-randomization}
For each \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(A\perp Y(a)\mid(X,R=1)\) under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional randomization in the trial, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-overlap]\label{ass:treatment-overlap}
The randomized propensity obeys \(\varepsilon\le P_n(A=1\mid X=x,R=1)\le1-\varepsilon\) on the trial covariate support.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strong treatment overlap, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:source-overlap]\label{ass:source-overlap}
The source score obeys \(P_n(R=1\mid X=x)\ge\varepsilon\) on the trial covariate support.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Trial-source positivity, \cite{ValanciusEtAl2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-moment]\label{ass:outcome-moment}
The outcome envelope satisfies \(\sup_n E_{P_n}[|Y|^q]\le C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform polynomial-moment condition, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKatoKoike2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:loss-smoothness]\label{ass:loss-smoothness}
The map \(\theta\mapsto L(z;\theta)\) is twice continuously differentiable on a fixed neighborhood of \(\theta_n\), uniformly in \(n\) and \(z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Twice continuously differentiable loss, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:loss-convexity]\label{ass:loss-convexity}
For every \(z\), the map \(\theta\mapsto L(z;\theta)\) is convex on \(\Theta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Convex influence-model loss, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-equation]\label{ass:score-equation}
The population optimizer is interior and satisfies \(E_{P_n}[\nabla_\theta L((X,Y);\theta_n)\mid R=1,A=0]=0\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior M-estimation score equation, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-moment]\label{ass:score-moment}
The randomized-control score obeys \(\sup_nE_{P_n}[\|\nabla_\theta L((X,Y);\theta_n)\|^q\mid R=1,A=0]\le C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (M-estimation score moment bound, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hessian-moment]\label{ass:hessian-moment}
On a fixed neighborhood \(\mathcal N(\theta_n)\), \(\sup_nE_{P_n}[\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal N(\theta_n)}\|\nabla_\theta^2L((X,Y);\theta)\|^{q/2}\mid R=1,A=0]\le C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Local Hessian envelope moment bound, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-equicontinuity]\label{ass:score-equicontinuity}
For every deterministic \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the fixed-stratum randomized-control empirical process of \(\nabla_\theta L((X,Y);\theta)-\nabla_\theta L((X,Y);\theta_n)\) is \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\) uniformly over \(\|\theta-\theta_n\|\le\delta_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Local stochastic equicontinuity for M-estimation scores, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hessian-equicontinuity]\label{ass:hessian-equicontinuity}
For every deterministic \(\delta_n\downarrow0\), the fixed-stratum randomized-control empirical Hessian differs from \(H_n\) by \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\) uniformly over \(\|\theta-\theta_n\|\le\delta_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Local stochastic equicontinuity for M-estimation Hessians, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hessian-curvature]\label{ass:hessian-curvature}
The control-risk Hessian satisfies \(\lambda_{\min}(H_n)\ge\varepsilon\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform strong curvature for influence-function M-estimation, \cite{YangLiWuYang2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-misspecification]\label{ass:local-misspecification}
The prefix biases satisfy \(\sup_n\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}|\sqrt n\,b_{n,k}|\le C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Focused local misspecification, \cite{ClaeskensHjort2003}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:tie-uniform]\label{ass:tie-uniform}
The auxiliary label satisfies \(V\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Continuous randomized tie breaking, \cite{MogstadRomanoShaikhWilhelm2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:tie-independent]\label{ass:tie-independent}
The auxiliary label obeys \(V\perp(X,A,R,Y(0),Y(1),Y)\) under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent randomized tie breaking, \cite{MogstadRomanoShaikhWilhelm2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nuisance-estimability]\label{ass:nuisance-estimability}
For each fold \(f\), there exist measurable learners based only on records outside that fold whose estimates of \(\pi_{n,k},e_{n,k},m_{n,k,0},m_{n,k,1}\) have worst-prefix \(L_2(P_n)\) error \(O_{\mathbb P_n}(r_n)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Finite-dimensional correctly specified outcome and source models, as well as uniformly regular sieve families with bounded complexity across nested empirical prefixes, satisfy this existential worst-prefix rate property.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nuisance-rate]\label{ass:nuisance-rate}
The nuisance rate obeys \(\sqrt n\,r_n^2\log(en)\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Log-uniform DML product-rate condition, \cite{ChernozhukovEtAl2018DML}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-margin]\label{ass:score-margin}
For every \(u>0\), \(\sup_n\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}P_n(|s_n(Z)-t|\le u\mid R=0)\le Cu\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Regular-value margin for an estimated sorting operator, \cite{ChernozhukovFernandezValLuo2018}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:generated-prefix-coupling]\label{thm:generated-prefix-coupling}
The asserted all-prefix coupling with covariance \(\Gamma_n\) is false on \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\) as defined.  For an admissible choice of the fixed constants there is a sequence \(\mathbf P\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\), an interior prefix \(k_n/n_{\mathcal E}\to1/2\), and oracle nuisance learners for which the generated ordering is, with probability tending to one, the ordinary order of a continuous score, but
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{n,k_n}-\tau_n-b_{n,k_n}\}
\]
has a non-Gaussian subsequential limit and hence cannot be coupled within \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\) to \(G_{n,k_n}\sim N(0,\Gamma_n(k_n,k_n))\).  The missing first-order term is the empirical fixed-cardinality quantile-boundary term; estimating the ranking score may add a further boundary derivative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because the theorem is existential, choose admissible fixed constants with \(q=5\), \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), and \(C\) large enough to dominate every bounded envelope below.  Put \(n_{\mathcal R}=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), \(n_{\mathcal E}=n-n_{\mathcal R}\), and complete the finitely many initial rows while preserving the same strict inequalities.  Let \(M_n=\max\{1,\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\}\), and fix sufficiently small \(a_0>0\) and \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1>0\).

Conditional on \(R=1\), let \(X\) have density one half on each of \([-2,-1]\) and \([1,2]\), let \(A\) be independent Bernoulli one half, and let \(E_0,E_1\) be mutually independent centered Rademacher variables taking values \(\pm\sigma_0\) and \(\pm\sigma_1\), respectively.  If \(X=1+u\), where \(0\leq u\leq1\), put
\[
 \Delta_n(u)=a_0\cos(2\pi M_nu),
 \qquad
 Y(a)=1+u-\Delta_n(u)+E_a,
\tag{1}
\]
and if \(X=-1-u\), put \(Y(a)=-1-u+E_a\).  Conditional on \(R=0\), let \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), set \(X=1+U\), \(A=0\), and set \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y=1+U\).  Finally take \(V\) uniform and independent of all preceding variables.  Thus consistency and trial randomization hold by construction.

Use the squared outcome-regression loss
\[
 L((x,y);\theta)=\{y-\mu_0(x;\theta)\}^2,
 \qquad \mu_0(x;\theta)=\theta,
 \qquad \Theta=\mathbb R.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(M_n\) is an integer, \(\int_0^1\Delta_n(u)\,du=0\).  The two trial covariate components have opposite means, and hence
\[
 E_{P_n}[Y\mid R=1,A=0]
 =\frac12\int_0^1\{1+u-\Delta_n(u)\}\,du
  +\frac12\int_0^1(-1-u)\,du=0.
\]
Therefore \(\theta_n=0\), \(H_n=2\), and the score equation holds.  For an external point \(z=(1+u,1+u,v)\), the definition of \(s_n\) gives
\[
 s_n(z)=2E_{P_n}[|Y|\mid R=1,A=0](1+u).
\tag{3}
\]
The coefficient in (3) is bounded above and bounded away from zero, because the negative trial component alone supplies a uniform positive lower bound when \(a_0\) and \(\sigma_0\) are sufficiently small.  Since \(U\) is uniform, the external law of \(s_n(Z)\) has a uniformly bounded density, establishing the score-margin condition.

The empirical minimizer in the displayed sample map is the trial-control sample mean \(\widehat\theta_n\), and \(\widehat H_n=2\).  Conditional on a nonempty trial-control arm,
\[
 \widehat s_n(1+u,1+u,v)
 =2\left\{n_{\mathcal R,0}^{-1}
   \sum_{i\leq n_{\mathcal R}:A_i=0}|Y_i-\widehat\theta_n|\right\}
   |1+u-\widehat\theta_n|.
\tag{4}
\]
Fixed-stratum independence and bounded second moments give \(E_{\mathbb P_n}[\widehat\theta_n^2\mid n_{\mathcal R,0}]\leq C/n_{\mathcal R,0}\) on a nonempty control arm.  Since \(n_{\mathcal R,0}/n\to1/4\) in probability and \(P(n_{\mathcal R,0}=0)\to0\), the bound \(P(|\widehat\theta_n|>\delta)\leq\delta^{-2}E\widehat\theta_n^2\) gives \(\widehat\theta_n=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\).  On the event \(|\widehat\theta_n|<1/2\), the last factor in (4) is \(1+u-\widehat\theta_n\), strictly increasing in \(u\).  The prefactor in braces is positive with probability tending to one: \(n_{\mathcal R,0}\geq2\) with probability tending to one, and the continuous trial covariate makes equality of all control outcomes a null event.  Thus, with probability tending to one, the generated ranks are exactly the ordinary ranks of the continuous variable \(U\), and \(V\) is not used.

For a population prefix fraction \(v=k/n_{\mathcal E}\), let
\[
 \pi_n^+=\frac{\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}/2}
 {\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}/2+\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}},
 \qquad
 B_n=\frac{1-\pi_n^+}{1-\pi_n^+/2}.
\]
On the retained positive component \(0\leq u\leq v\), direct conditioning gives
\[
 m_{n,k,0}(1+u)
 =1+u-\Delta_n(u)+B_n\Delta_n(u),
\tag{5}
\]
while outside that component \(m_{n,k,0}=m_{n,k,1}\).  Conditional residuals have mean zero by the definition of \(m_{n,k,a}\), so taking the expectation of \(\eta_{n,k}\) yields
\[
 b_{n,k}=T_{n,k}-\tau_n
 =E_{P_n}[m_{n,k,1}(X)-m_{n,k,0}(X)\mid R=1]
 =-\frac{B_n}{2}\int_0^v\Delta_n(u)\,du.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(\tau_n=0\), because the two potential outcomes have the same conditional mean.  Since \(0\leq B_n\leq1\),
\[
 \max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}|\sqrt n\,b_{n,k}|
 \leq\frac{a_0\sqrt n}{4\pi M_n}=O(1),
\tag{7}
\]
which verifies local misspecification.

The source posterior equals one on the negative trial-only component and is uniformly bounded away from zero on the common positive component; treatment overlap is exactly one half.  Outcomes, scores, and Hessians are uniformly bounded.  The loss is convex and twice continuously differentiable, its Hessian is the constant two, and both equicontinuity requirements hold identically.  The construction of \(V\) verifies the two tie assumptions.  On each fold choose the oracle nuisance functions, which are measurable constants relative to the training data and have zero estimation error.  With \(r_n=(n+1)^{-1/3}\), one has \(\sqrt n\,r_n^2\log(en)\to0\).  Together with (7), these checks verify every member property of \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\).  In particular, (2)--(4) give an assumption-by-assumption inclusion bridge for the squared outcome-regression empirical risk minimizer and its generated influence score.

Now restrict to the subsequence \(n=(2m)^2\).  Then \(n_{\mathcal R}=n_{\mathcal E}=n/2\), put \(k_n=n_{\mathcal E}/2\), and note \(p_{n,k_n}=3/4\).  At the population boundary \(u=1/2\),
\[
 q_{n,k_n}=\frac23,
 \qquad \pi_{n,k_n}(1+u)=\frac13,
 \qquad e_{n,k_n}(1+u)=\frac16
 \quad (u<1/2).
\]
Equation (5) becomes
\[
 m_{n,k_n,0}(1+u)=1+u-\frac15\Delta_n(u),
 \qquad
 m_{n,k_n,1}(1+u)=1+u-\Delta_n(u).
\]
Substitution into \(\eta_{n,k_n}\) gives, at an external record,
\[
 \eta_{n,k_n}(O)=-\frac3{25}\Delta_n(u)\quad(u<1/2),
 \qquad
 \eta_{n,k_n}(O)=-3\Delta_n(u)\quad(u>1/2).
\tag{8}
\]
The second equality uses the trial-only nuisance values \(\pi=1\), \(e=1/2\), and \(m_0=m_1=1+u-\Delta_n(u)\).  Equation (6), together with \(M_n=2m\), gives \(b_{n,k_n}=T_{n,k_n}=0\).

The two external score-side regions have exactly equal conditional mass,
\[
 P_n(U\leq1/2\mid R=0)=P_n(U>1/2\mid R=0)=\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover, because \(M_n=2m\) is an integer number of periods on each half interval,
\[
 E_{P_n}[\cos^2(2\pi\sqrt n\,U)\mid U\leq1/2,R=0]
 =E_{P_n}[\cos^2(2\pi\sqrt n\,U)\mid U>1/2,R=0]
 =\frac12.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus neither side of the rank boundary nor its oscillatory contrast degenerates.

Let \(\widetilde\tau_{n,k_n}\) be the oracle estimator with its external weight replaced by \(\mathbf1\{U_j\leq1/2\}\), let \(\widehat q_n=U_{(k_n)}\), and write
\[
 N_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathcal E}}\mathbf1\{U_j\leq1/2\},
 \qquad X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat q_n-1/2).
\]
By (4), the generated prefix is \(U_j\leq\widehat q_n\) with probability tending to one.  Therefore (8) gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,k_n}-\widetilde\tau_{n,k_n})
 =\frac1{p_{n,k_n}\sqrt n}
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle \frac{3a_0}{25}
 \sum_{\widehat q_n<U_j\leq1/2}
 \cos(2\pi\sqrt n\,U_j),&N_n>k_n,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle -3a_0
 \sum_{1/2<U_j\leq\widehat q_n}
 \cos(2\pi\sqrt n\,U_j),&N_n<k_n.
 \end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]

We derive the boundary limit.  A second-order expansion of the characteristic function of the centered Bernoulli count, with total third-order remainder tending to zero, gives
\[
 \frac{N_n-n_{\mathcal E}/2}{\sqrt n}\Rightarrow N(0,1/8).
\tag{12}
\]
For fixed \(B<\infty\), the number of observations in any interval of length at most \(2B/\sqrt n\) has variance \(O(\sqrt n)\).  We record the required tail calculation.  If independent centered variables \(\xi_j\) obey \(|\xi_j|\leq1\) and \(V_n=\sum_jE\xi_j^2\), then termwise comparison of the exponential series gives, for \(0<t<3\),
\[
 E\exp\!\left(t\sum_j\xi_j\right)
 \leq\exp\!\left\{\frac{t^2V_n}{2(1-t/3)}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, this follows from \(k!\geq2\cdot3^{k-2}\) for \(k\geq2\), independence, and \(1+x\leq e^x\).  Exponential Markov inequality with \(t=z/(V_n+z/3)\), applied also to \(-\xi_j\), therefore gives
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\sum_j\xi_j\right|>z\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{z^2}{2(V_n+z/3)}\right\}.
\]
For an interval count take \(V_n=O(\sqrt n)\) and \(z\) proportional to \(\sqrt n\); the bound is \(2e^{-c\sqrt n}\).  A union bound over a mesh of width \(n^{-1}\), followed by monotonicity between adjacent mesh points, now yields
\[
 \sup_{|x|\leq B}\left|
 \sqrt n\{F_{n,\mathcal E}(1/2+x/\sqrt n)
 -F_{n,\mathcal E}(1/2)-x/\sqrt n\}
 \right|=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\tag{13}
\]
The order-statistic identity \(F_{n,\mathcal E}(\widehat q_n)=1/2\), up to at most \(n_{\mathcal E}^{-1}\), and (12)--(13) imply
\[
 X_n=-2\frac{N_n-n_{\mathcal E}/2}{\sqrt n}+o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)
 \Rightarrow X,
 \qquad X\sim N(0,1/2).
\tag{14}
\]
The same mesh argument applied to the bounded summands \(\cos(2\pi\sqrt n\,U_j)\) times an interval indicator shows that, uniformly over these boundary intervals of length \(O(n^{-1/2})\), the centered sum divided by \(\sqrt n\) is \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\).  Thus the sums in (11) may be replaced by their expectations.  For fixed \(x\),
\[
 \sqrt n\int_{1/2}^{1/2+x/\sqrt n}
 \cos(2\pi\sqrt n\,u)\,du
 =\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi}.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}=1/2\) and \(p_{n,k_n}=3/4\), equations (11), (14), and (15) prove
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,k_n}-\widetilde\tau_{n,k_n})
 \Rightarrow
 H(X)=-\frac{a_0}{\pi}
 \left\{\mathbf1(X>0)+\frac1{25}\mathbf1(X<0)\right\}
 \sin(2\pi X).
\tag{16}
\]
This boundary term is quantitatively nondegenerate.  On \(1/8\leq X\leq3/8\),
\[
 |H(X)|\geq\frac{a_0}{\pi\sqrt2},
\]
and this event has probability
\[
 \Phi(3\sqrt2/8)-\Phi(\sqrt2/8)>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Together with values of \(X\) arbitrarily close to zero, (17) proves that \(H(X)\) is bounded and nonconstant.

Because \(T_{n,k_n}=0\), the fixed-threshold statistic satisfies the exact fixed-stratum identity
\[
 \sqrt n(\widetilde\tau_{n,k_n}-T_{n,k_n})
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \left[\psi_{n,k_n}(O_i)
 -E_{P_n}\{\psi_{n,k_n}(O_i)\mid R_i\}\right].
\tag{18}
\]
For each linear combination of (18) and the centered count in (12), the independent fixed-stratum summands are bounded and of order \(n^{-1/2}\).  Expanding their characteristic functions through second order has total third-order remainder \(O(n^{-1/2})\); multiplication across independent summands proves a bivariate Gaussian limit.  The covariance matrices converge by direct integration of \(1\), \(\cos(2\pi\sqrt n\,u)\), and \(\cos^2(2\pi\sqrt n\,u)\).  The covariance between (18) and the count is zero, because its only external term is proportional to
\[
 \int_0^{1/2}\cos(2\pi\sqrt n\,u)\,du=0.
\]
It follows that (18) converges to \(Z\sim N(0,v)\), that \(\Gamma_n(k_n,k_n)\to v\), and that \(Z\) is independent of \(X\).  Combining (16) and (18) yields
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{n,k_n}-\tau_n-b_{n,k_n}\}
 \Rightarrow Z+H(X).
\tag{19}
\]

The limit in (19) is not Gaussian.  Indeed, if it were Gaussian, independence would make the characteristic function of \(H(X)\) the quotient of two Gaussian characteristic functions.  A negative difference of variances would give modulus exceeding one, equality would make \(H(X)\) constant, and a positive difference would make \(H(X)\) a nondegenerate Gaussian.  Each alternative contradicts bounded nonconstancy from (17).  Since \(G_{n,k_n}\Rightarrow N(0,v)\), an \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\) coupling to that coordinate would contradict (19).  The omitted first-order term is the empirical fixed-cardinality quantile-boundary term; estimating the ranking score can add a further derivative term.  No theorem of Yang, Li, Wu, and Yang asserts the refuted uniform all-prefix coupling: the construction is a self-contained necessity boundary for extending their fixed-set and fixed-positive-gap results uniformly to local gaps.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The counterexample uses the squared outcome-regression empirical fit and generated score, and quantifies the boundary term by an event of probability \(\Phi(3\sqrt2/8)-\Phi(\sqrt2/8)>0\) on which its magnitude is at least \(a_0/(\pi\sqrt2)\). \textit{Gap.} It is a necessity boundary for a uniform local all-prefix extension; no Yang theorem asserts the coupling being refuted, and the theorem does not analyze selector choice. \textit{Consumer.} It rules out calibration based only on the frozen covariance process.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-support-tie-witness]\label{thm:finite-support-tie-witness}
Suppose \(\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq1-\varepsilon\) for every \(n\), \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\to\lambda\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), \(t\in(0,1]\), and \(\sigma_0^2,\sigma_1^2>0\).  Put
\[
 A_{\max}=\frac{2\sqrt2\,\sigma_0}{t\varepsilon^{3/2}},
\]
and assume
\[
 \frac{\sigma_0^q+\sigma_1^q}{2}\leq\frac C2,
 \qquad A_{\max}\leq\min\{C,(C/2)^{1/q}\}.
\]
With the full external potential-outcome law and the finite-row convention in the corrected construction of \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}support\text{-}witness}\), that construction belongs to \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\rho_{n,0}\), whereas
\[
 d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)^2\geq B_n^2(a_n^{\dagger})^2
 \longrightarrow
 D_\infty=2\sigma_0^2\left\{\lambda^{-1}-
 \frac{\lambda}{[\lambda+2t(1-\lambda)]^2}\right\}>0.
\]
Consequently \(\omega^{\star}(0)\geq\sqrt{D_\infty}>0\): exact leading-MSE ties need not be ATE-coordinate ties even with no covariates, convex squared-error ranking, bounded outcomes, and legal nested prefixes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 r=\lambda_{n,\mathcal R},\qquad
 s=t_n^{\dagger}\lambda_{n,\mathcal E},\qquad
 p=r+s,\qquad
 \varkappa=\frac rp,\qquad
 a=a_n^{\dagger},\qquad c=\frac a{\sqrt n}.
\tag{16}
\]
We first work on a tail row, where \(t_n^{\dagger}\geq t/2\).  By the corrected construction, every external record on such a row has \(A=0\), \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y=c\), and observed outcome \(Y=Y(0)\).  On each preceding row it instead has \(A=0\), \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y=0\).  Thus the external potential outcomes and observed outcome are defined on every row and satisfy consistency literally.

There are no covariates.  Under the prefix law at \(k_n^{\dagger}\), the definitions of \(q_{n,k}\) and \(e_{n,k}\), together with trial randomization at probability one half, give
\[
 q_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\varkappa,
 \qquad e_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\frac{\varkappa}{2}.
\]
The trial treatment mean is zero, and conditioning the control outcome on its trial and retained-external components gives
\[
 m_{n,k_n^{\dagger},1}=0,
 \qquad
 m_{n,k_n^{\dagger},0}
 =\frac{sc}{r/2+s}
 =\frac{2(1-\varkappa)}{2-\varkappa}c
 =B_nc.
\tag{17}
\]
Substitution of (17) into the displayed definition of \(\eta_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}\) yields
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(R,A)&\eta_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}\\ \hline
(1,1)&2Y(1)/\varkappa-B_nc/\varkappa,\\
(1,0)&-2Y(0)/(2-\varkappa)+2B_nc/(2-\varkappa)-B_nc/\varkappa,\\
(0,0)\text{ retained}&-2(1-B_n)c/(2-\varkappa).
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]
The three cases have respective probabilities \(\varkappa/2\), \(\varkappa/2\), and \(1-\varkappa\) under \(P_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}\).  Since
\[
 B_n=\frac{2(1-\varkappa)}{2-\varkappa},
 \qquad 1-B_n=\frac{\varkappa}{2-\varkappa},
\]
taking expectations in (18) gives
\[
 T_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=-B_nc,
 \qquad
 b_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=-\frac{B_na}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 \beta_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=-B_na.
\tag{19}
\]
At prefix zero the combined law is the trial law, both outcome regressions equal zero, and hence
\[
 T_{n,0}=\tau_n=0,\qquad \beta_{n,0}=0.
\tag{20}
\]

Adding \(B_nc\) to each entry in (18) centers it at \(T_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}\).  The deterministic coefficients of \(c\) are exactly the three components of \(u_n^{\dagger}\).  In the trial stratum their conditional-centering contribution to \(\Gamma_n(k_n^{\dagger},k_n^{\dagger})\) is
\[
 p^{-2}r\frac{(u_{n,1}-u_{n,0})^2}{4}c^2.
\]
In the external stratum the retained indicator has conditional probability \(t_n^{\dagger}\), so its contribution is
\[
 p^{-2}\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}t_n^{\dagger}(1-t_n^{\dagger})u_{n,\mathcal E}^2c^2.
\]
By the definition of \(K_n^{\dagger}\), the sum of these two terms is \(K_n^{\dagger}a^2/n\).  The centered outcome noise is present only in the trial.  Its treatment and control coefficients in \(\psi_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}\) are, respectively, \(2/r\) and \(-2/(r+2s)\).  Each \(Y(a)\) is centered and independent of \(A\), so every noise--shift cross term is zero.  Trial randomization and the variances \(\sigma_1^2\) and \(\sigma_0^2\) therefore give
\[
 \Gamma_n(k_n^{\dagger},k_n^{\dagger})
 =\frac{2\sigma_1^2}{r}
  +\frac{2r\sigma_0^2}{(r+2s)^2}
  +\frac{K_n^{\dagger}a^2}{n}.
\tag{21}
\]
At prefix zero the analogous computation gives
\[
 \Gamma_n(0,0)=\frac{2\sigma_1^2}{r}+\frac{2\sigma_0^2}{r}.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus, by the definition of \(D_n\),
\[
 \Gamma_n(k_n^{\dagger},k_n^{\dagger})
 =\Gamma_n(0,0)-D_n+\frac{K_n^{\dagger}a^2}{n}.
\tag{23}
\]
The displayed definition of the corrected amplitude is exactly
\[
 \left(B_n^2+\frac{K_n^{\dagger}}n\right)a^2=D_n.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (19), (20), (23), and (24) imply the exact equality
\[
 \rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}
 =B_n^2a^2+\Gamma_n(k_n^{\dagger},k_n^{\dagger})
 =\Gamma_n(0,0)
 =\rho_{n,0}.
\tag{25}
\]

We next verify membership in \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\) assumption by assumption.  In the trial, \(A\) is independent Bernoulli one half and is independent of the centered two-point potential outcomes, while observed \(Y\) is selected by consistency.  The full external law described above has \(A=0\) and also satisfies consistency.  Hence \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(\mathrm{ass:rct\text{-}randomization}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:treatment\text{-}overlap}\) hold.  The deterministic fixed-stratum sampling is precisely \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}stratum\text{-}sampling}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:stratum\text{-}fractions}\) is a premise.  With no covariates, the source probability is \(r\geq\varepsilon\), proving \(\mathrm{ass:source\text{-}overlap}\).  The independently generated uniform \(V\) proves \(\mathrm{ass:tie\text{-}uniform}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:tie\text{-}independent}\).

For \(L((x,y);\theta)=(y-\theta)^2/2\), the centered trial-control outcome gives \(\theta_n=0\), while \(H_n=1\).  The loss is convex and twice continuously differentiable, its Hessian is constant, and its score equation holds.  Its trial-control score has \(q\)-moment \(\sigma_0^q\leq C\), and its Hessian moment is one.  The score empirical-process increment is a centered constant and the Hessian is constant, so both equicontinuity conditions hold identically.  This verifies all loss, score, Hessian, curvature, and equicontinuity assumptions.

Every external outcome equals the same constant within a row.  In particular,
\[
 E_{P_n}\!\left[
 \left|\nabla_\theta L((X,Y);0)H_n^{-1}
 \nabla_\theta L((x,c);0)\right|
 \mathrel{\big|}R=1,A=0\right]
 =\sigma_0|c|,
\]
so all external scores are equal.  The two tie assumptions and the definition of \(U_n\) then give \(U_n(Z)=V\), and the population prefix is exactly \(V\leq t_n^{\dagger}\).

On a tail row, \(s=t_n^{\dagger}\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}\geq t\varepsilon/2\).  Since \(r+2s\leq2\),
\[
 B_n=\frac{2s}{r+2s}\geq\frac{t\varepsilon}{2},
 \qquad
 0<D_n\leq\frac{2\sigma_0^2}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Consequently (24) implies
\[
 0<a_n^{\dagger}\leq\frac{\sqrt{D_n}}{B_n}
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2\,\sigma_0}{t\varepsilon^{3/2}}
 =A_{\max}.
\tag{26}
\]
For an arbitrary prefix fraction \(v=k/n_{\mathcal E}\), the calculation (17)--(19), with \(v\) in place of \(t_n^{\dagger}\), gives
\[
 |\sqrt n\,b_{n,k}|=B_n(v)a_n^{\dagger}\leq a_n^{\dagger}\leq A_{\max}\leq C.
\]
On the initial convention rows the bias is zero.  This proves \(\mathrm{ass:local\text{-}misspecification}\).  Moreover, using \(n^{-q/2}\leq1\),
\[
 E_{P_n}|Y|^q
 \leq \frac{\sigma_0^q+\sigma_1^q}{2}
      +(a_n^{\dagger})^q
 \leq \frac C2+\frac C2=C,
\]
so \(\mathrm{ass:outcome\text{-}moment}\) holds.  We have now checked every member property of \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\).

Finally, the explicit premise \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\to\lambda\) and the definition of \(k_n^{\dagger}\) imply \(t_n^{\dagger}\to t\).  Hence
\[
 D_n\longrightarrow
 D_\infty
 =2\sigma_0^2\left\{\lambda^{-1}
 -\frac{\lambda}{[\lambda+2t(1-\lambda)]^2}\right\}.
\tag{27}
\]
The components of \(u_n^{\dagger}\) are bounded, \(p_n^{\dagger}\) stays bounded away from zero, and therefore \(K_n^{\dagger}=O(1)\).  Also
\[
 B_n\longrightarrow
 \frac{2t(1-\lambda)}{\lambda+2t(1-\lambda)}>0.
\]
Equation (24) now yields \(B_n^2(a_n^{\dagger})^2\to D_\infty\).  By the Hilbert-space definition of \(d_n\), its squared function-coordinate term is nonnegative, and therefore
\[
 d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)^2
 \geq(\beta_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}-\beta_{n,0})^2
 =B_n^2(a_n^{\dagger})^2
 \longrightarrow D_\infty.
\tag{28}
\]
Because \(t>0\) and \(\lambda<1\), one has \(\lambda+2t(1-\lambda)>\lambda\), so the expression in braces in (27) is strictly positive.  Applying \(\mathrm{def:selection\text{-}modulus}\) to the legal sequence and the exact equalities (25) proves \(\omega^{\star}(0)\geq\sqrt{D_\infty}>0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-quadratic-transfer]\label{lem:conditional-quadratic-transfer}
Suppose \(\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq1-\varepsilon\) for every \(n\).  On a joint probability space whose observed-data marginal is \(\mathbb P_n\), let \(G_n\sim N(0,\Gamma_n)\), and write \(\mathbb P_n\) also for the coupled law.  Suppose there is a deterministic sequence \(a_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 \max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}
 \left|
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,k}-\tau_n)
 -(\beta_{n,k}+G_{n,k})
 \right|=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1), \tag{1}
\]
and define
\[
 R_n=\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}
       |\widehat C_{n,k}-Q_{n,k}(G_n)|,
 \qquad
 \mathcal M_n(a)=
 \left\{k\in\mathcal K_n:
 Q_{n,k}(G_n)\leq
 \min_{l\in\mathcal K_n}Q_{n,l}(G_n)+2a
 \right\},
\]
\[
 D_n(a)=\max_{k,l\in\mathcal M_n(a)}d_n(k,l).
\]
If
\[
 \mathbb P_n(R_n\leq a_n)\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 D_n(a_n)\sqrt{\log(e|\mathcal K_n|)}
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1), \tag{2}
\]
then the bounded-Lipschitz distance between the law of
\(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)\) and the law of
\(\beta_{n,J_n(G_n)}+G_{n,J_n(G_n)}\) tends to zero.  This is a conditional transfer result only: it does not assert that (1)--(2) hold for the generated fixed-cardinality prefix estimator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(J_n=J_n(G_n)\).  On the event \(\{R_n\leq a_n\}\), optimality of \(\widehat k_n\) for \(\widehat C_n\) and of \(J_n\) for \(Q_n(G_n)\) gives, equation by equation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{n,\widehat k_n}(G_n)
 &\leq \widehat C_{n,\widehat k_n}+a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(R_n\)},\\
 &\leq \widehat C_{n,J_n}+a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(\widehat k_n\)},\\
 &\leq Q_{n,J_n}(G_n)+2a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(R_n\)},\\
 &=\min_{l\in\mathcal K_n}Q_{n,l}(G_n)+2a_n
 &&\text{by the definition of \(J_n\)}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\widehat k_n\in\mathcal M_n(a_n)\); also \(J_n\in\mathcal M_n(a_n)\) directly from the definition of that set.  Consequently,
\[
 d_n(\widehat k_n,J_n)\leq D_n(a_n) \tag{4}
\]
on \(\{R_n\leq a_n\}\).

For deterministic \(k,l\in\mathcal K_n\), the definition of \(d_n\) gives
\[
 |\beta_{n,k}-\beta_{n,l}|\leq d_n(k,l), \tag{5}
\]
and the definition of \(G_n\), followed by the covariance identity in the definition of \(d_n\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l})
 &=\Gamma_n(k,k)+\Gamma_n(l,l)-2\Gamma_n(k,l)\\
 &\leq d_n(k,l)^2. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) remains valid when \(\Gamma_n\) is singular.  Let \(m_n=|\mathcal K_n|\).  If \(d_n(k,l)\leq r\) for \(r>0\), then \(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l}\) is a centered Gaussian variable with variance at most \(r^2\).  Its moment-generating function is at most \(\exp(t^2r^2/2)\).  On the event \(\{G_{n,k}-G_{n,l}>u\}\), the nonnegative variable \(\exp\{t(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l})\}\) is at least \(e^{tu}\); hence, for \(u>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P_n(G_{n,k}-G_{n,l}>u)
 \leq \exp\{-tu+t^2r^2/2\}.
\]
Minimizing at \(t=u/r^2\), applying the same argument to the negative tail, and then taking a union bound over at most \(m_n^2\) ordered pairs yields, for every \(x>0\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb P_n\Bigg[
 \max_{\substack{k,l\in\mathcal K_n\\d_n(k,l)\leq r}}
 |(\beta_{n,k}+G_{n,k})-(\beta_{n,l}+G_{n,l})|\\
 >r+r\sqrt{2\{\log(2m_n^2)+x\}}
 \Bigg]\leq e^{-x}. 
 \end{split} \tag{7}
\]
Here (5) accounts for the first \(r\) on the threshold.  If \(r=0\), both differences in (5)--(6) vanish almost surely, so (7) is immediate by continuity.

Let \(L_n=\log(em_n)\).  The second condition in (2) implies that there is a deterministic sequence \(c_n>0\), \(c_n\downarrow0\), for which
\[
 \mathbb P_n\{D_n(a_n)\sqrt{L_n}>c_n\}\longrightarrow0. \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, this is the standard diagonal characterization of convergence in probability: choose successive positive thresholds tending to zero after indices at which their exceedance probabilities are at most the thresholds.  Set \(r_n=c_n/\sqrt{L_n}\).  Then
\[
 \mathbb P_n\{D_n(a_n)>r_n\}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 r_n\sqrt{L_n}=c_n\longrightarrow0. \tag{9}
\]
The sampling-fraction condition and \(n_{\mathcal E}=n(1-\lambda_{n,\mathcal R})\) imply
\[
 m_n=n_{\mathcal E}+1\geq\varepsilon n+1\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so \(L_n\to\infty\).  Apply (7) with \(r=r_n\) and \(x=L_n\).  Since \(\log(2m_n^2)\leq2L_n+\log2\), the probability bound \(e^{-L_n}\) tends to zero and the threshold in (7) is \(O(r_n\sqrt{L_n})=O(c_n)=o(1)\).  Equations (4), (8)--(9), and \(\mathbb P_n(R_n\leq a_n)\to1\) therefore imply
\[
 (\beta_{n,\widehat k_n}+G_{n,\widehat k_n})
 -(\beta_{n,J_n}+G_{n,J_n})=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{10}
\]

Evaluating the maximum in (1) at the data-dependent index \(\widehat k_n\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)
 -(\beta_{n,\widehat k_n}+G_{n,\widehat k_n})
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{11}
\]
Combining (10) and (11),
\[
 \Delta_n:=
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{n,\widehat k_n}-\tau_n)
 -(\beta_{n,J_n(G_n)}+G_{n,J_n(G_n)})
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1). \tag{12}
\]
For every real function \(f\) bounded by one and with Lipschitz constant at most one, the coupling and (12) give
\[
 \left|E f(X_n)-E f(Y_n)\right|
 \leq E\min\{2,|\Delta_n|\}, \tag{13}
\]
where \(X_n\) and \(Y_n\) are the two variables in (12).  For every \(u>0\), the right side of (13) is at most
\[
 u+2\mathbb P_n(|\Delta_n|>u),
\]
which tends to at most \(u\) by (12), and then to zero as \(u\downarrow0\).  Taking the supremum over the bounded-Lipschitz class proves the claimed convergence of laws.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma preserves the valid selection-transfer argument without asserting that its coupling and stability hypotheses follow from the current generated-ranking class. \textit{Gap.} The unresolved mathematical work lies in establishing a correct process and primitive stability, not in the deterministic argmin comparison. \textit{Consumer.} It transfers a future justified Gaussian rank-boundary process to the selected estimator; a non-Gaussian process requires the corresponding direct version.

\begin{theorem}[thm:frozen-prefix-boundary-obstruction]\label{thm:frozen-prefix-boundary-obstruction}
Two distinct obstructions hold on separate witnesses.  First, for an admissible choice of the fixed constants, there exist a sequence \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm B}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\) and deterministic interior prefixes \(k_n^{\mathrm B}/n_{\mathcal E}\to1/2\) such that
\[
 S_n^{\mathrm B}:=
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}-\tau_n-b_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}\}
\]
has a non-Gaussian subsequential limit.  Consequently the frozen all-prefix coupling to \(G_n\sim N(0,\Gamma_n)\) fails, and no repair that changes only the covariance of a centered Gaussian process can couple this coordinate with \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\) error.

Second, suppose \(\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq1-\varepsilon\) for every \(n\), \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\to\lambda\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), \(t\in(0,1]\), and \(\sigma_0^2,\sigma_1^2>0\).  Define
\[
 A_{\max}=\frac{2\sqrt2\,\sigma_0}{t\varepsilon^{3/2}},
\]
and suppose
\[
 \frac{\sigma_0^q+\sigma_1^q}{2}\leq\frac C2,
 \qquad
 A_{\max}\leq\min\{C,(C/2)^{1/q}\}.
\]
Then the separate finite-support construction gives a sequence \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm T}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\) for which, for every sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\rho_{n,0},
 \qquad
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)>0,
 \qquad
 \omega^{\star}(0)>0.
\]
Thus exact leading-risk tie invariance fails.  These two conclusions do not assert that the generated selector chooses the non-Gaussian coordinate, and they do not establish or refute any particular selected marginal limit law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the first obstruction, invoke \(\mathrm{thm:generated\text{-}prefix\text{-}coupling}\).  Its regular-class construction supplies \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm B}\), deterministic interior prefixes \(k_n^{\mathrm B}\), and the subsequence on which
\[
 S_n^{\mathrm B}\Rightarrow S^{\mathrm B}=Z+H(X),
\tag{29}
\]
where \(Z\) is Gaussian and independent of \(X\sim N(0,1/2)\), and
\[
 H(X)=-\frac{a_0}{\pi}
 \left\{\mathbf1(X>0)+\frac1{25}\mathbf1(X<0)\right\}
 \sin(2\pi X).
\tag{30}
\]
The underlying external score-side regions each have conditional probability one half, and the oscillatory contrast has conditional second moment one half on either region.  Moreover,
\[
 P\left\{|H(X)|\geq\frac{a_0}{\pi\sqrt2}\right\}
 \geq\Phi(3\sqrt2/8)-\Phi(\sqrt2/8)>0.
\tag{31}
\]
Thus the boundary term is quantitatively nondegenerate, and the proof of that theorem establishes that \(S^{\mathrm B}\) is non-Gaussian.

If the frozen all-prefix coupling held, its \(k_n^{\mathrm B}\) coordinate would satisfy
\[
 |S_n^{\mathrm B}-G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}|=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\tag{32}
\]
For every real \(f\) bounded by one and with Lipschitz constant at most one,
\[
 |E f(S_n^{\mathrm B})-E f(G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}})|
 \leq E\min\{2,|S_n^{\mathrm B}-G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}|\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
because for every \(u>0\) the expectation is bounded by
\[
 u+2\mathbb P_n\{|S_n^{\mathrm B}-G_{n,k_n^{\mathrm B}}|>u\}.
\]
Equation (32) would therefore transfer the Gaussian coordinate limit to (29), contradicting its non-Gaussian law.  Hence the frozen coupling fails.

The same argument excludes every covariance-only Gaussian repair.  Suppose centered Gaussian variables \(Z_n^{\circ}\sim N(0,v_n)\), with arbitrary \(v_n\geq0\), obeyed
\[
 |S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n^{\circ}|=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\tag{33}
\]
Tightness in (29) and (33) implies tightness of \(Z_n^{\circ}\), hence boundedness of \(v_n\).  Along a further subsequence, \(v_n\to v\geq0\).  If \(v=0\), then \(Z_n^{\circ}=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)\), and (33) contradicts the nondegenerate limit certified by (31).  If \(v>0\), then for every continuity point \(x\) of the distribution function of \(S^{\mathrm B}\) and every \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P_n(Z_n^{\circ}\leq x-\delta)
 -\mathbb P_n(|S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n^{\circ}|>\delta)
 \leq\mathbb P_n(S_n^{\mathrm B}\leq x)
\]
\[
 \leq\mathbb P_n(Z_n^{\circ}\leq x+\delta)
 +\mathbb P_n(|S_n^{\mathrm B}-Z_n^{\circ}|>\delta).
\]
Letting first \(n\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) would make the limit in (29) equal to \(N(0,v)\), again a contradiction.  Therefore changing only the covariance of a centered Gaussian process cannot represent the first-order fixed-cardinality boundary.  This is a necessity boundary for a uniform local all-prefix extension; no theorem of Yang, Li, Wu, and Yang asserts the coupling just refuted.

For the second, separate obstruction, the allocation limit, positivity, and moment inequalities in the statement are exactly the premises of the corrected \(\mathrm{thm:finite\text{-}support\text{-}tie\text{-}witness}\).  That theorem gives a different sequence \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm T}\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\) such that, eventually,
\[
 \rho_{n,k_n^{\dagger}}=\rho_{n,0},
 \qquad
 d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)^2
 \geq B_n^2(a_n^{\dagger})^2
 \longrightarrow D_\infty>0.
\]
Taking square roots gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}d_n(k_n^{\dagger},0)
 \geq\sqrt{D_\infty}>0,
\]
and the same theorem gives \(\omega^{\star}(0)\geq\sqrt{D_\infty}>0\).  The law sequences \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm B}\) and \(\mathbf P^{\mathrm T}\) are distinct.  None of these calculations analyzes the event that \(\widehat k_n\) selects the non-Gaussian coordinate, so they establish neither failure nor validity of a selected marginal limit law.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem faithfully combines, without merging, the quantitatively nondegenerate rank-boundary obstruction and the separate full-law finite-support tie calculation. \textit{Gap.} Neither witness proves a selected marginal limit law; the finite-support half now explicitly assumes \(\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\to\lambda\). \textit{Consumer.} It records the exact limitations of the frozen process and exact-tie invariance.

 \tag{1}

 \qquad \Theta=\mathbb R.
 \tag{3}

 \theta_n=E(Y\mid R=1,A=0)=0,

\]
The external outcomes in (1) are positive and strictly increasing in \(u\), so (5) is strictly increasing in \(u\).  It follows directly from the definition of \(U_n\) that \(U_n(Z)=U\) under \(R=0\); hence a population prefix with \(k/n_{\mathcal E}=v\) retains exactly \(U\leq v\), and the auxiliary label \(V\) never resolves a population-score tie.

The empirical Hessian equals two whenever the empirical control fit is defined, while the empirical minimizer is the trial-control sample mean.  Consequently
\[
 \widehat s_n(z)=
 2\left\{n_{\mathcal R,0}^{-1}
 \sum_{i\leq n_{\mathcal R}:A_i=0}|Y_i-\widehat\theta_n|\right\}
 |y-\widehat\theta_n|.
 \tag{6}
\]
The boundedness and centering just imposed give, by Chebyshev's inequality applied to the trial sums,
\[
 \frac{n_{\mathcal R,0}}{n_{\mathcal R}}=\frac12+O_{\mathbb P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n=O_{\mathbb P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{7}
\]
On an event whose probability tends to one there are at least two trial controls, the positive multiplier in braces in (6) is nonzero, and \(|\widehat\theta_n|<1/2\).  Conditional on the trial sample, equality between the absolute values in (6) for two distinct external observations is contained in the union of the two zero-Lebesgue-measure sets \(U_j=U_{j'}\) and
\[
 U_j+U_{j'}=2\{\widehat\theta_n-1-a_n/\sqrt n\}.
\]
Therefore the generated scores are almost surely all distinct on that event.  Moreover, since every external outcome is at least one, (6) is then strictly increasing in \(U_j\), so the generated order agrees with the population order.  Thus, with probability tending to one, the generated prefixes are the same strict top-prefixes and neither ranking uses \(V\).

We next compute the two leading risks exactly on the subsequence.  Fix a prefix fraction \(v=k/n_{\mathcal E}\in[0,1]\) and write \(\Delta_n=a_n/\sqrt n\).  From the equal stratum sizes and the retention event \(U\leq v\),
\[
 p(v)=\frac{1+v}{2},
 \qquad q(v)=\frac1{1+v}.
 \tag{8}
\]
On the retained common covariate region \(I_v=[1,1+v]\), the trial density before normalization is \(1/4\), the external density before normalization is \(1/2\), and hence
\[
 \pi^+=P_{n,k}(R=1\mid X=x)=\frac13,
 \qquad e^+=P_{n,k}(A=1\mid X=x)=\frac16.
 \tag{9}
\]
Define
\[
 B=\frac{1-\pi^+}{1-\pi^+/2}=\frac45,
 \qquad c_0=\frac{\pi^+}{1-\pi^+/2}=\frac25,
 \qquad A(v)=\frac{vB}{2}=\frac{2v}{5}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The treated regression uses trial observations only, whereas among controls in \(I_v\) the external share is \(B\).  Therefore
\[
 m_{n,k,1}(x)=x,
 \qquad
 m_{n,k,0}(x)=x+B\Delta_n\quad(x\in I_v),
 \tag{11}
\]
and both regressions equal \(x\) off \(I_v\).  The residual terms in \(E_{P_{n,k}}\eta_{n,k}\) have conditional mean zero by the definitions of these regressions.  Since a trial observation lies in \(I_v\) with probability \(v/2\), (8)--(11) give
\[
 T_{n,k}
 =E\{m_{n,k,1}(X)-m_{n,k,0}(X)\mid R=1\}
 =-\frac{vB}{2}\Delta_n
 =-A(v)\Delta_n.
 \tag{12}
\]
The causal trial effect is
\[
 \tau_n=E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid R=1\}=E(E_1-E_0)=0,
\]
so (12) and the definition of \(\beta_{n,k}\) imply
\[
 b_{n,k}=-A(v)\frac{a_n}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad \beta_{n,k}=-A(v)a_n.
 \tag{13}
\]

For the variance coordinate, first set \(\Delta_n=0\).  Substitution of (8)--(11) into \(\psi_{n,k}=p(v)^{-1}W_{n,k}(\eta_{n,k}-T_{n,k})\) shows that the coefficient of \(E_1\) on a treated trial record is four.  The coefficient of \(E_0\) on a control trial record is \(-4/5\) on \(I_v\) and \(-4\) off \(I_v\), while the external residual is zero.  These noise terms are already conditionally centered.  Weighting the two conditional variances by \(P_n(R=1)=P_n(R=0)=1/2\) therefore gives
\[
 V(v)=4\sigma_1^2+4\sigma_0^2-\frac{48v}{25}\sigma_0^2.
 \tag{14}
\]

It remains to retain the order-\(n^{-1}\) variance caused by \(\Delta_n\).  Up to addition of a constant within the trial stratum, which does not change a conditional variance, the unit-\(\Delta_n\) trial coefficient is
\[
 G_{\mathcal R}
 =\mathbf 1\{X\in I_v\}
 \left[-2B\mathbf 1\{A=1\}
 +2B(c_0-1)\mathbf 1\{A=0\}\right].
 \tag{15}
\]
Under the trial law, \(P_n(X\in I_v\mid R=1)=v/2\) and \(A\) is an independent fair coin.  Hence
\[
 \frac12\operatorname{Var}(G_{\mathcal R}\mid R=1)
 =\frac{32}{25}\left(\frac{17v}{50}-\frac{4v^2}{25}\right).
 \tag{16}
\]
For example, (16) follows by noting that the two nonzero values in (15) are \(-8/5\) and \(-24/25\), so that
\[
 E(G_{\mathcal R}^2\mid R=1)=\frac{544v}{625},
 \qquad
 E(G_{\mathcal R}\mid R=1)=-\frac{16v}{25}.
\]
For an external record, direct substitution in the control-residual term yields the unit-shift coefficient
\[
 d(v)\mathbf 1\{U\leq v\},
 \qquad
 d(v)=-\frac4{25}+\frac{4v}{5(1+v)}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Its weighted conditional variance is \(v(1-v)d(v)^2/2\).  The noise component in (14) has zero covariance with (15) conditional on the trial stratum because \(E(E_a\mid X,A,R=1)=0\), and there is no external noise component.  It follows that
\[
 \Gamma_n(k,k)=V(v)+\frac{a_n^2}{n}K(v),
 \tag{18}
\]
where
\[
 K(v)=
 \frac{32}{25}\left(\frac{17v}{50}-\frac{4v^2}{25}\right)
 +\frac12v(1-v)
 \left\{-\frac4{25}+\frac{4v}{5(1+v)}\right\}^{2}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Combining (13), (18), and the definition \(\rho_{n,k}=\beta_{n,k}^2+\Gamma_n(k,k)\) proves the exact identity
\[
 \rho_n(v)=V(v)+a_n^2A(v)^2+\frac{a_n^2}{n}K(v).
 \tag{20}
\]

Now take \(v_1=1/4\) and \(v_2=1/2\), and put \(k_{n,1}=n_{\mathcal E}/4\) and \(k_{n,2}=n_{\mathcal E}/2\).  These are strictly positive integers for \(n=8m\).  Equations (10), (14), and (19) give
\[
 V(v_1)-V(v_2)=\frac{12}{25}\sigma_0^2,
 \qquad
 A(v_2)^2-A(v_1)^2=\frac3{100},
\]
and
\[
 K(v_1)=\frac{12}{125},
 \qquad
 K(v_2)=\frac{944}{5625},
 \qquad
 K(v_2)-K(v_1)=\frac{404}{5625}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Substitution of (2) and (21) into (20) yields
\[
 \rho_{n,k_{n,2}}-\rho_{n,k_{n,1}}
 =-\frac{12}{25}\sigma_0^2
 +a_n^2\left\{\frac3{100}+\frac{404}{5625n}\right\}=0.
 \tag{22}
\]
The denominator in (2) is larger than \(3/100\), so \(0<a_n<4\sigma_0\), and (2) also gives \(a_n\to4\sigma_0\).  Since a norm dominates the absolute difference of its scalar coordinates, (13) implies
\[
 d_n(k_{n,1},k_{n,2})
 \geq |\beta_{n,k_{n,2}}-\beta_{n,k_{n,1}}|
 =\frac{a_n}{10}
 \longrightarrow\frac{2\sigma_0}{5}>0.
 \tag{23}
\]

We finally verify class membership.  The construction gives fixed-stratum independence, full-data consistency, conditional trial randomization, treatment propensity \(1/2\), and the two independent-uniform tie-label conditions.  On the negative trial support the source propensity is one.  On \([1,2]\) it is
\[
 \frac{\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}/2}
 {\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}/2+\lambda_{n,\mathcal E}}
 \geq\frac15,
\]
using \(1/3\leq\lambda_{n,\mathcal R}\leq2/3\); thus source overlap holds for the chosen \(\varepsilon\).  The outcomes are uniformly bounded.  The loss (3) is convex and twice continuously differentiable, its Hessian is the constant two, and (4) proves the score equation and curvature.  Its score and Hessian moments are uniformly bounded.  Because the gradient increment is the nonrandom constant \(2(\theta-\theta_n)\) and the Hessian is constant, both stated equicontinuity conditions hold identically.

Furthermore, (5) maps the external uniform variable affinely with slope
\[
 c_s=2E\{|X+E_0|\mid R=1,A=0\}\geq2(1-\sigma_0)>0.
\]
Therefore, for every \(u>0\) and every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 P_n\{|s_n(Z)-t|\leq u\mid R=0\}
 \leq \min\{1,2u/c_s\}
 \leq C u
\]
after enlarging \(C\), which verifies the score-margin condition.  For every fold and every prefix, the true piecewise formulas (8)--(11), with the analogous deterministic allocation weights off the subsequence, are measurable constant learners based on the training sigma-field and have zero \(L_2(P_n)\) error.  Taking \(r_n=(n+1)^{-1/3}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,r_n^2\log(en)\longrightarrow0,
\]
so the nuisance-estimability and nuisance-rate conditions hold.  Finally, (13) applies to every prefix on the subsequence, while the bias is zero off it.  Since \(0\leq A(v)\leq2/5\) and \(a_n<4\sigma_0\),
\[
 \sup_n\max_{k\in\mathcal K_n}|\sqrt n\,b_{n,k}|
 \leq\frac85\sigma_0<\infty,
\]
which is the local-misspecification condition.  Hence the constructed sequence belongs to \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{reg}}\), and therefore also to \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{legal}}\).

For this single legal sequence, (22) supplies an exact-risk pair in the maximization defining \(\omega^{\star}(0)\), while (23) supplies its distance.  Taking the limsup over all rows and retaining the subsequence gives
\[
 \omega^{\star}(0)
 \geq\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 d_{8m}(k_{8m,1},k_{8m,2})
 \geq\frac{2\sigma_0}{5}>0.
\]
This proves the asserted positive-prefix, anchor-loss counterexample to exact leading-risk tie invariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem supplies the requested anchor-exact inclusion bridge: the loss is the squared outcome-regression loss, the empirical influence score generates the ranking, both candidates are positive top-\(k\) prefixes, and the continuous score makes the auxiliary tie breaker inert. \textit{Gap.} The theorem refutes exact-risk tie invariance on the anchor-exact regular subclass; it does not determine the sharp excess modulus away from zero or the selected marginal law. \textit{Consumer.} It strengthens the exact-tie obstruction from the auxiliary no-borrowing witness to the candidate list actually used by the anchor selector.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The continuous-score boundary witness deliberately permits score-indexed outcome behavior oscillating at the empirical quantile scale; a density margin alone cannot exclude its non-Gaussian first-order term.  The smooth-localizable feasibility attempt adds finite-dimensional smooth score fitting, an interior density bounded above and away from zero, Hölder conditional moments, uniform nuisance entropy and rates, edge-risk exclusion, finitely many coercive wells, and nondegenerate joint noise.  Even this list does not imply differentiability of the fitted-score sublevel-set integral: pointwise score smoothness does not control the boundary trace as the fitted parameter moves.  A new primitive Hadamard or surface-differentiability condition and a proof of its uniform empirical remainder are needed before localization or multiplier calibration can begin.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved negative results remain logically separate.  One regular continuous-score sequence gives a deterministic interior prefix with a non-Gaussian rank-boundary contribution, invalidating the frozen \(\Gamma_n\)-Gaussian coupling and every covariance-only Gaussian repair.  A separate regular sequence gives an exact tie between two positive prefixes with nonvanishing ATE-coordinate distance, and the fully specified finite-support legal sequence gives an independent no-borrowing tie witness.  None proves that the MSE selector chooses the non-Gaussian coordinate, so no selected marginal law is established or refuted.  The conditional transfer lemma is not achievability for the generated path, and the multiplier definition is only a proposed handle with no current computability or coverage guarantee.  The smooth-localizable attempt fails at its first corrected-process gate; accordingly the sharp excess modulus, selected law, and tie-uniform interval remain open, and the current package is not a completed field-tier Stat contribution.
