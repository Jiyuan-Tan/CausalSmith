\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}absorbing\_\allowbreak{}four\_\allowbreak{}slice\_\allowbreak{}att\_\allowbreak{}recovery}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} This ExactID result uses four observed waves and fixed known \(2\leq J\leq m\).  Four corner slices determine the spectral basis; \(2m-1\) fixed selected anchor-star minors transport it across active anchors; and the remaining observable \(A_{xy}\) slices recover non-anchor transitions without additional rank requirements.  The map identifies active component sublaws, type contributions, at-risk effects, and the aggregate first-post ATT despite a common absorbing row.  No date-\(1\) absorption upgrades those objects to full type shares and full conditional effects, while an all-exit reallocation proves the sharp frontier.  Guarded inference is uniform for the explicit saturated four-wave multinomial functional, including singular correlation; restricted-model efficiency remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The exact-identification gap is an absorbing-boundary gap.  Existing full-primitive latent Markov reconstructions impose positivity, full-state rank, homogeneity, emission, or state-count conditions that do not cover a common absorbing row, and they do not separate identified active contributions from unidentified allocations of units already absorbed at the recovery cut.

\paragraph{Niche.} For fixed known \(J\leq m\), fixed active row and column selections, and an observable anchor star of \(2m-1\) nonsingular selected minors, four corner slices identify a common cycle-product basis.  The anchor star transports that basis over active anchors, while every non-anchor \(Q_j[x,y]\) is recovered from the corresponding unrestricted observable slice through a linear row-sum formula.  Stochastic normalization restores exit probabilities without requiring a full-rank absorbing row.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper supplies a constructive four-wave identifying map, proves its full-fiber active-versus-full causal frontier, verifies an exact rational \(J=m=2\) law and a generic initial-absorber counterexample, and derives an analytic saturated multinomial gradient with guarded uniform Gaussian-multiplier bands.  It is an ExactID contribution with a panel sampling design, not a regression or fixed-effect estimand theorem.

\section{Related work}

Ahn and Kasahara (2026), Proposition 14, is the closest theorem-level comparator.  Under their RSM, first-order M, and ID conditions, it identifies the full finite-mixture parameter \(\psi\) when \(T_0\geq k+1\), \(T\geq2(k+1)\), and \(J\leq|\mathcal X|^k\); \(k=1\) gives four periods and \(J\leq|\mathcal X|\).  Their ID(a) requires nonsingular factor matrices and distinct diagonal cycle values, ID(b) requires every relevant intermediate and terminal untreated transition probability to be strictly positive, and ID(c)--(d) require further invertible systems for the remaining primitive probabilities.  Their Proposition 15 adds TI-LT to identify latent-type ATT.  A common absorbing row violates ID(b) and the associated full-state rank architecture, so the present theorem is neither an application nor a subclass restatement.  Ahn--Kasahara has the broader full-primitive conclusion on its interior class; this paper fixes known \(J\leq m\), uses \(2m-1\) observable active anchor-star rank conditions and a simple common cycle signature, permits the singular absorbing row and zero non-anchor transitions, identifies active component sublaws, contributions, at-risk effects, and the aggregate ATT on the broad face, and identifies full shares and full conditional effects only on the no-date-\(1\)-absorption subface.

Higgins and Jochmans (2023) and Gupta, Kumar, and Vassilvitskii (2016) reconstruct Markov mixtures under their respective homogeneity, component-rank, state-count, and nondegeneracy regimes.  Their scaling and label arguments are prior architecture, but neither theorem supplies this time-varying causal active-sublaw map at a common absorbing boundary.  Gassiat, Cleynen, and Robin (2016), Theorem 1, identifies a finite-state nonparametric HMM with a known number of hidden states, linearly independent emission laws, and a full-rank hidden transition matrix from three consecutive observations, up to label permutation.  Allman, Matias, and Rhodes (2009), Theorem 6, generically identifies finite-HMM parameters from \(2k+1\) observations when their binomial hidden-state/emission-size condition holds.  In both HMM results the hidden state evolves and observations are conditionally independent emissions.  Here the latent type is time-invariant, the observed state is Markov conditional on type, kernels may vary with time and share a singular absorbing row, and the conclusion is causal active-sublaw and ATT identification with a sharp initial-absorber frontier; the model classes are not nested.

Deaner and Ku (2026) study aggregate duration difference-in-differences rather than latent-type multistate reconstruction.  Bonhomme, Jochmans, and Robin (2017) provide precedent for differentiating spectral estimators, but not the present projector, row-sum, pseudoinverse, and full-row transport functional.  Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2013, 2015) are neighboring multiplier-bootstrap and Gaussian anti-concentration references; the fixed-dimensional continuity argument used here is self-contained and allows singular correlation with positive reported variances.  Newey (1990) supplies only the general tangent-space motivation for the explicitly open restricted-model efficiency question because no restricted score chart is constructed here.  Andrews and Cheng (2012) concern weak and semi-strong identification regimes excluded by the \(\kappa\)-buffer and are not invoked in the regular proof.  Rao and Risch (2026) are an interpretation consumer only; this paper neither reanalyzes their data nor claims that their application satisfies the latent Markov, rank, support, or independent-sampling conditions.

  \item \texttt{W\_\allowbreak{}x\textasciicircum{}d} --- \texttt{selected predecessor intermediate}; \(J\times J\) real matrices; \texttt{pseudoinverse recovery intermediate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(W_x^d=A_{xy_0}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal S}(C_{y_0}^d)^\top\{C_{y_0}^d(C_{y_0}^d)^\top\}^{-1}\).
  \item \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}x\textasciicircum{}d} --- \texttt{state-\allowbreak{}specific stochastic normalizer}; \(\mathbb R^J\); \texttt{component scaling}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(c_x^d=(W_x^d)^{-1}m_x^{d,\mathcal U}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta\_\allowbreak{}J} --- \texttt{stacked contribution-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}aggregate ATT target}; \(\mathbb R^{(J+1)(m+1)}\); \texttt{reported target}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\theta_J=(\nu_1^\top,\ldots,\nu_J^\top,\tau^\top)^\top\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal L} --- \texttt{reported-\allowbreak{}coordinate set}; nonempty subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,(J+1)(m+1)\}\); \texttt{band index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The fixed coordinates of \(\theta_J\) reported jointly.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Phi} --- \texttt{off-\allowbreak{}model four-\allowbreak{}wave anchor-\allowbreak{}star contribution-\allowbreak{}recovery map}; a map from an open subset of the \(N_m\)-cell affine probability hyperplane to \(\mathbb R^{(J+1)(m+1)}\); \texttt{identifying functional}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The deterministic four-wave algorithm in \(\text{def:recovery-map}\), returning \(J\) unnormalized type-contribution vectors and their aggregate.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta} --- \texttt{guarded empirical-\allowbreak{}cell plug-\allowbreak{}in}; \(\mathbb R^{(J+1)(m+1)}\); \texttt{ATT estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\theta=\Phi(\widehat p)\) on \(\mathcal E_\kappa\).
  \item \texttt{J\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Phi\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{Jacobian of the four-\allowbreak{}wave recovery map}; \((J+1)(m+1)\times N_m\) real matrices; \texttt{pathwise derivative}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(J_{\Phi}(p)=D\Phi(p)\) on the analytic four-wave recovery domain.
  \item \texttt{G\_\allowbreak{}p} --- \texttt{saturated four-\allowbreak{}wave-\allowbreak{}model influence function}; maps from four-wave observed cells to \(\mathbb R^{(J+1)(m+1)}\); \texttt{canonical-\allowbreak{}gradient candidate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G_p(s)=J_{\Phi}(p)(e_s-p)\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Sigma(p)} --- \texttt{four-\allowbreak{}wave ATT covariance matrix}; positive-semidefinite \((J+1)(m+1)\times(J+1)(m+1)\) real matrices; \texttt{saturated information bound and limit covariance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\Sigma(p)=J_{\Phi}(p)\{\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\}J_{\Phi}(p)^\top=E_P[G_p(O)G_p(O)^\top]\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat G\_\allowbreak{}i} --- \texttt{estimated influence vector}; \(\mathbb R^{(J+1)(m+1)}\); \texttt{multiplier input}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat G_i=J_{\Phi}(\widehat p)(e_{O_i}-\widehat p)\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Sigma} --- \texttt{estimated ATT covariance matrix}; positive-semidefinite \((J+1)(m+1)\times(J+1)(m+1)\) real matrices; \texttt{studentization}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Sigma=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat G_i\widehat G_i^\top\).
  \item \texttt{T\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}star} --- \texttt{studentized Gaussian-\allowbreak{}multiplier maximum}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{simultaneous critical statistic}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(T^\star=\max_{\ell\in\mathcal L}|n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i\widehat G_{i,\ell}|/\sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat c\_\allowbreak{}\{1-\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{conditional multiplier critical value}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{band critical value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The conditional \(1-\alpha\) quantile of \(T^\star\).
  \item \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}ell,\allowbreak{}n\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{simultaneous confidence interval}; closed intervals in \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{reported band}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(I_{\ell,n}=[\widehat\theta_\ell\mathbin{-}\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}/n},\widehat\theta_\ell\mathbin{+}\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}/n}]\).
  \item \texttt{P\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}circ} --- \texttt{regular no-\allowbreak{}initial-\allowbreak{}absorption base law}; laws in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\); \texttt{counterexample input}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A regular base law satisfying \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon} --- \texttt{mixture weight}; \((0,1)\); \texttt{counterexample input}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{The probability assigned to the added all-\allowbreak{}exit path.}
  \item \texttt{h} --- \texttt{latent-\allowbreak{}type label}; \(\{1,\ldots,J\}\); \texttt{counterexample index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The type receiving the added all-exit mass in the first law, with \(h\neq k\).
  \item \texttt{k} --- \texttt{latent-\allowbreak{}type label}; \(\{1,\ldots,J\}\); \texttt{counterexample index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The type receiving the added all-exit mass in the second law, with \(k\neq h\).
  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon\textasciicircum{}A} --- \texttt{first generic outside-\allowbreak{}face law}; laws \(P\); \texttt{necessity counterexample}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The all-exit mass is assigned to type \(h\).
  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon\textasciicircum{}B} --- \texttt{second generic outside-\allowbreak{}face law}; laws \(P\); \texttt{necessity counterexample}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The all-exit mass is assigned to type \(k\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}zeta} --- \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}dimensional local parameter}; \texttt{a Euclidean space on a constant-\allowbreak{}rank support stratum}; \texttt{open efficiency parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Prospective coordinates for an explicit restricted observed-law parameterization \(p(\zeta)\).
  \item \texttt{S\_\allowbreak{}P} --- \texttt{restricted observed-\allowbreak{}law score matrix}; \texttt{finite real matrices}; \texttt{open efficiency score map}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(S_P=\operatorname{diag}(p)^{-1}\partial p(\zeta)/\partial\zeta^\top\) on a fixed positive support stratum of the observed-law image of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Pi\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}operatorname\{col\}\allowbreak{}(S\_\allowbreak{}P)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{weighted orthogonal score-\allowbreak{}space projection}; \texttt{linear operators on functions over the actual finite support}; \texttt{open efficiency projection}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The \(L_2(P)\)-orthogonal projection onto \(\operatorname{col}(S_P)\), when the explicit score matrix has been constructed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-panels]\label{ass:iid-panels}
The observations \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed with law induced by \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent panel sampling, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
For every \(t\), \(X_t=X_t(D)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-anticipation]\label{ass:no-anticipation}
\(X_t(1)=X_t(0)\) almost surely for every \(t\leq2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (No anticipation through the last pre-treatment date, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:two-types]\label{ass:two-types}
The latent variable \(Z\) has exactly the known support \(\{1,\ldots,J\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known finite latent-type count, \cite{HigginsJochmans2023MixturesDDC}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cohort-overlap]\label{ass:cohort-overlap}
\(P(D=d)>0\) for each \(d\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Treatment-cohort overlap, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:markov-paths]\label{ass:markov-paths}
Conditional on \((D,Z)\), each marginal potential path \((X_0(r),\ldots,X_3(r))\) is first-order Markov.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Type-conditional first-order Markov potential paths, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:absorbing-exit]\label{ass:absorbing-exit}
For each potential path, \(P(X_{t+1}(r)=e\mid X_t(r)=e,D,Z)=1\) whenever \(t<3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Absorbing outcome state, \cite{DeanerKu2026DurationDiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-date-one-absorption]\label{ass:no-date-one-absorption}
\(P(X_1=e\mid D=d)=0\) for each \(d\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This observable support-face restriction describes cohorts formed before any recorded exit, as with incumbent firms, enrolled students, or initially unemployed workers whose first analyzed spell begins while active.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-date-two-transition]\label{ass:common-date-two-transition}
For every \(j,x,y,d\), \(P(X_2(0)=y\mid X_1(0)=x,Z=j,D=d)=Q_j[x,y]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Type-specific transition independence at date two, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-first-post-untreated-transition]\label{ass:common-first-post-untreated-transition}
For every \(j,x,y,d\), \(P(X_3(0)=y\mid X_2(0)=x,Z=j,D=d)=U_j[x,y]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Type-specific transition independence at the first post-treatment date, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:four-slice-rank]\label{ass:four-slice-rank}
For every cohort \(d\),
\[
 \det M_{x_0y}^d\neq0\quad(y\in\mathcal A),
 \qquad
 \det M_{xy_0}^d\neq0\quad(x\in\mathcal A).
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Anchor-star selected-minor nonsingularity, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:simple-cycle-spectrum]\label{ass:simple-cycle-spectrum}
For every cohort \(d\), the \(J\) real eigenvalues \(\rho_1^d,\ldots,\rho_J^d\) of \(K^d\) are distinct, with \(g^d=\min_{j\neq k}|\rho_j^d-\rho_k^d|>0\), and are labelled in descending order.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Simple ordered cycle-product spectrum, \cite{AhnKasahara2026TransitionIndependence}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:margin-buffer]\label{ass:margin-buffer}
\(\min_k\mathfrak m_k(p)\geq2\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Compact separation from singular recovery boundaries, \cite{BonhommeJochmansRobin2017Nonexchangeable}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-buffer]\label{ass:variance-buffer}
\(\min_{\ell\in\mathcal L}\Sigma_{\ell\ell}(p)\geq2\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive marginal-variance condition allowing singular correlation, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2015AntiConcentration}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:face-model]\label{def:face-model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Broad fixed-\allowbreak{}known-\allowbreak{}J four-\allowbreak{}wave absorbing support model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\) is the set of laws \(P\) satisfying \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), \(\texttt{ass:no-anticipation}\), \(\texttt{ass:two-types}\), \(\texttt{ass:cohort-overlap}\), \(\texttt{ass:markov-paths}\), \(\texttt{ass:absorbing-exit}\), \(\texttt{ass:common-date-two-transition}\), \(\texttt{ass:common-first-post-untreated-transition}\), \(\texttt{ass:four-slice-rank}\), and \(\texttt{ass:simple-cycle-spectrum}\). Date-\(1\) absorption is allowed. The model concerns only the four waves \(X_0,\ldots,X_3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}no-\allowbreak{}anticipation}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}two-\allowbreak{}types}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}cohort-\allowbreak{}overlap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}markov-\allowbreak{}paths}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}absorbing-\allowbreak{}exit}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}common-\allowbreak{}date-\allowbreak{}two-\allowbreak{}transition}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}common-\allowbreak{}first-\allowbreak{}post-\allowbreak{}untreated-\allowbreak{}transition}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}four-\allowbreak{}slice-\allowbreak{}rank}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}simple-\allowbreak{}cycle-\allowbreak{}spectrum}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:compatible-fiber]\label{def:compatible-fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Broad compatible four-\allowbreak{}wave full-\allowbreak{}data fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let
\[
 N_m=2\sum_{r=0}^{4}m^r.
\]
Then \(\mathcal F(p)=\{\widetilde P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}:\widetilde P(O=s)=p_s\text{ for every one of the }N_m\text{ four-wave absorption-legal cells }s\}\). For \(m=2\), \(N_2=62\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{p}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal M\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{face\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:observed-slices]\label{def:observed-slices}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observed anchor-\allowbreak{}star slices and component-\allowbreak{}factor notation}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(x,y\in\mathcal A\), define
\[
 A_{xy}^d[u,v]=P(X_0=u,X_1=x,X_2=y,X_3=v\mid D=d),
 \qquad
 m_x^d[u]=P(X_0=u,X_1=x\mid D=d),
\]
for \(u\in\mathcal A\) and \(v\in\mathcal S\). For row set \(E\subseteq\mathcal A\) and column set \(F\subseteq\mathcal S\), write \(A_{xy}^{d,E,F}=A_{xy}^d[E,F]\) and \(m_x^{d,E}=m_x^d[E]\), and set \(M_{xy}^d=A_{xy}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\). Define
\[
 K^d=M_{x_0y_0}^d(M_{x_1y_0}^d)^{-1}M_{x_1y_1}^d(M_{x_0y_1}^d)^{-1}.
\]
For proof and recovery notation define
\[
 B_x^{d,\mathcal A}[u,j]=P(X_0=u,X_1=x,Z=j\mid D=d),\quad
 D_{xy}=\operatorname{diag}_{1\leq j\leq J}Q_j[x,y],
\]
\[
 C_y^d[j,v]=P(X_3=v\mid X_2=y,Z=j,D=d),
\]
and use superscripts \(\mathcal U\) and \(\mathcal V\) for the selected rows of \(B_x^{d,\mathcal A}\) and selected columns of \(C_y^d\), respectively. The equality \(M_{xy}^d=B_x^{d,\mathcal U}D_{xy}C_y^{d,\mathcal V}\) is a theorem consequence, not part of this definition.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{p}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:recovery-map]\label{def:recovery-map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Known-\allowbreak{}J anchor-\allowbreak{}star contribution recovery map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each cohort \(d\), order the eigenvalues of \(K^d\) decreasingly and set
\[
 P_j^d=\prod_{k\neq j}\frac{K^d-\rho_k^d I_J}{\rho_j^d-\rho_k^d},
 \qquad
 B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}=[P_1^dm_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U},\ldots,P_J^dm_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}].
\]
For every \(y\in\mathcal A\), set
\[
 F_y^d=(B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U})^{-1}A_{x_0y}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal S},\quad
 q_{x_0y}^d=F_y^d\mathbf 1_{m+1},\quad
 C_y^d=\operatorname{diag}(q_{x_0y}^d)^{-1}F_y^d.
\]
For every \(x\in\mathcal A\), set
\[
 W_x^d=A_{xy_0}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal S}(C_{y_0}^d)^\top
 \{C_{y_0}^d(C_{y_0}^d)^\top\}^{-1},\quad
 c_x^d=(W_x^d)^{-1}m_x^{d,\mathcal U},\quad
 B_x^{d,\mathcal U}=W_x^d\operatorname{diag}(c_x^d),
\]
and transport to every predecessor row by
\[
 B_x^{d,\mathcal A}=A_{xy_0}^{d,\mathcal A,\mathcal V}
 (C_{y_0}^{d,\mathcal V})^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(c_x^d).
\]
Finally set
\[
 q_{xy}^d=(B_x^{d,\mathcal U})^{-1}A_{xy}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal S}\mathbf 1_{m+1},
 \qquad s_j^d[x]=\mathbf 1_m^\top B_x^{d,\mathcal A}[:,j],\quad s_j^d[e]=0,
\]
complete \(\widehat Q_j^d\) from the active entries \(q_{xy}^d[j]\), the stochastic exit residual, and the absorbing row, and complete \(\widehat R_j^d\) from row \(j\) of each \(C_y^d\) and the absorbing row. Put
\[
 \widehat\alpha_j^d=\widehat s_j^d\mathbf 1_{m+1},\quad
 \widehat p_{2,j}^d=(\widehat\alpha_j^d)^{-1}\widehat s_j^d\widehat Q_j^d,
\]
\[
 \widehat\nu_j=\widehat s_j^1\widehat Q_j^1(\widehat R_j^1-\widehat R_j^0),
 \qquad \widehat\eta_j=\widehat\nu_j/\widehat\alpha_j^1,
\]
and
\[
 \Phi(p)=(\widehat\nu_1^\top,\ldots,\widehat\nu_J^\top,
 (\sum_{j=1}^J\widehat\nu_j)^\top)^\top.
\]
The same formulas, using \(\widehat Q_j^1\) in the contribution, define the off-model extension.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{p}; \texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}\{xy\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}d}; \texttt{m\_\allowbreak{}x\textasciicircum{}d}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:margin-vector]\label{def:margin-vector}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Complete four-\allowbreak{}wave known-\allowbreak{}J anchor-\allowbreak{}star recovery margin vector}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The finite vector \(\mathfrak m(p)\) contains the two cohort shares; every pairwise eigengap \(|\rho_j^d-\rho_k^d|\); the smallest singular values of all anchor-star matrices \(M_{x_0y}^d\) and \(M_{xy_0}^d\), all recovered \(B_x^{d,\mathcal U}\), all \(W_x^d\), all \(C_y^d(C_y^d)^\top\), and all \(C_y^{d,\mathcal V}\); the absolute values of every entry of \(q_{x_0y}^d\) and \(c_x^d\) used in a division; every recovered active mass \(\widehat\alpha_j^d\); and the descending-order label margins for the common untreated signatures. It is evaluated on the \(N_m\)-cell four-wave absorption-legal support and lists every denominator, inverse, eigengap, and label margin used by \(\Phi\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{p}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Phi}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:buffered-class]\label{def:buffered-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Compact buffered four-\allowbreak{}wave known-\allowbreak{}J recovery class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[
 \mathcal P_\kappa=\left\{P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}:
 \min_k\mathfrak m_k(p)\geq2\kappa,\quad
 \min_{\ell\in\mathcal L}\Sigma_{\ell\ell}(p)\geq2\kappa\right\},
\]
with closure taken inside the finite-parameter compatible image on the \(N_m\)-cell four-wave absorption-legal support.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}margin-\allowbreak{}buffer}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}buffer}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:guarded-estimator]\label{def:guarded-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Guarded empirical-\allowbreak{}cell four-\allowbreak{}wave known-\allowbreak{}J contribution plug-\allowbreak{}in}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} On the \(N_m\)-cell four-wave absorption-legal support, evaluate the entries of \(\mathfrak m(\widehat p)\) sequentially, stopping before any inverse or division whose required margin is at most \(\kappa\); also require legal absorbing support and \(\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}>\kappa\) for all \(\ell\in\mathcal L\). This event is \(\mathcal E_\kappa\). On \(\mathcal E_\kappa\), set \(\widehat\theta=\Phi(\widehat p)\), \(\widehat G_i=J_\Phi(\widehat p)(e_{O_i}-\widehat p)\), and \(\widehat\Sigma=n^{-1}\sum_i\widehat G_i\widehat G_i^\top\); otherwise return no interval.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{O}; \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}kappa}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal L}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:multiplier-band]\label{def:multiplier-band}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Gaussian-\allowbreak{}multiplier simultaneous four-\allowbreak{}wave known-\allowbreak{}J contribution band}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Conditionally on the four-wave sample and \(\mathcal E_\kappa\), draw \(\xi_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1)\), form
\[
 T^\star=\max_{\ell\in\mathcal L}
 \frac{|n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i\widehat G_{i,\ell}|}
 {\sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}}},
\]
take its exact conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile \(\widehat c_{1-\alpha}\), and report
\[
 I_{\ell,n}=\left[\widehat\theta_\ell-\widehat c_{1-\alpha}
 \sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}/n},
 \widehat\theta_\ell+\widehat c_{1-\alpha}
 \sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}/n}\right]
\]
for the fixed coordinates \(\mathcal L\subseteq\{1,\ldots,(J+1)(m+1)\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat G\_\allowbreak{}i}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Sigma}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal L}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:exact-witness]\label{def:exact-witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Exact rational four-\allowbreak{}wave m=\allowbreak{}J=\allowbreak{}2 absorbing-\allowbreak{}face witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Specialize to \(m=J=2\), \(\mathcal A=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathcal U=\mathcal V=\mathcal A\), \(x_0=y_0=a\), and \(x_1=y_1=b\). Let \(P(D=1)=1/2\), \(\pi_1^d=\pi_2^d=1/2\), and let \(X_0\) be uniform on \(\mathcal A\) conditional on \((Z,D)\). With row and column order \((a,b,e)\), set
\[
H_1=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4&0\\1/4&3/4&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
H_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&2/3&0\\2/3&1/3&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
Q_1=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&3/10&1/10\\1/5&1/2&3/10\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&2/5&2/5\\3/10&3/5&1/10\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
V_1=\begin{pmatrix}7/10&1/5&1/10\\1/5&3/5&1/5\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
V_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&1/2&3/10\\1/4&1/2&1/4\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:face-recovery]\label{thm:face-recovery}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\) satisfying \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\), the conclusions of \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) upgrade throughout \(\mathcal F(p)\) to
\[
 \pi_j^d=\alpha_j^d>0,\qquad \tau_j=\eta_j=\nu_j/\pi_j^1.
\]
Thus the same formulas recover the full type masses, the complete component law of \((Z,X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)\) in every cohort, every full conditional type effect, and the aggregate \(\tau\), in the common descending signature order. The result requires neither time homogeneity nor a date-\(4\) common untreated kernel and permits the rank-deficient common absorbing row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\) as in the statement and \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal F(p)\).  Cohort overlap makes conditioning on either cohort well defined.  Equality of the observed four-wave cell vector throughout \(\mathcal F(p)\), together with no date-\(1\) absorption for \(P\), gives
\[
 \widetilde P(X_1=e\mid D=d)=P(X_1=e\mid D=d)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(Z\) has the finite support \(\{1,\ldots,J\}\), nonnegativity and (1) imply
\[
 \widetilde P(Z=j,X_1=e\mid D=d)=0
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(j,d\).  Therefore, by the definitions of \(\pi_j^d\) and \(\alpha_j^d\),
\[
 \widetilde\pi_j^d
 =\widetilde P(Z=j,X_1\in\mathcal A\mid D=d)
 =\widetilde\alpha_j^d.
\tag{3}
\]
The theorem \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) identifies the right-hand side throughout the fiber and derives its strict positivity from anchor-star nonsingularity.  Applying that theorem to \(P\) and \(\widetilde P\) yields
\[
 \widetilde\pi_j^d=\widetilde\alpha_j^d
 =\alpha_j^d=\pi_j^d>0.
\tag{4}
\]

The same theorem recovers the complete unnormalized component law on \(\{X_1\in\mathcal A\}\).  Equations (1)--(4) show that this event has conditional probability one in every positive type--cohort cell.  Hence the recovered sublaw is the complete component law of \((Z,X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)\) in each cohort, and division by the positive mass in (4) recovers its type-conditional version.

Let
\[
 \Delta=\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(1))-\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(0)).
\]
Conditioning additionally on \(X_1\in\mathcal A\) removes a null event within \(\{D=1,Z=j\}\).  The causal definitions and the active-frontier identity therefore give
\[
 \tau_j=E_P(\Delta\mid D=1,Z=j)
 =E_P(\Delta\mid D=1,Z=j,X_1\in\mathcal A)
 =\eta_j=\frac{\nu_j}{\alpha_j^1}
 =\frac{\nu_j}{\pi_j^1}.
\tag{5}
\]
The aggregate equality \(\tau=\sum_j\nu_j\) and \(\Phi(p)=\theta_J\) follow from \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\).  Its construction inverts only selected active-state minors and full-row-rank terminal factors; the absorbing row is fixed by absorption and may be rank deficient.  No equality across dates is used, so time homogeneity is unnecessary.  Every input is a marginal of \((D,X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)\), and no later date is required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corollary proves exactly what the observable no-date-\(1\)-absorption subface adds: every recovered active mass is the positive full type mass, and every at-risk effect is the full conditional type effect. \textit{Gap.} Interior full-primitive reconstruction does not establish this subface upgrade while permitting the structurally singular absorbing row and using only selected active \(J\)-minors. \textit{Consumer.} Full latent-type conditional effects are justified only after the observed four-wave law verifies absence of absorption at the date-\(1\) recovery cut.

\begin{proposition}[prop:exact-boundary-witness]\label{prop:exact-boundary-witness}

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}

\[
 (1-\varepsilon)P^\circ(X_{0:3}\in\cdot\mid D=d)
 +\varepsilon\delta_{(e,e,e,e)}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the observed law is independent of whether the added atom receives label \(h\) or label \(k\).  The base law has no date-\(1\) absorbers.  Absorption then implies that it has no mass at \(X_0=e\).  Conditional on \((D,Z)\), the retained and added components are separated by the current state until absorption: an active current state can arise only from the retained component, while at \(e\) both components move deterministically to \(e\).  Hence each marginal potential path remains first-order Markov.  Consistency, no anticipation, absorption, the two common untreated transitions, exact latent support, and cohort overlap are preserved.

Every active slice and active predecessor mass is multiplied by \(1-\varepsilon\).  Each required order-\(J\) determinant is consequently multiplied by \((1-\varepsilon)^J\ne0\).  In the four-factor product defining \(K^d\), the two scalar factors and the two inverse scalar factors cancel.  Thus \(K^d\), its simple spectrum, and its labels are unchanged, and both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\).

For every type, the active quantities obey
\[
 \alpha_j^d=(1-\varepsilon)\alpha_j^{\circ,d},\quad
 \nu_j=(1-\varepsilon)\nu_j^\circ,\quad
 \eta_j=\eta_j^\circ,\quad
 \tau=(1-\varepsilon)\tau^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]
The added all-exit path has zero causal contrast.  If \(a_j\in\{0,1\}\) indicates that type \(j\) receives the added mass, then

 \pi_j^d=(1-\varepsilon)\pi_j^{\circ,d}+\varepsilon a_j,
 \quad
 \tau_j=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\nu_j^\circ}
 {(1-\varepsilon)\pi_j^{\circ,1}+\varepsilon a_j}.
\tag{3}

The denominator is positive because \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) derives \(\pi_j^{\circ,d}>0\).  Reallocating the atom therefore changes the shares of \(h\) and \(k\), and changes the full conditional effect of every reallocated type with nonzero contribution, while leaving all active targets and the aggregate equal between the two allocations.

Marginalizing the other continuation yields the Markov factorizations \((H_j,Q_j,Q_j)\) for \(r=0\) and \((H_j,Q_j,V_j)\) for \(r=1\).  The shared coordinates through date \(2\) establish no anticipation, \(X_t=X_t(D)\) establishes consistency, and (4) establishes absorption.  Also

so the latent support is exactly \(\{1,2\}\), cohort overlap holds, and the untreated kernels \(Q_j\) are common.  The active rows of \(H_j\) have zero exit probability, so \(P(X_1=e\mid D=d)=0\).

\tag{6}

for cohort \(0\), and

\begin{theorem}[thm:analytic-gradient]\label{thm:analytic-gradient}
For fixed \(m\) and known \(J\), the generalized map \(\Phi\) extends real analytically to a relative open neighborhood of every regular \(p\). In the saturated finite-multinomial model on its actual absorption-legal support stratum,
\[
 G_p(s)=J_\Phi(p)(e_s-p)
\]
is the canonical gradient of \(\Phi(p)\), \(\Sigma(p)\) is the information bound, and the empirical-cell plug-in attains that bound at every relative-interior law on the stratum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(q_d(p)=\sum_{s:D(s)=d}p_s\).  Cohort overlap gives \(q_d(p)>0\).  Every entry of \(A_{xy}^d\) and \(m_x^d\) is a linear marginal of the \(N_m\)-cell four-wave vector \(p\), divided by \(q_d(p)\), and is therefore real analytic near a regular law.  The proof of \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) shows that every inverse and scalar division in \(\Phi\) has a nonzero determinant or denominator there.

We verify analyticity of the nonsymmetric spectral step.  Fix a regular matrix \(K_0\), and surround each distinct real eigenvalue by a disjoint positively oriented complex circle \(\gamma_j\).  Uniformly for \(z\in\gamma_j\) and all sufficiently small real perturbations \(E\),
\[
 (zI_J-K_0-E)^{-1}
 =\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\{(zI_J-K_0)^{-1}E\}^r(zI_J-K_0)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]
The series converges uniformly because the circles are compact subsets of the resolvent set of \(K_0\).  Termwise integration shows that
\[
 \mathcal P_j(K_0+E)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}
 \int_{\gamma_j}(zI_J-K_0-E)^{-1}\,dz
\tag{2}
\]
is a convergent power series in the entries of \(E\).  A matrix with distinct eigenvalues is diagonalizable; substituting its partial-fraction resolvent into (2) shows that \(\mathcal P_j\) is its rank-one Lagrange projector.  Moreover,
\[
 \rho_j(K)=\operatorname{tr}\{K\mathcal P_j(K)\}
\tag{3}
\]
is the unique eigenvalue inside \(\gamma_j\) and is analytic.  For real \(K\) near \(K_0\), uniqueness and conjugate pairing imply that \(\rho_j(K)\) remains real; shrinking the neighborhood preserves descending order.  Hence
\[
 P_j=\prod_{k\ne j}\frac{K-\rho_kI_J}{\rho_j-\rho_k}
\tag{4}
\]
is real analytic on the simple-spectrum domain.  Matrix inversion is analytic by the adjugate-over-determinant formula, and
\[
 C^\dagger=C^\top(CC^\top)^{-1}
\tag{5}
\]
is analytic wherever \(C\) has full row rank.  Thus the finite composition in \(\texttt{def:recovery-map}\) extends real analytically to a relative open neighborhood in the affine hyperplane \(\mathbf1^\top p=1\).

Its Jacobian is explicitly computable.  Let \(h\) be an affine tangent direction, \(\mathbf1^\top h=0\), and use a dot for the directional derivative.  Cohort conditioning gives
\[
 \dot q_d=\sum_{s:D(s)=d}h_s,
 \quad
 \left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}
 \frac{p_s+\epsilon h_s}{q_d(p+\epsilon h)}\right|_{\epsilon=0}
 =\frac{h_s}{q_d}-\frac{p_s\dot q_d}{q_d^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Differentiating \(MM^{-1}=I\) gives \(\dot M^{-1}=-M^{-1}\dot M M^{-1}\).  Differentiating the spectral identities gives
\[
 \dot\rho_j=\operatorname{tr}(P_j\dot K),
 \quad
 \dot P_j=\sum_{k\ne j}
 \frac{P_k\dot K P_j+P_j\dot K P_k}{\rho_j-\rho_k}.
\tag{7}
\]
The first recovery stages satisfy
\[
 \dot B_{x_0}^{\mathcal U}[:,j]
 =\dot P_jm_{x_0}^{\mathcal U}+P_j\dot m_{x_0}^{\mathcal U},
 \quad
 \dot F_y=-B_{x_0}^{-1}\dot B_{x_0}F_y+B_{x_0}^{-1}\dot A_{x_0y},
 \quad
 \dot q_{x_0y}=\dot F_y\mathbf1_{m+1},
\tag{8}
\]
and row normalization gives
\[
 \dot C_y[j,:]
 =\frac{\dot F_y[j,:]-\dot q_{x_0y}[j]C_y[j,:]}{q_{x_0y}[j]}.
\tag{9}
\]
Writing \(N=(C_{y_0}C_{y_0}^\top)^{-1}\), differentiation of (5) gives
\[
 \dot C_{y_0}^\dagger
 =\dot C_{y_0}^\top N-C_{y_0}^\top N
 (\dot C_{y_0}C_{y_0}^\top+C_{y_0}\dot C_{y_0}^\top)N.
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \dot W_x=\dot A_{xy_0}C_{y_0}^\dagger+A_{xy_0}\dot C_{y_0}^\dagger,
 \quad
 \dot c_x=W_x^{-1}(\dot m_x-\dot W_xc_x),
\tag{11}
\]
\[
 \dot B_x^{\mathcal U}
 =\dot W_x\operatorname{diag}(c_x)+W_x\operatorname{diag}(\dot c_x).
\tag{12}
\]
The product rule applied to
\[
 B_x^{\mathcal A}=A_{xy_0}^{\mathcal A,\mathcal V}
 (C_{y_0}^{\mathcal V})^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(c_x)
\]
and then to the recovered \(Q_j,R_j^d,s_j^d\) supplies their derivatives.  Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot\nu_j
 &=\dot s_j^1Q_j(R_j^1-R_j^0)+s_j^1\dot Q_j(R_j^1-R_j^0)\\
 &\quad+s_j^1Q_j(\dot R_j^1-\dot R_j^0),
 \quad
 \dot\tau=\sum_{j=1}^J\dot\nu_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (6)--(13) determine \(J_\Phi(p)h\), including the derivative of every active mass and every transported predecessor factor.

Let \(\mathcal S_0\) be the actual finite support stratum and suppose \(p_s>0\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S_0\).  The tangent scores of the saturated multinomial model are exactly the functions \(a\) with \(E_Pa(O)=0\): for every such function, finiteness of \(\mathcal S_0\) ensures that \(p_{\epsilon,s}=p_s\{1+\epsilon a(s)\}\) is a probability vector for all sufficiently small \(|\epsilon|\).  Along this path,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Phi(p_\epsilon)\right|_0
 &=J_\Phi(p)\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_0}p_se_sa(s)\\
 &=E_P\!\left[J_\Phi(p)(e_O-p)a(O)\right]
 =E_P\{G_p(O)a(O)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Also \(E_PG_p(O)=0\).  Since the saturated tangent space is all of \(L_2^0(P)\), (14) proves that \(G_p\) is the canonical gradient.  Its covariance is
\[
 E_P[G_p(O)G_p(O)^\top]
 =J_\Phi(p)\{\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\}J_\Phi(p)^\top
 =\Sigma(p).
\tag{15}
\]
If a centered influence vector \(H\) represents the same derivative, then (14) implies \(E_P\{(H-G_p)a\}=0\) for every \(a\in L_2^0(P)\).  Taking each coordinate of \(H-G_p\) as a score gives \(H=G_p\) almost surely.  Thus (15) is the saturated information bound.

Independent sampling gives the exact identity
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat p-p)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n(e_{O_i}-p).
\tag{16}
\]
Taylor expansion of the analytic map and \(\Phi(p)=\theta_J\) from \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) yield
\[
 \sqrt n\{\Phi(\widehat p)-\Phi(p)\}
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nG_p(O_i)+o_P(1).
\tag{17}
\]
The finite-support summands are centered and bounded.  For each fixed vector \(u\), their characteristic function satisfies
\[
 E_P\exp\{\mathrm i u^\top G_p(O)/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{u^\top\Sigma(p)u}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\tag{18}
\]
Raising (18) to the \(n\)-th power gives the characteristic function of \(N(0,\Sigma(p))\), including when \(\Sigma(p)\) is singular.  The same expansion along differentiable local multinomial paths supplies the deterministic derivative shift and regularity on the stratum.  Since all algebraic recovery margins are strictly positive at a regular law and \(\widehat p\to p\), the unguarded plug-in is defined with probability tending to one; whenever the reporting guard is applicable it agrees with that plug-in.  Equation (17) shows that the plug-in has canonical influence function \(G_p\), so it attains the saturated finite-multinomial information bound.  No efficiency assertion is made for the smaller restricted Markov observed-law image.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Analyticity turns the explicit four-wave known-\(J\) map into a regular empirical-cell estimator and yields its canonical gradient on each saturated finite-multinomial support stratum. \textit{Gap.} Bonhomme--Jochmans--Robin provide spectral-estimator precedent, but not the derivative or saturated canonical gradient of this absorbing-boundary projector, stochastic normalization, pseudoinverse, and full-row transport map. \textit{Consumer.} The four-wave Jacobian propagates uncertainty in active masses and all transported factors.  It supports the saturated covariance calculation only and does not claim restricted Markov efficiency.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-bands]\label{thm:uniform-bands}
For fixed \(m,J,\mathcal L,\kappa\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_\kappa\),
\[
 P(\mathcal E_\kappa)\to1,\qquad
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta_J)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nG_p(O_i)+o_P(1),
 \qquad \widehat\Sigma-\Sigma(p)=o_P(1).
\]
Allowing \(\Sigma(p)\) and its correlation matrix to be singular while retaining the buffered reported diagonal,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa}P\{\theta_{J,\ell}\in I_{\ell,n}
 \text{ for every }\ell\in\mathcal L\}\longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal K_\kappa\) be the set of \(N_m\)-cell four-wave vectors induced by \(\mathcal P_\kappa\).  It is compact by \(\texttt{def:buffered-class}\).  The finite vector in \(\texttt{def:margin-vector}\) contains every inverse, eigengap, normalization, division, selected-minor singular value, and label margin used by \(\Phi\), while the variance buffer controls every reported diagonal.  Population margins are at least \(2\kappa\), whereas the sequential empirical guard stops at \(\kappa\).

Analyticity from \(\texttt{thm:analytic-gradient}\), continuity of the finite margin list, and compactness give \(r>0\) such that the relative closed \(r\)-neighborhood of \(\mathcal K_\kappa\) lies in the analytic recovery domain and passes every algebraic and variance guard at level \(\kappa\).  On that compact neighborhood there are finite constants
\[
 L_1=\sup_q\|J_\Phi(q)\|<\infty,
 \quad
 L_2=\sup_q\|D^2\Phi(q)\|<\infty.
\tag{1}
\]
The empirical vector has legal absorbing support almost surely.  Direct multinomial calculation under independent sampling gives
\[
 E_P\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2
 =\frac{1-\|p\|_2^2}{n}\leq\frac1n.
\]
Markov's inequality therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa}
 P(\|\widehat p-p\|_2>r)\leq\frac1{nr^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
This proves \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa}P(\mathcal E_\kappa)\to1\), including the buffered empirical variance check.

Taylor's formula on the complement of the event in (2) gives
\[
 \left\|\sqrt n\{\Phi(\widehat p)-\Phi(p)
 -J_\Phi(p)(\widehat p-p)\}\right\|
 \leq\frac{L_2}{2}\sqrt n\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2.
\tag{3}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), Markov's inequality and the preceding second-moment formula bound the probability that the right side exceeds \(\epsilon\) by \(L_2/(2\epsilon\sqrt n)\), uniformly over the class.  Combining (3), the exact empirical-cell identity, and \(\Phi(p)=\theta_J\) proves
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta_J)
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nG_p(O_i)+o_P(1)
\tag{4}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_\kappa\).

The definition of \(\widehat G_i\) gives the exact finite-multinomial identity
\[
 \widehat\Sigma=J_\Phi(\widehat p)
 \{\operatorname{diag}(\widehat p)-\widehat p\widehat p^\top\}
 J_\Phi(\widehat p)^\top.
\tag{5}
\]
The right-hand side is uniformly continuous on the compact neighborhood used in (1).  Equation (2) therefore proves \(\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma(p)=o_P(1)\) uniformly.  In particular, every reported empirical diagonal exceeds \(\kappa\) with probability tending to one uniformly.  No covariance inverse is used.

It remains to establish simultaneous coverage when the correlation matrix is singular.  Take an arbitrary sequence \(P_n\in\mathcal P_\kappa\).  Compactness supplies a subsequence, retained in the notation, for which \(p_n\to p_\star\), and continuity gives \(\Sigma(p_n)\to\Sigma(p_\star)\).  For \(\ell\in\mathcal L\), define
\[
 \sigma_{n,\ell}^2=\Sigma_{\ell\ell}(p_n)\geq2\kappa,
 \quad
 Y_{n,i,\ell}=G_{p_n,\ell}(O_i)/\sigma_{n,\ell}.
\tag{6}
\]
The vectors \(Y_{n,i}\) are centered and uniformly bounded by (1) and the diagonal buffer.  After a further subsequence, their positive-semidefinite unit-diagonal correlation matrices \(\Gamma_n\) converge to a positive-semidefinite unit-diagonal matrix \(\Gamma_\star\), which may be singular.  For each fixed vector \(u\), boundedness permits a third-order Taylor expansion with a uniform remainder:
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp\{\mathrm i u^\top Y_{n,1}/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{u^\top\Gamma_nu}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\tag{7}
\]
Independence raises (7) to the \(n\)-th power, which converges to \(\exp(-u^\top\Gamma_\star u/2)\).  Hence
\[
 n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nY_{n,i}
 \Longrightarrow Z_\star\sim N(0,\Gamma_\star),
\tag{8}
\]
including singular \(\Gamma_\star\).

Conditionally on the sample, the multiplier numerator is exactly Gaussian.  On \(\mathcal E_\kappa\), its studentized correlation matrix is
\[
 \widehat\Gamma_{\ell k}
 =\frac{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell k}}
 {\sqrt{\widehat\Sigma_{\ell\ell}\widehat\Sigma_{kk}}}.
\tag{9}
\]
Covariance consistency and the positive diagonal imply \(\widehat\Gamma\to\Gamma_\star\) in \(P_n\)-probability.  The established lemma \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-max-quantile-continuity}\) applies to positive-semidefinite unit-diagonal matrices, including singular limits.  If \(c_\star\) is the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\max_{\ell\in\mathcal L}|Z_{\star,\ell}|\), its sequential continuity, also applied to random convergent subsequences, gives
\[
 \widehat c_{1-\alpha}\to c_\star,
 \quad
 P\!\left(\max_{\ell\in\mathcal L}|Z_{\star,\ell}|\leq c_\star\right)
 =1-\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]
The equality uses the continuity and strict increase in that lemma.

Uniform linearization (4), covariance consistency (5), and the lower bound in (6) show that replacing the studentized linear term and its population standard deviations by the plug-in quantities changes the reported maximum by \(o_{P_n}(1)\).  The limit distribution in (10) is continuous, so the simultaneous coverage probability along the selected subsequence converges to \(1-\alpha\).  If this convergence were not uniform in absolute difference, a sequence of laws would remain a fixed positive distance from \(1-\alpha\); compactness would provide a subsequence to which the preceding argument applies, a contradiction.  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa}
 P\{\theta_{J,\ell}\in I_{\ell,n}\text{ for every }\ell\in\mathcal L\}
 \longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\tag{11}
\]
Only positive reported marginal variances were used; neither \(\Sigma(p)\) nor its correlation matrix was inverted or assumed nonsingular.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem combines the buffered four-wave empirical guard with fixed-dimensional Gaussian-max quantile continuity at singular correlation limits to obtain uniform simultaneous inference. \textit{Gap.} Multiplier-bootstrap and anti-concentration results alone do not prove uniform guard acceptance, Taylor control, and covariance consistency for this rational spectral recovery map. \textit{Consumer.} Users can jointly report all \(J\) contribution vectors and their aggregate even when exact state-sum and aggregation identities make the reported correlation matrix singular.

\begin{lemma}[lem:gaussian-max-quantile-continuity]\label{lem:gaussian-max-quantile-continuity}
Fix a positive integer \(q\).  Let \(\Gamma_n\) and \(\Gamma\) be positive-semidefinite \(q\times q\) matrices with unit diagonal and \(\Gamma_n\to\Gamma\).  If \(Z_n\sim N(0,\Gamma_n)\) and \(Z\sim N(0,\Gamma)\), allowing every matrix to be singular, then the distribution function of \(\max_{1\leq k\leq q}|Z_k|\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\), and the \(u\)-quantiles of \(\max_k|Z_{n,k}|\) converge to its \(u\)-quantile for every \(u\in(0,1)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(L=\operatorname{range}(\Gamma^{1/2})\).  Since every diagonal entry of \(\Gamma\) is one, \(L\neq\{0\}\), and the law of \(Z\) has a strictly positive Gaussian density with respect to Lebesgue measure on \(L\).  The restriction of \(x\mapsto\|x\|_\infty\) to \(L\) is a norm.  For \(0\leq s<t\), choose nonzero \(x\in L\) and rescale it so that \(s<\|x\|_\infty<t\).  The set
\[
 \{y\in L:s<\|y\|_\infty<t\}
\]
contains a nonempty relatively open subset of \(L\), and hence has strictly positive Gaussian probability.  Thus the distribution function of \(\|Z\|_\infty\) is strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\).

For every \(t\geq0\),
\[
 \{\|Z\|_\infty=t\}\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^q\{|Z_k|=t\}.
\]
Every marginal \(Z_k\) is \(N(0,1)\), even when the joint covariance is singular, so the finite union has probability zero.  The distribution function is continuous, starts at zero, and tends to one.  Its \(u\)-quantile \(c_u(\Gamma)\) is therefore unique for every \(u\in(0,1)\).

For every \(v\in\mathbb R^q\),
\[
 E\exp(i v^\top Z_n)=\exp(-v^\top\Gamma_nv/2)
 \longrightarrow\exp(-v^\top\Gamma v/2)=E\exp(i v^\top Z).
\]
Thus \(Z_n\) converges in distribution to \(Z\), including when \(\Gamma\) is singular.  Continuity of the maximum norm implies convergence in distribution of the maxima.  If \(F_n,F\) are their distribution functions and \(c=c_u(\Gamma)\), strict increase and continuity give, for every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 F(c-\varepsilon)<u<F(c+\varepsilon).
\]
Convergence at the two continuity points makes the same inequalities true for \(F_n\) for all large \(n\), whence
\[
 c-\varepsilon<c_u(\Gamma_n)<c+\varepsilon.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves quantile convergence without a lower eigenvalue bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:active-frontier-recovery]\label{thm:active-frontier-recovery}
Fix \(m=|\mathcal A|\) and a known \(J\) with \(2\leq J\leq m\). For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\), the anchor-star recovery map is well-defined and
\[
 \Phi(p)=\theta_J=(\nu_1^\top,\ldots,\nu_J^\top,\tau^\top)^\top
 \in\mathbb R^{(J+1)(m+1)}.
\]
Throughout \(\mathcal F(p)\), in the common descending cycle-product order, the formulas recover \(\alpha_j^d>0\), \(s_j^d\), every active row of \(Q_j\) and \(R_j^d\), the complete unnormalized component law of \((Z,X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)\) on \(\{X_1\in\mathcal A\}\),
\[
 \nu_j=s_j^1Q_j(R_j^1-R_j^0),\qquad
 \eta_j=\nu_j/\alpha_j^1,
 \qquad \tau=\sum_{j=1}^J\nu_j.
\]
Moreover \(\pi_j^d\geq\alpha_j^d>0\) is derived from anchor-star nonsingularity, rather than assumed. Four anchor slices determine the spectral basis, while the \(2m-1\) observable anchor-star slices transport recovery to all active states. Units with \(X_1=e\) have zero treatment effect by no anticipation and absorption. Without the no-date-\(1\)-absorption condition, the theorem does not identify \(\pi_j^d\) or the full conditional \(\tau_j\). No time homogeneity is required. Only four waves \(X_0,\ldots,X_3\) are required; if a fifth wave is observed it may be marginalized out.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\) and \(d\in\{0,1\}\).  By \(\texttt{ass:cohort-overlap}\), \(P(D=d)>0\), so all cohort-conditional probabilities below are defined.  For \(x,y\in\mathcal A\), the factors introduced in \(\texttt{def:observed-slices}\) have dimensions
\[
 B_x^{d,\mathcal A}\in\mathbb R_+^{m\times J},\quad
 D_{xy}\in\mathbb R_+^{J\times J},\quad
 C_y^d\in\mathbb R_+^{J\times(m+1)},
 \tag{1}
\]
and stochasticity gives \(C_y^d\mathbf1_{m+1}=\mathbf1_J\).  Consistency and no anticipation identify the observed coordinates through date \(2\) with the corresponding coordinates of the realized potential path.  Conditional first-order Markov factorization and \(\texttt{ass:common-date-two-transition}\) then give, for \(u,x,y\in\mathcal A\) and \(v\in\mathcal S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_{xy}^d[u,v]
 &=\sum_{j=1}^J P(X_0=u,X_1=x,Z=j\mid D=d)Q_j[x,y]
 P(X_3=v\mid X_2=y,Z=j,D=d)\\
 &=\sum_{j=1}^J B_x^{d,\mathcal A}[u,j]Q_j[x,y]C_y^d[j,v].
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently
\[
 A_{xy}^d=B_x^{d,\mathcal A}D_{xy}C_y^d,
 \quad
 M_{xy}^d=B_x^{d,\mathcal U}D_{xy}C_y^{d,\mathcal V}.
\tag{3}
\]

Every factor in the second equality of (3) is square of order \(J\).  The conditions \(\det M_{x_0y}^d\ne0\) for every \(y\in\mathcal A\) imply that \(B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}\), every \(D_{x_0y}\), and every \(C_y^{d,\mathcal V}\) are nonsingular.  The conditions \(\det M_{xy_0}^d\ne0\) for every \(x\in\mathcal A\) imply that every \(B_x^{d,\mathcal U}\) and every \(D_{xy_0}\) are nonsingular.  Since the diagonal entries of \(D_{xy}\) are transition probabilities,
\[
 Q_j[x_0,y]>0,\quad Q_j[x,y_0]>0
 \quad(j=1,\ldots,J;\ x,y\in\mathcal A).
\tag{4}
\]
These conclusions justify every inverse and stochastic normalization below.  In particular, \(M_{x_1y_1}^d\) occurs in the cycle product but is not inverted and is not required to be nonsingular.

Substituting (3) into \(K^d\) and cancelling adjacent invertible factors yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 K^d
 &=B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}D_{x_0y_0}C_{y_0}^{d,\mathcal V}
 (B_{x_1}^{d,\mathcal U}D_{x_1y_0}C_{y_0}^{d,\mathcal V})^{-1}\\
 &\quad\cdot B_{x_1}^{d,\mathcal U}D_{x_1y_1}C_{y_1}^{d,\mathcal V}
 (B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}D_{x_0y_1}C_{y_1}^{d,\mathcal V})^{-1}\\
 &=B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}
 \operatorname{diag}_{1\le j\le J}\!\left{
 \frac{Q_j[x_0,y_0]Q_j[x_1,y_1]}
 {Q_j[x_1,y_0]Q_j[x_0,y_1]}
 \right}
 (B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U})^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The diagonal entries in (5) are the eigenvalues \(\rho_j^d\), and \(\texttt{ass:simple-cycle-spectrum}\) makes them distinct and fixes their descending order.  If \(E_j\) is diagonal with \(E_j[j,j]=1\) and all other entries zero, direct evaluation of the Lagrange polynomial at the diagonal matrix in (5) gives
\[
 P_j^d
 =\prod_{k\ne j}\frac{K^d-\rho_k^dI_J}{\rho_j^d-\rho_k^d}
 =B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}E_j(B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U})^{-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
Summing over the latent label in the predecessor probabilities gives \(m_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}=B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}\mathbf1_J\).  Hence
\[
 P_j^dm_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}=B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}[:,j].
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the projector formula fixes the eigenvector scale and sign and recovers the true \(B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}\).

Equations (2), (4), and (7) imply, for every \(y\in\mathcal A\),
\[
 F_y^d=D_{x_0y}C_y^d,\quad
 q_{x_0y}^d=F_y^d\mathbf1_{m+1}=D_{x_0y}\mathbf1_J.
\tag{8}
\]
Every coordinate of \(q_{x_0y}^d\) is positive by (4), so row-sum normalization recovers
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(q_{x_0y}^d)^{-1}F_y^d=C_y^d.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(C_{y_0}^{d,\mathcal V}\) is nonsingular, \(C_{y_0}^d\) has row rank \(J\), and \(C_{y_0}^d(C_{y_0}^d)^\top\) is positive definite.  Therefore (2) and (9) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_x^d
 &=A_{xy_0}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal S}(C_{y_0}^d)^\top
 \{C_{y_0}^d(C_{y_0}^d)^\top\}^{-1}\\
 &=B_x^{d,\mathcal U}D_{xy_0}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
This matrix is nonsingular.  Since \(m_x^{d,\mathcal U}=B_x^{d,\mathcal U}\mathbf1_J\),
\[
 c_x^d=(W_x^d)^{-1}m_x^{d,\mathcal U}
 =D_{xy_0}^{-1}\mathbf1_J>0,
 \quad
 W_x^d\operatorname{diag}(c_x^d)=B_x^{d,\mathcal U}.
\tag{11}
\]

When \(J<m\), the selected rows do not contain all predecessor masses.  Restricting (2) to rows \(\mathcal A\) and columns \(\mathcal V\), and using (9)--(11), yields the full-row transport identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_{xy_0}^{d,\mathcal A,\mathcal V}(C_{y_0}^{d,\mathcal V})^{-1}
 \operatorname{diag}(c_x^d)
 &=B_x^{d,\mathcal A}D_{xy_0}\operatorname{diag}(c_x^d)\\
 &=B_x^{d,\mathcal A}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
For arbitrary \(x,y\in\mathcal A\), equations (2), (9), and (11) now give
\[
 q_{xy}^d=(B_x^{d,\mathcal U})^{-1}
 A_{xy}^{d,\mathcal U,\mathcal S}\mathbf1_{m+1}
 =D_{xy}\mathbf1_J.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(q_{xy}^d[j]=Q_j[x,y]\), including possibly zero non-anchor transitions.  Row stochasticity identifies
\[
 Q_j[x,e]=1-\sum_{y\in\mathcal A}Q_j[x,y],
\tag{14}
\]
and absorption supplies the row at \(e\).  Equation (9) identifies every active row of \(R_j^d\), and absorption again supplies its row at \(e\).

Summing the recovered predecessor probabilities gives
\[
 s_j^d[x]=\mathbf1_m^\top B_x^{d,\mathcal A}[:,j]
 =P(Z=j,X_1=x\mid D=d),
 \quad
 \alpha_j^d=\sum_{x\in\mathcal A}s_j^d[x].
\tag{15}
\]
The nonnegative square matrix \(B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}\) is nonsingular, so each of its columns is nonzero and has a strictly positive column sum.  Consequently
\[
 \alpha_j^d\geq\mathbf1_J^\top B_{x_0}^{d,\mathcal U}[:,j]>0,
 \quad
 \pi_j^d=\alpha_j^d+P(Z=j,X_1=e\mid D=d)\geq\alpha_j^d>0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus type positivity follows from the observable rank condition.

Equations (2) and (8)--(15) recover the complete unnormalized component law on \(\{X_1\in\mathcal A\}\).  Indeed, absorption implies \(X_1\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow X_0\in\mathcal A\).  For \(u,x,y\in\mathcal A\) and \(v\in\mathcal S\), the component cell mass conditional on cohort \(d\) is
\[
 B_x^{d,\mathcal A}[u,j]Q_j[x,y]R_j^d[y,v],
\tag{17}
\]
whereas for \(X_2=e\) it is
\[
 B_x^{d,\mathcal A}[u,j]Q_j[x,e]\mathbf1\{v=e\}.
\]
In particular, (16) makes the at-risk date-\(2\) law well defined and equal to
\[
 P(X_2\in\cdot\mid D=d,Z=j,X_1\in\mathcal A)
 =\frac{s_j^dQ_j}{\alpha_j^d}.
\tag{18}
\]

The cycle signatures in (5) depend only on the cohort-common \(Q_j\), so their strict descending order aligns labels across cohorts.  By consistency and \(\texttt{ass:common-first-post-untreated-transition}\), \(R_j^0=U_j\) on active rows.  No anticipation makes the treated cohort's date-\(2\) state identical under the two potential paths, while the common untreated first-post restriction makes its untreated continuation equal to \(U_j=R_j^0\).  If \(X_1=e\), no anticipation and absorption imply \(X_3(1)=X_3(0)=e\), so that event has zero causal contrast.  Iterated expectation using (18) therefore proves, rather than defines, the causal identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \nu_j
 &=E\!\left[\mathbf1\{Z=j\}
 \{\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(1))-\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(0))\}
 \mid D=1\right]\\
 &=s_j^1Q_j(R_j^1-R_j^0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Conditioning the same calculation on \(X_1\in\mathcal A\) and using \(\alpha_j^1>0\) gives
\[
 \eta_j=\frac{\nu_j}{\alpha_j^1}.
\tag{20}
\]
Finite additivity over the known support \(Z\in\{1,\ldots,J\}\) gives
\[
 \tau=\sum_{j=1}^J\nu_j.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (19)--(21) are exactly \(\Phi(p)=\theta_J\).

All recovered expressions are functions only of the observed four-wave cell vector.  The same argument therefore applies to every \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal F(p)\), proving fiberwise identification.  Precisely four corner slices enter (5) and determine the spectral basis.  The anchor star contains \(2m-1\) distinct selected slices whose \(J\)-by-\(J\) minors are required to be nonsingular and which transport the basis to all active predecessor and successor anchors.  Recovery of each non-anchor \(Q_j[x,y]\) additionally evaluates the observable slice \(A_{xy}\) through (13); these non-star inputs are not among the \(2m-1\) rank conditions and need not be nonsingular.  No equality of kernels across dates was used.  Only \(X_0,\ldots,X_3\) are required, and an observed fifth wave may be marginalized out.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the field-level ExactID theorem: for fixed known \(J\leq m\), four corner slices identify the spectral basis, \(2m-1\) nonsingular selected anchor-star slices transport it across active anchors, and unrestricted observable \(A_{xy}\) slices recover all remaining active transitions. \textit{Gap.} Ahn--Kasahara Proposition 14 identifies a broader full primitive vector under RSM, M, and ID, but ID(b)'s strict transition positivity and its full-state rank architecture exclude the common absorbing row.  The present result supplies recovery on that boundary and separates active causal identification from the unidentified allocation of initial absorbers. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst may marginalize any fifth wave and report latent-type contributions, at-risk effects, and the aggregate ATT when the four-wave guard passes; full shares and full conditional effects additionally require no date-\(1\) absorption.
