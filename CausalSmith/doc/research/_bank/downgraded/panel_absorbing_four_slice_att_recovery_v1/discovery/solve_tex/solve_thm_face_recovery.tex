Let \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\) satisfy \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\), and let \(p\) be its observed cell-probability vector.  By \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\), the map \(\Phi\) is well-defined at \(p\), it recovers in descending \(\rho_j^d\) order the quantities \(\alpha_j^d\), \(s_j^d\), the active rows of \(Q_j\) and \(R_j^d\), the complete unnormalized component law on \(\{X_1\in\mathcal A\}\), the contributions \(\nu_j\), and the effects \(\eta_j=\nu_j/\alpha_j^1\), and it satisfies
\[
 \Phi(p)=\bigl(\nu_1^\top,\nu_2^\top,(\nu_1+\nu_2)^\top\bigr)^\top
        =\theta,
 \qquad
 \tau=\nu_1+\nu_2.                                      \tag{1}
\]

It remains to prove that the active-cut objects supplied by that theorem are the corresponding full-type objects on the stated face, uniformly over the compatible fiber.  Fix \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal F(p)\).  For each cohort \(d\), define its observed mass
\[
 \gamma_d:=\sum_{s:\,D(s)=d}p_s.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:cohort-overlap}\), which is among the hypotheses of \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\), \(\gamma_d>0\).  The definition \(\texttt{def:compatible-fiber}\) and equality of every observed cell probability therefore give
\[
 \widetilde P(X_1=e\mid D=d)
 =\frac{\sum_{s:\,D(s)=d,\,X_1(s)=e}p_s}{\gamma_d}
 =P(X_1=e\mid D=d)
 =0,                                                       \tag{2}
\]
where the last equality is \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\).  Thus the observable face restriction is inherited by every law in \(\mathcal F(p)\), even though it is not part of the broad model defining that fiber.

Because \(Z\) has exactly the two support points specified in \(\texttt{ass:two-types}\), finite additivity and (2) imply
\[
 0=\widetilde P(X_1=e\mid D=d)
  =\sum_{j=1}^2\widetilde P(Z=j,X_1=e\mid D=d).             \tag{3}
\]
Every summand in (3) is nonnegative, so each is zero.  Since \(\mathcal S=\mathcal A\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{e\}\), it follows for every \(j,d\) that
\[
 \widetilde\pi_j^d
 :=\widetilde P(Z=j\mid D=d)
 =\widetilde P(Z=j,X_1\in\mathcal A\mid D=d)
 =:\widetilde\alpha_j^d.                                  \tag{4}
\]
The active-frontier theorem identifies \(\widetilde\alpha_j^d\) as the common recovered value \(\alpha_j^d\) throughout \(\mathcal F(p)\).  The original law \(P\) itself belongs to \(\mathcal F(p)\), so applying (4) first to an arbitrary \(\widetilde P\) and then to \(P\) identifies the full type mass and yields
\[
 \widetilde\pi_j^d=\widetilde\alpha_j^d=\alpha_j^d=\pi_j^d
 \quad\text{for every }\widetilde P\in\mathcal F(p).       \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, \(\texttt{ass:type-overlap}\), again inherited from membership in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\), gives \(\widetilde\pi_j^d>0\).  Division in the next display is therefore legitimate, and (4) gives
\[
 \widetilde P(X_1\in\mathcal A\mid D=d,Z=j)
 =\frac{\widetilde\alpha_j^d}{\widetilde\pi_j^d}=1.       \tag{6}
\]

To make the component-law conclusion explicit, for a set \(E\subseteq\mathcal S^4\) define the finite measure
\[
 \widetilde\mu_{j,d}(E)
 :=\widetilde P\bigl(Z=j,(X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)\in E\mid D=d\bigr).
\]
By (3),
\[
 \widetilde\mu_{j,d}(E)
 =\widetilde\mu_{j,d}\bigl(E\cap\{X_1\in\mathcal A\}\bigr). \tag{7}
\]
The right-hand side of (7) is precisely the unnormalized active component law recovered, and shown invariant over \(\mathcal F(p)\), by \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\).  Consequently \(\widetilde\mu_{j,d}\) itself is invariant over the fiber.  Its total mass is the identified positive number \(\pi_j^d\) from (5), so division by that mass also recovers the full conditional law of \((X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)\) given \((D=d,Z=j)\).  This proves the claimed full component-path recovery through date \(3\).

Let
\[
 \Delta:=\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(1))-\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(0)).
\]
The vector is integrable because it has finite support.  Equation (6) with \(d=1\) shows that conditioning additionally on \(X_1\in\mathcal A\) removes only a conditional null event.  Hence, by the definitions of \(\tau_j\) and \(\eta_j\),
\[
 \widetilde\tau_j
 =\widetilde E[\Delta\mid D=1,Z=j]
 =\widetilde E[\Delta\mid D=1,Z=j,X_1\in\mathcal A]
 =\widetilde\eta_j.                                       \tag{8}
\]
The active-frontier theorem identifies \(\widetilde\eta_j=\nu_j/\alpha_j^1\); combining this fact with (5) gives the advertised full-type formula
\[
 \widetilde\tau_j=\frac{\nu_j}{\pi_j^1}.                  \tag{9}
\]
All denominators in (9) are nonzero by \(\texttt{ass:type-overlap}\).  Alternatively, the conditional-expectation partition over the two latent types gives, componentwise,
\[
 \widetilde\nu_j
 =\widetilde E[\mathbf 1\{Z=j\}\Delta\mid D=1]
 =\widetilde\pi_j^1\widetilde\tau_j,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\tau=\sum_{j=1}^2\widetilde\pi_j^1\widetilde\tau_j
 =\sum_{j=1}^2\widetilde\nu_j,                            \tag{10}
\]
which agrees with (1) and proves invariance of both type-specific and aggregate effects over the entire fiber.

The only additional premise used beyond \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) is \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\).  In particular, neither time homogeneity nor a common untreated date-\(4\) kernel enters any display.  Under \(\texttt{ass:absorbing-exit}\), the exit row is \((0,0,1)\), so strict all-state transition positivity fails; and if an all-state terminal factor is rank deficient, no new inverse is required here.  The active-frontier theorem establishes well-definedness using the declared active matched-slice ranks, simple cycle spectrum, stochastic normalizations, and full-row-rank pseudoinverse step.  Therefore its conclusion, and hence this corollary, continues to hold under those absorbing-boundary failures of all-state positivity or terminal rank.
