%%% FIELD prop-exact-boundary-witness-proof
Write all state vectors in the order \( (a,b,e) \).  We first construct the full-data law promised by \(\texttt{def:exact-witness}\), so consistency and the cross-world restrictions are verified rather than inferred from an observed factorization.  For \(d\in\{0,1\}\), \(j\in\{1,2\}\), \(s_0\in\mathcal A\), and \(s_1,s_2,u_0,w_0,u_1,w_1\in\mathcal S\), assign
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P\{D=d,Z=j,X_0(0)=X_0(1)=s_0,
X_1(0)=X_1(1)=s_1,X_2(0)=X_2(1)=s_2,\\
&\hspace{38mm}X_3(0)=u_0,X_4(0)=w_0,
X_3(1)=u_1,X_4(1)=w_1\}\\
&\quad =\frac18 H_j[s_0,s_1]Q_j[s_1,s_2]
Q_j[s_2,u_0]Q_j[u_0,w_0]
V_j[s_2,u_1]V_j[u_1,w_1].
\end{aligned}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
All other configurations have probability zero.  Every matrix displayed in \(\texttt{def:exact-witness}\) is entrywise nonnegative and, by direct row addition, satisfies
\[
H_j\mathbf 1_3=Q_j\mathbf 1_3=V_j\mathbf 1_3=\mathbf 1_3,
\qquad
H_j[e,{:}]=Q_j[e,{:}]=V_j[e,{:}]=(0,0,1).                    \tag{2}
\]
Successive summation of (1), using (2), gives total mass
\(8\cdot(1/8)=1\), because there are two choices each of \(d\), \(j\), and \(s_0\).  Thus (1) defines a probability law.  Define the observed process by \(X_t=X_t(D)\).  This is exactly \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), and the three common potential coordinates through date \(2\) give \(\texttt{ass:no-anticipation}\).  Marginalizing in (1) over the continuation belonging to the other treatment path shows that, conditional on \((D,Z)\), the transition sequence is \((H_j,Q_j,Q_j,Q_j)\) for \(r=0\) and \((H_j,Q_j,V_j,V_j)\) for \(r=1\).  Hence \(\texttt{ass:markov-paths}\) holds.  Equation (2) proves support on \(\mathcal G\) and \(\texttt{ass:absorbing-exit}\).  Moreover,
\[
P(D=d)=\frac12,
\qquad
P(Z=j\mid D=d)=\frac12,                                    \tag{3}
\]
so \(\texttt{ass:cohort-overlap}\), \(\texttt{ass:type-overlap}\), and \(\texttt{ass:two-types}\) hold.  Taking the date-\(2\) common kernel to be \(Q_j\) and the untreated first-post kernel to be \(U_j=Q_j\) verifies \(\texttt{ass:common-date-two-transition}\) and \(\texttt{ass:common-first-post-untreated-transition}\).  The construction uses no common date-\(4\) restriction.  Finally, \(X_0\in\mathcal A\) almost surely and the active rows of \(H_j\) have zero exit probability, whence
\[
P(X_1=e\mid D=d)=0.                                         \tag{4}
\]
Thus the witness also satisfies \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\).

It remains to verify the rank and spectral requirements in \(\texttt{def:face-model}\).  For \(x\in\mathcal A\), define
\[
B_x[u,j]=P(X_0=u,X_1=x,Z=j\mid D=d)=\frac14H_j[u,x].         \tag{5}
\]
This matrix does not depend on \(d\), and substitution gives
\[
B_a=\begin{pmatrix}3/16&1/12\\1/16&1/6\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B_b=\begin{pmatrix}1/16&1/6\\3/16&1/12\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(B_a)=\frac5{192},\quad \det(B_b)=-\frac5{192}.        \tag{6}
\]
Let \(D_{xy}=\operatorname{diag}(Q_1[x,y],Q_2[x,y])\).  Let row \(j\) of \(C_y^0\) be row \(y\) of \(Q_j\), and let row \(j\) of \(C_y^1\) be row \(y\) of \(V_j\).  The Markov factorization of (1) gives, entry by entry,
\[
A_{xy}^d[u,v]=\sum_{j=1}^2B_x[u,j]Q_j[x,y]C_y^d[j,v],
\qquad
A_{xy}^d=B_xD_{xy}C_y^d.                                    \tag{7}
\]
Writing \(\bar C_y^d=C_y^d[:,\mathcal A]\), direct calculation gives
\[
\det(\bar C_a^0)=\frac9{50},\quad
\det(\bar C_b^0)=-\frac3{100},\quad
\det(\bar C_a^1)=\frac{31}{100},\quad
\det(\bar C_b^1)=-\frac1{20}.                              \tag{8}
\]
Because
\[
\det(\bar A_{xy}^d)
=\det(B_x)Q_1[x,y]Q_2[x,y]\det(\bar C_y^d),                 \tag{9}
\]
equations (6)--(8) yield, in the order \((aa,ab,ba,bb)\),
\[
\left(\det\bar A_{aa}^0,\det\bar A_{ab}^0,
\det\bar A_{ba}^0,\det\bar A_{bb}^0\right)
=\left(\frac9{16000},-\frac3{32000},-\frac9{32000},
\frac3{12800}\right),                                      \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\left(\det\bar A_{aa}^1,\det\bar A_{ab}^1,
\det\bar A_{ba}^1,\det\bar A_{bb}^1\right)
=\left(\frac{31}{32000},-\frac1{6400},-\frac{31}{64000},
\frac1{2560}\right).                                       \tag{11}
\]
All eight determinants are nonzero.  Every active slice is therefore nonsingular; since there are finitely many slices, their positive smallest singular values have a positive minimum.  This is \(\texttt{ass:four-slice-rank}\).

Restricting (7) to active terminal columns and using the inverses justified by (6), (8), and the strictly positive active entries of \(Q_1,Q_2\), the cycle product reduces to
\[
\begin{aligned}
K^d
&=B_aD_{aa}\bar C_a^d(B_bD_{ba}\bar C_a^d)^{-1}
B_bD_{bb}\bar C_b^d(B_aD_{ab}\bar C_b^d)^{-1}\\
&=B_aD_{aa}D_{ba}^{-1}D_{bb}D_{ab}^{-1}B_a^{-1}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{12}
\]
The diagonal factor is
\[
D_{aa}D_{ba}^{-1}D_{bb}D_{ab}^{-1}
=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\frac{(3/5)(1/2)}{(1/5)(3/10)},
\frac{(1/5)(3/5)}{(3/10)(2/5)}\right)
=\operatorname{diag}(5,1).                                  \tag{13}
\]
Since \(B_a=48^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}9&4\\3&8\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), equations (12)--(13) imply, for both cohorts,
\[
K^d=\frac1{60}
\begin{pmatrix}45&4\\15&8\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}8&-4\\-3&9\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}29/5&-12/5\\8/5&1/5\end{pmatrix}.          \tag{14}
\]
Its trace is \(6\) and its determinant is \(5\), so
\[
\det(\lambda I_2-K^d)=\lambda^2-6\lambda+5
=(\lambda-5)(\lambda-1),
\qquad
g^d=\sqrt{6^2-4\cdot5}=4>0.                                \tag{15}
\]
Thus the ordered spectrum is \((5,1)\), and \(\texttt{ass:simple-cycle-spectrum}\) holds.  Equations (2)--(4), (10)--(11), and (15), together with the preceding structural checks, prove \(\omega^\star\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\).

We next compute the causal effects from the potential-path law.  The row vector \((1/2,1/2,0)\) is invariant under both \(H_1\) and \(H_2\).  Therefore the type-specific date-\(2\) laws in the treated cohort are
\[
p_{2,1}^1=(1/2,1/2,0)H_1Q_1=(2/5,2/5,1/5),
\qquad
p_{2,2}^1=(1/2,1/2,0)H_2Q_2=(1/4,1/2,1/4).                  \tag{16}
\]
The two paths in (1) share \(X_2\).  Conditional on \((X_2,Z,D=1)\), their date-\(3\) kernels are \(V_j\) and \(Q_j\), respectively.  Iterated expectation and the definition of \(\tau_j\) consequently give
\[
\tau_j=p_{2,j}^1(V_j-Q_j).                                  \tag{17}
\]
The exact products are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_1
&=(2/5,2/5,1/5)
\begin{pmatrix}1/10&-1/10&0\\0&1/10&-1/10\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
=(1/25,0,-1/25),\\
\tau_2
&=(1/4,1/2,1/4)
\begin{pmatrix}0&1/10&-1/10\\-1/20&-1/10&3/20\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
=(-1/40,-1/40,1/20).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{18}
\]
By (3), total expectation over \(Z\) gives
\[
\tau=\frac12\tau_1+\frac12\tau_2
=(3/400,-1/80,1/200).                                       \tag{19}
\]
Equation (4) implies \(\alpha_j^d=P(Z=j\mid D=d)=1/2\).  As there are no date-\(1\) absorbers under \(\omega^\star\), the causal definition of the unnormalized contribution yields
\[
\nu_j=P(Z=j\mid D=1)\tau_j=\frac12\tau_j.                 \tag{20}
\]
Using (18),
\[
\nu_1=(1/50,0,-1/50),
\qquad
\nu_2=(-1/80,-1/80,1/40).                                   \tag{21}
\]
Conditioning additionally on \(X_1\in\mathcal A\) changes nothing under (4), so \(\eta_j=\tau_j\).

We now analyze \(P^A\) and \(P^B\) from \(\texttt{def:outside-face-pair}\).  On the added component, set \(X_t(0)=X_t(1)=e\) at every date.  For either cohort and \(C\in\{A,B\}\), marginalizing the latent label gives
\[
P^C(X_{0:4}=x_{0:4}\mid D=d)
=\frac45P_{\omega^\star}(X_{0:4}=x_{0:4}\mid D=d)
+\frac15\mathbf 1\{x_{0:4}=(e,e,e,e,e)\}.                  \tag{22}
\]
The right-hand side does not depend on \(C\), so (22) proves equality of the complete observed five-date laws.

Both laws satisfy every condition defining the broad class.  Consistency and no anticipation hold on each mixture component.  Conditional on \((D,Z)\), the added component starts at \(e\) and follows the absorbing row, while every retained active path follows exactly the kernels in (1).  Thus, at an active current state, the all-exit component has conditional probability zero; at current state \(e\), both components move to \(e\) with probability one.  It follows directly that each marginal potential path remains first-order Markov, that exit remains absorbing, and that the common date-\(2\) and untreated first-post kernels remain \(Q_j\).  Cohort overlap is unchanged, and each type retains active mass \((4/5)(1/2)=2/5>0\), so type overlap holds.  The all-exit component belongs to none of the events defining an active slice.  Hence, for \(x,y\in\mathcal A\),
\[
A_{xy}^{C,d}=\frac45A_{xy}^{\omega^\star,d},
\qquad
\bar A_{xy}^{C,d}=\frac45\bar A_{xy}^{\omega^\star,d}.      \tag{23}
\]
The determinants in (10)--(11) remain nonzero after this scaling.  In the four-factor cycle product the scalar multipliers cancel because
\[
(4/5)(4/5)^{-1}(4/5)(4/5)^{-1}=1.                           \tag{24}
\]
Therefore both laws have the cycle product in (14), with the common simple ordered signature \(5>1\); in particular, the latent labels agree across the two laws.  This proves \(P^A,P^B\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\).  The added component also gives
\[
P^A(X_1=e\mid D=d)=P^B(X_1=e\mid D=d)=\frac15.              \tag{25}
\]

The retained active component contributes \(2/5\) to each type in each cohort.  Assigning the additional \(1/5\) to type \(1\) under \(P^A\) and to type \(2\) under \(P^B\) gives
\[
(\pi_1^{A,d},\pi_2^{A,d})=(3/5,2/5),
\qquad
(\pi_1^{B,d},\pi_2^{B,d})=(2/5,3/5),                       \tag{26}
\]
whereas in both laws
\[
\alpha_1^d=\alpha_2^d=\frac25.                              \tag{27}
\]
Define the causal contrast vector
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(1))-
\operatorname{onehot}(X_3(0)).
\]
On \(\{D=1,X_1=e\}\), consistency gives \(X_1(1)=e\), no anticipation gives \(X_1(0)=X_1(1)=e\), and absorption along each potential path gives \(X_3(0)=X_3(1)=e\).  Consequently,
\[
\Delta=0\quad\text{on }\{D=1,X_1=e\}.                     \tag{28}
\]
The added component therefore contributes zero.  For both \(C=A,B\), the retained component contributes \((4/5)(1/2)\tau_j^{\omega^\star}\), so
\[
\nu_j^C=E_C\{\mathbf 1\{Z=j\}\Delta\mid D=1\}
=\frac25\tau_j^{\omega^\star}.                            \tag{29}
\]
Using (18), this becomes
\[
\nu_1^A=\nu_1^B=(2/125,0,-2/125),
\qquad
\nu_2^A=\nu_2^B=(-1/100,-1/100,1/50).                       \tag{30}
\]
The denominators in (27) are positive.  Equations (28)--(29) and the definition of the at-risk effect therefore imply
\[
\eta_j^C
=E_C(\Delta\mid D=1,Z=j,X_1\in\mathcal A)
=\frac{\nu_j^C}{\alpha_j^1}
=\tau_j^{\omega^\star}.                                    \tag{31}
\]
This gives the two common at-risk vectors stated in the proposition.

For the full conditional effects, (28) and total expectation within type give
\[
\tau_j^C=E_C(\Delta\mid D=1,Z=j)
=\frac{\nu_j^C}{\pi_j^{C,1}}.                              \tag{32}
\]
Every denominator in (32) is positive by (26).  Combining (26), (30), and (32) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_1^A&=(2/75,0,-2/75),&
\tau_2^A&=(-1/40,-1/40,1/20),\\
\tau_1^B&=(1/25,0,-1/25),&
\tau_2^B&=(-1/60,-1/60,1/30).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{33}
\]
Thus the two observationally equivalent laws disagree about both full type shares and both full conditional type effects.  In contrast, partitioning by the two support points of \(Z\) and applying the definition of \(\nu_j\) gives
\[
\tau^C=E_C(\Delta\mid D=1)=\sum_{j=1}^2\nu_j^C
=(3/500,-1/100,1/250),
\qquad C=A,B.                                                \tag{34}
\]
This proves equality of the entire aggregate ATT vector.

Because \(P^A\) and \(P^B\) induce the same observed law but differ in (26) and (33), no observed-law functional can equal the full type shares or full conditional type effects throughout \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{face}}\).  Conversely, equations (27), (30), (31), and (34) show directly that the exhibited ambiguity leaves every active-side target unchanged, while the already established \(\texttt{thm:active-frontier-recovery}\) proves that these targets are functions of the observed law over every broad compatible fiber.  Finally, under \(\texttt{ass:no-date-one-absorption}\), nonnegativity implies
\[
P(Z=j,X_1=e\mid D=d)=0
\quad\text{for every }j,d.
\]
Hence \(\pi_j^d=\alpha_j^d\), and (32) becomes \(\tau_j=\nu_j/\alpha_j^1=\eta_j\).  Thus the additional support restriction removes exactly the initial-absorber allocation ambiguity exhibited by the pair, completing the proof.
