%%% FIELD proof-target-separator
Write \(\mathcal A:=D_\ell(A)\), \(\mathcal B:=D_\ell(B)\), and \(\mu:=P_{X_{\mathcal A}}\).  By \(\texttt{ass:observed-mean-differentiability}\), the family \(\mathfrak F_{\mathcal A,\mathcal B}(P_X)\) is nonempty.  Let \(f_1,f_2\in\mathfrak F_{\mathcal A,\mathcal B}(P_X)\), defined on open neighborhoods \(\mathcal U_1,\mathcal U_2\) of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\).  They are versions of the same conditional mean, so
\[
 f_1(X_{\mathcal A})=f_2(X_{\mathcal A})
 \qquad P_X\text{-almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
Apply \(\texttt{ass:rankpeel-query-jet-determining}\) on \(\mathcal U_1\cap\mathcal U_2\) to every scalar coordinate of \(f_1-f_2\).  Intersecting the finitely many probability-one events gives
\[
 Df_1(X_{\mathcal A})=Df_2(X_{\mathcal A})
 \qquad P_X\text{-almost surely}. \tag{2}
\]
Thus all admissible representatives have the same essential-supremum derivative rank.  This proves \((\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\) for every endpoint pair actually requested by the canonical execution, including a pair at which the procedure would otherwise stop.

Now let \(Z\subseteq V\setminus V_0\) be a restricted pure-surrogate cut used for this query and suppose, as in the statement, that \(Z\) \(d\)-separates the measured endpoints.  The cut itself is supplied by \(\texttt{lem:pure-surrogate-cuts}\); directed Markovness in \(\texttt{ass:dag-markov}\) turns this graphical separation into the relevant conditional independence.  The additional pointwise regularity used in the present abstract theorem is stated separately: \(\texttt{ass:rankpeel-separator-fibre-coherence}\) supplies an open neighborhood \(\mathcal U\) of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\), a representative \(f^0\in\mathfrak F_{\mathcal A,\mathcal B}(P_X)\), and a map \(g\in C^1(\mathcal U;\mathbb R^{|Z|})\) such that
\[
 g(x)=g(x')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f^0(x)=f^0(x')
 \qquad(x,x'\in\mathcal U). \tag{3}
\]
Apply \(\texttt{lem:topological-bottleneck}\) with \(a=f^0\), \(b=g\), and \(k=|Z|\).  Its fibre hypothesis is exactly (3), and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}Df^0(x)\leq |Z|
 \qquad(x\in\mathcal U). \tag{4}
\]
Taking one of the two representatives in (2) to be \(f^0\) shows that every admissible representative has the same derivative as \(f^0\), \(\mu\)-almost surely.  Therefore (4) and \(\texttt{def:observed-rank-oracle}\) imply
\[
 R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\leq |Z|. \tag{5}
\]
Every \(|\mathcal B|\)-by-\(|\mathcal A|\) Jacobian has rank at most \(\min\{|\mathcal A|,|\mathcal B|\}\).  Combining this elementary dimension bound with (5) proves
\[
 R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X}(D_\ell(A),D_\ell(B))
 \leq
 \min\{|D_\ell(A)|,|D_\ell(B)|,|Z|\}.
\]
If either endpoint is empty, the conclusion follows directly from the stipulated zero value in \(\texttt{def:observed-rank-oracle}\).  Notice that this proof treats fibre coherence as an explicit latent, separator-relative hypothesis; it does not infer it from the observed law.

%%% FIELD statement-ae-bottleneck
Let \(m,q,k\in\mathbb N_0\), let \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be open, and let \(\mu\) be a probability measure whose restriction to \(\mathcal U\) is equivalent to Lebesgue measure and for which \(\mu(\mathcal U)=1\).  Suppose \(a\in C^1(\mathcal U;\mathbb R^q)\), \(b\in C^1(\mathcal U;\mathbb R^k)\), and there is a measurable \(\phi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R^q\) such that \(a=\phi\circ b\), \(\mu\)-almost surely.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}Da(x)\leq k
 \qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\in\mathcal U.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-ae-bottleneck
Let \(\lambda_d\) denote \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.  Equivalence of \(\mu|_{\mathcal U}\) and \(\lambda_m|_{\mathcal U}\) makes
\[
 E:=\{x\in\mathcal U:a(x)=\phi(b(x))\}
\]
a set of full \(\lambda_m\)-measure in \(\mathcal U\).

We first record, with proof, the elementary null-section fact used below.  If \(N\subseteq Q\times T\subseteq\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^e\) is Lebesgue null, then \(N_t:=\{u:(u,t)\in N\}\) is \(\lambda_d\)-null for \(\lambda_e\)-almost every \(t\).  Indeed, for each integer \(j\geq1\), cover \(N\) by countably many axis-parallel boxes \(I_{ji}\times J_{ji}\) whose total \((d+e)\)-volume is less than \(2^{-j}\), and set
\[
 s_j(t):=\sum_i\lambda_d(I_{ji})\mathbf 1_{J_{ji}}(t).
\]
Countable additivity for nonnegative simple functions gives
\[
 \int s_j(t)\,d\lambda_e(t)
 =\sum_i\lambda_d(I_{ji})\lambda_e(J_{ji})<2^{-j}. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(\lambda_e\{s_j>2^{-j/2}\}<2^{-j/2}\).  The union bound applied to the tails of this summable series shows that, outside a null set of \(t\)'s, \(s_j(t)\leq2^{-j/2}\) for all sufficiently large \(j\).  Since the displayed boxes cover \(N_t\) and \(\lambda_d^*(N_t)\leq s_j(t)\), its outer measure is zero.  This proves the null-section fact without imposing any regularity on \(N\) beyond measurability.

We next prove a dimension fact needed for the contradiction.  If \(W\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) is nonempty and open, \(n>k\), and \(c\in C^1(W;\mathbb R^k)\), then no full-Lebesgue-measure subset of \(W\) can be a set on which \(c\) is injective.  Let
\[
 r:=\max_{x\in W}\operatorname{rank}Dc(x),
\]
which exists because the possible ranks form the finite set \(\{0,\ldots,k\}\), and choose \(x_0\) attaining it.  If \(r=0\), then \(Dc=0\) on \(W\); on a sufficiently small convex box about \(x_0\), integration along line segments makes \(c\) constant, so injectivity fails on every full-measure subset of that box.

Suppose \(r>0\).  After permuting input and output coordinates, the upper-left \(r\)-by-\(r\) minor of \(Dc(x_0)\) is nonsingular.  Define
\[
 \Psi(x):=(c_1(x),\ldots,c_r(x),x_{r+1},\ldots,x_n).
\]
The derivative \(D\Psi(x_0)\) is invertible.  By \(\texttt{lem:inverse-function-theorem}\), after shrinking to a neighborhood \(W_0\) of \(x_0\), \(\Psi\) is a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism onto an open set.  Put \(\widetilde c:=c\circ\Psi^{-1}\).  Its first \(r\) coordinates are \((y_1,\ldots,y_r)\), while its total derivative has rank exactly \(r\), by maximality of \(r\).  Each partial derivative \(\partial\widetilde c_j/\partial y_s\) with \(s>r\) must therefore vanish: otherwise that entry together with the identity block in the first \(r\) rows and columns would form a nonzero \((r+1)\)-minor.  On a product box \(Q'\times Q''\) with closure contained in \(\Psi(W_0)\), integration along line segments in the convex box \(Q''\) now gives
\[
 \widetilde c(y',y'')=\beta(y') \tag{2}
\]
for a suitable \(\beta\).

A \(C^1\) map on a neighborhood of a compact box is Lipschitz there, by the line-segment integral bound after a finite convex subdivision.  A Lipschitz map from \(\mathbb R^n\) to \(\mathbb R^n\) sends Lebesgue-null sets to null sets: cover the null set by cubes of arbitrarily small total volume and cover each image cube by a dimension-dependent fixed number of cubes whose side lengths are multiplied by the Lipschitz constant.  It follows that the image under \(\Psi\) of any full-measure subset of \(W_0\) has full measure in \(Q'\times Q''\).  By the null-section fact, for almost every \(y'\), its section in \(Q''\) has full measure.  Since
\[
 \dim Q''=n-r\geq n-k\geq1,
\]
such a section contains two distinct points.  Equation (2) maps those points to the same value, contradicting injectivity.  This proves the dimension fact.

We now prove the asserted rank bound.  If \(m\leq k\), it is the elementary matrix-dimension bound, so suppose \(m>k\).  If \(\operatorname{rank}Da(x_0)>k\) at some \(x_0\in\mathcal U\), choose \(k+1\) input coordinates and \(k+1\) output coordinates giving a nonsingular minor.  Write the selected input coordinates as \(u\in\mathbb R^{k+1}\), the remaining ones as \(t\), and let \(F_t(u)\) be the selected \(k+1\) coordinates of \(a(u,t)\).  If \(A_0:=D_uF_{t_0}(u_0)\), continuity permits a convex product box \(Q\times T\) about \((u_0,t_0)\) on which
\[
 \left\|A_0^{-1}\bigl(D_uF_t(u)-A_0\bigr)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac12. \tag{3}
\]
For \(u,v\in Q\), the line-segment identity and (3) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\|A_0^{-1}\bigl(F_t(u)-F_t(v)\bigr)\right\|
 &\geq \|u-v\|
 -\int_0^1\left\|A_0^{-1}
 \bigl(D_uF_t(v+s(u-v))-A_0\bigr)(u-v)\right\|\,ds\\
 &\geq\frac12\|u-v\|. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Thus every \(F_t\) is injective on \(Q\).  The null-section fact applied to \((Q\times T)\setminus E\) supplies a \(t\) for which
\[
 E_t:=\{u\in Q:(u,t)\in E\}
\]
has full \((k+1)\)-dimensional measure in \(Q\); when there are no transverse coordinates, take \(E_t=E\cap Q\).  If \(u,v\in E_t\) and \(b(u,t)=b(v,t)\), then
\[
 a(u,t)=\phi(b(u,t))=\phi(b(v,t))=a(v,t),
\]
so (4) gives \(u=v\).  Hence the \(C^1\) map \(u\mapsto b(u,t)\) from the open subset \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\) into \(\mathbb R^k\) is injective on a full-measure subset, contradicting the dimension fact.  Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}Da(x)\leq k\) for every \(x\in\mathcal U\), which in particular proves the claimed \(\mu\)-almost-everywhere bound.

%%% FIELD statement-positive-density-jd
Let \(A\subseteq V_0\) be nonempty and put \(\mu_A:=P_{X_A}\).  Suppose there is a nonempty open set \(\mathcal U_A\subseteq\mathbb R^{|A|}\) such that \(\mu_A(\mathcal U_A)=1\) and \(\mu_A|_{\mathcal U_A}\) is equivalent to Lebesgue measure on \(\mathcal U_A\).  Then every open neighborhood \(\mathcal W\) of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu_A)\) is first-jet determining for \(\mu_A\): for every \(h\in C^1(\mathcal W;\mathbb R)\),
\[
 h(X_A)=0\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Dh(X_A)=0\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-positive-density-jd
Every \(x\in\mathcal U_A\) lies in \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu_A)\).  Indeed, if \(O\) is any open neighborhood of \(x\), then \(O\cap\mathcal U_A\) has positive Lebesgue measure, and equivalence on \(\mathcal U_A\) gives \(\mu_A(O)>0\).  Hence \(\mathcal U_A\subseteq\mathcal W\).

If \(h(X_A)=0\) almost surely, equivalence implies that \(h=0\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \(\mathcal U_A\).  If \(h(x_0)\neq0\) at some \(x_0\in\mathcal U_A\), continuity would give an open ball contained in \(\mathcal U_A\) on which \(|h|\) is bounded away from zero, contradicting its almost-everywhere vanishing.  Thus \(h\) is identically zero on the open set \(\mathcal U_A\), and consequently \(Dh=0\) there.  Since \(\mu_A(\mathcal U_A)=1\), the derivative vanishes \(P_X\)-almost surely, as claimed.

%%% FIELD statement-positive-density-upper
Let \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PNHZ}}\), and let \(\lambda_d\) denote \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.  Consider any singleton, pair, baseline-neutrality, or augmented-neutrality endpoint pair \((\mathcal A,\mathcal B)=(D_\ell(A),D_\ell(B))\) actually requested, including a pair at which the procedure would otherwise stop, by the canonical deterministic RankPeel execution.  If \(\mathcal A\neq\varnothing\), suppose that there is a nonempty open \(\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}\subseteq\mathbb R^{|\mathcal A|}\) with
\[
 P_{X_{\mathcal A}}(\mathcal U_{\mathcal A})=1,
 \qquad
 P_{X_{\mathcal A}}|_{\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}}
 \sim \lambda_{|\mathcal A|}|_{\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}}.
\]
Then \((\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\).  If a latent set \(Z\subseteq V\setminus V_0\) is one of the restricted pure-surrogate cuts used for the query and \(d\)-separates its measured endpoints in \(G\), then, without any pointwise fibre-coherence assumption,
\[
 R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
 \leq\min\{|\mathcal A|,|\mathcal B|,|Z|\}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-positive-density-upper
The case of an empty endpoint is immediate from \(\texttt{def:observed-rank-oracle}\), so suppose both endpoints are nonempty and write \(\mu:=P_{X_{\mathcal A}}\) and \(\mathcal U:=\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}\).

First consider version stability.  By \(\texttt{ass:observed-mean-differentiability}\), the set \(\mathfrak F_{\mathcal A,\mathcal B}(P_X)\) is nonempty.  If \(f_1,f_2\) are two members, each coordinate of \(f_1-f_2\) vanishes \(\mu\)-almost surely on the intersection of their open domains, which contains \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\).  Applying \(\texttt{lem:positive-density-jet-determining}\) coordinatewise gives
\[
 Df_1(X_{\mathcal A})=Df_2(X_{\mathcal A})
 \qquad P_X\text{-almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus all representatives have the same essential-supremum rank, and the queried pair belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\).

Now suppose \(Z\) is a stated restricted cut.  The graphical part of the claim is query-specific: \(\texttt{lem:pure-surrogate-cuts}\) supplies the cut, and the premise states that it \(d\)-separates the endpoints.  By \(\texttt{ass:dag-markov}\),
\[
 X_{\mathcal B}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{\mathcal A}\mid Z. \tag{2}
\]
The conditional means in \(\texttt{ass:observed-mean-differentiability}\) entail the finite first moments used below.  Let \(\eta(z)\) be a Borel version of \(\mathbb E[X_{\mathcal B}\mid Z=z]\), and let \(K_x\) be a regular conditional law of \(Z\) given \(X_{\mathcal A}=x\).  Conditional independence (2) and iterated conditional expectation yield, for any \(f^0\in\mathfrak F_{\mathcal A,\mathcal B}(P_X)\),
\[
 f^0(x)=\int\eta(z)\,K_x(dz)
 \qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x. \tag{3}
\]

Apply \(\texttt{ass:conditional-sufficiency}\) to the measured set \(\mathcal A\) and this latent set \(Z\).  In the regular-conditional-law meaning of that assumption, its Condition 3(ii) factorization supplies a map \(g=g_{\mathcal A,Z}\in C^1(\mathbb R^{|\mathcal A|};\mathbb R^{|Z|})\) and a Markov kernel \(t\mapsto\kappa_t\) such that
\[
 K_x=\kappa_{g(x)}
 \qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x. \tag{4}
\]
Define the measurable map
\[
 \phi(t):=\int\eta(z)\,\kappa_t(dz)
\]
where the integral exists, and set it to zero elsewhere.  Kernel measurability makes \(\phi\) measurable.  Equations (3)--(4) give the almost-sure, rather than pointwise, factorization
\[
 f^0(x)=\phi(g(x))
 \qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x. \tag{5}
\]
Restrict \(f^0\) and \(g\) to \(\mathcal U\).  The positive-density hypothesis and \(\texttt{lem:ae-bottleneck}\), applied to (5) with \(k=|Z|\), imply
\[
 \operatorname{rank}Df^0(x)\leq |Z|
 \qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x. \tag{6}
\]
No conclusion about equality on every fibre of \(g\) is used.  Equation (1) transfers (6) to every admissible representative.  Taking essential suprema and adding the two matrix-dimension bounds gives
\[
 R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
 \leq\min\{|\mathcal A|,|\mathcal B|,|Z|\},
\]
which is the desired separator upper bound derived from directed Markovness, the anchor's almost-sure conditional-sufficiency factorization, and positive density.

%%% FIELD statement-positive-density-reconstruction
Theorem 3 (Positive-density RankPeel corollary).  Let \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PNHZ}}\), and let \(\lambda_d\) denote \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.  Suppose that for every nonempty measured predictor set \(\mathcal A\) occurring in an endpoint pair actually requested, including a pair at which the procedure would otherwise stop, by the canonical deterministic execution of \(\operatorname{RankPeel}(P_X,R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X})\), there is a nonempty open set \(\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}\subseteq\mathbb R^{|\mathcal A|}\) such that
\[
 P_{X_{\mathcal A}}(\mathcal U_{\mathcal A})=1,
 \qquad
 P_{X_{\mathcal A}}|_{\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}}
 \sim\lambda_{|\mathcal A|}|_{\mathcal U_{\mathcal A}}.
\]
Then the execution does not return \(\bot\), terminates with \((\widehat G,\mathcal T)\), and satisfies \([\widehat G]=\theta_G\), with indeterminacy only from independent permutations of latent labels within layers.  It uses at most \(p^2+p\) combined membership-and-rank calls and \(O(p^3)\) elementary bookkeeping; for \(p\geq3\), its worst-case call count over this strict subclass is \(\Theta(p^2)\).  The emitted record has size \(O(p^3+p^2\log p)\), satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Verify}(P_X,R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X},\mathcal T,\widehat G)=1,
\]
and is an oracle-relative replay certificate whose reconstructed graph is structurally sound on this subclass.  This is a strict positive-density/open-support result derived without assuming pointwise latent-separator fibre coherence; it is not a full-class transfer from the anchor's Conditions 1--3.

%%% FIELD proof-positive-density-reconstruction
We prove correctness by induction on the true layer \(V_\ell\).  At \(\ell=0\), the active vertices are the measured vertices and \(D_0(v)=\{v\}\), so the retained-surrogate invariant in \(\texttt{def:pure-surrogates}\) holds.  Assume it holds at a reached layer \(\ell\).  By the positive-density hypothesis and \(\texttt{lem:positive-density-restricted-separator-upper}\), every endpoint pair that the canonical execution requests belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\).  Hence the execution cannot return \(\bot\) at this layer.

For each current vertex \(v\), \(\texttt{prop:parentless-test}\) applies because its singleton query is version stable and gives
\[
 \rho_\ell(\{v\},V_\ell\setminus\{v\})=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Pa}_G(v)=\varnothing. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the singleton stage removes exactly the parentless vertices from pair testing.

Consider distinct remaining vertices \(u,v\).  If \(\operatorname{Pa}_G(u)=\operatorname{Pa}_G(v)=\{h\}\), then \(Z=\{h\}\) is the restricted cut supplied by \(\texttt{lem:pure-surrogate-cuts}\), and the positive-density separator upper bound gives
\[
 \rho_\ell(\{u,v\},V_\ell\setminus\{u,v\})\leq1. \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, suppose their parent sets are not the same singleton.  Since neither vertex is parentless, choose distinct \(h\in\operatorname{Pa}_G(u)\) and \(g\in\operatorname{Pa}_G(v)\).  By \(\texttt{ass:two-pure-children}\), choose a pure child \(b_h\) of \(h\) and a pure child \(b_g\) of \(g\), both outside \(\{u,v\}\).  The choices can be made distinct: if \(u,v\) exhausted two pure children of one parent, they would be pure children of that same parent, contrary to the present case, and a pure child cannot have two different parents.  The forks
\[
 u\leftarrow h\to b_h,
 \qquad
 v\leftarrow g\to b_g
\]
are vertex-disjoint.  Their measured lifts are vertex-disjoint collider-free paths by \(\texttt{lem:pure-surrogate-cuts}\), so \(\texttt{lem:private-path-lower}\) gives rank at least two.  Together with (2), this proves that the nontrivial connected components of the pair relation are exactly the full pure blocks \(B_h\).  Because every \(h\in V_{\ell+1}\) has at least two pure children, every next-layer vertex is recovered.

It remains to recover the parents of a current nonpure, nonparentless vertex \(c\).  Let \(C\subseteq H=V_{\ell+1}\) be a candidate set actually evaluated by the canonical deletion execution, let \(k=|C|\), and use the canonical representatives \(S_C=\{s_h:h\in C\}\).  For every \(h\in C\), choose a second pure child \(b_h\neq s_h\).  The \(k\) vertex-disjoint forks \(s_h\leftarrow h\to b_h\) lift to measured collider-free paths with responses in \(T_C(c)\); hence \(\texttt{lem:private-path-lower}\) gives the baseline lower bound \(k\).  The latent set \(C\) is a restricted cut separating those source trees from the response trees, so \(\texttt{lem:positive-density-restricted-separator-upper}\) gives the matching upper bound:
\[
 \rho_\ell(S_C,T_C(c))=k. \tag{3}
\]

If \(\operatorname{Pa}_G(c)\subseteq C\), the same \(k\) paths remain after adding \(c\), and \(C\) contains all parents of every source root in \(S_C\cup\{c\}\).  The corresponding restricted cut therefore gives
\[
 \rho_\ell(S_C\cup\{c\},T_C(c))=k. \tag{4}
\]
If instead \(q\in\operatorname{Pa}_G(c)\setminus C\), choose a pure child \(b_q\) of \(q\).  Since \(c\) is nonpure and \(q\notin C\), the additional fork \(c\leftarrow q\to b_q\) is vertex-disjoint from the \(k\) preceding forks and has its response in \(T_C(c)\).  The private-path lower bound is therefore \(k+1\).  When \(\ell=0\), the measured predictor set has exactly \(k+1\) coordinates, giving the reverse inequality by matrix dimension.  When \(\ell\geq1\), the \(k+1\) latent current roots \(S_C\cup\{c\}\) form the restricted source-root cut in \(\texttt{lem:pure-surrogate-cuts}\), so the positive-density separator upper bound again gives the reverse inequality.  Consequently
\[
 \rho_\ell(S_C\cup\{c\},T_C(c))=
 \begin{cases}
 k,&\operatorname{Pa}_G(c)\subseteq C,\\
 k+1,&\operatorname{Pa}_G(c)\nsubseteq C.
 \end{cases} \tag{5}
\]

Initially \(C=H\) contains every parent of \(c\).  At a deletion step, (3)--(5) delete a tested \(h\) exactly when \(C\setminus\{h\}\) still contains \(\operatorname{Pa}_G(c)\).  Thus no true parent is deleted, every nonparent is deleted when processed, and the final candidate set is exactly \(\operatorname{Pa}_G(c)\).  Hence all edges between \(V_{\ell+1}\) and \(V_\ell\) are recovered.  The update
\[
 D_{\ell+1}(h)=\bigcup_{v\in B_h}D_\ell(v)
\]
preserves the disjoint retained pure-tree invariant by \(\texttt{lem:pure-surrogate-cuts}\), completing the induction.  Adjacent layering in \(\texttt{ass:adjacent-layering}\) rules out omitted nonadjacent edges, so the accumulated graph equals \(G\) up to the canonical within-layer latent relabelings and \([\widehat G]=\theta_G\).

Finally, the disjoint blocks \(B_h\) have size at least two, so the active layer sizes halve.  The unconditional counting part of \(\texttt{thm:query-complexity}\) therefore applies to this execution and gives at most \(p^2+p\) combined membership-and-rank calls and \(O(p^3)\) bookkeeping.

The quadratic order is attained within the stated subclass.  For each \(p\geq3\), take one latent \(H\sim N(0,1)\), independent noises \(\varepsilon_i\sim N(0,1)\), and
\[
 X_i=H+\sigma\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,p,
 \qquad \sigma>0. \tag{6}
\]
This scalar adjacent-layer DAG has \(p\) pure children of \(H\), equal measured-descendant depth, and the directed Markov property.  Every observed subvector has a nonsingular Gaussian law and hence a strictly positive density on its Euclidean space.  Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb E[H\mid X_A=x_A]
 =\frac{\sum_{i\in A}x_i}{\sigma^2+|A|}, \tag{7}
\]
with posterior variance depending only on \(|A|\), which verifies the differentiable conditional-sufficiency condition.  For nonempty disjoint \(A,B\), iterated expectation gives
\[
 \mathbb E[X_B\mid X_A=x_A]
 =\mathbf 1_B\frac{\sum_{i\in A}x_i}{\sigma^2+|A|}, \tag{8}
\]
whose Jacobian has rank one.  Thus there is no nongraphical rank defect, so the generalized rank-faithfulness condition holds for this one-factor graph.  Each singleton-versus-complement rank is one, so RankPeel marks no measured vertex parentless and issues all \(\binom p2\) pair queries.  It therefore makes at least \(\binom p2=\Omega(p^2)\) calls on (6), which together with the upper bound proves the claimed \(\Theta(p^2)\) worst-case order.

The unconditional replay-completeness part of \(\texttt{prop:replay-certificate}\) now gives the displayed verification equality, the transcript size \(O(p^3+p^2\log p)\), and replay within the same query and bookkeeping bounds.  The induction above supplies structural soundness on the positive-density subclass.  These numerical and replay conclusions concern the population oracle and do not include oracle evaluation or finite-sample estimation.
