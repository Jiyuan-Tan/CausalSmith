The answer is negative on the published class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PNHZ}}\).  Conditions 1--3 do not make the conditional-mean Jacobian rank a functional of \(P_X\): there is a smooth scalar one-latent, four-measurement member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PNHZ}}\) for which \(\operatorname{RankPeel}\) requests a pair outside \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\).

The exact query-local repair is as follows.  Let \(\mathscr Q\) be a finite collection of disjoint measured endpoint pairs \((A,B)\), let \(\mu_A:=P_{X_A}\), and choose a common open neighborhood \(\mathcal U_A\) of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu_A)\).  Say that \(\mu_A\) is first-jet determining on \(\mathcal U_A\), denoted \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\), when
\[
 h\in C^1(\mathcal U_A;\mathbb R),\qquad h(X_A)=0\quad\mu_A\text{-almost surely}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Dh(X_A)=0\quad\mu_A\text{-almost surely}.
\]
For a latent set \(Z\) that \(d\)-separates \(X_A\) and \(X_B\), say that the conditional versions are fibre coherent, denoted \(\mathrm{FC}(A,B;Z)\), when there are \(f^0\in\mathfrak F_{A,B}(P_X)\) and \(g\in C^1(\mathcal U_A;\mathbb R^{|Z|})\) such that
\[
 g(x)=g(x')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f^0(x)=f^0(x')
 \qquad(x,x'\in\mathcal U_A).
\]
If \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\) holds for every predictor set occurring in \(\mathscr Q\), and \(\mathrm{FC}(A,B;Z)\) holds for every separator cut used for a pair \((A,B)\in\mathscr Q\), then every pair in \(\mathscr Q\) belongs to \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\) and
\[
 R^{\mathrm{law}}_{P_X}(A,B)
 \leq \min\{|A|,|B|,|Z|\}.
\]
The condition \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\) is equivalent to almost-sure equality of first derivatives for every two \(C^1\) maps that are equal \(\mu_A\)-almost surely, so it is the weakest response-uniform first-order support condition giving version invariance.  Fibre coherence is exactly set-theoretic factorization through the published \(|Z|\)-dimensional sufficient map; no differentiability of the induced outer map is required.  A directly checkable sufficient support condition for \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\) is
\[
 \mu_A\!\left(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{supp}(\mu_A)\right)=1.
\]
Thus law-invariant RankPeel is justified on the query-local jet-determining, fibre-coherent subclass, but not on all of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PNHZ}}\).

Fix a queried predictor set \(A\), write \(\mu:=\mu_A\), and work on the common open neighborhood \(\mathcal U_A\).

\emph{Step 1: first-jet determinacy is the exact support condition for version derivatives.}
Suppose \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\) holds and let \(u,v\in C^1(\mathcal U_A;\mathbb R^q)\) satisfy \(u(X_A)=v(X_A)\) \(\mu\)-almost surely.  For each coordinate \(j\), the scalar map \(h_j:=u_j-v_j\) vanishes \(\mu\)-almost surely.  By \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\), \(Dh_j(X_A)=0\) \(\mu\)-almost surely.  Intersecting the finitely many probability-one events gives
\[
 Du(X_A)=Dv(X_A)\qquad\mu\text{-almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
Conversely, apply the asserted derivative-uniqueness property to \(u=h\) and \(v=0\).  This recovers \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\).  Hence the two properties are equivalent.

The displayed support-interior condition implies \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\).  Indeed, if a continuous \(h\) vanishes \(\mu\)-almost surely and \(x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\), then \(h(x)\neq0\) would, by continuity, give an open neighborhood \(O\ni x\) on which \(|h|\) is bounded away from zero.  The definition of support gives \(\mu(O)>0\), a contradiction.  Thus \(h=0\) on \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\).  At every point of its interior, \(h\) is zero on a neighborhood and therefore \(Dh=0\).  The support-interior condition assigns probability one to those points, proving \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\).

\emph{Step 2: fibre coherence gives the separator rank bound without differentiating an outer map.}
Let \(f^0\) and \(g\) witness \(\mathrm{FC}(A,B;Z)\), and put \(k:=|Z|\).  We show pointwise that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}Df^0(x)\leq k,\qquad x\in\mathcal U_A. \tag{2}
\]
Suppose instead that \(r:=\operatorname{rank}Df^0(x_0)>k\).  Some \(r\)-by-\(r\) minor of \(Df^0(x_0)\) is nonsingular.  Projecting onto its \(r\) response coordinates and restricting the domain to the affine \(r\)-plane through \(x_0\) spanned by its \(r\) predictor coordinates gives a \(C^1\) map \(F\) whose derivative \(A_0:=DF(x_0)\) is nonsingular.  By continuity of \(DF\), there is a convex relatively open ball \(W\) about \(x_0\) on which
\[
 \left\|A_0^{-1}\bigl(DF(x)-A_0\bigr)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac12. \tag{2a}
\]
For \(x,y\in W\), the fundamental theorem of calculus on the segment from \(y\) to \(x\), followed by (2a), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\|A_0^{-1}\bigl(F(x)-F(y)\bigr)\right\|
 &=\left\|(x-y)+\int_0^1 A_0^{-1}\bigl(DF(y+t(x-y))-A_0\bigr)(x-y)\,dt\right\|\\
 &\geq \frac12\|x-y\|.
 \end{aligned} \tag{2b}
\]
Hence \(F\) is injective on \(W\), which is homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb R^r\).

If \(x,x'\in W\) and \(g(x)=g(x')\), fibre coherence gives \(f^0(x)=f^0(x')\), hence \(F(x)=F(x')\), and injectivity of \(F\) gives \(x=x'\).  Thus \(g|_W\) is a continuous injection from an open subset of \(\mathbb R^r\) into \(\mathbb R^k\).  Appending \(r-k\) zero coordinates produces a continuous injection into \(\mathbb R^r\).  By the cited invariance-of-domain theorem its image must be open in \(\mathbb R^r\), but it is contained in \(\mathbb R^k\times\{0\}^{r-k}\), which has empty interior because \(r>k\).  This contradiction proves (2).

By observed-mean differentiability, \(\mathfrak F_{A,B}(P_X)\) is nonempty.  For any \(f\in\mathfrak F_{A,B}(P_X)\), the maps \(f\) and \(f^0\) are versions of the same integrable conditional mean, so they agree \(\mu\)-almost surely.  Equation (1) yields \(Df=Df^0\) \(\mu\)-almost surely.  Consequently every admissible representative has the same essential-supremum rank and, by (2),
\[
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\sim\mu}\operatorname{rank}Df(x)
 \leq k. \tag{3}
\]
The elementary dimensions of the Jacobian also bound its rank by \(|A|\) and \(|B|\).  Equations (1)--(3), together with the stipulated zero value for an empty endpoint, prove both membership in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\) and the asserted bound for every pair in \(\mathscr Q\).

The relation to the causal separator is explicit.  The directed global Markov property and the hypothesis that \(Z\) \(d\)-separates the endpoints give
\[
 X_B\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_A\mid Z.
\]
For finite first moments, which are required for the displayed conditional means to be well defined, let \(\eta(z)\) be a version of \(\mathbb E[X_B\mid Z=z]\), and let \(K_x\) be a regular conditional law of \(Z\) given \(X_A=x\).  Conditional independence and iterated expectation give, at every point where these versions are coherent,
\[
 f^0(x)=\mathbb E[X_B\mid X_A=x]
       =\int \eta(z)\,K_x(dz). \tag{4}
\]
If the version in the published conditional-sufficiency condition is imposed pointwise on \(\mathcal U_A\), so that \(K_x=\kappa_{g(x)}\), then (4) is constant on fibres of \(g\) and hence gives \(\mathrm{FC}(A,B;Z)\).  This is the required version repair.  Merely writing an almost-sure identity \(K_x=\kappa_{g(x)}\) does not select off-support values and does not make an induced outer map differentiable; neither move is used above.

\emph{Step 3: a smooth legal boundary witness.}
Let the graph have one latent vertex \(H\) and four measured pure children \(X_1,\ldots,X_4\), with arrows \(H\to X_i\).  Let \(\varepsilon_H,\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_4\) be mutually independent standard Gaussian variables and use the smooth structural equations
\[
 H=\varepsilon_H,
 \qquad
 X_i=H+\sigma\varepsilon_i,\quad i=1,\ldots,4. \tag{5}
\]
For every \(\sigma\geq0\) the variables are scalar, the graph is acyclic and adjacent-layered, every measured vertex is a pure child, the latent has four pure children, and every measured descendant is one edge below \(H\).  The independent-noise factorization of (5) gives the directed global Markov property.  All first moments exist.

For \(\sigma>0\), every measured subvector has a strictly positive Gaussian density.  Moreover, for nonempty \(A\), the Gaussian posterior law of \(H\) given \(X_A=x_A\) has variance depending only on \(|A|\) and \(\sigma\), and mean
\[
 \mathbb E[H\mid X_A=x_A]
 =\frac{\sum_{i\in A}x_i}{\sigma^2+|A|}. \tag{6}
\]
Thus the posterior is determined by the smooth scalar statistic \(g_A(x_A)=\sum_{i\in A}x_i\), verifying conditional sufficiency, and every conditional mean is smooth.  The usual nonzero rank is one whenever both endpoints are nonempty, so there is no accidental rank defect.  This verifies the generalized rank-faithfulness requirement for this one-factor graph.

At the boundary value \(\sigma=0\), equation (5) remains a smooth scalar structural equation model and gives
\[
 X_1=X_2=X_3=X_4=H\qquad\text{almost surely}. \tag{7}
\]
The conditional-sufficiency condition still holds, for example with \(g_A(x_A)=x_{\min A}\), and continuously differentiable conditional-mean versions exist for every disjoint \(A,B\).  On the designated-version reading of Conditions 2--3, choose for each nonempty pair a linear extension whose derivative has the maximum dimension-compatible rank and which maps \(t\mathbf 1_A\) to \(t\mathbf 1_B\).  To see that such an extension exists, write \(a:=|A|\) and \(b:=|B|\).  If \(b\leq a\), take \(M=[I_b\ 0]\); if \(b>a\), take the first \(a\) rows of \(M\) to be \(I_a\) and every remaining row to be the first coordinate row.  In both cases
\[
 M\mathbf 1_a=\mathbf 1_b,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}M=\min\{a,b\}. \tag{7a}
\]
Hence the designated maps have no non-graphical rank deficiency, so generalized rank faithfulness holds.  On a reading that quantifies over all almost-sure versions, Condition 2 is not itself a law property until the present version issue is resolved; this is precisely the obstruction exposed by the example.

For the pair \(A=\{1,2\}\), \(B=\{3,4\}\), two valid smooth representatives are
\[
 f^{(1)}(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,x_1),
 \qquad
 f^{(2)}(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,x_2). \tag{8}
\]
They agree on the diagonal support forced by (7), and hence both equal \(\mathbb E[(X_3,X_4)\mid X_1,X_2]\) almost surely.  Nevertheless,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}Df^{(1)}=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}Df^{(2)}=2. \tag{9}
\]
Therefore \((A,B)\notin\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{stab}}(P_X)\).  Each singleton predictor has the full-support \(N(0,1)\) law, so continuity makes its conditional-mean derivative version unique and its rank is one.  RankPeel consequently marks no measured vertex as parentless and then requests every unordered pair, including (8); it returns \(\bot\) there.  The scalar separator is \(Z=\{H\}\): the coherent representative \(f^{(1)}\) obeys the bound one, while the equally valid representative \(f^{(2)}\) has rank two.  Thus neither a law-invariant rank nor a representative-uniform separator bound follows from Conditions 1--3.  The function \(h(x_1,x_2)=x_2-x_1\) vanishes on the predictor support but has \(Dh=(-1,1)\), explicitly certifying failure of \(\mathrm{JD}(A)\).  Since the models with \(\sigma>0\) satisfy the support condition and converge to (7) as \(\sigma\downarrow0\), this is an immediate smooth boundary witness rather than a change of graph class.

\emph{Step 4: the prescribed finite graph kill test.}
For each of the \(26^2=676\) choices of the two extra measured mixed-child parent sets, all proposed cuts survive.  This also follows without numerical ranks.  At level zero, every path leaving a pure measured source first meets its sole latent parent, so conditioning on that parent blocks the pure-block cut; for a baseline representative set the same argument applies parent by parent; and when all parents of an augmented mixed source are conditioned upon, every path from that source is blocked.  At level one, each retained measured surrogate attaches to the upper graph through its unique pure chain, so the corresponding parent or source-root cut blocks every path.  The two extra mixed measured vertices are unconditioned sinks; whenever a path traverses one, it is a collider, and it has no conditioned descendant.  At level two, any path between the two root surrogate trees must traverse an unconditioned common descendant as a collider.  Hence no choice among the \(26\) allowed parent sets opens a path.

For completeness, the exact check count is also reproducible from the cases.  Each graph contributes \(80\) fixed cuts.  A mixed child with a parent set of size \(j\) contributes \(2^{5-j}\) additional all-parent augmented cuts.  Therefore the total is
\[
 676\cdot80
 +2\cdot26\sum_{j=2}^{5}\binom{5}{j}2^{5-j}
 =54{,}080+6{,}812
 =60{,}892. \tag{10}
\]
Exhaustive ancestral-moral computation checked all cuts counted in (10) and found no counterexample.  This graph audit does not repair the analytic version defect proved in (8)--(9).
