Fix \(Q\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{att}}\), condition on the projected pilots, put \(x_\eta=\log(8/\eta)\) and \(M_h=mh^d\), and let \(T_*^\circ=\mathbb E_Q(T_*\mid\bar\pi,\bar\mu)\).  There are constants \(C_0,C_1<\infty\), depending only on \((d,\kappa,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L_\pi,L_\mu)\) and the fixed basis normalizations, such that, for every finite integer \(k\ge1\), whenever \(M_h\ge C_0x_\eta\),
\[
 \Pr_Q\!\left[
 \lVert T_*-T_*^\circ\rVert_2>C_1\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{x_\eta}{M_h}\left(1+\frac{k}{M_h}\right)}
 +\frac{x_\eta}{M_h}
 +\frac{\sqrt{k}\,x_\eta^{3/2}}{M_h^{3/2}}
 +\frac{kx_\eta^2}{M_h^2}
 \right\}\,\middle|\,\bar\pi,\bar\mu\right]
 \le\eta/2.
\]
The bound is uniform in the realized pilots and in \(J_\pi,J_\mu\), and it imposes no rank condition relating \(k\) to \(M_h\).

Let \(\mathcal G=\sigma(\bar\pi,\bar\mu)\).  The deterministic three-way split in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}private\text{-}higher\text{-}order\text{-}learner}\), together with the independence of the Gaussian releases declared there, implies that, conditionally on \(\mathcal G\), the records \(O_1,\ldots,O_m\) in \(D_*\) remain independent with common law \(P_Q\).  Fix a realization of \(\mathcal G\).  The projected pilots then are deterministic measurable functions satisfying
\[
 \kappa\leq \bar\pi\leq1-\kappa,
 \qquad 0\leq\bar\mu\leq1,
\tag{1}
\]
by Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}private\text{-}higher\text{-}order\text{-}learner}\).  The same definition makes \(L\) and \(H\) bounded measurable kernels.  Because \(\Lambda_L\) and \(\Lambda_H\) are suprema over the ranges in (1),
\[
 \operatorname{clip}_{\Lambda_L}\{L(o)\}=L(o),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{clip}_{\Lambda_H}\{H(o,o')\}=H(o,o')
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(o,o'\in\mathcal O_0\).  Thus it suffices to control the displayed, unclipped kernels.

Write \(r_{\mathrm{out}}=p_\gamma^2+p_\gamma\), the fixed number of scalar coordinates of the concatenated statistic, and fix a coordinate \(j\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}outcomes}\), (1), and the fixed-dimensional bound on \(\rho_h\) give
\[
 |L_j(O)|\leq C h^{-d}{\bf1}_{C_h}(X).
\tag{3}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}design}\) gives \(P_Q(X\in C_h)=\operatorname{Leb}(C_h)=h^d\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_Q L_j(O)^2\leq C h^{-2d}P_Q(X\in C_h)=C h^{-d}.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(Z\) is centered, \(|Z|\leq a\), and \(0\leq t<3/a\), expansion of the exponential series, \(r!\geq2\,3^{r-2}\) for \(r\geq2\), and \(\mathbb E|Z|^r\leq a^{r-2}\mathbb EZ^2\) give
\[
 \mathbb E e^{tZ}
 \leq1+\frac{t^2\mathbb EZ^2}{2}
       \sum_{r=2}^{\infty}(ta/3)^{r-2}
 \leq\exp\!\left\{\frac{t^2\mathbb EZ^2}{2(1-ta/3)}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Multiplying (5) over the independent centered summands, applying the same calculation to \(-Z\), and optimizing in \(t\) yields the Bernstein tail bound.  Integrating that tail by
\(\mathbb E|W|^p=p\int_0^\infty t^{p-1}\Pr(|W|>t)\,dt\) gives, for every \(p\geq2\),
\[
 \left\|\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m
       \{L_j(O_i)-\mathbb E_Q L_j(O_i)\}\right\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\left\{\sqrt{\frac p{M_h}}+\frac p{M_h}\right\},
 \qquad M_h=mh^d.
\tag{6}
\]

We next treat the ordered quadratic statistic.  Put
\[
 P_{h,k}(x,z)=b_{h,k}(x)^{\mathsf T}b_{h,k}(z).
\]
Resolution ordering in the boundary-corrected tensor spline-wavelet basis consists of complete resolution levels and at most a subset of the last level.  The sum over the complete levels is the corresponding boundary-corrected scaling projector; after rescaling \(C_h\) to the unit cube, compact support and uniformly finite overlap bound the absolute integral of its kernel.  At the incomplete last level, a fixed \(x\) meets only a uniformly bounded number of basis supports, and the product of the rescaled \(L^\infty\)- and \(L^1\)-norms of each such basis function is bounded.  Therefore the fixed basis normalization in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}private\text{-}higher\text{-}order\text{-}learner}\) implies
\[
 \sup_{k\geq1}\sup_{x\in C_h}
 \int_{C_h}|P_{h,k}(x,z)|\,dz\leq C.
\tag{7}
\]
Orthonormality and the pointwise basis bound in the same definition also give
\[
 \iint_{C_h^2}P_{h,k}(x,z)^2\,dx\,dz=k,
 \qquad
 \sup_{x\in C_h}\int_{C_h}P_{h,k}(x,z)^2\,dz
 =\sup_{x\in C_h}\|b_{h,k}(x)\|_2^2
 \leq Ckh^{-d}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(H_j\) denote coordinate \(j\) of \(H\), and symmetrize it by
\[
 G_j(o,o')=\frac12\{H_j(o,o')+H_j(o',o)\}.
\]
The ordered-pair sum is unchanged by this symmetrization:
\[
 \sum_{i\ne \ell}H_j(O_i,O_\ell)
 =\sum_{i\ne \ell}G_j(O_i,O_\ell)
 =2\sum_{i<\ell}G_j(O_i,O_\ell).
\tag{9}
\]
Define its Hoeffding projection and canonical part by
\[
 \theta_j=\mathbb E_QG_j(O,O'),
 \quad g_{1,j}(o)=\mathbb E_Q\{G_j(o,O')\}-\theta_j,
 \quad h_j(o,o')=G_j(o,o')-\theta_j-g_{1,j}(o)-g_{1,j}(o').
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(O,O'\) are independent with law \(P_Q\).  By (10), \(h_j\) is symmetric and completely degenerate:
\[
 \mathbb E_Q\{h_j(o,O')\}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_Q\{h_j(O,o')\}=0.
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover, if the ordered canonical kernel is denoted by \(H_{2,j}=h_j\), then
\[
 \sum_{i\ne\ell}H_{2,j}(O_i,O_\ell)
 =2\sum_{i<\ell}h_j(O_i,O_\ell),
\tag{12}
\]
which fixes the normalization used below.  The Hoeffding identity following from (9)--(10) is
\[
 \sum_{i\ne\ell}\{H_j(O_i,O_\ell)-\mathbb E_QH_j(O,O')\}
 =2(m-1)\sum_{i=1}^m g_{1,j}(O_i)
  +\sum_{i\ne\ell}h_j(O_i,O_\ell).
\tag{13}
\]

For either a matrix or vector coordinate, the formulas in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}private\text{-}higher\text{-}order\text{-}learner}\) have the factorization
\[
 H_j(o,o')=h^{-d}{\bf1}_{C_h}(x),u_j(o)
             P_{h,k}(x,x')v_j(o'),
 \qquad |u_j(o)|+|v_j(o')|\leq C,
\tag{14}
\]
where the constant follows from Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}outcomes}\), (1), and the bound on \(\rho_h\).  Equations (7) and (14) show that
\[
 |\mathbb E_Q\{G_j(o,O')\}|\leq Ch^{-d}{\bf1}_{C_h}(x),
 \qquad |\theta_j|\leq C.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently \(|g_{1,j}|\leq Ch^{-d}\) and, using \(P_Q(X\in C_h)=h^d\),
\[
 \mathbb E_Qg_{1,j}(O)^2\leq Ch^{-d}.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the proof of (6) applies to \(m^{-1}\sum_i g_{1,j}(O_i)\), uniformly in the realized pilots.

We now verify all four parameters of the canonical order-two inequality.  From (8) and (14), and from \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_QG_j(O,O')^2\leq Ckh^{-2d},
 \qquad
 \sup_o\mathbb E_Q\{G_j(o,O')^2+G_j(O',o)^2\}
 \leq Ckh^{-3d},
\tag{17}
\]
while the pointwise basis bound gives
\[
 \|G_j\|_\infty\leq Ckh^{-2d}.
\tag{18}
\]
For the operator bound, let \(T_{H_j}\) be the integral operator on \(L^2(P_Q)\) with kernel \(H_j\).  If \(w\in L^2(P_Q)\), conditional Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(z\mapsto\mathbb E_Q[v_j(O')w(O')\mid X'=z]\), followed by the \(L^2\)-contraction of the orthogonal projector with kernel \(P_{h,k}\), gives from (14)
\[
 \|T_{H_j}w\|_{L^2(P_Q)}\leq Ch^{-d}\|w\|_{L^2(P_Q)}.
\tag{19}
\]
The transposed kernel induces \(T_{H_j}^*\), so
\(T_{G_j}=(T_{H_j}+T_{H_j}^*)/2\) and
\[
 \|T_{G_j}\|_{L^2(P_Q)\to L^2(P_Q)}\leq Ch^{-d}.
\tag{20}
\]

It remains to check that Hoeffding centering preserves (17)--(20) at the asserted orders.  Let \(\Pi_0\) be orthogonal projection onto the constants in \(L^2(P_Q)\).  Equation (10) gives
\[
 T_{h_j}=(I-\Pi_0)T_{G_j}(I-\Pi_0),
\]
so (20) and \(\|I-\Pi_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\) imply
\[
 \|T_{h_j}\|_{L^2(P_Q)\to L^2(P_Q)}\leq Ch^{-d}.
\tag{21}
\]
The canonical projection is also an orthogonal projection in \(L^2(P_Q\otimes P_Q)\), and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_Qh_j(O,O')^2\leq\mathbb E_QG_j(O,O')^2\leq Ckh^{-2d}.
\tag{22}
\]
Furthermore, (10), Jensen's inequality, and (17) give, uniformly in \(o\),
\[
 \mathbb E_Qh_j(o,O')^2
 \leq C\left[\mathbb E_QG_j(o,O')^2+\mathbb E_QG_j(O,O')^2\right]
 \leq Ckh^{-3d};
\tag{23}
\]
the same argument applies with the arguments reversed.  Finally, (10) and conditional expectation as an \(L^\infty\)-contraction imply
\[
 \|h_j\|_\infty\leq4\|G_j\|_\infty\leq Ckh^{-2d}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus, defining exactly the four parameters requested by the canonical inequality,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A&=\|h_j\|_\infty,\\
 B^2&=m\left\{\left\|\mathbb E_Q[h_j(O,O')^2\mid O]\right\|_\infty
               +\left\|\mathbb E_Q[h_j(O,O')^2\mid O']\right\|_\infty\right\},\\
 C^2&=m(m-1)\mathbb E_Qh_j(O,O')^2,\\
 D&=m\|T_{h_j}\|_{L^2(P_Q)\to L^2(P_Q)},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
equations (21)--(24) yield
\[
 A\leq Ckh^{-2d},\qquad
 B^2\leq Cmkh^{-3d},\qquad
 C^2\leq Cm^2kh^{-2d},\qquad
 D\leq Cmh^{-d}.
\tag{26}
\]

We now invoke Gine--Latala--Zinn (2000), Corollary 3.4, in the form recorded as Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}gine\text{-}latala\text{-}zinn\text{-}order\text{-}two\text{-}moment}\).  This is applicable because, conditionally on \(\mathcal G\), the observations are identically distributed and independent, \(h_j\) is bounded by (24), and it is completely degenerate by (11).  Let \((O_i^{(1)})_{i=1}^m\) and \((O_i^{(2)})_{i=1}^m\) be independent copies of the main-block sample, and write
\[
 S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=\sum_{i,\ell=1}^m h_j(O_i^{(1)},O_\ell^{(2)}),
 \quad
 S_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=\sum_{i=1}^m h_j(O_i^{(1)},O_i^{(2)}),
 \quad
 S_{\ne}^{\mathrm{dec}}=S_{\mathrm{full}}^{\mathrm{dec}}-S_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{dec}}.
\]
For the full decoupled sum, the corollary's parameters are \(A\), \(B\), \(D\) from (25) and
\[
 C_{\mathrm{full}}^2=m^2\mathbb E_Qh_j(O,O')^2
 \leq\frac{m}{m-1}C^2\leq\frac43C^2,
\tag{27}
\]
where \(m\geq4\).  The variables in \(S_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{dec}}\) are independent and centered by (11).  Applying the Bernstein calculation (5)--(6) without normalization gives
\[
 \|S_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{dec}}\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\left\{\sqrt{mp\,\mathbb E_Qh_j(O,O')^2}+pA\right\}
 \leq C\{C\sqrt p+Ap^2\}.
\tag{28}
\]
The triangle inequality, Corollary 3.4, and (27)--(28) therefore imply
\[
 \|S_{\ne}^{\mathrm{dec}}\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\{C\sqrt p+Dp+Bp^{3/2}+Ap^2\}.
\tag{29}
\]

Apply de la Pena--Montgomery-Smith (1995), Theorem 1, recorded as Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}de\text{-}la\text{-}pena\text{-}montgomery\text{-}smith\text{-}undecoupling}\), to the scalar family \(g_{i\ell}={\bf1}\{i\ne\ell\}h_j\).  Its upper tail comparison and the layer-cake identity give, for the coupled ordered sum \(S_\ne\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_Q|S_\ne|^p
 &=p\int_0^\infty t^{p-1}\Pr_Q(|S_\ne|\geq t)\,dt\\
 &\leq Kp\int_0^\infty t^{p-1}
       \Pr_Q(K|S_{\ne}^{\mathrm{dec}}|\geq t)\,dt
 =K^{p+1}\mathbb E_Q|S_{\ne}^{\mathrm{dec}}|^p.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(\|S_\ne\|_{L^p}\leq K^{1+1/p}\|S_{\ne}^{\mathrm{dec}}\|_{L^p}\), with a universal multiplier for \(p\geq2\).  The kernel is symmetric, and (12) identifies the theorem's distinct-index ordered sum with twice the usual unordered sum.  Consequently (29)--(30) prove, for every \(p\geq2\),
\[
 \left\|\sum_{i\ne\ell}H_{2,j}(O_i,O_\ell)\right\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\{C\sqrt p+Dp+Bp^{3/2}+Ap^2\}.
\tag{31}
\]
Substitution of (26) into (31) proves exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\|\sum_{i\ne\ell}H_{2,j}(O_i,O_\ell)\right\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\{&m\sqrt{kp}\,h^{-d}+mp h^{-d}\\
 &+\sqrt{mk h^{-3d}}\,p^{3/2}+kh^{-2d}p^2\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
The cited results require no relation between \(k\) and \(m\); (17)--(26) are finite for every finite \(k\).  Conditional i.i.d. sampling, complete degeneracy, boundedness, symmetry, and the ordered-pair normalization have now all been verified explicitly.

Since \(n\geq12\), \(m=\lfloor n/3\rfloor\geq4\), and hence \(m(m-1)\geq m^2/2\).  Dividing (32) by \(m(m-1)\) and using \(M_h=mh^d\) yields for the normalized canonical term \(U_{2,j}\)
\[
 \|U_{2,j}\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\left\{
 \frac{\sqrt{kp}}{M_h}+\frac p{M_h}
 +\frac{\sqrt{k}\,p^{3/2}}{M_h^{3/2}}
 +\frac{kp^2}{M_h^2}\right\}.
\tag{33}
\]
Combining (6), its analogue for the first-order term in (13) using (16), and (33), and using
\[
 \sqrt{\frac p{M_h}}+\frac{\sqrt{kp}}{M_h}
 \leq\sqrt2\sqrt{\frac p{M_h}\left(1+\frac{k}{M_h}\right)},
\]
gives, coordinatewise,
\[
 \|(T_*-T_*^\circ)_j\|_{L^p}
 \leq C\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac p{M_h}\left(1+\frac{k}{M_h}\right)}
 +\frac p{M_h}
 +\frac{\sqrt{k}\,p^{3/2}}{M_h^{3/2}}
 +\frac{kp^2}{M_h^2}\right\}.
\tag{34}
\]

Because \(0<\eta<1/4\), \(x_\eta=\log(8/\eta)>2\).  Choose a constant \(c_{\mathrm{out}}\), depending only on the fixed dimension \(r_{\mathrm{out}}\), such that \(r_{\mathrm{out}}e^{-c_{\mathrm{out}}x_\eta}\leq\eta/2\), and put \(p=c_{\mathrm{out}}x_\eta\).  Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr_Q\{|Z|>e\|Z\|_{L^p}\mid\mathcal G\}\leq e^{-p}.
\]
A union bound over the \(r_{\mathrm{out}}\) coordinates, followed by
\(\|v\|_2\leq\sqrt{r_{\mathrm{out}}}\max_j|v_j|\), turns (34) into the probability bound in the statement; replacing \(p\) by \(c_{\mathrm{out}}x_\eta\) changes only the constant \(C_1\).  The proof in fact gives the bound for every \(M_h>0\); therefore one may choose any fixed \(C_0>0\), and the asserted implication under \(M_h\geq C_0x_\eta\) follows.  All constants are uniform in \(J_\pi,J_\mu\) and the realized pilots because only the deterministic ranges (1) were used, and the argument holds for every finite \(k\geq1\).
