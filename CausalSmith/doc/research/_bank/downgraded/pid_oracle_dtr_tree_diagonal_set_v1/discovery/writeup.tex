\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}oracle\_\allowbreak{}dtr\_\allowbreak{}tree\_\allowbreak{}diagonal\_\allowbreak{}set}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} In a finite SMART, the observed law identifies each regime-outcome marginal but not the dependence needed for the perfect-information outcome \(M_Q=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}Y(d)\). The project characterizes every compatible discrete oracle-outcome pmf through local maximum-law capacity inequalities recursively linked over the full-history tree. Its field-tier headline is a projection-and-lift theorem universal over observed laws obeying known positive randomization: each recursively feasible array extends to one complete law \(Q\) preserving that observed margin, with consistency, exchangeability, single-world factorization, and reached-history modularity imposed on \(Q\). A separate kernel-extension lemma completes dormant zero-probability fibers without strengthening the causal model. Corollaries give a tree-sized polytope, exact certificates, attaining samplers, an explicit strictly nonrectangular positive witness, and finite-sample whole-set confidence inversion for oracle remission in SMART-BD.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Static sharp distributional bounds characterize pointwise limits for functions of two potential outcomes, and published oracle-DTR bounds identify sharp binary endpoints and two simultaneous CDF envelopes. Neither literature characterizes all attainable discrete oracle laws or verifies a complete dormant-branch lift for every interior law.

\paragraph{Niche.} The ordered finite outcome support turns local coupling compatibility into a small capacity system, while the full-history SMART tree supplies a laminar recursion that generic response-type programs do not exploit.

\paragraph{Fill.} For every observed law obeying known positive randomization, the tree-diagonal projection theorem equates this recursion with complete FFRCISTG laws whose observed margin is fixed and on which consistency, exchangeability, factorization, and reached-history modularity hold. A theorem-level finite kernel-extension argument coordinates null-fiber versions after the substantive lift, and the resulting corollaries compress the sharp set and map simultaneous multinomial uncertainty through the same program.

\section{Related work}

Shahn2026Oracle is the direct causal anchor: its Theorem 1 gives sharp binary oracle bounds, and Section 4 constructs two bounding CDFs for bounded outcomes. The present target is the distinct interior set of discrete oracle pmfs and its complete counterfactual lift. FanPark2010 Lemma 2.1 is the closest static sharp distributional-bounds comparator: it gives pointwise best-possible CDF bounds for \(Y(1)-Y(0)\) from two identified marginals and notes that the envelopes need not be uniformly sharp. Its static pairwise setting does not perform the first dynamic SMART-tree step here, namely coupling action-specific continuation maxima at every full history and gluing those conditional couplings across descendants into one observed-law-preserving complete FFRCISTG system. Artstein1983 provides the capacity-inequality language for random selections, and LaiRobbins1976 provides an extremal common-marginal maximum coupling; both inform the local construction without serving as claimed tightening targets. Ponomarev2024 Theorem 1 characterizes the smallest nonredundant Artstein-inequality collection for broad set-valued-prediction models and uses bipartite-graph connectivity in finite spaces. The local system here is structurally different: it targets the law of a maximum of heterogeneously margined coordinates, reduces locally to \(mK\) lower-set inequalities plus \(K\) singleton diagonal inequalities, and links them by mass conservation over a treatment tree; it does not claim to improve Ponomarev's minimal collection for his generic correspondence problem. RichardsonRobins2013SWIG fixes FFRCISTG semantics through single-world factorization and modularity on reached distributions; exact agreement of chosen conditional versions on dormant zero-probability fibers is supplied by a separate finite kernel-extension lemma. GreseleEtAl2022 supplies a finite response-function representation of categorical causal compatibility and motivates auditing global gluing. Russell2021 supplies the generic random-set and optimal-transport benchmark, whereas the treatment-tree formulation exploits reach rescaling and laminar structure. OberReynolds2023 and HuangEtAl2017 inform the inference layer. ShpitserTchetgen2016 clarifies why the result is stated under FFRCISTG and does not impose the stronger NPSEM-IE cross-world model.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-randomization]\label{ass:known-randomization}
\(P(H=h,A_h=a)=e_h(a)P(H=h)\quad\forall h\in\mathcal H,\ \forall a\in\mathcal A(h)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known sequential randomization in joint-kernel form, \cite{Shahn2026Oracle}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positivity]\label{ass:positivity}
\(e_h(a)\geq\varepsilon\quad\forall h\in\mathcal H,\ \forall a\in\mathcal A(h)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sequential positivity with a finite-tree lower bound, \cite{Shahn2026Oracle}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Q(O=O^d,\Gamma_d)=Q(\Gamma_d)\quad\forall d\in\mathcal D\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sequential consistency in joint-event form, \cite{Shahn2026Oracle}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sequential-exchangeability]\label{ass:sequential-exchangeability}
\(Q(A_h=a,\mathcal C_h\in B,H=h)=e_h(a)Q(\mathcal C_h\in B,H=h)\quad\forall h\in\mathcal H,\ \forall a\in\mathcal A(h),\ \forall B\subseteq\operatorname{range}(\mathcal C_h)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strong sequential exchangeability in joint-kernel form, \cite{Shahn2026Oracle}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:swig-factorization]\label{ass:swig-factorization}
\(Q(O^d=x)=\prod_{v\in\mathcal V_d}\kappa^d_{Q,v}(x_v\mid x_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v)})\quad\forall d\in\mathcal D,\ \forall x\in\Omega\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (FFRCISTG single-world Markov factorization, \cite{RichardsonRobins2013SWIG}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:swig-modularity]\label{ass:swig-modularity}
\(\kappa^d_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid u)=\kappa^{d'}_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid u')\) whenever \((d,u)\sim_v(d',u')\), \(Q\{O^d_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v)}=u\}>0\), and \(Q\{O^{d'}_{\operatorname{pa}_{d'}(v)}=u'\}>0\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Reached-history SWIG modularity, \cite{RichardsonRobins2013SWIG}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-law-compatibility]\label{ass:observed-law-compatibility}
\(Q(O\in B)=P(O\in B)\quad\forall B\subseteq\Omega\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Observed-law compatibility of a counterfactual completion, \cite{Shahn2026Oracle}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent multinomial sampling, \cite{HuangEtAl2017}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:ffrcistg-completions]\label{def:ffrcistg-completions}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Complete observed-\allowbreak{}law-\allowbreak{}preserving FFRCISTG completion class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak F(P)=\{Q:Q(O\in B)=P(O\in B)\ \forall B\subseteq\Omega,\ Q(O=O^d,\Gamma_d)=Q(\Gamma_d)\ \forall d\in\mathcal D,\ Q(A_h=a,\mathcal C_h\in B,H=h)=e_h(a)Q(\mathcal C_h\in B,H=h)\ \forall h,a,B,\ Q(O^d=x)=\prod_{v\in\mathcal V_d}\kappa^d_{Q,v}(x_v\mid x_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v)})\ \forall d,x,\ \kappa^d_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid u)=\kappa^{d'}_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid u')\text{ whenever }(d,u)\sim_v(d',u')\text{ and both parent fibers have positive probability}\}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}observed-\allowbreak{}law-\allowbreak{}compatibility}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}sequential-\allowbreak{}exchangeability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}swig-\allowbreak{}factorization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}swig-\allowbreak{}modularity}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:local-capacity]\label{def:local-capacity}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Local tree-\allowbreak{}diagonal capacity set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal C(p_1,\ldots,p_m)=\{q\in\Delta(\mathcal Y):F_q(k)\leq F_a(k)\ \forall a\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\ \forall k\in\mathcal Y,\ q(k)\leq\sum_{a=1}^m p_a(k)\ \forall k\in\mathcal Y\}\)
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:reach-rescaling]\label{def:reach-rescaling}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Intervention-\allowbreak{}reach rescaling}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(g(h)=\prod_{(\tilde h,\tilde a)\prec h}e_{\tilde h}(\tilde a),\quad\rho_h=P(H=h)/g(h),\quad U_h(k)=\rho_hF_h(k),\quad V_{ha}(k)=\rho_hG_{ha}(k)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{e\_\allowbreak{}h(a)}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal T}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:tree-polytope]\label{def:tree-polytope}

\begin{theorem}[thm:local-maximum-law]\label{thm:local-maximum-law}
For arbitrary \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) and \(q\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\), there exists a joint law of \((X_1,\ldots,X_m)\) with \(X_a\sim p_a\) and \(\max_a X_a\sim q\) if and only if \(q\in\mathcal C(p_1,\ldots,p_m)\). Every feasible \(q\) admits a finite monotone-residual transport and circle-cover sampler, and every infeasible \(q\) is separated by a violated displayed capacity inequality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(D_a(k)=\min\{p_a(k),q(k)\}\), \(r_a(k)=p_a(k)-D_a(k)\), and \(s_a(k)=q(k)-D_a(k)\).  Necessity follows from the two event inclusions
\[
 \{\max_bX_b\leq k\}\subseteq\{X_a\leq k\},
 \qquad
 \{\max_bX_b=k\}\subseteq\bigcup_{a=1}^m\{X_a=k\}.
\]
Taking probabilities gives \(F_q(k)\leq F_a(k)\) and \(q(k)\leq\sum_a p_a(k)\), respectively.

For sufficiency, fix \(a\).  The residual measures \(r_a\) and \(s_a\) are nonnegative and have the same mass
\[
 R_a:=\sum_jr_a(j)=1-\sum_jD_a(j)=\sum_js_a(j).
\]
Moreover, for each \(k\),
\[
 \sum_{j\leq k}r_a(j)-\sum_{j\leq k}s_a(j)
 =F_a(k)-F_q(k)\geq0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
If \(R_a>0\), let \(U\) be uniform on \((0,R_a)\), and let \(J_a\) and \(K_a\) be the left-continuous inverse distribution functions of the finite measures \(r_a\) and \(s_a\), evaluated at \(U\).  Equation (1) implies \(J_a\leq K_a\): indeed, if \(K_a=k\), then \(U\leq\sum_{j\leq k}s_a(j)\leq\sum_{j\leq k}r_a(j)\), whence \(J_a\leq k\).  Also \(r_a(k)s_a(k)=0\) for every \(k\), so this coupling assigns no mass to \((k,k)\); consequently \(J_a<K_a\) almost surely.  If \(R_a=0\), there is no residual part.  Adding mass \(D_a(k)\) at \((k,k)\) produces a finite table \(t_a(j,k)\) with
\[
 \sum_kt_a(j,k)=p_a(j),\qquad
 \sum_jt_a(j,k)=q(k),\qquad
 t_a(j,k)=0\text{ for }j>k,
 \qquad t_a(k,k)=D_a(k).                                  \tag{2}
\]
This is the claimed monotone-residual transport.

It remains to couple the tables in (2) so that some component hits each proposed maximum.  Draw \(Z\) with mass function \(q\).  For \(q(k)>0\), define
\[
 b_a(k)=\frac{D_a(k)}{q(k)}\in[0,1].
\]
If \(p_a(k)\geq q(k)\) for some \(a\), then \(b_a(k)=1\).  Otherwise, the atomic capacity inequality gives
\[
 \sum_ab_a(k)=\frac{\sum_ap_a(k)}{q(k)}\geq1.              \tag{3}
\]
Place consecutive arcs of lengths \(b_1(k),\ldots,b_m(k)\) on a unit circle.  Their union is the whole circle by (3).  Conditional on \(Z=k\), draw a uniform point on the circle.  Set \(X_a=k\) when the point lies in arc \(a\); off that arc draw \(X_a=j<k\) with probability
\[
 \frac{t_a(j,k)}{q(k)-D_a(k)}.
\]
The latter rule is invoked only when its denominator is positive; by (2) its probabilities sum to one.  Hence
\[
 \Pr(X_a=j\mid Z=k)=\frac{t_a(j,k)}{q(k)}.
\]
After mixing over \(k\), (2) gives \(X_a\sim p_a\).  Every \(X_a\leq Z\), and the covering property ensures that at least one \(X_a=Z\), so \(\max_aX_a=Z\) almost surely.  Values with \(q(k)=0\) require no conditional rule.  This proves sufficiency and supplies the finite sampler.  Finally, every defining inequality of \(\mathcal C(p_1,\ldots,p_m)\) is linear in \(q\); necessity shows that a failed inequality is itself a separating certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact cross-threshold compatibility result that turns scalar oracle bounds into one attainable law. \textit{Gap.} FanPark2010 Lemma 2.1 gives pointwise sharp bounds for the CDF of the static binary-treatment contrast \(Y(1)-Y(0)\) and explicitly notes that the resulting CDF envelopes need not be uniformly sharp; Artstein1983 gives general selection capacities and LaiRobbins1976 gives an extremal common-marginal construction. Ponomarev2024 Theorem 1 instead finds the smallest nonredundant Artstein collection for a supplied set-valued-prediction correspondence. None states this heterogeneous finite maximum-pmf iff: its closed-form lower-set and singleton-diagonal system characterizes one jointly attainable law across all thresholds and supplies an attaining sampler. \textit{Consumer.} Each decision node in the SMART recursion uses this criterion to propagate an entire continuation-outcome law rather than one threshold endpoint.

\begin{lemma}[lem:dormant-kernel-extension]\label{lem:dormant-kernel-extension}
For every \(Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\) and every family of ordinary versions \(\{\kappa^d_{Q,v}\}\) satisfying single-world factorization and reached-history modularity, there exists a simultaneous completion \(\{\bar\kappa_{Q,v}\}\) on every legal parent configuration such that \(\bar\kappa_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid d,u)=\kappa^d_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid u)\) on every positive-probability parent fiber, \(\bar\kappa_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid d,u)=\bar\kappa_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid d',u')\) whenever \((d,u)\sim_v(d',u')\), and \(Q(O^d=x)=\prod_{v\in\mathcal V_d}\bar\kappa_{Q,v}(x_v\mid d,x_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v)})\) for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(x\in\Omega\). Thus all-fiber agreement is a selectable version completion, not an additional causal-model restriction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a random vertex \(v\), and partition all legal pairs \((d,u)\) for that vertex into the finite equivalence classes induced by \(\sim_v\).  In one such class \(E\), let
\[
 E^+=\{(d,u)\in E:Q(O^d_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v)}=u)>0\}.
\]
If \(E^+\neq\varnothing\), reached-history modularity states that all kernels \(\kappa^d_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid u)\), \((d,u)\in E^+\), are the same stochastic vector; assign that vector to \(\bar\kappa_{Q,v}(\cdot\mid d,u)\) for every \((d,u)\in E\).  If \(E^+=\varnothing\), assign one arbitrary stochastic vector, common to every member of \(E\).  Finiteness makes these choices simultaneous over all \(v\) and all classes.  The resulting system agrees with every original kernel on every positive-probability parent fiber and is constant on each \(\sim_v\)-class.

It remains to verify that changing zero-fiber versions does not change a product formula.  Fix \(d\), take a topological ordering \(v_1,\ldots,v_s\) of \(G(d)\), and fix \(x\in\Omega\).  If \(Q(O^d=x)>0\), every parent event appearing along \(x\) has positive probability, so every factor is unchanged.  If \(Q(O^d=x)=0\), the original factorization gives a first index \(j\) for which
\[
 \kappa^d_{Q,v_j}(x_{v_j}\mid x_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v_j)})=0
\]
while all preceding factors are positive.  The probability of the topological prefix \((x_{v_1},\ldots,x_{v_{j-1}})\) is the product of those preceding factors: this follows by summing the later stochastic kernels, each of which sums to one.  Thus the parent fiber of \(v_j\) has positive probability.  Its zero factor is therefore unchanged, and the product formed with \(\bar\kappa_{Q,v}\) is still zero.  In both cases
\[
 Q(O^d=x)=\prod_{v\in\mathcal V_d}
 \bar\kappa_{Q,v}(x_v\mid d,x_{\operatorname{pa}_d(v)}).
\]
The construction changes only conditional versions on null fibers and never changes \(Q\), which proves the assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma separates harmless null-fiber version selection from the substantive global response-system lift. \textit{Gap.} RichardsonRobins2013SWIG constrains the FFRCISTG model on realized single-world distributions; the finite equivalence-class completion needed for an explicit dormant-coordinate construction is usually left implicit. \textit{Consumer.} The projection-and-lift theorem can use common dormant-fiber kernels without presenting that choice as a strengthened causal assumption.

\begin{theorem}[thm:tree-projection-lift]\label{thm:tree-projection-lift}
For every observed law \(P\in\Delta(\Omega)\) satisfying \(P(H=h,A_h=a)=e_h(a)P(H=h)\) and \(e_h(a)\geq\varepsilon>0\) for every legal node-action pair, let \(\mathfrak F(P)\) contain precisely the complete laws \(Q\) whose observed margin is \(P\) and on which joint consistency, joint sequential exchangeability, single-world factorization, and reached-history modularity hold. Then \(\Theta(P)=\operatorname{proj}_{q}\mathcal R(P)\). Moreover, every feasible array is attained by one \(Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\) whose complete counterfactual system contains every dormant branch; the dormant-kernel extension lemma selects agreed \(\bar\kappa_{Q,v}\) versions on all zero-probability fibers without imposing an additional model restriction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The observable map is the multinomial map \(O\mapsto P=(P\{O=\omega\}:\omega\in\Omega)\), whose affine dimension is at most \(|\Omega|-1\).  The unobserved target is \(q_Q=(Q\{M_Q=k\}:k\in\mathcal Y)\), of affine dimension \(K-1\), and its causal identified set is, by definition,
\[
 \Theta(P)=\{q_Q:Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\}.                   \tag{13}
\]

We first prove \(\Theta(P)\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_q\mathcal R(P)\).  Fix \(Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\).  For a full-history prefix ending at \(h\), choose a regime \(d_h\) that takes every treatment recorded in that prefix and initially define \(\Xi_h^{d_h}\) to be the event that \(O^{d_h}\) reaches \(h\).  We now prove that the choice of \(d_h\) is immaterial.  If \(D\subseteq\{H=h\}\) is measurable with respect to \(\mathcal C_h\), sequential exchangeability gives
\[
 Q(D,A_h=a)=e_h(a)Q(D).                                   \tag{14}
\]
Therefore \(Q(D,A_h=a)=0\), together with positivity, implies \(Q(D)=0\).  This cancellation proves prefix consistency by induction on the number of decisions.  For a regime \(d\) and a depth \(s\), let \(R_{d,s}\) be the event that the observed treatment variables follow \(d\) through depth \(s\), and let \(D_{d,s}\) be the event that \(O\) and \(O^d\) disagree at or before that depth.  Conditional on each possible finite future history after depth \(s\), repeated use of (14) shows that following \(d\) at all remaining decisions has conditional probability equal to the product of the corresponding \(e_h(d(h))\), hence at least \(\varepsilon^{T-s}\).  Thus \(Q(D_{d,s}\cap R_{d,s})>0\) would imply \(Q(D_{d,s}\cap\Gamma_d)>0\).  Consistency makes the latter event null, so
\[
 Q(D_{d,s}\cap R_{d,s})=0.                                \tag{15}
\]
Now let \(d\) and \(d'\) prescribe the same actions through a prefix.  On the event that the observed randomizers follow that common prefix, (15) makes both counterfactual prefixes equal to the observed prefix.  The event that the two counterfactual prefixes disagree is measurable in the successive future vectors \(\mathcal C_h\).  Starting at the last action in the common prefix and applying the cancellation following (14) removes each matching-treatment indicator.  Induction back to baseline shows that the two counterfactual prefixes, and their reach indicators, agree almost surely.  We may therefore write their common reach event as \(\Xi_h\).

Apply the same cancellations to the indicator of \(\Xi_h\).  On the event that the observed randomizers take the treatments recorded in \(h\), (15) equates observed and counterfactual reach.  Each cancellation contributes its known factor, so
\[
 P(H=h)=g(h)Q(\Xi_h),\qquad
 Q(\Xi_h)=\frac{P(H=h)}{g(h)}=\rho_h.                     \tag{16}
\]
No division by zero occurs because \(g(h)\geq\varepsilon^{T}>0\).  Equations (14)--(16) establish the shared-prefix fact rather than assuming a cross-world independence.

On \(\Xi_h\), let \(M_h\) be the maximum terminal outcome over all suffix policies from \(h\), and let \(M_{ha}\) be the corresponding maximum when the first action is fixed to \(a\).  Full histories retain their prefixes and \(\mathcal D\) contains every deterministic action map.  Consequently the following identities are pointwise, not distributional assumptions:
\[
 M_h=\max_{a\in\mathcal A(h)}M_{ha},
 \qquad
 M_{ha}=M_{h'}\text{ on }\Xi_{h'}\text{ for }h'\in\operatorname{ch}(h,a),  \tag{17}
\]
with \(M_{ha}=Y\) on a terminal branch.  Define the unnormalized arrays
\[
 U_h(k)=Q(\Xi_h,M_h\leq k),
 \qquad
 V_{ha}(k)=Q(\Xi_h,M_{ha}\leq k).                         \tag{18}
\]
Their boundary values and nonnegative increments follow from (16) and from their being subdistribution functions.  Conditional on \(\Xi_h\) when \(\rho_h>0\), the local maximum-law theorem applied to (17) gives
\[
 U_h(k)\leq V_{ha}(k),qquad
 \Delta U_h(k)\leq\sum_a\Delta V_{ha}(k).                \tag{19}
\]
When \(\rho_h=0\), (19) is the equality \(0\leq0\).  The disjoint chance-child events in (17) give
\[
 V_{ha}(k)=\sum_{h'\in\operatorname{ch}(h,a)}U_{h'}(k)    \tag{20}
\]
on a nonterminal branch.  Repeating the peeling calculation (14) through action \(a\) gives, on a terminal branch,
\[
 V_{ha}(k)=\frac{P(H=h,A_h=a,Y\leq k)}{g(h)e_h(a)}=b_{ha}(k). \tag{21}
\]
Baseline is not intervened upon, so its reach events partition the population.  Hence
\[
 F_{q_Q}(k)=Q(M_Q\leq k)=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_0}U_h(k).   \tag{22}
\]
Equations (18)--(22) are all constraints of \(\mathcal R(P)\), proving the first inclusion.

For the reverse inclusion, the positive universal-lift theorem constructs, from every feasible array in \(\mathcal R(P)\), one \(Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\) whose oracle mass function is its projected \(q\).  It also constructs every dormant response branch.  The dormant-kernel extension lemma changes only versions on null parent fibers and preserves all single-world product probabilities.  Thus \(\operatorname{proj}_q\mathcal R(P)\subseteq\Theta(P)\).  Combining the two inclusions proves equality.  Because every point of the projection is attained, this is a sharp identified set, not merely an outer bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Projection equality makes the local capacity recursion a sharp causal identified set rather than a relaxation, while the separate extension lemma prevents null-fiber conventions from strengthening the model. \textit{Gap.} FanPark2010 sharply bounds a static two-potential-outcome contrast one threshold at a time, and Shahn2026Oracle proves dynamic endpoint constructions. Neither establishes equality between a whole recursively feasible pmf polytope and the projection of a complete FFRCISTG response system; the new step is the history-by-history coupling and global gluing of continuation maxima under ordinary reached-history modularity. \textit{Consumer.} SMART-BD investigators can interpret every feasible oracle-remission distribution as arising from a causally admissible response system with the observed trial law unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[thm:compact-formulation]\label{thm:compact-formulation}
As a corollary of the projection-and-lift theorem, let \(N=\lvert\mathcal H\rvert\), \(A_\Sigma=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}\lvert\mathcal A(h)\rvert\), and \(E=\sum_{h,a}\lvert\operatorname{ch}(h,a)\rvert\). The formulation \(\mathcal R(P)\) has \(O\{K(N+A_\Sigma)\}\) variables and constraints and \(O\{K(N+A_\Sigma+E)\}\) nonzero coefficients, all affine in observed multinomial probabilities. Membership of \(q\) and \(\sigma_P(c)\) have exact primal and strong-dual certificates, and any feasible solution yields a recursive attaining sampler.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use coordinates for \(q\), all \(U_h(k)\), and all \(V_{ha}(k)\), including the two displayed boundary coordinates.  Their number is at most
\[
 K+(K+1)N+(K+1)A_\Sigma=O\{K(N+A_\Sigma)\}.              \tag{22}
\]
There are two boundary equations and one increment inequality per node or node-action CDF, \(K A_\Sigma\) domination inequalities, \(K N\) atomic-capacity inequalities, one branch equation per node-action-threshold, and \(K\) root equations.  Thus the number of rows is \(O\{K(N+A_\Sigma)\}\).  All rows except the nonterminal branch equations have a uniformly bounded number of nonzero coefficients, apart from the action sums in the atomic-capacity rows, whose total length is \(K A_\Sigma\).  The branch rows contribute \(K E\) child coefficients.  Consequently the complete matrix has
\[
 O\{K(N+A_\Sigma+E)\}                                    \tag{23}
\]
nonzero coefficients.

The coefficient matrix is fixed by the explicit tree and the known assignment kernels.  Indeed,
\[
 \rho_h=\frac{P(H=h)}{g(h)},qquad
 b_{ha}(k)=\frac{P(H=h,A_h=a,Y\leq k)}{g(h)e_h(a)},        \tag{24}
\]
and every denominator in (24) is a known positive number, bounded below by a power of \(\varepsilon\).  Therefore every right-hand side is linear, hence affine, in the observed multinomial vector \((P\{O=\omega\})_{\omega\in\Omega}\).

For completeness, we verify the certificate claim rather than hiding it in an optimization name.  After replacing each equality by two inequalities, membership of a fixed \(q\) has the form
\[
 Ax\leq b(P,q),                                           \tag{25}
\]
where \(x\) collects the remaining arrays.  A feasible \(x\) is the primal certificate.  If (25) is infeasible, finite elimination gives multipliers \(y\geq0\) such that
\[
 y^\top A=0,qquad y^\top b(P,q)<0,                       \tag{26}
\]
which is an exact infeasibility certificate, because (25) would imply \(0=y^\top Ax\leq y^\top b(P,q)\).  To see existence in (26), split every free coordinate into positive and negative parts and add nonnegative slacks.  The resulting feasibility question asks whether a fixed vector lies in a finitely generated cone.  Such a cone is closed: eliminate dependent generators from any representation until at most the ambient dimension remain, and take the finite union over linearly independent generator sets.  If the vector is outside, its Euclidean nearest point in the cone supplies a separating vector, which gives (26).

For support in direction \(c\), maximize \(c^\top q\) subject to the same finite rows.  The feasible set is closed and bounded: root and node increments are nonnegative and their totals are at most one, so every coordinate lies in \([0,1]\).  An optimum \(x^\star\) therefore exists.  Let \(A_Ix\leq b_I\) be its active inequalities and retain the equality rows as equalities.  If the objective normal were not a nonnegative combination of the active inequality normals plus a linear combination of the equality normals, separation of that finitely generated cone plus subspace would give a feasible direction increasing the objective; a sufficiently small step would preserve every inactive strict inequality, contradicting optimality.  Hence there are exact multipliers \(\lambda\geq0\) and unrestricted \(\nu\) satisfying dual feasibility and
\[
 c^\top q^\star=\lambda^\top b_I+\nu^\top d.             \tag{27}
\]
Weak duality shows that (27) is the common primal and dual value.  Thus the primal optimizer and these multipliers are exact strong-dual certificates.  By the projection-and-lift theorem this value equals \(\sigma_P(c)\), not merely the support of an outer relaxation.  Finally, normalizing any feasible \(U,V\) at positive-reach nodes and applying the finite local circle-cover sampler recursively gives the attaining response-object sampler constructed in the lift theorem; zero-reach nodes are filled arbitrarily.  This establishes every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The tree-sized fixed matrix makes full-law sharpness computationally usable once exact causal projection is established. \textit{Gap.} Russell2021 permits generic random-set and transport programs, GreseleEtAl2022 permits full response-function projection, and Ponomarev2024 minimizes the Artstein collection for a generic set-valued-prediction correspondence. None combines the maximum-specific local system with intervention reach and tree laminarity to obtain this dimension bound. \textit{Consumer.} A trial analyst can compute exact feasibility, quantile bounds, tail probabilities, or any linear oracle-law support value without enumerating exponentially many global response types.

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-nonrectangularity]\label{thm:strict-nonrectangularity}
For \(K\geq3\), there is a relatively open class of strictly positive two-stage binary-action SMART laws \(P\) for which \(\Theta(P)\subsetneq\mathcal B_{\mathrm S}(P)\). At the displayed witness, \(L_{P^\star}(0)=L_{P^\star}(1)=0\), \(U_{P^\star}(0)=0.45\), and \(U_{P^\star}(1)=0.50\), while \(F_{q^\star}(0)=0.05\) and \(F_{q^\star}(1)=0.40\). Thus \(q^\star\) is strictly inside the coordinatewise envelope, but the propagated tree-capacity facet \(q(1)\leq0.20\) excludes it because \(q^\star(1)=0.35\). The positive terminal-cell margin, envelope margin \(0.05\), and violation margin \(0.15\), together with continuity of the finite programs, preserve the separation on a relative open neighborhood of \(P^\star\) in the randomization model.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider first \(K=3\) and the law \(P^\star\) in the witness definition.  At each last-stage history and for either second-stage action, the conditional terminal mass function is \(r^\star=(0.45,0.05,0.50)\).  For a fixed threshold, the CDF of the maximum of two variables with component CDF values \(x_0,x_1\) ranges from \(\max\{0,x_0+x_1-1\}\) to \(\min\{x_0,x_1\}\): the upper inequality is event inclusion, the lower inequality is the union bound applied to the complements, and both endpoints are attained by the local maximum-law construction.  Chance-child mixing preserves the identical values here.  Applying this calculation at the last stage and then at the root gives
\[
 L_{P^\star}(0)=L_{P^\star}(1)=0,qquad
 U_{P^\star}(0)=0.45,qquad U_{P^\star}(1)=0.50.          \tag{28}
\]
Thus \(q^\star=(0.05,0.35,0.60)\), for which
\[
 F_{q^\star}(0)=0.05,qquad F_{q^\star}(1)=0.40,          \tag{29}
\]
is strictly inside all nonconstant coordinates of the envelope region \(\mathcal B_{\mathrm S}(P^\star)\).

It is nevertheless excluded by a cross-threshold facet.  A child \(h=(a_0,z)\) has \(\rho_h=1/2\), and each of its two terminal action branches has unnormalized mass \(0.025\) at outcome \(1\).  Its atomic-capacity row therefore gives
\[
 \Delta U_{(a_0,z)}(1)
 \leq\sum_{a_1=0}^1\Delta V_{(a_0,z),a_1}(1)=0.05.        \tag{30}
\]
The nonterminal branch equations and then the root atomic-capacity row imply
\[
 q(1)=\Delta U_{h_\varnothing}(1)
 \leq\sum_{a_0=0}^1\sum_{z=0}^1
       \Delta U_{(a_0,z)}(1)
 \leq0.20.                                                \tag{31}
\]
But \(q^\star(1)=0.35\).  The projection-and-lift theorem identifies the projection with \(\Theta(P^\star)\), so (31) proves \(q^\star\notin\Theta(P^\star)\) while (28)--(29) prove \(q^\star\in\mathcal B_{\mathrm S}(P^\star)\).

Every terminal trajectory cell has probability at least \(0.05/8>0\).  The backward scalar envelope uses only finitely many additions, minima, and maxima and is therefore continuous in the observed cells.  The right side of (31) is a linear function of those cells after the fixed positive reach rescaling.  Hence the envelope slack, whose minimum at the free thresholds is \(0.05\), and the exclusion slack \(0.15\) remain positive throughout some relative open neighborhood of \(P^\star\) inside the known-randomization affine model; shrinking the neighborhood preserves strict positivity.

For \(K>3\), choose \(\eta>0\) smaller than these strict margins and smaller than \(0.50\).  Move total mass \(\eta\) from outcome \(2\) to outcomes \(3,\ldots,K-1\), positively among all of them, in every terminal kernel and in the candidate mass function.  Equations (28)--(31) at thresholds \(0\) and \(1\) are unchanged, while all added outcome cells are positive.  The same relative-neighborhood argument applies.  This proves the strict inclusion for every \(K\geq3\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit strict witness shows that the whole-law result contains identifying information invisible in every separate threshold interval. \textit{Gap.} Shahn2026Oracle establishes attainable envelope functions but does not display the interior geometry, the excluding cross-threshold tree facet, or a positive open set with strict nonrectangularity. \textit{Consumer.} Joint remission-threshold, quantile, and stochastic-dominance conclusions in SMART-BD can be certified without combining mutually incompatible pointwise bounds.

\begin{theorem}[thm:whole-set-confidence]\label{thm:whole-set-confidence}
As a corollary of exact projection-and-lift and the compact fixed-matrix formulation, under independent sampling and known randomization, \(\Pr_P\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\mathcal O_n\}\geq1-\alpha\) for every \(n\geq1\) and every \(P\in\mathcal E_e\). Membership and every fixed linear support query for \(\mathcal O_n\) reduce to a linear program obtained by adjoining the constraints defining \(\mathcal K_n\) to \(\mathcal R(p)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For one cell \(\omega\), put \(X_i=\mathbf 1\{O_i=\omega\}\) and \(\mu=P(O=\omega)\).  We first derive the required concentration bound.  For any real \(\lambda\),
\[
 \phi_\mu(\lambda)
 =\log E\exp\{\lambda(X_i-\mu)\}
\]
satisfies \(\phi_\mu(0)=\phi_\mu'(0)=0\).  Its second derivative is the variance of a Bernoulli variable under exponential tilting and is at most \(1/4\).  Taylor's formula with integral remainder therefore gives \(\phi_\mu(\lambda)\leq\lambda^2/8\).  Independence and Markov's inequality yield
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat p(\omega)-P(\omega)\geq r\}
 \leq\inf_{\lambda>0}\exp\{-\lambda nr+n\lambda^2/8\}
 =e^{-2nr^2};                                             \tag{32}
\]
the same calculation with \(-\lambda\) gives the lower tail.  A union bound over \(|\Omega|\) cells and the definition of \(r_n\) give
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{\max_{\omega\in\Omega}
 |\widehat p(\omega)-P(\omega)|\leq r_n\right\}
 \geq1-2|\Omega|e^{-2nr_n^2}=1-\alpha.                   \tag{33}
\]
Known randomization means \(P\in\mathcal E_e\).  Thus the event in (33) implies \(P\in\mathcal K_n\).

On that event, take any \(q\in\Theta(P)\).  Exact projection and lift give arrays \(U,V\) with \((q,U,V)\in\mathcal R(P)\).  The definition of \(\mathcal O_n\) permits the witness \(p=P\), so \(q\in\mathcal O_n\).  Since this holds simultaneously for every \(q\in\Theta(P)\),
\[
 \Pr_P\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\mathcal O_n\}
 \geq\Pr_P\{P\in\mathcal K_n\}\geq1-\alpha.            \tag{34}
\]
No boundary differentiability, active-face uniqueness, or asymptotic limit is used.

Finally, the constraints \(p\in\mathcal E_e\), the cellwise box constraints defining \(\mathcal K_n\), and the fixed-matrix constraints of \(\mathcal R(p)\) are all linear in the joint variables \((p,q,U,V)\).  Fixing \(q\) gives one feasibility linear program for membership.  Maximizing \(c^\top q\) gives one linear program for any fixed support query, with the exact primal and dual certificates established in the compact-formulation theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The statement covers the entire random identified set in finite samples and remains valid at active-face ties. \textit{Gap.} HuangEtAl2017 handles static ordinal bound endpoints and OberReynolds2023 handles scalar transport values; neither gives finite-sample whole-polytope coverage for a longitudinal oracle distribution. \textit{Consumer.} SMART-BD can report an honest simultaneous region for every oracle-remission pmf, CDF, quantile, and prespecified linear summary.

\begin{proposition}[prop:binary-reduction]\label{prop:binary-reduction}
When \(K=2\), projection of \(\mathcal R(P)\) onto \(q(1)\) equals the sharp interval \([V^-(P),V^+(P)]\) from Shahn2026Oracle, so the construction introduces no extra restriction in the binary-outcome special case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When \(K=2\), a mass function \(q\) is uniquely determined by \(q(1)\), since \(q(0)=1-q(1)\).  The projection-and-lift theorem gives
\[
 \operatorname{proj}_{q(1)}\mathcal R(P)
 =\{Q(M_Q=1):Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\}.                       \tag{35}
\]
The cited sharp binary oracle theorem identifies the right side of (35) as \([V^-(P),V^+(P)]\).  Hence the full-law formulation has exactly the established binary interval and imposes no additional restriction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction is the required sanity check against the known sharp binary recursion. \textit{Gap.} This is a compatibility corollary rather than a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} Existing binary oracle analyses can adopt the full-law machinery without changing their established endpoints.

\begin{theorem}[thm:complete-ffrcistg-lift-positive]\label{thm:complete-ffrcistg-lift-positive}
Universal lifting holds: for every observed law \(P\) satisfying known sequential randomization and \(e_h(a)\geq\varepsilon>0\), every \((q,\{U_h(k)\},\{V_{ha}(k)\})\in\mathcal R(P)\) is attained by a single \(Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\) with \(q_Q=q\).  The attaining law may be carried by one finite recursive response object containing every action branch and every dormant chance-child branch.  Its observed margin is \(P\), joint consistency and joint sequential exchangeability hold, and every \(O^d\) obeys the required single-world factorization and reached-history modularity.  Applying the dormant-kernel extension lemma supplies agreed \(\bar\kappa_{Q,v}\) on all zero-probability parent fibers without changing \(Q\).  Hence no additional separating facet is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\Delta U_h(k)=U_h(k)-U_h(k-1)\) and similarly for \(V\).  Positivity makes every prefix factor \(g(h)>0\).  If \(\rho_h>0\), define the normalized mass functions
\[
 f_h(k)=\frac{\Delta U_h(k)}{\rho_h},
 \qquad
 g_{ha}(k)=\frac{\Delta V_{ha}(k)}{\rho_h}.                \tag{4}
\]
The boundary and monotonicity constraints in \(\mathcal R(P)\) make these probability mass functions.  Its two local capacity constraints become
\[
 \sum_{j\leq k}f_h(j)\leq\sum_{j\leq k}g_{ha}(j),
 \qquad
 f_h(k)\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal A(h)}g_{ha}(k).             \tag{5}
\]
Thus the local maximum-law theorem applies at every positive-reach node.  If \(\rho_h=0\), the boundary and monotonicity constraints force every \(U_h(k)\) and \(V_{ha}(k)\) to be zero; choose any probability mass function \(f_h\) and complete that node arbitrarily.  It receives zero weight from every positive-reach ancestor.

We now construct finite response-object laws \(\mu_h\) by backward induction.  A response object at \(h\) contains one complete branch object \(B_{ha}\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A(h)\).  At a terminal action branch, let \(B_{ha}=Y_{ha}\).  From known randomization,
\[
 \Pr(Y_{ha}=k)
 =\frac{\Delta b_{ha}(k)}{\rho_h}
 =\frac{P(H=h,A_h=a,Y=k)}{e_h(a)P(H=h)}                  \tag{6}
\]
whenever \(\rho_h>0\), so (6) is exactly the observed conditional outcome law.

At a nonterminal branch define
\[
 w_{ha}(h')=\frac{\rho_{h'}}{\rho_h},qquad
 h'\in\operatorname{ch}(h,a).                            \tag{7}
\]
The endpoint branch equality in \(\mathcal R(P)\) gives \(\sum_{h'}\rho_{h'}=\rho_h\), so (7) is a probability vector.  Moreover, because \(g(h')=g(h)e_h(a)\), known randomization yields
\[
 w_{ha}(h')
 =\frac{P(H=h')}{e_h(a)P(H=h)}
 =P\{H'=h'\mid H=h,A_h=a\}.                              \tag{8}
\]
Sample a chance child \(J_{ha}\) according to (7), and, conditional on \(J_{ha}=h'\), sample the selected child response object with law \(\mu_{h'}\).  Also attach independent copies with laws \(\mu_{\tilde h}\) for every nonselected \(\tilde h\in\operatorname{ch}(h,a)\); these copies are dormant but make the response system complete.  Let \(\nu_{ha}\) denote this entire branch law.  Its selected continuation maximum has mass function
\[
 \sum_{h'}w_{ha}(h')f_{h'}(k)
 =\frac{\sum_{h'}\Delta U_{h'}(k)}{\rho_h}
 =g_{ha}(k),                                               \tag{9}
\]
where the last equality is the nonterminal branch equation.  At a terminal branch the same conclusion follows from (6).

By (5) and the local maximum-law theorem, there is a coupling \(\lambda_h\) of variables \((Z_{ha}:a\in\mathcal A(h))\), with \(Z_{ha}\sim g_{ha}\), such that \(\max_aZ_{ha}\sim f_h\).  Conditional on the coupled values, sample the complete branch objects independently according to
\[
 B_{ha}\sim\nu_{ha}(\,\cdot\mid M(B_{ha})=Z_{ha}).        \tag{10}
\]
Only positive-mass fibers in (10) are used.  Disintegration is just division on a finite set.  Since \(Z_{ha}\) has the \(\nu_{ha}\)-marginal law of \(M(B_{ha})\), summing (10) over \(Z_{ha}\) restores exactly \(\nu_{ha}\).  Hence all transition and outcome marginals in (8)--(9) are preserved, while the assembled response object has maximum law \(f_h\).  This completes the backward induction.

At baseline, draw \(H_0=h\) with probability \(P(H=h)=\rho_h\), sample its response object from \(\mu_h\), and attach independent response objects for the other, dormant baseline nodes.  Because baseline masses sum to one and \(F_q(k)=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_0}U_h(k)\),
\[
 \Pr(M\leq k)=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_0}\rho_h
       \sum_{j\leq k}f_h(j)
 =\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_0}U_h(k)=F_q(k).                  \tag{11}
\]

Finally, independently of the complete response object, draw a categorical treatment variable at every legal history \(h\), with probabilities \(e_h(\cdot)\), and generate \(O\) by following the selected action and selected chance-child coordinates.  Equations (6) and (8), induction over the finite depth, and the ordinary chain rule give \(Q(O=\omega)=P(O=\omega)\) for each \(\omega\in\Omega\); zero-reach paths have probability zero on both sides.  For each \(d\), define \(O^d\) by evaluating the same response object with treatments fixed to \(d\).  On \(\Gamma_d\) this evaluation follows exactly the observed branches, so \(O=O^d\), proving joint consistency.  Since the fresh \(A_h\) is independent of the response object and of the other treatment draws,
\[
 Q(A_h=a,\mathcal C_h\in B,H=h)
 =e_h(a)Q(\mathcal C_h\in B,H=h),                         \tag{12}
\]
which is joint sequential exchangeability.

For a fixed \(d\), only one action branch at each reached node is evaluated.  Its unconditional marginal is \(\nu_{ha}\), notwithstanding the cross-action coupling in (10).  Equations (6), (8), and backward induction therefore factor the law of \(O^d\) into the baseline, chance-transition, and terminal kernels of \(G(d)\).  The same local kernel is used whenever two regimes have the same fixed inputs and random parents, so reached-history modularity holds.  We have constructed one \(Q\in\mathfrak F(P)\) containing all dormant branches and satisfying \(q_Q=q\).  The dormant-kernel extension lemma then completes the kernel versions on null parent fibers without changing any probability.  This proves universal lifting and rules out an additional facet.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:shahn-binary-oracle-sharpness]\label{lem:shahn-binary-oracle-sharpness}
When \(K=2\), under known sequential randomization and sequential positivity in the finite SMART FFRCISTG model, the sharp identified set for the oracle success probability \(Q\{\max_{d\in\mathcal D}Y(d)=1\}\) is the interval \([V^-(P),V^+(P)]\).
\end{lemma}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The transport construction and reduced LP calculations are diagnostics, not established theorems. An independent finite response-type calculation matched the reduced formulation in 160 support directions over ten heterogeneous positive two-stage binary-action, binary-intermediate, three-outcome kernels, with maximum absolute discrepancy \(6.7\times10^{-16}\). This numerical kill test checks effective response-tree projection but does not certify the complete FFRCISTG response-system embedding. The separate dormant-kernel extension is finite equivalence-class bookkeeping once a reached response system exists; it does not resolve the global lift. Any recursively feasible point lacking such a lift invalidates the projection-and-lift statement and requires replacing \(\mathcal R(P)\) by the projected global polytope or adding the first missing facet. The claimed complexity is in explicit tree size; it is not polynomial in horizon under a compressed state representation.

\section{Honest scope}

The local finite maximum-law iff has a concrete monotone-residual transport route. The complete dormant-branch FFRCISTG response-system lift under known randomization, positivity, and ordinary reached-history modularity remains the genuine open content and is the premise for the compact-LP, sampler, nonrectangularity-as-an-identified-set, and inference corollaries. Exact agreement of kernel versions on dormant null fibers is a separate theorem-level extension problem and is not maintained as a causal assumption. The nonrectangularity witness displays its envelope coordinates, interior candidate, excluding facet, positive-cell margin, and relative open-neighborhood perturbation argument. Static continuous copula diagonals, unknown-randomization inference, restricted policy classes, missing outcomes, and NPSEM-IE tightening are outside the theorem claims. The binary reduction to Shahn2026Oracle is mandatory.
