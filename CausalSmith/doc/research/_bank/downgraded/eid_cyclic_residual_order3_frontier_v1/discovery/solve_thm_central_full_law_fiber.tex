%%% FIELD observable-proof
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_3\). By \(KX=\varepsilon\), the structural equations give
\[
X_1-aX_2=\varepsilon _1,
\qquad
X_3-dX_2=\varepsilon _3.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:path\mbox{-}connected\mbox{-}set\mbox{-}markov}\) says \(\varepsilon _1\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon _3\). The finite-third-moment assumption makes all cumulants below finite. A joint cumulant containing variables from two independent blocks is zero: substituting the factorization of mixed moments under independence into the order-two and order-three moment--cumulant formulas cancels every term. Consequently
\[
F_{11}(a,d)=\kappa(\varepsilon _1,\varepsilon _3)=0,
\quad
F_{21}(a,d)=\kappa(\varepsilon _1,\varepsilon _1,\varepsilon _3)=0,
\quad
F_{12}(a,d)=\kappa(\varepsilon _1,\varepsilon _3,\varepsilon _3)=0.
\]
Multilinearity of covariance and Definition \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}cubics}\) give, for arbitrary complex \((\alpha,\delta)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{11}(\alpha,\delta)
 &=\Sigma_{13}-\delta\Sigma_{12}-\alpha\Sigma_{23}
     +\alpha\delta\Sigma_{22}\\
 &=r-p\delta-q\alpha+v\alpha\delta
   =l(\alpha)\delta-t(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(v\chi\neq0\), \(v\neq0\). If \(l(\alpha)=0\), then \(\alpha=p/v\), and hence
\[
F_{11}(p/v,\delta)=-t(p/v)
=-\left(qp/v-r\right)=\chi/v\neq0.
\]
Thus a residual common zero has \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), and its covariance equation uniquely gives
\[
\delta=\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}.
\]
Substitution in the two third-order equations and multiplication by the indicated nonzero powers of \(l(\alpha)\) yields
\[
P_3(\alpha)=l(\alpha)F_{21}\!\left(\alpha,\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}\right)=0,
\qquad
Q_3(\alpha)=l(\alpha)^2F_{12}\!\left(\alpha,\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}\right)=0.
\]
Conversely, if \(P_3(\alpha)=Q_3(\alpha)=0\) and \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), define \(\delta=t(\alpha)/l(\alpha)\). The displayed covariance identity gives \(F_{11}=0\), and division by \(l(\alpha)\) and \(l(\alpha)^2\), respectively, gives \(F_{21}=F_{12}=0\). Uniqueness of the lift follows from the linear equation \(l(\alpha)\delta=t(\alpha)\). Notice that the condition \(l(\alpha)\neq0\) on the converse is essential: if \(T_3=0\), then \(P_3=Q_3=0\) identically even though the value \(l(\alpha)=0\) does not solve \(F_{11}=0\).

and the endpoint third moments are unchanged. Thus all third moments remain finite. The endpoint marginals remain non-Gaussian. Write \(\mathcal P_6=\{P_m:m\in\mathfrak M_6\}\). Compactness of \(\mathcal P_6\) in sixth-Wasserstein distance has two consequences used below:
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_6}E_P\|X\|^6<\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_6}

Indeed, sixth-Wasserstein convergence is weak convergence together with convergence of sixth moments; if either display failed, a sequence in the compact set would have a convergent subsequence contradicting that characterization. Thus every polynomial in \(X\) of degree at most three has a uniformly square-integrable envelope, and every polynomial of degree at most six has a uniformly integrable envelope.

The entries of \(T_2,T_3\), and hence the coefficients of \(g_m(\vartheta)\), vary continuously in sixth-Wasserstein distance. We next identify the exact linear term created by empirical centering. At the truth let \(e_{1i}=\varepsilon_{1i}\), \(e_{3i}=\varepsilon_{3i}\). For covariance,

\end{aligned}
\]
After multiplication by \(\sqrt n\), the final three terms are uniformly \(o_{P_m}(1)\), by the uniform bounds through order six and endpoint independence. Interchanging indices \(1,3\) gives the corresponding formula for the \((1,2)\) cumulant. Therefore
\[
\sqrt n\,g_{n,m}(\theta(m))
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_m(X_{i,m}^{\mathrm{obs}})+o_{P_m}(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in }m.
\]
This also proves directly that the centering terms in the definition of \(\psi_m\) are necessary.

Let \(W_m=S_m^{-1}\) and \(A_m=J_m^\top W_mJ_m\). Uniform sixth-moment bounds bound \(\|S_m\|\) and \(\|J_m\|\), while the two singular-value assumptions give constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that
\[
cI_2\preceq A_m\preceq CI_2
\quad\text{for every }m\in\mathfrak M_6.
\]
On the event on which the consistent estimator lies in the common interior neighborhood, its first-order condition is
\[
0=\dot g_{n,m}(\widehat\theta_{n,m})^\top
\widehat S_{n,m}^{-1}g_{n,m}(\widehat\theta_{n,m}).
\]
Taylor expansion around \(\theta(m)\), uniform convergence of the first two derivatives, and the preceding eigenvalue bounds first imply
\(\sqrt n\|\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m)\|=O_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly, and then give
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))
=-A_m^{-1}J_m^\top S_m^{-1}\sqrt n\,g_{n,m}(\theta(m))+o_{P_m}(1)
\]
uniformly. Substitution of the exact centered-cumulant expansion proves the first asserted limit.

The same uniform polynomial laws give
\[
\widehat J_{n,m}-J_m=o_{P_m}(1),
\qquad
\widehat S_{n,m}-S_m=o_{P_m}(1)
\]
uniformly. Matrix inversion is uniformly Lipschitz on the eigenvalue-margin sets just displayed, so
\[
\widehat V_{n,m}
=(\widehat J_{n,m}^\top\widehat S_{n,m}^{-1}\widehat J_{n,m})^{-1}
=V_m+o_{P_m}(1)
\]
uniformly, proving the second asserted limit.

For the distributional conclusion, put
\[
H_m=A_m^{-1}J_m^\top S_m^{-1},
\qquad
\xi_m=V_m^{-1/2}H_m\psi_m.
\]
Then \(E\xi_m=0\), \(E[\xi_m\xi_m^\top]=I_2\), and the established matrix bounds plus uniform integrability of \(\|\psi_m\|^2\) imply the uniform Lindeberg condition
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_mE[\|\xi_m\|^2\mathbf1\{\|\xi_m\|>R\}]=0.
\]
Here is a self-contained characteristic-function verification. For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^2\), the bound
\[
|e^{iy}-1-iy+y^2/2|\le C y^2\min\{|y|,1\}
\]
and the Lindeberg display yield, uniformly in \(m\),
\[
E\exp\{it^\top\xi_m/\sqrt n\}
=1-\frac{\|t\|^2}{2n}+o(n^{-1}).
\]
Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives \(\exp(-\|t\|^2/2)+o(1)\), uniformly in \(m\). A contradiction-subsequence argument then gives uniform convergence of \(n^{-1/2}\sum_i\xi_{m,i}\) to \(N(0,I_2)\): any sequence violating uniform convergence contains a subsequence to which the preceding characteristic-function limit applies. The asymptotic linear expansion transfers this limit to \(V_m^{-1/2}\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))\). Since the squared Euclidean norm of \(N(0,I_2)\) has the continuous \(\chi^2_2\) distribution, uniform convergence of its distribution functions follows, proving the final display.

\quad\text{uniformly in }m.

Therefore replacing \(V_m^{-1}\) by \(\widehat V_{n,m}^{-1}\) changes the Wald quadratic form by \(o_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly. At the continuous quantile \(\chi^2_{2,1-\gamma}\),

\begin{aligned}

&=P_m\!\left\{n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))^\top

\le\chi^2_{2,1-\gamma}\right\}\\

\end{aligned}

where the remainder is uniform over \(m\in\mathfrak M_6\). Taking the supremum proves the coverage claim.

For the diagnostics, let \(\widehat D_{n,m}\) be the polynomial \(D\) evaluated at the empirical centered cumulants. The finite-dimensional uniform moment law proved in the GMM theorem and polynomial continuity give
\[
\sup_mP_m\bigl(|\widehat D_{n,m}-D(P_m)|>\epsilon\bigr)\to0.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality transfers this to \(|\widehat D_{n,m}|\). The same theorem gives \(\widehat J_{n,m}-J_m=o_{P_m}(1)\) and \(\widehat S_{n,m}-S_m=o_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly. For symmetric matrices and singular values,
\[
|\sigma_{\min}(A)-\sigma_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|,
\qquad
|\lambda_{\min}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(B)|\le\|A-B\|.
\]
Thus the plug-in smallest singular value and smallest eigenvalue consistently estimate the declared Jacobian and influence-covariance margins uniformly. These convergences only measure distance to the boundary of this sufficient chart. Because no converse statement is made on \(D=0\), they do not diagnose nonidentification on the chart complement.

If the disturbance law is centrally symmetric, then \(\varepsilon\stackrel{d}{=}-\varepsilon\). Since \(X=K^{-1}\varepsilon\) is a linear image,
\[
X\stackrel{d}{=}-X.
\]
Finite third moments exist by Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\). Every third-order joint moment, and hence every centered third cumulant, changes sign under \(X\mapsto-X\) while its distribution remains unchanged; it must therefore be zero. Thus \(T_3=0\). By multilinearity, \(F_{21}(\alpha,\delta)=F_{12}(\alpha,\delta)=0\) for all candidates, and Definition \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}cubics}\) gives \(P_3=Q_3=0\). In particular \(p_3=q_3=0\), so \(D=v\chi p_3q_3s_1=0\). The only remaining residual restriction through order three is the covariance equation \(F_{11}=0\). Since the mechanism belongs to \(\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{reg}}\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:order\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}query\mbox{-}fiber}\) supplies the smooth one-dimensional legal curve on that equation. Hence third-order symmetry leaves exactly the stated order-two ambiguity and places the law on the boundary of the sufficient chart.
