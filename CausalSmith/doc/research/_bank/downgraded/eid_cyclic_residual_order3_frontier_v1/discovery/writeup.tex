\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}cyclic\_\allowbreak{}residual\_\allowbreak{}order3\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} In the three-variable linear system with overlapping feedback pairs and a path-dependent disturbance vector, endpoint independence generates three observable residual cross-cumulants. Covariance elimination turns the two third-order restrictions into observable cubics; for a mechanism \(m\), nonvanishing of the explicit chart \(D(P_m)=v(P_m)\chi(P_m)p_3(P_m)q_3(P_m)s_1(P_m)\) makes their linear first subresultant recover the observable functional \(\theta(P_m)=\theta(m)\), with the entire complex residual fiber equal to the structural truth. A legal one-dimensional real covariance fiber makes order three minimal on a nonempty regular locus, while a two-parameter middle-residual shear leaves the complete observed law unchanged and therefore keeps \((b,c)\) as sensitivity parameters. The statistical frontier is uniform feasible optimal-weight GMM and a uniformly covering sandwich ellipse over structural mechanisms \(m\in\mathfrak M_6\), indexed coherently by their induced laws \(P_m\) and targets \(\theta(m)\).

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published dependent-error identification is complete for acyclic graphs but only partial for cycles, while existing higher-cumulant recovery assumes global source restrictions that exclude arbitrary middle-error dependence. Covariance nonidentification results do not establish the projected legal fiber required for an information-order lower bound, and nearby cumulant estimators provide pointwise rather than uniform inference.

\paragraph{Niche.} The fixed overlapping-cycle motif permits elimination of all central coefficients and nuisance cumulants from a two-dimensional outer query. Its residual equations are small enough for an explicit subresultant chart yet rich enough to separate query identification from full-model identification.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project supplies a rational order-three inverse \(\theta(P_m)=\theta(m)\), a legal real order-two query-moving fiber, and a complete-law central shear, then targets a uniform centered-cumulant GMM expansion and sandwich coverage over a single compact mechanism class under transparent quantitative margins.

\section{Related work}

Tramontano, Drton, and Etesami provide the connected-set Markov and independence-genericity framework, a complete acyclic direct-effect criterion, and only partial cyclic results; their whole-distribution transfer argument informs the residual construction but does not deliver the proposed finite-order rational map. Drton, Foygel, and Sullivant show that cyclic Gaussian covariance parametrizations can be noninjective, but their simple-cycle fiber does not establish a query-moving curve in the present non-Gaussian path-dependent class. Mesters and Zwiernik translate structured cumulant zeros into polynomial mixing restrictions and pointwise optimally weighted minimum distance; their standardized uncorrelated-source framework does not allow the middle-error cumulants retained here. Jiang identifies a full matrix from a diagonal single-order source cumulant by an eigenvector map, whereas the proposed witness has nonzero adjacent mixed third cumulants and uses sparse residual restrictions. Ribot, Seigal, and Zwiernik identify unrestricted mixing under pairwise mean independence and show failure after one pairwise restriction is dropped; that full-matrix obstruction leaves graph-constrained coordinate recovery open. Draisma, Kuhnt, and Zwiernik characterize transformation groups of Gaussian graphical models, so their theorem supplies provenance for the shear pattern but is not a cyclic non-Gaussian identification comparator. Lacerda, Spirtes, Ramsey, and Hoyer recover distribution-equivalence classes in cyclic LiNG models with mutually independent errors, a different disturbance class and target. Hansen supplies the pointwise GMM backbone, while Newey and McFadden supply compact extremum-estimator arguments; the proposed uniform centered-cumulant result adapts these tools rather than claiming to replace them. Jin and coauthors' BayCausal theorem uses disjoint cycles, so the prospective application described here is an extension outside their published guarantee, not a correction to it. Chen and coauthors give a latent-confounded higher-order-cumulant rank criterion for an acyclic model; that criterion informs the use of higher cumulants but does not subsume the fixed cyclic residual-elimination inverse or the legal real order-two fiber.

\begin{lemma}[lem:observable-cubic-reduction]\label{lem:observable-cubic-reduction}
For every member of \(\mathcal M_3\), \(F_{11}(a,d)=F_{21}(a,d)=F_{12}(a,d)=0\). If \(v\chi\neq0\), every complex common zero of the residual system satisfies \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), \(\delta=t(\alpha)/l(\alpha)\), and \(P_3(\alpha)=Q_3(\alpha)=0\). Conversely, every common root \(\alpha\) of \(P_3,Q_3\) for which \(l(\alpha)\neq0\) lifts uniquely, by \(\delta=t(\alpha)/l(\alpha)\), to a common zero of the residual system.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_3\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:regular\mbox{-}equilibrium}\) gives \(\Delta\neq0\), so \(K\) is invertible and \(KX=\varepsilon\).  Reading the first and third rows of this equation gives
\[
X_1-aX_2=\varepsilon_1,
\qquad
X_3-dX_2=\varepsilon_3.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) also invokes Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:finite\mbox{-}third\mbox{-}moments}\).  Since every coordinate of \(X=K^{-1}\varepsilon\) is a finite linear combination of the coordinates of \(\varepsilon\), the triangle inequality in \(L^3\) implies \(X_i\in L^3(P_m)\) for every \(i\in I\).  Hence all second- and third-order cumulants used below exist.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:centered\mbox{-}disturbances}\) and linearity of \(K^{-1}\) also give \(E_{P_m}[X]=0\).

By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:path\mbox{-}connected\mbox{-}set\mbox{-}markov}\), \(\varepsilon_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_3\).  For centered random variables the second cumulant is the product moment and the third cumulant is the third product moment.  Independence and centering therefore give, equation by equation,
\[
\kappa(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_3)
=E[\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_3]
=E[\varepsilon_1]E[\varepsilon_3]=0,
\]
\[
\kappa(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_3)
=E[\varepsilon_1^2\varepsilon_3]
=E[\varepsilon_1^2]E[\varepsilon_3]=0,
\]
and
\[
\kappa(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_3,\varepsilon_3)
=E[\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_3^2]
=E[\varepsilon_1]E[\varepsilon_3^2]=0.
\]
Substitution of the two structural residual identities into Definition \(\mathrm{def:residual\mbox{-}cumulants}\) now proves
\[
F_{11}(a,d)=F_{21}(a,d)=F_{12}(a,d)=0.
\]

It remains to establish the elimination and lifting claims.  Extend real cumulants complex-multilinearly when \((\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb C^2\).  By multilinearity of the second cumulant, symmetry of \(\Sigma\), and the coordinate definitions in \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}cubics}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{11}(\alpha,\delta)
&=\kappa(X_1-\alpha X_2,X_3-\delta X_2)\\
&=\Sigma_{13}-\delta\Sigma_{12}-\alpha\Sigma_{23}
  +\alpha\delta\Sigma_{22}\\
&=r-p\delta-q\alpha+v\alpha\delta\\
&=(v\alpha-p)\delta-(q\alpha-r)\\
&=l(\alpha)\delta-t(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\]
Suppose \(v\chi\neq0\) and \((\alpha,\delta)\) is a common zero of \(F_{11},F_{21},F_{12}\).  Then \(v\neq0\).  If \(l(\alpha)=0\), the definition \(l(z)=vz-p\) forces \(\alpha=p/v\).  The preceding identity and the definition \(\chi=vr-pq\) then yield
\[
F_{11}(p/v,\delta)
=-t(p/v)
=-\left(\frac{qp}{v}-r\right)
=\frac{vr-pq}{v}
=\frac{\chi}{v}\neq0,
\]
contradicting that \((\alpha,\delta)\) is a common zero.  Thus \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), and the equation \(F_{11}(\alpha,\delta)=0\) has the unique solution
\[
\delta=\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}.
\]
Using this equality and Definition \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}cubics}\), where the displayed rational expressions agree with their polynomial cancellations whenever \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), the remaining two zero equations imply
\[
P_3(\alpha)
=l(\alpha)F_{21}\!\left(\alpha,\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}\right)
=0
\]
and
\[
Q_3(\alpha)
=l(\alpha)^2F_{12}\!\left(\alpha,\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}\right)
=0.
\]

Conversely, let \(\alpha\in\mathbb C\) satisfy \(P_3(\alpha)=Q_3(\alpha)=0\) and \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), and define \(\delta=t(\alpha)/l(\alpha)\).  The covariance identity above gives
\[
F_{11}(\alpha,\delta)
=l(\alpha)\frac{t(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}-t(\alpha)=0.
\]
The two defining cancellation identities and the permitted divisions by the nonzero quantities \(l(\alpha)\) and \(l(\alpha)^2\) give
\[
F_{21}(\alpha,\delta)
=\frac{P_3(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)}=0,
\qquad
F_{12}(\alpha,\delta)
=\frac{Q_3(\alpha)}{l(\alpha)^2}=0.
\]
Thus \((\alpha,\delta)\) is a common zero of the residual system.  Finally, its lift is unique because, for fixed \(\alpha\) with \(l(\alpha)\neq0\), the equation \(F_{11}(\alpha,\delta)=0\) is the linear equation \(l(\alpha)\delta=t(\alpha)\), which has exactly the displayed solution.  This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the nuisance-elimination step that converts endpoint independence into two fixed observable cubics. \textit{Gap.} Existing tensor methods impose global source-cumulant zeros; no cited result derives this two-parameter residual elimination with unrestricted middle mixed cumulants. \textit{Consumer.} The subresultant theorem uses this reduction to recover the two outer coefficients without estimating \(b\), \(c\), or any disturbance cumulant.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rational-singleton-inverse]\label{thm:rational-singleton-inverse}
For every \(m\in\mathcal M_3\) with \(D(P_m)\neq0\), \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{res}}(P_m)=\{(a(m),d(m))\}\) over \(\mathbb C\), the residual ideal equals \(\langle\alpha-a(m),\delta-d(m)\rangle\), and the chart-induced observable functional is well defined and satisfies \[\theta(P_m)=\left(-\frac{s_0(P_m)}{s_1(P_m)},\frac{t_{P_m}(-s_0(P_m)/s_1(P_m))}{l_{P_m}(-s_0(P_m)/s_1(P_m))}\right)^\top=\theta(m).\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work over \(R=\mathbb C[\alpha,\delta]\), and abbreviate \(P=P_3\), \(Q=Q_3\). The chart condition \(D\neq0\) implies
\[
v\neq0,
\quad \chi\neq0,
\quad p_3q_3\neq0,
\quad s_1\neq0.
\]
By the preceding lemma, \((a,d)\) is a residual common zero, \(l(a)\neq0\), and \(P(a)=Q(a)=0\).

For completeness, the subresultant step can be checked without invoking an unrecorded gcd theorem. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf A(z)
&=q_3(p_2q_3-p_3q_2)z
+  \bigl(-p_1q_3^2+p_2q_2q_3+p_3q_1q_3-p_3q_2^2\bigr),\\
\mathsf B(z)
&=-p_3(p_2q_3-p_3q_2)z
+  \bigl(p_1p_3q_3-p_2^2q_3+p_2p_3q_2-p_3^2q_1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying by \(P=p_3z^3+p_2z^2+p_1z+p_0\) and \(Q=q_3z^3+q_2z^2+q_1z+q_0\), the coefficients of \(z^4,z^3,z^2\) cancel. Direct collection of the remaining two coefficients, using the displayed definitions of \(s_1,s_0\), gives the polynomial identity
\[
\mathsf A(z)P(z)+\mathsf B(z)Q(z)=-(s_1z+s_0).
\]
Evaluation at the known common root \(a\) yields \(s_1a+s_0=0\), whence
\[
a=-\frac{s_0}{s_1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
s_1z+s_0=s_1(z-a).
\]
Every common complex root of \(P,Q\) is therefore a root of \(s_1(z-a)\), and hence equals \(a\). Since \(l(a)\neq0\), the narrowed converse in the preceding lemma supplies the unique lift \(\delta=t(a)/l(a)=d\). This proves
\[
\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{res}}(P_m)=\{(a,d)\}.
\]

It remains to prove the asserted ideal equality, which does not follow from equality of zero sets alone. Let
\[
I=\langle F_{11},F_{21},F_{12}\rangle\subset R.
\]
The covariance identity gives the explicit unit certificate
\[
\chi=q\,l(\alpha)-v\,t(\alpha)
=l(\alpha)(q-v\delta)+vF_{11}(\alpha,\delta).
\]
Thus \(l(\alpha)\) is a unit modulo \(\langle F_{11}\rangle\), with inverse \((q-v\delta)/\chi\). If
\[
F_{21}=G_{21}(\alpha)-\delta H_{21}(\alpha),
\]
multilinearity gives
\[
P=lG_{21}-tH_{21}=lF_{21}+F_{11}H_{21}.
\]
Likewise, writing \(F_{12}=G_{12}(\alpha)-2\delta H_{12}(\alpha)+\delta^2C_{12}(\alpha)\), direct subtraction shows that \(Q-l^2F_{12}\) is divisible by \(F_{11}=l\delta-t\). Hence, because \(l\) is a unit modulo \(F_{11}\),
\[
I=\langle F_{11},P,Q\rangle.
\]
The Bézout identity above now implies \(\alpha-a\in I\). Also
\[
F_{11}(a,\delta)=l(a)\delta-t(a)=l(a)(\delta-d),
\]
and \(F_{11}(\alpha,\delta)-F_{11}(a,\delta)\) is divisible by \(\alpha-a\). Since \(l(a)\neq0\), it follows that \(\delta-d\in I\). Therefore
\[
\langle\alpha-a,\delta-d\rangle\subseteq I.
\]
The reverse inclusion holds because all three generators vanish at \((a,d)\). Thus
\[
I=\langle\alpha-a,\delta-d\rangle.
\]
Finally, \(s_1\neq0\) and \(l(a)\neq0\) make every division in Definition \(\mathrm{def:chart\mbox{-}induced\mbox{-}observable\mbox{-}target}\) valid, and the two coordinates just derived prove \(\theta(P_m)=\theta(m)\). This proves identification of the structural query by the stated observable functional rather than defining the two objects to be equal.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It gives an explicit finite-order recovery map and excludes every nontrue complex branch, which a local rank argument cannot do. \textit{Gap.} Tramontano, Drton, and Etesami give partial cyclic full-law results; Mesters and Zwiernik recover full mixing under stronger tensor zeros; neither gives this sparse rational inverse. \textit{Consumer.} A path-dependent overlapping-cycle analysis can report \((a,d)\) from second and third observed cumulants while treating the middle equation as unrestricted nuisance.

\qquad

\begin{theorem}[thm:order-two-query-fiber]\label{thm:order-two-query-fiber}
For every \(m\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{reg}}\), the order-two query fiber \(\mathcal F_2(P_m)\subset\mathbb R^2\) contains a smooth one-dimensional curve through \(\theta(m)=(a(m),d(m))^\top\). Restricting the candidates on this curve to \(\alpha_{\mathbb R},\delta_{\mathbb R}\in\mathbb R\), it is locally characterized by \(h=a(m)-\alpha_{\mathbb R}\), \(k=d(m)-\delta_{\mathbb R}\), and \[k(h)=-\frac{U_mh}{V_m+\Sigma_{22,m}h}.\] Both real coordinates vary along the curve. Every sufficiently nearby point is realized by a centered non-Gaussian five-source disturbance law satisfying endpoint independence and independence genericity and inducing the same observed covariance as \(P_m\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{reg}}\), write \(W=\Sigma_{22,m}>0\), and consider real candidate errors
\[
h=a(m)-\alpha_{\mathbb R},
\qquad
k=d(m)-\delta_{\mathbb R}.
\]
The candidate endpoint residuals are
\[
X_1-\alpha_{\mathbb R}X_2=\varepsilon_1+hX_2,
\qquad
X_3-\delta_{\mathbb R}X_2=\varepsilon_3+kX_2.
\]
Endpoint independence gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_3)=0\). Hence bilinearity of covariance and the definitions of \(U_m,V_m\) give the necessary equation
\[
0=F_{11}=U_mh+V_mk+Whk.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:covariance\mbox{-}transversality}\) says \(U_mV_m\neq0\). Therefore \(V_m+Wh\neq0\) for all sufficiently small \(h\), and the covariance equation is exactly
\[
k(h)=-\frac{U_mh}{V_m+Wh}.
\]
This is a smooth curve through \((h,k)=(0,0)\), with \(k'(0)=-U_m/V_m\neq0\). Since \(\alpha_{\mathbb R}=a-h\) and \(\delta_{\mathbb R}=d-k(h)\), both query coordinates vary along it.

We next prove that every sufficiently small point of this curve is legal, not merely a formal covariance solution. Keep \(b,c\) fixed, replace \((a,d)\) by \((a-h,d-k(h))\), and call the resulting structural matrix \(K(h)\). Nonzero coefficients and the nonzero equilibrium determinant persist by continuity. Set
\[
\Omega(h)=K(h)\Sigma K(h)^\top.
\]
It is positive definite because \(K(h)\) is invertible and \(\Sigma\succ0\). Its \((1,3)\) entry is exactly the candidate residual covariance above and hence is zero. Its adjacent entries remain nonzero by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:adjacent\mbox{-}disturbance\mbox{-}covariances}\) and continuity.

It remains to realize any such positive-definite path covariance \(\widetilde\Omega\) by a legal non-Gaussian law. Positive definiteness and \(\widetilde\Omega_{13}=0\) imply, by dividing the determinant inequality by \(\widetilde\Omega_{11}\widetilde\Omega_{33}>0\),
\[
\widetilde\Omega_{22}>
\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{12}^2}{\widetilde\Omega_{11}}
+\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{23}^2}{\widetilde\Omega_{33}}.
\]
Choose \(x\in(0,\widetilde\Omega_{11})\) and \(y\in(0,\widetilde\Omega_{33})\), sufficiently close to the respective upper endpoints, so that
\[
\rho^2:=\widetilde\Omega_{22}
-\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{12}^2}{x}
-\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{23}^2}{y}>0.
\]
For five independent centered unit-rate exponential sources define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\varepsilon_1
 &=\sqrt{\widetilde\Omega_{11}-x}\,S_1+\sqrt{x}\,S_{12},\\
\widetilde\varepsilon_2
 &=\rho S_2+\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{12}}{\sqrt{x}}S_{12}
                 +\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{23}}{\sqrt{y}}S_{23},\\
\widetilde\varepsilon_3
 &=\sqrt{\widetilde\Omega_{33}-y}\,S_3+\sqrt{y}\,S_{23}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Independence of the sources gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\varepsilon)=\widetilde\Omega\) entry by entry. All private loadings and all shared-edge loadings are nonzero. The endpoint source sets are disjoint, so \(\widetilde\varepsilon_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\widetilde\varepsilon_3\). The rational moment-generating-function factorization used in the witness proof applies with these nonzero loadings and shows independence genericity: private sources exclude equal-coordinate overlap, and each shared source excludes the two orientations of its path edge. Each marginal has a nonzero exponential loading, so its moment-generating function has a pole and cannot be Gaussian. Centering and all finite moments are immediate.

Applying this construction to \(\widetilde\Omega=\Omega(h)\) produces a member \(\widetilde m\in\mathcal M_3\) with structural matrix \(K(h)\), and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde X)
=K(h)^{-1}\Omega(h)K(h)^{-\top}=\Sigma.
\]
Thus the entire local curve is contained in \(\mathcal F_2(P_m)\). Conversely, every order-two-compatible member of \(\mathcal M_3\) has independent endpoint residuals and therefore must satisfy the same scalar equation \(F_{11}=0\). Since its derivative with respect to \(k\) at the true point is \(V_m\neq0\), the displayed curve is the local outer-coordinate fiber. This proves both the smooth one-dimensional lower-information fiber and its realization inside the stipulated probability model.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is an information lower bound inside the actual disturbance class, rather than failure of one identification certificate. \textit{Gap.} Drton, Foygel, and Sullivant prove covariance noninjectivity for cyclic Gaussian SEMs but do not construct this projected legal fiber with independent non-Gaussian endpoints. \textit{Consumer.} It tells users that covariance-only sensitivity analysis must vary both outer coefficients, even if the graph and path dependence pattern are accepted.

\begin{theorem}[thm:central-full-law-fiber]\label{thm:central-full-law-fiber}
For every \(m\in\mathcal M_3\), there is an open neighborhood of \((0,0)\) in \(\mathbb R^2\) such that, for every \((t_0,u_0)\) in that neighborhood, \(A_m(t_0,u_0)\) is invertible and the tuple \(m'_{t_0,u_0}=(a(m),b'_{m,t_0,u_0},c'_{m,t_0,u_0},d(m),\nu'_{m,t_0,u_0})\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_3\). More explicitly, \(K'_{m,t_0,u_0}=A_m(t_0,u_0)K_m=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a(m)&0\\-b'_{m,t_0,u_0}&1&-c'_{m,t_0,u_0}\\0&-d(m)&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(K'_{m,t_0,u_0}X=A_m(t_0,u_0)K_mX=A_m(t_0,u_0)\varepsilon=\varepsilon'_{m,t_0,u_0}\), and hence \(m'_{t_0,u_0}\) induces exactly \(P_m\). On this neighborhood, \(b'_{m,t_0,u_0}c'_{m,t_0,u_0}\neq0\), \(1-a(m)b'_{m,t_0,u_0}-c'_{m,t_0,u_0}d(m)=(1+a(m)t_0+d(m)u_0)\Delta(m)\neq0\), \(E_m[\varepsilon'_i]=0\), and \(E_m[|\varepsilon'_i|^3]<\infty\) for every \(i\in I\). The middle coordinate obeys \[\|\varepsilon'_2-\varepsilon_2\|_{L^3(m)}\le |t_0|\|\varepsilon_1\|_{L^3(m)}+|a(m)t_0+d(m)u_0|\|\varepsilon_2\|_{L^3(m)}+|u_0|\|\varepsilon_3\|_{L^3(m)}\longrightarrow0.\] Thus the neighborhood can be chosen so that its middle marginal is non-Gaussian, since otherwise Gaussian transformed middle marginals along a sequence converging to the origin would converge weakly to the non-Gaussian law of \(\varepsilon_2\); the endpoint marginals are unchanged and non-Gaussian. Moreover, \(\varepsilon'_1=\varepsilon_1\) and \(\varepsilon'_3=\varepsilon_3\), so \(\varepsilon'_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon'_3\) and the path connected-set Markov restriction is preserved. Independence genericity is preserved because any nonzero independent pair of linear forms in \(\varepsilon'\), pulled back by \(A_m(t_0,u_0)^\top\), must lie on the two opposite endpoint axes under the genericity of \(m\), while \(A_m(t_0,u_0)^{-\top}(1,0,0)^\top=(1,0,0)^\top\) and \(A_m(t_0,u_0)^{-\top}(0,0,1)^\top=(0,0,1)^\top\). The induced map \((t_0,u_0)\mapsto(b'_{m,t_0,u_0},c'_{m,t_0,u_0})\) has derivative determinant \(\Delta(m)\neq0\) at the origin. Hence the compatible full-law fiber in \((b,c)\) contains a two-dimensional neighborhood while the outer projection is a singleton whenever \(D(P_m)\neq0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_3\) and abbreviate \(a=a(m)\), \(b=b(m)\), \(c=c(m)\), \(d=d(m)\). Put
\[
s=1+at_0+du_0.
\]
Direct matrix multiplication gives
\[
A_m(t_0,u_0)K_m=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-a&0\\
t_0-sb&-at_0+s-du_0&u_0-sc\\
0&-d&1
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-a&0\\
-b'&1&-c'\\
0&-d&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
because \(-at_0+s-du_0=1\), \(b'=bs-t_0\), and \(c'=cs-u_0\). Also
\[
\det A_m(t_0,u_0)=s,
\qquad
\det K'_{m,t_0,u_0}=s\det K_m=s\Delta(m).
\]
At the origin, \(s=1\), \(b'=b\neq0\), \(c'=c\neq0\), and \(s\Delta(m)=\Delta(m)\neq0\). Continuity therefore gives an open neighborhood on which all four structural coefficients and both determinants are nonzero. Moreover,
\[
K'X=A_mK_mX=A_m\varepsilon=\varepsilon',
\]
so the transformed mechanism induces the same random vector \(X\), and hence exactly the same observed law \(P_m\).

The first and third transformed disturbances are unchanged, while
\[
\varepsilon'_2=t_0\varepsilon_1+s\varepsilon_2+u_0\varepsilon_3.
\]
Centering is preserved. The triangle inequality in \(L^3(m)\) gives exactly
\[
\|\varepsilon'_2-\varepsilon_2\|_3
\le |t_0|\|\varepsilon_1\|_3
+|at_0+du_0|\|\varepsilon_2\|_3
+|u_0|\|\varepsilon_3\|_3\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the endpoint third moments are unchanged. Thus all third moments remain finite. The endpoint marginals remain non-Gaussian. The middle marginal is also non-Gaussian throughout a sufficiently small neighborhood. Indeed, otherwise there would be \((t_j,u_j)\to(0,0)\) for which \(\varepsilon'_{2,j}\) is Gaussian. The displayed \(L^3\) convergence implies convergence of its variance to \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_2)\). Its characteristic function would therefore converge to the characteristic function of a centered Gaussian with that variance, while convergence in \(L^3\) also forces it to converge in distribution to \(\varepsilon_2\). This would make \(\varepsilon_2\) Gaussian, contradicting Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nongaussian\mbox{-}marginals}\).

Because \(\varepsilon'_1=\varepsilon_1\) and \(\varepsilon'_3=\varepsilon_3\), endpoint independence, and hence the path connected-set Markov condition, is unchanged. To check independence genericity, suppose \(x^\top\varepsilon'\perp\!\!\!\perp y^\top\varepsilon'\), and set \(r=A_m(t_0,u_0)^\top x\), \(w=A_m(t_0,u_0)^\top y\). Then \(r^\top\varepsilon\perp\!\!\!\perp w^\top\varepsilon\). The original genericity condition on the three-vertex path implies that, unless one form is zero, one of \(r,w\) is supported on coordinate \(1\) and the other on coordinate \(3\): any other pair of nonempty supports contains either an equal index or adjacent indices. Since \(A_m\) is invertible, a zero pulled-back form corresponds to a zero original form. Finally, direct inversion gives
\[
A_m(t_0,u_0)^{-\top}e_1=e_1,
\qquad
A_m(t_0,u_0)^{-\top}e_3=e_3.
\]
Hence the same opposite-endpoint support conclusion holds for \(x,y\), proving independence genericity for \(\varepsilon'\). All defining conditions of \(\mathcal M_3\) have now been verified.

The determinant identity requested in the statement follows also by direct substitution:
\[
1-ab'-c'd
=1-a(bs-t_0)-d(cs-u_0)
=s(1-ab-cd)=s\Delta(m)\neq0.
\]
Finally, the map from shear coordinates to central coefficients is affine:
\[
\binom{b'}{c'}=
\binom bc+
\begin{pmatrix}ab-1&bd\\ac&cd-1\end{pmatrix}
\binom{t_0}{u_0},
\]
and the determinant of its linear part is
\[
(ab-1)(cd-1)-abcd=1-ab-cd=\Delta(m)\neq0.
\]
It therefore maps every sufficiently small open shear neighborhood onto an open two-dimensional neighborhood of \((b,c)\). Every point in this neighborhood induces exactly \(P_m\), while \((a,d)\) is unchanged. When \(D(P_m)\neq0\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:rational\mbox{-}singleton\mbox{-}inverse}\) separately proves that the outer projection compatible with this law is the singleton \(\theta(m)\). Thus the full-law nuisance fiber is two-dimensional without compromising the identified outer query.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit pushforward law and two-sided genericity calculation certify that the same-law shear stays inside the maintained non-Gaussian path model, separating recoverable outer mechanisms from a legal central sensitivity fiber. \textit{Gap.} Lacerda and coauthors describe cyclic LiNG equivalence under mutually independent errors, while the Gaussian group theorem of Draisma, Kuhnt, and Zwiernik does not establish legality under this non-Gaussian path model. \textit{Consumer.} A prospective BayCausal or Women's Interagency HIV Study extension can report the outer coefficients and display \((b,c)\) as a same-law sensitivity surface.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-sandwich-ellipse]\label{thm:uniform-sandwich-ellipse}
If Open-ended Question 7 is resolved affirmatively, then for every fixed \(0<\gamma<1\), the feasible ellipse satisfies \[\sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_6}\left|P_m\{\theta(m)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}(1-\gamma)\}-(1-\gamma)\right|\longrightarrow0.\] Plug-in estimates consistently estimate \(|D(P_m)|\), \(\sigma_{\min}(J_m)\), and \(\lambda_{\min}(S_m)\) uniformly over \(m\in\mathfrak M_6\), thereby reporting proximity to a declared strong-chart boundary without diagnosing nonidentification on the chart complement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The affirmative resolution of Open-ended Question \(\mathrm{oeq:uniform\mbox{-}feasible\mbox{-}gmm}\) gives both the uniform \(\chi^2_2\) limit with \(V_m\) and \(\widehat V_{n,m}-V_m=o_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly. Its proof also supplies constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) for which
\[
cI_2\preceq V_m\preceq CI_2
\quad\text{uniformly in }m.
\]
Therefore replacing \(V_m^{-1}\) by \(\widehat V_{n,m}^{-1}\) changes the Wald quadratic form by \(o_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly. At the continuous quantile \(\chi^2_{2,1-\gamma}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_m\{\theta(m)\in\mathcal C_{n,m}(1-\gamma)\}
&=P_m\!\left\{n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))^\top
\widehat V_{n,m}^{-1}(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))
\le\chi^2_{2,1-\gamma}\right\}\\
&=1-\gamma+o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the remainder is uniform over \(m\in\mathfrak M_6\). Taking the supremum proves the coverage claim.

For the diagnostics, let \(\widehat D_{n,m}\) be the polynomial \(D\) evaluated at the empirical centered cumulants. The finite-dimensional uniform moment law proved in the GMM theorem and polynomial continuity give
\[
\sup_mP_m\bigl(|\widehat D_{n,m}-D(P_m)|>\epsilon\bigr)\to0.

The reverse triangle inequality transfers this to \(|\widehat D_{n,m}|\). The same theorem gives \(\widehat J_{n,m}-J_m=o_{P_m}(1)\) and \(\widehat S_{n,m}-S_m=o_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly. For symmetric matrices and singular values,

\qquad

\]
Thus the plug-in smallest singular value and smallest eigenvalue consistently estimate the declared Jacobian and influence-covariance margins uniformly. These convergences only measure distance to the boundary of this sufficient chart. Because no converse statement is made on \(D=0\), they do not diagnose nonidentification on the chart complement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:symmetric-third-order-reduction]\label{prop:symmetric-third-order-reduction}

\begin{proof}

\[
X\stackrel{d}{=}-X.
\]

Every third-order joint moment, and hence every centered third cumulant, changes sign under \(X\mapsto-X\) while its distribution remains unchanged; it must therefore be zero. Thus \(T_3=0\). By multilinearity, \(F_{21}(\alpha,\delta)=F_{12}(\alpha,\delta)=0\) for all candidates, and Definition \(\mathrm{def:observable\mbox{-}cubics}\) gives \(P_3=Q_3=0\). In particular \(p_3=q_3=0\), so \(D=v\chi p_3q_3s_1=0\). The only remaining residual restriction through order three is the covariance equation \(F_{11}=0\). Since the mechanism belongs to \(\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{reg}}\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:order\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}query\mbox{-}fiber}\) supplies the smooth one-dimensional legal curve on that equation. Hence third-order symmetry leaves exactly the stated order-two ambiguity and places the law on the boundary of the sufficient chart.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction verifies that the chart correctly fails when third cumulants contain no information. \textit{Gap.} Non-Gaussianity alone does not exclude symmetric laws; the proposal therefore makes no universal third-order identification claim. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can interpret a vanishing chart as a certificate failure that includes a familiar symmetric-error boundary.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-feasible-gmm]\label{thm:uniform-feasible-gmm}
The answer is affirmative. Under the assumptions defining \(\mathfrak M_6\) together with independent sampling, sixth-Wasserstein compactness, and global separation, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_6}P_m\!\left(\left\|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))+(J_m^\top S_m^{-1}J_m)^{-1}J_m^\top S_m^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_m(X_{i,m}^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\|>\epsilon\right)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and
\[
\sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_6}P_m\!\left(\|\widehat V_{n,m}-V_m\|>\epsilon\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover, writing \(F_{\chi^2_2}\) for the distribution function of a chi-square variable with two degrees of freedom,
\[
\sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_6}\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}
\left|P_m\!\left\{n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))^\top V_m^{-1}(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))\le x\right\}-F_{\chi^2_2}(x)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathcal P_6=\{P_m:m\in\mathfrak M_6\}\). Compactness of \(\mathcal P_6\) in sixth-Wasserstein distance has two consequences used below:
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_6}E_P\|X\|^6<\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_6}
E_P[\|X\|^6\mathbf 1\{\|X\|>R\}]=0.
\]
Indeed, sixth-Wasserstein convergence is weak convergence together with convergence of sixth moments; if either display failed, a sequence in the compact set would have a convergent subsequence contradicting that characterization. Thus every polynomial in \(X\) of degree at most three has a uniformly square-integrable envelope, and every polynomial of degree at most six has a uniformly integrable envelope.

The entries of \(T_2,T_3\), and hence the coefficients of \(g_m(\vartheta)\), vary continuously in sixth-Wasserstein distance. On the chart, the first coordinate \(-s_0/s_1\) is continuous: the chart margin, bounded observed moments, and \(D=v\chi p_3q_3s_1\) bound \(|s_1|\) away from zero. The covariance argument in the observable-cubic lemma gives \(l(a)\neq0\) at every law in the compact image. Since \(a=-s_0/s_1\) is continuous, compactness then bounds \(|l(a)|\) away from zero, so \(d=t(a)/l(a)\) is continuous as well. The rational identification theorem proves that this observable map equals the structural \(\theta(m)\). Consequently \(\{\theta(m):m\in\mathfrak M_6\}\) is a compact subset of \(\operatorname{int}(\Theta)\), and therefore has a positive distance from \(\Theta^c\). This is the uniform interior margin needed for the first-order condition; it is derived here, not silently appended to Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:interior\mbox{-}truth}\).

We record the uniform laws of large numbers used in the argument. For any finite vector \(H(X)\) with a uniformly integrable envelope over \(\mathcal P_6\), truncation at a fixed level makes the centered sample average uniformly small by Chebyshev's inequality, and the preceding tail display then makes the truncation error uniformly small. Hence
\[
\sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_6}
P_m\!\left(\left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH(X_{i,m}^{\mathrm{obs}})-E_{P_m}H(X)\right\|>\epsilon\right)\to0.
\]
The same statement holds uniformly over \(\vartheta\in\Theta\) for polynomial families whose coefficients are continuous in \(\vartheta\), because such a family is finite-dimensional and \(\Theta\) is compact. Applying this result to the monomials defining centered covariances and third cumulants gives
\[
\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\|g_{n,m}(\vartheta)-g_m(\vartheta)\|=o_{P_m}(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in }m,
\]
and the same conclusion for the first two derivatives with respect to \(\vartheta\).

At the truth, \(g_m(\theta(m))=0\) by endpoint independence. Uniform global separation and the preceding uniform convergence imply
\[
\sup_mP_m(\|\widetilde\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m)\|>\epsilon)\to0.
\]
The plug-in influence vector is a polynomial of degree at most three in \(X\), with coefficients continuous in \(\vartheta\in\Theta\); its outer product has degree at most six. The degree-six uniform law and preliminary consistency therefore give
\[
\widehat S_{n,m}-S_m=o_{P_m}(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in }m.
\]
The influence-covariance margin implies that \(\widehat S_{n,m}\) is invertible with probability tending to one uniformly and
\[
\widehat S_{n,m}^{-1}-S_m^{-1}=o_{P_m}(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in }m.
\]
The eigenvalues of these weights are uniformly bounded above and away from zero. Applying separation once more to the feasible weighted criterion yields uniform consistency of \(\widehat\theta_{n,m}\).

We next identify the exact linear term created by empirical centering. At the truth let \(e_{1i}=\varepsilon_{1i}\), \(e_{3i}=\varepsilon_{3i}\). For covariance,
\[
\sqrt n\,\widehat\kappa(e_1,e_3)
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^ne_{1i}e_{3i}
-\sqrt n\,\bar e_1\bar e_3.
\]
Uniform second-moment bounds make the last term \(o_{P_m}(1)\). For the \((2,1)\) centered third cumulant, exact expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1n\sum_i(e_{1i}-\bar e_1)^2(e_{3i}-\bar e_3)
&=\frac1n\sum_i(e_{1i}^2-Ee_1^2)e_{3i}\\
&\quad-\bar e_3\left(\frac1n\sum_ie_{1i}^2-Ee_1^2\right)
-2\bar e_1\frac1n\sum_ie_{1i}e_{3i}
+2\bar e_1^2\bar e_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
After multiplication by \(\sqrt n\), the final three terms are uniformly \(o_{P_m}(1)\), by the uniform bounds through order six and endpoint independence. Interchanging indices \(1,3\) gives the corresponding formula for the \((1,2)\) cumulant. Therefore
\[
\sqrt n\,g_{n,m}(\theta(m))
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_m(X_{i,m}^{\mathrm{obs}})+o_{P_m}(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in }m.
\]
This also proves directly that the centering terms in the definition of \(\psi_m\) are necessary.

Let \(W_m=S_m^{-1}\) and \(A_m=J_m^\top W_mJ_m\). Uniform sixth-moment bounds bound \(\|S_m\|\) and \(\|J_m\|\), while the two singular-value assumptions give constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that
\[
cI_2\preceq A_m\preceq CI_2
\quad\text{for every }m\in\mathfrak M_6.
\]
On the event on which the consistent estimator lies in the common interior neighborhood, its first-order condition is
\[
0=\dot g_{n,m}(\widehat\theta_{n,m})^\top
\widehat S_{n,m}^{-1}g_{n,m}(\widehat\theta_{n,m}).
\]
Taylor expansion around \(\theta(m)\), uniform convergence of the first two derivatives, and the preceding eigenvalue bounds first imply
\(\sqrt n\|\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m)\|=O_{P_m}(1)\) uniformly, and then give
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))
=-A_m^{-1}J_m^\top S_m^{-1}\sqrt n\,g_{n,m}(\theta(m))+o_{P_m}(1)
\]
uniformly. Substitution of the exact centered-cumulant expansion proves the first asserted limit.

The same uniform polynomial laws give
\[
\widehat J_{n,m}-J_m=o_{P_m}(1),
\qquad
\widehat S_{n,m}-S_m=o_{P_m}(1)
\]
uniformly. Matrix inversion is uniformly Lipschitz on the eigenvalue-margin sets just displayed, so
\[
\widehat V_{n,m}
=(\widehat J_{n,m}^\top\widehat S_{n,m}^{-1}\widehat J_{n,m})^{-1}
=V_m+o_{P_m}(1)
\]
uniformly, proving the second asserted limit.

For the distributional conclusion, put
\[
H_m=A_m^{-1}J_m^\top S_m^{-1},
\qquad
\xi_m=V_m^{-1/2}H_m\psi_m.
\]
Then \(E\xi_m=0\), \(E[\xi_m\xi_m^\top]=I_2\), and the established matrix bounds plus uniform integrability of \(\|\psi_m\|^2\) imply the uniform Lindeberg condition
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_mE[\|\xi_m\|^2\mathbf1\{\|\xi_m\|>R\}]=0.
\]
Here is a self-contained characteristic-function verification. For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^2\), the bound
\[
|e^{iy}-1-iy+y^2/2|\le C y^2\min\{|y|,1\}
\]
and the Lindeberg display yield, uniformly in \(m\),
\[
E\exp\{it^\top\xi_m/\sqrt n\}
=1-\frac{\|t\|^2}{2n}+o(n^{-1}).
\]
Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives \(\exp(-\|t\|^2/2)+o(1)\), uniformly in \(m\). A contradiction-subsequence argument then gives uniform convergence of \(n^{-1/2}\sum_i\xi_{m,i}\) to \(N(0,I_2)\): any sequence violating uniform convergence contains a subsequence to which the preceding characteristic-function limit applies. The asymptotic linear expansion transfers this limit to \(V_m^{-1/2}\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{n,m}-\theta(m))\). Since the squared Euclidean norm of \(N(0,I_2)\) has the continuous \(\chi^2_2\) distribution, uniform convergence of its distribution functions follows, proving the final display.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The chart \(D\neq0\) is a sufficient recovery chart, not a characterization of the full identification region. The exceptional set mixes genuine nonidentification, symmetric third-order degeneracy, degree specialization, and possible recovery by another chart. The uniform GMM expansion is the unresolved bottleneck: sixth-Wasserstein compactness of the induced-law image \(\{P_m:m\in\mathfrak M_6\}\) must be converted into a uniform centered-cumulant central limit argument, and the estimated weight, global minimizer, and sandwich matrix must be controlled simultaneously. No inference claim is made for mechanism sequences with \(D(P_m)\to0\), \(\sigma_{\min}(J_m)\to0\), or \(\lambda_{\min}(S_m)\to0\).

\section{Honest scope}

The proposal concerns one fixed three-variable overlapping-cycle motif and assumes that its structural zero pattern and disturbance path are independently justified. It does not learn the graph, classify every real fiber on \(D=0\), or identify \((b,c)\). The singleton residual ideal, legal covariance realization, preservation of independence genericity under the shear, and uniform GMM expansion all remain to be proved. Under centrally symmetric disturbances, third cumulants vanish, the chart correctly fails, and the model reduces to the covariance nonidentification boundary. The BayCausal and Women's Interagency HIV Study use is prospective and conditional; their published disjoint-cycle theorem is not claimed to cover this motif.
