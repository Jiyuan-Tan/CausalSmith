\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}mobius\_\allowbreak{}null\_\allowbreak{}compliance}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A randomized three-factor offer design can tolerate economically meaningful interactive compliance without restricting outcome heterogeneity. Within the present one-sided WARP ambient class, the result characterizes the unique largest response support containing every coordinate-separable map for which all eight perfect-complier potential-outcome means remain identified. The eight-map baseline is the one-sided three-factor specialization, and hence only a subclass, of Blackwell--Pashley's coordinate-specific receipt model. The envelope is the union of the perfect-complier map and the WARP maps with zero Boolean Möbius signatures; a symbolic choice argument shows that it contains exactly 29 maps, including 21 interactive maps and six maps for which every offer coordinate matters. Signed assignment-cell transforms identify the eight means, Walsh transforms identify all seven factorial effects, and an explicit Bernoulli null-space construction proves failure on every present-ambient support containing an excluded rational type. Joint influence functions yield simultaneous Wald inference away from a zero perfect-complier share, while Bonferroni--Fieller inversion remains honest about weak denominators.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Goff's finite-support criterion characterizes outcome-nonrestrictive identification abstractly and gives a two-factor WARP application, while Blackwell and Pashley identify arbitrary-factor perfect-complier effects in their coordinate-specific receipt model. The eight maps used here are only that model's one-sided three-factor specialization, not the published class itself. Neither accessible result supplies the symbolic one-sided three-factor WARP envelope that is simultaneously sufficient and maximal for the entire vector of perfect-complier means.

\paragraph{Niche.} Within the one-sided three-factor WARP ambient class, the Boolean lattice first admits rational null-signature uptake maps that are interactive in every assignment coordinate. This creates a sharp behavioral boundary beyond the eight-map one-sided coordinate-separable slice, without implying a comparison of the full published coordinate-specific class with the present WARP class.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives the Möbius coefficients from the eight-map one-sided baseline, proves the symbolic 28-map null classification and 29-map envelope, identifies all perfect-complier means and factorial contrasts, and constructs observationally equivalent Bernoulli laws for every forbidden present-ambient enlargement. The inference rung propagates the common signed denominator through all eight means and seven Walsh contrasts without claiming uniform adaptation as the denominator vanishes.

\section{Related work}

Goff's Theorems 1--2 provide the finite-support outcome-nonrestrictive row-space criterion that informs the algebraic endpoint here; the present theorem does not claim to improve that generic criterion. Blackwell and Pashley's perfect-complier construction identifies the same factorial targets in a coordinate-specific receipt model. The baseline \(\mathcal S_3\) here is only the eight-map one-sided three-factor specialization, and hence a subclass, of that published model; the theorem enlarges this one-sided slice from eight to 29 maps and does not assert containment of, or sufficiency for, the full published class. Heckman and Pinto connect rational discrete choice to binary response matrices, informing the contraction and incidence-matrix proof strategy without imposing the simultaneous Möbius-null equations proved here. Navjeevan, Pinto, and Santos give an operator-range and generalized-kappa characterization for latent moments, against which the signed recovery functional can be checked. Kormos, Lieli, and Huber show that coordinated two-factor takeup generally mixes causal interaction and heterogeneity, motivating an exact positive class rather than an unrestricted interpretation. Blackwell and Pashley's later bounds paper shows how weaker factorial receipt assumptions typically move the problem to partial identification. Picchetti supplies a current finite-population inference benchmark under factor-specific uptake, while Kang, Peck, and Keele provide the weak-IV inversion logic adapted in the final inference statement.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:one-sided-receipt]\label{ass:one-sided-receipt}
For every \(z\in\mathcal B\), \(g(z)\subseteq z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (one-sided noncompliance for bundle offers, \cite{AngristImbensRubin1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strict-rational-choice]\label{ass:strict-rational-choice}
There exists a strict total order \(\succ_g\) on \(\mathcal B\) such that \(g(z)\) is the \(\succ_g\)-maximal element of \(\{d\in\mathcal B:d\subseteq z\}\) for every \(z\in\mathcal B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (WARP rationalization on finite nested menus, \cite{Goff2026OutcomeNonrestrictive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:full-support-randomization]\label{ass:full-support-randomization}
For every \(z\in\mathcal B\), \(\pi_z>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (full-support factorial randomization, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:instrument-independence]\label{ass:instrument-independence}
\(Z\perp\!\!\!\perp\bigl(G,(Y(t))_{t\in\mathcal B}\bigr)\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (randomized-instrument independence, \cite{Goff2026OutcomeNonrestrictive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:receipt-consistency]\label{ass:receipt-consistency}
\(D=G(Z)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (response-map consistency, \cite{Goff2026OutcomeNonrestrictive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-exclusion]\label{ass:outcome-exclusion}
\(Y=Y(D)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (outcome exclusion and consistency, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:first-moments]\label{ass:first-moments}
For every \(t\in\mathcal B\), \(\mathbb E_P[|Y(t)|]<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite potential-outcome first moments, \cite{Goff2026OutcomeNonrestrictive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-perfect-share]\label{ass:positive-perfect-share}
\(p^\star>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (positive perfect-complier share, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:support-in-model]\label{ass:support-in-model}
\(P(G\in\mathcal M)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite response-support restriction, \cite{Goff2026OutcomeNonrestrictive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:separable-baseline]\label{ass:separable-baseline}
\(\mathcal S_3\subseteq\mathcal M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition retains all eight maps generated by independently responsive subsets of the three offered factors. By construction, these maps form the one-sided three-factor specialization, and hence a subclass, of Blackwell--Pashley's coordinate-specific receipt model; they are not the published class itself. Requiring this baseline prevents maximality within the present one-sided WARP ambient class from being manufactured by deleting ordinary one-sided compliance patterns.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed according to \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (i.i.d. superpopulation sampling, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:second-moment]\label{ass:second-moment}
\(\mathbb E_P[Y^2]<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite observed-outcome second moment, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strong-share]\label{ass:strong-share}
For a fixed constant \(\kappa>0\), \(p^\star\ge\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (denominator bounded away from zero, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:wald-nonsingularity]\label{ass:wald-nonsingularity}
\(\Omega\) is positive definite.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nonsingular multivariate asymptotic covariance, \cite{BlackwellPashley2023FactorialIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fieller-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:fieller-nondegeneracy}
For every \(t\in\mathcal B\) and fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\), \(\operatorname{Var}_P(\phi_{m,t}-u\phi_{p,t})>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate studentized Fieller moment, \cite{KangPeckKeele2018RandomizationIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:warp-class]\label{def:warp-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{WARP-\allowbreak{}rational one-\allowbreak{}sided response class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3=\{g\in\mathfrak G_3: g(z)\subseteq z\ \forall z\in\mathcal B\text{ and }\exists\succ_g\text{ a strict total order with }g(z)=\max_{\succ_g}\{d\in\mathcal B:d\subseteq z\}\ \forall z\in\mathcal B\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}one-\allowbreak{}sided-\allowbreak{}receipt}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}strict-\allowbreak{}rational-\allowbreak{}choice}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:separable-family]\label{def:separable-family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Coordinate-\allowbreak{}separable family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal S_3=\{g_{C_0}:C_0\subseteq U\}\), where \(g_{C_0}(z)=z\cap C_0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:mobius-signature]\label{def:mobius-signature}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Treatment-\allowbreak{}cell Möbius signature}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(a_t(z)=\mathbf 1\{t\subseteq z\}(-1)^{|z|-|t|}\) and \(\sigma_t(g)=\sum_{z\in\mathcal B}a_t(z)\mathbf 1\{g(z)=t\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:null-envelope]\label{def:null-envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Möbius-\allowbreak{}null envelope}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal N_3=\{g\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3:\sigma_t(g)=0\ \forall t\in\mathcal B\}\) and \(\mathcal E_3=\{g^\star\}\cup\mathcal N_3\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:family-template]\label{def:family-template}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Four symbolic null families}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal F_3\) is the set of maps of the following four disjoint forms, where \(a,b,c\) are distinct elements of \(U\): (i) \(g(z)=\varnothing\); (ii) for \(s\in\{\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\}\}\), choose \(\{a\}\) whenever \(a\in z\), otherwise choose \(s\) whenever \(s\ne\varnothing\) and \(s\subseteq z\), and otherwise choose \(\varnothing\); (iii) for an unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\) and \((\rho_a,\rho_b)\in\{0,1\}^2\), choose \(\{a,b\}\) when \(\{a,b\}\subseteq z\), otherwise choose \(\{a\}\) if \(a\in z\) and \(\rho_a=1\), otherwise choose \(\{b\}\) if \(b\in z\) and \(\rho_b=1\), and otherwise choose \(\varnothing\); (iv) for an unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\) with remaining factor \(c\), either choose by the priority \(\{a,b\}\succ\{b\}\succ\{c\}\succ\varnothing\) with \(\{a\}\) below \(\varnothing\), or symmetrically by \(\{a,b\}\succ\{a\}\succ\{c\}\succ\varnothing\) with \(\{b\}\) below \(\varnothing\), placing every unlisted bundle below \(\varnothing\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:irrelevant-coordinate]\label{def:irrelevant-coordinate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Irrelevant offer coordinates}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(I(g)=\{j\in U:g(z)=g(z\setminus\{j\})\ \forall z\in\mathcal B\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:outcome-nonrestrictive-id]\label{def:outcome-nonrestrictive-id}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Outcome-nonrestrictive identification on \(\mathcal M\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The vector \((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\) is outcome-nonrestrictively identified on \(\mathcal M\) when any two laws satisfying the maintained assumptions, supported on \(\mathcal M\), having \(p^\star>0\), and inducing the same \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), have the same vector \((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\), without any further restriction on the conditional law of \((Y(t))_{t\in\mathcal B}\) given \(G\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:mobius-proof-handle]\label{def:mobius-proof-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Contraction-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Möbius classification handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Start from contraction consistency \(g(z)=r\) and \(r\subseteq w\subseteq z\Rightarrow g(w)=r\); condition on the full-offer choice \(g(U)\); reduce \(\sigma_t(g)=0\) first at triples, then pairs, then singletons; and close each branch by an explicit strict ranking. The target object is a non-enumerative proof that the full-offer-pair branch has exactly four maps with the remaining factor unresponsive and two with it responsive.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:signed-transforms]\label{def:signed-transforms}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Signed probability and outcome transforms}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(p_t=\sum_{z\supseteq t}(-1)^{|z|-|t|}q_{zt}\) and \(m_t=\sum_{z\supseteq t}(-1)^{|z|-|t|}r_{zt}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[lem:mobius-coefficients]\label{lem:mobius-coefficients}
Fix \(t\in\mathcal B\). If \(\lambda_t(z)=0\) for \(z\not\supseteq t\) and \(\sum_{z\in\mathcal B}\lambda_t(z)\mathbf 1\{g_{C_0}(z)=t\}=\mathbf 1\{C_0=U\}\) for every \(C_0\subseteq U\), then uniquely \(\lambda_t(z)=a_t(z)\) for every \(z\in\mathcal B\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(V=U\setminus t\).  Because \(\lambda_t(z)=0\) unless \(z\supseteq t\), define
\[
\alpha(S)=\lambda_t(t\cup S),\qquad S\subseteq V.
\]
For every \(B\subseteq V\), take \(C_0=t\cup B\).  By the definition of \(g_{C_0}\),
\[
g_{t\cup B}(t\cup S)=(t\cup B)\cap(t\cup S)=t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\cap B=\varnothing.
\]
The assumed isolation equations therefore give
\[
\sum_{S\subseteq V\setminus B}\alpha(S)=\mathbf 1\{B=V\}.
\]
On writing \(Q=V\setminus B\), this becomes
\[
F(Q):=\sum_{S\subseteq Q}\alpha(S)=\mathbf 1\{Q=\varnothing\},
\qquad Q\subseteq V. \tag{1}
\]
For completeness, finite Boolean inversion follows directly by interchanging two finite sums:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{R\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|R|}F(R)
&=\sum_{R\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|R|}\sum_{T\subseteq R}\alpha(T)\\
&=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\alpha(T)
  \sum_{R:T\subseteq R\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|R|}\\
&=\alpha(S),
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
because the inner sum is \((1-1)^{|S\setminus T|}\), hence is one when \(T=S\) and zero otherwise.  Substituting (1) into (2) yields
\[
\alpha(S)=(-1)^{|S|}.
\]
Consequently, for \(z=t\cup S\),
\[
\lambda_t(z)=(-1)^{|z|-|t|}=a_t(z),
\]
and both sides are zero when \(z\not\supseteq t\).  Conversely, summing these signs over \(S\subseteq V\setminus B\) gives \((1-1)^{|V\setminus B|}=\mathbf 1\{B=V\}\), while a separable map indexed by \(C_0\not\supseteq t\) never chooses \(t\).  Thus \(a_t\) satisfies every displayed isolation equation.  Equation (2) proves uniqueness, since it reconstructs every \(\alpha(S)\) from the prescribed values of \(F\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The separable benchmark itself forces the Boolean Möbius weights, so the envelope is not chosen to fit a desired answer. \textit{Gap.} The generic row-space criterion does not explain why these coefficients are unique once every coordinate-separable type is retained. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma fixes the only candidate observable recovery map and reduces maximality to a transparent signature test.

\begin{theorem}[thm:classification]\label{thm:classification}
The Möbius-null WARP set satisfies \(\mathcal N_3=\mathcal F_3\). Its four disjoint families have cardinalities \(1\), \(9\), \(12\), and \(6\), respectively. Hence \(|\mathcal N_3|=28\), \(|\mathcal E_3|=29\), exactly eight elements of \(\mathcal E_3\) are coordinate-separable, exactly 21 are interactive, and exactly six maps \(g\in\mathcal N_3\) satisfy \(I(g)=\varnothing\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first record the revealed-choice implication used throughout.  If \(g(z)=r\) and \(r\subseteq w\subseteq z\), strict rationalization gives \(r\succ_g d\) for every \(d\subseteq z\) with \(d\ne r\).  Every bundle feasible from \(w\) is feasible from \(z\), while \(r\) is feasible from \(w\); hence
\[
g(z)=r,\quad r\subseteq w\subseteq z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g(w)=r. \tag{3}
\]
This is contraction consistency, derived here from the strict-order hypothesis.

For a pair \(e\subset U\) and a singleton \(\{j\}\subset U\), the nonempty-cell null equations are, respectively,
\[
0=\sigma_e(g)=\mathbf 1\{g(e)=e\}-\mathbf 1\{g(U)=e\}, \tag{4}
\]
and, writing \(U=\{j,k,\ell\}\),
\[
0=\sigma_{\{j\}}(g)
=\mathbf 1\{g(\{j\})=\{j\}\}
-\mathbf 1\{g(\{j,k\})=\{j\}\}
-\mathbf 1\{g(\{j,\ell\})=\{j\}\}
+\mathbf 1\{g(U)=\{j\}\}. \tag{5}
\]
Also \(0=\sigma_U(g)=\mathbf 1\{g(U)=U\}\).  One-sidedness therefore leaves only \(|g(U)|=0,1,2\).

If \(g(U)=\varnothing\), (3), applied to every \(w\subseteq U\), gives \(g(w)=\varnothing\).  This is family (i).

Suppose next that \(g(U)=\{a\}\), where \(U=\{a,b,c\}\).  Equation (3) forces
\[
g(\{a\})=g(\{a,b\})=g(\{a,c\})=\{a\}. \tag{6}
\]
By (4), \(g(\{b,c\})\ne\{b,c\}\); one-sidedness thus makes it one of \(\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\}\).  Applying (5) to \(b\) and \(c\), and using (6), gives exactly
\[
\mathbf 1\{g(\{b\})=\{b\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{g(\{b,c\})=\{b\}\},
\qquad
\mathbf 1\{g(\{c\})=\{c\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{g(\{b,c\})=\{c\}\}. \tag{7}
\]
Thus, after choosing \(a\), there are exactly three maps, according as the secondary choice from \(\{b,c\}\) is \(\varnothing\), \(\{b\}\), or \(\{c\}\).  They are rationalized by ranking \(\{a\}\) first, then the selected secondary singleton if there is one, then \(\varnothing\), and putting every unlisted bundle below \(\varnothing\) in an arbitrary strict order.  These are precisely family (ii), giving \(3\cdot3=9\) maps.

It remains to treat the full-offer-pair branch.  Let \(g(U)=\{a,b\}\) and let \(c\) be the remaining factor.  Contraction and (4) imply
\[
g(\{a,b\})=\{a,b\},\qquad
x:=g(\{a,c\})\in\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{c\}\},\qquad
y:=g(\{b,c\})\in\{\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\}\}. \tag{8}
\]
The three equations (5) reduce exactly to
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf 1\{g(\{a\})=\{a\}\}&=\mathbf 1\{x=\{a\}\},\\
\mathbf 1\{g(\{b\})=\{b\}\}&=\mathbf 1\{y=\{b\}\},\\
\mathbf 1\{g(\{c\})=\{c\}\}&=\mathbf 1\{x=\{c\}\}+\mathbf 1\{y=\{c\}\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
If \(g(\{c\})=\varnothing\), the last line of (9) excludes \(\{c\}\) from both \(x\) and \(y\).  Hence \(x\in\{\varnothing,\{a\}\}\) and \(y\in\{\varnothing,\{b\}\}\), independently, and the first two lines of (9) determine the singleton choices.  There are exactly four maps.  Each is rationalized by placing \(\{a,b\}\) first, then whichever of \(\{a\}\) and \(\{b\}\) are responsive in either strict order, then \(\varnothing\), with all unlisted bundles below \(\varnothing\) in an arbitrary strict order.  These are family (iii).

If \(g(\{c\})=\{c\}\), the last line of (9) says that exactly one of \(x,y\) equals \(\{c\}\).  Suppose \(x=\{c\}\).  Choice of \(\{c\}\) from its singleton menu gives \(\{c\}\succ_g\varnothing\).  Since \(\{c\}\) is feasible from \(\{b,c\}\), one cannot have \(y=\varnothing\).  The last line of (9) also excludes \(y=\{c\}\), so (8) forces \(y=\{b\}\).  The first two lines of (9) then force \(g(\{a\})=\varnothing\) and \(g(\{b\})=\{b\}\).  The strict ranking
\[
\{a,b\}\succ_g\{b\}\succ_g\{c\}\succ_g\varnothing\succ_g\{a\}, \tag{10}
\]
with all other unlisted bundles below \(\varnothing\), rationalizes this map.  The case \(y=\{c\}\) is symmetric and is rationalized by
\[
\{a,b\}\succ_g\{a\}\succ_g\{c\}\succ_g\varnothing\succ_g\{b\}. \tag{11}
\]
Thus the responsive-\(c\) branch has exactly two maps, not merely at most two.  They are precisely family (iv).  Across the three choices of the unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\), families (iii) and (iv) have \(3\cdot4=12\) and \(3\cdot2=6\) maps.

It remains only to check the empty-cell equation and the converse inclusion.  For every one-sided map,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t\subseteq U}(-1)^{|t|}\sigma_t(g)
&=\sum_{z\subseteq U}(-1)^{|z|}
  \sum_{t\subseteq z}\mathbf 1\{g(z)=t\}\\
&=\sum_{z\subseteq U}(-1)^{|z|}=(1-1)^3=0. 
\end{aligned} \tag{12}
\]
The inner indicator sum is one by one-sidedness.  Hence the seven nonempty equations imply \(\sigma_\varnothing(g)=0\).  Conversely, every map just listed has the displayed strict rationalization; equations (4), (5), and (9) verify all its nonempty signatures, and (12) verifies its empty signature.  The cases are disjoint because their full-offer choices differ, except for families (iii) and (iv), which differ according as \(g(\{c\})\) is empty or nonempty.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal N_3=\mathcal F_3,qquad
|\mathcal N_3|=1+9+12+6=28,qquad
|\mathcal E_3|=29. \tag{13}
\]

We finally establish the two qualitative counts.  For \(C_0\ne U\), if \(t\not\subseteq C_0\), then \(g_{C_0}(z)\ne t\) for every \(z\); if \(t\subseteq C_0\), then
\[
\sigma_t(g_{C_0})
=\sum_{R\subseteq U\setminus C_0}(-1)^{|R|}
=(1-1)^{|U\setminus C_0|}=0. \tag{14}
\]
Thus the seven maps \(g_{C_0}\) with \(C_0\ne U\) are null, whereas \(g_U=g^\star\) and \(\sigma_t(g^\star)=1\) for every \(t\).  Hence exactly eight members of \(\mathcal E_3\) are coordinate-separable, and (13) leaves exactly \(29-8=21\) interactive maps.

Family (i) has every coordinate irrelevant.  In family (ii), any coordinate distinct from the primary and optional secondary singleton is irrelevant.  In family (iii), the remaining coordinate \(c\) is irrelevant.  In contrast, for the representative (10), removal of \(a\) from \(\{a,b\}\) changes choice from \(\{a,b\}\) to \(\{b\}\), removal of \(b\) from \(\{b,c\}\) changes choice from \(\{b\}\) to \(\{c\}\), and removal of \(c\) from \(\{c\}\) changes choice from \(\{c\}\) to \(\varnothing\).  Thus \(I(g)=\varnothing\) for (10); the same argument after interchanging \(a,b\) proves it for (11).  These are all six family-(iv) maps, and no map in the other families has empty \(I(g)\).  The final count is therefore exactly six.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact count and the six fully interactive maps show that the admissible relaxation is substantively larger than coordinate-specific receipt. \textit{Gap.} No inspected paper gives a symbolic three-factor WARP classification subject to simultaneous zero-Möbius restrictions. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem provides a finite, interpretable compliance menu for \(\texttt{factiv::iv\_factorial}\) users and the New Haven factorial-IV analysis.

\begin{theorem}[thm:envelope-iff]\label{thm:envelope-iff}
Within the present one-sided WARP ambient class \(\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\), for every \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\) with \(\mathcal S_3\subseteq\mathcal M\), the vector \((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\) is outcome-nonrestrictively identified on \(\mathcal M\) if and only if \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal E_3\). Consequently, \(\mathcal E_3\) is the unique inclusion-maximal such support within \(\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\). Here \(\mathcal S_3\) is the eight-map one-sided three-factor specialization, and hence a subclass, of Blackwell--Pashley's coordinate-specific receipt model; it is not that published class itself. Relative to this one-sided three-factor slice, \(\mathcal E_3\) enlarges the response family from eight to 29 maps while preserving identification of the same eight perfect-complier means. No sufficiency claim is made for a broader two-sided or non-WARP class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an ordering \(U=\{j_1,j_2,j_3\}\).  For \(C_0\subseteq U\), put \(\eta_k=1\) when \(j_k\in C_0\) and \(\eta_k=-1\) otherwise, and rank bundles by
\[
u_{C_0}(d)=\sum_{k=1}^3 \eta_k4^k\mathbf 1\{j_k\in d\}.
\]
If \(d\ne d'\), the largest-index term on which they differ has absolute value greater than the sum of all lower-index terms, so \(u_{C_0}(d)\ne u_{C_0}(d')\).  Thus this utility induces a strict total order.  On the menu \(\{d:d\subseteq z\}\), its unique maximizer includes every offered element of \(C_0\) and excludes every element of \(U\setminus C_0\), and hence equals \(z\cap C_0=g_{C_0}(z)\).  Also \(g_{C_0}(z)\subseteq z\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\), every separable map therefore belongs to that ambient class.

We next verify that the baseline is contained in the candidate envelope.  If \(C_0=U\), then \(g_{C_0}=g^\star\) by the definitions.  Suppose \(C_0\ne U\), and fix \(s\in\mathcal B\).  If \(s\not\subseteq C_0\), then \(g_{C_0}(z)=z\cap C_0\ne s\) for every \(z\), so \(\sigma_s(g_{C_0})=0\).  If \(s\subseteq C_0\), then, among the assignments containing \(s\), the equality \(g_{C_0}(z)=s\) holds exactly when \(z=s\cup R\) for some \(R\subseteq U\setminus C_0\).  The definition of the Möbius signature and the elementary binomial identity give
\[
\sigma_s(g_{C_0})
=\sum_{R\subseteq U\setminus C_0}(-1)^{|R|}
=(1-1)^{|U\setminus C_0|}=0,
\]
where the last equality uses \(C_0\ne U\).  Hence \(g_{C_0}\in\mathcal N_3\) for every \(C_0\ne U\), while \(g_U=g^\star\), and therefore
\[
\mathcal S_3\subseteq\mathcal E_3. \tag{28}
\]

Now let \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal E_3\), and consider two laws satisfying the maintained assumptions, supported on \(\mathcal M\), having positive perfect-complier share, and inducing the same observed law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Full-support randomization makes each conditional cell quantity \(q_{zt}\) and \(r_{zt}\) an observed-law functional.  Thus the signed transforms \(p_t\) and \(m_t\) are also the same under the two laws.  By the signed-identification theorem, for every \(t\in\mathcal B\),
\[
p_t=p^\star>0,
\qquad
m_t=p^\star\mu_t^\star,
\qquad
\mu_t^\star=\frac{m_t}{p_t}. \tag{29}
\]
The division in (29) is justified by the positive-perfect-share assumption.  The right-hand side depends only on \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), so the two laws have the same vector \((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\).  By the definition of outcome-nonrestrictive identification, this proves sufficiency without restricting the conditional distribution of the potential-outcome vector given response type.

Conversely, suppose \(\mathcal S_3\subseteq\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\) but \(\mathcal M\not\subseteq\mathcal E_3\).  Choose \(h\in\mathcal M\setminus\mathcal E_3\).  The maximal-converse theorem gives two admissible finite laws supported on
\[
\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}\subseteq\mathcal M
\]
that have the same observed law, have positive perfect-complier share, and disagree on at least one component of \((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\).  Equivalently, its inclusion corollary says directly that outcome-nonrestrictive identification fails on \(\mathcal M\).  Therefore identification on \(\mathcal M\) implies \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal E_3\), proving the equivalence.

Equation (28) shows that \(\mathcal E_3\) is itself among the supports under comparison, and the sufficient direction shows that it identifies the target.  The necessary direction shows that every identifying support in the present ambient class which contains \(\mathcal S_3\) is a subset of \(\mathcal E_3\).  Thus \(\mathcal E_3\) is the greatest, and hence the unique inclusion-maximal, such support within \(\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\).  The Möbius-coefficient lemma further shows that retaining all eight baseline maps uniquely forces the signed isolating coefficients \(a_t(z)\); hence the recovery map in (29) is not chosen by fitting the added interactive maps.  Finally, \(C_0\mapsto g_{C_0}\) is injective because \(g_{C_0}(U)=C_0\), so \(|\mathcal S_3|=2^3=8\), while the classification theorem gives \(|\mathcal E_3|=29\), with 21 interactive maps.  This is precisely an enlargement of the one-sided three-factor slice from eight to 29 maps, not a containment or sufficiency claim about Blackwell--Pashley's full published class or any broader two-sided or non-WARP class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The equivalence identifies the unique largest support only within the present one-sided WARP ambient class, rather than presenting another sufficient compliance restriction. \textit{Gap.} Blackwell--Pashley's published coordinate-specific receipt model is broader than the eight-map one-sided three-factor specialization \(\mathcal S_3\); Goff supplies a generic criterion and two-factor boundary rather than this 29-map maximal frontier for the one-sided three-factor WARP class. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem tells factorial-IV analysts exactly which interactive one-sided WARP uptake behaviors can be added to the eight-map slice while leaving every perfect-complier factorial estimand identified.

\begin{theorem}[thm:signed-identification]\label{thm:signed-identification}
If \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal E_3\), then for every \(t\in\mathcal B\), \(p_t=p^\star\), \(m_t=p^\star\mu_t^\star\), and \(\mu_t^\star=m_t/p_t\). Therefore every nonempty perfect-complier factorial effect is identified by \(\theta_S=2^{-2}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B}\chi_S(t)m_t/p_t\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Full assignment support makes every conditional cell functional below observable.  By receipt consistency and instrument independence, for every \(z,t\in\mathcal B\),
\[
q_{zt}
=P(G(z)=t\mid Z=z)
=P(G(z)=t). \tag{15}
\]
On \(\{G(z)=t\}\), receipt consistency gives \(D=t\), and outcome exclusion gives \(Y=Y(t)\).  Therefore, using integrability and then instrument independence,
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{zt}
&=\mathbb E_P\!\left[Y\mathbf 1\{D=t\}\mid Z=z\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_P\!\left[Y(t)\mathbf 1\{G(z)=t\}\mid Z=z\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_P\!\left[Y(t)\mathbf 1\{G(z)=t\}\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{16}
\]
The support assumption and \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal E_3\) imply \(G\in\mathcal E_3\) almost surely.  For every \(g\in\mathcal E_3\), the null-envelope definition gives
\[
\sigma_t(g)=\mathbf 1\{g=g^\star\}. \tag{17}
\]
Indeed null maps have zero signature, while \(g^\star(z)=z\) makes the sole nonzero summand in \(\sigma_t(g^\star)\) the assignment \(z=t\), with coefficient one.  Taking the finite signed sums in (15) and (16), and using (17), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_t
&=\sum_z a_t(z)\,\mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{G(z)=t\}]
=\mathbb E_P[\sigma_t(G)]
=P(G=g^\star)=p^\star,\\
m_t
&=\sum_z a_t(z)\,\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mathbf 1\{G(z)=t\}]
=\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\sigma_t(G)]\\
&=\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mathbf 1\{G=g^\star\}]
=p^\star\mu_t^\star.
\end{aligned} \tag{18}
\]
The positive-share assumption licenses division by \(p_t=p^\star>0\), so \(\mu_t^\star=m_t/p_t\).  Substitution into the Walsh definition gives, for every nonempty \(S\subseteq U\),
\[
\theta_S=2^{-2}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B}\chi_S(t)\frac{m_t}{p_t}.
\]
Thus the causal means and effects are proved equal to, rather than defined as, the displayed observable functionals.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The formulas translate the behavioral envelope into estimands computable from randomized assignment cells. \textit{Gap.} The signed ratios coincide algebraically with existing perfect-complier formulas under separable receipt; the new claim is their validity on the strictly larger classified envelope. \textit{Consumer.} The result directly supplies all seven main and interaction effects required by a three-factor perfect-complier analysis.

\begin{theorem}[thm:maximal-converse]\label{thm:maximal-converse}
For every \(h\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\setminus\mathcal E_3\), there exist two finite laws \(P_+\) and \(P_-\), supported on \(\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}\), with full-support randomized \(Z\), common positive response-type masses, and mutually independent Bernoulli potential outcomes conditional on type, such that \(\mathcal L_{P_+}(Z,D,Y)=\mathcal L_{P_-}(Z,D,Y)\) but \(\mu_t^\star(P_+)\ne\mu_t^\star(P_-)\) for at least one \(t\). The construction keeps every Bernoulli probability in \([1/4,3/4]\). Consequently, for every \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\) with \(\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}\subseteq\mathcal M\), the vector \((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\) is not outcome-nonrestrictively identified on \(\mathcal M\). This necessity conclusion is within the present one-sided WARP ambient class and makes no sufficiency assertion for broader two-sided or non-WARP classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(h\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\setminus\mathcal E_3\).  First note that the nine response types used below are distinct and admissible.  Indeed, each \(g_{C_0}\) is one-sided and is rationalized by the strict utility ranking
\[
u_{C_0}(d)=\sum_{k=1}^3\eta_k4^k\mathbf 1\{j_k\in d\},
\qquad
\eta_k=2\mathbf 1\{j_k\in C_0\}-1,
\]
for any ordering \(U=\{j_1,j_2,j_3\}\): distinct bundles have distinct utilities because the largest differing power dominates all smaller powers, and the unique maximizer among \(d\subseteq z\) is \(z\cap C_0\).  Hence \(g_{C_0}\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\).  Moreover, if \(C_0\ne U\), then for every \(s\in\mathcal B\), either \(s\not\subseteq C_0\) and \(g_{C_0}\) never selects \(s\), or
\[
\sigma_s(g_{C_0})
=\sum_{R\subseteq U\setminus C_0}(-1)^{|R|}
=(1-1)^{|U\setminus C_0|}=0.
\]
Thus \(g_{C_0}\in\mathcal N_3\) for \(C_0\ne U\), whereas \(g_U=g^\star\), proving \(\mathcal S_3\subseteq\mathcal E_3\).  Since \(h\notin\mathcal E_3\), it is distinct from all eight separable maps.

Because \(\mathcal E_3=\{g^\star\}\cup\mathcal N_3\), the chosen \(h\) is not \(g^\star\) and is not Möbius-null.  Consequently there exists \(t\in\mathcal B\) with
\[
\sigma_t(h)\ne0. \tag{19}
\]
Set \(V=U\setminus t\) and, for \(S\subseteq V\), define
\[
v_h(S)=\mathbf 1\{h(t\cup S)=t\}.
\]
For \(B\subseteq V\), the subset-inversion construction defines
\[
\beta_{t\cup B}
=\sum_{Q\subseteq B}(-1)^{|B|-|Q|}v_h(V\setminus Q),
\]
and we put \(\beta_C=0\) for \(C\not\supseteq t\).  For each \(S\subseteq V\), interchange the two finite sums to obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{B\subseteq V\setminus S}\beta_{t\cup B}
&=\sum_{Q\subseteq V\setminus S}v_h(V\setminus Q)
  \sum_{B:\,Q\subseteq B\subseteq V\setminus S}(-1)^{|B|-|Q|}\\
&=v_h(S). \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]
The inner sum equals \((1-1)^{|(V\setminus S)\setminus Q|}\), so it vanishes unless \(Q=V\setminus S\), in which case it equals one; this proves the second line of (20).  Taking \(B=V\) in the definition and substituting \(S=V\setminus Q\) yields
\[
\beta_U
=\sum_{S\subseteq V}(-1)^{|S|}v_h(S)
=\sum_{z\supseteq t}(-1)^{|z|-|t|}\mathbf 1\{h(z)=t\}
=\sigma_t(h)\ne0, \tag{21}
\]
where the penultimate equality is the bijection \(z=t\cup S\) between supersets of \(t\) and subsets of \(V\), and the last equality uses the definition of \(\sigma_t\) and (19).

On \(\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}\), set
\[
n_h=1,
\qquad
n_{g_C}=-\beta_C \quad(C\subseteq U).
\]
We claim that this vector is observationally null in receipt cell \(t\).  If \(z\not\supseteq t\), one-sided receipt implies \(g(z)\ne t\) for every type in the support.  If \(z=t\cup S\) with \(S\subseteq V\), then
\[
g_{t\cup B}(z)=z\cap(t\cup B)=t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B\cap S=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B\subseteq V\setminus S.
\]
All separable coefficients with index not containing \(t\) are zero.  Therefore (20) gives, for every \(z\in\mathcal B\),
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}}n_g\mathbf 1\{g(z)=t\}
=v_h(S)-\sum_{B\subseteq V\setminus S}\beta_{t\cup B}
=0, \tag{22}
\]
with the first, one-sided case understood separately.  At the perfect-complier type, (21) gives
\[
n_{g^\star}=n_{g_U}=-\beta_U=-\sigma_t(h)\ne0. \tag{23}
\]

Give each of the nine types the common mass \(q_g=1/9\).  Let \(K=\max_g|n_g|\); since \(n_h=1\), one has \(K\ge1\).  With \(\varepsilon=(36K)^{-1}\),
\[
\left|\frac{\varepsilon n_g}{q_g}\right|
=\frac{|n_g|}{4K}\le\frac14. \tag{24}
\]
Choose any assignment probabilities satisfying \(\pi_z>0\) for all \(z\in\mathcal B\), use the same assignment law under both signs, and make \(Z\) independent of \(G\) and all potential outcomes.  Conditional on \(G=g\), let the eight potential outcomes be mutually independent Bernoulli variables, with
\[
P_\pm\{Y(s)=1\mid G=g\}=\frac12 \quad(s\ne t),
\qquad
P_\pm\{Y(t)=1\mid G=g\}
=\frac12\pm\frac{\varepsilon n_g}{q_g}. \tag{25}
\]
Equation (24) puts every success probability in \([1/4,3/4]\), so the finite product construction defines two valid laws with finite moments.  Set \(D=G(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(D)\).  These definitions enforce receipt consistency and outcome exclusion, while the product construction enforces instrument independence and full-support randomization.  The response-type probabilities are common and positive, and
\[
p^\star=P_\pm(G=g^\star)=\frac19>0. \tag{26}
\]

It remains to compare the complete observed laws.  Because the type masses, response maps, and assignment law coincide, the conditional receipt distribution given every assignment is the same.  For a received bundle \(d\ne t\), (25) makes the conditional law of \(Y(d)\) identical under the two signs for every response type.  For \(d=t\), the difference at the success atom, conditional on \(Z=z\), is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_+(D=t,Y=1\mid Z=z)-P_-(D=t,Y=1\mid Z=z)\\
&\quad=\sum_{g:\,g(z)=t}q_g
\left[
\left(\frac12+\frac{\varepsilon n_g}{q_g}\right)
-\left(\frac12-\frac{\varepsilon n_g}{q_g}\right)
\right]\\
&\quad=2\varepsilon
\sum_{g\in\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}}n_g\mathbf 1\{g(z)=t\}=0, \tag{27}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is (22).  The failure atom also agrees because it is the common receipt probability minus the success-atom probability.  Thus all conditional atoms of \((D,Y)\) given \(Z=z\) agree.  Since the assignment law is common,
\[
\mathcal L_{P_+}(Z,D,Y)=\mathcal L_{P_-}(Z,D,Y). \tag{28}
\]
On the other hand, (23) and (25) imply
\[
\mu_t^\star(P_+)-\mu_t^\star(P_-)
=\frac{2\varepsilon n_{g^\star}}{q_{g^\star}}
\ne0. \tag{29}
\]
Equations (24)--(29) prove the stated finite Bernoulli witness for the arbitrary excluded type \(h\).

Finally, let \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal G^{\mathrm{WARP}}_3\) satisfy \(\mathcal S_3\cup\{h\}\subseteq\mathcal M\).  Both witness laws are then supported on \(\mathcal M\), satisfy the maintained causal assumptions, have the positive share in (26), induce the same \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) by (28), and disagree on a target component by (29).  The definition of outcome-nonrestrictive identification therefore implies that the vector of perfect-complier means is not identified on \(\mathcal M\).  This inclusion argument establishes necessity only; it does not transfer the 29-map sufficiency theorem outside the present one-sided WARP ambient class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Complete equality of the observed law and the explicit inclusion corollary certify nonidentification on every present-ambient support containing the eight-map baseline and an excluded type. \textit{Gap.} Goff's row-space converse gives an abstract obstruction; the subset-inversion witness is finite, Bernoulli, and explicit for every excluded one-sided WARP addition, without asserting sufficiency beyond that ambient class. \textit{Consumer.} The countermodels prevent researchers from interpreting signed ratios after adding any response behavior outside the envelope to the one-sided three-factor slice, even when first-stage probability diagnostics cancel.

\begin{theorem}[thm:joint-inference]\label{thm:joint-inference}
Under i.i.d. sampling, finite second moments, \(p^\star\ge\kappa>0\), and positive definite \(\Omega\), \(\sqrt n(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}-\boldsymbol\theta)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\theta}(O_i)+o_P(1)\Rightarrow N(0,\Omega)\). The plug-in \(\widehat\Omega\) is consistent and \(P(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathcal C_{\theta})\to1-\alpha_0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All assertions are pointwise at a fixed law satisfying the displayed assumptions.  Since \(\mathcal B\) is finite and every \(\pi_z>0\), the finite-cell asymptotic lemma applies jointly to all cell estimators.  Linear combination with the fixed coefficients \(a_t(z)\) yields
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat p_t-p_t)
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{p,t}(O_i)+o_P(1),
\qquad
\sqrt n(\widehat m_t-m_t)
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{m,t}(O_i)+o_P(1). \tag{29}
\]
By signed identification, \(p_t=p^\star\) and \(m_t=p^\star\mu_t^\star\).  The lower bound \(p^\star\ge\kappa>0\) and (29) imply \(\widehat p_t\xrightarrow{P}p^\star\) and hence license the ratio expansion
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n(\widehat\mu_t-\mu_t^\star)
&=\frac{1}{\widehat p_t}\sqrt n
 \left[(\widehat m_t-m_t)-\mu_t^\star(\widehat p_t-p_t)\right]\\
&=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{\phi_{m,t}(O_i)-\mu_t^\star\phi_{p,t}(O_i)}{p^\star}
 +o_P(1)\\
&=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_{\mu,t}(O_i)+o_P(1).
\end{aligned} \tag{30}
\]
Stacking the eight equalities and left-multiplying by the fixed Walsh matrix \(H\) gives
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}-\boldsymbol\theta)
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_\theta(O_i)+o_P(1). \tag{31}
\]
Finite second moments follow for all coordinates because assignment weights are finite constants, receipt indicators are bounded, \(\mathbb E_P[Y^2]<\infty\), and \(p^\star\ge\kappa\).  The multivariate central limit conclusion in the cited lemma therefore gives
\[
n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_\theta(O_i)\ \Rightarrow\ N(0,\Omega). \tag{32}
\]

To make the plug-in assertion explicit, take \(\widehat\Omega\) to be the empirical second moment of \(\phi_\theta\) after replacing every arm probability and cell moment by its empirical counterpart and replacing \(p^\star,\mu_t^\star\) by \(\widehat p_t,\widehat\mu_t\), with any fixed convention on the event that a denominator is zero.  Positive arm probabilities and \(p^\star\ge\kappa\) make the probability of that exceptional event tend to zero.  The plug-in consistency part of the finite-cell lemma and continuous mapping yield
\[
\widehat\Omega\to_P\Omega. \tag{33}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(\Omega\) licenses inversion.  From (31)--(33) and the Wald conclusion of the cited lemma,
\[
n(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}-\boldsymbol\theta)^{\mathsf T}
\widehat\Omega^{-1}
(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}-\boldsymbol\theta)
\Rightarrow\chi_7^2. \tag{34}
\]
Taking the \((1-\alpha_0)\)-quantile in (34) proves
\[
P(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathcal C_\theta)\longrightarrow1-\alpha_0.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} All effects share signed cell moments and denominators, so joint covariance propagation is necessary for simultaneous claims. \textit{Gap.} Blackwell--Pashley provide the scalar and vector factorial-IV ingredients; this theorem records their adaptation to the Möbius vector and is an inference rung rather than the novelty kernel. \textit{Consumer.} The result permits family-wise statements about all seven factorial effects in software and applied reanalysis when the perfect-complier share is not weak.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fieller-inversion]\label{thm:fieller-inversion}
Under i.i.d. sampling, finite second moments, full assignment support, and Fieller nondegeneracy, \(P\bigl((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}\in\prod_{t\in\mathcal B}\mathcal F_t\bigr)\ge1-\alpha_0+o(1)\). Hence \(H(\prod_{t\in\mathcal B}\mathcal F_t)\) covers \(\boldsymbol\theta\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha_0+o(1)\); the set may be unbounded or disconnected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a bundle \(t\) and put \(u=\mu_t^\star\).  Signed identification gives the zero population moment
\[
m_t-u p_t=p^\star\mu_t^\star-\mu_t^\star p^\star=0. \tag{35}
\]
The finite-cell asymptotic lemma and linearity give
\[
\sqrt n\bigl[(\widehat m_t-u\widehat p_t)-(m_t-u p_t)\bigr]
=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n
\bigl(\phi_{m,t}(O_i)-u\phi_{p,t}(O_i)\bigr)+o_P(1). \tag{36}
\]
The assumed variance is strictly positive at this fixed \(u\), and finite second moments hold by the outcome moment assumption and bounded receipt indicators.  Thus the consistent plug-in variance in the definition of \(\mathcal F_t\) and the studentized conclusion of the cited lemma imply
\[
\frac{\sqrt n(\widehat m_t-u\widehat p_t)}
{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\phi_{m,t}-u\phi_{p,t})^{1/2}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1). \tag{37}
\]
Since \(z_{1-\alpha_0/16}\) is the two-sided normal critical value with tail probability \(\alpha_0/8\), (37) and the definition of \(\mathcal F_t\) give
\[
P(\mu_t^\star\in\mathcal F_t)=1-\frac{\alpha_0}{8}+o(1). \tag{38}
\]
There are exactly eight bundles.  The union bound, which requires no independence among the eight statistics, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\left((\mu_t^\star)_{t\in\mathcal B}
 \in\prod_{t\in\mathcal B}\mathcal F_t\right)
&\ge1-\sum_{t\in\mathcal B}P(\mu_t^\star\notin\mathcal F_t)\\
&\ge1-\alpha_0+o(1).
\end{aligned} \tag{39}
\]
Whenever the event in (39) occurs, the identity \(\boldsymbol\theta=H\boldsymbol\mu^\star\) implies
\[
\boldsymbol\theta\in H\left(\prod_{t\in\mathcal B}\mathcal F_t\right),
\]
which proves the simultaneous Walsh coverage claim.

For fixed data, the defining Fieller inequality is a quadratic inequality in \(u\), because both \((\widehat m_t-u\widehat p_t)^2\) and the empirical variance of \(\phi_{m,t}-u\phi_{p,t}\) are quadratic polynomials in \(u\).  Its leading coefficient need not be positive; consequently its solution can be a bounded interval, a half-line, all of \(\mathbb R\), or the union of two rays, the last case being disconnected.  Products of such sets can therefore be unbounded or disconnected, and because every column of \(H\) is nonzero, configurations with separated components can retain that disconnection after the Walsh map.  Hence neither boundedness nor connectedness is asserted for the simultaneous construction.  The argument is pointwise for a fixed law with \(p^\star>0\) and positive studentizing variances; it makes no uniform claim for sequences with \(p^\star\) vanishing.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Test inversion remains informative about identification weakness because it is allowed to return unbounded sets. \textit{Gap.} Existing factorial-IV work treats scalar Fieller intervals; the contribution here is the explicit Bonferroni product and Walsh image for the dependent eight-ratio system, not uniform weak-share adaptation. \textit{Consumer.} Applied analysts obtain a simultaneous fallback when the estimated perfect-complier share is too small for the Wald ellipsoid.

\begin{theorem}[thm:symbolic-null-classification]\label{thm:symbolic-null-classification}
The contraction--Möbius argument gives
\[
\mathcal N_3=\mathcal F_3,
\qquad
|\mathcal N_3|=1+9+12+6=28.
\]
More explicitly, fix a labeling \(U=\{a,b,c\}\) and abbreviate
\(\{a,b\}\) by \(ab\), and similarly for the other pairs.  In the
branch of a map \(g\in\mathcal N_3\) with \(g(U)=ab\), the four maps for which the remaining factor is
unresponsive alone, meaning \(g(\{c\})=\varnothing\), are indexed by
\((\rho_a,\rho_b)\in\{0,1\}^2\) and, for every \(z\subseteq U\), obey
\[
g(z)=
\begin{cases}
ab,&ab\subseteq z,\\
\{a\},&a\in z,\ \rho_a=1,\text{ and }ab\nsubseteq z,\\
\{b\},&b\in z,\ \rho_b=1,\text{ and }ab\nsubseteq z,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The two maps for which the remaining factor is responsive alone,
meaning \(g(\{c\})=\{c\}\), have, in the coordinate order
\(\{a\},\{b\},\{c\},ab,ac,bc,U\), respectively the value vectors
\[
(\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\},ab,\{c\},\{b\},ab)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\{a\},\varnothing,\{c\},ab,\{a\},\{c\},ab).
\]
Consequently the full-offer-pair branch contains exactly four maps
with \(c\) unresponsive alone and exactly two maps with \(c\)
responsive alone; together with the one full-offer-empty map and the
nine full-offer-singleton maps, these are exactly the four families in
\(\mathcal F_3\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathbf 1\{E\}\) for the indicator of an event or proposition
\(E\).  By one-sided receipt,
\(g(\varnothing)\subseteq\varnothing\), hence
\(g(\varnothing)=\varnothing\).  We first derive the consequence of
strict rational choice that will be used below.  If
\[
g(z)=r,
\qquad
r\subseteq w\subseteq z,
\]
then \(r\) is feasible from both offers \(z\) and \(w\).  By strict
rational choice, \(r\succ_g d\) for every other \(d\subseteq z\).
Every competitor feasible from \(w\) is also feasible from \(z\), so
\(r\) remains the unique \(\succ_g\)-maximal feasible bundle from
\(w\).  Therefore
\[
g(z)=r,\quad r\subseteq w\subseteq z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g(w)=r. \tag{1}
\]

For distinct \(j,k\in U\), abbreviate \(\{j,k\}\) by \(jk\).
Expanding the definition of the Möbius signature on the three-element
Boolean lattice gives
\[
\sigma_U(g)=\mathbf 1\{g(U)=U\}, \tag{2}
\]
\[
\sigma_{jk}(g)
=\mathbf 1\{g(jk)=jk\}-\mathbf 1\{g(U)=jk\}, \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\sigma_{\{j\}}(g)
=\mathbf 1\{g(\{j\})=\{j\}\}
-\sum_{k\in U\setminus\{j\}}
 \mathbf 1\{g(jk)=\{j\}\}
+\mathbf 1\{g(U)=\{j\}\}. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, these are precisely the terms with supersets of cardinality
three, at least two, and at least one, respectively, and their signs
are \((-1)^{|z|-|t|}\), as prescribed by the definition.

We prove \(\mathcal N_3\subseteq\mathcal F_3\).  Let
\(g\in\mathcal N_3\).  Thus all its signatures vanish.  Equation
\((2)\) yields \(g(U)\ne U\).  One-sided receipt then leaves only the
three possibilities
\[
|g(U)|=0,
\qquad
|g(U)|=1,
\qquad
|g(U)|=2. \tag{5}
\]

If \(g(U)=\varnothing\), apply \((1)\) with
\(r=\varnothing\) and arbitrary \(w\subseteq U\).  It follows that
\(g(w)=\varnothing\) for every \(w\), which is family (i).

Suppose next that \(g(U)=\{a\}\), and write the other two factors as
\(b,c\).  Applying \((1)\) to every intermediate offer containing
\(a\) gives
\[
g(\{a\})=g(ab)=g(ac)=g(U)=\{a\}. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(g(U)\ne bc\), equations \((3)\) and
\(\sigma_{bc}(g)=0\) imply \(g(bc)\ne bc\).  One-sided receipt
therefore gives
\[
g(bc)\in\{\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\}\}. \tag{7}
\]
Substituting \((6)\) and \(g(U)=\{a\}\) into \((4)\), first for
\(j=b\) and then for \(j=c\), gives the two exact equations
\[
\mathbf 1\{g(\{b\})=\{b\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{g(bc)=\{b\}\},
\qquad
\mathbf 1\{g(\{c\})=\{c\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{g(bc)=\{c\}\}. \tag{8}
\]
Because a one-sided choice from a singleton is either that singleton
or the empty bundle, \((7)\)--\((8)\) determine the response map
completely: outside offers containing \(a\), there is either no
responsive singleton, responsive singleton \(b\), or responsive
singleton \(c\).  These are exactly the three family-(ii) maps for
the fixed primary factor \(a\).  There are three choices of \(a\), so
this branch has nine maps.

It remains to analyze \(g(U)=ab\), where \(c\) is the remaining
factor.  Contraction \((1)\) gives
\[
g(ab)=ab. \tag{9}
\]
Set \(x=g(ac)\) and \(y=g(bc)\).  Equations \((3)\), applied to
\(ac\) and \(bc\), show that \(x\ne ac\) and \(y\ne bc\), since the
corresponding full-offer indicators vanish.  Together with one-sided
receipt, this yields
\[
x\in\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{c\}\},
\qquad
y\in\{\varnothing,\{b\},\{c\}\}. \tag{10}
\]
Substitution of \((9)\)--\((10)\) and \(g(U)=ab\) into the three
instances of \((4)\) gives
\[
\mathbf 1\{g(\{a\})=\{a\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{x=\{a\}\}, \tag{11}
\]
\[
\mathbf 1\{g(\{b\})=\{b\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{y=\{b\}\}, \tag{12}
\]
and
\[
\mathbf 1\{g(\{c\})=\{c\}\}
=\mathbf 1\{x=\{c\}\}+\mathbf 1\{y=\{c\}\}. \tag{13}
\]

If \(g(\{c\})=\varnothing\), the left side of \((13)\) is zero;
both indicators on its right side must therefore be zero.  Hence
\[
x\in\{\varnothing,\{a\}\},
\qquad
y\in\{\varnothing,\{b\}\}. \tag{14}
\]
The two choices in \((14)\) are independent, while \((11)\) and
\((12)\) determine the singleton responses.  Thus there are exactly
\(2\cdot2=4\) such maps, precisely the family-(iii) maps indexed by
\((\rho_a,\rho_b)\in\{0,1\}^2\).

If instead \(g(\{c\})=\{c\}\), equation \((13)\) says that exactly
one of \(x,y\) equals \(\{c\}\).  Suppose first that
\(x=\{c\}\).  Since \(x\) is the strictly rational choice from
\(ac\), one has \(\{c\}\succ_g\varnothing\).  The bundle
\(\{c\}\) is feasible from \(bc\), so strict maximality makes
\(y=\varnothing\) impossible.  By \((10)\), and because
\(y\ne\{c\}\), necessarily
\[
y=\{b\}. \tag{15}
\]
Equations \((11)\)--\((12)\) then force
\(g(\{a\})=\varnothing\) and \(g(\{b\})=\{b\}\).  This is the
first displayed responsive-\(c\) value vector in the statement.  If
\(y=\{c\}\), the same argument with \(a,b\) exchanged forces
\[
x=\{a\},
\qquad
g(\{a\})=\{a\},
\qquad
g(\{b\})=\varnothing, \tag{16}
\]
which is the second vector.  Thus the responsive-\(c\) branch has
exactly two maps.  In particular, the putative seventh pattern is
excluded: when one of \(x,y\) equals \(\{c\}\), strict preference of
\(\{c\}\) to \(\varnothing\) prevents the other choice from being
empty, while \((13)\) prevents it from also being \(\{c\}\).
This proves that every null rational map belongs to one of the four
families.

For the reverse inclusion, we exhibit strict rationalizations and
verify every signature.  Since \(\mathcal B\) is finite, each of the
following displayed priority lists can be completed to a strict total
order by ordering all bundles in an indicated residual block
arbitrarily.  For family (i), rank \(\varnothing\) above every
nonempty bundle.  For family (ii), rank the primary singleton
\(\{a\}\) first, then the optional secondary singleton when it is
nonempty, then \(\varnothing\), and put every unlisted bundle below
\(\varnothing\).  For family (iii), rank \(ab\) first, then the
singletons among \(\{a\},\{b\}\) whose corresponding
\(\rho\)-value is one, then \(\varnothing\), and put every unlisted
bundle below \(\varnothing\).  Finally, the two family-(iv) maps are
rationalized respectively by
\[
ab\succ_g\{b\}\succ_g\{c\}\succ_g\varnothing\succ_g\{a\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
ab\succ_g\{a\}\succ_g\{c\}\succ_g\varnothing\succ_g\{b\},
\tag{17}
\]
with every unlisted bundle below \(\varnothing\).  Inspection of each
principal menu \(\{d:d\subseteq z\}\) gives exactly its defining
family rule.  Hence every map in \(\mathcal F_3\) is one-sided and
strictly rational.

It remains only to check nullity; this check also makes the use of all
eight signature equations explicit.  In family (i), no nonempty
bundle is ever selected, so all seven nonempty signatures are zero.
In family (ii), equation \((2)\) is zero, all pair signatures in
\((3)\) are zero, and
\[
\sigma_{\{a\}}(g)=1-1-1+1=0. \tag{18}
\]
If the optional secondary singleton is, say, \(\{b\}\), then
\[
\sigma_{\{b\}}(g)=1-0-1+0=0, \tag{19}
\]
while every term of \(\sigma_{\{c\}}(g)\) is zero; when there is no
secondary singleton all terms for both \(b,c\) are zero, and the
case with secondary singleton \(\{c\}\) is symmetric.  In family
(iii), equation \((3)\) gives
\(\sigma_{ab}(g)=1-1=0\) and gives zero for the other two pairs,
while
\[
\sigma_{\{a\}}(g)=\rho_a-0-\rho_a+0=0,
\qquad
\sigma_{\{b\}}(g)=\rho_b-0-\rho_b+0=0,
\qquad
\sigma_{\{c\}}(g)=0. \tag{20}
\]
Equation \((2)\) again gives zero.  For the first family-(iv) map in
the statement, the pair signatures are
\(\sigma_{ab}(g)=1-1=0\) and zero for \(ac,bc\), and the singleton
signatures are
\[
\sigma_{\{a\}}(g)=0,
\qquad
\sigma_{\{b\}}(g)=1-0-1+0=0,
\qquad
\sigma_{\{c\}}(g)=1-1-0+0=0. \tag{21}
\]
The second family-(iv) map is symmetric, and its triple signature is
zero by \((2)\).

For completeness, the empty-cell signature follows from the other
seven and one-sided receipt rather than requiring a further case
check.  Interchanging the two finite sums in the definition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t\subseteq U}(-1)^{|t|}\sigma_t(g)
&=\sum_{z\subseteq U}(-1)^{|z|}
  \sum_{t\subseteq z}\mathbf 1\{g(z)=t\}\\
&=\sum_{z\subseteq U}(-1)^{|z|}\\
&=(1-1)^3=0.
\end{aligned} \tag{22}
\]
The inner indicator sum in the first line equals one because
one-sided receipt ensures that the unique value \(g(z)\) is a subset
of \(z\).  Since all nonempty signatures in
\((18)\)--\((21)\) and their stated symmetric cases vanish,
\((22)\) implies \(\sigma_\varnothing(g)=0\).  Thus
\(\mathcal F_3\subseteq\mathcal N_3\), completing the equality.

Finally, the empty, singleton, and pair cases are disjoint because
their full-offer choices have different cardinalities.  Within the
pair case, families (iii) and (iv) are separated by whether
\(g(\{c\})\) is empty or equals \(\{c\}\); within every branch the
displayed secondary or singleton choices determine the map.  Their counts are
\[
1,
