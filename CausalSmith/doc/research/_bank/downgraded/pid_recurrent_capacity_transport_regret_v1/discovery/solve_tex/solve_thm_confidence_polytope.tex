Let
\[
 \mathcal E_{n,\alpha}
 =\{((r_{bt}),(q_w))\in\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\}.             \tag{1}
\]
By the premise, \(\mathsf P(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})\ge 1-\alpha\).  Work on this event.  Because the observable law is admitted by \(\mathfrak M_H\), the converse in \(\text{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}\) and \(\text{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}\) imply \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\ne\varnothing\).  Fix \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\) and define
\[
 r^\circ_{bt}=\pi p_{bt},\qquad
 s^\circ_w=\pi q_w,\qquad
 x^\circ_{bw}=\pi x_{bw}.                                \tag{2}
\]
Landmark-survival positivity gives \(\pi>0\), while probability normalization gives
\[
 \sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}=\pi\le 1.                         \tag{3}
\]
Membership in the event (1) gives the cellwise constraints on \(r^\circ\), and, because \(s^\circ_w=q_w\sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}\),
\[
 \underline q_w\sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}
 \le s^\circ_w
 \le \overline q_w\sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}                 \tag{4}
\]
for every \(w\in\mathbb I_H\).  The row equations, column equations, and support restriction in \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\) give
\[
 \sum_wx^\circ_{bw}=\pi p_b=\sum_t r^\circ_{bt},\qquad
 \sum_bx^\circ_{bw}=\pi q_w=s^\circ_w,\qquad
 x^\circ_{bw}=0\quad\text{if }b+w>H.                    \tag{5}
\]
Together with nonnegativity, (2)--(5) prove
\[
 (\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ,\mathbf x^\circ)
 \in\widehat{\mathcal P}_{n,\alpha}.                    \tag{6}
\]
This argument holds for every \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\).  The corresponding image coordinate is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{b<m,t}t r^\circ_{bt}+\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x^\circ_{bw}
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m,t}t p_{bt}+\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x_{bw}\right)\\
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m}\mu_b+\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x_{bw}\right).
 \end{aligned}                                            \tag{7}
\]
By \(\text{thm:joint-value-set-and-support}\), as \(x\) ranges over \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\), the vector in (7) ranges over the entire sharp causal identified set.  Equations (6)--(7) therefore yield
\[
 \mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,\alpha}
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}.                  \tag{8}
\]

For deterministic optimizer coverage, positivity of \(\pi\) makes the scaling map bijective:
\[
 \mathsf X_H(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)
 =\{\pi x:x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\}.                          \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, one inclusion follows from the equations in (5), and the reverse inclusion follows by dividing every candidate plan by \(\pi>0\).  Substitution into \(\mathsf J_m\) and then into its transport optimum gives, simultaneously for every \(m,j\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 \mathsf D_{mj}(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)=D_{mj},
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_m(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)=R_m.       \tag{10}
\]
The true margin pair belongs to the projection of the confidence polytope by (6).  Hence
\[
 \underline R_m^{n,\alpha}\le R_m\le\overline R_m^{n,\alpha}
 \quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M.                  \tag{11}
\]
If \(m\in\mathcal M^\star\), then \(R_m=\min_jR_j\), and (11) gives
\[
 \underline R_m^{n,\alpha}
 \le R_m
 =\min_{j\in\mathcal M}R_j
 \le\min_{j\in\mathcal M}\overline R_j^{n,\alpha}.     \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(m\in\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\), proving
\[
 \mathcal M^\star\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}.                  \tag{13}
\]
No step makes a Hall inequality or regret comparison strict, so (8) and (13) remain valid on Hall faces and retain every tied deterministic minimizer.

On the event (1), (6) shows that the confidence polytope is nonempty.  It is closed and bounded: all its coordinates are nonnegative, its total row mass is at most one, and its row and column equations bound every \(s_w\) and \(x_{bw}\) by that total mass.  Hence it and its coordinate projections are compact polytopes, including candidate points of total mass zero.  The value confidence set is a linear image of this polytope.  For fixed candidate margins and each \((m,j)\), \(\mathsf D_{mj}\) is the optimum of the explicitly constrained finite transport program over \(\mathsf X_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\); the regret takes a maximum over the finite menu, and its envelopes optimize this finite-dimensional value over the displayed compact projection.  Thus all reported sets and envelopes are computable finite-dimensional optimization objects.  Taking probabilities in (8) and (13) proves both inclusions with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).

%%% FIELD randomized-confidence-definition
Put \(\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}\widehat{\mathcal P}_{n,\alpha}\).  For \((\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\in\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}\), define
\[
 \mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)
 =\{(\mathsf J_m(\mathbf r,\mathbf x))_{m\in\mathcal M}:
       \mathbf x\in\mathsf X_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\},
 \qquad
 h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}(\lambda)
 =\max_{v\in\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}\lambda^\top v,
\]
and
\[
 \mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta)
 =\max_{j\in\mathcal M}
   h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}(\delta-e_j),
 \qquad \delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}.
\]
When \(\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}\ne\varnothing\), set
\[
 \underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)
 =\inf_{(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\in\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}}
      \mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta),
 \qquad
 \overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)
 =\sup_{(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\in\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}}
      \mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta),
\]
and define the randomized-optimizer outer set by
\[
 \widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}
 =\left\{\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}:
   \underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)
   \le \inf_{\lambda\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}}
          \overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\lambda)\right\}.
\]
If \(\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}=\varnothing\), use the total convention \(\underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)=0\), \(\overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)=H\), and \(\widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}=\Delta_{\mathcal M}\).

%%% FIELD randomized-confidence-statement
If \(\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\) covers \((r_{bt},q_w)\) simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), then with the same probability \(\Delta^\star\subseteq\widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}\).  This inclusion retains every randomized minimizer without requiring uniqueness, strict Hall slack, or regret separation.  For candidate margins of total mass \(\tau\), \(\mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}\) is \(H\tau\)-Lipschitz on \(\Delta_{\mathcal M}\) in \(\ell^1\), and the two confidence envelopes are \(H\)-Lipschitz; consequently \(\widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}\) is closed and is computable by finite-dimensional transport-support optimization.  Moreover, when \(H=3\) and \(\mathcal M=\{1,2,3\}\), there are two triangular law sequences in \(\mathfrak M_H\) whose \(n\)-sample observed-data laws have total variation \(o(1)\), such that the first has the unique randomized optimizer \(e_3\) and the second has the unique randomized optimizer \((9/16,5/16,1/8)\).  Therefore, for \(0<\alpha<1/2\), every uniformly honest observed-data outer confidence region for \(\Delta^\star\) has a law sequence along which it contains both points with limiting lower probability at least \(1-2\alpha\).

%%% FIELD randomized-confidence-proof
First prove the finite-sample inclusion.  Work on the simultaneous first-stage event \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\) in (1) of the proof of \(\text{thm:confidence-polytope}\), and put
\[
 \mathbf r^\circ=(\pi p_{bt})_{b,t},\qquad
 \mathbf s^\circ=(\pi q_w)_w.                            \tag{1}
\]
That proof shows \((\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)\in\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}\).  Moreover, by \(\pi>0\), its scaling identity (9) and value identity (7) give
\[
 \mathsf V_H(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)
 =\mathcal V(p_{BT},q).                                  \tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)}=h_{\mathcal V}\), and the support representation in \(\text{thm:randomized-minimax-regret}\) yields
\[
 \mathsf R_{\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ}(\delta)
 =\max_{j\in\mathcal M}h_{\mathcal V}(\delta-e_j)
 =\mathcal R(\delta)                                    \tag{3}
\]
for every \(\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}\).  Thus the true candidate margins are one of the margin pairs over which both envelopes range, and
\[
 \underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)
 \le\mathcal R(\delta)
 \le\overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)             \tag{4}
\]
simultaneously for all \(\delta\).  If \(\delta^\star\in\Delta^\star\), then
\[
 \underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta^\star)
 \le\mathcal R(\delta^\star)
 =\inf_{\lambda\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}}\mathcal R(\lambda)
 \le\inf_{\lambda\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}}
      \overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\lambda).         \tag{5}
\]
The defining inequality therefore puts every \(\delta^\star\) in \(\widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}\).  Since the event has probability at least \(1-\alpha\), this proves the asserted simultaneous coverage, including all ties.

We next give the promised Lipschitz and computability details.  For candidate margins let
\[
 \tau=\sum_{b,t}r_{bt}=\sum_ws_w.
\]
Every \(\mathbf x\in\mathsf X_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\) has total mass \(\tau\).  Since \(0\le t,w\le H\), the two row sets in the definition of \(\mathsf J_m\) are disjoint and exhaust a subset of the total row mass, whence
\[
 0\le \mathsf J_m(\mathbf r,\mathbf x)\le H\tau
 \quad(m\in\mathcal M).                                 \tag{6}
\]
It follows directly from the support definition that, for all \(\lambda,\lambda'\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal M|}\),
\[
 \left|h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}(\lambda)
       -h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}(\lambda')\right|
 \le H\tau\,\|\lambda-\lambda'\|_1.                    \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the left side is at most \(\sup_{v\in\mathsf V_H}|(\lambda-\lambda')^\top v|\), and (6) bounds every coordinate of \(v\) by \(H\tau\).  The elementary inequality \(|\max_j a_j-\max_j a'_j|\le\max_j|a_j-a'_j|\) and (7) now imply
\[
 |\mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta)
   -\mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta')|
 \le H\tau\,\|\delta-\delta'\|_1.                      \tag{8}
\]
Every pair in \(\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}\) has \(0\le\tau\le1\).  Taking an infimum or a supremum of a family with the common Lipschitz constant \(H\) preserves that constant, so both envelopes are \(H\)-Lipschitz.  The simplex is compact; hence the infimum of the upper envelope over it is attained, and its weak sublevel inequality makes \(\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha}\) closed.  Finally, \(\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{n,\alpha}\) is the projection of the explicitly constrained finite polytope, each support value is a finite transport linear program over \(\mathsf X_H\), and only finitely many comparators are maximized.  Thus the envelopes and the outer set are finite-dimensional optimization objects; no differentiability or active-face stability is used.

It remains to prove the uniform obstruction.  Fix \(H=3\), \(\mathcal M=\{1,2,3\}\), and the common \((p,q)\) margins of \(\mathsf E_3\).  Their nontrivial Hall slacks are strictly positive, so \(\Gamma_3(p,q)\ne\varnothing\).  For law zero set \(T=0\) in every row.  Then \(\mu_b=0\) for all \(b\).  Capacity forces row \(b=3\) into column \(w=0\), so every compatible value vector satisfies
\[
 J_3=0,
 \qquad J_1\ge0,
 \qquad J_2\ge0.                                        \tag{9}
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal R(e_3)=0\).  To prove uniqueness, consider the feasible plan whose nonzero cells are
\[
 x_{00}=\frac15,\quad x_{03}=\frac15,\quad
 x_{11}=\frac1{10},\quad x_{12}=\frac15,\quad
 x_{21}=\frac15,\quad x_{30}=\frac1{10}.                 \tag{10}
\]
Its row and column sums are the displayed \(p\) and \(q\) margins of \(\mathsf E_3\), and every listed cell obeys \(b+w\le3\).  Under law zero it generates
\[
 (J_1,J_2,J_3)=\left(\frac7{10},\frac15,0\right).        \tag{11}
\]
For any \(\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}\), comparison with threshold three at this same latent law gives
\[
 \mathcal R(\delta)
 \ge \frac7{10}\delta_1+\frac15\delta_2.                \tag{12}
\]
The right side is positive unless \(\delta=e_3\).  Equations (9)--(12) prove that law zero has the unique randomized optimizer \(e_3\).

For law one, retain the same \((p,q)\) margins.  Conditional on \(B=1\), put probabilities \(5/6\) and \(1/6\) on \(T=1\) and \(T=2\), respectively; conditional on \(B=2\), put probabilities \(1/8\) and \(7/8\) on \(T=0\) and \(T=1\); set \(T=0\) in rows zero and three.  Multiplication by the row masses gives
\[
 (\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)
 =\left(0,\frac7{20},\frac7{40},0\right),               \tag{13}
\]
so this is the value geometry of \(\mathsf E_3\).  The exact calculation in \(\text{thm:randomized-minimax-regret}\) proves that its unique randomized optimizer is
\[
 \delta^\star=\left(\frac9{16},\frac5{16},\frac18\right). \tag{14}
\]

We now realize these two summary laws as nearly indistinguishable observed-data laws.  In both, randomize baseline treatment with probability \(1/2\), use no death, set \(S_k^0=S_k^1=1\), use the same pre-landmark intensive paths realizing the common \(B\) margin, and use the same standard-arm sustained paths realizing the common \(q\) margin.  Choose any feasible capped coupling, extend it to a triple coupling by \(\text{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}\), and use the cap-fibre construction in \(\text{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}\) to complete the switched and unused branches.  The resulting raw paths are nonnegative-integer valued, death is absorbing, and the construction enforces both capped residual transportability and \(B+W\le3\).  Independently of the response tree and assignment, observe every subject through \(k\), continue observation through \(K\) with probability
\[
 \varepsilon_n=n^{-2}>0,                                \tag{15}
\]
and otherwise censor at \(k\).  Thus baseline assignment, landmark survival, and every factual at-risk censoring continuation probability are positive.  Independence in (15) gives the arm-specific censoring-exchangeability equality.  Hence both triangular sequences lie in \(\mathfrak M_H\).

Couple one observation from the two sequences using the same assignment, censoring draw, pre-landmark history, and standard-arm history.  The observations can differ only if \(R=1\) and follow-up continues through \(K\), an event of probability \(\varepsilon_n/2\).  Coordinatewise coupling of \(n\) observations and the union bound therefore give
\[
 \left\|P_{0,n}^{\otimes n}-P_{1,n}^{\otimes n}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \frac{n\varepsilon_n}{2}
 =\frac1{2n}\longrightarrow0.                           \tag{16}
\]

Let \(\mathcal C_n\) be any observed-data outer confidence region satisfying
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathsf P\in\mathfrak M_H}
 \mathsf P^{\otimes n}\{\Delta^\star(\mathsf P)\subseteq\mathcal C_n\}
 \ge1-\alpha.                                           \tag{17}
\]
Applied to the two triangular sequences, (17) gives coverage of \(e_3\) under law zero and of \(\delta^\star\) under law one, each with probability at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\).  Total variation in (16) transfers the latter event to law zero at a cost \(o(1)\).  A union bound under law zero yields
\[
 P_{0,n}^{\otimes n}\{e_3,\delta^\star\in\mathcal C_n\}
 \ge1-2\alpha-o(1).                                     \tag{18}
\]
Taking the lower limit proves the final assertion.  The two laws have strict Hall slack and unique, separated randomized optimizers; the obstruction is solely the vanishing post-landmark observation probability permitted by pointwise censoring positivity.
