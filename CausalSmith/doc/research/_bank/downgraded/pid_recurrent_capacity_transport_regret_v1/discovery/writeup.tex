\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}recurrent\_\allowbreak{}capacity\_\allowbreak{}transport\_\allowbreak{}regret}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A randomized recurrent-event trial identifies separately capped baseline and residual margins while leaving their dependence unknown, and the raw recurrent paths remain unbounded. The externally prespecified sensitivity restriction \(B+W\le H\) creates a Ferrers transport problem and is not implied by separate capping. The cap-fibre response-tree construction proves every feasible triple coupling attainable, including in the published raw no-carry-over subclass. Exact endpoints and the common-law value polytope yield deterministic and randomized minimax regret and the cap-three recommendation reversal. For inference, a direct sequential-factorization proof now identifies every supported sustained-history atom and hence \((r_{bt},q_w)\); on the explicit quantitative-overlap and cross-fitted product-rate submodel, a longitudinal AIPW Bonferroni rectangle contracts at root-\(n\) rate and its perspective inversion covers the whole value set and every deterministic or randomized robust optimizer, including ties. On the broader pointwise-overlap class \(\mathfrak M_H\), uniformly shrinking inference remains impossible by the vanishing-information two-law construction. Thus the positive quantitative theorem and the negative base-class theorem have disjoint, explicit scopes.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The closest recurrent-event switching analysis supplies an uncapped no-carry-over equality, scalar sharp bounds, and deterministic decision criteria. BaerStrawdermanErtefaie2023 supplies the factual-arm sequential-CAR setup and influence-function architecture for recurrent-event mean and survival targets, but not an atom-by-atom identification theorem for the complete sustained history, the capped margin vector used here, shared-law deterministic and randomized regret inversion, or the positive-versus-negative uniform-inference boundary. Dahl2008 supplies staircase feasibility only.

\paragraph{Niche.} An externally prespecified joint-capacity sensitivity restriction on two separately capped summaries creates nested antidiagonal support without globally bounding the underlying recurrent-event paths. The attributed staircase feasibility result in Dahl2008, Theorem 4, then becomes an input to a causal sharpness and joint-decision problem rather than the source of novelty.

\paragraph{Fill.} The formal kernel combines the attributed staircase feasibility certificate with an explicit raw-path cap-fibre lift, derives the complete common-law value and regret geometry, and proves the missing inference chain. Sequential CAR and positivity give a finite product of observed transition kernels for every supported absorbing history atom. Explicit backward regressions, cumulative censoring hazards, longitudinal AIPW and ratio influence functions, cross-fitting, Hall-feasible projection, and conservative simultaneous inversion give root-\(n\) value-set and regret-excess contraction on a quantitative regularity submodel. A separate two-law theorem proves that pointwise positivity in the unchanged base class cannot yield uniform shrinkage.

\section{Related work}

JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025 supplies the uncapped residual no-carry-over equality and threshold-wise scalar analysis. The cap-fibre construction here transfers sharpness after intersecting that class with \(B+W\le H\). Dahl2008 supplies the reindexed staircase feasibility criterion, and Hoffman1952 supplies only the fixed-matrix polyhedral error bound used for the new perspective-Lipschitz argument. BaerStrawdermanErtefaie2023 is used for the factual-arm sequential-CAR formulation and motivates longitudinal influence-function estimation; it does not assert identification of the entire sustained-history law. That law is derived here atom by atom from randomization, consistency, sequential CAR, and positivity, then specialized to \(r_{bt}\) and the numerator--denominator representation of \(q_w\). ChernozhukovHongTamer2007, AndrewsSoares2010, and Gafarov2025 motivate inference with binding inequalities and changing faces. The present positive result is a finite-dimensional conservative cross-fitted inversion under quantitative overlap, while the headline two-point theorem shows why the same conclusion is impossible uniformly under pointwise overlap. MontielOleaQiuStoye2026 motivates the decision relevance of the shared payoff set and randomization.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:baseline-randomization]\label{ass:baseline-randomization}
\(R\perp U\) under \(\mathsf P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Baseline treatment randomization, \cite{JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(O\) equals the \(R\)-indexed path in \(U\) truncated at \(C\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Counterfactual consistency for recurrent-event histories; counterfactual recurrent-event setup in Sections 2--3, \cite{JanvinYoungRyalenStensrud2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:censoring-exchangeability]\label{ass:censoring-exchangeability}
For every \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), on \(\{R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0\}\), \(\mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,\mathcal H_K^a)=\mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Factual-arm sequential coarsening at random on the at-risk history; Proposition 1 and Corollary 1, \cite{BaerStrawdermanErtefaie2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:assignment-positivity]\label{ass:assignment-positivity}
\(\mathsf P(R=a)>0\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Baseline treatment positivity, \cite{JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:censoring-positivity]\label{ass:censoring-positivity}
For every \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), \(\mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a)>0\) almost surely on \(\{R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Factual-arm sequential censoring positivity through the analysis horizon; Corollary 1, \cite{BaerStrawdermanErtefaie2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:landmark-survival-positivity]\label{ass:landmark-survival-positivity}
\(\mathsf P(S_k^a=1)>0\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Landmark survival positivity, \cite{JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-anticipation]\label{ass:no-anticipation}
\((Y_{\ell}^{1\to0},D_{\ell}^{1\to0})=(Y_{\ell}^{1},D_{\ell}^{1})\) for every \(\ell\le k\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Single-world no-anticipation at a fixed intervention time, \cite{RichardsonRobins2013}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:capped-residual-transportability]\label{ass:capped-residual-transportability}
\(\mathsf P(W=w\mid S_k^1=1)=q_w\) for every \(w\in\mathbb I_H\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Capped-increment specialization of the no-carry-over condition in Assumption 1, \cite{JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:capped-capacity]\label{ass:capped-capacity}
\(\mathsf P(B+W\le H\mid S_k^1=1)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit sensitivity restriction, not a consequence of separately capping \(B\) and \(W\). It is satisfied by recurrent processes in which switching activates a nonrenewable reserve of \(H-B\) post-landmark event opportunities, for example a finite collection of vulnerable sites whose failures before \(k\) cannot recur and whose remaining sites are stabilized after switching. A clinical analysis must justify that mechanism and choose \(H\) externally; otherwise the theorem is reported only as a shape-restriction sensitivity analysis.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:quantitative-inference-regularity]\label{ass:quantitative-inference-regularity}
The quantitative-inference analysis is restricted to a triangular law--estimator sequence satisfying \(\operatorname{QIR}_{H,K,\varepsilon}(\mathsf P,\mathfrak I)\) for some fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (quantitative longitudinal AIPW overlap and cross-fitting regularity (BaerStrawde, \cite{BaerStrawdermanErtefaie2023}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-threshold-endpoints]\label{thm:sharp-threshold-endpoints}
Assume \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\ne\varnothing\). For every \(m\in\mathcal M\), the attainable selected-column subdistributions \(z_w\) are exactly those satisfying \(z_w=0\) for \(w>L_m\), \(0\le z_w\le q_w\), \(\sum_{w=0}^{L_m}z_w=a_m\), and \(\sum_{w=0}^{s}z_w\ge A_s\) for \(0\le s\le L_m\). Their pointwise maximal and minimal cumulative arrays are respectively \(Z_{ms}^{-}\) and \(Z_{ms}^{+}\) from \(\text{def:threshold-cumulative-envelopes}\). In particular, \(Z_{m,L_m}^{-}=Z_{m,L_m}^{+}=a_m\) and \(Z_{ms}^{+}=\max\{A_s,Z_{m,s+1}^{+}-q_{s+1}\}\) for \(s<L_m\). Consequently, \(\inf_{x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)}\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x_{bw}=L_ma_m-\sum_{s=0}^{L_m-1}Z_{ms}^{-}\) and \(\sup_{x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)}\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x_{bw}=L_ma_m-\sum_{s=0}^{L_m-1}Z_{ms}^{+}\), with both endpoints attained. The sharp endpoints for \(J_m\) add \(\pi\sum_{b<m}\mu_b\) and multiply the displayed selected contribution by \(\pi\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(L=L_m\) and \(a=a_m\).  We first prove the finite Ferrers transport fact used throughout.  Let \((\rho_b)_{b=0}^H\) and \((\sigma_w)_{w=0}^H\) be nonnegative arrays with the same total mass.  A nonnegative array with these margins and support \(b+w\le H\) can exist only if
\[
 \sum_{b=h}^H\rho_b\le \sum_{w=0}^{H-h}\sigma_w,
 \qquad h=0,\ldots,H.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, every row in the left-hand tail has its neighborhood inside the column prefix on the right.  Conversely, process rows \(b=H,H-1,\ldots,0\), filling row \(b\) from the remaining mass in columns \(0,\ldots,H-b\).  Immediately before row \(b\) is filled, the previously processed rows have consumed exactly \(\sum_{r>b}\rho_r\) units from that prefix.  Its remaining mass is therefore
\[
 \sum_{w=0}^{H-b}\sigma_w-\sum_{r>b}\rho_r\ge \rho_b       \tag{2}
\]
by (1), so row \(b\) can be filled, splitting a column if necessary.  After the last row, equality of total masses forces every column residual to be zero.  Thus (1) is also sufficient, constructively, without strict Hall slack or positive cell masses.  This is the nested-neighborhood specialization of the Hall transport criterion, derived here by the descending-row algorithm.

Apply necessity to any member of \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\).  With \(h=H-s\), it gives
\[
 A_s=\sum_{b=H-s}^Hp_b\le Q_s=\sum_{w=0}^sq_w,
 \qquad s=0,\ldots,H.                                      \tag{3}
\]
Fix \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\), define \(z_w=\sum_{b\ge m}x_{bw}\), and write \(Z_s=\sum_{w=0}^sz_w\).  If \(b\ge m\) and \(x_{bw}>0\), then \(w\le H-b\le H-m=L\).  Hence \(z_w=0\) for \(w>L\).  Nonnegativity and the column equations give \(0\le z_w\le q_w\), while the selected row equations give
\[
 Z_L=\sum_{b=m}^H\sum_wx_{bw}=\sum_{b=m}^Hp_b=a.            \tag{4}
\]
For \(0\le s\le L\), every row \(b\ge H-s\) is selected and can use only columns \(w\le s\), whence
\[
 Z_s\ge \sum_{b=H-s}^Hp_b=A_s.                             \tag{5}
\]

These conditions are sufficient.  Suppose \(z\) satisfies them.  Give rows \(b\ge m\) masses \(p_b\), rows \(b<m\) mass zero, and columns masses \(z_w\).  For \(h\ge m\), condition (1) is
\[
 \sum_{b=h}^Hp_b=A_{H-h}\le Z_{H-h};                       \tag{6}
\]
for \(h<m\), its left side is \(a\) and its right side is \(Z_L=a\).  The constructive sufficiency argument therefore produces a selected-row transport.  For the complementary rows, use row masses \(p_b\mathbf 1\{b<m\}\) and column masses \(q_w-z_w\).  The Hall inequalities are automatic for \(h\ge m\).  If \(h<m\), then \(H-h\ge L+1\), and the support and total-mass conditions on \(z\), followed by (3), give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{b=h}^{m-1}p_b
 &=\sum_{b=h}^Hp_b-a\\
 &\le Q_{H-h}-a
 =\sum_{w=0}^{H-h}(q_w-z_w).
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{7}
\]
Thus the same algorithm produces the complementary transport.  Adding the two arrays gives an element of \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\) inducing exactly \(z\).  This proves the asserted characterization.

Every feasible cumulative array obeys \(Z_s\le a\) and \(Z_s\le Q_s\), hence
\[
 Z_s\le \min\{a,Q_s\}=Z_{ms}^{-}.                        \tag{8}
\]
The array \(Z_{ms}^{-}\) is feasible: (3) and \(A_s\le a\) give \(A_s\le Z_{ms}^{-}\); the increments of \(\min\{a,Q_s\}\) are nonnegative and no larger than the increments \(q_s\) of \(Q_s\); and (3) at \(s=L\) gives \(Q_L\ge A_L=a\), so \(Z_{mL}^{-}=a\).  The preceding characterization therefore makes \(Z_m^{-}\) attainable, and (8) makes it the pointwise maximal attainable cumulative array.  This argument also covers \(a=0\).

For the opposite extremum, feasibility implies, whenever \(s\le u\le L\),
\[
 Z_u-Z_s=\sum_{w=s+1}^uz_w\le Q_u-Q_s
 \quad\text{and}\quad Z_u\ge A_u.
\]
Consequently
\[
 Z_s\ge Q_s+A_u-Q_u,
 \qquad
 Z_s\ge Q_s+\max_{u=s,\ldots,L}(A_u-Q_u)=Z_{ms}^{+}.       \tag{9}
\]
Because \(A_L=a\), the terminal value is
\[
 Z_{mL}^{+}=Q_L+A_L-Q_L=a.                                \tag{10}
\]
Separating the term \(u=s\) from the maximum and using \(Q_{s+1}=Q_s+q_{s+1}\) yields the backward-deficit recursion
\[
 Z_{ms}^{+}=\max\{A_s,Z_{m,s+1}^{+}-q_{s+1}\},
 \qquad s<L.                                               \tag{11}
\]
Now \(A_s\le A_{s+1}\le Z_{m,s+1}^{+}\), so (11) gives \(Z_{ms}^{+}\le Z_{m,s+1}^{+}\) and \(Z_{m,s+1}^{+}-Z_{ms}^{+}\le q_{s+1}\).  At \(s=0\), the term \(u=0\) in the maximum gives \(Z_{m0}^{+}\ge A_0\ge0\), whereas (3) gives \(A_u-Q_u\le0\) for every \(u\), and hence \(Z_{m0}^{+}\le Q_0=q_0\).  Thus every increment of \(Z_m^{+}\) lies in \([0,q_w]\); (10) gives total mass \(a\); and (11) gives \(Z_{ms}^{+}\ge A_s\).  The characterization already proved makes this array attainable.  Inequality (9) makes it the pointwise minimal attainable cumulative array.

For any subdistribution supported on \(0,\ldots,L\) with total mass \(a\), finite summation by parts gives
\[
 \sum_{w=0}^{L}wz_w
 =\sum_{s=0}^{L-1}\sum_{w=s+1}^{L}z_w
 =La-\sum_{s=0}^{L-1}Z_s.                                 \tag{12}
\]
The maximal cumulative array \(Z_m^{-}\) therefore minimizes (12), and the minimal cumulative array \(Z_m^{+}\) maximizes it.  Both extrema are attained by the transports constructed above.

Finally, the fixed \((B,T)\) margin and \(\mu_b=\sum_ttp_{bt}\) give, for every compatible plan,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_m
 &=\pi\,\mathsf E[T\mathbf 1\{B<m\}+W\mathbf 1\{B\ge m\}\mid S_k^1=1]\\
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m}\mu_b+\sum_{b\ge m,w}wx_{bw}\right).               \tag{13}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every attaining \(x\) extends to a triple coupling by \(\text{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}\), including zero rows because no division is then performed, and \(\text{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}\) realizes that triple by a raw-path law with the same observed pushforward.  Therefore (12)--(13) give the stated sharp causal endpoints, not merely outer algebraic bounds.  All arguments remain valid at Hall equalities.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Dahl2008, Theorem 4, supplies the attributed feasibility support; the new closed forms expose the backward capacity deficit that unrestricted sum bounds omit and permit exact threshold comparisons without repeated linear programs. \textit{Gap.} ZhangRichardson2025 resolves discrete sharpness for unrestricted fixed-marginal sums; the support deletion \(b+w>H\) changes both feasibility and the upper endpoint through the backward deficit. \textit{Consumer.} JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025's fixed-threshold switching loss becomes sharply bounded in an externally prespecified capacity-sensitivity analysis.

\begin{lemma}[lem:triple-projection-and-extension]\label{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}
The projection of \(\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\) onto \((B,W)\) equals \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\). Every \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\) extends to \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\) by \(\gamma_{btw}=p_{bt}x_{bw}/p_b\) when \(p_b>0\), with arbitrary zero-row values when \(p_b=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\) and define \(x_{bw}=\sum_t\gamma_{btw}\).  Nonnegativity is immediate, and the first triple-margin constraint gives
\[
 \sum_wx_{bw}=\sum_{t,w}\gamma_{btw}=\sum_tp_{bt}=p_b.
\]
The second triple-margin constraint gives
\[
 \sum_bx_{bw}=\sum_{b,t}\gamma_{btw}=q_w.
\]
If \(b+w>H\), every summand \(\gamma_{btw}\) is zero, so \(x_{bw}=0\).  Hence the projection belongs to \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\).

Conversely, fix \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\).  If \(p_b=0\), nonnegativity and \(p_b=\sum_tp_{bt}\) imply \(p_{bt}=0\) for every \(t\), while the row constraint for \(x\) implies \(x_{bw}=0\) for every \(w\).  Set \(\gamma_{btw}=0\) on such rows.  If \(p_b>0\), set
\[
 \gamma_{btw}=\frac{p_{bt}x_{bw}}{p_b}.                    \tag{1}
\]
The denominator is strictly positive in precisely the case where (1) is used.  Then
\[
 \sum_w\gamma_{btw}
 =\frac{p_{bt}}{p_b}\sum_wx_{bw}=p_{bt},                  \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{b,t}\gamma_{btw}
 =\sum_{b:p_b>0}\frac{x_{bw}}{p_b}\sum_tp_{bt}
 =\sum_bx_{bw}=q_w.                                       \tag{3}
\]
Finally, \(x_{bw}=0\) when \(b+w>H\), so (1) has the required support.  Equations (1)--(3) prove that \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\) and that its \((B,W)\) projection is exactly \(x\).  The displayed choice happens to make \(T\) and \(W\) conditionally independent given \(B\), but only existence of this extension is used; no such independence is imposed on general members of the triple polytope.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma shows that conditioning the switched residual on the observed intensive residual creates no hidden loss of sharpness. \textit{Gap.} Classical two-margin transport results do not verify compatibility with the identified \((B,T)\) joint law required by the recurrent-event estimand. \textit{Consumer.} The endpoint and policy calculations can optimize over the smaller Ferrers polytope without discarding information about continued intensive treatment.

\begin{theorem}[thm:causal-response-tree-lift]\label{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}
For every distribution of \(O\) that is the observed-data pushforward of at least one law in \(\mathfrak M_H\), every such sustained-arm full-data extension, and every \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\), the explicit algorithm \(\mathfrak L\) produces a finite-horizon FFRCISTG law on raw nonnegative-integer count paths with triple margin \(\gamma\), the same pushforward distribution of \(O\), absorbing death, baseline randomization, and the same arm-specific sequential censoring continuation probabilities conditional on the factual at-risk histories \(\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a\). On each constructed switched survivor branch, \(\min\{Y_K^{1\to0}-Y_k^{1\to0},H\}=W\); the uncapped switched residual may exceed \(H\), and no global restriction bounds raw counts by \(H\). Conversely, every law in \(\mathfrak M_H\) induces a member of \(\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\). The construction imposes neither censoring independence from the entire response tree nor any other cross-world independence beyond baseline randomization and the listed FFRCISTG conditions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(P_O\) be the stipulated observable distribution, and let \(\mathsf P_0\in\mathfrak M_H\) be the admitted sustained-arm full-data extension fixed in the statement. Write \(U_0\) for its response table and \(C_0\) for its censoring time. The finite horizon has finitely many treatment branches and time coordinates, and each raw count coordinate lies in the countable set \(\mathbb Z_+\). Thus the response-type space is countable, and the conditional probability kernels used below exist by discrete conditioning.

First retain jointly and without modification the two sustained raw histories in \(U_0\). On \(\{S_k^1=1,B=b,T=t\}\), define a draw \(\widetilde W\) by
\[
 \mathsf P(\widetilde W=w\mid U_0)
 =\frac{\gamma_{btw}}{p_{bt}}\quad\text{if }p_{bt}>0.     \tag{1}
\]
If \(p_{bt}=0\), the event being conditioned on has probability zero and the conditional law can be arbitrary. The row-margin constraint in \(\text{def:triple-polytope}\) states \(\sum_w\gamma_{btw}=p_{bt}\), so (1) is a probability mass function on every positive cell. It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathsf P(B=b,T=t,\widetilde W=w\mid S_k^1=1)\\
 &\qquad=p_{bt}\frac{\gamma_{btw}}{p_{bt}}=\gamma_{btw}
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{2}
\]
when \(p_{bt}>0\), while both sides vanish when \(p_{bt}=0\). Hence the constructed triple margin is \(\gamma\). Summing (2) over \((b,t)\) and using the column-margin constraint in \(\text{def:triple-polytope}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf P(\widetilde W=w\mid S_k^1=1)
 =\sum_{b,t}\gamma_{btw}=q_w.                            \tag{3}
\]

We next implement the cap-fibre part of \(\mathfrak L\). Under the retained standard sustained history put
\[
 Z=Y_K^0-Y_k^0\quad\text{on }\{S_k^0=1\}.               \tag{4}
\]
The landmark-survival positivity condition makes the conditional law in (4) well defined. By the definition of \(q_w\),
\[
 q_w=\mathsf P_0\{\min(Z,H)=w\mid S_k^0=1\}.             \tag{5}
\]
For \(q_w>0\), let \(\kappa_w\) be the conditional law in (5) further conditioned on \(Z\); for \(q_w=0\), use any law supported on the same cap fibre. Conditional on \(\widetilde W=w\), draw \(\widetilde Z\) from \(\kappa_w\). Applying \(\text{lem:cap-fibre-mixture}\) to the conditional standard-survivor law in (5) and the mass identity (3) yields
\[
 \min\{\widetilde Z,H\}=\widetilde W\quad\text{almost surely}, \tag{6}
\]
and, for every \(z\in\mathbb Z_+\),
\[
 \mathsf P(\widetilde Z=z\mid S_k^1=1)
 =\mathsf P_0(Z=z\mid S_k^0=1).                          \tag{7}
\]
Equation (7) is stronger than required for the corrected target: it records the uncapped residual equality used by the sharp-transfer result below.

Construct the switched raw history as follows. Through time \(k\), copy the sustained intensive history exactly, as required by \(\text{ass:no-anticipation}\). If death has occurred by \(k\), continue the absorbing death state and add no later events. If the unit is alive at \(k\), keep this otherwise unobserved branch alive through \(K\), add exactly \(\widetilde Z\) raw events at time \(k+1\), and keep its count constant thereafter. The inequality \(K\ge k+1\) makes this time available; \(\widetilde Z\in\mathbb Z_+\) makes the count nondecreasing; and the raw-path model permits multiple events in one interval. The branch is therefore legal and its raw post-landmark increment is \(\widetilde Z\). By (6), its separately capped residual is
\[
 W=\min\{Y_K^{1\to0}-Y_k^{1\to0},H\}
   =\min\{\widetilde Z,H\}=\widetilde W.                \tag{8}
\]
The support constraint \(\gamma_{btw}=0\) when \(b+w>H\), together with (2) and (8), gives
\[
 \mathsf P(B+W\le H\mid S_k^1=1)=1,                     \tag{9}
\]
which is exactly \(\text{ass:capped-capacity}\). When \(W=H\), the draw \(\widetilde Z\) may exceed \(H\); likewise the retained raw count at \(k\) may exceed \(H\). Thus (8)--(9) impose no global raw-count cap.

It remains to complete a finite response tree. Index a node at time \(\ell\) by its treatment prefix through \(\ell\). Place the retained sustained histories and the switched history just constructed on their prescribed prefix nodes. These prescriptions agree on every shared prefix: the switched and intensive paths agree through \(k\) by construction, and all retained paths came from the valid table \(U_0\). Traverse every remaining prefix node in increasing time order. At each unset child, copy its parent's count and death state; append an alive, zero-event state when the parent is alive and the absorbing state when it is dead. Every node has a unique parent, so the recursion is well defined. Values at time \(\ell\) depend only on the treatment prefix through \(\ell\); hence the resulting table \(U'\) is an FFRCISTG response tree. Each branch has nondecreasing integer counts and absorbing death.

To check randomization and censoring without silently imposing an NPSEM product structure, sample \((U_0,R,C_0)\) under its original law, sample the two auxiliary variables above from kernels depending on \(U_0\) but not on \(R\), and set \(C=C_0\). If \(E\) is any event in the completed response-table space, baseline randomization for \(\mathsf P_0\) gives, for \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathsf P(U'\in E,R=a)
 &=\int \mathsf P(U'\in E\mid U_0=u)\,
       \mathsf P_0(R=a,U_0\in du)\\
 &=\mathsf P_0(R=a)\int \mathsf P(U'\in E\mid U_0=u)\,
       \mathsf P_0(U_0\in du)\\
 &=\mathsf P_0(R=a)\mathsf P(U'\in E).
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(R\perp U'\), as required by \(\text{ass:baseline-randomization}\), and the positive assignment probabilities in \(\text{ass:assignment-positivity}\) are unchanged.

For \(R=a\), the factual sustained branch of \(U'\) is exactly the corresponding branch of \(U_0\), and \(C=C_0\). Consequently
\[
 (R,C,\mathcal H_K^R)\ \text{has the same joint law under }
 \mathsf P\text{ and }\mathsf P_0.                       \tag{11}
\]
By \(\text{ass:consistency}\), truncating that factual path at \(C\) produces \(O\); hence (11) proves equality of the complete observed pushforward, not merely equality of a terminal summary. For every \(a\) and \(\ell\), on the positive-probability factual at-risk event specified in the assumptions, (11) and \(\text{ass:censoring-exchangeability}\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,
                         \mathcal H_K^a)\\
 &=\mathsf P_0(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,
                           \mathcal H_K^a)\\
 &=\mathsf P_0(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,
                           \mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a)\\
 &=\mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,
                         \mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a).
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{12}
\]
The common continuation probability in (12) is strictly positive by \(\text{ass:censoring-positivity}\). The sustained survival probabilities are also unchanged and remain positive by \(\text{ass:landmark-survival-positivity}\). Equations (3), (8), and (9) verify \(\text{ass:capped-residual-transportability}\) and \(\text{ass:capped-capacity}\); the prefix construction verifies \(\text{ass:no-anticipation}\), nondecreasing counts, and absorbing death. Thus every condition in \(\text{def:model-class}\) holds.

No independence between \(C\) and the full response table was used: retaining \(C_0\) preserves whatever dependence on the original unassigned branches was present, subject only to (12). Likewise, the auxiliary conditional kernels construct one witness and are not asserted as cross-world restrictions on the class.

Conversely, let \(\mathsf P\in\mathfrak M_H\). The positivity condition \(\mathsf P(S_k^1=1)>0\) permits the definition
\[
 \gamma_{btw}=\mathsf P(B=b,T=t,W=w\mid S_k^1=1).         \tag{13}
\]
Its \((B,T)\) margin is \(p_{bt}\) by definition. Its \(W\) margin is \(q_w\) by \(\text{ass:capped-residual-transportability}\). Finally, \(\text{ass:capped-capacity}\) makes (13) vanish whenever \(b+w>H\). These are exactly the nonnegativity, margin, and support constraints in \(\text{def:triple-polytope}\), so \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\). This proves the converse and completes the corrected target.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Polyhedral sharpness is causal only if every extremal summary coupling can be realized by a global finite-horizon response tree without silently invoking an NPSEM cross-world product structure. \textit{Gap.} GreseleEtAl2022 studies causal-marginal gluing and RichardsonRobins2013 distinguishes FFRCISTG from NPSEM models; neither supplies an observed-law-preserving recurrent-event lift with death and censoring. \textit{Consumer.} Every endpoint and deterministic or randomized regret optimizer over the transport polytope is certified as attainable by an observed-law-preserving causal model, including within the published uncapped residual no-carry-over subclass intersected with the capacity restriction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:joint-value-set-and-support]\label{thm:joint-value-set-and-support}
The sharp identified set for the entire threshold-loss vector is exactly \(\mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\), and for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal M|}\) its support function equals \(h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)\) in \(\text{def:support-function}\). The same common-plan representation gives the signed pairwise derivation \(D_{mj}=\pi[\max_{x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)}\sum_{b=m}^{j-1}\sum_w w x_{bw}-\sum_{b=m}^{j-1}\mu_b]\) for \(m<j\), \(D_{mj}=\pi[\sum_{b=j}^{m-1}\mu_b-\min_{x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)}\sum_{b=j}^{m-1}\sum_w w x_{bw}]\) for \(m>j\), and \(D_{mm}=0\). Hence an off-diagonal \(D_{mj}\) may be negative, \(R_m=\max_{j\in\mathcal M}D_{mj}\ge0\), and \(\mathcal M^\star=\operatorname*{argmin}_{m\in\mathcal M}R_m\) is the complete deterministic minimax correspondence. In particular, optimizing coordinates separately generally produces a strict outer rectangle rather than the joint identified set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an observable law \(P_O\) admitted by the maintained model, and let \(p_{bt}\), \(q_w\), and \(\pi>0\) be its identified margins.  Distinguish the causal identified set from its proposed observed-law representation by defining
\[
 \Theta_I(P_O)=\left\{(J_m(\widetilde{\mathsf P}))_{m\in\mathcal M}:
 \widetilde{\mathsf P}\in\mathfrak M_H,
 \ \widetilde{\mathsf P}_O=P_O\right\}.                  \tag{1}
\]
For any compatible law, the converse in \(\text{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}\) supplies \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\).  Its projection \(x_{bw}=\sum_t\gamma_{btw}\) belongs to \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\) by \(\text{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}\).  Since the \((B,T)\) margin is fixed,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_m
 &=\pi\,\mathsf E[T\mathbf 1\{B<m\}+W\mathbf 1\{B\ge m\}\mid S_k^1=1]\\
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m}\sum_ttp_{bt}+\sum_{b\ge m,w}wx_{bw}\right)\\
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m}\mu_b+\sum_{b\ge m,w}wx_{bw}\right).              \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\Theta_I(P_O)\subseteq\mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\).  Conversely, for every \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\), the extension part of \(\text{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}\) produces \(\gamma\in\mathcal G_H(p_{BT},q)\), and the forward part of \(\text{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}\) realizes that triple under a law with observed pushforward \(P_O\).  Equation (2) gives the associated point of \(\mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\).  Hence
\[
 \Theta_I(P_O)=\mathcal V(p_{BT},q).                       \tag{3}
\]
This proves joint sharpness.  Since \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope and (2) is affine in \(x\), the identified set is compact and convex.

For any \(\lambda\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal M|}\), finite-sum rearrangement gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lambda^\top J(x)
 &=\pi\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\lambda_m\sum_{b<m}\mu_b
   +\pi\sum_{b,w}wx_{bw}\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\lambda_m\mathbf 1\{b\ge m\}\\
 &=\pi\left[\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}\lambda_m\sum_{b<m}\mu_b
   +\sum_{b,w}c_b(\lambda)wx_{bw}\right].                 \tag{4}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\), which is attained by compactness, is exactly \(h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)\) in \(\text{def:support-function}\).

Write \(v_b(x)=\sum_wwx_{bw}\).  If \(m<j\), cancellation outside rows \(m,\ldots,j-1\) in (2) gives
\[
 J_m(x)-J_j(x)=\pi\sum_{b=m}^{j-1}\{v_b(x)-\mu_b\}.       \tag{5}
\]
Maximizing (5) yields
\[
 D_{mj}=\pi\left[\max_{x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)}
 \sum_{b=m}^{j-1}\sum_wwx_{bw}-\sum_{b=m}^{j-1}\mu_b\right].              \tag{6}
\]
If \(m>j\), the same cancellation gives
\[
 J_m(x)-J_j(x)=\pi\sum_{b=j}^{m-1}\{\mu_b-v_b(x)\},       \tag{7}
\]
and maximizing (7) gives the stated formula with the transport minimum; \(D_{mm}=0\).  Off-diagonal signs are unrestricted.  For a concrete negative value, take \(H=2\), \(p_1=1\), \(q_0=1\), \(p_{1,1}=1\), \(\pi=1\), and \(\mathcal M=\{1,2\}\).  The unique capacity-feasible residual is \(W=0\), so \(J_1=0\), \(J_2=1\), and \(D_{12}=-1\).

For a fixed deterministic threshold \(m\), its shared-law worst-case regret is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{v\in\mathcal V}\left\{v_m-\min_{j\in\mathcal M}v_j\right\}
 &=\sup_{v\in\mathcal V}\max_{j\in\mathcal M}(v_m-v_j)\\
 &=\max_{j\in\mathcal M}\sup_{v\in\mathcal V}(v_m-v_j)
 =\max_{j\in\mathcal M}D_{mj}=R_m.                       \tag{8}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The interchange is exact because \(\mathcal M\) is finite: both sides are the supremum over \(\mathcal V\times\mathcal M\).  The comparator \(j=m\) gives \(R_m\ge0\).  Minimization over the nonempty finite menu therefore yields precisely \(\mathcal M^\star=\operatorname*{argmin}_{m\in\mathcal M}R_m\), retaining every tie.

Coordinate projection always gives
\[
 \mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\subseteq
 \prod_{m\in\mathcal M}[\underline J_m,\overline J_m].    \tag{9}
\]
It can be strict.  Take \(H=3\), \(p=(2/5,3/10,1/5,1/10)\), \(q=(3/10,3/10,1/5,1/5)\), \(\pi=1\), \(\mu=(0,7/20,7/40,0)\), and \(\mathcal M=\{1,2,3\}\).  The plan with nonzero cells
\[
 x_{00}=1/5,\quad x_{03}=1/5,\quad x_{11}=1/10,\quad
 x_{12}=1/5,\quad x_{21}=1/5,\quad x_{30}=1/10             \tag{10}
\]
is feasible and gives \(J_1=7/10\).  This is the upper endpoint: the total column first moment is \(13/10\), while row zero has mass \(2/5\) and must absorb the column-three mass \(1/5\), so its first moment is at least \(3/5\), leaving at most \(7/10\) for selected rows.  The plan with nonzero cells
\[
 x_{02}=1/5,\quad x_{03}=1/5,\quad x_{11}=3/10,\quad
 x_{20}=1/5,\quad x_{30}=1/10                              \tag{11}
\]
is feasible and gives \(J_2=7/20\), the lower endpoint because its residual contribution is nonnegative.  If one plan generated both coordinate extrema, (2) would imply \(v_2=0\) and \(v_1+v_2=7/10\), hence \(v_1=7/10\).  But row \(b=1\) has mass \(3/10\) and capacity ceiling \(w=2\), so \(v_1\le3/5<7/10\), a contradiction.  Thus (9) is in general a strict outer rectangle rather than the joint identified set.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} All candidate thresholds are functions of one latent switched law, so the theorem identifies their joint geometry and derives signed pairwise regret and every deterministic minimax threshold from that same law. \textit{Gap.} JanvinRyalenSarvetStensrud2025 bounds one threshold and MontielOleaQiuStoye2026 analyzes joint payoff restrictions in a Gaussian decision experiment; neither identifies the common-coupling threshold-value polytope. \textit{Consumer.} A clinician choosing among several prespecified switching thresholds obtains the exact feasible loss vectors and every robust deterministic threshold, including ties, rather than combining incompatible marginal extrema.

\begin{theorem}[thm:eligibility-greedy-support]\label{thm:eligibility-greedy-support}
For every direction \(\lambda\), process columns \(w=H,H-1,\ldots,0\); when column \(w\) is reached, make rows \(b\le H-w\) eligible and allocate its remaining mass to eligible rows in decreasing \(c_b(\lambda)\), splitting only at exhausted row capacities. Whenever \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\ne\varnothing\), whose fixed-margin staircase feasibility is supported by Dahl2008, Theorem 4 after reversing the row order, the algorithm is feasible with no sign or monotonicity restriction on the induced real coefficients \(c_b(\lambda)\); it attains \(h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)\), uses at most \(2H+1\) positive cells, and runs in \(O(H\log H)\) time with a heap.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Only the transport term in \(h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)\) must be optimized, so abbreviate \(c_b=c_b(\lambda)\).  Feasibility of \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\) implies the complementary Hall inequalities
\[
 \sum_{v=w}^{H}q_v\le\sum_{b=0}^{H-w}p_b,
 \qquad w=0,\ldots,H,                                    \tag{1}
\]
because columns \(v\ge w\) can receive mass only from rows \(b\le H-v\le H-w\).  When the algorithm reaches column \(w\), previously processed columns contain total mass \(\sum_{v>w}q_v\) and have consumed that amount only from currently eligible rows.  By (1), the residual capacity among those rows is at least
\[
 \sum_{b=0}^{H-w}p_b-\sum_{v>w}q_v\ge q_w.                \tag{2}
\]
Thus column \(w\) can always be filled.  After the final column, equality of total row and column mass forces all row capacities to be exhausted.  This proves feasibility, including zero margins and equality in (1).  It is also a self-contained proof of the staircase-feasibility fact attributed in the statement to Dahl's reversed-row formulation; no external theorem is used as a logical premise here.

We prove optimality by a finite exchange argument.  Consider the largest not-yet-fixed column \(w\), and let \(b\) be an eligible row with maximal coefficient among rows having residual capacity.  Start with an optimal residual plan.  If it does not allocate to \((b,w)\) as much as the greedy step does, then some positive mass in column \(w\) is assigned to another eligible row \(b'\), while an equal positive amount of the residual mass of row \(b\) is assigned to some column \(v<w\).  For
\[
 0<\delta\le\min\{x_{b'w},x_{bv}\},
\]
replace \(\delta\) units in cells \((b',w),(b,v)\) by \(\delta\) units in cells \((b,w),(b',v)\).  Both new cells are allowed: \(b\le H-w\), and \(b'\le H-w\le H-v\).  Margins are unchanged.  The objective change is
\[
 \delta\{c_bw+c_{b'}v-c_{b'}w-c_bv\}
 =\delta(c_b-c_{b'})(w-v)\ge0.                            \tag{3}
\]
Hence an optimal plan can be modified to agree with this greedy allocation.  Choose \(\delta\) to exhaust one of the two discrepant cells and repeat; finiteness of the table makes the process terminate.  Repeating for the next-highest coefficient row until column \(w\) is full, and then inducting on \(w=H,H-1,\ldots,0\), produces an optimal plan identical to the greedy plan.  The sign and ordering of the real numbers \(c_b\) never entered except through the comparison \(c_b\ge c_{b'}\), so signed and nonmonotone directions are covered.

Each positive allocation exhausts a residual row capacity or the current column, and the last allocation exhausts both.  There are \(H+1\) rows and \(H+1\) columns, so at most
\[
 (H+1)+(H+1)-1=2H+1                                      \tag{4}
\]
positive cells are created.  As \(w\) decreases, exactly one new row becomes eligible.  Insert that row once into a max-heap keyed by \(c_b\), remove a row once when its capacity is exhausted, and retain a partially used top row when a column is exhausted.  Thus there are \(O(H)\) heap operations, each costing \(O(\log H)\), and \(O(H)\) allocations.  The coefficients \(c_b=\sum_m\lambda_m\mathbf 1\{b\ge m\}\) are computed by one prefix scan in \(O(H)\) time because \(|\mathcal M|\le H\).  The total time is \(O(H\log H)\).  Substitution of the resulting optimal plan into \(\text{def:support-function}\) attains \(h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:confidence-polytope]\label{thm:confidence-polytope}
If \(\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\) covers \((r_{bt},q_w)\) simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), then with the same probability \(\mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,\alpha}\) and \(\mathcal M^\star\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\). Both inclusions hold without uniqueness, strict Hall slack, or separation between policy regrets, and the sets are computable by finite-dimensional optimization over explicitly given polytopes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 \mathcal E_{n,\alpha}
 =\{((r_{bt}),(q_w))\in\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\}.             \tag{1}
\]
By the premise, \(\mathsf P(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})\ge 1-\alpha\).  Work on this event.  Because the observable law is admitted by \(\mathfrak M_H\), the converse in \(\text{thm:causal-response-tree-lift}\) and \(\text{lem:triple-projection-and-extension}\) imply \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\ne\varnothing\).  Fix \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\) and define
\[
 r^\circ_{bt}=\pi p_{bt},\qquad
 s^\circ_w=\pi q_w,\qquad
 x^\circ_{bw}=\pi x_{bw}.                                \tag{2}
\]
Landmark-survival positivity gives \(\pi>0\), while probability normalization gives
\[
 \sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}=\pi\le 1.                         \tag{3}
\]
Membership in the event (1) gives the cellwise constraints on \(r^\circ\), and, because \(s^\circ_w=q_w\sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}\),
\[
 \underline q_w\sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}
 \le s^\circ_w
 \le \overline q_w\sum_{b,t}r^\circ_{bt}                 \tag{4}
\]
for every \(w\in\mathbb I_H\).  The row equations, column equations, and support restriction in \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\) give
\[
 \sum_wx^\circ_{bw}=\pi p_b=\sum_t r^\circ_{bt},\qquad
 \sum_bx^\circ_{bw}=\pi q_w=s^\circ_w,\qquad
 x^\circ_{bw}=0\quad\text{if }b+w>H.                    \tag{5}
\]
Together with nonnegativity, (2)--(5) prove
\[
 (\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ,\mathbf x^\circ)
 \in\widehat{\mathcal P}_{n,\alpha}.                    \tag{6}
\]
This argument holds for every \(x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\).  The corresponding image coordinate is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{b<m,t}t r^\circ_{bt}+\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x^\circ_{bw}
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m,t}t p_{bt}+\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x_{bw}\right)\\
 &=\pi\left(\sum_{b<m}\mu_b+\sum_{b\ge m,w}w x_{bw}\right).
 \end{aligned}                                            \tag{7}
\]
By \(\text{thm:joint-value-set-and-support}\), as \(x\) ranges over \(\Gamma_H(p,q)\), the vector in (7) ranges over the entire sharp causal identified set.  Equations (6)--(7) therefore yield
\[
 \mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,\alpha}
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}.                  \tag{8}
\]

For deterministic optimizer coverage, positivity of \(\pi\) makes the scaling map bijective:
\[
 \mathsf X_H(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)
 =\{\pi x:x\in\Gamma_H(p,q)\}.                          \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, one inclusion follows from the equations in (5), and the reverse inclusion follows by dividing every candidate plan by \(\pi>0\).  Substitution into \(\mathsf J_m\) and then into its transport optimum gives, simultaneously for every \(m,j\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 \mathsf D_{mj}(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)=D_{mj},
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_m(\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ)=R_m.       \tag{10}
\]
The true margin pair belongs to the projection of the confidence polytope by (6).  Hence
\[
 \underline R_m^{n,\alpha}\le R_m\le\overline R_m^{n,\alpha}
 \quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M.                  \tag{11}
\]
If \(m\in\mathcal M^\star\), then \(R_m=\min_jR_j\), and (11) gives
\[
 \underline R_m^{n,\alpha}
 \le R_m
 =\min_{j\in\mathcal M}R_j
 \le\min_{j\in\mathcal M}\overline R_j^{n,\alpha}.     \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(m\in\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\), proving
\[
 \mathcal M^\star\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}.                  \tag{13}
\]
No step makes a Hall inequality or regret comparison strict, so (8) and (13) remain valid on Hall faces and retain every tied deterministic minimizer.

On the event (1), (6) shows that the confidence polytope is nonempty.  It is closed and bounded: all its coordinates are nonnegative, its total row mass is at most one, and its row and column equations bound every \(s_w\) and \(x_{bw}\) by that total mass.  Hence it and its coordinate projections are compact polytopes, including candidate points of total mass zero.  The value confidence set is a linear image of this polytope.  For fixed candidate margins and each \((m,j)\), \(\mathsf D_{mj}\) is the optimum of the explicitly constrained finite transport program over \(\mathsf X_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)\); the regret takes a maximum over the finite menu, and its envelopes optimize this finite-dimensional value over the displayed compact projection.  Thus all reported sets and envelopes are computable finite-dimensional optimization objects.  Taking probabilities in (8) and (13) proves both inclusions with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-censoring-inference-limit]\label{thm:uniform-censoring-inference-limit}
The finite-dimensional inversion has the following exact scope. \begin{lemma}[lem:cap-fibre-mixture]\label{lem:cap-fibre-mixture}
Let \(Z\) be \(\mathbb Z_+\)-valued, let \(q_w=\mathsf P(\min\{Z,H\}=w)\) for \(w\in\mathbb I_H\), and let \(W\) have mass function \(q\). For \(q_w>0\), let \(\kappa_w\) be the conditional law of \(Z\) given \(\min\{Z,H\}=w\); for \(q_w=0\), let \(\kappa_w\) be any probability law supported on \(\{z\in\mathbb Z_+:\min\{z,H\}=w\}\). If \(\widetilde Z\) conditional on \(W=w\) has law \(\kappa_w\), then \(\min\{\widetilde Z,H\}=W\) almost surely and \(\widetilde Z\) has the same law as \(Z\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each \(w\in\mathbb I_H\), the fibre \(F_w=\{z\in\mathbb Z_+:\min\{z,H\}=w\}\) is nonempty: it is \(\{w\}\) when \(w<H\), and it is \(\{H,H+1,\ldots\}\) when \(w=H\). Hence every \(\kappa_w\) in the statement exists and is supported on \(F_w\). Conditional support therefore gives
\[
 \mathsf P\{\min(\widetilde Z,H)=W\mid W=w\}=1
 \quad\text{for every }w\text{ with }q_w>0.               \tag{1}
\]
The remaining values of \(w\) have probability zero, so (1) proves \(\min\{\widetilde Z,H\}=W\) almost surely. For any \(z\in\mathbb Z_+\), the law of total probability and \(\mathsf P(W=w)=q_w\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathsf P(\widetilde Z=z)
 &=\sum_{w=0}^{H}q_w\kappa_w(\{z\})\\
 &=\sum_{w:q_w>0}\mathsf P\{\min(Z,H)=w\}
      \mathsf P\{Z=z\mid\min(Z,H)=w\}\\
 &=\mathsf P(Z=z).
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{2}
\]
The terms with \(q_w=0\) vanish, so their arbitrary conditional laws do not affect (2). Equality at every atom of the countable support \(\mathbb Z_+\) proves equality of laws.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This elementary mixture identity is the load-bearing bridge from a capped coupling to the published uncapped residual equality. \textit{Gap.} A capped marginal alone does not specify which uncapped values in the top fibre are used; the conditional-fibre kernel supplies an explicit law-preserving choice. \textit{Consumer.} The response-tree lift and the raw-no-carry-over sharp-transfer corollary use the identity.

\begin{theorem}[thm:randomized-minimax-regret]\label{thm:randomized-minimax-regret}
For every \(\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}\), \(\mathcal R(\delta)=\max_{j\in\mathcal M}h_{\mathcal V}(\delta-e_j)\). Hence \(\Delta^\star\) is nonempty and is exactly the set of \(\delta\)-coordinates solving the explicit epigraph program \(\min_{\delta,\rho}\rho\) subject to \(\delta_m\ge0\), \(\sum_m\delta_m=1\), and \(\rho\ge h_{\mathcal V}(\delta-e_j)\) for every \(j\in\mathcal M\); each constraint is evaluated by the eligibility-greedy support algorithm. In \(\mathsf E_3\), the unique randomized minimax distribution is \((\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)=(9/16,5/16,1/8)\), with \(\mathcal R(\delta)=17/160<7/40=\min_mR_m\). Moreover, on a relatively open neighborhood of \(\mathsf E_3\) in \(\Theta_3\), randomized minimax regret remains strictly smaller than the regret of every deterministic threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}\). For \(e_j\) the \(j\)-th standard basis vector, finiteness of \(\mathcal M\) permits the interchange
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R(\delta)
 &=\sup_{v\in\mathcal V}\left\{\sum_m\delta_mv_m-min_jv_j\right\}\\
 &=\sup_{v\in\mathcal V}\max_{j\in\mathcal M}(\delta-e_j)^\top v\\
 &=\max_{j\in\mathcal M}\sup_{v\in\mathcal V}(\delta-e_j)^\top v
 =\max_{j\in\mathcal M}h_{\mathcal V}(\delta-e_j).
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{1}
\]
The third expression and the last expression are both the supremum over \(\mathcal V\times\mathcal M\). By \(\text{thm:joint-value-set-and-support}\), \(\mathcal V\) is compact. Its support function is therefore finite and continuous, since
\[
 |h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)-h_{\mathcal V}(\lambda')|
 \le \sup_{v\in\mathcal V}\|v\|_2\,\|\lambda-\lambda'\|_2. \tag{2}
\]
Equation (1) is a finite maximum of continuous convex functions of \(\delta\). The simplex \(\Delta_{\mathcal M}\) is nonempty and compact, so a minimizer exists. Introducing the scalar epigraph variable \(\rho\) gives exactly the variables and constraints displayed in the theorem. The support oracle in \(\text{thm:eligibility-greedy-support}\) evaluates every constraint.

We now calculate the cap-three instance exactly. The following three plans have the margins in \(\mathsf E_3\), are nonnegative, and use only cells satisfying \(b+w\le3\); all unlisted entries are zero:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|l|c}
 r&\text{nonzero cells of }x^{(r)}&(J_1,J_2,J_3)\\ \hline
 1&x_{02}=1/5, x_{03}=1/5, x_{10}=1/5, x_{11}=1/10,
      x_{21}=1/5, x_{30}=1/10&(3/10,11/20,21/40)\\
 2&x_{01}=1/5, x_{03}=1/5, x_{11}=1/10, x_{12}=1/5,
      x_{20}=1/5, x_{30}=1/10&(1/2,7/20,21/40)\\
 3&x_{00}=1/5, x_{03}=1/5, x_{11}=1/10, x_{12}=1/5,
      x_{21}=1/5, x_{30}=1/10&(7/10,11/20,21/40).
 \end{array}                                               \tag{3}
\]
The displayed values follow by substituting the plans and \((\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=(0,7/20,7/40,0)\) into the value map in \(\text{def:threshold-value-set}\).

For an arbitrary \(\delta=(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}\), nature may use the three plan--comparator pairs \((x^{(1)},1)\), \((x^{(2)},2)\), and \((x^{(3)},3)\). Their regrets are respectively
\[
 g_1(\delta)=\frac14\delta_2+\frac9{40}\delta_3,\qquad
 g_2(\delta)=\frac3{20}\delta_1+\frac7{40}\delta_3,\qquad
 g_3(\delta)=\frac7{40}\delta_1+\frac1{40}\delta_2.     \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal R(\delta)\ge\max_r g_r(\delta)\). The positive convex combination
\[
 \frac38g_1(\delta)+\frac18g_2(\delta)+\frac12g_3(\delta)
 =\frac{17}{160}(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
 =\frac{17}{160}                                         \tag{5}
\]
proves the universal lower bound \(\mathcal R(\delta)\ge17/160\).

Set \(\delta^*=(9/16,5/16,1/8)\). For comparator \(j=1,2,3\), the row coefficients \((c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)\) for the directions \(\delta^*-e_j\) are
\[
 (0,-7/16,-1/8,0),\qquad
 (0,9/16,-1/8,0),\qquad
 (0,9/16,7/8,0),                                          \tag{6}
\]
respectively. Processing columns from \(3\) down to \(0\) and eligible rows in decreasing order of (6) gives, respectively, the three plans in (3). The eligibility-greedy theorem proves that these plans maximize the three support directions, rather than merely furnishing lower bounds. Substitution into (4) gives
\[
 h_{\mathcal V}(\delta^*-e_1)
 =h_{\mathcal V}(\delta^*-e_2)
 =h_{\mathcal V}(\delta^*-e_3)=\frac{17}{160}.            \tag{7}
\]
Equations (1), (5), and (7) prove minimaxity and the stated value.

If another \(\delta\) attained \(17/160\), then each \(g_r(\delta)\le17/160\). Because every weight in (5) is strictly positive and their weighted average already equals \(17/160\), all three inequalities must be equalities. Solving
\[
 g_1(\delta)=g_2(\delta)=g_3(\delta)=17/160,\qquad
 \delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3=1                            \tag{8}
\]
gives \((\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)=(9/16,5/16,1/8)\). Hence the randomized minimizer is unique. The exact deterministic calculation in \(\text{prop:rational-recommendation-reversal}\) gives \(\min_mR_m=R_1=7/40=28/160\), so the improvement is strict.

 \Phi(\vartheta,\delta)=\max_jh_{\mathcal V_\vartheta}(\delta-e_j) \tag{9}

A sufficiently small relatively open neighborhood inside \(\Theta_3\) preserves its sign, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:randomized-confidence-polytope]\label{thm:randomized-confidence-polytope}
If \(\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\) covers \((r_{bt},q_w)\) simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), then with the same probability \(\Delta^\star\subseteq\widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}\). \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First prove the finite-sample inclusion.  Work on the simultaneous first-stage event \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\) in (1) of the proof of \(\text{thm:confidence-polytope}\), and put
\[
 \mathbf r^\circ=(\pi p_{bt})_{b,t},\qquad
 \mathbf s^\circ=(\pi q_w)_w.                            \tag{1}
\]

 =\mathcal V(p_{BT},q).                                  \tag{2}

 \mathsf R_{\mathbf r^\circ,\mathbf s^\circ}(\delta)
 =\max_{j\in\mathcal M}h_{\mathcal V}(\delta-e_j)
 =\mathcal R(\delta)                                    \tag{3}

for every \(\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}\).  Thus the true candidate margins are one of the margin pairs over which both envelopes range, and
\[
 \underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)
 \le\mathcal R(\delta)
 \le\overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)             \tag{4}
\]
simultaneously for all \(\delta\).  If \(\delta^\star\in\Delta^\star\), then
\[
 \underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta^\star)
 \le\mathcal R(\delta^\star)
 =\inf_{\lambda\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}}\mathcal R(\lambda)
 \le\inf_{\lambda\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}}
      \overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\lambda).         \tag{5}

The defining inequality therefore puts every \(\delta^\star\) in \(\widehat{\Delta}_{n,\alpha}\).  Since the event has probability at least \(1-\alpha\), this proves the asserted simultaneous coverage, including all ties.

 \quad(m\in\mathcal M).                                 \tag{6}

 \left|h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}(\lambda)
       -h_{\mathsf V_H(\mathbf r,\mathbf s)}(\lambda')\right|
 \le H\tau\,\|\lambda-\lambda'\|_1.                    \tag{7}

 |\mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta)
   -\mathsf R_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}(\delta')|

 \qquad
 \kappa_\ell^a(h_\ell\mid h_{0:\ell-1})
 =\mathsf P(O_\ell=h_\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell,
                    O_{0:\ell-1}=h_{0:\ell-1})
\]
for \(1\le\ell\le d(h)\).  Under baseline randomization, consistency, factual-arm sequential coarsening at random, assignment positivity, and factual at-risk censoring positivity,
\[
 \mathsf P(\mathcal H_K^a=h_{0:K})
 =\kappa_0^a(h_0)
   \prod_{\ell=1}^{d(h)}
       \kappa_\ell^a(h_\ell\mid h_{0:\ell-1}),
\]
where every factor after an earlier absorbing death is the deterministic factor one.  Thus the complete sustained-arm history law is identified atom by atom from the distribution of \(O\).  Consequently
\[
 r_{bt}=\mathsf P(S_k^1=1,B=b,T=t),\qquad
 \pi=\sum_{b,t}r_{bt},\qquad p_{bt}=r_{bt}/\pi,
\]
and, writing \(\nu_w=\mathsf P(S_k^0=1,\min\{Z,H\}=w)\) and
\(\sigma_0=\mathsf P(S_k^0=1)\),
\[
 q_w=\nu_w/\sigma_0
\]
are observed-law functionals.  The two divisions are valid by landmark-survival positivity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(a\) and a positive-probability absorbing atom \(h=h_{0:K}\).  For an alive prefix \(h_{0:\ell-1}\), put
\[
 g_\ell^a(h_{0:\ell-1})
 =\mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,
       D_{\ell-1}^a=0,\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a=h_{0:\ell-1}).       \tag{1}
\]
Factual-arm censoring positivity makes (1) strictly positive on every prefix of the chosen atom.  Sequential coarsening at random states, without suppressing its conditioning set, that
\[
 \mathsf P(C\ge\ell\mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,D_{\ell-1}^a=0,
                  \mathcal H_K^a=h)
 =g_\ell^a(h_{0:\ell-1}).                                  \tag{2}
\]
The chain rule and (2) give, for each \(j\le d(h)\),
\[
 \mathsf P(C\ge j\mid R=a,\mathcal H_K^a=h)
 =\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}g_\ell^a(h_{0:\ell-1}).                \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the \(\ell\)-th factor in the conditional chain rule is exactly (2).  The right side of (3), conditional on the prefix \(h_{0:j-1}\), does not depend on \(h_j,\ldots,h_K\).  Hence, for any supported alive prefix,
\[
 C\ge j\ \perp\!\!\!\perp\
 (\mathcal H_j^a,\ldots,\mathcal H_K^a)
 \mid R=a,\mathcal H_{j-1}^a.                              \tag{4}
\]
This conclusion has been derived from the displayed sequential restriction; no independence of censoring from the whole response tree has been added.

Now use Bayes' rule at an alive prefix.  By (2), (4), and the strict positivity of (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathsf P(\mathcal H_\ell^a=h_{0:\ell}
      \mid R=a,C\ge\ell,\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a=h_{0:\ell-1})\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{g_\ell^a(h_{0:\ell-1})
       \mathsf P(\mathcal H_\ell^a=h_{0:\ell}
          \mid R=a,C\ge\ell-1,
                 \mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a=h_{0:\ell-1})}
      {g_\ell^a(h_{0:\ell-1})}\\
 &\quad=
 \mathsf P(\mathcal H_\ell^a=h_{0:\ell}
      \mid R=a,\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a=h_{0:\ell-1}).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{5}
\]
The last equality follows from (4) with \(j=\ell-1\); for \(\ell=1\), the event \(C\ge0\) is certain.  Baseline randomization gives
\[
 \mathsf P(\mathcal H_K^a=h)=
 \mathsf P(\mathcal H_K^a=h\mid R=a),                      \tag{6}
\]
and consistency identifies every left side of (5), on \(R=a\) and \(C\ge\ell\), with the corresponding observed transition of \(O\).  Assignment positivity and (1) ensure that each observed conditional probability is well defined.  Applying the ordinary finite chain rule to (6), substituting (5), and stopping at the first death yields
\[
 \mathsf P(\mathcal H_K^a=h)
 =\kappa_0^a(h_0)
   \prod_{\ell=1}^{d(h)}
       \kappa_\ell^a(h_\ell\mid h_{0:\ell-1}).            \tag{7}
\]
Absorbing death makes every later coordinate deterministic, so no post-death censoring division is used.

The finite-horizon raw history space is countable because each count coordinate is in \(\mathbb Z_+\).  Therefore (7) identifies the law of the sustained history by countable summation of its atoms.  Summing the intensive-arm atoms over the event
\(\{S_k^1=1,B=b,T=t\}\) gives
\[
 r_{bt}=\mathsf P(S_k^1=1,B=b,T=t)=\pi p_{bt}.             \tag{8}
\]
Summing (8) over \(b,t\) gives \(\pi\), and landmark-survival positivity gives \(\pi>0\), so \(p_{bt}=r_{bt}/\pi\).  Likewise, summing standard-arm atoms gives
\[
 \nu_w=\mathsf P(S_k^0=1,\min\{Z,H\}=w),\qquad
 \sigma_0=\sum_w\nu_w=\mathsf P(S_k^0=1)>0.              \tag{9}
\]
The definition of conditional probability then gives \(q_w=\nu_w/\sigma_0\).  Equations (7)--(9) prove every asserted identification map.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma repairs the inferential foundation by deriving, rather than citing, the finite sequential factorization for every supported absorbing sustained-history atom. \textit{Gap.} Published recurrent-event CAR results used here formulate the coarsening condition and identify particular mean or survival targets, not the complete history law needed for every capped terminal cell. \textit{Consumer.} The explicit cell and ratio influence functions use the identified transition kernels to construct all backward outcome regressions.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hoffman-polyhedral-error-bound]\label{lem:hoffman-polyhedral-error-bound}
Fix a real matrix \(A\) and norms on its domain and codomain.  There is a constant \(c_A>0\), depending only on \(A\) and the norms, such that, whenever \(\{y:Ay\le b\}\) is nonempty, every \(x\) admits \(x_0\) with \(Ax_0\le b\) and
\[
 \lVert x-x_0\rVert\le c_A\lVert(Ax-b)_+\rVert.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:cross-fitted-margin-region]\label{thm:cross-fitted-margin-region}
Fix \(H\), \(K\), and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\).  Uniformly over triangular law--estimator sequences satisfying \(\operatorname{QIR}_{H,K,\varepsilon}(\mathsf P,\mathfrak I)\), the cross-fitted longitudinal estimators in \(\text{def:uniform-inference-handle}\) obey a joint asymptotic-linear expansion for the finite vector \(((r_{bt}),(q_w))\) with influence coordinates \(\varphi_{1,F_{bt}^1}\) and \(\varphi_{q_w}\).  The displayed Bonferroni--Chebyshev rectangle \(\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\) consequently has uniform simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) asymptotically and sup-norm diameter \(O(n^{-1/2})\).  The same probability statement simultaneously gives
\[
 \mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,\alpha},\qquad
 \mathcal M^\star\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha},\qquad
 \Delta^\star\subseteq\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha},
\]
without strict Hall slack, uniqueness, or exclusion of deterministic or randomized policy ties.  Uniformly on this quantitative submodel, the Hausdorff distance in sup norm between \(\widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,\alpha}\) and \(\mathcal V(p_{BT},q)\) is \(O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2})\), and
\[
 \sup_{m\in\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}}
   \{R_m-\min_jR_j\}=O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \sup_{\delta\in\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha}}
   \{\mathcal R(\delta)-\inf_\lambda\mathcal R(\lambda)\}
   =O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
If additionally the nonoptimal deterministic regret gap is uniformly bounded below by a positive constant, then \(\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal M^\star\) with probability tending to one.  If additionally there is a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
 \mathcal R(\delta)-\inf_\lambda\mathcal R(\lambda)
 \ge c\,\operatorname{dist}_1(\delta,\Delta^\star)
 \quad\text{for every }\delta\in\Delta_{\mathcal M}
\]
uniformly over the submodel, then
\(\sup_{\delta\in\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha}}
\operatorname{dist}_1(\delta,\Delta^\star)=O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2})\).  Without these gap or polyhedral-sharpness conditions, only simultaneous outer coverage and regret-excess contraction are asserted, so all ties remain admissible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the claims in five steps.

\emph{Step 1: observed nuisances and the longitudinal score.}
For every terminal indicator in (2) of \(\text{def:uniform-inference-handle}\), \(\text{lem:observed-margin-identification}\) identifies every supported transition of the sustained arm.  Consequently it identifies the backward regressions \(Q_{\ell,F}^a\) and censoring hazards \(g_\ell^a\).  At a dead prefix the terminal value is already determined by absorption, so the recursion uses no unsupported continuation.

For the true nuisances, condition the \(\ell\)-th summand of \(\Psi_{a,F}\) first on \(R=a\), \(C\ge\ell-1\), and \(\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a\).  Sequential coarsening at random and consistency give
\[
 \mathsf E\!\left[
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{C\ge\ell,D_{\ell-1}^a=0\}}{\bar G_\ell^a}
 \{Q_{\ell,F}^a-Q_{\ell-1,F}^a\}
 \mathrel{\Big|} R=a,\mathcal H_{\ell-1}^a\right]=0.       \tag{1}
\]
The division is valid because \(\operatorname{QIR}_{H,K,\varepsilon}\) gives \(\bar G_\ell^a\ge\varepsilon^\ell\) on the at-risk support.  Baseline randomization and \(g_a\ge\varepsilon\) then imply
\[
 \mathsf E\{\Psi_{a,F}(O)\}=\mathsf E\{Q_{0,F}^a(\mathcal H_0^a)\}=\theta_{a,F},
 \qquad \mathsf E(\varphi_{a,F})=0.                       \tag{2}
\]
Because \(0\le Q_{\ell,F}^a\le1\),
\[
 |\varphi_{a,F}|
 \le 2+K\varepsilon^{-(K+1)}=:C_0.                       \tag{3}
\]
Moreover \(\sigma_0\ge\varepsilon\), so (3) and the ratio formula give
\[
 |\varphi_{q_w}|\le 2C_0/\varepsilon=:C_{K,\varepsilon}. \tag{4}
\]
The latter constant also bounds the cell-mass influence coordinates after increasing it if necessary.

\emph{Step 2: cross-fitted asymptotic linearity.}
Consider one validation fold and condition on its training sample.  Add and subtract the score at the true nuisances:
\[
 \widehat\theta_{a,F}-\theta_{a,F}
 =(\mathbb P_n-\mathsf P)\varphi_{a,F}
 +(\mathbb P_n-\mathsf P)\{\Psi_{a,F}(\widehat\eta)-\Psi_{a,F}(\eta)\}
 +\mathsf P\{\Psi_{a,F}(\widehat\eta)-\Psi_{a,F}(\eta)\}. \tag{5}
\]
Here \(\eta\) denotes the finite collection of true regressions and hazards.  Clipping and the overlap bounds make the score Lipschitz in each nuisance with a constant depending only on \(K\) and \(\varepsilon\).  Conditional variance in the validation fold and uniform \(L^2\)-consistency therefore give
\[
 (\mathbb P_n-\mathsf P)\{\Psi_{a,F}(\widehat\eta)-\Psi_{a,F}(\eta)\}
 =o_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2}).                                \tag{6}
\]
For completeness, iterated expectation in the last term of (5), followed by adding and subtracting each true backward regression, telescopes all terms that contain only a regression error or only a censoring error.  Cauchy--Schwarz leaves the explicit bound
\[
 \left|\mathsf P\{\Psi_{a,F}(\widehat\eta)-\Psi_{a,F}(\eta)\}\right|
 \le C(K,\varepsilon)
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{K}
 \lVert\widehat Q_{\ell-1,F}^a-Q_{\ell-1,F}^a\rVert_2
 \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\lVert\widehat g_j^a-g_j^a\rVert_2.    \tag{7}
\]
To see why no first-order term is hidden in (7), the conditional mean of
\(Q_{\ell,F}^a-Q_{\ell-1,F}^a\) is zero by the defining backward recursion, while the conditional mean of the censoring indicator divided by its true continuation probability is one by sequential coarsening at random.  Thus every surviving summand contains one error of each kind.  The product-rate clause in \(\operatorname{QIR}_{H,K,\varepsilon}\) makes (7) \(o_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2})\).  Averaging the fixed number of folds in (5)--(7) proves, uniformly for all terminal indicators,
\[
 \widehat\theta_{a,F}-\theta_{a,F}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\varphi_{a,F}(O_i)+o_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2}). \tag{8}
\]

Apply (8) to \(\nu_w\) and \(\sigma_0\).  Since \(\sigma_0\ge\varepsilon\) and \(\widehat\sigma_0\to\sigma_0\) uniformly, the clipped denominator equals \(\widehat\sigma_0\) with probability tending to one.  The exact ratio identity
\[
 \frac{\widehat\nu_w}{\widehat\sigma_0}-\frac{\nu_w}{\sigma_0}
 =\frac{(\widehat\nu_w-\nu_w)-q_w(\widehat\sigma_0-\sigma_0)}{\sigma_0}
 -\frac{(\widehat\sigma_0-\sigma_0)
 \{(\widehat\nu_w-\nu_w)-q_w(\widehat\sigma_0-\sigma_0)\}}
 {\sigma_0\widehat\sigma_0}                              \tag{9}
\]
and (8) show that the second term is \(O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1})\) and that the first has influence coordinate (3) of the definition.  This proves the asserted joint expansion.

\emph{Step 3: the simultaneous rectangle.}
Let \(\vartheta=((r_{bt}),(q_w))\) and \(d=(H+1)^2+(H+1)\).  For every coordinate \(s\) of the influence vector, (4) and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 \mathsf P\!\left(
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\varphi_s(O_i)\right|
 >C_{K,\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac d{\alpha n}}\right)
 \le\frac\alpha d.                                      \tag{10}
\]
The union bound over the fixed \(d\) coordinates, (8)--(9), and uniformity of the remainders yield
\[
 \liminf_n\inf_{\operatorname{QIR}}
 \mathsf P\!\left\{
 \lVert\widehat\vartheta_n^{\mathrm{raw}}-\vartheta\rVert_\infty
 \le\frac{C_{K,\varepsilon}\sqrt{d/\alpha}+1}{\sqrt n}
 \right\}\ge1-\alpha.                                  \tag{11}
\]
The true margin vector belongs to the Hall-feasible projection domain.  If \(\widetilde\vartheta_n\) is a sup-norm projection of the raw estimate onto that domain, minimality and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \lVert\widetilde\vartheta_n-\vartheta\rVert_\infty
 \le2\lVert\widehat\vartheta_n^{\mathrm{raw}}-\vartheta\rVert_\infty. \tag{12}
\]
Thus the half-width (4) covers every true coordinate on the event in (11).  It is deterministic of order \(n^{-1/2}\), so the rectangle has the claimed sup-norm diameter.  Projection also ensures that the downstream confidence polytope is nonempty.

On the event of simultaneous margin coverage, \(\text{thm:confidence-polytope}\) gives
\(\mathcal V\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,\alpha}\) and
\(\mathcal M^\star\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\), while
\(\text{thm:randomized-confidence-polytope}\) gives
\(\Delta^\star\subseteq\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha}\).  These are set inclusions based on weak inequalities, so Hall equalities and all optimizer ties are retained.

\emph{Step 4: polyhedral contraction and regret excess.}
Write equivalently \(\vartheta=(\mathbf r,q)\), put \(\tau=\sum_{b,t}r_{bt}\), and
\(s_w=\tau q_w\).  By (12) and the width of the rectangle, every candidate
\((\mathbf r',\mathbf s')\) in its margin projection has positive total mass for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Indeed, if \(h_n\) is the common half-width and \(d_r=(H+1)^2\), its coordinatewise lower constraints give
\[
 \tau'=\sum_{b,t}r'_{bt}
 \ge\sum_{b,t}\max\{0,\widetilde r_{bt}-h_n\}
 \ge\varepsilon/2-d_rh_n\ge\varepsilon/4.                \tag{13}
\]
Define \(q'_w=s'_w/\tau'\).  The perspective inequalities in
\(\widehat{\mathcal P}_{n,\alpha}\) put every \(q'_w\) between the corresponding endpoints of \(\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\).  Hence
\[
 \lVert\mathbf r'-\mathbf r\rVert_\infty
 +\lVert\mathbf s'-\mathbf s\rVert_\infty
 =O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2})                                \tag{14}
\]
uniformly; the product \(s'_w=\tau' q'_w\) is Lipschitz because
\(0\le\tau',q'_w\le1\).  Encode nonnegativity, forbidden Ferrers cells, and the row and column equations of \(\mathsf X_H\) as one fixed finite inequality matrix \(A_H\).  Apply \(\text{lem:hoffman-polyhedral-error-bound}\) first to a candidate plan with the true right side and then to a true plan with the projected-center right side.  Since both right sides are feasible, it gives a constant \(L_H<\infty\), depending only on the fixed matrix, such that the correcting plans obey
\[
 \lVert\mathbf x'-\mathbf x\rVert_1
 \le L_H\{
 \lVert\mathbf r'-\mathbf r\rVert_1+
 \lVert\mathbf s'-\mathbf s\rVert_1\}.                  \tag{15}
\]
This is the only classical polyhedral sensitivity input; (13)--(14) are the note-specific perspective-margin calculation.

For every threshold, the value map is linear and \(0\le t,w\le H\).  Hence (14)--(15) imply
\[
 |\mathsf J_m(\mathbf r',\mathbf x')-
   \mathsf J_m(\mathbf r,\mathbf x)|
 \le H\lVert\mathbf r'-\mathbf r\rVert_1+
      H\lVert\mathbf x'-\mathbf x\rVert_1
 =O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2}).                               \tag{16}
\]
The two applications of (15) prove both directed Hausdorff bounds, so (16), uniformly over the finite menu, proves the asserted Hausdorff contraction of value sets.

If two compact value sets are within \(e\) in sup-norm Hausdorff distance, then directly from the support definition
\[
 |h_V(\lambda)-h_{V'}(\lambda)|
 \le e\lVert\lambda\rVert_1.                             \tag{17}
\]
Every deterministic pairwise direction and every randomized regret direction has \(\ell^1\)-norm at most two.  Taking finite maxima in (17) therefore shows that every candidate deterministic or randomized regret differs uniformly from its true counterpart by
\(e_n=O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2})\).  If
\(m\in\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\), the defining envelope inequality gives
\[
 R_m\le\underline R_m^{n,\alpha}+e_n
 \le\min_j\overline R_j^{n,\alpha}+e_n
 \le\min_jR_j+2e_n.                                     \tag{18}
\]
Likewise, if \(\delta\in\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha}\),
\[
 \mathcal R(\delta)
 \le\underline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\delta)+e_n
 \le\inf_\lambda\overline{\mathcal R}_{n,\alpha}(\lambda)+e_n
 \le\inf_\lambda\mathcal R(\lambda)+2e_n.               \tag{19}
\]
Equations (18)--(19) are the two regret-excess conclusions.

\emph{Step 5: exactly when geometric optimizer contraction follows.}
Suppose the deterministic gap
\[
 \inf_{\operatorname{QIR}}
 \min_{m\notin\mathcal M^\star}
 \{R_m-\min_jR_j\}>0.                                   \tag{20}
\]
Then (18) excludes every nonoptimizer with probability tending to one, proving
\(\widehat{\mathcal M}_{n,\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal M^\star\).  For mixtures, the stated polyhedral-sharpness inequality and (19) give
\[
 \sup_{\delta\in\widehat\Delta_{n,\alpha}}
 \operatorname{dist}_1(\delta,\Delta^\star)
 \le 2e_n/c=O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1/2}).                      \tag{21}
\]
Without (20) or the sharpness inequality, (18)--(19) control loss but not distance to a possibly flat optimizer face.  The proof therefore does not convert a Hall or policy tie into an unstated separation assumption.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives a computable positive inference result on exactly the quantitative submodel that excludes the headline vanishing-information sequences, and propagates root-\(n\) margin error through the transport geometry to values and decisions. \textit{Gap.} Set-theoretic confidence inversion alone does not supply a first-stage construction, a contraction rate, or the regularity boundary under censoring; prior CAR and support-function literatures do not provide this capped recurrent-event composition. \textit{Consumer.} A trial analysis satisfying prespecified quantitative overlap and cross-fitted nuisance-rate diagnostics can report one simultaneous region for all capped values and robust deterministic or randomized decisions, while retaining every tie.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The cap \(H\) constrains only the separately capped summaries and never the primitive raw recurrent counts. Its clinical mechanism is not established for SPRINT. The response-tree lift preserves factual-arm sequential censoring without assuming censoring independence from the whole response tree. The positive inference theorem is not a consequence of the maintained class: it uses a separate quantitative predicate with uniform assignment, survival, and factual censoring overlap and explicit nuisance product rates. The conservative Bonferroni--Chebyshev rectangle establishes uniform root-\(n\) order but does not claim semiparametric efficiency or shortest width. At Hall and policy ties, outer coverage and regret-excess contraction remain valid, but optimizer-set distance need not contract without the displayed gap or polyhedral-sharpness condition. The broad-class impossibility sequences violate every fixed quantitative overlap bound and therefore do not contradict the positive submodel theorem.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved scope includes exact endpoints, an observed-law-preserving raw-path causal lift, sharp transfer to the published raw no-carry-over subclass intersected with the cap, the complete common-law value set, deterministic and randomized minimax regret, and confidence-polytope inversion. Complete sustained-arm history identification is now proved directly by a finite observed-kernel product, rather than attributed to a source that identifies only particular functionals. Positive root-\(n\) margin, value-set, and regret-excess contraction holds only on the explicit quantitative inference regularity submodel with fixed \(H,K\), a fixed overlap lower bound, cross-fitting, clipped uniformly consistent nuisances, and longitudinal product-rate control. All deterministic and randomized ties retain simultaneous outer coverage. Geometric optimizer contraction is claimed only under an additional uniform deterministic regret gap or randomized polyhedral-sharpness inequality. On the unchanged pointwise-overlap class, uniformly honest regions need not shrink, as witnessed by the stated two-law sequences. SPRINT remains only a conditional capped-loss consumer requiring an externally defended cap at \(k=300\) and a feasible censoring-adjusted Hall system.
