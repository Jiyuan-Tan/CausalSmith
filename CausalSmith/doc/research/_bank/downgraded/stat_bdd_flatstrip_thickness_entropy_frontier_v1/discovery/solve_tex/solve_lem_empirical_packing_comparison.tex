%%% FIELD statement-same-radius-comparison
There are numerical constants \(c,C<\infty\) and events \(\mathcal E_n\) such that
\[
 \inf_{P\in\bigcup_{\kappa\in K}\mathrm{Base}_\kappa}P(\mathcal E_n)
 \geq 1-Cn^{-3}.
\]
If \(n_1\geq1\), then, on \(\mathcal E_n\), simultaneously for every
\(h\in H_n\) and \(u\in U_n\) satisfying
\(n_1u\geq C\log(en)\),
\[
 J_P(h,u)
 \leq \widehat J\!\left(h,c\{u+\log(en)/n_1\}\right)
 \leq C\left\{1\vee
 J_P\!\left(h,C\{u+\log(en)/n_1\}\right)\right\}.                 \tag{P1}
\]
On the same event,
\[
 a^L(x,h)\leq a_P(x,h)\leq a^U(x,h)                              \tag{P2}
\]
for every \(x\in B\) and \(h\in H_n\), and
\[
 \tfrac12a_P(x,h)\leq a^L(x,h)leq a_P(x,h)leq a^U(x,h)leq2a_P(x,h) \tag{P3}
\]
whenever \(n_1a_P(x,h)\geq C\log(en)\).  For every \(h\in H_n\), the
source-labeled sorting, duplicate removal, deterministic refinement, and
midpoint operations defining \(C_1(h)\) and \(G_1(h)\) are Borel functions
of \((X_i)_{i\in I_1}\), and \(\widehat J(h,v)\) is Borel for every
\(v\geq0\).  In particular, the upper comparison in \((\mathrm{P1})\) uses
the same radius \(h\), and the floor \(1\vee J_P\) covers an empty population
thin set.

%%% FIELD proof-same-radius-comparison
Put \(m=n_1\) and \(\ell=\log(en)\).  When \(m=0\), the condition
\(mu\geq C\ell\) has no solutions.  Moreover the definitions using
\(m\vee1\) give \(q_t^L=0\leq q_t\leq1=q_t^U\), hence \((\mathrm{P2})\).
It therefore suffices to consider \(m\geq1\).

For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(s\in[-1/2,1/2]\), and \(h\in H_n\), define
\[
 \mathcal C_{t,s,h}
 =\{z\in A_t:\|z-(0,s)\|_2\leq h\}.
\]
Fix deterministic points \(z_i=(r_i,s_i)\), \(1\leq i\leq N\).  If
\(z_i\in A_t\) and \(|r_i|\leq h\), then, as a function of \(s\),
membership of \(z_i\) in \(\mathcal C_{t,s,h}\) is the indicator of the
closed interval
\[
 \left[s_i-\sqrt{h^2-r_i^2},s_i+\sqrt{h^2-r_i^2}\right].           \tag{P4}
\]
Otherwise that membership indicator is identically zero.  Thus, for fixed
\((t,h)\), the trace on the \(N\) points changes only at the at most \(2N\)
endpoints in \((\mathrm{P4})\).  Since
\(|H_n|\leq2+\log_2 n\), the shatter coefficient of
\[
 \mathcal C_n
 =\{\mathcal C_{t,s,h}:t\in\{0,1\},\ s\in[-1/2,1/2],\ h\in H_n\}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \Pi_{\mathcal C_n}(N)
 \leq2|H_n|(2N+1)\leq C_0(N+1)\ell.                              \tag{P5}
\]

We next derive the relative empirical bound used below.  We first record a
finite-growth inequality.  Let \(\mathcal A\) be any class of measurable sets,
let \(P_m\) be the empirical measure of \(m\) independent observations, and
put
\[
 \Gamma=x+\log\{8\Pi_{\mathcal A}(2m)\},\qquad x\geq1.
\]
Then
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\exists C\in\mathcal A:
 |P_m(C)-P(C)|>
 \sqrt{\frac{2P_m(C)\Gamma}{m}}+\frac{7\Gamma}{3m}\right\}
 \leq2e^{-x}.                                                       \tag{P6}
\]
Here is a proof.  Introduce an independent empirical measure \(P'_m\).
Conditional on the pooled \(2m\) observations, randomly pair the observations
and independently choose one member of every pair for \(P_m\), the other for
\(P'_m\).  This has the uniform random-split law.  For a fixed pooled trace,
the difference of its two counts is a sum of independent random signs, one
for every discordant pair.  If
\(\bar p=\{P_m(C)+P'_m(C)\}/2\), the number of discordant pairs is at most
\(2m\bar p\).  Therefore \(\cosh(v)\leq\exp(v^2/2)\) and exponential Markov
give
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{|P_m(C)-P'_m(C)|>
 2\sqrt{\bar p z/m}\,\middle|\,\text{pooled observations}\right\}
 \leq2e^{-z}.                                                       \tag{P7}
\]
Peel \(\bar p\) into the cells
\(2^{-j-1}<\bar p\leq2^{-j}\), together with \(\bar p=0\).  In each cell,
apply \((\mathrm{P7})\) with
\(z=\Gamma+(j+1)\log2\), and take a union over the at most
\(\Pi_{\mathcal A}(2m)\) pooled traces.  The sum over \(j\geq0\) is bounded
by \(e^{-x}/4\).  If \(d=|P_m(C)-P(C)|\), an independent application of the
same sign calculation to the ghost half shows that a violation of the bound
in \((\mathrm{P6})\) has conditional probability at least \(1/4\) of
producing a split for which
\[
 |P_m(C)-P'_m(C)|>
 \sqrt{4\bar p\Gamma/m}+2\Gamma/(3m).
\]
Indeed, substitute \(P(C)\leq P_m(C)+d\) when the deviation is downward and
\(P(C)\leq P_m(C)\) when it is upward, and use
\(2\sqrt{d\Gamma/m}\leq d/2+2\Gamma/m\); the constants
\(2\) and \(7/3\) leave the displayed margin after moving \(d/2\) to the
left.  Multiplication by four for this ghost step and the preceding geometric
sum prove \((\mathrm{P6})\).  This proof depends only on pooled traces, so it
also applies when the supremum is not attained, by taking an increasing union
over finite trace subfamilies.

Apply \((\mathrm{P6})\) to \(\mathcal A=\mathcal C_n\) with \(x=3\ell\).
The explicit first bound in \((\mathrm{P5})\) implies
\[
 \Gamma=3\ell+\log\{8\Pi_{\mathcal C_n}(2m)\}\leq6\ell
\]
for \(n\geq2\), by direct substitution of \(m\leq n/2\) and
\(|H_n|\leq2+\log_2n\); the case \(n=1\) has \(m=0\).  Consequently
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\exists C\in\mathcal C_n:
 |P_m(C)-P(C)|>
 \sqrt{12P_m(C)\ell/m}+20\ell/m\right\}
 \leq C_1n^{-3}.                                                     \tag{P8}
\]
Closed intervals in \((\mathrm{P4})\) ensure that the pooled trace count
includes traces attained exactly at coincident or noncoincident breakpoints.

Let \(\mathcal E_n\) be the complement of the event in \((\mathrm{P8})\).
For \(x=(0,s)\),
\[
 P_m(\mathcal C_{t,s,h})=\widehat q_t^{(1)}(x,h).
\]
Therefore \((\mathrm{P8})\) and the definitions of \(b_n,q_t^L,q_t^U\)
give, simultaneously in \((x,t,h)\),
\[
 q_t^L(x,h)\leq q_t(x,h)\leq q_t^U(x,h).                          \tag{P9}
\]
Taking the minimum over \(t\) proves \((\mathrm{P2})\).  If
\(mq_t(x,h)\geq C_2\ell\), solving the two quadratic inequalities obtained
from \((\mathrm{P8})\) yields
\[
 \tfrac12q_t(x,h)\leq q_t^L(x,h)
 \leq q_t(x,h)\leq q_t^U(x,h)\leq2q_t(x,h).                       \tag{P10}
\]
When \(ma_P(x,h)\geq C_2\ell\), both side masses meet this premise.
Taking their minima in \((\mathrm{P10})\) proves \((\mathrm{P3})\).

We now construct the pattern-preserving representative.  Fix \(h\in H_n\).
Every endpoint in \((\mathrm{P4})\) generated by a first-half score and lying
in \([-1/2,1/2]\) occurs in the source-labeled candidate list.  Lexicographic
ordering is total; when labels have equal coordinates, retaining the least
label removes only duplicate labels and retains their common coordinate.
A deterministic mesh point tied with a breakpoint likewise occurs once in
the sorted union.  Define \(g_h((0,s))\) to equal \((0,s)\) when \(s\) is a
breakpoint, a deterministic mesh point, or an endpoint of \(B\).  Otherwise
there is a unique open cell \((w_j,w_{j+1})\) containing \(s\); define
\[
 g_h((0,s))=\left(0,\frac{w_j+w_{j+1}}2\right).
\]
No first-half membership indicator changes inside this open cell.  Because
\(M_h=\lceil16/h\rceil\), consecutive deterministic mesh points are at
distance at most \(h/16\).  Hence
\[
 \|g_h(x)-x\|_2\leq\frac1{2M_h}\leq\frac h{32}<\frac h4,
 \qquad
 \widehat q_t^{(1)}(g_h(x),h)=\widehat q_t^{(1)}(x,h)              \tag{P11}
\]
for \(t=0,1\).  At a breakpoint, equality follows from the closed-ball
convention and the choice \(g_h(x)=x\), so \((\mathrm{P11})\) also covers all
ties.

Let \(F\) be any \(h\)-separated subset of
\(\{x\in B:a_P(x,h)\leq u\}\).  For each \(x\in F\), choose a side
\(t_x\) with \(q_{t_x}(x,h)\leq u\).  On \(\mathcal E_n\), the inequality
\(|q_t-\widehat q_t^{(1)}|\leq b_n(\widehat q_t^{(1)})\) implies
\[
 \widehat q_{t_x}^{(1)}(x,h)\leq2u+64\ell/m.
\]
Indeed, putting \(z=\widehat q_{t_x}^{(1)}(x,h)\), one has
\(z\leq u+\sqrt{12z\ell/m}+20\ell/m\); solving this quadratic gives the
display.  Applying the definition of \(b_n\) once more and using
\(2\sqrt{uv}\leq u+v\), then using \((\mathrm{P11})\), gives
\[
 a^U(g_h(x),h)\leq C_3\{u+\ell/m\}.                               \tag{P12}
\]
For distinct \(x,x'\in F\), the triangle inequality and
\((\mathrm{P11})\) give
\[
 \|g_h(x)-g_h(x')\|_2
 \geq h-h/32-h/32=15h/16>h/2.                                    \tag{P13}
\]
The upward severity rounding in the estimator satisfies
\[
 u(v)\leq\min\{1,2v+m^{-1}\},\qquad v\geq0.                       \tag{P14}
\]
This follows directly from the adjacent ratio two of the positive dyadic
grid points; below its smallest positive point the excess is at most
\(m^{-1}\), and \(u(0)=0\).  Choose \(c\geq2C_3+1\).  Equations
\((\mathrm{P12})\)--\((\mathrm{P14})\) make \(g_h(F)\) feasible in the
maximum defining \(\widehat J(h,c\{u+\ell/m\})\), and
\((\mathrm{P13})\) makes \(g_h\) injective on \(F\).  Taking the maximum
over \(F\) proves the first inequality in \((\mathrm{P1})\).  The argument
remains well typed at \(u=1\), because the definition caps severity at one.

Conversely, let \(G\) be a nonempty feasible set in the finite maximum
defining \(\widehat J(h,v)\).  Equations \((\mathrm{P2})\) and
\((\mathrm{P14})\) imply, for every \(g\in G\),
\[
 a_P(g,h)\leq a^U(g,h)\leq u(v)
 \leq\min\{1,2v+m^{-1}\}.                                        \tag{P15}
\]
Order \(G=\{g_1<\cdots<g_M\}\) by boundary coordinate.  Feasibility gives
\(\|g_{j+1}-g_j\|_2\geq h/2\).  Because all points lie on the same line,
\[
 \|g_{j+2}-g_j\|_2
 =\|g_{j+2}-g_{j+1}\|_2+\|g_{j+1}-g_j\|_2\geq h.                 \tag{P16}
\]
Thus the odd and even sublists are each \(h\)-separated.  Equation
\((\mathrm{P15})\) already makes every one of their points thin at the same
radius \(h\).  The definition of \(J_P\) therefore yields
\[
 |G|\leq2J_P(h,2v+m^{-1}).                                        \tag{P17}
\]
If no nonempty feasible set exists, the maximum before flooring is zero and
the outer floor defining \(\widehat J\) equals one.  Combining the two cases,
substituting \(v=c\{u+\ell/m\}\), and enlarging \(C\) proves the second
inequality in \((\mathrm{P1})\).  Notice that no monotonicity in radius was
used: \((\mathrm{P15})\) precedes the odd--even split, so both population
packings in \((\mathrm{P17})\) are at radius \(h\).  The floor is necessary
precisely in the empty-thin-set case.

Finally, all the displayed sample maps are Borel.  The square-root endpoint
map is continuous on its closed domain.  Candidate inclusion and each
lexicographic comparison are Borel.  Sorting a list of bounded deterministic
length, retaining the least source label at a tied coordinate, adjoining the
deterministic mesh, and taking adjacent midpoints use finitely many Borel
comparisons and arithmetic operations.  The resulting \(G_1(h)\) is finite;
after encoding absent candidate slots by their Borel presence indicators,
the maximum defining \(\widehat J(h,v)\) is the maximum of finitely many
Borel feasibility indicators.  This proves the measurability assertion and
completes the proof.

%%% FIELD partial-downstream-obstruction
The pure first-half result is complete: equations \((\mathrm{P1})\)--\((\mathrm{P17})\)
give the same-radius comparison, the factor comparison for \(a^L,a_P,a^U\),
and Borel measurability.  What does not follow from the frozen class is the
claimed selected-interval stochastic inequality.  At a fixed small radius
\(h\), place \(M\) boundary centers inside an interval of length less than
\(h/2\), and on each treatment side put a small score component near the
point at normal distance \(h\) from its corresponding center.  The components
may be chosen disjoint, each of mass proportional to \(h^\kappa\), while a
diffuse radial-power component supplies the common lower mass.  Each radius-
\(h\) ball then contains its own component but none of the other components.
The \(h\)-packing number of these centers is one, whereas the conditional
Gaussian averages contain \(M\) independent coordinates and have expected
maximum of order
\[
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log M}{nh^\kappa}}.
\]
The frozen base class imposes no upper-mass, doubling, or sub-\(h\) entropy
condition that rules out this configuration.  Accordingly, a block-mass
bound proportional to one parent mass cannot be derived merely from
consecutiveness of individual membership paths.
