Fix \(P\in\mathrm{Base}_\kappa\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Define the finite Borel measure
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(C):=P^{\mathrm{obs}}\{X\in C,T=t\},\qquad C\in\mathcal B(D).
\]
By sharp assignment, \(\mathbf 1\{T=t\}=\mathbf 1\{X\in A_t\}\) almost surely.  In particular, for every Borel set \(C\subseteq A_t\), consistency, sharp assignment, the tower property, and the definition of \(m_{t,P}\) give, successively,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_C g_{t,P}(z)\,\mu_{t,P}(dz)
 &=E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}\!\left[Y\mathbf 1\{X\in C,T=t\}\right] \\
 &=E_P\!\left[Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in C,T=t\}\right] \\
 &=E_P\!\left[E_P\{Y(t)\mid X\}\mathbf 1\{X\in C,T=t\}\right] \\
 &=\int_C m_{t,P}(z)\,\mu_{t,P}(dz).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the indicator \(\mathbf 1\{X\in C,T=t\}\) is \(\sigma(X)\)-measurable because sharp assignment makes \(T\) a measurable function of \(X\).  The displayed identity for all such \(C\) proves
\[
 g_{t,P}=m_{t,P}\quad \mu_{t,P}\text{-almost everywhere on }A_t.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(m_{t,P}|_{A_t}\) is a version of the observed conditional regression on the restricted support.  This step uses no unconfoundedness premise: within \(A_t\), treatment is already fixed by \(X\).

Let \(E_{t,P}\subseteq A_t\) be a measurable set of full \(\mu_{t,P}\)-measure on which (1) holds.  For \(x\in B\) and \(0<h\le h_0\), sharp assignment and the definition of the side masses imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mu_{t,P}\bigl(B_2(x,h)\bigr)
 &=P\{X\in B_2(x,h),T=t\} \\
 &=P\{X\in B_2(x,h)\cap A_t\} \\
 &=q_t(x,h).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(P\in\mathrm{Base}_\kappa\), the base-mass assumption, \(c_m>0\), and \(h>0\) yield
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\bigl(B_2(x,h)\bigr)=q_t(x,h)\ge c_m h^\kappa>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(x\) belongs to the closure of the support of \(\mu_{t,P}\).  More precisely, because \(\mu_{t,P}(A_t\setminus E_{t,P})=0\), (2) gives
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\bigl(E_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h)\bigr)>0
\tag{3}
\]
for every admissible \(h\).  With \(h_j=h_0/(j+1)\), choose \(z_j\in E_{t,P}\cap B_2(x,h_j)\), which is possible by (3).  Then \(z_j\to x\), and every \(z_j\) lies in the closed \(h_0\)-neighborhood of \(B\).  The causal-mean Lipschitz assumption and (1) therefore imply
\[
 \left|g_{t,P}(z_j)-m_{t,P}(x)\right|
 =\left|m_{t,P}(z_j)-m_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\lVert z_j-x\rVert_2
 \le Lh_j\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]
By the definition \(\theta_{t,P}=m_{t,P}|_B\), equation (4) identifies the side trace as \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=m_{t,P}(x)\).

The value in (4) is independent of conditional-expectation versions.  Indeed, suppose \(v\) and \(w\) are two Lipschitz representatives on the relevant side that represent the same observed regression class.  Their equality set has full \(\mu_{t,P}\)-measure, so (3), applied to that equality set, supplies \(z_j\to x\) with \(v(z_j)=w(z_j)\).  Continuity then gives
\[
 |v(x)-w(x)|
 \le |v(x)-v(z_j)|+|w(z_j)-w(x)|
 \le 2L\lVert z_j-x\rVert_2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the observed regression equivalence class has exactly one Lipschitz extension value at each \(x\in B\).  Equivalently, if the conditional mean in the statement is represented by the smooth version \(m_{t,P}|_{A_t}\), then Lipschitz continuity directly yields the ordinary side limit
\[
 \lim_{\substack{z\to x\\ z\in A_t}}
 E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y\mid X=z,T=t]
 =\lim_{\substack{z\to x\\ z\in A_t}}m_{t,P}(z)
 =m_{t,P}(x)=\theta_{t,P}(x),
\tag{6}
\]
and (4)--(5) show that (6) is an observed-law functional rather than an artifact of the selected version.

For completeness, let \(P\) and \(P'\) be two members of \(\mathrm{Base}_\kappa\) having the same observed law.  They then have the same measure \(\mu_t\) and the same observed conditional-regression equivalence class.  Applying (3)--(5) to the two Lipschitz versions \(m_{t,P}|_{A_t}\) and \(m_{t,P'}|_{A_t}\) gives
\[
 m_{t,P}(x)=m_{t,P'}(x),\qquad x\in B,\quad t\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Hence each \(\theta_{t,P}\) is identified by \(P^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Finally, by the definitions of the two boundary traces and of the causal contrast,
\[
 \tau_P(x)=\theta_{1,P}(x)-\theta_{0,P}(x)
 =m_{1,P}(x)-m_{0,P}(x),\qquad x\in B,
\]
so \(\tau_P\) is identified on \(B\), as claimed.
