\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}spatial\_\allowbreak{}slope\_\allowbreak{}infill\_\allowbreak{}phase}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Three exact affine-annihilating local slopes reduce the stated signed Mat\'ern cross-tail model to a finite-dimensional mean cone and yield uniform actual-design Gaussian inference through vanishing amplitude. On the narrow parametric Gaussian, quasi-uniform, no-nugget class, the profile construction has asymptotic observed-law coverage, root-\(N\) diameter, and root-\(N\) estimation risk. An exact complete-data Gaussian two-point argument matches the diameter and squared-risk rates; its converse transfers conditionally to every superclass that contains the same fixed-design independent Mat\'ern-one-half \(A=0\) subexperiment with a common nontrivial slope interval. The exact least-favorable critical value is mathematically attained; the sole residual open question is a terminating certified numerical approximation of its continuum maximum with a proved error bound.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published spatial-confounding results identify or consistently estimate slopes under local spectral separation, and published fixed-domain results provide inference for selected covariance functionals. They do not deliver the stated design-uniform profile inference through a cross-tail local to zero. Nor does a broad covariance class inherit this note's upper theorem merely from containing similar Mat\'ern models; the upper expansion requires the precise signed-cross-tail, Gaussian, quasi-uniform, no-nugget structure used here.

\paragraph{Niche.} The narrow upper model isolates a nonregular finite-dimensional nuisance geometry in which three scales separate \(\beta\) uniformly and affine filters control actual irregular designs in dimensions one and two. The converse is structurally more robust: it uses only an exact fixed-design independent Mat\'ern-one-half \(A=0\) subexperiment and therefore transfers to any statistical superclass once that subexperiment, the observation law, and a common nontrivial slope interval are verified.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives the actual-design Gaussian experiment, feasible residual-scale studentization, exact mathematically attained least-favorable calibration, asymptotic observed-law coverage, root-\(N\) diameter and risk, and matching honest lower bounds on the stated class. A conditional superclass corollary formalizes converse monotonicity without enlarging the scope of the upper theorem. Certified effective computation of the continuum critical value remains the sole residual open question.

\section{Related work}

Guan, Page, Reich, Ventrucci and Yang motivate high-frequency adjustment for spatial confounding, but their two-dimensional noisy/common-range or parsimonious formulations differ from the exact fixed-design independent Mat\'ern-one-half, no-nugget subexperiment used in this converse; no automatic model-class inclusion is claimed. Datta and Stein characterize consistent infill estimability and construct local-difference and Laplacian estimators over broader Gaussian-field settings. Their framework is a candidate application of the superclass lower-bound transfer only if its observation scheme, deterministic design sequence, response construction, common slope interval, and inclusion of the exact \(A=0\) subexperiment are verified from that model; this match is not asserted here. Keshavarz, Nguyen and Scott provide irregular-lattice quadratic-form Gaussian theory, Loh, Sun and Wen develop higher-order variations on selected irregular designs, and Lim and Stein analyze filtered spatial cross-periodograms; these works inform the narrow-class upper proof but do not broaden its scope. Zhang and Li supply scalar Mat\'ern microergodic context, while Andrews and Mikusheva inform weak-identification calibration. Burman, Ogburn and Datta remain the conditional PM2.5 and COVID-19 county-centroid consumer when the stated Mat\'ern, no-nugget, and quasi-uniform-design diagnostics hold.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dimension]\label{ass:dimension}
\(d\in\{1,2\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (low-dimensional fixed-domain Mat\'ern regime, \cite{Zhang2004}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:domain]\label{ass:domain}
\(D\in\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{BL}}(d,D_0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded Lipschitz fixed domain, \cite{TuoWang2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fill]\label{ass:fill}
\(c_mN^{-1/d}\le h_N\le C_mN^{-1/d}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (quasi-uniform fill-distance rate, \cite{TuoWang2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:separation]\label{ass:separation}
\(\delta_N\ge c_sh_N\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded mesh-ratio separation, \cite{TuoWang2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:slope-space]\label{ass:slope-space}
\(\beta\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact interior target parameter, \cite{AndrewsMikusheva2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exponent-space]\label{ass:exponent-space}
\(1/2<p_{\min}\le p\le p_{\max}<1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (interior Mat\'ern cross-smoothness compactum, \cite{GneitingKleiberSchlather2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:amplitude-space]\label{ass:amplitude-space}
\(A\in[-A_{\max},A_{\max}]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (signed compact cross-covariance amplitude, \cite{GneitingKleiberSchlather2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nuisance-compact]\label{ass:nuisance-compact}
\((c_X,c_W,\alpha_X,\alpha_W,\alpha_C)\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}}\Subset(0,\infty)^5\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact Mat\'ern scale and range nuisance, \cite{Li2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-field]\label{ass:gaussian-field}
\((X,W)\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stat}}(F;\mathbb R^d)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (centered stationary multivariate Gaussian field, \cite{LimStein2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:spectral-family]\label{ass:spectral-family}
\(F(\omega)=\begin{pmatrix}f_X(\omega)&f_{XW}(\omega)\\f_{XW}(\omega)&f_W(\omega)\end{pmatrix}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (signed bivariate Mat\'ern spectral family, \cite{GneitingKleiberSchlather2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:determinant-margin]\label{ass:determinant-margin}
\(f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)-f_{XW}(\omega)^2\ge\rho f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)\quad\forall\omega\in\mathbb R^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform spectral-matrix positive-definiteness margin, \cite{GneitingKleiberSchlather2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observation-law]\label{ass:observation-law}
\((X(s_{i,N}),Y(s_{i,N}))_{i=1}^N=(X(s_{i,N}),\beta X(s_{i,N})+W(s_{i,N}))_{i=1}^N\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (homogeneous Gaussian spatial regression without nugget, \cite{DattaStein2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nominal-level]\label{ass:nominal-level}
\(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate confidence level, \cite{CaiLow2004}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:model-class]\label{def:model-class}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M=\{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)_{N\ge1}: d\in\{1,2\},\ D\in\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{BL}}(d,D_0),\ c_mN^{-1/d}\le h_N\le C_mN^{-1/d},\ \delta_N\ge c_sh_N,\ \beta\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B),\ 1/2<p_{\min}\le p\le p_{\max}<1,\ A\in[-A_{\max},A_{\max}],\ (c_X,c_W,\alpha_X,\alpha_W,\alpha_C)\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}},\ (X,W)\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{stat}}(F;\mathbb R^d),\ F=(f_{k\ell})_{k,\ell\in\{X,W\}},\ f_Xf_W-f_{XW}^2\ge\rho f_Xf_W,\ (Y(s_{i,N}))_{i=1}^N=(\beta X(s_{i,N})+W(s_{i,N}))_{i=1}^N\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}dimension}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}domain}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fill}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}separation}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}slope-\allowbreak{}space}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}exponent-\allowbreak{}space}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}amplitude-\allowbreak{}space}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}nuisance-\allowbreak{}compact}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}gaussian-\allowbreak{}field}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}spectral-\allowbreak{}family}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}determinant-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}observation-\allowbreak{}law}.

\begin{definition}[def:affine-stencil-algorithm]\label{def:affine-stencil-algorithm}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{three-\allowbreak{}scale exact affine stencil algorithm}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Partition the fixed cube \(D_0\) into macrocells of side \(S Lh_N\), assign balanced groups \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), select observed sites nearest the center and axis targets at radius \(2^jLh_N\), and set \(w_{Nja}=w^0-F_{Nja}^{\mathsf T}(F_{Nja}F_{Nja}^{\mathsf T})^{-1}F_{Nja}w^0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{design}; \texttt{fill}; \texttt{Dzero}; \texttt{L}; \texttt{Sspace}.

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:affine-stencils]\label{lem:affine-stencils}
For fixed sufficiently large \(L\) and then fixed sufficiently large \(S\), the three-scale exact affine stencil algorithm produces three disjoint groups with \(|\mathcal G_{N,j}|\asymp N\), uniformly bounded weights and stencil degree, \(\sum_{\ell}w_{Nja\ell}=0\), and \(\sum_{\ell}w_{Nja\ell}s_{Nja\ell}=0\). Consequently, constant and quadratic analytic covariance terms cancel exactly, boundary loss is \(O(1)\) as a fraction of \(N\), and the normalized off-diagonal filtered marginal covariances are bounded by \(C(1+r/h_N)^{-3}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let the side length of the fixed cube \(D_0\) be \(\ell_0>0\).  All constants below may depend on \(d,c_m,C_m,c_s,\ell_0,L,S\), but not on \(N\), the admissible design, or the covariance parameters.  Put \(h=h_N\).  The fill assumption gives an observed site within distance \(h\) of every target in \(D_0\), while the separation assumption implies that a ball of radius \(h\) contains at most a constant number of sites: indeed, the disjoint balls of radius \(c_sh/2\) about such sites fit in a ball of radius \((1+c_s/2)h\).  This packing bound uses \(d\in\{1,2\}\).

Tile the subcube at distance at least \(5Lh\) from \(\partial D_0\) by cells of side \(SLh\), retaining one center in every other cell and coloring retained cells with three colors whose cardinalities differ by at most one.  Since \(h\asymp N^{-1/d}\), the number of retained centers of each color satisfies
\[
 cN\le M_{N,j}:=|\mathcal G_{N,j}|\le CN,
 \qquad j=0,1,2.
\]
The discarded boundary strip has volume \(O(h)\), hence contains \(O(h^{1-d})\) cells; its proportion among \(O(h^{-d})\) possible cells is \(O(h)\), in particular \(O(1)\).  This proves the asserted boundary statement without using values outside \(D_0\).

At a color-\(j\) center place the ideal targets consisting of the center and the \(2d\) axis points at distance \(2^jLh\), and choose a nearest observed site to every target.  If \(L>4\), distinct targets select distinct sites because two sites selected for targets separated by at least \(Lh\) cannot coincide.  If next \(S>10\), supports belonging to different retained cells are disjoint.  Thus every site is used at most once; allowing a less sparse deterministic coloring only changes this to a fixed bound.  This proves bounded stencil degree.

Use coordinates relative to the cell center and divided by \(h\).  Let \(F^0_j\) be the affine reproduction matrix of the ideal stencil and \(F_{Nja}=F^0_j+E_{Nja}\) that of the selected sites.  Every column perturbation of \(E_{Nja}\) has norm at most one.  After rescaling the coordinate rows by \((2^jL)^{-1}\), the least singular value of the ideal matrix is bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(d\).  The variational definition of the least singular value gives
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A+E)\ge \sigma_{\min}(A)-\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\]
because \(\|(A+E)x\|\ge\|Ax\|-\|Ex\|\) for every unit vector \(x\).  Hence, for all sufficiently large fixed \(L\), \(F_{Nja}F_{Nja}^{\mathsf T}\) is invertible uniformly in \(N,j,a\).  Moreover the orthogonal projection
\[
 \Pi_{Nja}=F_{Nja}^{\mathsf T}(F_{Nja}F_{Nja}^{\mathsf T})^{-1}F_{Nja}
\]
has norm one, and \(F^0_jw^0=0\).  Therefore
\[
 w_{Nja}=(I-\Pi_{Nja})w^0,
 \qquad \|w_{Nja}\|\le \|w^0\|,
 \qquad F_{Nja}w_{Nja}=0.
\]
The first row and the coordinate rows of the last equality give, respectively,
\[
 \sum_\ell w_{Nja\ell}=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_\ell w_{Nja\ell}s_{Nja\ell}=0.
\]

For two possibly different stencils with weights \(w_\ell,v_r\) and sites \(x_\ell,y_r\), the two identities imply cancellation of the constant kernel.  They also give
\[
 \sum_{\ell,r}w_\ell v_r\|x_\ell-y_r\|^2
 =\sum_{\ell,r}w_\ell v_r
   (\|x_\ell\|^2+\|y_r\|^2-2x_\ell^{\mathsf T}y_r)=0.
\]
Thus every constant and every quadratic analytic covariance term cancels exactly.

Finally let the two stencil centers have distance \(r\).  When \(r\le C_0h\), bounded weights and fixed stencil radii give \(|R_{N,ab}|\le C\).  When \(r>C_0h\), apply the second-order Taylor formula first in the first stencil coordinates and then in the second.  The zero zeroth and first moments remove all terms of order below two in each set of coordinates.  Since the fourth derivatives of \(x\mapsto\|x\|\) are bounded by \(Cr^{-3}\) away from zero, the remainder is at most \(Ch^4r^{-3}\).  Division by \(h\), as in the definition of \(R_{N,ab}\), yields
\[
 |R_{N,ab}|\le C(1+r/h)^{-3}.
\]
All bounds are uniform over the allowed designs.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the design-uniform construction that removes the \(d=2\) first-difference bias and logarithmic variance obstruction. \textit{Gap.} Loh, Sun and Wen and Keshavarz, Nguyen and Scott use selected irregular designs or prescribed preconditioners; this lemma must work for every design in \(\mathcal M\). \textit{Consumer.} It supplies boundary-safe sparse filters for the joint experiment in Theorem 1.

\begin{lemma}[lem:moment-separation]\label{lem:moment-separation}
There is a constant \(c_{\star}>0\) such that, uniformly in the allowed designs and \(p,u\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), \(\inf_{b,b'\in\mathbb R}\|a\mathbf 1+bq_N(p)-b'q_N(u)\|\ge c_{\star}|a|\) for every \(a\in\mathbb R\). On the diagonal the tangent version is \(\inf_{b,c\in\mathbb R}\|a\mathbf1+bq_N(p)+cq_N'(p)\|\ge c_{\star}|a|\). For the ideal curve \(v(p)=(1,t,t^2)^{\mathsf T}\), where \(t=2^{2p-1}\), and a second exponent \(u\) with \(r=2^{2u-1}\), the normal vector is \((tr,-t-r,1)^{\mathsf T}\); it annihilates \(v(p)\) and \(v(u)\) and its inner product with \(\mathbf1\) is \((t-1)(r-1)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 v(p)=(1,t(p),t(p)^2)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad t(p)=2^{2p-1}.
\]
The stencil perturbation in the preceding proof is relative order \(L^{-1}\).  For every pair of distinct selected sites in one stencil, its normalized distance is bounded above and below by fixed multiples of \(2^jL\).  On \([p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), differentiating \(r^{2p}\) with respect to \(p\) introduces only factors \(2\log r\).  The weight projection is also a continuously differentiable function of the coordinate perturbation on the uniformly full-rank set.  The mean-value theorem therefore gives, uniformly in the design,
\[
 \sup_p\bigl\{\|q_N(p)-v(p)\|+\|q_N'(p)-v'(p)\|\bigr\}
 \le \varepsilon_L,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_L\le C\frac{1+\log L}{L}.
\]
Here the normalization by \(P_\star(p)\) cancels the common ideal first-scale value, and homogeneity gives the ideal coordinates \(1,2^{2p-1},4^{2p-1}\).  The denominators in \(P_N\) stay positive because the ideal value is positive and the perturbation is relative order \(L^{-1}\).

For \(p,u\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), put \(t=t(p)\), \(r=t(u)\), and
\[
 n(t,r)=(tr,-t-r,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 n(t,r)^{\mathsf T}v(p)=n(t,r)^{\mathsf T}v(u)=0,
 \qquad
 n(t,r)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1=(t-1)(r-1).
\]
Because \(t(p)\in[t_0,t_1]\) with \(1<t_0\le t_1<2\), this already proves for the ideal curve
\[
 \inf_{b,b'}\|a\mathbf 1+bv(p)-b'v(u)\|
 \ge
 \frac{(t_0-1)^2}{\sqrt{t_1^4+4t_1^2+1}}|a|.
\]

To make the perturbation uniform when \(u\to p\), define
\[
 d_N(p,u)=
 \begin{cases}
 \{q_N(u)-q_N(p)\}/(u-p),&u\ne p,\\
 q_N'(p),&u=p.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the ideal curve,
\[
 \det[\mathbf 1,v(p),d(p,u)]
 =(t-1)(r-1)\frac{r-t}{u-p},
\]
with diagonal value \(t'(p)(t-1)^2\).  Since
\[
 t'(p)=2(\log 2)t(p)\ge 2(\log2)t_0,
\]
the absolute determinant has a positive lower bound on the compact square, including its diagonal.  The displayed \(C^1\) approximation implies uniform convergence of divided differences, so for a sufficiently large fixed \(L\),
\[
 \inf_{p,u}
 \frac{|\det[\mathbf 1,q_N(p),d_N(p,u)]|}
      {\|q_N(p)\times d_N(p,u)\|}>0.
\]
The quotient is precisely the Euclidean distance from \(\mathbf 1\) to \(\operatorname{span}\{q_N(p),d_N(p,u)\}\).  For \(u\ne p\), this span equals \(\operatorname{span}\{q_N(p),q_N(u)\}\); on the diagonal it is the tangent span.  Multiplying the resulting distance bound by \(|a|\) proves both asserted inequalities.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Uniform secant and tangent separation identifies the slope even when the tail amplitude is zero and \(p\) disappears. \textit{Gap.} Neither spectral-adjustment identification nor scalar quadratic variation contains this three-scale divided-difference modulus on perturbed designs. \textit{Consumer.} It turns moment-space Gaussian radius bounds into root-\(N\) confidence-set diameter and risk bounds.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-experiment]\label{thm:uniform-experiment}
Uniformly over \((P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M\), \(\sqrt N\{m_N-\beta\mathbf 1-B_Nq_N(p)\}\) is approximated by \(N_3(0,\gamma H_N)\) in bounded-Lipschitz distance, \(0<c\le\lambda_{\min}(H_N)\le\lambda_{\max}(H_N)\le C<\infty\), and \(\widehat\gamma_N/\gamma\to1\) in probability. The approximation is uniform for signed \(A_N\), including \(A_N=0\), local \(\sqrt N B_N\), diverging \(|\sqrt N B_N|\), and \(p_N\) approaching either fixed endpoint. The proof bounds the exact finite covariance matrices and explicitly makes the boundary, sampling alias, denominator-linearization, and Mat\'ern Bessel remainders \(o(N^{-1/2})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(h=h_N\), and index all retained stencils by \(a\).  Let \(\Gamma_X,\Gamma_W,\Gamma_C\) denote the covariance matrices of the filtered vectors formed from \(X,W\), and their cross-covariance.  The spectral assumptions and radial Fourier inversion give, uniformly over the compact nuisance set and \(p\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), the small-distance expansions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_X(r)&=C_X(0)-c_Xk_dr+c_{X,2}r^2+O(r^3),\\
 C_W(r)&=C_W(0)-c_Wk_dr+c_{W,2}r^2+O(r^3),\\
 C_{XW}(r)&=C_{XW}(0)-Ak_{p,d}r^{2p}+c_{C,2}r^2
 +O\bigl(|A|(r^{2p+2}+r^4)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, insert
\[
 (\alpha^2+\|\omega\|^2)^{-a}
 =\Gamma(a)^{-1}\int_0^\infty t^{a-1}
 e^{-t(\alpha^2+\|\omega\|^2)}\,dt
\]
and evaluate the Gaussian Fourier integral.  This gives the defining integral for a constant multiple of \(r^{a-d/2}K_{a-d/2}(\alpha r)\).  Splitting that integral at one and expanding the exponential on each part gives the analytic even-power branch and the nonanalytic \(r^{2a-d}\) branch, with remainders uniform when \(a-d/2=p\) lies in the stated compact subinterval of \((1/2,1)\).  At \(p=1/2\) the same integral evaluates directly to an exponential.  The coefficients of the nonanalytic branches are exactly the declared \(k_d\) and \(k_{p,d}\), and the next powers are those displayed above.

The affine moment identities annihilate the constant and \(r^2\) terms.  Taylor's formula away from the diagonal, followed by shell counting of centers separated by a fixed multiple of \(h\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_X&=c_Xk_dhR_N+E_{X,N},&
 \|E_{X,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}&=o(h),\\
 \Gamma_W&=c_Wk_dhR_N+E_{W,N},&
 \|E_{W,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}&=o(h),\\
 \Gamma_C&=Ak_{p,d}h^{2p}S_N(p)+E_{C,N},&
 \|\Gamma_C\|_{\mathrm{op}}/h&=o(1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(S_N(p)_{ab}=-\sum_{\ell,r}w_{Na\ell}w_{Nbr}
\|(s_{Na\ell}-s_{Nbr})/h\|^{2p}\).  To verify the orders, the principal marginal kernel has fourth derivative \(O(r^{-3})\), giving the summable lattice envelope \((1+r/h)^{-3}\).  After cancellation, the marginal remainder has far-field size \(O(h^4/r)\); divided by its leading scale \(h\), its row sum is \(O(h^2\log(1/h))\) for \(d=1\) and \(O(h)\) for \(d=2\).  The principal cross kernel has far-field size \(O(h^4r^{2p-4})\); its normalized shell sum is bounded uniformly because \(p_{\max}<1\).  Its total scale relative to \(h\) is \(h^{2p-1}=o(1)\).  The cross remainder is smaller.  These are calculations on the exact finite covariance matrices, so no Fourier-coordinate independence or unaccounted aliasing approximation is used.

The diagonal entries of \(R_N\) are bounded above and below by positive constants times the fixed stencil radius.  By increasing the fixed macrocell spacing \(S\), the sum of its off-diagonal entries in every row is less than half the corresponding diagonal.  Thus, for constants independent of \(N\),
\[
 0<c\le\lambda_{\min}(R_N)\le\lambda_{\max}(R_N)\le C.
\]
The same diagonal-versus-off-diagonal calculation, now applied to the squared entries and using \(|\mathcal G_{N,j}|\asymp N\), gives
\[
 0<c\le\lambda_{\min}(H_N)le\lambda_{\max}(H_N)\le C.
\]

Let \(\mu_{N,j}=E D_{N,j}\) and \(\kappa_{N,j}=E\sum_{a\in\mathcal G_{N,j}}U_{Nja}W_{Nja}\).  Taking traces in the covariance expansions gives
\[
 \mu_{N,j}=c_Xk_dh\sum_{a\in\mathcal G_{N,j}}R_{N,aa}\{1+o(N^{-1/2})\}
\]
at the trace-ratio accuracy needed below, and
\[
 \frac{\kappa_{N,j}}{\mu_{N,j}}
 =\frac{A}{c_X}\frac{k_{p,d}}{k_d}h^{2p-1}P_{N,j}(p)+o(N^{-1/2})
 =B_Nq_{N,j}(p)+o(N^{-1/2}).
\]
Indeed, the per-stencil cross remainder divided by the marginal leading term is \(O(|A|(h^{2p+1}+h^3))\).  Multiplication by \(\sqrt N\), using \(h\asymp N^{-1/d}\) and \(d\le2\), gives \(O(h^{2p}+h^2)=o(1)\) in the least favorable case \(d=2\).  The marginal trace remainder only multiplies the principal bias \(B_N=O(h^{2p_{\min}-1})=o(1)\), and is of the same smaller order.  This explicitly controls the Mat\'ern remainder and the deterministic denominator expansion.

Set \(r_{N,j}=\kappa_{N,j}/\mu_{N,j}\) and
\[
 Z_{N,j}=\frac{\sqrt N}{\mu_{N,j}}
 \left[\sum_{a\in\mathcal G_{N,j}}U_{Nja}W_{Nja}
       -r_{N,j}(D_{N,j}-\mu_{N,j})-\kappa_{N,j}\right].
\]
For centered jointly Gaussian scalars, differentiating
\(E\exp(t^{\mathsf T}Z)=\exp(t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t/2)\) four times at zero gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(U_aW_a,U_bW_b)
 =\operatorname{Cov}(U_a,U_b)\operatorname{Cov}(W_a,W_b)
 +\operatorname{Cov}(U_a,W_b)\operatorname{Cov}(W_a,U_b).
\]
Applying this identity to the covariance expansions implies
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_N)=\gamma H_N+o(1).
\]
Terms involving \(\Gamma_C\) or \(r_{N,j}\) are \(o(1)\) because
\(\|\Gamma_C\|_{\mathrm{op}}/h=o(1)\) and \(\max_j|r_{N,j}|=O(h^{2p_{\min}-1})=o(1)\).  The remaining Gaussian fourth-moment contraction is
\[
 N\frac{c_Xc_W(k_dh)^2
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal G_{N,j},\,b\in\mathcal G_{N,k}}R_{N,ab}^2}
 {(c_Xk_dh\sum_{a\in\mathcal G_{N,j}}R_{N,aa})
  (c_Xk_dh\sum_{b\in\mathcal G_{N,k}}R_{N,bb})}
 =\gamma H_{N,jk}.
\]

For any unit vector \(u\in\mathbb R^3\), \(u^{\mathsf T}Z_N\) is a centered quadratic form in a finite standard Gaussian vector.  If its symmetric coefficient matrix is \(A_{N,u}\), the preceding operator bounds and \(|\mathcal G_{N,j}|\asymp N\) give
\[
 \|A_{N,u}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le CN^{-1/2},
 \qquad c\le2\|A_{N,u}\|_F^2\le C.
\]
Diagonalizing \(A_{N,u}\), its characteristic function is a product of those of \(\lambda_{k,N}(Z_k^2-1)\).  Taylor expansion of the logarithm has remainder bounded by
\(C|t|^3\|A_{N,u}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|A_{N,u}\|_F^2=o(1)\), uniformly for bounded \(t\) and unit \(u\).  Hence every linear combination converges uniformly to its centered Gaussian law with the covariance just displayed.  A finite net of the unit sphere, together with the uniform second-moment bound, upgrades this calculation to
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\bigl(\mathcal L(Z_N),N_3(0,\gamma H_N)\bigr)\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly over the model class.

The eigenvalue bounds on \(\Gamma_X/h\) imply, after diagonalization,
\[
 E\left|D_{N,j}/\mu_{N,j}-1\right|^4\le CN^{-2},
 \qquad
 E\left(\mu_{N,j}/D_{N,j}\right)^k\le C_k
\]
for every fixed \(k\) and all sufficiently large \(N\).  Therefore the exact identity
\[
 m_{N,j}-\beta-r_{N,j}
 =\frac{\mu_{N,j}}{D_{N,j}}\frac{Z_{N,j}}{\sqrt N}
\]
shows that replacing \(D_{N,j}\) by \(\mu_{N,j}\) contributes \(o_P(N^{-1/2})\), uniformly and also in \(L^2\).  Combining this with the trace-ratio display proves the asserted Gaussian approximation for \(m_N\).

It remains to verify feasibility.  Summing the same quadratic-form bounds over all groups gives
\[
 \widehat c_X=c_X+o_P(1),
 \qquad \sup E|\widehat c_X-c_X|^2=o(1).
\]
The pilot satisfies \(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta=O_P(B_N+N^{-1/2})=o_P(1)\).  Expanding the residual quadratic variation yields
\[
 \widehat c_W-c_W
 =O_P(N^{-1/2})
 -2(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)O_P(B_N)
 +(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2O_P(1)+o_P(1)=o_P(1).
\]
Projection onto fixed intervals containing the compact ranges of \(c_X,c_W\) is continuous at every admissible value and keeps the denominator away from zero.  Consequently \(\widehat\gamma_N/\gamma\to1\) uniformly in probability.  Every bound used only \(|A|\le A_{\max}\) and compact endpoint bounds for \(p\); it therefore remains uniform for signed, zero, local, diverging-local, and endpoint-approaching triangular sequences.  This completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The uniform actual-design experiment is the statistical kernel absent from the existing identification and fixed-domain covariance literature. \textit{Gap.} Lim and Stein prove filtered lattice cross-periodogram normality, and Keshavarz et al. prove scalar irregular-lattice quadratic-form normality; neither result covers these ratio moments, arbitrary quasi-uniform geometry, or weak disappearance of \(p\). \textit{Consumer.} It converts Guan et al.'s local-scale identifying idea into feasible frequentist inference without nugget variation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:profile-inference]\label{thm:profile-inference}
The studentized cone-profile confidence set \(C_N\) and estimator \(\widehat\beta_N\) satisfy \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}P_N\{\beta\in C_N\}\ge1-\alpha\), \(\sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(C_N)]\le C_{\alpha}N^{-1/2}\), and \(\sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}E_{P_N}[(\widehat\beta_N-\beta)^2]\le C N^{-1}\). These relations hold through every sequence \(A_N\to0\) without consistently estimating \(p\) or any Mat\'ern range.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the true slope, write
\[
 e_N=m_N-\beta\mathbf1-B_Nq_N(p).
\]
The cone \(\mathcal C_N\) contains the whole line \(\{bq_N(p):b\in\mathbb R\}\).  Hence
\[
 T_N(\beta)
 \le \frac{N}{\widehat\gamma_N}
 \inf_b\|e_N-bq_N(p)\|_{H_N}^2,
 \qquad \|x\|_{H_N}^2=x^{\mathsf T}H_N^{-1}x.
\]
Under the Gaussian reference law, the right-hand side with \(e_N=\sqrt{\gamma/N}G_N\) and \(\widehat\gamma_N=\gamma\) is the squared norm of the projection of a three-dimensional standard Gaussian vector onto the two-dimensional orthocomplement of \(H_N^{-1/2}q_N(p)\).  It therefore has the \(\chi^2_2\) law.  The uniform bounded-Lipschitz approximation, scale consistency, and continuity of the \(\chi^2_2\) distribution at \(c_\alpha\) imply
\[
 \liminf_N\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 P_N\{T_N(\beta)\le c_\alpha\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
This argument uses domination by the true nuisance line and does not require identification or estimation of \(p\) when \(A=0\).

For the diameter, take \(b_1,b_2\in C_N\).  For arbitrary \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(z_i\in\mathcal C_N\) with
\[
 \|m_N-b_i\mathbf1-z_i\|_{H_N}
 \le \sqrt{\widehat\gamma_Nc_\alpha/N}+\varepsilon,
 \qquad i=1,2.
\]
The triangle inequality and the upper eigenvalue bound on \(H_N\) give
\[
 \|(b_1-b_2)\mathbf1+z_1-z_2\|
 \le2\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(H_N)}
 \left(\sqrt{\widehat\gamma_Nc_\alpha/N}+\varepsilon\right).
\]
The moment-separation lemma, applied to the two representations of \(z_1,z_2\), bounds the left side below by \(c_\star|b_1-b_2|\).  Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), and using clipping to bound \(\widehat\gamma_N\), yields the pathwise inequality
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(C_N)
 \le \frac{2}{c_\star}
 \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(H_N)\widehat\gamma_Nc_\alpha/N}
 \le C_\alpha N^{-1/2}.
\]
It remains valid for an empty set, whose diameter is defined as zero.  Taking expectations proves the claimed expected-diameter bound.

Let \(\widehat z_N\in\mathcal C_N\) attain the cone distance at \(\widehat\beta_N\); existence follows after analytically minimizing the line loading and compactifying the remaining exponent interval, while coercivity in the line loading excludes escape to infinity.  Since the true pair \((\beta,B_Nq_N(p))\) is feasible for the minimization,
\[
 \|m_N-\widehat\beta_N\mathbf1-\widehat z_N\|_{H_N}
 \le \|e_N\|_{H_N}.
\]
Another triangle inequality, the eigenvalue bounds, and moment separation give
\[
 |\widehat\beta_N-\beta|
 \le C\|e_N\|_{H_N}.
\]
The proof of the uniform experiment supplied the \(L^2\) bound
\(\sup_P E_P\|e_N\|_{H_N}^2\le C/N\).  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}(\widehat\beta_N-\beta)^2\le C/N.
\]
All constants are uniform in \(A,p\) and the marginal ranges.  In particular the conclusions hold along every \(A_N\to0\), although \(p\) need not be consistently estimable there.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It shows that adaptation across the singular face \(A=0\) costs neither consistency nor the parametric rate. \textit{Gap.} Datta and Stein establish consistent estimability and local-difference estimators but do not provide uniform coverage, expected diameter, or risk through a vanishing signed cross-tail. \textit{Consumer.} Conditional on the Gaussian Mat\'ern and quasi-uniform diagnostics, it provides an honest homogeneous-slope analysis for the county-centroid PM2.5 and COVID-19 setting of Burman, Ogburn and Datta.

\begin{proposition}[prop:ou-reduction]\label{prop:ou-reduction}
When \(d=1\), \(D=[0,1]\), the sites are equally spaced, the stencil radii are integer lattice multiples, and \(|\mathcal G_{N,j}|/N\to\eta_j>0\), the affine-stencil construction satisfies \(q_N(p)\to(1,2^{2p-1},4^{2p-1})^{\mathsf T}\) and \(H_N\to\operatorname{diag}(\eta_0^{-1},\eta_1^{-1},\eta_2^{-1})\). In particular, if the balanced construction has common limiting stencil fraction \(\eta\), then \(H_N\to\eta^{-1}I_3\). The profile construction remains nonsingular at \(A=0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the equally spaced one-dimensional lattice, take \(L\) and the three radii \(2^jL\) to be integers.  Integer lattice radii and translation-equivariant tie-breaking make every selected triplet a common lattice translate of its target triplet, so \(F_{Nja}w^0=0\) and the projection formula gives \(w_{Nja}=w^0=(-2,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  Homogeneity of the power kernels yields exactly
\[
 q_N(p)=(1,2^{2p-1},4^{2p-1})^{\mathsf T}
\]
apart from the vanishing integer end-cell discrepancy.

For two affine-annihilating one-dimensional stencils whose support intervals are disjoint, \(|x-y|\) is affine separately in \(x\) and \(y\) on their product.  Hence its double filtered sum is exactly zero.  The spacing choice in the affine-stencil algorithm makes distinct supports disjoint, so
\[
 R_{N,ab}=0\quad(a\ne b),
 \qquad R_{N,aa}=4(2^jL)\quad(a\in\mathcal G_{N,j}).
\]
Substitution in the definition of \(H_N\) gives
\[
 H_{N,jk}=0\quad(j\ne k),
 \qquad H_{N,jj}=\frac{N}{|\mathcal G_{N,j}|}.
\]
Thus, if \(|\mathcal G_{N,j}|/N\to\eta_j>0\), then
\[
 H_N\longrightarrow\operatorname{diag}(\eta_0^{-1},\eta_1^{-1},\eta_2^{-1}).
\]
The balanced three-color construction has \(\eta_0=\eta_1=\eta_2=:\eta>0\), and hence \(H_N\to\eta^{-1}I_3\).  The value of \(\eta\) records the fixed macrocell spacing and the retained information fraction.  Finally the determinant computation in the moment-separation lemma shows that \(\mathbf1\) stays uniformly outside every secant and tangent nuisance plane, so profiling remains nonsingular at \(A=0\).  This is the actual Ornstein--Uhlenbeck lattice reduction of the frozen affine-stencil algorithm.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction is the required sanity check and fixes the covariance and mean normalizations in a fully tractable special case. \textit{Gap.} Known two-scale cancellation assumes \(p\) known; the three-scale profiled reduction remains regular for \(\beta\) when \(p\) is unidentified. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a reproducible benchmark for implementations and for checking all constants in the general-design theorem.

\begin{lemma}[lem:attainable-regimes]\label{lem:attainable-regimes}
Assume that the model class is nonempty and put
\[
 R_N(p)=\sqrt N\,h_N^{2p-1}.
\]
Then \(0<\rho\le1\), and every \((\lambda,p)\in\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\) satisfies
\[
 \lambda=\frac{\sqrt N B_N}{\sqrt\gamma}
 =\frac{A K_d(p)}{\sqrt{c_Xc_W}}R_N(p),
 \qquad |\lambda|\le C R_N(p).
\]
If \(0<\rho<1\), there is \(c>0\), independent of \(N\), the design, and \(p\), such that
\[
 [-cR_N(p),cR_N(p)]\times\{p\}
 \subseteq\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}
 \subseteq[-CR_N(p),CR_N(p)]\times\{p\}.
\]
If \(\rho=1\), instead \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}=\{0\}\times[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\).  Under strict slack \(0<\rho<1\), the following regimes result.  For \(d=1\), fixed \(p<3/4\) permits bounded local sequences and diverging sequences of either sign up to the physical order \(R_N(p)\), while every fixed sufficiently small nonzero \(A\) gives \(|\lambda|\to\infty\); at \(p=3/4\) all physical \(\lambda\)'s are bounded; and for fixed \(p>3/4\), \(\sup|\lambda|\to0\).  If
\[
 p_N=\frac34+\frac{z_N}{\log N},\qquad |z_N|\le C_z,
\]
then, with \(\kappa_N=Nh_N\),
\[
 R_N(p_N)=\kappa_N^{1/2+2z_N/\log N}e^{-2z_N},
\]
so the entire logarithmic transition window is bounded above and away from zero.  For \(d=2\), \(R_N(p)\to\infty\) uniformly over \(p\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\); hence every fixed exponent permits bounded local sequences and sub-capacity diverging sequences, including such sequences generated by \(A_N\to0\), and every fixed sufficiently small nonzero \(A\) produces divergence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The definition of \(B_N\) and \(\gamma=c_W/c_X\) gives, without approximation,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt N B_N}{\sqrt\gamma}
 =\sqrt N\frac{A}{c_X}K_d(p)h_N^{2p-1}
   \sqrt{\frac{c_X}{c_W}}
 =\frac{AK_d(p)}{\sqrt{c_Xc_W}}R_N(p).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Compactness of \([p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), positivity and continuity of \(K_d\), and compactness of \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}}\Subset(0,\infty)^5\) imply constants \(0<k_-\le k_+<\infty\) such that
\[
 k_-\le \frac{K_d(p)}{\sqrt{c_Xc_W}}\le k_+.                                \tag{2}
\]
Together with \(|A|\le A_{\max}\), (1)--(2) prove the outer bound.

For the inner bound, first expose the premise supplied by the determinant condition.  At radial frequency \(r=\|\omega\|\), that condition is equivalent to
\[
 A^2\le(1-\rho)\,
 c_Xc_W\frac{(\alpha_C^2+r^2)^{2p+d}}
 { (\alpha_X^2+r^2)^{(d+1)/2}(\alpha_W^2+r^2)^{(d+1)/2}}.                    \tag{3}
\]
Since the left side of the determinant inequality is at most \(f_Xf_W\), nonemptiness gives \(\rho\le1\); positivity of \(\rho\) was declared.  If \(\rho=1\), (3) forces \(A=0\), proving the asserted degenerate case.

Suppose now that \(0<\rho<1\).  The ratio on the right of (3), before multiplication by \(1-\rho\), is continuous and positive in the nuisance tuple, \(p\), and \(r\).  As \(r\to\infty\), it is asymptotic, uniformly on the compact parameter set, to \(c_Xc_W r^{4p-2}\).  Because \(4p_{\min}-2>0\), it tends to infinity uniformly.  Its infimum over \(r\ge0\), the nuisance compactum, and the exponent interval is therefore a number \(r_*>0\).  Thus every
\[
 |A|\le a_*:=\min\left\{\frac{A_{\max}}2,
          \frac12\sqrt{(1-\rho)r_*}\right\}>0                              \tag{4}
\]
satisfies (3), with either sign, for every permitted nuisance tuple and exponent.  Varying \(A\) over the interval in (4), and applying (1)--(2), gives the inner inclusion.

It remains only to calculate \(R_N\).  If \(d=1\), write \(h_N=\kappa_NN^{-1}\), where the fill-distance assumption gives \(c_m\le\kappa_N\le C_m\).  Then
\[
 R_N(p)=\kappa_N^{2p-1}N^{3/2-2p}.                                           \tag{5}
\]
The exponent in (5) is positive, zero, or negative according as \(p<3/4\), \(p=3/4\), or \(p>3/4\).  The two-sided physical inclusions convert these three alternatives into the claimed attainable regimes.  In particular, any prescribed \(\lambda_N=o(R_N(p))\) is obtained from (1) by choosing
\[
 A_N=\lambda_N\frac{\sqrt{c_Xc_W}}{K_d(p)R_N(p)},                             \tag{6}
\]
which tends to zero and eventually obeys (4).  For the transition sequence in the statement, substitution into (5) yields the exact identity
\[
 R_N(p_N)
 =\kappa_N^{1/2+2z_N/\log N}N^{-2z_N/\log N}
 =\kappa_N^{1/2+2z_N/\log N}e^{-2z_N}.                                       \tag{7}
\]
The bounds on \(\kappa_N\) and \(z_N\) prove the transition assertion.

If \(d=2\), write \(h_N=\kappa_NN^{-1/2}\), with \(c_m\le\kappa_N\le C_m\).  Now
\[
 R_N(p)=\kappa_N^{2p-1}N^{1-p}
 \ge cN^{1-p_{\max}}\longrightarrow\infty                                   \tag{8}
\]
uniformly in \(p\).  Equations (4), (6), and (8) prove all stated two-dimensional regimes.  This also shows why the elbow is physical rather than an artifact of differentiating \(h_N^{2p-1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:profile-geometry-compactification]\label{lem:profile-geometry-compactification}
For the allowed exact affine-stencil designs, the family \(\{q_N\}\) is bounded in \(C^2([p_{\min},p_{\max}];\mathbb R^3)\), and the matrices \(H_N\) range over a compact subset of the positive-definite cone.  Every sequence of translated profile cones
\[
 \mathcal C_N-\lambda_Nq_N(p_N)
\]
has a subsequence converging locally in Hausdorff distance either to a translated limiting cone when \(\lambda_N\) is bounded, or, when \(|\lambda_N|\to\infty\), to a limiting tangent plane or an endpoint tangent half-plane of the form
\[
 \{a q(p_0)+tq'(p_0):a\in\mathbb R,\ t\in I\},
\]
where \(I=\mathbb R\) or \(I\) is a closed half-line; finite values of \(|\lambda_N|\operatorname{dist}(p_N,\{p_{\min},p_{\max}\})\) give the half-line offsets.  For every finite \(M\), these limits imply the uniform Gaussian anti-concentration relation
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\ 
 \sup_{N,\mathcal S_N,\lambda,p}\ \sup_{0\le x\le M}
 \Pr\!\left(\left|d_{H_N}(G_N+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)^2-x\right|
 \le\varepsilon\right)=0.                                                    \tag{9}
\]
The corresponding quantile functions extend continuously, and hence uniformly continuously, to this bounded-amplitude/tangent/endpoint compactification, including nonconvergent allowed design sequences.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All normalized within-stencil distances are bounded above by a fixed constant and, off the diagonal, bounded away from zero by the separation assumption.  The proof of the affine-stencil lemma gives uniformly bounded weights, fixed stencil cardinality, and denominators bounded away from zero.  Differentiating a nonzero term \(r^{2p}\) zero, one, or two times introduces only the bounded factors \(1\), \(2\log r\), and \(4(\log r)^2\); diagonal terms are identically zero.  The quotient rule and positivity of \(P_\star\) therefore give
\[
 \sup_N\sup_{p\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]}
 \bigl(\|q_N(p)\|+\|q_N'(p)\|+\|q_N''(p)\|\bigr)<\infty.                 \tag{10}
\]
The eigenvalue bounds in the uniform-experiment theorem similarly place \(H_N\) in a compact subset of the positive-definite cone.

For completeness, (10) itself gives the needed sequential compactness without importing an empirical-process theorem.  On each finite rational grid, boundedness permits a convergent subsequence.  A diagonal choice over successively finer grids, together with
\[
 \|q_N'(u)-q_N'(v)\|\le C|u-v|,
 \qquad
 q_N(v)-q_N(u)=\int_u^v q_N'(r)\,dr,                                         \tag{11}
\]
extends the grid limits uniformly to a continuously differentiable curve \(q\), with \(q_N\to q\) in \(C^1\).  A further subsequence has \(H_N\to H\), where \(H\) remains positive definite.

The divided-difference argument in the moment-separation lemma also yields constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
 c\le \|q_N(p)\|\le C,
 \qquad
 \|q_N(p)\times d_N(p,u)\|\ge c,
 \quad
 d_N(p,u)=
 \begin{cases}
 \{q_N(u)-q_N(p)\}/(u-p),&u\ne p,\\
 q_N'(p),&u=p.
 \end{cases}                                                                 \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, the determinant of \([\mathbf1,q_N(p),d_N(p,u)]\) is uniformly separated from zero and all three columns are bounded.  In particular,
\[
 \|q_N(p)\times q_N(u)\|\ge c|p-u|.                                         \tag{13}
\]

Consider first a bounded sequence \(\lambda_N\).  After taking subsequences, \(\lambda_N\to\lambda\), \(p_N\to p_0\), and (11) holds.  Coercivity from the first bound in (12) shows that points of \(\mathcal C_N\) lying in a fixed ball have bounded line loading.  Uniform convergence of \(q_N\) then proves local Hausdorff convergence
\[
 \mathcal C_N-\lambda_Nq_N(p_N)
 \longrightarrow
 \{bq(r):b\in\mathbb R,\ r\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\}
 -\lambda q(p_0).                                                            \tag{14}
\]

Now suppose \(|\lambda_N|\to\infty\), and take a subsequence on which its sign is constant and \(p_N\to p_0\).  If
\[
 b_Nq_N(r_N)-\lambda_Nq_N(p_N)                                               \tag{15}
\]
stays bounded, (12)--(13) first imply \(|b_N|\asymp|\lambda_N|\) and \(|r_N-p_N|=O(|\lambda_N|^{-1})\).  Writing \(a_N=b_N-\lambda_N\) and \(t_N=b_N(r_N-p_N)\), and using the divided difference in (12), (15) becomes
\[
 a_Nq_N(p_N)+t_Nd_N(p_N,r_N).                                                \tag{16}
\]
The linear independence in (12) makes both \(a_N\) and \(t_N\) bounded whenever (16) is bounded.  Conversely, every convergent allowed pair \((a_N,t_N)\) in (16) is obtained by taking \(b_N=\lambda_N+a_N\) and \(r_N=p_N+t_N/b_N\).  Thus the local limit is
\[
 \{a q(p_0)+tq'(p_0):a\in\mathbb R,\ t\in I\}.                            \tag{17}
\]
If both scaled endpoint distances diverge, \(I=\mathbb R\).  If, for example, \(\lambda_N>0\), \(p_N\to p_{\min}\), and \(\lambda_N(p_N-p_{\min})\to\tau<\infty\), then the constraint \(r_N\ge p_{\min}\) becomes \(t\ge-\tau\).  The upper endpoint and negative-sign cases reverse the appropriate inequality.  These are exactly the asserted tangent half-planes.  Equations (14)--(17) prove compactification, including endpoint approaches of order \(|\lambda_N|^{-1}\).

We next verify atomlessness rather than assume it.  Every translated cone in (14) is the image of the two-dimensional parameterization \((b,r)\mapsto bq(r)-\lambda q(p_0)\); on bounded \(b\)-sets this map is Lipschitz.  Hence it has three-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero, as follows directly by covering its parameter rectangle by \(O(\delta^{-2})\) squares whose images lie in balls of radius \(C\delta\), of total volume \(O(\delta)\).  The planes and half-planes in (17) also have zero volume.

Fix any one of these closed sets \(K\) and a positive-definite metric \(H\).  For \(r>0\), a point at distance \(r\) from a regular point of the parameterized surface must lie on a metric-normal line, by differentiating the squared distance in its two parameter directions.  Such points are covered, on bounded pieces away from the cone vertex and parameter endpoints, by countably many two-dimensional Lipschitz offset charts.  Nearest points at the vertex are covered by a metric sphere, and nearest points at an endpoint edge are covered by two-dimensional normal half-sphere charts.  The same \(O(\delta)\) covering argument gives
\[
 \operatorname{Leb}_3\{x:d_H(x,K)=r\}=0,
 \qquad r\ge0.                                                               \tag{18}
\]
The case \(r=0\) was proved in the preceding paragraph.  Because a nondegenerate Gaussian has a Lebesgue density, its squared distance to \(K\) has no atoms.

The distribution is also strictly increasing on its positive support.  Each limit set contains a point, whereas moment separation puts the direction \(\mathbf1\) a positive distance from every secant or tangent direction.  Consequently distance along a suitable ray starting in the set is continuous, begins at zero, and tends to infinity.  Every nonempty open shell between two distance levels therefore has positive Gaussian probability.

Finally, if (9) failed, there would be \(\varepsilon_n\downarrow0\), levels \(x_n\in[0,M]\), and a sequence of geometries for which the displayed probability stayed above a fixed positive number.  Take the compactifying subsequence just constructed and also \(x_n\to x\).  Couple all Gaussian vectors as \(G_n=H_n^{1/2}Z\) with one \(Z\sim N_3(0,I_3)\).  Local Hausdorff convergence and Gaussian tightness imply
\[
 d_{H_n}(G_n,K_n)^2\longrightarrow d_H(H^{1/2}Z,K)^2
 \quad\text{in probability}.                                                 \tag{19}
\]
For any fixed \(\eta>0\), (19) bounds the offending probability by \(o(1)+\Pr(|d_H(H^{1/2}Z,K)^2-x|\le\eta)\).  Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) contradicts (18).  This proves (9).  The same coupling, atomlessness, and strict increase show that quantiles converge along every compactifying sequence.  Sequential compactness then makes the extension uniformly continuous.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-design-attainable-quantile]\label{lem:finite-design-attainable-quantile}
For each fixed \(N\) and allowed observed design, \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\) is compact.  The law of
\[
 d_{H_N}(G_N+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)^2
\]
is atomless and has a strictly increasing distribution function on \((0,\infty)\); its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is continuous in \((\lambda,p)\).  Hence the maximum defining \(c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\) is attained and
\[
 \inf_{(\lambda,p)\in\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}}
 \Pr\{d_{H_N}(G_N+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)^2
       \le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\}=1-\alpha.                           \tag{20}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 0<c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\le\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha},                       \tag{21}
\]
and both bounds are uniform over the allowed designs after compactification.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(N\) and an allowed design.  Let \(\Theta_N\) be the subset of
\[
[-A_{\max},A_{\max}]\times[p_{\min},p_{\max}]
   \times\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}}
\]
on which the determinant-margin inequality holds at every \(\omega\in\mathbb R^d\).  The displayed product is compact by the amplitude, exponent, and nuisance-compactness assumptions.  For each fixed \(\omega\), the determinant-margin inequality is a closed condition in the coordinates of this product, because the three spectra are continuous functions of those coordinates.  Hence \(\Theta_N\), an intersection of closed subsets of a compact set, is compact.  The ideal stencil contains finitely many fixed distances, so its power-law moment is a finite linear combination of functions \(r^{2p}\).  Thus \(P_\star(p)\), and hence \(K_d(p)\), is continuous on the exponent interval; positivity is part of their definitions.  Since \(h_N,c_X,c_W>0\), the map
\[
(A,p,c_X,c_W,\alpha_X,\alpha_W,\alpha_C)
 \longmapsto
 \left(\frac{A K_d(p)\sqrt N h_N^{2p-1}}{\sqrt{c_Xc_W}},p\right)             \tag{22}
\]
is continuous.  By the definition of the physically attainable set, its image is \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\).  An allowed model supplies at least one legal tuple, so this image is nonempty.  It is therefore compact.

Set
\[
S_{N,\lambda,p}=d_{H_N}(G_N+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)^2.
\]
Write \(F_{N,\lambda,p}\) for the distribution function of \(S_{N,\lambda,p}\).  The profile-geometry compactification lemma gives
\[
F_{N,\lambda,p}(0)=0,
\qquad F_{N,\lambda,p}\text{ is continuous on }[0,\infty),
\qquad F_{N,\lambda,p}\text{ is strictly increasing on }(0,\infty).         \tag{23}
\]
In particular the quantile
\[
k_N(\lambda,p)=\inf\{x\ge0:F_{N,\lambda,p}(x)\ge1-\alpha\}                 \tag{24}
\]
is positive, unique, and satisfies
\[
F_{N,\lambda,p}(k_N(\lambda,p))=1-\alpha.                                \tag{25}
\]
If \((\lambda_r,p_r)\to(\lambda,p)\), use the same Gaussian vector \(G_N\) for all indices.  Continuity of \(q_N\) and the fact that distance to a fixed closed set is continuous give
\[
S_{N,\lambda_r,p_r}\longrightarrow S_{N,\lambda,p}
\quad\text{almost surely}.                                                    \tag{26}
\]
Equation (23) and (26) imply pointwise convergence of the corresponding distribution functions.  If the quantiles in (24) failed to converge to \(k_N(\lambda,p)\), a subsequence would lie on one side of a fixed intermediate point.  Evaluating the distribution functions at that point and using (23) would contradict (24)--(25).  Thus \(k_N\) is continuous.  Compactness of \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\) now supplies a maximizer \((\lambda_*,p_*)\), and
\[
c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}=k_N(\lambda_*,p_*).                              \tag{27}
\]
For every \((\lambda,p)\) in the physical set, (25) and (27) give
\[
F_{N,\lambda,p}(c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}})\ge1-\alpha,
\]
while equality holds at \((\lambda_*,p_*)\).  This proves (20).

For the upper bound, the cone contains the line \(\{a q_N(p):a\in\mathbb R\}\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{N,\lambda,p}
&\le \inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
 \|G_N+\lambda q_N(p)-a q_N(p)\|_{H_N}^2\\
&=\inf_{b\in\mathbb R}\|G_N-bq_N(p)\|_{H_N}^2,                              \tag{28}
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\|x\|_{H_N}^2=x^{\mathsf T}H_N^{-1}x\).  After whitening, the last quantity is the squared norm of the projection of a standard three-dimensional Gaussian vector onto the two-dimensional orthocomplement of \(H_N^{-1/2}q_N(p)\).  It has the \(\chi^2_2\) law.  Equations (24) and (28) prove the upper inequality in (21).

Finally, the profile-geometry lemma extends the quantile map continuously to a sequentially compact family containing all bounded-amplitude, tangent-plane, and endpoint-half-plane limits, including limits of nonconvergent designs.  At every point of that compactification the limiting squared distance has probability zero at zero by the same lemma.  Since \(1-\alpha>0\), its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is strictly positive.  A continuous positive function on a compact set has a positive minimum; call it \(c_0\).  This proves the lower inequality in (21) and the asserted uniform continuity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-confidence-set-lower-bound]\label{thm:honest-confidence-set-lower-bound}
For every \(0<\alpha<1/2\), there are constants \(c_{\alpha},c>0\) such that every sequence \((I_N)_{N\geq 1}\) of measurable random subsets of \(\mathbb R\), with measurable diameter \(\operatorname{diam}(I_N)=\sup\{|u-v|:u,v\in I_N\}\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\), satisfying
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 P_N\{\beta\in I_N\}\geq 1-\alpha,
\]
obeys
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(I_N)]\geq c_{\alpha}.
\]
Moreover, every estimator sequence \((\widetilde\beta_N)_{N\geq1}\) obeys
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2]\geq c.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\nu=(d+1)/2\).  We first isolate an actual-design two-point subexperiment.  By the dimension, domain, fill, and separation assumptions, fix one admissible deterministic design sequence.  Fix a tuple \((c_X,c_W,\alpha_X,\alpha_W,\alpha_C)\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}}\), as permitted by nuisance compactness, and fix \(p^\circ\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), as permitted by the exponent-space assumption.  The Gaussian-field and spectral-family assumptions supply an admissible spectral matrix.  The determinant-margin condition and positivity of \(f_Xf_W\) then imply, for every admissible spectral matrix,
\[
 0<\rho\leq 1-\frac{f_{XW}(\omega)^2}{f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)}\leq1.
 \tag{1}
\]
The amplitude-space assumption permits \(A=0\).  At this value \(f_{XW}=0\), so (1) gives
\[
 f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)-f_{XW}(\omega)^2
 =f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)
 \geq\rho f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega).
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus the determinant margin continues to hold.

For completeness, this choice gives a genuine Gaussian subexperiment.  Both marginal spectra are strictly positive and integrable: they are bounded near the origin, and their radial integrands at infinity have order
\[
 r^{d-1}r^{-(d+1)}=r^{-2}.
\]
For any finite set of sites and any real coefficient vector \(z\), the covariance form induced by either marginal spectrum \(f\in\{f_X,f_W\}\) is
\[
 \kappa_d\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(\omega)
 \left|\sum_i z_i e^{\mathrm i\omega^{\mathsf T}s_i}\right|^2\,d\omega\geq0,
 \tag{3}
\]
where \(\kappa_d>0\) is the common Fourier-normalization constant.  The resulting centered Gaussian finite-dimensional laws are consistent because restriction to fewer sites takes the corresponding principal covariance submatrix.  Taking zero cross-covariance gives independent centered stationary Gaussian fields \(X\) and \(W\) with the prescribed diagonal spectral matrix.  The observation-law assumption then gives \(Y=\beta X+W\).  Consequently, as \(\beta\) varies over \(\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\), these laws form an \(A=0\) subexperiment \(\mathcal E_0\subset\mathcal M\).  The value \(p^\circ\) is immaterial when \(A=0\), so this construction does not presume identification of \(p\), including at either exponent endpoint.

Let \(X_N=(X(s_{1,N}),\ldots,X(s_{N,N}))^{\mathsf T}\), define \(W_N\) analogously, and write their covariance matrices as \(\Sigma_{X,N}\) and \(\Sigma_{W,N}\).  The separation condition implies that the sites are distinct.  If \(z\ne0\), choose \(v\in\mathbb R^d\) outside the finite union of hyperplanes orthogonal to the site differences.  Then \(v^{\mathsf T}s_{i,N}\) are distinct.  If the exponential sum in (3) vanished identically, restricting to \(\omega=tv\) and differentiating at \(t=0\) for orders \(0,\ldots,N-1\) would give a nonsingular Vandermonde system and hence \(z=0\), a contradiction.  The sum is therefore nonzero on an open set.  Since both spectra are strictly positive, (3) is strictly positive for every \(z\ne0\).  Hence \(\Sigma_{X,N}\) and \(\Sigma_{W,N}\) have full rank, and the Gaussian laws below have full support on \(\mathbb R^{2N}\).

Define the finite positive constant
\[
 C_\star=\frac{c_X}{c_W}
 \max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\alpha_W^2}{\alpha_X^2}\right)^\nu\right\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Its finiteness and positivity follow from nuisance compactness.  For every \(r\geq0\), the ratio \((\alpha_W^2+r)/(\alpha_X^2+r)\) lies between \(1\) and \(\alpha_W^2/\alpha_X^2\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac{f_X(\omega)}{f_W(\omega)}
 =\frac{c_X}{c_W}
 \left(\frac{\alpha_W^2+\|\omega\|^2}
 {\alpha_X^2+\|\omega\|^2}\right)^\nu
 \leq C_\star.
 \tag{5}
\]
Combining (3) and (5), for every \(z\in\mathbb R^N\),
\[
 z^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{X,N}z
 \leq C_\star z^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}z,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
 \Sigma_{X,N}\preceq C_\star\Sigma_{W,N}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Let \(\lambda_{1,N},\ldots,\lambda_{N,N}\) be the eigenvalues of \(\Sigma_{X,N}^{1/2}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N}^{1/2}\).  Congruence in (6), together with equality of the spectra of \(\Sigma_{X,N}^{1/2}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N}^{1/2}\) and \(\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{X,N}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 0<\lambda_{i,N}\leq C_\star,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_{i,N}
 =\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N})
 \leq NC_\star.
 \tag{7}
\]

Choose \(\beta_0\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\), which is permitted by the slope-space assumption.  For a fixed \(q>0\), put
\[
 \Delta_N=qN^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \beta_{1,N}=\beta_0+\Delta_N.
 \tag{8}
\]
Because \(\beta_0\) is an interior point, \(\beta_{1,N}\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).  Let \(P_{0,N}\) and \(P_{1,N}\) be the observed laws in \(\mathcal E_0\) with slopes \(\beta_0\) and \(\beta_{1,N}\), respectively, and with every other component fixed.  Although \(\beta_{1,N}\) changes with \(N\), at each \(N\) it is the \(N\)-th marginal of the class member whose slope is held equal to \(\beta_{1,N}\) at every sample size.  Thus both laws are among the alternatives over which the pointwise infimum and supremum in the theorem are taken.

Under \(P_{0,N}\), the vectors \(X_N\) and \(W_N=Y_N-\beta_0X_N\) are independent centered Gaussians.  Conditionally on \(X_N\), changing the slope by \(\Delta_N\) translates the mean of \(Y_N\) by \(\Delta_NX_N\) and leaves the conditional covariance equal to the invertible matrix \(\Sigma_{W,N}\).  Direct division of the two conditional Gaussian densities therefore gives the exact likelihood ratio on the complete observed vector \((X_N,Y_N)\):
\[
 L_N:=\frac{dP_{1,N}}{dP_{0,N}}
 =\exp\left\{\Delta_NX_N^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}W_N
 -\frac{\Delta_N^2}{2}X_N^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}X_N\right\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Taking \(P_{0,N}\)-expectations of minus the logarithm and using independence and centering yields the exact divergence
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})
 =\frac{\Delta_N^2}{2}
 \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N})
 \leq\frac{q^2C_\star}{2}.
 \tag{10}
\]
For the total-variation bound, conditioning the square of (9) on \(X_N\) and completing the one-dimensional Gaussian square gives
\[
 E_{0,N}[L_N^2\mid X_N]
 =\exp\{\Delta_N^2X_N^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}X_N\}.
\]
After diagonalizing the quadratic form against a standard normal vector, the elementary Gaussian integrals are finite when \(2\Delta_N^2C_\star<1\) and give
\[
 E_{0,N}L_N^2
 =\prod_{i=1}^N(1-2\Delta_N^2\lambda_{i,N})^{-1/2}.
 \tag{11}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), \(2q^2C_\star/N\leq1/2\).  Since \(-\log(1-x)\leq2x\) for \(0\leq x\leq1/2\), (7), (8), and (11) imply
\[
 \log E_{0,N}L_N^2
 \leq\frac{2q^2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_{i,N}
 \leq2q^2C_\star.
 \tag{12}
\]
The definition of total variation and Cauchy--Schwarz now yield
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})
 =\frac12E_{0,N}|L_N-1|
 \leq\frac12\{E_{0,N}(L_N-1)^2\}^{1/2}
 \leq\frac12\{e^{2q^2C_\star}-1\}^{1/2}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Equations (9)--(13) concern the exact actual-design observed likelihood.  They use neither independent Fourier coordinates nor a boundary, aliasing, stencil, or Mat\'ern remainder approximation; those issues are absent from this converse subexperiment.

We first prove the confidence-set assertion.  By the nominal-level assumption,
\[
 \eta_\alpha=1-2\alpha>0.
\]
Choose
\[
 q_\alpha=left\{\frac{1}{2C_\star}
 \log\left(1+\frac{\eta_\alpha^2}{4}\right)\right\}^{1/2}>0.
 \tag{14}
\]
Equation (13) gives \(\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\leq\eta_\alpha/4\) eventually.  For an arbitrary candidate sequence of measurable random sets, define
\[
 a_N=\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}P_N\{\beta\in I_N\}.
\]
The stated honesty condition is \(\liminf_Na_N\geq1-\alpha\).  Since both alternatives lie in \(\mathcal E_0\subset\mathcal M\), total variation and the union bound give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{0,N}\{\beta_0,\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}
 &\geq P_{0,N}\{\beta_0\in I_N\}
       +P_{0,N}\{\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}-1\\
 &\geq 2a_N-1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N}).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
On this event, the definition of diameter and (8) give \(\operatorname{diam}(I_N)\geq\Delta_N\); no connectedness of \(I_N\) is required.  Therefore (14)--(15) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(I_N)]
 &\geq q_\alpha\liminf_{N\to\infty}
 P_{0,N}\{\beta_0,\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}\\
 &\geq\frac34q_\alpha\eta_\alpha
 =:c_\alpha>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]

Finally, let \(\widetilde\beta_N\) be arbitrary and choose
\[
 q_0=\left(\frac{\log2}{2C_\star}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Equation (13) gives \(\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\leq1/2\) eventually.  Define the test
\[
 \phi_N=\mathbf1\!\left\{\widetilde\beta_N\geq
 \frac{\beta_0+\beta_{1,N}}2\right\}.
\]
On \(\{\phi_N=1\}\) under \(P_{0,N}\), the squared error is at least \(\Delta_N^2/4\); on \(\{\phi_N=0\}\) under \(P_{1,N}\), it is at least the same amount.  Writing \(\beta_{0,N}=\beta_0\) and
\[
 R_{k,N}=E_{k,N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta_{k,N})^2],
\]
we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max(R_{0,N},R_{1,N})
 &\geq\frac{\Delta_N^2}{8}
 \{P_{0,N}(\phi_N=1)+P_{1,N}(\phi_N=0)\}\\
 &\geq\frac{\Delta_N^2}{8}
 \{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\}
 \geq\frac{q_0^2}{16N}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
\]
The middle inequality is the testing identity \(P_0(\phi=1)+P_1(\phi=0)\geq1-\operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_1)\).  Since both alternatives belong to \(\mathcal M\), (17) yields
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2]
 \geq\frac{q_0^2}{16}
 =\frac{\log2}{32C_\star}=:c>0.
\]
Together with (16), this proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact complete observed-field Gaussian likelihood on an independent \(A=0\) subexperiment gives bounded total variation for \(N^{-1/2}\)-separated slopes without identifying \(p\). \textit{Gap.} Scalar microergodic limits and abstract modulus theory do not by themselves prove honest expected-length or squared-risk lower bounds for arbitrary procedures in the complete bivariate observed experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem matches the narrow-class upper rates; \texttt{prop:superclass-honest-lower-bound} is its superclass corollary and transfers only the converse to any class for which the exact embedded subexperiment is verified.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-attainable-calibration]\label{thm:exact-attainable-calibration}
For each \(N\) and allowed observed design, let \(c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\) be the exact attained critical value over \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\), and let
\[
C_N^{\mathrm{att}}=\{b\in\mathcal B:T_N(b)\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\}.
\]
The Gaussian reference quantile is uniformly continuous on the bounded-amplitude, tangent-plane, and endpoint-half-plane compactification, and
\[
\inf_{(\lambda,p)\in\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}}
\Pr\!\left\{d_{H_N}(G_N+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)^2
\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\right\}=1-\alpha.                           \tag{29}
\]
For the actual observed-law experiment,
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
P_N\{\beta\in C_N^{\mathrm{att}}\}=1-\alpha,                               \tag{30}
\]
and there is a finite constant \(C\), depending only on the fixed model and level constants, such that
\[
\operatorname{diam}(C_N^{\mathrm{att}})\le C N^{-1/2}\quad P_N\text{-almost surely}                              \tag{31}
\]
uniformly over the model class.  The conclusions include zero amplitude, bounded and diverging local amplitudes, exponent endpoints, the one-dimensional logarithmic elbow window, and nonconvergent allowed-design sequences.  Equation (30) is an asymptotic observed-law statement; at fixed \(N\), (29) is exact only for the Gaussian reference statistic.  The critical value is mathematically defined and attained, but no terminating certified numerical approximation to it is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an allowed model and design, and abbreviate
\[
E_N=\sqrt{\frac N\gamma}\{m_N-\beta\mathbf1-B_Nq_N(p)\},
\qquad
\lambda_N=\frac{\sqrt N B_N}{\sqrt\gamma},
\qquad
\Phi_{N,\lambda,p}(e)
=d_{H_N}(e+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)^2.                                   \tag{32}
\]
The nuisance mean \(\lambda q_N(p)\) belongs to \(\mathcal C_N\).  Because \(\mathcal C_N\) is a cone and \(m_N-\beta\mathbf1=\sqrt{\gamma/N}\{E_N+\lambda_Nq_N(p)\}\), homogeneity of distance gives the exact identity
\[
T_N(\beta)
=\frac{\gamma}{\widehat\gamma_N}
 \Phi_{N,\lambda_N,p}(E_N).                                                   \tag{33}
\]

We first transfer the reference distribution uniformly.  The uniform-experiment theorem, together with the upper and lower bounds on \(\gamma=c_W/c_X\) from nuisance compactness, yields
\[
\sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L_{P_N}(E_N),N_3(0,H_N)\}\longrightarrow0.        \tag{34}
\]
The eigenvalues of \(H_N\) are bounded above and away from zero by the same theorem.  On the Euclidean ball \(\{e:\|e\|\le R\}\), distance to a closed set is one-Lipschitz in its defining metric and comparison with the cone point \(\lambda q_N(p)\) gives
\[
d_{H_N}(e+\lambda q_N(p),\mathcal C_N)\le\|e\|_{H_N}.
\]
Consequently, for \(\|e\|,\|e'\|\le R\),
\[
|\Phi_{N,\lambda,p}(e)-\Phi_{N,\lambda,p}(e')|
\le \{\|e\|_{H_N}+\|e'\|_{H_N}\}\|e-e'\|_{H_N}
\le L_R\|e-e'\|,                                                            \tag{35}
\]
where \(L_R\) is independent of the design, \(\lambda\), and \(p\).  Equation (34) and a bounded Lipschitz cutoff imply uniform tightness of \(E_N\), because the centered Gaussian reference vectors are uniformly tight under the eigenvalue bound.  Smooth upper and lower approximations to the indicator of \(\{\Phi_{N,\lambda,p}\le x\}\), first on the ball in (35) and then outside it by uniform tightness, therefore show for every finite \(M\) that
\[
\begin{split}
\sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}\sup_{0\le x\le M}
\big|P_N\{\Phi_{N,\lambda_N,p}(E_N)\le x\}
{}-{}\Pr\{\Phi_{N,\lambda_N,p}(G_N)\le x\}\big|\longrightarrow0.          \tag{36}
\end{split}
\]
Indeed, for a smoothing band of width \(\varepsilon\), the bounded-Lipschitz error tends to zero by (34)--(35), and the remaining reference probability is bounded by
\[
\sup_{N,\mathcal S_N,\lambda,p}\sup_{0\le x\le M}
\Pr\{|\Phi_{N,\lambda,p}(G_N)-x|\le\varepsilon\},
\]
which tends to zero as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) by the uniform anti-concentration relation in the profile-geometry compactification lemma.  This proves (36) without requiring the design sequence to converge.

Let \(R_N=\gamma/\widehat\gamma_N\).  Feasible scale consistency in the uniform-experiment theorem gives \(R_N\to1\) in probability uniformly over the model.  The finite-design quantile lemma gives constants
\[
0<c_0\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\le M_\alpha:=\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}.        \tag{37}
\]
On \(\{|R_N-1|\le\eta\}\), with \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
\{\Phi_{N,\lambda_N,p}(E_N)\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}/(1+\eta)\}
\subseteq\{T_N(\beta)\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\}
\subseteq
\{\Phi_{N,\lambda_N,p}(E_N)\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}/(1-\eta)\}.     \tag{38}
\]
The two thresholds in (38) differ from \(c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\) by at most \(2M_\alpha\eta\).  Apply (36), then the same uniform anti-concentration bound, and finally let \(\eta\downarrow0\).  Uniform scale consistency removes the complement of the event in (38), giving
\[
\sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
\left|P_N\{T_N(\beta)\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\}
-\Pr\{\Phi_{N,\lambda_N,p}(G_N)\le c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\}\right|
\longrightarrow0.                                                            \tag{39}
\]

For each fixed allowed design, the finite-design attainable-quantile lemma proves (29).  Every observed-law parameter maps, by the observation-amplitude definition, to a pair in \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\).  Conversely, the definition of \(\Lambda_N^{\mathrm{phys}}\) makes each of its points the image of a legal spectral tuple, so the maximizing pair in (27) is available to the infimum over the model class.  Hence the infimum of the reference probability on the right side of (39) is exactly \(1-\alpha\) for every \(N\) and design.  Taking the uniform infimum in (39) proves both inequalities needed for (30).  Notice that the equality for the observed law follows only after the approximation error in (39) tends to zero; it is not a finite-sample observed-law equality.

It remains to prove the pathwise diameter bound.  Take \(b_1,b_2\in C_N^{\mathrm{att}}\).  For arbitrary \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(z_i\in\mathcal C_N\) such that, with \(r_i=m_N-b_i\mathbf1-z_i\),
\[
\|r_i\|_{H_N}\le
\sqrt{\widehat\gamma_Nc_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}/N}+\varepsilon,
\qquad i=1,2.                                                                \tag{40}
\]
The moment-separation lemma, the identity
\[
r_2-r_1=(b_1-b_2)\mathbf1+z_1-z_2,
\]
and the upper eigenvalue bound yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_\star|b_1-b_2|
&\le\|(b_1-b_2)\mathbf1+z_1-z_2\|\\
&\le\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(H_N)}
   \{\|r_1\|_{H_N}+\|r_2\|_{H_N}\}.                                      \tag{41}
\end{aligned}
\]
The deterministic clipping in \(\widehat\gamma_N\) gives
\[
0<\widehat\gamma_N\le4\overline c_W/\underline c_X.                        \tag{42}
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) in (40)--(41), and using (37), (42), and the uniform eigenvalue bound, proves
\[
|b_1-b_2|\le
\frac{2}{c_\star}
\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(H_N)\widehat\gamma_N
      c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}/N}
\le C N^{-1/2}.                                                               \tag{43}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(b_1,b_2\) proves (31), with the stated convention for an empty set.  The denominator control in the uniform-experiment theorem makes all local ratios well defined with probability one; defining the procedure arbitrarily on the null exceptional event preserves (31) almost surely.

Finally, the attainable-regimes lemma enumerates zero, bounded, divergent, endpoint, and one-dimensional logarithmic-elbow sequences in the physical set.  The profile-geometry lemma compactifies their tangent-plane and endpoint-half-plane limits and treats nonconvergent designs.  Since (36)--(43) are uniform over that entire compactification, no additional case-dependent assumption is used.  Neither lemma supplies an effective two-sided representation or convergence modulus for a terminating certified continuum optimization, so the proof establishes exact mathematical calibration but makes no certified-computability claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact attained Gaussian critical value yields asymptotic observed-law lower-envelope coverage equal to \(1-\alpha\), while moment separation gives a deterministic root-\(N\) diameter bound. \textit{Gap.} The theorem separates exact mathematical calibration from computation: a terminating certified numerical approximation of the continuum maximum remains the separately named residual \texttt{oeq:certified-attainable-critical-value-computation}, and no finite-sample observed-law equality is claimed. \textit{Consumer.} Conditional on the stated Mat\'ern and quasi-uniform-design diagnostics, the result supplies the exact least-favorable asymptotic profile calibration for the homogeneous county-centroid slope analysis.

Construct, for every \(N\) and every allowed actual design, a terminating certified numerical approximation, with a proved error bound, to the continuum maximum defining \(c_{N,\alpha}^{\mathrm{att}}\).
\end{openendedquestion}

\begin{proposition}[prop:superclass-honest-lower-bound]\label{prop:superclass-honest-lower-bound}
Let \(\mathfrak M^+\) be any statistical model of triangular arrays with the same observed vectors \((X(s_{i,N}),Y(s_{i,N}))_{i=1}^N\) and the same scalar slope target \(\beta\).  Suppose there exist \(d\in\{1,2\}\), a bounded Lipschitz domain \(D\), one deterministic design sequence satisfying \(c_mN^{-1/d}\leq h_N\leq C_mN^{-1/d}\) and \(\delta_N\geq c_sh_N\), one fixed tuple \((c_X,c_W,\alpha_X,\alpha_W,\alpha_C)\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}}\), one \(p^\circ\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\), and numbers \(\beta^\circ\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) with \([\beta^\circ-\varepsilon,\beta^\circ+\varepsilon]\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\), such that, for every \(b\) in this interval, \(\mathfrak M^+\) contains the full triangular-array law on that design generated by independent centered stationary Gaussian fields with marginal spectra \(f_X\) and \(f_W\), zero cross-spectrum \(A=0\), and response \(Y=bX+W\).  Then, for every \(0<\alpha<1/2\), there are constants \(c^+_\alpha,c^+>0\) such that every sequence \((I_N)_{N\geq1}\) of measurable random subsets of \(\mathbb R\), with measurable diameter and \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\), satisfying
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathfrak M^+}
 P_N\{\beta\in I_N\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
obeys
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathfrak M^+}
 E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(I_N)]\geq c^+_\alpha.
\]
Moreover, every estimator sequence \((\widetilde\beta_N)_{N\geq1}\) obeys
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathfrak M^+}
 E_{P_N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2]\geq c^+.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the embedded subexperiment asserted in the premise.  Its nuisance tuple is fixed and strictly positive, so the constant
\[
 C_\star=\frac{c_X}{c_W}
 \max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\alpha_W^2}{\alpha_X^2}\right)^{(d+1)/2}\right\}
\]
is finite and positive.  The exact actual-design likelihood calculation in the honest-confidence-set lower-bound theorem uses only this independent \(A=0\) subexperiment.  In particular, for \(\beta_{0,N}=\beta^\circ\), \(\beta_{1,N}=\beta^\circ+qN^{-1/2}\), and all sufficiently large \(N\) such that \(qN^{-1/2}<\varepsilon\), it gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})
 \leq\frac12\{e^{2q^2C_\star}-1\}^{1/2}.
 \tag{18}
\]
Both laws belong to \(\mathfrak M^+\) by the explicit inclusion premise.  This is the only point at which the ambient class enters the lower-bound proof.

Let \(a_N^+=\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathfrak M^+}P_N\{\beta\in I_N\}\), put \(\eta_\alpha=1-2\alpha\), and choose
\[
 q_\alpha=\left\{\frac{1}{2C_\star}
 \log\left(1+\frac{\eta_\alpha^2}{4}\right)\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
Uniform honesty over \(\mathfrak M^+\) implies \(\liminf_Na_N^+\geq1-\alpha\).  The same total-variation and union-bound calculation, now using the two embedded laws, gives
\[
 P_{0,N}\{\beta_{0,N},\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}
 \geq2a_N^+-1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N}).
\]
On this event \(\operatorname{diam}(I_N)\geq q_\alpha N^{-1/2}\).  Equation (18) therefore yields
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathfrak M^+}
 E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(I_N)]
 \geq\frac34q_\alpha(1-2\alpha)=:c_\alpha^+>0.
\]

For squared risk, take \(q_0=\{\log2/(2C_\star)\}^{1/2}\).  Then (18) is at most \(1/2\).  Thresholding any \(\widetilde\beta_N\) at \((\beta_{0,N}+\beta_{1,N})/2\) gives, exactly as in the theorem,
\[
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathfrak M^+}
 E_{P_N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2]
 \geq\frac{q_0^2}{16N}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).  Hence the second conclusion holds with
\[
 c^+=\frac{\log2}{32C_\star}>0.
\]
This is converse monotonicity with respect to inclusion of the exact subexperiment, not a claim that any named broader spatial model automatically contains it.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The two-point calculation uses only one exact fixed-design independent Mat\'ern-one-half subexperiment, so the lower bounds are monotone under inclusion of that subexperiment. \textit{Gap.} Broader spatial model classes inherit the converse only after their observation experiment, deterministic design sequence, response law, and common slope interval are shown to contain the specified subexperiment. \textit{Consumer.} This conditional transfer separates the narrow-class upper theory from the more robust scope of the root-\(N\) length and \(N^{-1}\) risk converse.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

First differences are insufficient in \(d=2\): the analytic covariance remainder creates an \(O(h_N)\) slope bias and normalized correlations of order \(1/r\) create a logarithmic variance factor. Exact affine annihilation removes the quadratic covariance term and yields summable fourth-difference dependence. Three-scale profile intervals may nevertheless have large constants when \(p_{\min}\) is close to \(1/2\); the proposal claims rate optimality, not an efficient constant.

\section{Honest scope}

All achievability claims---the actual-design Gaussian experiment, feasible studentization, profile confidence sets, exact mathematically attained calibration, asymptotic observed-law coverage, and root-\(N\) upper diameter and risk---remain restricted to the stated signed bivariate Mat\'ern, stationary Gaussian, deterministic quasi-uniform, no-nugget class in dimensions one and two. The lower bound is proved there and transfers conditionally to any statistical superclass containing the same fixed-design independent Mat\'ern-one-half \(A=0\) observed experiment over a common nontrivial slope interval. No such inclusion is asserted automatically for Guan et al.; Datta--Stein is only a candidate application pending explicit verification of the observation scheme, design sequence, response construction, and submodel inclusion. The scope excludes nuggets, unknown marginal-smoothness adaptation, nonstationarity, independent Fourier-coordinate approximations, and broader covariance classes for the upper result. The sole residual open question is \texttt{oeq:certified-attainable-critical-value-computation}: a terminating certified numerical approximation, with a proved error bound, of the continuum attainable critical value. The county-centroid application remains conditional on the model and design diagnostics and does not by itself establish a broader nonparametric causal estimand.
