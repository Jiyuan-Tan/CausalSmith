%%% FIELD honest-set-statement
For every \(0<\alpha<1/2\), there are constants \(c_{\alpha},c>0\) such that every sequence \((I_N)_{N\geq 1}\) of measurable random subsets of \(\mathbb R\), with measurable diameter \(\operatorname{diam}(I_N)=\sup\{|u-v|:u,v\in I_N\}\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\varnothing)=0\), satisfying
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 P_N\{\beta\in I_N\}\geq 1-\alpha,
\]
obeys
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(I_N)]\geq c_{\alpha}.
\]
Moreover, every estimator sequence \((\widetilde\beta_N)_{N\geq1}\) obeys
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2]\geq c.
\]
%%% FIELD honest-set-proof
Put \(\nu=(d+1)/2\).  We construct a two-point subexperiment inside the model class.  Fix an admissible deterministic design sequence and a nuisance tuple \((c_X,c_W,\alpha_X,\alpha_W,\alpha_C)\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{nuis}}\).  Fix also \(p^\circ\in[p_{\min},p_{\max}]\) and set \(A=0\), which is permitted by the amplitude-space assumption.  The determinant-margin assumption on the nonempty model implies \(0<\rho\leq1\), because
\[
 0\leq \frac{f_{XW}(\omega)^2}{f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)}
 \leq 1-\rho
\]
for every member and every \(\omega\).  Hence, at \(A=0\),
\[
 f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)-f_{XW}(\omega)^2
 =f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega)
 \geq\rho f_X(\omega)f_W(\omega).
 \tag{1}
\]
The marginal spectra are positive and integrable: near the origin they are bounded, while their radial integrands at infinity are of order \(r^{d-1}r^{-(d+1)}=r^{-2}\).  Their inverse Fourier transforms therefore define stationary covariance kernels.  For every finite collection of sites and coefficients, the corresponding covariance quadratic form is the integral of the positive spectrum times the squared modulus of a finite exponential sum, and hence is nonnegative.  The consistent centered Gaussian finite-dimensional distributions with these kernels define independent centered stationary Gaussian fields \(X\) and \(W\).  Together with (1), the spectral-family assumption, and \(Y=\beta X+W\), this produces an admissible \(A=0\) submodel of \(\mathcal M\) for every \(\beta\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\).  The parameter \(p^\circ\) is immaterial on this submodel, so no identification of \(p\) is being assumed.

For the fixed nuisance tuple define
\[
 C_\star=\frac{c_X}{c_W}
 \max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\alpha_W^2}{\alpha_X^2}\right)^\nu\right\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Nuisance compactness places every coordinate in \((0,\infty)\), so \(0<C_\star<\infty\).  The two marginal spectra satisfy, pointwise,
\[
 \frac{f_X(\omega)}{f_W(\omega)}
 =\frac{c_X}{c_W}
 \left(\frac{\alpha_W^2+\|\omega\|^2}
 {\alpha_X^2+\|\omega\|^2}\right)^\nu
 \leq C_\star.
 \tag{3}
\]
Let \(X_N=(X(s_{1,N}),\ldots,X(s_{N,N}))^{\mathsf T}\), define \(W_N\) analogously, and denote their covariance matrices by \(\Sigma_{X,N}\) and \(\Sigma_{W,N}\).  The spectral representation and (3) imply, for every \(z\in\mathbb R^N\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 z^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{X,N}z
 &=\kappa_d\int_{\mathbb R^d}f_X(\omega)
 \left|\sum_{i=1}^Nz_i e^{\mathrm i\omega^{\mathsf T}s_{i,N}}\right|^2\,d\omega\\
 &\leq C_\star\kappa_d\int_{\mathbb R^d}f_W(\omega)
 \left|\sum_{i=1}^Nz_i e^{\mathrm i\omega^{\mathsf T}s_{i,N}}\right|^2\,d\omega
 =C_\star z^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}z,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
where the Fourier-normalization constant \(\kappa_d>0\) is the same for both fields.  The separation assumption makes the sites distinct.  If \(z\neq0\), choose a vector \(v\in\mathbb R^d\) outside the finite union of hyperplanes orthogonal to the site differences.  Then the numbers \(v^{\mathsf T}s_{i,N}\) are distinct.  Were the exponential sum in (4) identically zero, its restrictions to \(\omega=tv\) and its first \(N\) derivatives at \(t=0\) would give a nonsingular Vandermonde system forcing \(z=0\), a contradiction.  Thus the sum is nonzero on an open set.  Since \(f_W>0\), its integral is strictly positive.  Therefore \(\Sigma_{W,N}\) is invertible and
\[
 \Sigma_{X,N}\preceq C_\star\Sigma_{W,N}.
 \tag{5}
\]
If \(\lambda_{1,N},\ldots,\lambda_{N,N}\) are the eigenvalues of
\(\Sigma_{X,N}^{1/2}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N}^{1/2}\), then (5), by congruence with \(\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1/2}\) and equality of the nonzero spectra of the two matrix products, yields
\[
 0<\lambda_{i,N}\leq C_\star,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_{i,N}
 =\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N})
 \leq NC_\star.
 \tag{6}
\]

Choose \(\beta_0\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\).  For fixed \(q>0\), let
\[
 \Delta_N=qN^{-1/2},\qquad \beta_{1,N}=\beta_0+\Delta_N.
 \tag{7}
\]
Because \(\beta_0\) is interior, \(\beta_{1,N}\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal B)\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).  Let \(P_{0,N}\) and \(P_{1,N}\) be the two \(A=0\) observed-field laws at slopes \(\beta_0\) and \(\beta_{1,N}\), respectively, with all other quantities fixed.  For each \(N\), the second law is the \(N\)-th marginal of a member of \(\mathcal M\) obtained by holding \(\beta_{1,N}\) fixed over that member's entire sample-size sequence.  Thus both alternatives are available to the supremum and infimum defining the theorem.

Under \(P_{0,N}\), \(X_N\) and \(W_N=Y_N-\beta_0X_N\) are independent centered Gaussian vectors.  Conditional on \(X_N\), changing the slope by \(\Delta_N\) changes the conditional mean of \(Y_N\) by \(\Delta_NX_N\) and leaves its covariance equal to \(\Sigma_{W,N}\).  Consequently the exact likelihood ratio on the complete observed vector \((X_N,Y_N)\) is
\[
 L_N:=\frac{dP_{1,N}}{dP_{0,N}}
 =\exp\left\{\Delta_NX_N^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}W_N
 -\frac{\Delta_N^2}{2}X_N^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}X_N\right\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
In particular, independence and centering give the exact divergence
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})
 =\frac{\Delta_N^2}{2}
 \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}\Sigma_{X,N})
 \leq\frac{q^2C_\star}{2}.
 \tag{9}
\]
For the sharper total-variation constant used below, condition (8) on \(X_N\).  The Gaussian moment-generating identity gives
\[
 E_{0,N}[L_N^2\mid X_N]
 =\exp\{\Delta_N^2X_N^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{W,N}^{-1}X_N\}.
\]
Diagonalizing this Gaussian quadratic form yields, whenever \(2\Delta_N^2C_\star<1\),
\[
 E_{0,N}L_N^2
 =\prod_{i=1}^N(1-2\Delta_N^2\lambda_{i,N})^{-1/2}.
 \tag{10}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), \(2q^2C_\star/N\leq1/2\).  Since \(-\log(1-x)\leq2x\) for \(0\leq x\leq1/2\), equations (6), (7), and (10) imply
\[
 \log E_{0,N}L_N^2
 \leq\frac{2q^2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_{i,N}
 \leq2q^2C_\star.
 \tag{11}
\]
By the definition of total variation and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})
 =\frac12E_{0,N}|L_N-1|
 \leq\frac12\{E_{0,N}(L_N-1)^2\}^{1/2}
 \leq\frac12\{e^{2q^2C_\star}-1\}^{1/2}.
 \tag{12}
\]
This bound concerns the actual observed-field likelihood and does not posit independent Fourier coordinates.

We first prove the confidence-set assertion.  Put \(\eta_\alpha=1-2\alpha>0\), using the nominal-level assumption, and choose
\[
 q_\alpha=left\{\frac{1}{2C_\star}
 \log\left(1+\frac{\eta_\alpha^2}{4}\right)\right\}^{1/2}>0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Equation (12) gives \(\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\leq\eta_\alpha/4\) eventually.  For an arbitrary candidate sequence of measurable random sets define
\[
 a_N=\inf_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}P_N\{\beta\in I_N\}.
\]
Honesty gives \(\liminf_Na_N\geq1-\alpha\).  Total variation and the union bound give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{0,N}\{\beta_0,\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}
 &\geq P_{0,N}\{\beta_0\in I_N\}
       +P_{0,N}\{\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}-1\\
 &\geq 2a_N-1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N}).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
On the event in (14), the definition of diameter and (7) give \(\operatorname{diam}(I_N)\geq\Delta_N\), without any connectedness assumption on \(I_N\).  It follows from (13)--(14) that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[\operatorname{diam}(I_N)]
 &\geq q_\alpha\liminf_{N\to\infty}
 P_{0,N}\{\beta_0,\beta_{1,N}\in I_N\}\\
 &\geq \frac34q_\alpha\eta_\alpha
 =:c_\alpha>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]

Finally, let \(\widetilde\beta_N\) be any estimator and take
\[
 q_0=\left(\frac{\log2}{2C_\star}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Equation (12) gives \(\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\leq1/2\) eventually.  Define
\(\phi_N=\mathbf 1\{\widetilde\beta_N\geq(\beta_0+\beta_{1,N})/2\}\).  On \(\{\phi_N=1\}\) under \(P_{0,N}\), the squared error is at least \(\Delta_N^2/4\); on \(\{\phi_N=0\}\) under \(P_{1,N}\), it is at least the same amount.  If
\(R_{k,N}=E_{k,N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta_{k,N})^2]\), with \(\beta_{0,N}=\beta_0\), then the definition of total variation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max(R_{0,N},R_{1,N})
 &\geq\frac{\Delta_N^2}{8}
 \{P_{0,N}(\phi_N=1)+P_{1,N}(\phi_N=0)\}\\
 &\geq\frac{\Delta_N^2}{8}
 \{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\}
 \geq\frac{q_0^2}{16N}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Both alternatives lie in \(\mathcal M\), so (16) yields
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}N
 \sup_{(P_N,\mathcal S_N)\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_N}[(\widetilde\beta_N-\beta)^2]
 \geq\frac{q_0^2}{16}
 =\frac{\log2}{32C_\star}=:c>0.
\]
Together with (15), this proves both lower bounds.
