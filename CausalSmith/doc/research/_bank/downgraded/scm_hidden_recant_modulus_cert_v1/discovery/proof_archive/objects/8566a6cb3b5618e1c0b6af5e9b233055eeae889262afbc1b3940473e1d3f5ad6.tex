We prove (17) by establishing both inclusions.  This also proves that the
optimization in (18) concerns the causal target and is not a definition of that
target.

Fix \(\mathcal S\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{WBC}}^{+}\).  Under the district
semantics of \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{WBC}}\), collect the exogenous variables for
the district \(\{H,Y\}\) into one block and retain separate exogenous blocks for
\(A,Z,W,M\).  The five resulting blocks are mutually independent.  Because all
endogenous alphabets are finite, every realization of a block induces a
deterministic response function on every possible parent configuration.  Push
the five exogenous laws forward to the corresponding response-type sets.  The
joint pushforward has the factorization
\[
 q_\theta(r)=\theta_A(r_A)\theta_{HY}(r_H,r_Y)
 \theta_Z(r_Z)\theta_W(r_W)\theta_M(r_M).
 \tag{22}
\]
This step uses only the district decomposition stated in
\(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{WBC}}\); dependence between the hidden-witness and outcome
responses remains unrestricted inside \(\theta_{HY}\).

Recursive evaluation of the structural equations gives exactly
\((a_r,h_r,z_r,w_r,m_r,y_r)\) as displayed in the theorem.  Consequently the
observed-cell law is \(p_\theta=P_{\mathcal S}^O\).  The same response functions
give
\[
 H_0=r_H(0),\quad M_{1,H_0}=r_M(1,r_H(0)),\quad
 W_{H_0}=r_W(r_H(0)),
\]
and hence, by the definition of the causal target,
\[
 \psi(\mathcal S)
 =\sum_rq_\theta(r)
 r_Y(0,r_H(0),r_M(1,r_H(0)),r_W(r_H(0)))
 =T(\theta).
 \tag{23}
\]
This is the required SCM-level link between the causal estimand and the program
objective.

It remains to check feasibility.  The two denominator assumptions in
\(\mathrm{def{:}wbc\text{-}class}\) are precisely (13).  Under (22),
\(u_h=P_{\mathcal S}(H_0=h)\),
\(v_{hm}=P_{\mathcal S}(M_{1,h}=m)\),
\(j^0_{hm}=P_{\mathcal S}(A=0,H=h,M=m)\), and
\(j^1_h=P_{\mathcal S}(A=1,H=h)\).  Thus the two latent transport-support
assumptions are precisely (14).  By latent-valid bridge existence, choose
\[
 x\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathcal S)
       \cap\mathcal H_{\mathrm{obs}}(P_{\mathcal S}^O).
\]
Membership in the observed fiber is exactly \(B(P)x=b(P)\).  When
\(u_hv_{hm}>0\), (14) makes \(j^0_{hm}>0\), so multiplying the first conditional
equation in \(\mathrm{def{:}latent\text{-}bridge\text{-}set}\) by this positive
denominator gives (15).  Conversely, division by \(j^0_{hm}>0\) recovers that
conditional equation.  Likewise, when \(u_h>0\), (14) makes \(j^1_h>0\), and
multiplication of the second latent bridge equation by \(j^1_h\) gives (16),
with division reversing the step.  Therefore \((\theta,x)\in\mathcal F(P)\),
and (23) proves the left-to-right inclusion in (17).

For the reverse inclusion, fix \((\theta,x)\in\mathcal F(P)\).  Draw
\(R_A,R_{HY},R_Z,R_W,R_M\) independently with the five displayed marginal
laws, writing \(R_{HY}=(R_H,R_Y)\).  Define a finite SCM by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A&=R_A,& H&=R_H(A),& Z&=R_Z(A,H),\\
 W&=R_W(H),& M&=R_M(A,H),& Y&=R_Y(A,H,M,W).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{24}
\]
The parent sets in (24) are exactly allowed by
\(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{WBC}}\), and the only possibly dependent response pair is
\((R_H,R_Y)\), which is exactly the district \(\{H,Y\}\).  Thus (24) is
compatible with the fixed graph.  Structural evaluation gives all five
consistency conditions.

We verify the remaining independence conditions rather than importing them
silently.  The pair
\(\{Y_{a,H_a,m,W_{H_a}},H_a\}\) is a function of
\((R_H,R_Y,R_W)\), whereas \(A\) is a function of the independent \(R_A\);
this proves sequential treatment independence.  Each \(M_{a,h}=R_M(a,h)\)
depends only on \(R_M\), proving sequential mediator independence from
\((H,A)\), and also proving cross-world independence from the preceding pair.
Conditional on \((H,A)\), the observed \(M=R_M(A,H)\) depends only on
\(R_M\), while \(Y_{A,H,m,W_H}\) depends only on
\((R_A,R_H,R_Y,R_W)\); independence of the response blocks proves sequential
outcome independence.  Conditional on \((H,A)\), \(Z\) depends only on
\(R_Z\), while \((Y,M)\) depends on the other response blocks, proving the
\(Z\)-proxy exclusion.  Conditional on \(H\), \(W\) depends only on
\(R_W\), while \((A,Z,M)\) depends on \((R_A,R_Z,R_M,R_H)\), proving the
\(W\)-proxy exclusion.  Conditioning here is legitimate on each positive cell;
the finite conditional-independence equalities extend to zero cells by their
cross-multiplied form.

The law generated by (24) has observed cell probabilities \(p_\theta=P\).
Conditions (13) and (14) therefore give the two observed denominator supports and
the two latent transport supports.  By the positive-denominator equivalences
proved above, (15)--(16) say exactly that the pair represented by \(x\) lies in
\(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathcal S_\theta)\), while
\(B(P)x=b(P)\) says that it lies in
\(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{obs}}(P)\).  Thus latent-valid bridge existence holds, so
every member property in \(\mathrm{def{:}wbc\text{-}class}\) has been verified
and \(\mathcal S_\theta\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{WBC}}^{+}\).  Direct evaluation
of its nested counterfactual, as in (23), gives
\(\psi(\mathcal S_\theta)=T(\theta)\).  This proves the reverse inclusion and
hence (17).

For an induced \(P\), the set \(\Gamma(P)\) is nonempty by (17) and is contained
in \([0,1]\) because (12) averages binary response values.  If
\(\underline\psi(P)=\overline\psi(P)=c\), then every
\(t\in\Gamma(P)\) satisfies
\(c\le t\le c\), so the full-law causal fiber is constant.  Conversely, if the
fiber is constant, (17) makes \(\Gamma(P)\) a singleton and its infimum and
supremum coincide.  This proves (19).  If an observable subclass has constancy on
every fiber, the same equivalence puts every law in that subclass inside
\(\mathcal I_{\max}\); hence (19) is maximal and unique.  Moreover, if two
feasible values differ, their infimum and supremum differ.  If the infimum is
strictly smaller than the supremum, \(\Gamma(P)\) cannot be a singleton, so two
of its elements, and by (17) two compatible SCMs, differ.  No endpoint attainment
or closure assumption is used in this argument.

Every set in the program is finite.  The simplex restrictions are polynomial
equalities and weak inequalities; (13)--(16) are finite Boolean combinations of
polynomial equalities and strict inequalities; \(p_\theta\) and \(T\) are the
finite polynomials displayed above.  The entries of \(B(P)\) and \(b(P)\) have
only the positive denominators in (13), so multiplying each observed bridge row
by its denominator yields polynomial constraints without changing its solution
set.  Therefore a proposed pair of feasible points with unequal objectives is
checked by finitely many substitutions and comparisons.  This proves the stated
certificate claim.

The already established target-span theorem puts every induced law satisfying
\(\ell(P)\in\operatorname{row}(B(P))\) in \(\mathcal I_{\max}\).  The already
established proxy-measurable treatment-zero rescue theorem does the same whenever
\(P(Y=f(W)\mid A=0)=1\) for some \(f:\mathcal X\to\mathcal X\).  These are
causal identification results; neither inclusion is being created by the
definition (19).

For the opposite stress test, \(\mathrm{prop{:}scm\text{-}counterpair}\) gives
two members inducing the same strictly positive \(P^{\dagger}\), with target
values \(19/48\) and \(7/16=21/48\), and proves failed row span.  Applying (17),
\[
 \underline\psi(P^{\dagger})\le\frac{19}{48}
 <\frac{21}{48}\le\overline\psi(P^{\dagger}),
\]
which proves (20).

Finally, feasibility implies \(b(P)\in\operatorname{col}(B(P))\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem{:}null\text{-}annihilator}\), under this solvability condition
\[
 \ell(P)\notin\operatorname{row}(B(P))
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists v\in\mathbb R^6\ [B(P)v=0,\ \ell(P)^\top v\ne0].
 \tag{25}
\]
By (17)--(19), the last universally quantified line in (21) is equivalent to
point identification on the class fiber.  Hence \(P\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{int}}\)
is exactly a strictly positive, failed-row-span counterexample to the proposed
universal positive-interior converse.  Such a counterexample exists if and only
if \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{int}}\ne\varnothing\), proving the last assertion and
isolating the precise residual tilt obligation.
