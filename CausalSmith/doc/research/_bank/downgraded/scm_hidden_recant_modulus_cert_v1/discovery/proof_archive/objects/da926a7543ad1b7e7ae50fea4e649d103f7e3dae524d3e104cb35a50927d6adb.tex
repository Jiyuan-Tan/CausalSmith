Write the original binary channel, with rows ordered by \(Z=0,1\) and columns by \(H=0,1,2\), as
\[
 R_{Z\mid H}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  3/4&1/2&1/4\\
  1/4&1/2&3/4
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant of its first two columns is
\[
 \det\!\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/2\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{3}{8}-\frac{1}{8}=\frac14\ne0,
\]
so this particular channel has rank two. More generally, every channel with a binary output and three latent input states is a \(2\times3\) matrix and therefore has rank at most two.

We first record the exact finite completeness calculation. Let \(S\) be a finite-valued proxy with channel matrix \(R=(R_{sh})_{s,h}\), where \(R_{sh}=P(S=s\mid H=h,C)\) under a conditioning event \(C\), and put \(\pi_h=P(H=h\mid C)\). Assume \(P(C)>0\), \(\pi_h>0\) for every \(h\in\mathcal H\), and \((R\pi)_s>0\) for every proxy state \(s\). Bayes' rule gives the conditional-expectation matrix
\[
 K_C(s,h)=P(H=h\mid S=s,C)
 =\frac{R_{sh}\pi_h}{(R\pi)_s},
 \qquad
 K_C=D_{R\pi}^{-1}R D_{\pi},
\]
where \(D_{\pi}\) and \(D_{R\pi}\) are diagonal matrices. Strict positivity makes both diagonal matrices invertible, and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(K_C)=\operatorname{rank}(R).
\]
For a real function \(g\) on the finite set \(\mathcal H\), the equations \(\mathbb E[g(H)\mid S,C]=0\) almost surely are exactly \(K_Cg=0\). Thus full column rank of \(R\) implies \(g=0\). Conversely, if \(R\) has rank below three, then \(K_C\) has a nonzero null vector, so completeness fails. All real functions on \(\mathcal H\) are square-integrable, and no integrability qualification is hidden in this finite argument.

By the conditional independence imposed in \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\text{-}proxy\text{-}augmentation}\), for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \(h\in\mathcal H\),
\[
 P(Z=z,V=v\mid H=h,A=a)
 =P(Z=z\mid H=h,A=a)P(V=v\mid H=h,A=a).
\]
Multiplying the displayed Bernoulli probabilities therefore gives
\[
 R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  9/16&1/8&1/8\\
  3/16&3/8&1/8\\
  3/16&1/8&3/8\\
  1/16&3/8&3/8
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its top-three-row minor equals
\[
 \frac{1}{16^3}
 \det\!\begin{pmatrix}9&2&2\\3&6&2\\3&2&6\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{9(36-4)-2(18-6)+2(6-18)}{4096}
 =\frac{240}{4096}=\frac{15}{256}\ne0.
\]
It follows that \(\operatorname{rank}(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H})=3\), the maximum possible column rank.

For the first WBC completeness clause, take \(C=\{A=1\}\). Treatment positivity makes \(P(C)>0\), and the hypothesis in the theorem makes every \(\pi_h=P(H=h\mid A=1)\) strictly positive. Every entry of \(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}\) is strictly positive, so every entry of \(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}\pi\) is positive. The preceding Bayes calculation therefore proves that
\[
 \mathbb E[g(H)\mid Z^{\star},A=1]=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g(H)=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
For the second clause, fix \(m\in\mathcal X\) and take \(C=\{M=m,A=0\}\). The denominator-support assumption gives \(P(C)>0\), because \(P(A=0,Z=z,M=m)>0\) for each \(z\). The proxy exclusions for \(Z\), together with the imposed conditional independence of \(V\) from \((Z,W,M,Y)\) given \((H,A)\), imply
\[
 P(Z^{\star}=s\mid H=h,M=m,A=0)
 =P(Z^{\star}=s\mid H=h,A=0)
 =(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H})_{sh}.
\]
The assumed strict positivity of \(P(H=h\mid M=m,A=0)\) and the same Bayes calculation now give
\[
 \mathbb E[g(H)\mid Z^{\star},M=m,A=0]=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g(H)=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
This holds for every supported \(m\), proving both WBC completeness clauses. With no added coordinate, rank three is impossible; with the single displayed binary coordinate, rank three holds. Therefore one added binary coordinate is both sufficient and necessary, and hence is the sharp minimum for uniform restoration for the fixed input channel.

The augmented pair also inherits both WBC proxy exclusions. Indeed, \(\mathrm{ass{:}z\text{-}proxy\text{-}exclusion}\) and the conditional independence imposed on \(V\) give the finite conditional factorization
\[
 p(z,v,y,m\mid h,a)
 =p(v\mid h,a)p(z\mid h,a)p(y,m\mid h,a),
\]
which is \(Z^{\star}\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}(Y,M)\mid(H,A)\). Likewise, \(\mathrm{ass{:}w\text{-}proxy\text{-}exclusion}\) and the same conditional independence give
\[
 p(w,a,z,v,m\mid h)
 =p(w\mid h)p(v\mid h,a)p(a,z,m\mid h)
 =p(w\mid h)p(a,z,v,m\mid h),
\]
which is \(W\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}(A,Z^{\star},M)\mid H\).

Now impose the two augmented bridge equations displayed in the proposed statement. They are exactly the finite, no-baseline-covariate version of WBC Assumption 3.3 with treatment-inducing proxy \(Z^{\star}\). Together with the two completeness conclusions just proved, the augmented proxy exclusions just derived, and the remaining causal, consistency, and support or positivity hypotheses in the proposed statement, all premises of \(\mathrm{lem{:}wbc\text{-}por\text{-}identification}\) hold. That cited theorem therefore yields
\[
 \psi(\mathcal S)
 =\sum_{w\in\mathcal X}h_1(w)P_{\mathcal S}^O(W=w\mid A=0),
\]
which is the asserted identification conclusion.

It remains to verify the two negative qualifications and to stress-test the bridge premise. Full rank concerns only the conditional channel of \(Z^{\star}\) given \((H,A)\). It cannot imply the remaining support assumptions: retain the displayed full-rank channel but set \(M=0\) almost surely. Then \(P(A=0,Z=z,M=1)=0\), so \(\mathrm{ass{:}a0\text{-}bridge\text{-}denominators}\) fails while the channel rank remains three.

More strongly, solvability of the frozen bridge system based only on \(Z\) does not imply bridge existence after conditioning on \(Z^{\star}\). Here is an explicit finite SCM witness. Let \(A\), \(M\), and \(W\) be mutually independent fair Bernoulli variables, let \(H\) be uniform on \(\mathcal H\), and let them be mutually independent. Generate \(Z\) and \(V\), conditionally independently given \((H,A)\), from the displayed channels. Put
\[
 g=(1,-2,1)^{\top},
 \qquad
 P(Y=1\mid H=h)=\frac12+\frac18g_h,
\]
using an additional independent uniform exogenous variable. This is an SCM compatible with the displayed graph: one may take \(H_a=H\), \(W_h=W\), \(M_{a,h}=M\), and \(Y_{a,h,m,w}=\mathbf 1\{U_Y\le 1/2+g_h/8\}\). Independence of the exogenous variables verifies the stated sequential and cross-world independences, and the construction verifies consistency and both proxy exclusions. All treatment and bridge-denominator probabilities are positive.

For \(D_{\pi}=\frac13I_3\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 R_{Z\mid H}D_{\pi}g=0.
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb E[Y\mid Z,M,A=0]=1/2\), so \(h_0(w,m)=1/2\) solves every first-stage equation based on \(Z\); taking \(h_1(w)=1/2\) also solves the second-stage equations. Hence the original observable condition \(b(P)\in\operatorname{col}(B(P))\) holds. In contrast,
\[
 R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}D_{\pi}g\ne0
\]
because \(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}\) has full column rank. Moreover, the ratios
\[
 \frac{(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}D_{\pi}g)_s}
 {(R_{Z^{\star}\mid H}\pi)_s}
\]
cannot be constant in \(s\): if they all equalled \(c\), then full column rank would imply \(D_{\pi}(g-c\mathbf 1)=0\), contrary to \(g=(1,-2,1)^{\top}\). Thus \(\mathbb E[Y\mid Z^{\star}=s,M,A=0]\) varies with \(s\), whereas independence of \(W\) makes
\[
 \sum_{w\in\mathcal X}h_0(w,M)P(W=w\mid Z^{\star}=s,M,A=0)
 =\frac{h_0(0,M)+h_0(1,M)}{2}
\]
constant in \(s\) for every candidate \(h_0\). No augmented first-stage bridge exists. This exact witness proves both that full augmented-channel rank does not establish bridge existence and that the original coarsened condition \(b(P)\in\operatorname{col}(B(P))\) is insufficient to invoke the WBC theorem with \(Z^{\star}\). The explicit augmented bridge premise in the proposed statement is therefore load-bearing.
