Fix a linear extension \(\pi\in\Pi_{\preceq}\), and write
\[
 S=\operatorname{Pred}_{\pi}(T).
\]
Because \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{p}\), its modified Cholesky factorization in the order \(\pi\) exists uniquely:
\[
 \Gamma=L_{\pi}D_{\pi}^{2}L_{\pi}^{\top},
\]
where \(L_{\pi}\) is unit lower triangular in that order and \(D_{\pi}\) has strictly positive diagonal.  Let \(Z\sim N(0,I_p)\) and realize the Gaussian divisor as \(G_{\Gamma}=L_{\pi}D_{\pi}Z\).  If \(Y\notin S\), the residual of \((G_{\Gamma})_T\) after linear projection on \((G_{\Gamma})_S\) is \((D_{\pi})_{TT}Z_T\).  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\gamma_{YT\cdot S}(\Gamma)}{\gamma_{TT\cdot S}(\Gamma)}
 &=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}\!\left((G_{\Gamma})_Y,(D_{\pi})_{TT}Z_T\right)}
         {\operatorname{Var}\!\left((D_{\pi})_{TT}Z_T\right)}\\
 &=\frac{(L_{\pi})_{YT}(D_{\pi})_{TT}^{2}}
         {(D_{\pi})_{TT}^{2}}
 =(L_{\pi})_{YT}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The division is legitimate because
\(\gamma_{TT\cdot S}(\Gamma)>0\): it is the Schur complement of the positive-definite principal block \(\Gamma_{SS}\), with the stated empty-set convention when \(S=\varnothing\).  If \(Y\in S\), conditioning on \(S\) makes \(\gamma_{YT\cdot S}(\Gamma)=0\), and triangularity gives \((L_{\pi})_{YT}=0\), so the same identity remains valid.

This identity proves the relation between the causal target and the observable covariance functional; it does not define that relation.  Indeed, by \(U=AH\), the full-column-rank condition on \(A\) gives a deterministic left inverse of \(A\), hence \(H\) is a measurable linear function of \(U\).  Thus \(U\perp G_{\Gamma}\) implies \(H\perp Z\).  Multiplying the equality \(X=AH+L_{\pi}D_{\pi}Z\) by \(L_{\pi}^{-1}\) gives the acyclic structural system
\[
 X=(I-L_{\pi}^{-1})X+L_{\pi}^{-1}AH+D_{\pi}Z.
\]
Its coefficient matrix is strictly lower triangular in the order \(\pi\), and its total directed-effect matrix is
\[
 \{I-(I-L_{\pi}^{-1})\}^{-1}=L_{\pi}.
\]
Consequently, surgical intervention on coordinate \(T\) changes the mean of coordinate \(Y\) at derivative \((L_{\pi})_{YT}\).  Premultiplication by the invertible matrix \(L_{\pi}^{-1}\) preserves the rank of the source loading, and the source independence and nonconstancy conditions are unchanged.  The greatest-divisor assumption makes the \(\Gamma\) used in this construction the stipulated law-indexed causal Gaussian covariance.

It remains to determine all possible sets \(S\).  Every predecessor set of \(T\) contains
\(D=\{v:v\prec T\}\), contains no strict successor of \(T\), and hence has the form \(D\cup J\) for some \(J\subseteq A\).  If \(w\in J\) and \(v\in A\) satisfies \(v\prec w\), then \(v\) precedes \(T\) in \(\pi\), so \(v\in J\).  Thus \(J\) is an ideal of \(\preceq|_A\).  Conversely, take \(J\in\mathcal J\).  The sets \(D\cup J\) and \(D\cup J\cup\{T\}\) are ideals of the full partial order: a predecessor of an element of \(D\) lies in \(D\); a predecessor of an element of \(J\) lies in \(D\cup J\); and every predecessor of \(T\) lies in \(D\).  Hence one may take a linear extension of \(D\cup J\), then place \(T\), and then take a linear extension of the complement.  This realizes \(D\cup J\) as exactly \(\operatorname{Pred}_{\pi}(T)\).  Substitution into the regression identity proves
\[
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)
 =\{\beta_J(\Gamma):J\in\mathcal J\}.
\]

For the singleton characterization, let \(J\to J\cup\{W\}\) be an ideal edge, set \(S=D\cup J\), and abbreviate
\[
 u=\gamma_{TT\cdot S},\qquad
 v=\gamma_{WW\cdot S},\qquad
 r=\gamma_{TW\cdot S},\qquad
 d=\gamma_{YT\cdot S},\qquad
 e=\gamma_{YW\cdot S},
\]
where every quantity is evaluated at \(\Gamma\).  Positive definiteness gives
\[
 u>0,\qquad v>0,\qquad uv-r^2>0.
\]
Schur complementation on \(W\) yields
\[
 \gamma_{YT\cdot(S\cup\{W\})}=d-\frac{re}{v},
 \qquad
 \gamma_{TT\cdot(S\cup\{W\})}=u-\frac{r^2}{v}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \beta_J-\beta_{J\cup\{W\}}
 &=\frac d u-\frac{d-re/v}{u-r^2/v}\\
 &=\frac{r(ue-dr)}{u(uv-r^2)}\\
 &=\frac{r\{e-dr/u\}}{uv-r^2}\\
 &=\frac{\gamma_{TW\cdot S}(\Gamma)
          \gamma_{YW\cdot(S\cup\{T\})}(\Gamma)}
         {uv-r^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The positive denominator shows that adjacent ideal values agree if and only if the product in the proposition vanishes.  The undirected graph of ideals is connected: from any nonempty ideal, deleting a maximal element leaves an ideal, and repeated deletion reaches \(\varnothing\).  Hence vanishing of every edge product makes all \(\beta_J\) equal.  Conversely, if the fiber is a singleton, every adjacent pair is equal, so every edge product vanishes.  If \(W=Y\), the second factor is the strictly positive conditional variance \(\gamma_{YY\cdot(S\cup\{T\})}\); if \(Y\in S\), both adjacent coefficients and the relevant conditional covariance are zero.  Thus no overlap, swap-margin, or genericity condition is being used at collision or boundary strata.

We construct the laws and verify each defining condition.  Fix
\[
 m=6,\qquad \eta=\frac1{10},\qquad \kappa=\frac1{20},
\]
and define
\[
 f_0(x)=(1-\eta)\frac{e^{-\sqrt2|x|}}{\sqrt2}
       +\eta c_6(1+x^2)^{-6},
 \qquad c_6=\frac{256}{63\pi}.
\]
The identities
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{-\sqrt2|x|}}{\sqrt2}\,dx=1,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+x^2)^{-6}\,dx
 =\frac{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(11/2)}{\Gamma(6)}
 =\frac{63\pi}{256}
\]
show that \(f_0\) is an even probability density.  Applying the gamma-integral representation of \((1+x^2)^{-6}\) and then the Gaussian Fourier integral gives
\[
 \phi_0(t)=\frac{1-\eta}{1+t^2/2}
 +\eta e^{-|t|}P_5(|t|),
\]
where
\[
 P_5(z)=1+z+\frac{4z^2}{9}+\frac{z^3}{9}
       +\frac{z^4}{63}+\frac{z^5}{945}.
\]
All coefficients are positive, so \(\phi_0(t)>0\) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\).

Choose an even \(C^\infty\) function \(\chi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\) equal to one on \([-1,1]\) and zero off \([-2,2]\).  For \(M\ge1\), put
\[
 \delta=\kappa M^{-2},\qquad
 \phi_{1,M}(t)=\phi_0(t)
 \left[1+\chi(t/M)\{e^{-\delta t^2/2}-1\}\right].
\]
Let \(\varphi_\delta\) denote the centered Gaussian density of variance \(\delta\), and define
\[
 \widehat h_M(t)=\phi_0(t)\{1-\chi(t/M)\}
                         \{1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\},
 \qquad
 f_{1,M}=f_0*\varphi_\delta+h_M.
\]
The Fourier transform of the displayed candidate is exactly \(\phi_{1,M}\).

We next prove that it is nonnegative.  With
\[
 b_M(z)=M^2\phi_0(Mz)\{1-\chi(z)\}
                    \{1-e^{-\kappa z^2/2}\},
\]
Fourier inversion gives
\[
 h_M(x)=\frac1{2\pi M}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iMxz}b_M(z)\,dz.
\]
For every integer \(0\le j\le12\), there is a constant \(B_j<\infty\), independent of \(M\ge1\), such that
\[
 \|b_M^{(j)}\|_1\le B_j.
\]
To verify this uniformly, note first that \(1-\chi\) vanishes on \(|z|\le1\).  On \(|z|\ge1\), for every \(0\le\ell\le12\), differentiation of the rational term gives
\[
 \left|\partial_z^\ell
   \frac{M^2}{1+M^2z^2/2}\right|
 =\left|\partial_z^\ell
   \frac{2}{z^2+2M^{-2}}\right|
 \le C_\ell |z|^{-2-\ell}.
\]
Every derivative of the Student Fourier term \(M^2e^{-M|z|}P_5(M|z|)\), away from zero, is a finite sum of terms bounded by
\[
 M^{2+\ell+r}|z|^r e^{-M|z|}
 =|z|^{-2-\ell}(M|z|)^{2+\ell+r}e^{-M|z|},
\]
with \(r\) in a fixed finite set.  Since \(M|z|\ge1\) and
\(\sup_{y\ge1}y^a e^{-y}<\infty\) for fixed \(a\), these terms are bounded by an integrable multiple of \(|z|^{-2-\ell}\), uniformly in \(M\).  Derivatives of \(1-\chi\) are supported on \(1\le|z|\le2\), and every derivative of \(1-e^{-\kappa z^2/2}\) is bounded.  The product rule proves the asserted \(L^1\) bounds.

Using the cases \(j=0\) and \(j=12\), and integrating by parts twelve times in the latter case, gives
\[
 |h_M(x)|
 \le \frac1{2\pi M}
 \min\!\left(B_0,\frac{B_{12}}{|Mx|^{12}}\right)
 \le A_{12}M^{-1}(1+M|x|)^{-12}
\]
for a constant \(A_{12}\) independent of \(M\).  Since \(\delta\le1\), retaining only the Student component and restricting the Gaussian convolution variable to \([-1,1]\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (f_0*\varphi_\delta)(x)
 &\ge \eta c_6 3^{-6}(1+x^2)^{-6}
       \Pr\{|N(0,\delta)|\le1\}\\
 &\ge a_6(1+x^2)^{-6},
 \qquad a_6=\eta c_6 3^{-7}>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(1+(x-z)^2\le3(1+x^2)\) for \(|z|\le1\), and
\(\Pr\{|N(0,\delta)|\le1\}\ge\Pr\{|N(0,1)|\le1\}>1/3\).  Because
\[
 (1+x^2)^6\le(1+M|x|)^{12}\qquad(M\ge1),
\]
we obtain
\[
 \frac{|h_M(x)|}{(f_0*\varphi_\delta)(x)}
 \le\frac{A_{12}}{a_6M}.
\]
Thus \(f_{1,M}\ge0\) for all \(M\ge2A_{12}/a_6\).  Moreover, \(\widehat h_M(0)=0\), so \(f_{1,M}\) integrates to one; it is even and hence centered.  The compensated construction is in the scalar fixed-envelope tradition of \cite{Meister2006}, while the positivity verification above is specific to the present splice.

The spatial bound implies \(\int |x|^j|h_M(x)|\,dx<\infty\) for \(0\le j\le8\).  Since \(\widehat h_M\) vanishes on a neighborhood of zero, differentiation at zero gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}x^j h_M(x)\,dx=0,
 \qquad 0\le j\le8.
\]
If \(S_6\) has density \(c_6(1+x^2)^{-6}\), beta integration yields
\[
 \mathbb E S_6^{2j}
 =\frac{\Gamma(j+1/2)\Gamma(11/2-j)}
        {\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(11/2)},
 \qquad 0\le j\le5.
\]
Combining this with the standardized-Laplace moments \((2j)!/2^j\), the even moments of \(f_0\) through order eight are
\[
 \mu_2=\frac{41}{45},\qquad
 \mu_4=\frac{227}{42},\qquad
 \mu_6=\frac{17011}{210},\qquad
 \mu_8=\frac{204121}{90}.
\]
Let \(H_0\) have density \(f_0\), and let \(H_{1,M}\) have density \(f_{1,M}\).  The vanishing moments of \(h_M\) show that the moments of \(H_{1,M}\) through order eight equal those of \(H_0+N(0,\delta)\).  Hence, for \(0\le\delta\le1\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E H_{1,M}^8
 &=\mu_8+28\delta\mu_6+210\delta^2\mu_4
   +420\delta^3\mu_2+105\delta^4\\
 &\le\frac{554293}{90}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The splice multiplier lies in \([e^{-2\kappa},1]\): if \(\chi(t/M)\ne0\), then \(|t|\le2M\), so \(e^{-\delta t^2/2}\ge e^{-2\kappa}\), and outside that support the multiplier is one.  The positive rational component of \(\phi_0\) therefore implies that both \(\phi_0\) and \(\phi_{1,M}\) are bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of \(|t|^{-2}\) for all sufficiently large \(|t|\), uniformly in large \(M\).  If either scalar law had a Gaussian convolution divisor of variance \(r>0\), the residual factor would have modulus
\[
 e^{rt^2/2}|\phi(t)|,
\]
which diverges as \(|t|\to\infty\), contradicting the unit modulus bound for a characteristic function.  Thus both scalar sources are Gaussian-indecomposable.

We now establish indistinguishability, including the order-six weighted Fourier estimate.  Fix \(v=1/10\) and set
\[
 q_{0,M}=f_0*\varphi_{v+\delta},\qquad
 q_{1,M}=f_{1,M}*\varphi_v,
 \qquad D_M=q_{1,M}-q_{0,M}.
\]
Then
\[
 \widehat D_M(t)=e^{-vt^2/2}\phi_0(t)
 \{1-\chi(t/M)\}\{1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\},
\]
so \(\widehat D_M\) vanishes on \([-M,M]\).  Retaining the Student component in \(q_{0,M}\) and restricting the Gaussian convolution variable to a fixed compact interval gives a constant \(a_v>0\), uniform in \(0\le\delta\le\kappa\), such that
\[
 q_{0,M}(x)\ge a_v(1+x^2)^{-6}.
\]
Because \(f_0\) has a finite sixth absolute moment,
\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}|\phi_0^{(\ell)}(t)|
 \le\mathbb E|H_0|^\ell<\infty,
 \qquad 0\le\ell\le6.
\]
For \(r\ge1\), derivatives of \(1-\chi(t/M)\) are bounded by constants times \(M^{-r}\) and supported on \(M\le|t|\le2M\); the zeroth derivative vanishes on \(|t|\le M\).  Also, for \(0\le r\le6\),
\[
 \sup_{M\ge1,t\in\mathbb R}
 \left|\partial_t^r\{1-e^{-\kappa t^2/(2M^2)}\}\right|<\infty,
\]
because for \(r\ge1\) the derivative is \(M^{-r}\) times a fixed polynomial in \(t/M\) times a Gaussian, while the zeroth derivative is at most one.  Finally, each derivative of \(e^{-vt^2/2}\) is a polynomial, of degree no larger than the derivative order, times the same Gaussian.  The product rule therefore gives constants \(C_j<\infty\), fixed independently of \(M\), such that
\[
 |\widehat D_M^{(j)}(t)|
 \le C_j(1+|t|)^j e^{-vt^2/2}
       \mathbf 1\{|t|\ge M\},
 \qquad 0\le j\le6.
\]
In particular, every displayed derivative lies in \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).  The distributional Fourier identity
\(\widehat{x^jD_M}=i^{-j}\widehat D_M^{(j)}\), first for smooth compactly supported Fourier truncations and then by \(L^2\) passage, together with \(\mathrm{lem:parseval\text{-}identity}\), yields
\[
 \|x^jD_M\|_2^2
 =\frac1{2\pi}\|\widehat D_M^{(j)}\|_2^2,
 \qquad 0\le j\le6.
\]
Expanding the weight and applying this identity term by term gives the exact formula
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+x^2)^6|D_M(x)|^2\,dx
 =\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{6}\binom6j
   \|\widehat D_M^{(j)}\|_2^2.
\]
For \(M\ge1\), integration by parts in the Gaussian tail gives, uniformly for \(0\le j\le6\),
\[
 \int_{|t|\ge M}(1+|t|)^{2j}e^{-vt^2}\,dt
 \le C'_j(1+M)^{12}e^{-vM^2}.
\]
The lower density bound and the weighted identity now imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})
 &=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{|D_M(x)|^2}{q_{0,M}(x)}\,dx\\
 &\le A(1+M)^{12}e^{-vM^2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
for a fixed \(A<\infty\).

Independence and Fubini give the product identity
\[
 1+\chi^2(q_{1,M}^{\otimes N},q_{0,M}^{\otimes N})
 =\{1+\chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})\}^{N}.
\]
Choose \(M^2=30\log N\).  Since \(vM^2=3\log N\),
\[
 N\chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})
 \le A N^{-2}(1+\sqrt{30\log N})^{12}\longrightarrow0.

Thus the product chi-square divergence tends to zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood-ratio difference gives
\[
 \|q_{1,M}^{\otimes N}-q_{0,M}^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\frac12
 \sqrt{\chi^2(q_{1,M}^{\otimes N},q_{0,M}^{\otimes N})}
 \longrightarrow0.
