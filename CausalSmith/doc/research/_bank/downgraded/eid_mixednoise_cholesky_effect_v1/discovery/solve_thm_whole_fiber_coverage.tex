%%% FIELD proof-whole-fiber-coverage
Fix \(P_X\in\mathcal M\), and for \(J\in\mathcal J\) put \(S_J=D\cup J\).  We first record two consequences of the spectral hypotheses that will be used on both branches of the construction.  Let \(H,H'\) be positive-definite matrices whose spectra are contained in \([\lambda,\Lambda]\), and write \(\delta=\lVert H-H'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  If \(Y\in S_J\), then the definition of partial covariance gives
\[
 \gamma_{YT\mathbin{\cdot}S_J}(H)
 =H_{YT}-H_{YS_J}H_{S_JS_J}^{-1}H_{S_JT}=0,
\]
because \(H_{YS_J}H_{S_JS_J}^{-1}\) is the coordinate row selecting \(Y\) from \(S_J\).  The denominator \(\gamma_{TT\mathbin{\cdot}S_J}(H)\) is strictly positive because it is the Schur complement of the positive-definite principal block \(H_{S_JS_J}\).  Hence \(\beta_J(H)=0\), and the same argument applies to \(H'\).

Suppose instead that \(Y\notin S_J\), and set \(B_J=S_J\cup\{T\}\).  The restriction \(T\ne Y\) in \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\text{-}targets}\) then gives \(Y\notin B_J\).  Block Gaussian elimination, equivalently the Schur-complement identity applied to the partition \(B_J=S_J\cup\{T\}\), yields
\[
 \beta_J(H)=e_T^{\mathsf T}H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}H_{B_JY}.
\]
Every principal submatrix of \(H\) and \(H'\) has smallest eigenvalue at least \(\lambda\), by \(\mathrm{ass:spectral\text{-}lower}\), while \(\mathrm{ass:spectral\text{-}upper}\) gives \(\lVert H_{B_JY}\rVert_2\le\Lambda\) and \(\lVert H'_{B_JY}\rVert_2\le\Lambda\).  Consequently
\[
 |\beta_J(H)|
 \le \lVert H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
       \lVert H_{B_JY}\rVert_2
 \le \frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}.
\]
Moreover, the inverse identity
\[
 H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}-(H'_{B_JB_J})^{-1}
 =H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}
   \bigl(H'_{B_JB_J}-H_{B_JB_J}\bigr)
   (H'_{B_JB_J})^{-1}
\]
and the fact that restriction to a submatrix cannot increase the operator norm imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\beta_J(H)-\beta_J(H')|
 &\le \lVert H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
       \lVert H_{B_JY}-H'_{B_JY}\rVert_2 \\
 &\quad+
       \lVert H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}-(H'_{B_JB_J})^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
       \lVert H'_{B_JY}\rVert_2 \\
 &\le \left(\lambda^{-1}+\Lambda\lambda^{-2}\right)\delta
 =L_\beta\delta .
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The preceding zero calculation shows that (1) also holds when \(Y\in S_J\).  Thus, for every \(J\in\mathcal J\),
\[
 |\beta_J(H)|\le\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda},
 \qquad
 |\beta_J(H)-\beta_J(H')|
 \le L_\beta\lVert H-H'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{2}
\]
This indexed estimate is consistent with the setwise bound in \(\mathrm{lem:fiber\text{-}lipschitz}\), but (2) also preserves the ideal label needed by the intervals in \(C_N\).

Consider first the fallback branch of \(\mathrm{def:ray\text{-}region}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:spectral\text{-}lower}\), \(\mathrm{ass:spectral\text{-}upper}\), and the first inequality in (2), every \(\beta_J(\Gamma)\) lies in \([ -\Lambda/\lambda,\Lambda/\lambda]\).  The exact identifying representation \(\mathrm{prop:ideal\text{-}fiber}\) therefore gives
\[
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)
 =\{\beta_J(\Gamma):J\in\mathcal J\}
 \subseteq[-\Lambda/\lambda,\Lambda/\lambda]
 =C_N
\]
with probability one on that branch.

Now suppose that the active-branch conditions \(t_N\ge1\) and \(m_N\ge2\varepsilon_N\) hold.  Let \(\mathcal E_N\) be the simultaneous event furnished by \(\mathrm{thm:fourier\text{-}region}\), on which all its ray inequalities hold and
\[
 \lVert\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le 2\Delta_N.
\tag{3}
\]
The same theorem, using \(\mathrm{ass:iid}\), gives uniformly over \(P_X\in\mathcal M\)
\[
 P_X^N(\mathcal E_N)\ge1-\alpha_N.
\tag{4}
\]
Spectral clipping in \(\mathrm{def:ray\text{-}region}\) puts the spectrum of \(\widehat\Gamma_N\) in \([\lambda,\Lambda]\); membership of \(P_X\) in \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathrm{ass:spectral\text{-}lower}\)--\(\mathrm{ass:spectral\text{-}upper}\) do the same for \(\Gamma\).  Applying (2) to (3) therefore gives, simultaneously for every ideal \(J\),
\[
 |\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)-\beta_J(\Gamma)|
 \le 2L_\beta\Delta_N.
\tag{5}
\]
Each \(\beta_J(\Gamma)\) is in the interval centered at \(\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)\) in \(\mathrm{def:fiber\text{-}region}\) by (5), and it is in the fixed clipping interval by (2).  Hence \(\mathrm{prop:ideal\text{-}fiber}\) implies on \(\mathcal E_N\) that
\[
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\subseteq C_N.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(\alpha_N=\min\{\alpha,(N+1)^{-2}\}\le\alpha\), equations (4)--(6), together with the probability-one fallback inclusion, yield for every \(N\)
\[
 \inf_{P_X\in\mathcal M}
 P_X^N\!\left\{\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\subseteq C_N\right\}
 \ge1-\alpha_N\ge1-\alpha.
\]

It remains to prove the directed excess assertion.  On the active branch and on \(\mathcal E_N\), take any \(x\in C_N\).  By the finite-union definition of \(C_N\), there is some \(J\in\mathcal J\) such that
\[
 |x-\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)|\le2L_\beta\Delta_N.
\]
Because \(\beta_J(\Gamma)\in\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\) by \(\mathrm{prop:ideal\text{-}fiber}\), (5) and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)}|x-\theta|
 \le |x-\beta_J(\Gamma)|
 \le4L_\beta\Delta_N.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(x\in C_N\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{ex}\!\left(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\right)
 \le4L_\beta\Delta_N.
\]
No step chooses a linear extension or divides by a separation between distinct effect values: positive denominators follow solely from the spectral lower bound, and repeated values of different ideal branches remain covered by the same simultaneous argument.  Finally, the empirical characteristic-function coordinates are measurable finite sums, the positive floor makes every logarithm measurable and finite, spectral clipping is continuous on symmetric matrices, and each \(\beta_J\) is continuous on the spectrally bounded positive-definite set.  Since \(\mathcal J\) is finite by \(\mathrm{ass:order}\) and finiteness of \(V\), \(C_N\) is a measurable nonempty compact random set on either branch, so all displayed coverage events are measurable.
