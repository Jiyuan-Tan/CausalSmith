%%% FIELD sr-class-construction
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\) is the class of centered observational laws \(P_X\) admitting a representation
\[
 X=AH+G_\Gamma,\qquad H\perp G_\Gamma,
\]
with \(1\le k\le p\), mutually independent coordinates \(H_1,\ldots,H_k\), \(\operatorname{Cov}(H)=I_k\), every \(H_j\) nonconstant and non-Gaussian and having no nondegenerate Gaussian convolution component, \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=k\), and the columns of \(A\) in ascending lexicographic order.  These are the observational restrictions in Sanjaroonpouri--Ramazi Assumptions 2--4; no uniform Fourier envelope, spectral compactness, loading bound, or eighth-moment bound is included in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\).

 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P_{0,N}),\preceq)=\{b(0)\},\qquad
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P_{1,N}),\preceq)=\{b(s_N)\}.

Since \(\Gamma(s)=\Gamma_\star+saa^\top\) and \(\|aa^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|a\|_2^2=54/25\), their covariance separation is
\[
 \delta_{\Gamma,N}:=\|\Gamma_{1,N}-\Gamma_{0,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =\frac{54}{25}s_N.

For an arbitrary measurable covariance estimator, define
\[
 E_N=\left\{\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma_{0,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\frac{\delta_{\Gamma,N}}2\right\}.

On \(E_N^c\), its loss under the first law is at least \(\delta_{\Gamma,N}/2\); on \(E_N\), the triangle inequality makes its loss under the second law greater than \(\delta_{\Gamma,N}/2\).  The event characterization of total variation gives

 \ge \frac{\delta_{\Gamma,N}}2(1-\tau_N).

The larger of the two risks is at least half their sum, and therefore every class containing the witnesses satisfies
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}}\sup_P
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \ge \frac{\delta_{\Gamma,N}}4(1-\tau_N)
 =\frac{27}{50}s_N(1-\tau_N).
\]
Because \(s_N=(600\log N)^{-1}\) and \(\tau_N\to0\), this is the asserted logarithmic covariance lower bound.

 \delta_{\Theta,N}:=d_H(\mathcal T_{0,N},\mathcal T_{1,N})
 =|b(s_N)-b(0)|

 \frac{s_N}{5}\le\delta_{\Theta,N}\le\frac{2s_N}{5}.

Applying the preceding event argument in the metric space \((\mathcal K(\mathbb R),d_H)\), with the ball of radius \(\delta_{\Theta,N}/2\) around \(\mathcal T_{0,N}\), gives

 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq)\right)
 \ge\frac{\delta_{\Theta,N}}4(1-\tau_N)
 =\Omega((\log N)^{-1}).

This proves both minimax lower bounds without equating covariance loss to causal-target loss.

 P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}(F_{0,N}\cap F_{1,N})
 \ge1-2\alpha-2r_N-\tau_N.

On this intersection, the definition of diameter yields

 \operatorname{diam}(S_N)\ge|b(s_N)-b(0)|.

Taking the lower limit proves the honest-diameter assertion.  The calculation of \(\delta_{\Theta,N}\) supplies its logarithmic order and uses exact singleton collisions, not a swap margin.

We construct the two laws and verify every asserted property.  Fix
\[
 m=6,\qquad \eta=\frac1{10},\qquad \kappa=\frac1{20},
\]
and define
\[
 f_0(x)=(1-\eta)\frac{e^{-\sqrt2|x|}}{\sqrt2}
       +\eta c_6(1+x^2)^{-6},
 \qquad c_6=\frac{256}{63\pi}.
\]
The beta integral shows that both summands integrate to one.  Direct evaluation of the gamma-integral representation of \((1+x^2)^{-6}\), followed by the Gaussian Fourier integral, gives the positive even characteristic function
\[
 \phi_0(t)=\frac{1-\eta}{1+t^2/2}+\eta e^{-|t|}P_5(|t|),
\]
where
\[
 P_5(z)=1+z+\frac{4z^2}{9}+\frac{z^3}{9}
       +\frac{z^4}{63}+\frac{z^5}{945}.
\]
In particular, \(\phi_0(t)>0\) for every real \(t\).

Choose an even \(C^\infty\) function \(\chi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\) that is one on \([-1,1]\) and zero outside \([-2,2]\).  For \(M\ge1\), let
\[
 \delta=\kappa M^{-2},\qquad
 \phi_{1,M}(t)=\phi_0(t)\left[1+\chi(t/M)
 \{e^{-\delta t^2/2}-1\}\right].
\]
We first verify, rather than assume, that \(\phi_{1,M}\) is a characteristic function for all sufficiently large \(M\).  If \(\varphi_\delta\) is the centered Gaussian density of variance \(\delta\), Fourier inversion gives the candidate density
\[
 f_{1,M}=f_0*\varphi_\delta+h_M,
 \qquad
 \widehat h_M(t)=\phi_0(t)\{1-\chi(t/M)\}
                    \{1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\}.
\]
Put
\[
 b_M(z)=M^2\phi_0(Mz)\{1-\chi(z)\}
                    \{1-e^{-\kappa z^2/2}\}.
\]
For each integer \(0\le j\le12\), there is a finite \(B_j\), independent of \(M\), such that
\[
 \|b_M^{(j)}\|_1\le B_j. 
\]
Here is the uniform derivative check.  The factor \(1-\chi\) vanishes on \(|z|\le1\).  On \(|z|\ge1\), for each \(\ell\le12\),
\[
 \left|\partial_z^\ell\frac{M^2}{1+M^2z^2/2}\right|
 =\left|\partial_z^\ell\frac{2}{z^2+2M^{-2}}\right|
 \le C_\ell |z|^{-2-\ell}.
\]
Every derivative of the Student term is a finite sum bounded by terms of the form
\[
 M^{2+\ell+r}|z|^r e^{-M|z|}
 =|z|^{-2-\ell}(M|z|)^{2+\ell+r}e^{-M|z|},
\]
with \(r\) in a fixed finite range.  Since \(\sup_{y\ge1}y^a e^{-y}<\infty\) for fixed \(a\), these terms have integrable bounds independent of \(M\).  Derivatives of \(\chi\) are supported on \(1\le|z|\le2\), and derivatives of \(1-e^{-\kappa z^2/2}\) are bounded by a polynomial times a Gaussian.  The product rule therefore proves the displayed \(L^1\) bounds with constants fixed before \(M\) varies.

Under the stated Fourier convention,
\[
 h_M(x)=\frac1{2\pi M}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iMxz}b_M(z)\,dz.
\]
The bounds for \(j=0\) and twelve integrations by parts using the bound for \(j=12\) give a fixed \(A_{12}<\infty\) such that
\[
 |h_M(x)|\le A_{12}M^{-1}(1+M|x|)^{-12}.
\]
For \(\delta\le1\), retaining the Student component of \(f_0\) and restricting the Gaussian convolution to \(|z|\le1\) yields
\[
 (f_0*\varphi_\delta)(x)
 \ge \eta c_6 3^{-6}(1+x^2)^{-6}
      \Pr\{|N(0,\delta)|\le1\}
 \ge a_6(1+x^2)^{-6},
 \qquad a_6=\eta c_6 3^{-7}>0.
\]
We used \(1+(x-z)^2\le3(1+x^2)\) for \(|z|\le1\) and \(\Pr\{|N(0,\delta)|\le1\}\ge1/3\).  Since \((1+x^2)^6\le(1+M|x|)^{12}\),
\[
 \frac{|h_M(x)|}{(f_0*\varphi_\delta)(x)}
 \le\frac{A_{12}}{a_6M}.
\]
Thus \(f_{1,M}\ge0\) once \(M\ge2A_{12}/a_6\).  Also \(\widehat h_M(0)=0\), so \(f_{1,M}\) integrates to one.  It is even and therefore centered.  This establishes global positivity without treating the splice multiplier itself as a characteristic function; the compensation architecture is the one used for scalar fixed-envelope lower pairs by \cite{Meister2006}.

The spatial bound makes \(|x|^8|h_M(x)|\) integrable, while \(\widehat h_M\) vanishes on a neighborhood of zero.  Differentiation of the Fourier transform at zero consequently gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}x^j h_M(x)\,dx=0,
 \qquad 0\le j\le8.
\]
For a random variable \(S_6\) with density \(c_6(1+x^2)^{-6}\), another beta-integral calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb E S_6^{2j}
 =\frac{\Gamma(j+1/2)\Gamma(11/2-j)}
        {\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(11/2)},
 \qquad 0\le j\le5.
\]
Combining this formula with the standardized-Laplace moments \((2j)!/2^j\), the even moments \(\mu_{2j}\) of \(f_0\) through order eight are
\[
 \mu_2=\frac{41}{45},\qquad
 \mu_4=\frac{227}{42},\qquad
 \mu_6=\frac{17011}{210},\qquad
 \mu_8=\frac{204121}{90}.

If \(H_{1,M}\) has density \(f_{1,M}\) and \(H_0\) has density \(f_0\), the zero-moment identities show that the first eight moments of \(H_{1,M}\) equal those of \(H_0+N(0,\delta)\).  Hence, for \(0\le\delta\le1\),

 =\mu_8+28\delta\mu_6+210\delta^2\mu_4
  +420\delta^3\mu_2+105\delta^4
 \le\frac{554293}{90}.

The splice multiplier is between \(e^{-2\kappa}\) and one: when \(\chi(t/M)\ne0\), \(|t|\le2M\) and \(e^{-\delta t^2/2}\ge e^{-2\kappa}\), while the multiplier is one off that support.  It follows from the positive rational term in \(\phi_0\) that both \(\phi_0\) and \(\phi_{1,M}\) are bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of \(|t|^{-2}\) for all sufficiently large \(|t|\).  If either source had a Gaussian divisor of variance \(r>0\), the residual characteristic function would have modulus

which diverges as \(|t|\to\infty\), contradicting the modulus bound one for a characteristic function.  Both scalar sources are therefore Gaussian-indecomposable.

We now verify the experiment comparison, including the uniform derivative and weighted-Parseval steps.  Fix \(v=1/10\) and define
\[
 q_{0,M}=f_0*\varphi_{v+\delta},\qquad
 q_{1,M}=f_{1,M}*\varphi_v,
\]
with difference \(D_M=q_{0,M}-q_{1,M}\).  Its Fourier transform, up to the immaterial sign corresponding to this ordering, is
\[
 \widehat D_M(t)=e^{-vt^2/2}\phi_0(t)
 \{1-\chi(t/M)\}\{1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\},
\]
and vanishes on \([-M,M]\).  Retaining the Student component and restricting a Gaussian convolution variable to a fixed compact interval gives a constant \(a_v>0\), uniform over \(0\le\delta\le\kappa\), such that
\[
 q_{0,M}(x)\ge a_v(1+x^2)^{-6}.
\]
Because \(f_0\) has finite sixth moment,
\[
 \sup_t|\phi_0^{(\ell)}(t)|\le\mathbb E|H_0|^\ell<\infty,
 \qquad 0\le\ell\le6.
\]
Derivatives of \(\chi(t/M)\) contribute bounded factors \(M^{-r}\), and derivatives of \(1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\), with \(\delta=\kappa M^{-2}\), are uniformly bounded for \(M\ge1\).  Derivatives of the Gaussian factor are polynomials of degree at most the derivative order times the same Gaussian.  The product rule therefore yields, for \(0\le j\le6\), constants \(C_j\) independent of \(M\) such that
\[
 |\widehat D_M^{(j)}(t)|
 \le C_j(1+|t|)^j e^{-vt^2/2}
       \mathbf 1\{|t|\ge M\}.
\]
This bound puts every displayed derivative in \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).  Fourier differentiation in \(L^2\), followed by \(\mathrm{lem:parseval\text{-}identity}\), gives
\[
 \|x^jD_M\|_2^2=\frac1{2\pi}\|\widehat D_M^{(j)}\|_2^2,
 \qquad 0\le j\le6.
\]
Expanding \((1+x^2)^6\) now proves the exact weighted identity
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+x^2)^6|D_M(x)|^2\,dx
 =\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{j=0}^6\binom6j
   \|\widehat D_M^{(j)}\|_2^2.
\]
For \(M\ge1\), one integration by parts in each Gaussian tail, after extracting the largest polynomial power, implies
\[
 \int_{|t|\ge M}(1+|t|)^{2j}e^{-vt^2}\,dt
 \le C'_j(1+M)^{12}e^{-vM^2},
 \qquad 0\le j\le6.
\]
The lower density bound and the weighted identity therefore give a fixed \(A<\infty\) with
\[
 \chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})
 =\int\frac{|D_M(x)|^2}{q_{0,M}(x)}\,dx
 \le A(1+M)^{12}e^{-vM^2}.
\]
Independence and Fubini yield
\[
 1+\chi^2(q_{1,M}^{\otimes N},q_{0,M}^{\otimes N})
 =\{1+\chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})\}^N.

Take \(M^2=30\log N\).  Since \(vM^2=3\log N\),

 N\chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})

Thus the product chi-square divergence tends to zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood-ratio difference gives
\[
 \|q_{1,M}^{\otimes N}-q_{0,M}^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(q_{1,M}^{\otimes N},q_{0,M}^{\otimes N})}

All spectral, envelope, loading, moment, greatest-divisor, and collision conditions have now been checked, so \(P_{0,N},P_{1,N}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{col}}\subseteq\mathcal M_\star\).  By \(\mathrm{prop:lower\text{-}class\text{-}sr\text{-}inclusion}\), these same \(k=1\) witnesses also lie in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\): independence is vacuous for one source, unit-variance normalization was explicit, rank is one, lexicographic ordering is vacuous, nonconstancy follows from positive variance, and Gaussian indecomposability implies both non-Gaussianity and absence of a Gaussian component.  This proves all assertions, including the published-class membership required for the lower-bound transfer.

Apply \(\mathrm{thm:splice\text{-}two\text{-}point\text{-}transfer}\) with \(\mathcal A=\mathcal M_\star\).  It gives constants \(c_\Gamma,c_\Theta>0\), independent of \(N\), such that for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \ge\frac{c_\Gamma}{\log N}
\]
and
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Theta_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Theta,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}
 d_H\!\left(\widetilde\Theta_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq)\right)
 \ge\frac{c_\Theta}{\log N}.
\]
These are two different losses: the second inequality follows from the explicitly separated singleton causal targets, not from defining effect loss to equal covariance loss.

For the matching upper bounds, \(p=3\) is fixed on \(\mathcal M_\star\) and its constants satisfy \(c=2/5<60=C\).  Thus \(\mathrm{thm:expected\text{-}log\text{-}rate}\), restricted from \(\mathcal M\) to \(\mathcal M_\star\), gives constants \(K_\Gamma,K_\Theta<\infty\) for which
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{K_\Gamma}{\log N}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}
 d_H\!\left(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq)\right)
 \le\frac{K_\Theta}{\log N}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).  The clipped covariance estimator is Borel measurable and therefore belongs to \(\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}\); the construction \(C_N\) is Borel measurable and nonempty compact-valued, including on its bounded fallback branch, and therefore belongs to \(\mathfrak E_{\Theta,N}\).  Taking these two single data-driven procedures as witnesses in the respective infima yields
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{K_\Gamma}{\log N}
\]
and
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Theta_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Theta,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}
 d_H\!\left(\widetilde\Theta_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq)\right)
 \le\frac{K_\Theta}{\log N}.
\]
Together with the lower inequalities, these prove both \(\asymp(\log N)^{-1}\) assertions over the fixed Fourier class.

Now let \((S_N)\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal M_\star)\).  The honest-confidence part of \(\mathrm{thm:splice\text{-}two\text{-}point\text{-}transfer}\), again specialized to \(\mathcal M_\star\), gives
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}
 \{\operatorname{diam}(S_N)\ge|b(s_N)-b(0)|\}\ge1-2\alpha.
\]
Its explicit separation calculation gives
\[
 |b(s_N)-b(0)|=\frac{(2/5)s_N}{1+s_N},
 \qquad s_N=\frac1{600\log N},
\]
and hence \(|b(s_N)-b(0)|\asymp(\log N)^{-1}\).  This completes the current theorem.  The same transfer theorem separately records \(\Omega((\log N)^{-1})\) covariance, causal-fiber, and honest-diameter lower bounds over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\) and over any statistical superclass containing \(\mathcal M_\star\) with the same targets on that subclass.  The matching upper bounds proved here use the additional fixed Fourier envelopes and compact spectral, loading, and moment restrictions, so no upper rate over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\) follows.
