Fix \(u\in\mathcal R\).  By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}additive\mbox{-}decomposition}\), the independence of \(U\) and \(G_\Gamma\), and the defining characteristic function of the centered Gaussian \(G_\Gamma\), for every \(t\in\mathbb R\) we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_X(tu)
&=\mathbb E\!\left[\exp\!\left\{it u^\top(U+G_\Gamma)\right\}\right]\\
&=\mathbb E\!\left[\exp\!\left\{it u^\top U\right\}\right]
  \mathbb E\!\left[\exp\!\left\{it u^\top G_\Gamma\right\}\right]\\
&=\phi_U(tu)\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{2}u^\top\Gamma u\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(|t|\geq 1\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}ray\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}envelope}\) gives \(|\phi_U(tu)|>0\).  Thus the logarithms below are finite.  Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}ray\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}envelope}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}ray\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}envelope}\) give, respectively,
\[
\log c-q_u\log(1+|t|)
\leq \log|\phi_U(tu)|
\leq \log C-q_u\log(1+|t|).
\]
Define the proof intermediate
\[
r_u(t):=-\frac{2}{t^2}\log|\phi_U(tu)|.
\]
Multiplication of the preceding inequalities by the negative number \(-2/t^2\) yields
\[
-\frac{2\log C}{t^2}
+\frac{2q_u\log(1+|t|)}{t^2}
\leq r_u(t)
\leq
-\frac{2\log c}{t^2}
+\frac{2q_u\log(1+|t|)}{t^2}.
\]
By the definition of the exponent map, \(0\leq q_u\leq Q<\infty\).  Since \(t^{-2}\to0\) and \(t^{-2}\log(1+|t|)\to0\) as \(|t|\to\infty\), both endpoints in this display converge to zero.  The squeeze theorem therefore gives
\[
r_u(t)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Taking moduli and logarithms in the characteristic-function factorization, which is legitimate because its two factors are nonzero for \(|t|\geq1\), now gives the exact identity
\[
-\frac{2}{t^2}\log|\phi_X(tu)|
=u^\top\Gamma u+r_u(t).
\]
Consequently,
\[
-\frac{2}{t^2}\log|\phi_X(tu)|
\longrightarrow u^\top\Gamma u
\qquad (|t|\to\infty).
\]

It remains to exhibit the recovery map.  The observed law \(P_X\) determines \(\phi_X(s)=\int_{\mathbb R^p}e^{is^\top x}\,dP_X(x)\).  For each \(u\in\mathcal R\), define the observable limit
\[
\ell_u(P_X):=\lim_{|t|\to\infty}
\left\{-\frac{2}{t^2}\log|\phi_X(tu)|\right\}.
\]
The preceding argument proves that this limit exists and equals \(u^\top\Gamma u\).  Hence coordinate rays give
\[
\Gamma_{ii}=\ell_{e_i}(P_X),
\]
and, for \(i<j\), pair-sum rays and polarization give
\[
\Gamma_{ij}=\Gamma_{ji}
=\frac{1}{2}\left\{
\ell_{e_i+e_j}(P_X)-\ell_{e_i}(P_X)-\ell_{e_j}(P_X)
\right\}.
\]
These displayed formulas are an explicit functional of \(P_X\) and recover every entry of the symmetric matrix \(\Gamma\).  In particular, any two admissible covariance matrices producing the same observed law under the stated ray envelopes have identical coordinate and pair-sum quadratic forms and therefore identical entries.  Finally, Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}greatest\mbox{-}divisor}\) specifies that this recovered \(\Gamma\), rather than an arbitrary covariance from another convolution representation, is the greatest Gaussian-divisor covariance that is the causal covariance target.  This proves both the asserted limit and identification from \(P_X\).
