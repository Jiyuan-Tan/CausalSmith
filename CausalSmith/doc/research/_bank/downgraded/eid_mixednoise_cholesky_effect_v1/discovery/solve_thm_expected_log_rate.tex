%%% FIELD proof-whole-fiber-coverage
Fix \(P_X\in\mathcal M\).  For \(J\in\mathcal J\), put \(S_J=D\cup J\).  We first establish the label-preserving bounds used by the intervals.  Let \(H,H'\) be positive-definite matrices whose spectra lie in \([\lambda,\Lambda]\), and write \(\delta=\|H-H'\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).

If \(Y\in S_J\), then
\[
 \gamma_{YT\cdot S_J}(H)
 =H_{YT}-H_{YS_J}H_{S_JS_J}^{-1}H_{S_JT}=0,
\]
because \(H_{YS_J}H_{S_JS_J}^{-1}\) is the row that selects coordinate \(Y\) from \(S_J\).  Also \(T\notin S_J\): every member of \(D\) strictly precedes \(T\), whereas every member of \(J\subseteq A\) is incomparable with \(T\).  Positive definiteness makes \(\gamma_{TT\cdot S_J}(H)>0\), since it is the Schur complement of \(H_{S_JS_J}\).  Thus the defining division is valid and \(\beta_J(H)=0\); the same argument applies to \(H'\).

Suppose now that \(Y\notin S_J\), and set \(B_J=S_J\cup\{T\}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\text{-}targets}\), \(Y\notin B_J\).  Eliminating the coordinates in \(S_J\) from the block normal equations for regression of coordinate \(Y\) on \(B_J\) gives
\[
 \beta_J(H)=e_T^\top H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}H_{B_JY}. \tag{1}
\]
The Schur-complement argument above again proves that the denominator in the defining ratio is strictly positive.  Principal-submatrix eigenvalues are at least \(\lambda\), and coordinate restriction cannot increase operator norm.  Hence
\[
 |\beta_J(H)|
 \le \|H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|H_{B_JY}\|_2
 \le {\Lambda\over\lambda}. \tag{2}
\]
Using the resolvent identity
\[
 H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}-(H'_{B_JB_J})^{-1}
 =H_{B_JB_J}^{-1}(H'_{B_JB_J}-H_{B_JB_J})(H'_{B_JB_J})^{-1}
\]
in (1), together with \(\|H_{B_JY}-H'_{B_JY}\|_2\le\delta\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\beta_J(H)-\beta_J(H')|
 &\le \lambda^{-1}\delta+\lambda^{-2}\delta\,\Lambda\\
 &=\bigl(\lambda^{-1}+\Lambda\lambda^{-2}\bigr)\delta
 =L_\beta\delta. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) also hold when \(Y\in S_J\), because both coefficients then vanish.

On the fallback branch, (2), \(\mathrm{prop:ideal\text{-}fiber}\), and \(\mathrm{def:fiber\text{-}region}\) give
\[
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)
 =\{\beta_J(\Gamma):J\in\mathcal J\}
 \subseteq[-\Lambda/\lambda,\Lambda/\lambda]=C_N. \tag{4}
\]
Thus coverage is certain on that branch.

On the active branch, let \(E_N\) be the simultaneous covariance event from \(\mathrm{thm:fourier\text{-}region}\).  Uniformly over \(P_X\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 P_X^N(E_N)\ge1-\alpha_N,
 \qquad
 \|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\Delta_N
 \quad\text{on }E_N. \tag{5}
\]
Spectral clipping puts \(\widehat\Gamma_N\) in the same spectral interval as \(\Gamma\).  Applying (3) to (5) gives, for every \(J\in\mathcal J\),
\[
 |\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)-\beta_J(\Gamma)|
 \le2L_\beta\Delta_N. \tag{6}
\]
Equation (2) places \(\beta_J(\Gamma)\) in the fixed clipping interval.  Therefore (6) places it in the clipped interval carrying the same label \(J\) in \(C_N\).  The ideal-fiber representation implies
\[
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\subseteq C_N
 \quad\text{on }E_N. \tag{7}
\]
Because \(\alpha_N\le\alpha\), equations (4)--(7) prove, for every \(N\),
\[
 \inf_{P_X\in\mathcal M}P_X^N
 \{\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\subseteq C_N\}
 \ge1-\alpha_N\ge1-\alpha. \tag{8}
\]

For nonempty sets \(A_0,B_0\subseteq\mathbb R\), write
\[
 \operatorname{ex}(A_0,B_0)
 =\sup_{a_0\in A_0}\inf_{b_0\in B_0}|a_0-b_0|.
\]
For the asserted excess bound, let \(x\in C_N\) on \(E_N\).  For some \(J\in\mathcal J\),
\[
 |x-\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)|\le2L_\beta\Delta_N.
\]
Since \(\beta_J(\Gamma)\in\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\), equation (6) and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)}|x-\theta|
 \le |x-\beta_J(\Gamma)|\le4L_\beta\Delta_N.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(x\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{ex}(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq))
 \le4L_\beta\Delta_N. \tag{9}
\]
The argument matches branches by their ideal labels and nowhere divides by a difference of effect values.  It consequently remains valid on exact collision strata; its only division is by the positive Schur complement protected by the spectral floor.

It remains to record measurability.  Equation (1) and continuity of inversion on matrices bounded below by \(\lambda I\) show that every map \(H\mapsto\beta_J(H)\) is continuous on the spectral set.  On the active branch, the endpoints for label \(J\) are
\[
 \max\{-\Lambda/\lambda,\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)-2L_\beta\Delta_N\},
 \qquad
 \min\{\Lambda/\lambda,\beta_J(\widehat\Gamma_N)+2L_\beta\Delta_N\}.
\]
They are measurable and correctly ordered by (2).  Since \(\mathcal J\) is finite and contains the empty ideal, their union is a measurable nonempty compact random set in the Hausdorff Borel sigma-field.  The fallback set is deterministic.  Thus the coverage events and losses used above are measurable, completing the proof.

%%% FIELD proof-expected-log-rate
Let \(d=|\mathcal R|=p(p+1)/2\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}dimension}\), \(d\) is fixed as \(N\to\infty\).  For all sufficiently large \(N\), \(\alpha_N=(N+1)^{-2}\), and \(\mathrm{def:ray\text{-}region}\) gives
\[
 t_N^2={\log N\over4\Lambda},\qquad
 m_N=c(1+t_N)^{-Q}N^{-1/4},\qquad
 \varepsilon_N=2\sqrt{{\log(4d(N+1)^2)\over N}}. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(c>0\), \(Q<\infty\), and \(\Lambda<\infty\) are fixed,
\[
 {\varepsilon_N\over m_N}
 ={2\over c}N^{-1/4}(1+t_N)^Q
 \sqrt{\log(4d(N+1)^2)}=o(1), \tag{2}
\]
since every fixed power of \(\log N\) is \(o(N^{1/4})\).  Consequently \(t_N\ge1\) and \(m_N\ge2\varepsilon_N\) eventually.  This branch choice is deterministic and uniform over \(\mathcal M\).  Since \(c<C\),
\[
 w_N=t_N^{-2}\left\{\log(C/c)+{4\varepsilon_N\over m_N}\right\}
 =O((\log N)^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \Delta_N={3p\over2}w_N=O((\log N)^{-1}). \tag{3}
\]
All implied constants depend only on the declared fixed class constants.

Let \(E_N\) be the event in \(\mathrm{thm:fourier\text{-}region}\).  On \(E_N\),
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\Delta_N,
\]
whereas, uniformly in \(P_X\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 P_X^N(E_N^c)\le\alpha_N=(N+1)^{-2}. \tag{4}
\]
On the complement, spectral clipping and \(\Gamma\preceq\Lambda I\) give
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|\widehat\Gamma_N\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2\Lambda. \tag{5}
\]
Splitting the expectation over \(E_N\) and \(E_N^c\), then using (3)--(5), yields
\[
 \sup_{P_X\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P_X^N}\|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2\Delta_N+2\Lambda\alpha_N
 =O((\log N)^{-1}). \tag{6}
\]

The deterministic inequality \(\mathrm{lem:fiber\text{-}lipschitz}\) applies on every sample outcome, including exact branch collisions, because both covariance matrices have spectra in \([\lambda,\Lambda]\).  Hence (6) implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P_X\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_{P_X^N}
 d_H\!\left(\Theta_{TY}(\widehat\Gamma_N,\preceq),
              \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq)\right)\\
 &\qquad\le L_\beta\sup_{P_X\in\mathcal M}
 \mathbb E_{P_X^N}\|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =O((\log N)^{-1}).
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]

On \(E_N\), \(\mathrm{thm:whole\text{-}fiber\text{-}coverage}\) gives inclusion of the true fiber in \(C_N\) and the directed-excess bound.  The reverse directed excess is therefore zero, and
\[
 d_H(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq))
 \le4L_\beta\Delta_N
 \quad\text{on }E_N. \tag{8}
\]
On \(E_N^c\), both sets are nonempty compact subsets of \([ -\Lambda/\lambda,\Lambda/\lambda]\).  For the true fiber this follows from \(\mathrm{prop:ideal\text{-}fiber}\) and the bound \(|\beta_J(\Gamma)|\le\Lambda/\lambda\) established in \(\mathrm{thm:whole\text{-}fiber\text{-}coverage}\).  For \(C_N\) it follows from \(\mathrm{def:fiber\text{-}region}\); nonemptiness follows because \(\mathcal J\) contains the empty ideal and each interval center is itself in the clipping interval.  Thus
\[
 d_H(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq))
 \le{2\Lambda\over\lambda}
 \quad\text{on }E_N^c. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (3), (4), (8), and (9) give
\[
 \sup_{P_X\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_{P_X^N}
 d_H(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma,\preceq))
 \le4L_\beta\Delta_N+{2\Lambda\over\lambda}\alpha_N
 =O((\log N)^{-1}). \tag{10}
\]
Equations (6), (7), and (10) are the three asserted uniform expectation bounds.  The finitely many sample sizes before the deterministic active branch begins do not affect these asymptotic statements.

Apply \(\mathrm{thm:splice\text{-}two\text{-}point\text{-}transfer}\) with \(\mathcal A=\mathcal M_\star\).  Since \(s_N=(600\log N)^{-1}\), that theorem provides constants \(c_\Gamma,c_\Theta>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \ge {c_\Gamma\over\log N} \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Theta_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Theta,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}
 d_H\!\left(\widetilde\Theta_N,
 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq)\right)
 \ge {c_\Theta\over\log N}. \tag{2}
\]
The second inequality uses the separation of the two singleton causal targets proved by \(\mathrm{thm:smooth\text{-}splice\text{-}pair}\); it does not define effect loss in terms of covariance loss.

On \(\mathcal M_\star\), \(p=3\) and \(c=2/5<60=C\).  Restricting \(\mathrm{thm:expected\text{-}log\text{-}rate}\) from \(\mathcal M\) to \(\mathcal M_\star\) gives finite constants \(K_\Gamma,K_\Theta\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widehat\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le {K_\Gamma\over\log N}, \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_\star}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}
 d_H(C_N,\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq))
 \le {K_\Theta\over\log N}. \tag{4}
\]
The measurability conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm:fourier\text{-}region}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:whole\text{-}fiber\text{-}coverage}\) show that \(\widehat\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}\) and \(C_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Theta,N}\).  These are single data-driven procedures, not law-dependent oracles.  Using them as witnesses in the respective infima turns (3)--(4) into upper bounds.  Combining those bounds with (1)--(2) proves both \(\asymp(\log N)^{-1}\) assertions.

Now let \((S_N)\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(\mathcal M_\star)\).  The honest-confidence conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:splice\text{-}two\text{-}point\text{-}transfer}\) gives

 \liminf_{N\to\infty}P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}
 \{\operatorname{diam}(S_N)\ge|b(s_N)-b(0)|\}
 \ge1-2\alpha. \tag{5}
\]
Direct calculation from \(b(s)=(3/5+s)/(1+s)\) gives
\[
 |b(s_N)-b(0)|={2s_N\over5(1+s_N)},
 \qquad s_N={1\over600\log N}. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(0<s_N\le1\) eventually, the quantity in (6) lies between \(s_N/5\) and \(2s_N/5\), and is therefore \(\asymp(\log N)^{-1}\).  Equations (1)--(6) prove every assertion.  The superclass lower conclusions, including those over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\), are separately stated in \(\mathrm{thm:splice\text{-}two\text{-}point\text{-}transfer}\); no upper rate over that larger class is used here.  The upper construction here uses the uniform Fourier envelopes and compact spectral bounds of \(\mathcal M\), not a global loading cap.

%%% FIELD proof-smooth-splice-pair
We construct the two laws and verify every class condition.  Fix
\[
 m=6,\qquad \eta={1\over10},\qquad \kappa={1\over20},
\]
and define
\[
 f_0(x)=(1-\eta){e^{-\sqrt2|x|}\over\sqrt2}
       +\eta c_6(1+x^2)^{-6},
 \qquad c_6={256\over63\pi}.
\]
The beta integral gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+x^2)^{-6}\,dx
 ={\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(11/2)\over\Gamma(6)}={63\pi\over256},
\]
whereas the first summand is the standardized-Laplace density.  Thus \(f_0\) is an even probability density.  Using
\[
 (1+x^2)^{-6}={1\over\Gamma(6)}
 \int_0^\infty r^5e^{-r(1+x^2)}\,dr
\]
and \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx-rx^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi/r}\,e^{-t^2/(4r)}\), followed by the half-integer gamma recursion, yields
\[
 \phi_0(t)={1-\eta\over1+t^2/2}+\eta e^{-|t|}P_5(|t|), \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
 P_5(z)=1+z+{4z^2\over9}+{z^3\over9}
       +{z^4\over63}+{z^5\over945}.
\]
Every coefficient is positive, so \(\phi_0(t)>0\).

Choose an even \(C^\infty\) function \(\chi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\), equal to one on \([-1,1]\) and zero outside \([-2,2]\).  For \(M\ge1\), set
\[
 \delta=\kappa M^{-2},\qquad
 \phi_{1,M}(t)=\phi_0(t)
 \left[1+\chi(t/M)\{e^{-\delta t^2/2}-1\}\right]. \tag{2}
\]
Let \(\varphi_\delta\) be the centered Gaussian density of variance \(\delta\).  Define \(h_M\) by the inverse Fourier transform of the first expression below, and then define \(f_{1,M}\) by
\[
 \widehat h_M(t)=\phi_0(t)\{1-\chi(t/M)\}
                 \{1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\},
 \qquad f_{1,M}=f_0*\varphi_\delta+h_M. \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) show algebraically that the Fourier transform of \(f_{1,M}\) is \(\phi_{1,M}\).

We prove positivity rather than presupposing that (2) is a characteristic function.  Put
\[
 b_M(z)=M^2\phi_0(Mz)\{1-\chi(z)\}
             \{1-e^{-\kappa z^2/2}\}.
\]
Fourier inversion and the substitution \(t=Mz\) give
\[
 h_M(x)={1\over2\pi M}
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iMxz}b_M(z)\,dz. \tag{4}
\]
For every integer \(0\le j\le12\), there is \(B_j<\infty\), independent of \(M\ge1\), such that
\[
 \|b_M^{(j)}\|_1\le B_j. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, \(1-\chi\) vanishes for \(|z|\le1\).  On \(|z|\ge1\), for \(0\le\ell\le12\),
\[
 \left|\partial_z^\ell{M^2\over1+M^2z^2/2}\right|
 =\left|\partial_z^\ell{2\over z^2+2M^{-2}}\right|
 \le C_\ell |z|^{-2-\ell}. \tag{6}
\]
By (1), every derivative of the Student term \(M^2e^{-M|z|}P_5(M|z|)\), away from zero, is a finite sum bounded by terms of the form
\[
 M^{2+\ell+r}|z|^re^{-M|z|}
 =|z|^{-2-\ell}(M|z|)^{2+\ell+r}e^{-M|z|}.
\]
Since \(M|z|\ge1\) and \(\sup_{y\ge1}y^ae^{-y}<\infty\) for every fixed \(a\), these terms have uniform integrable bounds after the product rule is applied.  Derivatives of \(1-\chi\) are supported on \(1\le|z|\le2\), and positive-order derivatives of \(1-e^{-\kappa z^2/2}\) are polynomial multiples of a Gaussian.  The zeroth-order factor is bounded by one.  These facts prove (5).

Using \(j=0\) in (5), and integrating (4) by parts twelve times using \(j=12\), gives a constant \(A_{12}\), independent of \(M\), such that
\[
 |h_M(x)|\le A_{12}M^{-1}(1+M|x|)^{-12}. \tag{7}
\]
For \(\delta\le1\), retain the Student component in the convolution and restrict the Gaussian variable to \([-1,1]\).  Since \(1+(x-z)^2\le3(1+x^2)\) there and \(\Pr\{|N(0,\delta)|\le1\}>1/3\),
\[
 (f_0*\varphi_\delta)(x)
 \ge a_6(1+x^2)^{-6},
 \qquad a_6=\eta c_6 3^{-7}>0. \tag{8}
\]
For \(M\ge1\), \((1+x^2)^6\le(1+M|x|)^{12}\).  Equations (7)--(8) imply
\[
 |h_M(x)|\le {A_{12}\over a_6M}(f_0*\varphi_\delta)(x).
\]
Thus \(f_{1,M}\ge0\) for \(M\ge2A_{12}/a_6\).  Also \(\widehat h_M(0)=0\), so \(f_{1,M}\) integrates to one; it is even and hence centered.

Equation (7) makes \(\int |x|^j|h_M(x)|\,dx\) finite for \(0\le j\le8\).  Since \(\widehat h_M\) vanishes on a neighborhood of zero, differentiating its Fourier transform at zero gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}x^jh_M(x)\,dx=0,
 \qquad 0\le j\le8. \tag{9}
\]
If \(S_6\) has density \(c_6(1+x^2)^{-6}\), beta integration gives
\[
 \mathbb ES_6^{2j}
 ={\Gamma(j+1/2)\Gamma(11/2-j)\over
   \sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(11/2)},
 \qquad0\le j\le5.
\]
Combining this identity with the standardized-Laplace moments \((2j)!/2^j\) yields
\[
 \mu_2={41\over45},\qquad
 \mu_4={227\over42},\qquad
 \mu_6={17011\over210},\qquad
 \mu_8={204121\over90}. \tag{10}
\]
Let \(H_0\) have density \(f_0\), and let \(H_{1,M}\) have density \(f_{1,M}\).  By (9), the moments of \(H_{1,M}\) through order eight equal those of \(H_0+N(0,\delta)\).  Therefore, for \(0\le\delta\le1\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb EH_{1,M}^8
 &=\mu_8+28\delta\mu_6+210\delta^2\mu_4
   +420\delta^3\mu_2+105\delta^4\\
 &\le {554293\over90}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{11}
\]

The bracketed multiplier in (2) lies in \([e^{-2\kappa},1]\).  Indeed, on the support of \(\chi(t/M)\), \(|t|\le2M\), whence \(e^{-\delta t^2/2}\ge e^{-2\kappa}\), and off that support the multiplier is one.  The positive rational summand in (1) therefore gives two-sided polynomial bounds of order \(|t|^{-2}\), with constants independent of sufficiently large \(M\), for both \(\phi_0\) and \(\phi_{1,M}\).  If either scalar law had a Gaussian divisor of variance \(r>0\), the residual characteristic-function modulus would be \(e^{rt^2/2}|\phi(t)|\), which diverges by the polynomial lower bound but must be at most one.  Both scalar sources are consequently Gaussian-indecomposable.

We next prove vanishing product divergence.  Fix \(v=1/10\), and let
\[
 q_{0,M}=f_0*\varphi_{v+\delta},\qquad
 q_{1,M}=f_{1,M}*\varphi_v,
 \qquad D_M=q_{1,M}-q_{0,M}.
\]
Their Fourier difference is
\[
 \widehat D_M(t)=e^{-vt^2/2}\phi_0(t)
 \{1-\chi(t/M)\}\{1-e^{-\delta t^2/2}\}, \tag{12}
\]
which vanishes on \([-M,M]\).  Retaining the Student component in \(q_{0,M}\) and restricting the Gaussian convolution variable to \([-1,1]\) gives a constant \(a_v>0\), uniform in \(0\le\delta\le\kappa\), such that
\[
 q_{0,M}(x)\ge a_v(1+x^2)^{-6}. \tag{13}
\]

The density \(f_0\) has a finite sixth absolute moment, so differentiation under its Fourier integral gives
\[
 \sup_t|\phi_0^{(\ell)}(t)|
 \le\mathbb E|H_0|^\ell<\infty,
 \qquad0\le\ell\le6. \tag{14}
\]
For \(r\ge1\), derivatives of \(1-\chi(t/M)\) are bounded by constants times \(M^{-r}\) and supported on \(M\le|t|\le2M\); its zeroth derivative vanishes on \(|t|\le M\).  Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{M\ge1,t\in\mathbb R}
 \left|\partial_t^r\{1-e^{-\kappa t^2/(2M^2)}\}\right|<\infty,
 \qquad0\le r\le6, \tag{15}
\]
because for \(r\ge1\) the derivative is \(M^{-r}\) times a fixed polynomial in \(t/M\) times a Gaussian, and the zeroth derivative is at most one.  Each derivative of \(e^{-vt^2/2}\) is a polynomial of degree at most its order times that Gaussian.  The product rule in (12), with (14)--(15), therefore supplies constants \(C_j\), independent of \(M\), for which
\[
 |\widehat D_M^{(j)}(t)|
 \le C_j(1+|t|)^je^{-vt^2/2}\mathbf 1\{|t|\ge M\},
 \qquad0\le j\le6. \tag{16}
\]
In particular, all displayed derivatives belong to \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).

To justify the weighted Fourier identity, multiply \(\widehat D_M\) by smooth compactly supported cutoffs converging to one.  For each cutoff, differentiation under Fourier inversion gives \(\widehat{x^jD}=i^{-j}\widehat D^{(j)}\).  The Parseval identity in \(\mathrm{lem:parseval\text{-}identity}\), together with (16), makes the functions and derivatives Cauchy in \(L^2\).  Their limits agree with \(D_M\) and \(x^jD_M\) as distributions, hence almost everywhere.  Thus
\[
 \|x^jD_M\|_2^2={1\over2\pi}\|\widehat D_M^{(j)}\|_2^2,
 \qquad0\le j\le6. \tag{17}
\]
Expanding \((1+x^2)^6\) and using (17) gives the exact identity
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+x^2)^6|D_M(x)|^2\,dx
 ={1\over2\pi}\sum_{j=0}^6{6\choose j}
 \|\widehat D_M^{(j)}\|_2^2. \tag{18}
\]
Repeated integration by parts in the one-dimensional Gaussian tail gives, uniformly for \(0\le j\le6\) and \(M\ge1\),
\[
 \int_{|t|\ge M}(1+|t|)^{2j}e^{-vt^2}\,dt
 \le C'_j(1+M)^{12}e^{-vM^2}. \tag{19}
\]
Equations (13), (16), (18), and (19) imply, for a constant \(A<\infty\) independent of \(M\),
\[
 \chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})
 =\int {D_M(x)^2\over q_{0,M}(x)}\,dx
 \le A(1+M)^{12}e^{-vM^2}. \tag{20}
\]
For product densities, multiplication of likelihood ratios and Fubini's theorem give
\[
 1+\chi^2(q_{1,M}^{\otimes N},q_{0,M}^{\otimes N})
 =\{1+\chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})\}^{N}. \tag{21}
\]
Choose \(M^2=30\log N\).  Since \(vM^2=3\log N\), (20) gives
\[
 N\chi^2(q_{1,M},q_{0,M})
 \le A N^{-2}(1+\sqrt{30\log N})^{12}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Equation (21) then implies that the product chi-square divergence tends to zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood-ratio difference yields
\[
 \|q_{1,M}^{\otimes N}-q_{0,M}^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le{1\over2}\sqrt{\chi^2(q_{1,M}^{\otimes N},q_{0,M}^{\otimes N})}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{22}
\]

Embed this scalar experiment in three dimensions.  Set
\[
 a=(1,2/5,1)^\top,\qquad
 \overline\Gamma=\Gamma_\star-vaa^\top.
\]
The successive leading principal minors of \(\overline\Gamma\) are
\[
 {9\over10},\qquad {189\over250},\qquad {294\over625},
\]
which are positive.  Gaussian elimination without row exchanges factors \(\overline\Gamma\) as \(R\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2/d_1,d_3/d_2)R^\top\), where \(R\) is nonsingular unit lower triangular and \(d_j\) is the \(j\)-th displayed leading principal minor.  All three diagonal entries are positive, so \(x^\top\overline\Gamma x>0\) for every nonzero \(x\), proving \(\overline\Gamma\succ0\).  Apply to both scalar experiments the same Markov kernel \(z\mapsto az+G_{\overline\Gamma}\), independently across observations.  Total variation contracts under a Markov kernel because integration of the image of a signed measure against a function bounded by one is bounded by the original total-variation norm.  The image laws, with labels matching the theorem, are
\[
 P_{0,N}=\mathcal L(aH_{1,M}+G_{\Gamma_\star}),
 \qquad
 P_{1,N}=\mathcal L(aH_0+G_{\Gamma_\star+\delta aa^\top}). \tag{23}

They possess convolution densities and are Borel laws.  Equations (22)--(23) imply
\[
 \|P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}-P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 s_N=\delta={\kappa\over M^2}={1\over600\log N}. \tag{24}

We verify membership in the fixed class.  The successive leading principal minors of \(\Gamma_\star-I/10\) are

 {9\over10},\qquad {13\over20},\qquad {8109\over25000},

and those of \(4I-\Gamma(1)\) are

 2,\qquad {126\over25},\qquad {44\over125}.

The same elimination factorization has positive pivots for both matrices.  Hence \(\Gamma_\star-I/10\succ0\) and \(4I-\Gamma(1)\succ0\).  Since \(s\mapsto\Gamma(s)\) is nondecreasing in Loewner order, it follows that
\[
 {1\over10}I\prec\Gamma(s)\prec4I,
 \qquad0\le s\le1. \tag{25}
\]

For a coordinate or pair-sum ray \(u\in\mathcal R\),
\[
 r:=|a^\top u|\in\left\{{2\over5},1,{7\over5},2\right\}.
\]
Elementary differentiation of the rational function, with the extrema attained among these endpoint values, gives, for \(t\ge1\),
\[
 {1\over2}\le{(1+t)^2\over1+r^2t^2/2}\le{27\over2}. \tag{26}

The rational component of (1) and the lower bound on the splice multiplier therefore imply
\[
 (1+t)^2|\phi_U(tu)|
 \ge{9\over10}{1\over2}e^{-2\kappa}
 ={9\over20}e^{-1/10}>{2\over5}. \tag{27}
\]
For the upper bound, expand \((1+z)^2P_5(z)=\sum_kd_kz^k\).  Direct summation gives
\[
 \sum_kd_kk!={4714\over63}.

Since \(e^{-z}z^k\le k!\) for \(z\ge0\), and \((1+t)^2\le(25/4)(1+rt)^2\) for \(r\ge2/5\), equations (1) and (26) yield

 \begin{aligned}
 (1+t)^2|\phi_U(tu)|
 &\le {9\over10}{27\over2}
 +{1\over10}{25\over4}{4714\over63}\\
 &={37117\over630}<60.
 \end{aligned} \tag{28}
\]
The splice multiplier is at most one, so (28) applies to both source laws; evenness handles \(t\le-1\).  Thus all named rays obey the common exponent-two envelopes with lower constant \(2/5\) and upper constant \(60\).

By (10)--(11), either scalar source used in (23) has eighth moment at most \(554293/90\).  If \(Z_3\sim N(0,I_3)\), then \(\mathbb E\|Z_3\|^8=3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot9\).  Since \(\|a\|_2^2=54/25\), \(\|\Gamma^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\) by (25), and \((x+y)^8\le2^7(x^8+y^8)\),

 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\|aH+G_\Gamma\|^8
 &\le2^7\left\{
 \left({54\over25}\right)^4{554293\over90}
 +4^4(3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot9)\right\}\\
 &<50000000. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{29}
\]
Normalize each scalar source by its positive standard deviation and multiply its loading by the same standard deviation.  This leaves \(U=aH\), all ray characteristic functions, and both observed laws unchanged, while producing a unit-variance source.  Its variance is at most \(41/45+1=86/45\), so the resulting one-column loading obeys
\[
 \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
 \le{54\over25}{86\over45}={516\over125}<9. \tag{30}
\]
It therefore has norm less than three and rank one.  Its first entry is positive, and the column-order condition is vacuous.  Unit variance gives nonconstancy; nonzero rescaling preserves Gaussian indecomposability, which also implies non-Gaussianity for this nonconstant source.

For arbitrary \(u\in\mathbb R^3\), the source characteristic function is \(\phi_H(ta^\top u)\).  If \(a^\top u\ne0\), the two-sided polynomial bounds imply \(\log|\phi_H(ta^\top u)|=O(\log|t|)\); if \(a^\top u=0\), it equals one.  Therefore each constructed law satisfies
\[
 -2t^{-2}\log|\phi_X(tu)|\longrightarrow u^\top\Gamma u. \tag{31}
\]
Let \(\Delta\) be the covariance of any Gaussian convolution divisor of that law.  Its residual characteristic function has modulus at most one, so
\[
 e^{t^2u^\top\Delta u/2}|\phi_X(tu)|\le1.
\]
Taking logarithms, dividing by \(t^2/2\), and using (31) gives \(u^\top\Delta u\le u^\top\Gamma u\) for every \(u\), hence \(\Delta\preceq\Gamma\).  The displayed \(\Gamma\) in (23) is itself a Gaussian divisor by the independent additive construction, so it is the greatest Gaussian-divisor covariance.

Finally, direct substitution into \(\Gamma(s)=\Gamma_\star+saa^\top\) gives

 \Gamma_{WY}(s)-{\Gamma_{WT}(s)\Gamma_{TY}(s)\over\Gamma_{TT}(s)}=0,
 \qquad
 {\Gamma_{TY}(s)\over\Gamma_{TT}(s)}
 ={3/5+s\over1+s}=b(s). \tag{32}

Under the partial order whose only relation is \(T\prec Y\), one has \(D=\varnothing\), \(A=\{W\}\), and ideals \(\varnothing\) and \(\{W\}\).  The first equality in (32) makes the sole ideal-edge collision product in \(\mathrm{prop:ideal\text{-}fiber}\) vanish, while the second gives the empty-ideal value.  Hence

 \Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(s),\preceq)=\{b(s)\},
 \qquad0\le s\le1. \tag{33}

Equations (24)--(33), together with (23), verify all spectral, envelope, moment, loading, rank, normalization, sign, order, greatest-divisor, and collision conditions.  Thus, for all sufficiently large \(N\),

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop:lower\text{-}class\text{-}sr\text{-}inclusion}\) places both witnesses in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\).  This completes the construction.

By \(\mathrm{thm:smooth\text{-}splice\text{-}pair}\), for all sufficiently large \(N\), both witnesses lie in \(\mathcal M_\star\),

and their target values are

 \Gamma_{0,N}=\Gamma(0),\quad \Gamma_{1,N}=\Gamma(s_N),

\]
By \(\mathrm{prop:lower\text{-}class\text{-}sr\text{-}inclusion}\), the witnesses also lie in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{SR}}\), and they lie in every stipulated superclass containing \(\mathcal M_\star\).  The required agreement of target maps makes the displayed target values unchanged in each such class.

Since \(\Gamma(s)=\Gamma_\star+saa^\top\) and \(\|aa^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|a\|_2^2=54/25\),
\[
 \delta_{\Gamma,N}:=\|\Gamma_{1,N}-\Gamma_{0,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 ={54\over25}s_N. \tag{1}
\]
For any \(\widetilde\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}\), define the measurable event
\[
 E_N=\left\{\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma_{0,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 <{\delta_{\Gamma,N}\over2}\right\}.
\]
On \(E_N^c\), the loss under \(P_{0,N}\) is at least \(\delta_{\Gamma,N}/2\).  On \(E_N\), the triangle inequality makes the loss under \(P_{1,N}\) greater than \(\delta_{\Gamma,N}/2\).  By the definition of total variation,
\[
 P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}(E_N)
 \ge P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}(E_N)-\tau_N.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_{P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}}
   \|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma_{0,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\mathbb E_{P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}}
   \|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma_{1,N}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &\qquad\ge{\delta_{\Gamma,N}\over2}(1-\tau_N).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The larger of the two risks is at least half their sum.  Thus every class \(\mathcal A\) containing the witnesses satisfies
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\Gamma_N\in\mathfrak E_{\Gamma,N}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal A}
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes N}}\|\widetilde\Gamma_N-\Gamma(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \ge {\delta_{\Gamma,N}\over4}(1-\tau_N)
 ={27\over50}s_N(1-\tau_N). \tag{2}
\]
Because \(s_N=(600\log N)^{-1}\), this is the asserted covariance lower bound.

For the distinct causal target,

 \delta_{\Theta,N}:=d_H(\mathcal T_{0,N},\mathcal T_{1,N})
 =|b(s_N)-b(0)|={2s_N\over5(1+s_N)}. \tag{3}

 {s_N\over5}\le\delta_{\Theta,N}\le{2s_N\over5}. \tag{4}

Finally, let \((S_N)\) be uniformly honest over such a class \(\mathcal A\), and put

 h_N=\inf_{P\in\mathcal A}P^{\otimes N}
 \{\Theta_{TY}(\Gamma(P),\preceq)\subseteq S_N\}.

The honesty assumption says \(\liminf_Nh_N\ge1-\alpha\).  Define

 r_N=\left[(1-\alpha)-\inf_{m\ge N}h_m\right]_++N^{-1}.

Then \(r_N\to0\) and \(h_N\ge1-\alpha-r_N\).  Let

 F_{0,N}=\{b(0)\in S_N\},\qquad
 F_{1,N}=\{b(s_N)\in S_N\}.

These events are measurable because, for fixed \(x\), the map \(K\mapsto\inf_{y\in K}|x-y|\) is one-Lipschitz in Hausdorff distance, and \(\{K:x\in K\}\) is its zero set.  Honesty at the two singleton targets gives

 P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}(F_{0,N})\ge1-\alpha-r_N,
 \qquad
 P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}(F_{1,N})\ge1-\alpha-r_N.
\]
Transferring the second event by total variation and applying the union bound under \(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}\) yields
\[
 P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}(F_{0,N}\cap F_{1,N})
 \ge1-2\alpha-2r_N-\tau_N. \tag{6}
\]
On this intersection, \(S_N\) contains both points, and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(S_N)\ge|b(s_N)-b(0)|.
