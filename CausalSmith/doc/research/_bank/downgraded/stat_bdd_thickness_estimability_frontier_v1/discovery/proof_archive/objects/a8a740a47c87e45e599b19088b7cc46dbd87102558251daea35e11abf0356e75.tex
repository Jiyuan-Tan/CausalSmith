Apply \(\mathrm{thm:global\text{-}honest\text{-}band\text{-}and\text{-}width\text{-}obstruction}\) with its displayed \(M_n\), side radii \(B_{t,n}(x)\), and interval \(\mathcal C_n(x)\). Its measurability and coverage conclusions give, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\),
\[
P\{\tau_P(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal B\}\ge1-\gamma. \tag{38}
\]
Taking the infimum over the declared nonempty union preserves (38). Because each radius is nonnegative, the displayed interval has diameter \(2\{B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)\}\). Thus its half-width satisfies
\[
W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x). \tag{39}
\]
The pervasive-width conclusion of the cited theorem and (39) imply, with a constant uniform over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
\mathbb E_P\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)\right]
\le Cr_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}} \tag{40}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For the last assertion, fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and put \(A_j=\{\tau_{P_j}(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\}\). Coverage and total variation give
\[
P_0(A_0)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n,
\qquad
P_0(A_1)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n-
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{41}
\]
Hence
\[
P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
\ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{42}
\]
On this intersection, the interval contains both target values, so its half-width is at least half their separation. Multiplying this implication by the indicator of the intersection, using the positive part of (42), and taking expectation under \(P_0\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)\ge
\frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
\left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+. \tag{43}
\]
Equations (38), (40), and (43) are precisely the three positive conclusions claimed. The invoked theorem uses a global \(\log M_n\) calibration and full-experiment total variation, so this deduction neither proves a uniform local-oracle width ratio nor replaces full-experiment distinguishability by score-law distinguishability.
