Define \(m\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) as in the statement.  First we check that they are well typed.  Positivity of \(c_m\), the restriction \(\kappa>2\), and \(h>0\) give \(m(h)>0\), while \(m\) is nondecreasing because \(\kappa>0\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), the certified constants make \(P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}\) a member of \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  Hence \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) and the fact that probabilities do not exceed one imply, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
 0<m(h)=c_mh^\kappa
 \le q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(x,h)
 \le1. \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(m:(0,h_0]\to[0,1]\).  For fixed \(h\), \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,\cdot)\) is a nondecreasing \(\mathbb N_0\)-valued function on \([0,1]\).  Therefore both objects are valid inputs to \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).

We next prove the two class inclusions.  Let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), \(P\) has the sharp-assignment, consistency, global boundary-entropy, Lipschitz-regression, bounded-trace, and exact Gaussian-error properties required by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  The polynomial lower-mass assumption additionally gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h). \tag{2}
\]
This is exactly \(\mathrm{ass:declared\text{-}modulus\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\).

For fixed \(h\), the sets
\[
 \left\{x\in\mathcal B:
 \min_{t\in\{0,1\}}q_{t,P}(x,h)\le u\right\}
\]
are nested increasingly in \(u\).  Their maximal \(h\)-separated cardinalities \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) are therefore nondecreasing in \(u\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then
\[
 \mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u). \tag{3}
\]
If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), monotonicity and \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\) give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{4}
\]
Because \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), equation (4) implies
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u). \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) verify \(\mathrm{ass:declared\text{-}profile\text{-}lower}\).  Together with (2) and the common member properties, the definition of the modulus--profile class yields
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{6}
\]

Now let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  The base-class part of \(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) again supplies all common properties and (2).  At and above the threshold, monotonicity in \(u\) and \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u),
 \qquad u\ge C_mh^\kappa. \tag{7}
\]
Below the threshold, (3) applies.  Hence the two declared conditions hold, and
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{8}
\]
The circular membership lemma supplies a member of each subclass.  In particular, (6)--(8) show that \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\) is nonempty, as required by \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).

We turn to the minimax lower bound.  If nonempty classes satisfy \(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\), then every sample-measurable estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\) obeys
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right]. \tag{9}
\]
Taking infima over the same estimator set in (9) proves monotonicity of \(R_n\) under class inclusion.  Apply this fact to (6), and then invoke the pervasive lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{10}
\]

For the upper bound, put
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Then \(s_n\downarrow0\), and
\[
 \frac{s_n}{h_0/n}
 =h_0^{-1}n^{1-1/(\kappa+2)}(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{11}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), equation (11) implies that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), there is a dyadic \(h_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, choose the smallest grid point not below \(s_n\); (11) keeps its index strictly between the two endpoints of the grid.

Since \(m(h_n)=c_mh_n^\kappa\), equations (12) and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C_0\log n\) for large \(n\) give
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c_0\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c_0\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{13}
\]
Consequently, for the fixed eligibility constant \(A\) in \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\), the bandwidth \(h_n\) is feasible in that theorem for every sufficiently large \(n\).  Moreover, \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), so (12) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Lh_n+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}
 &\le C_1\left\{s_n+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}\\
 &=C_2s_n. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{14}
\]
The clipped count-adaptive estimator in the arbitrary-modulus bracket theorem is a single sample-measurable estimator and therefore is admissible in the infimum defining \(R_n\).  Evaluate that theorem's uniform upper guarantee at the feasible \(h_n\).  Equations (13)--(14) imply
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \le C s_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{15}
\]
Together, (10) and (15) prove the asserted matched frontier under the unchanged exact Gaussian model.

Finally, the definitions of the two displayed polynomial rates give
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{16}
\]
Thus the class indexed by the one-site lower declaration contains the pervasive subclass and has the pervasive, rather than isolated, minimax rate.  The additional upper packing restriction required by \(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) is absent from \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\); an upper or intrinsic two-sided profile is therefore necessary to exclude this explicit counterexample and distinguish the two regimes.
