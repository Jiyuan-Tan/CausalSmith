Every member of \(\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\) belongs, by \(\mathrm{def:triangular\text{-}profile\text{-}union}\), to a polynomial base class and therefore satisfies the member conditions in \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\). Fix such a member \(P\). For \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(s>0\), set \(b=\min\{h_0,s/2\}\). The polynomial lower-mass condition gives
\[
 P_X\{X\in\mathcal A_t:d(X,x)<s\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,b)\ge c_mb^\kappa>0. \tag{22}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\). The Lipschitz side-regression representative is continuous at \(x\), so the trace definition implies
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{23}
\]
Complementarity of the assignment regions and sharp assignment make the observations in a side disk exactly those with \(T_i=t\). For a selected side fit with positive count \(N\), (23) and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) bound the conditional bias by \(L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\). If \(N=0\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\) makes the fit zero and \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\) bounds its error by \(L\).

Conditionally on the scores, \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) and \(|\mathscr S_n|\le M_n\) imply
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}
 >\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}\le\gamma. \tag{24}
\]
Therefore, with conditional probability at least \(1-\gamma\), every nonempty candidate average satisfies
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N}. \tag{25}
\]
Adding the corresponding bias bound, or the zero-count trace bound, shows simultaneously over \(x\) and \(t\) that the side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\). The triangle inequality then places \(\tau_P(x)=\theta_{1,P}(x)-\theta_{0,P}(x)\) in the displayed interval for every \(x\). Integrating the conditional probability proves the finite-sample guarantee uniformly over the triangular union. The counts and fits are Borel functions of the sample, the grid is finite, and the bandwidth rule has a deterministic tie break and an explicit empty-set fallback; hence the band is data measurable.

We next establish its base-class expected-width upper bound. Put
\[
 r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
\]
and choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\), possible for all sufficiently large \(n\) by \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\). A maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points and covers \(\mathcal B\), by global boundary entropy and maximality. At every net point and on both sides, polynomial lower mass gives binomial success probability at least \(c_m(h_*/2)^\kappa\). If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) gives
\[
 \Pr\{N\le nq/2\}
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq(1-\log2)/2}
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{26}
\]
A union bound over the net and both sides, followed by \(B(z,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\) when \(d(x,z)\le h_*/2\), constructs an event \(G_n\) such that
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,h_*)
 \ge cnh_*^\kappa,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnh_*^\kappa}. \tag{27}
\]
The fixed constants in (27) depend only on the declared envelopes. On \(G_n\),
\[
 \frac{nh_*^\kappa}{\log(e+n/h_*)}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad nh_*^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n, \tag{28}
\]
uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\). Thus \(h_*\) is admissible for every \(x,t\) eventually. The minimizing property of \(\widehat h_{t,n}\), comparison with \(h_*\), and (27)--(28) yield
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{29}
\]
Since \(M_n\le Cn^3(1+\log n)\), fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1)\) gives \(\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C_\gamma\log n\). Also \(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log n\) for \(0<b\le h_0<1\). Hence (29) implies
\[
 \sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\mathbf 1_{G_n}\le C_\gamma r. \tag{30}
\]
On \(G_n^c\), whether the selected count is zero or positive,
\[
 B_{t,n}(x)\le L+\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}. \tag{31}
\]
Equations (27)--(28) make the expectation of the right-hand side of (31), multiplied by \(\mathbf1_{G_n^c}\), equal to \(o(r)\). Since the displayed band has \(W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)\), (30)--(31) prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
 \le C_\gamma r. \tag{32}
\]

The converse is in fact valid more generally than the specialization written in the target statement. The geometry-general result \(\mathrm{lem:geometry\text{-}general\text{-}pervasive\text{-}band\text{-}width\text{-}obstruction}\) starts from an arbitrary member of any nonempty \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), retains its score law and assignment geometry, and derives a Gaussian disjoint-tent family satisfying the class conditions and a tunable entropy bound. It proves, for every fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
 \ge c_\gamma r. \tag{33}
\]
Thus neither circular geometry nor a small-miscoverage restriction is needed for (33). In particular, under the circular specialization appearing in the target statement, \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) verifies nonemptiness and (33) proves the stated lower clause whenever its displayed small-\(\gamma\) condition holds. Since \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\), any band honest over the base class is honest over the pervasive subclass and its base-class supremum is no smaller. Combining (32)--(33) proves the matched global expected-supremum half-width frontier. The same geometry-general lemma also proves a pointwise local-benchmark ratio lower bound on every nonempty pervasive component; it does not prove the local-oracle upper bound over an arbitrary triangular union.

It remains to prove the geometry-general two-law inequality. Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), and let \(A_j\) be the event that \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains \(\tau_{P_j}(x)\). Honesty gives \(P_j^{\otimes n}(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\). By the definition of total variation between the full observed product laws,
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 \ge P_1^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 -\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
The union bound therefore gives
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_0\cap A_1)
 \ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{34}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values, and hence
\[
 W_n(x)\ge\frac12|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|.
\]
Multiply this deterministic implication by the positive part of the lower bound in (34) and take \(P_0\)-expectations to obtain the displayed obstruction. This argument concerns full observed laws. Score-law total variation alone does not control the outcome perturbations used here, and the global calibration in (24) need not attain the local benchmark at isolated sites. These are exactly the conclusions left open by the theorem.
