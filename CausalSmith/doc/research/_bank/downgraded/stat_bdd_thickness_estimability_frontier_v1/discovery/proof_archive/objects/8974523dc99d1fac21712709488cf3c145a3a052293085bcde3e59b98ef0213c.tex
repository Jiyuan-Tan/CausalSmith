Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and define the finite Borel measure
\[
 \mu_t(E):=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t),
 \qquad E\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ Borel}.
 \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), this law satisfies each of the seven assumptions listed in that definition. In particular, the polynomial lower-mass, Lipschitz, bounded-trace, Gaussian-error, sharp-assignment, and consistency conditions used below all hold.

We first show that every boundary point belongs to both one-sided supports. Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\), and set
\[
 h:=\min\{h_0,r/2\}.
\]
Because \(h_0>0\), one has \(0<h\le h_0\) and \(h<r\). Since \(c_m>0\), \(\kappa>2\), and \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) holds,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_t(B(x,r))
 &\ge P\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X,x)\le h\}\\
 &=q_{t,P}(x,h)\\
 &\ge c_m h^\kappa>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus every open neighborhood of \(x\) has positive \(\mu_t\)-mass. By the definition of the support of a Borel measure,
\[
 x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)=\mathcal X_{t,P}.
 \tag{3}
\]
This proves (3) for every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and both values of \(t\).

Put
\[
 \delta_{\mathcal B}(z):=\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x),
 \qquad
 U_{t,P}:=\{z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z')|\le d(z,z'),
 \tag{5}
\]
so \(U_{t,P}\) is relatively open in \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\). For \(x\in\mathcal B\), (3) and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(x)=0<h_0\) imply \(x\in U_{t,P}\). If \(z\to x\) with \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), then eventually \(z\in U_{t,P}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently the defining trace limit exists uniquely and satisfies
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)
 =\lim_{z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}}g_{t,P}(z)
 =g_{t,P}(x).
 \tag{6}
\]

We next verify local integrability and identify the pointwise Lipschitz representative. For every \(z\in U_{t,P}\), compactness of \(\mathcal B\) yields \(x_z\in\mathcal B\) such that \(d(z,x_z)=\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)\). By (3), (6), \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g_{t,P}(z)|
 &\le |g_{t,P}(x_z)|+Ld(z,x_z)\\
 &=|\theta_{t,P}(x_z)|+L\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)\\
 &\le L(1+h_0).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
The complement of \(\mathcal X_{t,P}=\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\) is a union of open \(\mu_t\)-null sets. Since \(\mathbb R^2\) has a countable base, that complement is a countable union of such sets, and hence
\[
 \mu_t(\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal X_{t,P})=0.
 \tag{8}
\]
Let \(\varepsilon_t:=Y-g_{t,P}(X)\) on the one-sided support. By \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}regression\text{-}moments}\), outside a \(P_X\)-null subset of \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[|\varepsilon_t|\mid X=z]\le\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9), followed by integration with respect to \(P_X\), imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P\!\left[|Y|\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ \delta_{\mathcal B}(X)<h_0\}\right]\\
 &\quad\le
 \int_{\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}}
 \left(|g_{t,P}(z)|+\mathbb E_P[|\varepsilon_t|\mid X=z]\right)P_X(dz)\\
 &\quad\le
 \{L(1+h_0)+\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\}\,\mu_t(U_{t,P})<\infty.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
For every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), absolute integrability from (9) and conditional centering give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P\!\left[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}\}
       \{Y-g_{t,P}(X)\}\right]\\
 &\quad=
 \int_{\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}}b(z)
       \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]P_X(dz)=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
Therefore \(g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}\) is a representative of the observed conditional-regression equivalence class under \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\), restricted to \(U_{t,P}\). The model's Lipschitz condition gives
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(z')|\le Ld(z,z'),
 \qquad z,z'\in U_{t,P}.
 \tag{12}
\]

To prove pointwise uniqueness, let \(f_1,f_2:U_{t,P}\to\mathbb R\) be two \(L\)-Lipschitz representatives of this restricted equivalence class. Then
\[
 f_1=f_2\qquad \mu_t\text{-almost everywhere on }U_{t,P}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Suppose instead that for some \(z\in U_{t,P}\),
\[
 \Delta:=|f_1(z)-f_2(z)|>0.
\]
Because \(L>0\) and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\), choose
\[
 0<r<\min\left\{h_0-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z),\frac{\Delta}{4L}\right\}.
\]
By (5),
\[
 B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\subseteq U_{t,P}.
 \tag{14}
\]
For every \(z'\) in this relative ball, the triangle inequality and the two Lipschitz inequalities yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |f_1(z')-f_2(z')|
 &\ge \Delta-|f_1(z')-f_1(z)|-|f_2(z')-f_2(z)|\\
 &\ge \Delta-2Ld(z,z')>\frac{\Delta}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
Since \(z\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\), the definition of support and (8) give
\[
 \mu_t(B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P})
 =\mu_t(B(z,r))>0.
 \tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) contradict (13). Hence the Lipschitz representative is pointwise unique on \(U_{t,P}\); by (6), its boundary trace is unique as well.

Now let \(P,P'\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) induce the same observed law of \(O=(Y,X)\) under the same known assignment geometry. Their \(X\)-marginals, one-sided restricted measures (1), supports, and sets (4) coincide. By (10), the relevant locally restricted outcomes are integrable under both laws. Their conditional-regression equivalence classes on that common set therefore coincide because those classes are determined by the common joint law of \((Y,X)\). Pointwise uniqueness gives
\[
 g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}=g_{t,P'}|_{U_{t,P'}}.
\]
Taking boundary limits in (6) gives \(\theta_{t,P}=\theta_{t,P'}\). Since, by definition,
\[
 \tau_P=\theta_{1,P}-\theta_{0,P},
\]
one also has \(\tau_P=\tau_{P'}\). Thus the near-boundary regression representative, each boundary trace, and their contrast are observed-law functionals, without choosing a conditional-mean version at a null boundary point.

It remains to establish the potential-outcome compatibility. Define
\[
 S_t:=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\},
 \qquad
 D_t:=S_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Because \(D_t\subseteq\mathcal A_t\), while \(D_t\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\subseteq U_{t,P}\), equation (8) gives
\[
 P_X(D_t\setminus U_{t,P})=0.
 \tag{18}
\]
Equations (7), (9), (10), and (18) show that \(g_{t,P}(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) and \(Y\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) are integrable. On \(\{X\in D_t\}\), the definition of \(S_t\) and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) imply \(T=t\); then \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) implies \(Y=Y(t)\). Hence \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) is integrable as well.

Let \(p_t:=P_X(D_t)\). If \(p_t>0\), define the probability law obtained by normalization to this intersection by
\[
 Q_t(C):=\frac{P(C\cap\{X\in D_t\})}{p_t}.
 \tag{19}
\]
The division is valid because \(p_t>0\). For every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), the established integrability, the equality \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(\{X\in D_t\}\), (11), and (18) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_t}[b(X)\{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}]
 &=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\!\left[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}
       \{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\right]\\
 &=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\!\left[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}
       \{Y-g_{t,P}(X)\}\right]\\
 &=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{20}
\]
Indeed, the last equality is (11) applied with the bounded Borel multiplier \(b\mathbf 1_{D_t}\), with the \(P_X\)-null discrepancy in (18) removed. The defining integral characterization of conditional expectation now yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_t}[Y(t)\mid X]=g_{t,P}(X),
 \qquad Q_t\text{-almost surely}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Equivalently, the claimed equality holds \(P_X\)-almost everywhere on \(D_t\). If \(p_t=0\), that compatibility assertion is vacuous.

Finally, if \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), then \(S_0=\mathcal A_0\) and \(S_1=\mathcal A_1\). Thus
\[
 D_t=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\},
\]
and (21) holds \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\)-almost everywhere in the open \(h_0\)-tube. Only \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) was required to be integrable, so no integrability condition was imposed on the counterfactual away from its observed side.
