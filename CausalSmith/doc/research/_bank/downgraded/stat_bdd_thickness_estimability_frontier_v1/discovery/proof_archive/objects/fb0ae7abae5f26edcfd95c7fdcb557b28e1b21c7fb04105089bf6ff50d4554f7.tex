Fix \(t\in\{0,1\}\). By \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), there is a \(P_X\)-null set outside which, for every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), the conditional law of
\[
 \varepsilon_t:=Y-g_{t,P}(X)
\]
given \(X=z\) is \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\). Fix such a \(z\), and write \(\varepsilon\) for a random variable having this conditional law. Since \(\sigma>0\), its density is
\[
 \varphi_\sigma(u)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),\qquad u\in\mathbb R.
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), completing the square gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[\exp(\lambda\varepsilon_t)\mid X=z]
 &=\int_{\mathbb R}\exp(\lambda u)\varphi_\sigma(u)\,du\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}\right)
   \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
   \exp\!\left(-\frac{(u-\sigma^2\lambda)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)du\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
\]
The last integral equals one because its integrand is the density of a \(\mathcal N(\sigma^2\lambda,\sigma^2)\) variable. Thus the asserted conditional sub-Gaussian inequality holds, in fact with equality.

Absolute integrability follows directly from the same density:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[|\varepsilon_t|\mid X=z]
 &=\frac{2}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
   \int_0^\infty u\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)du\\
 &=\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}
 \le \sigma\sqrt{2\pi}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
Here the integral is \(\sigma^2\), by the substitution \(v=u^2/(2\sigma^2)\), and the final inequality uses \(\pi\ge1\). Because the integrand \(u\varphi_\sigma(u)\) is absolutely integrable by (2) and odd,
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]
 =\int_{\mathbb R}u\varphi_\sigma(u)\,du=0.
 \tag{3}
\]
Finally, \(g_{t,P}(z)\) is finite because \(g_{t,P}:\mathcal X_{t,P}\to\mathbb R\). Therefore \(Y=g_{t,P}(X)+\varepsilon_t\) is conditionally integrable at the fixed \(z\), and (3) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=z]
 =g_{t,P}(z)+\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]
 =g_{t,P}(z).
\]
The exceptional set was \(P_X\)-null, and \(t\) was arbitrary, so every conclusion holds for each \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and for \(P_X\)-almost every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), as claimed.
