The partition and sharp-assignment conditions imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  Every member of the triangular union belongs to a polynomial base class.  Positive polynomial lower mass puts each boundary point in both side supports; Lipschitz continuity and the trace definition then give \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  Thus a selected side fit with count \(N>0\) has conditional bias at most \(L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\), while for \(N=0\) the fit is zero and the bounded-trace property bounds its error by \(L\).

Conditional on the scores, independent sampling factorizes the score-conditional residual kernels.  Their MGF bounds imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\lambda\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right\}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2|S|/2} \tag{45}
\]
for every deterministic index set \(S\).  Chernoff's inequality, its lower-tail counterpart, and a union bound over at most
\[
 M_n=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj
\]
nonempty disk patterns give, with conditional probability at least \(1-\gamma\),
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N} \tag{46}
\]
simultaneously for every nonempty candidate set.  This is also the tail conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\).  Adding the bias or zero-count bound proves that each side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\), simultaneously over \(x,t\).  The triangle inequality gives conditional and hence unconditional simultaneous coverage.  Counts and fits are measurable, the grid is finite, and the bandwidth rule has a deterministic tie break, so the band is data measurable.  The argument is uniform over every component of the declared nonempty triangular union.

For the pervasive width assertion, put \(r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\) and choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\).  The net and binomial argument used in \(\mathrm{thm:pervasive\text{-}adaptive\text{-}frontier}\) gives an event \(G_n\) such that
\[
 \inf_{x,t}N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge cnc_mh_*^\kappa,\qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^\kappa}. \tag{47}
\]
The bandwidth \(h_*\) is admissible everywhere on \(G_n\) for large \(n\).  The minimizing property of \(\widehat h_{t,n}\) gives
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}{N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{48}
\]
Since \(\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C\log n\) and \(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log n\) for \(b\le h_0<1\), (48) implies \(B_{t,n}(x)\le Cr\) on \(G_n\).  Off \(G_n\), each radius is at most \(L+\sigma\sqrt{C\log n}\), and (47) makes its expected contribution \(o(r)\).  As \(W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)\), this proves the asserted expected-sup width.

For the obstruction, fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), and let \(A_j\) be the event that \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains \(\tau_{P_j}(x)\).  Honesty gives \(P_j(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\).  By total variation of the full observed product laws,
\[
 P_0(A_1)\ge P_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
The union bound therefore yields
\[
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)\ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{49}
\]
On this intersection the interval contains both target values, so its half-width is at least half their separation.  Multiplying by the positive part of (49) and taking expectations proves the displayed lower inequality.  Score-law total variation alone does not upper-bound the full observed-law distance, and the global calibration has \(\log M_n\asymp\log n\) even when local multiplicity is bounded.  Thus the proof establishes exactly the global band and full-law obstruction, without implying either disclaimed sharper conclusion.
