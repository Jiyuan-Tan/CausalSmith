Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), the sharp-assignment property carried by \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) gives \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  The maps in \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) use only \(\mathcal D_n\), the known assignment geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\).  The bandwidth grid is finite and the argmin has a deterministic numerical tie break.  Hence \(\widehat\tau_n\) is data measurable and uses neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), write
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
and take \(n\) sufficiently large that \(r<h_0\).  By \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), there is \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) satisfying
\[
 r\le h_*<2r. \tag{28}
\]
Let \(\{z_1,\ldots,z_J\}\) be a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  The global boundary-entropy property carried by \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) gives \(J\le2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\), while maximality gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), an index \(j(x)\) with \(d(x,z_{j(x)})<h_*/2\).  Therefore
\[
 B(z_{j(x)},h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*). \tag{29}
\]
For fixed \(j,t\), \(N_{t,n}(z_j,h_*/2)\) is binomial by \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), and the polynomial lower-mass property carried by \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) gives its mean \(\mu_{j,t}\) the lower bound
\[
 \mu_{j,t}=nq_{t,P}(z_j,h_*/2)
 \ge nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}. \tag{30}
\]
For completeness, the needed binomial lower-tail inequality follows directly from the moment-generating function.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), \(\mu=nq\), and \(s=\log2\), Markov's inequality and \(1-u\le e^{-u}\) give
\[
 \Pr\{N\le\mu/2\}
 \le e^{s\mu/2}\mathbb E(e^{-sN})
 =e^{s\mu/2}(1-q+qe^{-s})^n
 \le\exp\{-(1-\log2)\mu/2\}. \tag{31}
\]
A union bound over the \(2J\) center--side pairs, followed by (29), yields a score-measurable event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously for every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c n c_mh_*^{\kappa}, \tag{32}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}\exp\{-c n c_mh_*^{\kappa}\}. \tag{33}
\]
All constants here and below may depend on the fixed envelopes but not on \(n\), \(P\), or \(\kappa\).  Since \(h_*\ge r\), \(h_*<2r\), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\),
\[
 \frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{34}
\]
Thus, uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), (32) implies on \(G_n\), for all sufficiently large \(n\), that \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\).  By the minimizing property in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), (28), (32), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\),
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)
 +\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Lh_*+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_*)}{N_{t,n}(x,h_*)}}
 \le Cr. \tag{35}
\]

We next control estimation error conditionally on the scores.  The lower-mass bound implies \(q_{t,P}(x,h)>0\) for every \(h>0\), so \(x\) lies in the closure of \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\) from side \(t\).  Choose \(z_m\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) with \(z_m\to x\).  The definition of \(\theta_{t,P}\) and the Lipschitz property carried by \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) imply, for every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) with \(d(z,x)\le h_0\),
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
 =\lim_{m\to\infty}|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(z_m)|
 \le Ld(z,x). \tag{36}
\]
Consequently, if the selected count \(N=N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))\) is positive and \(S_{t,x}\) is its selected index set, then
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S_{t,x}}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{37}
\]
Define
\[
 Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{\left|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|}
 {\sigma\sqrt{|S|}},
\]
with the maximum equal to zero if \(\mathscr S_n\) is empty.  By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) and \(|\mathcal H_n|\le2+\log_2n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le\sqrt{2\log\{2(1\vee|\mathscr S_n|)\}}+1
 \le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{38}
\]
Every selected nonempty set belongs to \(\mathscr S_n\).  Moreover, \(h\le h_0<1\) entails \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\).  Hence (35), (37), and (38), followed by the triangle inequality over the two treatment sides, show that on \(G_n\)
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le Cr. \tag{39}
\]

On \(G_n^c\), a selected set arises either from an admissible bandwidth or from the fallback \(h_0\).  For a nonempty selected set, (36) bounds the conditional bias by \(Lh_0\); for an empty set, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), as carried by \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\), bounds the side error by \(L\).  Since \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\) for nonempty \(S\), (38) therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{40}
\]
Combining (33), (39), and (40), and using the uniform divergence in (34), whose exponential dominates \(h_*^{-1}\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}/r\), gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}h_*^{-1}
 e^{-c n c_mh_*^{\kappa}}
 \le Cr. \tag{41}
\]

For the converse, use the pervasive experiment from \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) at \(h=a_0r\), where \(0<a_0<1\) is fixed below.  In the metric \(\mathrm d_{\mathsf V}(u,v)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|u(x)-v(x)|\), distinct trace contrasts in that family have separation at least \(Lh/2\): at the active center of one tent, that contrast equals \(Lh/2\), while the baseline and every other tent vanish there because the centers are \(3h\)-separated.  The same lemma gives \(M=|\Xi_h^{\mathrm{perv}}|\asymp h^{-1}\) and
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}
 \left((\mathbb P_{\kappa,j,h}^{\mathrm{perv}})^{\otimes n},
 (\mathbb P_{\kappa,0,h}^{\mathrm{perv}})^{\otimes n}\right)
 \le CnL^2h^{\kappa+2}/\sigma^2
 =Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{42}
\]
Uniformly in \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), \(\log M\ge c\log n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and \(a_0^{\kappa+2}\le a_0^4\).  Choose \(a_0\) sufficiently small that the right side of (42) is at most \(a\log M\) for a fixed \(0<a<1/8\).  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) with \(\delta=Lh/4\), and using the class membership certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c h
 \ge c r. \tag{43}
\]
This is also the pervasive lower-bound clause of \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).

Finally, take a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net.  It has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\) points.  At every center and on each side, the radius-\(h_0/2\) count has mean at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^{\kappa}\), which uniformly over the exponent range eventually exceeds \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\).  Applying (31), taking a union bound over the fixed net, and using the ball inclusion (29) with \(h_*\) replaced by \(h_0\), we obtain
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 P\{\exists x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\}:
 N_{t,n}(x,h_0)<8\log(e+n/h_0)\}
 \le C\exp\{-c n c_mh_0^{\kappa}\}. \tag{44}
\]
Outside this event, \(h_0\) is admissible everywhere, so no fallback occurs.  Since \(n^3\ge1\), (44) implies the stated \(Cn^3\exp\{-c n c_mh_0^{\kappa}\}\) bound.  The fallback event is a subset of the exceptional score events controlled in (40)--(41), so its expected-loss contribution is already included in (41).  Equations (41), (43), and (44) prove every assertion.
