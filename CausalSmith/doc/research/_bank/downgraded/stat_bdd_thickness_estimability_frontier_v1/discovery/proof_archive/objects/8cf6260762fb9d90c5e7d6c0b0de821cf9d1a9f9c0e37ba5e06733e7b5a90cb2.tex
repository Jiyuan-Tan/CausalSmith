Under \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) gives \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  By the repaired definition of \(g_{t,P}\), the conditional observed-outcome mean of a sample point in \(\mathcal A_t\) is \(g_{t,P}(X_i)\).  Causal compatibility is asserted only on
\[
 S_t=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\mathbf1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\};
\]
here the partition gives \(S_t=\mathcal A_t\), so consistency supplies the potential-outcome interpretation on the full indexed side.  The statistical bias calculation below uses only the observed-outcome regression.

Inspection of \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) shows that \(\widehat\tau_n\) is a measurable function of \(\mathcal D_n\), the known geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\).  Neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density occurs in the formula.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), and write
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid in \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) contains \(h_*\) satisfying
\[
 r\le h_*<2r. \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, uniformly over the displayed range of \(\kappa\), eventually the smallest grid point is below \(r\) and \(r<h_0\); consecutive grid points differ by two.  Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) be a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  It covers \(\mathcal B\), and \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\).  For every \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose a center satisfying \(d(x,z_j)\le h_*/2\).  Then
\[
 B(z_j,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*),
\]
and \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) implies
\[
 \mathbb E_PN_{t,n}(x,h_*)
 \ge n q_{t,P}(z_j,h_*/2)
 \ge nc_m(h_*/2)^\kappa. \tag{16}
\]

For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{17}
\]
Apply (17) to both sides at every net center.  The ball inclusion and \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\) yield an event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously for every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c n c_mh_*^\kappa,
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-c n c_mh_*^\kappa}. \tag{18}
\]
Uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
 \frac{nh_*^\kappa}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\frac{nr^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{19}
\]
Thus, for all sufficiently large \(n\), (16)--(19) imply \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(G_n\).  The minimizing property in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\) then gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}\\
 &\qquad\le Lh_*+C\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_*)}{nc_mh_*^\kappa}}\\
 &\qquad\le Cr, \tag{20}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where (15) and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) justify the last line.

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\).  The selected bandwidths are then deterministic.  For any \(x\in\mathcal B\) and any open radius \(s>0\), polynomial lower mass at \(\min\{h_0,s/2\}\) gives positive \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\)-mass in \(B(x,s)\).  Hence \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\); the trace definition and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) then give \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  For a selected nonempty set \(S_{t,x,\widehat h}\), the same Lipschitz assumption therefore gives
\[
 \left|
 \frac1N\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{21}
\]
Let \(\mathscr S_n\) be the nonempty disk-pattern collection in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), and put
\[
 Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}.
\]
That lemma and \(|\mathcal H_n|\le2+\log_2n\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{22}
\]
Every selected nonempty set is in \(\mathscr S_n\).  Since \(h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\), (22) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P\left[
 \left.
 \sup_{x,t}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 \right|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]\\
 &\qquad\le C\sigma\sup_{x,t}
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}. \tag{23}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining (20), (21), and (23), then summing the two side errors, shows that on \(G_n\) the conditional expected sup loss of \(\widehat\tau_n\) is at most \(Cr\).

On \(G_n^c\), a selected set comes either from an admissible bandwidth or from the fallback \(h_0\).  Every score in a nonempty selected set is within \(h_0\) of the target boundary point.  The trace identity just derived, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), and the Lipschitz condition therefore bound its conditional mean by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\); an empty fit is zero.  Equation (22) and \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\) for nonempty \(S\) then give, without conditioning on \(G_n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \left.\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{24}
\]
Using (18), (20)--(24), and then (19),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\\
 &\quad\le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}
 h_*^{-1}e^{-c n c_mh_*^\kappa}\\
 &\quad\le Cr. \tag{25}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality is uniform in \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\): by (15) and (19), the exponential argument grows at least as a fixed multiple of \((n/\log n)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\log n\), while the prefactor is polynomial in \(n\).

For the converse, use the pervasive construction established in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), whose explicit circular instance is certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\).  At
\[
 h=a_0\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
that construction has \(M\ge c_Jh^{-1}\), sup separation \(Lh/12\), and product divergence at most \(Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n\).  Uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
 \log M\ge c(\overline\kappa)\log n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), and, because \(a_0<1\), \(a_0^{\kappa+2}\le a_0^4\).  Choosing \(a_0\) from the fixed envelopes and \(\overline\kappa\) makes the finite-information divergence condition hold simultaneously for all \(\kappa\) in the stated interval.  Therefore
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}. \tag{26}
\]
This proves the uniform lower bound.

It remains to control the empty-admissible-set fallback.  Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_{M_0}\) be a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net.  Then \(M_0\le2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B\) has a center for which
\[
 B(z_j,h_0/2)\subseteq B(x,h_0).
\]
At every center and on each side, polynomial lower mass gives expected count at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^\kappa\).  Uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), this exceeds \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Equation (17), a union bound over the fixed net, and \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\) show that, except on an event of probability at most
\[
 C\exp\{-c n c_mh_0^\kappa\}, \tag{27}
\]
every center-side count is at least \(8\log(e+n/h_0)\).  The ball inclusion then implies \(h_0\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\), so no admissible set is empty.  From (27),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 P\{\exists x\in\mathcal B,t\in\{0,1\}:\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)=\varnothing\}
 \le Cn^3e^{-c n c_mh_0^\kappa}.
\]
Equation (24) and the sharper event bound (18) show that the fallback contribution has already been included in (25).  Equations (25)--(27) prove every assertion of the theorem.
