Every member of \(\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\) belongs, by \(\mathrm{def:triangular\text{-}profile\text{-}union}\), to some polynomial base class and therefore satisfies all member properties in \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\).  The complementary assignment regions and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  For \(x\in\mathcal B\), side \(t\), and \(s>0\), set \(b=\min\{h_0,s/2\}\).  Polynomial lower mass gives
\[
 P_X\{X\in\mathcal A_t:d(X,x)<s\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,b)\ge c_mb^\kappa>0.
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and Lipschitz continuity together with the trace definition gives
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{22}
\]
For a selected side fit whose count \(N\) is positive, (22) and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) bound its conditional bias by \(L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\).  If \(N=0\), the fit in \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\) is zero, while \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\) bounds its error by \(L\).

Conditionally on the scores, \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) and the bound \(|\mathscr S_n|\le M_n\) imply
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}
 >\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}\le\gamma. \tag{23}
\]
Thus, with conditional probability at least \(1-\gamma\), every nonempty candidate average satisfies
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N}. \tag{24}
\]
Adding the bias bound or the zero-count trace bound shows that the side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\), simultaneously over \(x\) and \(t\).  The triangle inequality proves simultaneous containment of \(\tau_P\) in the displayed interval.  Integrating the conditional probability proves the finite-sample guarantee, uniformly over the triangular union.  All counts and fits are sample measurable, the grid is finite, and the bandwidth definition has a deterministic tie break, so the band is data measurable.

We next prove the expected-width upper bound on the base class.  Put \(r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\) and choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\).  A maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points by global boundary entropy.  At every net point and on both sides, polynomial lower mass gives success probability at least \(c_m(h_*/2)^\kappa\).  Under independent sampling the corresponding count is binomial, and Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) gives, exactly as in the displayed calculation
\[
 \Pr\{N\le nq/2\}
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\le e^{-nq/8}.
\]
A union bound over the finite net and two sides, followed by the ball inclusion \(B(z,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\) whenever \(d(x,z)\le h_*/2\), therefore constructs a score event \(G_n\) for which
\[
 \inf_{x,t}N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge cnc_mh_*^\kappa,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^\kappa}. \tag{25}
\]
On \(G_n\), \(h_*\) is admissible and the minimizing property of \(\widehat h_{t,n}\) gives
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{26}
\]
Since \(M_n\le Cn^3(1+\log n)\), fixed \(\gamma>0\) gives \(\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C_\gamma\log n\).  Also \(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log n\) for \(b\le h_0<1\).  Therefore (26) gives
\[
 \sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\mathbf 1_{G_n}\le C_\gamma r. \tag{27}
\]
On \(G_n^c\), the definition gives \(B_{t,n}(x)\le L+\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}\) whether the selected count is zero or positive.  Equation (25) and \(nh_*^\kappa/\log n\to\infty\) uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\) make the expected off-event contribution \(o(r)\).  Since \(W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)\le C_\gamma r. \tag{28}
\]

For the global expected-width converse, use the pervasive family in \(\mathrm{def:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}family}\) at
\[
 h=a_0(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is chosen below and \(n\) is large enough that \(h\le h_0/6\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), the laws \(Q_j=\mathbb P_{\kappa,j,h}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), \(0\le j\le M=|\Xi_h^{\mathrm{perv}}|\), lie in \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), satisfy \(M\asymp h^{-1}\), and obey
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le CnL^2h^{\kappa+2}/\sigma^2. \tag{29}
\]
Choose \(a_0\) so small that the right-hand side is at most \(a\log M\), with \(a<1/8\) as in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\).  The curves \(v_j=\tau_{Q_j}\) have pairwise supremum distance
\[
 \lVert v_j-v_k\rVert_\infty=\Delta:=Lh/2,
 \qquad j\ne k. \tag{30}
\]
Indeed, the active value is \(Lh/2\) by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), while the \(3h\)-separation of the centers and the radius-\(h\) tents make every other perturbation vanish at that center.  If \(\widehat J\) is any decoder taking values in \(\{0,\ldots,M\}\), applying \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) to the estimator \(v_{\widehat J}\), with \(2\delta=\Delta\), yields a universal numerical \(p_{\mathsf F}>0\) such that
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le M}Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}\ge p_{\mathsf F}. \tag{31}
\]
This follows because the loss of \(v_{\widehat J}\) is exactly \(\Delta\mathbf 1\{\widehat J\ne j\}\).

Now let an arbitrary data-measurable band be simultaneously honest at level \(1-\gamma\) over the pervasive class, and write \(W_n^*=\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)\).  At the finitely many active sites, define \(\widehat J\) as the smallest index whose vector \((v_j(x_1),\ldots,v_j(x_M))\) belongs to the product of the reported intervals, using zero if there is no such index.  On the simultaneous-coverage event under \(Q_j\), the vector for \(j\) belongs to that product.  If also \(W_n^*<\Delta/2\), no two code vectors can belong to it: by (30), at some active site they differ by \(\Delta\), while that interval has diameter at most \(2W_n^*<\Delta\).  Thus the decoder is correct on this intersection, and Markov's inequality gives, whenever \(\mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n^*<\infty\),
\[
 Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}
 \le \gamma+Q_j^{\otimes n}\{W_n^*\ge\Delta/2\}
 \le \gamma+\frac{2\mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n^*}{\Delta}. \tag{32}
\]
If the expectation is infinite, the desired lower bound is immediate.  Taking maxima in (32) and using (31) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_xW_n(x)
 \ge \frac{p_{\mathsf F}-\gamma}{2}\Delta. \tag{33}
\]
The testing lemma's risk constant is twice \(p_{\mathsf F}\).  Hence the stated restriction that \(\gamma\) be smaller than one quarter of that risk constant implies \(\gamma<p_{\mathsf F}/2\), and (33) is bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(h\asymp r\).  Because \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\), honesty over the base class and the supremum over that class only strengthen this lower bound.  Combining with (28) gives both matched global width frontiers.

It remains to prove the general two-law inequality.  Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), and let \(A_j\) be the event that \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains \(\tau_{P_j}(x)\).  Honesty gives \(P_j(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\).  Total variation between the full observed product laws gives
\[
 P_0(A_1)\ge P_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
Therefore the union bound yields
\[
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)\ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{34}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values, so its half-width is at least half their separation.  Multiplying this deterministic implication by the positive part of the right-hand side of (34) and taking \(P_0\)-expectations proves the displayed obstruction.  This argument uses the full observed laws.  It neither replaces their distance by score-law distance nor supplies local multiplicity calibration, which proves only the conclusions asserted.
