For a law \(P\), define the finite one-sided score measure
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t).
\]
If \(P\) belongs to either displayed class, \(x\in\mathcal B\), and \(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\). The polynomial lower-mass property inherited from the corresponding class definition gives
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)
 \ge c_ms^{\kappa}>0. \tag{23}
\]
Thus every boundary point belongs to \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\). The model-class Lipschitz property makes the distinguished representative continuous there, so the trace definition gives
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x),
 \qquad t\in\{0,1\},\quad x\in\mathcal B. \tag{24}
\]
Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), sharp assignment makes \(X\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T=t\). Thus all side averages below have conditional observed-outcome mean \(g_{t,P}(X)\). Consistency supplies the causal interpretation of the resulting trace contrast; the risk calculations concern its identified observed-law functional.

We first prove the lower bounds by experiments contained in the stated classes. Begin with any member of the nonempty class under consideration, retain its score law and geometry, take independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), and set \(Y(s)=\sigma Z_s\) for \(s\in\{0,1\}\). Call the resulting law \(Q_0\). Its side regressions and traces vanish. All design conditions are unchanged, its traces obey the envelope, and its observed residual is conditionally \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\), so \(Q_0\) remains in the same class.

For the isolated class let
\[
 h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is fixed below. For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\). The isolated thin-site property and the definitions of \(\mathsf J_P\) and \(a_P\) provide \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,P}(x_h,h)=a_P(x_h,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}. \tag{25}
\]
Set
\[
 \psi_h(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_h)}h\right)_+,
 \qquad \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12}.
\]
If \(t_h=1\), add \(\eta_h\psi_h(X)\) to \(Y(1)\); if \(t_h=0\), subtract it from \(Y(0)\); leave the other potential outcome unchanged. The function \(\eta_h\psi_h\) is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, its absolute value is at most \(Lh_0/12<L\), and the score law and conditional Gaussian error law are unchanged. Hence the perturbed law \(Q_1\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\). By (24),
\[
 \lVert\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\rVert_{\infty}\ge\eta_h. \tag{26}
\]
The two observed laws have the same score marginal. Conditional on an assigned-side score \(z\), their Gaussian means differ by \(\eta_h\psi_h(z)\). Direct evaluation of the Gaussian likelihood ratio and (25) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=\frac{\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}
   \int\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,P}(z)\\
 &\le\frac{C_mL^2}{288\sigma^2}h^{\kappa+2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{27}
\]
Relative entropy is additive under independent sampling, so
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le C a_0^{\kappa+2}.
\]
Choose \(a_0\) so the last bound is at most \(1/8\). The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with the supremum metric and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}})
 \ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{28}
\]

For the pervasive class let
\[
 h=a_0\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\). The pervasive thin-site property supplies an \(h\)-separated set \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) such that
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},
 \qquad a_P(x_j,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}\quad(1\le j\le M). \tag{29}
\]
Its finiteness follows from global boundary entropy. At each \(x_j\), choose a side attaining the minimum in \(a_P(x_j,h)\), and perturb that side by a radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) tent, upward on side one and downward on side zero. The membership argument above gives laws \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_M\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\). At \(x_j\), the target under \(Q_j\) differs from the zero target by \(Lh/12\), while every other tent vanishes there because the centers are \(h\)-separated. Thus the target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(Lh/12\) in supremum norm. The calculation in (27) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cnh^{\kappa+2}
 =Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{30}
\]
Equation (29) implies \(\log M\ge c_{\kappa}\log n\) eventually. Choose \(a_0\) smaller if necessary so that (30) is at most \(a\log M\), with \(a<1/8\) as in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\). Its multiple-law clause gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{31}
\]
The exact-Gaussian circular families in \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) provide explicit experiments with the same information budgets.

We next give data-driven upper-bound witnesses for the infimum defining \(R_n\). Let \(s_n\) denote either \(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\) or \(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\). By \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) there is \(b=b_n\in\mathcal H_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le b<2s_n,
 \qquad b/2\in\mathcal H_n,
 \qquad b\le h_0/2. \tag{32}
\]
Choose a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated set \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) in \(\mathcal B\). It is a finite \(b/2\)-net, and global boundary entropy gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\). Let \(j(x)\) be the least index satisfying \(d(x,z_{j(x)})\le b/2\), and define
\[
 K_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t, d(X_i,z_j)\le b\},
\]
\[
 \overline g_{t,j}
 =\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t, d(X_i,z_j)\le b\}}
 {K_{t,j}\vee1},
\]
and
\[
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)
 =\Pi_{[-2L,2L]}\{\overline g_{1,j(x)}-\overline g_{0,j(x)}\}. \tag{33}
\]
This is one estimator measurable in the sample and known geometry. Since the trace envelope gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\), projection cannot increase pointwise error. On \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), every included score is within \(3b/2\) of \(x\), so (24) and Lipschitz smoothness imply
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j(x)}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
       -\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le\frac{3Lb}{2}. \tag{34}
\]

We use the following elementary count bound. If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1-y\le e^{-y}\), gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{35}
\]

For the pervasive rate, take \(s_n=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\). The polynomial lower-mass property, (35), and a union bound over two sides and the net give an event \(H_b\) on which every \(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^{\kappa}/2\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-cnb^{\kappa}}. \tag{36}
\]
For fixed scores, every nonempty set defining \(\overline g_{t,j}\) is a disk pattern in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\). Since
\[
 |\mathcal H_n|\le C\log(en),
 \qquad \sum_{r=0}^3\binom nr\le C(1+n^3),
\]
that lemma bounds the conditional expected maximum normalized residual sum by \(C\sqrt{\log(en)}\). Hence, on \(H_b\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \max_{t,j}\left|
 \overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \right|\ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}{nc_mb^{\kappa}}}. \tag{37}
\]
Because \(nb^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n\), the right side is at most \(C_{\kappa}b\). On \(H_b^c\), clipping bounds the loss by \(4L\). Equations (34), (36), and (37), with the exponentially small remainder absorbed into \(b\), give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\lVert\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\rVert_{\infty}
 \le C_{\kappa}b
 \le C_{\kappa}r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{38}
\]

For the isolated rate take \(s_n=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\), so \(nb^{\kappa}\asymp b^{-2}\). Fix a side, choose \(D\ge C_m\), set \(u_\ell=\min\{1,D2^\ell b^{\kappa}\}\), and put a net center in group zero when \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\), and in group \(\ell\ge1\) when
\[
 u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
Only the first index with \(u_\ell=1\) can be terminal. Every center in group \(\ell\) satisfies
\[
 a_P(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
The centers are \(b/2\)-separated. Applying the isolated upper profile at radius \(b/2\), including the terminal group, gives
\[
 M_\ell\le C_{\kappa}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{39}
\]
In group zero, polynomial lower mass makes the mean count at least \(c_mnb^{\kappa}\ge cb^{-2}\); in every nonempty group \(\ell\ge1\), it is at least \(D2^{\ell-1}nb^{\kappa}\ge c2^\ell b^{-2}\). Equations (35) and (39), summed over groups and both sides, give an event \(H_b\) on which every group-\(\ell\) count is at least \(c2^\ell b^{-2}\) and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 &\le C_{\kappa}\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-c2^\ell/b^2}\\
 &\le C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}/b^2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{40}
\]
The last bound follows because, for sufficiently small \(b\), the ratio of consecutive summands is uniformly smaller than one.

Conditionally on the scores, each nonempty residual average is Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2/K_{t,j}\). On \(H_b\), the two-sided Gaussian tail bound and a union bound within groups yield, for \(z>0\),
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{
 \max_{t,j}\left|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\right|>zb
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_{\kappa}\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cz^22^\ell}. \tag{41}
\]
For all sufficiently large fixed \(z\), the series is bounded by \(C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}z^2}\). Integrating this tail and using the trivial bound one below that threshold makes the conditional expected stochastic maximum at most \(C_{\kappa}b\). Equations (34), (40), and the \(4L\) clipped-loss bound on \(H_b^c\) now imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 \mathbb E_P\lVert\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\rVert_{\infty}
 \le C_{\kappa}b
 \le C_{\kappa}r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{42}
\]

The estimator (33) is an admissible witness for the common infimum in \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\). Thus (38) and (42) prove the upper bounds, while (28) and (31) prove the matching lower bounds. The circular nonemptiness specifications are supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\). Finally,
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The two classes have the same scalar lower modulus \(c_mh^{\kappa}\), so that modulus alone cannot determine the expected-sup frontier; thin-site multiplicity separates the two risks.
