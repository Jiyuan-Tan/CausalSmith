Fix \(t\in\{0,1\}\). By \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), there is a \(P_X\)-null set outside which, for every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), the conditional law of
\[
 \varepsilon_t=Y-g_{t,P}(X)
\]
given \(X=z\) is \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\). Fix such a \(z\), and abbreviate the conditional residual by \(\varepsilon\). The declared noise scale satisfies \(\sigma>0\), so this conditional law has density
\[
 \varphi_\sigma(u)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),\qquad u\in\mathbb R.
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), completing the square gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[\exp(\lambda\varepsilon)\mid X=z]
 &=\int_{\mathbb R}\exp(\lambda u)\varphi_\sigma(u)\,du\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}\right)
   \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
   \exp\!\left(-\frac{(u-\sigma^2\lambda)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)du\\
 &=\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last integral equals one because it is the integral of the \(\mathcal N(\sigma^2\lambda,\sigma^2)\) density. In particular, the asserted conditional moment-generating-function inequality holds, indeed with equality.

For \(a>0\) and \(\lambda>0\), the pointwise inequality
\[
 e^{\lambda a}\mathbf 1\{\varepsilon>a\}\le e^{\lambda\varepsilon}
\]
and the preceding conditional moment-generating-function identity imply
\[
 \Pr_P(\varepsilon>a\mid X=z)
 \le \exp\!\left(-\lambda a+\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}\right).
\]
Because \(\sigma>0\), choosing \(\lambda=a/\sigma^2\) yields
\[
 \Pr_P(\varepsilon>a\mid X=z)\le \exp\!\left(-\frac{a^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-\varepsilon\), whose conditional moment-generating function is obtained by replacing \(\lambda\) by \(-\lambda\), and using the union bound gives
\[
 \Pr_P(|\varepsilon|>a\mid X=z)
 \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{a^2}{2\sigma^2}\right). \tag{1}
\]
The pointwise layer-cake identity
\[
 |\varepsilon|=\int_0^\infty \mathbf 1\{|\varepsilon|>a\}\,da
\]
has a nonnegative integrand. Conditional Tonelli's theorem and (1), followed by the substitution \(v=a/\sigma\), therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[|\varepsilon|\mid X=z]
 &=\int_0^\infty \Pr_P(|\varepsilon|>a\mid X=z)\,da\\
 &\le 2\int_0^\infty \exp\!\left(-\frac{a^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)da\\
 &=2\sigma\int_0^\infty e^{-v^2/2}\,dv
 =\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}<\infty.
 \end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
Thus the residual is conditionally integrable. By (2), the integral of \(u\varphi_\sigma(u)\) is absolutely convergent; since its integrand is odd,
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon\mid X=z]
 =\int_{\mathbb R}u\varphi_\sigma(u)\,du=0. \tag{3}
\]
Finally, \(g_{t,P}\) is real-valued and Borel on \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\). Hence (2) makes \(Y=g_{t,P}(X)+\varepsilon\) conditionally integrable at the fixed \(z\), while measurability of \(g_{t,P}\) and (3) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=z]
 =g_{t,P}(z)+\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon\mid X=z]
 =g_{t,P}(z).
\]
Since the argument applies outside the \(P_X\)-null exceptional set supplied by \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), every asserted conclusion holds for each \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(P_X\)-almost every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\).
