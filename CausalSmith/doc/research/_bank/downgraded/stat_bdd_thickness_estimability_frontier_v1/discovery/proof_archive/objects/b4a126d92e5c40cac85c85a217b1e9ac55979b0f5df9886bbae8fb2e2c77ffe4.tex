Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and define the finite Borel measure
\[
 \mu_t(E):=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t),
 \qquad E\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ Borel}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), every assumption listed in that definition holds for \(P\). The positive lower bound in \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) implies that \(\mathcal B\) is nonempty, and \(\mathcal B\) is compact by its declared space.

We first locate the boundary in both one-sided supports. Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\), and put
\[
 h:=\min\{h_0,r/2\}.
\]
Because \(h_0>0\), one has \(0<h\le h_0\) and \(h<r\). Since \(c_m>0\), the polynomial lower-mass condition gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_t\bigl(\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\bigr)
 &\ge P\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X,x)\le h\}\\
 &=q_{t,P}(x,h)\\
 &\ge c_m h^{\kappa}>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus every open neighborhood of \(x\) has positive \(\mu_t\)-mass. By the definition of measure support,
\[
 x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)=\mathcal X_{t,P}.
 \tag{1}
\]
This holds for every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and both \(t\in\{0,1\}\).

Define
\[
 \delta_{\mathcal B}(z):=\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x),
 \qquad
 U_{t,P}:=\{z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
\]
The triangle inequality yields
\[
 |\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z')|\le d(z,z'),
 \tag{2}
\]
so \(U_{t,P}\) is relatively open in \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\). If \(x\in\mathcal B\), then (1) and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(x)=0<h_0\) give \(x\in U_{t,P}\). If \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) tends to \(x\), then eventually \(z\in U_{t,P}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore, by the definition of \(\theta_{t,P}\),
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)
 =\lim_{z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}}g_{t,P}(z)
 =g_{t,P}(x).
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular, the trace exists at every boundary point.

We next establish the local integrability needed for the observed conditional regression. Since \(\mathcal B\) is nonempty and compact, for each \(z\in U_{t,P}\) there is \(x_z\in\mathcal B\) satisfying
\[
 d(z,x_z)=\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0.
\]
Equations (1) and (3), together with \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g_{t,P}(z)|
 &\le |g_{t,P}(x_z)|+Ld(z,x_z)\\
 &=|\theta_{t,P}(x_z)|+L\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)\\
 &\le L(1+h_0).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
Let \(\varepsilon_t:=Y-g_{t,P}(X)\) on the one-sided support. By \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}regression\text{-}moments}\), outside a \(P_X\)-null subset of \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[|\varepsilon_t|\mid X=z]\le\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The measure \(\mu_t\) is concentrated on \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\). Hence conditional Tonelli's theorem, (4), and (5) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P\!\left[|Y|\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}\}\right]\\
 &\quad\le
 \int_{\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}}
 \left(|g_{t,P}(z)|+\mathbb E_P[|\varepsilon_t|\mid X=z]\right)P_X(dz)\\
 &\quad\le
 \left\{L(1+h_0)+\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\right\}\mu_t(U_{t,P})<\infty.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus the observed outcome is integrable under the one-sided law restricted to \(U_{t,P}\), so its conditional-regression equivalence class there exists. Moreover, for every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), conditional Tonelli applied to the absolute value in (5), followed by the conditional centering in (5), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P\!\left[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}\}
       \{Y-g_{t,P}(X)\}\right]\\
 &\quad=
 \int_{\mathcal A_t\cap U_{t,P}}b(z)
       \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]P_X(dz)=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
Consequently \(g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}\) is a representative of the observed conditional-regression class under \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\) restricted to \(U_{t,P}\). The Lipschitz assumption also gives
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(z')|\le Ld(z,z'),
 \qquad z,z'\in U_{t,P}.
 \tag{8}
\]

We prove that this representative is pointwise unique. Let \(f_1,f_2:U_{t,P}\to\mathbb R\) be two \(L\)-Lipschitz representatives of the same restricted class. Then
\[
 f_1=f_2\qquad\mu_t\text{-almost everywhere on }U_{t,P}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Suppose that some \(z\in U_{t,P}\) instead satisfies \(\Delta:=|f_1(z)-f_2(z)|>0\). Since \(L>0\) and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\), choose
\[
 0<r<\min\left\{h_0-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z),\frac{\Delta}{4L}\right\}.
\]
By (2),
\[
 B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\subseteq U_{t,P}.
 \tag{10}
\]
For every \(z'\) in this relative ball, the triangle inequality and the two Lipschitz bounds imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |f_1(z')-f_2(z')|
 &\ge\Delta-|f_1(z')-f_1(z)|-|f_2(z')-f_2(z)|\\
 &\ge\Delta-2Ld(z,z')>\frac{\Delta}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
The complement of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\) is a union of open \(\mu_t\)-null sets. Since \(\mathbb R^2\) has a countable base, this complement is a countable union of such sets and is therefore \(\mu_t\)-null. As \(z\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\),
\[
 \mu_t\bigl(B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\bigr)
 =\mu_t\bigl(B(z,r)\bigr)>0.
 \tag{12}
\]
Equations (10)--(12) contradict (9). Hence \(f_1=f_2\) pointwise on \(U_{t,P}\). Together with (3), this also proves uniqueness of the boundary trace.

For observed-law identification, let \(P\) and \(P'\) be two members of \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) inducing the same law of \(O=(Y,X)\) under the same known assignment geometry. Their score marginals agree, so their restricted measures \(\mu_t\), their supports \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and their sets \(U_{t,P}\) agree. By (6), the relevant restricted observed outcomes are integrable under both laws. Their conditional-regression equivalence classes therefore agree because each is determined by the common joint law of \((Y,X)\). The pointwise uniqueness just proved gives
\[
 g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}=g_{t,P'}|_{U_{t,P'}}.
\]
Taking the boundary limits in (3) gives \(\theta_{t,P}=\theta_{t,P'}\). The definition
\[
 \tau_P=\theta_{1,P}-\theta_{0,P}
\]
then gives \(\tau_P=\tau_{P'}\). Thus all three claimed objects are observed-law functionals, without selecting a conditional-mean version at a null boundary point.

It remains to prove the potential-outcome compatibility. Define
\[
 S_t:=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\},
 \qquad
 D_t:=S_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
\]
Because \(D_t\subseteq\mathcal A_t\) and \(\mu_t\) is concentrated on \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\),
\[
 P_X(D_t\setminus U_{t,P})=0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Equations (4)--(6) and (13) show that both \(g_{t,P}(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) and \(Y\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) are integrable. On \(\{X\in D_t\}\), the definition of \(S_t\) and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) give \(T=t\); then \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) gives \(Y=Y(t)\). It follows that \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) is integrable.

Let \(p_t:=P_X(D_t)\). If \(p_t>0\), define the probability law
\[
 Q_t(C):=\frac{P(C\cap\{X\in D_t\})}{p_t}.
\]
Division is valid because \(p_t>0\). For every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), the integrability just established, the equality \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(\{X\in D_t\}\), and (7) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_t}\bigl[b(X)\{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]
 &=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\!\left[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}
       \{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\right]\\
 &=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\!\left[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}
       \{Y-g_{t,P}(X)\}\right]\\
 &=0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The defining characterization of conditional expectation therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_t}[Y(t)\mid X]=g_{t,P}(X),
 \qquad Q_t\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Equivalently, the equality holds \(P_X\)-almost everywhere on \(D_t\). If \(p_t=0\), this almost-everywhere assertion is vacuous.

Finally, if \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), then \(S_0=\mathcal A_0\) and \(S_1=\mathcal A_1\). Hence
\[
 D_t=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\},
\]
and the compatibility holds \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\)-almost everywhere in the open \(h_0\)-tube. The argument used only the integrability of \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\), so it imposes no integrability requirement on the counterfactual away from its observed side.
