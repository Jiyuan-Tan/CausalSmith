Positivity follows from the strictly positive integrand in \(\mathrm{def:exponential\text{-}modulus}\). With the substitution \(s=u^{-\alpha}\),
\[
 m_\alpha(h)=\frac2\alpha\int_{h^{-\alpha}}^\infty e^{-s}s^{-1/\alpha-1}\,ds.
\]
Let \(a=1+1/\alpha\) and \(x=h^{-\alpha}\). Integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_x^\infty e^{-s}s^{-a}\,ds
 =e^{-x}x^{-a}-a\int_x^\infty e^{-s}s^{-a-1}\,ds.
\]
The last integral is between zero and \(x^{-1}\int_x^\infty e^{-s}s^{-a}\,ds\). Hence the ratio of the first integral to \(e^{-x}x^{-a}\) lies between \((1+a/x)^{-1}\) and one and tends to one. Therefore
\[
 m_\alpha(h)\sim\frac2\alpha h^{\alpha+1}e^{-h^{-\alpha}}
 \qquad(h\downarrow0).
\]
This proves the asserted two-sided asymptotic order.

For all sufficiently large \(n\), choose
\[
 h_n=\left(\tfrac12\log n\right)^{-1/\alpha}.
\]
Then \(h_n\downarrow0\), \(e^{-h_n^{-\alpha}}=n^{-1/2}\), and the preceding equivalence gives
\[
 \frac{n m_\alpha(h_n)}{\log n}
 \asymp n^{1/2}(\log n)^{-(\alpha+1)/\alpha-1}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus a fixed positive exponential modulus admits a vanishing bandwidth with diverging effective sample size and does not by itself force order-one risk.
