Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\). By \(\mathrm{def:triangular\text{-}profile\text{-}union}\), \(P\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) for some declared profile triple. Hence it satisfies sharp assignment, consistency, global boundary entropy, Lipschitz side means, bounded side traces, exact conditional Gaussian errors, and polynomial lower mass. The partition condition in the theorem and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\). Polynomial lower mass implies that every boundary point lies in the closure of each restricted side support. Therefore, by taking a support sequence to the trace in the Lipschitz condition,
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x) \tag{19}
\]
whenever \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), \(x\in\mathcal B\), and \(d(z,x)\le h_0\).

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\). Counts and hence the selected bandwidths are now fixed. Let \(\mathscr S_n\) be the collection in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\). That lemma gives
\[
|\mathscr S_n|\le M_n:=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj \tag{20}
\]
and, for \(z>0\),
\[
\Pr_P\left\{
\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
\frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}
{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}>z
\,\middle|\,X_1,ldots,X_n\right\}
\le2|\mathscr S_n|e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{21}
\]
The hypotheses of (21) hold by independent sampling and the exact conditional Gaussian member property; the lemma includes the required conditional independence and Gaussian moment-generating-function calculation. With \(z=\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}\), (20)--(21) show that, conditionally on the scores, an event \(E_n\) of probability at least \(1-\gamma\) occurs on which every nonempty candidate set \(S\) satisfies
\[
\left|\frac1{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
\le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}{|S|}}. \tag{22}
\]
The claim is vacuous if \(\mathscr S_n\) is empty.

Fix \(x,t\), abbreviate \(\widehat h=\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\) and \(N=N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h)\), and first suppose \(N>0\). The selected observations satisfy \(d(X_i,x)\le\widehat h\le h_0\). Averaging (19) and using (22) yields on \(E_n\)
\[
|\widehat g_{t,n}(x;\widehat h)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
\le L\widehat h+
\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N}
=B_{t,n}(x). \tag{23}
\]
If \(N=0\), the fit equals zero by \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), while bounded side traces give
\[
|\widehat g_{t,n}(x;\widehat h)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
=|\theta_{t,P}(x)|\le L=B_{t,n}(x). \tag{24}
\]
Equations (23)--(24) hold simultaneously over \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), because (22) is simultaneous over every distinct candidate index set. The definition of \(\widehat\tau_n\) and the triangle inequality therefore give on \(E_n\)
\[
|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\le B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal B. \tag{25}
\]
Thus the displayed interval covers the entire trace on \(E_n\). Integrating the conditional probability lower bound over the scores gives coverage at least \(1-\gamma\) for this \(P\), and taking the infimum over the declared nonempty union preserves that bound. The grid is finite; every count and fit is sample measurable; and the bandwidth minimization is over a finite set with a deterministic numerical tie break and a stated empty-set fallback. Hence every endpoint of \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) is data measurable.

We next prove the pervasive expected-width assertion. Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), put \(r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\), and choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\). Take a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net. The entropy bound gives at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) centers, and lower mass gives mean count at least \(nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}\) at each center and side. If \(V\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)\), exponential Markov with \(\lambda=\log2\) gives
\[
\Pr\{V\le nq/2\}
\le e^{\lambda nq/2}(1-q+qe^{-\lambda})^n
\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{26}
\]
A union bound and ball inclusion consequently give an event \(G_n\) on which
\[
\inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,h_*)
\ge cnc_mh_*^{\kappa},
\qquad
P(G_n^c)\le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}. \tag{27}
\]
Uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
\frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
\ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{28}
\]
Thus \(h_*\) is admissible everywhere on \(G_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). The minimizing property of \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\), together with \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), yields
\[
L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+
\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
{N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
\le Cr \qquad\text{on }G_n. \tag{29}
\]
Because \(M_n\le Cn^3|\mathcal H_n|\), for fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1)\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C_\gamma\log n
\le C_\gamma\log(e+n/h) \qquad(h\in\mathcal H_n). \tag{30}
\]
Equations (29)--(30) show that \(\sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\le Cr\) on \(G_n\). On \(G_n^c\), if \(N=0\) then \(B_{t,n}(x)=L\); if \(N>0\), then \(N\ge1\), \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\le h_0<1\), and hence
\[
\sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{31}
\]
The interval definition and \(\mathrm{def:band\text{-}half\text{-}width}\) give the exact identity
\[
W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x). \tag{32}
\]
Using (27)--(32), and the fact from (28) that the exponential term dominates all displayed polynomial-logarithmic factors uniformly, gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
\le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}h_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}
\le Cr. \tag{33}
\]
This is the asserted rate and is consistent with the count-adaptive frontier established in \(\mathrm{thm:pervasive\text{-}adaptive\text{-}frontier}\).

Finally, consider any band satisfying the stated coverage over a class containing \(P_0,P_1\), fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), and set
\[
A_j=\{\tau_{P_j}(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\},\qquad j=0,1.
\]
Simultaneous coverage implies \(P_j(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\). By the definition of total variation,
\[
P_0(A_1)
\ge P_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})
\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n-
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{34}
\]
The union bound under \(P_0\) then gives
\[
P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
\ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{35}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values, so
\[
W_n(x)\ge\frac12|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|. \tag{36}
\]
Multiplying (36) by the indicator of \(A_0\cap A_1\), using the positive part of (35), and taking \(P_0\)-expectations proves
\[
\mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)\ge
\frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
\left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+. \tag{37}
\]
This argument requires the total variation of the full experiments carrying the band event. The score sample is only a measurable projection, so data processing gives score-law total variation no larger than full-experiment total variation, the wrong direction for replacing the distance in (37). Likewise, (30) uses the global factor \(\log M_n\asymp\log n\), while \(\log\{eK_P(x,h)/\gamma\}\) in \(r_{n,P}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\) may remain bounded at an isolated thin site. Therefore the proof establishes exactly the global band, its pervasive width rate, and the full-experiment two-law obstruction, without asserting either disclaimed sharper adaptation conclusion.
