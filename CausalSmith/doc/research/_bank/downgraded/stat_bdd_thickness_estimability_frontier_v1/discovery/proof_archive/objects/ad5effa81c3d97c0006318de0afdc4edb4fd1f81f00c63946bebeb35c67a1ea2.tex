For a law \(P\), define \(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\). If \(P\) belongs to either polynomial class, \(x\in\mathcal B\), and \(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\). The polynomial lower-mass member property gives
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^\kappa>0. \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) on both sides. The unique Lipschitz representative and the trace definition imply
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{12}
\]
The partition and sharp-assignment conditions identify side membership with realized treatment, and consistency supplies the causal interpretation of the observed-side trace contrast.

We first derive lower bounds by experiments contained in the same classes. For either nonempty class, retain the score marginal and geometry of any member and define a baseline \(Q_0\) on an extension by \(Y(t)=\sigma Z_t\), where \(Z_0,Z_1\) are independent standard normal variables independent of \(X\). All design properties are unchanged; the side regressions and traces vanish; and the response smoothness, trace bound, Gaussian-error, sharp-assignment, and consistency properties hold. Hence \(Q_0\) remains in the chosen class.

For the isolated class, set \(h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\), with \(a_0>0\) chosen below. For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\). The isolated-thin-site member property and \(\mathrm{def:minimum\text{-}side\text{-}mass}\) supply \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,Q_0}(x_h,h)=a_{Q_0}(x_h,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{13}
\]
Let \(\psi_h(z)=(1-3d(z,x_h)/h)_+\) and \(\eta_h=Lh/12\). If \(t_h=1\), add \(\eta_h\psi_h(X)\) to \(Y(1)\); if \(t_h=0\), subtract it from \(Y(0)\). The shifted regression is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, its trace is bounded by \(L\) because \(h<h_0<1\), its error remains Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2\), and the score law is unchanged. The alternative \(Q_1\) therefore belongs to \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\). Equation (12) gives
\[
 \|\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h. \tag{14}
\]
Direct integration of the Gaussian conditional log-likelihood ratio and (13) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}\int\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,Q_0}(z)\\
 &\le \frac{C_mL^2}{288\sigma^2}nh^{\kappa+2}
 =Ca_0^{\kappa+2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
Choose \(a_0\) so that the last quantity is at most \(1/8\). The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with the sup metric and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}=c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{16}
\]

For the pervasive class, set \(h=a_0(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\). Its thin-site member property supplies \(h\)-separated points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) satisfying
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},\qquad a_{Q_0}(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{17}
\]
The global boundary-entropy property makes \(M\) finite. At each point choose a minimizing side and apply the preceding radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) signed perturbation, obtaining \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_M\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\). At the center of one tent all other tents vanish because the centers are \(h\)-separated, so the target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(Lh/12\). As in (15),
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cnh^{\kappa+2}=Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{18}
\]
For fixed \(\kappa\), (17) implies \(\log M\ge c_\kappa\log n\) eventually. Choose \(a_0\) so that (18) is at most \(a\log M\), where \(a<1/8\) is the constant in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\). Its multiple-law clause gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{19}
\]

We now construct data-driven upper-bound witnesses. Fix \(0<b\le h_0/2\), choose a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated set \(z_1,\ldots,z_K\subset\mathcal B\), and let \(j(x)\) be the least index with \(d(x,z_{j(x)})\le b/2\). This set is a \(b/2\)-net and has \(K\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\). Define
\[
 K_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,z_j)\le b\},
\]
\[
 \overline g_{t,j}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,z_j)\le b\}}{K_{t,j}\vee1},
 \qquad
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)=\overline g_{1,j(x)}-\overline g_{0,j(x)}.
\]
This is one estimator measurable in the sample and known geometry. Whenever \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), every averaged score lies within \(3b/2\) of \(x\). Equations (11)--(12) and Lipschitz smoothness therefore give
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j(x)}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|\le\frac{3Lb}{2}. \tag{20}
\]
For later use, if \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), the calculation in (42) gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{21}
\]

For the pervasive upper bound, take \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\). Polynomial lower mass, (21), and a union bound over the two sides and \(K\) centers give an event \(H_b\) on which every \(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^\kappa/2\), with
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-cnc_mb^\kappa}. \tag{22}
\]
Conditionally on the scores and on \(H_b\), a Gaussian union bound over the \(2K\) local averages gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e/b)}{nc_mb^\kappa}}. \tag{23}
\]
Since \(nb^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) and \(\log(e/b)\le C_\kappa\log n\), the right-hand side is at most \(C_\kappa b\). On \(H_b^c\), the conditional means are bounded by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\), while the conditional expected Gaussian maximum is at most \(C\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\). Multiplying this bound by (22), and combining with (20)--(23), yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\|_\infty
 \le C_\kappa b. \tag{24}
\]

For the isolated upper bound, take \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\), so \(nb^\kappa=b^{-2}\). Fix a side, choose \(D\ge C_m\), let \(u_\ell=\min\{1,D2^\ell b^\kappa\}\), and group net centers according as \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\) or \(u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell\). Each center in group \(\ell\) satisfies
\[
 a_P(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
Because the centers are \(b/2\)-separated, the isolated upper-profile member property at radius \(b/2\), followed by global entropy for the terminal group, gives
\[
 K_\ell\le C_\kappa\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{25}
\]
The mean count is at least \(c_mb^{-2}\) in group zero and at least \(D2^{\ell-1}b^{-2}\) in group \(\ell\ge1\). Equations (21) and (25), summed over groups and sides, give an event \(H_b\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cb^{-2}2^\ell}
 \le C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa/b^2}. \tag{26}
\]
On \(H_b\), the Gaussian tail bound for a group-\(\ell\) average at threshold \(zb\), followed by (25), gives
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|>zb
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cz^22^\ell}.
\]
The series is bounded by \(C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa z^2}\) for all sufficiently large fixed \(z\); integration gives a conditional expected stochastic maximum of order \(b\). The bias in (20) is of order \(b\), and (26), together with the same conditional Gaussian maximum bound used after (23), makes the bad-event contribution \(o(b)\). Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\|_\infty
 \le C_\kappa b. \tag{27}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\), the two explicit estimators in (24) and (27) prove the upper bounds. Equations (16) and (19) prove the matching lower bounds. The circular nonemptiness specifications and independent concrete lower families are certified by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\). Since both classes have common scalar lower modulus \(c_mh^\kappa\), but their rates differ by \((\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\), the scalar modulus alone cannot determine expected-sup difficulty; thin-site multiplicity separates the two frontiers.
