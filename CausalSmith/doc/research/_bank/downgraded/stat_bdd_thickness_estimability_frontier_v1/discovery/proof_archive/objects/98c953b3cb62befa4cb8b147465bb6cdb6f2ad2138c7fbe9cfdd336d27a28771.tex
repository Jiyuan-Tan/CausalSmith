Write a point of the circular tube as
\[
 z=(1+s)(\cos\phi,\sin\phi),\qquad |s|\le h_0,\quad 0\le\phi<2\pi.
\]
Because \(h_0<1\), these are nonsingular coordinates with area element \((1+s)\,ds\,d\phi\). Hence the pervasive normalizing constant from \(\mathrm{def:circular\text{-}thinning\text{-}witnesses}\) is
\[
 Z_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 =2\pi\int_{-h_0}^{h_0}|s|^{\kappa-2}(1+s)\,ds
 =\frac{4\pi}{\kappa-1}h_0^{\kappa-1}.
\]
It is positive and finite uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\). The isolated integrand is nonnegative and bounded by one, so its normalizer is at most \(\operatorname{Leb}_2(\mathcal T)<\infty\). On the fixed positive-area subset \(\{z\in\mathcal T:d(z,x_{\star})\ge h_0/2\}\), it is at least \(2^{-(\overline\kappa-2)}\). Hence its normalizer is also bounded uniformly away from zero.

On a sufficiently small coordinate patch about any \(x\in S^1\), let \(s\) be signed normal depth and \(v\) signed arclength. Compactness of \(S^1\) and \(h_0<1\) give constants, uniform in \(x\) and \(\kappa\), for which the Jacobian is bounded above and below and
\[
 c(s^2+v^2)^{1/2}\le d(z,x)\le C(s^2+v^2)^{1/2}.
\]
Consequently, on either assignment side and for all sufficiently small \(h\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(x,h)
 &\asymp \int_0^h\int_{-\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}^{\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}
 s^{\kappa-2}\,dv\,ds\\
 &=h^{\kappa}\int_0^1 2w^{\kappa-2}\sqrt{1-w^2}\,dw
 \asymp h^{\kappa}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
The last integral is positive and bounded above and below uniformly on \([2,\overline\kappa]\). Compactness and positivity allow the constants to be enlarged so that (1) holds for every \(0<h\le h_0\). Choose \(c_m>0\) below the uniform lower constant and \(C_m>c_m\) above the uniform upper constant. Every boundary point then belongs to the thin set at level \(C_mh^{\kappa}\). If \(E\) is a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of \(S^1\), disjoint-arc and covering-arc comparisons with total arclength \(2\pi\) give
\[
 c h^{-1}\le |E|\le C h^{-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(h,C_mh^{\kappa})\asymp h^{-1}. \tag{2}
\]

For the isolated density put \(s_x=d(x,x_{\star})\). The same coordinate comparison gives, uniformly in the side \(t\),
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\ge
 \begin{cases}
 c h^{\kappa},&s_x\le2h,\\
 c h^2\min\{1,(s_x/h_0)^{\kappa-2}\},&s_x>2h.
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
For the first line, integrate over a side-specific subball of radius proportional to \(h\) whose distance from \(x_{\star}\) is proportional to \(h\). For the second, use a subball of radius \(h/2\) about \(x\) and the triangle inequality. Since \(s_x>2h\) in the second line and \(h_0<1\), both lines are at least a uniform constant times \(h^{\kappa}\). At \(x=x_{\star}\), polar integration on either side also yields
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x_{\star},h)
 \le C\int_0^h r^{\kappa-2}r\,dr
 \le C'h^{\kappa}. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) prove the common polynomial lower-mass condition and the asserted equivalence at \(x_{\star}\).

If \(a_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\le u\), equation (3) implies, after enlarging a uniform constant to cover the case in which the right-hand side exceeds the circle's diameter,
\[
 s_x\le C\left\{h+(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}. \tag{5}
\]
An arc of the length on the right-hand side of (5) contains at most
\[
 C\left\{1+h^{-1}(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}
\]
points separated by \(h\). This is \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\), after enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{iso}}\). At \(u=C_mh^{\kappa}\), this upper bound is constant, while (4) puts \(x_{\star}\) in the thin set. Hence
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(h,C_mh^{\kappa})\asymp1. \tag{6}
\]
The estimates extend to the remaining compact range of radii by changing constants. The circle satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) by the same arc argument. The exponent in (1) and (4) also agrees with \(\mathrm{lem:polynomial\text{-}horn\text{-}mass}\).

Finally, the assignment is sharp by construction and the displayed observed-outcome equation gives consistency. For either witness the kernel
\[
 \mathsf R_P(z,dy)=\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)(dy)
\]
is a single regular conditional law of observed \(Y\) given \(X=z\). On each side, \(g_{t,P}(z)=0\), so for every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), and with the empty null set,
\[
 \int |y-g_{t,P}(z)|\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)<\infty,
 \quad
 \int \{y-g_{t,P}(z)\}\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)=0,
 \quad
 \int e^{\lambda\{y-g_{t,P}(z)\}}\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)
 =e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}.
\]
Thus the corrected observed-side condition \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) holds exactly. Both observed-side regressions and traces are zero, so the Lipschitz and trace-envelope properties hold. Equations (1)--(6) and the class definitions prove
\[
 P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}},
 \qquad
 P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}.
\]
