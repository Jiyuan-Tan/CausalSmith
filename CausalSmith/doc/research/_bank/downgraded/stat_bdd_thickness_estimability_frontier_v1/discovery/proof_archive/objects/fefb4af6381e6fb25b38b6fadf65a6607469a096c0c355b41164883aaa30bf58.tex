For the two-law assertion, let an estimator induce a nearest-point test \(\widehat J\in\{0,1\}\). On \(\{\widehat J\ne j\}\), the triangle inequality and \(\mathrm d_{\mathsf V}(v_0,v_1)\ge2\delta\) imply \(\mathrm d_{\mathsf V}(\widehat v,v_j)\ge\delta\). The smallest average testing error is
\[
 \frac12\int \min(dQ_0,dQ_1)=\frac{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)}2. \tag{11}
\]
For every measurable event \(A\), the log-sum inequality for the partition \(\{A,A^c\}\) gives, with \(p=Q_1(A)\) and \(q=Q_0(A)\),
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 \ge p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}
 \ge2(p-q)^2. \tag{12}
\]
The last inequality follows by integrating the second derivative \(1/\{p(1-p)\}\ge4\) of the binary relative entropy as a function of \(p\), whose value and first derivative vanish at \(p=q\). Taking the supremum over \(A\) in (12) yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)/2}\le1/4.
\]
Equation (11) and the nearest-point implication therefore make the maximum risk at least \(3\delta/8\).

For \(M\ge2\), let \(J\) be uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,M\}\), draw \(Z\) from \(Q_J\), and put \(\overline Q=(M+1)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^M Q_j\). Expansion of the logarithms gives, for any dominating law \(Q\),
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q)
 =\sum_{j=0}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 +(M+1)\operatorname{KL}(\overline Q,Q). \tag{13}
\]
Taking \(Q=Q_0\) in (13) bounds the mutual information by
\[
 I(J;Z)=\frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=0}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 \le\frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=1}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q_0)
 \le a\log M. \tag{14}
\]
For any decoder, if \(p_e=\Pr(\widehat J\ne J)\), conditioning entropy first on the error indicator and then, on error, on at most \(M\) alternatives gives
\[
 H(J\mid Z)\le\log2+p_e\log M.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log(M+1)\) and \(I(J;Z)=H(J)-H(J\mid Z)\), (14) implies
\[
 p_e\ge
 \frac{\log(M+1)-a\log M-\log2}{\log M}. \tag{15}
\]
For \(M\ge2\) and \(0<a<1/8\), the right side has a positive numerical lower bound. Nearest-point decoding again implies \(\mathrm d_{\mathsf V}(\widehat v,v_J)\ge\delta\) on error. Averaging over \(J\), then bounding the average risk by the maximum risk, proves the stated conclusion. The argument concerns only the supplied laws, so replacing each by its product experiment leaves it unchanged.
