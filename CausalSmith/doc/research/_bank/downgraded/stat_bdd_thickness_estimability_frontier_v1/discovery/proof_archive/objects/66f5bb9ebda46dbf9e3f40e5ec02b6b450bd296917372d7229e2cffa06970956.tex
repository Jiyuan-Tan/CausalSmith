Positivity is immediate from the strictly positive integrand in \(\mathrm{def:exponential\text{-}modulus}\). With the substitution \(s=u^{-\alpha}\),
\[
 m_\alpha(h)=\frac2\alpha\int_{h^{-\alpha}}^\infty e^{-s}s^{-1/\alpha-1}\,ds.
\]
Let \(a=1+1/\alpha\) and \(x=h^{-\alpha}\). Integration by parts gives
\[
 \int_x^\infty e^{-s}s^{-a}\,ds
 =e^{-x}x^{-a}-a\int_x^\infty e^{-s}s^{-a-1}\,ds.
\]
The last integral lies between zero and \(x^{-1}\int_x^\infty e^{-s}s^{-a}\,ds\). Consequently the ratio of the left side to \(e^{-x}x^{-a}\) is squeezed between \((1+a/x)^{-1}\) and one, and tends to one. Therefore
\[
 m_\alpha(h)\sim\frac2\alpha h^{\alpha+1}e^{-h^{-\alpha}}
 \qquad(h\downarrow0),
\]
which proves the claimed two-sided asymptotic order.

Choose \(h_n=(\tfrac12\log n)^{-1/\alpha}\) for large \(n\). Then \(h_n\downarrow0\), \(e^{-h_n^{-\alpha}}=n^{-1/2}\), and the asymptotic equivalence gives
\[
 \frac{n m_\alpha(h_n)}{\log n}
 \asymp n^{1/2}(\log n)^{-(\alpha+1)/\alpha-1}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the fixed positive exponential modulus passes the effective-sample-size condition and cannot, by itself, imply an order-one lower bound.
