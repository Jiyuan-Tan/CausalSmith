The partition and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  The five estimator definitions show that \(\widehat\tau_n\) is measurable in \(\mathcal D_n\), the known geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\), and uses neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\) and put \(r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\).  Choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\).  A maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points.  At every center and side, polynomial lower mass makes the radius-\(h_*/2\) expected count at least \(nc_m(h_*/2)^\kappa\).  The binomial inequality
\[
 \Pr\{N\le nq/2\}\le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\le e^{-nq/8} \tag{23}
\]
and ball inclusion give an event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously in \(x,t\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge cnc_mh_*^\kappa,\qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^\kappa}. \tag{24}
\]
Uniformly over the exponent range,
\[
 \frac{nh_*^\kappa}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{25}
\]
Thus \(h_*\) is admissible everywhere on \(G_n\) for all large \(n\).  The minimizing property of the selected bandwidth and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) imply
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}{N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{26}
\]

Positive polynomial lower mass puts every boundary point in both one-sided score supports.  Hence Lipschitz continuity and the trace definition give \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  For a selected nonempty averaging set, the conditional bias is at most \(L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\).  Conditional on the scores, independence of the observations gives a product of the residual kernels in \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\); multiplying their MGF bounds shows that each normalized residual sum is sub-Gaussian with proxy one.  The simultaneous version recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\le C\sqrt{\log n}, \tag{27}
\]
where \(Z_n\) is the maximum normalized residual sum over all nonempty candidate disk patterns.  Because \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\) for \(h\le h_0<1\), (26)--(27), the bias bound, and the triangle inequality over sides bound the conditional expected supremum loss by \(Cr\) on \(G_n\).

Off \(G_n\), the selected set is either admissible or is the radius-\(h_0\) fallback.  The trace envelope and Lipschitz smoothness bound the conditional mean of any nonempty fit by \(2L\), while an empty fit is zero.  Equation (27) and \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\) thus give conditional expected supremum loss at most \(C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}\).  Combining this with (24)--(25) yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\le Cr. \tag{28}
\]
The constants are uniform for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), because dyadic factors are bounded by powers of two with exponent at most \(\overline\kappa\), while (25) is a common diverging lower bound.

The pervasive lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), with the exact centered-Gaussian experiments certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), gives \(R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge cr\).  A single sufficiently small scale constant works uniformly because the product divergence is at most \(Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n\le Ca_0^4\log n\).

For the fallback probability, use a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net.  Its cardinality is bounded by \(2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\), and every center-side radius-\(h_0/2\) count has mean at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^\kappa\).  Uniformly over the exponent range this eventually exceeds \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\).  Applying (23), taking a union bound, and using ball inclusion show that, except on an event of probability at most \(C\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^\kappa\}\), the radius \(h_0\) is admissible for every \(x,t\).  Enlarging \(C\) gives the stated \(Cn^3\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^\kappa\}\) bound.  The off-event estimate used in (28) includes its contribution, completing the proof.
