Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), define the finite one-sided score measure
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t).
\]
If \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\).  Positivity of \(m\) and \(\mathrm{ass:declared\text{-}modulus\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\), which is a member property in \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\), imply
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)
 \ge m(s)>0. \tag{40}
\]
Thus \(x\) belongs to \(\mathcal X_{t,P}=\operatorname{supp}(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t})\) for both sides.  The member property \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) makes the specified representative continuous at \(x\), so the definition of the trace gives
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{41}
\]
Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), the sharp-assignment member property makes \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T_i=t\).  Hence, conditionally on the scores, the mean of every observed side average is the corresponding average of \(g_{t,P}(X_i)\).  The bounded-trace member property gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\).  Euclidean projection onto that interval is nonexpansive relative to every point of the interval; therefore clipping cannot increase the pointwise error, and the clipped pointwise loss is at most \(4L\).

We first prove the upper bound.  Fix \(h\) such that \(h,2h\in\mathcal H_n\) and
\[
 nm(h)\ge A\log(e+n/h). \tag{42}
\]
Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_K\) be a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  Maximality makes it an \(h\)-net, since otherwise one could add an uncovered boundary point.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) gives \(K\le C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), for each \(j,t\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\sim\operatorname{Bin}\{n,q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\},
 \qquad q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\ge m(h). \tag{43}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1-y\le e^{-y}\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{N\le nq/2\}
 &\le 2^{nq/2}\mathbb E(2^{-N})
 =2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\\
 &\le\exp\{nq(\log 2-1)/2\}
 \le e^{-nq/8}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{44}
\]
Let \(H_h\) be the event that \(N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\ge nm(h)/2\) for every \(j\) and \(t\).  Equations (43)--(44) and the union bound imply
\[
 \Pr_P(H_h^c)\le2C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. \tag{45}
\]
For any \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose \(z_j\) with \(d(x,z_j)\le h\).  Since \(B(z_j,h)\subseteq B(x,2h)\), on \(H_h\),
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,2h)
 \ge\frac{nm(h)}2. \tag{46}
\]
Choose the fixed constant \(A\) large enough that (42) makes the right side of (46) at least \(8\log(e+n/(2h))\).  Then \(2h\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(H_h\), by \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\).

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\).  For any nonempty averaging set indexed by \((x,b,t)\), (41) and the Lipschitz member property give the deterministic bias bound
\[
 \left|
 \frac{1}{N_{t,n}(x,b)}
 \sum_{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,x)\le b}g_{t,P}(X_i)
 -\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|\le Lb. \tag{47}
\]
Let \(Z_n\) be the maximum normalized error over the nonempty disk patterns in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), with that lemma's zero convention if there is no pattern.  The exact conditional Gaussian condition in \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), routed through that lemma, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le
 \sqrt{2\log\!\left\{4|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj\right\}}+1
 \le C\sqrt{\log(e+n)}. \tag{48}
\]
On \(H_h\), every selected bandwidth is admissible and its averaging set is nonempty.  Conditional on the scores, the selected bandwidth is fixed because it depends only on the counts.  Equations (47)--(48), \(b\le h_0<1\), and \(\log(e+n)\le\log(e+n/b)\) show that the conditional expected error of a selected side fit is bounded by a constant times its criterion in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\).  The minimizing property of that definition and the admissible comparison bandwidth \(2h\), together with (46), therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \mathrel{\Big|}X_1,\ldots,X_n
 \right]
 \le C\left\{Lh+\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 \quad\text{on }H_h. \tag{49}
\]
Here the two side errors were added; the fixed numerical factors caused by \(2h\) and (46) are absorbed into \(C\).

Projection does not enlarge the error.  On \(H_h^c\), its supremum is at most \(4L\).  Taking expectations in (49) and using (45) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\\
 &\qquad\le C\left\{Lh+\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 +CLh^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{50}
\]
After increasing \(A\) to at least a fixed numerical constant, (42) implies
\[
 h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}
 \le h^{-1}(e+n/h)^{-A/8}
 \le h
\]
up to a constant depending only on \(h_0\).  Thus the last term of (50) is absorbed by \(CLh\).  Independently of (42), the clipped loss is at most \(4L\).  Taking the infimum over every qualifying dyadic pair, with the prescribed empty-set convention, proves the upper display.  The maps in \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\), followed by fixed projection, use neither \(m\) nor \(\mathsf J_{-}\).

We next prove the lower display inside the same class.  Choose a law \(P^\circ\) in the assumed nonempty class and retain its score marginal and assignment geometry.  On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define \(P_0\) by \(Y(t)=\sigma Z_t\).  Every score-dependent member property is unchanged.  The side regressions and traces are zero, so the Lipschitz, trace-envelope, Gaussian-error, sharp-assignment, and consistency properties in \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) all hold.  Hence
\[
 P_0\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{51}
\]
Let \(M=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\ge1\).  The lower-profile member property of \(P_0\) and the definition of \(\mathsf J_{P_0}\) supply \(h\)-separated points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) such that
\[
 \min_{t\in\{0,1\}}q_{t,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u.
\]
Choose \(t_j\) attaining that minimum, and set
\[
 \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12},
 \qquad
 \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+ . \tag{52}
\]
Retaining the score law and independent Gaussian errors, define \(P_j\) by shifting only the potential outcome on side \(t_j\): add \(\eta_h\psi_{j,h}(X)\) if \(t_j=1\), and subtract it if \(t_j=0\).  Since \(\psi_{j,h}\) is \(3/h\)-Lipschitz, the regression perturbation is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz.  Moreover, \(h\le h_0/3<1\) gives \(\eta_h<L\).  Thus the regression smoothness and trace envelope remain valid; all design conditions are unchanged, and the residual about the shifted observed conditional mean is still \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\).  Consequently
\[
 P_j\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}),
 \qquad 1\le j\le M. \tag{53}
\]
Equation (40) puts every center in both side supports.  The sign in the construction ensures that, whether \(t_j=0\) or \(t_j=1\), the causal contrast at \(x_j\) increases by \(\eta_h\).  For \(k\ne j\), \(d(x_j,x_k)\ge h\), whereas \(\psi_{k,h}\) is supported on \(B(x_k,h/3)\), so \(\psi_{k,h}(x_j)=0\).  Therefore
\[
 \|\tau_{P_j}-\tau_{P_k}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h,
 \qquad 0\le j<k\le M. \tag{54}
\]
To calculate relative entropy, if a conditional normal mean changes from zero to \(\mu\), direct substitution in the two Gaussian densities gives
\[
 \log\frac{\phi_\sigma(y-\mu)}{\phi_\sigma(y)}
 =\frac{\mu y}{\sigma^2}-\frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{Y\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)}
 \log\frac{\phi_\sigma(Y-\mu)}{\phi_\sigma(Y)}
 =\frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2}. \tag{55}
\]
The perturbation is present only when \(X\in\mathcal A_{t_j}\), and its tent is supported inside \(B(x_j,h/3)\subseteq B(x_j,h)\).  By (55), independent sampling, and \(q_{t_j,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}
 \int_{\mathcal A_{t_j}}\psi_{j,h}(z)^2\,dP_X(z)\\
 &\le\frac{nL^2h^2u}{288\sigma^2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{56}
\]
If \(M\ge1\) and the first information condition in the theorem holds, retain any one alternative and decrease the numerical \(a\), if needed, so that (56) is at most \(1/8\).  The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), applied with the sup metric and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), gives risk at least a numerical multiple of \(Lh\).  If \(M\ge2\) and the logarithmic condition holds, then
\[
 \frac{\log(M+1)}{\log M}\le\frac{\log3}{\log2}.
\]
Choosing \(a\) smaller by this fixed factor makes the average of the divergences in (56) no larger than the multiple-law threshold times \(\log M\).  The multiple-law clause of the same lemma and (54) again give
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}\ge cLh. \tag{57}
\]
This proves both alternatives in the lower display.

For a sequence of nonempty classes, suppose there are \(h_n\downarrow0\) with \(h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) and
\[
 \frac{nm_n(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{58}
\]
The eligibility inequality (42) then holds eventually, while \(Lh_n\to0\) and the square-root term in (50) tends to zero.  The upper display proves uniform consistency.  Conversely, let \(F_n\) be the set of radii satisfying either lower-bound information condition.  If \(\sup F_n\not\to0\), then along a subsequence there are \(h_n\in F_n\) bounded below by a positive constant, replacing the supremum by half its value if it is not attained.  Equation (57) then bounds the minimax risk away from zero.  Thus uniform consistency requires \(\sup F_n\to0\).

For the fixed exponential modulus, \(\mathrm{prop:exponential\text{-}horn\text{-}consistency}\) gives
\[
 m_\alpha(h)\asymp h^{\alpha+1}e^{-h^{-\alpha}}
 \quad\text{as }h\downarrow0.
\]
Set \(s_n=\{(\log n)/2\}^{-1/\alpha}\).  Then
\[
 \frac{nm_\alpha(s_n)}{\log(e+n/s_n)}
 \asymp\frac{n^{1/2}s_n^{\alpha+1}}{\log n}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{59}
\]
Because \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n\to\infty\), for all large \(n\) it can be rounded upward to \(h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) so that \(s_n\le h_n<2s_n\) and \(2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\).  Monotonicity of \(m_\alpha\) and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le\log(e+n/s_n)\) preserve (59), proving the stated sufficient condition.

For polynomial profiles, put \(u=C_mh^\kappa\).  If \(M\asymp1\), the first information condition in (57) holds at \(h\asymp n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\).  If \(M\asymp h^{-1}\), then \(\log(M+1)\asymp\log(1/h)\), and the second condition holds at
\[
 h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
 \qquad nh^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log(1/h).
\]
These lower rates match the corresponding achievability results in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), so the isolated and pervasive polynomial rates are exact.

Finally, the generic displays can differ when the declared lower packing profile is slack.  For the circular pervasive witness of \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), take \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) and declare \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) for \(u<C_mh^\kappa\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=1\) for \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\).  The lemma and the class definitions show that this is a legal declaration and that the resulting nonempty class contains the pervasive witness.  The declaration exposes only \(M=1\) in (57), whereas the upper proof covers a global \(h\)-net of order \(h^{-1}\) through \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\).  This explicitly certifies the possible gap between the two generic displays and completes the proof.
