Define \(m\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) as in the statement.  The function \(m\) is nondecreasing and strictly positive on \((0,h_0]\), and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing in its second argument.  We first prove both asserted inclusions.

Let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), the law \(P\) has all of the sharp-assignment, consistency, boundary-entropy, Lipschitz-regression, bounded-trace, and Gaussian-error properties required by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  Its polynomial lower-mass property gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h). \tag{1}
\]
Thus the declared-modulus lower-mass condition holds.  For fixed \(h\), the set
\[
 \{x\in\mathcal B:a_P(x,h)\le u\}
\]
is nondecreasing in \(u\).  Hence the maximal packing cardinality \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) is also nondecreasing in \(u\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u)\).  If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), the pervasive thin-site condition and monotonicity give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{2}
\]
Because \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), equation (2) implies \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\).  Equations (1)--(2) and \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) prove
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{3}
\]

The same argument applies to \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  The base-class definition again gives (1).  The isolated thin-site condition gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge1, \tag{4}
\]
so monotonicity in \(u\), together with the zero value of \(\mathsf J_{-}\) below the threshold, proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{5}
\]
The circular membership lemma supplies one member of each subclass.  Consequently the class on the right of (3)--(5) is nonempty, as required for the minimax risk and for application of \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).

We next derive the lower bound.  If \(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\), then directly from \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\), for every estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_P\|_{\infty}
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_P\|_{\infty}.
\]
Taking the infimum over the same set of sample-measurable estimators preserves the inequality.  Apply this fact to (3), and then use the pervasive lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), to obtain
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{6}
\]

For the matching upper bound, set
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Since \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(s_n/(h_0/n)\to\infty\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) there is \(h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad 2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{7}
\]
Moreover, by (7), monotonicity of \(m\), and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C\log n\),
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(h_n\) is eligible in the upper display of \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Also, using \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Lh_n+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}
 &\le C\left\{s_n+sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}\\
 &=Cs_n.
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The clipped count-adaptive estimator in that theorem is one admissible sample-measurable estimator in the infimum defining \(R_n\).  Its uniform guarantee, (8), and (9) therefore yield
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \le C s_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{10}
\]
Equations (6) and (10) prove the matched frontier for the displayed pair.

Finally, the definitions of the two polynomial rates give
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\to\infty. \tag{11}
\]
Thus the class indexed by the constant lower profile does not have the isolated rate: it contains the pervasive subclass and has the pervasive rate by (6)--(10).  The upper profile in \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\), which is present in \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\) but absent from \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), is exactly the structural condition that excludes this counterexample.  Hence no affirmative answer satisfying all requirements of the source question exists for the frozen two-argument class.
