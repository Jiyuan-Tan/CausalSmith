The partition condition and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\). Condition on the scores. The selected bandwidths are then fixed. If the selected count is positive, Lipschitz smoothness gives
\[
 \left|\frac1N\sum_{i\in S}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x).
\]
If the count is zero, the side fit is zero and the bounded-trace assumption gives error at most \(L\). By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), there are at most \(M_n\) nonempty candidate averaging sets. The conditional Gaussian tail and a union bound show that, with conditional probability at least \(1-\gamma\), every nonempty candidate satisfies
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)/N}.
\]
On this event each side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\), simultaneously in \(x\) and \(t\). The triangle inequality proves the conditional, and hence unconditional, coverage statement. The formula uses only measurable counts, fits, and a deterministic tie rule, so the band is data measurable.

For the pervasive width assertion, \(\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C\log n\). The count event and candidate-scale calculation in \(\mathrm{thm:pervasive\text{-}adaptive\text{-}frontier}\) show that, uniformly in \(x,t,P\), the selected bias plus the displayed count standard error is at most \(Cr_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\) off an event of probability \(Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnh_*^\kappa}\). On its complement \(B_{t,n}(x)\le L+\sigma\sqrt{C\log n}\), and the exceptional probability makes its expected contribution \(o(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})\). Since \(W_n=B_{0,n}+B_{1,n}\), the width bound follows.

For the obstruction, let \(A_j\) be the event that the interval \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains \(\tau_{P_j}(x)\). Honesty gives \(P_j(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\). By the definition of total variation,
\[
 P_0(A_1)\ge P_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
Therefore
\[
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)\ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values and hence has half-width at least half their separation. Multiplication by the last probability proves the lower display. This argument involves full observed experiments. The score marginal is only one measurable projection, so its total variation is a lower bound, not an upper bound, on full observed-law total variation; the stronger score-only equivalence requested in the question therefore requires an additional legal perturbation construction beyond this proved transfer inequality.
