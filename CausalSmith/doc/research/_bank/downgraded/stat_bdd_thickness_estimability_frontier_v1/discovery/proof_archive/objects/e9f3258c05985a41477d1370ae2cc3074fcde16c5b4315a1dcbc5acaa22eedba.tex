Define \(m\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) as in the theorem.  The map \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) is nondecreasing and strictly positive on \((0,h_0]\).  The constants are those certified by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\); because that lemma supplies a member satisfying \(q_{t,P}(x,h_0)\ge c_mh_0^\kappa\) and \(q_{t,P}\le1\), the declared modulus takes values in \([0,1]\).  The displayed indicator defining \(\mathsf J_{-}\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing in \(u\), so both declarations are well typed.

First take \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), this law has all the sharp-assignment, consistency, boundary-entropy, Lipschitz-regression, bounded-trace, and Gaussian-error properties required by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  Its polynomial lower-mass property gives
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h)
 \quad
 (x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\},\ 0<h\le h_0). \tag{60}
\]
For fixed \(h\), the thin-site set in the definition of \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) is nondecreasing in \(u\); therefore its maximal \(h\)-packing cardinality is nondecreasing in \(u\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u)\).  If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), the pervasive thin-site property and monotonicity imply
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{61}
\]
Since \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), (61) entails \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\).  Equations (60)--(61) and \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) prove
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{62}
\]

The same argument applies to \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  The base-class definition again gives (60).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge1, \tag{63}
\]
and monotonicity in \(u\), together with the zero value of \(\mathsf J_{-}\) below the threshold, proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{64}
\]
The circular membership lemma supplies one member of each subclass.  Consequently the containing class is nonempty, as required by \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).

For any two nonempty law classes \(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\) and every estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_P\|_\infty
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_P\|_\infty.
\]
Taking the infimum over the same estimator set in \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) preserves the inequality.  Apply it to (62) and then use the pervasive lower bound from \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\): for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{65}
\]

For the matching upper bound, set
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Then \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n/h_0\to\infty\).  Hence, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid in \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) contains \(h_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{66}
\]
By (66), monotonicity of \(m\), and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C\log n\),
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{67}
\]
Thus \(h_n\) meets the count-qualification condition in \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\) eventually.  Since \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), that theorem gives the explicit clipped count-adaptive estimator the bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Lh_n+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}
 &\le C\left\{s_n+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}\\
 &=Cs_n. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{68}
\]
This estimator is measurable in the sample and known assignment geometry, so it is admissible in the infimum defining \(R_n\).  Therefore
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \le Cs_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{69}
\]

Equations (65) and (69) establish the matched pervasive frontier for the displayed two-argument class.  Finally, the two defined polynomial rates satisfy
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{70}
\]
Thus a constant declared lower packing profile does not imply the isolated rate: the same class still contains the pervasive subclass and has the pervasive rate by (65)--(69).  The upper thin-site restriction in \(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\), absent from \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), is what excludes this counterexample.  Hence the frozen two-argument class cannot support the requested isolated-versus-pervasive arbitrary-modulus criterion, which proves the asserted negative answer.
