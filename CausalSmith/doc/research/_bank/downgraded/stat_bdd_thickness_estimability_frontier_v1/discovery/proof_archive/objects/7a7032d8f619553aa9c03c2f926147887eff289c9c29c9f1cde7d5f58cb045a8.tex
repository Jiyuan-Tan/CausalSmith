Under the partition condition, sharp assignment makes \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T_i=t\).  Positivity of \(m(r)\) at every radius and \(\mathrm{ass:declared\text{-}modulus\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) put every boundary point in both one-sided supports by the argument in (11).  Hence the Lipschitz trace identity (12) is available.  Since the bounded-trace assumption gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\), projection onto this interval cannot increase pointwise error and the clipped loss is at most \(4L\).

Fix \(h\) in the displayed infimum.  A maximal \(h\)-separated boundary net has at most \(C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}\) points and covers \(\mathcal B\).  If \(z\) is a net point within \(h\) of \(x\), then \(B(z,h)\subseteq B(x,2h)\).  The declared lower modulus and the binomial bound (21) therefore imply
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{\inf_{x\in\mathcal B,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,2h)<\frac12nm(h)\right\}
 \le Ch^{-1}e^{-cnm(h)}. \tag{40}
\]
If \(nm(h)\ge A\log(e+n/h)\), a fixed sufficiently large \(A\) makes \(2h\) admissible everywhere on the complementary event.  The minimizing property of \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\), the Lipschitz bias bound, and \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) then yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le C\left\{Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 +4LCh^{-1}e^{-cnm(h)}. \tag{41}
\]
Choosing \(A\) larger if necessary makes the last term no larger than a constant multiple of \(Lh\).  The deterministic bound \(4L\) applies if there is no qualifying scale.  Taking the infimum over qualifying dyadic pairs proves the stated upper bracket.  The construction of the estimator contains none of \(m,\mathsf J_{-},\mathsf J_{+}\); those objects enter only (40)--(41).

For the lower bracket, take any member of the nonempty class and retain its score marginal and geometry.  Replace both side regressions by zero and put independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) errors on the two potential outcomes.  This baseline \(P_0\) remains in the class: all mass and profile conditions depend only on the unchanged score law, while zero regressions satisfy the response conditions.

Let \(M=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\).  Since \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge M\), there are \(h\)-separated points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) at which one side has mass at most \(u\).  At each point choose such a side and perturb only that side with
\[
 \eta_h\psi_{j,h}(z),\qquad
 \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12},qquad
 \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+,
\]
using a positive sign on side one and a negative sign on side zero.  Positivity of \(m\) ensures that the center is in both one-sided supports, so the trace shift equals \(\eta_h\).  The perturbation is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, has trace bounded by \(L\), and changes neither the score law nor the Gaussian variance.  Every alternative \(P_j\) therefore belongs to the same class.  At the center \(x_j\), all other tents vanish because the sites are \(h\)-separated, so the target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(\eta_h\) in sup norm.  The Gaussian shift calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \le C\frac{nL^2h^2u}{\sigma^2}. \tag{42}
\]
If \(M=1\), the two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) and the first information condition give \(R_n\ge cLh\).  If \(M\ge2\), choose the numerical constant \(a\) in the theorem small enough that \(a\log(M+1)\) is at most the multiple-law threshold in that lemma, uniformly for \(M\ge2\).  Then (42) and its multiple-law clause give the same lower bound.  This proves both alternatives in the lower display.

For a sequence of classes, if \(h_n\downarrow0\), \(h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\), and
\[
 \frac{nm_n(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
then the two terms in braces in (41) tend to zero, proving sufficiency for uniform consistency.  Conversely, if the supremum of lower-bound-feasible radii failed to tend to zero, a subsequence would contain feasible \(h\ge\epsilon>0\), and the lower bracket would keep minimax risk above \(cL\epsilon\); this proves necessity in the stated form.

For the exponential modulus, \(\mathrm{prop:exponential\text{-}horn\text{-}consistency}\) gives its small-radius asymptotics.  More explicitly, taking
\[
 s_n=\{(\log n)/2\}^{-1/\alpha}
\]
gives \(ns_n^{\alpha+1}e^{-s_n^{-\alpha}}/\log n\to\infty\) and \(\log(e+n/s_n)\asymp\log n\).  Round \(s_n\) upward to the next dyadic grid point \(h_n\); then \(s_n\le h_n<2s_n\), \(2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) eventually, and monotonicity of \(m_{\alpha}\) preserves the divergence.  Thus the sufficient condition holds.

For polynomial profiles set \(u=C_mh^{\kappa}\).  If \(M\asymp1\), (42) is bounded when \(nh^{\kappa+2}\lesssim1\), giving \(h\asymp n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\).  If \(M\asymp h^{-1}\), it is bounded relative to \(\log M\) when \(nh^{\kappa+2}\lesssim\log(1/h)\), giving \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\).  The matching upper bounds in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\) make these exact.

Finally, slack profile declarations can make the generic displays differ.  In the circular geometry, take \(m(h)=c_mh^{\kappa}\), let \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) below \(C_mh^{\kappa}\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=1\) at and above that level, and take \(\mathsf J_{+}(h,u)=\lceil C/h\rceil\) with \(C\) larger than the circle-packing constant.  The pervasive circular witness belongs to this nonempty declared class by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\).  The generic lower profile exposes only \(M=1\), whereas (41) pays the global logarithm.  This legal triple proves the asserted possibility of a gap and explains why the result is a two-sided bracket for arbitrary declarations.
