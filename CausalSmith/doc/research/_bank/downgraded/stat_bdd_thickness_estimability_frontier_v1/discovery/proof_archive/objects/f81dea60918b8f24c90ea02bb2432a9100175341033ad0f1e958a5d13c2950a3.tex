Under the partition condition in the statement, \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) is equivalent to \(T_i=t\), so every side fit averages observations from the corresponding conditional regression. Positivity of \(m\) at every radius puts each boundary point in both one-sided supports, making the Lipschitz trace comparison used below valid. Projection onto an interval containing the target cannot increase absolute error. The bounded-trace assumption gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\), so it suffices to analyze the clipped estimator, whose loss is always at most \(4L\).

Fix \(h\) in the displayed infimum and use \(2h\) as a candidate bandwidth. A maximal \(h\)-separated boundary net has at most \(C/h\) points and covers \(\mathcal B\). If \(z\) is within \(h\) of \(x\), then \(B(z,h)\subset B(x,2h)\). The declared lower modulus and binomial Chernoff inequality therefore give
\[
 \Pr\left\{\inf_{x,t}N_{t,n}(x,2h)<\tfrac12nm(h)\right\}
 \le Ch^{-1}e^{-cnm(h)}.
\]
When \(nm(h)\ge A\log(e+n/h)\), choosing \(A\) large makes \(2h\) admissible everywhere on the complementary event. The count-adaptive minimization, Lipschitz bias bound, and \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) then give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\sup_x|\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le C\left\{Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 +4LCh^{-1}e^{-cnm(h)}.
\]
The last term is absorbed by the displayed rate after increasing \(A\) and \(C\). If no qualifying scale exists, the deterministic bound \(4L\) supplies the first branch. Taking the infimum proves the upper bracket. Notice that the algorithm itself is still the frozen count-based rule and hence does not read any declared profile.

For the lower bracket, choose any law in the nonempty class and retain only its score marginal. Replace both side regressions by zero and use independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) errors. This new baseline law remains in the class because every defining mass and packing condition depends only on the score law, while zero regressions satisfy the Lipschitz and trace envelopes, sharp assignment, consistency, and Gaussian-error assumptions.

Since \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=M\), there are \(M\) boundary sites separated by \(h\) at which one side has mass at most \(u\). At each site select such a side and add to that side regression a tent of radius \(h/3\) and height \(Lh/12\), leaving the other side at zero. The stated positivity of \(m\) puts every selected boundary point in both one-sided supports at every smaller radius, so the trace of the tent at its center equals its height. The tent is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, its trace is bounded by \(L\), and the score law is unchanged, so every alternative is in the same class. Distinct alternatives are separated in boundary-trace sup norm by at least \(Lh/12\). Each alternative differs from the baseline only over a subset of the selected side's \(h\)-ball, and the Gaussian shift formula gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 \le CnL^2h^2u/\sigma^2.
\]
For \(M=1\), the two-law part of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) proves the lower bound. For \(M\ge2\), its multiple-law part proves the logarithmic version. Taking the supremum over all feasible \((h,u)\) gives the necessary consistency condition.

The sufficient consistency assertion follows directly from the upper bracket. For a non-grid sequence, rounding upward to the next dyadic point changes \(h\) by at most a factor two and cannot decrease the nondecreasing modulus. The exponential assertion follows from \(\mathrm{prop:exponential\text{-}horn\text{-}consistency}\). Finally, setting \(u=C_mh^\kappa\) makes the information constraint \(nh^{\kappa+2}\lesssim1\) when \(M\asymp1\), and \(nh^{\kappa+2}\lesssim\log(1/h)\) when \(M\asymp h^{-1}\). Solving these balances gives the two stated polynomial rates, whose matching upper bounds were proved in the thickness--entropy theorem.

The possible gap is genuine for slack declarations. In the circular geometry take \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\), let \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) below \(C_mh^\kappa\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=1\) at and above that level, and let \(\mathsf J_{+}(h,u)=\lceil C/h\rceil\) with \(C\) larger than the global circle-packing constant. The pervasive circular witness belongs to this nonempty class. The lower-profile display sees only \(M=1\) and hence supplies the isolated balance, whereas the profile-free upper display has the global logarithmic balance. This explicit legal triple shows why arbitrary slack envelopes do not force the two generic formulas to coincide; the specialized pervasive lower construction closes the gap only when the actual or declared lower multiplicity is used.
