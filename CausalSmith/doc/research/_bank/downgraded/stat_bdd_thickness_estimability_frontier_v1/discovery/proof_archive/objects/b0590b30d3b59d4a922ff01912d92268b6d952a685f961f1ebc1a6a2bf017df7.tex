Fix \(t\in\{0,1\}\). By \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), there are a regular conditional law \(\mathsf R_P(z,dy)\) of \(Y\) given \(X=z\) and a \(\mu_{t,P}\)-null set \(N_{t,P}\) such that the three asserted integral conditions hold at every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\setminus N_{t,P}\). Fix such a \(z\), and set
\[
 Z_z(y)=y-g_{t,P}(z).
\]
The first integral condition in the assumption gives \(\int |Z_z(y)|\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)<\infty\), so the residual is conditionally integrable. The second condition gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_t\mid X=z]
 =\int Z_z(y)\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)=0,
\]
and the third gives, for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\exp(\lambda\varepsilon_t)\mid X=z]
 =\int e^{\lambda Z_z(y)}\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)
 \le e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}.
\]

It remains to derive the absolute-moment constant. For \(u>0\) and \(\lambda>0\), positivity of the exponential and its lower bound on \(\{Z_z\ge u\}\) imply
\[
 e^{\lambda u}\mathsf R_P(z,\{Z_z\ge u\})
 \le \int_{\{Z_z\ge u\}}e^{\lambda Z_z(y)}\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)
 \le e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}.
\]
Because \(\sigma>0\), choosing \(\lambda=u/\sigma^2\) yields
\[
 \mathsf R_P(z,\{Z_z\ge u\})\le e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}.
\]
Applying the same calculation to \(-Z_z\), using the moment bound at \(-\lambda\), gives
\[
 \mathsf R_P(z,\{Z_z\le-u\})\le e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}.
\]
The pointwise identity \(|Z_z|=\int_0^\infty\mathbf 1\{|Z_z|>u\}\,du\), followed by interchange of the two nonnegative integrals, therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int |Z_z(y)|\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)
 &=\int_0^\infty \mathsf R_P(z,\{|Z_z|>u\})\,du\\
 &\le 2\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}\,du
 =\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, conditional integrability and centering imply
\[
 \int y\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)
 =g_{t,P}(z)+\int Z_z(y)\,\mathsf R_P(z,dy)
 =g_{t,P}(z).
\]
Thus the specified conditional kernel has mean \(g_{t,P}(z)\) at every \(z\) outside \(N_{t,P}\). Since \(N_{t,P}\) is \(\mu_{t,P}\)-null by \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), the final almost-everywhere assertion follows.
