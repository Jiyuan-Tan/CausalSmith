Write a point of the tube as \(z=(1+s)(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\), where \(|s|\le h_0\). The area element is \((1+s)\,ds\,d\phi\). Thus the pervasive normalizing constant is
\[
 Z_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 =\int_{\mathcal T}|\rho(z)|^{\kappa-2}\,dz
 =2\pi\int_{-h_0}^{h_0}|s|^{\kappa-2}(1+s)\,ds
 =\frac{4\pi}{\kappa-1}h_0^{\kappa-1}.
\]
It is positive and finite, uniformly away from zero and infinity for \(\kappa\in(2,\overline\kappa]\). The isolated normalizer is also positive and finite; compactness of the tube and continuity in \(\kappa\) give uniform bounds over the same exponent interval.

On every sufficiently small coordinate patch about \(x\in S^1\), Euclidean distance is uniformly equivalent to \((s^2+v^2)^{1/2}\), where \(s\) is signed normal depth and \(v\) is arclength along the circle, and the Jacobian is bounded above and below by positive constants. On either assignment side this gives
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(x,h)
 \asymp \int_0^h\int_{-\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}^{\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}s^{\kappa-2}\,dv\,ds
 \asymp h^{\kappa},
\]
uniformly in \(x\) and \(t\). The bounds extend from small \(h\) to all \(0<h\le h_0\) after changing constants, by compactness and positivity. Choose \(c_m>0\) below the uniform lower constant and \(C_m>c_m\) above the uniform upper constant. Then every boundary point is in the thin set at level \(C_mh^\kappa\). A maximal \(h\)-separated subset of a circle has cardinality between constant multiples of \(h^{-1}\), because the arcs of radius \(h/2\) about its points are disjoint and the arcs of radius \(h\) cover the circle. Hence
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(h,C_mh^\kappa)\asymp h^{-1}.
\]
This proves the polynomial lower-mass and pervasive-profile conditions.

For the isolated density put \(s_x=d(x,x_\star)\). If \(s_x\le2h\), integration over a fixed sub-ball on either side gives \(q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\ge c h^\kappa\). If \(s_x>2h\), the triangle inequality gives \(d(z,x_\star)\ge s_x/2\) on \(B(x,h/2)\), and hence
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)
 \ge c h^2\min\{1,(s_x/h_0)^{\kappa-2}\}
 \ge c'h^\kappa.
\]
The matching upper calculation at \(x=x_\star\) is the polar integral \(\int_0^h r^{\kappa-2}r\,dr\asymp h^\kappa\). Thus the common lower mass condition holds and \(q_{t,P}(x_\star,h)\asymp h^\kappa\) on both sides.

Conversely, the preceding sub-ball lower bound shows that if \(q_{t,P}(x,h)\le u\), then
\[
 s_x\le C\left\{h+(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}.
\]
The same conclusion holds when the minimum over \(t\) is at most \(u\). An arc of this length contains at most
\[
 C\left\{1+h^{-1}(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}
\]
points separated by \(h\). This is precisely the isolated upper-profile condition after enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{iso}}\). At \(u=C_mh^\kappa\), the bound is constant, while \(x_\star\) belongs to the thin set, so \(\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\asymp1\). Constants can again be adjusted over the compact range of non-small \(h\).

Finally, the assignments are sharp by construction, the potential-outcome formula gives consistency, and the independent Gaussian noises give the declared conditional error law. The zero regressions are Lipschitz, their traces are zero, and the trace envelope holds. The class definitions therefore give \(P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\) and \(P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).
