Fix a law \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:triangular\text{-}profile\text{-}union}\), this law belongs to a polynomial base class, so it satisfies sharp assignment, the two-sided polynomial lower-mass condition, Lipschitz side means, bounded side traces, and Gaussian errors.  Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), sharp assignment gives \(T_i=t\) whenever \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\).

We first prove coverage.  Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\).  For \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(h\in\mathcal H_n\), write
\[
 S_t(x,h)=\{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,x)\le h\}.
\]
The lower-mass condition gives \(q_{t,P}(x,a)>0\) for every \(a>0\), hence \(x\) is in the closure of \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  If \(i\in S_t(x,h)\), then, on the score event of probability one on which every sampled point on side \(t\) belongs to \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\), the Lipschitz condition and the trace definition give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
 &=\lim_{z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}}
   |g_{t,P}(X_i)-g_{t,P}(z)|\\
 &\le L\lim_{z\to x}d(X_i,z)
 =Ld(X_i,x)
 \le Lh .
 \end{aligned} \tag{43}
\]
The limit uses the defining trace, and the Lipschitz inequality applies because both arguments lie within distance \(h_0\) of \(\mathcal B\).  Consequently, whenever \(N_{t,n}(x,h)>0\),
\[
 \left|
 \frac{1}{N_{t,n}(x,h)}\sum_{i\in S_t(x,h)}g_{t,P}(X_i)
 -\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|\le Lh . \tag{44}
\]

Put \(\varepsilon_i=Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\).  Under \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) and the Gaussian-error condition incorporated in the base class, conditional on the scores the variables \(\varepsilon_i\) are independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) variables.  By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), the collection of distinct nonempty sets \(S_t(x,h)\), over both sides, all boundary points, and all grid bandwidths, has cardinality at most
\[
 M_n=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj.
\]
Apply the tail bound in that lemma with
\[
 z_n=\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}.
\]
Since \(|\mathscr S_n|\le M_n\), conditionally on the scores the event
\[
 \mathcal G_n=
 \left\{
 \left|\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right|
 \le \sigma\sqrt{2|S|\log(2M_n/\gamma)}
 \text{ for every }S\in\mathscr S_n
 \right\} \tag{45}
\]
has probability at least
\[
 1-2M_n e^{-z_n^2/2}=1-\gamma. \tag{46}
\]

For the selected bandwidth \(h=\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\), if \(N=N_{t,n}(x,h)>0\), equations (44)--(45) and \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\) yield, on \(\mathcal G_n\),
\[
 |\widehat g_{t,n}(x;h)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
 \le Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}{N}}
 =B_{t,n}(x). \tag{47}
\]
If \(N=0\), the local-constant fit equals zero, while bounded side traces give
\[
 |\widehat g_{t,n}(x;h)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
 =|\theta_{t,P}(x)|\le L=B_{t,n}(x). \tag{48}
\]
Thus (47)--(48) hold simultaneously for every \(x\) and both sides.  The estimator definition and the triangle inequality then give
\[
 |\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x),\qquad x\in\mathcal B, \tag{49}
\]
on \(\mathcal G_n\).  Equations (46) and (49), first conditionally and then after integrating over the scores, prove the displayed simultaneous coverage guarantee.  Each count and fit is a Borel function of the data and \(x\); the bandwidth grid is finite; and the specified numerical tie-break makes the selected bandwidth measurable.  Therefore both endpoints of \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) are data measurable.

We next prove the pervasive expected-width rate without invoking an unrecorded oracle event.  Fix \(\kappa\in(2,\overline\kappa]\), put
\[
 r=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
   =\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
and, for all sufficiently large \(n\), choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) so that
\[
 r\le h_*<2r. \tag{50}
\]
Such a grid point exists by \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), because eventually the smallest grid point is below \(r\) and \(r<h_0\).

Let \(G_*\) be a maximal \((h_*/2)\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  Compactness makes it finite, maximality makes it an \((h_*/2)\)-net, and the global boundary-entropy condition gives
\[
 |G_*|\le 2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}. \tag{51}
\]
For \(y\in G_*\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), the count \(N_{t,n}(y,h_*/2)\) is binomial with success probability
\[
 p_{t,y}=q_{t,P}(y,h_*/2)
 \ge c_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}.
\]
Set \(\mu_*=nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}\).  For completeness, the required lower-tail estimate follows directly from the Bernoulli moment generating function.  With \(\lambda=\log2\), Markov's inequality and \(1+u\le e^u\) give, for a binomial variable \(Z\) with mean \(np\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{Z\le np/2\}
 &\le e^{\lambda np/2}\mathbb E e^{-\lambda Z}\\
 &=e^{\lambda np/2}\{1-p(1-e^{-\lambda})\}^{n}\\
 &\le\exp\{np(\lambda/2-1/2)\}
 \le e^{-np/8},
 \end{aligned} \tag{52}
\]
where \((1-\log2)/2>1/8\) proves the last inequality.  Since \(np_{t,y}\ge\mu_*\), (51)--(52) and a union bound show that the event
\[
 \mathcal E_*=
 \{N_{t,n}(y,h_*/2)\ge\mu_*/2
   \text{ for every }y\in G_*,\ t\in\{0,1\}\}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 P(\mathcal E_*^c)
 \le 4C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}e^{-\mu_*/8}. \tag{53}
\]
If \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose \(y\in G_*\) with \(d(x,y)\le h_*/2\).  Then
\[
 B(y,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*),
\]
and hence, on \(\mathcal E_*\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge\mu_*/2
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal B, t\in\{0,1\}. \tag{54}
\]
By (50),
\[
 \frac{\mu_*}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log n}
 \ge c\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{55}
\]
The convergence is uniform for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\).  Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\), (54)--(55) imply
\[
 h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)
 \quad\text{for every }x,t \quad\text{on }\mathcal E_*. \tag{56}
\]

Define the empirical selection criterion
\[
 Q_{t,n}(x,h)=Lh+sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+nh^{-1})}{N_{t,n}(x,h)\vee1}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), (54), and (56),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))
 &\le Q_{t,n}(x,h_*)\\
 &\le Lh_*+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(e+n/h_*)}{\mu_*}}\\
 &\le C\left\{r+sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nr^{\kappa}}}\right\}
 =Cr,
 \end{aligned} \tag{57}
\]
uniformly in \(x,t,P\), because \(r^{\kappa+2}=\log n/n\).  Moreover,
\[
 |\mathcal H_n|\le 2+\log_2 n,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj\le Cn^3,
\]
so
\[
 \log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C_\gamma\log n. \tag{58}
\]
Since every grid bandwidth is at most \(h_0<1\),
\[
 \log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))\ge\log n. \tag{59}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_*\), the admissible set is nonempty and the selected count is positive.  Combining (57)--(59) therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\}}B_{t,n}(x)
 \le C_\gamma r. \tag{60}
\]
On the complementary event, the zero-count definition gives \(B_{t,n}(x)=L\), while for a positive count, \(N\ge1\), \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\le h_0<1\), and (58) give the deterministic envelope
\[
 \sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\le C_\gamma\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{61}
\]
From (50), \(\mu_*\ge cnh_*^{\kappa}\ge c n^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\) for all large \(n\), up to an additional positive logarithmic factor.  Thus the right side of (53), multiplied by the envelope in (61), is \(o(r)\), uniformly over the declared exponent range.  Since \(\mathrm{def:band\text{-}half\text{-}width}\) and the displayed interval give
\[
 W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x), \tag{62}
\]
equations (53), (60)--(62) prove
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
 \le C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}. \tag{63}
\]
This count calculation also includes the empty-admissible-set fallback event, since that event is contained in \(\mathcal E_*^c\).

Finally, consider any band having simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\) under each of two laws \(P_0,P_1\) in its class.  Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and define
\[
 A_j=\{\tau_{P_j}(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\},\qquad j=0,1.
\]
Simultaneous coverage implies \(P_j^{\otimes n}(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\).  By the definition of total variation for the full observed-data experiments,
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 \ge P_1^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 -\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{64}
\]
The union bound and (64) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_0\cap A_1)
 &\ge P_0^{\otimes n}(A_0)+P_0^{\otimes n}(A_1)-1\\
 &\ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n
 -\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
 \end{aligned} \tag{65}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains both target values, so
\[
 W_n(x)\ge\frac12|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|. \tag{66}
\]
Multiplying (66) by the indicator of \(A_0\cap A_1\), using nonnegativity if the right side of (65) is negative, and taking expectation under \(P_0^{\otimes n}\) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)
 \ge\frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
 \left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n
 -\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+.
\]

The calibration proved above is genuinely global: the explicit formula for \(M_n\) gives \(\log M_n\asymp\log n\).  By contrast, \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}oracle\text{-}benchmark}\) calibrates with \(\log\{eK_P(x,h)/\gamma\}\).  To see the stated distinction concretely, at a scale-specific thin point in the isolated class, \(a_P(x,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}\); monotonicity of \(\mathsf J_P(h,\cdot)\), the isolated upper profile, and the definition of \(K_P\) give
\[
 K_P(x,h)
 \le 1\vee\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^{\kappa})
 \le1\vee C_{\mathrm{iso}}\{1+C_m^{1/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{67}
\]
Thus the local logarithm in that benchmark can be bounded while the global logarithm in (58) diverges, and (63) does not prove a uniform ratio to \(r_{n,P}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\).  Also, (64) necessarily uses total variation between the full observed-data laws.  If \(P_{X,0}\) and \(P_{X,1}\) denote the score marginals, then the definition of total variation and taking inverse images under the score projection give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{X,0}^{\otimes n},P_{X,1}^{\otimes n})
 \le\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{68}
\]
Hence score-law distance supplies no upper bound for the full-distance term in (64).  Without a same-class outcome perturbation controlling the full experiment, the two-law inequality is an obstruction but not an exact score-only necessary-and-sufficient adaptation criterion.  This proves precisely the stated partial resolution.
