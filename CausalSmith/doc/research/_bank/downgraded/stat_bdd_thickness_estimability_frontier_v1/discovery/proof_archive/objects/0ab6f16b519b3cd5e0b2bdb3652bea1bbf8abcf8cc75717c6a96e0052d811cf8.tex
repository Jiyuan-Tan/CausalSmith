The partition and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  Inspection of \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) shows that \(\widehat\tau_n\) is measurable in \(\mathcal D_n\), the known geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\), and uses neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), and put
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid contains \(h_*\) with
\[
 r\le h_*<2r. \tag{28}
\]
Take a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net.  It has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points by global boundary entropy.  If a net point \(z_j\) is within \(h_*/2\) of \(x\), then \(B(z_j,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\).  Polynomial lower mass therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_PN_{t,n}(z_j,h_*/2)\ge nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}. \tag{29}
\]
Apply the binomial inequality (21) to all center-side counts.  Ball inclusion then yields an event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously in \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c nc_mh_*^{\kappa},
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}. \tag{30}
\]
Uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
 \frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{31}
\]
Thus \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) everywhere on \(G_n\), for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The minimizing property of the selected bandwidth, (28), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) imply
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}{N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{32}
\]

Condition on the scores.  Polynomial lower mass puts every boundary point in both side supports exactly as in (11), so the trace identity (12) applies.  If the selected count \(N\) is nonzero, Lipschitz smoothness gives
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{33}
\]
Let
\[
 Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) and \(|\mathcal H_n|\le2+\log_2n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{34}
\]
Every selected nonempty set belongs to \(\mathscr S_n\).  Since \(h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\), equations (32)--(34), followed by the triangle inequality over the two sides, show that the conditional expected sup loss is at most \(Cr\) on \(G_n\).

On \(G_n^c\), a selected set comes from either an admissible bandwidth or the fallback \(h_0\).  The trace bound and Lipschitz smoothness bound the conditional mean of every nonempty fit by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\), while an empty fit is zero.  Equation (34), with \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{35}
\]
Using (30)--(35) and (31),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}h_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}
 \le Cr. \tag{36}
\]
The constants can be chosen uniformly in \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), because (31) gives a common diverging lower bound for the exponential argument.

The lower bound is the pervasive construction proved in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), with the explicit circular family certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\).  Choosing its scale as \(a_0r\) gives packing logarithm bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(\log n\), uniformly over the compact exponent range, while the product divergence is at most \(Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n\le Ca_0^4\log n\).  A fixed sufficiently small \(a_0\) and \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), as used in that theorem, yield
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge cr. \tag{37}
\]

It remains to verify the explicit fallback probability.  Take a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net; it has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\) points.  At every center and side the expected radius-\(h_0/2\) count is at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^{\kappa}\).  Uniformly in the exponent range, this is eventually at least \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\).  The binomial inequality (21), a union bound, and ball inclusion show that, outside an event of probability at most
\[
 C\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}, \tag{38}
\]
one has \(N_{t,n}(x,h_0)\ge8\log(e+n/h_0)\) for every \(x,t\).  Hence \(h_0\) is admissible everywhere and no fallback occurs.  Enlarging \(C\), (38) implies the stated \(Cn^3\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}\) bound.  Equation (35) and the sharper event estimate in (30) show that the fallback contribution is already included in (36).  Equations (36)--(38) prove the theorem.
