Choose an arbitrary \(P^\circ\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), whose existence is the stated nonemptiness premise, and retain its score marginal \(P_X^\circ\) and assignment geometry. On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define \(Q_0\) by \(Y(t)=\sigma Z_t\) and by the consistency equation for the observed outcome. The sharp-assignment and consistency conditions hold by construction. The boundary entropy, polynomial lower mass, and pervasive packing conditions depend only on the retained score law and geometry. Both side regressions and traces are zero, so the Lipschitz and bounded-trace conditions hold, and the residual kernels are exactly centered Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2\). Hence every member condition in \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) holds, and
\[
 Q_0\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{4}
\]

Put \(r_n=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\), fix \(a_\gamma=(1-\gamma)/4\), and let \(h=b_\gamma r_n\), where the fixed constant \(b_\gamma>0\) will be chosen below. For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0\). The pervasive thin-site condition and the definition of \(\mathsf J_{Q_0}\) provide an \(h\)-separated set \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_M\}\subseteq\mathcal B\) with
\[
 M\ge\lfloor c_Jh^{-1}\rfloor,
 \qquad
 a_{Q_0}(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{5}
\]
For each \(j\), choose \(t_j\in\{0,1\}\) attaining the minimum in \(a_{Q_0}(x_j,h)\), so that
\[
 q_{t_j,Q_0}(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{6}
\]
Set \(\eta=\min\{L/6,1\}\), \(\Delta=\eta h\), and
\[
 \phi_j(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+.
\]
For \(j\ge1\), retain the score law and independent Gaussian noises and define \(Q_j\) by
\[
 \begin{cases}
 Y(1)=\Delta\phi_j(X)+\sigma Z_1,\quad Y(0)=\sigma Z_0,&t_j=1,\\
 Y(1)=\sigma Z_1,\quad Y(0)=-\Delta\phi_j(X)+\sigma Z_0,&t_j=0.
 \end{cases} \tag{7}
\]
The perturbed side mean in (7) is \(3\eta\)-Lipschitz, and \(3\eta\le L/2<L\). Also \(0<h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\Delta\le\eta\le L/6<L\). Thus the Lipschitz and bounded-trace conditions hold. All score-dependent conditions are unchanged and the centered residual kernel remains \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\). The same member-by-member verification as in (4) therefore proves
\[
 Q_j\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}},\qquad 0\le j\le M. \tag{8}
\]
This membership conclusion is derived from the frozen class conditions and does not use the circular tent family.

The supports of the tents have radius \(h/3\), whereas the centers are \(h\)-separated. Consequently, for \(j\ne k\), \(\phi_j(x_k)=0\), while \(\phi_j(x_j)=1\). Continuity of the Lipschitz side means gives their boundary traces, so
\[
 \tau_{Q_0}(x_k)=0,
 \qquad
 \tau_{Q_j}(x_k)=\Delta\mathbf 1\{j=k\}. \tag{9}
\]
Only observations in \(\mathcal A_{t_j}\cap B(x_j,h/3)\) have a shifted observed conditional mean under \(Q_j\). The conditional Gaussian likelihood formula, (6), and \(0\le\phi_j\le1\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac{n\Delta^2}{2\sigma^2}
   \int_{\mathcal A_{t_j}}\phi_j(z)^2\,dP_X^\circ(z)\\
 &\le\frac{n\eta^2h^2}{2\sigma^2}q_{t_j,Q_0}(x_j,h)
 \le \frac{C_m\eta^2}{2\sigma^2}nh^{\kappa+2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
Independent sampling justifies both the product experiment and the additive product-law divergence in (10).

Since \(nh^{\kappa+2}=b_\gamma^{\kappa+2}\log n\), while (5) implies
\[
 \log M\ge \frac{1}{2(\kappa+2)}\log n \tag{11}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), one may choose \(b_\gamma>0\), depending on \(\gamma\) and the fixed class envelopes but not on \(n\), so that
\[
 \frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=0}^M
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le a_\gamma\log M. \tag{12}
\]
The explicit entropy-decoding inequality \(\mathrm{lem:explicit\text{-}entropy\text{-}decoding\text{-}bound}\) now yields, for every decoder \(\widehat J\),
\[
 \frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=0}^M Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}
 \ge p_n:=\frac{\log(M+1)-a_\gamma\log M-\log2}{\log M}. \tag{13}
\]
Because \(M\to\infty\),
\[
 p_n\longrightarrow1-a_\gamma=\frac{3+\gamma}{4}>\gamma. \tag{14}
\]
In particular, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(p_n-\gamma-\epsilon_n\ge(1-\gamma)/2\).

We first obtain the global-width conclusion. Put \(W_n^*=\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)\). From the reported intervals at \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\), let \(\widehat J\) be the smallest code index \(j\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\) for which the vector \((\tau_{Q_j}(x_1),\ldots,\tau_{Q_j}(x_M))\) lies in their Cartesian product, using zero if no index qualifies. On the simultaneous-coverage event under \(Q_j\), code \(j\) qualifies. If additionally \(W_n^*<\Delta/2\), no two codes qualify, because (9) makes two distinct codes differ by \(\Delta\) in some coordinate, whereas every reported interval has diameter less than \(\Delta\). Therefore Markov's inequality gives
\[
 Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}
 \le\gamma+\epsilon_n+
 \frac{2\mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n^*}{\Delta}. \tag{15}
\]
If one of these expectations is infinite, the desired bound is immediate. Otherwise, average (15), use (13)--(14), and obtain
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
 \ge\frac{\Delta}{2}(p_n-\gamma-\epsilon_n)
 \ge\frac{\eta b_\gamma(1-\gamma)}4 r_n. \tag{16}
\]
This proves the first conclusion with a constant depending on \(\gamma\), never on \(n\).

For the pointwise conclusion, define another decoder to equal zero if every \(\mathcal C_n(x_k)\) contains zero, and otherwise to equal the smallest \(k\) for which zero is absent. Under \(Q_0\), simultaneous coverage implies correct decoding. Under \(Q_j\), equation (9), simultaneous coverage, and \(W_n(x_j)<\Delta/2\) imply that every inactive interval contains zero while the active interval contains \(\Delta\) and excludes zero. Hence
\[
 Q_0^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne0\}\le\gamma+\epsilon_n,
 \qquad
 Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}
 \le\gamma+\epsilon_n+\frac{2\mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n(x_j)}\Delta. \tag{17}
\]
Average (17), compare with (13), and use (14). For at least one \(j\ge1\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n(x_j)
 \ge \frac{M+1}{2M}\Delta(p_n-\gamma-\epsilon_n)
 \ge c_\gamma r_n. \tag{18}
\]

It remains to compare (18) to the declared local benchmark. For all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid contains \(\overline h\) with \(h\le\overline h<2h\). Polynomial lower mass, retained by every \(Q_j\), gives
\[
 q_{t,Q_j}(x_j,\overline h)\ge c_m\overline h^\kappa,
 \qquad t\in\{0,1\}. \tag{19}
\]
Every \(\overline h\)-separated set counted by \(\mathsf J_{Q_j}(\overline h,a_{Q_j}(x_j,\overline h))\) is an \(\overline h\)-separated subset of the whole boundary. Global boundary entropy and \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}effective\text{-}multiplicity}\) therefore imply
\[
 K_{Q_j}(x_j,\overline h)\le1\vee C_{\mathcal B}\overline h^{-1}. \tag{20}
\]
Insert \(\overline h\) into the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}oracle\text{-}benchmark}\). Equations (19)--(20), \(\overline h\asymp r_n\), and \(nr_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_{n,Q_j}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x_j)
 &\le2L\overline h+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log\{e(1\vee C_{\mathcal B}\overline h^{-1})/\gamma\}}n
 \left\{q_{0,Q_j}(x_j,\overline h)^{-1}
       +q_{1,Q_j}(x_j,\overline h)^{-1}\right\}}\\
 &\le C_\gamma r_n. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{21}
\]
Combining (18) and (21) proves the pointwise ratio bound.

Finally, \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) by definition. A band honest over the base class is therefore honest over the pervasive subclass, and the supremum over the base class is no smaller. Thus (16) transfers to the base class. No step used circular geometry; the only geometric facts used were the frozen pervasive packing lower bound and global boundary packing upper bound.
