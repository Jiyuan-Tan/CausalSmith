For every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), the observed conditional-regression equivalence class under \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\) has exactly one \(L\)-Lipschitz representative \(g_{t,P}\) on \(\mathcal X_{t,P}=\operatorname{supp}(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t})\). Every \(x\in\mathcal B\) belongs to each \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\), so \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=\lim_{z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}}g_{t,P}(z)\) exists uniquely. Consequently \(g_{t,P}\), \(\theta_{t,P}\), and \(\tau_P\) are functionals of the observed law: two laws inducing the same law of \(O\) under the known assignment geometry induce the same three objects. Consistency implies that \(g_{t,P}\) is also compatible, \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\)-almost everywhere, with a version of \(z\mapsto\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid X=z]\); this supplies the potential-outcome interpretation without assigning a conditional mean at a null boundary point.
