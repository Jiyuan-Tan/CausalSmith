For a law \(P\), define the finite one-sided score measure \(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\). If \(P\) is in either stated class, \(x\in\mathcal B\), and \(r>0\), set \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\). The polynomial lower-mass member property gives
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^\kappa>0. \tag{18}
\]
Thus every boundary point lies in \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\). Lipschitz continuity of the distinguished regression representative and the definition of the trace imply
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x),\qquad t\in\{0,1\},\ x\in\mathcal B. \tag{19}
\]
The partition \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\) and the definition of \(T\) make \(X\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T=t\), so the side averages used below estimate the observed-law functional in (19). Consistency, which is a member property of both classes, supplies its causal interpretation as \(\tau_P\).

We first prove the lower bounds by experiments lying in the very classes over which risk is taken. Start from any member of the nonempty class at issue, retain its score law and geometry, take independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), and set \(Y(s)=\sigma Z_s\). Call the resulting law \(Q_0\). It remains in the same class: every design condition is unchanged, the side regressions and traces are zero, and the single observed-outcome kernel is \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\), which satisfies each restricted-side integrability, centering, and MGF clause in \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\).

For the isolated class set \(h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\). For large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\). The isolated thin-site property supplies \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,Q_0}(x_h,h)=a_{Q_0}(x_h,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{20}
\]
Let \(\psi_h(z)=(1-3d(z,x_h)/h)_+\) and \(\eta_h=Lh/12\). If \(t_h=1\), add \(\eta_h\psi_h(X)\) to \(Y(1)\); if \(t_h=0\), subtract it from \(Y(0)\); leave the other potential outcome unchanged. Since \(\eta_h\psi_h\) is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, is bounded by \(L\), and changes neither the score law nor the centered Gaussian observed residual kernel, the resulting law \(Q_1\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\). By (19),
\[
 \|\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h. \tag{21}
\]
Direct Gaussian likelihood-ratio integration and (20) give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}\int\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,Q_0}(z)
 \le \frac{nC_mL^2h^{\kappa+2}}{288\sigma^2}. \tag{22}
\]
Choose \(a_0>0\) so the last expression is at most \(1/8\). The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with supremum distance and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}=c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{23}
\]

For the pervasive class set \(h=a_0(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\). Its thin-site property supplies an \(h\)-separated set \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) with
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},\qquad a_{Q_0}(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{24}
\]
At each \(x_j\), choose a side attaining the minimum and perturb that side by a radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) tent, with the sign chosen to increase the contrast. Exactly as above, the resulting \(Q_j\) lie in \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\). Their target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(Lh/12\), because every other tent vanishes at \(x_j\). The calculation in (22) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cnh^{\kappa+2}=Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{25}
\]
Equation (24) implies \(\log M\ge c_\kappa\log n\) eventually. Taking \(a_0\) sufficiently small makes (25) at most \(a\log M\), where \(a<1/8\) is the constant in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\). Its multiple-law clause yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge c(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}=c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{26}
\]
The exact centered-Gaussian circular experiments of \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) give explicit witnesses to the same information budgets.

For the upper bounds, let \(s_n\) denote the rate claimed for the class at issue. Choose \(b\in\mathcal H_n\) so that, for all large \(n\),
\[
 s_n\le b<2s_n,\qquad b/2\in\mathcal H_n,\qquad b\le h_0/2. \tag{27}
\]
Take a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated boundary net \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\). Global boundary entropy gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\). Let \(j(x)\) be the least net index within \(b/2\) of \(x\), define the radius-\(b\) side counts \(K_{t,j}\) and means \(\bar g_{t,j}\), and use the single data-driven estimator
\[
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)=\Pi_{[-2L,2L]}\{\bar g_{1,j(x)}-\bar g_{0,j(x)}\}. \tag{28}
\]
It is measurable in the sample and known geometry. Projection cannot increase the error because \(|\tau_P(x)|\le2L\). On \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), every included score lies within \(3b/2\) of \(x\), so (19) and Lipschitz smoothness give conditional bias at most \(3Lb/2\).

If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) and \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) give
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)\le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{29}
\]
For the pervasive rate, polynomial lower mass, (29), and a union bound over the net and two sides give an event \(H_b\) on which all \(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^\kappa/2\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-cnb^\kappa}. \tag{30}
\]
Conditional on the scores, independent sampling factorizes the single observed-outcome kernels, and the corrected restricted-side MGF clauses apply at every selected score. Hence \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) gives conditional expected maximum normalized residual sum at most \(C\sqrt{\log(en)}\). Therefore, on \(H_b\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \max_{t,j}|\bar g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\bar g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}{nc_mb^\kappa}}\le C_\kappa b, \tag{31}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(nb^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n\). The clipped loss is at most \(4L\) on \(H_b^c\); equations (30)--(31) and the bias bound prove the pervasive upper rate.

For the isolated rate, \(nb^\kappa\asymp b^{-2}\). Fix a side, take \(D\ge C_m\), put \(u_\ell=\min\{1,D2^\ell b^\kappa\}\), and group net centers by \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\) or \(u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell\). Such centers obey \(a_P(z_j,b/2)\le u_\ell\). They are \(b/2\)-separated, so the isolated upper profile gives
\[
 M_\ell\le C_\kappa\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{32}
\]
In group zero the mean count is at least \(cb^{-2}\), and in group \(\ell\ge1\) it is at least \(c2^\ell b^{-2}\). Summing (29) with (32) over groups and sides produces an event \(H_b\) on which every group-\(\ell\) count is at least \(c2^\ell b^{-2}\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-c2^\ell/b^2}
 \le C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa/b^2}. \tag{33}
\]
The same conditional MGF factorization gives, for \(z>0\),
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{
 \max_{t,j}|\bar g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\bar g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|>zb
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cz^22^\ell}. \tag{34}
\]
For all sufficiently large fixed \(z\), the series is at most \(C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa z^2}\). Integrating this tail shows that the conditional expected stochastic maximum on \(H_b\) is at most \(C_\kappa b\). The bias bound, (33), and the \(4L\) clipped-loss bound off \(H_b\) prove the isolated upper rate.

Thus the estimator (28) witnesses both minimax upper bounds, while (23) and (26) prove the matching lower bounds. Finally,
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The common scalar lower modulus therefore cannot determine expected-sup risk; the declared thin-site multiplicity separates the two frontiers.
