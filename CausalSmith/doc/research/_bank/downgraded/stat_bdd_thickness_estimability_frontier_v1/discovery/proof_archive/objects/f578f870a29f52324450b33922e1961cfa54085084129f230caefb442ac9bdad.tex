The partition \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\) and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\). Inspection of \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) shows that \(\widehat\tau_n\) is measurable in \(\mathcal D_n\), the known geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\), and uses neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), and put
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid contains \(h_*\) with
\[
 r\le h_*<2r. \tag{43}
\]
Take a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net. It has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points by global boundary entropy. If a net point \(z_j\) is within \(h_*/2\) of \(x\), then \(B(z_j,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\). Polynomial lower mass gives
\[
 \mathbb E_PN_{t,n}(z_j,h_*/2)\ge nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}. \tag{44}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)\le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\le e^{-nq/8}.
\]
Applying this inequality to all center-side counts and using ball inclusion yields an event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously in \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c nc_mh_*^{\kappa},
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}. \tag{45}
\]
Uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
 \frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{46}
\]
Thus \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) everywhere on \(G_n\), for all sufficiently large \(n\). The minimizing property of the selected bandwidth, (43), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) imply
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}{N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{47}
\]

Condition on the scores. If \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(s=\min\{h_0,r_0/2\}\) for any \(r_0>0\), the one-sided restricted score measure satisfies
\[
 P_X\{X\in\mathcal A_t:d(X,x)<r_0\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^\kappa>0.
\]
Thus every boundary point belongs to both side supports. Lipschitz continuity and the trace definition give \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\). If the selected count \(N\) is nonzero, Lipschitz smoothness gives
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{48}
\]
Let
\[
 Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) and \(|\mathcal H_n|\le2+\log_2n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{49}
\]
Every selected nonempty set belongs to \(\mathscr S_n\). Since \(h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\), equations (47)--(49), followed by the triangle inequality over the sides, show that the conditional expected supremum loss is at most \(Cr\) on \(G_n\).

On \(G_n^c\), a selected set comes from either an admissible bandwidth or the fallback \(h_0\). The trace envelope and Lipschitz smoothness bound the conditional mean of every nonempty fit in absolute value by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\), while an empty fit is zero. Equation (49), with \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{50}
\]
Using (45)--(50), and the common divergence in (46),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 &\le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}h_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}\\
 &\le Cr. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{51}
\]
The constants are uniform in \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\): dyadic factors are bounded by powers of two with exponent at most \(\overline\kappa\), and (46) gives a common diverging lower bound for the exponential argument.

The pervasive lower construction in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), explicitly certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), applies at scale \(a_0r\). Its packing logarithm is bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(\log n\), uniformly over the exponent range, whereas the product divergence is at most
\[
 Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n\le Ca_0^4\log n
\]
for \(0<a_0<1\). A fixed sufficiently small \(a_0\) and \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) yield
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge cr. \tag{52}
\]

It remains to verify the fallback probability. Take a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net; it has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\) points. At every center and side, the expected radius-\(h_0/2\) count is at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^{\kappa}\), which uniformly in the exponent range is eventually at least \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\). The binomial inequality derived above, a union bound, and ball inclusion show that, outside an event of probability at most
\[
 C\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}, \tag{53}
\]
one has \(N_{t,n}(x,h_0)\ge8\log(e+n/h_0)\) for every \(x,t\). Thus \(h_0\) is admissible everywhere and no fallback occurs. Enlarging \(C\), (53) implies the claimed \(Cn^3\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}\) bound. Equation (50) and the sharper event estimate (45) show that the fallback contribution is already included in (51). Equations (51)--(53) prove all conclusions.
