Fix a score vector outside the \(P_X^{\otimes n}\)-null set on which either the conditional Gaussian assertion or the conditional product factorization below can fail.

First consider the deterministic trace count.  For a closed disk with center \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\), membership of a fixed point \(X_i\) is equivalent to
\[
 2c^{\mathsf T}X_i+s\ge \lVert X_i\rVert_2^2,
 \qquad s=r^2-\lVert c\rVert_2^2. \tag{53}
\]
Consequently, every disk trace on \(\{X_1,\ldots,X_n\}\) is a sign pattern induced by \(n\) affine hyperplanes in the three-dimensional parameter \((c_1,c_2,s)\).  A trace realized by a closed disk can be made strict without changing the trace: if some points are excluded, increase the radius by less than their strictly positive minimum distance from the disk, and if no point is excluded, use any sufficiently small increase.  Thus equality in (53) creates no additional trace.

Let \(A(n,v)\) be the largest number of open cells cut out by \(n\) affine hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^v\).  Adding the last hyperplane retains all old cells and splits at most \(A(n-1,v-1)\) of them, because the arrangement induced on that hyperplane has dimension \(v-1\).  Hence
\[
 A(n,v)\le A(n-1,v)+A(n-1,v-1),
 \qquad A(0,v)=A(n,0)=1. \tag{54}
\]
Induction on \((n,v)\), with Pascal's identity in (54), yields
\[
 A(n,3)\le\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj. \tag{55}
\]
Restricting to genuine disks, fixing \(h\), restricting the centers to \(\mathcal B\), or intersecting every trace with the fixed side set \(\{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t\}\) cannot increase the number of traces.  Multiplication by the two choices of \(t\) and by \(|\mathcal H_n|\), which is finite by \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), proves
\[
 |\mathscr S_n|\le2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj.
\]

It remains to prove the stochastic assertions.  If \(\mathscr S_n=\varnothing\), both follow from the stated zero-maximum convention.  Suppose \(|\mathscr S_n|\ge1\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), the observed pairs are independent, so their regular conditional laws given \((X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) factor.  Hence the residuals \(\varepsilon_i=Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\) are conditionally independent.  Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), an index in a side-\(t\) candidate set satisfies \(T_i=t\).  The restricted score measure assigns full mass to \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\): indeed, using the countable rational-ball base of \(\mathbb R^2\), its complement is a countable union of basic open sets of restricted mass zero.  Thus \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) applies to every relevant index outside the fixed null set and gives
\[
 \mathcal L_P(\varepsilon_i\mid X_i)=\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2).
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), completing the square in the Gaussian density therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[e^{\lambda\varepsilon_i}\mid X_i\right]
 &=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
 \int_{\mathbb R}\exp\!\left\{\lambda u-\frac{u^2}{2\sigma^2}\right\}\,du\\
 &=e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
 \int_{\mathbb R}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{(u-\sigma^2\lambda)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right\}\,du
 =e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{56}
\]
For a nonempty \(S\in\mathscr S_n\), put
\[
 Z_S=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i.
\]
Conditional independence and (56) imply, for every \(u\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[e^{uZ_S}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 &=\prod_{i\in S}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 e^{u\varepsilon_i/(\sigma\sqrt{|S|})}\mid X_i
 \right]\\
 &=\prod_{i\in S}e^{u^2/(2|S|)}
 =e^{u^2/2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{57}
\]
For \(z>0\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{zZ_S}\), followed by (57), yields
\[
 \Pr_P(Z_S>z\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le e^{-z^2}\mathbb E_P[e^{zZ_S}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]
 =e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{58}
\]
The same calculation for \(-Z_S\) and a union bound over \(S\in\mathscr S_n\) give
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|>z
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le2|\mathscr S_n|e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{59}
\]
This step does not require independence among the set-indexed sums.

Finally, let \(z_0=\sqrt{2\log\{2|\mathscr S_n|\}}\).  Since \(|\mathscr S_n|\ge1\), one has \(z_0>1\).  The tail-integral identity for a nonnegative random variable, (59), and
\[
 \int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz
 \le z_0^{-1}e^{-z_0^2/2}
\]
therefore imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 &\le z_0+2|\mathscr S_n|\int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\\
 &\le z_0+z_0^{-1}\le z_0+1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Together with the empty-family case, this is exactly the claimed bound with \(1\vee|\mathscr S_n|\), and the proof is complete.
