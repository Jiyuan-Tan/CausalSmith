For a law \(P\), define the finite one-sided score measure
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t).
\]
If \(P\) belongs to either class, \(x\in\mathcal B\), and \(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\).  By \(h_0>0\), \(c_m>0\), and \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\),
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^{\kappa}>0. \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  The repaired definition of \(g_{t,P}\), the Lipschitz assumption, and the definition of the trace imply
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{12}
\]
Under \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), sharp assignment makes \(X\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T=t\), so all averages below have conditional observed-outcome mean \(g_{t,P}(X)\).  Consistency supplies the causal interpretation on these assignment-compatible sides; the statistical calculations use only the observed regression.

We first prove the lower bounds by subexperiments inside the same classes.  Begin with any member of the nonempty class under consideration and retain its score law and geometry.  On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), set \(Y(s)=\sigma Z_s\), \(s\in\{0,1\}\), to obtain a baseline law \(Q_0\).  Its regressions and traces vanish.  The design conditions are unchanged, while the response conditions follow directly, so \(Q_0\) remains in the same class.

For the isolated class take
\[
 h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is chosen below.  For large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) and the definition of \(\mathsf J_P\) provide \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,P}(x_h,h)=a_P(x_h,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}. \tag{13}
\]
Set
\[
 \psi_h(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_h)}h\right)_+,
 \qquad \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12}.
\]
If \(t_h=1\), shift \(Y(1)\) upward by \(\eta_h\psi_h(X)\); if \(t_h=0\), shift \(Y(0)\) downward by the same amount, leaving the other potential outcome unshifted.  The perturbation is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, is bounded by \(L\), preserves the score law, and preserves Gaussian errors.  Hence the resulting law \(Q_1\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  By (12),
\[
 \|\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\|_{\infty}\ge\eta_h. \tag{14}
\]
Using the Gaussian identity (8), the support of the tent, and (13),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=\frac{\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}\int\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,P}(z)\\
 &\le\frac{C_mL^2}{288\sigma^2}h^{\kappa+2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
Additivity of relative entropy for product measures gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})\le Ca_0^{\kappa+2}.
\]
Choose \(a_0\) so that this is at most \(1/8\).  The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), applied with the sup metric and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}})
 \ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}=c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{16}
\]

For the pervasive class take
\[
 h=a_0\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\) supplies \(h\)-separated points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) with
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},\qquad a_P(x_j,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}. \tag{17}
\]
Finiteness follows from \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\).  Choose at each point a side attaining the minimum, and perturb that side with a radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) tent, upward on side one and downward on side zero.  The preceding membership argument gives laws \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_M\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  The supports of distinct tents do not contain the other centers, so their target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(Lh/12\) in sup norm.  The calculation in (15) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cnh^{\kappa+2}=Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{18}
\]
For fixed \(\kappa\), (17) implies \(\log M\ge c_{\kappa}\log n\) eventually.  Choose \(a_0\) small enough that (18) is at most \(a\log M\), with \(a<1/8\).  The multiple-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{19}
\]

We next give genuine data-driven upper bounds.  Fix \(0<b\le h_0/2\), and choose a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated set \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) in \(\mathcal B\).  It is a \(b/2\)-net, and global boundary entropy gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\).  Let \(j(x)\) be the least index with \(d(x,z_{j(x)})\le b/2\), and define
\[
 K_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,d(X_i,z_j)\le b\},
\]
\[
 \overline g_{t,j}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,d(X_i,z_j)\le b\}}{K_{t,j}\vee1},
 \qquad
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)=\overline g_{1,j(x)}-\overline g_{0,j(x)}.
\]
This is one estimator measurable in the sample and known geometry.  On \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), equations (11)--(12) and Lipschitz smoothness imply
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j(x)}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le\frac{3Lb}{2}. \tag{20}
\]

We shall use the following elementary count calculation.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) and \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) give
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{21}
\]
For the pervasive upper bound set \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  Polynomial lower mass, (21), and a union bound give an event \(H_b\) on which every \(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^{\kappa}/2\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-cnc_mb^{\kappa}}. \tag{22}
\]
Conditionally on the scores and on \(H_b\), Gaussian errors and a union bound over \(2M\) averages give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e/b)}{nc_mb^{\kappa}}}. \tag{23}
\]
Since \(nb^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), the right side is at most \(C_{\kappa}b\).  On \(H_b^c\), bounded traces and Lipschitz smoothness bound every conditional mean by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\), while a Gaussian union bound bounds the conditional expected maximum noise by \(C\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\).  Combining (20)--(23) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\|_{\infty}
 \le C_{\kappa}b+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}b^{-1}e^{-cnc_mb^{\kappa}}
 \le C_{\kappa}b. \tag{24}
\]

For the isolated upper bound set \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\), so \(nb^{\kappa}=b^{-2}\).  Fix a side, choose \(D\ge C_m\), put \(u_{\ell}=\min\{1,D2^{\ell}b^{\kappa}\}\), and group net centers according as \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\) or \(u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_{\ell}\).  Every center in group \(\ell\) obeys
\[
 a_P(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_{\ell}.
\]
Applying the isolated profile upper bound at radius \(b/2\), and the global entropy bound for the terminal group, yields
\[
 M_{\ell}\le C_{\kappa}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{25}
\]
In group zero, polynomial lower mass makes the mean count at least \(c_mb^{-2}\); in group \(\ell\ge1\), it exceeds \(D2^{\ell-1}b^{-2}\).  Equations (21) and (25), summed over groups and sides, give an event \(H_b\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le C_{\kappa}\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cb^{-2}2^{\ell}}
 \le C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}/b^2}. \tag{26}
\]
On \(H_b\), a Gaussian union bound at threshold \(zb\) gives
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|>zb
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_{\kappa}\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cz^22^{\ell}}.
\]
The series is at most \(C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}z^2}\) for all sufficiently large fixed \(z\); integration gives conditional expected stochastic maximum \(O_{\kappa}(b)\).  Equation (20) gives bias \(O(b)\), and the bad-event contribution is \(o(b)\) by (26) and the same Gaussian maximum bound used after (23).  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\|_{\infty}
 \le C_{\kappa}b. \tag{27}
\]
The definition of \(R_n\), applied to (24) and (27), proves both upper bounds.  Together with (16) and (19), this proves both matched rates.  The explicit nonempty circular specifications are supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), while \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) independently certifies concrete legal lower families.  Since the common scalar modulus is \(c_mh^{\kappa}\) but the rates differ by \((\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\), the scalar modulus alone cannot determine the expected-sup frontier.
