Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), and write \(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\).  If \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(r>0\), and \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\), positivity of \(m\) and the declared lower-mass member property in \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) give
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge m(s)>0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) on both sides.  The Lipschitz member property makes the specified representative \(g_{t,P}\) continuous at \(x\), so the definition of the boundary trace gives
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{2}
\]
The complementary assignment and sharp-assignment member property identify each side average with observations having \(T=t\).  The bounded-trace member property gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\).  Since Euclidean projection onto a closed interval containing the target is nonexpansive, projection cannot increase the pointwise error, and its pointwise loss is at most \(4L\).

For the upper bound, fix \(h\) such that \(h,2h\in\mathcal H_n\) and
\[
 nm(h)\ge A\log(e+n/h). \tag{3}
\]
Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_K\) be a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  Maximality makes it an \(h\)-net, while \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) gives \(K\le C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), and the lower-mass member property,
\[
 N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\sim\operatorname{Bin}\{n,q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\},
 \qquad q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\ge m(h). \tag{4}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1-y\le e^{-y}\), yields
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 \le 2^{nq/2}\mathbb E(2^{-N})
 =2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq(1-\log 2)/2}
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{5}
\]
Let \(H_h\) be the event that every center-side count is at least \(nm(h)/2\).  A union bound over the \(2K\) pairs in (4) gives
\[
 P(H_h^c)\le2C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. \tag{6}
\]
For each \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose a net center \(z_j\) with \(d(x,z_j)\le h\).  The triangle inequality gives \(B(z_j,h)\subseteq B(x,2h)\), and hence, on \(H_h\),
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,2h)ge nm(h)/2. \tag{7}
\]
Choose the fixed constant \(A\ge16\).  Equations (3) and (7), together with \(\log(e+n/(2h))\le\log(e+n/h)\), show that \(2h\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(H_h\).

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\).  The selected bandwidths are then fixed functions of the scores.  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) and the score-conditional sub-Gaussian member property, the residuals \(\varepsilon_i=Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\) are conditionally independent and, for every deterministic index set \(S\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\lambda\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right\}\middle|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le \exp\{\sigma^2\lambda^2|S|/2\}. \tag{8}
\]
The disk-pattern count and conditional tail calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) therefore apply.  If \(Z_n\) denotes the maximum normalized residual sum appearing there, then
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le\sqrt{2\log\!\left\{4|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj\right\}}+1
 \le C_0\sqrt{\log(e+n)}. \tag{9}
\]
For a nonempty side average at bandwidth \(b\), equation (2) and the Lipschitz member property give conditional bias at most \(Lb\).  Its stochastic error is at most \(\sigma Z_n/\sqrt{N_{t,n}(x,b)}\).  Since \(b\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log(e+n)\), equation (9) bounds the conditional expected stochastic error uniformly by a constant times the stochastic term in the selection criterion.  On \(H_h\), the selected set is nonempty and pointwise minimization of that criterion, followed by comparison with the admissible bandwidth \(2h\) from (7), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\middle|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C_1\left\{Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}. \tag{10}
\]
Here the factor two from the two sides is absorbed into \(C_1\).

Projection, the \(4L\) loss bound on \(H_h^c\), and (6) now yield
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le C_2\left\{Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 +C_3Lh^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. \tag{11}
\]
After increasing \(A\) so that \(A/8\ge2\), equation (3) implies
\[
 h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}
 \le h^{-1}(e+n/h)^{-2}
 \le h,
\]
so the final term in (11) is absorbed into the bias term.  Taking the infimum over qualifying dyadic pairs and combining with the unconditional \(4L\) bound proves the upper display.  The definitions of the empirical counts, admissible bandwidths, count-adaptive bandwidth, estimator, and fixed projection show directly that this single data-driven estimator uses neither \(m\) nor \(\mathsf J_{-}\).

We next prove the same-class converse.  Choose \(P^\circ\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), which exists by the theorem's nonemptiness premise, and retain its score marginal and assignment geometry.  On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define \(P_0\) by \(Y(t)=\sigma Z_t\) and define the observed outcome by consistency.  All score-dependent member properties are unchanged, both side regressions and traces vanish, and the residual kernel is exactly centered Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2\).  Thus all member conditions in \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) hold and \(P_0\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\).

Fix \(0<h\le h_0/3\), \(0<u\le1\), and put \(M=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\ge1\).  The declared-profile member property and the definition of \(\mathsf J_{P_0}\) supply \(h\)-separated points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) such that \(a_{P_0}(x_j,h)\le u\).  For every \(j\), choose \(t_j\in\{0,1\}\) with \(q_{t_j,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u\), and set
\[
 \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12},
 \qquad
 \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+. \tag{12}
\]
Define \(P_j\) by retaining the score law and independent Gaussian noises and setting
\[
 \begin{cases}
 Y(1)=\eta_h\psi_{j,h}(X)+\sigma Z_1,\quad Y(0)=\sigma Z_0,&t_j=1,\\
 Y(1)=\sigma Z_1,\quad Y(0)=-\eta_h\psi_{j,h}(X)+\sigma Z_0,&t_j=0.
 \end{cases} \tag{13}
\]
The perturbation in (13) is \(3\eta_h/h=L/4\)-Lipschitz.  Since \(hle h_0/3<1\), its absolute trace is at most \(\eta_h<L\).  It changes neither the score-dependent conditions nor the centered Gaussian residual kernel.  Therefore every \(P_j\) belongs to \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\); this derives membership from the class conditions rather than assigning it by construction.

At \(x_j\), the target under \(P_j\) equals \(\eta_h\).  If \(k\ne j\), the \(h\)-separation of the centers and the radius-\(h/3\) support of the tents imply \(\psi_{k,h}(x_j)=0\).  Consequently,
\[
 \|\tau_{P_j}-\tau_{P_k}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h,
 \qquad 0\le j<k\le M. \tag{14}
\]
Only observations with \(X\in\mathcal A_{t_j}\cap B(x_j,h/3)\) have a shifted observed conditional mean.  The exact centered-Gaussian likelihood formula and \(0\le\psi_{j,h}\le1\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}
   \int_{\mathcal A_{t_j}}\psi_{j,h}(z)^2\,dP_X(z)\\
 &\le\frac{nL^2h^2}{288\sigma^2}
   q_{t_j,P_0}(x_j,h)
 \le\frac{nL^2h^2u}{288\sigma^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
If \(nL^2h^2u/\sigma^2\le a\), retain one alternative and choose the universal \(a>0\) so that (15) is at most \(1/8\).  The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), applied with \(2\delta=\eta_h\), yields minimax risk at least \(cLh\).  If instead \(M\ge2\) and \(nL^2h^2u/\sigma^2\le a\log(M+1)\), use \(\log(M+1)/\log M\le\log3/\log2\) and decrease \(a\) by that universal factor.  Then (15) meets the multiple-law hypothesis of the same lemma, and (14) yields
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}\ge cLh. \tag{16}
\]

For a sequence of nonempty classes with moduli \(m_n\), the displayed sufficient condition supplies qualifying \(h_n\) eventually; equation (11) then tends to zero because \(h_n\downarrow0\) and \(nm_n(h_n)/\log(e+n/h_n)\to\infty\).  Conversely, let \(F_n\) be the set of radii for which some \(u\) satisfies either information condition in the lower display.  If \(\sup F_n\not\to0\), then along a subsequence there are feasible radii bounded below by a positive constant, and (16) keeps the minimax risk bounded away from zero.  This proves the stated necessary condition.

For \(m_\alpha\), \(\mathrm{prop:exponential\text{-}horn\text{-}consistency}\) gives \(m_\alpha(h)\asymp h^{\alpha+1}e^{-h^{-\alpha}}\).  Set \(s_n=\{(\log n)/2\}^{-1/\alpha}\).  Then
\[
 \frac{nm_\alpha(s_n)}{\log(e+n/s_n)}
 \asymp\frac{n^{1/2}s_n^{\alpha+1}}{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{17}
\]
Round upward to a dyadic \(h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(s_n\le h_n<2s_n\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), this point is not a grid endpoint, so \(2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\); monotonicity of \(m_\alpha\) and \(h_n<2s_n\) preserve (17).  Hence the sufficient condition holds for every fixed positive exponential modulus.

Finally, put \(u=C_mh^\kappa\).  When \(M\asymp1\), the first information condition balances \(nh^{\kappa+2}\asymp1\), and therefore gives \(h\asymp n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\).  When \(M\asymp h^{-1}\), the second balances \(nh^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log(1/h)\), and therefore gives \(h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\).  These lower rates match the two rates in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).  For a slack declaration, the circular pervasive witness can be paired with \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=\mathbf1\{u\ge C_mh^\kappa\}\): the declared lower profile then exposes only one site, whereas the global-net upper argument still pays for the full boundary entropy.  Thus the arbitrary-profile result is the claimed two-sided bracket and does not assert an unproved exact criterion.
