The established theorem \(\mathrm{thm:global\text{-}honest\text{-}band\text{-}and\text{-}width\text{-}obstruction}\) already supplies a data-measurable band with finite-sample simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\gamma\) on every declared nonempty triangular union, the global upper bound of order \((\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\), the matched global expected-supremum width lower bound on the certified circular pervasive and base classes in its stated small-miscoverage regime, and the general full-observed-law two-point inequality.  The following argument strengthens its circular pervasive converse from expected supremum width to the pointwise ratio appearing in the open question.

Fix the circular inside--outside specification certified by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), fix \(\kappa\in(2,\overline\kappa]\), and put
\[
 r_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
 \qquad h_n=a_0r_n,
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is sufficiently small.  Let \(Q_0,\ldots,Q_M\) be the pervasive tent laws \(\mathbb P_{\kappa,j,h_n}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), with centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), all these laws belong to \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), \(M\asymp h_n^{-1}\), and
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le CnL^2h_n^{\kappa+2}/\sigma^2.
\]
Because \(nh_n^{\kappa+2}=a_0^{\kappa+2}\log n\) and \(\log M\asymp\log n\), choosing \(a_0\) small enough makes the last display at most \(a\log M\), where \(a<1/8\) is the constant in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\).  Hence that lemma gives a numerical \(p_{\mathsf F}>0\) such that every decoder \(\widehat J\) satisfies
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le M}Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}
 \ge p_{\mathsf F}. \tag{1}
\]

Let a band have simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\) on \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), where \(\epsilon_n\to0\), and write \(I_k=\mathcal C_n(x_k)\).  Define \(\widehat J=0\) if every \(I_k\) contains zero, and otherwise define \(\widehat J\) as the smallest \(k\) for which \(0\notin I_k\).  Under \(Q_0\), simultaneous coverage implies that every \(I_k\) contains zero, so
\[
 Q_0^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne0\}\le\gamma+\epsilon_n. \tag{2}
\]
For \(j\ge1\), the separation of the tent centers gives
\[
 \tau_{Q_j}(x_j)=\Delta_n:=Lh_n/2,
 \qquad \tau_{Q_j}(x_k)=0\quad(k\ne j).
\]
On the simultaneous-coverage event, every \(I_k\) with \(k\ne j\) contains zero.  If also \(W_n(x_j)<\Delta_n/2\), then \(I_j\) has diameter less than \(\Delta_n\) and contains \(\Delta_n\), so it cannot contain zero.  Thus \(\widehat J=j\), and Markov's inequality yields
\[
 Q_j^{\otimes n}\{\widehat J\ne j\}
 \le \gamma+\epsilon_n+
 \frac{2\mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n(x_j)}{\Delta_n}. \tag{3}
\]
If \(\gamma<p_{\mathsf F}\), then for all sufficiently large \(n\), (1)--(3) imply that some \(j\ge1\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_j}W_n(x_j)
 \ge \frac{\Delta_n}{2}
 \{p_{\mathsf F}-\gamma-\epsilon_n\}
 \ge c_\gamma r_n. \tag{4}
\]

It remains to compare (4) with the declared local benchmark rather than merely with \(r_n\).  Choose \(\bar h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(h_n\le\bar h_n<2h_n\), which exists for all sufficiently large \(n\) by \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\).  Circular witness membership gives, uniformly in \(x\in S^1\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 q_{t,Q_j}(x,\bar h_n)\ge c\bar h_n^\kappa.
\]
Moreover, every \(\bar h_n\)-separated subset counted by \(\mathsf J_{Q_j}\) is an \(\bar h_n\)-separated subset of the whole boundary.  The global boundary-entropy assumption therefore gives
\[
 K_{Q_j}(x,\bar h_n)
 \le 1\vee C_{\mathcal B}\bar h_n^{-1}.
\]
Using this admissible bandwidth in \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}oracle\text{-}benchmark}\), and using \(n r_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_{n,Q_j}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x_j)
 &\le 2L\bar h_n+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log\{e(1\vee C_{\mathcal B}\bar h_n^{-1})/\gamma\}}{n}
 \left\{q_{0,Q_j}(x_j,\bar h_n)^{-1}
       +q_{1,Q_j}(x_j,\bar h_n)^{-1}\right\}}\\
 &\le C_\gamma r_n. \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining (4) and (5) proves the pointwise-ratio partial converse
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 \frac{\mathbb E_P W_n(x)}{r_{n,P}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)}
 \ge c_\gamma>0
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), under the certified circular specification and the stated small-miscoverage condition.  The same conclusion holds for any declared triangular union containing this pervasive component.  This proof uses a pointwise decoder and therefore does not infer a pointwise statement merely from the previously proved expected-supremum lower bound.
