Let \(P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:triangular\text{-}profile\text{-}union}\), \(P\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) for at least one \(\kappa\in(2,\overline\kappa]\), and hence it satisfies every member condition in \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\).  We first verify the trace relation used by the band.  Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(s>0\), and put \(b=\min\{h_0,s/2\}\).  The polynomial lower-mass condition contained in \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\) gives
\[
 P_X\{z\in\mathcal A_t:d(z,x)<s\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,b)\ge c_m b^\kappa>0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus every neighborhood of \(x\) has positive mass under the one-sided restricted score measure, so \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  The Lipschitz condition \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) makes \(g_{t,P}\) continuous at \(x\) on \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  Consequently, the definition of the boundary trace yields
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{2}
\]

Write
\[
 z_n=\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}.
\]
For fixed scores, denote by \(\mathcal S_n\) the disk-pattern family in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\).  Its cardinality is at most \(M_n\).  Substitution of \(z_n\) into that lemma gives, for \(P_X^{\otimes n}\)-almost every score vector,
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{\max_{S\in\mathcal S_n}
 \frac{\left|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|}
 {\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}>z_n
 \,\middle|\,X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le 2|\mathcal S_n|e^{-z_n^2/2}\le\gamma. \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) are exactly the sampling and conditional moment-generating-function premises of the established lemma.  Complementarity \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\) and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\), inherited through the base-class definition, ensure that a side-\(t\) disk pattern contains exactly observations with \(T_i=t\).

Consider the selected side fit at \((x,t)\), and abbreviate
\[
 \widehat h=\widehat h_{t,n}(x),\qquad
 N=N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h),\qquad
 S=\{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,x)\le\widehat h\}.
\]
If \(N>0\), then \(S\in\mathcal S_n\), and on the event complementary to that in (3),
\[
 \left|\frac1N\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{t,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le \sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}{N}}. \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, (2) and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) imply
\[
 \left|\frac1N\sum_{i\in S}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le \frac1N\sum_{i\in S}L\,d(X_i,x)\le L\widehat h. \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) and \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\) show that the side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\).  If \(N=0\), that definition makes the side fit equal to zero, while \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\) gives
\[
 |\widehat g_{t,n}(x;\widehat h)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|\le L=B_{t,n}(x). \tag{6}
\]
Because (3) controls every disk pattern simultaneously, (4)--(6) hold simultaneously for all \(x\in\mathcal B\) and both sides with conditional probability at least \(1-\gamma\).  Using \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) and the definition \(\tau_P=\theta_{1,P}-\theta_{0,P}\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\le B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)\qquad(x\in\mathcal B). \tag{7}
\]
Thus the displayed band contains \(\tau_P(x)\) for every \(x\) on this event.  Integrating (3) over the scores proves
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)}
 P\{\tau_P(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal B\}\ge1-\gamma. \tag{8}
\]
For measurability, each count and numerator is a finite sum of Borel functions of the data and \(x\); \(\mathcal H_n\) is finite; and \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\) specifies both a deterministic tie break and an empty-set value.  Hence \(\widehat h_{t,n}\), the selected fits, the radii \(B_{t,n}\), and the two interval endpoints are data measurable.

We next prove the expected-width upper bound.  Fix \(\kappa\in(2,\overline\kappa]\), put
\[
 r_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}, \tag{9}
\]
and, for all sufficiently large \(n\), choose \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) such that \(r_n\le h_*<2r_n\).  Such a grid point exists by \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), because \(r_n\downarrow0\), \(r_n<h_0\) eventually, and \(r_n/(h_0 2^{-\lceil\log_2 n\rceil})\to\infty\).

Let \(\mathcal N_*\) be a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  Compactness of \(\mathcal B\) and maximality make \(\mathcal N_*\) an \(h_*/2\)-net, while \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\), inherited by every base-class law, gives
\[
 |\mathcal N_*|\le 2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}. \tag{10}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal N_*\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), define the proof-intermediate count
\[
 \widetilde N_{t,n}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,z)\le h_*/2\}.
\]
Under \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), this is binomial with success probability
\[
 q_{t,P}(z,h_*/2)\ge c_m(h_*/2)^\kappa, \tag{11}
\]
where (11) is \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) through \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\).  For \(Z\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to the nonnegative variable \(2^{-Z}\) yields
\[
 \Pr\{Z\le nq/2\}
 \le 2^{nq/2}\mathbb E[2^{-Z}]
 =2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le \exp\{-nq(1-\log2)/2\}\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{12}
\]
The equality uses the binomial generating function, and the first exponential inequality uses \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) for \(0\le u<1\).

Take the union bound in (12) over (10) and both sides.  If \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose \(z\in\mathcal N_*\) with \(d(x,z)\le h_*/2\); then \(B(z,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\).  We therefore obtain an event \(G_n\) and constants \(c,C>0\), depending only on the declared envelopes, such that uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{base}}\),
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge cnh_*^\kappa,\qquad
 P(G_n^c)\le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnh_*^\kappa}. \tag{13}
\]
From (9) and \(h_*\asymp r_n\),
\[
 nh_*^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n,\qquad
 \frac{nh_*^\kappa}{\log(e+n/h_*)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) show that \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(G_n\), for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The minimizing property in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), comparison with \(h_*\), (13), and (14) now imply
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}\le C r_n \quad\text{on }G_n, \tag{15}
\]
simultaneously in \(x,t\).  Indeed, the objective at \(h_*\) is bounded by
\[
 Lh_*+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_*)}{cnh_*^\kappa}}\le C r_n, \tag{16}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(h_*\asymp r_n\) and \(nr_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\).

The elementary bounds \(|\mathcal H_n|\le 2+\log_2 n\) and \(\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj\le Cn^3\) give
\[
 \log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C_\gamma\log n
 \le C_\gamma\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x)), \tag{17}
\]
because \(0<\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\le h_0<1\).  On \(G_n\) the selected count is positive, so (15) and (17) give
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\}}B_{t,n}(x)\le C_\gamma r_n. \tag{18}
\]
On \(G_n^c\), the definition of \(B_{t,n}\), \(h_0<1\), and \(N\ge1\) in its positive-count branch give the deterministic bound
\[
 \sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\le L+\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}. \tag{19}
\]
Furthermore, (9), (13), and \(h_*\asymp r_n\) imply
\[
 h_*^{-1}e^{-cnh_*^\kappa}\sqrt{\log(2M_n/\gamma)}=o(r_n), \tag{20}
\]
because \(nh_*^\kappa\) grows as a positive power of \(n\), up to a logarithmic factor, and hence its exponential dominates every polynomial and logarithm.  Since the displayed interval has
\[
 W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x), \tag{21}
\]
splitting the expectation over \(G_n\) and \(G_n^c\), then applying (13) and (18)--(20), proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{base}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)\le
 C_\gamma\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}. \tag{22}
\]

Now suppose \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\ne\varnothing\), and let a band sequence have simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\) on that class, where \(\epsilon_n\downarrow0\).  All premises of the already-established \(\mathrm{lem:geometry\text{-}general\text{-}pervasive\text{-}band\text{-}width\text{-}obstruction}\) hold: the assignment regions are complementary, the geometry satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\), and the class definition supplies the base conditions together with \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\).  That lemma therefore gives, for every fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)\ge
 c_\gamma\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}, \tag{23}
\]
and also
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 \frac{\mathbb E_PW_n(x)}{r_{n,P}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)}\ge c_\gamma. \tag{24}
\]
These conclusions are geometry-general.  The role of \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) is only to exhibit one specification for which the pervasive class is nonempty.  Since \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\subseteq\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{base}}\), a band honest over the base class is honest over the pervasive subclass, and its base-class supremum is at least the supremum in (23).  Conversely, (22) applies to the displayed band on both the base class and its pervasive subclass.  Equations (22)--(23) thus prove its matched global expected-supremum half-width frontier on both classes, while (24) is the asserted local-benchmark converse.

Finally, we prove the two-law inequality independently of pervasive thinning.  Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), and for \(j=0,1\) let
\[
 A_j=\{\tau_{P_j}(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\}.
\]
Simultaneous coverage over a class containing \(P_0,P_1\) implies
\[
 P_j^{\otimes n}(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n,\qquad j=0,1. \tag{25}
\]
By the defining variational inequality for total variation of the full observed product laws,
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_1)\ge P_1^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 -\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{26}
\]
Combining (25)--(26) with \(P_0(A_0\cap A_1)\ge P_0(A_0)+P_0(A_1)-1\) gives
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_0\cap A_1)\ge
 1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{27}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains both target values, so its half-diameter satisfies
\[
 W_n(x)\ge\frac12|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|. \tag{28}
\]
Multiplying (28) by the indicator of \(A_0\cap A_1\), taking the \(P_0\)-expectation, and using the positive part of (27) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)\ge
 \frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
 \left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+.
\]
This last argument necessarily compares full observed laws.  It does not turn score-law total variation into full-law distinguishability, and the global factor in (17) need not match the bounded local multiplicity term in \(r_{n,P}^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  Accordingly, the theorem asserts neither local-oracle width achievability over an arbitrary triangular union nor an exact score-law adaptation criterion.
