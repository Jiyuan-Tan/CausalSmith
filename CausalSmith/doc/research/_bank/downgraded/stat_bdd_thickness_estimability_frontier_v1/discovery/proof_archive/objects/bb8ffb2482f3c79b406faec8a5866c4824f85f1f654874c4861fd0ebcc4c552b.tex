Fix \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), and put \(I_t=\{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t\}\).  For \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(r>0\), write \(D(c,r)=\{u:d(u,c)\le r\}\).  On introducing the third parameter
\[
 s=r^2-\lVert c\rVert_2^2,
\]
membership of \(X_i\) in this closed disk is equivalent to
\[
 X_i\in D(c,r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ell_i(c,s):=2c^{\mathsf T}X_i+s-\lVert X_i\rVert_2^2\ge0. \tag{1}
\]
This equivalence uses the definition of the Euclidean distance \(d\).

We first show that weak inequalities in (1) introduce no additional traces on the finite score set.  Suppose that \(J=\{i:X_i\in D(c,r)\}\).  If \(J\ne\{1,\ldots,n\}\), closedness of the disk gives
\[
 \delta:=\min_{i\notin J}\bigl\{d(X_i,c)-r\bigr\}>0.
\]
Choose \(r'\) with \(r<r'<r+\delta\).  If \(J=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), choose any \(r'>r\).  In either case,
\[
 i\in J\Longrightarrow d(X_i,c)\le r<r',\qquad
 i\notin J\Longrightarrow d(X_i,c)>r', \tag{2}
\]
so the same trace is represented by strict signs of the \(n\) affine functions in (1), with \(s'=r'^2-\lVert c\rVert_2^2\).

Let \(A(q,v)\) denote the maximum number of open cells formed by \(q\) affine hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^v\).  Adding the \(q\)-th hyperplane leaves all old cells and splits only cells met by that hyperplane.  The intersections of the first \(q-1\) hyperplanes with the last one form an arrangement of at most \(q-1\) affine hyperplanes in a space of dimension \(v-1\).  Consequently,
\[
 A(q,v)\le A(q-1,v)+A(q-1,v-1),\qquad
 A(0,v)=A(q,0)=1. \tag{3}
\]
Induction on \(q+v\), using Pascal's identity and the convention \(\binom qj=0\) for \(j>q\), yields
\[
 A(q,v)\le\sum_{j=0}^{v}\binom qj. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, substituting the induction hypotheses in (3) gives
\[
 A(q,v)\le\sum_{j=0}^{v}\binom{q-1}{j}
             +\sum_{j=0}^{v-1}\binom{q-1}{j}
          =\sum_{j=0}^{v}\binom qj.
\]
Every strict sign pattern of the functions \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n\) labels an open cell of their affine-hyperplane arrangement in the parameter \((c_1,c_2,s)\in\mathbb R^3\).  Equations (2)--(4) therefore imply that all closed disks induce at most
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj \tag{5}
\]
different traces on \(\{X_1,\ldots,X_n\}\).

For fixed \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(h\in\mathcal H_n\), the sets in the lemma equal
\[
 I_t\cap\{i:X_i\in D(x,h)\},\qquad x\in\mathcal B.
\]
Restricting disk centers to \(\mathcal B\), fixing their radius at \(h\), intersecting every trace with the fixed set \(I_t\), and discarding the empty trace cannot increase the number in (5).  Taking the union over the two possible sides and all \(h\in\mathcal H_n\), which is finite by \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), gives
\[
 |\mathscr S_n|\le\sum_{t=0}^{1}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_n}\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj
 =2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj. \tag{6}
\]

Assume now that \(\mathscr S_n\ne\varnothing\), as required by the stochastic part of the proposed statement.  All conditional assertions below are for \(P_X^{\otimes n}\)-almost every score vector, as required by the almost-everywhere conditional law in \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\).  A draw from a Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^2\) lies in its support almost surely: indeed, the complement of the support is covered by the countable collection of rational open balls having measure zero.  Hence, on \(\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t\}\), one has \(X_i\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) almost surely.

If index \(i\) occurs in some \(S\in\mathscr S_n\), then it occurs for at least one side \(t\).  If \(T_i=1\), the definition \(T_i=\mathbf 1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_1\}\) gives \(X_i\in\mathcal A_1\).  If \(T_i=0\), the witnessing side cannot be \(1\), so it is \(0\) and \(X_i\in\mathcal A_0\).  Thus every residual that occurs in the displayed maximum satisfies \(X_i\in\mathcal X_{T_i,P}\) almost surely.  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), the conditional joint law of the outcomes given \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) is the product of their one-observation conditional laws.  Applying \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) with \(t=T_i\) therefore gives, conditionally on the scores,
\[
 \varepsilon_i=Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\ \sim\ \mathcal N(0,\sigma^2), \tag{7}
\]
independently over all indices that occur in \(\mathscr S_n\).  In particular, for every nonempty \(S\in\mathscr S_n\), (7) and \(\sigma>0\) give
\[
 Z_S:=\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}\ \sim\ \mathcal N(0,1). \tag{8}
\]

For \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), completing the square in its density gives \(\mathbb E[e^{\lambda Z}]=e^{\lambda^2/2}\).  Markov's inequality with \(\lambda=z>0\), followed by the same calculation for \(-Z\), yields
\[
 \Pr\{|Z|>z\}\le2e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{9}
\]
The union bound, which does not require the variables \(Z_S\) to be mutually independent, now gives from (8)--(9)
\[
 \Pr\left\{\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{\left|\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}>z
 \,\middle|\,X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le\sum_{S\in\mathscr S_n}2e^{-z^2/2}
 =2|\mathscr S_n|e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{10}
\]

Finally, put \(m:=|\mathscr S_n|\).  The imposed nonemptiness condition gives \(m\ge1\), so set
\[
 M:=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|,\qquad
 z_0:=\sqrt{2\log(2m)}.
\]
The layer-cake identity for the nonnegative variable \(M\), followed by (10), gives
\[
 \mathbb E[M\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]
 \le z_0+2m\int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz. \tag{11}
\]
Since \(z_0>0\), direct integration of the derivative of \(e^{-z^2/2}\) shows
\[
 \int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz
 \le \frac{1}{z_0}\int_{z_0}^{\infty}z e^{-z^2/2}\,dz
 =\frac{e^{-z_0^2/2}}{z_0}.
\]
Because \(2m e^{-z_0^2/2}=1\) and \(z_0\ge\sqrt{2\log2}>1\), (11) implies
\[
 \mathbb E[M\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]\le z_0+\frac1{z_0}\le\sqrt{2\log\{2|\mathscr S_n|\}}+1,
\]
which is the claimed conditional expectation bound.
