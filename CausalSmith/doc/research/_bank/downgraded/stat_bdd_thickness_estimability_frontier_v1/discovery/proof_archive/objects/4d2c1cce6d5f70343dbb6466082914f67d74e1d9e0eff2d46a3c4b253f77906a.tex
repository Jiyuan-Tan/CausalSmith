Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), and define \(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\). If \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\). Positivity of \(m\) and the lower-mass member property give
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)
 \ge m(s)>0. \tag{54}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) on both sides. The Lipschitz member property makes the specified representative continuous at \(x\), so
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{55}
\]
The assignment partition and sharp assignment make \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T_i=t\). Hence, conditionally on the scores, each observed side average has mean equal to the corresponding average of \(g_{t,P}(X_i)\). The trace envelope gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\). Projection onto this interval cannot enlarge pointwise error, and the clipped loss is at most \(4L\).

We first prove the upper bound. Fix \(h\) such that \(h,2h\in\mathcal H_n\) and
\[
 nm(h)\ge A\log(e+n/h). \tag{56}
\]
Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_K\) be a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\). It is an \(h\)-net, and global boundary entropy gives \(K\le C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}\). By independent sampling, for each \(j,t\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\sim\operatorname{Bin}\{n,q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\},
 \qquad q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\ge m(h). \tag{57}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1-y\le e^{-y}\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{N\le nq/2\}
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{58}
\]
Let \(H_h\) be the event that \(N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\ge nm(h)/2\) for every \(j,t\). Equations (57)--(58) and the union bound imply
\[
 \Pr_P(H_h^c)\le2C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. \tag{59}
\]
For any \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose \(z_j\) with \(d(x,z_j)\le h\). Since \(B(z_j,h)\subseteq B(x,2h)\), on \(H_h\),
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,2h)
 \ge\frac{nm(h)}2. \tag{60}
\]
Choose the fixed constant \(A\) large enough that (56) makes the right side of (60) at least \(8\log(e+n/(2h))\). Then \(2h\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(H_h\).

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\). For any nonempty averaging set indexed by \((x,b,t)\), (55) and Lipschitz smoothness give
\[
 \left|
 \frac1{N_{t,n}(x,b)}
 \sum_{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,x)\le b}g_{t,P}(X_i)
 -\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|\le Lb. \tag{61}
\]
Let \(Z_n\) be the maximum normalized error over the nonempty disk patterns in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), with its zero convention. The exact conditional Gaussian hypothesis, as used in that lemma, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le\sqrt{2\log\left\{4|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj\right\}}+1
 \le C\sqrt{\log(e+n)}. \tag{62}
\]
On \(H_h\), every selected bandwidth is admissible and its set is nonempty. Conditional on the scores, it is fixed because it depends only on counts. For every candidate bandwidth \(b\le h_0<1\), \(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log(e+n)\) up to a fixed numerical factor. Equations (61)--(62) therefore bound the conditional expected side error by a constant times the selected criterion in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\). Its minimizing property and the admissible comparison bandwidth \(2h\), together with (60), imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n
 \right]
 \le C\left\{Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 \quad\text{on }H_h. \tag{63}
\]
The two side errors and fixed factors from \(2h\) and (60) are absorbed into \(C\).

Projection does not enlarge error, and on \(H_h^c\) its supremum is at most \(4L\). Taking expectations in (63) and using (59) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\\
 &\qquad\le C\left\{Lh+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 +CLh^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{64}
\]
Increasing \(A\) so \(A/8\ge2\), (56), \(h\le h_0<1\), and \(n\ge1\) imply
\[
 h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}
 \le h^{-1}(e+n/h)^{-A/8}
 \le h.
\]
Thus the last term in (64) is absorbed into \(CLh\). Independently of (56), the clipped loss is at most \(4L\). Taking the infimum over qualifying dyadic pairs, with the stated empty-set convention, proves the upper display. The explicit estimator definitions and the fixed projection use neither \(m\) nor \(\mathsf J_{-}\).

We next prove the lower display inside the same class. Choose \(P^\circ\) in the assumed nonempty class and retain its score marginal and geometry. On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define \(P_0\) by \(Y(t)=\sigma Z_t\). Every score-dependent member property is unchanged, while the side regressions and traces are zero. Therefore
\[
 P_0\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{65}
\]
Let \(M=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\ge1\). The lower-profile member property and the definition of \(\mathsf J_{P_0}\) supply \(h\)-separated points \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) such that
\[
 \min_{t\in\{0,1\}}q_{t,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u.
\]
Choose \(t_j\) attaining the minimum, and set
\[
 \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12},
 \qquad \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+. \tag{66}
\]
Retaining the score law and Gaussian errors, define \(P_j\) by shifting only the potential outcome on side \(t_j\): add \(\eta_h\psi_{j,h}(X)\) if \(t_j=1\), and subtract it if \(t_j=0\). Since \(\psi_{j,h}\) is \(3/h\)-Lipschitz, the perturbation is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz. Also \(h\le h_0/3<1\) gives \(\eta_h<L\). All design properties are unchanged and the residual about the shifted observed conditional mean remains \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\). Hence
\[
 P_j\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}),
 \qquad 1\le j\le M. \tag{67}
\]
Equation (54) puts each center in both side supports. The sign convention makes the causal contrast at \(x_j\) increase by \(\eta_h\), regardless of \(t_j\). For \(k\ne j\), \(d(x_j,x_k)\ge h\), while \(\psi_{k,h}\) is supported on \(B(x_k,h/3)\). Consequently
\[
 \|\tau_{P_j}-\tau_{P_k}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h,
 \qquad 0\le j<k\le M. \tag{68}
\]
If a conditional normal mean changes from zero to \(\mu\), direct substitution into the Gaussian densities gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{Y\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)}
 \log\frac{\phi_\sigma(Y-\mu)}{\phi_\sigma(Y)}
 =\frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2}. \tag{69}
\]
The perturbation is present only when \(X\in\mathcal A_{t_j}\), and the tent is supported in \(B(x_j,h/3)\subseteq B(x_j,h)\). Thus independent sampling, (69), and \(q_{t_j,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}
 \int_{\mathcal A_{t_j}}\psi_{j,h}(z)^2\,dP_X(z)\\
 &\le\frac{nL^2h^2u}{288\sigma^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{70}
\]
If \(M\ge1\) and \(nL^2h^2u/\sigma^2\le a\), retain one alternative and decrease \(a\), if needed, so that (70) is at most \(1/8\). The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with \(2\delta=\eta_h\), gives risk at least a numerical multiple of \(Lh\). If \(M\ge2\) and \(nL^2h^2u/\sigma^2\le a\log(M+1)\), use
\[
 \frac{\log(M+1)}{\log M}\le\frac{\log3}{\log2}
\]
and choose \(a\) smaller by this fixed factor. Then the average divergence in (70) is below the multiple-law threshold times \(\log M\). Equations (68) and the multiple-law clause give
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}\ge cLh. \tag{71}
\]
This proves both alternatives in the lower display.

For a sequence of nonempty classes, suppose there are \(h_n\downarrow0\), with \(h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\), such that
\[
 \frac{nm_n(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{72}
\]
The eligibility condition (56) then holds eventually, while \(Lh_n\to0\) and the square-root term in (64) tends to zero. The upper display proves uniform consistency. Conversely, let \(F_n\) be the set of radii satisfying either information condition in the lower display. If \(\sup F_n\not\to0\), then along a subsequence one can choose feasible \(h_n\) bounded below by a positive constant, taking a value above half the supremum when it is not attained. Equation (71) then bounds minimax risk away from zero. Thus uniform consistency requires \(\sup F_n\to0\).

For the fixed exponential modulus, \(\mathrm{prop:exponential\text{-}horn\text{-}consistency}\) gives
\[
 m_\alpha(h)\asymp h^{\alpha+1}e^{-h^{-\alpha}}.
\]
Set \(s_n=\{(\log n)/2\}^{-1/\alpha}\). Then
\[
 \frac{nm_\alpha(s_n)}{\log(e+n/s_n)}
 \asymp\frac{n^{1/2}s_n^{\alpha+1}}{\log n}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{73}
\]
Because \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n\to\infty\), for all large \(n\) it can be rounded upward to \(h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) so that \(s_n\le h_n<2s_n\) and \(2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\). Monotonicity of \(m_\alpha\) and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le\log(e+n/s_n)\) preserve (73).

For polynomial profiles, put \(u=C_mh^\kappa\). If \(M\asymp1\), the first information condition holds at \(h\asymp n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\). If \(M\asymp h^{-1}\), then \(\log(M+1)\asymp\log(1/h)\), and the second condition holds at
\[
 h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
 \qquad nh^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log(1/h).
\]
These lower rates match the corresponding achievability conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).

Finally, the generic displays can differ when the declared lower packing profile is slack. For the circular pervasive witness of \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), take \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) and declare \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) for \(u<C_mh^\kappa\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=1\) for \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\). This legal declaration exposes only \(M=1\) in (71), while the upper proof covers a global net of order \(h^{-1}\). Hence the arbitrary-profile result is exactly the claimed two-sided bracket, with equality of rates asserted only in the polynomial isolated and pervasive regimes already proved.
