We first prove simultaneous coverage.  Fix a member \(P\) of the triangular union.  By \(\mathrm{def:triangular\text{-}profile\text{-}union}\), it belongs to some \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{base}}\), so the exact Gaussian, Lipschitz, bounded-trace, and positive polynomial lower-mass member conditions all hold.  The complement condition on the assignment regions implies that \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) exactly when \(T_i=t\).  Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\); the counts and selected bandwidths are then fixed.

For \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<r\le h_0\), polynomial lower mass gives
\[
 P_X\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X,x)\le r\}\ge c_mr^\kappa>0. \tag{10}
\]
Therefore \(x\) belongs to the support of the restricted side measure.  Letting a support point \(z'\) tend to \(x\) in the Lipschitz inequality and using the definition of the trace gives
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x) \tag{11}
\]
for every relevant side-support point \(z\) in the \(h_0\)-tube.  If \(N=N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))>0\), every observation in the selected average has distance at most \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\le h_0\) from \(x\).  Averaging (11) therefore yields
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{12}
\]
If \(N=0\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\) makes the side fit zero, and the bounded-trace member condition bounds its error by \(L\).

By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), the number of nonempty averaging sets is at most
\[
 M_n=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj.
\]
Apply that lemma with \(z=\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}\).  Since \(0<\gamma<1\), its conditional union bound shows that, with conditional probability at least \(1-\gamma\), every nonempty candidate set satisfies
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N}. \tag{13}
\]
Equations (12)--(13), together with the zero-count case, bound each side error by \(B_{t,n}(x)\), simultaneously over \(x\) and \(t\).  The triangle inequality then puts \(\tau_P(x)\) in \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) for every \(x\), conditionally with probability at least \(1-\gamma\).  Integrating over the scores proves the finite-sample guarantee uniformly over the union.  The grid is finite, all counts and fits are Borel functions of the data and \(x\), and the numerical tie-break in \(\widehat h_{t,n}\) is deterministic; hence the displayed interval is data measurable.

We next prove the pervasive expected-width rate directly.  Put
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), choose \(b\in\mathcal H_n\) such that \(2r\le b<4r\); then \(b/2\in\mathcal H_n\) and \(b<h_0\).  Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_J\) be a maximal \(b/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  It is a \(b/2\)-net, and the global boundary-entropy member condition gives
\[
 J\le 2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}. \tag{14}
\]
For every \(j,t\), independent sampling and polynomial lower mass make \(N_{t,n}(z_j,b/2)\) binomial with mean at least
\[
 \mu_b=nc_m(b/2)^\kappa. \tag{15}
\]
For completeness, if \(N\) is binomial with mean \(\mu=nq\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) gives
\[
 \Pr\{N\le\mu/2\}
 \le 2^{\mu/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-\mu/8}, \tag{16}
\]
because \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) and \((\log2)/2-1/2<-1/8\).  A union bound over (14) shows that the event
\[
 E_b=\bigcap_{j,t}\{N_{t,n}(z_j,b/2)\ge\mu_b/2\}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(E_b^c)
 \le 4C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}e^{-\mu_b/8}. \tag{17}
\]
If \(x\in\mathcal B\), select \(z_j\) with \(d(x,z_j)\le b/2\).  Then \(B(z_j,b/2)\subseteq B(x,b)\), so on \(E_b\),
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,b)\ge\mu_b/2. \tag{18}
\]
Uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), the quantity \(\mu_b\) grows faster than \(\log(e+n/b)\); indeed, using \(b\ge2r\),
\[
 \mu_b\ge c n^{2/(\kappa+2)}(\log n)^{\kappa/(\kappa+2)}. \tag{19}
\]
Thus (18) exceeds \(8\log(e+n/b)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and \(b\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(E_b\).

For an admissible \(u\), write
\[
 Q_{t,x}(u)=Lu+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/u)}{N_{t,n}(x,u)}}.
\]
The minimizing definition of \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\), (18), and \(b\asymp r\) give on \(E_b\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_{t,x}(\widehat h_{t,n}(x))
 &\le Q_{t,x}(b)\\
 &\le Lb+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(e+n/b)}{\mu_b}}
 \le Cr. \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality follows from \(nb^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n\), with constants uniform over the declared compact exponent range.  Moreover,
\[
 M_n\le Cn^3(1+\log n),
 \qquad
 \log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))\ge\log(e+n), \tag{21}
\]
because \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\le h_0<1\).  Since the selected bandwidth is admissible on \(E_b\), its count is positive.  Equations (20)--(21) imply
\[
 \sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\le Cr \quad\text{on }E_b. \tag{22}
\]
On \(E_b^c\), the definition of \(B_{t,n}\), the bounds \(\widehat h_{t,n}\le h_0<1\) and \(N\ge1\) in its positive-count branch, and (21) give the deterministic envelope
\[
 \sup_{x,t}B_{t,n}(x)\le C(1+\sqrt{\log n}). \tag{23}
\]
The interval construction gives \(W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x)\).  Hence (17), (19), and (22)--(23) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
 &\le Cr+C(1+\sqrt{\log n})b^{-1}e^{-\mu_b/8}\\
 &\le Cr
 =C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)} \tag{24}
\end{aligned}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The exponential term is \(o(r)\) by (19).  This proves the asserted width rate, including every fallback configuration.

Finally fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and two laws \(P_0,P_1\) in a class over which the band has simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\).  Let
\[
 A_j=\{\tau_{P_j}(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\},\qquad j=0,1.
\]
Writing \(P_j^{\otimes n}\) for the induced full observed-data experiment, honesty gives \(P_j^{\otimes n}(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\).  The definition of total variation gives
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 \ge P_1^{\otimes n}(A_1)-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{25}
\]
The union bound for complements and (25) therefore imply
\[
 P_0^{\otimes n}(A_0\cap A_1)
 \ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{26}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values, so
\[
 W_n(x)\ge\frac12|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|. \tag{27}
\]
Multiplying (27) by the positive part of (26) and taking expectation proves the displayed two-law obstruction.

The distance in (25) is the total variation of the full observed experiments.  Score extraction is a measurable map, so data processing gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{0,X}^{\otimes n},P_{1,X}^{\otimes n})
 \le\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}), \tag{28}
\]
which is the wrong direction for replacing the full distance by the score distance in the lower bound.  Also, (13) uses \(\log M_n\asymp\log n\), while the local benchmark can have bounded \(\log\{eK_P(x,h)/\gamma\}\).  Thus the proved global band and full-law obstruction do not establish either the local-oracle width ratio or the requested score-law necessary-and-sufficient criterion, exactly as stated.
