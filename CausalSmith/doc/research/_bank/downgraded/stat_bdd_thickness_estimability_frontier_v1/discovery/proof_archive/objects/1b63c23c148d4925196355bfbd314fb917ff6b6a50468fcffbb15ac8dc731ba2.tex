Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and define the finite Borel measure
\[
\mu_t(E):=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t).
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), the law \(P\) satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\). Let \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\). With \(h:=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\), positivity of \(c_m\), \(h_0\), and \(h\), together with \(\kappa>2\), gives
\[
\mu_t\bigl(\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\bigr)
\ge P\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X,x)\le h\}
=q_{t,P}(x,h)
\ge c_m h^{\kappa}>0.
\]
The defining neighborhood characterization of the support of a Borel measure therefore yields
\[
x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)=\mathcal X_{t,P}.
\]
This proves the asserted two-sided support condition at every boundary point.

Put
\[
U_{t,P}:=\left\{z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}:\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x)<h_0\right\}.
\]
Existence of the claimed representative on \(U_{t,P}\) follows from \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\), because \(g_{t,P}\) is, by its definition, a Borel representative of the observed conditional-regression class and the displayed assumption gives
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(z')|\le Ld(z,z'),\qquad z,z'\in U_{t,P}.
\]
For uniqueness, let \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) be two representatives of the restricted equivalence class satisfying this inequality. Then \(f_1=f_2\), \(\mu_t\)-almost everywhere on \(U_{t,P}\). Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(\Delta:=|f_1(z)-f_2(z)|>0\) at some \(z\in U_{t,P}\). The distance-to-set map is one-Lipschitz, so one may choose
\[
0<r<\min\left\{h_0-\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x),\frac{\Delta}{4L}\right\}
\]
such that \(\{z'\in\mathcal X_{t,P}:d(z',z)<r\}\subseteq U_{t,P}\). For every such \(z'\), the triangle inequality and the two Lipschitz bounds imply
\[
|f_1(z')-f_2(z')|
\ge \Delta-|f_1(z')-f_1(z)|-|f_2(z')-f_2(z)|
>\Delta-2Lr
>\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\]
Because \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}=\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\), the relative ball in this display has positive \(\mu_t\)-measure. This contradicts \(f_1=f_2\), \(\mu_t\)-almost everywhere on \(U_{t,P}\). Hence the representative on \(U_{t,P}\) is unique.

Now fix \(x\in\mathcal B\). The first part gives \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and \(x\in U_{t,P}\) because its distance to \(\mathcal B\) is zero. Whenever \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) and \(d(z,x)<h_0\), one has \(z\in U_{t,P}\). Thus \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}.
\]
By the definition of \(\theta_{t,P}\), this proves
\[
\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x).
\]
In particular, the limit exists and is unchanged by any modification of a regression representative away from \(U_{t,P}\).

Suppose next that \(P\) and \(P'\) induce the same law of \(O=(Y,X)\) under the same known assignment geometry. The definition \(T=\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_1\}\) makes \(T\) a known measurable function of \(X\), so the two laws induce the same observed law of \((Y,X,T)\). They consequently induce the same \(P_X\), the same measures \(\mu_t\), the same supports \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and the same observed conditional-regression equivalence classes. The uniqueness just proved makes the near-boundary representatives identical. Their boundary limits are therefore identical, and the definition \(\tau_P=\theta_{1,P}-\theta_{0,P}\) then gives \(\tau_P=\tau_{P'}\). This proves the observed-law-functional assertion without defining a conditional expectation at a \(P_X\)-null boundary point.

Finally, set
\[
S_t:=\mathcal A_t\cap\left\{z:\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\right\}.
\]
For each \(z\in U_{t,P}\), compactness of \(\mathcal B\) supplies \(x_z\in\mathcal B\) with \(d(z,x_z)=\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x)<h_0\). The support conclusion already proved, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) give
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)|\le |g_{t,P}(x_z)|+Ld(z,x_z)
=|\theta_{t,P}(x_z)|+Ld(z,x_z)
\le L(1+h_0).
\]
Together with \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), this proves integrability of \(Y\) on each assigned near-boundary set, since
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[|Y|\mathbf 1\{X\in S_t\cap U_{t,P}\}\right]
\le L(1+h_0)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}<\infty.
\]
On \(\{X\in S_t\}\), \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) gives \(T=t\), while \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) gives \(Y=Y(t)\). Therefore, for every bounded Borel function \(\varphi\) supported in \(S_t\cap U_{t,P}\), the defining identities for the observed conditional-regression representative yield
\[
\int \varphi(z)g_{t,P}(z)\,dP_X(z)
=\mathbb E_P\!\left[\varphi(X)Y\right]
=\mathbb E_P\!\left[\varphi(X)Y(t)\right]
\]
If \(P_X(S_t\cap U_{t,P})>0\), division of the last display by this positive mass shows that \(g_{t,P}\) is the Radon--Nikodym conditional mean of \(Y(t)\) with respect to \(X\) under the probability law normalized to \(\{X\in S_t\cap U_{t,P}\}\). If the mass is zero, the claimed almost-everywhere agreement on that set is vacuous. This is precisely the asserted local compatibility and does not require \(Y(t)\) to be integrable off that set. If \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), then \(S_t=\mathcal A_t\) for both \(t=0\) and \(t=1\), proving the final specialization.
