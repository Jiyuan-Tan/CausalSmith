The partition condition in the corrected statement and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\), so the side fit has regression \(g_{t,P}\). The formula in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\) contains only the observed counts, \(L\), \(\sigma\), \(n\), and the known geometry; neither \(\kappa\) nor a score density occurs. Let
\[
 r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
\]
and choose a grid point \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\), which exists for all sufficiently large \(n\). Take a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net. Every \(x\) is within \(h_*/2\) of a net point \(z\), and \(B(z,h_*/2)\subset B(x,h_*)\). Polynomial lower mass therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_PN_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge nc_m(h_*/2)^\kappa.
\]
There are at most \(C/h_*\) net points. The binomial Chernoff bound and a union bound imply, uniformly in the pervasive class,
\[
 P(G_n^c)\le Ch_*^{-1}\exp\{-c n c_mh_*^\kappa\},
\]
where on \(G_n\), simultaneously for every \(x,t\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c n c_mh_*^\kappa.
\]
Since \(nh_*^\kappa/\log(e+n/h_*)\to\infty\) uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), the candidate \(h_*\) is admissible on \(G_n\). By the defining minimization,
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Lh_*+C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_*)}{n c_mh_*^\kappa}}
 \le Cr.
\]

Conditionally on the scores, the selected bandwidths are deterministic. Whenever their counts are positive, Lipschitz smoothness gives bias at most \(L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\). By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), the number of possible nonempty averaging sets over all centers, sides, and grid scales is polynomial in \(n\). Since \(h\le h_0<1\), \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log(en)\), and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[\left.
 \sup_{x,t,h}\frac{|\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,h}}\varepsilon_i|}
 {N_{t,n}(x,h)\vee1}\right|X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sup_{x,t,h:N>0}
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{N_{t,n}(x,h)}}.
\]
Combining this with the preceding objective inequality yields conditional expected sup error at most \(Cr\) on \(G_n\). On \(G_n^c\), bounded traces and Lipschitz smoothness bound all conditional means used within distance \(h_0\) by \(2L\), while the same finite-pattern lemma bounds the conditional expected Gaussian maximum by \(C\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\). Thus the exceptional contribution is at most
\[
 C(L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n})h_*^{-1}e^{-c n c_mh_*^\kappa}=o(r).
\]
This proves the stated expected-risk upper bound, including the fallback contribution. The lower bound is the pervasive lower bound already proved in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).

It remains to record the advertised fallback probability. Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) be a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net. If \(d(x,z_j)\le h_0/2\), then \(B(z_j,h_0/2)\subset B(x,h_0)\), so its side count is a lower bound for \(N_{t,n}(x,h_0)\). There are at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\) centers, and every corresponding mean is at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^\kappa\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), this is at least twice \(8\log(e+n/h_0)\). Chernoff and the union bound therefore give
\[
 \sup_P P\{\exists x,t:\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)=\varnothing\}
 \le C\exp\{-c n c_m(h_0/2)^\kappa\}
 \le Cn^3\exp\{-c' n c_mh_0^\kappa\},
\]
where \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\) makes \(c'>0\) uniform in \(\kappa\). This is the required operational bound.
