The map \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) is positive and nondecreasing.  The constants from \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), together with \(q_{t,P}\le1\), ensure \(m(h)\le1\), so it is well typed.  The declared \(\mathsf J_-\) is \(\mathbb N_0\)-valued and nondecreasing in \(u\).

For \(P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\), the base-class definition supplies every common member property of \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\), and polynomial lower mass gives \(q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge m(h)\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then \(\mathsf J_-(h,u)=0\).  If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), monotonicity of the thin-site set in \(u\) and pervasive thinning give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge c_Jh^{-1}>0,
\]
and integrality implies \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_-(h,u)\).  Hence \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\).  The identical argument with the isolated thin-site condition gives \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\), proving the displayed union inclusion.  Circular witness membership makes all three classes nonempty.

Minimax risk is monotone under class inclusion, because for every estimator the supremum over a larger class is no smaller, and taking the infimum preserves the inequality.  Therefore \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\) gives
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\}\ge R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{50}
\]
For the reverse inequality, put \(s_n=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\) and choose \(h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(s_n\le h_n<2s_n\) and \(2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\).  Then
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{51}
\]
Thus the explicit clipped estimator in \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\), evaluated at \(h_n\), satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)}\mathbb E_P\sup_x|\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le C\left\{s_n+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}=Cs_n. \tag{52}
\]
It is admissible in the infimum defining \(R_n\), so (50)--(52) prove the matched pervasive rate.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Although the declaration guarantees only one thin site at the threshold, its class still contains the pervasive subclass and consequently cannot have the isolated rate.  The absent upper packing restriction is exactly what permits this counterexample, proving the no-go conclusion.
