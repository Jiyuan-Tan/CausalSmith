For a law \(P\), define the finite one-sided score measure
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(E):=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t),\qquad E\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2).
\]
We first record the boundary-support fact used in every bias calculation.  If \(P\) belongs to either class in the theorem, \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(r>0\), set \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\).  The restrictions \(h_0>0\), \(c_m>0\), and \(\kappa>2\), together with \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\), give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}
 &\ge P\{X\in\mathcal A_t,d(X,x)\le s\}\\
 &=q_{t,P}(x,s)\\
 &\ge c_m s^\kappa>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus every open neighborhood of \(x\) has positive \(\mu_{t,P}\)-mass, so the definition of support yields \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  The repaired definition of \(g_{t,P}\) makes it the observed-outcome regression representative under \(\mu_{t,P}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}.
\]
Consequently, by the definition of the trace,
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{1}
\]
Under the partition condition \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) implies \(X\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T=t\).  Hence every side average below has conditional observed-outcome mean \(g_{t,P}(X)\).  The causal compatibility statement is distinct: with
\[
 S_t:=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\},
\]
consistency identifies this observed regression with the \(Y(t)\) regression only on \(S_t\); the partition makes \(S_t=\mathcal A_t\).  No counterfactual regression off \(S_t\) is used in the statistical argument.

We prove the two lower bounds by subexperiments that remain inside the respective classes.  Begin with any member of the nonempty class under consideration and retain its score marginal and assignment geometry.  On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define a baseline law \(Q_0\) by
\[
 Y(s)=\sigma Z_s,\qquad s\in\{0,1\},
\]
and induce \(T\) and \(Y\) using \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\).  Its observed-side regressions and traces vanish.  All mass, boundary-entropy, and thin-site conditions are unchanged because they depend only on the retained score law and geometry; the zero regressions satisfy the Lipschitz and trace envelopes, and the observed error is \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\).  Thus \(Q_0\) is in the same class.

For the isolated class, put
\[
 h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
where \(a_0\in(0,1)\) will be chosen below.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  The condition \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) and the definition of \(\mathsf J_P\) supply \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) such that
\[
 a_P(x_h,h)\le C_mh^\kappa.
\]
Choose \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) attaining the minimum in \(a_P(x_h,h)\), and define
\[
 \psi_h(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_h)}h\right)_+,
 \qquad \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12}.
\]
The map \(\eta_h\psi_h\) is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, is bounded by \(\eta_h\le L\), and vanishes outside \(B(x_h,h/3)\).  Define \(Q_1\) by setting, conditionally on \(X\),
\[
 (Y(1),Y(0))=
 \begin{cases}
 (\eta_h\psi_h(X)+\sigma Z_1,\ \sigma Z_0),&t_h=1,\\
 (\sigma Z_1,\ -\eta_h\psi_h(X)+\sigma Z_0),&t_h=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The score law and every design condition remain unchanged.  The displayed Lipschitz and boundedness calculations verify the response-side class conditions, and the conditional noise remains Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2\).  Hence \(Q_1\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  Equation (1) and \(\psi_h(x_h)=1\) give
\[
 \|\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\|_\infty\ge
 |\tau_{Q_1}(x_h)-\tau_{Q_0}(x_h)|=\eta_h. \tag{2}
\]
Direct integration of the Gaussian log-density ratio gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2),\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\}
 =\frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
The score marginal is common to \(Q_0,Q_1\), so conditioning on \(X\), using the support of the tent, and then using the selected-side mass bound yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=\frac1{2\sigma^2}\int \eta_h^2\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,P}(z)\\
 &\le\frac{\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}q_{t_h,P}(x_h,h)\\
 &\le\frac{C_mL^2}{288\sigma^2}h^{\kappa+2}. \tag{3}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Independence in \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) makes the product log likelihood a sum, whence
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =n\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 \le C a_0^{\kappa+2}.
\]
Choose \(a_0\) so that the last display is at most \(1/8\).  Applying the two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) to the sup metric, with \(2\delta=\eta_h\), and using (2), gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})
 \ge c\eta_h
 \ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{4}
\]

For the pervasive class, take
\[
 h=a_0\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\), the set \(\{x:a_P(x,h)\le C_mh^\kappa\}\) contains an \(h\)-separated collection \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) with
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1}.
\]
Finiteness follows also from the upper packing bound in \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\).  For each \(j\), choose \(t_j\) attaining \(a_P(x_j,h)\), put
\[
 \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+,
 \qquad \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12},
\]
and define \(Q_j\) by shifting side \(1\) upward by \(\eta_h\psi_{j,h}\) if \(t_j=1\), or shifting side \(0\) downward by that amount if \(t_j=0\).  The membership argument used for \(Q_1\) applies verbatim, so \(Q_j\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  Since \(d(x_j,x_k)\ge h>h/3\),
\[
 \tau_{Q_j}(x_j)=\eta_h,
 \qquad \tau_{Q_k}(x_j)=0\quad(k\ne j).
\]
Thus the target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(\eta_h\) in sup norm.  Repeating (3) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cn h^{\kappa+2}
 =C a_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{5}
\]
For fixed \(\kappa\), the lower bound on \(M\) and the displayed choice of \(h\) imply \(\log M\ge c_\kappa\log n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Choosing \(a_0\) sufficiently small makes the right side of (5) at most \(a\log M\), where \(a<1/8\) is the constant in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\).  Its multiple-law clause, again with \(2\delta=\eta_h\), gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\eta_h
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{6}
\]

We next construct upper-bound estimators that are measurable in the sample and known geometry.  Fix \(0<b\le h_0/2\), and choose a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated set \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) in \(\mathcal B\).  Compactness makes it a \(b/2\)-net, and \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\).  Let \(j(x)\) be the least index satisfying \(d(x,z_{j(x)})\le b/2\), and define
\[
 K_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,d(X_i,z_j)\le b\},
\]
\[
 \overline g_{t,j}
 =\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,d(X_i,z_j)\le b\}}
 {K_{t,j}\vee1},
 \qquad
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)=\overline g_{1,j(x)}-\overline g_{0,j(x)}.
\]
This is one data-driven estimator, not a law-indexed oracle.  On \(\{K_{t,j(x)}>0\}\), the repaired observed-regression definition, (1), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j(x)}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 &\le L\max_{i:X_i\in B(z_{j(x)},b)}d(X_i,x)\\
 &\le \frac{3Lb}{2}. \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]

We use an elementary count inequality.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1+y\le e^y\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 &\le 2^{nq/2}\mathbb E(2^{-N})\\
 &=2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\\
 &\le\exp\{nq((\log2)/2-1/2)\}\\
 &\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{8}
 \end{aligned}
\]

For the pervasive upper bound, set \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  Eventually \(b\le h_0/2\).  Polynomial lower mass gives \(\mathbb E_PK_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^\kappa\).  By (8), a union bound, and the net cardinality, there is an event \(H_b\) such that every \(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^\kappa/2\) on \(H_b\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-c n c_mb^\kappa}. \tag{9}
\]
Conditionally on the scores, \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) make the centered side averages Gaussian.  A union bound over the \(2M\) averages gives a tail of the form \(4M\exp\{-u^2nc_mb^\kappa/(4\sigma^2)\}\); integrating that tail yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n,H_b\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e/b)}{nc_mb^\kappa}}. \tag{10}
\]
Because \(nb^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) and \(\log(e/b)\le C_\kappa\log n\), (10) is at most \(C_\kappa b\).  Combining (7) for the two sides with (10) bounds the conditional expected sup loss by \(C_\kappa b\) on \(H_b\).

On \(H_b^c\), every nonempty average has conditional mean bounded by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\), using (1), \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), and the Lipschitz inequality; an empty average is zero.  A Gaussian union bound with variance at most \(\sigma^2\) gives the unconditional conditional bound \(C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}\) for the sup loss.  Multiplying by (9),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\|_\infty
 &\le C_\kappa b
 +C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}b^{-1}e^{-c n c_mb^\kappa}\\
 &\le C_\kappa b, \tag{11}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality holds for all sufficiently large \(n\), since \(nb^\kappa=(\log n)/b^2\to\infty\).  The definition of \(R_n\) and (11) prove \(R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\le C_\kappa r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).

For the isolated upper bound, set \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}=n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\), so
\[
 nb^\kappa=b^{-2}. \tag{12}
\]
Fix a side \(t\), choose \(D\ge C_m\), and set \(u_\ell=\min\{1,D2^\ell b^\kappa\}\).  Group the net centers by \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\) and, for \(\ell\ge1\), by \(u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell\), stopping once \(u_\ell=1\).  A center in group \(\ell\) satisfies
\[
 a_P(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
The centers are \(b/2\)-separated.  Applying \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\) at radius \(b/2\), and using the global entropy bound for the terminal group if needed, gives
\[
 M_\ell\le C_\kappa\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{13}
\]
Indeed, before the terminal group the nonconstant term in the profile bound is
\[
 (b/2)^{-1}\left\{\frac{D2^\ell b^\kappa}{(b/2)^2}\right\}^{1/(\kappa-2)}
 \le C_\kappa2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)},
\]
and the global bound is smaller than the right side of (13) at the terminal index.

In group zero, polynomial lower mass gives \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\ge c_mb^\kappa\); in every later group, \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)>u_{\ell-1}\).  Equations (8), (12), and (13), summed over the two sides, yield an event \(H_b\) on which every count is at least half its mean and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 &\le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-c b^{-2}2^\ell}\\
 &\le C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa/b^2}. \tag{14}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Conditionally on the scores and \(H_b\), the same grouping and a Gaussian union bound give, for all sufficiently large fixed \(z\),
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|>zb
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-c z^22^\ell}.
\]
The series is bounded by \(C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa z^2}\); integration in \(z\) therefore bounds the conditional expected stochastic maximum by \(C_\kappa b\).  Equation (7) contributes at most \(3Lb\) for the contrast.  On \(H_b^c\), the same bounded-mean and Gaussian-maximal calculation as above gives conditional expected loss at most \(C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}\), whose product with (14) is \(o(b)\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 \mathbb E_P\|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\|_\infty
 \le C_\kappa b,
\]
and the definition of minimax risk proves \(R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\le C_\kappa r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  Together with (4), (6), and (11), this proves both matched rates.

Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) supplies the explicit nonempty circular specification promised in the statement, and \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) verifies a concrete legal instance of the lower families.  Both classes impose the same scalar lower modulus \(c_mh^\kappa\), but their rates differ by \((\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\).  Therefore the scalar modulus alone cannot determine the expected-sup frontier; the declared thin-site multiplicity separates the two experiments.
