For a law \(P\), define the finite one-sided score measure \(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\).  If \(P\) is in either stated class, \(x\in\mathcal B\), and \(r>0\), set \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\).  The polynomial lower-mass member property gives
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^\kappa>0. \tag{6}
\]
Thus every boundary point lies in \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  Lipschitz continuity of the distinguished regression representative and the definition of the trace imply
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x),\qquad t\in\{0,1\},\ x\in\mathcal B. \tag{7}
\]
The partition \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\) and sharp assignment make \(X\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T=t\), so the side averages used below estimate the observed-law functional in (7).  Consistency supplies its causal interpretation as \(\tau_P\).

We first prove the lower bounds.  Start from any member of the nonempty class at issue, retain its score law and geometry, take independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), and set \(Y(s)=\sigma Z_s\).  Call the resulting law \(Q_0\).  It remains in the same class: every design condition is unchanged, the side regressions and traces are zero, and the conditional residual kernel is centered Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2\), hence satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\).

For the isolated class set \(h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\).  For large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  The isolated thin-site property supplies \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,Q_0}(x_h,h)=a_{Q_0}(x_h,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{8}
\]
Let \(\psi_h(z)=(1-3d(z,x_h)/h)_+\) and \(\eta_h=Lh/12\).  If \(t_h=1\), add \(\eta_h\psi_h(X)\) to \(Y(1)\); if \(t_h=0\), subtract it from \(Y(0)\); leave the other potential outcome unchanged.  Since \(\eta_h\psi_h\) is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, is bounded by \(L\), and changes neither the score law nor the centered Gaussian residual kernel, the resulting law \(Q_1\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  By (7),
\[
 \|\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h. \tag{9}
\]
Direct Gaussian likelihood-ratio integration and (8) give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}\int\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,Q_0}(z)
 \le \frac{nC_mL^2h^{\kappa+2}}{288\sigma^2}. \tag{10}
\]
Choose \(a_0>0\) so the last expression is at most \(1/8\).  The two-law clause of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with supremum distance and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}=c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{11}
\]

For the pervasive class set \(h=a_0(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\).  Its thin-site property supplies an \(h\)-separated set \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) with
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},\qquad a_{Q_0}(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa. \tag{12}
\]
At each \(x_j\), choose a side attaining the minimum and perturb that side by a radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) tent, with the sign chosen to increase the contrast.  Exactly as above, the resulting \(Q_j\) lie in \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  Their target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(Lh/12\), because every other tent vanishes at \(x_j\).  The calculation in (10) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}\operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cnh^{\kappa+2}=Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{13}
\]
Equation (12) implies \(\log M\ge c_\kappa\log n\) eventually.  Taking \(a_0\) sufficiently small makes (13) at most \(a\log M\), where \(a<1/8\) is the constant in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\).  Its multiple-law clause yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge c(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}=c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{14}
\]
The exact centered-Gaussian circular experiments of \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) give explicit witnesses to the same information budgets.

For the upper bounds, let \(s_n\) denote the rate claimed for the class at issue.  Choose \(b\in\mathcal H_n\) so that, for all large \(n\),
\[
 s_n\le b<2s_n,\qquad b/2\in\mathcal H_n,\qquad b\le h_0/2. \tag{15}
\]
Take a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated boundary net \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\).  Global boundary entropy gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\).  Let \(j(x)\) be the least net index within \(b/2\) of \(x\), define the radius-\(b\) side counts \(K_{t,j}\) and means \(\bar g_{t,j}\), and use the single data-driven estimator
\[
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)=\Pi_{[-2L,2L]}\{\bar g_{1,j(x)}-\bar g_{0,j(x)}\}. \tag{16}
\]
It is measurable in the sample and known geometry.  Projection cannot increase the error because \(|\tau_P(x)|\le2L\).  On \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), every included score lies within \(3b/2\) of \(x\), so (7) and Lipschitz smoothness give conditional bias at most \(3Lb/2\).

If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(2^{-N}\) and \(1-y\le e^{-y}\) give
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)\le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{17}
\]
For the pervasive rate, polynomial lower mass, (17), and a union bound over the net and two sides give an event \(H_b\) on which all \(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^\kappa/2\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-cnb^\kappa}. \tag{18}
\]
Conditional on the scores, independent sampling factorizes the residual kernels.  Their assumed conditional MGFs imply that every normalized residual sum is sub-Gaussian with proxy one.  Equivalently, applying \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) to the finite disk-pattern family gives conditional expected maximum at most \(C\sqrt{\log(en)}\).  Hence on \(H_b\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \max_{t,j}|\bar g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\bar g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}{nc_mb^\kappa}}\le C_\kappa b, \tag{19}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(nb^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n\).  The clipped loss is at most \(4L\) on \(H_b^c\); (18)--(19) and the bias bound prove the pervasive upper rate.

For the isolated rate, \(nb^\kappa\asymp b^{-2}\).  Fix a side, take a fixed \(D\ge\max\{C_m,2^\kappa C_m\}\), put \(u_\ell=\min\{1,D2^\ell b^\kappa\}\), and group net centers by \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\) or \(u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell\).  Such centers obey \(a_P(z_j,b/2)\le u_\ell\).  They are \(b/2\)-separated, so the isolated upper profile gives
\[
 M_\ell\le C_\kappa\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{20}
\]
In group zero the mean count is at least \(cb^{-2}\), and in group \(\ell\ge1\) it is at least \(c2^\ell b^{-2}\).  Summing (17) with (20) over groups and sides produces an event \(H_b\) on which every group-\(\ell\) count is at least \(c2^\ell b^{-2}\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-c2^\ell/b^2}
 \le C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa/b^2}. \tag{21}
\]
The same conditional MGF factorization gives, for \(z>0\),
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{
 \max_{t,j}|\bar g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\bar g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|>zb
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cz^22^\ell}. \tag{22}
\]
For all sufficiently large fixed \(z\), the series is at most \(C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa z^2}\).  Integrating this tail shows that the conditional expected stochastic maximum on \(H_b\) is at most \(C_\kappa b\).  The bias bound, (21), and the \(4L\) clipped-loss bound off \(H_b\) prove the isolated upper rate.

Thus the estimator (16) witnesses both minimax upper bounds, while (11) and (14) prove the matching lower bounds.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The common scalar lower modulus therefore cannot determine expected-sup risk; the declared thin-site multiplicity separates the two frontiers.
