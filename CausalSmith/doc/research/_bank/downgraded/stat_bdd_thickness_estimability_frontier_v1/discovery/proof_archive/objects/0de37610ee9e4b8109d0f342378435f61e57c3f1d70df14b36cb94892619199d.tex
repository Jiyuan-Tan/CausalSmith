Define \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) and
\[
 \mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=\mathbf 1\{u\ge C_mh^\kappa\}.
\]
Because \(c_m>0\), \(\kappa>2\), and \(h>0\), \(m\) is strictly positive on \((0,h_0]\); it is nondecreasing because \(\kappa>0\). For fixed \(h\), \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,\cdot)\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing. Thus these are well-typed inputs to \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).

The replacement of exact conditional normality by \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), now the centered conditional sub-Gaussian condition, does not alter the upper stochastic step. By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), the identical disk-pattern inequalities follow by multiplying the conditional moment-generating-function bounds under \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\). Conversely, every explicit Gaussian witness remains admissible because a centered \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) residual has moment-generating function \(\exp(\sigma^2\lambda^2/2)\). Hence the established Gaussian-submodel converses and the count-adaptive upper bracket remain available on the broadened classes.

Let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\). By \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), \(P\) has all common member properties in \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\). Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h). \tag{1}
\]
Thus the declared-modulus condition holds.

For fixed \(h\), the set \(\{x\in\mathcal B:a_P(x,h)\le u\}\) is nondecreasing in \(u\). By the definition of \(\mathsf J_P\), its maximal \(h\)-separated cardinality \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) is therefore nondecreasing in \(u\). If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then
\[
 \mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u). \tag{2}
\]
If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), (3) implies
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u). \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1)--(4) and the class definition prove
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{5}
\]

If \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\), the base-class definition again gives (1), while \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge1. \tag{6}
\]
Combining (6) with monotonicity in \(u\), and using (2) below the threshold, proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{7}
\]
The circular membership lemma supplies one law in each subclass, so the class on the right of (5) and (7) is nonempty.

Minimax risk is monotone under class inclusion. Indeed, if \(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\), then for every sample-measurable estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}\mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}\mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]. \tag{8}
\]
Taking the infimum over the same estimator set preserves (8). Apply it to (5), and then use the pervasive lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\). For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}\ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{9}
\]

For the upper bound, put
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Then \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(s_n/(h_0/n)\to\infty\). Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) supplies \(h_n\) with
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,\qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{10}
\]
Since \(m(h_n)=c_mh_n^\kappa\), (10) and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C\log n\) give
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\,\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(h_n\) is eligible in the upper display of \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\). Because \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), (10) also yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Lh_n+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}
 &\le C\left\{s_n+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}\\
 &=Cs_n.
 \end{aligned}\tag{12}
\]
The clipped count-adaptive estimator in that theorem is one sample-measurable estimator admissible in the infimum defining \(R_n\). Evaluating its guarantee at \(h_n\), using (11)--(12), gives
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}\le Cs_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{13}
\]
Equations (9) and (13) prove the matched frontier.

Finally, the definitions of the two rates give
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{14}
\]
Thus a declaration guaranteeing only one thin site does not entail the isolated frontier: the same class contains the pervasive subclass and has pervasive risk. The upper thin-site restriction in \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\), which is absent from \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), is precisely what excludes this counterexample. An upper or intrinsic two-sided profile is therefore necessary.
