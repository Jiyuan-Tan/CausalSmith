All statements below concern the independent product experiment of \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\).  First we check that the displayed declarations are well typed.  Since \(\kappa>2\) and \(c_m>0\), the map \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) is positive and nondecreasing on \((0,h_0]\).  The constants certified by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) satisfy \(c_mh^\kappa\le q_{t,P}(x,h)\le1\) for a circular witness at every admissible \(h\); hence \(m(h)\in(0,1]\).  The declared map \(\mathsf J_-\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing in its second argument.

We next prove the class inclusions.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), \(P\) has all of the common sharp-assignment, consistency, boundary-entropy, Lipschitz, bounded-trace, and conditional sub-Gaussian properties required by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  The polynomial lower-mass condition in that base-class definition gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h). \tag{1}
\]
It remains to verify the declared lower profile.  Directly from the definition of \(\mathsf J_P\), if \(u\le u'\), then
\[
 \{x\in\mathcal B:a_P(x,h)\le u\}
 \subseteq
 \{x\in\mathcal B:a_P(x,h)\le u'\},
\]
so \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\le\mathsf J_P(h,u')\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then \(\mathsf J_-(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u)\).  If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), monotonicity and \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge1
 =\mathsf J_-(h,u). \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) and \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) prove \(P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\).

Now fix \(P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  The same base-class argument proves (1).  For \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\) and the preceding monotonicity give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0.
\]
Because \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), this strict positivity implies \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_-(h,u)\); for \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), the inequality is again trivial.  Thus
\[
 \mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\cup
 \mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq \mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-). \tag{3}
\]
The circular membership lemma also supplies a member of each subclass, so all classes used below are nonempty.

For law classes \(\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal P\), \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) gives, for every sample-measurable estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|.
\]
Taking the infimum over the same estimator set on both sides proves \(R_n(\mathcal P)\ge R_n(\mathcal Q)\).  Apply this fact to the pervasive inclusion in (3), and then apply the pervasive lower bound from \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), whose nonemptiness and circular-geometry premises were just verified.  There is a constant \(c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{4}
\]

For the reverse inequality, set
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Because \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n\to\infty\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) the dyadic grid in \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) contains an element \(h_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, take the smallest dyadic grid point no smaller than \(s_n\); it is neither endpoint of the grid once \(h_0/n<s_n<h_0/2\).  From (5), \(m(h_n)=c_mh_n^\kappa\), and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C_0\log n\) for a fixed \(C_0\) and all large \(n\), we obtain
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge \frac{c_mns_n^\kappa}{C_0\log n}
 =\frac{c_m}{C_0}
 \left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{6}
\]
Consequently (6) eventually implies the count qualification \(nm(h_n)\ge A\log(e+n/h_n)\) required by \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).  Evaluate that theorem's explicit clipped, count-adaptive estimator at the admissible bandwidth \(h_n\).  Equations (5)--(6) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)| \\
 &\quad\le
 C_1\left\{Lh_n+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}\right\} \\
 &\quad\le C_2\left\{s_n+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}
 =C_3s_n, \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality follows from \(s_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n/n\).  This estimator is one of the sample-measurable competitors in \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\); hence (7) gives
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_-)\}
 \le C_3s_n
 =C_3r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{8}
\]
Combining (4) and (8) proves the asserted matched rate.

Finally, the two declared rate sequences satisfy
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}
 {n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{9}
\]
If the one-site lower declaration recovered the isolated frontier, the risk would be bounded above by a constant multiple of \(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\), contradicting (4) and (9).  The explicit witness is the pervasive circular subclass retained in (3).  Thus a lower packing declaration alone cannot distinguish isolated from pervasive thinning; an upper packing restriction or an intrinsic two-sided profile is indispensable.
