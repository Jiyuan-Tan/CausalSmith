The partition and sharp-assignment conditions imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  Condition on the scores, so all selected bandwidths are fixed.  Polynomial lower mass for every member of the triangular union puts each boundary point in each side support, and Lipschitz smoothness gives, whenever the selected count \(N\) is positive,
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{43}
\]
If \(N=0\), the side fit is zero and the bounded-trace assumption bounds its error by \(L\).

By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\), at most
\[
 M_n=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj
\]
nonempty averaging sets can occur.  Under the exact conditional Gaussian assumption, that lemma first computes each residual's conditional moment-generating function as \(\mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda\varepsilon_i}\mid X_i]=e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}\), and conditional independence multiplies these identities to give the normalized-sum bound \(\mathbb E_P[e^{uZ_S}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]\le e^{u^2/2}\).  Its resulting tail inequality, evaluated at \(z=\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}\), implies that, conditionally on the scores, with probability at least \(1-\gamma\), every nonempty candidate set satisfies
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N}. \tag{44}
\]
Equations (43)--(44), and the zero-count bound, show that each side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\), simultaneously over \(x\) and \(t\).  The triangle inequality proves conditional and hence unconditional simultaneous coverage.  All counts and fits are measurable, the grids are finite, and the bandwidth rule has a deterministic tie break, so the displayed band is data measurable.

For the pervasive width assertion, \(\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C\log n\).  The uniform count event and comparison scale in \(\mathrm{thm:pervasive\text{-}adaptive\text{-}frontier}\) show that, outside an event of probability \(Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnh_*^{\kappa}}\), the selected bias plus the standard-error term in (44) is bounded by \(Cr_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), uniformly in \(x,t,P\).  On the exceptional event it is at most \(L+\sigma\sqrt{C\log n}\); its expected contribution is \(o(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})\).  Since the constructed interval has
\[
 W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x),
\]
the asserted expected supremum width follows.

For the obstruction, fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and let \(A_j\) be the event that \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains \(\tau_{P_j}(x)\).  Honesty gives
\[
 P_j(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n,
 \qquad j=0,1.
\]
By the definition of total variation,
\[
 P_0(A_1)\ge P_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
Therefore
\[
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{45}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values, so its half-width is at least half their separation.  Multiplying this deterministic implication by the positive part of (45) and taking \(P_0\)-expectations proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)\ge\frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
 \left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+.
\]
This uses the total variation of the full observed experiments.  A score marginal is only a measurable projection, so its total variation cannot furnish the needed upper bound on the full distance without an additional same-class outcome construction.  Likewise, the global calibration in (44) has \(\log M_n\asymp\log n\), whereas the local benchmark may have bounded \(\log\{eK_P(x,h)/\gamma\}\).  The proved statements therefore establish the advertised global band and two-law obstruction, but not the explicitly disclaimed local-ratio or score-only equivalence.
