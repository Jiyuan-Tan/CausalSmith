For a disk with center \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\), membership of a fixed point \(x_i\) is equivalent to
\[
 2c^\top x_i+s\ge\lVert x_i\rVert_2^2,
 \qquad s=r^2-\lVert c\rVert_2^2.
\]
Thus every disk trace is a sign pattern cut out by \(n\) affine planes in the three-dimensional parameter \((c_1,c_2,s)\). A trace realized by a closed disk can be made strict by increasing its radius by less than the positive minimum distance to the finitely many excluded points, so boundary equalities create no additional traces. Let \(A(n,v)\) be the largest number of open cells made by \(n\) affine hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^v\). Adding the last hyperplane preserves every old cell and splits at most \(A(n-1,v-1)\) of them, because the induced arrangement on that hyperplane has dimension \(v-1\). Hence
\[
 A(n,v)\le A(n-1,v)+A(n-1,v-1),
 \qquad A(0,v)=A(n,0)=1.
\]
Induction gives \(A(n,3)\le\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj\). Restricting to the parameter subset corresponding to genuine disks, fixing a radius, or intersecting every range with the fixed indices satisfying \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) cannot increase this count. Multiplication by the two sides and by \(|\mathcal H_n|\) proves the cardinality assertion.

By independent sampling and the conditional Gaussian-error assumption, conditional on the scores the variables \(\varepsilon_i\) are independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\). Consequently, for each nonempty \(S\), \(\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i/(\sigma\sqrt{|S|})\) is standard normal. The Gaussian Chernoff calculation \(\Pr(|Z|>z)\le2e^{-z^2/2}\), followed by the union bound, proves the tail inequality without requiring independence across sets \(S\). Integrating that tail at \(z_0=\sqrt{2\log(2|\mathscr S_n|)}\) gives the stated expectation bound.
