Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and define the finite Borel measure
\[
\mu_t(E):=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t),\qquad E\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2).
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\), by \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), entails every assumption used below. In particular, \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) holds. Let \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\), and put \(h:=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\). The conditions \(h_0>0\), \(c_m>0\), and \(\kappa>2\) imply \(h\in(0,h_0]\) and \(c_mh^{\kappa}>0\). Since \(h<r\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_t\bigl(\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\bigr)
&\ge P\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X,x)\le h\}\\
&=q_{t,P}(x,h)\\
&\ge c_mh^{\kappa}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every open neighborhood of \(x\) has positive \(\mu_t\)-mass. By the definition of the support of a Borel measure,
\[
x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)=\mathcal X_{t,P}.
\]
This argument applies to both values of \(t\) and every \(x\in\mathcal B\).

Define the relatively open near-boundary set
\[
U_{t,P}:=\left\{z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\right\},
\qquad
\delta_{\mathcal B}(z):=\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x).
\]
The map \(\delta_{\mathcal B}\) is one-Lipschitz by the triangle inequality. By the definition of \(g_{t,P}\), its restriction to \(U_{t,P}\) represents the restricted observed conditional-regression equivalence class. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(z')|\le Ld(z,z'),
\qquad z,z'\in U_{t,P},
\]
so an \(L\)-Lipschitz representative exists.

To prove uniqueness, let \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) be two \(L\)-Lipschitz representatives of that restricted equivalence class. Then \(f_1=f_2\), \(\mu_t\)-almost everywhere on \(U_{t,P}\). Suppose that for some \(z\in U_{t,P}\),
\[
\Delta:=|f_1(z)-f_2(z)|>0.
\]
Because \(L>0\) and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\), choose
\[
0<r<\min\left\{h_0-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z),\frac{\Delta}{4L}\right\}.
\]
The one-Lipschitz property of \(\delta_{\mathcal B}\) implies
\[
B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\subseteq U_{t,P}.
\]
For every \(z'\) in this relative ball, the triangle inequality and the two Lipschitz inequalities yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
|f_1(z')-f_2(z')|
&\ge \Delta-|f_1(z')-f_1(z)|-|f_2(z')-f_2(z)|\\
&\ge \Delta-2L d(z,z')\\
&>\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\mathbb R^2\) has a countable base, the complement of the support of the finite Borel measure \(\mu_t\) is a countable union of \(\mu_t\)-null open sets; hence \(\mu_t(\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal X_{t,P})=0\). Since \(z\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\),
\[
\mu_t\bigl(B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\bigr)
=\mu_t\bigl(B(z,r)\bigr)>0.
\]
The preceding strict inequality therefore holds on a set of positive \(\mu_t\)-mass, contradicting \(f_1=f_2\) almost everywhere on \(U_{t,P}\). Thus \(f_1=f_2\) pointwise, and the unique representative is \(g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}\).

Now let \(x\in\mathcal B\). The first step gives \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(x)=0\), so \(x\in U_{t,P}\). Whenever \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) and \(z\to x\), eventually \(d(z,x)<h_0\), and then \(z\in U_{t,P}\). Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) consequently gives
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Using the definition of \(\theta_{t,P}\),
\[
\theta_{t,P}(x)
=\lim_{z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}}g_{t,P}(z)
=g_{t,P}(x).
\]
Existence follows from the displayed convergence, and uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the near-boundary representative.

We next verify the observed-law assertion. Let \(P\) and \(P'\) be two members of the class that induce the same law of \(O=(Y,X)\) under the same known assignment geometry. Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) makes \(T=\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal A_1\}\) a known measurable function of \(X\); hence the common observed law of \((Y,X)\) determines the same law of \((Y,X,T)\) under \(P\) and \(P'\). It follows successively that the two laws have the same score marginal \(P_X\), the same restricted measures \(\mu_t\), the same supports \(\mathcal X_{t,P}=\mathcal X_{t,P'}\), and the same observed conditional-regression equivalence classes. The pointwise uniqueness just proved then gives
\[
g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}=g_{t,P'}|_{U_{t,P'}}.
\]
Taking the already established boundary limits gives \(\theta_{t,P}=\theta_{t,P'}\). Finally, the definition \(\tau_P=\theta_{1,P}-\theta_{0,P}\) gives \(\tau_P=\tau_{P'}\). Thus the near-boundary regressions, both traces, and their contrast are functionals of the observed law; no value of an arbitrary conditional-expectation version at a null boundary point has been used.

It remains to prove the potential-outcome compatibility. Put
\[
S_t:=\mathcal A_t\cap\left\{z:\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\right\},
\qquad
D_t:=S_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
\]
Because \(D_t\subseteq\mathcal A_t\) and the restricted measure \(\mu_t\) is concentrated on \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\),
\[
P_X(D_t\setminus U_{t,P})=0.
\]
For each \(z\in U_{t,P}\), compactness of \(\mathcal B\) gives an \(x_z\in\mathcal B\) satisfying \(d(z,x_z)=\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\). The boundary-support result, the trace identity above, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|g_{t,P}(z)|
&\le |g_{t,P}(x_z)|+Ld(z,x_z)\\
&=|\theta_{t,P}(x_z)|+L\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)\\
&\le L(1+h_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) gives, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(z\in U_{t,P}\),
\[
\mathbb E_P\bigl[|Y-g_{t,P}(X)|\mid X=z\bigr]
=\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_P\bigl[|Y|\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\bigr]
\le \left\{L(1+h_0)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right\}P_X(D_t)<\infty.
\]
On \(\{X\in D_t\}\), the definition of \(S_t\) and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) give \(T=t\), while \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) gives \(Y=Y(t)\). Hence \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) is integrable.

Let \(p_t:=P_X(D_t)\). If \(p_t>0\), define the normalized law
\[
Q_t(C):=\frac{P\bigl(C\cap\{X\in D_t\}\bigr)}{p_t}.
\]
For every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), apply the defining conditional-regression identity for \(g_{t,P}\) to the bounded function \(z\mapsto b(z)\mathbf 1\{z\in D_t\}\). Since \(D_t\subseteq S_t\) and therefore \(T=t\) on \(\{X\in D_t\}\), that identity and consistency give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_t}\bigl[b(X)\{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]
&=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\bigl[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]\\
&=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\bigl[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\{Y-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The preceding integrability bounds permit the conditional-expectation characterization in this display. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_t}[Y(t)\mid X]=g_{t,P}(X),\qquad Q_t\text{-almost surely},
\]
or, equivalently, the equality holds \(P_X\)-almost everywhere on \(D_t\). The division by \(p_t\) is justified exactly in the stated positive-mass case. If \(p_t=0\), an almost-everywhere assertion on \(D_t\) is vacuous, as claimed.

Finally, if \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), then
\[
S_0=\mathcal A_0,\qquad S_1=\mathcal A_1.
\]
Thus \(D_t=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}\), and the compatibility just proved is precisely \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\)-almost-everywhere compatibility throughout the open \(h_0\)-tube. This conclusion is local to the observed side and imposes no integrability requirement on \(Y(t)\) away from that side.
