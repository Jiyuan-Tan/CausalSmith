Fix the scores. For a disk with center \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\), membership of \(X_i\) is equivalent to
\[
 2c^{\mathsf T}X_i+s\ge\lVert X_i\rVert_2^2,\qquad
 s=r^2-\lVert c\rVert_2^2. \tag{17}
\]
Thus every disk trace is a sign pattern induced by \(n\) affine planes in the three-dimensional parameter \((c_1,c_2,s)\). A trace realized by a closed disk can be made strict by increasing its radius by less than the positive minimum distance to the finitely many excluded points, so equality in (17) creates no additional trace.

Let \(A(n,v)\) be the largest number of open cells cut out by \(n\) affine hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^v\). Adding the last hyperplane retains every old cell and splits at most \(A(n-1,v-1)\) of them. Hence
\[
 A(n,v)\le A(n-1,v)+A(n-1,v-1),\qquad
 A(0,v)=A(n,0)=1. \tag{18}
\]
Induction using Pascal's identity gives
\[
 A(n,3)\le\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj. \tag{19}
\]
Restricting to genuine disks, fixing \(h\), restricting centers to \(\mathcal B\), or intersecting a trace with \(\{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t\}\) cannot increase the number of traces. Multiplication by the two choices of \(t\) and by \(|\mathcal H_n|\) proves the cardinality bound.

If \(\mathscr S_n=\varnothing\), the convention makes both stochastic claims immediate. Otherwise, \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) gives conditional independence of the residuals for almost every score vector, and \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_i\mid X_i]=0,\qquad
 \mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda\varepsilon_i}\mid X_i]\le e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}. \tag{20}
\]
Complementarity and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) ensure that \(T_i=t\) when \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\). Therefore, for nonempty \(S\in\mathscr S_n\) and \(u\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \exp\!\left\{u\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}\right\}
  \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 &=\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \exp\!\left\{\frac{u\varepsilon_i}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}\right\}
  \ \middle|\ X_i\right]\\
 &\le\prod_{i\in S}\exp\!\left\{\frac{u^2}{2|S|}\right\}
 =e^{u^2/2}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{21}
\]
For \(Z_S=(\sigma\sqrt{|S|})^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\), the pointwise inequality
\[
 e^{uz}\mathbf 1\{Z_S>z\}\le e^{uZ_S}
\]
and (21), optimized at \(u=z>0\), give
\[
 \Pr_P(Z_S>z\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\le e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{22}
\]
The same calculation for \(-Z_S\), followed by a union bound, yields
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|>z
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le2|\mathscr S_n|e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{23}
\]
No independence among the set-indexed sums is used.

Let \(z_0=\sqrt{2\log\{2|\mathscr S_n|\}}>1\). Integrating (23) and using
\(\int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\le z_0^{-1}e^{-z_0^2/2}\), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|
 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 &\le z_0+2|\mathscr S_n|\int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\\
 &\le z_0+z_0^{-1}\le z_0+1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
