Define \(m\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) as in the statement.  The function \(m\) is nondecreasing and strictly positive on \((0,h_0]\), while \(\mathsf J_{-}\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing in its second argument.

First let \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), the law \(P\) has all the sharp-assignment, consistency, boundary-entropy, Lipschitz-regression, bounded-trace, and Gaussian-error properties required by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  Its polynomial lower-mass property gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<h\le h_0\),
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h). \tag{1}
\]
For fixed \(h\), the set \(\{x\in\mathcal B:a_P(x,h)\le u\}\) is nondecreasing in \(u\); hence its maximal packing cardinality \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) is nondecreasing in \(u\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u)\).  If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), the pervasive thin-site property and monotonicity give
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{2}
\]
Since \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), (2) implies \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\).  Equations (1)--(2) and \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) prove
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{3}
\]

The same argument applies to \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  The base-class definition again gives (1).  The isolated thin-site property gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge1, \tag{4}
\]
so monotonicity in \(u\), together with the zero value of \(\mathsf J_{-}\) below the threshold, proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{5}
\]
The circular membership lemma supplies one member of each subclass.  Consequently \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\) is nonempty, as required by the definition of its minimax risk and by \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).

For the lower risk bound, observe directly from \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) that \(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\) implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}
 \mathbb E_P\lVert\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_P\rVert_\infty
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
 \mathbb E_P\lVert\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_P\rVert_\infty
\]
for every admissible estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\).  Taking the infimum over the same estimator set preserves this inequality.  Apply it to (3), and then invoke the pervasive lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), to obtain
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{6}
\]

For the matching upper bound, set
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Then \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(s_n/(h_0/n)\to\infty\).  Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid contains an \(h_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{7}
\]
By (7), monotonicity of \(m\), and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C\log n\),
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(h_n\) satisfies the count-qualification constraint in the upper display of \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Also, since \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Lh_n+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}
 &\le C\left\{s_n+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}\\
 &=Cs_n. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The clipped count-adaptive estimator in that theorem is one sample-measurable estimator admitted by \(R_n\).  Its uniform guarantee, (8), and (9) yield
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \le Cs_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{10}
\]

Equations (6) and (10) establish the matched pervasive frontier for the displayed two-argument class.  Finally, the definitions of the two polynomial rates give
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{11}
\]
Thus a constant declared lower packing profile does not imply the isolated rate: the class still contains the pervasive subclass and, by (6)--(10), has the pervasive rate.  The upper thin-site profile in \(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\), absent from \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), is precisely what excludes this counterexample.  Therefore the frozen two-argument class cannot support the requested isolated-versus-pervasive arbitrary-modulus criterion, proving the asserted negative answer.
