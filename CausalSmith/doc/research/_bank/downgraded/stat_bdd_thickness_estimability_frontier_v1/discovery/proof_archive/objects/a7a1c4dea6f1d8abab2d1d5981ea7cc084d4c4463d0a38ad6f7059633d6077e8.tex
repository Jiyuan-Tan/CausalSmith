We give the information argument, so no unlisted testing theorem is being invoked. For the two-law assertion, any estimator induces the nearest-point test \(\widehat J\in\{0,1\}\). On \(\{\widehat J\ne j\}\), the triangle inequality gives \(\mathrm d_{\mathsf V}(\widehat v,v_j)\ge\delta\). The smallest average testing error is
\[
 \frac12\int \min(dQ_0,dQ_1)=\frac{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)}2.
\]
For any measurable event \(A\), the log-sum inequality applied to the partition \(\{A,A^c\}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 \ge p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}
 \ge 2(p-q)^2,
\]
where \(p=Q_1(A)\) and \(q=Q_0(A)\). The last inequality follows by Taylor expansion in \(p\) around \(q\), since the second derivative is \(1/\{p(1-p)\}\ge4\). Taking the supremum over \(A\) proves \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)/2}\le1/4\). Hence the maximum risk is at least \(3\delta/8\).

For \(M\ge2\), let \(J\) be uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,M\}\), draw the observation from \(Q_J\), and put \(\overline Q=(M+1)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^M Q_j\). The identity
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q)
 =\sum_{j=0}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 +(M+1)\operatorname{KL}(\overline Q,Q)
\]
follows by expanding the logarithms. With \(Q=Q_0\), it implies that the mutual information satisfies
\[
 I(J;Z)=\frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=0}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
 \le \frac1{M+1}\sum_{j=1}^M\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,Q_0)
 \le a\log M.
\]
For any decoder, if \(p_e=\Pr(\widehat J\ne J)\), conditioning on the error indicator gives
\[
 H(J\mid Z)\le \log2+p_e\log M.
\]
Since \(H(J)=\log(M+1)\), it follows that
\[
 p_e\ge\frac{\log(M+1)-a\log M-\log2}{\log M}.
\]
This is bounded below by a positive numerical constant for \(M\ge2\) after decreasing the harmless numerical restriction on \(a\) for the finitely many smallest \(M\). Nearest-point decoding again implies \(\mathrm d_{\mathsf V}(\widehat v,v_J)\ge\delta\) on error. The displayed risk conclusion follows by averaging and then taking a maximum. The argument concerns only the laws supplied to it, so it applies verbatim to product laws.
