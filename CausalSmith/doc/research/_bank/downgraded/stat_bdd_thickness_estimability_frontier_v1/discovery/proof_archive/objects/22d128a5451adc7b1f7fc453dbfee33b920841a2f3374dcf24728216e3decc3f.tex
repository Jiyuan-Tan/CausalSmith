Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{base}}\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Define the finite Borel measure
\[
\mu_t(E):=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t),\qquad E\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ Borel}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), every assumption in that definition holds for \(P\).
The positive lower bound in \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) implies that \(\mathcal B\) is nonempty, and \(\mathcal B\) is compact by its declared space.

We first show that every boundary point is in the support of each one-sided measure.  Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(r>0\), and set
\[
h:=\min\{h_0,r/2\}.
\]
Since \(h_0>0\), one has \(0<h\le h_0\) and \(h<r\).  The conditions \(c_m>0\) and \(\kappa>2\), together with \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\), therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_t\bigl(\{z:d(z,x)<r\}\bigr)
&\ge P\{X\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X,x)\le h\}\\
&=q_{t,P}(x,h)\\
&\ge c_mh^{\kappa}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every open neighborhood of \(x\) has positive \(\mu_t\)-mass.  By the definition of the support of a Borel measure,
\[
x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)=\mathcal X_{t,P}.
\]
This holds for every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and both values of \(t\).

Put
\[
\delta_{\mathcal B}(z):=\inf_{x\in\mathcal B}d(z,x),
\qquad
U_{t,P}:=\{z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
\]
The triangle inequality implies
\[
|\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z')|\le d(z,z'),
\]
so \(U_{t,P}\) is relatively open in \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) implies, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\),
\[
\mathcal L_P\bigl(Y-g_{t,P}(X)\mid X=z\bigr)=\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2).
\]
The centered Gaussian law has finite first moment and mean zero.  Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=z]=g_{t,P}(z)
\]
for \(P_X\)-almost every \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and hence for \(\mu_t\)-almost every such \(z\).  Thus \(g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}\) is a representative of the observed-outcome conditional-regression equivalence class restricted to \(U_{t,P}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\),
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(z')|\le Ld(z,z'),
\qquad z,z'\in U_{t,P},
\]
so this representative obeys the required inequality.

We next prove pointwise uniqueness.  Let \(f_1,f_2:U_{t,P}\to\mathbb R\) be two \(L\)-Lipschitz representatives of the same restricted conditional-regression class.  Then
\[
f_1=f_2\qquad\mu_t\text{-almost everywhere on }U_{t,P}.
\]
Suppose, to obtain a contradiction, that some \(z\in U_{t,P}\) satisfies
\[
\Delta:=|f_1(z)-f_2(z)|>0.
\]
Because \(L>0\) and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\), choose
\[
0<r<\min\left\{h_0-\delta_{\mathcal B}(z),\frac{\Delta}{4L}\right\}.
\]
The one-Lipschitz inequality for \(\delta_{\mathcal B}\) yields
\[
B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\subseteq U_{t,P}.
\]
For every \(z'\) in this relative ball, the triangle inequality and the two Lipschitz bounds give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|f_1(z')-f_2(z')|
&\ge \Delta-|f_1(z')-f_1(z)|-|f_2(z')-f_2(z)|\\
&\ge \Delta-2Ld(z,z')\\
&>\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The complement of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\) is a union of open \(\mu_t\)-null sets.  Because \(\mathbb R^2\) has a countable base, that complement is a countable union of such sets and therefore is \(\mu_t\)-null.  Since \(z\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\), it follows that
\[
\mu_t\bigl(B(z,r)\cap\mathcal X_{t,P}\bigr)
=\mu_t\bigl(B(z,r)\bigr)>0.
\]
The preceding strict separation between \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) thus holds on a set of positive \(\mu_t\)-mass, contradicting their almost-everywhere equality.  Hence \(f_1=f_2\) pointwise on \(U_{t,P}\), and the unique \(L\)-Lipschitz representative is \(g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}\).

Now fix \(x\in\mathcal B\).  The support argument above gives \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), and \(\delta_{\mathcal B}(x)=0<h_0\), so \(x\in U_{t,P}\).  If \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) and \(z\to x\), then eventually \(d(z,x)<h_0\), whence \(z\in U_{t,P}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0.
\]
By the definition of \(\theta_{t,P}\),
\[
\theta_{t,P}(x)
=\lim_{z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}}g_{t,P}(z)
=g_{t,P}(x).
\]
This proves existence of the trace, while uniqueness follows from the pointwise uniqueness of the near-boundary representative.

To verify observed-law identification, let \(P\) and \(P'\) be two class members inducing the same law of \(O=(Y,X)\) under the same known assignment geometry.  Their score marginals coincide, so their restricted measures \(\mu_t\) and supports \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\) coincide.  Their observed conditional-regression equivalence classes also coincide because those classes are determined by the common joint law of \((Y,X)\).  Under each law, the preceding Gaussian-error argument shows that \(g_{t,P}\), respectively \(g_{t,P'}\), represents this common observed regression class on the common side support.  Pointwise uniqueness therefore gives
\[
g_{t,P}|_{U_{t,P}}=g_{t,P'}|_{U_{t,P'}}.
\]
Taking the boundary limits already established yields \(\theta_{t,P}=\theta_{t,P'}\).  Finally, the definition \(\tau_P=\theta_{1,P}-\theta_{0,P}\) yields \(\tau_P=\tau_{P'}\).  Thus the near-boundary side regressions, the boundary traces, and their contrast are functionals of the observed law; this argument never selects a version of a conditional mean at a null boundary point.

It remains to establish the stated potential-outcome compatibility.  Define
\[
S_t:=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal A_1\}=t\},
\qquad
D_t:=S_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}.
\]
Because \(D_t\subseteq\mathcal A_t\) and \(\mu_t\) is concentrated on its support,
\[
P_X(D_t\setminus U_{t,P})=0.
\]
Hence, for each \(z\in U_{t,P}\), there is \(x_z\in\mathcal B\) such that
\[
d(z,x_z)=\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0.
\]
The boundary-support result, the trace identity, \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|g_{t,P}(z)|
&\le |g_{t,P}(x_z)|+Ld(z,x_z)\\
&=|\theta_{t,P}(x_z)|+L\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)\\
&\le L(1+h_0).
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\), direct integration gives
\[
\mathbb E|Z|
=\frac{2}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^{\infty}u\exp\left(-\frac{u^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\,du
=\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}.
\]
Therefore \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), the preceding envelope, and \(P_X(D_t\setminus U_{t,P})=0\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\bigl[|Y|\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\bigr]
&\le \mathbb E_P\bigl[|g_{t,P}(X)|\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\bigr]
+\mathbb E_P\bigl[|Y-g_{t,P}(X)|\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\bigr]\\
&\le \left\{L(1+h_0)+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right\}P_X(D_t)<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
On \(\{X\in D_t\}\), the definition of \(S_t\) and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) give \(T=t\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) then gives \(Y=Y(t)\) there, so \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\) is integrable.

Let \(p_t:=P_X(D_t)\).  If \(p_t>0\), define the normalized law
\[
Q_t(C):=\frac{P(C\cap\{X\in D_t\})}{p_t}.
\]
For every bounded Borel function \(b:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), the observed-regression identity proved from \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\), followed by \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(\{X\in D_t\}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_t}\bigl[b(X)\{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]
&=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\bigl[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\{Y(t)-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]\\
&=\frac{1}{p_t}\mathbb E_P\bigl[b(X)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\{Y-g_{t,P}(X)\}\bigr]\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
All terms are integrable by the bounds above, and division is valid because \(p_t>0\).  The defining characterization of conditional expectation now yields
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_t}[Y(t)\mid X]=g_{t,P}(X),
\qquad Q_t\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Equivalently, this equality holds \(P_X\)-almost everywhere on \(D_t\).  If \(p_t=0\), the almost-everywhere compatibility assertion on \(D_t\) is vacuous.

Finally, if \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), then
\[
S_0=\mathcal A_0,
\qquad
S_1=\mathcal A_1.
\]
Hence \(D_t=\mathcal A_t\cap\{z:\delta_{\mathcal B}(z)<h_0\}\), and the compatibility just proved holds \(P_X|_{\mathcal A_t}\)-almost everywhere throughout the open \(h_0\)-tube.  Because the normalization and integrability arguments used only \(Y(t)\mathbf 1\{X\in D_t\}\), no integrability of the counterfactual away from its observed side is required.
