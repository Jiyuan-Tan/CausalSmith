Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\) and assignment is sharp in every member of \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\). The formulas in \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and \(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) show that \(\widehat\tau_n\) is a measurable function of \(\mathcal D_n\), the known assignment geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\). In particular, neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density occurs in this map.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), put
\[
r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
and take \(n\) sufficiently large below, uniformly over this exponent range. By \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), there is \(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) such that
\[
r\le h_*<2r. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, eventually \(r<h_0\) and the smallest grid point is smaller than \(r\), uniformly because \(nr\to\infty\) for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\).

Let \(\{z_1,\ldots,z_J\}\) be a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\). The upper half of the global boundary-entropy condition, which is a member property of \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\), gives
\[
J\le 2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}. \tag{2}
\]
Maximality makes this set an \(h_*/2\)-net. For each \(z_j\) and \(t\), the polynomial lower-mass member property gives
\[
\mu_{j,t}:=\mathbb E_P N_{t,n}(z_j,h_*/2)
=nq_{t,P}(z_j,h_*/2)
\ge nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}. \tag{3}
\]
For clarity, the required binomial lower-tail bound follows directly from exponential Markov. If \(V\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)\), then, with \(\lambda=\log2\),
\[
\Pr\{V\le nq/2\}
\le e^{\lambda nq/2}\mathbb E e^{-\lambda V}
=e^{\lambda nq/2}(1-q+qe^{-\lambda})^n
\le\exp\{nq(\lambda/2+e^{-\lambda}-1)\}
\le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{4}
\]
The penultimate inequality uses \(1+v\le e^v\), and the last uses \((\log2)/2-1/2<-1/8\). Applying (4) to (3), taking a union bound over the \(2J\) center-side pairs, and using (2), produces an event \(G_n\) such that
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
\le Ch_*^{-1}\exp\{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}\}, \tag{5}
\]
and, on \(G_n\), every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\) obey
\[
N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}. \tag{6}
\]
Here one chooses a net point with \(d(x,z_j)<h_*/2\) and uses
\(B(z_j,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\). Constants in (2)--(6) can be chosen uniformly in \(\kappa\), since \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\).

Furthermore,
\[
\inf_{2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa}
\frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
\ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}
\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, (6) implies, for all sufficiently large \(n\), that \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) simultaneously for all \(x,t\) on \(G_n\). The minimizing property in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), (1), (6), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) then give
\[
L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)
+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
{N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
\le Cr \qquad\text{on }G_n. \tag{8}
\]
All divisions in (8) are valid because an admissible bandwidth has a strictly positive count.

The lower-mass condition also implies that every ball centered at \(x\in\mathcal B\) has positive restricted score mass on each side. Hence \(x\) lies in the closure of each side support. Passing to the trace in the Lipschitz inequality yields, for \(z\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) with \(d(z,x)\le h_0\),
\[
|g_{t,P}(z)-\theta_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x). \tag{9}
\]
Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) and define
\[
Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
\frac{\left|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|}
{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}},
\]
with the zero convention when \(\mathscr S_n\) is empty. Independent sampling and exact conditional Gaussian errors permit application of \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\). Since \(|\mathcal H_n|\le2+\log_2n\), that lemma gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[Z_n\mid X_1,ldots,X_n]
\le\sqrt{2\log\{2(1\vee M_n)\}}+1
\le C\sqrt{\log n}, \qquad
M_n:=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj. \tag{10}
\]
On \(G_n\), the selected set is nonempty and belongs to \(\mathscr S_n\). By (9), its conditional bias is at most \(L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\). Also \(h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\). Thus (8)--(10) imply
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
|\widehat g_{t,n}(x;\widehat h_{t,n}(x))-\theta_{t,P}(x)|
\,\middle|\,X_1,ldots,X_n\right]
\mathbf1_{G_n}
\le Cr\mathbf1_{G_n}. \tag{11}
\]
The triangle inequality over \(t=0,1\) turns (11) into the same bound for \(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_P\).

On \(G_n^c\), every selected nonempty average still uses observations at distance at most \(h_0\) from its center, so (9) bounds its bias by \(Lh_0<L\). An empty fit equals zero by \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\), and its error is at most \(L\) by the bounded-trace member property. For a nonempty set, \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\); hence (10) gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\left[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\,\middle|\,X_1,ldots,X_n\right]
\le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{12}
\]
Combining (5), (11), and (12) yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
\mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
\le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}h_*^{-1}
e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}
\le Cr. \tag{13}
\]
The final inequality is uniform because (7) makes the exponential dominate the displayed polynomial and logarithmic factors at a rate bounded away from zero uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\).

For the converse, use the pervasive family certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) at \(h=a_0r\), where \(a_0\in(0,1)\) is fixed below. Its packing cardinality \(M=|\Xi_h^{\mathrm{perv}}|\asymp h^{-1}\) satisfies
\[
\log M\ge c\log n \tag{14}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\). Distinct target traces are separated in supremum norm by \(Lh/2\), because at the active center of either tent the other tent vanishes. The product divergence bound in that lemma is
\[
\operatorname{KL}\left(
(\mathbb P_{\kappa,j,h}^{\mathrm{perv}})^{\otimes n},
(\mathbb P_{\kappa,k,h}^{\mathrm{perv}})^{\otimes n}
\right)
\le C\frac{L^2}{\sigma^2}nh^{\kappa+2}
=C\frac{L^2}{\sigma^2}a_0^{\kappa+2}\log n
\le C\frac{L^2}{\sigma^2}a_0^4\log n. \tag{15}
\]
The constants are uniform on \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), as the explicit witness bounds used by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) are bounded on the closure \([2,\overline\kappa]\). Choose \(a_0\) so that (15) is at most \(a\log M\) for the numerical \(a<1/8\) in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\). Applying that lemma with the supremum metric and \(\delta=Lh/4\), then taking the infimum over estimators in \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\), proves
\[
R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge ch=cr. \tag{16}
\]
This is also the pervasive lower bound recorded in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).

It remains to verify the claimed fallback probability. Take a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net. It has at most \(2C_{\mathcal B}h_0^{-1}\) points. At every net point and on both sides, the radius-\(h_0/2\) count has mean at least
\[
nc_m(h_0/2)^{\kappa}\ge nc_m2^{-\overline\kappa}h_0^{\kappa}. \tag{17}
\]
Uniformly in \(\kappa\), (17) eventually exceeds \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\). Equations (2)--(4), now at the fixed scale \(h_0\), plus ball inclusion show that, outside an event of probability at most
\[
C\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}, \tag{18}
\]
one has \(N_{t,n}(x,h_0)\ge8\log(e+n/h_0)\) for every \(x,t\). Thus \(h_0\) is admissible everywhere, and no admissible set is empty. Enlarging \(C\), (18) is bounded by the stated \(Cn^3\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}\). Finally, on \(G_n\) the comparison bandwidth \(h_*\) is admissible everywhere, so every fallback occurrence is contained in \(G_n^c\); its expected-loss contribution was therefore included in the exceptional term of (13). Equations (13), (16), and (18) prove all claims.
