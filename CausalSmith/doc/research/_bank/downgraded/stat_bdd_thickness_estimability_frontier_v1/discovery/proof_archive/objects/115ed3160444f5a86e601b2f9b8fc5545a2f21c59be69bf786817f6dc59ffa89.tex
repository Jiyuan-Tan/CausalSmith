Write a point of the circular tube as
\[
 z=(1+s)(\cos\phi,\sin\phi),\qquad |s|\le h_0,quad 0\le\phi<2\pi .
\]
Because \(h_0<1\), these are nonsingular coordinates with area element \((1+s)\,ds\,d\phi\).  Hence the normalizing constant in \(\mathrm{def:circular\text{-}thinning\text{-}witnesses}\) is
\[
 Z_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 =2\pi\int_{-h_0}^{h_0}|s|^{\kappa-2}(1+s)\,ds
 =\frac{4\pi}{\kappa-1}h_0^{\kappa-1}.
\]
It is positive and finite, uniformly away from zero and infinity for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\).  The isolated integrand is nonnegative, bounded by one, and positive off the single point \(x_{\star}\); compactness of \(\mathcal T\) and continuity in \(\kappa\in[2,\overline\kappa]\) therefore give the same uniform positivity and finiteness for its normalizer.

On a sufficiently small coordinate patch about any \(x\in S^1\), let \(s\) be signed normal depth and \(v\) signed arclength.  Compactness of \(S^1\), together with \(h_0<1\), supplies constants independent of \(x\) and \(\kappa\) for which the Jacobian is bounded above and below and
\[
 c(s^2+v^2)^{1/2}\le d(z,x)\le C(s^2+v^2)^{1/2}.
\]
Consequently, on either assignment side and for all sufficiently small \(h\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(x,h)
 &\asymp \int_0^h\int_{-\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}^{\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}
 s^{\kappa-2}\,dv\,ds\\
 &=h^{\kappa}\int_0^1 2w^{\kappa-2}\sqrt{1-w^2}\,dw
 \asymp h^{\kappa}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
The last integral is positive and is bounded above and below uniformly on the compact exponent interval.  By compactness and positivity, the constants can be enlarged so that (1) holds for every \(0<h\le h_0\).  Choose \(c_m>0\) below the uniform lower constant and \(C_m>c_m\) above the uniform upper constant.  Every boundary point then belongs to the thin set at level \(C_mh^{\kappa}\).  If \(E\) is a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of \(S^1\), the arcs of radius proportional to \(h\) about points of \(E\) are disjoint while arcs with twice that radius cover the circle.  Comparison with its arclength \(2\pi\) gives
\[
 c h^{-1}\le |E|\le C h^{-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(h,C_mh^{\kappa})\asymp h^{-1}. \tag{2}
\]

For the isolated density put \(s_x=d(x,x_{\star})\).  The same coordinate comparison gives, uniformly in the side \(t\),
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\ge
 \begin{cases}
 c h^{\kappa},&s_x\le2h,\\
 c h^2\min\{1,(s_x/h_0)^{\kappa-2}\},&s_x>2h.
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, in the first case one integrates the density over a side-specific subball of radius proportional to \(h\) whose distance from \(x_{\star}\) is proportional to \(h\); in the second case, on a subball of radius \(h/2\) about \(x\), the triangle inequality bounds the distance from \(x_{\star}\) below by a fixed multiple of \(s_x\).  Since \(s_x>2h\) in the second line and \(h_0<1\), both lines of (3) are bounded below by \(c'h^{\kappa}\).  At \(x=x_{\star}\), polar integration on either side also gives
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x_{\star},h)
 \le C\int_0^h r^{\kappa-2}r\,dr
 \le C'h^{\kappa}. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) prove the common polynomial lower-mass condition and the asserted two-sided equivalence at \(x_{\star}\).

If \(a_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\le u\), (3) implies
\[
 s_x\le C\left\{h+(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}. \tag{5}
\]
An arc of the length on the right of (5) contains at most
\[
 C\left\{1+h^{-1}(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}
\]
points separated by \(h\).  This is exactly \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\), after enlarging \(C_{\mathrm{iso}}\).  At \(u=C_mh^{\kappa}\), (5) makes the upper bound constant, whereas (4) puts \(x_{\star}\) in the thin set.  Therefore
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(h,C_mh^{\kappa})\asymp1. \tag{6}
\]
All estimates extend to the remaining compact range of radii by changing constants.  The circle itself satisfies \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) by the same disjoint-arc and covering-arc calculation.  The exponent \(\kappa\) in (1) and (4) also agrees with the direct horn-volume calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:polynomial\text{-}horn\text{-}mass}\).

Finally, the assignment is sharp by the construction, the displayed potential-outcome formula implies consistency, and independence of the standard Gaussian variables gives \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\).  Both observed-side regressions and their traces are zero, hence satisfy \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\).  Equations (1)--(6), together with the class definitions, prove
\[
 P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}},
 \qquad
 P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}.
\]
