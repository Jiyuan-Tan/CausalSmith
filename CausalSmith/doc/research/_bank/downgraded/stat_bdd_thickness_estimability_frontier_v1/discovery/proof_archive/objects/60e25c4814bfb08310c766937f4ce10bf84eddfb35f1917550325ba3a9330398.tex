Write a point of the circular tube uniquely as
\[
 z=(1+s)(\cos\phi,\sin\phi),\qquad |s|\le h_0,
 \qquad 0\le\phi<2\pi.
\]
Because \(h_0<1\), this coordinate system is nonsingular and its area element is \((1+s)\,ds\,d\phi\).  Hence the pervasive normalizing constant in \(\mathrm{def:circular\text{-}thinning\text{-}witnesses}\) is
\[
 Z_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 =2\pi\int_{-h_0}^{h_0}|s|^{\kappa-2}(1+s)\,ds
 =\frac{4\pi}{\kappa-1}h_0^{\kappa-1}. \tag{1}
\]
It is positive and finite uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\).  For the isolated normalizer, the integrand is bounded above by one.  On any fixed positive-area subset of \(\mathcal T\) whose distance from \(x_{\star}\) is at least \(h_0/2\), it is bounded below by \(2^{-(\overline\kappa-2)}\).  Thus that normalizer is also bounded above and away from zero uniformly in \(\kappa\).

On a sufficiently small coordinate patch about any \(x\in S^1\), let \(s\) denote signed normal depth and \(v\) signed arclength.  Compactness of \(S^1\) and \(h_0<1\) give constants, uniform in \(x\) and \(\kappa\), such that the Jacobian is bounded above and below and
\[
 c(s^2+v^2)^{1/2}\le d(z,x)\le C(s^2+v^2)^{1/2}. \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, on either assignment side and for all sufficiently small \(h\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(x,h)
 &\asymp \int_0^h\int_{-\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}^{\sqrt{h^2-s^2}}
 s^{\kappa-2}\,dv\,ds\\
 &=h^{\kappa}\int_0^1 2w^{\kappa-2}\sqrt{1-w^2}\,dw
 \asymp h^{\kappa}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
The last integral is continuous and strictly positive on the compact exponent interval \([2,\overline\kappa]\).  Monotonicity in \(h\), compactness, and positivity allow the constants to be enlarged so that (3) holds for every \(0<h\le h_0\).  Choose \(c_m>0\) below its uniform lower constant and \(C_m>c_m\) above its uniform upper constant.  Then every boundary point belongs to the thin set at level \(C_mh^{\kappa}\).  If \(E\) is a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of \(S^1\), disjoint-arc and covering-arc comparisons with total arclength \(2\pi\) give
\[
 c h^{-1}\le |E|\le C h^{-1}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}}(h,C_mh^{\kappa})
 \asymp h^{-1}. \tag{4}
\]

For the isolated density put \(s_x=d(x,x_{\star})\).  The same coordinate comparison gives, uniformly in \(t\),
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\ge
 \begin{cases}
 c h^{\kappa},&s_x\le2h,\\
 c h^2\min\{1,(s_x/h_0)^{\kappa-2}\},&s_x>2h.
 \end{cases} \tag{5}
\]
For the first line, integrate over a side-specific annular subball of radius proportional to \(h\) whose points have distance proportional to \(h\) from \(x_{\star}\).  For the second, integrate over a side-specific subball of radius proportional to \(h\) about \(x\); the triangle inequality makes its distance from \(x_{\star}\) comparable to \(s_x\).  Since \(s_x>2h\) in the second line, both lower bounds are at least a uniform constant times \(h^{\kappa}\).  At \(x=x_{\star}\), polar integration on either side also yields
\[
 q_{t,P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x_{\star},h)
 \le C\int_0^h r^{\kappa-2}r\,dr
 \le C'h^{\kappa}. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) prove the common polynomial lower-mass condition and the asserted equivalence at \(x_{\star}\).

If \(a_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(x,h)\le u\), (5) implies, after enlarging a uniform constant to cover the case in which the right side exceeds the circle's diameter,
\[
 s_x\le C\left\{h+(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}. \tag{7}
\]
An arc of this length contains at most
\[
 C\left\{1+h^{-1}(u/h^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}
\]
points separated by \(h\).  This is the upper-profile condition \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\).  At \(u=C_mh^{\kappa}\), the upper bound is constant, while (6) puts \(x_{\star}\) in the thin set.  Therefore
\[
 \mathsf J_{P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}}(h,C_mh^{\kappa})\asymp1. \tag{8}
\]
The estimates extend to the remaining compact range of radii by changing constants.  The same arc comparison verifies \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\), and the exponent in (3) and (6) agrees with \(\mathrm{lem:polynomial\text{-}horn\text{-}mass}\).

Finally, the assignment is sharp by construction and the displayed potential-outcome formula gives consistency.  For each side and every score, the regular conditional residual kernel is \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\).  It is integrable, centered, and satisfies
\[
 \int e^{\lambda u}\,\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)(du)
 =e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2},\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R, \tag{9}
\]
so it satisfies the broadened condition \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) with equality.  Both observed-side regressions and traces are zero, so the Lipschitz and trace-envelope conditions hold.  Equations (1)--(9) and the two class definitions prove
\[
 P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}},
 \qquad
 P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso},0}\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}.
\]
