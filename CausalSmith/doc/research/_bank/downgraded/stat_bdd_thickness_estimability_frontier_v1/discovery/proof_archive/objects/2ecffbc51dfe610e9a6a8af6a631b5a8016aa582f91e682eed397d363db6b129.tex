Write \(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\).  We first record a consequence used on both sides of the minimax argument.  If \(P\) belongs to either class in the theorem, then \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) gives, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\), \(t\in\{0,1\}\), and \(0<s\le h_0\),
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<2s\}\ge q_{t,P}(x,s)
 \ge c_m s^\kappa>0.
\]
Here positivity uses \(c_m>0\), \(s>0\), and \(\kappa>2\).  The neighborhood characterization of support therefore gives \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\),
\[
 |g_{t,P}(z)-g_{t,P}(x)|\le Ld(z,x)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }z\to x,\ z\in\mathcal X_{t,P},
\]
so the definition of the trace implies \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  The partition condition \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), together with \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\), also implies
\[
 X\in\mathcal A_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T=t.
\]
Consequently an average over \(\mathcal A_t\) has conditional mean \(g_{t,P}(X)\).  These observations justify every trace and bias comparison below.

We next prove the two lower bounds by legal subexperiments of the same classes.  Fix a law in the nonempty class under consideration and retain its score marginal.  On an extension carrying independent \(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define a baseline law \(Q_0\) by \(Y(s)=\sigma Z_s\), \(s\in\{0,1\}\), and use the frozen sharp assignment and consistency equations.  Its side regressions and traces vanish.  All mass, entropy, and thin-site conditions are unchanged because they are functionals of the retained score law, while the zero regressions satisfy \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\), \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\).  Hence \(Q_0\) remains in the same class.

For the isolated class, take
\[
 h=a_0 n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}
\]
with \(a_0\in(0,1)\) to be fixed.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\), there are \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and \(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,P}(x_h,h)=a_P(x_h,h)\le C_mh^\kappa.
\]
Put
\[
 \psi_h(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_h)}h\right)_+,
 \qquad \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12}.
\]
The function \(\eta_h\psi_h\) is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz and is supported by \(B(x_h,h/3)\).  Define \(Q_1\) by replacing the baseline mean on the thin side by
\[
 (g_{1,Q_1},g_{0,Q_1})=
 \begin{cases}
 (\eta_h\psi_h,0),&t_h=1,\\
 (0,-\eta_h\psi_h),&t_h=0,
 \end{cases}
\]
and setting \(Y(s)=g_{s,Q_1}(X)+\sigma Z_s\).  The score law is unchanged, the two regressions have Lipschitz constant at most \(L\), and \(\eta_h\le L\) because \(h<h_0<1\).  Thus all defining conditions of \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}\) continue to hold.  The support conclusion above and continuity of the tent give
\[
 \tau_{Q_1}(x_h)-\tau_{Q_0}(x_h)=\eta_h.
\]
Conditionally on \(X=z\), the observed outcome under \(Q_1\) is a normal location shift of the outcome under \(Q_0\).  Direct integration of the two normal log densities gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2),\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\}
 =\frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
Therefore, using the support and size of the tent,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=\frac1{2\sigma^2}\int g_{t_h,Q_1}(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,P}(z)\\
 &\le\frac{\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}q_{t_h,P}(x_h,h)
 \le \frac{C_mL^2}{288\sigma^2}h^{\kappa+2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Independence in \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) makes the product log likelihood a sum, whence
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =n\operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 \le C a_0^{\kappa+2}.
\]
Choose \(a_0\) sufficiently small that the last quantity is at most \(1/8\).  Applying the two-law case of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) in the sup metric, with its separation parameter equal to \(\eta_h/2\), yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})
 \ge c\eta_h
 \ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}.
\]

For the pervasive class, set
\[
 h=a_0\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\), there is an \(h\)-separated collection \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) satisfying
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},\qquad
 a_P(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa.
\]
The upper entropy bound in \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) makes this collection finite.  At each \(x_j\), choose a minimizing side \(t_j\), use the tent
\[
 \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+,
\]
and perturb side \(1\) upward when \(t_j=1\) or side \(0\) downward when \(t_j=0\), always by \(\eta_h=Lh/12\).  Exactly the preceding membership calculation shows that the resulting law \(Q_j\) is in \(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  Moreover,
\[
 \tau_{Q_j}(x_j)=\eta_h,
 \qquad \tau_{Q_k}(x_j)=0\quad(j\ne k),
\]
because \(d(x_j,x_k)\ge h>h/3\).  Hence all target curves are pairwise separated by at least \(\eta_h\) in sup norm.  The same normal calculation gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le CnL^2h^{\kappa+2}/\sigma^2
 =Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n.
\]
Uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), the lower bound on \(M\) and the displayed choice of \(h\) imply, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \log M\ge c\log(1/h)\ge c'\log n.
\]
Taking \(a_0\) small enough, uniformly over this interval of \(\kappa\), makes the average divergence at most \(a\log M\), where \(a<1/8\) is as in \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\).  Its multiple-law conclusion therefore gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\eta_h
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}.
\]
The circular families in \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) give the promised explicit nonempty specification and an explicit instance of these legal subexperiments.

We now prove the upper bounds with a single sample-measurable estimator for each fixed class and sample size.  Let \(0<b\le h_0/2\), and choose a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated set \(\{z_1,\ldots,z_M\}\subset\mathcal B\).  Compactness and maximality make it a \(b/2\)-net, and \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\).  Let \(j(x)\) be the least index with \(d(x,z_{j(x)})\le b/2\), and define
\[
 K_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t, d(X_i,z_j)\le b\},
\]
\[
 \overline g_{t,j}=
 \frac{\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t, d(X_i,z_j)\le b\}}
 {K_{t,j}\vee1},
 \qquad
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)=\overline g_{1,j(x)}-\overline g_{0,j(x)}.
\]
This is a map of \(\mathcal D_n\) and the known geometry, with no law-dependent input.  On any event on which \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) and the preliminary trace identity give
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j(x)}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)-\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|
 \le L\{b+d(z_{j(x)},x)\}
 \le\frac{3Lb}{2}.
\]

We shall use the following elementary count bound.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), then, for \(s=\log2\), Markov's inequality and the binomial generating function give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 &\le e^{snq/2}\mathbb E(e^{-sN})\\
 &=e^{snq/2}(1-q+qe^{-s})^n\\
 &\le\exp\{nq(s/2-1+e^{-s})\}
 \le e^{-nq/8}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

For the pervasive upper bound, take \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  For sufficiently large \(n\), \(b\le h_0/2\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\), every \(K_{t,j}\) has success probability at least \(c_mb^\kappa\).  Hence, outside an event \(H_b^c\) satisfying
\[
 \Pr_P(H_b^c)\le Cb^{-1}\exp(-c n c_mb^\kappa),
\]
all \(2M\) counts are at least \(nc_mb^\kappa/2\).  Conditionally on the scores, \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) makes every centered average Gaussian.  A union bound over the \(2M\) averages and integration of its tail give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n,H_b\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e/b)}{n c_mb^\kappa}}.
\]
Since \(nb^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) and \(\log(e/b)\le C\log n\), the right-hand side is at most \(Cb\).  Together with the two side-bias bounds, this proves conditional expected sup error at most \(Cb\) on \(H_b\).

On \(H_b^c\), every conditional mean appearing in a nonempty average has absolute value at most \(L(1+h_0)<2L\), by \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\) and Lipschitz smoothness.  A second Gaussian union bound, now using only \(K_{t,j}\ge1\) for nonempty averages, gives the unconditional conditional bound \(C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}\) for the sup error.  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le Cb+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}b^{-1}e^{-c n c_mb^\kappa}
 \le Cb,
\]
where the last inequality holds for all sufficiently large \(n\) because \(n b^\kappa=(\log n)/b^2\to\infty\).  The definition of \(R_n\) then gives \(R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\le Cr_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).

For the isolated upper bound, put \(b=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}=n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\), so that
\[
 nb^\kappa=b^{-2}.
\]
Fix a side \(t\).  Choose a fixed \(D\ge C_m\), set \(u_\ell=D2^\ell b^\kappa\), and group the centers according to \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\) or \(u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell\), stopping when the upper endpoint reaches one.  If a center belongs to group \(\ell\), then
\[
 a_P(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b/2)
 \le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
The centers are \(b/2\)-separated.  Applying \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}profile\text{-}upper}\) at radius \(b/2\), and using the global entropy bound for the terminal group if needed, therefore yields
\[
 M_\ell\le C_\kappa\left\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\right\}.
\]
Polynomial lower mass supplies \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\ge c_mb^\kappa\) in the first group, while group \(\ell\ge1\) has \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)>D2^{\ell-1}b^\kappa\).  Applying the elementary count bound group by group and summing over both sides gives
\[
 \Pr_P(H_b^c)
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \left\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\right\}
 \exp(-c b^{-2}2^\ell)
 \le C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa/b^2}=o(b),
\]
where on \(H_b\) every count is at least half its mean.  Conditionally on the scores and \(H_b\), the Gaussian tail bound at level \(zb\) is
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{max_{t,j}|\overline g_{t,j}-
 \mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)|>zb
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_\kappa\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \left\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\right\}e^{-c z^22^\ell}.
\]
For all \(z\) above a constant depending only on the fixed \(\kappa\), the series is bounded by \(C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa z^2}\).  Integration in \(z\) proves that the conditional expected stochastic maximum is at most \(C_\kappa b\).  The bias is at most \(3Lb\) for the contrast.  On \(H_b^c\), the same bounded-mean and Gaussian-maximal calculation used above gives conditional expected loss at most \(C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}\), whose product with \(C_\kappa e^{-c_\kappa/b^2}\) is \(o(b)\).  Thus
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \le C_\kappa b,
\]
and \(R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\le C_\kappa r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).

Combining the four bounds proves the two asserted matched rates.  The constants are independent of \(n\); the pervasive lower construction can be calibrated uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), since all powers of two and the lower bound for \(\log M/\log n\) are uniformly controlled on this interval.  Both classes impose the identical scalar lower modulus \(c_mh^\kappa\), yet their exact expected-sup rates differ by the factor \((\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\).  Therefore that scalar modulus alone cannot determine the frontier; the declared multiplicity of thin sites distinguishes the two experiments.
