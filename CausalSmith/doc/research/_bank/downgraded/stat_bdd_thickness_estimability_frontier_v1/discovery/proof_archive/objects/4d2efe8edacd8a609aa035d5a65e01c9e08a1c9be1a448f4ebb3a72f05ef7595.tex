Define \(m\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) as in the theorem.  The map \(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) is nondecreasing and strictly positive on \((0,h_0]\).  The constants are those certified by \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\).  That lemma supplies a member for which the polynomial lower-mass inequality holds, while the definition of \(q_{t,P}\) gives \(q_{t,P}(x,h)\le 1\).  Hence \(c_mh^\kappa\le1\) for \(0<h\le h_0\), so \(m:(0,h_0]\to[0,1]\) is well typed.  The displayed indicator defining \(\mathsf J_{-}\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing in \(u\).

First take \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), this law has the sharp-assignment, consistency, boundary-entropy, Lipschitz-regression, bounded-trace, and conditional sub-Gaussian residual properties required by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) gives
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h)
 \quad
 (x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\},\ 0<h\le h_0). \tag{60}
\]
For fixed \(h\), the thin-site set in the definition of \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) is nondecreasing in \(u\); consequently its maximal \(h\)-packing cardinality is nondecreasing in \(u\).  If \(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u)\).  If \(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:pervasive\text{-}thin\text{-}sites}\) and this monotonicity imply
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{61}
\]
Because \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), (61) entails \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\).  Thus (60)--(61), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:declared\text{-}modulus\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:declared\text{-}profile\text{-}lower}\), and \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) yield
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{62}
\]

Now take \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  The base-class definition again gives (60) and all common member properties.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:isolated\text{-}thin\text{-}site}\) gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge1. \tag{63}
\]
The same monotonicity in \(u\), together with \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) below the threshold, verifies the declared lower profile and proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{64}
\]
Equations (62) and (64) prove the asserted union inclusion.  Moreover, \(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) supplies one member of each subclass.  The containing class is therefore nonempty, as required by \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).

We next use the elementary monotonicity of minimax risk.  If nonempty classes satisfy \(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\), then every estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\) obeys
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right].
\]
Taking the infimum over the identical collection of sample-measurable estimators in \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) preserves the inequality.  Apply it to (62), and then invoke the pervasive lower bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{65}
\]

For the reverse inequality, put
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Then \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n/h_0\to\infty\).  Let \(j_n=\lfloor\log_2(h_0/s_n)\rfloor\) and \(h_n=2^{-j_n}h_0\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(1\le j_n\le\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), and \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) therefore gives
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{66}
\]
Since \(h_n\ge s_n\), the polynomial form of \(m\) and \(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C\log n\) imply
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{67}
\]
In particular, the count-qualification inequality \(nm(h_n)\ge A\log(e+n/h_n)\) from \(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\) holds eventually.  Because \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), the upper half of that theorem, evaluated at the admissible pair \(h_n,2h_n\), yields for its explicit clipped count-adaptive estimator \(\overline\tau_n\)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 &\le C\left\{Lh_n+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}\right\}\\
 &\le C\left\{s_n+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}
 =Cs_n. \tag{68}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The estimator \(\overline\tau_n\) is a measurable function of \(\mathcal D_n\) and the known assignment geometry by the construction in that theorem, so it is admissible in the infimum defining \(R_n\).  Consequently
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \le Cs_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{69}
\]
Together, (65) and (69) establish the matched pervasive frontier for the displayed two-argument class.

Finally, the two defined polynomial rates satisfy
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{70}
\]
Thus the declaration \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=\mathbf 1\{u\ge C_mh^\kappa\}\), which guarantees only one thin site at the threshold, does not imply the isolated rate: by (62) it still admits the pervasive subclass, and by (65)--(69) its full class has the pervasive rate.  The upper thin-site restriction in \(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\), absent from \(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), excludes precisely this counterexample.  Hence a lower profile alone cannot express the desired isolated-versus-pervasive distinction; an upper packing restriction or an intrinsic two-sided profile is required.  This proves the asserted negative answer.
