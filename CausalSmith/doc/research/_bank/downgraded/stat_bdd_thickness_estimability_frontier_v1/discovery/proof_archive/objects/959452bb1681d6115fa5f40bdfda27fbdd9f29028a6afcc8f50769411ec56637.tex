Under the partition condition in the statement, \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}assignment}\) gives \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\), so the fit in \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\) averages observations having conditional regression \(g_{t,P}\).  Inspection of \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\), and \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\) shows that the estimator uses only \(\mathcal D_n\), the known geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\).  Neither \(\kappa\) nor the score density enters its formula.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), and write
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid in \(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) contains \(h_*\) satisfying
\[
 r\le h_*<2r.
\]
Indeed, eventually \(h_0/n<r<h_0\), and adjacent grid points differ by a factor two.  Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) be a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\).  It covers \(\mathcal B\), and \(\mathrm{ass:global\text{-}boundary\text{-}entropy}\) gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}/h_*\).  For every \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose a center with \(d(x,z_j)\le h_*/2\).  Then
\[
 B(z_j,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*),
\]
and \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}lower\text{-}mass}\) implies
\[
 \mathbb E_P N_{t,n}(x,h_*)
 \ge n q_{t,P}(z_j,h_*/2)
 \ge n c_m(h_*/2)^\kappa.
\]

For completeness, if \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), then Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-(\log2)N}\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N<nq/2)
 \le \exp\{nq((\log2)/2-1/2)\}
 \le e^{-nq/8}.
\]
Apply this inequality to both sides at all net centers.  Since the count in \(B(x,h_*)\) dominates the corresponding net count in \(B(z_j,h_*/2)\), there is an event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously for every \(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c n c_mh_*^\kappa,
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}\exp(-c n c_mh_*^\kappa).
\]
The constants can be chosen uniformly in \(\kappa\), because \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\).

Uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
 \frac{n h_*^\kappa}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\frac{n r^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \ge c\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus, for every sufficiently large \(n\), \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(G_n\).  The minimizing property in \(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\) then gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}\\
 &\qquad\le Lh_*+C\sigma
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_*)}{n c_mh_*^\kappa}}\\
 &\qquad\le Cr,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last step uses \(h_*<2r\), \(\log(e+n/h_*)\le C\log n\), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\).

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\).  The selected bandwidths are then deterministic.  Polynomial lower mass and the support argument at the start of the proof of \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\) give \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  Hence, whenever the selected count is positive, \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) gives the exact bias bound
\[
 \left|
 \frac1N\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x).
\]
Let
\[
 Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathcal S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\) and the cardinality of \(\mathcal H_n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,ldots,X_n)\le C\sqrt{\log n}.
\]
where \(\mathcal S_n\) denotes the collection of nonempty index sets in \(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\).  Every selected nonempty set belongs to \(\mathcal S_n\).  Since \(h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P\left[
 \left.
 \sup_{x,t}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 \right|X_1,ldots,X_n\right]\\
 &\qquad\le C\sigma\sup_{x,t}
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining the last three displays and summing the two side errors shows that, on \(G_n\), the conditional expected sup loss of \(\widehat\tau_n\) is at most \(Cr\).

On \(G_n^c\), a selected set may arise from an admissible bandwidth or from the fallback \(h_0\).  If it is nonempty, every one of its score points is within \(h_0\) of the target boundary point.  Thus \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\text{-}side\text{-}traces}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:lipschitz\text{-}side\text{-}means}\) bound its conditional mean in absolute value by \(L(1+h_0)<2L\); if it is empty, the frozen fit equals zero.  The same variable \(Z_n\), together with \(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\) for nonempty \(S\), consequently gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \left.\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \right|X_1,ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}
\]
without any conditioning on \(G_n\).  It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\\
 &\quad\le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}
 h_*^{-1}e^{-c n c_mh_*^\kappa}
 \le Cr.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality holds uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\): the exponent is bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(n r^\kappa\), which grows at least polynomially as \((n/\log n)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}\), whereas the prefactor is polynomial in \(n\).  This proves the adaptive upper bound and includes the fallback contribution.

The lower construction in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\) gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}.
\]
Its constants are uniform for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\): choose the tent-scale constant \(a_0<1\) using its worst power \(a_0^4\), use \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\), and use \(\log M/\log n\ge c/(\overline\kappa+2)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  This proves the stated uniform converse.

It remains to bound the fallback probability itself.  Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_{M_0}\) be a maximal \(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net.  Then \(M_0\le2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\), and every \(x\in\mathcal B\) has a center satisfying
\[
 B(z_j,h_0/2)\subseteq B(x,h_0).
\]
For every center and side, polynomial lower mass gives expected count at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^\kappa\).  Uniformly in \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), this exceeds \(16\log(e+n/h_0)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  The binomial bound already derived and a union bound imply that, except on an event of probability at most
\[
 C\exp\{-c n c_m(h_0/2)^\kappa\},
\]
every center-side count is at least \(8\log(e+n/h_0)\).  On that event's complement, the inclusion of balls shows that \(h_0\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\); hence no admissible set is empty.  Finally, \(2^{-\kappa}\ge2^{-\overline\kappa}\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 P\{\exists x\in\mathcal B, t\in\{0,1\}:
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)=\varnothing\}\\
 &\qquad\le C\exp(-c n c_mh_0^\kappa)
 \le Cn^3\exp(-c n c_mh_0^\kappa).
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the claimed operational fallback bound and completes the proof.
