We prove the lower and upper bounds on the same frozen classes.

Choose any law in the nonempty isolated class and retain its score marginal. Giving it zero side regressions and independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) errors produces another law in the same class, because every thickness condition depends only on the unchanged score law. For \(h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\), the isolated-site condition supplies an \(x_h\) with \(a_P(x_h,h)\le C_mh^\kappa\); select a side attaining that minimum. Polynomial lower mass is strictly positive at every smaller radius, so \(x_h\) lies in the selected one-sided support. Add on that side a tent of radius \(h/3\) and height \(Lh/12\). The Lipschitz, trace, and Gaussian calculations in \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) apply to this translated tent: the alternative remains in the same class, its trace contrast is separated from the baseline by \(Lh/12\), and
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{1,h}^{\otimes n},P_{0,h}^{\otimes n})
 \le CnL^2h^2q_{t,P}(x_h,h)/\sigma^2
 \le CnL^2h^{\kappa+2}/\sigma^2.
\]
Take \(a_0>0\) small enough and \(n\) large enough that \(h\le h_0/6\) and the last display is at most \(1/8\). The two-law part of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\ge c h
 =c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For the pervasive lower bound, begin from any score marginal in the nonempty pervasive class and use the same zero-regression baseline. At \(h=a_0(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\), the pervasive-site condition supplies an \(h\)-separated set of \(M\ge c_Jh^{-1}\) sites with \(a_P(x_j,h)\le C_mh^\kappa\). At each site choose a minimizing side and put on it a radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) tent. The supports of distinct tents are disjoint, all alternatives stay in the same class, and their trace contrasts are separated by at least \(Lh/12\) in sup norm. The same Gaussian shift calculation gives
\[
 \max_j\operatorname{KL}(P_{j,h}^{\otimes n},P_{0,h}^{\otimes n})
 \le C a_0^{\kappa+2}\log n.
\]
Global entropy also gives \(M\le C_\mathcal B h^{-1}\), so uniformly for \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), \(\log M\asymp\log n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Taking \(a_0\) small and applying the multiple-law part of the testing lemma gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge c(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]

For the upper bounds we exhibit estimators measurable in the sample and the known boundary. The partition condition in the corrected statement and sharp assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\). Fix a scale \(b\), let \(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) be a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated subset of \(\mathcal B\), and map each \(x\in\mathcal B\) to the least-index center \(z(x)\) within distance \(b/2\). At each center and side, average the observations in \(\mathcal A_t\cap B(z_j,b)\), using zero if the count is zero, and subtract the two side averages. This is one data-driven estimator, not a law-dependent oracle. Whenever the count is nonzero, Lipschitz smoothness and the trace definition give side bias at most
\[
 L\{d(x,z(x))+b\}\le 3Lb/2.
\]

For the pervasive class set \(b=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\). Global boundary entropy gives \(M\le Cb^{-1}\), and polynomial lower mass gives every center-side probability at least \(c_mb^\kappa\). The binomial Chernoff calculation
\[
 \Pr\{N<nq/2\}\le e^{-nq/8}
\]
follows by applying Markov's inequality to \(e^{-sN}\) and optimizing at \(s=\log2\). A union bound therefore shows that all center-side counts exceed half their means except on an event of probability at most \(Cb^{-1}e^{-cnb^\kappa}\). Conditional on the scores and on that event's complement, the Gaussian union bound gives stochastic expected supremum at most
\[
 C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e/b)}{nb^\kappa}}\le Cb,
\]
because \(nb^{\kappa+2}=\log n\). On the exceptional event the conditional expected supremum is at most \(C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log(e/b)}\}\); multiplying by its exponentially small score probability is \(o(b)\). Thus the exhibited estimator has risk at most \(Cb\).

For the isolated class set \(b=n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\). For a fixed side, group centers according to their probability \(q=q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\). Put \(u_0=C_0b^\kappa\), where \(C_0\) is large enough to dominate the lower-mass constants, and use dyadic groups \(u_{\ell-1}<q\le u_\ell\), \(u_\ell=2^\ell u_0\), together with the first group \(q\le u_0\). Because the centers are \(b/2\)-separated and \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\), the isolated profile upper bound implies
\[
 M_\ell\le C\left\{1+b^{-1}(u_\ell/b^2)^{1/(\kappa-2)}\right\}
 \le C\left\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\right\}.
\]
Also \(q\ge c_mb^\kappa\). The same Chernoff bound, summed over groups, makes the probability of any count smaller than half its mean at most
\[
 C\sum_{\ell\ge0}\{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}
 \exp\{-cnb^\kappa2^\ell\}=o(b).
\]
On the complementary event, conditional Gaussian tails and another sum over groups yield
\[
 \mathbb E\max_{j,t}\left|\frac1{N_{t,j}}
 \sum_{i\in S_{t,j}}\varepsilon_i\right|
 \le C\sup_{\ell\ge0}
 \sqrt{\frac{1+\log(1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)})}{nb^\kappa2^\ell}}
 \le Cb,
\]
where \(nb^{\kappa+2}=1\) and \(\sup_{z\ge1}(1+\log z)/z<\infty\). More explicitly, integrating the union bound \(\sum_\ell2M_\ell\exp\{-s^2nb^\kappa2^\ell/(4\sigma^2)\}\) proves the displayed expectation without an extra \(\log\log n\) factor. The exceptional-event contribution is again \(o(b)\). Bias is at most \(3Lb\), so \(R_n(\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{iso}})\le Cb\).

All constants may depend on the fixed envelopes and on \(\overline\kappa\), but not on \(n\). Enlarging them handles the finitely many \(n\) below the asymptotic threshold except that the pervasive rate vanishes at \(n=1\); this is why the corrected statement explicitly says ``for all sufficiently large \(n\).'' The lower and upper displays prove both matched rates. Since both classes have the same scalar lower modulus but their rates differ by a logarithmic factor, no frontier depending only on that scalar modulus can represent both.
