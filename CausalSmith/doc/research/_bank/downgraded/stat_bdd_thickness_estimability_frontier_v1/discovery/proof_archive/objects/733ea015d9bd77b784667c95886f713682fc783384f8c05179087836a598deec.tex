Fix the scores.

For a disk with center \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) and radius \(r\), membership of a fixed point \(x_i\) is equivalent to
\[
 2c^{\mathsf T}x_i+s\ge\lVert x_i\rVert_2^2,
 \qquad s=r^2-\lVert c\rVert_2^2. \tag{53}
\]
Thus every disk trace is a sign pattern induced by \(n\) affine planes in the three-dimensional parameter \((c_1,c_2,s)\). A trace realized by a closed disk can be made strict by increasing the radius by less than the positive minimum distance to the finitely many excluded points, so equality in (53) creates no additional trace.

Let \(A(n,v)\) be the largest number of open cells cut out by \(n\) affine hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^v\). Adding the last hyperplane retains every old cell and splits at most \(A(n-1,v-1)\) of them, because the induced arrangement on that hyperplane has dimension \(v-1\). Hence
\[
 A(n,v)\le A(n-1,v)+A(n-1,v-1),
 \qquad A(0,v)=A(n,0)=1. \tag{54}
\]
Induction in \((n,v)\), using Pascal's identity in (54), gives
\[
 A(n,3)\le\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj. \tag{55}
\]
Restricting the parameters to genuine disks, fixing \(h\), restricting the centers to \(\mathcal B\), or intersecting every trace with the fixed side set \(\{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t\}\) cannot increase the number of traces. Multiplying (55) by the two choices of \(t\) and by \(|\mathcal H_n|\) proves the asserted cardinality bound.

We next prove the stochastic assertion. If \(\mathscr S_n=\varnothing\), the adopted convention makes both stochastic conclusions immediate. Suppose henceforth that \(|\mathscr S_n|\ge1\). By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), conditional on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), the residuals are independent. The replacement condition \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) gives, for every \(i\) that belongs to a set in \(\mathscr S_n\) and every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_i\mid X_i]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda\varepsilon_i}\mid X_i]
 \le e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}. \tag{56}
\]
The complementarity of the assignment regions ensures that \(T_i=t\) whenever \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\), so the relevant residual in (56) is the one used by the corresponding side average. In particular, for every nonempty \(S\in\mathscr S_n\) and \(u\in\mathbb R\), conditional independence and (56) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \exp\!\left\{u\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i}
  {\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}\right\}
  \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 &=\prod_{i\in S}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \exp\!\left\{\frac{u\varepsilon_i}{\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}\right\}
  \ \middle|\ X_i\right]\\
 &\le\prod_{i\in S}\exp\!\left\{\frac{u^2}{2|S|}\right\}
 =e^{u^2/2}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{57}
\]
For \(Z_S=(\sigma\sqrt{|S|})^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\), Markov's inequality and (57), with \(u=z>0\), give
\[
 \Pr_P(Z_S>z\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le e^{-uz}\mathbb E_P[e^{uZ_S}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]
 \le e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{58}
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-Z_S\) and then taking a union bound over \(S\in\mathscr S_n\) proves
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|>z
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le2|\mathscr S_n|e^{-z^2/2}. \tag{59}
\]
No independence among the set-indexed sums is used in this union bound.

Finally set \(z_0=\sqrt{2\log\{2|\mathscr S_n|\}}\). Since \(|\mathscr S_n|\ge1\), one has \(z_0>1\). Integrating (59), using probability at most one below \(z_0\), and using \(\int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\le z_0^{-1}e^{-z_0^2/2}\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}|Z_S|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 &\le z_0+2|\mathscr S_n|\int_{z_0}^{\infty}e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\\
 &\le z_0+z_0^{-1}\le z_0+1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves every assertion.
