\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}waoss\_\allowbreak{}conditional\_\allowbreak{}completion\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Marginal quasi-stayers do not identify the weighted WAOSS singleton claimed by de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024): at a regular heterogeneous-baseline witness, the published completion class contains an affine recession ray and its WAOSS image is all of \(\mathbb R\). The positive result is instead a sharp sensitivity-class characterization after the published singleton failure. Its completion class adds a finite \(K\) conditional mean-movement bound, bounded globally \(L\)-Lipschitz baseline trends, a continuous support version of \(g\), the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition, and a separate realized-baseline conditional-mean identity with a continuous baseline trend; the affine lifts satisfy both trend clauses. Marginal quasi-stayers are not required for this characterization, and its finite attained obstacle interval does not transfer to the unrestricted source class. Baseline-conditional zero support recovers the proceedings paper's weighted singleton formula; arXiv:2201.06898v1 Theorem 3 remains an analogous result for the distinct unweighted target \(\delta_1\). The finite outer program yields a uniformly honest interval on the fixed-support regular subclass, while minimaxity along compatible fixed-support sequences with \(R(P)\to0\) remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The proceedings paper states weighted WAOSS point identification under Assumptions 1--4, but marginal quasi-stayers do not imply the baseline-conditional positivity invoked immediately before display (4) of its Appendix proof. The regular witness gives a generic-class certificate of failure: the published completion class admits a legal affine recession ray whose WAOSS image is \(\mathbb R\). The source's per-dose parallel-trends clause also does not by itself justify substituting the random diagonal dose; the sensitivity model therefore retains that per-dose clause and separately imposes the realized-baseline identity \(E_Q[Y_2^Q(D_1)-Y_1^Q(D_1)\mid X,Z]=m_Q(X)\), with \(m_Q\) a continuous version of the corresponding conditional mean given \(X\).

\paragraph{Niche.} The paper separates three objects that cannot be conflated: the unrestricted published class, which is unbounded at the witness; a bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity completion class with continuous \(g\), the source's per-dose parallel trends, and a separate realized-baseline identity, which has an exact finite obstacle interval; and the baseline-conditional zero-support regime, in which that interval collapses to the published weighted formula. On source-admissible observed laws, only source completions also satisfying the added sensitivity restrictions are characterized; no global class equality or inclusion is asserted.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the source-class recession-ray counterexample and then characterizes the strengthened sensitivity class by a full-observed-law affine lift that satisfies both trend clauses, attained interval geometry, the exact criterion \(R(P)=0\) on the compatible domain, conditional-support singleton recovery, obstacle compatibility and constant-one clipping, certified finite programs, and uniform outer coverage on a fixed rectangle-support subclass. The common-zero-baseline scalar interval from arXiv:2405.04465v4 is retained solely as prior-art validation, and the source paper's separate parametric estimator is outside the challenge.

\section{Related work}

de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024, Assumptions 1--4, Theorem 1, and the Appendix argument immediately before display (4)) state a weighted WAOSS singleton under marginal quasi-stayers, although the proof invokes baseline-conditional positivity. The accepted source-class recession-ray proposition shows that the issue is substantive: at the regular witness the published completion class generates every value in \(\mathbb R\). Only this non-representability result transfers directly to that class. The positive finite interval is a sharp sensitivity-class characterization after the published singleton failure; it adds a finite \(K\) movement bound, bounded globally \(L\)-Lipschitz trends, continuous \(g\), the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition, and a separate realized-baseline identity with a continuous \(m_Q\). The affine lifts satisfy both trend clauses. Under baseline-conditional zero support the interval collapses to the proceedings paper's weighted formula. Section 4.1, Assumption 5, and Theorem 3 of arXiv:2201.06898v1 provide an analogous conditional-support result for the distinct unweighted target \(\delta_1\). The common-zero-baseline bounded-slope interval of de Chaisemartin, Ciccia, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Knau (2024, arXiv:2405.04465v4, Section 4.2.1, Theorem 4, and Appendix B.2) is prior art used only as an algebraic sanity check. Andrews and Shi (2013) provide general conditional-moment-inequality inference, rather than the necessary-and-sufficient support-local obstacle certificate proved here. Beresteanu, Molchanov, and Molinari (2011) develop convex sharp-region logic; Mogstad, Santos, and Torgovitsky (2018) optimize weighted functionals under convex restrictions; and Freyberger and Horowitz (2015) obtain linear-program bounds on finite discrete support. Voronin (2025) studies values of programs that are already finite. None of those results supplies the exact clipping and Hausdorff certificate taking this continuum WAOSS class to a mesh. Freyberger (2017) studies completeness and consistency in nonparametric instrumental-variable models and data-driven bounds on identified-set diameter, not support-local WAOSS obstacles or endpoint inference. Sasaki and Ura (2025) develop bias correction and limit theory for point-identified slow-mover panel estimands, not this partially identified completion frontier. For inference, Kaido, Molinari, and Stoye (2019) establish uniform calibrated projection for finite moment systems, Belloni, Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2015) give uniform series-regression theory, and Cattaneo, Chandak, Jansson, and Ma (2024) give support-edge-adaptive conditional-density inference. The inference theorem makes no general advance in those component theories: for this WAOSS model it narrowly composes a primitive support-edge cell-mean band, a signed-measure band, the certified continuum discretization, a denominator band, and measurable ratio optimization. The proceedings paper's separate parametric estimator is not invalidated, and no current result supplies the open fixed-support minimax lower bound over compatible laws.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(\mathcal Z_n\sim P^{\otimes n}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-stayers]\label{ass:no-stayers}
\(P(Z=0)=0\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (no-stayer condition, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:marginal-quasi-stayers]\label{ass:marginal-quasi-stayers}
\(P(|Z|\le\eta)>0\quad\forall\eta>0\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (marginal quasi-stayer condition, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:continuous-regression]\label{ass:continuous-regression}
\(g\in C(H)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This support-version regularity is imposed transparently beyond the proceedings paper. It holds, for example, in location-regression data-generating processes \(\Delta Y=g(X,Z)+\varepsilon\) with \(E[\varepsilon\mid X,Z]=0\) and a continuous structural mean on each compact observed component; it does not require zero treatment change to be observed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-integrability]\label{ass:observed-integrability}
\(E_P[|Y_1|+|Y_2|]<\infty\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (integrable observed outcomes, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-law-match]\label{ass:observed-law-match}
\(\mathcal L_Q(Y_1,Y_2,D_1,D_2)=P\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (observed-law compatibility, \cite{BeresteanuMolchanovMolinari2011ConvexMomentPredictions}).

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y_1=Y_1^Q(D_1)\) and \(Y_2=Y_2^Q(D_2)\), \(Q\)-almost surely
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (potential-outcome consistency, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-integrability]\label{ass:latent-integrability}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (integrable potential outcomes, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-parallel-trends]\label{ass:conditional-parallel-trends}
For every fixed \(d\in X_0\), there are versions of the conditional expectations such that
\[
E_Q\!\left[Y_2^Q(d)-Y_1^Q(d)\mid X,Z\right]
=E_Q\!\left[Y_2^Q(d)-Y_1^Q(d)\mid X\right]
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (per-dose conditional parallel trends, \cite{deChaisemartinDHaultfoeuilleVazquezBare2024NoStayers}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mean-movement-bound]\label{ass:mean-movement-bound}
The conditional mean of the integrable realized causal movement satisfies
\[
\left|E_Q\!\left[Y_2^Q(D_2)-Y_2^Q(D_1)\mid X,Z\right]\right|
\leq K|Z|
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This heterogeneous-baseline conditional mean-movement restriction is substantive and is not inherited from the common-zero-baseline bounded-CAS result. It is satisfied by random-coefficient location models \(Y_2^Q(d)=a(X,U)+c(X,U)d\) with \(|c(X,U)|\le K\) almost surely, and more generally by absolutely continuous dose-response schedules whose individual marginal effects are bounded by \(K\). Such caps are credible for policy doses or temperature exposures over a prespecified compact range.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:trend-bound]\label{ass:trend-bound}
\(\|m_Q\|_\infty\le M\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded baseline trend, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018OptimalInference}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:trend-lipschitz]\label{ass:trend-lipschitz}
\(m_Q\in\{v\in C(X_0):|v(x)-v(x')|\le L|x-x'|\ \forall x,x'\in X_0\}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (continuous Lipschitz shape restriction, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2018OptimalInference}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rectangle-support]\label{ass:rectangle-support}
\(H=\bigcup_{R\in\mathscr R}R\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed finite-rectangle support, \cite{CattaneoChandakJanssonMa2024BoundaryConditionalDensity}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-lower]\label{ass:density-lower}
\(\inf_{(x,z)\in H}f_{XZ}(x,z)\ge f_0\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (density bounded away from zero, \cite{CattaneoChandakJanssonMa2024BoundaryConditionalDensity}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
\(P(|Y_1|\le B,\ |Y_2|\le B)=1\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded outcomes, \cite{KaidoMolinariStoye2019CalibratedProjection}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-regression]\label{ass:holder-regression}
\(|g(x,z)-g(x',z')|\le G\|(x,z)-(x',z')\|^\beta\quad\text{within each connected component of }H\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed Hölder regression class, \cite{BelloniChernozhukovChetverikovKato2015Series}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:denominator-separated]\label{ass:denominator-separated}
\(A\ge a_0\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (denominator separation, \cite{GrahamPowell2012IrregularCRC}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity completion class is
\[

On an observed law satisfying the published assumptions, comparison with the source class concerns only source completions that also satisfy these additional restrictions. No global equality or inclusion of completion classes is asserted, and the finite sharp interval for this sensitivity class does not transfer upward to the unrestricted published class.

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:full-law-sharpness]\label{thm:full-law-sharpness}
Under the declared observed-law conditions, the source's per-dose conditional-parallel-trends condition, and the separate realized-baseline identity, the bounded-slope/Lipschitz completion class \(\mathfrak Q(P)\) is nonempty if and only if \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\). Whenever these sets are nonempty,
\[
\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}=\Theta(P).
\]
For every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), the affine one-argument static-schedule lift produces \(Q_u\in\mathfrak Q(P)\), preserves the entire joint law \(P\), satisfies \(m_{Q_u}=u\in C(X_0)\), and makes both period schedules individually \(K\)-Lipschitz. Moreover, on one common full-probability event,
\[
Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)=\Delta Y-g(X,Z)+u(X)
\qquad\text{for every }d\in I.
\]
Consequently the lift satisfies the per-dose parallel-trends condition for every fixed dose and, separately, the realized-baseline identity; in fact its displayed equality yields common versions for all \(d\in I\). It also satisfies \(\theta(Q_u)=\{T-J_P(u)\}/A\). This theorem does not require marginal quasi-stayers. Its model is not the class defined by Assumptions 1--4 of de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024): the theorem additionally imposes realized-diagonal and movement integrability, the finite \(K\) conditional mean-movement bound, bounded globally \(L\)-Lipschitz baseline trends, continuity of the observed regression version \(g\), and the explicit realized-baseline identity. Thus no equality or global inclusion of the two observed-law models is asserted. On an observed law that satisfies the published assumptions, \(\mathfrak Q(P)\) is only the restricted slice satisfying these extra conditions, and \(\Theta(P)\) is not asserted to be the published-class identified set. The result is a sharp bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity repair after failure of the published singleton claim, not a sharp characterization of the unrestricted published class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because the declared observed law \(P\) is supported on \(\mathbb R^2\times I^2\) and \(I\) is compact,
\[
|Z|=|D_2-D_1|\leq\operatorname{diam}(I)
\qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The no-stayer condition gives \(|Z|>0\) almost surely, and hence
\[
0<A=E_P|Z|\leq\operatorname{diam}(I)<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
Observed integrability gives
\[
E_P|\Delta Y|\leq E_P(|Y_1|+|Y_2|)<\infty.
\]
Since \(|S|=1\) almost surely under the no-stayer condition,
\[
|T|=|E_P[S\Delta Y]|\leq E_P|\Delta Y|<\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Thus all ratios used below have a finite, strictly positive denominator.

Take \(Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\), and abbreviate
\[
B_Q=Y_2^Q(D_1)-Y_1^Q(D_1),
\qquad
C_Q=Y_2^Q(D_2)-Y_2^Q(D_1).
\]
The two variables are integrable by the explicit realized-diagonal and causal-movement requirements in \(\text{def:legal-completion-class}\). Consistency and the observed-law match give
\[
\Delta Y=C_Q+B_Q
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{3}
\]
The separate realized-baseline identity gives directly, without substituting a random dose into the per-dose parallel-trends condition,
\[
E_Q[B_Q\mid X,Z]=m_Q(X)
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]
Because \(Q\) matches the complete observed law \(P\), the declared support version \(g\) is also a version of \(E_Q[\Delta Y\mid X,Z]\). Taking conditional expectations in (3), using linearity for the integrable summands and then (4), yields
\[
E_Q[C_Q\mid X,Z]=g(X,Z)-m_Q(X)
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{5}
\]
The mean-movement bound therefore implies
\[
|g(x,z)-m_Q(x)|\leq K|z|
\qquad P_{XZ}\text{-almost everywhere}. \tag{6}
\]
The function
\[
(x,z)\longmapsto |g(x,z)-m_Q(x)|-K|z|
\]
is continuous on \(H\): \(g\in C(H)\), and the continuous-version condition gives \(m_Q\in C(X_0)\). If this function were positive at some \((x_0,z_0)\in H=\operatorname{supp}_P(X,Z)\), continuity would make it positive on the intersection of \(H\) with an open neighborhood of \((x_0,z_0)\). By the definition of support that neighborhood has positive \(P_{XZ}\)-probability, contradicting (6). Hence
\[
|g(x,z)-m_Q(x)|\leq K|z|
\qquad\text{for every }(x,z)\in H. \tag{7}
\]
The level and global Lipschitz restrictions on \(m_Q\) are also member conditions of \(\mathfrak Q(P)\). Comparing them and (7) with \(\text{def:feasible-trend-class}\) proves
\[
m_Q\in\mathcal C_P. \tag{8}
\]
In particular, \(\mathfrak Q(P)\neq\varnothing\) implies \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).

We next derive the causal-functional relationship. By the causal definition of \(\theta(Q)\), equation (3), the bounded \((X,Z)\)-measurable variable \(S\), equation (4), and iterated expectations,
\[
\begin{aligned}
A\theta(Q)
&=E_Q[SC_Q]\\
&=E_P[S\Delta Y]-E_Q[SB_Q]\\
&=T-E_Q\!\left[S E_Q[B_Q\mid X,Z]\right]\\
&=T-E_P[Sm_Q(X)].
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
For every bounded Borel function \(v\) on \(X_0\), the definition
\(\nu_P(B)=E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B\}]\), first for simple functions and then by bounded convergence, gives
\[
\int_{X_0}v(x)\,d\nu_P(x)=E_P[Sv(X)]. \tag{10}
\]
Applying (10) to the bounded continuous function \(m_Q\), and then using \(\text{def:signed-functional}\), turns (9) into
\[
\theta(Q)=\frac{T-J_P(m_Q)}{A}. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (8) and (11), followed by \(\text{def:waoss-set}\), prove
\[
\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}\subseteq\Theta(P). \tag{12}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, fix arbitrary \(u\in\mathcal C_P\). Retain the observed vector on its \(P\)-probability space. On \(\{Z\neq0\}\), define
\[
b_u=\frac{g(X,Z)-u(X)}{Z},
\]
and put \(b_u=0\) on the null event \(\{Z=0\}\). The construction is measurable, and the support obstacle in \(\text{def:feasible-trend-class}\) gives
\[
|b_u|\leq K
\qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{13}
\]
For every \(d\in I\), define the affine schedules from \(\text{def:affine-lift}\):
\[
Y_1^{Q_u}(d)=Y_1+b_u(d-D_1),
\qquad
Y_2^{Q_u}(d)=Y_2+b_u(d-D_2). \tag{14}
\]
They are jointly measurable in the random argument and \(d\). At the realized doses,
\[
Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)=Y_1,
\qquad
Y_2^{Q_u}(D_2)=Y_2. \tag{15}
\]
Thus the complete observed law remains \(P\), and consistency holds. For each fixed \(d\in I\), compactness of \(I\), (13), and observed integrability imply
\[
|Y_t^{Q_u}(d)|\leq |Y_t|+K\operatorname{diam}(I),
\qquad
E_{Q_u}|Y_t^{Q_u}(d)|<\infty. \tag{16}
\]
Moreover, for \(d,d'\in I\),
\[
|Y_t^{Q_u}(d)-Y_t^{Q_u}(d')|
=|b_u|\,|d-d'|
\leq K|d-d'|, \tag{17}
\]
so each period schedule is individually \(K\)-Lipschitz.

Direct subtraction in (14) gives, pointwise off \(\{Z=0\}\) and simultaneously for all \(d\in I\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)
&=\Delta Y+b_u(D_1-D_2)\\
&=\Delta Y-b_uZ\\
&=\Delta Y-g(X,Z)+u(X).
\end{aligned} \tag{18}
\]
The right side is integrable because
\[
E_P|g(X,Z)|
\leq E_P\!\left[E_P(|\Delta Y|\mid X,Z)\right]
=E_P|\Delta Y|<\infty
\]
and \(|u(X)|\leq M\). Since \(g(X,Z)\) is a version of \(E_P[\Delta Y\mid X,Z]\), (18) gives, for every fixed \(d\in I\),
\[
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)\mid X,Z]=u(X). \tag{19}
\]
Taking the conditional expectation of (19) given \(X\) gives
\[
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)\mid X]=u(X). \tag{20}
\]
Thus (19)--(20) verify the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition. In fact, because the random difference in (18) is independent of \(d\), the displayed versions can be chosen identically for every \(d\in I\); this stronger fact is a property derived for the lift, not an assumption used to evaluate a random diagonal.

Taking \(d=D_1=X\) in the pointwise schedule identity (18), rather than in a fixed-dose conditional-expectation statement, gives
\[
Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)-Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)
=\Delta Y-g(X,Z)+u(X). \tag{21}
\]
Its integrability was proved above. Conditioning (21) on \((X,Z)\) and on \(X\), respectively, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)-Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)\mid X,Z]&=u(X),\\
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)-Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)\mid X]&=u(X).
\end{aligned} \tag{22}
\]
Because \(u\in C(X_0)\), equation (22) proves both that \(m_{Q_u}=u\) is a continuous version and that the separate realized-baseline identity holds. Its level and global \(L\)-Lipschitz restrictions follow from \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).

The realized period-two causal movement is
\[
Y_2^{Q_u}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)
=b_uZ=g(X,Z)-u(X). \tag{23}
\]
By feasibility and bounded treatment support,
\[
|g(X,Z)-u(X)|\leq K|Z|\leq K\operatorname{diam}(I), \tag{24}
\]
so the movement is integrable. Since it is \((X,Z)\)-measurable, (24) also gives
\[
\left|E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)\mid X,Z]\right|
=|g(X,Z)-u(X)|
\leq K|Z|. \tag{25}
\]
Equations (13)--(25) verify every member condition in \(\text{def:legal-completion-class}\). Therefore
\[
Q_u\in\mathfrak Q(P), \tag{26}
\]
which also proves \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\Rightarrow\mathfrak Q(P)\neq\varnothing\).

Finally, the causal definition, (23), and iterated expectations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta(Q_u)
&=\frac{E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}]}{A}\\
&=\frac{E_P[S\Delta Y]-E_P[Su(X)]}{A}\\
&=\frac{T-J_P(u)}{A},
\end{aligned} \tag{27}
\]
where the second equality uses \(E_P[Sg(X,Z)]=E_P[S\Delta Y]\) and the third uses (10). Since \(u\) was arbitrary, (26)--(27) prove \(\Theta(P)\subseteq\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}\). Combined with (12), this proves the sharp set equality and full-law attainment.

No step uses marginal quasi-stayers. The forward direction uses the realized-baseline identity directly; the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition is retained as a class restriction but is not misused to justify a random-diagonal substitution. The affine lifts satisfy both clauses, as (19)--(22) show. The finite movement bound, bounded globally Lipschitz trend, continuous support version of \(g\), explicit integrability conditions, and realized-baseline identity remain additional sensitivity restrictions. Therefore the equality proved here characterizes \(\mathfrak Q(P)\) only and does not transfer the finite interval to the unrestricted published completion class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the causal kernel: it replaces an unsupported conditional limit by a two-sided, full-observed-law sharp characterization. \textit{Gap.} Assumption 4 of de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024) imposes only \(P(|\Delta D|\le\eta)>0\), while the Appendix proof of Theorem 1 invokes \(P(|\Delta D|\le\eta\mid D_1)>0\) immediately before display (4). Under the source's parallel-trends condition on the baseline-treatment support, the present theorem gives a sharp sensitivity-class characterization after failure of the published singleton claim. Assumption 5 of de Chaisemartin et al. (arXiv:2201.06898v1, Section 4.1) supplies an analogous baseline-conditional support regime, but its Theorem 3 identifies the unweighted switcher-slope target \(\delta_1\), not WAOSS. \textit{Consumer.} Applied continuous-dose DiD analyses with changing temperatures can report a robustness set without imposing a parametric extrapolation across baseline-specific support holes.

\begin{theorem}[thm:attained-interval]\label{thm:attained-interval}
If \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), then \(\mathcal C_P\) is compact and convex in \(C(X_0)\), the extrema \(V_P^-\) and \(V_P^+\) are attained, and \(\Theta(P)=[(T-V_P^+)/A,(T-V_P^-)/A]\subseteq[-K,K]\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because \(X\in I\) almost surely and \(I\) is compact, \(X_0=\operatorname{supp}_P(X)\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(I\).  Every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) satisfies
\[
 \|u\|_\infty\leq M,
 \qquad
 |u(x)-u(x')|\leq L|x-x'|
 \quad(x,x'\in X_0).
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C_P\) is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous.  It is closed in the uniform norm: if \(u_j\in\mathcal C_P\) and \(\|u_j-u\|_\infty\to0\), passage to the limit in each defining inequality gives, for all \(x,x'\in X_0\) and \((x,z)\in H\),
\[
 |u(x)|\leq M,
 \qquad
 |u(x)-u(x')|\leq L|x-x'|,
 \qquad
 |g(x,z)-u(x)|\leq K|z|.
\]
Hence \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).

For completeness, compactness follows without invoking an undeclared compactness theorem.  Given a sequence \((u_j)\) in \(\mathcal C_P\), choose a countable dense set \(D\subseteq X_0\).  Boundedness and successive subsequence extraction give a subsequence \((u_{j_k})\) converging at every point of \(D\).  If \(L>0\), for any \(\eta>0\) choose a finite \(\eta/(3L)\)-net \(D_\eta\subseteq D\).  For sufficiently large \(k,k'\), convergence on this finite set gives
\[
 \max_{d\in D_\eta}|u_{j_k}(d)-u_{j_{k'}}(d)|<\eta/3.
\]
For \(x\in X_0\), choose \(d\in D_\eta\) with \(|x-d|<\eta/(3L)\).  Then
\[
 |u_{j_k}(x)-u_{j_{k'}}(x)|
 \leq L|x-d|+|u_{j_k}(d)-u_{j_{k'}}(d)|+L|d-x|<\eta.
\]
If \(L=0\), all the functions are constant on \(X_0\), and convergence at one point gives the same uniform Cauchy conclusion.  Thus the chosen subsequence converges uniformly.  Closedness places its limit in \(\mathcal C_P\).  Sequential compactness is compactness in the metric induced by the uniform norm, so \(\mathcal C_P\) is compact.  Every defining inequality is preserved under convex combinations; hence \(\mathcal C_P\) is convex.

For every finite Borel partition \((B_j)_{j=1}^m\) of \(X_0\), the definition of \(\nu_P\), the triangle inequality, and \(|S|=1\) almost surely give
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m|\nu_P(B_j)|
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\left|E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B_j\}]\right|
 \leq E_P\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf 1\{X\in B_j\}\right]=1.
\]
Therefore \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), and the definition of \(J_P\) implies
\[
 |J_P(u)-J_P(v)|
 \leq \|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|u-v\|_\infty
 \leq\|u-v\|_\infty.
\]
Thus \(J_P\) is continuous.  On the assumed nonempty compact class it attains its infimum and supremum, which equal \(V_P^-\) and \(V_P^+\) by \(\text{def:frontier-values}\).  Linearity of \(J_P\) and convexity of \(\mathcal C_P\) make \(J_P(\mathcal C_P)\) convex; compactness and nonemptiness then give
\[
 J_P(\mathcal C_P)=[V_P^-,V_P^+].
\]

The no-stayer condition used by \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), together with boundedness of \(Z\in I-I\), gives
\[
 0<A=E_P|Z|\leq\operatorname{diam}(I)<\infty.
\]
The affine map \(j\mapsto(T-j)/A\) is continuous and reverses order.  The definition of \(\Theta(P)\), equivalently the sharp characterization in \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), therefore yields
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right].
\]
It remains to establish the asserted deterministic bound.  Since \(S\) is measurable with respect to \((X,Z)\), the defining conditional-mean property of \(g\) gives
\[
 T=E_P[S\Delta Y]=E_P[Sg(X,Z)].
\]
Also, the definition of \(\nu_P\) gives \(J_P(u)=E_P[Su(X)]\).  Hence, for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), its support-cone restriction implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |T-J_P(u)|
 &=\left|E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}]\right|\\
 &\leq E_P|g(X,Z)-u(X)|
 \leq K E_P|Z|=KA.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Division by \(A>0\) proves \((T-J_P(u))/A\in[-K,K]\) for every feasible \(u\), and therefore \(\Theta(P)\subseteq[-K,K]\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Attainment turns an abstract image into reportable sharp endpoints and rules out closure-only bounds. \textit{Gap.} Finite-support shape-restriction results attain LP endpoints, but the present feasible object is a continuous function constrained by support-local cones. \textit{Consumer.} Endpoint attainment supplies explicit least-favorable causal completions for robustness reporting and specification diagnostics.

\begin{theorem}[thm:obstacle-compatibility]\label{thm:obstacle-compatibility}
The envelopes satisfy \(\mathcal C_P=\{u\in C(X_0):\operatorname{Lip}(u)\le L,\ \ell_P\le u\le r_P\}\), and \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\) if and only if \(\ell_P(x)\le r_P(x)\) for every \(x\in X_0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the declared support types, \(X_0\) is compact and \(H\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(I\times(I-I)\).  Moreover, if \((d,z)\in H\), then \(d\in X_0\).  Indeed, let \(U\) be any relative open neighborhood of \(d\) in \(I\), and let \(V\) be any relative open neighborhood of \(z\) in \(I-I\).  Since \((d,z)\in\operatorname{supp}_P(X,Z)\),
\[
P\{(X,Z)\in U\times V\}>0.
\]
Therefore \(P(X\in U)>0\).  This holds for every such \(U\), so \(d\in\operatorname{supp}_P(X)=X_0\).

By \(\mathrm{ass:continuous\mbox{-}regression}\), \(g\) is continuous on the compact set \(H\), so there is a finite constant \(G_0\) such that \(|g(d,z)|\le G_0\) for every \((d,z)\in H\).  Compactness of \(I\) also bounds \(|z|\) and \(|x-d|\) on \(H\times X_0\).  Consequently, the supremum and infimum in \(\mathrm{def:obstacle\mbox{-}envelopes}\) are finite at every \(x\in X_0\).

For each \((d,z)\in H\), introduce the functions
\[
a_{d,z}(x)=g(d,z)-K|z|-L|x-d|,
\qquad
c_{d,z}(x)=g(d,z)+K|z|+L|x-d|.
\]
For all \(x,x'\in X_0\), the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
|a_{d,z}(x)-a_{d,z}(x')|
=L\bigl||x-d|-|x'-d|\bigr|
\le L|x-x'|,
\]
and the same bound holds for \(c_{d,z}\).  If every member \(f_j\) of a pointwise finite family is \(L\)-Lipschitz, then
\[
\sup_j f_j(x)-\sup_j f_j(x')
\le \sup_j\{f_j(x)-f_j(x')\}
\le L|x-x'|.
\]
Interchanging \(x\) and \(x'\) proves that \(\sup_j f_j\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz.  Applying the same argument to \(-f_j\) proves the claim for a pointwise infimum.  Finally, the scalar maps \(v\mapsto\max\{-M,v\}\) and \(v\mapsto\min\{M,v\}\) are one-Lipschitz.  Therefore \(\ell_P\) and \(r_P\), as defined in \(\mathrm{def:obstacle\mbox{-}envelopes}\), are finite-valued and \(L\)-Lipschitz on \(X_0\), and in particular belong to \(C(X_0)\).

We now prove the asserted class equality.  Let \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  Fix \(x\in X_0\) and \((d,z)\in H\).  Since \(d\in X_0\), the Lipschitz and support-cone clauses of \(\mathrm{def:feasible\mbox{-}trend\mbox{-}class}\) give
\[
u(x)
\ge u(d)-L|x-d|
\ge g(d,z)-K|z|-L|x-d|
=a_{d,z}(x).
\]
The level clause gives \(u(x)\ge -M\).  Taking the supremum over \((d,z)\in H\) and then the maximum with \(-M\) yields
\[
u(x)\ge \ell_P(x).
\]
Likewise,
\[
u(x)
\le u(d)+L|x-d|
\le g(d,z)+K|z|+L|x-d|
=c_{d,z}(x),
\]
and \(u(x)\le M\).  Taking the infimum over \((d,z)\in H\) and then the minimum with \(M\) yields
\[
u(x)\le r_P(x).
\]
Because \(x\) was arbitrary and membership in \(\mathcal C_P\) already imposes continuity and Lipschitz constant at most \(L\), we obtain
\[
\mathcal C_P
\subseteq
\{u\in C(X_0):\operatorname{Lip}(u)\le L,\ \ell_P\le u\le r_P\}.
\]

Conversely, let \(u\in C(X_0)\) satisfy \(\operatorname{Lip}(u)\le L\) and \(\ell_P\le u\le r_P\), where the inequalities are pointwise.  The outer truncations in \(\mathrm{def:obstacle\mbox{-}envelopes}\) imply
\[
\ell_P(x)\ge -M,
\qquad
r_P(x)\le M
\qquad (x\in X_0).
\]
Hence \(-M\le u(x)\le M\) for every \(x\in X_0\), so \(\|u\|_\infty\le M\).  For an arbitrary \((d,z)\in H\), evaluating the defining supremum and infimum at that same support point gives
\[
\ell_P(d)
\ge g(d,z)-K|z|-L|d-d|
=g(d,z)-K|z|
\]
and
\[
r_P(d)
\le g(d,z)+K|z|+L|d-d|
=g(d,z)+K|z|.
\]
Combining these inequalities with \(\ell_P(d)\le u(d)\le r_P(d)\) yields
\[
g(d,z)-K|z|\le u(d)\le g(d,z)+K|z|,
\]
or equivalently
\[
|g(d,z)-u(d)|\le K|z|.
\]
Thus \(u\) satisfies the level, Lipschitz, and support-cone clauses of \(\mathrm{def:feasible\mbox{-}trend\mbox{-}class}\), and therefore \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  This proves
\[
\mathcal C_P
=
\{u\in C(X_0):\operatorname{Lip}(u)\le L,\ \ell_P\le u\le r_P\}.
\]

If \(\mathcal C_P\ne\varnothing\), choose \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  The class equality just proved gives
\[
\ell_P(x)\le u(x)\le r_P(x)
\qquad\text{for every }x\in X_0,
\]
so \(\ell_P\le r_P\) pointwise.  Conversely, suppose \(\ell_P(x)\le r_P(x)\) for every \(x\in X_0\).  Set \(u=\ell_P\).  The first part of the proof shows that \(u\in C(X_0)\) and \(\operatorname{Lip}(u)\le L\); the assumed order gives \(\ell_P\le u\le r_P\).  The established class equality then implies \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  Hence \(\mathcal C_P\ne\varnothing\), completing both directions of the compatibility criterion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result converts latent-model compatibility into an observable necessary-and-sufficient envelope test. \textit{Gap.} Existing conditional-moment methods provide general tests, but no exact obstacle certificate for these coupled baseline-specific cones. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers can distinguish rejection of the maintained bounded-trend model from mere failure of point identification.

\begin{proposition}[prop:point-identification]\label{prop:point-identification}
If \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), then WAOSS is point identified if and only if \(\Theta(P)\) is a singleton, if and only if \(V_P^+=V_P^-\), if and only if \(R(P)=0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) and \(A>0\),
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right].
\]
Its length is
\[
 \frac{T-V_P^-}{A}-\frac{T-V_P^+}{A}
 =\frac{V_P^+-V_P^-}{A}
 =\frac{R(P)}{A},
\]
where the last equality is \(\text{def:frontier-values}\).  A nonempty closed interval is a singleton exactly when its length is zero.  Because \(A>0\), this is equivalent successively to \(V_P^+=V_P^-\) and to \(R(P)=0\).  Finally, \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), inherited through \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\), identifies \(\Theta(P)\) with \(\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}\).  Thus singleton geometry is exactly point identification of the causal WAOSS functional, rather than uniqueness of an unrelated optimization functional.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This corollary records the exact singleton condition implied by the attained endpoint formula and is not presented as an independent diagnostic or novelty claim. \textit{Gap.} The published conditional-support results give sufficient singleton regimes; this algebraic corollary only records when the new sharp interval collapses. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers can report whether the computed sharp obstacle interval is a singleton without attributing additional identifying content to a reparameterized width condition.

\begin{proposition}[prop:nested-conditional-support]\label{prop:nested-conditional-support}
Let \(M_{\delta}=\mathbf 1\{|Z|>\delta\}\), and suppose \(P(M_{\delta}=1\mid X)<1\) almost surely for every \(\delta>0\), so thresholds with empty mover events are harmless. This baseline-conditional support condition parallels Assumption 5 in de Chaisemartin et al. (arXiv:2201.06898v1, Section 4.1). Under compatibility, the declared continuous support version of \(g\), and parallel trends on \(X_0\), it implies \(\mathcal C_P=\{x\mapsto g(x,0)\}\), \(R(P)=0\), and \(\Theta(P)=\{[T-\int g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x)]/A\}\). This weighted WAOSS singleton recovers the quasi-stayer-limit formula in Theorem 1 of de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024) under the stronger baseline-conditional support condition. Theorem 3 of arXiv:2201.06898v1 is an analogous conditional-support result for the unweighted switcher-slope target \(\delta_1\); this proposition does not claim to nest that estimand. Failure of baseline-conditional support on a set of baseline values having positive \(P\)-probability can yield \(R(P)>0\) and a non-singleton \(\Theta(P)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each positive integer \(k\), choose a regular conditional probability and define
\[
 q_k(x)=P(|Z|\leq 1/k\mid X=x).
\]
Since \(M_{1/k}=\mathbf 1\{|Z|>1/k\}\), the assumed inequality \(P(M_{1/k}=1\mid X)<1\) says that \(q_k(X)>0\) almost surely.  Fix \(x\in X_0\), and put \(U_k=(x-1/k,x+1/k)\).  The definition of support gives \(P(X\in U_k)>0\), while positivity of \(q_k\) for \(P_X\)-almost every baseline gives
\[
 P(X\in U_k,|Z|\leq1/k)
 =\int_{U_k}q_k(x')\,dP_X(x')>0.
\]
Every positive-probability Borel set meets the support \(H=\operatorname{supp}_P(X,Z)\).  Hence there is \((x_k,z_k)\in H\) with \(|x_k-x|<1/k\) and \(|z_k|\leq1/k\).  Thus \((x_k,z_k)\to(x,0)\).  Since \(H\) is closed,
\[
 (x,0)\in H\qquad(x\in X_0).
\]
This support argument uses only the countable thresholds \(1/k\) and never conditions on a null singleton.

Under the stated compatibility clause, \(\mathfrak Q(P)\neq\varnothing\); by \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  For any \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), applying its support-cone inequality at \((x,0)\) gives
\[
 |g(x,0)-u(x)|\leq K|0|=0
 \qquad(x\in X_0).
\]
Every feasible function is therefore \(g(\cdot,0)\), and nonemptiness shows that this function is itself feasible.  Consequently
\[
 \mathcal C_P=\{x\mapsto g(x,0)\},
 \qquad
 V_P^-=V_P^+=\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x),
 \qquad
 R(P)=0.
\]
Substitution into \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) yields
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left\{\frac{T-\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x)}{A}\right\}.
\]
This is the weighted WAOSS quasi-stayer-limit formula identified in Theorem 1 of de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024).  The comparison to Assumption 5 and Theorem 3 of arXiv:2201.06898v1 is only a comparison of conditional-support mechanisms: that theorem concerns the distinct unweighted target \(\delta_1\), and no equality of estimands is used here.

For the final possibility claim, \(\text{prop:source-class-recession-ray}\) supplies the declared continuous law \(P^{\circ}\).  At that law, baseline-conditional zero support fails on a set of \(P_X^{\circ}\)-probability \(p_{\circ}>0\), whereas the bounded sensitivity class has
\[
 \Theta(P^{\circ})=
 \left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
        \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]
This interval is nondegenerate.  The already proved equivalence in \(\text{prop:point-identification}\) therefore gives \(R(P^{\circ})>0\).  This proves that failure of conditional support can produce a positive radius; it does not assert that every such failure does so.
\end{proof}

Neither gives the sharp weighted WAOSS set when baseline-conditional support fails. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers can use one support diagnostic to decide whether to report the published singleton formula or the sharp obstacle interval.

\begin{proposition}[prop:continuous-hole-witness]\label{prop:continuous-hole-witness}
For every \(p_{\circ}\in(0,1)\), the \((X,Z)\)-marginal of \(P^{\circ}\) is absolutely continuous with density bounded and strictly positive on each of its two support rectangles, while observed outcomes are bounded, exact stayers have probability zero, and marginal quasi-stayers exist. Conditional quasi-stayer support fails for every \(x\in[-1,0)\), a baseline region of probability \(p_{\circ}\). The observed objects are \(X_0=[-1,1]\), \(H=[-1,0]\times[1/2,1]\cup[0,1]\times[-1/4,1/4]\), \(g=0\), \(A=(1+5p_{\circ})/8\), \(T=0\), and \(d\nu_P(x)=p_{\circ}\mathbf 1\{x\in[-1,0]\}dx\). The obstacle envelopes equal \(r_P(x)=-\ell_P(x)=\min\{-x,1/2\}\) on \([-1,0]\) and vanish on \([0,1]\), yielding \(\Theta(P^{\circ})=[-3p_{\circ}/(1+5p_{\circ}),3p_{\circ}/(1+5p_{\circ})]\). The displayed affine laws \(Q_+^{\circ}\) and \(Q_-^{\circ}\) both belong to the continuous-trend legal completion class, preserve the entire observed law, and satisfy parallel trends on the baseline-treatment support and the stronger all-\(I\) equality because their period differences are respectively \(u_+^{\circ}(X)\) and \(u_-^{\circ}(X)\) at every dose. They satisfy the new conditional mean-movement bound and attain respectively \(-3p_{\circ}/(1+5p_{\circ})\) and \(3p_{\circ}/(1+5p_{\circ})\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each support rectangle in \(\text{def:continuous-hole-witness}\) has Lebesgue area \(1/2\).  Their density integrals are therefore \(p_{\circ}\) and \(1-p_{\circ}\), so the displayed density integrates to one, is bounded, and is strictly positive on each rectangle because \(p_{\circ}\in(0,1)\).  The observed outcomes are identically zero.  Absolute continuity gives \(P^{\circ}(Z=0)=0\), while the second rectangle gives
\[
 P^{\circ}(|Z|\leq\eta)>0\qquad(\eta>0).
\]
For each \(x\in[-1,0)\), however, the conditional support of \(Z\) is \([1/2,1]\), so conditional quasi-stayer support fails there.  Integrating over the first rectangle's \(z\)-coordinate gives marginal density \(p_{\circ}\) for \(X\) on \([-1,0]\), and the failure region therefore has probability \(p_{\circ}\).

The definitions give
\[
 X_0=[-1,1],
 \qquad
 H=[-1,0]\times[1/2,1]\ \cup\ [0,1]\times[-1/4,1/4],
 \qquad g=0,
 \qquad T=0.
\]
On the first rectangle \(S=1\); on the second, the conditional law of \(Z\) is uniform and symmetric about zero, so its conditional sign mean is zero.  Hence
\[
 d\nu_{P^{\circ}}(x)=p_{\circ}\mathbf 1\{x\in[-1,0]\}\,dx.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A
 &=p_{\circ}E[Z\mid Z\in[1/2,1]]
 +(1-p_{\circ})E[|Z|\mid Z\in[-1/4,1/4]]\\
 &=\frac{3p_{\circ}}4+\frac{1-p_{\circ}}8
 =\frac{1+5p_{\circ}}8.
 \end{aligned}
\]

For \(x\in[0,1]\), the support contains \((x,0)\), so feasibility forces \(u(x)=0\).  For \(x\in[-1,0]\), the support point \((x,1/2)\) gives \(|u(x)|\leq1/2\), while global one-Lipschitz continuity and \(u(0)=0\) give \(|u(x)|\leq-x\).  Conversely, the functions \(u_+^{\circ}\) and \(u_-^{\circ}=-u_+^{\circ}\) are bounded by one, globally one-Lipschitz, vanish on \([0,1]\), and obey \(|u_s^{\circ}(x)|\leq1/2\leq|z|\) on the first rectangle.  Thus both are feasible, and direct substitution in \(\text{def:obstacle-envelopes}\) gives
\[
 r_{P^{\circ}}(x)=-\ell_{P^{\circ}}(x)
 =\begin{cases}
 \min\{-x,1/2\},&x\in[-1,0],\\
 0,&x\in[0,1].
 \end{cases}
\]
The two envelopes are themselves feasible, so \(\text{thm:obstacle-compatibility}\) and the signed density above imply
\[
 V_{P^{\circ}}^+
 =p_{\circ}\int_{-1}^0\min\{-x,1/2\}\,dx
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}8,
 \qquad
 V_{P^{\circ}}^-=-\frac{3p_{\circ}}8.
\]
Therefore \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) yields
\[
 \Theta(P^{\circ})=
 \left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
         \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]

It remains to verify full-law attainment.  For \(s\in\{+,-\}\), evaluation of the displayed schedules at the realized doses gives
\[
 Y_1^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_1)=0=Y_1,
 \qquad
 Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_2)=0=Y_2,
\]
so the entire observed law is preserved.  Outside the null event \(\{Z=0\}\), direct subtraction gives, on one event common to every \(d\in[-2,2]\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_s^{\circ}}(d)
 &=-\frac{u_s^{\circ}(X)}Z\{(d-D_2)-(d-X)\}\\
 &=u_s^{\circ}(X).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the conditional expectations given \((X,Z)\) and given \(X\) both equal \(u_s^{\circ}(X)\), simultaneously for every dose.  This verifies the per-dose parallel-trends clause, and evaluation at \(d=D_1\) separately verifies the realized-baseline identity with \(m_{Q_s^{\circ}}=u_s^{\circ}\).  The trends are continuous, bounded by one, and globally one-Lipschitz.

The affine slope has magnitude \(|u_s^{\circ}(X)/Z|\leq1\): on the first rectangle \(|u_s^{\circ}(X)|\leq1/2\leq|Z|\), and on the second it is zero.  Consequently both potential-outcome schedules are individually one-Lipschitz and integrable on compact \(I\).  The realized period-two movement is
\[
 Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_1)=-u_s^{\circ}(X),
\]
which is integrable and obeys the conditional mean-movement bound because \(|u_s^{\circ}(X)|\leq|Z|\).  All clauses in \(\mathfrak Q(P^{\circ})\) therefore hold.  Finally,
\[
 \theta(Q_+^{\circ})
 =-\frac{E_{P^{\circ}}[Su_+^{\circ}(X)]}{A}
 =-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
 \qquad
 \theta(Q_-^{\circ})
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
\]
so the two legal completions attain the two endpoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:clipping-stability]\label{thm:clipping-stability}
If \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), then for every \(\varepsilon\ge0\), \(\Pi_P\) maps \(\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon}\) into \(\mathcal C_P\) and \(\|v-\Pi_P(v)\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\). The constant one is sharp. For every component-respecting mesh, the uniform-norm Hausdorff distance satisfies \(d_H(\mathcal C_{P,h},\mathcal C_P)=e_h\); hence each signed support value over \(\mathcal C_{P,h}\) differs from its full-class counterpart by at most \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}e_h\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  By \(\text{thm:obstacle-compatibility}\), \(\ell_P\leq r_P\), and both obstacles are \(L\)-Lipschitz.  If \(v\in\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon}\), its unrelaxed level and Lipschitz restrictions and its relaxed support cones imply, for every \(x\in X_0\),
\[
 \ell_P(x)-\varepsilon\leq v(x)\leq r_P(x)+\varepsilon.
\]
Indeed, for every \((d,z)\in H\),
\[
 v(x)\geq v(d)-L|x-d|
 \geq g(d,z)-K|z|-\varepsilon-L|x-d|,
\]
and taking the supremum and adjoining the bound \(v(x)\geq-M\) proves the lower inequality; the upper inequality follows symmetrically.

Pointwise projection onto \([\ell_P(x),r_P(x)]\) is \(\Pi_P(v)(x)\), so
\[
 |v(x)-\Pi_P(v)(x)|\leq\varepsilon.
\]
For real numbers,
\[
 |\max\{a,b\}-\max\{c,d\}|\leq\max\{|a-c|,|b-d|\},
\]
and the analogous inequality holds for minima.  Hence maxima and minima of \(L\)-Lipschitz functions are \(L\)-Lipschitz.  The clipped function is therefore \(L\)-Lipschitz and lies between the exact obstacles.  Obstacle compatibility gives
\[
 \Pi_P(v)\in\mathcal C_P,
 \qquad
 \|v-\Pi_P(v)\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon.
\]
The factor one is uniformly sharp.  Take \(X_0\) to be a singleton, one observed support point with nonzero \(z\), \(g=0\), \(K=0\), and \(M\geq\varepsilon>0\).  Then \(\mathcal C_P=\{0\}\), while \(\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon}\) contains the constant function \(v=\varepsilon\), whose distance from \(\mathcal C_P\) is exactly \(\varepsilon\).

For the mesh class, omission of support constraints gives
\[
 \ell_{P,h}\leq\ell_P\leq r_P\leq r_{P,h}.
\]
Any fixed member of \(\mathcal C_P\) lies between the mesh obstacles, so they are ordered.  Both are \(L\)-Lipschitz; hence \(\ell_{P,h},r_{P,h}\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\), and \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal C_{P,h}\).  For \(v\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\), exact clipping belongs to \(\mathcal C_P\), and pointwise
\[
 |v-\Pi_P(v)|
 \leq\max\{\ell_P-\ell_{P,h},r_{P,h}-r_P\}
 \leq e_h.
\]
This proves the Hausdorff upper bound.  Conversely, for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\),
\[
 \|u-\ell_{P,h}\|_\infty
 \geq\|\ell_P-\ell_{P,h}\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|u-r_{P,h}\|_\infty
 \geq\|r_{P,h}-r_P\|_\infty,
\]
because \(u\geq\ell_P\) and \(u\leq r_P\).  Since the two mesh obstacles belong to \(\mathcal C_{P,h}\), they furnish the matching lower bounds.  Therefore
\[
 d_H(\mathcal C_{P,h},\mathcal C_P)=e_h.
\]
Finally, \(|J_P(f)-J_P(f')|\leq\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|f-f'\|_\infty\).  Pairing each element of either nonempty compact class with an element of the other within Hausdorff distance \(e_h\), and then taking suprema or infima, shows that each signed support value changes by at most \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}e_h\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact clipping replaces uncheckable strict-feasibility and active-set assumptions by a global deterministic stability certificate. \textit{Gap.} Generic LP perturbation theory begins from a finite program and does not give this constant-one continuum feasibility retraction. \textit{Consumer.} The certificate makes support gridding auditable and allows conservative endpoint computation even at equality constraints.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-program]\label{thm:finite-program}
For every component-respecting \(h\)-net, \(e_h\leq\bar e_h=(K+L)h+Gh^\beta\). If \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), then the extrema of the finite outer linear objective \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}\) over \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) are attained and differ from \(V_P^-\) and \(V_P^+\) by at most \(\bar e_h+Lh\), using \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\). Under the same compatibility condition, every value of \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}\) is attained by a member of \(\mathcal C_P\), and the inner objective range is within \(2Lh\) of the full objective range. Consequently, when \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), the induced inner and outer WAOSS intervals have Hausdorff error at most \(A^{-1}(\bar e_h+2Lh)\). The unconditional mesh-error bound, and, under compatibility, all of the stated attainment, objective-range, and WAOSS conclusions, continue to hold when rectangle boundaries touch, when a connected component is separated from \(z=0\), and when obstacle constraints bind.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a component-respecting \(h\)-net \(H_h\).  We first prove the claim that does not require compatibility.  For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), the net property supplies \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\in H_h\) in the same connected component with \(\|w-w_j\|_\infty\leq h\).  The H\"older restriction and the reverse triangle inequality give, for every \(x\in X_0\),
\[
 |g(d,z)-g(d_j,z_j)|\leq Gh^\beta,
 \quad
 \bigl||z|-|z_j|\bigr|\leq h,
 \quad
 \bigl||x-d|-|x-d_j|\bigr|\leq h.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \left|[g(d,z)-K|z|-L|x-d|]-[g(d_j,z_j)-K|z_j|-L|x-d_j|]\right|
 \leq Gh^\beta+(K+L)h=\bar e_h,
\]
and the same bound holds with both minus signs replaced by plus signs.  Since the mesh indexing set is contained in the full indexing set, taking the defining suprema and infima, and using that scalar truncation at \(-M\) or \(M\) is one-Lipschitz, gives
\[
 0\leq\ell_P(x)-\ell_{P,h}(x)\leq\bar e_h,
 \qquad
 0\leq r_{P,h}(x)-r_P(x)\leq\bar e_h.
\]
Taking suprema over \(x\) proves unconditionally that
\[
 e_h\leq\bar e_h=(K+L)h+Gh^\beta.
\]

Now assume \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  Restricting any \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) to \(B_h\) satisfies every constraint of \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), so this polytope is nonempty.  It is an intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces with \([-M,M]^{B_h}\), hence is compact.

If \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), its bounded McShane extension \(E_hv\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, is bounded by \(M\), and equals \(v\) on \(B_h\).  Explicitly, these facts follow by clipping
\[
 x\longmapsto\min_{y\in B_h}\{v(y)+L|x-y|\}
\]
to \([-M,M]\): the pairwise node inequalities force equality at each node, and minima and clipping preserve the Lipschitz constant.  Therefore, for every \((d,z)\in H_h\),
\[
 |g(d,z)-E_hv(d)|=|g(d,z)-v(d)|\leq K|z|,
\]
so \(E_hv\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\).  Conversely, \(f\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\) implies \(f|_{B_h}\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\).

The pushforward identity \(\nu_{P,h}=(\pi_h)_\#\nu_P\) gives
\[
 J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)
 =\int_{X_0}v(\pi_h(x))\,d\nu_P(x).
\]
Since \(E_hv=v\) on \(B_h\), \(|x-\pi_h(x)|\leq h\), and \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\),
\[
 \left|J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-J_P(E_hv)\right|
 \leq L\int|x-\pi_h(x)|\,d|\nu_P|(x)
 \leq Lh.
\]
Here \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\) follows directly from \(\nu_P(B)=E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B\}]\) and \(|S|=1\).

By \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\), \(d_H(\mathcal C_{P,h},\mathcal C_P)=e_h\).  Hence every \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) has, for every \(\eta>0\), some \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) satisfying
\[
 \|E_hv-u\|_\infty\leq e_h+\eta,
 \qquad
 |J_P(E_hv)-J_P(u)|\leq e_h+\eta.
\]
Conversely, for \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), the vector \(v=u|_{B_h}\) is outer-feasible and
\[
 |J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-J_P(u)|
 =\left|\int\{u(\pi_h(x))-u(x)\}\,d\nu_P(x)\right|
 \leq Lh.
\]
The outer objective is linear and continuous on a nonempty compact polytope, so both extrema are attained.  The preceding two comparisons, followed by \(\eta\downarrow0\), prove
\[
 \left|\min_{v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}}J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-V_P^-\right|
 \leq e_h+Lh,
 \qquad
 \left|\max_{v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}}J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-V_P^+\right|
 \leq e_h+Lh.
\]
The unconditional envelope bound makes both right sides at most \(\bar e_h+Lh\).

For every outer-feasible \(v\), clipping stability gives
\[
 \Pi_P(E_hv)\in\mathcal C_P.
\]
Thus every value \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}(v)=J_P(\Pi_P(E_hv))\) is attained by an exact feasible trend.  The extension, clipping, and signed-integration maps are continuous in the relevant sup norms, so the inner extrema are also attained on the compact polytope.

Fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), let \(v=u|_{B_h}\), and use the nearest node \(\pi_h(x)\).  The functions \(u\) and \(E_hv\) are both \(L\)-Lipschitz and agree at that node; therefore
\[
 |E_hv(x)-u(x)|
 \leq|E_hv(x)-v(\pi_h(x))|+|u(\pi_h(x))-u(x)|
 \leq2Lh.
\]
Because \(u(x)\in[\ell_P(x),r_P(x)]\), scalar projection onto this interval cannot increase distance from \(u(x)\).  Hence
\[
 \|\Pi_P(E_hv)-u\|_\infty\leq2Lh,
 \qquad
 |J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}(v)-J_P(u)|\leq2Lh.
\]
The inner objective range is contained in \(J_P(\mathcal C_P)\), while the last display places an inner value within \(2Lh\) of every full-class value.  Moreover, \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) is convex and hence connected, and \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}\) is continuous, so its real-valued image is an interval.  Thus the lower and upper endpoints of the two objective ranges, and equivalently the ranges in Hausdorff distance, differ by at most \(2Lh\).

On the domain under consideration \(A>0\).  The map \(j\mapsto(T-j)/A\) reverses endpoints and multiplies distances by \(A^{-1}\).  The outer and inner induced WAOSS intervals consequently have errors at most \(A^{-1}(\bar e_h+Lh)\) and \(A^{-1}(2Lh)\), respectively, and both are bounded by
\[
 A^{-1}(\bar e_h+2Lh).
\]
Every approximation above stays within a connected component and uses weak inequalities.  Touching rectangles belong to their common connected component; no step takes \(z\to0\) or divides by \(z\), so a component separated from zero changes is harmless; and equality in any obstacle constraint is admitted.  This proves all stated boundary and binding cases.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem makes the sharp continuous-support set computable with a certified deterministic error. \textit{Gap.} Beresteanu, Molchanov, and Molinari (2011) provide convex sharp-region logic; Mogstad, Santos, and Torgovitsky (2018) optimize weighted functionals under convex restrictions; Freyberger and Horowitz (2015) study linear-program bounds on finite discrete support; and Voronin (2025) analyzes values of programs already finite. None provides this exact clipping and Hausdorff certificate from the continuum obstacle class to the mesh programs. \textit{Consumer.} A software implementation can report sharp WAOSS bounds together with a mesh certificate rather than an undocumented gridding tolerance.

\begin{proposition}[prop:common-baseline-reduction]\label{prop:common-baseline-reduction}
Sanity check: When \(P(X=0)=1\) and \(Z>0\) almost surely, the obstacle program collapses to the scalar interval for the common baseline trend and reproduces the bounded-conditional-average-slope WAOSS bounds of de Chaisemartin, Ciccia, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Knau (2024).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(P(X=0)=1\) and \(Z>0\) almost surely.  Then \(S=1\), \(X_0=\{0\}\), and
\[
 \nu_P=\delta_0.
\]
A continuous baseline trend is a single scalar \(c=u(0)\); there is no nontrivial cross-baseline Lipschitz restriction.  The obstacle definitions reduce to
\[
 \underline c
 =\max\left\{-M,\sup_{(0,z)\in H}\{g(0,z)-Kz\}\right\},
 \qquad
 \overline c
 =\min\left\{M,\inf_{(0,z)\in H}\{g(0,z)+Kz\}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, \(c\in[\underline c,\overline c]\) is equivalent term by term to
\[
 |c|\leq M,
 \qquad
 |g(0,z)-c|\leq Kz
 \quad((0,z)\in H).
\]
Thus \(\text{thm:obstacle-compatibility}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal C_P=\{c\in\mathbb R:\underline c\leq c\leq\overline c\},
\]
with emptiness exactly when \(\underline c>\overline c\).  Under compatibility the endpoints are ordered.  Since \(J_P(c)=c\), \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) becomes
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-\overline c}{A},
       \frac{T-\underline c}{A}\right].
\]
This is exactly the scalar intersection program for the common-zero-baseline bounded conditional-average-slope model in de Chaisemartin, Ciccia, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Knau (2024, arXiv:2405.04465v4, Section 4.2.1, Theorem 4, and Appendix B.2).  The argument establishes algebraic recovery of that prior-art interval and makes no novelty claim for the scalar case.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction is the required sanity check and fixes the boundary between prior art and the varying-baseline contribution. \textit{Gap.} The scalar common-zero-baseline result is published; the proposition claims equivalence, not novelty. \textit{Consumer.} Readers can verify that existing heterogeneous-adoption bounds are nested exactly within the new frontier.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-band-to-program]\label{thm:uniform-band-to-program}
For the explicit rectangle-subordinate cell-mean estimator, signed empirical measure, moment bands, and finite outer program, the lexicographically selected endpoints are Borel measurable and \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}\ge1-\alpha\). On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), together with \(J_P(u)\), \(T\), and \(A\), is feasible through \((u|_{\widehat B_n},J_P(u),T,A)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\), and the empirical endpoints obey \(\widehat L_n\le(T-V_P^+)/A\) and \(\widehat U_n\ge(T-V_P^-)/A\). On the same event both endpoint gaps are nonnegative and each is at most a fixed-class multiple of \(\{r_n+(K+L)s_n+q_n+t_n+Ka_n\}/A\); in particular, signed-measure uncertainty enters as \(q_n/A\) and denominator uncertainty as \(Ka_n/A\). Thus both excess endpoints are \(O(\{\log n/n\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}+n^{-1/2})\) uniformly, without density smoothness, strict feasibility, or optimizer uniqueness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(D_I=\operatorname{diam}(I)\) and \(D_X=\operatorname{diam}(X_0)\).  Constants below may depend on the fixed family \(\mathscr R\), \(\alpha,\beta,G,B,f_0,a_0,K,L,M,D_I\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) or \(n\).  Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) supplies the finite-rectangle support, density lower bound, bounded outcomes, within-component H\"older condition, and \(A\geq a_0>0\).

First construct the boundary-aware partition.  Draw every horizontal and vertical line containing an edge of a rectangle in \(\mathscr R\).  The positive-area elementary rectangles of the resulting arrangement that lie in \(H\) have disjoint interiors and fixed positive side lengths.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), subdivide an elementary side of length \(a\) into \(m=\lceil\sqrt2a/h_n\rceil\) equal intervals.  Since eventually \(h_n<\sqrt2a\),
\[
 \frac{h_n}{2\sqrt2}\leq\frac{a}{m}\leq\frac{h_n}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Every resulting cell has Euclidean diameter at most \(h_n\), area at least \(h_n^2/8\), and lies within one connected component of \(H\).  Assign shared edges and corners deterministically to a cell whose closure contains them, using a lexicographic half-open convention.  This gives a Borel partition of all of \(H\), including touching boundaries.  Since \(\mathscr R\) is fixed and finite, for some \(C_{\mathscr R}<\infty\),
\[
 N_n\leq C_{\mathscr R}h_n^{-2}.
\]

For a cell \(C\), write \(p_C=P\{(X,Z)\in C\}\) and let \(N_C\) be its count.  Cell boundaries have Lebesgue measure zero, and the density lower bound gives
\[
 p_C\geq f_0\operatorname{area}(C)\geq f_0h_n^2/8.
\]
For \(N_C\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p_C)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-\lambda N_C}\), followed by \(1+y\leq e^y\), gives
\[
 P(N_C<np_C/2)
 \leq\exp\{np_C(\lambda/2+e^{-\lambda}-1)\}.
\]
At \(\lambda=\log2\), the exponent is at most \(-np_C/8\).  A union bound therefore shows that, except on an event of probability at most
\[
 N_n\exp(-nf_0h_n^2/64),
\]
every cell contains at least \(nf_0h_n^2/16\) observations.

Conditionally on the design points, \(\xi_i=\Delta Y_i-g(X_i,Z_i)\) are independent and conditionally mean zero.  Since \(\Delta Y_i\in[-2B,2B]\), each \(\xi_i\), conditionally on its design, lies in an interval of length \(4B\).  The exponential chord inequality yields
\[
 E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid X_i,Z_i]\leq e^{2\lambda^2B^2}.
\]
Markov optimization for a cell containing \(m\) observations then gives
\[
 P\!\left(\left|m^{-1}\sum_{i:(X_i,Z_i)\in C}\xi_i\right|>x
 \mathrel{\Big|}(X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^n\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{mx^2}{8B^2}\right).
\]
On the count event, take
\[
 x_n=2B\sqrt{\frac{128\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nf_0h_n^2}}.
\]
The conditional probability that any cell average of \(\xi_i\) exceeds \(x_n\) is at most
\[
 2N_n\exp\{-4\log(8N_n/\alpha)\}\leq\alpha/4.
\]
For any \(w\) assigned to a cell, all design points in that cell are in the same component and within distance \(h_n\); the H\"older bias is therefore at most \(Gh_n^\beta\).  By the definition of \(r_n\),
\[
 P\!\left\{\sup_{w\in H}|\widehat g_n(w)-g(w)|\leq r_n\right\}
 \geq1-\alpha/4-N_ne^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}.
\]
Because \(nh_n^2/\log n\to\infty\) and \(N_n=O(h_n^{-2})\), the last term is \(o(1)\), uniformly in \(P\).  No density smoothness or density upper bound was used, and a component separated from \(z=0\) causes no change.

Second, prove the signed-measure band.  Let \(\mathcal U\) be all \(u:X_0\to[-M,M]\) with Lipschitz constant at most \(L\), and put \(f_u(s,x)=su(x)\).  This class has a countable uniform-norm dense subclass, so the following supremum is measurable.  For an independent ghost empirical law \(P_n'\) and independent Rademacher signs \(\eta_i\), conditional Jensen, exchangeability, and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|
 &\leq E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P_n')f_u|\\
 &\leq2E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_iS_iu(X_i)\right|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Extend \(u\) to \([a,b]=\operatorname{conv}(X_0)\) with the same Lipschitz constant and clip it to \([-M,M]\).  It is absolutely continuous, \(|u'|\leq L\) almost everywhere, and
\[
 u(X_i)=u(a)+\int_a^b u'(t)\mathbf1\{X_i\geq t\}\,dt.
\]
Conditionally on the data, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\right|\leq\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\mathbf1\{X_i\geq t\}\right|\leq\sqrt n.
\]
Taking the supremum after the integral representation proves
\[
 E_\eta\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_i\eta_iS_iu(X_i)\right|
 \leq\frac{M+LD_X}{\sqrt n}.
\]

Let \(W_n=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|\).  Replacing one observation changes \(W_n\) by at most \(2M/n\).  The bounded-difference exponential argument therefore yields, for \(M>0\),
\[
 P(W_n-EW_n>x)\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{2M^2}\right);
\]
if \(M=0\), then \(W_n=0\).  Combining the last three displays with \(x=M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/n}\) shows, with probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\),
\[
 W_n\leq\frac{2(M+LD_X)+M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}}{\sqrt n}\leq q_n.
\]
For the last inequality, put \(c_\alpha=\sqrt{2\log(8/\alpha)}\); then \(c_\alpha\geq2\) and \(c_\alpha\geq\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}\), while \(q_n\sqrt n=(2M+LD_X)c_\alpha\).  Since \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal U\), this establishes
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal C_P}
 \left|\int_{X_0}u\,d(\widehat\nu_n-\nu_P)\right|\leq q_n.
\]

The variables \(S\Delta Y\) and \(|Z|\) lie in intervals of lengths \(4B\) and \(D_I\).  The same exponential inequality and the definitions of \(t_n,a_n\) give
\[
 P(|\widehat T_n-T|>t_n)\leq\alpha/8,
 \qquad
 P(|\widehat A_n-A|>a_n)\leq\alpha/8.
\]
The four failure probabilities sum to at most \(\alpha+o(1)\), uniformly over the regular class; independence among the events is unnecessary.

Third, on their intersection fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  Its restriction to \(\widehat B_n\) satisfies the level and pairwise Lipschitz inequalities.  At every support-mesh point \((d_j,z_j)\),
\[
 |\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \leq|\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-g(d_j,z_j)|+|g(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \leq r_n+K|z_j|.
\]
Thus \(u|_{\widehat B_n}\in\widehat{\mathcal U}_n^{\mathrm{out}}\), and the signed-measure and moment bands, together with \(A\in[a_0,D_I]\), imply simultaneously
\[
 (u|_{\widehat B_n},J_P(u),T,A)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n
 \qquad(u\in\mathcal C_P).
\]
This is the last clause defining \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), so
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]

Fourth, suppose \(\mathcal E_n(P)\) occurs.  Feasibility of every population tuple implies, after taking the infimum or supremum over \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) and using \(\text{def:frontier-values}\),
\[
 \inf_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},
 \qquad
 \sup_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \geq\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.
\]
For every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), iterated expectation and the observed cone give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |T-J_P(u)|
 &=\left|E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}]\right|\\
 &\leq E_P|g(X,Z)-u(X)|\leq KA.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the population interval is contained in \([-K,K]\), and endpoint clipping preserves the two outward inclusions.

For the reverse proximity bound, fix \((v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\).  The node inequalities permit the bounded Lipschitz extension
\[
 E_nv(x)=\max\{-M,\min\{M,\min_{x_k\in\widehat B_n}[v(x_k)+L|x-x_k|]\}\},
\]
which equals \(v\) at every node.  For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), choose a same-component support-mesh point \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\) with \(\|w-w_j\|_\infty\leq s_n\).  Euclidean distance is at most \(\sqrt2s_n\).  The H\"older restriction, regression band, empirical cone, reverse triangle inequality, and Lipschitz property give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g(d,z)-E_nv(d)|
 &\leq2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta+r_n+(K|z_j|+r_n)+Ls_n\\
 &\leq K|z|+\varepsilon_n,\\
 \varepsilon_n&:=2r_n+(K+L)s_n+2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta\\
 &\leq4r_n+(K+L)s_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s_n\leq h_n\), \(r_n\geq Gh_n^\beta\), and \(2^{\beta/2}<2\).  Hence \(E_nv\in\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon_n}\).  Compatibility and \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\) produce
\[
 u^*=\Pi_P(E_nv)\in\mathcal C_P,
 \qquad
 \|E_nv-u^*\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon_n.
\]

Every observed \(X_i\) belongs to \(\widehat B_n\), so \(E_nv(X_i)=v(X_i)\).  The program constraint on \(j\), \(\|\widehat\nu_n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), the uniform signed-measure band, and the last display imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |j-J_P(u^*)|
 &\leq\left|j-\int E_nv\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
 +\left|\int(E_nv-u^*)\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
 +\left|\int u^*\,d\widehat\nu_n-J_P(u^*)\right|\\
 &\leq\varepsilon_n+2q_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Likewise, feasibility and the population bands give
\[
 |t-T|\leq2t_n,
 \qquad
 |a-A|\leq2a_n.
\]
Both denominators are at least \(a_0\), and \(|T-J_P(u^*)|/A\leq K\).  Adding and subtracting \((T-J_P(u^*))/a\) proves
\[
 \left|\frac{t-j}{a}-\frac{T-J_P(u^*)}{A}\right|
 \leq\frac{\varepsilon_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.
\]
Every empirical feasible ratio is therefore within this distance of the population interval.  Together with the outward inclusions, and because clipping only reduces excess, this gives
\[
 0\leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A}-\widehat L_n\leq d_n,
 \qquad
 0\leq\widehat U_n-\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\leq d_n,
\]
where
\[
 d_n=\frac{4r_n+(K+L)s_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), the empirical feasible set is nonempty and the two inclusions rule out crossing, so the reset rule is not invoked.  Since \(A\leq D_I\), \(d_n\) is at most a fixed-class multiple of
\[
 \frac{r_n+(K+L)s_n+q_n+t_n+Ka_n}{A}.
\]
This exhibits signed-measure uncertainty as \(q_n/A\) and denominator uncertainty as \(Ka_n/A\).  All constraints are weak; no strict feasibility or optimizer uniqueness has entered.

Fifth, establish measurability.  Order deterministic mesh nodes and observed baseline coordinates lexicographically and remove duplicates by the first-index rule.  For fixed \(n\), the finitely many ordering and coincidence patterns are Borel strata.  On each stratum, cell counts, cell sums, the empty-cell convention, node coordinates, and all program coefficients are Borel functions of the sample.  Since \(a\in[a_0,D_I]\), set
\[
 c=1/a,
 \qquad \widetilde v=cv,
 \qquad \widetilde j=cj,
 \qquad \widetilde t=ct.
\]
For example, the denominator band becomes \(|1-c\widehat A_n|\leq ca_n\), and the signed-objective band becomes
\[
 \left|\widetilde j-\int\widetilde v\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|\leq cq_n.
\]
Each absolute value is two linear inequalities; all remaining scaled constraints are also linear, and the ratio objective is \(\widetilde t-\widetilde j\).  The bounds \(D_I^{-1}\leq c\leq a_0^{-1}\), together with the scaled variable bounds, make the transformed feasible set a compact polytope whenever it is nonempty.

On each Borel stratum, enumerate the finitely many possible active-constraint bases.  On every nonzero-determinant sub-stratum, a candidate vertex is a Borel rational function of the coefficients; feasibility and objective comparisons are finite Borel comparisons.  A nonempty compact polytope has a vertex, and successive minimization of the objective and then each coordinate selects the lexicographically least optimizer among these candidates.  Thus the selected optima are Borel on every stratum.  Empty-set testing, clipping, crossing, and resetting are Borel comparisons as well.  Hence \(\widehat L_n\), \(\widehat U_n\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\) are Borel measurable.

Finally, \(N_n=O(h_n^{-2})\) and the definition of \(h_n\) imply
\[
 h_n^\beta
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nh_n^2}}
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right).
\]
Because \(0<\beta\leq1\), \(s_n\leq h_n=O(h_n^\beta)\), while \(q_n,t_n,a_n=O(n^{-1/2})\).  Substitution into the displayed bound for \(d_n\) proves uniformly over compatible \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) that
\[
 d_n=O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}+n^{-1/2}\right).
\]
This proves the stated probability, inclusion, ratio-error, rate, and measurability conclusions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This root inference theorem proves boundary-cell coverage, signed-measure propagation, measurable finite-program endpoints, and the ratio bound on one simultaneous event over compatible laws. \textit{Gap.} Andrews and Shi (2013) address conditional-moment inference; Kaido, Molinari, and Stoye (2019) give uniform calibrated projection for finite moment systems; Belloni, Chernozhukov, Chetverikov, and Kato (2015) give uniform series-regression theory; Cattaneo, Chandak, Jansson, and Ma (2024) give boundary-adaptive conditional-density inference; and Voronin (2025) analyzes already-finite LP values. This theorem claims only their problem-specific composition through a primitive boundary-cell mean band, signed-measure band, certified continuum discretization, denominator band, and measurable ratio optimization for this WAOSS model, not a general advance in any component theory. \textit{Consumer.} The event and endpoint inequalities give empirical WAOSS analyses an auditable route from primitive sampling conditions to honest outer inference.

\begin{proposition}[prop:honest-outer-ci]\label{prop:honest-outer-ci}
As an immediate corollary of the simultaneous-event theorem, \(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n=[\widehat L_n,\widehat U_n]\) satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}P\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}\ge1-\alpha\). The interval is intersected with \([-K,K]\), and coverage allows \(R(P)=0\), nonunique optimizers, and binding obstacle constraints.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) with \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), \(\text{thm:uniform-band-to-program}\) gives
\[
 \widehat L_n\leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},
 \qquad
 \widehat U_n\geq\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.
\]
Membership in the regular class gives \(A\geq a_0>0\), while \(V_P^-\leq V_P^+\).  By \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\),
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right]
 \subseteq[-K,K].
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathcal E_n(P)
 \subseteq
 \{\Theta(P)\subseteq[\widehat L_n,\widehat U_n]\}
 =\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}.
\]
The endpoint measurability established in \(\text{thm:uniform-band-to-program}\) makes this an event.  Taking probabilities, then the infimum over compatible \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\), and finally the lower limit gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}\\
 &\qquad\geq
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because the population set lies in \([-K,K]\), intersecting the empirical interval with that range cannot remove a target value.  No step assumes \(R(P)>0\), strict obstacle inequalities, or a unique optimizer; the conclusion therefore includes singleton sets, binding constraints, and nonunique optima.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Uniform coverage is the direct reportable consequence of the root inclusion-event and endpoint theorem. \textit{Gap.} Kaido, Molinari, and Stoye (2019) treat finite moment systems, whereas this corollary inherits a primitive uniform boundary-regression and signed-measure inclusion theorem for a continuum completion class. It does not characterize the source paper's separate parametric estimator. \textit{Consumer.} County-level temperature and other continuously changing policy panels can report one confidence interval covering every full-law-compatible WAOSS value.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:minimax-transition]\label{oeq:minimax-transition}
On the fixed known support \(H\) defining \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\), is the uniform excess-endpoint rate \(\{\log n/n\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}+n^{-1/2}\) minimax, up to logarithmic factors, along sequences of laws \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) satisfying \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), along which \(R(P)\to0\)? More sharply, can a matching lower bound distinguish the cost of estimating the obstacle boundary from the cost of estimating the signed objective? A shrinking support-hole transition lies outside \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) and requires a separately defined varying-support triangular-array class.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The upper rate becomes economically interpretable only after determining whether its obstacle and signed-objective terms are unavoidable along compatible fixed-support sequences with \(R(P)\to0\). \textit{Gap.} The known fixed rectangle support and uniform positive density lower bound exclude a shrinking support-hole transition from \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\). Existing results provide neither a matching fixed-support lower bound over compatible laws nor a separately formalized varying-support class. \textit{Consumer.} A matching fixed-support lower bound would show whether shorter uniformly honest sensitivity intervals require stronger functional restrictions or merely a better estimator; a support-topology claim must await a distinct class.

\begin{proposition}[prop:source-class-recession-ray]\label{prop:source-class-recession-ray}
Let \(\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\) be the published Assumptions 1--4 completion class and let \(Q_c^{\circ}\) be the affine completion generated by \(c\in\mathbb R\) and \(\phi\). The law \(P^{\circ}\) has a bounded density that is strictly positive on each rectangle of its finite rectangle support and has uniformly bounded observed outcomes. It satisfies marginal quasi-stayers, whereas baseline-conditional positivity fails for every \(x\in[-1,0)\), a region of \(P^{\circ}_X\)-probability \(p_{\circ}\). For every finite \(c\), \(Q_c^{\circ}\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\), its schedules are affine and integrable, it preserves the entire observed law, and it satisfies parallel trends simultaneously for every \(d\in[-2,2]\). Moreover,
\[
\theta(Q_c^{\circ})=-\frac{3c p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
\qquad
\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\}=\mathbb R.
\]
For the strengthened sensitivity class with \(K=L=M=1\), \(Q_+^{\circ}=Q_1^{\circ}\) and \(Q_-^{\circ}=Q_{-1}^{\circ}\) attain the endpoints
\[
\Theta(P^{\circ})=
\left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
       \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]
Thus the identification elbow has three distinct stages: the unrestricted published class gives all of \(\mathbb R\) at this regular witness; the bounded-slope/Lipschitz class gives the displayed finite obstacle interval; and, under baseline-conditional zero support, the obstacle restriction fixes \(u(x)=g(x,0)\) and yields the published weighted singleton formula. Only the negative conclusion for the published singleton transfers to the published class; the finite interval does not.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first rectangle in \(\text{def:continuous-hole-witness}\) has area \(1/2\) and density \(2p_{\circ}\), and the second has area \(1/2\) and density \(2(1-p_{\circ})\).  They therefore have probabilities \(p_{\circ}\) and \(1-p_{\circ}\).  The density is bounded and strictly positive on each rectangle, and observed outcomes are identically zero.  Absolute continuity gives
\[
 P^{\circ}(Z=0)=0.
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), the second rectangle has positive probability in \(\{|Z|\leq\eta\}\), proving marginal quasi-stayers directly.  In contrast, for every \(x\in[-1,0)\),
\[
 P^{\circ}(|Z|\leq\eta\mid X=x)=0
 \qquad(0<\eta<1/2),
\]
and this baseline region has \(P_X^{\circ}\)-probability \(p_{\circ}\).

Fix \(c\in\mathbb R\) and take \(Q_c^{\circ}\) from \(\text{def:source-recession-ray}\).  On the first rectangle \(|\phi(X)|\leq1/2\leq|Z|\), while on the second \(\phi(X)=0\).  Hence, outside the null event \(\{Z=0\}\),
\[
 \left|\frac{c\phi(X)}Z\right|\leq|c|.
\]
Since \(D_1,D_2,d\in[-2,2]\), each fixed-dose schedule is bounded by \(4|c|\), is integrable, is affine in \(d\), and has the finite derivative \(-c\phi(X)/Z\).  At realized doses,
\[
 Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_1)=0=Y_1,
 \qquad
 Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_2)=0=Y_2,
\]
so consistency and the entire observed law are preserved.

For every \(d\in[-2,2]\), on one event common to all doses,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)
 &=-\frac{c\phi(X)}Z\{(d-D_2)-(d-X)\}\\
 &=c\phi(X).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The right side is \(X\)-measurable.  Thus common versions satisfy
\[
 E_{Q_c^{\circ}}[Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)\mid X,Z]
 =c\phi(X)
 =E_{Q_c^{\circ}}[Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)\mid X]
\]
simultaneously for every \(d\in[-2,2]\).  All requirements in \(\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\) therefore hold for every finite \(c\).

The realized period-two causal movement is
\[
 Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_1)=-c\phi(X).
\]
On the first rectangle \(S=1\), and on the second \(\phi(X)=0\), so
\[
 E_{P^{\circ}}[S\phi(X)]
 =p_{\circ}\int_{-1}^{0}\min\{-x,1/2\}\,dx
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}8.
\]
Also,
\[
 A=E_{P^{\circ}}|Z|
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}4+\frac{1-p_{\circ}}8
 =\frac{1+5p_{\circ}}8>0.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \theta(Q_c^{\circ})
 =-\frac{cE_{P^{\circ}}[S\phi(X)]}{A}
 =-\frac{3cp_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}.
\]
The coefficient of \(c\) is nonzero, and every finite \(c\) yields a legal source completion.  Hence the source-class image contains \(\mathbb R\).  Conversely, source-class integrability and \(A>0\) make every \(\theta(Q)\) finite, so
\[
 \{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\}=\mathbb R.
\]

For the bounded sensitivity class, \(g=0\) and \(K=L=M=1\).  The support points \((x,0)\) for \(x\in[0,1]\) force every feasible \(u\) to vanish there.  For \(x\in[-1,0]\), the point \((x,1/2)\) and global one-Lipschitz continuity imply
\[
 |u(x)|\leq\min\{-x,1/2\}=\phi(x).
\]
Both \(\phi\) and \(-\phi\) satisfy every level, Lipschitz, and support-cone restriction, so they are feasible.  Conditional sign symmetry on the second rectangle and positivity on the first give
\[
 d\nu_{P^{\circ}}(x)=p_{\circ}\mathbf1\{x\in[-1,0]\}\,dx.
\]
Therefore
\[
 -\frac{3p_{\circ}}8\leq J_{P^{\circ}}(u)\leq\frac{3p_{\circ}}8,
\]
with equality at \(-\phi\) and \(\phi\).  Since \(T=0\), \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\) and \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) yield
\[
 \Theta(P^{\circ})=
 \left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
        \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]
The constructions give \(Q_+^{\circ}=Q_1^{\circ}\) and \(Q_-^{\circ}=Q_{-1}^{\circ}\); the formula for \(\theta(Q_c^{\circ})\) proves endpoint attainment.  For \(c\in\{-1,1\}\), the slope bound above is one, the trend \(c\phi\) is bounded and globally one-Lipschitz, and the movement \(-c\phi(X)\) obeys \(|c\phi(X)|\leq|Z|\).  Together with observed-law preservation, consistency, integrability, and the common all-dose identity, these facts verify every clause of the strengthened sensitivity class, including both per-dose parallel trends and the separate realized-baseline identity.

Finally, under baseline-conditional zero support, \((x,0)\in H\) at every relevant baseline, so the obstacle restriction forces \(u(x)=g(x,0)\).  The bounded-sensitivity set then collapses to
\[
 \left\{\frac{T-\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x)}{A}\right\}.
\]
Thus the unrestricted published class gives all of \(\mathbb R\) at this witness, the explicit bounded-slope/Lipschitz class gives the finite interval above, and conditional zero support yields the weighted singleton.  Only the failure of the source singleton transfers to the unrestricted source class; the finite sensitivity interval does not.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The affine recession ray proves that the published-class failure is unbounded, while the bounded-slope/Lipschitz model has a finite sharp interval. \textit{Gap.} Marginal quasi-stayers do not supply the baseline-conditional support used before display (4) in the proof of the published theorem; the regular witness makes the resulting source-class identified set equal to \(\mathbb R\). \textit{Consumer.} The result separates the invalid published singleton, the finite sensitivity interval under explicit bounds, and recovery of the weighted singleton under conditional zero support.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The positive identified set is exact only for the strengthened bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity completion class, which retains the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition and separately imposes a realized-baseline identity with a continuous conditional-mean version. The inference theorem additionally fixes a known finite rectangle union, \(\beta\), a uniform positive density lower bound, bounded outcomes, and denominator separation, and uses a conservative cell-mean radius. It proves boundary-cell coverage, signed-measure control, measurable finite-program endpoints, and ratio propagation without density smoothness, strict feasibility, or optimizer uniqueness. Because \(H\) is fixed and \(f_{XZ}\ge f_0>0\), support topology cannot approach failure within \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\). A matching lower bound as \(R(P)\to0\) along compatible laws on fixed \(H\) remains open; shrinking-hole asymptotics require a new varying-support class.

\section{Honest scope}

The negative conclusion is exact for the published class at the regular witness: its legal affine recession ray generates all of \(\mathbb R\), so the claimed nonparametric singleton is not identified there. The finite set \(\Theta(P)\) is instead the sharp causal set for a strengthened bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity class with a finite \(K\) movement bound, bounded globally \(L\)-Lipschitz trends, continuous \(g\), the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition, and a separate realized-baseline identity with a continuous \(m_Q\); the affine lifts satisfy both clauses. Marginal quasi-stayers are unnecessary for that sensitivity characterization. No global equality or inclusion with the source class is claimed, and its finite interval does not transfer upward. Baseline-conditional zero support recovers the proceedings paper's weighted singleton; arXiv:2201.06898v1 Theorem 3 concerns unweighted \(\delta_1\); and the common-zero-baseline scalar bounds in arXiv:2405.04465v4 are prior art. The source's separate parametric estimator is not challenged. Uniform outer inference is confined to the fixed known rectangle-support, fixed-H\"older, density-bounded-below, bounded-outcome, denominator-separated subclass. No lower-bound optimality result is claimed; support-topology asymptotics, unknown support, adaptive smoothness, vanishing \(A\), carry-over, clustered sampling, bounded counterfactual levels, and optimal interval length are also outside scope.
