Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) such that \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  By \(\text{thm:uniform-band-to-program}\), on the event \(\mathcal E_n(P)\) the population tuple associated with every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) is feasible for the empirical program, and its selected endpoints satisfy
\[
 \widehat L_n\leq \frac{T-V_P^+}{A}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \widehat U_n\geq \frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.
\tag{1}
\]
The denominator in (1) is positive: membership of \(P\) in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) entails the denominator-separation condition \(A\geq a_0>0\).  Since \(V_P^-\leq V_P^+\), division by \(A>0\) gives
\[
 \frac{T-V_P^+}{A}\leq \frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.
\tag{2}
\]
By \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\), nonemptiness of \(\mathcal C_P\) implies
\[
 \Theta(P)=\left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right]
 \subseteq[-K,K].
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (1)--(3) with the definition
\(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n=[\widehat L_n,\widehat U_n]\) yields the event inclusion
\[
 \mathcal E_n(P)
 \subseteq
 \left\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, on \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), (1)--(2) place the left endpoint of \(\Theta(P)\) weakly above \(\widehat L_n\) and its right endpoint weakly below \(\widehat U_n\).  The same theorem also guarantees Borel measurability of the lexicographically selected endpoints, so the event on the right of (4) is measurable.

Taking probabilities in (4) gives, for every admissible \(P\),
\[
 P\!\left\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\right\}
 \geq P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Infimizing (5) over
\(\{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\}\), taking lower limits, and invoking the uniform probability assertion of \(\text{thm:uniform-band-to-program}\) proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\!\left\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\right\}
 \geq
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}
 \geq 1-\alpha.
\]
Finally, (3) shows why intersecting the empirical interval with \([-K,K]\) cannot delete any element of the target set.  No step uses \(R(P)>0\), strict obstacle inequalities, or uniqueness of an optimizer.  Hence the argument also covers \(R(P)=0\), binding obstacle constraints, and nonunique optimizers.
