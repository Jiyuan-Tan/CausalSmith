For every component-respecting \(h\)-net, \(e_h\leq\bar e_h=(K+L)h+Gh^\beta\). If \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), then the extrema of the finite outer linear objective \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}\) over \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) are attained and differ from \(V_P^-\) and \(V_P^+\) by at most \(\bar e_h+Lh\), using \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\). Under the same compatibility condition, every value of \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}\) is attained by a member of \(\mathcal C_P\), and the inner objective range is within \(2Lh\) of the full objective range. Consequently, when \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\), the induced inner and outer WAOSS intervals have Hausdorff error at most \(A^{-1}(\bar e_h+2Lh)\). The unconditional mesh-error bound, and, under compatibility, all of the stated attainment, objective-range, and WAOSS conclusions, continue to hold when rectangle boundaries touch, when a connected component is separated from \(z=0\), and when obstacle constraints bind.
%%% FIELD finite-program-proof
Fix a component-respecting \(h\)-net \(H_h\). We first prove the assertion that does not require compatibility. For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), the net property in \(\rho_H\) supplies \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\in H_h\) in the same connected component such that
\[
 \|w-w_j\|_\infty\leq h.
\]
The H\"older restriction in \(\text{ass:holder-regression}\), applied within that component, and the reverse triangle inequality give
\[
 |g(d,z)-g(d_j,z_j)|\leq Gh^\beta,
 \qquad \bigl||z|-|z_j|\bigr|\leq h,
 \qquad \bigl||x-d|-|x-d_j|\bigr|\leq h                         \tag{1}
\]
for every \(x\in X_0\). Hence
\[
 \left|[g(d,z)-K|z|-L|x-d|]-[g(d_j,z_j)-K|z_j|-L|x-d_j|]\right|
 \leq Gh^\beta+(K+L)h=\bar e_h,                                  \tag{2}
\]
and the same bound holds with both minus signs replaced by plus signs. The mesh indexing set is contained in the full indexing set. Taking the relevant suprema and infima in the definition of the obstacles, while observing that the outer truncations at \(-M\) and \(M\) are one-Lipschitz scalar maps, therefore yields
\[
 0\leq \ell_P(x)-\ell_{P,h}(x)\leq\bar e_h,
 \qquad
 0\leq r_{P,h}(x)-r_P(x)\leq\bar e_h.                            \tag{3}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(x\in X_0\) proves
\[
 e_h\leq\bar e_h=(K+L)h+Gh^\beta.                                \tag{4}
\]
No member of \(\mathcal C_P\) has been used in (1)--(4), so this part is unconditional.

Now assume \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\). In particular, restriction of any \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) to \(B_h\) satisfies all constraints defining \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), so that polytope is nonempty. It is an intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces with the box \([-M,M]^{B_h}\), and is therefore compact.

We next relate node vectors to the mesh class. If \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), its bounded McShane extension \(E_hv\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, bounded by \(M\), and equals \(v\) on \(B_h\). These properties also follow directly from the formula obtained by clipping
\(
 x\mapsto\min_{y\in B_h}\{v(y)+L|x-y|\}
\)
to \([-M,M]\): minima of \(L\)-Lipschitz functions are \(L\)-Lipschitz, the pairwise node inequalities force equality at every node, and clipping preserves both equality at the bounded node values and the Lipschitz constant. At every \((d,z)\in H_h\), therefore,
\[
 |g(d,z)-E_hv(d)|=|g(d,z)-v(d)|\leq K|z|.                         \tag{5}
\]
By the mesh-obstacle definition, (5) is equivalent to
\(
 \ell_{P,h}\leq E_hv\leq r_{P,h}
\), and thus \(E_hv\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\). Conversely, if \(f\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\), then \(f|_{B_h}\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\). Thus the finite node restrictions contain exactly the information used by the outer objective.

The definition \(\nu_{P,h}=(\pi_h)_\#\nu_P\) implies
\[
 J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)
 =\int_{X_0}v(\pi_h(x))\,d\nu_P(x).
\]
Since \(E_hv=v\) on \(B_h\), \(E_hv\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, and \(|x-\pi_h(x)|\leq h\),
\[
 \left|J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-J_P(E_hv)\right|
 \leq L\int_{X_0}|x-\pi_h(x)|\,d|\nu_P|(x)
 \leq Lh\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.                               \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, from the definition \(\nu_P(B)=E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B\}]\), for every Borel \(f\) with \(\|f\|_\infty\leq1\),
\[
 \left|\int f\,d\nu_P\right|=|E_P[Sf(X)]|\leq E_P|S|\leq1.
\]
Taking the supremum over such \(f\) gives \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), so the last member of (6) is at most \(Lh\).

By \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\), the Hausdorff distance between \(\mathcal C_{P,h}\) and \(\mathcal C_P\) is exactly \(e_h\). Consequently, for every \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) and every \(\eta>0\), there is \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) with
\[
 \|E_hv-u\|_\infty\leq e_h+\eta,
 \qquad
 |J_P(E_hv)-J_P(u)|\leq e_h+\eta.                                \tag{7}
\]
In the reverse direction, for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), the vector \(v=u|_{B_h}\) belongs to \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), and
\[
 |J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-J_P(u)|
 =\left|\int\{u(\pi_h(x))-u(x)\}\,d\nu_P(x)\right|
 \leq Lh.                                                         \tag{8}
\]
The outer objective is linear and continuous on a nonempty compact finite-dimensional polytope, so both of its extrema are attained. Combining (6)--(8), then letting \(\eta\downarrow0\), shows separately at the lower and upper endpoints that
\[
 \left|\min_{v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}}J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-V_P^-\right|
 \leq e_h+Lh,
 \qquad
 \left|\max_{v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}}J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-V_P^+\right|
 \leq e_h+Lh.                                                    \tag{9}
\]
Equation (4) turns the right sides in (9) into at most \(\bar e_h+Lh\).

For the inner program, (5) and \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\) give, for every \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\),
\[
 \Pi_P(E_hv)\in\mathcal C_P.                                    \tag{10}
\]
Thus every reported value \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}(v)=J_P(\Pi_P(E_hv))\) is attained by an exact feasible trend. The maps \(v\mapsto E_hv\), \(f\mapsto\Pi_P(f)\), and \(f\mapsto J_P(f)\) are continuous in the relevant sup norms; indeed the first two are nonexpansive under the displayed minimum and pointwise clipping formulas, while
\(
 |J_P(f)-J_P(f')|\leq\|f-f'\|_\infty\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\).
Therefore the inner extrema are attained on the same compact polytope.

To obtain the quantitative inner certificate, fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), put \(v=u|_{B_h}\), and take the nearest node \(\pi_h(x)\). Since both \(u\) and \(E_hv\) are \(L\)-Lipschitz and agree at \(\pi_h(x)\),
\[
 |E_hv(x)-u(x)|
 \leq |E_hv(x)-v(\pi_h(x))|+|u(\pi_h(x))-u(x)|
 \leq2Lh.                                                        \tag{11}
\]
By obstacle compatibility, \(u(x)\in[\ell_P(x),r_P(x)]\). Scalar projection onto this interval cannot increase distance from \(u(x)\); hence
\[
 \|\Pi_P(E_hv)-u\|_\infty\leq2Lh,
 \qquad
 |J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}(v)-J_P(u)|\leq2Lh.                       \tag{12}
\]
The inner objective range is contained in the full objective range by (10), while (12) places a point of the inner range within \(2Lh\) of every point of the full range. In particular, their lower and upper endpoints differ by at most \(2Lh\).

On the domain of the WAOSS functional one has \(A>0\). The affine map \(j\mapsto(T-j)/A\) reverses endpoints and multiplies all distances by \(A^{-1}\). Thus (9), (12), and \(e_h\leq\bar e_h\) show that the Hausdorff errors of the outer and inner induced WAOSS intervals are bounded respectively by
\[
 A^{-1}(\bar e_h+Lh)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 A^{-1}(2Lh),
\]
and both are bounded by \(A^{-1}(\bar e_h+2Lh)\).

Finally, every approximation in (1)--(12) is within a connected component and uses only weak inequalities. Rectangles that touch are assigned to their common connected component; no step takes \(z\to0\) or divides by \(z\), so a component separated from \(z=0\) is harmless; and equality in an obstacle constraint is admitted throughout. This proves the asserted boundary and binding cases.
%%% FIELD uniform-band-proof
Put \(D_I=\operatorname{diam}(I)\) and \(D_X=\operatorname{diam}(X_0)\). Constants below may depend on the fixed rectangle family \(\mathscr R\), \(\alpha,\beta,G,B,f_0,a_0,K,L,M,D_I\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) or on \(n\). Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) supplies the finite-rectangle support, density lower bound, bounded outcomes, within-component H\"older condition, and \(A\geq a_0>0\).

We first construct the boundary-aware partition and prove its simultaneous regression band. Draw every horizontal and vertical line containing an edge of a rectangle in the fixed finite family \(\mathscr R\). The positive-area elementary rectangles of the resulting arrangement that lie in \(H\) have disjoint interiors and fixed positive side lengths. For all sufficiently large \(n\), subdivide an elementary side of length \(a\) into
\(
 m=\lceil\sqrt2a/h_n\rceil
\)
equal intervals. Since eventually \(h_n<\sqrt2a\),
\[
 \frac{h_n}{2\sqrt2}\leq\frac{a}{m}\leq\frac{h_n}{\sqrt2}.       \tag{13}
\]
Every resulting two-dimensional cell therefore has Euclidean diameter at most \(h_n\), area at least \(h_n^2/8\), and lies in one connected component of \(H\). Assign shared edges and corners by a deterministic lexicographic half-open convention to a cell whose closure contains them. This is a finite Borel partition of all of \(H\), including touching rectangle boundaries. Because \(\mathscr R\) is fixed and finite, there is a constant \(C_{\mathscr R}<\infty\) such that
\[
 N_n\leq C_{\mathscr R}h_n^{-2}.                                 \tag{14}
\]

For a cell \(C\), write \(p_C=P\{(X,Z)\in C\}\) and let \(N_C\) be its sample count. Boundaries have Lebesgue measure zero, and the density lower bound and (13) give
\[
 p_C\geq f_0\operatorname{area}(C)\geq\frac{f_0h_n^2}{8}.         \tag{15}
\]
For \(N_C\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p_C)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-\lambda N_C}\) and the inequality \(1+y\leq e^y\) imply
\[
 P(N_C<np_C/2)
 \leq\exp\{np_C(\lambda/2+e^{-\lambda}-1)\}.
\]
Taking \(\lambda=\log2\) and using \((\log2)/2-1/2<-1/8\) yields
\[
 P(N_C<np_C/2)\leq e^{-np_C/8}\leq e^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}.            \tag{16}
\]
Thus, except on an event of probability at most \(N_ne^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}\), every cell has at least \(nf_0h_n^2/16\) observations.

Conditionally on the design points, \(\xi_i=\Delta Y_i-g(X_i,Z_i)\) are independent and conditionally mean zero by iid sampling and the defining conditional-mean property of \(g\). Bounded outcomes give \(\Delta Y_i\in[-2B,2B]\), so each conditional \(\xi_i\) lies in an interval of length at most \(4B\). The chord bound for the exponential of a centered variable in an interval of length \(4B\), followed by completing the square, gives
\[
 E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid X_i,Z_i]\leq e^{2\lambda^2B^2}.
\]
Applying Markov's inequality to the exponential of a sum and optimizing over \(\lambda\) gives, for a cell containing \(m\) observations,
\[
 P\!\left(\left|m^{-1}\sum_{i:(X_i,Z_i)\in C}\xi_i\right|>x
 \mathrel{\Big|}(X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^n\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{mx^2}{8B^2}\right).                   \tag{17}
\]
On the count event, substitute
\[
 x_n=2B\sqrt{\frac{128\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nf_0h_n^2}}.
\]
Equations (15)--(17) and a union bound make the conditional probability that any cell average of \(\xi_i\) exceeds \(x_n\) no larger than
\[
 2N_n\exp\{-4\log(8N_n/\alpha)\}\leq\alpha/4.                  \tag{18}
\]
If \(w\) is assigned to \(C\), every design point in \(C\) lies in the same component and is within Euclidean distance \(h_n\) of \(w\). The H\"older condition therefore bounds the difference between \(g(w)\) and the within-cell average of \(g(X_i,Z_i)\) by \(Gh_n^\beta\). By the definition of \(r_n\),
\[
 P\!\left\{\sup_{w\in H}|\widehat g_n(w)-g(w)|\leq r_n\right\}
 \geq1-\frac\alpha4-N_ne^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}.                        \tag{19}
\]
Since \(nh_n^2/\log n\to\infty\), (14) makes the last term \(o(1)\), uniformly over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\). Only the density lower bound was used; no density smoothness or upper bound enters this argument. A component separated from \(z=0\) causes no change because the construction neither divides by \(z\) nor requires cells to approach \(z=0\).

Second, consider the signed-measure and scalar-moment bands. Let \(\mathcal U\) be all functions \(u:X_0\to[-M,M]\) with Lipschitz constant at most \(L\), and set \(f_u(s,x)=su(x)\). Bounded Lipschitz functions on a compact subset of the line have a countable uniform-norm dense subclass, so the suprema below are measurable. Let \(P_n'\) denote an independent empirical law and let \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_n\) be independent Rademacher signs. Conditional Jensen's inequality, exchangeability of each original--ghost pair, and the triangle inequality give the symmetrization calculation
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|
 &\leq E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P_n')f_u|\\
 &=E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \eta_i\{S_iu(X_i)-S_i'u(X_i')\}\right|\\
 &\leq2E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_iS_iu(X_i)\right|. \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]
Extend \(u\) to \([a,b]=\operatorname{conv}(X_0)\) by its Lipschitz extension and clip it to \([-M,M]\). The extension is absolutely continuous, has \(|u'|\leq L\) almost everywhere, and obeys
\[
 u(X_i)=u(a)+\int_a^b u'(t)\mathbf1\{X_i\geq t\}\,dt.
\]
Conditionally on the data, Cauchy--Schwarz and \(|S_i|\leq1\) give
\[
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\right|\leq\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\mathbf1\{X_i\geq t\}\right|\leq\sqrt n.
\]
Taking the supremum after the integral representation yields
\[
 E_\eta\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_i\eta_iS_iu(X_i)\right|
 \leq\frac{M+LD_X}{\sqrt n}.                                    \tag{21}
\]

Write \(W_n=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|\). Replacing one observation changes \(W_n\) by at most \(2M/n\). Successive conditional centering along the coordinate filtration and the same exponential chord bound imply
\[
 E\exp\{\lambda(W_n-EW_n)\}\leq
 \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2M^2}{2n}\right),
\]
and Markov optimization therefore gives
\[
 P(W_n-EW_n>x)\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{2M^2}\right)          \tag{22}
\]
when \(M>0\); if \(M=0\), then \(W_n=0\). From (20)--(22), with \(x=M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/n}\), the probability is at least \(1-\alpha/2\) that
\[
 W_n\leq\frac{2(M+LD_X)+M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}}{\sqrt n}\leq q_n. \tag{23}
\]
For the last inequality, with \(c_\alpha=\sqrt{2\log(8/\alpha)}\), one has \(c_\alpha\geq2\) and \(c_\alpha\geq\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}\), so the numerator in the middle of (23) is at most \((2M+LD_X)c_\alpha=q_n\sqrt n\). Because \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal U\), (23) implies
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal C_P}
 \left|\int_{X_0}u\,d(\widehat\nu_n-\nu_P)\right|\leq q_n.       \tag{24}
\]

The variables \(S\Delta Y\) and \(|Z|\) lie in intervals of lengths \(4B\) and \(D_I\), respectively. The exponential calculation used in (17), now for \(n\) observations, gives under the definitions of \(t_n\) and \(a_n\)
\[
 P(|\widehat T_n-T|>t_n)\leq\alpha/8,
 \qquad
 P(|\widehat A_n-A|>a_n)\leq\alpha/8.                            \tag{25}
\]
No independence among (19), (24), and (25) is needed. Their failure probabilities sum to at most \(\alpha+o(1)\), uniformly in the regular class.

Third, we show that these four bands imply the entire event \(\mathcal E_n(P)\). Fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\). Its restriction to \(\widehat B_n\) satisfies the level and pairwise Lipschitz inequalities. At every support-mesh point \((d_j,z_j)\), the regression band and the defining observed cone for \(\mathcal C_P\) imply
\[
 |\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \leq|\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-g(d_j,z_j)|+|g(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \leq r_n+K|z_j|.                                                \tag{26}
\]
Thus \(u|_{\widehat B_n}\in\widehat{\mathcal U}_n^{\mathrm{out}}\). Equations (24)--(25), together with \(A\in[a_0,D_I]\), give simultaneously
\[
 (u|_{\widehat B_n},J_P(u),T,A)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n
 \qquad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal C_P.                        \tag{27}
\]
This is the final clause in the definition of \(\mathcal E_n(P)\). Consequently,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}\geq1-\alpha.                             \tag{28}
\]

Fourth, suppose \(\mathcal E_n(P)\) occurs. From (27), optimizing over the feasible points indexed by exact trends gives
\[
 \inf_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},
 \qquad
 \sup_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \geq\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.                                         \tag{29}
\]
For every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), iterated expectation and the definition of \(g\) yield
\[
 T-J_P(u)
 =E_P[S\{\Delta Y-u(X)\}]
 =E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}].                                        \tag{30}
\]
The observed cone restriction then implies
\[
 |T-J_P(u)|\leq E_P|g(X,Z)-u(X)|\leq KE_P|Z|=KA.                 \tag{31}
\]
Hence the full WAOSS interval lies in \([-K,K]\), so clipping empirical endpoints to that interval preserves (29).

For the converse proximity bound, fix \((v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\). The node inequalities permit the explicit bounded Lipschitz extension
\[
 E_nv(x)=\max\{-M,\min\{M,\min_{x_k\in\widehat B_n}[v(x_k)+L|x-x_k|]\}\},
\]
which equals \(v\) on \(\widehat B_n\). For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), choose a same-component support-mesh point \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\) with \(\|w-w_j\|_\infty\leq s_n\). The H\"older restriction, regression band, empirical cone, reverse triangle inequality for \(|z|\), and Lipschitz property of \(E_nv\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |g(d,z)-E_nv(d)|
 &\leq |g(w)-g(w_j)|+|g(w_j)-\widehat g_n(w_j)|
      +|\widehat g_n(w_j)-v(d_j)|+|v(d_j)-E_nv(d)|\\
 &\leq 2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta+r_n+K|z_j|+r_n+Ls_n\\
 &\leq K|z|+\varepsilon_n,\\
 \varepsilon_n&:=2r_n+(K+L)s_n+2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta
 \leq4r_n+(K+L)s_n.                                             \tag{32}
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s_n\leq h_n\), \(r_n\geq Gh_n^\beta\), and \(2^{\beta/2}\leq\sqrt2<2\). Therefore \(E_nv\in\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon_n}\). Compatibility is in force in (28), so \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\) gives
\[
 u^*=\Pi_P(E_nv)\in\mathcal C_P,
 \qquad \|E_nv-u^*\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon_n.                   \tag{33}
\]
This is the empirical analogue of the certified continuum discretization in \(\text{thm:finite-program}\); importantly, (32) derives its additional regression-band relaxation rather than assuming feasibility.

Every observed \(X_i\) is in \(\widehat B_n\), so \(E_nv(X_i)=v(X_i)\). The program constraint on \(j\), total variation \(\|\widehat\nu_n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), (24), and (33) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |j-J_P(u^*)|
 &\leq\left|j-\int E_nv\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
 +\left|\int(E_nv-u^*)\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
 +\left|\int u^*\,d\widehat\nu_n-J_P(u^*)\right|\\
 &\leq q_n+\varepsilon_n+q_n
 =\varepsilon_n+2q_n.                                           \tag{34}
\end{aligned}
\]
Likewise feasibility and (25) give
\[
 |t-T|\leq2t_n,
 \qquad |a-A|\leq2a_n.                                          \tag{35}
\]
Both \(a\) and \(A\) are at least \(a_0\), and (31) applied to \(u^*\) gives \(|T-J_P(u^*)|/A\leq K\). Adding and subtracting \((T-J_P(u^*))/a\) and using (34)--(35) therefore yields
\[
 \left|\frac{t-j}{a}-\frac{T-J_P(u^*)}{A}\right|
 \leq\frac{\varepsilon_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.                  \tag{36}
\]
Thus every feasible empirical ratio lies within the right side of (36) of a point in the full WAOSS interval. Combining this fact with (29), and noting that clipping can only reduce outward excess while preserving inclusion, proves
\[
 0\leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A}-\widehat L_n\leq d_n,
 \qquad
 0\leq\widehat U_n-\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\leq d_n,                   \tag{37}
\]
where
\[
 d_n=\frac{4r_n+(K+L)s_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.                  \tag{38}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), (27) makes the empirical feasible set nonempty, and (29) makes crossing impossible after clipping, so the reset rule is not invoked. Since \(A\leq D_I\), (38) is bounded by a fixed-class multiple of
\[
 \frac{r_n+(K+L)s_n+q_n+t_n+Ka_n}{A}.                            \tag{39}
\]
This displays the signed-measure and denominator contributions as \(q_n/A\) and \(Ka_n/A\), up to the same fixed-class multiplier. Every constraint used above is weak, so neither strict feasibility nor optimizer uniqueness is required.

Fifth, we establish Borel measurability. Order deterministic mesh nodes and observed baseline coordinates lexicographically and remove duplicates by a first-index rule. For fixed \(n\), the finitely many ordering and coincidence patterns form Borel strata. On each stratum, cell counts, cell sums, the empty-cell convention, node coordinates, and every coefficient of \(\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\) are Borel functions of the sample. Since \(a\in[a_0,D_I]\), apply the Charnes--Cooper substitution
\[
 c=1/a,
 \qquad \widetilde v=cv,
 \qquad \widetilde j=cj,
 \qquad \widetilde t=ct.
\]
For example, \(|a-\widehat A_n|\leq a_n\) becomes
\(
 |1-c\widehat A_n|\leq ca_n
\), and \(|j-\int v\,d\widehat\nu_n|\leq q_n\) becomes
\(
 |\widetilde j-\int\widetilde v\,d\widehat\nu_n|\leq cq_n
\); each absolute-value constraint is two linear inequalities. The remaining scaled constraints are also linear in \((\widetilde v,\widetilde j,\widetilde t,c)\), and the ratio objective becomes \(\widetilde t-\widetilde j\). The bounds \(D_I^{-1}\leq c\leq a_0^{-1}\), together with the scaled level, moment, and objective constraints, make the transformed feasible set a compact polytope whenever it is nonempty.

On each Borel stratum enumerate the finitely many candidate active-constraint bases. On every nonzero-determinant sub-stratum, a candidate vertex is a Borel rational function of the program coefficients, and feasibility and objective comparisons are finite Borel comparisons. A nonempty compact polytope has a vertex; successive minimization of the objective and then of each coordinate selects the lexicographically least optimizer among these candidates. The selected optima are therefore Borel on each stratum. Empty-set testing, clipping, crossing, and resetting use Borel comparisons as well. Hence \(\widehat L_n\), \(\widehat U_n\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\) are Borel measurable.

Finally, (14) and the definition of \(h_n\) give
\[
 h_n^\beta
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nh_n^2}}
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right). \tag{40}
\]
Because \(0<\beta\leq1\), \(s_n\leq h_n=O(h_n^\beta)\), while the declared radii satisfy \(q_n,t_n,a_n=O(n^{-1/2})\). Substitution into (38)--(40) proves uniformly over compatible laws in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\)
\[
 d_n=O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}+n^{-1/2}\right).
\]
This proves every assertion of the theorem.
