%%% FIELD proof-attained-interval
Because \(X\in I\) almost surely and \(I\) is compact, \(X_0=\operatorname{supp}_P(X)\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(I\).  Every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) satisfies
\[
 \|u\|_\infty\leq M,
 \qquad
 |u(x)-u(x')|\leq L|x-x'|
 \quad(x,x'\in X_0).
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C_P\) is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous.  It is closed in the uniform norm: if \(u_j\in\mathcal C_P\) and \(\|u_j-u\|_\infty\to0\), passage to the limit in each defining inequality gives, for all \(x,x'\in X_0\) and \((x,z)\in H\),
\[
 |u(x)|\leq M,
 \qquad
 |u(x)-u(x')|\leq L|x-x'|,
 \qquad
 |g(x,z)-u(x)|\leq K|z|.
\]
Hence \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).

For completeness, compactness follows without invoking an undeclared compactness theorem.  Given a sequence \((u_j)\) in \(\mathcal C_P\), choose a countable dense set \(D\subseteq X_0\).  Boundedness and successive subsequence extraction give a subsequence \((u_{j_k})\) converging at every point of \(D\).  If \(L>0\), for any \(\eta>0\) choose a finite \(\eta/(3L)\)-net \(D_\eta\subseteq D\).  For sufficiently large \(k,k'\), convergence on this finite set gives
\[
 \max_{d\in D_\eta}|u_{j_k}(d)-u_{j_{k'}}(d)|<\eta/3.
\]
For \(x\in X_0\), choose \(d\in D_\eta\) with \(|x-d|<\eta/(3L)\).  Then
\[
 |u_{j_k}(x)-u_{j_{k'}}(x)|
 \leq L|x-d|+|u_{j_k}(d)-u_{j_{k'}}(d)|+L|d-x|<\eta.
\]
If \(L=0\), all the functions are constant on \(X_0\), and convergence at one point gives the same uniform Cauchy conclusion.  Thus the chosen subsequence converges uniformly.  Closedness places its limit in \(\mathcal C_P\).  Sequential compactness is compactness in the metric induced by the uniform norm, so \(\mathcal C_P\) is compact.  Every defining inequality is preserved under convex combinations; hence \(\mathcal C_P\) is convex.

For every finite Borel partition \((B_j)_{j=1}^m\) of \(X_0\), the definition of \(\nu_P\), the triangle inequality, and \(|S|=1\) almost surely give
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m|\nu_P(B_j)|
 =\sum_{j=1}^m\left|E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B_j\}]\right|
 \leq E_P\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf 1\{X\in B_j\}\right]=1.
\]
Therefore \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), and the definition of \(J_P\) implies
\[
 |J_P(u)-J_P(v)|
 \leq \|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|u-v\|_\infty
 \leq\|u-v\|_\infty.
\]
Thus \(J_P\) is continuous.  On the assumed nonempty compact class it attains its infimum and supremum, which equal \(V_P^-\) and \(V_P^+\) by \(\text{def:frontier-values}\).  Linearity of \(J_P\) and convexity of \(\mathcal C_P\) make \(J_P(\mathcal C_P)\) convex; compactness and nonemptiness then give
\[
 J_P(\mathcal C_P)=[V_P^-,V_P^+].
\]

The no-stayer condition used by \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), together with boundedness of \(Z\in I-I\), gives
\[
 0<A=E_P|Z|\leq\operatorname{diam}(I)<\infty.
\]
The affine map \(j\mapsto(T-j)/A\) is continuous and reverses order.  The definition of \(\Theta(P)\), equivalently the sharp characterization in \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), therefore yields
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right].
\]
It remains to establish the asserted deterministic bound.  Since \(S\) is measurable with respect to \((X,Z)\), the defining conditional-mean property of \(g\) gives
\[
 T=E_P[S\Delta Y]=E_P[Sg(X,Z)].
\]
Also, the definition of \(\nu_P\) gives \(J_P(u)=E_P[Su(X)]\).  Hence, for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), its support-cone restriction implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |T-J_P(u)|
 &=\left|E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}]\right|\\
 &\leq E_P|g(X,Z)-u(X)|
 \leq K E_P|Z|=KA.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Division by \(A>0\) proves \((T-J_P(u))/A\in[-K,K]\) for every feasible \(u\), and therefore \(\Theta(P)\subseteq[-K,K]\).

%%% FIELD proof-point-identification
By \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) and \(A>0\),
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right].
\]
Its length is
\[
 \frac{T-V_P^-}{A}-\frac{T-V_P^+}{A}
 =\frac{V_P^+-V_P^-}{A}
 =\frac{R(P)}{A},
\]
where the last equality is \(\text{def:frontier-values}\).  A nonempty closed interval is a singleton exactly when its length is zero.  Because \(A>0\), this is equivalent successively to \(V_P^+=V_P^-\) and to \(R(P)=0\).  Finally, \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), inherited through \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\), identifies \(\Theta(P)\) with \(\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}\).  Thus singleton geometry is exactly point identification of the causal WAOSS functional, rather than uniqueness of an unrelated optimization functional.

%%% FIELD proof-nested-conditional-support
For each positive integer \(k\), choose a regular conditional probability and define
\[
 q_k(x)=P(|Z|\leq 1/k\mid X=x).
\]
Since \(M_{1/k}=\mathbf 1\{|Z|>1/k\}\), the assumed inequality \(P(M_{1/k}=1\mid X)<1\) says that \(q_k(X)>0\) almost surely.  Fix \(x\in X_0\), and put \(U_k=(x-1/k,x+1/k)\).  The definition of support gives \(P(X\in U_k)>0\), while positivity of \(q_k\) for \(P_X\)-almost every baseline gives
\[
 P(X\in U_k,|Z|\leq1/k)
 =\int_{U_k}q_k(x')\,dP_X(x')>0.
\]
Every positive-probability Borel set meets the support \(H=\operatorname{supp}_P(X,Z)\).  Hence there is \((x_k,z_k)\in H\) with \(|x_k-x|<1/k\) and \(|z_k|\leq1/k\).  Thus \((x_k,z_k)\to(x,0)\).  Since \(H\) is closed,
\[
 (x,0)\in H\qquad(x\in X_0).
\]
This support argument uses only the countable thresholds \(1/k\) and never conditions on a null singleton.

Under the stated compatibility clause, \(\mathfrak Q(P)\neq\varnothing\); by \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\), \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  For any \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), applying its support-cone inequality at \((x,0)\) gives
\[
 |g(x,0)-u(x)|\leq K|0|=0
 \qquad(x\in X_0).
\]
Every feasible function is therefore \(g(\cdot,0)\), and nonemptiness shows that this function is itself feasible.  Consequently
\[
 \mathcal C_P=\{x\mapsto g(x,0)\},
 \qquad
 V_P^-=V_P^+=\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x),
 \qquad
 R(P)=0.
\]
Substitution into \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) yields
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left\{\frac{T-\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x)}{A}\right\}.
\]
This is the weighted WAOSS quasi-stayer-limit formula identified in Theorem 1 of de Chaisemartin, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Vazquez-Bare (2024).  The comparison to Assumption 5 and Theorem 3 of arXiv:2201.06898v1 is only a comparison of conditional-support mechanisms: that theorem concerns the distinct unweighted target \(\delta_1\), and no equality of estimands is used here.

For the final possibility claim, \(\text{prop:source-class-recession-ray}\) supplies the declared continuous law \(P^{\circ}\).  At that law, baseline-conditional zero support fails on a set of \(P_X^{\circ}\)-probability \(p_{\circ}>0\), whereas the bounded sensitivity class has
\[
 \Theta(P^{\circ})=
 \left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
        \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]
This interval is nondegenerate.  The already proved equivalence in \(\text{prop:point-identification}\) therefore gives \(R(P^{\circ})>0\).  This proves that failure of conditional support can produce a positive radius; it does not assert that every such failure does so.

%%% FIELD proof-continuous-hole-witness
Each support rectangle in \(\text{def:continuous-hole-witness}\) has Lebesgue area \(1/2\).  Their density integrals are therefore \(p_{\circ}\) and \(1-p_{\circ}\), so the displayed density integrates to one, is bounded, and is strictly positive on each rectangle because \(p_{\circ}\in(0,1)\).  The observed outcomes are identically zero.  Absolute continuity gives \(P^{\circ}(Z=0)=0\), while the second rectangle gives
\[
 P^{\circ}(|Z|\leq\eta)>0\qquad(\eta>0).
\]
For each \(x\in[-1,0)\), however, the conditional support of \(Z\) is \([1/2,1]\), so conditional quasi-stayer support fails there.  Integrating over the first rectangle's \(z\)-coordinate gives marginal density \(p_{\circ}\) for \(X\) on \([-1,0]\), and the failure region therefore has probability \(p_{\circ}\).

The definitions give
\[
 X_0=[-1,1],
 \qquad
 H=[-1,0]\times[1/2,1]\ \cup\ [0,1]\times[-1/4,1/4],
 \qquad g=0,
 \qquad T=0.
\]
On the first rectangle \(S=1\); on the second, the conditional law of \(Z\) is uniform and symmetric about zero, so its conditional sign mean is zero.  Hence
\[
 d\nu_{P^{\circ}}(x)=p_{\circ}\mathbf 1\{x\in[-1,0]\}\,dx.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A
 &=p_{\circ}E[Z\mid Z\in[1/2,1]]
 +(1-p_{\circ})E[|Z|\mid Z\in[-1/4,1/4]]\\
 &=\frac{3p_{\circ}}4+\frac{1-p_{\circ}}8
 =\frac{1+5p_{\circ}}8.
 \end{aligned}
\]

For \(x\in[0,1]\), the support contains \((x,0)\), so feasibility forces \(u(x)=0\).  For \(x\in[-1,0]\), the support point \((x,1/2)\) gives \(|u(x)|\leq1/2\), while global one-Lipschitz continuity and \(u(0)=0\) give \(|u(x)|\leq-x\).  Conversely, the functions \(u_+^{\circ}\) and \(u_-^{\circ}=-u_+^{\circ}\) are bounded by one, globally one-Lipschitz, vanish on \([0,1]\), and obey \(|u_s^{\circ}(x)|\leq1/2\leq|z|\) on the first rectangle.  Thus both are feasible, and direct substitution in \(\text{def:obstacle-envelopes}\) gives
\[
 r_{P^{\circ}}(x)=-\ell_{P^{\circ}}(x)
 =\begin{cases}
 \min\{-x,1/2\},&x\in[-1,0],\\
 0,&x\in[0,1].
 \end{cases}
\]
The two envelopes are themselves feasible, so \(\text{thm:obstacle-compatibility}\) and the signed density above imply
\[
 V_{P^{\circ}}^+
 =p_{\circ}\int_{-1}^0\min\{-x,1/2\}\,dx
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}8,
 \qquad
 V_{P^{\circ}}^-=-\frac{3p_{\circ}}8.
\]
Therefore \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) yields
\[
 \Theta(P^{\circ})=
 \left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
         \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]

It remains to verify full-law attainment.  For \(s\in\{+,-\}\), evaluation of the displayed schedules at the realized doses gives
\[
 Y_1^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_1)=0=Y_1,
 \qquad
 Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_2)=0=Y_2,
\]
so the entire observed law is preserved.  Outside the null event \(\{Z=0\}\), direct subtraction gives, on one event common to every \(d\in[-2,2]\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_s^{\circ}}(d)
 &=-\frac{u_s^{\circ}(X)}Z\{(d-D_2)-(d-X)\}\\
 &=u_s^{\circ}(X).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the conditional expectations given \((X,Z)\) and given \(X\) both equal \(u_s^{\circ}(X)\), simultaneously for every dose.  This verifies the per-dose parallel-trends clause, and evaluation at \(d=D_1\) separately verifies the realized-baseline identity with \(m_{Q_s^{\circ}}=u_s^{\circ}\).  The trends are continuous, bounded by one, and globally one-Lipschitz.

The affine slope has magnitude \(|u_s^{\circ}(X)/Z|\leq1\): on the first rectangle \(|u_s^{\circ}(X)|\leq1/2\leq|Z|\), and on the second it is zero.  Consequently both potential-outcome schedules are individually one-Lipschitz and integrable on compact \(I\).  The realized period-two movement is
\[
 Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_s^{\circ}}(D_1)=-u_s^{\circ}(X),
\]
which is integrable and obeys the conditional mean-movement bound because \(|u_s^{\circ}(X)|\leq|Z|\).  All clauses in \(\mathfrak Q(P^{\circ})\) therefore hold.  Finally,
\[
 \theta(Q_+^{\circ})
 =-\frac{E_{P^{\circ}}[Su_+^{\circ}(X)]}{A}
 =-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
 \qquad
 \theta(Q_-^{\circ})
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
\]
so the two legal completions attain the two endpoints.

%%% FIELD proof-clipping-stability
Assume \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  By \(\text{thm:obstacle-compatibility}\), \(\ell_P\leq r_P\), and both obstacles are \(L\)-Lipschitz.  If \(v\in\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon}\), its unrelaxed level and Lipschitz restrictions and its relaxed support cones imply, for every \(x\in X_0\),
\[
 \ell_P(x)-\varepsilon\leq v(x)\leq r_P(x)+\varepsilon.
\]
Indeed, for every \((d,z)\in H\),
\[
 v(x)\geq v(d)-L|x-d|
 \geq g(d,z)-K|z|-\varepsilon-L|x-d|,
\]
and taking the supremum and adjoining the bound \(v(x)\geq-M\) proves the lower inequality; the upper inequality follows symmetrically.

Pointwise projection onto \([\ell_P(x),r_P(x)]\) is \(\Pi_P(v)(x)\), so
\[
 |v(x)-\Pi_P(v)(x)|\leq\varepsilon.
\]
For real numbers,
\[
 |\max\{a,b\}-\max\{c,d\}|\leq\max\{|a-c|,|b-d|\},
\]
and the analogous inequality holds for minima.  Hence maxima and minima of \(L\)-Lipschitz functions are \(L\)-Lipschitz.  The clipped function is therefore \(L\)-Lipschitz and lies between the exact obstacles.  Obstacle compatibility gives
\[
 \Pi_P(v)\in\mathcal C_P,
 \qquad
 \|v-\Pi_P(v)\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon.
\]
The factor one is uniformly sharp.  Take \(X_0\) to be a singleton, one observed support point with nonzero \(z\), \(g=0\), \(K=0\), and \(M\geq\varepsilon>0\).  Then \(\mathcal C_P=\{0\}\), while \(\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon}\) contains the constant function \(v=\varepsilon\), whose distance from \(\mathcal C_P\) is exactly \(\varepsilon\).

For the mesh class, omission of support constraints gives
\[
 \ell_{P,h}\leq\ell_P\leq r_P\leq r_{P,h}.
\]
Any fixed member of \(\mathcal C_P\) lies between the mesh obstacles, so they are ordered.  Both are \(L\)-Lipschitz; hence \(\ell_{P,h},r_{P,h}\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\), and \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal C_{P,h}\).  For \(v\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\), exact clipping belongs to \(\mathcal C_P\), and pointwise
\[
 |v-\Pi_P(v)|
 \leq\max\{\ell_P-\ell_{P,h},r_{P,h}-r_P\}
 \leq e_h.
\]
This proves the Hausdorff upper bound.  Conversely, for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\),
\[
 \|u-\ell_{P,h}\|_\infty
 \geq\|\ell_P-\ell_{P,h}\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|u-r_{P,h}\|_\infty
 \geq\|r_{P,h}-r_P\|_\infty,
\]
because \(u\geq\ell_P\) and \(u\leq r_P\).  Since the two mesh obstacles belong to \(\mathcal C_{P,h}\), they furnish the matching lower bounds.  Therefore
\[
 d_H(\mathcal C_{P,h},\mathcal C_P)=e_h.
\]
Finally, \(|J_P(f)-J_P(f')|\leq\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|f-f'\|_\infty\).  Pairing each element of either nonempty compact class with an element of the other within Hausdorff distance \(e_h\), and then taking suprema or infima, shows that each signed support value changes by at most \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}e_h\).

%%% FIELD proof-finite-program
Fix a component-respecting \(h\)-net \(H_h\).  We first prove the claim that does not require compatibility.  For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), the net property supplies \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\in H_h\) in the same connected component with \(\|w-w_j\|_\infty\leq h\).  The H\"older restriction and the reverse triangle inequality give, for every \(x\in X_0\),
\[
 |g(d,z)-g(d_j,z_j)|\leq Gh^\beta,
 \quad
 \bigl||z|-|z_j|\bigr|\leq h,
 \quad
 \bigl||x-d|-|x-d_j|\bigr|\leq h.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \left|[g(d,z)-K|z|-L|x-d|]-[g(d_j,z_j)-K|z_j|-L|x-d_j|]\right|
 \leq Gh^\beta+(K+L)h=\bar e_h,
\]
and the same bound holds with both minus signs replaced by plus signs.  Since the mesh indexing set is contained in the full indexing set, taking the defining suprema and infima, and using that scalar truncation at \(-M\) or \(M\) is one-Lipschitz, gives
\[
 0\leq\ell_P(x)-\ell_{P,h}(x)\leq\bar e_h,
 \qquad
 0\leq r_{P,h}(x)-r_P(x)\leq\bar e_h.
\]
Taking suprema over \(x\) proves unconditionally that
\[
 e_h\leq\bar e_h=(K+L)h+Gh^\beta.
\]

Now assume \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  Restricting any \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) to \(B_h\) satisfies every constraint of \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), so this polytope is nonempty.  It is an intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces with \([-M,M]^{B_h}\), hence is compact.

If \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\), its bounded McShane extension \(E_hv\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, is bounded by \(M\), and equals \(v\) on \(B_h\).  Explicitly, these facts follow by clipping
\[
 x\longmapsto\min_{y\in B_h}\{v(y)+L|x-y|\}
\]
to \([-M,M]\): the pairwise node inequalities force equality at each node, and minima and clipping preserve the Lipschitz constant.  Therefore, for every \((d,z)\in H_h\),
\[
 |g(d,z)-E_hv(d)|=|g(d,z)-v(d)|\leq K|z|,
\]
so \(E_hv\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\).  Conversely, \(f\in\mathcal C_{P,h}\) implies \(f|_{B_h}\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\).

The pushforward identity \(\nu_{P,h}=(\pi_h)_\#\nu_P\) gives
\[
 J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)
 =\int_{X_0}v(\pi_h(x))\,d\nu_P(x).
\]
Since \(E_hv=v\) on \(B_h\), \(|x-\pi_h(x)|\leq h\), and \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\),
\[
 \left|J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-J_P(E_hv)\right|
 \leq L\int|x-\pi_h(x)|\,d|\nu_P|(x)
 \leq Lh.
\]
Here \(\|\nu_P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\) follows directly from \(\nu_P(B)=E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B\}]\) and \(|S|=1\).

By \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\), \(d_H(\mathcal C_{P,h},\mathcal C_P)=e_h\).  Hence every \(v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) has, for every \(\eta>0\), some \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) satisfying
\[
 \|E_hv-u\|_\infty\leq e_h+\eta,
 \qquad
 |J_P(E_hv)-J_P(u)|\leq e_h+\eta.
\]
Conversely, for \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), the vector \(v=u|_{B_h}\) is outer-feasible and
\[
 |J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-J_P(u)|
 =\left|\int\{u(\pi_h(x))-u(x)\}\,d\nu_P(x)\right|
 \leq Lh.
\]
The outer objective is linear and continuous on a nonempty compact polytope, so both extrema are attained.  The preceding two comparisons, followed by \(\eta\downarrow0\), prove
\[
 \left|\min_{v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}}J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-V_P^-\right|
 \leq e_h+Lh,
 \qquad
 \left|\max_{v\in\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}}J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{out}}(v)-V_P^+\right|
 \leq e_h+Lh.
\]
The unconditional envelope bound makes both right sides at most \(\bar e_h+Lh\).

For every outer-feasible \(v\), clipping stability gives
\[
 \Pi_P(E_hv)\in\mathcal C_P.
\]
Thus every value \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}(v)=J_P(\Pi_P(E_hv))\) is attained by an exact feasible trend.  The extension, clipping, and signed-integration maps are continuous in the relevant sup norms, so the inner extrema are also attained on the compact polytope.

Fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), let \(v=u|_{B_h}\), and use the nearest node \(\pi_h(x)\).  The functions \(u\) and \(E_hv\) are both \(L\)-Lipschitz and agree at that node; therefore
\[
 |E_hv(x)-u(x)|
 \leq|E_hv(x)-v(\pi_h(x))|+|u(\pi_h(x))-u(x)|
 \leq2Lh.
\]
Because \(u(x)\in[\ell_P(x),r_P(x)]\), scalar projection onto this interval cannot increase distance from \(u(x)\).  Hence
\[
 \|\Pi_P(E_hv)-u\|_\infty\leq2Lh,
 \qquad
 |J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}(v)-J_P(u)|\leq2Lh.
\]
The inner objective range is contained in \(J_P(\mathcal C_P)\), while the last display places an inner value within \(2Lh\) of every full-class value.  Moreover, \(\mathcal U_h^{\mathrm{out}}\) is convex and hence connected, and \(J_{P,h}^{\mathrm{in}}\) is continuous, so its real-valued image is an interval.  Thus the lower and upper endpoints of the two objective ranges, and equivalently the ranges in Hausdorff distance, differ by at most \(2Lh\).

On the domain under consideration \(A>0\).  The map \(j\mapsto(T-j)/A\) reverses endpoints and multiplies distances by \(A^{-1}\).  The outer and inner induced WAOSS intervals consequently have errors at most \(A^{-1}(\bar e_h+Lh)\) and \(A^{-1}(2Lh)\), respectively, and both are bounded by
\[
 A^{-1}(\bar e_h+2Lh).
\]
Every approximation above stays within a connected component and uses weak inequalities.  Touching rectangles belong to their common connected component; no step takes \(z\to0\) or divides by \(z\), so a component separated from zero changes is harmless; and equality in any obstacle constraint is admitted.  This proves all stated boundary and binding cases.

%%% FIELD proof-common-baseline-reduction
Suppose \(P(X=0)=1\) and \(Z>0\) almost surely.  Then \(S=1\), \(X_0=\{0\}\), and
\[
 \nu_P=\delta_0.
\]
A continuous baseline trend is a single scalar \(c=u(0)\); there is no nontrivial cross-baseline Lipschitz restriction.  The obstacle definitions reduce to
\[
 \underline c
 =\max\left\{-M,\sup_{(0,z)\in H}\{g(0,z)-Kz\}\right\},
 \qquad
 \overline c
 =\min\left\{M,\inf_{(0,z)\in H}\{g(0,z)+Kz\}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, \(c\in[\underline c,\overline c]\) is equivalent term by term to
\[
 |c|\leq M,
 \qquad
 |g(0,z)-c|\leq Kz
 \quad((0,z)\in H).
\]
Thus \(\text{thm:obstacle-compatibility}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal C_P=\{c\in\mathbb R:\underline c\leq c\leq\overline c\},
\]
with emptiness exactly when \(\underline c>\overline c\).  Under compatibility the endpoints are ordered.  Since \(J_P(c)=c\), \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) becomes
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-\overline c}{A},
       \frac{T-\underline c}{A}\right].
\]
This is exactly the scalar intersection program for the common-zero-baseline bounded conditional-average-slope model in de Chaisemartin, Ciccia, D'Haultf\oe{}uille, and Knau (2024, arXiv:2405.04465v4, Section 4.2.1, Theorem 4, and Appendix B.2).  The argument establishes algebraic recovery of that prior-art interval and makes no novelty claim for the scalar case.

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-band-to-program
Put \(D_I=\operatorname{diam}(I)\) and \(D_X=\operatorname{diam}(X_0)\).  Constants below may depend on the fixed family \(\mathscr R\), \(\alpha,\beta,G,B,f_0,a_0,K,L,M,D_I\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) or \(n\).  Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) supplies the finite-rectangle support, density lower bound, bounded outcomes, within-component H\"older condition, and \(A\geq a_0>0\).

First construct the boundary-aware partition.  Draw every horizontal and vertical line containing an edge of a rectangle in \(\mathscr R\).  The positive-area elementary rectangles of the resulting arrangement that lie in \(H\) have disjoint interiors and fixed positive side lengths.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), subdivide an elementary side of length \(a\) into \(m=\lceil\sqrt2a/h_n\rceil\) equal intervals.  Since eventually \(h_n<\sqrt2a\),
\[
 \frac{h_n}{2\sqrt2}\leq\frac{a}{m}\leq\frac{h_n}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Every resulting cell has Euclidean diameter at most \(h_n\), area at least \(h_n^2/8\), and lies within one connected component of \(H\).  Assign shared edges and corners deterministically to a cell whose closure contains them, using a lexicographic half-open convention.  This gives a Borel partition of all of \(H\), including touching boundaries.  Since \(\mathscr R\) is fixed and finite, for some \(C_{\mathscr R}<\infty\),
\[
 N_n\leq C_{\mathscr R}h_n^{-2}.
\]

For a cell \(C\), write \(p_C=P\{(X,Z)\in C\}\) and let \(N_C\) be its count.  Cell boundaries have Lebesgue measure zero, and the density lower bound gives
\[
 p_C\geq f_0\operatorname{area}(C)\geq f_0h_n^2/8.
\]
For \(N_C\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p_C)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-\lambda N_C}\), followed by \(1+y\leq e^y\), gives
\[
 P(N_C<np_C/2)
 \leq\exp\{np_C(\lambda/2+e^{-\lambda}-1)\}.
\]
At \(\lambda=\log2\), the exponent is at most \(-np_C/8\).  A union bound therefore shows that, except on an event of probability at most
\[
 N_n\exp(-nf_0h_n^2/64),
\]
every cell contains at least \(nf_0h_n^2/16\) observations.

Conditionally on the design points, \(\xi_i=\Delta Y_i-g(X_i,Z_i)\) are independent and conditionally mean zero.  Since \(\Delta Y_i\in[-2B,2B]\), each \(\xi_i\), conditionally on its design, lies in an interval of length \(4B\).  The exponential chord inequality yields
\[
 E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid X_i,Z_i]\leq e^{2\lambda^2B^2}.
\]
Markov optimization for a cell containing \(m\) observations then gives
\[
 P\!\left(\left|m^{-1}\sum_{i:(X_i,Z_i)\in C}\xi_i\right|>x
 \mathrel{\Big|}(X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^n\right)
 \leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{mx^2}{8B^2}\right).
\]
On the count event, take
\[
 x_n=2B\sqrt{\frac{128\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nf_0h_n^2}}.
\]
The conditional probability that any cell average of \(\xi_i\) exceeds \(x_n\) is at most
\[
 2N_n\exp\{-4\log(8N_n/\alpha)\}\leq\alpha/4.
\]
For any \(w\) assigned to a cell, all design points in that cell are in the same component and within distance \(h_n\); the H\"older bias is therefore at most \(Gh_n^\beta\).  By the definition of \(r_n\),
\[
 P\!\left\{\sup_{w\in H}|\widehat g_n(w)-g(w)|\leq r_n\right\}
 \geq1-\alpha/4-N_ne^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}.
\]
Because \(nh_n^2/\log n\to\infty\) and \(N_n=O(h_n^{-2})\), the last term is \(o(1)\), uniformly in \(P\).  No density smoothness or density upper bound was used, and a component separated from \(z=0\) causes no change.

Second, prove the signed-measure band.  Let \(\mathcal U\) be all \(u:X_0\to[-M,M]\) with Lipschitz constant at most \(L\), and put \(f_u(s,x)=su(x)\).  This class has a countable uniform-norm dense subclass, so the following supremum is measurable.  For an independent ghost empirical law \(P_n'\) and independent Rademacher signs \(\eta_i\), conditional Jensen, exchangeability, and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|
 &\leq E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P_n')f_u|\\
 &\leq2E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_iS_iu(X_i)\right|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Extend \(u\) to \([a,b]=\operatorname{conv}(X_0)\) with the same Lipschitz constant and clip it to \([-M,M]\).  It is absolutely continuous, \(|u'|\leq L\) almost everywhere, and
\[
 u(X_i)=u(a)+\int_a^b u'(t)\mathbf1\{X_i\geq t\}\,dt.
\]
Conditionally on the data, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\right|\leq\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\mathbf1\{X_i\geq t\}\right|\leq\sqrt n.
\]
Taking the supremum after the integral representation proves
\[
 E_\eta\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_i\eta_iS_iu(X_i)\right|
 \leq\frac{M+LD_X}{\sqrt n}.
\]

Let \(W_n=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|\).  Replacing one observation changes \(W_n\) by at most \(2M/n\).  The bounded-difference exponential argument therefore yields, for \(M>0\),
\[
 P(W_n-EW_n>x)\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{2M^2}\right);
\]
if \(M=0\), then \(W_n=0\).  Combining the last three displays with \(x=M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/n}\) shows, with probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\),
\[
 W_n\leq\frac{2(M+LD_X)+M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}}{\sqrt n}\leq q_n.
\]
For the last inequality, put \(c_\alpha=\sqrt{2\log(8/\alpha)}\); then \(c_\alpha\geq2\) and \(c_\alpha\geq\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}\), while \(q_n\sqrt n=(2M+LD_X)c_\alpha\).  Since \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal U\), this establishes
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal C_P}
 \left|\int_{X_0}u\,d(\widehat\nu_n-\nu_P)\right|\leq q_n.
\]

The variables \(S\Delta Y\) and \(|Z|\) lie in intervals of lengths \(4B\) and \(D_I\).  The same exponential inequality and the definitions of \(t_n,a_n\) give
\[
 P(|\widehat T_n-T|>t_n)\leq\alpha/8,
 \qquad
 P(|\widehat A_n-A|>a_n)\leq\alpha/8.
\]
The four failure probabilities sum to at most \(\alpha+o(1)\), uniformly over the regular class; independence among the events is unnecessary.

Third, on their intersection fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  Its restriction to \(\widehat B_n\) satisfies the level and pairwise Lipschitz inequalities.  At every support-mesh point \((d_j,z_j)\),
\[
 |\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \leq|\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-g(d_j,z_j)|+|g(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \leq r_n+K|z_j|.
\]
Thus \(u|_{\widehat B_n}\in\widehat{\mathcal U}_n^{\mathrm{out}}\), and the signed-measure and moment bands, together with \(A\in[a_0,D_I]\), imply simultaneously
\[
 (u|_{\widehat B_n},J_P(u),T,A)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n
 \qquad(u\in\mathcal C_P).
\]
This is the last clause defining \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), so
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]

Fourth, suppose \(\mathcal E_n(P)\) occurs.  Feasibility of every population tuple implies, after taking the infimum or supremum over \(u\in\mathcal C_P\) and using \(\text{def:frontier-values}\),
\[
 \inf_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},
 \qquad
 \sup_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \geq\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.
\]
For every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), iterated expectation and the observed cone give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |T-J_P(u)|
 &=\left|E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}]\right|\\
 &\leq E_P|g(X,Z)-u(X)|\leq KA.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the population interval is contained in \([-K,K]\), and endpoint clipping preserves the two outward inclusions.

For the reverse proximity bound, fix \((v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\).  The node inequalities permit the bounded Lipschitz extension
\[
 E_nv(x)=\max\{-M,\min\{M,\min_{x_k\in\widehat B_n}[v(x_k)+L|x-x_k|]\}\},
\]
which equals \(v\) at every node.  For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), choose a same-component support-mesh point \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\) with \(\|w-w_j\|_\infty\leq s_n\).  Euclidean distance is at most \(\sqrt2s_n\).  The H\"older restriction, regression band, empirical cone, reverse triangle inequality, and Lipschitz property give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g(d,z)-E_nv(d)|
 &\leq2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta+r_n+(K|z_j|+r_n)+Ls_n\\
 &\leq K|z|+\varepsilon_n,\\
 \varepsilon_n&:=2r_n+(K+L)s_n+2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta\\
 &\leq4r_n+(K+L)s_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s_n\leq h_n\), \(r_n\geq Gh_n^\beta\), and \(2^{\beta/2}<2\).  Hence \(E_nv\in\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon_n}\).  Compatibility and \(\text{thm:clipping-stability}\) produce
\[
 u^*=\Pi_P(E_nv)\in\mathcal C_P,
 \qquad
 \|E_nv-u^*\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon_n.
\]

Every observed \(X_i\) belongs to \(\widehat B_n\), so \(E_nv(X_i)=v(X_i)\).  The program constraint on \(j\), \(\|\widehat\nu_n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq1\), the uniform signed-measure band, and the last display imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |j-J_P(u^*)|
 &\leq\left|j-\int E_nv\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
 +\left|\int(E_nv-u^*)\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
 +\left|\int u^*\,d\widehat\nu_n-J_P(u^*)\right|\\
 &\leq\varepsilon_n+2q_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Likewise, feasibility and the population bands give
\[
 |t-T|\leq2t_n,
 \qquad
 |a-A|\leq2a_n.
\]
Both denominators are at least \(a_0\), and \(|T-J_P(u^*)|/A\leq K\).  Adding and subtracting \((T-J_P(u^*))/a\) proves
\[
 \left|\frac{t-j}{a}-\frac{T-J_P(u^*)}{A}\right|
 \leq\frac{\varepsilon_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.
\]
Every empirical feasible ratio is therefore within this distance of the population interval.  Together with the outward inclusions, and because clipping only reduces excess, this gives
\[
 0\leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A}-\widehat L_n\leq d_n,
 \qquad
 0\leq\widehat U_n-\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\leq d_n,
\]
where
\[
 d_n=\frac{4r_n+(K+L)s_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), the empirical feasible set is nonempty and the two inclusions rule out crossing, so the reset rule is not invoked.  Since \(A\leq D_I\), \(d_n\) is at most a fixed-class multiple of
\[
 \frac{r_n+(K+L)s_n+q_n+t_n+Ka_n}{A}.
\]
This exhibits signed-measure uncertainty as \(q_n/A\) and denominator uncertainty as \(Ka_n/A\).  All constraints are weak; no strict feasibility or optimizer uniqueness has entered.

Fifth, establish measurability.  Order deterministic mesh nodes and observed baseline coordinates lexicographically and remove duplicates by the first-index rule.  For fixed \(n\), the finitely many ordering and coincidence patterns are Borel strata.  On each stratum, cell counts, cell sums, the empty-cell convention, node coordinates, and all program coefficients are Borel functions of the sample.  Since \(a\in[a_0,D_I]\), set
\[
 c=1/a,
 \qquad \widetilde v=cv,
 \qquad \widetilde j=cj,
 \qquad \widetilde t=ct.
\]
For example, the denominator band becomes \(|1-c\widehat A_n|\leq ca_n\), and the signed-objective band becomes
\[
 \left|\widetilde j-\int\widetilde v\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|\leq cq_n.
\]
Each absolute value is two linear inequalities; all remaining scaled constraints are also linear, and the ratio objective is \(\widetilde t-\widetilde j\).  The bounds \(D_I^{-1}\leq c\leq a_0^{-1}\), together with the scaled variable bounds, make the transformed feasible set a compact polytope whenever it is nonempty.

On each Borel stratum, enumerate the finitely many possible active-constraint bases.  On every nonzero-determinant sub-stratum, a candidate vertex is a Borel rational function of the coefficients; feasibility and objective comparisons are finite Borel comparisons.  A nonempty compact polytope has a vertex, and successive minimization of the objective and then each coordinate selects the lexicographically least optimizer among these candidates.  Thus the selected optima are Borel on every stratum.  Empty-set testing, clipping, crossing, and resetting are Borel comparisons as well.  Hence \(\widehat L_n\), \(\widehat U_n\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\) are Borel measurable.

Finally, \(N_n=O(h_n^{-2})\) and the definition of \(h_n\) imply
\[
 h_n^\beta
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nh_n^2}}
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right).
\]
Because \(0<\beta\leq1\), \(s_n\leq h_n=O(h_n^\beta)\), while \(q_n,t_n,a_n=O(n^{-1/2})\).  Substitution into the displayed bound for \(d_n\) proves uniformly over compatible \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) that
\[
 d_n=O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}+n^{-1/2}\right).
\]
This proves the stated probability, inclusion, ratio-error, rate, and measurability conclusions.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) with \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).  On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), \(\text{thm:uniform-band-to-program}\) gives
\[
 \widehat L_n\leq\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},
 \qquad
 \widehat U_n\geq\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.
\]
Membership in the regular class gives \(A\geq a_0>0\), while \(V_P^-\leq V_P^+\).  By \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\),
\[
 \Theta(P)=
 \left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right]
 \subseteq[-K,K].
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathcal E_n(P)
 \subseteq
 \{\Theta(P)\subseteq[\widehat L_n,\widehat U_n]\}
 =\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}.
\]
The endpoint measurability established in \(\text{thm:uniform-band-to-program}\) makes this an event.  Taking probabilities, then the infimum over compatible \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\), and finally the lower limit gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}\\
 &\qquad\geq
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because the population set lies in \([-K,K]\), intersecting the empirical interval with that range cannot remove a target value.  No step assumes \(R(P)>0\), strict obstacle inequalities, or a unique optimizer; the conclusion therefore includes singleton sets, binding constraints, and nonunique optima.

%%% FIELD proof-source-class-recession-ray
The first rectangle in \(\text{def:continuous-hole-witness}\) has area \(1/2\) and density \(2p_{\circ}\), and the second has area \(1/2\) and density \(2(1-p_{\circ})\).  They therefore have probabilities \(p_{\circ}\) and \(1-p_{\circ}\).  The density is bounded and strictly positive on each rectangle, and observed outcomes are identically zero.  Absolute continuity gives
\[
 P^{\circ}(Z=0)=0.
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), the second rectangle has positive probability in \(\{|Z|\leq\eta\}\), proving marginal quasi-stayers directly.  In contrast, for every \(x\in[-1,0)\),
\[
 P^{\circ}(|Z|\leq\eta\mid X=x)=0
 \qquad(0<\eta<1/2),
\]
and this baseline region has \(P_X^{\circ}\)-probability \(p_{\circ}\).

Fix \(c\in\mathbb R\) and take \(Q_c^{\circ}\) from \(\text{def:source-recession-ray}\).  On the first rectangle \(|\phi(X)|\leq1/2\leq|Z|\), while on the second \(\phi(X)=0\).  Hence, outside the null event \(\{Z=0\}\),
\[
 \left|\frac{c\phi(X)}Z\right|\leq|c|.
\]
Since \(D_1,D_2,d\in[-2,2]\), each fixed-dose schedule is bounded by \(4|c|\), is integrable, is affine in \(d\), and has the finite derivative \(-c\phi(X)/Z\).  At realized doses,
\[
 Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_1)=0=Y_1,
 \qquad
 Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_2)=0=Y_2,
\]
so consistency and the entire observed law are preserved.

For every \(d\in[-2,2]\), on one event common to all doses,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)
 &=-\frac{c\phi(X)}Z\{(d-D_2)-(d-X)\}\\
 &=c\phi(X).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The right side is \(X\)-measurable.  Thus common versions satisfy
\[
 E_{Q_c^{\circ}}[Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)\mid X,Z]
 =c\phi(X)
 =E_{Q_c^{\circ}}[Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)\mid X]
\]
simultaneously for every \(d\in[-2,2]\).  All requirements in \(\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\) therefore hold for every finite \(c\).

The realized period-two causal movement is
\[
 Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_1)=-c\phi(X).
\]
On the first rectangle \(S=1\), and on the second \(\phi(X)=0\), so
\[
 E_{P^{\circ}}[S\phi(X)]
 =p_{\circ}\int_{-1}^{0}\min\{-x,1/2\}\,dx
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}8.
\]
Also,
\[
 A=E_{P^{\circ}}|Z|
 =\frac{3p_{\circ}}4+\frac{1-p_{\circ}}8
 =\frac{1+5p_{\circ}}8>0.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \theta(Q_c^{\circ})
 =-\frac{cE_{P^{\circ}}[S\phi(X)]}{A}
 =-\frac{3cp_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}.
\]
The coefficient of \(c\) is nonzero, and every finite \(c\) yields a legal source completion.  Hence the source-class image contains \(\mathbb R\).  Conversely, source-class integrability and \(A>0\) make every \(\theta(Q)\) finite, so
\[
 \{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\}=\mathbb R.
\]

For the bounded sensitivity class, \(g=0\) and \(K=L=M=1\).  The support points \((x,0)\) for \(x\in[0,1]\) force every feasible \(u\) to vanish there.  For \(x\in[-1,0]\), the point \((x,1/2)\) and global one-Lipschitz continuity imply
\[
 |u(x)|\leq\min\{-x,1/2\}=\phi(x).
\]
Both \(\phi\) and \(-\phi\) satisfy every level, Lipschitz, and support-cone restriction, so they are feasible.  Conditional sign symmetry on the second rectangle and positivity on the first give
\[
 d\nu_{P^{\circ}}(x)=p_{\circ}\mathbf1\{x\in[-1,0]\}\,dx.
\]
Therefore
\[
 -\frac{3p_{\circ}}8\leq J_{P^{\circ}}(u)\leq\frac{3p_{\circ}}8,
\]
with equality at \(-\phi\) and \(\phi\).  Since \(T=0\), \(\text{thm:full-law-sharpness}\) and \(\text{thm:attained-interval}\) yield
\[
 \Theta(P^{\circ})=
 \left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
        \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]
The constructions give \(Q_+^{\circ}=Q_1^{\circ}\) and \(Q_-^{\circ}=Q_{-1}^{\circ}\); the formula for \(\theta(Q_c^{\circ})\) proves endpoint attainment.  For \(c\in\{-1,1\}\), the slope bound above is one, the trend \(c\phi\) is bounded and globally one-Lipschitz, and the movement \(-c\phi(X)\) obeys \(|c\phi(X)|\leq|Z|\).  Together with observed-law preservation, consistency, integrability, and the common all-dose identity, these facts verify every clause of the strengthened sensitivity class, including both per-dose parallel trends and the separate realized-baseline identity.

Finally, under baseline-conditional zero support, \((x,0)\in H\) at every relevant baseline, so the obstacle restriction forces \(u(x)=g(x,0)\).  The bounded-sensitivity set then collapses to
\[
 \left\{\frac{T-\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x)}{A}\right\}.
\]
Thus the unrestricted published class gives all of \(\mathbb R\) at this witness, the explicit bounded-slope/Lipschitz class gives the finite interval above, and conditional zero support yields the weighted singleton.  Only the failure of the source singleton transfers to the unrestricted source class; the finite sensitivity interval does not.
