%%% FIELD legal-completion-construction
The bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity completion class is
\[
\mathfrak Q(P)=\left\{Q:\begin{array}{l}
Q\text{ is a law over the declared one-argument static schedules on }I
\text{ and matches the entire observed law }P;\\
\text{consistency and fixed-dose latent integrability hold, and }g\in C(H);\\
Y_2^Q(D_1)-Y_1^Q(D_1)\text{ and }Y_2^Q(D_2)-Y_2^Q(D_1)\text{ are integrable};\\
\text{for every fixed }d\in X_0\text{, the per-dose conditional-parallel-trends equality holds};\\
E_Q[Y_2^Q(D_1)-Y_1^Q(D_1)\mid X]\text{ admits the continuous version }m_Q(X);\\
E_Q[Y_2^Q(D_1)-Y_1^Q(D_1)\mid X,Z]=m_Q(X)\quad Q\text{-almost surely};\\
\left|E_Q[Y_2^Q(D_2)-Y_2^Q(D_1)\mid X,Z]\right|\leq K|Z|quad Q\text{-almost surely};\\
\|m_Q\|_\infty\leq M,\quad
|m_Q(x)-m_Q(x')|\leq L|x-x'|\quad\text{for all }x,x'\in X_0
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
No marginal quasi-stayer restriction is part of this definition. The source's per-dose parallel-trends condition is retained without a common-event strengthening. The realized-baseline identity is imposed separately and is not obtained by substituting \(d=X\) into that fixed-dose condition. Realized-diagonal and causal-movement integrability, the finite \(K\) movement bound, the bounded globally \(L\)-Lipschitz trend, and the continuous support version \(g\) are explicit sensitivity restrictions. On an observed law satisfying the published assumptions, comparison with the source class concerns only source completions that also satisfy these additional restrictions. No global equality or inclusion of completion classes is asserted, and the finite sharp interval for this sensitivity class does not transfer upward to the unrestricted published class.

Under the declared observed-law conditions, the source's per-dose conditional-parallel-trends condition, and the separate realized-baseline identity, the bounded-slope/Lipschitz completion class \(\mathfrak Q(P)\) is nonempty if and only if \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\). \]

Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)=\Delta Y-g(X,Z)+u(X)
\qquad\text{for every }d\in I.
\]
Consequently the lift satisfies the per-dose parallel-trends condition for every fixed dose and, separately, the realized-baseline identity; in fact its displayed equality yields common versions for all \(d\in I\). %%% FIELD full-sharpness-proof
Because the declared observed law \(P\) is supported on \(\mathbb R^2\times I^2\) and \(I\) is compact,
\[
|Z|=|D_2-D_1|\leq\operatorname{diam}(I)
\qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The no-stayer condition gives \(|Z|>0\) almost surely, and hence
\[
0<A=E_P|Z|\leq\operatorname{diam}(I)<\infty. \tag{1}
\]
Observed integrability gives
\[
E_P|\Delta Y|\leq E_P(|Y_1|+|Y_2|)<\infty.
\]
Since \(|S|=1\) almost surely under the no-stayer condition,
\[
|T|=|E_P[S\Delta Y]|\leq E_P|\Delta Y|<\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Thus all ratios used below have a finite, strictly positive denominator.

Take \(Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\), and abbreviate
\[
B_Q=Y_2^Q(D_1)-Y_1^Q(D_1),
\qquad
C_Q=Y_2^Q(D_2)-Y_2^Q(D_1).
\]
The two variables are integrable by the explicit realized-diagonal and causal-movement requirements in \(\text{def:legal-completion-class}\). Consistency and the observed-law match give
\[
\Delta Y=C_Q+B_Q
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{3}
\]
The separate realized-baseline identity gives directly, without substituting a random dose into the per-dose parallel-trends condition,
\[
E_Q[B_Q\mid X,Z]=m_Q(X)
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{4}
\]
Because \(Q\) matches the complete observed law \(P\), the declared support version \(g\) is also a version of \(E_Q[\Delta Y\mid X,Z]\). Taking conditional expectations in (3), using linearity for the integrable summands and then (4), yields
\[
E_Q[C_Q\mid X,Z]=g(X,Z)-m_Q(X)
\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}. \tag{5}
\]
The mean-movement bound therefore implies
\[
|g(x,z)-m_Q(x)|\leq K|z|
\qquad P_{XZ}\text{-almost everywhere}. \tag{6}
\]
The function
\[
(x,z)\longmapsto |g(x,z)-m_Q(x)|-K|z|
\]
is continuous on \(H\): \(g\in C(H)\), and the continuous-version condition gives \(m_Q\in C(X_0)\). If this function were positive at some \((x_0,z_0)\in H=\operatorname{supp}_P(X,Z)\), continuity would make it positive on the intersection of \(H\) with an open neighborhood of \((x_0,z_0)\). By the definition of support that neighborhood has positive \(P_{XZ}\)-probability, contradicting (6). Hence
\[
|g(x,z)-m_Q(x)|\leq K|z|
\qquad\text{for every }(x,z)\in H. \tag{7}
\]
The level and global Lipschitz restrictions on \(m_Q\) are also member conditions of \(\mathfrak Q(P)\). Comparing them and (7) with \(\text{def:feasible-trend-class}\) proves
\[
m_Q\in\mathcal C_P. \tag{8}
\]
In particular, \(\mathfrak Q(P)\neq\varnothing\) implies \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\).

We next derive the causal-functional relationship. By the causal definition of \(\theta(Q)\), equation (3), the bounded \((X,Z)\)-measurable variable \(S\), equation (4), and iterated expectations,
\[
\begin{aligned}
A\theta(Q)
&=E_Q[SC_Q]\\
&=E_P[S\Delta Y]-E_Q[SB_Q]\\
&=T-E_Q\!\left[S E_Q[B_Q\mid X,Z]\right]\\
&=T-E_P[Sm_Q(X)].
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
For every bounded Borel function \(v\) on \(X_0\), the definition
\(\nu_P(B)=E_P[S\mathbf 1\{X\in B\}]\), first for simple functions and then by bounded convergence, gives
\[
\int_{X_0}v(x)\,d\nu_P(x)=E_P[Sv(X)]. \tag{10}
\]
Applying (10) to the bounded continuous function \(m_Q\), and then using \(\text{def:signed-functional}\), turns (9) into
\[
\theta(Q)=\frac{T-J_P(m_Q)}{A}. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (8) and (11), followed by \(\text{def:waoss-set}\), prove
\[
\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}\subseteq\Theta(P). \tag{12}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, fix arbitrary \(u\in\mathcal C_P\). Retain the observed vector on its \(P\)-probability space. On \(\{Z\neq0\}\), define
\[
b_u=\frac{g(X,Z)-u(X)}{Z},
\]
and put \(b_u=0\) on the null event \(\{Z=0\}\). The construction is measurable, and the support obstacle in \(\text{def:feasible-trend-class}\) gives
\[
|b_u|\leq K
\qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{13}
\]
For every \(d\in I\), define the affine schedules from \(\text{def:affine-lift}\):
\[
Y_1^{Q_u}(d)=Y_1+b_u(d-D_1),
\qquad
Y_2^{Q_u}(d)=Y_2+b_u(d-D_2). \tag{14}
\]
They are jointly measurable in the random argument and \(d\). At the realized doses,
\[
Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)=Y_1,
\qquad
Y_2^{Q_u}(D_2)=Y_2. \tag{15}
\]
Thus the complete observed law remains \(P\), and consistency holds. For each fixed \(d\in I\), compactness of \(I\), (13), and observed integrability imply
\[
|Y_t^{Q_u}(d)|\leq |Y_t|+K\operatorname{diam}(I),
\qquad
E_{Q_u}|Y_t^{Q_u}(d)|<\infty. \tag{16}
\]
Moreover, for \(d,d'\in I\),
\[
|Y_t^{Q_u}(d)-Y_t^{Q_u}(d')|
=|b_u|\,|d-d'|
\leq K|d-d'|, \tag{17}
\]
so each period schedule is individually \(K\)-Lipschitz.

Direct subtraction in (14) gives, pointwise off \(\{Z=0\}\) and simultaneously for all \(d\in I\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)
&=\Delta Y+b_u(D_1-D_2)\\
&=\Delta Y-b_uZ\\
&=\Delta Y-g(X,Z)+u(X).
\end{aligned} \tag{18}
\]
The right side is integrable because
\[
E_P|g(X,Z)|
\leq E_P\!\left[E_P(|\Delta Y|\mid X,Z)\right]
=E_P|\Delta Y|<\infty
\]
and \(|u(X)|\leq M\). Since \(g(X,Z)\) is a version of \(E_P[\Delta Y\mid X,Z]\), (18) gives, for every fixed \(d\in I\),
\[
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)\mid X,Z]=u(X). \tag{19}
\]
Taking the conditional expectation of (19) given \(X\) gives
\[
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(d)-Y_1^{Q_u}(d)\mid X]=u(X). \tag{20}
\]
Thus (19)--(20) verify the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition. In fact, because the random difference in (18) is independent of \(d\), the displayed versions can be chosen identically for every \(d\in I\); this stronger fact is a property derived for the lift, not an assumption used to evaluate a random diagonal.

Taking \(d=D_1=X\) in the pointwise schedule identity (18), rather than in a fixed-dose conditional-expectation statement, gives
\[
Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)-Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)
=\Delta Y-g(X,Z)+u(X). \tag{21}
\]
Its integrability was proved above. Conditioning (21) on \((X,Z)\) and on \(X\), respectively, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)-Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)\mid X,Z]&=u(X),\\
E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)-Y_1^{Q_u}(D_1)\mid X]&=u(X).
\end{aligned} \tag{22}
\]
Because \(u\in C(X_0)\), equation (22) proves both that \(m_{Q_u}=u\) is a continuous version and that the separate realized-baseline identity holds. Its level and global \(L\)-Lipschitz restrictions follow from \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).

The realized period-two causal movement is
\[
Y_2^{Q_u}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)
=b_uZ=g(X,Z)-u(X). \tag{23}
\]
By feasibility and bounded treatment support,
\[
|g(X,Z)-u(X)|\leq K|Z|leq K\operatorname{diam}(I), \tag{24}
\]
so the movement is integrable. Since it is \((X,Z)\)-measurable, (24) also gives
\[
\left|E_{Q_u}[Y_2^{Q_u}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_u}(D_1)\mid X,Z]\right|
=|g(X,Z)-u(X)|
\leq K|Z|. \tag{25}
\]
Equations (13)--(25) verify every member condition in \(\text{def:legal-completion-class}\). Therefore
\[
Q_u\in\mathfrak Q(P), \tag{26}
\]
which also proves \(\mathcal C_P\neq\varnothing\Rightarrow\mathfrak Q(P)\neq\varnothing\).

Finally, the causal definition, (23), and iterated expectations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta(Q_u)
&=\frac{E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}]}{A}\\
&=\frac{E_P[S\Delta Y]-E_P[Su(X)]}{A}\\
&=\frac{T-J_P(u)}{A},
\end{aligned} \tag{27}
\]
where the second equality uses \(E_P[Sg(X,Z)]=E_P[S\Delta Y]\) and the third uses (10). Since \(u\) was arbitrary, (26)--(27) prove \(\Theta(P)\subseteq\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q(P)\}\). Combined with (12), this proves the sharp set equality and full-law attainment.

No step uses marginal quasi-stayers. The forward direction uses the realized-baseline identity directly; the source's per-dose parallel-trends condition is retained as a class restriction but is not misused to justify a random-diagonal substitution. The affine lifts satisfy both clauses, as (19)--(22) show. The finite movement bound, bounded globally Lipschitz trend, continuous support version of \(g\), explicit integrability conditions, and realized-baseline identity remain additional sensitivity restrictions. Therefore the equality proved here characterizes \(\mathfrak Q(P)\) only and does not transfer the finite interval to the unrestricted published completion class.

Let \(\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\) be the published Assumptions 1--4 completion class and let \(Q_c^{\circ}\) be the affine completion generated by \(c\in\mathbb R\) and \(\phi\). The law \(P^{\circ}\) has a bounded density that is strictly positive on each rectangle of its finite rectangle support and has uniformly bounded observed outcomes. It satisfies marginal quasi-stayers, whereas baseline-conditional positivity fails for every \(x\in[-1,0)\), a region of \(P^{\circ}_X\)-probability \(p_{\circ}\). For every finite \(c\), \(Q_c^{\circ}\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\), its schedules are affine and integrable, it preserves the entire observed law, and it satisfies parallel trends simultaneously for every \(d\in[-2,2]\). Moreover,
\[
\theta(Q_c^{\circ})=-\frac{3c p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
\qquad
\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\}=\mathbb R.
\]
For the strengthened sensitivity class with \(K=L=M=1\), \(Q_+^{\circ}=Q_1^{\circ}\) and \(Q_-^{\circ}=Q_{-1}^{\circ}\) attain the endpoints
\[
\Theta(P^{\circ})=
\left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
       \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right].
\]
Thus the identification elbow has three distinct stages: the unrestricted published class gives all of \(\mathbb R\) at this regular witness; the bounded-slope/Lipschitz class gives the displayed finite obstacle interval; and, under baseline-conditional zero support, the obstacle restriction fixes \(u(x)=g(x,0)\) and yields the published weighted singleton formula. Only the negative conclusion for the published singleton transfers to the published class; the finite interval does not.
%%% FIELD source-ray-proof
The first rectangle in \(\text{def:continuous-hole-witness}\) has area \(1/2\) and density \(2p_{\circ}\), hence probability \(p_{\circ}\). The second rectangle also has area \(1/2\) and density \(2(1-p_{\circ})\), hence probability \(1-p_{\circ}\). Since \(p_{\circ}\in(0,1)\), the density is bounded and has a strictly positive constant value on each rectangle. The observed outcomes are identically zero and are therefore uniformly bounded. Absolute continuity gives
\[
P^{\circ}(Z=0)=0. \tag{28}
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), the intersection of the second rectangle with \(\{|z|\leq\eta\}\) has positive Lebesgue measure. Its density is positive, so
\[
P^{\circ}(|Z|\leq\eta)>0. \tag{29}
\]
Thus marginal quasi-stayers are verified directly by the witness; no marginal quasi-stayer assumption is a premise of this proposition. In contrast, for every \(x\in[-1,0)\), the conditional support of \(Z\) is \([1/2,1]\), and hence
\[
P^{\circ}(|Z|\leq\eta\mid X=x)=0
\qquad(0<\eta<1/2). \tag{30}
\]
The first-rectangle marginal density of \(X\) is \(p_{\circ}\) on \([-1,0]\), so the set in (30) has \(P_X^{\circ}\)-probability \(p_{\circ}\).

Fix finite \(c\in\mathbb R\) and take \(Q_c^{\circ}\) from \(\text{def:source-recession-ray}\). On the first rectangle, \(|\phi(X)|\leq1/2\leq|Z|\), while on the second rectangle \(\phi(X)=0\). Therefore, outside the null event in (28),
\[
\left|\frac{c\phi(X)}{Z}\right|\leq |c|. \tag{31}
\]
Because \(D_1,D_2,d\in[-2,2]\), each schedule is bounded in absolute value by \(4|c|\) for every fixed \(d\). It is thus integrable and affine in \(d\), with everywhere finite derivative \(-c\phi(X)/Z\). At the realized doses,
\[
Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_1)=0=Y_1,
\qquad
Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_2)=0=Y_2. \tag{32}
\]
Hence consistency holds and the entire observed vector retains law \(P^{\circ}\).

For every \(d\in[-2,2]\), direct subtraction gives on the same event for all \(d\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)
&=-\frac{c\phi(X)}{Z}\{(d-D_2)-(d-X)\}\\
&=c\phi(X).
\end{aligned} \tag{33}
\]
The right side is measurable with respect to \(X\). Consequently one may choose versions, simultaneously for every \(d\in[-2,2]\), such that
\[
E_{Q_c^{\circ}}[Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)\mid X,Z]
=c\phi(X)
=E_{Q_c^{\circ}}[Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)-Y_1^{Q_c^{\circ}}(d)\mid X]. \tag{34}
\]
This verifies the per-dose parallel-trends restriction in \(\text{def:published-source-completion-class}\), and indeed the stronger all-dose version. Equations (31)--(34), together with the support properties, prove
\[
Q_c^{\circ}\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})
\qquad\text{for every finite }c. \tag{35}
\]

The realized period-two causal movement is
\[
Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_2)-Y_2^{Q_c^{\circ}}(D_1)=-c\phi(X). \tag{36}
\]
On the first rectangle \(S=1\); on the second, \(\phi(X)=0\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{P^{\circ}}[S\phi(X)]
&=p_{\circ}\int_{-1}^{0}\min\{-x,1/2\}\,dx\\
&=\frac{3p_{\circ}}8.
\end{aligned} \tag{37}
\]
Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{P^{\circ}}|Z|
&=p_{\circ}E[Z\mid Z\in[1/2,1]]
 +(1-p_{\circ})E[|Z|\mid Z\in[-1/4,1/4]]\\
&=\frac{3p_{\circ}}4+\frac{1-p_{\circ}}8
=\frac{1+5p_{\circ}}8>0.
\end{aligned} \tag{38}
\]
The causal definition of \(\theta\), together with (36)--(38), yields
\[
\theta(Q_c^{\circ})
=-\frac{cE_{P^{\circ}}[S\phi(X)]}{E_{P^{\circ}}|Z|}
=-\frac{3cp_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}. \tag{39}
\]
Because the coefficient of \(c\) is nonzero and every finite \(c\) gives a legal source completion by (35), the source-class image contains \(\mathbb R\). Conversely, the source class requires integrability of the movement entering \(\theta\), and (38) gives a positive denominator, so every value of \(\theta(Q)\) is a finite real number. Therefore
\[
\{\theta(Q):Q\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathrm{src}}(P^{\circ})\}=\mathbb R. \tag{40}
\]

For the bounded-sensitivity calculation, \(\Delta Y=0\) under \(P^{\circ}\), so \(g=0\) is a continuous conditional-mean support version. Set \(K=L=M=1\). For every \(u\in\mathcal C_{P^{\circ}}\), the points \((x,0)\in H\) for \(x\in[0,1]\) imply
\[
|u(x)|=|g(x,0)-u(x)|\leq |0|=0,
\]
and therefore \(u=0\) on \([0,1]\). In particular \(u(0)=0\). For \(x\in[-1,0]\), the support point \((x,1/2)\) gives \(|u(x)|\leq1/2\), while global one-Lipschitz continuity and \(u(0)=0\) give \(|u(x)|\leq -x\). Thus
\[
|u(x)|\leq\phi(x)
\qquad(x\in[-1,1]). \tag{41}
\]
Both \(\phi\) and \(-\phi\) are continuous, bounded by one, and globally one-Lipschitz. On the first rectangle their absolute value is at most \(1/2\leq|z|\), and on the second they vanish. Hence
\[
\phi,-\phi\in\mathcal C_{P^{\circ}}. \tag{42}
\]

On the first rectangle \(S=1\). On the second, conditional symmetry of \(Z\) gives \(E[S\mid X]=0\). Therefore
\[
d\nu_{P^{\circ}}(x)=p_{\circ}\mathbf 1\{x\in[-1,0]\}\,dx. \tag{43}
\]
Equations (37), (41)--(43) imply
\[
-\frac{3p_{\circ}}8
\leq J_{P^{\circ}}(u)
\leq\frac{3p_{\circ}}8, \tag{44}
\]
with the lower and upper values attained by \(-\phi\) and \(\phi\), respectively. Here \(T=0\), and (38) gives \(A=(1+5p_{\circ})/8\). The full-law sharpness theorem and the already established attained-interval theorem therefore yield
\[
\Theta(P^{\circ})=
\left[-\frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}},
       \frac{3p_{\circ}}{1+5p_{\circ}}\right]. \tag{45}
\]
The definitions give \(Q_+^{\circ}=Q_1^{\circ}\) and \(Q_-^{\circ}=Q_{-1}^{\circ}\). Equation (39) shows that these laws attain the lower and upper endpoints, respectively. As affine lifts, they satisfy both the per-dose condition and the separate realized-baseline identity; this also follows directly from (33)--(34), because the realized baseline contrast is \(c\phi(X)\).

Finally, if baseline-conditional zero support holds, then \((x,0)\in H\) for every relevant \(x\). The obstacle defining \(\mathcal C_P\) gives
\[
|g(x,0)-u(x)|\leq K|0|=0,
\]
so every feasible trend satisfies \(u(x)=g(x,0)\). Substitution into \(\text{def:waoss-set}\) gives the singleton
\[
\left\{\frac{T-\int_{X_0}g(x,0)\,d\nu_P(x)}{A}\right\}. \tag{46}
\]
Thus the source class gives all of \(\mathbb R\) at this witness, the explicit bounded-slope/Lipschitz sensitivity class gives (45), and conditional zero support gives (46). Only the failure of the source singleton transfers to the unrestricted source class; the finite sensitivity interval does not.
