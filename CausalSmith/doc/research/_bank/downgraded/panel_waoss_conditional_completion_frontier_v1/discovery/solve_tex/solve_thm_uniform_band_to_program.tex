%%% FIELD uniform-proof
Put \(D_I=\operatorname{diam}(I)\) and \(D_X=\operatorname{diam}(X_0)\).  Throughout the proof, constants may depend on the fixed rectangle family \(\mathscr R\), \(\alpha,\beta,G,B,f_0,a_0,K,L,M,D_I\), but not on \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) or on \(n\).  We prove the result in five steps.

First, we verify the boundary-aware partition and the regression band.  Draw every horizontal and vertical line containing an edge of a rectangle in the fixed finite family \(\mathscr R\).  The resulting positive-area elementary rectangles contained in \(H\) have disjoint interiors and fixed positive side lengths.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), subdivide an elementary side of length \(a\) into \(m=\lceil \sqrt{2}a/h_n\rceil\) equal intervals.  Since \(h_n<\sqrt{2}a\) eventually,
\[
 \frac{h_n}{2\sqrt{2}}\le \frac{a}{m}\le \frac{h_n}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
Consequently every resulting two-dimensional cell has Euclidean diameter at most \(h_n\), area at least \(h_n^2/8\), and is contained in one connected component of \(H\).  Assign every shared edge and corner by a fixed lexicographic half-open convention to a cell whose closure contains it.  This gives a deterministic Borel partition of all of \(H\), including touching rectangle boundaries, and finiteness of \(\mathscr R\) gives
\[
 N_n\le C_{\mathscr R}h_n^{-2}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
for a finite constant \(C_{\mathscr R}\).  Rectangles which touch belong to the same connected component, so the boundary assignment never violates the component restriction.

For a cell \(C\), write \(p_C=P\{(X,Z)\in C\}\) and let \(N_C\) be its sample count.  The density-lower condition in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) and the area bound imply
\[
 p_C\ge f_0\operatorname{area}(C)\ge \frac{f_0h_n^2}{8}.                         \tag{2}
\]
Here and below boundary sets have Lebesgue, hence \(P\)-, measure zero.  For completeness, if \(N_C\) is binomial with parameters \((n,p_C)\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-\lambda N_C}\), followed by \(1+y\le e^y\), yields
\[
 P(N_C<np_C/2)
 \le \exp\{np_C(\lambda/2+e^{-\lambda}-1)\}.
\]
Taking \(\lambda=\log 2\) and using \((\log 2)/2-1/2<-1/8\) gives
\[
 P(N_C<np_C/2)\le e^{-np_C/8}\le e^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}.                              \tag{3}
\]
Thus, outside an event of probability at most
\[
 N_ne^{-nf_0h_n^2/64},                                                            \tag{4}
\]
every cell contains at least \(nf_0h_n^2/16\) observations.

Conditionally on the design points \((X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^n\), the variables
\[
 \xi_i=\Delta Y_i-g(X_i,Z_i)
\]
are independent and conditionally mean zero by the definition of \(g\) and iid sampling.  The bounded-outcome condition gives \(\Delta Y_i\in[-2B,2B]\), so, conditionally on \((X_i,Z_i)\), each \(\xi_i\) lies in an interval of length at most \(4B\).  The elementary convexity bound for a centered random variable in an interval of length \(4B\) is
\[
 E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid X_i,Z_i]\le e^{2\lambda^2B^2}.
\]
Applying Markov's inequality to the exponential of a sum and optimizing over \(\lambda\) therefore gives, for a cell containing \(m\) observations,
\[
 P\!\left(\left|m^{-1}\sum_{i:(X_i,Z_i)\in C}\xi_i\right|>x
       \ \middle|\ (X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^n\right)
 \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{mx^2}{8B^2}\right).                                  \tag{5}
\]
On the count event, take
\[
 x_n=2B\sqrt{\frac{128\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nf_0h_n^2}}.
\]
Equations (2)--(5) and a union bound make the conditional probability that any cell average of \(\xi_i\) exceeds \(x_n\) no larger than
\[
 2N_n\exp\{-4\log(8N_n/\alpha)\}\le \frac{\alpha}{4}.                         \tag{6}
\]
If \(w\in H\) is assigned to \(C\), every design point in \(C\) is in the same component as \(w\) and is at Euclidean distance at most \(h_n\) from it.  The H\"older condition in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) hence gives
\[
 \left|N_C^{-1}\sum_{i:(X_i,Z_i)\in C}g(X_i,Z_i)-g(w)\right|\le Gh_n^\beta.
\]
Together with the definition of \(r_n\), this proves
\[
 P\!\left\{\sup_{w\in H}|\widehat g_n(w)-g(w)|\le r_n\right\}
 \ge 1-\frac{\alpha}{4}-N_ne^{-nf_0h_n^2/64}.                                   \tag{7}
\]
By the definition of \(h_n\), \(nh_n^2/\log n\to\infty\); (1) then shows that the last term in (7) is \(o(1)\), uniformly over the regular class.  Notice that this argument used the density lower bound only; neither density smoothness nor a density upper bound was used.

Second, we establish the signed-measure and scalar-moment bands.  Let \(\mathcal U\) be the class of all functions \(u:X_0\to[-M,M]\) with Lipschitz constant at most \(L\), and put \(f_u(s,x)=su(x)\).  This class has a countable uniform-norm dense subset obtained by restricting bounded piecewise-linear functions with rational approximation data; hence every supremum below equals a countable supremum and is measurable.  Since \(\mathcal C_P\subseteq\mathcal U\), it suffices to control \(\mathcal U\).  Introducing an iid ghost sample and then independent Rademacher signs \((\eta_i)\), conditional Jensen's inequality and the symmetry of each sample pair give
\[
 E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|
 \le 2E\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\eta_iS_i u(X_i)\right|.                             \tag{8}
\]
To bound the last display directly, extend \(u\) from \(X_0\) to its convex hull by the McShane formula and clip the extension to \([-M,M]\).  This preserves its values on \(X_0\), its bound, and its Lipschitz constant.  On the interval \([a,b]=\operatorname{conv}(X_0)\), the extension is absolutely continuous, has an almost-everywhere derivative bounded by \(L\), and satisfies
\[
 u(X_i)=u(a)+\int_a^b u'(t)\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge t\}\,dt.
\]
Conditionally on the observations, the variables \(\eta_iS_i\) are independent and symmetric with absolute value at most one (and are Rademacher whenever \(|S_i|=1\)).  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\right|\le\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 E_\eta\left|\sum_i\eta_iS_i\mathbf 1\{X_i\ge t\}\right|\le\sqrt n.
\]
Taking the supremum after the integral representation therefore yields
\[
 E_\eta\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_i\eta_iS_i u(X_i)\right|
 \le \frac{M+LD_X}{\sqrt n}.                                                     \tag{9}
\]
Let \(W_n=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}|(P_n-P)f_u|\).  Replacing one observation changes \(W_n\) by at most \(2M/n\).  Iterating the same centered exponential convexity bound conditionally along the coordinate filtration gives
\[
 P(W_n-EW_n>x)\le \exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{2M^2}\right)                        \tag{10}
\]
when \(M>0\); for \(M=0\), \(W_n=0\).  Combining (8)--(10), with \(x=M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/n}\), gives probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\) to the inequality
\[
 W_n\le \frac{2(M+LD_X)+M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}}{\sqrt n}\le q_n.             \tag{11}
\]
For the last inequality, put \(\ell=\log(8/\alpha)\).  Then \(\sqrt{2\ell}\ge2\) and \(\sqrt{2\ell}\ge\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}\), whence
\[
 2(M+LD_X)+M\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}
 \le (2M+LD_X)\sqrt{2\ell}=q_n\sqrt n.
\]
By the definitions of \(\widehat\nu_n\), \(\nu_P\), and \(J_P\), (11) is exactly
\[
 \sup_{u\in\mathcal C_P}\left|\int_{X_0}u\,d(\widehat\nu_n-\nu_P)\right|
 \le q_n.                                                                         \tag{12}
\]

The variables \(S\Delta Y\) and \(|Z|\) lie in intervals of lengths \(4B\) and \(D_I\), respectively.  Repeating the exponential calculation leading to (5), now for an average of \(n\) variables, and using the definitions of \(t_n\) and \(a_n\), gives
\[
 P(|\widehat T_n-T|>t_n)\le\frac{\alpha}{8},
 \qquad
 P(|\widehat A_n-A|>a_n)\le\frac{\alpha}{8}.                                  \tag{13}
\]
No independence among (7), (12), and (13) is required.  Their failure probabilities sum to at most \(\alpha+o(1)\).  Hence
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\ne\varnothing}
 P\{\text{the four bands (7), (12), and (13) hold}\}\ge1-\alpha.               \tag{14}
\]

Third, the four bands imply the complete event \(\mathcal E_n(P)\).  Fix \(u\in\mathcal C_P\).  Its restriction to \(\widehat B_n\) satisfies the level and all pairwise \(L\)-Lipschitz inequalities by the definition of \(\mathcal C_P\).  At each support-mesh point \((d_j,z_j)\), the regression band and the observed cone restriction give
\[
 |\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \le |\widehat g_n(d_j,z_j)-g(d_j,z_j)|+|g(d_j,z_j)-u(d_j)|
 \le r_n+K|z_j|.                                                                  \tag{15}
\]
Thus \(u|_{\widehat B_n}\in\widehat{\mathcal U}_n^{\mathrm{out}}\).  Equations (12)--(13), together with \(A\in[a_0,D_I]\) from denominator separation and compact treatment support, give
\[
 (u|_{\widehat B_n},J_P(u),T,A)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n
 \qquad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal C_P.                                      \tag{16}
\]
This is the last clause in the definition of \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), so (14) proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\ne\varnothing}
 P\{\mathcal E_n(P)\}\ge1-\alpha.                                             \tag{17}
\]

Fourth, we prove outer inclusion and the quantitative endpoint bound.  Because (16) holds for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\), taking the infimum and supremum over those feasible points and using the definitions of \(V_P^+\) and \(V_P^-\) gives
\[
 \inf_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \le \frac{T-V_P^+}{A},
 \qquad
 \sup_{(v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n}\frac{t-j}{a}
 \ge \frac{T-V_P^-}{A}.                                                         \tag{18}
\]
The definitions of \(T\), \(J_P\), and \(\nu_P\), followed by iterated expectation and the definition of \(g\), give, for every \(u\in\mathcal C_P\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 T-J_P(u)
 &=E_P[S\{\Delta Y-u(X)\}]\\
 &=E_P[S\{g(X,Z)-u(X)\}].
\end{aligned}
\]
The observed cone restriction in the definition of \(\mathcal C_P\) therefore implies
\[
 |T-J_P(u)|
 \le E_P|g(X,Z)-u(X)|
 \le K E_P|Z|=KA.                                                               \tag{18a}
\]
Hence \(\Theta(P)\subseteq[-K,K]\), and clipping the empirical endpoints preserves (18).

It remains to show that the empirical program cannot extend much farther.  Fix any \((v,j,t,a)\in\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\).  The pairwise inequalities and level constraints permit the explicit bounded McShane extension
\[
 E_nv(x)=\max\{-M,\min\{M,\min_{x_k\in\widehat B_n}[v(x_k)+L|x-x_k|]\}\},
\]
which is bounded by \(M\), is \(L\)-Lipschitz, and equals \(v\) on \(\widehat B_n\).  For \(w=(d,z)\in H\), take a same-component support-mesh point \(w_j=(d_j,z_j)\) with \(\|w-w_j\|_\infty\le s_n\).  The H\"older restriction, regression band, empirical cone constraint, and Lipschitz property imply, term by term,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |g(d,z)-E_nv(d)|
 &\le |g(w)-g(w_j)|+|g(w_j)-\widehat g_n(w_j)|
      +|\widehat g_n(w_j)-v(d_j)|+|v(d_j)-E_nv(d)|\\
 &\le 2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta+r_n+K|z_j|+r_n+Ls_n\\
 &\le K|z|+\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n:=2r_n+(K+L)s_n+2^{\beta/2}Gs_n^\beta\\
 &\le K|z|+4r_n+(K+L)s_n,
\end{aligned}                                                                     \tag{19}
\]
where the factor \(2^{\beta/2}\) converts the component-respecting sup-norm mesh radius to Euclidean distance, and the last line uses \(s_n\le h_n\), \(r_n\ge Gh_n^\beta\), and \(2^{\beta/2}<2\).  Thus \(E_nv\in\mathcal C_P^{\varepsilon_n}\).  The clipping-stability theorem now gives
\[
 u^*=\Pi_P(E_nv)\in\mathcal C_P,
 \qquad \|E_nv-u^*\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_n.                                  \tag{20}
\]
Every observed \(X_i\) belongs to \(\widehat B_n\), so \(E_nv(X_i)=v(X_i)\).  The program constraint on \(j\), the fact that the total variation of the empirical signed measure is at most one, and the signed band (12), now yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |j-J_P(u^*)|
 &\le \left|j-\int E_nv\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
      +\left|\int(E_nv-u^*)\,d\widehat\nu_n\right|
      +\left|\int u^*\,d\widehat\nu_n-J_P(u^*)\right|\\
 &\le q_n+\varepsilon_n+q_n
 =\varepsilon_n+2q_n.                                                            \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly, feasibility and (13) imply
\[
 |t-T|\le2t_n,
 \qquad |a-A|\le2a_n.                                                            \tag{22}
\]
Both \(a\) and \(A\) are at least \(a_0\).  Moreover, (18a), applied to \(u^*\), gives \(|T-J_P(u^*)|/A\le K\).  Using (21)--(22) and adding and subtracting \((T-J_P(u^*))/a\), we obtain
\[
 \left|\frac{t-j}{a}-\frac{T-J_P(u^*)}{A}\right|
 \le \frac{\varepsilon_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.                                \tag{23}
\]
Thus every feasible ratio is within the displayed distance of a point of \(\Theta(P)\).  Combining this fact with (18), and observing that clipping can only reduce outward excess while preserving inclusion, shows that
\[
 0\le \frac{T-V_P^+}{A}-\widehat L_n\le d_n,
 \qquad
 0\le \widehat U_n-\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\le d_n,                                  \tag{24}
\]
where
\[
 d_n=\frac{4r_n+(K+L)s_n+2q_n+2t_n+2Ka_n}{a_0}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), (16) makes the feasible set nonempty, and (18) shows that the clipped extrema cannot cross, so the reset rule is not invoked.  Since \(A\le D_I\), (24) is at most a fixed-class multiple of
\[
 \frac{r_n+(K+L)s_n+q_n+t_n+Ka_n}{A},                                           \tag{25}
\]
as claimed.  Closed inequalities were used throughout, so neither strict feasibility nor uniqueness is needed.

Fifth, we verify measurability and the rate.  Order the deterministic mesh nodes and the observed baseline coordinates lexicographically and remove duplicates by the first-index rule.  There are only finitely many order and coincidence patterns, each defining a Borel stratum of the sample space.  On each stratum, cell counts, cell sums, the empty-cell convention, node coordinates, and hence every coefficient of \(\widehat{\mathscr F}_n\), are Borel functions of \(\mathcal Z_n\).  Since \(a\in[a_0,D_I]\), set \(c=1/a\) and define \(\widetilde v=cv\), \(\widetilde j=cj\), and \(\widetilde t=ct\), replacing the scaled denominator by the normalization \(ca=1\).  For example, \(|a-\widehat A_n|\le a_n\) becomes \(|1-c\widehat A_n|\le ca_n\), and \(|j-\int v\,d\widehat\nu_n|\le q_n\) becomes \(|\widetilde j-\int\widetilde v\,d\widehat\nu_n|\le cq_n\); each absolute-value display denotes two linear inequalities.  All remaining scaled constraints are linear in \((\widetilde v,\widetilde j,\widetilde t,c)\), while the ratio objective is \(\widetilde t-\widetilde j\).  The bounds \(D_I^{-1}\le c\le a_0^{-1}\) and the original bounded constraints make the transformed feasible set a compact polytope whenever it is nonempty.

For a fixed stratum, enumerate the finitely many candidate active-constraint bases.  On each Borel nonzero-determinant sub-stratum, the corresponding candidate vertex is a Borel rational function of the program coefficients; feasibility and objective comparisons are finite Borel comparisons.  A nonempty bounded polytope has a vertex, and successive minimization of the objective and then of each coordinate selects its lexicographically least optimizer among these finitely many candidates.  Therefore both selected optima are Borel on every stratum.  The empty-set, clipping, crossing, and reset operations are also Borel comparisons.  It follows that \(\widehat L_n\), \(\widehat U_n\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\) are Borel measurable without optimizer uniqueness.

Finally, (1) and the declared bandwidth give
\[
 h_n^\beta
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(8N_n/\alpha)}{nh_n^2}}
 =O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}\right).       \tag{26}
\]
Because \(0<\beta\le1\), \(s_n\le h_n=O(h_n^\beta)\), while the definitions give \(q_n,t_n,a_n=O(n^{-1/2})\).  Substituting these relations and the definition of \(r_n\) into (24)--(25) proves, uniformly over the stated regular class,
\[
 d_n=O\!\left(\left\{\frac{\log n}{n}\right\}^{\beta/(2\beta+2)}+n^{-1/2}\right).
\]
This completes every assertion.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}\) with \(\mathcal C_P\ne\varnothing\).  On \(\mathcal E_n(P)\), the uniform band-to-program theorem gives
\[
 \widehat L_n
 \le \frac{T-V_P^+}{A}
 \le \frac{T-V_P^-}{A}
 \le \widehat U_n.                                                               \tag{1}
\]
The attained-interval theorem identifies the middle interval in (1) with
\[
 \Theta(P)=\left[\frac{T-V_P^+}{A},\frac{T-V_P^-}{A}\right].
\]
Therefore, by the definition \(\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n=[\widehat L_n,\widehat U_n]\),
\[
 \mathcal E_n(P)
 \subseteq
 \{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}.                                \tag{2}
\]
Taking probabilities in (2), then the infimum over the displayed class of \(P\), and finally the lower limit, the probability conclusion of the uniform band-to-program theorem yields
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{reg}}:\,\mathcal C_P\ne\varnothing}
 P\{\Theta(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{CI}}_n\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
The attained-interval theorem also gives \(\Theta(P)\subseteq[-K,K]\), so clipping the empirical endpoints to \([-K,K]\) cannot remove a true value.  Neither implication (2) nor the uniform event bound requires \(R(P)>0\), strict obstacle inequalities, or a unique optimizer.  The conclusion therefore includes singleton frontiers, binding obstacle constraints, and nonunique finite-program solutions.
