\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}finiteplate\_\allowbreak{}bernstein\_\allowbreak{}ate\_\allowbreak{}bounds}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Fixed-size cluster counts reveal only degree-\(m\) Bernstein moments of latent four-cell exposure--outcome laws, so the cluster-equally-weighted ATE is generally an interval rather than a point. Each hierarchical SCM carries \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\), draws \(Q_i\sim\mu\), and calibrates the observed four-cell law to \(Q_i\). For every relative-interior count law, the sharp interval has attained generalized-moment primal and Bernstein-polynomial dual programs, with compatible endpoint SCMs using at most \({m+3\choose3}\) latent laws. Its worst-case width is \(2d_m(\epsilon)\), and the shifted-Chebyshev construction gives \(0<d_m(\epsilon)\leq\operatorname{sech}(m\operatorname{arcosh}(1/(1-2\epsilon)))\leq(1-2\epsilon)^m\), with the last inequality strict for \(m\geq2\). A generic contact-hull theorem characterizes equality with valid degree-\(m\) count-measurable envelopes and gives strict improvement regions. Exact multinomial likelihood inversion yields a semialgebraic ATE confidence set recoverable by terminating CAD, and the entire relative boundary admits a finite truth-invariant semialgebraic stratification with constant identification-dimension and minimum endpoint-support labels.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The São Paulo DOT analysis shows that city random-effect choices move the posterior ATE, while fixed-plate mixture theory establishes finite-moment information loss without characterizing this causal functional. The accepted-bank note stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match uses polynomial approximation of the same four-cell reciprocal integrand for finite-category iid minimax MSE, but it does not address an unrestricted mixing law behind fixed-plate cluster counts or the resulting population identified set. Existing limited-pooling ATE bounds use particular valid polynomials, and generalized-moment theory does not supply the hierarchical SCM semantics, the exact best-approximation width identity, or the shifted-Chebyshev uniform plate-size guarantee proved here.

\paragraph{Niche.} The observable cluster-count law is finite dimensional, but the latent city law is unrestricted and the causal target is a nonlinear reciprocal functional. Unlike finite-category iid estimation, the object here is a population fiber over all mixing laws compatible with a fixed within-cluster plate. This creates a tractable convex moment geometry in which exact fiber width is governed by uniform polynomial approximation and can be bounded constructively across plate sizes.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper derives the sharp primal--dual interval, proves an iff Bernstein-span identification frontier and strict width throughout the relative interior, constructs finite-latent endpoint SCMs, and identifies the exact worst-case fixed-plate fiber width as \(2d_m(\epsilon)\) with the full shifted-Chebyshev bound \(0<d_m(\epsilon)\leq\operatorname{sech}(m\operatorname{arcosh}(1/(1-2\epsilon)))\leq(1-2\epsilon)^m\), the final inequality being strict for \(m\geq2\). This width identity and its identified-set certificate are not a finite-category minimax-MSE result. It gives an exact contact-hull criterion and strict improvement regions for every valid degree-\(m\) envelope, with the scalar-reference count-law-only Lee--Weidner transplant as a corollary. It also turns exact multinomial inversion into a one-dimensional semialgebraic confidence set with terminating CAD recovery and proves a finite truth-invariant semialgebraic stratification of the entire relative boundary, including constant identification-dimension and minimum endpoint-support labels on each cell.

\section{Related work}

Nobre, Schmidt, Moodie, and Stephens (2023, Sections 5--6 and Figure 3) motivate the problem by documenting ATE sensitivity to city random effects in the São Paulo DOT study. The accepted-bank note stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match is the closest internal approximation precedent: it constructs a Chebyshev-type polynomial approximation to the same overlap-restricted four-cell reciprocal ATE integrand for finite-category iid minimax MSE estimation using factorial-moment lifts. Its estimand is not a fixed-plate count-law moment fiber over an unrestricted Borel mixing law, so it neither proves the identity max\_b{U\_m(b)-L\_m(b)}=2d\_m(epsilon) nor gives the shifted-Chebyshev identified-set-width certificate established here. Wood (1999, Introduction and Sections 2--4, especially Example 2 on pp. 1717--1718) develops fixed-binomial convex geometry and exhibits a polygonal nonidentifiable slice but does not study a four-cell causal functional. Wood (1992, p. 1167) connects binomial-mixture membership to infinite extendibility of a finite exchangeable Bernoulli law; this is background for the repeated-observation interpretation, not a causal-functional comparator. Rosenbaum and Rubin (1983) provide the standard conditional-ignorability framework and Rubin (1974) the potential-outcome framework underlying consistency; here ignorability is imposed conditional on latent \(Q_i\), and these assumptions are used in \texttt{lem:scm-moment-reduction} to map calibrated observed cells to the causal integrand. Lee and Weidner (2026, Section 4.2 and Proposition 2) study pointwise population envelopes in an observed-\(X\) conditional-factorization class that may allow continuous or discrete \(X\). Only that pointwise population envelope is transplanted here into the explicitly defined scalar-reference, count-law-only construction for the latent-\(Q\) experiment; their estimator and the asymptotic results in Section 5 and Theorem 3 do not transfer and are not compared. Bertsimas and Popescu (2005, Theorem 2.2) and Halaseh, Magron, and Skomra (2026, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.9) inform the strong-duality and full-support arguments. Bayer and Teichmann (2006, Theorem 1) inform atomic compression, while the endpoint contact face yields the causal support cap. Sachs, Jonzon, Sjölander, and Gabriel (2023, Sections 2--4) derive tight symbolic bounds by linear programming and vertex enumeration for categorical causal DAGs after reducing latent influences to a finite response-function state space, with linear observed-probability constraints and a linear causal query. They do not treat an unrestricted Borel mixing law over a continuous four-cell simplex, Bernstein moment fibers with the rational ATE functional, the required finite-atom reduction, or CAD recovery of an exact-inversion confidence set. Duarte et al. (2023, Proposition 1 and Algorithm 1) compute numerical polynomial causal bounds for finite discrete SCMs; the present continuous-latent problem first requires endpoint compression and then exact quantifier elimination. England, Bradford, and Davenport (2020, Theorem 1 and Algorithm 1) supply the CAD machinery. Bugni (2010, Theorems 1--3) motivates whole-set coverage, while Basu, Brill, and Yekutieli (2026, Section 4) motivate exact finite-sample inversion; neither source proves that the inverted hierarchical ATE set is semialgebraic or CAD-recoverable.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:clusters-iid]\label{ass:clusters-iid}
\((Q_i,(A_{ij},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1))_{j=1}^m)\) are independent and identically distributed across \(i\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent hierarchical cluster sampling, \cite{NobreSchmidtMoodieStephens2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:environment-law]\label{ass:environment-law}
\(Q_i\sim\mu\) for every \(1\le i\le n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Random-effects mixing law, \cite{NobreSchmidtMoodieStephens2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-iid-plate]\label{ass:conditional-iid-plate}
Conditional on \(Q_i=q\), the vectors \((A_{ij},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1))\) are identically distributed and mutually independent over \(j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional iid repeated-observation model, \cite{LeeWeidner2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-cell-calibration]\label{ass:observed-cell-calibration}
\(\Pr((A_{ij},Y_{ij})=(a,y)\mid Q_i)=Q_{i,ay}\) almost surely for every \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), \(1\le i\le n\), and \(1\le j\le m\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional categorical calibration, \cite{NobreSchmidtMoodieStephens2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-ignorability]\label{ass:latent-ignorability}
\((Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1))\perp A_{ij}\mid Q_i\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional treatment ignorability, \cite{RosenbaumRubin1983}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}(1)+(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}(0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{Rubin1974}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[lem:scm-moment-reduction]\label{lem:scm-moment-reduction}
Every \((\mu,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\) has \(Q_i\sim\mu\), conditional count law \(C_i\mid Q_i=q\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(m,q)\), marginal count law \(\Pr(C_i=c)=\int B_c^{(m)}(q)\,d\mu(q)\), and cluster-equally-weighted ATE \(\tau(\mu)=\int\{q_{11}/p(q)-q_{01}/(1-p(q))\}\,d\mu(q)=\int f\,d\mu\). Conversely, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\), draw \(Q_i\sim\mu\); conditional on \(Q_i=q\), draw \(A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p(q))\) and conditionally treatment-independent binary potential outcomes \(Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1)\) with means \(q_{01}/(1-p(q))\) and \(q_{11}/p(q)\), respectively, independently over \(j\), and set \(Y_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}(1)+(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}(0)\). This construction belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\) and has conditional cell probabilities \(\Pr((A_{ij},Y_{ij})=(a,y)\mid Q_i)=Q_{i,ay}\) almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((\mu,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\). All conditional equalities below are asserted for \(\mu\)-almost every \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\) at which the stipulated regular conditional laws are defined. Put
\[
 r=p(q)=q_{10}+q_{11}.
\]
By observed-cell calibration,
\[
 \Pr(A_{ij}=1\mid Q_i=q)=q_{10}+q_{11}=r,
 \qquad
 \Pr(A_{ij}=0\mid Q_i=q)=q_{00}+q_{01}=1-r.
\]
Because \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\),
\[
 r\geq\epsilon>0,
 \qquad
 1-r\geq\epsilon>0,
\]
so the conditional probabilities below have nonzero denominators. Latent ignorability gives, for \(a\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E\{Y_{ij}(a)\mid Q_i=q\}
 =\mathbb E\{Y_{ij}(a)\mid A_{ij}=a,Q_i=q\}.
\]
On the event \(A_{ij}=a\), consistency gives \(Y_{ij}=Y_{ij}(a)\). Hence calibration, followed by division by the positive treatment probability, yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{Y_{ij}(1)\mid Q_i=q\}
 &=\Pr(Y_{ij}=1\mid A_{ij}=1,Q_i=q)
 =\frac{q_{11}}{r},\\
 \mathbb E\{Y_{ij}(0)\mid Q_i=q\}
 &=\Pr(Y_{ij}=1\mid A_{ij}=0,Q_i=q)
 =\frac{q_{01}}{1-r}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting the two equations and using the definition of \(f\) proves that the conditional causal contrast is
\[
 \mathbb E\{Y_{ij}(1)-Y_{ij}(0)\mid Q_i=q\}=f(q).
\]
The environment-law assumption gives \(Q_i\sim\mu\). Since the potential outcomes are binary, they are integrable, and iterated expectation therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{Y_{ij}(1)-Y_{ij}(0)\}
 &=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}
 \left\{\frac{q_{11}}{p(q)}-\frac{q_{01}}{1-p(q)}\right\}\,d\mu(q)\\
 &=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}f(q)\,d\mu(q)
 =\tau(\mu).
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the cluster-equally-weighted ATE because clusters have the common fixed plate size \(m\), clusters are identically distributed by independent cluster sampling, and individuals are conditionally identically distributed by the conditional iid plate assumption.

Conditional on \(Q_i=q\), the causal vectors are mutually independent over \(j\) by the conditional iid plate assumption. Each observed pair \(O_{ij}=(A_{ij},Y_{ij})\) is a measurable function of its causal vector by consistency, so the observed pairs are conditionally iid. Calibration gives their four cell probabilities as \(q\). Thus, for every \(c\in\mathcal I_m\), the multinomial formula gives
\[
 \Pr(C_i=c\mid Q_i=q)
 =\frac{m!}{\prod_{a,y}c_{ay}!}\prod_{a,y}q_{ay}^{c_{ay}}
 =B_c^{(m)}(q).
\]
Integrating this conditional probability with respect to \(Q_i\sim\mu\) yields
\[
 \Pr(C_i=c)=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}(q)\,d\mu(q).
\]

Conversely, fix \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\). Independently over clusters, draw \(Q_i\sim\mu\). Given \(Q_i=q\), put \(r=p(q)\) and, independently over \(j\), draw mutually independent variables
\[
 A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(r),\qquad
 Y_{ij}(0)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac{q_{01}}{1-r}\right),\qquad
 Y_{ij}(1)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac{q_{11}}{r}\right).
\]
Define \(Y_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}(1)+(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}(0)\). The overlap restrictions above make both denominators positive. Moreover, because \(q\in\Delta_3\),
\[
 0\leq q_{11}\leq q_{10}+q_{11}=r,
 \qquad
 0\leq q_{01}\leq q_{00}+q_{01}=1-r,
\]
so both Bernoulli means lie in \([0,1]\). The construction is therefore well defined. Mutual independence of \(A_{ij}\), \(Y_{ij}(0)\), and \(Y_{ij}(1)\) conditional on \(Q_i\) proves latent ignorability; the independent repetitions prove the conditional iid plate condition; the displayed definition of \(Y_{ij}\) proves consistency; and the independent cluster draws prove independent identically distributed cluster sampling and the environment-law condition.

It remains to verify calibration. Conditional on \(Q_i=q\), direct multiplication and the simplex identity give
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \Pr(A_{ij}=1,Y_{ij}=1\mid q)
 =r\dfrac{q_{11}}r=q_{11},
 &
 \Pr(A_{ij}=1,Y_{ij}=0\mid q)
 =r-q_{11}=q_{10},\\[6pt]
 \Pr(A_{ij}=0,Y_{ij}=1\mid q)
 =(1-r)\dfrac{q_{01}}{1-r}=q_{01},
 &
 \Pr(A_{ij}=0,Y_{ij}=0\mid q)
 =1-r-q_{01}=q_{00}.
 \end{array}
\]
Thus the constructed model satisfies every defining property of \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\). The forward calculation, now applied to this construction, gives its stated multinomial count law and causal target.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This establishes that the moment program is exactly the compatible hierarchical SCM problem rather than a relaxation. \textit{Gap.} Duarte et al. construct finite response-type programs, while this lemma reconstructs a continuous-latent hierarchical SCM from every feasible mixing measure. \textit{Consumer.} It lets an applied analyst interpret every reported endpoint as an attainable data-generating process for the São Paulo city hierarchy.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-primal-dual]\label{thm:sharp-primal-dual}
Let \(H_{m,\epsilon}:=\operatorname{conv}\{(g_m(q),f(q)):q\in K_{\epsilon}\}\). The set \(H_{m,\epsilon}\) is compact and equals
\[
 \left\{\left(\int g_m\,d\nu,\int f\,d\nu\right):
 \nu\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\right\}.
\]
Every pair in \(H_{m,\epsilon}\) has a representing probability measure supported on at most \(N_m+1\) points of \(K_{\epsilon}\), and therefore has a compatible hierarchical SCM in \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\) with at most \(N_m+1\) latent cluster laws. Consequently, for every \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), the sharp fiber \(\Theta_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) is a nonempty attained compact interval \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\), and each endpoint has a representing measure, and hence a compatible hierarchical SCM, supported on at most \(N_m={m+3\choose3}\) latent laws. If \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), then \(D_m(b)=(L_m(b),U_m(b))\) with both dual extrema attained. If an optimal dual polynomial has contact image of affine dimension \(r\), its associated endpoint admits at most \(r+1\) atoms.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(N=N_m\), \(K=K_{\epsilon}\),
\[
 h(q)=(g_m(q),f(q)),\qquad S=h(K),\qquad H=\operatorname{conv}(S).
\tag{1}
\]
The general fact that extreme probability measures under finitely many equality moments have finite support is classical; in particular, Winkler (1988, Theorem 2.1(b)) gives the corresponding moment-set substrate.  We give a self-contained exposed-face derivation because the vertical endpoint bound, contact-rank refinement, and compatible-SCM reconstruction are the claims needed here.

The set \(K\) is the intersection of the compact simplex with the closed inequalities \(\epsilon\leq p(q)\leq1-\epsilon\), so it is compact.  Both denominators in \(f\) are at least \(\epsilon>0\) on \(K\); hence \(f\), \(g_m\), and \(h\) are continuous.  The multinomial theorem gives
\[
 \mathbf 1^{\top}g_m(q)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}B_c^{(m)}(q)
 =\left(\sum_{a,y}q_{ay}\right)^m=1.
\tag{2}
\]
The set
\[
 K^{\circ}=\{q\in\Delta_3:q_{ay}>0\text{ for all }(a,y),\ \epsilon<p(q)<1-\epsilon\}
\tag{3}
\]
is nonempty and relatively open in the simplex; the uniform four-cell vector belongs to it.  Suppose
\[
 P(q)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}a_cB_c^{(m)}(q)=0\qquad(q\in K).
\tag{4}
\]
Substitution of \(q_{11}=1-q_{00}-q_{01}-q_{10}\) makes the homogeneous degree-\(m\) polynomial \(P\) a polynomial in three variables vanishing on a nonempty open set.  Applying successively the elementary univariate fact that a polynomial with an interval of zeros is the zero polynomial shows that it vanishes identically on the affine hyperplane \(\sum q_{ay}=1\).  For \(v\in\mathbb R^4\) with \(s=\sum v_{ay}\neq0\), homogeneity gives
\[
 P(v)=s^mP(v/s)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The set \(\{v:s\neq0\}\) is open, so \(P\) is identically zero on \(\mathbb R^4\).  Distinct monomials of total degree \(m\) are linearly independent and all Bernstein normalizations are nonzero; hence every \(a_c=0\).  Thus the \(N\) Bernstein coordinates are linearly independent on \(K\).

Equation (2) puts \(g_m(K)\) in
\[
 A_0=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf1^{\top}x=1\}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\operatorname{aff}g_m(K)\) were a proper affine subspace of \(A_0\), an affine functional nonconstant on \(A_0\) would be constant on \(g_m(K)\).  Absorbing its constant with (2) would contradict the linear independence just proved.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{aff}g_m(K)=A_0,
 \qquad \dim\operatorname{aff}g_m(K)=N-1.
\tag{7}
\]
The set \(H\) lies in \(A_0\times\mathbb R\).  If its affine dimension were \(N-1\), projection to its first \(N\) coordinates would be a bijection of translation spaces onto that of \(A_0\), and the last coordinate would be affine in the first.  Using (2) to absorb the constant would give \(f(q)=\lambda^{\top}g_m(q)\) on \(K\), hence \(f\in V_m\), contrary to the finite-plate nonpolynomial lemma.  Therefore
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}H=N,
 \qquad \operatorname{aff}H=A_0\times\mathbb R.
\tag{8}
\]

Every finite convex representation in \(H\) with more than \(N+1\) positive weights is affinely dependent.  Indeed, if
\[
 z=\sum_{j=1}^kw_js_j,\qquad w_j>0,qquad k>N+1,
\]
there are \(a_j\), not all zero, with \(\sum_ja_j=0\) and \(\sum_ja_js_j=0\).  Both signs occur.  With
\[
 \rho=\min_{j:a_j>0}\frac{w_j}{a_j},
 \qquad w'_j=w_j-\rho a_j,
\tag{9}
\]
the new weights are nonnegative, sum to one, give the same \(z\), and at least one is zero.  Iteration leaves at most \(N+1\) weights.  Hence \(H\) is the continuous image of \(\Delta_N\times K^{N+1}\) under the barycenter map and is compact.

Every point of \(H\) is an integral pair under a finite atomic measure.  Conversely, let \(\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\) and \(z=\int h\,d\nu\).  If \(z\notin H\), compactness supplies a closest \(z_0\in H\).  Differentiating squared distance at zero along the segment from \(z_0\) to any \(x\in H\) gives
\[
 (z-z_0)^{\top}(x-z_0)\leq0.
\tag{10}
\]
Taking \(x=h(q)\), integrating, and using \(z=\int h\,d\nu\) yields \(\lVert z-z_0\rVert^2\leq0\), a contradiction.  Thus
\[
 H=\left\{\left(\int g_m\,d\nu,\int f\,d\nu\right):\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\right\},
\tag{11}
\]
and every pair has an \((N+1)\)-atom representation.  The converse construction in the SCM moment-reduction lemma converts each such representation into a compatible hierarchical SCM.

Fix \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  By the moment-body definition and (11),
\[
 H_b:=\{t:(b,t)\in H\}
 =\left\{\int f\,d\nu:\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
This section is nonempty, compact, and convex, hence a closed interval.  The definitions of \(L_m\), \(U_m\), and \(\Theta_{m,\epsilon}\) give
\[
 H_b=[L_m(b),U_m(b)]=\Theta_{m,\epsilon}(b),
\tag{13}
\]
and both endpoints are attained.

Let \(z_-=(b,L_m(b))\).  It is in \(\operatorname{relbd}H\), since (8) and relative interior membership would permit a small downward vertical perturbation in \(H\), contradicting (13).  Choose \(z_k\in\operatorname{aff}H\setminus H\) with \(z_k\to z_-\), let \(h_k\) be a closest point of \(H\) to \(z_k\), and set
\[
 n_k=\frac{h_k-z_k}{\lVert h_k-z_k\rVert}.
\]
Then \(h_k\to z_-\), and the nearest-point inequality along segments from \(h_k\) to \(y\in H\) is
\[
 n_k^{\top}(y-h_k)\geq0.
\tag{14}
\]
A subsequence converges to a unit vector \(n\) in the translation space of \(\operatorname{aff}H\), and
\[
 n^{\top}(y-z_-)\geq0\qquad(y\in H).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(F_-:=\{y\in H:n^{\top}(y-z_-)=0\}\) is a proper exposed face containing \(z_-\), and \(\dim\operatorname{aff}F_-\leq N-1\).  In any positive-weight representation of \(z_-\), each support point has nonnegative slack in (15), while the weighted average slack is zero; every support point therefore lies in \(S\cap F_-\).  Applying (9) inside \(\operatorname{aff}F_-\) reduces the endpoint representation to at most
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(S\cap F_-)+1\leq N
\tag{16}
\]
atoms.  The same argument from above the section applies to \(z_+=(b,U_m(b))\).  The SCM reduction therefore gives compatible endpoint models with at most \(N=N_m\) latent laws for every feasible \(b\).  This is the promised self-contained instantiation of the known finite-moment extreme-point principle; (16) additionally identifies the relevant endpoint face.

Now assume \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  Put \(A=\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), let \(V\) be its translation space, and define
\[
 E_-=H+\{(0,s):s\geq0\}
 =\{(x,t):x\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon},\ t\geq L_m(x)\}.
\tag{17}
\]
This set is closed: in a convergent sequence \(h_k+(0,s_k)\), compactness supplies a convergent subsequence of \(h_k\), after which \(s_k\) has a nonnegative limit.  Project \((b,L_m(b)-k^{-1})\) onto \(E_-\) and pass to a limiting normalized nearest-point vector.  There is a unit \((a,\beta)\in V\times\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 a^{\top}(x-b)+\beta\{t-L_m(b)\}\geq0
 \qquad((x,t)\in E_-).
\tag{18}
\]
Upward closure forces \(\beta\geq0\).  If \(\beta=0\), the relative ball about \(b\) and \(x=b\pm\delta v\), \(v\in V\), force \(a^{\top}v=0\) for every \(v\), hence \(a=0\), contradicting unit norm.  Thus \(\beta>0\); rescale so \(\beta=1\), and set
\[
 \lambda^-=-a+\{L_m(b)+a^{\top}b\}\mathbf1.
\tag{19}
\]
By (2) and (18), evaluated at \(h(q)\in H\),
\[
 (\lambda^-)^{\top}g_m(q)\leq f(q),
 \qquad (\lambda^-)^{\top}b=L_m(b).
\tag{20}
\]
For any feasible minorant \(\eta\) and \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\),
\[
 \eta^{\top}b=\int\eta^{\top}g_m\,d\nu\leq\int f\,d\nu.
\tag{21}
\]
Weak duality (21) and (20) prove lower equality and dual attainment.

Similarly, for \(E_+=H-\{(0,s):s\geq0\}\), projection from above produces \(a_+\in V\) such that
\[
 a_+^{\top}(x-b)-\{t-U_m(b)\}\geq0\qquad((x,t)\in E_+).
\]
Then
\[
 \lambda^+=a_++\{U_m(b)-a_+^{\top}b\}\mathbf1
\tag{22}
\]
satisfies \((\lambda^+)^{\top}g_m(q)\geq f(q)\) on \(K\) and \((\lambda^+)^{\top}b=U_m(b)\).  Integrating any feasible majorant gives the converse weak-duality inequality.  Hence
\[
 D_m(b)=(L_m(b),U_m(b))
\tag{23}
\]
with both extrema attained.

For a lower optimizer let
\[
 T_-=\{q\in K:(\lambda^-)^{\top}g_m(q)=f(q)\}.
\]
If \(\nu_-\) attains the lower endpoint, then by (20)
\[
 0=\int_K\{f(q)-(\lambda^-)^{\top}g_m(q)\}\,d\nu_-(q).
\]
The integrand is continuous and nonnegative, so \(\nu_-(T_-)=1\).  Hence \(b\in\operatorname{conv}g_m(T_-)\).  If \(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}g_m(T_-)\), the elimination (9) on that affine space gives a measure on at most \(r+1\) contact points; contact makes its objective \((\lambda^-)^{\top}b=L_m(b)\).  The same argument applies to the upper contact set.  This last calculation uses only dual feasibility and equality with the corresponding attained primal endpoint, not relative interior.  It therefore proves the \(r+1\) bound whenever an optimal lower or upper dual polynomial exists, including at a boundary point where one happens to attain.  This proves every primal, causal-witness, dual, and contact-support assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies the all-feasible sharp causal fiber and compatible \(N_m\)-atom endpoint SCMs, together with an attained evaluable dual on the relative interior. \textit{Gap.} Winkler (1988, Theorem 2.1(b)) already supplies the general linear-independence extreme-point substrate implying finite support.  With \(N_m-1\) independent Bernstein equalities plus normalization, it implies the \(N_m\) cap.  The theorem's contribution is its self-contained exposed-face instantiation for the Bernstein count-law causal hull, the contact-rank refinement, compatible hierarchical-SCM reconstruction, and attained relative-interior Bernstein dual; endpoint compression itself is not claimed to be unprecedented. \textit{Consumer.} It replaces random-effect specification sensitivity in the DOT analysis with the smallest ATE interval implied by the count law and overlap, plus compatible endpoint SCMs using at most \(N_m\) latent laws even on the boundary.

\begin{lemma}[lem:full-support-perturbation]\label{lem:full-support-perturbation}
For every \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), there exists \(\nu_b\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) with full support on \(K_{\epsilon}\). If \(r_b=f-\Pi_{V_m}^{L^2(\nu_b)}f\), then \(r_b\) is a bounded continuous function, \(\int B_c^{(m)}r_b\,d\nu_b=0\) for every \(c\), and \(\int fr_b\,d\nu_b=\int r_b^2\,d\nu_b\). Whenever \(f\notin V_m\), sufficiently small positive \(s\) makes \(d\nu_b^{\pm}=(1\pm sr_b)d\nu_b\) two distinct members of the same fiber with different ATEs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because \(K_{\epsilon}\) is a compact subset of Euclidean space, choose a dense sequence \((x_j)_{j\geq1}\) in it and positive numbers \(a_j\) summing to one.  The probability measure \(\rho=\sum_{j\geq1}a_j\delta_{x_j}\) has full support.  Let \(a=\int g_m\,d\rho\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  If \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), then for all sufficiently small \(0<\delta<1\),
\[
 c_{\delta}=\frac{b-\delta a}{1-\delta}
 =b+\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}(b-a)
 \in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon};
\]
this is exactly the relative-interior ball property in the affine hull of the moment body.  By the definition of that body, choose \(\eta_{\delta}\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\) with \(\int g_m\,d\eta_{\delta}=c_{\delta}\).  Then
\[
 \nu_b=\delta\rho+(1-\delta)\eta_{\delta}
\]
has full support and satisfies \(\int g_m\,d\nu_b=b\), so \(\nu_b\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).

On the finite-dimensional space \(V_m\), the bilinear form \(\langle v,w\rangle=\int vw\,d\nu_b\) is an inner product: if a continuous \(v\) is not identically zero, it is bounded away from zero on some nonempty relatively open subset of \(K_{\epsilon}\), which has positive \(\nu_b\)-mass by full support.  Thus the \(L^2(\nu_b)\) projection \(v_b=\Pi_{V_m}^{L^2(\nu_b)}f\) exists uniquely.  Both \(v_b\) and \(f\) are continuous and bounded on compact \(K_{\epsilon}\), so \(r_b=f-v_b\) is continuous and bounded.  Orthogonality and \(B_c^{(m)}\in V_m\) give
\[
 \int B_c^{(m)}r_b\,d\nu_b=0\quad(c\in\mathcal I_m).
\]
Also \(1=\sum_cB_c^{(m)}\in V_m\), and hence \(\int r_b\,d\nu_b=0\).  Finally,
\[
 \int fr_b\,d\nu_b
 =\int(v_b+r_b)r_b\,d\nu_b
 =\int r_b^2\,d\nu_b.
\]
If \(f\notin V_m\), then \(r_b\) is not zero in \(L^2(\nu_b)\): otherwise continuity and full support would imply \(f=v_b\) everywhere.  Choose \(0<s<\|r_b\|_{\infty}^{-1}\).  The functions \(1\pm sr_b\) are strictly positive, integrate to one, and define probability measures
\[
 d\nu_b^{\pm}=(1\pm sr_b)\,d\nu_b.
\]
The displayed orthogonality relations imply \(\int B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_b^{\pm}=b_c\) for every \(c\), whereas
\[
 \int f\,d\nu_b^{\pm}
 =\int f\,d\nu_b\pm s\int r_b^2\,d\nu_b.
\]
The last squared norm is positive, so the two fiber members are distinct and have different ATEs.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the missing bridge from mixing-law nonuniqueness to strict variation of the particular causal functional on every interior fiber. \textit{Gap.} Halaseh et al. supply the full-support representative, but not the residual perturbation that separates a nonlinear causal functional while preserving all observed Bernstein moments. \textit{Consumer.} It certifies that reporting a point ATE for any interior fixed-plate count law necessarily uses structure beyond latent ignorability and overlap.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-plate-nonpolynomial]\label{lem:finite-plate-nonpolynomial}
For every finite \(m\ge1\) and every \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \(V_m\) is the restriction to \(K_{\epsilon}\) of polynomials of total degree at most \(m\) on the simplex, whereas \(f(q)=q_{11}/p(q)-q_{01}/(1-p(q))\) does not belong to \(V_m\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each \(B_c^{(m)}\) is a scalar multiple of a monomial of total degree \(m\), so \(V_m\) consists of restrictions of polynomials of total degree at most \(m\).  Conversely, on the simplex \(\sum_{a,y}q_{ay}=1\).  If \(q^{\gamma}\) is a monomial of degree \(d\leq m\), then
\[
 q^{\gamma}=q^{\gamma}\left(\sum_{a,y}q_{ay}\right)^{m-d},
\]
and the multinomial expansion on the right is a linear combination of total-degree-\(m\) monomials, hence of the Bernstein polynomials \(B_c^{(m)}\).  Thus \(V_m\) is exactly the asserted restriction space.

Suppose for contradiction that a polynomial \(P\) agrees with \(f\) on \(K_{\epsilon}\).  Put \(a=\epsilon/2>0\), and for \(r\in(\epsilon,1-\epsilon)\) consider
\[
 q(r)=(q_{00},q_{01},q_{10},q_{11})=(1-r,0,r-a,a).
\]
All four coordinates are nonnegative and \(p(q(r))=r\), so this path lies in \(K_{\epsilon}\).  The function \(R(r)=P(q(r))\) is a polynomial in \(r\), while
\[
 f(q(r))=\frac{a}{r}.
\]
Consequently the polynomial \(rR(r)-a\) vanishes on the nonempty open interval \((\epsilon,1-\epsilon)\) and therefore is identically zero.  Evaluating that polynomial identity at \(r=0\) gives \(-a=0\), a contradiction.  Hence \(f\) is not the restriction of any polynomial, and in particular \(f\notin V_m\) for every finite \(m\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rational propensity denominators are the exact component hidden by every finite Bernstein plate. \textit{Gap.} Wood establishes finite-moment aliasing of the mixing law, but does not test membership of this four-cell ATE functional in the observable function span. \textit{Consumer.} This gives a model-free diagnosis of why increasing the number of clusters cannot restore point identification when the within-cluster plate remains fixed.

\begin{theorem}[thm:identification-frontier]\label{thm:identification-frontier}
For a continuous functional \(h:K_{\epsilon}\to\mathbb R\), \(\int h\,d\nu\) is identical for all \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) and every feasible \(b\) if and only if \(h\in V_m\). For \(h=f\), universal point identification therefore fails for every finite \(m\), and every \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) satisfies \(L_m(b)<U_m(b)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose that \(h\in V_m\). By the definition of \(V_m\), there are real coefficients \((\gamma_c)_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\) such that
\[
h(q)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c B_c^{(m)}(q)
\qquad(q\in K_{\epsilon}).
\]
Fix a feasible \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) and a measure \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\). The defining moment equalities for \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), together with linearity of integration over the finite sum, give
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}h\,d\nu
=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c
  \int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu
=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c b_c.
\]
The last expression depends only on \(b\). Hence \(\int h\,d\nu\) is identical for all \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), for every feasible \(b\).

Conversely, suppose that \(h\notin V_m\). We first construct a point in \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). The set \(K_{\epsilon}\) is nonempty: the vector
\[
q^{\circ}=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)
\]
belongs to the simplex and satisfies \(p(q^{\circ})=1/2\), so \(q^{\circ}\in K_{\epsilon}\) because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\). Let \(d\) be the dimension of \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). By \(\mathrm{def{:}bernstein\text{-}moment\text{-}body}\),
\[
\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}
=\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q):q\in K_{\epsilon}\}.
\]
The definition of affine dimension therefore permits a choice of \(q_0,\ldots,q_d\in K_{\epsilon}\) for which \(g_m(q_0),\ldots,g_m(q_d)\) are affinely independent and have affine hull \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). Set
\[
b_0=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{k=0}^{d}g_m(q_k).
\]
Then \(b_0\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\). Relative to the affine basis \(g_m(q_0),\ldots,g_m(q_d)\), every barycentric coordinate of \(b_0\) is \(1/(d+1)>0\). By continuity of these affine coordinate maps, a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(b_0\) in \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) has all barycentric coordinates positive and is therefore contained in
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q_0),\ldots,g_m(q_d)\}
\subseteq\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}.
\]
Thus \(b_0\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).

By \(\mathrm{lem{:}full\text{-}support\text{-}perturbation}\), there is a measure \(\nu_0\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\) with support equal to \(K_{\epsilon}\). Regard \(V_m\) as a subspace of \(L^2(\nu_0)\). It is finite dimensional and hence closed. Moreover, the embedding of \(V_m\) into \(L^2(\nu_0)\) is injective: if \(v\in V_m\) vanishes \(\nu_0\)-almost surely, then continuity of \(v\) and full support of \(\nu_0\) force \(v=0\) at every point of \(K_{\epsilon}\). Indeed, if \(v(q_*)\ne0\), continuity gives a relatively open neighborhood of \(q_*\) on which \(|v|\) is bounded below by a positive constant, while full support gives that neighborhood positive \(\nu_0\)-measure, a contradiction.

Let \(P_0h\in V_m\) be the \(L^2(\nu_0)\)-orthogonal projection of \(h\) onto \(V_m\), and define
\[
r=h-P_0h.
\]
The function \(P_0h\) belongs to \(V_m\subset C(K_{\epsilon})\); hence \(r\) is continuous and bounded on the compact set \(K_{\epsilon}\). Orthogonality and the definition of \(V_m\) imply, for every \(c\in\mathcal I_m\),
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}r\,d\nu_0=0.
\]
The multinomial identity gives, pointwise on \(K_{\epsilon}\),
\[
\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}B_c^{(m)}(q)
=\left(\sum_{a,y}q_{ay}\right)^m
=1.
\]
Thus the constant function \(1\) lies in \(V_m\), and consequently
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}r\,d\nu_0=0.
\]
Also,
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}hr\,d\nu_0
=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}(P_0h+r)r\,d\nu_0
=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}r^2\,d\nu_0.
\]
This last quantity is strictly positive. Otherwise \(r=0\) \(\nu_0\)-almost surely, and the continuity and full-support argument would give \(h=P_0h\) everywhere on \(K_{\epsilon}\), contrary to \(h\notin V_m\).

In particular, \(\lVert r\rVert_{\infty}>0\). Choose
\[
0<s<\lVert r\rVert_{\infty}^{-1}
\]
and define two Borel measures by
\[
d\nu_0^{\pm}=(1\pm sr)\,d\nu_0.
\]
The density \(1\pm sr\) is strictly positive on \(K_{\epsilon}\), and the preceding zero-mean identity yields
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}(1\pm sr)\,d\nu_0=1.
\]
Thus \(\nu_0^+\) and \(\nu_0^-\) are probability measures. For each \(c\in\mathcal I_m\), orthogonality gives
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_0^{\pm}
=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_0
 \pm s\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}r\,d\nu_0
=b_{0,c}.
\]
Therefore \(\nu_0^+,\nu_0^-\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\). Their \(h\)-functionals differ because
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}h\,d\nu_0^+
-\int_{K_{\epsilon}}h\,d\nu_0^-
=2s\int_{K_{\epsilon}}hr\,d\nu_0
=2s\int_{K_{\epsilon}}r^2\,d\nu_0
>0.
\]
Hence the functional cannot be identical throughout every feasible fiber when \(h\notin V_m\). This proves the equivalence.

Now take \(h=f\). By \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}plate\text{-}nonpolynomial}\), \(f\notin V_m\) for every finite \(m\ge1\), so the equivalence proves failure of universal point identification. Finally fix any \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). The final assertion of \(\mathrm{lem{:}full\text{-}support\text{-}perturbation}\), applied using \(f\notin V_m\), supplies \(\nu_b^+,\nu_b^-\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) with unequal values of \(\int f\,d\nu\). The definitions in \(\mathrm{def{:}sharp\text{-}ate\text{-}fiber}\) then imply
\[
L_m(b)
\le
\min\left\{\int f\,d\nu_b^-,\int f\,d\nu_b^+\right\}
<
\max\left\{\int f\,d\nu_b^-,\int f\,d\nu_b^+\right\}
\le
U_m(b).
\]
Therefore \(L_m(b)<U_m(b)\) at every relative-interior feasible count law.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The iff span criterion separates recovery of a causal functional from recovery of the mixing law; its growing-plate strengthening identifies the exact worst-case interval width and supplies an explicit exponential plate-size elbow. \textit{Gap.} Wood and Basu et al. establish bounded-plate mixing nonidentification in different settings, but do not give this necessary-and-sufficient Bernstein-span criterion, strict ATE width at every relative-interior law, or the exact uniform width \(2d_m(\epsilon)\) with its constructive exponential bound. \textit{Consumer.} Investigators can check whether a hierarchical estimand lies in the observable Bernstein span and, for the ATE, choose \(m\) from the displayed elbow to cap worst-case identification ambiguity by a prescribed tolerance; sampling uncertainty in \(n\) remains separate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:boundary-dirac-regression]\label{prop:boundary-dirac-regression}
For \(m\ge2\) and any \(q^*\in K_{\epsilon}\), the boundary moment vector \(b=g_m(q^*)\) has \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)=\{\delta_{q^*}\}\) and hence \(L_m(b)=U_m(b)=f(q^*)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cell coordinate \(k=(a,y)\).  Expanding a multinomial count into its \(m\) indicator summands gives the first two factorial-moment identities
\[
 \sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\frac{c_k}{m}B_c^{(m)}(q)=q_k,
 \qquad
 \sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\frac{c_k(c_k-1)}{m(m-1)}B_c^{(m)}(q)=q_k^2,
\]
where the second identity uses \(m\geq2\).  Let \(b=g_m(q^*)\) and \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).  Applying these two linear combinations to the fiber equations yields
\[
 \int q_k\,d\nu=q_k^*,
 \qquad
 \int q_k^2\,d\nu=(q_k^*)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \int(q_k-q_k^*)^2\,d\nu=0,
\]
so \(q_k=q_k^*\) almost surely.  Repeating for all four cells gives \(q=q^*\) almost surely and \(\nu=\delta_{q^*}\).  Thus the fiber is the claimed singleton and its causal image is \(L_m(b)=U_m(b)=f(q^*)\).

It remains to justify the boundary descriptor.  If
\[
 g_m(q^*)=\theta b^{(1)}+(1-\theta)b^{(2)},\qquad 0<\theta<1,
\]
with \(b^{(1)},b^{(2)}\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), choose representing measures
\(\nu_1,\nu_2\) for these two moment vectors.  Then
\(\theta\nu_1+(1-\theta)\nu_2\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(g_m(q^*))\).
The singleton-fiber conclusion forces this mixture to equal \(\delta_{q^*}\);
nonnegativity then forces \(\nu_1=\nu_2=\delta_{q^*}\), and hence
\(b^{(1)}=b^{(2)}=g_m(q^*)\).  Thus \(g_m(q^*)\) is extreme.  The first
factorial-moment identity also yields
\[
 q_{ay}=\frac1m\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}c_{ay}B_c^{(m)}(q),
\]
so \(g_m\) is injective.  The set \(K_{\epsilon}\) contains distinct
perturbations of the uniform vector that keep \(p(q)=1/2\), so
\(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) is not a singleton.  An extreme point of a
non-singleton convex set cannot belong to its relative interior.  Since the
moment body is compact, \(g_m(q^*)\) lies in its relative boundary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The second Bernstein moments force zero latent variance and show exactly why the relative-interior restriction cannot be dropped. \textit{Gap.} General mixture nonidentification statements do not record that fixed-plate causal fibers can collapse on exposed boundary images. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition prevents confidence procedures and empirical diagnostics from treating near-boundary laws as uniformly wide partially identified cases.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-envelope-domination]\label{thm:sharp-envelope-domination}
For every \((\ell,u)\in\mathcal E_m\) and every \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq[\ell^\top b,u^\top b]\). The lower equality \(L_m(b)=\ell^\top b\) holds if and only if \(b\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q):q\in K_{\epsilon},\ \ell(q)=f(q)\}\), and the upper equality \(U_m(b)=u^\top b\) holds if and only if \(b\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q):q\in K_{\epsilon},\ u(q)=f(q)\}\). If \(m\ge2\), each contact hull is a proper closed convex subset of \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\); consequently, for each endpoint separately there is a nonempty relatively open subset of \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) on which the sharp endpoint strictly improves its valid envelope endpoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((\ell,u)\in\mathcal E_m\), \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), and \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).  The envelope inequalities and the moment equations imply
\[
 \ell^{\top}b=\int\ell\,d\nu
 \leq\int f\,d\nu
 \leq\int u\,d\nu=u^{\top}b.
\]
Taking the infimum and supremum proves \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq[\ell^{\top}b,u^{\top}b]\).

Let \(T_{\ell}=\{q\in K_{\epsilon}:\ell(q)=f(q)\}\).  This set is compact.  If \(b\in\operatorname{conv}g_m(T_{\ell})\), an atomic probability measure supported on \(T_{\ell}\) belongs to the fiber and has objective \(\ell^{\top}b\), so \(L_m(b)=\ell^{\top}b\).  Conversely, compactness of the attainable moment--target hull, established in thm:sharp-primal-dual, gives an attaining lower measure \(\nu\).  If equality holds, then
\[
 0=\int(f-\ell)\,d\nu.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative, so \(\nu(T_{\ell})=1\); its moment barycenter therefore lies in the compact convex set \(\operatorname{conv}g_m(T_{\ell})\).  This proves the lower equivalence.  Replacing \(f-\ell\) by \(u-f\) proves the upper equivalence with \(T_u=\{q:u(q)=f(q)\}\).

Assume \(m\geq2\).  By lem:finite-plate-nonpolynomial, neither contact set equals all of \(K_{\epsilon}\), for equality everywhere would put \(f\) in \(V_m\).  Choose \(q^*\notin T_{\ell}\).  If \(g_m(q^*)\) belonged to \(\operatorname{conv}g_m(T_{\ell})\), a probability measure supported on \(T_{\ell}\) would have moment vector \(g_m(q^*)\), contradicting the singleton-fiber conclusion of prop:boundary-dirac-regression.  Thus the lower contact hull is a proper subset of the moment body.  It is closed because it is the convex hull of a compact subset of finite-dimensional space.  The same reasoning applies to the upper contact hull.

A proper closed convex subset of a compact convex set cannot contain the latter's relative interior, whose closure is the whole set.  Therefore the complement of each contact hull contains a nonempty relatively open subset of \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  On the lower complement the already-proved weak inequality is strict, \(L_m(b)>\ell^{\top}b\); on the upper complement it is strict in the opposite direction, \(U_m(b)<u^{\top}b\).  The two relatively open subsets need not coincide.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The contact-hull criterion identifies exactly where any valid degree-\(m\) count-measurable bound is already sharp and where optimizing the complete moment fiber must improve it. \textit{Gap.} Published limited-pooling bounds provide particular valid polynomial envelopes, but do not give a generic sharp-envelope domination theorem or an exact contact-hull equality criterion for the complete count law. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can test a proposed count-measurable ATE envelope by computing its contact hull and can localize observable laws where the sharp fiber yields a strict gain.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-cad-recovery]\label{thm:exact-cad-recovery}
For rational \(\epsilon\) and rational \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), the relative-interior branch of \(\operatorname{CAD}_m(b,\epsilon)\) terminates and returns the algebraic numbers \(L_m(b)\) and \(U_m(b)\) by minimal polynomials and isolating intervals, endpoint measures with at most \(N_m\) atoms, compatible endpoint models in \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\), and matching globally feasible Bernstein dual certificates. For arbitrary rational \(b\), the boundary-capable branch first decides primal feasibility using no dual variables. If \(b\notin\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), it certifies infeasibility. If \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), it terminates and returns both algebraic primal endpoints, endpoint measures with at most \(N_m\) atoms, and compatible endpoint models, then separately decides whether matching globally feasible dual certificates exist; absence of a matching certificate returns a boundary/nonattainment flag and does not alter the primal endpoints or witnesses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and rational \(b\), and put \(N=N_m\).  For \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\), define
\[
 d(q)=p(q)\{1-p(q)\},
 \qquad a(q)=q_{11}\{1-p(q)\}-q_{01}p(q).
\tag{1}
\]
The overlap restriction gives \(d(q)\geq\epsilon(1-\epsilon)>0\) and \(f(q)=a(q)/d(q)\).

Let \(\Phi_N(b,t)\) assert the existence of locations \(q^1,\ldots,q^N\in K_{\epsilon}\) and weights \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N\geq0\) such that
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^N\omega_k=1,
 \qquad
 b_c=\sum_{k=1}^N\omega_kB_c^{(m)}(q^k)
 \quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 t\prod_{k=1}^Nd(q^k)
 =\sum_{k=1}^N\omega_ka(q^k)
   \prod_{\substack{1\leq r\leq N\\r\neq k}}d(q^r).
\tag{3}
\]
Every factor cleared in (3) is strictly positive, so (3) is equivalent to \(t=\sum_k\omega_kf(q^k)\).  The simplex, overlap, mass, moment, and objective restrictions are therefore a finite first-order polynomial formula with rational coefficients.

Every \(t\) satisfying \(\Phi_N(b,t)\) is generated by a measure in \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).  Conversely, the all-feasible endpoint compression in the sharp primal--dual theorem supplies \(N\)-atom measures attaining both endpoints whenever \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  Therefore
\[
 \exists t\,\Phi_N(b,t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon},
\tag{4}
\]
and, when feasible,
\[
 \min\{t:\Phi_N(b,t)\}=L_m(b),
 \qquad
 \max\{t:\Phi_N(b,t)\}=U_m(b).
\tag{5}
\]
This uses only the \(N\)-atom endpoint result and does not assert the same cap for every interior value.  The parameter space \(\Delta_{N-1}\times K_{\epsilon}^N\) is compact and the fixed-\(b\) constraints are closed, so the extrema and their witnesses are attained.

The formulas
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_L(b,t)&\Longleftrightarrow
 \Phi_N(b,t)\wedge\forall s\{\Phi_N(b,s)\Rightarrow t\leq s\},\\
 G_U(b,t)&\Longleftrightarrow
 \Phi_N(b,t)\wedge\forall s\{\Phi_N(b,s)\Rightarrow s\leq t\}
 \end{split}
\tag{6}
\]
define exactly the lower and upper endpoints.  The cited complete CAD quantifier-elimination result applies to (1)--(6): it terminates, decides (4), and, if feasible, returns algebraic endpoint values and algebraic sample points for endpoint locations and weights.  Each endpoint has a rational defining polynomial and rational isolating interval.  Factoring the defining polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\), selecting the unique irreducible factor with a root in the isolating interval, and refining that interval if necessary terminates and returns its minimal polynomial and a rational isolating interval.

Suppose \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  The sharp primal--dual theorem supplies attained \(\lambda^-\) and \(\lambda^+\).  Lower global feasibility is
\[
 q\in K_{\epsilon}\Longrightarrow
 d(q)\sum_c\lambda_cB_c^{(m)}(q)\leq a(q),
\tag{7}
\]
and upper feasibility reverses the inequality.  Positivity of \(d(q)\) makes (7) equivalent to the rational Bernstein minorant inequality, and every term is polynomial.  Lower complementary contact for the variables in (2)--(3) is
\[
 \omega_k\left\{a(q^k)-d(q^k)\sum_c\lambda_cB_c^{(m)}(q^k)\right\}=0
 \quad(1\leq k\leq N),
\tag{8}
\]
together with \(\lambda^{\top}b=t\); the upper system uses the reversed slack.  Thus the relative-interior primal--dual branch is a first-order real-algebraic formula.  Strong duality and contact from the sharp primal--dual theorem prove feasibility exactly at \(L_m(b)\) and \(U_m(b)\), so complete CAD returns matching globally feasible dual certificates and at most \(N\) endpoint atoms.

For arbitrary rational \(b\), the boundary branch uses only (2)--(6); dual nonattainment cannot affect primal feasibility or either endpoint.  After the primal outputs, matching lower-certificate existence is the separate first-order sentence
\[
 \exists\lambda\left[
 \lambda^{\top}b=L_m(b),\quad
 \forall q\{q\in K_{\epsilon}\Rightarrow d(q)\lambda^{\top}g_m(q)\leq a(q)\}
 \right],
\tag{9}
\]
and the upper sentence reverses the inequality and substitutes \(U_m(b)\).  Complete CAD decides both.  If either is false, the procedure reports the boundary/nonattainment flag while retaining the certified primal endpoint and witness.  Finally, the SCM moment-reduction lemma converts each atomic measure into a compatible member of \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\) with the same count law and causal endpoint.  This proves exact termination and the stated output semantics; it makes no practical-runtime claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts each infinite-dimensional endpoint problem into a terminating exact certificate with an all-feasible \(N_m\)-atom witness. \textit{Gap.} Symbolic bounds for finite response-function models and generic CAD do not cover an unrestricted Borel mixing law, rational Bernstein-fiber ATE endpoints, the all-feasible \(N_m\)-atom endpoint reduction, or exact compatible-SCM witness recovery. \textit{Consumer.} For any fixed rational count law, the theorem gives an in-principle auditable algebraic endpoint and compatible witness procedure, without claiming a completed numerical run or practical runtime.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-complexity-inference]\label{thm:finite-complexity-inference}
For fixed \(m,n\), observed \(z^{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathcal H_n\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), the nonrandomized likelihood-ratio test that rejects \(b'\) when \(P_n(b';z^{\mathrm{obs}})\le\alpha\), with every equality \(T_n(w;b')=T_n(z^{\mathrm{obs}};b')\) included in the upper tail, has rejection probability at most \(\alpha\) under \(Z\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,b')\). Its inversion \(\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is semialgebraic: on each zero-pattern cell, every deviance comparison is equivalent after exponentiation and positive-denominator clearing to a monomial polynomial inequality, and \(P_n\) is a finite polynomial sum. If \(\epsilon\), \(\alpha\), and \(z^{\mathrm{obs}}\) are rational, then \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq[-1,1]\) is a one-dimensional semialgebraic set. The procedure \(\operatorname{CAD}^{\mathrm{conf}}_{m,n}\) terminates after reducing each feasible \((b',t)\) to at most \(N_m+1\) atoms and returns either the complete disjoint decomposition into points and intervals with every algebraic endpoint and open-or-closed endpoint flag, or the certified endpoint hull with its attainability flags.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(b'\in\Delta_{N_m-1}\), and let \(W\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,b')\) on the finite set \(\mathcal H_n\).  Write
\[
 v_1>v_2>\cdots>v_J
\]
for the distinct extended-real values of \(T_n(w;b')\), and set
\[
 E_j=\{w:T_n(w;b')\geq v_j\},
 \qquad \pi_j=\Pr_{b'}(W\in E_j).
\tag{1}
\]
The tie-inclusive definition gives \(P_n(b';w)=\pi_j\) whenever \(T_n(w;b')=v_j\).  Since \(E_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_J\), one has \(\pi_1\leq\cdots\leq\pi_J\).  If no \(j\) has \(\pi_j\leq\alpha\), rejection is impossible.  Otherwise, for the largest such \(j_*\), the rejection event is exactly \(E_{j_*}\), including the entire tie at \(v_{j_*}\).  Hence
\[
 \Pr_{b'}\{P_n(b';W)\leq\alpha\}
 =\pi_{j_*}\leq\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
If a deviance is infinite because a positive count falls in a zero-probability cell, that sample point has probability zero under \(b'\), so (2) also covers infinite values.

For each \(S\subseteq\mathcal I_m\), consider the zero-pattern cell
\[
 D_S=\{b'\in\Delta_{N_m-1}:b'_c>0\Longleftrightarrow c\in S\}.
\tag{3}
\]
There are finitely many such semialgebraic cells.  For \(x\in\mathcal H_n\), put
\[
 a_x=\prod_{c:x_c>0}\left(\frac{x_c}{n}\right)^{x_c}\in\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\tag{4}
\]
On \(D_S\), \(T_n(x;b')\) is finite exactly when \(\{c:x_c>0\}\subseteq S\).  If both \(T_n(w;b')\) and \(T_n(z;b')\) are finite, strict monotonicity of exponentiation and positivity of \(b'_c\) on \(S\) give
\[
 T_n(w;b')\geq T_n(z;b')
 \Longleftrightarrow
 a_w\prod_{c\in S}(b'_c)^{z_c}
 \geq a_z\prod_{c\in S}(b'_c)^{w_c}.
\tag{5}
\]
After clearing a positive integer denominator, the right side is a rational polynomial inequality.  If a deviance is infinite, the extended-real comparison has a fixed truth value on \(D_S\).  Thus every fixed deviance comparison is semialgebraic on each zero-pattern cell.

Partition each \(D_S\) further according to the finitely many comparisons with \(z=z^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  On a resulting cell the tail set \(J(b')\) is constant, and
\[
 P_n(b';z^{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\sum_{w\in J(b')}\frac{n!}{\prod_cw_c!}\prod_c(b'_c)^{w_c}
\tag{6}
\]
is a polynomial.  Hence \(P_n(b';z^{\mathrm{obs}})>\alpha\) is a strict polynomial inequality there.  The finite union over all cells proves that \(\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is semialgebraic.

The attainable-pair result and \((N_m+1)\)-atom representation in the sharp primal--dual theorem show that \(t\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is equivalent to the existence of \(b'\), \(q^1,\ldots,q^{N_m+1}\in K_{\epsilon}\), and \(\omega_k\geq0\) such that
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\sum_{k=1}^{N_m+1}\omega_k=1,
 \qquad b'_c=\sum_{k=1}^{N_m+1}\omega_kB_c^{(m)}(q^k)
 \quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),\\
 &t=\sum_{k=1}^{N_m+1}\omega_kf(q^k),
 \qquad b'\in\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}}).
 \end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
Zero masses pad shorter representations.  Thus (7) is exactly the whole causal union, not a relaxation over unrelated moments.

If \(\epsilon\), \(\alpha\), and \(z^{\mathrm{obs}}\) are rational, membership in \(K_{\epsilon}\) is expressed by rational polynomial simplex and overlap inequalities.  Every Bernstein equation is polynomial.  Since each \(p(q^k)\) and \(1-p(q^k)\) is at least \(\epsilon>0\), multiplication of the objective equation by their product preserves equivalence and yields a rational polynomial equality.  Together with (3)--(6), formula (7) is therefore first order over \(\mathbb Q\).  The cited complete CAD result eliminates all variables except \(t\), terminates, and returns a semialgebraic decomposition of the resulting subset of the real line.

For every \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\), positivity and the simplex inequalities imply
\[
 0\leq\frac{q_{11}}{p(q)}\leq1,
 \qquad 0\leq\frac{q_{01}}{1-p(q)}\leq1.
\]
Hence \(-1\leq f(q)\leq1\), so (7) implies \(t\in[-1,1]\).  A semialgebraic subset of the line is a finite disjoint union of points and intervals.  Complete CAD represents finite boundaries by rational defining polynomials and rational isolating intervals; evaluation of the quantifier-free sign formula at a boundary decides inclusion.  Thus \(\operatorname{CAD}^{\mathrm{conf}}_{m,n}\) returns the full decomposition with open-or-closed flags.  Alternatively, it may select the leftmost and rightmost boundaries and test membership there, yielding the certified endpoint hull with attainability flags.  If the set is empty, it returns the empty certificate.  This proves the stated finite exact semantics, without a runtime bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem makes exact finite-sample inversion a finite real-algebraic computation rather than an abstract union over count laws and mixing measures. \textit{Gap.} Sachs, Jonzon, Sjölander, and Gabriel derive tight symbolic bounds by linear programming and vertex enumeration for finite response-function causal models, while Basu, Brill, and Yekutieli invert exact tests for particular univariate mixing functionals.  Neither treats the unrestricted Borel four-cell mixing law, rational Bernstein ATE fiber, finite-atom reduction, and exact semialgebraic whole-set inversion proved here. \textit{Consumer.} For fixed rational inputs, the theorem characterizes the exact confidence set as a finite semialgebraic object recoverable in principle by complete CAD; no scalability claim is made.

\begin{proposition}[prop:whole-set-coverage]\label{prop:whole-set-coverage}
Corollary. For every \(n,m\), every \(0<\alpha<1\), and every \((\mu,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\) with true count law \(b\), exact count-law coverage gives \(\Pr_{\mathcal M}(b\in\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(Z))\ge1-\alpha\). On this event, the defining union for \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(Z)\) contains \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\); hence \(\Pr_{\mathcal M}([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(Z))\ge1-\alpha\), uniformly over \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\), including boundary laws with singleton fibers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the SCM moment-reduction lemma, under every \((\mu,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\), the cluster counts are iid with categorical law \(b\), so \(Z\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,b)\), and \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  The size bound in the finite-complexity inference theorem, applied at the true \(b\), gives
\[
 \Pr_{\mathcal M}\{b\notin\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(Z)\}
 =\Pr_{\mathcal M}\{P_n(b;Z)\leq\alpha\}\leq\alpha.
\]
On the complementary event, \(b\) is one of the candidates in \(\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(Z)\cap\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  The defining union for \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(Z)\) therefore contains its entire sharp fiber:
\[
 [L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(Z).
\]
Taking probabilities proves the \(1-\alpha\) guarantee.  The argument uses no law-specific constant, hence is uniform over \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\).  It invokes neither relative interior nor positive width and thus includes boundary laws with singleton fibers.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This corollary is solely the exact count-law coverage event combined with the defining union for the ATE confidence set. \textit{Gap.} The inference contribution is computational semialgebraicity and exact recovery; the coverage statement records the resulting finite-sample guarantee without presenting it as a separate difficult theorem. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts obtain uniform coverage of the full nonparametric ATE fiber even near point-identified faces.

\begin{proposition}[prop:m-one-regression]\label{prop:m-one-regression}
When \(m=1\), \(\epsilon=1/4\), and \(b=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\), the exact sharp interval is \([-1/3,1/3]\). The upper dual certificate is \(1-\frac43(q_{01}+q_{10})\), the lower certificate is \(\frac13-\frac43(q_{01}+q_{10})\), and each endpoint is attained by a mixing law with at most four atoms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(m=1\), the Bernstein coordinates are the four cell coordinates themselves.  Put \(r=p(q)\in[1/4,3/4]\).  Since \(r\leq3/4\) and \(1-r\leq3/4\),
\[
 \frac{q_{11}}r=1-\frac{q_{10}}r\leq1-\frac43q_{10},
 \qquad
 -\frac{q_{01}}{1-r}\leq-\frac43q_{01}.
\]
Adding proves the global majorant
\[
 f(q)\leq 1-\frac43(q_{01}+q_{10}).
\]
Likewise
\[
 \frac{q_{11}}r\geq\frac43q_{11},
 \qquad
 -\frac{q_{01}}{1-r}=-1+\frac{q_{00}}{1-r}\geq-1+\frac43q_{00},
\]
and, using \(q_{00}+q_{01}+q_{10}+q_{11}=1\), their sum is the global minorant
\[
 \frac13-\frac43(q_{01}+q_{10})\leq f(q).
\]
At \(b=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\), the minorant and majorant expectations are respectively \(-1/3\) and \(1/3\).  Thus weak duality gives \(L_1(b)\geq-1/3\) and \(U_1(b)\leq1/3\).

Both values are attained.  For the upper endpoint take equal mass on
\[
 (1/4,1/2,0,1/4)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (1/4,0,1/2,1/4).
\]
Their mean is \(b\), both have overlap in \([1/4,3/4]\), and direct substitution gives \(f=1/3\) at each.  For the lower endpoint take equal mass on
\[
 (1/2,1/4,1/4,0)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (0,1/4,1/4,1/2).
\]
Again the mean is \(b\), overlap holds, and \(f=-1/3\) at each.  Hence the exact interval is \([-1/3,1/3]\).  The two displayed affine functions are precisely the claimed Bernstein dual certificates, and each attaining measure uses two, hence at most \(N_1=4\), atoms.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The closed-form base case checks the signs, overlap convention, dual orientation, and support cap. \textit{Gap.} Published binomial-mixture examples do not give this four-cell causal ATE regression case. \textit{Consumer.} The example is a unit test for symbolic implementations and a transparent teaching case for fixed-plate causal nonidentification.

\begin{theorem}[thm:boundary-fiber-atlas]\label{thm:boundary-fiber-atlas}
For every fixed \(m\geq1\) and rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), the relative boundary \(\operatorname{relbd}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), taken in \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), admits a finite truth-invariant semialgebraic stratification on each cell of which the indicator of \(L_m(b)=U_m(b)\), the affine dimension \(0\) or \(1\) of \(\Theta_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), and the ordered pair of minimum lower- and upper-endpoint support sizes are constant.  Both minimum sizes lie in \(\{1,\ldots,N_m\}\).  The construction \(\mathcal A_{m,\epsilon}\), completed by the quantified endpoint and support-size tests in the proof, is a terminating CAD procedure that returns defining formulas, the constant support labels, and one algebraic sample point with endpoint witnesses of the certified minimum sizes for every nonempty cell.  It does not return a uniform symbolic endpoint-witness map over a positive-dimensional cell.  For any separately supplied algebraic \(b\) on the relative boundary, specialization followed by a fresh terminating QE/CAD run returns endpoint witnesses having the certified minimum support sizes at that \(b\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\geq1\) and rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and write \(N=N_m\) and \(K=K_{\epsilon}\).  For \(q\in K\), put
\[
 d(q)=p(q)\{1-p(q)\},
 \qquad a(q)=q_{11}\{1-p(q)\}-q_{01}p(q).
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(d(q)\geq\epsilon(1-\epsilon)>0\) and \(f(q)=a(q)/d(q)\).

For \(1\leq k\leq N\), let \(\Phi_k(b,t)\) assert that there exist \(q^1,\ldots,q^k\in K\) and \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_k\geq0\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^k\omega_j=1,
 \qquad
 b_c=\sum_{j=1}^k\omega_jB_c^{(m)}(q^j)
 \quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 t\prod_{j=1}^kd(q^j)
 =\sum_{j=1}^k\omega_ja(q^j)
 \prod_{\substack{1\leq r\leq k\\r\neq j}}d(q^r).
\tag{3}
\]
Strict positivity in (1) makes (3) equivalent to \(t=\sum_j\omega_jf(q^j)\).  Thus \(\Phi_k\) is a first-order formula in rational polynomial equalities and weak inequalities.  Zero weights and repeated locations show that it expresses an atomic witness with support size at most \(k\).

Let \(\Phi=\Phi_N\).  Every \(\Phi(b,t)\) witness lies in the moment fiber.  Conversely, the all-feasible endpoint result of the sharp primal--dual theorem gives, for every feasible \(b\), measures with at most \(N\) atoms attaining both endpoints.  Hence
\[
 \Psi(b):\Longleftrightarrow\exists t\,\Phi(b,t)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
 \min\{t:\Phi(b,t)\}=L_m(b),
 \qquad
 \max\{t:\Phi(b,t)\}=U_m(b).
\tag{5}
\]
The parameter space \(\Delta_{N-1}\times K^N\) is compact and the barycenter map is continuous, so both extrema in (5) are attained.  Complete CAD quantifier elimination applied to (4) gives a quantifier-free semialgebraic definition of the moment body.

We define the relative rather than ambient boundary.  A first-order definition of \(A=\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) is
\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(x):\Longleftrightarrow
 \exists z^0,\ldots,z^N\ \exists\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_N
 \left[
 \bigwedge_{j=0}^N\Psi(z^j),\quad
 \sum_{j=0}^N\alpha_j=1,\quad
 x=\sum_{j=0}^N\alpha_jz^j
 \right].
\tag{6}
\]
The right side lies in the affine hull.  Conversely, affine dependence shortens any affine representation in \(\mathbb R^N\) to at most \(N+1\) points while preserving the coefficient sum, so (6) is exact.  Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{RI}(b):\Longleftrightarrow{}&\Psi(b)\wedge
 \exists\rho>0\ \forall x\left[
 \operatorname{Aff}(x)\wedge\sum_c(x_c-b_c)^2<\rho^2
 \Rightarrow\Psi(x)\right],\\
 \operatorname{RB}(b):\Longleftrightarrow{}&\Psi(b)\wedge\neg\operatorname{RI}(b).
 \end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
Formula \(\operatorname{RI}\) is precisely relative interior in \(A\).  The moment body is compact by (4), hence closed in \(A\); therefore \(\operatorname{RB}\) defines exactly \(\operatorname{relbd}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  Complete quantifier elimination makes these predicates semialgebraic.

Define endpoint graphs by
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_L(b,t)&\Longleftrightarrow
 \Phi(b,t)\wedge\forall s\{\Phi(b,s)\Rightarrow t\leq s\},\\
 G_U(b,t)&\Longleftrightarrow
 \Phi(b,t)\wedge\forall s\{\Phi(b,s)\Rightarrow s\leq t\}.
 \end{split}
\tag{8}
\]
By compactness and (5), each graph has exactly one value over every feasible \(b\), namely \(L_m(b)\) or \(U_m(b)\).  Consequently
\[
 P_0(b):\Longleftrightarrow\exists t\{G_L(b,t)\wedge G_U(b,t)\}
\tag{9}
\]
is exactly the predicate \(L_m(b)=U_m(b)\).  Since the full sharp fiber is the nonempty interval \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\), its affine dimension is zero when (9) holds and one otherwise.

For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), \(1\leq k\leq N\), define
\[
 E_{e,k}(b):\Longleftrightarrow
 \exists t\{G_e(b,t)\wedge\Phi_k(b,t)\},
 \qquad E_{e,0}(b):\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{false}.
\tag{10}
\]
These predicates are semialgebraic after quantifier elimination.  Endpoint compression gives \(E_{e,N}(b)\) for every feasible \(b\).  Hence the minimum endpoint support size equals \(k\) exactly when
\[
 S_{e,k}(b):\Longleftrightarrow E_{e,k}(b)\wedge\neg E_{e,k-1}(b),
 \qquad 1\leq k\leq N.
\tag{11}
\]
The negated predicate certifies that no smaller support attains the endpoint.

Complete \(\mathcal A_{m,\epsilon}\) by adjoining (7), (9), and (11) to its face stratification, eliminating all quantified variables, and taking a common truth-invariant CAD in \(b\)-space.  Retain exactly the cells satisfying \(\operatorname{RB}\).  The retained family is finite and semialgebraic.  On each cell, (9), exactly one \(S_{L,k}\), and exactly one \(S_{U,l}\) have constant truth values.  Thus point identification, fiber dimension, and the ordered minimum-support pair are constant, with both support labels in \(\{1,\ldots,N_m\}\).

All coefficients are rational.  The cited complete Collins CAD/QE result therefore proves termination and returns a defining sign formula and an algebraic CAD sample point \(b_C\) for each nonempty retained cell \(C\).  Specialize (8), (10), and the true labels \(S_{L,k}(b_C)\), \(S_{U,l}(b_C)\) at that sample point.  Algebraic coordinates can be encoded by rational minimal-polynomial and isolating-interval constraints, so a further complete rational-coefficient QE/CAD call terminates and returns algebraic endpoint values, atom locations, and weights for \(b_C\).  If a returned \(k\)-slot representation had zero mass or two equal positive-mass locations, deleting or merging them would give a \((k-1)\)-slot representation, contradicting \(S_{e,k}\).  Thus the cell's sample-point witnesses have the certified minimum sizes.

This construction gives one witness at \(b_C\), not a uniform symbolic choice of atoms and weights over all \(b\in C\).  If an arbitrary algebraic \(b\in C\) is supplied separately, encode each coordinate by a rational polynomial plus isolating interval, specialize the same formulas, and run complete QE/CAD afresh.  The same argument terminates and returns minimum-size endpoint witnesses at that \(b\).  For rational boundary inputs this is the boundary branch of the exact CAD recovery theorem.  No concrete atlas has been executed and no runtime guarantee is inferred from termination.

Finally, the boundary Dirac-regression proposition shows that the point-identified label is nonvacuous when \(m\geq2\):
\[
 \mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(g_m(q^*))=\{\delta_{q^*}\},
 \qquad L_m(g_m(q^*))=U_m(g_m(q^*))=f(q^*).
\tag{12}
\]
Such a moment vector is extreme, since a nontrivial convex decomposition, followed by mixing representations of its terms, would contradict the singleton fiber.  Moreover, the multinomial first-moment identity gives
\[
 q_{ay}=\frac1m\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}c_{ay}B_c^{(m)}(q),
\tag{13}
\]
so \(g_m\) is injective.  The overlap slab contains distinct perturbations of the uniform vector keeping propensity \(1/2\), so the body is not a singleton.  An extreme point of a non-singleton convex set is not in its relative interior.  Hence (12) lies on the relative boundary and receives the zero-dimensional label in (9).  This completes the narrowed constructive classification.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gives a finite truth-invariant classification of relative-boundary collapse and minimum endpoint support, with one certified algebraic sample-point witness per nonempty cell and on-demand recovery at any separately supplied algebraic moment vector. \textit{Gap.} Truncated-moment geometry and symbolic causal-bound methods do not provide this relative-boundary truth-invariant stratification with support-minimum labels and compatible endpoint-SCM recovery semantics. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can distinguish boundary precision from regularization and, for a supplied algebraic count law, recover minimum-support endpoint witnesses through a fresh exact run; no executed atlas, uniform witness map, or runtime guarantee is claimed.

\begin{proposition}[prop:optimized-limited-pooling-intersection]\label{prop:optimized-limited-pooling-intersection}
Corollary. For every \(p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), \((\ell_{m,p_*},u_{m,p_*})\in\mathcal E_m\). For every \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), the extrema defining \(I_{LW,m}^{\cap}(b)\) are attained and \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq I_{LW,m}^{\cap}(b)=\bigcap_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}I_{LW,m,p_*}(b)\). If \(m\ge2\), there is one nonempty relatively open set \(\mathcal R\subseteq\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) such that, for every \(b\in\mathcal R\), \(L_m(b)>\max_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}\ell_{m,p_*}^{\top}b\) and \(U_m(b)<\min_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}u_{m,p_*}^{\top}b\). The optimized interval above is the intersection of the scalar-reference, \(b\)-only transplant; no inclusion comparison with their full observed-covariate, covariate-dependent procedure is asserted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\geq 1\), \(p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), and \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\). Define
\[
r=p(q),\qquad y_1=\frac{q_{11}}{r},\qquad y_0=\frac{q_{01}}{1-r},
\qquad w_1=w_m(r,p_*),\qquad w_0=w_m(1-r,1-p_*).
\]
By the definition of \(K_{\epsilon}\),
\[
\epsilon\leq r\leq1-\epsilon,
\]
and the declared restriction \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) therefore gives
\(r,1-r,p_*,1-p_*>0\). Since \(q\in\Delta_3\), its four cells are
nonnegative, \(q_{10}+q_{11}=r\), and \(q_{00}+q_{01}=1-r\); hence
\(0\leq y_0,y_1\leq1\). Put \(a_1=1-w_1\) and \(a_0=1-w_0\). The
definition of \(w_m\) gives
\[
a_1=
\begin{cases}
(1-r)\left((p_*-r)/p_*\right)^{m-1},&m\text{ odd},\\
\left((p_*-r)/p_*\right)^m,&m\text{ even},
\end{cases}
\]
and gives the corresponding expression for \(a_0\) after replacing
\((r,p_*)\) by \((1-r,1-p_*)\). In both cases the power is even; its
coefficient is nonnegative and all denominators are positive. Thus
\[
a_0\geq0,\qquad a_1\geq0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
If \(m\geq2\) and \(r\neq p_*\), the powered ratios are nonzero and the
coefficients \(r\) and \(1-r\) that occur in the odd case are positive, so
\[
a_0>0,\qquad a_1>0.                                             \tag{2}
\]

Substitution into the definitions of the explicitly displayed scalar-reference
envelopes yields
\[
\ell_{m,p_*}(q)=y_1w_1-y_0w_0-a_0,
\qquad
u_{m,p_*}(q)=y_1w_1-y_0w_0+a_1.
\]
Because \(f(q)=y_1-y_0\), subtraction gives the two pointwise identities
\[
f(q)-\ell_{m,p_*}(q)=y_1a_1+(1-y_0)a_0,
\qquad
u_{m,p_*}(q)-f(q)=(1-y_1)a_1+y_0a_0.                             \tag{3}
\]
Every factor on the right-hand sides is nonnegative by (1) and
\(0\leq y_0,y_1\leq1\). Therefore
\[
\ell_{m,p_*}(q)\leq f(q)\leq u_{m,p_*}(q)
\quad\text{for every }q\in K_{\epsilon}.                         \tag{4}
\]

It remains to verify the degree condition in def:valid-envelope-class. For
fixed \(p_*\), the polynomial \(r\mapsto w_m(r,p_*)\) has degree at most
\(m\) and satisfies \(w_m(0,p_*)=0\). It is consequently divisible by
\(r\), with quotient of degree at most \(m-1\). Applying the same observation
to \(w_m(1-r,1-p_*)\) shows that this polynomial is divisible by \(1-r\).
Thus all apparent denominators in the definitions of \(\ell_{m,p_*}\) and
\(u_{m,p_*}\) cancel, and each envelope is a polynomial of total degree at
most \(m\) in the four cell coordinates of \(q\). For any monomial of degree
\(d<m\), multiplication by
\[
(q_{00}+q_{01}+q_{10}+q_{11})^{m-d}=1
\]
on the simplex homogenizes it to degree \(m\); the multinomial expansion then
writes it as a linear combination of the \(B_c^{(m)}\). Hence
\(\ell_{m,p_*},u_{m,p_*}\in V_m\). Combining this membership with (4) and
def:valid-envelope-class proves
\[
(\ell_{m,p_*},u_{m,p_*})\in\mathcal E_m.                          \tag{5}
\]
This derivation concerns exactly the displayed scalar-reference pair and uses
no property of any observed-covariate procedure.

Now fix \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\). By
def:bernstein-moment-body there exists
\(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\). Since both envelopes belong to
\(V_m\), their Bernstein coefficient vectors and the defining moment equalities
give
\[
A(p_*;b):=\ell_{m,p_*}^{\top}b
=\int\ell_{m,p_*}(q)\,d\nu(q),
\qquad
H(p_*;b):=u_{m,p_*}^{\top}b
=\int u_{m,p_*}(q)\,d\nu(q).                                    \tag{6}
\]
In particular, these values do not depend on the chosen representing measure.
The displayed envelope formulas are jointly continuous in \((q,p_*)\) on the
compact set \(K_{\epsilon}\times[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\): the overlap
restrictions bound every denominator \(r\), \(1-r\), \(p_*\), and
\(1-p_*\) away from zero. Uniform continuity and (6) therefore imply, as
\(s\to p_*\),
\[
|A(s;b)-A(p_*;b)|
\leq\sup_{q\in K_{\epsilon}}
|\ell_{m,s}(q)-\ell_{m,p_*}(q)|\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the analogous inequality holds for \(H\) and \(u\). Thus \(A(\cdot;b)\)
and \(H(\cdot;b)\) are continuous on the compact interval
\([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\). A convergent-subsequence argument for maximizing
and minimizing sequences shows that
\[
\max_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}A(p_*;b)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\min_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}H(p_*;b)                       \tag{7}
\]
are attained.

For each fixed \(p_*\), (5) and thm:sharp-envelope-domination give
\[
A(p_*;b)\leq L_m(b)\leq U_m(b)\leq H(p_*;b).                     \tag{8}
\]
Taking the attained maximum of the left envelope and the attained minimum of
the right envelope proves
\[
[L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq I_{LW,m}^{\cap}(b).                      \tag{9}
\]
Moreover, every interval \([A(p_*;b),H(p_*;b)]\) contains the common nonempty
interval \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\). The endpoint formula for an intersection of
intervals, together with (6) and (7), consequently gives
\[
I_{LW,m}^{\cap}(b)
=\bigcap_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}I_{LW,m,p_*}(b).            \tag{10}
\]

Assume henceforth that \(m\geq2\). For
\(s\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), define the contact sets
\[
\mathcal C_s^-=
\{q\in K_{\epsilon}:\ell_{m,s}(q)=f(q)\},
\qquad
\mathcal C_s^+=
\{q\in K_{\epsilon}:u_{m,s}(q)=f(q)\}.
\]
When \(p(q)\neq s\), (2) makes both \(a_0\) and \(a_1\) strictly positive.
The first identity in (3) can then vanish only when \(y_1=0\) and \(y_0=1\),
and the second can vanish only when \(y_1=1\) and \(y_0=0\). The positivity
of \(p(q)\) and \(1-p(q)\) converts these conditions into the contact
containments
\[
\mathcal C_s^-
\subseteq\{q:p(q)=s\}\cup\{q:q_{11}=q_{00}=0\},
\qquad
\mathcal C_s^+
\subseteq\{q:p(q)=s\}\cup\{q:q_{10}=q_{01}=0\}.                  \tag{11}
\]

For every polynomial \(h\) of total degree at most two and every
\(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), define
\[
M_h(b)=\int h(q)\,d\nu(q),
\qquad \nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b).
\]
This quantity is well defined. Indeed, since \(m\geq2\), the same
homogenization argument used above puts \(h\) in \(V_m\), so its integral is
a fixed linear combination of the Bernstein moments \(b_c\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_-(b)&=M_{p q_{11}}(b)M_{q_{00}}(b)
-M_{p q_{00}}(b)M_{q_{11}}(b),\\
D_+(b)&=M_{p q_{10}}(b)M_{q_{01}}(b)
-M_{p q_{01}}(b)M_{q_{10}}(b).
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{12}
\]
Each \(M_h\) in (12) is linear in \(b\); hence \(D_-\) and \(D_+\) are
polynomials on \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).

Suppose that
\(L_m(b)=\max_s A(s;b)\), and let \(s_b\) be a maximizer, whose existence
was proved in (7). By the lower equality characterization in
thm:sharp-envelope-domination,
\[
b\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q):q\in\mathcal C_{s_b}^-\}.
\]
Thus a finite convex combination of points in \(\mathcal C_{s_b}^-\) supplies
a measure \(\nu_b^-\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\). By (11), every support
point of \(\nu_b^-\) satisfies
\[
(p-s_b)q_{11}=0,
\qquad
(p-s_b)q_{00}=0.
\]
Integration, followed by the definition (12), gives
\[
M_{p q_{11}}(b)=s_bM_{q_{11}}(b),
\qquad
M_{p q_{00}}(b)=s_bM_{q_{00}}(b),
\qquad
D_-(b)=0.
\]
We have therefore proved
\[
L_m(b)=\max_s A(s;b)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_-(b)=0.           \tag{13}
\]
The upper equality characterization in thm:sharp-envelope-domination, the
second containment in (11), and the identical argument yield
\[
U_m(b)=\min_s H(s;b)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_+(b)=0.           \tag{14}
\]

The two obstruction polynomials are simultaneously nonzero at a feasible
moment vector. Let
\[
q^{(0)}=(1-\epsilon,0,\epsilon,0),
\qquad
q^{(1)}=(0,\epsilon,0,1-\epsilon),
\qquad
\nu^\dagger=\tfrac12\delta_{q^{(0)}}+\tfrac12\delta_{q^{(1)}},
\]
and set \(b^\dagger=\int g_m(q)\,d\nu^\dagger(q)\). Both support points lie
in \(K_{\epsilon}\), with treatment probabilities \(\epsilon\) and
\(1-\epsilon\), respectively. Hence
\(b^\dagger\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), and direct substitution into (12)
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_-(b^\dagger)
&=\frac14\left\{(1-\epsilon)^3-\epsilon(1-\epsilon)^2\right\}
=\frac14(1-\epsilon)^2(1-2\epsilon)>0,\\
D_+(b^\dagger)
&=\frac14\left\{\epsilon^3-\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)\right\}
=\frac14\epsilon^2(2\epsilon-1)<0.                              \tag{15}
\end{aligned}
\]
The strict signs use exactly \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).

Let \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) and define
\[
\mathcal R=
\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})
\cap\{b\in\mathcal A:D_-(b)D_+(b)\neq0\}.                        \tag{16}
\]
This set is relatively open. To prove that it is nonempty, choose
\(b^\circ\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). Such a point
exists because a maximal affinely independent subset of the finite-dimensional
convex body has a simplex with the same affine hull, and the relative interior
of that simplex is contained in the relative interior of the body. For
\(0<t\leq1\), put
\[
b_t=(1-t)b^\dagger+t b^\circ.
\]
If the relative ball in \(\mathcal A\) of radius \(\rho>0\) about
\(b^\circ\) is contained in \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), convexity shows that
the relative ball of radius \(t\rho\) about \(b_t\) is also contained in
\(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\). Thus
\(b_t\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) for every \(t>0\).
By (15), \(D_-(b^\dagger)D_+(b^\dagger)\neq0\). Continuity of the polynomial
\(D_-D_+\) and \(b_t\to b^\dagger\) as \(t\downarrow0\) show that
\(D_-(b_t)D_+(b_t)\neq0\) for all sufficiently small \(t>0\). Hence
\(\mathcal R\neq\varnothing\).

Finally, if \(b\in\mathcal R\), (13) rules out equality between the lower
endpoint and the optimized lower envelope in (8), while (14) rules out equality
between the upper endpoint and the optimized upper envelope. The weak
inequalities in (8) are therefore strict on both sides:
\[
L_m(b)>\max_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}
\ell_{m,p_*}^{\top}b,
\qquad
U_m(b)<\min_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}
u_{m,p_*}^{\top}b.
\]
This is the asserted common relatively open strict-improvement region.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This compares the sharp fiber with the strongest homogeneous \(b\)-only interval obtained by intersecting the entire scalar-reference transplant, and certifies a common observable region of strict improvement at both endpoints. \textit{Gap.} Lee and Weidner establish validity of their higher-order analytic family and discuss intersections, but do not compare its optimized homogeneous count-law transplant with the sharp continuous-mixture fiber or prove simultaneous strictness on an open region. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst who optimizes the scalar reference before reporting limited-pooling bounds can still diagnose an observable open region where the complete cluster-count law strictly tightens both ATE endpoints.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hahn-banach-quotient-norm]\label{lem:hahn-banach-quotient-norm}
Let \(X\) be a real normed vector space, let \(M\subseteq X\) be a closed linear subspace, and let \(x\in X\).  There is a bounded real linear functional \(\Lambda\in X^*\) such that \(\Lambda|_M=0\), \(\|\Lambda\|\leq1\), and \(\Lambda(x)=\inf_{z\in M}\|x-z\|\).  If \(x\notin M\), it can be chosen with \(\|\Lambda\|=1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:riesz-markov-representation]\label{lem:riesz-markov-representation}
If \(K\) is a compact Hausdorff space, every bounded real linear functional \(\Lambda\) on \(C(K)\) has a unique representation \(\Lambda(u)=\int_Ku\,d\sigma\) by a finite signed regular Borel measure \(\sigma\), with \(\|\Lambda\|=|\sigma|(K)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:jordan-decomposition-signed-measure]\label{lem:jordan-decomposition-signed-measure}
Every finite signed measure \(\sigma\) has a Jordan decomposition \(\sigma=\sigma^+-\sigma^-\) into mutually singular finite positive measures, and its total-variation measure satisfies \(|\sigma|=\sigma^++\sigma^-\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:growing-plate-identification-modulus]\label{thm:growing-plate-identification-modulus}
For every finite \(m\geq1\) and every \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), let \(d_m(\epsilon)\) be the uniform approximation modulus in the preceding definition.  Put \(\rho=1-2\epsilon\) and
\[
 E_m(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{T_m(1/\rho)}
 =\operatorname{sech}\!\left(m\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\rho)\right),
\]
where \(T_m\) is the degree-\(m\) Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.  Then
\[
 \max_{b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}}\{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}
 =\sup_{b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})}\{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}
 =2d_m(\epsilon),
\]
and
\[
 0<d_m(\epsilon)\leq E_m(\epsilon)\leq\rho^m.
\]
Moreover, \(E_m(\epsilon)<\rho^m\) whenever \(m\geq2\).  Consequently every feasible fiber has width at most
\[
 2\operatorname{sech}\!\left(m\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\frac{1}{1-2\epsilon}\right)\right).
\]
For every \(0<\delta\leq2\), the integer plate-size condition
\[
 m\geq
 \frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(2/\delta)}
 {\operatorname{arcosh}(1/(1-2\epsilon))}
\]
guarantees \(U_m(b)-L_m(b)\leq\delta\) simultaneously for all \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(K=K_{\epsilon}\), \(V=V_m\), and \(d=d_m(\epsilon)\).  The overlap restriction gives \(p(q)\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), so the denominators in \(f\) are positive on \(K\).  Hence \(f\in C(K)\), because \(K\) is compact and the cell-coordinate maps are continuous.  The multinomial identity gives
\[
 \sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}B_c^{(m)}(q)
 =\left(\sum_{a,y}q_{ay}\right)^m=1,
\]
so \(1\in V\).

First fix \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) and \(\nu_1,\nu_0\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).  The signed measure \(\zeta=\nu_1-\nu_0\) satisfies, by the defining moment constraints,
\[
 \int_K B_c^{(m)}\,d\zeta=0
 \qquad(c\in\mathcal I_m).
\]
Consequently \(\int_Kv\,d\zeta=0\) for every \(v\in V\).  Because \(\nu_1\) and \(\nu_0\) are probability measures, \(\lVert\zeta\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq2\).  Thus every \(v\in V\) obeys
\[
 \left|\int_K f\,d\nu_1-\int_K f\,d\nu_0\right|
 =\left|\int_K(f-v)\,d\zeta\right|
 \leq2\lVert f-v\rVert_{\infty,K}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(v\in V\) and then the supremum over ordered pairs in the nonempty fiber yields
\[
 U_m(b)-L_m(b)\leq2d. \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\sup S-\inf S=\sup_{s_1,s_0\in S}(s_1-s_0)\) for every nonempty bounded \(S\subset\mathbb R\), so endpoint attainment is not being assumed.

We next show that equality in (1) occurs for some feasible moment vector.  The space \(V\) is finite dimensional and therefore closed in \(C(K)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:finite-plate-nonpolynomial}\), \(f\notin V\), and hence \(d>0\).  Applying \(\texttt{lem:hahn-banach-quotient-norm}\) to \(C(K)\), \(V\), and \(f\) gives \(\Lambda\in C(K)^*\) such that
\[
 \lVert\Lambda\rVert=1,
 \qquad \Lambda(v)=0\quad(v\in V),
 \qquad \Lambda(f)=d. \tag{2}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:riesz-markov-representation}\) and \(\texttt{lem:jordan-decomposition-signed-measure}\), there are mutually singular finite positive measures \(\sigma^+\) and \(\sigma^-\) on \(K\) such that \(\sigma=\sigma^+-\sigma^-\),
\[
 \Lambda(h)=\int_Kh\,d\sigma
 \quad(h\in C(K)),
 \qquad
 \sigma^+(K)+\sigma^-(K)=|\sigma|(K)=1. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(1\in V\), equations (2)--(3) imply
\[
 0=\Lambda(1)=\sigma^+(K)-\sigma^-(K).
\]
Together with (3), this gives \(\sigma^+(K)=\sigma^-(K)=1/2\).  Therefore \(\nu_+=2\sigma^+\) and \(\nu_-=2\sigma^-\) are probability measures on \(K\).  For every \(c\in\mathcal I_m\), equation (2) gives
\[
 \int_KB_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_+
 -\int_KB_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_-
 =2\Lambda(B_c^{(m)})=0.
\]
Thus \(\nu_+\) and \(\nu_-\) have a common moment vector \(b_*\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), whereas
\[
 \int_Kf\,d\nu_+-\int_Kf\,d\nu_-
 =2\Lambda(f)=2d. \tag{4}
\]
Combining (1) and (4) proves
\[
 \max_{b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}}
 \{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}=2d. \tag{5}
\]

For completeness, the relative interior of the moment body is nonempty without any extra support assumption.  If its affine dimension is \(r\), choose affinely independent \(z_0,\ldots,z_r\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) whose affine hull is \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  The barycenter \(b_0=(r+1)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^rz_j\) has a relative open ball contained in the simplex \(\operatorname{conv}\{z_0,\ldots,z_r\}\), hence \(b_0\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  By \(\texttt{def:bernstein-moment-body}\), choose \(\eta\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\).  For \(0\leq t<1\), define
\[
 \nu_{t,+}=(1-t)\eta+t\nu_+,
 \qquad
 \nu_{t,-}=(1-t)\eta+t\nu_-,
 \qquad
 b_t=(1-t)b_0+tb_*.
\]
Linearity of all Bernstein moments puts both measures in \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_t)\).  If the relative ball of radius \(r_0>0\) about \(b_0\) lies in \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), then the relative ball of radius \((1-t)r_0\) about \(b_t\) also lies there: each of its points has the form \((1-t)(b_0+h)+tb_*\) with \(\lVert h\rVert<r_0\), and convexity applies.  Hence \(b_t\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  Equation (4) now gives
\[
 U_m(b_t)-L_m(b_t)
 \geq\int_Kf\,d\nu_{t,+}-\int_Kf\,d\nu_{t,-}
 =2td.
\]
Letting \(t\uparrow1\) and using (1) proves
\[
 \sup_{b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})}
 \{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}=2d. \tag{6}
\]

It remains to establish the constructive Chebyshev bound.  Define the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind by
\[
 T_0(x)=1,\qquad T_1(x)=x,
 \qquad T_{k+1}(x)=2xT_k(x)-T_{k-1}(x).
\]
Induction in this recurrence shows that \(T_m\) is a polynomial of degree \(m\) and that
\[
 T_m(-x)=(-1)^mT_m(x). \tag{7}
\]
The angle-addition identities, followed by induction in the same recurrence, give
\[
 T_m(\cos\theta)=\cos(m\theta),
 \qquad
 T_m(\cosh a)=\cosh(ma). \tag{8}
\]
The first identity in (8) implies \(|T_m(x)|\leq1\) for \(|x|\leq1\).

Put \(\rho=1-2\epsilon\in(0,1)\) and define the degree-\(m\) polynomial
\[
 R_m(p)=(-1)^m
 \frac{T_m((2p-1)/\rho)}{T_m(1/\rho)}. \tag{9}
\]
The denominator in (9) is positive: for
\[
 a=\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\rho)>0,
\]
the second identity in (8) gives \(T_m(1/\rho)=\cosh(ma)>0\).  By (7),
\[
 R_m(0)=(-1)^m\frac{T_m(-1/\rho)}{T_m(1/\rho)}=1, \tag{10}
\]
and, again by (7),
\[
 R_m(1-p)=(-1)^mR_m(p). \tag{11}
\]
For \(p\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), one has \(|(2p-1)/\rho|\leq1\).  Therefore (8)--(9) imply
\[
 |R_m(p)|\leq \frac{1}{T_m(1/\rho)}
 =\operatorname{sech}(ma)=E_m(\epsilon). \tag{12}
\]

Because \(R_m\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(m\) and (10) says that \(1-R_m\) vanishes at zero, the polynomial factor theorem gives a polynomial
\[
 P_{m-1}(p)=\frac{1-R_m(p)}{p} \tag{13}
\]
of degree at most \(m-1\).  On \(K\), set
\[
 y_1=\frac{q_{11}}{p(q)},
 \qquad
 y_0=\frac{q_{01}}{1-p(q)}.
\]
The overlap inequalities make both divisions valid.  Moreover,
\[
 0\leq y_1\leq1,
 \qquad
 0\leq y_0\leq1, \tag{14}
\]
because \(q_{11}\leq p(q)\) and \(q_{01}\leq1-p(q)\).  With \(p=p(q)\), define
\[
 v_m(q)=q_{11}P_{m-1}(p)-q_{01}P_{m-1}(1-p)
 +\mathbf 1_{\{m\ {\rm odd}\}}R_m(p). \tag{15}
\]
Each of the first two terms in (15) is a cell coordinate multiplied by a polynomial of degree at most \(m-1\) in the linear polynomial \(p\), and the last term has degree at most \(m\).  Thus \(v_m\) is the restriction to the simplex of a polynomial of total degree at most \(m\).  By \(\texttt{lem:finite-plate-nonpolynomial}\), whose first assertion identifies the degree-\(m\) polynomial restrictions with \(V_m\), we have \(v_m\in V_m\).

Equations (11), (13), and (15), together with \(q_{11}=py_1\), \(q_{01}=(1-p)y_0\), and \(f=y_1-y_0\), yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f(q)-v_m(q)
 &=R_m(p)y_1-R_m(1-p)y_0
   -\mathbf 1_{\{m\ {\rm odd}\}}R_m(p)\\
 &=
 \begin{cases}
 R_m(p)(y_1-y_0),&m\text{ even},\\
 R_m(p)(y_1+y_0-1),&m\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\end{aligned} \tag{16}
\]
Both brackets in (16) have absolute value at most one by (14).  Hence (12) and \(v_m\in V_m\) show
\[
 d_m(\epsilon)
 \leq\lVert f-v_m\rVert_{\infty,K}
 \leq E_m(\epsilon). \tag{17}
\]

The hyperbolic formula and its comparison with the geometric bound are elementary consequences of (8).  Since \(\cosh a=1/\rho\),
\[
 E_m(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{T_m(1/\rho)}
 =\frac{1}{\cosh(ma)}=\operatorname{sech}(ma). \tag{18}
\]
For \(m=1\), (18) gives \(E_1(\epsilon)=\rho\).  For every integer \(m\geq2\), the function \(x\mapsto x^m\) has positive second derivative on \((0,\infty)\); strict midpoint convexity and \(e^a\ne e^{-a}\) therefore give
\[
 \cosh(ma)=\frac{e^{ma}+e^{-ma}}{2}
 >\left(\frac{e^a+e^{-a}}{2}\right)^m
 =\cosh^m(a).
\]
Taking positive reciprocals yields
\[
 0<E_m(\epsilon)<\operatorname{sech}^m(a)=\rho^m
 \qquad(m\geq2), \tag{19}
\]
while equality holds in the comparison when \(m=1\).  Equations (17), (19), and \(d>0\) prove
\[
 0<d_m(\epsilon)\leq E_m(\epsilon)\leq\rho^m,
\]
with the asserted strict second inequality for \(m\geq2\).  Combining (1) and (17) gives the claimed uniform width bound \(2\operatorname{sech}(ma)\).

Finally, let \(0<\delta\leq2\).  Then \(\operatorname{arcosh}(2/\delta)\) is defined and nonnegative.  If
\[
 m\geq\frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(2/\delta)}{a},
\]
then \(ma\geq\operatorname{arcosh}(2/\delta)\).  Since \(\cosh\) is increasing on \([0,\infty)\),
\[
 \cosh(ma)\geq\frac{2}{\delta},
 \qquad
 2E_m(\epsilon)=\frac{2}{\cosh(ma)}\leq\delta.
\]
Together with (1) and (17), this proves \(U_m(b)-L_m(b)\leq\delta\) for every \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  The argument establishes a uniform identification-width bound in the plate size \(m\); it asserts neither \(d_m(\epsilon)=E_m(\epsilon)\) nor an exact asymptotic formula.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies the exact worst-case width of the fixed-plate Bernstein moment fiber as twice a best-approximation modulus and gives a shifted-Chebyshev hyperbolic-secant certificate, while retaining strict nonidentification for every finite plate size. \textit{Gap.} The accepted-bank note stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match also approximates the same overlap-restricted four-cell reciprocal ATE integrand by Chebyshev-type polynomials, but for minimax MSE estimation in a finite-category iid experiment with factorial-moment lifts. It does not study an unrestricted Borel mixing law observed only through fixed-plate multinomial counts, nor does it supply the population Bernstein-fiber identity max\_b{U\_m(b)-L\_m(b)}=2d\_m(epsilon) or the shifted-Chebyshev certificate for that identified-set width. Fixed-plate mixture nonidentification results likewise do not provide those conclusions. \textit{Consumer.} For \(0<\delta\leq2\), a design analyst may choose \(m\geq\operatorname{arcosh}(2/\delta)/\operatorname{arcosh}(1/(1-2\epsilon))\) to cap worst-case latent-mixture identification width by \(\delta\); this population identified-set guarantee is distinct from the accepted-bank note's sampling-MSE result and is not a sampling-error rate in \(n\) or a CAD runtime claim.

\section{Interpretation}

The equality \(\max_b\{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}=2d_m(\epsilon)\) turns worst-case latent-mixture ambiguity into a concrete uniform-approximation problem. The shifted-Chebyshev polynomial supplies a computable upper certificate, so choosing \(m\geq\operatorname{arcosh}(2/\delta)/\operatorname{arcosh}(1/(1-2\epsilon))\) guarantees width at most \(\delta\) for every feasible count law. Although the accepted-bank stat\_discrete\_ate\_minimax\_loggap\_polynomial\_upper\_match also uses polynomial approximation of this reciprocal integrand, this result concerns a population fixed-plate mixing-law fiber and its identified-set width, not the accepted note's finite-category iid minimax MSE. It neither equates \(d_m(\epsilon)\) with the Chebyshev certificate nor controls sampling error in \(n\) or symbolic-computation time.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

No concrete CAD run, concretely executed boundary atlas, or practical-runtime evidence is supplied. The CAD results establish exact termination and algebraic endpoint and witness recovery in principle for fixed rational inputs. The boundary theorem proves a finite truth-invariant semialgebraic stratification of the entire relative boundary, but it does not provide a uniform symbolic endpoint-witness map over positive-dimensional cells; a separately supplied algebraic boundary law instead requires specialization and a fresh terminating QE/CAD run. The full displayed shifted-Chebyshev guarantee is retained, but exact asymptotics of \(d_m(\epsilon)\) beyond the proved worst-case-width identity and upper bound are a separate future project. Heterogeneous plate sizes are also outside the present scope.

\section{Honest scope}

The central identification theorems concern relative-interior count laws and fixed finite \(m\). The Dirac-fiber proposition records point-identified boundary images for \(m\geq2\), so strict width is not asserted on the boundary. The boundary-fiber-atlas theorem nevertheless proves, for the entire relative boundary, a finite truth-invariant semialgebraic stratification on whose cells identification dimension and minimum lower- and upper-endpoint support sizes are constant; it also supplies a terminating construction with defining formulas, labels, and sample-point witnesses. What remains outside scope is a concretely executed atlas, practical CAD runtime guarantees, and a uniform symbolic endpoint-witness map over positive-dimensional cells. Whole-set coverage is only the immediate corollary of exact count-law coverage and the defining confidence-set union, not an independent solve target. The exact \(m=1\), \(\epsilon=1/4\), uniform-count proposition is a sanity check. Exact asymptotics of \(d_m(\epsilon)\) beyond the proved worst-case identity and shifted-Chebyshev upper bound, as well as growing or heterogeneous plate-size extensions, belong to separate future projects.
