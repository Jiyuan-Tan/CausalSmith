Fix a candidate law \(b'\) and let \(W\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,b')\) on the finite set \(\mathcal H_n\).  Let \(t_1>\cdots>t_J\) be the distinct extended-real values of \(T_n(w;b')\), and put
\[
 A_j=\{w:T_n(w;b')\geq t_j\},
 \qquad p_j=\Pr_{b'}(W\in A_j).
\]
For every \(w\) with statistic \(t_j\), the definition including all ties gives \(P_n(b';w)=p_j\).  Since \(A_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_J\), the rejection set \(\{w:P_n(b';w)\leq\alpha\}\) is either empty or equals \(A_{j_*}\) for the largest index with \(p_{j_*}\leq\alpha\).  Therefore
\[
 \Pr_{b'}\{P_n(b';W)\leq\alpha\}=p_{j_*}\leq\alpha.
\tag{5}
\]
This proves exact finite-sample size control, including atoms and deviance ties.

We next derive semialgebraicity.  Split the candidate simplex into its finitely many zero-pattern cells.  On a fixed cell, \(T_n(x;b')\) is finite exactly when every positive component of \(x\) corresponds to a positive component of \(b'\); all comparisons involving \(+\infty\) are then fixed Boolean values.  When both deviances are finite,
\[
 \frac12\{T_n(w;b')-T_n(z;b')\}
 =\sum_c\left[w_c\log\frac{w_c}{n}-z_c\log\frac{z_c}{n}
 +(z_c-w_c)\log b'_c\right],
\]
with zero-count terms omitted.  Exponentiation is order preserving.  Moving negative integer powers of the positive \(b'_c\)'s to the opposite side turns the comparison into an inequality between two rational constants times monomials in \(b'\), hence a polynomial inequality after clearing positive integer denominators.  There are finitely many \(w\), so the ordering cells are semialgebraic.  On each such cell the upper-tail index set is fixed and
\[
 P_n(b';z)=\sum_{w\text{ in that tail}}
 \frac{n!}{\prod_cw_c!}\prod_c(b'_c)^{w_c}
\]
is a polynomial.  Thus \(P_n(b';z)>\alpha\), and hence \(\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z)\), is semialgebraic.

For the confidence set, introduce \(b'\), at most \(N_m+1\) locations \(q^k\in K_{\epsilon}\), masses \(\omega_k\geq0\), and impose
\[
 \sum_k\omega_k=1,
 \quad b'_c=\sum_k\omega_kB_c^{(m)}(q^k),
 \quad t=\sum_k\omega_kf(q^k),
 \quad b'\in\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{6}
\]
The affine-dependence reduction in thm:sharp-primal-dual shows that (6) represents exactly the defining union \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Because \(p(q^k)\) and \(1-p(q^k)\) are positive on \(K_{\epsilon}\), clearing their denominators is equivalence preserving.  With rational \(\epsilon\) and \(\alpha\), (6) is therefore a first-order formula over \(\mathbb Q\).  The cited fact lem:cad-quantifier-elimination implies that its projection onto \(t\) is semialgebraic and that \(\operatorname{CAD}^{\mathrm{conf}}_{m,n}\) terminates.  Since \(-1\leq f\leq1\), it lies in \([-1,1]\).  Every one-dimensional semialgebraic set is a finite disjoint union of points and intervals; CAD records strict versus weak signs at every endpoint, and its finite endpoints are algebraic sections represented by defining polynomials and isolating intervals.  Reporting all cells gives the complete decomposition.  Projecting only to the least and greatest feasible \(t\), while retaining whether (6) holds at either value, gives the certified endpoint hull and its attainability flags.
