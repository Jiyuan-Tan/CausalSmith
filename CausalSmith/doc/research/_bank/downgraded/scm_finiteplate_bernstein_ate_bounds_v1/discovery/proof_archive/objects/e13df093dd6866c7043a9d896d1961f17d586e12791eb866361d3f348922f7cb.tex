Because \(K_{\epsilon}\) is compact and metrizable, choose a dense sequence \((x_j)_{j\geq1}\) and positive numbers \(a_j\) summing to one.  Then \(\rho=\sum_{j\geq1}a_j\delta_{x_j}\) is a probability measure with full support.  Put \(a=\int g_m\,d\rho\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  If \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), the relative-interior ball property implies that, for all sufficiently small \(0<\delta<1\),
\[
 c_{\delta}=\frac{b-\delta a}{1-\delta}
 =b+\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}(b-a)
 \in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}.
\]
Choose \(\eta_{\delta}\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\) with \(\int g_m\,d\eta_{\delta}=c_{\delta}\).  Then
\[
 \nu_b=\delta\rho+(1-\delta)\eta_{\delta}
\]
has full support and satisfies \(\int g_m\,d\nu_b=b\), so \(\nu_b\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).

On the finite-dimensional space \(V_m\), define \(\langle v,w\rangle=\int vw\,d\nu_b\).  This is an inner product: a nonzero continuous \(v\) is bounded away from zero on some nonempty relatively open subset of \(K_{\epsilon}\), which full support gives positive mass.  Thus the \(L^2(\nu_b)\) projection \(v_b=\Pi_{V_m}^{L^2(\nu_b)}f\) exists uniquely.  Both \(v_b\) and \(f\) are continuous and bounded on compact \(K_{\epsilon}\), so \(r_b=f-v_b\) is continuous and bounded.  Orthogonality and \(B_c^{(m)}\in V_m\) give
\[
 \int B_c^{(m)}r_b\,d\nu_b=0\qquad(c\in\mathcal I_m).
\]
Since \(1=\sum_cB_c^{(m)}\in V_m\), also \(\int r_b\,d\nu_b=0\).  Finally,
\[
 \int fr_b\,d\nu_b
 =\int(v_b+r_b)r_b\,d\nu_b
 =\int r_b^2\,d\nu_b.
\]
If \(f\notin V_m\), then \(r_b\) is nonzero in \(L^2(\nu_b)\), because continuity and full support turn almost-sure equality into equality everywhere.  Choose \(0<s<\lVert r_b\rVert_{\infty}^{-1}\).  The functions \(1\pm sr_b\) are strictly positive and integrate to one, so
\[
 d\nu_b^{\pm}=(1\pm sr_b)\,d\nu_b
\]
define probability measures.  Orthogonality gives \(\int B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_b^{\pm}=b_c\), whereas
\[
 \int f\,d\nu_b^{\pm}
 =\int f\,d\nu_b\pm s\int r_b^2\,d\nu_b.
\]
The squared norm is positive, so these are distinct members of the same fiber with different ATEs.
