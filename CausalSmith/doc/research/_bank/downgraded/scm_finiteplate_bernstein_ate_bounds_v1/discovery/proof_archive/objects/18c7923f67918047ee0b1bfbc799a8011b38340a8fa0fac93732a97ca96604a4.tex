Write \(K=K_{\epsilon}\), \(V=V_m\), and \(d=d_m(\epsilon)\).  Compactness of \(K\) and continuity of \(f\) make the uniform norm finite.  The identity \(\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}B_c^{(m)}=1\) shows that \(1\in V\).

We first prove the universal upper bound by moment cancellation.  Fix \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) and \(\nu_1,\nu_0\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\).  For \(\zeta=\nu_1-\nu_0\), the defining constraints of the fiber imply
\[
 \int B_c^{(m)}\,d\zeta=0\quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),
\]
and hence \(\int v\,d\zeta=0\) for every \(v\in V\).  Since \(\nu_1\) and \(\nu_0\) are probabilities, \(\|\zeta\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq2\).  Thus, for every \(v\in V\),
\[
 \left|\int f\,d\nu_1-\int f\,d\nu_0\right|
 =\left|\int(f-v)\,d\zeta\right|
 \leq\|f-v\|_{\infty,K}\|\zeta\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq2\|f-v\|_{\infty,K}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(v\), and then the supremum over ordered pairs in the same nonempty fiber, gives
\[
 U_m(b)-L_m(b)\leq2d. \tag{1}
\]
Here the equality of the width with the pairwise supremum follows directly from \(\sup a-\inf a=\sup_{a_1,a_0}(a_1-a_0)\); endpoint attainment is not needed for this step.

We next prove that the factor two in (1) is exact.  The space \(V\) is finite dimensional and therefore closed in \(C(K)\).  The finite-plate nonpolynomial lemma gives \(f\notin V\).  The Hahn--Banach quotient-norm theorem therefore yields a bounded real linear functional \(\Lambda\) on \(C(K)\) such that
\[
 \|\Lambda\|=1,\qquad \Lambda(v)=0\ (v\in V),\qquad \Lambda(f)=d,
\]
where the sign is chosen so that the last display is nonnegative.  The Riesz--Markov representation and Jordan decomposition give a finite signed regular Borel measure \(\sigma=\sigma^+-\sigma^-\) satisfying
\[
 \Lambda(u)=\int_Ku\,d\sigma,qquad
 \sigma^+(K)+\sigma^-(K)=|\sigma|(K)=1. \tag{2}
\]
The normalization is forced, rather than assumed.  Since \(1\in V\),
\[
 0=\Lambda(1)=\sigma(K)=\sigma^+(K)-\sigma^-(K).
\]
Combining this equality with (2) gives
\[
 \sigma^+(K)=\sigma^-(K)=\frac12.
\]
Therefore \(\nu_+=2\sigma^+\) and \(\nu_-=2\sigma^-\) are probability measures.  For every \(c\in\mathcal I_m\), annihilation of \(V\) gives
\[
 \int B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_+-\int B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_-
 =2\int B_c^{(m)}\,d\sigma=0.
\]
They consequently share a feasible moment vector \(b_*\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), while
\[
 \int f\,d\nu_+-\int f\,d\nu_-
 =2\int f\,d\sigma=2\Lambda(f)=2d. \tag{3}
\]
Equation (1) and the two compatible measures in (3) prove
\[
 \max_{b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}}\{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}=2d; \tag{4}
\]
in particular, the maximum in (4) is attained at \(b_*\).

It remains to show that restricting to relative-interior moment vectors leaves the same supremum.  Choose \(b_0\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) and, by the definition of the moment body, choose one representing probability \(\eta\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\).  For \(0\leq t<1\), set
\[
 \nu_{t,+}=(1-t)\eta+t\nu_+,qquad
 \nu_{t,-}=(1-t)\eta+t\nu_-,qquad
 b_t=(1-t)b_0+tb_*.
\]
Linearity of the Bernstein moments puts both measures in \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_t)\).  Also \(b_t\) is in the relative interior: if a relative ball of radius \(r>0\) about \(b_0\) lies in the moment body, then the relative ball of radius \((1-t)r\) about \(b_t\) lies there by convexity.  From (3),
\[
 U_m(b_t)-L_m(b_t)
 \geq\int f\,d\nu_{t,+}-\int f\,d\nu_{t,-}
 =2td.
\]
Letting \(t\uparrow1\), then using (1), proves
\[
 \sup_{b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})}
 \{U_m(b)-L_m(b)\}=2d. \tag{5}
\]

We now bound \(d\) constructively.  Put
\[
 x=2p(q)-1,qquad \rho=1-2\epsilon.
\]
The overlap restriction \(\epsilon\leq p(q)\leq1-\epsilon\) gives \(|x|\leq\rho<1\), while \(1+x=2p(q)>0\) and \(1-x=2\{1-p(q)\}>0\).  Define
\[
 v_m(q)=2q_{11}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-x)^k
          -2q_{01}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x^k
          -\mathbf 1_{\{m\ {\rm odd}\}}x^m. \tag{6}
\]
The function in (6) is the restriction to the simplex of a polynomial of total degree at most \(m\): \(x\) has degree one, and the first two terms multiply powers of degree at most \(m-1\) by one cell coordinate.  By the finite-plate polynomial-span lemma, \(v_m\in V_m\).

The finite geometric identities
\[
 \frac1{1+x}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-x)^k+\frac{(-x)^m}{1+x},
 \qquad
 \frac1{1-x}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x^k+\frac{x^m}{1-x}
\]
and the equality \(f=2q_{11}/(1+x)-2q_{01}/(1-x)\) give two cases.  If \(m\) is even, then
\[
 f(q)-v_m(q)
 =2x^m\left\{\frac{q_{11}}{1+x}-\frac{q_{01}}{1-x}\right\}. \tag{7}
\]
If \(m\) is odd, the correction \(-x^m\) in (6) gives
\[
 f(q)-v_m(q)
 =x^m\left[1-2\left\{\frac{q_{11}}{1+x}+\frac{q_{01}}{1-x}\right\}\right]. \tag{8}
\]
Because \(q_{11}\leq p(q)=(1+x)/2\) and \(q_{01}\leq1-p(q)=(1-x)/2\),
\[
 0\leq\frac{q_{11}}{1+x}\leq\frac12,
 \qquad
 0\leq\frac{q_{01}}{1-x}\leq\frac12. \tag{9}
\]
The bracket multiplying \(x^m\) has absolute value at most one in both (7) and (8), by (9).  Hence
\[
 \|f-v_m\|_{\infty,K_{\epsilon}}\leq\rho^m=(1-2\epsilon)^m,
\]
which proves \(d_m(\epsilon)\leq(1-2\epsilon)^m\).

Finally, finite dimensionality makes \(V_m\) closed in \(C(K_{\epsilon})\).  The finite-plate nonpolynomial lemma gives \(f\notin V_m\), so its distance from this closed subspace is strictly positive: \(d_m(\epsilon)>0\).  Combining this fact with (4), (5), and the constructive upper bound proves all modulus assertions.  For \(0<\delta\leq2\), the displayed plate-size condition and \(-\log(1-2\epsilon)>0\) imply \(2(1-2\epsilon)^m\leq\delta\); applying (1) proves the final simultaneous guarantee.  This is a deterministic uniform identification-width rate in \(m\), not a sampling rate in \(n\).
