Fix rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and rational \(b\), and put \(N=N_m\).  For \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\), define
\[
 d(q)=p(q)\{1-p(q)\},
 \qquad a(q)=q_{11}\{1-p(q)\}-q_{01}p(q).
\tag{1}
\]
The overlap restriction gives \(d(q)\geq\epsilon(1-\epsilon)>0\) and \(f(q)=a(q)/d(q)\).

Let \(\Phi_N(b,t)\) assert the existence of locations \(q^1,\ldots,q^N\in K_{\epsilon}\) and weights \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N\geq0\) such that
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^N\omega_k=1,\qquad
 b_c=\sum_{k=1}^N\omega_kB_c^{(m)}(q^k)\quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 t\prod_{k=1}^Nd(q^k)
 =\sum_{k=1}^N\omega_ka(q^k)
   \prod_{\substack{1\leq r\leq N\\r\neq k}}d(q^r).
\tag{3}
\]
Because every factor cleared in (3) is strictly positive, (3) is equivalent to \(t=\sum_k\omega_kf(q^k)\).  The simplex, overlap, mass, Bernstein-moment, and objective restrictions (1)--(3) are finite polynomial formulas with rational coefficients.

Every value satisfying \(\Phi_N(b,t)\) is generated by a measure in \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), and hence lies in \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\).  Conversely, the all-feasible endpoint compression in the sharp primal--dual theorem supplies \(N\)-atom measures attaining both endpoints whenever \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).  Therefore
\[
 \exists t\,\Phi_N(b,t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon},
\tag{4}
\]
and, when (4) holds,
\[
 \min\{t:\Phi_N(b,t)\}=L_m(b),\qquad
 \max\{t:\Phi_N(b,t)\}=U_m(b).
\tag{5}
\]
This does not assert that every interior value of the full fiber needs only \(N\) atoms; it uses only that the two vertical endpoints do.  The parameter space \(\Delta_{N-1}\times K_{\epsilon}^N\) is compact, and the constraints with fixed \(b\) are closed, so the extrema in (5) and their witnesses are attained.

The first-order formulas
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_L(b,t)&:\Longleftrightarrow
 \Phi_N(b,t)\ \wedge\ \forall s\{\Phi_N(b,s)\Rightarrow t\leq s\},\\
 G_U(b,t)&:\Longleftrightarrow
 \Phi_N(b,t)\ \wedge\ \forall s\{\Phi_N(b,s)\Rightarrow s\leq t\}
 \end{split}
\tag{6}
\]
thus define exactly the lower and upper endpoints.  The established CAD quantifier-elimination lemma applies to (1)--(6): it terminates, decides (4), and, when feasible, returns algebraic endpoint values and algebraic sample points for the endpoint atoms and weights.  A returned endpoint is a root of a rational defining polynomial isolated from its other real roots by rational endpoints.  Factoring that polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\), selecting the unique irreducible factor with a root in the isolating interval, and refining the interval if necessary terminates and returns the minimal polynomial and a rational isolating interval.

Suppose next that \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  The sharp primal--dual theorem supplies attained vectors \(\lambda^-\) and \(\lambda^+\).  Lower global feasibility is the quantified implication
\[
 q\in K_{\epsilon}\Longrightarrow
 d(q)\sum_c\lambda_cB_c^{(m)}(q)\leq a(q),
\tag{7}
\]
and upper feasibility reverses the inequality.  Positivity of \(d(q)\) proves that (7) is equivalent to the rational Bernstein minorant inequality; all terms in (7) are polynomial.  The lower complementary-contact equations for the primal variables in (2)--(3) are
\[
 \omega_k\left\{a(q^k)-d(q^k)\sum_c\lambda_cB_c^{(m)}(q^k)\right\}=0
 \quad(1\leq k\leq N),
\tag{8}
\]
with the sign-reversed slack for the upper system, together with \(\lambda^{\top}b=t\).  Thus the relative-interior primal--dual branch is a first-order real-algebraic formula.  Strong duality and contact from the sharp primal--dual theorem prove feasibility of these systems exactly at \(L_m(b)\) and \(U_m(b)\); the CAD lemma therefore returns matching globally feasible dual certificates and at most \(N\) endpoint atoms.

For arbitrary rational \(b\), the boundary-capable branch uses only (2)--(6), so no failure of dual attainment can affect primal feasibility or either endpoint.  After the primal outputs have been obtained, the existence of a matching lower certificate is the separate first-order sentence
\[
 \exists\lambda\left[
 \lambda^{\top}b=L_m(b),\quad
 \forall q\{q\in K_{\epsilon}\Rightarrow
 d(q)\lambda^{\top}g_m(q)\leq a(q)\}
 \right],
\tag{9}
\]
and the upper sentence reverses the last inequality and substitutes \(U_m(b)\).  CAD decides both.  If either is false, the procedure reports the prescribed boundary/nonattainment flag while retaining the already certified primal endpoint and witness.  Finally, the SCM moment-reduction lemma converts each returned atomic measure into a compatible member of \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\) with the same count law and causal endpoint.  This proves termination, exactness, the all-feasible \(N_m\)-atom endpoint cap, and the stated witness semantics.
