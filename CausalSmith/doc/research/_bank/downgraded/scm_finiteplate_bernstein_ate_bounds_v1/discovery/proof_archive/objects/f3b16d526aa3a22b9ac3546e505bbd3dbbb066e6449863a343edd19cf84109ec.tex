Fix a candidate count law \(b'\in\Delta_{N_m-1}\), and let \(W\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,b')\) on the finite set \(\mathcal H_n\).  Write
\[
 v_1>v_2>\cdots>v_J
\]
for the distinct values in \([0,+\infty]\) taken by \(T_n(w;b')\) as \(w\) ranges over \(\mathcal H_n\), and define
\[
 E_j=\{w\in\mathcal H_n:T_n(w;b')\geq v_j\},
 \qquad \pi_j=\Pr_{b'}(W\in E_j).
\tag{1}
\]
The tie-inclusive definition in def:exact-multinomial-region gives \(P_n(b';w)=\pi_j\) whenever \(T_n(w;b')=v_j\).  Moreover, \(E_1\subseteq E_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_J\), so \(\pi_1\leq\pi_2\leq\cdots\leq\pi_J\).  If no \(j\) satisfies \(\pi_j\leq\alpha\), the rejection event is empty.  Otherwise let \(j_*\) be the largest such index.  The rejection event is then exactly \(E_{j_*}\), including every point tied at \(v_{j_*}\).  Consequently,
\[
 \Pr_{b'}\{P_n(b';W)\leq\alpha\}
 =\Pr_{b'}(W\in E_{j_*})
 =\pi_{j_*}\leq\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
This establishes the asserted finite-sample size bound without a continuity assumption or auxiliary randomization.  The argument also covers \(+\infty\)-valued deviances: such sample points have zero probability under \(b'\) whenever their infinity is caused by a positive count in a zero-probability cell.

We next prove semialgebraicity of the inversion.  For each subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal I_m\), consider the candidate-law cell
\[
 D_S=\left\{b'\in\Delta_{N_m-1}:b'_c>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ c\in S\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
There are finitely many such cells, and each is semialgebraic.  For \(x\in\mathcal H_n\), put
\[
 a_x=\prod_{c:x_c>0}\left(\frac{x_c}{n}\right)^{x_c}\in\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\tag{4}
\]
On \(D_S\), the deviance \(T_n(x;b')\) is finite exactly when \(\{c:x_c>0\}\subseteq S\).  If both \(T_n(w;b')\) and \(T_n(z;b')\) are finite, exponentiation, which is strictly increasing, and positivity of every \(b'_c\) for \(c\in S\) give the equivalence
\[
 T_n(w;b')\geq T_n(z;b')
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a_w\prod_{c\in S}(b'_c)^{z_c}
 \geq
 a_z\prod_{c\in S}(b'_c)^{w_c}.
\tag{5}
\]
The right-hand side is a polynomial inequality with rational coefficients after multiplying by a common positive integer denominator.  If one or both deviances are infinite, the truth value of their extended-real comparison is fixed on \(D_S\).  Hence, for every fixed \(w,z\in\mathcal H_n\), the comparison \(T_n(w;b')\geq T_n(z;b')\) is semialgebraic on each \(D_S\).

Partition each \(D_S\) further according to the finitely many comparisons with \(z=z^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  On each resulting semialgebraic cell the tail set
\[
 J(b')=\{w\in\mathcal H_n:T_n(w;b')\geq T_n(z^{\mathrm{obs}};b')\}
\]
is constant.  On such a cell, the exact upper-tail probability is the polynomial
\[
 P_n(b';z^{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\sum_{w\in J(b')}
   \frac{n!}{\prod_c w_c!}\prod_c(b'_c)^{w_c},
\tag{6}
\]
where a zero exponent contributes one.  Thus the condition \(P_n(b';z^{\mathrm{obs}})>\alpha\) is a strict polynomial inequality on each cell.  Taking the finite union over all zero patterns and comparison cells proves from def:exact-multinomial-region that \(\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is semialgebraic.

It remains to turn the union in def:whole-set-confidence into a finite first-order formula.  The all-feasible-law clause of thm:sharp-primal-dual states that the attainable pairs are the compact hull
\[
 \left\{\left(\int g_m\,d\nu,\int f\,d\nu\right):
 \nu\in\mathcal P(K_{\epsilon})\right\}
 =\operatorname{conv}\{(g_m(q),f(q)):q\in K_{\epsilon}\},
\tag{7}
\]
that the section over each \(b'\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) is the attained interval \([L_m(b'),U_m(b')]\), and that every pair in the hull has a representation with at most \(N_m+1\) atoms.  Therefore, padding a shorter representation with zero masses, the definition of \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\) is equivalent to the existence of \(q^1,\ldots,q^{N_m+1}\in K_{\epsilon}\) and \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_{N_m+1}\geq0\) such that
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\sum_{k=1}^{N_m+1}\omega_k=1,\qquad
 b'_c=\sum_{k=1}^{N_m+1}\omega_k B_c^{(m)}(q^k)
       \quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),\\
 &t=\sum_{k=1}^{N_m+1}\omega_k f(q^k),
 \qquad b'\in\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}}).
 \end{split}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (8) represents exactly the whole causal identified-set union, rather than a relaxation over unrelated moment vectors.

Suppose now that \(\epsilon\), \(\alpha\), and \(z^{\mathrm{obs}}\) are rational.  Membership of each \(q^k\) in \(K_{\epsilon}\) is given by the polynomial conditions \(q^k_{ay}\geq0\), \(\sum_{a,y}q^k_{ay}=1\), and
\[
 \epsilon\leq q^k_{10}+q^k_{11}\leq1-\epsilon.
\tag{9}
\]
The Bernstein equalities in (8) have rational coefficients.  By (9) and \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), every denominator \(p(q^k)\) and \(1-p(q^k)\) in the equation for \(t\) is strictly positive.  Multiplication by their product therefore preserves equivalence and converts that equation into a polynomial equality with rational coefficients.  Equations (3)--(6) show that the remaining condition \(b'\in\mathcal R_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is a finite Boolean combination of rational polynomial sign conditions.  Consequently (8), with all variables except \(t\) existentially quantified, is a first-order formula over \(\mathbb Q\).

By the cited quantifier-elimination result lem:cad-quantifier-elimination, cylindrical algebraic decomposition terminates on this formula and returns an equivalent semialgebraic cell decomposition on the real \(t\)-line.  In addition, for every \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\),
\[
 0\leq\frac{q_{11}}{p(q)}\leq1,
 \qquad
 0\leq\frac{q_{01}}{1-p(q)}\leq1,
\]
because \(q_{11}\leq p(q)\), \(q_{01}\leq1-p(q)\), and both denominators are positive.  Hence \(-1\leq f(q)\leq1\), and (8) implies \(t\in[-1,1]\).  Thus \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(z^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is a semialgebraic subset of \([-1,1]\).

A semialgebraic subset of the real line has a finite disjoint decomposition into points and intervals.  The cited CAD result represents every finite boundary by a defining polynomial with rational coefficients and a rational isolating interval.  Evaluating the quantifier-free sign description at a boundary determines whether that boundary is included, so strict and weak inequalities yield the claimed open-or-closed flags.  Accordingly, \(\operatorname{CAD}^{\mathrm{conf}}_{m,n}\), as specified in def:confidence-set-cad, may return all cells.  Alternatively, when the set is nonempty it may select the leftmost and rightmost cell boundaries, report their algebraic representations, and test membership at each boundary to certify its attainability; when the set is empty it reports the empty certificate.  This is precisely the certified endpoint-hull output with attainability flags, and proves every assertion.
