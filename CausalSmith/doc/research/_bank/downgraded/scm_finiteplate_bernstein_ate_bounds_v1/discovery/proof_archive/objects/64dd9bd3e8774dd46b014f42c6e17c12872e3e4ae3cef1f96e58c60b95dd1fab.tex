For \(m=1\), the Bernstein coordinates are the four cell coordinates.  Put \(r=p(q)\in[1/4,3/4]\).  Since \(r\leq3/4\) and \(1-r\leq3/4\),
\[
 \frac{q_{11}}r=1-\frac{q_{10}}r\leq1-\frac43q_{10},
 \qquad
 -\frac{q_{01}}{1-r}\leq-\frac43q_{01}.
\]
Adding gives the global majorant
\[
 f(q)\leq1-\frac43(q_{01}+q_{10}).
\]
Likewise,
\[
 \frac{q_{11}}r\geq\frac43q_{11},
 \qquad
 -\frac{q_{01}}{1-r}=-1+\frac{q_{00}}{1-r}\geq-1+\frac43q_{00},
\]
and the simplex identity makes their sum the global minorant
\[
 \frac13-\frac43(q_{01}+q_{10})\leq f(q).
\]
At \(b=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\), their expectations are \(-1/3\) and \(1/3\).  Weak duality gives \(L_1(b)\geq-1/3\) and \(U_1(b)\leq1/3\).

For the upper endpoint put equal mass on
\[
 (1/4,1/2,0,1/4)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (1/4,0,1/2,1/4).
\]
Their mean is \(b\), their propensities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\), and direct substitution gives \(f=1/3\) at each.  For the lower endpoint put equal mass on
\[
 (1/2,1/4,1/4,0)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (0,1/4,1/4,1/2).
\]
Again the mean is \(b\), overlap holds, and \(f=-1/3\) at each.  Hence the exact interval is \([-1/3,1/3]\).  The two affine functions displayed above are the claimed Bernstein dual certificates, and each attaining measure uses two, hence at most \(N_1=4\), atoms.
