Fix \(m\geq 1\) and rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and write \(N=N_m\), \(K=K_{\epsilon}\), and
\[
h(q)=\bigl(g_m(q),f(q)\bigr)\in\mathbb R^{N+1}.
\]
The vector with all four coordinates equal to \(1/4\) belongs to \(K\), because its propensity is \(1/2\); thus \(K\) is nonempty.  It is compact because it is the intersection of the compact simplex \(\Delta_3\) with the closed restrictions \(\epsilon\leq p(q)\leq1-\epsilon\).  On \(K\), both denominators in \(f\) are at least \(\epsilon>0\), so \(f\), and hence \(h\), is continuous.

We first derive the finite representation needed by the parameterized CAD formulas.  Put
\[
\Gamma=\left\{\left(\int g_m\,d\nu,\int f\,d\nu\right):\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\right\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
H=\operatorname{conv}\{h(q):q\in K\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every point \(h(q)\) satisfies \(\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}B_c^{(m)}(q)=1\), by the multinomial theorem.  Thus \(h(K)\) lies in an affine hyperplane of \(\mathbb R^{N+1}\) of dimension at most \(N\).  Consider a finite convex representation \(x=\sum_{j=1}^s\omega_jh(q^j)\) with all \(\omega_j>0\) and \(s>N+1\).  Affine dependence gives real numbers \(a_j\), not all zero, for which
\[
\sum_{j=1}^s a_j=0,
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^s a_jh(q^j)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Both signs occur among the \(a_j\).  With
\[
\delta=\min_{j:a_j>0}\frac{\omega_j}{a_j}>0,
\qquad
\omega'_j=\omega_j-\delta a_j,
\tag{3}
\]
the new weights are nonnegative, at least one is zero, and (2) gives \(\sum_j\omega'_j=1\) and \(\sum_j\omega'_jh(q^j)=x\).  Iterating proves that every point of \(H\) is a convex combination of at most \(N+1\) points of \(h(K)\).  Consequently \(H\) is the continuous image of the compact space \(\Delta_N\times K^{N+1}\), and is compact.

Every finite convex combination in \(H\) is the pair of integrals of its atomic probability measure, so \(H\subseteq\Gamma\).  Conversely, let \(z=\int h\,d\nu\) for \(\nu\in\mathcal P(K)\).  If \(z\notin H\), compactness supplies a point \(z_0\in H\) minimizing \(\lVert z-z_0\rVert\).  For every \(x\in H\), applying minimality to \(z_0+s(x-z_0)\in H\), \(0\leq s\leq1\), and differentiating the resulting quadratic inequality at \(s=0\) gives
\[
(z-z_0)^{\top}(x-z_0)\leq0.
\tag{4}
\]
Taking \(x=h(q)\), integrating (4), and using \(z=\int h\,d\nu\) yields \(\lVert z-z_0\rVert^2\leq0\), a contradiction.  Hence \(\Gamma=H\).  In particular, \(\Gamma\) is compact and convex, and every one of its points has a representing probability measure with at most \(N+1\) atoms.

The projection of \(\Gamma\) onto its first \(N\) coordinates is exactly \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), by Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}bernstein\text{-}moment\text{-}body}\).  Therefore, for every \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), the section
\[
\Gamma_b=\{t\in\mathbb R:(b,t)\in\Gamma\}
\tag{5}
\]
is nonempty, compact, and convex.  It is consequently a closed interval.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}sharp\text{-}ate\text{-}fiber}\), its endpoints are \(L_m(b)\) and \(U_m(b)\), and both have probability-measure witnesses with at most \(N+1\) atoms.  This also derives, rather than assumes, the finite endpoint witnesses used below.

We next express every classification record by a first-order formula over the reals.  For an atom \(q^j\), set
\[
d_j=p(q^j)\{1-p(q^j)\},
\qquad
a_j=q^j_{11}\{1-p(q^j)\}-q^j_{01}p(q^j).
\tag{6}
\]
On \(K\), \(d_j\geq\epsilon(1-\epsilon)>0\) and \(f(q^j)=a_j/d_j\).  Let \(\Phi(b,t)\) assert the existence of \(N+1\) locations \(q^j\in K\) and weights \(\omega_j\geq0\) such that
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\omega_j=1,
\qquad
b_c=\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\omega_jB_c^{(m)}(q^j)\quad(c\in\mathcal I_m),
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
t\prod_{j=1}^{N+1}d_j
=
\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\omega_ja_j\prod_{\substack{1\leq r\leq N+1\\r\neq j}}d_r.
\tag{8}
\]
Because every \(d_j\) is strictly positive, (8) is equivalent to \(t=\sum_j\omega_jf(q^j)\).  The simplex restrictions on each \(q^j\), the overlap restrictions, (7), and (8) are finitely many polynomial equalities and weak inequalities with rational coefficients.  The representation above proves
\[
\Phi(b,t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(b,t)\in\Gamma.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\Phi\) is an exact formula, not a relaxation.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}cad\text{-}quantifier\text{-}elimination}\), its quantified variables can be eliminated.  In particular,
\[
\Psi(b)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\exists t\,\Phi(b,t)
\tag{10}
\]
is a semialgebraic definition of \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\).

It is important here to use the actual affine hull, which can be smaller than the count-law hyperplane.  A first-order definition of \(A=\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) is
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(x)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
\exists z^0,\ldots,z^N\ \exists\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_N:
\left[
\bigwedge_{j=0}^N\Psi(z^j),\quad
\sum_{j=0}^N\alpha_j=1,\quad
x=\sum_{j=0}^N\alpha_jz^j
\right].
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, the right side is contained in the affine hull.  Conversely, any finite affine combination of points in \(\mathbb R^N\) with more than \(N+1\) nonzero coefficients can be shortened: an affine dependence as in (2), without a sign restriction, allows one coefficient to be set to zero while preserving both the coefficient sum and the represented point.  Hence (11) equals \(A\).

Define
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{RI}(b)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;&\Psi(b)\ \wedge\ 
\exists\rho>0\ \forall x\,
\left[
\operatorname{Aff}(x)\ \wedge\ \sum_{c}(x_c-b_c)^2<\rho^2
\ \Longrightarrow\ \Psi(x)
\right],\\
\operatorname{RB}(b)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;&\Psi(b)\ \wedge\ \neg\operatorname{RI}(b).
\end{split}
\tag{12}
\]
Formula \(\operatorname{RI}\) is exactly relative interior in \(A\).  Since \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) is the compact projection of \(\Gamma\), it is closed in \(A\); hence \(\operatorname{RB}\) defines
\(\operatorname{relbd}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), with relative boundary taken in \(A\).  Equations (10)--(12) and the cited CAD result show that this relative boundary is semialgebraic.  This step also explains why the ambient boundary in the unrepaired claim was not the intended object: \(\sum_cb_c=1\) makes the ambient interior empty.

The lower- and upper-endpoint graphs are the first-order relations
\[
\begin{split}
G_L(b,t)&\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\Phi(b,t)\ \wedge\ 
\forall s\,\{\Phi(b,s)\Longrightarrow t\leq s\},\\
G_U(b,t)&\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\Phi(b,t)\ \wedge\ 
\forall s\,\{\Phi(b,s)\Longrightarrow s\leq t\}.
\end{split}
\tag{13}
\]
Compactness of (5) proves that these graphs are nonempty over every feasible \(b\), and (9) proves that their unique values are exactly \(L_m(b)\) and \(U_m(b)\).  Consequently
\[
P_0(b)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\exists t\,\{G_L(b,t)\wedge G_U(b,t)\}
\tag{14}
\]
is the semialgebraic point-identification predicate \(L_m(b)=U_m(b)\).  Since (5) is a nonempty interval, the affine dimension of \(\Theta_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) is \(0\) when (14) holds and \(1\) otherwise.

For \(1\leq k\leq N+1\), let \(\Phi_k(b,t)\) be (7)--(8) with exactly \(k\) displayed locations and weights.  Zero weights and repeated locations make this equivalent to the existence of an atomic witness having support size at most \(k\).  For \(e\in\{L,U\}\), define
\[
E_{e,k}(b)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;
\exists t\,\{G_e(b,t)\wedge\Phi_k(b,t)\},
\qquad
E_{e,0}(b)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\mathrm{false}.
\tag{15}
\]
These predicates are semialgebraic after quantifier elimination.  The endpoint representation proved after (5) gives \(E_{e,N+1}(b)\) for every feasible \(b\).  Thus the minimum support size for endpoint \(e\) is \(k\) exactly on
\[
S_{e,k}=\{b:E_{e,k}(b)\wedge\neg E_{e,k-1}(b)\},
\qquad 1\leq k\leq N+1.
\tag{16}
\]
The negated predicate in (16) is the certificate that no smaller endpoint support is possible.

Complete the construction \(\mathcal A_{m,\epsilon}\) from Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}boundary\text{-}atlas\text{-}handle}\) by adjoining the formulas (12), (14), and (16), eliminating all their quantified variables, and taking one common truth-invariant CAD of the \(b\)-space.  Retain precisely the cells satisfying \(\operatorname{RB}(b)\).  There are finitely many such semialgebraic cells.  On each retained cell, (14), exactly one \(S_{L,k}\), and exactly one \(S_{U,l}\) have constant truth values.  Hence point identification, the affine dimension \(0\) or \(1\), and the ordered pair \((k,l)\) of minimum endpoint support sizes are constant there.  This is the asserted finite semialgebraic stratification of the relative boundary.

All input coefficients are rational because \(m\) is fixed and \(\epsilon\) is rational.  The termination assertion follows from \(\mathrm{lem{:}cad\text{-}quantifier\text{-}elimination}\).  CAD discards an empty truth cell by evaluating its defining formula at the cell sample.  For every retained cell its recursive lifting data provide a sample \(b\); applying witness extraction to the existential formulas in (13) and (15) returns atom locations, nonnegative weights, and endpoint values.  The membership in \(S_{e,k}\) certifies that the returned \(k\)-atom witness is of minimum size.  For a separately supplied algebraic \(b\) in the cell, specialization of the same finite formulas and a further CAD run terminates and returns its endpoint witnesses as well.  On rational boundary inputs this witness semantics agrees with the boundary-capable primal branch of \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}cad\text{-}recovery}\).

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}boundary\text{-}dirac\text{-}regression}\) gives \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(g_m(q^*))=\{\delta_{q^*}\}\) for \(m\geq2\).  This moment vector is an extreme point of \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\): if \(g_m(q^*)=\theta b_1+(1-\theta)b_2\) for \(0<\theta<1\), mixing representing measures for \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) gives a representing measure for \(g_m(q^*)\), so the singleton-fiber conclusion forces both measures to equal \(\delta_{q^*}\), and hence \(b_1=b_2=g_m(q^*)\).  Moreover,
\[
q_{ay}=\frac1m\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}c_{ay}B_c^{(m)}(q)
\tag{17}
\]
by differentiating the multinomial identity, so \(g_m\) is injective.  The set \(K\) contains more than one point, for a sufficiently small nonzero perturbation of the uniform vector within one treatment arm preserves its propensity \(1/2\).  Hence \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) is not a singleton.  An extreme point of a non-singleton convex set cannot lie in its relative interior, since a sufficiently short line segment through a relative-interior point would express it as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points.  Thus \(g_m(q^*)\) lies on the relative boundary and is assigned the point-identified alternative (14), confirming that no premise of universal boundary width has been used.
