The cited real-algebraic fact lem:cad-quantifier-elimination is used only for termination and exact representation; all reductions to its hypotheses are derived here.

First consider arbitrary rational \(b\) and rational \(\epsilon\).  Set \(N=N_m\).  Introduce \(N+1\) locations \(q^k\), masses \(\omega_k\geq0\), and a value \(t\), with
\[
 q^k\in K_{\epsilon},\qquad
 \sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\omega_k=1,
 \qquad
 b_c=\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\omega_kB_c^{(m)}(q^k),
 \qquad
 t=\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\omega_k f(q^k).
\tag{1}
\]
All simplex, overlap, mass, and moment restrictions in (1) are polynomial with rational coefficients.  Each denominator in the last equality is a product of propensities \(p(q^k)\{1-p(q^k)\}\), bounded below by \(\epsilon^2>0\); multiplying by their product therefore preserves equality and produces a polynomial formula.

The attainable-pair argument in thm:sharp-primal-dual showed that \((b,t)\) belongs to the moment--target hull if and only if it has a representation with at most \(N+1\) atoms.  Therefore (1) is feasible for some \(t\) exactly when \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), and its feasible \(t\)-section is exactly \([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\).  The quantified formulas
\[
 \Phi(b,t)\ \wedge\ \forall s\{\Phi(b,s)\Rightarrow t\leq s\},
 \qquad
 \Phi(b,t)\ \wedge\ \forall s\{\Phi(b,s)\Rightarrow s\leq t\},
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\Phi\) denotes (1) with its atom variables existentially quantified, define the lower and upper endpoints.  The parameter space for (1) is compact, so both extrema and witnesses exist.  By lem:cad-quantifier-elimination, CAD terminates on (1)--(2), decides infeasibility, and otherwise returns algebraic endpoint values and algebraic sample points for endpoint witnesses.  An algebraic endpoint has an effectively computable square-free defining polynomial; factoring over \(\mathbb Q\), selecting its irreducible factor, and refining the CAD section interval produces its minimal polynomial and a rational isolating interval.

For \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\), thm:sharp-primal-dual improves the atom cap to \(N\) and supplies attained dual vectors.  The lower dual conditions are
\[
 \forall q\in K_{\epsilon},\quad
 \sum_c\lambda_cB_c^{(m)}(q)\leq f(q),
 \qquad
 \lambda^{\top}b=t,
\tag{3}
\]
together with (1) using \(N\) atoms and complementary contact
\[
 \omega_k\left\{f(q^k)-\sum_c\lambda_cB_c^{(m)}(q^k)\right\}=0
 \quad(1\leq k\leq N).
\tag{4}
\]
The upper system reverses (3) and uses the analogous nonnegative majorant slack in (4).  Multiplication by \(p(q)\{1-p(q)\}>0\) converts each global rational inequality to an equivalent polynomial inequality without changing its direction.  Thus (1), (3), and (4) are first-order real-algebraic formulas.  The primal--dual theorem proves that both systems are feasible exactly at their respective endpoints, so CAD returns matching global certificates, endpoint atoms, and the algebraic endpoint representations claimed.

On the boundary-capable branch, (1)--(2) contain no dual variables and therefore recover the primal endpoints whether or not a continuous dual optimizer exists.  Afterward, existence of a matching minorant or majorant is the separate first-order sentence obtained from (3) and \(\lambda^{\top}b=L_m(b)\) or \(U_m(b)\); CAD decides each sentence.  A negative answer changes neither compact primal feasibility nor the witnesses already returned, and so is correctly reported as a nonattainment flag.  Finally, lem:scm-moment-reduction converts every returned atomic mixing measure into a compatible model in \(\mathfrak M_{m,\epsilon}\), with exactly the same moment law and causal endpoint.
