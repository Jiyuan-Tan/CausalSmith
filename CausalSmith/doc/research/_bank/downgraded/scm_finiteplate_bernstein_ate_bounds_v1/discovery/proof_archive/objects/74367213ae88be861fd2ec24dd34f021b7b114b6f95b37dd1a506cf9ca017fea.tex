Fix \(m\geq1\), \(p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), and \(q\in K_{\epsilon}\). Put
\[
 r=p(q),\qquad y_1=\frac{q_{11}}r,\qquad y_0=\frac{q_{01}}{1-r},
 \qquad w_1=w_m(r,p_*),\qquad w_0=w_m(1-r,1-p_*).
\]
The definition of \(K_{\epsilon}\) and \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) give
\(r,1-r,p_*,1-p_*>0\), while the four-cell simplex restrictions give
\(0\leq y_0,y_1\leq1\). Define
\(a_1=1-w_1\) and \(a_0=1-w_0\). From the definition of \(w_m\),
\[
 a_1=
 \begin{cases}
 (1-r)\left((p_*-r)/p_*\right)^{m-1},&m\text{ odd},\\
 \left((p_*-r)/p_*\right)^m,&m\text{ even},
 \end{cases}
\]
and the same formula with \((r,p_*)\) replaced by
\((1-r,1-p_*)\) gives \(a_0\). The relevant powers are even, so
\(a_0,a_1\geq0\). Moreover, if \(m\geq2\) and \(r\neq p_*\), then
\(a_0,a_1>0\).

Direct substitution in the definitions of the two envelopes gives
\[
 \ell_{m,p_*}=y_1w_1-y_0w_0-a_0,
 \qquad
 u_{m,p_*}=y_1w_1-y_0w_0+a_1.
\]
Since \(f=y_1-y_0\), it follows equation by equation that
\[
 f-\ell_{m,p_*}=y_1a_1+(1-y_0)a_0\geq0,
 \qquad
 u_{m,p_*}-f=(1-y_1)a_1+y_0a_0\geq0.                 \tag{1}
\]
These inequalities used only positivity and the simplex restrictions stated above.

For fixed \(p_*\), the polynomial \(r\mapsto w_m(r,p_*)\) has degree at most
\(m\) and satisfies \(w_m(0,p_*)=0\); hence it is divisible by \(r\), with
quotient of degree at most \(m-1\). Likewise
\(w_m(1-r,1-p_*)\) is divisible by \(1-r\). Consequently the apparent
denominators in \(q_{11}w_m(r,p_*)/r\) and in the corresponding untreated
terms cancel, and both \(\ell_{m,p_*}\) and \(u_{m,p_*}\) are polynomials of
total degree at most \(m\) in the four cells. If a monomial has degree
\(d<m\), multiplying it by
\((q_{00}+q_{01}+q_{10}+q_{11})^{m-d}=1\) on the simplex homogenizes it to
degree \(m\). The multinomial expansion then writes it as a linear combination
of the degree-\(m\) Bernstein polynomials. Thus
\(\ell_{m,p_*},u_{m,p_*}\in V_m\), and (1), together with
def:valid-envelope-class, proves
\((\ell_{m,p_*},u_{m,p_*})\in\mathcal E_m\). This verifies every member of
the homogeneous, constant-coefficient, scalar-reference binary-limit family
named in the statement; no property of the full observed-covariate procedure is
used.

The same membership argument covers the more general display-(14) population
envelopes named in the statement. By the meaning of a population-envelope
pair, their binary-limit substitution satisfies the pointwise lower and upper
inequalities on \(K_{\epsilon}\). Homogeneity of order \(m\) and constant
coefficients make both substituted components polynomials of total degree at
most \(m\) in the four cell probabilities. The homogenization argument above
therefore puts both components in \(V_m\), so
def:valid-envelope-class puts every such pair in \(\mathcal E_m\).
Consequently thm:sharp-envelope-domination, including its contact-hull equality
criterion, applies to each one.

Now fix \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) and choose
\(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), whose existence follows from
def:bernstein-moment-body. Because the two envelopes lie in \(V_m\),
\[
 A(p_*;b):=\ell_{m,p_*}^{\top}b=\int\ell_{m,p_*}\,d\nu,
 \qquad
 H(p_*;b):=u_{m,p_*}^{\top}b=\int u_{m,p_*}\,d\nu                     \tag{2}
\]
do not depend on the chosen representing measure. Positivity bounds every
denominator in these integrands away from zero, so their displayed formulas
are jointly continuous in \((q,p_*)\) on the compact set
\(K_{\epsilon}\times[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\). Hence \(A(\,\cdot\,;b)\) and
\(H(\,\cdot\,;b)\) are continuous; explicitly, as \(s\to p_*\),
\[
 |A(s;b)-A(p_*;b)|
 \leq\sup_{q\in K_{\epsilon}}|\ell_{m,s}(q)-\ell_{m,p_*}(q)|
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
and the same bound holds with \(H\) and \(u\). A maximizing sequence for \(A\) has a
convergent subsequence in the compact reference interval, and continuity makes
its limit a maximizer; the identical argument applied to a minimizing sequence
for \(H\) gives a minimizer.

For every fixed \(p_*\), thm:sharp-envelope-domination and the membership just
proved yield
\[
 A(p_*;b)\leq L_m(b)\leq U_m(b)\leq H(p_*;b).                         \tag{3}
\]
Taking the attained maximum on the left and attained minimum on the right proves
\[
 [L_m(b),U_m(b)]\subseteq I_{LW,m}^{\cap}(b).
\]
The intervals in (3) have the nonempty common subinterval
\([L_m(b),U_m(b)]\); therefore the elementary endpoint description of an
intersection of intervals and (2) give
\[
 I_{LW,m}^{\cap}(b)=
 \bigcap_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}I_{LW,m,p_*}(b).                \tag{4}
\]

It remains to obtain simultaneous strictness. Assume \(m\geq2\). For a fixed
reference \(s\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\), let
\[
 \mathcal C_s^-:=\{q\in K_{\epsilon}:\ell_{m,s}(q)=f(q)\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_s^+:=\{q\in K_{\epsilon}:u_{m,s}(q)=f(q)\}.
\]
If \(p(q)\neq s\), both \(a_0\) and \(a_1\) are strictly positive. The first
identity in (1) can then vanish only if \(y_1=0\) and \(y_0=1\), whereas the
second can vanish only if \(y_1=1\) and \(y_0=0\). Since positivity makes the
denominators in the definitions of \(y_0,y_1\) nonzero, this proves the contact
containments
\[
 \mathcal C_s^-\subseteq\{p=s\}\cup\{q_{11}=q_{00}=0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_s^+\subseteq\{p=s\}\cup\{q_{10}=q_{01}=0\}.              \tag{5}
\]

For every polynomial \(h\) of degree at most two and every
\(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\), define
\[
 M_h(b):=\int h(q)\,d\nu(q),\qquad \nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b).
\]
This is well defined: because \(m\geq2\), homogenization as above gives
\(h\in V_m\), so every two representing measures with moment vector \(b\)
have the same integral. Define the two observable polynomial functions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_-(b)&=M_{p q_{11}}(b)M_{q_{00}}(b)
          -M_{p q_{00}}(b)M_{q_{11}}(b),\\
 D_+(b)&=M_{p q_{10}}(b)M_{q_{01}}(b)
          -M_{p q_{01}}(b)M_{q_{10}}(b).
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{6}
\]
Each \(M_h\) is a linear Bernstein-moment functional of \(b\), so
\(D_-\) and \(D_+\) are polynomial functions on the affine hull of the moment
body.

Suppose first that
\(L_m(b)=\max_s A(s;b)\), and let \(s_b\) attain the maximum. The lower equality
criterion in thm:sharp-envelope-domination gives
\(b\in\operatorname{conv}g_m(\mathcal C_{s_b}^-)\). Hence a finite convex
combination of points of \(\mathcal C_{s_b}^-\) defines a representing measure
\(\nu_b^-\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\). By (5), every point in its support
satisfies
\[
 (p-s_b)q_{11}=0,
 \qquad
 (p-s_b)q_{00}=0.
\]
Integration against \(\nu_b^-\) yields
\(M_{p q_{11}}(b)=s_bM_{q_{11}}(b)\) and
\(M_{p q_{00}}(b)=s_bM_{q_{00}}(b)\), whence
\[
 L_m(b)=\max_s A(s;b)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_-(b)=0.             \tag{7}
\]
The upper equality criterion, the second containment in (5), and the same
argument give
\[
 U_m(b)=\min_s H(s;b)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_+(b)=0.             \tag{8}
\]

Neither obstruction polynomial is identically zero, and in fact they are
simultaneously nonzero at one feasible moment vector. Set
\[
 q^{(0)}=(1-\epsilon,0,\epsilon,0),
 \qquad
 q^{(1)}=(0,\epsilon,0,1-\epsilon),
 \qquad
 \nu^\dagger=\tfrac12\delta_{q^{(0)}}+\tfrac12\delta_{q^{(1)}},
\]
and \(b^\dagger=\int g_m\,d\nu^\dagger\). Both support points belong to
\(K_{\epsilon}\), with treatment probabilities \(\epsilon\) and
\(1-\epsilon\), so \(b^\dagger\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\). Direct evaluation
of (6) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_-(b^\dagger)
 &=\frac14\left\{(1-\epsilon)^3-\epsilon(1-\epsilon)^2\right\}
   =\frac14(1-\epsilon)^2(1-2\epsilon)>0,\\
 D_+(b^\dagger)
 &=\frac14\left\{\epsilon^3-\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)\right\}
   =\frac14\epsilon^2(2\epsilon-1)<0.                                \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Let \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) and set
\[
 \mathcal R=
 \operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})
 \cap\{b:D_-(b)D_+(b)\neq0\}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
This set is relatively open. It is nonempty as well. To see this directly,
choose any \(b^\circ\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).
Such a point exists: take a maximal affinely independent finite subset of the
moment body; its simplex has the same affine hull as the moment body, and the
barycenter of that simplex has a relative open ball contained in the simplex.
For
\(0<t\leq1\), relative convexity gives
\[
 b_t=(1-t)b^\dagger+t b^\circ
 \in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}).
\]
Indeed, if the relative ball of radius \(\rho>0\) about \(b^\circ\) is
contained in the moment body, convexity puts the relative ball of radius
\(t\rho\) about \(b_t\) in the moment body.
The polynomial \(D_-D_+\) is continuous and, by (9), is nonzero at
\(b^\dagger\). Since \(b_t\to b^\dagger\) as \(t\downarrow0\), it remains
nonzero for all sufficiently small positive \(t\). Such a \(b_t\) belongs to
\(\mathcal R\), proving nonemptiness.

Finally, for \(b\in\mathcal R\), implication (7) rules out equality in the
left side of (3), while implication (8) rules out equality in its right side.
The weak inequalities in (3) therefore become simultaneously
\[
 L_m(b)>\max_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}\ell_{m,p_*}^{\top}b,
 \qquad
 U_m(b)<\min_{p_*\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}u_{m,p_*}^{\top}b.
\]
This proves the claimed common strict-improvement region and completes the
corollary.
