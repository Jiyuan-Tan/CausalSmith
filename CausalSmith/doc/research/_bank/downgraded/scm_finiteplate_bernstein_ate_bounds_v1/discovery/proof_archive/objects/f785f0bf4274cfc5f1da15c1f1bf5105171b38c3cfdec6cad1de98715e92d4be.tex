First suppose that \(h\in V_m\). By the definition of \(V_m\), there are real coefficients \((\gamma_c)_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\) such that
\[
h(q)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c B_c^{(m)}(q)
\qquad(q\in K_{\epsilon}).
\]
Fix a feasible \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) and a measure \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\). The defining moment equalities for \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), together with linearity of integration over the finite sum, give
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}h\,d\nu
=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c
  \int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu
=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c b_c.
\]
The last expression depends only on \(b\). Hence \(\int h\,d\nu\) is identical for all \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), for every feasible \(b\).

Conversely, suppose that \(h\notin V_m\). We first construct a point in \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). The set \(K_{\epsilon}\) is nonempty: the vector
\[
q^{\circ}=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)
\]
belongs to the simplex and satisfies \(p(q^{\circ})=1/2\), so \(q^{\circ}\in K_{\epsilon}\) because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\). Let \(d\) be the dimension of \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). By \(\mathrm{def{:}bernstein\text{-}moment\text{-}body}\),
\[
\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}
=\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q):q\in K_{\epsilon}\}.
\]
The definition of affine dimension therefore permits a choice of \(q_0,\ldots,q_d\in K_{\epsilon}\) for which \(g_m(q_0),\ldots,g_m(q_d)\) are affinely independent and have affine hull \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). Set
\[
b_0=\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{k=0}^{d}g_m(q_k).
\]
Then \(b_0\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\). Relative to the affine basis \(g_m(q_0),\ldots,g_m(q_d)\), every barycentric coordinate of \(b_0\) is \(1/(d+1)>0\). By continuity of these affine coordinate maps, a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(b_0\) in \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\) has all barycentric coordinates positive and is therefore contained in
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{g_m(q_0),\ldots,g_m(q_d)\}
\subseteq\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}.
\]
Thus \(b_0\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).

By \(\mathrm{lem{:}full\text{-}support\text{-}perturbation}\), there is a measure \(\nu_0\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\) with support equal to \(K_{\epsilon}\). Regard \(V_m\) as a subspace of \(L^2(\nu_0)\). It is finite dimensional and hence closed. Moreover, the embedding of \(V_m\) into \(L^2(\nu_0)\) is injective: if \(v\in V_m\) vanishes \(\nu_0\)-almost surely, then continuity of \(v\) and full support of \(\nu_0\) force \(v=0\) at every point of \(K_{\epsilon}\). Indeed, if \(v(q_*)\ne0\), continuity gives a relatively open neighborhood of \(q_*\) on which \(|v|\) is bounded below by a positive constant, while full support gives that neighborhood positive \(\nu_0\)-measure, a contradiction.

Let \(P_0h\in V_m\) be the \(L^2(\nu_0)\)-orthogonal projection of \(h\) onto \(V_m\), and define
\[
r=h-P_0h.
\]
The function \(P_0h\) belongs to \(V_m\subset C(K_{\epsilon})\); hence \(r\) is continuous and bounded on the compact set \(K_{\epsilon}\). Orthogonality and the definition of \(V_m\) imply, for every \(c\in\mathcal I_m\),
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}r\,d\nu_0=0.
\]
The multinomial identity gives, pointwise on \(K_{\epsilon}\),
\[
\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}B_c^{(m)}(q)
=\left(\sum_{a,y}q_{ay}\right)^m
=1.
\]
Thus the constant function \(1\) lies in \(V_m\), and consequently
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}r\,d\nu_0=0.
\]
Also,
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}hr\,d\nu_0
=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}(P_0h+r)r\,d\nu_0
=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}r^2\,d\nu_0.
\]
This last quantity is strictly positive. Otherwise \(r=0\) \(\nu_0\)-almost surely, and the continuity and full-support argument would give \(h=P_0h\) everywhere on \(K_{\epsilon}\), contrary to \(h\notin V_m\).

In particular, \(\lVert r\rVert_{\infty}>0\). Choose
\[
0<s<\lVert r\rVert_{\infty}^{-1}
\]
and define two Borel measures by
\[
d\nu_0^{\pm}=(1\pm sr)\,d\nu_0.
\]
The density \(1\pm sr\) is strictly positive on \(K_{\epsilon}\), and the preceding zero-mean identity yields
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}(1\pm sr)\,d\nu_0=1.
\]
Thus \(\nu_0^+\) and \(\nu_0^-\) are probability measures. For each \(c\in\mathcal I_m\), orthogonality gives
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_0^{\pm}
=\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu_0
 \pm s\int_{K_{\epsilon}}B_c^{(m)}r\,d\nu_0
=b_{0,c}.
\]
Therefore \(\nu_0^+,\nu_0^-\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\). Their \(h\)-functionals differ because
\[
\int_{K_{\epsilon}}h\,d\nu_0^+
-\int_{K_{\epsilon}}h\,d\nu_0^-
=2s\int_{K_{\epsilon}}hr\,d\nu_0
=2s\int_{K_{\epsilon}}r^2\,d\nu_0
>0.
\]
Hence the functional cannot be identical throughout every feasible fiber when \(h\notin V_m\). This proves the equivalence.

Now take \(h=f\). By \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}plate\text{-}nonpolynomial}\), \(f\notin V_m\) for every finite \(m\ge1\), so the equivalence proves failure of universal point identification. Finally fix any \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\). The final assertion of \(\mathrm{lem{:}full\text{-}support\text{-}perturbation}\), applied using \(f\notin V_m\), supplies \(\nu_b^+,\nu_b^-\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) with unequal values of \(\int f\,d\nu\). The definitions in \(\mathrm{def{:}sharp\text{-}ate\text{-}fiber}\) then imply
\[
L_m(b)
\le
\min\left\{\int f\,d\nu_b^-,\int f\,d\nu_b^+\right\}
<
\max\left\{\int f\,d\nu_b^-,\int f\,d\nu_b^+\right\}
\le
U_m(b).
\]
Therefore \(L_m(b)<U_m(b)\) at every relative-interior feasible count law.
