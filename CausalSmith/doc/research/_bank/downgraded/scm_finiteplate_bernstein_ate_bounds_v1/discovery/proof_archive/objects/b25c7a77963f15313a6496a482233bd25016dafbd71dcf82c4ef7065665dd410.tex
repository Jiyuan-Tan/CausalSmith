Nobre, Schmidt, Moodie, and Stephens (2023, Sections 5--6 and Figure 3) motivate the problem by documenting ATE sensitivity to city random effects in the S\~ao Paulo DOT study. Wood (1999, Introduction and Sections 2--4, especially Example 2 on pp. 1717--1718) develops fixed-binomial convex geometry and exhibits a polygonal nonidentifiable slice but does not study a four-cell causal functional. Wood (1992, p. 1167) connects binomial-mixture membership to infinite extendibility of a finite exchangeable Bernoulli law; this is background for the repeated-observation interpretation, not a causal-functional comparator. Lee and Weidner (2026, display (14), Section 4.2, and Proposition 2) give a higher-order analytic family and establish validity of its population envelopes; their discussion also permits intersections across reference choices. The present corollary transplants the homogeneous, constant-coefficient binary-outcome scalar-reference family directly to \(K_{\epsilon}\), intersects it using the observable pooled count law \(b\), and proves that the sharp fiber improves both optimized endpoints simultaneously on one nonempty relatively open interior region. This is not an inclusion transfer: Lee and Weidner's full procedure observes covariates and permits covariate-dependent reference propensities and coefficients, whereas the present model has latent cluster laws and observes only counts. Neither full model class contains the other. Their local optimality within the display-(14) form therefore does not imply optimality in \(\mathcal E_m\). Bertsimas and Popescu (2005, Theorem 2.2) and Halaseh, Magron, and Skomra (2026, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.9) inform the strong-duality and full-support arguments. Bayer and Teichmann (2006, Theorem 1) inform atomic compression, while the endpoint contact face yields the causal support cap. Sachs, Jonzon, Sj\"olander, and Gabriel (2023, Sections 2--4) derive tight symbolic bounds by linear programming and vertex enumeration for categorical causal DAGs after reducing latent influences to a finite response-function state space, with linear observed-probability constraints and a linear causal query. They do not treat an unrestricted Borel mixing law over a continuous four-cell simplex, Bernstein moment fibers with the rational ATE functional, the required finite-atom reduction, or CAD recovery of an exact-inversion confidence set. Duarte et al. (2023, Proposition 1 and Algorithm 1) compute numerical polynomial causal bounds for finite discrete SCMs; the present continuous-latent problem first requires endpoint compression and then exact quantifier elimination. England, Bradford, and Davenport (2020, Theorem 1 and Algorithm 1) supply the CAD machinery. Bugni (2010, Theorems 1--3) motivates whole-set coverage, while Basu, Brill, and Yekutieli (2026, Section 4) motivate exact finite-sample inversion; neither source proves that the inverted hierarchical ATE set is semialgebraic or CAD-recoverable.
