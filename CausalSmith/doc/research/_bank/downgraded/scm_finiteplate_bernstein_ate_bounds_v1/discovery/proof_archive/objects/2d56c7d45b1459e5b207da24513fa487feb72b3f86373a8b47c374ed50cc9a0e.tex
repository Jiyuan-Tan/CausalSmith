First suppose that \(h\in V_m\).  By the definition of \(V_m\), there are real coefficients \((\gamma_c)_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\) such that \(h=\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_cB_c^{(m)}\) on \(K_{\epsilon}\).  Hence, for every feasible \(b\in\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) and every \(\nu\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\), the defining moment restrictions give
\[
 \int h\,d\nu
 =\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_c\int B_c^{(m)}\,d\nu
 =\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_m}\gamma_cb_c.
\]
Thus the value is identical over every feasible fiber.

Conversely, suppose that \(h\notin V_m\).  The set \(K_{\epsilon}\) is nonempty because \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and, for example, a four-cell law with treatment probability \(1/2\) belongs to it.  Therefore the nonempty finite-dimensional convex set \(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon}\) has nonempty relative interior.  Fix \(b_0\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  By the full-support perturbation lemma, there is \(\nu_0\in\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\) whose support is all of \(K_{\epsilon}\).

The space \(V_m\) is finite dimensional.  Its image in \(L^2(\nu_0)\) is therefore closed: if a member of \(V_m\) is zero \(\nu_0\)-almost surely, continuity and full support make it zero on all of \(K_{\epsilon}\).  Let \(P_0h\) be the \(L^2(\nu_0)\)-orthogonal projection of \(h\) onto \(V_m\), and put \(r=h-P_0h\).  Both \(h\) and \(P_0h\) are continuous on the compact set \(K_{\epsilon}\), so \(r\) is bounded and continuous.  Orthogonality gives
\[
 \int B_c^{(m)}r\,d\nu_0=0\quad(c\in\mathcal I_m).
\]
Because \(\sum_cB_c^{(m)}=1\), the constant function belongs to \(V_m\), and hence \(\int r\,d\nu_0=0\).  Moreover,
\[
 \int hr\,d\nu_0
 =\int (P_0h+r)r\,d\nu_0
 =\int r^2\,d\nu_0>0.
\]
Indeed, equality to zero would make \(r=0\) \(\nu_0\)-almost surely and then everywhere by continuity and full support, contradicting \(h\notin V_m\).

Choose \(s\) with \(0<s<\|r\|_{\infty}^{-1}\), and define probability measures by
\[
 d\nu_0^{\pm}=(1\pm sr)\,d\nu_0.
\]
The densities are strictly positive by the choice of \(s\), and their integrals are one because \(\int r\,d\nu_0=0\).  Their Bernstein moments coincide because each \(\int B_c^{(m)}r\,d\nu_0\) is zero, so both measures lie in \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b_0)\).  On the other hand,
\[
 \int h\,d\nu_0^+-\int h\,d\nu_0^-
 =2s\int hr\,d\nu_0
 =2s\int r^2\,d\nu_0>0.
\]
Thus constancy on every feasible fiber is impossible when \(h\notin V_m\), proving the equivalence.

For \(h=f\), the finite-plate nonpolynomial lemma gives \(f\notin V_m\) for every finite \(m\ge1\), so universal point identification fails.  Finally, fix any \(b\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathcal B_{m,\epsilon})\).  The full-support perturbation lemma supplies two members of \(\mathcal F_{m,\epsilon}(b)\) having distinct integrals of \(f\).  Since \(L_m(b)\) and \(U_m(b)\) are respectively the infimum and supremum of those integrals, \(L_m(b)<U_m(b)\), as claimed.
