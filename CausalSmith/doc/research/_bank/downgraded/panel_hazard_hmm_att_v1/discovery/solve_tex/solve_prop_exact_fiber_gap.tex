Work throughout at the fixed values \(K=3\), \(T=5\), \(g=0\), and \(r=1\) in \(\mathfrak W_{2/185}\).  Put
\[
 v=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 e=(1/5,1/5,4/5)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 B_1=\operatorname{diag}(e),\qquad B_0=I_3-B_1.
\]
Thus both emission probabilities are strictly positive in every state.  From the displayed \(P\) and \(h\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}negative\text{-}fiber}\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 K_0=(I_3-\operatorname{diag}(h))P
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 27/50&27/100&9/100\\
 7/50&7/20&21/100\\
 11/200&11/100&77/200
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The inverse of the specified mixing matrix and the transformed initial distribution and hazard are
\[
 S_{\star}^{-1}
 =\begin{pmatrix}9/8&-1/8&0\\-1/8&9/8&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \alpha'=\alpha S_{\star}=(31/100,39/100,3/10),
 \qquad
 h'=S_{\star}^{-1}h=(3/40,13/40,9/20)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 K_0'=S_{\star}^{-1}K_0S_{\star}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 557/1000&293/1000&3/40\\
 117/1000&333/1000&9/40\\
 121/2000&209/2000&77/200
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and de-killing according to the construction yields
\[
 P'=(I_3-\operatorname{diag}(h'))^{-1}K_0'
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 557/925&293/925&3/37\\
 13/75&37/75&1/3\\
 11/100&19/100&7/10
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every displayed entry of \(\alpha,\alpha',P,P'\), both emission probability vectors, and \(h,h'\) is positive.  The entries of each initial row sum to one.  The rows of \(P'\) sum respectively to
\[
 \frac{557+293+75}{925}=1,\qquad
 \frac{13+37+25}{75}=1,\qquad
 \frac{11+19+70}{100}=1,
\]
and the row-stochasticity of \(P\) follows in the same way from its displayed entries.  Moreover every coordinate of both hazards belongs to \([1/20,19/20]\).  This proves strict positivity, stochasticity, and hazard overlap for both realizations.  The killed dynamics are invertible because
\[
 \det(K_0)=\frac{4851}{100000}>0,
 \qquad
 \det(K_0')=\det(S_{\star}^{-1}K_0S_{\star})
 =\det(K_0)>0.
\]

We next prove equality of the observed stopped laws rather than presupposing it.  Since the first two states have the same emission distribution, \(B_aS_{\star}=S_{\star}B_a\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\); also \(S_{\star}v=v\).  Hence, for the killed observable operators of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}stopped\text{-}series}\),
\[
 M_a'=B_aK_0'
 =B_aS_{\star}^{-1}K_0S_{\star}
 =S_{\star}^{-1}M_aS_{\star}.
\]
For every word \(w=(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)\), including the empty word with \(M_\epsilon=I_3\), telescoping therefore gives
\[
 M_w'=S_{\star}^{-1}M_wS_{\star}.
\]
Using \(\alpha'=\alpha S_{\star}\), \(h'=S_{\star}^{-1}h\), and \(S_{\star}v=v\), the two terminal series agree term by term:
\[
 F_R'(w)=\alpha'M_w'v
 =\alpha M_wv=F_R(w),
 \qquad
 F_A'(w)=\alpha'M_w'h'
 =\alpha M_wh=F_A(w).
\]
This equality holds for all word lengths and hence, in particular, for every stopped-word observable available on dates \(0,\ldots,5\).  Each atom of a stopped panel is specified by its pre-adoption word together with an adoption or risk terminal; after adoption, all outcome coordinates are the same fixed constant in the two constructions.  Thus the preceding equalities imply equality of the complete observed laws \(P_W'=P_W\), not merely equality of selected moments.

For completeness, consider the prefix tuple \((\epsilon,0,1)\) and terminal-suffix tuple \(((\epsilon,R),(\epsilon,A),(0,R))\).  Substitution into Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}stopped\text{-}series}\) gives the stopped-Hankel minor
\[
 H_\star=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&57/200&473/1000\\
 473/1000&24629/200000&242957/1000000\\
 121/500&7843/100000&46519/500000
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det(H_\star)=\frac{42471}{625000000}\ne0.
\]
A three-state linear realization has predictive Hankel rank at most three, because every Hankel entry factors through a row and a column in \(\mathbb R^3\).  The nonzero \(3\)-by-\(3\) minor gives rank at least three.  Hence the predictive rank is exactly \(K=3\).  Since all stopped series coordinates coincide, this same minor certifies full predictive rank for the primed realization.

It remains to calculate the causal counterfactuals and to keep them distinct from the common observed law.  The time-zero adoption mass is the empty-word adoption terminal, so
\[
 p_0=F_A(\epsilon)=\alpha h=\frac{57}{200},
 \qquad
 p_0'=\alpha'h'=\alpha S_{\star}S_{\star}^{-1}h
 =\frac{57}{200}>0.
\]
In either constructed realization, conditional on \(S_0=s\), adoption at zero has probability \(h_s\), and the never-adopt untreated chain subsequently uses transition matrix \(P\); the primed realization has the analogous property with \(h'\) and \(P'\).  Thus Bayes' rule and iterated expectation give, for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 \Pr(S_0=s\mid G=0)=\frac{\alpha_sh_s}{p_0},
 \qquad
 \mu^0_{0,j}=\frac{\alpha\operatorname{diag}(h)P^je}{p_0},
\]
and
\[
 \Pr'(S_0=s\mid G=0)=\frac{\alpha_s'h_s'}{p_0'},
 \qquad
 (\mu^0_{0,j})'=\frac{\alpha'\operatorname{diag}(h')(P')^je}{p_0'}.
\]
These are also exactly the latent-coordinate evaluations of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}counterfactual\text{-}functional}\), where \(L=\operatorname{diag}(h)\), \(P_{\mathrm{dk}}=P\), and \(E v=e\), and similarly for the primed realization.  The division is valid by the displayed strict positivity of \(p_0\).

The required products are
\[
 \alpha\operatorname{diag}(h)
 =(3/100,3/25,27/200),
 \qquad
 \alpha'\operatorname{diag}(h')
 =(93/4000,507/4000,27/200),
\]
\[
 Pe=(13/50,19/50,31/50)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 P'e=(46/185,2/5,31/50)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
It follows first that
\[
 \alpha\operatorname{diag}(h)e
 =\alpha'\operatorname{diag}(h')e
 =\frac{69}{500},
 \qquad
 \mu^0_{0,0}=(\mu^0_{0,0})'
 =\frac{69/500}{57/200}=\frac{46}{95}.
\]
For the next-period untreated outcome,
\[
 \alpha\operatorname{diag}(h)Pe=\frac{1371}{10000},
 \qquad
 \alpha'\operatorname{diag}(h')P'e=\frac{51867}{370000},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \mu^0_{0,1}=\frac{1371/10000}{57/200}=\frac{457}{950},
 \qquad
 (\mu^0_{0,1})'=\frac{51867/370000}{57/200}
 =\frac{17289}{35150}.
\]
Their exact separation is
\[
 (\mu^0_{0,1})'-\mu^0_{0,1}
 =\frac{17289}{35150}-\frac{457}{950}
 =\frac{2}{185}>0.
\]
Finally, if the common post-adoption outcome constant is \(c\), then the common observed treated mean is \(\mu^1_{0,1}=c\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}att\text{-}functional}\) consequently yields
\[
 (\tau_{0,1})'-\tau_{0,1}
 =\{c-(\mu^0_{0,1})'\}-\{c-\mu^0_{0,1}\}
 =-\frac{2}{185}.
\]
Here \(g+r=1\le T=5\), so no boundary convention is invoked, and every denominator used above is positive.  No limiting argument is used.  Thus an identical observed-panel law has two distinct causal ATT values, with the asserted exact gap.
