Write \(S=S_{\star}\), and choose \(\varepsilon>0\) below small enough for every continuity argument that follows. We first verify legality rather than assuming it. Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \alpha^{(1)}=\alpha_{\star}S
 =\left(\frac{31}{100},\frac{39}{100},\frac3{10}\right),
 \qquad
 h^{(1)}(\eta)=S^{-1}h^{(0)}(\eta)
 =\left(\frac3{20}-\frac54\eta,\frac3{20}+\frac54\eta,\frac9{20}\right)^{\!\top}. \tag{1}
\]
At \(\eta=0\), de-killing the second killed transition gives
\[
 P^{(1)}(0)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 49/80&5/16&3/40\\
 3/16&39/80&13/40\\
 11/100&19/100&7/10
 \end{pmatrix}.                                                \tag{2}
\]
All entries of both rows in (1), all entries in (2), and all entries of \(\alpha_{\star}\) and \(P_{\star}\) are strictly positive. The entries of \(P^{(1)}(\eta)\) are continuous wherever the components of \(1-h^{(1)}(\eta)\) are positive. Thus, after reducing \(\varepsilon\), both initial rows, both transitions, and all hazards and survival probabilities are strictly positive for \(0\le\eta<\varepsilon\). Their finitely many hazard and survival coordinates consequently have a common positive lower bound on the closed rectangle, which supplies the hazard-overlap constant required by the model class. Stochasticity of the transformed transition is an identity, not an additional premise. Since \(S\mathbf 1_3=\mathbf 1_3\),
\[
 K_0^{(1)}(\eta)\mathbf 1_3
 =S^{-1}K_0^{(0)}(\eta)S\mathbf 1_3
 =S^{-1}\{\mathbf 1_3-h^{(0)}(\eta)\}
 =\mathbf 1_3-h^{(1)}(\eta),                                  \tag{3}
\]
and hence \(P^{(1)}(\eta)\mathbf 1_3=\mathbf 1_3\). For \(0<\zeta<\varepsilon\), the three probabilities \(1/5\), \(1/5+\zeta\), and \(4/5\) are distinct and strictly between zero and one; the complementary zero-emission probabilities have the same properties. Therefore all three emission profiles are distinct, so the within-emission-profile hazard condition defining \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{3,5}\) holds vacuously.

The remaining model-class conditions also hold. The displayed construction is a time-homogeneous hidden Markov law. Sampling adoption at each risk date from the current state before drawing the current and future untreated innovations enforces the current-state hazard and future-innovation-independence conditions; defining the common post-adoption outcome path and setting the observed path equal to its corresponding potential path enforces consistency and no anticipation. The horizon equality is
\[
 T=5=2\cdot3-1=2K-1.                                         \tag{4}
\]
For killed invertibility, direct evaluation gives \(\det(P_{\star})=7/50\), whence
\[
 \det K_0^{(0)}(\eta)
 =\prod_{s=1}^3\{1-h_s^{(0)}(\eta)\}\det(P_{\star})\ne0,
 \qquad
 \det K_0^{(1)}(\eta)=\det K_0^{(0)}(\eta)\ne0.              \tag{5}
\]
Every cohort mass through date five is positive because, for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 p_g^{(j)}=\alpha^{(j)}\{K_0^{(j)}(\eta)\}^{g}h^{(j)}(\eta)>0,
 \qquad 0\le g\le5,                                          \tag{6}
\]
each factor in this scalar product having strictly positive entries. In particular,
\[
 p_0^{(0)}=\alpha_{\star}h^{(0)}(\eta)=\frac{5\eta+12}{50}>0. \tag{7}
\]

It remains to check predictive rank. At \((\eta,\zeta)=(0,0)\), direct multiplication of the stopped-series probabilities for the predeclared chart gives
\[
 H=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&6/25&2847/25000\\
 509/1000&2847/25000&1506459/25000000\\
 251/1000&3651/50000&601221/25000000
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det H=\frac{257499}{31250000000}\ne0.                      \tag{8}
\]
The two laws have the same stopped series at this boundary, as will also follow from (10) below. Each entry of their respective chart matrices is continuous in \((\eta,\zeta)\). Therefore (8) makes both declared minors nonsingular on a sufficiently small rectangle. A nonsingular three-by-three stopped-Hankel minor gives full-array rank at least three, while a three-state realization gives rank at most three; hence the predictive rank is exactly \(K=3\). Equations (1)--(8), together with the stated sequential construction, prove that both positive-parameter laws belong to \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{3,5}\).

We next establish observational collision at the boundary \(\zeta=0\). There the first two diagonal entries of every \(B_a^{\star}(0)\) agree, so
\[
 SB_a^{\star}(0)=B_a^{\star}(0)S,
 \qquad
 M_a^{(1)}(\eta,0)=S^{-1}M_a^{(0)}(\eta,0)S,                 \tag{9}
\]
where \(M_a^{(j)}=B_a^{\star}K_0^{(j)}\). Together with \(\alpha^{(1)}=\alpha_{\star}S\), \(h^{(1)}=S^{-1}h^{(0)}\), and \(S\mathbf 1_3=\mathbf 1_3\), multiplication of (9) over any word \(w\) yields
\[
 \alpha^{(1)}M_w^{(1)}\mathbf 1_3
 =\alpha_{\star}M_w^{(0)}\mathbf 1_3,
 \qquad
 \alpha^{(1)}M_w^{(1)}h^{(1)}
 =\alpha_{\star}M_w^{(0)}h^{(0)}.                            \tag{10}
\]
By the definition of the two stopped terminal series, (10) is equality of every risk-terminal and adoption-terminal stopped-word probability. The treated-outcome law is common by construction, so
\[
 P_0^{\eta,0}=P_1^{\eta,0}                                  \tag{11}
\]
for every sufficiently small \(\eta\), although this boundary law need not itself satisfy the hazard-in-emission-algebra restriction when \(\eta>0\).

The support \(\mathcal X\) of a six-date stopped panel with the common deterministic treated path is finite. On the feasible common support, the positive parameter bounds above give a constant \(b>0\) such that the cell probabilities \(p_{\eta,\zeta}(x)\) and \(q_{\eta,\zeta}(x)\) under the two laws are at least \(b\), uniformly on a sufficiently small closed rectangle. Every cell probability is a finite sum of finite products of parameter entries, hence is analytic there. Equation (11), followed by the mean-value theorem in \(\zeta\), therefore gives a finite constant \(C_1\) such that
\[
 \max_{x\in\mathcal X}|p_{\eta,\zeta}(x)-q_{\eta,\zeta}(x)|
 \le C_1\zeta.                                               \tag{12}
\]
Using \(\log u\le u-1\), normalization of both cell vectors, (12), and the lower bound \(b\), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_0^{\eta,\zeta},P_1^{\eta,\zeta})
 &\le \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{\eta,\zeta}(x)
       \left\{\frac{p_{\eta,\zeta}(x)}{q_{\eta,\zeta}(x)}-1\right\}\\
 &=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
   \frac{\{p_{\eta,\zeta}(x)-q_{\eta,\zeta}(x)\}^2}
        {q_{\eta,\zeta}(x)}
 \le \frac{|\mathcal X|C_1^2}{b}\zeta^2.                   \tag{13}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the uniform one-panel divergence bound.

We now calculate the omitted dictionary singular value. For the declared chart, the latent suffix matrix is
\[
 C(\eta,\zeta)
 =\begin{bmatrix}
 \mathbf 1_3&h^{(0)}(\eta)&B_0^{\star}(\zeta)K_0^{(0)}(\eta)h^{(0)}(\eta)
 \end{bmatrix}.                                               \tag{14}
\]
The canonical-realization factorization and the dictionary \((I_3,\bar B_1,\bar B_1^2)\) give
\[
 V_D(\eta,\zeta)
 =C(\eta,\zeta)^{-1}
 \begin{bmatrix}
 \mathbf 1_3&B_1^{\star}(\zeta)\mathbf 1_3&
 \{B_1^{\star}(\zeta)\}^2\mathbf 1_3
 \end{bmatrix}.                                               \tag{15}
\]
The inverse exists near the origin by (8), because the Hankel factorization \(H=RC\) with square factors makes \(C(0,0)\) invertible. Exact rational simplification of (15) at \(\eta=0\) gives
\[
 V_D(0,0)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-1/10&-13/50\\0&2&2\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det V_D(0,\zeta)
 =-\frac{500\zeta(5\zeta-3)}{51(5\zeta-1)}.                 \tag{16}
\]
At \(\zeta=0\), the first two entries of each vector in the right matrix of (15) agree, and that matrix has rank exactly two. Thus \(\det V_D(\eta,0)=0\) for every small \(\eta\). Since the determinant is continuously differentiable near the origin, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \det V_D(\eta,\zeta)=\zeta d(\eta,\zeta),
 \qquad
 d(\eta,\zeta)=\int_0^1
 \partial_{\zeta}\det V_D(\eta,t\zeta)\,dt.                \tag{17}
\]
The second formula in (16) implies \(d(0,0)=-500/17\). Hence continuity makes \(|d(\eta,\zeta)|\) bounded above and bounded away from zero on a smaller rectangle.

For a rank-two three-by-three matrix, expanding the coefficient of degree one in the characteristic polynomial of its Gram matrix shows directly that the product of the two nonzero squared singular values equals the sum of the squares of all two-by-two minors. Applying this elementary identity to the first matrix in (16) gives
\[
 \sigma_1\{V_D(0,0)\}\sigma_2\{V_D(0,0)\}
 =\frac{2\sqrt{1266}}{25}.                                   \tag{18}
\]
Singular values are continuous in the matrix entries. Combining (17)--(18) with
\[
 \sigma_1(V_D)\sigma_2(V_D)\sigma_3(V_D)=|\det V_D|          \tag{19}
\]
therefore yields, uniformly up to fixed multiplicative constants,
\[
 \sigma_3\{V_D(\eta,\zeta)\}=\Theta(\zeta),
 \qquad
 \frac{\sigma_3\{V_D(\eta,\zeta)\}}{\zeta}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{500/17}{2\sqrt{1266}/25}
 =\frac{6250}{17\sqrt{1266}}                                 \tag{20}
\]
as \((\eta,\zeta)\to(0,0)\) through positive values.

We next derive the causal separation. Because \(g=0\), \(r=1\), and \(\nu(1)=1\), \(\nu(0)=0\), the untreated cohort means in the two legal latent coordinates are
\[
 (\mu^0_{0,1})^{(j)}
 =\frac{\alpha^{(j)}\operatorname{diag}\{h^{(j)}(\eta)\}
 P^{(j)}(\eta)B_1^{\star}(\zeta)\mathbf 1_3}
 {\alpha^{(j)}h^{(j)}(\eta)},
 \qquad j=0,1.                                                \tag{21}
\]
The denominators are positive by (6). Substituting the rational matrices from the construction into (21), putting the two fractions over their common denominator, and collecting monomials gives
\[
 \Delta(\eta,\zeta)
 =-\frac{3\{87500\eta^3\zeta+18000\eta^2\zeta+9000\eta^2+22015\eta\zeta+53550\eta-3468\zeta\}}
 {400(5\eta+12)(25\eta-17)(25\eta+17)}.                    \tag{22}
\]
The denominator is nonzero near the origin. Taylor expansion of this rational function at the origin, with \(\eta\zeta=O(\zeta)\) on a bounded rectangle, gives
\[
 \Delta(\eta,\zeta)=\frac{63}{544}\eta+O(\eta^2+\zeta).   \tag{23}
\]
At the collision boundary and any sufficiently small fixed \(\eta_0>0\), the exact value
\[
 \Delta(\eta_0,0)
 =-\frac{3(9000\eta_0^2+53550\eta_0)}
 {400(5\eta_0+12)(625\eta_0^2-289)}>0                       \tag{24}
\]
is nonzero. Since the treated outcome is the same deterministic constant under the two laws, the difference of their ATT values is \(-\Delta(\eta,\zeta)\); in particular, its absolute magnitude is \(|\Delta(\eta,\zeta)|\).

Let now \(a_N,b_N>0\) satisfy \(Nb_N^2\to0\) and \(b_N=o(a_N)\). Define the one-panel chi-square quantity in the direction used in (13) by
\[
 \chi_N^2=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
 \frac{\{p_{a_N,b_N}(x)-q_{a_N,b_N}(x)\}^2}
 {q_{a_N,b_N}(x)}=O(b_N^2).                                  \tag{25}
\]
Factoring the finite product sum proves directly that
\[
 1+\chi^2\!\left((P_0^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N},
                  (P_1^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}\right)
 =(1+\chi_N^2)^N.                                             \tag{26}
\]
The right side minus one is at most \(\exp(N\chi_N^2)-1=o(1)\). Cauchy--Schwarz then gives
\[
 \left\|(P_0^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}
       -(P_1^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2\!\left((P_0^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N},
                  (P_1^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}\right)}
 \longrightarrow0.                                           \tag{27}
\]
If \(a_N\to0\), (23) and \(b_N=o(a_N)\) give
\[
 |\tau_{0,1}(P_1^{a_N,b_N})-
   \tau_{0,1}(P_0^{a_N,b_N})|
 =\left(\frac{63}{544}+o(1)\right)a_N.                      \tag{28}
\]
If \(a_N\equiv\eta_0>0\), continuity of (22) and (24) instead gives convergence of the absolute ATT gap to \(\Delta(\eta_0,0)>0\). Moreover, (20) and \(Nb_N^2\to0\) imply
\[
 \frac{\sigma_3(V_D)}{\rho_N}
 =O\!\left(\frac{b_N\sqrt N}{\sqrt{\log N}}\right)
 =o(1).                                                       \tag{29}
\]
For every \(N\), positivity of \(b_N\) and (17)--(20) gives \(\sigma_3(V_D)>0\). Thus eventually \(0<\sigma_3(V_D)\le\rho_N\), which is precisely the sampling-noise component of the declared gray zone.

We finally prove the two statistical impossibilities using the fixed-\(\eta_0\) sequence, for example with \(b_N=N^{-1}\). Let \(\vartheta_{j,N}=\tau_{0,1}(P_j^{\eta_0,b_N})\) and let \(\delta=\Delta(\eta_0,0)>0\). By (24), \(|\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|\to\delta\), while (27) gives merging product laws. If an estimator \(\widehat\vartheta_N\) were uniformly consistent over any class containing these laws, then for all large \(N\) the events
\[
 A_{j,N}=\{|\widehat\vartheta_N-\vartheta_{j,N}|<\delta/3\},
 \qquad j=0,1,                                                 \tag{30}
\]
would have probability tending to one under their respective product laws. Total-variation merging transfers the probability of \(A_{1,N}\) to the zeroth product law. But \(A_{0,N}\cap A_{1,N}=\varnothing\) for large \(N\), contradicting that both then have probability tending to one under the zeroth law. Uniform consistency is therefore impossible.

Similarly, let \(C_N\) be a random confidence set with coverage at least \(1-\alpha_0-o(1)\) under both product laws, where \(\alpha_0<1/2\). Total-variation merging and the union bound imply under the zeroth product law that
\[
 \Pr\{\vartheta_{0,N}\in C_N,\ \vartheta_{1,N}\in C_N\}
 \ge1-2\alpha_0-o(1)>0.                                      \tag{31}
\]
On the event in (31), \(\operatorname{diam}(C_N)\ge|\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|\to\delta\). Hence uniformly honest diameter \(o(1)\) in probability is impossible.

For the declared specialization \(\eta_N=N^{-1/4}\), \(\zeta_N=N^{-1}\), the sequence premises hold because \(N\zeta_N^2=N^{-1}\to0\) and \(\zeta_N/\eta_N=N^{-3/4}\to0\). Equations (20), (27)--(29) become
\[
 \sigma_3(V_D)=\left(\frac{6250}{17\sqrt{1266}}+o(1)\right)N^{-1},
 \quad
 \|(P_{W,N}^{(0)})^{\otimes N}-(P_{W,N}^{(1)})^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to0,
 \quad
 |\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|=\left(\frac{63}{544}+o(1)\right)N^{-1/4}. \tag{32}
\]
Repeating (31) with the last separation in (32) rules out uniformly honest diameter \(o(N^{-1/4})\) over that pair. This proves every assertion.

\]
so the path eventually lies in \(0<\sigma_3(V_D)\le\rho_N\), the sampling-noise component of \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{gray},N}\).

Finally, let \(C_N\) be any confidence band with coverage at least \(1-\alpha_0-o(1)\) under both product laws, and denote their two ATT values by \(\vartheta_{0,N}\) and \(\vartheta_{1,N}\). Total-variation merging transfers the second law's coverage event to the first law, and the union bound gives
\[
 \Pr_{(P_{W,N}^{(0)})^{\otimes N}}\{\vartheta_{0,N}\in C_N,\ \vartheta_{1,N}\in C_N\}
 \ge1-2\alpha_0-o(1)>0.                                      \tag{39}
\]
On this event, (37) forces
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(C_N)\ge|\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|
 =\left(\frac{63}{544}+o(1)\right)N^{-1/4}.                 \tag{40}
\]
Thus diameter \(o(N^{-1/4})\) in probability under both laws is incompatible with uniform \(1-\alpha_0\) coverage. Equations (33)--(40) prove every assertion of the target.
