\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}hazard\_\allowbreak{}hmm\_\allowbreak{}att}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} State-dependent adoption usually destroys parallel-trends comparisons because future untreated outcomes are missing precisely for selected latent states. A finite stopped-panel law nevertheless identifies every adopter cohort's untreated path law when the adoption hazard is constant within each current-emission alias class. Predictive rank yields a chart-independent minimal realization, while displayed coordinates and inference use one ordered Hankel minor fixed before outcomes and a total guarded estimator with a preregistered fallback. A generic legal fiber proves failure when aliased states have unequal hazards and forecasts; the exact positive \(T=5\), \(g=0\), \(r=1\) witness has ATT gap \(2/185\), and the local collision pair limits this proposal's plug-in at \(N^{-1/4}\). The exceptional \(K=3\) one-alias-block frontier and the Kim--Lee empirical certificate remain first-class open questions.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Staggered-DiD and interactive-fixed-effects methods use observed histories or adoption-date cohort moments. Causal latent-Markov procedures impose their own model-based latent recovery and ignorability structures, while HMM identification results either exclude exact aliasing or characterize latent ambiguity rather than observed-stopped-law-determined cohort continuation. Ordinary spectral results also do not select a preregistered stopped-Hankel minor.

\paragraph{Niche.} The adoption event is an observable state-dependent mark. Its terminal vector can identify a similarity-invariant hazard operator even when positive non-permutation latent realizations fit the same stopped law; a fixed ordered minor is needed only to compute coordinates reproducibly, not to define the invariant.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves hazard-lift path-law invariance, derives the generic converse and exact \(2/185\) stress test, supplies a total guarded fixed-chart estimator and regular-subset simultaneous inference, limits its own plug-in on an explicit collision path, and isolates both a concrete one-alias-block frontier and an operational empirical certificate as open questions.

\section{Related work}

Weiss and Nadler characterize non-permutation equivalence for HMMs with two aliased states; their similarities inform the legal fiber but do not target counterfactual cohort continuation. Allman, Matias, and Rhodes establish generic finite-window identification, while Gassiat, Cleynen, and Robin and Alexandrovich, Holzmann, and Leister use separation conditions that exclude exact repeated emissions. Bartolucci and Farcomeni provide a killed-forward latent event-history model without the proposed causal invariant. Pennoni, Paas, and Bartolucci use propensity-weighted hidden-Markov likelihood, and Clouth, Bijlsma, Pauws, and Vermunt use a fitted latent Markov measurement model inside a parametric g-formula. The generic converse diagnoses transporting those workflows to stopped panels while also demanding that continuation be determined by the observed stopped law: unequal within-alias hazards and forecasts preclude uniqueness absent another restriction. Those papers impose their own structural assumptions and are not contradicted, and the hazard-lift theorem does not identify their latent-state estimands. Callaway and Sant'Anna identify group-time ATT and simultaneous inference under conditional parallel trends. Callaway and Tsyawo use interactive fixed effects and adoption-date moments; their functional has neither a state-dependent stopping mark nor alias-fiber invariance, while hazard lifting does not impose their factor structure. Kim and Lee use observed-history matching in the six-period Netflix--IPTV panel, and Ishimaru uses repeated measurements outside evolving hidden selection. The exact pair is a stress test for transported fitted-state workflows and observed-history matching, not a contradiction of either paper because their assumptions are not asserted on the pair. Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang provide ordinary-HMM observable operators, but full predictive rank only ensures that some minor is nonsingular and does not validate the preregistered minor used here. Tran, Kim, and Doshi-Velez provide a moment-estimation template but not the stopped-chart, de-killing, or frame conditions. Andrews and Cheng provide weak-identification precedent; the collision proposition instead limits this proposal's fixed-chart plug-in and does not contradict the separated regular domains of Tran, Kim, and Doshi-Velez or Callaway and Sant'Anna. These sources are informed-by comparators, not results claimed to be strictly tightened.

  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\{W,\allowbreak{}N\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{(0)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{first one-\allowbreak{}panel law on the local collision path}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the \(T=5\) stopped-panel law induced by \(\alpha_{\star},P_{\star},h_N^{(0)},B_{0,N}^{\star},B_{1,N}^{\star}\) and the common deterministic treated-outcome law
  \item \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}\{W,\allowbreak{}N\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{(1)\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{second one-\allowbreak{}panel law on the local collision path}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the \(T=5\) stopped-panel law induced by \(\alpha_N^{(1)},P_N^{(1)},h_N^{(1)},B_{0,N}^{\star},B_{1,N}^{\star}\) and the common deterministic treated-outcome law
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak C\_\allowbreak{}N} --- \texttt{ordered pair of legal local collision laws}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathfrak C_N=(P_{W,N}^{(0)},P_{W,N}^{(1)})\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat H} --- \texttt{empirical predeclared stopped-\allowbreak{}Hankel minor}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the predeclared minor \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\) evaluated at \(\widehat m\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}bar K\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}0} --- \texttt{empirical canonical killed transition}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{the canonical killed transition evaluated after the Hankel half-\allowbreak{}gap gate,\allowbreak{} and the zero matrix otherwise}
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat V\_\allowbreak{}D} --- \texttt{empirical full dictionary evaluation matrix}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat V_D=[C(\widehat{\bar B})\widehat{\bar v}:C\in\mathcal D_{K-1}]\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat p\_\allowbreak{}g} --- \texttt{empirical cohort mass}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat p_g=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf 1\{G_i=g\}\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Sigma} --- \texttt{total empirical influence covariance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the empirical covariance of the estimated panel-level influence vectors on the guarded inverse branch and \(I_{|\mathcal G|}\) on the fallback branch
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Pi\_\allowbreak{}F} --- \texttt{empirical frame projector}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Pi_F=\widehat V_F\widehat V_F^{+}\), where the Moore--Penrose inverse makes the projector a total Borel function
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat R\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{HDK\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{empirical hazard-\allowbreak{}in-\allowbreak{}emission-\allowbreak{}algebra residual}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat R_{\mathrm{HDK}}=\|(I_K-\widehat\Pi_F)\widehat{\bar h}\|_2\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{cert\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{fixed familywise diagnostic error level}; \((0,1)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{HDK\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{fixed substantively negligible HDK-\allowbreak{}residual threshold}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}omega\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}max\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{fixed maximum acceptable simultaneous ATT half-\allowbreak{}width}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat q\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{cert\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{joint diagnostic multiplier critical value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the conditional \(1-\alpha_{\mathrm{cert}}\) quantile of the maximum studentized panel-multiplier perturbation over all preregistered diagnostic and ATT coordinates on the guarded branch, and the fixed value \(1\) on the fallback branch
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}operatorname\{se\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{panel-\allowbreak{}multiplier standard-\allowbreak{}error map}; a scalar diagnostic or ATT coordinate \(a\mapsto\widehat{\operatorname{se}}(a)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the plug-in standard error on the guarded branch and the fixed value \(1\) for every coordinate on the fallback branch
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}operatorname\{LCB\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}N} --- \texttt{joint lower confidence endpoint}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\operatorname{LCB}_N(a)=\widehat a-\widehat q_{\mathrm{cert}}\widehat{\operatorname{se}}(a)/\sqrt N\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}operatorname\{UCB\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}N} --- \texttt{joint upper confidence endpoint}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\operatorname{UCB}_N(a)=\widehat a+\widehat q_{\mathrm{cert}}\widehat{\operatorname{se}}(a)/\sqrt N\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}omega\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}max\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{largest simultaneous ATT half-\allowbreak{}width}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\omega_{\max}=\max_{(g,r)\in\mathcal G}\widehat q_{\mathrm{cert}}\widehat{\operatorname{se}}((\widehat\theta)_{g,r})/\sqrt N\)
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal C\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{KL\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{Kim-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Lee joint empirical pass event}; \texttt{one preregistered stratum passes all simultaneous rank,\allowbreak{} HDK,\allowbreak{} conditioning,\allowbreak{} support,\allowbreak{} gray-\allowbreak{}zone,\allowbreak{} and precision gates}
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-panels]\label{ass:iid-panels}
\(W_1,\ldots,W_N\) are independent and identically distributed with law \(P_W\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent-panel sampling, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
For every \(t\), the observed outcome equals \(Y_t(G)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-anticipation]\label{ass:no-anticipation}
For every \(g\) and every \(t<g\), \(Y_t(g)=Y_t(\infty)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (No anticipation under staggered adoption, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:homogeneous-untreated-hmm]\label{ass:homogeneous-untreated-hmm}
The law of \(((S_t),(Y_t(\infty)))\) factors with initial row \(\alpha\), time-homogeneous transition \(P\), and time-homogeneous emissions \(\{B_a:a\in\mathcal Y\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite time-homogeneous hidden Markov law, \cite{HsuKakadeZhang2012SpectralHMM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:current-state-hazard]\label{ass:current-state-hazard}
Conditional on \(S_t\) and survival to \(t\), adoption at \(t\) is Bernoulli with mean \(h_{S_t}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition models adoption as a decision driven by the current latent behavioral regime, as when subscription propensity responds to a household's current engagement state.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:future-innovation-independence]\label{ass:future-innovation-independence}
Conditional on \(S_t\) and survival to \(t\), adoption at \(t\) is independent of \(Y_t(\infty)\) and all future untreated transition and emission innovations.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A fresh subscription decision may depend on the current engagement regime without foreknowledge of future viewing shocks. This structural timing condition is plausible when future content and idiosyncratic viewing innovations have not yet been realized.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:horizon]\label{ass:horizon}
\(T\ge 2K-1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The horizon ties observable history length to a known finite predictive order. For \(K=3\), a six-date panel such as the Kim--Lee application supplies exactly the required window.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hazard-overlap]\label{ass:hazard-overlap}
There is a fixed \(\varepsilon_h>0\) such that \(\varepsilon_h\le h_s\le1-\varepsilon_h\) for every hidden state \(s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform treatment-hazard overlap, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictive-rank]\label{ass:predictive-rank}
The full finite two-terminal stopped Hankel array with prefix and suffix words \(u,w\in\mathcal Y^{\le K-1}\), terminal labels \(b\in\{R,A\}\), and entries \(F_b(uw)\) has rank \(K\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This tailored stopped-law realization condition concerns the full paired risk-and-adoption Hankel array. Every entry is an observable stopped-word probability, and rank \(K\) guarantees that some \(K\times K\) minor is nonsingular. It does not guarantee that the particular tuple \(\mathcal U,\mathcal V\) fixed before observing outcomes selects such a minor; that separate requirement is directly auditable from its observable entries.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:declared-hankel-chart]\label{ass:declared-hankel-chart}
\(\det H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\ne0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This preregistered selected-minor condition makes the displayed coordinates reproducible: the ordered word tuples are fixed before outcomes are inspected, and the determinant is an observable polynomial in stopped-word probabilities. Full predictive rank ensures that some minor works but does not privilege this declared one.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:frontier-support]\label{ass:frontier-support}
The frontier support tuple is \((K,T,\mathcal Y)=(3,5,\{0,1\})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This fixed finite instance is the smallest six-date binary configuration that contains a two-state alias block and a distinct state while matching the named empirical horizon.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:frontier-alias-geometry]\label{ass:frontier-alias-geometry}
After one latent permutation, states \(1\) and \(2\) have identical emission profiles, while state \(3\) has a different emission profile.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition isolates the empirically relevant case of two behavioral substates with the same current binary response and one observably distinct regime; it is the exact non-generic geometry the frontier question classifies.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:frontier-positive-interior]\label{ass:frontier-positive-interior}
Every entry of \(\alpha\) and \(P\), every emission probability, and every component of \(h\) and \(1-h\) is strictly positive.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Strictly positive latent transitions, binary response probabilities, and adoption hazards describe panels without structural zeros and keep the frontier focused on alias geometry rather than boundary support failures.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:killed-invertible]\label{ass:killed-invertible}
\(\det(K_0)\ne0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Full-rank transition condition for observable operators, \cite{HsuKakadeZhang2012SpectralHMM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-cohorts]\label{ass:positive-cohorts}
\(p_g>0\) for every \((g,r)\in\mathcal G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive cohort support, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hazard-in-emission-algebra]\label{ass:hazard-in-emission-algebra}
For all hidden states \(s,s'\), if \(B_a(s,s)=B_a(s',s')\) for every \(a\in\mathcal Y\), then \(h_s=h_{s'}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The restriction says that observationally indistinguishable current regimes have the same adoption propensity. It holds when adoption responds to the current observable engagement profile rather than to distinctions that have no contemporaneous behavioral manifestation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-hankel-gap]\label{ass:fixed-hankel-gap}
\(\sigma_{\min}(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V})\ge\kappa_H\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This custom declared-chart condition requires the particular observable Hankel minor used by the estimator to remain stably invertible. Its empirical audit is the simultaneous lower confidence bound for \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\) against the preregistered threshold \(\kappa_H\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-killed-gap]\label{ass:fixed-killed-gap}
\(\sigma_{\min}(\bar K_0)\ge\kappa_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This custom de-killing condition keeps inversion of the canonical killed dynamics stable in the declared coordinates. Its empirical audit compares a simultaneous lower confidence bound for \(\sigma_{\min}(\bar K_0)\) with \(\kappa_0\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-frame-gap]\label{ass:fixed-frame-gap}
\(\sigma_{\min}(V_F)\ge\kappa_F\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This custom coefficient-normalized frame condition stabilizes the hazard-algebra Gram solve for the exact written-order dictionary. Its empirical audit compares a simultaneous lower confidence bound for \(\sigma_{\min}(V_F)\) with \(\kappa_F\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:frame-spans-algebra]\label{ass:frame-spans-algebra}
\(\operatorname{span}(V_F)=\operatorname{span}(V_D)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A predeclared low-degree frame records which current emission profiles are empirically distinguishable. Repeated emissions generate a stable lower-dimensional frame in finite-alphabet panels, including binary engagement panels with latent behavioral substates.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-cohort-gap]\label{ass:uniform-cohort-gap}
\(\min_{(g,r)\in\mathcal G}p_g\ge\kappa_p\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform cohort overlap, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:moment-envelope]\label{ass:moment-envelope}
\(\sup_{P_W\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}}\mathbb E_{P_W}\|Z(W)\|^{2+\delta}\le C_Z<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Lyapunov moment condition, \cite{VanDerVaart1998Asymptotic}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-influence-variance]\label{ass:positive-influence-variance}
\(\min_{j\le |\mathcal G|}\Sigma_{jj}\ge\kappa_V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nondegenerate studentization, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021DiD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:canonical-realization]\label{def:canonical-realization}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\mathcal U=(u_1,\ldots,u_K)\) with \(u_1=\epsilon\) and \(\mathcal V=((w_1,b_1),\ldots,(w_K,b_K))\), set \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}[i,j]=F_{b_j}(u_iw_j)\) and \(H_a[i,j]=F_{b_j}(u_i a w_j)\). On \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{K,T}(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)\), set \(\bar M_a=H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}^{-1}H_a\), \(\bar\alpha\) equal to the empty-prefix coordinate row, \(\bar v=H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}^{-1}f_R\), and \(\bar h=H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}^{-1}f_A\), where \(f_R,f_A\) are the corresponding risk and adoption terminal columns.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P_W\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{K,T}(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)\); \((g,r)\).

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{legal}}\) is the set of matrices \(J\) for which some invertible change of coordinates makes \(\bar v\) the all-ones vector, every \(\bar B_a\) a nonnegative emission diagonal summing to \(I_K\), \(J\) an interior diagonal hazard, \(\bar\alpha\) a probability row, \((I_K-J)^{-1}\bar K_0\) a nonnegative stochastic matrix, and \(J\bar v=\bar h\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\bar\alpha\); \(\bar K_0\); \(\bar B_a\); \(\bar v\); \(\bar h\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:exact-negative-fiber]\label{def:exact-negative-fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Exact positive three-state negative fiber \(\mathfrak W_{2/185}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(K=3\), \(T=5\), \(g=0\), \(r=1\), \(\mathcal Y=\{0,1\}\), \(\alpha=(3/10,4/10,3/10)\), emission means \((1/5,1/5,4/5)\), \(P=((3/5,3/10,1/10),(1/5,1/2,3/10),(1/10,1/5,7/10))\), \(h=(1/10,3/10,9/20)\), and \(S_{\star}=((9/10,1/10,0),(1/10,9/10,0),(0,0,1))\). The second realization is obtained by \(\alpha'=\alpha S_{\star}\), \(K_0'=S_{\star}^{-1}K_0S_{\star}\), \(h'=S_{\star}^{-1}h\), unchanged emissions, and \(P'=(I_3-\operatorname{diag}(h'))^{-1}K_0'\). The two laws are restricted to the same six observed dates \(0,\ldots,5\), and every treated outcome is the same fixed constant in both realizations.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:regular-stratum]\label{def:regular-stratum}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Declared regular chart stratum \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}=\{P_W\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{K,T}(\mathcal U,\mathcal V):\sigma_{\min}(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V})\ge\kappa_H,\ \sigma_{\min}(\bar K_0)\ge\kappa_0,\ \sigma_{\min}(V_F)\ge\kappa_F,\ \operatorname{span}(V_F)=\operatorname{span}(V_D),\ \min_{(g,r)\in\mathcal G}p_g\ge\kappa_p,\ \min_j\Sigma_{jj}\ge\kappa_V\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}declared-\allowbreak{}hankel-\allowbreak{}chart}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}hankel-\allowbreak{}gap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}killed-\allowbreak{}gap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}frame-\allowbreak{}gap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}frame-\allowbreak{}spans-\allowbreak{}algebra}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}uniform-\allowbreak{}cohort-\allowbreak{}gap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}positive-\allowbreak{}influence-\allowbreak{}variance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fixed-chart-estimator]\label{def:fixed-chart-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Total guarded fixed-\allowbreak{}frame plug-\allowbreak{}in estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix \(\theta^{\circ}\) before sampling. From \(\widehat m\), first form the polynomial matrix \(\widehat H\) and continue only if \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat H)>\kappa_H/2\). On that branch compute the canonical realization and \(\widehat{\bar K}_0\), continuing only if \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat{\bar K}_0)>\kappa_0/2\). Next compute \(\widehat{\bar B}_a\) and \(\widehat V_F\), continuing only if \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat V_F)>\kappa_F/2\). Then form \(\widehat c\) and \(\widehat L\), continuing only if \(\sigma_{\min}(I_K-\widehat L)>\tau_N\), \(\min_{(g,r)\in\mathcal G}\widehat p_g>\kappa_p/2\), every other inverse not protected by a declared fixed gap has smallest singular value greater than \(\tau_N\), every remaining scalar denominator in the ATT and influence formulas has absolute value greater than \(\tau_N\), and the computed marginal influence variances exceed \(\kappa_V/2\). On the final pass branch, evaluate the ATT, covariance, studentization, and multiplier critical value by their displayed plug-in formulas. At the first failed gate, return \(\widehat\theta=\theta^{\circ}\), \(\widehat\Sigma=I_{|\mathcal G|}\), unit standard errors, and critical value \(1\). Ties take the fallback branch. This sequential piecewise rule is a total Borel map \(f\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(W_1,\ldots,W_N\); \(\mathcal U\); \(\mathcal V\); \(\mathcal F\); \(\mathcal G\).
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-realization]\label{thm:finite-realization}
Under \(T\ge2K-1\), rank \(K\) of the full two-terminal stopped Hankel array implies that the stopped series has a minimal \(K\)-dimensional linear realization, and any two minimal realizations are related by one common invertible similarity. Predictive rank guarantees that some \(K\times K\) minor is nonsingular but does not guarantee the preregistered minor \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\). If \(P_W\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{K,T}(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)\), the fixed \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}^{-1}\) formulas recover the displayed realization from stopped words of length at most \(2K-1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\beta_R=v\) and \(\beta_A=h\).  For the selected prefixes and terminal suffixes define
\[
 R=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha M_{u_1}\\ \vdots\\ \alpha M_{u_K}\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 C=\begin{bmatrix}M_{w_1}\beta_{b_1}&\cdots&M_{w_K}\beta_{b_K}\end{bmatrix}.
\]
By the stopped-series definition, for every \(i,j\) and \(a\in\mathcal Y\),
\[
 H[i,j]=\alpha M_{u_i}M_{w_j}\beta_{b_j},
 \qquad
 H_a[i,j]=\alpha M_{u_i}M_aM_{w_j}\beta_{b_j}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 H=RC,
 \qquad
 H_a=RM_aC.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The hidden Markov construction is a \(K\)-dimensional linear realization, so every stopped Hankel submatrix has rank at most \(K\).  Conversely, any \(d\)-dimensional linear realization factors every Hankel submatrix as a prefix matrix with \(d\) columns times a suffix matrix with \(d\) rows and therefore has Hankel rank at most \(d\).  The assumed rank \(K\) of the full finite two-terminal array thus implies that no realization of dimension less than \(K\) exists.  The given realization is therefore minimal, and rank \(K\) also implies that at least one \(K\)-by-\(K\) minor is nonsingular.  This rank assertion alone says nothing about which minor is nonsingular, so it does not imply \(\det H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\ne0\) for a tuple fixed in advance.

Now suppose the declared minor is valid.  Since \(R\) and \(C\) in (1) are square and \(RC=H\) is invertible, both \(R\) and \(C\) are invertible.  Hence
\[
 H^{-1}H_a=C^{-1}M_aC.                                         \tag{2}
\]
Because \(u_1=\epsilon\), the first row of \(H=RC\) is \(\alpha C\); this is precisely the empty-prefix coordinate row, and therefore
\[
 \bar\alpha=\alpha C.                                         \tag{3}
\]
For the terminal columns \(f_R=(F_R(u_i))_{i=1}^K=Rv\) and \(f_A=(F_A(u_i))_{i=1}^K=Rh\), equation (1) gives
\[
 H^{-1}f_R=C^{-1}v,
 \qquad
 H^{-1}f_A=C^{-1}h.                                            \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) are exactly the displayed canonical formulas:
\[
 \bar M_a=C^{-1}M_aC,
 \qquad
 \bar\alpha=\alpha C,
 \qquad
 \bar v=C^{-1}v,
 \qquad
 \bar h=C^{-1}h.                                               \tag{5}
\]

If a second minimal \(K\)-dimensional realization generates the same stopped series, applying the same observable minor to it again produces the unique matrices and vectors on the left side of (5).  If its suffix matrix is \(\widetilde C\), then both original realizations are related to this common canonical realization, and hence to each other, by the single invertible similarity \(C\widetilde C^{-1}\) (with the corresponding inverse convention for columns).  Thus one common similarity transports every letter operator and both terminal columns.

Finally, each entry of \(H\) uses a word of length at most \(2K-2\), while each entry of \(H_a\) uses a word of length at most \((K-1)+1+(K-1)=2K-1\).  The terminal columns use no longer words.  The horizon assumption \(T\ge2K-1\) therefore makes every quantity in (5) a coordinate of the observed stopped law.  This proves all three assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separates chart-independent minimal realization from the extra preregistered-minor condition needed for reproducible inverse coordinates. \textit{Gap.} Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang give observable operators for an ordinary HMM, while Weiss and Nadler analyze alias similarities. Neither paper states the tailored two-terminal stopped Hankel rank condition or constructs the risk-and-adoption chart used here. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem supplies finite observable inputs from a six-date binary panel when \(K=3\), provided the declared minor rather than an outcome-selected minor is nonsingular.

\begin{theorem}[thm:hazard-lift-identification]\label{thm:hazard-lift-identification}
For every \(P_W\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{K,T}\), any valid stopped-Hankel chart yields an equation \(L\bar v=\bar h\) with a unique solution in \(\mathcal A_O\). For every \((g,r)\in\mathcal G\), the resulting \(\pi^0_{g,r}\) equals the conditional law of \((Y_g(\infty),\ldots,Y_{g+r}(\infty))\) given \(G=g\), is invariant over the choice of valid chart and all legal non-permutation aliased realizations, and identifies \(\tau_{g,r}\). On \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{HL}}_{K,T}(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)\), this invariant is evaluated by the displayed predeclared-coordinate formulas.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work first in a legal latent coordinate system, where \(v=\mathbf 1_K\), \(B_a\) is diagonal, and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(h)\).  Define the emission profile of state \(s\) by
\[
 o_s=(B_a(s,s):a\in\mathcal Y).
\]
Every element of the unital algebra generated by the \(B_a\)'s is diagonal and constant on each equivalence class \(s\sim s'\) defined by \(o_s=o_{s'}\).  For the converse inclusion, choose coefficients \((x_a:a\in\mathcal Y)\) outside the finite union of hyperplanes
\[
 \left\{x:\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}x_a\bigl(B_a(s,s)-B_a(s',s')\bigr)=0\right\}
\]
indexed by pairs of distinct profiles.  Then \(X=\sum_a x_aB_a\) has a different diagonal value \(\lambda_c\) on every profile class \(c\).  The Lagrange polynomials
\[
 q_c(X)=\prod_{c'\ne c}\frac{X-\lambda_{c'}I_K}{\lambda_c-\lambda_{c'}}
\]
are the diagonal projectors onto those classes.  Thus
\[
 \mathcal A_O
 =\{\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_K):x_s=x_{s'}\text{ whenever }o_s=o_{s'}\}.       \tag{6}
\]
The hazard-in-emission-algebra assumption says exactly that \(D\) belongs to the right side of (6).  Hence \(D\in\mathcal A_O\) and \(Dv=h\).  Moreover, if \(A\in\mathcal A_O\) and \(Av=0\), then every diagonal entry of \(A\) is zero.  Evaluation at \(v\) is therefore injective on \(\mathcal A_O\), so \(D\) is the unique solution of \(Av=h\) in that algebra.

By the finite-realization theorem, a valid chart is related to the latent realization by a single invertible matrix \(S\).  Thus
\[
 \bar M_a=S^{-1}M_aS,
 \quad \bar K_0=S^{-1}K_0S,
 \quad \bar B_a=S^{-1}B_aS,
 \quad \bar\alpha=\alpha S,
 \quad \bar v=S^{-1}v,
 \quad \bar h=S^{-1}h.                                        \tag{7}
\]
Conjugating (6) shows that the unique canonical solution is
\[
 L=S^{-1}DS,
 \qquad L\bar v=\bar h.                                      \tag{8}
\]
Hazard overlap gives \(1-h_s\ge\varepsilon_h>0\), so \(I_K-D\) and therefore \(I_K-L\) are invertible.  Since \(K_0=(I_K-D)P\), equations (7)--(8) yield
\[
 P_{\mathrm{dk}}=(I_K-L)^{-1}\bar K_0=S^{-1}PS,
 \qquad
 E=S^{-1}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}\nu(a)B_a\right)S.     \tag{9}
\]

The timing and future-innovation-independence conditions imply, by conditioning successively on the hidden states, that
\[
 \Pr\!\left(G=g,\,Y_g(\infty)=z_0,\ldots,Y_{g+r}(\infty)=z_r\right)
 =\alpha K_0^gDB_{z_0}PB_{z_1}\cdots PB_{z_r}v,               \tag{10}
\]
where survival for the first \(g\) dates contributes \(K_0^g\), adoption at date \(g\) contributes \(D\), and future counterfactual evolution is not killed.  Summing (10) over all \(z_0,ldots,z_r\), using \(\sum_zB_z=I_K\) and \(Pv=v\), gives
\[
 p_g=\Pr(G=g)=\alpha K_0^gDv=\alpha K_0^gh.                  \tag{11}
\]
The positive-cohort condition permits division by \(p_g\).  Substitution of (7)--(9) into (10), followed by cancellation of adjacent \(S\) and \(S^{-1}\), gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(Y_g(\infty)=z_0,\ldots,Y_{g+r}(\infty)=z_r\mid G=g\right)
 =\frac{\bar\alpha\bar K_0^gL\bar B_{z_0}P_{\mathrm{dk}}\bar B_{z_1}\cdots P_{\mathrm{dk}}\bar B_{z_r}\bar v}{p_g}. \tag{12}
\]
This is the displayed \(\pi^0_{g,r}\).  It is nonnegative in latent coordinates, and summing its cells gives one by (11), so it is a probability law rather than merely a linear-realization functional.

Multiplying (12) by \(\nu(z_r)\) and summing all cells yields
\[
 \mathbb E\{\nu(Y_{g+r}(\infty))\mid G=g\}
 =\frac{\bar\alpha\bar K_0^gLP_{\mathrm{dk}}^rE\bar v}{p_g}.
                                                                    \tag{13}
\]
The observed treated mean \(\mu^1_{g,r}\) is a conditional mean under \(P_W\), and consistency identifies it with the corresponding mean of the adoption-at-\(g\) potential outcome.  Hence subtracting (13) from \(\mu^1_{g,r}\) proves that the observed-law ATT functional equals the stated causal effect, rather than defining that equality.

Every other valid chart conjugates all factors in (12)--(13) by one common similarity, so the scalar cells are unchanged.  Likewise, in any legal non-permutation realization of the same stopped law, the transported operator in (8) belongs to its current-emission algebra and maps its risk terminal to its adoption terminal.  Injectivity of evaluation forces that operator to equal the realization's actual hazard diagonal.  Thus every legal realization yields (12), proving invariance over the complete legal alias fiber.  On the preregistered chart, the finite-realization formulas compute every factor from words of length at most \(2K-1\), which establishes the last assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result identifies an entire cohort counterfactual law even when the latent transition and initial distribution are not identified. \textit{Gap.} Weiss and Nadler characterize aliased parameter fibers, and Ishimaru identifies ATT by repeated measurements outside evolving staggered selection. Pennoni, Paas, and Bartolucci use propensity-weighted latent-state likelihood, while Clouth, Bijlsma, Pauws, and Vermunt use a fitted latent Markov measurement model inside the parametric g-formula. None provides a stopped-law hazard operator or proves causal functional invariance on a legal non-permutation alias fiber. \textit{Consumer.} Kim and Lee's binary VoD panel can replace observed-history extrapolation by a latent-state selection correction whenever the observable certificate passes.

\begin{theorem}[thm:generic-converse]\label{thm:generic-converse}
Let \(P_W\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{stop}}_{K,T}\) admit a legal realization with strictly positive \(\alpha\) and \(P\), interior hazards and emissions, and fix the realization stratum on which the entire partition induced by equality of the profiles \(\{B_a(s,s):a\in\mathcal Y\}\) is held constant. If distinct states \(i,j\) lie in the same emission block, \(h_i\ne h_j\), and \((Pe)_i\ne(Pe)_j\), then every relatively open neighborhood of the given realization in that fixed-emission-partition stratum contains a continuum of legal, non-permutation realizations having the same complete observed stopped-panel law \(P_W\) but different \(\mu^0_{g,1}\) for every supported cohort \(g\). Along the alias-preserving path constructed in the proof,
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\mu^0_{g,1}(t)\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{(\alpha K_0^g)_i(h_j-h_i)\{(Pe)_j-(Pe)_i\}}{(1-h_i)p_g}.
\]
Consequently, if the observed treated-outcome law is held fixed, the corresponding \(\tau_{g,1}\) is not determined by the complete observed stopped-panel law on this stratum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1_K\), let \(\mathbf e_k\) be the \(k\)-th coordinate column, and put
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(h),\qquad Q=I_K-D,\qquad
 A=\mathbf e_i(\mathbf e_j-\mathbf e_i)^{\top},\qquad
 S(t)=I_K+tA.                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The equality of the emission profiles of states \(i\) and \(j\) gives
\[
 A\mathbf 1=0,\qquad AB_a=B_aA\quad(a\in\mathcal Y).         \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the first identity follows from \((\mathbf e_j-\mathbf e_i)^{\top}\mathbf 1=0\), and the second follows because the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th diagonal entries of every \(B_a\) coincide. The matrix \(S(t)\) agrees with \(I_K\) outside row \(i\), while row \(i\) has entries \(1-t\) in column \(i\) and \(t\) in column \(j\). Expansion along all unchanged rows therefore gives
\[
 \det S(t)=1-t.                                                 \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(S(t)\) is invertible for all \(t\) in a neighborhood of zero, and (2) yields \(S(t)\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\) and \(S(t)B_a=B_aS(t)\).

For such \(t\), define
\[
 \alpha(t)=\alpha S(t),\qquad h(t)=S(t)^{-1}h,\qquad
 K_0(t)=S(t)^{-1}K_0S(t),                                      \tag{4}
\]
\[
 D(t)=\operatorname{diag}\{h(t)\},\qquad
 P(t)=\{I_K-D(t)\}^{-1}K_0(t),                                \tag{5}
\]
and leave all emission matrices \(B_a\) fixed. At \(t=0\), the entries of \(\alpha\) and \(P\), all hazards and survivals, and all emission probabilities are strictly positive by the hypotheses of the theorem. These are finitely many strict inequalities. The maps in (3)--(5) are continuous wherever \(S(t)\) and \(I_K-D(t)\) are invertible. Hence, after shrinking the neighborhood of zero, \(\alpha(t)\), \(P(t)\), \(h(t)\), and the survivals remain strictly positive. Because the emissions do not move, the entire emission-equality partition, and not merely the equality of the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th profiles, is fixed along the path.

The matrices in (4)--(5) obey the probability normalizations. Since \(K_0=QP\), stochasticity of \(P\) implies
\[
 K_0\mathbf 1=Q\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1-h.                         \tag{6}
\]
Using \(S(t)\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\) in (4) and then (6),
\[
 K_0(t)\mathbf 1
 =S(t)^{-1}K_0\mathbf 1
 =S(t)^{-1}(\mathbf 1-h)
 =\mathbf 1-h(t).                                               \tag{7}
\]
Equations (5) and (7) therefore give \(P(t)\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\). Likewise,
\[
 \alpha(t)\mathbf 1=\alpha S(t)\mathbf 1=\alpha\mathbf 1=1. \tag{8}
\]
Thus each sufficiently small perturbation is a legal homogeneous stopped-HMM realization. Conditional adoption coins can be generated with probabilities \(h(t)\) independently of current and future untreated innovations given the current hidden state; hence the current-state hazard and future-innovation independence conditions remain satisfied. Moreover,
\[
 \det K_0(t)=\det\{S(t)^{-1}K_0S(t)\}=\det K_0\ne0,          \tag{9}
\]
and the predictive rank is preserved by the observable-law equalities proved next. Thus the path remains in the fixed-emission-partition realization stratum over \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{stop}}_{K,T}\).

For \(a\in\mathcal Y\), let \(M_a(t)=B_aK_0(t)\). Commutation in (2) and (4) imply
\[
 M_a(t)=B_aS(t)^{-1}K_0S(t)=S(t)^{-1}M_aS(t).                 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently, for every word \(w=(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)\), including the empty word,
\[
 M_w(t)=S(t)^{-1}M_wS(t).                                     \tag{11}
\]
The risk- and adoption-terminal stopped series therefore satisfy
\[
 \alpha(t)M_w(t)\mathbf 1
 =\alpha M_w\mathbf 1,\qquad
 \alpha(t)M_w(t)h(t)=\alpha M_wh.                             \tag{12}
\]
By the definition of the stopped series, (12) proves equality of every pre-adoption stopped-word probability, for every available word length. For every member of the path, take the conditional distribution of post-adoption realized outcomes given the adoption time and observed stopped history to equal that under the original realization. This construction can be implemented by common post-adoption innovation kernels, preserves consistency and no anticipation, and, together with (12), gives exactly the same complete observed-panel law \(P_W\) for all sufficiently small \(t\).

The path is a genuine continuum. Differentiating \(h(t)=S(t)^{-1}h\) at zero gives
\[
 \dot h(0)=-Ah=-(h_j-h_i)\mathbf e_i\ne0.                    \tag{13}
\]
The derivative of the \(i\)-th coordinate is nonzero at zero, so continuity of that derivative permits the interval to be shrunk until \(h_i(t)\) is strictly monotone. The realization path is then injective on that interval. Every permutation-equivalence class has cardinality at most \(K!\); hence the quotient of this injective interval by latent permutations is still uncountable and contains a continuum of non-permutation representatives. Since the path converges to the original realization as \(t\to0\), every relatively open neighborhood in the fixed-partition stratum contains such a continuum.

It remains to calculate the causal continuation. Equality of the two emission profiles also gives \(e_i=e_j\), and therefore
\[
 Ae=0,\qquad S(t)e=e,\qquad S(t)^{-1}e=e.                    \tag{14}
\]
The cohort mass is constant along the path:
\[
 p_g(t)=\alpha(t)K_0(t)^gh(t)
 =\alpha K_0^gh=p_g.                                          \tag{15}
\]
To express the alternative causal numerator in the original coordinates, set
\[
 J(t)=S(t)D(t)S(t)^{-1},\qquad
 P_J(t)=\{I_K-J(t)\}^{-1}K_0.                                 \tag{16}
\]
Equations (4), (5), (14), and (16) yield
\[
 \alpha(t)K_0(t)^gD(t)P(t)e
 =\alpha K_0^gJ(t)P_J(t)e.                                    \tag{17}
\]
The current-state hazard condition and future-innovation independence give, under the realization at \(t\),
\[
 \mathbb E_t\!\left[\nu\{Y_{g+1}(\infty)\}\mathbf 1\{G=g\}\right]
 =\alpha(t)K_0(t)^gD(t)P(t)e.                                  \tag{18}
\]
Here \(K_0(t)^g\) propagates through the \(g\) survived periods, \(D(t)\) selects adoption from the current hidden state, \(P(t)\) propagates the untreated state one period, and \(e\) integrates the next untreated emission. Thus (15), (17), and (18), with division justified by \(p_g>0\), give
\[
 \mu^0_{g,1}(t)=\frac{\alpha K_0^gJ(t)P_J(t)e}{p_g}.          \tag{19}
\]

We now differentiate this quotient. From \(S'(0)=A\), \((S^{-1})'(0)=-A\), and (13),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot J(0)
 &=AD-\operatorname{diag}(Ah)-DA\\
 &=[A,D]-\operatorname{diag}(Ah)\\
 &=(h_j-h_i)\mathbf e_i(\mathbf e_j-\mathbf e_i)^{\top}.
                                                                    \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, \(\dot J(0)\mathbf 1=0\). The defining identity \(\{I_K-J(t)\}P_J(t)=K_0\) has derivative \(-\dot J(0)P+(I_K-D)\dot P_J(0)=0\). Since every survival is positive, \(I_K-D\) is invertible, and hence
\[
 \dot P_J(0)=(I_K-D)^{-1}\dot J(0)(I_K-D)^{-1}K_0
 =(I_K-D)^{-1}\dot J(0)P.                                    \tag{21}
\]
Since \(I_K+D(I_K-D)^{-1}=(I_K-D)^{-1}\), equations (19)--(21) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\mu^0_{g,1}(t)\right|_{t=0}
 &=\frac{\alpha K_0^g\{\dot J(0)Pe+D(I_K-D)^{-1}\dot J(0)Pe\}}{p_g}\\
 &=\frac{\alpha K_0^g(I_K-D)^{-1}\dot J(0)Pe}{p_g}\\
 &=\frac{(\alpha K_0^g)_i(h_j-h_i)\{(Pe)_j-(Pe)_i\}}
 {(1-h_i)p_g}.                                                  \tag{22}
\end{aligned}
\]
Every denominator in (22) is positive. Specifically, the interior-hazard condition gives \(1-h_i>0\); strict positivity of \(P\) and of \(1-h\) makes \(K_0=QP\) entrywise positive; strict positivity of \(\alpha\) then makes every coordinate of \(\alpha K_0^g\) positive; and multiplication by the positive hazard column gives \(p_g=\alpha K_0^gh>0\). The two contrasts in the hypotheses are nonzero, so (22) is nonzero for every supported \(g\). There are only finitely many such cohort indices. Continuity of their derivatives and a minimum over finitely many neighborhoods give one interval on which each derivative keeps its nonzero sign. Every corresponding function \(\mu^0_{g,1}(t)\) is therefore strictly monotone on that interval, so distinct path points give different values for every supported cohort. The observed treated-outcome law was held fixed, so \(\mu^1_{g,1}\) is constant and \(\tau_{g,1}=\mu^1_{g,1}-\mu^0_{g,1}\) changes with the opposite sign. This proves every assertion of the narrowed theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:standard-reductions]\label{prop:standard-reductions}
If \(K=1\), write \(b_a=\Pr(Y_t(\infty)=a)\) and let \(h\) be the scalar hazard. Then \(P=1\), \(K_0=1-h\), \(p_g=(1-h)^gh\), \(L=h\), \(P_{\mathrm{dk}}=1\), \(\pi^0_{g,r}(z_0,\ldots,z_r)=\prod_{j=0}^r b_{z_j}\), and \(\mu^0_{g,r}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}\nu(a)b_a\). If the \(K\) emission-profile vectors \(\{(B_a(s,s):a\in\mathcal Y):1\le s\le K\}\) are pairwise distinct, multivariate polynomial interpolation gives every diagonal matrix in the latent-coordinate emission algebra; hence the canonical \(L\) is similar to \(D\), \(P_{\mathrm{dk}}\) is similar to \(P\), and the observable formulas reduce exactly to \(\pi^0_{g,r}(z_0,\ldots,z_r)=\alpha K_0^gDB_{z_0}PB_{z_1}\cdots PB_{z_r}v/p_g\) and \(\mu^0_{g,r}=\alpha K_0^gDP^re/p_g\). If \(h_s=\lambda\) for every state, then \(L=\lambda I_K\) and \(P_{\mathrm{dk}}\) is similar to \(P\). For \(r=0\), \(\mu^0_{g,0}=\bar\alpha\bar K_0^gE\bar h/p_g\) without the hazard-algebra restriction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a law on a valid predeclared stopped-Hankel chart, so that \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\) is nonsingular, and fix \((g,r)\) with \(0\leq g\leq g+r\leq T\) and \(p_g>0\).  The horizon and predictive-rank assumptions, together with the finite-realization theorem, imply that the latent stopped realization and the canonical realization are related by an invertible matrix \(S\).  Choose the orientation of \(S\) so that
\[
 \bar\alpha=\alpha S,\qquad
 \bar M_a=S^{-1}B_aK_0S,\qquad
 \bar v=S^{-1}v,qquad
 \bar h=S^{-1}h.
\]
The emission probabilities sum to one in each hidden state under the homogeneous untreated HMM assumption, hence \(\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}B_a=I_K\).  Therefore, using the definitions of \(\bar K_0\) and \(\bar B_a\), and using nonsingularity of \(K_0\),
\[
 \bar K_0=\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}\bar M_a
 =S^{-1}K_0S,
 \qquad
 \bar B_a=\bar M_a\bar K_0^{-1}=S^{-1}B_aS.                                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular, the canonical emission algebra is the similarity transform of the algebra generated by the diagonal matrices \(B_a\).

Suppose first that \(K=1\).  Row stochasticity of the one-by-one transition matrix gives \(P=1\), while \(B_a=b_a\), \(\sum_a b_a=1\), \(D=h\), and \(Q=1-h\).  Thus
\[
 K_0=QP=1-h.
\]
The current-state hazard assumption gives, for every supported cohort \(g\),
\[
 p_g=\Pr(G=g)=\alpha K_0^g h=(1-h)^g h,                                      \tag{2}
\]
because \(\alpha=v=1\).  Hazard overlap gives \(0<h<1\), so every division and the inverse below are valid.  In one dimension the emission algebra is the scalar algebra.  Since \(\bar h=h\bar v\) and \(\bar v\neq0\) (indeed \(\bar\alpha\bar v=F_R(\epsilon)=1\)), the defining equation \(L\bar v=\bar h\) has the unique scalar solution \(L=h\).  Consequently
\[
 P_{\mathrm{dk}}=(1-L)^{-1}\bar K_0=(1-h)^{-1}(1-h)=1.                         \tag{3}
\]
Substitution of (2)--(3) and \(\bar B_a=b_a\) into the counterfactual functional yields
\[
 \pi^0_{g,r}(z_0,\ldots,z_r)
 =\frac{\bar\alpha(1-h)^gh\bigl(\prod_{j=0}^r b_{z_j}\bigr)\bar v}
 {(1-h)^gh}
 =\prod_{j=0}^r b_{z_j}.                                                        \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, \(E=\sum_a\nu(a)b_a\), and the same substitution gives
\[
 \mu^0_{g,r}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}\nu(a)b_a.                                  \tag{5}
\]
Thus the first reduction follows, including the asserted support and positivity conditions.

Now suppose that the emission-profile vectors are pairwise distinct.  Write
\[
 x_s=(B_a(s,s):a\in\mathcal Y)\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal Y|}.
\]
For each ordered pair \(s\neq t\), choose \(a(s,t)\in\mathcal Y\) such that \(B_{a(s,t)}(s,s)\neq B_{a(s,t)}(t,t)\); such a coordinate exists by pairwise distinctness.  Define the explicit interpolation polynomial
\[
 q_s(x)=\prod_{t\neq s}
 \frac{x_{a(s,t)}-B_{a(s,t)}(t,t)}
 {B_{a(s,t)}(s,s)-B_{a(s,t)}(t,t)}.                                             \tag{6}
\]
Every denominator in (6) is nonzero by its construction.  The factor indexed by \(t\) vanishes at \(x_t\), whereas every factor equals one at \(x_s\); hence \(q_s(x_u)=\mathbf 1\{u=s\}\).  Since the \(B_a\)'s are commuting diagonal matrices, polynomial evaluation in (6) gives
\[
 q_s((B_a)_{a\in\mathcal Y})=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf 1\{u=s\}:1\leq u\leq K).
\]
These \(K\) coordinate projectors span every diagonal matrix, so the latent-coordinate emission algebra contains \(D\).

By (1), \(\widetilde L=S^{-1}DS\) belongs to \(\mathcal A_O\), and
\[
 \widetilde L\bar v=S^{-1}DS S^{-1}v=S^{-1}Dv=S^{-1}h=\bar h.                  \tag{7}
\]
The evaluation map on \(\mathcal A_O\) is injective: every \(A\in\mathcal A_O\) has the form \(A=S^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(d)S\), and
\[
 A\bar v=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 S^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(d)v=S^{-1}d=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A=0.                                               \tag{8}
\]
Equations (7)--(8) and the definition of the hazard lift therefore give
\[
 L=S^{-1}DS.                                                                     \tag{9}
\]
Because hazard overlap implies that \(Q=I_K-D\) is invertible, (1), (9), and \(K_0=QP\) imply
\[
 P_{\mathrm{dk}}
 =(I_K-L)^{-1}\bar K_0
 =S^{-1}Q^{-1}K_0S
 =S^{-1}PS.                                                                       \tag{10}
\]
Also, with \(E_0=\sum_a\nu(a)B_a\),
\[
 E=S^{-1}E_0S,
 \qquad E_0v=e.                                                                  \tag{11}
\]
Substituting (1) and (9)--(11) into the displayed counterfactual functional and cancelling adjacent factors \(SS^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \pi^0_{g,r}(z_0,\ldots,z_r)
 &=\frac{\alpha K_0^gDB_{z_0}PB_{z_1}\cdots PB_{z_r}v}{p_g},\\
 \mu^0_{g,r}
 &=\frac{\alpha K_0^gDP^re}{p_g}.
 \end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{12}
\]
The current-state hazard and future-innovation-independence assumptions also verify the causal content of (12), rather than merely its algebraic form.  Conditional on the state at date \(g\), adoption is independent of the current untreated emission and all later untreated innovations, so
\[
 \Pr\{G=g,Y_g(\infty)=z_0,\ldots,Y_{g+r}(\infty)=z_r\}
 =\alpha K_0^gDB_{z_0}PB_{z_1}\cdots PB_{z_r}v.                                 \tag{13}
\]
Division of (13) by the positive cohort mass \(p_g\) proves that the first line of (12) is the conditional untreated path law.  Summing its last coordinate after weighting by \(\nu\), and summing all preceding coordinates, proves the second line of (12).  Thus the observed-law functional equals the stated counterfactual quantity under the declared causal assumptions; it is not being made causal by definition.

Next suppose \(h_s=\lambda\) for every state.  Since \(h\in(0,1)^K\), \(0<\lambda<1\), and
\[
 D=\lambda I_K,\qquad Q=(1-\lambda)I_K,qquad \bar h=S^{-1}h=\lambda\bar v.
\]
The matrix \(\lambda I_K\) lies in the unital algebra \(\mathcal A_O\) and solves \(L\bar v=\bar h\).  Injectivity follows by the same argument as (8), without pairwise distinctness: every polynomial in the simultaneously diagonal matrices \(B_a\) is diagonal, so its similarity transform annihilates \(\bar v=S^{-1}v\) only if that diagonal is zero.  Hence the hazard-lift definition gives \(L=\lambda I_K\).  Using (1) and \(K_0=(1-\lambda)P\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{dk}}
 =(1-\lambda)^{-1}\bar K_0
 =S^{-1}PS,                                                                       \tag{14}
\]
which proves the constant-hazard reduction.

Finally take \(r=0\), and do not impose the hazard-in-emission-algebra restriction.  On any valid canonical chart of the ambient stopped model, current-state hazard and conditional independence of adoption from \(Y_g(\infty)\) give, for every \(a\in\mathcal Y\),
\[
 \Pr\{G=g,Y_g(\infty)=a\}=\alpha K_0^gB_ah.
\]
Consequently, using (1), the terminal similarity relations, and \(p_g>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu^0_{g,0}
 &=\frac{\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}\nu(a)\alpha K_0^gB_ah}{p_g}\\
 &=\frac{\bar\alpha\bar K_0^g
 \left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}\nu(a)\bar B_a\right)\bar h}{p_g}
 =\frac{\bar\alpha\bar K_0^gE\bar h}{p_g}.
 \end{aligned}                                                                  \tag{15}
\]
This last derivation uses the directly observed adoption-terminal stopped probabilities and never constructs \(L\); therefore it remains valid without the hazard-algebra restriction.  Equations (4), (5), (12), (14), and (15) establish all four reductions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-chart-inference]\label{thm:fixed-chart-inference}
Uniformly over compact subsets of \(\mathcal P_{\kappa}\) satisfying independent-panel sampling and the moment envelope, the guarded estimator takes its inverse-formula branch with probability tending to one, so \(\sqrt N(\widehat\theta-\theta)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N\varphi(W_i)+o_{P_W}(1)\), and \(\widehat\Sigma\) consistently estimates \(\Sigma\). A panel-level Gaussian multiplier critical value yields asymptotically simultaneous \(1-\alpha_0\) coverage for the fixed grid \(\mathcal G\). The fixed fallback values therefore do not change the regular-subset expansion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact subset \(\mathcal K\) of the declared stratum.  On \(\mathcal K\), the fixed lower bounds on \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\), \(\sigma_{\min}(\bar K_0)\), \(\sigma_{\min}(V_F)\), cohort mass, and marginal influence variance keep the corresponding population quantities away from every guarded boundary.  Hazard overlap makes \(I_K-L\) invertible.  Every remaining population inverse and scalar denominator appearing on the inverse-formula branch is continuous and nonzero at each law where that branch represents the displayed functional; compactness then gives a positive lower bound for it on \(\mathcal K\).  Since \(\tau_N=N^{-1/8}\to0\), there is consequently an open neighborhood \(U\) of the population moment set on which, for all sufficiently large \(N\), every gate selects the inverse-formula branch.

The moment envelope implies a uniform second-moment bound.  By independence and Chebyshev's inequality,
\[
 \sup_{P_W\in\mathcal K}P_W\!\left(\|\widehat m-m\|>\epsilon\right)
 \le \frac{C}{N\epsilon^2},                                  \tag{20}
\]
for a constant \(C\) depending only on the fixed dimension \(q\).  Hence \(\widehat m\in U\) with probability tending to one uniformly on \(\mathcal K\), and the fallback branch is asymptotically irrelevant.

On \(U\), every chart coordinate is affine in \(m\), and the inverse-formula branch is a finite composition of matrix addition and multiplication, fixed polynomial evaluation, matrix inversion, and division by cohort masses.  It is therefore twice continuously differentiable.  The load-bearing differentials are
\[
 d(H^{-1})=-H^{-1}(dH)H^{-1},
 \qquad
 d\bar B_a=(d\bar M_a)\bar K_0^{-1}-\bar B_a(d\bar K_0)\bar K_0^{-1}. \tag{21}
\]
Writing \(G_F=V_F^{\top}V_F\) and \(c=G_F^{-1}V_F^{\top}\bar h\), direct differentiation yields
\[
 dc=G_F^{-1}\!\left\{(dV_F)^{\top}\bar h+V_F^{\top}d\bar h
 -\bigl[(dV_F)^{\top}V_F+V_F^{\top}dV_F\bigr]c\right\}.       \tag{22}
\]
The ordinary product rule through every fixed written-order monomial gives \(dL\).  De-killing and propagation then give
\[
 dP_{\mathrm{dk}}=(I_K-L)^{-1}\{d\bar K_0+(dL)P_{\mathrm{dk}}\},
 \qquad
 d(P_{\mathrm{dk}}^r)=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}P_{\mathrm{dk}}^s(dP_{\mathrm{dk}})P_{\mathrm{dk}}^{r-1-s}. \tag{23}
\]
Finally, the quotient rule differentiates each counterfactual mean, each observed treated mean, and their difference.  Equations (21)--(23) therefore define the advertised joint derivative \(Df(m)\), including the covariance between treated and reconstructed counterfactual components because both are functions of the same panel moment vector.

Compactness and the fixed gaps bound the second derivative of this branch by a common constant.  Taylor's theorem and (20) imply
\[
 \widehat\theta-\theta
 =Df(m)(\widehat m-m)+R_N,
 \qquad
 \|R_N\|\le C\|\widehat m-m\|^2=O_{P_W}(N^{-1}),             \tag{24}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).  Identification gives \(f(m)=\theta\); it is not imposed as a definition of the causal target.  Since
\[
 Df(m)(\widehat m-m)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^NDf(m)\{Z(W_i)-m\}
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\varphi(W_i),                            \tag{25}
\]
multiplication of (24) by \(\sqrt N\) proves the displayed influence expansion.

For any sequence \(P_{W,N}\in\mathcal K\), the uniform \((2+\delta)\)-moment bound implies the Lindeberg condition for \(N^{-1/2}\varphi(W_i)\).  Every subsequence of the bounded covariance matrices has a further convergent subsequence, and the cited multivariate Lindeberg--Feller theorem applies there.  This subsequence argument gives the required uniform fixed-dimensional Gaussian approximation.  =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\widehat\varphi_i\widehat\varphi_i^{\top}+o_{P_W}(1)
 \longrightarrow_{P_W}\mathbb E_{P_W}[\varphi(W)\varphi(W)^{\top}]=\Sigma, \tag{26}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal K\), where \(\widehat\varphi_i=Df(\widehat m)\{Z(W_i)-\widehat m\}\) on the pass branch.

\]

\begin{lemma}[lem:alias-centralizer-hazard-jacobian]\label{lem:alias-centralizer-hazard-jacobian}
In the full two-state alias-block centralizer used by \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{lift}}\), write
\[
 S(a,b)=\begin{pmatrix}a&1-a\\ b&1-b\end{pmatrix},\qquad a\ne b,
\]
and let \(h_{12}=(h_1,h_2)^{\top}\). If \(h_1\ne h_2\), the rational map
\[
 \Phi(a,b)=S(a,b)\operatorname{diag}\{S(a,b)^{-1}h_{12}\}S(a,b)^{-1}
\]
has differential rank two wherever \(a\ne b\). In fact, the Jacobian minor formed from \(\Phi_{11}\) and \(\Phi_{21}\) is
\[
 \det\frac{\partial(\Phi_{11},\Phi_{21})}{\partial(a,b)}
 =-\frac{(h_1-h_2)^2}{(a-b)^4}.
\]
At every point at which all legality conditions are strict, their positivity inequalities leave a relatively open two-dimensional legal region; they do not reduce this local dimension.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The determinant of \(S(a,b)\) is \(a-b\), so the hypothesis \(a\ne b\) gives
\[
 S(a,b)^{-1}=\frac{1}{a-b}
 \begin{pmatrix}1-b&a-1\\-b&a\end{pmatrix}.                  \tag{23}
\]
Consequently, if \(u=S(a,b)^{-1}h_{12}\), then
\[
 u_1=\frac{(1-b)h_1+(a-1)h_2}{a-b},\qquad
 u_2=\frac{-bh_1+ah_2}{a-b}.                                  \tag{24}
\]
Multiplication of \(S(a,b)\operatorname{diag}(u)S(a,b)^{-1}\), using (23)--(24), gives the two entries
\[
 \Phi_{11}
 =\frac{a^2h_2-2abh_1+ah_1-ah_2+b^2h_1}{(a-b)^2},\qquad
 \Phi_{21}=\frac{b(1-b)(h_1-h_2)}{(a-b)^2}.                   \tag{25}
\]
Applying the quotient rule to the two rational functions in (25), expanding the resulting two-by-two determinant, and collecting the coefficients of \(h_1^2\), \(h_1h_2\), and \(h_2^2\) yields
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 \partial_a\Phi_{11}&\partial_b\Phi_{11}\\
 \partial_a\Phi_{21}&\partial_b\Phi_{21}
 \end{pmatrix}
 =-\frac{h_1^2-2h_1h_2+h_2^2}{(a-b)^4}
 =-\frac{(h_1-h_2)^2}{(a-b)^4}.                               \tag{26}
\]
The right-hand side of (26) is nonzero under \(h_1\ne h_2\) and \(a\ne b\), proving differential rank two. Every transformed initial probability, hazard, emission probability, and transition entry appearing in the definition of a legal lift is a continuous rational function of \((a,b)\) on \(a\ne b\), with the inverse-survival denominators nonzero at an interior legal point. A finite conjunction of strict positivity inequalities therefore remains true on some open ball around that point. Hence the local legal parameter set contains a two-dimensional open neighborhood, as claimed.
\end{proof}

Let now \(a_N,b_N>0\) satisfy \(Nb_N^2\to0\) and \(b_N=o(a_N)\). Define the one-panel chi-square quantity in the direction used in (13) by
\[
 \chi_N^2=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}
 \frac{\{p_{a_N,b_N}(x)-q_{a_N,b_N}(x)\}^2}
 {q_{a_N,b_N}(x)}=O(b_N^2).                                  \tag{25}
\]
Factoring the finite product sum proves directly that
\[
 1+\chi^2\!\left((P_0^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N},
                  (P_1^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}\right)
 =(1+\chi_N^2)^N.                                             \tag{26}
\]
The right side minus one is at most \(\exp(N\chi_N^2)-1=o(1)\). Cauchy--Schwarz then gives
\[
 \left\|(P_0^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}
       -(P_1^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2\!\left((P_0^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N},
                  (P_1^{a_N,b_N})^{\otimes N}\right)}
 \longrightarrow0.                                           \tag{27}
\]
If \(a_N\to0\), (23) and \(b_N=o(a_N)\) give
\[
 |\tau_{0,1}(P_1^{a_N,b_N})-
   \tau_{0,1}(P_0^{a_N,b_N})|
 =\left(\frac{63}{544}+o(1)\right)a_N.                      \tag{28}
\]
If \(a_N\equiv\eta_0>0\), continuity of (22) and (24) instead gives convergence of the absolute ATT gap to \(\Delta(\eta_0,0)>0\). Moreover, (20) and \(Nb_N^2\to0\) imply
\[
 \frac{\sigma_3(V_D)}{\rho_N}
 =O\!\left(\frac{b_N\sqrt N}{\sqrt{\log N}}\right)
 =o(1).                                                       \tag{29}
\]
For every \(N\), positivity of \(b_N\) and (17)--(20) gives \(\sigma_3(V_D)>0\). Thus eventually \(0<\sigma_3(V_D)\le\rho_N\), which is precisely the sampling-noise component of the declared gray zone.

We finally prove the two statistical impossibilities using the fixed-\(\eta_0\) sequence, for example with \(b_N=N^{-1}\). Let \(\vartheta_{j,N}=\tau_{0,1}(P_j^{\eta_0,b_N})\) and let \(\delta=\Delta(\eta_0,0)>0\). By (24), \(|\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|\to\delta\), while (27) gives merging product laws. If an estimator \(\widehat\vartheta_N\) were uniformly consistent over any class containing these laws, then for all large \(N\) the events
\[
 A_{j,N}=\{|\widehat\vartheta_N-\vartheta_{j,N}|<\delta/3\},
 \qquad j=0,1,                                                 \tag{30}
\]
would have probability tending to one under their respective product laws. Total-variation merging transfers the probability of \(A_{1,N}\) to the zeroth product law. But \(A_{0,N}\cap A_{1,N}=\varnothing\) for large \(N\), contradicting that both then have probability tending to one under the zeroth law. Uniform consistency is therefore impossible.

Similarly, let \(C_N\) be a random confidence set with coverage at least \(1-\alpha_0-o(1)\) under both product laws, where \(\alpha_0<1/2\). Total-variation merging and the union bound imply under the zeroth product law that
\[
 \Pr\{\vartheta_{0,N}\in C_N,\ \vartheta_{1,N}\in C_N\}
 \ge1-2\alpha_0-o(1)>0.                                      \tag{31}
\]
On the event in (31), \(\operatorname{diam}(C_N)\ge|\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|\to\delta\). Hence uniformly honest diameter \(o(1)\) in probability is impossible.

For the declared specialization \(\eta_N=N^{-1/4}\), \(\zeta_N=N^{-1}\), the sequence premises hold because \(N\zeta_N^2=N^{-1}\to0\) and \(\zeta_N/\eta_N=N^{-3/4}\to0\). Equations (20), (27)--(29) become
\[
 \sigma_3(V_D)=\left(\frac{6250}{17\sqrt{1266}}+o(1)\right)N^{-1},
 \quad
 \|(P_{W,N}^{(0)})^{\otimes N}-(P_{W,N}^{(1)})^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to0,
 \quad
 |\vartheta_{1,N}-\vartheta_{0,N}|=\left(\frac{63}{544}+o(1)\right)N^{-1/4}. \tag{32}
\]
Repeating (31) with the last separation in (32) rules out uniformly honest diameter \(o(N^{-1/4})\) over that pair. This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The empirical algebra cannot be generated by exact commutativity or exact repeated eigenvalues after sampling noise perturbs population moments. The estimator therefore uses a fixed written-order polynomial dictionary and least-squares off-model extension. Full stopped-Hankel rank ensures that some \(K\)-minor works but not that the ordered \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\) fixed before outcomes does; all Euclidean separation claims are tied to that declared chart and coefficient normalization. The total estimator uses inverse formulas only behind fixed half-gap gates and the uncovered-inverse threshold \(\tau_N=N^{-1/8}\), and otherwise returns fixed defaults. Along \(\eta_N=N^{-1/4}\) and \(\zeta_N=N^{-1}\), the omitted singular value is \((10.33271+o(1))\zeta_N\), the displayed product laws merge in total variation, and \(\tau_{0,1}\) remains separated at order \(N^{-1/4}\).

\section{Honest scope}

The point-identification theorem fixes the hidden order and a finite alphabet, uses \(T\ge2K-1\), assumes time-homogeneous untreated dynamics, invertible killed dynamics, positive cohort masses, and sequential hazard randomization. It identifies finite untreated cohort path laws but not latent labels, transitions, or initial probabilities. Predictive rank yields a chart-independent minimal realization and only guarantees existence of some nonsingular minor; the displayed formulas and inference additionally require the predeclared \(H_{\mathcal U,\mathcal V}\). The generic converse concerns future lags. The exact \(2/185\) witness and local lower bound apply only to \(K=3\), \(T=5\), \(g=0\), and \(r=1\). The lower bound limits this proposal's own fixed-chart plug-in and does not challenge comparator methods on their maintained regular domains. The total estimator has fixed fallback outputs, whose probability vanishes only on compact regular subsets. The open legal-lift classification is restricted to the positive interior binary \(K=3\), \(T=5\), one-two-state-alias-block stratum at \((g,r)=(0,1)\); it demands explicit branch invariants and a complexity improvement over generic elimination. The Kim--Lee data are unavailable, so no empirical certificate is claimed. Unknown order, continuous outcomes, approximate homogeneity, sharp tolerance bounds, adaptive chart selection, constrained-model efficiency, and finite-sample remainders remain open.
