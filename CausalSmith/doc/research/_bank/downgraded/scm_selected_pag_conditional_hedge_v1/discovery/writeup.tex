\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}selected\_\allowbreak{}pag\_\allowbreak{}conditional\_\allowbreak{}hedge}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Branch-sCIDP is a direct published-class soundness specialization and finite formula search, not a new completeness theorem. A general selection-equalizer lift now converts any two positive rational finite SCMs with the same observational law but different ordinary interventional outcome laws into selected models with the same full selected observational law, a common positive conditioning denominator, and different joint numerators and conditional effects. The connected binary table is an exact instance, while the affine rank compiler gives a second exact construction for a supplied represented family. Whether every all-branches failure yields a represented locked construction remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Failure of selected joint identification does not by itself preserve the conditioning denominator, and the published conditional algorithm supplies soundness rather than selected conditional completeness. Ordinary selected hedges therefore leave a genuine graph-to-lock construction problem.

\paragraph{Niche.} The paper separates three levels cleanly: the published positive-discrete Branch-sCIDP soundness result, exact algebraic or equalizer constructions of denominator-locked rational witnesses, and the still-open graphical implication from universal branch failure to one of those constructions.

\paragraph{Fill.} The proved contribution is a general constructive selection-equalizer lift, an exact affine rank compiler for a supplied represented model family, and a connected positive rational binary instance with a same-graph perturbation showing that locking is tuned rather than graph-imposed. The residual forest-to-lock implication is retained as an open question.

\section{Related work}

Chen and Mooij provide the selected-COPAG model, complete unconditional sIDP, and sound conditional sCIDP. Branch-sCIDP is presented only as a direct input-free-COPAG specialization and exhaustive restatement of that published soundness architecture. Their unconditional residual forests motivate the open graph-to-lock question but do not establish denominator locking. Abouei and coauthors study selected hedges for an unconditional selected intervention target in a known augmented graph, whereas the present equalizer and rank constructions coordinate a postintervention conditioning denominator. Shpitser and Pearl and, without selection, Jaber and coauthors provide the fixed-graph and PAG conditional benchmarks that motivate exhaustive role exchange. Duarte and coauthors motivate exact finite discrete fiber computation; the present rational constructions target checkable nonidentification certificates rather than sharp-bound optimization.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:input-free-copag]\label{ass:input-free-copag}
\(\mathcal P\in\operatorname{COPAG}_0(V)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Input-free COPAG model, \cite{ChenMooij2026SelectedPAG}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:disjoint-query-sets]\label{ass:disjoint-query-sets}
\(X\cap Y=X\cap C=Y\cap C=\varnothing\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Disjoint treatment, outcome, and conditioning sets, \cite{JaberRibeiroZhangBareinboim2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonempty-outcome]\label{ass:nonempty-outcome}
\(Y\neq\varnothing\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonempty outcome set, \cite{ChenMooij2026SelectedPAG}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-discrete-selected-model]\label{ass:positive-discrete-selected-model}
\(\mathbf M\in\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive discrete selected SCM represented by a COPAG, \cite{ChenMooij2026SelectedPAG}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-certificate-support]\label{ass:binary-certificate-support}
Every endogenous and latent variable of \(\mathbf M\) is binary.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Binary treatments, outcomes, eligibility variables, and latent response types are standard finite approximations in selected-cohort analyses and form the first nontrivial exact certificate class.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rational-interior-mechanisms]\label{ass:rational-interior-mechanisms}
Every local stochastic-mechanism entry of \(\mathbf M\) is rational and strictly between zero and one.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Finite rational probability tables cover recorded designs and discretized scientific mechanisms, are dense in the positive table simplex, and allow a countermodel certificate to be reproduced with exact arithmetic.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:branch-soundness]\label{thm:branch-soundness}
Every symbolic formula returned by \(\operatorname{Branch\text{-}sCIDP}(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)\) equals \(\psi_{\mathbf M}(y;x,c)\) for every \(\mathbf M\in\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\). The role graph has at most \(2^{|X\cup C|}\) states, every nonrepeating trace has length at most \(2^{|X\cup C|}-1\), and the shared formula DAG can be constructed in \(O(3^n\operatorname{poly}(n)+L_{\mathrm{form}})\) symbolic operations. No implication from \(\operatorname{AllFail}\) to nonidentification is asserted here.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an arbitrary represented model \(\mathbf M\in\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\) and assignments \(x\), \(c\), and \(y\) in their declared discrete support spaces.  For every \(A\subseteq V\), put
\[
 \mathcal X_A(\mathbf M)
 :=\prod_{v\in A}\operatorname{supp}_{\mathbf M}(V_v),
\]
with \(\mathcal X_{\varnothing}(\mathbf M)\) equal to the singleton containing the empty assignment.  Each coordinate support is at most countable because the model is discrete, and \(V\) is finite by the definitions of \(V\) and \(n\).  Hence every \(\mathcal X_A(\mathbf M)\), in particular \(\mathcal X_Y(\mathbf M)\), is at most countable.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}disjoint\text{-}query\text{-}sets}\) makes the assignment \(a\in\mathcal X_W(\mathbf M)\), defined by \(a_X=x\) and \(a_C=c\), well defined.  At a role state \(\rho=(\bar X,\bar C)\), write \(\bar x=a_{\bar X}\) and \(\bar c=a_{\bar C}\), and define the proof-local conditional kernel
\[
 K_{\rho,\mathbf M}(y)
 :=P_{\mathbf M}\!\left(
 Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X=\bar x),
 \bar C=\bar c,S=\mathbf 1\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
The sets in (1) are pairwise compatible: the definition of a role state gives \(\bar X\mathbin{\dot\cup}\bar C=W=X\cup C\), while \(Y\cap W=\varnothing\) follows from Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}disjoint\text{-}query\text{-}sets}\).  The positivity condition in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}positive\text{-}discrete\text{-}selected\text{-}model}\) gives, at every assignment under consideration,
\[
 P_{\mathbf M}\!\left(
 \bar C=\bar c,S=\mathbf 1
 \mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X=\bar x)\right)>0.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(P_{\mathbf M}(S=\mathbf 1\mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X=\bar x))\) is at least the quantity in (2) and is positive.  Thus every conditional probability and every division below is defined, including the boundary case \(\bar C=\varnothing\).

We first prove invariance along the role-state graph.  At its root \(\rho_0=(X,C)\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}target\text{-}query}\) gives
\[
 K_{\rho_0,\mathbf M}(y)
 =P_{\mathbf M}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x),C=c,S=\mathbf 1)
 =\psi_{\mathbf M}(y;x,c).
 \tag{3}
\]
Consider an edge that moves a nonempty current bucket intersection \(T\subseteq\bar X\) from treatment to conditioning.  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}reachable\text{-}role\text{-}graph}\) includes this edge only when the corresponding Rule--2 separation holds.  Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}selected\text{-}rule\text{-}two}\) with
\[
 A_0=Y,\qquad B_0=T,\qquad C_0=\bar C,
 \qquad D_0=\bar X\setminus T.
\]
These four sets are pairwise disjoint by the role partition and \(Y\cap W=\varnothing\).  Using the restrictions of \(a\) on both sides of the Rule--2 equality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 K_{(\bar X,\bar C),\mathbf M}(y)
 &=P_{\mathbf M}\!\left(
 Y=y\mid \bar C=\bar c,
 \operatorname{do}(T=a_T,\bar X\setminus T=a_{\bar X\setminus T}),
 S=\mathbf 1\right)\\
 &=P_{\mathbf M}\!\left(
 Y=y\mid T=a_T,\bar C=\bar c,
 \operatorname{do}(\bar X\setminus T=a_{\bar X\setminus T}),
 S=\mathbf 1\right)\\
 &=K_{(\bar X\setminus T,\,\bar C\cup T),\mathbf M}(y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
If instead an edge moves a nonempty \(T\subseteq\bar C\) from conditioning to treatment, apply the same lemma with
\[
 A_0=Y,\qquad B_0=T,\qquad C_0=\bar C\setminus T,
 \qquad D_0=\bar X.
\]
Reading its equality from right to left gives
\[
 K_{(\bar X,\bar C),\mathbf M}(y)
 =K_{(\bar X\cup T,\,\bar C\setminus T),\mathbf M}(y).
 \tag{5}
\]
Induction on the length of a directed trace from \(\rho_0\), using (3)--(5), proves
\[
 K_{\rho,\mathbf M}(y)=\psi_{\mathbf M}(y;x,c)
 \quad\text{for every reachable role state }\rho.
 \tag{6}
\]

Suppose now that Branch-sCIDP succeeds at \(\rho=(\bar X,\bar C)\).  Its terminal sIDP call has outcome set \(A_0=Y\cup\bar C\) and intervention set \(B_0=\bar X\).  They are disjoint, and \(A_0\neq\varnothing\) by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}nonempty\text{-}outcome}\).  The instruction to run sIDP in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\text{-}scidp}\) invokes the published algorithm, including the continuous-version choice at every Rule L2 application required in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}sidp\text{-}soundness}\).  That lemma, with its positive-discrete specialization licensed by the cited Remark 4.4(2), therefore gives, pointwise for every \(y'\in\mathcal X_Y(\mathbf M)\),
\[
 F_\rho(y',\bar c;\bar x;P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M})
 =J_{\rho,\mathbf M}(y',\bar c;\bar x).
 \tag{7}
\]
This conclusion is on the full published class \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\), not merely on the rational binary certificate subclass.  The events indexed by \(y'\in\mathcal X_Y(\mathbf M)\) are disjoint and exhaust the outcome assignment space.  That space is at most countable, as shown above, and the summands in (7) are nonnegative point masses.  Countable additivity of the selected interventional probability measure and the definition of \(D_{\rho,\mathbf M}\) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{y'\in\mathcal X_Y(\mathbf M)}
 F_\rho(y',\bar c;\bar x;P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M})
 &=\sum_{y'\in\mathcal X_Y(\mathbf M)}
 J_{\rho,\mathbf M}(y',\bar c;\bar x)\\
 &=P_{\mathbf M}\!\left(
 \bar C=\bar c\mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X=\bar x),S=\mathbf 1\right)\\
 &=D_{\rho,\mathbf M}(\bar c;\bar x)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The last inequality follows by dividing the positive quantity in (2) by the positive selected-intervention normalizer established immediately after (2).  Dividing (7) by (8), applying the definition of conditional probability, and then applying (6), we obtain
\[
 \frac{F_\rho(y,\bar c;\bar x;P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M})}
 {\sum_{y'\in\mathcal X_Y(\mathbf M)}
 F_\rho(y',\bar c;\bar x;P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M})}
 =K_{\rho,\mathbf M}(y)
 =\psi_{\mathbf M}(y;x,c).
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus the normalized returned expression is an observed-law identifying map for the causal target.  Its equality with the target has been derived from Rule--2 invariance, countable additivity, positivity, and sIDP soundness, rather than imposed by definition.

It remains to prove the graph-search and cache bounds.  A role state is uniquely determined by \(\bar X\subseteq W\), since \(\bar C=W\setminus\bar X\).  Hence
\[
 |\Omega(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)|\leq 2^{|W|}=2^{|X\cup C|}.
 \tag{10}
\]
A trace with no repeated state and \(r\) visited states has \(r-1\) edges, so (10) gives the asserted upper bound \(2^{|X\cup C|}-1\) on its length.

At each visited state, the outer enumeration tests at most two directions for each of at most \(n\) buckets.  Each manipulated graph has size polynomial in \(n\); the displayed Rule--2 separation is decided by reachability in its finite graph of admissible edge-incidence states, while bucket formation, anterior closure, and region formation are also graph searches of polynomial size.  Since \(|X\cup C|\leq n\), all outer role-state work is therefore
\[
 O\!\left(2^n\operatorname{poly}(n)\right).
 \tag{11}
\]
By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}sidp\text{-}recursion\text{-}structure}\), a deterministic region-decomposition subproblem is keyed by one region \(R\subseteq V\), and a fixing subproblem is keyed by one pair \(R\subseteq T_0\subseteq V\).  There are \(2^n\) keys of the first kind and
\[
 \#\{(R,T_0):R\subseteq T_0\subseteq V\}
 =\sum_{T_0\subseteq V}2^{|T_0|}
 =(1+2)^n=3^n
 \tag{12}
\]
keys of the second kind.  The last equality follows by assigning each vertex independently to \(R\), to \(T_0\setminus R\), or to \(V\setminus T_0\).  The same recursion lemma states that region decomposition descends only to proper region children and that every successful fixing iteration removes a nonempty bucket.  Therefore each deterministic key terminates.  Under Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}shared\text{-}formula\text{-}cache}\), it is evaluated at most once and incurs only \(\operatorname{poly}(n)\) graph work.  Equations (11) and (12) thus bound all cached graph computation by
\[
 O\!\left(3^n\operatorname{poly}(n)\right).
 \tag{13}
\]
Each product, quotient, and assembly operation contributes a constant-arity node to the shared formula DAG.  A marginalization over a discrete support is represented by one symbolic summation-binder node whose body and support expression point to cached nodes; it is not expanded into one node per support point.  Thus allowing an at most countable discrete support does not change the graph-search count in (13).  Constructing and emitting all formula nodes, including each such discrete-support summation binder, costs \(O(L_{\mathrm{form}})\) by the definition of \(L_{\mathrm{form}}\).  Combining this output cost with (13) proves
\[
 O\!\left(3^n\operatorname{poly}(n)+L_{\mathrm{form}}\right).
\]
The proof invoked sIDP soundness only after a terminal call succeeded.  It therefore gives no implication from \(\operatorname{AllFail}\) to nonidentification, exactly as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exhaustive legal exchanges preserve the target and isolate the converse difficulty without sacrificing a usable positive identification procedure. \textit{Gap.} Chen--Mooij prove soundness for their conditional procedure; the branch closure and shared-cache accounting are not stated as this explicit formula-search theorem. \textit{Consumer.} PAGId can return selected-cohort formulas with a finite auditable role trace even before a complete failure theorem is available.

\begin{theorem}[thm:affine-lock-compiler]\label{thm:affine-lock-compiler}
For every supplied rational interior one-mechanism affine family, the following are equivalent:
\[
 \exists q\in\mathbb Q^{\dim(T)}:\ E_Tq=0\ \text{ and }\ b_Tq\neq0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}E_T\\ b_T\end{bmatrix}>
 \operatorname{rank}(E_T).
\]
This condition is independent of the rational basis \(R_T\).  Whenever it holds, rational row reduction yields \(v=R_Tq\), and there is a computable \(\delta\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) such that every \(t\in\mathbb Q\) with \(0<|t|<\delta\) keeps \(\theta+t v\) normalized and strictly positive, keeps \(\mathbf M_0=\mathbf M(0)\) and \(\mathbf M_1=\mathbf M(t)\) in the same represented positive rational binary model class, and gives
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M_0}=P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M_1},\qquad
 D_{\rho,\mathbf M_0}=D_{\rho,\mathbf M_1}>0,
 \qquad N_{\rho,\mathbf M_0}\neq N_{\rho,\mathbf M_1}.
\]
Conditional on \(\pi\) being the supplied displayed legal trace in \(\Omega(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)\) from \((X,C)\) to \(\rho\), with each state using the restrictions of \((x,c)\), Rule--2 transfer gives \(\psi_{\mathbf M_0}(y;x,c)\neq\psi_{\mathbf M_1}(y;x,c)\).  Thus \(\operatorname{LockCompile}\) returns \(\Gamma\), which certifies nonidentification of \(\psi\), in \(\operatorname{poly}(L_{\mathrm{temp}}+L_{\Gamma})\) bit operations, and \(\operatorname{Verify}(\mathcal P;Y,X,C,\Gamma)\) checks the expanded certificate in \(\operatorname{poly}(|\mathcal P|+L_{\Gamma})\) bit operations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work with the post-change construction in \texttt{def:affine-mechanism-family}.  Thus the supplied data include a terminal state \(\rho=(\bar X,\bar C)\), a displayed trace
\[
 \pi:\rho_0=(X,C)\longrightarrow\rho_1\longrightarrow\cdots
 \longrightarrow\rho_m=\rho                                      \tag{1}
\]
in \(\Omega(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)\), the restrictions of the assignment \((x,c)\) at every state, represented graphs \(\mathcal M,\mathcal G\), and nested forests satisfying the terminal selected-hedge predicates for the entire intervention \(\bar X\).  No role-trace datum will be inferred from a forest.  The algebraic part of the proof uses only the supplied affine family; the transfer to \(\psi\) will use exactly (1).

By \texttt{def:rational-binary-class}, \texttt{ass:binary-certificate-support}, and \texttt{ass:rational-interior-mechanisms}, the base model \(\mathbf M_{\mathrm{base}}\) has finite binary state spaces and rational local stochastic-mechanism entries in \((0,1)\).  Fix either the observational regime or the terminal regime \(\operatorname{do}(\bar X=\bar x)\), and fix a complete assignment \(\omega\) of its nonintervened endogenous and latent variables.  Its truncated factorization contains the varied local table at most once.  If that factor is retained and its coordinate at \(\omega\) is \(j(\omega)\), then
\[
 p_t(\omega)
 =\bigl(\theta_{j(\omega)}+t v_{j(\omega)}\bigr)
   \prod_{\ell\ne j(\omega)}p_\ell(\omega)
 =p_0(\omega)+t\dot p_v(\omega).                              \tag{2}
\]
If the governed variable is intervened upon, truncation removes that factor and \(p_t(\omega)=p_0(\omega)\).  The full assignment spaces are finite, so marginalizing (2) gives rational linear forms in \(v\), denoted by dots, for which
\[
\begin{aligned}
 o_z(t)&=o_z(0)+t\dot o_z(v),&
 Z_o(t)&=Z_o(0)+t\dot Z_o(v),\\
 h(t)&=h(0)+t\dot h(v),&
 k(t)&=k(0)+t\dot k(v),&
 Z_x(t)&=Z_x(0)+t\dot Z_x(v).
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{3}
\]
Thus these identities are exact for finite \(t\), rather than first-order approximations.

At \(t=0\), strict positivity of every local factor implies strict positivity of every complete-assignment mass compatible with an intervention.  Since \(S=\mathbf 1\) and the assigned value \(\bar C=\bar c\) are in their binary supports, the relevant finite sums satisfy
\[
 Z_o(0)>0,\qquad Z_x(0)>0,\qquad h(0)>0.                       \tag{4}
\]
The same statements hold at every perturbation for which all local entries remain strictly positive.  By the definitions of \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(D_\rho\), and \(N_\rho\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M(t)}(z)=\frac{o_z(t)}{Z_o(t)},\qquad
 D_{\rho,\mathbf M(t)}(\bar c;\bar x)=\frac{h(t)}{Z_x(t)},
 \qquad
 N_{\rho,\mathbf M(t)}(y,\bar c;\bar x)=\frac{k(t)}{Z_x(t)}. \tag{5}
\]
Substitution of (3) into (5), followed by exact cross-multiplication, yields
\[
 \frac{o_z(t)}{Z_o(t)}-\frac{o_z(0)}{Z_o(0)}
 =\frac{t\{Z_o(0)\dot o_z(v)-o_z(0)\dot Z_o(v)\}}
        {Z_o(t)Z_o(0)},                                        \tag{6}
\]
\[
 D_{\rho,\mathbf M(t)}-D_{\rho,\mathbf M(0)}
 =\frac{t\{Z_x(0)\dot h(v)-h(0)\dot Z_x(v)\}}
        {Z_x(t)Z_x(0)},                                        \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 N_{\rho,\mathbf M(t)}-N_{\rho,\mathbf M(0)}
 =\frac{t\{Z_x(0)\dot k(v)-k(0)\dot Z_x(v)\}}
        {Z_x(t)Z_x(0)}.                                        \tag{8}
\]
The products of the two slopes cancel in each numerator.  Consequently (6)--(8) are exact identities for every admissible \(t\).  By \texttt{def:affine-rank-system}, after writing \(v=R_Tq\), the braces in all instances of (6), followed by the brace in (7), are the coordinates of \(E_Tq\), while the brace in (8) is \(b_Tq\).

We next establish the rank criterion over \(\mathbb Q\).  If \(b_T\) belongs to the row space of \(E_T\), then \(b_Tq=0\) for every \(q\in\ker(E_T)\).  Conversely, suppose that \(b_Tq=0\) for every \(q\in\ker(E_T)\).  The prescription
\[
 \ell(E_Tq)=b_Tq                                                \tag{9}
\]
is then a well-defined rational linear functional on \(\operatorname{im}(E_T)\), because two preimages differ by an element of \(\ker(E_T)\).  Extend a rational basis of \(\operatorname{im}(E_T)\) to one of its codomain and extend \(\ell\) arbitrarily on the added basis vectors.  Equation (9) then gives \(b_T=\ell E_T\), so \(b_T\) lies in the row space of \(E_T\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\exists q\in\mathbb Q^{\dim(T)}:\ E_Tq=0\ \text{and}\ b_Tq\ne0\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow b_T\notin\operatorname{row}(E_T)
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}E_T\\ b_T\end{bmatrix}
 >\operatorname{rank}(E_T).                                    \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
Exact rational row reduction decides (10) and, in the strict-rank case, returns a rational null vector \(q\) with \(b_Tq\ne0\).

Let \(R_T'\) be another rational basis matrix for \(T\).  There is an invertible rational matrix \(H\) such that \(R_T'=R_TH\).  Therefore
\[
 ER_T'=E_TH,\qquad bR_T'=b_TH.                                  \tag{11}
\]
Right multiplication by \(H\) preserves each rank in (10).  Moreover, \(q'=H^{-1}q\) represents the same mechanism direction, since \(R_T'q'=R_Tq\).  This proves basis independence.

Assume (10), take the returned \(q\), and put \(v=R_Tq\).  Since \(b_Tq=bv\ne0\), one has \(v\ne0\).  Define
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{pos}}
 =\frac12\min_{j:v_j\ne0}
 \left\{\frac{\theta_j}{|v_j|},
              \frac{1-\theta_j}{|v_j|}\right\}.               \tag{12}
\]
This is a positive rational number.  Membership \(v\in T\) says that the coordinates of \(v\) sum to zero in every probability block.  Hence \(\theta+t v\) stays normalized, and (12) gives \(0<\theta_j+t v_j<1\) for every coordinate whenever \(|t|<\delta_{\mathrm{pos}}\).

We also check represented-class membership.  The corrected affine-family construction fixes the state spaces, parent indexing, directed and latent incidences, selection vertices, and all unvaried local tables.  Thus no perturbation can create an incidence not displayed by \(\mathcal G\).  If representation permits displayed incidences with inactive numerical effects, this already proves compatibility for every positive perturbation.  Under the minimal active-incidence convention, let \(\mathcal A\) be the finite set of displayed incidences whose activity is witnessed in the varied table.  Because \(\mathbf M_{\mathrm{base}}\) realizes \(\mathcal G\), for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) there are two coordinates \(j_a,j_a'\), differing only in the argument indexed by that incidence, such that
\[
 \alpha_a=\theta_{j_a}-\theta_{j_a'}\ne0.                     \tag{13}
\]
Put \(\beta_a=v_{j_a}-v_{j_a'}\) and define
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{rep}}
 =\begin{cases}
 1,&\text{if }\beta_a=0\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal A,\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle\min\!\left\{1,
   \frac12\min_{a:\beta_a\ne0}\frac{|\alpha_a|}{|\beta_a|}\right\},
 &\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{14}
\]
This number is positive and rational.  If \(|t|<\delta_{\mathrm{rep}}\), then for every \(a\) with \(\beta_a\ne0\),
\[
 |\alpha_a+t\beta_a|
 \ge |\alpha_a|-|t|\,|\beta_a|
 >\frac12|\alpha_a|>0,                                        \tag{15}
\]
while a contrast with \(\beta_a=0\) is unchanged.  Thus every required active incidence remains active.  All other structural and latent incidences were fixed by construction, so the same represented realization is retained.

Set
\[
 \delta=\min\{\delta_{\mathrm{pos}},\delta_{\mathrm{rep}}\}. \tag{16}
\]
Equations (12)--(16) make \(\delta\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) exactly computable.  For each rational \(t\) with \(0<|t|<\delta\), all changed entries are rational, normalized, and strictly positive, and the binary state spaces and represented realization are unchanged.  Therefore, with \(\mathbf M_0=\mathbf M(0)\) and \(\mathbf M_1=\mathbf M(t)\),
\[
 \mathbf M_0,\mathbf M_1
 \in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{rat,bin}}^+(\mathcal P)
 \subseteq\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P).                         \tag{17}
\]

All denominators in (6)--(8) are positive by (4), (12), and (17).  Since \(E_Tq=0\), equations (6)--(7) give, for every full observed cell \(z\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M_0}(z)=P_{\mathrm{obs},\mathbf M_1}(z),
 \qquad
 D_{\rho,\mathbf M_0}(\bar c;\bar x)
 =D_{\rho,\mathbf M_1}(\bar c;\bar x)>0.                     \tag{18}
\]
Thus the normalized selected observational law and the selected terminal denominator are both preserved exactly.  Since \(b_Tq\ne0\) and \(t\ne0\), equation (8) gives
\[
 N_{\rho,\mathbf M_0}(y,\bar c;\bar x)
 \ne N_{\rho,\mathbf M_1}(y,\bar c;\bar x).                 \tag{19}
\]
Combining the common positive denominator in (18) with (19) yields
\[
 \frac{N_{\rho,\mathbf M_0}(y,\bar c;\bar x)}
      {D_{\rho,\mathbf M_0}(\bar c;\bar x)}
 \ne
 \frac{N_{\rho,\mathbf M_1}(y,\bar c;\bar x)}
      {D_{\rho,\mathbf M_1}(\bar c;\bar x)}.                 \tag{20}
\]

We now use the explicit trace (1), which is the stated condition for transferring (20) to the original query.  Write \(\rho_i=(\bar X_i,\bar C_i)\).  If the edge from \(\rho_i\) to \(\rho_{i+1}\) moves a nonempty set \(B_i\) from intervention to conditioning, then
\[
 \bar X_i=B_i\mathbin{\dot\cup}\bar X_{i+1},
 \qquad
 \bar C_{i+1}=B_i\mathbin{\dot\cup}\bar C_i.                \tag{21}
\]
Apply \texttt{lem:selected-rule-two} with \(A_0=Y\), \(B_0=B_i\), \(C_0=\bar C_i\), and \(D_0=\bar X_{i+1}\).  These four sets are pairwise disjoint because each role state partitions \(W=X\mathbin{\dot\cup}C\) and \texttt{ass:disjoint-query-sets} gives \(Y\cap W=\varnothing\).  The required Rule--2 separation is exactly the legality label of the edge in (1), and its positive-assignment premise follows from (17).  Using the inherited value restrictions specified in \texttt{def:affine-mechanism-family}, the lemma gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &P_{\mathbf M_j}\!\left(Y=y\mid
       \operatorname{do}(\bar X_i=\bar x_i),
       \bar C_i=\bar c_i,S=\mathbf1\right)\\
 &\quad=P_{\mathbf M_j}\!\left(Y=y\mid
       \operatorname{do}(\bar X_{i+1}=\bar x_{i+1}),
       \bar C_{i+1}=\bar c_{i+1},S=\mathbf1\right),
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.                                         \tag{22}
 \end{aligned}
\]
A reverse move is the same equality read from right to left.  Induction over the finite displayed trace, and then the definitions of \(D_\rho\), \(N_\rho\), and \(\psi\), give
\[
 \frac{N_{\rho,\mathbf M_j}(y,\bar c;\bar x)}
      {D_{\rho,\mathbf M_j}(\bar c;\bar x)}
 =P_{\mathbf M_j}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x),C=c,S=\mathbf1)
 =\psi_{\mathbf M_j}(y;x,c),
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.                                         \tag{23}
\]
Equations (18), (20), and (23) exhibit two members of the published class with the same \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) but different \(\psi\).  By \texttt{def:identification}, this proves nonidentification over \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\).  This is only the valid negative transfer from the subclass inclusion in (17); it asserts no positive completeness transfer.  The post-change \texttt{def:lock-compiler} copies \(\rho\), the actual trace (1), its assignment restrictions, the represented graph data, and the forests for the entire terminal intervention into \(\Gamma\).  Together with (17)--(19) and the expanded exact tables, these are precisely the certificate data in \texttt{def:locked-certificate}.

It remains to prove the bit-complexity assertions.  Clear the encoded rational denominators of \(E_T\) and \(b_T\), and perform pivoted fraction-free elimination.  With a nonzero pivot, an uneliminated entry is updated by
\[
 a_{ij}^{(r+1)}
 =\frac{a_{rr}^{(r)}a_{ij}^{(r)}-a_{ir}^{(r)}a_{rj}^{(r)}}
        {a_{r-1,r-1}^{(r-1)}},                                 \tag{24}
\]
where the initial previous pivot is one; row and column permutations provide a nonzero pivot whenever one exists.  Expanding the determinants in (24) around the pivot shows inductively that the division is exact and that each stored nonzero integer is, up to sign, a minor of the integerized input.  If the matrix dimensions are at most \(s\) and its entries have bit length at most \(B\), the Leibniz formula bounds an \(r\)-by-\(r\) minor by \(r!2^{rB}\), and hence bounds its bit length by
\[
 rB+\lceil\log_2(r!)\rceil+1
 =O\!\left(s(B+\log s)\right).                               \tag{25}
\]
There are polynomially many updates, and (25) bounds all intermediate bit lengths polynomially in the encoded matrix length.  Back-substitution performs polynomially many rational operations at those bit lengths and returns \(q\).  Scanning the finite varied table finds the activity witnesses in (13), after which (12), (14), (16), and a choice such as \(t=\delta/2\) use polynomially many exact rational operations.  Thus the ranks, \(q\), \(v\), \(\delta\), and \(t\) are computed in \(\operatorname{poly}(L_{\mathrm{temp}})\) bit operations.

The compiler copies the already supplied finite graph, trace, assignment, and forest data, and expands the two model evaluations.  Every complete-assignment atom is a finite product of encoded rational local entries, and every reported marginal is a finite exact sum of such atoms.  The dense atoms and arithmetic trace are part of the output measured by \(L_\Gamma\); sequential multiplication and addition have operand lengths polynomial in the total input-plus-output length.  Construction and writing therefore take \(\operatorname{poly}(L_{\mathrm{temp}}+L_\Gamma)\) bit operations.

Finally, the verifier checks endpoint compatibility, acyclicity, ancestry, the displayed latent projection, legality of every edge of \(\pi\), and the nested rooted-forest predicates by adjacency scans, separation tests, and directed-reachability searches on the displayed graphs.  These operations are polynomial in the displayed graph data.  If equivalence-class representation is instead checked by exhaustive equality of separation models, there are at most \(4^n\) disjoint vertex triples, whereas the dense binary observational table in \(\Gamma\) has at least \(2^n\) cells; hence \(4^n\le L_\Gamma^2\), so this check is still polynomial in the expanded certificate length.  The verifier also checks exact normalization and strict positivity of every rational block, the retained activity contrasts in (13)--(15), the SCM factorization of every dense atom, the inherited assignments along \(\pi\), and the equalities and inequality in (18)--(19) by exact cross-multiplication.  The number of operands and their bit lengths are bounded by \(|\mathcal P|+L_\Gamma\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Verify}(\mathcal P;Y,X,C,\Gamma)
 \quad\text{runs in}\quad
 \operatorname{poly}(|\mathcal P|+L_\Gamma)
\]
bit operations, completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem turns a numerical or symbolic fiber direction into an exact, portable, positive rational causal nonidentification certificate. \textit{Gap.} Discrete polynomial programs describe observational fibers, but the checked literature does not isolate an exact simplex-tangent criterion that simultaneously locks a selected conditional denominator and separates its numerator. \textit{Consumer.} A graph search or algebraic solver can hand one passing template to the compiler and obtain a certificate that a third party verifies without trusting the search.

\begin{proposition}[prop:informative-lock-witness]\label{prop:informative-lock-witness}
The mechanisms in \(\text{def:informative-lock-template}\) are strictly positive and rational and induce \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{inf}}\), whose latent projections are \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{inf}}\) and \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{inf}}=X_i-Y_i-C_i\), with \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}\in\operatorname{COPAG}_0(\{X_i,Y_i,C_i\})\), \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{inf}}\in[\mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}]_{\mathsf M}\), and \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{inf}}\in[\mathcal M_{\mathrm{inf}}]_{\mathsf G}\). Both models therefore belong to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{rat,bin}}^+(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}})\). Moreover \(P_{\sigma}(U_i=1\mid X_i=a)=r_a\) and \(P_{\sigma}(Y_i=1\mid X_i=a)=1/2\), while \(P(C_i=d,S_i=1\mid Y_i=b)=3/16\) for every \(b,d\in\{0,1\}\). Hence \(P_{\sigma}(X_i=a,Y_i=b,C_i=d,S_i=1)=3/64\), \(P_{\sigma}(S_i=1)=3/8\), and the two full selected observational laws assign probability \(1/8\) to every observed cell. Under \(\operatorname{do}(X_i=0)\), \(P_+(Y_i=1)=9/16\), \(P_-(Y_i=1)=7/16\), and both selection normalizers equal \(3/8\). At the root role state, \(D_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},+}}(1;0)=D_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},-}}(1;0)=1/2\), whereas \(N_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},+}}(1,1;0)=9/32\) and \(N_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},-}}(1,1;0)=7/32\), so the conditional effects are \(9/16\) and \(7/16\). The connected \(C_i\) is informative before selection because \(P(C_i=1\mid Y_i=1)=3/4\neq1/4=P(C_i=1\mid Y_i=0)\). The pair \((F_{\mathrm{inf}},F'_{\mathrm{inf}})\) consists of nested \(R_{\mathrm{inf}}\)-rooted C-forests, with \(V(F_{\mathrm{inf}})\cap\{X_i\}=\{X_i\}\), \(V(F'_{\mathrm{inf}})\cap\{X_i\}=\varnothing\), and \(R_{\mathrm{inf}}\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{\mathcal G_{\mathrm{inf}},\operatorname{do}(X_i)}(\{Y_i,C_i,S_i\})\); \(D_{\max,\mathrm{inf}}=\varnothing\), so they form the terminal selected hedge for \((\{Y_i,C_i,S_i\},\{X_i\})\). Finally, replacing only \(P(S_i=1\mid Y_i=1,C_i=1)\) by \(1/2\) preserves the graph and equality of the two selected observational laws but yields denominators \(25/41\) and \(23/39\); therefore the displayed denominator equality is a tuned informative cancellation, not a structural consequence of the graph.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All variables in the informative-lock template are binary.  The displayed local success probabilities and their Bernoulli complements belong to
\[
 \left\{\frac14,\frac12,\frac34,
              \frac5{16},\frac7{16},\frac9{16},\frac{11}{16}\right\}.
\]
Thus every local-table entry is rational and strictly between zero and one, as required by \(\text{ass:rational-interior-mechanisms}\), and binary support is supplied by \(\text{ass:binary-certificate-support}\).  The conditional law of \(X_i\) changes with \(U_i\), since
\[
 P(X_i=1\mid U_i=1)-P(X_i=1\mid U_i=0)=\frac12.
\]
For either \(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\), the outcome table in \(\text{def:informative-lock-template}\) satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &P_\sigma(Y_i=1\mid X_i=a,U_i=1)
   -P_\sigma(Y_i=1\mid X_i=a,U_i=0)=\frac{\sigma}{4},\\
 &P_\sigma(Y_i=1\mid X_i=1,U_i=u)
   -P_\sigma(Y_i=1\mid X_i=0,U_i=u)=-\frac{\sigma}{8}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Hence both \(U_i\) and \(X_i\) are active parents of \(Y_i\).  Likewise,
\[
 P(C_i=1\mid Y_i=1)-P(C_i=1\mid Y_i=0)=\frac12,
\]
and the selection success probability changes from \(1/4\) to \(3/4\) when either \(Y_i\) or \(C_i\) is changed while the other coordinate is fixed.  Thus every displayed parent is active, and the induced directed graph is exactly \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{inf}}\).

We next check the two projections in \(\text{def:informative-graph-certificate}\).  Marginalizing the latent vertex \(U_i\) preserves \(X_i\to Y_i\) and induces \(X_i\leftrightarrow Y_i\) from \(X_i\leftarrow U_i\to Y_i\); the arrows \(Y_i\to C_i\), \(Y_i\to S_i\), and \(C_i\to S_i\) remain.  Every path capable of connecting another nonadjacent pair has an observed nonendpoint noncollider, so it creates no further latent-projection edge.  The selected acyclic directed mixed graph is therefore precisely \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{inf}}\).

For the selected latent projection onto \(\{X_i,Y_i,C_i\}\), all three observed vertices are ancestors of \(S_i\), so the endpoint marks on the adjacencies \(X_i-Y_i\) and \(Y_i-C_i\) are tails.  There is no \(X_i-C_i\) adjacency: every path from \(X_i\) to \(C_i\) has either \(Y_i\) as an observed nonendpoint noncollider or fails the inducing-path criterion, equivalently, in the relevant ancestral moral graph, conditioning on and removing \(Y_i\) and \(S_i\) disconnects \(C_i\) from the component containing \(X_i\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{inf}}=X_i-Y_i-C_i.                 \tag{1}
\]
The skeleton in (1) has the sole conditional independence \(X_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}C_i\mid Y_i\), and its middle vertex is a noncollider.  The directed chains and fork obtained by orienting this skeleton without the collider \(X_i\to Y_i\leftarrow C_i\) have the same separation relations; across those representatives each endpoint mark takes both possible orientations.  Thus no endpoint mark is invariant, and the input-free completed partial graph is
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}
 =X_i\mathbin{\circ\!-\!\circ}Y_i
       \mathbin{\circ\!-\!\circ}C_i.
\]
These direct projection and equivalence-class checks give
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}\in\operatorname{COPAG}_0(\{X_i,Y_i,C_i\}),
 \qquad \mathcal M_{\mathrm{inf}}\in[\mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}]_{\mathsf M},
 \qquad \mathcal G_{\mathrm{inf}}\in[\mathcal M_{\mathrm{inf}}]_{\mathsf G}. \tag{2}
\]
By (2), each constructed positive discrete SCM is represented by \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}\).  Since every endogenous and latent variable is binary and every local entry is rational and strictly positive, \(\text{def:rational-binary-class}\) yields
\[
 \mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},+},\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},-}
 \in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{rat,bin}}^+(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{inf}}). \tag{3}
\]

We now compute the observational and interventional laws.  Since \(P(U_i=1)=1/2\), the treatment marginals are positive and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(X_i=1)&=\tfrac12\tfrac14+\tfrac12\tfrac34=\tfrac12,
 &P(U_i=1\mid X_i=1)&=\frac{(1/2)(3/4)}{1/2}=\tfrac34=r_1,\\
 P(X_i=0)&=\tfrac12\tfrac34+\tfrac12\tfrac14=\tfrac12,
 &P(U_i=1\mid X_i=0)&=\frac{(1/2)(1/4)}{1/2}=\tfrac14=r_0.
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{4}
\]
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the law of total probability and the outcome table in \(\text{def:informative-lock-template}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_\sigma(Y_i=1\mid X_i=a)
 &=\sum_{u=0}^1
   \left\{\frac12+\frac{\sigma}{4}(u-r_a)\right\}
   P(U_i=u\mid X_i=a)\\
 &=\frac12+\frac{\sigma}{4}
   \{P(U_i=1\mid X_i=a)-r_a\}=\frac12,                 \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality uses (4).  Therefore both signs have the same unselected observed law and
\[
 P_\sigma(X_i=a,Y_i=b)=P(X_i=a)P_\sigma(Y_i=b\mid X_i=a)=\frac14
 \quad\text{for every }a,b\in\{0,1\}.                  \tag{6}
\]
Under \(\operatorname{do}(X_i=0)\), SCM modularity removes the local factor for \(X_i\) and leaves \(U_i\) fair.  Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_+(Y_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))
 &=\frac12+\frac14\left(\frac12-\frac14\right)=\frac9{16},\\
 P_-(Y_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))
 &=\frac12-\frac14\left(\frac12-\frac14\right)=\frac7{16}. \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]

The appended \(C_i,S_i\) tables instantiate \(\text{lem:selection-equalizer-lift}\).  Namely, define
\[
 g_b(d):=P(C_i=d\mid Y_i=b),
 \qquad \lambda_0=\lambda_1:=\frac3{16},
 \qquad \Lambda:=\lambda_0+\lambda_1=\frac38.          \tag{8}
\]
For every \(b,d\in\{0,1\}\), direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 (b,d)&(0,0)&(0,1)&(1,0)&(1,1)\\ \hline
 g_b(d)P(S_i=1\mid Y_i=b,C_i=d)
 &(3/4)(1/4)&(1/4)(3/4)&(1/4)(3/4)&(3/4)(1/4)
\end{array}
=\frac3{16}.                                             \tag{9}
\]
Moreover \(0<3/16<1/4=\min_b g_b(d)\) for both \(d\), so every positivity premise of that lemma holds.  Apply \(\text{lem:selection-equalizer-lift}\) with \(\mathcal W=(X_i,Y_i)\), \(\mathcal X=X_i\), and \(\mathcal Y=Y_i\).  The two base models are finite, have binary endogenous and latent variables, and have positive rational tables; equality of their observational laws is (6), and their interventional outcome probabilities differ by (7).  The map \(b\mapsto g_b(\cdot)\) is nonconstant, and for either fixed value of \(b\), the map \(d\mapsto\lambda_d/g_b(d)\) is nonconstant.  Thus the lemma's graph qualification holds, as was also checked directly above.  Applying its observational identity to (6) yields
\[
 P_\sigma(X_i=a,Y_i=b,C_i=d,S_i=1)
 =P_\sigma(X_i=a,Y_i=b)\lambda_d
 =\frac14\frac3{16}=\frac3{64}.                         \tag{10}
\]
There are eight observed cells, so finite additivity in (10) gives \(P_\sigma(S_i=1)=8(3/64)=3/8>0\).  Division by this positive normalizer gives
\[
 P_\sigma(X_i=a,Y_i=b,C_i=d\mid S_i=1)
 =\frac{3/64}{3/8}=\frac18.                              \tag{11}
\]
Thus the two complete selected observational laws coincide and have full support.

The interventional identities in \(\text{lem:selection-equalizer-lift}\), together with (7)--(8), give the common terminal selection normalizer \(3/8\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},\sigma}}(1;0)
 &=P_\sigma(C_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0),S_i=1)
   =\frac{\lambda_1}{\Lambda}=\frac12,\\
 N_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},+}}(1,1;0)
 &=P_+(Y_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))
   \frac{\lambda_1}{\Lambda}=\frac9{32},\\
 N_{\rho,\mathbf M_{\mathrm{inf},-}}(1,1;0)
 &=P_-(Y_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))
   \frac{\lambda_1}{\Lambda}=\frac7{32}.              \tag{12}
\end{aligned}
\]
The common conditioning denominator in (12) is strictly positive.  By the definition of conditional probability, division of each selected joint numerator by that denominator gives conditional causal effects \(9/16\) and \(7/16\), respectively.  The common \(C_i\) table also gives
\[
 P(C_i=1\mid Y_i=1)=\frac34\ne\frac14
 =P(C_i=1\mid Y_i=0),                                   \tag{13}
\]
so the connected conditioning vertex is informative before selection.

It remains to verify the terminal forest assertions in \(\text{def:informative-graph-certificate}\).  The graph \(F_{\mathrm{inf}}\) is bidirected-connected, every vertex has at most one directed child, and its unique childless root is \(Y_i\).  The singleton \(F'_{\mathrm{inf}}\) is a proper C-subforest with the same root.  Also
\[
 V(F_{\mathrm{inf}})\cap\{X_i\}=\{X_i\},
 \qquad
 V(F'_{\mathrm{inf}})\cap\{X_i\}=\varnothing.          \tag{14}
\]
After intervention on \(X_i\), the root \(Y_i\) belongs to the terminal set and has directed paths to \(C_i\) and \(S_i\).  Hence
\[
 R_{\mathrm{inf}}=\{Y_i\}
 \subseteq\operatorname{An}_{\mathcal G_{\mathrm{inf}},\operatorname{do}(X_i)}
 (\{Y_i,C_i,S_i\}).                                     \tag{15}
\]
For the maximal selection-exchange coordinate, \(S=\{S_i\}\), so the only nonempty candidate is \(D=\{S_i\}\).  In the corresponding input-augmented graph, the path
\[
 I_{S_i}\longrightarrow S_i\longleftarrow C_i           \tag{16}
\]
is open when conditioning on \(S_i\): its only internal vertex is a conditioned collider, and it has no noncollider at which the path could be blocked.  Thus \(I_{S_i}\) and \(C_i\) fail the required id-separation, making \(\{S_i\}\) inadmissible.  The empty set is admissible vacuously and is the only remaining subset.  Consequently
\[
 D_{\max,\mathrm{inf}}=\varnothing.                     \tag{17}
\]
The bidirected-connectivity, common-root, treatment-intersection, ancestry, and maximal-exchange checks (14)--(17) are exactly the terminal selected-hedge predicates named in \(\text{def:informative-graph-certificate}\).  Thus \((F_{\mathrm{inf}},F'_{\mathrm{inf}})\) is the asserted terminal selected hedge for \((\{Y_i,C_i,S_i\},\{X_i\})\).

Finally, consider the same-graph alternative \(\widetilde P\), obtained by changing only \(P(S_i=1\mid Y_i=1,C_i=1)\) from \(1/4\) to \(1/2\), as specified by \(\widetilde P\).  The altered success probability and its complement are strictly positive rational numbers.  At fixed \(C_i=0\), the selection table still changes with \(Y_i\), and at fixed \(Y_i=0\), it still changes with \(C_i\); hence both arrows into \(S_i\) remain active and the represented graph is unchanged.  Equations (4)--(6) are unaffected, so the two signs retain the same unselected observed law.  Their \(C_i\) and modified \(S_i\) mechanisms are common; factorization therefore gives equal unnormalized selected observational masses cell by cell, and normalization by the same positive total gives equal full selected observational laws.

Put \(p=P_\sigma(Y_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))\).  When \(Y_i=0\), summing the two unchanged products in (9) gives \(P(S_i=1\mid Y_i=0)=3/8\).  When \(Y_i=1\), the \(C_i=0\) product remains \(3/16\), whereas the modified \(C_i=1\) product is \((3/4)(1/2)=3/8\); hence \(P(S_i=1\mid Y_i=1)=9/16\).  It follows from the law of total probability that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde P(S_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))
 &=(1-p)\frac38+p\frac9{16}=\frac{6+3p}{16},\\
 \widetilde P(C_i=1,S_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))
 &=(1-p)\frac3{16}+p\frac38=\frac{3+3p}{16}.            \tag{18}
\end{aligned}
\]
The first quantity in (18) is positive because \(0\le p\le1\).  Therefore the modified selected conditioning denominator is
\[
 \widetilde D_\sigma(1;0)
 =\frac{\widetilde P(C_i=1,S_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))}
        {\widetilde P(S_i=1\mid\operatorname{do}(X_i=0))}
 =\frac{3+3p}{6+3p}=\frac{1+p}{2+p}.                    \tag{19}
\]
Substituting \(p=9/16\) and \(p=7/16\) from (7) gives
\[
 \widetilde D_+=\frac{25}{41},
 \qquad
 \widetilde D_-=\frac{23}{39}.                         \tag{20}
\]
These fractions differ because \(25\cdot39=975\ne943=23\cdot41\).  Hence equality of the denominators in (12) is the tuned informative cancellation supplied by (8)--(9), rather than a structural consequence of the represented graph.  This proves every assertion of the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:no-selection-reduction]\label{prop:no-selection-reduction}
When \(S=\varnothing\), \(\operatorname{Branch\text{-}sCIDP}(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)\) and \(\operatorname{CIDP}(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)\) agree on the identification decision for
\[
 P_{\mathbf M}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x),C=c);
\]
that is, one returns a formula if and only if the other returns a formula.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(S=\varnothing\).  The selection event is then certain, so Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}target\text{-}query}\) gives
\[
 \psi_{\mathbf M}(y;x,c)
 =P_{\mathbf M}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x),C=c).
 \tag{1}
\]
The conditioning stratum in (1) is positive by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}positive\text{-}discrete\text{-}selected\text{-}model}\).

First suppose that Branch-sCIDP returns a formula.  By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}branch\text{-}soundness}\), that formula equals (1) for every \(\mathbf M\in\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\).  It is therefore an observed-law identifying functional for (1).  The completeness direction in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}cidp\text{-}no\text{-}selection\text{-}contract}\) says that CIDP cannot return \(\operatorname{FAIL}\) for an identifiable conditional effect.  Hence CIDP returns a formula.

Conversely, suppose that CIDP returns a formula.  By the transition contract in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}cidp\text{-}no\text{-}selection\text{-}contract}\), its successful execution produces a sequence
\[
 (X,C)=\rho_0\longrightarrow\rho_1\longrightarrow\cdots
 \longrightarrow\rho_k=(\bar X_\star,\bar C_\star),
 \tag{2}
\]
where every arrow moves a nonempty intersection of a current role set with a bucket and is taken only after the corresponding PAG Rule--2 separation test succeeds.  These are exactly the edge predicates in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}reachable\text{-}role\text{-}graph}\).  Consequently
\[
 \rho_\star=(\bar X_\star,\bar C_\star)
 \in\Omega(\mathcal P;Y,X,C).
 \tag{3}
\]
The same cited contract says that the final marginal-algorithm call returns an observed-law expression for
\[
 Q_{\rho_\star}
 :=P_{\mathbf M}(Y,\bar C_\star
       \mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X_\star)).
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus (4) is identified pointwise on the represented observational-law domain and, in particular, over its positive discrete subclass \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\).  This uses only the source-attested terminal-call contract; it does not assert equality between the output expressions of CIDP and Branch-sCIDP.

The hypotheses of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}sidp\text{-}completeness}\) apply to the terminal joint query with
\[
 A_0=Y\cup\bar C_\star,
 \qquad B_0=\bar X_\star.
\]
Indeed, \(A_0\neq\varnothing\) by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}nonempty\text{-}outcome}\).  Moreover, \(A_0\cap B_0=\varnothing\): Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}reachable\text{-}role\text{-}graph}\) keeps \(\bar X_\star\mathbin{\dot\cup}\bar C_\star=X\cup C\), while Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}disjoint\text{-}query\text{-}sets}\) gives \(Y\cap(X\cup C)=\varnothing\).  If the sIDP call at \(\rho_\star\) returned \(\operatorname{Fail}\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}sidp\text{-}completeness}\) would say that (4) is not identified over \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P)\), contradicting the preceding paragraph.  Hence that sIDP call succeeds.

By Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}branch\text{-}scidp}\), Branch-sCIDP enumerates every state in \(\Omega(\mathcal P;Y,X,C)\) and calls sIDP at each state.  In view of (3), it therefore succeeds either before reaching \(\rho_\star\) or at \(\rho_\star\).  Its discrete marginalization and normalization at any successful state are defined by positivity.  In particular, at \(\rho_\star\),
\[
 \sum_{y'}Q_{\rho_\star}(y',\bar c_\star;\bar x_\star)
 =P_{\mathbf M}(\bar C_\star=\bar c_\star
       \mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X_\star=\bar x_\star))>0.
\]
The summation is over the full discrete outcome support; when it is countably infinite, the displayed equality follows from countable additivity of nonnegative point masses.  In the boundary case \(\bar C_\star=\varnothing\), the sum equals one.  We have proved both implications between the two success decisions, which is the claimed equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The no-selection special case is the required regression against the settled conditional-PAG boundary. \textit{Gap.} This is a sanity reduction rather than a novelty claim; it rules out a selected extension that changes the established no-selection answer. \textit{Consumer.} PAGId retains its current CIDP behavior whenever no selection node is present.

 A_0\mathrel{\perp_{\mathsf{id}}}I_{B_0}\mid B_0\cup C_0\cup D_0
 \quad\text{in the graph manipulated by }\operatorname{do}(I_{B_0},D_0)

holds, then, pointwise at every positive discrete assignment,

\]
\end{lemma}

Suppose \(\operatorname{sIDP}(\mathcal P;A_0,B_0,P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) returns a proxy kernel \(H(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), with a continuous version chosen at every application of Rule L2 as required by Theorem 4.14(3). \begin{lemma}[lem:sidp-recursion-structure]\label{lem:sidp-recursion-structure}
For a fixed deterministic choice rule, sIDP first decomposes every requested observed region only into proper region children.  At a terminal region \(R\), its fixing routine has a current set \(T_0\supseteq R\) and every successful iteration removes a nonempty bucket from \(T_0\).  Consequently every graph-only recursive or fixing subproblem is determined by either a region \(R\subseteq V\) or a pair \(R\subseteq T_0\subseteq V\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the deterministic tie-breaking rule in the statement.  We prove the claim
by unfolding the BuildTree and AttachKernel routines that define sIDP.

Consider a call \(\operatorname{BuildTree}(R,\mathcal P)\).  If its splitting
guard is false, it returns the one-node tree labelled by \(R\).  If the guard
is true, it selects a nonempty \(\mathcal P_R\)-bucket \(B\) and sets
\[
 R_1=\operatorname{re}_{\mathcal P_R}(B),
 \qquad
 R_2=\operatorname{re}_{\mathcal P_R}(R\setminus R_1).
 \tag{14}
\]
The guard itself states \(R_1\subsetneq R\).  Both regions in (14) are subsets
of \(R\), because they are computed in the induced graph \(\mathcal P_R\).
They are nonempty: \(B\subseteq R_1\), while
\(R\setminus R_1\ne\varnothing\) and every vertex belongs to its own region.
We also have \(R_2\subsetneq R\).  Indeed, the elementary closure property in
the definition of a region gives
\[
 B\cap
 \operatorname{re}_{\mathcal P_R}
       \!\left(R\setminus\operatorname{re}_{\mathcal P_R}(B)\right)
 =\varnothing.
 \tag{15}
\]
To verify (15), if a vertex of \(B\) were in the displayed second region, a
pc-connecting path witnessing that membership, concatenated with the
no-arrowhead path witnessing membership in the bucket \(B\), would place its
starting vertex in \(\operatorname{re}_{\mathcal P_R}(B)\), contrary to its
membership in \(R\setminus\operatorname{re}_{\mathcal P_R}(B)\).  Since
\(B\ne\varnothing\), (15) excludes at least one vertex of \(R\) from \(R_2\).
Thus each recursive call is on a proper nonempty subset of its parent.  In
particular, every recursive chain strictly decreases cardinality, and all
graph-only information required by such a call is determined by the single
key \(R\subseteq V\) together with the fixed graph and tie-breaking rule.

Now consider AttachKernel.  At an internal assembly-tree node it only invokes
itself on the two already determined child subtrees, passing the same ambient
set and kernel.  At a leaf labelled \(R\), it initializes a current set
\(T_0\supseteq R\).  Its while-guard selects a
\(\mathcal P_{T_0}\)-bucket \(B\) satisfying
\[
 \varnothing\ne B\subseteq T_0\setminus R.
 \tag{16}
\]
On a successful iteration its set update is exactly
\[
 T_1=T_0\setminus B.
 \tag{17}
\]
Equations (16)--(17) imply
\[
 R\subseteq T_1\subsetneq T_0,
 \qquad |T_1|=|T_0|-|B|<|T_0|.
 \tag{18}
\]
Therefore at most \(|V|-|R|\) successful fixing iterations are possible.
The next guard, the chosen bucket, the possible-descendant and pc-component
sets, and the terminal test \(T_0=R\) are all computed from
\((\mathcal P,R,T_0)\).  With deterministic tie-breaking, the graph-only
fixing subproblem is consequently determined by the key
\((R,T_0)\) with \(R\subseteq T_0\subseteq V\).  This proves both the claimed
strict progress and the asserted cache-key characterization directly from the
two routines.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:cidp-no-selection-contract]\label{lem:cidp-no-selection-contract}
When \(S=\varnothing\), Algorithm~2 CIDP exchanges only nonempty intersections of the current intervention or conditioning set with a bucket, and performs each exchange only after the corresponding displayed PAG Rule--2 separation test succeeds.  On success it reaches a role state \(\rho_\star=(\bar X_\star,\bar C_\star)\), its final marginal-algorithm call returns an observed-law expression for \(P_{\mathbf M}(Y,\bar C_\star\mid\operatorname{do}(\bar X_\star))\), and CIDP divides that expression by its sum over the full discrete support of \(Y\).  Theorem~4 states the completeness direction for identification of \(P_{\mathbf M}(Y\mid\operatorname{do}(X),C)\) from an input-free PAG: if this conditional effect is identifiable, CIDP does not return \(\operatorname{FAIL}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:selection-equalizer-lift]\label{lem:selection-equalizer-lift}
Let \(\mathcal W\) be a finite observed vector containing a treatment subvector \(\mathcal X\) and a single finite outcome vertex \(\mathcal Y\). Let \(\mathbf Q_0\) and \(\mathbf Q_1\) be finite SCMs with positive rational local tables such that \(P_0(\mathcal W=w)=P_1(\mathcal W=w)\) for every observed cell \(w\), but, for some assignments \(x,y\),
\[
 P_0(\mathcal Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
 \ne
 P_1(\mathcal Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x)).
\]
Append binary endogenous vertices \(\mathcal C\) and \(\mathcal S\), driven by private exogenous variables independent of the old exogenous variables, and use the same new kernels in both SCMs. For rational numbers \(g_u(c)\) satisfying \(g_u(c)>0\) and \(\sum_{c=0}^1g_u(c)=1\) for every outcome value \(u\), choose rational \(\lambda_c\) such that
\[
 0<\lambda_c<\min_u g_u(c),
 \qquad c\in\{0,1\},
 \qquad
 \Lambda:=\lambda_0+\lambda_1,
\]
and set
\[
 P(\mathcal C=c\mid\mathcal Y=u)=g_u(c),
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal S=1\mid\mathcal Y=u,\mathcal C=c)
   =\frac{\lambda_c}{g_u(c)}.
\]
Then the two extended SCMs are finite, positive, and rational and, for \(j\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_j(\mathcal W=w,\mathcal C=c,\mathcal S=1)
   &=P_j(\mathcal W=w)\lambda_c,\\
 P_j(\mathcal S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
   &=\Lambda,\\
 P_j(\mathcal C=c\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x),\mathcal S=1)
   &=\frac{\lambda_c}{\Lambda},\\
 P_j(\mathcal Y=y,\mathcal C=c\mid
       \operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x),\mathcal S=1)
   &=P_j(\mathcal Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
     \frac{\lambda_c}{\Lambda}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently the full selected observational laws and every positive conditioning denominator agree, whereas the displayed selected joint numerators and conditional causal effects disagree.

Graph and model-class membership have the following conditional form only. Suppose the two base SCMs have a common represented graph \(\mathcal H\), the map \(u\mapsto g_u(\cdot)\) is nonconstant, and \(c\mapsto\lambda_c/g_u(c)\) is nonconstant for at least one fixed \(u\). Then every proposed parent is active and both extensions have the common represented graph \(\mathcal H^+\) obtained from \(\mathcal H\) by adjoining \(\mathcal Y\to\mathcal C\), \(\mathcal Y\to\mathcal S\), and \(\mathcal C\to\mathcal S\). Let \(\mathcal P^+\) be the input-free COPAG represented by the selected projection of \(\mathcal H^+\). Both extensions belong to \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P^+)\). If, in addition, every endogenous and every latent variable of each base SCM is binary, the private exogenous variables for the two appended kernels may be chosen binary and both extensions belong to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{rat,bin}}^+(\mathcal P^+)\). Without this binary-support hypothesis, the construction certifies only finite positive-rational membership in \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P^+)\), and does not by itself produce a binary certificate pair \(\Gamma\) without an additional encoding.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(j\in\{0,1\}\). Because \(g_u(0)\) and \(g_u(1)\) are positive and sum to one, each belongs to \((0,1)\). By the strict inequalities imposed on \(\lambda_c\),
\[
 0<\frac{\lambda_c}{g_u(c)}<1
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 0<1-\frac{\lambda_c}{g_u(c)}<1                         \tag{1}
\]
for every \(u\) and \(c\). Thus the displayed kernel for \(\mathcal S\), including its complementary probability for \(\mathcal S=0\), is strictly positive. The kernel for \(\mathcal C\) is strictly positive by hypothesis. All entries of both kernels are rational. Since the base SCMs have finitely many states and positive rational local tables, adjoining these two binary kernels gives finite positive-rational SCMs. Moreover, for any fixed \(u\), normalization of \(g_u\) and the strict bounds on \(\lambda_c\) give
\[
 0<\Lambda=\lambda_0+\lambda_1
 <g_u(0)+g_u(1)=1.                                      \tag{2}
\]
In particular, every division by \(\Lambda\) below is legitimate.

Appending child vertices changes no old structural equation, and their private exogenous variables are independent of all old exogenous variables. Hence the marginal law of \(\mathcal W\) remains \(P_j\). If the \(\mathcal Y\)-component of \(w\) is \(u\), the SCM factorization, the common \(\mathcal C\)-kernel, and the common \(\mathcal S\)-kernel yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_j(\mathcal W=w,\mathcal C=c,\mathcal S=1)
 &=P_j(\mathcal W=w)
   P(\mathcal C=c\mid\mathcal Y=u)
   P(\mathcal S=1\mid\mathcal Y=u,\mathcal C=c)\\
 &=P_j(\mathcal W=w)g_u(c)\frac{\lambda_c}{g_u(c)}\\
 &=P_j(\mathcal W=w)\lambda_c.                           \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
The cancellation in the last equality is valid because \(g_u(c)>0\). Summing (3) over the finitely many \(w\) and over \(c\in\{0,1\}\) gives
\[
 P_j(\mathcal S=1)=\sum_wP_j(\mathcal W=w)
                    \sum_{c=0}^1\lambda_c
                  =\Lambda.                              \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) therefore imply
\[
 P_j(\mathcal W=w,\mathcal C=c\mid\mathcal S=1)
 =P_j(\mathcal W=w)\frac{\lambda_c}{\Lambda}.            \tag{5}
\]
The observational-equality premise says that the right-hand side of (5) is the same for \(j=0\) and \(j=1\). Thus the two full selected observational laws agree cell by cell.

Under \(\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x)\), SCM modularity replaces only the structural equations for \(\mathcal X\); it leaves both appended child kernels unchanged. Marginalizing the interventional analogue of (3) over the finitely many old coordinates other than \(\mathcal Y\) gives, for every outcome value \(u\),
\[
 P_j(\mathcal Y=u,\mathcal C=c,\mathcal S=1
      \mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
 =P_j(\mathcal Y=u\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
  \lambda_c.                                             \tag{6}
\]
Write \(p_j^x(u)=P_j(\mathcal Y=u\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))\). Since \(\mathcal Y\) has finite support, \(\sum_u p_j^x(u)=1\). Summing (6) first over \(u,c\) and then only over \(u\) proves, respectively,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_j(\mathcal S=1\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
   &=\sum_u p_j^x(u)\sum_{c=0}^1\lambda_c=\Lambda,\\
 P_j(\mathcal C=c,\mathcal S=1
      \mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x))
   &=\sum_u p_j^x(u)\lambda_c=\lambda_c.                 \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
Dividing the second line of (7) by the first, using \(\Lambda>0\), yields the common positive conditioning denominator
\[
 D_j(c;x):=P_j(\mathcal C=c\mid
       \operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x),\mathcal S=1)
 =\frac{\lambda_c}{\Lambda}>0.                           \tag{8}
\]
Dividing (6) at \(u=y\) by the first line of (7) gives the selected joint numerator
\[
 N_j(y,c;x):=P_j(\mathcal Y=y,\mathcal C=c\mid
       \operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x),\mathcal S=1)
 =p_j^x(y)\frac{\lambda_c}{\Lambda}.                     \tag{9}
\]
The interventional-inequality premise gives \(p_0^x(y)\ne p_1^x(y)\). Since \(\lambda_c/\Lambda>0\), equation (9) implies \(N_0(y,c;x)\ne N_1(y,c;x)\), while (8) gives \(D_0(c;x)=D_1(c;x)\). Finally, division of (9) by (8), again justified by strict positivity, gives
\[
 P_j(\mathcal Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(\mathcal X=x),
       \mathcal C=c,\mathcal S=1)
 =p_j^x(y).                                               \tag{10}
\]
Thus the selected conditional causal effects differ, even though the full selected observational laws and conditioning denominators coincide.

It remains to verify the conditional graph and model-class claims. Under the common-graph hypothesis, the two base graphs both equal \(\mathcal H\). The independent private exogenous variables introduce no latent common cause involving an old vertex, and the two new kernels name no old observed parent other than \(\mathcal Y\), together with \(\mathcal C\) as a parent of \(\mathcal S\). Consequently no observed arrow other than the three displayed arrows is added. Nonconstancy of \(u\mapsto g_u(\cdot)\) makes \(\mathcal Y\) an active parent of \(\mathcal C\). It also supplies values \(u,u'\) and a value \(c\) for which \(g_u(c)\ne g_{u'}(c)\); because \(\lambda_c>0\),
\[
 \frac{\lambda_c}{g_u(c)}
 \ne
 \frac{\lambda_c}{g_{u'}(c)},                            \tag{11}
\]
so \(\mathcal Y\) is an active parent of \(\mathcal S\). The separate hypothesis that \(c\mapsto\lambda_c/g_u(c)\) is nonconstant for some fixed \(u\) makes \(\mathcal C\) an active parent of \(\mathcal S\). Hence both extensions have the common represented graph \(\mathcal H^+\) specified in the statement.

Now define \(\mathcal P^+\) from that graph, rather than using \(\mathcal H^+\) as a model-class index: \(\mathcal P^+\) is the input-free COPAG represented by the selected projection of \(\mathcal H^+\). The two extended models have that common represented selected projection. Their state spaces are finite, their old tables are positive by the base hypotheses, and their new tables are positive by (1) and the positivity of \(g_u(c)\). Therefore, by the definition of the published positive discrete represented-model class, both extensions lie in \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P^+)\).

For the stronger binary-certificate conclusion, assume explicitly that every endogenous variable and every latent variable of each base SCM is binary. The old endogenous and latent coordinates then retain binary support, while the newly appended endogenous coordinates \(\mathcal C\) and \(\mathcal S\) are binary by construction. The private noise can also be chosen with binary support without changing either displayed kernel: take mutually independent variables
\[
 U^{\mathcal C}_u\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(g_u(1))
 \quad\text{for each }u,
 \qquad
 U^{\mathcal S}_{u,c}\sim
 \operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac{\lambda_c}{g_u(c)}\right)
 \quad\text{for each }(u,c),                              \tag{12}
\]
independent also of all old exogenous variables, and set \(\mathcal C=U^{\mathcal C}_{\mathcal Y}\) and \(\mathcal S=U^{\mathcal S}_{\mathcal Y,\mathcal C}\). Conditional on \(\mathcal Y=u\), the first equation has success probability \(g_u(1)\), hence gives the prescribed \(\mathcal C\)-kernel; conditional on \((\mathcal Y,\mathcal C)=(u,c)\), the second has success probability \(\lambda_c/g_u(c)\), hence gives the prescribed \(\mathcal S\)-kernel. Every new private variable in (12) is binary. Its two probabilities are rational and strictly positive: this follows for \(U^{\mathcal C}_u\) from positivity and normalization of \(g_u\), and for \(U^{\mathcal S}_{u,c}\) from (1). Thus every endogenous and latent variable in each extension is binary, and every local probability is rational and lies in \((0,1)\). By the definition of the rational binary subclass, both extensions lie in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{rat,bin}}^+(\mathcal P^+)\).

If the binary-support hypothesis on both the endogenous and latent base variables is not imposed, the construction neither changes nor encodes any nonbinary base coordinate. The preceding argument still proves finite positive-rational membership in \(\mathfrak M_d^+(\mathcal P^+)\), but it cannot certify membership in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{rat,bin}}^+(\mathcal P^+)\) or supply a binary pair \(\Gamma\). This proves every asserted qualification.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lift converts an ordinary positive rational causal witness into a denominator-locked selected conditional witness by a constructive common child and selection mechanism. \textit{Gap.} An ordinary hedge pair need not preserve a selected conditioning denominator, so an explicit equalizing construction is required. \textit{Consumer.} The informative table proposition instantiates this lift, and future graphical synthesis may use it whenever a common represented base witness is available.

\section{Honest scope}

No selected conditional completeness theorem is claimed. Branch-sCIDP soundness is stated over the published positive discrete class and allows countable discrete coordinate supports; because the observed vertex set is finite, every joint outcome assignment space used in normalization is at most countable. The affine compiler and the explicit tabular witnesses lie unconditionally in the positive rational binary subclass. The generic selection-equalizer lift instead supplies a positive rational finite witness in the published discrete class. Under its common-graph and active-parent hypotheses, the model classes are indexed by the input-free COPAG \(\mathcal P^+\) represented by the selected projection of the common extended graph, rather than by that graph itself. The lift lies in the positive rational binary subclass only when every endogenous and latent variable of each base SCM is binary; the appended private noises can then also be chosen binary. Without that support hypothesis, the lift does not itself yield a binary certificate pair. A countermodel pair in the binary subclass refutes identification in the larger class, but this negative transfer supplies no positive completeness implication. The affine rank equivalence is conditional on a specified perturbation interval that remains in the represented class. The connected table proves nonvacuity and the same-graph perturbation proves that its lock is not structural. The implication from all reachable terminal failures to a represented residual forest with a locked rational pair, together with the advertised synthesis bound, remains exactly the graph-to-lock open question. Continuous variables, intervention inputs, faithfulness-only claims, and general finite-alphabet certificate completeness are outside scope.
