\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Torous, Gunsilius, and Rigollet (2024), Section 3.1, Definition 1 and Theorem 2, identify a nonlinear difference-in-differences counterfactual law under cyclical comonotonicity, but do not give the symmetric-cross-Hessian Euclidean frontier, the Hessian-commutation cyclic-gradient frontier, the global margin, or inference for it. Ribeiro, Santhirasekaram, and Glocker's displayed dynamic-transport assumptions admit the audited open-neighborhood Brenier counterexample even though its symmetric cross-Hessian is indefinite. Existing statistical optimal-transport results provide integrated map risk or pointwise map limits, not a bounded-domain class-uniform raw-Hessian band. In particular, the cited response and elliptic papers do not supply the order-zero Hessian modulus, quantitative quadratic remainder, boundary-wavelet and extension-kernel implementation, or influence and concentration estimates needed for the matching upper frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} The delivered field-tier contribution is a graph-native common-rank Brenier counterfactual frontier: exact Euclidean and cyclic structural characterizations, an observable rank margin, a robust published-claim counterexample, a lower testing frontier unconditional relative to the endpoint-response program under its stated strict-slack class-constant condition, and a deterministic event-to-margin certificate. The class-uniform upper band and matching upper rate are isolated as a conditional program with every analytic and stochastic premise exposed.

\paragraph{Fill.} The symmetric cross-Hessian theorem characterizes Euclidean monotonicity, the commutation theorem characterizes the cyclic-gradient subfamily, and the open-neighborhood matrices separate the full common-rank Brenier class from the stronger commuting subclass and provide an unconditional counterexample to the audited Euclidean strict-monotonicity implication under the displayed dynamic assumptions. The estimator uses a positive density fallback and \(m/4\) spectral clipping on every sample. The event-to-band theorem proves that, whenever \(\max_p\lVert\widetilde H_p-H_p\rVert_\infty\le r_{H,n}\le m/2\), clipping is inactive and all margin and threshold conclusions follow. A separate conditional corollary lists the endpoint-response, boundary-wavelet, extension-kernel, nonlinear-remainder, influence-kernel, increment, and Bernstein-net premises that would yield probability at least \(1-\delta\) and the selected upper rate. The matching-frontier proposition now invokes that corollary, while the fixed-support quadratic-bump mixture proves the lower rate independently of the endpoint-response program under the stated strict-slack class-constant condition. The validated certificate continues to use its distinct stronger source-product trigger.

\section{Related work}

De Lara and Ganassali (2025), Section 5.3, leave the cyclically monotone SCM characterization unresolved. Torous, Gunsilius, and Rigollet (2024), Section 3.1, Definition 1 and equation (6), define cyclical comonotonicity and show that it makes the relevant inverse composition cyclically monotone; their Theorem 2 uses that condition to identify a treated counterfactual law in nonlinear difference-in-differences. Their framework does not supply the pointwise symmetric-cross-Hessian Euclidean frontier, the Hessian-commutation cyclic-gradient frontier, the sharp global margin, or margin inference, and the common-rank Brenier framework does not claim their difference-in-differences identification result. Ribeiro, Santhirasekaram, and Glocker (NeurIPS 2025), Theorem 4.12 and Appendix Theorem B.11, assert strict Euclidean monotonicity under their displayed dynamic-transport conditions, but their proof uses a state-dependent weighted inner product; the audited open-neighborhood Brenier pair satisfies those displayed conditions while its Euclidean symmetric cross-Hessian is indefinite. Carlier, Chernozhukov, and Galichon (2016), Theorem 2.1, provide common-reference vector quantiles, not inverse-composition geometry. Hutter and Rigollet (2021) establish integrated map-risk rates. Manole et al. (2024) provide plug-in and boundary-wavelet machinery, and Manole et al. (2023) provide pointwise torus map limits, but neither source proves this bounded-domain simultaneous raw-Hessian band. Caja-Lopez, Delgadino, and Kitagawa (2026), Theorem 1.3, control \(C^{1,\alpha}\) potential error, while Theorem 1.5 gives a \(C^{2,\beta}\)-valued differentiable path and its conormal response; neither gives the class-uniform order-zero Hessian modulus or quantitative quadratic remainder required by the conditional program. Dong, Lee, and Kim (2020), Theorem 1.7, gives \(C^1\) regularity for a divergence-form conormal problem, whereas Theorem 1.10 concerns a different nondivergence oblique problem; neither furnishes the missing Hessian estimate. Their paper is in Indiana University Mathematics Journal 69(6), 1815--1853 (2020), DOI 10.1512/iumj.2020.69.8028. Ghosal and Sen (2022) study rank maps rather than cross-Hessian margins, and Chen and Fang (2019) motivate caution for ordinary bootstrap claims at degenerate nonsmooth functionals. None of these sources supplies the complete premise chain needed for the matching upper frontier or the validated source-product interval search; the latter's sufficient trigger remains distinct from the scalar margin endpoint except in the proved small-radius comparison.

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Pi\_\allowbreak{}\{m/\allowbreak{}4\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{deterministic spectral clipping map}; \(\Pi_{m/4}:\mathbb S^d\to\mathbb S_{++}^d\); \texttt{total SPD regularizer}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dimension]\label{ass:dimension}
The outcome dimension satisfies \(d\ge2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Multivariate Brenier setting, \cite{CarlierChernozhukovGalichon2016VQR}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:regimes]\label{ass:regimes}
The number of regimes satisfies \(2\le R<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite regime family, \cite{deLaraGanassali2025GoodMatching}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:smoothness-index]\label{ass:smoothness-index}
The known smoothness index satisfies \(s>d/2+3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-smoothness Brenier regularity with two derivative slack, \cite{ManoleBalakrishnanNilesWeedWasserman2024Plugin}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:domain-smoothness]\label{ass:domain-smoothness}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\), the boundary \(\partial D_p\) is of class \(C^{s+2}\) with chart norm at most \(L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Smooth second-boundary-value domains, \cite{CajaLopezDelgadinoKitagawa2026Stability}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-convexity]\label{ass:uniform-convexity}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\), the principal curvatures of \(\partial D_p\) lie in \([L^{-1},L]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly convex support, \cite{HutterRigollet2021MinimaxOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-bounds]\label{ass:density-bounds}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\) and \(y\in D_p\), the density satisfies \(b\le f_p(y)\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nondegenerate density bounds, \cite{CajaLopezDelgadinoKitagawa2026Stability}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-holder]\label{ass:density-holder}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\), the density satisfies \(\lVert f_p\rVert_{C^s(D_p)}\le L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Holder density ball, \cite{ManoleBalakrishnanNilesWeedWasserman2024Plugin}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:potential-holder]\label{ass:potential-holder}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\), the normalized potential satisfies \(\lVert\varphi_p\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\le L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Smooth Brenier potential class, \cite{HutterRigollet2021MinimaxOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hessian-ellipticity]\label{ass:hessian-ellipticity}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\) and \(u\in\Omega\), the Hessian satisfies \(mI_d\preceq H_p(u)\preceq MI_d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly strongly convex Brenier potentials, \cite{HutterRigollet2021MinimaxOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:shared-rank-scm]\label{ass:shared-rank-scm}
For every \(p\in\mathcal A\), the potential outcome obeys the structural equation \(Y(p)=F_p(U)\) with the same \(U\sim\mu_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Rank-invariant SCM solution-map representation, \cite{deLaraGanassali2025GoodMatching}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-regime-samples]\label{ass:iid-regime-samples}
For each \(p\in\mathcal A\), the data \(Y_{p,1},\ldots,Y_{p,n_p}\) are independent draws from \(P_p\), and the regime samples are mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent two-sample optimal-transport experiment, \cite{ManoleBalakrishnanNilesWeedWasserman2023CLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sample-balance]\label{ass:sample-balance}
The fixed constant \(c_n\in(0,1]\) satisfies \(n_p\ge c_n\max_{q\in\mathcal A}n_q\) for every \(p\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Comparable regime sample sizes, \cite{ManoleBalakrishnanNilesWeedWasserman2024Plugin}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:model-class]\label{def:model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Smooth common-\allowbreak{}rank Brenier SCM class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M_s=\{((P_p,D_p,\varphi_p))_{p\in\mathcal A}:D_p\subset\mathbb R^d\text{ is a known nonempty compact uniformly convex body with }C^{s+2}\text{ boundary charts of norm at most }L;\ P_p\text{ has density }f_p\text{ with }\int_{D_p}f_p=1,\ b\le f_p\le B,\ \lVert f_p\rVert_{C^s(D_p)}\le L;\ (\nabla\varphi_p)_{\#}\mu_0=P_p,\ \int_\Omega\varphi_p=0,\ \lVert\varphi_p\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\le L,\ mI_d\preceq D^2\varphi_p\preceq MI_d,\text{ and ass:shared-rank-scm holds for all }p\in\mathcal A\}\). Noninteger \(C^s\) is the usual Holder class; integer \(C^s\) has bounded classical derivatives through order \(s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}dimension}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}regimes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}smoothness-\allowbreak{}index}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}domain-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}uniform-\allowbreak{}convexity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}bounds}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}density-\allowbreak{}holder}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}potential-\allowbreak{}holder}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}hessian-\allowbreak{}ellipticity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}shared-\allowbreak{}rank-\allowbreak{}scm}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:endpoint-response-class]\label{def:endpoint-response-class}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak E_s=\{(D_p,f):D_p\subset\mathbb R^d\text{ is a known nonempty compact uniformly convex body with }C^{s+2}\text{ boundary-chart norm at most }L\text{ and principal curvatures in }[L^{-1},L];\ \int_{D_p}f(y)\,dy=1,\ b\le f\le B,\ \lVert f\rVert_{C^s(D_p)}\le L;\ (\nabla\varphi_f)_{\#}\mu_0=f(y)\,dy,\ \int_\Omega\varphi_f=0,\ \lVert\varphi_f\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\le L,\ mI_d\preceq D^2\varphi_f\preceq MI_d\}\), with the same Holder/classical convention as \(\mathcal M_s\).

\begin{definition}[def:cross-hessian-margin]\label{def:cross-hessian-margin}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(p,q\in\mathcal A\), set \(S_{pq}(u)=\operatorname{sym}(H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1})\) and \(\gamma_{pq}=\inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}(S_{pq}(u))\).

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:euclidean-frontier]\label{thm:euclidean-frontier}
For every \(p\ne q\) and every member of \(\mathcal M_s\), the counterfactual map \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is Euclidean monotone on \(D_p\) if and only if \(S_{pq}(u)\succeq0\) for all \(u\in\Omega\). Its sharp strong-monotonicity modulus satisfies \[\inf_{x\ne y\in D_p}\frac{\langle C_{q\leftarrow p}(x)-C_{q\leftarrow p}(y),x-y\rangle}{\lVert x-y\rVert_2^2}=\gamma_{pq}.\] In particular, \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is strongly Euclidean monotone if and only if \(\gamma_{pq}>0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix distinct \(p,q\in\mathcal A\) and a member of \(\mathcal M_s\), and write \(T=C_{q\leftarrow p}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:counterfactual\text{-}map}\), \(T=F_q\circ F_p^{-1}\).  The ellipticity in \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) makes \(F_p\) a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism between the interiors of \(\Omega\) and \(D_p\), with continuous extension to their boundaries.  Indeed, its derivative is \(H_p\succeq mI_d\), and
\[
 \langle F_p(u)-F_p(v),u-v\rangle
 =\int_0^1 (u-v)^{\mathsf T}H_p(v+t(u-v))(u-v)\,dt
 \ge m\lVert u-v\rVert_2^2,
\]
so it is injective, while its image is \(D_p\) by \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).  The chain rule therefore gives, for \(u\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\),
\[
 DT(F_p(u))=H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sym}DT(F_p(u))=S_{pq}(u).
 \tag{1}
\]
All fields in (1) extend continuously to the boundary by \(\mathrm{ass:potential\text{-}holder}\) and the lower eigenvalue bound.

Let
\[
 \mathfrak m(T):=\inf_{x\ne y\in D_p}
 \frac{\langle T(x)-T(y),x-y\rangle}{\lVert x-y\rVert_2^2}.
\]
The support \(D_p\) is convex by \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).  Hence, with \(v=x-y\), the fundamental theorem of calculus along the factual chord gives
\[
 \langle T(x)-T(y),v\rangle
 =\int_0^1v^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{sym}DT(y+tv)v\,dt
 \ge\gamma_{pq}\lVert v\rVert_2^2,
 \tag{2}
\]
where the last step uses \(\mathrm{def:cross\text{-}hessian\text{-}margin}\) and (1).  Thus \(\mathfrak m(T)\ge\gamma_{pq}\).

For the reverse inequality, take \(x\in\operatorname{int}(D_p)\), a unit vector \(a\), and \(t\ne0\) small enough that \(x+ta\in\operatorname{int}(D_p)\).  Differentiability yields
\[
 \lim_{t\to0}
 \frac{\langle T(x+ta)-T(x),ta\rangle}{t^2}
 =a^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{sym}DT(x)a.
 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(\mathfrak m(T)\le\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym}DT(x))\).  Taking the infimum over interior \(x\), using \(x=F_p(u)\), and then using continuity and density of \(\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\) in the compact ball \(\Omega\), gives
\[
 \mathfrak m(T)
 \le\inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}(S_{pq}(u))
 =\gamma_{pq}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) prove the displayed equality.

If \(S_{pq}(u)\succeq0\) everywhere, (2) with \(\gamma_{pq}\ge0\) proves Euclidean monotonicity.  Conversely, if \(T\) is Euclidean monotone, every quotient on the left of the definition of \(\mathfrak m(T)\) is nonnegative.  Equation (3), followed by boundary continuity, then gives \(S_{pq}(u)\succeq0\) for every \(u\in\Omega\).  Finally, a strong-monotonicity constant \(c>0\) exists exactly when \(\mathfrak m(T)>0\), which by (4) is exactly \(\gamma_{pq}>0\).  This proves all three assertions without identifying strict monotonicity with strong monotonicity.

The causal and observed-law objects remain distinct in this argument.  By \(\mathrm{ass:shared\text{-}rank\text{-}scm}\), observing the factual state \(Y(p)=x\) fixes the common rank at \(F_p^{-1}(x)\), whence the same unit has \(Y(q)=T(x)\).  By contrast, \(S_{pq}\) and \(\gamma_{pq}\) are functionals of the two regime-specific Brenier mechanisms supplied by the observed laws in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\); equations (1)--(4), rather than a definition, prove that this functional is the causal map's exact Euclidean modulus.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem replaces mechanism-by-mechanism monotonicity with the exact cross-regime Euclidean criterion and identifies the sharp margin rather than a sufficient lower bound. \textit{Gap.} DeLaraGanassali2025GoodMatching leaves a precise cyclic-monotonicity characterization open, while RibeiroSanthirasekaramGlocker2025DynamicOT, NeurIPS Theorem 4.12 and Appendix Theorem B.11, asserts cross-regime strict Euclidean monotonicity without the necessary cross-Hessian condition. \textit{Consumer.} A practitioner using a Brenier counterfactual generator obtains an identified population certificate for whether its same-unit cross-regime map preserves Euclidean ranks.

\begin{theorem}[thm:cyclic-frontier]\label{thm:cyclic-frontier}
For every \(p\ne q\) and every member of \(\mathcal M_s\), the map \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is cyclically monotone on \(D_p\) if and only if \(H_p(u)H_q(u)=H_q(u)H_p(u)\) for every \(u\in\Omega\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\ne q\), put \(T=C_{q\leftarrow p}\), and use the Jacobian identity (proved directly again from \(\mathrm{def:counterfactual\text{-}map}\))
\[
 DT(F_p(u))=H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1}.
 \tag{5}
\]
We first prove the differentiable cyclic-monotonicity criterion needed here.  Adopt the convention that a map \(G\) is cyclically monotone when, for every finite cycle \(x_0,\ldots,x_N=x_0\),
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\langle G(x_i),x_i-x_{i+1}\rangle\ge0.
 \tag{6}
\]
If \(G\in C^1\) satisfies (6) on a convex domain, apply (6) to successively finer partitions of any closed piecewise \(C^1\) curve \(\Gamma\) in the interior.  The Riemann sums converge and give \(-\int_\Gamma G\cdot dx\ge0\).  Applying the same argument to the reversed curve gives the opposite inequality, hence
\[
 \int_\Gamma G\cdot dx=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Apply (7) to an axis-parallel rectangle of side lengths \(h,k\), divide by \(hk\), and let \(h,k\to0\).  The fundamental theorem of calculus on its four sides gives \(\partial_jG_i=\partial_iG_j\).  Thus \(DG\) is symmetric.  Applying (6) to a two-point cycle gives
\[
 \langle G(x)-G(y),x-y\rangle\ge0;
\]
letting \(y=x+ta\) and \(t\to0\) shows \(a^{\mathsf T}DG(x)a\ge0\).  Hence a \(C^1\) cyclically monotone map has symmetric positive-semidefinite Jacobian.

Conversely, suppose \(DG\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite.  Choose an interior base point \(x_0\) and define
\[
 V(x)=\int_0^1\langle G(x_0+t(x-x_0)),x-x_0\rangle\,dt.
\]
Writing \(v=x-x_0\), symmetry of \(DG\) and differentiation under the integral give, for every direction \(a\),
\[
 DV(x)[a]
 =\int_0^1\langle G(x_0+tv)+tDG(x_0+tv)v,a\rangle\,dt
 =\langle G(x),a\rangle.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(\nabla V=G\).  Along every chord the second derivative of \(V\) is \(a^{\mathsf T}DG\,a\ge0\), so \(V\) is convex.  The supporting-hyperplane inequalities
\[
 V(x_{i+1})\ge V(x_i)+\langle G(x_i),x_{i+1}-x_i\rangle
\]
sum to (6).  Continuity extends the conclusion to the boundary.  We have therefore derived, rather than invoked, that \(G\) is cyclically monotone if and only if \(DG\) is symmetric positive semidefinite in the present \(C^1\), convex-domain setting.

For \(G=T\), symmetry in (5) is equivalent to
\[
 H_qH_p^{-1}=(H_qH_p^{-1})^{\mathsf T}=H_p^{-1}H_q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_pH_q=H_qH_p,
 \tag{9}
\]
where both multiplications are legal because \(H_p\succeq mI_d\) by \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\).  If the matrices commute, the symmetric matrix in (5) has positive eigenvalues: it is similar to
\[
 H_p(u)^{-1/2}H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1/2}\succ0.
\]
It is therefore positive definite, so the converse half established through (8) makes \(T\) cyclically monotone.  If \(T\) is cyclically monotone, its Jacobian is symmetric by (7), and (9) forces pointwise commutation.  This proves the equivalence on all of \(\Omega\), boundary points following from continuity under \(\mathrm{ass:potential\text{-}holder}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Pointwise commutation is the exact integrability obstruction separating a composition of two Brenier maps from a Brenier counterfactual map. \textit{Gap.} CarlierChernozhukovGalichon2016VQR supplies regime-specific convex-gradient maps, and DeLaraGanassali2025GoodMatching shows pairwise Brenier maps can fail counterfactual algebra, but neither gives this local if-and-only-if frontier for an inverse composition. \textit{Consumer.} The result lets vector-quantile researchers identify commuting subclasses, including separable and jointly diagonalizable mechanisms, in which same-rank counterfactuals remain optimal transports.

\begin{proposition}[prop:dynamic-ot-counterexample]\label{prop:dynamic-ot-counterexample}
Let \(U\sim\mu_{\square}\) be independent of a binary regime label. The maps in \(\mathrm{def:quadratic\text{-}counterexample}\) satisfy the displayed hypotheses of RibeiroSanthirasekaramGlocker2025DynamicOT, Theorem 4.12 and Appendix Theorem B.11: \(d=2>1\), the source is continuous uniform on \([0,1]^2\) up to its null boundary, each target has a strictly positive bounded density on the bounded open convex support \(A_j\Omega_{\square}\), and each \(F_j\) is a time-one dynamic optimal-transport map of a Lipschitz displacement flow. Yet \(\det(\operatorname{sym}(A_1A_0^{-1}))=-7/81<0\). At \(u=(1/2,1/2)^{\mathsf T}\), for \(z=(-2/9,1/18)^{\mathsf T}\), both endpoints lie in \(\Omega_{\square}\) and \[\langle F_1(u+z)-F_1(u),F_0(u+z)-F_0(u)\rangle=z^{\mathsf T}A_1A_0z=-7/324<0.\] The failure persists on an open SPD-matrix neighborhood. Thus the separate Brenier conditions do not imply the Euclidean strict-monotonicity conclusion in equations (13) and (72).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The independence of the binary regime label and \(U\) is a premise of the proposition.  We verify the remaining hypotheses and conclusions from \(\mathrm{def:quadratic\text{-}counterexample}\).

First, \(d=2>1\), \(\Omega_{\square}=(0,1)^2\), and \(\mu_{\square}\) has Lebesgue density one on \(\Omega_{\square}\).  Thus it is the continuous uniform law on \([0,1]^2\) after adjoining or removing the Lebesgue-null boundary.  The eigenvalues of \(A_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,9)\) are \(1\) and \(9\), while the eigenvalues of
\[
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/5\\4/5&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
are \(1-4/5=1/5\) and \(1+4/5=9/5\).  Hence both matrices are symmetric positive definite and invertible.  Consequently
\[
F_j(u)=A_j u=\nabla\!\left(\frac12u^{\mathsf T}A_j u\right),
\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
and each displayed potential is strictly convex.

For completeness, the optimal-transport property follows directly from the quadratic structure.  Fix \(j\in\{0,1\}\), put \(\phi_j(x)=x^{\mathsf T}A_jx/2\), and put \(\phi_j^*(y)=y^{\mathsf T}A_j^{-1}y/2\).  Completing the square gives, for every \(x,y\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
\phi_j(x)+\phi_j^*(y)-x^{\mathsf T}y
=\frac12\bigl(y-A_jx\bigr)^{\mathsf T}A_j^{-1}
       \bigl(y-A_jx\bigr)\ge 0,
\]
with equality when \(y=A_jx\).  If a coupling has marginals \(\mu_{\square}\) and \((A_j)_{\#}\mu_{\square}\), integration of this inequality bounds its value of \(\mathbb E[X^{\mathsf T}Y]\) above by a quantity depending only on the two marginals; equality is attained by \(Y=A_jX\).  Since
\[
\mathbb E\lVert X-Y\rVert^2
=\mathbb E\lVert X\rVert^2+\mathbb E\lVert Y\rVert^2
 -2\mathbb E[X^{\mathsf T}Y],
\]
the map \(F_j\) minimizes quadratic transport cost.

Because an invertible linear map is an open map and preserves boundedness and convexity, \(A_j\Omega_{\square}\) is bounded, open, and convex.  The change-of-variables formula gives the target density
\[
f_j(y)=\frac{1}{\det A_j}\,
\mathbf 1_{A_j\Omega_{\square}}(y).
\]
Here \(\det A_0=9\) and \(\det A_1=1-16/25=9/25\).  Thus the respective densities on their supports are the strictly positive finite constants \(1/9\) and \(25/9\).

It remains to check the asserted dynamic realization.  For \(j\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), define
\[
B_{j,\lambda}=(1-\lambda)I_2+\lambda A_j,
\qquad T_{j,\lambda}(u)=B_{j,\lambda}u.
\]
Every \(B_{j,\lambda}\) is symmetric positive definite because, for nonzero \(w\),
\[
w^{\mathsf T}B_{j,\lambda}w
=(1-\lambda)\lVert w\rVert^2
 +\lambda w^{\mathsf T}A_jw>0.
\]
The same quadratic argument used above therefore shows that \(T_{j,\lambda}\) is an optimal map from \(\mu_{\square}\) to \((T_{j,\lambda})_{\#}\mu_{\square}\).  Moreover, the Eulerian velocity on \(B_{j,\lambda}\Omega_{\square}\) is
\[
v_{j,\lambda}(x)=(A_j-I_2)B_{j,\lambda}^{-1}x,
\]
and it satisfies
\[
\frac{d}{d\lambda}T_{j,\lambda}(u)
=(A_j-I_2)u
=v_{j,\lambda}\bigl(T_{j,\lambda}(u)\bigr).
\]
Since
\[
\lambda_{\min}(B_{j,\lambda})
\ge \min\{1,\lambda_{\min}(A_j)\}>0,
\]
the maps \(v_{j,\lambda}\) are uniformly Lipschitz in space.  Finally, \(T_{j,0}=I_2\), \(T_{j,1}=F_j\), and \(T_{j,\lambda}=(1-\lambda)I_2+\lambda F_j\) is exactly the displacement interpolation induced by the optimal endpoint coupling.  This proves the claimed Lipschitz dynamic optimal-transport realization.

We now compute the Euclidean obstruction exactly.  Since
\[
A_0^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1/9\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_1A_0^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/45\\4/5&1/9\end{pmatrix},
\]
we have
\[
\operatorname{sym}(A_1A_0^{-1})
=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/9\\4/9&1/9\end{pmatrix}
\]
and hence
\[
\det\!\left(\operatorname{sym}(A_1A_0^{-1})\right)
=\frac19-\frac{16}{81}=-\frac7{81}<0.
\]
For the specified points,
\[
u=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u+z=\begin{pmatrix}1/2-2/9\\1/2+1/18\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}5/18\\5/9\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All four coordinates lie strictly between zero and one, so both endpoints belong to \(\Omega_{\square}\), and \(z\ne0\).  Linearity and symmetry of \(A_1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle F_1(u+z)-F_1(u),F_0(u+z)-F_0(u)\right\rangle
&=\langle A_1z,A_0z\rangle\\
&=z^{\mathsf T}A_1A_0z.
\end{aligned}
\]
Directly,
\[
A_1z=\begin{pmatrix}-8/45\\-11/90\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_0z=\begin{pmatrix}-2/9\\1/2\end{pmatrix},
\]
so
\[
z^{\mathsf T}A_1A_0z
=\frac{16}{405}-\frac{11}{180}
=-\frac7{324}<0.
\]
Thus the two distinct source points yield a strictly negative Euclidean cross-regime increment, contradicting the asserted strict-monotonicity conclusion.

Finally, the cone of symmetric positive-definite matrices is open.  On that cone the maps
\[
(B_0,B_1)\longmapsto
\det\!\left(\operatorname{sym}(B_1B_0^{-1})\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(B_0,B_1)\longmapsto z^{\mathsf T}B_1B_0z
\]
are continuous.  Their values at \((A_0,A_1)\) are respectively \(-7/81\) and \(-7/324\).  Therefore both remain negative on some open neighborhood of \((A_0,A_1)\) within the SPD pairs.  Every pair in a sufficiently small such neighborhood retains all properties checked above, while the same fixed chord remains a Euclidean monotonicity violation.  Hence the separate Brenier and dynamic-transport conditions do not imply the Euclidean strict-monotonicity conclusions in equations (13) and (72).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-margin-band]\label{thm:uniform-margin-band}
Assume that \(r_{H,n}\le m/2\).  On the explicitly defined event
\(\mathcal B_n\), simultaneously for every \(p\in\mathcal A\),
\[
 \widehat H_p=\widetilde H_p,
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat H_p-H_p\rVert_\infty\le r_{H,n},
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat H_p^{-1}\rVert_\infty\le \frac{2}{m}.
\]
For every ordered pair \(p\ne q\),
\[
 \sup_{u\in\Omega}
 \lVert\widehat S_{pq}(u)-S_{pq}(u)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le w_n,
 \qquad
 \lvert\widehat\gamma_{pq}-\gamma_{pq}\rvert\le w_n,
\]
and hence \(\gamma_{pq}\in I_{pq,n}\).  For the declared ordered pair, the test obeys the deterministic implications
\[
 \gamma_{pq}\le0\Longrightarrow\Psi_n=0,
 \qquad
 \gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\Longrightarrow\Psi_n=1
\]
on \(\mathcal B_n\).  This theorem is event-to-band: it makes no assertion about \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\mathcal B_n)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sample outcome in \(\mathcal B_n\), and write \(r_0=r_{H,n}\).  By Definition \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\), for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \(u\in\Omega\),
\[
 \lVert\widetilde H_a(u)-H_a(u)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le r_0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) gives \(H_a(u)\succeq mI_d\).  Thus, for every unit vector \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), (1) and the assumed inequality \(r_0\le m/2\) give
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}\widetilde H_a(u)x
 \ge x^{\mathsf T}H_a(u)x-r_0
 \ge m-r_0
 \ge \frac m2>\frac m4.
 \tag{2}
\]
The field \(\widetilde H_a\) is symmetric by Definition \(\mathrm{def:regularized\text{-}hessian\text{-}estimator}\).  Consequently every eigenvalue in (2) exceeds the clipping threshold \(m/4\), so the definition of \(\Pi_{m/4}\) yields
\[
 \widehat H_a(u)=\widetilde H_a(u),
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat H_a-H_a\rVert_\infty\le r_0,
 \qquad
 \widehat H_a(u)\succeq\frac m2I_d.
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular, the spectral theorem, (3), and \(H_a(u)\succeq mI_d\) imply
\[
 \lVert\widehat H_a(u)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac2m,
 \qquad
 \lVert H_a(u)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac1m.
 \tag{4}
\]
This proves all three simultaneous Hessian assertions in the theorem.

Fix an ordered pair \(p\ne q\).  Adding and subtracting \(H_q\widehat H_p^{-1}\), and using the exact identity
\[
 \widehat H_p^{-1}-H_p^{-1}
 =\widehat H_p^{-1}(H_p-\widehat H_p)H_p^{-1},
 \tag{5}
\]
we obtain, pointwise in \(u\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat H_q\widehat H_p^{-1}-H_qH_p^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 &\le
 \lVert\widehat H_q-H_q\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert\widehat H_p^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &\quad+
 \lVert H_q\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert\widehat H_p^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert H_p-\widehat H_p\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \lVert H_p^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\\
 &\le
 \left(\frac2m+\frac{2M}{m^2}\right)r_0
 =w_n.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{6}
\]
Here submultiplicativity was used in the first inequality; (3)--(4) and the upper ellipticity bound \(\lVert H_q(u)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le M\) from \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) were used in the second; and the last equality is Definition \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\).  For every square matrix \(A\), the triangle inequality and transpose invariance of the operator norm give
\[
 \lVert\operatorname{sym}(A)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac12\{\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}+\lVert A^{\mathsf T}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 =\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Definitions \(\mathrm{def:cross\text{-}hessian\text{-}margin}\) and \(\mathrm{def:regularized\text{-}hessian\text{-}estimator}\), followed by (6)--(7), therefore give
\[
 \sup_{u\in\Omega}
 \lVert\widehat S_{pq}(u)-S_{pq}(u)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le w_n.
 \tag{8}
\]

We next derive the needed eigenvalue inequality.  If \(A\) and \(B\) are symmetric and \(x_B\) is a unit eigenvector associated with \(\lambda_{\min}(B)\), the Rayleigh representation gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(A)
 \le x_B^{\mathsf T}Ax_B
 \le\lambda_{\min}(B)+\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Interchanging \(A\) and \(B\) proves
\[
 \lvert\lambda_{\min}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(B)\rvert
 \le\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Moreover, for any real functions \(a,b\) on the same nonempty set,
\[
 \left\lvert\inf_u a(u)-\inf_u b(u)\right\rvert
 \le\sup_u\lvert a(u)-b(u)\rvert,
 \tag{10}
\]
because \(a(u)\ge b(u)-\sup_v\lvert a(v)-b(v)\rvert\) for every \(u\), and conversely after interchanging \(a,b\).  Apply (9) pointwise in (8), then apply (10), and use Definition \(\mathrm{def:cross\text{-}hessian\text{-}margin}\) and the definition of \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\).  The result is
\[
 \lvert\widehat\gamma_{pq}-\gamma_{pq}\rvert\le w_n.
 \tag{11}
\]
The definition \(I_{pq,n}=[\widehat\gamma_{pq}-w_n,\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n]\) turns (11) into \(\gamma_{pq}\in I_{pq,n}\).

Finally, if \(\gamma_{pq}\le0\), then (11) implies \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\le w_n\); the strict-threshold definition \(\Psi_n=\mathbf 1\{\widehat\gamma_{pq}>w_n\}\) therefore gives \(\Psi_n=0\).  If \(\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\), then (11) gives \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\ge2w_n>w_n\), where \(w_n>0\) follows from \(m,M,r_{H,n}>0\).  Hence \(\Psi_n=1\).  Every step was pointwise on \(\mathcal B_n\), and no probability assertion about that event was used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the deterministic event-to-margin theorem: it turns a verified simultaneous raw-Hessian event into clipping inactivity, inverse-product stability, interval coverage, and correct separated decisions. \textit{Gap.} It deliberately makes no class-uniform probability or selected-rate claim, because those require the open analytic and stochastic chain recorded on the OEQ. \textit{Consumer.} Downstream certificate arguments may use its deterministic conclusions on \(\mathcal B_n\); probability-calibrated inference must additionally invoke the conditional class-uniform corollary.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-separation-lower]\label{thm:sharp-separation-lower}
Suppose the existing class constants admit the strict-slack clause in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\). Then the family \(\mathfrak F_{\mathrm{LB}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_s\) for all sufficiently small \(h\). Indeed, \(H_p\equiv\overline A_0\), while \(\operatorname{sym}(B_t\overline A_0^{-1})\) has first block
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/9\\4t/9&1-8t/9\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det=1-8t/9-16t^2/81,
\]
so its smaller eigenvalue crosses zero transversally at \(t_\star=(9/4)(\sqrt2-1)\).  =\frac{\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}}{\det D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)}

 \gamma_{pq}(\mathbf P^+)\ge c_0\theta,
 \qquad

because on \(\lVert u-u_j\rVert_2\le h/3\), \(D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)=B_{t_\star+\theta/2}\). Thus every component is in the composite null and the alternative has separation \(\rho\asymp\theta\).  \qquad
 \int_{D_q}\frac{f_q^{(j)}(y)f_q^{(k)}(y)}{f_q^+(y)}\,dy=1
 \quad(j\ne k).

Consequently the fuzzy-null mixture satisfies

 \chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n},\mathbb P_n^+)
 =\frac{[1+\chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)]^{n_q}-1}{N}
 \le\frac{\exp(Cn_q\theta^2h^d)-1}{N}.

Choose \(h\asymp(\log n_\ast/n_\ast)^{1/(2s+d)}\) with its constant small enough that sample balance gives \(Cn_q\theta^2h^d\le\tfrac14\log N\). Then total variation tends to zero, and the mixture testing inequality gives type-II error greater than \(0.20\) for every size-\(0.05\) test. Hence there is \(c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\),
\[
 \rho_n^\ast\ge c\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}.
\]
This lower bound is independent of the unresolved endpoint-response assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All constants in this proof depend only on the fixed class constants, the fixed interior packing region, and \(\kappa\), never on \(h\), \(j\), or \(n_\ast\).  Write \(A=\overline A_0\), \(\Delta=\overline A_1-\overline A_0\), and
\[
 S(t)=\operatorname{sym}(B_tA^{-1}).
\]
On the first two coordinates,
\[
 B_t=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/5\\4t/5&9-8t\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/9\\4t/9&1-8t/9\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The remaining block is \(I_{d-2}\).  Thus
\[
 \det S(t)_{1:2,1:2}=1-\frac{8t}{9}-\frac{16t^2}{81}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The positive root of (11) is \(t_\star=(9/4)(\sqrt2-1)\in(0,1)\), its derivative there is \(-8/9-32t_\star/81<0\), and the other eigenvalue is positive.  Therefore the zero eigenvalue is simple.  Continuity of the other eigenvalue and the identity \(\lambda_{\min}=\det/\lambda_{\max}\) near \(t_\star\) give constants \(c_0,c_1>0\) such that, for all sufficiently small \(\theta>0\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(S(t_\star-\theta/2))\ge c_0\theta,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(S(t_\star+\theta/2))\le-c_1\theta.
 \tag{12}
\]

For \(b_j(u)=\theta h^2\kappa((u-u_j)/h)\), the scaling rule for every derivative of order \(\ell\) is
\[
 \lVert D^\ell b_j\rVert_\infty
 \le C_\kappa\theta h^{2-\ell}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The analogous Holder seminorm at order \(s+2\) obeys the same scaling.  Since \(\theta=ah^s\), (13) gives
\[
 \lVert D^2b_j\rVert_\infty\le C_\kappa\theta,
 \qquad
 \lVert b_j\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\le C_\kappa a.
 \tag{14}
\]
Choose \(a\) so small relative to the strict slack \(\eta\) that every perturbation in (14), including the harmless normalization constant, uses at most \(\eta/2\) of that slack.  For sufficiently small \(h\), (14) also gives \(D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}\succeq mI_d\).  Indeed, for distinct \(u,v\in\Omega\), integration along their chord gives
\[
 (u-v)^{\mathsf T}\{\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)-\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(v)\}
 =\int_0^1 (u-v)^{\mathsf T}D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(v+\tau(u-v))(u-v)\,d\tau
 \ge m\lVert u-v\rVert_2^2>0,
\]
so every perturbed gradient is injective.

The bump and its gradient vanish in a neighborhood of \(\partial U_j\).  The homotopy
\[
 T_\tau(u)=B_{t_-}u+\tau\nabla b_j(u),
 \qquad 0\le\tau\le1,
\]
has everywhere nonsingular positive-definite derivative after the preceding choice of \(a\), and it equals \(B_{t_-}u\) on \(\partial U_j\).  For a point not on the common boundary image, its number of preimages in \(U_j\) cannot change along \(\tau\): local inverses prevent creation or cancellation in the interior, compactness supplies a convergent preimage at any first change, and the fixed boundary prevents escape through \(\partial U_j\).  At \(\tau=0\) this number is one on \(B_{t_-}U_j\) and zero outside its closure.  Hence
\[
 \nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(U_j)=B_{t_-}U_j=E_j.
 \tag{15}
\]
The same argument on \(\Omega\), or injectivity together with equality to the base map outside \(U_j\), gives the common target support \(D_q=B_{t_-}\Omega\).  The factual and non-focal supports are unchanged by construction.

Let \(c_\Omega=\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}\).  The change-of-variables identity for the diffeomorphism \(T_j=\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}\) is
\[
 f_q^{(j)}(T_j(u))\det D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)=c_\Omega.
 \tag{16}
\]
The Hessians range in one fixed compact subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^d\).  The determinant and reciprocal determinant are Lipschitz there, so (14) and (16), together with the fact that \(f_q^+=c_\Omega/\det B_{t_-}\) is constant, imply
\[
 \lVert f_q^{(j)}-f_q^+\rVert_\infty\le C\theta.
 \tag{17}
\]
Moreover, the inverse-function derivatives of \(T_j^{-1}\) through order \(s+1\) are uniformly bounded because the smallest singular value of \(DT_j\) is at least \(m\).  Applying the product and composition rules to (16), and using \(\lVert D^2b_j\rVert_{C^s}\le Ca\), yields
\[
 \lVert f_q^{(j)}\rVert_{C^s(D_q)}
 \le\lVert f_q^+\rVert_{C^s(D_q)}+Ca.
 \tag{18}
\]
Equations (14), (17), and (18), together with the admitted strict slack in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\), verify the potential norm, ellipticity, density bounds, and density norm.  The supports themselves are the slack ellipsoids, so their chart and curvature bounds are unchanged.  Equation (16) also proves normalization and positivity.  Thus \(\mathbf P^+,\mathbf P_j\in\mathcal M_s\) for all sufficiently small \(h\), with the common-rank structural equation imposed exactly as in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).

The alternative has constant pair \((H_p,H_q)=(A,B_{t_-})\), so (12) gives \(\gamma_{pq}(\mathbf P^+)\ge c_0\theta\).  On \(\lVert u-u_j\rVert_2\le h/3\), the defining property of \(\kappa\) gives
\[
 D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)=B_{t_-}+\theta\Delta
 =B_{t_\star+\theta/2}.
\]
The second inequality in (12) therefore gives
\[
 \gamma_{pq}(\mathbf P_j)\le-c_1\theta<0.
 \tag{19}
\]
Every component is in the composite null, whereas the causal rank margin of the alternative is at least \(c_0ah^s\).

Because \(T_j=B_{t_-}\) outside \(U_j\), (15)--(16) show that \(e_j=f_q^{(j)}-f_q^+\) is supported on \(E_j\).  Both pushforwards give \(E_j\) mass \(\mu_0(U_j)\), hence
\[
 \int_{E_j}e_j(y)\,dy=0.
 \tag{20}
\]
The cells are disjoint because the base linear map is injective.  The baseline density is the positive constant \(c_\Omega/\det B_{t_-}\).  Consequently (17), \(\lvert E_j\rvert\le Ch^d\), and the positive lower bound for \(f_q^+\) give
\[
 \chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)
 :=\int_{D_q}\frac{e_j(y)^2}{f_q^+(y)}\,dy
 \le C\theta^2h^d.
 \tag{21}
\]
The left side of (21) is the same for every \(j\).  Indeed, in the coordinate \(u=u_j+hv\),
\[
 T_j(u)=B_{t_-}u_j+h\{B_{t_-}v+\theta\nabla\kappa(v)\},
 \qquad
 DT_j(u)=B_{t_-}+\theta D^2\kappa(v),
\]
so both the local likelihood profile and its integration region differ between components only by translation through \(B_{t_-}u_j\).  For \(j\ne k\), disjointness and (20) give exactly
\[
 \int_{D_q}\frac{f_q^{(j)}f_q^{(k)}}{f_q^+}
 =1+\int_{D_q}e_j+\int_{D_q}e_k+\int_{D_q}\frac{e_je_k}{f_q^+}=1.
 \tag{22}
\]

Only regime \(q\) changes.  If \(L_j=d\mathbb P_{j,n}/d\mathbb P_n^+\), independence in \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}regime\text{-}samples}\) and (21)--(22) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_+[L_jL_k]=1\quad(j\ne k),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_+[L_j^2]=\{1+\chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)\}^{n_q}.
\]
Therefore, by direct expansion of the square of \(N^{-1}\sum_jL_j-1\),
\[
 \chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n},\mathbb P_n^+)
 =\frac{\{1+\chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)\}^{n_q}-1}{N}
 \le\frac{\exp(Cn_q\theta^2h^d)-1}{N}.
 \tag{23}
\]
The packing has constants \(0<c_N<C_N<\infty\) with \(c_Nh^{-d}\le N\le C_Nh^{-d}\).  Sample balance implies \(n_q\le n_\ast/c_n\).  Choose
\[
 h=K\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{1/(2s+d)}
\]
with fixed \(K>0\) sufficiently small.  Since \(\theta=ah^s\),
\[
 Cn_q\theta^2h^d
 \le \frac{Ca^2}{c_n}n_\ast h^{2s+d}
 =\frac{Ca^2K^{2s+d}}{c_n}\log n_\ast
 \le\frac14\log N
 \tag{24}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), after reducing \(K\).  Equations (23)--(24) imply \(\chi^2\le N^{-3/4}\to0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for any two laws \(Q\ll P\),
\[
 \lVert Q-P\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\frac12\mathbb E_P\left\lvert\frac{dQ}{dP}-1\right\rvert
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q,P)},
 \tag{25}
\]
so the total variation in (23) tends to zero.

Let \(\psi\in[0,1]\) be any randomized test having type-I error at most \(0.05\) at every null component.  Then
\[
 \mathbb E_{\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n}}\psi
 =N^{-1}\sum_j\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{j,n}}\psi\le0.05.
\]
By (25), \(\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n^+}\psi\le0.05+o(1)\), and hence its type-II error at \(\mathbf P^+\) is at least \(0.95-o(1)>0.20\).  Thus no test meeting \(\mathrm{def:testing\text{-}frontier}\) works at separation \(c_0ah^s\).  Since the admissible testing problem only becomes easier as its required separation increases,
\[
 \rho_n^\ast\ge c_0aK^s
 \left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), after decreasing the displayed constant if necessary.  This is the claimed inequality with \(c=c_0aK^s>0\), and the argument uses no endpoint-response assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:conditional-matching-separation]\label{prop:conditional-matching-separation}
At confidence level \(0.05\), suppose every conditional premise (A), (W1)--(W2), (K), (R), and (I1)--(I4) stated in \(\mathrm{prop:conditional\text{-}uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\) holds with the asserted class-uniform constants. The lower inequality is unconditional relative to that program.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume the complete conditional analytic and stochastic program stated in \(\mathrm{prop:conditional\text{-}uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\), including its corrected envelope-constant condition, and specialize that proposition to \(\delta=0.05\).  Since \(0.05\in(0,1/4)\), the proposition supplies a finite threshold \(n_0\) such that, whenever \(n_\ast\ge n_0\), the declared-pair test \(\Psi_n\) obeys

 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s:\,\gamma_{pq}\le0}
 \mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\Psi_n=1)\le0.05

and

 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s:\,\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n}

\begin{aligned}

 &=3\left(\frac2m+\frac{2M}{m^2}\right)r_{H,n}\\

\end{aligned}

 \log(C_0Rn_\ast/0.05)=\log A+\log n_\ast\le2\log n_\ast.

\begin{proposition}[prop:finite-negative-certificate]\label{prop:finite-negative-certificate}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), \(C_0>1\), and \(C_1>0\), with all three quantities fixed as \(n_\ast\) varies.  Put
\[
 D_0=\frac{4(m+M)}{m}+1.
\]
There exists \(n_0<\infty\) such that \(r_{H,n}\le m/(2D_0)<m/2\) for every \(n_\ast\ge n_0\).  For every such \(n_\ast\), every ordered pair \(p,q\in\mathcal A\) with \(p\ne q\), and every member of \(\mathcal M_s\), the following deterministic implication holds on \(\mathcal B_n\).  If \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n<0\), then clipping at \(m/4\) is inactive and
\[
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(
 \operatorname{sym}\{\widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\}\right)
 +(2M+r_{H,n})r_{H,n}<0.
\]
Given the validated oracle \(\mathcal O_n\), the exhaustive procedure in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) therefore terminates and returns \(\mathcal C_{pq,n}\ne\bot\).  Its output contains rational \(u_0,z,t_{\mathrm{ch}}\), with \(z\ne0\) and \(u_0,u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\), two outward-rounded interval enclosures containing the population increments \(F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\) and \(F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0)\), and a rational upper bound strictly below zero for their inner product.  Consequently,
\[
 \left\langle F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0),
 F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\right\rangle<0,
\]
so \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is not Euclidean monotone on \(D_p\).  This proposition is purely event-to-certificate: it asserts no probability bound for \(\mathcal B_n\), uses no endpoint-response premise, and does not assert equivalence between the source-product trigger and \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n<0\) outside the displayed eventual-radius regime.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 K:=\frac{2}{m}+\frac{2M}{m^2}=\frac{2(m+M)}{m^2},
 \qquad
 D_0:=\frac{4(m+M)}{m}+1,
 \qquad
 \overline r:=\frac{m}{2D_0}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The declared ranges \(0<m<M\) imply \(D_0>1\), so the constants in (1) are positive and \(\overline r<m/2\).  For every \(0\le r\le\overline r\), direct expansion gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K(m-r)^2-(2M+r)
 &=2m-D_0r+Kr^2\\
 &\ge 2m-D_0\overline r
 =\frac{3m}{2}>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]

We first obtain the eventual radius without invoking any endpoint-response or probability assertion.  By the definitions of \(\ell_n\), \(h_n\), and \(r_{H,n}\),
\[
 r_{H,n}=C_1h_n^s
 =C_1\left\{\frac{\log(C_0Rn_\ast/\delta)}{n_\ast}\right\}^{s/(2s+d)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(C_0>1\), \(C_1>0\), and \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\) are fixed; \(R<\infty\) by \(\mathrm{ass:regimes}\); and \(s/(2s+d)>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:smoothness\text{-}index}\) and the declared ranges of \(s\) and \(d\).  If \(A=C_0R/\delta\), then
\[
 0\le \frac{\log(An_\ast)}{n_\ast}
 =\frac{\log A}{n_\ast}+\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}
 \longrightarrow0;
 \tag{4}
\]
for example, \(\log x\le\sqrt{x}\) for all sufficiently large \(x\), so the second summand is at most \(n_\ast^{-1/2}\) eventually.  Equations (3)--(4) imply \(r_{H,n}\to0\).  Hence there exists \(n_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 r:=r_{H,n}\le\overline r=\frac{m}{2D_0}<\frac m2
 \qquad(n_\ast\ge n_0).
 \tag{5}
\]

Fix \(n_\ast\ge n_0\), an ordered pair \(p\ne q\), a member of \(\mathcal M_s\), and a sample point in \(\mathcal B_n\).  Because (5) is the radius premise of \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\), that deterministic theorem and \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) imply, uniformly in \(u\in\Omega\) and for \(a\in\{p,q\}\),
\[
 \widehat H_a(u)=\widetilde H_a(u),
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat H_a(u)-H_a(u)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le r,
 \qquad
 (m-r)I_d\preceq\widehat H_a(u)\preceq(M+r)I_d.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus clipping at \(m/4\) is inactive.  Notice that no probability bound for \(\mathcal B_n\) has been used.

Define, within this proof,
\[
 \widehat S(u):=\operatorname{sym}\!\left(
   \widehat H_q(u)\widehat H_p(u)^{-1}\right),
 \qquad
 \widehat A(u):=\operatorname{sym}\!\left(
   \widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\right).
 \tag{7}
\]
The fields are continuous by \(\mathrm{def:regularized\text{-}hessian\text{-}estimator}\), and inversion is continuous on the matrices bounded below in (6).  Since \(\Omega\) is compact, the Rayleigh formula
\(\lambda_{\min}(G)=\min_{\lVert v\rVert_2=1}v^{\mathsf T}Gv\) shows that \(u\mapsto\lambda_{\min}(\widehat S(u))\) is continuous and attains its minimum.  Choose a minimizer \(u_\ast\) and a unit eigenvector \(v_\ast\) corresponding to its smallest eigenvalue.  Symmetry of the Hessian fields gives the exact congruence identity
\[
 \widehat A(u)=\widehat H_p(u)\widehat S(u)\widehat H_p(u).
 \tag{8}
\]
With \(z_\ast:=\widehat H_p(u_\ast)^{-1}v_\ast\), equations (6)--(8) yield
\[
 z_\ast^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_\ast)z_\ast
 =v_\ast^{\mathsf T}\widehat S(u_\ast)v_\ast
 =\widehat\gamma_{pq},
 \qquad
 0<\lVert z_\ast\rVert_2\le(m-r)^{-1}.
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n<0\), then \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}<-Kr<0\), because \(w_n=Kr\) by \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\).  Applying the Rayleigh formula to \(\widehat A(u_\ast)\), and using \(\lVert z_\ast\rVert_2^{-2}\ge(m-r)^2\) together with the negativity of \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}(\widehat A(u))
 &\le \lambda_{\min}(\widehat A(u_\ast))\\
 &\le
 \frac{z_\ast^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_\ast)z_\ast}
      {\lVert z_\ast\rVert_2^2}\\
 &\le(m-r)^2\widehat\gamma_{pq}\\
 &<-K(m-r)^2r\\
 &<-(2M+r)r,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
where the last inequality is (2).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(
 \operatorname{sym}\{\widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\}\right)
 +(2M+r)r<0.
 \tag{11}
\]
This proves only the required one-way implication.

We next prove finite termination.  The function
\[
 (u,z)\longmapsto
 z^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u)z+(2M+r)r\lVert z\rVert_2^2
 \tag{12}
\]
is continuous.  Equation (11) and the Rayleigh formula supply a point and a nonzero direction at which (12) is negative.  The interior of the closed unit ball is dense in \(\Omega\), rational interior points are dense there, and \(\mathbb Q^d\setminus\{0\}\) is dense in \(\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\).  Strictness and continuity therefore give
\[
 u_0\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb Q^d,
 \qquad z\in\mathbb Q^d\setminus\{0\},
 \tag{13}
\]
such that
\[
 z^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_0)z
 +(2M+r)r\lVert z\rVert_2^2<0.
 \tag{14}
\]
For all sufficiently small \(t>0\), the compact chord \(u_0+[0,t]z\) lies in \(\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\).  On such a chord set
\[
 \widehat a(t):=\int_0^t\widehat H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau,
 \qquad
 \widehat b(t):=\int_0^t\widehat H_q(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau.
 \tag{15}
\]
Continuity of the Hessian fields implies
\[
 t^{-1}\widehat a(t)\longrightarrow\widehat H_p(u_0)z,
 \qquad
 t^{-1}\widehat b(t)\longrightarrow\widehat H_q(u_0)z
 \quad(t\downarrow0).
 \tag{16}
\]
Since a scalar quadratic form depends only on the symmetric part of its matrix, (7) and (16) give
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}t^{-2}
 \langle\widehat b(t),\widehat a(t)\rangle
 =z^{\mathsf T}\widehat H_q(u_0)\widehat H_p(u_0)z
 =z^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_0)z.
 \tag{17}
\]
By (14), (17), and density of \(\mathbb Q_{>0}\), there is a rational \(t_{\mathrm{ch}}>0\) for which both chord endpoints are interior and
\[
 \langle\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}}),
          \widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rangle
 +(2M+r)r t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2<0.
 \tag{18}
\]

Apply the validated range oracle from \(\mathrm{def:validated\text{-}range\text{-}oracle}\) to rational boxes covering successive rational subdivisions of this chord.  Multiplication of each interval matrix by \(z\), followed by multiplication by the rational segment length and interval addition, encloses each vector integral in (15).  The excess interval diameter tends to zero by that definition, and the true range diameter on each segment tends to zero by uniform continuity of the clipped fields on the compact chord.  Consequently the two vector enclosures converge to the integrals in (15).  Continuity of the finite-dimensional scalar product shows that their outward-rounded upper endpoints \(U_J\) obey
\[
 U_J\longrightarrow
 \langle\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}}),
          \widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rangle
 \quad(J\to\infty).
 \tag{19}
\]
The strict inequality (18) therefore gives a finite refinement level \(J\) such that
\[
 U_J+(2M+r)r t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2<0.
 \tag{20}
\]
Outward rational rounding returns a negative rational upper endpoint once (20) holds.  The enumeration in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) is exhaustive, so it eventually examines the finite-height triple (13), (18) and refines it through level \(J\).  Thus the procedure terminates and returns \(\mathcal C_{pq,n}\ne\bot\).

It remains to verify population validity.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:potential\text{-}holder}\) makes \(F_a=\nabla\varphi_a\) continuously differentiable on the chord, with derivative \(H_a\).  The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a_0&:=F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)
 =\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau,\\
 b_0&:=F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0)
 =\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}H_q(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
The error bound in (6), integrated along the chord, yields
\[
 \lVert a_0-\widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rVert_2
 \le rt_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2,
 \qquad
 \lVert b_0-\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rVert_2
 \le rt_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2.
 \tag{22}
\]
Every coordinate error is bounded by its Euclidean norm, so the coordinatewise enlargements prescribed in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) contain the two population increments in (21).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) and (6) also give
\[
 \lVert a_0\rVert_2\le Mt_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2,
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rVert_2
 \le(M+r)t_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2.
 \tag{23}
\]
Using the exact identity
\[
 \langle b_0,a_0\rangle-
 \langle\widehat b,\widehat a\rangle
 =\langle b_0-\widehat b,a_0\rangle
  +\langle\widehat b,a_0-\widehat a\rangle
\]
at \(t=t_{\mathrm{ch}}\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and (22)--(23), gives
\[
 \left|\langle b_0,a_0\rangle-
 \langle\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}}),
          \widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rangle\right|
 \le(2M+r)r t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus the scalar allowance in the certificate is valid, and (20) supplies a strict negative rational upper bound for \(\langle b_0,a_0\rangle\).  In particular,
\[
 \left\langle F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0),
 F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\right\rangle<0.
 \tag{25}
\]

Finally, \(t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\ne0\), and the lower ellipticity bound gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle a_0,t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\rangle
 &=\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}
 t_{\mathrm{ch}}z^{\mathsf T}H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau\\
 &\ge m t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]
Therefore \(x_0:=F_p(u_0)\) and \(x_1:=F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)\) are distinct points of \(D_p\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:counterfactual\text{-}map}\), with its causal interpretation supplied by \(\mathrm{ass:shared\text{-}rank\text{-}scm}\), gives
\[
 C_{q\leftarrow p}(F_p(u))=F_q(u).
 \tag{27}
\]
A map \(T\) is Euclidean monotone when \(\langle T(x_1)-T(x_0),x_1-x_0\rangle\ge0\) for every pair in its domain.  Applying (27) at the two chord endpoints makes this inner product equal to the strictly negative quantity in (25).  Hence \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is not Euclidean monotone on \(D_p\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} For fixed \(C_0>1\), \(C_1>0\), and \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), the proposition derives \(r_{H,n}\to0\) directly and proves that the negative margin endpoint forces the distinct source-product trigger, a terminating interval search, and a validated population chord on \(\mathcal B_n\). \textit{Gap.} The located Brenier estimation and multivariate-rank papers provide norm bounds or scalar tests, not a purely deterministic terminating interval certificate whose stronger source-product trigger is quantitatively forced by the scalar negative-margin endpoint in the proved eventual-radius regime. \textit{Consumer.} Model auditors retain the scalar margin test and, on \(\mathcal B_n\) at all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), a negative upper endpoint additionally yields rational source points, validated population-increment enclosures, and a strict sign witness; no probability guarantee for \(\mathcal B_n\) is asserted here.

\begin{proposition}[prop:commuting-sanity-reduction]\label{prop:commuting-sanity-reduction}
If all fields \(H_p(u)\) are simultaneously diagonal in one fixed orthonormal basis for every \(p\in\mathcal A\) and \(u\in\Omega\), then every \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is cyclically monotone and \(\gamma_{pq}=\inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}(H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1})>0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p,q\in\mathcal A\).  In the common fixed orthonormal basis from the hypothesis, write
\[
 H_p(u)=\operatorname{diag}(h_{p1}(u),\ldots,h_{pd}(u)),
 \qquad
 H_q(u)=\operatorname{diag}(h_{q1}(u),\ldots,h_{qd}(u)).
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\),
\[
 m\le h_{pj}(u),h_{qj}(u)\le M
 \qquad(1\le j\le d).
\]
The two Hessians commute pointwise, so \(\mathrm{thm:cyclic\text{-}frontier}\) makes \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) cyclically monotone.  Moreover,
\[
 H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1}
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{h_{q1}(u)}{h_{p1}(u)},\ldots,
 \frac{h_{qd}(u)}{h_{pd}(u)}\right)
 \succeq\frac mM I_d.
\]
It is already symmetric, and \(\mathrm{def:cross\text{-}hessian\text{-}margin}\) therefore gives
\[
 \gamma_{pq}
 =\inf_{u\in\Omega}\min_j\frac{h_{qj}(u)}{h_{pj}(u)}
 \ge\frac mM>0.
\]
This also agrees with the strong Euclidean monotonicity conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:euclidean\text{-}frontier}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction recovers ordinary scalar quantile rank preservation and a transparent multivariate commuting subclass. \textit{Gap.} CarlierChernozhukovGalichon2016VQR recovers scalar conditional quantiles, but the commuting reduction checks that the new cross-Hessian notation collapses to that known benchmark. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers can verify that the frontier does not alter classical scalar changes-in-changes or separable vector-quantile mechanisms.

\begin{proposition}[prop:test-threshold-sanity]\label{prop:test-threshold-sanity}
For one ordered pair, the deterministic margin event \(\lvert\widehat\gamma_{pq}-\gamma_{pq}\rvert\le w_n\) reduces exactly as follows: if \(\gamma_{pq}\le0\), then \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\le w_n\) and \(\Psi_n=0\); if \(\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\), then \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\ge2w_n>w_n\) and \(\Psi_n=1\). Thus the strict rejection rule has no unresolved equality case at the advertised power boundary.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the declared ordered pair \(p\ne q\) and suppose that the deterministic margin event
\[
 \left\lvert\widehat\gamma_{pq}-\gamma_{pq}\right\rvert\le w_n
\]
holds. By the definition of absolute value, this event gives the two inequalities
\[
 \widehat\gamma_{pq}\le \gamma_{pq}+w_n
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \widehat\gamma_{pq}\ge \gamma_{pq}-w_n.
\]
If \(\gamma_{pq}\le0\), the first inequality yields
\[
 \widehat\gamma_{pq}\le \gamma_{pq}+w_n\le w_n.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\) specifies the strict-threshold rule
\(\Psi_n=\mathbf 1\{\widehat\gamma_{pq}>w_n\}\). Therefore its indicator event is false and \(\Psi_n=0\).

If instead \(\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\), the second inequality gives
\[
 \widehat\gamma_{pq}
 \ge \gamma_{pq}-w_n
 \ge 3w_n-w_n
 =2w_n.
\]
The same definition gives
\[
 w_n=\left(\frac{2}{m}+\frac{2M}{m^2}\right)r_{H,n}.
\]
The declared ranges \(m>0\), \(M>m\), and \(r_{H,n}>0\) make the coefficient and hence \(w_n\) strictly positive. Consequently \(2w_n>w_n\), so \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}>w_n\), and the strict-threshold rule gives \(\Psi_n=1\). In particular, equality in the power premise \(\gamma_{pq}=3w_n\) still implies strict rejection, so no equality case remains unresolved.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This explicit reduction repairs the weak-boundary arithmetic and records the exact deterministic implication used for size and power. \textit{Gap.} The calculation is a required soundness reduction for the new band construction rather than a separate literature claim. \textit{Consumer.} Reviewers and implementers can audit the strict testing threshold without relying on an implicit equality convention.

 \lVert B_{h_n}v\rVert_\infty\le C_{\mathrm{aux}}\lVert v\rVert_{C^2(\Omega)},
 \quad
 \lVert B_{h_n}v\rVert_\infty\le C_{\mathrm{aux}}h_n^{-d/2}\lVert v\rVert_{H^2(\Omega)},
 \quad
 \lVert\nabla B_{h_n}v\rVert_\infty\le C_{\mathrm{aux}}h_n^{-d/2-1}\lVert v\rVert_{H^2(\Omega)}.
 \tag{K}
\]
Third, on \(\mathcal G_n\), if \(e_p=\widehat f_p-f_p\), the normalized response satisfies
\[
 \widehat\varphi_p-\varphi_p
 =\mathscr L_{f_p}e_p+\operatorname{Rem}_{f_p}(e_p),
 \quad
 \lVert\operatorname{Rem}_{f_p}(e_p)\rVert_{C^2(\Omega)}\le C_{\mathrm{aux}}\eta_n^2,
 \quad
 \lVert B_{h_n}\mathscr L_{f_p}(Q_{k_n}f_p-f_p)\rVert_\infty
 \le C_{\mathrm{aux}}k_n^s\{1+\log(1/k_n)\}.
 \tag{R}
\]
Fourth, define the centered influence kernel
\[
 G_{p,h_n,k_n}(u,y)
 =B_{h_n}\mathscr L_{f_p}\!\left[Q_{k_n}(\delta_y-P_p)\right](u).
\]
For all matrix-coordinate indices \(\iota,\jmath\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), its entries satisfy
\[
 \sup_{u\in\Omega}\mathbb E_{P_p}
   \lvert G_{p,h_n,k_n}(u,Y_{p,i})_{\iota\jmath}\rvert^2\le C_{\mathrm{aux}}h_n^{-d},
 \quad
 \sup_{u,y}\lVert G_{p,h_n,k_n}(u,y)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{aux}}(h_nk_n)^{-d/2},
 \tag{I1}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{u\ne v,\,y}
 \frac{\lVert G_{p,h_n,k_n}(u,y)-G_{p,h_n,k_n}(v,y)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}}
      {\lVert u-v\rVert_2}
 \le C_{\mathrm{aux}}h_n^{-1}(h_nk_n)^{-d/2}.
 \tag{I2}
\]
The resulting matrix-Bernstein and source-net event \(\mathcal Z_n\) obeys
\[
 \inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s}\mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\mathcal Z_n)
 \ge1-\frac\delta2,
 \tag{I3}
\]
and, on \(\mathcal Z_n\), simultaneously in \(p\),
\[
 \sup_{u\in\Omega}\left\lVert
 \frac1{n_p}\sum_{i=1}^{n_p}G_{p,h_n,k_n}(u,Y_{p,i})
 \right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C_{\mathrm{aux}}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast h_n^d}}
 +\frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast(h_nk_n)^{d/2}}
 \right\}.
 \tag{I4}
\]
Then the raw Hessian envelope is class-uniform:
\[
 \inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s}
 \mathbb P_{\mathbf P}\!\left\{
 \max_{p\in\mathcal A}\lVert\widetilde H_p-H_p\rVert_\infty
 \le\mathfrak R_{H,n}\right\}\ge1-\delta.
 \tag{B1}
\]
At \(h_n=(\ell_n/n_\ast)^{1/(2s+d)}\) and \(k_n=h_n\ell_n^{-2/s}\), there are \(C_1<\infty\) and \(n_0<\infty\), with \(n_0\) allowed to depend on the fixed \(\delta\), such that, for \(n_\ast\ge n_0\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{H,n}\le r_{H,n}=C_1h_n^s\le\frac m2,
 \qquad
 \inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s}\mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\mathcal B_n)\ge1-\delta.
 \tag{B2}
\]
Consequently, for every ordered pair \(p\ne q\),
\[
 \inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s}
 \mathbb P_{\mathbf P}\{\gamma_{pq}\in I_{pq,n}\}\ge1-\delta,
\]
and the declared-pair test has size at most \(\delta\) over \(\gamma_{pq}\le0\) and power at least \(1-\delta\) over \(\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\).  The selected upper radius has order
\[
 w_n\asymp
 \left\{\frac{\log(C_0Rn_\ast/\delta)}{n_\ast}\right\}^{s/(2s+d)}.
\]
The premises (A), (W1)--(W2), (K), (R), and (I1)--(I4) are a conditional analytic and stochastic program, not membership assumptions on \(\mathcal M_s\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume all premises in the proposed statement.  On \(\mathcal G_n\), (W2) makes the fallback inactive.  Since \(\widetilde f_p=Q_{k_n}P_{p,n_p}\) by Definition \(\mathrm{def:regularized\text{-}hessian\text{-}estimator}\), linearity of \(Q_{k_n}\) gives
\[
 e_p=\widehat f_p-f_p
 =Q_{k_n}(P_{p,n_p}-P_p)+(Q_{k_n}f_p-f_p).
 \tag{1}
\]
Apply \(B_{h_n}\) to the response expansion (R), use linearity of \(\mathscr L_{f_p}\), and recall that \(\widetilde H_p=B_{h_n}\widehat\varphi_p\).  We obtain the exact decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde H_p-H_p
 &=\{B_{h_n}\varphi_p-H_p\}
 +B_{h_n}\mathscr L_{f_p}(Q_{k_n}f_p-f_p)\\
 &\quad+B_{h_n}\mathscr L_{f_p}Q_{k_n}(P_{p,n_p}-P_p)
 +B_{h_n}\operatorname{Rem}_{f_p}(e_p).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
The empirical-measure definition and the definition of the centered influence kernel identify the third term as
\[
 B_{h_n}\mathscr L_{f_p}Q_{k_n}(P_{p,n_p}-P_p)
 =\frac1{n_p}\sum_{i=1}^{n_p}G_{p,h_n,k_n}(\,\cdot\,,Y_{p,i}).
 \tag{3}
\]
The first inequality in (K), the last inequality in (R), and (I4) bound the first three terms of (2).  The \(C^2\)-operator inequality in (K) and the remainder inequality in (R) give the separate, composed estimate
\[
 \lVert B_{h_n}\operatorname{Rem}_{f_p}(e_p)\rVert_\infty
 \le C_{\mathrm{aux}}^2\eta_n^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
Set \(C_\dagger=\max\{C_{\mathrm{aux}},C_{\mathrm{aux}}^2\}\).  On \(\mathcal G_n\cap\mathcal Z_n\), equations (2)--(4) imply, simultaneously over \(p\in\mathcal A\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widetilde H_p-H_p\rVert_\infty
 \le C_\dagger\Bigg\{&h_n^s+k_n^s[1+\log(1/k_n)]
 +\sqrt{\frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast h_n^d}}\\
 &+\frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast(h_nk_n)^{d/2}}+\eta_n^2\Bigg\}
 \le\mathfrak R_{H,n}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
The last inequality uses (C), hence in particular \(C\ge C_\dagger\), and Definition \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}resolution\text{-}envelope}\).  Notice that (4) is exactly why the strengthened condition (C) is needed.

For each \(\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s\), the union bound, (W1), and (I3) give
\[
 \mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\mathcal G_n\cap\mathcal Z_n)
 \ge1-\mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\mathcal G_n^{\mathsf c})
       -\mathbb P_{\mathbf P}(\mathcal Z_n^{\mathsf c})
 \ge1-\delta.
 \tag{6}
\]
Combining (5)--(6), then taking the infimum over \(\mathbf P\in\mathcal M_s\), proves (B1).

We next simplify the deterministic envelope at the selected resolutions.  Put \(A_\delta=C_0R/\delta\).  The restrictions \(C_0>1\), \(R\ge2\), and \(0<\delta<1/4\) imply \(A_\delta>1\), and
\[
 \ell_n=\log(A_\delta n_\ast)\asymp\log n_\ast,
 \qquad
 h_n\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7}
\]
The definition of \(h_n\) is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast}=h_n^{2s+d},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast h_n^d}}=h_n^s.
 \tag{8}
\]
Since \(k_n=h_n\ell_n^{-2/s}\), equations (7)--(8) yield
\[
 \log(1/k_n)=\log(1/h_n)+\frac2s\log\ell_n=O(\ell_n)
 \tag{9}
\]
and therefore
\[
 k_n^s\{1+\log(1/k_n)\}
 =h_n^s\ell_n^{-2}\{1+\log(1/k_n)\}
 =O(h_n^s/\ell_n),
 \tag{10}
\]
while
\[
 \frac{\ell_n}{n_\ast(h_nk_n)^{d/2}}
 =h_n^{2s}\ell_n^{d/s}=o(h_n^s).
 \tag{11}
\]
For (11), after division by \(h_n^s\) the remaining factor is \(h_n^s\ell_n^{d/s}\), which tends to zero by (7).

Substitution of \(k_n=h_n\ell_n^{-2/s}\) and \(\ell_n/n_\ast=h_n^{2s+d}\) into the definition of \(\eta_n\) gives
\[
 \eta_n
 =h_n^{s-r}\ell_n^{-2(s-r)/s}
 +h_n^{s-r}\ell_n^{(d+2r)/s}
 +h_n^{2s-r}\ell_n^{2(d+r)/s}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:dimension}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:smoothness\text{-}index}\) state \(d\ge2\) and \(s>d/2+3\), so \(s>4\).  From the declared definition \(r=(s+4)/4\),
\[
 s-2r=\frac{s-4}{2}>0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Using \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), divide the square of (12) by \(h_n^s\).  The resulting three upper-bound terms are
\[
 h_n^{s-2r}\ell_n^{-4(s-r)/s},
 \qquad
 h_n^{s-2r}\ell_n^{2(d+2r)/s},
 \qquad
 h_n^{3s-2r}\ell_n^{4(d+r)/s}.
 \tag{14}
\]
The powers of \(h_n\) in (14) are positive by (13).  More explicitly, for every fixed \(\beta>0\) and finite \(K\), (7) gives
\[
 h_n^\beta\ell_n^K
 =n_\ast^{-\beta/(2s+d)}
   \ell_n^{K+\beta/(2s+d)}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{15}
\]
Equations (14)--(15) prove
\[
 \eta_n^2=o(h_n^s).
 \tag{16}
\]
Equations (8), (10), (11), and (16), inserted into Definition \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}resolution\text{-}envelope}\), show that there is a finite constant \(C'\), depending only on the fixed displayed constants and the fixed \(\delta\), such that
\[
 \mathfrak R_{H,n}\le C'h_n^s
 \tag{17}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\).  Choose the fixed radius constant \(C_1\ge C'\).  Since \(m>0\) and \(h_n^s\to0\), there is a finite \(n_0\), allowed to depend on the fixed \(\delta\), such that for \(n_\ast\ge n_0\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{H,n}\le r_{H,n}=C_1h_n^s\le\frac m2.
 \tag{18}
\]
By Definition \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\), the event in (B1) is contained in \(\mathcal B_n\) when (18) holds.  Thus (B1) proves the probability assertion in (B2).

On \(\mathcal B_n\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\) applies by (18).  Its deterministic conclusions give \(\gamma_{pq}\in I_{pq,n}\) for every ordered pair.  For the declared pair, they also show that rejection under \(\gamma_{pq}\le0\), or failure to reject under \(\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\), can occur only on \(\mathcal B_n^{\mathsf c}\).  The probability assertion in (B2) therefore proves the stated coverage, size, and power bounds.  Finally, Definition \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\) and the definitions of \(h_n\) and \(\ell_n\) give the exact equality
\[
 w_n=\left(\frac2m+\frac{2M}{m^2}\right)C_1
 \left\{\frac{\log(C_0Rn_\ast/\delta)}{n_\ast}\right\}^{s/(2s+d)}.
\]
Its prefactor is finite and strictly positive, proving the asserted order.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This corollary audits every premise needed to promote the deterministic band to class-uniform probability and the selected upper rate. \textit{Gap.} Its endpoint, boundary-wavelet, extension-kernel, nonlinear-response, influence-envelope, increment, and concentration premises are not established by the cited literature and remain conditional. \textit{Consumer.} It is the proper input for the conditional matching upper frontier and any familywise interpretation of the rank-margin intervals.

\begin{proposition}[prop:lower-family-nonvacuity]\label{prop:lower-family-nonvacuity}
For every fixed \(d\ge2\) and \(s>d/2+3\), there is a finite threshold depending only on \(d\) and \(s\) such that the strict-slack clause in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\) holds for every tuple of existing class constants satisfying
\[
 0<m<\frac13,\qquad M>9,\qquad
 0<b<\frac{1}{9\lvert\Omega\rvert},\qquad
 B>\frac{2}{\lvert\Omega\rvert},
\]
and with \(L\) larger than that threshold.  In particular, these inequalities describe a nonempty open class-constant region on which the fixed-support lower family is admitted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(d\ge2\) and \(s>d/2+3\), and put \(c_\Omega=\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}\).  The first block of \(B_t\) is
\[
 B_t^{(2)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/5\\4t/5&9-8t\end{pmatrix},
\]
and every remaining eigenvalue is one.  At \(t_\star=(9/4)(\sqrt2-1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}B_{t_\star}^{(2)}=28-18\sqrt2,
 \qquad
 \det B_{t_\star}^{(2)}
 =\frac{144}{25}(\sqrt2-1)^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
The elementary inequalities \(24/17<\sqrt2<3/2\) imply
\[
 0<\operatorname{tr}B_{t_\star}^{(2)}<\frac{44}{17}<3,
 \qquad
 \frac{576}{625}<\det B_{t_\star}^{(2)}<\frac{36}{25}<2.
\]
In particular, the determinant is greater than \(1/2\).  Since the block is symmetric positive definite, its larger eigenvalue is at most its trace, while its smaller eigenvalue is its determinant divided by its larger eigenvalue.  Therefore
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(B_{t_\star}^{(2)})<3,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(B_{t_\star}^{(2)})
 >\frac{576}{625}\frac{17}{44}>\frac13.
 \tag{27}
\]
The functions \(t\mapsto\lambda_{\min}(B_t)\), \(t\mapsto\lambda_{\max}(B_t)\), and \(t\mapsto\det B_t\) are continuous.  Since \(t_\star\in(0,1)\), equations (26)--(27) give an \(\varepsilon>0\), depending on neither the class constants nor the lower-bound conclusion, such that
\[
 [t_\star-\varepsilon,t_\star+\varepsilon]\subset(0,1),
 \qquad
 \frac13 I_d\preceq B_t\preceq3I_d,
 \qquad
 \frac12\le\det B_t\le2
 \tag{28}
\]
whenever \(\lvert t-t_\star\rvert\le\varepsilon\).  Also \(I_d\preceq\overline A_0\preceq9I_d\) and \(\det\overline A_0=9\).

Consider the compact matrix family
\[
 \mathcal K=\{\overline A_0\}\cup
 \{B_t:\lvert t-t_\star\rvert\le\varepsilon\}.
\]
For \(A\in\mathcal K\), let
\[
 D_A=A\Omega,
 \qquad
 \phi_A(u)=\frac12u^{\mathsf T}Au
 -\frac{1}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}\int_\Omega\frac12v^{\mathsf T}Av\,dv.
\]
Then \(\int_\Omega\phi_A=0\), \(D^2\phi_A=A\), and the pushforward of \(\mu_0\) by \(\nabla\phi_A=A(\cdot)\) has the constant density
\[
 f_A(y)=\frac{c_\Omega}{\det A},\qquad y\in D_A.
 \tag{29}
\]
Equations (28)--(29) imply, uniformly over \(A\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \frac{c_\Omega}{9}\le f_A\le2c_\Omega.
 \tag{30}
\]

It remains to make all geometric and norm bounds uniform.  The ellipsoid boundary \(\partial D_A\) is the zero set of
\[
 y\longmapsto y^{\mathsf T}A^{-2}y-1.
\]
The spectral bounds in (28) and the corresponding bounds for \(\overline A_0\) make its gradient nonzero uniformly on this zero set.  The implicit-function theorem, applied on finitely many coordinate patches and then over the compact set \(\mathcal K\), gives a finite common bound \(K_{\mathrm{chart}}(d,s)\) for the \(C^{s+2}\) chart norms.  The principal curvatures are continuous positive functions of \((A,y)\) on the compact set \(\{(A,y):A\in\mathcal K,\ y\in\partial D_A\}\).  Hence there are finite constants
\[
 0<K_{\mathrm{curv},-}(d,s)
 \le K_{\mathrm{curv},+}(d,s)<\infty
 \tag{31}
\]
that bound every principal curvature from below and above.  Compactness of \(\mathcal K\), the displayed quadratic formula for \(\phi_A\), and (29) likewise give finite common bounds \(K_\phi(d,s)\) and \(K_f(d,s)\) for \(\lVert\phi_A\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\) and \(\lVert f_A\rVert_{C^s(D_A)}\).

Choose a finite threshold strictly larger than
\[
 K_{\mathrm{chart}},\quad K_\phi,\quad K_f,
 \quad K_{\mathrm{curv},+},\quad K_{\mathrm{curv},-}^{-1}.
 \tag{32}
\]
Fix any constants in the open region stated in the proposition, with \(L\) above this threshold.  Every number in the following finite list is then strictly positive:
\[
 \varepsilon,\\quad \frac13-m,\\quad M-9,\\quad
 \frac{c_\Omega}{9}-b,\\quad B-2c_\Omega,\\quad
 L-K_{\mathrm{chart}},\\quad L-K_\phi,\\quad L-K_f,\\quad
 K_{\mathrm{curv},-}-L^{-1},\\quad L-K_{\mathrm{curv},+}.
 \tag{33}
\]
Choose \(\eta>0\) smaller than every entry in (33).  Then \(\lvert t-t_\star\rvert\le\eta\) implies (28); the Hessian eigenvalues of both \(\overline A_0\) and \(B_t\) lie in \([m+\eta,M-\eta]\); (30) lies in \([b+\eta,B-\eta]\); the potential, density, and chart norms are at most \(L-\eta\); and (31) lies in \([L^{-1}+\eta,L-\eta]\).  These are exactly all the strict-slack requirements in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\).  Finally, the displayed inequalities are simultaneous strict inequalities and therefore form an open set; it is nonempty, for example by choosing one interior value for each of \(m,M,b,B\) and then taking \(L\) above (32).  This proves nonvacuity and does not invoke the lower-bound theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition proves that the strict-slack condition used by the lower theorem holds on an explicit nonempty open region of the existing class constants. \textit{Gap.} The lower theorem is conditional on nondegenerate class constants; this proposition shows concretely that its condition is not vacuous without changing the model class or assuming the lower-bound conclusion. \textit{Consumer.} Readers can verify admission of the fixed-support fuzzy-hypothesis family directly from open inequalities on the existing class constants.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The raw Hessian response is order zero and may accumulate a logarithm, while signed high-order smoothing need not preserve positive definiteness. Spectral clipping at \(m/4\) therefore totalizes the statistic. The proved deterministic theorem makes clipping inactive only on the explicitly defined event \(\mathcal B_n\) when \(r_{H,n}\le m/2\); it does not prove that event's probability. The conditional probability program still needs a uniform boundary-wavelet projection, a bounded polynomial-reproducing extension kernel, the endpoint linearization and quadratic remainder, influence-kernel variance and envelope bounds, an increment bound, and matrix-Bernstein/net concentration. Consequently the matching upper frontier cannot be routed through the deterministic event-to-band theorem alone. The finite certificate additionally uses a source-product allowance \((2M+r_{H,n})r_{H,n}t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2\), and its trigger is not equivalent to the scalar margin trigger at arbitrary radii. Validated interval arithmetic remains necessary, and the lower bound retains its strict-slack nondegeneracy qualification.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact Euclidean and cyclic structural frontiers, the NeurIPS-assumption-aligned open-neighborhood witness, the deterministic event-to-margin theorem, the explicit fixed-support Le Cam lower family, and the small-radius validated-certificate implication are proved components. The boundary-uniform Hessian endpoint modulus, quantitative quadratic remainder, boundary-wavelet and extension-kernel construction, and influence, increment, and concentration chain are open; none is supplied by the cited sources. Accordingly, class-uniform coverage, the upper separation rate, and a matching two-sided testing frontier are conditional on every premise explicitly listed in \(\mathrm{prop:conditional\text{-}uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\), whereas the lower separation rate is unconditional relative to that program under its strict-slack qualification. The certificate's source-product trigger is a distinct stronger sufficient condition, with no arbitrary-radius equivalence to the scalar negative-margin endpoint and no termination claim at zero margin. No adaptation, irregular-support, coordinate-invariance, unvalidated-numerics, or empirical-violation claim is made. The scalar and simultaneously diagonal cases still reduce to positive derivative ratios.
