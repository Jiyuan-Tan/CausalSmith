%%% FIELD proof-prop-dynamic-ot-counterexample
The independence of the binary regime label and \(U\) is a premise of the proposition.  We verify the remaining hypotheses and conclusions from \(\mathrm{def:quadratic\text{-}counterexample}\).

First, \(d=2>1\), \(\Omega_{\square}=(0,1)^2\), and \(\mu_{\square}\) has Lebesgue density one on \(\Omega_{\square}\).  Thus it is the continuous uniform law on \([0,1]^2\) after adjoining or removing the Lebesgue-null boundary.  The eigenvalues of \(A_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,9)\) are \(1\) and \(9\), while the eigenvalues of
\[
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/5\\4/5&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
are \(1-4/5=1/5\) and \(1+4/5=9/5\).  Hence both matrices are symmetric positive definite and invertible.  Consequently
\[
F_j(u)=A_j u=\nabla\!\left(\frac12u^{\mathsf T}A_j u\right),
\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
and each displayed potential is strictly convex.

For completeness, the optimal-transport property follows directly from the quadratic structure.  Fix \(j\in\{0,1\}\), put \(\phi_j(x)=x^{\mathsf T}A_jx/2\), and put \(\phi_j^*(y)=y^{\mathsf T}A_j^{-1}y/2\).  Completing the square gives, for every \(x,y\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
\phi_j(x)+\phi_j^*(y)-x^{\mathsf T}y
=\frac12\bigl(y-A_jx\bigr)^{\mathsf T}A_j^{-1}
       \bigl(y-A_jx\bigr)\ge 0,
\]
with equality when \(y=A_jx\).  If a coupling has marginals \(\mu_{\square}\) and \((A_j)_{\#}\mu_{\square}\), integration of this inequality bounds its value of \(\mathbb E[X^{\mathsf T}Y]\) above by a quantity depending only on the two marginals; equality is attained by \(Y=A_jX\).  Since
\[
\mathbb E\lVert X-Y\rVert^2
=\mathbb E\lVert X\rVert^2+\mathbb E\lVert Y\rVert^2
 -2\mathbb E[X^{\mathsf T}Y],
\]
the map \(F_j\) minimizes quadratic transport cost.

Because an invertible linear map is an open map and preserves boundedness and convexity, \(A_j\Omega_{\square}\) is bounded, open, and convex.  The change-of-variables formula gives the target density
\[
f_j(y)=\frac{1}{\det A_j}\,
\mathbf 1_{A_j\Omega_{\square}}(y).
\]
Here \(\det A_0=9\) and \(\det A_1=1-16/25=9/25\).  Thus the respective densities on their supports are the strictly positive finite constants \(1/9\) and \(25/9\).

It remains to check the asserted dynamic realization.  For \(j\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), define
\[
B_{j,\lambda}=(1-\lambda)I_2+\lambda A_j,
\qquad T_{j,\lambda}(u)=B_{j,\lambda}u.
\]
Every \(B_{j,\lambda}\) is symmetric positive definite because, for nonzero \(w\),
\[
w^{\mathsf T}B_{j,\lambda}w
=(1-\lambda)\lVert w\rVert^2
 +\lambda w^{\mathsf T}A_jw>0.
\]
The same quadratic argument used above therefore shows that \(T_{j,\lambda}\) is an optimal map from \(\mu_{\square}\) to \((T_{j,\lambda})_{\#}\mu_{\square}\).  Moreover, the Eulerian velocity on \(B_{j,\lambda}\Omega_{\square}\) is
\[
v_{j,\lambda}(x)=(A_j-I_2)B_{j,\lambda}^{-1}x,
\]
and it satisfies
\[
\frac{d}{d\lambda}T_{j,\lambda}(u)
=(A_j-I_2)u
=v_{j,\lambda}\bigl(T_{j,\lambda}(u)\bigr).
\]
Since
\[
\lambda_{\min}(B_{j,\lambda})
\ge \min\{1,\lambda_{\min}(A_j)\}>0,
\]
the maps \(v_{j,\lambda}\) are uniformly Lipschitz in space.  Finally, \(T_{j,0}=I_2\), \(T_{j,1}=F_j\), and \(T_{j,\lambda}=(1-\lambda)I_2+\lambda F_j\) is exactly the displacement interpolation induced by the optimal endpoint coupling.  This proves the claimed Lipschitz dynamic optimal-transport realization.

We now compute the Euclidean obstruction exactly.  Since
\[
A_0^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1/9\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_1A_0^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/45\\4/5&1/9\end{pmatrix},
\]
we have
\[
\operatorname{sym}(A_1A_0^{-1})
=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/9\\4/9&1/9\end{pmatrix}
\]
and hence
\[
\det\!\left(\operatorname{sym}(A_1A_0^{-1})\right)
=\frac19-\frac{16}{81}=-\frac7{81}<0.
\]
For the specified points,
\[
u=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u+z=\begin{pmatrix}1/2-2/9\\1/2+1/18\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}5/18\\5/9\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All four coordinates lie strictly between zero and one, so both endpoints belong to \(\Omega_{\square}\), and \(z\ne0\).  Linearity and symmetry of \(A_1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\langle F_1(u+z)-F_1(u),F_0(u+z)-F_0(u)\right\rangle
&=\langle A_1z,A_0z\rangle\\
&=z^{\mathsf T}A_1A_0z.
\end{aligned}
\]
Directly,
\[
A_1z=\begin{pmatrix}-8/45\\-11/90\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_0z=\begin{pmatrix}-2/9\\1/2\end{pmatrix},
\]
so
\[
z^{\mathsf T}A_1A_0z
=\frac{16}{405}-\frac{11}{180}
=-\frac7{324}<0.
\]
Thus the two distinct source points yield a strictly negative Euclidean cross-regime increment, contradicting the asserted strict-monotonicity conclusion.

Finally, the cone of symmetric positive-definite matrices is open.  On that cone the maps
\[
(B_0,B_1)\longmapsto
\det\!\left(\operatorname{sym}(B_1B_0^{-1})\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(B_0,B_1)\longmapsto z^{\mathsf T}B_1B_0z
\]
are continuous.  Their values at \((A_0,A_1)\) are respectively \(-7/81\) and \(-7/324\).  Therefore both remain negative on some open neighborhood of \((A_0,A_1)\) within the SPD pairs.  Every pair in a sufficiently small such neighborhood retains all properties checked above, while the same fixed chord remains a Euclidean monotonicity violation.  Hence the separate Brenier and dynamic-transport conditions do not imply the Euclidean strict-monotonicity conclusions in equations (13) and (72).
