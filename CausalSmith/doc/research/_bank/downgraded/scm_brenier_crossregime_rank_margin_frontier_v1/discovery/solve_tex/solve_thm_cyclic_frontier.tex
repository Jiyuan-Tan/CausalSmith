%%% FIELD proof-euclidean
Fix distinct \(p,q\in\mathcal A\) and a member of \(\mathcal M_s\), and write \(T=C_{q\leftarrow p}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:counterfactual\text{-}map}\), \(T=F_q\circ F_p^{-1}\).  The ellipticity in \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) makes \(F_p\) a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism between the interiors of \(\Omega\) and \(D_p\), with continuous extension to their boundaries.  Indeed, its derivative is \(H_p\succeq mI_d\), and
\[
 \langle F_p(u)-F_p(v),u-v\rangle
 =\int_0^1 (u-v)^{\mathsf T}H_p(v+t(u-v))(u-v)\,dt
 \ge m\lVert u-v\rVert_2^2,
\]
so it is injective, while its image is \(D_p\) by \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).  The chain rule therefore gives, for \(u\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\),
\[
 DT(F_p(u))=H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sym}DT(F_p(u))=S_{pq}(u).
 \tag{1}
\]
All fields in (1) extend continuously to the boundary by \(\mathrm{ass:potential\text{-}holder}\) and the lower eigenvalue bound.

Let
\[
 \mathfrak m(T):=\inf_{x\ne y\in D_p}
 \frac{\langle T(x)-T(y),x-y\rangle}{\lVert x-y\rVert_2^2}.
\]
The support \(D_p\) is convex by \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).  Hence, with \(v=x-y\), the fundamental theorem of calculus along the factual chord gives
\[
 \langle T(x)-T(y),v\rangle
 =\int_0^1v^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{sym}DT(y+tv)v\,dt
 \ge\gamma_{pq}\lVert v\rVert_2^2,
 \tag{2}
\]
where the last step uses \(\mathrm{def:cross\text{-}hessian\text{-}margin}\) and (1).  Thus \(\mathfrak m(T)\ge\gamma_{pq}\).

For the reverse inequality, take \(x\in\operatorname{int}(D_p)\), a unit vector \(a\), and \(t\ne0\) small enough that \(x+ta\in\operatorname{int}(D_p)\).  Differentiability yields
\[
 \lim_{t\to0}
 \frac{\langle T(x+ta)-T(x),ta\rangle}{t^2}
 =a^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{sym}DT(x)a.
 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(\mathfrak m(T)\le\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym}DT(x))\).  Taking the infimum over interior \(x\), using \(x=F_p(u)\), and then using continuity and density of \(\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\) in the compact ball \(\Omega\), gives
\[
 \mathfrak m(T)
 \le\inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}(S_{pq}(u))
 =\gamma_{pq}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) prove the displayed equality.

If \(S_{pq}(u)\succeq0\) everywhere, (2) with \(\gamma_{pq}\ge0\) proves Euclidean monotonicity.  Conversely, if \(T\) is Euclidean monotone, every quotient on the left of the definition of \(\mathfrak m(T)\) is nonnegative.  Equation (3), followed by boundary continuity, then gives \(S_{pq}(u)\succeq0\) for every \(u\in\Omega\).  Finally, a strong-monotonicity constant \(c>0\) exists exactly when \(\mathfrak m(T)>0\), which by (4) is exactly \(\gamma_{pq}>0\).  This proves all three assertions without identifying strict monotonicity with strong monotonicity.

The causal and observed-law objects remain distinct in this argument.  By \(\mathrm{ass:shared\text{-}rank\text{-}scm}\), observing the factual state \(Y(p)=x\) fixes the common rank at \(F_p^{-1}(x)\), whence the same unit has \(Y(q)=T(x)\).  By contrast, \(S_{pq}\) and \(\gamma_{pq}\) are functionals of the two regime-specific Brenier mechanisms supplied by the observed laws in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\); equations (1)--(4), rather than a definition, prove that this functional is the causal map's exact Euclidean modulus.

%%% FIELD proof-cyclic
Fix \(p\ne q\), put \(T=C_{q\leftarrow p}\), and use the Jacobian identity (proved directly again from \(\mathrm{def:counterfactual\text{-}map}\))
\[
 DT(F_p(u))=H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1}.
 \tag{5}
\]
We first prove the differentiable cyclic-monotonicity criterion needed here.  Adopt the convention that a map \(G\) is cyclically monotone when, for every finite cycle \(x_0,\ldots,x_N=x_0\),
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\langle G(x_i),x_i-x_{i+1}\rangle\ge0.
 \tag{6}
\]
If \(G\in C^1\) satisfies (6) on a convex domain, apply (6) to successively finer partitions of any closed piecewise \(C^1\) curve \(\Gamma\) in the interior.  The Riemann sums converge and give \(-\int_\Gamma G\cdot dx\ge0\).  Applying the same argument to the reversed curve gives the opposite inequality, hence
\[
 \int_\Gamma G\cdot dx=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Apply (7) to an axis-parallel rectangle of side lengths \(h,k\), divide by \(hk\), and let \(h,k\to0\).  The fundamental theorem of calculus on its four sides gives \(\partial_jG_i=\partial_iG_j\).  Thus \(DG\) is symmetric.  Applying (6) to a two-point cycle gives
\[
 \langle G(x)-G(y),x-y\rangle\ge0;
\]
letting \(y=x+ta\) and \(t\to0\) shows \(a^{\mathsf T}DG(x)a\ge0\).  Hence a \(C^1\) cyclically monotone map has symmetric positive-semidefinite Jacobian.

Conversely, suppose \(DG\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite.  Choose an interior base point \(x_0\) and define
\[
 V(x)=\int_0^1\langle G(x_0+t(x-x_0)),x-x_0\rangle\,dt.
\]
Writing \(v=x-x_0\), symmetry of \(DG\) and differentiation under the integral give, for every direction \(a\),
\[
 DV(x)[a]
 =\int_0^1\langle G(x_0+tv)+tDG(x_0+tv)v,a\rangle\,dt
 =\langle G(x),a\rangle.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(\nabla V=G\).  Along every chord the second derivative of \(V\) is \(a^{\mathsf T}DG\,a\ge0\), so \(V\) is convex.  The supporting-hyperplane inequalities
\[
 V(x_{i+1})\ge V(x_i)+\langle G(x_i),x_{i+1}-x_i\rangle
\]
sum to (6).  Continuity extends the conclusion to the boundary.  We have therefore derived, rather than invoked, that \(G\) is cyclically monotone if and only if \(DG\) is symmetric positive semidefinite in the present \(C^1\), convex-domain setting.

For \(G=T\), symmetry in (5) is equivalent to
\[
 H_qH_p^{-1}=(H_qH_p^{-1})^{\mathsf T}=H_p^{-1}H_q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_pH_q=H_qH_p,
 \tag{9}
\]
where both multiplications are legal because \(H_p\succeq mI_d\) by \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\).  If the matrices commute, the symmetric matrix in (5) has positive eigenvalues: it is similar to
\[
 H_p(u)^{-1/2}H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1/2}\succ0.
\]
It is therefore positive definite, so the converse half established through (8) makes \(T\) cyclically monotone.  If \(T\) is cyclically monotone, its Jacobian is symmetric by (7), and (9) forces pointwise commutation.  This proves the equivalence on all of \(\Omega\), boundary points following from continuity under \(\mathrm{ass:potential\text{-}holder}\).

%%% FIELD partial-oeq
Let \(f_t=f+te\), \(F_t=\nabla\varphi_t\), \(H_t=D^2\varphi_t\), and \(\psi_t=\partial_t\varphi_t\), wherever the normalized density-to-potential map is differentiable.  Differentiating
\[
 \log\det H_t(u)+\log f_t(F_t(u))=\log\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}
\]
gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(H_t^{-1}D^2\psi_t)
 +\nabla\log f_t(F_t)\cdot\nabla\psi_t
 =-\frac{e(F_t)}{f_t(F_t)}.
\]
The cofactor-divergence identity and the transport equation turn this into the normalized conormal problem
\[
 -\operatorname{div}(H_t^{-1}\nabla\psi_t)
 =\frac{e(F_t)}{f_t(F_t)},\qquad
 n\cdot H_t^{-1}\nabla\psi_t=0,qquad
 \int_\Omega\psi_t=0.
\]
Its compatibility condition is exact because change of variables gives
\[
 \int_\Omega\frac{e(F_t(u))}{f_t(F_t(u))}\,du
 =\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{D_p}e(y)\,dy=0.
\]
The desired logarithmic endpoint modulus follows if one proves, uniformly over the coefficient and boundary class, the global estimate
\[
 \lVert D^2v\rVert_\infty
 \le C\left\{\lVert r_0\rVert_\infty+
 \int_0^1\frac{\omega_{r_0}(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho\right\}
\]
for the preceding normalized conormal operator.  Indeed, here
\(\lVert r_0\rVert_\infty\le C\varepsilon\) and
\(\omega_{r_0}(\rho)\le\min\{C\varepsilon,C\rho^\beta\}\) for some fixed \(\beta>0\), so splitting at \(\rho=\varepsilon^{1/\beta}\) gives
\[
 \lVert D^2\psi_t\rVert_\infty
 \le C\varepsilon\{1+\log(C/\varepsilon)\}.
\]
Integrating in \(t\) produces the first requested bound.  A second differentiation gives a linear oblique problem whose interior and boundary data are quadratic in \(\psi_t\); a uniform normalized \(C^2\) inverse would then yield
\[
 \lVert\varphi_{f+e}-\varphi_f-\mathscr L_fe\rVert_{C^2(\Omega)}
 \le C\lVert e\rVert_{C^r(D_p)}^2.
\]
Thus the exact unclosed step is the stated boundary-uniform conormal Dini estimate together with a uniform twice-differentiable neighborhood for the nonlinear second-boundary-value map; neither property is contained in the frozen definition of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{resp}}\).

the remainder of the theorem is deterministic.  Weyl's inequality gives \(\widetilde H_p\succeq(m-r_{H,n})I_d\succeq(m/2)I_d\), so clipping at \(m/4\) is inactive.  The inverse identity gives

 \max_{p\ne q}\lvert\widehat\gamma_{pq}-\gamma_{pq}\rvert\le w_n.

 \eta_n

All constants in this proof depend only on the fixed class constants, the fixed interior packing region, and \(\kappa\), never on \(h\), \(j\), or \(n_\ast\).  Write \(A=\overline A_0\), \(\Delta=\overline A_1-\overline A_0\), and

 S(t)=\operatorname{sym}(B_tA^{-1}).

On the first two coordinates,
\[
 B_t=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/5\\4t/5&9-8t\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/9\\4t/9&1-8t/9\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The remaining block is \(I_{d-2}\).  Thus
\[
 \det S(t)_{1:2,1:2}=1-\frac{8t}{9}-\frac{16t^2}{81}.
 \tag{11}

The positive root of (11) is \(t_\star=(9/4)(\sqrt2-1)\in(0,1)\), its derivative there is \(-8/9-32t_\star/81<0\), and the other eigenvalue is positive.  Therefore the zero eigenvalue is simple.  Continuity of the other eigenvalue and the identity \(\lambda_{\min}=\det/\lambda_{\max}\) near \(t_\star\) give constants \(c_0,c_1>0\) such that, for all sufficiently small \(\theta>0\),

 \lambda_{\min}(S(t_\star-\theta/2))\ge c_0\theta,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(S(t_\star+\theta/2))\le-c_1\theta.
 \tag{12}

For \(b_j(u)=\theta h^2\kappa((u-u_j)/h)\), the scaling rule for every derivative of order \(\ell\) is
\[
 \lVert D^\ell b_j\rVert_\infty
 \le C_\kappa\theta h^{2-\ell}.
 \tag{13}

The analogous Hölder seminorm at order \(s+2\) obeys the same scaling.  Since \(\theta=ah^s\), (13) gives
\[
 \lVert D^2b_j\rVert_\infty\le C_\kappa\theta,
 \qquad
 \lVert b_j\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\le C_\kappa a.
 \tag{14}

Choose \(a\) so small relative to the strict slack \(\eta\) that every perturbation in (14), including the harmless normalization constant, uses at most \(\eta/2\) of that slack.  For sufficiently small \(h\), (14) also gives \(D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}\succeq mI_d\), so every perturbed gradient is injective by the same chord integration used in the Euclidean-frontier proof.

The bump and its gradient vanish in a neighborhood of \(\partial U_j\).  The homotopy

 T_\tau(u)=B_{t_-}u+\tau\nabla b_j(u),\qquad 0\le\tau\le1,

has everywhere nonsingular positive-definite derivative after the preceding choice of \(a\), and it equals \(B_{t_-}u\) on \(\partial U_j\).  For a point not on the common boundary image, its number of preimages in \(U_j\) cannot change along \(\tau\): local inverses prevent creation or cancellation in the interior, compactness supplies a convergent preimage at any first change, and the fixed boundary prevents escape through \(\partial U_j\).  At \(\tau=0\) this number is one on \(B_{t_-}U_j\) and zero outside its closure.  Hence

 \nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(U_j)=B_{t_-}U_j=E_j.
 \tag{15}

The same argument on \(\Omega\), or injectivity together with equality to the base map outside \(U_j\), gives the common target support \(D_q=B_{t_-}\Omega\).  The factual and non-focal supports are unchanged by construction.

Let \(c_\Omega=\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}\).  The change-of-variables identity for the diffeomorphism \(T_j=\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}\) is
\[
 f_q^{(j)}(T_j(u))\det D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)=c_\Omega.
 \tag{16}

The Hessians range in one fixed compact subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^d\).  The determinant and reciprocal determinant are Lipschitz there, so (14) and (16) imply
\[
 \lVert f_q^{(j)}-f_q^+\rVert_\infty\le C\theta.
 \tag{17}
\]
Moreover, the inverse-function derivatives of \(T_j^{-1}\) through order \(s+1\) are uniformly bounded because the smallest singular value of \(DT_j\) is at least \(m\).  Applying the product and composition rules to (16), and using \(\lVert D^2b_j\rVert_{C^s}\le Ca\), yields

 \lVert f_q^{(j)}\rVert_{C^s(D_q)}
 \le\lVert f_q^+\rVert_{C^s(D_q)}+Ca.
 \tag{18}
\]
Equations (14), (17), and (18), together with the admitted strict slack in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\), verify the potential norm, ellipticity, density bounds, and density norm.  The supports themselves are the slack ellipsoids, so their chart and curvature bounds are unchanged.  Equation (16) also proves normalization and positivity.  Thus \(\mathbf P^+,\mathbf P_j\in\mathcal M_s\) for all sufficiently small \(h\), with the common rank structural equation imposed exactly as in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).

 \gamma_{pq}(\mathbf P_j)\le-c_1\theta<0.
 \tag{19}

Because \(T_j=B_{t_-}\) outside \(U_j\), (15)--(16) show that \(e_j=f_q^{(j)}-f_q^+\) is supported on \(E_j\).  Both pushforwards give \(E_j\) mass \(\mu_0(U_j)\), hence

 \chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)

 \tag{21}

 T_j(u)=B_{t_-}u_j+h\{B_{t_-}v+\theta\nabla\kappa(v)\},
 \qquad

 \int_{D_q}\frac{f_q^{(j)}f_q^{(k)}}{f_q^+}

 \tag{22}

 \chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n},\mathbb P_n^+)
 =\frac{\{1+\chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)\}^{n_q}-1}{N}
 \le\frac{\exp(Cn_q\theta^2h^d)-1}{N}.
 \tag{23}

The packing has constants \(0<c_N<C_N<\infty\) with \(c_Nh^{-d}\le N\le C_Nh^{-d}\).  Sample balance implies \(n_q\le n_\ast/c_n\).  Choose

 h=K\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{1/(2s+d)}

 Cn_q\theta^2h^d
 \le \frac{Ca^2}{c_n}n_\ast h^{2s+d}

 \le\frac14\log N
 \tag{24}

for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), after reducing \(K\).  Equations (23)--(24) imply \(\chi^2\le N^{-3/4}\to0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for any two laws \(Q\ll P\),

 \lVert Q-P\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\frac12\mathbb E_P\left\lvert\frac{dQ}{dP}-1\right\rvert
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q,P)},
 \tag{25}
\]
so the total variation in (23) tends to zero.

Let \(\psi\in[0,1]\) be any randomized test having type-I error at most \(0.05\) at every null component.  Then
\[
 \mathbb E_{\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n}}\psi
 =N^{-1}\sum_j\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{j,n}}\psi\le0.05.
\]
By (25), \(\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n^+}\psi\le0.05+o(1)\), and hence its type-II error at \(\mathbf P^+\) is at least \(0.95-o(1)>0.20\).  Thus no test meeting the definition of \(\rho_n^\ast\) works at separation \(c_0ah^s\).  Since the admissible testing problem only becomes easier as its required separation increases,
\[
 \rho_n^\ast\ge c_0aK^s
 \left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), after decreasing the displayed constant if necessary.  No endpoint-response assertion was used.

%%% FIELD stmt-conditional-asymptotic
If \(\mathrm{oeq:global\text{-}endpoint\text{-}response}\) is resolved affirmatively, there is a finite constant \(C\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\),
\[
 \rho_n^\ast\le C\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}.
\]
Together with Theorem 6, this gives, for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), the conditional matching frontier
\[
 c\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}
 \le\rho_n^\ast\le
 C\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-conditional
Assume the endpoint-response OEQ is resolved affirmatively and apply \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\) at the fixed level \(\delta=0.05\).  Its test has type-I error at most \(0.05\) over \(\gamma_{pq}\le0\), and type-II error at most \(0.05<0.20\) over \(\gamma_{pq}\ge3w_n\).  It is therefore admissible in \(\mathrm{def:testing\text{-}frontier}\), which gives
\[
 \rho_n^\ast\le3w_n
 =3\left(\frac2m+\frac{2M}{m^2}\right)C_1
 \left\{\frac{\log(C_0Rn_\ast/0.05)}{n_\ast}\right\}^{s/(2s+d)}.
 \tag{26}
\]
For fixed \(C_0,R\), there are constants \(C_{\log}<\infty\) and \(n_1\) such that
\[
 \log(C_0Rn_\ast/0.05)\le C_{\log}\log n_\ast
 \qquad(n_\ast\ge n_1).
\]
Absorbing the fixed factors in (26) into \(C\) proves the proposed upper inequality for all \(n_\ast\ge n_1\).  The proposed asymptotic lower inequality is exactly \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\text{-}separation\text{-}lower}\); taking the larger of the two thresholds proves the matching display.

%%% FIELD def-finite-corrected
If
\[
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\operatorname{sym}\{\widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\}\right)
 +(2M+r_{H,n})r_{H,n}\ge0,
\]
return \(\mathcal C_{pq,n}=\bot\). Otherwise enumerate rational triples \((u_0,z,t_{\mathrm{ch}})\) by increasing height, subject to \(u_0,u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\). For each triple, subdivide its chord into rational segments and use \(\mathcal O_n\) to form outward-rounded interval vectors enclosing
\[
 \widehat A=\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}\widehat H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau,
 \qquad
 \widehat B=\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}\widehat H_q(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau.
\]
Also output coordinatewise enlargements by \(t_{\mathrm{ch}}r_{H,n}\lVert z\rVert_2\), which, on \(\mathcal B_n\), enclose the corresponding population increments. Refine until the rational upper endpoint for \(\langle\widehat B,\widehat A\rangle\), plus
\[
 (2M+r_{H,n})r_{H,n}t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2,
\]
is strictly below zero. Return the triple, the two enlarged population-increment interval vectors, and that negative rational upper bound. The strict source-product trigger and continuity of the clipped fields make this search finite on every triggered sample; on \(\mathcal B_n\), the added scalar term is a valid population inner-product error bound.

%%% FIELD stmt-finite-corrected
On \(\mathcal B_n\), given the validated oracle \(\mathcal O_n\) for the clipped fields, suppose
\[
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\operatorname{sym}\{\widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\}\right)
 +(2M+r_{H,n})r_{H,n}<0.
\]
Then clipping is inactive and the corrected exhaustive procedure in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) terminates. Its output \(\mathcal C_{pq,n}\) contains rational \(u_0,z,t_{\mathrm{ch}}\) with \(u_0,u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\), two outward-rounded interval enclosures for the population increments \(F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\) and \(F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0)\), and a rational upper bound strictly below zero for their inner product. Hence
\[
 \langle F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0),F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\rangle<0,
\]
so \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is not Euclidean monotone.

%%% FIELD proof-commuting
Fix \(p,q\in\mathcal A\).  In the common fixed orthonormal basis from the hypothesis, write
\[
 H_p(u)=\operatorname{diag}(h_{p1}(u),\ldots,h_{pd}(u)),
 \qquad
 H_q(u)=\operatorname{diag}(h_{q1}(u),\ldots,h_{qd}(u)).
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\),
\[
 m\le h_{pj}(u),h_{qj}(u)\le M
 \qquad(1\le j\le d).
\]
The two Hessians commute pointwise, so \(\mathrm{thm:cyclic\text{-}frontier}\) makes \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) cyclically monotone.  Moreover,
\[
 H_q(u)H_p(u)^{-1}
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{h_{q1}(u)}{h_{p1}(u)},\ldots,
 \frac{h_{qd}(u)}{h_{pd}(u)}\right)
 \succeq\frac mM I_d.
\]
It is already symmetric, and \(\mathrm{def:cross\text{-}hessian\text{-}margin}\) therefore gives
\[
 \gamma_{pq}
 =\inf_{u\in\Omega}\min_j\frac{h_{qj}(u)}{h_{pj}(u)}
 \ge\frac mM>0.
\]
This also agrees with the strong Euclidean monotonicity conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:euclidean\text{-}frontier}\).
