%%% FIELD sharp-separation-proof
All constants in this proof depend only on the fixed class constants, the fixed interior packing region, and \(\kappa\), never on \(h\), \(j\), or \(n_\ast\).  Write \(A=\overline A_0\), \(\Delta=\overline A_1-\overline A_0\), and
\[
 S(t)=\operatorname{sym}(B_tA^{-1}).
\]
On the first two coordinates,
\[
 B_t=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/5\\4t/5&9-8t\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/9\\4t/9&1-8t/9\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The remaining block is \(I_{d-2}\).  Thus
\[
 \det S(t)_{1:2,1:2}=1-\frac{8t}{9}-\frac{16t^2}{81}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The positive root of (11) is \(t_\star=(9/4)(\sqrt2-1)\in(0,1)\), its derivative there is \(-8/9-32t_\star/81<0\), and the other eigenvalue is positive.  Therefore the zero eigenvalue is simple.  Continuity of the other eigenvalue and the identity \(\lambda_{\min}=\det/\lambda_{\max}\) near \(t_\star\) give constants \(c_0,c_1>0\) such that, for all sufficiently small \(\theta>0\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(S(t_\star-\theta/2))\ge c_0\theta,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(S(t_\star+\theta/2))\le-c_1\theta.
 \tag{12}
\]

For \(b_j(u)=\theta h^2\kappa((u-u_j)/h)\), the scaling rule for every derivative of order \(\ell\) is
\[
 \lVert D^\ell b_j\rVert_\infty
 \le C_\kappa\theta h^{2-\ell}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The analogous Holder seminorm at order \(s+2\) obeys the same scaling.  Since \(\theta=ah^s\), (13) gives
\[
 \lVert D^2b_j\rVert_\infty\le C_\kappa\theta,
 \qquad
 \lVert b_j\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\le C_\kappa a.
 \tag{14}
\]
Choose \(a\) so small relative to the strict slack \(\eta\) that every perturbation in (14), including the harmless normalization constant, uses at most \(\eta/2\) of that slack.  For sufficiently small \(h\), (14) also gives \(D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}\succeq mI_d\).  Indeed, for distinct \(u,v\in\Omega\), integration along their chord gives
\[
 (u-v)^{\mathsf T}\{\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)-\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(v)\}
 =\int_0^1 (u-v)^{\mathsf T}D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(v+\tau(u-v))(u-v)\,d\tau
 \ge m\lVert u-v\rVert_2^2>0,
\]
so every perturbed gradient is injective.

The bump and its gradient vanish in a neighborhood of \(\partial U_j\).  The homotopy
\[
 T_\tau(u)=B_{t_-}u+\tau\nabla b_j(u),
 \qquad 0\le\tau\le1,
\]
has everywhere nonsingular positive-definite derivative after the preceding choice of \(a\), and it equals \(B_{t_-}u\) on \(\partial U_j\).  For a point not on the common boundary image, its number of preimages in \(U_j\) cannot change along \(\tau\): local inverses prevent creation or cancellation in the interior, compactness supplies a convergent preimage at any first change, and the fixed boundary prevents escape through \(\partial U_j\).  At \(\tau=0\) this number is one on \(B_{t_-}U_j\) and zero outside its closure.  Hence
\[
 \nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}(U_j)=B_{t_-}U_j=E_j.
 \tag{15}
\]
The same argument on \(\Omega\), or injectivity together with equality to the base map outside \(U_j\), gives the common target support \(D_q=B_{t_-}\Omega\).  The factual and non-focal supports are unchanged by construction.

Let \(c_\Omega=\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}\).  The change-of-variables identity for the diffeomorphism \(T_j=\nabla\varphi_q^{(j)}\) is
\[
 f_q^{(j)}(T_j(u))\det D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)=c_\Omega.
 \tag{16}
\]
The Hessians range in one fixed compact subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^d\).  The determinant and reciprocal determinant are Lipschitz there, so (14) and (16), together with the fact that \(f_q^+=c_\Omega/\det B_{t_-}\) is constant, imply
\[
 \lVert f_q^{(j)}-f_q^+\rVert_\infty\le C\theta.
 \tag{17}
\]
Moreover, the inverse-function derivatives of \(T_j^{-1}\) through order \(s+1\) are uniformly bounded because the smallest singular value of \(DT_j\) is at least \(m\).  Applying the product and composition rules to (16), and using \(\lVert D^2b_j\rVert_{C^s}\le Ca\), yields
\[
 \lVert f_q^{(j)}\rVert_{C^s(D_q)}
 \le\lVert f_q^+\rVert_{C^s(D_q)}+Ca.
 \tag{18}
\]
Equations (14), (17), and (18), together with the admitted strict slack in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\), verify the potential norm, ellipticity, density bounds, and density norm.  The supports themselves are the slack ellipsoids, so their chart and curvature bounds are unchanged.  Equation (16) also proves normalization and positivity.  Thus \(\mathbf P^+,\mathbf P_j\in\mathcal M_s\) for all sufficiently small \(h\), with the common-rank structural equation imposed exactly as in \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\).

The alternative has constant pair \((H_p,H_q)=(A,B_{t_-})\), so (12) gives \(\gamma_{pq}(\mathbf P^+)\ge c_0\theta\).  On \(\lVert u-u_j\rVert_2\le h/3\), the defining property of \(\kappa\) gives
\[
 D^2\varphi_q^{(j)}(u)=B_{t_-}+\theta\Delta
 =B_{t_\star+\theta/2}.
\]
The second inequality in (12) therefore gives
\[
 \gamma_{pq}(\mathbf P_j)\le-c_1\theta<0.
 \tag{19}
\]
Every component is in the composite null, whereas the causal rank margin of the alternative is at least \(c_0ah^s\).

Because \(T_j=B_{t_-}\) outside \(U_j\), (15)--(16) show that \(e_j=f_q^{(j)}-f_q^+\) is supported on \(E_j\).  Both pushforwards give \(E_j\) mass \(\mu_0(U_j)\), hence
\[
 \int_{E_j}e_j(y)\,dy=0.
 \tag{20}
\]
The cells are disjoint because the base linear map is injective.  The baseline density is the positive constant \(c_\Omega/\det B_{t_-}\).  Consequently (17), \(\lvert E_j\rvert\le Ch^d\), and the positive lower bound for \(f_q^+\) give
\[
 \chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)
 :=\int_{D_q}\frac{e_j(y)^2}{f_q^+(y)}\,dy
 \le C\theta^2h^d.
 \tag{21}
\]
The left side of (21) is the same for every \(j\).  Indeed, in the coordinate \(u=u_j+hv\),
\[
 T_j(u)=B_{t_-}u_j+h\{B_{t_-}v+\theta\nabla\kappa(v)\},
 \qquad
 DT_j(u)=B_{t_-}+\theta D^2\kappa(v),
\]
so both the local likelihood profile and its integration region differ between components only by translation through \(B_{t_-}u_j\).  For \(j\ne k\), disjointness and (20) give exactly
\[
 \int_{D_q}\frac{f_q^{(j)}f_q^{(k)}}{f_q^+}
 =1+\int_{D_q}e_j+\int_{D_q}e_k+\int_{D_q}\frac{e_je_k}{f_q^+}=1.
 \tag{22}
\]

Only regime \(q\) changes.  If \(L_j=d\mathbb P_{j,n}/d\mathbb P_n^+\), independence in \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}regime\text{-}samples}\) and (21)--(22) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_+[L_jL_k]=1\quad(j\ne k),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_+[L_j^2]=\{1+\chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)\}^{n_q}.
\]
Therefore, by direct expansion of the square of \(N^{-1}\sum_jL_j-1\),
\[
 \chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n},\mathbb P_n^+)
 =\frac{\{1+\chi^2(f_q^{(j)},f_q^+)\}^{n_q}-1}{N}
 \le\frac{\exp(Cn_q\theta^2h^d)-1}{N}.
 \tag{23}
\]
The packing has constants \(0<c_N<C_N<\infty\) with \(c_Nh^{-d}\le N\le C_Nh^{-d}\).  Sample balance implies \(n_q\le n_\ast/c_n\).  Choose
\[
 h=K\left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{1/(2s+d)}
\]
with fixed \(K>0\) sufficiently small.  Since \(\theta=ah^s\),
\[
 Cn_q\theta^2h^d
 \le \frac{Ca^2}{c_n}n_\ast h^{2s+d}
 =\frac{Ca^2K^{2s+d}}{c_n}\log n_\ast
 \le\frac14\log N
 \tag{24}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), after reducing \(K\).  Equations (23)--(24) imply \(\chi^2\le N^{-3/4}\to0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for any two laws \(Q\ll P\),
\[
 \lVert Q-P\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\frac12\mathbb E_P\left\lvert\frac{dQ}{dP}-1\right\rvert
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q,P)},
 \tag{25}
\]
so the total variation in (23) tends to zero.

Let \(\psi\in[0,1]\) be any randomized test having type-I error at most \(0.05\) at every null component.  Then
\[
 \mathbb E_{\overline{\mathbb P}_{0,n}}\psi
 =N^{-1}\sum_j\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{j,n}}\psi\le0.05.
\]
By (25), \(\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n^+}\psi\le0.05+o(1)\), and hence its type-II error at \(\mathbf P^+\) is at least \(0.95-o(1)>0.20\).  Thus no test meeting \(\mathrm{def:testing\text{-}frontier}\) works at separation \(c_0ah^s\).  Since the admissible testing problem only becomes easier as its required separation increases,
\[
 \rho_n^\ast\ge c_0aK^s
 \left(\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}\right)^{s/(2s+d)}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n_\ast\), after decreasing the displayed constant if necessary.  This is the claimed inequality with \(c=c_0aK^s>0\), and the argument uses no endpoint-response assertion.
%%% FIELD lower-family-nonvacuity-statement
For every fixed \(d\ge2\) and \(s>d/2+3\), there is a finite threshold depending only on \(d\) and \(s\) such that the strict-slack clause in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\) holds for every tuple of existing class constants satisfying
\[
 0<m<\frac13,\qquad M>9,\qquad
 0<b<\frac{1}{9\lvert\Omega\rvert},\qquad
 B>\frac{2}{\lvert\Omega\rvert},
\]
and with \(L\) larger than that threshold.  In particular, these inequalities describe a nonempty open class-constant region on which the fixed-support lower family is admitted.
%%% FIELD lower-family-nonvacuity-proof
Fix \(d\ge2\) and \(s>d/2+3\), and put \(c_\Omega=\lvert\Omega\rvert^{-1}\).  The first block of \(B_t\) is
\[
 B_t^{(2)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&4t/5\\4t/5&9-8t\end{pmatrix},
\]
and every remaining eigenvalue is one.  At \(t_\star=(9/4)(\sqrt2-1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}B_{t_\star}^{(2)}=28-18\sqrt2,
 \qquad
 \det B_{t_\star}^{(2)}
 =\frac{144}{25}(\sqrt2-1)^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
The elementary inequalities \(24/17<\sqrt2<3/2\) imply
\[
 0<\operatorname{tr}B_{t_\star}^{(2)}<\frac{44}{17}<3,
 \qquad
 \frac{576}{625}<\det B_{t_\star}^{(2)}<\frac{36}{25}<2.
\]
In particular, the determinant is greater than \(1/2\).  Since the block is symmetric positive definite, its larger eigenvalue is at most its trace, while its smaller eigenvalue is its determinant divided by its larger eigenvalue.  Therefore
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(B_{t_\star}^{(2)})<3,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(B_{t_\star}^{(2)})
 >\frac{576}{625}\frac{17}{44}>\frac13.
 \tag{27}
\]
The functions \(t\mapsto\lambda_{\min}(B_t)\), \(t\mapsto\lambda_{\max}(B_t)\), and \(t\mapsto\det B_t\) are continuous.  Since \(t_\star\in(0,1)\), equations (26)--(27) give an \(\varepsilon>0\), depending on neither the class constants nor the lower-bound conclusion, such that
\[
 [t_\star-\varepsilon,t_\star+\varepsilon]\subset(0,1),
 \qquad
 \frac13 I_d\preceq B_t\preceq3I_d,
 \qquad
 \frac12\le\det B_t\le2
 \tag{28}
\]
whenever \(\lvert t-t_\star\rvert\le\varepsilon\).  Also \(I_d\preceq\overline A_0\preceq9I_d\) and \(\det\overline A_0=9\).

Consider the compact matrix family
\[
 \mathcal K=\{\overline A_0\}\cup
 \{B_t:\lvert t-t_\star\rvert\le\varepsilon\}.
\]
For \(A\in\mathcal K\), let
\[
 D_A=A\Omega,
 \qquad
 \phi_A(u)=\frac12u^{\mathsf T}Au
 -\frac{1}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}\int_\Omega\frac12v^{\mathsf T}Av\,dv.
\]
Then \(\int_\Omega\phi_A=0\), \(D^2\phi_A=A\), and the pushforward of \(\mu_0\) by \(\nabla\phi_A=A(\cdot)\) has the constant density
\[
 f_A(y)=\frac{c_\Omega}{\det A},\qquad y\in D_A.
 \tag{29}
\]
Equations (28)--(29) imply, uniformly over \(A\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \frac{c_\Omega}{9}\le f_A\le2c_\Omega.
 \tag{30}
\]

It remains to make all geometric and norm bounds uniform.  The ellipsoid boundary \(\partial D_A\) is the zero set of
\[
 y\longmapsto y^{\mathsf T}A^{-2}y-1.
\]
The spectral bounds in (28) and the corresponding bounds for \(\overline A_0\) make its gradient nonzero uniformly on this zero set.  The implicit-function theorem, applied on finitely many coordinate patches and then over the compact set \(\mathcal K\), gives a finite common bound \(K_{\mathrm{chart}}(d,s)\) for the \(C^{s+2}\) chart norms.  The principal curvatures are continuous positive functions of \((A,y)\) on the compact set \(\{(A,y):A\in\mathcal K,\ y\in\partial D_A\}\).  Hence there are finite constants
\[
 0<K_{\mathrm{curv},-}(d,s)
 \le K_{\mathrm{curv},+}(d,s)<\infty
 \tag{31}
\]
that bound every principal curvature from below and above.  Compactness of \(\mathcal K\), the displayed quadratic formula for \(\phi_A\), and (29) likewise give finite common bounds \(K_\phi(d,s)\) and \(K_f(d,s)\) for \(\lVert\phi_A\rVert_{C^{s+2}(\Omega)}\) and \(\lVert f_A\rVert_{C^s(D_A)}\).

Choose a finite threshold strictly larger than
\[
 K_{\mathrm{chart}},\quad K_\phi,\quad K_f,
 \quad K_{\mathrm{curv},+},\quad K_{\mathrm{curv},-}^{-1}.
 \tag{32}
\]
Fix any constants in the open region stated in the proposition, with \(L\) above this threshold.  Every number in the following finite list is then strictly positive:
\[
 \varepsilon,quad \frac13-m,quad M-9,quad
 \frac{c_\Omega}{9}-b,quad B-2c_\Omega,quad
 L-K_{\mathrm{chart}},quad L-K_\phi,quad L-K_f,quad
 K_{\mathrm{curv},-}-L^{-1},quad L-K_{\mathrm{curv},+}.
 \tag{33}
\]
Choose \(\eta>0\) smaller than every entry in (33).  Then \(\lvert t-t_\star\rvert\le\eta\) implies (28); the Hessian eigenvalues of both \(\overline A_0\) and \(B_t\) lie in \([m+\eta,M-\eta]\); (30) lies in \([b+\eta,B-\eta]\); the potential, density, and chart norms are at most \(L-\eta\); and (31) lies in \([L^{-1}+\eta,L-\eta]\).  These are exactly all the strict-slack requirements in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}support\text{-}lower\text{-}family}\).  Finally, the displayed inequalities are simultaneous strict inequalities and therefore form an open set; it is nonempty, for example by choosing one interior value for each of \(m,M,b,B\) and then taking \(L\) above (32).  This proves nonvacuity and does not invoke the lower-bound theorem.
