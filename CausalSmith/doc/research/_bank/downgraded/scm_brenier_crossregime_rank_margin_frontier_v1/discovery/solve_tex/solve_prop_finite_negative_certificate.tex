%%% FIELD prop-statement
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), \(C_0>1\), and \(C_1>0\), with all three quantities fixed as \(n_\ast\) varies.  Put
\[
 D_0=\frac{4(m+M)}{m}+1.
\]
There exists \(n_0<\infty\) such that \(r_{H,n}\le m/(2D_0)<m/2\) for every \(n_\ast\ge n_0\).  For every such \(n_\ast\), every ordered pair \(p,q\in\mathcal A\) with \(p\ne q\), and every member of \(\mathcal M_s\), the following deterministic implication holds on \(\mathcal B_n\).  If \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n<0\), then clipping at \(m/4\) is inactive and
\[
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(
 \operatorname{sym}\{\widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\}\right)
 +(2M+r_{H,n})r_{H,n}<0.
\]
Given the validated oracle \(\mathcal O_n\), the exhaustive procedure in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) therefore terminates and returns \(\mathcal C_{pq,n}\ne\bot\).  Its output contains rational \(u_0,z,t_{\mathrm{ch}}\), with \(z\ne0\) and \(u_0,u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\), two outward-rounded interval enclosures containing the population increments \(F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\) and \(F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0)\), and a rational upper bound strictly below zero for their inner product.  Consequently,
\[
 \left\langle F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0),
 F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\right\rangle<0,
\]
so \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is not Euclidean monotone on \(D_p\).  This proposition is purely event-to-certificate: it asserts no probability bound for \(\mathcal B_n\), uses no endpoint-response premise, and does not assert equivalence between the source-product trigger and \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n<0\) outside the displayed eventual-radius regime.
%%% FIELD prop-proof
Set
\[
 K:=\frac{2}{m}+\frac{2M}{m^2}=\frac{2(m+M)}{m^2},
 \qquad
 D_0:=\frac{4(m+M)}{m}+1,
 \qquad
 \overline r:=\frac{m}{2D_0}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The declared ranges \(0<m<M\) imply \(D_0>1\), so the constants in (1) are positive and \(\overline r<m/2\).  For every \(0\le r\le\overline r\), direct expansion gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K(m-r)^2-(2M+r)
 &=2m-D_0r+Kr^2\\
 &\ge 2m-D_0\overline r
 =\frac{3m}{2}>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]

We first obtain the eventual radius without invoking any endpoint-response or probability assertion.  By the definitions of \(\ell_n\), \(h_n\), and \(r_{H,n}\),
\[
 r_{H,n}=C_1h_n^s
 =C_1\left\{\frac{\log(C_0Rn_\ast/\delta)}{n_\ast}\right\}^{s/(2s+d)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(C_0>1\), \(C_1>0\), and \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\) are fixed; \(R<\infty\) by \(\mathrm{ass:regimes}\); and \(s/(2s+d)>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:smoothness\text{-}index}\) and the declared ranges of \(s\) and \(d\).  If \(A=C_0R/\delta\), then
\[
 0\le \frac{\log(An_\ast)}{n_\ast}
 =\frac{\log A}{n_\ast}+\frac{\log n_\ast}{n_\ast}
 \longrightarrow0;
 \tag{4}
\]
for example, \(\log x\le\sqrt{x}\) for all sufficiently large \(x\), so the second summand is at most \(n_\ast^{-1/2}\) eventually.  Equations (3)--(4) imply \(r_{H,n}\to0\).  Hence there exists \(n_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 r:=r_{H,n}\le\overline r=\frac{m}{2D_0}<\frac m2
 \qquad(n_\ast\ge n_0).
 \tag{5}
\]

Fix \(n_\ast\ge n_0\), an ordered pair \(p\ne q\), a member of \(\mathcal M_s\), and a sample point in \(\mathcal B_n\).  Because (5) is the radius premise of \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}margin\text{-}band}\), that deterministic theorem and \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) imply, uniformly in \(u\in\Omega\) and for \(a\in\{p,q\}\),
\[
 \widehat H_a(u)=\widetilde H_a(u),
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat H_a(u)-H_a(u)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le r,
 \qquad
 (m-r)I_d\preceq\widehat H_a(u)\preceq(M+r)I_d.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus clipping at \(m/4\) is inactive.  Notice that no probability bound for \(\mathcal B_n\) has been used.

Define, within this proof,
\[
 \widehat S(u):=\operatorname{sym}\!\left(
   \widehat H_q(u)\widehat H_p(u)^{-1}\right),
 \qquad
 \widehat A(u):=\operatorname{sym}\!\left(
   \widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\right).
 \tag{7}
\]
The fields are continuous by \(\mathrm{def:regularized\text{-}hessian\text{-}estimator}\), and inversion is continuous on the matrices bounded below in (6).  Since \(\Omega\) is compact, the Rayleigh formula
\(\lambda_{\min}(G)=\min_{\lVert v\rVert_2=1}v^{\mathsf T}Gv\) shows that \(u\mapsto\lambda_{\min}(\widehat S(u))\) is continuous and attains its minimum.  Choose a minimizer \(u_\ast\) and a unit eigenvector \(v_\ast\) corresponding to its smallest eigenvalue.  Symmetry of the Hessian fields gives the exact congruence identity
\[
 \widehat A(u)=\widehat H_p(u)\widehat S(u)\widehat H_p(u).
 \tag{8}
\]
With \(z_\ast:=\widehat H_p(u_\ast)^{-1}v_\ast\), equations (6)--(8) yield
\[
 z_\ast^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_\ast)z_\ast
 =v_\ast^{\mathsf T}\widehat S(u_\ast)v_\ast
 =\widehat\gamma_{pq},
 \qquad
 0<\lVert z_\ast\rVert_2\le(m-r)^{-1}.
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}+w_n<0\), then \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}<-Kr<0\), because \(w_n=Kr\) by \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\text{-}band}\).  Applying the Rayleigh formula to \(\widehat A(u_\ast)\), and using \(\lVert z_\ast\rVert_2^{-2}\ge(m-r)^2\) together with the negativity of \(\widehat\gamma_{pq}\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}(\widehat A(u))
 &\le \lambda_{\min}(\widehat A(u_\ast))\\
 &\le
 \frac{z_\ast^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_\ast)z_\ast}
      {\lVert z_\ast\rVert_2^2}\\
 &\le(m-r)^2\widehat\gamma_{pq}\\
 &<-K(m-r)^2r\\
 &<-(2M+r)r,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
where the last inequality is (2).  Therefore
\[
 \inf_{u\in\Omega}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(
 \operatorname{sym}\{\widehat H_p(u)\widehat H_q(u)\}\right)
 +(2M+r)r<0.
 \tag{11}
\]
This proves only the required one-way implication.

We next prove finite termination.  The function
\[
 (u,z)\longmapsto
 z^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u)z+(2M+r)r\lVert z\rVert_2^2
 \tag{12}
\]
is continuous.  Equation (11) and the Rayleigh formula supply a point and a nonzero direction at which (12) is negative.  The interior of the closed unit ball is dense in \(\Omega\), rational interior points are dense there, and \(\mathbb Q^d\setminus\{0\}\) is dense in \(\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\).  Strictness and continuity therefore give
\[
 u_0\in\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb Q^d,
 \qquad z\in\mathbb Q^d\setminus\{0\},
 \tag{13}
\]
such that
\[
 z^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_0)z
 +(2M+r)r\lVert z\rVert_2^2<0.
 \tag{14}
\]
For all sufficiently small \(t>0\), the compact chord \(u_0+[0,t]z\) lies in \(\operatorname{int}(\Omega)\).  On such a chord set
\[
 \widehat a(t):=\int_0^t\widehat H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau,
 \qquad
 \widehat b(t):=\int_0^t\widehat H_q(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau.
 \tag{15}
\]
Continuity of the Hessian fields implies
\[
 t^{-1}\widehat a(t)\longrightarrow\widehat H_p(u_0)z,
 \qquad
 t^{-1}\widehat b(t)\longrightarrow\widehat H_q(u_0)z
 \quad(t\downarrow0).
 \tag{16}
\]
Since a scalar quadratic form depends only on the symmetric part of its matrix, (7) and (16) give
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}t^{-2}
 \langle\widehat b(t),\widehat a(t)\rangle
 =z^{\mathsf T}\widehat H_q(u_0)\widehat H_p(u_0)z
 =z^{\mathsf T}\widehat A(u_0)z.
 \tag{17}
\]
By (14), (17), and density of \(\mathbb Q_{>0}\), there is a rational \(t_{\mathrm{ch}}>0\) for which both chord endpoints are interior and
\[
 \langle\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}}),
          \widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rangle
 +(2M+r)r t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2<0.
 \tag{18}
\]

Apply the validated range oracle from \(\mathrm{def:validated\text{-}range\text{-}oracle}\) to rational boxes covering successive rational subdivisions of this chord.  Multiplication of each interval matrix by \(z\), followed by multiplication by the rational segment length and interval addition, encloses each vector integral in (15).  The excess interval diameter tends to zero by that definition, and the true range diameter on each segment tends to zero by uniform continuity of the clipped fields on the compact chord.  Consequently the two vector enclosures converge to the integrals in (15).  Continuity of the finite-dimensional scalar product shows that their outward-rounded upper endpoints \(U_J\) obey
\[
 U_J\longrightarrow
 \langle\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}}),
          \widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rangle
 \quad(J\to\infty).
 \tag{19}
\]
The strict inequality (18) therefore gives a finite refinement level \(J\) such that
\[
 U_J+(2M+r)r t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2<0.
 \tag{20}
\]
Outward rational rounding returns a negative rational upper endpoint once (20) holds.  The enumeration in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) is exhaustive, so it eventually examines the finite-height triple (13), (18) and refines it through level \(J\).  Thus the procedure terminates and returns \(\mathcal C_{pq,n}\ne\bot\).

It remains to verify population validity.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:potential\text{-}holder}\) makes \(F_a=\nabla\varphi_a\) continuously differentiable on the chord, with derivative \(H_a\).  The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a_0&:=F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)
 =\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau,\\
 b_0&:=F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0)
 =\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}H_q(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
The error bound in (6), integrated along the chord, yields
\[
 \lVert a_0-\widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rVert_2
 \le rt_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2,
 \qquad
 \lVert b_0-\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rVert_2
 \le rt_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2.
 \tag{22}
\]
Every coordinate error is bounded by its Euclidean norm, so the coordinatewise enlargements prescribed in \(\mathrm{def:finite\text{-}negative\text{-}certificate}\) contain the two population increments in (21).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:hessian\text{-}ellipticity}\) and (6) also give
\[
 \lVert a_0\rVert_2\le Mt_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2,
 \qquad
 \lVert\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rVert_2
 \le(M+r)t_{\mathrm{ch}}\lVert z\rVert_2.
 \tag{23}
\]
Using the exact identity
\[
 \langle b_0,a_0\rangle-
 \langle\widehat b,\widehat a\rangle
 =\langle b_0-\widehat b,a_0\rangle
  +\langle\widehat b,a_0-\widehat a\rangle
\]
at \(t=t_{\mathrm{ch}}\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and (22)--(23), gives
\[
 \left|\langle b_0,a_0\rangle-
 \langle\widehat b(t_{\mathrm{ch}}),
          \widehat a(t_{\mathrm{ch}})\rangle\right|
 \le(2M+r)r t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus the scalar allowance in the certificate is valid, and (20) supplies a strict negative rational upper bound for \(\langle b_0,a_0\rangle\).  In particular,
\[
 \left\langle F_q(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_q(u_0),
 F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)-F_p(u_0)\right\rangle<0.
 \tag{25}
\]

Finally, \(t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\ne0\), and the lower ellipticity bound gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle a_0,t_{\mathrm{ch}}z\rangle
 &=\int_0^{t_{\mathrm{ch}}}
 t_{\mathrm{ch}}z^{\mathsf T}H_p(u_0+\tau z)z\,d\tau\\
 &\ge m t_{\mathrm{ch}}^2\lVert z\rVert_2^2>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]
Therefore \(x_0:=F_p(u_0)\) and \(x_1:=F_p(u_0+t_{\mathrm{ch}}z)\) are distinct points of \(D_p\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def:counterfactual\text{-}map}\), with its causal interpretation supplied by \(\mathrm{ass:shared\text{-}rank\text{-}scm}\), gives
\[
 C_{q\leftarrow p}(F_p(u))=F_q(u).
 \tag{27}
\]
A map \(T\) is Euclidean monotone when \(\langle T(x_1)-T(x_0),x_1-x_0\rangle\ge0\) for every pair in its domain.  Applying (27) at the two chord endpoints makes this inner product equal to the strictly negative quantity in (25).  Hence \(C_{q\leftarrow p}\) is not Euclidean monotone on \(D_p\).
