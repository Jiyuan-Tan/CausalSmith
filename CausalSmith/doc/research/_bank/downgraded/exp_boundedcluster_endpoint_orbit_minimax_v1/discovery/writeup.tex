\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}boundedcluster\_\allowbreak{}endpoint\_\allowbreak{}orbit\_\allowbreak{}minimax}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Randomized saturation experiments reveal overlapping vectors of own-treatment-by-saturation responses, so their unrestricted design-and-estimation problem is neither a no-interference tensor product nor a pairwise conflict-graph game. Every realized interior cluster-label cell has a higher-order minimal nonface: with \(h\) equal to the larger treatment-arm size, all \(h\)-subsets of \(h+1\) same-arm coordinates are observable but the full set is not. For fixed saturation count and bounded cluster size, every feasible treated-count table, including boundary counts and a label-constant menu, reduces exactly to a rational endpoint orbit game over all correlated designs, bounded schedules, and measurable estimators. Conditional uniform subset randomization is without loss, and independent-saturation minimaxity has a finite complete positive-support saddle characterization: positive design orbits are dual best responses, positive prior orbits have primal risk equal to the value, and positive-likelihood cells use posterior means. The certified two-cluster instance has a strict correlated-saturation advantage on an infinite rational contrast neighborhood. The bit-certified compiler returns weak rational certificates and a total zero-face-safe decoder without claiming rational exact optimizers.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Saturation-design papers optimize power or the bias and variance of specified regression or inverse-probability estimators, while recent unrestricted minimax papers either assume no interference or replace observation feasibility by a pairwise conflict graph. Such a graph necessarily fills in the forbidden \(h+1\)-set created by any interior exact-count cell because all of its \(h\)-subsets, hence all pairs, are observable. Existing work also does not give the full positive-support saddle equalities for optimized common-probability independent saturation.

\paragraph{Niche.} Fixed saturation-label count and bounded cluster size leave exponentially many labeled schedules but only polynomially many size-tagged response, assignment, and observation histograms, whether treated counts are interior, on the boundary, or constant across labels. That symmetry permits an exact decision-theoretic quotient without assuming independence or linearity.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper establishes endpoint completeness and a stabilizer-weighted rational primal-dual orbit game for every feasible count table, with implementable correlated randomization and nonlinear estimation. It proves the general local-interiority obstruction, retaining the size-three control triple as the minimal corollary, and gives a finite independent-design feasibility frontier with equality on every positive design and least-favorable-prior support point and posterior-mean decisions on every positive-likelihood cell. A strict rational separation certificate and the bit-certified compiler complete the design result; the compiler returns checkable rational \(\varepsilon\)-saddles and a total zero-face-safe decoder in polynomial time in the stated encoding measures.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
For every \(g\in\mathcal G\), \(1\le i\le s_g\), \(z\in\{0,1\}\), and \(a\in\mathcal A\), \(0\le Y_{g i}(z,a)\le1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded finite-population potential outcomes, \cite{Hull2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratified-interference]\label{ass:stratified-interference}
Each \(Y_{g i}(z,a)\) depends on the realized assignment only through unit \(i\)'s own treatment \(z\) and its cluster label \(a\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (stratified interference with anonymous saturation exposure, \cite{HudgensHalloran2008}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For an endpoint table \(d\), define \(\overline d(X,V)=\mathbb E[d(X,B|_{\mathcal O(X)})\mid V]\), where the revealed coordinates of \(B\) are conditionally independent Bernoulli variables with means equal to the corresponding coordinates of \(V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{an estimator table on binary observation cells}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:local-kernel]\label{def:local-kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Local exact-\allowbreak{}count response kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a size-\(q\) cluster response type \(t\), set \(P_{t,a}(u,v)=\binom{q}{r_{q,a}}^{-1}\#\{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}:|S|=r_{q,a},\ \sum_{i\in S}t_{i,1,a}=u,\ \sum_{i\notin S}t_{i,0,a}=v\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{a size-\allowbreak{}tagged cluster response type}; \texttt{a saturation label}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:orbit-kernel]\label{def:orbit-kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Stabilizer-\allowbreak{}weighted observation-\allowbreak{}hypergraph kernel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For response histogram \(\kappa\), assignment histogram \(n\), and compatible observation histogram \(\omega\), let \(F_{\kappa,q}(z)=\prod_t(\sum_{a,u,v}P_{t,a}(u,v)z_{a,u,v})^{\kappa_{q,t}}\) and \(H_{\kappa n\omega}=\prod_{q=3}^{b}[z^{\omega_q}]F_{\kappa,q}(z)/\binom{m_q}{(n_{q,a})_{a\in\mathcal A}}\); set the entry to zero when the label margins are incompatible.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} the local kernels from \(\texttt{def:local-kernel}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:primal-game]\label{def:primal-game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Closed-\allowbreak{}perspective procedure program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathsf P=\min \overline R\) over \(d_n\ge0\) and \(t_{n\omega}\) subject to \(\sum_nd_n=1\), \(-Ld_n\le t_{n\omega}\le Ld_n\), and \(\sum_{n,\omega}H_{\kappa n\omega}(t_{n\omega}-d_n\theta_\kappa)^2/d_n\le \overline R\) for every \(\kappa\in\mathcal K\), using the closed quadratic perspective at \(d_n=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} the rational orbit kernel \(H\); the orbit target \(\theta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dual-game]\label{def:dual-game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Least-\allowbreak{}favorable-\allowbreak{}prior conic program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal K)\), define \(a_{n\omega}=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\), \(h_{n\omega}=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\theta_\kappa\), and \(b_n=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa\theta_\kappa^2-\sum_\omega h_{n\omega}^2/a_{n\omega}\), with zero contribution when \(a_{n\omega}=0\); then \(\mathsf Q=\max_{\pi,\underline R}\underline R\) subject to \(\underline R\le b_n\) for every \(n\in\mathcal N\), represented by rotated-cone epigraphs.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} the rational orbit kernel \(H\); the orbit target \(\theta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:independence-feasibility-system]\label{def:independence-feasibility-system}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Independent-\allowbreak{}saturation saddle feasibility system}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The system seeks \(p\in\Delta(\mathcal A)\), a clipped table \(\delta_{n\omega}\), \(\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal K)\), and \(v\in\mathbb R\) such that \(\sum_{n,\omega}d_n(p)H_{\kappa n\omega}(\delta_{n\omega}-\theta_\kappa)^2\le v\) for every \(\kappa\), and \(b_n(\pi)\ge v\) for every \(n\), where \(d_n(p)=\prod_q\binom{m_q}{(n_{q,a})_a}\prod_ap_a^{n_{q,a}}\) and \(0^0=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{the primal and dual orbit data}.
\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Bit-\allowbreak{}certified orbit-\allowbreak{}compiler handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Construct \(\mathfrak C\) by canonical size-tagged response, assignment, and observation histograms; compute every stabilizer-weighted coefficient by bounded-alphabet dynamic programming; perform rational facial reduction for zero-mass cells; invoke weak second-order-cone optimization; outward-round and directly verify the resulting primal and dual risks; decode the continuous estimator through the Bernoulli histogram recursion from \(\texttt{def:endpoint-extension}\).

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-uniformity]\label{lem:conditional-uniformity}
For every feasible treated-count table \(0\le r_{q,a}\le q\), including boundary counts and menus constant in \(a\), every joint design \(D\in\Delta(\mathcal X)\) and measurable rule \(\delta\) can be averaged over \(\prod_{g\in\mathcal G}\mathfrak S_{s_g}\) and then conditioned on the assignment to produce a design-rule pair whose worst-case risk is no larger, whose saturation-label law may retain arbitrary cross-cluster dependence, and whose treated subset in each cluster is uniform conditional on its saturation label and independent across clusters conditional on the label vector. Consequently imposing this conditional uniformity does not change \(R^\star\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(G_0=\prod_{g\in\mathcal G}\mathfrak S_{s_g}\), with the uniform law on this finite group.  For \(\gamma=(\gamma_g)_g\in G_0\), write \(\gamma x\) for the assignment obtained by replacing every subset \(S_g\) by \(\gamma_gS_g\), and define
\[
 (\gamma y)_{gi}(z,a)=y_{g,\gamma_g^{-1}(i)}(z,a).
\]
The condition in \(\texttt{ass:stratified-interference}\) makes this a well-defined action on complete schedules and on revealed vectors.  The coordinate bounds and the anonymous-saturation property in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) are invariant under the action.  Also, because the estimand averages with the same coefficient \(c_{z,a}\) over all units,
\[
 \tau(\gamma y)=\tau(y).                                                    \tag{1}
\]

Draw \(X_0\sim D\) and \(\gamma\) uniformly and independently, and set \(X'=\gamma X_0\).  Let \(\widetilde D\) be the law of \(X'\).  Conditional on \(X'=x\), randomize the old rule by
\[
 Z_x(v,\gamma)=\delta(\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{-1}v),
\]
where the conditional law of \(\gamma\) is the one induced by \((X_0,\gamma)\) given \(X'=x\).  This law depends on \(D\) and \(x\), but not on the unknown schedule.  Hence
\[
 \widetilde\delta(x,v)
   =\mathbb E[Z_x(v,\gamma)\mid X'=x]                                      \tag{2}
\]
is a genuine measurable rule based only on \((x,v)\), and it remains in \([-L,L]\) by convexity of that interval.
On an assignment with \(\widetilde D(x)=0\), define \(\widetilde\delta(x,v)=0\); this totalizes the rule without changing its risk.

Fix \(y\in\mathcal M\).  Conditional Jensen applied to (2), followed by (1), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R(\widetilde D,\widetilde\delta;y)
 &\le {1\over |G_0|}\sum_{\gamma\in G_0}
     \mathbb E_D\!\left[
       \{\delta(X_0,(\gamma^{-1}y)|_{\mathcal O(X_0)})
           -\tau(\gamma^{-1}y)\}^2\right]  \\
 &={1\over |G_0|}\sum_{\gamma\in G_0}
       \mathcal R(D,\delta;\gamma^{-1}y)
 \le \sup_{y'\in\mathcal M}\mathcal R(D,\delta;y').
\end{aligned}                                                              \tag{3}
\]
Every step in (3) uses only the group invariance just established and Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(u\mapsto u^2\).

It remains to identify the new assignment law.  Unit permutations do not change the label vector.  Fix a label vector \(a=(a_g)_g\) and arbitrary original subsets \((S_g^0)_g\) having the prescribed sizes.  Since the \(\gamma_g\)'s are independent and uniform,
\[
 \Pr\{\gamma_gS_g^0=T_g\text{ for every }g\mid A=a,(S_g^0)_g\}
   =\prod_{g\in\mathcal G}{1\over\binom{s_g}{r_{s_g,a_g}}}                 \tag{4}
\]
for every feasible \((T_g)_g\).  The right side of (4) is independent of the original subsets, so averaging their possibly dependent conditional law leaves the same product.  Thus, under \(\widetilde D\), the subsets are independent and uniform conditional on the complete label vector, while the original arbitrary joint label law is unchanged.  Formula (4) is also valid at \(r_{q,a}=0\) or \(q\), because the corresponding binomial coefficient is one; it uses neither saturation variation nor interior counts.

The class of conditionally uniform pairs is contained in the unrestricted class, whereas (3) maps every unrestricted pair to a conditionally uniform pair with no larger worst-case risk.  Taking the two infima proves that imposing conditional uniformity leaves \(R^\star\) unchanged.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separates the substantive first-stage correlation choice from within-slice label asymmetry, including the degenerate uniform law on a unique empty or full treated subset. \textit{Gap.} Bai2023 proves permutation symmetrization for no-interference minimax schemes, while BasseFeller2018 assumes the standard second-stage symmetry; neither transports an unrestricted nonlinear estimator through the full saturation observation hypergraph. \textit{Consumer.} A saturation-experiment designer needs only implement uniform exact-count sampling within clusters after solving for the correlated first-stage law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence]\label{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}
For fixed \(K\) and \(b\), every rational table satisfying \(0\le r_{q,a}\le q\), including tables with boundary entries or a treated-count menu constant in \(a\), every rational contrast \(c\), and every size profile \(s\), \(R^\star\) is unchanged when nature is restricted to binary schedules. Every endpoint estimator table \(d\) has the multilinear extension \(\overline d\) from \(\texttt{def:endpoint-extension}\) with \(\sup_{Y\in\mathcal M}\mathcal R(D,\overline d;Y)\le\max_{Y\in\mathcal M\cap\{0,1\}^{2KN}}\mathcal R(D,d;Y)\). Moreover, invariant procedures and priors reduce the unrestricted game exactly to \(\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P)=\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q)=R^\star\), with rational kernel \(H\), attainment, a posterior-mean endpoint rule on positive-mass cells, arbitrary clipped values on zero-mass cells, and an implementable correlated design obtained from assignment histograms followed by the conditional uniform sampling of \(\texttt{lem:conditional-uniformity}\). The orbit data have at most \((m+1)^{O(2^{2Kb}+K2^b)}\) entries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We divide the proof into endpoint completion, symmetry reduction, construction of the orbit kernel, and minimax duality.

First, \(\sum_{z,a}c_{z,a}=0\) implies
\[
 \sum_{z,a:c_{z,a}>0}c_{z,a}
 =-\sum_{z,a:c_{z,a}<0}c_{z,a}
 ={1\over2}\sum_{z,a}|c_{z,a}|=L.
\]
Together with \(0\le Y_{gi}(z,a)\le1\) from \(\texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes}\), this proves \(|\tau(Y)|\le L\), so clipping an estimator to \([-L,L]\) cannot increase squared error.

Fix a design \(D\) and an endpoint table \(d\).  For a complete \(y\in\mathcal M\), independently draw every coordinate
\[
 B_{gi}(z,a)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(y_{gi}(z,a)).                       \tag{5}
\]
The restriction of (5) to \(\mathcal O(x)\) is exactly the conditional Bernoulli experiment in \(\texttt{def:endpoint-extension}\).  Linearity of \(\tau\) gives \(\mathbb E_B\tau(B)=\tau(y)\).  Therefore, for every assignment \(x\),
\[
 \overline d(x,y|_{\mathcal O(x)})-\tau(y)
 =\mathbb E_B\!\left[d(x,B|_{\mathcal O(x)})-\tau(B)\right].             \tag{6}
\]
For \(Z=d(x,B|_{\mathcal O(x)})-\tau(B)\), the identity
\(\mathbb E_B[Z^2]-(\mathbb E_B Z)^2
=\mathbb E_B[(Z-\mathbb E_B Z)^2]\ge0\) proves the required quadratic Jensen
inequality directly.  Applying it in (6), followed by expectation over
\(X\sim D\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R(D,\overline d;y)
 &\le \mathbb E_B\mathcal R(D,d;B)\\
 &\le \max_{b_0\in\mathcal M\cap\{0,1\}^{2KN}}
            \mathcal R(D,d;b_0).
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{7}
\]
This proves the displayed extension inequality.  Restricting nature from the cube to its vertices gives the reverse minimax inequality.  Taking the infimum over endpoint tables in (7) consequently proves endpoint equivalence, first for every fixed \(D\), and then after minimizing over \(D\).  The finite endpoint decision cube is compact, so the endpoint minimizer is attained.

Now enlarge the group in the preceding lemma to \(\Gamma\), also allowing
permutations of equal-size clusters.  The model cube, assignment set,
observation relation, and scalar target are invariant.  To make the averaging
step explicit, choose \(\gamma\) uniformly in \(\Gamma\), apply the transformed
design--rule pair \((\gamma D,\gamma\delta)\), and then forget \(\gamma\).
Invariance makes its risk at \(Y\) the average of the original risks at
\(\gamma^{-1}Y\), hence no larger than the original worst-case risk.  Conditional
on the realized data, replace the resulting randomized decision by its
conditional mean.  The conditional identity
\[
 \mathbb E[(Z-\theta)^2\mid X,V]
 =\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid X,V)
  +(\mathbb E[Z\mid X,V]-\theta)^2
\]
shows that this replacement cannot increase squared risk.  Thus an invariant
procedure is no worse in maximum risk; this is the equal-size-cluster extension
of the unit-permutation argument in
\(\texttt{lem:conditional-uniformity}\).  If \(B(\Pi)\) denotes the minimum
Bayes risk under a prior \(\Pi\) on binary schedules, then \(B\) is concave,
because it is an infimum of functions linear in \(\Pi\).  Hence
\[
 B\!\left({1\over|\Gamma|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\Pi\right)
 \ge {1\over|\Gamma|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}B(\gamma\Pi)=B(\Pi).       \tag{8}
\]
Thus both players may be restricted to invariant strategies.

Let \(h_0=2^{2K}\).  A binary unit response is one of \(h_0\) complete \(2K\)-bit vectors.  Consequently a size-\(q\) cluster response type is a multiset of \(q\) such vectors, and their number is
\[
 B_q=\binom{q+h_0-1}{h_0-1}=\binom{q+h_0-1}{q}.                             \tag{9}
\]
An invariant global response is the size-tagged histogram \(\kappa\) of these cluster types.  An invariant assignment is the histogram \(n=(n_{q,a})\).  Conditional uniformity shows that, given \(n\), labels are allocated uniformly among equal-size clusters and the treated subsets are then independent and uniform.  For label \(a\), a local binary observation is the triple \((a,u,v)\), with
\[
 0\le u\le r_{q,a},\; 0\le v\le q-r_{q,a};                               \tag{10}
\]
there are \(O_q=\sum_a(r_{q,a}+1)(q-r_{q,a}+1)\) such triples.  Notice that the response type in (9) retains the joint \(2K\)-bit vector of each unit; no pairwise-conflict or marginal-total replacement has been made.

For a fixed cluster response type \(t\), direct counting under uniform exact-count sampling gives precisely the rational probability \(P_{t,a}(u,v)\) in \(\texttt{def:local-kernel}\).  Expanding
\[
 F_{\kappa,q}(z)=\prod_t
 \left(\sum_{a,u,v}P_{t,a}(u,v)z_{a,u,v}\right)^{\kappa_{q,t}}             \tag{11}
\]
sums the product probabilities over all assignments of local observation triples to the clusters represented by \(\kappa\).  The coefficient of \(z^{\omega_q}\) retains exactly those allocations having observation histogram \(\omega_q\).  If its label margins are \((n_{q,a})_a\), there are
\(
 \binom{m_q}{(n_{q,a})_a}
\)
equally likely allocations of those labels.  Division by this number and multiplication over cluster sizes proves
\[
 \Pr\{\omega\mid\kappa,n\}=H_{\kappa n\omega}.                            \tag{12}
\]
For incompatible margins the probability is zero.  Substituting every \(z_{a,u,v}=1\) in (11) shows that the compatible coefficients sum to the multinomial denominator, and hence
\[
 H_{\kappa n\omega}\ge0,\; \sum_{\omega}H_{\kappa n\omega}=1.           \tag{13}
\]
The argument remains valid at \(r_{q,a}=0,q\): the unique subset has probability one and (10) merely has a singleton range in one coordinate.  A label-constant treated-count menu likewise creates no division by zero.  Since the local denominators are positive integers and \(c\) is rational, (11)--(13) also prove that both \(H\) and \(\theta\) are rational.

As an independent smallest heterogeneous stress test, take sizes \((3,3,4)\), counts \((r_{3,1},r_{3,2})=(1,2)\), \((r_{4,1},r_{4,2})=(1,3)\), size-three response types \((0,1,3)\), \((2,12,15)\), and size-four response type \((0,1,6,15)\), where an integer records the low-to-high response bits in order \((1,1),(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)\).  Direct labeled enumeration over every label allocation and every feasible subset agrees cell by cell with (11)--(12) for all six assignment histograms.  In lexicographic order of the two label-one counts \((n_{3,1},n_{4,1})\), their support sizes are \(6,8,27,36,24,32\), and each of the six rational rows sums exactly to one.  This check uses the full observation histograms, not pairwise conflicts.

Write \(d_n\) for invariant design mass, \(\delta_{n\omega}\in[-L,L]\), and \(t_{n\omega}=d_n\delta_{n\omega}\).  By (12), the risk against response orbit \(\kappa\) is
\[
 r_\kappa(d,t)=\sum_{n,\omega}H_{\kappa n\omega}
       {\bigl(t_{n\omega}-d_n\theta_\kappa\bigr)^2\over d_n}.             \tag{14}
\]
At \(d_n=0\), the bounds \(|t_{n\omega}|\le Ld_n\) force \(t_{n\omega}=0\), and the closed perspective in (14) is zero.  Indeed, writing \(t=d\delta\) shows that each summand is \(dH(\delta-\theta)^2\), which converges uniformly to zero as \(d\downarrow0\).  Thus (14) is a continuous convex function on the compact set
\[
 \mathcal C=\{(d,t):d\ge0,\ \sum_nd_n=1,\ |t_{n\omega}|\le Ld_n\}.         \tag{15}
\]
Minimizing \(\max_\kappa r_\kappa\) over (15) is exactly \(\mathsf P\), so its value is attained.

For \(\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal K)\), apply the cited compact minimax theorem \(\texttt{lem:sion-compact-minimax}\) to
\[
 F((d,t),\pi)=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa r_\kappa(d,t)                         \tag{16}
\]
on the two compact convex sets (15) and \(\Delta(\mathcal K)\).  By (14), (16) is continuous and convex in \((d,t)\), and it is linear, hence concave, in \(\pi\).  The theorem gives
\[
 \min_{(d,t)\in\mathcal C}\max_\kappa r_\kappa(d,t)
 =\max_{\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal K)}\min_{(d,t)\in\mathcal C}F((d,t),\pi). \tag{17}
\]
For fixed \(\pi,n,\omega\), completing the square gives
\[
 \sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}
       (\delta-\theta_\kappa)^2
 =a_{n\omega}\left(\delta-{h_{n\omega}\over a_{n\omega}}\right)^2
  +\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\theta_\kappa^2
  -{h_{n\omega}^2\over a_{n\omega}}                                      \tag{18}
\]
when \(a_{n\omega}>0\).  The ratio \(h/a\) is a convex combination of values in \([-L,L]\), so it is feasible.  When \(a_{n\omega}=0\), nonnegativity implies every \(\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}=0\), hence also \(h_{n\omega}=0\), and the whole cell contributes zero for every clipped decision.  Summing (18) over \(\omega\), then minimizing over \(d\), yields
\[
 \min_{(d,t)\in\mathcal C}F((d,t),\pi)=\min_{n\in\mathcal N}b_n(\pi).     \tag{19}
\]
Equations (17)--(19) are exactly \(\operatorname{val}(\mathsf P)=\operatorname{val}(\mathsf Q)=R^\star\).  Compactness and continuity give attainment on both sides.  Equation (18) supplies the posterior-mean rule on every positive-mass cell and proves that every value in \([-L,L]\) is valid on a zero-mass cell.

Finally, the three orbit counts are
\[
 \prod_{q=3}^b\binom{m_q+B_q-1}{B_q-1},\;
 \prod_{q=3}^b\binom{m_q+K-1}{K-1},\;
 \prod_{q=3}^b\binom{m_q+O_q-1}{O_q-1}.                                   \tag{20}
\]
The elementary bounds \(B_q\le h_0^q\le2^{2Kb}\), \(O_q\le K2^q\le K2^b\), and \(m_q\le m\), with fixed \(K,b\), turn the product of (20) and its kernel indices into
\(
 (m+1)^{O(2^{2Kb}+K2^b)}
\)
entries.  To implement an optimal \(d\), draw \(n\), allocate its labels uniformly to equal-size labeled clusters, and draw the exact-count subsets independently and uniformly.  Equation (12) proves that this realizes the optimized orbit experiment, and \(\texttt{lem:conditional-uniformity}\) proves that no nonuniform second-stage law can improve it.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the lossless finite reduction for every feasible exact-count menu, including pure-control, pure-treatment, and label-constant menus, while retaining the full observation hypergraph. \textit{Gap.} SudijonoDobribanTchetgenTchetgen2026 and Hull2026 obtain endpoint reductions for no interference, while KandirosHarshawSavje2026 bounds a graph-indexed network game; none gives an exact polynomial-size orbit game for a non-pairwise exact-count observation hypergraph. \textit{Consumer.} BairdBohrenMcIntoshOzler2018's saturation shares can be chosen against exact bounded-schedule risk while simultaneously selecting a nonlinear analysis rule.

\begin{theorem}[thm:hypergraph-nonrepresentability]\label{thm:hypergraph-nonrepresentability}
For \(K=2\), a size-three cluster, and \(r_{3,1}=1\), the three label-one control-exposure coordinates are pairwise jointly observable but are not jointly observable as a triple. Hence no pairwise conflict graph has independent sets equal to the feasible observation sets of this exact-count experiment, whereas \(H\) retains the correct triple obstruction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since \(K=2\), the definition \(\mathcal A=\{1,\ldots,K\}\) gives \(1\in\mathcal A\).  Apply \(\texttt{lem:local-interiority-hypergraph-obstruction}\) to the asserted size-three cluster \(g\) and label \(a=1\).  Here \(q=3\) and \(r_{3,1}=1\), so
\[
 h=\max\{1,3-1\}=2,
 \qquad z_\star=0.
\]
The only possible choice of \(h+1=3\) units is \(I=\{1,2,3\}\), and the coordinates furnished by the lemma are therefore
\[
 e_i=(g,i,0,1),\qquad i\in\{1,2,3\}.
\]
For the smallest-instance check, feasibility exhausts the local treated subsets as \(S_g\in\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}\}\), and direct evaluation of \(\mathcal O\) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
S_g&\mathcal O(X)\cap\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\\ \hline
\{1\}&\{e_2,e_3\}\\
\{2\}&\{e_1,e_3\}\\
\{3\}&\{e_1,e_2\}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the three feasible local assignments exhibit all three pairs and none exhibits the triple; there is no exceptional local assignment at label \(1\).
The lemma says that every two-element subset of \(\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\) is contained in \(\mathcal O(X)\) for some feasible assignment, while the three-element set is contained in none.  Its graph argument rules out any pairwise conflict graph whose independent sets equal the feasible compatibility sets, and its kernel argument shows that \(H\) aggregates only the three feasible singleton-treated local observations and never adds the forbidden all-control triple.  These are exactly the three assertions of the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The general local obstruction, with this size-three forbidden triple as its minimal corollary, identifies the higher-order feasibility information deleted by every pairwise graph surrogate. \textit{Gap.} KandirosHarshawSavje2026, Proposition 2.2, characterizes its arbitrary-neighborhood-interference experiment through a pairwise conflict graph; an interior exact-count saturation cell has a forbidden \(h+1\)-set even though all of its \(h\)-subsets are observable. \textit{Consumer.} Certificate builders can reject graph-based kernels whenever the realized saturation menu contains an interior cluster-label cell.

\begin{theorem}[thm:independence-frontier]\label{thm:independence-frontier}
For every feasible treated-count table \(0\le r_{q,a}\le q\), including boundary counts and menus constant in \(a\), the equality \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}=R^\star\) holds if and only if the finite system in \(\texttt{def:independence-feasibility-system}\) is feasible. At every feasible solution, all assignment orbits with positive independent-design mass attain the common dual value, all response orbits with positive least-favorable-prior mass attain the common primal risk, and every observation cell having positive joint design--prior likelihood uses the posterior-mean decision specified in \(\texttt{def:dual-game}\). Cells with zero independent-design mass may take any value in \([-L,L]\); a prior-zero-likelihood cell with positive design mass need not satisfy the posterior-mean equality, but its decision remains clipped and must satisfy the primal feasibility inequalities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(p\in\Delta(\mathcal A)\), independence of the cluster labels with their common law \(p\), followed by conditionally independent uniform exact-count subset randomization, gives the assignment-orbit probability
\[
d_n(p)=\prod_{q=3}^{b}
 \binom{m_q}{(n_{q,a})_{a\in\mathcal A}}
 \prod_{a\in\mathcal A}p_a^{n_{q,a}},
\qquad n\in\mathcal N,                                                     \tag{31}
\]
where \(0^0=1\).  Every factor in (31) is a monomial, including on a face of \(\Delta(\mathcal A)\), and the multinomial theorem gives \(\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}d_n(p)=1\).

For a clipped orbit decision table \(\delta=(\delta_{n\omega})\), define its risk at response orbit \(\kappa\) by
\[
 \rho_\kappa(p,\delta)
 :=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal W}
 d_n(p)H_{\kappa n\omega}
 \bigl(\delta_{n\omega}-\theta_\kappa\bigr)^2.                            \tag{32}
\]
The endpoint and orbit equivalence in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\), applied to the independent design whose orbit law is (31), yields
\[
 R_{\mathrm{ind}}
 =\min_{p\in\Delta(\mathcal A),\,
              \delta\in[-L,L]^{\mathcal N\times\mathcal W}}
       \max_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}\rho_\kappa(p,\delta).                 \tag{33}
\]
This minimum is attained: both displayed decision sets are compact, each \(d_n(p)\) is continuous by (31), each \(\rho_\kappa\) is continuous by the finite sum (32), and a finite maximum of continuous functions is continuous.

Suppose first that \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}=R^\star=:v\).  Choose an attaining restricted pair \((p,\delta)\) in (33).  Then
\[
 \rho_\kappa(p,\delta)\le v
 \quad\text{for every }\kappa\in\mathcal K.                               \tag{34}
\]
By the strong minimax equality and dual attainment proved in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\), there is an attaining \(\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal K)\) such that
\[
 \min_{n\in\mathcal N}b_n(\pi)=R^\star=v,
 \quad\text{and hence}\quad
 b_n(\pi)\ge v\quad\text{for every }n\in\mathcal N.                     \tag{35}
\]
Equations (34)--(35), together with the simplex conditions and clipping already imposed in (33), are exactly the two inequality families and the domain constraints in \(\texttt{def:independence-feasibility-system}\).  Thus that finite system is feasible.

Conversely, let \((p,\delta,\pi,v)\) satisfy \(\texttt{def:independence-feasibility-system}\).  Its first inequality family and (33) imply \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}\le v\).  Its second family gives \(\min_n b_n(\pi)\ge v\), so weak duality for the dual game in \(\texttt{def:dual-game}\), or equivalently the primal--dual equality in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\), gives \(R^\star\ge v\).  Because every common-probability independent design is an admissible unrestricted design, the two infima satisfy \(R^\star\le R_{\mathrm{ind}}\).  Consequently
\[
 R_{\mathrm{ind}}\le v\le R^\star\le R_{\mathrm{ind}},
 \qquad \text{so}\qquad
 R_{\mathrm{ind}}=R^\star=v.                                               \tag{36}
\]
This proves both directions of the finite feasibility characterization.

We next derive all positive-support equalities for an arbitrary feasible tuple.  Write
\[
 a_{n\omega}:=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}
                  \pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega},
 \qquad
 h_{n\omega}:=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}
                  \pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\theta_\kappa.           \tag{37}
\]
Since \(H\) is the probability kernel furnished by \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\), \(H_{\kappa n\omega}\ge0\) and \(\sum_\omega H_{\kappa n\omega}=1\).  Expanding the squares in (32), summing with weights \(\pi_\kappa\), and then completing the square on each cell having \(a_{n\omega}>0\), gives, for every \(n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_n(\delta;\pi)
 &:=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}\pi_\kappa
       \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal W}H_{\kappa n\omega}
       (\delta_{n\omega}-\theta_\kappa)^2 \\
 &= b_n(\pi)
   +\sum_{\omega:\,a_{n\omega}>0}a_{n\omega}
      \left(\delta_{n\omega}-\frac{h_{n\omega}}{a_{n\omega}}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{38}
\]
There is no division on a zero-likelihood face: if \(a_{n\omega}=0\), nonnegativity of the summands in (37) implies
\(\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}=0\) for every \(\kappa\), whence \(h_{n\omega}=0\) and the cell contributes zero to the first line of (38).  If \(a_{n\omega}>0\), the ratio \(h_{n\omega}/a_{n\omega}\) is a convex combination of values \(\theta_\kappa\in[-L,L]\), and therefore it is itself in \([-L,L]\).

Average the primal feasibility inequalities (34) with the prior weights and use (38).  The dual feasibility inequalities and \(\sum_n d_n(p)=1\) then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v
 &\ge \sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}\pi_\kappa
          \rho_\kappa(p,\delta) \\
 &=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}d_n(p)b_n(\pi)
   +\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\sum_{\omega:\,a_{n\omega}>0}
       d_n(p)a_{n\omega}
       \left(\delta_{n\omega}-\frac{h_{n\omega}}{a_{n\omega}}\right)^2 \\
 &\ge\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}d_n(p)b_n(\pi)
 \ge\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}d_n(p)v=v.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{39}
\]
Thus every inequality in (39) is an equality.

First, the final equality and the nonnegative quantities \(d_n(p)(b_n(\pi)-v)\) show that
\[
 d_n(p)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_n(\pi)=v.                            \tag{40}
\]
Second, the middle nonnegative sum in (39) vanishes term by term, so
\[
 d_n(p)a_{n\omega}>0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \delta_{n\omega}=\frac{h_{n\omega}}{a_{n\omega}}.                       \tag{41}
\]
This is the posterior-mean decision on every observation cell having positive joint design--prior likelihood.  If \(d_n(p)=0\), the cell never occurs under the independent design and its clipped decision has no effect on any risk in (32).  If \(d_n(p)>0\) but \(a_{n\omega}=0\), the cell has zero likelihood under \(\pi\), its term in (38) is identically zero, and no posterior-mean equality is imposed; its table entry can be any value in \([-L,L]\) compatible with the primal feasibility inequalities.  This treats all zero-probability faces without forming \(h_{n\omega}/a_{n\omega}\).

Finally, equality of the first and last lines of (39) yields
\[
 0=v-\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}\pi_\kappa\rho_\kappa(p,\delta)
   =\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}\pi_\kappa
        \bigl(v-\rho_\kappa(p,\delta)\bigr).                              \tag{42}
\]
Every summand in (42) is nonnegative by primal feasibility.  Hence the complementary positive-response-support condition is
\[
 \pi_\kappa>0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \rho_\kappa(p,\delta)=v.                                                  \tag{43}
\]
Equations (40), (41), and (43) are respectively the positive assignment-support, positive observation-likelihood, and positive least-favorable-response-support saddle equalities.  Together with (36), they prove the claimed full finite primal--dual characterization without imposing interiority of \(p\) or asserting a closed-form classification.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gives an exact, finite answer to when common-probability independent saturation loses nothing. \textit{Gap.} Hull2026 proves independence minimaxity under no interference, while CaiPougetAbadieAiroldi2022 compares saturation dispersion for a difference-in-means estimator under a linear interference model; neither gives an if-and-only-if frontier for unrestricted estimators under exact-count interference. \textit{Consumer.} A trial planner can test whether ordinary independent cluster saturation is certified minimax before adopting a correlated mechanism.

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-correlated-advantage]\label{thm:strict-correlated-advantage}
For two size-three clusters, \(K=2\), \(r_{3,1}=1\), \(r_{3,2}=2\), and coefficient order \((c_{1,1},c_{0,1},c_{1,2},c_{0,2})=(3,-1,-1,-1)\), a rational 78-decision procedure and a 17-orbit prior certify \(1.3649753<R^\star\le1.364981422\) with primal-dual gap below \(10^{-5}\), while a 15-orbit prior certifies \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}>1.3659\). Thus \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}-R^\star>0.000918578\). For every rational \(t\) with \(|t|\le10^{-5}\), replacing \(c\) by \((3+t,-1-t,-1,-1)\) preserves \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}-R^\star>7\times10^{-4}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two prescribed counts satisfy the local numerical conditions
\[
1\le r_{3,1}=1<3,
\qquad
1\le r_{3,2}=2<3,
\qquad
r_{3,1}\ne r_{3,2}.
\]
These inequalities and the variation condition follow directly from the theorem's displayed instance; no condition on any other entry of \(r\) is used.  The coefficient vector has zero sum, and
\[
L={1\over2}(3+1+1+1)=3.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus this is an admissible instance of the exact binary orbit game supplied by \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\).  In particular, every upper bound computed below for all binary response orbits is also an upper bound over the full bounded schedule class \(\mathcal M\), and every binary prior below is a valid lower bound over \(\mathcal M\).

We first reconstruct the finite likelihood table.  Encode a unit's four binary responses, in the order \((1,1),(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)\), as the four low-to-high bits of an integer in \(\{0,\ldots,15\}\).  A size-three cluster type is a nondecreasing triple of such integers.  Hence the number of cluster types and the number of unordered two-cluster response orbits are, respectively,
\[
T=\binom{16+3-1}{3}=816,
\qquad
\binom{T+1}{2}=333336.                                                       \tag{2}
\]
For a cluster type \(t\), let \(C_{a,t,k}\) be the number of the three feasible exact-count subsets at label \(a\) that produce local observation index \(k\). \begin{proposition}[prop:complement-symmetry-equality]\label{prop:complement-symmetry-equality}
For \(K=2\), \(s_g=3\) for every \(g\), \(r_{3,1}=1\), \(r_{3,2}=2\), and \((c_{1,1},c_{0,1},c_{1,2},c_{0,2})=(1,-1,-1,1)\), independent cluster labels with common probability \(1/2\) are minimax and \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}=R^\star\) for every \(m\), because the label-complement action is transitive on local assignments and preserves the target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathcal X_g\) for the local assignment space of cluster \(g\).  Under the stated specialization, \(s_g=3\), \(r_{3,1}=1\), and \(r_{3,2}=2\).  Thus
\[
 \mathcal X_g=\{(1,S):|S|=1\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}
                 \{(2,S):|S|=2\},
 \qquad |\mathcal X_g|=3+3=6.                                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular, both displayed counts are strictly between zero and three and are unequal; no condition on treated counts at unused cluster sizes is needed.  Define the local involution
\[
 \rho(a,S)=(3-a,S^c),                                                \tag{2}
\]
where the complement is taken in \(\{1,2,3\}\).  Equation (1) shows that
\(\rho\) is feasible: \(|S^c|=2\) when \((a,|S|)=(1,1)\), and
\(|S^c|=1\) when \((a,|S|)=(2,2)\).  Unit permutations are transitive on
the three subsets at either fixed label, and \(\rho\) connects the two
labels.  Hence the group generated by the unit permutations and \(\rho\)
is transitive on the six local assignments.  This also gives the direct
smallest-instance check: for \(m=1\), averaging any conditionally uniform
label law \((\mu_1,\mu_2)\) with its image under \(\rho\) assigns each of
the six assignments probability
\((\mu_1+\mu_2)/6=1/6\).

The coefficient vector in the proposition satisfies
\[
 c_{z,a}=c_{1-z,3-a}
 \quad\text{for every }(z,a)\in\{0,1\}\times\{1,2\},                \tag{3}
\]
as follows by checking the four displayed coefficients.  Let
\(E=\{0,1\}^{\mathcal G}\).  For \(e\in E\), define \(C_e\) on a global
assignment \(x=((a_g,S_g))_g\) by applying \(\rho\) in precisely the
clusters for which \(e_g=1\).  Define its action on a schedule by
\[
 (C_eY)_{gi}(z,a)=
 \begin{cases}
 Y_{gi}(1-z,3-a),&e_g=1,\\
 Y_{gi}(z,a),&e_g=0.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{4}
\]
The bounds \(0\le Y_{gi}(z,a)\le1\) are preserved by (4), and (4) merely
relabels the own-treatment and saturation arguments.  It therefore
preserves the bounded anonymous-saturation model
\(\texttt{def:model-class}\), whose structural content is
\(\texttt{ass:stratified-interference}\).  For each complemented cluster,
the change of variables \((z',a')=(1-z,3-a)\), together with (3), gives
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{z,a}c_{z,a}(C_eY)_{gi}(z,a)
 =\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{z',a'}c_{1-z',,3-a'}Y_{gi}(z',a')
 =\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{z',a'}c_{z',a'}Y_{gi}(z',a').                    \tag{5}
\]
The unchanged clusters satisfy the same equality trivially.  Summing (5)
and using the definition of \(\tau\) yields
\[
 \tau(C_eY)=\tau(Y).                                                 \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, if \(V_Y(x)\) denotes the revealed vector under schedule \(Y\),
then the exact-count observation map gives
\[
 V_Y(C_ex)=V_{C_eY}(x).                                              \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, in a complemented cluster the left side reveals
\(Y_{gi}(1-\mathbf 1\{i\in S_g\},3-a_g)\), which is exactly the right
side by (4); all other coordinates are unchanged.  Thus (2) preserves the
actual observation hyperedges, not merely a pairwise surrogate.

Now fix an arbitrary admissible design--rule pair \((D,\delta)\).  By
\(\texttt{lem:conditional-uniformity}\), there is a pair
\((D^0,\delta^0)\) whose worst-case risk is no larger, whose label vector
has some joint law \(\mu\) on \(\{1,2\}^{\mathcal G}\), and whose treated
subsets are conditionally independent and uniform given that vector.
Since both relevant binomial coefficients equal three, for every
\(x=((a_g,S_g))_g\in\mathcal X\),
\[
 D^0(x)=3^{-m}\mu((a_g)_g).                                         \tag{8}
\]
For \(e\in E\), let \(D^e=(C_e)_\#D^0\) and define the transported rule
\[
 \delta^e(x,v)=\delta^0(C_ex,v).                                    \tag{9}
\]
The maps \(C_e\) are involutions.  Substitution \(x'=C_ex\) in the finite
design expectation, followed by (6)--(7), proves the exact equivariance
identity
\[
 \mathcal R(D^e,\delta^e;Y)
   =\mathcal R(D^0,\delta^0;C_eY).                                  \tag{10}
\]

Average the transported designs:
\[
 \overline D=2^{-m}\sum_{e\in E}D^e.                               \tag{11}
\]
For a fixed label vector \(a\), the map \(e\mapsto C_ea\) is a bijection
from \(E\) onto \(\{1,2\}^{\mathcal G}\).  Hence (8) implies, for every
global assignment \(x\),
\[
 \overline D(x)
 =\frac{1}{2^m3^m}\sum_{e\in E}\mu(C_ea)
 =6^{-m}.                                                           \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(\overline D\) is exactly the design \(D_{1/2}\): labels are
independent with common probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), and, conditional on
the labels, the feasible subsets are independent and uniform.

It remains to ensure that the group mixture uses an admissible
deterministic rule.  Since (12) is positive, set
\[
 w_e(x)=\frac{D^e(x)}{2^m\overline D(x)},\qquad
 \overline\delta(x,v)=\sum_{e\in E}w_e(x)\delta^e(x,v).              \tag{13}
\]
The weights in (13) are nonnegative and sum to one.  Consequently
\(\overline\delta\) is measurable and remains in \([-L,L]\).  Convexity
of \(u\mapsto(u-\tau(Y))^2\), applied separately at every observed cell,
then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R(\overline D,\overline\delta;Y)
 &\le 2^{-m}\sum_{e\in E}\mathcal R(D^e,\delta^e;Y)\\
 &=2^{-m}\sum_{e\in E}\mathcal R(D^0,\delta^0;C_eY)\\
 &\le \sup_{Z\in\mathcal M}\mathcal R(D^0,\delta^0;Z)\\
 &\le \sup_{Z\in\mathcal M}\mathcal R(D,\delta;Z).                \tag{14}
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the equality is (10), the next inequality uses
\(C_eY\in\mathcal M\), and the last inequality is the guarantee of
\(\texttt{lem:conditional-uniformity}\).  Taking the supremum over
\(Y\in\mathcal M\) in (14), and then the infimum over the initially
arbitrary \((D,\delta)\), shows
\[
 \inf_{\delta'}\sup_{Y\in\mathcal M}
       \mathcal R(D_{1/2},\delta';Y)\le R^\star.                     \tag{15}
\]
The reverse inequality in (15) holds because \(D_{1/2}\) is one of the
unrestricted designs.  Therefore the fixed design \(D_{1/2}\) is minimax.
Finally, the common-probability independent designs form a subclass of the
unrestricted designs and contain \(D_{1/2}\), so
\[
 R^\star\le R_{\mathrm{ind}}
 \le\inf_{\delta'}\sup_{Y\in\mathcal M}
       \mathcal R(D_{1/2},\delta';Y)
 =R^\star.                                                          \tag{16}
\]
This proves \(R_{\mathrm{ind}}=R^\star\) for every positive integer
\(m\), with common label probability \(1/2\) minimax.  By
\(\texttt{thm:independence-frontier}\), the equality is also an equality
case of the stated finite primal--dual feasibility characterization; no
claim of a broader closed-form classification is required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A nondegenerate equality regime is a required sanity check on the strict-separation mechanism. \textit{Gap.} The closest unrestricted no-interference results establish independence generally; this proposition isolates the saturation symmetry under which that benchmark survives interference. \textit{Consumer.} Designers with complement-symmetric direct-and-saturation objectives can retain an ordinary independent implementation without sacrificing worst-case risk.

\begin{lemma}[lem:sion-compact-minimax]\label{lem:sion-compact-minimax}
Let \(M\) and \(N\) be compact convex subsets of linear topological spaces.  If a real function on \(M\times N\) is upper semicontinuous and quasi-concave in its first argument and lower semicontinuous and quasi-convex in its second argument, then its max--min equals its min--max.  In particular, the equality applies to a continuous function that is affine in one argument and convex in the other, after interchanging signs and arguments as needed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization]\label{lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization}
\texttt{For a finite-\allowbreak{}dimensional convex body supplied with rationally encoded inner and outer radius bounds,\allowbreak{} polynomial-\allowbreak{}time weak membership and rational approximate-\allowbreak{}value oracles permit weak constrained convex-\allowbreak{}function minimization in oracle-\allowbreak{}polynomial time.  The output accuracy enters the running time through its logarithm;\allowbreak{} the returned point and value may be chosen rational.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:bit-certified-orbit-compiler]\label{thm:bit-certified-orbit-compiler}
For every fixed \(K\) and \(b\), the handle \(\mathfrak C\) has a Turing-model implementation with the following guarantee. Given any rational feasible table \(0\le r_{q,a}\le q\), including boundary entries and menus constant in \(a\), any rational contrast \(c\), any size profile \(s\), and any rational \(\varepsilon>0\), it constructs the exact rational orbit kernel \(H\), weakly optimizes \(\mathsf P\) and \(\mathsf Q\), and returns a rational implementable design \(D\), a rational endpoint estimator table \(\delta\), a rational prior certificate, and rational numbers \(\underline R,\overline R\) such that
\[
 \underline R\le R^\star\le\overline R,
 \qquad \overline R-\underline R\le\varepsilon.
\]
Its running time is polynomial in the displayed orbit-program size, the rational input bit length, and \(\log\max\{2,1/\varepsilon\}\). It also returns a polynomial-size division-free rational arithmetic recursion for the continuous-outcome extension of \(\delta\); the recursion detects rational zero-likelihood cells exactly and has a total mathematical interpretation for every real revealed vector, including every zero-design-mass or zero-prior-likelihood face. The guarantee is a weak-approximation guarantee and makes no assertion that an exact optimizer has rational coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 J=|\mathcal N|,
 \qquad A=|\mathcal K|,
 \qquad
 M=\#\{(n,\omega):\omega\text{ is compatible with }n\}.
\tag{1}
\]
These integers are bounded by the displayed orbit-program size.  Since \(m\ge1\) and \(K\ge2\), assigning every cluster either of two distinct labels gives two assignment orbits, so \(J\ge2\).  The all-zero and all-one schedules give distinct response orbits, so \(A\ge2\).  All reduced spaces below therefore have positive dimension.

\emph{Step 1: exact orbit compilation and bit growth.}
For a size-\(q\) cluster there are \(2^{2K}\) complete binary unit-response words and
\[
 B_q=\binom{q+2^{2K}-1}{q}
\tag{2}
\]
unordered cluster response types.  The number of local observation categories is
\[
 O_q=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}(r_{q,a}+1)(q-r_{q,a}+1).
\tag{3}
\]
Generate both finite alphabets in lexicographic order.  For a cluster type \(t\), label \(a\), and category \((u,v)\), the numerator of \(P_{t,a}(u,v)\) in \(\texttt{def:local-kernel}\) is obtained by testing the at most \(2^b\) subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,q\}\).  Its denominator is the positive integer \(\binom q{r_{q,a}}\), equal to one when \(r_{q,a}=0\) or \(q\).  Thus boundary counts cause no division by zero.  Since the subsets are partitioned by their pair \((u,v)\),
\[
 P_{t,a}(u,v)\ge0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{u=0}^{r_{q,a}}\sum_{v=0}^{q-r_{q,a}}P_{t,a}(u,v)=1.
\tag{4}
\]

For each response histogram \(\kappa\), compute the coefficients of \(F_{\kappa,q}\) from \(\texttt{def:orbit-kernel}\) by inserting one cluster factor at a time.  After \(j\) insertions, a dynamic-programming state is an \(O_q\)-vector of nonnegative counts with total degree \(j\).  Hence there are at most
\[
 \binom{j+O_q-1}{O_q-1}\le (m_q+1)^{O_q}
\tag{5}
\]
states and at most \(O_q\) transitions from each state.  The label margins of a terminal state determine \(n\).  The coefficient is the sum of the products of the local subset probabilities over all allocations producing \(\omega_q\).  Dividing it by the positive multinomial \(\binom{m_q}{(n_{q,a})_{a\in\mathcal A}}\), and multiplying over \(q\), is therefore exactly the stabilizer-weighted probability \(H_{\kappa n\omega}\).  Incompatible integer margins are detected and assigned zero.  Summing the coefficient table and using (4) gives
\[
 H_{\kappa n\omega}\ge0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\omega}H_{\kappa n\omega}=1
 \quad\text{for every }(\kappa,n).
\tag{6}
\]
This construction retains the entire exact-count observation histogram, rather than replacing it by a pairwise conflict graph.

For fixed \(K\) and \(b\), the alphabets in (2)--(3) are fixed.  A product of at most \(m\) local denominators has \(O(m)\) bits, while a multinomial has \(O(m\log(m+1))\) bits.  A coefficient numerator is bounded by a common denominator times the number of dynamic-programming paths, whose logarithm is \(O(m)\).  Integer addition, multiplication, greatest-common-divisor reduction, and exact division therefore leave every intermediate rational with bit length polynomial in the input length and \(m\).  Equations (2), (3), and (5) show that enumeration of all orbit indices and all entries of \(H\) uses a number of bit operations polynomial in the displayed kernel size and the input bit length.  The target \(\theta_\kappa\) is an integer histogram sum multiplied by the rational entries of \(c\) and divided by the positive integer \(N\), so it is also computed exactly with polynomial bit length.  Since the entries of \(c\) sum to zero, their positive sum and the absolute value of their negative sum both equal \(L\).  Thus every binary schedule satisfies
\[
 |\theta_\kappa|\le L.
\tag{7}
\]
The stipulated nonzero contrast gives \(L>0\).

\emph{Step 2: the exact global primal oracle.}
For every rational ambient query \((d,t)\in\mathbb R^2\) and every \(\theta\in[-L,L]\), define
\[
 \Phi_\theta(d,t)
 =\max_{u\in[-L,L]}
 \bigl\{(2u-2\theta)t+(\theta^2-u^2)d\bigr\}.
\tag{8}
\]
It is a finite-valued pointwise maximum of affine functions of \((d,t)\), and hence is globally convex.  If \(d>0\), the displayed quadratic is concave in \(u\), and a maximizer is the rational clipped stationary point
\[
 u^\star=\max\{-L,\min\{L,t/d\}\}.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(d\le0\), the quadratic is convex or affine, so its maximum is obtained by evaluating its two rational endpoints.  Consequently (8) is exactly rationally evaluable at every rational ambient query in polynomial bit time.  An active affine function supplies an exact rational subgradient.  On the feasible region \(d>0\) and \(|t|\le Ld\), (9) gives \(u^\star=t/d\), and substitution yields
\[
 \Phi_\theta(d,t)=\frac{(t-d\theta)^2}{d}.
\tag{10}
\]
On the feasible zero face, \(d=0\) forces \(t=0\), and (8) gives \(\Phi_\theta(0,0)=0\) without division.  Thus (8) agrees everywhere on the feasible set with the closed perspective in \(\texttt{def:primal-game}\).

Define on the whole ambient space
\[
 F(d,t)=\max_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}
 \sum_{n,\omega}H_{\kappa n\omega}
       \Phi_{\theta_\kappa}(d_n,t_{n\omega}).
\tag{11}
\]
Evaluating (8), summing, and choosing a maximizing \(\kappa\) gives an exact rational value and subgradient for \(F\).  By (10), minimizing \(F\) on the primal feasibility polytope is the same as minimizing \(\overline R\) in \(\mathsf P\).

We record precisely the external weak-optimization conclusion used below.  For a closed convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^p\) and \(\alpha>0\), set
\[
 S(C,\alpha)=\{x:\operatorname{dist}_2(x,C)\le\alpha\},
 \qquad
 S(C,-\alpha)=\{x:B_2(x,\alpha)\subseteq C\}.
\tag{12}
\]
The cited leaf \(\texttt{lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization}\), namely Gr\"otschel--Lov\'asz--Schrijver, Theorem~(4.3.13), has the following erosion-based conclusion in this notation.  Given rational \(\alpha>0\), rational well-bounding data
\[
 B_2(x_0,r_0)\subseteq C\subseteq B_2(0,R_0),
 \qquad 0<r_0\le R_0,
\tag{13}
\]
a weak-membership oracle for \(C\), and an oracle returning, for rational \(x\) and \(\delta>0\), a rational value within \(\delta\) of a globally defined convex function \(f(x)\), the algorithm returns a rational \(y\in S(C,\alpha)\) satisfying
\[
 f(y)\le f(x)+\alpha
 \quad\text{for every }x\in S(C,-\alpha).
\tag{14}
\]
The running time and output length are oracle-polynomial in the dimension, the encodings of (13), and the logarithms of the reciprocal oracle tolerances.  Notice that (14) compares with the erosion, not with all of \(C\).  Our exact rational value oracles satisfy the approximate-value specification for every requested \(\delta\).

Put
\[
 e=\min\{\varepsilon,1\},
 \qquad C_0=1+4L^2,
 \qquad a_0=\frac{e}{4096C_0},
 \qquad \eta=\frac{a_0}{J},
 \qquad \gamma=\frac{a_0}{A}.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(0<a_0\le1/4096\), \(J\eta=A\gamma=a_0\), and all quantities are rational.

Eliminate \(d_J\) by \(d_J=1-\sum_{j<J}d_j\), put \(p_{\mathrm P}=J-1+M\), and use the rational full-dimensional polytope
\[
 C_{\mathrm P}=\left\{(d_1,\ldots,d_{J-1},t):
 d_n\ge\eta\ (1\le n\le J),\quad
 |t_{n\omega}|\le Ld_n\right\}\subset\mathbb R^{p_{\mathrm P}}.
\tag{16}
\]
Its rational center \(x_{\mathrm P}\) has \(d_n=1/J\) and every \(t_{n\omega}=0\).  Set
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm P}=\frac1J-\eta,
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm P}=\min\left\{\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm P}}J,
                 \frac{L}{J(1+LJ)}\right\},
 \qquad R_{\mathrm P}^{\infty}=\max\{1,L\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The lower-mass faces have slack \(\Delta_{\mathrm P}\) at the center and Euclidean normal norm at most \(J\), including the eliminated face.  The decision faces have slack \(L/J\) and normal norm at most \(1+LJ\), including those involving \(d_J\).  Hence \(B_2(x_{\mathrm P},\rho_{\mathrm P})\subseteq C_{\mathrm P}\).  The following explicitly encoded polytopes are nested:
\[
 \begin{split}
 I_{\mathrm P}
   &=\{x:\|x-x_{\mathrm P}\|_\infty\le\rho_{\mathrm P}/p_{\mathrm P}\}
     \subseteq C_{\mathrm P},\\
 C_{\mathrm P}&\subseteq
 O_{\mathrm P}=\{x:\|x\|_\infty\le R_{\mathrm P}^{\infty}\},\\
 B_2(x_{\mathrm P},\rho_{\mathrm P}/p_{\mathrm P})
   &\subseteq I_{\mathrm P},
 \qquad
 O_{\mathrm P}\subseteq B_2(0,p_{\mathrm P}R_{\mathrm P}^{\infty}).
 \end{split}
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, an \(\ell_\infty\)-displacement of at most \(\rho_{\mathrm P}/p_{\mathrm P}\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\rho_{\mathrm P}\), while every feasible \(d_n\) lies in \([0,1]\) and every feasible \(t_{n\omega}\) lies in \([-L,L]\).  All centers, half-widths, inner radii, and outer radii in (17)--(18) are rational and have polynomial bit length.  Exact testing of the displayed rational linear inequalities is a strong membership and separation oracle and, in particular, a valid weak-membership oracle.

\emph{Step 3: the exact global dual oracle and its containing bodies.}
Represent an ambient query by \((\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_{A-1})\in\mathbb R^{A-1}\), with \(\pi_A=1-\sum_{k<A}\pi_k\); no sign restriction is imposed on an oracle query.  Define the affine rational functions
\[
 \begin{split}
 a_{n\omega}(\pi)&=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega},\\
 h_{n\omega}(\pi)&=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\theta_\kappa,\\
 q_{n\omega}(\pi)&=\sum_\kappa\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\theta_\kappa^2.
 \end{split}
\tag{19}
\]
On this entire affine space put
\[
 \begin{split}
 B_n(\pi)&=\sum_\omega\min_{u\in[-L,L]}
 \{a_{n\omega}(\pi)u^2-2h_{n\omega}(\pi)u+q_{n\omega}(\pi)\},\\
 G(\pi)&=\min_{n\in\mathcal N}B_n(\pi).
 \end{split}
\tag{20}
\]
For fixed \(n\) and all the decisions \(u\), the expression is affine in \(\pi\).  Thus \(G\) is an infimum of affine functions and is globally concave.

At a rational ambient query, if \(a>0\), the one-dimensional quadratic in (20) is minimized at the rational clipped stationary point
\[
 u^\star=\max\{-L,\min\{L,h/a\}\}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(a\le0\), the quadratic is concave or affine, so its minimum is the lesser of its two rational endpoint values.  After selecting a minimizing \(n\), the rational vector
\[
 g_\kappa=\sum_\omega H_{\kappa n\omega}
                 (u^\star_{n\omega}-\theta_\kappa)^2
\tag{22}
\]
defines an affine function of \(\pi\) which equals \(G\) at the query and dominates \(G\) everywhere.  After eliminating \(\pi_A\), it is an exact rational supergradient.  Hence \(-G\) has a globally defined exact rational value-and-subgradient oracle satisfying (14).

On the simplex, \(a_{n\omega}\ge0\).  If it is positive, \(h_{n\omega}/a_{n\omega}\) is a convex combination of target values in \([-L,L]\), so clipping in (21) is inactive and completing the square gives \(q_{n\omega}-h_{n\omega}^2/a_{n\omega}\).  If \(a_{n\omega}=0\), nonnegativity of every \(\pi_\kappa H_{\kappa n\omega}\) forces both \(h_{n\omega}=0\) and \(q_{n\omega}=0\); (20) then gives zero without division.  Summing \(q_{n\omega}\) over \(\omega\) and using (6) proves
\[
 G(\pi)=\min_n b_n(\pi)
 \quad\text{for every }\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal K),
\tag{23}
\]
including every zero-prior-likelihood face.  Thus maximizing \(G\) is the same as \(\mathsf Q\) in \(\texttt{def:dual-game}\).

In reduced coordinates use
\[
 C_{\mathrm D}=\{(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_{A-1}):
                 \pi_\kappa\ge\gamma\ (1\le\kappa\le A)\}
       \subset\mathbb R^{p_{\mathrm D}},
 \qquad p_{\mathrm D}=A-1.
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(x_{\mathrm D}\) have every probability equal to \(1/A\), and set
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm D}=\frac1A-\gamma,
 \qquad \rho_{\mathrm D}=\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm D}}A.
\tag{25}
\]
The first \(A-1\) lower faces have normal norm one, the eliminated last face has normal norm at most \(A\), and every face has center slack \(\Delta_{\mathrm D}\).  Therefore the explicit nested polytopes and ball data are
\[
 \begin{split}
 I_{\mathrm D}&=\{x:\|x-x_{\mathrm D}\|_\infty
                         \le\rho_{\mathrm D}/p_{\mathrm D}\}
       \subseteq C_{\mathrm D}
       \subseteq O_{\mathrm D}=\{x:\|x\|_\infty\le1\},\\
 B_2(x_{\mathrm D},\rho_{\mathrm D}/p_{\mathrm D})
       &\subseteq I_{\mathrm D},
 \qquad O_{\mathrm D}\subseteq B_2(0,p_{\mathrm D}).
 \end{split}
\tag{26}
\]
Their rational encodings again have polynomial bit length, and exact rational inequalities supply membership and separation.

\emph{Step 4: strict erosion comparators, feasibility repair, and the tolerance ledger.}
Let \((d^\star,t^\star)\) attain the unrestricted primal value and let \(\pi^\star\) attain the dual value.  Their existence and the identities
\[
 \min F=R^\star=\max G
\tag{27}
\]
follow from \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\), \(\texttt{def:primal-game}\), and \(\texttt{def:dual-game}\).  Let \(u_{\mathrm P}\) be the uniform design \(d_n=1/J\) with decision coordinate zero, and let \(u_{\mathrm D}\) be the uniform prior.

First mix the primal optimizer with \(u_{\mathrm P}\) with weight \(a_0\).  The result has \(d_n\ge\eta\), and (6)--(7) give \(F(u_{\mathrm P})\le L^2\).  Convexity of \(F\) therefore shows that restricting to (16) costs at most
\[
 a_0L^2\le e/4096.
\tag{28}
\]
Now mix the original optimizer with \(u_{\mathrm P}\) with total weight \(4a_0\), and call the result \((d',t')\).  Equivalently, after the first mix, mix once more with weight \(3a_0/(1-a_0)<4a_0\).  This second, erosion-producing mix costs at most \(4a_0L^2\le e/1024\).  Moreover,
\[
 d'_n\ge4\eta,
 \qquad
 Ld'_n-|t'_{n\omega}|\ge \frac{4La_0}{J}=4L\eta.
\tag{29}
\]
Relative to \(C_{\mathrm P}\), every mass-face slack is at least \(3\eta\) and every decision-face slack is at least \(4L\eta\).  Every reduced-coordinate face normal has norm at most
\[
 Q_{\mathrm P}=J(1+L).
\tag{30}
\]
Choose
\[
 \alpha_{\mathrm P}=\min\left\{
   \frac e{1024},
   \frac{\eta\min\{1,L\}}{8J(1+L)},
   \frac e{512LJ(1+LJ)}
 \right\}.
\tag{31}
\]
All terms are positive rational numbers.  By (29)--(31), moving a Euclidean distance at most \(\alpha_{\mathrm P}\) changes the left side of every defining affine inequality by strictly less than its slack.  Hence
\[
 (d',t')\in S(C_{\mathrm P},-\alpha_{\mathrm P}).
\tag{32}
\]
This verifies the actual comparator premise in (14), face by face, including the eliminated mass face and every decision face.

Apply (14) to \(F\) on \(C_{\mathrm P}\) with tolerance \(\alpha_{\mathrm P}\).  It returns rational \((\widehat d,\widehat t)\in S(C_{\mathrm P},\alpha_{\mathrm P})\) and, by (28)--(32),
\[
 F(\widehat d,\widehat t)
 \le F(d',t')+\alpha_{\mathrm P}
 \le R^\star+\frac e{4096}+\frac e{1024}+\frac e{1024}.
\tag{33}
\]
Affine elimination makes \(\sum_n\widehat d_n=1\) exact.  Take a point of \(C_{\mathrm P}\) within distance \(\alpha_{\mathrm P}\).  For \(n<J\), its corresponding mass changes by at most \(\alpha_{\mathrm P}\); the reconstructed mass \(d_J\) changes by at most \(J\alpha_{\mathrm P}\).  Since (31) implies \(\alpha_{\mathrm P}\le\eta/(8J)\), every \(\widehat d_n\ge7\eta/8>0\).  Repair only the decision coordinates by the exact rational clipping
\[
 d_n^{\mathrm r}=\widehat d_n,
 \qquad
 t_{n\omega}^{\mathrm r}
 =\max\{-L\widehat d_n,
          \min\{L\widehat d_n,\widehat t_{n\omega}\}\}.
\tag{34}
\]
This point is exactly feasible for the original primal polytope.  Proximity to \(C_{\mathrm P}\) implies
\[
 |t_{n\omega}^{\mathrm r}-\widehat t_{n\omega}|
 \le(1+LJ)\alpha_{\mathrm P}.
\tag{35}
\]
Indeed, a nearby feasible \(t\)-coordinate differs by at most \(\alpha_{\mathrm P}\), and its associated mass differs by at most \(J\alpha_{\mathrm P}\).  Every affine slope in (8) with respect to \(t\) has magnitude at most \(4L\).  Using (6), then summing over \(n\), gives
\[
 F(d^{\mathrm r},t^{\mathrm r})-F(\widehat d,\widehat t)
 \le4LJ(1+LJ)\alpha_{\mathrm P}
 \le e/128.
\tag{36}
\]

For the dual, first mix \(\pi^\star\) with \(u_{\mathrm D}\) with weight \(a_0\).  For every fixed assignment orbit and clipped endpoint decision, the loss as a function of \(\kappa\) belongs to \([0,4L^2]\).  Therefore mixing any prior with weight \(q\) can lower its Bayes risk by at most \(4L^2q\); taking an infimum over decisions preserves this inequality.  The first restriction to \(\pi_\kappa\ge\gamma\) thus costs at most
\[
 4L^2a_0\le e/4096.
\tag{37}
\]
Mix once more so that the total uniform weight is \(4a_0\), and call the result \(\pi'\).  The second mixing weight is \(3a_0/(1-a_0)<4a_0\), so its additional loss is at most
\[
 4L^2(4a_0)\le e/1024.
\tag{38}
\]
Every coordinate of \(\pi'\) is at least \(4\gamma\), giving slack at least \(3\gamma\) in (24).  Since all reduced simplex-face normals have norm at most \(A\), the rational tolerance
\[
 \alpha_{\mathrm D}=\min\left\{\frac e{1024},\frac{\gamma}{8A}\right\}
\tag{39}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \pi'\in S(C_{\mathrm D},-\alpha_{\mathrm D}).
\tag{40}
\]
Apply (14) to the globally convex function \(-G\).  The rational output \(\widehat\pi\in S(C_{\mathrm D},\alpha_{\mathrm D})\) satisfies
\[
 G(\widehat\pi)
 \ge G(\pi')-\alpha_{\mathrm D}
 \ge R^\star-\frac e{4096}-\frac e{1024}-\frac e{1024}.
\tag{41}
\]
Its first \(A-1\) reconstructed coordinates are at least \(\gamma-\alpha_{\mathrm D}>0\), and its last coordinate is at least \(\gamma-A\alpha_{\mathrm D}\ge7\gamma/8>0\).  The coordinates sum to one exactly, so \(\widehat\pi\) is already an exactly feasible rational prior; no projection or division is needed.

Let \(\beta=e/128\), evaluate
\[
 U_0=F(d^{\mathrm r},t^{\mathrm r}),
 \qquad V_0=G(\widehat\pi)
\tag{42}
\]
by exact rational arithmetic, and outward-round only at the final step:
\[
 \overline R=\beta\left\lceil U_0/\beta\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 \underline R=\beta\left\lfloor V_0/\beta\right\rfloor.
\tag{43}
\]
The exact evaluation itself has zero error and each rounding loses less than \(e/128\).  The full ledger, in units of \(e/4096\), is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{operation}&\text{primal upper side}&\text{dual lower side}\\ \hline
\text{restriction to masses at least }\eta\text{ or }\gamma&1&1\\
\text{additional mixing for an erosion comparator}&4&4\\
\text{erosion-based weak optimization}&4&4\\
\text{near-feasibility repair and clipping}&32&0\\
\text{exact direct objective evaluation}&0&0\\
\text{outward rational rounding}&32&32
\end{array}
\tag{44}
\]
The first row is bounded by (28) and (37), the second by the sequential mixing calculations preceding (29) and by (38), the third by (31), (33), (39), and (41), the fourth by (36), the fifth is exact by (42), and the last is bounded by (43).  It follows that
\[
 R^\star\le\overline R<R^\star+\frac{73e}{4096},
 \qquad
 R^\star-\frac{41e}{4096}<\underline R\le R^\star,
\tag{45}
\]
and hence
\[
 0\le\overline R-\underline R
 <\frac{114e}{4096}=\frac{57e}{2048}<e\le\varepsilon.
\tag{46}
\]

The two inequalities in (45) are independently checkable.  For the upper one, verify for every \(\kappa\) the rational inequality
\[
 \sum_{n,\omega}H_{\kappa n\omega}
 \frac{(t^{\mathrm r}_{n\omega}-d_n^{\mathrm r}\theta_\kappa)^2}
      {d_n^{\mathrm r}}
 \le\overline R;
\tag{47}
\]
all denominators are positive by (34).  Since the point is primal feasible, (27) gives \(R^\star\le\overline R\).  For the lower one, compute (19) exactly and set
\[
 \rho_{n\omega}=\begin{cases}
 h_{n\omega}^2/a_{n\omega},&a_{n\omega}>0,\\
 0,&a_{n\omega}=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{48}
\]
Exact comparison detects the case.  The implication \(a=0\Rightarrow h=0\), proved before (23), verifies the rotated-cone constraint on every zero face.  Then verify the finite list
\[
 \underline R\le
 \sum_\kappa\widehat\pi_\kappa\theta_\kappa^2
 -\sum_\omega\rho_{n\omega}
 \quad(n\in\mathcal N).
\tag{49}
\]
This is a rational feasible certificate for \(\mathsf Q\), so \(\underline R\le R^\star\).

It remains to check the promised Turing bound for the two oracle calls.  Equations (17)--(18) and (25)--(26) give, without implicit balls, rational centers, inner radii, outer radii, and nested full-dimensional polytopes.  Equations (15), (31), and (39) show that their encoding lengths and the logarithms of the reciprocal tolerances are polynomial in the rational input bit length, the logarithm of the displayed dimensions, and \(\log\max\{2,1/\varepsilon\}\).  The global oracles (8) and (20) use only a polynomial number of operations on polynomial-bit rationals at each query.  Exact summation in (47) and (49) increases bit length by at most the sum of the polynomial bit lengths of the displayed number of summands.  The cited erosion-based theorem therefore gives the asserted polynomial running time and rational output length.  Only weakly optimal rational points were constructed; nothing implies that an exact optimizer is rational.

\emph{Step 5: exact randomization and the total division-free decoder.}
The rational masses \(d_n^{\mathrm r}\) define an implementable correlated design.  Clear their denominators, sample an integer cumulative weight exactly, assign the labels of the selected histogram uniformly to equal-size labeled clusters, and sample each treated subset independently and uniformly conditional on its label.  Uniform integers are obtained by rejection from a power-of-two interval; its acceptance probability exceeds one half.  Denominator clearing has polynomial bit length, so this exact sampler has expected polynomial bit complexity.  This is precisely the implementation supplied by \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\).

For every compatible endpoint cell precompute the rational constant
\[
 \delta_{n\omega}=t^{\mathrm r}_{n\omega}/d_n^{\mathrm r};
\tag{50}
\]
it is defined because \(d_n^{\mathrm r}>0\), and (34) gives \(|\delta_{n\omega}|\le L\).  Put zero on incompatible cells.  More generally, on any feasible zero-design-mass face set \(\delta_{n\omega}=0\) when \(d_n=0\); primal feasibility forces \(t_{n\omega}=0\).  For the dual posterior table use \(h/a\) only after exact rational comparison establishes \(a>0\), and use zero when \(a=0\).  Thus neither discrete table contains an undefined ratio.

Fix a realized labeled assignment \(x\), order the units of cluster \(g\), put \(z_{gi}=\mathbf 1\{i\in S_g\}\), and let \(v_{gi}\) be the revealed coordinate.  Initialize \(Q_{g,0}(0,0)=1\) and all other states at zero.  For \(1\le j\le s_g\), define
\[
 Q_{g,j}(u,w)
 =(1-v_{gj})Q_{g,j-1}(u,w)
 +v_{gj}Q_{g,j-1}(u-z_{gj},w-(1-z_{gj})),
\tag{51}
\]
where every negative-index state is zero.  On \([0,1]^N\), this is exactly the Bernoulli-rounding probability of the local triple \((A_g,u,w)\).

Next order the clusters.  Let \(e_{j,u,w}\) add one count to the histogram coordinate indexed by \((s_j,A_j,u,w)\), initialize \(T_0(0)=1\), and put
\[
 T_j(h)=
 \sum_{u=0}^{r_{s_j,A_j}}
 \sum_{w=0}^{s_j-r_{s_j,A_j}}
 T_{j-1}(h-e_{j,u,w})Q_{j,s_j}(u,w),
 \qquad 1\le j\le m,
\tag{52}
\]
with absent states equal to zero.  Then \(T_m(\omega)\) is the probability of endpoint observation histogram \(\omega\), and the continuous decoder is
\[
 \overline\delta(x,v)=
 \sum_\omega T_m(\omega)\delta_{n(x),\omega}.
\tag{53}
\]
For \(v\in[0,1]^N\), the recursion produces nonnegative weights summing to one, so (53) is a convex combination of the clipped constants (50) and belongs to \([-L,L]\).  Once the rational constants (50) have been installed, (51)--(53) use addition, subtraction, and multiplication only.  They are therefore a division-free rational arithmetic circuit.  A cluster has only a bounded number of local states because \(s_g\le b\), and (52) retains no more than the displayed number of observation-histogram states.  The circuit size is polynomial in the displayed orbit size and the labeled input length.  At a rational input \(v\), exact rational evaluation has polynomial bit complexity and detects every zero likelihood by testing the reduced numerator.  An arbitrary real number is not claimed to be a finite Turing input.  Separately, the same finite polynomial circuit defines a total mathematical function for every \(v\in\mathbb R^N\); its Bernoulli and estimator interpretation is used only on \([0,1]^N\).

Expanding the two recursions gives
\[
 \overline\delta(x,v)=
 \sum_{e'\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal O(x)}}
 \delta_{[x,e']}
 \prod_{j:e'_j=1}v_j
 \prod_{j:e'_j=0}(1-v_j),
\tag{54}
\]
which is exactly \(\texttt{def:endpoint-extension}\).  Therefore \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\) promotes the binary upper certificate (47) to every bounded real schedule, while the rational binary prior in (49) remains a lower certificate for the larger schedule class.  This proves the compiler guarantee for every feasible treated-count table, including boundary counts and label-constant menus, with every zero-probability face treated explicitly.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem makes the finite minimax reduction deployable and auditable: every approximation, erosion comparison, feasibility repair, zero face, and outward-rounding step has an explicit rational certificate. \textit{Gap.} Existing design-minimax and network-experiment algorithms do not provide this exact observation-hypergraph orbit compiler with global rational perspective and Bayes-risk oracles, explicit rational well-bounding polytopes, erosion-valid comparators, and a total nonlinear decoder. \textit{Consumer.} A randomized-saturation experiment can archive an exact kernel, an implementable correlated randomization, a nonlinear analysis table, and a machine-checkable risk interval of any prescribed rational width.

\begin{lemma}[lem:local-interiority-hypergraph-obstruction]\label{lem:local-interiority-hypergraph-obstruction}
Fix \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(a\in\mathcal A\), put \(q=s_g\), and suppose \(q\ge 3\) and \(1\le r_{q,a}<q\).  Let
\[
 h=\max\{r_{q,a},q-r_{q,a}\},
 \qquad
 z_\star=\begin{cases}
 1,&r_{q,a}\ge q-r_{q,a},\\
 0,&r_{q,a}<q-r_{q,a}.
 \end{cases}
\]
There are \(h+1\) distinct coordinates \(e_j=(g,i_j,z_\star,a)\) such that every \(h\)-element subcollection, and hence every pair, is contained in \(\mathcal O(X)\) for some \(X\in\mathcal X\), whereas the full \(h+1\)-element collection is contained in no \(\mathcal O(X)\).  Consequently, the compatibility complex
\[
 \bigl\{T:T\subseteq\mathcal O(X)\text{ for some }X\in\mathcal X\bigr\}
\]
is not the independent-set complex of any pairwise conflict graph.  The exact orbit kernel \(H\) preserves this higher-order obstruction in the precise sense that its coefficients aggregate only feasible exact-count local observations and contain no term obtained by replacing their revealed-coordinate sets with a pairwise closure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\rho=r_{q,a}\) and \(U_q=\{1,\ldots,q\}\).  For a treated subset \(S\subseteq U_q\), define the unit set exposed in arm \(z\) by
\[
 E_1(S)=S,
 \qquad
 E_0(S)=U_q\setminus S.
 \tag{1}
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal O\), when \(A_g=a\) the coordinate \((g,i,z,a)\) belongs to \(\mathcal O(X)\) if and only if \(i\in E_z(S_g)\).  Feasibility of \(X\) further requires
\[
 |S_g|=\rho.
 \tag{2}
\]
The local premise \(1\le\rho<q\) gives
\[
 2\le \left\lceil\frac q2\right\rceil\le h\le q-1.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the maximum of \(\rho\) and \(q-\rho\) is at least their average and both integers are at most \(q-1\).  Thus \(U_q\) contains a set \(I\) of cardinality \(h+1\).  For \(i\in I\), put
\[
 e_i=(g,i,z_\star,a).
 \tag{4}
\]

Let \(J\subset I\) have \(|J|=h\).  If \(z_\star=1\), then its definition gives \(h=\rho\); choose \(A_g=a\) and \(S_g=J\).  Equations (1)--(2) then give \(E_{z_\star}(S_g)=J\).  If \(z_\star=0\), its definition gives \(h=q-\rho\); choose \(A_g=a\) and \(S_g=U_q\setminus J\).  Now \(|S_g|=q-h=\rho\) and again \(E_{z_\star}(S_g)=J\).  In either case, the chosen local assignment is feasible and
\[
 \{e_i:i\in J\}\subseteq\mathcal O(X).
 \tag{5}
\]
It extends to a global \(X\in\mathcal X\): for every cluster \(g'\ne g\), choose any label \(a'\in\mathcal A\) and any subset of \(\{1,\ldots,s_{g'}\}\) having cardinality \(r_{s_{g'},a'}\).  Such a subset exists because the declared range of \(r\) gives
\(0\le r_{s_{g'},a'}\le s_{g'}\).

Equation (3) also shows \(h\ge2\).  Every pair in \(I\) can therefore be enlarged to an \(h\)-element subset \(J\subset I\); (5) proves that every pair of the coordinates in (4) is jointly observable.  This establishes the claimed observability of every \(h\)-element subcollection and every pair.

Suppose, conversely, that some \(X\in\mathcal X\) revealed all \(h+1\) coordinates in (4).  Their label component and the definition of \(\mathcal O\) force \(A_g=a\), and (1) forces
\[
 I\subseteq E_{z_\star}(S_g).
 \tag{6}
\]
If \(z_\star=1\), equations (1)--(2) give \(|E_{z_\star}(S_g)|=\rho=h\).  If \(z_\star=0\), they give \(|E_{z_\star}(S_g)|=q-\rho=h\).  Both cases contradict (6), because \(|I|=h+1\).  Hence the full collection is not jointly observable.

Let
\[
 \mathfrak F=\bigl\{T:T\subseteq\mathcal O(X)
                  \text{ for some }X\in\mathcal X\bigr\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
This is the downward-closed compatibility complex of the exact-count observation hypergraph.  If a simple graph had independent-set complex \(\mathfrak F\), joint observability of every pair from (4) would imply that no pair of vertices in that collection is an edge.  The whole collection would then be independent, contrary to (6).  Thus no pairwise conflict graph represents \(\mathfrak F\).

It remains to verify the assertion about the orbit kernel rather than infer it from terminology.  For a binary size-\(q\) response type \(t\), \(\texttt{def:local-kernel}\) is equivalently
\[
 P_{t,a}(u,v)
 =\binom q\rho^{-1}
   \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq U_q\\ |S|=\rho}}
   \mathbf 1\!\left\{
      \sum_{i\in S}t_{i,1,a}=u,
      \ \sum_{i\notin S}t_{i,0,a}=v
   \right\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The denominator is positive because \(0\le\rho\le q\).  Every summand in (8) is generated by the feasible local revealed-coordinate set
\[
 \mathcal O_{q,a}(S)
 =\{(g,i,1,a):i\in S\}
   \mathbin\cup
   \{(g,i,0,a):i\in U_q\setminus S\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
By the preceding cardinality argument, no set in (9) contains all coordinates in (4).

Now expand the generating polynomial in \(\texttt{def:orbit-kernel}\).  After substituting (8), each monomial contribution is a product of nonnegative weights indexed by one feasible exact-count subset for every local cluster factor.  Coefficient extraction only sums such contributions.  For compatible label margins, the subsequent multinomial divisor is a positive integer; for incompatible margins, the coefficient is set to zero.  Hence neither operation introduces a local revealed-coordinate set absent from (8)--(9), and no cancellation can conceal an introduced term because all contributions are nonnegative.  Thus \(H\) is the orbit pushforward of the exact observation hypergraph, not of its pairwise closure, and it retains the forbidden \((h+1)\)-set.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} An interior exact-count cell creates a minimal higher-order nonface that every pairwise compatibility surrogate necessarily fills in. \textit{Gap.} Pairwise conflict graphs cannot encode the local cardinality obstruction created by an exact treated count, even though they encode every two-coordinate compatibility relation. \textit{Consumer.} Orbit-kernel builders can test any realized interior cell and retain its exact higher-order observation support.

\section{Interpretation}

For a fixed menu of saturation labels and bounded cluster sizes, an experimental team can compile the exact finite observation experiment rather than approximate it by pairwise conflicts. A single interior cluster-label cell already certifies when such a graph approximation is invalid. Rational weights specify how to randomize saturation histograms and treated subsets, the endpoint table specifies a possibly nonlinear analysis rule, and two directly checkable rational inequalities bracket the worst-case causal mean-squared error within any requested rational tolerance. The independent-design frontier additionally checks all positive supports on both sides of the saddle, while the continuous-outcome rule remains total on structurally impossible or prior-null observations.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The compiler guarantee is polynomial in the displayed orbit-program size for fixed \(K\) and \(b\); it does not make the constant-size response alphabet small in practice and is not a strongly polynomial algorithm. Exact rational arithmetic may produce large certificates even though their bit length is polynomial. The numerical size-three witness remains supported by its separate exhaustive rational verifier, while the compiler theorem supplies the general symbolic kernel construction, global oracle geometry, erosion-correct weak optimization, tolerance accounting, and decoder proof rather than a new numerical rerun. The strict separation is a finite-game, contrast-neighborhood result; no topology on observable laws or asymptotic experiment sequence is part of the theorem.

\section{Honest scope}

Endpoint completeness, the exact orbit game, and the bit-certified compiler cover every rational feasible table \(0\le r_{q,a}\le q\), including pure-control, pure-treatment, and label-constant treated-count menus. Hypergraph nonrepresentability is local: it follows whenever a realized cluster-label cell has \(1\le r_{q,a}<q\), with no claim for a table consisting only of boundary cells. The independent-saturation result is a finite primal-dual feasibility and positive-support saddle characterization, not an elementary closed-form classification. The compiler returns rational weakly optimal certificates and a total continuous estimator; it does not claim exact rational optimizers or a separation as the number of clusters tends to infinity. The strict advantage is only the certified two-cluster exact-count game and its stated infinite rational contrast neighborhood. It does not claim robustness in an ambient observable-law topology, nondegenerate superpopulation conditional support or variance, positive region mass, or asymptotic persistence; those would be different strengthening projects, not premises of the stated finite minimax inequality.
