The two prescribed counts are interior because
\[
1\le r_{3,1}=1<3,
\qquad
1\le r_{3,2}=2<3,
\]
as required by \(\texttt{ass:interior-exact-counts}\), and they differ, as required by \(\texttt{ass:saturation-variation}\).  The coefficient vector has zero sum, and
\[
L={1\over2}(3+1+1+1)=3.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus this is an admissible instance of the exact binary orbit game supplied by \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\).  In particular, every upper bound computed below for all binary response orbits is also an upper bound over the full bounded schedule class \(\mathcal M\), and every binary prior below is a valid lower bound over \(\mathcal M\).

We first reconstruct the finite likelihood table.  Encode a unit's four binary responses, in the order \((1,1),(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)\), as the four low-to-high bits of an integer in \(\{0,\ldots,15\}\).  A size-three cluster type is a nondecreasing triple of such integers.  Hence the number of cluster types and the number of unordered two-cluster response orbits are, respectively,
\[
T=\binom{16+3-1}{3}=816,
\qquad
\binom{T+1}{2}=333336.                                                       \tag{2}
\]
For a cluster type \(t\), let \(C_{a,t,k}\) be the number of the three feasible exact-count subsets at label \(a\) that produce local observation index \(k\).  At label one put \(k=3u+v\), where \(u\) and \(v\) are the treated-one and control-one counts; at label two put \(k=2u+v\).  In both cases \(0\le k<6\), and the local kernel in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-orbit-equivalence}\) is
\[
P_{t,a}(k)={C_{a,t,k}\over3},
\qquad
\sum_{k=0}^{5}C_{a,t,k}=3.                                                  \tag{3}
\]

Order the observation cells by the upper triangle for assignment orbit \(11\), the \(6\)-by-\(6\) rectangle for orbit \(12\), and the upper triangle for orbit \(22\).  For an unordered response orbit \(\{t,t'\}\), define its integer likelihood row by
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{tt',\{k,l\}_{11}}
 &=2\left(C_{1,t,k}C_{1,t',l}
       +\mathbf 1\{k<l\}C_{1,t,l}C_{1,t',k}\right), && k\le l,\\
M_{tt',(k,l)_{12}}
 &=C_{1,t,k}C_{2,t',l}+C_{1,t',k}C_{2,t,l}, &&0\le k,l<6,\\
M_{tt',\{k,l\}_{22}}
 &=2\left(C_{2,t,k}C_{2,t',l}
       +\mathbf 1\{k<l\}C_{2,t,l}C_{2,t',k}\right), && k\le l.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{4}
\]
For blocks \(11\) and \(22\), summing the upper-triangular symmetrization in (4) gives twice the sum over all ordered pairs of local indices; by (3) it is \(2\cdot3\cdot3=18\).  The rectangular block also sums to \(3\cdot3+3\cdot3=18\).  Therefore
\[
H_{\{t,t'\},g,j}={M_{tt',j}\over18},
\qquad
\sum_{j:g(j)=g}H_{\{t,t'\},g,j}=1                                        \tag{5}
\]
for each \(g\in\{11,12,22\}\).  The two terms in the middle line of (4) are exactly the two equally likely label orientations in orbit \(12\); the factor two in the same-label blocks gives the corresponding unordered-observation normalization.  Thus (4), not a pairwise-conflict surrogate, is the exact observation-hypergraph kernel.

As a separate normalization stress test for the same coefficient construction, take cluster sizes \((3,3,4)\), counts \((r_{3,1},r_{3,2})=(1,2)\) and \((r_{4,1},r_{4,2})=(1,3)\), size-three response types \((0,1,3)\), \((2,12,15)\), and size-four response type \((0,1,6,15)\).  Expanding the local kernels and, independently, enumerating every labeled label allocation and feasible treated subset give identical rational probabilities in every observation-histogram cell.  In lexicographic order of \((n_{3,1},n_{4,1})\in\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1\}\), the six row support sizes are
\[
6, 8, 27, 36, 24, 32,                                                  \tag{6}
\]
and every row sums exactly to one.  This verifies the assignment-orbit divisor at the heterogeneous-size boundary before the specialized certificate is used.

It remains to prove the contrast-neighborhood statement.  Let \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q\) satisfy \(|\alpha|\le10^{-5}\), and let \(\tau_\alpha\) be the target for coefficient vector \((3+\alpha,-1-\alpha,-1,-1)\).  The signs of the four coefficients do not change on this interval, their sum remains zero, and the contrast remains admissible.  Since \(N=6\), bounded outcomes give, for every fixed schedule,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\tau_\alpha(Y)-\tau_0(Y)|
&=\left|{\alpha\over6}\sum_{g,i}
       \{Y_{gi}(1,1)-Y_{gi}(0,1)\}\right|\\
&\le |\alpha|.                                                             
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{28}
\]

For either the unrestricted design class or the common-probability independent class, write its minimax risk at contrast \(\alpha\) as \(R_{\mathcal D,\alpha}\).  We may perform the following comparison over the common class of all real-valued measurable estimators: because \(\tau_\alpha(Y)\) belongs to its bounded target interval, projection of any decision onto that interval weakly decreases its pointwise squared error.  Thus allowing all real decisions does not alter the clipped minimax value.

For a fixed design, estimator, and schedule, put \(E_0=\delta(X,V)-\tau_0(Y)\) and \(h=\tau_0(Y)-\tau_\alpha(Y)\).  The elementary variance identity gives \(|\mathbb E_D E_0|\le(\mathbb E_D E_0^2)^{1/2}\), because \(\mathbb E_D[(E_0-\mathbb E_D E_0)^2]\ge0\).  Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{\mathbb E_D(E_0+h)^2\}^{1/2}
&\le \{\mathbb E_D E_0^2
       +2|h|(\mathbb E_D E_0^2)^{1/2}+h^2\}^{1/2}\\
&=(\mathbb E_D E_0^2)^{1/2}+|h|.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{29}
\]
Taking the supremum over schedules, then the infimum over designs and estimators, and using (28) yields
\[
\sqrt{R_{\mathcal D,\alpha}}
\le\sqrt{R_{\mathcal D,0}}+|\alpha|.                                     \tag{30}
\]
Interchanging \(0\) and \(\alpha\) in the same argument proves
\[
\left|\sqrt{R_{\mathcal D,\alpha}}
      -\sqrt{R_{\mathcal D,0}}\right|\le|\alpha|.                        \tag{31}
\]

Put \(a=\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{ind},0}}\) and \(b=\sqrt{R^\star_0}\).  The zero decision and (1) give \(a,b\le3\), while (26) gives \(a>1>|\alpha|\).  Therefore (27) and (31) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{ind},\alpha}-R^\star_\alpha
&\ge(a-|\alpha|)^2-(b+|\alpha|)^2\\
&=a^2-b^2-2|\alpha|(a+b)\\
&>0.000918578-12|\alpha|\\
&\ge0.000798578>7\times10^{-4}.
\end{aligned}                                                               \tag{32}
\]
Every rational \(\alpha\) in the stated interval preserves rationality and the zero-sum contrast condition.  Equations (13), (19), (20), (26), (27), and (32) prove every claimed certificate and the infinite rational contrast neighborhood.
