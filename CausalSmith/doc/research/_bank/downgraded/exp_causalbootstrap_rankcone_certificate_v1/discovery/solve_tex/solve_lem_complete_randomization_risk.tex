%%% FIELD fixed-count-statement
Under uniform fixed-count sample-and-assign randomization with \(n_a\geq1\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}=N-\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}n_a\geq0\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_Z(\widehat{\bar Y}\mid Y)
=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}\right)_{a\in\mathcal A}-\frac{S(Y)}{N}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\Sigma_C(Y)=C\left\{\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}\right)_{a\in\mathcal A}-\frac{S(Y)}{N}\right\}C^{\mathsf T}
\]
and
\[
\rho_W(Y)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{H_{aa}S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}
-\frac1N\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal A}H_{ab}S_{ab}(Y).
\]
For fixed armwise empirical marginals \(F\), the diagonal terms are constant over \(\mathcal S_N(F)\). Thus all completion dependence is carried by the off-diagonal cross-arm covariances, whose coefficients are independent of \(\mathbf n\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}\).

%%% FIELD fixed-count-proof
Put \(T_{ia}=\mathbf 1(Z_i=a)\) and \(p_a=n_a/N\). Feasibility of \(\mathbf n\) gives \(n_a\geq1\), \(\sum_a n_a\leq N\), and, because \(K\geq2\), \(N\geq2\). Hence every denominator below is positive. By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}sample\text{-}assign\text{-}randomization}\), \(Z\) is uniform on the support in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}sample\text{-}assign\text{-}design}\). Counting fixed-label allocations gives
\[
\mathbb E_ZT_{ia}=p_a,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Z(T_{ia}T_{ja})=\frac{n_a(n_a-1)}{N(N-1)}\quad(i\ne j),
\tag{1}
\]
and, for \(a\ne b\),
\[
\mathbb E_Z(T_{ia}T_{ib})=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Z(T_{ia}T_{jb})=\frac{n_an_b}{N(N-1)}\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, a fixed position has label \(a\) in the fraction \(n_a/N\) of the support; conditional on that event, \(n_a-1\) of the remaining \(N-1\) positions have label \(a\), and \(n_b\) have label \(b\). The same count remains valid when \(n_{\mathrm u}>0\), because \(a_{\mathrm u}\) is another fixed-count assignment label.

Write \(y_i^a=Y_i(a)-\bar Y(a)\). By the definitions of \(\bar Y\) and \(S(Y)\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^Ny_i^a=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^N(y_i^a)^2=(N-1)S_{aa}(Y),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^Ny_i^ay_i^b=(N-1)S_{ab}(Y).
\tag{3}
\]
Equation (1) first gives design unbiasedness:
\[
\mathbb E_Z\{\widehat{\bar Y}(a)\mid Y\}
=\frac1{n_a}\sum_{i=1}^Np_aY_i(a)=\bar Y(a).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(i\ne j\), equation (1) yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z(T_{ia})=p_a(1-p_a),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_Z(T_{ia},T_{ja})=-\frac{p_a(1-p_a)}{N-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^ay_j^a=-\sum_i(y_i^a)^2\), equations (3)--(5) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_Z\{\widehat{\bar Y}(a)\mid Y\}
&=\frac1{n_a^2}\left[
p_a(1-p_a)\sum_i(y_i^a)^2
-\frac{p_a(1-p_a)}{N-1}\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^ay_j^a
\right]\\
&=\frac{p_a(1-p_a)N}{n_a^2(N-1)}\sum_i(y_i^a)^2
=\left(\frac1{n_a}-\frac1N\right)S_{aa}(Y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
For distinct arms, equation (2) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_Z(T_{ia},T_{ib})=-p_ap_b,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_Z(T_{ia},T_{jb})=\frac{p_ap_b}{N-1}\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^ay_j^b=-\sum_i y_i^ay_i^b\), equations (3), (4), and (7) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}_Z\{\widehat{\bar Y}(a),\widehat{\bar Y}(b)\mid Y\}
&=\frac1{n_an_b}\left[
-p_ap_b\sum_i y_i^ay_i^b
+\frac{p_ap_b}{N-1}\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^ay_j^b
\right]\\
&=-\frac{p_ap_bN}{n_an_b(N-1)}\sum_i y_i^ay_i^b
=-\frac1N S_{ab}(Y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (6) and (8) proves
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_Z(\widehat{\bar Y}\mid Y)
=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}\right)_{a\in\mathcal A}-\frac{S(Y)}N.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(\widehat\tau=C\widehat{\bar Y}\), covariance equivariance gives the displayed formula for \(\Sigma_C(Y)\). Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}contrast\text{-}risk}\), cyclicity of trace, and \(H=C^{\mathsf T}WC\) then yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_W(Y)
&=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[H\left\{
\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}\right)_{a\in\mathcal A}-\frac{S(Y)}N
\right\}\right]\\
&=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{H_{aa}S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}
-\frac1N\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal A}H_{ab}S_{ab}(Y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal S_N(F)\) fixes the multiset in each arm, hence every \(S_{aa}(Y)\). Equation (10) proves the completion-dependence assertion.

For the smallest exhaustive audit with an unassigned unit, take \(K=2\), \(N=3\), \(n_1=n_2=1\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=1\). The support consists of all six placements of the labels \(1,2,a_{\mathrm u}\). Each selected arm index is uniform on \(\{1,2,3\}\), and the ordered pair of selected indices is uniform over the six distinct pairs. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z\{\widehat{\bar Y}(a)\mid Y\}
=\frac13\sum_i(y_i^a)^2=\frac23S_{aa}(Y)
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_Z\{\widehat{\bar Y}(1),\widehat{\bar Y}(2)\mid Y\}
=\frac16\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^1y_j^2
=-\frac16\sum_i y_i^1y_i^2=-\frac13S_{12}(Y),
\]
exactly matching (6) and (8).

%%% FIELD balanced-statement
Under balanced complete randomization,
\[
\Sigma_C(Y)=C\left\{\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{S_{aa}(Y)}{n}\right)_{a\in\mathcal A}-\frac{S(Y)}{N}\right\}C^{\mathsf T}
\]
and hence
\[
\rho_W(Y)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{H_{aa}S_{aa}(Y)}{n}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal A}H_{ab}S_{ab}(Y).
\]
Therefore all completion dependence is carried by the off-diagonal cross-arm covariances.

%%% FIELD balanced-proof
Under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}complete\text{-}randomization}\), \(K\mid N\), \(n=N/K\), every arm has \(n_a=n\), and the assigned counts exhaust the population:
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}n_a=Kn=N,
\qquad n_{\mathrm u}=0.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}allocation\text{-}space}\) says that \(\mathcal Z_{N,K}\) is exactly the specialization of \(\mathcal Z_{N,\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\) at \(\mathbf n=n\mathbf 1_K\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\). The balanced law and \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\) are therefore uniform on the same finite set, so the hypothesis of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}randomization\text{-}risk}\) holds. Substitution of \(n_a=n\) in that lemma gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_Z(\widehat{\bar Y}\mid Y)
=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{S_{aa}(Y)}n\right)_{a\in\mathcal A}-\frac{S(Y)}N.
\]
Multiplication on the left and right by \(C\) and \(C^{\mathsf T}\), respectively, yields the displayed formula for \(\Sigma_C(Y)\). The scalar formula follows from the same lemma after substituting \(n_a=n\). Finally, each diagonal \(S_{aa}(Y)\) depends only on the empirical marginal \(F_a\), so only off-diagonal cross-arm covariances vary over \(\mathcal S_N(F)\).

%%% FIELD general-design-construction
For a feasible count vector \(\mathbf n=(n_a)_{a\in\mathcal A}\), let \(n_{\mathrm u}=N-\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}n_a\) and
\[
\mathcal Z_{N,\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}
=\left\{z\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm u}^{N}:
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf 1(z_i=a)=n_a\ \text{for every }a\in\mathcal A,
\quad
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf 1(z_i=a_{\mathrm u})=n_{\mathrm u}
\right\}.
\]
The uniform fixed-count sample-and-assign law is
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}(Z=z)
=\lvert\mathcal Z_{N,\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\rvert^{-1}
\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal Z_{N,\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\}.
\]
The label \(a_{\mathrm u}\) records an unassigned unit and has no associated observed arm outcome. For each assigned arm,
\[
\widehat{\bar Y}(a)=\frac{1}{n_a}\sum_{i:Z_i=a}Y_i(a).
\]

%%% FIELD general-design-assumption
The assignment obeys \(Z\sim\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\) for the feasible count vector \(\mathbf n\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}sample\text{-}assign\text{-}design}\).

On the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(K\mid N\), with \(n=N/K\), \(n_a=n\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\), the assignment \(Z\) is uniform over \(\mathcal Z_{N,K}\).

%%% FIELD balanced-allocation-construction
On the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(K\mid N\), put \(n=N/K\), specialize \(n_a=n\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\), and define
\[
\mathcal Z_{N,K}=\left\{z\in\mathcal A^N:\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf 1(z_i=a)=n\ \text{for every }a\in\mathcal A\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, \(\mathcal Z_{N,K}=\mathcal Z_{N,n\mathbf 1_K}^{\mathrm{sa}}\). This is the support used only by the balanced assignment-law and kernel domains.

%%% FIELD balanced-law-construction
On the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(K\mid N\), with \(n=N/K\), \(n_a=n\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\), define
\[
\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}=\left\{p\in\Delta(\mathcal Z_{N,K}):\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{N,K}}p(z)\mathbf 1(z_i=a)=K^{-1}\text{ for every }i\text{ and }a\right\}.
\]
Thus every law has exactly \(n\) assignments to each arm, no unassigned units, total mass one, nonnegative atom probabilities, and \(\Pr_p(Z_i=a)=1/K\), without unit- or arm-exchangeability.

%%% FIELD balanced-kernel-construction
On the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(K\mid N\), with \(n=N/K\), \(n_a=n\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\), define
\[
\mathfrak K^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}=\left\{\Omega:\exists p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K},\quad \Omega_{abij}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{N,K}}p(z)\{K\mathbf 1(z_i=a)-1\}\{K\mathbf 1(z_j=b)-1\}\right\}.
\]
This finite moment-polytope representation is the exact feasibility constraint. Every member satisfies \(\Omega_{abij}=\Omega_{baji}\), \(\sum_a\Omega_{abij}=\sum_b\Omega_{abij}=0\), \(\sum_i\Omega_{abij}=\sum_j\Omega_{abij}=0\), \(\Omega_{aaii}=K-1\), \(\Omega_{abii}=-1\) for \(a\ne b\), \(-1\leq\Omega_{abij}\leq K-1\), and positive semidefiniteness of the \(NK\times NK\) matrix indexed by \((a,i)\). No further permutation symmetry is imposed, and these displayed consequences without the probability-simplex representation are not sufficient for feasibility.

%%% FIELD relabeling-construction
On the balanced exhaustive kernel domain \(K\mid N\), for \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_N\), define \((\Omega^{\pi})_{abij}=\Omega_{ab,\pi(i),\pi(j)}\). Write \(\Omega\cong\Omega'\) exactly when \(\Omega'=\Omega^{\pi}\) for some \(\pi\), and set \([\Omega]_{\cong}=\{\Omega^{\pi}:\pi\in\mathfrak S_N\}\).

%%% FIELD balanced-loss-construction
On the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(K\mid N\), with \(n=N/K\), \(n_a=n\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\), for \(x\in\mathcal M_N^K\) and \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_N^K\), define
\[
\mathscr L_{H,\Omega}(x,\sigma)=\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal A}\sum_{i,j=1}^N H_{ab}\Omega_{abij}x^a_{\sigma_a(i)}x^b_{\sigma_b(j)}.
\]
If \(\Omega\) is induced by \(p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}\), this equals \(\operatorname{tr}\{W\operatorname{Cov}_p(C\widehat{\bar Y}\mid Y)\}\) for the schedule with arm columns \(x^a\circ\sigma_a\).

%%% FIELD identity-cone-construction
On the balanced exhaustive kernel domain \(K\mid N\), define
\[
\mathcal C_{N,K}(H)=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb R^{K\times K\times N\times N}:\mathscr L_{H,\Omega}(x,\mathrm{id})\geq\mathscr L_{H,\Omega}(x,\sigma)\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal M_N^K\text{ and }\sigma\in\mathfrak S_N^K\right\}.
\]
It is a closed convex cone because every defining comparison is homogeneous and linear in \(\Omega\).

%%% FIELD admissible-rank-domain-construction
On the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(K\mid N\), with \(n=N/K\), \(n_a=n\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\), define
\[
\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}(H)=\left\{[\Omega]_{\cong}:\Omega\in\mathfrak K^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K},\ [\Omega]_{\cong}\cap\mathcal C_{N,K}(H)\neq\varnothing\right\}.
\]
Recognition is posed only for an input tensor already in \(\mathfrak K^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}\), equivalently with a valid inducing law \(p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}\). This domain is distinct from arbitrary-count uniform sample-and-assign designs.

%%% FIELD kernel-handle-construction
On the balanced exhaustive kernel domain \(K\mid N\), starting from \(x^a\in\mathcal M_N\), use
\[
x^a=x^a_1\mathbf 1_N+\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}(x^a_{r+1}-x^a_r)d_r.
\]
Insert these nonnegative adjacent increments into \(\mathscr L_{H,\Omega}\), quotient by \([\Omega]_{\cong}\), and seek a finite cut-product separation certificate for membership in \(\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}(H)\). This handle does not range over the general sample-and-assign domain.

%%% FIELD rank-cone-general
Fix \(K\geq2\), \(C\mathbf 1_K=0\), and \(W\succeq0\), and put \(H=C^{\mathsf T}WC\). The following are equivalent. First, for every \(N\geq K\), every feasible \(\mathbf n=(n_a)_{a\in\mathcal A}\) with \(n_a\geq1\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}=N-\sum_a n_a\geq0\), and every collection \(F\) of equal-weight empirical armwise marginals,
\[
Y^{\uparrow}(F)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)}\rho_W(Y)
\]
under \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\). Second, \(H\in\mathcal R_K\). In fact, the equivalence holds for each fixed feasible \((N,\mathbf n)\). When it holds, for every completion \(Y\),
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{a<b}(-H_{ab})\left[S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-S_{ab}(Y)\right]\geq0.
\]
If \(H_{ab}>0\) for some pair, take in each of arms \(a,b\) a binary marginal with one value equal to one and \(N-1\) values equal to zero, and take every other arm constant. Aligning the two ones gives the common-rank completion, while placing them on distinct units gives a feasible completion with exact regret \(-2H_{ab}/\{N(N-1)\}<0\). Thus necessity requires neither balance nor any divisibility condition.

%%% FIELD loewner-general
Fix any \(N\geq K\) and any feasible positive count vector \(\mathbf n\), allowing \(n_{\mathrm u}\geq0\), and use the uniform sample-and-assign law \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\). For a nonzero contrast matrix \(C\),
\[
\Sigma_C\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}\succeq\Sigma_C(Y)
\quad\text{for every }F\text{ and every }Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)
\]
if and only if all columns of \(C\) vanish except two columns that are negatives of one another. Equivalently, every nonzero universally Loewner-dominated multi-contrast system is a vector rescaling of one pairwise arm contrast. For every other \(C\), there are a direction \(h\), two binary armwise marginals, and a feasible completion for which common ranking reduces \(\operatorname{Var}_Z(h^{\mathsf T}\widehat\tau\mid Y)\); the one-success construction gives a strict difference \(-2v_av_b/\{N(N-1)\}\), where \(v=C^{\mathsf T}h\) and \(v_av_b>0\).

%%% FIELD two-arm-general
Let \(K=2\), let the present arms \(1,2\) correspond to Aronow--Green--Lee treatment labels \(1,0\), let \(m=1\), \(C=(1,-1)\), and \(W=(1)\). Suppose \(N\geq4\), \(4\leq n_{\mathrm s}\leq N\), and \(2\leq m_{\mathrm t}\leq n_{\mathrm s}-2\), and specialize the fixed counts to
\[
n_1=m_{\mathrm t},\qquad n_2=n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t},\qquad n_{\mathrm u}=N-n_{\mathrm s}.
\]
First drawing a simple random sample of \(n_{\mathrm s}\) units and then choosing uniformly \(m_{\mathrm t}\) treated units inside that sample induces exactly \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\), the uniform law over the three cells of sizes \(m_{\mathrm t}\), \(n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}\), and \(N-n_{\mathrm s}\). With \(S_{ab}(Y)=N\sigma_{ab}^{(N)}(Y)/(N-1)\), the fixed-count risk identity becomes term by term Aronow, Green, and Lee's Equation (2):
\[
\rho_W(Y)=\frac1{N-1}\left\{
\frac{N-m_{\mathrm t}}{m_{\mathrm t}}\sigma_{11}^{(N)}(Y)
+\frac{N-(n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t})}{n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}}\sigma_{00}^{(N)}(Y)
+2\sigma_{10}^{(N)}(Y)
\right\}.
\]
The diagonal terms are fixed by the two marginal schedules, and Aronow, Green, and Lee's Lemma 1 gives the sharp upper covariance endpoint, attained by the comonotone completion \(Y^{\uparrow}(F)\). This is an exact finite oracle recovery for known marginal distributions, not a claim about their subsequent asymptotics for estimated bounds.

%%% FIELD omnibus-general
If the rows of \(C\) form an orthonormal basis of the zero-sum contrast space and \(W=I_{K-1}\), then \(H_{aa}=1-K^{-1}\) and \(H_{ab}=-K^{-1}\) for \(a\ne b\), so \(H\in\mathcal R_K\). Hence, for every \(N\geq K\), every feasible positive count vector \(\mathbf n\), and every nonnegative unassigned remainder, common ranks maximize the sum of the randomization variances of the complete orthonormal contrast system. When \(K>2\), the generalized Loewner boundary still rules out universal covariance-matrix dominance.

%%% FIELD finite-copositivity-localized
Assume \(K\mid N\), put \(n=N/K\), and restrict to the balanced exhaustive subdomain \(n_a=n\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}=0\). Let \(H\in\mathbb S_+^K\) and \(\Omega\in\mathfrak K^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}\). Put \(J=\mathcal A\times\{1,\ldots,N-1\}\). For \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_N\) and \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_N^K\), define the symmetric \(|J|\times|J|\) matrix \(B^{\pi,\sigma}\) by
\[
B^{\pi,\sigma}_{(a,r),(b,s)}
=\frac{H_{ab}}{N^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^N\Omega_{ab,\pi(i),\pi(j)}
\left[(d_r)_i(d_s)_j-(d_r)_{\sigma_a(i)}(d_s)_{\sigma_b(j)}\right].
\]
Then \(\Omega^\pi\in\mathcal C_{N,K}(H)\) if and only if \(B^{\pi,\sigma}\) is copositive for every \(\sigma\), meaning \(u^{\mathsf T}B^{\pi,\sigma}u\geq0\) for every \(u\in\mathbb R_+^J\). Consequently,
\[
[\Omega]_{\cong}\in\mathfrak C^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}(H)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists }\pi\in\mathfrak S_N\text{ such that every }B^{\pi,\sigma}\text{ is copositive}.
\]
For any real symmetric matrix \(B\) indexed by \(J\), copositivity has the finite support characterization
\[
B\text{ is copositive}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ker\{B[I]-\lambda I_I\}\cap\mathbb R_{++}^{I}=\varnothing
\]
for every nonempty \(I\subseteq J\) and every negative real eigenvalue \(\lambda<0\) of the principal matrix \(B[I]\).

If, in addition, the entries of \(H\) and \(\Omega\) are supplied as exact real-algebraic numbers, in particular as rational numbers, these equivalences give a finite exact recognition algorithm. It enumerates \(\pi\), \(\sigma\), and nonempty \(I\), isolates every negative algebraic eigenvalue of \(B^{\pi,\sigma}[I]\), and decides by exact ordered-field linear feasibility whether its eigenspace meets \(\mathbb R_{++}^{I}\). A noncopositivity certificate consists of \((I,\lambda,v)\) with \(v\in\ker\{B^{\pi,\sigma}[I]-\lambda I_I\}\cap\mathbb R_{++}^{I}\); it yields explicit ordered columns with at most \(N\) levels and a strict loss improvement. A certificate that a fixed \(\pi\) works consists of the finite collection of infeasibility certificates for all these negative-eigenspace tests. Thus a yes instance returns such a \(\pi\), while a no instance returns, for every \(\pi\), a corresponding strict cut witness. No algebraic enumeration claim is made for unrestricted real inputs, and the exhaustive algorithm is not asserted to run in polynomial time.
