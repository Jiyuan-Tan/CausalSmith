%%% FIELD rank-statement
Under uniform fixed-count sample-and-assign randomization, fix \(K\geq2\).  For every \(N\geq K\) and every positive count vector \(\mathbf n=(n_a)_{a\in\mathcal A}\) satisfying \(\sum_a n_a\leq N\), with \(n_{\mathrm u}=N-\sum_a n_a\), the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every collection \(F\) of equal-weight \(N\)-point empirical armwise marginals,
\[
Y^{\uparrow}(F)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)}\rho_W(Y);
\]
\item \(H=C^{\mathsf T}WC\in\mathcal R_K\).
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold, for every \(Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)\),
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)
=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{a<b}(-H_{ab})
\left[S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-S_{ab}(Y)\right]\geq0.
\]
If \(H_{ab}>0\) for some distinct \(a,b\), give arms \(a\) and \(b\) the marginal \((1-N^{-1})\delta_0+N^{-1}\delta_1\) and every other arm the degenerate marginal \(\delta_0\).  Moving the unique success of arm \(b\) to a row different from the unique success of arm \(a\) gives, for every \(N\geq K\) and every feasible \(\mathbf n\), a completion \(Y\) satisfying
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)
=-\frac{2H_{ab}}{N(N-1)}<0.
\]
Thus the graph-Laplacian condition is necessary pointwise for every feasible positive fixed-count design, including an optional unassigned remainder.
%%% FIELD rank-proof
Because \(W\succeq0\), for every \(x\in\mathbb R^K\),
\[
x^{\mathsf T}Hx=(Cx)^{\mathsf T}W(Cx)\geq0,
\]
so \(H\succeq0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}causal\text{-}contrast}\) gives \(C\mathbf 1_K=0\), whence
\[
H\mathbf 1_K=C^{\mathsf T}WC\mathbf 1_K=0. \tag{1}
\]
In view of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}rank\text{-}cone}\), (1) shows that, for this Gram matrix, \(H\in\mathcal R_K\) is equivalent to \(H_{ab}\leq0\) for every \(a\ne b\).

We first prove the finite rearrangement fact needed below.  Let \(u_1\leq\cdots\leq u_N\), let \(v_1\leq\cdots\leq v_N\), and let \(\eta\) be a permutation of \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\).  If \(p<q\) and \(\eta(p)>\eta(q)\), replacing these two matched pairs by the order-preserving pairs changes the paired sum by
\[
\begin{aligned}
&u_pv_{\eta(q)}+u_qv_{\eta(p)}
-u_pv_{\eta(p)}-u_qv_{\eta(q)}\\
&\qquad=(u_q-u_p)\{v_{\eta(p)}-v_{\eta(q)}\}\geq0. \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
Repeatedly removing inversions terminates at the identity permutation, so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N u_iv_i\geq\sum_{i=1}^N u_iv_{\eta(i)}. \tag{3}
\]
Every column of a schedule in \(\mathcal S_N(F)\) is a permutation of its nondecreasing quantile column.  For a fixed pair of arms \(a,b\), a common relabelling of rows therefore turns their pairing into the right side of (3), while their means are fixed by \(F_a,F_b\).  Subtracting \(N\bar Y(a)\bar Y(b)\) from both paired sums and dividing by \(N-1>0\) proves
\[
S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}\geq S_{ab}(Y)
\quad(a<b),\qquad Y\in\mathcal S_N(F). \tag{4}
\]

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}randomization\text{-}risk}\), which applies because every \(n_a\geq1\) and \(n_{\mathrm u}\geq0\), gives
\[
\rho_W(Y)=\sum_a\frac{H_{aa}S_{aa}(Y)}{n_a}
-\frac1N\sum_{a,b}H_{ab}S_{ab}(Y). \tag{5}
\]
The empirical marginal \(F_a\) fixes \(S_{aa}(Y)\), so the diagonal part of (5) cancels between any two completions.  Since both \(H\) and \(S(Y)\) are symmetric,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)
&=-\frac1N\sum_{a\ne b}H_{ab}
\left[S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-S_{ab}(Y)\right]\\
&=\frac2N\sum_{a<b}(-H_{ab})
\left[S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-S_{ab}(Y)\right]. \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(H\in\mathcal R_K\), each weight \(-H_{ab}\) and each covariance difference in (6) is nonnegative by (4).  This proves common-rank optimality and the exact regret identity for every feasible count vector.  Notice explicitly that the counts occur only in the diagonal term of (5), which has canceled.

Conversely, suppose \(H\notin\mathcal R_K\).  The positive-semidefinite and zero-row-sum properties established in (1) leave only the off-diagonal sign condition in the cone definition, so there are distinct arms \(a,b\) with \(H_{ab}>0\).  Since \(N\geq K\geq2\), choose two distinct rows.  In arms \(a\) and \(b\), take \(N-1\) zeros and one one; take every other arm to be identically zero.  Common ranking places the two successes in the same row.  Because both nonconstant means equal \(1/N\), its cross-covariance is
\[
S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}
=\frac1{N-1}\left\{\left(1-\frac1N\right)^2
+(N-1)\frac1{N^2}\right\}=\frac1N. \tag{7}
\]
Construct \(Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)\) by moving only the success in arm \(b\) to a row different from the success in arm \(a\).  Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{ab}(Y)
&=\frac1{N-1}\left\{-2\left(1-\frac1N\right)\frac1N
+(N-2)\frac1{N^2}\right\}\\
&=-\frac1{N(N-1)}. \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
Every covariance involving a third, constant arm is zero, and all marginal variances remain fixed.  Substituting (7)--(8) into (6) yields
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)
=-\frac{2H_{ab}}N\left\{\frac1N+\frac1{N(N-1)}\right\}
=-\frac{2H_{ab}}{N(N-1)}<0. \tag{9}
\]
The construction does not involve \(\mathbf n\) or \(n_{\mathrm u}\), while (5) is valid for every feasible such choice.  Hence it disproves universal common-rank optimality for every positive fixed-count vector, proving necessity and the claimed pointwise equivalence.
%%% FIELD loewner-statement
Let \(C\ne0\) satisfy \(C\mathbf 1_K=0\).  Under uniform fixed-count sample-and-assign randomization, for every \(N\geq K\) and every positive count vector \(\mathbf n\) with \(\sum_a n_a\leq N\), the dominance
\[
\Sigma_C\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}\succeq\Sigma_C(Y)
\quad\text{for every }F\text{ and every }Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)
\]
holds if and only if there exist distinct locally bound arms \(a,b\in\mathcal A\) and a locally bound nonzero vector \(v\in\mathbb R^m\) such that
\[
C=v(e_a-e_b)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Equivalently, all columns of \(C\) vanish except columns \(a\) and \(b\), which equal \(v\) and \(-v\).  If \(C\) is not of this form, there are a locally bound direction \(h\in\mathbb R^m\), distinct locally bound arms \(a,b\), and the one-success binary marginals from Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\) for which
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z\!\left\{h^{\mathsf T}\widehat\tau\mid Y^{\uparrow}(F)\right\}
-\operatorname{Var}_Z\!\left(h^{\mathsf T}\widehat\tau\mid Y\right)
=-\frac{2(C^{\mathsf T}h)_a(C^{\mathsf T}h)_b}{N(N-1)}<0.
\]
Thus the classification is valid on the same arbitrary-count design class, including an optional unassigned remainder.
%%% FIELD loewner-proof
Fix \(N\geq K\), a feasible positive count vector, \(F\), and \(Y\in\mathcal S_N(F)\), and write
\[
D(Y)=\Sigma_C\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\Sigma_C(Y).
\]
Because \(D(Y)\) is symmetric, \(D(Y)\succeq0\) if and only if \(h^{\mathsf T}D(Y)h\geq0\) for every \(h\in\mathbb R^m\).  For a fixed \(h\), put \(q=C^{\mathsf T}h\).  Then
\[
h^{\mathsf T}\widehat\tau=q^{\mathsf T}\widehat{\bar Y},
\qquad
h^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_C(Y)h
=\operatorname{Var}_Z(q^{\mathsf T}\widehat{\bar Y}\mid Y), \tag{10}
\]
and the scalar contrast has Gram matrix \(qq^{\mathsf T}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}causal\text{-}contrast}\) gives
\[
q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_K=h^{\mathsf T}C\mathbf 1_K=0. \tag{11}
\]
Applying Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\) with scalar weight one to (10), on this same fixed-count design, shows that universal Loewner dominance is equivalent to
\[
q_aq_b\leq0
\quad\text{for every }a\ne b
\quad\text{and every }q\in L:=\operatorname{range}(C^{\mathsf T}). \tag{12}
\]

We classify the subspaces \(L\subseteq\mathbf 1_K^{\perp}\) satisfying (12).  Any nonzero \(q\in L\) has at most one positive coordinate and at most one negative coordinate.  By (11) it has both signs and the two nonzero coordinates sum to zero.  Thus there are distinct indices \(a,b\) and \(t\ne0\) such that
\[
q=t(e_a-e_b). \tag{13}
\]
All nonzero vectors in \(L\) must lie on the same line of this form.  Indeed, if \(q\) and \(r\) in \(L\) belonged to lines associated with different unordered pairs, their supports would have a union of at least three coordinates.  For \(q+\lambda r\), choose a nonzero real \(\lambda\) outside the finite set of values that cancel one of those coordinates.  Then \(q+\lambda r\in L\) has at least three nonzero coordinates, contradicting (13).  Hence for one fixed pair \(\{a,b\}\),
\[
L\subseteq\operatorname{span}(e_a-e_b). \tag{14}
\]
Since \(C\ne0\), \(L\ne\{0\}\), so equality holds in (14).  Every row of \(C\) lies in this row space; consequently there is a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb R^m\) with
\[
C=v(e_a-e_b)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{15}
\]
This proves the necessity of the two-opposite-columns form with the indices and vector explicitly bound.

Conversely, if (15) holds, then for every \(h\),
\[
C^{\mathsf T}h=(v^{\mathsf T}h)(e_a-e_b),
\]
whose distinct coordinate products are nonpositive.  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\) and (10) therefore give \(h^{\mathsf T}D(Y)h\geq0\) for every \(h\), hence \(D(Y)\succeq0\).

Finally, if \(C\) is not of form (15), the contrapositive of the proved subspace classification provides \(h\) and distinct \(a,b\) such that \(q_aq_b=(C^{\mathsf T}h)_a(C^{\mathsf T}h)_b>0\).  The one-success construction in (7)--(9), applied to the scalar contrast \(q^{\mathsf T}\), gives
\[
h^{\mathsf T}D(Y)h=-\frac{2q_aq_b}{N(N-1)}<0. \tag{16}
\]
Thus common ranking fails Loewner dominance in the exhibited direction for every feasible count vector.  This proves both directions and the explicit finite witness.
%%% FIELD two-arm-statement
Take \(K=2\), label arm \(1\) control and arm \(2\) treatment, and set \(C=(-1,1)\) and \(W=(1)\).  Let \(N\geq4\), \(4\leq n_{\mathrm s}\leq N\), \(2\leq m_{\mathrm t}\leq n_{\mathrm s}-2\), and set \(n_2=m_{\mathrm t}\), \(n_1=n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}\), and \(n_{\mathrm u}=N-n_{\mathrm s}\).  Sequentially drawing a simple random sample of \(n_{\mathrm s}\) units and then drawing \(m_{\mathrm t}\) treated units uniformly from that sample induces exactly the uniform three-cell law \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\).  Moreover,
\[
\rho_W(Y)=\frac1{N-1}\left\{
\frac{N-m_{\mathrm t}}{m_{\mathrm t}}\sigma_{22}^{(N)}(Y)
+\frac{N-(n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t})}{n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}}\sigma_{11}^{(N)}(Y)
+2\sigma_{12}^{(N)}(Y)\right\}. 
\]
After relabelling treatment as \(1\) and control as \(0\), this is Equation (2) of Aronow, Green, and Lee term by term.  Its first two terms are fixed by the empirical marginals, and their Lemma 1 makes the comonotone covariance attained by \(Y^{\uparrow}(F)\) the sharp oracle upper endpoint.  Equivalently, \(H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathcal R_2\), so this endpoint is the \(K=2\) specialization of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\).  This is an exact finite-population oracle statement, not a claim about estimation of the marginal laws.
%%% FIELD two-arm-proof
Let \(S\) denote the sampled set and \(T\subseteq S\) the treated set.  Under the stated sequential design, each \(S\) of size \(n_{\mathrm s}\) has probability \(\binom N{n_{\mathrm s}}^{-1}\), and, conditionally on \(S\), each \(T\) of size \(m_{\mathrm t}\) has probability \(\binom{n_{\mathrm s}}{m_{\mathrm t}}^{-1}\).  A labelled allocation is recovered uniquely from \((S,T)\): units in \(T\) receive arm \(2\), units in \(S\setminus T\) receive arm \(1\), and units outside \(S\) receive \(a_{\mathrm u}\).  Hence every allocation with cell counts \((m_{\mathrm t},n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t},N-n_{\mathrm s})\) has probability
\[
\binom N{n_{\mathrm s}}^{-1}\binom{n_{\mathrm s}}{m_{\mathrm t}}^{-1}
=\left\{\frac{N!}{m_{\mathrm t}!(n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t})!(N-n_{\mathrm s})!}\right\}^{-1}. \tag{17}
\]
The quantity in braces is exactly the cardinality of \(\mathcal Z_{N,\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\).  Equation (17) therefore proves equality of the sequential law and \(\mathbb P_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{sa}}\), not merely equality of their first two moments.

Direct multiplication gives
\[
H=C^{\mathsf T}WC=
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{18}
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}fixed\text{-}count\text{-}randomization\text{-}risk}\), with \(n_2=m_{\mathrm t}\) and \(n_1=n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_W(Y)
&=\frac{S_{22}(Y)}{m_{\mathrm t}}
+\frac{S_{11}(Y)}{n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}}
-\frac1N\{S_{11}(Y)+S_{22}(Y)-2S_{12}(Y)\}\\
&=\left(\frac1{m_{\mathrm t}}-\frac1N\right)S_{22}(Y)
+\left(\frac1{n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}}-\frac1N\right)S_{11}(Y)
+\frac2N S_{12}(Y). \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]
By the definition of the Aronow--Green--Lee normalization,
\(S_{ab}(Y)=N\sigma_{ab}^{(N)}(Y)/(N-1)\).  Substitution into each term of (19) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_W(Y)
=\frac1{N-1}\left\{
\frac{N-m_{\mathrm t}}{m_{\mathrm t}}\sigma_{22}^{(N)}(Y)
+\frac{N-(n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t})}{n_{\mathrm s}-m_{\mathrm t}}\sigma_{11}^{(N)}(Y)
+2\sigma_{12}^{(N)}(Y)\right\}. \tag{20}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the treated variance, control variance, and covariance coefficients agree respectively with Equation (2) of Aronow, Green, and Lee after their treatment/control labels \((1,0)\) are identified with our arms \((2,1)\).

For fixed \(F_1,F_2\), the first two terms of (20) are fixed.  The cited Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}agl\text{-}oracle\text{-}variance\text{-}and\text{-}comonotone\text{-}bound}\), specifically Aronow, Green, and Lee's Lemma 1, states that the cross-covariance is sharply maximized by the comonotone coupling, which here is exactly \(Y^{\uparrow}(F)\).  Its coefficient in (20) is \(2/(N-1)>0\), so the resulting variance is the sharp oracle upper endpoint.  Independently, (18) is positive semidefinite, annihilates \(\mathbf 1_2\), and has nonpositive off-diagonal entries; hence \(H\in\mathcal R_2\) by Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}rank\text{-}cone}\), and Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\) gives the same maximizer.  No step replaces the fixed marginal laws by estimates, so the conclusion is exactly the finite oracle result claimed.
%%% FIELD omnibus-statement
Under uniform fixed-count sample-and-assign randomization, suppose the rows of \(C\) form an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbf 1_K^{\perp}\) and \(W=I_{K-1}\).  Then
\[
H=C^{\mathsf T}C=I_K-K^{-1}\mathbf 1_K\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T},
\qquad H_{aa}=1-K^{-1},\qquad H_{ab}=-K^{-1}\ (a\ne b),
\]
so \(H\in\mathcal R_K\).  Consequently, for every \(N\geq K\), every feasible positive count vector \(\mathbf n\) with optional unassigned remainder, and every empirical marginal collection \(F\), common ranks maximize
\(\rho_{I_{K-1}}(Y)=\operatorname{tr}\{\Sigma_C(Y)\}\), the sum of the randomization variances of the complete orthonormal contrast system.  If \(K>2\), this scalar omnibus endpoint does not yield universal Loewner dominance.
%%% FIELD omnibus-proof
Because the rows of \(C\) are an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbf 1_K^{\perp}\) and \(W=I_{K-1}\), the matrix \(H=C^{\mathsf T}C\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\mathbf 1_K^{\perp}\).  The orthogonal projector onto this subspace is uniquely
\[
P=I_K-\frac1K\mathbf 1_K\mathbf 1_K^{\mathsf T}, \tag{21}
\]
since \(P\mathbf 1_K=0\), \(Px=x\) whenever \(x^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_K=0\), and \(P=P^{\mathsf T}\).  Therefore
\[
H_{aa}=1-\frac1K,
\qquad H_{ab}=-\frac1K\quad(a\ne b). \tag{22}
\]
Equations (21)--(22) show that \(H\succeq0\), \(H\mathbf 1_K=0\), and every off-diagonal entry is nonpositive.  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}rank\text{-}cone}\) therefore gives \(H\in\mathcal R_K\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\), on the arbitrary-count uniform sample-and-assign class, now proves for every feasible \(\mathbf n\) that \(Y^{\uparrow}(F)\) maximizes
\[
\rho_{I_{K-1}}(Y)=\operatorname{tr}\{I_{K-1}\Sigma_C(Y)\}
=\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}\operatorname{Var}_Z(\widehat\tau_r\mid Y). \tag{23}
\]
If \(K>2\), then \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=K-1>1\).  A matrix with only two nonzero opposite columns has rank at most one, so \(C\) cannot have the form classified by Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}loewner\text{-}boundary}\).  That theorem rules out universal Loewner dominance while leaving the scalar maximum (23) intact.
%%% FIELD graph-audit-proof
The two displayed rows of \(C\) each sum to zero, as required by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}causal\text{-}contrast}\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
H=C^{\mathsf T}C=
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is positive semidefinite because it is a Gram matrix, it satisfies \(H\mathbf 1_3=0\), and its off-diagonal entries are \(-1,0,-1\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}rank\text{-}cone}\) therefore gives \(H\in\mathcal R_3\).

Put \(q=(0,0,0,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  Its mean is \(1/2\), and hence
\[
\frac1{6-1}\sum_{i=1}^6(q_i-1/2)^2
=\frac15\,6\left(\frac14\right)=\frac3{10}. \tag{24}
\]
Every column of \(Y^{\uparrow}(F)\) equals \(q\), so \(S_{ab}\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}=3/10\) for all \(a,b\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}complete\text{-}randomization\text{-}risk}\), with \(N=6\), \(n=2\), and \(H\mathbf 1_3=0\), gives
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}
=\frac12(1+2+1)\frac3{10}
-\frac16\frac3{10}\mathbf 1_3^{\mathsf T}H\mathbf 1_3
=\frac35. \tag{25}
\]

Let \(\widetilde q=\mathbf 1_6-q\).  Since \(\widetilde q_i-1/2=-(q_i-1/2)\),
\[
\frac15\sum_{i=1}^6(q_i-1/2)(\widetilde q_i-1/2)=-\frac3{10}. \tag{26}
\]
Thus the schedule \(Y=(q,\widetilde q,q)\) has \(S_{12}=S_{23}=-3/10\), \(S_{13}=3/10\), and all three diagonal variances equal to \(3/10\).  Every column still contains three zeros and three ones, so \(Y\in\mathcal S_6(F)\).  Its covariance contraction is
\[
\sum_{a,b=1}^3H_{ab}S_{ab}(Y)
=4\frac3{10}+2\left\{(-1)\left(-\frac3{10}\right)+(-1)\left(-\frac3{10}\right)\right\}
=\frac{12}{5}. \tag{27}
\]
The risk identity therefore yields
\[
\rho_W(Y)=\frac35-\frac16\frac{12}{5}=\frac15. \tag{28}
\]
Finally, the exact regret formula in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}rank\text{-}cone\text{-}iff}\) gives
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)
=\frac13\left\{\frac3{10}-\left(-\frac3{10}\right)
+\frac3{10}-\left(-\frac3{10}\right)\right\}=\frac25, \tag{29}
\]
which agrees with (25)--(28) and audits the sign, normalization, and factor of two.
%%% FIELD nonlap-audit-proof
Writing \(c=(1,1,-2)^{\mathsf T}\), direct multiplication gives \(H=cc^{\mathsf T}\), namely the displayed matrix.  Thus \(H\succeq0\) and \(H\mathbf 1_3=0\), but \(H_{12}=1>0\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}rank\text{-}cone}\) therefore gives \(H\notin\mathcal R_3\).

Let \(q=(0,0,0,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  Equation (24) gives its finite-population variance \(3/10\).  Under the common-rank schedule \((q,q,0)\),
\[
S_{11}=S_{22}=S_{12}=\frac3{10},
\]
and all covariances involving arm \(3\) vanish.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}complete\text{-}randomization\text{-}risk}\), with \(N=6\) and \(n=2\), gives
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}
=\frac12\left(\frac3{10}+\frac3{10}\right)
-\frac16\left(\frac3{10}+\frac3{10}+2\frac3{10}\right)
=\frac1{10}. \tag{30}
\]
Reverse only the second column, using \(\widetilde q=\mathbf 1_6-q\).  The resulting schedule remains in \(\mathcal S_6(F)\), has \(S_{11}=S_{22}=3/10\) and \(S_{12}=-3/10\) by (26), and satisfies
\[
\rho_W(Y)
=\frac3{10}-\frac16\left(\frac3{10}+\frac3{10}-2\frac3{10}\right)
=\frac3{10}. \tag{31}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\rho_W\{Y^{\uparrow}(F)\}-\rho_W(Y)
=\frac13(-1)\left\{\frac3{10}-\left(-\frac3{10}\right)\right\}
=-\frac15<0. \tag{32}
\]
This verifies feasibility, every claimed covariance, and the exact finite loss, and it supplies the advertised non-Laplacian witness.
