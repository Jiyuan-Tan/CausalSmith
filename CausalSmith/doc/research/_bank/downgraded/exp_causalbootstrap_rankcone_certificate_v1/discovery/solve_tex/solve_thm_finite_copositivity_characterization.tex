Because \(\Omega\in\mathfrak K^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}\), the balanced domain in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}admissible\text{-}balanced\text{-}kernel}\) entails \(K\mid N\), \(n=N/K\), and the existence of \(p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{bal}}_{N,K}\) such that, on writing
\[
 A_i^a(z)=K\mathbf 1(z_i=a)-1,
\]
one has
\[
 \Omega_{abij}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{N,K}}p(z)A_i^a(z)A_j^b(z). \tag{1}
\]
Every \(z\in\mathcal Z_{N,K}\) assigns exactly \(n\) units to each arm.  Hence, for every \(a\in\mathcal A\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N A_i^a(z)=Kn-N=0. \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2), with finite sums interchanged, give
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^N\Omega_{abij}
 =\sum_zp(z)\left\{\sum_iA_i^a(z)\right\}A_j^b(z)=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^N\Omega_{abij}=0. \tag{3}
\]
Moreover, commutativity of multiplication in (1) gives
\[
 \Omega_{abij}=\Omega_{baji}. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero-sum identities and covariance symmetry used below follow directly from the exact balanced-law representation, rather than from any additional permutation symmetry.  By Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}unit\text{-}relabeling\text{-}equivalence}\), equations (3)--(4) remain valid with \(\Omega\) replaced by \(\Omega^\pi\).

Fix \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_N\) and \(\sigma\in\mathfrak S_N^K\).  The adjacent increments of an arbitrary \(x^a\in\mathcal M_N\) are
\[
 \alpha_{ar}=x^a_{r+1}-x^a_r\geq0,
 \qquad 1\leq r<N,
\]
and the cut decomposition from Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}kernel\text{-}cut\text{-}handle}\) is the identity
\[
 x^a=x_1^a\mathbf 1_N+\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}\alpha_{ar}d_r. \tag{5}
\]
Conversely, any \(\alpha=(\alpha_{ar})_{(a,r)\in J}\in\mathbb R_+^J\), together with arbitrary constants \(x_1^a\), defines columns in \(\mathcal M_N\) through (5).  Substitute (5) in both losses in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}balanced\text{-}kernel\text{-}loss}\).  Every term containing at least one constant column is zero by (3): if the other factor depends on \(j\), sum first over \(i\), and if it depends on \(i\), sum first over \(j\).  The remaining increment terms give exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathscr L_{H,\Omega^\pi}(x,\mathrm{id})
 -\mathscr L_{H,\Omega^\pi}(x,\sigma)\\
 &\quad=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal A}\sum_{r,s=1}^{N-1}
 \alpha_{ar}\alpha_{bs}\frac{H_{ab}}{N^2}
 \sum_{i,j=1}^N\Omega_{ab,\pi(i),\pi(j)}
 \left\{(d_r)_i(d_s)_j-(d_r)_{\sigma_a(i)}(d_s)_{\sigma_b(j)}\right\}\\
 &\quad=\alpha^{\mathsf T}B^{\pi,\sigma}\alpha. \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(H\) is symmetric and (4) holds, interchanging \((a,r,i)\) with \((b,s,j)\) in the defining sum shows
\[
 B^{\pi,\sigma}_{(a,r),(b,s)}=B^{\pi,\sigma}_{(b,s),(a,r)}, \tag{7}
\]
so the displayed matrix is indeed symmetric.  Because (5) realizes every vector in \(\mathbb R_+^J\), equation (6) proves
\[
 \mathscr L_{H,\Omega^\pi}(x,\mathrm{id})
 \geq\mathscr L_{H,\Omega^\pi}(x,\sigma)
 \text{ for every }x\in\mathcal M_N^K
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B^{\pi,\sigma}\text{ is copositive}. \tag{8}
\]
Taking the conjunction of (8) over the finite set \(\mathfrak S_N^K\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}identity\text{-}optimal\text{-}kernel\text{-}cone}\) yields
\[
 \Omega^\pi\in\mathcal C_{N,K}(H)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B^{\pi,\sigma}\text{ is copositive for every }\sigma\in\mathfrak S_N^K. \tag{9}
\]
Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}unit\text{-}relabeling\text{-}equivalence}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}admissible\text{-}rank\text{-}kernel\text{-}domain}\) say that the orbit class is admissibly rank-preserving precisely when one of its finitely many relabellings lies in this cone.  Therefore (9), followed by the disjunction over \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_N\), proves the second displayed equivalence in the theorem.

We next prove the asserted support characterization for an arbitrary real symmetric matrix \(B\) indexed by \(J\).  Suppose first that there are a nonempty \(I\subseteq J\), a \(\lambda<0\), and a \(v\in\mathbb R_{++}^I\) satisfying \(B[I]v=\lambda v\).  Extending \(v\) by zero outside \(I\) produces \(\widetilde v\in\mathbb R_+^J\) and
\[
 \widetilde v^{\mathsf T}B\widetilde v
 =v^{\mathsf T}B[I]v
 =\lambda\lVert v\rVert_2^2<0. \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(B\) is not copositive.

Conversely, suppose that \(B\) is not copositive.  The set
\[
 \mathcal D=\{u\in\mathbb R_+^J:\lVert u\rVert_2=1\}
\]
is nonempty and compact.  Continuity of \(u\mapsto u^{\mathsf T}Bu\) therefore gives a minimizer \(u^*\in\mathcal D\).  Normalizing any nonnegative vector at which the quadratic form is negative shows that
\[
 \lambda:=(u^*)^{\mathsf T}Bu^*<0. \tag{11}
\]
Let \(I=\{q\in J:u_q^*>0\}\), which is nonempty.  For any \(w\in\mathbb R^I\) with \(w^{\mathsf T}u_I^*=0\), strict positivity of every coordinate of \(u_I^*\) ensures that both signs of \(u_I^*+tw\) remain positive for all sufficiently small \(t\).  Applying first-order minimality to the normalized curve
\[
 t\longmapsto\frac{u_I^*+tw}{\lVert u_I^*+tw\rVert_2}
\]
at \(t=0\), and using \(w^{\mathsf T}u_I^*=0\), gives
\[
 0=2w^{\mathsf T}B[I]u_I^*. \tag{12}
\]
Equation (12) for every vector orthogonal to \(u_I^*\) implies that \(B[I]u_I^*\) is a scalar multiple of \(u_I^*\).  Taking its inner product with the unit vector \(u_I^*\), and using (11) together with the fact that coordinates outside \(I\) vanish, identifies that scalar:
\[
 B[I]u_I^*=\lambda u_I^*,
 \qquad \lambda<0,
 \qquad u_I^*\in\mathbb R_{++}^I. \tag{13}
\]
Equations (10) and (13) prove the finite support characterization in both directions.

Finally suppose that every entry of \(H\) and \(\Omega\) is supplied as an exact real-algebraic number.  There are finitely many triples \((\pi,\sigma,I)\), because \(\mathfrak S_N\), \(\mathfrak S_N^K\), and the nonempty subsets of \(J\) are finite.  For each triple, every entry of \(B^{\pi,\sigma}[I]\) is real algebraic by its displayed finite-sum formula.  Exact algebraic-number arithmetic computes its characteristic polynomial.  Square-free factorization, a Sturm remainder sequence, and interval bisection using exact algebraic sign tests isolate all of its distinct real roots and decide which are negative; these procedures terminate because the characteristic polynomial has finite degree, each Euclidean remainder has strictly smaller degree, and each distinct algebraic root has a disjoint rational isolating interval after finitely many bisections.

For each isolated negative eigenvalue \(\lambda\), homogeneity of the kernel equations gives the exact equivalence
\[
 \ker\{B[I]-\lambda I_I\}\cap\mathbb R_{++}^I\neq\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists v:\ (B[I]-\lambda I_I)v=0,
 \quad v_q\geq1\ \text{for every }q\in I. \tag{14}
\]
Indeed, a vector on the left can be multiplied by the reciprocal of its smallest coordinate, while every vector on the right is strictly positive.  The right-hand side of (14) is a finite system of linear equalities and weak inequalities over the ordered field of real algebraic numbers.  Replace each equality by two weak inequalities and eliminate variables successively: for a selected variable, every lower bound is paired with every upper bound, and bounds of only one sign impose no cross-condition.  This Fourier--Motzkin calculation uses only exact field operations and order tests and preserves feasibility in both directions at each elimination.  It terminates after finitely many variables.  Back-substitution constructs an algebraic feasible vector when one exists.  If none exists, retaining the nonnegative combinations used at each elimination yields a finite exact infeasibility certificate ending in a false constant inequality.  Thus (14) is exactly decidable.

If a test in (14) is feasible, extend its \(v\) by zero to \(\alpha\in\mathbb R_+^J\) and define
\[
 x_i^a=\sum_{r<i}\alpha_{ar},
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq N. \tag{15}
\]
Every column in (15) is ordered and has at most \(N\) levels.  Equations (6), (13), and (15) give the strict, exactly checkable loss comparison
\[
 \mathscr L_{H,\Omega^\pi}(x,\mathrm{id})
 -\mathscr L_{H,\Omega^\pi}(x,\sigma)
 =\alpha^{\mathsf T}B^{\pi,\sigma}\alpha
 =\lambda\lVert v\rVert_2^2<0. \tag{16}
\]
Thus a feasible eigenspace test returns the stated strict cut witness.  If every negative-eigenspace test is infeasible for a fixed \(\pi\), the support characterization proves every \(B^{\pi,\sigma}\) copositive, and the finite collection of elimination traces certifies that \(\pi\) works.  If no \(\pi\) works, finiteness and (9) ensure that, for every \(\pi\), some \(\sigma\) and some feasible triple \((I,\lambda,v)\) produce (16).  This is the claimed exact recognition algorithm and its yes/no certificates.  Its exhaustive enumeration is finite but supplies neither a polynomial running-time bound nor any algorithm for unrestricted, non-algebraically-presented real inputs.
