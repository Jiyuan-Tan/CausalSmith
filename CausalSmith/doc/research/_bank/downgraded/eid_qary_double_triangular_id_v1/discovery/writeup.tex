\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}qary\_\allowbreak{}double\_\allowbreak{}triangular\_\allowbreak{}id}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The headline result is a terminating exact-real algorithm \(A_q\) for \(q\ge3\) latent states and heterogeneous finite responses with \(m_j\ge q\).  A product of local Gram determinants \(c_{a,k,u}=\det(L_{a,k,u}^{\mathsf T}L_{a,k,u})\) certifies full column rank of two rectangular triangular views, exact three-view recovery supplies \(q^K\) anonymous components, and the observable equality lattice aligns them with \(K\) categorical coordinates and a latent MEC.  The two-view rank also forces every finite latent measurement representation to have at least \(q^K\) joint states and gives \(K_{\min}(P_X)=K\).  In the equal-\(q\) case the original determinant product remains an exact if-and-only-if frontier.  That converse cannot extend to all larger alphabets: a strictly positive \(q=3\) witness has a singular earlier local table but a full-rank rectangular view.  The dense ternary no-pure-child witness remains an exact regression fixture, and the sole open rung is finite-sample multiplicity calibration for a \(\texttt{cdmTools}\)-style diagnostic.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Binary double triangularity uses scalar contrasts, while categorical and heterogeneous responses require local column-rank certificates.  Generic Kruskal theory neither identifies the failing predecessor context nor aligns \(q^K\) anonymous mixture labels with \(K\) coordinates and a latent MEC.  A further subtlety is that local-rank necessity fails when a later observed alphabet is larger than \(q\), so an honest heterogeneous theorem must distinguish a sufficient Gram certificate from the exact equal-alphabet frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} The project gives an explicit rectangular certificate \(c_{a,k,u}=\det(L_{a,k,u}^{\mathsf T}L_{a,k,u})=\sum_R\det(L_{a,k,u}[R,:])^2\), an embedded positive perturbation proving open-dense/full-measure nonvanishing, and observable \(q^K\)-row minors that support exact simultaneous diagonalization without pure children or positive measurement-cell assumptions.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note delivers the finite exact algorithm \(A_q\), the rectangular certificate implication and sharp equal-\(q\) determinant corollary, a counterexample to the impossible heterogeneous converse, an arbitrary finite-latent-state lower bound, intrinsic coordinate dimension, exact same-\(K\) quotient identification, coordinate alignment, and the dense \(q=3\) no-pure-child regression fixture.

\section{Related work}

Lee and Gu (2026) study binary latent coordinates with arbitrary observed variables on nondegenerate separable metric spaces under their standing BLCM assumptions, including faithfulness.  The reduced-assumption binary-response rank class used here is a strengthening proved directly; the published Theorem 1 transfers only on its faithful intersection.  The binary-response identification comparator is a subclass of their Theorem 2 domain.  For \(q\ge3\), the present result is a categorical-latent analogue with an additional local Gram-rank certificate, not an inclusion transfer.  Chen et al. (2025) obtain mixed-type recovery through completeness and two sufficient measurements.  Their unchanged published class and the full heterogeneous finite-observed certified class are incomparable: the first noncontainment is witnessed by the equal-alphabet dense ternary member, whose observed and latent supports both have size three and therefore fail Chen et al.'s strict larger-support requirement, while the reverse noncontainment is witnessed by Chen-class members with continuous measurements, which cannot belong to the finite-observed certified class.  The support-cardinality argument is confined to the equal-alphabet witness; heterogeneous certified members may overlap Chen's class without altering either noncontainment.  Allman, Matias, and Rhodes (2009) give generic latent-class uniqueness under Kruskal inequalities; here certified full-rank row minors and third-view separation yield exact uniqueness, after which the equality lattice supplies the missing coordinate product, measurement adjacency, and latent MEC.  Gu (2026) gives constructive unfolding queries under pure-item conditions, and Kivva et al. (2021) recover structure from a mixture oracle; \(A_q\) instead enumerates exact row minors, coordinate partitions, nonnegative normalized measurement tables, and latent DAGs directly from \(P_X\).  Gu and Xu (2021) and Gu et al. (2018) motivate the software consumer and its still-open calibrated empirical layer.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:measurement]\label{ass:measurement}
The graph \(G\) has no edge between observed vertices and no edge from an observed vertex to a latent vertex.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (measurement-model graph, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:markov]\label{ass:markov}
The law factorizes as \(P(H,X)=\prod_{k=1}^KP(H_k\mid H_{\operatorname{pa}_{\Lambda}(k)})\prod_{j=1}^JP(X_j\mid H_{\operatorname{pa}_{\Gamma}(j)})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (causal Markov factorization, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:faithfulness]\label{ass:faithfulness}
The conditional-independence relations of \(P(H,X)\) equal the d-separation relations of \(G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (causal faithfulness, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-support]\label{ass:positive-support}
For every \(h\in\mathcal Q^K\), \(\pi_h>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (positive latent support, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-tables]\label{ass:positive-tables}
Every entry of every latent conditional table and every measurement conditional table compatible with \(\Lambda\) and \(\Gamma\) is strictly positive.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (interior categorical parameterization, \cite{AllmanMatiasRhodes2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:measurement-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:measurement-nondegeneracy}
For every observed coordinate \(j\), distinct configurations of \(H_{\operatorname{pa}_{\Gamma}(j)}\) induce distinct conditional distributions of \(X_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (categorical measurement nondegeneracy, \cite{KivvaRajendranRavikumarAragam2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:double-triangular]\label{ass:double-triangular}
There exist disjoint row sets \(S_1,S_2\) and orders \(\rho_1,\rho_2\) such that \(\Gamma_{s(a,k),\rho_a(k)}=1\) and \(\Gamma_{s(a,k),\rho_a(\ell)}=0\) for every \(a\in\{1,2\}\), \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), and \(\ell>k\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a categorical graph condition: it permits each selected item to depend on its diagonal latent state and arbitrary earlier categorical states, so it is not inherited from a binary scalar-contrast theorem. Polytomous skill batteries with two ordered prerequisite-sensitive item blocks satisfy it even when no block is pure; the eight-row ternary graph \(\Gamma^{\star}\) is an explicit dense example.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:third-view-coverage]\label{ass:third-view-coverage}
Every latent coordinate has at least one observed child indexed by \(S_3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (third-view coverage, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:subset]\label{ass:subset}
For distinct \(k,\ell\), neither \(\operatorname{ch}_X(H_k)\) nor \(\operatorname{ch}_X(H_\ell)\) contains the other.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (subset condition, \cite{KivvaRajendranRavikumarAragam2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:certificate]\label{ass:certificate}
The double-triangular certificate satisfies \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition consists of finitely many strict context-specific categorical contrasts. On every fixed double-triangular graph, the explicit diagonal-dominant family has local determinants \(\eta_{a,k,u}^{q-1}\), so the nonvanishing locus is a nonempty relatively open dense, full-measure subset of the positive categorical-table parameter space. Such contrasts can be checked in polytomous cognitive-diagnosis designs without pure children.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-measurement]\label{ass:binary-measurement}
The graph \(G_{\mathrm{bin}}\) has no edge between binary observed vertices and no edge from a binary observed vertex to a binary latent vertex.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (binary measurement-model graph, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-markov]\label{ass:binary-markov}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (binary causal Markov factorization, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-positive-support]\label{ass:binary-positive-support}
For every \(h\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{bin}}^K\), \((\pi_{\mathrm{bin}})_h>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (binary positive latent support, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:binary-nondegeneracy}
For every binary observed coordinate \(j\), distinct configurations of its latent parents induce distinct conditional distributions of \((X_{\mathrm{bin}})_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Lee--Gu binary measurement nondegeneracy, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-double-triangular]\label{ass:binary-double-triangular}
There exist disjoint ordered row sets \((S_1)_{\mathrm{bin}},(S_2)_{\mathrm{bin}}\) and orders \(\rho_{\mathrm{bin}}\) such that \((\Gamma_{\mathrm{bin}})_{s_{\mathrm{bin}}(a,k),(\rho_{\mathrm{bin}})_a(k)}=1\) and \((\Gamma_{\mathrm{bin}})_{s_{\mathrm{bin}}(a,k),(\rho_{\mathrm{bin}})_a(\ell)}=0\) for every \(a\in\{1,2\}\), \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), and \(\ell>k\).

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Lee--Gu binary third-view coverage, \cite{LeeGu2026DTBLCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-subset]\label{ass:binary-subset}
For distinct \(k,\ell\), neither \(\operatorname{ch}_{X_{\mathrm{bin}}}((H_{\mathrm{bin}})_k)\) nor \(\operatorname{ch}_{X_{\mathrm{bin}}}((H_{\mathrm{bin}})_\ell)\) contains the other.

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:local-factorization]\label{thm:local-factorization}
For each \(a\in\{1,2\}\), let \(L_{a,k,u}\in\mathbb R^{m_{s(a,k)}\times q}\) be the rectangular local tables and let \(M_a\in\mathbb R^{n_a\times q^K}\), where \(n_a=\prod_{k=1}^Km_{s(a,k)}\).  Then
\[
 c_{a,k,u}=\det(L_{a,k,u}^{\mathsf T}L_{a,k,u})
 =\sum_{\substack{R\subseteq\mathcal X_{s(a,k)}\\|R|=q}}
 \det(L_{a,k,u}[R,:])^2,
\]
and \(\Delta_a\neq0\) implies \(\operatorname{rank}(M_a)=q^K\).  If every selected alphabet has size \(q\), then \(B_{a,k,u}=L_{a,k,u}\) and the sharper identity
\[
 \det(M_a)=\varepsilon_{q,K}
 \prod_{k=1}^K\prod_{u\in\mathcal Q^{k-1}}
 \det(B_{a,k,u})^{q^{K-k}}
 =\varepsilon_{q,K}\prod_{k,u}d_{a,k,u}^{q^{K-k}}
\]
holds; in that case \(\Delta_a=\det(M_a)^2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(a\in\{1,2\}\), suppress \(a\), and relabel the latent coordinates in the order \(\rho_a\).  Put \(X_k=X_{s(a,k)}\), \(H_k=H_{\rho_a(k)}\), and \(r_k=m_{s(a,k)}\).  Assumption~\textnormal{ass:double-triangular} says that the latent parents of \(X_k\) are contained in \(H_{1:k}\), and Assumption~\textnormal{ass:markov} gives conditional independence of the observed vertices given \(H\).  Therefore, for \(1\le m\le K\),
\[
 P(X_{1:m}=x_{1:m}\mid H=h)
 =\prod_{k=1}^m
 P(X_k=x_k\mid H_{1:k}=h_{1:k})
 =\prod_{k=1}^mL_{k,h_{1:k-1}}(x_k,h_k).
\tag{1}
\]
The right side does not depend on \(h_{m+1:K}\).

We first verify the Gram formula without importing a rank theorem.  For any \(L\in\mathbb R^{r\times q}\), expand \(\det(AB)\) by the Leibniz formula with \(A=L^{\mathsf T}\) and \(B=L\).  Grouping the expanded terms by the \(q\) intermediate row indices, every tuple with a repeated index cancels because the determinant is alternating.  Each remaining \(q\)-element set \(R\) contributes
\[
 \det(L^{\mathsf T}[:,R])\det(L[R,:])
 =\det(L[R,:])^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \det(L^{\mathsf T}L)=
 \sum_{\substack{R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\\|R|=q}}
 \det(L[R,:])^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, for every \(v\in\mathbb R^q\),
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}L^{\mathsf T}Lv=\|Lv\|_2^2.
\]
Thus \(L^{\mathsf T}L\) is nonsingular exactly when \(\ker L=\{0\}\), and hence \(c_{k,u}\neq0\) exactly when \(L_{k,u}\) has column rank \(q\).  In particular \(c_{k,u}\ge0\).

Let \(M^{(m)}\) be the \(\prod_{k=1}^mr_k\)-by-\(q^m\) matrix whose \((x_{1:m},h_{1:m})\) entry is the product in (1).  In the induced lexicographic orders, insertion of the intermediate pair \((h_{1:m-1},x_m)\) gives
\[
 M^{(m)}=(M^{(m-1)}\otimes I_{r_m})
 \operatorname{blockdiag}_{u\in\mathcal Q^{m-1}}L_{m,u},
 \qquad M^{(1)}=L_{1,\varnothing},
\tag{3}
\]
up to the fixed row convention.  Indeed, the \((x_{1:m},h_{1:m})\) entry of the right side is
\[
 \sum_{u,y}M^{(m-1)}(x_{1:m-1},u)
 \mathbf1\{u=h_{1:m-1}\}\mathbf1\{x_m=y\}
 L_{m,u}(y,h_m),
\]
which is (1).

Suppose every \(c_{k,u}\neq0\).  By (2), every block \(L_{m,u}\) has trivial kernel, so the block-diagonal factor in (3) has trivial kernel.  Inductively \(M^{(m-1)}\) has trivial kernel; hence \(M^{(m-1)}\otimes I_{r_m}\) also has trivial kernel, since a vector split into \(r_m\) coordinate slices is killed only when every slice is in \(\ker M^{(m-1)}\).  The composition in (3) is therefore injective.  At \(m=K\), this proves
\[
 \Delta_a\neq0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{rank}(M_a)=q^K,
\]
because every exponent in the defining finite product is positive.

Now assume \(r_k=q\) for every selected row.  All matrices in (3) are square.  Determinant multiplicativity, the block-diagonal determinant formula, and direct diagonalization of \(C\otimes I_q\) after triangularizing \(C\) give
\[
 \det(M^{(m)})=\epsilon_m\det(M^{(m-1)})^q
 \prod_{u\in\mathcal Q^{m-1}}\det(B_{m,u}),
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\epsilon_m\in\{-1,1\}\) records only the fixed row ordering.  Iterating (4), a determinant introduced at level \(k\) is raised to the \(q\)-th power at each later level, and hence
\[
 \det(M_a)=\varepsilon_{q,K}
 \prod_{k=1}^K\prod_{u\in\mathcal Q^{k-1}}
 d_{a,k,u}^{q^{K-k}}.
\]
Since \(c_{a,k,u}=d_{a,k,u}^2\) in this square case, squaring the last display gives \(\Delta_a=\det(M_a)^2\).  All products are finite, and no division or limiting step has been used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The recursive factorization exposes every rectangular local rank condition and retains the exact determinant exponents in the equal-alphabet case. \textit{Gap.} The binary recursion and categorical obstruction do not provide the all-\(q\), all-context Gram formula or its rectangular sufficiency proof. \textit{Consumer.} A diagnostic can return the actual nonsingular \(q\)-row minor for every certified context.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certificate-frontier]\label{thm:certificate-frontier}
For heterogeneous finite observed alphabets \(m_j\ge q\), \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0\) implies \(\operatorname{rank}(M_1)=\operatorname{rank}(M_2)=q^K\).  If every selected alphabet has size \(q\), the implication is an equivalence, and a vanishing local determinant \(d_{a,k,u}\) forces view \(a\) to be rank deficient.  For \(q=3\), a strictly positive square local table can have pairwise distinct conditional columns and zero determinant.  With a larger later observed alphabet, a view can instead have full column rank while an earlier \(c_{a,k,u}\) vanishes, so the heterogeneous converse is false.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\textnormal{def:certificate}, \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0\) is equivalent to \(c_{a,k,u}\neq0\) for both \(a\in\{1,2\}\) and every \(k,u\), because all exponents \(q^{K-k}\) are positive integers.  The rectangular implication in Theorem~\textnormal{thm:local-factorization} therefore yields
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{rank}(M_1)=\operatorname{rank}(M_2)=q^K.
\tag{5}
\]

If every selected \(m_j=q\), Theorem~\textnormal{thm:local-factorization} gives \(\Delta_a=\det(M_a)^2\).  A square \(q^K\)-by-\(q^K\) matrix has rank \(q^K\) exactly when its determinant is nonzero: if the determinant is nonzero its columns have no nontrivial linear relation, and if the columns are independent the associated linear map is bijective and its determinant cannot vanish.  Hence in the equal-alphabet case
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{rank}(M_1)=\operatorname{rank}(M_2)=q^K.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(d_{a,k,u}=0\), the determinant product for that view vanishes, so (6) makes view \(a\) rank deficient.

The separation condition is weaker than the square certificate already at \(q=3\).  Let
\[
 b=\begin{pmatrix}1/3\\1/3\\1/3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 v=\begin{pmatrix}\delta\\-\delta\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}b-v&b&b+v\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<\delta<1/3.
\]
The columns are pairwise distinct strictly positive probability vectors, but the first plus the third is twice the second.  Thus \(d=\det(B)=0\) and \(c=d^2=0\).

Finally, Proposition~\textnormal{prop:rectangular-local-necessity-fails} supplies strictly positive \(q=3\) local tables with one vanishing \(c_{a,k,u}\) and a full-column-rank global view.  That explicit witness disproves the requested heterogeneous converse, while (5) and (6) establish the maximal valid frontier.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem states the strongest true frontier and certifies the obstruction to the requested heterogeneous equivalence. \textit{Gap.} Neither global Kruskal bounds nor ordinary column separation reveal that high-cardinality later responses can compensate for local rank failure. \textit{Consumer.} The checker reports sufficiency for heterogeneous banks and exact necessity only for selected equal-\(q\) blocks.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certificate-genericity]\label{thm:certificate-genericity}
For every fixed categorical double-triangular graph \(\Gamma\), latent DAG \(\Lambda\), and observed alphabet vector with \(m_j\ge q\), \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\) is a rational function on \(\Theta_{\Gamma,\Lambda}^{+}\) with strictly positive denominators and a nonzero polynomial numerator \(N_{\mathrm{DT}}\).  In the positive perturbation family, every \(c_{a,k,u}>0\); more precisely, for the first \(q\) observed states and \(\beta_{a,k,u}=\sum_{x=0}^{q-1}b_{a,k,u}(x)>0\),
\[
 \det(L_{a,k,u}[0{:}q-1,:])
 =\beta_{a,k,u}\eta_{a,k,u}^{q-1}.
\]
When \(m_{s(a,k)}=q\), this reduces to \(d_{a,k,u}=\eta_{a,k,u}^{q-1}\) and \(c_{a,k,u}=\eta_{a,k,u}^{2(q-1)}\).  Consequently the nonvanishing locus of \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\) is nonempty, relatively open and dense, and its complement has Lebesgue measure zero in the affine product of simplex interiors.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use free coordinates for the latent and measurement conditional probability tables in \(\Theta_{\Gamma,\Lambda}^{+}\).  Assumption~\textnormal{ass:markov} makes every joint cell probability a polynomial in these coordinates.  Every local-table entry is
\[
 L_{a,k,u}(x,r)
 =\frac{P(X_{s(a,k)}=x,
 H_{\rho_a(1:k)}=(u,r))}
 {P(H_{\rho_a(1:k)}=(u,r))}.
\tag{7}
\]
The numerator and denominator are polynomial after marginalization.  The denominator is strictly positive throughout \(\Theta_{\Gamma,\Lambda}^{+}\), because every latent conditional-table entry is positive, so every complete latent configuration, and therefore every displayed marginal event, has positive mass.  The Gram determinant and the finite products in Definition~\textnormal{def:certificate} therefore make \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\) rational with a strictly positive common denominator.  Let \(N_{\mathrm{DT}}\) be the numerator after clearing it.

We show that this numerator is not the zero polynomial by evaluating the corrected positive family.  For \(j=s(a,k)\), the construction gives
\[
 L=b\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+\eta E_jQ_q.
\]
Every column sums to one because \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}b=1\) and \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}E_jQ_q=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Q_q=0\).  All entries are positive when \(\eta>0\) is sufficiently small, since \(b\) has finitely many strictly positive entries.  Dependence on \(u\) occurs only through the earlier parents, so the table is graph-compatible.  Independent uniform latent coordinates and conditional sampling from these tables give a point of \(\Theta_{\Gamma,\Lambda}^{+}\).

Restrict \(L\) to the first \(q\) observed states.  The resulting square matrix is
\[
 D=b_{0:q-1}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+\eta Q_q.
\]
On the zero-sum subspace \(V_0=\{z:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}z=0\}\), \(Dz=\eta z\).  Also
\[
 \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}D
 =\left(\sum_{x=0}^{q-1}b(x)\right)\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}
 =\beta\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},
\]
so the induced map on the one-dimensional quotient \(\mathbb R^q/V_0\) has eigenvalue \(\beta>0\).  Choosing a basis adapted to \(V_0\) yields
\[
 \det(D)=\beta\eta^{q-1}>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The Gram expansion proved in Theorem~\textnormal{thm:local-factorization} now gives
\[
 c_{a,k,u}
 =\sum_{|R|=q}\det(L_{a,k,u}[R,:])^2
 \ge \beta_{a,k,u}^2\eta_{a,k,u}^{2(q-1)}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}>0\) at this interior point, and its positive denominator implies that \(N_{\mathrm{DT}}\) is not identically zero.  If \(m_j=q\), then \(\beta=1\), \(L=B\), (8) gives \(d=\eta^{q-1}\), and \(c=d^2=\eta^{2(q-1)}\).

It remains to justify the topology and measure assertions.  A nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many roots.  Induct on the number of affine coordinates: write a nonzero polynomial as a polynomial in its last coordinate with coefficient polynomials in the preceding coordinates.  Outside the zero set of one nonzero coefficient, the last-coordinate section has finitely many roots.  Fubini's theorem and the induction hypothesis then give measure zero for the full zero set.  The same section argument shows that a polynomial vanishing on a nonempty relatively open box must have every coefficient vanish there and hence be the zero polynomial; thus a nonzero polynomial zero set has empty relative interior.  Applying these facts to \(N_{\mathrm{DT}}\) proves density and full relative measure of its complement.  Relative openness follows from continuity of the rational function where the denominator is positive, and nonemptiness follows from (9).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} An explicit embedded contrast supplies a positive \(q\)-row minor on every fixed heterogeneous alphabet vector. \textit{Gap.} A generic-rank slogan alone does not establish that the exceptional polynomial is nonzero on the graph-compatible parameter space. \textit{Consumer.} Designers may perturb a positive heterogeneous bank away from the certificate boundary while preserving its graph and response alphabets.

\begin{theorem}[thm:chen-class-relation]\label{thm:chen-class-relation}
The full heterogeneous finite-observed certified class \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\) and \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\) are incomparable in the ambient mixed-type latent-structure universe.  The noncontainment \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\not\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\) is witnessed by the equal-alphabet dense ternary member: in the equal-alphabet subfamily every selected observed variable and every latent coordinate have support cardinality \(q\), so Chen et al.'s discrete sufficient-measurement requirement \(\lvert\operatorname{supp}(X_j)\rvert>\lvert\operatorname{supp}(H_k)\rvert\) fails, and the ternary witness is outside their two-sufficient-measurement class.  Conversely, \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\) contains members with continuous measurements and permits graphs without two disjoint triangular views, whereas \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\) requires finite observed alphabets.  Completeness in Chen et al. supplies a discretization with full-rank tables, whereas this project derives full rank from the finite local certificate \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\) without assuming completeness or larger-support measurements.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 \mathcal C_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(q)}(K,J)
 =\bigl\{(G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J):m_j=q
   \text{ for every }j\bigr\}.
\]
This is a subfamily of the full heterogeneous class, not a replacement for it.

We first prove \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\not\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\).  Proposition~\textnormal{prop:ternary-witness} gives a rational faithful law \(\widetilde P\), on the fixed graph \(\Gamma^{\star}\), in
\(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(3)}(3,8)\).  In particular,
\[
 (\Gamma^{\star},\widetilde P)
 \in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(3)}(3,8)
 \subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(3)}(3,8).
\]
Strictly positive latent probabilities give
\(\lvert\operatorname{supp}_{\widetilde P}(H_k)\rvert=3\) for every \(k\).  Every observed alphabet in this witness is \(\{0,1,2\}\), and the chosen neighborhood retains strictly positive measurement cells, so
\(\lvert\operatorname{supp}_{\widetilde P}(X_j)\rvert=3\) for every \(j\).  By Lemma~\textnormal{lem:chen-mixed-sufficient-measurement}, a discrete sufficient measurement for a latent parent must have strictly larger support.  Here that inequality would be \(3>3\), which is false.  Thus none of the witness measurements is sufficient in Chen et al.'s discrete sense, the two-sufficient-measurement requirement fails, and
\[
 (\Gamma^{\star},\widetilde P)\notin\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}.
\]
Since the witness belongs to the full certified class, this single equal-alphabet member proves noncontainment of the full class.  Notice that no support-cardinality claim has been made about certified models with \(m_j>q\).

For the reverse noncontainment, it is useful to exhibit a member of the continuous branch recorded in Lemma~\textnormal{lem:chen-mixed-sufficient-measurement}.  Let \(H\) be uniform on \(\mathcal Q\), choose distinct real numbers \(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_{q-1}\), and, conditionally on \(H=h\), let three pure children be independent with
\[
 X_i=\mu_h+\epsilon_i,\qquad
 \epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1).
\]
Relabel the states so that \(\mu_0<\cdots<\mu_{q-1}\).  The conditional densities \(f_h(x)=\phi(x-\mu_h)\) are linearly independent.  Indeed, if
\(\sum_h a_h f_h(x)=0\) almost everywhere, continuity makes the equality hold for every \(x\).  Multiplication by \(\sqrt{2\pi}\exp(x^2/2)\) gives
\[
 \sum_{h=0}^{q-1}a_h\exp(-\mu_h^2/2)\exp(\mu_h x)=0.
\]
Divide by \(\exp(\mu_{q-1}x)\) and let \(x\to\infty\); every term but the last tends to zero, so \(a_{q-1}=0\).  Repetition proves \(a_h=0\) for every \(h\).  Consequently, if \(g:\mathcal Q\to\mathbb R\) satisfies \(E\{g(H)\mid X_i\}=0\), multiplication by the positive marginal density of \(X_i\) yields
\[
 \sum_{h=0}^{q-1}q^{-1}g(h)f_h(x)=0
 \quad\text{for almost every }x,
\]
and linear independence gives \(g=0\).  Thus each continuous child satisfies the required completeness condition and has distinct conditional laws across latent states; in particular, any two of the three children are sufficient measurements in the continuous branch.

This star law is also faithful.  Given any subset \(C\) of the other children and any value \(x_C\), the posterior weights are
\[
 w_h(x_C)
 =\frac{q^{-1}\prod_{i\in C}\phi(x_i-\mu_h)}
        {\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}q^{-1}\prod_{i\in C}\phi(x_i-\mu_r)}>0.
\]
Because the \(\mu_h\) are distinct, \(\operatorname{Var}_{w(x_C)}(\mu_H)>0\).  For two children not in \(C\), conditional independence given \(H\) and the zero-mean independent noises imply
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j\mid X_C=x_C)
 =\operatorname{Var}_{w(x_C)}(\mu_H)>0.
\]
Also, the conditional law \(N(\mu_h,1)\) of any remaining child depends on \(h\), while every posterior weight is positive, so \(H\) and that child are dependent given \(X_C=x_C\).  These two calculations rule out every conditional independence not implied by d-separation in a one-parent star; conditioning on \(H\) gives precisely the child independences implied by the Markov factorization.  The model is therefore faithful.  It has a one-factor latent set and three pure children, so it meets the support-identification branch as well as the completeness, nondegeneracy, and two-sufficient-measurement conditions defining \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\).  Hence it is a member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\).

Each \(X_i\) in this member has support \(\mathbb R\).  By Definition~\textnormal{def:certified-class} and the underlying finite-observed model class, every observed coordinate of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\) instead has a finite state space \(\mathcal X_j=\{0,\ldots,m_j-1\}\).  The displayed continuous member therefore belongs to no such certified class, and
\[
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{Chen}}\not\subseteq
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J).
\]
The cited Chen lemma also records that their class does not impose two disjoint triangular views, providing a separate structural distinction.

Finally, Lemma~\textnormal{lem:chen-mixed-sufficient-measurement} attributes Chen et al.'s full-rank discretization to completeness.  Here Theorem~\textnormal{thm:local-factorization} derives rectangular view rank from the local Gram minors, and Theorem~\textnormal{thm:certificate-frontier} packages their product as \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\), with necessity only in the equal-alphabet case.  Thus the two rank mechanisms are different, and both asserted noncontainments follow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The class relation prevents the new certificate from being presented as a disguised instance of completeness or two sufficient measurements. \textit{Gap.} Chen et al. (2025, Proposition 4.2, Definition D.3, and Theorem 4.19) cover mixed-type measurements through completeness and two sufficient measurements.  The full heterogeneous finite-observed certified class is incomparable with that unchanged published class; equal support is used only for the ternary witness proving one direction. \textit{Consumer.} It tells practitioners whether a mixed-type tensor-rank guarantee or the local categorical certificate applies to a given item bank.

\begin{theorem}[thm:latent-dimension]\label{thm:latent-dimension}
For every \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\), \(\operatorname{rank}(T_{12})=q^K\), and consequently \(K_{\min}(P_X)=K\) even when a competing realization with fewer \(q\)-state coordinates is not double triangular.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\textnormal{lem:finite-latent-state-lower-bound} gives \(\operatorname{rank}(T_{12})=q^K\) and proves the stronger fact that every finite latent measurement representation has at least \(q^K\) positive-mass joint latent states.  In particular, consider a competing measurement model with \(L\) latent coordinates, each having \(q\) states.  Its joint latent vector has at most \(q^L\) positive-mass states.  The lemma therefore gives
\[
 q^K\le |\operatorname{supp}(H')|\le q^L.
\]
Since \(q\ge3>1\), \(L\ge K\).  The original certified realization itself has \(K\) such coordinates, so the defining minimum satisfies \(K_{\min}(P_X)\le K\); the preceding inequality gives the reverse bound.  Hence
\[
 K_{\min}(P_X)=K.
\]
The target \(K\) is thereby proved equal to the observed-law functional \(K_{\min}(P_X)\); the equality is not being imposed by definition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The stronger joint-state lower bound makes the equality \(K_{\min}(P_X)=K\) a proved consequence of the observed law. \textit{Gap.} The binary rank exclusion does not supply the heterogeneous categorical certificate. \textit{Consumer.} It selects the number of \(q\)-state skills before model fitting.

\begin{theorem}[thm:coordinate-alignment]\label{thm:coordinate-alignment}
Suppose \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\) and a three-view decomposition has recovered \(\mathscr L(P_X)\) and the component conditional columns up to one arbitrary permutation. The observable family \(\mathcal E(P_X)\), together with measurement nondegeneracy and the subset condition, determines a bijection \(\varphi_{\mathrm{coord}}:\mathscr L(P_X)\to\mathcal Q^K\) uniquely modulo \(\mathfrak G\). Transporting the recovered weights and conditional columns through this bijection identifies \(\Gamma,\pi,p\); faithfulness then identifies \([\Lambda]_{\mathrm{MEC}}\). Thus the passage from \(q^K\) anonymous mixture labels to \(K\) \(q\)-state coordinates and a latent MEC is an additional identification step not supplied by generic three-view Kruskal uniqueness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\).  Membership in the certified class invokes the measurement-model factorization, positive latent support, measurement nondegeneracy, third-view coverage, the subset condition, faithfulness, and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\ne0\).  By Theorem~\(\textnormal{thm:certificate-frontier}\),
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M_1)=\operatorname{rank}(M_2)=q^K.
\]
Let \(\tau:\mathscr L(P_X)\to\mathcal Q^K\) denote the unknown bijection between the labels returned by the assumed three-view decomposition and the true latent configurations.  This bijection is used only to prove that the observable construction below returns the true coordinate structure; it is not an input to that construction.  For a label \(\ell\), write
\[
 Q_j^\ell(x)=P\!\left(X_j=x\mid H=\tau(\ell)\right)
\]
for the recovered conditional column of observed coordinate \(j\).

First, the component weights are themselves determined by the observed law and the recovered columns.  Define the observable matrix
\[
 \widetilde M_1(x,\ell)=\prod_{j\in S_1}Q_j^\ell(x_j),
 \qquad x\in\prod_{j\in S_1}\mathcal X_j,
\]
and let \(v_1(x)=P_X(X_{S_1}=x)\).  The Markov factorization in the definition of the certified class makes the observed coordinates conditionally independent given \(H\), so \(\widetilde M_1\) is \(M_1\) with its columns permuted by \(\tau\).  Hence it has column rank \(q^K\).  If \(\omega_\ell=\pi_{\tau(\ell)}\), finite marginalization gives the observable mixture equation
\[
 v_1=\widetilde M_1\omega.
\]
Full column rank makes \(\widetilde M_1^{\mathsf T}\widetilde M_1\) positive definite: for \(z\ne0\),
\[
 z^{\mathsf T}\widetilde M_1^{\mathsf T}\widetilde M_1z
 =\lVert\widetilde M_1z\rVert_2^2>0.
\]
Thus the weights are recovered uniquely by the explicit observable formula
\[
 \omega
 =\left(\widetilde M_1^{\mathsf T}\widetilde M_1\right)^{-1}
   \widetilde M_1^{\mathsf T}v_1.
\]

We next recover the product coordinates without using \(\tau\).  For each observed coordinate set
\[
 A_j=\{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:H_k\to X_j\text{ in }G\}.
\]
For every \(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}\), define an equivalence relation on \(\mathcal Q^K\) by
\[
 h\mathrel{E_A}h'
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h_A=h'_A.
\]
The measurement-model factorization gives
\[
 P(X_j=\mathord\cdot\mid H=h)
 =P(X_j=\mathord\cdot\mid H_{A_j}=h_{A_j}).
\]
Consequently, \(h_{A_j}=h'_{A_j}\) implies equality of the two conditional columns.  Conversely, Assumption~\(\textnormal{ass:measurement-nondegeneracy}\) says that distinct configurations of \(H_{A_j}\) induce distinct conditional distributions of \(X_j\).  Therefore
\[
 Q_j^\ell=Q_j^{\ell'}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \tau(\ell)\mathrel{E_{A_j}}\tau(\ell').
\]
Thus the observable relation \(E_j\) is exactly the pullback \(\tau^{-1}E_{A_j}\tau\).

Order equivalence relations by inclusion, so their meet is intersection and their join is the smallest equivalence relation containing their union.  For all \(A,B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}\),
\[
 E_A\wedge E_B=E_{A\cup B},
 \qquad
 E_A\vee E_B=E_{A\cap B}.
\]
The meet identity follows directly from simultaneous agreement on \(A\) and \(B\).  For the join identity, every \(E_A\)- or \(E_B\)-step preserves the coordinates in \(A\cap B\), so \(E_A\vee E_B\subseteq E_{A\cap B}\).  Conversely, if \(h_{A\cap B}=h'_{A\cap B}\), choose \(z\in\mathcal Q^K\) with
\[
 z_A=h_A,
 \qquad z_B=h'_B;
\]
the prescriptions agree on \(A\cap B\), and the remaining coordinates may be chosen arbitrarily because \(\mathcal Q\ne\varnothing\).  Then \(h\mathrel{E_A}z\mathrel{E_B}h'\), proving the reverse inclusion.  Induction over lattice expressions now shows that every element of the finite lattice generated by \(\{E_j:1\le j\le J\}\) is the pullback of some \(E_A\).  Moreover,
\[
 \#(\mathcal Q^K/E_A)=q^{|A|}.
\]
The map \(A\mapsto E_A\) is injective: if \(k\in A\setminus B\), two configurations which differ only at coordinate \(k\) are \(E_B\)-equivalent but not \(E_A\)-equivalent.

For each latent coordinate define its observed-child set
\[
 C_k=\{j:k\in A_j\}=\operatorname{ch}_X(H_k).
\]
Third-view coverage makes \(C_k\) nonempty.  For \(\ell\ne k\), the subset condition gives \(C_k\nsubseteq C_\ell\), so there is a \(j\in C_k\setminus C_\ell\).  Equivalently, \(k\in A_j\) and \(\ell\notin A_j\).  It follows coordinate by coordinate that
\[
 \bigcap_{j\in C_k}A_j=\{k\}.
\]
Repeated use of the join identity therefore yields
\[
 E_{\{k\}}=\bigvee_{j\in C_k}E_{A_j}.
\]
Hence every one-coordinate relation occurs in the observable generated lattice.  Conversely, because \(q\ge3\), an element \(E_A\) of this lattice has exactly \(q\) equivalence classes if and only if \(|A|=1\).  The observable operation ``generate the finite relation lattice and retain its elements with exactly \(q\) classes'' therefore returns precisely
\[
 \{E_{\{1\}},\ldots,E_{\{K\}}\}
\]
transported to \(\mathscr L(P_X)\), without using the unknown map \(\tau\).

Choose any ordering \(R_1,\ldots,R_K\) of these \(K\) observable relations and, independently for each \(r\), choose a bijection
\[
 \lambda_r:\mathscr L(P_X)/R_r\longrightarrow\mathcal Q.
\]
Define
\[
 \varphi_{\mathrm{coord}}(\ell)_r
 =\lambda_r\!\left([\ell]_{R_r}\right).
\]
Because the \(R_r\)'s are exactly the one-coordinate relations,
\[
 \bigwedge_{r=1}^KR_r=E_{\{1,\ldots,K\}},
\]
which is the equality relation.  Thus \(\varphi_{\mathrm{coord}}\) is injective.  Its domain and codomain both have cardinality \(q^K\), so it is bijective.  Changing the order of the \(R_r\)'s permutes the \(K\) coordinates, and changing their class labels independently permutes the \(q\) states within each coordinate.  Conversely, any two choices differ in exactly this way.  Thus the bijection is unique modulo
\[
 \mathfrak G=\mathfrak S_K\ltimes(\mathfrak S_q)^K.
\]

All remaining recovery maps are now explicit.  In the chosen coordinate labeling,
\[
 \Gamma_{jk}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 k\in A_j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 E_j\subseteq R_k,
\]
because agreement on \(A_j\) implies agreement on coordinate \(k\) exactly when \(k\in A_j\).  Hence \(\Gamma\) is recovered from observable relation inclusion.  For \(h\in\mathcal Q^K\), let \(\ell=\varphi_{\mathrm{coord}}^{-1}(h)\) and set
\[
 \pi_h=\omega_\ell,
 \qquad
 p_j(x\mid h)=Q_j^\ell(x).
\]
The preceding mixture equation and conditional-column equivalence prove that these observable formulas equal the true latent weights and measurement tables after the chosen coordinate and state relabeling; they do not define the causal target by fiat.

It remains to identify the latent Markov equivalence class.  Positive latent support gives \(\pi_h>0\) for every \(h\), so every conditioning event formed from latent coordinates has positive probability.  The recovered \(\pi\) decides every latent conditional-independence statement by finite marginalization: for pairwise disjoint coordinate sets \(U,V,W\),
\[
 H_U\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}H_V\mid H_W
\]
holds if and only if, for every \((u,v,w)\),
\[
 \pi_{U\cup V\cup W}(u,v,w)\,\pi_W(w)
 =\pi_{U\cup W}(u,w)\,\pi_{V\cup W}(v,w),
\]
where each term is the corresponding marginal sum of \(\pi\).  This equivalence follows by multiplying the defining conditional-probability equality by the positive quantity \(\pi_W(w)^2\).

The measurement assumption makes every observed vertex a sink and forbids observed--observed edges.  Consequently, a trail between latent vertices that enters an observed vertex has that observed vertex as a collider; because an observed sink has no descendants and the conditioning set contains only latent vertices, such a trail is blocked.  Hence, for latent endpoints and latent conditioning sets, d-separation in \(G\) is exactly d-separation in the latent subgraph \(\Lambda\).  Assumption~\(\textnormal{ass:faithfulness}\) therefore identifies the recovered conditional-independence pattern with the d-separation pattern of \(\Lambda\).

For a terminating recovery map, enumerate the finite set of directed acyclic graphs on the recovered \(K\) coordinate labels, compute each graph's d-separation relations, and retain exactly those whose relations equal the recovered pattern.  The true \(\Lambda\) is retained by faithfulness.  Lemma~\(\textnormal{lem:dag-markov-equivalence-characterization}\) says that equality of d-separation patterns is equivalent to equality of skeletons and unshielded colliders, so the retained set is exactly \([\Lambda]_{\mathrm{MEC}}\).  Every operation above is finite and determined by \(P_X\) and the assumed recovered component columns.  It recovers \(\Gamma\), \(\pi\), \(p\), and \([\Lambda]_{\mathrm{MEC}}\) modulo \(\mathfrak G\), which proves the theorem and exhibits the coordinate-alignment step beyond anonymous three-view label recovery.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Anonymous latent-class labels are insufficient for a causal measurement graph; the equality-pattern theorem supplies the missing coordinate product and MEC. \textit{Gap.} Allman, Matias, and Rhodes (2009) stop at label swapping for one \(q^K\)-class variable, while Kivva et al. (2021) obtain coordinate structure from a supplied mixture oracle; neither proves this observable coordinate-alignment result for certified categorical BLCMs. \textit{Consumer.} It lets an identifiability checker report named latent coordinates, their observed-child sets, and a latent MEC rather than an uninterpreted \(q^K\)-component mixture.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-k-identification]\label{thm:fixed-k-identification}
For every \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\) with \(m_j\ge q\), the observed law \(P_X\) determines, by exact three-view uniqueness using observable nonsingular \(q^K\)-by-\(q^K\) row minors, an unstructured \(q^K\)-label set \(\mathscr L(P_X)\) and all component conditional columns up to one permutation.  The component-equality pattern \(\mathcal E(P_X)\) then uniquely aligns those labels with \(K\) \(q\)-state coordinates through \(\varphi_{\mathrm{coord}}\), modulo \(\mathfrak G\), and latent conditional independences determine \([\Lambda]_{\mathrm{MEC}}\).  Consequently \(P_X\) uniquely determines \(\mathcal R(P_X)=[\Gamma,\pi,p,[\Lambda]_{\mathrm{MEC}}]_{\sim}\), and every realization in \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\) belongs to this same equivalence class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(r=q^K\), \(n_a=\prod_{j\in S_a}m_j\), and regard the observed law on the three disjoint views as a three-way tensor.  For \(z\in\prod_{j\in S_3}\mathcal X_j\), let \(T_z\in\mathbb R^{n_1\times n_2}\) be the slice at \(X_{S_3}=z\), and let
\[
 m_3(z\mid h)=P(X_{S_3}=z\mid H=h).
\]
The Markov measurement factorization gives
\[
 T_z=M_1\operatorname{diag}
 \bigl(\pi_hm_3(z\mid h):h\in\mathcal Q^K\bigr)M_2^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 T_{12}=\sum_zT_z=M_1\operatorname{diag}(\pi)M_2^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{11}
\]
Theorem~\textnormal{thm:certificate-frontier} makes both \(M_a\) have column rank \(r\).  Hence each contains an invertible \(r\)-by-\(r\) row submatrix.  Choose row-index sets \(I_a\) of size \(r\), put \(A_a=M_a[I_a,:]\), and define
\[
 \overline T=T_{12}[I_1,I_2],
 \qquad
 \overline T_z=T_z[I_1,I_2].
\]
Assumption~\textnormal{ass:positive-support} makes \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\) invertible, so \(\overline T=A_1DA_2^{\mathsf T}\) is invertible and
\[
 C_z:=\overline T_z\overline T^{-1}
 =A_1\operatorname{diag}(m_3(z\mid h))A_1^{-1}.
\tag{12}
\]

The third-view component columns are pairwise distinct.  If \(h\neq h'\), choose \(k\) with \(h_k\neq h'_k\).  Assumption~\textnormal{ass:third-view-coverage} supplies \(j\in S_3\) with \(H_k\to X_j\).  The two parent configurations at \(j\) differ, so Assumption~\textnormal{ass:measurement-nondegeneracy} makes the conditional marginals of \(X_j\) distinct.  Therefore the joint columns \(m_3(\cdot\mid h)\) and \(m_3(\cdot\mid h')\) are distinct.

There is a real vector \(t=(t_z)_z\) for which all values
\[
 \lambda_h=\sum_zt_zm_3(z\mid h)
\]
are distinct: the forbidden vectors lie in the finite union of proper hyperplanes \(t\cdot(m_3(\cdot\mid h)-m_3(\cdot\mid h'))=0\).  Thus the observable matrix \(C(t)=\sum_zt_zC_z\) has simple real spectrum.  Equation (12) shows that an eigenvector matrix has the form
\[
 V=A_1S
\tag{13}
\]
after one common permutation, where \(S\) is an invertible diagonal scaling matrix.

Unlike the equal-alphabet argument, the selected rows of a probability column need not sum to one, so scaling cannot be removed by normalizing the eigenvectors in (13).  Instead use the full observable table.  Define
\[
 U=V^{-1}T_{12}[I_1,:]
 =S^{-1}D M_2^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \alpha_h=\sum_{x_2}U(h,x_2)=S_{hh}^{-1}\pi_h.
\]
Every \(\alpha_h\neq0\), and row normalization recovers
\[
 M_2^{\mathsf T}(h,:)=\frac{U(h,:)}{\alpha_h}.
\tag{14}
\]
Let the now known \(A_2=M_2[I_2,:]\) be substituted into
\[
 F=T_{12}[:,I_2](A_2^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=M_1D.
\]
Because every column of \(M_1\) is a conditional probability mass function and \(\pi_h>0\),
\[
 \pi_h=\sum_{x_1}F(x_1,h),
 \qquad
 M_1=F D^{-1}.
\tag{15}
\]
Finally, (12)--(13) give
\[
 m_3(z\mid h)=\bigl(V^{-1}C_zV\bigr)_{hh}.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (14)--(16) recover the weights and all three full view columns up to the one permutation.  Marginalization recovers the conditional column of every observed coordinate.  This is exact uniqueness on the certified class; no generic qualifier is used.

The already established Theorem~\textnormal{thm:coordinate-alignment} applies to these anonymous labels and recovered columns.  It turns them into the unique \(K\)-coordinate product modulo \(\mathfrak G\), recovers \(\Gamma,\pi,p\), and uses faithfulness together with Lemma~\textnormal{lem:dag-markov-equivalence-characterization} to recover \([\Lambda]_{\mathrm{MEC}}\).  Therefore \(P_X\) determines \(\mathcal R(P_X)\).

For the comparison claim, take any element of \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\).  It gives another \(r\)-component product decomposition of (11) with every weight positive.  Since \(\overline T\) is invertible, both of its restricted alternative factor matrices are invertible.  Repeating (12) for the alternative shows that its first restricted factor diagonalizes the same observable family \(C_z\).  The simple-spectrum matrix \(C(t)\) forces its eigenvectors to equal those in (13), up to scaling and the same kind of label permutation.  Formulas (14)--(16), which use only observable arrays and probability normalization, then force the same weights and all component columns.  The alternative's measurement nondegeneracy and subset condition feed the same equality-pattern lattice in Theorem~\textnormal{thm:coordinate-alignment}, giving the same coordinate factors and \(\Gamma\) modulo \(\mathfrak G\); its faithfulness gives the same latent Markov-equivalence class.  Hence every member of \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\) lies in the single claimed quotient class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Observable square row minors give exact rectangular three-view recovery, after which the equality lattice recovers the coordinate product and MEC. \textit{Gap.} Generic latent-class uniqueness stops at anonymous labels and does not resolve rectangular eigenvector scaling. \textit{Consumer.} Recovered skill graphs are certified against every same-\(K\) BLCM realization in \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\).

\begin{proposition}[prop:ternary-witness]\label{prop:ternary-witness}
The explicitly dimensioned law \(P^{\star}\) is a strictly positive Markov law for the graph with measurement matrix \(\Gamma^{\star}\) and empty latent DAG; it satisfies measurement nondegeneracy, double triangularity, third-view coverage, and the subset condition. The third latent coordinate has no pure child, and the displayed selected matrices satisfy \(\det(B_{a,k,u})=(c^{\star}_{s(a,k),\rho_a(k)})^2>0\), so \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}>0\). These support, graph, nondegeneracy, and certificate properties persist on a relatively open neighborhood of \(P^{\star}\). Every such neighborhood contains a rational faithful law on the same graph; each such law belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(3)}(3,8)\), has an identified recovery target, and supplies a dense ternary no-pure-child witness.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The rows \(1,2,3\) of \(\Gamma^{\star}\) have diagonal parents \(1,2,3\), respectively, in the order \(\rho_1=(1,2,3)\), and none has a parent later than its diagonal parent.  The rows \(4,5,6\) have diagonal parents \(2,1,3\), respectively, in the order \(\rho_2=(2,1,3)\), again with no later parent.  Thus \(S_1,S_2\) satisfy double triangularity.  Rows \(7,8\) have parent sets \(\{1,3\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), so \(S_3\) covers every latent coordinate.

Directly from \(\Gamma^{\star}\), the observed-child sets of \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) are
\[
 C_1=\{1,2,3,5,6,7\},\qquad
 C_2=\{2,3,4,5,6,8\},\qquad
 C_3=\{3,6,7,8\}.
\]
For \(C_1,C_2\), the elements \(1\) and \(4\) witness the two failed containments; for \(C_1,C_3\), the elements \(1\) and \(8\) do so; and for \(C_2,C_3\), the elements \(4\) and \(7\) do so.  Hence the subset condition holds.  Every child of \(H_3\), namely \(3,6,7,8\), has at least one additional latent parent, so \(H_3\) has no pure child.

Fix an observed row \(j\).  For every parent \(\ell\),
\[
 \sum_{x=0}^2\left(\mathbf 1\{x=h_\ell\}-\frac13\right)=0,
\]
so the displayed formula for \(P^{\star}(X_j=x\mid H=h)\) sums to one.  Put
\(S_j=\sum_{\ell:\Gamma^{\star}_{j\ell}=1}c^{\star}_{j\ell}\).  The geometric definition of the coefficients gives
\[
 S_j\le 2^{-(4+j)}\sum_{\ell=1}^3 2^{-3\ell}<\frac1{224}.
\]
Since \(-1/3\le \mathbf 1\{x=h_\ell\}-1/3\le2/3\), every cell obeys
\[
 \frac13-\frac{S_j}{3}
 \le P^{\star}(X_j=x\mid H=h)
 \le\frac13+\frac{2S_j}{3}.
\]
The lower bound is positive and the upper bound is less than one.  Together with \(\pi_h^{\star}=3^{-3}>0\), the empty latent DAG, and conditional independence of the observed coordinates given \(H\), this proves that \(P^{\star}\) is a strictly positive law with the required Markov factorization.

For measurement nondegeneracy, take two different configurations of the parents of row \(j\), and let \(\ell_0\) be their smallest differing parent index.  Evaluate the difference of their conditional mass functions at the state equal to the first configuration's value of \(h_{\ell_0}\).  All contributions with \(\ell<\ell_0\) cancel, while the \(\ell_0\) contribution has absolute value \(c^{\star}_{j\ell_0}\).  The absolute value of the sum of all later contributions is at most
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\ell>\ell_0\\\Gamma^{\star}_{j\ell}=1}}c^{\star}_{j\ell}
 \le c^{\star}_{j\ell_0}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}2^{-3r}
 =\frac{c^{\star}_{j\ell_0}}7
 <c^{\star}_{j\ell_0}.
\]
The two conditional mass functions therefore differ.

For a selected row, double triangularity says that all its parents other than its diagonal parent occur in the predecessor context.  Separating the diagonal term in the formula for \(P^{\star}\) consequently gives, for every displayed context,
\[
 B_{a,k,u}
 =b^{\star}_{s(a,k),u}\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}
  +c^{\star}_{s(a,k),\rho_a(k)}Q_3.
\]
For any probability vector \(b\) and scalar \(t\),
\[
 b\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}+tQ_3
 =tI_3+\left(b-\frac t3\mathbf 1\right)\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
When \(t>0\), multilinearity of the determinant in its columns shows directly that
\(\det(tI_3+u\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T})=t^3+t^2\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u\): the term using no column of the rank-one matrix is \(t^3\), the three terms using one such column sum to \(t^2\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u\), and every term using two is zero because those columns are proportional.  Taking \(u=b-t\mathbf 1/3\) and using \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}b=1\) yields
\[
 \det\!\left(b\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}+tQ_3\right)
 =t^3\left(1+t^{-1}\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}
       \left(b-\frac t3\mathbf 1\right)\right)
 =t^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \det(B_{a,k,u})
 =\bigl(c^{\star}_{s(a,k),\rho_a(k)}\bigr)^2>0.
\]
Definition~\textnormal{def:local-minors} gives \(c_{a,k,u}=\det(B_{a,k,u})^2>0\), and Definition~\textnormal{def:certificate} then gives \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}>0\).

Work now in the finite-dimensional product of conditional-table simplices for the fixed graph.  Strict cell positivity, inequality of each of the finitely many pairs of measurement columns, and nonvanishing of the finitely many displayed determinants are relatively open conditions.  The graph conditions are fixed.  Hence all the support, graph, nondegeneracy, and certificate properties just verified hold throughout some relative neighborhood \(U\) of the table parameter of \(P^{\star}\).

By Lemma~\textnormal{lem:positive-faithful-parameters-dense}, faithful strictly positive parameters are relatively open and dense, so \(U\) contains a nonempty relatively open set \(U_{\mathrm f}\) of faithful parameters.  The affine simplex coordinates and all defining equations have rational coefficients; rational points are dense in their relative interiors.  Thus \(U_{\mathrm f}\) contains a rational table parameter.  Its induced joint probabilities are finite sums and products of rational numbers and are therefore rational.  Every such chosen law is faithful and retains every property defining \(U\); by Definition~\textnormal{def:certified-class} it belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(3)}(3,8)\).  Theorem~\textnormal{thm:fixed-k-identification} identifies its recovery target.  Since the graph still has no pure child of \(H_3\), this is the required dense ternary no-pure-child witness.  The argument asserts only that every neighborhood contains a rational faithful perturbation, not that the displayed parameter \(P^{\star}\) itself is faithful.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The witness demonstrates a positive no-pure-child ternary region and makes every certificate matrix inspectable. \textit{Gap.} Lee and Gu (2026) give binary no-pure-child examples and ternary failures, but no positive certified ternary model supporting the full chain. \textit{Consumer.} It is an exact regression test and simulation fixture for a \(\texttt{cdmTools}\)-style checker.

\begin{proposition}[prop:binary-reduction]\label{prop:binary-reduction}
Let \(q_{\mathrm{bin}}=2\).  On
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{LG},1}^{\mathrm{bin}}(K,J)\), as defined by the binary
measurement graph, Markov factorization, positive latent support, binary
measurement nondegeneracy, and binary double triangularity, both
\((M_{\mathrm{bin}})_1\) and \((M_{\mathrm{bin}})_2\) are nonsingular,
\(\operatorname{rank}((T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}})=2^K\), and
\((K_{\min})_{\mathrm{bin}}((P_X)_{\mathrm{bin}})=K\).  These are direct
reduced-assumption binary-response conclusions and do not use faithfulness.
Lee and Gu's BLCM instead has binary latent variables, permits arbitrary
observed variables on nondegenerate separable metric spaces, and imposes
faithfulness as a standing assumption; accordingly, their published
Theorem~1 can be invoked here on the faithful intersection of the first
comparator class, not as the justification for the conclusion on all of that
class.  On
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{LG},2}^{\mathrm{bin}}(K,J)\), the added faithfulness,

class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((G_{\mathrm{bin}},P_{\mathrm{bin}})\in
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{LG},1}^{\mathrm{bin}}(K,J)\).  By
Definition~\textnormal{def:binary-rank-comparator-class}, the binary
measurement-graph, Markov, positive-latent-support,
measurement-nondegeneracy, and double-triangular assumptions hold.  Apply
Lemma~\textnormal{lem:binary-triangular-view-nonsingularity} separately to
the two disjoint triangular views.  It gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\bigl((M_{\mathrm{bin}})_a\bigr)=2^K,
 \qquad a\in\{1,2\}.
\]
Each \((M_{\mathrm{bin}})_a\) is a square matrix of order \(2^K\), so both
matrices are nonsingular.  The cited lemma uses the measurement-graph,
Markov, measurement-nondegeneracy, and double-triangular assumptions, and
does not use faithfulness or positive latent support.

Positive latent support states that
\((\pi_{\mathrm{bin}})_h>0\) for every
\(h\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{bin}}^K\).  Consequently
\(D:=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_{\mathrm{bin}})\) is nonsingular, with
\[
 D^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}
 \left(\bigl((\pi_{\mathrm{bin}})_h^{-1}\bigr)_
 {h\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{bin}}^K}\right).
\]
By the definition of \((T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}\),
\[
 (T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}
 =(M_{\mathrm{bin}})_1D(M_{\mathrm{bin}})_2^{\mathsf T}.
\]
All three factors are nonsingular, and direct multiplication verifies that
\[
 (T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}^{-1}
 =(M_{\mathrm{bin}})_2^{-\mathsf T}D^{-1}
  (M_{\mathrm{bin}})_1^{-1}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\bigl((T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}\bigr)=2^K.
\]

We now distinguish the structural latent dimension \(K\) from the
observed-law functional \((K_{\min})_{\mathrm{bin}}\).  The present model is
a binary measurement-model realization with \(K\) latent coordinates, so
Definition~\textnormal{\((K_{\min})_{\mathrm{bin}}\)} gives
\[
 (K_{\min})_{\mathrm{bin}}((P_X)_{\mathrm{bin}})\leq K.
\]
For the reverse inequality, let an arbitrary binary measurement-model
realization of the same observed law have \(L\) latent coordinates
\(\widetilde H\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{bin}}^L\), latent mass vector
\(\widetilde\pi\), and view tables
\[
 \widetilde M_a(x,h)
 :=\widetilde P\bigl((X_{\mathrm{bin}})_{(S_a)_{\mathrm{bin}}}=x
          \mid \widetilde H=h\bigr),
 \qquad
 \widetilde M_a\in\mathbb R^{2^K\times 2^L}.
\]
The measurement-graph restriction and Markov factorization in such a
realization imply conditional independence of distinct observed vertices
given the full latent vector.  Since the two view sets are disjoint, for
every pair of view configurations \((x_1,x_2)\), the law of total probability
therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}(x_1,x_2)
 &=\sum_{h\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{bin}}^L}
   \widetilde P\bigl((X_{\mathrm{bin}})_{(S_1)_{\mathrm{bin}}}=x_1
                   \mid\widetilde H=h\bigr)
   \widetilde\pi_h
   \widetilde P\bigl((X_{\mathrm{bin}})_{(S_2)_{\mathrm{bin}}}=x_2
                   \mid\widetilde H=h\bigr) \\
 &=\bigl[\widetilde M_1\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde\pi)
          \widetilde M_2^{\mathsf T}\bigr](x_1,x_2).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the image of \((T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is contained in the image of
the \(2^K\)-by-\(2^L\) matrix \(\widetilde M_1\), whence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\bigl((T_{12})_{\mathrm{bin}}\bigr)
 \leq\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde M_1)\leq 2^L.
\]
Combining this inequality with the already proved rank \(2^K\) yields
\(2^K\leq2^L\), hence \(K\leq L\).  This holds for every realization in
the defining minimum.  Together with the upper bound furnished by the true
realization, it proves
\[
 (K_{\min})_{\mathrm{bin}}((P_X)_{\mathrm{bin}})=K.
\]

The scope of the comparison with Lee and Gu is separate from the foregoing

 (\pi_{\mathrm{bin}})_h=P_{\mathrm{bin}}(H_{\mathrm{bin}}=h)

 (p_{\mathrm{bin}})_j(x\mid h)

        {(\pi_{\mathrm{bin}})_h}.

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-exact-aq-reconstruction]\label{thm:finite-exact-aq-reconstruction}
There is a finite exact-real-arithmetic algorithm \(A_q\), obtained by making every enumeration and query in \(\mathsf H_q\) explicit, which halts for every probability mass function \(P_X\). On every \(P_X\) generated by \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\), it recovers \(K\), retains only exact realizations of \(P_X\), represents every member of \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\), and returns exactly \(\mathcal R(P_X)\). Its recursive classification stage terminates under the lexicographic measure consisting of the number of unclassified observed rows followed by the number of unresolved component pairs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All operations below occur in the exact-real-arithmetic model; equality, sign, rank, polynomial coefficients, roots of simple real spectra, and finite conditional-independence identities are therefore queried without numerical tolerance.

First recover the latent dimension.  For each integer \(L\ge1\) with \(2L+1\le J\), enumerate the finitely many ordered pairs of disjoint \(L\)-coordinate sets \(R_1,R_2\).  Their joint table has \(\prod_{j\in R_1}m_j\) rows and \(\prod_{j\in R_2}m_j\) columns, each at least \(q^L\).  Enumerate all \(q^L\)-by-\(q^L\) row-and-column minors and test their determinants.  Let \(\widehat K\) be the largest \(L\) for which some such minor is nonsingular, and return a failure symbol if none exists.  On a certified input, Theorem~\textnormal{thm:latent-dimension} gives rank \(q^K\) for the true pair, so a nonsingular \(q^K\)-by-\(q^K\) minor exists.  For \(L>K\), every two-group table factors through the true \(q^K\) latent configurations and has rank at most \(q^K<q^L\).  Therefore \(\widehat K=K\).

Set \(r=q^{\widehat K}\).  Enumerate every ordered pair of disjoint \(\widehat K\)-coordinate views, every pair \(I_1,I_2\) of \(r\) joint-response configurations, and use the nonempty complement as the third view.  Retain a branch only when \(\overline T=T_{12}[I_1,I_2]\) is nonsingular.  For each third-view response \(z\), form \(\overline T_z\) and \(C_z=\overline T_z\overline T^{-1}\).  Let
\[
 g(t)=\operatorname{disc}\!\left(\sum_zt_zC_z\right).
\]
This is an observable polynomial of finite degree.  Compute its coefficients exactly and discard the branch if it is identically zero.  Otherwise, if \(d\) bounds its degree in each coordinate, the grid \(\{0,\ldots,d\}^{|\mathcal X_{S_3}|}\) contains a point where \(g\neq0\).  Indeed, induction on the number of variables, using that a nonzero univariate polynomial of degree at most \(d\) cannot vanish at \(d+1\) distinct points, proves this grid assertion.  Exhaustive evaluation therefore finds such a \(t\) in finitely many steps.

Diagonalize \(C(t)=\sum_zt_zC_z\).  Discard branches without a simple real spectrum.  For every ordering of its \(r\) eigenvectors form \(V\), and apply the scale-safe formulas from Theorem~\textnormal{thm:fixed-k-identification}:
\[
 U=V^{-1}T_{12}[I_1,:],
 \qquad
 \alpha_h=\sum_{x_2}U(h,x_2),
 \qquad
 M_2^{\mathsf T}(h,:)=U(h,:)/\alpha_h,
\tag{17}
\]
rejecting if an \(\alpha_h\) vanishes.  Put \(A_2=M_2[I_2,:]\), reject if it is singular, and set
\[
 F=T_{12}[:,I_2](A_2^{\mathsf T})^{-1},
 \qquad
 \pi_h=\sum_{x_1}F(x_1,h),
 \qquad
 M_1[:,h]=F[:,h]/\pi_h,
\tag{18}
\]
rejecting if a \(\pi_h\) vanishes.  Recover \(m_3(z\mid h)=(V^{-1}C_zV)_{hh}\).  Keep the branch only if \(\pi_h>0\) for every \(h\), all three recovered component arrays are real, nonnegative, and normalized, and their mixture reproduces every cell of \(P_X\) exactly.  Measurement cells are not required to be strictly positive.  The proof of Theorem~\textnormal{thm:fixed-k-identification} shows that a certified branch passes: its component columns are distinct, so \(g\) is nonzero, and (17)--(18) recover the exact decomposition even when some measurement cells vanish.

Marginalize the recovered joint-view columns to obtain every component-conditional distribution of every \(X_j\), and verify that the product of the individual columns reproduces each joint-view column.  On the finite label set, enumerate every ordered \(K\)-tuple of equivalence relations having \(q\) classes whose common class map is a bijection onto the product of the \(K\) class sets.  This explicitly enumerates all coordinate alignments, including those suggested by \(\mathcal E(P_X)\).  For every alignment enumerate the finite set of binary matrices \(\Gamma\in\{0,1\}^{J\times K}\).  Retain \(\Gamma\) precisely when, for each \(j\), the recovered column of \(X_j\) is constant over labels having the same parent configuration and distinct parent configurations give distinct distributions.  The resulting entries \(p_j(x\mid h_{\operatorname{pa}_\Gamma(j)})\) are enumerated candidate measurement tables; require only that they are nonnegative and normalized.  Check the subset condition and record every rectangular \(c_{a,k,u}\) and every observable nonsingular row minor as a certificate trace.

For each retained \((\Gamma,\pi,p)\), enumerate the finite set of DAGs \(\Lambda\) on the \(K\) recovered coordinates.  Retain \(\Lambda\) only if \(\pi\) factorizes over it.  Construct
\[
 P(H=h,X=x)=\pi_h\prod_{j=1}^J
 p_j(x_j\mid h_{\operatorname{pa}_{\Gamma}(j)}).
\]
Use division-free discrete conditional-independence identities, such as \(P(v,w,c)P(c)=P(v,c)P(w,c)\), so zero-probability observed cells cause no undefined conditioning operation.  Enumerate all such identities for this finite complete-data law and all d-separation statements of \(G=(\Lambda,\Gamma)\); retain the graph exactly when the two lists agree.  Finally check the measurement graph, measurement nondegeneracy, the subset condition, and the exact marginal identity with \(P_X\).  These are exactly the defining requirements of \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\), so every retained object is a member: this proves soundness without silently imposing positive measurement cells, double triangularity, the certificate, or third-view coverage on alternatives.

Conversely, any member of \(\mathcal C_K(P_X)\) supplies an \(r\)-term product decomposition of the same tensor with positive weights.  The observable nonsingular minor and simple-spectrum argument of Theorem~\textnormal{thm:fixed-k-identification} force its anonymous component decomposition to be the recovered one up to label permutation.  Its product-coordinate partition, \(\Gamma\), nonnegative normalized tables, and latent DAG all occur in the respective finite exhaustive enumerations, and its defining equalities pass every check.  Thus it is represented.  Theorem~\textnormal{thm:fixed-k-identification} makes all retained realizations members of the single quotient \(\mathcal R(P_X)\), which \(A_q\) returns.

Every outer enumeration is finite: there are finitely many coordinate subsets, response-configuration minors, label permutations, equivalence relations, binary measurement graphs, and latent DAGs.  The grid search is finite by its degree bound.  For the recursive presentation in \(\mathsf H_q\), assign each branch
\[
 \mu=(u,v)\in\mathbb N^2,
\]
where \(u\) is the number of unclassified observed rows and \(v\) the number of component pairs unresolved by the queried equality relations.  A row-classification transition decreases \(u\); after \(u=0\), a pair-resolution transition decreases \(v\); pruning terminates the branch.  Lexicographic order on \(\mathbb N^2\) is well founded, so no recursive branch is infinite.  This proves termination on every input and the asserted certified-class behavior.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:positive-faithful-parameters-dense]\label{lem:positive-faithful-parameters-dense}
For any fixed directed acyclic graph \(G\) on finitely many \(q\)-state vertices, the table parameters in the strictly positive Markov parameterization whose induced law \(P\) is faithful to \(G\) form a relatively open dense set of full Lebesgue measure in the affine product of conditional-table simplex interiors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\Omega\) be the affine product of the open conditional-table simplices for the fixed DAG \(G\).  In each table omit its last cell, so that \(\Omega\) is an open subset of a finite-dimensional real affine space with coordinates \(\theta\).  By the Markov factorization in Assumption~\textnormal{ass:markov}, each joint cell probability is a polynomial in \(\theta\), after the omitted cells are replaced by one minus the sum of the retained cells.

There are only finitely many triples \((V,W,C)\), where \(V,W\) are distinct vertices and \(C\) is a subset of the remaining vertices.  For fixed states \(v,v',w,w',c\), define
\[
 F_{v,v',w,w',c}(\theta)
 =P_\theta(v,w,c)P_\theta(v',w',c)
  -P_\theta(v,w',c)P_\theta(v',w,c),
\]
where variables outside \(\{V,W\}\cup C\) are summed out.  This is a polynomial.  Because all cells are positive on \(\Omega\),
\[
 V\mathrel{\perp}_{P_\theta}W\mid C
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 F_{v,v',w,w',c}(\theta)=0
 \text{ for every }v,v',w,w',c.
\]

We next show directly that, whenever \(V\) and \(W\) are d-connected given \(C\), at least one polynomial in this collection is nonzero.  Choose a simple active path and split it at its colliders into collider-free sections.  A collider-free section is a trek: it has a source from which its arrows point along the two arms.  At a boundary point of the table polytope, give that source a fair bit and make every other vertex on the section copy the bit along the relevant arrow.  At each collider, set its bit equal to the exclusive-or of the two adjacent section bits.  If the collider is not itself in \(C\), activity supplies a directed path from it to a descendant in \(C\); transmit the collider bit along such a path.  Where several transmission paths merge, transmit their exclusive-or, processing vertices in topological order.  Assign each collider to one chosen endpoint in \(C\), and set every unused vertex and every unused member of \(C\) to zero.  These assignments are deterministic functions of parents and hence define a boundary Markov table for \(G\).

Conditioning the members of \(C\) to be zero imposes, group by group, that the exclusive-or of the assigned collider bits is zero.  Taking the exclusive-or of all these equations makes every internal section bit occur twice and cancel; the result is equality of the endpoint bits at \(V\) and \(W\).  The conditioning event has positive probability.  Moreover, simultaneously flipping all section-source bits leaves every collider bit and every conditioning equation unchanged, so both endpoint values occur with positive conditional probability.  Hence the conditional \(2\)-by-\(2\) table of \((V,W)\) has positive diagonal entries and zero off-diagonal entries, and one of its cross-product polynomials is nonzero.  For \(q>2\), use states \(0,1\) in this construction and give the remaining states probability zero at the boundary.  Thus the same polynomial, regarded as a polynomial on the \(q\)-state parameter space, is not identically zero.  This establishes the required active-path witness for every d-connected triple.

For each d-connected triple choose one such nonzero polynomial \(F_{V,W,C}\).  A parameter that is Markov but unfaithful must satisfy an additional conditional independence for some d-connected triple and therefore lies in
\[
 \bigcup_{(V,W,C):\,V\not\!\perp_G W\mid C}
 \{\theta\in\Omega:F_{V,W,C}(\theta)=0\}.
\]
This is a finite union.  A nonzero real polynomial on \(\mathbb R^d\) has a zero set of Lebesgue measure zero: for \(d=1\) it has finitely many roots, and induction on \(d\), after expanding in the last coordinate and applying Fubini outside the common zero set of its nonzero coefficient polynomials, proves the claim.  The same induction shows that its zero set has empty interior.  Restricting to the affine coordinates of \(\Omega\) preserves both conclusions.  Therefore the unfaithful parameters have measure zero and empty relative interior, and faithful parameters are dense and have full relative Lebesgue measure.

Finally, for a fixed triple, failure of its conditional independence means that at least one of finitely many continuous polynomials is nonzero, an open condition.  Faithfulness is the intersection of these conditions over finitely many d-connected triples, so the faithful locus is relatively open.  The Markov factorization already supplies every d-separation independence; exclusion of all additional independences therefore proves precisely faithfulness to \(G\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:binary-triangular-view-nonsingularity]\label{lem:binary-triangular-view-nonsingularity}
For every
\((G_{\mathrm{bin}},P_{\mathrm{bin}})\in
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{LG},1}^{\mathrm{bin}}(K,J)\) and every
\(a\in\{1,2\}\), the square conditional table
\((M_{\mathrm{bin}})_a\) is nonsingular.  This conclusion requires the
binary measurement-graph, Markov, measurement-nondegeneracy, and
double-triangular conditions, but neither faithfulness nor positive latent
support.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a view \(a\).  Permute its selected observed coordinates and the latent
coordinates according to \((\rho_{\mathrm{bin}})_a\); row and column
permutations preserve nonsingularity.  In this order the selected row at stage
\(t\) has \(H_t\) as a parent and has no latent parent among
\(H_{t+1},\ldots,H_K\), by binary double triangularity.  By the measurement
graph and Markov factorization, conditional on all latent variables the
selected observed variables are independent, and the conditional law of the
stage-\(t\) observation depends only on latent parents among
\(H_1,\ldots,H_t\).

For \(t=0,1,\ldots,K\), let \(A_t\) be the \(2^t\times2^t\) conditional
table of the first \(t\) selected observed coordinates given
\((H_1,\ldots,H_t)\), with \(A_0=(1)\).  Suppose inductively that
\(A_{t-1}\) is nonsingular.  For every
\(u\in\{0,1\}^{t-1}\), define the \(2\times2\) table
\[
 C_u(x,r)=P_{\mathrm{bin}}
 \bigl((X_{\mathrm{bin}})_{s_{\mathrm{bin}}(a,t)}=x
       \mid H_{1:t-1}=u,H_t=r\bigr),
 \qquad x,r\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The diagonal one in the triangular row says that \(H_t\) is a parent of this
observation.  Binary measurement nondegeneracy therefore makes the two
conditional Bernoulli laws indexed by \(r=0\) and \(r=1\) distinct.  If
\(\theta_{u,r}:=P_{\mathrm{bin}}((X_{\mathrm{bin}})_{s_{\mathrm{bin}}(a,t)}=1
\mid H_{1:t-1}=u,H_t=r)\), then
\[
 C_u=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1-\theta_{u,0}&1-\theta_{u,1}\\
 \theta_{u,0}&\theta_{u,1}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det(C_u)=\theta_{u,1}-\theta_{u,0}\neq0.
\]
Thus every \(C_u\) is nonsingular; no entrywise positivity is needed.

Indexing rows by \((z,x)\) and columns by \((u,r)\), conditional independence
gives the entrywise identity
\[
 A_t((z,x),(u,r))=A_{t-1}(z,u)C_u(x,r).
\]
Equivalently, after the indicated lexicographic ordering,
\[
 A_t=(A_{t-1}\otimes I_2)
     \operatorname{blockdiag}_{u\in\{0,1\}^{t-1}}(C_u).
\]
The first factor is invertible with inverse
\(A_{t-1}^{-1}\otimes I_2\), and the second is invertible with blockwise
inverse \(\operatorname{blockdiag}_u(C_u^{-1})\).  Hence \(A_t\) is
nonsingular.  Induction from \(A_0=(1)\) proves that \(A_K\) is nonsingular.
After undoing the initial permutations, \(A_K=(M_{\mathrm{bin}})_a\).  Since
\(a\) was arbitrary, the result holds for both views.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:rectangular-local-necessity-fails]\label{prop:rectangular-local-necessity-fails}
For either fixed view label \(a\in\{1,2\}\), when \(q=3\) and \(K=2\) there are strictly positive column-stochastic local tables with \(m_{s(a,1)}=3\) and \(m_{s(a,2)}=9\) such that the first table has pairwise distinct columns and \(c_{a,1,\varnothing}=0\), while the resulting triangular-view matrix \(M_a\) has column rank \(3^2=9\).  Hence, when observed alphabets may exceed \(q\), full column rank of a triangular view does not imply nonvanishing of every local Gram certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 b=\begin{pmatrix}1/3\\1/3\\1/3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 v=\begin{pmatrix}\delta\\-\delta\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 L_{1,\varnothing}=\begin{pmatrix}b-v&b&b+v\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<\delta<1/3.
\]
Its columns are strictly positive probability vectors and are pairwise distinct, but the first plus the third equals twice the second.  Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(L_{1,\varnothing})=2\) and, by the Gram identity in Theorem~\textnormal{thm:local-factorization}, \(c_{1,\varnothing}=0\).

Index the nine coordinates of \(\mathbb R^9\) by \((u,r)\in\mathcal Q^2\), and choose \(0<\epsilon<1\).  Set
\[
 V=(1-\epsilon)I_9+\frac{\epsilon}{9}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Every entry of \(V\) is positive, every column sums to one, and \(V\) is invertible: its eigenvalue on \(\operatorname{span}(\mathbf1)\) is \(1\), while its eigenvalue on the zero-sum subspace is \(1-\epsilon\).  For predecessor state \(u\in\mathcal Q\), let \(L_{2,u}\) consist of the three columns of \(V\) indexed by \((u,r)\), \(r\in\mathcal Q\).  Each \(L_{2,u}\) has column rank three because it is a subfamily of the columns of the invertible matrix \(V\).

The nine columns of the resulting triangular-view matrix are
\[
 \ell_u\otimes V[:,(u,r)],
 \qquad (u,r)\in\mathcal Q^2,
\]
where \(\ell_u\) is column \(u\) of \(L_{1,\varnothing}\).  Applying the invertible row transformation \(I_3\otimes V^{-1}\) sends these columns to
\[
 \ell_u\otimes e_{(u,r)}.
\]
These nine vectors are linearly independent because their second tensor factors are distinct coordinate vectors and every \(\ell_u\neq0\).  Hence \(\operatorname{rank}(M_a)=9\) despite \(c_{1,\varnothing}=0\).  The graph with first item parent \(H_1\) and second item parents \(H_1,H_2\) is triangular in the order \((H_1,H_2)\), so the construction is a valid triangular-view witness.  It also shows that strict positivity and ordinary column separation do not restore the failed converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-latent-state-lower-bound]\label{lem:finite-latent-state-lower-bound}
For every \((G,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cert}}^{(q)}(K,J)\) with heterogeneous finite observed alphabets \(m_j\ge q\), \(\operatorname{rank}(T_{12})=q^K\).  If the same \(P_X\) has any finite latent measurement representation with a joint latent variable \(Z\) having \(R=|\operatorname{supp}(Z)|\) positive-mass states and with the observed coordinates conditionally independent given \(Z\), then \(R\ge q^K\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(r=q^K\).  Membership in the certified class and Assumption~\textnormal{ass:certificate} give \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0\).  Theorem~\textnormal{thm:certificate-frontier} therefore makes each rectangular matrix \(M_a\) have column rank \(r\).  Hence there are left inverses \(L_a^{\dagger}\) satisfying \(L_a^{\dagger}M_a=I_r\).  Assumption~\textnormal{ass:positive-support} makes \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\) invertible.  Using the observed factorization \(T_{12}=M_1DM_2^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
 L_1^{\dagger}T_{12}(L_2^{\dagger})^{\mathsf T}=D.
\]
Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}(T_{12})\ge\operatorname{rank}(D)=r\), while the displayed factorization gives \(\operatorname{rank}(T_{12})\le r\).  Thus
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(T_{12})=q^K.
\tag{10}
\]

Now let \(Z\) be the joint latent variable in an arbitrary finite latent measurement representation of the same observed law, after discarding any zero-mass states, and write \(R=|\operatorname{supp}(Z)|\).  Conditional independence of the observed coordinates given \(Z\) gives
\[
 T_{12}=\widetilde M_1\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde\pi)
 \widetilde M_2^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \widetilde M_a\in\mathbb R^{n_a\times R}.
\]
The image of this product is contained in the image of \(\widetilde M_1\), whose dimension is at most \(R\).  Combining this fact with (10) yields \(q^K\le R\), as claimed.  No triangularity, alphabet restriction, positivity of measurement cells, or coordinate-product structure was imposed on the competing representation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The observed two-view rank rules out every finite latent measurement representation with fewer than \(q^K\) joint states, not only smaller \(q\)-state coordinate products. \textit{Gap.} Coordinate-count comparisons alone do not cover competing latent variables with arbitrary finite state spaces. \textit{Consumer.} Model-selection software obtains a representation-independent lower bound before imposing a coordinate factorization.

\section{Interpretation}

For heterogeneous responses, \(\Delta_{\mathrm{DT}}\neq0\) is a finite, checkable sufficient certificate, not a necessary characterization of global view rank.  The exact frontier survives when the selected alphabets all have size \(q\), where \(c_{a,k,u}=d_{a,k,u}^2\) and \(\Delta_a=\det(M_a)^2\).  Larger downstream alphabets can encode predecessor context and compensate for a singular earlier local table; the explicit positive construction with \(m_{s(a,1)}=3\) and \(m_{s(a,2)}=9\) proves this is a structural phenomenon rather than a boundary artifact.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The population algorithm assumes exact access to \(P_X\) and exact-real equality, rank, discriminant, and conditional-independence queries.  It permits zero measurement cells and requires positivity only of latent weights.  Finite-sample soundness cannot be obtained by arbitrary thresholding near intersecting rank varieties; multiplicity-calibrated testing under a declared separation margin is the sole remaining open question.

\section{Honest scope}

For \(q\ge3\) and finite observed alphabets \(m_j\ge q\), the proved population results are rectangular certificate sufficiency, open-dense/full-measure genericity, exact three-view and coordinate-aligned quotient identification, the \(q^K\) lower bound for every finite latent measurement representation, \(K_{\min}(P_X)=K\), and a terminating sound-and-complete exact algorithm over the maximal same-\(K\) comparison domain.  The determinant product is necessary and sufficient only when every selected alphabet has size \(q\); the note explicitly disproves the unrestricted heterogeneous converse.  The existing dense \(q=3,K=3,J=8\) no-pure-child witness remains within the equal-alphabet subfamily.  Unequal latent-coordinate cardinalities, observed alphabets smaller than \(q\), observed-to-observed edges, orientation beyond the latent MEC, and finite-sample multiplicity calibration are outside the proved scope.
