%%% FIELD phase-diagram-statement
There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on \((\ell,u)\), for which the following three-regime description holds uniformly over all \(n\geq4\) and \(1\leq B\leq\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), with an empty displayed integer range omitted:
\[
\Psi^{=}_{n,B}\asymp_{\ell,u}
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{B}{n}\bigl\{(n+1)^{1/B}-1\bigr\},
&1\leq B\leq\lfloor\log(n+1)\rfloor,\\[6pt]
\displaystyle \frac{\log(n+1)}{n},
&\lceil\log(n+1)\rceil\leq B\leq
 \lfloor\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}\rfloor,\\[6pt]
\displaystyle \left(\frac{B}{n}\right)^2,
&\lceil\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}\rceil\leq B\leq\lfloor n/4\rfloor.
\end{cases}
\]
Here \(X\asymp_{\ell,u}Y\) means \(cY\leq X\leq CY\). Moreover,
\[
cL_{n,B}\leq\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}\leq CL_{n,B},
\qquad
\Psi_\infty(n)\asymp_{\ell,u}\frac{\log(n+1)}{n},
\]
and there is a constant \(A_0>1\), depending only on \((\ell,u)\), such that every fixed \(A\geq A_0\) satisfies
\[
B_{\mathrm{sat}}(n,A)\asymp_{\ell,u,A}\log(n+1).
\]
Thus useful updates saturate at order \(\log n\), whereas the harm from compulsory exact batching becomes rate-dominant only at order \(\sqrt{n\log n}\). No conclusion for the literal choice \(A=2\) is asserted. Finally, if \(n=4B\) and \([\ell,u]^2\) contains \((\sigma_1,\sigma_0)=(2,1)\), then \(\Psi^{=}_{n,B}\geq1/3\).

%%% FIELD phase-diagram-proof
Put \(h=\log(n+1)\), and put \(q_{n,B}=(B/n)^2\). By the exact-batch frontier theorem and the definition of \(R_{n,B}\), there are constants \(c_0,C_0>0\), depending only on \((\ell,u)\), such that
\[
c_0\{L_{n,B}+q_{n,B}\}
\leq \Psi^{=}_{n,B}
\leq C_0\{L_{n,B}+q_{n,B}\}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Since \((n+1)^{1/B}=e^{h/B}\),
\[
L_{n,B}=\frac{B}{n}\{e^{h/B}-1\}.               \tag{2}
\]

First suppose \(B\leq h\). The elementary inequality \(e^x-1\geq x\), applied at \(x=h/B\), gives
\[
L_{n,B}\geq \frac{h}{n}.                         \tag{3}
\]
For \(n\geq4\), the tangent inequality \(\log x\leq x-1\) gives \(h\leq n\); hence \(B\leq h\) implies
\[
q_{n,B}=\frac{B^2}{n^2}leq\frac{h^2}{n^2}leq\frac{h}{n}leq L_{n,B}. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1) and (4) prove the first regime.

Now suppose \(B\geq h\), and set \(x=h/B\in(0,1]\). Convexity of the exponential function on \([0,1]\), together with its tangent inequality at zero, yields
\[
x\leq e^x-1\leq(e-1)x.
\]
Using (2),
\[
\frac{h}{n}\leq L_{n,B}\leq(e-1)\frac{h}{n}.     \tag{5}
\]
If \(B^2\leq nh\), then \(q_{n,B}\leq h/n\), and (1) and (5) give
\(\Psi^{=}_{n,B}\asymp_{\ell,u}h/n\). If \(B^2\geq nh\), then \(q_{n,B}\geq h/n\), and (1) and (5) give
\(\Psi^{=}_{n,B}\asymp_{\ell,u}q_{n,B}\). The stated floors and ceilings are exactly the integer forms of \(B\leq h\), \(h\leq B\leq\sqrt{nh}\), and \(B\geq\sqrt{nh}\). At an integer endpoint the adjacent formulae are comparable by (3) or (5), and empty ranges for small \(n\) require no assertion. This proves the three-regime display uniformly, including all feasible small \(n\) after enlargement of the same constants.

For the at-most frontier, define \(g(x)=x(e^{h/x}-1)\) for \(x>0\). With \(y=h/x>0\), differentiation gives
\[
g'(x)=e^y(1-y)-1<0,                               \tag{6}
\]
because the derivative of \(y\mapsto e^y(1-y)\) is \(-ye^y<0\), and its value at zero is one. Thus \(b\mapsto L_{n,b}=g(b)/n\) is decreasing. From (1), the definition of the at-most frontier, and nonnegativity of \(q_{n,b}\),
\[
\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}
=\min_{1\leq b\leq B}\Psi^{=}_{n,b}
\geq c_0\min_{1\leq b\leq B}L_{n,b}
=c_0L_{n,B}.                                      \tag{7}
\]
For the reverse bound let \(b_* =\min\{B,\lceil h\rceil\}\). If \(B\leq h\), then \(b_*=B\), and (4) and (1) give
\(\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}\leq 2C_0L_{n,B}\). If \(B\geq\lceil h\rceil\), then \(b_*=\lceil h\rceil\leq h+1\). Since \(h\geq\log5>1\) and \(h\leq n\), (5), now at \(b_*\), gives
\[
R_{n,b_*}
\leq(e-1)\frac{h}{n}+\frac{(h+1)^2}{n^2}
\leq C_1\frac{h}{n}
\leq C_1L_{n,B}.                                  \tag{8}
\]
Therefore \(\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}\leq C_0C_1L_{n,B}\). Together with (7), this proves the envelope bound.

Let \(N=\lfloor n/4\rfloor\). For \(n\geq20\), the inequality \(N\geq n/4-1\geq\log(n+1)=h\) follows by checking it at \(n=20\) and observing that the derivative of \(x/4-1-\log(x+1)\) is positive for \(x\geq20\). Thus (5) yields \(L_{n,N}\asymp h/n\); the finitely many remaining \(4\leq n<20\) are absorbed into fixed positive constants. Applying (7) at \(B=N\), and applying (8) with the feasible index \(b_*=\lceil h\rceil\) for \(n\geq20\), gives
\[
\Psi_\infty(n)=\Psi^{\leq}_{n,N}\asymp_{\ell,u}\frac{h}{n}. \tag{9}
\]

We next justify the saturation assertion without claiming a factor-two result. Define
\[
\phi(x)=\frac{e^x-1}{x},\qquad x>0.
\]
Its derivative has numerator \(e^x(x-1)+1\), whose derivative is \(xe^x>0\) and whose value at zero is zero. Hence \(\phi\) is increasing and \(\phi(x)\to\infty\). By (2),
\[
\frac{L_{n,B}}{h/n}=\phi(h/B).                    \tag{10}
\]
Let \(K<\infty\) be an upper constant in (9). For any fixed \(A>1\), choose \(\kappa_A\in(0,1)\) so small that
\(c_0\phi(1/\kappa_A)>AK\). If \(B\leq\kappa_Ah\), then (1), (9), and (10) imply
\[
\Psi^{=}_{n,B}\geq c_0L_{n,B}>AK\frac{h}{n}\geq A\Psi_\infty(n). \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(B_{\mathrm{sat}}(n,A)\geq\kappa_Ah\), up to an immaterial integer adjustment. Conversely, (1) and (8) give
\(\Psi^{=}_{n,\lceil h\rceil}\leq K'h/n\), while (9) gives
\(\Psi_\infty(n)\geq kh/n\) for some \(k>0\). Therefore, with
\(A_0=\max\{2,K'/k\}\), every fixed \(A\geq A_0\) obeys
\[
B_{\mathrm{sat}}(n,A)\leq\lceil h\rceil.          \tag{12}
\]
Equations (11) and (12) prove the stated order. They deliberately do not cover \(A=2\) unless the unknown constants happen to permit it.

Finally assume \(n=4B\) and that the variance box contains \((2,1)\). Membership in the exact-batch design class forces \(m_b=4\) for every \(b\), hence \(t_b=1/B\), while exact-count overlap forces \(p_b\in\{1/4,1/2,3/4\}\) pathwise. Direct calculation from the oracle-loss definition gives
\[
\min_{p\in\{1/4,1/2,3/4\}} f_{(2,1)}(p)
=\min\left\{\frac{52}{3},10,\frac{28}{3}\right\}
=\frac{28}{3},
\qquad V^*(2,1)=9.                                \tag{13}
\]
The risk decomposition, rearranged using
\(\sum_b(w_b-t_b)^2/t_b=\sum_bw_b^2/t_b-1\), therefore yields for every admissible design
\[
\mathcal R_{n,B}(d;\mu,(2,1))
=B\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b^2\,
  \mathbb E[f_{(2,1)}(p_b)]-9
\geq\frac{28}{3}B\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b^2-9
\geq\frac{28}{3}-9=\frac13,                       \tag{14}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(\sum_bw_b=1\) and the identity
\[
B\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b^2-\left(\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b\right)^2
=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq B}(w_a-w_b)^2\geq0.
\]
Taking \(\mu=(0,0)\in[-M,M]^{\mathcal A}\), then taking the supremum over the parameter box and the infimum over designs, proves the endpoint witness.

%%% FIELD envelope-target-proof
Let \(h=\log(n+1)\) and \(q_{n,b}=(b/n)^2\). The exact-batch frontier theorem and the definition of \(R_{n,b}\) provide constants \(c_0,C_0>0\), depending only on \((\ell,u)\), such that
\[
c_0\{L_{n,b}+q_{n,b}\}\leq\Psi^{=}_{n,b}
\leq C_0\{L_{n,b}+q_{n,b}\}                       \tag{1}
\]
at every feasible integer \(b\). Define \(g(x)=x(e^{h/x}-1)\) for \(x>0\). If \(y=h/x\), then
\[
g'(x)=e^y(1-y)-1<0,                                 \tag{2}
\]
because \(d\{e^y(1-y)\}/dy=-ye^y<0\) and \(e^0(1-0)=1\). Hence \(b\mapsto L_{n,b}=g(b)/n\) decreases. It follows from (1) and the definition of \(\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}\) that
\[
\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}
=\min_{1\leq b\leq B}\Psi^{=}_{n,b}
\geq c_0\min_{1\leq b\leq B}L_{n,b}
=c_0L_{n,B}.                                        \tag{3}
\]

For the upper bound put \(b_*=\min\{B,\lceil h\rceil\}\). The inequality \(e^x-1\geq x\) gives \(L_{n,B}\geq h/n\). If \(B\leq h\), then \(b_*=B\), and, since the tangent inequality \(\log x\leq x-1\) gives \(h\leq n\),
\[
q_{n,B}\leq\frac{h^2}{n^2}\leq\frac{h}{n}\leq L_{n,B}. \tag{4}
\]
Thus (1) gives \(\Psi^{\leq}_{n,B}\leq2C_0L_{n,B}\). If \(B\geq\lceil h\rceil\), then \(b_*=\lceil h\rceil\). For \(0\leq x\leq1\), convexity of the exponential gives
\(x\leq e^x-1\leq(e-1)x\). Because \(h/b_*\leq1\), \(h\geq\log5>1\), and \(h\leq n\),
\[
R_{n,b_*}
\leq(e-1)\frac{h}{n}+\frac{(h+1)^2}{n^2}
\leq C_1\frac{h}{n}
\leq C_1L_{n,B}.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Equations (1) and (5) give the desired upper bound.

With \(N=\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), one has \(N\geq n/4-1\geq h\) for \(n\geq20\): the second inequality holds at \(n=20\), and the derivative of \(x/4-1-\log(x+1)\) is positive thereafter. Hence
\[
\frac{h}{n}\leq L_{n,N}\leq(e-1)\frac{h}{n}.       \tag{6}
\]
The finitely many remaining \(n\geq4\) are absorbed by fixed positive constants. Applying (3) at \(B=N\), and using the feasible choice \(b_*=\lceil h\rceil\) in (5), proves
\[
\Psi_\infty(n)=\Psi^{\leq}_{n,N}
\asymp_{\ell,u}\frac{\log(n+1)}{n}
\asymp_{\ell,u}\frac{\log n}{n}.                  \tag{7}
\]

For saturation set \(\phi(x)=(e^x-1)/x\). Its derivative has numerator \(e^x(x-1)+1\), which is positive for \(x>0\) because its derivative is \(xe^x>0\) and its value at zero is zero. Thus \(\phi\) increases to infinity, and
\[
\frac{L_{n,B}}{h/n}=\phi(h/B).                       \tag{8}
\]
Let \(K\) be an upper constant in (7). For fixed \(A>1\), choose \(\kappa_A\in(0,1)\) with \(c_0\phi(1/\kappa_A)>AK\). If \(B\leq\kappa_Ah\), then (1), (7), and (8) imply
\[
\Psi^{=}_{n,B}>A\Psi_\infty(n),                    \tag{9}
\]
so \(B_{\mathrm{sat}}(n,A)\geq\kappa_Ah\), up to integer adjustment. On the other hand, (1) and (5) imply
\(\Psi^{=}_{n,\lceil h\rceil}\leq K'h/n\), whereas (7) implies
\(\Psi_\infty(n)\geq kh/n\). Therefore every fixed
\(A\geq A_0:=\max\{2,K'/k\}\) satisfies
\[
B_{\mathrm{sat}}(n,A)\leq\lceil h\rceil.           \tag{10}
\]
This proves the claimed saturation order for every sufficiently large fixed comparison factor, without asserting the literal factor-two case.

It remains to verify the endpoint witness. If \(n=4B\), the positive-size constraint \(m_b\geq4\) and \(\sum_bm_b=n\) force \(m_b=4\) and \(t_b=1/B\) for every batch. At \((\sigma_1,\sigma_0)=(2,1)\), exact-count overlap gives \(p_b\in\{1/4,1/2,3/4\}\), and the oracle-loss definition gives
\[
\min_{p\in\{1/4,1/2,3/4\}}f_{(2,1)}(p)
=\min\left\{\frac{52}{3},10,\frac{28}{3}\right\}
=\frac{28}{3},
\qquad V^*(2,1)=9.                                  \tag{11}
\]
Rearranging the risk decomposition therefore gives, for every admissible \(d\),
\[
\mathcal R_{n,B}(d;\mu,(2,1))
=B\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b^2\mathbb E[f_{(2,1)}(p_b)]-9
\geq\frac{28}{3}B\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b^2-9
\geq\frac13,                                        \tag{12}
\]
where \(\sum_bw_b=1\) and
\[
B\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b^2-\left(\sum_{b=1}^Bw_b\right)^2
=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq B}(w_a-w_b)^2\geq0
\]
give \(B\sum_bw_b^2\geq1\). Taking \(\mu=(0,0)\in[-M,M]^{\mathcal A}\), then taking the supremum over the parameter box and the infimum over \(d\in\mathfrak D_{n,B}\), proves \(\Psi^{=}_{n,B}\geq1/3\).
