Put \(q=1/3\) and \(u=2/3\).  By the left-extension convention, \(p^{\mathrm{ext}}_{-1}=p^{\mathrm{ext}}_0=0\).  Therefore Definition \(\mathrm{def:mean\text{-}design}\), specialized to \(r=0\), \(L=2\), \(T=4\), and the schedule in the statement, gives
\[
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&q&0&0\\
1&q&q&0\\
1&u&q&q\\
1&1&u&q
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtracting the first row from each later row and expanding along the first column yields
\[
\det(X)
=\det\!\begin{pmatrix}
0&q&0\\
u-q&q&q\\
1-q&u&q
\end{pmatrix}
=q^2(1-u)=\frac{1}{27}>0.
\]
Thus \(X\) and \(X^{\mathsf T}\) are invertible.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
X^{\mathsf T}(-3,0,3,0)^{\mathsf T}
=(0,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}=c.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:unbiased\text{-}representers}\) consequently gives
\(\mathcal W_c(p)=\{w\}\), where \(w=(-3,0,3,0)^{\mathsf T}\).  Since the feasible set in Definition \(\mathrm{def:oracle\text{-}blue}\) is this singleton, its minimum-risk, minimum-Euclidean-norm member is \(w_\Gamma=w\), including when \(\Gamma\) is singular.

Fix any deterministic signs \(x_i,y_i\in\{-1,1\}\), and set
\[
a_{i,0}=-x_i,\qquad b_{i,0}=x_i,\qquad b_{i,1}=0,
\qquad b_{i,2}=y_i.
\tag{1}
\]
The signs and hence these coefficients are fixed before randomization, as required by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}secular\text{-}trend}\) gives \(g_{i,t}=-x_i\), and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:additive\text{-}distributed\text{-}lag}\) then gives, for every history \(z\),
\[
Y_{i,t}(z)=-x_i+x_i z_t+y_i z_{t-2}.
\tag{2}
\]
The displayed schedule is nondecreasing, begins at \(p_0=0\), and ends at \(p_4=1\), so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:irreversible\text{-}endpoints}\) holds.

We next enumerate the observable path support exactly.  The assignment definition uses the weak inequality \(Z_{i,s}=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le p_s^{\mathrm{ext}}\}\).  Thus \(U_i=0\) produces an exceptional prehistory with \(Z_{i,s}=1\) even for \(s\le0\); this event has probability zero under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nested\text{-}hash\text{-}randomization}\).  Substitution in (2), including this null path rather than silently discarding it, gives
\[
Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=
\begin{cases}
(y_i,y_i,y_i,y_i)^{\mathsf T},&U_i=0,\\
(0,0,y_i,y_i)^{\mathsf T},&0<U_i\le1/3,\\
(-x_i,-x_i,0,0)^{\mathsf T},&1/3<U_i\le2/3,\\
(-x_i,-x_i,-x_i,0)^{\mathsf T},&2/3<U_i\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
These are all histories generated as a score ranges over \([0,1]\).  Every coordinate in (3) has absolute value at most one, and the first path has a coordinate of absolute value one.  Hence the definition of the primitive envelope gives \(B_i=1\).  It follows, for every \(n\), that
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}B_i^4=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_n}B_i^2=n>0,
\qquad
\frac{\max_{i\in I_n}B_i^2}{\sum_{j\in I_n}B_j^2}
=\frac1n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:finite\text{-}population\text{-}fourth\text{-}moment}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:max\text{-}unit\text{-}lindeberg}\) hold, with \(C_4=1\).  Equations (1)--(2), together with the stipulated construction of \(g\) and \(Y\), also verify the two structural assumptions for every member of \(\mathcal C_n\).  The only experiment randomness remains the vector \(U\).

Let \(H_i=w^{\mathsf T}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\).  From (3),
\[
H_i=
\begin{cases}
0,&U_i=0,\\
3y_i,&0<U_i\le1/3,\\
3x_i,&1/3<U_i\le2/3,\\
0,&2/3<U_i\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
Each positive-probability interval in (5) has probability \(1/3\).  Therefore the independent-uniform assignment assumption implies
\[
\mathbb E_U(H_i)=x_i+y_i=\sum_{\ell=0}^2b_{i,\ell},
\tag{6}
\]
and, since \(x_i^2=y_i^2=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_U(H_i)
&=\mathbb E_U(H_i^2)-\{\mathbb E_U(H_i)\}^2\\
&=3y_i^2+3x_i^2-(x_i+y_i)^2
=4-2x_i y_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(M=n^{-1}\sum_iY_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\), equations (5)--(6) show directly that
\[
\mathbb E_U(w^{\mathsf T}M)
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}(x_i+y_i)
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}\sum_{\ell=0}^2b_{i,\ell}
=\theta.
\tag{8}
\]
This proves design unbiasedness for the fixed finite-population causal contrast, rather than defining that contrast through the estimator.

The coordinates \(U_i\) are independent and the potential outcomes are fixed, so \(H_i\) and \(H_j\) have zero design covariance for \(i\ne j\).  By (7), Definition \(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}covariance}\), and \(w_\Gamma=w\),
\[
R_\Gamma
=\operatorname{Var}_U(w^{\mathsf T}M)
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i\in I_n}(4-2x_i y_i).
\tag{9}
\]
Writing \(\bar\xi_n=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i y_i\), equation (9) becomes
\[
nR_\Gamma=4-2\bar\xi_n.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(-1\le\bar\xi_n\le1\), one has \(2\le nR_\Gamma\le6\) for every sign array and every \(n\).  Hence every triangular sequence from \(\mathcal C\) satisfies
\[
0<2\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}nR_\Gamma
\le\limsup_{n\to\infty}nR_\Gamma\le6<\infty,
\]
which verifies Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}oracle\text{-}risk}\).

It remains to prove the uniform impossibility.  The following distributions average over fixed sign arrays only for the minimax contradiction; they do not alter the design, whose sole randomness remains \(U\).  For \(\rho\in\{0,1/2\}\), let \(Q_\rho\) make the pairs \((x_i,y_i)\) independent over units with
\[
Q_\rho\{x_i=x,y_i=y\}=\frac{1+\rho xy}{4},
\qquad (x,y)\in\{-1,1\}^2.
\tag{11}
\]
The four probabilities in (11) are nonnegative and sum to one.  Under either \(Q_\rho\), each marginal sign is uniform, while
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_\rho}(x_i y_i)=\rho.
\tag{12}
\]
Conditional on any value of \(U_i\), equation (3) reveals either only \(y_i\), only \(x_i\), or neither; it never reveals their product or both signs.  This assertion includes the threshold points and the null point \(U_i=0\).  Since the two mixtures have the same uniform marginal law for each sign, \(U_i\) is independent of the signs, and \(Z_i\) is a deterministic function of \((U_i,p)\), the joint law of \((Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}},U_i,Z_i)\) is identical under \(Q_0\) and \(Q_{1/2}\).  Independence across units then makes the full observed-data law of \((Y^{\mathrm{obs}},U,Z,p)\) identical under the two mixtures for every \(n\).  Consequently every statistic measurable with respect to those data, including \(n\widehat V\), has the same distribution under the two mixtures.

By independence in (11), (12), and \(|x_i y_i|=1\),
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_\rho}\!\left[(\bar\xi_n-\rho)^2\right]
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}_{Q_\rho}(x_i y_i)
\le\frac1n.
\tag{13}
\]
For every \(\delta>0\), the pointwise inequality
\(\delta^2\mathbf 1\{|\bar\xi_n-\rho|>\delta\}\le
(\bar\xi_n-\rho)^2\), followed by expectations and (13), gives
\[
Q_\rho\{|\bar\xi_n-\rho|>\delta\}
\le\frac1{n\delta^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (10) and (14) imply that \(nR_\Gamma\) converges in \(Q_0\)-probability to \(4\), but in \(Q_{1/2}\)-probability to \(3\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a nonnegative observed-data-measurable statistic \(\widehat V\) obeyed the uniform ratio-consistency display in the theorem.  For either \(\rho\), averaging the design-randomization error probability over the fixed arrays under \(Q_\rho\) gives
\[
(Q_\rho\otimes\mathbb P_U)
\!\left\{\left|\frac{\widehat V}{R_\Gamma}-1\right|>\epsilon\right\}
\le
\sup_{(x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_n,y_n)\in\{-1,1\}^{2n}}
\mathbb P_U
\!\left\{\left|\frac{\widehat V}{R_\Gamma}-1\right|>\epsilon\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
The denominator in (15) is positive by \(2\le nR_\Gamma\).  Moreover,
\[
|n\widehat V-nR_\Gamma|
=nR_\Gamma\left|\frac{\widehat V}{R_\Gamma}-1\right|
\le6\left|\frac{\widehat V}{R_\Gamma}-1\right|.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus (15)--(16), followed by the triangle inequality and the two limits after (14), imply
\[
n\widehat V\xrightarrow[Q_0\otimes\mathbb P_U]{\mathbb P}4,
\qquad
n\widehat V\xrightarrow[Q_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb P_U]{\mathbb P}3.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular,
\[
(Q_0\otimes\mathbb P_U)\{n\widehat V>7/2\}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
(Q_{1/2}\otimes\mathbb P_U)\{n\widehat V>7/2\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{18}
\]
But the identical observed-data laws proved above force the two probabilities in (18) to be equal for every \(n\), a contradiction.  Therefore no uniformly ratio-consistent \(\widehat V\) exists.  An admissible output from Definition \(\mathrm{def:hajek\text{-}inference\text{-}handle}\) is observed-data measurable, and variance-ratio consistency is one of the four conclusions demanded simultaneously.  Its impossibility alone therefore proves the final assertion, without assuming or needing to refute a pointwise central limit theorem.

Finally, the smallest \(n=1\) instance is already fully stress-tested by (3)--(7): every score region, including all boundary points, is enumerated; \(w^{\mathsf T}M=H_1\) has exact mean \(x_1+y_1=\theta\) and exact variance \(4-2x_1y_1\).  Thus neither estimator bias nor an omitted endpoint history drives the obstruction; the obstruction is the unobservability, uniformly over fixed populations, of the cross-sign product determining the oracle risk.
