Fix \(T\ge\max\{2,L+1\}\) and a schedule with panel endpoint overlap.  All potential outcomes and coefficients are fixed by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\), and the only randomness below is therefore the vector \(U\).  By Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nested\text{-}hash\text{-}randomization}\),
\[
 \mathbb P_U(A_i=1)=p_1,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_U(A_i=0)=1-p_1,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_U(C_i=1)=1-p_{T-1}.
 \tag{7}
\]
These probabilities are positive by Definition \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}endpoint\text{-}overlap}\).

At stage \(1\), every lagged threshold with index at most zero equals zero.  Under the weak-threshold convention \(Z_{i,s}=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le p_s^{\mathrm{ext}}\}\), the only exceptional score is \(U_i=0\), which has probability zero under the continuous uniform law.  Hence Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:additive\text{-}distributed\text{-}lag}\) gives, almost surely,
\[
 Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,1}
 =\begin{cases}
 g_{i,1}+b_{i,0},&A_i=1,\\
 g_{i,1},&A_i=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Using (7), fixedness of \(g_{i,1}\) and \(b_{i,0}\), and positivity of the denominators,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_U\left[
 \frac{A_iY^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,1}}{p_1}
 -\frac{(1-A_i)Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,1}}{1-p_1}
 \right]
 &=(g_{i,1}+b_{i,0})-g_{i,1}\\
 &=b_{i,0}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
This establishes the theorem immediately when \(L=0\): averaging (8) over \(i\) gives \(\mathbb E_U[\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{panel}}]=n^{-1}\sum_i b_{i,0}=\theta\).

Now suppose \(L\ge1\).  Since \(T\ge L+1\), every relevant final-stage index satisfies \(T-\ell\ge1\).  If \(A_i=1\), then monotonicity from Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:irreversible\text{-}endpoints}\) implies
\[
 U_i\le p_1\le p_{T-\ell}
 \qquad(0\le\ell\le L),
\]
so all \(L+1\) final-stage exposures equal one.  If \(C_i=1\), then \(U_i>p_{T-1}\ge p_{T-\ell}\) for \(1\le\ell\le L\), whereas \(Z_{i,T}=1\) because \(p_T=1\).  The additive distributed-lag formula therefore yields
\[
 Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,T}
 =g_{i,T}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}b_{i,\ell}
 \quad\text{on }\{A_i=1\},
 \qquad
 Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,T}=g_{i,T}+b_{i,0}
 \quad\text{on }\{C_i=1\}.
\]
Together with (7), this gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_U\left[
 \frac{A_iY^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,T}}{p_1}
 -\frac{C_iY^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,T}}{1-p_{T-1}}
 \right]
 &=\left(g_{i,T}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}b_{i,\ell}\right)
   -(g_{i,T}+b_{i,0})\\
 &=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}b_{i,\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Adding (8) and (9), summing over units, and applying the definition of \(\theta\) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_U[\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{panel}}]
 =\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}b_{i,\ell}
 =\theta.
\]
The estimator is an explicit function of \((Y^{\mathrm{obs}},U,p)\), hence is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(Y^{\mathrm{obs}},U,Z,p)\).  Its denominators are positive and, for a fixed finite population, each summand takes only finitely many finite values.  It is therefore integrable and belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{panel}}(p)\).

For the risk identity, write \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{panel}}=n^{-1}\sum_i\psi_i(U_i)\), with the appropriate piecewise summand from Definition \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}ht\text{-}estimator}\).  The additive own-unit exposure model makes \(\psi_i(U_i)\) a function only of \(U_i\) and fixed unit \(i\) quantities.  Independence of the coordinates of \(U\) therefore gives independence across units, while (8)--(9) give \(\mathbb E_U[\psi_i(U_i)]=\theta_i\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{panel}})
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i\in I_n}\operatorname{Var}_U(\psi_i(U_i))\\
 &=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i\in I_n}
 \left\{\int_0^1\psi_i(u)^2\,du-\theta_i^2\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The single function \(\psi_i(u)\) contains both stage contributions, so no independence between repeated stages has been asserted.

It remains to prove sharpness over all observed-panel rules.  First let \(L\ge1\) and \(T\le L\).  Consider two fixed finite populations.  In the first, set every \(g_{i,t}=0\) and every \(b_{i,\ell}=0\).  In the second, keep \(g_{i,t}=0\), set \(b_{i,L}=1\), and set every other \(b_{i,\ell}=0\).  For each measured \(t\le T\), we have \(t-L\le0\), so \(p_{t-L}^{\mathrm{ext}}=0\) and
\[
 Z_{i,t-L}=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le0\}=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Thus \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\) is the zero panel almost surely under both populations.  The joint law of \((Y^{\mathrm{obs}},U,Z,p)\) is identical, but the two values of \(\theta\) are zero and one.  No integrable statistic measurable in these data can have expectation equal to both values, so \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{panel}}(p)=\varnothing\) for every endpoint schedule at such \(T\).

Finally let \(L=0\) and \(T=1\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:irreversible\text{-}endpoints}\) forces \(p_1=1\), hence \(Z_{i,1}=1\) surely.  Compare the all-zero population with the population having \(g_{i,1}=-1\) and \(b_{i,0}=1\) for every unit.  The observed outcomes are again identically zero under both populations, while \(\theta\) is respectively zero and one.  Therefore \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{panel}}(p)=\varnothing\) when \((L,T)=(0,1)\).

If \(L=0\), every positive-integer deficit below \(\max\{2,L+1\}=2\) is the last case.  If \(L\ge1\), then \(\max\{2,L+1\}=L+1\), and every deficit satisfies \(T\le L\), the preceding case.  Conversely, at \(T=\max\{2,L+1\}\) an endpoint-overlap schedule exists, for example \(p_t=t/T\), and the constructed estimator is unbiased.  The two bounds prove the claimed frontier.
