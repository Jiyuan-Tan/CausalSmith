Put \(q=1/3\) and \(u=2/3\).  The endpoint convention gives \(p^{\mathrm{ext}}_{-1}=p^{\mathrm{ext}}_0=0\).  Hence Definition \(\mathrm{def:mean\text{-}design}\), with \(r=0\), \(L=2\), and the displayed schedule, gives
\[
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&q&0&0\\
1&q&q&0\\
1&u&q&q\\
1&1&u&q
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtracting the first row from the other three and expanding along the first column yields
\[
\det(X)=
\det\!\begin{pmatrix}
0&q&0\\
u-q&q&q\\
1-q&u&q
\end{pmatrix}
=q^2(1-u)=\frac1{27}>0.
\]
Thus \(X^{\mathsf T}\) is invertible.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
X^{\mathsf T}(-3,0,3,0)^{\mathsf T}
=(0,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}=c.
\]
By Definition \(\mathrm{def:unbiased\text{-}representers}\), it follows that
\(\mathcal W_c(p)=\{w\}\) with \(w=(-3,0,3,0)^{\mathsf T}\).  In particular, Definition \(\mathrm{def:oracle\text{-}blue}\) forces \(w_\Gamma=w\), regardless of whether \(\Gamma\) is singular.

We now construct the finite-population witness.  For arbitrary fixed signs \(x_i,y_i\in\{-1,1\}\), set
\[
a_{i,0}=-x_i,\qquad b_{i,0}=x_i,\qquad b_{i,1}=0,
\qquad b_{i,2}=y_i.
\]
The polynomial-trend assumption holds with \(g_{i,t}=-x_i\).  The additive distributed-lag assumption then gives, for every treatment history \(z\),
\[
Y_{i,t}(z)=-x_i+x_i z_t+y_i z_{t-2}.
\]
Because assignment uses a weak threshold inequality, the irreversible schedule has one null endpoint history at \(U_i=0\) in addition to three positive-probability histories.  Enumerating the full path support gives
\[
Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=
\begin{cases}
(y_i,y_i,y_i,y_i)^{\mathsf T},&U_i=0,\\
(0,0,y_i,y_i)^{\mathsf T},&0<U_i\le1/3,\\
(-x_i,-x_i,0,0)^{\mathsf T},&1/3<U_i\le2/3,\\
(-x_i,-x_i,-x_i,0)^{\mathsf T},&2/3<U_i\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
This enumeration also proves \(B_i=1\): each coordinate in every history in \(\mathcal Z(p)\) has absolute value at most one, and at least one coordinate has absolute value one.  It follows that
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}B_i^4=1,
\qquad
\frac{\max_{i\in I_n}B_i^2}{\sum_{j\in I_n}B_j^2}=\frac1n\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:finite\text{-}population\text{-}fourth\text{-}moment\) and \(\mathrm{ass:max\text{-}unit\text{-}lindeberg\) hold, with no superpopulation randomness in the experiment.

Let \(H_i=w^{\mathsf T}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) denote unit \(i\)'s contribution before averaging.  Equation (1) and \(w=(-3,0,3,0)^{\mathsf T}\) imply
\[
H_i=
\begin{cases}
0,&U_i=0,\\
3y_i,&0<U_i\le1/3,\\
3x_i,&1/3<U_i\le2/3,\\
0,&2/3<U_i\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
By the independent-uniform hash assumption,
\[
\mathbb E_U H_i=x_i+y_i=\sum_{\ell=0}^2b_{i,\ell},
\]
so \(w^{\mathsf T}M=n^{-1}\sum_iH_i\) is unbiased for the finite-population causal contrast \(\theta=n^{-1}\sum_i(x_i+y_i)\).  Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(H_i)
=3x_i^2+3y_i^2-(x_i+y_i)^2
=4-2x_i y_i.
\]
The scores are independent across units, so the fixed-potential-outcome and randomization assumptions give zero cross-unit covariances.  Using Definition \(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}covariance}\) and \(w_\Gamma=w\),
\[
R_\Gamma
=\operatorname{Var}_U(w^{\mathsf T}M)
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i\in I_n}(4-2x_i y_i),
\qquad
nR_\Gamma=4-2\bar\xi_n,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\bar\xi_n=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i y_i\).  Since \(-1\le\bar\xi_n\le1\), equation (2) proves \(2\le nR_\Gamma\le6\) for every \(n\) and every sign array.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}oracle\text{-}risk\) therefore holds along every triangular array in \(\mathcal C\).

It remains to prove that the oracle risk cannot be estimated uniformly from the observed panel.  This is a minimax argument over fixed finite populations; the following two distributions on the signs are used only to average a putative uniform guarantee.  They are not added to the experiment's sources of randomness.  For \(\rho\in\{0,1/2\}\), independently over units draw
\[
Q_\rho\{x_i=x,y_i=y\}=\frac{1+\rho xy}{4},
\qquad (x,y)\in\{-1,1\}^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Under either \(Q_\rho\), each of \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) is a uniform sign.  Conditional on \(U_i\), equation (1) reveals only \(y_i\) when \(U_i\le1/3\) and only \(x_i\) when \(U_i>1/3\); the exceptional formula at \(U_i=0\) also depends only on \(y_i\).  Because \(U_i\) is independent of the signs and \(Z_i\) is a deterministic function of \(U_i\), the joint law of \((Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}},U_i,Z_i)\) is therefore identical under \(Q_0\) and \(Q_{1/2}\).  Independence across units shows that the full observed-data law is identical under these two mixtures for every \(n\).  Consequently every statistic measurable with respect to \((Y^{\mathrm{obs}},U,Z,p)\), including \(n\widehat V\), has the same mixture distribution under \(Q_0\) and \(Q_{1/2}\).

On the other hand, (3) gives \(\mathbb E_{Q_\rho}(x_i y_i)=\rho\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_{Q_\rho}(x_i y_i)\le1\).  Independence therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_\rho}\bigl[(\bar\xi_n-\rho)^2\bigr]
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}_{Q_\rho}(x_i y_i)
\le\frac1n.
\]
For every \(\delta>0\), the pointwise inequality
\(\delta^2\mathbf 1\{|\bar\xi_n-\rho|>\delta\}\le(\bar\xi_n-\rho)^2\) thus implies
\[
Q_\rho\{|\bar\xi_n-\rho|>\delta\}\le\frac1{n\delta^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Combining this with (2), \(nR_\Gamma\) converges in \(Q_0\)-probability to \(4\) and in \(Q_{1/2}\)-probability to \(3\).

Suppose, for contradiction, that the displayed uniform ratio consistency in the theorem statement held.  Every deterministic sign array belongs to \(\mathcal C\), so averaging its design-randomization error probability under either distribution (3) preserves convergence to zero.  Since \(2\le nR_\Gamma\le6\),
\[
|n\widehat V-nR_\Gamma|
=nR_\Gamma\left|\frac{\widehat V}{R_\Gamma}-1\right|
\le6\left|\frac{\widehat V}{R_\Gamma}-1\right|.
\]
It follows that \(n\widehat V\) converges in probability to \(4\) under the \(Q_0\) mixture and to \(3\) under the \(Q_{1/2}\) mixture.  Hence
\[
Q_0\{n\widehat V>7/2\}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
Q_{1/2}\{n\widehat V>7/2\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This contradicts the equality of the two observed-data laws, under which the two displayed probabilities are equal for every \(n\).  Therefore no such uniformly ratio-consistent \(\widehat V\) exists.  Since variance-ratio consistency is one of the four jointly requested conclusions, no admissible quadruple can satisfy all four.  The four cases in (1), including the null endpoint path forced by the weak threshold inequality, are also the exact smallest-instance stress test: for \(n=1\), all possible assignment histories have been enumerated, the target weight remains unbiased, and the exact oracle variance is \(4-2x_1y_1\), even though the realized data almost surely never reveal the product \(x_1y_1\).
