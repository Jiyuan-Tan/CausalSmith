Let
\[
 T=\max\{r+2,L+1\},\qquad m=r+1,
 \qquad D=\binom{T+r}{r+1}.
\]
Then \(T\ge r+2\), \(T\ge L+1\), and \(D>0\).  For integers \(s\) and nonnegative integers \(k\), define the auxiliary sequence
\[
 F_k(s)=\mathbf 1\{s\ge1\}\binom{s+k-1}{k}.
\]
Writing \((\nabla f)(s)=f(s)-f(s-1)\), Pascal's identity, together with a separate check at \(s=1\), gives for every \(k\ge1\) and every integer \(s\),
\[
 \nabla F_k(s)=F_{k-1}(s).
\]
Indeed both sides vanish for \(s\le0\); at \(s=1\) both equal one; and for \(s\ge2\) the equality is
\[
 \binom{s+k-1}{k}-\binom{s+k-2}{k}
 =\binom{s+k-2}{k-1}.
\]
Iteration therefore yields
\[
 \nabla^{r+1}F_{r+1}(s)=F_0(s)=\mathbf 1\{s\ge1\}.
 \tag{1}
\]

For \(0\le t\le T\), the proposed schedule is \(p_t^\star=F_{r+1}(t)/D\).  Thus \(p_0^\star=0\), \(p_T^\star=1\), and, for \(1\le t\le T\), Pascal's identity gives
\[
 p_t^\star-p_{t-1}^\star
 =\frac{\binom{t+r-1}{r}}{D}>0.
\]
Hence the schedule obeys Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:irreversible\text{-}endpoints}\), with strict inequalities at all measured stages.

It remains to verify the representer equations.  Since \(T\ge r+2\), every index \(t=T-k\) with \(0\le k\le r+1\) belongs to \(J_T\).  For each \(0\le j\le r\), the definition of \(w^\star\) gives
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}w_t^\star t^j
 =D\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}(-1)^k\binom{r+1}{k}(T-k)^j
 =D\,\nabla^{r+1}[s\mapsto s^j](T)
 =0. \tag{2}
\]
The last equality follows directly by expanding \((s-1)^j\): one application of \(\nabla\) lowers the degree of a polynomial by at least one, so \(r+1\) applications annihilate every polynomial of degree at most \(r\).

Fix a lag \(0\le\ell\le L\).  Every argument \(T-\ell-k\) below is at most \(T\), and the left-extension convention agrees with \(F_{r+1}(s)/D\) when that argument is nonpositive.  Consequently, by (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}w_t^\star p_{t-\ell}^{\mathrm{ext}}
 &=\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}(-1)^k\binom{r+1}{k}F_{r+1}(T-\ell-k)\\
 &=\nabla^{r+1}F_{r+1}(T-\ell)\\
 &=F_0(T-\ell)=1,
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
where the final equality uses \(T-\ell\ge T-L\ge1\).  Equations (2) and (3) are exactly
\[
 X(p^\star)^{\mathsf T}w^\star
 =(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{r+1},
   \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{L+1})^{\mathsf T}=c.
\]
Thus the displayed design attains feasibility with \(T=\max\{r+2,L+1\}\).

For sharpness, let \(T<\max\{r+2,L+1\}\).  Since all quantities are integers, either \(T\le L\) or \(T\le r+1\).  If \(T\le L\), define \(h^{(L)}\in\mathbb R^{r+L+2}\) to have coefficient one on \(\beta_L\) and zero on every other coordinate.  For every \(t\in J_T\), \(t-L\le0\), so the endpoint convention gives \(p_{t-L}^{\mathrm{ext}}=0\).  Hence
\[
 Xh^{(L)}=0,
 \qquad c^{\mathsf T}h^{(L)}=1. \tag{4}
\]
If \(T\le r+1\), let \(q_p\) be the polynomial in Definition \(\mathrm{def:deficit\text{-}alias}\).  Lagrange interpolation at the distinct nodes \(1,\ldots,T\) gives
\[
 \deg(q_p)\le T-1\le r,
 \qquad q_p(t)=-p_t\quad(t\in J_T).
\]
Here every denominator \(s-u\) in the defining Lagrange basis is nonzero because its product ranges only over \(u\ne s\).  
Write \(q_p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}q_jx^j\), padding its coefficient vector by zeros through degree \(r\), and define
\[
 h^{(0)}=(q_0,\ldots,q_r,1,0,\ldots,0)^{\mathsf T},
\]
where the one is the \(\beta_0\) coordinate.  Then for every measured stage,
\[
 X_{t,\cdot}h^{(0)}=q_p(t)+p_t=0,
 \qquad c^{\mathsf T}h^{(0)}=1. \tag{5}
\]
Thus each possible support deficit has an explicit target-active null direction.  By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:row\text{-}space\text{-}certificate}\), (4) or (5) implies \(\mathcal W_c(p)=\varnothing\) for every schedule at that \(T\).  Combining this lower bound with the attaining construction proves
\[
 T_{\min}(r,L)=\max\{r+2,L+1\}.
\]
