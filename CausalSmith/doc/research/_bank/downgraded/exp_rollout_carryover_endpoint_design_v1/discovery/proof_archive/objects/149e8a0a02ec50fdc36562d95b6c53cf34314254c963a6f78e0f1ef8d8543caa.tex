Fix \(H\in\mathbb N\), instantiate \(T=H\), and fix a schedule satisfying Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:irreversible\text{-}endpoints}\).  We first characterize one-wave identification exactly.  Let \(t\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\), and abbreviate
\[
 a=p^{\mathrm{ext}}_{t-L},\qquad q=p_t.
\]
Monotonicity and \(t-L\leq t\) give \(0\leq a\leq q\leq1\).

Suppose \(0<a\leq q<1\).  On \(\{U_i\leq a\}\), monotonicity gives \(U_i\leq p^{\mathrm{ext}}_{t-\ell}\) for every \(0\leq\ell\leq L\); hence all relevant exposures equal one.  On \(\{U_i>q\}\), one has \(U_i>p^{\mathrm{ext}}_{t-\ell}\) for every such \(\ell\); hence all relevant exposures equal zero.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:additive\text{-}distributed\text{-}lag}\) therefore implies
\[
 Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,t}=g_{i,t}+\theta_i
 \quad\text{on }\{U_i\leq a\},
 \qquad
 Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,t}=g_{i,t}
 \quad\text{on }\{U_i>q\},
 \tag{4}
\]
where \(\theta_i=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}b_{i,\ell}\).  By uniformity of \(U_i\), these two events have probabilities \(a\) and \(1-q\).  Applying (4) to Definition \(\mathrm{def:interior\text{-}one\text{-}wave\text{-}ht\text{-}estimator}\) gives, unit by unit,
\[
 \mathbb E_U[\phi_{i,t}(U_i)]
 =(g_{i,t}+\theta_i)-g_{i,t}=\theta_i.
 \tag{5}
\]
The statistic uses only \(U,Z,p\) and outcomes at time \(t\), its denominators are positive, and it is integrable for the fixed finite population.  Averaging (5) proves
\[
 \widehat\theta_{\mathrm{one}}(t)
 \in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{wave}}(p,\{t\}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_U[\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{one}}(t)]=\theta.
\]

The same disjoint-event calculation yields
\[
 \mathbb E_U[\phi_{i,t}(U_i)^2]
 =\frac{(g_{i,t}+\theta_i)^2}{a}
  +\frac{g_{i,t}^2}{1-q}.
\]
The coordinates \(U_i\) are independent by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nested\text{-}hash\text{-}randomization}\), so the unit summands are independent.  Subtracting the squared mean in (5), summing the unit variances, and restoring \(a=p^{\mathrm{ext}}_{t-L}\) and \(q=p_t\) proves the stated exact risk formula.

For necessity, first suppose \(a=0\).  Compare the all-zero fixed population with the population in which \(b_{i,L}=1\) for every unit and all other \(g_{i,s}\) and \(b_{i,\ell}\) are zero.  At the sole observed time \(t\),
\[
 Z_{i,t-L}=\mathbf 1\{U_i\leq a\}=0
 \quad\text{almost surely},
\]
so the two models have the same joint law of the data allowed by Definition \(\mathrm{def:outcome\text{-}wave\text{-}unbiased\text{-}rules}\), while their target values are zero and one.  Thus \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{wave}}(p,\{t\})=\varnothing\).  Next suppose \(q=1\).  Compare the all-zero population with the population having \(b_{i,0}=1\), \(g_{i,t}=-1\), and all other relevant entries zero.  Since \(Z_{i,t}=1\) surely, the observed outcome at \(t\) is zero in both populations, while again the target values are zero and one.  Therefore \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{wave}}(p,\{t\})=\varnothing\).  Since \(0\leq a\leq q\leq1\), these two aliases and the sufficient construction prove
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{wave}}(p,\{t\})\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0<a\leq q<1.
 \tag{6}
\]

We now derive the bivariate frontier.  No zero-wave statistic can be uniformly unbiased: with no outcomes revealed, the all-zero population and the population with \(b_{i,0}=1\) have identical observed \((U,Z,p)\) but target values zero and one.  Thus every feasible wave count is at least one.

If \(L\geq1\) and \(H\leq L\), then for every possible observed time \(t\leq H\), \(t-L\leq0\).  Changing every \(b_{i,L}\) from zero to one leaves every revealed outcome unchanged almost surely, regardless of \(\mathcal S\), and changes \(\theta\) by one.  Hence no number \(1\leq m\leq H\) is feasible.  If \(L=0\) and \(H=1\), the only possible wave is terminal and \(p_1=1\); the contemporaneous-treatment alias used above proves infeasibility.

Let \(L\geq1\) and \(H=L+1\).  If \(t<H\), then \(t-L\leq0\), so the lag-\(L\) alias proves that a wave at \(t\) alone is insufficient.  If \(t=H\), then \(p_H=1\), so universal contemporaneous treatment aliases \(b_{i,0}\) with the unrestricted \(g_{i,H}\).  Thus every one-wave placement is impossible.  On the other hand, choose any schedule with
\[
 0=p_0<p_1\leq\cdots\leq p_{H-1}<p_H=1.
\]
Such a schedule exists, for example \(p_s=s/H\).
The endpoint estimator in Definition \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}ht\text{-}estimator}\) uses outcomes only at \(1\) and \(H\).  Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\text{-}observed\text{-}panel\text{-}stage\text{-}frontier}\), applied with \(T=H=L+1\), proves its exact unbiasedness.  Its displayed formula is measurable with respect to the two-wave sigma-field, so it belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{wave}}(p,\{1,H\})\).  Therefore the minimum is exactly two.

If \(L\geq1\) and \(H\geq L+2\), choose the strictly increasing schedule \(p_s=s/H\) and the occasion \(t=L+1\).  Then
\[
 0<p_{t-L}=p_1\leq p_t=p_{L+1}<1
\]
because \(L+1\leq H-1\).  Equation (6) proves feasibility with one wave, and the zero-wave alias proves minimality.  If \(L=0\) and \(H\geq2\), the same schedule with \(t=1\) satisfies \(0<p_t<1\), so (6) again gives a one-wave estimator and minimality.

Finally, whenever \((H,m_0)\) is feasible and \(m_0\leq m\leq H\), enlarge the measured set to cardinality \(m\) and use the same statistic while ignoring the added outcomes.  This proves every upper portion of the two sets displayed in the claim; the preceding aliases exclude every omitted pair.  The formula for \(m_{\min}^{\mathrm{wave}}(H,L)\) follows directly from Definition \(\mathrm{def:bivariate\text{-}rollout\text{-}wave\text{-}frontier}\).  When all threshold stages are measured, Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\text{-}observed\text{-}panel\text{-}stage\text{-}frontier}\) gives the diagonal convention \(T_{\min}^{\mathrm{panel}}(L)=\max\{2,L+1\}\).  All arguments permit unrestricted \(g\), so this bivariate frontier likewise does not depend on \(r\).
