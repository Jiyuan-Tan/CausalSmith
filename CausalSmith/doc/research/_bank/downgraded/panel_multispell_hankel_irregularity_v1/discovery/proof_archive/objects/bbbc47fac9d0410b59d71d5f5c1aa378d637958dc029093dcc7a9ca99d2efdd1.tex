Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_\Sigma(T,K,s,q,\mathbf c)\) and \(k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\). By \(\text{ass:at-least-square}\),
\[
 r=T-K-1\ge K+1=p.
\]
Define \(x^*\in\mathbb R^d\) by \(x^*_{K+1}=1\) and \(x^*_a=0\) for \(a\ne K+1\). This coordinate exists because \(d=T-1\ge K+1\). For \(1\le i\le p\) and \(0\le j\le K\), the definition of the Hankel map gives
\[
 M(x^*)_{i,j+1}=x^*_{K+i-j}=\mathbf 1\{i=j+1\}.
\]
Thus the first \(p\) rows of \(M(x^*)\) form \(I_p\), so \(M(x^*)\) has column rank \(p\) and
\[
 Q(x^*)=\det(M(x^*)'M(x^*))>0.
\]
In particular, \(Q\) is not the zero polynomial.

For completeness, a nonzero real polynomial on \(\mathbb R^d\) has a Lebesgue-null zero set. This follows by induction on \(d\). In dimension one it has finitely many roots. In dimension \(d>1\), write it as a polynomial in the last coordinate whose coefficient polynomials depend on the first \(d-1\) coordinates. By the induction hypothesis, the set on which all nonzero coefficient polynomials vanish is null; outside that set, each one-dimensional section has finitely many roots. Fubini's theorem then makes the full zero set null. Applying this fact to \(Q\), and then using the absolutely continuous conditional mover law in \(\text{ass:mover-density}\), yields
\[
 P\{Q(X)=0\mid X\ne0\}
 =\int_{S\cap\{Q=0\}}f_X(x)\,dx=0.
\]
This proves \(P\{Q(X)>0\mid X\ne0\}=1\).

If \(Q(x)>0\), then \(M(x)'M(x)\) is nonsingular and \(M(x)\) has column rank \(p\). Hence \(\operatorname{Im}(M(x)')=\mathbb R^p\), so \(e_k\in\operatorname{Im}(M(x)')\). At \(x=0\), the definition of \(M\) gives \(M(0)=0\), whose row space is \(\{0\}\); because \(e_k\ne0\), it is not eligible. The only remaining histories are nonzero singular movers, a null event by the preceding display. Therefore
\[
 \{e_k\in\operatorname{Im}(M(X)')\}
 =\{X\ne0\}
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]

Next, \(\Pi(M)=I_r-MM^+\) is an orthogonal projector and hence is positive semidefinite. Since \(M(0)=0\), \(\Pi(M(0))=I_r\). The stayer-mass assumption therefore gives
\[
 E_P[\Pi(M(X))]
 =\pi_0 I_r+E_P[\Pi(M(X))\mathbf 1\{X\ne0\}]
 \succeq\pi_0I_r
 \succeq\underline\pi I_r
 \succ0.
\]
Thus \(E_P[\Pi(M(X))]\) is invertible. Also \(T\ge2K+2\) implies
\[
 T-1-K\ge K+1>0,
\]
so \(K<T-1\). By \(\text{ass:q-moments}\) and \(q>4\), Hölder's inequality gives
\[
 E_P\|B\|_2^2
 \le \{E_P\|B\|_2^q\}^{2/q}
 \le C_q^{2/q}<\infty.
\]
Because \(M(X)\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), the tower property and \(\text{ass:conditional-mean-zero}\) give
\[
 E_P[\varepsilon\mid M(X)]
 =E_P\!\left[E_P[\varepsilon\mid X]\mid M(X)\right]=0.
\]
Together with the reduced-form equation in \(\text{ass:distributed-lag-model}\) and independent sampling in \(\text{ass:iid-groups}\), these displayed conditions verify the reduced-form dimension, moment, exclusion, and projector-invertibility requirements recorded in \(\text{lem:published-reduced-form-scope}\). This is a bridge between observed-law experiments only; the class definition does not impose the source paper's separate no-anticipation and parallel-trends conditions on potential outcomes.

The almost-sure event equality and \(\text{ass:stayer-mass}\), which implies \(P(X\ne0)=1-\pi_0>0\), now yield
\[
 \overline{\beta}_k
 =E_P[B_k\mid e_k\in\operatorname{Im}(M(X)')]
 =E_P[B_k\mid X\ne0]
 =\tau_k.
\]
This equality relates the causal target to the induced reduced-form eligible mean; it is not part of either definition.

It remains to compare the two cutoff normalizations. Put
\[
 R_k=e_k'M(X)^+(\Delta Y-\Gamma),
 \qquad
 E_C=\{X\ne0,H_k(X)\le C\}.
\]
On \(E_C\), full column rank holds almost surely, so \(M(X)^+M(X)=I_p\). The distributed-lag equation gives
\[
 R_k=B_k+e_k'M(X)^+\varepsilon
 \quad\text{almost surely on }E_C.
\]
For finite \(C\), the error term is integrable on \(E_C\): by conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the definition of \(H_k\),
\[
 E_P\!\left[|e_k'M(X)^+\varepsilon|\mathbf 1_{E_C}\right]
 \le C\,E_P[\|\varepsilon\|_2]<\infty,
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(\text{ass:q-moments}\). Since \(E_C\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), conditional mean zero implies
\[
 E_P[e_k'M(X)^+\varepsilon\mathbf 1_{E_C}]=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 E_P[R_k\mathbf 1\{H_k(X)\le C\}\mid X\ne0]
 =E_P[B_k\mathbf 1\{H_k(X)\le C\}\mid X\ne0].
\]
Whenever \(\rho_{k,C}=P(E_C\mid X\ne0)>0\), the eligibility-event equality likewise gives
\[
 E_P[R_k\mid e_k\in\operatorname{Im}(M(X)'),H_k(X)\le C]
 =\frac{E_P[B_k\mathbf 1\{H_k(X)\le C\}\mid X\ne0]}{\rho_{k,C}}.
\]
Multiplying the last display by \(\rho_{k,C}\) proves the asserted finite-cutoff relation.

The Moore--Penrose inverse of every finite real matrix is finite, so \(H_k(X)<\infty\) almost surely. Hence \(\rho_{k,C}\uparrow1\) as \(C\to\infty\). The moment condition makes \(B_k\) integrable, and dominated convergence gives
\[
 E_P[B_k\mathbf 1\{H_k(X)\le C\}\mid X\ne0]
 \longrightarrow E_P[B_k\mid X\ne0]=\tau_k.
\]
The indicator/mover-denominator functional therefore converges to \(\tau_k\), while division by \(\rho_{k,C}\to1\) shows that the conditional-cutoff functional has the same limit. This proves equality only at the limit and records their generally different finite-\(C\) normalizations.

Finally, membership in \(\mathcal P_\Sigma\) imposes, by \(\text{def:fixed-signature-class}\), the fixed compact full-dimensional semialgebraic support and face signature, mover absolute continuity with a two-sided density envelope, positive stayer mass, \(q\)-moment and conditional-variance envelopes, the normal-link certificate, and original-coordinate Whitney smoothness. The preceding argument adds full-rank movers almost surely. These restrictions make it a specialization of the published reduced-form experiment. The specialization is strict: retain the same \(T\) and \(K\), take \(X=0\) and \(X=x^*\) with probabilities one half each, and set \(B=0\), \(\varepsilon=0\), \(\Gamma=0\), and \(\Delta Y=0\). Then
\[
 E[\Pi(M(X))]
 =\tfrac12 I_r+\tfrac12\Pi(M(x^*))
 \succeq\tfrac12 I_r\succ0,
\]
the reduced-form equation, exclusion, finite-moment, dimension, and independent-sampling requirements hold, but the conditional mover distribution is the point mass at \(x^*\), so it violates \(\text{ass:mover-density}\) and belongs to no such absolutely continuous fixed-signature class. Thus the inclusion is strict. The additional no-stayer overidentified and component-specific-eligibility cases recorded in \(\text{lem:published-reduced-form-scope}\) further illustrate why neither the fixed atlas nor any theorem depending on it is being extended to the unrestricted published experiment.
