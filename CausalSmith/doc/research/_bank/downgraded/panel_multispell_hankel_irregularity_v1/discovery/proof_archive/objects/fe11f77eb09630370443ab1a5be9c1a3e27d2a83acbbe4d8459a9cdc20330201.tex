For \(K=1\) and \(T=5\), write the three maximal minors as
\[
F_1=x_2^2-x_1x_3,\qquad
F_2=x_2x_3-x_1x_4,\qquad
F_3=x_3^2-x_2x_4.
\]
At \(x^\star=(1,-1,1,-1)\), their gradients are
\[
\nabla F_1=(-1,-2,-1,0),\quad
\nabla F_2=(1,1,-1,-1),\quad
\nabla F_3=(0,1,2,1),
\]
and \(\nabla F_2=-\nabla F_1-\nabla F_3\). The first and third vectors are independent, so the Jacobian rank is two. Also
\[
M(x^\star)=
\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
which has right kernel spanned by \((1,1)'\). Thus both canonical targets have nonzero alignment with the unit kernel vector \(v_0=2^{-1/2}(1,1)'\).

Choose \(\rho>0\) small enough that \((F_1,F_3)\) has rank two throughout \(U_\rho\), the rank-one locus is its zero set, and both alignment numerators are positive there. The constant-rank change of variables supplies local coordinates \((y,z)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\) in which the rank-loss locus is \(\{y=0\}\). The minor identity \(Q=F_1^2+F_2^2+F_3^2\) and the local dependence of \(F_2\) on \((F_1,F_3,z)\) imply
\[
Q(y,z)=y'R(z)y+O(\|y\|_2^3),
\]
with \(R(z)\) uniformly positive definite. At \(x^\star\), \(M'M\) has nonzero eigenvalue six and normalized null vector \(v_0\), whence
\[
A_k(x^\star)=e_k'\operatorname{adj}(M'M)e_k
=6(e_k'v_0)^2=3,
\qquad k=0,1.
\]
After decreasing \(\rho\), both \(A_k(y,z)\) are bounded above and away from zero.

The identity \(H_k^2=A_k/Q\) now gives
\[
\{H_k>u\}=\{Q/A_k<u^{-2}\}.
\]
For each tangential \(z\), the normal section is an ellipse of area
\[
u^{-2}\frac{\pi A_k(0,z)}{\sqrt{\det R(z)}}+o(u^{-2}),
\]
uniformly away from the lower-dimensional intersections with the cube boundary. The coordinate Jacobian is continuous and bounded above and away from zero. Dominated convergence, with the boundary intersections contributing zero tangential measure, therefore yields
\[
u^2P\{H_k(X)>u\mid X\in U_\rho\}
\longrightarrow
C_{k,\rho},
\]
where \(C_{k,\rho}\) is the constant-density normalization times the integral of the displayed strictly positive continuous section coefficient over the rank-one locus in the cube. Hence \(0<C_{k,
ho}<\infty\) for both targets.

As a reproducible numerical check, a scrambled Sobol rule with seed \(1729\), \(2^{22}\) points, and \(\rho=0.05\), evaluating \(H_k^2\) from the two diagonal entries of \((M'M)^{-1}\), gave
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}

u^2\widehat P(H_0>u)&174.33&178.30&179.64&180.38&180.70\\
u^2\widehat P(H_1>u)&174.37&178.19&179.69&180.27&181.07
\end{array}
\]
which verifies the predicted finite positive limit for both coordinates.

For \(K=1,T=4\),
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}x_2&x_1\\x_3&x_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det M=x_2^2-x_1x_3.
\]
Direct inversion of \(M'M\) gives
\[
H_0=\frac{\sqrt{x_1^2+x_2^2}}{|x_2^2-x_1x_3|},
\qquad
H_1=\frac{\sqrt{x_2^2+x_3^2}}{|x_2^2-x_1x_3|}.
\]
At a smooth target-active point the determinant is a transverse coordinate and the numerators are bounded away from zero. Coarea therefore gives a tail proportional to \(u^{-1}\), with no logarithm, proving \((\alpha_{\Sigma,k},m_{\Sigma,k})=(1,0)\).
