On the full-rank set, the proposed pseudo-inverse lemma gives \(H_k^2=A_k/Q\). Fix a disjoint canonical chart cell and one realizable simultaneous-zero face. After absorbing coordinates bounded away from zero into positive units, the certified monomializations yield
\[
H_k(\phi_j(z))
=u_{Hj}(z)\prod_{a=1}^h z_a^{-c_{kja}},
\quad
c_{kja}=\frac{q_{ja}-a_{kja}}2,
\quad
|\det D\phi_j(z)|=u_{Jj}(z)\prod_{a=1}^h z_a^{b_{ja}},
\]
where \(u_{Hj}\) and \(u_{Jj}\) are bounded above and away from zero. Coordinates with \(c_{kja}\le0\) cannot create an upper tail and are integrated into the bounded unit. For the remaining coordinates set \(y_a=-\log z_a\), \(w_a=b_{ja}+1\), and \(t=\log u\). Up to fixed multiplicative constants, the local tail volume is
\[
I_j(t)=\int_{\{y_a\ge0:\ \sum_ac_{kja}y_a>t+O(1)\}}
\exp\!\left(-\sum_aw_ay_a\right)dy.
\]
Let \(\alpha_j=\min_a w_a/c_{kja}\), and let \(h_j\) be the number of incident coordinates attaining this minimum. Integrating first the nonminimizing exponentials and then the sum of the \(h_j\) tied minimizing exponentials gives
\[
I_j(t)\asymp e^{-\alpha_jt}t^{h_j-1}.
\]
Indeed, after the rescaling \(s_a=c_{kja}y_a\), the tied variables have common exponential rate \(\alpha_j\); their sum has density equal to a positive constant times \(e^{-\alpha_j s}s^{h_j-1}\), while every non-tied convolution has a finite moment-generating function at \(\alpha_j\). This proves the chart rule
\[
\alpha_j=\min_a\frac{b_{ja}+1}{c_{kja}},
\qquad m_j=h_j-1.
\]

For the distance-weighted integral, the factor
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\phi_j(z),\mathcal Z)^s
=u_{Dj}(z)^s\prod_a z_a^{s d_{ja}}
\]
replaces \(w_a\) by \(b_{ja}+1+s d_{ja}\). The identical calculation yields the stated \((\zeta_{\Sigma,k,s},\ell_{\Sigma,k,s})\) rule. Analytic expansion of the positive unit at the incident face shows that its nonzero constant term produces the first Mellin pole, while every higher monomial shifts a candidate pole to the right. Thus the numbers of tied minimizing coordinates are precisely the first-pole orders minus one. Recorded cutoff vanishing orders delete a face whose constant term is zero.

There are finitely many cells. Summing their nonnegative contributions selects the smallest power and, among cells having that power, the largest logarithmic exponent. Contributions from different cells add positive residues and cannot create an additional logarithm. Moreover, \(Z_k\) and \(Z_{k,s}\) are integrals in the original coordinates. Their abscissae of convergence are therefore independent of a resolution. The tail calculation and the Mellin identity
\[
\int_{\{H_k>1\}}H_k^z,dx
=z\int_1^\infty u^{z-1}\operatorname{Leb}\{x\in S:H_k(x)>u,Q(x)>0\}\,du
+\operatorname{Leb}\{H_k>1,Q>0\}
\]
show that the first-pole order is intrinsic as well. Hence every allowed resolution gives the same two pairs.

If a realizable incident coordinate has \(c_{kja}>0\), its chart contains sequences on which \(H_k\to\infty\); all exponents above are positive and finite. If no such coordinate exists in any chart, the simultaneous-germ condition in the proposed pseudo-inverse lemma makes \(H_k\) bounded on each compact chart. Finiteness of the atlas then gives \(\overline H_{\Sigma,k}<\infty\), and both tails vanish above it.

Finally, conditional on moving, the density assumption gives for every measurable \(E\subseteq S\)
\[
\underline f\operatorname{Leb}(E)\le P\{X\in E\mid X\ne0\}
\le\overline f\operatorname{Leb}(E).
\]
Applying this to the leverage-tail cells transfers the two-sided Lebesgue estimates uniformly to every \(P\) in the class. All constants are finite combinations of the fixed unit, link-mass, density, and partition bounds, so they depend only on \(\Sigma\) and \(\mathbf c\). This also proves the assertions concerning active faces, tangencies, higher-rank intersections, within-germ ties, across-cell ties, and target-inactive divisors.
