The first assertion follows from \(\text{lem:zero-coefficient-certified-witness}\): on that nonempty certified class the zero estimator and the singleton interval have risk and length zero, whereas \(R_{G,\Sigma,0}^{\star}=\sigma^2/(6G)>0\). Thus no positive factor can be information-theoretically unavoidable uniformly over all nonempty frozen classes.

For the second assertion, \(\text{def:invariant-indices}\) constructs \((\alpha_{\Sigma,k},m_{\Sigma,k},\zeta_{\Sigma,k,s},\ell_{\Sigma,k,s})\) from the certified atlas, and the atlas is itself fixed by \(\Sigma\). Hence these indices are measurable deterministic functions of an input already supplied to the procedure. There are not two laws with the same \(\Sigma\) but different values of these indices, so no within-signature testing lower bound for learning them can be formulated. Finally, \(\text{def:adaptive-handle}\) names exceedance counts and comparisons but supplies no comparison formula, threshold, or interval radius. It therefore does not define the random map whose uniform coverage could be proved. The counterexample and the deterministic-index observation jointly prove the negative answer and identify the additional structure required for a meaningful adaptation theorem.
