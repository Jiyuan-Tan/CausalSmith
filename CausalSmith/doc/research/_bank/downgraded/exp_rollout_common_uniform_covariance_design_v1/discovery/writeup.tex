\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}rollout\_\allowbreak{}common\_\allowbreak{}uniform\_\allowbreak{}covariance\_\allowbreak{}design}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A monotone production ramp assigns unit \(i\) at every fraction by the same threshold rule \(Z_i(u)=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le u\}\). Over the declared permutation-invariant ellipsoid for aggregate coefficients of a \(\beta\)-order finite-population schedule, the exact cross-stage covariance is a subset-intersection kernel rather than the diagonal or total-variation envelope used in existing rollout bounds. The kernel reduces the exponentially large robust eigenvalue to \(\beta+1\) subset-lattice isotypic blocks and turns joint node-and-weight choice into an attained exact spectral minimum. A leading eigenvector gives an explicit legal finite-valued least-favourable schedule for every fixed design, while a uniform-node Lagrange readout and the degree-moment barrier prove the sharp fixed-parameter small-budget law \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}=\Theta(q^{1-2\beta})\), uniformly over admissible spacing rules. The same design-computable radius certificate yields a finite-sample Chebyshev confidence interval without a central limit theorem or an outcome-estimated variance. An analytic global optimizer, the exact leading constant, and the Chebyshev--Lobatto regime map remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Cortez, Eichhorn, and Yu derive polynomial-interpolation estimators for nested Bernoulli rollouts and control their variance by spacing bounds, while the accepted Chebyshev-envelope result optimizes a positive-semidefinite diagonal envelope. Neither characterizes the covariance matrices attainable when every stage thresholds the same unit uniforms, supplies a legal least-favourable schedule for that covariance on the declared ellipsoid, or establishes the sharp interaction-order-dependent small-budget exponent for the exact-covariance robust design problem.

\paragraph{Niche.} The common-uniform path imposes exact intersection-cardinality identities across stages and interference monomials. Those identities create a robust design problem that is neither classical independent-error polynomial design nor a free positive-semidefinite covariance envelope; its small-budget behavior is governed jointly by all \(\beta\) polynomial exactness moments and a feasible nested-rollout construction.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project identifies the exact monomial covariance kernel, exploits permutation symmetry to reduce the robust covariance operator to small subset-lattice blocks, proves attainment of the joint rollout-node and polynomial-exact-weight minimum, constructs the least-favourable schedule from the active block eigenvector, and derives a finite-sample Chebyshev interval from the design-computable radius certificate. It also combines a degree-moment lower barrier with an explicit uniform-node Lagrange design to establish the sharp fixed-parameter small-budget exponent \(1-2\beta\), uniformly over every admissible spacing rule.

\section{Related work}

CortezEichhornYu2022 is the setup and writing anchor. Its Theorems 2 and 3 on pp. 5--6 prove unbiasedness and spacing- and magnitude-based variance upper bounds for polynomial interpolation under monotone Bernoulli and complete rollouts; the Bernoulli argument bounds cross-stage monomial covariances instead of retaining their exact common-uniform values. It supplies neither an exact-covariance minimax lower bound nor a matching two-sided \(q^{1-2\beta}\) law. ChenLi2024 is the closest public ramp-up analysis: it studies regression estimation from merged sequential experiments under network interference, including the benefit of distinct treatment proportions, but it neither fixes a shared-threshold potential-outcome covariance law nor derives a polynomial-exact linear extrapolation saddle, a spectral ellipsoidal support value, or a legal least-favourable schedule. The accepted \(\texttt{exp\_rollout\_chebyshev\_minimax\_tv\_envelope\_rollout\_design}\) result optimizes a total-variation/diagonal envelope and explicitly leaves exact nested-covariance minimaxity open; its envelope value implies neither the degree-moment lower barrier nor a feasible upper design for the common-uniform covariance saddle. The present small-budget result is therefore only the fixed-\((n,\beta,k,B,\omega)\), uniform-in-admissible-\(\delta\) exponent, not an exact leading constant or a population-uniform frontier. Kallus2021, Section 5, supplies the prior eigenvalue support-function template: starting from an externally specified ellipsoidal outcome class and design covariance \(Q(\sigma)\), it obtains the support value \(C\lambda_{\max}\{K^{1/2}Q(\sigma)K^{1/2}\}\) and optimizes a two-arm assignment law. It does not derive the common-uniform subset-intersection kernel, prove that an active eigenvector is realizable by a legal low-order potential-outcome schedule, impose polynomial-exact cross-stage readout constraints, or jointly optimize continuous rollout nodes and weights. Thus the shared eigenvalue support-function step is prior art; the derived nested kernel, legal converse schedule, and attained joint node--weight saddle are the distinct theorem content here. ChenLiDengWang2023 makes assignment covariance a design variable under one-hop interference, but optimizes an upper bound for a single-stage Horvitz--Thompson estimator and explicitly notes that covariance feasibility is delicate. ThiyageswaranEtAl2026 is the closest robust covariance-design comparator: it optimizes worst-case MSE by selecting covariance structures for a single assignment under a network-interference, homophily, and heterogeneous-variation model. That covariance-design framework does not subsume the present theorem, whose covariance is not freely chosen but exactly induced by one common-uniform nested rollout, whose readout must satisfy all polynomial exactness equations, and whose result gives the exact ellipsoidal support and an attaining finite-population adversary. Conversely, this note does not optimize their broader covariance-feasibility or homophily trade-off. EichhornKhanUganderYu2024 treats low-order outcomes under general and clustered designs and gives design-dependent variance bounds, but does not characterize common-uniform cross-stage covariance or optimize rollout nodes. BasseAiroldi2018 supplies the finite-population interference framework and documents the limitations of design-based inference under arbitrary and network interference; the closest dependency-graph and network-HAC asymptotic inference literature instead seeks Gaussian limits or outcome-based variance estimation under additional setwise-dependence or approximation conditions. This paper claims neither deliverable: its interval and coverage calculation are the classical Chebyshev inequality applied to the newly derived design-known radius certificate, yielding only conservative finite-sample known-radius inference. XuChenFernandezSinnoBhasin2015 and LiJamkhandeKochetkovLiu2022 document that production experimentation platforms use shared deterministic hashes to preserve assignment as traffic ramps; their balance diagnostics do not characterize interference-induced cross-threshold covariance.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-finite-population]\label{ass:fixed-finite-population}
The schedule \(Y\) is fixed before the draw of \(U\), and all probability statements condition on \(Y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Neyman finite-population randomization, \cite{BasseAiroldi2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-uniform-rollout]\label{ass:common-uniform-rollout}
The coordinates \((U_i)_{i\in I_n}\) are independent \(\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) variables and \(Z_i(r)=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le r\}\) for every \(r\in[0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (monotone staggered Bernoulli rollout, \cite{CortezEichhornYu2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:low-order-schedule]\label{ass:low-order-schedule}
For every \(i\in I_n\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\), \(Y_i(z)=c_{i,\varnothing}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded-order polynomial interference, \cite{CortezEichhornYu2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:aggregate-coefficient-ellipsoid]\label{ass:aggregate-coefficient-ellipsoid}
The aggregate coefficient vector satisfies \(a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a\le B^2/n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This permutation-invariant energy restriction models platforms with many weak interactions whose root-mean-square aggregate strength is stable within each interaction order. It contains dense exchangeable spillovers, sparse local interactions, and mixtures across every order up to \(\beta\), while allowing the order weights to be calibrated from domain bounds or a pilot ramp.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:published-low-order-class]\label{def:published-low-order-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Published low-\allowbreak{}order schedule class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(E\subseteq I_n\times I_n\) be a static directed graph containing every self-loop and set \(\mathcal N_i(E)=\{j\in I_n:(j,i)\in E\}\).  The noise-free CEY schedule-envelope union is
\[
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}
=\bigcup_{\substack{E\subseteq I_n\times I_n\\(i,i)\in E\ \forall i}}
\left\{(Y,c):
\begin{array}{l}
c_{i,S}=0\text{ whenever }S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\text{ and }S\nsubseteq\mathcal N_i(E),\\
Y_i(z)=c_{i,\varnothing}+\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\mathcal N_i(E)\\|S|\le\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j
\text{ for every }i\in I_n,\ z\in\{0,1\}^n
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
It is the potential-outcome-schedule projection of the published static-graph model after setting observation noise to zero; the union imposes no common bound across schedules.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}low-\allowbreak{}order-\allowbreak{}schedule}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class]\label{def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Ellipsoidal common-\allowbreak{}uniform coefficient class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)=\{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}:a_S=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_n}c_{i,S}\text{ for every }S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta},\ a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a\le B^2/n\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}low-\allowbreak{}order-\allowbreak{}schedule}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}aggregate-\allowbreak{}coefficient-\allowbreak{}ellipsoid}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:feasible-node-class]\label{def:feasible-node-class}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{k}; \texttt{q}; \texttt{delta}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:unbiased-weight-class]\label{def:unbiased-weight-class}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)=\{w\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:\sum_{t=0}^kw_t=0,\ \sum_{t=0}^kw_tp_t^d=1\text{ for }d=1,\ldots,\beta\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{beta}; \texttt{p}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rollout-estimator]\label{def:rollout-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Linear rollout estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\widehat\tau_{p,w}=\sum_{t=0}^kw_tM(p_t)\) for \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\) and \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{p}; \texttt{w}; \texttt{M}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:exact-covariance-operator]\label{def:exact-covariance-operator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Exact common-\allowbreak{}uniform covariance operator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(S,T\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\), set \(K_{st}(S,T)=\min(p_s,p_t)^{|S\cap T|}p_s^{|S\setminus T|}p_t^{|T\setminus S|}-p_s^{|S|}p_t^{|T|}\) and \(G(p,w)_{S,T}=\sum_{s,t=0}^kw_sw_tK_{st}(S,T)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{p}; \texttt{w}; \texttt{S\_\allowbreak{}beta}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:exact-saddle]\label{def:exact-saddle}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{beta}; \texttt{k}; \texttt{q}; \texttt{delta}; \texttt{V\_\allowbreak{}rob}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:class-inclusion]\label{prop:class-inclusion}
For every finite \(n\), \(1\le\beta\le n\), positive \(B\), and positive order weights \(\omega\), \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\subsetneq\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\). Strictness is witnessed by the published-class schedule \(c_{i,\{1\}}=2B\sqrt{\omega_1/n}\) for every \(i\in I_n\), with all other coefficients zero: its nonzero aggregate coefficient satisfies \(a_{\{1\}}=2B\sqrt{\omega_1/n}\) and hence \(a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a=4B^2/n>B^2/n\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix finite \(n\), \(1\le\beta\le n\), \(B>0\), and \(\omega_j>0\) for every \(1\le j\le\beta\). First let \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\). By the membership condition in Definition \(\texttt{def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class}\), one necessarily has \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\subseteq\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]

It remains to prove that the inclusion is strict. Since \(1\le\beta\le n\), the singleton \(\{1\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\). Put
\[
d=2B\sqrt{\frac{\omega_1}{n}},
\qquad
c_{i,\{1\}}=d
\quad(i\in I_n),
\]
and set \(c_{i,S}=0\) for every other coefficient index \(S\in\{\varnothing\}\cup\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\). Define \(Y_i\) from these coefficients by the polynomial formula in Assumption \(\texttt{ass:low-order-schedule}\), so that \(Y_i(z)=d z_1\) for every \(i\in I_n\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\).

To check published-class membership, take the complete directed graph \(E=I_n\times I_n\). It contains every self-loop and satisfies \(\mathcal N_i(E)=I_n\) for each \(i\in I_n\). Hence the only nonzero interaction index \(\{1\}\) is contained in every \(\mathcal N_i(E)\), and it has cardinality one, which is at most \(\beta\). The displayed polynomial representation and the support condition in Definition \(\texttt{def:published-low-order-class}\) therefore give
\[
(Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]

For this schedule, the definition \(a_S=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_n}c_{i,S}\) yields
\[
a_{\{1\}}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n d
=d,
\qquad
a_S=0
\quad(S\ne\{1\}).
\]
Because \(\Omega\) is diagonal with \(\Omega_{S,S}=\omega_{|S|}\) and every \(\omega_j\) is positive, \(\Omega^{-1}\) exists. Consequently,
\[
a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a
=\frac{a_{\{1\}}^2}{\omega_1}
=\frac{d^2}{\omega_1}
=\frac{4B^2}{n}
>\frac{B^2}{n},
\]
where the strict inequality uses \(B>0\) and \(n\ge1\). Thus the ellipsoid condition in Assumption \(\texttt{ass:aggregate-coefficient-ellipsoid}\), equivalently the final membership condition in Definition \(\texttt{def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class}\), fails. Hence \((Y,c)\notin\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\), while it belongs to \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\). This proves the asserted proper inclusion and the stated witness.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This records exactly what transfers from the published low-order model and prevents an ellipsoidal achievability result from being presented as a theorem for the full bounded-outcome class. \textit{Gap.} CortezEichhornYu2022 works with low-order schedules and coefficient-size bounds but does not isolate the permutation-invariant aggregate ellipsoid used by the exact covariance saddle. \textit{Consumer.} A reader can identify which parts of the design certificate remain valid when platform-specific coefficient bounds replace the ellipsoid.

\begin{lemma}[lem:common-uniform-monomial-covariance]\label{lem:common-uniform-monomial-covariance}
For every \(S,T\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) and stages \(s,t\), \[\operatorname{Cov}_U\!\left(\prod_{i\in S}Z_i(p_s),\prod_{j\in T}Z_j(p_t)\right)=K_{st}(S,T).\] Consequently, \(\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a\) for every low-order schedule.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(S,T\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) and write
\[
X_S(r)=\prod_{i\in S}Z_i(r).
\]
By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), the coordinates of \(U\) are independent and \(\Pr_U(U_i\le r)=r\).  Hence
\[
\mathbb E_U X_S(p_s)=p_s^{|S|}.
\]
For a coordinate in \(S\cap T\),
\[
\mathbf 1\{U_i\le p_s\}\mathbf 1\{U_i\le p_t\}
=\mathbf 1\{U_i\le\min(p_s,p_t)\}.
\]
Coordinates in \(S\setminus T\) and \(T\setminus S\) occur at only one stage.  Independence across coordinates therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E_U\{X_S(p_s)X_T(p_t)\}
=\min(p_s,p_t)^{|S\cap T|}p_s^{|S\setminus T|}p_t^{|T\setminus S|}.
\]
Subtracting \(\mathbb E_U X_S(p_s)\mathbb E_U X_T(p_t)=p_s^{|S|}p_t^{|T|}\) proves the displayed covariance formula, which is \(K_{st}(S,T)\) by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\).

By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\) and the definition of \(a\), finite summation over units yields
\[
M(r)=\bar c_{\varnothing}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a_SX_S(r),
\qquad
\bar c_{\varnothing}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}c_{i,\varnothing}.
\]
Consequently, Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}rollout\text{-}estimator}\) gives
\[
\widehat\tau_{p,w}
=\bar c_{\varnothing}\sum_{t=0}^kw_t
+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a_S\sum_{t=0}^kw_tX_S(p_t).
\]
The first term is nonrandom.  Bilinearity of covariance, the monomial identity just proved, and finiteness of \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
&=\sum_{S,T}a_Sa_T\sum_{s,t=0}^kw_sw_tK_{st}(S,T)\\
&=\sum_{S,T}a_SG(p,w)_{S,T}a_T
=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second equality is Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\).  This proves both claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the first design-specific proof step: exact overlap cardinalities determine every cross-stage term under the shared uniform, and the result applies to the entire noise-free CEY graph-union envelope. \textit{Gap.} CortezEichhornYu2022 bounds the relevant cross-stage terms under static-neighborhood magnitude controls; it does not state the full subset-intersection kernel retained here. \textit{Consumer.} A shared-hash rollout scheduler can compare candidate ramps using their actual nested covariance rather than an independent-stage or spacing envelope.

\begin{lemma}[lem:subset-lattice-block-reduction]\label{lem:subset-lattice-block-reduction}
Because \(\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\) depends on \((S,T)\) only through \((|S|,|T|,|S\cap T|)\), there is an explicit orthogonal subset-lattice transform that block diagonalizes it into isotypic blocks \(H_h(p,w)\), \(h=0,\ldots,\beta\), with block dimension at most \(\beta-h+1\). Each entry of \(H_h(p,w)\) is a finite sum of binomial coefficients and the kernel values \(K_{st}(S,T)\), and \[\lambda_{\max}\{\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\}=\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}.\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\mathcal X_r=\{S\subseteq I_n:|S|=r\}\).  For \(0\le j\le r\le n\), let \(R_{rj}\) be the inclusion matrix with rows indexed by \(\mathcal X_r\), columns indexed by \(\mathcal X_j\), and entries
\[
(R_{rj})_{S,A}=\mathbf 1\{A\subseteq S\}.
\]
For every admissible \(r,\ell,j\), direct counting gives
\[
(R_{rj}R_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T})_{S,T}=\binom{|S\cap T|}{j}.
\tag{1}
\]

Define the down operator \(D_j=R_{j,j-1}^{\mathsf T}\), the up operator \(U_j=R_{j+1,j}\), and
\[
\mathcal L_0=\mathbb R,
\qquad
\mathcal L_h=\ker(D_h)\quad(h\ge1).
\]
If \(g\) is a function on \(\mathcal X_j\), counting separately the term that leaves a set unchanged and the terms that exchange one element proves
\[
D_{j+1}U_jg-U_{j-1}D_jg=(n-2j)g.
\tag{2}
\]
Iterating (2) gives, for \(0\le h\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), \(g_1,g_2\in\mathcal L_h\), and \(h\le r\le n-h\),
\[
\langle R_{rh}g_1,R_{rh}g_2\rangle
=\binom{n-2h}{r-h}\langle g_1,g_2\rangle,
\tag{3}
\]
and the lifted spaces arising from distinct \(h\)'s are orthogonal.  Moreover,
\[
\mathbb R^{\mathcal X_r}
=\bigoplus_{h=0}^{\min(r,n-r)}R_{rh}\mathcal L_h.
\tag{4}
\]
For completeness, taking the inner product of (2) with a vector in \(\ker D_j\) gives injectivity of \(U_j\) when \(j<n/2\) and the squared-norm multiplier \(n-2j\).  Successive application from level \(h\) to level \(r\), together with \(R_{rh}=U_{r-1}\cdots U_h/(r-h)!\), proves (3).  Moving down operators to the other side of an inner product proves orthogonality because they annihilate the harmonic vector.  Finally, rank--nullity gives \(\dim\mathcal L_h=\binom nh-\binom n{h-1}\); summing over \(h\le\min(r,n-r)\) gives \(\binom nr\), proving (4).

Choose an orthonormal basis \(e_{h,1},\ldots,e_{h,d_h}\) of each \(\mathcal L_h\), where \(d_h=\dim\mathcal L_h\).  By (3)--(4), the vectors
\[
q_{r,h,j}
=\binom{n-2h}{r-h}^{-1/2}R_{rh}e_{h,j},
\qquad
h\le r\le\min(\beta,n-h),
\tag{5}
\]
over all admissible \((r,h,j)\), form an orthonormal basis of the direct sum of the nonempty subset levels \(1,\ldots,\beta\).  Thus (5) constructs the orthogonal transform \(Q\) from finite incidence matrices and orthonormal bases of explicit null spaces.

Set
\[
A=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\), \(G(p,w)_{S,T}\) depends on \((S,T)\) only through \((|S|,|T|,|S\cap T|)\); multiplication by \(\Omega^{1/2}\), whose diagonal entry on level \(r\) is \(\sqrt{\omega_r}\), preserves this property.  Hence the level-\((r,\ell)\) block of \(A\) has entries \(\phi_{r\ell}(|S\cap T|)\) for an explicit function \(\phi_{r\ell}\).  If \((\Delta h)(c)=h(c+1)-h(c)\), the finite Newton expansion
\[
\phi_{r\ell}(c)
=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,\ell)}(\Delta^j\phi_{r\ell})(0)\binom cj
\tag{6}
\]
and (1) express this level block as a finite linear combination of \(R_{rj}R_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T}\).

If \(g\in\mathcal L_h\), repeated use of (2), or direct counting followed by \(D_hg=0\), gives
\[
R_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T}R_{\ell h}g
=\begin{cases}
0,&j<h,\\
\displaystyle\binom{n-h-j}{\ell-j}R_{jh}g,&h\le j\le\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
For the second coefficient, use \(R_{\ell j}R_{jh}=\binom{\ell-h}{j-h}R_{\ell h}\), take the inner product with \(R_{jh}g\), and apply (3); the resulting ratio is
\[
\frac{\binom{\ell-h}{j-h}\binom{n-2h}{\ell-h}}
{\binom{n-2h}{j-h}}
=\binom{n-h-j}{\ell-j}.
\]
Writing \(\gamma_{r\ell j}=(\Delta^j\phi_{r\ell})(0)\), equations (6)--(7) and \(R_{rj}R_{jh}=\binom{r-h}{j-h}R_{rh}\) yield
\[
A_{r\ell}R_{\ell h}g
=\left\{
\sum_{j=h}^{\min(r,\ell)}
\gamma_{r\ell j}
\binom{n-h-j}{\ell-j}
\binom{r-h}{j-h}
\right\}R_{rh}g.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus every matrix in (6) preserves harmonic order and acts independently of the harmonic basis index; distinct harmonic orders are orthogonal by (4).  Therefore \(Q^{\mathsf T}AQ\) is block diagonal.  For fixed \(h\), its nonzero level coordinates are indexed by
\[
\mathcal R_h=\{r:1\le r\le\beta,\ h\le r\le n-h\},
\]
so the block \(H_h(p,w)\) has size \(|\mathcal R_h|\le\beta-h+1\) and is repeated \(d_h\) times; absent coordinates may be padded by zero rows and columns.  Equations (1), (5), and (8) show that each entry is a finite sum of binomial coefficients and quantities
\[
\sqrt{\omega_r\omega_\ell}\sum_{s,t=0}^kw_sw_tK_{st}(S,T).
\]
Orthogonal similarity preserves eigenvalues, and repetitions do not change their maximum.  Hence
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A)
=\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\},
\]
which proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction converts an operator indexed by all subsets of up to \(\beta\) units into a collection of small matrices without discarding covariance compatibility. \textit{Gap.} Neither Kallus2021 nor ChenLiDengWang2023 has the cross-level subset-intersection symmetry created by nested low-order monomials. \textit{Consumer.} A platform can evaluate and globally optimize the robust risk for small \(\beta\) without forming a matrix whose dimension grows as \(n^\beta\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-covariance-saddle]\label{thm:exact-covariance-saddle}
For every feasible \((n,\beta,k,q,\delta,B,\omega)\) with \(1\le\beta\le n\),
\[
\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}\sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})=\frac{B^2}{n}\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\]
For every fixed \((p,w)\), if \(v^*\) is a unit leading eigenvector in an active block and \(Q_h\) is its explicit subset-lattice lift, then \(a^*=(B/\sqrt n)\Omega^{1/2}Q_hv^*\) attains the inner supremum. The schedule \(c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S\), \(c^*_{i,\varnothing}=0\), and \(Y_i^*(z)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c^*_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j\) satisfies \(\max_{i\in I_n,z\in\{0,1\}^n}|Y_i^*(z)|\le\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|a_S^*|<\infty\) and is a legal least-favourable member of \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\). For fixed nodes \(p\), the weight problem has a semidefinite epigraph and attains its value after quotienting the common null directions of the covariance blocks.  The joint node--weight minimum is attained for every declared feasible tuple.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a declared feasible tuple.  Since \(\omega_d>0\) for every \(1\le d\le\beta\), the diagonal matrix \(\Omega\) is positive definite.  For a fixed feasible pair \((p,w)\), define
\[
A(p,w)=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2},
\qquad b=\Omega^{-1/2}a.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}aggregate\text{-}coefficient\text{-}ellipsoid}\) is exactly
\[
\lVert b\rVert_2^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a\le \frac{B^2}{n}.
\tag{1}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}rollout\text{-}estimator}\) give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a
=b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b.
\tag{2}
\]
The matrix \(A(p,w)\) is real symmetric and positive semidefinite.  To verify the latter property directly, take any \(a\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}\), put \(c_{i,S}=a_S\), \(c_{i,\varnothing}=0\), and use the resulting fixed degree-at-most-\(\beta\) schedule.  Equation~(2), whose covariance identity uses Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\), then identifies \(a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a\) with a variance, so it is nonnegative.  Expanding \(b\) in an orthonormal eigenbasis of \(A(p,w)\) now yields
\[
\sup_{\lVert b\rVert_2^2\le B^2/n}b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The algebraic supremum in (3) equals the supremum over the declared schedule class.  Membership in \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\) implies (1), giving the upper inequality.  Conversely, every \(a\) obeying (1) is realized by the fixed array \(c_{i,S}=a_S\), \(c_{i,\varnothing}=0\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}cey\text{-}envelope\text{-}union\text{-}equivalence}\) places this array in \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\), while (1) places it in \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\).  Thus no algebraic direction in (3) is lost by the schedule restriction.

We next exhibit an attaining schedule.  By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), choose an active block \(h\) whose leading eigenvalue equals \(\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}\), a unit leading eigenvector \(v^*\) of that block, and its unit orthogonal lift \(Q_hv^*\).  Set
\[
a^*=\frac{B}{\sqrt n}\Omega^{1/2}Q_hv^*.
\tag{4}
\]
Orthogonality and block diagonalization give
\[
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a^*=\frac{B^2}{n},
\qquad
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a^*
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For every \(i\in I_n\), define
\[
c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S,
\qquad c^*_{i,\varnothing}=0,
\qquad
Y_i^*(z)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a^*_S\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\tag{6}
\]
The array (6) is fixed before \(U\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}finite\text{-}population}\), and has interaction order at most \(\beta\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}cey\text{-}envelope\text{-}union\text{-}equivalence}\), equivalently the complete-graph member of the defining union, places (6) in \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\).  Its aggregate coefficient vector is \(a^*\), and the first equality in (5) verifies the ellipsoid condition.  Finally, \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) is finite, so
\[
\max_{i\in I_n,\ z\in\{0,1\}^n}|Y_i^*(z)|
\le \sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|a_S^*|<\infty.
\tag{7}
\]
Hence (6) is a legal bounded least-favourable member of \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\), and the second equality in (5) proves attainment of (3).

Combining (2)--(7), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\), we obtain for every fixed feasible pair
\[
\sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=\frac{B^2}{n}\max_{0\le h\le\beta}
\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}
=V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w).
\tag{8}
\]
Taking the two design minima in (8), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}design\text{-}attainment}\) shows that both minima are attained.  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) therefore gives
\[
\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=\frac{B^2}{n}
\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\tag{9}
\]
This proves the saddle equality, the stated least-favourable schedule, and joint attainment.

It remains to give the claimed fixed-node semidefinite epigraph.  Write \(d_\beta=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|\).  For fixed \(p\), let \(\mathbb K(p)\) be the matrix indexed by \((s,S)\) and \((t,T)\), with entry \(K_{st}(S,T)\).  By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), \(\mathbb K(p)\) is the covariance matrix of the vector whose \((s,S)\)-coordinate is \(\prod_{i\in S}Z_i(p_s)\); hence \(\mathbb K(p)\) is positive semidefinite.  Define the linear maps
\[
D_p(w)b=
\bigl(w_0\Omega^{1/2}b,\ldots,w_k\Omega^{1/2}b\bigr)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
L_p(w)=\mathbb K(p)^{1/2}D_p(w).
\]
Direct block multiplication using Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) gives
\[
A(p,w)=D_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\mathbb K(p)D_p(w)
=L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w).
\tag{10}
\]
Let \(C(p)\in\mathbb R^{(\beta+1)\times(k+1)}\) have entries \(C(p)_{0,t}=1\) and \(C(p)_{r,t}=p_t^r\) for \(1\le r\le\beta\), and put \(e=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) says exactly that \(C(p)w=e\).  For every scalar \(\rho\), completing the square in the quadratic form shows
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_{d_\beta}&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d_\beta}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho I_{d_\beta}-L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w)\succeq0,
\tag{11}
\]
because the quadratic form at \((x,y)\) is
\[
x^{\mathsf T}\{\rho I_{d_\beta}-L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w)\}x
+\lVert y+L_p(w)x\rVert_2^2.
\]
It follows from (10)--(11) that, up to the positive factor \(B^2/n\), the fixed-node problem is the explicit semidefinite program
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\operatorname{minimize}_{w,\rho}&\rho\\
\operatorname{subject\ to}&C(p)w=e,\\
&\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_{d_\beta}&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d_\beta}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
Every entry in (12) is affine in \((w,\rho)\), and its value is \(\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}\).  The subspace
\[
\mathcal N_p=\{d\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:C(p)d=0,\ L_p(d)=0\}
\]
consists precisely of feasible directions invisible to the full covariance operator, hence to every reduced covariance block.  If \(w\) is feasible and \(d\in\mathcal N_p\), then \(w+d\) is feasible and \(L_p(w+d)=L_p(w)\), so both the objective and the epigraph constraint are constant on these cosets.  Thus (12) descends to the quotient by the common null directions.  Its fixed-node value is attained by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}design\text{-}attainment}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:quadratic-witness]\label{prop:quadratic-witness}
For the schedule in \(\text{def:quadratic-witness}\), \[\Gamma(u,v)=\operatorname{Cov}_U\{M(u),M(v)\}=\frac{\min(u,v)^2-u^2v^2}{9}.\] The nonbaseline interpolation weights are \(w_x=-\{x(1-2x)\}^{-1}\) and \(w_{1/2}=4(1-x)/(1-2x)\), and \[\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})=\frac{4(1-x)}{9(1-2x)}.\] This risk is strictly increasing on \([1/8,3/8]\), with values \(14/27\) at \(x=1/8\) and \(2/3\) at \(x=1/4\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}quadratic\text{-}witness}\),
\[
M(r)=\frac13Z_1(r)Z_2(r).
\]
By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), or by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) with \(S=T=\{1,2\}\),
\[
\Gamma(u,v)=\frac19\{\min(u,v)^2-u^2v^2\}.
\tag{35}
\]
Let the weights at \(x\) and \(1/2\) be \(w_x\) and \(w_{1/2}\).  The degree-one and degree-two equations in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) are
\[
xw_x+\frac12w_{1/2}=1,
\qquad
x^2w_x+\frac14w_{1/2}=1.
\]
Their determinant is \(x(1-2x)/4>0\) for \(x\in[1/8,3/8]\), so their unique solution is
\[
w_x=-\frac1{x(1-2x)},
\qquad
w_{1/2}=\frac{4(1-x)}{1-2x}.
\tag{36}
\]
The remaining equation fixes \(w_0=-w_x-w_{1/2}\); it contributes no random term because \(M(0)=0\).

Using (35) at the three required pairs gives
\[
9\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=w_x^2(x^2-x^4)
+2w_xw_{1/2}\frac{3x^2}{4}
+w_{1/2}^2\frac3{16}.
\]
Substitution of (36) and collection over \((1-2x)^2\) give
\[
9\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=\frac{1-x^2-6x(1-x)+3(1-x)^2}{(1-2x)^2}
=\frac{4(1-x)}{1-2x}.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\frac{4(1-x)}{9(1-2x)}
=\frac4{9(1-2x)^2}>0,
\]
so the risk is strictly increasing.  Direct evaluation gives \(14/27\) at \(x=1/8\) and \(2/3\) at \(x=1/4\), proving the proposition and verifying the smallest-instance common-uniform arithmetic.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The witness verifies that exact shared-randomizer covariance changes the design criterion and that a familiar interior node need not dominate a more asymmetric node. \textit{Gap.} Published rollout bounds do not contain a legal finite-population example with a strict node-risk ordering generated by cross-stage covariance. \textit{Consumer.} A design reviewer can use this calculation as a regression test that implementation code has not silently replaced shared-hash stages by independent assignments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-sample-robust-inference]\label{thm:finite-sample-robust-inference}
For every \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), and \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\), \[\mathbb E_U[\widehat\tau_{p,w}]=\tau,\qquad \operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})\le V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w).\] If \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)>0\), then for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), \[\Pr_U\!\left\{|\widehat\tau_{p,w}-\tau|\le\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)/\alpha}\right\}\ge1-\alpha,\] equivalently \(\Pr_U\{\tau\in\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{Ch}}_{1-\alpha}(p,w)\}\ge1-\alpha\). If \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)=0\), then \(\widehat\tau_{p,w}=\tau\) almost surely and the singleton interval \(\{\widehat\tau_{p,w}\}\) has coverage one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), and \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\).  Condition on the fixed schedule as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}finite\text{-}population}\), and put \(\bar c_{\varnothing}=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_n}c_{i,\varnothing}\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\) and independence in Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\) imply, for every \(r\in[0,1]\),
\[
\mathbb E_U M(r)
=\bar c_{\varnothing}
+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a_Sr^{|S|},
\tag{13}
\]
because
\[
\mathbb E_U\!\left[\prod_{j\in S}Z_j(r)\right]
=\Pr_U(U_j\le r\text{ for every }j\in S)=r^{|S|}.
\]
Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) gives \(\sum_{t=0}^kw_t=0\) and \(\sum_{t=0}^kw_tp_t^d=1\) for each \(1\le d\le\beta\).  Applying these identities term by term to (13), and using Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}rollout\text{-}estimator}\), yields
\[
\mathbb E_U\widehat\tau_{p,w}
=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a_S.
\tag{14}
\]
Separately, the fixed potential-outcome expansion in Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\) gives the causal contrast
\[
\tau
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}\{Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0)\}
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}
=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a_S.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations~(14)--(15) prove design unbiasedness and, in particular, derive the relationship between the observed-law estimator and the fixed causal estimand.

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a.
\]
Since \(\omega_d>0\) for all \(d\), define \(b=\Omega^{-1/2}a\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}aggregate\text{-}coefficient\text{-}ellipsoid}\) gives \(\lVert b\rVert_2^2\le B^2/n\).  The quadratic-form inequality, Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}saddle}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\) therefore yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
&=b^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}b\\
&\le \frac{B^2}{n}
\lambda_{\max}\{\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\}\\
&=V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Suppose first that \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)>0\).  Set
\[
X=\widehat\tau_{p,w}-\tau,
\qquad
R=\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)/\alpha}.
\]
Equations~(14)--(16) give \(\mathbb E_UX=0\) and \(\mathbb E_UX^2\le V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)\).  The pointwise inequality
\[
X^2\ge R^2\mathbf1\{|X|>R\}
\]
and positivity of \(R^2\) imply
\[
\Pr_U(|X|>R)
\le\frac{\mathbb E_UX^2}{R^2}
\le\alpha.
\tag{17}
\]
Taking complements in (17) and applying Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}chebyshev\text{-}interval}\) proves both displayed coverage formulations.

If \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)=0\), then (16) and unbiasedness give \(\mathbb E_UX^2=0\).  Since \(X^2\ge0\), this forces \(X=0\) almost surely.  Therefore \(\widehat\tau_{p,w}=\tau\) almost surely and the resulting singleton interval covers with probability one.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The interval and its coverage proof are the classical Chebyshev inequality applied to the newly derived design-known radius certificate; Chebyshev coverage itself is not claimed as novel. The deliverable is deliberately limited to a conservative finite-sample interval using the declared coefficient radius and the implemented design. \textit{Gap.} BasseAiroldi2018 supplies the finite-population interference framework, while dependency-graph and network-HAC asymptotic inference work addresses Gaussian approximation or outcome-based variance estimation under additional dependence or approximation conditions. This theorem supplies neither a central limit theorem nor an identifiable outcome-estimated covariance; it only converts the exact common-uniform ellipsoidal variance certificate into known-radius finite-sample coverage. \textit{Consumer.} The owner of a shared-hash ramp can report a finite-sample interval from the implemented nodes, weights, and declared radius, while keeping its robustness class explicit.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:analytic-node-weight-frontier]\label{oeq:analytic-node-weight-frontier}
Characterize every jointly minimizing node--weight pair for \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}\) by explicit necessary-and-sufficient global active-block conditions and a matching dual certificate over both nodes and weights. Determine the nondegenerate region of \((\beta,k,q,\delta,\omega)\) on which shifted Chebyshev--Lobatto nodes attain the minimum, are strictly suboptimal, or tie with other designs. The resolution must specialize at \((n,\beta,k,q)=(3,2,2,1/2)\) consistently with \(\text{prop:quadratic-witness}\).
\end{openendedquestion}
Put \(D=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|\), and, for fixed \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), form the \((k+1)D\)-by-\((k+1)D\) block matrix
\[
 \mathsf C(p)_{st}
 =\Omega^{1/2}\bigl[K_{st}(S,T)\bigr]_{S,T\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), this is the covariance matrix of the stacked, centered, order-weighted monomial vectors at the \(k+1\) stages; hence \(\mathsf C(p)\succeq0\).  Choose any square root \(L_pL_p^{\mathsf T}=\mathsf C(p)\), and define
\[
 B_p(w)=(w^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_D)L_p,
 \qquad
 A_p(w)=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
Block multiplication and the definition of \(G\) give, entry by entry,
\[
 A_p(w)
 =(w^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_D)\mathsf C(p)(w\otimes I_D)
 =B_p(w)B_p(w)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Let \(E(p)\in\mathbb R^{(\beta+1)\times(k+1)}\) have entries \(E(p)_{d,t}=p_t^d\) for \(0\le d\le\beta\), and let \(b=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  The spacing restriction gives \(p_0<\cdots<p_k\), while \(k\ge\beta\); therefore the first \(\beta+1\) columns contain a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix and \(E(p)\) has row rank \(\beta+1\).  Thus \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)=\{w:E(p)w=b\}\) is nonempty.  For this fixed \(p\), its exact optimal weight value is the semidefinite program
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \rho(p)=\min_{\rho\in\mathbb R,\,w\in\mathbb R^{k+1}}\quad&\rho\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&E(p)w=b,\\
 &\begin{bmatrix}
 \rho I_D&B_p(w)\\
 B_p(w)^{\mathsf T}&I_{(k+1)D}
 \end{bmatrix}\succeq0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the lower-right block is positive definite, so the Schur-complement identity makes the displayed matrix inequality equivalent to
\[
 \rho I_D-B_p(w)B_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\succeq0,
\]
which in turn is equivalent to \(\rho\ge\lambda_{\max}\{A_p(w)\}\).  Consequently, using \(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\),
\[
 V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
 =\frac{B^2}{n}\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}\rho(p),
 \qquad
 \rho(p)=\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
 \max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}.
\]
Joint attainment follows from \(\mathrm{thm{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}saddle}\).  The boundary pieces are already exact: \(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\) gives all minimizers when \(q=1\), all minimizers and the value when \(\beta=1\), and, for \(0<q<1\), the computable lower bound

 V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}

 \ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\frac{1-q}{q}.
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}quadratic\text{-}witness}\) proves that at \((n,\beta,k,q)=(3,2,2,1/2)\) one legal schedule has interpolation risk \(4(1-x)/\{9(1-2x)\}\), with values \(14/27\) at \(x=1/8\) and \(2/3\) at \(x=1/4\).  This last comparison concerns one member of the schedule class and therefore is not, by itself, a comparison of the robust values \(\rho(p)\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:hilbert-corner-inverse]\label{lem:hilbert-corner-inverse}
For every integer \(\beta\ge1\), the \(\beta\)-by-\(\beta\) Hilbert Gram matrix \(\mathsf H_\beta\), whose \((d,e)\)-entry is \((d+e-1)^{-1}\), is positive definite and
\[
(\mathsf H_\beta^{-1})_{\beta,\beta}
=(2\beta-1)\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(m=\beta-1\), and index the coordinates of \(\mathsf H_\beta\) by \(0,\ldots,m\).  For every \(a=(a_0,\ldots,a_m)^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb R^{m+1}\), the definition of \(\mathsf H_\beta\) gives
\[
a^{\mathsf T}\mathsf H_\beta a
=\sum_{j=0}^m\sum_{\ell=0}^m\frac{a_ja_\ell}{j+\ell+1}
=\int_0^1\left(\sum_{j=0}^m a_jx^j\right)^2\,dx.
\]
If \(a\ne0\), the polynomial in the last integrand is nonzero.  Its square is continuous, nonnegative, and strictly positive on a nonempty open subinterval of \([0,1]\), so the integral is strictly positive.  Thus \(\mathsf H_\beta\) is positive definite and hence invertible.

We next compute the squared distance of \(x^m\) from the polynomials of degree less than \(m\).  Define
\[
L_m(x)=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{d^m}{dx^m}\left\{x^m(x-1)^m\right\}.
\]
The coefficient of \(x^m\) in \(L_m\) is
\[
\frac{1}{m!}\frac{(2m)!}{m!}=\binom{2m}{m}.
\]
If \(h\) is any polynomial with \(\deg(h)<m\), integration by parts \(m\) times yields
\[
\int_0^1h(x)L_m(x)\,dx
=\frac{(-1)^m}{m!}\int_0^1h^{(m)}(x)x^m(x-1)^m\,dx
=0.
\]
Indeed, every boundary term vanishes because \(x^m(x-1)^m\) and each of its derivatives of order less than \(m\) vanish at both endpoints, while \(h^{(m)}=0\).  The same integration-by-parts identity with \(h=L_m\), now using \(L_m^{(m)}=m!\binom{2m}{m}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1L_m(x)^2\,dx
&=\binom{2m}{m}\int_0^1x^m(1-x)^m\,dx\\
&=\binom{2m}{m}\frac{(m!)^2}{(2m+1)!}
=\frac{1}{2m+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, the middle integral follows without an external formula: if \(I_{a,b}=\int_0^1x^a(1-x)^b\,dx\), one integration by parts gives \(I_{a,b}=bI_{a+1,b-1}/(a+1)\); iterating from \((a,b)=(m,m)\) and using \(I_{2m,0}=1/(2m+1)\) yields \(I_{m,m}=(m!)^2/(2m+1)!\).

Consequently
\[
P_m(x)=\binom{2m}{m}^{-1}L_m(x)
\]
is monic of degree \(m\), is orthogonal in \(L^2[0,1]\) to every polynomial of degree less than \(m\), and satisfies
\[
\int_0^1P_m(x)^2\,dx
=\frac{1}{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^{2}}.
\]
Since \(x^m-P_m(x)\) has degree less than \(m\), orthogonality shows, for every polynomial \(g\) of degree less than \(m\),
\[
\int_0^1\{x^m-g(x)\}^2\,dx
=\int_0^1P_m(x)^2\,dx
+\int_0^1\{x^m-P_m(x)-g(x)\}^2\,dx.
\]
It follows that the first term on the right is the minimum squared norm among all monic degree-\(m\) polynomials.

When \(m\ge1\), partition the positive-definite Gram matrix as
\[
\mathsf H_\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&b\\
b^{\mathsf T}&c
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(A\) corresponds to \(1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}\).  The preceding minimum and completion of the square give
\[
s:=c-b^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b
=\min_{u\in\mathbb R^m}
\begin{pmatrix}u\\1\end{pmatrix}^{\!\mathsf T}
\mathsf H_\beta
\begin{pmatrix}u\\1\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{1}{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^{2}}.
\]
Here \(A\) is invertible because it is a principal submatrix of the positive-definite matrix \(\mathsf H_\beta\), and \(s>0\) by the displayed minimum.  Direct multiplication, rather than an undeclared inverse formula, shows
\[
\mathsf H_\beta
\begin{pmatrix}-A^{-1}b\\1\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}0\\s\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Therefore the last coordinate of \(\mathsf H_\beta^{-1}e_{m+1}\) is \(s^{-1}\), so
\[
(\mathsf H_\beta^{-1})_{\beta,\beta}
=s^{-1}
=(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^{2}
=(2\beta-1)\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}.
\]
If \(m=0\), then \(\mathsf H_1=(1)\), and the same formula equals \(1\).  This proves both assertions for every integer \(\beta\ge1\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This finite-dimensional identity supplies the leading constant in the small-budget moment-barrier asymptotic. \textit{Gap.} The required corner formula is derived directly so that no unstated matrix-inverse result enters the proof spine. \textit{Consumer.} Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\) uses it to compute the exact leading power and constant.

\begin{lemma}[lem:joint-design-attainment]\label{lem:joint-design-attainment}
For every feasible \((n,\beta,k,q,\delta,B,\omega)\) with \(1\le\beta\le n\),
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
=\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w),
\]
and at least one joint minimizer exists.  For every fixed \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), the minimum over \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\) is also attained.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The feasibility conditions give \(k\ge\beta\), \(q>0\), and \(0<\delta\le q/k\).  Hence \(p_t=tq/k\) is feasible.  For any feasible \(p\), the nodes are distinct.  Let \(C(p)\) be the \((\beta+1)\)-by-\((k+1)\) matrix with entries \(C(p)_{d,t}=p_t^d\) for \(0\le d\le\beta\).  A row vector annihilating \(C(p)\) defines a polynomial of degree at most \(\beta\) vanishing at the \(k+1\ge\beta+1\) distinct nodes; it is therefore zero.  Thus \(C(p)\) has full row rank and
\[
C(p)w=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}
\]
has a solution.  This is precisely an element of \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), so the joint feasible set is nonempty and the infimum is finite.

For a feasible pair define
\[
f(u)=\sum_{t=0}^kw_t\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\},
\qquad
r_j=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
Degree-one exactness gives
\[
\int_0^1f(u)\,du=\sum_{t=0}^kw_tp_t=1.
\tag{46}
\]
Apart from finitely many endpoints, \(f=r_j\) on \((p_{j-1},p_j]\) and \(f=0\) on \((q,1]\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), the singleton case of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), and (46) give, for every singleton \(S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}\\
&=\int_0^1\{f(u)-1\}^2du\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge\delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) and the singleton coordinate Rayleigh quotient therefore imply
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
\ge\frac{B^2\omega_1\delta}{n}
\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\tag{48}
\]
The multiplier is strictly positive because \(B>0\), \(\omega_1>0\), \(n\ge1\), and \(\delta>0\).

Let \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\) be a joint minimizing sequence.  Its objective values are bounded, so (48) bounds every \(r_j^{(\ell)}\).  The identities
\[
w_k=r_k,
\qquad
w_j=r_j-r_{j+1}\ (1\le j<k),
\qquad
w_0=-r_1
\tag{49}
\]
where the last follows from \(\sum_tw_t=0\), then bound every coordinate of \(w^{(\ell)}\).  The node class is a closed and bounded finite-dimensional polytope, hence compact.  After taking a subsequence, \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\to(p^*,w^*)\).  The endpoint equalities, spacing inequalities, and exactness equations are closed, so the limit is feasible.  The entries of \(G(p,w)\) are continuous because finite sums and products preserve continuity and \((x,y)\mapsto\min(x,y)\) is continuous.  The largest eigenvalue of a finite symmetric matrix is continuous.  Therefore
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^*,w^*)
=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\]
This proves joint attainment.  With \(p\) fixed, the same argument using (48)--(49) and the closed affine set \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\) proves fixed-node attainment.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The first-moment singleton identity makes objective sublevels coercive in the weight coordinates, which turns compactness of the node polytope into joint attainment. \textit{Gap.} A spectral infimum alone does not certify that an implementable rollout attains it. \textit{Consumer.} The saddle theorem and the strict higher-order finite-stage barrier both require an attained design.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rollout-budget-elbow]\label{prop:rollout-budget-elbow}
For every declared feasible tuple, let \(R_\beta(q)\) and \(\Psi_\beta(q)\) be as in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}degree\text{-}moment\text{-}barrier}\).  For \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), define
\[
f(u)=\sum_{t=0}^kw_t\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\},
\qquad r_j=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
Then \(R_\beta(q)\) is positive definite and, for \(d=1,\ldots,\beta\),
\[
\int_0^q d u^{d-1}f(u)\,du=1.
\]
For every singleton \(S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\int_0^q f(u)^2\,du-1\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge \Psi_\beta(q)\ge\frac{1-q}{q},
\qquad
G(p,w)_{S,S}\ge\delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}\ge
\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\]
Moreover, \(\Psi_1(q)=(1-q)/q\); if \(\beta\ge2\) and \(0<q<1\), then
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)>\frac{1-q}{q},
\qquad
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}>
\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q),
\]
and, for each fixed \(\beta\),
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)\sim
\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2}
\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}q^{1-2\beta}
\qquad(q\downarrow0).
\]
At \(q=1\), the set of all joint minimizers is exactly
\[
\left\{(p,w):p\in\mathcal P_{k,1,\delta},\quad
w_0=-1,\quad w_k=1,\quad w_t=0\ (0<t<k)\right\},
\]
and \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,1,\delta}=0\).  If \(\beta=1\) and \(0<q<1\), every feasible node vector is jointly minimizing and its unique minimizing weights are \(w_0=-1/q\), \(w_k=1/q\), and \(w_t=0\) for \(0<t<k\); hence
\[
V^*_{n,1,k,q,\delta}=\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\frac{1-q}{q}.
\]

Finally, fix \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\) with \(1\le\beta\le n\), \(k\ge\beta\), \(B>0\), and \(\omega_d>0\), and write
\[
D=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|,\qquad
\omega_{\max}=\max_{1\le d\le\beta}\omega_d,\qquad
A_\beta=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2}\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}.
\]
There exists \(q_0>0\), depending only on \(\beta\), such that for every \(0<q\le q_0\) and every \(0<\delta\le q/k\),
\[
\frac{B^2\omega_1A_\beta}{2n}q^{1-2\beta}
\le V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\le \frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
Consequently, for fixed \((n,\beta,k,B,\omega)\),
\(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}=\Theta(q^{1-2\beta})\) as \(q\downarrow0\), uniformly over all spacing rules satisfying \(0<\delta(q)\le q/k\).  The constants are not uniform in \(k\), and this assertion does not identify the exact leading constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a declared feasible tuple and a feasible pair \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Let \(f\) and \(r_j\) be as in the statement.  Because the sum over \(t\) is finite, Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) gives, for every \(1\le d\le\beta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^q d u^{d-1}f(u)\,du
&=\sum_{t=0}^k w_t\int_0^q d u^{d-1}\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\}\,du\\
&=\sum_{t=0}^k w_t p_t^d=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(0\le p_t\le q\) follows from Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}feasible\text{-}node\text{-}class}\).  Put \(\phi_d(u)=d u^{d-1}\) in \(L^2[0,q]\).  Their Gram matrix is
\[
\langle\phi_d,\phi_e\rangle
=\int_0^q de\,u^{d+e-2}\,du
=R_\beta(q)_{d,e}.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(b\in\mathbb R^\beta\) is nonzero, then \(\sum_{d=1}^\beta b_d d u^{d-1}\) is a nonzero polynomial and hence cannot vanish almost everywhere on the interval \([0,q]\), where \(q>0\).  Therefore
\[
b^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)b
=\int_0^q\left\{\sum_{d=1}^\beta b_d\phi_d(u)\right\}^2du>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(R_\beta(q)\) is positive definite, so the inverse in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}degree\text{-}moment\text{-}barrier}\) exists.

Let \(\lambda=R_\beta(q)^{-1}\mathbf 1_\beta\) and set
\[
g_\beta(u)=\sum_{e=1}^\beta\lambda_e\phi_e(u).
\]
Equation~(2) implies \(\langle g_\beta,\phi_d\rangle=1\) for every \(d\), while (1) says \(\langle f,\phi_d\rangle=1\).  Hence \(f-g_\beta\) is orthogonal to the span of \(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_\beta\), and Pythagoras gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^q f(u)^2du
&=\int_0^q g_\beta(u)^2du
+\int_0^q\{f(u)-g_\beta(u)\}^2du\\
&\ge \lambda^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)\lambda
=\mathbf 1_\beta^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)^{-1}\mathbf 1_\beta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

For a singleton \(S=\{i\}\), Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) identify
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}
=\operatorname{Var}_U\!\left\{\sum_{t=0}^k w_tZ_i(p_t)\right\}
=\operatorname{Var}_U\{f(U_i)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The degree-one case of (1) is \(\int_0^qf(u)du=1\).  Since \(f(u)=0\) for \(u>q\), (5) therefore becomes
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}=\int_0^q f(u)^2du-1.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (4) and (6) proves \(G(p,w)_{S,S}\ge\Psi_\beta(q)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz on \([0,q]\) also yields
\[
1=\left\{\int_0^qf(u)du\right\}^2
\le q\int_0^qf(u)^2du,
\tag{7}
\]
so (6) gives \(G(p,w)_{S,S}\ge(1-q)/q\).

On \((p_{j-1},p_j]\), precisely the indicators indexed by \(t\ge j\) equal one, so \(f=r_j\) there.  Consequently, using \(\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})=q\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})r_j=\int_0^qf=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})r_j^2-1\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge \delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The last inequality uses \(p_j-p_{j-1}\ge\delta>0\) and \(1-q\ge0\).

Let \(A=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\).  Its singleton diagonal coordinate is \(A_{S,S}=\omega_1G(p,w)_{S,S}\).  The coordinate Rayleigh quotient gives \(\lambda_{\max}(A)\ge A_{S,S}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) identifies this eigenvalue with the maximum over the blocks used in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\).  Thus every feasible \((p,w)\) satisfies
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\]
Taking the two infima in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) proves
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\tag{9}
\]

For \(\beta=1\), \(R_1(q)=(q)\), and hence
\[
\Psi_1(q)=q^{-1}-1=\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(\beta\ge2\), first impose only the constraint \(\langle f,\phi_1\rangle=1\).  Its unique minimum-norm solution is the constant \(q^{-1}\), with squared norm \(q^{-1}\), by equality in (7).  That constant obeys the second constraint only when
\[
\int_0^q2u q^{-1}du=q=1.
\]
Thus, if \(0<q<1\), imposing the second constraint strictly increases the squared minimum norm.  By (4), (7), and the definition of \(\Psi_\beta\),
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)>q^{-1}-1=\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{11}
\]

For the small-budget expansion, factor
\[
R_\beta(q)=D(q)\mathsf H_\beta D(q),
\qquad D(q)_{d,d}=d q^{d-1/2},
\qquad (\mathsf H_\beta)_{d,e}=\frac{1}{d+e-1}.
\tag{12}
\]
Writing \(b_d(q)=\{d q^{d-1/2}\}^{-1}\), equation~(12) gives
\[
\mathbf 1_\beta^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)^{-1}\mathbf 1_\beta
=b(q)^{\mathsf T}\mathsf H_\beta^{-1}b(q).
\tag{13}
\]
For fixed \(\beta\), the \((\beta,\beta)\) summand in (13) is the unique summand with power \(q^{1-2\beta}\); every other summand has a strictly larger power of \(q\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}hilbert\text{-}corner\text{-}inverse}\) therefore yields
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)
\sim A_\beta q^{1-2\beta},
\qquad
A_\beta=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2}
\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose next that \(\beta\ge2\) and \(0<q<1\).  Equality in (4) would force \(f=g_\beta\) almost everywhere.  The polynomial \(g_\beta\) is not constant, because a constant satisfying its first moment constraint must equal \(q^{-1}\), and the second moment constraint would then force \(q=1\).  On the other hand, \(f\) is constant on each nonempty open interval \((p_{j-1},p_j)\), whose length is at least \(\delta>0\).  If a polynomial agrees almost everywhere with a constant on such an interval, continuity makes it equal that constant throughout the interval, and the polynomial identity theorem then makes it constant everywhere.  This contradiction proves that (4) is strict.  Hence every feasible pair satisfies
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}>\Psi_\beta(q).
\tag{15}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}design\text{-}attainment}\) supplies an attaining pair for \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}\).  Applying the singleton Rayleigh bound at that pair, and using \(B>0\), \(\omega_1>0\), and (15), proves
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}>
\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\tag{16}
\]

We now establish the two-sided small-budget bound in the appended clause.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\text{-}small\text{-}budget\text{-}upper\text{-}certificate}\) applies for every \(0<q\le1\) and every \(0<\delta\le q/k\), and gives
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\le \frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\tag{17}
\]
The multiplier in (17) depends only on the fixed \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\), and not on \(q\) or \(\delta\).  By (14), there exists \(q_0\in(0,1]\), depending only on \(\beta\), such that
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)\ge \frac{A_\beta}{2}q^{1-2\beta}
\qquad(0<q\le q_0).
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (9) and (18), for every \(0<q\le q_0\) and every \(0<\delta\le q/k\), gives
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1A_\beta}{2n}q^{1-2\beta}.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations~(17) and (19) are the claimed pointwise two-sided bounds.  Since neither multiplier depends on an admissible spacing rule \(\delta(q)\), they also prove, uniformly over all such rules,
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}=\Theta(q^{1-2\beta})
\qquad(q\downarrow0).
\tag{20}
\]
The displayed upper multiplier depends on \(k\), so this argument asserts neither uniformity in \(k\) nor an exact leading constant.

It remains to prove the endpoint characterizations.  From the definition \(r_j=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t\) and the exactness equation \(\sum_{t=0}^kw_t=0\),
\[
w_0=-r_1,
\qquad w_j=r_j-r_{j+1}\ (1\le j<k),
\qquad w_k=r_k.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(q=1\), fix arbitrary feasible nodes and take \(w_0=-1\), \(w_k=1\), and \(w_t=0\) for \(0<t<k\).  These weights satisfy every exactness equation because \(p_0=0\) and \(p_k=1\).  Every nonempty treatment monomial is zero at \(p_0\) and one almost surely at \(p_k\), so its weighted readout is constant.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) then gives \(G(p,w)=0\), and Definitions~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) give \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,1,\delta}=0\).

Conversely, a joint minimizer at \(q=1\) has zero certificate.  The matrix \(\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\) is positive semidefinite because, by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), it is a covariance matrix after diagonal rescaling by the positive numbers \(\omega_d^{1/2}\).  Zero largest eigenvalue therefore forces every diagonal entry to vanish.  In particular, (5) gives \(\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}=0\).  Its expectation is one by (1), so \(f=1\) almost everywhere on \([0,1]\).  Each rollout interval has positive length, hence \(r_j=1\) for all \(j\).  Equation~(21) yields exactly \(w_0=-1\), \(w_k=1\), and zero intermediate weights.

Finally, suppose \(\beta=1\) and \(0<q<1\).  If \(S\ne T\) are singletons, then \(S\cap T=\varnothing\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) gives \(G(p,w)_{S,T}=0\).  All diagonal entries equal \(\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}\) by (5).  Since \(\Omega=\omega_1I\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\) imply
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
=\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equality in (7) holds exactly when \(f=q^{-1}\) almost everywhere on \([0,q]\).  Because every rollout interval has positive length, this is equivalent to \(r_j=q^{-1}\) for every \(j\).  Equation~(21) then gives the unique minimizing weights \(w_0=-q^{-1}\), \(w_k=q^{-1}\), and \(w_t=0\) for \(0<t<k\).  They are feasible for every node vector and attain variance \(q^{-1}-1=(1-q)/q\).  Together with (10) and (22), this proves the final equality and the assertion that every feasible node vector is jointly minimizing.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} All \(\beta\) exactness equations produce a degree-sensitive moment barrier, its small-budget law, strict finite-stage separation for higher order, and the complete endpoint characterizations. \textit{Gap.} Spacing-only bounds retain only the first-moment obstruction and therefore miss both the higher-order growth of \(\Psi_\beta(q)\) and the strict gap between its continuous relaxation and every finite-stage design. \textit{Consumer.} A rollout designer can quantify the unavoidable budget loss by interaction order, recover the unique first-order endpoint readout, and recognize that the higher-order barrier is not itself a finite-stage optimum.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cey-envelope-union-equivalence]\label{lem:cey-envelope-union-equivalence}
The noise-free graph-union schedule envelope in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}published\text{-}low\text{-}order\text{-}class}\) equals the unrestricted algebraic degree-at-most-\(\beta\) class:
\[
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}
=\left\{(Y,c):Y_i(z)=c_{i,\varnothing}+
\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j
\text{ for every }i\in I_n,\ z\in\{0,1\}^n\right\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove both inclusions directly from Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}published\text{-}low\text{-}order\text{-}class}\).  First take \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\).  Then some directed graph \(E\subseteq I_n\times I_n\), containing every self-loop, witnesses membership.  For each \(i\in I_n\), the definition requires \(c_{i,S}=0\) whenever \(S\nsubseteq\mathcal N_i(E)\), and it gives
\[
Y_i(z)=c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\mathcal N_i(E)\\|S|\le\beta}}
c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\]
Adding the zero terms with \(S\nsubseteq\mathcal N_i(E)\) turns this display into
\[
Y_i(z)=c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j,
\]
which proves inclusion in the unrestricted algebraic degree-at-most-\(\beta\) class.

Conversely, suppose the last display holds for every \(i\in I_n\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\).  Choose the complete directed graph \(E=I_n\times I_n\).  It contains every self-loop and satisfies \(\mathcal N_i(E)=I_n\) for every \(i\).  Hence every \(S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) is a subset of \(\mathcal N_i(E)\); the support restriction in the graph-union definition is vacuous, and its required polynomial representation is exactly the assumed display.  Thus this complete graph witnesses \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\), proving the reverse inclusion and the equality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The complete graph proves that the noise-free union over unknown static graphs is exactly the unrestricted algebraic low-order schedule class. \textit{Gap.} The comparison class must distinguish the published fixed-graph noisy model from its noise-free schedule projection. \textit{Consumer.} The exact covariance theorem and the unnormalized-envelope witness use the corrected graph-union interpretation without changing their algebra.

\begin{proposition}[prop:unnormalized-published-envelope-dichotomy]\label{prop:unnormalized-published-envelope-dichotomy}
For the unnormalized published schedule envelope,
\[
\inf_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=
\begin{cases}
+\infty,&0<q<1,\\
0,&q=1.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(0<q<1\), the divergence is witnessed by the legal singleton schedules \(c_{i,\{1\}}=A\), with all other coefficients zero, as \(|A|\to\infty\).  Thus a common coefficient or outcome radius is necessary for a finite robust precision guarantee below full rollout.  This statement concerns the unnormalized schedule-envelope union, not the full fixed-graph, noisy model of CortezEichhornYu2022 and not a uniformly \(Y_{\max}\)-bounded comparator class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0<q<1\), a feasible \(p\), and \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Since \(\beta\ge1\), the singleton \(\{1\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\).  For each \(A\in\mathbb R\), define the fixed schedule
\[
c^{(A)}_{i,\{1\}}=A\quad(i\in I_n),
\qquad
c^{(A)}_{i,R}=0\quad(R\ne\{1\}),
\qquad
Y_i^{(A)}(z)=Az_1.
\tag{18}
\]
This schedule obeys Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}finite\text{-}population}\) and has degree one, so it obeys Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\).  The complete-graph direction of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}cey\text{-}envelope\text{-}union\text{-}equivalence}\) shows that (18) belongs to \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\) for every finite \(A\).

For (18), \(M(r)=AZ_1(r)\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) and Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\) therefore give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=A^2G(p,w)_{\{1\},\{1\}}
\ge A^2\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{19}
\]
The factor \((1-q)/q\) is strictly positive.  Letting \(|A|\to\infty\) in (19) proves that the inner supremum is \(+\infty\) for every feasible \((p,w)\); taking the two infima leaves the value \(+\infty\).

Now let \(q=1\).  For an arbitrary feasible node vector, choose \(w_0=-1\), \(w_k=1\), and \(w_t=0\) for \(0<t<k\).  The weights sum to zero and, for every \(1\le d\le\beta\),
\[
\sum_{t=0}^kw_tp_t^d=-0^d+1^d=1,
\]
so they belong to \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Under Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), \(Z(0)=\mathbf0\) and \(Z(1)=\mathbf1\) almost surely.  Thus, for every fixed finite schedule in the noise-free graph-union envelope,
\[
\widehat\tau_{p,w}
=M(1)-M(0)
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}\{Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0)\}
=\tau
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{20}
\]
The design variance in (20) is zero.  Since every variance is nonnegative, this feasible endpoint construction proves that the minimax value is exactly zero.  The divergence in (19) uses only the absence of a common coefficient or outcome radius in the noise-free schedule-envelope union; it makes no assertion about a uniformly bounded subclass or about observation noise in the full published model.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition separates the noise-free CEY graph-union conclusion from both the full noisy fixed-graph model and the finite-radius ellipsoidal saddle. \textit{Gap.} Without one common coefficient or outcome radius across schedules, the graph-union envelope has no finite robust precision guarantee below full rollout. \textit{Consumer.} Platform users can see exactly why a common scale restriction is necessary and why schedule-specific bounds in the published model do not create a uniform minimax radius by themselves.

\begin{proposition}[prop:uniform-coefficient-envelope-transfer]\label{prop:uniform-coefficient-envelope-transfer}
Let \(L>0\), put \(\omega_{\min}=\min_{1\le d\le\beta}\omega_d\), and suppose \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\) satisfies
\[
\max_{i\in I_n}\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|c_{i,S}|\le L.
\]
Then, with \(B^\dagger=\sqrt n\,L/\sqrt{\omega_{\min}}\), one has \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B^\dagger,\omega)\).  Consequently, for every feasible \((p,w)\), the estimator is design-unbiased for \(\tau\), its variance is bounded by the certificate obtained from \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)\) after substituting \(B=B^\dagger\), and the corresponding interval in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}chebyshev\text{-}interval}\) has design coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Positivity of the finitely many order weights gives
\[
\omega_{\min}=\min_{1\le d\le\beta}\omega_d>0,
\]
so \(B^\dagger=\sqrt n\,L/\sqrt{\omega_{\min}}\) is well defined.  For every \(S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\), the aggregate coefficient identity and the triangle inequality give
\[
|a_S|\le \frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}|c_{i,S}|.
\]
Summing over \(S\), interchanging the two finite sums, and using the assumed uniform envelope yield
\[
\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|a_S|
\le\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}
\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|c_{i,S}|
\le L.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(\Omega\) is diagonal with entry \(\omega_{|S|}\), (21) implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a
&=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}\frac{a_S^2}{\omega_{|S|}}\\
&\le\frac1{\omega_{\min}}\sum_Sa_S^2
\le\frac1{\omega_{\min}}
\left(\sum_S|a_S|\right)^2
\le\frac{L^2}{\omega_{\min}}
=\frac{(B^\dagger)^2}{n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\) is assumed, and (22) is exactly the additional ellipsoid condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ellipsoidal\text{-}coefficient\text{-}class}\).  Consequently \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B^\dagger,\omega)\).  Applying Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}sample\text{-}robust\text{-}inference}\) with \(B=B^\dagger\) proves design unbiasedness, the stated substituted variance certificate, and the corresponding coverage bound.  The argument is a conservative class embedding and does not claim exact minimaxity for the smaller uniformly bounded class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A genuinely uniform coefficient bound has a computable conservative embedding into the aggregate ellipsoid. \textit{Gap.} Schedule-specific published bounds do not themselves define a common minimax radius, while a uniform bound can be transferred only conservatively. \textit{Consumer.} A platform with a common coefficient envelope can use the radius certificate without claiming exact minimaxity for its smaller class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-small-budget-upper-certificate]\label{lem:uniform-small-budget-upper-certificate}
Fix \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\) with \(1\le\beta\le n\), \(k\ge\beta\), \(B>0\), and \(\omega_d>0\) for \(1\le d\le\beta\).  Write \(D=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|\) and \(\omega_{\max}=\max_{1\le d\le\beta}\omega_d\).  For every \(q\in(0,1]\) and \(0<\delta\le q/k\), set \(\bar p_t=tq/k\).  On the nodes \(\bar p_0,\ldots,\bar p_\beta\), let \(\ell_t\) be the degree-\(\beta\) Lagrange cardinal polynomial and define \(\bar w_t=\ell_t(1)-\ell_t(0)\) for \(0\le t\le\beta\) and \(\bar w_t=0\) for \(t>\beta\).  Then \(\bar p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(\bar w\in\mathcal W_\beta(\bar p)\), and
\[
\|\bar w\|_1\le
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)q^{-\beta}.
\]
For every feasible node vector \(p\), all nonempty \(S,T\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\), and all stages \(s,t\),
\[
|K_{st}(S,T)|\le2q.
\]
For the displayed uniform-node construction,
\[
\lambda_{\max}\!\left\{\Omega^{1/2}G(\bar p,\bar w)\Omega^{1/2}\right\}
\le 2D\omega_{\max}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta},
\]
and consequently
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\le\frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(h=q/k>0\), so \(\bar p_t=th\).  Every gap of \(\bar p\) is \(h\ge\delta\), while \(\bar p_0=0\) and \(\bar p_k=q\); hence Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}feasible\text{-}node\text{-}class}\) gives \(\bar p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\).

For \(0\le t\le\beta\), define
\[
\ell_t(x)=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le\beta\\j\ne t}}
\frac{x-jh}{(t-j)h}.
\tag{22}
\]
The denominators are nonzero because \(h>0\).  The Lagrange interpolation identity, which follows by observing that the difference of its two sides is a degree-at-most-\(\beta\) polynomial vanishing at the \(\beta+1\) distinct nodes, is
\[
a(x)=\sum_{t=0}^\beta a(th)\ell_t(x)
\quad\text{for every polynomial \(a\) of degree at most \(\beta\)}.
\tag{23}
\]
Taking the difference of (23) at \(x=1\) and \(x=0\) gives
\[
\sum_{t=0}^k\bar w_ta(\bar p_t)=a(1)-a(0).
\tag{24}
\]
For \(a(x)=1\), equation (24) gives \(\sum_t\bar w_t=0\); for \(a(x)=x^d\), \(1\le d\le\beta\), it gives \(\sum_t\bar w_t\bar p_t^d=1\).  Thus Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) yields \(\bar w\in\mathcal W_\beta(\bar p)\).

Because \(\ell_t(0)=\mathbf 1\{t=0\}\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
\|\bar w\|_1
\le1+\sum_{t=0}^\beta|\ell_t(1)|.
\tag{25}
\]
The denominator product in (22) has absolute value \(h^\beta t!(\beta-t)!\).  Moreover, \(0\le jh=jq/k\le1\) for \(0\le j\le\beta\), because \(q\le1\) and \(\beta\le k\).  Hence \(|1-jh|\le1\), and
\[
|\ell_t(1)|
\le\frac{h^{-\beta}}{t!(\beta-t)!}.
\]
Summing and using \(\sum_{t=0}^\beta\{t!(\beta-t)!\}^{-1}=2^\beta/\beta!\),
\[
\sum_{t=0}^\beta|\ell_t(1)|
\le\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}q^{-\beta}.
\]
Since \(q^{-\beta}\ge1\), (25) proves
\[
\|\bar w\|_1
\le\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)q^{-\beta}.
\tag{26}
\]

Now take any feasible \(p\), nonempty \(S,T\), and stages \(s,t\).  Write the two nonnegative terms in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) as
\[
A_{st}=\min(p_s,p_t)^{|S\cap T|}p_s^{|S\setminus T|}p_t^{|T\setminus S|},
\qquad
B_{st}=p_s^{|S|}p_t^{|T|}.
\]
All node coordinates lie in \([0,q]\).  If \(S\cap T\ne\varnothing\), then \(A_{st}\le\min(p_s,p_t)\le q\).  If \(S\cap T=\varnothing\), nonemptiness of both \(S\) and \(T\) gives \(A_{st}\le p_sp_t\le q^2\le q\).  Likewise \(B_{st}\le p_sp_t\le q\).  Therefore
\[
|K_{st}(S,T)|=|A_{st}-B_{st}|
\le A_{st}+B_{st}\le2q.
\tag{27}
\]

Apply (27) to the uniform construction.  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\), the triangle inequality, and (26) yield, for all \(S,T\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|G(\bar p,\bar w)_{S,T}|
&\le\sum_{s,t=0}^k|\bar w_s||\bar w_t||K_{st}(S,T)|\\
&\le2q\|\bar w\|_1^2\\
&\le2\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Set \(C=1+2^\beta k^\beta/\beta!\) and \(A=\Omega^{1/2}G(\bar p,\bar w)\Omega^{1/2}\).  Since \(\Omega\) is diagonal, (28) and the definition of \(\omega_{\max}\) imply
\[
|A_{S,T}|\le2\omega_{\max}C^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\tag{29}
\]
For any \(x\in\mathbb R^D\) with \(\|x\|_2=1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|x\|_1^2\le D\), so
\[
|x^{\mathsf T}Ax|
\le\max_{S,T}|A_{S,T}|\,\|x\|_1^2
\le2D\omega_{\max}C^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\tag{30}
\]
The matrix \(A\) is symmetric by the displayed kernel formula.  Taking a unit eigenvector for its largest eigenvalue in (30) proves the asserted eigenvalue bound.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\) therefore give
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(\bar p,\bar w)
\le\frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}C^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
Finally, the candidate \((\bar p,\bar w)\) is feasible, so Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) bounds its twofold infimum by this candidate value, proving the final display.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Uniform rollout nodes and an explicit Lagrange readout give the covariance-aware upper certificate that matches the degree-moment lower rate. \textit{Gap.} The continuous moment barrier alone supplies no feasible finite-stage design attaining its small-budget exponent. \textit{Consumer.} The rollout-budget proposition uses this legal design witness to close the robust small-budget minimax rate uniformly over admissible spacing rules.

\begin{proposition}[prop:small-budget-minimax-rate]\label{prop:small-budget-minimax-rate}
Fix feasible \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\), with \(1\le\beta\le n\), \(k\ge\beta\), \(B>0\), and \(\omega_d>0\) for every \(1\le d\le\beta\).  For every function \(\delta:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)\) satisfying \(0<\delta(q)\le q/k\) for every \(q\),
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}=\Theta(q^{1-2\beta})
\qquad(q\downarrow0),
\]
uniformly over all such spacing functions.  More precisely, if
\[
A_\beta=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2}
\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2},
\quad D=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|,
\quad \omega_{\max}=\max_{1\le d\le\beta}\omega_d,
\]
then for some \(q_0>0\) depending only on \(\beta\), every such \(\delta\) and every \(0<q\le q_0\) satisfy
\[
\frac{B^2\omega_1A_\beta}{2n}q^{1-2\beta}
\le V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}
\le\frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
The constants are for fixed \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\); no uniform-in-\(k\) assertion or exact leading constant is claimed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\) gives, for every admissible spacing value,
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q),
\qquad
\Psi_\beta(q)\sim A_\beta q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
Since
\[
A_\beta=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2}
\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}>0,
\]
the definition of asymptotic equivalence, with relative error \(1/2\), supplies \(q_0>0\), depending only on \(\beta\), such that
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)\ge \frac{A_\beta}{2}q^{1-2\beta}
\]
whenever \(0<q\le q_0\). Substitution in the preceding variance lower bound yields
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1A_\beta}{2n}q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\text{-}small\text{-}budget\text{-}upper\text{-}certificate}\) applies separately at every \(q\), because its uniform node gaps equal \(q/k\ge\delta(q)>0\), and gives
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}
\le \frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\]
The quantities \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\) are fixed, \(A_\beta>0\), \(D=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|<\infty\), and \(0<\omega_1\le\omega_{\max}<\infty\). Hence both displayed constants are finite and strictly positive, and neither they nor \(q_0\) depends on the admissible spacing function \(\delta\). The two inequalities are therefore exactly the asserted uniform relation
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}=\Theta\!\left(q^{1-2\beta}\right)
\qquad(q\downarrow0),
\]
with no claim of uniformity in \(k\) or identification of an exact leading constant.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition establishes only the fixed-\((n,\beta,k,B,\omega)\) two-sided exponent \(q^{1-2\beta}\), uniformly over admissible spacing rules \(0<\delta(q)\le q/k\). It claims neither an exact leading constant, a uniform-in-\(k\) statement, nor a population-uniform node--weight frontier. \textit{Gap.} The accepted \(\texttt{exp\_rollout\_chebyshev\_minimax\_tv\_envelope\_rollout\_design}\) theorem optimizes a total-variation/diagonal envelope and explicitly leaves exact nested-covariance minimaxity open, so its value yields neither the degree-moment lower barrier nor a feasible exact-covariance upper design. CortezEichhornYu2022 supplies polynomial interpolation with spacing- and magnitude-based variance upper bounds, but no exact-covariance minimax lower bound and no matching two-sided \(q^{1-2\beta}\) law. \textit{Consumer.} A shared-hash rollout designer can read off the unavoidable order of robust variance loss as the terminal traffic budget shrinks.

\section{Interpretation}

Below full rollout, enforcing exactness through interaction order \(\beta\) has an unavoidable covariance cost of order \(q^{1-2\beta}\) as the budget \(q\) vanishes. The lower bound is intrinsic to the polynomial moment constraints, while the matching upper bound is achieved in rate by a concrete feasible uniform ramp. This rate statement is uniform over operational spacing choices \(0<\delta(q)\le q/k\) for fixed \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\), but its constants are not uniform in \(k\) and it does not determine the exact optimal ramp.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The permutation-invariant aggregate-coefficient ellipsoid is a strict subclass of the noise-free CEY graph-union schedule envelope.  The unnormalized union has infinite minimax variance whenever \(q<1\); a fixed-radius product-\(\ell^1\) minimax problem remains distinct and open.  A common uniform coefficient bound transfers conservatively into the ellipsoid but need not share its least-favourable schedule, so the transfer is not an exact minimax equivalence.  The finite three-unit witness is a covariance regression test, not a universal node optimum.  The spectral program and existence of minimizers are proved, but a closed-form global node dual and a full Chebyshev--Lobatto phase map are not.

\section{Honest scope}

Design unbiasedness, the exact common-uniform monomial covariance identity, and the subset-lattice covariance-operator and block reduction hold over the entire noise-free CEY graph-union envelope, equivalently the unrestricted algebraic degree-at-most-\(\beta\) schedule class, without imposing an ellipsoid radius. The exact minimax saddle and its least-favourable adversary, the radius-calibrated variance certificate and Chebyshev coverage, and the \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}\) small-budget minimax law are restricted to \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\). Joint node--weight attainment is proved, but the necessary-and-sufficient analytic node--weight frontier posed by the open question remains open. A design-based central limit theorem is outside the formal claims because pairwise non-independence does not supply a genuine setwise dependency graph. The exact \(\beta=1\) Bernoulli design factor agrees with the noise-free \((1-q)/q\) factor in CEY Corollary 4, while the robustness classes differ. When \(q=1\), the target is observed at the endpoint and the design reduces to the no-extrapolation estimator \(M(1)-M(0)\).
