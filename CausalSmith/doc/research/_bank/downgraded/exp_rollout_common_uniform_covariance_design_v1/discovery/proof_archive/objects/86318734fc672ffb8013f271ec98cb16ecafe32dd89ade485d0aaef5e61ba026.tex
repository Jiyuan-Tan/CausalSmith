Fix \(n\in\mathbb N\), \(1\leq\beta\leq n\), \(B>0\), and \(\omega\in(0,\infty)^\beta\).  First let \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\).  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ellipsoidal\text{-}coefficient\text{-}class}\), membership in this class includes the condition \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\).  Hence
\[
\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)
\subseteq
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]

It remains to prove strictness while respecting the graph-union meaning of the class on the right.  Because \(1\leq\beta\leq n\), one has \(1\in I_n\) and \(\{1\}\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\).  Define coefficients \(c^*\) by
\[
c^*_{i,\{1\}}=2B\sqrt{\frac{\omega_1}{n}}
\quad\text{for every }i\in I_n,
\qquad
c^*_{i,R}=0
\quad\text{for }R\in\{\varnothing\}\cup\mathcal S_{n,\beta},\ R\neq\{1\},
\]
and define the fixed potential-outcome schedule
\[
Y_i^*(z)
=c^*_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{R\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c^*_{i,R}
  \prod_{j\in R}z_j,
\qquad i\in I_n,quad z\in\{0,1\}^n.
\]
This is an algebraic expansion of degree one and therefore of degree at most \(\beta\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}cey\text{-}envelope\text{-}union\text{-}equivalence}\), whose reverse inclusion is realized by the complete self-looped directed graph, then gives
\[
(Y^*,c^*)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]
Thus the witness is in the corrected noise-free graph-union envelope, not merely in an unrelated algebraic class.

For every \(R\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\), the aggregate-coordinate definition \(a_R=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_n}c_{i,R}\) yields
\[
a_R^*
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}c^*_{i,R}
=
\begin{cases}
2B\sqrt{\omega_1/n},&R=\{1\},\\
0,&R\neq\{1\}.
\end{cases}
\]
The positivity condition \(\omega_r>0\) for \(r=1,\ldots,\beta\) implies that the diagonal matrix \(\Omega\), with diagonal entry \(\omega_{|R|}\) at coordinate \(R\), is invertible and that the corresponding diagonal entry of \(\Omega^{-1}\) is \(\omega_{|R|}^{-1}\).  Consequently,
\[
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a^*
=\sum_{R\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}
  \frac{(a_R^*)^2}{\omega_{|R|}}
=\frac{4B^2\omega_1/n}{\omega_1}
=\frac{4B^2}{n}.
\]
Since \(B>0\) and \(n\geq1\),
\[
\frac{4B^2}{n}>\frac{B^2}{n}.
\]
The witness therefore violates the ellipsoid inequality required by Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ellipsoidal\text{-}coefficient\text{-}class}\), so
\[
(Y^*,c^*)\notin\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega).
\]
Combining this exclusion with its graph-union membership and the inclusion proved in the first display establishes
\[
\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)
\subsetneq
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]
