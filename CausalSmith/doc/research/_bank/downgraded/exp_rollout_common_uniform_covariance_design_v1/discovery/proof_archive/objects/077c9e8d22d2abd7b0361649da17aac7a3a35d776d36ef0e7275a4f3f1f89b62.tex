Fix a declared feasible tuple.  Since \(\omega_d>0\) for every \(1\le d\le\beta\), the diagonal matrix \(\Omega\) is positive definite.  For a fixed feasible pair \((p,w)\), define
\[
A(p,w)=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2},
\qquad b=\Omega^{-1/2}a.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}aggregate\text{-}coefficient\text{-}ellipsoid}\) is exactly
\[
\lVert b\rVert_2^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a\le \frac{B^2}{n}.
\tag{1}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}rollout\text{-}estimator}\) give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a
=b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b.
\tag{2}
\]
The matrix \(A(p,w)\) is real symmetric and positive semidefinite.  To verify the latter property directly, take any \(a\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}\), put \(c_{i,S}=a_S\), \(c_{i,\varnothing}=0\), and use the resulting fixed degree-at-most-\(\beta\) schedule.  Equation~(2), whose covariance identity uses Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\), then identifies \(a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a\) with a variance, so it is nonnegative.  Expanding \(b\) in an orthonormal eigenbasis of \(A(p,w)\) now yields
\[
\sup_{\lVert b\rVert_2^2\le B^2/n}b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The algebraic supremum in (3) equals the supremum over the declared schedule class.  Membership in \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\) implies (1), giving the upper inequality.  Conversely, every \(a\) obeying (1) is realized by the fixed array \(c_{i,S}=a_S\), \(c_{i,\varnothing}=0\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}cey\text{-}envelope\text{-}union\text{-}equivalence}\) places this array in \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\), while (1) places it in \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\).  Thus no algebraic direction in (3) is lost by the schedule restriction.

We next exhibit an attaining schedule.  By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), choose an active block \(h\) whose leading eigenvalue equals \(\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}\), a unit leading eigenvector \(v^*\) of that block, and its unit orthogonal lift \(Q_hv^*\).  Set
\[
a^*=\frac{B}{\sqrt n}\Omega^{1/2}Q_hv^*.
\tag{4}
\]
Orthogonality and block diagonalization give
\[
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a^*=\frac{B^2}{n},
\qquad
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a^*
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For every \(i\in I_n\), define
\[
c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S,
\qquad c^*_{i,\varnothing}=0,
\qquad
Y_i^*(z)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a^*_S\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\tag{6}
\]
The array (6) is fixed before \(U\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}finite\text{-}population}\), and has interaction order at most \(\beta\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}cey\text{-}envelope\text{-}union\text{-}equivalence}\), equivalently the complete-graph member of the defining union, places (6) in \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\).  Its aggregate coefficient vector is \(a^*\), and the first equality in (5) verifies the ellipsoid condition.  Finally, \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) is finite, so
\[
\max_{i\in I_n,\ z\in\{0,1\}^n}|Y_i^*(z)|
\le \sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|a_S^*|<\infty.
\tag{7}
\]
Hence (6) is a legal bounded least-favourable member of \(\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\), and the second equality in (5) proves attainment of (3).

Combining (2)--(7), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\), we obtain for every fixed feasible pair
\[
\sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=\frac{B^2}{n}\max_{0\le h\le\beta}
\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}
=V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w).
\tag{8}
\]
Taking the two design minima in (8), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}design\text{-}attainment}\) shows that both minima are attained.  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) therefore gives
\[
\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=\frac{B^2}{n}
\min_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\min_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\tag{9}
\]
This proves the saddle equality, the stated least-favourable schedule, and joint attainment.

It remains to give the claimed fixed-node semidefinite epigraph.  Write \(d_\beta=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|\).  For fixed \(p\), let \(\mathbb K(p)\) be the matrix indexed by \((s,S)\) and \((t,T)\), with entry \(K_{st}(S,T)\).  By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), \(\mathbb K(p)\) is the covariance matrix of the vector whose \((s,S)\)-coordinate is \(\prod_{i\in S}Z_i(p_s)\); hence \(\mathbb K(p)\) is positive semidefinite.  Define the linear maps
\[
D_p(w)b=
\bigl(w_0\Omega^{1/2}b,\ldots,w_k\Omega^{1/2}b\bigr)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
L_p(w)=\mathbb K(p)^{1/2}D_p(w).
\]
Direct block multiplication using Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) gives
\[
A(p,w)=D_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\mathbb K(p)D_p(w)
=L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w).
\tag{10}
\]
Let \(C(p)\in\mathbb R^{(\beta+1)\times(k+1)}\) have entries \(C(p)_{0,t}=1\) and \(C(p)_{r,t}=p_t^r\) for \(1\le r\le\beta\), and put \(e=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) says exactly that \(C(p)w=e\).  For every scalar \(\rho\), completing the square in the quadratic form shows
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_{d_\beta}&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d_\beta}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho I_{d_\beta}-L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w)\succeq0,
\tag{11}
\]
because the quadratic form at \((x,y)\) is
\[
x^{\mathsf T}\{\rho I_{d_\beta}-L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w)\}x
+\lVert y+L_p(w)x\rVert_2^2.
\]
It follows from (10)--(11) that, up to the positive factor \(B^2/n\), the fixed-node problem is the explicit semidefinite program
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\operatorname{minimize}_{w,\rho}&\rho\\
\operatorname{subject\ to}&C(p)w=e,\\
&\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_{d_\beta}&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d_\beta}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
Every entry in (12) is affine in \((w,\rho)\), and its value is \(\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}\).  The subspace
\[
\mathcal N_p=\{d\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:C(p)d=0,\ L_p(d)=0\}
\]
consists precisely of feasible directions invisible to the full covariance operator, hence to every reduced covariance block.  If \(w\) is feasible and \(d\in\mathcal N_p\), then \(w+d\) is feasible and \(L_p(w+d)=L_p(w)\), so both the objective and the epigraph constraint are constant on these cosets.  Thus (12) descends to the quotient by the common null directions.  Its fixed-node value is attained by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}design\text{-}attainment}\), completing the proof.
