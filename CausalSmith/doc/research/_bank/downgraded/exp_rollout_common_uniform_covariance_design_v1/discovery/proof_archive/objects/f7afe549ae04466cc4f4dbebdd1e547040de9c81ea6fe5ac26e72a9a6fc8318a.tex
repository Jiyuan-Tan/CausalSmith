Fix finite \(n\), \(1\le\beta\le n\), \(B>0\), and \(\omega_j>0\) for every \(1\le j\le\beta\). First let \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\). By the membership condition in Definition \(\texttt{def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class}\), one necessarily has \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\subseteq\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]

It remains to prove that the inclusion is strict. Since \(1\le\beta\le n\), the singleton \(\{1\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\). Put
\[
d=2B\sqrt{\frac{\omega_1}{n}},
\qquad
c_{i,\{1\}}=d
\quad(i\in I_n),
\]
and set \(c_{i,S}=0\) for every other coefficient index \(S\in\{\varnothing\}\cup\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\). Define \(Y_i\) from these coefficients by the polynomial formula in Assumption \(\texttt{ass:low-order-schedule}\), so that \(Y_i(z)=d z_1\) for every \(i\in I_n\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\).

To check published-class membership, take the complete directed graph \(E=I_n\times I_n\). It contains every self-loop and satisfies \(\mathcal N_i(E)=I_n\) for each \(i\in I_n\). Hence the only nonzero interaction index \(\{1\}\) is contained in every \(\mathcal N_i(E)\), and it has cardinality one, which is at most \(\beta\). The displayed polynomial representation and the support condition in Definition \(\texttt{def:published-low-order-class}\) therefore give
\[
(Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}.
\]

For this schedule, the definition \(a_S=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_n}c_{i,S}\) yields
\[
a_{\{1\}}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n d
=d,
\qquad
a_S=0
\quad(S\ne\{1\}).
\]
Because \(\Omega\) is diagonal with \(\Omega_{S,S}=\omega_{|S|}\) and every \(\omega_j\) is positive, \(\Omega^{-1}\) exists. Consequently,
\[
a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a
=\frac{a_{\{1\}}^2}{\omega_1}
=\frac{d^2}{\omega_1}
=\frac{4B^2}{n}
>\frac{B^2}{n},
\]
where the strict inequality uses \(B>0\) and \(n\ge1\). Thus the ellipsoid condition in Assumption \(\texttt{ass:aggregate-coefficient-ellipsoid}\), equivalently the final membership condition in Definition \(\texttt{def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class}\), fails. Hence \((Y,c)\notin\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)\), while it belongs to \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\). This proves the asserted proper inclusion and the stated witness.
