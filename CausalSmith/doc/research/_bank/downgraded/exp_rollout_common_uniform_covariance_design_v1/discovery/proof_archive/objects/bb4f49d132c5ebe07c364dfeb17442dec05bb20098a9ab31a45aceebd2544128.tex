Fix a declared feasible tuple and a feasible pair \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Let \(f\) and \(r_j\) be as in the statement.  Because the sum over \(t\) is finite, Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) gives, for every \(1\le d\le\beta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^q d u^{d-1}f(u)\,du
&=\sum_{t=0}^k w_t\int_0^q d u^{d-1}\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\}\,du\\
&=\sum_{t=0}^k w_t p_t^d=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(0\le p_t\le q\) follows from Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}feasible\text{-}node\text{-}class}\).  Put \(\phi_d(u)=d u^{d-1}\) in \(L^2[0,q]\).  Their Gram matrix is
\[
\langle\phi_d,\phi_e\rangle
=\int_0^q de\,u^{d+e-2}\,du
=R_\beta(q)_{d,e}.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(b\in\mathbb R^\beta\) is nonzero, then \(\sum_{d=1}^\beta b_d d u^{d-1}\) is a nonzero polynomial and hence cannot vanish almost everywhere on the interval \([0,q]\), where \(q>0\).  Therefore
\[
b^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)b
=\int_0^q\left\{\sum_{d=1}^\beta b_d\phi_d(u)\right\}^2du>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(R_\beta(q)\) is positive definite, so the inverse in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}degree\text{-}moment\text{-}barrier}\) exists.

Let \(\lambda=R_\beta(q)^{-1}\mathbf 1_\beta\) and set
\[
g_\beta(u)=\sum_{e=1}^\beta\lambda_e\phi_e(u).
\]
Equation~(2) implies \(\langle g_\beta,\phi_d\rangle=1\) for every \(d\), while (1) says \(\langle f,\phi_d\rangle=1\).  Hence \(f-g_\beta\) is orthogonal to the span of \(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_\beta\), and Pythagoras gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^q f(u)^2du
&=\int_0^q g_\beta(u)^2du
+\int_0^q\{f(u)-g_\beta(u)\}^2du\\
&\ge \lambda^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)\lambda
=\mathbf 1_\beta^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)^{-1}\mathbf 1_\beta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

For a singleton \(S=\{i\}\), Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) identify
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}
=\operatorname{Var}_U\!\left\{\sum_{t=0}^k w_tZ_i(p_t)\right\}
=\operatorname{Var}_U\{f(U_i)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The degree-one case of (1) is \(\int_0^qf(u)du=1\).  Since \(f(u)=0\) for \(u>q\), (5) therefore becomes
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}=\int_0^q f(u)^2du-1.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (4) and (6) proves \(G(p,w)_{S,S}\ge\Psi_\beta(q)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz on \([0,q]\) also yields
\[
1=\left\{\int_0^qf(u)du\right\}^2
\le q\int_0^qf(u)^2du,
\tag{7}
\]
so (6) gives \(G(p,w)_{S,S}\ge(1-q)/q\).

On \((p_{j-1},p_j]\), precisely the indicators indexed by \(t\ge j\) equal one, so \(f=r_j\) there.  Consequently, using \(\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})=q\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})r_j=\int_0^qf=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})r_j^2-1\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge \delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The last inequality uses \(p_j-p_{j-1}\ge\delta>0\) and \(1-q\ge0\).

Let \(A=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\).  Its singleton diagonal coordinate is \(A_{S,S}=\omega_1G(p,w)_{S,S}\).  The coordinate Rayleigh quotient gives \(\lambda_{\max}(A)\ge A_{S,S}\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) identifies this eigenvalue with the maximum over the blocks used in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\).  Thus every feasible \((p,w)\) satisfies
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\]
Taking the two infima in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) proves
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\tag{9}
\]

For \(\beta=1\), \(R_1(q)=(q)\), and hence
\[
\Psi_1(q)=q^{-1}-1=\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(\beta\ge2\), first impose only the constraint \(\langle f,\phi_1\rangle=1\).  Its unique minimum-norm solution is the constant \(q^{-1}\), with squared norm \(q^{-1}\), by equality in (7).  That constant obeys the second constraint only when
\[
\int_0^q2u q^{-1}du=q=1.
\]
Thus, if \(0<q<1\), imposing the second constraint strictly increases the squared minimum norm.  By (4), (7), and the definition of \(\Psi_\beta\),
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)>q^{-1}-1=\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{11}
\]

For the small-budget expansion, factor
\[
R_\beta(q)=D(q)\mathsf H_\beta D(q),
\qquad D(q)_{d,d}=d q^{d-1/2},
\qquad (\mathsf H_\beta)_{d,e}=\frac{1}{d+e-1}.
\tag{12}
\]
Writing \(b_d(q)=\{d q^{d-1/2}\}^{-1}\), equation~(12) gives
\[
\mathbf 1_\beta^{\mathsf T}R_\beta(q)^{-1}\mathbf 1_\beta
=b(q)^{\mathsf T}\mathsf H_\beta^{-1}b(q).
\tag{13}
\]
For fixed \(\beta\), the \((\beta,\beta)\) summand in (13) is the unique summand with power \(q^{1-2\beta}\); every other summand has a strictly larger power of \(q\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}hilbert\text{-}corner\text{-}inverse}\) therefore yields
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)
\sim A_\beta q^{1-2\beta},
\qquad
A_\beta=\frac{2\beta-1}{\beta^2}
\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^{\!2}>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose next that \(\beta\ge2\) and \(0<q<1\).  Equality in (4) would force \(f=g_\beta\) almost everywhere.  The polynomial \(g_\beta\) is not constant, because a constant satisfying its first moment constraint must equal \(q^{-1}\), and the second moment constraint would then force \(q=1\).  On the other hand, \(f\) is constant on each nonempty open interval \((p_{j-1},p_j)\), whose length is at least \(\delta>0\).  If a polynomial agrees almost everywhere with a constant on such an interval, continuity makes it equal that constant throughout the interval, and the polynomial identity theorem then makes it constant everywhere.  This contradiction proves that (4) is strict.  Hence every feasible pair satisfies
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}>\Psi_\beta(q).
\tag{15}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}design\text{-}attainment}\) supplies an attaining pair for \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}\).  Applying the singleton Rayleigh bound at that pair, and using \(B>0\), \(\omega_1>0\), and (15), proves
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}>
\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\Psi_\beta(q).
\tag{16}
\]

We now establish the two-sided small-budget bound in the appended clause.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\text{-}small\text{-}budget\text{-}upper\text{-}certificate}\) applies for every \(0<q\le1\) and every \(0<\delta\le q/k\), and gives
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\le \frac{2B^2D\omega_{\max}}{n}
\left(1+\frac{2^\beta k^\beta}{\beta!}\right)^2q^{1-2\beta}.
\tag{17}
\]
The multiplier in (17) depends only on the fixed \(n,\beta,k,B,\omega\), and not on \(q\) or \(\delta\).  By (14), there exists \(q_0\in(0,1]\), depending only on \(\beta\), such that
\[
\Psi_\beta(q)\ge \frac{A_\beta}{2}q^{1-2\beta}
\qquad(0<q\le q_0).
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (9) and (18), for every \(0<q\le q_0\) and every \(0<\delta\le q/k\), gives
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1A_\beta}{2n}q^{1-2\beta}.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations~(17) and (19) are the claimed pointwise two-sided bounds.  Since neither multiplier depends on an admissible spacing rule \(\delta(q)\), they also prove, uniformly over all such rules,
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta(q)}=\Theta(q^{1-2\beta})
\qquad(q\downarrow0).
\tag{20}
\]
The displayed upper multiplier depends on \(k\), so this argument asserts neither uniformity in \(k\) nor an exact leading constant.

It remains to prove the endpoint characterizations.  From the definition \(r_j=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t\) and the exactness equation \(\sum_{t=0}^kw_t=0\),
\[
w_0=-r_1,
\qquad w_j=r_j-r_{j+1}\ (1\le j<k),
\qquad w_k=r_k.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(q=1\), fix arbitrary feasible nodes and take \(w_0=-1\), \(w_k=1\), and \(w_t=0\) for \(0<t<k\).  These weights satisfy every exactness equation because \(p_0=0\) and \(p_k=1\).  Every nonempty treatment monomial is zero at \(p_0\) and one almost surely at \(p_k\), so its weighted readout is constant.  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) then gives \(G(p,w)=0\), and Definitions~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) give \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,1,\delta}=0\).

Conversely, a joint minimizer at \(q=1\) has zero certificate.  The matrix \(\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\) is positive semidefinite because, by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), it is a covariance matrix after diagonal rescaling by the positive numbers \(\omega_d^{1/2}\).  Zero largest eigenvalue therefore forces every diagonal entry to vanish.  In particular, (5) gives \(\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}=0\).  Its expectation is one by (1), so \(f=1\) almost everywhere on \([0,1]\).  Each rollout interval has positive length, hence \(r_j=1\) for all \(j\).  Equation~(21) yields exactly \(w_0=-1\), \(w_k=1\), and zero intermediate weights.

Finally, suppose \(\beta=1\) and \(0<q<1\).  If \(S\ne T\) are singletons, then \(S\cap T=\varnothing\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) gives \(G(p,w)_{S,T}=0\).  All diagonal entries equal \(\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}\) by (5).  Since \(\Omega=\omega_1I\), Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\) imply
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
=\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equality in (7) holds exactly when \(f=q^{-1}\) almost everywhere on \([0,q]\).  Because every rollout interval has positive length, this is equivalent to \(r_j=q^{-1}\) for every \(j\).  Equation~(21) then gives the unique minimizing weights \(w_0=-q^{-1}\), \(w_k=q^{-1}\), and \(w_t=0\) for \(0<t<k\).  They are feasible for every node vector and attain variance \(q^{-1}-1=(1-q)/q\).  Together with (10) and (22), this proves the final equality and the assertion that every feasible node vector is jointly minimizing.
