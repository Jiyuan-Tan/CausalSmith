Let \(\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\) denote the class on the right-hand side of the asserted equality.  We prove both inclusions.

First take \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\).  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}published\text{-}low\text{-}order\text{-}class}\), there is a directed graph \(E\subseteq I_n\times I_n\) containing every self-loop such that, with
\[
\mathcal N_i(E)=\{j\in I_n:(j,i)\in E\},
\]
the schedule has a graph-supported expansion of order at most \(\beta\), and
\[
c_{i,S}=0
\quad\text{whenever }S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}
\text{ and }S\nsubseteq\mathcal N_i(E).
\]
Consequently, for every \(i\in I_n\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j
&=c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\\S\subseteq\mathcal N_i(E)}}
c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j
+\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\\S\nsubseteq\mathcal N_i(E)}}
c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j\\
&=c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\\S\subseteq\mathcal N_i(E)}}
c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j\\
&=Y_i(z).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses the off-neighborhood zero restriction, and the third uses the graph-supported expansion in the same definition.  Thus \((Y,c)\in\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\), proving
\(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\subseteq\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\).

Conversely, take \((Y,c)\in\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\) and choose the complete directed graph
\[
E^*=I_n\times I_n.
\]
This graph contains every self-loop, and for each \(i\in I_n\),
\[
\mathcal N_i(E^*)
=\{j\in I_n:(j,i)\in I_n\times I_n\}
=I_n.
\]
Every \(S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) is a subset of \(I_n=\mathcal N_i(E^*)\).  Hence the graph-support restriction is vacuous for \(E^*\), while the defining equality for membership in \(\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\) is precisely the required degree-at-most-\(\beta\) graph-supported expansion.  The pair \((Y,c)\) is a fixed schedule with no observation-noise component, so it belongs to the noise-free schedule projection witnessed by \(E^*\).  Therefore \((Y,c)\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\), which proves \(\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\subseteq\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\).  Combining the two inclusions gives the claimed equality.
