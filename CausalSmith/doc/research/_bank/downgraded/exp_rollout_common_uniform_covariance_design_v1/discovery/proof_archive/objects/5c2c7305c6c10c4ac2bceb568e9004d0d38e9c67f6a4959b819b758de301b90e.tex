The feasibility conditions give \(k\ge\beta\), \(q>0\), and \(0<\delta\le q/k\).  Hence the equally spaced vector \(p_t=tq/k\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\).  For any feasible \(p\), all \(k+1\) nodes are distinct.  Let \(C(p)\) be the \((\beta+1)\)-by-\((k+1)\) matrix with entries \(C(p)_{d,t}=p_t^d\) for \(0\le d\le\beta\).  If a row vector annihilates \(C(p)\), its coefficients define a polynomial of degree at most \(\beta\) vanishing at the \(k+1\ge\beta+1\) distinct nodes, and hence that polynomial is zero.  Thus \(C(p)\) has full row rank.  The system
\[
C(p)w=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}
\]
therefore has a solution, which is exactly an element of \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  The joint feasible set is nonempty and the infimum defining \(V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}\) is finite.

For a feasible pair define
\[
f(u)=\sum_{t=0}^kw_t\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\},
\qquad
r_j=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
The degree-one exactness equation gives
\[
\int_0^1f(u)\,du=\sum_{t=0}^kw_tp_t=1.
\tag{5}
\]
Apart from finitely many endpoints, \(f=r_j\) on \((p_{j-1},p_j]\) and \(f=0\) on \((q,1]\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), the singleton case of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), and (5) give, for every singleton \(S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}\\
&=\int_0^1\{f(u)-1\}^2du\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge\delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), the variance certificate equals
\[
\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\}.
\]
The coordinate Rayleigh quotient at a singleton and (6) imply
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
\ge\frac{B^2\omega_1\delta}{n}
\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(B>0\), \(\omega_1>0\), \(n\ge1\), and \(\delta>0\) by the declared symbol domains.

Let \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\) be a joint minimizing sequence.  Its objective values are bounded, so (7) bounds every \(r_j^{(\ell)}\).  The identities
\[
w_k=r_k,\qquad
w_j=r_j-r_{j+1}\ (1\le j<k),\qquad
w_0=-r_1
\tag{8}
\]
where the last identity follows from \(\sum_tw_t=0\), then bound every coordinate of \(w^{(\ell)}\).  The node class is a closed and bounded finite-dimensional polytope, hence is compact.  After taking a subsequence, \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\) converges to some \((p^*,w^*)\).  The spacing inequalities, endpoint equalities, and polynomial exactness equations are closed, so the limit is feasible.  The entries of \(G(p,w)\) are continuous because finite sums and products preserve continuity and \((x,y)\mapsto\min(x,y)\) is continuous.  The largest eigenvalue of a finite symmetric matrix is continuous, so
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^*,w^*)
=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\]
This proves joint attainment and the displayed equality with a minimum.  If \(p\) is fixed, the identical argument, using (7)--(8) and the closed affine set \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), proves fixed-node weight attainment.
