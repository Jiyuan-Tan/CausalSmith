Define the displayed robust criterion using the published schedule class from Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}published\text{-}low\text{-}order\text{-}class}\).  Fix \(0<q<1\), a feasible \(p\), and \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Because \(\beta\ge1\), \(\{1\}\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\).  For each \(A\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
c^{(A)}_{i,\{1\}}=A\quad(i\in I_n),
\qquad
c^{(A)}_{i,R}=0\quad
\text{for }R\in\{\varnothing\}\cup\mathcal S_{n,\beta},\ R\ne\{1\},
\]
and let \(Y_i^{(A)}(z)=Az_1\).  This is a degree-one, hence degree-at-most-\(\beta\), fixed schedule, so Assumptions~\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}finite\text{-}population}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\) and the published-class definition show that \((Y^{(A)},c^{(A)})\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{CEY}}_{n,\beta}\) for every finite \(A\).

For this schedule, \(M(r)=AZ_1(r)\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=A^2G(p,w)_{\{1\},\{1\}}
\ge A^2\frac{1-q}{q},
\tag{10}
\]
where the equality follows from Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}rollout\text{-}estimator}\) and Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), and the inequality is Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\).  Since \((1-q)/q>0\), letting \(|A|\to\infty\) in (10) proves that the inner supremum is \(+\infty\) for every feasible \((p,w)\).  Taking the two infima leaves it equal to \(+\infty\).

If \(q=1\), choose the endpoint weights \(w_0=-1\), \(w_k=1\), and zero intermediate weights.  As verified in Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\), these weights are polynomial-exact for every order up to \(\beta\).  Under the common-uniform design, \(Z(0)=\mathbf0\) and \(Z(1)=\mathbf1\) almost surely.  Consequently, for every fixed finite schedule in the published class,
\[
\widehat\tau_{p,w}=M(1)-M(0)
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in I_n}\{Y_i(\mathbf1)-Y_i(\mathbf0)\}=\tau
\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
so its design variance is zero.  Variances are nonnegative; hence the displayed minimax value is zero.  The divergence in (10) uses precisely the absence of a common coefficient or outcome radius.  It therefore does not apply to a subclass with a uniform \(Y_{\max}\) bound, nor does it make a claim about additional graph or observation-noise restrictions in the full published model.
