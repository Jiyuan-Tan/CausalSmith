The feasibility conditions give \(k\ge\beta\), \(q>0\), and \(0<\delta\le q/k\).  Hence \(p_t=tq/k\) is feasible.  For any feasible \(p\), the nodes are distinct.  Let \(C(p)\) be the \((\beta+1)\)-by-\((k+1)\) matrix with entries \(C(p)_{d,t}=p_t^d\) for \(0\le d\le\beta\).  A row vector annihilating \(C(p)\) defines a polynomial of degree at most \(\beta\) vanishing at the \(k+1\ge\beta+1\) distinct nodes; it is therefore zero.  Thus \(C(p)\) has full row rank and
\[
C(p)w=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}
\]
has a solution.  This is precisely an element of \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), so the joint feasible set is nonempty and the infimum is finite.

For a feasible pair define
\[
f(u)=\sum_{t=0}^kw_t\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\},
\qquad
r_j=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
Degree-one exactness gives
\[
\int_0^1f(u)\,du=\sum_{t=0}^kw_tp_t=1.
\tag{46}
\]
Apart from finitely many endpoints, \(f=r_j\) on \((p_{j-1},p_j]\) and \(f=0\) on \((q,1]\).  Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), the singleton case of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\), and (46) give, for every singleton \(S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\operatorname{Var}\{f(U_i)\}\\
&=\int_0^1\{f(u)-1\}^2du\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge\delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\) and the singleton coordinate Rayleigh quotient therefore imply
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)
\ge\frac{B^2\omega_1\delta}{n}
\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\tag{48}
\]
The multiplier is strictly positive because \(B>0\), \(\omega_1>0\), \(n\ge1\), and \(\delta>0\).

Let \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\) be a joint minimizing sequence.  Its objective values are bounded, so (48) bounds every \(r_j^{(\ell)}\).  The identities
\[
w_k=r_k,
\qquad
w_j=r_j-r_{j+1}\ (1\le j<k),
\qquad
w_0=-r_1
\tag{49}
\]
where the last follows from \(\sum_tw_t=0\), then bound every coordinate of \(w^{(\ell)}\).  The node class is a closed and bounded finite-dimensional polytope, hence compact.  After taking a subsequence, \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\to(p^*,w^*)\).  The endpoint equalities, spacing inequalities, and exactness equations are closed, so the limit is feasible.  The entries of \(G(p,w)\) are continuous because finite sums and products preserve continuity and \((x,y)\mapsto\min(x,y)\) is continuous.  The largest eigenvalue of a finite symmetric matrix is continuous.  Therefore
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^*,w^*)
=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\]
This proves joint attainment.  With \(p\) fixed, the same argument using (48)--(49) and the closed affine set \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\) proves fixed-node attainment.
