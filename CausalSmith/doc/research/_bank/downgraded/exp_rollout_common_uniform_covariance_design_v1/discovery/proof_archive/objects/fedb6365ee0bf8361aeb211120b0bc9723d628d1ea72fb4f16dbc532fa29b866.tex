Fix a feasible \((n,\beta,k,q,\delta,B,\omega)\).  Every \(\omega_d\) is positive, so \(\Omega\) is positive definite.  For fixed \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\) and \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\), define
\[
A(p,w)=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2},
\qquad b=\Omega^{-1/2}a.
\]
Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}aggregate\text{-}coefficient\text{-}ellipsoid}\) is exactly
\[
\|b\|_2^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a\le \frac{B^2}{n}.
\tag{11}
\]
By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a=b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b.
\tag{12}
\]
The matrix \(A(p,w)\) is positive semidefinite, because the right side of (12) is a variance for every aggregate vector \(a\), and every such vector is realized by the fixed coefficient array \(c_{i,S}=a_S\).  The finite-dimensional Rayleigh quotient and (11) give
\[
\sup_{\|b\|_2^2\le B^2/n}b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{13}
\]

The supremum in (13) is attained by a legal schedule.  By Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), choose an active block \(h\), a unit leading eigenvector \(v^*\) of \(H_h(p,w)\), and its unit orthogonal lift \(Q_hv^*\).  Put
\[
a^*=\frac B{\sqrt n}\Omega^{1/2}Q_hv^*.
\tag{14}
\]
Orthogonality of the lift yields
\[
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a^*=\frac{B^2}{n},
\qquad
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a^*
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{15}
\]
For every \(i\in I_n\), define
\[
c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S,
\qquad c^*_{i,\varnothing}=0,
\qquad
Y_i^*(z)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a^*_S\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\tag{16}
\]
The array (16) is fixed before \(U\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}finite\text{-}population}\), and has degree at most \(\beta\), as required by Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}low\text{-}order\text{-}schedule}\).  Its aggregate coefficient is \(n^{-1}\sum_i c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S\), and (15) verifies the ellipsoid condition.  Since \(n\) and \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) are finite and every coordinate of \(a^*\) is real,
\[
\max_{i\in I_n,\ z\in\{0,1\}^n}|Y_i^*(z)|
\le\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|a^*_S|<\infty.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus (16) belongs to Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}ellipsoidal\text{-}coefficient\text{-}class}\) and attains (13).

Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}subset\text{-}lattice\text{-}block\text{-}reduction}\), Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) now imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\inf_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
  \inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
  \sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}
  \operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})\\
&\qquad=\frac{B^2}{n}
\inf_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

We next prove that the joint infimum in (18) is attained.  The node class is nonempty because \(p_t=tq/k\) is feasible when \(\delta\le q/k\), and for every feasible \(p\) its nodes are distinct.  Since \(k\ge\beta\), the \((\beta+1)\)-row Vandermonde matrix on those nodes has full row rank, so \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\) is nonempty.  Thus the value in (18) is finite.

Let \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\) be a joint minimizing sequence.  For \(1\le j\le k\), put \(r_j^{(\ell)}=\sum_{t=j}^kw_t^{(\ell)}\).  The exact singleton identity in Proposition~\(\mathrm{prop{:}rollout\text{-}budget\text{-}elbow}\), followed by the coordinate Rayleigh quotient, gives
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1\delta}{n}
\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j^{(\ell)}-1)^2.
\tag{19}
\]
The left side is bounded along a minimizing sequence.  Since \(B>0\), \(\omega_1>0\), and \(\delta>0\), all the tail sums \(r_j^{(\ell)}\) are uniformly bounded.  The identities
\[
w_k^{(\ell)}=r_k^{(\ell)},
\qquad
w_j^{(\ell)}=r_j^{(\ell)}-r_{j+1}^{(\ell)}\ (1\le j<k),
\qquad
w_0^{(\ell)}=-r_1^{(\ell)}
\tag{20}
\]
where the last follows from \(\sum_tw_t^{(\ell)}=0\), show that \(w^{(\ell)}\) is uniformly bounded.  The node polytope \(\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\) is closed and bounded in finite dimension, hence compact.  After taking a subsequence, \((p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})\to(p^*,w^*)\).  The polynomial exactness equations and node inequalities are closed, so \(p^*\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\) and \(w^*\in\mathcal W_\beta(p^*)\).  The kernel entries are continuous in \(p\), including across the diagonal because \(\min(\cdot,\cdot)\) is continuous; finite sums, matrix eigenvalues, and multiplication by \(w\) preserve continuity.  Therefore
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^*,w^*)
=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p^{(\ell)},w^{(\ell)})
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}.
\tag{21}
\]
Combining (13)--(18) with (21) replaces both joint infima in (18) by minima and proves the asserted joint attainment.

For completeness, fix \(p\) and expose the promised computable semidefinite epigraph.  Let \(d=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|\), and let \(\mathbb K(p)\) be the block matrix indexed by \((s,S)\) and \((t,T)\), with entry \(K_{st}(S,T)\).  Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) identifies \(\mathbb K(p)\) as a covariance matrix, so \(\mathbb K(p)\succeq0\).  Define
\[
D_p(w)b=(w_0\Omega^{1/2}b,\ldots,w_k\Omega^{1/2}b)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
L_p(w)=\mathbb K(p)^{1/2}D_p(w).
\]
Direct block multiplication gives
\[
A(p,w)=D_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\mathbb K(p)D_p(w)
=L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w).
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(C(p)_{r,t}=p_t^r\) for \(0\le r\le\beta\), and let \(e=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  The weight constraints are \(C(p)w=e\).  Completing the square in a quadratic form shows directly that
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_d&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho I_d-L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w)\succeq0.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, the quadratic form at \((x,y)\) equals
\(x^{\mathsf T}\{\rho I_d-L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w)\}x+
\|y+L_p(w)x\|_2^2\).  By (22), the fixed-node epigraph is therefore the semidefinite system
\[
C(p)w=e,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_d&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\tag{24}
\]
whose entries are affine in \((w,\rho)\).  Fixed-node attainment follows either from the joint sublevel argument (19)--(20) with \(p\) fixed, or after quotienting by the common null space \(\mathcal N_p=\ker\{w\mapsto L_p(w)\}\): the objective is constant on its cosets, and (19)--(20) provide a bounded representative for every feasible objective sublevel.  A convergent subsequence remains feasible by closedness of (24) and attains the fixed-node value.  This proves the semidefinite and attainment assertions without claiming the still-open global analytic node certificate.
