Fix a declared feasible tuple and \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  For a singleton \(S=\{i\}\), define
\[
f(u)=\sum_{t=0}^k w_t\mathbf 1\{u\le p_t\},
\qquad
r_j=\sum_{t=j}^k w_t\quad(1\le j\le k),
\qquad r_{k+1}=0.
\]
The exactness equations in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) give
\[
\mathbb E_U f(U_i)=\sum_{t=0}^k w_tp_t=1.
\tag{1}
\]
The separation condition in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}feasible\text{-}node\text{-}class}\) gives \(p_j-p_{j-1}\ge\delta>0\).  Apart from interval endpoints, which have uniform probability zero, \(f(U_i)=r_j\) when \(U_i\in(p_{j-1},p_j]\), and \(f(U_i)=0\) when \(U_i\in(q,1]\).  By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), equation (1), and direct integration,
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(p,w)_{S,S}
&=\operatorname{Var}_U\{f(U_i)\}\\
&=\int_0^1\{f(u)-1\}^2\,du\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^k(p_j-p_{j-1})(r_j-1)^2+(1-q)\\
&\ge \delta\sum_{j=1}^k(r_j-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The first equality is also the singleton case of Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\).

There is a second lower bound which is sharp.  Since \(f=0\) off \([0,q]\), Cauchy--Schwarz for the ordinary Lebesgue integral gives
\[
1=\left\{\int_0^q f(u)\,du\right\}^2
\le q\int_0^q f(u)^2\,du.
\]
Thus \(\mathbb E_U f(U_i)^2\ge q^{-1}\), and (1) implies
\[
G(p,w)_{S,S}=\operatorname{Var}_U\{f(U_i)\}
\ge \frac1q-1=\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(S\) is a singleton, the corresponding diagonal entry of \(\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}\) is \(\omega_1G(p,w)_{S,S}\).  The Rayleigh quotient evaluated at the coordinate vector indexed by \(S\), (3), Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}variance\text{-}certificate}\), and Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}saddle}\) yield, for \(0<q<1\),
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta}
\ge \frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\frac{1-q}{q}.
\tag{4}
\]

Suppose next that \(q=1\).  For any feasible node vector take
\[
w_0=-1,\qquad w_k=1,\qquad w_t=0\quad(0<t<k).
\tag{5}
\]
For each \(1\le d\le\beta\), \(p_0^d=0\) and \(p_k^d=1\), so (5) belongs to \(\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Every nonempty monomial equals zero at \(p_0=0\) and one almost surely at \(p_k=1\).  Its weighted readout is therefore constant, so \(G(p,w)=0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)=0\).  Nonnegativity of a variance certificate gives
\[
V^*_{n,\beta,k,1,\delta}=0.
\tag{6}
\]

Finally suppose \(\beta=1\).  The interaction subsets are the singletons.  If \(S\ne T\), then \(S\cap T=\varnothing\), and the formula in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}covariance\text{-}operator}\) gives \(K_{st}(S,T)=p_sp_t-p_sp_t=0\).  All diagonal entries of \(G(p,w)\) equal \(\operatorname{Var}_U\{f(U_i)\}\), while \(\Omega=\omega_1I\).  Hence
\[
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(p,w)=\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\operatorname{Var}_U\{f(U_i)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
For every feasible \(p\), the endpoint weights
\[
w_0=-q^{-1},\qquad w_k=q^{-1},\qquad w_t=0\quad(0<t<k)
\tag{8}
\]
belong to \(\mathcal W_1(p)\) and give \(f(u)=q^{-1}\mathbf 1\{u\le q\}\) almost everywhere.  Therefore their variance is \((1-q)/q\).  Equations (3) and (7) prove
\[
V^*_{n,1,k,q,\delta}=\frac{B^2\omega_1}{n}\frac{1-q}{q}
\qquad(0<q\le1).
\tag{9}
\]
To prove uniqueness, equality in the Cauchy--Schwarz step above holds only when \(f\) is constant almost everywhere on \([0,q]\).  Its integral is one, so the constant must be \(q^{-1}\).  Every interval \((p_{j-1},p_j]\) has positive length, and hence \(r_j=q^{-1}\) for all \(j\).  Since \(w_k=r_k\), \(w_j=r_j-r_{j+1}\) for \(1\le j<k\), and \(w_0=-r_1\) by \(\sum_tw_t=0\), the weights in (8) are the unique minimizer for every fixed feasible \(p\).
