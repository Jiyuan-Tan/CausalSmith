Fix finite \(n\in\mathbb N\) and \(1\leq\beta\leq n\).  First take
\((Y,c)\in\mathcal U_{n,\beta}\).  By the definition of the union there is
an \(E\in\mathfrak G_n\) such that, for every \(i\in I_n\), every
\(S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) not contained in \(\mathcal N_i(E)\) has
\(c_{i,S}=0\), and the graph-supported expansion holds.  Consequently, for
every \(i\in I_n\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_i(z)
&=c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\\S\subseteq\mathcal N_i(E)}}
c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j\\
&=c_{i,\varnothing}
+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality follows because each term added to the second sum has
\(S\nsubseteq\mathcal N_i(E)\) and hence has zero coefficient.  Thus
\((Y,c)\in\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\), proving
\(\mathcal U_{n,\beta}\subseteq\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\).

Conversely, take \((Y,c)\in\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\) and choose the complete
static directed graph
\[
E^{\mathrm{all}}=I_n\times I_n.
\]
For each \(i\in I_n\), \((i,i)\in E^{\mathrm{all}}\), so
\(E^{\mathrm{all}}\in\mathfrak G_n\), and
\[
\mathcal N_i(E^{\mathrm{all}})
=\{j\in I_n:(j,i)\in I_n\times I_n\}
=I_n.
\]
Every \(S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) is a subset of \(I_n\).  Hence the
coefficient-vanishing requirement outside \(\mathcal N_i(E^{\mathrm{all}})\)
is vacuous, and
\[
\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\\
S\subseteq\mathcal N_i(E^{\mathrm{all}})}}
c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j
=
\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}c_{i,S}\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\]
The defining expansion of \(\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\) is therefore the
graph-supported expansion for \(E^{\mathrm{all}}\).  It follows that
\((Y,c)\in\mathcal U_{n,\beta}\), proving
\(\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\subseteq\mathcal U_{n,\beta}\).  The two inclusions
give \(\mathcal U_{n,\beta}=\mathcal A_{n,\beta}\).
