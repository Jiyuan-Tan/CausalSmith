Put \(m=\beta-1\).  The matrix \(\mathsf H_\beta\) is the Gram matrix in \(L^2[0,1]\) of the linearly independent functions \(1,x,\ldots,x^m\).  Indeed, for every \(b=(b_0,\ldots,b_m)^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
b^{\mathsf T}\mathsf H_\beta b
=\int_0^1\left(\sum_{j=0}^m b_jx^j\right)^2dx.
\]
If the right side is zero, the polynomial vanishes almost everywhere and hence identically, so \(b=0\).  Thus \(\mathsf H_\beta\) is positive definite.

Define the shifted Rodrigues polynomial
\[
L_m(x)=\frac1{m!}\frac{d^m}{dx^m}\{x^m(x-1)^m\}.
\]
The coefficient of \(x^m\) in \(L_m\) is
\[
\frac1{m!}\frac{(2m)!}{m!}=\binom{2m}{m}.
\tag{1}
\]
For any polynomial \(h\) of degree less than \(m\), integrating by parts \(m\) times gives
\[
\int_0^1h(x)L_m(x)\,dx
=\frac{(-1)^m}{m!}\int_0^1h^{(m)}(x)x^m(x-1)^m\,dx=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Every boundary term is zero because \(x^m(x-1)^m\) and its derivatives of orders below \(m\) vanish at both endpoints.  Applying the same calculation with \(h=L_m\), using \(L_m^{(m)}=m!\binom{2m}{m}\) from (1), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1L_m(x)^2dx
&=\binom{2m}{m}\int_0^1x^m(1-x)^m\,dx\\
&=\binom{2m}{m}\frac{(m!)^2}{(2m+1)!}
=\frac1{2m+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
For completeness, if \(I_{a,b}=\int_0^1x^a(1-x)^b dx\), integration by parts gives
\[
I_{a,b}=\frac{b}{a+1}I_{a+1,b-1}.
\]
Iterating this identity from \((a,b)=(m,m)\) and using \(I_{2m,0}=(2m+1)^{-1}\) gives \(I_{m,m}=(m!)^2/(2m+1)!\), which is the middle equality in (3).

By (1)--(2),
\[
P_m(x)=\binom{2m}{m}^{-1}L_m(x)
\]
is the unique monic degree-\(m\) polynomial orthogonal to every polynomial of degree at most \(m-1\).  It is therefore the residual from the least-squares projection of \(x^m\) onto \(\operatorname{span}(1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1})\).  By (3), its squared residual norm is
\[
\|P_m\|_2^2
=\frac1{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Writing \(\mathsf H_\beta\) in its last-coordinate block form, elementary completion of the square shows that the minimum in (4) is the Schur complement of the leading \(m\)-by-\(m\) block.  The block inverse identity makes the reciprocal of this Schur complement equal to \((\mathsf H_\beta^{-1})_{\beta,\beta}\).  Hence
\[
(\mathsf H_\beta^{-1})_{\beta,\beta}
=(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^2
=(2\beta-1)\binom{2\beta-2}{\beta-1}^2,
\]
as required.  When \(m=0\), the same calculation reads \(\mathsf H_1=(1)\), so the conclusion also holds.
