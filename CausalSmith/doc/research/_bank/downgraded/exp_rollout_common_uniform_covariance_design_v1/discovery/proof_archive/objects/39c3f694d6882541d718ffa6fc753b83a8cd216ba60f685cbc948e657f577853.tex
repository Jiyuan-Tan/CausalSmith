Under Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}quadratic\text{-}witness}\),
\[
M(r)=\frac13Z_1(r)Z_2(r).
\]
By Assumption~\(\mathrm{ass{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}rollout}\), or by Lemma~\(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}uniform\text{-}monomial\text{-}covariance}\) with \(S=T=\{1,2\}\),
\[
\Gamma(u,v)=\frac19\{\min(u,v)^2-u^2v^2\}.
\tag{35}
\]
Let the weights at \(x\) and \(1/2\) be \(w_x\) and \(w_{1/2}\).  The degree-one and degree-two equations in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}unbiased\text{-}weight\text{-}class}\) are
\[
xw_x+\frac12w_{1/2}=1,
\qquad
x^2w_x+\frac14w_{1/2}=1.
\]
Their determinant is \(x(1-2x)/4>0\) for \(x\in[1/8,3/8]\), so their unique solution is
\[
w_x=-\frac1{x(1-2x)},
\qquad
w_{1/2}=\frac{4(1-x)}{1-2x}.
\tag{36}
\]
The remaining equation fixes \(w_0=-w_x-w_{1/2}\); it contributes no random term because \(M(0)=0\).

Using (35) at the three required pairs gives
\[
9\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=w_x^2(x^2-x^4)
+2w_xw_{1/2}\frac{3x^2}{4}
+w_{1/2}^2\frac3{16}.
\]
Substitution of (36) and collection over \((1-2x)^2\) give
\[
9\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=\frac{1-x^2-6x(1-x)+3(1-x)^2}{(1-2x)^2}
=\frac{4(1-x)}{1-2x}.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\frac{4(1-x)}{9(1-2x)}
=\frac4{9(1-2x)^2}>0,
\]
so the risk is strictly increasing.  Direct evaluation gives \(14/27\) at \(x=1/8\) and \(2/3\) at \(x=1/4\), proving the proposition and verifying the smallest-instance common-uniform arithmetic.
