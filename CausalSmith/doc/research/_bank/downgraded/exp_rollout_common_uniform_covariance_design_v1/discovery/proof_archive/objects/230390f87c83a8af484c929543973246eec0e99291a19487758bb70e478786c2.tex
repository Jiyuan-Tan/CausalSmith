Fix feasible \((n,\beta,k,q,\delta,B,\omega)\), a node vector \(p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}\), and weights \(w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)\).  Positivity of every \(\omega_d\) makes \(\Omega\) positive definite.  Define
\[
A(p,w)=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2},
\qquad b=\Omega^{-1/2}a.
\]
The aggregate-coefficient condition in ass:aggregate-coefficient-ellipsoid is exactly
\[
\|b\|_2^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a\le \frac{B^2}{n}.
\tag{7}
\]
By lem:common-uniform-monomial-covariance,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})
=a^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a=b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b.
\tag{8}
\]
The matrix \(A(p,w)\) is positive semidefinite because the right side of (8) is a variance for every \(a\).  The Rayleigh quotient and (7) therefore yield
\[
\sup_{\|b\|_2^2\le B^2/n}b^{\mathsf T}A(p,w)b
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is not merely an upper bound.  By lem:subset-lattice-block-reduction, choose an active block \(h\), a unit leading eigenvector \(v^*\) of that block, and its unit lift \(Q_hv^*\).  Set
\[
a^*=\frac B{\sqrt n}\Omega^{1/2}Q_hv^*.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}\Omega^{-1}a^*=\frac{B^2}{n}
\quad\text{and}\quad
(a^*)^{\mathsf T}G(p,w)a^*
=\frac{B^2}{n}\lambda_{\max}\{A(p,w)\},
\tag{11}
\]
so (10) attains (9).

It remains to verify that this algebraic maximizer is a legal fixed potential-outcome schedule, rather than an unattainable covariance direction.  Define
\[
c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S,
\qquad c^*_{i,\varnothing}=0,
\qquad
Y_i^*(z)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}a^*_S\prod_{j\in S}z_j.
\tag{12}
\]
The array in (12) is fixed before \(U\), as required by ass:fixed-finite-population, and has degree at most \(\beta\), as required by ass:low-order-schedule.  Its aggregate coefficient is
\(n^{-1}\sum_i c^*_{i,S}=a^*_S\), and (11) verifies ass:aggregate-coefficient-ellipsoid.  Since \(n\) is finite, \(\mathcal S_{n,\beta}\) is finite and every coordinate of \(a^*\) is real; hence
\[
\max_{i\in I_n,\ z\in\{0,1\}^n}|Y_i^*(z)|
\le\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_{n,\beta}}|a^*_S|<\infty.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus (12) belongs to def:ellipsoidal-coefficient-class and proves that the supremum over that class equals (9).

Applying lem:subset-lattice-block-reduction to (9), and then taking the two displayed infima, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\inf_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
  \inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
  \sup_{(Y,c)\in\mathcal C_{n,\beta}(B,\omega)}
  \operatorname{Var}_U(\widehat\tau_{p,w})\\
&\qquad=\frac{B^2}{n}
\inf_{p\in\mathcal P_{k,q,\delta}}
\inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\beta(p)}
\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}
=V^*_{n,\beta,k,q,\delta},
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
where the last equality is def:exact-saddle together with the definition of \(V_{\mathrm{rob}}\).

Finally fix \(p\).  Let \(d=|\mathcal S_{n,\beta}|\), and let \(\mathbb K(p)\) be the block matrix indexed by \((s,S)\) and \((t,T)\) with entry \(K_{st}(S,T)\).  The first part of lem:common-uniform-monomial-covariance identifies \(\mathbb K(p)\) as a covariance matrix, so \(\mathbb K(p)\succeq0\).  Define the linear map
\[
D_p(w)b=(w_0\Omega^{1/2}b,\ldots,w_k\Omega^{1/2}b)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad L_p(w)=\mathbb K(p)^{1/2}D_p(w).
\]
Then direct block multiplication gives
\[
A(p,w)=D_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\mathbb K(p)D_p(w)=L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}L_p(w).
\tag{15}
\]
Let \(C(p)_{r,t}=p_t^r\) for \(r=0,\ldots,\beta\), and let \(e=(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  The weight restrictions are \(C(p)w=e\).  By the Schur-complement identity, the epigraph of the fixed-node objective is the semidefinite system
\[
C(p)w=e,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho I_d&L_p(w)^{\mathsf T}\\
L_p(w)&I_{(k+1)d}
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\tag{16}
\]
because (16) is equivalent to \(\rho I_d-A(p,w)\succeq0\).  The entries in (16) are affine in \((w,\rho)\).  The gaps in def:feasible-node-class make \(p_0,\ldots,p_k\) distinct; since \(k\ge\beta\), the Vandermonde matrix \(C(p)\) has row rank \(\beta+1\), so the affine constraint is nonempty.

To prove attainment, set \(\mathcal N_p=\ker\{w\mapsto L_p(w)\}\).  The objective is unchanged along \(\mathcal N_p\).  On \(\mathcal N_p^\perp\), injectivity and finite-dimensional norm equivalence imply that bounded \(\|L_p(w)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) forces the orthogonal projection of \(w\) onto \(\mathcal N_p^\perp\) to be bounded.  For a feasible minimizing sequence write \(w_j=x_j+y_j\), with \(x_j\in\mathcal N_p^\perp\) and \(y_j\in\mathcal N_p\).  Replace \(y_j\) by the minimum-norm solution in \(\mathcal N_p\) of
\[
C(p)y=e-C(p)x_j.
\]
Such a solution exists because the original \(y_j\) is one; it is the value of a fixed Moore--Penrose inverse applied to a bounded vector, and is therefore bounded.  This replacement preserves feasibility and the objective.  A convergent subsequence then exists, its limit satisfies (16), and continuity gives the minimum.  This is precisely attainment after quotienting the common covariance-null directions.  Equation (14), the legal schedule (12), and the fixed-node certificate (16) prove every assertion of the theorem; no assertion about joint node--weight attainment is needed.
