Put \(\mathcal X_r=\{S\subseteq I_n:|S|=r\}\).  For \(0\le j\le r\le n\), let \(R_{rj}\) be the inclusion matrix with rows indexed by \(\mathcal X_r\), columns indexed by \(\mathcal X_j\), and entries
\[
(R_{rj})_{S,A}=\mathbf 1\{A\subseteq S\}.
\]
These matrices are explicit finite zero--one matrices.  For every admissible \(r,\ell,j\), direct counting gives
\[
(R_{rj}R_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T})_{S,T}
=\binom{|S\cap T|}{j}.
\tag{1}
\]

We first give a self-contained orthogonal decomposition for these incidence matrices.  Define the down operator \(D_j=R_{j,j-1}^{\mathsf T}\), the up operator \(U_j=R_{j+1,j}\), and
\[
\mathcal L_0=\mathbb R,
\qquad
\mathcal L_h=\ker(D_h)\quad (h\ge1).
\]
If \(f\) is a function on \(\mathcal X_j\), then counting separately the term that leaves a set unchanged and the terms that exchange one element proves
\[
D_{j+1}U_jf-U_{j-1}D_jf=(n-2j)f.
\tag{2}
\]
Iterating (2) shows the following three facts.  For \(0\le h\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), \(f,g\in\mathcal L_h\), and \(h\le r\le n-h\),
\[
\langle R_{rh}f,R_{rh}g\rangle
=\binom{n-2h}{r-h}\langle f,g\rangle;
\tag{3}
\]
the lifted spaces arising from distinct values of \(h\) are orthogonal; and
\[
\mathbb R^{\mathcal X_r}
=\bigoplus_{h=0}^{\min(r,n-r)}R_{rh}\mathcal L_h.
\tag{4}
\]
For completeness, the induction step is obtained by taking the inner product of (2) with a vector in \(\ker D_j\); it gives injectivity of \(U_j\) when \(j<n/2\) and the squared-norm multiplier \(n-2j\).  Applying the same step successively from level \(h\) to level \(r\), and using \(R_{rh}=U_{r-1}\cdots U_h/(r-h)!\), gives (3).  Orthogonality follows by moving the down operators to the other side of the inner product, where they annihilate the harmonic vector.  Finally, rank--nullity applied at each step gives
\(\dim\mathcal L_h=\binom nh-\binom n{h-1}\); summing these dimensions over \(h\le\min(r,n-r)\) gives \(\binom nr\), proving (4).

Set \(d_h=\dim\mathcal L_h\), and choose an orthonormal basis \(e_{h,1},\ldots,e_{h,d_h}\) of each \(\mathcal L_h\).  By (3)--(4), the vectors
\[
q_{r,h,j}
=\binom{n-2h}{r-h}^{-1/2}R_{rh}e_{h,j},
\qquad
h\le r\le \min(\beta,n-h),
\tag{5}
\]
over all admissible \((r,h,j)\), form an orthonormal basis of the direct sum of the nonempty subset levels \(1,\ldots,\beta\).  Thus (5) constructs the orthogonal transform \(Q\) in the statement using only finite incidence matrices and orthonormal bases of explicit null spaces.

Let
\[
A=\Omega^{1/2}G(p,w)\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
By def:exact-covariance-operator, \(G(p,w)_{S,T}\) depends on \((S,T)\) only through \((|S|,|T|,|S\cap T|)\); multiplication by \(\Omega^{1/2}\), whose diagonal entry on level \(r\) is \(\sqrt{\omega_r}\), preserves this property.  Therefore the level-
\((r,\ell)\) part of \(A\) has entries \(\phi_{r\ell}(|S\cap T|)\) for an explicitly known function \(\phi_{r\ell}\).  Define the forward difference by \((\Delta f)(c)=f(c+1)-f(c)\).  The finite Newton expansion
\[
\phi_{r\ell}(c)
=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,\ell)}(\Delta^j\phi_{r\ell})(0)\binom cj,
\qquad 0\le c\le\min(r,\ell),
\tag{6}
\]
and (1) express this level block as a finite linear combination of \(R_{rj}R_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T}\).

The scalar action needed to diagonalize (6) is also explicit.  If \(f\in\mathcal L_h\), repeated use of (2), or equivalently a direct count followed by the cancellation \(D_hf=0\), gives
\[
R_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T}R_{\ell h}f
=\begin{cases}
0,&j<h,\\
\displaystyle\binom{n-h-j}{\ell-j}R_{jh}f,&h\le j\le\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
The second coefficient can be checked without any representation-theoretic premise: since
\(R_{\ell j}R_{jh}=\binom{\ell-h}{j-h}R_{\ell h}\), taking the inner product with \(R_{jh}f\) and using (3) gives
\[
\frac{\binom{\ell-h}{j-h}\binom{n-2h}{\ell-h}}
{\binom{n-2h}{j-h}}
=\binom{n-h-j}{\ell-j}.
\]
Writing \(\gamma_{r\ell j}=(\Delta^j\phi_{r\ell})(0)\), equations (6)--(7) and the identity
\(R_{rj}R_{jh}=\binom{r-h}{j-h}R_{rh}\) now yield
\[
A_{r\ell}R_{\ell h}f
=\left\{
\sum_{j=h}^{\min(r,\ell)}
\gamma_{r\ell j}
\binom{n-h-j}{\ell-j}
\binom{r-h}{j-h}
\right\}R_{rh}f.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus each matrix in (6) preserves the harmonic order and acts independently of the harmonic basis index; distinct harmonic orders are orthogonal by (4).  Hence \(Q^{\mathsf T}AQ\) is block diagonal.  For fixed \(h\), its nonzero level coordinates are indexed by
\[
\mathcal R_h=\{r:1\le r\le\beta,\ h\le r\le n-h\},
\]
so the corresponding block \(H_h(p,w)\) has size \(|\mathcal R_h|\le\beta-h+1\) and is repeated \(d_h\) times.  Orders with \(\mathcal R_h=\varnothing\) contribute no block.  Equations (1), (5), and (8) also show directly that every block entry is a finite sum of binomial coefficients and the quantities
\(\sqrt{\omega_r\omega_\ell}\sum_{s,t}w_sw_tK_{st}(S,T)\).  Thus the entries have exactly the computable form asserted in the statement.  Orthogonal similarity preserves eigenvalues, and repeated blocks do not change their maximum, so
\[
\lambda_{\max}(A)
=\max_{0\le h\le\beta}\lambda_{\max}\{H_h(p,w)\}.
\]
This completes the reduction.
