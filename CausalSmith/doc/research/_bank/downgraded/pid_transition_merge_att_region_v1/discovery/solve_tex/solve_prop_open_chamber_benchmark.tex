Fix \(\eta\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,3/20)\) and \(\vartheta\in\mathbb R\) with \(|\vartheta|<1/100\).  We use four elementary techniques: direct full-path factorization to compare the two proposed representations, a two-component spectral factorization to recover the treated arm, affine-mixture elimination to recover the control arm, and finite enumeration of the two possible cross-arm alignments.

First consider support and the probability box.  The only primitive that varies with \(\vartheta\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{29}{100}<c_{130}(\vartheta)=\frac{3}{10}+\vartheta<\frac{31}{100}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Every other primitive coordinate in either displayed representation belongs to \([1/5,4/5]\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}eta\text{-}domain}\) gives \(\eta<3/20<1/5\) and \(U=1-\eta>17/20>4/5\).  Thus (1) and the definition of \(\Theta_\eta\) imply
\[
 \psi^{\diamond}_{\vartheta},\ \psi^{\diamond,\mathrm{sw}}_{\vartheta}\in\Theta_\eta,
 \tag{2}
\]
indeed with strict slack at every face of the box.  In particular, every event on which we condition below has strictly positive probability.

For \(\psi^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}\), direct use of the Bernoulli factorization defining \(F\) gives
\[
 p_1=\frac12\frac35+\frac12\frac25=\frac12,
 \qquad p_0=\frac12,
 \qquad (w_{10},w_{20})=\left(\frac25,\frac35\right),
 \qquad (w_{11},w_{21})=\left(\frac35,\frac25\right).
 \tag{3}
\]
For \(\psi^{\diamond,\mathrm{sw}}_{\vartheta}\), the latent masses are \((3/5,2/5)\) and both assignment probabilities are \(1/2\), so its posterior weights are \((3/5,2/5)\) in each arm.  Its treated components and their weights therefore equal those in the first representation.  In the control arm it assigns weight \(3/5\) to the former second component and weight \(2/5\) to the former first component.  Hence, for each \(i,j,k,l,d\in\mathcal A\), commutativity of the two summands in the defining formula for \(F\) yields
\[
 F(\psi^{\diamond}_{\vartheta})_{ijkld}
 =F(\psi^{\diamond,\mathrm{sw}}_{\vartheta})_{ijkld}
 =P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta,ijkld}.
 \tag{4}
\]

We next prove that (4) exhausts the full global fiber.  For an arm \(d\), define the observable \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix, with row index \(l\) and column index \(i\),
\[
 Q_{jk}^{d}(l,i)
 =P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}(X_1=i,X_2=j,X_3=k,X_4=l\mid D=d).
 \tag{5}
\]
The division in (5) is valid because (3) gives \(p_d=1/2\).  In any two-type representation of this conditional law, put
\[
 q_{zj}=\kappa_{zd3j},\qquad v_{zk}=\kappa_{zd4k},\qquad
 V_k=\begin{pmatrix}1-v_{1k}&1-v_{2k}\\ v_{1k}&v_{2k}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad S_j(z,i)=w_{zd}B(u_{zd};i)B(a_{zi};j).
 \tag{6}
\]
Expanding the definition of \(F\) cell by cell gives the exact factorization
\[
 Q_{jk}^{d}
 =V_k\operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(B(q_{1j};k),B(q_{2j};k)\bigr)S_j.
 \tag{7}
\]

Consider \(d=1\).  In the displayed representation,
\[
 |\det V_0|=\frac35,
 \qquad |\det V_1|=\frac25.
 \tag{8}
\]
Since \(a_{10}=a_{20}=3/10\) and \(a_{11}=a_{21}=4/5\), the determinant in (6) is
\[
 \det S_j=w_{11}w_{21}B(3/10;j)B(4/5;j)(u_{21}-u_{11}),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \det S_0=\frac{21}{3125},
 \qquad \det S_1=\frac{36}{3125}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Every factor \(B(q_{zj};k)\) in the treated version of (7) is at least \(1/5\) for \(|\vartheta|<1/100\).  Taking determinants in (7) and applying (8)--(9) proves the uniform observable rank bound
\[
 |\det Q_{jk}^{1}|
 \ge \frac25\left(\frac15\right)^2\frac{21}{3125}
 =\frac{42}{390625}>0
 \qquad(j,k\in\mathcal A).
 \tag{10}
\]

For completeness, we now perform the spectral recovery rather than merely invoking mixture identifiability.  Cancellation in (7), justified by (10), gives
\[
 Q_{01}^{1}(Q_{00}^{1})^{-1}Q_{10}^{1}(Q_{11}^{1})^{-1}
 =V_1\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\lambda_1(\vartheta),\lambda_2\bigr)V_1^{-1},
 \tag{11}
\]
where
\[
 \lambda_1(\vartheta)
 =\frac{\operatorname{odds}(3/10+\vartheta)}{\operatorname{odds}(7/10)}
 =\frac{3(3+10\vartheta)}{7(7-10\vartheta)},
 \qquad
 \lambda_2
 =\frac{\operatorname{odds}(3/5)}{\operatorname{odds}(1/5)}=6.
 \tag{12}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{odds}(r)=r/(1-r)\), and all denominators are positive by (1).  Monotonicity of the first rational function on the interval in (1) gives
\[
 0<\lambda_1(\vartheta)<\frac{31}{161},
 \qquad
 \bigl(\lambda_2-\lambda_1(\vartheta)\bigr)^2
 >\left(\frac{935}{161}\right)^2>0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus the two one-dimensional eigenspaces of the observable matrix on the left side of (11) are distinct and are exactly the two column spans of \(V_1\).  Each such span has a unique probability-vector representative, obtained by normalizing its positive entries to sum to one.  This recovers \(V_1\) up to permutation.  In the same ordering,
\[
 Q_{00}^{1}(Q_{01}^{1})^{-1}V_1
 =V_0\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{1-q_{10}}{q_{10}},
                                  \frac{1-q_{20}}{q_{20}}\right),
 \tag{14}
\]
so positive column normalization recovers \(V_0\).  Finally, (7) implies
\[
 S_j=(V_0)^{-1}Q_{j0}^{1}+(V_1)^{-1}Q_{j1}^{1}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, the two summands in (15) are respectively
\(\operatorname{diag}(1-q_{1j},1-q_{2j})S_j\) and
\(\operatorname{diag}(q_{1j},q_{2j})S_j\).  Their positive row sums determine \(q_{zj}\); the row sums of \(S_0+S_1\) determine \(w_{z1}\); and positive entry ratios determine \(u_{z1}\) and \(a_{zi}\).  Since the matrices \(Q_{jk}^{1}\) are fixed by the observed law, (10)--(15) apply to every alternative representation in the fiber.  Consequently the entire treated decomposition is unique up to a common type permutation.  In particular, every compatible representation satisfies
\[
 a_{10}=a_{20}=\frac3{10},
 \qquad a_{11}=a_{21}=\frac45.
 \tag{16}
\]

It remains to recover the control arm, for which the conditional law does not depend on \(\vartheta\).  Define the observable conditional moments
\[
 g_{ij}=P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}(X_3=1\mid X_1=i,X_2=j,D=0),
 \qquad
 h_{ij}=P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}(X_3=1,X_4=1\mid X_1=i,X_2=j,D=0).
 \tag{17}
\]
Their conditioning events have positive mass by (2).  Exact substitution into (4) gives
\[
 (g_{ij})=
 \begin{pmatrix}41/115&111/230\\[2pt]23/45&11/30\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 (h_{ij})=
 \begin{pmatrix}103/575&174/575\\[2pt]49/225&14/75\end{pmatrix},
 \tag{18}
\]
and
\[
 P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}(X_4=1\mid X_1=i,X_2=j,X_3=0,D=0)=\frac12
 \quad(i,j\in\mathcal A).
 \tag{19}
\]

In any compatible control decomposition, label its types temporarily and put
\[
 \lambda_i=P(Z=1\mid X_1=i,D=0),
 \qquad q_{zj}=\kappa_{z0,3,j},
 \qquad v_{zk}=\kappa_{z0,4,k}.
\]
Equation (16) makes the conditional law of \(X_2\) given \((X_1,D=0,Z=z)\) independent of \(z\).  Bayes' formula, with positive denominators ensured by (2), therefore shows that the posterior type probability after additionally conditioning on \(X_2=j\) is still \(\lambda_i\).  Hence
\[
 g_{ij}=\lambda_iq_{1j}+(1-\lambda_i)q_{2j}.
 \tag{20}
\]
The observable differences computed from (18) are
\[
 g_{00}-g_{10}=-\frac{32}{207},
 \qquad
 g_{01}-g_{11}=\frac8{69}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Since \(g_{0j}-g_{1j}=(\lambda_0-\lambda_1)(q_{1j}-q_{2j})\), (21) proves
\[
 \lambda_0\ne\lambda_1,
 \qquad q_{1j}\ne q_{2j}\quad(j\in\mathcal A).
 \tag{22}
\]

Multiplying (19) by the positive conditional mass of \(X_3=0\), and using (20), gives for each \(i,j\)
\[
 \lambda_i(1-q_{1j})(v_{10}-1/2)
 +(1-\lambda_i)(1-q_{2j})(v_{20}-1/2)=0.
 \tag{23}
\]
For fixed \(j\), the determinant of the two equations indexed by \(i=0,1\) equals
\[
 (\lambda_0-\lambda_1)(1-q_{1j})(1-q_{2j}).
 \tag{24}
\]
It is nonzero by (22) and the strict upper box bound \(q_{zj}\le U<1\).  The unique solution of (23) is therefore
\[
 v_{10}=v_{20}=\frac12.
 \tag{25}
\]
This derives, rather than assumes, the single collided control terminal row.

For the noncollided row, (18) and (21) yield
\[
 \beta_j=\frac{h_{0j}-h_{1j}}{g_{0j}-g_{1j}},
 \qquad (\beta_0,\beta_1)=\left(\frac14,1\right),
 \tag{26}
\]
and
\[
 h_{ij}-\beta_jg_{ij}=c_j,
 \qquad (c_0,c_1)=\left(\frac9{100},-\frac9{50}\right).
 \tag{27}
\]
Both sides of (27) are affine functions of \(\lambda_i\).  Since \(\lambda_0\ne\lambda_1\), equality at these two distinct arguments forces equality of the two component intercepts and slopes.  Thus
\[
 q_{z0}(v_{z1}-1/4)=\frac9{100},
 \qquad
 q_{z1}(v_{z1}-1)=-\frac9{50}
 \quad(z=1,2).
 \tag{28}
\]
Likewise, the two identities obtained from (18),
\[
 g_{i1}=\frac34(1-g_{i0})\quad(i=0,1),
\]
hold at the two distinct mixing probabilities \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1\).  Applying the same affine-mixture argument to (20) gives
\[
 q_{z1}=\frac34(1-q_{z0})\quad(z=1,2).
 \tag{29}
\]
Writing \(x=q_{z0}\) and \(v=v_{z1}\), equations (28)--(29) reduce to
\[
 x(v-1/4)=\frac9{100},
 \qquad
 (1-x)(1-v)=\frac6{25}.
\]
Eliminating \(x\) yields the factored equation
\[
 50v^2-55v+14=(5v-2)(10v-7)=0.
 \tag{30}
\]
The two roots are distinct.  Equation (22) rules out assigning the same root to both types.  Substitution back into (28)--(29) therefore recovers the unordered control triples exactly as
\[
 (q_{z0},q_{z1},v_{z1})
 \in\left\{
 \left(\frac35,\frac3{10},\frac25\right),
 \left(\frac15,\frac35,\frac7{10}\right)
 \right\}.
 \tag{31}
\]

Label the second triple in (31) as type one.  Equations (18) and (20) then give
\[
 (\lambda_0,\lambda_1)=\left(\frac{14}{23},\frac29\right).
 \tag{32}
\]
The observed control probability \(P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}(X_1=0\mid D=0)=23/50\), together with Bayes' formula and (32), gives
\[
 w_{10}=\frac{23}{50}\frac{14}{23}+\frac{27}{50}\frac29=\frac25,
 \qquad
 u_{10}=\frac{(27/50)(2/9)}{2/5}=\frac3{10}.
 \tag{33}
\]
The other control component consequently has weight \(3/5\) and initial-state probability \(7/10\).  Thus the control decomposition is unique up to permutation.  As an explicit rank check, the determinants in (24), for \(j=0,1\), are respectively
\[
 \frac{128}{1035}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{112}{1035},
 \tag{34}
\]
so no division or inversion in the control recovery approaches a zero denominator along the \(\vartheta\)-family.

The treated and control decompositions are now separately unique up to permutation.  Their only remaining ambiguity is their relative alignment.  The definitions of \(w_{zd}\), \(p_d\), and \(e_z\) imply
\[
 \pi_z=p_0w_{z0}+p_1w_{z1},
 \qquad
 e_z=\frac{p_1w_{z1}}{\pi_z}.
 \tag{35}
\]
Pairing the control weights \((2/5,3/5)\) with treated weights \((3/5,2/5)\) in the displayed order gives \((\pi_1,\pi_2)=(1/2,1/2)\) and \((e_1,e_2)=(3/5,2/5)\).  Crossing the pairing gives \((\pi_1,\pi_2)=(3/5,2/5)\) and \((e_1,e_2)=(1/2,1/2)\).  Both satisfy the strict box bounds in (2), and the two possible bijections between two recovered component sets leave no third alignment.  Therefore
\[
 \widetilde\Phi_\eta(P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta})
 =\{\sigma\psi^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}:\sigma\in\mathfrak S_2\}
 \cup
 \{\sigma\psi^{\diamond,\mathrm{sw}}_{\vartheta}:\sigma\in\mathfrak S_2\}.
 \tag{36}
\]

For either relative alignment and either simultaneous labeling, the first terminal difference in the definition of \(\Theta_{\eta,0}^{1\mathrm c}\) vanishes, while the absolute values of the other three terminal differences are
\[
 \left(\left|\frac7{10}-\frac25\right|,
       \left|\frac15-\frac45\right|,
       \left|\frac7{10}-\frac3{10}\right|\right)
 =\left(\frac3{10},\frac35,\frac25\right).
 \tag{37}
\]
Thus every member of the nonempty global fiber (36) belongs to \(\Theta_{\eta,0}^{1\mathrm c}\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal M_{\eta,0}^{1\mathrm c}\), equations (2), (4), (36), and (37) prove
\[
 P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}\in\mathcal M_{\eta,0}^{1\mathrm c},
 \]
and they also prove that the global and one-collision quotient fibers coincide and contain exactly the two classes represented by \(\psi^{\diamond}_{\vartheta}\) and \(\psi^{\diamond,\mathrm{sw}}_{\vartheta}\).

It remains to evaluate the causal target on these two attainable representations.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}quotient\text{-}att}\) proves that \(\tau\) is invariant under simultaneous type permutation, so one representative of each quotient class suffices.  For the displayed alignment, the recursive definitions of \(m_{z2},m_{z3},m_{z4}\) give
\[
 (m_{12},m_{13},m_{14})=\left(\frac12,\frac25,\frac{29}{50}\right),
 \qquad
 (m_{22},m_{23},m_{24})=\left(\frac35,\frac{21}{50},\frac{229}{500}\right).
 \tag{38}
\]
Only the type-one treated transition from \(X_2=0\) changes with \(\vartheta\).  By (3) and \(P(X_2=0\mid Z=1,D=1)=1-m_{12}=1/2\), direct expectation under \(F\) yields
\[
 O_3(P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta})
 =\frac{111}{250}+w_{11}(1-m_{12})\vartheta
 =\frac{111}{250}+\frac{3\vartheta}{10}.
 \tag{39}
\]
For that type, \(c_{141}-c_{140}=7/10-1/5=1/2\), so iterated expectation gives
\[
 O_4(P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta})
 =\frac{259}{500}+w_{11}(1-m_{12})(c_{141}-c_{140})\vartheta
 =\frac{259}{500}+\frac{3\vartheta}{20}.
 \tag{40}
\]
For the crossed alignment, the same untreated recursions instead give
\[
 (m_{12},m_{13},m_{14})=\left(\frac12,\frac9{20},\frac{91}{200}\right),
 \qquad
 (m_{22},m_{23},m_{24})=\left(\frac35,\frac{11}{25},\frac{147}{250}\right).
 \tag{41}
\]
Using the treated posterior weights \((3/5,2/5)\), equations (38) and (41) imply, respectively,
\[
 \left(\sum_z w_{z1}m_{z3},\sum_z w_{z1}m_{z4}\right)
 =\left(\frac{51}{125},\frac{332}{625}\right)
 \tag{42}
\]
and
\[
 \left(\sum_z w_{z1}m_{z3},\sum_z w_{z1}m_{z4}\right)
 =\left(\frac{223}{500},\frac{2541}{5000}\right).
 \tag{43}
\]
Subtracting (42)--(43) from the observed treated means (39)--(40), exactly as required by the definition of \(\tau\), gives
\[
 \tau(\psi^{\diamond}_{\vartheta};P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta})
 =\left(\frac9{250}+\frac{3\vartheta}{10},
         -\frac{33}{2500}+\frac{3\vartheta}{20}\right)
 \tag{44}
\]
and
\[
 \tau(\psi^{\diamond,\mathrm{sw}}_{\vartheta};P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta})
 =\left(-\frac1{500}+\frac{3\vartheta}{10},
         \frac{49}{5000}+\frac{3\vartheta}{20}\right).
 \tag{45}
\]
Fiber exhaustion in (36) proves attainability and excludes every other value, so (44)--(45) are the sharp joint identified image rather than merely an outer bound.  Their date-three difference is \(19/500\), and their date-four difference in absolute value is \(23/1000\), independently of \(\vartheta\).  Projection gives the asserted marginal two-point images.  Setting \(\vartheta=0\) yields
\[
 \mathcal I_{0,3}^{1\mathrm c}(P^{\diamond})=\{-1/500,9/250\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal I_{0,4}^{1\mathrm c}(P^{\diamond})=\{-33/2500,49/5000\}.
 \tag{46}
\]

Finally, direct expansion of the full-path cell \((i,j,k,l,d)=(0,0,1,1,1)\) gives
\[
 P^{\diamond}_{\vartheta,00111}-P^{\diamond}_{0,00111}
 =\left(\frac12\frac35\frac35\frac7{10}\frac7{10}\right)\vartheta
 =\frac{441}{5000}\vartheta.
 \tag{47}
\]
Every cell is affine in \(\vartheta\), because only one Bernoulli factor appearing to the first power varies; the nonzero coefficient in (47) proves injectivity.  Hence the image of \((-1/100,1/100)\) is a nondegenerate one-dimensional affine segment contained in the one-collision model.  When \(\vartheta\) is rational, every primitive and every full-path probability is rational, and rational \(\vartheta\)'s are dense in this interval.  This proves the stated positive-dimensional rational persistence family.  The argument establishes no relative-openness property in any larger collision variety, in agreement with the scope of the proposition.
