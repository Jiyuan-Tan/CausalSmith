Assume that \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\ne\varnothing\).  For each \(G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\), define the proof-local feasible set
\[
K_G(p)=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p\}
\]
and the set of observationally feasible refinements
\[
\mathfrak G_p=\{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S):K_G(p)\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{1}
\]
These are abbreviations inside the proof, not additions to the causal target.

Fix \(G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\).  Apply \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\) with a singleton query index set whose sole query is the micro CDE in \(\text{def:micro-cde}\).  If \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M(G,p)\), the forward part of that lemma supplies a raw canonical branch and independent factor weights \(\lambda\in\Lambda_G\) for which
\[
P_G(\lambda)=p,
\qquad
q_G(\lambda)=q(\mathcal M).
\tag{2}
\]
The first equality in (2) uses \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\), and the second uses preservation of the specified interventional query by the finite-support reduction.  Thus \(\lambda\in K_G(p)\).

Conversely, let \(\lambda\in K_G(p)\).  By the converse part of \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\), the branch's deterministic tables and factor weights reconstruct a finite binary semi-Markovian SCM \(\mathcal M_\lambda\) on \(G\).  It satisfies \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\), and drawing the private and pairwise edge parents from their separate simplex factors gives exactly \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\).  The defining equality \(P_G(\lambda)=p\) supplies \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\).  Hence \(\mathcal M_\lambda\in\mathfrak M(G,p)\).  The observational and interventional recursions used by \(\text{def:raw-polynomial-compiler}\) are the recursions of this reconstructed SCM, so
\[
q(\mathcal M_\lambda)=q_G(\lambda).
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) prove, including when both sides are empty,
\[
\{q(\mathcal M):\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M(G,p)\}
=\{q_G(\lambda):\lambda\in K_G(p)\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Taking the union of (4) over all refinements and invoking \(\text{def:summary-fiber}\) and \(\text{def:sharp-envelope}\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta(\mathcal S,L,H,p)
&=\bigcup_{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}
  \{q_G(\lambda):\lambda\in K_G(p)\}\\
&=\bigcup_{G\in\mathfrak G_p}
  \{q_G(\lambda):\lambda\in K_G(p)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The second equality deletes only empty sets.  Because the original SCM fiber is nonempty, its image under \(q\) is nonempty; (5) therefore shows that \(\mathfrak G_p\ne\varnothing\).

It remains to prove that all extrema written in the corrected display exist.  For a fixed branch \(r\in\mathcal R_G\), put
\[
\Lambda_{G,r}=\prod_a\Delta(\mathcal K_{G,r,a}).
\]
There are finitely many factors \(a\), and every \(\mathcal K_{G,r,a}\) is finite.  Each probability simplex is a closed and bounded subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, hence compact; therefore the finite product \(\Lambda_{G,r}\) is compact.  By \(\text{def:raw-polynomial-compiler}\), the restriction of \(P_G\) to this branch is polynomial and thus continuous.  Consequently
\[
K_{G,r}(p)=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G,r}:P_G(\lambda)=p\}
\tag{6}
\]
is closed in \(\Lambda_{G,r}\) and is compact.  The raw catalogue \(\mathcal R_G\) is finite, so \(K_G(p)\), the disjoint union of the sets in (6), is a finite union of compact branch sets and is compact.  The objective \(q_G\) is polynomial and continuous on every branch by \(\text{def:raw-polynomial-compiler}\).  Hence, whenever \(G\in\mathfrak G_p\), the nonempty compact set \(K_G(p)\) has points \(\lambda_G^-\) and \(\lambda_G^+\) satisfying
\[
q_G(\lambda_G^-)=\min_{\lambda\in K_G(p)}q_G(\lambda),
\qquad
q_G(\lambda_G^+)=\max_{\lambda\in K_G(p)}q_G(\lambda).
\tag{7}
\]

By \(\text{lem:finite-refinements}\), \(\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) is finite.  Its nonempty subset \(\mathfrak G_p\) is therefore finite and nonempty.  Taking the least and greatest of the finitely many attained values in (7), equation (5) and the endpoint definitions in \(\text{def:sharp-envelope}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\underline q(p)
&=\min_{\substack{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\\K_G(p)\ne\varnothing}}
  \min_{\lambda\in K_G(p)}q_G(\lambda),\\
\overline q(p)
&=\max_{\substack{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\\K_G(p)\ne\varnothing}}
  \max_{\lambda\in K_G(p)}q_G(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Replacing the proof-local abbreviation \(K_G(p)\) in (8) by \(\{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p\}\) is exactly the displayed formula in the corrected statement.  In particular, no minimum or maximum is taken over an empty set.

Finally, the converse construction used in (3) applies to every feasible response-weight point, including the four levels of optimizers in (7)--(8).  It assigns one mutually independent finite exogenous variable to each private index and each displayed pairwise bidirected-edge index.  Several pairwise parents may be incident to the same observed vertex, but they remain distinct independent factors, so overlapping latent-parent incidence is preserved exactly.  Conversely, (2) represents every compatible SCM with the same observed law and micro CDE.  Thus (5) is equality of attainable images, the endpoints in (8) are attained by compatible SCMs, and the interval is sharp rather than merely a valid outer bound.
